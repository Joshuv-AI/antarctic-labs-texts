Non Kazhdan’s groups and a new approximation property for tracial von Neumann algebras
Paul Jolissaint

Abstract
We define a new approximation property for tracial von Neumann algebras, called weakly mixing approximation property which, for discrete groups and II 1 factors, is equivalent to the negation of Kazhdan’s property (T).



Mathematics Subject Classification:
46L10, 22D55

Key words:
Weakly mixing representations, weakly almost periodic functions, tracial von Neumann algebras, Kazhdan’s property (T).

1
Introduction

For several years, many approximation properties for discrete groups have been translated with success to their associated von Neumann algebras; it was first the case for amenability, then for the Haagerup property, and for Kazhdan’s property (T), starting in
[
11
]
. More precisely, property (T) for finite von Neumann algebras defined in the latter article is equivalent to Kazhdan’s property (T) for groups with finitely many finite conjugacy classes only, see
[
13
, Theorem A]
. Next, equivalent definitions of property (T) for finite von Neumann algebras (and for pairs of such algebras) were defined and studied rather extensively: see for instance
[
1
]
and
[
21
]
.
Thus, we think that it is interesting to have another characterization for its negation, and that is what is presented in our note.

Our approach is based on the notion of coefficients of unitary representations on bimodules coming from completely positive maps on tracial von Neumann algebras; as these coefficients are weakly almost periodic functions (as are all coefficients of unitary representations), this allows to consider weakly mixing representations (see Section 2). Thus, we introduce an approximation property for tracial von Neumann algebras, called
weakly mixing approximation property
(WMAP), which means that, for a given tracial von Neumann algebra
(M,\tau)
, there exists a net of completely positive maps
(\phi_{i})
on
M
such that
\lim_{i}\|\phi_{i}(x)-x\|_{2}=0
for every
x\in M
and such that the unitary representation
\pi_{i}
of the unitary group
U_{M}
on the
M
-bimodule
\mathcal{H}_{\phi_{i}}
defined by
\pi_{i}(u)\xi=u\xi u^{*}
is weakly mixing; see Definition
3.1
.

Although we had hoped to have discovered a complete characterization of tracial von Neumann algebras which do not have property (T), we realized that it holds only for infinite discrete groups and their associated von Neumann algebras, and for II
1
factors. In fact, we give below a rather simple example of a tracial von Neumann algebra that has neither property (T) nor the WMAP.

Here is our main result:

Theorem 1.1

(1)

If
(M,\tau)
is a tracial von Neumann algebra that has the WMAP, and if
\tau^{\prime}
is another normal, faithful tracial state on
M
, then
(M,\tau^{\prime})
has the same property.

(2)

If
G
is a discrete group, then it does not have Kazhdan’s property (T) if and only if its associated von Neumann algebra
L(G)
has the WMAP.

(3)

If
(M,\tau)
is a tracial von Neumann algebra that has the WMAP, then it does not have property (T).

(4)

If moreover
(M,\tau)
is
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor, then it has the WMAP if and only if it does not have property (T).

As will be reminded in Section 3, every finite, atomic von Neumann algebra with separable predual has property (T), and thus it does not have the WMAP.

Here is the promised example of a tracial, diffuse von Neumann algebra that has neither property (T) nor the WMAP:

Example 1.2

Let
A
be a II
1
factor with property (T) and let
B
be a II
1
factor which does not have property (T). Set
M=A\oplus B
. Then, as property (T) is stable by reduction by nonzero projections of
M
(
[
21
, Proposition 4.7]
),
M
does not have property (T). As is proven in Proposition
3.3
, the WMAP is also preserved by reduction by nonzero central projections. Hence by Theorem
1.1
(3),
M
cannot have the WMAP either.

The rest of the article is organized as follows: Section 2 contains known material on weakly mixing representations that will be needed in Section 3, the latter being devoted to the definition and study of WMAP, including the proof of the main results. Our undefined notations stick to those of the monograph
[
1
]
, except that our scalar products are linear in the first variable.

2
Unitary representations of topological groups, non Kazhdan groups and weak mixing

We gather in this section known definitions and results that will be useful in Section 3.

Let
G
be a topological group, which is assumed to be Hausdorff throughout the article, and denote by
e
the identity element of
G
. Let
C_{\mathrm{b}}(G)
(resp.
C_{\mathrm{b,r}}(G)
) be the commutative
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded, continuous (resp. uniformly right continuous)
functions on
G
equipped with the uniform norm
\|f\|_{\infty}\coloneqq\sup_{s\in G}|f(s)|
. As in
[
17
]
, for
g\in G
and
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, we denote by
g\cdot f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
(resp.
f\cdot g
) the left (resp. right) translate of
f
by
g
, i.e.

(g\cdot f)(s)=f(g^{-1}s)\quad\textrm{and}\quad(f\cdot g)(s)=f(sg)

for all
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
g,s\in G
, the corresponding left (resp. right) orbit being denoted by
Gf
(resp.
fG
).

Thus, a function
f\in C_{\mathrm{b}}(G)
belongs to
C_{\mathrm{b,r}}(G)
if and only if
\|g\cdot f-f\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
g\to e
.

A function
f\in C_{\mathrm{b,r}}(G)
is
weakly almost periodic
if its orbit
Gf
is weakly relatively compact in
C_{\mathrm{b,r}}(G)
. It turns out that if
f\in C_{\mathrm{b,r}}(G)
is weakly almost periodic, then its right orbit
fG
is also weakly relatively compact (
[
9
, Corollary 1.12]
).

We denote by
\operatorname{WAP}(G)
the sub-
C^{*}
-algebra of weakly almost periodic functions on
G
. (We observe that the right uniform continuity of almost periodic functions is used to prove that
\operatorname{WAP}(G)
is indeed a
C^{*}
-algebra: see the proof of
[
17
, Proposition 5.4]
.)

The main feature of
\operatorname{WAP}(G)
is that it admits a unique bi-invariant mean (see for instance
[
9
, Chapter 1]
or
[
17
, Theorem 5.5]
), more precisely:

Theorem 2.1

Let
G
be a topological group. There exists a unique linear functional
\mathfrak{m}:\operatorname{WAP}(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with the following properties:

(i)

\mathfrak{m}(f)\geq 0
for every
f\geq 0
;

(ii)

\mathfrak{m}(1)=1
;

(iii)

\mathfrak{m}(g\cdot f)=\mathfrak{m}(f\cdot g)=\mathfrak{m}(f)
for all
f\in\operatorname{WAP}(G)
and

g\in G
;

(iv)

for every
f\in\operatorname{WAP}(G)
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a convex combination
\displaystyle{\psi:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}t_{j}{g_{j}}\cdot f}
(with
g_{j}\in G
and
t_{j}\geqslant 0
,
\sum\limits_{j}t_{j}=1
) such that

\|\psi-\mathfrak{m}(f)\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon,

and there exists a convex combination
\displaystyle{\varphi:=\sum_{i}s_{i}f\cdot h_{i}}
(with
h_{i}\in G
and
s_{i}\geqslant 0
,
\displaystyle{\sum_{i}s_{i}=1}
) such that

\|\varphi-\mathfrak{m}(f)\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon.

The following result is used in the next section, and as we do not know if it is already known, we provide a proof.

Proposition 2.2

Let
G
be a topological group and
H
a closed subgroup of
G
. Let us denote by
\mathfrak{m}_{G}
(resp.
\mathfrak{m}_{H}
) the bi-invariant mean on
\operatorname{WAP}(G)
(resp.
\operatorname{WAP}(H)
). Then
\mathfrak{m}_{H}(f|_{H})=\mathfrak{m}_{G}(f)
for every
f\in\operatorname{WAP}(G)
.

Proof.
Let us fix
f\in\operatorname{WAP}(G)
; replacing it by
f-\mathfrak{m}_{G}(f)
, we assume that
\mathfrak{m}_{G}(f)=0
. Referring to the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 5.5]
, let us set the following notations:

•

Q_{\mathrm{l}}(f,G)
(resp.
Q_{\mathrm{r}}(f,G)
) denotes the norm-closed convex hull of the left orbit
Gf
(resp. right orbit
fG
) in
C_{\mathrm{b,r}}(G)
.

•

Similarly,
Q_{\mathrm{l}}(f|_{H},H)
and
Q_{\mathrm{r}}(f|_{H},H)
denote the corresponding sets in
C_{\mathrm{b,r}}(H)
.

•

Finally,
K_{\mathrm{l}}(f)
(resp.
K_{\mathrm{r}}(f)
) denotes the norm-closed convex hull of
Hf
(resp.
fH
) in
C_{\mathrm{b,r}}(G)
.

Then obviously,
K_{\mathrm{l}}(f)\subset Q_{\mathrm{l}}(f,G)
and
K_{\mathrm{r}}(f)\subset Q_{\mathrm{r}}(f,G)
, so that
K_{\mathrm{l}}(f)
and
K_{\mathrm{r}}(f)
are weakly compact. As the left and right actions of
H
on
K_{\mathrm{l}}(f)
and
K_{\mathrm{r}}(f)
respectively are obviously distal, by Ryll-Nardzewski Theorem,
K_{\mathrm{l}}(f)
and
K_{\mathrm{r}}(f)
both contain constant functions, and, by a standard convexity argument, these constant functions are unique and equal, which is denoted by
c(f)
. As
K_{\mathrm{l}}(f)\cap K_{\mathrm{r}}(f)\subset Q_{\mathrm{l}}(f,G)\cap Q_{\mathrm{r}}(f,G)
, one has necessarily
c(f)=\mathfrak{m}_{G}(f)=0
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
; there exists
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}>0
,
\sum_{j}t_{j}=1
, and
h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in H
such that

\sup_{g\in G}\left|\sum_{j}t_{j}f(h_{j}^{-1}g)\right|<\varepsilon.

A fortiori,

\sup_{h\in H}\left|\sum_{j}t_{j}f(h_{j}^{-1}h)\right|<\varepsilon,

which shows that
0\in Q_{\mathrm{l}}(f|_{H},H)\cap Q_{\mathrm{r}}(f|_{H},H)
hence that
\mathfrak{m}_{H}(f|_{H})=0
.

\square

A
unitary representation
of
G
(often called a
representation
hereafter for short) is a pair
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
where
\pi:G\rightarrow U(\mathcal{H})
is a continuous homomorphism from
G
to the unitary group of
\mathcal{H}
equipped with the strong operator topology. This means that, for every
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, the map
g\mapsto\pi(g)\xi
is continuous.

Given such a representation and vectors
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, the associated
coefficient function

\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}
is defined on
G
by

\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}(g)\coloneqq\langle\pi(g)\xi|\eta\rangle\quad(g\in G).

One has for all
g,h\in G
:

g\cdot(\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta})\cdot h=(\pi(h)\xi)*_{\pi}(\overline{\pi(g)\eta})

and it is well known and easy to verify that every coefficient function belongs to
C_{\mathrm{b,r}}(G)
, and even to
\operatorname{WAP}(G)
; the latter assertion follows from the weak compactness of the orbit
\{\pi(g)\eta\colon g\in G\}
in
\mathcal{H}
and from the continuity of the map
\pi(g)\eta\mapsto\xi*_{\pi}\overline{\pi(g)\eta}=g\cdot(\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta})
with respect to the weak topologies (see for instance
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
).

If
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
is a unitary representation of
G
, then the function
\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\xi}
is of positive type for every
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, and conversely, to every function of positive type
\varphi
, the GNS construction associates a unique triple (up to unitary isomorphism)
(\pi_{\varphi},\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},\xi_{\varphi})
where
(\pi_{\varphi},\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
is a unitary representation of
G
,
\xi_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
is a cyclic vector, i.e. the orbit
\pi_{\varphi}(G)\xi_{\varphi}
is a total subset of
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
, and

\varphi=\xi_{\varphi}*_{\pi_{\varphi}}\overline{\xi_{\varphi}}
. See for instance
[
5
, Theorem C.4.10]
.

For later use, given a representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, let us recall the following useful formula for
\mathfrak{m}(\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta})
:
denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\pi}
the subspace of invariant vectors under the action of
\pi(G)
, and by
P_{\pi}
the orthogonal projection from
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathcal{H}^{\pi}
. Then one has

\mathfrak{m}(\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta})=\langle P_{\pi}\xi|\eta\rangle=\langle\xi|P_{\pi}\eta\rangle.

(1)

It is a consequence of Theorem
2.1
(iv) and of the fact that
P_{\pi}\zeta
is the element of minimal norm in the norm-closed convex hull of
\pi(G)\zeta
for every
\zeta\in\mathcal{H}
.

Definition 2.3

Let
G
be a topological group.

(1)

(
[
8
, Definition 1.1]
) A representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
of
G
is
weakly mixing
if for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, one has
\mathfrak{m}(|\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|)=0
.

(2)

Let
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
be a unitary representation of
G
. Then
\pi

almost has invariant vectors
if, for every compact subset
Q\not=\emptyset
of
G
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a unit vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\sup_{g\in Q}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|<\varepsilon.

We denote that property by
1_{G}\prec\pi
, as it corresponds to the weak containment of the trivial representation in the case of locally compact groups.

(3)

(
[
15
, Definition 1.1(2)]
) The topological group
G
has
Kazhdan’s property (T)
if, for every unitary representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
of
G
such that
1_{G}\prec\pi
, there exists a nonzero vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\pi(g)\xi=\xi
for every
g\in G
.

As one more application of Theorem
2.1
(iv), it is easy to see that, for
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, one has
\mathfrak{m}(|\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|)=0
if and only if
\mathfrak{m}(|\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|^{2})=0
, and it is
obvious that, if a representation contains a nonzero, invariant vector
\xi
, then the associated coefficient is a nonzero constant, hence the representation cannot be weakly mixing. This implies that if
G
has a weakly mixing representation that almost has invariant vectors, then
G
does not have property (T).

Conversely, if
G
is moreover locally compact and
\sigma
-compact, then
the converse is true, as the following result shows (see
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
where the result is implicit). In case
G
is moreover separable, see also
[
7
, Theorem 1]
. In fact, it rests essentially on the fact that a locally compact,
\sigma
-compact group
G
does not have property (T) if and only if it admits an unbounded real-valued function conditionally of negative type: see
[
13
, Lemma 4.4]
and the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 2.4

Suppose that
G
is a locally compact,
\sigma
-compact group. Then it admits a weakly mixing representation such that
1_{G}\prec\pi
if and only if it does not have Kazhdan’s property (T).

Given
f\in\operatorname{WAP}(G)
, it can be somewhat complicated to verify that
\mathfrak{m}(|f|)=0
. The following criterium for coefficients of representations will be used here. It is essentially contained in
[
8
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.9]
, which are stated and proved there for locally compact,
\sigma
-compact groups, but their proofs hold for arbitrary topological groups. We provide here an essentially self-contained proof for the sake of completeness and for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 2.5

Let
G
be a topological group,
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
a unitary representation of
G
and
\mathcal{T}
any total subset of
\mathcal{H}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

[respectively (a’)] the representation
\pi
is weakly mixing [respectively, for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{T}
, one has
\mathfrak{m}(|\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|)=0
];

(b)

[respectively (b’)] for all vectors
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
[respectively for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{T}
], for every finite set
S\subset G
and for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
g\in G
such that

\max_{s\in S}|\langle\pi(gs)\xi|\eta\rangle|<\varepsilon;

(c)

the representation
\pi
does not contain any nonzero finite-dimensional subrepresentation.

Proof.
First, it follows from property (iv) of Theorem
2.1
that, if
f\in\operatorname{WAP}(G)
is a real-valued function, then

\inf_{g\in G}f(g)\leq\mathfrak{m}(f)\leq\sup_{g\in G}f(g).

(2)

Equivalences between (a) and (a’) on the one hand, and of (b) and (b’) on the other hand, follow from the positivity of the mean
\mathfrak{m}
, from standard approximation arguments and from the following general inequality valid for all vectors
\xi,\xi^{\prime},\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
:

\|\xi*_{\pi},\bar{\eta}-\xi^{\prime}*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\xi-\xi^{\prime}\|\|\eta\|+\|\xi^{\prime}\|\|\eta-\eta^{\prime}\|.

(a)\ \Rightarrow\ (b)
. Assume that
\pi
is weakly mixing and let
\xi,\eta,S
and
\varepsilon>0
be as in (b). By invariance of
\mathfrak{m}
, the equality
\mathfrak{m}(|\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|)=0
implies that

\mathfrak{m}\Big(\sum_{s\in S}|\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|\cdot s\Big)=\mathfrak{m}\Big(\sum_{s\in S}|(\pi(s)\xi)*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|\Big)=0

and thus, by the first inequality in (
2
),

\inf_{g\in G}\Big(\sum_{s\in S}|\langle\pi(gs)\xi|\eta\rangle|\Big)=0

which implies in turn the existence of some
g\in G
such that

\sum_{s\in S}|\langle\pi(gs)\xi|\eta\rangle|<\varepsilon.

(b)\ \Rightarrow\ (a)
. Let
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Set
f=|\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|
. Again by Theorem
2.1
(iv), there exists
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\geq 0
,
\sum_{j}t_{j}=1
, such that

\sup_{g\in G}\left|\sum_{j}t_{j}f(g_{j}^{-1}g)-\mathfrak{m}(f)\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

But, as
f(g_{j}^{-1}g)=|\langle\pi(g_{j}^{-1}g)\xi|\eta\rangle|=|\langle\pi(g^{-1}g_{j})\eta|\xi\rangle|
, by (b), there is some
g\in G
such that
f(g_{j}^{-1}g)<\varepsilon/2
for every
j=1,\ldots,n
, hence

\mathfrak{m}(f)=|\mathfrak{m}(f)|\leq\left|\mathfrak{m}(f)-\sum_{j}t_{j}f(g_{j}^{-1}g)\right|+\sum_{j}t_{j}f(g_{j}^{-1}g)<\varepsilon.

This proves that
\pi
is weakly mixing.

(b)\ \Rightarrow\ (c)
. If
\pi
contains a nonzero finite-dimensional subrepresentation, then there exists a unit vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
whose orbit
\pi(G)\xi
is relatively compact with respect to the norm topology. Then, let
\varepsilon>0
be small enough so that
\varepsilon^{2}/2+\varepsilon<1/2
. There exists a finite set
S\subset G
such that

\pi(G)\xi\subset\bigcup_{s\in S}\{\eta\colon\|\eta-\pi(s)\xi\|<\varepsilon\}.

If
\pi
satisfies (b), there exists
g\in G
such that
|\langle\pi(g)\xi|\pi(s)\xi\rangle|<\varepsilon
for every
s\in S
. Thus, let
s=s(g)\in S
be such that

\|\pi(g)\xi-\pi(s)\xi\|<\varepsilon
. Then we would get

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|\pi(g)\xi-\pi(s)\xi\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2-2\mathrm{Re}\langle\pi(g)\xi|\pi(s)\xi\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 2(1-\varepsilon),

which would imply that
1\leq\varepsilon^{2}/2+\varepsilon<1/2
, a contradiction.

(c)\ \Rightarrow\ (a)
. Assume that
\pi
is not weakly mixing. Then there exist unit vectors
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathfrak{m}(|\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|^{2})>0
. But

|\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|^{2}(g)=\langle\pi\otimes\bar{\pi}(g)\xi\otimes\bar{\xi}|\eta\otimes\bar{\eta}\rangle

where
(\bar{\pi},\bar{\mathcal{H}})
denotes the conjugate representation of
\pi
(
[
5
, Definition A.1.10]
).
Hence, by formula (
1
),

\mathfrak{m}(|\xi*_{\pi}\bar{\eta}|^{2})=\langle P_{\pi\otimes\bar{\pi}}\xi\otimes\bar{\xi}|\eta\otimes\bar{\eta}\rangle>0

which shows that the representation
\pi\otimes\bar{\pi}
contains a nonzero invariant vector, and by
[
5
, Proposition A.1.12]
, that
\pi
has a nonzero finite-dimensional subrepresentation.

\square

The following proposition is a consequence of the fact that every unitary representation is a direct sum of cyclic representations

.

Proposition 2.6

Let
G
be a topological group. Then it admits a weakly mixing representation such that
1_{G}\prec\pi
if and only if there exists a generalized sequence
(\varphi_{i})
of normalized functions of positive type on
G
such that:

•

for every
i
,
\mathfrak{m}(|\varphi_{i}|)=0
;

•

for every compact subset
Q\not=\emptyset
of
G
, one has

\lim_{i}\Big(\sup_{g\in Q}|\varphi_{i}(g)-1|\Big)=0.

For future use, recall that a topological group
G
is
minimally almost periodic
if its only finite-dimensional unitary representation is the trivial one (see
[
4
, Section 4.C.d]
). Then we make the following observation.

Proposition 2.7

Let
G
be a minimally almost periodic group and let
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
be a unitary representation of
G
. Then
\pi
is weakly mixing if and only if it has no nonzero invariant vector.

3
A weakly mixing approximation property for tracial von Neumann algebras

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra where
\tau
is a normal, faithful tracial state on
M
.
We denote by
(M)_{1}
the unit ball of
M
with respect to the operator norm, by
U_{M}
its unitary group and by
Z_{M}
its center.

We denote by
L^{2}M
the usual
M
-bimodule obtained by completion of
M
for the scalar product
(x,y)\mapsto\tau(y^{*}x)
, and by
\|\cdot\|_{2}
the corresponding norm, and when it is necessary to specify which trace is used, it is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{2,\tau}
. We denote by
x\mapsto\hat{x}
the embedding of
M
into the corresponding dense subspace of
L^{2}M
. The bimodule structure comes from the natural product
x\hat{y}z=\widehat{xyz}
for all
x,y,z\in M
.

The group
U_{M}
is equipped with the bi-invariant metric
d(u,v)\coloneqq\|u-v\|_{2}
which makes it a complete metric topological group (which is Polish if
M
has separable predual).

We denote by
\mathrm{CP}(M)
the convex set of all normal, completely positive maps
\phi:M\rightarrow M
, and we say that
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
is
subunital
if moreover
\phi(1)\leq 1
.

The set
\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(M)
is the subset of all
subtracial
(i.e.
\tau\circ\phi\leq\tau
) elements of
\mathrm{CP}(M)
. Let
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(M)
; the linear map
\hat{\phi}:\hat{x}\mapsto\widehat{\phi(x)}
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}M
, and moreover, by
[
21
, Lemma 1.1.5]
, there exists a
\phi^{*}\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
such that
\tau(\phi(x)y)=\tau(x\phi^{*}(y))
for all
x,y\in M
. It is subunital, and if
\phi
is subunital, then
\phi^{*}
belongs to
\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(M)
. Finally, one has
\widehat{\phi^{*}}=\hat{\phi}^{*}
.

To every
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
corresponds a
pointed

M
-bimodule
(\mathcal{H}_{\phi},\xi_{\phi})
characterized by the following properties (see
[
21
, Subsection 1.1.2]
) :

•

the vector
\xi_{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
is
cyclic
in the sense that the subspace formed by all finite sums
\sum_{i}x_{i}\xi_{\phi}y_{i}
,
x_{i},y_{i}\in M
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
; the latter subspace is denoted by
\operatorname{Span}(M\xi_{\phi}M)
;

•

the scalar product on
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
is entirely determined by the following equality:

\langle x_{1}\xi_{\phi}y_{1}|x_{2}\xi_{\phi}y_{2}\rangle=\tau(\phi(x_{2}^{*}x_{1})y_{1}y_{2}^{*})\quad(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in M).

(3)

As above, if we need to specify that the particular trace
\tau
is used with the cp map
\phi
, we will write
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle_{\tau}
for the above scalar product.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
M
-bimodule. Its
adjoint

\overline{\mathcal{H}}
is the conjugate Hilbert space of
\mathcal{H}
equiped with the actions of
M
defined by
x\cdot\overline{\xi}\cdot y=\overline{y^{*}\xi x^{*}}
for all
x,y\in M
and
\overline{\xi}\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}
. If
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(M)
then one has
(\overline{\mathcal{H}},\overline{\xi_{\phi}})=(\mathcal{H}_{\phi^{*}},\xi_{\phi^{*}})
.

Following
[
21
, Section 1.1.2]
, one associates a cp map to the following pairs of pointed
M
-bimodules
(\mathcal{H},\xi)
: let
(\mathcal{H},\xi)
be a pointed
M
-bimodule such that
\langle\xi\cdot|\xi\rangle\leq c\tau
for some
c>0
. Let
T:L^{2}M\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be the unique bounded operator defined by
T\hat{y}=\xi y
for every
y\in M
. Then
\langle\xi y|\xi y\rangle\leq c\|\hat{y}\|_{2}^{2}
, so that
\|T\|\leq\sqrt{c}
. It turns out that
\phi_{(\mathcal{H},\xi)}\coloneq T^{*}xT
belongs to
M
for every
x\in M
, so that it defines a cp map on
M
. If we denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
the closure of
\operatorname{Span}(M\xi M)
, then
U:\mathcal{H}_{\phi}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, defined by
U(x\xi_{\phi}y)=x\xi y
, is easily seen to be an
M
-bimodule isomorphism.

By functional calculus, it is easy to prove that, given a Hilbert
M
-bimodule
\mathcal{H}
, the subset
\mathcal{H}^{0}
of
left and right bounded vectors

\xi\in\mathcal{H}
for which there exists a constant
c>0
such that
\langle\xi\cdot|\xi\rangle\leq c\tau
and
\langle\cdot\ \xi|\xi\rangle\leq c\tau
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
; thus every Hilbert
M
-bimodule is a direct sum of bimodules associated to cp maps of the form
\phi_{(\mathcal{H},\xi)}
with
\xi\in\mathcal{H}^{0}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
M
-bimodule. The following unitary representation
(\pi_{\mathcal{H}},\mathcal{H})
of
U_{M}
plays a central role here; it is defined by

\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\xi=u\xi u^{*}\quad(u\in U_{M},\xi\in\mathcal{H}).

For
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
, we write
\pi_{\phi}
instead of
\pi_{\mathcal{H}_{\phi}}
.

Motivated by Proposition
2.6
, we propose the following definition.

Definition 3.1

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra.

(1)

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
. Then we say that it is
weakly mixing
if the representation
\pi_{\phi}
is weakly mixing; we denote by
\operatorname{WM}(M)
the set of all weakly mixing elements of
\mathrm{CP}(M)
, and we also set
\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)=\operatorname{WM}(M)\cap\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(M)
.

(2)

We say that
(M,\tau)
has the
weakly mixing approximation property
(
WMAP
) if there exists a generalized sequence
(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\operatorname{WM}(M)
such that

\lim_{i}\|\phi_{i}(x)-x\|_{2,\tau}=0\quad(x\in M).

Remark 3.2

A notion of (left) weakly mixing property for
M
-bimodules was introduced in
[
20
]
and was used by H. Tan in
[
22
]
to prove among others in
[
22
, Theorem 1.3]
that, for a II
1
factor
M
, the negation of property (T) for
M
implies the existence of weakly mixing bimodules almost having
M
-central vectors.

Recall that the
M
-bimodule
\mathcal{H}
is
(left) weakly mixing
if it satisfies the following equivalent conditions (see
[
6
, Theorem A.2.2]
):

(i)

there exists a sequence
(u_{n})\subset U_{M}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\sup_{b\in(M)_{1}}|\langle u_{n}\xi b|\eta\rangle|\Big)=0

for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
;

(ii)

the
M
-bimodule
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\overline{\mathcal{H}}
has no nonzero central vector.

Given
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
, the weakly mixing property of
\phi
defined here is certainly weaker than the left weakly mixing property of the
M
-bimodule
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
. Indeed, if
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
is (left) weakly mixing, then by condition (i) above,
\pi_{\phi}
satisfies condition (b) in Proposition
2.5
, which means that
\phi
is weakly mixing in our sense. We do not know whether the converse holds true, though.

Nevertheless, we make the following observation: suppose that
M
is diffuse; condition (ii) above applied to the
M
-bimodule
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
means that the unitary representation
\pi_{\mathcal{H}_{\phi}\otimes_{M}\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\phi}}}
of
U_{M}
by conjugation has no nonzero invariant vector. But, by
[
17
, Theorem 4.4]
,
U_{M}
is minimally almost periodic and thus Proposition 2.7 implies that
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
is (left) weakly mixing if and only if
\pi_{\mathcal{H}_{\phi}\otimes_{M}\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\phi}}}
is a weakly mixing representation.

Before studying WMAP further, let us prove the following result, which states in particular that, if
M
is diffuse and if it has the Haagerup property (
[
10
,
14
]
), then it has the WMAP. Following a suggestion of the referee, we remind the reader that the Haagerup property is characterized in
[
2
, Theorem 3.4]
and in
[
18
, Theorem 9]
by the existence of a
strictly mixing

M
-bimodule that weakly contains the identity
M
-bimodule
L^{2}M
; for bimodules, strict mixing is stronger than weak mixing (
[
18
, Definition 4]
).

Proposition 3.3

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra.

(1)

If
(M,\tau)
has the WMAP then so does
(zM,\tau_{z})
for every nonzero central projection
z
.

(2)

If
M
is diffuse and if
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(M)
is
L^{2}
-compact, i.e. the operator
\hat{\phi}\in B(L^{2}M)
is compact, then
\phi\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)
. In particular, if
M
has the Haagerup property then it has the WMAP.

(3)

If
M
is atomic, then
\operatorname{WM}(M)=\{0\}
, and hence
M
does not have the WMAP.

Proof.
(1) Recall first that the normalized trace
\tau_{z}
on
zM
is defined by

\tau_{z}(zx)=\frac{1}{\tau(z)}\tau(zx)\quad(x\in M).

Let then
(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\operatorname{WM}(M)
be as in Definition
3.1
. For every
i\in I
, let
\psi_{i}\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
be defined by
\psi_{i}(zx)=z\phi_{i}(zx)
for every
x\in M
. Then one has for all
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in M
and
u\in U_{M}
:

\displaystyle[(zx_{1})\xi_{\psi_{i}}(zy_{1})*_{\pi_{\psi_{i}}}\overline{(zx_{2})\xi_{\psi_{i}}(zy_{2})}](zu)

\displaystyle=\langle zu(zx_{1})\xi_{\psi_{i}}(zy_{1})(zu^{*})|(zx_{2})\xi_{\psi_{i}}(zy_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau(z)}\tau(\phi_{i}(zx_{2}^{*}zux_{1})zy_{1}uy_{2}^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau(z)}\langle u(zx_{1})\xi_{\phi_{i}}(zy_{1})u^{*}|(zx_{2})\xi_{\phi_{i}}(zy_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau(z)}\cdot[(zx_{1})\xi_{\phi_{i}}(zx_{2})*_{\pi_{\phi_{i}}}\overline{(zx_{2})\xi_{\phi_{i}}(zy_{2})}](u).

This proves that
(\psi_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\operatorname{WM}(zM)
. Finally, one has for every
x\in M
:

\|\psi_{i}(zx)-zx\|_{2,\tau_{z}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau(z)}}\|z\phi_{i}(zx)-zx\|_{2,\tau}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau(z)}}\|\phi_{i}(zx)-zx\|_{2,\tau}\to_{i}0.

(2) Let
x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2}\in M
and
S\subset U_{M}
be a finite set. Since
M
is diffuse,
U_{M}
contains a sequence
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
which converges weakly to
0
. Using Proposition
2.5
(b), it suffices to prove that

\sum_{s\in S}|\langle\pi_{\phi}(u_{n}s)x_{1}\xi_{\phi}y_{1}|x_{2}\xi_{\phi}y_{2}\rangle|\to 0

(4)

as
n\to\infty
.

We have, for
s\in S
and
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{\phi}(u_{n}s)x_{1}\xi_{\phi}y_{1}|x_{2}\xi_{\phi}y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{n}sx_{1}\xi_{\phi}y_{1}|x_{2}\xi_{\phi}y_{2}u_{n}s\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau(\phi(x_{2}^{*}u_{n}sx_{1})y_{1}s^{*}u_{n}^{*}y_{2}^{*})

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{\phi}(\widehat{x_{2}^{*}u_{n}sx_{1}})|\widehat{y_{2}u_{n}sy_{1}^{*}}\rangle.

But
\widehat{x_{2}^{*}u_{n}sx_{1}}\to 0
weakly as
n\to\infty
, thus, as
\hat{\phi}
is a compact operator, one gets

\|\hat{\phi}(\widehat{x_{2}^{*}u_{n}sx_{1}})\|_{2}\to_{n\to\infty}0.

Finally,

\sum_{s\in S}|\langle\pi_{\phi}(u_{n}s)x_{1}\xi_{\phi}y_{1}|x_{2}\xi_{\phi}y_{2}\rangle|\leq\sum_{s\in S}\|\hat{\phi}(\widehat{x_{2}^{*}u_{n}sx_{1}})\|_{2}\|y_{2}\|\|y_{1}\|_{2}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
, which ends the proof of (
4
).

(3) Assume that
M
is a finite, atomic von Neumann algebra. As is well-known, it admits a direct sum decomposition

M=\bigoplus_{i\in I}N_{i}

where
N_{i}
is a matrix algebra
M_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{C})
with
n_{i}\geq 1
for every
i
. If
I
was uncountable, then
M
would admit no faithful, normal tracial state, thus
I
is at most countable. The faithful, normal tracial state
\tau
on
M
is of the form

\tau(\oplus_{i}x_{i})=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}\mathrm{Tr}_{n_{i}}(x_{i})\quad(\oplus_{i}x_{i}\in M)

where
\mu_{i}>0
for every
i\in I
and
\sum_{i}\mu_{i}n_{i}=1
. The topology on
U_{M}
is metrisable with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{2}
and
U_{M}=\prod_{i}U(n_{i})
is a compact group. This implies that no nonzero element
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
is weakly mixing, as all non trivial unitary representations of
U_{M}
are direct sums of finite-dimensional ones.

\square

First, the next lemma displays stability properties of suitably modified weakly mixing cp maps.

Lemma 3.4

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra.

(1)

\operatorname{WM}(M)
is a subcone of
\mathrm{CP}(M)
: for all
\lambda\geq 0,\phi,\psi\in\operatorname{WM}(M)
, one has
\lambda\phi+\psi\in\operatorname{WM}(M)
.

(2)

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{WM}(M)
and
a\in(M)_{1}
. Define
\psi,\rho:M\rightarrow M
by
\psi(x)=a^{*}\phi(x)a
and
\rho(x)=\phi(a^{*}xa)
for every
x\in M
. Then
\psi
and
\rho
belong to
\operatorname{WM}(M)
and more precisely, one has the following equalities for all
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in M
:

(x_{1}\xi_{\psi}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\psi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\psi}y_{2}})=(x_{1}\xi_{\phi}ay_{1})*_{\pi_{\phi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\phi}ay_{2}})

(5)

and

(x_{1}\xi_{\rho}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\rho}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\rho}y_{2}})=(x_{1}a\xi_{\phi}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\phi}}(\overline{x_{2}a\xi_{\phi}y_{2}}).

(6)

Moreover, if
\phi\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)
then
\psi,\rho\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)
.

(3)

Let
z\in Z_{M}
be a nonzero projection and let
\theta\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau_{z}}(zM)
, where
\tau_{z}
is as in Proposition
3.3
. Set
\phi(x)=\theta(zx)=z\theta(zx)
for
x\in M
. Then
\phi\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)
, and we have for all
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in M
and
u\in U_{M}
:

(x_{1}\xi_{\phi}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\phi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\phi}y_{2}})(u)=\tau(z)(zx_{1}\xi_{\theta}zy_{1})*_{\pi_{\theta}}(\overline{zx_{2}\xi_{\theta}zy_{2}})(zu).

(7)

Proof.
(1) It is obvious that
\lambda\phi\in\operatorname{WM}(M)
if
\phi\in\operatorname{WM}(M)
. Next, it is easily seen that, for all
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in M
one has

(x_{1}\xi_{\phi+\psi}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\phi+\psi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\phi+\psi}y_{2}})=(x_{1}\xi_{\phi}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\phi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\phi}y_{2}})+(x_{1}\xi_{\psi}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\psi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\phi}y_{2}}),

which implies that

\mathfrak{m}(|(x_{1}\xi_{\phi+\psi}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\phi+\psi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\phi+\psi}y_{2}})|)\\
\leq\mathfrak{m}(|(x_{1}\xi_{\phi}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\phi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\phi}y_{2}})|)+\mathfrak{m}(|(x_{1}\xi_{\psi}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\psi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\phi}y_{2}})|)=0.

(2) The maps
\psi
and
\rho
are obviously cp.
Let us establish equality (
5
), which will prove that
\psi\in\operatorname{WM}(M)
: one has for every
u\in U_{M}
,

\displaystyle(x_{1}\xi_{\psi}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\psi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\psi}y_{2}})(u)

\displaystyle=\langle ux_{1}\xi_{\psi}y_{1}u^{*}|x_{2}\xi_{\psi}y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau(\psi(x_{2}^{*}ux_{1})y_{1}u^{*}y_{2}^{*})=\tau(\phi(x_{2}^{*}ux_{1})ay_{1}u^{*}(ay_{2})^{*})

\displaystyle=\langle ux_{1}\xi_{\phi}ay_{1}u^{*}|x_{2}\xi_{\phi}ay_{2}\rangle=(x_{1}\xi_{\phi}ay_{1})*_{\pi_{\phi}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\phi}ay_{2}})(u).

Equality (
6
) is verified similarly.

If moreover
\phi\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)
then one has for every

\tau(\psi(x^{*}x))=\tau(a^{*}\phi(x^{*}x)a)=\tau(\phi(x^{*}x)^{1/2}aa^{*}\phi(x^{*}x)^{1/2})\leq\tau(\phi(x^{*}x)\leq\tau(x^{*}x),

which proves that
\psi\in\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(M)
, and similarly for
\rho
.

(3) The verification of the fact that

and equality (

\square

The following result is strongly influenced by
[
2
, Theorem 22]
. We present a detailed proof for the sake of completeness. We use Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4 of
[
2

, which are parts of
[

Lemma 3.5

(
[
2
, Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4]
)
Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumanna algebra.

(1)

Let
\varphi\in\operatorname{WM}(M)
. Set
a=1\vee\varphi(1)
, and define
\psi\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
by
\psi(x)=a^{-1/2}\varphi(x)a^{-1/2}
for every
x\in M
. Then
\psi\in\operatorname{WM}(M)
, it is subunital, and it has the following properties:
\tau\circ\psi\leq\tau\circ\varphi
and

\|\psi(x)-x\|_{2}\leq\|\varphi(x)-x\|_{2}+2\|\varphi(1)-1\|_{2}^{1/2}\quad(x\in(M)_{1}).

(2)

Let
\psi\in\operatorname{WM}(M)
be subunital, and set

b=1\vee\Big(\frac{d\tau\circ\psi}{d\tau}\Big),

and define
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
by
\phi(x)=\psi(b^{-1/2}xb^{-1/2})
for every
x\in M
. Then
\phi\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)
, and it satisfies the following inequalities:

\phi(1)\leq\psi(1)\leq 1
and

\|\phi(x)-x\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|\psi(x)-x\|_{2}+5\|\tau\circ\psi-\tau\|^{1/2}\quad(x\in(M)_{1}).

Proof.
(1) As
a\geq 1
, we have
0\leq a^{-1}\leq 1
, and, for every
x\in(M)_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle\|\psi(x)-x\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|a^{-1/2}(\varphi(x)-x)a^{-1/2}\|_{2}+\|a^{-1/2}x(a^{-1/2}-1)\|_{2}+\|(a^{-1/2}-1)x\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(x)-x\|_{2}+2\|a^{-1/2}-1\|_{2}\leq\|\varphi(x)-x\|_{2}+2\|a^{-1}-1\|_{1}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(x)-x\|_{2}+2\|a^{-1}\|\|1-a\|_{1}^{1/2}\leq\|\varphi(x)-x\|_{2}+2\|1-a\|_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(x)-x\|_{2}+2\|\varphi(1)-1\|_{2}^{1/2},

where we used Powers-Størmer inequality in the second line, and
\|1-a\|_{1}\leq\|1-a\|_{2}
by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

(2) We divide this part into two steps.

Step 1
The following inequalities hold true:

\tau(b)\leq 2,\quad\|y\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|y\|\|y\|_{1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|\phi(y)\|_{1}\leq\|y\|_{1}\quad(y\in M).

Indeed, one has
\tau(b)\leq\tau(1+\psi(1))\leq 2
since
\psi(1)\leq 1
, and
\|y\|_{2}^{2}=\tau(|y|^{2})\leq\|y\|\tau(|y|)=\|y\|\|y\|_{1}
. In order to prove the last inequality, recall that, as
\phi
is subtracial, there exists a unique
\phi^{*}\in\mathrm{CP}(M)
such that
\tau(x\phi(y))=\tau(\phi^{*}(x)y)
for all
x,y\in M
. It is straightforward to check that furthermore
\phi^{*}(1)\leq 1
. Then we have for every
y\in M
:

\|\phi(y)\|_{1}=\sup\{|\tau(\phi(y)z)|\colon z\in(M)_{1}\}\\
=\sup\{|\tau(y\phi^{*}(z))|\colon z\in(M)_{1}\}\leq\sup\{|\tau(yz)|\colon z\in(M)_{1}\}=\|y\|_{1}.

Step 2
Fix
x\in(M)_{1}
. Then, by Step 1

\displaystyle\|\phi(x)-x\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi(x)-x\|\|\phi(x)-x\|_{1}\leq 2\|\phi(x)-x\|_{1}\leq 2\|\phi(x)-\underbrace{\phi(b^{1/2}xb^{1/2})}_{=\psi(x)}\|_{1}

\displaystyle+2\underbrace{\|\psi(x)-x\|_{1}}_{\leq\|\psi(x)-x\|_{2}}\leq 2\|x-b^{1/2}xb^{1/2}\|_{1}+2\|\psi(x)-x\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(\|x(1-b^{1/2})\|_{1}+\|(1-b^{1/2})xb^{1/2}\|_{1}+\|\psi(x)-x\|_{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2(\|x\|_{2}\|1-b^{1/2}\|_{2}+\|1-b^{1/2}\|_{2}\underbrace{\|xb^{1/2}\|_{2}}_{\leq\tau(b)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{2}}+\|\psi(x)-x\|_{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\psi(x)-x\|_{2}+(2+2\sqrt{2})\|1-b^{1/2}\|_{2}\leq 2\|\psi(x)-x\|_{2}+5\|1-b\|_{1}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\psi(x)-x\|_{2}+5\|\tau\circ\psi-\tau\|^{1/2},

where we used Powers-Størmer inequality once again.

\square

Theorem 3.6

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

(M,\tau)
has the WMAP.

(2)

There exists a generalized sequence
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\subset\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)
such that

\phi_{j}(1)\leq 1
for every

and

\lim_{j}\|\phi_{j}(x)-x\|_{2}=0

.

(3)

There exists a generalized sequence of pointed

(a)

\displaystyle{\lim_{j}(\|\langle\cdot\ \xi_{j}|\xi_{j}\rangle-\tau\|+\|\langle\xi_{j}\cdot|\xi_{j}\rangle-\tau\|)=0}
;

(b)

\displaystyle{\lim_{j}\|x\xi_{j}-\xi_{j}x\|=0}
for every

;

the unitary representation

is weakly mixing for every

.

Proof.

(1)\ \Rightarrow\ (2)
.

(\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\operatorname{WM}(M)
be a generalized sequence as in part (2) of Definition

and

. Then, by part (1) of Lemma
3.5
, each
\psi_{i}
is subunital and we have

\lim_{i}\|\psi_{i}(x)-x\|_{2}=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{i}\tau\circ\psi_{i}(x)=\tau(x)\quad(x\in M).

(8)

Let
K
be the weak closure of the convex hull of
(\tau\circ\psi_{i})_{i\in I}
in
M_{*}
. As
K
is the norm closure of the above mentioned convex hull, there exists a net
(\psi^{\prime}_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
of convex combinations of the
\psi_{i}

\lim_{j}\|\tau\circ\psi^{\prime}_{j}-\tau\|=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{j}\|\psi^{\prime}_{j}(x)-x\|_{2}=0\quad(x\in M).

Moreover, the
\psi^{\prime}_{j}
’s are subunital and belong to
\operatorname{WM}(M)
by Lemma
3.4
(1).

For every
j\in\mathcal{J}
, set

b_{j}=1\vee\Big(\frac{d\tau\circ\psi^{\prime}_{j}}{d\tau}\Big)

and define

by
\phi_{j}(x)=\psi^{\prime}_{j}(b_{j}^{-1/2}xb_{j}^{-1/2})
for
x\in M
. Then, by part (2) of Lemma
3.5
, every
\phi_{j}
is subunital,
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\subset\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)
and
\|\phi_{j}(x)-x\|_{2}\to 0
for every
x\in M
.

(2)\ \Rightarrow\ (3)
. Let
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\subset WM_{\tau}(M)
be as in (2). Set
(\mathcal{H}_{j},\xi_{j})=(\mathcal{H}_{\phi_{j}},\xi_{\phi_{j}})
for every
j\in\mathcal{J}
.

Let
x\in(M)_{1}
. One has

|\langle x\xi_{j}|\xi_{j}\rangle-\tau(x)|=|\tau(\phi_{j}(x)-x)|=|\tau(x(\phi_{j}^{*}(1)-1))|\leq\|x\|_{2}\|\phi_{j}^{*}(1)-1\|_{2}\leq\|\phi_{j}^{*}(1)-1\|_{2}

and, as
\phi_{j}^{*}
is subunital,

\|\phi_{j}^{*}(1)-1\|_{2}^{2}=\tau(\phi_{j}^{*}(1)^{2}-2\phi_{j}^{*}(1)+1)\leq\tau(1-\phi_{j}^{*}(1))=\tau(1-\phi_{j}(1))\leq\|1-\phi_{j}(1)\|_{2}\to_{j}0.

Similarly,

|\langle\xi_{j}x|\xi_{j}\rangle-\tau(x)|=|\tau(\phi_{j}(1)x-x)|\leq\|x\|_{2}\|\phi_{j}(1)-1\|_{2}\leq\|\phi_{j}(1)-1\|_{2}\to_{j}0.

This proves (a), and (c) is obvious. In order to prove (b), we observe first that, for all
j\in\mathcal{J}
and
x\in M
,

\|\xi_{j}x\|_{2}^{2}=\langle\xi_{\phi_{i}}x|\xi_{\phi_{i}}x\rangle=\tau(\phi_{j}(1)xx^{*})\leq\|x\|_{2}^{2}

and similarly
\|x\xi_{j}\|^{2}=\tau(\phi_{j}(x^{*}x))\leq\|x\|_{2}^{2}
. Hence, one gets for every
x\in M

\|x\xi_{j}-\xi_{j}x\|^{2}=\|x\xi_{j}\|^{2}+\|\xi_{j}x\|^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}\langle x\xi_{j}|\xi_{j}x\rangle\\
\leq 2\|x\|_{2}^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}\tau(\phi_{j}(x)x^{*})=2\mathrm{Re}\tau((x-\phi_{j}(x)x^{*})\leq 2\|x-\phi_{j}(x)\|_{2}\|x\|_{2}\to_{j}0

which proves (b).

(3)\ \Rightarrow\ (1)
. By density of the set
\mathcal{H}_{j}^{0}
of left and right bounded vectors in
\mathcal{H}_{j}
, we assume that every element of the generalized sequence
(\xi_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
is left and right bounded, and we denote by
T_{j}:L^{2}M\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{j}
the operator
T_{j}\hat{y}=\xi_{j}y
for every
y\in M
. We set then
\phi_{j}(x)=T_{j}^{*}xT_{j}
for every
x\in M
, so that
\phi_{j}\in\operatorname{WM}(M)
. One has for every
x\in M
and every
j\in\mathcal{J}
:

\langle x\xi_{j}|\xi_{j}x\rangle=\langle x\xi_{j}|T_{j}\hat{x}\rangle=\langle\xi_{j}|x^{*}T_{j}\hat{x}\rangle=\langle\hat{1}|T_{j}^{*}x^{*}T_{j}\hat{x}\rangle=\tau(\phi_{j}(x)x^{*}).

Hence, for fixed
x\in(M)_{1}
,

\displaystyle\|\phi_{j}(x)-x\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\|x\|_{2}^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}\tau(\phi_{j}(x)x^{*})=2\|x\|_{2}^{2}-\mathrm{Re}\langle x\xi_{j}|\xi_{j}x\rangle

\displaystyle=(\tau(x^{*}x)-\langle x^{*}x\xi_{j}|\xi_{j}\rangle)+(\tau(xx^{*})-\langle\xi_{j}xx^{*}|\xi_{j}\rangle)

\displaystyle+\|x\xi_{j}\|^{2}+\|\xi_{j}x\|^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}\langle x\xi_{j}|\xi_{j}x\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\langle\cdot\ \xi_{j}|\xi_{j}\rangle-\tau\|+\|\langle\xi_{j}\cdot|\xi_{j}\rangle-\tau\|+\|x\xi_{j}-\xi_{j}x\|^{2}\to_{j}0

This shows that
(M,\tau)
has the WMAP.

\square

We prove now that the WMAP does not depend on the chosen trace.

Proposition 3.7

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra which has the WMAP, and let
\tau^{\prime}
be a normal, normalized, faithful trace on
M
. Then
(M,\tau^{\prime})
has the WMAP.

Proof.
Let us prove first the following statement:

Let
(z_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset Z_{M}
be an increasing sequence of nonzero projections which converges strongly to
1
. Let
\tau_{n}
be the normalized trace
z_{n}x\mapsto\tau(z_{n}x)/\tau(z_{n})
on
z_{n}M
. If each pair
(z_{n}M,\tau_{n})
has the WMAP, then so does
(M,\tau)
.

Indeed, let
F\subset M
be a finite set and let
\varepsilon>0
. Choose first
n
large enough so that
\|(z_{n}-1)x\|_{2,\tau}<\varepsilon/2
for every
x\in F
. Next, choose
\theta\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau_{n}}(z_{n}M)
so that

\max_{x\in F}\|\theta(z_{n}x)-z_{n}x\|_{2,\tau_{n}}<\varepsilon/2.

Define
\phi\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)
as in Lemma
3.4
(2), and observe that, by definition,
\phi(x)=\phi(z_{n}x)
for every
x
. We get for every
x\in F

\displaystyle\|\phi(x)-x\|_{2,\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi(z_{n}x)-z_{n}x\|_{2,\tau}+\|(z_{n}-1)x\|_{2,\tau}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tau(z_{n})}\|\theta(z_{n}x)-z_{n}x\|_{2,\tau_{n}}+\|(z_{n}-1)x\|_{2,\tau}<\varepsilon,

where we have used the equality
\|zy\|_{2,\tau}=\sqrt{\tau(z)}\|zy\|_{2,\tau_{z}}
which holds for any nonzero projection
z\in Z_{M}
and every
y\in M
.
This proves that
(M,\tau)
has the WMAP.

Let now
\tau^{\prime}
be another trace on
M
.
There exists a positive, invertible operator
h
affiliated to
Z_{M}
such that
\tau^{\prime}(x)=\tau(hx)
for every
x\in M
.

Let us assume first that
h
and
h^{-1}
are bounded, and let
(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(M)
be such that

\lim_{i}\|\phi_{i}(x)-x\|_{2,\tau}=0\quad(x\in M).

Set for all
i\in I
and
x\in M
:
\psi_{i}(x)\coloneqq h^{-1}\phi_{i}(hx)
. Then
\psi_{i}
is cp and, for every
x\in M
,

\tau^{\prime}\circ\psi_{i}(x^{*}x)=\tau(\phi_{i}(hx^{*}x))\leq\tau(hx^{*}x)=\tau^{\prime}(x^{*}x),

which shows that
\psi_{i}\in\mathrm{CP}_{\tau^{\prime}}(M)
as well. Let us prove next that
\psi_{i}\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau^{\prime}}(M)
; as in Lemma
3.4
, it is easy to check that one has for all
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in M
, where the scalar product on the left hand side is
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle_{\tau^{\prime}}
:

(x_{1}\xi_{\psi_{i}}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\psi_{i}}}(\overline{x_{2}\xi_{\psi_{i}}y_{2}})=(x_{1}\xi_{\phi_{i}}y_{1})*_{\pi_{\phi_{i}}}(\overline{hx_{2}\xi_{\phi_{i}}y_{2}}),

which implies that
\psi_{i}\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau^{\prime}}(M)
.

Next, for
x\in M
,

\displaystyle\|\psi_{i}(x)-x\|^{2}_{2,\tau^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{\prime}((\psi_{i}(x^{*})-x^{*})(\psi_{i}(x)-x))

\displaystyle=\tau(h(h^{-1}(\phi_{i}(hx^{*})-x^{*})(h^{-1}\phi_{i}(hx)-x))

\displaystyle=\tau((\phi_{i}(hx^{*})-hx^{*})h^{-1}(\phi_{i}(hx)-hx))=\tau(h^{-1}|\phi_{i}(hx)-hx|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{-1}\|\|\phi_{i}(hx)-hx\|^{2}_{2,\tau}\to 0

as
i\to\infty
.

If
h
or
h^{-1}
is not bounded, let
z_{n}
be the spectral projection of
h
corresponding to the interval
[1/n,n]
. Then
h_{n}=z_{n}h
and its inverse are bounded operators of the center of
z_{n}M
, and the pair
(z_{n}M,\tau^{\prime}_{n})
has the WMAP for every
n
. By the first part of the proof,
(M,\tau^{\prime})
has the WMAP.

\square

As for the Haagerup property for discrete groups, one has:

Theorem 3.8

Let
G
be a discrete group. Then
G
does not have Kazhdan’s property (T) if and only if its von Neumann algebra
L(G)
has the WMAP.

Proof.
Assume that
G
does not have Kazhdan’s property (T), and let
(\varphi_{i})
be a generalized sequence of normalized functions of positive type as in Proposition
2.6
. For each
i
, let
m_{\varphi_{i}}
be the multiplier on
L(G)
characterized by

m_{\varphi_{i}}(\lambda(g))=\varphi_{i}(g)\lambda(g)\quad(g\in G),

where
\lambda
denotes the left regular representation whose range generates
L(G)
(see for instance
[
10
, Lemma 2]
). Then
m_{\varphi_{i}}\in\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(L(G))
for every
i
, and even
\tau\circ m_{\varphi_{i}}=\tau
.
By standard approximation, one has
\|m_{\varphi_{i}}(x)-x\|_{2}\to 0
for every
x\in L(G)
; see
[
10
, Theorem 3]
.

Thus, we just need to prove that
m_{\varphi_{i}}\in\operatorname{WM}_{\tau}(L(G))
for every fixed
i
. Set
\phi=m_{\varphi_{i}}
,
\xi=\xi_{\phi}
(which is a unit vector) and
\pi=\pi_{\phi}
for short.
By Proposition
2.5
(b’), all we need to prove is that, for all
s,t,r,w\in G
, for every finite set
F\subset U_{L(G)}
and for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
g\in G
such that

\max_{u\in F}|\langle\pi(\lambda(g)u)\lambda(s)\xi\lambda(t)|\lambda(r)\xi\lambda(w)\rangle|<\varepsilon.

For every
u\in F
, let us choose
v_{u}\in L(G)
which has finite support and such that
\|v_{u}\|_{2}\leq 1
and
\|u-v_{u}\|_{2}<\varepsilon/4
; let then
K=K^{-1}\subset G
be a finite, symmetric subset such that
v_{u}(h)=0
for all
h\notin K
and
u\in F
.

For
g\in G
and
u\in F
we have

\displaystyle|\langle\pi(\lambda(g)u)\lambda(s)\xi\lambda(t)|\lambda(r)\xi\lambda(w)\rangle|\leq{}

\displaystyle|\langle\lambda(g)(u-v_{u})\lambda(s)\xi\lambda(t)|\lambda(r)\xi\lambda(wg)u\rangle|

\displaystyle+|\langle\lambda(g)v_{u}\lambda(s)\xi\lambda(t)|\lambda(r)\xi\lambda(wg)v_{u}\rangle|

\displaystyle+|\langle\lambda(g)v_{u}\lambda(s)\xi\lambda(t)|\lambda(r)\xi\lambda(wg)(u-v_{u})\rangle|

\displaystyle<{}

\displaystyle\varepsilon/2+|\langle\lambda(g)v_{u}\lambda(s)\xi\lambda(t)|\lambda(r)\xi\lambda(wg)v_{u}\rangle|.

But

\displaystyle\langle\lambda(g)v_{u}\lambda(s)\xi\lambda(t)|\lambda(r)\xi\lambda(wg)v_{u}\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau(\phi[\lambda(r^{-1}g)v_{u}\lambda(s)]\lambda(tg^{-1}w^{-1})v_{u}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{h,k\in K}v_{u}(h)\overline{v_{u}(k)}\tau(\phi[\lambda(r^{-1}ghs)]\lambda(tg^{-1}w^{-1}k^{-1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{h,k\in K}v_{u}(h)\overline{v_{u}(k)}\varphi_{i}(r^{-1}ghs)\tau(\lambda(r^{-1}ghstg^{-1}w^{-1}k^{-1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in K}v_{u}(h)\overline{v_{u}(r^{-1}ghstg^{-1}w^{-1})}\varphi_{i}(r^{-1}ghs)

for every
g\in G
. As
\mathfrak{m}(|\varphi_{i}|)=0
, by Proposition
2.5
, there exists
g\in G
such that

\max_{h\in K}|\varphi_{i}(r^{-1}ghs)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2|K|},

which shows that

\max_{u\in F}|\langle\pi(\lambda(g)u)\lambda(s)\xi\lambda(t)|\lambda(r)\xi\lambda(w)|<\varepsilon.

Conversely, suppose that
M\coloneqq L(G)
has the WMAP and let
(\phi_{i})\subset\operatorname{WM}(M)
be as in Definition
3.1
. For every
i
, define
\varphi_{i}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\varphi_{i}(g)=\tau(\phi_{i}(\lambda(g))\lambda(g^{-1}))\quad(g\in G).

Then, by
[
10
, Lemma 1]
,
\varphi_{i}
is a function of positive type on
G
, and it is straightforward to check that
\varphi_{i}(g)=\langle\pi_{\phi_{i}}(\lambda(g))\xi_{\phi_{i}}|\xi_{\phi_{i}}\rangle
for every
g\in G
, which proves that
\mathfrak{m}(|\varphi_{i}|)=0
for every
i
. Finally, one obviously has
\lim_{i}\varphi_{i}(g)=1
for every
g\in G
, which proves that
G
does not have property (T).

\square

Let us recall property (T) for tracial von Neumann algebras from
[
21
]
and
[
19
]
.

Definition 3.9

A tracial von Neumann algebra
(M,\tau)
has
property (T)
if it satisfies one (hence both) of the following equivalent conditions:

(1)

for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a finite set
F=F(\varepsilon)\subset M
and
0<\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon
such that, if
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(M)
is subunital and if

\max_{x\in F}\|\phi(x)-x\|_{2}\leq\delta,

then

\sup_{x\in(M)_{1}}\|\phi(x)-x\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon.

(2)

there exists
F_{0}\subset M
finite and
\delta_{0}>0
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
0<\delta(\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon
so that if
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert
M
-bimodule with a vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\|y\xi-\xi y\|\leq\delta_{0}
for every
y\in F_{0}
,
\|\langle\cdot\ \xi|\xi\rangle-\tau\|<\delta(\varepsilon)
and
\|\langle\xi\cdot|\xi\rangle-\tau\|<\delta(\varepsilon)
, then there exists
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
x\xi_{0}=\xi_{0}x
for every
x\in\mathcal{H}
and
\|\langle\cdot\ \xi_{0}|\xi_{0}\rangle-\tau\|<\varepsilon
and
\|\langle\xi_{0}\cdot|\xi_{0}\rangle-\tau\|<\varepsilon
. (Such a vector
\xi_{0}
is called a
central vector
).

It turns out that property (T) for
(M,\tau)
does not depend on the trace
\tau
: see for instance
[
19
, Theorem 1]
. Moreover, if
M
is a II
1
factor, the control on the nonzero central vector
\xi_{0}
is unnecessary. Indeed, due to
[
11
, Proposition 1]
which proves that condition (wT) below implies (sT), the following three conditions on the II
1
factor
M
are equivalent:

(wT)

M
has property (T): there exists
F\subset M
finite and
\varepsilon>0
such that, for every Hilbert
M
-bimodule
\mathcal{H}
and unit vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
with

\max_{x\in F}\|x\xi-\xi x\|<\varepsilon

there is a unit vector
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
which is central.

(mT)

There exists
F_{0}\subset M
finite such that, for every
0<\varepsilon<1
, there exists
0<\delta(\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon
such that, if
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert
M
-bimodule with a unit vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying

\max_{y\in F_{0}}\|y\xi-\xi y\|<\delta(\varepsilon)

and

\|\langle\cdot\ \xi|\xi\rangle-\tau\|+\|\langle\xi\cdot|\xi\rangle-\tau\|<\varepsilon

then there exists a unit vector
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
which is central.

(sT)

There exists
F_{1}\subset M
finite,
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
K>0
such that, for every
0<\delta\leq\varepsilon_{1}
, for every pointed
M
-bimodule
(\mathcal{H},\xi)
where
\xi
is a unit vector such that

\max_{x\in F_{1}}\|x\xi-\xi x\|<\delta

then there is a unit vector
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, which is central and such that

\|\eta-\xi\|<K\delta
.

Here is our last main result.

Theorem 3.10

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra.

(1)

If
M
has the WMAP, then it does not have property (T).

(2)

Assume that
M
is a
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor. If it does not have property (T) then it has the WMAP.

Proof.
(1) Assume that
M
has property (T) and take
\varepsilon=1/2
in Definition
3.9
(1); let
F\subset M
and
\delta>0
be the associated data. If
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(M)
is subunital and such that

\max_{x\in F}\|\phi(x)-x\|_{2}\leq\delta,

then we have in particular

\sup_{u\in U_{M}}\|\phi(u)-u\|_{2}\leq 1/2.

We get for every
u\in U_{M}

|\xi_{\phi}*_{\pi_{\phi}}\overline{\xi_{\phi}}(u)-1|=|\tau(\phi(u)u^{*})-1)|=|\tau((\phi(u)-u)u^{*})|\leq\|\phi(u)-u\|_{2}\leq 1/2.

This shows that
\|\xi_{\phi}*_{\pi_{\phi}}\overline{\xi_{\phi}}-1\|_{\infty}\leq 1/2
. Hence,

\||\xi_{\phi}*_{\pi_{\phi}}\overline{\xi_{\phi}}|-1\|_{\infty}\leq\|\xi_{\phi}*_{\pi_{\phi}}\overline{\xi_{\phi}}-1\|_{\infty}\leq 1/2

and this implies that
|\mathfrak{m}(|\xi_{\phi}*_{\pi_{\phi}}\overline{\xi_{\phi}}|-1|\leq\|\xi_{\phi}*_{\pi_{\phi}}\overline{\xi_{\phi}}-1\|_{\infty}\leq 1/2
and thus
\phi
cannot belong to
WM_{\tau}(M)
.

(2) Let
M
be a II
1
factor that does not have property (T), hence
M
satisfies the negation of condition (mT) above. Consider then the set
\mathcal{J}
of pairs
(F,n)
where
1\in F\subset M
is finite and
n\geq 1
is an integer. We define the following partial order on
\mathcal{J}
:
(F,n)\leq(F^{\prime},n^{\prime})
if and only if
F\subset F^{\prime}
and
n\leq n^{\prime}
. Thus
\mathcal{J}
is a directed set. As
M
does not have property (T), for every
j=(F,n)\in\mathcal{J}
, there exists a pointed Hilbert
M
-bimodule
(\mathcal{H}_{j},\xi_{j})
such that

\max_{x\in F}\|x\xi_{j}-\xi_{j}x\|\leq 1/n

and

\|\langle\cdot\ \xi_{j}|\xi_{j}\rangle-\tau\|+\|\langle\xi_{j}\cdot|\xi_{j}\rangle-\tau\|\leq 1/n

but such that
\mathcal{H}_{j}
has no nonzero central vector.
As
M
is diffuse, it follows from
[
17
, Theorem 4.4]
that its unitary group is minimally almost periodic, which means by Proposition 2.7 that
\pi_{\mathcal{H}_{j}}
is weakly mixing. By Theorem
3.6
,
M
has the WMAP.

\square

Remark 3.11

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial, atomic von Neumann algebra. It follows from Proposition
3.3
that
M
does not have the WMAP. We claim that it has property (T).
Indeed, recall from the proof of the above mentioned proposition that
U_{M}
is a metrizable, compact group with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{2}
.
Hence, if
\varepsilon>0
is fixed, there exists a finite set
1\in F\subset U_{M}
such that

\inf\{\|u-v\|_{2}\colon u\in U_{M},v\in F\}<\delta\coloneqq\frac{\varepsilon}{6}.

Thus, if
\phi\in\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(M)
is subunital and such that

\max_{v\in F}\|\phi(v)-v\|_{2}<\delta,

then
\|\phi(u)-u\|_{2}\leq 3\delta
for every
u\in U_{M}
, and as every
x\in(M)_{1}
is of the form
x=\frac{1}{2}(v+v^{*})+\frac{i}{2}(w+w^{*})
for suitable

, we get that

\sup_{x\in(M)_{1}}\|\phi(x)-x\|_{2}<\varepsilon.

This proves that
(M,\tau)
satisfies condition (1) of Definition
3.9
.

\square

Finally, we give a characterization of II
1
factors with separable predual which do not have property (T) in terms of (relative) property gamma; it extends the case of group algebras as is presented in
[
16
, Theorem 4.6]
.

More precisely, let
N\subset M
be a pair of II
1
factors such that
N^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}
, namely
N
is
irreducible
in
M
; recall from
[
16
]
that
N
has
property gamma relative to

M
if, for any free ultrafilter
\omega
on
\mathbb{N}
, one has
N^{\prime}\cap N^{\omega}\subsetneq N^{\prime}\cap M^{\omega}
. Then we proved in
[
16
, Theorem 4.6]
that if
G
is an icc, non Kazhdan’s group, there is an action of
G
on the hyperfinite II
1
factor
R
so that
L(G)
is irreducible in the crossed product
R\rtimes G
and
L(G)
has property gamma relative to
R\rtimes G
.

Proposition 3.12

Let
N
be a
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor with separable predual which does not have property (T). Then either
N
has Murray and von Neumann property gamma or
N
is full and there exists a
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor
M\supset N
such that
N
is irreducible in
M
and
N^{\prime}\cap M^{\omega}
is diffuse. In particular,
N
has property gamma relative to
M
.

Proof.
If
N
does not have property gamma, it is full, hence
N^{\prime}\cap N^{\omega}=\mathbb{C}
. By
[
22

, there is a tracial von Neumann algebra

, and such that

. It follows that
M
is a II

\square

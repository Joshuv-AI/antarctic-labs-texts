Adaptive Accelerated Gradient Method for Smooth Convex Optimization Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Zepeng Wang, Juan Peypouquet

Abstract
We propose an adaptive accelerated gradient method for solving smooth convex optimization problems. The method incorporates a scheme to determine the step size adaptively, by means of a local estimation of the smoothness constant, which is assumed unknown, without resorting to line search procedures. The sequence generated by this method converges weakly to a minimizer of the objective function, and the function values converge at a fast rate of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 k 2 ) \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right) . Moreover, if the objective function is strongly convex, the function values converge at a linear rate.



keywords

adaptive step size, accelerated gradient descent, convex optimization

1
Introduction

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space and
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be convex and
L
-smooth, for some
L>0
. In this paper, we are interested in the following minimization problem:

\min_{x\in H}f(x),

(1)

and write
x^{*}=\argmin(f)
,
f^{*}=f(x^{*})
hereafter for simplicity. A basic method to solve this problem is gradient descent:

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-s\nabla f(x_{k}),

(2)

where
s>0
is the step size. If the global smoothness parameter
L
is known, one can set
s\in\left(0,\frac{2}{L}\right)
and obtain
f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)

[
16
]
. But in practice,
L
is usually unknown and requires some estimation procedures, such as line search
[
1
]
, which can be computationally expensive for large scale problems.

As an alternative, the authors in
[
13
]
leveraged a local estimate of
L
, namely

L_{k}=\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k-1})\|}{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|},

and computed the current step size in relation to this estimate, by

s_{k}=\min\left\{\sqrt{1+\frac{s_{k-1}}{s_{k-2}}}s_{k-1},\frac{1}{2L_{k}}\right\}.

The result is an
adaptive
variant of (
2
) that can guarantee a convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)
for the function values. Since the smoothness estimate satisfies
L_{k}\leq L
, a larger step size than
\frac{1}{L}
is allowed in principle and can speed up the convergence rate. The interested reader is referred to
[
19
,
8
]
for further progress (including the convergence of the iterates to an optimal solution) on the adaptive gradient method for smooth functions, and
[
14
,
10
,
19
]
for the composite case. If the objective function is smooth and
strongly convex
, a linear convergence rate was established in
[
19
,
8
]
.

An inertial variant of (
2
) is Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method
[
15
]
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-s\nabla f(x_{k}),\\[3.0pt]
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{\theta_{k}-1}{\theta_{k+1}}(y_{k+1}-y_{k}),\end{array}\right.

(3)

where
s>0
and
(\theta_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is given by

\theta_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}1,&\quad\hbox{if}&k=0,\\[3.0pt]
\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4\theta_{k-1}}}{2},&\quad\hbox{if}&k\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

(4)

If the global smoothness parameter
L
is known, one can set
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right]
and obtain
f(y_{k+1})-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)

[
15
]
, which improves upon the rate of gradient descent (
2
). A linear convergence rate was obtained for strongly convex functions
[
11
,
3
,
18
]
. Convergence of the iterates was proved in
[
7
,
2
,
9
,
6
]
.

Inspired by
[
13
]
, there has been a growing interest to develop an adaptive variant of (
3
) which is line-search-free but still preserves a fast convergence rate
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
. The first adaptive accelerated gradient algorithm to achieve this goal was developed by
[
12
]
, followed by
[
17
]
and
[
4
]
. The method in
[
17
]
, upon which this work is based, can be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-s_{k}\nabla f(x_{k}),\\[3.0pt]
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{\theta_{k+2}-1}{\theta_{k+3}}(y_{k+1}-y_{k})+\frac{\theta_{k+2}}{\theta_{k+3}}(\alpha_{k}-1)(y_{k+1}-x_{k}),\end{array}\right.

(5)

where
(\theta_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is given by (
4
),
(s_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is the (adaptive) step size and
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a suitable positive sequence. If
\alpha_{k}\equiv 1
, (
5
) reduces to (
3
). Under these assumptions, one has
f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
and
\min_{i\in\{0,\cdots,k\}}\|\nabla f(x_{i})\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{3}}\right)

[
17
]
. In this paper, we revisit the behavior of the iterates generated by (
5
), with different parameter choices. On the one hand, we show the previously unknown weak convergence of the iterates to a solution of (
1
). On the other (and more importantly!), we prove that linear convergence holds when the algorithm is applied to strongly convex functions.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present an overview of the adaptive accelerated gradient method. In Section
3
, we introduce an energy sequence, and derive some preliminary estimations. In Section
4
, we provide an alternative proof of the
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
convergence rate, and use the arguments to prove the convergence of the iterates. In Section
5
, we derive a linear convergence rate for the function values under strong convexity. Some conclusions are given in Section
6
.

2
Adaptive accelerated gradient method

In this section, we present an overview of the method and comment on the choices of the parameter sequences.

Consider the Adaptive Accelerated Gradient Method:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k}-s_{k}\nabla f(x_{k}),\\[4.0pt]
x_{k+1}&=&y_{k+1}+\frac{t_{k}-1}{t_{k+1}}(y_{k+1}-y_{k})+\frac{(\gamma-1)t_{k}}{t_{k+1}}(y_{k+1}-x_{k}),\\[4.0pt]
L_{k+1}&=&\frac{\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}}{\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle-\left(f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})\right)},\\[4.0pt]
s_{k+1}&=&T(s_{k},L_{k+1}),\end{array}\right.

(AdaAGM)

where
\gamma>0
,
(s_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is the step size, and
T(s_{k},L_{k+1})
is an adaptive scheme to determine
s_{k+1}
based on the previous step size
s_{k}
and local smoothness parameter
L_{k+1}
. From (
AdaAGM
), we have

\displaystyle t_{k+1}(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})

\displaystyle=(t_{k}-1)(y_{k+1}-y_{k})+(\gamma-1)t_{k}(y_{k+1}-x_{k})

(6)

\displaystyle=t_{k}(x_{k}-y_{k})+\gamma t_{k}(y_{k+1}-x_{k})-(y_{k+1}-y_{k}).

2.1
The sequence
(t_{k})
of inertial parameters

The sequence
(t_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is defined by

t_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}t_{0},&\quad\hbox{if}&k=0,\\[3.0pt]
\frac{m+\sqrt{m^{2}+4t_{k-1}^{2}}}{2},&\quad\hbox{if}&k\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

(7)

where
m\in(0,1]
and
t_{0}\geq 1
. It follows that
t_{k+1}^{2}=t_{k}^{2}+mt_{k+1}
and

\frac{mk}{2}+t_{0}\leq t_{k}\leq mk+t_{0},\quad\forall k\geq 0.

Remark 1
.

The sequence
(t_{k})_{k\geq 0}
reduces to
(\theta_{k})_{k\geq 0}
in (
4
) if
t_{0}=m=1
. As will be shown in Subsection
2.3
, letting
t_{0}\geq 1
free, and
m\in(0,1)
are two enabling components for an increasing step size. Also, with
m\in(0,1)
, we still have
t_{k}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(k^{2})
.

2.2
The sequence
(L_{k})
of local smoothness estimates

We define

L_{k+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}0,&\text{if}&\nabla f(x_{k+1})=\nabla f(x_{k}),\\[4.0pt]
\frac{\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}}{\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle-\left(f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})\right)},&\text{if}&\nabla f(x_{k+1})\neq\nabla f(x_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(8)

Observe that
L_{k+1}=0
occurs only when
\nabla f(x_{k+1})=\nabla f(x_{k})
. Moreover, since
f
is convex and
L
-smooth, we have

\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle-\left(f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})\right)\geq\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

This means, on the one hand, that
L_{k+1}\leq L
, and, on the other, that
\nabla f(x_{k+1})=\nabla f(x_{k})
whenever
\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle-\left(f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})\right)=0
. Since no confusion should arise, we follow the convention
\frac{0}{0}=0
and write (
8
) in a compact form as

L_{k+1}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}}{\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle-\left(f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})\right)},

(9)

for convenience. In a similar fashion, we set

\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}}{L_{k+1}}=0,

whenever
\nabla f(x_{k+1})=\nabla f(x_{k})
.

2.3
The sequence
(s_{k})
of step sizes

The step size
s_{k+1}
is determined inductively as follows: pick
s_{0}>0
,
\omega\in[0,1)
,
\delta\in[0,1)
and
\beta>0
. Given
s_{k}
, compute
s_{k+1}
by:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \qquad s_{k+1}&=\min\left\{A_{k}s_{k},B_{k}s_{k},\frac{C_{k}}{L_{k+1}}\right\},\\
\text{where}&\\
A_{k}&=\frac{t_{k}^{2}}{t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)}=\frac{t_{k+1}-m}{t_{k+1}-1},\\[3.0pt]
B_{k}&=\frac{2}{(1+\beta)\gamma}\left(1-\frac{1}{t_{k+1}}\right),\\[3.0pt]
C_{k}&=\frac{1-\omega}{\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{k+1}}{t_{k+1}-1}+\frac{t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)}{\beta(1-\delta)\gamma t_{k}^{2}}}=\frac{1-\omega}{\frac{2}{B_{k}}+\frac{1}{\beta(1-\delta)\gamma A_{k}}}.\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

Some comments are in order:

First, since
m\in(0,1]
, we have

A_{k}=\frac{t_{k}^{2}}{t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)}=\frac{t_{k+1}-m}{t_{k+1}-1}\geq 1,

with strict inequality if
m\in(0,1)
. On the other hand,

B_{k}=\frac{2}{(1+\beta)\gamma}\left(1-\frac{1}{t_{k+1}}\right)>\frac{2}{(1+\beta)\gamma}\left(1-\frac{1}{t_{0}}\right).

As a consequence, if the parameters
\beta,\gamma,t_{0}
satisfy

\frac{2}{(1+\beta)\gamma}\left(1-\frac{1}{t_{0}}\right)\geq 1,

(11)

then
B_{k}>1
. This means that an increasing step size
s_{k+1}>s_{k}
is possible if
m\in(0,1)
and (
11
) holds. Note that, for (
11
) to hold, it is necessary that
\gamma\in(0,2)
.

Finally, since
A_{k}\geq 1
and
B_{k}>\frac{2}{(1+\beta)\gamma}\left(1-\frac{1}{t_{0}}\right)
, we have

\frac{1-\omega}{C_{k}}=\frac{2}{B_{k}}+\frac{1}{\beta\gamma(1-\delta)A_{k}}\leq\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{0}}{t_{0}-1}+\frac{1}{\beta\gamma(1-\delta)},

so that

\frac{C_{k}}{L_{k+1}}\geq\frac{1-\omega}{L_{k+1}\left(\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{0}}{t_{0}-1}+\frac{1}{\beta\gamma(1-\delta)}\right)}\geq\frac{1-\omega}{L\left(\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{0}}{t_{0}-1}+\frac{1}{\beta\gamma(1-\delta)}\right)}.

This implies that
(s_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is bounded from below by
\frac{q}{L}
, where

q:=\frac{1-\omega}{\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{0}}{t_{0}-1}+\frac{1}{\beta\gamma(1-\delta)}},

(12)

as shown in the following:

Proposition 2
.

Let
m\in(0,1]
, let (
11
) hold, and let the sequence
(s_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be defined by (
10
). If

s_{0}\geq\frac{q}{L}
, then
s_{k}\geq\frac{q}{L}
for every
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

We prove the argument by contradiction. Let
s_{K+1}
be the first term in the sequence
(s_{k})_{k\geq 0}
to satisfy
s_{k}<\frac{q}{L}
. This means that

s_{K+1}<\frac{q}{L}\leq s_{K}
. Since
A_{K},B_{K}\geq 1
, we have

s_{K+1}=\min\left\{A_{K}s_{K},B_{K}s_{K},\frac{C_{K}}{L_{K+1}}\right\}\geq\min\left\{s_{K},\frac{C_{K}}{L_{K+1}}\right\}\geq\frac{q}{L},

which is impossible.

On the other hand, the sequence
(s_{k})_{k\geq 0}
can grow at most as a power of
k
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
m\in(0,1]
. Let
(s_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be defined by (
10
). For every
k\geq 0
, we have

s_{k}\leq\left[s_{0}e^{\frac{2(1-m)}{m}}\right]k^{\frac{2(1-m)}{m}}.

Proof.

By definition,

s_{k+1}\leq A_{k}s_{k}=\left(\frac{t_{k+1}-m}{t_{k+1}-1}\right)s_{k}=\left(1+\frac{1-m}{t_{k+1}-1}\right)s_{k}

for every
k\geq 0
. If
m=1
, the sequence is bounded from above. Otherwise, we write

\ln(s_{k+1})-\ln(s_{k})\leq\ln\left(1+\frac{1-m}{t_{k+1}-1}\right)\leq\frac{1-m}{t_{k+1}-1}\leq\frac{2(1-m)}{m(k+1)}.

Summing for
k=0,\dots,K-1
, we obtain

\ln(s_{K})-\ln(s_{0})\leq\frac{2(1-m)}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{k}\leq\frac{2(1-m)}{m}\left(1+\int_{1}^{K}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}\right)=\frac{2(1-m)}{m}\big(1+\ln(K)\big),

which proves the result.

Remark 4
.

A different bound on the growth of
s_{k}
can be obtained using the inequality
s_{k+1}\leq B_{k}s_{k}
.

,

,

and

. Set
t_{0}\geq 1
and

, where

). Suppose that (

) holds. Let the sequences
(t_{k})_{k\geq 0}
,
(L_{k})_{k\geq 0}

be given by (

) and (

3
Energy estimations

Our convergence analysis centers around the energy sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
, given by

E_{k}=E_{k}(x^{*}):=\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+\gamma t_{k}^{2}s_{k}\left(f(x_{k})-f^{*}\right),

(13)

where
\beta>0
,
x^{*}\in\argmin(f)
(arbitrary, but fixed) and

\displaystyle\phi_{k}:

\displaystyle=t_{k+1}(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})+(y_{k+1}-x^{*})

(14)

\displaystyle=t_{k}(x_{k}-y_{k})+\gamma t_{k}(y_{k+1}-x_{k})+(y_{k}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=(t_{k}-1)(x_{k}-y_{k})+\gamma t_{k}(y_{k+1}-x_{k})+(x_{k}-x^{*}),

in view of (
6
).

We have the following:

Lemma 7
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be generated by (
AdaAGM
), and consider the sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
defined by (
13
). Then, for every
\omega\in[0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\left[t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}-t_{k}^{2}s_{k}\right](f(x_{k})-f^{*})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1+\beta}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}s_{k}\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),\nabla f(x_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(1-\omega)\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{2L_{k+1}}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\omega\mu\gamma}{2}t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}t_{k+1}s_{k+1}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Proof.

By (
13
), we have

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}\right)

(15)

\displaystyle+\gamma\left[t_{k+1}^{2}s_{k+1}\left(f(x_{k+1})-f^{*}\right)-t_{k}^{2}s_{k}\left(f(x_{k})-f^{*}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\beta\gamma^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}t_{k+1}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\right).

Using (
14
) and (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}

\displaystyle=t_{k+2}(x_{k+2}-y_{k+2})-t_{k+1}(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})+(y_{k+2}-y_{k+1})

(16)

\displaystyle=\gamma t_{k+1}(y_{k+2}-x_{k+1}),

which gives

\|\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\|^{2}=\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}\|y_{k+2}-x_{k+1}\|^{2},

and

\displaystyle\quad\langle\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k},\phi_{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\gamma t_{k+1}\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},(t_{k+1}-1)(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})+\gamma t_{k+1}(y_{k+2}-x_{k+1})+(x_{k+1}-x^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle=\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\rangle+\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}\|y_{k+2}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma t_{k+1}\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle.

Notice that in the first term,

\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\rangle=\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle+\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},x_{k}-y_{k+1}\rangle,

so that

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k},\phi_{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},y_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}\|y_{k+2}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}+\gamma t_{k+1}\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle.

Since

\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}=\langle\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k},\phi_{k+1}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}-\phi_{k}\|^{2},

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

(17)

\displaystyle-\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},y_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}\|y_{k+2}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\gamma t_{k+1}\langle y_{k+2}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle-\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}s_{k}\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),\nabla f(x_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle,

by using
y_{k+2}=x_{k+1}-s_{k+1}\nabla f(x_{k+1})
and
y_{k+1}=x_{k}-s_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})
. The fact that
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex gives

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq f(x_{k+1})-f^{*}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2},

(18)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}.

(19)

Keeping in mind that
\omega\in[0,1)
and

\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle=f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})+\frac{1}{2L_{k+1}}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2},

(20)

we combine (
19
) and (
20
) to obtain

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})+\frac{1-\omega}{2L_{k+1}}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\omega\mu}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}.

Using this inequality, together with (
18
), in (
17
) gives

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}(f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k}))-\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}(f(x_{k+1})-f^{*})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}-\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}s_{k}\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),\nabla f(x_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(1-\omega)\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{2L_{k+1}}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\omega\mu\gamma}{2}t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}t_{k+1}s_{k+1}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Using this inequality in (
15
) gives the desired result.

We proceed to present a crucial result that will enable our adaptive accelerated gradient method possible.

Lemma 8
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be generated by (
AdaAGM
), with
\gamma\in(0,2)
, and consider the sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
defined by (
13
). If Hypothesis
5
holds, then

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\omega\mu\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\mu\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\beta\delta\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
7
, we have

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\omega\mu\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\mu\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\beta\delta\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}}{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+R_{k},

where

\displaystyle R_{k}

\displaystyle=\gamma\left[t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}-t_{k}^{2}s_{k}\right](f(x_{k})-f^{*})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1+\beta}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}-\frac{\beta(1-\delta)}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}s_{k}\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),\nabla f(x_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(1-\omega)\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{2L_{k+1}}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

If we can show
R_{k}\leq 0
, then the proof is done. Since
s_{k+1}\leq A_{k}s_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\beta}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}s_{k+1}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}-\frac{\beta(1-\delta)}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}s_{k}\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),\nabla f(x_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(1-\omega)\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{2L_{k+1}}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Suppose first that
\nabla f(x_{k+1})=\nabla f(x_{k})
. Then,

\displaystyle R_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}\left[\frac{1+\beta}{2}\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}-(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k}\right]\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\beta(1-\delta)}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2},

which implies that
R_{k}\leq-\frac{\beta(1-\delta)}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq 0
since
s_{k+1}\leq B_{k}s_{k}
.

If, on the other hand,
\nabla f(x_{k+1})\neq\nabla f(x_{k})
, then

\displaystyle R_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{(1-\omega)\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{2L_{k+1}}\left(1-\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}L_{k+1}}{(1-\omega)(t_{k+1}-1)}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(1-\omega)\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{2L_{k+1}}\left(1+\frac{\beta(1-\delta)\gamma t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}L_{k+1}}{(1-\omega)t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(1-\omega)\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{L_{k+1}}\left(\frac{s_{k}L_{k+1}}{1-\omega}-1\right)\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),\nabla f(x_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{(1-\omega)\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{2L_{k+1}}W_{k},

where

\displaystyle W_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}L_{k+1}}{(1-\omega)(t_{k+1}-1)}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(1+\frac{\beta(1-\delta)\gamma t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}L_{k+1}}{(1-\omega)t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\left(\frac{s_{k}L_{k+1}}{1-\omega}-1\right)\langle\nabla f(x_{k+1}),\nabla f(x_{k})\rangle.

The discriminant of the quadratic form
\frac{1}{2}W_{k}
is

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{s_{k}L_{k+1}}{1-\omega}-1\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(1-\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}L_{k+1}}{(1-\omega)(t_{k+1}-1)}\right)\left(1+\frac{\beta(1-\delta)\gamma t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}L_{k+1}}{(1-\omega)t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[1-\left(1-\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}L_{k+1}}{(1-\omega)(t_{k+1}-1)}\right)\frac{\beta(1-\omega)(1-\delta)\gamma t_{k}^{2}}{t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}L_{k+1}}\right]\left(\frac{s_{k}L_{k+1}}{1-\omega}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2L_{k+1}}{1-\omega}\left[\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{k+1}}{2(t_{k+1}-1)}s_{k+1}-s_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left[1-\left(1-\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}L_{k+1}}{(1-\omega)(t_{k+1}-1)}\right)\frac{\beta(1-\omega)(1-\delta)\gamma t_{k}^{2}}{t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}L_{k+1}}\right]\left(\frac{s_{k}L_{k+1}}{1-\omega}\right)^{2},

because
s_{k+1}\leq B_{k}s_{k}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\qquad\left(1-\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}L_{k+1}}{(1-\omega)(t_{k+1}-1)}\right)\frac{\beta(1-\omega)(1-\delta)\gamma t_{k}^{2}}{t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}L_{k+1}}\geq 1

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left[\frac{(1+\beta)\gamma t_{k+1}}{(1-\omega)(t_{k+1}-1)}+\frac{t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)}{\beta(1-\omega)(1-\delta)\gamma t_{k}^{2}}\right]s_{k+1}L_{k+1}\leq 1

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\frac{s_{k+1}L_{k+1}}{C_{k}}\leq 1,

which is is true by the definition of
s_{k+1}
. Therefore, we have
\Delta_{k}\leq 0
, which gives
W_{k}\geq 0
, whence
R_{k}\leq 0
. This completes the proof.

4
Convergence analysis I: the convex case

In this section, we establish a fast convergence rate of the function values, and prove the weak convergence of the iterates for (
AdaAGM
), when
f
is convex.

4.1
Convergence of the function values

In this subsection, we prove the convergence rate of the function values.

Theorem 9
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be convex and
L
-smooth. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be generated by (
AdaAGM
), with
\gamma\in(0,2)
, and assume Hypothesis
5
to hold with
\omega=\delta=0
. Given
x_{0}=y_{0}
, for every
k\geq 0
, we have

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{DL}{t_{k}^{2}},

where

D=\frac{1}{q}\left[\tfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\tfrac{s_{0}t_{0}\big((1+\beta)\gamma s_{0}t_{0}L-1\big)}{2L}\|\nabla f(x_{0})\|^{2}+s_{0}t_{0}(t_{0}-1)(f(x_{0})-f^{*})\right].

In particular, every weak subsequential limit point of
x_{k}
, as
k\to\infty
, minimizes
f
.

Proof.

Setting
\mu=0
,
\omega=0
and
\delta=0
in Lemma
8
, we obtain
E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq 0
, which gives

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{E_{k}}{\gamma t_{k}^{2}s_{k}}\leq\frac{E_{0}}{\gamma t_{k}^{2}s_{k}}.

With (
14
) and
x_{0}=y_{0}
in mind, we have

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|-\gamma s_{0}t_{0}\nabla f(x_{0})+(x_{0}-x^{*})\|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{0}^{2}s_{0}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{0})\|^{2}

(21)

\displaystyle+\gamma t_{0}^{2}s_{0}(f(x_{0})-f^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1+\beta}{2}\gamma^{2}t_{0}^{2}s_{0}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{0})\|^{2}+\gamma t_{0}(t_{0}-1)s_{0}(f(x_{0})-f^{*})

\displaystyle-\gamma s_{0}t_{0}\left(\langle\nabla f(x_{0}),x_{0}-x^{*}\rangle-(f(x_{0})-f^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma s_{0}t_{0}\left[(1+\beta)\gamma s_{0}t_{0}-\frac{1}{L}\right]\|\nabla f(x_{0})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\gamma t_{0}(t_{0}-1)s_{0}(f(x_{0})-f^{*}).

From Proposition
2
, we obtain
s_{k}\geq\frac{q}{L}
. As a result,

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{E_{0}}{\gamma t_{k}^{2}s_{k}}\leq\frac{E_{0}L}{q\gamma t_{k}^{2}}\leq\frac{DL}{t_{k}^{2}},

which allows us to conclude. For the minimizing property, it suffices to observe that
f
is weakly lower-semicontinuous, and that
t_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

Remark 10
.

Since
\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\leq 2L(f(x)-f^{*})
and
\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}
, we can bound

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{q}\min\left\{\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+s_{0}t_{0}\left[t_{0}\big((1+\beta)\gamma s_{0}L+1\big)-2\right](f(x_{0})-f^{*}),\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\frac{1}{2\gamma}\Big[1+\gamma s_{0}t_{0}L\big((1+\beta)\gamma s_{0}t_{0}L-1\big)\Big]\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+s_{0}t_{0}(t_{0}-1)(f(x_{0})-f^{*})\right\}.

For the reader’s convenience, we give explicit convergence rates for two particular choices of the parameters:

The case
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
,
\beta=1
and
t_{0}=2
.

9
gives:

with

.

4.2
Convergence of the iterates

In this subsection, we prove the weak convergence of the iterates generated by (
AdaAGM
), following
[
9
]
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be convex and
L
-smooth. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be generated by (
AdaAGM
), with
\gamma\in(0,2)
. If Hypothesis
5
holds, then

(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
are bounded.

Proof.

Recall that the sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is given by (
13
), where
x^{*}
is an arbitrary minimizer of
f
. By Lemma
8
,
E_{k+1}\leq E_{k}
, and
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}E_{k}
exists.
It follows that

\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}\leq 2E_{k}\leq 2E_{0}.

Writing

z_{k+1}=t_{k+1}(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})+y_{k+1},

we obtain

\|z_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq 2E_{0},

so that
z_{k}
is bounded, say
\|z_{k}\|\leq M
for some constant
M>0
. By (
16
), we have

z_{k+1}-z_{k}=\gamma t_{k}(y_{k+1}-x_{k}),

(22)

and so

\gamma t_{k}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k}\|=\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\|\leq 2M,

which gives

\|y_{k+1}\|\leq\|x_{k}\|+\frac{2M}{\gamma t_{k}}.

Since
z_{k}=t_{k}(x_{k}-y_{k})+y_{k}
, we deduce that

\|x_{k}\|\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{t_{k}}\right)\|y_{k}\|+\frac{M}{t_{k}},

(23)

whence

\displaystyle\|y_{k+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{t_{k}}\right)\|y_{k}\|+\frac{M}{t_{k}}\left(1+\frac{2}{\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\|y_{k}\|,\left(1+\frac{2}{\gamma}\right)M\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\|y_{0}\|,\left(1+\frac{2}{\gamma}\right)M\right\}.

It follows that
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is bounded and, by (
23
), so is
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
.

The following result
[
5
, Lemma A.4]
will be useful in the sequel:

Lemma 15
.

Let
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a real sequence and
(\zeta_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be positive such that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}}=\infty
. If

\lim_{k\to\infty}[u_{k+1}+\zeta_{k}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})]=b\in\mathbb{R},

then,

\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}=b.

We are now ready to prove the weak convergence of the iterates of (
AdaAGM
).

Theorem 16
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be convex and
L
-smooth. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be generated by (
AdaAGM
), with
\gamma\in(0,2)
. If Hypothesis
5
holds, then
x_{k}
and
y_{k}
converge weakly, as
k\to\infty
, to the same point in
\argmin(f)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
9
, every weak subsequential limit point of
x_{k}
, as
k\to\infty
, is a minimizer of
f
. Since, by Lemma
14
,
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is bounded, it suffices to prove that it cannot have more than one such limit point. Suppose, then, that
x_{m_{k}}\rightharpoonup z^{*}
and
x_{n_{k}}\rightharpoonup\tilde{z}^{*}
, as
k\to\infty
. Then
z^{*}
and
\tilde{z}^{*}
belong to
\argmin(f)
. Now, set

U_{k}:=E_{k}(z^{*})-E_{k}(\tilde{z}^{*}),

and recall
z_{k+1}=t_{k+1}(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})+y_{k+1}
. It follows from Lemma
8
that
\lim_{k\to\infty}U_{k}
exists. Using (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle U_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|z_{k+1}-z^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z_{k+1}-\tilde{z}^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle z_{k+1},z^{*}-\tilde{z}^{*}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|z^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{z}^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle t_{k+1}x_{k+1}-(t_{k+1}-1)y_{k+1},z^{*}-\tilde{z}^{*}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|z^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{z}^{*}\|^{2},

so that

-2\langle t_{k+1}x_{k+1}-(t_{k+1}-1)y_{k+1},z^{*}-\tilde{z}^{*}\rangle+\|z^{*}\|^{2}-\|\tilde{z}^{*}\|^{2}=2U_{k}.

Define

\displaystyle u_{k}

\displaystyle=\|x_{k+1}-z^{*}\|^{2}-\|x_{k+1}-\tilde{z}^{*}\|^{2}=-2\langle x_{k+1},z^{*}-\tilde{z}^{*}\rangle+\|z^{*}\|^{2}-\|\tilde{z}^{*}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\xi_{k}

\displaystyle=\|y_{k+1}-z^{*}\|^{2}-\|y_{k+1}-\tilde{z}^{*}\|^{2}=-2\langle y_{k+1},z^{*}-\tilde{z}^{*}\rangle+\|z^{*}\|^{2}-\|\tilde{z}^{*}\|^{2},

so that

t_{k+1}u_{k}-(t_{k+1}-1)\xi_{k}=2U_{k}.

(24)

Using (
22
), we have
z_{k+1}-z_{k}=\gamma t_{k}(y_{k+1}-x_{k})
, which gives

y_{k+1}=x_{k}+\frac{1}{\gamma t_{k}}(z_{k+1}-z_{k}).

(25)

With this, we can rewrite
\xi_{k}
as

\displaystyle\xi_{k}

\displaystyle=-2\left\langle x_{k}+\frac{z_{k+1}-z_{k}}{\gamma t_{k}},z^{*}-\tilde{z}^{*}\right\rangle+\|z^{*}\|^{2}-\|\tilde{z}^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=u_{k-1}-\frac{2}{\gamma t_{k}}\langle z_{k+1}-z_{k},z^{*}-\tilde{z}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=u_{k-1}+\frac{2}{\gamma t_{k}}(U_{k}-U_{k-1}).

Substituting this equality into (
24
) gives

u_{k}+(t_{k+1}-1)(u_{k}-u_{k-1})=2U_{k}+\frac{2(t_{k+1}-1)}{\gamma t_{k}}(U_{k}-U_{k-1}).

Since
\frac{t_{k+1}-1}{t_{k}}<\frac{t_{k}-(1-m)}{t_{k}}<1
, and
\lim_{k\to\infty}U_{k}
exists, we deduce that the right-hand side has a limit. Invoking Lemma
15
, we deduce that
\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}
exists. Replacing
k
by
m_{k}
, and then by
n_{k}
in the definition of
u_{k}
, and taking limits, we obtain

-\|z^{*}-\tilde{z}^{*}\|=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}=\|\tilde{z}^{*}-z^{*}\|^{2},

which implies that
z^{*}=\tilde{z}^{*}
. It ensues that
x_{k}
converges weakly to a minimizer of
f
. From (
25
), it easily follows that
y_{k}
converges weakly to the same limit.

5
Convergence analysis II: the strongly convex case

In this section, we establish a linear convergence rate of the function values when

is strongly convex.

Lemma 17
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth, where
L\geq\mu>0
. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be generated by (
AdaAGM
), with
\gamma\in(0,2)
. Let Hypothesis
5
hold with
\omega=\delta=\frac{1}{2}
. Consider the sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
defined by (
13
). Then,

\displaystyle E_{k+1}-E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\mu\gamma t_{k+1}(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k+1}}{4}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\mu\gamma t_{k+1}s_{k+1}}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\beta\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}}{4}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Proof.

The result follows by setting
\omega=\frac{1}{2}
and
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
in Lemma
8
.

In what follows, we give an upper bound for
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth, where
L\geq\mu>0
. Let
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be defined by (
13
). Then,

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(t_{k+1}-1)^{2}(1+\eta+\sigma)}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sigma}+\lambda\right)\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{s_{k}^{2}}{2}\left[\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)(t_{k+1}-1)^{2}+\beta\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}+\frac{\gamma t_{k}^{2}}{\mu s_{k}}\right]\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2},

for every
\eta,\sigma,\lambda>0
.

Proof.

We begin with (
14
), to obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{k}

\displaystyle=t_{k+1}(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})+(y_{k+1}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=(t_{k+1}-1)(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})+(x_{k+1}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=(t_{k+1}-1)(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+(t_{k+1}-1)s_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})+(x_{k+1}-x^{*}),

so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(t_{k+1}-1)^{2}(1+\eta+\sigma)}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sigma}+\lambda\right)\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(t_{k+1}-1)^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)s_{k}^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2},

for every
\eta,\sigma,\lambda>0
. Since
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex, we have

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}}{2\mu}.

Using the last two inequalities in (
13
), we arrive at the desired result.

We are now in a position to derive the convergence results under strong convexity.

Theorem 19
.

Let
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth, where
L\geq\mu>0
. Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be generated by (
AdaAGM
), with
\gamma\in(0,2)
. Let Hypothesis
5
hold with
\omega=\delta=\frac{1}{2}
. Given
x_{0}=y_{0}
, for every
k\geq 0
, we have

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{DL}{t_{k}^{2}}(1-\rho)^{k},

where

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\frac{\mu\gamma q}{4L},\frac{\mu q}{\frac{2L}{\beta\gamma}+\left(\frac{8}{\beta\gamma^{2}}+2\right)\mu q}\right\},

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\left[\tfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\tfrac{s_{0}t_{0}\left((1+\beta)\gamma s_{0}t_{0}L-1\right)}{2L}\|\nabla f(x_{0})\|^{2}+t_{0}(t_{0}-1)s_{0}(f(x_{0})-f^{*})\right].

Proof.

Using Lemmas
17
and
18
, we obtain

E_{k+1}-E_{k}\leq-rE_{k},

where

r=\max_{\eta,\sigma,\lambda>0}\min\left\{\frac{\mu\gamma s_{k+1}t_{k+1}}{2(t_{k+1}-1)(1+\eta+\sigma)},\frac{\mu\gamma s_{k+1}t_{k+1}}{1+\frac{1}{\sigma}+\lambda},\frac{\mu s_{k}}{2\left(\frac{1}{\beta\gamma}+\varphi_{k}\mu s_{k}\right)}\right\},

with

\varphi_{k}=\frac{1+\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\lambda}}{\beta\gamma^{2}}\left(\frac{t_{k+1}-1}{t_{k}}\right)^{2}+1\leq\frac{1+\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\lambda}}{\beta\gamma^{2}}+1.

Setting
\eta=\sigma=\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda=1
, we obtain

r\geq\min\left\{\frac{\mu\gamma s_{k+1}}{4},\frac{\mu s_{k}}{\frac{2}{\beta\gamma}+\left(\frac{8}{\beta\gamma^{2}}+2\right)\mu s_{k}}\right\}.

By Proposition
2
, we have
s_{k}\geq\frac{q}{L}
for all
k\geq 0
, so that

r\geq\min\left\{\frac{\mu\gamma q}{4L},\frac{\mu q}{\frac{2L}{\beta\gamma}+\left(\frac{8}{\beta\gamma^{2}}+2\right)\mu q}\right\}:=\rho.

It follows that

E_{k+1}\leq(1-\rho)E_{k},

so that

E_{k}\leq(1-\rho)^{k}E_{0},\quad\forall k\geq 0.

As a result,

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{E_{k}}{\gamma t_{k}^{2}s_{k}}\leq\frac{E_{0}}{\gamma t_{k}^{2}s_{k}}(1-\rho)^{k}.

Given
x_{0}=y_{0}
, (
21
) yields

E_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma s_{0}t_{0}\left[(1+\beta)\gamma s_{0}t_{0}-\frac{1}{L}\right]\|\nabla f(x_{0})\|^{2}+\gamma t_{0}(t_{0}-1)s_{0}(f(x_{0})-f^{*}).

Recalling that
s_{k}\geq\frac{q}{L}
, we obtain

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{E_{0}L}{\gamma qt_{k}^{2}}(1-\rho)^{k}\leq\frac{DL}{t_{k}^{2}}(1-\rho)^{k},

as claimed.

Remark 20
.

In case
f
is only convex, we still have

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leq\frac{DL}{t_{k}^{2}}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right).

Also, in view of Lemma
17
, we have

\frac{\beta\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}s_{k}^{2}}{4}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq E_{k}-E_{k+1},\quad\forall k\geq 0,

which implies

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}<\infty.

6
Conclusions

We have developed an adaptive accelerated gradient method for solving smooth convex optimization problems. The method is free of line search procedures. It provides a convergence guarantee of the iterates and ensures a fast convergence rate
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{L}{k^{2}}\right)
for the function values when the objective function
f
is convex and
L
-smooth, and a linear convergence rate
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{L}{k^{2}}(1-\rho)^{k}\right)
, with
\rho=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\mu}{L}\right)
, in case
f
is
\mu
-strongly convex.

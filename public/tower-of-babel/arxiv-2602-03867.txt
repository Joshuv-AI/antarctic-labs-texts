Subgroup perfect codes of S n S_{n} in Cayley graphs
Ankan Shaw, Shibesh Kotal, Satya Bagchi

Abstract
A perfect code in a graph Γ = ( V , E ) \Gamma=(V,E) is a subset C C of V V such that no two vertices in C C are adjacent and every vertex in V ∖ C V\setminus C is adjacent to exactly one vertex in C C . A subgroup H H of a group G G is called a subgroup perfect code of G G if there exists a Cayley graph of G G which admits H H as a perfect code. In this work, we present a classification of cyclic 2-subgroup perfect codes in S n S_{n} . We analyze these subgroup codes, detailing their structure and properties. We extend our discussion to various classes of subgroup codes in the symmetric group S n S_{n} , encompassing both commutative and non-commutative cases. We provide numerous examples to illustrate and support our findings.

In coding theory, the question of whether perfect codes exist under a particular metric is crucial. In the early

Now,

It is clear that

does not contain an involution. Hence,

is not perfect code in
S_{n}.

∎

\displaystyle=\{y^{-k^{(t_{1}-i)}}x_{3}^{-i}x_{1}^{-jk^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}\hskip 50.64586pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn3}))

\displaystyle=\{yy^{-(k^{(t_{1}-i)}+1)}x_{3}^{-i}x_{1}^{-jk^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\},

and these hold for
1\leq i\leq t-1.
Since
k
is odd, therefore, there exist
m^{\prime},r^{\prime},n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
k^{t_{1}-i}+1=2m^{\prime}
and
m^{\prime}=n^{\prime}2^{l}+r^{\prime}
with
0\leq r^{\prime}<2^{l}
. Then

\displaystyle[yx_{3}^{i}\langle x_{1}\rangle]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\{yy^{-2m^{\prime}}x_{3}^{-i}x_{1}^{-jk^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}.

\displaystyle=\{yx_{1}^{m^{\prime}}x_{3}^{-i}x_{1}^{-jk^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}

\displaystyle=\{yx_{1}^{r^{\prime}}x_{3}^{-i}x_{1}^{-jk^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}

\displaystyle=\{yx_{1}^{2^{l}-(2^{l}-r^{\prime})}x_{3}^{-i}x_{1}^{-jk^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}

\displaystyle=\{yx_{3}^{-i}x_{1}^{2^{l}-(2^{l}-r^{\prime})k^{i}}x_{1}^{-jk^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}\hskip 55.48277pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn5}))

\displaystyle=\{yx_{3}^{-i}x_{1}^{(r^{\prime}-j)k^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}

\displaystyle=yx_{3}^{-i}\langle x_{1}\rangle.

Now,

\displaystyle[yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}\langle x_{1}\rangle]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\{yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}x_{1}^{j}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\{x_{1}^{-j}x_{2}x_{3}^{-i}y^{-1}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}

\displaystyle=\{x_{1}^{-j}x_{2}y^{-k^{(t_{1}-i)}}x_{3}^{-i}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}\hskip 65.44142pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn3}))

\displaystyle=\{x_{2}x_{1}^{-jk}y^{-k^{(t_{1}-i)}}x_{3}^{-i}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}\hskip 14.22636pt(\text{with the help of}\;(\ref{eqn2}))

\displaystyle=\{x_{2}y^{2jk-(k^{(t_{1}-i)})}x_{3}^{-i}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}\hskip 46.94687pt(\text{as}\;y^{2}=x_{1}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\{x_{2}y^{k}y^{2jk-(k^{(t_{1}-i)}-k)}x_{3}^{-i}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}

and these holds for
1\leq i\leq t-1.
Since
k
is odd, therefore, there exists
m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
2jk-k^{(t_{1}-i)}-k=-2m_{j}
.

Case A:
When
m_{j}\leq 0
. Then

\displaystyle[yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}\langle x_{1}\rangle]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\{yx_{2}x_{1}^{m_{j}}x_{3}^{-i}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}\hskip 28.45274pt(\text{by the given condition})

\displaystyle=\{yx_{2}x_{1}^{-|m_{j}|}x_{3}^{-i}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}

\displaystyle=\{yx_{2}x_{3}^{-i}x_{1}^{-|m_{j}|k^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}\hskip 68.28644pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn5}))

\displaystyle=\{yx_{3}^{-is}x_{2}x_{1}^{m_{j}k^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}\hskip 75.39963pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn1}))

\displaystyle=yx_{3}^{-is}x_{2}\langle x_{1}\rangle.

Case B:
When
m_{j}>0
. Then there exist
n_{j},r_{j}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
m_{j}=n_{j}2^{l}+r_{j}
with
0\leq r_{j}<2^{l}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle[yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}\langle x_{1}\rangle]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\{yx_{2}x_{1}^{r_{j}}x_{3}^{(t_{1}-i)}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}

\displaystyle=\{yx_{2}x_{1}^{2^{l}-(2^{l}-r_{j})}x_{3}^{(t_{1}-i)}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}

\displaystyle=\{yx_{2}x_{3}^{t_{1}-i}x_{1}^{2^{l}-(2^{l}-r_{j})k^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}\hskip 42.67912pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn5}))

\displaystyle=\{yx_{3}^{-is}x_{2}x_{1}^{r_{j}k^{i}}:j=0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}\hskip 78.81425pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn1}))

\displaystyle=yx_{3}^{-is}x_{2}\langle x_{1}\rangle.

The above arguments and the Theorem
7
evidence that
yH_{1}
is inverse-closed. We consider the following four cases to show that
yH_{1}
contains no involution.

Case I:
When
x_{3}^{i}=x_{3}^{-i}
for some
i\in\{1,2,\dots,t-1\}.
Then
[yx_{3}^{i}\langle x_{1}\rangle]^{-1}=yx_{3}^{i}\langle x_{1}\rangle.
Suppose
yx_{3}^{i}\langle x_{1}\rangle
contains an involution. Then there exists
k_{1}\in\{0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}
such that
yx_{3}^{i}x_{1}^{k_{1}}=(yx_{3}^{i}x_{1}^{k_{1}})^{-1}.
This implies
yx_{3}^{i}x_{1}^{k_{1}}=x_{1}^{-k_{1}}x_{3}^{-i}y^{-1}.
Then it follows from equations
(\ref{eqn3})
and
(\ref{eqn5})
that
yx_{1}^{k^{i}k_{1}}x_{3}^{i}=x_{1}^{-k_{1}}y^{-k^{t_{1}-i}}x_{3}^{-i}.
Consequently,
y^{1-2k_{1}k^{i}}=y^{2k_{1}-k^{t_{1}-i}}.
Therefore,
y^{1-2k_{1}k^{i}-2k_{1}+k^{i}}=1.
This implies
(2k_{1}-1)(k^{i}+1)=s^{\prime}2^{l+1}
for some
s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}.
Then
k^{i}\equiv-1\pmod{2^{l+1}}.
Since
k<2^{l}
and
k
is odd, therefore, we arrive at a contradiction due to the Lemma
4.1
.

Case II:
When
x_{3}^{i}\neq x_{3}^{-i}
for
i\in\{1,2,\dots,t-1\}.
Then
[yx_{3}^{i}\langle x_{1}\rangle]
does not contain an involution.

Case III:
When
x_{3}^{i}=x_{3}^{-is}
for some
i\in\{1,2,\dots,t-1\}
. Suppose
[yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}\langle x_{1}\rangle]
contains an involution. Then there exists
m\in\{0,1,\dots,2^{l}-1\}
such that

\displaystyle yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}x_{1}^{m}

\displaystyle=(yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}x_{1}^{m})^{-1}

\displaystyle\iff yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}x_{1}^{m}

\displaystyle=x_{2}x_{1}^{-mk}x_{3}^{-i}y^{-1}\hskip 85.35826pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn4}))

\displaystyle\iff yx_{2}x_{3}^{is}x_{1}^{m}

\displaystyle=x_{2}x_{1}^{-mk}y^{-k^{t_{1}-i}}x_{3}^{-i}\hskip 59.75095pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn1}),\;(\ref{eqn3}))

\displaystyle\iff x_{2}y^{k}x_{3}^{is}x_{1}^{m}

\displaystyle=x_{2}x_{1}^{-mk}y^{-k^{t_{1}-i}}x_{3}^{-i}\hskip 59.75095pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn7}))

\displaystyle\iff y^{k}x_{3}^{-i}x_{1}^{m}

\displaystyle=x_{1}^{-mk}y^{-k^{t_{1}-i}}x_{3}^{-i}\hskip 69.90843pt(\text{as}\;x_{3}^{is}=x_{3}^{-i})

\displaystyle\iff y^{k}x_{3}^{t_{1}-i}x_{1}^{m}

\displaystyle=x_{1}^{-mk}y^{-k^{t_{1}-i}}x_{3}^{-i}

\displaystyle\iff y^{k}x_{1}^{mk^{t_{1}-i}}x_{3}^{-i}

\displaystyle=x_{1}^{-mk}y^{-k^{t_{1}-i}}x_{3}^{-i}\hskip 71.13188pt(\text{from}\;(\ref{eqn8}))

\displaystyle\iff y^{k-2mk^{t_{1}-i}}

\displaystyle=y^{2mk-k^{t_{1}-i}}.

This shows that,
{k-2mk^{t_{1}-i}-2mk+k^{t_{1}-i}}\equiv 0\pmod{2^{l+1}}
. This follows that
(2m-1)(1+k^{t_{1}-i-1})\equiv 0\pmod{2^{l+1}}
as
k
is odd. Consequently,
(k^{t_{1}-i-1}+1)\equiv 0\pmod{2^{l+1}}
, which is a contradiction due to the Lemma
4.1
.

Case IV:
When
x_{3}^{i}\neq x_{3}^{-is}
for some
i\in\{1,2,\dots,t-1\}.
Then
[yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}\langle x_{1}\rangle]^{-1}\neq yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}\langle x_{1}\rangle
. Consequently,
yx_{3}^{i}x_{2}\langle x_{1}\rangle
contains no involution.

Hence, it follows from equation

yH_{1}
is inverse-closed and contains no involution.

5
Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive classification of cyclic
2
-subgroup perfect codes in
S_{n}
. Through detailed analysis, we have elucidated the structure and properties of these subgroup codes. Furthermore, our findings extend to a broader range of subgroup codes within the symmetric group
S_{n}
, addressing both commutative and non-commutative instances. Moreover, these results help to examine subgroup perfect codes in any finite group if we classify square elements of that group.

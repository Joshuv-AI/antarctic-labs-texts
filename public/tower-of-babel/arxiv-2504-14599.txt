Interpolated multiple t t -values of general level with fixed weight, depth and height
Zhonghua Li and Zhenlu Wang a. School of Mathematical Sciences,Key Laboratory of Intelligent Computing and Applications (Ministry of Education),Tongji University, Shanghai 200092, Chinab. School of Electronics and Information Engineering,Taizhou University, Taizhou, Zhejiang 318000, China

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the interpolated multiple t t -values of general level and represent a generating function for sums of interpolated multiple t t -values of general level with fixed weight, depth, and height in terms of a generalized hypergeometric function F 2 3 \,{}_{3}F_{2} evaluated at 1 1 . Furthermore, we explore several special cases of our results. The theorems presented in this paper extend earlier results on multiple zeta values and multiple t t -values of general level.

Introduction

The theory of multiple zeta values (MZVs) originated in the early 1990s, independently developed by Hoffman
[
2
]
and Zagier
[
11
]
. These values, which Euler first studied in special cases, have since played a significant role in both number theory and mathematical physics. For an index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
with
k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we define three key quantities:

•

the weight:
\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{k})=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}
,

•

the depth:
\operatorname{dep}(\mathbf{k})=n
,

•

the height:
\operatorname{ht}(\mathbf{k})=|\{i\mid 1\leq i\leq n,\,k_{i}\geq 2\}|
.

An index
\mathbf{k}
is called admissible if
k_{1}>1
. For such an index, the MZV
[
2
,
11
]
is defined by the following convergent series

\zeta(\mathbf{k})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{n}>0}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}.

A closely related variant, the multiple zeta-star values (MZSVs), has also been widely investigated. For an admissible index
\mathbf{k}
, the MZSV
[
2
]

is given by

\zeta^{\star}(\mathbf{k})=\zeta^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum_{m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{n}>0}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}.

It is well-known that numerous
\mathbb{Q}
-linear relations exist among MZVs and MZSVs; see
[
12
]
for further details. Nevertheless, their exact algebraic structure remains largely mysterious, and many profound questions in this area await resolution.

Hoffman
[
3
]
introduced and studied an odd variant of MZVs (resp. MZSVs), namely the multiple
t
-values (MtVs) (resp. the multiple
t
-star values (MtSVs)). Recent research has revealed that MtVs and MtSVs possess rich arithmetic and algebraic structures remarkably similar to those of their classical counterparts. Moreover, these odd variants appear to be deeply connected to the algebraic structure of the space generated by all MZVs. For detailed investigations of these connections, we refer to

[
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
]
.

We considered the level
N
variants in
[
6
]
. Let
N
be a fixed positive integer. For an admissible index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
and any
a\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
, the MtV of level
N
and the MtSV of level
N
are defined by

t_{N,a}(\mathbf{k})=t_{N,a}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}\equiv a\pmod{N}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}

and

t_{N,a}^{\star}(\mathbf{k})=t_{N,a}^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}\equiv a\pmod{N}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}},

respectively. Note that if the index is empty, we treat the values
t_{N,a}(\varnothing)=t_{N,a}^{\star}(\varnothing)=1
.
In the case of
N=2
and
a=1
, we recover the classical MtVs
[
3
]

t(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=t_{2,1}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}:\text{odd}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}

and the MtSVs
[
3
]

t^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=t^{\star}_{2,1}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}:\text{odd}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}

respectively. Moreover, it is easy to see that
t_{1,1}(\mathbf{k})=\zeta(\mathbf{k})
and
t_{1,1}^{\star}(\mathbf{k})=\zeta^{\star}(\mathbf{k})

which are MZVs and MZSVs respectively. We also have
t_{N,N}(\mathbf{k})=N^{-\operatorname{wt}({\mathbf{k}})}\zeta(\mathbf{k})
and
t_{N,N}^{\star}(\mathbf{k})=N^{-\operatorname{wt}({\mathbf{k}})}\zeta^{\star}(\mathbf{k})
.

Yamamoto
[
10
]
introduced the interpolated MZVs, which can be regarded as interpolation polynomials of MZVs and MZSVs. Li
[
5
]
established a generating function for sums of interpolated MZVs with fixed weight, depth, and height, which generalizes Ohno-Zagier relation for MZVs
[
8
]
and the analogous result for MZSVs proved by Aoki, Kombu and Ohno
[
1
]
. In this paper, we study interpolation polynomials of MtVs and MtSVs of general level. Let
r
be a variable. For an admissible index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, the interpolated MtV of level
N
is defined as

\displaystyle t_{N,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k})=t_{N,a}^{r}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{p}}r^{n-\operatorname{dep}(\mathbf{p})}t_{N,a}(\mathbf{p}),

where the summation extends over all indices
\mathbf{p}
obtained from the pattern

(k_{1}\Box k_{2}\Box\cdots\Box k_{n})

by replacing each placeholder
\Box
with either a comma “
,
” or a plus “
+
”. Note that
t_{N,a}^{0}(\mathbf{k})=t_{N,a}(\mathbf{k})
and
t_{N,a}^{1}(\mathbf{k})=t_{N,a}^{\star}(\mathbf{k})
. The parameter
r
thus continuously interpolates between the non-star and star variants of MtVs of level
N
, providing a unified framework for studying these objects.

Our main object of study is the generating function for sums of interpolated MtVs of level
N
with fixed weight, depth, and height. This generating function can be expressed in terms of the generalized hypergeometric function
{}_{3}F_{2}
evaluated at
1
. Here for a positive integer
m
and complex numbers
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m+1}
,
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
with none of
c_{i}
is zero or a negative integer, the generalized hypergeometric function
{}_{m+1}F_{m}
is defined by

\displaystyle{}_{m+1}F_{m}{\left(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{b_1,\ldots,b_{m+1}}{c_1,\ldots,c_m};z\right)}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(b_{1})_{n}\cdots(b_{m+1})_{n}}{(c_{1})_{n}\cdots(c_{m})_{n}}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

where the Pochhammer symbol
(b)_{n}
denotes the rising factorial:

(b)_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(b+n)}{\Gamma(b)}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad n=0,\\
b(b+1)\cdots(b+n-1)&\text{if}\quad n>0.\end{cases}

The above series is absolutely and uniformly convergent for
|z|<1
and the convergence also extends over the unit circle if
\Re\left(\sum c_{i}-\sum b_{i}\right)>0
.

This paper is organized as follows. We obtain a representation of the generating function for sums of interpolated MtVs of level
N
with fixed weight, depth, and height in Section
2
. Based on our main theorem, we discuss several special cases in Section
3
and get a generating function for sums of interpolated MtVs of level
N
with maximal height and a weighted sum formula of sums of interpolated MtVs of level
N
with fixed weight and depth.

2
Main theorems

Let
I_{0}(k,n,s)
be the set of all admissible indices of weight
k
, depth
n
and height
s
. Notice that
I_{0}(k,n,s)
is nonempty if and only if
k\geq n+s
and
n\geq s\geq 1
. Let

\displaystyle X_{0}^{r}(k,n,s)=X_{N,a,0}^{r}(k,n,s)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n,s)}t_{N,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k})

be the sum of interpolated MtVs of level
N
with weight
k
, depth
n
, and height
s
. Then a generating function for these sums can be represented by the generalized hypergeometric function
{}_{3}F_{2}
evaluated at
1
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For formal variables
u,v,w
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\geq n+s\atop n\geq s\geq 1}X_{0}^{r}(k,n,s)u^{k-n-s}v^{n-s}w^{2s-2}=\frac{1}{(a-\alpha_{1})(a-\alpha_{2})}{}_{3}F_{2}{\left(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{\frac{a+\beta_1}{N},\frac{a+\beta_2}{N},1}{\frac{a-\alpha_1}{N}+1,\frac{a-\alpha_2}{N}+1};1\right)},

where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are determined by
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=u+vr,\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}=r(uv-w^{2})
, and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
are determined by
\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=-u+v(1-r),\beta_{1}\beta_{2}=(r-1)(uv-w^{2})
.

Theorem
2.1
unifies our previous results on MtVs and MtSVs of level
N
and extends the corresponding result on interpolated MZVs. Specifically:

(i)

The case
r=0

[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
for the MtVs of level
N

(ii)

The case

[

N
;

(iii)

The case
N=a=1
reduces to
[

concerning the interpolated MZVs through application of the transformation formula

We prove the theorem similarly as that in
[
6
, Theorems 2.1, 2.2]
. For an index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N,a}(\mathbf{k};z)=\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}\equiv a\pmod{N}}\frac{z^{m}_{1}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}},

which converges absolutely for
|z|<1
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{L}_{N,a}(\mathbf{k};1)=t_{N,a}(\mathbf{k})
for any admissible index
\mathbf{k}
, which is a MtV of level
N
. Define that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k};z)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{p}}r^{n-\operatorname{dep}(\mathbf{p})}\mathcal{L}_{N,a}(\mathbf{p};z),

where
\mathbf{p}
runs over all sequences of the form

\mathbf{p}=(k_{1}\Box k_{2}\Box\cdots\Box k_{n})

in which each
\Box
is filled by the comma “
,
” or the plus “
+
”. Then the right-hand side of the definition equation of
\mathcal{L}_{N,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k};z)
converges locally uniformly in the domain
|z|<1
, and converges in points
|z|=1
if
\mathbf{k}
is admissible with the value of it coincides with
t_{N,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k})
. We have the following iterated integral representation

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k};z)=\int\limits_{z>z_{1}>\cdots>z_{k}>0}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(z_{i})dz_{i},

where
k=\operatorname{wt}(\mathbf{k})=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}
and

\displaystyle f_{i}(z)=\begin{cases}\frac{r}{z}+\frac{z^{N-1}}{1-z^{N}}&\text{if}\quad i=k_{1},k_{1}+k_{2},\ldots,k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n-1},\\
\frac{z^{a-1}}{1-z^{N}}&\text{if}\quad i=k,\\
\frac{1}{z}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is easy to find that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{L}_{N,a}^{r}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n};z)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{z}\mathcal{L}_{N,a}^{r}(k_{1}-1,k_{2},\ldots,k_{n};z)&\text{if}\quad k_{1}>1,\\
\left(\frac{r}{z}+\frac{z^{N-1}}{1-z^{N}}\right)\mathcal{L}_{N,a}^{r}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{n};z)&\text{if}\quad k_{1}=1,n\geq 2,\\
\frac{z^{a-1}}{1-z^{N}}&\text{if}\quad k_{1}=n=1.\end{cases}

(2.1)

For nonnegative integers
k,n,s
, we denote by
I(k,n,s)
the set of all indices of weight
k
, depth
n
and height
s
. We define the following two sums

\displaystyle X^{r}(k,n,s;z)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I(k,n,s)}\mathcal{L}_{N,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k};z)\quad\text{and}\quad X_{0}^{r}(k,n,s;z)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n,s)}\mathcal{L}_{N,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k};z).

One can see that
X^{r}(k,n,s;z)
and
X_{0}^{r}(k,n,s;z)
also depend on
N
and
a
, but we do not add extra subscripts
N,a
for brevity. By convention, the above sums are defined to be zero if the index set is empty. Additionally, we set
X^{r}(0,0,0;z)=1
.

For integers
k,n,s
, by (
2.1
), we obtain

(1)

if
k\geq n+s
and

, then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}X_{0}^{r}(k,n,s;z)=\frac{1}{z}\left[X^{r}(k-1,n,s-1;z)+X_{0}^{r}(k-1,n,s;z)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.-X_{0}^{r}(k-1,n,s-1;z)\right];

(2.2)

(2)

if
k\geq n+s
,
n\geq s\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\left[X^{r}(k,n,s;z)-X_{0}^{r}(k,n,s;z)\right]=\left(\frac{r}{z}+\frac{z^{N-1}}{1-z^{N}}\right)X^{r}(k-1,n-1,s;z).

(2.3)

Now we define the generating functions for
X^{r}(k,n,s;z)
and
X^{r}_{0}(k,n,s;z)

\Phi^{r}(z)=\Phi^{r}(u,v,w;z)=\sum\limits_{k,n,s\geq 0}X^{r}(k,n,s;z)u^{k-n-s}v^{n-s}w^{2s}

and

\Phi_{0}^{r}(z)=\Phi^{r}_{0}(u,v,w;z)=\sum\limits_{k,n,s\geq 0}X^{r}_{0}(k,n,s;z)u^{k-n-s}v^{n-s}w^{2s-2},

respectively. Notice that we do not add extra subscripts

to
\Phi^{r}(z)
and
\Phi^{r}_{0}(z)
for brevity. Using (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\Phi_{0}^{r}(z)=\frac{1}{vz}\left(\Phi^{r}(z)-1-w^{2}\Phi^{r}_{0}(z)\right)+\frac{u}{z}\Phi_{0}^{r}(z),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\left(\Phi^{r}(z)-w^{2}\Phi_{0}^{r}(z)\right)=v\left(\frac{r}{z}+\frac{z^{N-1}}{1-z^{N}}\right)\left(\Phi^{r}(z)-1\right)+\frac{vz^{a-1}}{1-z^{N}}.

Eliminating
\Phi^{r}(z)
, we obtain the differential equation satisfied by
\Phi_{0}^{r}(z)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

\Phi_{0}^{r}=\Phi_{0}^{r}(z)
satisfies the following differential equation

\displaystyle z^{2}(1-z^{N})(\Phi_{0}^{r})^{\prime\prime}+z[(1-u)(1-z^{N})-v\left(r+(1-r)z^{N}\right)](\Phi_{0}^{r})^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(r+(1-r)z^{N}\right)(uv-w^{2})\Phi_{0}^{r}=z^{a}.

(2.4)

The solution to the differential equation (
2.4
) is presented below.

Theorem 2.3
.

For formal variables
u,v,w
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{r}_{0}(u,v,w;z)=\frac{z^{a}}{(a-\alpha_{1})(a-\alpha_{2})}{}_{3}F_{2}{\left(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{\frac{a+\beta_1}{N},\frac{a+\beta_2}{N},1}{\frac{a-\alpha_1}{N}+1,\frac{a-\alpha_2}{N}+1};z^{N}\right)},

where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are determined by
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=u+vr,\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}=r(uv-w^{2})
, and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
are determined by
\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=-u+v(1-r),\beta_{1}\beta_{2}=(r-1)(uv-w^{2})
.

Proof.
Assume that
\Phi_{0}^{r}(z)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}z^{n}
, then we have
p_{1},\ldots,p_{a-1},p_{a+1},\ldots,p_{N}=0
,

\displaystyle p_{a}=\frac{1}{a(a-u-vr)+r(uv-w^{2})}

and

\displaystyle p_{n+N}=\frac{n(n-u+v-vr)-(1-r)(uv-w^{2})}{(n+N)(n+N-u-vr)+r(uv-w^{2})}p_{n},\quad n\geq 1.

Hence for any
m\geq 1
that satisfies
m\not\equiv a\pmod{N}
, we have
p_{m}=0
. And for any
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle p_{a+nN}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a+(n-1)N)(a+(n-1)N-u+v-vr)-(1-r)(uv-w^{2})}{(a+nN)(a+nN-u-vr)+r(uv-w^{2})}p_{a+(n-1)N}.

Then we get

\displaystyle p_{a+nN}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a+(n-1)N+\beta_{1})(a+(n-1)N+\beta_{2})}{(a+nN-\alpha_{1})(a+nN-\alpha_{2})}p_{a+(n-1)N}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\frac{a+\beta_{1}}{N}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{a+\beta_{2}}{N}\right)_{n}}{\left(\frac{a-\alpha_{1}}{N}+1\right)_{n}\left(\frac{a-\alpha_{2}}{N}+1\right)_{n}}\frac{1}{(a-\alpha_{1})(a-\alpha_{2})}.

Hence, we finish the proof.
∎

Sum of height one

Corollary 3.1
.

For formal variables

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\geq n+1,n\geq 1}t_{N,a}^{r}(k-n+1,\{1\}^{n-1})u^{k-n-1}v^{n-1}=\frac{1}{(a-u)(a-vr)}{}_{3}F_{2}{\left(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{\frac{a-u}{N},\frac{a+v(1-r)}{N},1}{\frac{a+N-u}{N},\frac{a+N-vr}{N}};1\right)}.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}t_{N,a}^{r}(m,\{1\}^{n-1})v^{n-1}=\frac{1}{a^{m-1}(a-vr)}{}_{m+1}F_{m}{\left(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{1,\frac{a+v(1-r)}{N},\{\frac{a}{N}\}^{m-1}}{\frac{a+N-vr}{N},\{\frac{a+N}{N}\}^{m-1}};1\right)}.

Sum of maximal height

Setting

in Theorem

, then we get
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=u,\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}=-rw^{2}

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}{\left(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a,b,1}{c,2+a+b-c};1\right)}=\frac{1+a+b-c}{(1+a-c)(1+b-c)}\left(1-c+\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(1+a+b-c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\right)

N
with maximal height.

3.3
A weighted sum formula

Let
I_{0}(k,n)
be the set of admissible indices of weight
k
and depth
n
. We have the following generating function for weight sums of interpolated MtVs of level
N
.

Corollary 3.3
.

For a formal variable
u
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k>n\geq 1}\left((1-2r)^{k-n-1}2^{n-1}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n)}t_{2a,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k})\right)u^{k-2}

\displaystyle=a^{-2}2^{\frac{2u}{a}}t(2)\exp\left(\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{4(2^{n-1}-1)t(n)}{na^{n}(2^{n}-1)}u^{n}\right).

Proof.
Setting

in Theorem

, then we have
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=u+vr,\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=-u+v(1-r)

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}=\beta_{1}\beta_{2}=0

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k>n\geq 1}\left(\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n)}t_{2a,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k})\right)u^{k-n-1}v^{n-1}=\frac{1}{a(a-u-vr)}{}_{3}F_{2}{\left(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{\frac{a-u+v(1-r)}{N},\frac{a}{N},1}{\frac{a-u-vr}{N}+1,\frac{a}{N}+1};1\right)}.

We discuss the special case of
N=2a
. Let
u=(1-2r)x
and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k>n\geq 1}\left((1-2r)^{k-n-1}2^{n-1}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n)}t_{2a,a}^{r}(\mathbf{k})\right)x^{k-2}=\frac{1}{a(a-x)}{}_{3}F_{2}{\left(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{\frac{a+x}{2a},\frac{1}{2},1}{\frac{3a-x}{2a},\frac{3}{2}};1\right)}.

Using the summation formula
[

, 7.4.4.21]

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}{\left(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a,b,c}{1+a-b,1+a-c};1\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{a}}\frac{\Gamma(1+a-b)\Gamma(1+a-c)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{a}{2}-b-c\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+a}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{a}{2}-b\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{a}{2}-c\right)\Gamma(1+a-b-c)},\quad\Re(a-2b-2c)>-2,

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{8a^{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{x}{2a}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(1-\frac{x}{2a}\right)^{2}}.

Note that

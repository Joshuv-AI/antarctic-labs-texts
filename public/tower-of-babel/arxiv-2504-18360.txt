On the Generalization of Kitaev Codes as Generalized Bicycle Codes
François Arnault 1 , Philippe Gaborit 1 , Nicolas Saussay 1

Abstract
Surface codes have historically been the dominant choice for quantum error correction due to their superior error threshold performance. However, recently, a new class of Generalized Bicycle (GB) codes, constructed from binary circulant matrices with three non-zero elements per row, achieved comparable performance with fewer physical qubits and higher encoding efficiency. In this article, we focus on a subclass of GB codes, which are constructed from pairs of binary circulant matrices with two non-zero elements per row. We introduce a family of codes that generalizes both standard and optimized Kitaev codes. These codes exhibit parameters of the form [ | 2 n , 2 , ≥ n | ] [|2n,2,\geq\sqrt{n}|] where n n is a factor of 1 + d 2 1+d^{2} . For code lengths below 200, our analysis yields 21 21 codes, including 7 7 codes from Pryadko and Wang’s database, and unveils 14 14 new codes with enhanced minimum distance compared to standard Kitaev codes. Among these, 3 3 surpass all previously known weight-4 GB codes for distances 4 4 , 8 8 , and 12 12 .



I
Introduction

I-A

Motivation

Quantum computers hold the promise of revolutionizing computation by efficiently solving problems that are intractable for standard computers, as demonstrated Shor’s algorithm
[
1
]
. However, the inherent fragility of quantum information (decoherence) and the presence of environmental noise make quantum computers susceptible to errors during computation. These errors accumulate over time, potentially leading to incorrect syndrome measurements and erroneous results.

To enable long-duration quantum computation, quantum error correction is essential.
This process involves employing quantum codes, syndrome measurement circuits, and decoding algorithms to detect and correct errors as they occur. As errors can accumulate even during syndrome measurement and decoding, the error correction process must be both fast and reliable. To ensure reliable quantum computation, the physical error rate must be kept below a critical threshold, allowing for effective error suppression
[
2
]
.

I-B

Previous results:

Kitaev codes, introduced in 2003, were the first quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes with minimum distance scaling linearly with the square root of the code length
[
3
]
. Since then, various generalizations of Kitaev codes have been developed in an effort to improve their parameters. Notable examples include Kitaev’s optimized 45-degree rotated codes
[
4
]
, which offer enhanced minimum distance, and surface codes, which provide higher rates.
However, neither of these generalizations has managed to surpass the square root scaling of the minimum distance.

Until recently, all known qLDPC codes were limited by a square root scaling of the minimum distance. However, in recent years, significant progress has been made, with the theoretical existence of asymptotically good qLDPC codes, possessing positive rate and linear minimum distance scaling, being established
[
5
,
6
]
.
While these codes offer superior theoretical performance, their practical implementation remains challenging compared to surface codes, which exhibit high error thresholds and efficient decoding algorithms.

I-C

Contributions

Currently, Generalized Bicycle (GB) codes, denoted as
(a,b)
-GB when constructed from circulant matrix pairs with
a
and
b
non-zero elements per row, offer the most promising alternative to surface codes for practical implementation.

In their 2024 paper
[
7
]
, the IBM research team constructed a family of
(3,3)
-GB codes, from pairs of circulant matrices with three non-zero elements per row, achieving comparable error thresholds with significantly lower encoding overhead.

(2,2)
-GB codes, which generalize Kitaev codes and their optimized versions
[
11
]
, offer the advantages of simple structures, planar layouts, and local weight-four generators, making them highly attractive for physical implementation.

Despite, the good
(2,2)
-GB codes identified in the numerical searches for lengths
2p
where
p
is a prime for which
2
is a primitive root of unity
[
13
]
, the literature lacks
(2,2)
-GB codes with minimum distance scaling linearly with the square root of the code length, beyond Kitaev codes.

Building on Haah, Hastings, and O’Donnell’s suggestion
[
8
]
that toric code geometry alterations could yield length
N
codes with minimum distance scaling as
\sqrt{cN}
where
c>\frac{1}{2}
we introduce a new class of
(2,2)
-GB codes based on
2D
lattices with acute angles between basis vectors, achieving a minimum distance intermediate between standard and optimized Kitaev codes.

While Kitaev-optimized codes are restricted to parameters of the form
[|1+d^{2},2,d|]
with
d
odd, our proposed codes offer a more flexible parametrization of the form
[|2n,\,2,\,\geq\sqrt{n}|]
where
n
can be any divisor of
1+d^{2}
for any positive
d
.

Notably, for code lengths below 200, we identify 21 codes, encompassing 7 from Pryadko and Wang’s database
[
13
]
, and reveal 14 novel codes that outperform standard Kitaev codes in minimum distance. Among these, 3 surpass all previously known
(2,2)
-GB codes for distances 4, 8, and 12. This provides more options for designing small-scale quantum error correction codes, which is currently a critical requirement for the practical implementation of quantum computing.

II
Notations and relevant facts

II-A

Background on quantum codes

In this section, we start by reviewing CSS codes and then we introduce the notions of circulant matrices and GB codes.

II-A
1
CSS codes

Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) codes
[
9
,
10
]
form an important class of quantum error-correcting codes. These codes can be described by two binary matrices
\mathbf{H}_{X}
and
\mathbf{H}_{Z}
whose row spaces are orthogonal, i.e.,
\mathbf{H}_{X}\mathbf{H}_{Z}^{\intercal}=\mathbf{0}
. The code length
n
, representing the number of physical qubits, is the number of columns of
\mathbf{H}_{X}
(or of
\mathbf{H}_{Z}
). The code dimension
k
, corresponding to the number of logical qubits, is given by
k=n-\mathrm{rank}\,\mathbf{H}_{X}-\mathrm{rank}\,\mathbf{H}_{Z}
. The minimum distance
d
of the code is defined as the minimum of
d_{X}
and
d_{Z}
where
d_{X}
(resp.
d_{Z}
) denotes the smallest Hamming weight of a codeword of
\ker\mathbf{H}_{X}
(resp.
\ker\mathbf{H}_{Z}
) that is not in
rs(\mathbf{H}_{Z})
the row space of
\mathbf{H}_{Z}
(resp. not in
rs(\mathbf{H}_{X}
) the row space of
\mathbf{H}_{X}
).

II-A
2
Circulant matrices

Let
n>1
be an integer and let
A=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{\leq n-1}
be a polynomial. The circulant matrix associated to
A
and
n
is given by

Circ(A,\,n)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&a_{n-1}&\dots&a_{1}\\
a_{1}&a_{0}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&a_{n-1}\\
a_{n-1}&\dots&a_{1}&a_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

Since any circulant matrix is a polynomial in the cyclic permutation matrix given by the companion matrix of
x
, the product of two circulant matrices is commutative.

II-A
3
Generalized bicycle (GB) codes

The generalized bicycle (GB) codes introduced by Pryadko and Kovalev in their 2013 paper
[
11
]
extend Mackay et al.’s bicycle codes
[
12
]
.

While bicycle codes’ parity check matrices
\mathbf{H}_{X}=\mathbf{H}_{Z}=[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A}^{\intercal}]
are defined using a single circulant matrix
\mathbf{A}
, GB codes employ two circulant matrices
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
to construct their parity-check matrices.
Specifically, for binary polynomials
A,\,B\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{\leq n-1}
, the GB code
GB(A,B,n)
is defined by the parity-check matrices
\mathbf{H}_{X}=[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]
and
\mathbf{H}_{Z}=[\mathbf{B}^{\intercal},\mathbf{A}^{\intercal}]
where
\mathbf{A}=Circ(A,\,n)
and
\mathbf{B}=Circ(B,\,n)
. Due to the equivalence of
GB(A,B,n)
and
GB(B,A,n)
, the code parameters are
[|2n,\,2\deg(\gcd(A,B,x^{n}-1)),\,d_{X}=d_{Z}|]
.

In this work, we aim to maximize the minimum distance of
(2,2)
-GB codes generated by two binary polynomials
A
and
B
with exactly two non-zero terms.

As sums up the next proposition, in our study of large distance
(2,2)
-GB codes, we only need to investigate cases where
A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[X]_{\leq n-1}
have non-zero constant terms.

Proposition II.1

Let
n>0
and
A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\left[x\right]_{\leq n-1}
be two polynomials. Let
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}
be three integers.

•

If
k
is coprime with
n
then
GB(A,B,n)
and
GB(A(x^{k})\mod x^{n}-1,B(x^{k})\mod x^{n}-1,n)
are equivalent
[
13
]
. In particular, when
r\geq 1
and
n
are coprime we have:

d(GB(1+x^{r},1+x^{s},\,n))=d(GB(1+x,1+x^{\alpha},\,n))

where
\alpha=\,\,st\,\,mod\,n
with
t=(r\,\,mod\,n)^{-1}

•

GB(Ax^{i}\mod x^{n}-1,Bx^{j}\mod x^{n}-1,n)
and
GB(A,B,n)
are equivalent

Proof of the
2^{nd}
point:

Let
R_{i}
and
S_{j}
be the residue of
Ax^{i}
and
Bx^{j}
modulo
x^{n}-1
. We set

\mathbf{A}=Circ(A,n),\,\mathbf{B}=Circ(B,n)
and
\mathbf{C}=Circ(x,n)
.

The map
h
defined by

\begin{array}[]{ccccl}h&:&\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}&\to&\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}\\
&&\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}&\mapsto&\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{C}^{i}u\\
\mathbf{C}^{j}v\end{pmatrix}\end{array}

induces isometries (for the Hamming weight) between:

•

the right kernels of
\left[\mathbf{A},\,\mathbf{B}\right]
and
\left[\mathbf{C}^{i}\mathbf{A},\,\mathbf{C}^{j}\mathbf{B}\right]

•

the row spaces of
\left[\mathbf{B}^{\intercal},\,\mathbf{A}^{\intercal}\right]
and the row space of
\left[(\mathbf{C}^{j}\mathbf{B})^{\intercal},\,(\mathbf{C}^{i}\mathbf{A})^{\intercal}\right]

Since
Circ(R_{i},n)=\mathbf{C}^{i}\mathbf{A}
and
Circ(S_{j},n)=\mathbf{C}^{j}\mathbf{B}
then the two codes
GB(A,B,n)
and
GB(R_{i},S_{j},n)
have the same parameters.
∎

II-B

Background on lattices

This section presents the theoretical foundation for our lower bound on the minimum distance of
(2,2)
-GB code.

II-B
1
Lattices

A 2D-lattice is a set

\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Z}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}e_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
where
(e_{1},\,e_{2})
is a
\mathbb{R}
-basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Its determinant
det(\mathbb{L})
given by
|det(e_{1},\,e_{2})|
only depends on
\mathbb{L}
and not on the basis
(e_{1},e_{2})
. When
\mathbb{L}
is a 2D sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, its determinant is equal to the cardinality of the quotient
\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\mathbb{L}
.

To any
(2,2)
-GB generated by two polynomials
A=1+x^{u}
,
B=1+x^{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\left[x\right]_{\leq n-1}
one can associate a lattice using
u
and
v
.

II-B
2
Lattice associated to a
(2,2)
-GB code

The 2D-lattice associated to
GB(A,B,n)
is given by the Kernel of the
\mathbb{Z}-
linear application

\begin{array}[]{ccccl}\Phi&:&\mathbb{Z}^{2}&\to&\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\\
&&(x,y)&\mapsto&xu+yv\mod n\end{array}

which is equal to
\mathbb{Z}(n,0)\oplus\mathbb{Z}(-\alpha,1)
when
A=1+x
and
B=1+x^{\alpha}
.

Our main result states that for any vector
\mathbf{v}
in the kernel of
[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]
that is not a linear combination of the rows of
[\mathbf{B}^{\intercal},\mathbf{A}^{\intercal}]
, the Hamming weight of
\mathbf{v}
is bounded below by the length of the shortest non-zero vector in the kernel of
\Phi
.

Theorem II.2

For two integers
n\geq 6
and
1\leq\alpha\leq n-1
the minimum distance
d_{min}
of
GB(1+x,\,1+x^{\alpha},\,n)
verifies:

d_{min}\geq\lambda(\mathbb{L})=\min\{\,||\mathbf{u}||\,\,|\,\,\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{L}\backslash\{0\}\}

(1)

where
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Z}(n,0)\bigoplus\mathbb{Z}(-\alpha,1)
and
||\cdot||
is the Euclidean norm.

Our proof strategy for this theorem is the following :

•

Graph reinterpretation:
We construct a graph on the vertices of
\mathbb{L}
in a way that each vector
\mathbf{v}
of
\ker[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]
corresponds to a graph cycle
\tilde{v}
whose length is
\mathsf{wt}(\mathbf{v})
the Hamming weight of
\mathbf{v}
.

•

Lower bound on the cycle length:
We prove that when
\mathbf{v}
does not belong to the row-space of
[\mathbf{B}^{\intercal},\mathbf{A}^{\intercal}]
then the length of
\tilde{v}
is lower bounded by the
\lambda(\mathbb{L})
the shortest length of a non-zero vector of the lattice associated to
GB(A,B,n)
.

•

Let

For

III-C

From
\mathbb{G}
-walks to

Since

•

IV
Application of Theorem
II.2

Quantum coding theory seeks quantum codes with maximum minimum distance, as this improves fault tolerance.
As all
(2,2)
-GB codes are surface codes, their minimum distance is limited to a square root scaling with the code length. Nevertheless, by applying Theorem
II.2
, we can construct
(2,2)
-GB codes of length
2n
and minimum distance
\Omega(n^{\frac{1}{2}})
through the design of lattices
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Z}(n,0)\oplus\mathbb{Z}(-\alpha,1)
that possess a shortest non-zero vector length of
\sqrt{n}
.

To achieve this, we use the following arithmetic result:

Lemma IV.1

Let
0<\alpha
and let
n
be a factor of
1+\alpha^{2}
.
If
T
is a non-zero element of
\mathbb{L}:=\mathbb{Z}(n,0)\bigoplus\mathbb{Z}(-\alpha,1)

then
n\leq||T||^{2}
.
In particular
n^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\lambda(\mathbb{L})

Proof:
If
T\neq(0,0)
then
\exists(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
such that
T=(nu-\alpha v,v)
. As
\|T\|^{2}=n^{2}u^{2}-2n\alpha uv+(1+\alpha^{2})v^{2}
,
n
divides
\|T\|^{2}
. Thus,
n\leq\|T\|^{2}
since
\|T\|^{2}>0
.
∎

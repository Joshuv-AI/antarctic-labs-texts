On Oscillatory Integral Operators Satisfying the cinematic curvature condition
Xiangyu Wang

Abstract
. We study the oscillatory integral operators satisfying the cinematic curvature condition. First, we formulate a conjecture for this class of operators, motivated by certain necessary conditions arising from counterexamples. We then establish an estimate for these operators by combining Wolff’s two-ends reduction with refined decoupling inequalities.

Introduction

1.1.
Statement of results

Let
n\geq 3
and
B^{m}_{r}(y)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote the ball of radius
r
centered at the

.

Let

\mathbb{A}:=\{(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:1\leq\xi_{n-1}\leq 2,|\xi^{\prime}|\leq\xi_{n-1}\}.

Suppose

is non-negative and supported in

. Let
\phi:B^{n}_{1}(0)\times\mathbb{A}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
satisfy the Cinematic Curvature Condition:

\bullet

(H1) Homogeneous condition.

\phi(x;\xi)
is homogeneous of degree 1 in
\xi
and smooth away from
\xi=0

\bullet

(H2) Non-degeneracy condition.

rank
\partial^{2}_{x^{\prime}\xi}\phi(x^{\prime},x_{n};\xi)=n-1
for all
(x^{\prime},x_{n};\xi)\in{\rm supp}\ a
with
\xi\neq 0
.

\bullet

(H3) Positive definiteness condition

Consider the Gauss map
G:{\rm supp}\ a\rightarrow S^{n-1}
by
G(x;\xi):=\frac{G_{0}(x;\xi)}{|G_{0}(x;\xi)|}
, where

G_{0}(x;\xi):=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n-1}\partial_{\xi_{j}}\partial_{x}\phi(x;\xi).

Then for all
(x,\xi_{0})\in{\rm supp}\ a
,

\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\langle\partial_{z}\phi(x,\xi),G(x,\xi_{0})\rangle|_{\xi=\xi_{0}}

has rank
n-2
with
n-2
positive eigenvalues.

For any
\lambda\geq 1
let
a^{\lambda}(x;\omega):=a(x/\lambda;\omega)
,
\phi^{\lambda}(x;\omega):=\lambda\phi(x/\lambda;\omega)
and define the operator
T^{\lambda}
by

T^{\lambda}f(x):=\int e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(x;\xi)}a^{\lambda}(x;\xi)f(\xi)d\xi.

for all integrable

. In this case

is said to be an oscillatory integral operator satisfying the cinematic curvature condition (or cone condition).

The main result of this article is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
T^{\lambda}
is an oscillatory integral operator satisfying the cinematic curvature condition, then the estimate

(1.1)

\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{\phi,a}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

holds uniformly for
\lambda\geq 1
whenever

(1.2)

p>2+\frac{8}{3n-5}

Observe that the Knapp example indicates that estimate (
1.1
) may fail when
n=3
and
p\leq 4
. Consequently, the range (
1.2
) is sharp in the case
n=3
.

We denote by
B_{r}^{m}
any ball of radius
r
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. By an
\varepsilon
-removal trick, Theorem
1.1
can be reduced to

Proposition 1.2
.

Suppose
T^{\lambda}
is an oscillatory integral operator satisfying the cinematic curvature condition. Then the estimate

\|T^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(B^{n}_{R})}\lesssim_{\phi,a,\varepsilon,p}R^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

holds uniformly for
1\leq R\leq\lambda
and all

whenever

(1.3)

\displaystyle p\geq 2+\frac{8}{3n-5}

Although estimates for oscillatory integral operators satisfying the cinematic curvature condition may be regarded as a generalized extension of cone restriction estimates, the polynomial partitioning techniques that have recently proved effective in the study of the cone restriction problem (see
[
11
]
and
[
14
]
) do not seem to apply directly in this setting. The main obstacle lies in the fact that the transverse equidistribution estimate, which plays a crucial role in the polynomial partitioning framework, may not hold for oscillatory integral operators under cinematic curvature.

Our proof of Proposition
1.2
relies primarily on Wolff’s two-ends reduction and refined decoupling inequalities. This methodological framework has recently been applied in the study of local smoothing estimates by Gan-Wu
[
4
]
and in the the restriction problem by Wang–Wu
[
13
]
. Accordingly, our argument may be viewed as similar in spirit to their approach in the present setting of oscillatory integral operators satisfying the cinematic curvature condition.

In addition, we provide a counterexample showing that when the estimate (
1.1
) may fail. This example is inspired by the work of Bourgain-Guth
[
2
]
and arises from the Kakeya compression phenomenon, where tubes are arranged within a neighborhood of a low-dimensional set. A detailed discussion of this example is given in Section
2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
T^{\lambda}
is an oscillatory integral operator satisfying the cinematic curvature condition. The estimate (
1.1
) may fail when

(1.4)

\displaystyle p<2+\frac{8}{3n-1}

if
n
is odd;

\displaystyle p<2+\frac{8}{3n-2}

if
n
is even.

Estimates for oscillatory integral operators satisfying the cinematic curvature condition are closely related to several central problems in modern harmonic analysis, most notably the local smoothing problem and the cone restriction problem. These operators, by incorporating variable coefficients and geometric curvature conditions, provide a unifying analytic framework that captures many classical oscillatory phenomena while extending beyond the constant-coefficient cone scenario. Because sharp bounds in this setting would deepen our understanding of decoupling, restriction, and dispersive regularity estimates, progress on this problem has the potential to inform a broad range of techniques in Fourier analysis, partial differential equations, and geometric measure theory. Accordingly, the study of such estimates carries substantial intrinsic value, both from a conceptual standpoint and in terms of possible applications to longstanding conjectures in harmonic analysis.

3.2.
Wave packet decomposition

Fix
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. In this subsection, we recall the wave packet decomposition for a function
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
at scale
R
satisfying
1\ll R\leq\lambda^{1-\varepsilon}
, following the presentation in
[
4
, Section 3.3]
.

Condition
(H1)
guarantees that the mapping

\partial_{\xi}\phi(\cdot,x_{n};\xi)
is locally invertible. More precisely, for each
fixed pair
(x_{n},\xi)
, the map
x^{\prime}\mapsto\partial_{\xi}\phi(x;\xi)
has a
non-vanishing Jacobian determinant. After a suitable rescaling, we may further assume
that

(3.2)

\partial_{\xi}\phi(\cdot,x_{n};\xi)\quad\text{defines a diffeomorphism on}\quad B_{1}^{n-1}(0)\quad\text{for all}(x_{n},\xi)\in[-1,1]\times\mathbb{A}.

Consequently, this
mapping admits a well-defined inverse, which we introduce below.

Definition 3.3
.

[
4
, Definition 3.3]

For
(x_{n},\xi)\in[-1,1]\times\mathbb{A}
, let
\Phi(u,x_{n};\xi)
be the unique solution to

(3.3)

\partial_{\xi}\phi(\Phi(u,x_{n};\xi),x_{n};\xi)=u.

Here, we may assume the equation holds for
u
in a small neighborhood of the origin.

Since the phase function
\phi
is homogeneous of degree one in
\xi
, we have

\partial_{\xi}\phi(u;\xi)=\partial_{\xi}\phi(u;s\xi)
for any
s>0
. Consequently,

(3.4)

\Phi(u;\xi)=\Phi(u;s\xi)

Moreover, by invoking the inverse function theorem together with conditions

(\textup{D1}_{\bf A})
and
(\textup{D2}_{\bf A})
, we obtain uniform
L^{\infty}
bounds for

\Phi
and its derivatives. Differentiating (
3.3
) with respect to
u
yields

\partial^{2}_{\xi,x^{\prime}=\Phi(u,x_{n};\xi)}\phi\cdot\partial_{u}\Phi=I_{n}.

As a consequence,

\det\partial_{u}\Phi(u,x_{n};\xi)\gtrsim 1,

That is, the matrix
\partial_{u}\Phi(u,x_{n};\xi)
is quantitatively non-singular.

Define
\gamma^{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{A}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
by

\gamma^{\lambda}(u,x_{n};\xi):=\lambda\Phi(u/\lambda,x_{n}/\lambda;\xi).

Then one has

(3.5)

\partial_{\xi}\phi^{\lambda}(\gamma^{\lambda}(u,x_{n};\xi),t;\xi)=u.

For each fixed pair
(u,\xi)
, the mapping
x_{n}\mapsto(\gamma^{\lambda}(u,x_{n};\xi),x_{n})
, with
x_{n}\in[-R,R]
, traces out a curve. The wave packets considered here will be defined as certain non-isotropic neighborhoods surrounding these curves.

We proceed to describe the wave packet decomposition of
T^{\lambda}f
at scale
R
. Cover
B^{n-2}_{1}(0)
by finite-overlapping caps
\theta
of diameter
\sim R^{-1/2}
. Let
\Theta_{R^{-1/2}}:=\{\theta\}
and
\{\psi_{\theta}\}
be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to this cover. For each
\theta
let
\omega_{\theta}
denote the center of
\theta
and define
\tilde{\psi_{\theta}}
by

\tilde{\psi_{\theta}}(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n-1}):=\begin{cases}\psi_{\theta}(\xi^{\prime}/\xi_{n-1}),&\text{if }(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n-1})\in\mathbb{A},\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
f_{\theta}:=\tilde{\psi}_{\theta}f
. Now we can write

f=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{r^{-1/2}}}f_{\theta}

Let
\tilde{\theta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
be a box of dimensions
1\times R^{-1/2}\times...\times R^{-1/2}
such that

\{(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n-1}):\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi_{n-1}}\in\theta,1\leq\xi_{n-1}\leq 2\}\subset\tilde{\theta}

In addition, we denote
C\tilde{\theta}
by a

We first introduce some notations before decomposing in physical space.

.

[

at scale

Fixed points of Personalized PageRank centrality: From irreducible to reducible networks
David Aleja 1,2,3 , Julio Flores 1,2 , Eva Primo 1,2 , Daniel Rodríguez 1,2 and Miguel Romance 1,2,3

Abstract
In this paper we analyze the PageRank of a complex network as a function of its personalization vector. By using this approach, a complete characterization of the existence and uniqueness of fixed points of PageRank of a graph is given in terms of the number and nature of its strongly connected components. The method presented includes the use of a feedback-PageRank in order to compute exactly the fixed points following the classic Power’s Method in terms of the (left-hand) Perron vector of each strongly connected components. Keywords: PageRank, fixed-point iterations, irreducible matrix, Perron-Frobenius theory, strongly connected network, Power Iteration Method AMS subject classifications: 15A60, 47J26

Introduction

PageRank is one of the most relevant and successful examples of a centrality measure in complex networks that jumps from theory to real-life applications. Originally developed by Larry Page and Sergey Brin in the late 1990s to rank web pages in Google’s search engine
[
7
,
19
]
, PageRank has since demonstrated an exceptional degree of ubiquity, being applied to a wide range of real-world problems beyond its original domain. Actual applications of PageRanks include problems in Social Sciences (for example measuring the influence of users on on-line platforms, where social interactions form complex interaction patterns,
[
20
]
), the protein-protein interaction networks or metabolic pathways in Biology
[
15
]
, systemic risk in financial networks, identifying key institutions whose failure could cascade through the system in Economics
[
26
]
, brain connectivity in Neuroscience
[
25
]
, the scientific impact of academic papers
[
23
]
or public transportation modeling and analysis
[
9
]
, among many others.

Roughly speaking, PageRank in a complex network
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
is the steady-state distribution of a random walk navigating on
\mathcal{G}
such that at each step, the walker randomly chooses one of the available outgoing links from the current node, but with a given probability, he may
“teleport"
to any other node in the network instead of following a link (see, for example
[
12
,
17
,
19
]
). As we will see in more details in Section
2
, the basic ingredients for computing the PageRank in a complex network
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
are:

1.

the probability that the random walker follows a link rather than
“teleporting"
to a random node (the so called
damping factor
),

2.

a probability distribution vector on the nodes of the network that chooses a random node once the walker decides teleporting instead of using the network link’s structure (
personalization vector
).

There is a long list of excellent references that studied analytically the behavior of PageRank, as well as the influence and sensibility of the damping factor and personalization vectors in PageRank, such as
[
3
,
6
,
12
,
16
,
17
]
.

If we take a complex network
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
of
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
nodes, and we fix a damping factor
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we observe that the basic features of PageRank allows for it to be mathematically understood as a function of the personalization vector
[
8
]
, that is, if we denote the
(N-1)
-simplex

\Delta_{N}^{+}=\left\{\mathbb{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}\,:\,\kern 5.0ptx_{1}+\cdots+x_{N}=1,\,x_{i}>0,\,{\text{for all }}1\leq i\leq N\,\right\},

then the PageRank is a function
PR_{\lambda}
from
\Delta_{N}^{+}
into itself. Hence, if we take a vector
\mathbb{v}\in\Delta_{N}^{+}
, then
PR_{\lambda}(\mathbb{v})
is the PageRank of network
\mathcal{G}
with damping factor
\lambda
and personalization vector
\mathbb{v}
(further details about the nature and behavior of such function will be given in Section
2
).

This functional model approach to PageRank suggests to employ the classical language of Functional Analysis and consider functional properties of the operator
PR_{\lambda}
, such as the existence of fixed points, spectral properties and so on, as they may provide valuable information about the centrality of nodes of the underlying complex network
\mathcal{G}
. In this sense, the main goal of this paper is to analyze the existence and uniqueness, as well as the computation, of the fixed points of
PR_{\lambda}
in terms of the structure of
\mathcal{G}
. More precisely, we will prove that if
\mathcal{G}
is strongly connected, then there is always a unique fixed point of
PR_{\lambda}
(with an analytical result that computes it precisely), while if
\mathcal{G}
is not strongly connected, the existence of fixed points is proven and the uniqueness is related to the structure of the strongly connected components of the graph (sinks). Note that a fixed point of
PR_{\lambda}
can be understood as a vector
\mathbb{v}
, such that the PageRank of
\mathcal{G}
, with damping factor
\lambda
and personalization vector
\mathbb{v}
, is
\mathbb{v}
itself; in other words the teleportation vector considered gives exactly the PageRank. As we will see, the method used includes the use of a
feedback-PageRank
in order to compute the fixed point - following the classic
Power’s Method
for computing the fixed point of an operator. At this point, it is worth mentioning that some well-known results in Functional Analysis concerning fixed points (for instance, the Banach fixed point Theorem) no longer apply in this context, since the operator
PR_{\lambda}
from
\Delta_{N}^{+}
into itself is a non-contractive mapping as its spectral radius is equal to 1.

In addition to the intrinsic mathematical interest of analyzing the fixed points of
PR_{\lambda}
as an operator, the proposed methods and results are potentially useful in some applications. If we consider a temporal network (i.e. a complex network whose topological structure changes over time) and a random walker such that the time scale of the structure evolution is much slower that the time scale of the random walker itself, then it seems natural that the personalization vector could be modified along time. In this regard the equally natural question arises: how can we select a personalization vector that is related to the structural properties of the network itself? In some applications, it may be natural to teleport to more central nodes with higher probability than to the less popular ones. Let us, for example, consider a web graph, where the random walkers (web-surfers) use the PageRank of the nodes in a previous day as the personalization vector to select the destination of a teleportation. By using this model, the steady-state of this stochastic process should be the
feedback-PageRank
, if it exists, and therefore it should be the fixed point of the operator
PR_{\lambda}
, as pointed out before.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we fix the notation and some preliminary definitions related to the PageRank algorithm. In Section
3
we present some results concerning the row-stochasticity of the matrix
X(\lambda)
and we state the main result of this paper on the convergence of the iterative process of the PageRank vector
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
under the action of the matrix
X(\lambda)
, specifically for the case when the matrix
P_{A}
is row-stochastic and irreducible. In Section
4
we extend this convergence to the case that
P_{A}
is row-stochastic but no longer irreducible. To this aim, we investigate three basic cases: the diagonal case, the zero-block column case and the general reducible case. It is worth mentioning that not only we prove the convergence of this iterative process, but also explicitly obtain the form of the limit vector, which is expressed in terms of the left-hand Perron vector of the irreducible parts of the matrix
P_{A}
. Finally, in Section
5
, we apply the results obtained in the previous sections to show that the iterative process of the PageRank vector under the action of the matrix
X(\lambda)
converges for networks that are not strongly connected. In fact, the limit vector of this iterative process is related to the left-hand Perron vector of the matrices corresponding to the dangling components of the network.

2

Notation and preliminary definitions

We recall some standard notation that will be used throughout the paper. Vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
will be denoted by column matrices and we will use the superscript
T
to indicate matrix transposition. The vector of
\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
with all its components equal to 1 will be denoted by
\mathbf{e}
, that is,
\mathbf{e}=(1,\dots,1)^{T}
. A matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j}
will be called
non-negative (respectively positive ) if all its entries satisfy
a_{ij}\geq 0
(respectively
a_{ij}>0
). The same applies to vectors when being column matrices.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be a directed graph where
V=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
is the set of nodes and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. The pair
(i,j)
belongs to the set
E
if and only if there exists a link connecting node
i
to node
j
. The adjacency matrix of
\mathcal{G}
is an
N\times N
-matrix

A=(a_{ij})_{i,j}\quad\text{ where }\quad a_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if $(i,j)$ is a link of $\mathcal{G}$,}\\
0,\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

A link
(i,j)
is said to be an outlink for node
i
and an inlink for node
j
. We denote
k_{out}(i)
the outdegree of node
i
, i.e, the number of outlinks of a node
i
. Notice that
k_{out}(i)=\sum_{k}a_{ik}
. A node will be referred to as a source if it has no inlinks. Also, the graph
\mathcal{G}
may have dangling nodes, which are nodes
i\in V
with zero outdegree. Dangling nodes are characterized by a vector
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\cdots,d_{N})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
with components
d_{i}
defined by

d_{i}=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if $i$ is a dangling node of $\mathcal{G}$,}\\
0,\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Without loss of generality, the directed graph
\mathcal{G}
can be weighted with
a_{ij}\geq 0
. We remark that the results in the paper remain valid if
\mathcal{G}
is weighted.

At this point, let
P_{A}=(p_{ij})_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
be the row-stochastic matrix associated to directed graph
\mathcal{G}
defined in the following way:

1.

If
i
is a dangling node, then
p_{ij}=0
for all
j=1,\dots,N
.

2.

Otherwise,
p_{ij}=a_{ij}/k_{out}(i)=a_{ij}/\sum_{k}a_{ik}
.

Roughly speaking, each coefficient
p_{ij}
is the probability of moving from the node
i
to the node
j
. The matrix
P_{A}
will be referred to as the row-normalization of
A
. Note that if we take

D=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}k_{out}(1)&0&\cdots&0\\
0&k_{out}(2)&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\cdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&k_{out}(N)\end{array}\right),

(1)

then the matrix
P_{A}
is given by
P_{A}=D^{-1}A
.

One of the most remarkable features of the PageRank algorithm is that it contemplates the possibility to travel from one node to any other node in the graph. This teleportation probability is given by the personalization vector
\textbf{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
which, in fact, is a probability distribution vector. In addition, in the presence of dangling nodes, a probability vector
\mathbb{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
should be considered to provide an extra probability of jumping from these nodes. Finally, let
\lambda\in(0,1)
be a decision parameter, or damping factor, which determines the likelihood to move through the graph by using a path in
\mathcal{G}
or by randomly jumping instead, according to the personalization vector. For more information, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
.

Formally, let
G=G(\lambda,\mathbb{u},\mathbb{v})
be the Google matrix, with
\lambda\in(0,1)
defined as

G=\lambda(P_{A}+\mathbb{du}^{T})+(1-\lambda)\mathbb{ev}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}.

(2)

A straightforward observation is that the Google matrix
G
is row-stochastic, i.e.,
G\mathbb{e}=\mathbb{e}
where
\mathbb{e}=(1,\dots,1)^{T}
. Recall that the personalization vector
\mathbb{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
is such that all its entries are positive (i.e.
\mathbb{v}>0
) and satisfies
\mathbb{v}^{T}\mathbb{e}=1
. In a similar way, the vector
\mathbb{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
is such that
\mathbb{u}>0
and
\mathbb{u}^{T}\mathbb{e}=1
.

Notice that the Google matrix
G
is positive and thus, by the Perron-Frobenius Theorem (see
[
18
, Section 8.3]
) there is a unique vector
\pi=\pi(\lambda;\mathbb{u};\mathbb{v})>0
satisfying
\pi^{T}\textbf{e}=1
and
G^{T}\pi=\pi
, or equivalently
\pi^{T}G=\pi^{T}
. This vector is the PageRank vector
\pi
of
G
.

The existence of dangling nodes will not affect the results presented for the matrix
G
(see the end of paper
[
1
]
for more details). In this sense, for the sake of simplicity, the graph
\mathcal{G}
will be assumed to have no dangling nodes.

From the definition of the Google matrix in equation (
2
) and the properties of the PageRank vector
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
, a straightforward calculation shows that
\pi
satisfies the equation

\pi^{T}=\lambda\pi^{T}P_{A}+(1-\lambda)\mathbb{v}^{T}.

Notice that
\pi^{T}(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})=(1-\lambda)\mathbf{v}^{T}
, where
I_{N}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
is the identity square matrix of order
N
. Therefore, the PageRank vector
\pi
can be explicitly calculated as

\pi^{T}=(1-\lambda)\mathbf{v}^{T}(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}=\mathbf{v}^{T}X(\lambda),

(3)

where
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)R(\lambda)
and
R(\lambda)=(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
is the resolvent of
P_{A}
defined for all suitable damping factor
\lambda\in(0,1)
(see
[
6
]
for more information on this topic).

At this point we might ask, what happens if we compute a new PageRank vector using
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
as the personalization vector? Is there a limit for the iteration of the PageRank vector
\pi
under the action of the matrix
X(\lambda)
? Does the limit depend of the initial personalization vector
\mathbb{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
? In the next section, we present a result concerning the convergence of this iterative process of the PageRank vector
\pi
under the action of the matrix
X(\lambda)
, specifically for the case when the row-normalization matrix
P_{A}
is irreducible. Additionally, we will show that the limit vector of this iteration is, in fact, the left-hand Perron vector associated to irreducible row-normalization matrix
P_{A}
.

In what follows, and unless otherwise specified, the notation
\mathbb{0}
will denote for simplicity the zero matrix without further reference to the dimension (which may not necessarily be square).

3

Iteration for a non-negative irreducible probability matrix
P_{A}

In this section, we present a result on the convergence of the iterative process of the PageRank vector
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
under the action of the matrix
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
, whenever
P_{A}
is the row-normalization of a non-negative and irreducible square matrix
A
of order
N
. To this aim, let us recall some useful definitions and results.

A square matrix
A
of order
N\geq 2
is said to be reducible if there exists a permutation matrix
R
such
that

R^{T}AR=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}X&Y\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&Z\end{array}\right),

where
X
and
Z
are both square matrices (see
[
18
, Section 4.4]
). Otherwise,
A
is said to be irreducible. A non-negative square matrix
A
of order
N
is primitive if it is irreducible and has only one non-zero eigenvalue of maximum modulus (
[
14
, Section 8.5]
). It is worth mentioning that primitivity plays a key role for the convergence to a unique vector when applying the Power Iteration Method to the matrix
X(\lambda)
(see
[
14
, Problem 8.5.P16]
).

A directed graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
of
N
nodes is strongly connected if for any ordered pair
(i,j)\in V\times V
,
1\leq i,j\leq N
, there exists a directed path of edges in
E
leading from node
i
to node
j
(see
[
24
, Section 1]
). In fact, the following result establishes an equivalence between the irreducibility of a matrix in terms of its strong connectivity. More precisely,

Lemma 1
.

(
[
24
, Theorem 1.17]
)
An
N\times N
matrix
A
is irreducible if and only if its directed graph
\mathcal{G}
associated to
A
is strongly connected.

Notice that a non-negative matrix
A\neq 0
is irreducible if and only if for every
1\leq i,j\leq N
, there exists a positive integer
k\geq 1
such that the
(i,j)
-entry of
A^{k}
is positive (see
[
2
, Chapter 2, Theorem 2.1]
).

In the context of the Perron-Frobenius theory, if
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
is a non-negative and irreducible matrix, there exists a unique vector
\mathbb{q}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
, called the left-hand Perron vector of the matrix
A
, such that

\mathbb{q}^{T}A=r\mathbb{q}^{T}\,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\,\mathbb{q}>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{q}^{T}\mathbb{e}=1,

where
r=\rho(A)
is the spectral radius of the matrix
A
(see
[
18
, Section 8.3]
).

For
p\geq 1
, the
p
-norm of a vector
\mathbb{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
is defined as
\|\mathbb{x}\|_{p}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|x_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}
. Observe that condition
\mathbb{x}^{T}\mathbb{e}=1
for a non-negative vector
\mathbb{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
can be expressed in terms of the
1
-norm as
\|\mathbb{x}\|_{1}=1
. Throughout the paper we mainly use the
p
-norm with
p=1
.

The following result asserts that the positivity, irreducibility and row-stochasticity properties of the matrix
P_{A}
(the row-normalization of the adjacency matrix
A
) are preserved for the matrix
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
. More precisely,

Lemma 2
.

Let
A
be a non-negative irreducible square matrix of order
N
and let
P_{A}
be its row-normalization described in Section
2
. For a fixed
\lambda\in(0,1)
, the matrix
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
is positive, irreducible and row-stochastic, i.e.
X(\lambda)\mathbb{e}=\mathbb{e}
.

Proof.

Firstly, since
P_{A}\geq 0
is irreducible and
\rho(P_{A})=1
, the matrix

X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
exists and is non-negative for all
\lambda\in(0,1)
(see
[
11
, Theorem 3, Section 3, Chapter XIII]
). In fact, the matrix
X(\lambda)
which is given by the Neumann series

\displaystyle X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}=(1-\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\lambda P_{A}\right)^{i}=(1-\lambda)\left[I_{N}+(\lambda P_{A})+(\lambda P_{A})^{2}+\dots\right]

is clearly positive by Lemma
1
applied to the irreducible matrix
P_{A}
.
If we denote by

B=I_{N}+(\lambda P_{A})^{2}+(\lambda P_{A})^{3}+\dots=I_{N}+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\left(\lambda P_{A}\right)^{i},

we get
R(\lambda)=(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}=\lambda P_{A}+B
. This implies that
R(\lambda)
is irreducible since
\lambda P_{A}
is so and
B
is non-negative with the same order (see
[
2
,
21
,
22
]
for details). Therefore, for a fixed
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we conclude that the matrix
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)R(\lambda)
is a positive and irreducible square matrix of order
N
.

Now, since
P_{A}
is row-stochastic we have
P_{A}\mathbb{e}=\mathbb{e}
, which implies that
(P_{A})^{i}\mathbb{e}=\mathbb{e}
, for all
i\geq 1
. Therefore, since
\lambda\in(0,1)
we have

X(\lambda)\mathbb{e}=(1-\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\lambda P_{A}\right)^{i}\mathbb{e}=(1-\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{i}\,\left(P_{A}^{i}\mathbb{e}\right)=(1-\lambda)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{i}\mathbb{e}=(1-\lambda)\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\mathbb{e}=\mathbb{e},

and the proof is completed.

∎

Remark 1
.

Notice that the previous result implies that
X(\lambda)
is a linear
\|\cdot\|_{1}
-isometry in the positive cone, that is: if
\mathbb{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
with
\mathbb{x}\geq 0
and
\|\mathbb{x}\|_{1}=1
, then
\|\mathbb{x}^{T}X(\lambda)\|_{1}=1
. This is visualized as the matrix
X(\lambda)
mapping vectors from the unit circle lying in the positive cone into the unit circle, and has a nice consequence when the Power Iteration Method is applied to the positive matrix
X(\lambda)
(see
[
14
, Problem 8.5.P16 and Theorem 8.2.8]
), in as far as the
\|\cdot\|_{1}
-normalization step in this method is no longer needed.

At this point, we are in position to state the main result of this paper. It shows that the iteration of the PageRank vector
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
under the action of the matrix
X(\lambda)
converges to the left-hand Perron vector of the row-normalization matrix
P_{A}
whenever
A
is a non-negative and irreducible square matrix. Remarkably, the convergence to the left-hand Perron vector of
P_{A}
occurs regardless of the personalization vector
\mathbb{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
considered. More precisely,

Theorem 1
.

Let
A
be a non-negative irreducible square matrix of order
N
and let
P_{A}
be its row-normalization described in Section
2
. For a fixed
\lambda\in(0,1)
, let consider
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
. Then, for any personalization vector
\mathbb{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
with
\|\mathbb{v}\|_{1}=1
, the recursive sequence
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
, with
\mathbb{x}_{0}=\mathbb{v}
, defined as

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{k-1}^{T}X(\lambda)=\mathbb{v}^{T}X(\lambda)^{k}\,,\quad k\geq 1,

converges to the left-hand Perron vector
\mathbb{c}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
of the matrix
P_{A}
.

Proof.

From the assumption trivially follows that the row-normalization matrix
P_{A}
is also irreducible. On the other hand, from the definition of the entries
p_{ij}
in the non-negative matrix
P_{A}
, the inequality
\min_{i}\sum_{j}p_{ij}\leq\rho(P_{A})\leq\max_{i}\sum_{j}p_{ij}
implies that
\rho(P_{A})=1
(see
[
14
, Theorem 8.1.22]
). Now, by the Perron-Frobenius Theorem (see
[
18
, Section 8.3]
) applied to the irreducible and non-negative matrix
P_{A}
, there exists a unique positive vector
\mathbb{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
with
\|\mathbb{c}\|_{1}=1
(the left-hand Perron vector) associated to the eigenvalue
\rho(P_{A})=1
, such that
\mathbb{c}^{T}P_{A}=\mathbb{c}^{T}
.

Now, by Lemma
2
and for a fixed
\lambda\in(0,1)
, the matrix
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
is positive, irreducible and row-stochastic. Moreover, by the Perron-Frobenius Theorem applied to the matrix
X(\lambda)
, there exists a unique positive vector
\mathbb{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
with
\|\mathbb{u}\|_{1}=1
such that
\mathbb{u}^{T}X(\lambda)=r\mathbb{u}^{T}
where
r
is the spectral radius of
X(\lambda)
. It is worth mentioning that
r=1
also follows from the observation that the eigenvectors of
X(\lambda)
are the same as the eigenvectors of
P_{A}
, and the corresponding eigenvalues are
\{(1-\lambda)(1-\lambda\beta_{i})^{-1}\}
, where
\{\beta_{i}\}
are the eigenvalues of
P_{A}
. A straightforward computation shows that
|(1-\lambda)(1-\lambda\beta_{i})^{-1}|\leq 1
whenever
|\beta_{i}|\leq 1
and
(1-\lambda)(1-\lambda\beta_{i})^{-1}=1
if and only if
\beta_{i}=1
. In fact, the uniqueness of the left-hand Perron vector for the positive matrix
X(\lambda)
implies that
\mathbb{u}=\mathbb{c}
.

Finally, for a given vector
\mathbb{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
, let us consider a recursive sequence of vectors
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
defined by

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{k-1}^{T}X(\lambda),\quad(k\geq 1)\,,\,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\|\mathbb{x_{0}}\|_{1}=1.

(4)

A straightforward computation shows that
\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{v}^{T}X(\lambda)^{k}
,
k\geq 1
, with
\mathbb{x}_{0}=\mathbb{v}>0
. Since
X(\lambda)
is a positive matrix, by the Power Iteration Method (see
[
14
, Problem 8.5.P16 and Theorem 8.2.8]
) and Remark
1
applied to the matrix
X(\lambda)
we conclude that the sequence
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
in equation (
4
) converges to a non-zero vector
\mathbb{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
with
\|\mathbb{x}\|_{1}=1
such that

\mathbb{x}^{T}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{v}^{T}X^{k}(\lambda)=\mathbb{c}^{T},

where
\mathbb{c}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
is the left-hand Perron vector of the matrix
P_{A}
associated to the eigenvalue
\rho(P_{A})=1
.

In the next section, we will investigate the convergence of the PageRank vector
\pi
under the iteration of the matrix
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
when the row-normalization matrix

.

Note that it is straightforward to check that Theorem
1
solves the existence and uniqueness of fixed points of the PageRank as a function
PR_{\lambda}:\Delta_{N}^{+}\longrightarrow\Delta_{N}^{+}
for every damping factor
\lambda\in(0,1)
if the network considered is strongly connected. Therefore, it can be rewritten as follows,

Corollary 1
.

If
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be a strongly connected directed graph, then for every damping factor
\lambda\in(0,1)
, there is a unique fixed point of the PageRank as a function
PR_{\lambda}:\Delta_{N}^{+}\longrightarrow\Delta_{N}^{+}
. Furthermore, such fixed point is the left-hand Perron vector of the matrix
P_{A}
.

Since Corollary
1
states that the only fixed point of the PageRank is the left-hand Perron vector of the matrix
P_{A}
in the case of strongly connected directed graphs, the computation of such fixed point can be computationally simplified in some cases, as the following result shows.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be a graph where
V=\{1,\dots,N\}
is the set of nodes.

(i)

If
\mathcal{G}
is an undirected and connected graph, then the left-hand Perron vector of the matrix
P_{A}
is

\mathbb{c}=\frac{1}{2|E|}\left(k(1),\cdots,k(N)\right)^{T},

where
k(i)
denotes the degree of node
i
, for every
1\leq i\leq N
.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{G}
is a directed and strongly connected graph, then then the left-hand Perron vector of the matrix
P_{A}
is

\mathbb{c}=\frac{1}{|E|}\left(k_{out}(1),\cdots,k_{out}(N)\right)^{T}

if and only if
\mathcal{G}
admits some Eulerian path (i.e. there is a closed path in
\mathcal{G}
that visits every edge exactly once).

Proof.

Before completing the proof, the following fact must be checked:

Fact 1.

If
A,\,D
are two square matrices and
D
is diagonal, then
\mathbb{w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
is a left eigenvector of
D^{-1}A
associated to the eigenvalue
\mu
if and only if
D^{-1}\mathbb{w}
is a left eigenvector of
AD^{-1}
associated to the eigenvalue
\mu
, where
D
is any diagonal matrix.

In order to prove this fact notice, on the one hand, that if
\mathbb{w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
is a left eigenvector of
D^{-1}A
associated to the eigenvalue
\mu
, then

(D^{-1}\mathbb{w})^{T}AD^{-1}=\mathbb{w}^{T}(D^{-1})^{T}AD^{-1}=\left(\mathbb{w}^{T}D^{-1}A\right)D^{-1}=\mu\mathbb{w}^{T}D^{-1}=\mu\left(D^{-1}\mathbb{w}\right)^{T},

since
D
is a diagonal matrix. Hence,
D^{-1}\mathbb{w}
is a left eigenvector of
AD^{-1}
associated to the eigenvalue
\mu
.

On the other hand, if
D^{-1}\mathbb{w}
is a left eigenvector of
AD^{-1}
associated to the eigenvalue
\mu
, then

\mathbb{w}^{T}\left(D^{-1}A\right)=\mathbb{w}^{T}D^{-1}AD^{-1}D=\left(D^{-1}\mathbb{w}\right)^{T}AD^{-1}D=\mu\left(D^{-1}\mathbb{w}\right)^{T}D=\mu\mathbb{w}^{T}\left(D^{-1}\right)^{T}D=\mu\mathbb{w}^{T},

so
\mathbb{w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
is a left eigenvector of
D^{-1}A
associated to the eigenvalue
\mu
.

Once
Fact 1
has been proved, we can start proving
(i)
. Since
P_{A}=D^{-1}A
is row-stochastic matrix and
\mathcal{G}
is connected, it follows that
\rho(P_{A})=\rho(P_{A}^{T})=1
and the corresponding (left) eigenspaces associated to eigenvalue
\mu=1
are 1-dimensional. In addition to this, since
P_{A}^{T}=(D^{-1}A)^{T}=AD^{-1}
is column-stochastic, then
\mathbb{e}=(1,\cdots,1)^{T}
is a left eigenvector of
P_{A}^{T}
associated to eigenvalue
\mu=1
. Hence, by
Fact 1
,
D\mathbb{e}
is a left eigenvector of
P_{A}
associated to eigenvalue
\mu=1
and therefore

\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sum_{i}k(i)}D\mathbb{e}=\frac{1}{2|E|}\left(k(1),\cdots,k(N)\right)^{T}

is the left-hand Perron vector of
P_{A}
.

In order to proof
(ii)
, note that since
P_{A}=D^{-1}A
, then

AD^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{a_{11}}{k_{out}(1)}&\cdots&\frac{a_{1N}}{k_{out}(N)}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\frac{a_{N1}}{k_{out}(1)}&\cdots&\frac{a_{NN}}{k_{out}(N)}\end{array}\right).

Hence the sum of each column of
AD^{-1}
is
k_{in}(1)/k_{out}(1),\,\cdots,\,k_{in}(N)/k_{out}(N)
respectively, where
k_{in}(j)=\sum_{i}a_{ij}
for every
1\leq j\leq N
. Therefore, by using
Fact 1
,

\mathbb{c}=\frac{1}{|E|}\left(k_{out}(1),\cdots,k_{out}(N)\right)^{T}

is the normalized left-hand Perron vector of
D^{-1}A
if and only if
\mathbb{e}
is a left eigenvector of
AD^{-1}
associated to eigenvalue
\mu=1
, but

\mathbb{e}^{T}AD^{-1}=\left(\frac{k_{in}(1)}{k_{out}(1)},\,\cdots,\,\frac{k_{in}(N)}{k_{out}(N)}\right),

which makes that
\mathbb{e}
is the normalized left-hand Perron vector of
AD^{-1}
if and only
k_{in}(j)=k_{out}(j)
for every
1\leq j\leq N
, but this is equivalent to the fact that
\mathcal{G}
admits some Eulerian paths (see, for example
[
10
]
).
∎

4

Iteration for a non-negative reducible probability matrix
P_{A}

In this section, we investigate the convergence of the Power Iteration Method applied to the matrix
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
for the case when the row-normalization matrix
P_{A}
is no longer irreducible. To this aim, we will assume (see
[
24
, Section 2.3]
) that for the reducible square adjacency matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j}
of order
N
defined in Section
2
there exists a permutation matrix
S
such that the matrix
SAS^{T}
has an upper-triangular form

SAS^{T}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A_{1}&B\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&A_{2}\end{array}\right),

where
A_{1}
is either an irreducible or identically zero square matrix of order
n_{1}
and
A_{2}
is an irreducible square matrix of order
n_{2}
, with
n_{1}+n_{2}=N
, and
B
is a
n_{1}\times n_{2}
matrix. Notice that the absence of dangling nodes (see Section
2
) means that
A_{2}
cannot be the zero matrix. At this point, we distinguish three different situations for the matrix
SAS^{T}
:

Diagonal case:

Where
B=\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
, but
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are non-zero square matrices (Theorem
3
).

Zero-block column case:

Where
A_{1}=\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times n_{1}}
, but
B
and
A_{2}
are non-zero matrices (Theorem
4
).

General reducible case:

Where
A_{1},B
, and
A_{2}
are non-zero matrices (Theorem
5
).

For all three cases we will prove not only the convergence of the Power Iteration Method applied to the matrix
X(\lambda)
, but also the form of the vector to which the recursive sequence
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
defined as

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{k-1}^{T}X(\lambda)=\mathbb{v}^{T}X^{k}(\lambda)\,,\quad(k\geq 1),

(6)

converges. Notice that the row-stochasticity of the matrix
P_{A}
together with the damping factor being strictly smaller than 1, imply the existence of the non-negative matrix
X(\lambda)=(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
, but it can be no longer assumed irreducible.

For instance, in the
Diagonal case
, we will show that the sequence in equation (
6
) converges to a non-zero vector of the form
(\kern 5.0pt\alpha_{1}\mathbb{c}_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad\alpha_{2}\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
, for some
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\geq 0
and
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=1
, where
\mathbb{c}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}\times 1}
and
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
are the left-hand Perron vectors for the row-normalization matrices
P_{A_{1}}
and
P_{A_{2}}
, respectively. Roughly speaking, in the diagonal case where clusters
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are disjoint, the
1
-norm of the PageRank vector
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
is distributed (perhaps not equitably) between vectors
\mathbb{c}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}\times 1}
and
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
.

On the other hand, for the
Zero-block column case
and the
General reducible case
where nodes in cluster
A_{1}
are connected to nodes in cluster
A_{2}
(but not conversely), we will show that the sequence in equation (
6
) converges to non-zero vector of the form
(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times 1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
, where
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
is the left-hand Perron vector of matrix
P_{A_{2}}
. This fact can be understood as follows: The
1
-norm of the PageRank vector
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
is divided between clusters
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. However, over time, the portion of this
1
-norm in cluster
A_{1}
is transferred to cluster
A_{2}
through the connections represented by matrix
B
. Ultimately, all the
1
-norm of vector
\pi
asymptotically ends up in cluster
A_{2}
and finally, by Theorem
1
applied to matrix
P_{A_{2}}
, the sequence
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
defined in equation (
6
) converges to the vector
(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times 1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
. In this sense, the cluster
A_{2}
is what we call a Dangling Cluster, that is, a set of nodes that has no outgoing links to other clusters within the network.

Now, once we have an idea of how the iteration of the matrix
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
behaves when the adjacency matrix
A
is reducible (and so is its row-normalization
P_{A}
), we are in a position to state and prove the results concerning the different cases mentioned above, starting with the
Diagonal case
. In what follows, for simplicity of notation, we will refer to the reduced matrix
SAS^{T}
simply as
A
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A
be a non-negative reducible square matrix of order
N
of the form

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A_{1}&\mathbb{0}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&A_{2}\end{array}\right),

where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are irreducible square matrices of order
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, respectively, with
n_{1}+n_{2}=N
. Let
P_{A_{1}}
and
P_{A_{2}}
be the row-normalization of matrices
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, respectively. For a fixed
\lambda\in(0,1)
consider
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
. Then for any personalization vector
\mathbb{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
with
\|\mathbb{v}\|_{1}=1
, the recursive sequence
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
, with
\mathbb{x}_{0}=\mathbb{v}
, defined as

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{k-1}^{T}X(\lambda)=\mathbb{v}^{T}X(\lambda)^{k}\,,\quad(k\geq 1),

converges to the vector
\mathbb{x}^{T}=(\kern 5.0pt\alpha_{1}\mathbb{c}_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad\alpha_{2}\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
, for some
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\geq 0
with
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=1
, where
\mathbb{c}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}\times 1}
and
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
are the left-hand Perron vector for matrices
P_{A_{1}}
and
P_{A_{2}}
, respectively.

Proof.

Let
P_{A}
be the row-stochastic reducible matrix obtained from
A
by row-normalization.

P_{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}P_{A_{1}}&\mathbb{0}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&P_{A_{2}}\end{array}\right).

As it was mentioned in Section
2
, the Google matrix
G
without dangling nodes given by

G=\lambda P_{A}+(1-\lambda)\mathbb{ev}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

is a row-stochastic square matrix for which there exists a unique positive vector
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
(called PageRank vector),
\pi^{T}\mathbb{e}=1
, satisfying the equation

\pi^{T}=(1-\lambda)\mathbf{v}^{T}(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}=\mathbf{v}^{T}X(\lambda),

(7)

where
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
. A straightforward computation shows that the inverse of the reducible square matrix
I_{N}-\lambda P_{A}
is the reducible matrix given by

\displaystyle(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda P_{A_{1}})^{-1}&\mathbb{0}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\end{array}\right).

Therefore, in this diagonal case, equation (
7
) can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\pi^{T}=\mathbb{v}^{T}X(\lambda)=\mathbb{v}^{T}(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{v}^{T}(1-\lambda)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda P_{A_{1}})^{-1}&\mathbb{0}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{v}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda P_{A_{1}})^{-1}&\mathbb{0}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{v}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}X_{1}(\lambda)&\mathbb{0}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&X_{2}(\lambda)\end{array}\right),

where
X_{1}(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda P_{A_{1}})^{-1}
and
X_{2}(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}
. Now, let us consider a recursive sequence of vectors
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}\}_{k\geq 0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
defined by

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{k-1}^{T}X(\lambda)=\mathbb{v}^{T}X(\lambda)^{k}\,,\quad(k\geq 1)\,,\,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\,\mathbb{x}_{0}=\mathbb{v}>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\mathbb{v}\|_{1}=1.

(8)

Let
\mathbb{v}^{T}=(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
be a decomposition of the personalization vector
\mathbb{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
where
\mathbb{v}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}\times 1}
and
\mathbb{v}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
. Clearly

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{v}^{T}X^{k}(\lambda)=(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}X_{1}^{k}(\lambda)&\mathbb{0}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&X_{2}^{k}(\lambda)\end{array}\right)=\left(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}X_{1}^{k}(\lambda)\quad\Big|\quad\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}X_{2}^{k}(\lambda)\kern 5.0pt\right).

Now, since
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are non-negative irreducible square matrices, by Theorem
1
in Section
3
we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}X_{1}^{k}(\lambda)=\|\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}\|_{1}\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}}{\|\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}\|_{1}}X_{1}^{k}(\lambda)=\|\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}\|_{1}\mathbb{c}_{1}^{T}\qquad,\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}X_{2}^{k}(\lambda)=\|\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}\|_{1}\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}}{\|\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}\|_{1}}X_{2}^{k}(\lambda)=\|\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}\|_{1}\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T},

(9)

where
\mathbb{c}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}\times 1}
and
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
are the left-hand Perron vectors for
P_{A_{1}}
and
P_{A_{2}}
, respectively. Finally, the sequence
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
defined in equation (
8
) converges to a non-zero vector
\mathbb{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
with
\|\mathbb{x}\|_{1}=1
such that

\mathbb{x}^{T}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{v}^{T}X^{k}(\lambda)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}X_{1}^{k}(\lambda)\quad\Big|\quad\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}X_{2}^{k}(\lambda)\kern 5.0pt\right)=(\kern 5.0pt\|\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}\|_{1}\mathbb{c}_{1}^{T}\quad\Big|\quad\|\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}\|_{1}\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt).

In fact, since
\|\mathbb{c}_{1}\|_{1}=1
,
\|\mathbb{c}_{2}\|_{1}=1
and
\|\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}\|_{1}+\|\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}\|_{1}=\|\mathbb{v}^{T}\|_{1}=1
, we conclude the vector
\mathbb{x}^{T}
is a linear convex combination of the vectors
\left(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{c}_{1}^{T}\quad\Big|\quad\mathbb{0}_{n_{2}\times 1}^{T}\kern 5.0pt\right)
and
\left(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times 1}^{T}\quad\Big|\quad\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt\right)
.

As with Theorem
1
we can reinterpret Theorem
3
to prove the existence of fixed points for the PageRank as a function
PR_{\lambda}:\Delta_{N}^{+}\longrightarrow\Delta_{N}^{+}
for every damping factor
\lambda\in(0,1)
, when the network considered has two completely disconnected strongly connected components. More precisely,

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be a directed graph with two completely disconnected strongly connected components
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
, then for every damping factor
\lambda\in(0,1)
, there are infinite many fixed points of the PageRank as a function
PR_{\lambda}:\Delta_{N}^{+}\longrightarrow\Delta_{N}^{+}
. Furthermore, the set of such fixed points is the convex hull in
\Delta_{N}^{+}
of the left-hand Perron vectors of the matrices
P_{A_{1}}
,
P_{A_{2}}
given by
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
.

In Figure
3
we present a numerical simulation of Theorem

In the sequel, we will make use of the following remark concerning the matrix norm.

Remark 2
.

Given a square matrix
Q
of order
N
, the matrix norm
\|Q\|_{\infty}=\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\sum_{j}|a_{ij}|
coincides with the operator norm
\|Q\|=\max_{\|x\|_{1}=1}\|Qx\|_{1}
associated to
\|\cdot\|_{1}
(see
[
14
]
, Definition 5.6.1 and Example 5.6.5). Also if we define the operator
\psi(x)\doteqdot x^{T}Q
, then the inequality
\|\psi x\|_{1}\leq\|\psi\|\ \|x\|_{1}
becomes
\|x^{T}Q\|_{1}\leq\|x^{T}\|_{1}\|Q\|_{\infty}=\|x^{T}\|_{1}\|Q\|
. In particular, if
Q
is row-stochastic this means that
\|x^{T}Q\|_{1}\leq\|x^{T}\|_{1}
.

Now, we proceed with the
Zero-block column case
. In contrast to Theorem
3
, in this case, the iteration of the personalization vector converges to a vector whose first
n_{1}
-entries are equal to zero, while the remaining
n_{2}
-entries are the left-hand Perron vector
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
of the row-normalization
P_{A_{2}}
, which corresponds to the dangling cluster part of the network. More precisely,

Theorem 4
.

Let
A
be a non-negative reducible square matrix of order
N
of the form

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathbb{0}&B\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&A_{2}\end{array}\right),

where
A_{2}
is a non-negative and irreducible square matrix of order
n_{2}
and
B
is a non-negative matrix of size
n_{1}\times n_{2}
, with
n_{1}+n_{2}=N
. Let
P_{B}
and
P_{A_{2}}
be the row-normalization of matrices
B
and
A_{2}
, respectively, defined in Section
2
. Then for any personalization vector
\mathbb{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
with
\|\mathbb{v}\|_{1}=1
, the recursive sequence
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
, with
\mathbb{x}_{0}=\mathbb{v}
, defined as

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{k-1}^{T}X(\lambda)=\mathbb{v}^{T}X(\lambda)^{k}\,,\quad(k\geq 1),

converges to the vector
\mathbb{x}^{T}=(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times 1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
, where
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
is the left-hand Perron vector of the matrix
P_{A_{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
P_{A}
be the row-stochastic reducible matrix obtained from
A
by row-normalization.

P_{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathbb{0}&P_{B}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&P_{A_{2}}\end{array}\right).

Notice that the
{1,2}
-block of
P_{A}
coincides with
P_{B}
, the row-normalization of
B
, while the
{2,2}
-block of
P_{A}
coincides with
P_{A_{2}}
, the row-normalization of
A_{2}
, and thus both
P_{B}
and
P_{A_{2}}
clearly are row-stochastic. As it was mentioned in Section
2
, the Google matrix
G
without dangling nodes defined as

G=\lambda P_{A}+(1-\lambda)\mathbb{ev}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

is a row-stochastic and positive square matrix for which there exists a unique positive vector
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
(called PageRank vector), with
\pi^{T}\mathbb{e}=1
, satisfying the equation

\pi^{T}=(1-\lambda)\mathbf{v}^{T}(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}=\mathbf{v}^{T}X(\lambda),

(10)

where
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
. A straightforward computation shows that the inverse of the reducible matrix
I_{N}-\lambda P_{A}
is the reducible matrix

(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I_{n_{1}}&\lambda P_{B}(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\end{array}\right).\\

Therefore, in this zero-block column case, equation (
10
) can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\pi^{T}=\mathbb{v}^{T}X(\lambda)=\mathbb{v}^{T}(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{v}^{T}(1-\lambda)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I_{n_{1}}&\lambda P_{B}(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{v}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(1-\lambda)I_{n_{1}}&\lambda(1-\lambda)P_{B}(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{v}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(1-\lambda)I_{n_{1}}&\lambda P_{B}X_{2}(\lambda)\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&X_{2}(\lambda)\end{array}\right),

where
X_{2}(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}
. Now, let us consider a recursive sequence of vectors
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}\}_{k\geq 0}
defined by

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{k-1}^{T}X(\lambda)=\mathbb{x}_{0}^{T}X(\lambda)^{k}\,,\quad(k\geq 1)\,,\,\quad\text{with}\quad\,\mathbb{x}_{0}=\mathbb{v}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathbb{v}\|_{1}=1.

(11)

Let
\mathbb{v}^{T}=(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
be a decomposition of vector
\mathbb{v}
where
\mathbb{v}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}\times 1}
and
\mathbb{v}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
.
Since

X^{k}(\lambda)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(1-\lambda)I_{n_{1}}&\lambda P_{B}X_{2}(\lambda)\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&X_{2}(\lambda)\end{array}\right)^{k}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(1-\lambda)^{k}I_{n_{1}}&\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1-\lambda)^{k-i}P_{B}X_{2}^{i}(\lambda)\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&X_{2}(\lambda)^{k}\end{array}\right),\quad(k\geq 1).

we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{v}^{T}X^{k}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(1-\lambda)^{k}I_{n_{1}}&\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1-\lambda)^{k-i}P_{B}X_{2}^{i}(\lambda)\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&X_{2}(\lambda)^{k}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\kern 5.0pt(1-\lambda)^{k}\mathbb{v}^{T}_{1}\quad\Big|\quad\lambda\mathbb{v}^{T}_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1-\lambda)^{k-i}P_{B}X_{2}^{i}(\lambda)+\mathbb{v}^{T}_{2}X^{k}_{2}(\lambda)\kern 5.0pt\right),\quad(k\geq 1).

That is, the first
n_{1}
entries of the decomposition
\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=(\kern 5.0pt(\mathbb{x}_{k})^{T}_{1}\quad|\quad(\mathbb{x}_{k})^{T}_{2}\kern 5.0pt)
are obtained from the expression
(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{1}^{T}=(1-\lambda)^{k}\mathbb{v}^{T}_{1}
, while the remaining
n_{2}=N-n_{1}
entries satisfy
(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{2}^{T}=\lambda\mathbb{v}^{T}_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1-\lambda)^{k-i}P_{B}X_{2}^{i}(\lambda)+\mathbb{v}^{T}_{2}X^{k}_{2}(\lambda)
.

Alternatively, the vector sequence in (
11
) can be recursively expressed as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{x}_{k+1}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}X(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\kern 5.0pt(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(1-\lambda)I_{n_{1}}&\lambda P_{B}X_{2}(\lambda)\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&X_{2}(\lambda)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\kern 5.0pt(1-\lambda)(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad\lambda(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{1}^{T}P_{B}X_{2}(\lambda)+(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{2}^{T}X_{2}(\lambda)\kern 5.0pt\right).

(14)

Observe that, since
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}(1-\lambda)^{k}\mathbb{v}^{T}_{1}=\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times 1}^{T}
. Thus, for a given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive integer
K\geq 1
such that for every
k\geq K
the condition

\left\|(\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T})_{1}\right\|_{1}=\left\|(1-\lambda)^{k}\mathbb{v}^{T}_{1}\right\|_{1}<\varepsilon

(15)

holds. Then, from equation (
4
), for every
k\geq K
we can write the vector
\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}
as follows

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{K}^{T}X^{k-K}(\lambda)=\left(\kern 5.0pt(1-\lambda)^{k-K}(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{1}^{T}\quad\Big|\quad\lambda(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{1}^{T}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k-K}(1-\lambda)^{k-K-i}P_{B}X_{2}^{i}(\lambda)+(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}^{T}X_{2}^{k-K}(\lambda)\kern 5.0pt\right).

(16)

That is, for
k\geq K
, the vector
\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}
can be written in terms of
\mathbb{x}_{K}^{T}
. Now, we compute a bound for the
n_{2}
components of the vector
\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}
, that is, the first half of the right-hand part of the vector in equation (
16
). Since
P_{B}
and
X_{2}
are row-stochastic matrices and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we can use Remark
2
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda(\mathbb{x}_{K})^{T}_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k-K}(1-\lambda)^{k-K-i}P_{B}X_{2}^{i}(\lambda)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})^{T}_{1}\right\|_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k-K}(1-\lambda)^{k-K-i}\|P_{B}\|\,\,\|X_{2}^{i}(\lambda)\|=\lambda\left\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})^{T}_{1}\right\|_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k-K}(1-\lambda)^{k-K-i}

\displaystyle=\lambda\left\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})^{T}_{1}\right\|_{1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-K-1}(1-\lambda)^{j}\leq\lambda\left\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})^{T}_{1}\right\|_{1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-\lambda)^{j}=\lambda\left\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})^{T}_{1}\right\|_{1}\frac{1}{1-(1-\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\lambda\left\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})^{T}_{1}\right\|_{1}\frac{1}{\lambda}=\left\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})^{T}_{1}\right\|_{1}<\varepsilon.

(17)

By Remark
1
, the sequence of vectors
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}\}_{k\geq 0}
defined in equation (
11
) satisfies
\|\mathbb{x}_{k}\|_{1}=1
, for all
k\geq 0
.
In addition, from equation (
15
) follows
1-\varepsilon<\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}\|_{1}\leq 1
, since
X(\lambda)
is row-stochastic. Now, for a fixed
\lambda\in(0,1)
we apply Theorem
1
to the (non-negative irreducible) matrix
A_{2}
and the index
K
above

\lim_{k\to\infty}(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}^{T}X_{2}^{k}(\lambda)=\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}^{T}\|_{1}\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}^{T}}{\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}^{T}\|_{1}}X_{2}^{k}(\lambda)=\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}^{T}\|_{1}\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T},

where
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
is the left-hand Perron vector for
P_{A_{2}}
. Therefore, for a given
\varepsilon>0
and for the fixed vector
(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
with index
K
is provided by the condition in equation (
15
), there is a positive integer
L>1
with
L>K
such that for every
k>L
we have

\left\|\kern 5.0pt(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}^{T}X_{2}^{k-K}(\lambda)-\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}^{T}\|_{1}\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt\right\|_{1}<\varepsilon\qquad\text{with}\qquad 1-\varepsilon<\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}\|_{1}\leq 1.

(18)

In summary, combining equations (
15
), (
4
) and (
18
) with the fact that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\|(\mathbb{x}_{K})_{2}\|_{1}=1
, we have proved that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive integer
k
large enough such that the estimates

\left\|(\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T})_{1}\right\|_{1}<\varepsilon\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\|\kern 5.0pt(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{2}^{T}-\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt\right\|_{1}<\varepsilon\quad

(19)

hold for the vector decomposition
\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=(\kern 5.0pt(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
. Therefore, the sequence
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
in equation (
11
) converges to a non-zero vector
\mathbb{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
with
\|\mathbb{x}\|_{1}=1
such that

\mathbb{x}^{T}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{v}^{T}X^{k}(\lambda)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\kern 5.0pt(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad(\mathbb{x}_{k})_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt\right)=(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times 1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt),

where
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
is the left-hand Perron vector associated with the non-negative and irreducible square matrix
P_{A_{2}}
.

∎

In this case, Theorem
3
shows that if we consider a non-strongly connected directed graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
with a strongly connected component and some source nodes, then, for any damping factor
\lambda\in(0,1)
, PageRank has no fixed points as a function
PR_{\lambda}:\,~\Delta_{N}^{+}\,~\to\,~\Delta_{N}^{+}
, but it has a unique fixed point if we consider it as a function
PR_{\lambda}:\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\Delta_{N}
where

\Delta_{N}=\left\{\mathbb{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}\,:\,\kern 5.0ptx_{1}+\cdots+x_{N}=1,\,x_{i}\geq 0,\,{\text{for all }}1\leq i\leq N\,\right\},

as the following result shows.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
be a non-strongly connected directed graph such that it has only one strongly connected component
\mathcal{G}_{2}
and the rest nodes are source nodes. Then, for every damping factor
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
PR_{\lambda}:\Delta_{N}^{+}\longrightarrow\Delta_{N}^{+}
has no fixed points but
PR_{\lambda}:\Delta_{N}\longrightarrow\Delta_{N}
has a unique fixed point. Furthermore, this fixed point is of the form
\mathbb{x}^{T}=(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times 1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
, where
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
is the left-hand Perron vector of the row-normalization square matrix
P_{A_{2}}
of order
n_{2}
associated to the component
\mathcal{G}_{2}
.

Finally, we present the
General reducible case
in which, as in the previous result, the iteration of the personalization vector converges to the vector
(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times 1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
, where
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
is the left-hand Perron vector of the matrix
P_{A_{2}}
. We remark that although the arguments to show that the first
n_{1}
-entries tend to zero are only slightly different from the ones in Theorem
4
, the conclusion for the remaining entries requires quite a harder work. Before stating this result, recall that for real matrices
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j}
and
B=(b_{ij})_{i,j}
of the same order, the inequality
A\geq B
means that
a_{ij}\geq b_{ij}
for all
i,j
.

Now, we are in position to state the convergence result for the
General reducible case
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
A
be a non-negative reducible square matrix of order
N
of the form

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A_{1}&B\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&A_{2}\end{array}\right),

where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are non-negative irreducible square matrices of order
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, respectively, and
B
is a non-negative, different from zero, matrix of size
n_{1}\times n_{2}
, with
n_{1}+n_{2}=N
. Let
P_{A}
and
P_{A_{2}}
be the row-normalization of matrices
A
and
A_{2}
, respectively. For a fixed
\lambda\in(0,1)
, let consider
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
. Then for any personalization vector
\mathbb{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
with
\|\mathbb{v}\|_{1}=1
, the recursive sequence
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times 1}
, with
\mathbb{x}_{0}=\mathbb{v}
, defined as

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{k-1}^{T}X(\lambda)=\mathbb{v}^{T}X(\lambda)^{k}\,,\quad(k\geq 1),

converges to the vector
\mathbb{x}^{T}=(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{0}_{n_{1}\times 1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{c}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
, where
\mathbb{c}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
is the left-hand Perron vector of the matrix
P_{A_{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
P_{A}
be the row-stochastic reducible matrix obtained from
A
by row-normalization.

P_{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}Q_{A_{1}}&Q_{B}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&P_{A_{2}}\end{array}\right).

Notice that
P_{A_{2}}
is row-stochastic while matrices
Q_{A_{1}}
and
Q_{B}
are not, still they are jointly row-stochastic, that is,

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}Q_{A_{1}}&Q_{B}\end{array}\right)_{i,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\left(Q_{A_{1}}\right)_{i,j}\,+\,\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}\left(Q_{B}\right)_{i,j}=1,\quad\text{for all }i=1,2,\dots,n_{1}.

As it was mentioned in Section
2
, the Google matrix
G
without dangling nodes defined as

G=\lambda P_{A}+(1-\lambda)\mathbb{ev}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

(20)

is a row-stochastic square matrix for which there exists a unique positive vector
\pi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times 1}
(called PageRank vector), with
\pi^{T}\mathbb{e}=1
, satisfying the equation

\pi^{T}=(1-\lambda)\mathbf{v}^{T}(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}=\mathbf{v}^{T}X(\lambda),

(21)

where
X(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}
. As above, the inverse of the reducible square matrix
I_{N}-\lambda P_{A}
is the reducible matrix

(I_{N}-\lambda P_{A})^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda Q_{A_{1}})&-\lambda Q_{B}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})\end{array}\right)^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda Q_{A_{1}})^{-1}&\lambda(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda Q_{A_{1}})^{-1}Q_{B}(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\end{array}\right).\\

Therefore, equation (
21
) can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\pi^{T}=\mathbb{v}^{T}X(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{v}^{T}(1-\lambda)(I_{N}-\lambda Q_{A})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{v}^{T}(1-\lambda)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda Q_{A_{1}})^{-1}&\lambda(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda Q_{A_{1}})^{-1}Q_{B}(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{v}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda Q_{A_{1}})^{-1}&\lambda(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda Q_{A_{1}})^{-1}Q_{B}(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{v}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}X_{1}(\lambda)&\lambda X_{1}(\lambda)Q_{B}(I-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&X_{2}(\lambda)\end{array}\right)=\mathbb{v}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}X_{1}(\lambda)&\lambda(1-\lambda)^{-1}X_{1}(\lambda)Q_{B}X_{2}(\lambda)\\
\hline\cr\mathbb{0}&X_{2}(\lambda)\end{array}\right)

where
X_{1}(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{1}}-\lambda Q_{A_{1}})^{-1}
(the existence of which if justified as in Proof of Step 1 below) and
X_{2}(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)(I_{n_{2}}-\lambda P_{A_{2}})^{-1}
. Now, let us consider a recursive sequence of vectors
\{\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}\}_{k\geq 0}
defined by

\mathbb{x}_{k}^{T}=\mathbb{x}_{k-1}^{T}X(\lambda)\,,\quad(k\geq 1)\,,\,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\,\mathbb{x}_{0}=\mathbb{v}>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\mathbb{v}\|_{1}=1.

(22)

Let
\mathbb{v}^{T}=(\kern 5.0pt\mathbb{v}_{1}^{T}\quad|\quad\mathbb{v}_{2}^{T}\kern 5.0pt)
be a decomposition of vector
\mathbb{v}
where
\mathbb{v}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}\times 1}
and
\mathbb{v}_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}\times 1}
. Since

X^{k}(\lambda)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}X_{1}(\lambda)&\lambda(1-\lambda)^{-1}X_{1}(\lambda)Q_{B}X_{2}(\lambda)\\

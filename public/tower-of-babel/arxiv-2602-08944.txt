1.
Introduction

The celebrated works
[
15
,
16
]
of Calderón and Zygmund on singular integrals in the 1950s lie at the heart of modern harmonic analysis and pave the way for the analysis of the optimal integrability and differentiability properties of solutions to equations with linear differential operators. To make things concrete, suppose
u
solves the Poisson equation
-\Delta u=f
in a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the theory of Calderón and Zygmund yields that

f\in L^{r}_{\loc}(\Omega)\quad\overset{\text{Calder\'{o}n-Zygmund}}{\implies}\quad|D^{2}u|\in L^{r}_{\loc}(\Omega)\quad\overset{\text{Sobolev}}{\implies}\quad\nabla u\in L^{\frac{rn}{n-r}}_{\loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

for any
r\in(1,n)
.
In other words, the integrability of
\nabla u
can be calibrated by that of
f
in a sharp way. Such results motivated the study of analogous properties for nonlinear
problems, such as the
p
-Poisson equation

-\Delta_{p}u=-\operatorname{div}\big(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\big)=f.

In particular, one has

f\in L^{r}_{\loc}(\Omega)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\nabla u\in L^{\frac{rn(p-1)}{n-r}}_{\loc}(\Omega)

for every

r\in\bigg(\max\Big\{1,\frac{np}{n(p-1)+p}\Big\},\,n\bigg);

see
[
2
,
14
,
18
,
30
,
31
]
. In these works, the right-hand side is given in divergence form.
To obtain the above implication, one first needs to solve

\operatorname{div}(|F|^{p-2}F)=f
. The absence of representation formulae poses tangible challenges, entailing new perspectives as well as the introduction of novel tools capable of emulating representation formulae. This field, known as
nonlinear Calderón–Zygmund theory
, has continued to expand its scope over the past four decades. For more details, see the survey of Mingione
[
37
]
.

1.1.
Fractional
p
-Poisson type equations

In recent years research into the regularity theory for fractional equations of the
p
-Laplacian type, written as
\left(-\Delta_{p}\right)^{s}u=0
, has gained considerable momentum. Such an equation is motivated by considerations from the calculus of variations, similar to the
p
-Laplace operator. The focus of current interest is on gradient regularity; several groups of authors have made significant contributions in this direction. In particular, it has been proven in
[
11
,
8
,
9
,
22
]
under various conditions that weak solutions, which are merely fractionally differentiable, possess gradient, which actually lies in proper Lebesgue spaces. The method of proof is based on finite differences, originally devised in
[
11
,
12
]
for the fractional
p
-Laplacian equation. Subsequently, and more recently, boundedness and continuity of the gradient have been obtained in
[
6
,
7
,
28
]
using completely different approaches. Notwithstanding important progress, a theory in the vein of Calderón–Zygmund eludes these works.

Here in this work, we aim to pinpoint how the integrability of
f
determines that of
\nabla u
, thereby establishing a complete analogue of the Calderón-Zygmund type estimates for the fractional
p
-Poisson equation.
To be specific, consider the
(s,p)
-Poisson equation with a coefficient, that is,

(1.1)

\left(-a\Delta_{p}\right)^{s}u=f\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

where
s\in(0,1)
,
p>1
and
f
is given in a proper Lebesgue space whereas

(-a\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x):=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}y.

The coefficient
a(\cdot,\cdot)\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is measurable, symmetric and satisfies

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{o}\leq a(x,y)\leq C_{1}\quad\mbox{a.e. $(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}$}\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in B_{R}(x_{o})}|a(x,y)-a(x_{o},x_{o})|\leq C_{1}R^{\chi}\quad\mbox{for any $R\in(0,R_{o}]$}\end{array}\right.

for any
x_{o}\in\Omega
and for some
C_{1}\geq C_{o}>0
,
R_{o}>0
, and
\chi\in(0,1)
.

As we explained earlier, the issue is to quantify how the integrability of
f
determines that of
\nabla u
. Previously in
[
10
]
we established, under the restrictions that
a\equiv 1
and
s\in(\frac{p-1}{p},1)
, the following gradient regularity

f\in L^{r}_{\loc}(\Omega)\quad\implies\quad\nabla u\in L^{r(p-1)}_{\loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

where
r
is any number greater than
\frac{p}{p-1}
.

The main objective here is to upgrade such gradient regularity and establish the sharp version.
Moreover, non-differentiable coefficients as in (
1.2
) are considered. The precise statement is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>1
and
s\in(0,1)
with
sp^{\prime}>1
. Assume that
f\in L^{r}_{\loc}(\Omega)
for some

(1.3)

r\in\bigg(\max\bigg\{1,\frac{np}{(p-1)\big[n+p(sp^{\prime}-1)\big]}\bigg\},\frac{n}{(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}\bigg).

Then any local weak solution
u
to (
1.1
) satisfying (
1.2
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
belongs to

u\in W^{1,q}_{\loc}(\Omega),\qquad q=\frac{rn(p-1)}{\,n-r(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}.

Moreover, there exist a constant
c=c(n,p,s,C_{o},C_{1},\chi,r)>0
and a radius
\widetilde{R}_{o}=\widetilde{R}_{o}(n,p,s,C_{o},C_{1},\chi,R_{o},r)\in(0,R_{o}]
such that

\displaystyle\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\!\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\!\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{c}{R}\,\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{R}}\big|R^{sp}f\big|^{r}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\!\frac{1}{r(p-1)}}+\frac{c}{R}\,\mathrm{Tail}\big(u-(u)_{R};B_{R}\big),

for every ball
B_{4R}\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,\widetilde{R}_{o}]
.

One quickly realizes that formally letting
s=1
in this theorem yields the integrability of
\nabla u
that has been known for the classical
p
-Laplace equation; letting
p=2
we recover the integrability illustrated in
[
21
, Eqn. (1.24)]
. Moreover, we have
q\to\infty
as
r\uparrow\frac{n}{(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}
. As a matter of fact, the integrability of
\nabla u
in Theorem
1.1
is optimal as shown by the explicit example in §
1.2
. In other words, Theorem
1.1
gives an optimal quantification of the integrability of
\nabla u
above the
L^{p}
-level.

Remark 1.2
.

As a byproduct of Chapter 4, in particular Theorem
4.4
, the
L^{p}
-norm of the gradient
appearing in the theorem can be controlled by the

L^{p}
-norm of
u
together with the relevant tail term. The explicit quantitative estimate is then given by

\displaystyle\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R}\,\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{c}{R}\,\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{R}}\big|R^{sp}f\big|^{r}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\!\frac{1}{r(p-1)}}+\frac{c}{R}\,\mathrm{Tail}\big(u;B_{R}\big),

for every ball
B_{4R}\subset\Omega
with
R\in(0,\widetilde{R}_{o}]
.

An important difference from the classical
p
-Poisson equation is that the concept of “gradient” is not inherent in the notion of weak solutions for the fractional
p
-Poisson equation. Prior to our work, it has been known from
[
22
]
that
f\in L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}_{\loc}(\Omega)
guarantees
\nabla u\in L^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. An essential contribution of our work is that the integrability requirement of
f
has been significantly reduced. In fact, the required amount of integrability corresponds to the lower bound of
r
in (
1.3
). More precisely, we have the following result which is an abridged statement of Theorem
4.4
; see Theorem
3.5
for the
(s,p)
-Poisson equation without coefficients.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p>1
and
s\in(0,1)
with
sp^{\prime}>1
. Assume that

f\in L^{r}_{\loc}(\Omega)\quad\text{for some}\quad r>r_{*}:=\max\bigg\{1,\frac{np}{(p-1)\,[\,n+p(sp^{\prime}-1)\,]}\bigg\}.

Then any local weak solution to (
1.1
) satisfying (
1.2
)
in the sense of Definition
2.1
belongs to
W^{1+\gamma,p}_{\loc}(\Omega)

for some
\gamma=\gamma(n,p,s,\chi,r)\in(0,1)
.

Remark 1.4
.

For the parameter
r_{\ast}
, we note that

\displaystyle r_{\ast}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\frac{np}{(p-1)[n+p(sp^{\prime}-1)]},&\mbox{if $\displaystyle s\in(\tfrac{p-1}{p},s_{o}]$,}\\[12.0pt]
1,&\mbox{if $\displaystyle s\in(s_{o},1)$.}\end{array}\right.

where

s_{o}:=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\bigg[1+\frac{n}{p(p-1)}\bigg].

In the case
s\in(s_{o},1)
, the statement of Theorem
1.3
continues to hold for
f\in L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega)
.

As the reader may have noted, the target Lebesgue space of
\nabla u
in our theorems aims at the
L^{p}
-scale or higher, and the threshold integrability of
f
is provided by Theorem
1.3
for this purpose.
This is precisely in line with the philosophy of nonlinear Calderón–Zygmund theory.

1.2.
Optimality

Let
sp<n
and

u(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{\gamma}},\qquad\gamma=\frac{n}{r(p-1)}-sp^{\prime},

where

r\in\bigg(\max\bigg\{1,\frac{np}{(p-1)\big[n+p(sp^{\prime}-1)\big]}\bigg\},\frac{n}{(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}\bigg).

A direct computation shows that

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=f\quad\text{pointwise in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

where

f(x)=\frac{C}{|x|^{\frac{n}{r}}},\qquad C={\rm p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|1-|y|^{-\gamma}|^{p-2}(1-|y|^{-\gamma})}{|e-y|^{n+sp}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}y

and
e
denotes a unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Moreover,

u\in W^{s,p}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

Combining these facts with the pointwise identity in

\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, it follows that
u
is a local weak solution of

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=f\qquad\text{in }B_{1}.

Furthermore,
f\in L^{\tilde{r}}(B_{1})
for every
\tilde{r}<r
, and

|\nabla u|\in L^{\tilde{q}}(B_{1})\quad\text{for every }\tilde{q}<q=\frac{rn(p-1)}{\,n-r(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)},

while

|\nabla u|\notin L^{q}(B_{1}).

This shows that the integrability assumption
f\in L^{r}_{\rm loc}
in the
Calderón–Zygmund estimate is optimal to conclude that

|\nabla u|\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}
. In particular, it cannot be relaxed to

f\in L^{\tilde{r}}_{\rm loc}
for some
\tilde{r}<r
while still guaranteeing

|\nabla u|\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}
.

1.3.
Comments on techniques

Theorem
1.3
rests upon our previous works
[
8
,
9
]
on gradient estimates of
(s,p)
-harmonic functions, that is, local solutions to (
1.1
) with
a\equiv 1
and
f\equiv 0
.
The improvement is achieved by a rather involved comparison technique, coupled with delicate finite iteration arguments as developed in
[
8
,
9
]
. In essence, the comparison scheme unfolds in two stages: First, we consider
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=f
in §
3
; second, we deal with coefficients in §
4
. Unlike local equations, a fractional equation cannot be differentiated directly to obtain the equation satisfied by the gradient of solutions. As a result, the strategy consists in implementing the comparison argument at a discrete level by considering second order finite differences. Moreover, the low integrability of
f
leads to a loss of increment in the standard finite difference scheme. To remedy this issue, a finite difference scheme must be implemented at a finer scale, sometimes called atomic decomposition. Such an issue is not unique to fractional equations. In fact, this idea appeared in
[
25
,
32
,
33
,
35
,
36
,
5
,
19
]
in different contexts and has been recently adapted to certain fractional equations in
[
21
,
34
]
. To the best of our knowledge, it is developed here for the first time in the setting of
(s,p)
-harmonic functions. Once a refined estimate is found for second order differences, we employ finite iterations to upgrade the preset
W^{s,p}
-regularity of solutions to the
W^{1+\gamma,p}
-regularity.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is built on Theorem
1.3
, which essentially serves to quantify the difference between solutions and their
(s,p)
-harmonic replacement at the gradient level. Together with the gradient estimates obtained in
[
8
,
9
]
for
(s,p)
-harmonic functions in higher Lebesgue spaces, we will obtain gradient estimates on level sets, within a family of carefully selected balls. These balls are small enough to capture the essential largeness of the gradient above the designated level, yet large enough to generate a covering of the level set by appropriate enlargement. This scheme was already presented in our previous work
[
10
]
; it further develops the technique of
[
2
,
3
,
14
]
in a different context and features direct use of the equation under consideration, without resorting to standard tools of harmonic analysis. In contrast to
[
2
,
3
]
our argument is based on the weakest possible a priori estimates and does not use boundedness of the gradient of
(s,p)
-harmonic functions or
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimates; this point stems from
[
26
,
33
]
.

Last but not least, most of the literature on nonlinear Calderón–Zygmund theory focuses on inhomogeneities in
divergence-form
, cf.
[
2
,
14
,
18
,
30
,
31
]
, while the corresponding results in the
non-divergence
case can be obtained as a corollary via a so-called Bogovskiı argument.
Here, our argument provides a new approach that directly handles
inhomogeneities in non-divergence form, and may be applicable in
settings where the Bogovskiı argument fails.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we write
p^{\prime}:=\tfrac{p}{p-1}
for the Hölder conjugate of
p
, and
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
for the set of nonnegative integers.
The symbol
c
denotes a positive constant whose value may vary from line to line.
Whenever it is relevant to record the dependence of such constants on specific quantities, this will be made explicit by writing, for instance,
c=c(n,p,s)
to indicate dependence on the parameters
n,p,s
.
We denote by
B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the open ball of radius
R>0
centered at
x_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, the average of a function
f\in L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{o}))
is denoted by
(f)_{x_{o},R}:=-\mskip-16.5mu\int_{B_{R}(x_{o})}f\,\mathrm{d}x
.
If the center plays no essential role, or when it is clear from the context, we simply write
B_{R}
instead of
B_{R}(x_{o})
and
(f)_{R}
instead of
(f)_{x_{o},R}
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set.
For
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
q\in[1,\infty)
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
fractional Sobolev space

W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
as the collection of all measurable maps
w\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
for which

\|w\|_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}:=\|w\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}+[w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}<\infty.

The Gagliardo seminorm
[w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}
is given by

[w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}:=\biggl[\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+\gamma q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr]^{1/q}.

Whenever the target dimension
k
is understood from the context (or when
k=1
), we abbreviate

[w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega)}:=[w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}
.
In particular, if
w=\nabla v
for some scalar function
v\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we shall write

[\nabla v]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega)}
in place of

[\nabla v]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.
Further properties of fractional Sobolev spaces are collected in §
2.3
; for a comprehensive reference, see
[
24
]
.

In what follows, we set

\displaystyle p_{s}^{\ast}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{np}{n-sp},&\mbox{if $sp<n$,}\\[5.0pt]
\infty,&\mbox{if $sp>n$,}\end{array}\right.\quad\mbox{and}\quad(p_{s}^{\ast})^{\prime}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{np}{np-n+sp},&\mbox{if $sp<n$,}\\[5.0pt]
1,&\mbox{if $sp>n$.}\end{array}\right.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
s\in(0,1)
, and
f\in L^{r}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, with

r\geq(p_{s}^{\ast})^{\prime}\quad\mbox{if $sp\not=n$,}\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad r>1\quad\mbox{if $sp=n$.}

A function
u\in W^{s,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)

is called a
local weak solution
to the fractional
p
-Poisson equation (
1.1
) in
\Omega
if and only if

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{(1+|x|)^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

and

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=2\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

for every
\varphi\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\operatorname{spt}(\varphi)\Subset\Omega
.

If a function
u
satisfies the integrability condition (
2.1
), we shall write

u\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and refer to this as the
weighted Lebesgue space
.
Given a point
x_{o}\in\Omega
and a radius
R>0
such that
B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
, we associate to
u
the nonlocal quantity

\operatorname{Tail}\big(u;B_{R}(x_{o})\big):=\biggl[R^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{o})}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{o}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Next, to construct comparison functions, we also need to consider Dirichlet problems

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(-a\Delta_{p})^{s}u=0&\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\[6.0pt]
u=g&\mbox{a.e.\penalty\ in $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega$.}\end{array}\right.

Here, the boundary datum
g
belongs to
L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
This problem is well-posed in the following sense.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
. A function
u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called a
weak solution
to the Dirichlet problem (
2.2
) in
\Omega
if and only if
u=g
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega

and

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=0

for every
\varphi\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
\varphi\equiv 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
.

2.1.
Algebraic inequalities

We recall the following elementary inequality from
[
10

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\gamma\in[1,\infty)
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

. Then
for every

Let
p\geq 1
,
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
and

\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k,i}\Big|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{k,i}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

A covering Lemma

We record the following elementary geometric covering lemma. See
[
21

.

Lemma 2.5

(Covering lemma)
.

Let
x_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
R>0
and
r\in(0,R/2)
. There exists a constant
c=c(n)
, a finite index set

2.3.
Fractional Sobolev spaces

We recall several auxiliary results concerning fractional Sobolev spaces.
The fractional Sobolev embedding is well known; see
[
4
, Chapter 7]
. An elementary approach can be found in
[
24
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
u\in W^{\gamma,q}(B_{1})
for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
q\geq 1
.
Then, we have

\|u\|_{L^{\kappa}(B_{1})}\leq c\,\|u\|_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{1})},

where
c=c(n,\gamma,q,\kappa)
and

(2.4)

\kappa=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{nq}{n-\gamma q},&\gamma q<n,\\[5.0pt]
\text{any number in}\>[1,\infty),&\gamma q=n,\\[5.0pt]
\infty,&\gamma q>n.\end{array}\right.

The dependence of the constant
c
on
\kappa
disappears when
\gamma q\neq n
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

,

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
q\geq 1
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

, and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Then there is a constant

Remark 2.9
.

It is straightforward to compute that there exists some
c=c(\gamma,q,n)
, such that

[u]_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{1})}^{q}\leq c\left(\|u\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}^{q}+[u]_{W^{\sigma,q}(B_{1})}^{q}\right),

for any
0<\gamma<\sigma<1
.
Since
[u]_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{1})}=[u-(u)_{1}]_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{1})}
, we can use the fractional Poincaré inequality (Lemma
2.7
) to obtain that

[u]_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{1})}^{p}\leq c\,[u]_{W^{\sigma,q}(B_{1})}^{p},

or its scaled version:

(2.5)

R^{\gamma q}[u]_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{R})}^{q}\leq c\,R^{\sigma q}[u]_{W^{\sigma,q}(B_{R})}^{q}.

The following embedding is well-known.

Lemma 2.10

(Embedding
W^{1,q}\hookrightarrow W^{\gamma,q}
)
.

Let

and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Then for any

Fractional differentiability can be translated to the language of finite differences. Here, we record some of such results. The first one can be found in
[
11
, Proposition 2.6]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Then, there exists a constant
c=c(n,q)
such that for any
w\in W^{\gamma,q}(B_{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{7R/8}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}w|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,|h|^{\gamma q}\Big[[w]^{q}_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{R})}+R^{-\gamma q}\|w\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\Big]

for any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
that satisfies
|h|\leq\frac{1}{8}R
. In particular, if
w\in W^{\gamma,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has a compact support, then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}w|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,|h|^{\gamma q}[w]^{q}_{W^{\gamma,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The second result is a consequence of the previous one.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Then, there exists a constant
c=c(n,q)
such that for any
w\in W^{1+\gamma,q}(B_{2R})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{7R/8}}|\bm{\tau}^{2}_{h}w|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,|h|^{(1+\gamma)q}\Big[[\nabla w]^{q}_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{2R})}+R^{-\gamma q}\|\nabla w\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{2R})}\Big]

for any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
that satisfies
|h|\leq\frac{1}{8}R
.

Proof..

First of all, for any
v\in W^{1,q}(B_{R})
it is well known that

\|\bm{\tau}_{h}v\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{7R/8})}\leq c(n,q)|h|^{q}\|\nabla v\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}

for all
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
that satisfy
|h|\leq\frac{1}{8}R
. On the other hand, since
\nabla w\in W^{\gamma,q}(B_{R})
, we apply Lemma
2.11
to get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla w|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,|h|^{\gamma q}\Big[[\nabla w]^{q}_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{2R})}+R^{-\gamma q}\|\nabla w\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{2R})}\Big]

To conclude, we use the first estimate with
v=\bm{\tau}_{h}w
and combine it with the second estimate.
∎

We start with a basic estimate that compares the tail of a function over concentric balls of different radii.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let

\displaystyle\Tail\big(u;B_{r}(x_{o})\big)^{p-1}\leq c(n)\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{n}\Big[\Tail\big(u,B_{R}(x_{o})\big)+R^{-\frac{n}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}(x_{o}))}\Big]^{p-1}

Proof..

Consider

B_{r}(x_{o})\Subset B_{R}(x_{o})
and estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\Tail

\displaystyle\big(u;B_{r}(x_{o})\big)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=r^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{o})}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{o}|^{n+sp}}\mathrm{d}x+r^{sp}\int_{B_{R}(x_{o})\setminus B_{r}(x_{o})}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{o}|^{n+sp}}\mathrm{d}x

Finally, if two functions coincide outside a certain ball, then their mean-subtracted tails are comparable up to the
L^{p}
-difference.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
.
There exists a constant
c=c(n,s,p)
such that the following holds.
Let
B_{r}(x_{o})\subset B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
be concentric balls and let

satisfy

on

Then

\displaystyle\Tail\big(v

\displaystyle-(v)_{x_{o},r};B_{r}(x_{o})\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\Tail\big(u-(u)_{x_{o},r};B_{r}(x_{o})\big)+c\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{\frac{n}{p-1}}\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}(x_{o})}|u-v|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof..

By adding and subtracting
u-(u)_{x_{o},r}
and using Minkowski’s inequality
when
p\geq 2

(up to a multiplicative constant) when
1<p<2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Tail\big(v-(v)_{x_{o},r};B_{r}(x_{o})\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\Tail\big(v-u;B_{r}(x_{o})\big)

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+c\Tail\big(u-(u)_{x_{o},r};B_{r}(x_{o})\big)

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+c\Tail\big((u)_{x_{o},r}-(v)_{x_{o},r};B_{r}(x_{o})\big).

It remains to estimate the first and the third term on the right-hand side.
Using the assumption
v\equiv u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{o})
, the radial
dependence of the kernel, and Hölder’s inequality, we infer

\displaystyle\Tail\big(v-u;B_{r}(x_{o})\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg[r^{sp}\int_{B_{R}(x_{o})\setminus B_{r}(x_{o})}\frac{|v-u|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{o}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg[\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{n}-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}(x_{o})}|u-v|^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{\frac{n}{p-1}}\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}(x_{o})}|u-v|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The third term can be treated analogously. Indeed,

\displaystyle\Tail\big((u)_{x_{o},r}-(v)_{x_{o},r};B_{r}(x_{o})\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\,|(u)_{x_{o},r}-(v)_{x_{o},r}|

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{r}(x_{o})}|u-v|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{\frac{n}{p}}\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}(x_{o})}|u-v|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Combining the above estimates yields the claim.
∎

3.

p
-Poisson equation

In this section we consider inhomogeneous fractional
p
-Laplace equations of the type

(3.1)

\left(-\Delta_{p}\right)^{s}u=f\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

Our goal is to prove higher differentiability of local weak solutions of order
W^{1+\gamma,p}
for some
\gamma>0
as stated in Theorem
3.5
whose abridged version has been stated in Theorem
1.3
. The proof relies on the results for the homogeneous problem and a comparison argument.

3.1.
Definition and Role of the Parameters

Due to the definition of
r_{*}
from Theorem
1.3
, we shall
distinguish between the cases

s\leq s_{o}\quad\text{and}\quad s>s_{o}\quad\mbox{where }s_{o}:=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\bigg[1+\frac{n}{p(p-1)}\bigg].

More precisely, in the latter regime
the assumption
f\in L_{\loc}^{1}(\Omega)
already suffices to guarantee

\nabla u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
In contrast, when

s\leq s_{o}
,
one needs to impose the stronger condition

f\in L_{\loc}^{\frac{np}{(p-1)[\,n+p(sp^{\prime}-1)\,]}}(\Omega)

in order to ensure the same local integrability of the gradient. To unify the two regimes, we introduce the parameter

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle:=\max\bigg\{\frac{n}{(p-1)\,[\,n+p(sp^{\prime}-1)\,]}\,,\,\frac{1}{p}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\frac{n}{(p-1)[n+p(sp^{\prime}-1)]},&\mbox{if $\displaystyle s\in(\tfrac{p-1}{p},s_{o}]$,}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p},&\mbox{if $\displaystyle s\in(s_{o},1)$.}\end{array}\right.

Note that
\alpha\in[\tfrac{1}{p},\,\tfrac{1}{p-1})
in either case.
This necessitates an improved integrability assumption on the right-hand side
f
.
Accordingly, we shall frequently work with a parameter

\tilde{\alpha}\in[\alpha,\tfrac{1}{p-1}]
and introduce

(3.4)

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle:=sp^{\prime}-1-\frac{n}{p}\bigg[\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}(p-1)}-1\bigg]\in[0,\,sp^{\prime}-1].

To see that
\varepsilon\geq 0
, we replace
\tilde{\alpha}
with
\alpha
in
(
3.4
) and invoke the case distinction in the definition of
\alpha

in (
3.1
).

The following lemma illustrates the interplay of the relevant parameters in controlling the integral of
f
against a function
w
supported in a smaller ball
B_{R}
. The parameter
\tilde{\alpha}
is chosen at least
\alpha
to guarantee sufficient integrability of
f
, while the requirement
\varepsilon>0
quantifies a small gain in the scaling with respect to the radius
R
(see (
3.4
)).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p>1
and
s\in(0,1)
with
sp^{\prime}>1
, and let
\tilde{\alpha}\in[\alpha,\frac{1}{p-1}]
be such that
\varepsilon>0
, where
\alpha
and
\varepsilon
are defined in (
3.1
) and (
3.4
).
Then, there exists a constant

c=c(n,s,p,\tilde{\alpha})>0
such that for every ball

B_{2R}\equiv B_{2R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
, every function

w\in W^{s,p}(B_{2R})
with
\operatorname{spt}(w)\subset B_{R}
,
every
f\in L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})
, and every
\tilde{\delta}\in(0,1]
,
the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}fw\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\tilde{\delta}\,[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}+c\,\tilde{\delta}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}R^{(1-s+\varepsilon)p}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{p^{\prime}}

Proof..

We introduce the rescaled functions

\widetilde{w}(x):=R^{-s}w(Rx),\qquad\widetilde{f}(x):=R^{s}f(Rx).

With this choice we have
\widetilde{w}\in W^{s,p}(B_{2})
,

\operatorname{spt}(\widetilde{w})\subset B_{1}
, and

\widetilde{f}\in L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{1})
.
Since
\tilde{\alpha}p>\alpha p\geq 1
, Hölder’s inequality yields

\int_{B_{1}}\widetilde{f}\,\widetilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\|\widetilde{w}\|_{L^{\frac{\tilde{\alpha}p}{\tilde{\alpha}p-1}}(B_{1})}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{1})}.

Then the fractional Sobolev embedding Lemma
2.6
yields

(3.5)

\|\widetilde{w}\|_{L^{\kappa}(B_{1})}\leq c\,\|\widetilde{w}\|_{W^{s,p}(B_{2})},

where
\kappa
is as in (
2.4
) with
\gamma=s
and
c=c(n,s,p,\kappa)
and
Note that the dependence of the embedding constant on the exponent
\kappa
appears only in the borderline case
sp=n
.
To combine the preceding two estimates, it suffices to check that

\frac{\tilde{\alpha}p}{\tilde{\alpha}p-1}\leq\kappa,

which indeed holds in all relevant cases. For
sp\geq n
the claim is immediate, since
\tilde{\alpha}p\in(1,\infty)
and hence
\frac{\tilde{\alpha}p}{\tilde{\alpha}p-1}\in(1,\infty)
. In the case
sp<n
, one first observes that
p(sp^{\prime}-1)<sp^{\prime}
, and hence

\tilde{\alpha}>\alpha\geq\frac{n}{(p-1)[n+p(sp^{\prime}-1)]}>\frac{n}{(p-1)[n+sp^{\prime}]}=\frac{n}{n(p-1)+sp}.

It follows that

\frac{\tilde{\alpha}p}{\tilde{\alpha}p-1}=1+\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}p-1}<1+\frac{n(p-1)+sp}{n-sp}=\frac{np}{n-sp}=\kappa,

as desired. Hence, we may choose
\kappa=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}p}{\tilde{\alpha}p-1}
in (
3.5
), yielding

\int_{B_{1}}\widetilde{f}\,\widetilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\,\|\widetilde{w}\|_{W^{s,p}(B_{2})}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{1})}\leq c\,[\widetilde{w}]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2})}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{1})},

where in the second inequality we used Lemma
2.8
together with the fact that
\widetilde{w}=0
almost everywhere on
B_{2}\setminus B_{1}
.
Scaling back to
(B_{R},B_{2R})
, we note that

[\widetilde{w}]_{W^{s,p}(B_{1})}=R^{-s}[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})},\qquad\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{1})}=R^{s}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}.

Hence, the preceding inequality becomes

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}fw\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=R^{n}\int_{B_{1}}\widetilde{f}\,\widetilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,R^{n}[\widetilde{w}]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2})}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle=c\,R^{n-s}[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}\,R^{s-\frac{n}{\tilde{\alpha}p}}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle=c\,R^{n-\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n}{\tilde{\alpha}p}+s}[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}.

Using the definition of
\varepsilon
in (
3.4
), we rewrite the exponent of

as

\displaystyle n-\frac{n}{p}+s-\frac{n}{\tilde{\alpha}p}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(p-1)}{p}+s-(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)-\frac{n(p-1)}{p}+\varepsilon(p-1)

\displaystyle=s-(sp+p-1)+\varepsilon(p-1)

\displaystyle=(1-s+\varepsilon)(p-1).

Hence, the preceding inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}fw\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,R^{(1-s+\varepsilon)(p-1)}[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\delta}\,[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}+c\,\tilde{\delta}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}R^{(1-s+\varepsilon)p}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{p^{\prime}},

where Young’s inequality was used in the second step.
∎

, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varrho_{j_{o}+1}}(y_{o})}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,|h|^{p}\Bigg[\frac{\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{K}}}^{p}}{\varrho^{\theta_{j_{o}}p}}(h_{o}\varrho_{j_{o}})^{(\theta_{j_{o}}-1)p}+\frac{\|u-(u)_{y_{o},\varrho_{j_{o}}}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{\varrho_{j_{o}}}(y_{o}))}}{(h_{o}\varrho_{j_{o}})^{p}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\,\frac{|h|^{p}}{\varrho^{p}}\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{K}}}^{p}.

To obtain the last line, we applied the fractional Poincaré inequality from Lemma
2.7
.
The constant
c
may depend on
j_{o}
.
By a standard property of difference quotients, we may therefore conclude
that the weak gradient
\nabla u
exists and satisfies

\nabla u\in L^{p}(B_{\varrho_{j_{o}+1}}(y_{o});\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Moreover, the following quantitative estimate holds:

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{\varrho_{j_{o}+1}}(y_{o}))}^{p}\leq\frac{c}{\varrho^{p}}\,\widetilde{{\bm{\mathsf{K}}}}^{p}.

Having established the existence of the weak gradient, we may apply
inequality (
3.20
) to conclude fractional differentiability
of
\nabla u
. More precisely, Lemma
2.15
applied with
(w,q,M,\gamma,\beta,R,d)
replaced by

\Big(u,p,c\,\varrho^{-\theta_{j_{o}}p}\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{K}}},\theta_{j_{o}}-1,\gamma,\varrho_{j_{o}+2},\tfrac{1}{2}h_{o}\varrho_{j_{o}}\Big)

ensures that
u\in W^{1+\gamma,p}(B_{\varrho_{j_{o}+2}}(y_{o}))
and that

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{1+\gamma,p}(B_{\varrho_{j_{o}+2}}(y_{o}))}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\,\big(h_{o}\varrho_{j_{o}}\big)^{(\theta_{j_{o}}-1-\gamma)p}\bigg[\frac{\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{K}}}^{p}}{\varrho_{j_{o}}^{\theta_{j_{o}}p}}+\frac{\varrho^{p}}{(h_{o}\varrho_{j_{o}})^{\theta_{j_{o}}p}}\int_{B_{\varrho_{j_{o}+1}}(y_{o})}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]

(3.22)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{c}{\varrho^{(1+\gamma)p}}\widetilde{\bm{\mathsf{K}}}^{p}.

Here, in the last inequality we used (
3.21
), and the constant

c
depends only on
n,p,s
and
j_{o}
.
As the choice of
B_{\varrho}(y_{o})\subset\Omega
was arbitrary, we conclude
that
u\in W^{1+\gamma,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

Step 4: Covering argument and final estimate.

Let
\varrho=\tfrac{1}{2}R
.
We choose a finite covering of
B_{\frac{1}{2}R}
by balls

\{B_{\varrho_{j_{o}+2}}(y_{\ell})\}_{\ell=1}^{M}
such that the enlarged balls

B_{\varrho}(y_{\ell})
have uniformly bounded overlap, namely each point of

\mathbb{R}^{n}
belongs to at most
c(n,j_{o})
of them, and

B_{\varrho}(y_{\ell})\subset B_{R}
for every
\ell=1,\dots,M
.
We then apply inequalities (
3.21
) and (
3.3
) on
each ball
B_{\varrho}(y_{\ell})
and sum over
\ell=1,\dots,M
. In this way, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla u

\displaystyle\|_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}^{p}+R^{\gamma p}[\nabla u]_{W^{1+\gamma,p}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\Big[\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{\varrho_{j_{o}+1}}(y_{\ell}))}+R^{\gamma p}[\nabla u]_{W^{1+\gamma,p}(B_{\varrho_{j_{o}+2}}(y_{\ell}))}^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R^{p}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\bigg[\varrho^{sp}[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{\varrho}(y_{\ell}))}+\varrho^{n}\Tail\big(u-(u)_{y_{\ell},\varrho};B_{\varrho}(y_{\ell})\big)^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\varrho^{(1+\varepsilon)p}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{\varrho}(y_{\ell}))}^{p^{\prime}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R^{p}}\bigg[R^{sp}[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+R^{n}\Tail\big(u-(u)_{R};B_{R}\big)^{p}+R^{(1+\varepsilon)p}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{p^{\prime}}\bigg].

To obtain the last line we used Lemma
2.18
and the fractional Poincaré inequality from Lemma
2.7
.
∎

4.
Higher differentiability for variable coefficients

We study nonlocal operators
(-a\Delta_{p})^{s}
with variable coefficients

a\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying the ellipticity, boundedness, and continuity conditions in (
1.2
).

4.1.
Comparison estimate

In order to compare the local weak solution
u
of the nonhomogeneous problem (
1.1
) with a simpler reference,
we first freeze the coefficient
a(x,y)
inside the ball
B_{R}(x_{o})
and consider the associated homogeneous Dirichlet problem
(
4.2
). The function
v
is then the unique weak solution to this frozen-coefficient problem.

The precise setup is as follows. Let
B_{R}(x_{o})\subset\Omega
, and define the frozen coefficient

(4.1)

a_{x_{o},R}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(a)_{x_{o},R},&\mbox{if $(x,y)\in B_{R}(x_{o})\times B_{R}(x_{o})$,}\\[6.0pt]
a(x,y),&\mbox{if $(x,y)\notin B_{R}(x_{o})\times B_{R}(x_{o})$;}\end{array}\right.

Denote by
v\in W^{s,p}(B_{R}(x_{o}))\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the unique weak solution to the corresponding homogeneous Dirichlet problem

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(-a_{x_{o},R}\Delta_{p})^{s}v=0,&\mbox{in $B_{R}(x_{o})$,}\\[6.0pt]
v=u,&\mbox{a.e.\penalty\ in $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{o})$.}\end{array}\right.

and set
w:=u-v
. The forthcoming Lemma
4.1
provides precise control of
w
,
capturing the effect of the inhomogeneity
f
as well as the frozen coefficients,
for both the superquadratic (
p\geq 2
) and subquadratic (
p\in(1,2)
) cases.

Lemma 4.1

(Comparison estimate with variable coefficients)
.

Let
p>1
and
s\in(0,1)
with
sp^{\prime}>1
, and let
\tilde{\alpha}\in[\alpha,\frac{1}{p-1}]
be such that
\varepsilon>0
, where
\alpha
and
\varepsilon
are defined in (
3.1
) and (
3.4
).
Then there exists a constant

c=c(n,p,s,C_{o},C_{1},\tilde{\alpha})>0
with the following property.
Whenever
u
is a local weak solution to (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
with coefficients
a
satisfying (
1.2
),
f\in L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(\Omega)
,
B_{2R}\equiv B_{2R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
with
R\leq R_{o}
where
R_{o}
is from (
1.2
), and
v\in W^{s,p}(B_{R}(x_{o}))\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the unique weak solution to the homogeneous Dirichlet problem (
4.2
) with frozen coefficients
a_{x_{o},R}
in the sense of Definition
2.2
, the function

w:=u-v

satisfies the following estimates:

(i) Superquadratic case.

If
p\in[2,\infty)
, then

\displaystyle R^{-sp}\|w\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}+[w]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq c\,R^{\frac{\chi p}{p-1}}[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+c\,R^{(1-s+\varepsilon)p}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{p^{\prime}}.

(ii) Subquadratic case.

If
p\in(1,2]
, then for every
\delta\in(0,1]
we have

\displaystyle R^{-sp}\|w\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}

\displaystyle+[w]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[\delta+\frac{R^{\frac{\chi p}{p-1}}}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}p^{\prime}}}\bigg][u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}+\frac{c}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}p^{\prime}}}R^{(1-s+\varepsilon)p}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{p^{\prime}}.

Proof..

Testing the equations for
u
and
v
with
w:=u-v
and then taking the difference, we obtain

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{\big[a(x,y)\bm{(u(x)-u(y))}^{p-1}-a_{x_{o},R}(x,y)\bm{(v(x)-v(y))}^{p-1}\big]}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot[w(x)-w(y)]\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}fw\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x,

where for
a\in\mathbb{R}
we write

\bm{a}^{p-1}:=|a|^{p-2}a\quad\mbox{if $a\not=0$}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\bm{a}^{p-1}:=0\quad\mbox{if $a=0$}.

Properly splitting the integrand on the left-hand side, we get

{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}={\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}+{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}},

where

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}a_{x_{o},R}(x,y)\frac{{\bm{\mathsf{D}}}(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}[a_{x_{o},R}(x,y)-a(x,y)]\frac{\bm{(u(x)-u(y))}^{p-1}(w(x)-w(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}y,

and

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{R}}fw\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x.

Here, we defined

{\bm{\mathsf{D}}}(x,y):=\big(\bm{(u(x)-u(y))}^{p-1}-\bm{(v(x)-v(y))}^{p-1}\big)(w(x)-w(y)).

We emphasize that
{\bm{\mathsf{D}}}(x,y)\geq 0
for a.e.
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
which follows from the monotonicity of the map

\bm{\xi}\mapsto\bm{\xi}^{p-1}

and

(4.3)

w(x)-w(y)=(u(x)-u(y))-(v(x)-v(y)).

When
p\geq 2
, we have the pointwise inequality

\big(\bm{\eta}^{p-1}-\bm{\xi}^{p-1}\big)(\eta-\xi)\geq\tfrac{1}{c(p)}|\eta-\xi|^{p}\quad\mbox{for any $\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Using (
4.3
) and the ellipticity bound
a_{x_{o},R}\geq C_{o}>0
, we obtain a lower bound for the left-hand side:

\frac{C_{o}}{c(p)}[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}\leq\iint_{B_{2R}\times B_{2R}}a_{x_{o},R}(x,y)\frac{{\bm{\mathsf{D}}}(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}y\leq{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}+{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}.

In the sub-quadratic case
p\in(1,2)
, a lower bound for
{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}
follows from
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
; cf.
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
,
[
27
, Lemma 2.2]
.
More precisely, we have

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{D}}}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{c(p)}\big(|u(x)-u(y)|+|v(x)-v(y)|\big)^{p-2}|w(x)-w(y)|^{2}.

Applying Young’s inequality with exponents
(\frac{2}{2-p},\frac{2}{p})
and using the previous display, we get

\displaystyle|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}

\displaystyle=\delta^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\big(|u(x)-u(y)|+|v(x)-v(y)|\big)^{\frac{(2-p)p}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\delta^{-\frac{2-p}{2}}\big(|u(x)-u(y)|+|v(x)-v(y)|\big)^{\frac{(p-2)p}{2}}|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\big(|u(x)-u(y)|+|v(x)-v(y)|\big)^{p}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta^{-\frac{2-p}{p}}\big(|u(x)-u(y)|+|v(x)-v(y)|\big)^{p-2}|w(x)-w(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8\delta|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}+2\delta|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}+\frac{c(p)}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p}}}\,{\bm{\mathsf{D}}}(x,y).

Choosing
\delta\in\bigl(0,\tfrac{1}{4}\bigr]
in the preceding inequality,
the second term on the right-hand side can be absorbed into the left-hand side.
As a consequence, we obtain

\displaystyle|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}\leq 16\,\delta\,|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}+\frac{c(p)}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p}}}\,{\bm{\mathsf{D}}}(x,y).

Multiplying both sides by
a_{x_{o},R}(x,y)\,|x-y|^{-n-sp}
and integrating over

B_{2R}\times B_{2R}
, we infer, using the ellipticity bounds

C_{o}\leq a_{x_{o},R}\leq C_{1}
and the nonnegativity of the integrand in
{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}
, that

\displaystyle\frac{C_{o}}{c(p)}

\displaystyle[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16C_{1}}{c(p)}\delta[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}+\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p}}}\iint_{B_{2R}\times B_{2R}}a_{x_{o},R}(x,y)\frac{{\bm{\mathsf{D}}}(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16C_{1}}{c(p)}\delta[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}+\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{16C_{1}}{c(p)}\delta[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}+\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p}}}\big[{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}+{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}\big].

The term
{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}
on the right-hand side can be estimated by exploiting
the Hölder continuity of the coefficient, Hölder’s inequality, and Young’s
inequality. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p}}}\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}\big[(a)_{x_{o},R}-a(x,y)\big]\frac{\bm{(u(x)-u(y))}^{p-1}\,(w(x)-w(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c\,R^{\chi}}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p}}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}^{p-1}[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{o}}{2c(p)}[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}^{p}+\frac{c\,R^{\frac{\chi p}{p-1}}}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{o}}{2c(p)}[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}^{p}+\frac{c\,R^{\frac{\chi p}{p-1}}}{\delta^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\delta[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}^{p}.

Inserting this estimate into the previous inequality and absorbing the first
term on the right-hand side into the left-hand side, we arrive at

\displaystyle[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\delta\bigg[1+\Big(\frac{R^{\chi p}}{\delta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigg][u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}+c\,\frac{{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}}.

It remains to estimate the term involving the inhomogeneity
f
.
By Lemma
3.1
, we have for every
\tilde{\delta}\in(0,1)
that

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}\leq\tilde{\delta}\,[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}+c\tilde{\delta}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}R^{(1-s+\varepsilon)p}\,\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{p^{\prime}}.

Choosing
\tilde{\delta}=\frac{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}}{2c}
, we can re-absorb
\frac{1}{2}[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}
into the left-hand side with the result

\displaystyle[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}\leq c\,\delta\bigg[1+\Big(\frac{R^{\chi p}}{\delta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigg][u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{2R})}^{p}+\frac{c}{\delta^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}p^{\prime}}}R^{(1-s+\varepsilon)p}\,\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{p^{\prime}}.

Substituting
c\,\delta
by
\delta
we obtain the comparison estimate for the
W^{s,p}
-norm of
w
in the remaining case
p\in(1,2)
. The inequality for its
L^{p}
-norm now follows from Sobolev’s inequality, as in
[
10
, Corollary 3.3]
.
∎

4.2.
Higher differentiability of the gradient

The following lemma provides higher differentiability and gradient estimates for solutions
to the fractional
p
-Laplace equation with frozen coefficients, and parallels Lemma
2.23
.

Lemma 4.2

(Higher differentiability for frozen-coefficient solutions)
.

Let
p>1
,
s\in(0,1)
with
sp^{\prime}>1
, and let
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Whenever
v\in W^{s,p}_{\rm loc}(B_{R})\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a local weak solution to

(-a_{x_{o},R}\Delta_{p})^{s}v=0\quad\text{in }B_{R},

then

v\in W^{1+\gamma,p}_{\rm loc}(B_{R})\quad\text{for every }\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{o}),\qquad\gamma_{o}:=\frac{\theta-p}{\theta-sp+\bar{\varepsilon}p}\,\bar{\varepsilon},

where
\bar{\varepsilon}:=\min\{1,p-1\}\,(sp^{\prime}-1)
and
\theta
is defined in (
2.8
).
Moreover, there exists a constant

c=c(n,p,s,C_{o},C_{1},\gamma)>0
such that for every such
v
one has

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{1}{4}R})}

\displaystyle+R^{\gamma}[\nabla v]_{W^{\gamma,p}(B_{\frac{1}{4}R})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R}\Big[R^{s}[v]_{W^{s,p}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}+R^{\frac{n}{p}}\Tail\big(v-(v)_{\frac{1}{2}R};B_{\frac{1}{2}R}\big)\Big],

and, for every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
with
|h|\leq\frac{1}{32}R
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{4}R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}^{2}v|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{(1+\gamma)p}\Big[R^{sp}[v]_{W^{s,p}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}^{p}+R^{n}\Tail(v-(v)_{\frac{1}{2}R};B_{\frac{1}{2}R})^{p}\Big].

Moreover, for every
q\in(1,\infty)
, we have
v\in W^{1,pq}_{\rm loc}(B_{R})
, and there exists a constant

c=c(n,p,s,C_{o},C_{1},q)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{L^{pq}(B_{\frac{1}{4}R})}

\displaystyle\leq c\,R^{-\frac{n}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)-1}\Big[R^{s}[v]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+R^{\frac{n}{p}}\Tail\big(v-(v)_{\frac{1}{2}R};B_{\frac{1}{2}R}\big)\Big].

Proof..

Throughout the proof we omit the reference to the center
x_{o}
.
Let
\varphi\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\operatorname{spt}(\varphi)\subset B_{\frac{1}{2}R}
.
By the weak formulation of
v
and a straightforward algebraic manipulation, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\bm{(v(x)-v(y))}^{p-1}(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle={\bm{\mathsf{I}}},

where

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(a)_{R}-a_{R}(x,y)}{(a)_{R}}\frac{\bm{(v(x)-v(y))}^{p-1}(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

Since
(a)_{R}=a_{x_{o},R}(x,y)
in
B_{R}\times B_{R}
and
\operatorname{spt}(\varphi)\subset B_{R/2}
, the right-hand side reduces to

{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}=2\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{2}R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})}\frac{(a)_{R}-a_{R}(x,y)}{(a)_{R}}\frac{\bm{(v(x)-v(y))}^{p-1}\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Hence,
v
satisfies

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}v=g\quad\text{in }B_{\frac{1}{2}R},

where the inhomogeneity
g
is given by

g(x):=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{(a)_{R}-a_{x_{o},R}(x,y)}{(a)_{R}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p-2}(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\mathrm{d}y.

Since
v\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(B_{R})\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
|x-y|\geq\frac{1}{2}R
for
x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}R}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}
, we have
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})
. Hence, we may apply Theorem
3.5
(see also
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
) with
\tilde{\alpha}=\frac{1}{p-1}
to conclude that

v\in W^{1+\gamma,p}_{\rm loc}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})\quad\text{for any }\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{o}),

Moreover, Theorem
3.5
yields the quantitative estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla

\displaystyle v\|_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{1}{4}R})}+R^{\gamma}[\nabla v]_{W^{\gamma,p}(B_{\frac{1}{4}R})}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R}\Big[R^{s}[v]_{W^{s,p}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}+R^{\frac{n}{p}}\Tail\big(v-(v)_{\frac{1}{2}R};B_{R/2}\big)+R^{sp^{\prime}}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\Big],

where
c=c(n,p,s,\gamma)
. Note that for our choice
\tilde{\alpha}=\frac{1}{p-1}
, one has
\varepsilon=sp^{\prime}-1
,
see (
3.4
).
In the preceding estimates it remains to estimate the
L^{p^{\prime}}
-norm of
g
.
Using the assumptions (
1.2
) on the coefficient
a
, we have

\displaystyle|g(x)|

\displaystyle\leq c\,\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq c\,\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|v(x)-(v)_{\frac{1}{2}R}|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg|+c\,\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|v(y)-(v)_{\frac{1}{2}R}|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R^{sp}}|v(x)-(v)_{\frac{1}{2}R}|^{p-1}+c\,\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}\frac{|v(y)-(v)_{\frac{1}{2}R}|^{p-1}}{|y-x_{o}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg|

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R^{sp}}\Big[|v(x)-(v)_{\frac{1}{2}R}|^{p-1}+\Tail\big(v-(v)_{\frac{1}{2}R};B_{\frac{1}{2}R}\big)^{p-1}\Big],

for a.e.
x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}R}
.
Taking both sides to the power
p^{\prime}
, integrating over
B_{\frac{1}{2}R}
and using Lemma
2.7
yields

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}^{p^{\prime}}

) gives the first asserted bound for

The second claimed estimate for the second-order finite differences of

\displaystyle\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\frac{1}{4}R}}

\displaystyle|\nabla v|^{pq}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg[\tilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}+\frac{R^{\varepsilon}}{\tilde{\delta}^{\frac{(2-p)_{+}}{(p-1)^{2}}}}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigg]^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}+R^{\frac{n}{p}-1}\Tail\big(u-(u)_{R};B_{R}\big)+R^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\bigg[\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}+R^{\frac{n}{p}-1}\Tail\big(u-(u)_{R};B_{R}\big)+R^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+c\delta^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\bigg[\tilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}+\frac{R^{\varepsilon}}{\tilde{\delta}^{\frac{(2-p)_{+}}{(p-1)^{2}}}}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigg].

We now set

\tilde{\delta}:=\frac{\delta^{\frac{p(1+\gamma)}{\gamma}}}{(2c)^{p}},

so that

c\,\tilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{p}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta^{1+\frac{1}{\gamma}}
.
Note that this choice of
\tilde{\delta}
together with
R\leq R_{o}
requires

R\leq\min\Bigg\{R_{o},\bigg(\frac{\delta^{\frac{1+\gamma}{\gamma}}}{2c}\bigg)^{\frac{p-1}{\chi}\left[1+\frac{(2-p)_{+}}{p-1}p^{\prime}\right]}\Bigg\}=:\widetilde{R}_{o},

so that the preceding estimates are valid. Moreover, with this choice of
\tilde{\delta}
, the preceding inequality becomes

\displaystyle\|\nabla u

\displaystyle-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\Big[\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}+R^{\frac{n}{p}-1}\Tail\big(u-(u)_{R};B_{R}\big)\Big]+\frac{c\,R^{\varepsilon}}{\delta^{\frac{1}{\gamma}+\frac{(\gamma+1)(2-p)_{+}}{\gamma(p-1)}p^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Noting that the exponent

\frac{1}{\gamma}+\frac{(\gamma+1)(2-p)_{+}}{\gamma(p-1)}p^{\prime}

depends only on
p
,
s
,
\chi
, and
\tilde{\alpha}
, we thus obtain the comparison estimate with this exponent.
Finally, by a suitable redefinition of the parameter
\gamma
, the comparison estimate holds uniformly for all
p>1
.
∎

6.
Proof of the gradient estimate

6.1.
Choice of Parameters

Let
s\in(0,1)
with
sp^{\prime}>1
and

r\in\bigg(\alpha p\,,\,\frac{n}{(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}\bigg),

where
\alpha
is defined in (
3.1
). Next, we set

q=\frac{rn(p-1)}{n-r(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}>p\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu=\frac{r}{q}=\frac{n}{(p-1)\,[\,n+q(sp^{\prime}-1)\,]},

where the inequality
q>p
follows from the fact that
q
is monotonically increasing with respect to
r
and the fact that
r>\alpha p
.
We now specify the choice of the parameter
\tilde{\alpha}
.
In the case
s\in(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}},s_{o}]
we have

\alpha=\frac{n}{(p-1)\,[\,n+p(sp^{\prime}-1)\,]}<\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{p-1}\,,\,\frac{n}{p(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}\bigg\}.

Since the mapping

(1,\infty)\ni q\longmapsto\frac{nq}{(p-1)[\,n+q(sp^{\prime}-1)\,]}

is increasing, the assumption
q>p
implies

\mu q>\alpha p\geq 1.

In the case
s\in(s_{o},1)
we have

\alpha=\frac{1}{p}<\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{p-1}\,,\,\frac{n}{p(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}\bigg\}.

We additionally assume that

q>\frac{n(p-1)}{n-(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}=\frac{n(p-1)}{n-1+p(1-s)},

which guarantees that
\mu q>1=\alpha p
.

Hence, in any case we may choose

(6.1)

\tilde{\alpha}\in\bigg(\alpha\,,\,\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{p-1},\frac{n}{p(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}\bigg\}\bigg)\quad\text{such that}\quad\tilde{\alpha}p<\mu q,

and define
\varepsilon
according to (
3.4
).
Note that this choice guarantees
\varepsilon>0
. In fact, in the case
s\in(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}},s_{o}]

we have

\varepsilon>sp^{\prime}-1-\frac{n}{p}\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha(p-1)}-1\Big)=0,

while in the case
s\in(s_{o},1)

we have

\varepsilon>sp^{\prime}-1-\frac{n}{p}\Big(\frac{p}{p-1}-1\Big)=sp^{\prime}-1-\frac{n}{p(p-1)}>0.

Moreover, the condition
\tilde{\alpha}<\frac{n}{p(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}
implies that

\varepsilon<sp^{\prime}-1-\frac{n}{p}\bigg[\frac{p(sp^{\prime}-1)}{n}-1\bigg]=\frac{n}{p}.

Recall that
r=\mu q
. Then, we can show that for every
f\in L^{\mu q}(B_{R})
it holds

(6.2)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{R})}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\frac{n\mu p}{\,n-\varepsilon p\,}}(B_{R})}^{\mu}\|f\|_{L^{\mu q}(B_{R})}^{\frac{\mu q(sp^{\prime}-1)}{n}}.

The estimate follows from Hölder’s inequality and can be interpreted
as an interpolation between the spaces

L^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}(B_{R})
and
L^{\mu q}(B_{R})
. Since the exponents involved are rather intricate, we spell out the
argument, including the application of Hölder’s inequality and the
explicit computation of the corresponding exponents. By Hölder’s inequality applied with the conjugate exponents

\frac{1}{1-\theta}
and
\frac{1}{\theta}
, where
\theta\in(0,1)

will be chosen later, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{\tilde{\alpha}p}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{\tilde{\alpha}p-\theta\mu q}|f|^{\theta\mu q}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}p}}

(6.3)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{\frac{\tilde{\alpha}p-\theta\mu q}{1-\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg]^{\frac{1-\theta}{\tilde{\alpha}p}}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{\mu q}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg]^{\frac{\theta}{\tilde{\alpha}p}}.

We now choose

\theta:=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}p(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)}{n}.

Note that
\theta\in(0,1)
by the choice of
\tilde{\alpha}
and the assumption
sp^{\prime}>1
. We compute

\displaystyle\frac{1-\theta}{\tilde{\alpha}(p-1)}=\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}(p-1)}-\frac{p(sp^{\prime}-1)}{n}=1-\frac{\varepsilon p}{n}

and hence

1-\theta=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}(p-1)(n-\varepsilon p)}{n}.

In order to proceed, we need to compute the integrability exponent of
|f|

appearing in the first integral on the right-hand side. We obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\alpha}p-\theta\mu q}{1-\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{\tilde{\alpha}(p-1)(n-\varepsilon p)}\bigg[\tilde{\alpha}p-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}p(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)\mu q}{n}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{n-\varepsilon p}\frac{n-(p-1)(sp^{\prime}-1)\mu q}{p-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}\bigg[\frac{n}{\mu(p-1)}-q(sp^{\prime}-1)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}>0,

since
\varepsilon<\frac{n}{p}
. In particular, this also implies that the exponent
\tilde{\alpha}p-\theta\mu q
of
|f|
is positive. Inserting the definition of
\theta
, the value of
1-\theta
computed above and the preceding identity into (
6.1
), yields the claim (
6.2
).

\displaystyle\leq c\,2^{-\ell sp^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{R}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{n}{p-1}}R\lambda_{o}

\displaystyle=c\,2^{-\ell sp^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{R}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{n}{p-1}+1}(r_{2}-r_{1})\lambda_{o}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\underbrace{2^{\ell}2^{-\ell sp^{\prime}}}_{\leq 1}\underbrace{\Big(\frac{2^{7}R}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{n}{p-1}+1}}_{={\sf B}}\lambda_{o}\varrho_{i}

\displaystyle\leq c\,{\sf B}\lambda_{o}\varrho_{i}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\lambda\varrho_{i}.

Using Poincaré’s inequality, (
6.10
) and the assumption
sp^{\prime}>1
, we estimate

\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}({\bm{\mathsf{T}}}_{2,k})^{\tau}\right]^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq c\lambda\varrho_{i}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}2^{-k\tau(sp^{\prime}-1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq c\lambda\varrho_{i}.

Combining the above estimates, we obtain

(6.12)

\mathrm{Tail}\big(u-(u)_{B_{i}^{(6)}};B_{i}^{(6)}\big)\leq c\,\lambda\varrho_{i}.

Substituting the preceding inequalities into the right-hand side of the
above estimate for
{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}\leq c\bigg[\delta+\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}\mathsf{M}^{p}}\bigg]|B_{i}|\lambda^{p}.

The estimate holds for all
\delta\in(0,1]
.

To estimate the term
{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}
in (
6.4
), we apply
Lemma
4.2
to
v_{i}
on
B_{i}^{(6)}
. For the resulting tail term we recall that

v_{i}=u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{i}^{(6)}
, which allows us to apply
Lemma
2.20
. In this way, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{(\mathsf{A}\lambda)^{\theta-p}}|B_{i}|\left[\varrho_{i}^{-p(1-s)-n}[v_{i}]_{W^{s,p}(B_{i}^{(5)})}^{p}+\varrho_{i}^{-p}\mathrm{Tail}\big(v_{i}-(v_{i})_{B_{i}^{(4)}};B_{i}^{(4)}\big)^{p}\right]^{\frac{\theta}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{(\mathsf{A}\lambda)^{\theta-p}}|B_{i}|\big[{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}_{1}+{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}_{2}+{\bm{\mathsf{T}}}_{1}+{\bm{\mathsf{T}}}_{2}\big]^{\frac{\theta}{p}},

where
c=c(n,p,s,\theta)>0
and we have set

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}_{1}:=\varrho_{i}^{-(1-s)p-n}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{i}^{(5)})}^{p},\qquad{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}_{2}:=\varrho_{i}^{-p(1-s)-n}[v_{i}-u_{i}]_{W^{s,p}(B_{i}^{(5)})}^{p}

and

{\bm{\mathsf{T}}}_{1}:=\varrho_{i}^{-p}\mathrm{Tail}\big(u-(u)_{B_{i}^{(4)}};B_{i}^{(4)}\big)^{p},\qquad{\bm{\mathsf{T}}}_{2}:=\varrho_{i}^{-p-n}\int_{B_{i}^{(6)}}|u-v_{i}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The first term can be estimated by means of Lemma
2.10

and inequality (
6.10
). Indeed, we have

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}_{1}\leq c\,\varrho_{i}^{-n}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{i}^{(5)})}^{p}\leq c\,\lambda^{p}.

Moreover, applying Lemma
2.18
with

(x_{o},r,y_{o},R)=(x_{i},2^{4}\varrho_{i},x_{i},2^{6}\varrho_{i})
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{T}}}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\varrho_{i}^{-p}\mathrm{Tail}\big(u-(u)_{B_{i}^{(6)}};B_{i}^{(6)}\big)^{p}+c\,\varrho_{i}^{-p}-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i}^{(6)}}\big|u-(u)_{B_{i}^{(6)}}\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,\varrho_{i}^{-p}\mathrm{Tail}\big(u-(u)_{B_{i}^{(6)}};B_{i}^{(6)}\big)^{p}+c-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i}^{(6)}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,\lambda^{p},

where in the second inequality we used Poincaré’s inequality,
while the last estimate follows from
(
6.10
) and (
6.12
).
From the comparison estimate in Lemma
4.1
,
applied with
\delta=1
, we infer

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}_{2}+{\bm{\mathsf{T}}}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\varrho_{i}^{-(1-s)p-n}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{i}^{(6)})}+c\,\varrho_{i}^{\varepsilon p-n}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{i}^{(6)})}^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq c-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i}^{(6)}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+c\,\frac{\lambda^{p}}{\mathsf{M}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\lambda^{p}.

Here, the second inequality follows from Lemma
2.10
,
while the last estimate is a consequence of the definition of

\varepsilon
in (
3.4
) together with
(
6.10
).
Moreover, we used that
\varrho_{i}\leq 1
and
\mathsf{M}\geq 1
in the application
of Lemma
4.1
and in the passage to the final line.
Overall, we have shown that

{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}\leq\frac{c}{\mathsf{A}^{\theta-p}}\lambda^{p}|B_{i}|,

for a constant
c=c(n,p,s,C_{o},C_{1},\theta)>0
.
Combining this estimate with the bound for
{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}
, we conclude that

(6.13)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}^{(3)}\cap\left\{|\nabla u|>\mathsf{A}\lambda\right\}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\leq c\,{\sf Q}\,\lambda^{p}|B_{i}|,

where

(6.14)

{\sf Q}:=\delta+\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}\mathsf{M}^{p}}+\frac{1}{\mathsf{A}^{\theta-p}}.

6.5.
Estimates on super-level sets

We estimate the second term on the left-hand side of (
6.9
). By the definition of
\varepsilon
in (
3.4
) and Young’s inequality with exponents
\frac{n}{\varepsilon p}
and
\frac{n}{n-\varepsilon p}
, we have

\displaystyle{\sf M}^{p}|B_{i}|^{\frac{p}{n}(sp^{\prime}-1)}

\displaystyle\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{i}}|f|^{\tilde{\alpha}p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}(p-1)}}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{M}^{p}|B_{i}|^{\frac{\varepsilon p}{n}-1}\|f\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{i})}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n}}\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n}}\mathsf{M}^{p}|B_{i}|^{\frac{\varepsilon p}{n}-1}\|f\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda^{p}+4^{\frac{\varepsilon p}{n-\varepsilon p}}|B_{i}|^{-1}\Big[\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n}}\mathsf{M}^{p}\|f\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{i})}\Big]^{\frac{n}{n-\varepsilon p}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda^{p}+|B_{i}|^{-1}\Big[4^{\frac{\varepsilon p}{n}}\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n}}\mathsf{M}^{p}\Big]^{\frac{n}{n-\varepsilon p}}\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{i})}^{\frac{np^{\prime}}{n-\varepsilon p}}.

By the interpolation inequality (
6.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{\alpha}p}(B_{i})}^{\frac{np^{\prime}}{n-\varepsilon p}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{F}^{\frac{p(sp^{\prime}-1)}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{i}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

\mathsf{F}:=\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{\mu q}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Combining the previous estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle{\sf M}^{p}|B_{i}|^{\frac{p}{n}(sp^{\prime}-1)}

\displaystyle\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{i}}|f|^{\tilde{\alpha}p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda^{p}+|B_{i}|^{-1}\mathsf{\lambda}^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n-\varepsilon p}}\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{i}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

\mathsf{K}:=\big[4^{\varepsilon p}\mathsf{F}^{p(sp^{\prime}-1)}\big]^{\frac{1}{np}}\mathsf{M}.

We insert this inequality into (
6.9
) and decompose the domain of integration into the super-level sets
B_{i}\cap\{|\nabla u|>\tfrac{1}{8}\lambda\}
and
B_{i}\cap\{|f|^{\mu}>\frac{1}{8\sf{K}}\lambda\}

and their complements. On the corresponding sub-level sets,
u
and
f
are estimated by their upper bounds. Consequently,

\displaystyle|B_{i}|\lambda^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{i}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+|B_{i}|^{\frac{p}{n}(sp^{\prime}-1)+1}{\sf M}^{p}\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i}}|f|^{\tilde{\alpha}p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{\tilde{\alpha}(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda^{p}|B_{i}|+\int_{B_{i}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n-\varepsilon p}}\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{i}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda^{p}|B_{i}|

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+\int_{B_{i}\cap\{|\nabla u|>\lambda/8\}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n-\varepsilon p}}\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{i}\cap\{|f|^{\mu}>\lambda/(8\mathsf{K})\}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+\int_{B_{i}\cap\{|\nabla u|\leq\lambda/8\}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n-\varepsilon p}}\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{i}\cap\{|f|^{\mu}\leq\lambda/(8\mathsf{K})\}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{1}{8^{p}}+\tfrac{1}{8^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}}\Big]|B_{i}|\lambda^{p}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{i}\cap\{|\nabla u|>\lambda/4\}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{i}\cap\{|f|^{\mu}>\lambda/(8\mathsf{K})\}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x.

On the right-hand side the pre-factor of
|B_{i}|\lambda^{p}
is less than
\frac{1}{2}
. Absorbing

\tfrac{1}{2}|B_{i}|\lambda^{p}

into the left-hand side and inserting the resulting estimate into (
6.13
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}^{(3)}\cap\left\{|\nabla u|>\mathsf{A}\lambda\right\}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\,{\sf Q}\bigg[\int_{B_{i}\cap\{|\nabla u|>\lambda/4\}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{i}\cap\{|f|^{\mu}>\lambda/(8\mathsf{K})\}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg].

Since
\{B_{i}^{(3)}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
covers the super-level set
\bm{E}(\lambda,r_{1})
, and hence
\bm{E}(\mathsf{A}\lambda,r_{1})
, and the balls
B_{i}
are pairwise disjoint and contained in
B_{r_{2}}
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{E}(\mathsf{A}\lambda,r_{1})}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{B_{i}^{(3)}\cap\left\{|\nabla u|>\mathsf{A}\lambda\right\}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\,{\sf Q}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\bigg[\int_{B_{i}\cap\{|\nabla u|>\lambda/4\}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\;+\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{i}\cap\{|f|^{\mu}>\lambda/(8\mathsf{K})\}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\,{\sf Q}\bigg[\int_{\bm{E}(\lambda/4,r_{2})}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{r_{2}}\cap\{|f|^{\mu}>\lambda/(8\mathsf{K})\}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg].

Instead of
\bm{E}(\lambda,r_{1})
, we consider, for
k\geq{\sf B}\lambda_{o}
, the set

\bm{E}_{k}(\lambda,r_{1}):=\Big\{x\in B_{r_{1}}:x\text{ is a Lebesgue point of }|\nabla u|\text{ and }|\nabla u|_{k}(x)>\lambda\Big\},

where
|\nabla u|_{k}:=\min\{|\nabla u|,k\}
denotes the truncation of
|\nabla u|
at height
k
.
We rewrite the last inequality in terms of
\bm{E}_{k}
and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{E}_{k}(\mathsf{A}\lambda,r_{1})}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\,{\sf Q}\bigg[\int_{\bm{E}_{k}(\lambda/4,r_{2})}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{r_{2}}\cap\{|f|^{\mu}>\lambda/(4\mathsf{K})\}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg].

Multiplying the previous inequality by
\lambda^{q-p-1}
and integrating over
({\sf B}\lambda_{o},\infty)
with respect to
\lambda
gives

\displaystyle\int_{{\sf B}\lambda_{o}}^{\infty}\lambda^{q-p-1}\int_{\bm{E}_{k}(\mathsf{A}\lambda,r_{1})}|\nabla u|_{k}^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\,\>\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq c\,{\sf Q}\big[{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}+{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}]

where

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\sf B}\lambda_{o}}^{\infty}\lambda^{q-p-1}\int_{\bm{E}_{k}(\lambda/4,r_{2})}|\nabla u|_{k}^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}\lambda,

and

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}

\displaystyle:=\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{{\sf B}\lambda_{o}}^{\infty}\lambda^{q-p-1}\lambda^{-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{r_{2}}\cap\{|f|^{\mu}>\lambda/(8\mathsf{K})\}}|f|^{\frac{n\mu p}{n-\varepsilon p}}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Applying a Fubini-type argument to the integral on the left-hand side yields

\displaystyle\int_{{\sf B}\lambda_{o}}^{\infty}\lambda^{q-p-1}\int_{\bm{E}_{k}(\mathsf{A}\lambda,r_{1})}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\>\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{q-p}\bigg[\mathsf{A}^{p-q}\int_{B_{r_{1}}}|\nabla u|_{k}^{q-p}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x-({\sf B}\lambda_{o})^{q-p}\int_{B_{r_{1}}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg].

The terms on the right-hand side are estimated similarly. For
{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}
, one has

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4^{q-p}}{q-p}\int_{B_{r_{2}}}|\nabla u|_{k}^{q-p}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x,

and for
{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}
, noting that
\tilde{\alpha}p<\mu q
implies
q>\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}
, we find

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}{\bm{\mathsf{I}}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{K}^{\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\frac{(8\mathsf{K})^{q-\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}}{q-\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\int_{B_{r_{2}}}|f|^{\mu q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x=\frac{8^{q-\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}}{q-\frac{np}{n-\varepsilon p}}\mathsf{K}^{q}\int_{B_{r_{2}}}|f|^{\mu q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x.

Combining these estimates and recalling that
\mu q=r
results in

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{1}}}

\displaystyle|\nabla u|_{k}^{q-p}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{\ast}\mathsf{A}^{q-p}{\sf Q}\int_{B_{r_{2}}}|\nabla u|_{k}^{q-p}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+(\mathsf{A}{\sf B}\lambda_{o})^{q-p}\int_{B_{r_{1}}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+c_{\ast}\mathsf{A}^{q-p}{\sf Q}\mathsf{M}^{q}\mathsf{F}^{\frac{q(sp^{\prime}-1)}{n}}\int_{B_{r_{2}}}|f|^{r}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x,

where
{\sf Q}
is defined in (
6.14
).

The strategy is now to absorb the first term on the right-hand side into the left-hand side. This requires careful choices of the parameters in
{\sf Q}
, i.e. of
\theta
,
\mathsf{A}
,
\delta
, and
\mathsf{M}
.
We start by choosing
\theta=2q
. Next, we choose
\mathsf{A}
large enough such that

\frac{c_{\ast}}{\mathsf{A}^{q}}=\tfrac{1}{6}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathsf{A}=(6c_{\ast})^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Subsequently, we choose
\delta

small enough such that

c_{\ast}\mathsf{A}^{q-p}\delta\equiv c_{\ast}(6c_{\ast})^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\delta=\tfrac{1}{6}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\delta=(6c_{\ast})^{\frac{p}{q}-2}.

This also fixes
\widetilde{R}_{o}
in dependence on
n,p,q,s,C_{o},C_{1},\chi,R_{o}
. Finally we choose
\mathsf{M}
large enough such that

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\ast}\mathsf{A}^{q-p}}{\delta^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}\mathsf{M}^{p}}\equiv\frac{c_{\ast}(6c_{\ast})^{\frac{q-p}{q}+\frac{p}{\gamma}(2-\frac{p}{q})}}{\mathsf{M}^{p}}=\tfrac{1}{6}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathsf{M}^{p}=(6c_{\ast})^{(2-\frac{p}{q})(1+\frac{p}{\gamma})}.

These choices ensure that, on the one hand,
c_{\ast}\mathsf{A}^{q-p}{\sf Q}=\frac{1}{2}
, and on the other hand
\mathsf{M}
depends only on
n,p,q,s,C_{o},C_{1},\chi
(recall that
\gamma=\gamma(p,s,\chi,\tilde{\alpha})
and
\tilde{\alpha}=\tilde{\alpha}(n,p,q,s)
).
With the aforementioned choices, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{1}}}

\displaystyle|\nabla u|_{k}^{q-p}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r_{2}}}|\nabla u|_{k}^{q-p}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+(\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}\lambda_{o})^{q-p}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathsf{M}^{q}\left[\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{r}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{q(sp^{\prime}-1)}{n}}\int_{B_{r_{2}}}|f|^{r}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r_{2}}}|\nabla u|_{k}^{q-p}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c\,R^{\beta}\,\lambda_{o}^{q-p}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{\beta}}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+c\left[\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{r}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{1}{\mu(p-1)}},

where
\beta=(\frac{n}{p-1}+1)(q-p)
. To obtain the last line we also used the definition of
\sf B
in (
6.6
). Note that the last inequality holds for any
\frac{1}{2}R\leq r_{1}<r_{2}\leq R
. Therefore, the standard iteration lemma
[
29
, Lemma 6.1]
can be applied. The application results in

\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}|\nabla u|_{k}^{q-p}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\leq c\,\lambda_{o}^{q-p}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+c\left[\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{r}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{1}{\mu(p-1)}}.

Using Fatou’s lemma we pass to the limit
k\to\infty
and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,\lambda_{o}^{q-p}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x+c\left[\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{r}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{1}{\mu(p-1)}}.

Recalling the definition of
\lambda_{o}
from (
6.2
), passing to the mean values and using Young’s inequality with exponents
\frac{q}{p}
and
\frac{q}{q-p}
, and Hölder’s inequality together with the fact that
\tilde{\alpha}p<\mu q=r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}+cR^{sp^{\prime}-1}\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{R}}|f|^{r}\,\>\!\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{\mu q(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+\frac{c}{R}\,\mathrm{Tail}\big(u-(u)_{R};B_{R}\big).

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

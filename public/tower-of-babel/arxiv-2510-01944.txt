Uniform-in-time convergence bounds for Persistent Contrastive Divergence Algorithms
Paul Felix Valsecchi Oliva, Ö. Deniz Akyildiz, Andrew Duncan

Abstract
We propose a continuous-time formulation of persistent contrastive divergence (PCD) for maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) of unnormalised densities. Our approach expresses PCD as a coupled, multiscale system of stochastic differential equations (SDEs) , which perform optimisation of the parameter and sampling of the associated parametrised density, simultaneously. From this novel formulation, we are able to derive explicit bounds for the error between the PCD iterates and the MLE solution for the model parameter. This is made possible by deriving uniform-in-time (UiT) bounds for the difference in moments between the multiscale system and the averaged regime. An efficient implementation of the continuous-time scheme is introduced, leveraging a class of explicit, stable intregators, stochastic orthogonal Runge–Kutta Chebyshev (S–ROCK) , for which we provide explicit error estimates in the long-time regime. This leads to a novel method for training energy-based models (EBMs) with explicit error guarantees.

Background and preliminary results

Let
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}^{\star}_{\text{pop}}\in\operatornamewithlimits{argsup}_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\theta}}}\mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}\left[\log p_{\theta}(Y)\right].

Let

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}^{\star}\in\operatornamewithlimits{argsup}_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\theta}}}\mathbb{E}_{p^{M}_{\text{data}}}\left[\log p_{\theta}(Y)\right]=\operatornamewithlimits{argsup}_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\theta}}}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\log p_{\theta}(y_{j}).

(4)

(5)

We observe that the gradient of the potential

\nabla_{\theta}V(\theta)=-\int\nabla_{\theta}E(\theta,x)p_{\theta}(\mathrm{d}x)+\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\nabla_{\theta}E(\theta,y_{j}).

Main Results

The goal of this paper is to characterise the difference in behaviour between numerical schemes based on

, and the
MLE

and the error between

and its numerical integrators can combined to obtain the difference between a large class of

To approach this problem we look to some new results presented in

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}F\leq e^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{k}t}F+\tilde{\gamma}^{\theta}_{k}\leq 2e^{-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{k}}{2}t}F+\mathbbm{1}_{t>T}\tilde{\gamma}^{\theta}_{k}.

This result follows from the fact that the inequality holds for
t=T
and so must hold for all previous times. In the following, we will suppose that
k=2m_{x}
and that
T
is chosen for the case
k=2
. This choice is due to the fact that
T
decreases for larger values of
k
. Further, suppose now that the fixed
c
is equal to
\|\theta\|^{m_{\theta}}+1
.

Let us now turn our attention to the case where
t>T
, and in particular, recall, Thm.
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
. Combining these we obtain,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi(\theta,z)-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi(\theta,z^{\prime})\|\leq

\displaystyle 4|\nabla\phi|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}W_{2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{*}z,\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{*}z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\times(1+\|\theta\|^{m_{\theta}}+(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\|z\|^{2m_{x}})^{\frac{1}{2}}+(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\|z^{\prime}\|^{2m_{x}})^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4|\nabla\phi|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}\sqrt{\frac{3\tilde{c}_{\theta}(1+\tilde{\gamma}^{\theta}_{2})}{\tilde{r}}}e^{-\frac{\tilde{r}}{6}(t-T)}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(1+\|z\|^{2}+\|z^{\prime}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\times(1+\|\theta\|^{m_{\theta}}+(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\|z\|^{2m_{x}})^{\frac{1}{2}}+(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\|z^{\prime}\|^{2m_{x}})^{\frac{1}{2}}).

We now take advantage of (
20
) to observe that,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(1+\|z\|^{2m_{x}}+\|z^{\prime}\|^{2m_{x}})\leq

\displaystyle 2e^{-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{k}}{2}T}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t-T}(1+\|z\|^{2m_{x}}+\|z^{\prime}\|^{2m_{x}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2e^{-\frac{2\tilde{r}}{3}T}(1+\|z\|^{2m_{x}}+\|z^{\prime}\|^{2m_{x}}),

following from the positivity of the semi-group and the fact that
\tilde{r}\leq\tilde{\alpha}_{k}
. Let us further recall that,
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(1+\|z\|^{2}+\|z^{\prime}\|^{2})\leq 1+2\tilde{\gamma}^{\theta}_{2}
, to obtain,

\|\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi(\theta,z)-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi(\theta,z^{\prime})\|\leq 8|\nabla\phi|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}\sqrt{\frac{3\tilde{c}_{\theta}}{\tilde{r}}}(1+2\tilde{\gamma}^{\theta}_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{\tilde{r}}{6}t}(1+\|z\|^{m_{x}}+(\mathbb{E}\|z^{\prime}\|^{2m_{x}})^{\frac{1}{2}}).

(21)

The unconventional choice for the right hand side will become apparent later in the proof, when we will integrate against
z^{\prime}
.

Finally, we may “stitch” the two time periods,
t<T
and
t\geq T
, together:

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi(\theta,z)-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi(\theta,z^{\prime})\|\leq

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{t\leq T}|\phi|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}F(z)+\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}F(z^{\prime}))+\mathbbm{1}_{t>T}\|\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi(\theta,z)-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi(\theta,z^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 9\|\phi\|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}\sqrt{\frac{3\tilde{c}_{\theta}}{\tilde{r}}}(1+2\tilde{\gamma}^{\theta}_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{\tilde{r}}{6}t}(1+\|z\|^{m_{x}}+\|\theta\|^{m_{\theta}}+(\mathbb{E}\|z^{\prime}\|^{2m_{x}})^{\frac{1}{2}}).

To complete the proof we consider the case where
z^{\prime}
is initialised as
p_{\theta}^{\otimes N}
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

For all
t\geq 0
and
\phi\in C^{2}_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}
satisfying

(

A
p

)

Let us begin by considering

(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{z})\|\nabla_{z}f_{t}\|^{2}=2\langle\nabla_{z}f_{t},\nabla_{z}\mathcal{G}_{z}f_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{z}\nabla_{z}f_{t}\rangle-2\|\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}\|_{F}^{2}.

Now,

\nabla_{z}\mathcal{G}_{z}f_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{z}\nabla_{z}f_{t}=-\nabla^{2}_{z}\bar{E}\nabla_{z}f_{t},

(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{z})\|\nabla_{z}f_{t}\|^{2}\leq-2\langle\nabla_{z}f_{t},\nabla_{z}^{2}\bar{E}\nabla_{z}f_{t}\rangle-2\|\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}\|_{F}^{2}.

(22)

(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{z})\|\nabla^{2}_{z}f_{t}\|^{2}=2\Tr(\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}(\nabla_{z}^{2}\mathcal{G}_{z}f_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{z}\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t})^{\top})-2\|\nabla_{z}^{3}f_{t}\|_{F}^{2}.

\nabla_{z}^{2}\mathcal{G}_{z}f_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{z}\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}=-\nabla_{z}^{3}\bar{E}\nabla_{z}f_{t}-\nabla_{z}^{2}\bar{E}\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}-(\nabla_{z}^{2}\bar{E}\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t})^{\top},

(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{z})\|\nabla^{2}_{z}f_{t}\|^{2}\leq-2\Tr(\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}(\nabla_{z}^{3}\bar{E}\nabla_{z}f_{t}+2\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}\nabla_{z}^{2}\bar{E})^{\top})-2\|\nabla_{z}^{3}f_{t}\|_{F}^{2}.

(23)

Now note that by combining (
22

23

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{z})(\|\nabla_{z}f_{t}\|^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}_{z}f_{t}\|^{2})\leq

\displaystyle-2\big(\langle\nabla_{z}f_{t},\nabla_{z}^{2}\bar{E}\nabla_{z}f_{t}\rangle+\Tr(\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}(\nabla_{z}^{3}\bar{E}\nabla_{z}f_{t})^{\top})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+2\Tr(\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}\nabla^{2}_{z}f_{t}\nabla_{z}^{2}\bar{E})+\|\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\nabla_{z}^{3}f_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\tilde{\kappa}\big(\|\nabla_{z}f_{t}\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\big)

where the last line follows from

(

~
A

κ

)

.

By Prop. 3.4 in
[
8
]
, this gives us the following bound on the semi-group’s time derivative,

\partial_{s}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t-s}\big(\|\nabla_{z}f_{t}\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}\|^{2}\big)\leq-2\tilde{\kappa}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t-s}\big(\|\nabla_{z}f_{t}\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\big).

Applying Gronwall’s Lemma, we observe,

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t-s}\big(\|\nabla_{z}f_{t}\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{t}\|^{2}\big)\leq e^{-2\tilde{\kappa}s}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\big(\|\nabla_{z}f_{0}\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{0}\|_{F}^{2}\big)

(24)

and let us also recall that by

p

)

, Lemma

and the positivity of the Markov semi-group,

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\big(\|\nabla_{z}f_{0}\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{z}^{2}f_{0}\|_{F}^{2}\big)\leq 2(|\nabla_{z}\phi|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}^{2}+|\nabla_{z}^{2}\phi|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}^{2})(1+\tilde{\gamma}^{\theta}_{2m_{x}}+\|\theta\|^{2m_{\theta}}+\|z\|^{2m_{x}}).

Theorem 4.8
.

(Strong Exponential Stability for Derivative Estimates)
The semi-group
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}

(

~

κ

Let us begin by observing that, by

(

A
p

)

\displaystyle\left\|(\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{G}_{z})\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{s}\phi(\theta,z)\right\|

We now introduce the transfer formula, established in Remark 3.3

(

~
A

μ

)

(

~
A

μ

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi)(\theta,z)-\lim_{t\to\infty}

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi)(\theta,z)

\displaystyle\,(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\nabla_{\theta}\phi)(\theta,z)-\int\nabla_{\theta}\phi(\theta,z)p_{\theta}^{\otimes N}(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle\|\text{II}\|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t-s}-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\infty})\left(\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{G}_{z}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{s}\phi\right)\right\|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{18}{\tilde{\kappa}}\sqrt{\frac{3\tilde{c}_{\theta}}{\tilde{r}}}|\nabla^{2}\bar{E}|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}(1+2\tilde{\gamma}^{\theta}_{m_{x}})^{\frac{5}{2}}e^{-\tilde{\kappa}t}(1+\|\theta\|^{2m_{\theta}}+\|z\|^{2m_{x}}),

from a simple application of (

\displaystyle\|\text{III}\|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\int\left\|\left(\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{G}_{z}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{s}\phi\right)(\theta,z)\right\|p_{\theta}^{\otimes N}(\mathrm{d}z)\mathrm{d}s

where the last line follows from Lemma

. Hence, combining these results we get,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi)(\theta,z)-\lim_{t\to\infty}\nabla_{\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\phi)(\theta,z)\|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{18}{\tilde{\kappa}}\sqrt{\frac{3\tilde{c}_{\theta}}{\tilde{r}}}(1+|\nabla^{2}\bar{E}|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}})\|\nabla\phi\|_{m_{\theta},m_{x}}e^{-\tilde{\kappa}t}

\displaystyle\times(1+2\tilde{\gamma}^{\theta}_{2m_{x}})^{\frac{5}{2}}(1+\|\theta\|^{2m_{\theta}}+\|z\|^{2m_{x}}).

This last result follows from the fact that typically

, or
\tilde{\kappa}

∎

To verify the efficacy of the proposed discretisation we conduct a series of numerical simulations to compare the proposed multiscale system (

8.1
Synthetic Dataset

We begin by considering a simple distribution in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

x=(x_{1},x_{2})
,

p(\mathrm{d}x)\propto\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}^{2}+(2x_{2}-x_{1}^{2})^{2})\right)\mathrm{d}x.

This variant is chosen as it can be quickly and accurately sampled from, as

9
Discussion

In this paper we introduced a novel continuous-time, diffusion-based, framework for the analysis of
PCD
schemes. Through this lens, we introduce a weak
UiT
error bound for Langevin-based
PCD
schemes, exploiting recent results from
[
9
]
. With this characterisation of
PCD
, we are able to directly and explicitly bound the error between
PCD
analogues and the
MLE
gradient flow. Further, we demonstrated how this continuous-time perspective paves the way to novel
PCD
algorithms, which exploit explicit time discretisations of
SDEs
, empirically demonstrating improvements in training stability. To this end, we introduced a
S–ROCK
discretisation and have shown a novel ergodic bound for the scheme, to obtain a
UiT
bound for the numerical integrator’s error.

Due to the need for strong exponential stability
[
9
,
7
,
34
]
, our theory requires a restrictive set of assumptions. However, we expect such bounds to hold outside this regime, as has been demonstrated in the numerical experiments. Future work will explore how these assumptions can be weakened, for example leveraging the semigroup gradient bound estimates presented in
[
9
,
34
]
, which avoid

(

¯
A

κ

)

, perhaps at the cost of not having explicit constants.

This paper builds on a growing body of works which exploit multiscale dynamics for sampling and optimisation, particularly relevant to developing novel approaches in machine learning and computational statistics. We believe that the use of stabilised numerical integrators, as presented in this paper, further extend the applicability of such approaches, and hope that this framework will continue to motivate the exploration of such schemes.

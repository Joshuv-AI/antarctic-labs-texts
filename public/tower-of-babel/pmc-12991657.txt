Effect of Palliative Treatment and Viddhakarma (Puncture Therapy in Ayurveda) in an Androgenetic Alopecia (Khalitya)—A Case Report
Authors: Rugaved R. Gudadhe, Gaurav R. Sawarkar, Amol M. Deshpande
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHair plays a significant role in personality and aesthetics, with androgenetic alopecia affecting up to 80% of males. This case study evaluates the efficacy of palliative treatment and Viddhakarma (Puncture Therapy in Ayurveda) in managing androgenetic alopecia (Khalitya). A 37-year-old man presented with hair loss and a mild burning sensation on the scalp for six to seven years, primarily in the vertex and frontal regions. Treatment included Viddhakarma, Nilibhringadi Taila application (Shiroabhyanga), and palliative medications for four months, with weekly monitoring. New hair roots appeared within two weeks, existing hair improved in quality, and warmth sensation normalized after a month. Significant regrowth was observed without adverse effects, highlighting the effectiveness of personalized Ayurvedic management for androgenetic alopecia.

PATIENT INFORMATION

A 37-year-old man came to OPD with chief complaints of hair loss with a mild burning sensation in the scalp for six to seven years. The hair loss is mainly on the vertex, frontal area. Only the occipital and temporal area maintains a significant amount of hairs.

HISTORY

The patient experienced gradual hair loss with a mild burning sensation on the scalp for six to seven years, primarily in the vertex and frontal regions. Persistent burning and warmth at the hair roots led to stress and reduced self-confidence. Despite undergoing treatments, he found no relief. He has no significant medical or surgical history but suffers from chronic constipation and recurrent stomatitis. A paternal family history of similar hair loss is noted.

His personal history includes a mixed diet with a preference for spicy and junk food, increased hunger, disturbed bowel habits with constipation, irregular sleep, and tobacco chewing with occasional smoking. He works in marketing, frequently traveling around 100 km daily by bike.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND FINDINGS

The patient experienced gradual hair loss along with a mild burning sensation on the scalp for six to seven years, a warmth sensation on the hair root of the frontal and vertex region of the head. The significant hairs are only on the temporal and occipital areas.

LOCAL EXAMINATION (SCALP)

The scalp was assessed with the Norwood–Hamilton scale of male pattern baldness. The hair loss is mainly in the frontal and vertex regions. The temporal and occipital region maintains a significant amount of hairs.

DIAGNOSTIC ASSESSMENT

The Norwood–Hamilton scale classifies male pattern baldness into seven types. Type I shows no hair loss, while Types II and III indicate progressive frontal hairline recession, with Type III-Vertex affecting both the frontal and vertex regions. Types IV to VI depict worsening hair loss, leading to Type VII, where only the occipital scalp retains significant hair. Based on this scale, the patient falls under Type VI, with extensive frontal and vertex hair loss.

DIAGNOSIS

The patient presents with alopecia, with differential diagnoses including male androgenetic alopecia (Khalitya) and alopecia areata (Indralupta). Male androgenetic alopecia involves progressive hair thinning in a specific pattern, while alopecia areata causes sudden patchy hair loss with possible nail changes. Clinical evaluation confirms male androgenetic alopecia (Khalitya), classified as type VI on the Norwood–Hamilton scale.

THERAPEUTIC INTERVENTIONS

The treatment plan includes two main interventions: Purification Therapy and Drug Therapy. Purification Therapy involves Puncture Therapy (Viddhakarma) with a 26G needle, conducted weekly for eight sessions.

Drug Therapy consists of:

Chitrakadi Vati—two tablets twice daily with lukewarm water for seven days.Asthiposhak Vati—twice daily for 15 days.Aaragwadh Kapila Vati—once nightly for 15 days with lukewarm water.Panchatikta Ghrit—twice daily for 15 days with lukewarm water, followed by Panchatikta Ghrit Guggulu tablets—twice daily for 15 days.Rasayan Churna—once daily in the morning with honey, starting after seven days and continuing for four months.Nilibhringadi Taila—applied to the scalp every alternate day.

The patient was on regular follow-up and assessment once a week for four consecutive months. After two weeks, the patient starts new hair roots growing. And the quality of existing hair is increased. DISCUSSION

Alopecia (Khalitya) involves the vitiation of Vata, Pitta, Kapha, and Rakta, necessitating a treatment approach that balances these Doshas along with rejuvenation therapy (Rasayana) for hair regrowth. While normal hair loss is 50–100 strands daily with natural replacement, active management is required when this process fails. The patient’s history indicates Pitta-dominant Vata vitiation along with Kapha and Rakta imbalance, warranting both purification and drug therapy.

Purification Therapy: Bloodletting via Viddha Karma or cupping stimulates scalp circulation and regulates Pitta and Rakta.[1]

Drug Therapy:

It exhibits various pharmacological properties, including antioxidant, immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective effects.[8]

CONCLUSION

This case study aims to demonstrate the effective handling of an androgenetic alopecia (Khalitya) case. There is undoubtedly a massive need for more research, but this effort proves that, with the correct diagnosis and treatment plan, Ayurveda can be very helpful in managing the scenario. Long-lasting advantages can be anticipated by adding dietary and lifestyle-related guidance. The recovery in the given case was encouraging and justified some documentation.

Anti-pre-Novikov algebras, quasi-triangular and factorizable anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras
Qinxiu Sun, Xingyu Zeng

Abstract
. Firstly, we introduce a notion of anti-pre-Novikov algebras as a new framework for decomposing Novikov algebras. Anti- 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Novikov algebras are developed to provide an algebraic framework for constructing anti-pre-Novikov algebras. Secondly, we introduce a notion of anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras as the bialgebra structures corresponding to a double construction of symmetric quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras, which is characterized by certain matched pairs of Novikov algebras as well as the compatible anti-pre-Novikov algebras. The study of the coboundary case induces the anti-pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation (APN-YBE), whose skew-symmetric solutions yield coboundary anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras. The notion of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on anti-pre-Novikov algebras is studied to construct skew-symmetric solutions of the APN-YBE. Thirdly, we investigate quasi-triangular and factorizable anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras as a special class of coboundary anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras. The solutions of the APN-YBE whose symmetric parts are invariant give rise to a quasi-triangular anti-pre-Novikov bialgebra. Moreover, relative Rota-Baxter operators with weights are introduced to demonstrate solutions of the APN-YBE whose symmetric parts are invariant. Finally, we introduce a notion of quadratic Rota-Baxter anti-pre-Novikov algebras, which is one to one correspondence to a factorizable anti-pre-Novikov bialgebra.

Introduction

Novikov algebras emerged during the study of Hamiltonian operators in formal variational calculus (
[
13
,
29
]
)
and Poisson brackets of hydrodynamic type (
[

Explicitly, a Novikov algebra is a vector space
A
equipped with a binary operation

\circ
satisfying the following
conditions:

(1)

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\circ z-x\circ(y\circ z)=(y\circ x)\circ z-y\circ(x\circ z),

(2)

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\circ z=(x\circ z)\circ y,\quad x,y,z\in A.

Novikov algebras are a special class of pre-Lie algebras (also called left-symmetric algebras),
which are tightly connected with many fields in mathematics and physics such as affine manifolds and affine
structures on Lie groups
[
18
]
, convex homogeneous cones
[
27
]
, vertex algebras
[
2
,
6
]

and so on.

The notion of pre-Novikov algebras and quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras naturally appeared in the study of Novikov bialgebras
[
14
]
.
In detail, a pre-Novikov algebra is a vector space
A
together with
two binary operations
\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright:A\otimes A\rightarrow A

satisfying

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright(b\vartriangleright c)

\displaystyle=(a\circ b)\vartriangleright c+b\vartriangleright(a\vartriangleright c)-(b\circ a)\vartriangleright c,

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright(b\vartriangleleft c)

\displaystyle=(a\vartriangleright b)\vartriangleleft c+b\vartriangleleft(a\circ c)-(b\vartriangleleft a)\vartriangleleft c,

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\vartriangleright c

\displaystyle=(a\vartriangleright c)\vartriangleleft b,

\displaystyle(a\vartriangleleft b)\vartriangleleft c

\displaystyle=(a\vartriangleleft c)\vartriangleleft b,

for all
a,b,c\in A
, where
a\circ b=a\vartriangleleft b+a\vartriangleright b
.
The sum
\circ=\succ+\prec
gives a Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
.
More importantly, there is a Novikov algebra associated with a pre-Novikov algebra and pre-Novikov algebras can
give skew-symmetric solutions of Novikov Yang-Baxter equation and hence Novikov bialgebras
[
14
]
.
Furthermore, pre-Novikov algebras correspond to a class of left-symmetric conformal algebras
[
15
]
and there have
related connections between pre-Novikov algebras and Zinbiel algebras with a derivation (see
[
17
,
30
]
)

In the study of the underlying
algebraic structures for non-degenerate commutative 2-cocycles on Lie algebras, G. Liu and C. Bai
introduced a notion of anti-pre-Lie algebras
[
21
]
.
Later, Gao, Liu and Bai in
[
12
]
introduced a notion of anti-dendriform algebras. Anti-dendriform algebras still
have the property of splitting the associativity, but it is the negative left and right multiplication operators that compose the
bimodules of the sum associative algebras, instead of the left and right multiplication operators doing so for dendriform algebras.
Motivated by this work, we introduce anti-pre-Novikov algebras as the underlying algebraic
structures for symmetric quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras. Analogously, anti-pre-Novikov algebras split the operations.
However, in this case, it is the negative left and right multiplication operators that form the bimodules of the sum Novikov algebra,
in contrast to pre-Novikov algebras, where the (positive) left and right multiplication operators fulfill this role.

A bialgebraic structure consists of an algebra structure and a coalgebra structure coupled by
certain compatible conditions between the multiplication and comultiplication. In the early 1980s,
Drinfeld established Lie bialgebra theories in
[
9
]
, which are closely
related to the classical Yang-Baxter equation and play an
important role in the infinitesimalization of a quantum group. Based on Drinfeld’s work, V. Zhelyabin
introduced a notion of associative D-bialgebras
[
31
,
32
]
.
The associative analog of the Lie bialgebra
is antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras, which were developed by Aguiar
[
1
]
, which is equivalent
to double constructions of Frobenius algebras
[
4
]
. Subsequently,
analogous methods were extended to develop bialgebra theories for various algebraic structures, including
pre-Lie algebras
[
3
]
, Leibniz
algebras
[
26
]
, perm algebras
[
16
]
, Novikov algebras
[
14
]
,
3-Lie algebras
[
5
]
and pre-Novikov algebra
[
20
]
.
A Manin triple of Poisson algebras is equivalent to
a Poisson bialgebra
[
23
]
, which naturally fits into a framework to construct compatible Poisson
brackets in integrable systems. Transposed Poisson algebras are dual to Poisson algebras.
The Poisson bialgebra approach fails to yield a bialgebra theory for transposed Poisson algebras.
Alternatively,
Bai and Liu developed bialgebra theories for
anti-pre-Lie algebras, transposed Poisson algebras and anti-pre-Lie Poisson algebras
[
22
]

through the Manin triples
with respect to the commutative 2-cocycles on Lie algebras. In
[
25
]
, we explore the bialgebra theory for
anti-dendriform algebras, which is characterized by double constructions of associative algebras with
respect to the commutative Connes cocycles
and certain matched pairs of associative algebras as well as anti-dendriform algebras.

Under the context of Lie bialgebras, coboundary Lie bialgebras especially quasi-triangular Lie bialgebras play fundamental roles
in mathematical physics. Factorizable Lie bialgebras, a specialized subclass
of quasi-triangular Lie bialgebras, establish a crucial connection between classical
r
-matrices and specific factorization problems.
They find diverse applications in integrable systems, see
[
19
]
and references therein.
Recently, these results concerning factorizable and quasi-triangular structures have been successfully generalized to
antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras
[
24
]
, pre-Lie bialgebras
[
28
]
and Leibniz bialgebras
[
7
]

It is natural to investigate the bialgebra structures for anti-pre-Novikov algebras. This is another motivation
for writing this paper. Explicitly,
we introduce a notion of an anti-pre-Novikov bialgebra, which is interpreted in terms of a double construction
of symmetric quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras
and certain matched pairs of Novikov algebras.
The study of coboundary anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras leads to the introduction of the anti-pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter
equation (APN-YBE). Furthermore, quasi-triangular anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras and
factorizable anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras
as a special class of coboundary anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras
are studied, which ‌are induced by solutions of the APN-YBE without imposing skew-symmetry constraints.
The double space of any anti-pre-Novikov bialgebra
admits a factorizable anti-pre-Novikov bialgebra structure.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators with weights are introduced to characterize the solutions
of the APN-YBE whose symmetric parts are invariant.
Furthermore, we give a characterization of factorizable anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras
in terms of quadratic Rota-Baxter anti-pre-Novikov algebras.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2,
we introduce a notion of anti-pre-Novikov algebras and its representations.
The anti-
\mathcal{O}

In Section 3, we introduce a notion of anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras, which is equivalent to

certain matched pairs of Novikov algebras. The study of coboundary case leads to
the introduction of the APN-YBE, whose

The notion of
\mathcal{O}

a special class of quasi-triangular anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras.
We show that the double of an anti-pre-Novikov bialgebra is naturally a factorizable anti-pre-Novikov bialgebra.
In section 5, we introduce a notion of quadratic Rota-Baxter anti-pre-Novikov algebras, which corresponds to
a factorizable anti-pre-Novikov bialgebra.

All algebras are finite-dimensional, although many results still hold in the infinite-dimensional case.

2.
Anti-pre-Novikov algebras

We introduce a notion of anti-pre-Novikov algebras as a new approach of splitting operations,
whose negative left and right multiplication operators compose the bimodules of the
associated Novikov algebra. The notions of anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Novikov algebras are developed
to interpret anti-pre-Novikov algebras.

2.1.
Anti-pre-Novikov algebras and Representations

Let us begin to recall some basic knowledge on Novikov algebras.

A
representation (bimodule)
of a Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
is a triple
(V,l,r)
,
where
V
is a vector space and
l,r:A\rightarrow\text{End}_{\bf k}(V)
are linear maps satisfying

(3)

\displaystyle l(x\circ y-y\circ x)v=l(x)l(y)v-l(y)l(x)v,

(4)

\displaystyle l(x)r(y)v-r(y)l(x)v=r(x\circ y)v-r(y)r(x)v,

(5)

\displaystyle l(x\circ y)v=r(y)l(x)v,\ \ \ r(x)r(y)v=r(y)r(x)v,

for all
x,y\in A,\;v\in V.

By Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
), we have

(6)

\displaystyle l_{\star}(x\circ y-y\circ x)v=l_{\star}(x)l_{\star}(y)v-l_{\star}(y)l_{\star}(x)v,\ \ where\ \ l_{\star}=l_{\circ}+r_{\circ}.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
14
]

Let
(A,\circ)
and
(B,\bullet)
be Novikov algebras. If
(B,l_{A},r_{A})
is a representation
of
(A,\circ)
,
(A,l_{B},r_{B})
is a representation of
(B,\bullet)
and the following conditions are satisfied:

(7)

\displaystyle l_{B}(a)(x\circ y)=-l_{B}(l_{A}(x)a-r_{A}(x)a)y+(l_{B}(a)x-r_{B}(a)x)\circ y+r_{B}(r_{A}(y)a)x+x\circ(l_{B}(a)y),

(8)

\displaystyle r_{B}(a)(x\circ y-y\circ x)=r_{B}(l_{A}(y)a)x-r_{B}(l_{A}(x)a)y+x\circ(r_{B}(a)y)-y\circ(r_{B}(a)x),

(9)

\displaystyle l_{A}(x)(a\bullet b)=-l_{A}(l_{B}(a)x-r_{B}(a)x)b+(l_{A}(x)a-r_{A}(x)a)\bullet b+r_{A}(r_{B}(b)x)a+a\bullet(l_{A}(x)b),

(10)

\displaystyle r_{A}(x)(a\bullet b-b\bullet a)=r_{A}(l_{B}(b)x)a-r_{A}(l_{B}(a)x)b+a\bullet(r_{A}(x)b)-b\bullet(r_{A}(x)a),

(11)

\displaystyle(l_{B}(a)x)\circ y+l_{B}(r_{A}(x)a)y=(l_{B}(a)y)\circ x+l_{B}(r_{A}(y)a)x,

(12)

\displaystyle(r_{B}(a)x)\circ y+l_{B}(l_{A}(x)a)y=r_{B}(a)(x\circ y),

(13)

\displaystyle l_{A}(r_{B}(a)x)b+(l_{A}(x)a)\bullet b=l_{A}(r_{B}(b)x)a+(l_{A}(x)b)\bullet a,

(14)

\displaystyle l_{A}(l_{B}(a)x)b+(r_{A}(x)a)\bullet b=r_{A}(x)(a\bullet b),\quad x,y\in A,~a,b\in B,

then there is a Novikov algebra structure on the direct sum
A\oplus B
of the underlying vector spaces of
A
and
B
given by

\displaystyle(x+a)\cdot(y+b)=(x\circ y+l_{B}(a)y+r_{B}(b)x)+(a\bullet b+l_{A}(x)b+r_{A}(y)a),\;a,b\in A,\;x,y\in B.

(A,B,l_{A},r_{A},l_{B},r_{B})
satisfying the above conditions is called a
matched pair of Novikov algebras.

Conversely, any Novikov algebra that can be decomposed into a direct sum of
two Novikov subalgebras is obtained from a matched pair of Novikov algebras.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a vector space. An
anti-pre-Novikov algebra
is a vector space
A
together with two binary operations

\succ,\prec:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
satisfying

(15)

\displaystyle(x\circ y-y\circ x)\succ z=y\succ(x\succ z)-x\succ(y\succ z),

(16)

\displaystyle x\prec(y\circ z)=(y\succ x)\prec z-(x\prec y)\prec z-y\succ(x\prec z),

(17)

\displaystyle(x\circ y)\succ z=-(x\succ z)\prec y,

(18)

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\prec z=(x\prec z)\prec y,

(19)

\displaystyle(x\circ y-y\circ x)\prec z=x\succ(y\circ z)-y\succ(x\circ z),

for all
x,y,z\in A
, where
x\circ y=x\succ y+x\prec y
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(A,\succ,\prec)
be a 1-dimensional anti-pre-Novikov algebra with a basis
\{e\}
.
Suppose that
e\succ e=pe,~e\prec e=qe

p,q\in k
. Then

(p+q)q=-q^{2},~p^{2}+pq=-pq

p+2q=0

Let
(A,\succ,\prec)

whose non-zero products are given by
e_{1}\succ e_{1}=e_{2},~e_{1}\succ e_{2}=e_{3}

Let

Definition 2.6

Let

\displaystyle l_{\circ_{2}}(a)x\succ_{1}y+l_{\succ_{2}}(r_{\circ_{1}}(x)a)y=-l_{\succ_{2}}(a)y\prec_{1}x-l_{\prec_{2}}(r_{\succ_{1}}(y)a)x,

\displaystyle r_{\prec_{2}}(a)(x\prec_{1}y)=r_{\prec_{2}}(a)x\prec_{1}y+l_{\prec_{2}}(l_{\prec_{1}}(x)a)y,

\displaystyle l_{\prec_{2}}(a)x\prec_{1}y+l_{\prec_{2}}(r_{\prec_{1}}(x)a)y=l_{\prec_{2}}(a)y\prec_{1}x+l_{\prec_{2}}(r_{\prec_{1}}(y)a)x,

\displaystyle r_{\prec_{2}}(a)(x\circ_{1}y-y\circ_{1}x)=x\succ_{1}r_{\circ_{2}}(a)y-y\succ_{1}r_{\circ_{2}}(a)x+r_{\succ_{2}}(l_{\circ_{1}}(y)a)x-r_{\succ_{2}}(l_{\circ_{1}}(x)a)y,

\displaystyle((r_{\circ_{2}}-l_{\circ_{2}})(a)x)\prec_{1}y-l_{\prec_{2}}((l_{\circ_{1}}-r_{\circ_{1}})(x)a)y=x\succ_{1}l_{\circ_{2}}(a)y+r_{\succ_{2}}(r_{\circ_{1}}(y)a)x-l_{\succ_{2}}(a)(x\circ_{1}y),

\displaystyle x\prec_{1}r_{\circ_{2}}(a)y+r_{\prec_{2}}(l_{\circ_{1}}(y)a)x=r_{\prec_{2}}(a)(y\succ_{1}x-x\succ_{1}y)-y\succ_{1}r_{\prec_{2}}(a)x-r_{\succ_{2}}(l_{\prec_{1}}(x)a)y,

\displaystyle x\prec_{1}l_{\circ_{2}}(a)y+r_{\prec_{2}}(r_{\circ_{1}}(y)a)x=(l_{\succ_{2}}(a)-r_{\prec_{2}}(a))x\prec_{1}y+l_{\prec_{2}}((r_{\succ_{1}}-l_{\prec_{1}})(x)a)y-l_{\succ_{2}}(a)(x\prec_{1}y),

\displaystyle l_{\prec_{2}}(a)(x\circ_{1}y)=((r_{\succ_{2}}-l_{\prec_{2}})(a)x)\prec_{1}y+l_{\prec_{2}}((l_{\succ_{1}}-r_{\prec_{1}})(x)a)y-x\succ_{1}(l_{\prec_{2}}(a)y)-r_{\succ_{2}}(r_{\prec_{1}}(y)a)x,

\displaystyle r_{\succ_{1}}(x)(a\circ_{2}b-b\circ_{2}a)=b\succ_{2}r_{\succ_{1}}(x)a-a\succ_{2}r_{\succ_{1}}(x)b+r_{\succ_{1}}(l_{\prec_{2}}(a)x)b-r_{\succ_{1}}(l_{\prec_{2}}(b)x)a,

\succ

\prec

Definition 2.12
.

Let
(V,l,r)
be a representation of a Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
. A linear map
T:V\rightarrow A
is
called an
anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator
on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(V,l,r)
if
T
satisfies

(34)

T(u)\circ T(v)=-T(l(T(u))v+r(T(v))u),\ \ \ \forall~u,v\in V.

Furthermore,
T
is called strong if

(35)

l(T(u)\circ T(v)-T(v)\circ T(u))w+r(T(u)\circ T(w))v-r(T(v)\circ T(w))u=0,\ \ \ \forall~u,v,w\in V.

In particular, an anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
on
(A,\circ)
associated to
the representation
(A,L_{\circ},R_{\circ})
is called an anti-Rota-Baxter operator, that is,
T:A\longrightarrow A

is a linear map satisfying

(36)

T(x)\circ T(y)=-T(T(x)\circ y)+x\circ T(y)),\ \ \ \forall~x,y\in A.

An anti-Rota-Baxter operator
T
is called strong if
T
satisfies

(37)

[T(x),T(y)]\circ z+y\circ(T(x)\circ T(z))-x\circ(T(y)\circ T(z))=0,\ \ \ \forall~x,y,z\in A,

where
[x,y]=x\circ y-y\circ x
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra and
(V,l,r)
be a representation of
(A,\circ)
. Assume that

T:V\longrightarrow A
is an anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(V,l,r)
. Define two
binary operations
\succ,\prec
on
V
respectively by

(38)

u\succ v=-l(T(u))v,\ \ \ u\prec v=-r(T(v))u,\ \ \ \forall~u,v\in V.

Then we have

(a)

For all
u,v,w\in V
, denote
u\cdot v=u\succ v+u\prec v
, the following equations hold:

(39)

\displaystyle(v\cdot u-u\cdot v)\succ w=u\succ(v\succ w)-v\succ(u\succ w),

(40)

\displaystyle(u\succ w)\prec v-u\succ(w\prec v)=w\prec(u\cdot v)+(w\prec u)\prec v,

(41)

\displaystyle(u\cdot v)\succ w=-(u\succ w)\prec v,\ \ \ (w\prec v)\prec u=(w\prec u)\prec v.

(b)

(V,\succ,\prec)
is an anti-pre-Novikov algebra if and only if
T
is strong. In this case,
T

is a homomorphism of Novikov algebras from
(V,\cdot)
to
(A,\circ)
. Moreover, there is an induced
anti-pre-Novikov algebra structure on
T(V)=\{T(u)|u\in V\}\subseteq A
given by

T(u)\succ T(V)=T(u\succ v),\ \ \ T(u)\prec T(V)=T(u\prec v),\ \ ~\forall~u,v\in V

and
T

is a homomorphism of anti-pre-Novikov algebras.

(c)

If
T
is invertible, then
T
is strong.

Proof.

(a)
Using Eqs. (
3
) and (
38
), for all
u,v,w\in V
we have

\displaystyle u\succ(v\succ w)-v\succ(u\succ w),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l(Tu)l(Tv)w-l(Tv)l(Tu)w=l(Tu\circ Tv-Tv\circ Tu)w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-l(T(l(Tu)v+r(Tv)u)-l(T(l(Tv)u+r(Tu)v)w=-(u\cdot v-v\cdot u)\succ w,

which yields that Eq. (
39
) holds. Analogously, we can prove that Eqs. (
40
)-(
41
) hold.

(b)
(V,\succ,\prec)
is an anti-pre-Novikov algebra if and only if Eqs. (
39
)-(
41
) hold and

(42)

(u\cdot v-v\cdot u)\prec w=u\succ(v\cdot w)-v\succ(u\cdot w),\ \ \ \forall~u,v,w\in V.

By Eqs. (
3
)-(
4
) and (
34
)-(
38
), we get

\displaystyle(u\cdot v-v\cdot u)\prec w-u\succ(v\cdot w)+v\succ(u\cdot w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r(Tw)(l(Tu)v+r(Tv)u-r(Tw)(l(Tv)u+r(Tu)v)-l(Tu)(l(Tv)w+r(Tw)v)

\displaystyle+l(Tv)(l(Tu)w+r(Tw)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r(Tv\circ Tw)u-r(Tu\circ Tw)v-l(Tu\circ Tv-Tv\circ Tu)w.

Thus, Eq. (
42
) holds if and only if Eq. (
35
) holds.
The other conclusions follow immediately.

(c) In view of Eqs. (
34
) and (
38
), we have

\displaystyle(Tu\circ Tv)\circ Tw=-T(l(Tu)v+r(Tv)u)\circ Tw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T[l(T(l(Tu)v+r(Tv)u))w+r(Tw)(l(Tu)v+r(Tv)u)]=T[(u\cdot v)\succ w+(u\cdot v)\prec w].

By the same token,

\displaystyle(Tv\circ Tu)\circ Tw=T[(v\cdot u)\succ w+(v\cdot u)\prec w],

\displaystyle Tu\circ(Tv\circ Tw)=T[u\succ(v\cdot w)+u\prec(v\cdot w)],

\displaystyle Tv\circ(Tu\circ Tw)=T[v\succ(u\cdot w)+v\prec(u\cdot w)].

Since
(A,\circ)
is a Novikov algebra and
T
is
invertible, we obtain

(u\cdot v)\cdot w-(v\cdot u)\cdot w=u\cdot(v\cdot w)-v\cdot(u\cdot w).

Combining Items (a) and (b), we get that Eq. (
42
) holds. Thus,
(V,\succ,\prec)
is an anti-pre-Novikov algebra.
It follows that
T
is strong.
∎

Definition 2.17
.

Let
(A,\succ,\prec)

\omega

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
(A,\circ,\omega)
be a symmetric quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra.
Then there exists a compatible anti-pre-Novikov algebra structure
(A,\succ,\prec)

on
(A,\circ)
defined by Eq. (
44
),
such that
(A,\circ)
is the associated Novikov algebra of
(A,\succ,\prec)
. This anti-pre-Novikov algebra is called
the compatible anti-pre-Novikov algebra of
(A,\circ,\omega)
. Moreover,
(A,\succ,\prec,\omega)
is a quadratic anti-pre-Novikov algebra.
Conversely, assume that
(A,\succ,\prec,\omega)
is a quadratic anti-pre-Novikov algebra.
Then
(A,\circ,\omega)
is a symmetric quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra.

Proof.

Let
(A,\circ,\omega)
be a symmetric quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra.
Define a linear map

(45)

T:A^{*}\rightarrow A,\ \ \ \omega(T(u),x)=\langle u,x\rangle,\ \ \ u\in A^{*},x\in A.

Due to
\omega
being non-degenerate,
T
is
invertible.
For all
x,y,z\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\omega(x\succ y,z)

\displaystyle=\omega(y,x\circ z+z\circ x)

\displaystyle=\langle T^{-1}(y),x\circ z+z\circ x\rangle=\langle T^{-1}(y),(L_{\circ}+R_{\circ})(x)z\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle(L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(x)T^{-1}(y),z\rangle=-\omega(T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(x)T^{-1}(y)),z),

and

\displaystyle\omega(x\prec y,z)

\displaystyle=-\omega(x,z\circ y)=-\langle T^{-1}(x),z\circ y\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle T^{-1}(x),R_{\circ}(y)z\rangle=\langle R_{\circ}^{*}(y)T^{-1}(x),z\rangle

\displaystyle=-\omega(T((-R_{\circ}^{*})(b)T^{-1}(a)),c).

Thus,

(46)

\displaystyle x\succ y=-T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(x)T^{-1}(y)),\;\;x\prec y=-T((-R_{\circ}^{*})(y)T^{-1}(x)),\;\;x,y\in A.

Let
x=T(u),\ y=T(v)
. Define

\displaystyle u\rhd v:=-(L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(u))v,\ \ \ \ u\lhd v:=-(-R_{\circ}^{*})(T(v))u,\quad u,v\in A^{*}.

Then we have

\displaystyle x\succ y=T(u)\succ T(v)=T(u\rhd v),\ \ \ \ x\prec y=T(u)\prec T(v)=T(u\lhd v),\;\;x,y\in A.

If
(A^{*},\rhd,\lhd)
is an anti-pre-Novikov algebra,
then
(A,\succ,\prec)
is an anti-pre-Novikov algebra and
T
is an anti-pre-Novikov algebra isomorphism.
In fact, by Eq. (
43
), for all
u,v,w\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle w,T(u)\circ T(v)+T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(u))v-R_{\circ}^{*}(T(v))u)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(T(w),T(u)\circ T(v))+\omega(T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(u))v-R_{\circ}^{*}(T(v))u),T(w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(T(w),T(u)\circ T(v))+\langle(L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(u))v-R_{\circ}^{*}(T(v))u,T(w)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(T(w),T(u)\circ T(v))-\langle v,T(u)\circ T(w)+T(w)\circ T(u)\rangle+\langle u,T(w)\circ T(v)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(T(w),T(u)\circ T(v))-\omega(T(v),T(u)\circ T(w)+T(w)\circ T(u))+\omega(T(u),T(w)\circ T(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which yields that

\displaystyle T(u)\circ T(v)+T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(u))v-R_{\circ}^{*}(T(v))u)=0,

that is,
T:A^{*}\rightarrow A
defined by Eq. (
45
) is an invertible anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(A^{*},L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*},-R_{\circ}^{*})
.
By Proposition
2.13
,
(A^{*},\rhd,\lhd)
is an anti-pre-Novikov algebra.
It follows that
(A,\succ,\prec,\omega)
is a quadratic anti-pre-Novikov algebra.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle x\succ y+x\prec y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(x)T^{-1}(y))+T((R_{\circ}^{*})(y)T^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(u)\circ T(v)=x\circ y.

Thus,
(A,\circ)
is the associated Novikov algebra of
(A,\succ,\prec)
.
The converse part is obviously.
We complete the proof.
∎

3.
Anti-pre-Novikov
bialgebras

In this section, we introduce a notion of anti-pre-Novikov
bialgebras
as the bialgebra structures corresponding
to double constructions of symmetric quasi-Novikov algebras. Both of them
are equivalent to certain matched pairs of Novikov algebras as well
as the compatible anti-pre-Novikov algebras. The study of coboundary case leads to the introduction of the APN-YBE, whose
skew-symmetric solutions induce coboundary anti-pre-Novikov
bialgebras.
The notion of
\mathcal{O}
-operators on
anti-pre-Novikov algebras is introduced to construct skew-symmetric solutions
of the APN-YBE.

3.1.
Anti-pre-Novikov bialgebras

Definition 3.1
.

An anti-pre-Novikov coalgebra is a triple
(A,\Delta_{\succ},\Delta_{\prec})
, where

A
is a vector space and
\Delta_{\succ},\Delta_{\prec}:A\longrightarrow A\otimes A
are linear maps such that
the following conditions hold:

(48)

(\Delta\otimes I)\Delta_{\succ}-(\tau\otimes I)(\Delta\otimes I)\Delta_{\succ}=(\tau\otimes I)(I\otimes\Delta_{\succ})\Delta_{\succ}-(I\otimes\Delta_{\succ})\Delta_{\succ},

(49)

(I\otimes\Delta)\Delta_{\prec}=(\tau\otimes I)(\Delta_{\succ}\otimes I)\Delta_{\prec}-(\Delta_{\prec}\otimes I)\Delta_{\prec}-(\tau\otimes I)(I\otimes\Delta_{\prec})\Delta_{\succ},

(50)

(\Delta\otimes I)\Delta_{\succ}=-(I\otimes\tau)(\Delta_{\succ}\otimes I)\Delta_{\prec},

(51)

(\Delta_{\prec}\otimes I)\Delta_{\prec}=(I\otimes\tau)(\Delta_{\prec}\otimes I)\Delta_{\prec},

(52)

(\Delta\otimes I)\Delta_{\prec}-(\tau\otimes I)(\Delta\otimes I)\Delta_{\prec}=(I\otimes\Delta)\Delta_{\succ}-(\tau\otimes I)(I\otimes\Delta)\Delta_{\succ},

where
\Delta=\Delta_{\succ}+\Delta_{\prec}
and
\tau:A\otimes A\longrightarrow A\otimes A~~\tau(x\otimes y)=y\otimes x,~\forall~x,y\in A
.

Definition 3.2
.

An anti-pre-Novikov bialgebra is a quintuple
(A,\succ,\prec,\Delta_{\succ},\Delta_{\prec})

such that

is an anti-pre-Novikov algebra,

\displaystyle(\tau\Delta_{\prec}+\Delta_{\succ})(x\circ y)=(I\otimes L_{\circ}(x)-(L_{\succ}+2R_{\prec})(x)\otimes I)(\tau\Delta_{\prec}+\Delta_{\succ})(y)

\displaystyle+(I\otimes R_{\circ}(y))(2\tau\Delta_{\prec}+\Delta_{\succ})(x)+(R_{\prec}(y)\otimes I)\tau\Delta_{\prec}(x),

(54)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\prec}([y,x])=(L_{\circ}(y)\otimes I-I\otimes L_{\odot}(x))\Delta_{\prec}(x)+(I\otimes L_{\odot}(y)-L_{\circ}(x)\otimes I)\Delta_{\prec}(y),

(55)

\displaystyle\Delta(x\odot y)=((L_{\succ}+2R_{\prec})(x)\otimes I)\Delta(y)+(L_{\prec}(y)\otimes I)\Delta_{\prec}(x)

\displaystyle+(I\otimes L_{\odot}(x))\Delta(y)-(I\otimes R_{\odot}(y))\Delta_{\succ}(x)-2(I\otimes R_{\odot}(y))\tau\Delta_{\prec}(x),

(56)

\displaystyle(\tau\Delta-\Delta)(y\prec x)=(I\otimes L_{\prec}(y))(\Delta_{\succ}+\tau\Delta_{\prec})(x)-(I\otimes R_{\prec}(x))\Delta(y)

\displaystyle+(R_{\prec}(x)\otimes I)\tau\Delta(y)-(L_{\prec}(y)\otimes I)(\tau\Delta_{\succ}+\Delta_{\prec})(x),

(59)

\displaystyle(R_{\odot}(y)\otimes I)\Delta_{\prec}(x)-(I\otimes L_{\odot}(x))\tau\Delta(y)=(I\otimes R_{\odot}(y))\tau\Delta_{\prec})(x)-(L_{\odot}(x)\otimes I)\Delta(y),

where
\circ=\succ+\prec,~[x,y]=x\circ y-y\circ x,~~x\odot y=x\succ y+y\prec x,~\Delta=\Delta_{\succ}+\Delta_{\prec}

Lemma 4.2

Let

(101)

(103)

For all

\displaystyle\langle(I\otimes L_{\star}(x)-L_{\succ}(x)\otimes I)s,\zeta\otimes\eta\rangle=

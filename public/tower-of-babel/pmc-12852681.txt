Green approach in saponin extraction from Acanthophyllum caryophyllaceae root based on ultrasonication coupled with solvent extraction method and its lyophilization
Authors: Sama Yavarzadeh, Hoda Jafarizadeh-Malmiri, Omid Ahmadi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSaponin, as nonionic emulsifier, has been broadly used in food products and formulations, such as ice cream, chocolates, mayonnaises and bakery food. Acanthophyllum caryophyllaceae root is known as Chubak in Iran and is one of the main sources of saponin. In the present study, saponin was extracted from dried A. caryophyllaceae root using hydro-alcoholic solvent in combination to ultrasonication. UV–Vis spectroscopy indicated that pure and extracted saponin (ES) had absorbance at 520 nm. Effects of the ethanol percentage in the hydro-alcoholic solution (10 to 80% V/V) and ultrasonication time (10 to 20 min) on the absorbance, emulsification index and antioxidant activity of the colloidal solution containing ES were evaluated. Colloidal solution with maximum absorbance (0.157% a.u), emulsification index (0.588) and antioxidant activity (25.55%), was obtained using optimum amount of ethanol in solvent and sonication time of 34% and 13 min, respectively. Finally, lyophilized saponin was prepared using colloidal solution having saponin with concentration of 1.4% W/V, and characterized by DLS, FT-IR, SEM, DSC, BET, XRD and DPPH methods. Lyophilized saponin shown spherical shape and semi-crystalline structure with particle size, polydispersity index, zeta potential, melting point and specific surface area of 7 nm, 0.341, − 15.3 mV, 123.7 °C and 72.14 m2 g−1, respectively. Current research provided a basis for study on further application of lyophilized saponin and development of food emulsions.Keywords: Acanthophyllum caryophyllaceae root, Emulsifying properties, Green extraction, Lyophilization, SaponinSubject terms: Biochemistry, Chemistry, Plant sciences

Introduction

Saponins are heterogeneously group of secondary metabolites which have structures containing two parts namely; triterpenoid aglycone and one or more sugar chains. Hydrophilic properties of its glycosides part and its lipophilic attributes of the steroid part give amphiphilic character to the saponin1. In chemical industries, saponin can be used as foaming agent, wetting compound, improver of the heavy crude oil and surfactant2. In food industries, saponins have been widely used as additives due to those emulsifying, stabilizing and antimicrobial activities3,4. Emulsifiers are surface-active compounds that can be adsorbed at the oil–water interface, and reduced the interfacial tension and preventing the aggregation of the droplets in the emulsion5. Saponin, as nonionic emulsifier, and several natural and synthetic emulsifiers including Tweens, Spans, lecithin and gums (e.g. Xanthan and Arabic gums) have been widely utilized in food formulations and products, such as ice cream, chocolates, mayonnaises and bakery food6,7. In Yazd, center providence of Iran, saponin has been widely used in the production of a traditionally food namely, Halva Ardeh, based on sesame oil and pomace. In fact, due to lyobipolar character of the saponin, it can decrease surface tension of the aqueous solutions and interact with cytoplasmic membrane of the cells including, bacteria, fungi, algae and virus strains8. In the medicine area, saponin due to its neuroprotective effect, has great potential to be used for Alzheimer diseases prevention and it also exhibit cholesterol-lowering action in animals and humans. In cosmetics, saponin also can be used as dispersion agent in the personal care products9. Saponins can be found in numerus natural fruits, vegetables and legumes including Peas, soya beans, oat, yam, black burry, Allium and solanum species, tomato, tea, peanut, asparagus, spinach, sugar beet and sunflower10,11. A. caryophyllaceae root, is known as Chubak in Iran and it is one of the main sources of saponin. Traditionally, Chubak root have been widely as soap, shampoo and detergent in Iran and many medalist countries12,13. As already mentioned, addition of Chubak into the Halva Ardeh formulation had several advantages based on emulsifying properties, texture improvement, and contribution in subtle flavour notes of the final product10.

As compared to the synthetic emulsifiers and other natural emulsifiers, saponin has salt-free nature which, makes it likely to be influenced by acidic or alkaline conditions14. Therefore, saponin extraction from the natural rich sources has gained more interests by the scientists, manufacturers and suppliers, recently. Generally, there are two main extraction techniques for saponin extraction namely, green and conventional techniques. Conventional extraction techniques are based on solvent extraction using heat reflux, Soxhlet and maceration, and different chemical solvents including ethanol, methanol and acetone11,13. Green extraction methods are based on using water and ethanol, as the main green solvents, in combination to other accelerated heating methods including, microwave irradiation, ultrasonication and hydrothermal11,15. These extraction techniques are also known as clean methods which, those are fast, simple, eco-friendly and cost-effective. Ethanol is safe for use in food, pharmaceutical and cosmetic applications, and has balanced polarity, both polar and non-polar characteristics, which that makes easy dissolving amphiphilic compounds, such as saponin in itself16. Several studies indicated that type and amount of the saponin natural sources, type and concentration of the green solvents, extraction conditions (e.g. pH, temperature and pressure) type of accelerated heating (e.g. microwave, ultrasound and autoclave) and specifications of those devices (e.g. power and frequency) had main effects on the qualitative and quantitative properties of the ES11,13. Ultrasound-assisted extraction is an efficient method in extraction of valuable natural bioactive compounds from plant tissue, with using lower amount of chemical solvents17. Mechanical effect of ultrasonication can result in the turbulence and acoustic streaming in the solution, which can drastically increase the solute coefficient of mass transfer and diffusion rate18. Using this extraction technique, phytochemicals existed in the plants can be release more easily due to the effect of acoustic cavitation. In fact, ultrasonic cavitation creates shear forces, which that disrupts cell wall and increase mass transfer process19.

The present study suggests a novel combination of green solvent extraction and ultrasonication, coupled with lyophilization, for saponin extraction from Chubak roots and prepared it in lyophilized form. This approach aligns with the principles of green chemistry by minimizing solvent use and cost, and utilizing environmentally friendly techniques. Present study focused on to (i) extract saponin from Chubak root using hydro-alcoholic solvent and ultrasonication, and optimize extraction conditions namely ultrasonication time (UT) and ethanol percentage in the hydro-alcoholic solvent, (ii) prepare saponin powder using lyophilization method and (iii) characterize of the lyophilized saponin.

Materials and methods

Materials

Chubak (A. Caryophyllaceae) dried root was purchased from herbal local market in Tabriz, a city of Iran. Ethanol and distilled water were bought from Dr. Mojallali Company (Tahran Iran). 2,2-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) was provided from Merck Company (Darmstadt, Germany). Pure saponin was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Company (Steinheim, Germany).

Saponin extraction and preparation of its lyophilized powder

A. caryophyllaceae dried root was powdered using domestic miller. After that, 13 hydro-alcoholic solutions containing 10 to 80% ethanol were provided according to the central composite design (CCD) and 10 g of the provided powder was added into 100 mL of each solution containing mixture of ethanol and water. After that the mixture solutions were exposed to the bath ultrasonication with constant frequency and power of 28 kHz and 240 W, for different times between 10 to 20 min (according to the CCD). In order to minimize saponin degradation, temperature of water in the ultrasonic device adjusted to 30 °C, by addition of ice into the bath and control of the water temperature. Finally, the mixture solutions were filtered using Whatman filter paper (No. 1) and stored at dark room for further analyses20,21.

To prepare lyophilized saponin, the provided colloidal solutions having ES were put in a freeze dryer (Sahand Bioprocess Equipment Co.,Tabriz, Iran) with its chamber conditions including pressure and temperature of 100 Pa and − 70 °C, for 48 h. The fabricated lyophilized saponin was then placed at closed bottle and stored at ambient temperature22.

Physico-chemical analyses

Dynamic light scattering (DLS)

Particle size, poloydispersity index (PDI) and zeta potential of the prepared saponin powder were measured with a particle size analyzer (Nanotrac Wave, Microtrace, Pennsylvania, USA) was utilized. For this analysis, a solution containing 0.01% saponin in the powder form was prepared22.

Saponin concentration

For evaluate the concentration of saponin in the solution, absorbance of the solutions was measured using UV–Vis spectrophotometry technique (Jenway UV–Vis spectrophotometer 6705, Staffordshire, UK18. Furthermore, high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC, D-14,163 Knauer, Berline, Germany) with a C 18 column (Eurospher 100–5 C18) adjusted at wavelength of 203 nm and diode-array detector was used to determine exact saponin concentration. Mobile phase was based on Acetonitrile and water with ratio of 40:60 was used as mobile phase and sample was injected into the device at flow rate 1 mL/min. internal standard curve was provided using serial dilutions of the provided pure saponin (0.1 to 1% W/V)11.

Fourier transforms infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR)

Main groups existed in the ES from A. caryophyllaceae root was detected by FT-IR spectroscopy (Tensor 27, Germany) ranged from 4000 to 400 cm−1 and using KBr pellets23.

Morphology

Size and shape of the prepared saponin powder using lyophilization technique were determined using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, mv 2300, Com scan, USA). For this analysis a 0.01% saponin solution using the prepared saponin powder was used22.

X-ray diffractometry (XRD)

Microstructure state of the prepared saponin in the powder form and its crystalline size were analyzed using an XRD (D5000, Siemens Co., Germany) using Cu Ka radiation, and Scherer equation was utilized to calculate size of the fabricated crystalline particles. All samples were analyzed in 2θ ranging 10–80 with scan rate of 2° min−1 and step size of 0.02° 2θ23.

Differential scanning calorimeter (DSC)

Thermal attributed of the prepared lyophilized saponin, including resistance and decomposition were monitored using a DSC (TA Instrument, SDT-Q600, USA). In fact,1 mg of the lyophilized saponin was placed in aluminum pans and 5 µL water was added into that to increase uniformity of the samples. After that, the pans were hermetically sealed and heated with rate of 10 °C/min, from 20 to 200 °C24.

Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET)

For assessment of the specific surface area of the lyophilized saponin a BET analyzer (Quantachrome ChemBET-3000, Germany) based on the detecting N2 absorption–desorption at -196.15 °K was used24.

Emulsification index

In order to evaluate of the emulsification potential of the provided saponin extract in the colloidal form, 5 mL of the sunflower oil was added into the 5 mL of ES solution and shacked vigorously using tube shaker for 5 min. After that the provided solution was kept at room temperature for 24 h and finally thickness of the emulsion layer between oil and water phases was measured25.

Antioxidant activity

Free radical scavenging method using 2, 2 diphenyl 1- picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) was used to measure antioxidant activity of the ES solutions according to the CCD. In this method, 0.004% DPPH solution based on methanol was prepared and mixed with 2 mL of the sample. Solutions were then put in dark place for 15 min and after that, their absorbance at wavelength of 517 was recorded (ASamples). Wavelength of control sample (AControl), without the sample in the DPPH solution, was also recorded using UV–visible spectroscopy. Free radical scavenging of DPPH (I %) was considered by Eq. 126.

Design of experiments, optimization of the extraction process and data statistical analysis

Central composite design (CCD) based on two factors namely, amount of ethanol (AE) in the hydro-alcoholic solution (10 to 80% V/V) and UT (10 to 20 min) was used to design of experiments including 13 randomized runs based on five repeated center point (AE: 45% V/V and UT: 15 min). Influences of the extractive factors on the absorbance, emulsification index and antioxidant activity of the solutions having ES, were assessed via response surface methodology (RSM). Second-order polynomial equation (Eq. 2) based on all constant (A0), linear (Ai), quadratic (Aii) and interaction (Aij) terms was utilized to predict responses as function of the extractive variables in the defined ranges for the processing parameters27.

R2 (coefficient of determination) with P-value of the lack of fit were utilized to assess ability and accurateness of the predicting models. Analysis of variance was used to show importance of the model term based on p-value of less than 0.0528. By using the produced models, contour and surface plots were stabilized and optimum values for the extraction variables was obtained using graphical and numerical optimizations. Design of experiments, model generation, optimization, comparison test and all data statistical analyses was accomplished by Minitab statistical software (v.16, Minitab Inc., PA, USA).

Results and discussion

Absorbance of saponin in the colloidal solution containing ES

Several studies indicated that ES from different plant sources had different maximum absorbance. Sharma et al. reported that eh ES from Chlorophytum borivilianum had absorbances at 260 and 450 nm29. Mora-Ocación et al. also stated that the ES from Chenopodium quinoa Willd. had maximum absorbance at wavelength of 388 nm30. Wirman et al. also found that the ES from Sida rhombifolia L. had maximum absorbance at wavelength of 449 nm31. In order to determine saponin concentration, an extracted saponin in the colloidal form using center point conditions (AE: 45% V/V and UT: 15 min) was provided and its absorbance was measured in wavelength ranging 300 to 600 nm. Results indicated that the sample had sharp peak at 520 nm. Therefore, absorbance of the extracted saponin samples was used as index for saponin concentration in the present study. By increasing the saponin concentration in the extracted sample, its absorbance increases and vice versa29.

Model generation

According to the CCD (Table 1) and RSM, three models were generated to predict emulsification index, absorbance (saponin concentration) and antioxidant activity of the samples having ES as function of ethanol percentage in solvent and UT. Table 2 shows coefficient and P-value of the model terms, and coefficient of determination and lack-of-fit for the produced models. Results indicated that main effect of UT had non-significant effects on absorbance and antioxidant activity of the prepared samples containing saponin. Furthermore, quadratic term of UT had non-significant effect of emulsification index of the samples. In addition to, interaction term of the independent variables had insignificant effect on absorbance of the colloidal samples. Results also indicated that all generated models had higher coefficient of determination (R2) than 90% with P-values of lack-of-fit higher than 0.05 (p > 0.05) which those shown high quoracy and adequately of the created models24,27.

Effects of independent factors on emulsification index of the solution containing ES

Results indicated that emulsification index of the extracted samples varied from 0.270 to 0.667 (Table 2). Emulsification index shows stability of biosurfactant to improve contact between water and oil. In fact, a biosurfactant with high emulsification index has a strong and stable collaboration between water and lipid compounds, and effectively reduces interfacial tension and forms a stable emulsion32. Effects of the EA and UT on emulsification index of the samples present in Fig. 1A and B. Based on Fig. 1A, at constant UTs, by increasing ethanol percentage in the extracted solvent, emulsification index increased and decreased, respectively. Ethanol is moderately and its low to moderate concentrations in the solvent composition, enhanced the solubility of saponin, which are amphiphilic molecule. It seems that at high ethanol concentrations, the solvent becomes too non-polar for optimal saponin activity, reducing its emulsification index16. On the other hand, at low and constant AE, by rising the UT, emulsification index was constant. However, at high and constant ethanol percentages, by increasing UT, increased. These opposite trends revealed that interaction effect of the independent variables had significant (p < 0.05) effect on emulsification index of the extracted sample containing saponin and verified obtained result of the Table 2. Figure 1B indicates that maximum emulsification index was obtained using minimum AE, and also with maximum AE in the solvent and UT. In fact, by rising AE in the solvent composition, hydrogen band was increased between ethanol and cellulosic plant cell wall components which that caused swelling of the cell wall20. Application of ultrasonication could easily break hydrogen bands, due to increase shear forces and temperature of the system by cavitation, and enhance diffusion process to increase saponin extraction yield. Gavrila et al. also found that by increasing AE in the hydro-alcoholic solvent, saponin extraction yield increased20. Bustos et al. also optimized the process of saponin extraction from Quinoa residues based on pH, temperature and salinity of the extracted solution and found that emulsification index of the samples varied from 0.30 to 0.6733.

Effects of extractive factors on absorbance of the solution containing ES

According to Table 1, absorbance of the extracted solutions containing saponin changed from 0.071 to 0.406% a.u.. Effects of the extracted parameters on absorbance of the extracted solutions show in Fig. 2A and B.

Surface (A) and contour (B) plots for the absorbance of the colloidal solution containing ES as function of the AE in the solvent and UT.

As can be seen in Fig. 2A, at constant UTs, by increasing AE in the extracted solvent, absorbance of the sample containing ES decreased. On the other hand, at constant amounts of ethanol in the solvent, by increasing UT, absorbance of the samples decreased and increased, respectively. The results revealed that interaction effect of the extracted variables had insignificant effect on the absorbance of sample and was in line with observed result in Table 2. According to Fig. 2B, maximum absorbance was obtained for the extracted samples having saponin using minimum AE in the extracted hydro-alcoholic solvent. This result can be related to the fact that at low percentage of ethanol in the solvent composition, saponin could be easily diffuse into the plant cell wall surrounded layer and by increasing UT, other plant cell materials also extracted and released into the system, which those increased absorbance of the sample34.

Effectiveness of extractive factors on antioxidant activity of the solution containing ES

Results indicated that antioxidant activity of the solutions containing ES changed from 20.3 to 26.7%. Effects of the chosen independent variables on antioxidant activity of the extracted solution having saponin show in Fig. 3A and B. Based on Fig. 3A, at low to moderate and constant UTs, by increasing AE in the solvent, antioxidant activity of the samples increased and decreased, respectively. However, at highest UTs by increasing AE, antioxidant activity of the extracted solution containing saponin increased. Results can be explained by the facts that at highest ultrasonication duration times, cavitation could drastically disrupt plant cell wall, which that increased ethanol penetration into the plant cells and extraction more saponins. Furthermore, higher concentration of ethanol in the extractive solvent, decreased viscosity and surface tension of solvent, which those improved mass transfer and diffusion of saponin from plant tissues into the solvent35. On the other hand, at low and high amounts of ethanol in the solvent, by increasing UT, antioxidant activity of the samples decreased and increased, respectively. Presence of curvature in Fig. 3A demonstrated that interaction term of the independent variables had significant (p < 0.05) effect on antioxidant activity of the extracted samples containing saponin. This result was in line with obtained statistical results in Table 2.

Surface (A) and contour (B) plots for the antioxidant activity of the colloidal solution containing ES as function of the AE in the solvent and UT.

According to the Fig. 3B, maximum antioxidant activity was obtained for the extracted samples using moderate to high AE in the solvent and UTs. The results can be explained by the fact that using moderate concentration of ethanol in the solvent composition, plant cell swelling increased without blocking diffusion process. The choice of extraction solvent depends on the chemical attributes of the secondary metabolites being extracted. A water-alcohol solvent mixture is commonly utilized, resulting in extra efficient extraction because water hydrates plant walls as the alcohol is chemically similar to most active components extracts from the plant materials34. On the other hand, at moderate UTs, temperature of the colloidal solution could not increase and cause saponin lost20.

Process optimization and validation

Extraction of saponin from the A. caryophyllaceae root powder in the colloidal form was optimized when emulsification index, absorbance and antioxidant activity of the colloidal solution containing ES were in higher values. Figure 4 shows graphical optimization for the extraction process parameters to achieve responses in those higher values and with equal importance. White zone in this figure indicated optimum area for the independent variables. Furthermore, numerical optimization revealed that higher emulsification index (0.588), absorbance (0.157% a.u.) and antioxidant activity (25.55%) were obtained for the extracted solution containing saponin using optimum AE in solvent and sonication time of 34% and 13 min, respectively. Bustos et al. also ES from Quinoa residues and found that maximum emulsification index (0.63) for the ES was obtained using optimum conditions based on a pH of 9.05, a temperature of 66 °C and salinity of 10,000 ppm for NaCl33. Three additional tests also were done using obtained optimum conditions and statistical comparison test indicated in significant differences between predicted and experimental values of the responses, which that validate the adequacy of the generated models26,28.

Conclusions

Saponin was extracted from A. caryophyllaceae root using mixture of water and ethanol as green solvent. Results of the present study indicated induced cavitation by ultrasonication, increased shear forces and temperature of colloidal solution and disintegrated root cell tissue and increased saponin extraction yield. By optimization of the UT, it was possible to reduce negative effects of that on qualitative and quantitative specifics of the ES. The successful extraction and lyophilization of the saponin in nano-scale from A. caryophyllaceae root, an abundant plant, demonstrates the potential for its applications in food, pharmaceutical, cosmetics and health care formulations and products. Furthermore, lyophilization can effectively increase shelf life of the ES without need specific storage and packaging conditions. Developed extraction method in the present study shows promise for industrial scalability when integrated into the food processing lines and can be also used in extraction of saponin from other plant-based sources.

elastic scattering by locally rough interfaces
Chengyu Wu, Yushan Xue, Jiaqing Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we present the first well-posedness result for elastic scattering by locally rough interfaces in both two and three dimensions. Inspired by the Helmholtz decomposition, we first discover a fundamental identity for the stress vector, revealing an intrinsic relationship among the generalized stress vector, the Lamé constants and certain tangential differential operators. This identity leads to two key limits for surface integrals involving scattered solutions, from which we deduce the first uniqueness result of direct problem for all frequencies. Through a detailed analysis, applying the steepest descent method, subsequently we derive the existence and uniqueness of the corresponding two-layered Green’s tensor along with its explicit expression when the transmission coefficient equals 1. Finally, by leveraging properties of the Green’s tensor, we establish the existence of solutions via the variational method and the boundary integral equation, thereby achieving the first well-posedness result for elastic scatteing by rough interfaces.



keywords

elastic scattering, locally rough interfaces, two-layered Green’s tensor, steepest descent method, well-posedness.

AMS

35J08, 35B40, 74B05.

Introduction

The present paper concerns the time-harmonic elastic scattering by a penetrable locally rough surface in both two and three dimensions. Such problems can find substantial applications in various fields, such as geophysics, ocean acoustics and seismology.

Let the scattered interface be denoted by
\Gamma:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{d}=f(x^{\prime})\}

(d=2,3)
, where
f
is assumed to be a Lipschitz continuous function with compact support. This means that
\Gamma
is just a local perturbation of the plannar interface
\Gamma_{0}:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{d}=0\}
. The whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is then separated by
\Gamma
into the upper half-space
D^{+}:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{d}>f(x^{\prime})\}
and the lower half-space
D^{-}:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{d}<f(x^{\prime})\}
. Let
B_{r}(x)
denote the open ball centered at
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with radius
r>0
. For balls centered at the origin, we abbreviate by
B_{r}
.

Suppose that the whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is fullfilled with a piecewise isotropic and homogeneous elastic medium characterized by the Lamé constants
\lambda,\mu
and the mass density
\rho
satisfying that
\mu>0,d\lambda+2\mu>0
and
\rho=\rho_{\pm}>0
in
D^{\pm}
with
\rho_{+}\neq\rho_{-}
. Given an incident field
\mathbf{u}^{in}
, consider the time-harmonic elastic scattering by the locally rough interface
\Gamma
, which is governed by the Navier equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Delta^{*}\mathbf{u}_{\pm}+\rho_{\pm}\omega^{2}\mathbf{u}_{\pm}=0~~~\text{in}~D^{\pm},

where
\Delta^{*}:=\mu\Delta+(\lambda+\mu)\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits{\rm div\,}
,
\omega>0
is the angular frequency,
\mathbf{u}_{+}
is the scattered field in
D^{+}
and
\mathbf{u}_{-}
is the transmitted field in
D^{-}
.

In order to state the boundary conditions for
\mathbf{u}_{\pm}
at the interface
\Gamma
, we introduce the following notations. In two dimensions, for
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2})^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, define
\mathbf{y}^{\perp}:=(y_{2},-y_{1})^{\top}
. Further, we will make use of the differential operators

\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits^{\perp}u:=(\partial_{2}u,-\partial_{1}u)^{\top}~~~~\text{and}~~~~{\rm div\,}^{\perp}\mathbf{u}:=\partial_{2}u_{1}-\partial_{1}u_{2}.

Denote by
\bm{\nu}
the unit normal vector pointing out of
D^{+}
and let
\bm{\tau}=\bm{\nu}^{\perp}
be the unit tangential vector if
d=2
. The generalizd stress vector on
\Gamma
is then defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\mu+\tilde{\mu})\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbf{u}+\tilde{\lambda}\bm{\nu}{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}-\tilde{\mu}\bm{\tau}{\rm div\,}^{\perp}\mathbf{u},&{\rm if}~d=2,\\
(\mu+\tilde{\mu})\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbf{u}+\tilde{\lambda}\bm{\nu}{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}+\tilde{\mu}\bm{\nu}\times{\rm curl\,}\mathbf{u},&{\rm if}~d=3,\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}
are real numbers satisfying
\tilde{\mu}+\tilde{\lambda}=\mu+\lambda
. Note that
\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}
is equal to the physical stress vector
\mathbf{T}\mathbf{u}
for the choice
\tilde{\mu}=\mu
and
\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda
. The transmission boundary conditions for
\mathbf{u}_{\pm}
are then given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{+}-\mathbf{u}_{-}=-\mathbf{u}^{in}~~~~\text{and}~~~~\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}_{+}-a_{0}\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}_{-}=-\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}^{in}~~~\text{on}~\Gamma,

where the transmission coefficient
a_{0}>0
.

To assure the uniqueness of the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), it is necessary to impose radiation conditions on the scattered field
\mathbf{u}_{+}
and the transmitted field
\mathbf{u}_{-}
at infinity. To this end, we note that the solutions
\mathbf{u}_{\pm}
to the Navier equation admit the Helmholtz decomposition

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{\pm}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-k_{p,\pm}^{-2}\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits\phi_{p,\pm}-k_{s,\pm}^{-2}\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits^{\perp}\phi_{s,\pm},&{\rm if}~d=2,\\[2.84526pt]
\displaystyle-k_{p,\pm}^{-2}\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits\phi_{p,\pm}+k_{s,\pm}^{-2}{\rm curl\,}\bm{\phi}_{s,\pm},&{\rm if}~d=3,\end{array}\right.

where the wave numbers
k_{p,\pm}
and
k_{s,\pm}
satisfy

k_{p,\pm}^{2}=\frac{\rho_{\pm}\omega^{2}}{2\mu+\lambda},~~~k_{s,\pm}^{2}=\frac{\rho_{\pm}\omega^{2}}{\mu},

and the compressional part
\phi_{p,\pm}:={\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}_{\pm}
, the shear part
\phi_{s,\pm}:={\rm div\,}^{\perp}\mathbf{u}_{\pm},\bm{\phi}_{s,\pm}:={\rm curl\,}\mathbf{u}_{\pm}
satisfy

\Delta\phi_{a,\pm}+k_{a,\pm}^{2}\phi_{a,\pm}=0,~a=p,s,~\text{and}~{\rm curl\,}{\rm curl\,}\bm{\phi}_{s,\pm}-k_{s,\pm}^{2}\bm{\phi}_{s,\pm}=0.

In view of the above equations, we require the following Kupradze radiation conditions on the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
),

(1.3a)

\displaystyle d=2:~\partial_{r}\phi_{a,\pm}-ik_{a,\pm}\phi_{a,\pm}=o(r^{-1/2}),~a=p,s,~

(1.3b)

\displaystyle d=3:~\partial_{r}\phi_{p,\pm}-ik_{p,\pm}\phi_{p,\pm}=o(r^{-1}),~{\rm curl\,}\bm{\phi}_{s,\pm}\times\mathbf{\hat{x}}-ik_{s,\pm}\bm{\phi}_{s,\pm}=o(r^{-1}),~

uniformly in
\mathbf{\hat{x}}:=\mathbf{x}/r\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{\pm}
as
r:=|\mathbf{x}|\rightarrow\infty
, where

{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{\pm}:={\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\pm}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\pm}:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{d}\gtrless 0\}
. Clearly, (
1.3
) is a combination of the classical Sommerfeld and Silver-Müller radiation condition in the acoustic and electromagnetic scattering (c.f.
[
8
,
10
]
).

The elastic scattering problems involving rough surfaces have attracted considerable attention over the last twenty years. We refer to
[
1
,
2
,
11
,
12
,
14
]
for periodic structures and locally rough surfaces, and
[
3
,
4
,
13
]
for general global rough surfaces. However, the majority of the preceding work were only concerning the half-plane problem, while significantly less work existed for the whole space problem, which requires a substantially more elaborate analysis due to the presence of the transmitted wave and the complexity of the stress vector. Therefore, establishing a well-posedness theory for the elastic scattering by general rough interfaces continues to be an open problem. The primary difficulties lie in proving the uniqueness of the solution, a cornerstone of the scattering theory. For bounded scatterers, the Kupradze radiation condition was shown to be equivalent to an radiation condition about the surface integral between scattered solutions, which is easy to be applied for the uniqueness of solutions. Nevertheless, for locally rough surfaces, such equivalence has not been discovered yet, and thus it is hard to utilize the half-plane Kupradze radiation condition when considering the uniqueness of solution. Consequently, the uniqueness results for locally rough surfaces remain distinctly blank till now.

Inspried by the Helmholtz decomposition, in this paper we discover a fundamental identity for the stress vector, which reveals an intrinsic relationship among the genearlized stress vector, the Lamé constants and certain tangential differential operators. This identity leads to two key limits for surface integrals concerning scattered solutions, which are instrumental in deducing both the uniqueness of solution and its Green’s representation. Further, they in some extents indicate the equivalence between two aforementioned radiation conditions in the locally rough surface case. Utilizing these limits, and the uniqueness results in the acoustic and electromagnetic scattering, we prove the first uniqueness theorem of problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) for all frequencies. Furthermore, this framework enables the systematic extension of substantial uniqueness results from bounded elastic scatterers to locally rough surfaces..

Beyong its impressive application to rough surfaces, the discovered identity for the stress vector presents a fundamental contribution to the elastic scattering. Since in the definition of the genearlized stress vector, the dependence on the constants
\tilde{\lambda}
and
\tilde{\mu}
is rather vague, considerable existing works only hold under some constraints on the value of
\tilde{\lambda}
and
\tilde{\mu}
. In contrast, our identity illustrate explicitly the relationship between the genearlized stress vector and
\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu}
. It appears that the term involving
\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu}
corresponds to some tangential differential operators, which would vanish when considering the surface integral. This profound insight allows for the removal of these constraints in a wide range of previous results, significantly enhancing their generality and applicability. Moreover, utilizing this identity, it is possible to completely decouple the elastic waves to compressional and shear waves, thus simplifying the study of Lamé system to the investigation of two vectorial Helmholtz systems.

The second part of this paper is about the two-layered Green’s tensor for problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) with
a_{0}=1
. The two-layered Green’s function in the acoustic scattering, which plays a pivotal role in both direct and inverse scattering in two-layered media, has been rigorously studied in
[
5
,
6
,
18
,
20
]
. Nevertheless, since the Navier equation has a more complex form than the Helmholtz equation and there is little work concerning the direct problem, to the best of the author’s knowledge, there is no rigorous mathematical study about the two-layered elastic Green’s tensor till now. In this paper, employing the aforementioned identity about the stress vector, we derive the existence and uniqueness of the corresponding two-layered Green’s tensor. Using the Fourier transform and its inverse, we deduce the explicit expression of the Green’s tensor in both two and three dimensions by solving several complicated systems of linear equations. Subsequently, following the arguments in
[
5
,
6
,
18
,
20
]
, utilizing the steepest descent method, we obtain the asymptotic behaviors of the corresponding expressions in two dimensions, while in three dimensions the analysis is reduced to the two-dimensional case after changing the Fourier transform to the Hankel transform and the asymptotic behaviors follows, which in turn implies the existence and uniqueness of the Green’s tensor in both two and three dimensions.

Finally, similarily as in
[
7
,
14
]
, applying the properties of the two-layered Green’s tensor, we propose an equivalent variational formulation to problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) with the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map derived from the integral representation of the scattered field. The variational formulation is then shown to be of Fredholm type, and thus the existence of solutions follows from the uniqueness, which achieves the first well-posedness result for elastic scattering by penetrable rough surfaces at all frequencies.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we first prove an identity for the stress vector, which implies two significant limits about the surface integral. Applying these limits, we obtain the uniqueness of solutions to problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) for all frequencies. In section
3
, we derive the existence and uniqueness of the two-layered Green’s tensor for problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) with
a_{0}=1
by computing its explicit form and verifying that it satisfies the radiation condition (
1.3
). In section
4
, we prove the existence of solutions via the variational method and the boundary integral equaiton.

2
Uniqueness of solution

In this section, we shall derive the uniqueness result for problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
), which is based on a first discovered identity for the stress vector and two significant limits of the surface integral.

Lemma 1
.

The following identities hold

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\mu+\tilde{\mu})(\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\mathbf{u})^{\perp}+(2\mu+\lambda)\bm{\nu}{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}+\mu\bm{\tau}{\rm div\,}^{\perp}\mathbf{u},&{\rm if}~d=2,\\
(\mu+\tilde{\mu})\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\nu}}(\mathbf{u})+(2\mu+\lambda)\bm{\nu}{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}-\mu\bm{\nu}\times{\rm curl\,}\mathbf{u},&{\rm if}~d=3,\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\nu}}
is the differential operator defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\nu}}(\mathbf{u}):=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\nu_{2}\partial_{1}u_{2}-\nu_{1}\partial_{2}u_{2}+\nu_{3}\partial_{1}u_{3}-\nu_{1}\partial_{3}u_{3}\\
\nu_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}-\nu_{2}\partial_{1}u_{1}+\nu_{3}\partial_{2}u_{3}-\nu_{2}\partial_{3}u_{3}\\
\nu_{1}\partial_{3}u_{1}-\nu_{3}\partial_{1}u_{1}+\nu_{2}\partial_{3}u_{2}-\nu_{3}\partial_{2}u_{2}\end{array}\right)

for
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})^{\top}
and
\bm{\nu}=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})^{\top}
.

Proof.

From the definition of the generalized stress vector
\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}
, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbf{u}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\mathbf{u})^{\perp}+\bm{\nu}{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}+\bm{\tau}{\rm div\,}^{\perp}\mathbf{u},&{\rm if}~d=2,\\
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\nu}}(\mathbf{u})+\bm{\nu}{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}-\bm{\nu}\times{\rm curl\,}\mathbf{u},&{\rm if}~d=3.\end{array}\right.

Direct calculation yields that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\;(\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\mathbf{u})^{\perp}+\bm{\nu}{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}+\bm{\tau}{\rm div\,}^{\perp}\mathbf{u}\\
=&\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\nu_{2}\partial_{1}u_{2}-\nu_{1}\partial_{2}u_{2}+\nu_{1}(\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2})+\nu_{2}(\partial_{2}u_{1}-\partial_{1}u_{2})\\
-\nu_{2}\partial_{1}u_{1}+\nu_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}+\nu_{2}(\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2})-\nu_{1}(\partial_{2}u_{1}-\partial_{1}u_{2})\end{array}\right)\\
=&\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\nu_{1}\partial_{1}u_{1}+\nu_{2}\partial_{2}u_{1}\\
\nu_{1}\partial_{1}u_{2}+\nu_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}\end{array}\right)=\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbf{u},\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\;\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\nu}}(\mathbf{u})+\bm{\nu}{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}-\bm{\nu}\times{\rm curl\,}\mathbf{u}\\
=&\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\nu_{2}\partial_{1}u_{2}-\nu_{1}\partial_{2}u_{2}+\nu_{3}\partial_{1}u_{3}-\nu_{1}\partial_{3}u_{3}\\
\nu_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}-\nu_{2}\partial_{1}u_{1}+\nu_{3}\partial_{2}u_{3}-\nu_{2}\partial_{3}u_{3}\\
\nu_{1}\partial_{3}u_{1}-\nu_{3}\partial_{1}u_{1}+\nu_{2}\partial_{3}u_{2}-\nu_{3}\partial_{2}u_{2}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\nu_{1}(\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}+\partial_{3}u_{3})\\
\nu_{2}(\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}+\partial_{3}u_{3})\\
\nu_{3}(\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}+\partial_{3}u_{3})\end{array}\right)\\
&\;-\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\nu_{2}(\partial_{1}u_{2}-\partial_{2}u_{1})-\nu_{3}(\partial_{3}u_{1}-\partial_{1}u_{3})\\
\nu_{3}(\partial_{2}u_{3}-\partial_{3}u_{2})-\nu_{1}(\partial_{1}u_{2}-\partial_{2}u_{1})\\
\nu_{1}(\partial_{3}u_{1}-\partial_{1}u_{3})-\nu_{2}(\partial_{2}u_{3}-\partial_{3}u_{2})\end{array}\right)\\
=&\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\nu_{1}\partial_{1}u_{1}+\nu_{2}\partial_{2}u_{1}+\nu_{3}\partial_{3}u_{1}\\
\nu_{1}\partial_{1}u_{2}+\nu_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}+\nu_{3}\partial_{3}u_{2}\\
\nu_{1}\partial_{1}u_{3}+\nu_{2}\partial_{2}u_{3}+\nu_{3}\partial_{3}u_{3}\end{array}\right)=\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbf{u},\end{aligned}

which are the desired identities.
∎

Remark 2
.

It is known that

\displaystyle\bm{\nu}\times\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits\mathbf{u}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nu_{2}\partial_{3}u_{1}-\nu_{3}\partial_{2}u_{1}&\nu_{3}\partial_{1}u_{1}-\nu_{1}\partial_{3}u_{1}&\nu_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}-\nu_{2}\partial_{1}u_{1}\\
\nu_{2}\partial_{3}u_{2}-\nu_{3}\partial_{2}u_{2}&\nu_{3}\partial_{1}u_{2}-\nu_{1}\partial_{3}u_{2}&\nu_{1}\partial_{2}u_{2}-\nu_{2}\partial_{1}u_{2}\\
\nu_{2}\partial_{3}u_{3}-\nu_{3}\partial_{2}u_{3}&\nu_{3}\partial_{1}u_{3}-\nu_{1}\partial_{3}u_{3}&\nu_{1}\partial_{2}u_{3}-\nu_{2}\partial_{1}u_{3}\end{array}\right),

which implies that
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\nu}}(\mathbf{u})
depends fully on
\bm{\nu}\times\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits\mathbf{u}
. Therefore, it is deduced that for arbitrarily fixed
\tilde{\mu}
and
\tilde{\lambda}
, if
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}=0
on
\Gamma
, then
\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbf{u}=0
on
\Gamma
and hence
\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits\mathbf{u}=0
on
\Gamma
. That is, problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) with
a_{0}=1
is independent of the choice of
\tilde{\mu}
and
\tilde{\lambda}
.

Corollary 3
.

Suppose
\mathbf{u}^{in}=0
and
a_{0}=1
, then we have

\displaystyle d=2:\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{p,+}=\phi_{p,-},~\phi_{s,+}=\phi_{s,-},\\[2.84526pt]
-k_{p,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{p,+}+k_{s,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\phi_{s,+}=-k_{p,-}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{p,-}+k_{s,-}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\phi_{s,-},\\[2.84526pt]
k_{p,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\phi_{p,+}+k_{s,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{s,+}=k_{p,-}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\phi_{p,-}+k_{s,-}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{s,-},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle d=3:\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{p,+}=\phi_{p,-},~\bm{\nu}\times\bm{\phi}_{s,+}=\bm{\nu}\times\bm{\phi}_{s,-},\\[2.84526pt]
-k_{p,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{p,+}+k_{s,+}^{-2}\bm{\nu}\cdot{\rm curl\,}\bm{\phi}_{s,+}=-k_{p,-}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{p,-}+k_{s,-}^{-2}\bm{\nu}\cdot{\rm curl\,}\bm{\phi}_{s,-},\\[2.84526pt]
-k_{p,+}^{-2}\bm{\nu}\times\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits\phi_{p,+}+k_{s,+}^{-2}\bm{\nu}\times{\rm curl\,}\bm{\phi}_{s,+}\\[2.84526pt]
=-k_{p,-}^{-2}\bm{\nu}\times\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits\phi_{p,-}+k_{s,-}^{-2}\bm{\nu}\times{\rm curl\,}\bm{\phi}_{s,-},\end{array}\right.

on the boundary
\Gamma
.

Proof.

We only prove the two dimensional case, while the three dimensional case follows from a completely similar analog combining Lemma
1
and Remark
2
.

Since
\mathbf{u}^{in}=0
, we have
\mathbf{u}_{+}-\mathbf{u}_{-}=0
on
\Gamma
, which further implies
\bm{\nu}\cdot(\mathbf{u}_{+}-\mathbf{u}_{-})=\bm{\tau}\cdot(\mathbf{u}_{+}-\mathbf{u}_{-})=0
on
\Gamma
. By the Helmholtz decomposition it is seen that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -k_{p,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{p,+}+k_{s,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\phi_{s,+}&=-k_{p,-}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{p,-}+k_{s,-}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\phi_{s,-},\\
k_{p,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\phi_{p,+}+k_{s,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{s,+}&=k_{p,-}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\phi_{p,-}+k_{s,-}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{s,-},\end{aligned}

where we note that
\bm{\nu}\cdot\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits=\bm{\tau}\cdot\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits^{\perp}=\partial_{\bm{\nu}}
and
\bm{\nu}\cdot\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits^{\perp}=-\bm{\tau}\cdot\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits=-\partial_{\bm{\tau}}
. Moreover, it follows from
\mathbf{u}_{+}-\mathbf{u}_{-}=0
that
(\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\mathbf{u}_{+})^{\perp}-(\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\mathbf{u}_{-})^{\perp}=0
on
\Gamma
, which indicates by Lemma
1
that

0=\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}_{+}-\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}_{-}=(2\mu+\lambda)\bm{\nu}({\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}_{+}-{\rm div\,}\mathbf{u}_{-})+\mu\bm{\tau}({\rm div\,}^{\perp}\mathbf{u}_{+}-{\rm div\,}^{\perp}\mathbf{u}_{-})

on
\Gamma
. This clearly leads to
\phi_{p,+}=\phi_{p,-},~\phi_{s,+}=\phi_{s,-}
on
\Gamma
, which ends the proof.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Assume
d=2
. Suppose
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
satisfy the Navier equaiton (
1.1
) and the radiation condition (
1.3
) in
D^{+}
with
\phi_{p},\varphi_{p}
being the compressional part and
\phi_{s},\varphi_{s}
being the shear part, respectively. Then we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}\left(\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u}\right)ds=0,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left({\rm Im}\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{\overline{u}}ds-(2\mu+\lambda)k_{p,+}^{-1}\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}|\phi_{p}|^{2}ds-\mu k_{s,+}^{-1}\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}|\phi_{s}|^{2}ds\right)=0,

where
\partial B_{R}^{\pm}:=\partial B_{R}\cap D^{\pm}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
and the Helmholtz decomposition, it is derived that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=\;&(\mu+\tilde{\mu})(\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\mathbf{u})^{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{v}+(2\mu+\lambda)\phi_{p}(-k_{p,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\varphi_{p}+k_{s,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\varphi_{s})\\
&-\mu\phi_{s}(k_{p,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}\varphi_{p}+k_{s,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\varphi_{s}),\end{aligned}

which implies that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u}=\;&(\mu+\tilde{\mu})\partial_{\bm{\tau}}(\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{v})+\frac{(2\mu+\lambda)\mu}{\rho_{+}\omega^{2}}\partial_{\bm{\tau}}(\phi_{p}\varphi_{s}-\phi_{s}\varphi_{p})\\
&-(2\mu+\lambda)k_{p,+}^{-2}(\phi_{p}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\varphi_{p}-\varphi_{p}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{p})-\mu k_{s,+}^{-2}(\phi_{s}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\varphi_{s}-\varphi_{s}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{s}).\end{aligned}

Therefore, for
R>0
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}\left(\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u}\right)ds\\
=\;&(\mu+\tilde{\mu})[(\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{v})(R,0)-(\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{v})(-R,0)]+\frac{(2\mu+\lambda)\mu}{\rho_{+}\omega^{2}}[(\phi_{p}\varphi_{s})(R,0)-(\phi_{p}\varphi_{s})(-R,0)]\\
&-\frac{(2\mu+\lambda)\mu}{\rho_{+}\omega^{2}}[(\phi_{s}\varphi_{p})(R,0)-(\phi_{s}\varphi_{p})(-R,0)]-(2\mu+\lambda)k_{p,+}^{-2}\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}(\phi_{p}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\varphi_{p}-\varphi_{p}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{p})ds\\
&-\mu k_{s,+}^{-2}\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}(\phi_{s}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\varphi_{s}-\varphi_{s}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{s})ds,\end{aligned}

which tends to zero as
R\rightarrow\infty
by the radiation condition (
1.3
).

Further, again by (
2.1
) we see that

Theorem 5
.

Assume
d=3
. Suppose
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
satisfy the Navier equaiton (
1.1
) and the radiation condition (
1.3
) in

with
\phi_{p},\varphi_{p}
being the compressional part and
\bm{\phi}_{s},\bm{\varphi}_{s}
being the shear part, respectively. Then we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}\left(\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u}\right)ds=0,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left({\rm Im}\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{\overline{u}}ds-(2\mu+\lambda)k_{p,+}^{-1}\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}|\phi_{p}|^{2}ds-\mu k_{s,+}^{-1}\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}|\bm{\phi}_{s}|^{2}ds\right)=0.

Proof.

The proof is similar as Theorem
4
with some modifications. It follows from Lemma
1
and the Helmholtz decomposition that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=\;&(\mu+\tilde{\mu})\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\nu}}(\mathbf{u})\cdot\mathbf{v}+(2\mu+\lambda)\phi_{p}(-k_{p,+}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\varphi_{p}+k_{s,+}^{-2}\bm{\nu}\cdot{\rm curl\,}\bm{\varphi}_{s})\\
&-\mu\bm{\phi}_{s}\cdot(-k_{p,+}^{-2}\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits\varphi_{p}\times\bm{\nu}+k_{s,+}^{-2}{\rm curl\,}\bm{\varphi}_{s}\times\bm{\nu}).\end{aligned}

Denote by
{\rm Div\,}
the surface divergence. By direct calculation we derive that

\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\nu}}(\mathbf{u})\cdot\mathbf{v}-\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\nu}}(\mathbf{v})\cdot\mathbf{u}=\bm{\nu}\cdot{\rm curl\,}(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v})=-{\rm Div\,}(\bm{\nu}\times(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v})),

and thus

{\rm Im}(\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\nu}}(\mathbf{u})\cdot\mathbf{\overline{u}})=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Im}(\bm{\nu}\cdot{\rm curl\,}(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{\overline{u}}))=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Im}({\rm Div\,}(\bm{\nu}\times(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{\overline{u}}))).

Therefore, it is deduced that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\;\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u}\\
=\;&-(\mu+\tilde{\mu}){\rm Div\,}(\bm{\nu}\times(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}))-\frac{(2\mu+\lambda)\mu}{\rho_{+}\omega^{2}}{\rm Div\,}(\bm{\nu}\times(\phi_{p}\bm{\varphi}_{s}-\varphi_{p}\bm{\phi}_{s}))\\
&-(2\mu+\lambda)k_{p,+}^{-2}(\phi_{p}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\varphi_{p}-\varphi_{p}\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\phi_{p})-\mu k_{s,+}^{-2}(\bm{\phi}_{s}\cdot{\rm curl\,}\bm{\varphi}_{s}\times\bm{\nu}-\bm{\varphi}_{s}\cdot{\rm curl\,}\bm{\phi}_{s}\times\bm{\nu}),\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\;{\rm Im}(\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{\overline{u}})\\
=\;&-\frac{(\mu+\tilde{\mu})}{2}{\rm Im}({\rm Div\,}(\bm{\nu}\times(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{\overline{u}})))-\frac{(2\mu+\lambda)\mu}{\rho_{+}\omega^{2}}{\rm Im}({\rm Div\,}(\bm{\nu}\times(\phi_{p}\bm{\overline{\phi}}_{s})))\\
&-(2\mu+\lambda)k_{p,+}^{-2}{\rm Im}(\phi_{p}(\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\overline{\phi}_{p}+ik_{p,+}\overline{\phi}_{p}))-\mu k_{s,+}^{-2}{\rm Im}(\bm{\phi}_{s}\cdot({\rm curl\,}\bm{\overline{\phi}}_{s}\times\bm{\nu}+ik_{s,+}\bm{\overline{\phi}}_{s}))\\
&+(2\mu+\lambda)k_{p,+}^{-1}|\phi_{p}|^{2}+\mu k_{s,+}^{-1}|\bm{\phi}_{s}|^{2},\end{aligned}

which finishes the proof by the Stokes formula and the radiation condition (
1.3
).
∎

\displaystyle\left[k_{p}^{-2}\partial_{x_{2}}\hat{U}_{p,j}(\xi,x_{2},y_{2})-i\xi k_{s}^{-2}\hat{U}_{s,j}(\xi,x_{2},y_{2})\right]=[i\xi k_{s}^{-2}\hat{\widetilde{G}}_{s,j}(\xi,x_{2},y_{2})],

\displaystyle\left[i\xi k_{p}^{-2}\hat{U}_{p,j}(\xi,x_{2},y_{2})+k_{s}^{-2}\partial_{x_{2}}\hat{U}_{s,j}(\xi,x_{2},y_{2})\right]=-[i\xi k_{p}^{-2}\hat{\widetilde{G}}_{p,j}(\xi,x_{2},y_{2})].

Substituting the expressions (

\displaystyle A_{p,1}=\frac{C_{0}\xi}{D}i\left[k_{s,+}^{-2}\beta_{s,+}e^{-\beta_{s,+}y_{2}}-\frac{(k_{s,+}^{-2}-k_{s,-}^{-2})k_{p,+}^{-2}\xi^{2}}{k_{p,+}^{-2}\beta_{p,+}+k_{p,-}^{-2}\beta_{p,-}}e^{-\beta_{p,+}y_{2}}\right],

\displaystyle B_{p,1}=\frac{C_{0}\xi}{D}i\left[-k_{s,-}^{-2}\beta_{s,-}e^{\beta_{s,-}y_{2}}-\frac{(k_{s,+}^{-2}-k_{s,-}^{-2})k_{p,-}^{-2}\xi^{2}}{k_{p,+}^{-2}\beta_{p,+}+k_{p,-}^{-2}\beta_{p,-}}e^{\beta_{p,-}y_{2}}\right],

\displaystyle A_{s,1}=\frac{C_{0}\xi}{D}\left[-\frac{(k_{p,+}^{-2}-k_{p,-}^{-2})k_{s,+}^{-2}\beta_{s,+}\xi}{k_{s,+}^{-2}\beta_{s,+}+k_{s,-}^{-2}\beta_{s,-}}e^{-\beta_{s,+}y_{2}}+k_{p,+}^{-2}\xi e^{-\beta_{p,+}y_{2}}\right],

\displaystyle B_{s,1}=\frac{C_{0}\xi}{D}\left[\frac{(k_{p,+}^{-2}-k_{p,-}^{-2})k_{s,-}^{-2}\beta_{s,-}\xi}{k_{s,+}^{-2}\beta_{s,+}+k_{s,-}^{-2}\beta_{s,-}}e^{\beta_{s,-}y_{2}}+k_{p,-}^{-2}\xi e^{\beta_{p,-}y_{2}}\right],

\displaystyle B_{p,2}=\frac{C_{0}\xi}{D}\left[k_{s,-}^{-2}\xi e^{\beta_{s,-}y_{2}}+\frac{(k_{s,+}^{-2}-k_{s,-}^{-2})k_{p,-}^{-2}\beta_{p,-}\xi}{k_{p,+}^{-2}\beta_{p,+}+k_{p,-}^{-2}\beta_{p,-}}e^{\beta_{p,-}y_{2}}\right],

\displaystyle A_{s,2}=\frac{C_{0}\xi}{D}i\left[\frac{(k_{p,+}^{-2}-k_{p,-}^{-2})k_{s,+}^{-2}\xi^{2}}{k_{s,+}^{-2}\beta_{s,+}+k_{s,-}^{-2}\beta_{s,-}}e^{-\beta_{s,+}y_{2}}-k_{p,+}^{-2}\beta_{p,+}e^{-\beta_{p,+}y_{2}}\right],

\displaystyle B_{s,2}=\frac{C_{0}\xi}{D}i\left[\frac{(k_{p,+}^{-2}-k_{p,-}^{-2})k_{s,-}^{-2}\xi^{2}}{k_{s,+}^{-2}\beta_{s,+}+k_{s,-}^{-2}\beta_{s,-}}e^{\beta_{s,-}y_{2}}+k_{p,-}^{-2}\beta_{p,-}e^{\beta_{p,-}y_{2}}\right],

3.2
Three-dimensional case

For any source point
\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{-}
, the two-layered Green’s tensor
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
in three dimensions is defined by:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{*}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\rho_{\pm}\omega^{2}\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=-\delta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mathbf{I},&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{\pm},\\
\left[\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\right]=0,~\left[\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}^{(\mathbf{x})}(\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}))\right]=0&{\rm on}~\Gamma_{0},\\
\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}r\left(\partial_{r}\mathbf{G}_{p}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})-ik_{p,\pm}\mathbf{G}_{p}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\right)=0,&\mathbf{\hat{x}}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}_{\pm},\\
\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}r({\rm curl\,}\mathbf{G}_{s}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\times\hat{\mathbf{x}}-ik_{s,\pm}\mathbf{G}_{s}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}))=0,&\mathbf{\hat{x}}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}_{\pm},\end{array}\right.

where

denotes the Dirac delta distribution,

is the

\Delta^{*}_{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}^{(\mathbf{x})},{\rm div\,}_{\mathbf{x}}
and

are acting on

, where

and
\mathbf{G}_{s,j}:={\rm curl\,}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{G}_{j}=(G_{s,j}^{(1)},G_{s,j}^{(2)},G_{s,j}^{(3)})^{\top}
with

From Theorem
6

defined above is unique. Again by the Helmholtz decomposition and Corollary

\displaystyle\left[k_{p}^{-2}\bm{\nu}\times\mathop{\operator@font grad}\nolimits_{\mathbf{x}}G_{p,j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})-k_{s}^{-2}\bm{\nu}\times{\rm curl\,}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{G}_{s,j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\right]=0,&{\rm on}~\Gamma_{0},\\

Note that here

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{\pm}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}\widetilde{G}_{p,j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+k_{p}^{2}\widetilde{G}_{p,j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=-(2\mu+\lambda)^{-1}{\rm div\,}_{\mathbf{x}}(\delta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mathbf{e}_{j}),&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\\

\displaystyle\left[k_{p}^{-2}\partial_{\bm{\nu}(\mathbf{x})}\widetilde{G}_{p,j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\right]=0,~\displaystyle\left[k_{s}^{-2}\bm{\nu}\times{\rm curl\,}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{\widetilde{G}}_{s,j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\right]=0,&{\rm on}~\Gamma_{0},\\

Set
\bm{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},0)
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{p,j}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\frac{1}{2\mu+\lambda}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi_{k_{p,+}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}A_{p,j}^{+}\zeta_{j}e^{-\beta_{p,+}x_{3}}e^{i\bm{\zeta}\cdot(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime})}d\zeta_{1}d\zeta_{2},&\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{+},\;\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{+},\\

Lemma 14
.

For
\alpha\in[0,2\pi]
, the following hold

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{it\cos(\gamma-\alpha)}d\gamma=J_{0}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos\gamma e^{it\cos(\gamma-\alpha)}d\gamma=iJ_{1}(t)\cos\alpha,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin\gamma e^{it\cos(\gamma-\alpha)}d\gamma=iJ_{1}(t)\sin\alpha,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin\gamma\cos\gamma e^{it\cos(\gamma-\alpha)}d\gamma=-J_{2}(t)\sin\alpha\cos\alpha,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{2}\gamma e^{it\cos(\gamma-\alpha)}d\gamma=\frac{1}{2}\left(J_{0}(t)-J_{2}(t)\cos 2\alpha\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin^{2}\gamma e^{it\cos(\gamma-\alpha)}d\gamma=\frac{1}{2}\left(J_{0}(t)+J_{2}(t)\cos 2\alpha\right).

Proof.

By basic calculation we see that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{it\cos(\gamma-\alpha)}d\gamma=&\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{-\alpha}^{0}+\int_{0}^{2\pi-\alpha}\right)e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma\\
=&\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma=J_{0}(t),\end{aligned}

where the last equality comes from the integral representation of the Bessel function
[
21
]
. From the recurrence formula of the Bessel functions
[
21
]
, it follows that

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)=-J_{0}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{-i}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma.

Further, it is easily deduced that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma=0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos\gamma e^{it\cos(\gamma-\alpha)}d\gamma\\
=&\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\alpha}^{2\pi-\alpha}\cos(\gamma+\alpha)e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma\\
=&\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\cos\alpha\int_{-\alpha}^{2\pi-\alpha}\cos\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma-\sin\alpha\int_{-\alpha}^{2\pi-\alpha}\sin\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma\right)\\
=&\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\cos\alpha\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma-\sin\alpha\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma\right)\\
=&\;iJ_{1}(t)\cos\alpha,\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin\gamma e^{it\cos(\gamma-\alpha)}d\gamma\\
=&\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\alpha}^{2\pi-\alpha}\sin(\gamma+\alpha)e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma\\
=&\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\sin\alpha\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma+\cos\alpha\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma\right)\\
=&\;iJ_{1}(t)\sin\alpha.\end{aligned}

Finally, it can be verified that

J_{2}(t)=J_{0}(t)-2J_{1}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{-1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos 2\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma

and

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin 2\gamma e^{it\cos\gamma}d\gamma=0.

Thus, following the similar procedure we can obtain the left three equalities and finish the proof.
∎

defined by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}\mathbf{g})(\mathbf{x}):=\int_{\partial B_{R}}\bm{\Pi}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}^{(2)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{y})ds(\mathbf{y}),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}\mathbf{g})(\mathbf{x}):=\int_{\partial B_{R}}\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{y})ds(\mathbf{y}).

We note that the mapping properties and the jump relations of these operators remain valid due to the smoothness of
\mathbf{G}-\bm{\Pi}_{\pm}
and our special choice for
\tilde{\mu}
and
\tilde{\lambda}
(see details in
[
14
,
19
,
16
]
). Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{u}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{u}_{+}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a}),&\mathbf{x}\in B_{R}\cap D^{+},\\[2.84526pt]
\mathbf{u}_{-}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})-\mathbf{u}^{in}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a}),&\mathbf{x}\in B_{R}\cap D^{-}.\end{array}\right.

Utilizing the Betti formula for
\mathbf{\hat{u}}
in
B_{R}
, we then obtain the equivalent variational formualtion of problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
): find
(\mathbf{\hat{u}},\mathbf{p})\in[H^{1}(B_{R})]^{d}\times[H^{-1/2}(\partial B_{R})]^{d}:=\mathbf{X}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}((\mathbf{\hat{u}},\mathbf{p}),(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi}))=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}b_{1}((\mathbf{\hat{u}},\mathbf{p}),(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi}))\\
b_{2}((\mathbf{\hat{u}},\mathbf{p}),(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi}))\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}L_{1}(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi})\\
L_{2}(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi})\end{array}\right)

for all
(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi})\in\mathbf{X}
, where

\displaystyle b_{1}((\mathbf{\hat{u}},\mathbf{p}),(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi})):=\int_{B_{R}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}(\mathbf{\hat{u}},\overline{\bm{\varphi}})-\rho\omega^{2}\mathbf{\hat{u}}\cdot\overline{\bm{\varphi}}\right)d\mathbf{x}-\int_{\partial B_{R}}\mathbf{p}\cdot\overline{\bm{\varphi}}ds,

\displaystyle b_{2}((\mathbf{\hat{u}},\mathbf{p}),(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi})):=\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left((\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{K})(\mathbf{\hat{u}}|_{\partial B_{R}})+\mathcal{S}\mathbf{p}\right)\cdot\overline{\bm{\chi}}ds,

\displaystyle L_{1}(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi}):=-\int_{B_{R}\cap D^{-}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}(\mathbf{u}^{in},\overline{\bm{\varphi}})-\rho_{-}\omega^{2}\mathbf{u}^{in}\cdot\overline{\bm{\varphi}}\right)d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\partial(B_{R}\cap D^{-})}\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}^{in}\cdot\overline{\bm{\varphi}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}^{re}_{+}\cdot\overline{\bm{\varphi}}ds+\int_{\partial B_{R}^{-}}\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}\mathbf{u}^{re}_{-}\cdot\overline{\bm{\varphi}}ds

\displaystyle L_{2}(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi}):=\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left((\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{K})(\mathbf{u}^{re}_{+}|_{\partial B_{R}^{+}}+\mathbf{u}^{re}_{-}|_{\partial B_{R}^{-}}-\mathbf{u}^{in}|_{\partial B_{R}^{-}})\right)\cdot\overline{\bm{\chi}}ds,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}(\mathbf{\hat{u}},\overline{\bm{\varphi}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\mu+\tilde{\mu})\nabla\mathbf{\hat{u}}\cdot{\rm NewA}\overline{\bm{\varphi}}+\tilde{\lambda}{\rm div\,}\mathbf{\hat{u}}{\rm div\,}\overline{\bm{\varphi}}-\tilde{\mu}{\rm div\,}^{\perp}\mathbf{\hat{u}}{\rm div\,}^{\perp}\overline{\bm{\varphi}},&{\rm if}~d=2,\\
(\mu+\tilde{\mu})\nabla\mathbf{\hat{u}}\cdot{\rm NewA}\overline{\bm{\varphi}}+\tilde{\lambda}{\rm div\,}\mathbf{\hat{u}}{\rm div\,}\overline{\bm{\varphi}}-\tilde{\mu}{\rm curl\,}\mathbf{\hat{u}}\cdot{\rm curl\,}\overline{\bm{\varphi}},&{\rm if}~d=3.\end{array}\right.

Denote by
(\cdot,\cdot)
the duality between
[H^{1}(B_{R})]^{d}
and
[H^{-1}(B_{R})]^{d}
, and by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the duality between
[H^{1/2}(\partial B_{R})]^{d}
and
[H^{-1/2}(\partial B_{R})]^{d}
. From the Riesz representation theorem, there exist linear bounded operators

\displaystyle T_{1},J_{1}:

\displaystyle\;[H^{1}(B_{R})]^{d}\rightarrow[H^{-1}(B_{R})]^{d},

\displaystyle T_{2}:

\displaystyle\;[H^{-1/2}(\partial B_{R})]^{d}\rightarrow[H^{-1}(B_{R})]^{d},

\displaystyle T_{3}:

\displaystyle\;[H^{-1/2}(\partial B_{R})]^{d}\rightarrow[H^{1/2}(\partial B_{R})]^{d},

\displaystyle J_{2}:

\displaystyle\;[H^{1}(B_{R})]^{d}\rightarrow[H^{1/2}(\partial B_{R})]^{d},

such that for
(\mathbf{\hat{u}},\mathbf{p}),(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi})\in\mathbf{X}
,

\displaystyle(T_{1}\mathbf{\hat{u}},\bm{\varphi})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\lambda}}(\mathbf{\hat{u}},\overline{\bm{\varphi}})+\rho\omega^{2}\mathbf{\hat{u}}\cdot\overline{\bm{\varphi}}\right)d\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle(J_{1}\mathbf{\hat{u}},\bm{\varphi})

\displaystyle:=-\int_{B_{R}}\rho\omega^{2}\mathbf{\hat{u}}\cdot\overline{\bm{\varphi}}d\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle\langle T_{2}\mathbf{p},\bm{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\mathbf{p}\cdot\overline{\bm{\varphi}}ds,

\displaystyle\langle T_{3}\mathbf{p},\bm{\chi}\rangle

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial B_{R}}\mathcal{S}\mathbf{p}\cdot\overline{\bm{\chi}}ds,

\displaystyle\langle J_{2}\mathbf{\hat{u}},\bm{\chi}\rangle

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\partial B_{R}}\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{\hat{u}}|_{\partial B_{R}})\cdot\overline{\bm{\chi}}ds.

Therefore, we may rewrite
\mathbb{B}:\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{X}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
as

\mathbb{B}((\mathbf{\hat{u}},\mathbf{p}),(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi}))=\langle\mathbb{B}_{1}((\mathbf{\hat{u}},\mathbf{p}),(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi}))\rangle+\langle\mathbb{B}_{2}((\mathbf{\hat{u}},\mathbf{p}),(\bm{\varphi},\bm{\chi}))\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality bwtween
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}
, and the operators
\mathbb{B}_{j}:\mathbf{X}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}^{\prime}
,
j=1,2
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{1}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}T_{1}&-T_{2}\\
T_{2}^{*}&T_{3}\end{array}\right),~~~\mathbb{B}_{2}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}J_{1}&0\\
J_{2}&0\end{array}\right).

To show the existence of solutions, it then suffices to prove that
\mathbb{B}
is of Fredholm type since the uniqueness follows from Theorem
6
. By
[
14
, Lemma 3.2]
,
T_{1}
is coercive over
[H^{1}(B_{R})]^{d}
. Combining the regularity of
\mathbf{G}-\bm{\Pi}_{\pm}
and
[
19
, Theorem 7.6]
, it can be derived that
T_{3}
is a strongly elliptic operator over
[H^{-1/2}(\partial B_{R})]^{d}
, which implies that the real part of
\mathbb{B}_{1}
, given by

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\mathbb{B}_{1}:=\frac{\mathbb{B}_{1}+\mathbb{B}_{1}^{*}}{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}T_{1}&\\
&T_{3}\end{array}\right),

is strongly elliptic over
\mathbf{X}
. Moreover, by the Sobolev embedding,
J_{1}
is compact. Since from our choice for
\tilde{\mu}
and
\tilde{\lambda}
, the operator
\mathcal{K}:[H^{1/2}(\partial B_{R})]^{d}\rightarrow[H^{1/2}(\partial B_{R})]^{d}
is compact, which indicates that
J_{2}
and thus
\mathbb{B}_{2}
is compact. Hence, the operator
\mathbb{B}
is Fredholm with index zero. In summary, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 20
.

For any
\mathbf{z}\in D^{+}
and
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
, problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) with
a_{0}=1
admits a unique solution
(\mathbf{u}_{+},\mathbf{u}_{-})\in H^{1}(B_{R}\cap D^{+})\times H^{1}(B_{R}\cap D^{-})
such that
\mathbf{u}_{+}=\mathbf{\hat{u}}
in
B_{R}\cap D^{+}
and
\mathbf{u}_{-}=\mathbf{\hat{u}}+\mathbf{u}^{in}
in
B_{R}\cap D^{-}
.

Remark 21
.

If the Green’s tensor for problem(
1.1
)–(
1.3
) with
a_{0}\neq 1
is computed, then the corresponding existence of solutions can be proved analogously as in this section.

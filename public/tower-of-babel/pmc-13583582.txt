Adjuvant pembrolizumab plus belzutifan versus placebo in clear cell renal cell carcinoma: a reconstructed survival data analysis of the KEYNOTE-564 and the LITESPARK-022 trials
Authors: A. Cigliola, A. Larcher, B.A. Maiorano, D. Robesti, G. Fallara, C. Mercinelli, C. Re, G. Musso, F. Cei, V. Tateo, M. Piacentini, A. Bertini, N. Disabato, C. Piccolo, G. Rosiello, A. Briganti, A. Necchi, F. Montorsi, A. Martini, U. Capitanio
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAdjuvant pembrolizumab improves disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) versus placebo in high-risk clear cell renal cell carcinoma after nephrectomy. Recently, pembrolizumab plus belzutifan showed superior DFS over pembrolizumab alone. We assessed the efficacy of adjuvant pembrolizumab plus belzutifan versus placebo using reconstructed data from two pivotal phase III trials.MethodsIndividual time-to-event data were reconstructed from digitised KEYNOTE-564 and LITESPARK-022 Kaplan–Meier curves using a validated algorithm based on numbers at risk. Datasets were pooled to enable indirect comparisons across strategies. The primary endpoint was DFS and OS was secondary. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated using Cox proportional hazards models.FindingsThe pooled dataset included 2835 patients. Baseline characteristics were balanced, except for race and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), although PD-L1 assessment was limited by incomplete reporting. Pembrolizumab plus belzutifan significantly improved DFS versus placebo (HR 0.55, 95% CI 0.45–0.69, P < 0.001). Despite immature OS data, the combination showed an OS benefit over placebo (HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.35-0.89, P = 0.014).ConclusionsAdjuvant pembrolizumab plus belzutifan showed a substantial DFS benefit compared with placebo. In a context where factors beyond efficacy demand robust evidence of clinical benefit, these findings reinforce the value of combined immune and hypoxia-inducible factor 2α inhibition.

Introduction

Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) remains a major cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality worldwide, with a substantial proportion of patients experiencing disease recurrence following surgical resection.1 Among patients with high-risk disease,2 the likelihood of relapse remains considerable despite complete nephrectomy, underscoring the need for effective adjuvant strategies.3

The phase III KEYNOTE-564 trial established pembrolizumab as a standard option in the adjuvant setting,4,5 demonstrating a significant improvement in disease-free survival (DFS) and, with longer follow-up, overall survival (OS) compared with placebo.6,7 However, the magnitude of benefit observed across subgroups, together with the non-negligible risk of treatment-related morbidity and financial toxicity, has raised concerns regarding the optimal selection of patients for adjuvant therapy.7, 8, 9 In particular, the use of hazard ratios (HRs) derived from heterogeneous risk populations may mask clinically relevant variability in treatment effect, potentially leading to both undertreatment of patients at highest risk and overtreatment of those less likely to benefit.10

In this context, there remains a clear need for more effective and biologically rational strategies that can enhance therapeutic benefit while enabling more informed patient selection. Combination approaches targeting complementary oncogenic pathways represent a promising avenue to address these unmet needs.

Belzutifan is a selective inhibitor of hypoxia-inducible factor 2α (HIF-2α), a key downstream mediator of Von Hippel–Lindau loss and a central driver of ccRCC biology.11 By modulating tumour hypoxia signalling, angiogenesis, and cellular metabolism, HIF-2α inhibition provides a mechanistically distinct approach that may synergise with immune checkpoint blockade.12 Early clinical evidence from the LITESPARK-022 trial suggests that combining belzutifan with pembrolizumab may improve outcomes compared with pembrolizumab alone.13

However, no direct head-to-head comparisons between pembrolizumab plus belzutifan and placebo are currently available. In rapidly evolving therapeutic landscapes, where the optimal candidates for adjuvant therapy remain under debate and active surveillance after nephrectomy remains widely adopted in routine clinical practice, indirect comparisons based on reconstructed survival data can provide clinically meaningful insights when individual patient data are not accessible.

In this context, we used a validated methodology to reconstruct individual survival data from published Kaplan–Meier (KM) curves of phase III trials evaluating adjuvant pembrolizumab-based strategies. Such analyses may help inform patient selection by better defining the magnitude of benefit associated with different adjuvant strategies. Our primary objective was to assess the efficacy of pembrolizumab plus belzutifan compared with placebo and to explore indirect comparisons with pembrolizumab monotherapy within a unified analytical framework.

Methods

Study design and data sources

We relied on reconstructed survival data abstracted from published KM curves from phase III randomised trials evaluating adjuvant systemic therapy in ccRCC. Baseline characteristics across trials were extracted and compared with assess cross-trial comparability.

Reconstruction of individual patient data

Individual time-to-event data were reconstructed from KM curves using the algorithm described by Guyot et al.14 Briefly, survival probabilities were digitised from published curves and combined with numbers at risk and numbers of events/censoring in order to abstract individual event, time to event, and censoring times through an iterative algorithm.

Digitisation was carried out using WebPlotDigitizer (version 5.7; Ankit Rohatgi, Pacifica, CA) and reconstruction was implemented in R (version 4.5.3; R Core Team, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) using validated scripts and methods.15, 16, 17 Reconstructed datasets were visually and quantitatively validated by comparing derived KM curves with the original published curves and by verifying consistency with reported HRs and event numbers.

Data integration and indirect comparisons

Reconstructed datasets from each trial were merged into a unified pseudoindividual survival data dataset. Treatments were categorised as pembrolizumab monotherapy, pembrolizumab plus belzutifan, and placebo. Given the absence of head-to-head comparisons, indirect treatment comparisons were carried out within the reconstructed individual survival data framework.18

Outcomes

The primary outcome was DFS, defined as time from randomisation to recurrence or death from any cause. OS was analysed as a secondary outcome.

Statistical analysis

The chi-square test was employed to test the differences in the distribution of categorical descriptive variables between study arms.

Survival curves were estimated using the KM method. The log-rank test was used to test the equality of the survivor functions. HRs and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated using Cox proportional hazards models.

All analyses were carried out using R (version 4.5.3). A two-sided P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethical considerations

This study is based on reconstructed survival data derived from previously published clinical trial results. No new patient-level data were collected; therefore, ethical approval and informed consent were not required.

Discussion

In this reconstructed individual survival data analysis, we provide indirect comparative evidence across contemporary adjuvant strategies for renal cell carcinoma by integrating data from the KEYNOTE-5647 and LITESPARK-02213 trials. Using a validated reconstruction framework, we observed that the addition of belzutifan to pembrolizumab was associated with a substantial improvement in DFS compared with placebo (HR 0.55, 95% CI 0.45-0.69), corresponding to an approximate 45% relative reduction in the risk of recurrence or death. While cross-trial comparisons should be interpreted with caution, adjuvant pembrolizumab monotherapy compared with placebo in the KEYNOTE-564 trial was associated with an HR for DFS of 0.68 (95% CI 0.53-0.87).7

Importantly, the validity of this indirect comparison is strengthened by the substantial overlap in eligibility criteria between the KEYNOTE-564 and LITESPARK-022 trials. Both studies enrolled patients with ccRCC at increased risk of recurrence, including those with pT2 grade 4, pT3 disease, node-positive disease, and M1 NED after complete resection.2 This alignment in inclusion criteria, together with broadly comparable baseline characteristics, enhances the clinical comparability of the analysed populations, and approximates the conditions of a target trial framework, thereby increasing the interpretability of cross-trial comparisons despite the absence of direct randomisation.

Although formal heterogeneity metrics cannot be applied due to nonidentical control arms and the lack of a meta-analytic framework, the close overlap of reconstructed pembrolizumab survival curves between KEYNOTE-564 and the pembrolizumab arm of LITESPARK-022 (DFS and OS) indicates comparable baseline hazards. This supports the assumption of exchangeability across trials and strengthens the robustness of the indirect comparison. Beyond relative measures, the magnitude of benefit observed translates into a clinically meaningful absolute gain. Inspection of reconstructed KM curves suggests an absolute improvement in DFS of ∼15% at 24 months, corresponding to a number needed to treat of ∼7-10 patients to prevent one recurrence or death within this time frame. In this context, the observed HR of 0.55 should not be interpreted solely as a statistical measure but rather as reflecting a clinically meaningful reduction in recurrence risk, particularly in patients at highest risk of recurrence. This benefit should, however, be balanced against the additional treatment-related toxicity associated with combination therapy, which would otherwise be avoided with placebo.7,13 From a biological standpoint, these findings are consistent with the known role of HIF-2α as a central oncogenic driver in ccRCC. Inhibition of HIF-2α may modulate tumour angiogenesis, metabolism, and immune evasion, thereby potentially enhancing the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade. The observed pattern of early and sustained separation in DFS curves is compatible with a mechanism that affects micrometastatic disease control.

Our study adopts a reconstructed individual survival data approach, which has been increasingly used to enable cross-trial comparisons when individual patient data are not accessible. We implemented several methodological safeguards to enhance robustness, including incorporation of numbers at risk, validation against reported event counts and HRs and demonstrated absence of significant difference between the pembrolizumab (alone or plus placebo) arms between the two trials included in our analysis.

Nonetheless, several limitations must be acknowledged. Firstly, reconstructed datasets are approximations of original patient-level data and depend on the quality and resolution of published KM curves, and minor deviations in censoring patterns cannot be excluded. Secondly, although the substantial overlap in eligibility criteria between trials strengthens comparability, residual heterogeneity in patient selection, follow-up, and endpoint assessment may still introduce confounding. In particular, within the relatively small M1 NED subgroup, differences in trial design may have influenced outcomes: in KEYNOTE-564 the interval between nephrectomy and metastasectomy was required to be ≤1 year, versus ≤2 years in LITESPARK-022, potentially enriching the latter for patients with more favourable prognosis. We did not apply matching-adjusted indirect comparison or other reweighting approaches, as these require more granular covariate data and introduce additional assumptions; therefore, our results should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating. Thirdly, OS data remain immature, with a limited number of events and relatively short follow-up. Although early signals favour pembrolizumab plus belzutifan, the long-term survival impact remains uncertain, particularly in the context of effective therapies at recurrence. Consequently, DFS should be interpreted cautiously as a surrogate endpoint pending longer follow-up. Fourthly, the assumption of comparability between control arms across trials warrants consideration. While placebo groups showed broadly similar survival trajectories, subtle differences in follow-up intensity and event ascertainment could influence indirect estimates. However, the consistency of HRs within trials and the alignment between reconstructed and published curves provide reassurance regarding internal coherence.

Despite these limitations, our findings provide clinically relevant insight in a setting where direct comparative evidence is unlikely to be available in the near term, and in the adjuvant context, where considerations beyond efficacy—such as toxicity, treatment duration, patient preferences, and costs—may play an even greater role in treatment decision-making compared with other settings.19,20 The magnitude and consistency of DFS benefit observed with pembrolizumab plus belzutifan support the rationale for combined immune checkpoint and HIF-2α inhibition and suggest that this approach may offer a more favourable benefit–risk profile than observation alone in patients with high-risk disease. These results directly inform the clinical dilemma of whether to treat or observe following nephrectomy9 and provide a basis for future risk-stratified analyses to identify which subgroups derive the greatest benefit from adjuvant therapy.

From a broader perspective, this study highlights both the utility and the boundaries of reconstructed individual survival data methodologies in modern oncology. As the therapeutic landscape continues to expand, such approaches may represent a pragmatic framework to contextualise emerging evidence, provided that their assumptions are transparently acknowledged. Future research should aim to refine patients selection through prospective validation of biomarkers and risk-stratification strategies, enabling treatment to be focused on those most likely to benefit while avoiding unnecessary toxicity in lower-risk patients. In addition, integration of reconstructed data with real-world evidence and more advanced indirect comparison methods may further strengthen the evidence base in this rapidly evolving field.

Conclusions

In this reconstructed individual survival data analysis of phase III trials, adjuvant pembrolizumab plus belzutifan was associated with a substantial and consistent improvement in DFS compared with placebo. Within the limitations inherent to cross-trial comparisons, these findings support the biological and clinical rationale for combined immune checkpoint and HIF-2α inhibition and confirm that this strategy may represent a promising approach for patients with high-risk ccRCC.

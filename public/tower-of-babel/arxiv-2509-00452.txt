On a transform of the Vincze-statistic and its exact and asymptotic distribution
Dietmar Ferger

Abstract
We motivate a new nonparametric test for the one-sided two-sample problem, which is based on a transform T T of the Vincze-statistic ( R , D ) (R,D) . The exact and asymptotic distribution of T T is derived. The fundamental idea can also be applied to the two-sided problem. Here too, a limit theorem can be proved.

Introduction

Let

and

be two independent samples of i.i.d. real random variables with
continuous distribution functions

and

or the one-sided alternative

D_{nm}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\{F_{n}(x)-G_{m}(x)\}
, where
F_{n}
and

are the empirical distribution functions of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
and
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
, respectively.
The distributional behavior of
D_{nm}
under the hypothesis
H_{0}
is well-known for a long time.
Smirnov

shows in case

\mathbb{P}(\sqrt{n/2}D_{nn}\leq x)\rightarrow 1-\exp(-2x^{2})\quad\text{ for all }x\geq 0.

Gnedenko and Korolyuk
Gnedenko
find the exact distribution of

and later on Korolyuk

extends the result to

, when

is an integral multiple of

,
m=np
with

.

Next, let

be the
pooled sample
, i.e.
Z_{i}:=X_{i},1\leq i\leq n,
and
Z_{n+i}:=Y_{i},1\leq i\leq m
.
If
Z_{(1)}\leq Z_{(2)}\leq\ldots,Z_{(n+m)}
denote the pertaining order statistics, then

Thus

R_{nm}:=\min\{1\leq i\leq n+m:F_{n}(Z_{(i)})-G_{m}(Z_{(i)})=D_{nm}\}

is the smallest index among those order statistics
Z_{(i)}
at which the two-sample process

\{F_{n}(x)-G_{m}(x):x\in\mathbb{R}\}
attains its maximal value
D_{nm}
. In the special simple test situation

H_{0}^{*}:F=G=U(0,1)
against
H_{1}^{a}:F=U(0,1),G=U(a,1)
, for a fixed

, Vinzce

;

uses the pair

instead of the single

. More precisely, in the case

he determines the likelihood ratio of

for

and

and then uses the corresponding Neyman-Pearson test without randomizing part.
As it turns out the Neyman-Pearson test has a significantly superior power in comparison to the Smirnov-test, confer also Koul and Quine

.
Under
H_{0}
the exact distribution of the so-called
Vincze-statistic

(R_{nm},D_{nm})
has been computed by Vincze

for equal sample sizes
(m=n)
and for general sample sizes by Steck and Simmons

. Gutjahr

1

Note that

is a discrete random variable with range

\{1,\ldots,n+m\}\times\{\frac{k}{m}:0\leq k\leq m\}.

(1)

Moreover, it is distribution free under

. Therefore, we can apply Corollary 2.2.7 and Remark 2.2.9 of Gutjahr

Proposition 1
.

(
Gutjahr
) Assume that
F=G
and that
m=np
with
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for every

and every

such that

s:=(r+k)\frac{n}{n+m}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\;\text{ and }\;s\leq\min\{r,n\}

the following equation holds:

\displaystyle P_{nm}(r,k):=\mathbb{P}(R_{nm}=r,D_{nm}=\frac{k}{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{\binom{n+m-r+1}{n-s}}{\binom{n+m}{n}}\frac{k+1}{n+m-r+1}\sum_{j=0}^{[\frac{n}{n+m}k]}\frac{\frac{m}{n}}{r+k-1-j\frac{n+m}{n}}\binom{j\frac{n+m}{n}-k}{j}\binom{r+k-1-j\frac{n+m}{n}}{s-1-j}

If

in the range (

With a view to Vincze’s idea, our approach is to use a transform

of the Vincze-statistic

By Remark

has positive probability

and therefore cannot be ignored.

Exact null-distribution of
T_{nm}

To motivate our test-statistic we investigate
T_{nm}
under the usual limiting regime, that is
\min\{n,m\}\rightarrow\infty
and

\frac{n}{n+m}\rightarrow\lambda\in(0,1).

(3)

Let

be the empirical distribution function pertaining to the pooled sample

. Observe that

H_{nm}=\frac{n}{n+m}F_{n}+\frac{m}{n+m}G_{m}\rightarrow\lambda F+(1-\lambda)G=:H\;\text{almost surely (a.s.)}

(4)

uniformly on
\mathbb{R}
by the Glivenko-Cantelli Theorem. Assume that

and
G
in the alternative are such that

has a unique maximizing point

(as for instance when

arises from

as in Example

below). According to Lemma
1
in the appendix

and

(n+m)T_{nm}\rightarrow\frac{\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\{F(x)-G(x)\}}{\sqrt{H(\tau)(1-H(\tau))}}\;a.s.

(5)

whereas

D_{nm}\rightarrow\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\{F(x)-G(x)\}\;a.s.

(6)

Notice that the limit in (

) is positive. On the other hand,

in probability under the hypothesis, see Lemma

in the appendix.
Therefore, given a level of significance

, where the

under

. If

Notice that the limit in (

.
Now, if for instance

is small, because

Consequently, the factor becomes significantly greater than

, then the factor is greater than

. For that reason we strongly expect that the V-test is much more likely to detect the alternative than the Smirnov-test.

Example 1
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta>1
. Define
F=F_{\tau,\delta}
by

F(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad,\quad}l}\delta G(x)&x\leq\tau\\
\beta(G(x)-G(\tau))+\delta G(\tau)&x>\tau,\end{array}\right.

If
\delta G(\tau)<1
and
\beta>0
satisfies (*)
\delta G(\tau)+\beta(1-G(\tau))=1
, then

is a continuous distribution function. Further, one verifies that

D(x):=F(x)-G(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad,\quad}l}(\delta-\beta)(1-G(\tau))G(x)&x\leq\tau\\
(\delta-\beta)G(\tau)(1-G(x))&x>\tau.\end{array}\right.

Since
\delta>1
and therefore by (*)
\beta<1
we see that
\delta-\beta
is positive and hence

. Moreover, it follows that
\tau
is a maximizing point of

with

. This shows that

and

lie in the alternative

. If in addition

is the unique maximizer.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
F=G
and that
m=np
with
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for each

the distribution function

of

is given by

J_{nm}(x)=\mathbb{P}(T_{nm}\leq x)=\sum_{r=1}^{n+m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{[\kappa x]\wedge m}P_{nm}(r,k)+\frac{m}{(n+m)(n+m-1)},

(7)

where

\kappa=m\sqrt{r(n+m-r)}.

For
x<0
the probability is equal to zero.

Proof.

Since the set in (
1
) is the range of
(R_{nm},D_{nm})
, a decomposition yields:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(T_{nm}\leq x)=\sum_{r=1}^{n+m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}(R_{nm}=r,D_{nm}=\frac{k}{m},T_{nm}\leq x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{n+m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}\Big(R_{nm}=r,D_{nm}=\frac{k}{m},\frac{\frac{k}{m}}{\sqrt{r(n+m-r)}}\leq x\Big)

(8)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}\Big(R_{nm}=n+m,D_{nm}=\frac{k}{m},0\leq x\Big).

(9)

Since
\frac{\frac{k}{m}}{\sqrt{r(n+m-r)}}\leq x\Leftrightarrow k\leq[m\sqrt{r(n+m-r)}\;x]
, it follows that the sum in (
8
) is equal to the first summand in (
7
). As to the sum in (
9
) observe that for
r=n+m
the second requirement in (
2
) is equivalent to

. Consequently,
P_{nm}(n+m,k)=0
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and therefore (by

),

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}\Big(R_{nm}=n+m,D_{nm}=\frac{k}{m},0\leq x\Big)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}P_{nm}(n+m,k)=P_{nm}(n+m,0).

(10)

The pair
(n+m,0)
with

satisfies condition (
2
), whence after some simple algebra one obtains that
P_{nm}(n+m,0)=\frac{m}{(n+m)(n+m-1)}
, which is the second summand in (

). Finally, since

, it follows that
\mathbb{P}(T_{nm}\leq x)=0
for all negative

.
∎

3
Asymptotic null-distribution

We derive the asymptotic distribution of
T_{nm}
under the hypothesis
H_{0}
. It turns out that
\sqrt{nm(n+m)}T_{nm}
converges to the

Maxwell-Boltzmann
distribution, which has distribution function

K(x)=2\Phi(x)-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}x\exp\{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\}-1,\;x\geq 0,

and
K(x)=0
for all negative
x
. Here,
\Phi
is the distribution function of the standard normal law
N(0,1)
.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
H_{0}
holds. Then under the limiting regime (
3
),

K_{nm}(x):=\mathbb{P}\Big(\sqrt{nm(n+m)}T_{nm}\leq x\Big)\rightarrow K(x)

for all
x\geq 0
. For negative
x
the
probabilities
K_{nm}(x),n,m\in\mathbb{N},
are equal to zero.

Proof.

Recall that
(R_{nm},D_{nm})
is distribution free under
H_{0}
, whence we can assume that
F
and
G
both are equal to the distribution function of
U(0,1)
, the uniform distribution on the
unit interval
(0,1)
. So,
F
is equal to the identity map
Id
on
[0,1]
. We start with the representation

\sqrt{nm(n+m)}\;T_{nm}:=\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{\frac{nm}{n+m}}D_{nm}}{\sqrt{\frac{R_{nm}}{n+m}(1-\frac{R_{nm}}{n+m})}}&\text{ if }R_{nm}\in\{1,\ldots,n+m-1\},\\
0&\text{ if }R_{nm}=n+m.\end{cases}

(11)

Note that
D_{nm}=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\{F_{n}(x)-G_{m}(x)\}
a.s.
Observe that by (
4
)

||H_{nm}-Id||:=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|H_{nm}(x)-x|\rightarrow\ \;\text{ a.s.}

(12)

Introduce

\tau_{nm}:=Z_{(R_{nm})}
.
Since
\frac{R_{nm}}{n+m}=H_{nm}(Z_{(R_{nm})})=H_{nm}(\tau_{nm})
, it follows from (
12
) that

\bigg|\frac{R_{nm}}{n+m}-\tau_{nm}\bigg|=|H_{nm}(\tau_{nm})-\tau_{nm}|\leq||H_{nm}-Id||\rightarrow 0\;\text{ a.s.}

As a consequence the sequences

(\sqrt{\frac{nm}{n+m}}D_{nm},\frac{R_{nm}}{n+m})
and
(\sqrt{\frac{nm}{n+m}}D_{nm},\tau_{nm})
are asymptotical stochastically equivalent.

Our basic idea is to write
(\sqrt{\frac{nm}{n+m}}D_{nm},\tau_{nm})
as a functional of the two-sample empirical process

\alpha_{nm}(x):=\sqrt{\frac{nm}{n+m}}\{F_{n}(x)-G_{m}(x)\},\;x\in[0,1].

To that let
(D[0,1],s)
be the Shorokhod-space. For every
f\in D[0,1]
define
M(f):=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}f(t)
and
A(f):=\{t\in[0,1]:\max\{f(t),f(t-)\}=M(f)\}
with the convention
f(0-):=f(0)
. By Lemma A.2 in Ferger
Ferger1

A(f)
is a non-empty compact subset of [0,1], whence the
argsup-functional

a(f):=\min A(f)
is well defined. By definition of

R_{nm}
the random variable
\tau_{nm}
is the smallest maximizing point of
F_{n}-G_{m}
, i.e.
\tau_{nm}=a(F_{n}-G_{m})
.
Since
a(cf)=a(f)
for every positive constant
c
, it follows that

\tau_{nm}=a(\alpha_{nm})
. Similarly,
M(cf)=cM(f)
and thus
\sqrt{\frac{nm}{n+m}}D_{nm}=M(\alpha_{nm})
. The functional
L:=(M,a):(D[0,1],s)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is Borel-measurable by Lemma A.3 in Ferger
Ferger1
and
continuous on the subset
C_{u}:=\{f\in C[0,1]:f\text{ has a unique maximizing point}\}\subseteq D[0,1]
. To see this
note that
M
is continuous on
C[0,1]\supseteq C_{u}
and
a
is continuous on
C_{u}
by Lemma A.4 in Ferger
Ferger1
. It is well-known that

\alpha_{nm}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\rightarrow}}B
in
(D[0,1],s)
, where
B
is a Brownian bridge.
Now,
B\in C_{u}
almost surely, whence an application of the
Continuous Mapping Theorem (CMT) yields that

(\sqrt{\frac{nm}{n+m}}D_{nm},\tau_{nm})=L(\alpha_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\rightarrow}}L(B)=(M(B),a(B))

and by the asymptotical stochastical equivalence we obtain that

(\sqrt{\frac{nm}{n+m}}D_{nm},\frac{R_{nm}}{n+m})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\rightarrow}}(M(B),a(B)).

Let
h:\mathbb{R}\times(0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined by
h(x,y):=x/\sqrt{y(1-y)}
for
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)=:G
and
h(x,y):=0
otherwise.
Obviously

is continuous on
G
. Moreover,
a(B)\in(0,1)
almost surely. (Indeed,

is uniformly distributed on
(0,1)

, which says that the distribution of

Two-sided alternatives

Consider the test problem
H_{0}:F=G
against the alternative
H_{1}^{*}:F\neq G.
Here, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test rejects the hypothesis in the case of large values of
D_{nm}^{*}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|F_{n}(x)-G_{m}(x)|.
Since
D_{nm}^{*}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n+m}|F_{n}(Z_{(i)})-G_{m}(Z_{(i)})|
, the random variable
R_{nm}^{*}:=\min\{1\leq i\leq n+m:|F_{n}(Z_{(i)})-G_{m}(Z_{(i)})|=D_{nm}^{*}\}

is the smallest index among those order statistics
Z_{(i)}
at which the reflected two-sample process

\{|F_{n}(x)-G_{m}(x)|:x\in\mathbb{R}\}
attains its maximal value. Now, the counterpart
T_{nm}^{*}
of
T_{nm}
is given by:

T_{nm}^{*}:=\begin{cases}\frac{D_{nm}^{*}}{\sqrt{R_{nm}^{*}(n+m-R_{nm}^{*})}}&\text{ if }R_{nm}^{*}\in\{1,\ldots,n+m-1\},\\
0&\text{ if }R_{nm}^{*}=n+m.\end{cases}

Theorem 4
.

If
F=G
, then
\mathbb{P}(\sqrt{nm(n+m)}T_{nm}^{*}\leq x)\rightarrow K^{*}(x)
,
where

\displaystyle K^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16\sum_{0\leq j<l<\infty}(-1)^{j+l}\frac{\alpha_{j}\alpha_{l}}{\alpha_{l}^{2}-\alpha_{j}^{2}}[\frac{\Phi(\alpha_{j}x)-1/2}{\alpha_{j}}-\frac{\Phi(\alpha_{l}x)-1/2}{\alpha_{l}}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4\sum_{0\leq j<\infty}[\frac{\Phi(\alpha_{j}x)-1/2}{\alpha_{j}}-x\varphi(\alpha_{j}x)],\quad x\geq 0.

Here,
\alpha_{j}=2j+1,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
and

.

Proof.

One shows completely analogously to the proof of Theorem

\sqrt{nm(n+m)}T_{nn}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\rightarrow}}h(M(|B|),a(|B|))=:Z^{*}.

Thus the assertion follows from Theorem 1.1 of

, which gives the distribution of

.
∎

Lemma 1

Let

(15)

It follows from Theorem 3.3 in Ferger

that
\tau_{nm}\rightarrow\tau
, whence (

Conclude with (

(16)

We know that

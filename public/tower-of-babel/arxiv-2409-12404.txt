Cycle-assigning polynomials

In this section, we focus on the study of cycle-assigning polynomials, exploring their structural properties and their role in counting
(A,f)
-colorings. Let us review some necessary notations and definitions on graphs. Throughout this paper, we always denote by
S^{E}
the collection of mappings from a finite set
E
to a set
S
, unless otherwise stated. The elements of
S^{E}
are considered as vectors indexed by
E
. An edge with identical ends is called a
loop
, and an edge with distinct ends is a
link
. A graph
H
is a
subgraph
of
G
if
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
. When
V(H)=V(G)
, the subgraph
H
of
G
is referred to as a

. For an edge subset
S\subseteq E(G)
,
G-S
is the subgraph obtained by removing the edges in
S
. Denote by
G|S
the spanning subgraph with edges
S
, that is,
G|S=G-(E(G)\smallsetminus S)
. Conversely, suppose
H
is a proper subgraph containing no edge
e\in E(G)
, we denote by
H+e
the subgraph of
G
by adding the edge
e
. In addition, if
e
is a link of
G
, the graph

is obtained by contracting

, that is, identifying the ends of

to create a single new vertex and then removing
e
.

Unless otherwise stated, we always choose an arbitrary but fixed reference orientation
D
for
G
. For every cycle
C
of
G
, the orientation
D
of
G
gives rise to a map
\eta_{D(C)}
from the edge set

to the set

defined by

\eta_{D(C)}(e):=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if $e\in E(C)$, the direction of $D(e)$ is clockwise with respect to $C$};\\
-1,&\mbox{if $e\in E(C)$, the direction of $D(e)$ is counterclockwise with respect to $C$};\\
0,&\mbox{if $e\in E(G)\smallsetminus E(C)$},\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
D(e)
denotes the corresponding arc of the edge

with respect to
D
.
Remarkably, each function
f:E(G)\to A
automatically induces an assigning
\alpha_{D,f}
on

such that for each cycle

of

\alpha_{D,f}(C)=\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{ if }\sum_{e\in E(C)}\eta_{D(C)}(e)f(e)=0;\\
1,&\mbox{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

We say that

is

-

, or simply

Recall from
Section 1
that the counting function
P\big(D(G),f;A\big)
is the number of
(A,f)
-colorings of

associated with the orientation

and a function

. To derive an explicit formula for

, the Möbius Inversion Formula in

is required. It states that if the function

is defined as

g(S):=\sum_{S\subseteq X\subseteq E(G)}h(X)\quad\mbox{ for }\quad\forall\,S\subseteq E(G),

then, for all
S\subseteq E(G)
,

h(S)=\sum_{S\subseteq X\subseteq E(G)}(-1)^{|X|-|S|}g(X),

(2.2)

where

is a function from the family

of all edge subsets of

to

.

The following result shows that the counting function
P\big(D(G),f;A\big)
can be expressed in terms of the
\alpha_{D,f}
-compatible spanning subgraph expansion.

Theorem 2.1

(Counting Formula)
.

Let
f:E(G)\to A
and
\alpha=\alpha_{D,f}
. Then

P\big(D(G),f;A\big)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mbox{$H$ is an $\alpha$-compatible}\\
\mbox{spanning subgraph of $G$}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|E(H)|}|A|^{c(H)}.

Proof.

For any
S\subseteq E(G)
, let
C_{S}\big(D(G),f;A\big)
be the number of vertex colorings
c:V(G)\to A
such that
c(v)-c(u)=f(e)
for each edge
e=uv\in S
with
D(e)=(u,v)
, and
P_{S}\big(D(G),f;A\big)
be the number of vertex colorings
c:V(G)\to A
such that
c(v)-c(u)=f(e)
for each edge
e=uv\in S
with
D(e)=(u,v)
and
c(v)-c(u)\neq f(e)
for each edge
e=uv\in E(G)\smallsetminus S
with
D(e)=(u,v)
. Then we have

C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,f;A\big)=C_{S}\big(D(G),f;A\big),\quad\quad P\big(D(G),f;A\big)=P_{\emptyset}\big(D(G),f;A\big)

and

C_{\emptyset}\big(D(G),f;A\big)=\sum_{S\subseteq E(G)}P_{S}\big(D(G),f;A\big).

It follows from (
2.2
) that

P\big(D(G),f;A\big)=\sum_{S\subseteq E(G)}(-1)^{|S|}C_{S}\big(D(G),f;A\big)=\sum_{S\subseteq E(G)}(-1)^{|S|}C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,f;A\big).

(2.3)

In addition,
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
demonstrates that
G
admits a vertex coloring
c
such that
c(v)-c(u)=f(e)
for any
e=uv\in E(G)
with
D(e)=(u,v)
if and only if the function
f
satisfies the condition
\sum_{e\in E(C)}\eta_{D(C)}(e)f(e)=0
for every cycle
C
of
G
. This condition is equivalent to
\alpha(C)=0
for all cycles
C
of
G
since
\alpha=\alpha_{D,f}
. Therefore,
C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,f;A\big)=0
whenever
G|S
is an
\alpha
-incompatible subgraph of
G
. Then,
P\big(D(G),f;A\big)
in (
2.3
) can be simplified to the following form

P\big(D(G),f;A\big)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq E(G),\\
G|S\mbox{ is $\alpha_{G|S}$-compatible}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|S|}C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,f;A\big).

(2.4)

Denote by
C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,f_{S};A\big)
the collection of vertex colorings
c:V(G)\to A
of
G|S
such that
c(v)-c(u)=f(e)
for each edge
e=uv\in S
with
D(e)=(u,v)
. Then, for a fixed vertex coloring
c\in C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,f_{S};A\big)
, every vertex coloring

can gives rise to a unique vertex coloring
c^{\prime}-c\in C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,0_{S};A\big)
. Conversely, every vertex coloring
c^{\prime\prime}\in C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,0_{S};A\big)
also gives rise to a unique vertex coloring
c+c^{\prime\prime}\in C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,f_{S};A\big)
. Notice

C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,f;A\big)=|C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,f_{S};A\big)|\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,0;A\big)=|C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,0_{S};A\big)|.

It follows that

C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,f;A\big)=C_{S}\big(D(G)|S,0;A\big)=|A|^{c(G|S)}.

Together with (
2.4
), we have

P\big(D(G),f;A\big)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq E(G),\\
G|S\mbox{ is $\alpha_{G|S}$-compatible}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|S|}|A|^{c(G|S)},

which completes the proof.

Theorem 2.4

(Deletion-contraction Formula)
.

Let
\alpha
be an assigning of
G
. Then the cycle-assigning polynomial satisfies

P(G,\alpha;k)=\begin{cases}k^{|V(G)|},&\mbox{ if }E(G)=\emptyset;\\
\frac{k-1}{k}P(G-e,\alpha_{G-e};k),&\mbox{ if }e\mbox{ is a bridge};\\
\alpha(e)P(G-e,\alpha_{G-e};k),&\mbox{ if }e\mbox{ is a loop};\\
P(G-e,\alpha_{G-e};k)-P(G/e,\alpha_{G/e};k),&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{e}
be the family of
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraphs of
G
containing
e
and
\mathcal{S}_{-e}
be the family of
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraphs of
G
that do not contain
e
. If
e
is a bridge, then a subgraph
H
containing
e
is an
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G
if and only if
H-e
is an
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G
. Therefore, we conclude that every
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph
H
of
G
containing
e
gives rise to a unique
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph
H-e
of
G
that does not contain
e
, and vice versa. Then we have

P(G,\alpha;k)=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{-e}}(-1)^{|E(H+e)|}k^{c(H+e)}+\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{-e}}(-1)^{|E(H)|}k^{c(H)}.

Since
e
is a bridge,
c(H+e)=c(H)-1
for any
H\in\mathcal{S}_{-e}
. Hence, we deduce

P(G,\alpha;k)=\frac{k-1}{k}P(G-e,\alpha_{G-e};k).

If
e
is a loop, we need to consider both cases:
\alpha(e)=0
and
\alpha(e)=1
. For the former case, similarly to the case where
e
is a bridge, we can easily derive
P(G,\alpha;k)=0
. For the latter case, it is clear that
H
is an
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G
if and only if
H
is an
\alpha_{G-e}
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G-e
. So, we arrive at
P(G,\alpha;k)=P(G-e,\alpha_{G-e};k)
.

If
e
is neither a bridge nor a loop, then
H
is an
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G
containing no
e
if and only if
H
is an
\alpha_{G-e}
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G-e
, and
H
is an
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G
containing
e
if and only if
H/e
is an
\alpha_{G/e}
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G/e
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle P(G,\alpha;k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{-e}}(-1)^{|E(H)|}k^{c(H)}+\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{e}}(-1)^{|E(H)|}k^{c(H)}

\displaystyle=P(G-e,\alpha_{G-e};k)+\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{e}}(-1)^{|E(H/e)|+1}k^{c(H/e)}

\displaystyle=P(G-e,\alpha_{G-e};k)-P(G/e,\alpha_{G/e};k),

which finishes the proof.
∎

In addition, the cycle-assigning polynomial
P(G,\alpha;k)
has the following simple decomposition formula.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\alpha
be an assigning of
G
and
G=G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup G_{i}
, where
G_{j}
is the component of
G
for
j=1,2,\ldots,i
. Then

P(G,\alpha;k)=\prod_{j=1}^{i}P(G_{j},\alpha_{G_{j}};k).

Proof.

Since
G
decomposes into the union of the components
G_{j}
of
G
, each
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G
is partitioned into the disjoint union of the
\alpha_{G_{j}}
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G_{j}
, and vice versa. Therefore, we can deduce
P(G,\alpha;k)=\prod_{j=1}^{i}P(G_{j},\alpha_{G_{j}};k)
, which finishes the proof.
∎

At the end of the section, we list the cycle-assigning polynomials for the empty graph, a tree and the cycle on
n
vertices.

Example 2.6
.

(1)

Let
O_{n}
be the empty graph on
n
vertices. Then
\alpha\equiv 0
and

P(O_{n},0;k)=k^{n}.

(2)

Let
T_{n}
be a tree on
n
vertices. Then
\alpha\equiv 0
and

P(T_{n},0;k)=k(k-1)^{n-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\binom{n-1}{i-1}k^{i}.

(3)

Let
C_{n}
be a cycle on
n
vertices. Combining part (2) and
Theorem 2.4
, we have

P(C_{n},\alpha;k)=\begin{cases}(k-1)^{n}+(-1)^{n}(k-1),&\mbox{ if }\alpha(C_{n})=0;\\
(k-1)^{n}+(-1)^{n+1},&\mbox{ if }\alpha(C_{n})=1.\end{cases}

3
Polynomials counting nowhere-zero chains associated with homomorphisms

In this section, we mainly clarify the close relation between the cycle-assigning polynomial and the
\alpha
-assigning polynomial, which was first introduced by Kochol
[
13
]
to count nowhere-zero chains in graphs–nonhomogeneous analogues of nowhere-zero flows. Most recently, Kochol
[
14
]
further extended the approach to regular matroids. For this purpose, we first review some basic definitions about regular chain groups associated to regular matroids. For additional properties of regular chain groups, refer to the references
[
1
,
29
]
.

A regular matroid
M=M(U)
on a ground set
E
is represented by a totally unimodular matrix
U
. A more general setting and background on matroids can be found in Oxley’s book
[
25
]
. A
chain
on
E
over an Abelian group
A
is a map
f\in A^{E}
, and its support
\sigma(f)
is
\sigma(f):=\{f(e)\neq 0\mid e\in E\}
. We say that
f
is
proper
if
\sigma(f)=E
. Associated with
U
or
M(U)
, a
regular chain group

N
on
E
over
\mathbb{Z}
is the set of integral vectors orthogonal to each row of
U
. The set of chains orthogonal to every chain of
N
forms a chain group called
orthogonal to

N
, denoted by
N^{\perp}
. Actually,
N^{\perp}
consists of the integral vectors from the line combinations of the rows of
U
. A chain
f
of
N
is
elementary
if there is a no nonzero chain
g
of
N
such that
\sigma(g)\subset\sigma(f)
. An elementary chain
f
of
N
is said to be
primitive
if
f(e)\in\{\pm 1,0\}
for all
e\in E
. In this section, we always assume that a regular chain group
N
is associated with a totally unimodular matrix
U(N)
representing a regular matroid
M=M(N)
.

Let
A
be an additive Abelian group. We shall consider
A
as a (right)
\mathbb{Z}
-module such that the scalar multiplication
a\cdot z
of
a\in A
by
z\in\mathbb{Z}
equals zero if
z=0
,
\sum_{1}^{z}a
if
z>0
, and
\sum_{1}^{-z}(-a)
if
z<0
. Analogously, if
a\in A
and
f\in\mathbb{Z}^{E}
, then define
a\cdot f\in A^{E}
such that
(a\cdot f)(e)=a\cdot f(e)
for every
e\in E
. We shall denote by
A(N)
the
A
-chain group generated by the regular chain group
N
, that is,

A(N):=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\cdot f_{i}\mid a_{i}\in A,f_{i}\in N,m\geq 1\Big\}.

Let
A[N]
be the collection of all proper
A
-chains from
A(N)
, i.e.,

A[N]:=\big\{f\in A(N)\mid\sigma(f)=E\big\}.

From
[
1
, Proposition 1]
, we have

g\in A^{E}\text{ is from }A(N)\Longleftrightarrow\sum_{e\in E}g(e)\cdot f(e)=0\text{ for any }f\in N^{\perp}.

(3.1)

Let
\psi
be a homomorphism from a regular chain group
N
to
A
. Define by

A_{\psi}[N]:=\Big\{g\in(A-0)^{E}\mid\sum_{e\in E}g(e)\cdot f(e)=\psi(f)\text{ for all }f\in N\Big\}.

Each element from
A_{\psi}[N]
is called a
proper
(N,\psi)
-chain
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(N)
be the set of primitive chains of a regular chain group
N
, and
\mathcal{C}(M)
be the family of circuits of
M=M(N)
. By
[
29
]
,
\mathcal{P}(N)
and
\mathcal{C}(M)
have the following relationship

\mathcal{C}(M)=\big\{\sigma(c)\mid c\in\mathcal{P}(N)\big\}.

(3.2)

An
assigning
of
M
is any mapping
\alpha
from
\mathcal{C}(M)
to
\{0,1\}
. Write
\alpha\equiv 0
if
\alpha(C)=0
for all
C\in\mathcal{C}(M)
or
\mathcal{C}(M)=\emptyset
. Let
e\in E
. It follows from the relation
\mathcal{C}(M-e)\subseteq\mathcal{C}(M)
that an assigning
\alpha
of
M
naturally gives rise to an assigning
\alpha[-e]
of
M-e
for which

\alpha[-e](C)=\alpha(C),\quad C\in\mathcal{C}(M-e).

By
[
25
, Proposition 3.1.10]
, we know that

if
C\in\mathcal{C}(M/e)
, then either
C\in\mathcal{C}(M)
or
C\cup e\in\mathcal{C}(M)
.

Similarly, the assigning
\alpha
of
M
also induces an assigning
\alpha[/e]
of
M/e
such that
\alpha[/e](C)=\alpha(C)
if
C\in\mathcal{C}(M/e)\cap\mathcal{C}(M)
, and
\alpha[/e](C)=\alpha(C\cup e)
otherwise.

Moreover, from (
3.2
), the homomorphism
\psi
automatically leads to an assigning
\alpha_{N,\psi}
of
M
such that for every
c\in\mathcal{P}(N)
,

\alpha_{N,\psi}\big(\sigma(c)\big):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\psi(c)=0;\\
1,&\text{if }\psi(c)\neq 0.\end{cases}

An assigning
\alpha
of
M
is said to be
homogeneous
if there is a homomorphism
\psi:N\to A
such that
\alpha=\alpha_{N,\psi}
. Furthermore,
\psi
is
proper
and
\alpha=\alpha_{N,\psi}
is called a
proper assigning
of
M
if
A_{\psi}[N]\neq\emptyset
.

In 2024, Kochol showed in
[
13
, Theorem 1]
that there exists a polynomial
p(M,\alpha;k)
counting the number of nowhere-zero chains in
A_{\psi}[N]
. The polynomial
p(M,\alpha;k)
is called an
\alpha
-assigning polynomial
of
M
by Kochol.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
14
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Suppose
M
is a regular matroid on
E
and
\alpha
is a homogeneous assigning of
M
. Then, there exists a polynomial
p(M,\alpha;k)
, such that
p(M,\alpha;k)=|A_{\psi}[N]|
for every regular chain group
N
on
E
and
M(N)=M
, every Abelian group
A
of order
k
, and every homomorphism
\psi:N\to A
satisfies
\alpha_{N,\psi}=\alpha
. If
\alpha
is proper, then
p(M,\alpha;k)
has degree
r(M)
, and
p(M,\alpha;k)=0
otherwise. Furthermore, for any
e\in E
,
\alpha[-e]
and
\alpha[/e]
are homogeneous assignings of
M-e
and
M/e
, respectively, and

p(M,\alpha;k)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $E=\emptyset$};\\
(k-1)p\big(M-e,\alpha[-e];k\big),&\text{if $e$ is a bridge};\\
\alpha(e)p\big(M-e,\alpha[-e];k\big),&\text{if $e$ is a loop};\\
p\big(M-e,\alpha[-e];k\big)-p\big(M/e,\alpha[/e];k\big),&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{cases}

Below we shall explore some applications of the
\alpha
-assigning polynomial to colorings and tensions of
G
. The
incidence matrix
of
D(G)
is the
|V(D)|\times|A(D)|
matrix
M_{D(G)}:=(m_{va})
whose rows and columns are indexed by the vertices and arcs, where, for a vertex
v
and arc
a

m_{va}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if arc }a\text{ is a link and vertex }v\text{ is the head of }a;\\
-1,&\text{ if arc }a\text{ is a link and vertex }v\text{ is the tail of }a;\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
c\in A^{V(G)}
, then the
coboundary
of
c
, with respect to the orientation
D
, is the map
\delta c\in A^{E(G)}
given by

\delta c(e):=\sum_{v\in V(G)}m_{vD(e)}c(v).

In this sense, we call a mapping
t\in A^{E(G)}
a
tension
or an
A
-tension
if there exists a vertex coloring
c\in A^{V(G)}
such that
t=\delta c
. Similar to
(A,f)
-coloring associated with a mapping
f\in A^{E(G)}
, a tension
t
is referred to as an
(A,f)
-tension
if it satisfies
t(e)\neq f(e)
for all
e\in E(G)
. In particular, taking
f\equiv 0
, the
(A,0)
-tension is exactly the
nowhere-zero
A
-tension
. It is clear that all
A
-tensions form an (additive) Abelian subgroup of
A^{E(G)}
, denoted by
\Delta(G,A)
. Let
\Delta_{f}(G,A)
be the set of all
(A,f)
-tensions of
G
for
f\in A^{E(G)}
. The number of nowhere-zero tensions is evaluated by a polynomial
\tau(G;k)
in
k
introduced by Tutte
[
28
]
. This was developed and formally named the
tension polynomial
of
G
by Kochol
[
11
]
in 2002. Motivated by their work, a natural question arises:

•

Is there a polynomial function of
|A|
that generalizes the tension polynomial and counts
(A,f)
-tensions in graphs?

The answer to the question was implicit in the work of Kochol
[
14
]
. By applying
Theorem 3.1
, we shall present a precise answer in
Section 3
.

Let
M(G)
be the cycle matroid of
G
represented by the incidence matrix
M_{D(G)}
(
M(G)
is a regular matroid, see
[
25
, Proposition 5.1.5]
), and
\Gamma(G)
be the regular chain group on
E(G)
associated with
M_{D(G)}
. From
[
29
]
, the mapping
\eta_{D(C)}
defined in (
2.1
) is the primitive chain of
\Gamma(G)
for every cycle
C
of
G
, and
\Gamma(G)
is generated by
\big\{\eta_{D(C)}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}(G)\big\}
. In addition, the regular chain group
\Delta(G)
on
E(G)
over
\mathbb{Z}
consisting of all line combinations of rows from the matrix
M_{D(G)}
is exactly orthogonal
to
\Gamma(G)
, i.e.,
\Delta(G)=\Gamma(G)^{\perp}
. Again, from
[
29
]
, the chain group
\Delta(G,A)
can be generated by
\Delta(G)
. Then we have the following result.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\alpha
be an admissible assigning of
G
. Then
\tau\big(G,\alpha;k\big)
and
p\big(M(G),\alpha_{\Gamma(G),\psi};k\big)
are the same polynomial, and they satisfy that

\tau\big(G,\alpha;k\big)=p\big(M(G),\alpha_{\Gamma(G),\psi};k\big)=|A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]|=|\Delta_{f}(G,A)|

for every Abelian group
A
of order
k
, every function
f:E(G)\to A
satisfying
\alpha_{D,f}=\alpha
, and every homomorphism
\psi:\Gamma(G)\to A
such that
\psi\big(\eta_{D(C)}\big)=-\sum_{e\in E(G)}f(e)\cdot\eta_{D(C)}(e)
for any cycle
C
of
G
. Furthermore,
\tau\big(G,\alpha;k\big)
has degree
|V(G)|-c(G)
if
\alpha(C)=1
for any loop
C
of
G
and equals zero otherwise, and
\tau\big(G,\alpha;k\big)
also satisfies

\tau(G,\alpha;k)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $E(G)=\emptyset$};\\
(k-1)\tau(G-e,\alpha_{G-e};k),&\text{if $e$ is a bridge};\\
\alpha(e)\tau(G-e,\alpha_{G-e};k),&\text{if $e$ is a loop};\\
\tau\big(G-e,\alpha_{G-e};k\big)-\tau(G/e,\alpha_{G/e};k),&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{cases}

To prove
Section 3
, the following lemmas are required.

Lemma 3.3
.

Given a mapping
f\in A^{E(G)}
. Let

A_{f}[G]=\big\{g\in(A-0)^{E(G)}\mid g+f\in\Delta(G,A)\big\}.

Then the mapping
\phi:\Delta_{f}(G,A)\to A_{f}[G]
sending
g\in\Delta_{f}(G,A)
to
\phi(g)=g-f
is a bijection.

Proof.

Taking any element
g\in\Delta_{f}(G,A)
, by
g(e)\neq f(e)
for all
e\in E(G)
, then
\phi(g)\in(A-0)^{E(G)}
and
\phi(g)+f=g\in\Delta_{f}(G,A)\subseteq\Delta(G,A)
. Namely,
\phi
is well-defined. Clearly,
\psi
is injective.

Conversely, define the mapping
\phi^{-1}:A_{f}[G]\to\Delta_{f}(G,A)
sending
g\in A_{f}[G]
to
\phi^{-1}(g)=g+f
. Given an element
g\in A_{f}[G]
. Since
g(e)\neq 0
for every
e\in E(G)
,
\phi^{-1}(g)\in\Delta_{f}(G,A)
. Thus,
\phi^{-1}
is well-defined. The mapping
\phi^{-1}
is an injection obviously. Therefore,
\phi
is bijective. We complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
f\in A^{E(G)}
and the homomorphism
\psi:\Gamma(G)\to A
such that
\psi\big(\eta_{D(C)}\big)=-\sum_{e\in E(G)}f(e)\cdot\eta_{D(C)}(e)
for any cycle
C
of
G
. Then
A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]=A_{f}[G]
.

Proof.

We first show
A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]\subseteq A_{f}[G]
. Note that the chain group
\Delta(G,A)
is generated by the regular chain group
\Delta(G)
and
\Delta(G)
is orthogonal to
\Gamma(G)
. Then, from (
3.1
), this proof reduces to verifying that for any
g\in A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]
,
\sum_{e\in E(G)}(g+f)(e)\cdot h(e)=0
for all
h\in\Gamma(G)
. Since
\Gamma(G)
is generated by
\big\{\eta_{D(C)}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}(G)\big\}
, the proof is further equivalent to checking that for any
g\in A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]
,

\sum_{e\in E(G)}(g+f)(e)\cdot\eta_{D(C)}(e)=0,\quad\forall\,C\in\mathcal{C}(G).

Since
\sum_{e\in E(G)}g(e)\cdot\eta_{D(C)}(e)=\psi\big(\eta_{D(C)}\big)
for every
g\in A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]
, it follows from
\psi\big(\eta_{D(C)}\big)=-\sum_{e\in E(G)}f(e)\cdot\eta_{D(C)}(e)
that
\sum_{e\in E(G)}(g+f)(e)\cdot\eta_{D(C)}(e)
is indeed equal to zero. This implies that
g+f\in\Delta(G,A)
for any
g\in A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]
, that is,
A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]\subseteq A_{f}[G]
.

For the opposite inclusion, for any
g\in A_{f}[G]
, we have
g+f\in\Delta(G,A)
. Again, using (
3.1
), we have
\sum_{e\in E(G)}(g+f)(e)\cdot\eta_{D(C)}(e)=0
for all cycles
C
of
G
. It follows that

\sum_{e\in E(G)}g(e)\cdot\eta_{D(C)}(e)=-\sum_{e\in E(G)}f(e)\cdot\eta_{D(C)}(e)=\psi\big(\eta_{D(C)}\big).

This means
g\in A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]
for each
g\in A_{f}[G]
. Namely, we have
A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]\supseteq A_{f}[G]
. Then we arrive at
A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]=A_{f}[G]
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Now we proceed to prove
Section 3
.

Proof of
Section 3
.

Note that any
(A,f)
-coloring
c
of
G
yields an
(A,f)
-tension
\delta c
of
G
, and vice versa. We assert that for every
(A,f)
-tension
t
, there are exactly
|A|^{c(G)}

(A,f)
-colorings
c
satisfying
\delta c=t
. Suppose
t
is an
(A,f)
-tension of
G
satisfying
t=\delta c
for some
c\in A^{V(G)}
. We construct a mapping
c^{\prime}\in A^{V(G)}
such that
c^{\prime}(u)-c(u)=c^{\prime}(v)-c(v)
whenever vertices
u
and
v
belong to the same component of
G
. It follows that
c^{\prime}
is a
(A,f)
-coloring and
\delta c^{\prime}=t
. On the other hand, if
\delta c^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=t
for another
(A,f)
-coloring
c^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
, then
c^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(u)-c(u)=c^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(v)-c(v)
whenever
u
and
v
are in the same component of
G
. Thus, we have shown the assertion. It follows from
Theorem 2.2
that

\tau\big(G,\alpha;k\big)=|\Delta_{f}(G,A)|.

(3.3)

Applying
Theorem 3.1
, we have

p\big(M(G),\alpha_{\Gamma(G),\psi};k\big)=|A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]|.

(3.4)

In addition, by
Section 3
and
Section 3
, we obtain

|\Delta_{f}(G,A)|=|A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]|.

(3.5)

Immediately, by integration of (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we arrive at

\tau\big(G,\alpha;k\big)=p\big(M(G),\alpha_{\Gamma(G),\psi};k\big)=|A_{\psi}[\Gamma(G)]|=|\Delta_{f}(G,A)|.

It should be noted that both
\tau(G,\alpha;k)
and
p\big(M(G),\alpha_{\Gamma(G),\psi};k\big)
are also polynomials in
k
of degree at most
|V(G)|-c(G)
and these two
polynomials agree on infinitely many positive integers
k
. Hence, from the fundamental theorem of algebra,
\tau(G,\alpha;k)
and
p\big(M(G),\alpha_{\Gamma(G),\psi};k\big)
are the same polynomial.

In fact, we have
\alpha_{\Gamma(G),\psi}=\alpha_{D,f}=\alpha
from (
2.1
). Moreover,
[
14
, Theorem 3]
states that

\text{a homogeneous assigning $\alpha$ of $M(G)$ is proper $\Longleftrightarrow$ $\alpha(e)=1$ for each loop $e$ of $M(G)$}.

Together with
Section 3
and
r(M(G))=|V(G)|-c(G)
, we have that
\tau\big(G,\alpha;k\big)
has degree
|V(G)|-c(G)
if
\alpha(C)=1
for any loop
C
of
G
and equals zero otherwise. Finally, the remaining part is a direct result of
Theorem 2.4
via
\tau(G,\alpha;k)=k^{-c(G)}P(G,\alpha;k)
. We complete the proof.
∎

Notably,
[
11

The upcoming theorem expresses the coefficients of

in terms of
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraphs that do not contain broken

-compatible cycles, which is an extended version of the Whitney’s celebrated Broken Cycle Theorem given by Whitney
[

]
. It is worth noting that the coefficients

in

are independent of the choice of edge order.

Theorem 4.3

(Generalized Whitney’s Broken Cycle Theorem)
.

Let
\alpha
be an admissible assigning of
G
and
\prec
a linear ordering on
E(G)
. Write
P(G,\alpha;k)=\sum_{i=0}^{r(G)}(-1)^{i}w_{i}(G,\alpha)k^{|V(G)|-i}
. Then each unsigned coefficient
w_{i}(G,\alpha)
is the number of
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraphs of
G
that have exactly
i
edges and do not contain broken
\alpha
-compatible cycles with respect to
\prec
.

Proof.

Suppose
G
has
q\geq 0
broken
\alpha
-compatible cycles. For every broken
\alpha
-compatible cycle
B
, let
e_{B}
be the maximal member in
B
with respect to the linear ordering
\prec
. We can arrange the broken
\alpha
-compatible cycles
B_{1},\ldots,B_{q}
such that
e_{B_{1}}\preceq e_{B_{2}}\cdots\preceq e_{B_{q}}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{1}
be the collection of
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraphs of
G
containing the broken
\alpha
-compatible cycle
B_{1}
. For
i=2,\ldots,q
, let
\mathcal{S}_{i}
be the collection of
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraphs of
G
containing the broken
\alpha
-compatible cycle
B_{i}
but not any of
B_{1},\ldots,B_{i-1}
. In addition, let
\mathcal{S}_{q+1}
be the collection of
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraphs of
G
containing no broken
\alpha
-compatible cycles. Then
\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{q+1}\}
decomposes the set of
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraphs of
G
. Combining
Theorem 2.2
, the cycle-assigning polynomial
P(G,\alpha;k)
can be written in the form

P(G,\alpha;k)=\sum_{i=1}^{q+1}\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}(-1)^{|E(H)|}k^{c(H)}.

(4.1)

We first consider
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Assume
B_{1}=C_{1}-e_{C_{1}}
for some
\alpha
-compatible cycle
C_{1}
of
G
, where
e_{C_{1}}
is the maximal member in
C_{1}
with respect to
\prec
. Clearly each member
H
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
containing
e_{C_{1}}
gives rise to a unique member
H-e_{C_{1}}
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
that does not contain
e_{C_{1}}
. Conversely, according to
Section 4
, each member
H
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
containing no
e_{C_{1}}
gives rise to a unique member
H+e_{C_{1}}
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
containing
e_{C_{1}}
. In addition, since
B_{1}+e_{C_{1}}=C_{1}
is a cycle of
G
, for each
H\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
containing
e_{C_{1}}
, we have
c(H)=c(H-e_{C_{1}})
. Thus, we derive

\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\,e_{C_{1}}\in E(H)}\big((-1)^{|E(H)|}k^{c(H)}+(-1)^{|E(H)|-1}k^{c(H-e_{C_{1}})}\big)=0.

Namely, we arrive at
\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}(-1)^{|E(H)|}k^{c(H)}=0
in (
4.1
).

Consider
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Assume
B_{2}=C_{2}-e_{C_{2}}
for some
\alpha
-compatible cycle
C_{2}
of
G
, where
e_{C_{2}}
is the maximal member in
C_{2}
with respect to
\prec
. It follows from the ordering
e_{B_{1}}\preceq e_{B_{2}}
that
e_{B_{1}}\preceq e_{B_{2}}\prec e_{C_{2}}
. So,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
do not contain
e_{C_{2}}
. Combining this, using the same argument as the case
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, we conclude that every element
H
in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
containing no
e_{C_{2}}
gives rise to a unique member
H+e_{C_{2}}
in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
containing
e_{C_{2}}
, and vice versa. Hence, we also obtain
\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}(-1)^{|E(H)|}k^{c(H)}=0
in (
4.1
).

Likewise, we can deduce the total contribution of the members in
\mathcal{S}_{i}
to
P(G,\alpha;k)
is zero for each
i=1,2,\ldots,q
. Therefore,
P(G,\alpha;k)
in (
4.1
) can be written as

P(G,\alpha;k)=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{q+1}}(-1)^{|E(H)|}k^{c(H)}.

(4.2)

Since every element
H\in\mathcal{S}_{q+1}
is an
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G
and contains no broken
\alpha
-compatible cycles, we can deduce that
H
contains no cycles. Therefore,
H
is a forest. This implies that for any member
H\in\mathcal{S}_{q+1}
,

|E(H)|\leq r(G)\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad c(H)=|V(G)|-|E(H)|.

Thus,
P(G,\alpha;k)
in (
4.2
) can be further simplified to the following form

P(G,\alpha;k)=\sum_{i=0}^{r(G)}(-1)^{i}w_{i}(G,\alpha)k^{|V(G)|-i},

which finishes the proof.

Corollary 4.4

Let

be an admissible assigning of

is the number of

that do not contain broken

Notably, the cycle-assigning polynomial

agrees with the chromatic polynomial

of

is

-compatible. When

is

\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph,

-compatible cycle and broken

, respectively. Hence,

deduces the following Whitney’s Broken Cycle Theorem in
[

(

5
Coefficient comparisons and further explorations

In this section, we primarily apply the Generalized Broken Cycle Theorem to explore how the coefficients of cycle-assigning polynomials vary with changes in admissible assignings and to handle certain group coloring problems in graphs. We first establish a unified order-preserving relation from admissible assignings to cycle-assigning polynomials when both are naturally ordered. Let
\alpha
be an assigning of
G
and
\prec
be a linear ordering on
E(G)
. For convenience, we denote by
\mathcal{C}(G,\alpha)
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(G,\alpha)
the sets of
\alpha
-compatible cycles and broken
\alpha
-compatible cycles of
G
with respect to
\prec
, respectively. Additionally, let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(G,\alpha)
represent the set of
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraphs of
G
that do not contain broken
\alpha
-compatible cycles with respect to
\prec
.

Theorem 5.1

(Comparison of Coefficients)
.

Let
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
be two admissible assignings of
G
. If
\alpha(C)\leq\alpha^{\prime}(C)
for all cycles
C\in\mathcal{C}(G)
, then the unsigned coefficients of the cycle-assigning polynomials
P(G,\alpha;k)
and
P(G,\alpha^{\prime};k)
satisfy

w_{i}(G,\alpha)\leq w_{i}(G,\alpha^{\prime})\quad\mbox{ for }\quad i=0,1,\ldots,r(G).

In particular,
w_{i}(G)=w_{i}(G,0)\leq w_{i}(G,\alpha)
for any admissible assigning
\alpha
of
G
.

Proof.

By
Theorem 4.3
, the proof reduces to showing that every member
H
of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(G,\alpha)
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(G,\alpha^{\prime})
. Given an element
H\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(G,\alpha)
. As
H
is an
\alpha
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G
containing no broken
\alpha
-compatible cycles,
H
does not contain cycles. This implies that
H
is
\alpha^{\prime}
-compatible. On the other hand, we have
\mathcal{C}(G,\alpha)\supseteq\mathcal{C}(G,\alpha^{\prime})
since
\alpha(C)\leq\alpha^{\prime}(C)
for any cycle
C
of
G
. This derives
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(G,\alpha)\supseteq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(G,\alpha^{\prime})
. This further deduces that
H
does not contain broken
\alpha^{\prime}
-compatible cycles. Then, we can conclude that
H
is an
\alpha^{\prime}
-compatible spanning subgraph of
G
containing no broken
\alpha^{\prime}
-compatible cycles. Namely,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(G,\alpha)\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(G,\alpha^{\prime})
. This completes the proof.
∎

When
G
is a loopless graph, each unsigned coefficient
w_{i}(G)
of
P(G;k)
is a positive integer, which is easily obtained from
Theorem 4.5
. Following
Theorem 5.1
, we directly obtain the following result.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\alpha
be an admissible assigining of
G
. Then each unsigned coefficient
w_{i}(G,\alpha)
of the cycle-assigning polynomial
P(G,\alpha;k)
is a positive integer, unless
\alpha(e)=0
for some loop
e
of
G
. Moreover,
w_{0}(G,\alpha)=1
whenever
P(G,\alpha;k)
is not the zero polynomial.

According to the relationship
P(G,\alpha;k)=k^{c(G)}\tau(G,\alpha;k)
, it is worth noting that the coefficients of the
\alpha
-assigning polynomial
\tau(G,\alpha;k)
shares the same properties as the coefficients of the cycle-assigning polynomial
P(G,\alpha;k)
, as outlined in
Theorem 2.5
,
Theorem 4.3
,
Section 4
,
Theorem 5.1
and
Section 5
.

It is a pleasant surprise that the Generalized Broken Cycle Theorem provides a new perspective on addressing certain group coloring problems in graphs. A well-known property in
[
27
]
says that if a graph has a nowhere-zero
A
-flow, then it admits a nowhere-zero
A^{\prime}
-flow for any Abelian group
A^{\prime}
whose order is at least the order of
A
. This does not extend to the more general concept of group connectivity observed by Jaeger et al.
[
10
]
. For a connected planar graph
G
, Jaeger et al.
[
10
]
also showed that
G
is
A
-colorable if and only if the dual graph
G^{*}
is
A
-connected. Naturally, there is a corresponding problem in group colorings: for any Abelian groups
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
with the same order, is a
A_{1}
-colorable graph also
A_{2}
-colorable?

Most recently, Langhede and Thomassen in

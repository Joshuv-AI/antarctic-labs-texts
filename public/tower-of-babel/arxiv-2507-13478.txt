Abstract
. We study the Laplace operator on domains subject to Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions. We show that these operators admit a bounded H ∞ H^{\infty} -functional calculus on weighted Sobolev spaces, where the weights are powers of the distance to the boundary. Our analysis applies to bounded C 1 , λ C^{1,\lambda} -domains with λ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \lambda\in[0,1] , revealing a crucial trade-off: lower domain regularity can be compensated by enlarging the weight exponent. As a primary consequence, we establish maximal regularity for the corresponding heat equation. This extends the well-posedness theory for parabolic equations to domains with minimal smoothness, where classical methods are inapplicable.

Given a bounded

\displaystyle=f,

In particular, one can consider spatial weights of the form

Theorem 1.1

(
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Dirichlet Laplacian)
.

Let

,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
. Furthermore, suppose that

\lambda>1-\tfrac{\gamma+1}{p}\qquad\text{or, equivalently}\qquad\gamma>(1-\lambda)p-1

and
\mathcal{O}
is a bounded
C^{1,\lambda}
-domain.
Then for all
\mu\geq 0
the operator

\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}\quad\text{ on }\quad W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma+kp}^{\partial\mathcal{O}})\quad\text{ with }\quad D(\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})=W^{k+2,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma+kp}^{\partial\mathcal{O}})

has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle zero.

Theorem
1.1

[
69

Furthermore, for
\gamma=p-1
the domain characterisation in Theorem
1.1
in terms of spaces with vanishing traces fails, see
[

, and for this reason we omit this case.

The main novelties of our results are the following.

(1)

We prove the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus, which is, in general, much harder to prove than maximal regularity and yields the boundedness of many singular integral operators
[
41
]
. In particular, boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus implies (stochastic) maximal regularity
[
35
,
75
]
. Maximal regularity and higher-order regularity results for the heat equation with Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions are contained in Section
6.1
. In particular, we recover some maximal regularity results for the Dirichlet Laplacian from
[
51
]
(for bounded
C^{1}
-domains) and
[
53
]
(for bounded
C^{1,\lambda}
-domains and
k=0
). For the latter case, our results with
k\geq 1
are new.
The Neumann Laplacian on the half-space is studied on weighted Sobolev spaces in
[
20
,
21
]
(for
k=0
) and
[
67
]
, but a systematic study on bounded domains seems to be unavailable until now.

(2)

The smoothness of the domain
\mathcal{O}
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
is
independent
of the smoothness
k
of the Sobolev space. The reason for this is that we do not use the standard localisation procedure from the half-space to domains (see, e.g.,
[
16
,
25
,
58
]
). This standard localisation procedure typically works for
C^{k+2}
-domains. Instead, we apply a more sophisticated
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism suitable for the weighted setting. We discuss this in more detail below.

The key ingredient in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
is the perturbation of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus on the half-space (obtained in
[
67
]
) to
special domains
, i.e. domains above the graph of a function with compact support. A common method is to relate the Laplacian on the half-space and on a special domain via a diffeomorphism. However, due to the low regularity of the domain, we cannot use the standard diffeomorphism as in, e.g.,
[
16
,
25
,
58
]
. Instead, we employ the
Dahlberg–Kenig–Stein pullback
. This diffeomorphism dates back to
[
10
]
and is often employed for problems on Lipschitz or
C^{1}
-domains, see for instance
[
12
,
26
,
42
]
and the references therein. This diffeomorphism straightens the boundary and preserves the distance to the boundary. Moreover, higher-order derivatives exist, but blow up near the boundary of the domain. This blow-up is compensated by the weights in our spaces. We consider this diffeomorphism on domains with fractional smoothness by extending the result contained in
[
51
, Lemma 2.6]
and
[
66
]
.

With estimates on this diffeomorphism at hand, we can employ perturbation theorems for the
H^{\infty}
-calculus to extend the results to special domains. Another difficulty arising in this perturbation argument is that, if the regularity of the domain is too low, then the perturbations are of the same order as the Laplacian. It is known that the
H^{\infty}
-calculus is not stable under small perturbations
[
72
]
. Additionally, we need the perturbations to be well behaved with respect to a fractional power of the original operator. This requires the identification of certain complex interpolation spaces and fractional domains to perform the perturbation argument.
Finally, by another localisation argument, based on lower-order perturbations, the
H^{\infty}
-calculus on special domains is transferred to bounded domains.

We comment on some related and open problems. Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
provide the bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus on Sobolev spaces with integer smoothness, and with complex interpolation, the bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus can also be obtained on spaces with fractional smoothness. However, an intrinsic characterisation of these complex interpolation spaces seems unavailable. Furthermore, we expect that our results can be extended to spaces with negative smoothness via duality. Some results for the weak (Dirichlet) Laplacian on weighted spaces are contained in
[
7
,
77
]
.

An interesting question regarding the smoothness of the domain is whether for
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
the assumption of
C^{1}
-domains can be weakened to Lipschitz domains. In general, the analysis for Lipschitz domains becomes much more involved and different techniques are required than for
C^{1}
-domains, see for instance
[
36
,
37
,
38
,
86
]
and the references therein. We believe that our method should work for domains with a small Lipschitz character. The
H^{\infty}
-calculus on Lipschitz domains could be important for studying SPDEs in the weighted setting, see
[
46
,
48
,
49
,
50
]
, where the range of weights is significantly smaller than
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
.

Outline

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
we introduce some preliminary concepts and results needed throughout the paper. In Section
3
we study weighted Sobolev spaces on domains and prove characterisations for these spaces. In Section
4
, results on the fractional domains of the Laplacian on the half-space are proved, which are required for perturbation of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus. In Section
5
we perturb the
H^{\infty}
-calculus from the half-space to special domains, and in Section
6
we perform a localisation procedure to obtain the
H^{\infty}
-calculus on bounded domains. Moreover, as a consequence, we obtain maximal regularity for the heat equation and boundedness of Riesz transforms. Finally, in Appendix
A
we prove a lemma about the Dahlberg–Kenig–Stein pullback.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

We denote by
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\mathbb{N}_{1}
the set of natural numbers starting at
0
and
1
, respectively.
For

, we use the notation
(a)_{+}=a
if

(a)_{+}=0

For

\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, where
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,\infty)
and for

we write
x=(x_{1},\widetilde{x})
with

and
\widetilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. For
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
open and

we define the power weight
w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}}(x):=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\mathcal{O})^{\gamma}
.

For two topological vector spaces
X
and
Y
, the space of continuous linear operators is
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
and
\mathcal{L}(X):=\mathcal{L}(X,X)
. Unless specified otherwise,
X
will always denote a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
and the dual space is
X^{\prime}:=\mathcal{L}(X,\mathbb{C})
.

For a linear operator
A:X\supseteq D(A)\to X
on a Banach space
X
we denote by
\sigma(A)
and
\rho(A)
the spectrum and resolvent set, respectively. For
\lambda\in\rho(A)
, the resolvent operator is given by
R(\lambda,A)=(\lambda-A)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
.

We write
f\lesssim g
(resp.
f\gtrsim g
) if there exists a constant
C>0
, possibly depending on parameters which will be clear from the context or will be specified in the text, such that
f\leq Cg
(resp.
f\geq Cg
). Furthermore,
f\eqsim g
means
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
.

For an open and non-empty
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
, the space
C^{\ell}(\mathcal{O};X)
denotes the space of
\ell
-times continuously differentiable functions from
\mathcal{O}
to some Banach space
X
. In the case
\ell=0
we write
C(\mathcal{O};X)
for
C^{0}(\mathcal{O};X)
. Furthermore, we write
C^{\ell}_{\rm b}(\mathcal{O};X)
for the space of all functions
f\in C^{\ell}(\mathcal{O};X)
such that
\partial^{\alpha}f
is bounded on
\mathcal{O}
for all multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\alpha|\leq\ell
.

Let
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O};X)
be the space of compactly supported smooth functions on
\mathcal{O}
equipped with its usual inductive limit topology. The space of
X
-valued distributions is given by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{O};X):=\mathcal{L}(C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O});X)
. Moreover,
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{O}};X)
is the space of smooth functions with their support in a compact set contained in
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
.

We denote the Schwartz space by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X):=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d});X)

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{O};X):=\{u|_{\mathcal{O}}:u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)\}

Finally, for

and a compatible couple
(X,Y)
of Banach spaces, the complex interpolation space is denoted by

2.2.
Holomorphic functional calculus

In this section, we collect the required preliminaries on sectorial operators with a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus.

2.2.1.
Definitions

For
\omega\in(0,\pi)
, let
\Sigma_{\omega}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:|\operatorname{arg}(z)|<\omega\}
be a sector in the complex plane.

Definition 2.1
.

An injective, closed linear operator
(A,D(A))
with dense domain and dense range on a Banach space
X
is called
sectorial
if there exists a
\omega\in(0,\pi)
such that
\sigma(A)\subseteq\overline{\Sigma_{\omega}}
and

\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\omega}}}\|\lambda R(\lambda,A)\|<\infty.

Furthermore, the angle of sectoriality
\omega(A)
is defined as the infimum over all possible
\omega>0
.

To continue, we introduce the following Hardy spaces. Let
\omega\in(0,\pi)
, then
H^{1}(\Sigma_{\omega})
is the space of all holomorphic functions
f:\Sigma_{\omega}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\omega})}:=\sup_{|\nu|<\omega}\|t\mapsto f(e^{{\rm i}\nu}t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t})}<\infty.

Moreover, let
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\omega})
be the space of all bounded holomorphic functions on the sector with norm

\|f\|_{H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\omega})}:=\sup_{z\in\Sigma_{\omega}}|f(z)|.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a sectorial operator on a Banach space
X
and let
\omega\in(\omega(A),\pi)
,
\nu\in(\omega(A),\omega)
and
f\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{\omega})
. We define the operator

f(A):=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}f(z)R(z,A)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}z,

where
\partial\Sigma_{\nu}
is oriented counterclockwise. The operator
A
has a
bounded
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\omega})
-calculus
if there exists a
C>0
such that

\|f(A)\|\leq C\|f\|_{H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\omega})}\quad\text{ for all }f\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{\omega})\cap H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\omega}).

Furthermore, the angle of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A)
is defined as the infimum over all possible
\omega>\omega(A)
.

For more details on the
H^{\infty}
-calculus, the reader is referred to
[
32
]
and
[
34
, Chapter 10]
.

2.2.2.
Fractional domains

Let
A
be a sectorial operator and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. To define fractional powers
A^{\alpha}
, we need a functional calculus allowing for holomorphic functions of polynomial growth. This is known as the
extended functional calculus
and the reader is referred to
[
35
, Chapter 15]
or
[
64
, Appendix 15.C]
for a detailed study of extended functional calculi and fractional powers. In particular,
A^{\alpha}
is again sectorial.

A sectorial operator
A
on a Banach space
X
has
bounded imaginary powers
(
\BIP
) if
A^{{\rm i}s}
extends to a bounded operator on
X
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
. The angle is given by
\omega_{\BIP}(A)=\inf\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}:\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}e^{-\omega|s|}\|A^{{\rm i}s}\|<\infty\}
.
Moreover, a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus implies
\BIP
and
\omega_{\BIP}(A)\leq\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A)
, see
[
35
, Section 15.3]
.

We recall a result on the interpolation of fractional domains. For details on interpolation theory, the reader is referred to
[
6
]
and
[
82
]
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
35
, Corollary 15.3.10]
)
.

Let
A
be a sectorial operator on a Banach space
X
and assume that
A
has
\BIP
. Then for all
\theta\in(0,1)
and
0\leq\alpha<\beta
we have

D(A^{(1-\theta)\alpha+\theta\beta})=[D(A^{\alpha}),D(A^{\beta})]_{\theta}.

Moreover, by
[
35
, Proposition 15.2.12]
we have for a sectorial operator
A
that
D((\mu+A)^{\alpha})=D(A^{\alpha})
for all
\mu\geq 0
and
\alpha>0
.

In the case of a lower-order perturbation, we have the following result.

(

)

Theorem 2.6

(

)
.

Let

be a sectorial operator on a Banach space

A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\omega})
-calculus. Let
B
be a linear operator on
X
such that
D(B)\supseteq D(A)
. Suppose that there is an
\eta>0
such that

(1)

\lVert Bu\rVert_{X}\leq\eta\,\lVert Au\rVert_{X},\qquad u\in D(A).

Moreover, suppose that at least one of the following relative bounds is satisfied:

(2)

there exists an
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
B(D(A^{1+\alpha}))\subseteq D(A^{\alpha})
and

\lVert A^{\alpha}Bu\rVert_{X}\leq C\lVert A^{1+\alpha}u\rVert_{X},\qquad u\in D(A^{1+\alpha}),

(3)

there exists an
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\lVert A^{-\alpha}Bu\rVert_{X}\leq C\lVert A^{1-\alpha}u\rVert_{X},\qquad u\in D(A^{1-\alpha}),

for some
C>0
. Then there exists an
\widetilde{\eta}>0
such that, if
1
holds with
\eta<\widetilde{\eta}
, then
A+B
with
D(A+B)=D(A)
has a bounded
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\omega})
-calculus.

Remark 2.7
.

Theorem
2.6
is taken from
[
35
, Theorem 16.2.8]
, where it should be noted that their condition of
R
-sectoriality on
B
is redundant, see also
[
63
]
and the errata to
[
35
]
.
A version of Theorem
2.6
for positive fractional powers also appeared in
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
.

2.3.
The
\UMD
property

Throughout this paper, we work mostly with vector-valued Sobolev spaces (although our results are also new for the scalar-valued case), and for this, we need the
\UMD
property for Banach spaces. We recall that a Banach space
X
satisfies the condition
\UMD
(unconditional martingale differences) if and only if the Hilbert transform extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R};X)
. We list the following relevant properties of
\UMD
spaces, see for instance
[
33
, Chapter 4 & 5]
.

(1)

Hilbert spaces are
\UMD
Banach spaces. In particular,
\mathbb{C}
is a
\UMD
space.

(2)

If
p\in(1,\infty)
,
(S,\Sigma,\mu)
is a
\sigma
-finite measure space and
X
is a
\UMD
Banach space, then
L^{p}(S;X)
is a
\UMD
Banach space.

(3)

\UMD
Banach spaces are reflexive.

The UMD property is known to be necessary for many results on vector-valued Sobolev spaces (see
[
5
]
,
[
33
, Section 5.6]
, and
[
35
, Corollary 13.3.9]
). Moreover, the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of
-\Delta
on spaces such as
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
also is equivalent to the UMD property (see
[
34
, Section 10.5]
).

2.4.
Domains

Let
\lambda\in(0,1]
and let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
be open. A function
h:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
uniformly
\lambda
-Hölder continuous on
\mathcal{O}
if

[h]_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathcal{O}\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|h(x)-h(y)|}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}<\infty.

In addition, for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define the space of
\lambda
-Hölder continuous functions by

C_{{\rm b}}^{\ell,\lambda}(\mathcal{O}):=\{f\in C_{{\rm b}}^{\ell}(\mathcal{O}):[\partial^{\alpha}h]_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}<\infty\text{ for all }|\alpha|\leq\ell\}.

For
\lambda=0
we write
C_{\rm b}^{\ell,0}(\mathcal{O}):=C^{\ell}_{\rm b}(\mathcal{O})
. By
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}(\mathcal{O})
we denote the subset of functions in
C^{\ell,\lambda}(\mathcal{O})
with compact support in
\mathcal{O}
. Moreover, on
C_{\rm b}^{\ell,\lambda}(\mathcal{O})
we define the norm

\|h\|_{C^{\ell,\lambda}(\mathcal{O})}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\ell}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{O}}|\partial^{\alpha}h(x)|+\sum_{|\alpha|=\ell}[\partial^{\alpha}h]_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a domain, i.e., a connected open set. Let
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

(1)

We call
\mathcal{O}
a
special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain
if, after translation and rotation, it is of the form

(2.2)

\mathcal{O}=\{(x_{1},\widetilde{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{1}>h(\widetilde{x})\}

for some
h\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}({\mathbb{R}}^{d-1};\mathbb{R})
.

(2)

Given a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain
\mathcal{O}
, we define

[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}:=\lVert h\rVert_{C^{\ell,\lambda}({\mathbb{R}}^{d-1})},

where
h\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}({\mathbb{R}}^{d-1};\mathbb{R})
is such that, after rotation and translation, (
2.2
) holds. Note that
[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}
is uniquely defined due to the compact support of
h
.

(3)

We call
\mathcal{O}
a
C^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain
if every boundary point
x\in\partial\mathcal{O}
admits an open neighbourhood
V
such that

\mathcal{O}\cap V=W\cap V\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial\mathcal{O}\cap V=\partial W\cap V

for some special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain
W
.

If
\lambda=0
, then we write
C^{\ell}
for
C^{\ell,0}
in the definitions above.

For any
\delta>0
and
C^{\ell}
-domain
\mathcal{O}
, the special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell}
-domains
W
can always be chosen such that
[W]_{C^{\ell}}<\delta
. If
\lambda\in(0,1]
,
\varepsilon\in(0,\lambda)
and
\mathcal{O}
is a
C^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain, then for any
\delta>0
, the special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}
-domains
W
can be chosen such that
[W]_{C^{\ell,\lambda-\varepsilon}}<\delta
. Indeed, if
h\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1};\mathbb{R})
is associated with
W
, then for any
|\alpha|=\ell
, we have

[\partial^{\alpha}h]_{\lambda-\varepsilon,\mathcal{O}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathcal{O}\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|\partial^{\alpha}h(x)-\partial^{\alpha}h(y)|}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}|x-y|^{\varepsilon}<\delta,

whenever
|x-y|^{\varepsilon}
is small enough. Note that for
\varepsilon=0
, the quantity
[\partial^{\alpha}h]_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}
cannot be made arbitrarily small.

We provide the construction of a diffeomorphism between special domains and the half-space. In the literature, this diffeomorphism is sometimes referred to as the
Dahlberg–Kenig–Stein pullback
, which dates back to
[
10
,
11
]
and is, for instance, applied in
[
12
,
26
,
42
]
. It preserves the distance to the boundary and straightens the boundary smoothly in the interior of a special domain with suitable blow-up behaviour of higher-order derivatives near the boundary. We will motivate the use of this diffeomorphism in more detail in Remark
3.10
.

The Dahlberg–Kenig–Stein pullback is often used for domains with low regularity (less than
C^{1}
), see the above-mentioned literature. To our knowledge, estimates on higher-order derivatives of the pullback in the case of more regular domains (more than
C^{1}
) have not appeared anywhere in the literature before. The following lemma is an extension of the result for
C^{1}
-domains in
[
51
, Lemmas 2.6 & 3.8]
, which is based on the work
[
66
]
about regularised distances. We provide the proof of the lemma in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{0,1}
-domain.
Then there exist continuous functions
h_{1}\colon\overline{\mathcal{O}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
h_{2}\colon\overline{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with the following properties.

(1)

The map
\Psi:\mathcal{O}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}

given by

\Psi(x)=(x_{1}-h_{1}(x),\widetilde{x}),\qquad x=(x_{1},\widetilde{x})\in\mathcal{O},

is a
C^{0,1}
-diffeomorphism with inverse
\Psi^{-1}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}\to\mathcal{O}
given by

\Psi^{-1}(y)=(y_{1}+h_{2}(y),\widetilde{y}),\qquad y=(y_{1},\widetilde{y})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}.

(2)

We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\Psi(x),\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+})

\displaystyle\eqsim\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\mathcal{O}),\qquad

\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{O},

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\Psi^{-1}(y),\partial\mathcal{O})

\displaystyle\eqsim\mathrm{dist}(y,\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}),\qquad

\displaystyle y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},

where the implicit constants depend on
\max\{1,[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{0,1}}\}
.

(3)

We have
h_{1}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
and
h_{2}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+})
.

In addition, let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
and let
\mathcal{O}
be a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain with
[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}\leq 1
.

(4)

The map
\Psi
in
1
is a
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}
-diffeomorphism and for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
,
\ell_{0}\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
and
\lambda_{0}\in[0,\lambda]
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}h_{1}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\mathcal{O})^{-(|\alpha|-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}},\qquad

\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{O},

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}h_{2}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(y,\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+})^{-(|\alpha|-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}},\qquad

\displaystyle y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},

where the constant
C>0
only depends on
\ell,\lambda,\alpha
and
d
.

Remark 2.10
.

We make the following remarks about Lemma
2.9
.

(1)

Statements
1
,
2
and
3
are standard results for localisation. Nonetheless, for the standard localisation procedure one can take
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
equal to
h
(see, e.g.,
[
25
, Appendix C.1]
). In our case, since
h
is not smooth enough, we need to use a mollifier to make
h_{2}
smooth. Afterwards,
h_{1}
is determined using the implicit function theorem, see Appendix
A
for details.

(2)

Our main contribution to the statement of Lemma
2.9
is
4
. This part allows us to estimate higher-order derivatives of the diffeomorphism
\Psi
and its inverse. If the number of derivatives exceeds the smoothness of the domain, then there is a blow-up near the boundary. We note that the construction of
\Psi
is independent of
\ell
and
\lambda
.

(3)

The condition
[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}\leq 1
slightly simplifies the proof and the statement of the lemma. However, this condition is not necessary and can be removed at the cost of obtaining powers of
[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}
in the estimates in
4
. For our application in Section
6
, imposing this condition is not a restriction since
[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}
can be made arbitrarily small in our localisation procedure.

3.
Weighted Sobolev spaces and trace characterisations

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a domain with non-empty boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
. A locally integrable function
w:\mathcal{O}\to(0,\infty)
is called a
weight
. For
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
we define the spatial power weight
w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma}
on
\mathcal{O}
by

w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma}(x):=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\mathcal{O})^{\gamma},\qquad x\in\mathcal{O},

and denote
w_{\gamma}:=w_{\gamma}^{\smash{\partial\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}}
.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
X
a Banach space we define the weighted Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)
as the Bochner space consisting of all strongly measurable
f\colon\mathcal{O}\to X
such that

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)}:=\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\|f(x)\|^{p}_{X}\>w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma}(x)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x\Bigr)^{1/p}<\infty.

Let
w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}}
be such that
(w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathcal{O})
. The
k
-th order weighted Sobolev space for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
is defined as

W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{O};X):\forall|\alpha|\leq k,\partial^{\alpha}f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)\right\}

equipped with the canonical norm. If
\gamma=0
, then we simply write
W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O};X)
.

Remark 3.1
.

The local
L^{1}
condition for
(w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}})^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
ensures that all the derivatives
\partial^{\alpha}f
are locally integrable in
\mathcal{O}
. If
\mathcal{O}
is the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
or a bounded domain, then this condition holds for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. For
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
the local
L^{1}
condition holds only for weights
w_{\gamma}(x)=|x_{1}|^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\in(-\infty,p-1)
. For
\gamma\geq p-1
, one has to be careful with defining the weighted Sobolev spaces on the full space because functions might not be locally integrable near
x_{1}=0
, see
[
61
]
. This explains why, for example, we cannot employ classical reflection arguments from
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
\gamma>p-1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\gamma>-1
and let
X
be a Banach space. To impose zero boundary conditions, we define

(3.1)

\accentset{\circ}{W}_{0}^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X):=\overline{C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O};X)}^{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)}.

Furthermore, to impose Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions, we set

\displaystyle C^{\infty}_{{\rm c,Dir}}(\overline{\mathcal{O}};X)

\displaystyle:=C^{\infty}(\mathcal{O};X)\cap\bigl\{f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathcal{O}};X):f|_{\partial\mathcal{O}}=0\bigr\},

\displaystyle C^{\infty}_{{\rm c,Neu}}(\overline{\mathcal{O}};X)

\displaystyle:=C^{\infty}(\mathcal{O};X)\cap\bigl\{f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{O}};X):(\partial_{1}f)|_{\partial\mathcal{O}}=0\bigr\},

which contain functions that are smooth in the interior of
\mathcal{O}
, satisfy the boundary condition and have compact support at infinity (in the case of unbounded domains). We define

(3.2)

\displaystyle\accentset{\circ}{W}^{k,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle:=\overline{C^{\infty}_{{\rm c,Dir}}(\overline{\mathcal{O}};X)}^{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)},

\displaystyle\accentset{\circ}{W}^{k,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle:=\overline{C^{\infty}_{{\rm c,Neu}}(\overline{\mathcal{O}};X)}^{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)}.

The notation
\accentset{\circ}{W}_{0}^{k,p}
,
\accentset{\circ}{W}_{\rm Dir}^{k,p}
and
\accentset{\circ}{W}_{\rm Neu}^{k,p}
as in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) will mean that the spaces are defined as the closure of some space of test functions. Alternative characterisations of these spaces with boundary conditions in terms of traces (which will be denoted by
W_{0}^{k,p}
,
W_{\rm Dir}^{k,p}
and
W_{\rm Neu}^{k,p}
) are derived in Sections
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
. The characterisations involving traces are also used in
[
67
,
69
]
to define Sobolev spaces with boundary conditions.

We recall from
[
69
, Lemma 3.1]
that for
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\gamma\in(-\infty,p-1)
and
X
a Banach space, we have the Sobolev embedding

W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)\hookrightarrow C([0,\infty);X).

Hardy’s inequality plays a central role in the analysis of weighted Sobolev spaces. We state a version on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
from
[
69
, Lemma 3.2]
. A version for
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
will be given in Corollary
3.4
. For Hardy’s inequality on more general domains, the reader is referred to
[
60
, Section 8.8]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Hardy’s inequality on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and let
X
be a Banach space. Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
and assume either

(1)

\gamma<p-1
and
u(0)=0
, or,

(2)

\gamma>p-1
.

Then

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\gamma-p};X)}\leq C_{p,\gamma}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}.

3.1.
Trace characterisations for weighted Sobolev spaces on the half-space

In the following three sections, we present characterisations of the spaces in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) as closed subspaces of
W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)
with vanishing traces. In this section, we start with the special case
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{jp-1:j\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\}
and
X
a Banach space, we define the following spaces with vanishing traces

\displaystyle W^{k,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},w_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},w_{\gamma};X):\operatorname{Tr}(\partial^{\alpha}f)=0\text{ if }k-|\alpha|>\tfrac{\gamma+1}{p}\right\},

\displaystyle W^{k,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},w_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},w_{\gamma};X):\operatorname{Tr}(f)=0\text{ if }k>\tfrac{\gamma+1}{p}\right\},

\displaystyle W^{k,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},w_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},w_{\gamma};X):\operatorname{Tr}(\partial_{1}f)=0\text{ if }k-1>\tfrac{\gamma+1}{p}\right\}.

All the traces in the above definitions are well defined, see
[
67
, Section 3.1]
. Although we will not consider weights
w_{\gamma}
with
\gamma\leq-1
, we can nonetheless define

W^{k,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X):=W^{k,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X):=W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X),

see
[
69
, Lemma 3.1(2)]
.

In
[
69
]
the above spaces are also used to define weighted Sobolev spaces on domains. However, since we consider domains with low regularity, we cannot do this, as will be explained in Remark
3.10
. Therefore, we first defined the Sobolev spaces as the closure of test functions in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). The following proposition relates the spaces
W^{k,p}_{{\rm BC}}
and
\accentset{\circ}{W}^{k,p}_{{\rm BC}}
, where
{\rm BC}\in\{0,{\rm Dir},{\rm Neu}\}
stands for boundary conditions. That is, we prove that certain classes of test functions are dense in Sobolev spaces with zero trace conditions.

Proposition 3.3

(Trace characterisation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{jp-1:j\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\}
and let
X
be a Banach space.
For
{\rm BC}\in\{0,{\rm Dir},{\rm Neu}\}
we have the trace characterisations

\accentset{\circ}{W}_{{\rm BC}}^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)=W_{{\rm BC}}^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X).

Proof.

From
[
69
, Proposition 3.8]
we have that
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)
is dense in
W^{k,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
and therefore the trace characterisation for
\accentset{\circ}{W}^{k,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
follows.

Let
({\rm BC},j)\in\{({\rm Dir},0),({\rm Neu},1)\}
. Then
[
79
, Proposition 4.8]
implies that

\overline{\bigl\{f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}};X):(\partial_{1}^{j}f)|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}=0\bigr\}}^{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}=W^{k,p}_{{\rm BC}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},w_{\gamma};X).

Since

\bigl\{f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}};X):(\partial_{1}^{j}f)|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}=0\bigr\}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{{\rm c,BC}}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}};X),

the trace characterisations for the Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions follow.
∎

Before we continue with trace characterisations on domains, we record the following Hardy inequalities. As a corollary of Hardy’s inequality on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(Lemma
3.2
), we have the following Hardy’s inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
, see also
[
69
, Corollary 3.4]
.

Corollary 3.4

(Hardy’s inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and let
X
be a Banach space. Then

\displaystyle W_{0}^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-p};X)

\displaystyle\text{ if }\gamma<p-1,

\displaystyle W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow W^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-p};X)

\displaystyle\text{ if }\gamma>p-1,

\displaystyle W_{0}^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow W_{0}^{k-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-p};X)

\displaystyle\text{ if }\gamma\notin\{jp-1:j\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\}.

Moreover, as a consequence of Hardy’s inequality above, we obtain the following non-sharp Hardy’s inequality.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{jp-1:j\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\}
,
s\in[0,\infty)
such that
\gamma>sp-1
and let
X
be a Banach space. Then for any integer
k\geq s
it holds that

W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-sp};X).

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};[0,1])
such that
\varphi_{1}(x_{1})=0
for
x_{1}\geq 2
and
\varphi_{2}(x_{1})=0
for
x_{1}\leq 1
. In addition, take
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
such that
\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}=1
. Let
f\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
, with Hardy’s inequality (Corollary
3.4
using that
\gamma>sp-1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-sp};X)}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\varphi_{1}\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+(k-s)p};X)}+\|f\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-sp};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\varphi_{1}\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}+\|f\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)},

where we have used that
w_{\gamma+(k-s)p}(x)\lesssim w_{\gamma}(x)
for
x_{1}\leq 2
(since
k\geq s
) and
w_{\gamma-sp}(x)\lesssim w_{\gamma}(x)
for
x_{1}\geq 1
.
∎

3.2.
Trace characterisations for weighted Sobolev spaces on special domains

For
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
we have shown in Proposition
3.3
that the definition of weighted Sobolev spaces in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) is equivalent to setting certain traces to zero. To define Sobolev spaces with vanishing traces for a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain
\mathcal{O}
, we will employ the diffeomorphism
\Psi:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
from Lemma
2.9
to construct an isomorphism between Sobolev spaces on
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and let
X
be a Banach space. Let
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{jp-1:j\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\}
be such that
\gamma>(k-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}p-1
.
Moreover, let
\mathcal{O}
be a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain with
[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}\leq 1
.
Let
\Psi\colon\mathcal{O}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}
be as in Lemma
2.9
and consider the change of coordinates mappings

(3.3a)

\displaystyle\Psi_{*}

\displaystyle\colon W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)\to W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X),

(3.3b)

\displaystyle\Psi_{*}

\displaystyle\colon\accentset{\circ}{W}_{{\rm BC}}^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)\to\accentset{\circ}{W}_{{\rm BC}}^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)\quad\text{ for }\,{\rm BC}\in\{0,{\rm Dir},{\rm Neu}\},

defined by
\Psi_{*}f:=f\circ\Psi^{-1}
. Then
\Psi_{*}
is an isomorphism of Banach spaces for which
(\Psi^{-1})_{*}
acts as inverse.

Proof.

Step 1: proof of (
3.3a
).
We start with some preparations. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and
f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}(\overline{\mathcal{O}};X)
. Note that by Lemma
2.9

we have that
\Psi_{*}f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}};X)
.
Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
with
|\alpha|\leq k
, then by
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
we have the multivariate Faà di Bruno’s formula

\partial^{\alpha}\Psi_{*}f=\sum_{1\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}(\Psi_{*}\partial^{\beta}f)\sum_{s=1}^{|\alpha|}\sum_{p_{s}(\alpha,\beta)}\prod_{j=1}^{s}c_{\alpha,\bm{k}_{j},\bm{\ell}_{j}}[\partial^{\bm{\ell}_{j}}\Psi^{-1}]^{\bm{k}_{j}},

for some constants
c_{\alpha,\bm{k}_{j},\bm{\ell}_{j}}
and sets
p_{s}(\alpha,\beta)
contained in

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Big\{(\bm{k}_{1},\ldots,\bm{k}_{s};\bm{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\ell}_{s})\in({\mathbb{N}}^{d}_{0}\setminus\{0\})^{s}\times({\mathbb{N}}^{d}_{0}\setminus\{0\})^{s}:\sum_{j=1}^{s}|\bm{k}_{j}|=|\beta|,\sum_{j=1}^{s}|\bm{k}_{j}||\bm{\ell}_{j}|=|\alpha|\Big\}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\Psi_{*}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\sum_{1\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}\sum_{s=1}^{|\alpha|}\sum_{p_{s}(\alpha,\beta)}\|(\Psi_{*}\partial^{\beta}f)\prod_{j=1}^{s}[\partial^{\bm{\ell}_{j}}\Psi^{-1}]^{\bm{k}_{j}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\sum_{1\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}\sum_{s=1}^{|\alpha|}\sum_{p_{s}(\alpha,\beta)}\|\Psi_{*}\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}|\bm{k}_{j}|p};X)}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{s}\|y\mapsto y_{1}^{(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}}\partial^{\bm{\ell}_{j}}\Psi^{-1}(y)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{|\bm{k}_{j}|}.

From Lemma
2.9
4
we obtain

(3.6)

\prod_{j=1}^{s}\|y\mapsto y_{1}^{(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}}\partial^{\bm{\ell}_{j}}\Psi^{-1}(y)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{|\bm{k}_{j}|}\lesssim 1.

Step 1a: proof of (
3.3a
) if
\ell+\lambda\geq k
.
If
k=0
, then (
3.3a
) follows immediately from Lemma
2.9
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and note that
|\bm{\ell}_{j}|\leq|\alpha|\leq k\leq\ell+\lambda
. Therefore,
(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}=0
in (
3.5
) and the case
k=0
implies

(3.7)

\|\Psi_{*}\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\lesssim\|\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)}\leq\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}};X)},\qquad 1\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|,

and we find

\|\Psi_{*}f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}};X)},\qquad f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}(\overline{\mathcal{O}};X),

and by density the estimate extends to
f\in W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}};X)
. Recall from Lemma
2.9
that
\Psi
is invertible and thus
(\Psi^{-1})_{*}
is the inverse of
\Psi_{*}
.
The estimate for the inverse
(\Psi^{-1})_{*}
can be shown using similar estimates as in (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.7
). This shows that
\Psi_{*}
in (
3.3a
) is an isomorphism if
\ell+\lambda\geq k
.

Step 1b: proof of (
3.3a
) if
\ell+\lambda<k
.

We claim that in (
3.5
) we have

(3.8)

\gamma-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}|\bm{k}_{j}|p>-1.

Indeed, if
|\bm{\ell}_{j}|\leq\ell+\lambda
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, then

\gamma-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}|\bm{k}_{j}|p=\gamma>(k-(\ell+\lambda))p-1>-1,

and if
|\bm{\ell}_{j_{0}}|>\ell+\lambda
for some
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, then with (
3.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}|\bm{k}_{j}|p

\displaystyle=\gamma-\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq j_{0}\end{subarray}}^{s}(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}|\bm{k}_{j}|+(|\bm{\ell}_{j_{0}}|-(\ell+\lambda))|\bm{k}_{j_{0}}|\Big)p

\displaystyle\geq\gamma-\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq j_{0}\end{subarray}}^{s}|\bm{\ell}_{j}||\bm{k}_{j}|+|\bm{\ell}_{j_{0}}||\bm{k}_{j_{0}}|-(\ell+\lambda)\Big)p

\displaystyle=\gamma-(|\alpha|-(\ell+\lambda))p\geq\gamma-(k-(\ell+\lambda))p>-1.

Moreover, again by (
3.4
) we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}|\bm{k}_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{s}|\bm{\ell}_{j}||\bm{k}_{j}|-|\beta|=|\alpha|-|\beta|\leq k-|\beta|.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.5
(using (
3.8
) and (
3.9
)) and Step 1a, we have for
1\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|\leq k=\ell+1
that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{*}\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}|\bm{k}_{j}|p};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Psi_{*}\partial^{\beta}f\|_{W^{k-|\beta|,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial^{\beta}f\|_{W^{k-|\beta|,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)},\qquad f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}(\overline{\mathcal{O}};X).

Now, density and (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.10
) yield that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Psi_{*}

\displaystyle:W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}};X)\to W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)

is bounded for
k=\ell+1
.

The general case
k\geq\ell+1
follows by induction on
k
. Assume that (
3.11
) holds for some
k\geq\ell+1
and let
1\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|\leq k+1
. Using the induction hypothesis instead of Step 1a in (
3.10
), we obtain the estimate

\|\Psi_{*}\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}|\bm{k}_{j}|p};X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)},

which proves (
3.11
) for

.

The estimate for the inverse can be shown directly using similar estimates as in (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), together with the estimate

\displaystyle\|(\Psi^{-1})_{*}\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}|\bm{k}_{j}|p};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|\bm{\ell}_{j}|-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}|\bm{k}_{j}|p};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial^{\beta}f\|_{W^{k-|\beta|,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)},\qquad f\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X),

which follows from Step 1a and Lemma
3.5
.
This completes the proof of (
3.3a
).

Step 2: proof of (
3.3b

The proof (
3.3b
) is similar to the proof of (
3.3a
) if we work with a suitable dense subspace, i.e.,

•

if

•

if
{\rm BC}\in\{{\rm Dir},{\rm Neu}\}

see (

) and (

+

ensures that
\Psi_{*}f
is in the same dense subspace on

.
∎

Remark 3.7

-domain.

Definition 3.8
.

Let

,

Proposition 3.9

(Trace characterisation on special domains)
.

,

be a Banach space. Let
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{jp-1:j\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\}

\gamma>(k-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}p-1
.
Moreover, let
\mathcal{O}
be a special

and let
\Psi_{*}
be the isomorphism from Proposition
3.6
.
For
{\rm BC}\in\{0,{\rm Dir},{\rm Neu}\}

Remark 3.10
.

If

defines a

-diffeomorphism. Moreover, the change of coordinates mapping

becomes an isomorphism between

and

for

. In

3.3.
Trace characterisations for weighted Sobolev spaces on bounded domains

In this section, we define Sobolev spaces with vanishing traces for bounded domains
\mathcal{O}
. To this end, we will employ a localisation procedure to relate spaces on bounded domains with spaces on special domains. We start with a lemma containing a decomposition of weighted Sobolev spaces, see also
[
69
, Section 2.2]
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
and let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded
C^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain. Then for any
\delta>0
, the following statements hold.

(1)

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,\lambda)
there exists a finite open cover
(V_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
of
\partial\mathcal{O}
, together with special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}
-domains
(\mathcal{O}_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
which satisfy
[\mathcal{O}_{n}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda-\varepsilon}}<\delta
, such that

\mathcal{O}\cap V_{n}=\mathcal{O}_{n}\cap V_{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial\mathcal{O}\cap V_{n}=\partial\mathcal{O}_{n}\cap V_{n},\quad n\in\{1,\dots,N\}.

If
\lambda=0
, then the special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell}
-domains
(\mathcal{O}_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
can be chosen such that
[\mathcal{O}_{n}]_{C^{\ell}}<\delta
.

(2)

There exist
\eta_{0}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
and
\eta_{n}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(V_{n})
for
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
0\leq\eta_{n}\leq 1
for
n\in\{0,\dots,N\}
and
\sum_{n=0}^{N}\eta_{n}^{2}=1
on
\mathcal{O}
(partition of unity).

(3)

For
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
X
a Banach space, the space
W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}};X)
has the direct sum decomposition

(3.12)

\mathbb{W}^{k,p}_{\gamma}:=W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{N}W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O}_{n},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}_{n}};X).

Moreover, the mappings

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\colon W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}};X)\to\mathbb{W}^{k,p}_{\gamma}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{W}^{k,p}_{\gamma}\to W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}};X)

given by

(3.13)

\mathcal{I}f:=(\eta_{n}f)_{n=0}^{N}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{P}(f_{n})_{n=0}^{N}:=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\eta_{n}f_{n},

satisfy
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{I}=\operatorname{id}
. Thus,
\mathcal{P}
is a retraction with coretraction
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

We note that the result in
1
follows from the discussion after Definition
2.8
in Section
2.4
. The partition of unity in
2
is standard, see for instance
[
58
, Section 8.4]
(noting that a
C^{2}
-domain is not required for constructing the partition of unity). Finally, using the partition of unity and the (co)retraction in (
3.13
), the direct sum decomposition in
3
follows. Indeed,
\eta_{0}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
and we can extend to the full space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
without a weight since there is no boundary. Furthermore, for
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
we have
\eta_{n}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(V_{n})
, so the weight
w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}}(x)
can be replaced by
w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}_{n}}(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
.
∎

With Lemma
3.11
we can now define traces of functions in
W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}};X)
if
\mathcal{O}
is a bounded
C^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain. Furthermore, we define the following spaces with vanishing traces at the boundary.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and let
X
be a Banach space. Let
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{jp-1:j\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\}

\gamma>(k-(\ell+\lambda))_{+}p-1
.
Moreover, let
\mathcal{O}
be a bounded
C^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain, let
(\mathcal{O}_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
be special

-domains and let

be the coretraction from Lemma

\displaystyle W_{0}^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{f\in W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X):\mathcal{I}f\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{N}W_{0}^{k,p}(\mathcal{O}_{n},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}_{n}};X)\Bigr\},

\displaystyle W^{k,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{f\in W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X):\mathcal{I}f\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{N}W_{{\rm Dir}}^{k,p}(\mathcal{O}_{n},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}_{n}};X)\Bigr\},

Similar to Propositions
3.3

Proposition 3.13

(Trace characterisation on bounded domains)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and let
X
be a Banach space. Let
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{jp-1:j\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\}

.
Moreover, let
\mathcal{O}
be a bounded
C^{\ell,\lambda}

\accentset{\circ}{W}_{{\rm BC}}^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)=W_{{\rm BC}}^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X).

Proof.

We only prove the statement for
{\rm BC}=0
since the proof for the other cases is similar. Let

. Proposition
3.9
and the fact that

is dense in

, allows us to approximate

by a sequence

where
(g_{0,m})_{m\geq 1}\subseteq C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)
and
(g_{n,m})_{m\geq 1}\subseteq C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}_{n};X)
for all
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. Using Lemma
3.11
we see that
f=\mathcal{P}\mathcal{I}f
can be approximated by the sequence
\mathcal{P}g\subseteq C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O};X)
.
∎

4.
Fractional domains of the Laplacian on the half-space

In this section, we establish properties of the Laplacian on the half-space that are required for Sections
5
and
6
. There, we will transfer the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Laplacian from
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
to domains using the perturbation results in Section
2.2
. The aim of the present section is to recall the bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
from
[
67
]
and to characterise the relevant fractional domains and interpolation spaces. These characterisations are one of the key ingredients in the perturbation theorems in Section
5
.

Throughout this section, the Dirichlet and Neumann Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
will be defined as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space.

(1)

Let
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
. The
Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
on
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
is defined by

\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}u:=\Delta u\quad\text{ with }\quad D(\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}):=W^{k+2,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X).

(2)

Let
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
and
j\in\{0,1\}
. The
Neumann Laplacian
\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
on
W^{k+j,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},

w_{\gamma+kp};X)
is defined by

\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}u:=\Delta u\quad\text{ with }\quad D(\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}):=W^{k+j+2,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X).

Note that equivalently we can write
\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
on
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+(k-1)p};X)
where
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
, or,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
. This matches the notation in Theorem
1.2
.

We recall from
[
67
]
that these Laplace operators admit a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
67
, Theorem 1.1 & Remark 1.3(i)]
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Let
\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
on
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
be as in Definition
4.1
1
. Then for all
\mu>0
we have that

(1)

\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
is sectorial of angle
\omega(\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})=0
,

(2)

\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})=0
.

Moreover, the statements hold for
\mu=0
as well if
\gamma+kp\in(-1,2p-1)
.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
67
, Theorem 1.2 & Remark 1.3(i)]
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
,
j\in\{0,1\}
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Let
\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
on
W^{k+j,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
be as in Definition
4.1
2
.
Then for all
\mu>0
we have that

(1)

\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
is sectorial of angle
\omega(\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}})=0
,

(2)

\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}})=0
.

Moreover, the statements hold for
\mu=0
as well if
k=0
.

Remark 4.4
.

The domain
D(A)
of an operator
A
on a Banach space
Y
is endowed with the graph norm
\|u\|_{Y}+\|Au\|_{Y}
for
u\in D(A)
. It follows from Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
that the graph norm is equivalent to the norm of the domain in Definition
4.1
. Under the conditions of Theorem
4.2
, we have for the Dirichlet Laplacian that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{k+2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}

\displaystyle\eqsim_{p,k,\gamma,\mu,X}\|u\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}+\|(\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})u\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}

\displaystyle\eqsim_{p,k,\gamma,\mu,X}\|(\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})u\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)},\quad u\in W_{\rm Dir}^{k+2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),

where the latter identity only holds for
\mu>0
. A similar norm equivalence holds for the Neumann Laplacian.

To transfer the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Laplacian from
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
to domains, we need to identify certain fractional domains and interpolation spaces. This will be done in Section
4.1
and
4.2
for the Dirichlet and Neumann Laplacian, respectively. We additionally define for
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{jp-1:j\in\mathbb{N}_{1}\}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the following weighted Sobolev spaces with boundary conditions (cf.
[
69
, Section 6.3]
)

\displaystyle W^{k,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle:=\Big\{u\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X):\operatorname{Tr}(\Delta^{j}u)=0,\forall j<\tfrac{1}{2}\big(k-\tfrac{\gamma+1}{p}\big)\Big\},

\displaystyle W^{k,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)

\displaystyle:=\Big\{u\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X):\operatorname{Tr}(\Delta^{j}\partial_{1}u)=0,\forall j<\tfrac{1}{2}\big(k-1-\tfrac{\gamma+1}{p}\big)\Big\}.

4.1.
Fractional domains for the Dirichlet Laplacian

We begin with an elliptic regularity result for the shifted Dirichlet Laplacian on spaces with additional boundary conditions.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
,
\mu>0
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Then for all
f\in W^{k+1,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
there exists a unique
u\in W^{k+3,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
such that
\mu u-\Delta u=f
. Moreover, this solution satisfies

\|u\|_{W^{k+3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)},

where the constant
C>0
only depends on
p,k,\gamma,\mu,d
and
X
.

Proof.

Step 1: the case
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
.
Let
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
and note that

W^{k+1,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)=W^{k+1,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)=W^{k+1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),

which has
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)
as a dense subspace, see Proposition
3.3
. We claim that for
f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)
there exists a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)
to
\mu u-\Delta u=f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
that satisfies
u(0,\cdot)=(\Delta u)(0,\cdot)=0
. Indeed, by the proof of
[
67
, Lemma 5.3]
we obtain an odd function
\overline{u}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)
which solves
\mu\overline{u}-\Delta\overline{u}=f_{\operatorname{odd}}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We recall from
[
67
]
that
f_{\operatorname{odd}}(x)=\operatorname{sign}(x_{1})f(|x_{1}|,\widetilde{x})
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the odd extension of
f
with respect to
x_{1}=0
. Since
\overline{u}
is odd, it follows that
\Delta\overline{u}
is odd as well. Then
u:=\overline{u}|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)
is a solution to
\mu u-\Delta u=f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and satisfies
u(0,\cdot)=(\Delta u)(0,\cdot)=0
. The uniqueness follows from
[
69
, Corollary 4.3]
. This proves the claim.

Let
f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)
and let
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)
be the solution to
\mu u-\Delta u=f
as follows from the claim. In particular, we have that
\operatorname{Tr}(\partial_{1}^{2}u)=0
. We define
v_{0}:=u
and
v_{j}:=\partial_{j}u
for
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. These functions satisfy the equations

\begin{split}\mu v_{0}-\Delta v_{0}&=f\\
\mu v_{1}-\Delta v_{1}&=\partial_{1}f\\
\mu v_{j}-\Delta v_{j}&=\partial_{j}f\end{split}\qquad\begin{split}v_{0}(0,\cdot)&=u(0,\cdot)=0,\\
(\partial_{1}v_{1})(0,\cdot)&=(\partial_{1}^{2}u)(0,\cdot)=0,\\
v_{j}(0,\cdot)&=0,\quad j\in\{2,\dots,d\}.\end{split}

Therefore, by
[
67
, Propositions 5.4 & 5.6]
we have for
j\in\{0,\dots,d\}
the estimates

\|v_{j}\|_{W^{k+2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)},

where the constant
C
only depends on
p,k,\gamma,\mu,d
and
X
. This implies that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{k+3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}

\displaystyle\eqsim\sum_{j=0}^{d}\|v_{j}\|_{W^{k+2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)},

where the constant only depends on
p,k,\gamma,\mu,d
and
X
. A density argument, similar to the proof of
[
67
, Proposition 5.4]
, yields the desired result for the case
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
. Note that the uniqueness of
u\in W^{k+3,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\hookrightarrow W^{k+2,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
follows from
[
67
, Proposition 5.4]
.

Step 2: the case
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
.
Note that for
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
we have

W^{k+1,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)=W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-p+(k+1)p};X).

Since
\gamma-p\in(-1,p-1)
and

W^{k+3,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)=W^{(k+1)+2,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-p+(k+1)p};X),

the result follows from Theorem
4.2
(see also
[
67
, Proposition 5.4]
).
∎

We can now proceed with characterising fractional domains of the Dirichlet Laplacian.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
,
\mu>0
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Let
\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
on
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
as in Definition
4.1
. Then

\displaystyle D\big((\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=W^{k+1,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),

\displaystyle D\big((\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{\frac{3}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=W^{k+3,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X).

Proof.

We write
A_{\rm Dir}:=\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
.
For
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
it holds that
A_{\rm Dir}
has
\BIP
by Theorem
4.2
, so Propositions
2.3
and
3.14
imply

D(A_{\rm Dir}^{\frac{1}{2}})=[W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+2,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\frac{1}{2}}=W^{k+1,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X).

By
[
35
, Theorem 15.2.5]
and the characterisation of
D(A_{\rm Dir}^{\frac{1}{2}})
we find

(4.1)

\displaystyle D(A_{\rm Dir}^{\frac{3}{2}})

\displaystyle=\{u\in D(A_{\rm Dir}):A_{\rm Dir}u\in D(A_{\rm Dir}^{\frac{1}{2}})\}

\displaystyle=\{u\in W^{k+2,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X):A_{\rm Dir}u\in W^{k+1,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\}.

It is straightforward to check that the embedding
W^{k+3,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\hookrightarrow D(A_{\rm Dir}^{\frac{3}{2}})
holds. The converse embedding follows from (
4.1
) and Lemma
4.5
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
4.6
, we can characterise the fractional domains as complex interpolation spaces as well.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
k_{0},k_{1}\in\{0,1,2,3\}
,
\theta\in(0,1)
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space. For
\mu>0
and
\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
on
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
be as in Definition
4.1
.

(1)

If
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
, then

D\big((\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{\frac{(1-\theta)k_{0}+\theta k_{1}}{2}}\big)=\big[W^{k+k_{0},p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+k_{1},p}_{\Delta,{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\big]_{\theta}.

(2)

If
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
, then

D\big((\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{\frac{(1-\theta)k_{0}+\theta k_{1}}{2}}\big)=\big[W^{k+k_{0},p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+k_{1},p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\big]_{\theta}.

Proof.

The fractional domains of the shifted Dirichlet Laplacian on
W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
form a complex interpolation scale by Proposition
2.3
and Theorem
4.2
, so the statements are a direct consequence of Proposition
4.6
.
∎

We close this section about the Dirichlet Laplacian with a complex interpolation identification, which follows from reiteration and the work of Šneĭberg
[
80
,
81
]
on the openness of the set of
\theta\in(0,1)
for which a bounded operator
T\colon[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}\to[Y_{0},Y_{1}]_{\theta}
is invertible.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
k_{0}\in\{0,1,2\}
,
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Then there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
\theta\in\big(0,\frac{2-k_{0}}{3-k_{0}}+\varepsilon\big)
we have

\displaystyle\big[W^{k+k_{0},p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}

\displaystyle,w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+3,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\big]_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\big[W^{k+k_{0},p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+3,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\big]_{\theta}.

Proof.

Let
\mu>0
and define
A_{\rm Dir}:=\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
on
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
as in Definition
4.1
.
First consider the case
k_{0}=0
and
\theta=\frac{2}{3}
, in which case we have by Corollary
4.7
and
[
79
, Proposition 6.2]

(4.2)

\displaystyle{}_{\frac{2}{3}}=D(A_{\rm Dir})

\displaystyle=W^{k+2,p}_{{\rm Dir}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)=W_{0}^{k+2,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)

\displaystyle=[W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W_{0}^{k+3,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\frac{2}{3}}.

Next, for
\theta\in(0,\frac{2}{3})
, we set
\widetilde{\theta}=\theta\cdot\frac{3}{2}\in(0,1)
. Then, by reiteration for the complex interpolation method (see
[
6
, Theorem 4.6.1]
) and (
4.2
) we have

\displaystyle[W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}

\displaystyle,w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+3,p}_{{\rm Dir}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\bigl[W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),[W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+3,p}_{{\rm Dir}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\frac{2}{3}}\bigr]_{\widetilde{\theta}}

\displaystyle=\bigl[W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),[W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+3,p}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\frac{2}{3}}\bigr]_{\widetilde{\theta}}

\displaystyle=[W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W_{0}^{k+3,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\theta}.

Note that the identity mapping is bounded on
W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
and

\id:W^{k+3,p}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\to W_{{\rm Dir}}^{k+3,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\quad\text{is bounded.}

Moreover, we have proved that it is invertible as a mapping

\displaystyle\id:[W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}

\displaystyle,w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+3,p}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\theta}

\displaystyle\to[W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+3,p}_{{\rm Dir}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\theta}

for
\theta\in(0,\frac{2}{3}]
. Since the collection of
\theta\in(0,1)
for which this mapping is invertible is open (see
[
23
, Theorem 1.3.24]
), the proposition in the case
k_{0}=0
follows.

Finally, for
k_{0}\in\{1,2\}
, let
\varepsilon>0
be such that the proposition holds for
k_{0}=0
and fix
\theta\in\big(0,\frac{2-k_{0}}{3-k_{0}}+\varepsilon\big)
. Then we have

\displaystyle(1-\theta)\tfrac{k_{0}}{3}+\theta=\tfrac{k_{0}}{3}+(\tfrac{3-k_{0}}{3})\theta<\tfrac{k_{0}}{3}+\tfrac{2-k_{0}}{3}+\varepsilon=\tfrac{2}{3}+\varepsilon.

Therefore, using
[
79
, Proposition 6.2]
, reiteration for the complex interpolation method and the case
k_{0}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle[W^{k+k_{0},p}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}

\displaystyle,w_{\gamma+kp};X),W_{0}^{k+3,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\bigl[[W^{k,p}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W_{0}^{k+3,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\frac{k_{0}}{3}},W_{0}^{k+3,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\bigr]_{\theta}

\displaystyle=[W^{k,p}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W_{0}^{k+3,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{(1-\theta)\frac{k_{0}}{3}+\theta}

\displaystyle=[W^{k,p}_{\rm Dir}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+3,p}_{\rm Dir}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{(1-\theta)\frac{k_{0}}{3}+\theta}.

Using Corollary
4.7
two more times, we have

\displaystyle[W^{k,p}_{{\rm Dir}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)

\displaystyle,W^{k+3,p}_{\rm Dir}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{(1-\theta)\frac{k_{0}}{3}+\theta}

\displaystyle=D(A_{\rm Dir}^{(1-\theta)\frac{k_{0}}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\theta})

\displaystyle=[W_{{\rm Dir}}^{k+k_{0},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+3,p}_{{\rm Dir}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\theta},

proving the proposition.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

We conjecture that, e.g., in the case
k=k_{0}=0
, there is actually the equality of complex interpolation spaces

(4.3)

[L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X),W^{3,p}_{{\rm Dir}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)]_{\theta}=[L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X),W_{0}^{3,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)]_{\theta}

for all
\theta\in\big(0,\frac{1}{3}(1+\frac{\gamma+1}{p})\big)
, which is suggested by results on interpolation with boundary conditions as studied in
[
68
,
79
]
.
However, at the moment, the case
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
of (
4.3
) for the parameter range
\theta\in\big(\tfrac{2}{3}+\varepsilon,\tfrac{1}{3}\bigl(1+\tfrac{\gamma+1}{p}\bigr)\big)
is an interesting open problem that seems to require a novel approach to interpolation with boundary conditions.

4.2.
Fractional domains for the Neumann Laplacian

Similar to the Dirichlet Laplacian above, we now characterise fractional domains for the Neumann Laplacian. The proofs are similar to those in Section
4.1
, but for the convenience of the reader, we provide the details.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{-1\}
,
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
such that
\gamma+kp>-1
,
\mu>0
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Then for all
f\in W^{k+2,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
there exists a unique
u\in W^{k+4,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
such that
\mu u-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}u=f
. Moreover, this solution satisfies

\|u\|_{W^{k+4,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{k+2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)},

where the constant
C>0
only depends on
p,k,\gamma,\mu,d
and
X
.

Proof.

Step 1: the case
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
and
k\geq-1
.
Note that for
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
we have

W^{k+2,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)=W^{k+2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-p+(k+1)p};X).

Since
\gamma-p\in(-1,p-1)
and

W^{k+4,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)=W^{(k+2)+2,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma-p+(k+1)p};X),

the result follows from Theorem
4.3
(see also
[
67
, Proposition 5.6]
).

Step 2: the case
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
and
k\geq 0
.
Note that for
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
we have

W^{k+2,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)=W^{k+2,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),

which has

C^{\infty}_{{\rm c},1}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}};X):=\{f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}};X):\partial_{1}f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)\}

as a dense subspace, see
[
79
, Proposition 4.9]
. For
f\in C^{\infty}_{{\rm c},1}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}};X)
there exists a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)
to
\mu u-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}u=f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
that satisfies
(\partial_{1}u)(0,\cdot)=(\Delta\partial_{1}u)(0,\cdot)=0
. This can be proved similarly as in Lemma
4.5
now using an even extension (cf.
[
67
, Lemma 5.5]
).

Take
f\in C^{\infty}_{{\rm c},1}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}};X)
and let
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)
be the solution to
\mu u-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}u=f
as above. We define
v_{0}:=u
and
v_{j}:=\partial_{j}u
for
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. These functions satisfy the estimates

\begin{split}\mu v_{0}-\Delta v_{0}&=f\\
\mu v_{1}-\Delta v_{1}&=\partial_{1}f\\
\mu v_{j}-\Delta v_{j}&=\partial_{j}f\end{split}\qquad\begin{split}(\partial_{1}v_{0})(0,\cdot)&=0,\\
v_{1}(0,\cdot)&=0,\\
(\partial_{1}v_{j})(0,\cdot)&=0,\quad j\in\{2,\dots,d\}.\end{split}

If
j=1
, then by Lemma
4.5
(using that
(\partial_{1}f)|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}=0
) we have the estimate

(4.4)

\|v_{1}\|_{W^{k+3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}\leq C\|\partial_{1}f\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}.

If
j\in\{2,\dots,d\}
, then applying Step 1 with
k-1
and
\gamma+p\in(p-1,2p-1)
, yields

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|v_{j}\|_{W^{k+3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}

\displaystyle=\|v_{j}\|_{W^{(k-1)+4,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+p+(k-1)p};X)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{j}f\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)},

and similarly for
j=0
we obtain

(4.6)

\|v_{j}\|_{W^{k+3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}.

The estimates (
4.4
), (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) imply that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{k+4,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}

\displaystyle\eqsim\sum_{j=0}^{d}\|v_{j}\|_{W^{k+3,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|\partial_{j}f\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k+2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)},

where the constant only depends on
p,k,\gamma,\mu,d
and
X
.
A density argument, similar to the proof of
[
67
, Proposition 5.4]
, yields the result. Note that the uniqueness of
u\in W^{k+4,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\hookrightarrow W^{k+3,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
follows from
[
67
, Proposition 5.6]
.
∎

We continue with the characterisation of fractional domains of the Neumann Laplacian.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{-1\}
,
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
such that
\gamma+kp>-1
,
\mu>0
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Let
\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
on
W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
as in Definition
4.1
. Then

\displaystyle D\big((\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=W^{k+2,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),

\displaystyle D\big((\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}})^{\frac{3}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=W^{k+4,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X).

Proof.

We write
A_{\rm Neu}:=\mu-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
.
For
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
it holds that
A_{\rm Neu}
has
\BIP
by Theorem
4.3
, so Propositions
2.3
and
3.15
imply

D(A_{\rm Neu}^{\frac{1}{2}})=[W^{k+1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+3,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\frac{1}{2}}=W^{k+2,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X).

By
[
35
, Theorem 15.2.5]
and the characterisation of
D(A_{\rm Neu}^{\frac{1}{2}})
we find

\displaystyle D(A_{\rm Neu}^{\frac{3}{2}})

\displaystyle=\{u\in D(A_{\rm Neu}):A_{\rm Neu}u\in D(A_{\rm Neu}^{\frac{1}{2}})\}

\displaystyle=\{u\in W^{k+3,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X):A_{\rm Neu}u\in W^{k+2,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\}.

From this, the embedding
W^{k+4,p}_{\Delta,{\rm Neu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)\hookrightarrow D(A_{\rm Neu}^{\frac{3}{2}})
is straightforward and the converse embedding follows from Lemma
4.10
.
∎

In contrast to the Dirichlet case, we do not need a version of Proposition
4.8
for the Neumann Laplacian. This is simply due to the fact that we cannot consider the Neumann Laplacian on
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma+kp};X)
with
\gamma>p-1
, see Theorem
4.3
.

5.
Functional calculus for the Laplacian on special domains

To derive the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Dirichlet and Neumann Laplacian on bounded domains, we will proceed in two steps:

(1)

Using the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Laplacian on the half-space (Theorems

and
4.3
) and known perturbation theorems for the
H^{\infty}
-calculus (Section

(2)

Performing a localisation procedure to transfer the

-calculus for the Laplacian on special domains to bounded domains.

In this section, we will perform Step

, while Step

is postponed to Section

.
While localisation procedures are standard in the literature (see, e.g.,
[

25

5.1.
Preliminary estimates

In the proofs of Theorems
5.2
and
5.3
, we derive the
H^{\infty}
-calculus on special domains by perturbing the corresponding calculus for the Laplacian on the half-space. To relate the Laplacian on special domains and the half-space, let
h_{1},h_{2}
and
\Psi
be as in Lemma
2.9
defining a diffeomorphism between a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}
-domain and the half-space. Recall that
\Psi_{*}f=f\circ\Psi^{-1}
for
f\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathcal{O};X)
and define
\Delta^{\Psi}:W^{2,1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)\to L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};X)
by

\Delta^{\Psi}:=\Psi_{*}\circ\Delta\circ(\Psi^{-1})_{*}.

An elementary computation shows that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\Psi}

\displaystyle=\Delta+|(\nabla h_{1})\circ\Psi^{-1}|^{2}\,\partial_{1}^{2}-2((\nabla h_{1})\circ\Psi^{-1})\cdot\nabla\partial_{1}-((\Delta h_{1})\circ\Psi^{-1})\partial_{1}

\displaystyle=:\Delta+B_{1}+B_{2}+B_{3}.

Note that
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are second-order differential operators since
(\nabla h_{1})\circ\Psi^{-1}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
if
\mathcal{O}
is a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}
-domain, see Lemma
2.9
. The order of the perturbation term
B_{3}
depends on the smoothness of the domain.

•

If
\mathcal{O}
is a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}
-domain, then
(\Delta h_{1})\circ\Psi^{-1}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and
B_{3}
is a first-order differential operator (and thus a lower-order perturbation term).

•

If
\mathcal{O}
is a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}
-domain, then
(\Delta h_{1})(\Psi^{-1}(y))
blows up like
y_{1}^{-1}
in the neighbourhood of
y_{1}=0
, see Lemma
2.9
. Therefore, estimating, say, the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma})
-norm of
B_{3}
gives that
the weight exponent effectively decreases. However, this loss can be compensated by applying Hardy’s inequality, which allows us to recover the original weight
w_{\gamma}
. In this way, we also obtain an additional derivative from Hardy’s inequality, meaning that
B_{3}
is a perturbation of the same order as
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
.

This demonstrates that if the smoothness of the domain is too low, then the perturbation term
B_{3}
is more difficult to deal with.
In the following lemmas, we provide precise estimates for the perturbation term

B_{1}
,
B_{2}
and
B_{3}
.
We start with the estimates for
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
.

(Estimates on

\displaystyle\big|\langle

\displaystyle\lesssim[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{1,\lambda}}\Big(\sum_{|\mu|=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\langle\partial^{\mu}\partial_{1}u,v\rangle_{X\times X^{\prime}}|\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}x_{1}^{-(1-\lambda)}|\langle\partial_{1}u,v\rangle_{X\times X^{\prime}}|\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\leq[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{1,\lambda}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}x_{1}^{\gamma}\|\partial_{1}u\|^{p}_{X}\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Big[\sum_{|\mu|=1}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}x_{1}^{\gamma^{\prime}}\|\partial^{\mu}v\|^{p^{\prime}}_{X^{\prime}}\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}+\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}x_{1}^{\gamma^{\prime}-(1-\lambda)p^{\prime}}\|v\|^{p^{\prime}}_{X^{\prime}}\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{1,\lambda}}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\|v\|_{W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma^{\prime}};X^{\prime})}\lesssim[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{1,\lambda}}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}.

This proves the desired estimate.
∎

Definition 6.1

If

as in Definition

(1)

The spectrum

is discrete, contained in

,

,

(2)

Remark 6.6
.

(1)

It is an open question whether Theorems
6.2
and
6.3
(in the case where
\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
is defined as in Definition
6.1
3
) with general
\UMD
Banach spaces
X
also hold for

. In the following special cases, one can actually conclude the result of Theorems
6.2
and
6.3
with

.

•

If

is a Hilbert space or isomorphic to a closed subspace of an

-space, then by redoing the proofs of

for Sobolev spaces, one sees that the results in the scalar case with

(Theorems

•

If

is a
\UMD

and that the semigroup corresponding to the Laplacian is positive and uniformly exponentially stable, we can obtain the bounded

For the general case (

and

\UMD

(2)

The

-independence of the spectra of the Laplacian on

-spaces is well-studied. Moreover, in

However, the power weights

6.1.
Consequences of the bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus

In this section, we discuss two consequences of the bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the Laplacian: maximal regularity and boundedness of the Riesz transform.

6.1.1.
Maximal
L^{q}
-regularity

Let
T\in(0,\infty]
. We study the time-dependent heat equation on
I:=(0,T)
given by

\partial_{t}u(t)-\Delta u(t)=f(t),\qquad t\in I,

on a bounded domain
\mathcal{O}
with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions and zero initial condition. For an extensive introduction to maximal regularity, the reader is referred to
[
35
, Chapter 17]
.

The following two corollaries on maximal regularity for the heat equation follow immediately from Theorems
6.2
,
6.3
,
6.4
,
6.5
and
[
35
, Theorems 17.3.18, 17.2.39 & Proposition 17.2.7]
.

Corollary 6.7

(Maximal regularity for
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
)
.

Assume that the conditions from Theorem
6.2
hold. In addition, let
q\in(1,\infty)
,
T\in(0,\infty)
and
v\in A_{q}(I)
. Then
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
on
W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)
has maximal
L^{q}(v)
-regularity on
I
, i.e., for all

f\in L^{q}(I,v;W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))

there exists a unique

u\in W^{1,q}(I,v;W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))\cap L^{q}(I,v;W^{k+2,p}_{{\rm Dir}}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))

such that
\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}u=f
with
u(0)=0
and

\|u\|_{W^{1,q}(I,v;W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))}+\|u\|_{L^{q}(I,v;W^{k+2,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q}(I,v;W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))},

where the constant only depends on
p,q,k,\gamma,v,T,d
and
X
.
Moreover, if
X=\mathbb{C}
, then the above statement holds for
I=\mathbb{R}_{+}
as well.

Corollary 6.8

(Maximal regularity for
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
)
.

Assume that the conditions from Theorem
6.3
hold. In addition, let
q\in(1,\infty)
,
T\in(0,\infty)
and
v\in A_{q}(I)
. Then
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
on
W^{k+j,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)
has maximal
L^{q}(v)
-regularity on
I
, i.e., for all

f\in L^{q}(I,v;W^{k+j,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))

there exists a unique

u\in W^{1,q}(I,v;W^{k+j,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))\cap L^{q}(I,v;W^{k+2+j,p}_{{\rm Neu}}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))

such that
\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}u=f
with
u(0)=0
and

\|u\|_{W^{1,q}(I,v;W^{k+j,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))}+\|u\|_{L^{q}(I,v;W^{k+2+j,p}_{\rm Neu}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q}(I,v;W^{k+j,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X))},

where the constant only depends on
p,q,k,\gamma,j,v,T,d
and
X
. Moreover, the above statement also holds if we consider
\Delta_{\operatorname{Neu}}
on the spaces without constant functions as in Definition
6.1
3
. In this case, if additionally
X=\mathbb{C}
, the statement also holds for
I=\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Remark 6.9
.

(1)

Similar results as in Corollaries
6.7
and
6.8
for
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
are obtained in
[
67
, Section 8]
.

(2)

Corollaries
6.7
and
6.8
concern the heat equation with zero initial data. Well-posedness for the heat equation with non-zero initial data can be obtained as a consequence, see
[
27
, Section 4.4]
and
[
35
, Section 17.2.b]
.

We connect the above results to the existing literature about PDE on homogeneous weighted Sobolev spaces, see
[
55
,
56
,
70
]
. For
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
a bounded
C^{1}
-domain, the homogeneous Sobolev spaces are given by

H^{k}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{O}):\forall|\alpha|\leq k,\partial^{\alpha}f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\theta-d+|\alpha|p})\Big\},

see for instance
[
70
, Proposition 2.2]
. Note that
L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma})=H^{0}_{p,\gamma+d}(\mathcal{O})
. In the setting for the Dirichlet Laplacian with
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
we have the following relation between the involved homogeneous and inhomogeneous spaces:

\displaystyle W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp})

\displaystyle=H^{k}_{p,\gamma+d}(\mathcal{O}),

\displaystyle W^{k+2,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp})

\displaystyle=H^{k+2}_{p,\gamma+d-2p}(\mathcal{O}).

The first characterisation follows from the fact that
\mathcal{O}
is bounded and Hardy’s inequality using that
\gamma+kp>-1
. The second characterisation follows similarly using that
W^{k+2,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp})=W^{k+2,p}_{0}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp})
for
\gamma\in(p-1,2p-1)
. Note that we have used that the domain is bounded, for unbounded domains the homogeneous and inhomogeneous spaces cannot be compared.

In
[
51
]
, the authors use homogeneous spaces to study spatial regularity for boundary value problems with Dirichlet boundary conditions on bounded

C^{1}
-domains. There, the boundary condition is encoded implicitly within the function space. In contrast, our approach imposes boundary conditions explicitly, allowing greater flexibility – particularly when extending to more regular domains or handling smaller weight exponents and Neumann boundary conditions.
In the homogeneous setting, some results for the Neumann Laplacian on the half-space (in the special case
k=0
) are contained in
[
20
,
21
]
, but a general study on bounded domains seems to be unavailable.

Finally, we remark that maximal
L^{q}
-regularity for the Dirichlet Laplacian on
L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma})
is also obtained in
[
53
]
. Here they treat bounded
C^{1,\lambda}
-domains with
\gamma\in((1-\lambda)p-1,2p-1)
which corresponds to our result in Corollary
6.7
with
k=0
.

6.1.2.
Riesz transforms

In this section, we discuss the boundedness of the Riesz transform associated with the Dirichlet Laplacian on the half-space and bounded domains. For an elaborate study of Riesz transforms associated with the Laplacian on the half-space, the reader is referred to
[
22
]
.

We start with an extension of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
from scalar-valued Lebesgue spaces to vector-valued Lebesgue spaces, see also Remark
6.6
. This extends the result in
[
69
, Theorem 6.1 & Corollary 6.2]
.

Corollary 6.10

(
H^{\infty}
-calculus for
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
on
L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
\gamma\in((1-\lambda)p-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Let
\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
on
L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)
be as in Definition
6.1
. Then the operator
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus with
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})=0
.

Proof.

We define the operators

\displaystyle\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}\quad\text{ on }L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma})\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{X}

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}\quad\text{ on }L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma};X)

as in Definition
6.1
. Theorem
6.4
implies that
0\in\rho(-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{\mathbb{C}})
and it follows from
[
35
, Proposition K.2.3]
that the analytic semigroup
S_{t}
generated by
\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{\mathbb{C}}
is uniformly exponentially stable. Moreover, the resolvent
R(\lambda,\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{\mathbb{C}})
is positive for
\lambda>0
(this follows from the
L^{2}
-case and consistency in Lemma
6.14
) and
[
24
, Theorem VI.1.8]
yields that
S_{t}
is positive. Therefore, by
[
33
, Theorem 2.1.3]
the operator
S_{t}\otimes\id_{X}
defined by

(S_{t}\otimes\id_{X})(f\otimes x):=S_{t}f\otimes x,\qquad f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}}),\;x\in X,

extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}};X)
with equal operator norm. It is straightforward to verify that
S_{t}\otimes\id_{X}
is generated by
\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{X}
and that
R(\lambda,\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{X})(f\otimes x)=(R(\lambda,\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{\mathbb{C}})f)\otimes x
for
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{O},w_{\gamma}^{\partial\mathcal{O}})
,
x\in X
and
\lambda\in\rho(\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{\mathbb{C}})\cap\rho(\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{X})
. The semigroup
S_{t}\otimes\id_{X}
is also uniformly exponentially stable, which shows that
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}^{X}
is sectorial. Proposition
2.4
and Theorem
6.2
now give the desired result.
∎

We have the following result for the Riesz transform associated with the Dirichlet Laplacian.

Corollary 6.11

(Riesz transform associated with
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
and let
X
be a
\UMD
Banach space. Assume that either

(1)

\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
,
k=0
,
\gamma\in(-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
and
X
is a
\UMD
Banach space, or,

(2)

\mathcal{O}
is a bounded
C^{1,\lambda}
-domain,
k=0
,
\gamma\in((1-\lambda)p-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
and
X
is a
\UMD
Banach space, or,

(3)

\mathcal{O}
is a bounded
C^{1,\lambda}
-domain,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\gamma\in((1-\lambda)p-1,2p-1)\setminus\{p-1\}
and
X=\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
on
W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)
be as in Definition
4.1
or
6.1
. Then

\|\nabla(-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)},\qquad f\in W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X),

for some
C>0
which only depends on
p,k,\gamma,\mathcal{O}
and
X
.

Proof.

First, we claim that

(6.1)

(-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}:W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)\to W^{k+1,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)

is bounded. Indeed, since

(-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{-1}:W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)\to W^{k+2,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)

is bounded (see Theorems
4.2
,
6.4
and Corollary
6.10
) and the identity operator is bounded on
W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)
, it holds by Stein interpolation
[
84
, Theorem 2.1]
that

(-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}:W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)\to[W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+2,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\frac{1}{2}}

is bounded. To verify the conditions for Stein interpolation, one uses that
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
has
\BIP
, which follows again from the bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus in Theorem
4.2
, Theorem
6.4
and Corollary
6.10
. The claim (
6.1
) now follows from Proposition
3.14
.

Therefore, (
6.1
), Proposition
3.14
and Proposition
2.3
(using that
-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}
has
\BIP
), imply

\displaystyle\|\nabla(-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)}

\displaystyle\leq\|(-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{W^{k+1,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)}

\displaystyle\eqsim\|(-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{[W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X),W^{k+2,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)]_{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\eqsim\|(-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{D((-\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}})^{\frac{1}{2}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp};X)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.13

(

\mathcal{P}D(\widetilde{A})\subseteq D(A)

(3)

(\mathcal{I}A-\widetilde{A}\mathcal{I})\mathcal{P}\colon D(\widetilde{A})\to\widetilde{Y}

\mathcal{I}(A\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}\widetilde{A})\colon D(\widetilde{A})\to\widetilde{Y}

\omega_{H^{\infty}}(\mu+A)\leq\sigma
.

Lemma 6.14
.

Let

,

,

,

\displaystyle A_{p,k,\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{\operatorname{Dir}}\quad\text{ on }W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp})\text{ with }D(A_{p,k,\gamma})=W^{k+2,p}_{\rm Dir}(\mathcal{O},w^{\partial\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma+kp})

Take

,

Appendix A
Estimates on the Dahlberg–Kenig–Stein pullback

In this appendix, we prove the estimates on the Dahlberg–Kenig–Stein pullback as stated in Lemma
2.9
. These estimates rely on regularised distances to the boundary and provide control over higher-order derivatives of the coordinate transformation that flattens the boundary.
We start with some preliminaries from
[
66
]
on regularised distances (see also
[
28
]
).

We consider
d\geq 2
. Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open with non-empty boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
. Then we define the
signed distance
as

(A.1)

d(x):=\begin{cases}\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\mathcal{O})&\mbox{if }x\in\mathcal{O},\\
-\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\mathcal{O})&\mbox{if }x\notin\mathcal{O}.\end{cases}

A function
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\partial\mathcal{O})\cap C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called a
regularised distance
if the ratios
\rho(x)/d(x)
and
d(x)/\rho(x)
are positive and bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\partial\mathcal{O}
.

The following proposition provides the existence and regularity of regularised distances.

Lemma A.1

(
[
66
, Lemma 1.1 & Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open with a non-empty boundary and let
g\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
g(x)/d(x)
and
d(x)/g(x)
are positive and bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\partial\mathcal{O}
. Let
L>0
be such that

|g(x)-g(y)|\leq\frac{L}{2}|x-y|,\qquad\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and let
\phi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be non-negative such that
\text{\rm supp\,}(\phi)\subseteq B_{1}(0)
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x=1
. Define

(A.2)

G(x,\tau):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x-(\tau/L)z)\phi(z)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}z,\qquad(x,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}.

Then the unique solution
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
to

\rho(x)=G(x,\rho(x)),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

is a regularised distance for
\mathcal{O}
. In addition, if
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
g\in C^{\ell,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\partial\mathcal{O})\cap C^{\ell,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

The results follow from
[
66
, Lemma 1.1 & Theorem 1.3]
upon noting that since
\phi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have that
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\partial\mathcal{O})
.
∎

We note that every domain
\mathcal{O}
with a non-empty boundary has a regularised distance. Indeed, this follows from Lemma
A.1
with

, see
[
66
, Corollary 1.2]
.

Using the regularised distances, we construct a diffeomorphism that preserves the distance to the boundary and straightens the boundary smoothly in the interior of a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell}
-domain. Moreover, we provide estimates on the higher-order derivatives. The following lemma extends the result for special

-domains in

Lemma

\mathcal{O}
be a special

, we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}h_{1}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\mathcal{O})^{-(|\alpha|-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}},\qquad

\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{O},

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}h_{2}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(y,\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+})^{-(|\alpha|-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}},\qquad

\displaystyle y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+},

where the constant
C>0
only depends on
\ell,\lambda,\alpha
and
d
.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
be a non-negative and even function with
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\eta(x_{1})\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x_{1}=1
and let
\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d-1})
be a non-negative function such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}}\varphi(\widetilde{x})\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}=1
.
Then
\phi:=\eta\otimes\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
satisfies
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(x)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}x=1
. Moreover,
\eta
and
\phi
can be chosen such that
\text{\rm supp\,}(\phi)\subseteq B_{1}(0)
. Define
h\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1};\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathcal{O}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{1}>h(\widetilde{x})\}
, see Definition
2.8
.

Step 1: proof of
1
,
2
and
3
.

Let
d(x)
be the signed distance to
\partial\mathcal{O}
as defined in (
A.1
) and define
g\in C^{0,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by

g(x):=x_{1}-h(\widetilde{x}),\qquad x=(x_{1},\widetilde{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Then the ratios
g(x)/d(x)
and
d(x)/g(x)
are positive and bounded on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\partial\mathcal{O}
.
The function
g
satisfies the Lipschitz estimate

(A.3)

\displaystyle|g(x)-g(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|x_{1}-y_{1}|+[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{0,1}}|\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y}|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}(1+[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{0,1}})|x-y|,\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Define
G
as in (
A.2
) with
L=2\sqrt{2}(1+[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{0,1}})
, then by Lemma
A.1
there exists a unique function
\rho:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
that solves the equation

(A.4)

\rho(x)=G(x,\rho(x)),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Moreover,
\rho\in C^{0,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\partial\mathcal{O})
and the ratios
\rho(x)/d(x)
and
d(x)/\rho(x)
are positive and bounded on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\partial\mathcal{O}
.
Upon noting that for
(x,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
we have, using the properties of
\eta,\phi
and
\varphi
, that

\displaystyle G(x,\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}g(x-(\tau/L)z)\phi(z)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left[(x_{1}-(\tau/L)z_{1})-h(\widetilde{x}-(\tau/L)\widetilde{z})\right]\phi(z)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=x_{1}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}}h(\widetilde{x}-(\tau/L)\widetilde{z})\varphi(\widetilde{z})\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{z}

\displaystyle=:x_{1}-h_{2}(\tau,\widetilde{x}),

the equation (
A.4
) can be rewritten as

(A.5)

\rho(x)=x_{1}-h_{2}(\rho(x),\widetilde{x}),\qquad x=(x_{1},\widetilde{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

with
h_{2}\in C^{0,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+})
.

In addition, define
h_{1}(x):=x_{1}-\rho(x)
. We will now prove that
h_{1}
and
h_{2}

satisfy the desired properties
1
,

2
and
3
.
Define the functions
\Psi,\overline{\Psi}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by

\displaystyle\Psi(x)

\displaystyle:=(\rho(x),\widetilde{x})=(x_{1}-h_{1}(x),\widetilde{x}),\qquad x=(x_{1},\widetilde{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}(y)

\displaystyle:=(y_{1}+h_{2}(y),\widetilde{y}),\qquad y=(y_{1},\widetilde{y})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Then
\Psi\in C^{0,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\partial\mathcal{O};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\overline{\Psi}\in C^{0,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
We claim that
\overline{\Psi}
is the inverse of
\Psi
.
We first show that
\overline{\Psi}\circ\Psi=\id
. For
x=(x_{1},\widetilde{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, it holds

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}(\Psi(x))

\displaystyle=\overline{\Psi}(\rho(x),\widetilde{x})=\big(\rho(x)+h_{2}(\rho(x),\widetilde{x}),\widetilde{x}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:rho_reg_distance_implicit;F}}}{{=}}(x_{1},\widetilde{x})=x.

In order to prove that
\Psi\circ\overline{\Psi}=\id
, let
y=(y_{1},\widetilde{y})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
As the ratios
\rho/d
and
d/\rho
are positive and bounded on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\partial\mathcal{O}
while
\lim_{x_{1}\to\pm\infty}d(x_{1},\widetilde{y})=\pm\infty
, we have that
\lim_{x_{1}\to\pm\infty}\rho(x_{1},\widetilde{y})=\pm\infty
.
Since
\rho(\,\cdot\,,\widetilde{y})
is continuous, it follows from the intermediate value theorem that this function is surjective.
In particular, there exists
x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\rho(x_{1},\widetilde{y})=y_{1}
.
We find that

\displaystyle\Psi(\overline{\Psi}(y))=

\displaystyle\;\Psi(y_{1}+h_{2}(y),\widetilde{y})=\Psi\big(\rho(x_{1},\widetilde{y})+h_{2}(\rho(x_{1},\widetilde{y}),\widetilde{y}),\widetilde{y}\big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:rho_reg_distance_implicit;F}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\Psi(x_{1},\widetilde{y})=(\rho(x_{1},\widetilde{y}),\widetilde{y})=(y_{1},\widetilde{y})=y.

This proves the claim that
\Psi
and
\overline{\Psi}
are inverses and have the desired regularity. Moreover, the distance to the boundary is preserved since
\rho(x)\eqsim d(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{O}
. This completes the proof of
1
,

2
and
3
.

Step 2: proof of estimates on
h_{2}
in
4
.
Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
,
\lambda\in[0,1]
and let
\mathcal{O}
be a special
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ell,\lambda}
-domain with
[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}\leq 1
. Then one can take
L=4\sqrt{2}
in (
A.3
) and from Step 1 and Lemma
A.1
, it is clear that the regularity of the diffeomorphism
\Psi
improves to
C^{\ell,\lambda}
.
For multi-indices we write
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\widetilde{\alpha})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d-1}
. If
\ell_{0}\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
and
|\alpha|\leq\ell_{0}
, then we compute

(A.6)

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}h_{2}(x_{1},\widetilde{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(-L)^{\alpha_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\sum_{|\nu|=\alpha_{1}}(\partial^{\nu+\widetilde{\alpha}}h)(\widetilde{x}-(x_{1}/L)\widetilde{z})\widetilde{z}^{\nu}\varphi(\widetilde{z})\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{z}.

Indeed, if
\alpha_{1}=1
, then by the chain rule it holds that

\partial_{x_{1}}h(\widetilde{x}-(x_{1}/L)\widetilde{z})=(\nabla h)(\widetilde{x}-(x_{1}/L)\widetilde{z})\cdot\frac{-\widetilde{z}}{L}=-L^{-1}\sum_{|\nu|=1}(\partial^{\nu}h)(\widetilde{x}-(x_{1}/L)\widetilde{z})\widetilde{z}^{\nu},

and by iteration one can check (
A.6
) for any
\alpha_{1}\geq 1
.
From (
A.6
) it follows that

|\partial^{\alpha}h_{2}(x_{1},\widetilde{x})|\leq C\|h\|_{C^{\ell}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}\sum_{|\nu|=\alpha_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\widetilde{z}^{\nu}\varphi(\widetilde{z})\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{z}\leq C[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}},

which proves the estimate for
|\alpha|\leq\ell_{0}
. Now let
|\alpha|\geq\ell_{0}+1
and let
\beta,\overline{\beta}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
be such that
\beta+\overline{\beta}=\alpha
with
|\beta|=\ell_{0}
and
|\overline{\beta}|=\alpha-\ell_{0}
.
From (
A.6
) and a substitution
\widetilde{z}=((\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L)/x_{1}
it follows that

(A.7)

\displaystyle\partial^{\beta}h_{2}(x_{1},\widetilde{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(-L)^{\beta_{1}}}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{L}\Big)^{1-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\sum_{|\nu|=\beta_{1}}(\partial^{\nu+\widetilde{\beta}}h)(\widetilde{y})\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)^{\nu}\varphi\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{y}.

By computing the
\overline{\beta}
-derivatives using (
A.7
), we claim that

(A.8)

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}h_{2}(x_{1},\widetilde{x})

\displaystyle=\partial^{\overline{\beta}}\partial^{\beta}h_{2}(x_{1},\widetilde{x})

\displaystyle=C\frac{1}{x_{1}^{|\alpha|-\ell_{0}}}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{L}\Big)^{1-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\sum_{|\nu|=\beta_{1}}(\partial^{\nu+\widetilde{\beta}}h)(\widetilde{y})\varphi_{\beta,\overline{\beta},\nu}\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{y},

where
\varphi_{\beta,\overline{\beta},\nu}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
and
\int\varphi_{\beta,\overline{\beta},\nu}(\widetilde{z})\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{z}=0
. Indeed, if
\overline{\beta}=e_{j}
is the
j
-th unit vector for some
j\in\{2,\dots,d\}
, then by writing
\widetilde{x}=(x_{2},\dots,x_{d})
and
\widetilde{y}=(y_{2},\dots,y_{d})
, a calculation shows that

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{j}}\Big[\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)^{\nu}\varphi\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)\Big]=

\displaystyle\;\frac{L}{x_{1}}\Big[\nu_{j}\Big(\frac{(x_{j}-y_{j})L}{x_{1}}\Big)^{\nu_{j}-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=2\\
n\neq j\end{subarray}}^{d}\Big(\frac{(x_{n}-y_{n})L}{x_{1}}\Big)^{\nu_{n}}\varphi\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)^{\nu}(\partial_{j}\varphi)\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\;x_{1}^{-1}\varphi_{\beta,e_{j},\nu}\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big).

Moreover, note that

(A.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\varphi_{\beta,e_{j},\nu}(\widetilde{z})\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{z}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{L}\Big)^{1-d}x_{1}\partial_{x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)^{\nu}\varphi\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{y}

\displaystyle=x_{1}\partial_{x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\widetilde{z}^{\nu}\varphi(\widetilde{z})\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{z}=0,

and clearly we have
\varphi_{\beta,e_{j},\nu}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
. This shows (
A.8
) for
\overline{\beta}=e_{j}
with
j\in\{2,\dots,d\}
. If
\overline{\beta}=e_{1}
, then a calculation shows that

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{1}}\Big[\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{L}\Big)^{1-d}

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)^{\nu}\varphi\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{x_{1}}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{L}\Big)^{1-d}\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)^{\nu}\Big[(1-d-\beta_{1})\varphi\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\;-(\nabla\varphi)\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\;x_{1}^{-1}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{L}\Big)^{1-d}\varphi_{\beta,e_{1},\nu}\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big).

The properties of
\varphi_{\beta,e_{1},\nu}
follow similarly as in (
A.9
). Therefore, we have proved (
A.8
) for
|\overline{\beta}|=1
. For
|\overline{\beta}|\geq 2
we can argue by induction to show that

\partial_{x}^{\overline{\beta}}\Big[\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{L}\Big)^{1-d}\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)^{\nu}\varphi\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big)\Big]=C\frac{1}{x_{1}^{|\alpha|-\ell_{0}}}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{L}\Big)^{1-d}\varphi_{\beta,\overline{\beta},\nu}\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L}{x_{1}}\Big).

This follows in the same manner as for
|\overline{\beta}|=1
by considering the
\partial_{x_{1}}
(taking into account the additional
x_{1}^{-(|\alpha|-\ell_{0})}
factor) and
\partial_{x_{j}}
separately. Therefore, (
A.8
) follows.

Performing the substitution
\widetilde{z}=((\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y})L)/x_{1}
in (
A.8
) and using that
\varphi_{\beta,\overline{\beta},\nu}
integrates to zero, gives

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}h_{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\,x_{1}^{-(|\alpha|-\ell_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\sum_{|\nu|=\beta_{1}}\big|(\partial^{\nu+\widetilde{\beta}}h)(\widetilde{x}-(x_{1}/L)\widetilde{z})-(\partial^{\nu+\widetilde{\beta}}h)(\widetilde{x})\big|\,|\varphi_{\beta,\overline{\beta},\nu}(\widetilde{z})|\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{z}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|h\|_{C^{\ell,\lambda_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}\,x_{1}^{-(|\alpha|-\ell_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}|(x_{1}/L)\widetilde{z}|^{\lambda_{0}}|\varphi_{\beta,\overline{\beta},\nu}(\widetilde{z})|\hskip 2.0pt\mathrm{d}\widetilde{z}

\displaystyle\leq C\,[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}\,x_{1}^{-(|\alpha|-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})}.

This implies the estimate for
h_{2}
in
4
.

Step 3: proof of estimates on
h_{1}
in
4
.

It remains to prove the estimates for
h_{1}(x)=x_{1}-\rho(x)
, which we achieve by using the implicit function theorem and the estimates for
h_{2}
.
Consider the function

E(x,\tau):=\tau+h_{2}(\tau,\widetilde{x})-x_{1},\qquad(x,\tau)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}.

We first establish some properties of
E
. Note that
E(x,\rho(x))=0
by (
A.5
). Furthermore, it holds that

\partial_{\tau}E(x,\tau)=1+\partial_{\tau}h_{2}(\tau,\widetilde{x})=1-\partial_{\tau}G(x,\tau).

As
|G(x,\tau_{1})-G(x,\tau_{2})|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|
by (
A.3
) (see
[
66
, (1.3)]
), we have
|\partial_{\tau}G(x,\tau)|\leq\frac{1}{2}
and thus

(A.10)

|\partial_{\tau}E(x,\tau)|\geq 1-|\partial_{\tau}G(x,\tau)|\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Furthermore, using that
\rho\eqsim d
and the estimates for
h_{2}
, we have for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
,
\ell_{0}\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
and
\lambda_{0}\in[0,\lambda]
that

(A.11)

\displaystyle|d(x)|^{(|\alpha|-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}}|(\partial^{\alpha}E)(x,\rho(x))|

\displaystyle\eqsim|\rho(x)|^{(|\alpha|-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}}|(\partial^{\alpha}E)(x,\rho(x))|

\displaystyle\leq C\,[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}}.

Recalling that
\tau=\rho(x)
is the unique solution of the equation
E(x,\tau)=0
, we obtain with the implicit function theorem that

(A.12)

(\partial_{x_{j}}\rho(x))(\partial_{\tau}E)(x,\rho(x))=-(\partial_{x_{j}}E)(x,\rho(x)),\qquad j\in\{1,\dots,d\}.

By (
A.12
) and the product rule we obtain for
\overline{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
that

(A.13)

\displaystyle(\partial^{\overline{\alpha}}\partial_{x_{j}}\rho(x))(\partial_{\tau}E)(x,\rho(x))=

\displaystyle-\partial^{\overline{\alpha}}\big((\partial_{x_{j}}E)(x,\rho(x))\big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\mu|\leq|\overline{\alpha}|-1\\
|\mu|+|\nu|=|\overline{\alpha}|\end{subarray}}c_{\overline{\alpha},\mu,\nu}(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{x_{j}}\rho(x))\partial^{\nu}\big((\partial_{\tau}E)(x,\rho(x))\big).

Let
z(x):=(x,\rho(x))
and
F\in\{\partial_{x_{j}}E,\partial_{\tau}E\}
. By the multivariate Faà di Bruno’s formula
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
we have that
\partial_{x}^{\nu}F(z(x))
for
|\nu|\leq|\overline{\alpha}|
can be written as a linear combination of

(A.14)

(\partial^{\beta}F)(z(x))\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{|\beta|}\partial^{\delta_{i}}z_{j_{i}}(x),

where
1\leq|\beta|\leq|\nu|
,
\delta_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\delta_{i}|\geq 1
for
i\in\{1,\dots,|\beta|\}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{|\beta|}|\delta_{i}|=|\nu|
. Moreover, for
j_{i}\in\{1,\dots,d+1\}
,
z_{j_{i}}(x)
denotes the
j_{i}
-th coordinate of
z(x)=(x,\rho(x))
. Note that if
j_{i}\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, then
|\delta_{i}|=1
or else the entire expression in (
A.14
) equals zero. Setting
r\in\{0,\dots,|\beta|\}
the number of
j_{i}
such that
j_{i}=d+1
, then by reindexing we can write (
A.14
) as

(A.15)

(\partial^{\beta}F)(z(x))\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}\partial^{\delta_{i}}\rho(x).

Moreover, it holds that

(A.16)

|\nu|=\sum_{i=1}^{|\beta|}|\delta_{i}|=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\delta_{i}|+|\beta|-r.

If
r=0
, then the product over
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
is considered to be one and the sum over
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
is considered to be zero.

Using the function
E
and its properties mentioned above, we will show that

(A.17)

|d(x)|^{(|\alpha|-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}}|\partial^{\alpha}\rho(x)|\leq C\,[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}},\qquad x\in\mathcal{O},

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
\ell_{0}\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
and
\lambda_{0}\in[0,\lambda]
. Note that (
A.17
) implies the desired estimates on
h_{1}(x)=x_{1}-\rho(x)
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. For
\alpha=0
the estimate on
h_{1}(x)=h_{2}(\rho(x),\widetilde{x})
follows from the estimate on
h_{2}
. Therefore, it remains to prove (
A.17
).

For
|\alpha|=1
the estimate (
A.17
) follows from (
A.12
) together with (
A.10
) and (
A.11
). We proceed by induction on
|\alpha|
. Let
m\geq 1
and assume that (
A.17
) holds for any
|\alpha|\leq m
,
\ell_{0}\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
and
\lambda_{0}\in[0,\lambda]
. It remains to prove (
A.17
) for
|\alpha|=m+1
. Consider (
A.13
) with
|\overline{\alpha}|=m
multiplied by
d(x)^{(m+1-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}}
. By (
A.10
) and (
A.15
) it suffices to show uniform boundedness of

(A.18)

d(x)^{(m+1-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}}\big|\partial^{\mu}\partial_{x_{j}}\rho(x)\big|\big|(\partial^{\beta}F)(z(x))\big|\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}\big|\partial^{\delta_{i}}\rho(x)\big|,

where
F\in\{\partial_{x_{j}}E,\partial_{\tau}E\}
,
0\leq|\mu|\leq m-1
,
|\mu|+|\nu|=m
,
1\leq|\beta|\leq|\nu|
and
r\in\{0,\dots,|\beta|\}
such that
|\delta_{i}|\geq 1
for
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
and (
A.16
) holds. We have to distribute the weights
d(x)
over the terms with derivatives on
F
and
\rho
so that we can apply (
A.11
) and the induction hypothesis to obtain that (
A.18
) is uniformly bounded.
Suppose that we have
\kappa_{\mu},\kappa_{\beta},\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{r}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(A.19)

\displaystyle(|\mu|+1-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{\mu}\leq|\mu|+1,

\displaystyle(|\beta|+1-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{\beta}\leq|\beta|+1,

\displaystyle(|\delta_{i}|-\ell_{0})_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{i}\,\leq|\delta_{i}|,\qquad i\in\{1,\dots,r\},

and

(A.20)

\kappa_{\mu}+\kappa_{\beta}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\kappa_{i}|=(m+1-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}.

Then, (
A.18
) can be estimated as

(A.21)

\big|d(x)^{\kappa_{\mu}}\partial^{\mu}\partial_{x_{j}}\rho(x)\big|\big|d(x)^{\kappa_{\beta}}(\partial^{\beta}F)(z(x))\big|\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}\big|d(x)^{\kappa_{i}}\partial^{\delta_{i}}\rho(x)\big|\leq C[\mathcal{O}]_{C^{\ell,\lambda}},\quad x\in\mathcal{O},

where we have used (
A.11
) and the induction hypothesis (
A.17
) (note that
|\mu|+1\leq m
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\delta_{i}|\leq|\nu|\leq m
). It remains to show the existence of
\kappa
’s satisfying (
A.19
) and (
A.20
). We distinguish several cases.

If
m\leq\ell_{0}-1
, then
(m+1-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0})_{+}=0
and we can take
\kappa_{\mu}=\kappa_{\beta}=\kappa_{1}=\dots=\kappa_{r}=0
. From now on, we assume that
m\geq\ell_{0}
. If
|\mu|\geq\ell_{0}
, then we can take

\kappa_{\mu}=|\mu|+1-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0}\geq 0,\quad\kappa_{\beta}=|\beta|\geq 1\quad\text{ and }\quad\kappa_{i}=|\delta_{i}|-1\geq 0\text{ for }i\in\{1,\dots,r\},

and (
A.16
) implies that (
A.20
) is satisfied. Similarly, if
|\beta|\geq\ell_{0}
, then we can take

\kappa_{\mu}=|\mu|\geq 0,\quad\kappa_{\beta}=|\beta|+1-\ell_{0}-\lambda_{0}\geq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad\kappa_{i}=|\delta_{i}|-1\geq 0\text{ for }i\in\{1,\dots,r\}.

Finally, it only remains to consider the case
|\mu|\leq\ell_{0}-1

Cohen-Lenstra flag universality for random matrix products
Yifeng Huang, Hoi H. Nguyen, Roger Van Peski

Abstract
. For n × n n\times n random integer matrices M 1 , … , M k M_{1},\ldots,M_{k} , the cokernels of the partial products cok ( M 1 ⋯ M i ) , 1 ≤ i ≤ k \mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\cdots M_{i}),1\leq i\leq k naturally define a random flag of abelian p p -groups. We prove that as n → ∞ n\to\infty , this flag converges universally, for any nondegenerate entry distribution, to the Cohen-Lenstra type measure which weights each flag inversely proportional to the size of its automorphism group. As a corollary, we prove universality of certain formulas for the limiting conditional distribution of cok ⁡ ( M 1 ​ M 2 ) \mathrm{cok}(M_{1}M_{2}) given cok ⁡ ( M 1 ) , cok ⁡ ( M 2 ) \mathrm{cok}(M_{1}),\mathrm{cok}(M_{2}) in terms of Hall-Littlewood structure constants, which were previously obtained only for Haar matrices over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} . Our proofs combine the general technology of Sawin-Wood [ SW22 ] , matrix product moment computations following those of [ NVP24b ] , and the computation done previously for Haar p p -adic matrices in [ Hua24 ] .

1.3.
A corollary: universality of conditional convolution

Because the determinant is multiplicative and
\lvert\mathrm{cok}(M)\rvert=\lvert\det(M)\rvert
for nonsingular
M\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
,

(1.10)

\lvert\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}M_{2})_{P}\rvert=\lvert\mathrm{cok}(M_{1})_{P}\rvert\cdot\lvert\mathrm{cok}(M_{2})_{P}\rvert.

In particular, the cokernel
\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}M_{2})_{P}
should not have the same asymptotic Cohen-Lenstra distribution since it is typically larger—and never smaller—than
\mathrm{cok}(M_{1})

\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}M_{2})_{P}

\mathrm{cok}(M_{1})_{P}
and
\mathrm{cok}(M_{2})_{P}

\mathrm{cok}(M_{1})_{P}
and
\mathrm{cok}(M_{2})_{P}
and then consider the distribution of
\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}M_{2})_{P}
, i.e. to consider the probabilities

(1.11)

{\mathbf{P}}(\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}M_{2})_{P}\simeq G\mid\mathrm{cok}(M_{1})_{P}\simeq H\text{ and }\mathrm{cok}(M_{2})_{P}\simeq K)

for any three abelian
P
-groups

Corollary 1.4

Let

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{P}}(\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}M_{2})_{P}\simeq G\mid\mathrm{cok}(M_{1})_{P}\simeq H\text{ and }\mathrm{cok}(M_{2})_{P}\simeq K)\\
=\frac{\lvert\Aut(K)\rvert\cdot\lvert\Aut(H)\rvert}{\lvert\Aut(G)\rvert}\lvert\{N\leq G:N\simeq K,G/N\simeq H\}\rvert.

be a finite set of primes. The goal of this section is to define a subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
of
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
that contains all objects and all isomorphisms in
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
. We will perform the moment method of Sawin and Wood
[
SW22
]
on
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
. It will turn out that the use of
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
instead of
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
makes it easier to apply the techniques in
[
SW22
]
.

2.1.
Injective flags

For a finite abelian
P
-group
G
, an
injective
k
-flag
in
G
is a sequence of nested subgroups

(2.1)

\mathcal{F}=(H_{1}\leq\dots\leq H_{k-1}\leq G).

We say
H_{i}
is the
i
-th
step
of
\mathcal{F}
. Note that there are only
k-1
steps
H_{1},\dots,H_{k-1}
. By convention, we say the
0
-th step of
\mathcal{F}
is
0
and the
k
-th step of
\mathcal{F}
is
G
. We denote by
s_{i}(\mathcal{F})
the
i
-th step of
\mathcal{F}
, for
0\leq i\leq k
, which is useful when
H_{i}
is not mentioned in the context.

Recall
\mathbf{FinMod}_{P}
is the category of finite abelian
P
-groups. Consider the functor
\mathcal{F}l_{k}:\mathbf{FinMod}_{P}\to\mathbf{Sets}
that, object-wise, associates to an abelian
P
-group
G
the set of injective
k
-flags in
G
. Morphism-wise, the functor
\mathcal{F}l_{k}
is defined as follows. For a homomorphism
\alpha:G\to G^{\prime}
of abelian
P
-groups and an injective
k
-flag

(2.2)

\mathcal{F}=(H_{1}\leq\dots\leq H_{k-1}\leq G)\in\mathcal{F}l_{k}(G),

define
\alpha(\mathcal{F})\in\mathcal{F}l_{k}(G^{\prime})
to be the injective
k
-flag
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}

(2.3)

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=(\alpha(H_{1})\leq\dots\leq\alpha(H_{k-1})\leq G^{\prime}).

Therefore, the
i
-th step of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is

(2.4)

s_{i}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\alpha(s_{i}(\mathcal{F}))

for
0\leq i\leq k-1
. However, it is not true that
s_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\alpha(s_{k}(\mathcal{F}))
unless
\alpha
is surjective, as
s_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=G^{\prime}
by convention.

It is clear that
\mathcal{F}l_{k}
by our definition is a functor.

2.2.
The category of finite abelian
P
-groups together with an injective
k
-flag

Definition 2.1
.

Define
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
to be the category
(\mathbf{FinMod}_{P},\mathcal{F}l_{k})
in the notation of
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.22]
. Namely, the objects of
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
are pairs
(G,\mathcal{F})
, where
G\in\mathbf{FinMod}_{P}
and
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{F}l_{k}(G)
. A morphism
\alpha:(G,\mathcal{F})\to(G^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is a homomorphism
\alpha:G\to G^{\prime}
such that
\alpha(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The category
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
is a diamond category in the sense of
[
SW22
]
. Moreover, a morphism
\alpha:(G,\mathcal{F})\to(G^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is an epimorphism in
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
if and only if
\alpha
is surjective.

Proof.

By
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.1]
with
R={\mathbb{Z}}_{(P)}
(see (
1.4
)), the category
\mathbf{FinMod}_{P}
is a diamond category. The first assertion then follows directly from
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.22]
.

The “if” part of the second assertion follows from the fact that the forgetful functor
{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathbf{FinMod}_{P}
is faithful (note that
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}((G,{\mathcal{F}}),(G^{\prime},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}))
is a subset of
\Hom_{\mathbf{FinMod}_{P}}(G,G^{\prime})
by construction). The “only if” part of the second assertion follows from the proof of
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.22]
. More precisely, the first two paragraphs of that proof show that if a morphism
\alpha:G\to G^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
such that
\alpha(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is an epimorphism
(G,\mathcal{F})\to(G^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
, then
\alpha
is an epimorphism in
\mathbf{FinMod}_{P}
.
∎

2.3.
Comparison with surjective flags

We define a functor
Q:{\mathcal{C}}_{P}\to\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
as follows. Given
(G,\mathcal{F})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
with

(2.5)

\mathcal{F}=(H_{1}\leq\dots\leq H_{k-1}\leq G)\in\mathcal{F}l_{k}(G),

define
Q((G,\mathcal{F}))
to be the surjective
k
-flag

(2.6)

G/\mathcal{F}:=(G\twoheadrightarrow G/H_{1}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G/H_{k-1}).

Given two objects
(G,\mathcal{F})
and
(G^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
in
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
, we note that a morphism from
G/\mathcal{F}\to G^{\prime}/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
is a homomorphism
\alpha:G\to G^{\prime}
such that the following commutative diagram factors for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
:

(2.7)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/s_{i}({\mathcal{F}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G^{\prime}/s_{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

This is equivalent to the condition that
\alpha(s_{i}({\mathcal{F}}))\subseteq s_{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
. On the other hand, a morphism from
(G,{\mathcal{F}})
to
(G^{\prime},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
in
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
is a homomorphism
\alpha:G\to G^{\prime}
such that
\alpha(s_{i}({\mathcal{F}}))=s_{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
. This shows that
Q
is well-defined as a functor in the obvious way, together with

Lemma 2.2
.

The functor
Q
is faithful and essentially surjective, identifying
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
as a subcategory of
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
that contains every object up to isomorphism. Moreover,
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
contains every isomorphism in
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
.

Proof.

The faithfulness and the fact that
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
contains every isomorphism in
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
follows from the description of Hom sets above. It only remains to show that
Q
is essentially surjective. Given
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{1})\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
, define
H_{i}=\ker(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k-i})
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
. Clearly,

(2.8)

{\mathcal{F}}=(H_{1}\leq\dots\leq H_{k-1}\leq G_{k})

forms an injective
k
-flag in
G_{k}
, and
Q((G_{k},{\mathcal{F}}))\simeq_{\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}}\mathbf{G}
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Our choice to work with
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
rather than (the presumably more natural choice)
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
is out of technical convenience. It is not hard to show directly (or by
[
SW22
, Proposition 6.18]
) that
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
is a diamond category. However, we will use
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.23]
to quickly verify that the moment function we will find on
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
is well-behaved (so their machinery works), and it is not immediate that this lemma is applicable to
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
. As is remarked right after
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.23]
, analogue of this lemma need not hold for categories that fit into the framework of
[
SW22
, Proposition 6.18]
but do not fit into the framework of
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.22]
.

From now on, we will interchangeably interpret an object in
\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
(equivalently, an object in
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
) as a surjective flag
\mathbf{G}
or a group together with an injective flag
(G,{\mathcal{F}})
, and write
\mathbf{G}=(G,{\mathcal{F}})
if
\mathbf{G}=Q((G,{\mathcal{F}}))
. However, we will use
\Hom_{{\mathcal{C}}_{P}}
and
\mathrm{Sur}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{P}}
to make clear that we are talking about morphisms (resp. epimorphisms) in the category
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
.

2.4.
From single prime to multiple primes

In order to draw results about random
p
-adic matrices for a single prime
p
and apply to our situation with multiple primes
p
, we need some simple observations in
[
SW22
]
about product categories. For two categories
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, let
C_{1}\times C_{2}
be the
product category
whose objects are ordered pairs of an object of
C_{1}
and an object of
C_{2}
and whose morphisms are ordered pairs of morphisms. By
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.16]
, if
C_{1},C_{2}
are diamond categories, then
C_{1}\times C_{2}
is a diamond category.

Lemma 2.3
.

As categories,
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}\simeq\prod_{p\in P}{\mathcal{C}}_{p}
.

Proof.

Recall that for any
p\in P
, we have a functor
(\cdot)_{p}:\mathbf{FinMod}_{P}\to\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
defined as
(\cdot)_{p}=(\cdot)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. Objects of
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
are injective
k
-flags of finite abelian
P
-groups

(2.9)

(G,\mathcal{F})=(H_{1}\leq\dots\leq H_{k-1}\leq G).

For
p\in P
, define

(2.10)

(G,\mathcal{F})_{p}=((H_{1})_{p}\leq\dots\leq(H_{k-1})_{p}\leq G_{p})

by taking
p
-Sylows on each step. Recall that a morphism
\alpha:(G,\mathcal{F})\to(G^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
in
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
is a morphism
\alpha:G\to G^{\prime}
of finite abelian
P
-groups such that
\alpha(s_{i}(\mathcal{F}))=\alpha(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
. For
p\in P
, define
\alpha_{p}:(G,\mathcal{F})_{p}\to(G^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})_{p}
by taking the
(\cdot)_{p}
functor on each step.

Define the functor
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}\to\prod_{p\in P}{\mathcal{C}}_{p}
object-wise by
(G,\mathcal{F})\mapsto((G,\mathcal{F})_{p})_{p\in P}
and morphism-wise by
\alpha\mapsto(\alpha_{p})_{p\in P}
. This is an equivalence of categories because given objects
(G,\mathcal{F})
and
(G^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
of
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}
, a morphism
\alpha:G\to G^{\prime}
satisfies
\alpha(s_{i}(\mathcal{F}))=\alpha(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
if and only if
\alpha_{p}(s_{i}(\mathcal{F})_{p})=\alpha_{p}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{p})
for all
p\in P
.
∎

The formalism of moments in
[
SW22
]
behaves nicely with respect to products of diamond categories.
By a
moment function
on any category
C
, we mean an assignment of a real number to each isomorphism class of objects in
C
. We denote a general moment function by
M=(M_{G})_{G\in C/\simeq}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{C/\simeq}
. A moment function
(M_{G})_{G}
is
nonnegative
if
M_{G}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
for all
G\in C/\simeq
. For a probability measure
\nu
on
C/\simeq
, the
moment function associated with
\nu
is the moment function
M^{\nu}=(M^{\nu}_{G})_{G}
with

(2.11)

M^{\nu}_{G}:=\int_{H\in C/\simeq}\left\lvert\mathrm{Epi}_{C}(H,G)\right\rvert\,d\nu.

The moment function associated with
\nu
exists if the integral defining
M^{\nu}_{G}
converges for all
G\in C/\simeq
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
C_{1},C_{2}
be diamond categories, and
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
be probability measures on
C_{1},C_{2}
respectively whose moment functions exist. Consider the product measure
\nu=\nu_{1}\times\nu_{2}
on the product category
C=C_{1}\times C_{2}
. Then the moment function associated with
\nu
exists, and

(2.12)

M^{\nu}_{(G_{1},G_{2})}=M^{\nu_{1}}_{G_{1}}M^{\nu_{2}}_{G_{2}},\quad\text{ for }G_{1}\in C_{1}/\simeq,\,G_{2}\in C_{2}/\simeq.

Proof.

Recall the standard fact that a morphism
(f_{1},f_{2})
in
C_{1}\times C_{2}
is an epimorphism if and only if both
f_{1},f_{2}
are epimorphisms. Then the result follows from Fubini’s theorem:

(2.13)

\displaystyle M^{\nu}_{(G_{1},G_{2})}

\displaystyle=\int_{(H_{1},H_{2})\in C_{1}\times C_{2}/\simeq}\left\lvert\mathrm{Epi}_{C_{1}\times C_{2}}((H_{1},H_{2}),(G_{1},G_{2}))\right\rvert\,d\nu_{1}d\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{(H_{1},H_{2})\in C_{1}\times C_{2}/\simeq}\left\lvert\mathrm{Epi}_{C_{1}}(H_{1},G_{1})\times\mathrm{Epi}_{C_{2}}(H_{2},G_{2})\right\rvert\,d\nu_{1}d\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{H_{1}\in C_{1}/\simeq}\left\lvert\mathrm{Epi}_{C_{1}}(H_{1},G_{1})\right\rvert\,d\nu_{1}\int_{H_{2}\in C_{2}/\simeq}\left\lvert\mathrm{Epi}_{C_{2}}(H_{2},G_{2})\right\rvert\,d\nu_{2}=M^{\nu_{1}}_{G_{1}}M^{\nu_{2}}_{G_{2}},

where each equality is in the sense that if the right-hand side converges then so does the left-hand side.
∎

For a diamond category
C
and a moment function
M
on
C
, we have the notion of
well-behavedness
defined in
[
SW22
, p. 4]
, a condition saying

does not grow too fast that is sufficient to guarantee that

is the moment function associated with a unique (signed) measure, among other niceness properties. For the reader’s benefit let us note that it will not matter what the actual definition is if one accepts the results in
[
SW22
]
, as we appeal to results of
[
SW22
]
to verify it and then use it as input to other results of
[
SW22
]
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.17]
)
.

Suppose a nonnegative moment function
M
on a product of diamond categories
C_{1}\times C_{2}
is
separable
, i.e., there are nonnegative moment functions
M^{1}
on
C_{1}
and
M^{2}
on
C_{2}
such that
M_{(G_{1},G_{2})}=M^{1}_{G_{1}}M^{2}_{G_{2}}
for all
(G_{1},G_{2})\in C_{1}\times C_{2}/\simeq
. If
M^{1}
and
M^{2}
are both well-behaved, then so is
M^{1}\times M^{2}
.

The final ingredient we need in order to apply the formalism of
[
SW22
]
is a statement that our desired limiting moment is well-behaved.

For any prime
p
, consider the moment function
\boldsymbol{1}
on

that assigns

to all
\mathbf{G}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{p}
. Then
\boldsymbol{1}
is well-behaved.

We will prove this later in
Section

.

.

For a finite set of primes

, consider the moment function
\boldsymbol{1}
on

that assigns

to all
\mathbf{G}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{P}

Viewing

as
\prod_{p\in P}{\mathcal{C}}_{p}
according to

, the moment function

is separable and is the product of the moment functions

3.
Proof of
Theorem

1.1
conditional on moment computations

The following theorem, which we will prove in
Sections

4
and
6
, gives the desired limiting moments.

Theorem 3.1
.

Fix a surjective
k
-flag
\mathbf{G}\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}
,
P
a finite set of primes, and
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. For each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, let
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
be independent
\alpha,P
-balanced random matrices. Then

(3.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})}[\lvert\mathrm{Sur}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{P}}(\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})_{P},\mathbf{G})\rvert]=1.

To motivate the results of
Proposition

2.6
and
Theorem

3.1
which we have just introduced, we show how they imply our main Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Viewing
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{k})_{P}
and
\mathbf{G}
as elements of
\mathcal{C}_{P}
by
Lemma

2.2
, we have by the same lemma that the event
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{k})_{P}\simeq_{\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}}\mathbf{G}
in
Theorem

1.1
is equivalent to
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{k})_{P}\simeq_{\mathcal{C}_{P}}\mathbf{G}
. So we work with
\mathcal{C}_{P}
from now on.

Now consider arbitrary random matrices
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem

1.1
. By
Proposition

2.6
, the moment function
\boldsymbol{1}_{P}:=\boldsymbol{1}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{P}}
on
\mathcal{C}_{P}
is well-behaved. By
Theorem

3.1
, the moment of
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{k})_{P}
converges to
\boldsymbol{1}_{P}
. Hence
[
SW22
, Theorem 1.8]
implies that the following are true:

(1)

There is a unique probability measure
\nu_{P}
on
\mathcal{C}_{P}
with moment
\boldsymbol{1}_{P}
.

(2)

\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{k})_{P}
converges in distribution to
\nu_{P}
as
n\to\infty
.

Hence, it suffices to show that the measure
\nu_{P}
associated with moment
\boldsymbol{1}_{P}
is given by

(3.2)

\nu_{P}(\mathbf{G})=\frac{1}{\lvert\Aut_{\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}}(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\left\lparen\prod_{p\in P}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-p^{-i})\right\rparen^{k}.

While computing the distribution given the moment is a difficult task in general, we are able to avoid this task by using the result of
[
Hua24
]
on Haar random matrices over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, as we will do for the rest of the proof. The discussion above applied to the special case
P=\{p\}
gives rise to a unique probability measure
\nu_{p}
on
{\mathcal{C}}_{p}
with moment
\boldsymbol{1}_{p}
. Since
\boldsymbol{1}_{P}=\prod_{p\in P}\boldsymbol{1}_{p}
, by
Lemma

2.4
the moment associated with the product measure
\prod_{p\in P}\nu_{p}
on
{\mathcal{C}}_{P}/\simeq
is
\boldsymbol{1}_{P}
. By the uniqueness statement defining
\nu_{P}
, we conclude
\nu_{P}=\prod_{p\in P}\nu_{p}
, or in other words

(3.3)

\nu_{P}(\mathbf{G})=\prod_{p\in P}\nu_{p}(\mathbf{G}_{p})\text{ for }\mathbf{G}\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}.

Since
\Aut_{\mathbf{Fl}_{k,P}}(\mathbf{G})=\prod_{p\in P}\Aut_{\mathbf{Fl}_{k,p}}(\mathbf{G}_{p})
, to prove (
3.2
) it suffices to prove the special case
P=\{p\}
, which we do next.

Let
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{1})\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k,p}
be fixed. We note from the uniqueness part of statement (1) applied to the case
P=\{p\}
that the measure
\nu_{p}
does not depend on the random matrices
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
. In particular, let
N
be an integer such that
p^{N-1}G_{k}=0
, and let
M_{1}^{(N)},\ldots,M_{k}^{(N)}
be iid matrices with iid entries distributed by the uniform measure on
\{0,1,\dots,p^{N}-1\}
, then statement (2) applied to this special case yields

(3.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{cok}(M_{1}^{(N)},\ldots,M_{k}^{(N)})_{p}\simeq\mathbf{G})=\nu_{p}(\mathbf{G}).

We claim that the left-hand side is unchanged if we replace
M_{i}^{(N)}
by iid matrices
M_{i}^{\prime}
with iid entries distributed by the Haar measure on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. Indeed, since
p^{N-1}G_{k}=0
, the two events below for a random flag
\mathbf{G^{\prime}}
of abelian
p
-groups are the same:

(3.5)

\left\{\mathbf{G^{\prime}}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{N}{\mathbb{Z}}\simeq\mathbf{G}\right\}=\left\{\mathbf{G^{\prime}}\simeq\mathbf{G}\right\}.

Since
M_{i}^{(N)}
and
M_{i}^{\prime}
are identically distributed modulo
p^{N}
, and taking cokernels commute with taking tensor products, it follows that

(3.6)

{\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{cok}(M_{1}^{(N)},\ldots,M_{k}^{(N)})_{p}\simeq\mathbf{G})={\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{cok}(M_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,M_{k}^{\prime})_{p}\simeq\mathbf{G}).

On the other hand,
[
Hua24
, Theorem 1.1]
states that

(3.7)

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{P}}(\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,M_{k}^{\prime})\simeq\mathbf{G})=\frac{1}{\lvert\Aut_{\mathbf{Fl}_{k,p}}(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-p^{-i})\right)^{k}.

Combining the equalities, we get

(3.8)

\nu_{p}(\mathbf{G})=\frac{1}{\lvert\Aut_{\mathbf{Fl}_{k,p}}(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-p^{-i})\right)^{k},

and the proof is complete.
∎

4.
Reducing
Theorem

3.1
to spans over a finite ring

In this section, we convert the moment computation problem in Theorem
3.1
into a more explicit form,
Proposition

4.1
, which is what we will prove subsequently.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and
R={\mathbb{Z}}/a{\mathbb{Z}}
. Given real
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, we say an
R
-valued random variable
\xi
is
\alpha
-balanced
if for every prime
p|a
we have

(4.1)

\max_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbf{P}}(\xi\equiv r\pmod{p})\leq 1-\alpha.

We say that a random matrix with
R
-valued entries is
\alpha
-balanced for some
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
if the entries are independent and
\alpha
-balanced (but not necessarily iid).

Note that for
P=\{p\text{ prime}:p\mid a\}
, trivially a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued random variable
\xi
is
(\alpha,P)
-balanced if and only if the
R
-valued random variable
\xi\pmod{a}
is
\alpha
-balanced.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
,
R={\mathbb{Z}}/a{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
. For each
n
, let
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
be independent and
\alpha
-balanced random matrices in
\Mat_{n}(R)
. Let
G
be an
R
-module (i.e., finite
a
-torsion abelian group), and
0=H_{0}\leq H_{1}\leq\dots\leq H_{k-1}\leq H_{k}=G
be an injective flag of subgroups. Then

(4.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{F\in G^{n}}{\mathbf{P}}\Big(\mathrm{span}(M_{i}\cdots M_{k}F)=H_{i-1}\text{ for all $1\leq i\leq k+1$}\Big)=1.

Here, we sum over all column vectors
F
with entries in
G
, and the span of a column vector in
G^{n}
means the subgroup of

&=\frac{1}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}+O\left(\frac{\exp(-cn)}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}\right)+O\left(\frac{\exp(-\alpha n/4)}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}\right),\end{split}

completing the estimates for codes.

Non-codes.
We next prove (ii) by working with
F_{k}
of
\delta
-depth
D_{k}\geq 2
, where
D_{k}
also divides
|H_{k}|=\lvert G\rvert
. Similarly to the previous part, we again compute the probability that
M_{k}F_{k}
spans
H_{k-1}
in the two possible ways:

(1)

M_{k}F_{k}=F_{k-1}
, where
F_{k-1}
is a given code of distance
\delta n
in
H_{k-1}
;

(2)

M_{k}F_{k}=F_{k-1}
, where the given
F_{k-1}
is not a code of distance
\delta n
, and hence has
\delta
-depth
D_{k-1}\geq 2
in
H_{k-1}
.

For the first case, by Lemma
5.6
, the probability with respect to
M_{k}
is bounded by
K\exp(-\alpha n)\frac{D_{k}^{n}}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}
.

Hence, by induction and by the independence of
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{k-1}\in H_{k-1}^{n}\\
\text{$F_{k-1}$ is code of distance $\delta n$}\end{subarray}}{\mathbf{P}}(M_{k}F_{k}=F_{k-1})\sum_{F_{k-2}\in H_{k-2}^{n},\dots,F_{1}\in H_{1}^{n},\mathrm{span}(F_{i})=H_{i}}{\mathbf{P}}(M_{k-1}F_{k-1}=F_{k-2},\dots,M_{1}F_{1}=0)

\displaystyle=|{\mathcal{C}}(H_{k-1})|K\exp(-\alpha n)\frac{D_{k}^{n}}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}(\frac{1+K\exp(-cn)}{|H_{k-1}|^{n}})

\displaystyle=O\left(\exp(-\alpha n/2)\frac{D_{k}^{n}}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}\right).

For the second case (2), the probability with respect to
M_{k}
, by Lemma
5.4
and Lemma
5.6
, is bounded by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{k-1}\in H_{k-1}^{n}\\
\text{$F_{k-1}$ has $\delta$-depth $D_{k-1}$}\end{subarray}}{\mathbf{P}}(M_{k}F_{k}=F_{k-1})\leq K\binom{n}{\lceil\ell(D_{k-1})\delta n\rceil-1}|H_{k-1}|^{n}D_{k-1}^{-n+\ell(D_{k-1})\delta n}\times K^{\prime}\exp(-\alpha n)\frac{D_{k}^{n}}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}.

Hence, by induction (applied to
M_{1}\cdots M_{k-1}
with the starting vector
F_{k-1}
)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{k-1}\in H_{k-1}^{n}\\
\text{$F_{k-1}$ has $\delta$-depth $D_{k-1}$}\end{subarray}}{\mathbf{P}}(M_{k}F_{k}=F_{k-1})\sum_{F_{k-2}\in H_{k-2}^{n},\dots,F_{1}\in H_{1}^{n},\mathrm{span}(F_{i})=H_{i}}{\mathbf{P}}(M_{k-1}F_{k-1}=F_{k-2},\dots,M_{1}F_{1}=0)

\displaystyle\leq K\binom{n}{\lceil\ell(D_{k-1})\delta n\rceil-1}|H_{k-1}|^{n}D_{k-1}^{-n+\ell(D_{k-1})\delta n}\times K^{\prime}\exp(-\alpha n)\frac{D_{k}^{n}}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}\times K\exp(-\alpha n/2)\frac{D_{k-1}^{n}}{|H_{k-1}|^{n}}

\displaystyle=O\left(\exp(-\alpha n/2)\frac{D_{k}^{n}}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}\right),

provided that
\delta
was chosen sufficiently small and
n
is sufficiently large.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

4.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{k}\in H_{k}^{n},\dots,F_{1}\in H_{1}^{n},\mathrm{span}(F_{i})=H_{i}}{\mathbf{P}}(M_{k}F_{k}=F_{k-1},\dots,M_{1}F_{1}=0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{k}\in H_{k}^{n}\\
\text{$F_{k}$ is code of distance $\delta n$}\end{subarray}}\sum_{F_{k-1}\in H_{k-1}^{n},\dots,F_{1}\in H_{1}^{n},\mathrm{span}(F_{i})=H_{i}}{\mathbf{P}}(M_{k}F_{k}=F_{k-1},\dots,M_{1}F_{1}=0)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D_{k}\geq 2\\
D_{k}\big||H_{k}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{k}\in H_{k}^{n}\\
\text{$F_{k}$ has $\delta$-depth $D_{k}$}\end{subarray}}\sum_{F_{k-1}\in H_{k-1}^{n},\dots,F_{1}\in H_{1}^{n},\mathrm{span}(F_{i})=H_{i}}{\mathbf{P}}(M_{k}F_{k}=F_{k-1},\dots,M_{1}F_{1}=0)

\displaystyle=S_{1}+S_{2}.

The first sum, by Claim
5.2
and by (i) of Proposition
6.1
, can be estimated as

S_{1}=|{\mathcal{C}}(G)|\frac{1}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}(1+K\exp(-cn))=1+O(\exp-cn)+O(\exp-c_{\delta}^{\prime}n).

The second sum, by Lemma
5.4
and by (i) of Proposition
6.1
, can be estimated as

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D_{k}\geq 2\\
D_{k}\big|\lvert G\rvert\end{subarray}}K\binom{n}{\lceil\ell(D_{k})\delta n\rceil-1}\lvert G\rvert^{n}D_{k}^{-n+\ell(D_{k})\delta n}\times K\exp(-\alpha n/2)\frac{D_{k}^{n}}{\lvert G\rvert^{n}}

\displaystyle=O(\exp(-\alpha n/4)),

provided that
\delta
was chosen sufficiently small and
n
is sufficiently large.
∎

Remark 6.1

By using more explicit bounds and keeping track of the errors more carefully after each step of induction (see for instance

\sum_{F\in G^{n}}{\mathbf{P}}_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}\Big(\mathrm{span}(M_{i}\cdots M_{k}F)=H_{i-1}\text{ for all $1\leq i\leq k+1$}\Big)=1+o_{n}(1)

of

seems to hold even when

and
\lvert G\rvert=|H_{k}|\leq e^{n^{c}}
for some sufficiently small constant

. However, within the setting of

with

, while (

) seems surprising, Theorem
1.1
is rather vacuous.

Remark 6.2

For fixed

instead of those from Section 5 above, it seems possible that our results (Propositions

7.
Verifying well-behavedness

Here we prove Proposition
2.6
. We first apply
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.23]
to reduce it to a well-behavedness statement on the category of finite abelian
p
-groups.

Lemma 7.1
.

For an abelian
p
-group
G
, let
n_{k}(G)
be the number of injective
k
-flags in
G
, c.f.
[
NVP24b
, Def. 6]
. Then the moment function
\boldsymbol{1}
is well-behaved on
{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
G\mapsto n_{k}(G)
is well-behaved on
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
.

Proof.

Recall that
{\mathcal{C}}=(\mathbf{FinMod}_{p},\mathcal{F}l_{k})
. By
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.23]
,
\boldsymbol{1}
is well-behaved on
{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if the moment function

(7.1)

G\mapsto\sum_{{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathcal{F}l_{k}(G)}\boldsymbol{1}_{(G,{\mathcal{F}})}

is well-behaved on
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
. But the sum is precisely
\lvert\mathcal{F}l_{k}(G)\rvert=n_{k}(G)
.
∎

We will use an upper bound of
n_{k}(G)
from
[
NVP24b
]
. Given an integer partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{l})
, define

(7.2)

G_{\lambda}=\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{p^{\lambda_{1}}{\mathbb{Z}}}\oplus\cdots\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{p^{\lambda_{l}}{\mathbb{Z}}}.

By the classification of modules over PID, for each
G\in\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
, there is a unique partition
\lambda
, called the
type
of
G
, such that
G\simeq G_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 7.2

(
[
NVP24b
, Lemma 8.5]
)
.

Given a prime
p
and an integer
k\geq 1
, there are constants
f_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
0<c_{k}<1
depending only on
k
, such that

(7.3)

n_{k}(G_{\lambda})\leq p^{f_{k}\lambda_{1}+\frac{1-c_{k}}{2}\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}^{\prime 2}},

for any partition
\lambda
, where
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}:=\lvert\{j:\lambda_{j}\geq i\}\rvert
is the
i
-part of the conjugate partition of
\lambda
.

We will use this upper bound to show that
n_{k}(\cdot)
is well-behaved on
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
. Unfortunately, neither of the two general-purpose results
[
SW22
, Corollary 6.5]
nor
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.9]
applies directly:
[
SW22
, Corollary 6.5]
does not allow the “quadratic growth”
\sum\lambda_{i}^{\prime 2}
in the exponent, while
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.9]
does not allow the extra “linear growth”
f_{k}\lambda_{1}
in the exponent. To this end, we will prove a slightly stronger version of
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.9]
for our current setting as Lemma
7.4
below. For a quick glance, it essentially says that the constraint on the coefficient of the linear growth is only relevant when the coefficient of the quadratic growth is the maximal allowed (namely, the quadratic part grows like
\sum\lambda_{i}^{\prime 2}/2
). We will state Lemma
7.4
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
for simplicity, but the same holds if
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is replaced by any discrete valuation ring with finite residue field.

For
G\in\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
, let
\wedge^{2}G
denote the quotient of the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-module
G\otimes G
by the submodule generated by elements of the form
g\otimes g
for
g\in G
. Recall for any partition
\lambda
that
\lvert G_{\lambda}\rvert=p^{\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}}
and (see e.g.
[
Woo17
, p. 925]
)

(7.4)

\lvert\wedge^{2}G_{\lambda}\rvert=p^{\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-1)}{2}}.

For
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, let
\mathbf{FinMod}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the full subcategory of
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
consisting of all
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules, or equivalently, all
G_{\lambda}
with
\lambda_{1}\leq s
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
N=(N_{G})_{G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}/\simeq}
be a moment function on
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
. Suppose for each
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, there exist
\varepsilon,c>0
(possibly depending on
s
) such that
\lvert N_{G}\rvert\leq c\lvert G\rvert^{1-\varepsilon}\lvert\wedge^{2}G\rvert
for all
G\in\mathbf{FinMod}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}}
. Then
N
is well-behaved on
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
.

Proof.

As is in the proof of
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.9]
, it suffices to prove for each fixed
G\in\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
that

(7.5)

\frac{1}{\lvert\Aut(G)\rvert}\sum_{e=0}^{\infty}\left\lparen\frac{1}{\lvert\Hom(G,{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\rvert^{e}\prod_{j=1}^{e}(p^{j}-1)}\right\rparen\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(N,({\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{e})}\lvert N_{G_{\alpha}}\rvert<\infty,

where
0\to G\to G_{\alpha}\to({\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{e}
is the extension determined by
\alpha
. Now let
s=\lambda_{1}+1
, where
\lambda
is the type of
G
. Then the extension sequence implies that
G_{\alpha}\in\mathbf{FinMod}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}}
for every
\alpha
. Choose
\varepsilon,c>0
based on this
s
, then the sum in (
7.5
) is bounded above by

(7.6)

\frac{1}{\lvert\Aut(G)\rvert}\sum_{e=0}^{\infty}\left\lparen\frac{1}{\lvert\Hom(G,{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\rvert^{e}\prod_{j=1}^{e}(p^{j}-1)}\right\rparen\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(N,({\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{e})}c\lvert G\rvert^{1-\varepsilon}\lvert\wedge^{2}G\rvert,

which is shown to be finite in the proof of
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.9]
.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
N=(N_{G})_{G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}/\simeq}
be a moment function on
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
. Suppose there exist
\varepsilon,r,c>0
such that
\lvert N_{G}\rvert\leq c\lvert G\rvert^{r}\lvert\wedge^{2}G\rvert^{1-\varepsilon}
for all
G\in\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
. Then
N
is well-behaved on
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and consider all partitions
\lambda
with
\lambda_{1}\leq s
, so that
G_{\lambda}\in\mathbf{FinMod}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=0
for
i>s
. Cauchy–Schwarz inequality implies that

(7.7)

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}\leq\sqrt{s}\left\lparen\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}^{\prime 2}\right\rparen^{1/2}.

Since there are only finitely many integer-valued vectors
(\lambda_{i}^{\prime})_{i=1}^{s}
with bounded
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}^{\prime 2}
, the above estimate implies that for every
f\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there exists
C
depending on
s
and
f
such that

(7.8)

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}^{\prime 2}-f\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}\geq-C

for all partitions
\lambda
with
\lambda_{1}\leq s
. Combined with (
7.4
), we may absorb
\lvert G\rvert^{r}
into
\lvert\wedge^{2}G\rvert^{-\varepsilon}
to conclude that there exists
c^{\prime}
depending on
s
such that

(7.9)

\lvert N_{G}\rvert\leq c^{\prime}\lvert\wedge^{2}G\rvert

for all
G\in\mathbf{FinMod}_{{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}}
. Since
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
is arbitrary, the conclusion follows from Lemma
7.3
.
∎

Remark 7.1
.

(1)

By
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.9]
and the remark thereafter,
N_{G}=\lvert G\rvert^{r}\lvert\wedge^{2}G\rvert
is not well-behaved on
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
if and only if
r\geq 1
. Note that this does not contradict Lemma
7.4
.

(2)

While
[
SW22
, Corollary 6.5]
is not a special case of
[
SW22
, Lemma 6.9]
, it is now a special case of both Lemma
7.3
and Lemma
7.4
.

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

In (
7.3
), note that
\lambda_{1}\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
and the coefficient
\frac{1-c_{k}}{2}
in front ot
\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}^{\prime 2}
is strictly less than
1/2
. Comparing this coefficient with (
7.4
), bounding the factors involving
p^{\sum\lambda_{i}^{\prime}}
from above by
\lvert G\rvert^{r}
for some large
r
, and applying Lemma
7.4
, it follows that
G\mapsto n_{k}(G)
is well-behaved on
\mathbf{FinMod}_{p}
. By Lemma
7.1
, we conclude that
\boldsymbol{1}
is well-behaved on
{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}

Proof of

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{P}}\Big(\corank(M_{1}M_{2})=c\mid\corank(M_{1})=a\text{ and }\corank(M_{2})=b\Big)

, but rather than compute this sum we will simply notice that it suffices to compute the limit directly in the case where

are uniformly distributed over

on both the right and left, and there is a unique distribution supported on

Appendix A
Symmetric polynomial formulas and parallelism

This Appendix elaborates on the discussion in the Introduction regarding structural connections with eigenvalues/singular values of complex matrix products. It is still relatively terse and impressionistic, but includes references to longer treatments.

A.1.
Additive and multiplicative free convolution

We first give a very brief overview of free convolutions, we refer the reader to
[
AGZ
,
NS06
]
for more details. Let
\mu
and
\nu
be probability measures on
{\mathbb{R}}
with compact support. If
a,b
are two freely independent random variables in a noncommutative probability space
({\mathcal{A}},\phi)
with law
\mu,\nu
respectively, then the laws of
a+b
and
ab
are defined by
\mu\boxplus\nu
and
\mu\boxtimes\nu
respectively. We next give an analytical description of the convolutions. Consider the Cauchy transform of
\mu
,

G_{\mu}(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{d\mu(t)}{z-t}.

As
\mu
has compact support, we can express it as formal series about
z=\infty

G_{\mu}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}m_{n}z^{-n-1}=\frac{1}{z}M_{\mu}(\frac{1}{z}),

where
m_{n}
are the moments of
\mu
and
M_{\mu}(z)
is the moment generating function.

Let
K_{\mu}(z)
be the formal inverse of
G_{\mu}
, that is
G_{\mu}(K_{\mu}(z))=z
. Then
K_{\mu}(z)
can be written as a formal series

K_{\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{n}z^{n}.

Define the
R
-transform as

R_{\mu}(z):=K_{\mu}(z)-1/z.

Alternatively, we can define
R_{\mu}(z)
as
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\kappa_{n+1}z^{n}
, where
\kappa_{n}
are the free cumulants of
\mu
. Then
\mu\boxplus\nu
is the measure whose
{\mathbb{R}}
-transform satisfies

R_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(z)=R_{\mu}(z)+R_{\nu}(z).

For the multiplicative free convolution, define the
S
-transform of
\mu
by

S_{\mu}(z):=\frac{1+z}{z}M_{\mu}^{-1}(z),

where
M_{\mu}^{-1}(.)
is the compositional inverse. Then
\mu\boxtimes\nu
is the measure whose
S
-transform satisfies

S_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(z)=S_{\mu}(z)S_{\nu}(z).

A.2.
Symmetric polynomials and functions

The treatment here follows
[
GM20
]
and
[
VP21
]
. The Macdonald polynomials
P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,t)
are symmetric polynomials associated to integer partitions
\lambda
of length at most
n
, which is an
n
-tuple of non-negative integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}),\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}
. These are usually defined as eigenfunctions of some differential operator on the algebra
{\mathbb{Q}}(q,t)[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{S_{n}}
, and span the
{\mathbb{Q}}(q,t)
-vector space of such polynomials. More specifically, let
\Lambda_{n}
be the algebra of symmetric polynomials in
n
variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Define a difference operator
D_{q,t}
acting in
\Lambda_{n}
as follows

D_{q,t}f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod\frac{tx_{i}-x_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}T_{q,x_{i}}f,

where
(T_{q,x_{i}}f)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},qx_{i},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n})
.

The operator
D_{q,t}
has a complete set of eigenfunctions in
\Lambda_{n}
, which are the Macdonald polynomials
P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,t)
, where
D_{q,t}P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};q,t)=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}q^{\lambda_{i}}t^{n-i})P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};q,t)
.
We also define the normalized polynomial

(A.1)

\hat{P}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};q,t):=\frac{P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};q,t)}{P_{\lambda}(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1};q,t)}.

We list below a few special cases.

A.2.1.
Schur polynomials.

This is the Macdonald polynomial
P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};q,q)
(that is
t=q
), which can be shown to have the following determinantal form independent of
q
:

P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};q,q)=s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\frac{\det[x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+n-j}]_{i,j=1}^{n}}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})}.

A.2.2.
Hall-Littlewood polynomials

It can be shown that when setting
q=0
, one has explicit formulas:

P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};0,t)=\frac{1}{v_{\lambda}(t)}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\sigma\left(x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{x_{i}-tx_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right),\mbox{ where }v_{\lambda}(t)=\prod_{i\geq 0}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{i}(\lambda)}\frac{1-t^{j}}{1-t}.

This case is known as a
Hall-Littlewood polynomial
, and relevant in combinatorics of abelian
p
-groups,
p
-adic groups, and
p
-adic random matrices (all of which are related).

A.2.3.
The Heckman–Opdam hypergeometric functions and multivariate Bessel functions

Let
\alpha_{1}>\dots>\alpha_{n}
and
\theta>0
. Define the (type A) Heckman-Opdam hypergeometric function by

{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n};\theta):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\theta n(n-1)/2}P_{\lfloor\alpha_{1}/\varepsilon\rfloor,\dots,\lfloor\alpha_{n}/\varepsilon\rfloor}(e^{\varepsilon z_{1}},\dots,e^{\varepsilon z_{n}};e^{-\varepsilon},e^{-\theta\varepsilon}).

Let

\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\alpha}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};\theta):=\frac{{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};\theta)}{{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(0,-\theta,\dots,(1-n)\theta;\theta)}.

A further limit

B_{\alpha}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};\theta):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon\alpha}(\varepsilon^{-1}z_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon^{-1}z_{n};\theta)

is referred to as the multivariate Bessel function.

A.2.4.
The coefficients

We can express
P_{\lambda}P_{\mu}
as a linear combination of the
P_{\nu}
,

P_{\lambda}P_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu}c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(q,t)P_{\nu}

for some coefficients
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(q,t)
(strictly speaking, in our definition we have fixed
n
and these coefficients
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(q,t)
depend on
n
, but they stabilize and take the same value for all
n
large enough). The coefficients
\hat{c}_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(q,t)
can be defined by
\hat{P}_{\lambda}\hat{P}_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu}\hat{c}_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(q,t)\hat{P}_{\nu}
, or written explicitly as

(A.2)

\hat{c}_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(q,t)=\frac{P_{\nu}(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1};q,t)}{P_{\lambda}(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1};q,t)P_{\mu}(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1};q,t)}c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(q,t).

These depend implicitly on
n
, and converge as
n\to\infty
to finite limits, though they do not stabilize as do their un-normalized counterparts.

In parallel, for multivariate Bessel functions and Heckman–Opdam hypergeometric functions,

\hat{B}_{\alpha}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};\theta)\hat{B}_{\beta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};\theta)=\int_{\gamma}b_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(\theta)\hat{B}_{\gamma}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};\theta)d\gamma_{Leb}

and

\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\alpha}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};\theta)\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\beta}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};\theta)=\int_{\gamma}c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(\theta)\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\gamma}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n};\theta)d\gamma_{Leb}.

These
b_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}
and
c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}
are also limits of the Macdonald structure constants
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(q,t)
.

A.3.
The convolutions, again

The following was shown in
[
GM20

, which are the measures mentioned in Subsection
1.3

Theorem A.1
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})

d(\delta_{\alpha}\boxplus_{2}\delta_{\beta})=b_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(1)d\gamma_{Leb},

d(\delta_{\alpha}\boxtimes_{2}\delta_{\beta})=c_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(1)d\gamma_{Leb}.

The following was shown in

Theorem A.2
.

Let

{\mathbf{P}}\left(\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}M_{2})\simeq G_{\nu}\mid\mathrm{cok}(M_{1})\simeq G_{\lambda},\mathrm{cok}(M_{2})\simeq G_{\mu}\right)=\hat{c}_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(0,1/p)=:(\delta_{\lambda}\boxtimes_{HL,n}\delta_{\mu})(G_{\nu}).

In other words, both singular values/eigenvalues of complex matrices and cokernels of

A.4.
Asymptotic description of Hall-Littlewood convolution

Recall that the normalized structure constants
\hat{c}_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}(q,t)
implicitly depend on
n

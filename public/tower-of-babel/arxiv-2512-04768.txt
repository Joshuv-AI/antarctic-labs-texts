Bohr-Rogosinski Inequalities for bounded analytic functions
Molla Basir Ahamed, Partha Pratim Roy

Abstract
. In this article, we analyze refined and improved versions of the classical Bohr inequality for the function class ℬ \mathcal{B} , which consists of analytic self- mappings defined on the unit disk 𝔻 \mathbb{D} . We improve the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality for the class ℬ \mathcal{B} of analytic self-maps by incorporating the area measure of sub-disks 𝔻 r \mathbb{D}_{r} in 𝔻 \mathbb{D} .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
denote the open unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
. Harald Bohr in
1914
(see
[
22
]
) proved that if
H_{\infty}
denotes the class of all bounded analytic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
, then the following inequality holds

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{0}(f,r):=|a_{0}|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq||f||_{\infty}:=\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;0\leq r\leq\frac{1}{3},

where
a_{k}=f^{(k)}(0)/k!
for
k\geq 0
. The constant
1/3
is called the Bohr radius and the inequality in (
1.1
) is called Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{B}
of analytic self-maps on
\mathbb{D}
. Henceforth, if there exists a positive real number
r_{0}
such that an inequality of the form (
1.1
) holds for every elements of a class
\mathcal{M}
for
0\leq r\leq r_{0}
and fails when
r>r_{0}
, then we shall say that
r_{0}
is an sharp bound for
r
in the inequality w.r.t. to class
\mathcal{M}
.

The constant
1/3
and the inequality (
1.1
) are called respectively, the Bohr radius and the Bohr’s inequality, for the class
\mathcal{B}
. Moreover, for
f_{a}(z):={(a-z)}/{(1-az)},
where
a\in[0,1)
, it follows easily that
\mathcal{B}_{0}(f_{a},r)>||f||_{\infty}
if, and only if,
r>1/(1+2a)
, which shows that the constant
1/3
is best possible as the limiting case
a\rightarrow 1^{-}
suggests. Several other proofs of this interesting inequality were given in different articles (see, e.g.
[
46
,
57
,
56
]
).

In the majorant series
\mathcal{B}_{0}(f,r)
of the function
f\in\mathcal{B}
, the beginning terms play some significant in the related discussion about the Bohr inequality. For instance, in the case of
|a_{0}|=0
, Tomic
[
57
]
has proved the inequality (
1.1
) for
0\leq r\leq{1}/{2}
and if the term

is replaced by

, then the constant
1/3
can be replaced by
1/2
. Moreover, if the term
|a_{0}|
in
\mathcal{B}_{0}(f,r)
is replaced by
|f(z)|
, then the constant
1/3
can be replaced by sharp constant
\sqrt{5}-2
established in
[
37
]
(see also
[
35
]
).

Bohr’s theorem received greater interest after it was used by Dixon

1.1.
Basic Notations

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\{f\in H_{\infty}\;:\;||f||_{\infty}\leq 1\}.

Also, for
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
and
f_{0}(z):=f(z)-f(0)
, we let for convenience

\displaystyle B_{k}(f,r):=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\;\mbox{for }k\geq 0,\;||f_{0}||^{2}_{r}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\;\mbox{and}\;||f_{1}||^{2}_{r}:=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}

Further, we define the quantity
A(f_{0},r)
by

\displaystyle A(f_{0},r):=\left(\dfrac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\dfrac{r}{1-r}\right)||f_{0}||^{2}_{r}.

The concept of the Rogosinski radius, like that of the Bohr radius, was introduced in
[
54
]
. However, the Rogosinski radius has not been studied as extensively as the Bohr radius (see
[
34
,
55
]
). The definition of the Rogosinski radius is provided below.

Theorem A
.

(Rogosinski Theorem)
If
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B},
then for every
N\geq 1
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}b_{n}z^{n}\bigg|\leq 1\;\mbox{for}\;|z|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

The radius
1/2
is best possible and is called Rogosinski radius.

Inspired by the articles of
[
37
]
and
[
35
]
, the Bohr-Rogosinski sum,
R_{n}^{f}(z)
, for a function
f\in\mathcal{B}
with Taylor series
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, is defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle R^{f}_{n}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n},\;|z|=r.

An interesting observation is that for
N=1
, the quantity defined in
(
1.3
)
is related to the classical Bohr sum where
|f(0)|
is replaced by
|f(z)|
. The inequality
R_{n}^{f}(z)\leq 1
is known as the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality. If
B
and
R
denote the Bohr radius and the Bohr–Rogosinski radius, respectively, it is easy to see that
B=1/3<1/2=R
. For recent developments concerning the Bohr-Rogosinski phenomenon, the reader is referred to the articles by Das
[
23
]
, Kayumov
et al.

[
35
]
, and the references cited therein.

It is worth mentioning that various questions concerning the related new concept, the Bohr-Rogosinski phenomenon, including its refined forms, are currently being studied (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
29
,
43
]
). However, to the best of our knowledge, unlike the refined versions of the Bohr inequality for different classes of functions, there is no strengthened version of the Rogosinski inequality
(
1.2
)
that holds for
r\leq 1/2
and for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Motivated by this gap, we refine the Rogosinski inequality for the classes of functions
\mathcal{B}
and the subordination class. We also improve a certain refined Rogosinski inequality in terms of the quantity
S_{r}
, which is the planar integral of an analytic function defined on
\mathbb{D}
. Consequently, in this article, the Bohr–Rogosinski inequality is considered to be analogous to a Bohr-type inequality, which is a variant of the inequality found in
(
1.1
)
or
(
1.3
)
.

For a function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, we define
f_{0}(z):=f(z)-f(0)
for convenience. Throughout this paper, we will use the following notations

\displaystyle{||f_{0}||}^{2}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;B_{N}(f,r):=\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Using the foundation of the Rogosinski inequality and the Rogosinski radius (first studied in
[
54
]
), Kayumov
et al.

[
35
]
(and
[
37
]
) obtained the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality and Bohr-Rogosinski radius for the class
\mathcal{B}
.

Theorem B
.

[
35
, Corollary 1]

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
. Then for
N\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle|f(z)|+B_{N}(f,r)\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq R_{N},

where
R_{N}
is the positive root of the equation
2(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r)^{2}=0
. The radius
R_{N}
is the best possible. Moreover,

\displaystyle|f(z)|^{2}+B_{N}(f,r)\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq R^{\prime}_{N},

where
R^{\prime}_{N}
is the positive root of the equation
(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r)^{2}=0
. The radius
R^{\prime}_{N}
is the best possible.

Following the initiation of work by Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
31
]
, Liu et al.
[

considered several Bohr-type inequalities for the family
\mathcal{B}
, where the Taylor coefficients of the classical Bohr inequality were partly or completely replaced by higher-order derivatives of

Theorem C
.

[
42
, Theorem 2.1]

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. Then the following sharp inequality holds:

\displaystyle|f(z)|+|f^{\prime}(z)|r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq\frac{\sqrt{17}-3}{4}.

In

, Liu et al.
[

]
presented an improved version of the above theorem.

Theorem D
.

[
43
, Theorem 7]

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle|f(z)|+|f^{\prime}(z)|r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+A(f_{0},r)\leq 1

for

and the constant

is best possible.

In a recent work, Ponnusamy and Vijayakumar
[

presented an extension of Theorem B that applies to harmonic quasiconformal maps, under which Theorem B and Corollary A are obtained as special cases.

Extensive investigations have been carried out on Theorem B and Corollary A to derive their generalized or strengthened versions for different classes of functions. (and its integral powers) and its analogues for certain classes of harmonic mappings or operator-valued functions. A number of results in this area have been established in the last couple of years following the publication of the paper by Kayumov and Ponnusamy

1.2.
Improved Bohr inequalities for the class
\mathcal{B}
.

Let
f
be holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
, and for
0<r<1
, let
\mathbb{D}_{r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}
.
Throughout the paper,
S_{r}=S_{r}(f)
denotes the planar integral

\displaystyle S_{r}=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z).

Note that if
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then
S_{r}=\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}.
If
f
is a univalent function, then
S_{r}
is the area of
f(\mathbb{D}_{r})
.

In
2018
, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
36
]
obtained the following result, which is an improved version of the Bohr inequality derived using the sharp bounds of the quantity
S_{r}
. Let us now recall a couple of recent results for our reference.

Theorem E
.

[
36
, Theorem 1]

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}.
Then

(i)

B_{0}(f,r)+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\mbox{for}\;r\leq\frac{1}{3},

and the numbers
1/3
,
16/9
cannot be improved. Moreover,

(ii)

|a_{0}|^{2}+B_{1}(f,r)+\frac{9}{8}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\mbox{for}\;r\leq\frac{1}{2},
and the numbers
1/2
,
9/8
cannot be improved.

In
2020
, Ismagilov
et al.

[
33
]
continued investigating on Theorem
Theorem E
further and proved the following sharp results in view of addition of two degree polynomial expression of the quantity
S_{r}/\pi
with some suitable coefficients.

Theorem F
.

[
33
, Theorem 1, Theorem 2]

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
. Then

(i)

where

Subsequently, Huang

[

presented a further improvement of the inequality (i) of Theorem F by considering the addition of the square of

, which yielded the following sharp inequality.

and proved that the equality can be achieved for the function

We now turn our attention to a different setting of the quantity

. Recently, Ismagilov

[

\displaystyle\dfrac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\leq\dfrac{r^{2}(1-|a_{0}|^{2})^{2}}{(1-r^{2})(1-r^{2}|a_{0}|^{4})}.

It will be an interesting question to find sharp improved Bohr-type inequalities or Bohr-Rogosinski inequalities in terms of the addition of the quantity
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
. However, with the help of this new setting, Ismagilov

.

[

Suppose that

. Then

\displaystyle B_{0}(f,r)+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\right)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3},

Inspired by Theorem F in view of Theorem G , we continue the study with the quantity

further and proved the following sharp result.

Problem 1.1
.

Whether we can derive sharp version of Theorem D in the setting of Theorem F and Theorem G with the additional two non-negative term
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
instead of

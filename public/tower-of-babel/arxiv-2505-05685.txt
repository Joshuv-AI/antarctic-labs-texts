Convergence from the Log-Gamma Polymer to the Directed Landscape
Xinyi Zhang

Abstract
. We define the log-gamma sheet and the log-gamma landscape in terms of the 2 2 -parameter and 4 4 -parameter free energy of the log-gamma polymer model and prove that they converge to the Airy sheet and the directed landscape, which are central objects in Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) universality class. Our proof of the convergence to the Airy sheet relies on the invariance of free energy through the geometric RSK correspondence and the monotonicity of the free energy. To upgrade the convergence to the directed landscape, tail bounds in both spatial and temporal directions are required. However, due to the lack of scaling invariance in the discrete log-gamma polymer—unlike the Brownian setting of the O’Connell–Yor model—existing on-diagonal fluctuation bounds are insufficient. We therefore develop new off-diagonal local fluctuation estimates for the log-gamma polymer.



1.
Introduction

The study of stochastic growth models has long been one of the central topics in probability theory
[
BS95
,
CSY04
,
KL13
,
HHZ95
,
Mea98
,
Maj07
,
Spo06
]
. The well-known central limit theorem characterizes the magnitude of the fluctuation of a sum of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables with
1/2
exponent. The limiting distribution of the fluctuation after proper scaling will be Gaussian. However, with certain dependence of variables introduced to the system, we observe totally different limiting behavior. As suggested by various experimental evidence and numerical simulations
[
TS10
,
MMK+97
,
MMMT05
,
WZG03
]
, a large class of random growth models including random growth interfaces, interacting particle systems, and directed polymer models have fluctuations of
1/3
scaling exponent and have completely different limiting distributions. These random growth models fall into the
Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) universality class
.

There have been many important breakthroughs in the last twenty-five years in understanding the KPZ universality, including the work
[
DOV22
]
where Dauvergne, Ortmann and Virág constructed central objects in the KPZ universality class: the Airy sheets and the directed landscape. Not only do the Airy sheets and the directed landscape capture the intricate geometry of random growth phenomena but they are also believed to be the final limiting objects of growth models in the KPZ universality class. The Airy sheets and the directed landscape have been identified as scaling limits for a few zero-temperature models. Notable examples include Poissonian, exponential, and geometric Last Passage Percolation, as demonstrated in the works of Dauvergne-Nica-Virág
[
DV21b
]
and
[
DNV23
]
. However, proving such convergences are more challenging in positive temperature models and hence there are few results. Namely, the solutions to the KPZ equation by Wu
[
Wu23
]
and the colored ASEP by Aggarwal, Corwin, and Hegde
[
ACH24
]
and the recent results by Dauvergne and Zhang that upgrade the KPZ fixed point convergence to the Directed landscape convergence
[
DZ24
]
.

The challenges of the positive temperature models come from the following two reasons. First, there are generally no determinantal structures from which we can extract exact formulas in the positive temperature environment. Second, there is no direct metric composition law as in the zero-temperature models and in the limit of the directed landscape. However, the lack of direct metric composition law is remedied by a variational formula of the free energy in the cases of the KPZ equation and the log-gamma polymer model.

In this paper, we establish the convergence of another positive temperature model, the log‐gamma polymer, to the directed landscape. Our analysis builds on the geometric Robinson–Schensted–Knuth (RSK) correspondence, which plays a crucial role in relating the partition functions of the log‐gamma polymer to the corresponding free energy on the dual line ensemble. Inspired by the continuous limit of the geometric RSK correspondence
[
Cor20
]
that preserves certain polymer free energy used in
[
Wu23
]
and the melon map used in
[
DOV22
]
that preserves certain last passage values, we exploit the invariance properties of the free energy via the discrete geometric RSK to obtain an equality (
2.20
). This equality together with the variational formula (
2.23
) provides a key tool to prove the convergence to the Airy sheet as it enables us to disintegrate the free energy from the bottom-left point to the top-right point of the line ensemble into two parts that only pertain the information of few top curves of the line ensemble. Similar to
[
Wu23
]
, we also leverage the monotonicity properties of Busemann functions to show that the contribution coming from the bottom part of the line ensemble becomes negligible in the scaling limit.

Due to lack of a nice coupling between the KPZ line ensemble and the KPZ sheet at the time that
[
Wu23
]
was written, Wu worked with the O’Connell Yor polymer which is the pre-limit of the KPZ line ensemble and has nice Brownian environment. In our case, the situation simplifies as there already exists a nice coupling between the log-gamma polymer and log-gamma line ensemble through the geometric RSK correspondence and hence we only need to take limit once instead of twice.

However, the continuous Brownian environment in the work of
[
Wu23
]
is not present in the log-gamma polymer model. Although the Brownian symmetry is approximated by the i.i.d. environment of the polymer, lack of Brownian scaling has created challenges in proving the tightness of the log-gamma landscape. It is no longer sufficient to prove a tail bound for local fluctuations of the free energy along the diagonal line. Untypical off-diagonal fluctuations are also needed. We solved this by generalizing the methods used in
[
BSS24
]
for the diagonal case to the off-diagonal case.

1.3.
Airy line ensemble, the Airy sheets, and the directed landscape

In this section, we follow the presentation of
[
DOV22
]
and introduce the central limiting objects in the KPZ universality class: the Airy line ensemble, Airy sheets, and the directed landscape.

The finite-dimensional distributions of the stationary Airy line ensemble were first introduced by Prähofer and Spohn in
[
PS02
]
, where it was referred to as the ”multi-line Airy process”. Subsequently, Corwin and Hammond
[
CH14
]
proved that this ensemble can be realized as a collection of continuous random functions indexed by
\N\times\R
, governed by the Brownian Gibbs property.

Definition 1.7
.

The stationary Airy line ensemble, denoted by
\tilde{A}=\{\tilde{A}_{1}>\tilde{A}_{2}>\cdots\}
, is an infinite collection of random functions from
\R
to
\R
indexed by the natural numbers. The distribution of
\tilde{A}
is uniquely characterized by its determinantal structure: for any finite set
I=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
, the point process on
I\times\mathbb{R}
defined by
\{(s,\tilde{A}_{i}(s)):i\in\mathbb{N},s\in I\}
is a determinantal point process with kernel

(1.6)

K(s_{1},x_{1};s_{2},x_{2})=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z(s_{1}-s_{2})}\mathrm{Ai}(x_{1}+z)\mathrm{Ai}(x_{2}+z)\,dz&\text{if }s_{1}\geq s_{2},\\
-\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-z(s_{1}-s_{2})}\mathrm{Ai}(x_{1}+z)\mathrm{Ai}(x_{2}+z)\,dz&\text{if }s_{1}<s_{2},\end{cases}

where
\mathrm{Ai}
denotes the Airy function.

As suggested by the name, the stationary Airy line ensemble
\tilde{A}(t)=(\tilde{A}_{1}(t),\tilde{A}_{2}(t),\cdots)
is stationary in
t
. Our focus, however, is on the parabolic Airy line ensemble.

Definition 1.8
.

The parabolic Airy line ensemble
A=\{A_{1}>A_{2}>\cdots\}
can be derived from
\tilde{A}
via

(1.7)

A_{i}(x):=\tilde{A}_{i}(x)-x^{2}.

Fix any real number
q>0
, we define the Airy line ensemble of scale
q
,
A^{q}=\{A^{q}_{1}>A^{q}_{2}>\cdots\}
, to be

(1.8)

A_{i}^{q}(x)=q^{-1}A_{i}(q^{2}x).

The Airy sheet was constructed by Dauvergne, Ortmann and Virág in
[
DOV22
]
via a last passage percolation framework on the Airy line ensemble. Thus, we need to first define the last passage time on a general line ensemble.

Definition 1.9
.

Let

be a sequence of functions from
\R
to
\R

to

where

and

(1.9)

f[(x,\ell)\xrightarrow{\infty}(y,m)]:=\max_{x=t_{\ell+1}\leq t_{\ell}\leq t_{\ell-1}\leq\cdots\leq t_{m+1}\leq t_{m}=y}\sum_{j=m}^{l}\bigg(f_{j}(t_{j})-f_{j}(t_{j+1})\bigg).

Definition 1.10
.

Let
g=(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots)
be a sequence of functions from
\R
to
\R
, we define the backwards first passage value from
(x,1)
to
(y,m)
where

and

(1.10)

g[(x,1)\rightarrow_{f}(y,m)]:=\min_{x=t_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq\cdots\leq t_{m}=y}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigg(g_{j}(t_{j})-g_{j}(t_{j-1})\bigg).

.

The Airy sheet, denoted by

, the Airy

Definition 1.12

The directed landscape, denoted by

2.
Geometric RSK correspondence: from partition functions to free energy

In this section, we will consider the directed polymers with a deterministic environment and introduce some basic definitions and properties. We will perform the discrete geometric Pitman transform, which is equivalent to the geometric RSK correspondence, to the polymer weight. From the invariance of the polymer partition functions under the geometric RSK correspondence, we will deduce the equivalence of the polymer partition function and the free energy on the dual line ensemble.

Instead of

(x,\ell),(y,m)\in\Z_{\geq 1}\times\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket

x\leq y
and

\Pi[(x,\ell)\rightarrow(y,m)]

(x,\ell)

\pi\rightarrow(t_{\ell},\cdots,t_{m+1})

t_{\ell+1}=x,t_{m}=y

We define the discrete free energy as follows.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
r_{1}\leq\cdots\leq r_{n}
be
n

be an
n
-tuple of functions where
f_{i}:\Z_{\geq r_{i}}\rightarrow\R
for

f_{i}(r_{i}-1)=1
for all

. For any up-right path

(2.8)

f(\tau):=\sum_{j=m}^{l}f_{j}(t_{j})-f_{j}(t_{j+1}-1).

For any multipath

contained in the domain of

(2.9)

Similarly, let

be an endpoint pair contained in the domain of

\beta

to

with respect to

(2.10)

f[U\xrightarrow{\beta}V]:=\beta^{-1}\log\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[U\rightarrow V]}\exp(\beta f(\pi)).

The following lemma connects the partition function and the free energy of the polymers.

Lemma 2.6
.

Fix
n

r_{1}\leq\cdots\leq r_{n}
. Let
D=(D_{1},\cdots,D_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of functions where
D_{i}:\Z_{\geq r_{i}}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
for

D_{i}(r_{i}-1)=1
for all

f=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n})
where
f_{i}:\Z_{\geq r_{i}}\rightarrow\R
are functions such that
f_{i}(x)=\log D_{i}(x)
for all

such that

and

f_{i}(r_{i}-1)=0
for all

(U,V)
contained in the domain of

(2.11)

f[U\xrightarrow{1}V]=\log D[U\rightarrow V].

Proof.

The proof follows directly from the definitions.
Let
U=\{(u_{i},\ell_{i})\}_{i=1}^{k},V=\{(v_{i},m_{i})\}_{i=1}^{k}
.
For an up-right path
\pi_{i}
, let
t_{j}^{i}=\max\{t\in\llbracket x,y\rrbracket|(t,j)\in\pi_{i}\}
.

\begin{split}f[U\xrightarrow{1}V]&=\log\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[U\rightarrow V]}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\exp(\sum_{j=m_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}f_{j}(t_{j}^{i})-f_{j}(t_{j+1}^{i}-1))\\
&=\log\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[U\rightarrow V]}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=m_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}\frac{D_{j}(t_{j}^{i})}{D_{j}(t_{j+1}^{i}-1)}\\
&=\log\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[U\rightarrow V]}\prod_{i=1}^{k}D(\pi_{i})\\
&=\log D[U\rightarrow V].\end{split}

∎

Let

2.5.
Variational formula and monotonicity

While the positive temperature polymer models do not satisfy a direct metric composition law for their free energy, they admit the following variational formula. This structure is particularly useful in the analysis of the scaling limit: in the convergence to the Airy sheet, only the contribution from the top part of the free energy survives in the limit, while the lower part becomes negligible.

Lemma 2.15
.

For any
m,k,\ell\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
,
m\leq k<\ell
,
x,y\in\Z_{\geq 1}
and
x<y
, we have

(2.23)

\exp(f[(x,\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y,m)])=\sum_{i=x}^{y}\exp(f[(x,\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(i,k+1)]+f[(i,k)\xrightarrow{1}(y,m)]).

Proof.

\begin{split}\exp(f[(x,\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y,m)])&=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[(x,\ell)\rightarrow(y,m)]}\exp(f(\pi))\\
&=\sum_{i=x}^{y}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[(x,\ell)\rightarrow(i,k+1)]}\sum_{\tilde{\pi}\in\Pi[(i,k)\rightarrow(y,m)]}\exp(f(\pi))\exp(f(\tilde{\pi}))\\
&=\sum_{i=x}^{y}\exp(f[(x,\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(i,k+1)]+f[(i,k)\xrightarrow{1}(y,m)])\end{split}

∎

The variational formula thus provides a crucial mechanism for distinguishing the asymptotically dominant contributions. Building on this, we now establish a monotonicity property of the free energy, which serves as a key tool in demonstrating that the contribution from the lower part becomes negligible in the scaling limit. Intuitively, the following lemma implies that path coalescence is energetically more favorable.

Lemma 2.16
.

For any
m,\ell\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
where
m\leq\ell
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\Z_{\geq 1}
where
x_{1}\leq x_{2}
, the following inequality holds for any
y_{1},y_{2}\in\Z_{\geq x_{2}}
where
y_{1}\leq y_{2}
:

(2.24)

f[(x_{2},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y_{1},m)]-f[(x_{1},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y_{1},m)]\leq f[(x_{2},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y_{2},m)]-f[(x_{1},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y_{2},m)].

Proof.

We will first do induction on
\ell-m
. When
\ell=m
, the lemma follows trivially because
f[(x,m)\xrightarrow{1}(y,m)]=f_{m}(y)-f_{m}(x-1)
. Fix
\ell
and assume that the inequality holds for
m+1
, and we will prove it for
m
. It suffices to consider
y_{1}=y
and
y_{2}=y+1
.

Let us rewrite the free energy using the variational formula (
2.23
):

(2.25)

\exp(f[(x,\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y,m)])=\sum_{i=x}^{y}\exp(f[(x,\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(i,m+1)]+f_{m}(y)-f_{m}(i-1)).

For
i,j\in\{1,2\}
, let

(2.26)

A(x_{i},x_{j})=\sum_{i=x_{i}}^{y}\exp(f[(x_{j},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(i,m+1)]-f_{m}(i-1))

(2.27)

B(x_{i})=\exp(f[(x_{i},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y+1,m+1)]-f_{m}(y)).

By the variational formula, the inequality for
m
is just

(2.28)

\frac{A(x_{2},x_{2})+B(x_{2})}{A(x_{1},x_{1})+B(x_{1})}\geq\frac{A(x_{2},x_{2})}{A(x_{1},x_{1})},

which is equivalent to

(2.29)

\frac{A(x_{1},x_{1})}{A(x_{2},x_{2})}\geq\frac{B(x_{1})}{B(x_{2})}.

This follows by applying the induction hypothesis

\begin{split}&A(x_{2},x_{2})\\
&=\sum_{i=x_{2}}^{y}\exp(f[(x_{2},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(i,m+1)]-f[(x_{1},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(i,m+1)]+f[(x_{1},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(i,m+1)]-f_{m}(i-1))\\
&\leq\exp(f[(x_{2},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y+1,m+1)]-f[(x_{1},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y+1,m+1)])A(x_{2},x_{1})\\
&\leq\exp(f[(x_{2},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y+1,m+1)]-f[(x_{1},\ell)\xrightarrow{1}(y+1,m+1)])A(x_{1},x_{1}).\end{split}

∎

(2.30)

In this section, we define the log-gamma line ensemble following the conventions of

Definition 3.1
.

We use

(3.1)

(3.2)

Let

Lastly, we define the function

(3.3)

d_{\theta}(x)=\Bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x}{(n+g_{\theta}^{-1}(x))^{3}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x}{(n+\theta-g_{\theta}^{-1}(x))^{3}}\Bigg)^{1/3}.

\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}(L_{i}^{N}(\frac{1}{2}N)-(p/2)N)&\text{if }s\geq\frac{1}{2}N^{1/3}.\end{cases}

For
i\geq N+1
, we define
A^{N}_{i}\equiv 0
.

With the log-gamma line ensemble

now defined, we are ready to state the convergence result established in
[
AH23
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
q=2^{-5/6}\sigma_{p}d_{\theta}(1)^{-1}

3.2.
Log-gamma sheet and monotonicity

We begin by introducing the appropriate scaling operations. Let
\overline{x}_{N}=\lfloor N^{2/3}xq^{-2}\rfloor+1
,
\widehat{y}_{N}=\lfloor N^{2/3}yq^{-2}\rfloor+2N
.

Definition 3.4

(Log-gamma sheet)
.

For
x,y\in\R
, we define the log-gamma sheet by

(3.7)

h^{N}(x,y)=2^{-1/2}q\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}[\log D^{N}((\overline{x}_{N},2N)\rightarrow(\widehat{y}_{N},1))-p(\widehat{y}_{N}-\overline{x}_{N}+2N)].

Given Theorem
2.3
, we can also express the log-gamma sheet in the following alternative form when
x>0
, which will be the main focus in our analysis of convergence to the Airy sheet.

h^{N}(x,y)=2^{-1/2}q\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}[Wf^{N}[(\overline{x}_{N},\overline{x}_{N}\wedge 2N)\rightarrow(\widehat{y}_{N},1)]-p(\widehat{y}_{N}-\overline{x}_{N}+2N)].

To apply the variational formula (
2.23
) in decomposing the log-gamma sheet into two components, we need to work with the unscaled version of the log-gamma sheet.

Definition 3.5
.

Fix an positive integer
k\leq N-1
. Let
x>0
and
z,y\in\R
such that
z\leq y
. We define the unscaled log-gamma sheet and its two component functions as follows:

(3.8)

{\h}^{N}(x,y)=Wf^{N}[(\overline{x}_{N},\overline{x}_{N}\wedge 2N)\rightarrow(\widehat{y}_{N},1)]-p(\widehat{y}_{N}-\overline{x}_{N}+2N)

(3.9)

\F_{k}^{N}(x,z)=Wf^{N}[(\overline{x}_{N},\overline{x}_{N}\wedge 2N)\rightarrow(\widehat{z}_{N},k+1)]-(Wf^{N})_{k+1}(\widehat{z}_{N})+p(\overline{x}_{N}-N^{2/3}k)

(3.10)

\G_{k}^{N}(z,y)=Wf^{N}[(\widehat{z}_{N},k)\rightarrow(\widehat{y}_{N},1)]+(Wf^{N})_{k+1}(\widehat{z}_{N})-p(\widehat{y}_{N}-N^{2/3}k+2N).

For notational flexibility when working with integer lattices, we define

(3.11)

\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(x,z)=Wf^{N}[(x,x\wedge 2N)\rightarrow(z,k+1)]-(Wf^{N})_{k+1}(z)+p(x-N^{2/3}k)

(3.12)

\overline{\G}_{k}^{N}(z,y)=Wf^{N}[(z,k)\rightarrow(y,1)]+(Wf^{N})_{k+1}(z)-p(y-N^{2/3}k+2N)

for
x,y,z\in\Z_{\geq 1}
.

Lastly, we define the rescaled versions of the component functions.

(3.13)

F_{k}^{N}(x,z)=2^{-1/2}q\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}\F_{k}^{N}(x,z)

(3.14)

G_{k}^{N}(z,y)=2^{-1/2}q\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}\G_{k}^{N}(z,y)

The following definition connects the unscaled log-gamma sheet
\h^{N}
and its two component functions
\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}
and
\overline{\G}_{k}^{N}
through a naturally associated probability measure:

Definition 3.6
.

We denote by
\mu^{N}_{k,x,y}
the random probability measure on the discrete set
\llbracket\overline{x}_{N},\widehat{y}_{N}\rrbracket
as follows:

(3.15)

\mu^{N}_{k,x,y}(\widehat{z}_{N})=\exp(-\h^{N}(x,y)+\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{N},\widehat{z}_{N})+\overline{\G}^{N}_{k}(\widehat{z}_{N},\widehat{y}_{N})).

The fact that
\mu_{k,x,y}^{N}
defines a probability measure follows from Lemma
2.15
.

We also set its c.d.f. to be

(3.16)

A_{k}^{N}(x,y;\widehat{z}_{N})=\sum_{i=\widehat{z}_{N}}^{\widehat{y}_{N}}\mu^{N}_{k,x,y}(i),

(3.17)

B_{k}^{N}(x,y;\widehat{z}_{N})=\sum_{i=\overline{x}_{N}}^{\widehat{z}_{N}}\mu^{N}_{k,x,y}(i).

We have the following monotonicity corollary of the log-gamma sheet and its component functions from Lemma
2.16
and Lemma
2.17
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Given
0<x_{1}\leq x_{2}
and
y_{1}\leq y_{2}
,
\F^{N}_{k}(x_{2},z)-\F^{N}_{k}(x_{1},z)
is increasing in
z\geq x_{2}
and
\G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{2})-\G^{N}_{k}(z,y_{1})
is increasing in
z\leq y_{1}
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Given
x_{1}\leq x_{2}
,
y_{1}\leq y_{2}
, and
\overline{x}_{2_{N}}\leq\widehat{y}_{1_{N}}
,

(3.18)

h^{N}(x_{1},y_{1})+h^{N}(x_{2},y_{2})\geq h^{N}(x_{1},y_{2})+h^{N}(x_{2},y_{1}).

We now use the monotonicity results above to derive a series of inequalities. The key idea is to bound the difference between
h^{N}(x,y_{2})-h^{N}(x,y_{1})
and
G_{k}^{N}(x,y_{2})-G_{k}^{N}(x,y_{1})
in terms of the measure
\mu^{N}_{k}
. Subsequently, we control the behavior of
\mu^{N}_{k}
through the component function
F_{k}^{N}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
0<x_{1}\leq x_{2}
and
\widehat{y}_{N}\geq\widehat{z}_{N}\geq\overline{x}_{2_{N}}
.

(3.19)

\F_{k}^{N}(x_{2},z)-\F_{k}^{N}(x_{1},z)\leq\h^{N}(x_{2},y)-\h^{N}(x_{1},y)-\log A_{k}^{N}(x_{1},y;\widehat{z}_{N})

(3.20)

\F_{k}^{N}(x_{2},z)-\F_{k}^{N}(x_{1},z)\geq\h^{N}(x_{2},y)-\h^{N}(x_{1},y)+\log B_{k}^{N}(x_{2},y;\widehat{z}_{N})

Proof.

\begin{split}&\exp(\h^{N}(x_{2},y)-\h^{N}(x_{1},y))\\
&=\sum_{w=\lfloor N^{2/3}x_{2}q^{-2}\rfloor}^{\lfloor N^{2/3}yq^{-2}\rfloor+2N}\exp(\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{2_{N}},w)+\overline{\G}_{k}^{N}(w,\widehat{y}_{N})-\h^{N}(x_{1},y)+\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{1_{N}},w)-\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{1_{N}},w))\\
&\geq\sum_{w=\widehat{z}_{N}}^{\lfloor N^{2/3}yq^{-2}\rfloor+2N}\exp(\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{2_{N}},w)+\overline{\G}_{k}^{N}(w,\widehat{y}_{N})-\h^{N}(x_{1},y)+\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{1_{N}},w)-\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{1_{N}},w))\\
&\geq\sum_{w=\widehat{z}_{N}}^{\lfloor N^{2/3}yq^{-2}\rfloor+2N}\exp(\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{2_{N}},\widehat{z}_{N})+\overline{\G}_{k}^{N}(w,\widehat{y}_{N})-\h^{N}(x_{1},y)+\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{1_{N}},w)-\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{1_{N}},\widehat{z}_{N}))\\
&=\exp(\F_{k}^{N}(x_{2},z)-\F_{k}^{N}(x_{1},z))A_{k}^{N}(x_{1},y;\widehat{z}_{N})\end{split}

where we used Corollary
3.7
in the last inequality.

Similarly,

\begin{split}&\exp(\h^{N}(x_{1},y)-\h^{N}(x_{2},y))\\
&=\sum_{w=\lfloor N^{2/3}x_{1}q^{-2}\rfloor}^{\lfloor N^{2/3}yq^{-2}\rfloor+2N}\exp(-\h^{N}(x_{2},y)+\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{1_{N}},w)+\overline{\G}_{k}^{N}(w,\widehat{y}_{N})-\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{2_{N}},w)+\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{2_{N}},w))\\
&\geq\sum_{w=\lfloor N^{2/3}x_{2}q^{-2}\rfloor}^{\widehat{z}_{N}}\exp(-\h^{N}(x_{2},y)+\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{1_{N}},w)+\overline{\G}_{k}^{N}(w,\widehat{y}_{N})-\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{2_{N}},w)+\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{2_{N}},w))\\
&\geq\sum_{w=\lfloor N^{2/3}x_{2}q^{-2}\rfloor}^{\widehat{z}_{N}}\exp(-\h^{N}(x_{2},y)+\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{1_{N}},\widehat{z}_{N})+\overline{\G}_{k}^{N}(w,\widehat{y}_{N})-\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{2_{N}},\widehat{z}_{N})+\overline{\F}_{k}^{N}(\overline{x}_{2_{N}},w))\\
&=\exp(\F_{k}^{N}(x_{1},z)-\F_{k}^{N}(x_{2},z))B_{k}^{N}(x_{2},y;\widehat{z}_{N})\end{split}

∎

Lemma 3.10
.

For
\widehat{z}_{N}\geq\overline{x}_{N}
and
z\leq y_{1}\leq y_{2}
, we have

(3.21)

\G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{2})-\G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{1})\leq\h^{N}(x,y_{2})-\h^{N}(x,y_{1})-\log A_{k}^{N}(x,y_{1};\widehat{z}_{N})

(3.22)

\G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{2})-\G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{1})\geq\h^{N}(x,y_{2})-\h^{N}(x,y_{1})+\log B_{k}^{N}(x,y_{2};\widehat{z}_{N})

The proof is analogous to the proof above.

Corollary 3.11
.

Given that
A_{k}^{N}(x,y_{1};\widehat{z}_{N}+1)+B_{k}^{N}(x,y_{2};\widehat{z}_{N})=1
, we have

(3.23)

\G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{2})-\G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{1})-\h^{N}(x,y_{2})+\h^{N}(x,y_{1})\leq-\log(1-B_{k}^{N}(x,y_{1};\widehat{z}_{N}-1))

(3.24)

\G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{2})-\G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{1})-\h^{N}(x,y_{2})+\h^{N}(x,y_{1})\geq\log(1-A_{k}^{N}(x,y_{2};\widehat{z}_{N}+1))

For large

(3.26)

F_{k}^{N}(x_{2},z)-F_{k}^{N}(x_{1},z)\geq h^{N}(x_{2},y)-h^{N}(x_{1},y)+\log B_{k}^{N}(x_{2},y;\widehat{z}_{N})

(3.27)

G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{2})-G_{k}^{N}(z,y_{1})-h^{N}(x,y_{2})+h^{N}(x,y_{1})\leq-\log(1-B_{k}^{N}(x,y_{1};\widehat{z}_{N}-1))

(3.28)

for
0<x_{1}\leq x_{2}
and
y_{2}\geq y_{1}\geq z
.

3.3.
Change of coordinates

As the log-gamma line ensemble converges to the Airy line ensemble, our ultimate goal is to show that the free energy of the log-gamma line ensemble converges to the last passage value of the Airy line ensemble. Since the mesh size of the discrete model is shrinking in the line ensemble convergence, we need to establish necessary formulas of the effect of coordinate change on the free energy.

Definition 3.13
.

Given a discrete subset
C\subset\R
and let

x\leq y

t_{\ell+1}=x,t_{m}=y.

Definition 3.14
.

For any discrete subset
C\subset\R
and
n

\beta

Lemma 3.15
.

Fix
n

f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}:\Z_{\geq 1}\rightarrow\R
and constants
a_{1},a_{2}>0
,
a_{3},a_{4}\in\R
and
\{a_{5,i}\}_{i\in\N}
. Define the functions

and

with
x\leq y
and
l\geq k
, we have

(3.32)

g[(x,\ell)\xrightarrow{\beta}(y,k)]=a_{1}f[(a_{2}x+a_{3},l)\xrightarrow{a_{1}\beta}(a_{2}y+a_{3},k)]+a_{4}(y-x)+a_{4}a_{2}^{-1}(l-k+1).

Proof.

\begin{split}g[(x,\ell)\xrightarrow{\beta}(y,k)]&=\beta^{-1}\log\sum_{x\leq t_{\ell}\leq\cdots\leq t_{k+1}\leq y;t_{i}\in C}\exp\bigg(\beta\sum_{j=k}^{l}g_{j}(t_{j})-g_{j}(t_{j+1}-a_{2}^{-1})\bigg)\\
&=\beta^{-1}\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{2}x+a_{3}\leq t_{\ell}\leq\cdots\leq t_{k+1}\leq a_{2}y+a_{3}\\
t_{i}\in\Z_{\geq 1}\end{subarray}}\exp\bigg(\beta\sum_{j=k}^{l}g_{j}(\frac{t_{j}-a_{3}}{a_{2}})-g_{j}(\frac{t_{j+1}-a_{3}-1}{a_{2}})\bigg)\\

&\quad\times\exp\bigg(\beta a_{4}a_{2}^{-1}\sum_{j=k}^{l}(t_{j}-t_{j+1}+1)\bigg)\\
&=a_{1}f[(a_{2}x+a_{3},l)\xrightarrow{a_{1}\beta}(a_{2}y+a_{3},k))]+a_{4}(y-x)+a_{4}a_{2}^{-1}(l-k+1)\\
\end{split}

∎

Similarly, we can define the reverse free energy on any discrete lattice.

Proposition 4.1

Proof.

(4.1)

\begin{split}\tilde{h}^{N}(0,0)&=2^{-1/2}q\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}[Wf^{N}_{1}(2N)-(4N-1)p]\\

[
BCD21

Let

\begin{split}g^{N}(y)&=2^{-1/2}q\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}[Wf^{N}_{1}(\widehat{y}_{N})-p(\widehat{y}_{N}-1+2N)]\\

\epsilon>0
and

and

\PP(w(g^{N},\delta^{\prime})\geq\epsilon)\leq\eta,

where the modulus of continuity of

is defined by

(4.4)

|x-y|\leq\delta\end{subarray}}|g(x)-g(y)|.

We now show that the modulus of continuity of
\tilde{h}^{N}
can be controlled via the modulus of continuity of

. It suffices to develop modulus of continuity control for pair of points

such that

(4.5)

(4.6)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\PP(|R_{k}(x_{1},-2^{-1/2}k^{1/2}x_{2}^{-1/2})|>\epsilon k^{1/2})<\infty.

Hence, by Borel-Cantelli lemma, we conclude that
|k^{-1/2}R_{k}(x_{1},-2^{-1/2}k^{1/2}x_{2}^{-1/2})|<\epsilon
infinitely often almost surely. Therefore, with probability one,

(4.17)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|k^{-1/2}R_{k}(x_{1},-2^{-1/2}k^{1/2}x_{2}^{-1/2})|=0.

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
h
be any distributional limit of
\tilde{h}^{N}
along some subsequence. Then there exists a coupling of
h
and the Airy line ensemble
A
such that the following holds:

(1)

h(0,\cdot)=A_{1}(\cdot)

(2)

h(\cdot+t,\cdot+t)\overset{d}{=}h(\cdot,\cdot)

(3)

Almost surely, for all
x>0
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\R
, we have

(4.18)

\begin{split}&\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}A[(-2^{1/2}k^{1/2}x^{-1/2},k)\xrightarrow{\infty}(y_{2},1)]-A[(-2^{1/2}k^{1/2}x^{-1/2},k)\xrightarrow{\infty}(y_{1},1)]=h(x,y_{2})-h(x,y_{1})\end{split}

Then,
h
has the same law as the Airy sheet. Hence
\tilde{h}^{N}
converges in distribution to the Airy sheet.

Proof.

Let
h
be the distributional limit of
h^{N}
along some sequence
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in\Z_{\geq 1}}
. From Proposition
4.3
, we can find a sequence
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\Z_{\geq 1}}
such that the assertions in Proposition
4.3
hold. We can further augment the probability space to accomodate an Airy sheet
S
such that on an event with probability one,

(4.19)

\begin{split}&\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}A[(-2^{1/2}k^{1/2}x^{-1/2},k)\xrightarrow{\infty}(y_{2},1)]-A[(-2^{1/2}k^{1/2}x^{-1/2},k)\xrightarrow{\infty}(y_{1},1)]=S(x,y_{2})-S(x,y_{1})\end{split}

for all
x>0
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\R
. Fix the event
\Omega_{0}
with probability one such that for any
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
, all the assertions in Proposition
4.3
and (
4.19
) hold. We will show that when
\Omega_{0}
occurs,

(4.20)

h(x,y_{2})-h(x,y_{1})=S(x,y_{2})-S(x,y_{1})

for all
x>0
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\R
.

Fix
x_{1}<x_{2}\in\Q^{+}
and
y_{1}\leq y_{2}\in\Q
. We want to show that

(4.21)

h(x_{2},y_{2})-h(x_{2},y_{1})\geq S(x_{1},y_{2})-S(x_{1},y_{1}).

Let
z_{k}=-2^{-1/2}k^{1/2}x_{1}^{-1/2}
. By Corollary
3.12
, we have

(4.22)

G_{k}^{N}(z_{k},y_{1})-G_{k}^{N}(z_{k},y_{2})+h^{N}(x_{2},y_{2})-h^{N}(x_{2},y_{1})\geq\log(1-B_{k}^{N}(x_{2},y_{1};\widehat{z}_{k_{N}}-1))

Because
\tilde{h}^{N}
is the linear interpolation of
h^{N}
, if
\tilde{h}^{N}
converges to some function
h
uniformly on compact subsets, then so does
h^{N}
. Thus,

(4.23)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in\{M_{i}\}\\
i\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}(\text{LHS of }(\ref{h_G}))=S(x_{1},y_{1})-S(x_{1},y_{2})+h^{N}(x_{2},y_{2})-h^{N}(x_{2},y_{1}).

Hence it suffices to show that

(4.24)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in\{M_{i}\}\\
i\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}(\log B_{k}^{N}(x_{2},y_{1};\widehat{z}_{k_{N}}))=-\infty.

This follows by applying Corollary
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
. A similar argument yields

(4.25)

h(x_{2},y_{2})-h(x_{2},y_{1})\leq S(x_{3},y_{2})-S(x_{3},y_{1})

for all
x_{2}<x_{3}\in\Q^{+}
and
y_{1}\leq y_{2}\in\Q
. By continuity,

(4.26)

h(x,y_{2})-h(x,y_{1})=S(x,y_{2})-S(x,y_{1})

for all
x>0
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\R
.

Finally, we verify the stationarity property
h(\cdot+t,\cdot+t)\overset{d}{=}h(\cdot,\cdot)
. This follows from the corresponding property for the prelimit objects: as processes on the discrete lattice
N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z\times N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
, we have

(4.27)

h^{N}(\cdot+t_{N},\cdot+t_{N})\overset{d}{=}h^{N}(\cdot,\cdot)

for
t_{N}\in N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
.
Since the mesh size
N^{-2/3}q^{2}
vanishes as

, for any fixed

, we can choose a sequence
\{t_{N}\}\subset N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
such that
t_{N}\rightarrow t
. Taking limit on both sides of (
4.27
) yields the desired result.
∎

5.
Convergence to the directed landscape

Recall that in previous sections, we relabeled the coordinate
(x,i)\in\Z_{\geq 1}\times\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
as
(x,n-i+1)
to align with the conventions of the line ensemble notations. In this section, however, since we are focusing on the four-parameter free energy of the polymer, we revert to the original coordinate system. We also recall the notation
\R^{4}_{+}=\{(x,s;y,t)\in\R^{4}:s<t\}
for the domain of time-ordered space-time points.

Definition 5.1
.

For
t,x\in\R
, we define the scaling operations
\overline{x}_{N}=\lfloor N^{2/3}xq^{-2}\rfloor+1
,
\check{t}_{N}=\lfloor 2Nt\rfloor
. Given
(x,s;y,t)\in\R_{+}^{4}
, we define the unscaled and scaled log-gamma landscape as follows:

(5.1)

\begin{split}\h^{N}(x,s;y,t)&=\log D[(\overline{x}_{N}+\check{s}_{N}+1,\check{s}_{N}+1)\rightarrow(\overline{y}_{N}+\check{t}_{N},\check{t}_{N})]-p(\overline{y}_{N}-\overline{x}_{N}+4N(t-s)),\end{split}

(5.2)

\begin{split}h^{N}(x,s;y,t)=2^{-1/2}q\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}\h^{N}(x,s;y,t).\end{split}

5.1.
Tightness

Let
\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,t)
be the linear interpolated
h^{N}(x,s;y,t)
such that they agree on tuples
(x,s;y,t)\in\R^{4}_{+}
where
x,y\in N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
and
s,t\in 2^{-1}N^{-1}\Z
. The goal of this section is to prove the tightness of
\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,t)
in
C(\R^{4}_{+},\R)
. To achieve this goal, we will use the following lemma by Dauvergne and Virag
[
DV21a
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
Q=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{d}
denote the Cartesian product of real intervals with lengths
L_{1},\ldots,L_{d}
. Let
\mathcal{G}:Q\rightarrow\R
be a continuous random function. Let
e_{i}
be the
i
-th coordinate vector of
\R^{d}
. Suppose that for some
a,c>0
and for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
, there exist constants
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1)
,
\beta_{i},r_{i}>0
such that for all
m>0
,
u\in(0,r_{i})
and
t,t+ue_{i}\in Q
, the following inequality holds:

(5.3)

\mathbb{P}\left(|\mathcal{G}(t+ue_{i})-\mathcal{G}(t)|\geq mu^{\alpha_{i}}\right)\leq ce^{-am^{\beta_{i}}}.

Let
\beta=\min_{i}\beta_{i}
,
\alpha=\max_{i}\alpha_{i}
, and
r=\max_{i}r_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
. Then, with probability one, for all
t,t+s\in Q
such that
|s_{i}|\leq r_{i}
for all
i
, we have

(5.4)

|\mathcal{G}(t+s)-\mathcal{G}(t)|\leq C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|s_{i}|^{\alpha_{i}}\log^{1/\beta_{i}}\left(\frac{2r^{1/\alpha_{i}}}{|s_{i}|}\right)\right),

where
C
is a random constant with the tail bound:

(5.5)

\mathbb{P}(C>m)\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\right)cc_{0}e^{-c_{1}m^{\beta}},

for some constants
c_{0},c_{1}>0
depending only on on
\{\alpha_{i}\},\{\beta_{i}\},d
and
a
.

Because
\tilde{h}^{N}
is the linear interpolated version of
h^{N}
, it is difficult to directly prove the tail bound for
\tilde{h}^{N}
as required in Lemma
5.2
. However, the following proposition justifies the sufficiency to only prove the tail bound for the integer points of
h^{N}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that there exists positive constants
C_{1},C_{2},r
and
N_{0}\in\N
such that for all
N\geq N_{0}
,
d_{1}\in(0,r]\cap N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
,
d_{2}\in(0,r]\cap 2^{-1}N^{-1}\Z
,
x,y\in N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
,
s,t\in 2^{-1}N^{-1}\Z
,
s<t
, and every
K\geq 0
, we have

(5.6)

\PP(|h^{N}(x,s;y,t+d_{2})-h^{N}(x,s;y,t)|\geq Kd_{2}^{1/3})\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}K^{1/10}}

(5.7)

\PP(|h^{N}(x,s+d_{2};y,t)-h^{N}(x,s;y,t)|\geq Kd_{2}^{1/3})\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}K^{1/10}}

(5.8)

\PP(|h^{N}(x,s;y+d_{1},t)-h^{N}(x,s;y,t)|\geq Kd_{1}^{1/3})\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}K^{1/10}}

(5.9)

\PP(|h^{N}(x+d_{1},s;y,t)-h^{N}(x,s;y,t)|\geq Kd_{1}^{1/3})\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}K^{1/10}}.

Then all of the inequalities naturally extend to
\tilde{h}^{N}
with different constants
\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2}
.

Proof.

We will prove the extension of (
5.6
) and the rest will follow analogously. Let us first assume that
x,y\in N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
and
s\in 2^{-1}N^{-1}\Z
. We want to bound the following probability

(5.10)

\PP(|\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,t_{2})-\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,t_{1})|\geq K|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{1/3}).

We first consider the case when
\lfloor 2Nt_{1}\rfloor=\lfloor 2Nt_{2}\rfloor
. In this setting, we let
w_{1}=\lfloor 2Nt_{1}\rfloor
and
w_{2}=\lceil 2Nt_{1}\rceil
. Since
\tilde{h}^{N}
is a linear interpolation of
h^{N}
,

|\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,t_{2})-\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,t_{1})|=|h^{N}(x,s;y,w_{1})-h^{N}(x,s;y,w_{2})|\frac{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|}.

Thus, the probability (
5.10
) is bounded by

\begin{split}&\PP\left(|\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,w_{2})-\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,w_{1})|\geq K\left(\frac{|w_{1}-w_{2}|}{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}\right)^{2/3}|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/3}\right)\\
&\leq\PP(|\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,w_{2})-\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,w_{1})|\geq K|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/3})\\
&\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}K^{1/10}}.\end{split}

For the remaining case, we may assume without loss of generality that
\lfloor 2Nt_{1}\rfloor<\lfloor 2Nt_{2}\rfloor
. In this case, set
w_{1}=\lceil 2Nt_{1}\rceil
and
w_{2}=\lfloor 2Nt_{2}\rfloor
. Thus, the probability (
5.10
) is bounded by

\begin{split}&\PP(|\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,t_{2})-\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,w_{2})|\geq\frac{1}{3}K|w_{2}-t_{2}|^{1/3})\\
&\quad+\PP(|\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,t_{1})-\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,w_{1})|\geq\frac{1}{3}K|t_{1}-w_{1}|^{1/3})\\
&\quad+\PP(|\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,w_{2})-\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,w_{1})|\geq\frac{1}{3}K|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{1/3}).\end{split}

Here, the first two terms can be bounded using the argument from the first case, while the last term is controlled directly via equation (
5.6
).

We now proceed to relax the constraint on the remaining coordinates one at a time. Suppose
x\in N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
,
s\in 2^{-1}N^{-1}\Z
, and
y\in\R
. Let
y=\lambda y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2}
for some
\lambda\in[0,1)
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
. Then,

\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,t_{1})=\lambda\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y_{1},t_{1})+(1-\lambda)\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y_{2},t_{1})

\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y,t_{2})=\lambda\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y_{1},t_{2})+(1-\lambda)\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y_{2},t_{2}).

Thus, the probability (
5.10
) can be bounded by the following

(5.11)

\begin{split}&\PP(\lambda|\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y_{1},t_{1})-\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y_{1},t_{2})|\geq\lambda K|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{1/3})\\
&\quad+\PP((1-\lambda)|\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y_{2},t_{1})-\tilde{h}^{N}(x,s;y_{2},t_{2})|\geq(1-\lambda)K|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{1/3}).\end{split}

It then follows from the above argument. The constraints for the remaining coordinates can be relaxed in this inductive manner.
∎

In view of Proposition
5.3
, it suffices to establish the desired tail bounds at lattice points. In this setting, we can exploit the following symmetries arising from the i.i.d. environment of the log-gamma polymer.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\text{Lattice}_{N}=\{(x,s;y,t)|s<t,s,t\in 2^{-1}N^{-1}\Z,x,y\in N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z\}
. As a random function from
\text{Lattice}_{N}
to
\R
,
h^{N}
has the following symmetries: for any
s,t,a\in 2^{-1}N^{-1}\Z
and
x,y,b\in N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
,

h^{N}(x+b,s+a;y+b,t+a)\overset{d}{=}h^{N}(x,s;y,t)

h^{N}(x,s;y,t)\overset{d}{=}h^{N}(-y,-t;-x,-s).

Consequently, it suffices to establish only the following two probability bounds, as the remaining cases in Lemma
5.2
follow by symmetry.

Proposition 5.5
.

Fix
t_{0}>0
and
M\in\Z_{\geq 1}
. There exists positive constants
C_{1},C_{2},r_{0}>0
and
N_{0}\in\Z_{\geq 1}
such that for any
N\in\Z_{\geq N_{0}}
,
d_{1}\in(0,r_{0}]\cap 2^{-1}N^{-1}\Z
and
d_{2}\in(0,r_{0}]\cap N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z
,
K\geq 0
,
y\in N^{-2/3}q^{2}\Z,|y|\leq b
, and
t\in 2^{-1}N^{-1}\Z\cap(t_{0},\infty)
, we have

(5.12)

\PP(|h^{N}(0,0;y;t+d_{1})-h^{N}(0,0;y,t)|\geq Kd_{1}^{1/3})\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}K^{1/10}}

(5.13)

\PP(|h^{N}(0,0;y+d_{2};t)-h^{N}(0,0;y,t)|\geq Kd_{2}^{1/3})\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}K^{1/10}}.

We defer the proof of this proposition to the next section and proceed under the assumption that it holds.

Theorem 5.6
.

\tilde{h}^{N}
is tight in
C(\R_{+}^{4},\R)
.

Proof.

Fix
b\in\Z_{\geq 1}
and define

(5.14)

Q_{b}=[-b,b]^{4}\cap\{(x,s;y,t)\in\R^{4}:t-s\geq b^{-1}\}.

Because
Q_{b}
is compact,
Q_{b}
can be written as the union of finitely many hypercubes
Q_{b,i}
where

(5.15)

Q_{b,i}=\{(x,s;y,t)\in Q_{b}:(x,s;y,t)=(x_{b,i},s_{b,i};y_{b_{i}},t_{b_{i}})+[0,r_{0}]^{4}\}.

To show that
\tilde{h}^{N}
is tight in
C(\R_{+}^{4},\R)
, it suffices to show that
\tilde{h}^{N}
is tight in
Q_{b,i}
because any compact subsets of
\R^{4}_{+}
will be contained in some
Q_{b}
for some large enough
b
. By the convergence result in
[
BCD21
, Theorem 1.2]
, the sequence
\{h^{N}(x_{N},s_{N};y_{N},t_{N})\}_{N\geq 1}
is tight whenever
(x_{N},s_{N};y_{N},t_{N})\rightarrow(x,s;y,t)
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Since each
\tilde{h}^{N}(x_{b,i},s_{b,i};y_{b_{i}},t_{b_{i}})
is defined via linear interpolation of finitely many
h^{N}(x_{N_{j}},s_{N_{j}};y_{N_{j}},t_{N_{j}})
where
(x_{N_{j}},s_{N_{j}};y_{N_{j}},t_{N_{j}})\rightarrow(x,s;y,t)
, the seqeunce
\{\tilde{h}^{N}(x_{b,i},s_{b,i};y_{b_{i}},t_{b_{i}})\}_{N\geq 1}
is also tight. Thus, it remains to develop the modulus of continuity control.

To apply Proposition
5.5
, we take
t_{0}=b^{-1}
and
M=b
. Together with Lemma
5.4
and Proposition
5.3
, we could directed apply Lemma
5.2
and conclude that almost surely for any
(x_{1},s_{1};y_{1},t_{1}),(x_{2},s_{2};y_{2},t_{2})\in Q_{b,i}
,

\begin{split}|\tilde{h}^{N}(s_{1},x_{1};y_{1},t_{1})-\tilde{h}^{N}(s_{2},x_{2};y_{2},t_{2})|&\leq C^{N}\bigg(|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{1/3}\log^{10}(2r_{0}/|x_{1}-x_{2}|)+|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{1/3}\log^{10}(2r_{0}/|y_{1}-y_{2}|)\\
&\quad+|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{1/3}\log^{10}(2r_{0}/|t_{1}-t_{2}|)+|s_{1}-s_{2}|^{1/3}\log^{10}(2r_{0}/|s_{1}-s_{2}|)\bigg)\end{split}

where
\PP(C^{N}>m)\leq De^{-Dm^{1/10}}
for some constant
D
that depends on
b
only. Therefore, by the Kolmogorov-Chentsov criterion, we see that
\tilde{h}^{N}
is tight in
Q_{b,i}
.
∎

5.2.
Convergence

Given the tightness of
\tilde{h}^{N}
, we now introduce the following variational formula – an analogue of Lemma
2.15
– which will serve as a key tool in establishing the convergence of
\tilde{h}^{N}
to the directed landscape.

Lemma 5.7
.

For
l,k,m\in\Z
such that
l<k\leq m
,

(5.16)

D[(x,\ell)\rightarrow(y,m)]=\sum_{i=x}^{y}D[(x,\ell)\rightarrow(i,k+1)]D[(i,k)\rightarrow(y,m)].

Thus, for
s<r<t

(5.17)

\exp(\h^{N}(x,s;y,t))=\sum_{\overline{z}_{N}=\overline{x}_{N}+1+\lfloor 2Ns\rfloor}^{\overline{y}_{N}+\lfloor 2Nt\rfloor}\exp(\h^{N}(x,s;z,r)+\h^{N}(z,r;y,t))

(5.18)

\begin{split}&h^{N}(x,s;y,t)=2^{-1/2}q\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}\log\sum_{\overline{z}_{N}=\overline{x}_{N}+1+\lfloor 2Ns\rfloor}^{\overline{y}_{N}+\lfloor 2Nt\rfloor}\exp(2^{1/2}q^{-1}\sigma_{p}N^{1/3}[h^{N}(x,s;z,r)+h^{N}(z,r;y,t)]).\end{split}

Proof.

Observe that equation (
5.16
) follows directly from summing over all possible intermediate points where an up-right path
\pi
from
(x,\ell)
to
(y,m)
may intersect the
i
-th row.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

\tilde{h}^{N}
converges to
\mathcal{L}
in distribution as
C(\R^{4}_{+},\R)
-random variables.

Proof.

Since
\tilde{h}^{N}
is tight, let
h
denote a distributional limit along some subsequence
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in\N}
. By the Skorokhod’s representation theorem, there exists a coupling under which
\tilde{h}^{N_{i}}
converges to
h
almost surely in
C(\R^{4}_{+},\R)
. Let
\Omega_{0}
denote the event on which this convergence holds; then
\PP(\Omega_{0})=1
. For any finite set of disjoint intervals
\{(s_{j},t_{j})\}_{j=1}^{m}
,
\{h(\cdot,s_{j};\cdot,t_{j})\}_{j=1}^{m}
are independent because
\{\tilde{h}^{N_{i}}(\cdot,s_{j};\cdot,t_{j})\}_{j=1}^{m}
are independent.

Moreover, since
\tilde{h}^{N_{i}}\rightarrow h
on
\Omega_{0}
in
C(\R^{4}_{+},\R)
, it follows that
h^{N_{i}}\rightarrow h
uniformly over compact subsets on
\Omega_{0}
as well. For any
r>0
, to show that the process
h(\cdot,s;\cdot,s+r^{3})
has the law of Airy sheet of scale
r
, consider sequences
\{s_{i}\}
and
\{r_{i}\}
such that
2Ns_{i},2N(s_{i}+r_{i}^{3})\in\Z
and
(s_{i},r_{i})\rightarrow(s,r)
as
i\rightarrow\infty.
Notice that
h^{N_{i}}(\cdot,s_{i};\cdot,s_{i}+r_{i}^{3})\rightarrow h(\cdot,s;\cdot,s+r^{3})
on
\Omega_{0}
. Let
M_{i}=N_{i}r_{i}^{3}
and take
M_{i}
to infinity. Then by Theorem
4.4
, we know that
h(\cdot,s;\cdot,s+r^{3})
must be distributed as an Airy sheet of scale
r
.

The only thing left to prove is that for any
t_{i}<t_{j}<t_{k}
,
x,y\in\R
, the following holds with probability one:

(5.19)

h(x,t_{i};y,t_{k})=\max_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left(h(x,t_{i};z,t_{j})+h(z,t_{j};y,t_{k})\right).

From
[
DOV22
, Proposition 9.2]
, we know that the right-hand side of (
5.19
) is well-defined as a random variable on
C(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
and is distributed as an Airy sheet of scale
(t_{k}-t_{i})^{1/3}
. Since
h(\cdot,t_{i};\cdot,t_{k})
is also distributed as an Airy sheet of scale
(t_{k}-t_{i})^{1/3}
, it is enough to show that almost surely for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,

(5.20)

h(x,t_{i};y,t_{k})\geq\max_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left(h(x,t_{i};z,t_{j})+h(z,t_{j};y,t_{k})\right).

Let
\Omega_{1}
denote the event on which the right-hand side of (
5.19
) defines a continuous function in
x
and
y
. Then
\PP(\Omega_{1})=1
. Let us denote a maximizer of the function
h(x,t_{i};z,t_{j})+h(z,t_{j};y,t_{k})
by
Z_{j}(x,t_{i};y,t_{k})
. Note that on the event
\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{1}
,
Z_{j}(x,t_{i};y,t_{k})\neq\emptyset
. For
M>0
, consider the event
\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{1}\cap\{Z_{j}(x,t_{i};y,t_{k})\cap[-M,M]\neq\emptyset\}
. When such an event occurs, we have

(5.21)

\begin{split}&\max_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left(h(x,t_{i};z,t_{j})+h(z,t_{j};y,t_{k})\right)\\
&=\max_{z\in[-M,M]}\left(h(x,t_{i};z,t_{j})+h(z,t_{j};y,t_{k})\right)\\
&=\lim_{N\to\infty}2^{-1/2}q\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}\log\int_{-M}^{M}\exp\left[2^{1/2}q^{-1}\sigma_{p}N^{1/3}(h^{N}(x,t_{i};z,t_{j})+h^{N}(z,t_{j};y,t_{k}))\right]dz\\
&\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}2^{-1/2}q\sigma_{p}N^{-1/3}\log\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in[-M,M]\\
\overline{z}_{N}=\overline{x}_{N}+1+\lfloor 2Nt_{i}\rfloor\end{subarray}}^{\overline{y}_{N}+\lfloor 2Nt_{k}\rfloor}\frac{1}{N^{-2/3}q^{2}}\exp\left[2^{1/2}q^{-1}\sigma_{p}N^{1/3}(h^{N}(x,t_{i};z,t_{j})+h^{N}(z,t_{j};y,t_{k}))\right]\\
&\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}h^{N}(x,t_{i};y,t_{k})\\
&=h(x,t_{i};y,t_{k}).\end{split}

The second equality follows from the Laplace method, while the last inequality is a consequence of equation (
5.18
). Since
\PP(\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{1})=1
and
\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{1}\cap\{Z_{j}(x,t_{i};y,t_{k})\cap[-M,M]\neq\emptyset\}=\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{1}
, we conclude that (
5.20
) holds with probability one.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.5

In this section, we focus on the proof of Proposition
5.5
. Throughout the section, we assume that all coordinates under consideration are integer-valued. Since we will frequently refer to various lemmas and propositions from
[
BSS24
]
, we introduce the following notational conventions for convenience, so that our notations are in accordance with those used in that work.

For
u,v\in\Z^{2}
, let
Z_{u,v}=D[u\rightarrow v]
and let
\ZZ_{u,v}=\frac{Z_{u,v}}{d_{u}}
.

For
(N,N),(M,M)\in\Z^{2}
, we abbreviate notation as
\log\ZZ_{N,M}=\log\ZZ_{(N,N),(M,M)}
.

For any
w\in\Z^{2}
, define the anti-diagonal through
w
as
L_{w}=\{w+(i,-i):i\in\Z\}
and its truncation by
k
as
L_{w}^{k}=\{x\in L_{w}:||x-w||_{\infty}\leq k\}
.

For subsets
A,B\subset\Z^{2}
, let
\ZZ_{A,B}=\sum_{a\in A,b\in B}\ZZ_{a,b}
and let
\ZZ_{A,B}^{\max}=\max_{a\in A,b\in B}\ZZ_{a,b}
.

We define the characteristic direction of the polymer model as a function of
\rho\in(0,\theta)
by

(6.1)

\xi[\rho]=(\frac{\Psi_{1}(\rho)}{\Psi_{1}(\rho)+\Psi_{1}(\theta-\rho)},\frac{\Psi_{1}(\theta-\rho)}{\Psi_{1}(\rho)+\Psi_{1}(\theta-\rho)})

where
\Psi_{1}
denotes the trigamma function. Since
\Psi_{1}
is strictly decreasing and smooth on
\R_{>0}
, the map
\xi
defines a continuous bijection between
\rho\in(0,\theta)
and vectors in the open line segment between
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
.

Corresponding to each vector
\xi[\rho]
, we define the shape function as

(6.2)

\Lambda(\xi[\rho])=-\frac{\Psi_{1}(\rho)}{\Psi_{1}(\rho)+\Psi_{1}(\theta-\rho)}\Psi(\theta-\rho)-\frac{\Psi_{1}(\theta-\rho)}{\Psi_{1}(\rho)+\Psi_{1}(\theta-\rho)}\Psi(\theta-\rho)

where
\Psi
is the digamma function.

Let
\Lambda(N,N):=\Lambda(\xi[\frac{\theta}{2}])=-\Psi(\frac{\theta}{2})
to denote the shape function in the diagonal direction. Note that this value is consistent with our earlier definitions:
h_{\theta}(1)=2\Psi(\frac{\theta}{2})
and
p=-\Psi(\frac{\theta}{2})
.

Now, for
w=(N+yN^{2/3},N-yN^{2/3})
, we can express
w
in terms of its characteristic direction as
w=2N\xi[\frac{\theta}{2}+z_{w}]
for some
z_{w}\in\R
. We then define the shape function at
w
by
\Lambda(w):=2N\Lambda(\xi[\frac{\theta}{2}+z_{w}])
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
w=(N+yN^{2/3},N-yN^{2/3})
. We will show that for there exists constant
c_{1},c_{2},C_{1}>0
and
N_{0}\in\Z_{\geq 1}
such that for
N\geq N_{0}
,
1\leq K\leq c_{1}N^{1/2}
,
0\leq y\leq c_{2}K^{1/10}
and
a\in\Z_{\geq 0}
, it holds that

(6.3)

\PP(\log Z_{0,L_{w}^{a}}-\log Z_{0,w}>K\sqrt{a})\leq e^{-C_{1}\min\{K^{2},K\sqrt{a}\}}.

Proof.

We will prove the statement by considering two separate cases. We begin with the case where
a>K^{2/3}N^{2/3}
. By
[
BSS24
, Proposition 3.5]
, the following inequality holds

(6.4)

|\Lambda(w)-\Lambda(N,N)|\leq Cy^{2}N^{1/3}.

for some constant
C>0
.
Thus, we can bound the difference between the free energy using the one-point estimate:

(6.5)

\begin{split}&\PP(\log Z_{0,L_{w}^{a}}-\log Z_{0,w}>K\sqrt{a})\\
&\leq\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,L_{N}}-\log\ZZ_{0,w}>K^{4/3}N^{1/3})\\
&\leq\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,L_{N}}-\Lambda(w)>\frac{1}{2}K^{4/3}N^{1/3})+\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,w}-\Lambda(w)<-\frac{1}{2}K^{4/3}N^{1/3}).\end{split}

The second term can be directly bounded by
[
BSS24
, Proposition 3.8]
. For the first term, we can apply the bound between the shape function:

(6.6)

\begin{split}&\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,L_{N}}-\Lambda(w)>\frac{1}{2}K^{4/3}N^{1/3})\\
&=\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,L_{N}}-\Lambda(N,N)+\Lambda(N,N)-\Lambda(w)>\frac{1}{2}K^{4/3}N^{1/3})\\
&\leq\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,L_{N}}-\Lambda(N,N)>\frac{1}{2}K^{4/3}N^{1/3}-Cy^{2}N^{1/3}).\end{split}

Now, choose
c_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4C}}
. Then, under the assumption that
0\leq y\leq c_{2}K^{1/10}
, we have

(6.7)

\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,L_{N}}-\Lambda(N,N)>\frac{1}{2}K^{4/3}N^{1/3}-Cy^{2}N^{1/3})\leq\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,L_{N}}-\Lambda(N,N)>\frac{1}{4}K^{4/3}N^{1/3}).

The right-hand side of equation (
6.7
) can then be upper bounded according to
[
BSS24
, Proposition 3.7]
.

The case where
a\leq K^{2/3}N^{2/3}
follows from a similar random walk approximation based on the stationary polymer measure, as in
[
BSS24
, Proposition 4.1]
. Notice that

\log Z_{0,L_{w}^{a}}\leq\log Z^{\max}_{0,L_{w}^{a}}+10\log(a+1).

By applying a union bound, it is sufficient to bound the following probability for some constant
C^{\prime}
:

(6.8)

\mathbb{P}\left(\log Z_{0,L_{w}^{a,+}}^{\max}-\log Z_{0,w}\geq C^{\prime}K\sqrt{a}\right)

where
\mathcal{L}_{w}^{a,+}
denotes the subset of
\mathcal{L}_{w}^{a}
lying to the left and above
w
. For any fixed
k=0,\ldots,a
, we redefine the difference of free energy along the anti-diagonal as:

(6.9)

\log Z_{0,w+(-k,k)}-\log Z_{0,w}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log Z_{0,w+(-i,i)}-\log Z_{0,w+(-i+1,i-1)}=S_{k}.

This reformulation allows us to study the behavior of the walk
S_{k}
via its running maximum:

(6.10)

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq k\leq a}S_{k}\geq C^{\prime}K\sqrt{a}\right).

Although the increments of
S_{k}
are neither independent nor identically distributed,
[
BSS24
, Theorem 3.28]
provides a coupling with an i.i.d. random walk
\check{s}_{k}
which upper bounds
S_{k}
with high probability. Specifically, we take the down-right path
\Theta_{2a}
will be the staircase from
w+(-a,a)
to
w
and define the perturbed parameter to be
\eta=\frac{\theta}{2}+z_{w}-q_{0}K^{2/3}N^{-1/3}
. Under this perturbation, the increments of
\check{s}_{k}
are i.i.d. with distribution
\log(\text{Gamma}^{-1}(\eta))-\log(\text{Gamma}^{-1}(\theta-\eta))
.

Let
A
denote the event that
\log\frac{10}{9}+\check{s}_{k}\geq S_{k}
for all
k\in\llbracket 0,a\rrbracket
. Then we have the bound:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq k\leq a}S_{k}\geq C^{\prime}\sqrt{a}K^{3/4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\max_{0\leq k\leq a}S_{k}\geq C^{\prime}\sqrt{a}K^{3/4}\right\}\cap A\right)+\mathbb{P}(A^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\log\frac{10}{9}+\max_{0\leq k\leq a}\check{s}_{k}\geq C^{\prime}\sqrt{a}K^{3/4}\right\}\right)+\mathbb{P}(A^{c}).

By
[
BSS24
, Theorem 3.28]
, we know that
\mathbb{P}(A^{c})\leq e^{-C^{\prime\prime}K^{2}}
for some constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0
. Absorbing the constant
\log(10/9)
into the constant
C^{\prime}
, it suffices to obtain the upper bound:

(6.11)

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq k\leq a}\check{s}_{k}\geq C^{\prime}K\sqrt{a}\right)\leq e^{-C_{1}\min\{K^{2},K\sqrt{a}\}}.

This is a standard estimate on the running maximum of an i.i.d. random walk with sub-exponential increments, as proved in
[
BSS24
, Appendix D]
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
w=(N+yN^{2/3},N-yN^{2/3})
. There exists a positive integer
N_{0}
such that for each
N\geq N_{0}
,
K\geq 1
,
a\in\Z_{\geq 0}
, and

where

, we have

(6.12)

\PP(\log Z_{0,L_{w}^{a}}-\log Z_{0,w}\geq K\sqrt{a})\leq e^{-K^{1/10}}.

Proof.

Because of Proposition
6.1
, we only need to establish the inequality in the regime where
K\geq c_{1}N^{1/2}
. Let
K=zN^{1/2}
for some

. Then,

(6.13)

\begin{split}&\PP(\log Z_{0,L_{w}^{a}}-\log Z_{0,w}\geq K\sqrt{a})\\
&\leq\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,L_{w}^{a}}-\log\ZZ_{0,w}\geq zN^{1/6}N^{1/3})\\
&\leq\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,L_{w}^{a}}-\Lambda(w)\geq\frac{1}{2}zN^{1/6}N^{1/3})+\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,w}-\Lambda(w)\leq-\frac{1}{2}zN^{1/6}N^{1/3})\\

&\leq e^{-K^{1/10}}.\end{split}

We once again absorb the term
Cy^{2}N^{1/3}
into
\frac{1}{2}zN^{1/6}N^{1/3}
in the second to last inequality, using the bound
0\leq y\leq c_{2}K^{1/10}
. The final inequality then follows from
[
BSS24
, Proposition 3.8]
together with
[
BSS24
, Proposition A.2]
.
∎

Let
0\leq r\leq\frac{N}{2}
, and define
x^{*}
be the random maximizer of the following across the anti-diagonal line
L_{r}
.

(6.14)

x^{*}=\arg\max_{x\in L_{r}}\{\log Z_{0,x}+\log Z_{x,w}\}.

Proposition 6.3
.

There exist positive constants
C_{2},c_{3},K_{0},N_{0},\epsilon_{0}
such that for each
N\geq N_{0}
,
c_{3}\leq r\leq\epsilon_{0}(N-r)
,
K\geq K_{0}
,
0\leq y\leq c_{2}K^{1/10}
where
c_{2}
is the constant from Proposition
6.1
, we have:

(6.15)

\mathbb{P}(||x^{*}-(r,r)||_{\infty}>Kr^{2/3})\leq e^{-C_{2}K^{3}}.

Proof.

Let
J_{h}=L^{r^{2/3}}_{(r-2hr^{2/3},r+2hr^{2/3})}
denote the segment of the anti-diagonal line
L_{r}
that consists of points within a window of width
4hr^{2/3}
centered at
r
. We now bound the probability

(6.16)

\begin{split}&\PP\left(\left||x^{*}-(r,r)\right||_{\infty}>Kr^{2/3}\right)\\
&\leq\PP\left(\max_{x\in L_{r}\setminus L_{r}^{Kr^{2/3}}}\{\log Z_{0,x}+\log Z_{x,w}\}>\log Z_{0,r}+\log Z_{r,w}\right)\\

&\leq\sum_{|h|=\lfloor K/2\rfloor}^{r^{1/3}}\PP(\log Z_{0,J^{h}}^{\max}-\log Z_{0,r}\geq-Dh^{2}r^{1/3})+\PP(\log Z^{\max}_{J^{h},w}-\log Z_{r,w}\geq Dh^{2}r^{1/3})\end{split}

for some small positive constant

to be chosen later.

For the first term in the summation, assuming

is sufficiently large, we use
[
BSS24

and
[
BSS24

\begin{split}\PP(\log Z_{0,J^{h}}^{\max}-\log Z_{0,r}\geq-Dh^{2}r^{1/3})&\leq\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,J^{h}}^{\max}-2rp\geq-2Dh^{2}r^{1/3})+\PP(\log\ZZ_{0,r}-2rp\geq Dh^{2}r^{1/3})\\

[

\begin{split}\PP(\log Z^{\max}_{J^{h},w}-\log Z_{r,w}\geq Dh^{2}r^{1/3})&\leq\PP(\log Z^{\max}_{L_{r}^{4|h|r^{2/3}},w}-\log Z_{r,w}\geq Dh^{2}r^{1/3})\\

We can now apply Proposition
6.1
by setting

(6.19)

\PP(\log Z^{\max}_{J^{h},w}-\log Z_{r,w}\geq Dh^{2}r^{1/3})\leq e^{-C_{1}K^{2}}\leq e^{-C|h|^{3}}.

(6.20)

\begin{split}&\PP\left(\left||x^{*}-(r,r)\right||_{\infty}>Kr^{2/3}\right)\\

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
w=(N+yN^{2/3},N-yN^{2/3})
. There exists positive constants
C_{3},K_{0}
and
N_{0}\in\Z_{\geq 1}
such that for all
N\geq N_{0},c_{3}\leq r\leq\epsilon_{0}(N-r)
,

, and

6.3

6.1
, we have

(6.21)

\PP(\log Z_{0,w}-\log Z_{0,r}-\log Z_{r,w}\geq Kr^{1/3})\leq e^{-C_{3}K^{1/10}}

Proof.

Notice that it suffices to replace
\log Z_{0,w}
by
\max_{x\in L_{r}}\{\log Z_{0,x}+\log Z_{x,w}\}
. This is because

(6.22)

\log Z_{0,w}\leq\max_{x\in L_{r}}\{\log Z_{0,x}+\log Z_{x,w}\}+2\log(r).

Thus, we only need to consider the following probability

(6.23)

\PP(\max_{x\in L_{r}}\{\log Z_{0,x}+\log Z_{x,w}\}-\log Z_{0,r}-\log Z_{r,w}\geq Kr^{1/3}).

We can split this into two cases by a union bound. The first case is that
x^{*}\notin L^{Kr^{2/3}}_{r}
and the second case is that
x^{*}\in L^{Kr^{2/3}}_{r}
. By Proposition
6.3

x^{*}\in L^{Kr^{2/3}}_{r}

(6.24)

\begin{split}&\PP(\max_{x\in L^{Kr^{2/3}}_{r}}\{\log Z_{0,x}+\log Z_{x,w}\}-\log Z_{0,r}-\log Z_{r,w}\geq Kr^{1/3})\\
&\leq\PP(\log Z^{\max}_{0,L_{r}^{Kr^{2/3}}}-\log Z_{0,r}\geq\frac{1}{2}Kr^{1/3})+\PP(\log Z^{\max}_{L_{r}^{Kr^{2/3}},w}-\log Z_{r,w}\geq\frac{1}{2}Kr^{1/3})\\
&\leq e^{-C_{3}K^{1/10}}\end{split}

where the last inequality comes from Proposition
6.2
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Proposition

, which is restated below.

Proposition 6.5
.

Fix
t_{0}>0
and integer

. There exist positive constants
C_{4},C_{5},r_{0}
,

,

(6.25)

\PP(|h^{N}(0,0;y;t+d_{1})-h^{N}(0,0;y,t)|\geq Kd_{1}^{1/3})\leq C_{4}e^{-C_{5}K^{1/10}}

(6.26)

\PP(|h^{N}(0,0;y+d_{2};t)-h^{N}(0,0;y,t)|\geq Kd_{2}^{1/3})\leq C_{4}e^{-C_{5}K^{1/10}}.

Proof.

) first. We consider two separate cases.

:
2Nd_{1}\geq c_{3}
, where
c_{3}
is the constant in Theorem
6.4
. In this case, we can apply Theorem
6.4
by requiring
N_{1}
to be sufficiently large so that
2N_{1}t_{0}\geq N_{0}
and
r_{0}

r_{0}N_{1}\leq\epsilon_{0}(N_{1}t_{0}-r_{0}N_{1})

N_{0}
and
\epsilon_{0}

6.4

K_{0}

c_{2}K_{0}^{1/10}\geq(2t_{0})^{-2/3}M

c_{2}
in Proposition
6.1

BSS24

Case 2
:

,

(6.27)

\begin{split}&\PP(|\log Z_{1,w}-\log Z_{1,w^{\prime\prime}}-4pNd_{1}|\geq Kd_{1}^{1/3}N^{1/3}2^{1/2}q^{-1}\sigma_{p})\\

&\leq\PP(|\log Z_{1,w}-\log Z_{1,w^{\prime}}|\geq\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}K)+\PP(|\log Z_{1,w^{\prime}}-\log Z_{1,w^{\prime\prime}}|\geq\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}K)\\

where

.

We then apply
[
BSS24
, Theorem 3.28]
with
s=K^{2/3}
. Together with the random walk approximation for sub-exponential variables, we have that

(6.28)

\PP(|\log Z_{1,w}-\log Z_{1,w^{\prime}}|\geq\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}K)+\PP(|\log Z_{1,w^{\prime}}-\log Z_{1,w^{\prime\prime}}|\geq\frac{1}{2}C^{\prime}K)\leq C_{4}e^{-C_{5}K}.

For

, we note that

can be bounded by some large constant independent of

, we can directly bound the probability as follows:

\begin{split}&\PP(|\log Z_{1,w}-\log Z_{1,w^{\prime\prime}}-4pNd_{1}|\geq CK)\\

&\leq C_{4}e^{-C_{5}K^{1/10}}.\end{split}

The last inequality follows from the one-point tail bound in

BSS24
, Proposition 3.6]
and
[
BSS24

.

We now turn to the proof of equation (

). Let

\PP(|\log Z_{1,w^{\prime}}-\log Z_{1,w}-pN^{2/3}d_{2}q^{-2}|\geq Kd_{2}^{1/3}N^{1/3}2^{1/2}q^{-1}\sigma_{p}).

For

We can view each summand as a step in the walk and

BSS24
, Theorem 3.28]

where

Thus, the distribution of the steps of

and the distribution of the steps of

\PP(A^{c})\leq e^{-CK^{2}}

Notice that for

(6.34)

Let

(6.35)

(6.37)

\begin{split}|\E[S_{a}^{1}]-pN^{2/3}d_{2}q^{-2}|&=|-a\Psi(\theta-\lambda)+a\Psi(\frac{\theta}{2})|\\

&\leq ac_{1}(K^{2/3}+C^{\prime})N^{-1/3}\\
&\leq d_{2}C^{\prime\prime}K^{2/3}N^{1/3}\end{split}

where we absorb the constant

(6.38)

\begin{split}&\PP(S_{a}^{1}-pN^{2/3}d_{2}q^{-2}\leq Kd_{2}^{1/3}N^{1/3}2^{1/2}q^{-1}\sigma_{p})\\
&=\PP(S_{a}^{1}-\E[S_{a}^{1}]+\E[S_{a}^{1}]-pN^{2/3}d_{2}q^{-2}\geq Kd_{2}^{1/3}N^{1/3}2^{1/2}q^{-1}\sigma_{p})\end{split}

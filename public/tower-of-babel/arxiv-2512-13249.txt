Introduction

In a recent preprint
[
5
]
, Raja introduced a new quantitative invariant of Banach spaces, the
covering index

\Theta_{X}(n)
, defined via convex coverings of the unit ball by finitely many pieces each containing a large finite-codimensional ball. More precisely, for a Banach space
X
and each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the quantity
\Theta_{X}(n)
is the infimum, over all coverings of
B_{X}
by
n
closed convex sets, of the maximum
essential inradius
among those sets. This invariant measures how resistant the unit ball is to being decomposed into convex parts, and turns out to be closely related to asymptotic uniform smoothness and the Szlenk index.

The covering index provides a natural quantitative refinement of classical decomposition techniques in Banach space theory. While various notions of asymptotic geometry—such as asymptotic uniform smoothness, asymptotic uniform convexity, and the Szlenk index—have been extensively studied and applied to problems in renorming theory, the structure of reflexive spaces, and fixed-point theory, the covering index offers a fresh perspective by encoding geometric information through optimal decompositions of the unit ball. In particular, the decay rate of
\Theta_{X}(n)
as
n\to\infty
captures subtle structural features of
X
that are not immediately apparent from standard moduli alone. Raja’s framework establishes that the covering index sits naturally between local and global geometric properties: on the one hand, it can be estimated via asymptotic smoothness moduli; on the other hand, its precise value depends on the interplay between finite-dimensional and infinite-dimensional features of the space.

Raja developed a general framework connecting the decay of
\Theta_{X}(n)
as
n\to\infty
with the asymptotic geometry of
X
. In particular, he proved that if
X
admits an equivalent
p
-asymptotically uniformly smooth (
p
-AUS) norm, then one necessarily has a power-type lower bound

\Theta_{X}(n)\ \gtrsim\ n^{-1/p}.

Raja computed or estimated
\Theta_{X}
for several concrete classes of spaces and posed a number of open problems about exact values in specific cases. One of these asks for the precise value of
\Theta_{\ell_{2}}(2)
; more generally, while his methods identify the correct exponent for many classical sequence spaces, they do not yield sharp constants. Another question (Problem 4.7 in
[
5
]
) asks to what extent the behaviour of
\Theta_{X}
is governed by the asymptotic moduli of uniform convexity and uniform smoothness of
X
.

The purpose of this note is threefold.

First, we solve Raja’s problem for Hilbert spaces by showing that for every infinite-dimensional Hilbert space
H
one has

\Theta_{H}(2)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

Raja explicitly asked for the exact value of
\Theta_{\ell_{2}}(2)
and recorded only the explicit bounds

0.707\leqslant\Theta_{\ell_{2}}(2)\leqslant 0.931
(Problem 4.6 in
[
5
]
). Our argument is elementary and relies only on an orthogonal decomposition of
H
into two infinite-dimensional subspaces; it does not use the general AUS machinery.

Second, we give an explicit block decomposition of the unit ball in the scalar-valued Lebesgue spaces
L_{p}(\mu)
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
, which produces coverings by
n
closed convex sets of essential inradius
n^{-1/p}
. This yields the upper bound

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\ \leqslant\ n^{-1/p}\qquad(1\leqslant p<\infty,\ n\in\mathbb{N}),

and, in particular,
\Theta_{\ell_{p}}(n)\leqslant n^{-1/p}
for the sequence spaces. For
1<p<\infty
, the spaces
L_{p}(\mu)
admit equivalent
p
-AUS norms
[
1
,
6
]
, so Raja’s general theorem supplies a matching lower bound of order
n^{-1/p}
. We therefore obtain the sharp asymptotic behaviour

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\ \asymp\ n^{-1/p}\qquad(1<p<\infty),

and a completely explicit covering which exhibits the optimal decay rate.

Third, we use these scalar results to obtain lower bounds in more general settings and to comment on Raja’s Problem 4.7. For any Banach space
E
and any non-atomic
\sigma
-finite measure space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
, the canonical embedding

L_{p}(\mu)\hookrightarrow L_{p}(\mu;E)

shows that the covering index of the Bochner space
L_{p}(\mu;E)
satisfies

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu;E)}(n)\ \gtrsim\ n^{-1/p}\qquad(1<p<\infty,\ n\in\mathbb{N}),

with constants depending only on
p
and
\mu
and not on the geometry of
E
. Since the asymptotic moduli of
L_{p}(\mu;E)
can vary dramatically as
E
ranges over separable Banach spaces (including spaces that are not AUSable at all), this shows that, at least on the level of power-type lower bounds, the covering index cannot faithfully encode the asymptotic uniform smoothness of
L_{p}(\mu;E)
; this provides a partial negative answer to Problem 4.7 of
[
5
]
.

Finally, we briefly discuss non-commutative
L_{p}
-spaces. If
(M,\tau)
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal semifinite trace, then the associated non-commutative spaces
L_{p}(M,\tau)
,
1<p<\infty
, are uniformly convex and uniformly smooth. Non-commutative Clarkson inequalities show that their modulus of smoothness has power type
r=\min\{p,2\}

[
4
]
, which in turn implies that
L_{p}(M,\tau)
is
r
-AUS for
1<p<\infty
. Applying Raja’s general theorem we obtain power-type lower estimates

\Theta_{L_{p}(M,\tau)}(n)\ \gtrsim\ n^{-1/r},\qquad r=\min\{p,2\},

for the covering index of non-commutative
L_{p}
-spaces. Even in the commutative case these bounds are not optimal when
p>2
, and we do not attempt to determine the sharp exponent or constants in the non-commutative setting.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
X
be a Banach space with unit ball
B_{X}
.

Definition 1.1

(Essential inradius
[
5
]
)
.

For a set
A\subset X
, define

\varrho(A)=\sup\Bigl\{r>0:\exists x\in A\ \exists Y\leqslant X\ \text{closed},\dim X/Y<\infty,\dim Y=\infty,\ x+r(B_{X}\cap Y)\subset A\Bigr\}.

Definition 1.2

(Covering index
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(X)
denote the family of closed, convex, bounded subsets of
X
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the
covering index
of
X
at level
n
by

\Theta_{X}(n)=\inf\Bigl\{\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\varrho(A_{k})\;:\;A_{k}\in\mathcal{C}(X)\ (k=1,\dots,n),\ B_{X}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}A_{k}\Bigr\}.

The index is
isomorphic
: equivalent norms change
\Theta_{X}
only up to absolute multiplicative constants
[
5
, Sec. 1]
.

For completeness, we record the standard AUS definitions and Raja’s general lower bound.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. For
t\geqslant 0
, we define the
(global) modulus of smoothness

\rho_{X}(t)=\sup_{x\in S_{X}}\sup_{y\in S_{X}}(\|x+ty\|-1),

the
symmetric modulus of smoothness

\rho^{\prime}_{X}(t)=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\sup_{\|y\|=1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|x+ty\|+\frac{1}{2}\|x-ty\|-1\right),

and the
asymptotic modulus of smoothness

\overline{\rho}_{X}(t)=\sup_{x\in S_{X}}\ \inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\leqslant X\\
\dim(X/Y)<\infty\end{subarray}}\ \sup_{y\in S_{Y}}(\|x+ty\|-1).

We say that
X
is
asymptotically uniformly smooth
(AUS) if
\overline{\rho}_{X}(t)=o(t)
as
t\to 0
.
For
p\in(1,\infty)
we say
X
is
p
-AUS
if
\overline{\rho}_{X}(t)\leqslant Ct^{p}
for some
C>0
and all
t>0
.

Remark 1.4
.

It is straightforward to see that the global modulus of smoothness is equivalent to the symmetric modulus of smoothness, and that

\overline{\rho}_{X}(t)\leqslant\rho_{X}(t)\qquad(t>0).

Definition 1.5

(
p
-AUSable space)
.

A Banach space
X
is
p
-AUSable
if it admits an equivalent
p
-AUS norm.

Theorem 1.6

(Raja’s lower bound
[
5
, Thm. 3.5]
)
.

If
X
is
p
-AUSable, then there exists
c>0
such that

\Theta_{X}(n)\ \geqslant\ c\,n^{-1/p}\qquad(n\geqslant 1).

For
1<p<\infty
, the classical spaces
\ell_{p}
and
L_{p}(\mu)
are uniformly smooth with modulus of smoothness of power type
p
and admit equivalent
p
-AUS norms. Thus Theorem
1.6
yields lower bounds of the form
\Theta_{\ell_{p}}(n)\gtrsim n^{-1/p}
and
\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\gtrsim n^{-1/p}
. Our aim is to complement these with explicit upper bounds of the same order in the scalar-valued setting.

2.
The Hilbert space

Let
H
be an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. We begin with the exact value of the covering index for two pieces.

Theorem 2.1
.

\Theta_{H}(2)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

Proof.

Let
H=H_{1}\oplus H_{2}
be an orthogonal decomposition into infinite-dimensional closed subspaces, and write
P_{j}
for the orthogonal projection onto
H_{j}
. Define

A_{j}=\Big\{x\in B_{H}:\|P_{j}x\|\leqslant 1/\sqrt{2}\Big\},\qquad j=1,2.

These sets are closed, convex, and bounded. Since

\|P_{1}x\|^{2}+\|P_{2}x\|^{2}=\|x\|^{2}\leqslant 1,

at least one of
\|P_{j}x\|
is at most
1/\sqrt{2}
, so
B_{H}=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
.

If
\|y\|\leqslant 1/\sqrt{2}
, then
\|P_{j}y\|\leqslant\|y\|\leqslant 1/\sqrt{2}
, and hence
y\in A_{j}
. Thus
\varrho(A_{j})\geqslant 1/\sqrt{2}
.

Suppose
\varrho(A_{j})>1/\sqrt{2}
. Then there exist
x\in A_{j}
, a finite-codimensional subspace
Y\leqslant H
, and
r>1/\sqrt{2}
such that

x+r(B_{H}\cap Y)\subset A_{j}.

Since
Y\cap H_{j}
is infinite dimensional, we can choose
u\in Y\cap H_{j}
with
\|u\|=1
and
u\perp P_{j}x
. Taking
y=ru\in Y
yields
y\in r(B_{H}\cap Y)
and thus
x+y\in A_{j}
. On the other hand,

\|P_{j}(x+y)\|=\|P_{j}x+ru\|=\sqrt{\|P_{j}x\|^{2}+r^{2}}\geqslant r>1/\sqrt{2},

a contradiction. Hence
\varrho(A_{j})\leqslant 1/\sqrt{2}
, and therefore
\varrho(A_{j})=1/\sqrt{2}
for
j=1,2
. It follows that
\Theta_{H}(2)=1/\sqrt{2}
. ∎

3.
The scalar
L_{p}(\mu)
-spaces

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a measure space and
1\leqslant p<\infty
.
We assume throughout that
L_{p}(\mu)
is infinite-dimensional and that, for each

n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a partition

\Omega=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{n}E_{k}

into pairwise disjoint measurable sets of positive finite measure. This is
satisfied, for instance, whenever
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite and
L_{p}(\mu)
is
infinite dimensional.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1\leqslant p<\infty
and suppose that
L_{p}(\mu)
is infinite dimensional.
Then, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\ \leqslant\ n^{-1/p}.

Proof.

For each
k=1,\dots,n
define the projection

P_{k}f:=f\cdot\mathbf{1}_{E_{k}},\qquad f\in L_{p}(\mu),

so that
P_{k}:L_{p}(\mu)\to L_{p}(\mu)
is a contractive projection onto the closed
subspace
L_{p}(E_{k})
.

For
f\in L_{p}(\mu)
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|P_{k}f\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{E_{k}}|f|^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{n}E_{k}}|f|^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

In particular, if
f\in B_{L_{p}(\mu)}
, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|P_{k}f\|_{p}^{p}\leqslant 1.

Define, for
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
,

A_{k}:=\bigl\{f\in B_{L_{p}(\mu)}:\ \|P_{k}f\|_{p}\leqslant n^{-1/p}\bigr\}.

Each
A_{k}
is closed, convex and bounded.

If
f\in B_{L_{p}(\mu)}
, then the preceding inequality implies that

\|P_{k}f\|_{p}^{p}\leqslant 1/n
for some
k
, that is

\|P_{k}f\|_{p}\leqslant n^{-1/p}
and hence
f\in A_{k}
. Thus

B_{L_{p}(\mu)}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}A_{k}.

Lower bound on
\varrho(A_{k})
.

If
\|g\|_{p}\leqslant n^{-1/p}
, then

\|P_{k}g\|_{p}\leqslant\|g\|_{p}\leqslant n^{-1/p}
, so
g\in A_{k}
. Taking

x=0
and
Y=L_{p}(\mu)
in the definition of the essential inradius, we get

0+n^{-1/p}(B_{L_{p}(\mu)}\cap Y)=n^{-1/p}B_{L_{p}(\mu)}\subset A_{k},

so
\varrho(A_{k})\geqslant n^{-1/p}
.

Upper bound on
\varrho(A_{k})
.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\varrho(A_{k})>n^{-1/p}
. Then there exist

f\in A_{k}
, a finite-codimensional subspace
Y\leqslant L_{p}(\mu)
, and a radius

r>n^{-1/p}
such that

f+r(B_{L_{p}(\mu)}\cap Y)\subset A_{k}.

Let
v:=P_{k}f\in L_{p}(E_{k})
; then
\|v\|_{p}\leqslant n^{-1/p}
.

Since
Y
has finite codimension, there exist
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m}\in L_{p}(\mu)^{*}
such that

Y=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\ker\varphi_{i}.

By standard measure-theoretic arguments we may partition
E_{k}
into a sequence
of pairwise disjoint measurable sets
(B_{j})_{j\geqslant 1}
such that

0<\mu(B_{j})<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|v\mathbf{1}_{B_{j}}\|_{p}\longrightarrow 0\quad(j\to\infty).

For each
j
we further partition
B_{j}
into
m+1
pairwise disjoint measurable
subsets
B_{j,0},\dots,B_{j,m}
, each of positive measure, and consider the
finite-dimensional subspace spanned by the simple functions

f_{j,\ell}:=\mathbf{1}_{B_{j,\ell}}
,
\ell=0,\dots,m
, together with the
linear map

T_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{m+1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m},\qquad T_{j}(a_{0},\dots,a_{m})=\bigl(\varphi_{i}(\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}a_{\ell}f_{j,\ell})\bigr)_{i=1}^{m}.

Since
\dim\mathbb{R}^{m+1}>\dim\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the kernel of
T_{j}
is
non-trivial, so there exists a non-zero

w_{j}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}a_{\ell}f_{j,\ell}\in Y

supported in
B_{j}
. After normalising, we may assume that

u_{j}:=w_{j}/\|w_{j}\|_{p}
satisfies
\|u_{j}\|_{p}=1
and is supported in
B_{j}
.

Thus we obtain a sequence
(u_{j})_{j\geqslant 1}\subset Y\cap L_{p}(E_{k})
with
pairwise disjoint supports
B_{j}
and
\|u_{j}\|_{p}=1
for all
j
. Set

a_{j}:=\|v\mathbf{1}_{B_{j}}\|_{p}=\Bigl(\int_{B_{j}}|v|^{p}\,d\mu\Bigr)^{1/p}\longrightarrow 0\qquad(j\to\infty).

Fix
r>n^{-1/p}
as above and consider the continuous function

F(a)=\|v\|_{p}^{p}-a^{p}+(r-a)^{p},\qquad a\geqslant 0.

We have
F(0)=\|v\|_{p}^{p}+r^{p}>r^{p}>1/n
, so, by continuity, there exists

\delta>0
such that

0<\delta<r\quad\text{and}\quad F(a)>1/n\ \text{whenever}\ 0\leqslant a\leqslant\delta.

Choose
j_{0}
large enough so that
a_{j_{0}}\leqslant\delta
, and set
u:=u_{j_{0}}
,

B:=B_{j_{0}}
. Then
u\in Y
,
\|u\|_{p}=1
, and

\|v\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{p}=a_{j_{0}}\leqslant\delta
. Decompose

v=v_{1}+v_{2}
, where
v_{1}=v\mathbf{1}_{E_{k}\setminus B}
and

v_{2}=v\mathbf{1}_{B}
. Since
u
is supported in
B
, we have

\|v+ru\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{E_{k}\setminus B}|v_{1}|^{p}\,d\mu+\int_{B}|v_{2}+ru|^{p}\,d\mu=\|v_{1}\|_{p}^{p}+\|v_{2}+ru\|_{p}^{p}.

By the triangle inequality in
L_{p}
,

\|v_{2}+ru\|_{p}=\|ru+v_{2}\|_{p}\geqslant\bigl|r\|u\|_{p}-\|v_{2}\|_{p}\bigr|=|r-a_{j_{0}}|,

so, since
a_{j_{0}}\leqslant\delta<r
, we have

\|v_{2}+ru\|_{p}^{p}\ \geqslant\ (r-a_{j_{0}})^{p}.

Using
\|v\|_{p}^{p}=\|v_{1}\|_{p}^{p}+a_{j_{0}}^{p}
we obtain

\|v+ru\|_{p}^{p}\geqslant\|v_{1}\|_{p}^{p}+(r-a_{j_{0}})^{p}=\|v\|_{p}^{p}-a_{j_{0}}^{p}+(r-a_{j_{0}})^{p}=F(a_{j_{0}})>1/n.

Finally set
y:=ru\in Y
. Then
\|y\|_{p}=r
and

\|P_{k}(f+y)\|_{p}^{p}=\|v+ru\|_{p}^{p}>1/n,

so
\|P_{k}(f+y)\|_{p}>n^{-1/p}
and therefore
f+y\notin A_{k}
. This contradicts
the assumption that
f+r(B_{L_{p}(\mu)}\cap Y)\subset A_{k}
.

Thus no such
r>n^{-1/p}
exists and
\varrho(A_{k})\leqslant n^{-1/p}
. Combining
with the lower bound we obtain
\varrho(A_{k})=n^{-1/p}
for each
k
, and hence

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\leqslant\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\varrho(A_{k})=n^{-1/p}.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Specialising to the counting measure on
\mathbb{N}
we recover the same upper
bound for the sequence spaces
\ell_{p}
.

The next simple observation records the monotonicity of the covering index
with respect to contractively complemented subspaces.

Lemma 3.3

(Monotonicity for contractively complemented subspaces)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
P\colon X\to X
be a contrative projection. Set
Y:=P(X)
.
Then, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Theta_{Y}(n)\ \leqslant\ \Theta_{X}(n).

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\varepsilon>0
. Choose a covering

B_{Y}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}B_{k}

by sets
B_{k}\in\mathcal{C}(Y)
such that

\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\varrho_{Y}(B_{k})\leqslant\Theta_{Y}(n)+\varepsilon.

Define, for each
k=1,\dots,n
,

A_{k}:=\{x\in B_{X}:\ Px\in B_{k}\}.

Each
A_{k}
is closed, convex and bounded in
X
. Moreover, since

\|P\|\leqslant 1
, we have
P(B_{X})\subset B_{Y}
. Hence, for every
x\in B_{X}
there
exists
k
such that
Px\in B_{k}
; that is,
x\in A_{k}
. Thus

B_{X}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}A_{k}.

We claim that
\varrho_{Y}(B_{k})\leqslant\varrho_{X}(A_{k})
for each
k
. Fix
k
and
let
r<\varrho_{Y}(B_{k})
. By the definition of the essential inradius there
exist
y\in B_{k}
and a finite-codimensional subspace
Z\leqslant Y
such that

y+r(B_{Y}\cap Z)\subset B_{k}.

Set

W:=P^{-1}(Z)=\{x\in X:\ Px\in Z\}.

Then
W
is a closed subspace of
X
, and the quotient map

X/W\to Y/Z
given by
[x]\mapsto[Px]
is a well-defined linear isomorphism.
In particular,
X/W
is finite dimensional, so
W
has finite codimension
in
X
.

Now take
x:=y\in Y\subset X
. If
w\in B_{X}\cap W
, then
Pw\in Z
and

\|Pw\|\leqslant\|w\|\leqslant 1
, so
Pw\in B_{Y}\cap Z
and hence
y+rPw\in B_{k}
.
Therefore
x+rw\in A_{k}
for all
w\in B_{X}\cap W
, and we have

x+r(B_{X}\cap W)\subset A_{k}.

By the definition of
\varrho_{X}(A_{k})
this shows that
r\leqslant\varrho_{X}(A_{k})
.
Since this holds for every
r<\varrho_{Y}(B_{k})
, we conclude that

\varrho_{Y}(B_{k})\leqslant\varrho_{X}(A_{k})
.

Consequently,

\Theta_{X}(n)+\varepsilon\geqslant\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\varrho_{X}(A_{k})\geqslant\max_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\varrho_{Y}(B_{k})\geqslant\Theta_{Y}(n).

As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this yields
\Theta_{Y}(n)\leqslant\Theta_{X}(n)
.
∎

Combining Proposition
3.1
with Raja’s general lower bound gives matching power-type lower estimates for scalar
L_{p}(\mu)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and suppose
L_{p}(\mu)
is infinite-dimensional. Then there
exists a constant
c_{p,\mu}>0
such that

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\ \geqslant\ c_{p,\mu}\,n^{-1/p}\qquad(n\geqslant 1).

In particular,

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\ \asymp\ n^{-1/p}\qquad(1<p<\infty).

Proof.

As recalled in Section
1
,
L_{p}(\mu)
is uniformly smooth
with modulus of smoothness of power type
r=\min\{p,2\}
, and admits an
equivalent
p
-AUS norm

[
1
,
6
]
. Hence
Theorem
1.6
yields a constant
c_{p,\mu}>0
such that

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\ \geqslant\ c_{p,\mu}\,n^{-1/p}\qquad(n\geqslant 1),

while Proposition
3.1
supplies the matching upper bound.
∎

Remark 3.5

(A lower-bound version of Problem 4.7 of
[
5
]
)
.

In
[
5
, Problem 4.7]
Raja asks whether the behaviour of the
covering index
\Theta_{X}(n)
“really depends” on both the asymptotic moduli of
uniform convexity and uniform smoothness (AUC and AUS). Even without a full
upper-bound theory for vector-valued spaces
L_{p}(\mu;E)
, one can already see at
the level of
lower bounds
that
\Theta_{X}
cannot reflect the AUS/AUC
geometry in a very precise way.

Fix
1<p<\infty
and a non-atomic
\sigma
-finite measure space

(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
, and let
E
be any non-zero Banach space. Choose
e\in S_{E}
and consider the isometric embedding

J:L_{p}(\mu)\longrightarrow L_{p}(\mu;E),\qquad J(f)(\omega)=f(\omega)e.

By the Hahn–Banach theorem there exists
\varphi\in E^{*}
with
\|\varphi\|=1

and
\varphi(e)=1
. Define
P\colon L_{p}(\mu;E)\to L_{p}(\mu;E)
by

P(F)(\omega):=\varphi(F(\omega))\,e,\qquad F\in L_{p}(\mu;E).

Then
P
is a norm-one projection with range
J(L_{p}(\mu))
. Hence, by
Lemma
3.3
and Corollary
3.4
, we have

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu;E)}(n)\ \geqslant\ \Theta_{J(L_{p}(\mu))}(n)\ =\ \Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\ \gtrsim\ n^{-1/p}\qquad(n\geqslant 1),

with constants depending only on
p
and
\mu
.

The range
J(L_{p}(\mu))
is a closed subspace of
L_{p}(\mu;E)
, so by
Lemma
3.3
and Corollary
3.4
we have

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu;E)}(n)\ \geqslant\ \Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\ \gtrsim\ n^{-1/p}\qquad(n\geqslant 1),

with constants depending only on
p
and
\mu
, but not on
E
.

On the other hand, the asymptotic moduli of
L_{p}(\mu;E)
can vary
dramatically with
E
. By results of Knaust–Odell–Schlumprecht and Raja
(see
[
1
,
6
]
and also the survey

[
2
]
), a separable Banach space is AUSable if and only
if its Szlenk index is at most
\omega
. There exist separable Banach spaces
with arbitrarily large Szlenk index (and hence with no equivalent AUS norm);
see, for instance, the universal examples constructed in

[
3
]
. Taking two such spaces
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
with
very different Szlenk indices (for example,
E_{1}=\ell_{2}
and
E_{2}
as in

[
3
]
), we have

\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu;E_{1})}(n)\ \gtrsim\ \Theta_{L_{p}(\mu;E_{2})}(n)\ \gtrsim\ n^{-1/p}\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

even though
L_{p}(\mu;E_{1})
admits an equivalent
p
-AUS norm, while

L_{p}(\mu;E_{2})
does not admit any AUS renorming at all.

Thus, already at the level of lower bounds, the covering index
\Theta_{X}
is
insensitive to large variations in the AUS behaviour of the coefficient space

E
, and in this sense one cannot hope for a simple correspondence between the
asymptotic moduli of
L_{p}(\mu;E)
and the precise shape of
\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu;E)}
.

4.
Non-commutative
L_{p}
-spaces of von Neumann algebras

We briefly indicate how Raja’s lower bound extends to non-commutative
L_{p}
-spaces. Throughout this section,
(M,\tau)
denotes a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
.

We recall the standard non-commutative
L_{p}
-spaces; see Pisier–Xu
[
4
]
.

Definition 4.1

(Non-commutative
L_{p}
-spaces)
.

Let
1\leqslant p<\infty
. The non-commutative
L_{p}
-space
L_{p}(M,\tau)
is defined as the completion of the set of
\tau
-measurable operators
x
affiliated with
M
for which

\|x\|_{p}:=\bigl(\tau(|x|^{p})\bigr)^{1/p}<\infty.

For
1<p<\infty
, the spaces
L_{p}(M,\tau)
are uniformly convex and uniformly smooth; non-commutative Clarkson inequalities (see
[
4
, Section 34.5]
) show that their modulus of smoothness is of power type

r=\min\{p,2\}.

In particular,
L_{p}(M,\tau)
is asymptotically uniformly smooth of power type
r
(see the appendix for details), and hence
r
-AUS.

Combining this with Theorem
1.6
yields:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra and
1<p<\infty
, and set

r=\min\{p,2\}
.
Then there exists
c_{p}(M)>0
such that

\Theta_{L_{p}(M,\tau)}(n)\ \geqslant\ c_{p}(M)\,n^{-1/r}\qquad(n\geqslant 1).

Thus the covering index of any non-commutative
L_{p}
-space decays at least like
n^{-1/r}
with
r=\min\{p,2\}
.

Remark 4.3
.

If
M=L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
and
\tau(f)=\int_{\Omega}f\,d\mu
, then
L_{p}(M,\tau)
is just
L_{p}(\mu)
and Proposition
4.2
yields a lower bound of order
n^{-1/r}
with
r=\min\{p,2\}
. Our explicit covering in the commutative case (Proposition
3.1
) shows that in this situation the optimal exponent is
1/p
, and in fact
\Theta_{L_{p}(\mu)}(n)\asymp n^{-1/p}
. Thus the abstract non-commutative argument is not sharp even in the commutative setting when
p>2
.

If
M=B(H)
with the canonical trace, then
L_{p}(M,\tau)
is the Schatten class
S_{p}
on
H
. Proposition
4.2
then shows that

\Theta_{S_{p}}(n)\ \gtrsim\ n^{-1/r},\qquad r=\min\{p,2\}.

Obtaining matching upper bounds with the optimal exponent and sharp constants in the non-commutative setting (even for Schatten classes) appears to be an open problem.

Appendix A
Non-commutative Clarkson inequalities and AUS for
L_{p}(M,\tau)

Let
(M,\tau)
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal
semifinite trace, and let
L_{p}(M,\tau)
be the associated non-commutative

L_{p}
-space as in
[
4
]
. Pisier and Xu prove that analogues of
the Clarkson inequalities hold in this setting; see in particular

[
4
, Section 34.5]
. In instance, for
1<p<\infty
, the space

L_{p}(M,\tau)
is uniformly convex and uniformly smooth, and its modulus of
smoothness satisfies

\rho_{L_{p}(M,\tau)}(t)\ \leqslant\ C_{p}\,t^{r},\qquad r=\min\{p,2\},

for all
t>0
, with a constant
C_{p}
depending only on
p
(and not on

M
or
\tau
). Thus non-commutative
L_{p}
-spaces enjoy global uniform
smoothness of some power type
r>1
.

By Remark
1.4
, any uniform smoothness estimate of power type for
\rho_{X}
immediately yields an asymptotic uniform smoothness estimate of the same power type for
\overline{\rho}_{X}
.

Applying this observation to
X=L_{p}(M,\tau)
, we conclude that, for
1<p<\infty
,

\overline{\rho}_{L_{p}(M,\tau)}(t)\ \leqslant\ C_{p}\,t^{r},\qquad r=\min\{p,2\},

and hence
L_{p}(M,\tau)
is asymptotically uniformly smooth of power type
r>1
. In particular, the natural norm on
L_{p}(M,\tau)
is
r
-AUS with
r=\min\{p,2\}
. This is the input used in the proof of Proposition
4.2
: once
L_{p}(M,\tau)
is
r
-AUS, Raja’s general theorem (Theorem
1.6
) yields a power-type lower bound

\Theta_{L_{p}(M,\tau)}(n)\ \gtrsim\ n^{-1/r}.

Determining the
precise
optimal AUS exponent and the corresponding sharp covering-index decay rate for specific non-commutative
L_{p}
-spaces remains an interesting open problem, even for classical examples such as the Schatten classes
S_{p}=L_{p}(B(H),\mathrm{Tr})
.

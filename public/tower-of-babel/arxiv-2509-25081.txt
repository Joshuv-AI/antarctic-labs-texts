Steady Gradient Ricci Solitons with O ⁡ ( p ) × O ⁡ ( q ) O(p)\times O(q) Symmetry
Lucas Lavoyer, Luke T. Peachey

Abstract
. We find new examples of steady gradient Ricci solitons with positive curvature operator in dimensions four and above. Utilising a procedure first introduced by Lai, we construct examples with O ⁡ ( p ) × O ⁡ ( q ) O(p)\times O(q) symmetry in dimension p + q p+q for any pair of integers p , q ≥ 2 p,q\geq 2 and discuss their asymptotic geometry.



1.
Introduction

Ricci solitons correspond to Ricci flows which are self-similar modulo scaling and reparameterisation. Amongst them, steady Ricci solitons correspond to those invariant under reparameterisation only and can be used to model the formation of Type II singularities under the Ricci flow
[
Ham95
]
. A triple
(M,g,f)
consisting of a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and a smooth function
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called a steady gradient Ricci soliton if

(1.1)

{\mathrm{Ric}}(g)=\nabla^{2}f.

In this case, the steady soliton structure naturally induces an eternal Ricci flow on
M,

g(t)=\phi_{t}^{*}g
, where
\phi_{t}
is the flow of the vector field
-\nabla f
and
t\in(-\infty,\infty).

Examples of Ricci solitons can be found by imposing symmetry ansatz. The strongest symmetry assumption one can make is rotational symmetry, which reduces (
1.1
) to an ODE. The only non-flat rotationally symmetric steady gradient Ricci soliton is Hamilton’s cigar soliton in dimension two
[
Ham88
]
, and Bryant’s example in dimension three or higher
[
Bry05
]
. One may instead impose weaker symmetry assumptions. Lai constructed the first examples of
O(1)\times O(n-1)
symmetric steady gradient Ricci solitons with positive curvature operator in dimensions three and above
[
Lai24
]
, solving a conjecture of Hamilton. These solitons are referred to as flying wings due to their shape at infinity, as the link of their asymptotic cone is an interval of length
\alpha\in(0,\pi).
It is a consequence of their asymptotic geometry that these flying wings are collapsed in dimension three and non-collapsed in dimensions four and above.

More generally, one may consider the symmetry ansatz of the group
O(p)\times O(q)
for any pair of integers
p,q\geq 2
in dimension
p+q\geq 4
. If
(M^{p+q},g,f)
denotes a complete steady gradient Ricci soliton with
O(p)\times O(q)
symmetry, the isotropy representation at the unique fixed point of the action, denoted by
o
, decomposes the tangent space into two irreducible subspaces. We denote by
\lambda_{p}
and
\lambda_{q}
the sectional curvatures of any plane contained entirely within one of these subspaces (see Section
2
for more details).

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, there exists a steady gradient Ricci soliton
\mathcal{S}^{p,q}_{\theta}
of dimension
p+q
with positive curvature operator and
O(p)\times O(q)
symmetry, such that the invariant sectional curvatures satisfy
\lambda_{p}=\theta\cdot\lambda_{q}
at the vertex
o
.

Remark 1.2
.

If
B^{n}
denotes Bryant’s rotationally symmetric soliton for
n\geq 3
and Hamilton’s cigar soliton when
n=2
, then for any pair
p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
, we can view the
1
-parameter family of solitons
\theta\mapsto\mathcal{S}^{p,q}_{\theta}
as interpolating between
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\times B^{q}
as
\theta\downarrow 0
, and
B^{p+q}
as
\theta\uparrow 1
.

As with flying wings, we wish to understand the geometry at infinity for these solitons. We know that the ideal boundary of our Ricci soliton also admits
O(p)\times O(q)
symmetry, from which we can deduce that it has the structure of a (not necessarily smooth) doubly warped product (see Theorem
5.1
). This structure leaves only a few possibilities for its topology. It would be interesting to check if the orbits of the
O(p)\times\{1_{q}\}
subgroup in the ideal boundary are non-collapsed, in which case the ideal boundary would be homeomorphic to a closed disk of dimension
p
(see Remark
5.2
).

In fact, assuming the above to be true, we obtain some extra information on the asymptotic geometry. It is known from
[
CMZ25
]
that non-collapsed steady gradient Ricci solitons with
\operatorname{sec}\geq 0
always dimension reduce at infinity. In our case, following points going to infinity along the totally geodesic submanifold fixed by the action of the
\{1_{p}\}\times O(q)
subgroup, we show that we can split a
p
-dimensional Euclidean factor under the same rescaling.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, let
\mathcal{S}^{p,q}_{\theta}=(M,g,f,o)
denote the steady Ricci soliton constructed in Theorem
1.1
. If we assume the ideal boundary
\mathcal{S}^{p,q}_{\theta}(\infty)
to be homeomorphic to a closed disk of dimension
p
as in Remark
5.2
, then for any divergent sequence of points
x_{m}
fixed by the action of the
\{1_{p}\}\times O(q)
subgroup, the rescaled sequence
(M,\mathcal{R}_{g}(x_{m})\cdot g(\mathcal{R}_{g}(x_{m})^{-1}t),x_{m})
converges locally smoothly along a subsequence to the product of flat Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
with a rotationally symmetric ancient Ricci flow with positive curvature operator
(N^{q},g_{N}(t),x_{\infty})
.

Remark 1.4
.

As a corollary to the theorem above, by following the same argument as in
[
Lai24
, Corollary 3.5]
we would find that the solitons are non-collapsed if
q\geq 3
.

Outline.
In Section
2
we introduce terminology and state useful results on gradient Ricci solitons, as well as from the theory of Alexandrov spaces and manifolds with symmetry. Sections
3
and
4
contain the proof of Theorem
1.1
which we briefly explain here in more detail. In
[
Lai24
]
, Lai observed that given a family of expanding solitons with positive curvature operator asymptotic to cones with smooth links, as in
[
Der16
]
(see also Theorem
2.4
), if the asymptotic volume ratio (
\operatorname{AVR}
) is going to zero along the sequence, by performing a blow-up analysis on the points of highest curvature, one extracts a steady Ricci soliton. The
\operatorname{AVR}
going to zero is obtained by finding a suitable family of collapsing metrics on the sphere; it is not hard to see that if the volume of the links of the corresponding asymptotic cones goes to zero along the sequence, so does the asymptotic volume ratio of the expanding solitons. One of the main steps is therefore finding a family of asymptotic cones with asymptotic volume ratios going to zero which can be smoothed out by Deruelle’s expanders. In Section
3
, we carefully construct metrics on the sphere that satisfy these requirements and are, additionally,
O(p)\times O(q)
symmetric. We complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section
4
. Finally, in Section
5
we study the geometry at infinity of the steady solitons we have constructed and give a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Preliminaries

We collect here some well known results that will be useful later. We begin with a brief discussion on gradient Ricci solitons, with emphasis on the expanding and steady cases.

Definition 2.1
.

A triple
(M,g,f)
consisting of a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and a smooth function
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
, referred to as the soliton’s potential function, is called a gradient Ricci soliton if it satisfies the equation

(2.1)

{\mathrm{Ric}}(g)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot g=\nabla^{2}f,

for some
\varepsilon\in\{-1,0,1\}
. If
\varepsilon=1
(
\varepsilon=0
), we say that
(M,g)
is an expanding (steady) gradient Ricci soliton.

From now on, we assume
(M,g,f)
is an expanding or steady gradient Ricci soliton with positive Ricci curvature. Equation (
2.1
) then implies that
f
is a strictly convex function and hence has a unique critical point
o
which we refer to as the
vertex
of the soliton. We will often use the quadruple
(M,g,f,o)
when representing the soliton to emphasize the vertex. By the addition of a constant we can always ensure that
f\geq 0
and
f(o)=0
. The following soliton identities can be found in
[
CPN06
,
CD15
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,o)
be a steady or expanding gradient Ricci soliton with positive Ricci curvature. If we assume the normalisation that
f(o)=0
, then the following identities hold:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{g}+\frac{n}{2}=\Delta f,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{g}+|\nabla f|^{2}-f=\sup_{M}\mathcal{R}_{g}=\mathcal{R}_{g}(o),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nabla\mathcal{R}_{g}+2{\mathrm{Ric}}(\nabla f)=0,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}d_{g}^{2}(o,\cdot)\leq f\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(d_{g}(o,\cdot)+2\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_{g}(o)}\right)^{2}

where
\mathcal{R}_{g}
denotes the scalar curvature.

The following lemma relates the scalar curvature at the vertex with the asymptotic volume ratio. See
[
CN09
, Corollary 1.2]
,
[
CMZ22
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For an expanding gradient Ricci soliton
(M,g,f,o)
with positive Ricci curvature, we have the inequality

(2.6)

\frac{\textnormal{vol}(B(o,\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_{g}(o)})}{(\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_{g}(o)})^{n}}\leq 4^{n}\cdot\textnormal{AVR}(g).

Proof.

Normalising so that
f(o)=0
and using (
2.5
), for every
s\geq\mathcal{R}(o)
we have the inclusions

(2.7)

\displaystyle B\left(o,2\sqrt{s}-2\mathcal{R}(o)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\subseteq\{f\leq s\}\subseteq B\left(o,2\sqrt{s}\right),

and hence

\textnormal{AVR}(g)=\lim_{s\uparrow\infty}\frac{\textnormal{vol}(\{f\leq s\})}{2^{n}\cdot s^{\frac{n}{2}}}.

Using the soliton equations and the co-area formula we have that

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\{f\leq s\})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{f\leq s\}}\Delta fd\mu=\int_{\{f=s\}}|\nabla f|d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq(s+\mathcal{R}_{g}(o))\int_{\{f=s\}}|\nabla f|^{-1}d\sigma

\displaystyle=(s+\mathcal{R}_{g}(o))\frac{d}{ds}\operatorname{vol}(\{f\leq s\}),

which when integrated between
0<r<s<\infty
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\{f\leq s\})}{\operatorname{vol}(\{f\leq r\})}\geq\exp\left(\frac{n}{2}\cdot\int_{r}^{s}(x+\mathcal{R}_{g}(o))^{-1}dx\right)=\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}_{g}(o)+s}{\mathcal{R}_{g}(o)+r}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Combining this with the formula for the asymptotic volume ratio and taking
s\uparrow\infty
gives

(2.8)

\frac{\textnormal{vol}(\{f\leq r\})}{\left(r+\mathcal{R}_{g}(o)\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\leq 2^{n}\cdot\textnormal{AVR}(g).

Finally, setting
r=\frac{9}{4}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{g}(o)
in (
2.8
) and using the inclusion (
2.7
), we have (
2.6
).
∎

The following result of Deruelle
[
Der16
]
shows the existence of Ricci expanders resolving metric cones with positive curvature operator. Moreover, these Ricci expanders are unique within a suitable class of smoothly asymptotically conical Ricci expanders (see
[
Der16
, Definition 1.2]
for a precise definition of an expander being smoothly asymptotic to a metric cone).

Theorem 2.4
.

(Theorem 1.3,
[
Der16
]
)
Let
h
be a smooth Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with
\operatorname{Rm}(h)\geq 1.
Then there exists a unique expanding gradient Ricci soliton with non-negative curvature operator smoothly asymptotic to
(C(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),dr^{2}+r^{2}h,\tfrac{r}{2}\partial_{r}).

2.1.
Metric geometry

Here we recall here some basic facts from Alexandrov geometry. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
BBI01
]
. For a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with non-negative sectional curvature, let
xyz
be a geodesic triangle in
M
and consider the corresponding comparison triangle
\overline{xyz}
in the Euclidean plane formed by sides of length
d_{g}(x,y),d_{g}(y,z)
and
d_{g}(x,z)
. Denote by
\measuredangle xyz
the angle at
y
inside
xyz
and
\widetilde{\measuredangle}xyz
the angle at
\overline{y}
in the comparison triangle
\overline{xyz}
, for which
\widetilde{\measuredangle}xyz\leq\measuredangle xyz
.

Fix
o\in M
and denote by
\Gamma_{o}
the collection of unit speed geodesic rays
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M
emanating from
o=\gamma(0)
. Given a pair of geodesic rays
\gamma,\sigma\in\Gamma_{o}
, the comparison angle
s\mapsto\widetilde{\measuredangle}\gamma(s)o\sigma(s)
is decreasing, and in particular the limit

\lim_{s\to\infty}\widetilde{\measuredangle}\gamma(s)o\sigma(s)\in[0,\pi]

exists and is the angle at infinity between
\gamma
and
\sigma
. The ideal boundary
M(\infty)
is then the space of equivalence classes of rays in
\Gamma_{o}
equipped with this metric, and forms a compact length space. The asymptotic cone is a metric cone over the ideal boundary, and it is isometric to the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff limit of any blow-down sequence of the manifold. The asymptotic cone being non-negatively curved can then be characterised by a lower bound on the curvature of the ideal boundary in the Alexandrov sense.

Proposition 2.5
.

(
[
BGP92
, Prop. 4.2.3]
)
Let
X
be a complete metric space. Then
X
is an Alexandrov space of curvature
\geq 1
if and only if the metric cone
C(X)
is not a line and is an Alexandrov space of curvature
\geq 0.

For each geodesic ray
\gamma\in\Gamma_{o}
, we may also consider its associated Busemann function

B_{\gamma}(x):=\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}t-d(x,\gamma(t)),\quad\textnormal{ for each }x\in M.

Taking the supremum over all such rays
B:=\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}}B_{\gamma}
gives the Busemann function about
o
, which is a well-defined convex function
[
CG72
, Proposition 1.3]
whose level sets are convex hypersurfaces, which are homeomorphic to codimension one spheres in the case that the sectional curvature is positive
[
GK97
, Proposition 2.4.3]
. The following result of Guijarro and Kapovitch states that rescaled level sets of the Busemann function equipped with their intrinsic metric converge to the ideal boundary.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
GK97
, Lemma 3.6]
)
.

For each
s>0
, let
i_{s}
denote the intrinsic metric on
\{B=s\}
viewed as a subset of
M
. Then the rescaled level sets

\left(\{B=s\},s^{-1}\cdot i_{s}\right)\rightarrow M(\infty),

in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense as
s\uparrow\infty
.

2.2.
Symmetric Manifolds

For any
p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
, consider the natural action of
O(p)\times O(q)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{q}={\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}.
We refer to this action as the standard action of
O(p)\times O(q)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}
.

Definition 2.7
.

A Riemannian manifold
(M^{p+q},g)
is
O(p)\times O(q)
symmetric if it admits an isometric
O(p)\times O(q)
action and there exists a diffeomorphism
M\to{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}
which is equivariant with respect to its standard action on
{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}
.

If a Riemannian manifold is
O(p)\times O(q)
symmetric, we denote by
\Sigma_{p}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
and
\Sigma_{q}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
the totally geodesic submanifolds given by the fixed points of the subgroups
\{1_{p}\}\times O(q)
and
O(p)\times\{1_{q}\}
respectively. Away from these points, our group action is free, and hence we have a Riemannian submersion into the orbit space. If
(M,g,f,o)
is an expanding or steady gradient Ricci soliton with
\operatorname{Rm}(g)>0,
where
o
is the unique critical point of the potential function, and additionally, if the soliton is
O(p)\times O(q)
symmetric, then the vertex
o
is the unique fixed point by the action. We can therefore decompose the tangent space
T_{o}M=T_{o}\Sigma_{p}\oplus T_{o}\Sigma_{q}.

Following the discussion above, we may consider the sectional curvature of a
2-
plane
\pi_{p}\subset T_{o}\Sigma_{p}
(respectively
\pi_{q}\subset T_{o}\Sigma_{q}
). Theorem
1.1
then says that for any
\pi_{p}\subset T_{o}\Sigma_{p},

\pi_{q}\subset T_{o}\Sigma_{q},
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have

\operatorname{sec}(\pi_{p})=\lambda_{p}=\theta\cdot\lambda_{q}=\theta\cdot\operatorname{sec}(\pi_{q}).

Finally, given a function
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
and a compact Lie group
G
acting on
M,
we can define the
G-
average of
f
as follows.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group acting smoothly by isometries on a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. For any function
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we define its
G-
average by

f_{G}(x):=\int_{G}f(\rho\cdot x)\ d\mu_{G}(\rho),

where
d\mu_{G}
denotes the unit (left) Haar measure on
G
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group acting smoothly by isometries on a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. If a function
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
\lambda-
concave, then its
G-
average is also
\lambda-
concave.

Proof.

For any unit speed geodesic
\gamma:I\to M
and
\rho\in G
, its image
\rho\cdot\gamma
remains a unit speed geodesic, and hence

\displaystyle(f_{G}\circ\gamma)^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}(f\circ(\rho\cdot\gamma))^{\prime\prime}(t)\ d\mu_{G}(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\lambda\ d\mu_{G}(\rho)=\lambda,

where in the second inequality we applied the definition of
f
being
\lambda-
concave.
∎

3.
Smooth Collapsing Links

The purpose of this section is to construct a continuous family of smooth
O(p)\times O(q)
symmetric metrics on the sphere which have uniformly positive curvature operator, but which collapse to a upper hemisphere of lower dimension. These will be used later as the links of metric cones which we will resolve by gradient Ricci expanders.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
, there exists a continuous family of smooth
O(p)\times O(q)
symmetric metrics on the sphere
({\mathbb{S}}^{p+q-1},h(t))_{t\in(0,1]}
such that

(i)

The curvature operator
{\mathrm{Rm}}(h(t))\geq 1
for any
t\in(0,1]
;

(ii)

({\mathbb{S}}^{p+q-1},h(t))\rightarrow{\mathbb{S}}_{+}^{p}
in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense as
t\downarrow 0
.

To understand this theorem, consider the round sphere as a doubly warped product

{\mathbb{S}}^{p+q-1}\cong\left[0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right]\times_{\cos(r)}{{\mathbb{S}}^{p-1}}\times_{\sin(r)}{\mathbb{S}}^{q-1}.

For each
t\in(0,1]
modify the warping function on the
{\mathbb{S}}^{q-1}
fibre by a factor of
t
, so that as
t\downarrow 0
, these singular metrics collapse to
\left[0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right]\times_{\cos(r)}{{\mathbb{S}}^{p-1}}\cong{\mathbb{S}}^{p}_{+}
. The desired family of metrics are given by suitable smoothing and rescaling of these singular metrics. Thus in order to prove Theorem
3.1
we need a smooth warping function interpolating between
\sin(r)
and
t\cdot\sin(r)
which preserves the lower curvature bound.

To begin, we construct a suitable family of smooth approximations to the Heaviside function.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a continuous family of smooth functions
\{\chi_{\delta}:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,1]\}_{\delta\in(0,1]}
with the following properties

(i)

For each
\delta\in(0,1]
,
\chi_{\delta}(\cdot)
is a decreasing function.

(ii)

For each
\delta\in(0,1]
, there exists
\eta_{\delta}>0
such that

\chi_{\delta}(x)=\begin{cases}1&:x<\eta_{\delta}\\
0&:x>\frac{\pi}{2}-\eta_{\delta}\end{cases}.

(iii)

We have pointwise convergence to the Heaviside function,
\chi_{\delta}\rightarrow\mathbbm{1}_{\{\leq 0\}}
, as
\delta\downarrow 0
.

(iv)

We have the following integral decay of the weighted derivative

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\sup_{r\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})}\frac{\int_{0}^{r}-\chi_{\delta}^{\prime}(x)\sin(x)dx}{\sin(r)}=0.

Proof.

Begin by defining smooth increasing functions
\beta,\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,\infty)
such that

•

\beta(x)=\gamma(x)=0
for
x\leq 0
;

•

\beta(x)\geq x
and
\gamma(x)=1
for
x\geq\frac{1}{2}
;

•

\beta^{\prime}(x),\beta^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

For each
\delta\in(0,1]
, we then reparameterise these functions as

\beta_{\delta}(x):=\beta\left(\frac{x^{\delta}}{\delta}-1\right),\quad\gamma_{\delta}(x):=\gamma\left(1+\frac{x}{\delta}-\frac{\pi}{2\delta}\right),

and define

\chi_{\delta}(x):=(1-\gamma_{\delta}(x))\exp(-\beta_{\delta}(x)),\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Since
\beta
and
\gamma
are increasing, (
i
) follows immediately. Moreover

•

\gamma_{\delta}(x)=1
for
x\geq\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\delta}{2}
;

•

\gamma_{\delta}(x)=0
for
x\leq\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta
;

•

\beta_{\delta}(x)=0
for
x\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{\delta}}
.

Thus (
ii
) follows with
\eta_{\delta}:=\min\{\delta^{\frac{1}{\delta}},\frac{\delta}{2}\}
. Also, (
iii
) is an immediate consequence of the fact that
\frac{x^{\delta}}{\delta}\uparrow\infty
as
\delta\downarrow 0
for any
x>0
. It remains to show (
iv
). We first note that

0\leq-\chi_{\delta}^{\prime}(x)\leq(\gamma_{\delta}^{\prime}(x)+\beta_{\delta}^{\prime}(x))\exp(-\beta_{\delta}(x)),

and

\displaystyle\limsup_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{\int_{0}^{r}\gamma_{\delta}^{\prime}(x)\exp(-\beta_{\delta}(x))\sin(x)dx}{\sin(r)}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\delta\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{r}\gamma_{\delta}^{\prime}(x)\exp(-\beta_{\delta}(x))dx

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\delta\downarrow 0}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\gamma_{\delta}^{\prime}(x)\exp(-\beta_{\delta}(x))dx

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\delta\downarrow 0}\exp(-\beta_{\delta}(\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta))=0.

Therefore, we may assume for a contradiction that there are sequences
\delta_{j}\downarrow 0
and
r_{j}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
satisfying a uniform lower bound

(3.1)

\frac{\int_{0}^{r_{j}}\beta_{\delta_{j}}^{\prime}(x)\exp(-\beta_{\delta_{j}}(x))\sin(x)dx}{\sin(r_{j})}\geq c>0.

Using the definition of
\beta_{\delta}
, it follows that

R_{j}:=\frac{r_{j}^{\delta_{j}}}{\delta_{j}}-1>0,

and so after potentially passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
R_{j}\rightarrow R\in[0,\infty]
. Utilising the change of variable
y=\frac{x^{\delta_{j}}}{\delta_{j}}-1
, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r_{j}}\beta_{\delta_{j}}^{\prime}(x)\exp(-\beta_{\delta_{j}}(x))\sin(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta_{j}^{\frac{1}{\delta_{j}}}}^{r_{j}}\beta_{\delta}^{\prime}(x)\exp(-\beta_{\delta}(x))\sin(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R_{j}}\beta^{\prime}(y)\exp(-\beta(y))\sin\left(\left(\delta_{j}(y+1)\right)^{1/\delta_{j}}\right)dy,

and hence (
3.1
) becomes

(3.2)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta^{\prime}(y)\exp(-\beta(y))f_{j}(y)dy\geq c,

where
f_{j}:(0,\infty)\to[0,1]
is the measurable function

f_{j}(y):=\frac{\sin\left(\left(\delta_{j}(y+1)\right)^{1/\delta_{j}}\right)}{\sin(r_{j})}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{(0,R_{j})}(y).

It follows that
f_{j}(y)\downarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
for almost every
y>0
. Indeed, in the case
R=0
there is nothing to show, so we may assume
R\in(0,\infty]
. If
y>R
, then
f_{j}(y)
is eventually zero by virtue of the indicator function. If
0<y<R
, for
j
sufficiently large,
r_{j}>\left(\delta_{j}(y+\eta+1)\right)^{1/\delta_{j}}
for some
\eta>0
, which when combined with
x/2\leq\sin(x)\leq x
for small positive
x
yields

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\uparrow\infty}f_{j}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{j\uparrow\infty}\frac{\sin\left(\left(\delta_{j}(y+1)\right)^{1/\delta_{j}}\right)}{\sin\left(\left(\delta_{j}(y+\eta+1)\right)^{1/\delta_{j}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{j\uparrow\infty}2\left(\frac{y+1}{y+\eta+1}\right)^{1/\delta_{j}}=0.

Finally, using the fact that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta^{\prime}(y)\exp(-\beta(y))dy=1,

the dominated convergence theorem implies that the ratio on the left hand side of
(
3.2
)
converges to zero. This is a contradiction and therefore (
iv
) is true.
∎

We may now use the smooth approximations of the Heaviside function from the previous lemma to construct an appropriate family of warping functions.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a continuous family of smooth functions
\{\varphi_{t}:[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]\to[0,1]\}_{t\in(0,1]}
with the following properties

(1)

\varphi_{t}^{(even)}(0)=0,\ \varphi_{t}^{\prime}(0)=1,\ \varphi_{t}^{(odd)}(\pi/2)=0,\ \varphi_{t}(\pi/2)>0.

(2)

\varphi_{t}(r)\leq 3t\cdot\sin(r),\quad\forall r\in(t,\frac{\pi}{2}].

(3)

\frac{-\varphi_{t}^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi_{t}}\geq\tan\cdot\frac{\varphi_{t}^{\prime}}{\varphi_{t}}\geq\max\{t,1-t\}.

Proof.

Consider the family of smooth approximations of the Heaviside function
\{\chi_{\delta}\}_{\delta\in(0,1]}
constructed in Lemma
3.2
. It follows immediately from properties (
iii
) and (
iv
) of these functions that there exists a continuous decreasing function
\delta:(0,1]\to(0,1]
such that

(a)

\chi_{\delta(t)}(t)\leq t
for every
t\in(0,1]
;

(b)

\int_{0}^{r}-\chi_{\delta(t)}^{\prime}(x)\sin(x)dx\leq\min\{\frac{t^{2}}{1-t},t\}\cdot\sin(r)
, for every
t\in(0,1]
and
r\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
.

From now on we use the shorthand
\chi_{t}:=\chi_{\delta(t)}
. Consider the interpolation

\varphi_{t}(r):=\int_{0}^{r}t\cos(x)+(1-t)\cdot\chi_{t}(x)\cos(x)\ dx.

It is straightforward that
t\sin(r)\leq\varphi_{t}(r)\leq\sin(r)
. By (
ii
) of Lemma
3.2
,
\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(r)=\cos(r)
for

in a neighbourhood of
0

\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(r)=t\cdot\cos(r)
for

in a neighbourhood of
\frac{\pi}{2}
. This gives us the boundary data (
1

\chi_{t}
is decreasing

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(r)

\displaystyle=\tan(r)\cdot\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(r)-(1-t)\int_{0}^{r}\chi_{t}^{\prime}(x)\sin(x)dx\geq\tan(r)\cdot\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(r),

\displaystyle-\varphi_{t}^{\prime\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\tan(r)\cdot\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(r)-(1-t)\chi^{\prime}_{t}(r)\cos(r)\geq\tan(r)\cdot\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(r).

Together these yield the lower bound

\frac{-\varphi_{t}^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\varphi_{t}(r)}\geq\tan(r)\cdot\frac{\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(r)}{\varphi_{t}(r)}\geq 1-\frac{(1-t)}{t}\cdot\frac{\int_{0}^{r}-\chi_{t}^{\prime}(x)\sin(x)dx}{\sin(r)}.

Using (

3

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\tan(r)\cdot\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(r)+\int_{0}^{r}-\chi_{t}^{\prime}(x)\sin(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq 3t\sin(r),

for any

b
), and the third inequality we used (

∎

4.
Constructing the Steady solitons

Recall that any metric cone whose link is a sphere
({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1},h)
with
{\mathrm{Rm}}(h)\geq 1
can be uniquely resolved by an expander with non-negative curvature operator. The following lemma, originally due to Lai in the case of

[

, Lemma 2.3]
, shows that along a sequence of expanders whose links have collapsing volume, we can extract a steady soliton.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X_{m}:=({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1},h_{m})
denote a sequence of smooth metrics, such that

(1)

The curvature operator
{\mathrm{Rm}}(h_{m})\geq 1
;

(2)

\Vol_{h_{m}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})\downarrow 0
as
m\rightarrow\infty
.

Denote by
(M_{m},g_{m},f_{m},o_{m})
the expanding Ricci soliton resolving the metric cone
C(X_{m})
. After potentially passing to a subsequence, we have the smooth local convergence of the rescaled expanders

(M_{m},\mathcal{R}_{g_{m}}(o_{m})\cdot g_{m},f_{m},o_{m})\xrightarrow[C^{\infty}_{loc}]{m\rightarrow\infty}(M,g,f,o),

where
(M,g,f)
is a steady gradient Ricci soliton, and
o
denotes a critical point of the potential function
f
. Furthermore, if for some compact Lie group
G
, the links
X_{m}
admit faithful isometric
G
-actions, then the steady soliton
(M,g,f)
is
G
-symmetric.

Proof.

Since the rescaled expanders are complete with positive sectional curvature bounded above by one, by a result of Toponogov (see e.g.
[
BZ77
]
) we have a uniform lower injectivity radius bound at each vertex
o_{m}
. Coupled with the global curvature bounds, by standard compactness theory a subsequence converges in a locally smooth sense to some complete Riemannian manifold

(M_{m},\mathcal{R}_{g_{m}}(o_{m})\cdot g_{m},o_{m})\xrightarrow[C^{\infty}_{loc}]{m\rightarrow\infty}(M,g,o).

Claim 4.2
.

\mathcal{R}_{g_{m}}(o_{m})\uparrow\infty
as
m\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof of Claim.

If not, then after passing to a subsequence we have an upper bound
\mathcal{R}_{g_{m}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(o_{m})\leq C<\infty
. Coupled with Bishop–Gromov, the smooth local convergence and (
2.6
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{vol}(B(o,C))}{C^{n}}=\lim_{m\uparrow\infty}\frac{\textnormal{vol}(B(o_{m},C))}{C^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{m\uparrow\infty}\frac{\textnormal{vol}(B(o_{m},\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_{g_{m}}(o_{m})}))}{(\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_{g_{m}}(o_{m})})^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{m\uparrow\infty}4^{n}\cdot\textnormal{AVR}(g_{m}),

which contradicts our assumption that the asymptotic volume ratio of the expanders decays to zero.
∎

Since the rescaled metrics
\tilde{g}_{m}:=\mathcal{R}_{g_{m}}(o_{m})\cdot g_{m}
solve the equation

(4.1)

{\mathrm{Ric}}(\tilde{g}_{m})+\frac{1}{2\mathcal{R}_{g_{m}}(o_{m})}\tilde{g}_{m}=\nabla^{2}f_{m},

the local smooth convergence implies that the potential functions
f_{m}
converge locally smoothly to a function

on
M
, and hence
(M,g,f)
satisfies the steady gradient Ricci soliton equation. It also follows trivially from the smooth convergence that
\nabla f(o)=0
. Furthermore, if the links admit some faithful isometric
G
-action, then by the uniqueness in Theorem
2.4
, the expanders resolving them must be
G
-symmetric. The symmetry of the limiting steady soliton then follows immediately from the locally smooth convergence.
∎

Geometry at infinity

There exists a sequence of smooth

We can restate this convergence in terms of comparison angles

\begin{cases}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\measuredangle}y_{m,j}^{+}x_{m}y_{m,j}^{-}=\pi&:\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,p\},\\
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\measuredangle}y_{m,j}^{\pm}x_{m}y_{m,k}^{\pm}=\frac{\pi}{2}&:\forall k\neq j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}.\\
\end{cases}

In particular, arguing the same way as in
[
Zhu23
, Lemma 4.4]
, the geodesics joining
x_{m}
to
y_{m,j}^{\pm}
converge pointwise to some geodesic rays
\sigma_{j}^{\pm}:[0,\infty)\to(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(0))
emanating from
\sigma_{j}^{\pm}(0)=x_{\infty}
, for
j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Moreover, these rays are fixed by the action of the
\{1_{p}\}\times O(q)
subgroup, and it follows from the previous inequalities that

\begin{cases}\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\measuredangle}\sigma_{j}^{+}(s)x_{\infty}\sigma_{j}^{-}(s)=\pi&:\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,p\},\\
\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\measuredangle}\sigma_{j}^{\pm}(s)x_{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{\pm}(s)=\frac{\pi}{2}&:\forall k\neq j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}.\\
\end{cases}

Therefore, the asymptotic cone of
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(0))
splits
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
, and hence
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(0))
itself must split
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
by the splitting theorem. By the strong maximum principle, the Ricci flow splits
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
at all times. The symmetry and positivity of the curvature of
g_{N}(t)
then follows trivially from the smooth convergence.
∎

We have shown the appropriate splitting along our divergent sequence of points up to rescaling by the volume scale. By using the symmetry, we claim that the volume scale and the curvature scale are in fact comparable.

Claim 5.7
.

There exists
c>0
such that
R_{m}\cdot v_{m}^{2}\geq c
, for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof of Claim.

If not, then
\mathcal{R}_{g_{\infty}}(x_{\infty})=0
in the limiting Ricci flow from Proposition
5.6
, which implies
g_{\infty}(t)
is flat by the strong maximum principle. However, since
g_{N}(t)
is
O(q)
symmetric,
({\mathbb{R}}^{p}\times N^{q},g_{\infty}(t))
must isometric to flat Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}
and we have a contradiction to
\Vol_{g_{\infty}}(B(x_{\infty},1))=\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}
.
∎

Therefore, the curvature is comparable to our volume scale along the sequence
x_{m}
and hence we may conclude that the sequence
(M,\mathcal{R}_{g}(x_{m})g(t\mathcal{R}_{g}(x_{m})^{-1}),x_{m})

also splits
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
, which finishes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

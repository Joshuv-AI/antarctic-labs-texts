Non-Abelian Symmetry Operators from Hanging Branes in 𝑨 ​ 𝒅 ​ 𝑺 𝟓 × 𝑺 𝟓 AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
Ibrahima Bah, Federico Bonetti, Mufaro Chitoto, Enoch Leung

Abstract
We investigate the holographic realization of topological operators for continuous non-Abelian symmetries in quantum field theories. As a concrete case study, we focus on Type IIB string theory on A ​ d ​ S 5 × S 5 AdS_{5}\times S^{5} which admits an S ​ O ​ ( 6 ) SO(6) isometry, dual to the R-symmetry in 4d 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 super Yang-Mills theory. We argue that symmetry operators for continuous symmetries are generally realized by bound states of D5-branes and KK monopoles hanging from the conformal boundary. Together they account for the contributions to the Gauss’ law constraints from the self-dual 5-form flux and the Einstein-Hilbert term respectively. We also demonstrate how the D5-KK bound state measures the representation of the endpoint of Wilson lines constructed by D3-branes.

Introduction

The AdS/CFT correspondence and holography have served as a powerful playground for exploring fundamental aspects of quantum field theory (QFT). For example, with the advent of generalized symmetries in QFTs, many important questions have been analyzed through their dual descriptions in string theory. In particular, for a dual holographic pair, the global symmetry of the field theory is related to the bulk gauge symmetry.

From a modern perspective, the data of symmetries is characterized by the topological sector of the field theory

VI
Fusion of Hanging Branes

So far we have picked a specific choice of maximal torus
T\cong U(1)^{3}
in order to explicitly construct a symmetry operator. It is true that such a choice exists for every element in
SO(6)
. However, to exhibit the full non-Abelian structure of the
SO(6)
symmetry, one needs to relate the D5-KK bound states constructed with respect to different choices of maximal tori. In other words, there is a change of basis involved when we try to compose the action of two symmetry operators (
37
).

For concreteness, let us fix a reference maximal torus
T_{0}
. Any given element
g\in SO(6)
is always related to some element
t\in T_{0}
by conjugation, i.e.
g=hth^{-1}
for some
h\in SO(6)
, so a symmetry operator
U_{g}
labeled by
g
can be expressed similarly to (
37
) as

U_{g}=\exp\bigg(\frac{i}{2g^{2}}\,\alpha(hmh^{-1})^{ab}\int_{M^{3}}\ast F_{ab}\bigg)\,.

(45)

Geometrically, this “rotates” the internal 2d subspace,

\Sigma^{2}\to\tfrac{1}{2}(hmh^{-1})^{ab}\Sigma^{2}_{ab}=\tfrac{1}{2}m^{ab}(h^{-1}\Sigma^{2}h)_{ab}\,.

(46)

The difference between the two expressions can be understood as active vs. passive rotations. Importantly, the coefficients
\widetilde{m}^{ab}=(hmh^{-1})^{ab}
can now be non-zero for generic
(a,b)
unlike before, so we have effectively moved out of the maximal torus
T_{0}
.

The parallel fusion
U_{g}U_{g^{\prime}}=U_{gg^{\prime}}
of two D5-KK bound states labeled by generic
g,g^{\prime}\in SO(6)
is non-commutative.
Let us see how this manifests at the level of the operators.
Using
g=\exp(\alpha\widetilde{m})
and
g^{\prime}=\exp(\alpha^{\prime}\widetilde{m}^{\prime})
, the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula gives

\displaystyle U_{g}U_{g^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg((\alpha\widetilde{m}+\alpha^{\prime}\widetilde{m}^{\prime})^{ab}X_{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\,\alpha\widetilde{m}^{ab}\alpha^{\prime}(\widetilde{m}^{\prime})^{cd}[X_{ab},X_{cd}]+\dots\bigg)

(47)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(\Big(\alpha\widetilde{m}+\alpha^{\prime}\widetilde{m}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\,[\alpha\widetilde{m},\alpha^{\prime}\widetilde{m}^{\prime}]+\dots\Big)^{ab}X_{ab}\bigg)\ ,

where we have introduced the shorthand notation
X_{ab}=\frac{i}{2g^{2}}\int_{M^{3}}\ast F_{ab}
for compactness. In the second step above, we noted that
X_{ab}
satisfies the
\mathfrak{so}(6)
algebra
[X_{ab},X_{cd}]=\tfrac{1}{2}f_{ab,cd}{}^{ef}X_{ef}
,

f_{ab,cd}{}^{ef}=8\delta_{[a}{}^{[e}\delta_{b][c}\delta_{d]}{}^{f]}
.
The commutator terms are trivial if and and only if
g
and
g^{\prime}
commute. Nonetheless, it is instructive to consider a less restrictive case where
h^{-1}h^{\prime}
is in the centralizer of
(h^{\prime})^{-1}g^{\prime}h^{\prime}
, then

U_{g}U_{g^{\prime}}=\exp\bigg(\frac{i(h(\alpha m+\alpha^{\prime}m^{\prime})h^{-1})^{ab}}{2g^{2}}\int_{M^{3}}\ast F_{ab}\bigg)\,.

(48)

By the same token, we obtain a similar fusion rule with
h\to h^{\prime}
if
h^{-1}h^{\prime}
is in the centralizer of
h^{-1}gh
.

In particular, when
m^{ab}=(m^{\prime})^{ab}
, the stringy mechanism responsible for the addition of the holonomies
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
admits a simple classical description as follows. Thanks to the surjectivity of the exponential map for
SO(6)
, we observe that (
48
) effectively describes a fusion of two D5-KK bound states sharing the same worldvolume
\gamma^{1}\times\Sigma^{2}\times M^{3}
, where
\Sigma^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\widetilde{m}^{ab}\Sigma^{2}_{ab}
. The two bound states have independent pairs of worldvolume gauge fields
(a_{1},v_{1})
and
(a_{1}^{\prime},v_{1}^{\prime})
respectively giving rise to the holonomies
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
when integrated over the arc
\gamma^{1}
. We will focus below on the Chan-Paton gauge field
a_{1}
. The argument for the KK monopole gauge field
v_{1}
is completely analogous.

Let us regard
\gamma^{1}=\Gamma\sqcup\overline{\Gamma}
as a disjoint union of two half-arcs with opposite orientations, on which the D5-brane and the
\overline{\text{D}5}
-brane in the hanging brane solution are respectively supported. The holonomy
\alpha_{\text{D5}}=\alpha_{\Gamma}-\alpha_{\overline{\Gamma}}
is then a sum of the contributions from these two components, where, say,
\alpha_{\Gamma}=\int_{\Gamma}a_{1}
. As we bring two such hanging branes towards each other, each of them (momentarily) breaks up into a D5-
\overline{\text{D}5}
pair, then the two outer components recombine to form a single hanging D5-brane supported on
\Gamma\sqcup\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}
as illustrated in Figure
3
. It has the same orientation as both of the original arcs
\gamma^{1}
and
(\gamma^{1})^{\prime}
, so the associated holonomy is
\widetilde{\alpha}=\alpha_{\Gamma}-\alpha^{\prime}_{\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}}
. To ensure the inner components also recombine in a way compatible with their orientations, we see that they must move past each other and then recombine into
\Gamma^{\prime}\sqcup\overline{\Gamma}
, with holonomy
\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}-\alpha_{\overline{\Gamma}}
.

Two hanging D5-branes sitting next to each other on
\Gamma\sqcup\overline{\Gamma}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\sqcup\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}
respectively split and recombine into two hanging D5-branes on

and

The resultant configuration is a stack of two hanging D5-branes whose effective holonomy is simply

, as desired. More precisely, the effective holonomy is that associated with the center-of-mass mode of the stack of D5-branes

\mathfrak{su}(2)
-valued gauge fields, the enhanced non-Abelian component of the Chan-Paton gauge field does not contribute to the topological coupling of interest. The detailed dynamics underlying the brane recombination mechanism outlined above will be described in

If instead the coefficients
m^{ab}
and
(m^{\prime})^{ab}
are non-zero along independent
\mathfrak{u}(1)

SO(6)
. In this sense, the two D5-KK bound states are supported on orthogonal 2d subspaces
\Sigma^{2}
. What appears as a parallel fusion in the low energy effective theory is really two non-coincident D5-KK bound states in the full 10d string theory background sharing the same external support
\gamma^{1}\times M^{3}
. In fact, this is precisely the reason why we could build a symmetry operator labeled by a generic element in the maximal torus
T_{0}
in the first place, parametrized by three angles
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})\in\mathfrak{u}(1)^{3}
. Symmetry operators “supported” along orthogonal
\mathfrak{u}(1)
directions commute, and so the associated holonomies do not directly add, but form a linear combination as in (
48
).

In the most general scenario without imposing any centralizer condition, the internal supports

\Sigma^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\widetilde{m}^{ab}\Sigma^{2}_{ab}
and
(\Sigma^{2})^{\prime}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\widetilde{m}^{\prime})^{ab}\Sigma^{2}_{ab}
of the D5-KK bound states are neither coincident nor orthogonal. Fusing them therefore involves a manipulation of the commutators
[\widetilde{m},\widetilde{m}^{\prime}]
as in (
47
), which determines the internal support of the final product when regarded as a single hanging D5-KK bound state.

VII
Discussion and Outlook

To summarize, our work serves as one of the first explicit brane realizations of continuous non-Abelian symmetry operators in holography. We showed that there is a clear geometric interpretation of the continuous operator labels in terms of the hanging D5-KK bound state. Such kind of brane configurations is in stark contrast to the standard (non-hanging) branes generating finite symmetries

Note that for finite symmetries, we typically consider branes wrapping internal homological cycles, and in most cases they commute with each other, thus making them good candidates to model Abelian symmetries. However, for continuous symmetries, our proposal is that the branes do not wrap cycles, but rather subspaces defined by the Killing vectors associated with the isometries. This is in fact a feature but not a bug, since these subspaces satisfy a non-trivial algebra that is needed precisely when the symmetry in question is non-Abelian. It will be interesting to see whether this description can be extended to non-Abelian symmetries that do not originate from isometries.

Another intriguing problem, which we have not been able to address properly in this paper, is the factor-of-2 difference in (
36
) between the holonomies arising respectively from the D5-brane and the KK monopole. Certainly such a bound state is allowed to exist, but there may exist an independent mechanism, other than the Gauss’ law constraints, to fix this ratio of holonomies.

We have restricted the scope of this paper to be a case study of Type IIB string theory on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. In an upcoming work
Bah et al.

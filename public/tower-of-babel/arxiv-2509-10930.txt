Correlated interlayer quantum Hall state in alternating twisted trilayer graphene
Dohun Kim, Gyeoul Lee, Nicolas Leconte, Seyoung Jin, Takashi Taniguchi, Kenji Watanabe, Jeil Jung, Gil Young Cho, Youngwook Kim

Abstract
Trilayer graphene offers systematic control of its electronic structure through stacking sequence and twist geometry, providing a versatile platform for correlated states. Here we report magnetotransport in alternating twisted trilayer graphene with a twist angle of about 5 ∘ 5^{\circ} . The data reveal an electron–hole asymmetry that can be captured by introducing layer-dependent potential shifts. At charge neutrality ( ν tot = 0 \nu_{\rm tot}=0 ), three low-resistance states appear, which Hartree–Fock mean-field analysis attributes to emerging spin-resolved helical edge modes resembling those of quantum spin Hall insulators. At ν tot = − 1 \nu_{\rm tot}=-1 , we also observe suppressed resistance when the middle and bottom layers are each half filled while the top layer remains inert at ν = − 2 \nu=-2 , consistent with an interlayer excitonic quantum Hall state. These results demonstrate correlated interlayer quantum Hall phases in alternating twisted trilayer graphene, encompassing spin-resolved edge transport and excitonic order.

Such spin-resolved interlayer helical edge configurations are a unique feature of ATTG, realized for example by the filling sequence
(\nu_{\rm top},\nu_{\rm mid},\nu_{\rm bot})=(\pm 1,\pm 1,\mp 2)

To further confirm this interpretation, we carried out non-local transport measurements, which directly test the helical character of the edge channels. The details of these measurements, including probe configurations and field dependence, are provided in Supplementary Note 4, Supplementary Fig. 4, and Supplementary Table 2.

VI
Discussion

In conclusion, we have investigated the quantum Hall states of ATTG and identified correlated interlayer quantum Hall phases that are not accessible in conventional bilayer and trilayer systems. The middle layer responds primarily to carrier density while the outer layers are strongly influenced by both density and displacement field, producing a landscape of filling factor combinations unique to this geometry. At
\nu_{\rm tot}=0
, a reduction in resistance appears. Comparison with Hartree–Fock calculations establishes that these low-resistance states arise from spin-resolved helical edge modes, closely analogous to those in a quantum spin Hall insulator. Unlike the bilayer case, here all three graphene layers participate, enabling configurations such as
(\pm 2,0,\mp 2)
and
(0,\pm 2,\mp 2)
, which reveal how interlayer separation governs the strength of backscattering. This demonstrates that alternating trilayers provide a broader and more tunable platform for exploring spin-polarized edge conduction than bilayer systems.

A further characteristic of this system is the emergence of an interlayer excitonic quantum Hall state at
\nu_{\rm tot}=-1
. In this regime the top sheet forms a conventional integer quantum Hall state at
\nu_{\rm top}=-2
, while the middle and bottom sheets are simultaneously half filled, creating conditions for exciton condensation across adjacent layers. The resulting suppression of
R_{xx}
mirrors excitonic states reported previously in twisted bilayer and double bilayer graphene, but here it coexists with a fully filled outer layer, providing a new trilayer realization of a bilayer hallmark. The field dependence of this feature confirms its interaction-driven origin. In summary, ATTG combines conventional quantum Hall features with emergent interlayer correlated phases, highlighting correlated interlayer quantum Hall phases unique to trilayer geometries with tunable layer asymmetry.

VII
Methods

Device fabrication

We fabricated a graphite/h-BN/twisted trilayer graphene/h-BN/graphite/h-BN (from bottom to top) using dry pick up method with Elvacite stamp. Both top and bottom graphite were used as gates. The assembled heterostructure was patterned into a Hall bar geometry using electron-beam lithography followed by reactive ion etching with mixture of
\text{CF}_{4}
and
\text{O}_{2}
at power of 40 W. Finally, a metal electrode was deposited with Cr/Au (5/100nm) by e-beam evaporator.

Transport measurements

Transport measurements were performed with the help of low frequency lock-in techniques using an ac current of 100 nA and a frequency of 13.333 Hz in a He
4
refrigerator with a base temperature of 1.5 K. In order to reduce contact resistance in areas not covered by the graphite gates, the silicon substrate was biased at

, potential energy terms induced by the displacement fields and Zeeman effect, and the capacitive energy term modeled by three infinite plate (See Supplementary Note 4 of SI for details). We focus on the 12-dimensional lowest Landau levels, given by layer
\times
valley
\times

3\times 2\times 2
. We determine the ground state using the Hartree–Fock method within a valley-, layer-, and spin-polarized ansatz. Applying Hartree-Fock method and Fierz identity together, we reduce the interactions into a sum of one-body pseudospin operators expressed in the direct product of Pauli-matrix basis, and represent the state by 12-component spinor
Dhochak et al. 2015
. We performed a brute-force search restricted to diagonal projectors. Further details are provided in Supplementary Note 4 of the SI.

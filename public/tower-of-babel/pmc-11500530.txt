Effectiveness and safety of B cell-targeting biologics in the treatment of lupus nephritis: a systematic review and network meta‑analysis
Authors: Xi Zhao, Si-Qi Yang, Man Li, Yao-Guang Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo evaluate the effectiveness and safety of different B cell-targeting biological agents combining with standard of care in patients with lupus nephritis (LN).MethodsComprehensive literature searches were conducted using PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Central in the Cochran Library, spanning from inception to May 20th, 2024. Randomized control trials (RCTs) comparing rituximab (RTX), belimumab, ocrelizumab, obinutuzumab, and anifrolumab in LN were selected. The primary outcomes of interest were related to complete renal remission (CRR), and partial renal remission (PRR). Additionally, we delved into safety outcomes, examining the occurrence of serious adverse events (SAEs), infections, and the discontinuation rates due to adverse events.ResultsA total of 6 RCTs with 1150 patients applying various B cell-targeting biological agents were included. Notably, ranking probability based on the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) indicated that obinutuzumab (SUCRA 85.2%) has the highest potential superiority in improving CRR, followed by belimumab, ocrelizumab. Regarding the improvement in PRR, obinutuzumab (SUCRA 83.0%) has the highest potential superiority. In terms of safety, with a focus on SAEs, infections, and the discontinuation rates due to adverse events, the results were: SUCRA-based ranking indicated that RTX (SUCRA 74.1%) had the highest probability of postponing SAEs, followed by belimumab and obinutuzumab. Concerning infection reduction, anifrolumab (SUCRA 78.7%) had the highest potential superiority. Safety events monitoring infection occurred better with RTX than with standard therapy (OR = 3.57, 95% CI 1.02, 12.66) and were statistically different. For the discontinuation rates due to adverse events, RTX (SUCRA 88.6%) demonstrated the highest potential superiority.ConclusionsConcerning the effectiveness and safety outcomes, obinutuzumab, belimumab, and RTX plus standard of care may be superior to the current standard therapy as treatments for LN. This study protocol has been registered with PROSPERO, with a registration number of CRD42024548522.

Introduction

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), a significant autoimmune disorder with elusive etiology, affects multiple systems, and is characterized by relentless immune-mediated inflammatory responses. Lupus nephritis (LN) is the primary cause of morbidity and mortality related to renal impairment in SLE patients, making it the most severe complication of visceral organ damage in SLE [1]. The progression and outcomes of LN are closely associated with the quality of life and survival rate of patients. Renal impairment occurs in up to 60% of SLE cases, of which 30% progress to end stage kidney disease (ESKD) within 10 years [2,3]

Various immunosuppressive drugs and glucocorticoids (GC) have proven effective in the treatment of LN, resulting in improved renal function and reduced mortality [4,5]. The current recommended first-line therapy for LN is a combination of glucocorticoids (GC) with intravenous (IV) cyclophosphamide (CTX) or oral mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) [6,7]. However, the long-term use of CTX is limited due to adverse effects, such as infection, gonadal suppression, gastrointestinal toxicity, liver function, and kidney function damage, while MMF imposes a significant economic burden on LN patients. Some patients relapse after discontinuing the medication, or the overall treatment proves ineffective, restricting the application of both CTX and MMF [8].

As a result, safer and more effective targeted treatment methods are required. Advances in scientific research and comprehension of LN pathophysiological mechanisms have fueled the development of novel biological agents, particularly those targeting B-lymphocytes, which have demonstrated improved efficacy. As B-lymphocytes have a dual function, participating in antigen presentation with dendritic cells and autoantibody generation, B-lymphocytes have a significant modulating effect on the pathogenesis of SLE and LN [9]. These targeted B-lymphocyte biological agents aim at B-lymphocyte stimulator (BLyS) or soluble B cell activating factor (BAFF), blocking abnormal proliferation of B cells and development signals, promoting B cell exhaustion and apoptosis, reducing autoantibody production and cytokine-mediated inflammatory responses, and ultimately alleviating damage to various organs in the body [10].

In 2011, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) of the United States sanctioned the utilization of belimumab, the pioneering inhibitor of BLyS, for treating adult patients with SLE who exhibit autoantibody positivity [11]. Belimumab interrupts the binding of soluble BLyS to BLyS receptors on B cells [12]. In a clinical trial comparing the impact of belimumab versus standard-of-care (SoC) on organ damage, patients treated with belimumab exhibited significantly lower changes in the Systemic Lupus International Collaborating Clinics/American College of Rheumatology Damage Index (SDI) scores at the fifth year, indicating a certain advantage of belimumab in the treatment of active SLE [13]. In addition to belimumab, B-cell targeted biological agents include rituximab (RTX), ocrelizumab, obinutuzumab, and anifrolumab [14]. Rituximab is an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody (mAb) that specifically binds to CD20-positive cells, leading to antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and complement-mediated lysis of B cells, leading to reduction in B-cell numbers and autoantibody production [14]. The NOBILITY study, a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II clinical trial, has showcased remarkable improvment in renal outcomes for patients with proliferative LN when obinutuzumab is combined with standard therapy [15]. Furthermore, the addition of obinutuzumab did not elevate the occurrence of SAEs. The findings of clinical trials about B-cell targeted biologic agents provide novel and effective treatment options for LN. However, it is still unclear which therapy is the most effective option for LN to achieve complete renal remission and reduce drug-related adverse events. The study focuses on assessing the effectiveness and safety of B-cell-targeted biologic agents in treating LN through network meta-analysis (NMA) of randomized controlled trials (RCT).

Methods

The study was conducted in accordance with the guidelines outlined in the Preferred Reporting Items [16] for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) 2020 statement and was registered with PROSPERO (CRD42024548522).

Search strategies

The database search was conducted utilizing PubMed, Embase, The Cochrane Library, and Web of Science without any restrictions on the language of publications from inception until May 20, 2024. The major search headings focused on ‘lupus nephritis,’ ‘B cell-targeting biological agents,’ ‘belimumab,’ ‘rituximab,’ ‘ocrelizumab,’ ‘obinutuzumab,’ ‘anifrolumab.’ Both medical subject headings (Mesh) terms and free terms were searched to ensure a broad and inclusive search (Supplementary Table S1).

Study selection and data extraction

The process of identifying potential trials for inclusion in the study was conducted by two reviewers, Xi Zhao and Siqi Yang, who worked independently to screen the article titles, abstracts, and full texts according to the following inclusion criteria:patients with biopsy-proven LN;a treatment regimen combining B-cell-targeted biological agents with SoC, with no restrictions on usage or dosage in the study group; a SoC regimen such as MMF, CTX, and GCs were administered, with no limitations on medication duration or follow-up time in the control group;at least one of the following outcomes must be included in the RCTs: complete renal remission (CRR), partial renal remission (PRR), SAEs, or infection. By requiring at least one of these outcomes to be reported, the researchers aimed to ensure that RCTs included in the analysis provided valuable information that could inform clinical decision-making and improve outcomes. The process of data extraction and verification described highlights the importance of ensuring the accuracy and completeness of the information collected from the included studies. By having two reviewers (Xi Zhao and Man Li) independently extract the data, followed by a check and discussion with a third member of the study team (Siqi Yang), the researchers aimed to minimize errors and biases in the data extraction process. The information extracted from the studies included the trial name, sponsor, follow-up periods, study drug, mean age, number of female patients, sample size, and outcome data.

The definitions and kidney biopsy evidence of LN differed among trials (Supplementary Table S2) but commonly based on the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) classification criteria and/or International Society of Nephrology and the Renal Pathology Society (ISN/RPS).

Risk of bias and quality assessment

The Risk-of-Bias 2 tool, developed by The Cochrane Collaboration, for assessing risk of bias) will be used to assess and report the risk of bias in individual studies [17].

Outcomes

The outcomes of efficacy evaluated in the NMA encompass CRR and PRR. While the definitions of CRR varied among the various trials (see Supplementary Table S3), they generally entailed a composite measure that incorporated the decreases in urine protein creatinine ratios (UPCR) or serum creatinine levels, as well as improvements in eGFR.

Statistical analysis calculated for each study to assess the effect sizes

For the statistical analysis of the Bayesian network meta-analysis, we employed the METABIN, BUGSnet, and NETMETA packages within the R programming environment (version 4.3.3). A random effects model or fixed effects model was employed to pool the data based on statistical heterogeneity as assessed by the chi-squared test. According to the Cochrane review guidelines, if severe heterogeneity was present at I2 > 50%, p > 0.05, the random effect models were chosen; otherwise, the fixed effect models were used. To quantify the effect sizes for dichotomous variables, we calculated odds ratios (ORs), with all results presented alongside 95% confidence intervals (CIs) to ensure robustness. For ranking the treatments according to their performance in each outcome, we utilized both the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) and mean ranks, where a higher SUCRA value signifies superior efficacy for that particular outcome. Given the absence of closed loops in the network, direct estimates were not feasible in this review. Lastly, statistical significance was determined by a P-value threshold of less than 0.05.

Serious adverse events

The analyses of SAEs included six studies, collectively enrolling a total of 1150 participants.

In the six studies included, there was low statistical heterogeneity (p = 0.53, I2=0%, see Figure 4(C)), hence a fixed-effects model was employed for the analysis. The results indicated that the SAEs outcome of B cell-targeting biological agents for LN was 13.3% (77/577), compared to 24.6% (141/573) in the control group. The combined effect was statistically significant (OR = 0.47, 95% CI 0.35-0.64), suggesting that SAEs in the experimental group was inferior compared to the control group with statistically significant difference.

The rankogram (Figure 5(C) and Table 2C) showed that RTX was associated with a lower likelihood of having SAEs (74.09%), followed by belimumab (64.9%), obinutuzumab (61.9%). The risk of SAEs was significantly lower with RTX, belimumab, and obinutuzumab than with standard therapy (Figure 6(C)).

Infection

The analyses of infection included six studies, collectively enrolling a total of 1150 participants.

In the six studies included, there was low statistical heterogeneity (p = 0.15, I2=39%, see Figure 4(D)), hence a fixed-effects model was employed for the analysis. The results indicated that the infection outcome of B cell-targeting biological agents for LN was 13.3% (77/577), compared to CRR of 24.6% (141/573) in the control group (Figure 5(D) and Table 2D). The combined effect was statistically significant (OR = 0.47, 95% CI 0.35-0.64), suggesting that infection in the experimental group was inferior compared to the control group.

The rankogram (Figure 5(D), Table 2D) showed that anifrolumab was associated with a lower likelihood of having infection (78.66%), followed by RTX (72.55%), obinutuzumab (67.46%), belimumab (34.37%), ocrelizumab (33.40%) and standard therapy (13.54%). The risk of infection was significantly higher with standard therapy than with anifrolumab, RTX, obinutuzumab, belimumab, and ocrelizumab (Figure 6(D)).

The discontinuation rates due to adverse events

The analyses of the discontinuation rates due to adverse events included six studies, collectively enrolling a total of 1150 participants.

In the six studies included, there was low statistical heterogeneity (p = 0.25, I2=24%, see Figure 4(E)), hence a fixed-effects model was employed for the analysis. The results indicated that the discontinuation rates due to adverse events of B cell-targeting biological agents for LN was 9.5% (55/577), compared to CRR of 11.7% (67/573) in the control group. The combined effect was not statistically significant (OR = 0.76, 95%CI 0.44-1.34).

The rankogram (Figure 5(E), Table 2E) showed that RTX was associated with a lower likelihood of having the discontinuation rates due to adverse events (88.63%), followed by obinutuzumab (73.23%), anifrolumab (45.87%), belimumab (37.46%). The risk of discontinuation rates due to adverse events was significantly lower with RTX than with obinutuzumab, anifrolumab, and belimumab (Figure 6(E)).

Discussion

Here, we have systematically collected and analyzed the available data with the aim of evaluating and comparing the relative efficacy and safety of various biological agents targeting B-cells used in the therapeutic treatment of LN. Our data showed that obinutuzumab was the most effective therapy for CRR and PRR, all of which outperformed the standard therapy. In terms of safety outcomes, RTX emerged as the safest treatment option. This was followed closely by obinutuzumab, belimumab, and anifrolumab, which also demonstrated favorable safety options.

Current guidelines for therapy regiment in LN patients

The primary goals in LN management are long-term renal function improvement, flare-up prevention, mitigation of treatment-related adverse events, disease activity control, and ultimately, patient survival [23]. The treatment of LN typically comprises two phases: the induction phase, aimed at swiftly suppressing disease activity, and the maintenance phase, focused on sustaining remission and preventing relapses.

The current standard therapy for induction and maintenance primarily relies on CTX, azathioprine (AZA), or MMF as the basic drugs. In the induction phase, GC pulse therapy is administered, followed by oral prednisone and non-immunosuppressive agents like hydroxychloroquine (HCQ). Following the guidelines from the Joint European Renal Association-European Dialysis and Transplant Association (EULAR/ERA-EDTA) and the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO), induction treatment with MMF or mycophenolic acid (MPA), both combined with GC, is recommended for LN class III or IV [24,25]. The KDIGO guideline offers an alternative therapeutic regimen for patients with lupus nephritis who present with nephrotic-range proteinuria and adverse prognostic indicators. This alternative comprises a combination therapy of MMF and calcineurin inhibitors (CNIs), particularly tacrolimus, along with the administration of high-dose CTX. MMF exhibits a lower tendency of ovarian failure compared to CTX, making it more suitable for young patients with fertility aspirations [26].

Despite the traditional treatment options, primarily comprising of GC and immunosuppressive agents, which have been effective in controlling the progression of LN, a subset of patients still fails to achieve low disease activity and experiences notable adverse events, including osteoporosis, cardiovascular events, infections, malignancies, fetal malformations, and premature ovarian failure [27].

In comparison to traditional therapeutic agents, novel biological agents have garnered significant attention and emerged as an entry point for LN, owing to the targeted therapy strategies, enhancement of survival quality without significant toxic side effects, and a more favorable safety outcome [28]. Due to the complexity of LN mechanisms, no single biological agent has shown perfect efficacy in treating LN. Therefore, for the treatment of LN, it may be necessary to timely switch to another biological agent with a different mechanism when the current agent fails to show efficacy or LN recurs. A combination therapy of multiple biological agents with different mechanisms may be required.

Advantages of B cell-targeting biological agents in LN patients

With the deepening of research into the pathogenesis of LN, a series of biological agents that selectively interfere with B lymphocytes have been developed and applied to SLE and LN patients, offering new perspectives and hope for further improving patient prognosis in the future.

LN primarily arises from the dysregulation of autoimmune system, where autoantibodies bind to corresponding antigens and deposit in the glomerulus, or directly deposit in the kidneys, activating complement and complement-dependent cytotoxic effects, thereby resulting in renal damage [9]. Notably, the stimulation and maturation of B cells play a pivotal role in its pathogenesis. B lymphocytes constitute a significant component of the adaptive immune system [29]. In SLE patients, a breach in B-cell immune tolerance generates autoreactive B cells and proliferation of B cells. Abnormalities in B-cell antigen receptor signaling prevent early B cells that recognize self-antigens from undergoing apoptosis, lowering the threshold for activation in peripheral B lymphocytes, subsequently triggering the production of a substantial quantity of autoantibodies, which cause inflammatory damage [30].

B Lymphocyte stimulator

B lymphocyte stimulator (BLyS), also known as B-cell activating factor (BAFF), is a member of the tumor necrosis factor ligand family. BLyS is expressed in dendritic cells, macrophages, monocytes, granulocyte colony-stimulating factor-activated neutrophils, and activated T-cells, existing in two forms, membrane-bound BAFF and soluble BAFF [31]. Its production is stimulated by proinflammatory cytokines such as interferon-γ, interleukin-10 (IL-10), and granulocyte colony-stimulating factor. Studies have indicated a positive correlation between increased serum BLyS concentrations and disease activity in LN [26]. Therefore, targeting BLyS therapy, which neutralizes B-cell surface receptors, reduces inflammatory responses, and decreases abnormal B-cell proliferation, may represent an effective treatment approach for LN.

Belimumab, a recombinant human IgG-1λ mAb that inhibits BLyS, has been approved as an add-on therapy for adult patients suffering from active SLE (Table 2). Additionally, it has proven effective in the treatment of active LN, as evidenced by positive outcomes in multiple phase III RCTs. The 2019 EULAR/ERA-EDTA recommendations suggest considering belimumab as an add-on to standard therapy in active LN and RTX in refractory SLE [24]. Belimumab can cause a depletion of 60%-70% of CD20+ B cells, resulting in a reduction in the number of naive B lymphocytes and plasma cells, as well as a decrease in anti-dsDNA antibody titers and total Ig levels [32].

B-cell-depleting anti-CD20 antibody

CD20 is a differentiation antigen expressed on the surface of B cells, ranging from the pre-B cell to the mature B cell, but not plasma cells. Anti-CD20 antibodies exert their effects by depleting B cells, inducing apoptosis, and mitigating abnormal proliferation via antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-mediated cytotoxicity (CDC) to delay the progression of SLE and LN [33].

RTX, a chimeric human-mouse IgG mAb that specifically targets the CD20 antigen on B cells, depletes peripheral B cells through mechanisms such as ADCC, CDC, and apoptosis induction [34] (Table 2). Despite the failure of RTX to achieve the primary endpoint in two large RCTs for SLE (EXPLORER [35] and LUNAR [19]), numerous open-label studies treating SLE have reported its favorable therapeutic effects [36]. And it is noteworthy that a possible explanation for the negative results of the LUNAR study could be the already sufficient strength of the standard combination therapy, which includes high-dose GCs and high-dose MMF. According to evidence- and practice-based guidance, rituximab is recommended as a first-line therapy for SLE patients with active disease (SLEDAI > 20), severe hemolytic anemia, severe kidney disease, or severe central nervous system disease [37]. CD20 is expressed throughout the developmental stages of B cells. However, CD20-targeted therapies are unable to effectively target early B cells and plasma cells [38]. Consequently, some patients demonstrate a clinical non-response during the treatment, while others experience relapse following repeated rituximab infusions. The relapse is associated with a feedback loop, characterized by ever rising BAFF levels, a significant surge in autoantibody production and worsening of disease [39]. Clinical studies have shown that B-cell depletion effect of RTX treatment is transient, and some patients who initially respond well experience relapse following B-cell re-proliferation. Notably, the plasma blast count at 6 months post-rituximab (the initial clinical response time), exceeding 0.0008 × 109/L, serves as a marker to predict clinical relapse [40]. It is commonly theorized that the failure of SLE treatments employing agents that specifically target B-cell surface antigens, notably CD20, can be attributed to the enduring presence of long-lived plasma cells. This persistence arises from the absence of CD20 expression on the surface of these cells, thereby rendering them unaffected by CD20-directed therapeutic interventions. Consequently, these plasma cells continue to contribute to the pathogenesis of SLE, despite treatment efforts [41].

The immunogenicity of RTX triggers the production of anti-drug antibodies, thereby diminishing its therapeutic efficacy. Consequently, a new generation of humanized anti-CD20 mAbs has been developed, which not only mitigates immunogenicity but also strengthens the affinity to Fc receptors on B cells, along with ADCC and CDC, thus contributing to improved therapeutic effectiveness. The study by Condon et al. [42]. suggests that maintenance therapy with MMF after RTX treatment for refractory LN can achieve a CRR of 52% without the need for GC therapy, marking the first time that the possibility of steroid-free maintenance after LN treatment has been proposed, shedding new light on future treatment options.

Ocrelizumab, a type I humanized anti-CD20 mAb, exhibits higher ADCC activity and lower CDC effects in patients with SLE, compared to RTX (Table 2). The primary endpoint was achieved in the ocrelizumab-treated groups regarding the rheumatoid arthritis trial [43], and ocrelizumab was further investigated in SLE [22]. However, development for these indications was subsequently discontinued due to an increase in opportunistic infections, some of which were reported as fatal.

Obinutuzumab, a glycoengineered, type II humanized anti-CD20 mAb that exhibits lower CDC, yet possesses greater ADCC, enabling it to be more effective than RTX in depleting B cells (Table 2). In the phase II NOBILITY trial, Obinutuzumab, a next-generation mAb against CD20, demonstrated superiority over placebo in achieving CRR and ORR among patients with proliferative LN. The therapeutic advantage was observed when obinutuzumab was administered in conjunction with MMF and GCs [15]. Encouraging results, coupled with a favorable safety profile, have further paved the way for the ongoing phase III REGENCY trial (NCT04221477), with primary completion expected in 2024. In the two trials, obinutuzumab is examined as an adjunct to standard immunosuppression therapy, with treatment administered at baseline and again after six months, challenging the placebo [44].

Drugs targeting type I interferons

Patients with SLE exhibit elevated type I interferon (IFN-I) levels and an increased expression of IFN-stimulated genes (known as the IFN signature), which has been linked to disease activity, thereby rendering IFN-I a key driver in the treatment of SLE [45]. Blocking the activity of IFN-I can effectively reduce the severity of SLE.

Anifrolumab, a human mAb that binds to subunit 1 of the type I interferon receptor (IFNAR1), antagonizes IFN-I and inhibits its biological activity [46] (Table 2). In 2021, Anifrolumab received approval by FDA for the treatment of moderate to severe SLE in adult patients receiving standard therapy. A phase 2 RCT (TULIP-LN) revealed the positive impact of anifrolumab on a range of clinically important overall and organ-specific indices of disease activity in patients with active LN receiving standard therapy [21]. Although the trial did not meet its primary endpoint, the intensified regimen of anifrolumab demonstrated numerically superior outcomes in several clinical parameters, including a complete renal response (CRR) rate of 46% at week 52, compared to 31% in the placebo group. This observation provided justification for the subsequent phase III trial, which further examined the efficacy of anifrolumab as an adjunctive therapy to standard immunosuppression, commencing from the initial intervention phase of the trial. The IRIS study is an ongoing Phase 3 RCT aimed at evaluating the intensified dose of anifrolumab in proliferative LN, with the initial results anticipated for completion in 2025 (NCT05138133).

The related meta-analysis

In a network meta-analysis assessing the safety and efficacy of immunosuppressive drugs in LN [47], the result demonstrated that the combination of voclosporin and MMF achieved the highest CRR, followed by belimamab and MMF. Nevertheless, the combination of voclosporin and MMF carried a higher risk of infection, thereby rendering it a less secure option. Previous meta-analysis [48] found that the combination of belimumab and standard therapy demonstrated greater efficacy and a favorable safety outcome in patients with LN. Our study also aligned with these findings. The safety of RTX induction therapy in LN patients necessitates additional concern [49–51]. Previous investigations have highlighted infections as a prevalent concern for healthcare. Our researcher also indicated that RTX does not elevate infection risk. Therefore, it appears that the utilization of RTX in LN is safe.

Limitations

While our findings offer valuable insights, it is crucial to approach their interpretation with caution, given the inherent limitations of our study. Firstly, the inclusion of only a handful of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) with relatively small sample sizes poses a significant constraint. The limited availability of studies for each biological drug, with some having only one or two relevant trials, and the underrepresentation of patients with lupus nephritis (LN) in three of these studies (with patient counts below 100) may have hindered our ability to achieve the study’s objectives with precision and could have introduced bias into the results. Some of the included studies did not specifically describe the blinding methods, which may lead to potential bias in implementation and measurement. Additionally, there are significant differences in the total sample sizes among the different studies included, and combining large and small samples for analysis may introduce certain biases in the results. The current guidelines exclude the latest biological agents. The present meta-analysis indicates that recent RCTs conducted from 2015 onwards, which evaluated newer biological agents, demonstrated superior efficacy compared to those conducted between 2012 and 2014. These findings may reflect advancements in both the biological agents and the criteria for patient selection, which suggests a potential enhancement in patient selection practices over time, along with progress in standard therapy. These limitations in the analysis may potentially reduce the accuracy and reliability of the results. In the future, there is still a need for more large-scale multicenter RCTs with high quality and long-term follow-up time to refine the analysis data and provide a more comprehensive validation of the efficacy and long-term effects of B-cell targeted biologics in the treatment of LN.

Secondly, the diversity in study designs and patient characteristics across the included trials adds another layer of complexity. For instance, the response to immunosuppressive medications for LN is known to vary across different ethnic groups, yet our analysis did not account for such variations. Furthermore, all patients received a standard of care comprising a combination of glucocorticoids and immunosuppressive agents, such as cyclophosphamide (CTX) or mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), which may have influenced the outcomes.

Lastly, our study’s narrow focus on the number of patients achieving complete response or experiencing serious adverse events (SAEs) as primary efficacy and safety outcomes limits its comprehensiveness. By focusing solely on these metrics, we may have overlooked other important aspects of the biological drugs’ profiles. In contrast to individual studies, a network meta-analysis has the potential to enhance statistical power and precision by combining the results of multiple analyses; however, our study’s limitations have restricted this potential to some extent.

Despite the significant efficacy achieved by the application of biological agents in patients with SLE and LN, proliferative LN still relies heavily on the use of GC combined with immunosuppressants as the primary induction and maintenance therapy. Whether targeted B cell-targeting biologics can be used as first-line therapy, whether they can reduce the use of GC and cytotoxic drugs, the optimal timing and dosage for treating refractory LN, as well as the presence of long-term adverse effects, all require extensive, large-sample, multi-center, randomized, controlled studies and long-term post-marketing surveillance. Therefore, the use of B cell-targeting biologics remains a key of our long-term and ongoing research.

There are more promising therapeutic effects of novel biological agents that are worth anticipating. Clinicians also need to rationally understand the insights and reflections brought about by clinical trials of biological agents. Whether the results of researches are positive or not is determined by a combination of factors such as experimental design, sample size, patient population included, endpoint definitions, combination background drug regimens, and follow-up time. In clinical practice, it needs to be explored further through clinical practice and research about identifying patient subgroups suitable for biological agents, using biological agents appropriately, and reducing the use of GC.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our Bayesian NMA encompassing six RCTs revealed obinutuzumab plus SoC as the most effective biological therapy for LN patients. RTX demonstrated the greatest potential for safety. The superior CRR and PRR, coupled with more favorable safety outcomes, suggest that obinutuzumab, RTX, and belimumab represent superior therapeutic options for LN compared to alternative treatments. Nevertheless, further research, particularly in larger patient cohorts and through head-to-head comparisons, is essential to definitively assess the relative efficacy and safety of novel biological agents.

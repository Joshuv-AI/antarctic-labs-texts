Averaging principles for time-inhomogeneous multi-scale SDEs via nonautonomous Poisson equations
Xiaobin Sun, Jian Wang, Yingchao Xie

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to establish asymptotic behaviors of time-inhomogeneous multi-scale stochastic differential equations (SDEs). To achieve them, we analyze the evolution system of measures for time-inhomogeneous Markov semigroups, and investigate regular properties of nonautonomous Poisson equations. The strong and the weak averaging principle for time-inhomogeneous multi-scale SDEs, as well as explicit convergence rates, are provided. Specifically, we show the slow component in the multi-scale stochastic system converges strongly or weakly to the solution of an averaged equation, whose coefficients retain the dependence of the scaling parameter. When the coefficients of the fast component exhibit additional asymptotic or time-periodic behaviors, we prove the slow component converges strongly or weakly to the solution of an averaged equation, whose coefficients are independent of the scaling parameter. Finally, two examples are given to indicate the effectiveness of all the averaged equations mentioned above.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivations

Multi-scale phenomena is very nature in numerous systems, and multi-scale systems are widely used in various fields including nonlinear oscillations, chemical kinetics, biology and climate dynamics, e.g. see
[
16
]
. For example, the macroscopic scale of fluid meters or millimeters, which are accurately described by density, velocity and temperature fields, follow a continuous Navier-Stokes equation; while the microscopic scale of nanometers obey molecular dynamics in Newton’s law, which give the actual position and velocity of each atom that makes up the fluid. Therefore, different physical laws are needed to describe the system with different scales.

The averaging principle is used to describe asymptotic behaviors of multi-scale models, and has been extensively studied for a long time. Since Bogoliubov and Mitropolsky
[
4
]
established the averaging principle for ordinary differential equations, Khasminskii
[
26
]
extended it to stochastic differential equations (SDEs), which opens up a new chapter for the study of the averaging principle for multi-scale SDEs. Here the multi-scale SDEs usually can be written as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=b(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{1}_{t},\\
dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}={\varepsilon}^{-1}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\varepsilon^{-1/2}g(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{2}_{t},\end{array}\right.

where
W^{1}:=\{W^{1}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
W^{2}:=\{W^{2}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
are two independent standard Brownian motions on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
,
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter describing the ratio of the time scale between the slow component
X^{\varepsilon}:=\{X_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{t\geq 0}
and the fast component
Y^{\varepsilon}:=\{Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Note that the coefficients are independent of time in the above system, which is called the time-homogeneous/autonomous multi-scale SDEs. There are numerous literatures devoted to this subject, e.g. see
[
2
,
5
,
7
,
10
,
15
,
17
,
19
,
21
,
22
,
24
,
26
,
28
,
29
,
34
,
41
,
43
,
45
]
.

It is widely recognized that the research on time-homogeneous SDEs has yielded significant theories and valuable results. Nonetheless, the investigation of time-inhomogeneous SDEs has lagged considerably owing to the lack of necessary research tools and methods. Time-inhomogeneous systems will change over time due to external factors or internal variations. For example, in certain complex multi-scale systems, such as the learning models related to neuronal activity (see
[
20
]
), the behaviors of the systems can fluctuate over time. Thus, these systems are influenced by parameters, inputs or disturbances that depend on the time variable, and may exhibit quite different behaviors from these for the autonomous systems. Let us take the following simple time-inhomogeneous multi-scale stochastic system on
\mathbb{R}
for example:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\,dt+dW^{1}_{t},\quad X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}=-\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha(t/\varepsilon)Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\,dt+[\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha(t/\varepsilon)]^{1/2}\,dW^{2}_{t},\quad Y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=y\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where
\alpha(t)=c_{0}(1+t)^{\beta}
with
c_{0}>0
and
\beta\in(-1,\infty)
.
As claimed in Example
6.1
in Section 6, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}[\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}]^{1/2}\asymp\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\varepsilon^{(1+\beta)/2},\quad\quad\quad\beta\in(-1,1),\\
\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\log{1/\varepsilon}\right)^{1/2},\quad\beta=1,\\
\displaystyle\varepsilon,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\beta\in(1,\infty),\end{array}\right.

where
\bar{X}_{t}=x+W^{1}_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
. This indicates that the external factor
\alpha(t)
influences the strong convergence rate, potentially exceeding the optimal strong convergence rate of
\varepsilon^{1/2}
established in the classical time-homogeneous setting. Consequently, investigating asymptotic behaviors of time-inhomogeneous stochastic systems is quite significant.

In general, mathematical models of time-inhomogeneous multi-scale SDEs can be expressed in the following manner:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=b(t,t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(t,t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{1}_{t},\\
dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\varepsilon^{-1}f(t,t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\varepsilon^{-1/2}g(t,t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{2}_{t}.\end{array}\right.

Note that, by reviewing
\{(t,X^{\varepsilon}_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
as a new process, one can focus on the case where the coefficients only depend on the variables
\{(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
. A detailed discussion of this direction can be found in
[
29
]
. On the other hand, if we consider
t/\varepsilon
as a rapidly varying component within the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
, then we can apply the classical theory of the averaging principle (see
[
18
,
41
]
).
Consequently, we can turn to the following simpler time-inhomogeneous system:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=b(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{1}_{t},\\
dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\varepsilon^{-1}f(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\varepsilon^{-1/2}g(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{2}_{t}.\end{array}\right.

To the best of our knowledge, there are very limited existing works on the time-inhomogeneous stochastic system such like
(
1.1
)
. If
f
and
g
are time periodic, i.e., there exists
\tau>0
such that
f(t+\tau,x,y)=f(t,x,y)
and
g(t+\tau,x,y)=g(t,x,y)
, Wainrib
[
42
]
and Uda
[
40
]
studied the strong averaging principle for the stochastic system
(
1.1
)

when
\sigma\equiv 0
. Later, Cerrai and Lunardi
[
8
]
explored the averaging principle for time-inhomogeneous slow-fast stochastic reaction diffusion equations, where the coefficients in the fast equation satisfy the almost periodic condition. However, the convergence rates are not considered in these mentioned references. It is well known that finding optimal/explicit convergence rates is very important in multi-scale numerical problem. For instance, Bréhier
[
6
]
mentioned that
the analysis of the full error of the scheme requires as a preliminary step to estimate the error in the averaging principle
.

1.2.
Main techniques

Undoubtedly, a crucial prerequisite for understanding the averaging principle lies in comprehending the corresponding averaging equation, and the most important thing is how to accurately describe the averaging coefficients that heavily depends on the long time behavior of the solution to the so-called frozen equation. Note that in the present setting the corresponding frozen equation corresponding to the stochastic system
(
1.1
)
is the following time-inhomogeneous SDEs:

(1.2)

dY^{x,y}_{t}=f(t,x,Y^{x,y}_{t})\,dt+g(t,x,Y^{x,y}_{t})\,dW^{2}_{t},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

According to ergodic theory of Markov processes, the unique invariant measure plays a crucial role in analyzing the long-time behavior of time-homogeneous SDEs. However, an invariant measure may not exist if concerning the SDE
(
1.2
)
. A natural generalization of the concept of invariant measure is an evolution system of measures for the time-inhomogeneous SDE
(
1.2
)
, see
[
12
,
13
]
. Recall that,
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is an evolution system of measures for the time-inhomogeneous semigroup
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
, if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy),\quad s\leqslant t,\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

From this observation, it makes sense to construct the averaged coefficients by taking the average of the original coefficients
b
and
\sigma
with respect to this evolution system of measures
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.

In order to obtain optimal convergence rates, we will apply the method that is based on the Poisson equation, which has been confirmed as a powerful technique that has successfully been used to obtain optimal strong or weak convergence rates, the diffusion approximation and the central limit theorem in various stochastic systems; see the pioneer results in
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
, and
[
6
,
11
,
25
,
33
,
35
,
36
,
38
,
39
]
and references therein for related topics.

It is noteworthy that all the quoted papers above focus on an autonomous Poisson equation associated with time-homogeneous SDEs. Since the SDEs
(
1.2
)
are time-inhomogeneous, the first novel contribution of this paper is to introduce and study a class of nonautonomous Poisson equations as formulated below:

\partial_{s}\Phi(s,x,y)+\mathscr{L}^{x}(s)\Phi(s,x,\cdot)(y)=-H(s,x,y),\quad s\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

where

\mathscr{L}^{x}_{s}
is the generator of SDE
(
1.2
)
, that is,

\mathscr{L}^{x}(s)\varphi(y):=\langle f(s,x,y),\nabla\varphi(y)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[(gg)^{\ast}(s,x,y)\nabla^{2}\varphi(y)],\quad\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

In order to solve the nonautonomous Poisson equations above, it is imperative that the function
H:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
adheres to the
centering condition
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}H(s,x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)=0,\quad s\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Under additional conditions, the aforementioned nonautonomous Poisson equations admit the solutions of the form:

\Phi(s,x,y):=\int^{\infty}_{s}\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\,dr,

where
\{Y^{s,x,y}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
is the strong solution to the SDE
(
1.2
)
with
Y^{s,x,y}_{s}=y
. To realize our approach, we need regularity properties of
\Phi(s,x,y)
. In particular, we will investigate the first and second-order derivatives of
\Phi(s,x,y)
with respect to the variables
x
and
y
respectively.

1.3.
Summary of main results

Using the nonautonomous Poisson equations above, we aim to obtain the optimal strong and weak convergence rates. In the following, we roughly state the contribution of our paper.

Strong averaging principle
: Suppose additionally that
\sigma(x,y)=\sigma(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We demonstrate that

(1.3)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant C_{T,|x|,|y|}\varepsilon^{2}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}+\int^{T/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds\right],

where
{X}^{\varepsilon}:=\{{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the slow component in the multi-scale SDE
\{(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
given by (
1.1
), and
\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}:=\{\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the solution to the following averaged equation

(1.4)

d\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\bar{b}(t/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{1}_{t},\quad\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x

with

(1.5)

\bar{b}(s,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{s}(dy),

and
\Lambda_{\gamma}(s)=\int^{+\infty}_{s}e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(v)\,dv}\,dr
with some
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\Lambda(s)=\Lambda_{1}(s)
. Roughly speaking, the function
\alpha(t)
above is employed to describe the convergence rate of the time-inhomogeneous semigroup
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geq s}
to its evolution system of measures
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
; that is, for any Lipschitz continuous function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\Big|P^{x}_{s,t}\phi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(z)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dz)\Big|\leqslant Ce^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(r)\,dr}.

Weak averaging principle
: It is crucial to note that the assumption of
\sigma(x,y)=\sigma(x)
is essential for establishing the strong averaging principle, otherwise it may not hold (see a counter-example in
[
28
, section 4.1]
). Instead, in the general case we will analysis the weak averaging principle. As an application of nonautonomous Poisson equations, we establish

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\mathbb{E}\varphi(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})|\leqslant C_{\phi,T,|x|,|y|}\varepsilon\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Lambda(t/\varepsilon),\quad\varphi\in C^{4}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}:=\{\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the solution to the averaged equation

(1.7)

d\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\bar{b}(t/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\bar{\sigma}(t/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,d\bar{W}_{t},\quad\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x

with
\bar{b}(t,x)
being defined by (
1.5
),

\bar{\sigma}(t,x):=\left[\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}(t,x)\right]^{1/2}:=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left(\sigma\sigma^{\ast}\right)(x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy)\right]^{1/2},

and
\bar{W}:=\{\bar{W}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
being a standard
n
-dimensional Brownian motion.

To the best of our knowledge, the statements
(
1.3
)
and
(
1.6
)
are new.
If
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}\leqslant C\int^{T/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds
holds for small enough
\varepsilon>0
, then rates of the strong and weak averaging principle are reduced into
\varepsilon[\int^{1/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds]^{1/2}
and
\varepsilon\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Lambda(t/\varepsilon)
respectively, which are optimal (at least) for the strong averaging principle in some specific setting (see Example
6.1
below). When
\alpha(t)
is a constant function, it is evident that the strong convergence order is
1/2
and that the weak convergence order is
1
, which are consistent with the classic result (see e.g.
[
28
]
).

It is worth mentioning that the coefficients in
(
1.4
)
and
(
1.7
)
still include the parameter
\varepsilon
. This is natural in the time-inhomogeneous setting, see Example
6.2
below. Therefore, in order to achieve coefficients of the averaged equation independent of
\varepsilon
, we should additionally assume that the coefficients
f
and
g
exhibit convergence and periodicity with respect to time, respectively.

For
f
and
g
being convergence, that is,

(1.8)

\displaystyle|f(t,x,y)-\bar{f}(x,y)|+\|g(t,x,y)-\bar{g}(x,y)\|\leqslant\phi(t)(1+|x|+|y|),\quad t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

with
\phi(t)\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
, we need to verify that
|\mu^{x}_{t}(\varphi)-\mu^{x}(\varphi)|\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
for any Lipschitz continuous function
\varphi
, where
\mu^{x}
is the unique invariant measure of some time-homogeneous SDE with drift and diffusion coefficients being
\bar{f}(x,\cdot)
and
\bar{g}(x,\cdot)
respectively. The condition
(
1.8
)
is very nature for analyzing the long-time behavior of the time-inhomogeneous SDE, for instance, see
[
1
,
23
]
. In this context, it guarantees that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\bar{b}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(x,y)\,\mu^{x}(dy)=:\bar{b}_{c}(x),

and

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left(\sigma\sigma^{\ast}\right)(x,y)\,\mu^{x}(dy)=:\left(\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}\right)_{c}(x).

As a result, the new averaged equation that does not depend on
\varepsilon
can be formulated by using the drift coefficient
\bar{b}_{c}
and the diffusion coefficient
(\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}})_{c}
. See the SDEs
(
4.9
)
and
(
4.14
)
below. Additionally, specific convergence rates relying on
\phi
are discussed for the strong and the weak convergences.

For
f
and
g
being
\tau
-periodic, the measures
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
will be
\tau
-periodic too, which indicates that
\{\bar{b}(t,x)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and

\{\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}(t,x)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
are also
\tau
-periodic for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this framework, we define the new averaged drift and diffusion coefficients as follows:

\bar{b}_{p}(x):=\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}\bar{b}(t,x)\,dt,\quad\left(\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}\right)_{p}(x):=\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}(t,x)\,dt.

Similarly, we can formulate the averaged equation with the drift coefficient
\bar{b}_{p}
and the diffusion coefficient
(\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}})_{p}
, see the SDEs
(
5.2
)
and
(
5.7
)
below. Meanwhile, we also give the specific convergence rates in the strong and the weak convergences.

For simplicity, in our paper we will assume that the coefficients are sufficiently smooth. However, such kind smoothness conditions, which are closely related with regular properties of the evolution system of measures and nonautonomous Poisson equations, can be relaxed under specific frameworks, e.g. see
[
31
]
for autonomous Poisson equations. Our focus here is on applying the technique via nonautonomous Poisson equations to establish the averaging principle and explicit convergence rates for time-inhomogeneous multi-scale SDEs. By utilizing the established nonautonomous Poisson equations, we can further explore the central limit theorem and the diffusion approximation for time-inhomogeneous multi-scale SDEs. We believe that there are several interesting issues that merit further investigation.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we explore regularity properties of solutions to time-inhomogeneous frozen SDEs and nonautonomous Poisson equations. As an application, we study the strong and the weak averaging principle for a class of time-inhomogeneous multi-scale SDEs with explicit convergence rates in Section 3. Sections 4 and 5 focus on the cases involving convergent and time-periodic coefficients, respectively. We give two concrete one-dimensional examples to illustrate our main results in the last section.

2.
Time-inhomogeneous frozen SDEs and nonautonomous Poisson equations

This section focuses on a class of time-inhomogeneous frozen SDEs and nonautonomous Poisson equations. In Subsection 2.1 we mainly investigate the differentiability of solutions to a class of time-inhomogeneous frozen SDEs. In Subsection 2.2 we consider the existence and the uniqueness of evolution system of measures associated with this class of time-inhomogeneous frozen SDEs. Subsection 2.3 is devoted to establishing the well-posedness and regularities of nonautonomous Poisson equations corresponding to the time-inhomogeneous SDEs above.

Denote by
|\cdot|
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the Euclidean vector norm and the usual Euclidean inner product, respectively. Let
\|\cdot\|
be the matrix norm or the operator norm if there is no confusion.
For a function
\varphi(x)
being vector-valued or matrix-valued and defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, or a function
\varphi(x,y)
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the notation
\partial_{i}\varphi(x)
denotes the
i
-th order derivative of
\varphi(x)
,
and the notation
\partial^{i}_{x}\partial^{j}_{y}\phi(x,y)
denotes the
i
-th and the
j
-th partial derivatives of
\varphi(x,y)
with respect to
x
and
y
respectively. For
l_{1},l_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, let
C^{k_{1},k_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{l_{1}\times l_{2}})
be the set of functions
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{l_{1}\times l_{2}}
such that
\partial^{i}_{x}\partial^{j}_{y}\varphi(x,y)
are continuous with respect to
x
and
y
for any
0\leq i\leq k_{1}
and
0\leq j\leq k_{2}
, respectively.
Throughout this paper, we use
C
and

to represent constants whose values may vary from line to line, and we use

to emphasize that the constant depends on

.

2.1.
Time-inhomogeneous frozen SDEs

For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, consider the following time-inhomogeneous frozen SDE

(2.1)

dY_{t}=f(t,x,Y_{t})\,dt+g(t,x,Y_{t})\,dW^{2}_{t},\quad Y_{s}=y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},s\in\mathbb{R},

where
f(t,\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{2,3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
g(t,\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{2,3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m\times d_{2}})
and
W^{2}:=(W^{2}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a
d_{2}
-dimensional standard Brownian motion.

Throughout this paper, we always suppose that the following assumption holds:

A1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.2)

\begin{split}&2\langle y_{1}-y_{2},f(t,x_{1},y_{1})-f(t,x_{2},y_{2})\rangle+3\|g(t,x_{1},y_{1})-g(t,x_{2},y_{2})\|^{2}\\
&\leq-2\alpha(t)|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+C\alpha(t)|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2},\end{split}

and for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
i=0,1,2
and
j=0,1,2,3
with
1\leqslant i+j\leqslant 3
,

(2.3)

\begin{split}&\|\partial^{i}_{x}\partial^{j}_{y}f(t,x,y)\|+\|\partial^{i}_{x}\partial^{j}_{y}g(t,x,y)\|^{2}\leqslant C\alpha(t),\\
&|f(t,x,y)|\leqslant C\alpha(t)(1+|x|+|y|),\\
&\|g(t,x,y)\|^{2}\leqslant C\alpha(t)(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}),\end{split}

where
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
satisfies

(2.4)

\min\left\{\int_{-\infty}^{0}\alpha(u)\,du,\penalty\ \int_{0}^{+\infty}\alpha(u)\,du\right\}=\infty,\quad\Lambda_{\gamma}(t):=\int^{+\infty}_{t}e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{t}\alpha(u)\,du}\,dr<\infty,\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}

for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

Remark 2.1
.

We give the following comments on Assumption
1
:

(i)

It is easy to see that under Assumption
1
the SDE
(
2.1
)
admits a unique strong solution, denoted by
Y^{s,x,y}:=(Y^{s,x,y}_{t})_{t\geq s}
, which is a time-inhomogeneous Markov process.
(
2.2
)
together with (
2.4
) is a dissipative assumption, guaranteeing the existence and the uniqueness of evolution system of measures for the SDE (
2.1
), see Subsection 2.2 for the details. In particular, the condition (
2.4
) yields the long-time stability of the frozen equation (
2.1
).

(ii)

It follows from
(
2.2
)
that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y,l\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\delta>0
,

2\langle f(x,y+\delta l)-f(x,y),\delta l\rangle+3\|g(t,x,y+\delta l)-g(t,x,y)\|^{2}\leqslant-2\alpha(t)\delta^{2}|l|^{2},

which is equivalent to saying that

2\langle\delta^{-1}[f(x,y+\delta l)-f(x,y)],l\rangle+3\|\delta^{-1}(g(t,x,y+\delta l)-g(t,x,y))\|^{2}\leqslant-2\alpha(t)|l|^{2}.

Then, if
f(t,x,y)
and
g(t,x,y)
are differentiable with respect to
y
, letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
in the inequality above yields that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y,l\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

(2.5)

2\langle\partial_{y}f(t,x,y)\cdot l,l\rangle+3\|\partial_{y}g(t,x,y)\cdot l\|^{2}\leq-2\alpha(t)|l|^{2}.

(iii)

Smooth conditions on the coefficients

in the mean square sense with respect to

and

(iv)

The conditions (

) and (

) together imply that for any
\beta\in(0,1)
there exists a constant
C_{\beta}>0
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

(2.6)

2\langle y,f(t,x,y)\rangle+3\|g(t,x,y)\|^{2}\leqslant-2\beta\alpha(t)|y|^{2}+C_{\beta}\alpha(t)(1+|x|^{2}).

The constants
2
and
3
involved in

)
guarantees that the solution

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that

(i)

for
t\geq s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.7)

\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{4}\leq e^{-4\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}|y|^{4}+C(1+|x|^{4});

(ii)

for
t\geq s
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.8)

\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}|^{4}\leq e^{-4\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{4}+C|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{4},

where
\gamma\in(0,1)
is the constant in (
2.4
).

Proof.

(i) Recall that

Y_{t}^{s,x,y}=y+\int_{s}^{t}f(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})\,dr+\int_{s}^{t}g(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})\,dW^{2}_{r},\quad t\geq s.

According to the Itô formula, for all
t\geq s
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{4}=

\displaystyle|y|^{4}+4\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{t}|Y_{r}^{s,x,y}|^{2}\langle Y_{r}^{s,x,y},f(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})\rangle\,dr

\displaystyle+4\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{t}|g^{*}(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})\cdot Y_{r}^{s,x,y}|^{2}\,dr+2\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{t}|Y_{r}^{s,x,y}|^{2}\|g(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})\|^{2}\,dr.

Hence, by
(
2.6
)
and Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{4}\leqslant

\displaystyle 4\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{2}\langle Y_{t}^{s,x,y},f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})\rangle]+6\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{2}\|g(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-4\gamma\alpha(t)\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{4}+C\alpha(t)(1+|x|^{4}).

This yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{4}\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{-4\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}|y|^{4}+C(1+|x|^{4})\int^{t}_{s}e^{-4\gamma\int^{t}_{u}\alpha(r)\,dr}\alpha(u)\,du

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{-4\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}|y|^{4}+C(1+|x|^{4}).

(ii) The proof is similar to (i). Note that, for
t\geq s
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle d(Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t}-Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})=

\displaystyle[f(t,x_{1},Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t})-f(t,x_{2},Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})]\,dt

\displaystyle+[g(t,x_{1},Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t})-g(t,x_{2},Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})]\,dW^{2}_{t}

with
Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{s}-Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{s}=y_{1}-y_{2}
.
Using Itô’s formula and taking the expectation on both sides of the equality above, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}|^{4}

\displaystyle=|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{4}+4\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{t}|Y_{r}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{r}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}|^{2}\langle Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{r}-Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{r},f(r,x_{1},Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{r})-f(r,x_{2},Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{r})\rangle\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+4\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{t}|(g(r,x_{1},Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{r})-g(r,x_{2},Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{r}))^{\ast}(Y_{r}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{r}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}})|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|Y_{r}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{r}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}|^{2}\|g(r,x_{1},Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{r})-g(r,x_{2},Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{r})\|^{2}\,dr.

Then, by
(
2.2
)
and Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}|^{4}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}|^{2}\langle Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t}-Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t},f(t,x_{1},Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t})-f(t,x_{2},Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})\rangle]

\displaystyle\quad+6\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}|^{2}\|g(t,x_{1},Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t})-g(t,x_{2},Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}|^{2}(-4\alpha(t)|Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}|^{2}+C\alpha(t)|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2})]

\displaystyle\leqslant-4\gamma\alpha(t)\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{x_{2},y_{2}}|^{4}+C\alpha(t)|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{4}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}|^{4}\leq

\displaystyle e^{-4\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{4}+C\int^{t}_{s}e^{-4\gamma\int^{t}_{r}\alpha(u)\,du}\alpha(r)\,dr|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-4\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{4}+C|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{4}.

The proof is complete.

Let

\partial_{y}\Psi(x,y)=(\partial_{y_{1}}\Psi(x,y),\cdots,\partial_{y_{m}}\Psi(x,y))

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\left|\frac{\Psi(x,y+\delta e_{i})-\Psi(x,y)}{\delta}-\partial_{y_{i}}\Psi(x,y)\right|^{2}=0

in the mean square sense with respect to

Lemma 2.3
.

x
and
y

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\|\partial_{y}Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\|^{4}\leqslant e^{-4\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du},\quad\mathbb{E}\|\partial^{2}_{y}Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\|^{2}\leqslant e^{-2\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du},\\

(2.10)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left|\frac{Y^{s,x,y+\delta l}_{t}-Y^{s,x,y}_{t}}{\delta}-\partial_{y}Y^{s,x,y}_{t}\cdot l\right|^{2}=0,

\partial_{y}Y^{s,x,y}_{t}\cdot l

with
\partial_{y}Y_{s}^{s,x,y}\cdot l=l
.
To do this, denoted by

Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}:=\frac{Y^{s,x,y+\delta l}_{t}-Y^{s,x,y}_{t}}{\delta}-\partial_{y}Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\cdot l.

Then
\{Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}\}_{t\geq s}
satisfies the following equation

\begin{cases}\displaystyle dZ^{s,\delta,l}_{t}=\delta^{-1}[f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y+\delta l})-f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})-\delta\partial_{y}f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})(\partial_{y}Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\cdot l)]\,dt\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad+\delta^{-1}[g(t,x,Y_{t}^{x,y+\delta l})-g(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})-\delta\partial_{y}g(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})(\partial_{y}Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\cdot l)]\,dW_{t}^{2},\\
\,\,Z^{s,\delta,l}_{s}=0.\end{cases}

Using Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}|Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\delta^{-1}\mathbb{E}\langle f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y+\delta l})-f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})-\delta\partial_{y}f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})(\partial_{y}Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\cdot l),Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=:Q_{1}(t)+Q_{2}(t).

, by Taylor’s formula,
(
2.3
)
and
(
2.8
)

\displaystyle Q_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle 2\delta^{-1}\mathbb{E}\langle f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y+\delta l})-f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})-\partial_{y}f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})\cdot(Y^{s,x,y+\delta l}_{t}-Y^{s,x,y}_{t}),Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\langle\partial_{y}f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})\cdot Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t},Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\alpha(t)\delta^{-1}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{s,x,y+\delta l}-Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{2}|Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}|]+2\mathbb{E}\langle\partial_{y}f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})\cdot Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t},Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(2-2\gamma)\alpha(t)\mathbb{E}|Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}|^{2}+2\mathbb{E}\langle\partial_{y}f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})\cdot Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t},Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}\rangle+C\alpha(t)e^{-4\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}\delta^{2}|l|^{4},

where

2.4

\displaystyle Q_{2}(t)\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\delta^{-2}\mathbb{E}\|g(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y+\delta l})-g(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})-\partial_{y}g(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})(Y^{s,x,y+\delta l}_{t}-Y^{s,x,y}_{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}|Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle(2-2\gamma){\alpha(t)}\mathbb{E}|Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}|^{2}+C\alpha(t)e^{-4\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}\delta^{2}|l|^{4}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[2\langle\partial_{y}f(x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})\cdot Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t},Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}\rangle+2\|\partial_{y}g(x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})\cdot Z^{s,\delta,l}_{t}\|^{2}].

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}|\partial_{y}Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\cdot l|^{4}\leqslant

)
,

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathbb{E}|Z^{h,x,y}_{s,t}|^{2}}{dt}\leqslant-2\alpha(t)\mathbb{E}|Z^{h,x,y}_{s,t}|^{2}.

This along with
(
2.7
)
yields that for any
h>0
,
t\geq s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Z^{h,x,y}_{s,t}|^{2}\leqslant\mathbb{E}|Y_{s}^{s-h,x,y}-y|^{2}e^{-2\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}\leqslant C(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})e^{-2\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

By this and
(
2.4
)
, we know that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\{Y^{s,x,y}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
s\to-\infty
. Hence, there exists a random variable
\eta^{x,y}_{t}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that for any
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
with
t\geq s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.17)

\mathbb{E}|{Y_{t}^{s,x,y}}-\eta^{x,y}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant C(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})e^{-2\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

Next, we are going to prove that
\eta^{x,y}_{t}
is independent of
y
. Indeed,
(
2.8
)
implies that for any
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\lim_{s\to-\infty}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x,y_{2}}|^{2}=0.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\eta^{x,y_{1}}_{t}-\eta^{x,y_{2}}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}|\eta^{x,y_{1}}_{t}-Y_{t}^{s,x,y_{1}}|^{2}+C\mathbb{E}|\eta^{x,y_{2}}_{t}-Y_{t}^{s,x,y_{2}}|^{2}+C\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x,y_{2}}|^{2}.

Taking
s\rightarrow-\infty
and using (
2.17
), we get
\mathbb{E}|\eta^{x,y_{1}}_{t}-\eta^{x,y_{2}}_{t}|^{2}=0
. So
\eta^{x,y_{1}}_{t}=\eta^{x,y_{2}}_{t}
in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Below, we denote by
\eta_{t}^{x,y}
by
\eta_{t}^{x}
.

Furthermore, according to (
2.7
), (
2.17
) and
(
2.4
)
,

(2.18)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}|\eta^{x}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant C(1+|x|^{2}).

On the other hand, using
(
2.17
)
and taking the limit as
h
goes to
+\infty
on both sides of
(
2.16
)
, we get that for any
t\geqslant s
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\eta^{x}_{t}=\eta^{x}_{s}+\int^{t}_{s}f(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\,dr+\int^{t}_{s}g(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\,dW^{2}_{r}.

This finishes the proofs of (
2.13
) and (
2.14
).

(ii) Note that for any
k\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}Y_{t}^{s-h,x,y}\cdot k

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}Y_{s}^{s-h,x,y}\cdot k+\int^{t}_{s}[\partial_{x}f(r,x,Y_{r}^{s-h,x,y})\cdot k+\partial_{y}f(r,x,Y_{r}^{s-h,x,y})\cdot(\partial_{x}Y_{r}^{s-h,x,y}\cdot k)]\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{s}[\partial_{x}g(r,x,Y_{r}^{s-h,x,y})\cdot k+\partial_{y}g(r,x,Y_{r}^{s-h,x,y})\cdot(\partial_{x}Y_{r}^{s-h,x,y}\cdot k)]\,dW_{r}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\partial_{x}Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\cdot k=

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{s}[\partial_{x}f(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})\cdot k+\partial_{y}f(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})\cdot(\partial_{x}Y_{r}^{s,x,y}\cdot k)]\,dr

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{s}[\partial_{x}g(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})\cdot k+\partial_{y}g(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})\cdot(\partial_{x}Y_{r}^{s,x,y}\cdot k)]\,dW_{r}^{2}.

Then, following the same argument as in (i) and applying the Young inequality, we can obtain that

\mathbb{E}|\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{t}\cdot k-\partial_{x}Y^{s-h,x,y}_{t}\cdot k|^{2}\leqslant C(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})|k|^{2}e^{-2\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du},

where
\gamma\in(0,1)
is the constant in (
2.4
).
In particular, due to (
2.4
),
\{\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{t}\cdot k\}_{t\geqslant s}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
s\to-\infty
. Furthermore, we can also repeat the argument in (i) and obtain that there exists a random variable
\xi^{x,k}_{t}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
which is independent of
y
such that for all
t\geq s
,
x,k\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.19)

\mathbb{E}|\partial_{x}{Y_{t}^{s,x,y}}\cdot k-\xi^{x,k}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant C(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})|k|^{2}e^{-2\gamma\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

Note that
\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{t}\cdot k
is linear with respect to the vector
k
, so there exists
\xi^{x}_{t}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m\times n})
such that
\xi^{x,k}_{t}=\xi^{x}_{t}\cdot k
.

By
(
2.13
)
,
(
2.19
)
and the fact that
Y^{s,x,y}_{t}
is differentiable in the mean square sense with respect to
x
, we know that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x,k\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\left|\frac{\eta^{x+\delta k}_{t}-\eta^{x}_{t}}{\delta}-\xi^{x}_{t}\cdot k\right|^{2}=0.

This yields that
\eta^{x}_{t}
is differentiable in the mean square sense with respect to
x
, and
\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{t}=\xi^{x}_{t}
. Thus the first inequality in
(
2.15
)
holds.
Furthermore, according to
(
2.9
)
and
(
2.19
)
,
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\|\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{t}\|^{2}\leqslant C
.

The other assertions in
(
2.15
)
for the second derivative
\partial^{2}_{x}\eta^{x}_{t}
can be proved similarly, and the details are omitted here.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

From the proof above, we can obtain that,
if for any
0\leqslant i\leqslant 4
and
0\leqslant i\leqslant 5
with
1\leqslant i+j\leqslant 5
,

\|\partial^{i}_{x}\partial^{j}_{y}f(t,x,y)\|+\|\partial^{i}_{x}\partial^{j}_{y}g(t,x,y)\|^{2}\leqslant C\alpha(t),

then
\eta^{x}_{t}
is fourth differentiable in the mean square sense with respect to
x
, and

(2.20)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\sum^{4}_{i=1}\mathbb{E}\|\partial^{i}_{x}\eta^{x}_{t}\|^{4}\leqslant C.

In the following, let
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geq s}
be the semigroup of the process
\{Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\}_{t\geqslant s}
, i.e., for any bounded measurable function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)=\mathbb{E}\varphi(Y_{t}^{s,x,y}),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Let
\eta^{x}_{t}
be given in Lemma
2.4
, and, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, denote by
\mu^{x}_{t}
the law of
\eta^{x}_{t}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds. Then,
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is an evolution system of measures for the semigroup
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
, i.e., for any
t\geq s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

(2.21)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy).

Moreover, there is a constant
C>0
such that for any
t\geq s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and Lipschitz continuous function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.22)

\left|P^{x}_{s,t}\phi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(z)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dz)\right|\leqslant C{\rm Lip}(\phi)(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du},

where
{\rm Lip}(\phi):=\sup_{x\neq y}|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|/|x-y|
.
Furthermore, if
\{\nu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is another evolution system of measures for
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
and satisfies that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\,|z|\,\nu^{x}_{t}(dz)<\infty,

then
\nu^{x}_{t}=\mu^{x}_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

(i) According to (
2.13
), for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

\lim_{s\to-\infty}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)=\lim_{s\to-\infty}\mathbb{E}\varphi(Y^{s,x,y}_{t})=\mathbb{E}\varphi(\eta^{x}_{t})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy).

By this and the fact that
P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\lim_{r\to-\infty}P^{x}_{r,s}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)=\lim_{r\to-\infty}P^{x}_{r,t}\varphi(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy).

This proves (
2.21
).

For any Lipschitz function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
(
2.13
)
yields that

\displaystyle\left|P^{x}_{s,t}\phi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(z)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dz)\right|={\rm Lip}(\phi)\mathbb{E}|Y^{s,x,y}_{t}-\eta^{x}_{t}|\leq C{\rm Lip}(\phi)(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du},

and so
(
2.22
)
holds.

(ii) Let
\{\nu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be another evolution system of measures for
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
so that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\,|z|\,\nu^{x}_{t}(dz)<\infty.

It holds that for any
\varphi\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\,\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\,\nu^{x}_{t}(dy).

In order to prove
\nu^{x}_{t}=\mu^{x}_{t}
, it is sufficient to verify that for any
\varphi\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})

(2.23)

\lim_{s\to-\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\,\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy).

Indeed, note that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\,\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left(P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(z)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dz)\right)\,\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy).

(
2.22
)
in turn yields that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left(P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(z)\mu^{x}_{t}(dz)\right)\,\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(1+|x|+|y|)\,\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(1+|x|+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|z|\,\nu^{x}_{t}(dz)\right)e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

It shows that
(
2.23
)
holds by taking
s\to-\infty
in the inequality above.
∎

2.3.
Nonautonomous Poisson equations

Let
H:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy the following
centering condition

(2.24)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}H(s,x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)=0,\quad s\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and that for
i=0,1,2
and
j=1,2,3
with
1\leqslant i+j\leqslant 3
,

(2.25)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\|\partial_{x}^{i}\partial_{y}^{j}H(t,x,y)\|<\infty.

For fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we consider the following nonautonomous Poisson equation:

(2.26)

\partial_{s}\Phi(s,x,y)+\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(s)\Phi(s,x,\cdot)(y)=-H(s,x,y),\quad s\in\mathbb{R},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

where
\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(s)
is the generator of the SDE
(
2.1
)
, that is,

(2.27)

\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(s)\varphi(y):=\langle f(s,x,y),\nabla\varphi(y)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[(gg)^{\ast}(s,x,y)\nabla^{2}\varphi(y)],\quad\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds. For any
H:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying (
2.24
) and (
2.25
), define

(2.28)

\Phi(s,x,y):=\int^{+\infty}_{s}\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\,dr.

Then,
\Phi(s,x,y)
is a solution to the nonautonomous Poisson equation (
2.26
). Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.29)

\begin{split}&|\Phi(s,x,y)|\leqslant C(1+|x|+|y|)\Lambda(s),\\
&\|\partial_{x}\Phi(s,x,y)\|+\|\partial^{2}_{x}\Phi(s,x,y)\|\leqslant C(1+|x|+|y|)\Lambda_{\gamma}(s),\\
&\|\partial_{y}\Phi(s,x,y)\|\leqslant C\Lambda(s),\quad\|\partial^{2}_{y}\Phi(s,x,y)\|\leqslant C\Lambda_{\gamma}(s),\end{split}

where
\Lambda_{\gamma}(s)
is defined in (
2.4
) and
\Lambda(s)=\Lambda_{1}(s)
.

Remark 2.8
.

Here we do not claim that the solution to nonautonomous Poisson equation
(
2.26
)
is unique. Furthermore, additionally assume that the solution
\Phi(t,x,y)
satisfies the
centering condition
(i.e.,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Phi(s,x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)=0
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and
(
2.29
)
. Then the solution is unique. In fact, if
\tilde{\Phi}(t,x,y)
is another solution to
(
2.26
)
and satisfies the
centering condition
and
(
2.29
)
, then, applying Itô’s formula and taking expectation on both sides, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\tilde{\Phi}(t,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{t})=

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}(s,x,y)+\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{s}(\partial_{r}\tilde{\Phi}(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})+\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(r)\tilde{\Phi}(r,x,\cdot)(Y^{s,x,y}_{r}))\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}(s,x,y)-\int^{t}_{s}\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\,dr.

On the other hand, by (
2.22
) and (
2.29
),

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\tilde{\Phi}(t,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{t})|=

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\tilde{\Phi}(t,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{t})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\tilde{\Phi}(t,x,y)\,\mu_{t}^{x}(dy)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\tilde{\Phi}(t,x,\cdot)\|_{{\rm Lip}}(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}\leq C(1+|x|+|y|)\Lambda(t)e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

Then,
\lim_{t\to+\infty}|\mathbb{E}\tilde{\Phi}(t,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{t})|=0
and so

0=\tilde{\Phi}(s,x,y)-\Phi(s,x,y)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Proof of Proposition
2.7
.

(i) It is well known (see e.g.
[
13
, (1.2)]
) that
P^{x}_{s,r}H(r,x,\cdot)(y)=\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})
and

\frac{d}{ds}P^{x}_{s,r}H(r,x,\cdot)(y)=-\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(s)[P^{x}_{s,r}H(r,x,\cdot)(y)].

Hence, according to the definition of
\Phi(s,x,y)
,

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\Phi(s,x,y)=

\displaystyle-H(s,x,y)+\int^{+\infty}_{s}\frac{d}{ds}P^{x}_{s,r}H(r,x,\cdot)(y)\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H(s,x,y)+\int^{+\infty}_{s}-\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(s)[P^{x}_{s,r}H(r,x,\cdot)(y)]\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H(s,x,y)-\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(s)\Phi(s,x,y),

which implies
\Phi(s,x,y)
is a solution to the equation
(
2.26
)
.

Note that for any
l,l_{1},l_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\partial_{y}\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot l=\mathbb{E}[\partial_{y}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{y}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot l)]

and

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{y}\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(l_{1},l_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\partial^{2}_{y}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{y}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot l_{1},\partial_{y}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot l_{2})]+\mathbb{E}[\partial_{y}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(\partial^{2}_{y}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot(l_{1},l_{2}))].

Then, according to
(
2.25
)
and
(
2.9
)
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\|\leqslant Ce^{-\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du},\quad\|\partial^{2}_{y}\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\|\leqslant Ce^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}\Phi(s,x,y)\|\leqslant

\displaystyle\int^{+\infty}_{s}\|\partial_{y}\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\|\,dr\leqslant C\int^{+\infty}_{s}e^{-\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}\,dr\leqslant C\Lambda(s).

Similarly,

\|\partial^{2}_{y}\Phi(s,x,y)\|\leqslant C\Lambda_{\gamma}(s).

Hence, we prove the third inequality in (
2.29
).

(ii) Under the centering condition
(
2.24
)
, for
r\geqslant s
,

\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})=\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})-\mathbb{E}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r}).

Note that, by
(
2.25
)
,

|H(r,x,y_{1})-H(r,x,y_{2})|\leqslant C|y_{1}-y_{2}|.

Then, according to
(
2.22
)
,

\displaystyle|\Phi(s,x,y)|\leqslant

\displaystyle\int^{+\infty}_{s}\left|\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})-\mathbb{E}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\right|\,dr

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(1+|x|+|y|)\int^{+\infty}_{s}e^{-\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(1+|x|+|y|)\Lambda(s),

which proves the first inequality in (
2.29
).

(iii) Thanks to
(
2.24
)
again, it also holds that for any unit vector
k\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot k

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbb{E}\partial_{x}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot k-\mathbb{E}\partial_{x}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\cdot k\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\partial_{y}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\partial_{y}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k)\right]

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

It follows from (
2.13
) that

|I_{1}|\leqslant C[\mathbb{E}|Y^{s,x,y}_{r}-\eta^{x}_{r}|^{2}]^{1/2}\leqslant C(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

On the other hand, (
2.13
),
(
2.15
)
and
(
2.9
)
show that

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leqslant

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\partial_{y}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k)]-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{y}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k)]|

\displaystyle+|\mathbb{E}[\partial_{y}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k)]-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{y}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k)]|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(\mathbb{E}|Y^{s,x,y}_{r}-\eta^{x}_{r}|^{2})^{1/2}(\mathbb{E}|\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k|^{2})^{1/2}+C(\mathbb{E}|\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k-\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k|^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}\Phi(s,x,y)\|\leqslant C(1+|x|+|y|)\int^{+\infty}_{s}e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}\,dr\leqslant C(1+|x|+|y|)\Lambda_{\gamma}(s).

Furthermore, note that for any unit vectors
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{x}\{\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})-\mathbb{E}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\}\cdot(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\partial^{2}_{x}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(k_{1},k_{2})]-\mathbb{E}[\partial^{2}_{x}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\cdot(k_{1},k_{2})]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}[\partial_{y}\partial_{x}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(k_{1},\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{2})]-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{y}\partial_{x}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\cdot(k_{1},\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k_{2})]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}[\partial_{x}\partial_{y}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{1},k_{2})]-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{x}\partial_{y}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k_{1},k_{2})]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}[\partial^{2}_{y}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{1},\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{2})]-\mathbb{E}[\partial^{2}_{y}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\cdot(\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k_{1},\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k_{2})]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}[\partial_{y}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})\cdot(\partial^{2}_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot(k_{1},k_{2}))]-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{y}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\cdot(\partial^{2}_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot(k_{1},k_{2}))]

\displaystyle=:\sum^{5}_{j=1}\tilde{I}_{j}.

From
(
2.13
)
and (
2.25
), one has

|\tilde{I}_{1}|\leqslant C\mathbb{E}|Y^{s,x,y}_{r}-\eta^{x}_{r}|\leqslant C(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

Using
(
2.9
)
,
(
2.13
)
,
(
2.15
)
and (
2.25
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\tilde{I}_{2}|\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left|Y^{s,x,y}_{r}-\eta^{x}_{r}\right|\left|\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{2}\right|+C\mathbb{E}\left|\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{2}-\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

Similarly, we can get that

|\tilde{I}_{3}|\leqslant C(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

For
\tilde{I}_{4}
and
\tilde{I}_{5}
, we apply
(
2.9
)
,
(
2.13
)
,
(
2.15
)
and (
2.25
) to obtain

\displaystyle|\tilde{I}_{4}|\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}(|Y^{s,x,y}_{r}-\eta^{x}_{r}||\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{1}||\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{2}|)

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}(|\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{1}-\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k_{1}||\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{2}|)

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}(|\partial_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot k_{2}-\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k_{2}||\partial_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot k_{1}|)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}

and

\displaystyle|\tilde{I}_{5}|\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}(|Y^{s,x,y}_{r}-\eta^{x}_{r}||\partial^{2}_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot(k_{1},k_{2})|)+C\mathbb{E}|\partial^{2}_{x}Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\cdot(k_{1},k_{2})-\partial^{2}_{x}\eta^{x}_{r}\cdot(k_{1},k_{2})|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\partial^{2}_{x}\Phi(s,x,y)\|\leqslant

\displaystyle\int^{+\infty}_{s}\|\partial^{2}_{x}\{\mathbb{E}H(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r})-\mathbb{E}H(r,x,\eta^{x}_{r})\}\|\,dr

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(1+|x|+|y|)\int^{+\infty}_{s}e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(1+|x|+|y|)\Lambda_{\gamma}(s).

Combining with all the estimates above, we obtain the second inequality in
(
2.29
)
. The proof is complete.

3.
Time-inhomogeneous multi-scale SDEs: general case

As an application of nonautonomous Poisson equations discussed in Section 2, we study the averaging principle of the following time-inhomogeneous multi-scale SDE:

(3.1)

\begin{cases}dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=b(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{1}_{t},\quad X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\vskip 2.84526pt\\
dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\varepsilon^{-1}f(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\varepsilon^{-1/2}g(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{2}_{t},\quad Y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\end{cases}

where
b\in C^{2,3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\sigma\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_{1}})
,
f(t,\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{2,3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
g(t,\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{2,3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m\times d_{2}})
for any
t\geq 0
,
and
W^{1}:=\{W_{t}^{1}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
W^{2}:=\{W_{t}^{2}\}_{t\geq 0}
are two independent
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
dimensional standard Brownian motions respectively. From this section, we always suppose that the following conditions hold.

B1
.

Suppose
f
and
g
satisfies Assumption
1
for any
t\geqslant 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\alpha:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
, which fulfills

(3.2)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\alpha(u)\,du=\infty,\quad\int^{+\infty}_{t}e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{t}\alpha(u)\,du}\,dr<\infty\hbox{ for all }t\in[0,\infty)

with some
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

B2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(3.3)

|b(x_{1},y_{1})-b(x_{2},y_{2})|+\|\sigma(x_{1},y_{1})-\sigma(x_{2},y_{2})\|\leqslant C(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|y_{1}-y_{2}|),

and for any
i=0,1,2
and
j=1,2,3
with
1\leqslant i+j\leqslant 3
,

(3.4)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\|\partial^{i}_{x}\partial^{j}_{y}b(x,y)\|\leqslant C,\quad\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\|\sigma(x,y)\|\leqslant C.

Remark 3.1
.

To make full use of the evolution system of measures in Section 2, we need to extend the fast component
\{Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
in the multi-scale SDE (
3.1
) to
\{Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. Take another
d_{2}
-dimensional standard Brownian motion
\{\tilde{W}^{2}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
that is independent of
\{W^{2}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
, and set

\hat{W}^{2}_{t}=\begin{cases}W^{2}_{t},\quad\,\,t\geqslant 0,\\
\tilde{W}^{2}_{-t},\quad t<0.\end{cases}

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, define

\hat{\alpha}(t)=\begin{cases}\alpha(t),\quad\,\,t\geqslant 0,\\
\alpha(-t),\quad t<0,\end{cases}\hat{f}(t,x,y)=\begin{cases}f(t,x,y),\quad\,\,t\geqslant 0,\\
f(-t,x,y),\quad t<0,\end{cases}\hat{g}(t,x,y)=\begin{cases}g(t,x,y),\quad t\geqslant 0,\\
g(-t,x,y),\quad t<0.\end{cases}

Thus,
\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}
satisfy Assumption
1
with the function
\hat{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{R}
. Hence, the associated time-inhomogeneous frozen SDE

dY_{t}=\hat{f}(t,x,Y_{t})\,dt+\hat{g}(t,x,Y_{t})\,d\hat{W}^{2}_{t},\quad Y_{s}=y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},s\in\mathbb{R},

admits a unique strong solution
Y^{s,x,y}:=\{Y^{s,x,y}_{t}\}_{t\geq s}
. Let
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geq s}
be the semigroup of the process
\{Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\}_{t\geqslant s}
. In the rest of this paper, we always let
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be an evolution system of measures of the semigroup
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
given in Proposition
2.6
.

Remark 3.2
.

(i)

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, the coefficients of the SDE (
3.1
) satisfy the one-sided Lipschitz condition and the globally linear growth condition. Therefore, there exists a unique solution
\{(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
to the SDE (
3.1
) for any
\varepsilon>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

(ii)

The first condition in
(
3.4
)
corresponds to (
2.25
) as we will take
H(t,x,y)=b(t,x,y)-\bar{b}(t,x)
, where
\bar{b}(t,x)
is defined by (
3.8
).
On the other hand, the second condition in
(
3.4
)
, i.e.,
\sigma
is bounded, which will be used in the proof of our first main result Theorem
3.5
below; however, such kind requirement can be weaken if the solution
\{(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
has higher finite moment.

We now establish some uniform estimates of the solution
\{(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. Then, for any
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
such that

(3.5)

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\Big)\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{4}+|y|^{4})

and

(3.6)

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{4}+|y|^{4}).

Proof.

By Itô’s formula and
(
2.6
)
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}(|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\langle Y_{t}^{\varepsilon},f(t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})\rangle)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}(|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\|g(t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})\|^{2})+\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}(|g^{*}(t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}(|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\langle Y_{t}^{\varepsilon},f(t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})\rangle)+\frac{6}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}(|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\|g(t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-\frac{4\gamma\alpha(t/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}+\frac{C\alpha(t/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}(1+\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}).

This along with the Grownall inequality and (
2.4
) implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{4\gamma}{\varepsilon}\int^{t}_{0}\alpha(u/\varepsilon)\,du}|y|^{4}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\frac{4\gamma}{\varepsilon}\int^{t}_{u}\alpha(r/\varepsilon)\,dr}\alpha(u/\varepsilon)(1+\mathbb{E}|X_{u}^{\varepsilon}|^{4})\,du

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{-4\gamma\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(u)\,du}|y|^{4}+C\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}(1+\mathbb{E}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}).

On the other hand, by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{4}\Big)\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}(1+|x|^{4})+C_{T}\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{4}\,ds+C_{T}\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{E}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{4}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}(1+|x|^{4}+|y|^{4})+C_{T}\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{u\in[0,s]}|X^{\varepsilon}_{u}|^{4}\Big)\,ds.

Using the Grownall inequality again, we find that

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{4}\Big)\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{4}+|y|^{4}),

which implies
(
3.5
)
. As a consequence,
(
3.6
)
holds obviously.
∎

3.1.
Strong averaging principle: general case

In this subsection, we focus on the strong averaging principle of the stochastic system
(
3.1
)
. A counterexample in
[
28
, Section 4.1]
shows that the strong averaging principle may fail if the diffusion coefficient
\sigma(x,y)
depends on
y
. Hence, in this part we always assume that
\sigma(x,y)\equiv\sigma(x)
, i.e.,
\sigma(x,y)
is independent of
y
. Then, we consider the following averaged equation:

(3.7)

d\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\bar{b}(t/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{1}_{t},\quad\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x,

where

(3.8)

\displaystyle\bar{b}(t,x)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy),\quad t\geqslant 0.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
hold, and that
\sigma(x,y)=\sigma(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varepsilon>0
, the time-inhomogeneous SDE (
3.7
) has a unique solution, denoted by
\{\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Moreover, for
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
such that

(3.9)

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\Big)\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}).

Proof.

According to
(
2.22
)
,
(
3.3
)
and
(
2.8
)
, for any
t\geqslant 0
,
t\geq s
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle|\bar{b}(t,x_{1})-\bar{b}(t,x_{2})|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(x_{1},y)\mu^{x_{1}}_{t}(dy)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(x_{2},y)\mu^{x_{2}}_{t}(dy)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(x_{1},y)\mu^{x_{1}}_{t}(dy)-\mathbb{E}b(x_{1},Y^{s,x_{1},0}_{t})\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(x_{2},y)\mu^{x_{2}}_{t}(dy)-\mathbb{E}b(x_{2},Y^{s,x_{2},0}_{t})\right|

\displaystyle+|\mathbb{E}b(x_{1},Y^{s,x_{1},0}_{t})-\mathbb{E}b(x_{2},Y^{s,x_{2},0}_{t})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+|x_{1}|+|x_{2}|)e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}+C|x_{1}-x_{2}|+C\mathbb{E}|Y^{s,x_{1},0}_{t}-Y^{s,x_{2},0}_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+|x_{1}|+|x_{2}|)e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}+C|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Letting
s\to-\infty
in the inequality above and using (
2.4
),

(3.10)

|\bar{b}(t,x_{1})-\bar{b}(t,x_{2})|\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

On the other hand, by (
2.13
), there exists
C>0
such that for any
t\geqslant 0
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle|\bar{b}(t,x_{1})|\leqslant\mathbb{E}|b(x,\eta^{x}_{t})|\leqslant C(1+|x|+\mathbb{E}|\eta^{x}_{t}|)\leqslant C(1+|x|).

Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.10
) with (
3.3
), we can easily see that the SDE
(
3.7
)
admits a unique solution, and
(
3.9
)
holds obviously.
∎

The following is the first main result of our paper.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
hold, and
\sigma(x,y)=\sigma(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
\{(X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\bar{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the solutions to (
3.1
) and (
3.7
) respectively. Then for
T>0
, there exists
C_{T}>0
such that for
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.12)

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{4}+|y|^{4})\varepsilon^{2}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}+\int^{T/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds\right].\end{split}

Proof.

Note that, by (
3.1
) and (
3.7
),

\displaystyle X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(b(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}(\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}(\sigma(X_{s}^{\varepsilon})-\sigma(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\,dW^{1}_{s}.

Hence, according to (
3.10
) and
(
3.3
)
, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{s}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{2}\leqslant C_{T}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{s}_{0}(b(X^{\varepsilon}_{r},Y^{\varepsilon}_{r})-\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{r}))\,dr\right|^{2}+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{2}\,ds.

Consequently,

\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant C_{T}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{0}(b(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\,ds\right|^{2}.

Denote by
H(s,x,y)=b(x,y)-\bar{b}(s,x)
. By the definition of
\bar{b}(s,x)
and
(
3.4
)
, it is easy to check that
H(s,x,y)
satisfies
(
2.24
)
and
(
2.25
)
for any
s\geqslant 0
. According to Proposition
2.7
, there exists a solution
\Phi(s,x,y)
satisfying the following equation

\partial_{s}\Phi(s,x,y)+\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(s)\Phi(s,x,\cdot)(y)=-H(s,x,y),\quad s\geqslant 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

and
(
2.29
)
holds for any
s\geqslant 0
, where
\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(s)
is defined by
(
2.27
)
. Thus,

(3.13)

\begin{split}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant C_{T}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{0}(\partial_{s}\Phi(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})+\mathscr{L}^{X_{s}^{\varepsilon}}_{2}(s/\varepsilon)\Phi(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},\cdot)(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\,ds\right|^{2}.\end{split}

On the other hand, by Itô’s formula,

\displaystyle\Phi(t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})=

\displaystyle\Phi(0,x,y)+\varepsilon^{-1}\int^{t}_{0}(\partial_{s}\Phi(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})+\mathscr{L}^{X_{s}^{\varepsilon}}_{2}(s/\varepsilon)\Phi(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},\cdot)(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\,ds

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}^{Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}}_{1}\Phi(s/\varepsilon,\cdot,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})(X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds+M^{\varepsilon,1}_{t}+M^{\varepsilon,2}_{t},

where

(3.14)

\mathscr{L}^{y}_{1}\phi(x):=\langle b(x,y),\nabla\phi(x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[(\sigma\sigma)^{\ast}(x)\nabla^{2}\phi(x)],\quad\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),

and

M^{\varepsilon,1}_{t}
and
M^{\varepsilon,2}_{t}
are local martingales defined respectively by

M^{\varepsilon,1}_{t}:=\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{x}\Phi(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\cdot\sigma(X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,dW^{1}_{s},

M^{\varepsilon,2}_{t}:=\varepsilon^{-1/2}\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{y}\Phi(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\cdot g(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,dW^{2}_{s}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle-\int^{t}_{0}(\partial_{s}\Phi(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})+\mathscr{L}^{X_{s}^{\varepsilon}}_{2}(s/\varepsilon)\Phi(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},\cdot)(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\,ds

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\Phi(0,x,y)-\Phi(t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}))+\varepsilon\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}^{Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}}_{1}\Phi(s/\varepsilon,\cdot,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})(X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds+\varepsilon M^{\varepsilon,1}_{t}+\varepsilon M^{\varepsilon,2}_{t}.

Combining this with
(
3.13
)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|\Phi(0,x,y)-\Phi(t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})|^{2}+C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\mathscr{L}^{Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}}_{1}(s/\varepsilon)\Phi(s/\varepsilon,\cdot,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})(X^{\varepsilon}_{s})|^{2}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|M^{\varepsilon,1}_{t}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|M^{\varepsilon,2}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=:\sum^{4}_{i=1}I^{\varepsilon}_{i}(T).

It follows from (
2.29
) and Lemma
3.3
as well as
\Lambda(0)\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Lambda(t/\varepsilon)
that

I^{\varepsilon}_{1}(T)\leqslant C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\Lambda(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}.

According to (
2.29
), Assumption
2
and Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle I^{\varepsilon}_{2}(T)\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}\left|\partial_{x}\Phi(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\cdot b(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[(\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(X_{s}^{\varepsilon})\partial^{2}_{x}\Phi(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})]\right|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}(1+|x|^{4}+|y|^{4})\int^{T}_{0}\Lambda^{2}_{\gamma}(s/\varepsilon)\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}(1+|x|^{4}+|y|^{4})\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2},

where the boundedness of
\sigma
is used in the second inequality.
Lemma
3.3
together with (
3.4
) and (
2.29
) yield that

\displaystyle I^{\varepsilon}_{3}(T)\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}\int^{T}_{0}\Lambda^{2}_{\gamma}(s/\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}(1+|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{2})\,ds\leqslant C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}.

Furthermore, by (
2.29
), (
2.3
) and Lemma

, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle I^{\varepsilon}_{4}(T)\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\varepsilon\int^{T}_{0}\alpha(s/\varepsilon)\Lambda^{2}(s/\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}(1+|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{2})\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\int^{T/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds.

Hence, the desired assertion follows by putting all the estimates above together.
∎

In the following, we make some remarks on convergence rates in Theorem

.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that
\alpha(\cdot)
is differentiable on
(0,\infty)
such that

(3.15)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\alpha(t)\in[0,\infty],\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{e^{-\gamma\int^{t}_{0}\alpha(u)\,du}}{\alpha(t)}=0,\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}[1/\alpha(t)]^{\prime}=0.

Then for any
T>0
, there are constants
\varepsilon_{0},C>0
so that
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}\leqslant C\int^{T/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds.

Proof.

(i) According to
(
2.4
)
,
\Lambda_{\gamma}(0)=\int^{+\infty}_{0}e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{0}\alpha(u)\,du}\,dr<\infty
. This along with (
3.15
) yields that

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\alpha(t)\Lambda_{\gamma}(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\int^{+\infty}_{t}e^{-\gamma\int^{r}_{0}\alpha(u)du}dr}{e^{-\gamma\int^{t}_{0}\alpha(u)du}/\alpha(t)}=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma-[1/\alpha(t)]^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\gamma}.

Similarly, it holds that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\alpha(t)\Lambda(t)=1
. In particular,

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\Lambda_{\gamma}(t)}{\Lambda(t)}=\frac{1}{\gamma}

and

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\Lambda_{\gamma}(t)=\gamma^{-1}\lim_{t\to+\infty}1/\alpha(t).

When
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\alpha(t)\in(0,\infty]
, it is clear that there are constants
T_{0},C_{0}>0
such that for all
t\geq T_{0}
,

(3.16)

\Lambda^{2}_{\gamma}(t)\leqslant C_{0}\int^{t}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds.

When
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\alpha(t)=0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\Lambda^{2}_{\gamma}(t)}{\int^{t}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds}=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\Lambda^{2}(t)}{\int^{t}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds}=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{2\Lambda(t)[\Lambda(t)\alpha(t)-1]}{\alpha(t)\Lambda^{2}(t)}=0,

and so
(
3.16
)
is also satisfied.

(ii) For any
T>0
, take
\varepsilon_{0}>0
with
T/\varepsilon_{0}=T_{0}
. Then, by
(
3.16
)
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
,

\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon T/\varepsilon_{0},T]}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}\leqslant C_{0}\int^{T/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds.

Note also that
\Lambda_{\gamma}(t)
is continuous on
[0,T/\varepsilon_{0}]
, and so

\sup_{t\in[0,\varepsilon T/\varepsilon_{0}]}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}=\sup_{t\in[0,T/\varepsilon_{0}]}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t)|^{2}\leqslant C_{T}.

Therefore, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon T/\varepsilon_{0},T]}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,\varepsilon T/\varepsilon_{0}]}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\int^{T/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds.

The proof is complete. ∎

Remark 3.7
.

Let
\alpha(t)=c_{0}(1+t)^{\beta}
with
c_{0}>0
and
\beta\in(-1,\infty)
. Then, (
3.15
) is satisfied.
According to the proof of Lemma

, there exist two positive constants

such that

Thus, by Theorem

In particular, when

Remark 3.8
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
3
hold. Let
\{(X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\bar{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the solutions to (
3.1
) and (
3.21
) respectively. Then for any
T>0
and
\varphi\in C^{4}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there is a constant
C_{\varphi,T}>0
so that for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.28)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\mathbb{E}\varphi(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})|\leqslant C_{\varphi,T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\varepsilon\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon).

Proof.

Fix
t\in(0,T]
, and denote by
\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(s,x)=u^{\varepsilon}(s,t,x)
for any
s\in[0,t]
. Itô’s formula implies

\displaystyle\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(t,X^{\varepsilon}_{t})=\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(0,x)+\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{s}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(s,X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds+\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}^{Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}}_{1}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(s,\cdot)(X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds+\tilde{M}_{t},

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{y}_{1}\varphi(x):=\langle b(x,y),\nabla\varphi(x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\big[({\sigma\sigma})^{\ast}(x,y)\nabla^{2}\varphi(x)\big],\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and

\tilde{M}_{t}:=\int^{t}_{0}\langle\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(s,X_{s}^{\varepsilon}),\sigma(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,dW_{s}^{1}\rangle

is a local martingale.

Since
\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(t,X^{\varepsilon}_{t})=\varphi(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})
,
\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(0,x)=\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})
, and, thanks to
(
3.24
),

\partial_{s}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(s,x)=-\bar{\mathscr{L}}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(s,\cdot)(x),

we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\varphi(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})|=

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}^{Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}}_{1}(s)\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(s,\cdot)(X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds-\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\bar{\mathscr{L}}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(s,\cdot)(X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\bigg(\langle b(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s}),\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(s,X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\big[(\sigma\sigma^{\ast}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\partial_{x}^{2}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(s,X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\big]\bigg)\,ds\bigg|.

For
r\in[0,t]
,
s\geqslant 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, define

\displaystyle F^{t}(r,s,x,y):=\langle b(x,y)-\bar{b}(s,x),\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(r,x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[(\sigma\sigma^{*}(x,y)-\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}(s,x))\partial_{x}^{2}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon,t}(r,x)].

It is easy to check that
F^{t}
satisfies the following centering condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}F^{t}(r,s,x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)=0,\quad r\in[0,t],s\geqslant 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and
(
2.25
)
as well as

\sup_{0\leqslant r\leqslant t\leqslant T,s\geqslant 0,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}|\partial_{y}\partial_{r}F^{t}(r,s,x,y)|\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|),

thanks to (
3.26
), (
3.27
) and
(
3.19
)
.
Let
\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(s)
be the operator given by (
2.27
).
Consider the following nonautonomous Poisson equation

(3.29)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(r,s,x,y)+\mathscr{L}^{x}_{2}(s)\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(r,s,x,y)=-F^{t}(r,s,x,y).

Then, according to Proposition
2.7
and its proof, we can claim that (
3.29
) admits a solution

\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(r,s,x,y)=\int^{+\infty}_{s}\mathbb{E}F^{t}(r,u,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{u})\,du,

and for any
T>0
, there exists
C_{T}>0
such that for any
s\geqslant 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant r\leqslant t\leqslant T}\{|\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(r,s,x,y)|+|\partial_{r}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(r,s,x,y)|+\|\partial_{x}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(r,s,x,y)\|+\|\partial_{x}^{2}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(r,s,x,y)\|\}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{T}\Lambda_{\gamma}(s)(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}).

Using Itô’s formula, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(t,t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(0,0,x,y)+\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{r}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds+\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}^{Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}}_{1}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,\cdot,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})(X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}(\partial_{s}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})+\mathscr{L}^{X_{s}^{\varepsilon}}_{2}(s/\varepsilon)\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},\cdot)(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\,ds,

which implies that

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}[\partial_{s}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})+\mathscr{L}^{X_{s}^{\varepsilon}}_{2}(s/\varepsilon)\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},\cdot)(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})]\,ds

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\Big[\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(0,0,x,y)-\mathbb{E}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(t,t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})+\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{r}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}^{Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}}_{1}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,\cdot,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})(X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds\Big].

According to all the conclusions above and Lemma
3.3
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\mathbb{E}\varphi(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})|

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}F^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,ds\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}(\partial_{s}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})+\mathscr{L}^{X_{s}^{\varepsilon}}_{2}(s/\varepsilon)\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},\cdot)(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\Bigg[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(0,0,x,y)|+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\mathbb{E}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(t,t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})|+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}|\partial_{r}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})|\,ds

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}|\mathscr{L}^{Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}}_{1}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,s/\varepsilon,\cdot,Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})(X_{s}^{\varepsilon})|\,ds\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\varepsilon\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon).

The proof is complete.
∎

and

Remark 4.2
.

We note that for any

,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\eta(t-r)}\phi^{2}(r)\,dr=0.

Indeed, for any

, it follows from the fact
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\phi(t)=0
that there exists
N_{1}>0
so that
\phi^{2}(t)\leqslant\eta\delta/2
for all
t\geqslant N_{1}.

On the other hand, since
\phi
is locally bounded, there exists
N_{2}>0
such that for all

,

Then, for any

,

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\eta(t-r)}\phi^{2}(r)\,dr=e^{-\eta t}\int^{N_{1}}_{0}e^{\eta r}\phi^{2}(r)\,dr+e^{-\eta t}\int^{t}_{N_{1}}e^{\eta r}\phi^{2}(r)\,dr\leqslant\delta/2+\delta/2=\delta.

In particular,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\eta(t-r)}\phi^{2}(r)\,dr=0,

which proves the desired assertion
(

)

5.

\displaystyle f(t+\tau,x,y)=f(t,x,y),\quad g(t+\tau,x,y)=g(t,x,y).

We start with two simple lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a bounded and
\tau
-periodic function on

. Then, for any

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{1}{T}\int^{T+a}_{a}h(s)\,ds-\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}h(s)\,ds\right|\leqslant\frac{2\tau M}{T},

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 5.2

Suppose that Assumptions

and
5

where

(
2.27
)
. It is easy to see that

.
Since

and

are

for

.
Hence, noting that the law of

convergence weakly to
\mu^{x}_{t}
by
(
2.13
)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\mu^{x}_{t+\tau}(dy)=\lim_{s\to-\infty}P^{x}_{s+\tau,t+\tau}\varphi(0)=\lim_{s\to-\infty}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\mu^{x}_{t}(dy).

Hence,
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is
\tau
-periodic.
∎

5.1.
Strong averaging principle: periodic coefficients case

In this subsection, we suppose that
f
and
g
fulfill Assumptions
1
and
5
, and
b
and
\sigma=\sigma(x)
fulfill Assumption
2
. Consider the following averaged SDE:

(5.2)

d\bar{X}_{t}=\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(\bar{X}_{t})\,dW^{1}_{t},\quad\bar{X}_{0}=x,

where

(5.3)

\displaystyle\bar{b}_{p}(x):=\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}\bar{b}(t,x)\,dt=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy)\,dt.

By
(
3.10
)
, it is easy to see that for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(5.4)

\displaystyle|\bar{b}_{p}(x_{1})-\bar{b}_{p}(x_{2})|\leqslant C|x_{1}-x_{2}|,

and so the SDE
(
5.2
)
admits a unique solution
\{\bar{X}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Moreover, by (
5.4
) and Assumption
2
, for any
T>0
, there exists
C_{T}>0
such that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}_{t}-\bar{X}_{s}|^{2}\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})|t-s|.

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
,
2
and
5
hold. Let
\{(X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\bar{X}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the solutions to the SDEs (
3.1
) and (
5.2
) respectively. Then, for any
T>0
, there exists
C_{T}>0
such that for any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\varepsilon>0
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}(1+|x|^{4}+|y|^{4})\Big[\varepsilon^{-4/3}+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)|^{2}+\int^{T/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds\Big].

Proof.

Let
\{\bar{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the solution to the SDE (
3.7
). Then,

\bar{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}[\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{s})]\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}[\sigma(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\sigma(\bar{X}_{s})]\,dW^{1}_{s}.

According to
(
3.10
)
and
(
3.3
)
, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{s}|^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}_{s})|^{2}\,ds+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\|\sigma(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\sigma(\bar{X}_{s})\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+C_{T}\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}_{s})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{s})\,ds\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{s}|^{2}\,ds+C_{T}\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}_{s})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{s})\,ds\right|^{2}.

Thus, the Gronwall inequality,
(
4.13
)
and
(
3.9
)
yield that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{T}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}[\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}_{s})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{s})]\,ds\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{T}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}[\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}_{s(\delta)})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{s(\delta)})]\,ds\Big|^{2}+C_{T}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}_{s})-\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}_{s(\delta)})|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+C_{T}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{s})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{s(\delta)})|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=:\sum^{3}_{i=1}J_{i}(T),

where
s(\delta)=\left[{s}/{\delta}\right]\delta
and
[s/{\delta}]
is the integer part of
s/{\delta}
. Here,
\delta>0
depends on
\varepsilon
, which will be chosen later.

According to
(
3.10
)
,
(
5.4
)
and
(
5.5
)
,

(5.6)

\displaystyle J_{2}(T)+J_{3}(T)\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\delta.

On the other hand, since
\bar{b}(\cdot,x)
is
\tau
-periodic by Lemma
5.2
, Proposition
5.1
, (
3.10
) and (
5.4
) yield that

\displaystyle J_{1}(T)\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\mathbb{E}\left|\sum^{[T/\delta]-1}_{k=0}\int^{(k+1)\delta}_{k\delta}[\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}_{k\delta})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{k\delta})]\,ds\right|^{2}+C_{T}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int^{T}_{[T/\delta]\delta}[\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}_{k\delta})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{k\delta})]\,ds\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\mathbb{E}\left|\sum^{[T/\delta]-1}_{k=0}\delta\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\int^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}}_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}}\bar{b}(s,\bar{X}_{k\delta})\,ds-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{k\delta})\right)\right|^{2}+C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\left|\sum^{[T/\delta]-1}_{k=0}(1+|\bar{X}_{k\delta}|)\right|^{2}+C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta^{2}}+C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\delta^{2}.

Combining all the estimates above and taking
\delta=\varepsilon^{2/3}
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\varepsilon^{2/3}.

Therefore, the desired assertion immediately follows from the inequality above and
(
3.12
)
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Assume that Assumptions
1
,
2
and
5
hold with the rate function
\alpha
being a positive constant. Then the functions
\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{\gamma}
are constants. Additionally, if
\sigma\equiv 0
, then we can improve the estimate
(
5.5
)
into

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}_{t}-\bar{X}_{s}|\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|)|t-s|.

5.2.
Weak averaging principle: periodic coefficients case

In this subsection, we suppose that
f
and
g
satisfy Assumptions
1
and
5
, and
b
and
\sigma
satisfy Assumption
3
. We consider the averaged SDE

(5.7)

d\bar{X}_{t}=\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}_{t})\,dt+\bar{\sigma}_{p}(\bar{X}_{t})\,d\bar{W}_{t},\quad\bar{X}_{0}=x,

where
\bar{b}_{p}(x)
is defined in
(
5.3
)
,

\bar{\sigma}_{p}(x):=\left[\left(\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}\right)_{p}(x)\right]^{1/2}:=\left[\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left(\sigma\sigma^{\ast}\right)(x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy)\,dt\right]^{1/2},

and
\{\bar{W_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a
n
-dimensional Brownian motion.

Similar to (
4.15
), now it holds that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum^{4}_{i=1}[\|\partial^{i}_{x}\bar{b}_{p}(x)\|+\|\partial^{i}_{x}\bar{\sigma}_{p}(x)\|]\leqslant C.

Hence, the SDE
(
5.7
)
admits a unique solution
\{\bar{X}^{x}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
.

For any
\varphi\in C^{4}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, consider the following Kolmogorov equation:

(5.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\bar{\mathscr{L}}_{p}u(t,x),\quad u(0,x)=\varphi(x),

where
\bar{\mathscr{L}}_{p}
is the generator of the SDE
(
5.7
)
and is given by

\bar{\mathscr{L}}_{p}\varphi(x):=\langle\bar{b}_{p}(x),\nabla\varphi(x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}[\left(\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}\right)_{p}(x)\nabla^{2}\varphi(x)].

We can verify that the equation
(
5.9
)
has a unique solution which is given by
u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}^{x}_{t})
for all
t\geq 0,
and that, for all
T>0
,

(5.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum^{4}_{i=1}\|\partial^{i}_{x}u(t,x)\|\leqslant C_{T},\\
&\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\sum^{2}_{i=1}\|\partial_{t}(\partial^{i}_{x}u(t,x))\|\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|).\end{split}

Now, we state the last main result in this paper.

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
,
3
and
5
hold. Let
\{(X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\bar{X}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the solutions to the SDEs (
3.1
) and (
5.7
) respectively. Then, for
T>0
and
\varphi\in C^{4}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there is a constant
C_{\varphi,T}>0
such that for any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\mathbb{E}\varphi(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}_{t})|\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{\varphi,T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\varepsilon\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Lambda_{\gamma}(t/\varepsilon)+\varepsilon^{-2/3}\Big].

Proof.

Let
\{\bar{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the solution to the SDE (
3.21
).
Fix
t\leq T
, and denote by
\tilde{u}^{t}(s,x)=u(t-s,x)
for all
s\leq t
. Following the proof of Theorem
4.5
, we find that

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}_{t})|\\
&\leqslant\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\langle\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}),\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{t}(s,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\rangle\,ds\Big|\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\text{Tr}\big[((\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}})(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})-(\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}})_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\partial_{x}^{2}\tilde{u}^{t}(s,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\big]\,ds\Big|\\
&\leqslant\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\left[\langle\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}),\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{t}(s,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\rangle\right.\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\left.\langle\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)}),\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{t}({s(\delta)},\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)})\rangle\right]\,ds\Big|\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\left\{\text{Tr}\big[((\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}})(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})-(\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}})_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\partial_{x}^{2}\tilde{u}^{t}(s,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\big]\right.\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\left.\text{Tr}\big[((\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}})(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)})-(\overline{\sigma\sigma}^{\ast})_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)}))\partial_{x}^{2}\tilde{u}^{t}(s(\delta),\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)})\big]\right\}\,ds\Big|\\
&\quad+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\langle\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)}),\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{t}({s(\delta)},\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)})\rangle\,ds\Big|\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\text{Tr}\big[((\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}})(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)})-(\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}})_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)})\partial_{x}^{2}\tilde{u}^{t}({s(\delta)},\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)})\big]\,ds\Big|=:\sum^{4}_{i=1}\tilde{J}_{i}(T).\end{split}

By (
3.22
),
(
5.8
)
and
(
5.10
)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{2}\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})|t-s|,

which combines with
(
3.5
)
and
(
5.10
)
imply that

\tilde{J}_{1}(T)+\tilde{J}_{2}(T)\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\delta^{1/2}.

Using Proposition
5.1
, (
5.10
) and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{3}(T)\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\mathbb{E}\left|\sum^{[T/\delta]-1}_{k=0}\int^{(k+1)\delta}_{k\delta}\langle\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta}),\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{t}(k\delta,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta})\rangle\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\quad+C_{T}\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{T}_{[T/\delta]\delta}\langle\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)})-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)}),\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{t}(s(\delta),\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)}))\rangle\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum^{[T/\delta]-1}_{k=0}\delta\left|\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\int^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}}_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}}\bar{b}(s,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta})\,ds-\bar{b}_{p}(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta})\right||\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{t}(k\delta,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta})|\right]+C_{T}(1+|x|)\delta

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\left|\sum^{[T/\delta]-1}_{k=0}(1+|\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta}|)\right|+C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\delta

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}(1+|x|)\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}+C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\delta.

Similarly, it holds that

(5.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{4}(T)\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|)\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}+C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\delta.

Putting all the estimates together and taking
\delta=\varepsilon^{2/3}
yield that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\varphi(\bar{X}_{t})\right|\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})\varepsilon^{1/3}.

Therefore, the desired assertion follows from the inequality above and
(
3.28
)
.
∎

6.
Examples

In this section, we give two concrete examples to illustrate main results of the paper. In particular, the first example demonstrates that the convergence rate given in Theorem
3.5
is optimal. For simplicity, we only consider one-dimensional setting.

Example 6.1
.

Let
\alpha:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
satisfy
(
3.15
)
. Consider the following SDE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\,dt+dW^{1}_{t},\quad X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}=-\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha(t/\varepsilon)Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\,dt+[\varepsilon^{-1}\alpha(t/\varepsilon)]^{1/2}\,dW^{2}_{t},\quad Y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=y\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where
W^{1}:=\{W^{1}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
W^{2}:=\{W^{2}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be two independent one-dimensional Brownian motions.
It is easy to see that the solution to the SDE above is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle X^{\varepsilon}_{t}=x+\int^{t}_{0}Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}\,ds+W^{1}_{t},\\
\displaystyle Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}=e^{-\varepsilon^{-1}\int^{t}_{0}\alpha(r/\varepsilon)\,dr}y+\varepsilon^{-1/2}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\varepsilon^{-1}\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(v/\varepsilon)\,dv}\alpha^{1/2}(s/\varepsilon)\,dW^{2}_{s}.\end{array}\right.

On the other hand, the associated frozen equation

(6.1)

dY_{t}=-\alpha(t)Y_{t}\,dt+\alpha^{1/2}(t)\,dW^{2}_{t},\quad Y_{s}=y

has a unique solution

Y^{s,y}_{t}=e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}y+\int^{t}_{s}e^{-\int^{t}_{r}\alpha(u)\,du}\alpha^{1/2}(r)\,dW^{2}_{r},

whose distribution is
N\Big(e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}\,y,\frac{1}{2}\big(1-e^{-2\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(r)\,dr}\big)\Big)
.
In particular, letting
s\to-\infty
, we get that

\mu_{t}:=N\left(0,1/2\right),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

is an evolution family of measures for the SDE
(
6.1
)
.
Hence, the corresponding averaged equation (
3.7
) is given by

d\bar{X}_{t}=dW^{1}_{t},\quad\bar{X}_{0}=x,

which is independent of
\varepsilon
.

As a result, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{0}Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}\,ds\right|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{0}Y^{0,y}_{s/\varepsilon}\,ds\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}Y^{0,y}_{s}\,ds\right|^{2}=2\varepsilon^{2}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{r}\mathbb{E}(F(s)F(r))\,ds\,dr,

where

F(s):=e^{-\int^{s}_{0}\alpha(u)\,du}y+\int^{s}_{0}e^{-\int^{s}_{u}\alpha(v)\,dv}\alpha^{1/2}(u)\,dW^{2}_{u}.

Furthermore, for
s\geqslant r
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(F(s)F(r))=

\displaystyle e^{-\int^{s}_{0}\alpha(u)\,du}e^{-\int^{r}_{0}\alpha(u)\,du}y^{2}+\int^{r}_{0}e^{-\int^{s}_{u}\alpha(v)\,dv}e^{-\int^{r}_{u}\alpha(v)\,dv}\alpha(u)\,du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(y^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)e^{-\int^{s}_{0}\alpha(u)\,du}e^{-\int^{r}_{0}\alpha(u)\,du}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-\int^{s}_{r}\alpha(u)\,du}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}=

\displaystyle(2y^{2}-1)\varepsilon^{2}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{r}e^{-\int^{s}_{0}\alpha(u)\,du}e^{-\int^{r}_{0}\alpha(u)\,du}\,ds\,dr+\varepsilon^{2}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{r}e^{-\int^{s}_{r}\alpha(u)\,du}\,ds\,dr

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\Lambda(s)\,ds\asymp\varepsilon^{2}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\alpha(s)\Lambda^{2}(s)\,ds,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

where
\Lambda(s)=\int^{+\infty}_{s}e^{-\int^{t}_{s}\alpha(u)\,du}\,dt
. Here,
f(\varepsilon)\asymp g(\varepsilon)
means there are positive constants
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
such that
c_{1}\leq f(\varepsilon)/g(\varepsilon)\leq c_{2}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

Take
\alpha(t)=c_{0}(1+t)^{\beta}
with
c_{0}>0
and
\beta\in(-1,\infty)
. According to the argument in Remark
3.7
, we also have

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|\asymp\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\varepsilon^{{1+\beta}},\quad\quad\quad-1<\beta<1,\\
\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\log{1/\varepsilon},\quad\beta=1,\\
\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2},\quad\quad\quad\quad\beta>1.\end{array}\right.

Example 6.2
.

Let
\phi:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a bounded function. Consider the following SDE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\,dt+dW^{1}_{t},\quad X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\varepsilon^{-1}\left[\phi(t/\varepsilon)-Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\right]\,dt+\varepsilon^{1/2}\,dW^{2}_{t},\quad Y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=y\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where
W^{1}:=\{W^{1}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
W^{2}:=\{W^{2}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
are two independent one-dimensional Brownian motions. The SDE above
has the solution

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle X^{\varepsilon}_{t}=x+\int^{t}_{0}Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}\,ds+W^{1}_{t},\\
\displaystyle Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}=e^{-\varepsilon^{-1}t}y+\varepsilon^{-1}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\varepsilon^{-1}(t-r)}\phi(r/\varepsilon)\,dr+\varepsilon^{-1/2}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\varepsilon^{-1}(t-r)}\,dW^{2}_{r}.\\
\end{array}\right.

On the other hand, the associated frozen equation

(6.2)

dY_{t}=[\phi(t)-Y_{t}]\,dt+dW^{2}_{t},\quad Y_{s}=y

admits a unique solution

Y^{s,y}_{t}=e^{-(t-s)}y+\int^{t}_{s}e^{-(t-r)}\phi(r)\,dr+\int^{t}_{s}e^{-(t-r)}\,dW^{2}_{r},\quad t\geq s,

and the associated distribution is

N\Big(e^{-(t-s)}y+\int^{t}_{s}e^{-(t-r)}\phi(r)\,dr,\frac{1}{2}(1-e^{-2(t-s)})\Big).

Letting
s\to-\infty
, we get

\mu_{t}=N\left(\int^{t}_{-\infty}e^{-(t-r)}\phi(r)\,dr,\frac{1}{2}\right),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

is an evolution system of measures for the SDE
(
6.2
)
.
Hence, the corresponding averaged equations (
3.7
) and (
3.21
) are same, and both are given by

(6.3)

\displaystyle d\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\psi(t/\varepsilon)\,dt+dW^{1}_{t},\quad\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x,

where
\psi(t):=\int^{t}_{-\infty}e^{-(t-r)}\phi(r)\,dr
.
One has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}-\psi(s/\varepsilon))\,ds\right|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{0}(Y^{0,y}_{s/\varepsilon}-\psi(s/\varepsilon))\,ds\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}(Y^{0,y}_{s}-\psi(s))\,ds\right|^{2}=2\varepsilon^{2}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{r}\mathbb{E}(\tilde{F}(s)\tilde{F}(r))\,ds\,dr,

where

\tilde{F}(s):=ye^{-s}-\int^{0}_{-\infty}h(u)e^{-(s-u)}\,du+\int^{s}_{0}e^{-(s-u)}\,dW^{2}_{u}.

Furthermore, for any
s\geqslant r
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\tilde{F}(s)\tilde{F}(r)=

\displaystyle\left[ye^{-s}-\int^{0}_{-\infty}h(u)e^{-(s-u)}\,du\right]\left[ye^{-r}-\int^{0}_{-\infty}h(u)e^{-(r-u)}\,du\right]+\int^{r}_{0}e^{-(s-u)}e^{-(r-u)}\,du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((y-c)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)e^{-(s+r)}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-s+r},

where
c=\int^{0}_{-\infty}e^{r}\phi(r)\,dr
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}=2\varepsilon^{2}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{r}\left[\left((y-c)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)e^{-(s+r)}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-s+r}\right]\,ds\,dr=O(\varepsilon).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\mathbb{E}\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|=

\displaystyle\left|\int^{t}_{0}\left(\mathbb{E}Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}-\psi(s/\varepsilon)\right)\,ds\right|=\left|\int^{t}_{0}(\mathbb{E}Y^{0,y}_{s/\varepsilon}-\psi(s/\varepsilon))\,ds\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\Big|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}(\mathbb{E}Y^{0,y}_{s}-\psi(s))\,ds\Big|=\varepsilon\Big|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\left(ye^{-s}-\int^{0}_{-\infty}e^{-(s-r)}\phi(r)\,dr\right)\,ds\Big|=O(\varepsilon).

We observe that both Assumptions
1
and
3
hold with
\alpha(t)\equiv 1
and
\Lambda(t)\equiv 1
in this case. Theorems
3.5
and
3.9
imply that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\mathbb{E}X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\mathbb{E}\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|\leqslant C_{T,x,y}\varepsilon,

which coincides with the assertion above. Hence, the assertion above indicates the effectiveness of averaged equations (
3.7
) and (
3.21
).

(

\varepsilon
, if some additional assumptions imposed on

.

Convergent coefficient case
: Assume the following condition hold:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\phi(t)=0.

In this situation, it is easy to check that both of the associated averaged equations (
4.9
) and (
4.14
) are given by

d\bar{X}_{t}=dW^{1}_{t},\quad\bar{X}_{0}=x.

Thus, we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}Y^{0,y}_{s}\,ds\Big|^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}(Y^{0,y}_{s}-\psi(s)+\psi(s))\,ds\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon^{2}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{r}\mathbb{E}\left[(\tilde{F}(s)+\psi(s))(\tilde{F}(r)+\psi(r))\right]\,ds\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\varepsilon)+C\varepsilon^{2}\Big(\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\psi(s)\,ds\Big)^{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

On the other hand, it also holds that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\mathbb{E}\bar{X}_{t}|=

\displaystyle\left|\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{E}Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}\,ds\right|=\varepsilon\Big|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\mathbb{E}Y^{0,y}_{s}\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle=\penalty\

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}(\mathbb{E}Y^{0,y}_{s}-\psi(s)+\psi(s))\,ds\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\Big|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\left(e^{-s}y+\int^{s}_{0}e^{-(s-r)}\phi(r)\,dr\right)\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\varepsilon)+C\varepsilon\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\psi(s)\,ds\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

Hence, the assertion above indicates the effectiveness of the averaged equations (
4.9
) and (
4.14
).

Periodic coefficient case
: Assume
\phi
is
\tau
-periodic, that is,
\phi(t+\tau)=\phi(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}.

In this situation, it is easy to check that the associated averaged equations (
5.2
) and (
5.7
) are given by

d\bar{X}_{t}=b\,dt+dW^{1}_{t},\quad\bar{X}_{0}=x,

where
b=\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}\psi(t)\,dt
. Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}(Y^{0,y}_{s}-b)\,ds\Big|^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}(Y^{0,y}_{s}-\psi(s)+\psi(s)-b)\,ds\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\varepsilon)+C_{T}\Big|\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\psi(s)\,ds-b\Big|^{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

where we used the fact that
\psi
is
\tau
-periodic.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\mathbb{E}\bar{X}_{t}|=

\displaystyle\Big|\int^{t}_{0}(\mathbb{E}Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}-b)\,ds\Big|=\varepsilon\Big|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}(\mathbb{E}Y^{0,y}_{s}-b)\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\Big|\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}(\mathbb{E}Y^{0,y}_{s}-\psi(s)+\psi(s)-b)\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\varepsilon)+C\Big|\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\int^{t/\varepsilon}_{0}\psi(s)\,ds-b\Big|\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

Hence, the assertion above indicates the effectiveness of the averaged equations (
5.2
) and (
5.7
).

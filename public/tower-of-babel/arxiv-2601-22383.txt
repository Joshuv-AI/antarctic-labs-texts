Classical double copy of black strings in an Anti-de Sitter background
G. Alencar, C. R. Muniz, M. S. Oliveira

Abstract
We study the classical double copy for static black string solutions in an Anti–de Sitter (AdS) background. By casting the black string metric into Kerr–Schild form over a cylindrical AdS geometry, we construct the corresponding single and zeroth copies. The single copy describes a gauge field satisfying Maxwell-like equations and sourced by an effective line of color charge, while the zeroth copy is given by a scalar field conformally coupled to the AdS background. We also extend the analysis to charged black strings, identifying the associated modifications in the gauge sector. These results show that the classical double copy consistently applies to extended gravitational objects in curved spacetimes.

I
Introduction

In recent years, considerable efforts have been devoted to investigating the connections between non-Abelian gauge theories and gravity. In this context, the BCJ approach (Bern, Carrasco and Johansson)
10
;
12
;
13

13
;
14

44

Taken together, these results indicate that Yang–Mills theory plays a fundamental role in understanding the structure of gravitational amplitudes, suggesting that gravity can be interpreted, at least perturbatively, as a theory composed of two appropriate copies of gauge theories.

Although originally formulated in the context of scattering amplitudes

;
15
;
47
;
11

62
, who showed that the correspondence can be realized exactly for spacetimes admitting a Kerr–Schild (KS) form. In this framework, the spacetime metric is expressed as a linear deformation of a fixed background,
g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+2\,\mathcal{H}(r)\,k_{\mu}k_{\nu},
, where
k_{\mu}
is a null and geodesic vector field. This structure naturally induces a hierarchy of associated fields: the so-called
single copy
, corresponding to a gauge potential, and the
zeroth copy
, described by a bi-adjoint scalar field. Since its introduction, this classical double copy construction has been extensively explored in a variety of settings, including non-singular black holes
25
, construction of spacetime metrics through a perturbative expansion
56
, higher-dimensional black holes
23
, solutions in three-dimensional space-time
18
;
37
;
3
;
35
;
4
, cosmology
48
;
40
, gravitational radiation
60
, bigravity
31
, Taub–NUT–type
58
;
9
;
22
;
28
, and Kasner
46
geometries. Furthermore, the correspondence has been investigated in the context of the fluid-gravity duality
45
and scattering on plane wave backgrounds
1
.

Despite the initial success in asymptotically flat backgrounds, generalizing the classical double copy to curved spacetimes presents additional challenges. Recent studies have begun to address the KS formulation in maximally symmetric backgrounds, such as AdS space
61
;
19
, which provides a natural setting for these extensions, showing that although the linear structure of the ansatz is preserved, the background curvature plays a crucial role in defining the equations satisfied by the single and zeroth copy fields.

In this work, we investigate the classical double copy applied to solutions with cylindrical symmetry in an AdS background, specifically black strings. Black strings are topological generalizations of black holes with horizon topology
\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}
, and have been the subject of intense study since the fundamental works of Lemos and others
50
;
51
. Furthermore, the treatment of string-like extended objects within the classical double copy formalism has been previously explored in
34
. The goal here is to verify the consistency of the generalized KS construction for these cylindrical geometries with AdS backgrounds and determine the nature of the effective sources in the gauge and scalar sectors.

We first analyze the neutral and static black string, demonstrating that the resulting gauge potential (single copy) corresponds to an electrostatic field generated by an infinite line of color charge, consistent with Maxwell’s equations in a cylindrical AdS background. Next, we extend the analysis to the case of the charged black string, where the energy-momentum tensor of the gravitational electromagnetic field induces an effective distributed current in the gauge theory. Furthermore, we discuss the zeroth copy, showing that the scalar profile associated with the KS perturbation satisfies the equation of a scalar field conformally coupled to the AdS geometry.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we briefly review the static black string solution in an AdS background. In Sec.
III
, we recast this geometry into KS form and develop its classical double copy, explicitly constructing the corresponding single and zeroth copies for the neutral configuration and subsequently extending the analysis to the charged black string. Finally, our conclusions are presented in Sec.
IV
.

II
Standard black strings

The general form of the metric describing a static black string in a
3+1
-dimensional spacetime, with a negative cosmological constant
50
;
17
, is given as follows

ds^{2}=-F(r)\,dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{F(r)}+r^{2}\,d\phi^{2}+\alpha^{2}r^{2}\,dz^{2}.

(1)

For such a spacetime, the metric (
1
) possesses cylindrical symmetry, where
0\leq r<\infty
,
0\leq\phi<2\pi
, and
-\infty<z<\infty
define,
respectively, the radial, angular, and axial coordinates. Furthermore,

\alpha^{2}\equiv-\Lambda/3>0
, where
\Lambda
is the cosmological constant, which appears directly in the action that characterizes the neutral and static black string,

S=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,(R-2\Lambda),

(2)

where
\kappa^{2}=8\pi G
and
R
is the Ricci scalar, which results in the complete form of Einstein’s field equations of general relativity, expressed in the form

G^{\mu}_{\nu}+\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\Lambda=\kappa^{2}T^{\mu}_{\nu}.

(3)

For our purposes, it is sufficient to use the
G_{0}^{0}
component of the Einstein tensor,
in order to obtain

G^{0}_{0}=\frac{rF^{\prime}(r)+F(r)}{r^{2}}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{d}{dr}[r\,F(r)],

(4)

with
F^{\prime}(r)=dF(r)/dr
, assuming
T^{\mu}_{\nu}=0
for
r\neq 0
, we obtain

G^{0}_{0}+\delta^{0}_{0}\Lambda=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{d}{dr}[r\,F(r)]-3\alpha^{2}=0.

(5)

Solving the differential equation above, we obtain the solution for the function
F(r)
of the black string spacetime, expressed as follows

F(r)=\alpha^{2}r^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha r}.

(6)

This equation is the result for the case of a static and neutral black string, where
\lambda
is associated with the mass density of the black string, and the event horizon can be determined simply by setting
F(r)=0
, that is,

r\equiv r_{h}=\frac{\lambda^{1/3}}{\alpha}.

(7)

For the case of the charged black string
51
, we consider the presence of an electromagnetic field in the action (
2
), thus the function given in (
6
) can be extended to

F(r)=\alpha^{2}r^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha r}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}r^{2}},

(8)

where
\beta
is a constant associated with the charge density of the black string.We find that, for distant observers, this solution extends uniformly along the
z
axis, yielding a
\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}
topology. It is observed that, both in the neutral case and in charged extensions, there exists a singularity at
r=0
. Moreover, we also note that this spacetime is not asymptotically flat; instead, as
r\rightarrow\infty
, the black string spacetime is asymptotically AdS.

III
The Classical Double Copy

The correspondence known in the literature as the classical double copy establishes a mapping between classical gravitational solutions and configurations in gauge theories. A systematic analysis of the classical double copy for statically symmetric KS metrics was developed in
66
. In addition, several other examples and applications of double copy relations have been obtained, both for flat spacetimes
62
;
25
;
7
and for extensions to curved backgrounds
8
;
65
and also in dual-field and supergravity theories
49
. Beyond the formulation based on the Kerr–Schild ansatz, which has been extensively applied to Schwarzschild, Reissner–Nordström, Kerr, Kerr–Newman and Banados–Teitelboim–Zanelli (BTZ) black holes, including extensions to (A)dS backgrounds
19
;
7
;
5
, alternative approaches to the classical double copy have also been proposed. In particular, the so-called Weyl double copy
26
;
33
;
32
;
20
;
69
;
38
;
39
;
59
;
27
;
2
;
6
;
71
establishes a direct correspondence involving the gravitational Weyl tensor and has proven particularly successful for type-D gravitational solutions
57
;
45
.

In our analysis, we employ the generalized KS form on an AdS background
67
;
70
;
61
;
64
, which can be written as

g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+2\,\mathcal{H}(r)\,k_{\mu}k_{\nu},

(9)

where
k_{\mu}
is a null vector with respect to both the background (A)dS metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
and the full metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, while
\mathcal{H}(r)
is the scalar function that characterizes the KS perturbation of the gravitational metric

Within the classical double copy formalism, starting from a gravitational solution written in the form (
9
), we define the associated single copy as a gauge potential obtained by replacing one of the null vectors with a constant vector in color space, according to the gravity–gauge correspondence. The associated gauge potential is given by

A_{\mu}^{\,a}=c^{a}\,\mathcal{H}(r)\,k_{\mu},

(10)

where
c^{a}
is a constant vector in color space. The zeroth copy, in turn, is simply described by the scalar profile
\mathcal{H}(r)
. These constructions establish a gravity–gauge–scalar correspondence in which the function
\mathcal{H}(r)
plays a central role, simultaneously encoding the gravitational perturbation, the associated gauge potential, and the scalar field appearing in the zeroth copy.

III.1
Neutral black string in Kerr–Schild form

We then apply the ansatz (
9
) to the neutral and static black string, in order to identify the scalar profile
\mathcal{H}(r)
and the null vector
k_{\mu}
on the cylindrical AdS background. Our goal is to cast the black string metric, Eq. (
1
), into the KS form, and for this first case, we’re dealing with a neutral black string. To achieve this, we introduce Eddington–Finkelstein (EF) coordinates, following a procedure analogous to the Schwarzschild metric for black holes in flat spacetime
68
. We consider the following ingoing coordinate transformation,

d\tau=dt+\frac{dr}{F(r)}.

(11)

Substituting this transformation into Eq. (
1
), we obtain the full metric

ds^{2}=-F(r)\,d\tau^{2}+2\,d\tau\,dr+r^{2}d\phi^{2}+\alpha^{2}r^{2}dz^{2}+2\mathcal{H}(r)\,(d\tau)^{2},

(12)

with

k_{\mu}dx^{\mu}=d\tau,\qquad\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}k_{\mu}k_{\nu}=0.

(13)

Since the central idea of the KS ansatz is to treat the mass parameter as a perturbation on a known background, we first identify the massless part of the metric function, corresponding to the AdS background,

F_{0}(r)=\alpha^{2}r^{2}.

(14)

Accordingly, the background metric can be written as

\bar{d}s^{2}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-\alpha^{2}r^{2}d\tau^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{\alpha^{2}r^{2}}+r^{2}d\phi^{2}+\alpha^{2}r^{2}dz^{2},

(15)

which, in EF coordinates, takes the form

\bar{d}s^{2}=-\alpha^{2}r^{2}d\tau^{2}+2\,d\tau\,dr+r^{2}d\phi^{2}+\alpha^{2}r^{2}dz^{2}.

(16)

This metric describes the cylindrical AdS spacetime, a curved but vacuum geometry with no matter or mass contribution. It plays a role analogous to flat spacetime in the Schwarzschild case with a Minkowski background
62
;
19
;
23
. Comparing the temporal components of the full metric and the background metric, we find

g_{\tau\tau}=-F(r)=-\alpha^{2}r^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2\alpha r},\qquad\bar{g}_{\tau\tau}=-F_{0}(r)=-\alpha^{2}r^{2}.

(17)

In the KS form, only the
\tau\tau
component receives a correction, such that

g_{\tau\tau}=\bar{g}_{\tau\tau}+2\mathcal{H}(r),

(18)

this allows us to directly identify the KS scalar function as

\mathcal{H}(r)=\frac{\lambda}{2\alpha r}.

(19)

Having explicitly identified the KS scalar function
\mathcal{H}(r)
, we are now in a position to construct the corresponding single copy in the gauge theory.

III.2
The single copy

In ingoing EF coordinates
(\tau,r,\phi,z)
, the KS null vector takes the simple form

k_{\mu}dx^{\mu}=d\tau,\qquad\implies\qquad k_{\mu}=(1,0,0,0),

(20)

so that the gauge potential has only a single nonvanishing component,

A_{\tau}^{a}=c^{a}\frac{\lambda}{2\alpha r},\qquad A_{r}^{a}=A_{\phi}^{a}=A_{z}^{a}=0.

(21)

Unlike the Schwarzschild case on a flat background, where an additional gauge transformation is required to eliminate the radial component of the potential, for the black string in EF coordinates the gauge potential is already purely temporal, and no further gauge transformation is necessary. The associated field strength tensor therefore describes a cylindrical electrostatic field, corresponding to the presence of an infinite line of color charge along the axis of the black string. The resulting field strength has a single nonvanishing component,

F_{\tau r}^{a}=-\,\partial_{r}A_{\tau}^{a}=c^{a}\frac{\lambda}{2\alpha r^{2}},

(22)

while all other components of
F_{\mu\nu}
vanish.

To verify that the potential (
21
) satisfies Maxwell’s equations in the cylindrical AdS bottom, we initially consider the sourceless sector for
r\neq 0
, evaluating

\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=0,

(23)

where, for the AdS background in EF coordinates, we have that

\sqrt{-\bar{g}}=\alpha r^{2},\qquad\bar{g}^{\tau r}=1,\qquad\bar{g}^{rr}=\alpha^{2}r^{2},\qquad\bar{g}^{\tau\tau}=0.

(24)

It then follows that

\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\tau}=\frac{1}{\alpha r^{2}}\,\partial_{r}\!\left(\alpha r^{2}F^{r\tau}\right)=0,

(25)

while the other components cancel each other out. Therefore, for
r\neq 0
the single copy corresponds to a sourceless solution of Maxwell’s equations in the AdS background. This configuration describes a purely radial electric field in the transverse plane and uniform along the axial direction
z
. As in the Schwarzschild case, the associated color charge arises only through a localized distributed source at the origin
62
.

Regarding the interpretation of the source, up to this point in the analysis of the single copy we have assumed
T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=0
for
r\neq 0
, which is sufficient to determine the functional form of
F(r)
and of the scalar function
\mathcal{H}(r)
. However, as in the case of the Schwarzschild black hole, the complete solution should be interpreted as being generated by a localized source associated with the mass of the black string. In the spherically symmetric Schwarzschild case, the gravitational source is described by a pointlike energy–momentum tensor

62
;
19
,

T^{\mu\nu}=M\,u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{x}),

(26)

corresponding to a mass concentrated at the origin of three-dimensional space. For our neutral and static black string case, the situation is analogous, with the difference that cylindrical symmetry implies that the source is not pointlike, but rather extended along the axial
z
direction. In this case, the appropriate source is characterized by a linear mass density
\lambda
, associated with a delta distribution on the transverse
(r,\phi)
plane. Accordingly, the energy–momentum tensor can then be written in the following form

T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\sim\lambda\,u^{\mu}u_{\nu}\,\delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{x}_{\perp}),

(27)

where
\mathbf{x}_{\perp}
denotes the coordinates transverse to the string. In cylindrical coordinates, the two-dimensional delta function takes the form

\delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{x}_{\perp})=\frac{\delta(r)}{2\pi r},

(28)

Indeed, the source is localized at
r=0
and is uniform along the
z
direction. This structure clarifies why solving Einstein’s equations for
r\neq 0
yields a purely vacuum solution: all information about the matter content is encoded in the integration constant
\lambda
appearing in (
6
). This parameter directly measures the strength of the underlying source and signals the presence of a localized mass distribution at the axis. From this perspective, the black string is naturally interpreted as the gravitational field generated by an infinite line of mass embedded in a cylindrical AdS background.

Due to the singular behavior of the field at the axis (
A_{\tau}^{a}\sim 1/r
and
F_{\tau r}^{a}\sim 1/r^{2}
), Maxwell’s equations must be understood in the distributional sense and written with sources, as follows

\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu\,a}=J^{\nu\,a}.

(29)

By cylindrical symmetry, the current has only a temporal component, and the most general form compatible with these symmetries is

J^{\tau a}=q^{a}\,\delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{x}_{\perp}),\qquad J^{r\,a}=J^{\phi\,a}=J^{z\,a}=0,

(30)

where
q^{a}
denotes the color charge per unit length along the
z
direction. Fixing the normalization through Gauss’s law, we obtain the equivalent form

J^{\tau a}=\frac{c^{a}\lambda}{2}\,\frac{\delta(r)}{r},

(31)

with

q^{a}=\pi\,c^{a}\,\lambda.

(32)

Thus, we see that the single copy describes an infinite line of color charge along the axis of the black string.

III.3
The zeroth copy

The zeroth copy is obtained by removing the KS null vector from the single copy, leaving only the scalar profile
\mathcal{H}(r)
which characterizes the KS perturbation of the metric. In our case, this scalar function was identified in Eq. (
19
).
In the context of the classical double copy in maximally symmetric curved spacetimes, such as the Schwarzschild–(A)dS black hole in
d=4
spacetime dimensions, the scalar function does not simply satisfy a Laplace equation
19
. Instead, it obeys the differential equation associated with a conformally coupled scalar field
19
;
58
, which can be written as

\left(\bar{\nabla}^{2}-\frac{\bar{R}}{6}\right)\,\phi=j,

(33)

where
\bar{R}
is the Ricci scalar of the background spacetime. Here,
\phi
plays the role of our scalar function, while
j
represents a scalar source. In the study of the black string on a cylindrical AdS background, there is no localized scalar source, and we therefore set
j=0
. Consequently, one expects that in this background the function
\mathcal{H}(r)
satisfies the equation of a conformally coupled scalar field,

\left(\bar{\nabla}^{2}-\frac{\bar{R}}{6}\right)\mathcal{H}=0.

(34)

Since in this spacetime one has
\bar{R}=4\Lambda
(in
d=4
, with
\Lambda<0
), the above equation can be written as

\bar{\nabla}^{2}\mathcal{H}(r)-\zeta\,\Lambda\,\mathcal{H}(r)=0,

(35)

where
\zeta
depends on the conventions adopted in the curved-space double copy. For AdS backgrounds, the appropriate choice is
\zeta=2/3
, in agreement with previous analyses of the double copy in curved spacetime
19
. Computing explicitly the covariant Laplacian on the cylindrical AdS background, and assuming that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(r)
, we obtain

\bar{\nabla}^{2}\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{\alpha r^{2}}\partial_{r}\!\left(\alpha r^{2}\bar{g}^{rr}\partial_{r}\mathcal{H}\right)=-\frac{\lambda\alpha}{2r}.

(36)

Using the fact that in
\mathrm{AdS}_{4}
one has
\Lambda=-3\alpha^{2}
, we verify that the differential equation (
35
) is indeed satisfied, without the need to introduce localized source terms. Thus, in the case of the AdS black string, the curvature of spacetime itself effectively plays the role of a source for the scalar function. This completes the gravity–gauge–scalar correspondence within the framework of the classical double copy, showing that the same scalar function governing the gravitational perturbation in the KS decomposition also consistently determines the structures associated with the single and zeroth copies for the black string.

III.4
Double copy of the charged black string

In contrast to the neutral case, the charged black string is a non-vacuum solution of Einstein’s equations, due to the explicit contribution of the electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor. Therefore, once we have established the KS–AdS formulation and the corresponding simple, zero-order copies for the neutral case, we can then extend the same classical double-copy construction to the case of the loaded black string. That is, we consider the static cylindrically symmetric Einstein–Maxwell solution with a negative cosmological constant, starting directly from the result given in Eq. (
8
). We note that the KS decomposition is completely analogous to the neutral black string case, allowing us to avoid unnecessary repetitions and to make use of the results obtained previously, performing only an extension of the neutral configuration by adding the charge correction term. In
19
;
24
, a brief construction for black strings and black branes in an
\mathrm{AdS}_{d}
background with
d>4
is presented, starting from a base metric following the formulation of
21
;
36
;
41
for black strings in AdS space. In our case, we adopt the black string approach of
50
;
51
;
17
in a four-dimensional spacetime.

For the single copy, starting from (
10
), we obtain the following expression for the gauge potential

A_{\tau}^{a}=c^{a}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2\alpha r}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\alpha^{2}r^{2}}\right),\qquad A_{r}^{\,a}=A_{\phi}^{\,a}=A_{z}^{\,a}=0.

(37)

while the associated Maxwell field tensor, with its only nonvanishing component, is expressed as

F_{\tau r}^{a}=-\partial_{r}A_{\tau}^{a}=c^{a}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2\alpha r^{2}}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}r^{3}}\right).

(38)

This is the radial electric field associated with the single copy of the charged black string. The first term is related to the mass contribution of the solution, while the second term reflects the explicit presence of the electric charge, in close analogy with the spherically symmetric Reissner–Nordström–AdS case discussed in
19
;
5
.

Regarding the sources in the single copy, in the neutral case the configuration obtained in the single copy satisfies Maxwell’s equations on the cylindrical AdS background in the absence of distributed sources for
r\neq 0
, that is,
\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu\,a}=0
in the bulk. For the charged black string, however, the gravitational solution is not a vacuum solution; it solves the Einstein–Maxwell equations with
\Lambda<0
, so that the energy–momentum tensor of the electromagnetic field is nonvanishing for
r\neq 0
. As a consequence, one expects the gauge-theory side to satisfy Maxwell’s equations with a source given by (
29
). Since the only relevant component is
\nu=\tau
, we can determine the effective current in the following way.

\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\tau\,a}=\frac{1}{\alpha r^{2}}\,\partial_{r}\!\left(\alpha r^{2}F^{r\tau\,a}\right)=J^{\tau\,a},

(39)

From Eq. (
38
), it follows that

\alpha r^{2}F^{r\tau\,a}=\alpha r^{2}\,c^{a}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2\alpha r^{2}}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}r^{3}}\right)=c^{a}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha r}\right).

(40)

Thus, for
r\neq 0
we have

J^{\tau\,a}_{\rm bulk}(r)=\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\tau\,a}=\frac{c^{a}\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}r^{4}},

(41)

while the remaining components of the current vanish. Moreover, the presence of the
\sim 1/r
term in the potential (
37
), associated with the parameter
\lambda
, implies, in the distributional sense, the existence of a source localized along the axis, as in the neutral case discussed previously, corresponding to an infinite line of color charge. Therefore, for our case, the complete form of the current can be expressed as

J^{\tau\,a}=q^{a}\,\delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{x}_{\perp})+\frac{c^{a}\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}r^{4}},\qquad J^{r\,a}=J^{\phi\,a}=J^{z\,a}=0.

(42)

Finally, for the zeroth copy, upon removing the null vector
k_{\mu}
from the single copy, the scalar profile is written in the following form

\mathcal{H}(r)=\frac{\lambda}{2\alpha r}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\alpha^{2}r^{2}},

(43)

On an AdS background, this profile satisfies the equation of a scalar field conformally coupled to the background, with no need to introduce an additional localized scalar source, as in the neutral case.

IV
CONCLUSION

In this work, we explore the classical double copy in the context of gravitational solutions with cylindrical symmetry, taking as a case study the static black string of Lemos
50
;
51
in an AdS background. Unlike the cases most commonly studied in the literature, which are dominated by spherically symmetric solutions and pointlike sources, black strings constitute gravitational objects with extended horizons and linearly distributed sources. We begin by analyzing the geometric structure of this solution, which can be explicitly written in KS form over an AdS background. This decomposition allows for a clear identification of the scalar profile responsible for the gravitational perturbation, providing the necessary framework for applying the classical double copy formalism. Based on this structure, we construct the associated single copy, obtaining a gauge potential that describes a cylindrical electrostatic field, radial in the transverse plane and uniform along the axial direction of the string.

An analysis of Maxwell’s equations shows that, in the neutral case, the solution is source-free in the bulk for
r\neq 0
, while the
1/r
-type singularity in the gauge potential indicates, in the distributional sense, the presence of a current localized along the axis of the black string, interpreted as an infinite line of color charge, in analogy with the spherically symmetric Schwarzschild case
62
. In turn, the zeroth copy is obtained by removing the KS null vector, and the resulting scalar profile satisfies, on an AdS background, the equation of a conformally coupled scalar field, without the need to introduce additional localized scalar sources, since the spacetime curvature itself effectively closes the equations.

We extend the analysis to the charged black string case by considering the static cylindrically symmetric solution of Einstein–Maxwell theory with a negative cosmological constant. We observe that the inclusion of electric charge affects exclusively the scalar profile of the KS ansatz, preserving both the linearity of the decomposition and the essential structure of the classical double copy. In the single copy sector, the electric charge gives rise to an additional contribution to the gauge current, which manifests itself as a distributed term in the bulk, in addition to the localized source along the axis associated with the mass parameter. In the zeroth copy, we verify that the modified scalar profile continues to satisfy the equation of a scalar field conformally coupled to the AdS background, with no need to introduce additional scalar sources.

As future perspectives, we can naturally highlight an extension of this study to the case of rotating blackstrings, which can be presented analogously to the spherically symmetric case expressed in Kerr–Newman geometry in standard Boyer–Lindquist coordinates
51
, in which angular momentum and the presence of cross-coordinate terms introduce new structures into the KS ansatz, as well as into the interpretation of the single and zeroth copies. Moreover, a particularly interesting scenario that has not been widely explored in the literature concerns regular black strings, especially those arising from black-bounce–type regularizations
55
;
29
;
54
;
16
;
52
;
53
, which may provide new avenues for investigating the classical double copy formalism in the presence of smoothed sources. In an analogous manner, these considerations also apply to other regularizations of black holes
25
, such as those emerging from string T-duality
63
;
30
;
42
;
43
, opening up new possibilities for studying the double copy in gravitational geometries more realistic. As well as several other important applications that have been investigated in the literature within the context of the Kerr–Schild classical double copy.

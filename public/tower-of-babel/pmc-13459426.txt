Influence of posterior malleolar fragment size on clinical function and spatiotemporal gait parameters in trimalleolar ankle fractures
Authors: Ali Aydilek, Yusuf Erdem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPosterior malleolar fractures accompanying trimalleolar ankle injuries may compromise joint stability and lead to long-term functional impairment. However, the impact of posterior malleolar fragment (PMF) size on postoperative functional recovery remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the association between PMF size and morphology with postoperative clinical outcomes using both functional scoring systems and smartphone-based gait analysis.MethodsSeventy-two patients who underwent surgical treatment for trimalleolar fractures between 2017 and 2022 and met the inclusion criteria were retrospectively reviewed. Patients were categorized into two groups based on PMF size: <25% and ≥ 25%. Fragment configuration was classified according to the Bartoníček system. Functional outcomes were assessed using AOFAS, FAAM, SF-36, and VAS scores, while gait performance was analyzed through spatiotemporal parameters obtained with the GaitAnalyzer application. Results were compared with an age- and sex-matched control group of 72 healthy individuals.ResultsPatients with PMF ≥ 25% demonstrated significantly higher grades of post-traumatic osteoarthritis (p = 0.020). The < 25% group had significantly better SF-36 physical function (p = 0.040) and FAAM-Sport scores (p = 0.023). No significant differences were observed between groups for AOFAS, FAAM-ADL, or VAS scores (p > 0.05). Compared with controls, both patient groups showed pronounced impairments in gait velocity, step time, step length, and cadence (p < 0.001); however, these parameters did not differ significantly between the PMF < 25% and ≥ 25% groups (p > 0.05). In multivariate regression, FAAM-Sport was independently associated with PMF ≥ 25% (β=–15.589; p = 0.022), the presence of joint step-off (β=–21.338; p = 0.002), and male sex (β=+16.448; p = 0.014). Surgical fixation of the posterior fragment was associated with improved FAAM-Sport scores (β=+17.171; p = 0.046).ConclusionA posterior malleolar fragment size ≥ 25% is associated with a higher incidence of post-traumatic osteoarthritis and greater impairment in sport-related functional activities. In contrast, objective spatiotemporal gait parameters appear to be independent of fragment size. Multivariable analysis demonstrates that functional outcomes are influenced not only by fragment size but also by factors such as the presence of articular step-off and the development of post-traumatic osteoarthritis. These findings indicate that postoperative functional recovery following trimalleolar ankle fractures is a multifactorial process.Keywords: Trimalleolar fracture, Posterior malleolus, Fragment size, Gait analysis, Smartphone application

Introduction

Ankle fractures represent a significant portion of lower extremity injuries encountered in orthopaedic practice and may lead to long-term functional impairment if inadequately treated [1, 2]. Numerous studies have reported poorer postoperative functional outcomes in cases involving a concomitant posterior malleolar fracture [3, 4]. The posterior malleolus refers to the posterior aspect of the distal tibial articular surface, and fractures involving this region are commonly referred to as posterior malleolar fractures (PMF). Consequently, posterior malleolar injuries have been the focus of extensive research, particularly regarding fragment size, the choice between surgical and conservative management, and optimal fixation strategies [5–7].

Previous investigations have demonstrated that increasing posterior malleolar fragment (PMF) size and greater articular step-off are associated with worse functional and radiographic outcomes in ankle fractures. Accordingly, there is broad consensus that surgical fixation should be considered when the PMF involves more than 25–33% of the tibial plafond and/or when an articular step-off of ≥ 2 mm is present [8, 9]. However, despite these commonly used thresholds, the extent to which fragment size alone influences postoperative functional recovery remains a subject of ongoing debate. In recent years, functional recovery following ankle fractures has increasingly been evaluated not only using clinical scoring systems but also through objective gait assessment. Spatiotemporal gait analysis provides quantitative information regarding walking speed, step length, cadence, and gait symmetry, thereby offering an objective assessment of functional performance.

The widespread availability and relatively low cost of smartphone-based gait analysis systems have further facilitated their use in clinical and research settings [10, 11] .

Although the relationship between posterior malleolar fractures and surgical decision-making has been widely discussed in the literature, the independent impact of PMF size on postoperative functional outcomes particularly when evaluated using objective gait parameters has not been fully clarified. Notably, studies simultaneously evaluating fragment size, clinical functional scores, and spatiotemporal gait parameters remain limited.

Therefore, the aim of this study was to investigate the influence of PMF size on postoperative functional outcomes in patients with trimalleolar ankle fractures, using both validated clinical scoring systems and smartphone-based spatiotemporal gait analysis.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient selection

This retrospective study included 72 patients who underwent surgical treatment for trimalleolar ankle fractures between January 2017 and December 2022 and completed a minimum clinical follow-up of 12 months. Only patients with unilateral injuries, no prior trauma or surgery to the lower extremities, and adequate follow-up were eligible for inclusion. Posterior malleolar fractures were present in all cases; however, the decision to fix the posterior fragment depended on fracture morphology and surgeon preference. Patients with open fractures, bilateral injuries, a history of spinal trauma, or neurological or systemic conditions that could affect gait patterns were excluded.

The control group consisted of age- and sex-matched healthy volunteers with no history of lower extremity trauma or surgery. Individuals with any clinical condition that could influence gait performance were not included in the control group.

Treatment protocol

The surgical approach was determined according to the fracture pattern. Indications for posterior malleolar fixation included a fragment size ≥ 25% of the tibial plafond or an articular step-off ≥ 2 mm. A posterolateral approach was preferred in most cases, while a posteromedial approach was used for medially oriented fragments. Following anatomic reduction, fixation was performed using screws or plates depending on fragment configuration. Fibular and medial malleolar fractures were stabilized using techniques appropriate for the specific fracture morphology. Syndesmotic stability was evaluated intraoperatively using the Cotton test, and syndesmotic screw fixation was performed in cases with positive findings.

Functional and radiological assessment

Functional outcomes were evaluated using AOFAS, FAAM, SF-36, and VAS scores. Radiological assessment included PMF size, Bartoníček classification, articular step-off, and the degree of post-traumatic osteoarthritis. Post-traumatic osteoarthritis was graded according to the Kellgren–Lawrence classification [12]. Posterior malleolar fragment size was measured on sagittal computed tomography (CT) images as the percentage of the tibial plafond articular surface involved. Articular step-off was assessed on postoperative radiographs and categorized as present or absent. Other radiographic parameters were assessed digitally using the final follow-up radiographs, while functional outcome scores were recorded during the patients’ last clinical visit.

Gait analysis

Gait analysis was performed using the GaitAnalyzer smartphone application (Control One LLC, NM, USA). The application was operated on a Samsung Galaxy Note 10 Plus device. A belt with a dedicated phone pouch was secured around each participant’s waist, and the smartphone was positioned vertically at the level of the L3 spinous process along the midline. Prior to data acquisition, participants completed a 30-second walk to ensure sensor calibration. All individuals then walked along a 25-meter straight corridor at a self-selected natural pace while wearing athletic shoes. Each participant completed four trials (two out-and-back walks), and the mean values were used for analysis (Fig. 1).

The automatically generated spatiotemporal parameters included gait velocity, step time, step length, cadence, step-time symmetry, step-length symmetry, and vertical center-of-mass displacement (vert-COM) (Fig. 2).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using the SPSS version 21.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). A post hoc power analysis was performed for the primary gait parameters, and the current sample size was found to provide adequate statistical power for the analyses conducted. Continuous variables were presented in mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (min-max), while categorical variables were presented in number and frequency. The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For comparisons of continuous variables and functional scores between two groups, the independent samples t-test was used for normally distributed data, and the Mann–Whitney U test was applied for non-normally distributed data. Comparisons of scale scores across three-group variables were performed using the Kruskal–Wallis analysis of variance, and in cases of statistical significance, post-hoc pairwise differences were examined with the Kruskal–Wallis multiple comparison test. Since multiple functional outcome measures were evaluated, the potential risk of type I error was considered. However, correction for multiple comparisons was not applied, as each assessment tool (AOFAS, FAAM, SF-36, and VAS) was designed to evaluate distinct clinical and functional domains. Therefore, the results were interpreted cautiously, particularly for findings with borderline statistical significance. Correlations between functional scores and gait parameters were evaluated using Spearman’s correlation coefficient. To identify independent predictors of FAAM-Sport scores, a multivariate linear regression analysis was conducted. A p value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Multivariable linear regression analysis identified several independent predictors of FAAM-Sport performance. A PMF involving more than 25% of the tibial plafond (β = − 15.589; p = 0.022), the presence of articular step-off (β = − 21.338; p = 0.002), and a higher grade of post-traumatic osteoarthritis were found to be significant negative determinants of functional outcome. In contrast, surgical fixation of the posterior malleolus (β = +17.171; p = 0.046) and male sex (β = +16.448; p = 0.014) independently predicted superior FAAM-Sport scores. Age, body mass index, and the Bartoníček classification demonstrated no significant association with functional performance (p > 0.05) (Table 5).

Discussion

Trimalleolar ankle fractures involving the posterior malleolus are associated with inferior functional and radiological outcomes due to disruption of the articular surface and compromised joint stability [3, 13, 14]. Despite this established association, the precise role of PMF size in determining long-term clinical and functional outcomes remains controversial. While several studies have suggested that larger posterior fragments are associated with worse postoperative recovery, others have failed to demonstrate a consistent relationship. These conflicting findings indicate that PMF size alone may not sufficiently explain functional impairment and should be interpreted within a broader biomechanical and clinical context. In addition, the use of fixed size-based thresholds, particularly the 25% cutoff, remains controversial. Fragment size alone may not provide sufficient discriminatory power, and factors such as articular step-off and fracture morphology may be more relevant. Therefore, the 25% threshold should be interpreted as a conventional classification rather than an absolute prognostic criterion.

In the present study, patients with PMF ≥ 25% demonstrated significantly lower SF-36 physical function and FAAM-Sport scores compared with those with smaller fragments. These findings suggest that larger PMFs are associated with more pronounced limitations during activities requiring higher physical demand. In contrast, no significant differences were observed between PMF groups in FAAM-ADL, AOFAS, or VAS scores, indicating that the functional impact of fragment size becomes more apparent during sport-related or high-demand activities rather than during routine daily living. This observation supports the concept that posterior fragment size preferentially affects higher-level functional performance rather than general activities of daily life.

When functional outcomes such as AOFAS, FAAM, and SF-36 scores are evaluated in the literature, the relationship between PMF size and clinical results appears inconsistent across studies. Mingo-Robinet et al. reported a significant reduction in AOFAS scores in cases with fragment sizes ≥ 25% [6]., whereas Xu et al. did not demonstrate any statistically significant differences in AOFAS or VAS scores across varying fragment-size groups [15]. Similarly, although Abarquero-Diezhandino et al. reported lower AOFAS scores in patients with fragment sizes > 25%, they found no significant differences in SF-36 or VAS scores [16]. Conversely, Neumann and Rammelt demonstrated that PMF size was not associated with OMAS, AOFAS, FFI, or SF-36 scores on long-term follow-up [17]. Taken together, these findings suggest that increases in fragment size do not translate uniformly across all functional outcome measures; rather, the sensitivity of the assessment tools and the activity level of the patient population may influence the detectability of functional impairment. Chong et al. observed that FAAM-Sport scores were numerically lower in cases with PMF ≥ 25%, although this difference did not reach statistical significance [18]. This observation indicates that sport-related and high-demand activities may capture the functional impact of posterior fragments more effectively, yet may not consistently produce significant differences across all cohorts.

In our study, the significantly lower FAAM-Sport scores observed in patients with PMF ≥ 25% suggest that larger posterior fragments may lead to more pronounced functional limitations during high-demand physical activities. The absence of a marked difference in daily living activities indicates that this effect becomes more apparent at the level of athletic performance, emphasizing that fragment size should be considered clinically, particularly in patients with higher activity levels.

Although FAAM-Sport scores appeared to be associated solely with fragment size in the between-group analysis, the multivariate regression model demonstrated that multiple independent variables contribute to sport-related performance. The finding that articular step-off was the strongest negative predictor implies that the reduction in performance may be linked to irregularities of the joint surface. In addition, post-traumatic osteoarthritis was identified as an independent negative determinant of FAAM-Sport scores, suggesting that degenerative joint changes further contribute to impaired sport-related function. In contrast, surgical fixation of the posterior fragment and male sex were associated with higher FAAM-Sport scores.

The association between male sex and higher FAAM-Sport scores may be related to differences in physical activity levels, muscle strength, and sports participation. However, as these variables were not directly assessed, residual confounding cannot be excluded, and this finding should be interpreted with caution.

In addition, the present study was not adequately powered to compare different fixation methods. Therefore, further studies with larger and more balanced subgroups are needed to determine whether the observed beneficial effect is attributable to fixation itself or is partly driven by the plate fixation subgroup. Considering the potential biomechanical advantages of posterior buttress plating, this issue warrants further investigation in future studies. These results indicate that sport-specific functional capacity is influenced not only by fragment size but also by the quality of reduction and the structural integrity of the ankle joint.

While functional outcome scores are valuable in clinical assessment, they inherently contain subjective components. Therefore, gait analysis emerges as an important complementary tool, providing objective and quantitative measures of functional capacity. Recent studies have increasingly focused on evaluating recovery in ankle fracture patients through both traditional clinical scores and spatiotemporal gait parameters.

Segal et al. compared unimalleolar, bimalleolar, and trimalleolar fracture patterns and reported no significant differences between fracture types; however, they observed a marked reduction in gait velocity across all groups when compared with healthy controls [19]. Similarly, Sander van Hoeve et al. demonstrated that patients with trimalleolar fractures exhibit decreased gait speed and reduced talocrural range of motion, both of which correlated with SF-36 physical function scores [20]. Collectively, these findings indicate that post-traumatic alterations in joint biomechanics impair gait performance irrespective of fracture classification.

Studies specifically evaluating posterior malleolar fractures are more limited. Schoenmakers et al. reported that increasing posterior fragment size was associated with reduced gait velocity and diminished talocrural range of motion, accompanied by parallel declines in AOFAS, FADI, and SF-36 scores [21]. In contrast, Andrew et al. observed pronounced early gait impairment in patients with trimalleolar fractures, yet noted that fixation of the posterior fragment did not result in a significant improvement in gait parameters [22].

Consistent with these observations, patients in the present study demonstrated marked impairments in spatiotemporal gait parameters—including walking speed, step time, step length, and cadence—when compared with healthy controls. Importantly, no statistically significant differences were observed between the PMF < 25% and PMF ≥ 25% groups across any gait parameter. This finding indicates that gait impairment following trimalleolar ankle fractures reflects the global functional impact of ankle trauma rather than posterior fragment size alone. The revised presentation of gait data further clarifies that the primary distinction lies between injured patients and healthy controls, rather than between fragment-size subgroups.

The absence of significant differences in gait parameters between the PMF subgroups may suggest that fragment size has a limited effect on gait performance. However, these findings should be interpreted with caution.

To explore potential mechanisms underlying gait impairment, pain severity was evaluated as a possible mediating factor. Correlation analyses demonstrated significant associations between VAS pain scores and global spatiotemporal gait parameters, including walking speed, step time, step length, and cadence, whereas no meaningful relationships were observed between pain and symmetry-related parameters or vertical center-of-mass displacement. These findings suggest that residual pain predominantly influences overall gait performance rather than balance or symmetry, providing a plausible mechanistic explanation for persistent gait abnormalities independent of fragment size.

In recent years, smartphone-based gait analysis systems have emerged as portable, low-cost, and reliable alternatives to traditional laboratory-based methods [23–25]. These systems enable objective and reproducible assessment of functional outcomes owing to their ease of use in routine clinical practice. Çankaya et al. demonstrated that the Gait-Analyzer application provides reliable measurement of gait patterns in patients following knee arthroplasty [10]. Using the same methodology, our study objectively evaluated the impact of posterior malleolar fractures on gait parameters, thereby representing one of the first examples in the literature to assess this fracture pattern with a smartphone-based gait analysis approach.

Postoperative rehabilitation constitutes an important determinant of functional recovery and clinical outcomes following ankle fractures. Although a standardized rehabilitation protocol was prescribed at our institution, individual patient adherence could not be objectively verified due to the retrospective nature of the study. As a result, rehabilitation-related variables could not be quantitatively incorporated into the statistical analyses and were therefore considered a potential confounding factor. Nevertheless, the observation of comparable impairments in functional scores and spatiotemporal gait parameters across both PMF subgroups when compared with healthy controls suggests that the observed clinical and functional limitations are unlikely to be attributable solely to differences in rehabilitation adherence.

Our study has several limitations. First, the retrospective design inherently restricts data control and limits the standardization of clinical variables. Second, surgeries were performed by different surgeons, and variability in operative timing and postoperative rehabilitation adherence could not be objectively assessed, preventing complete homogeneity in the treatment process. Also, the limited number of patients precluded meaningful comparative analyses of fixation methods and Bartoníček subgroups. Furthermore, the present study was not adequately powered to compare different fixation methods. Therefore, further studies with larger and more balanced subgroups are needed to determine whether the observed beneficial effect is attributable to fixation itself or is partly driven by the plate fixation subgroup. Considering the potential biomechanical advantages of posterior buttress plating, this issue warrants further investigation in future studies. The inability to quantify soft-tissue injury and the lack of standardized radiological assessment intervals across patients represent additional methodological constraints. Furthermore, gait analysis was performed at a single time point, and the smartphone-based system used in this study provides only spatiotemporal parameters rather than comprehensive kinematic data, thereby limiting the scope of functional evaluation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that PMF size alone is not the principal determinant of postoperative gait performance or basic functional outcomes following trimalleolar ankle fractures. Although patients with PMF ≥ 25% exhibited a higher incidence of post-traumatic osteoarthritis and significantly lower SF-36 physical function and FAAM-Sport scores, no meaningful differences were observed between fragment-size groups with respect to activities of daily living or objective spatiotemporal gait parameters. These findings indicate that the clinical impact of PMF size becomes more evident in high-demand functional activities rather than routine daily performance.

Moreover, multivariable analysis highlights the importance of reduction quality and joint integrity, as articular step-off and osteoarthritis grade emerged as independent predictors of sport-specific functional outcomes. Collectively, these results support a multifactorial interpretation of postoperative recovery in trimalleolar ankle fractures and suggest that treatment strategies should prioritize restoration of articular congruence and long-term joint preservation rather than relying solely on fragment size as a prognostic indicator.

EGFR-directed antibodies promote HER2 ADC internalization and efficacy
Authors: Avantika Gupta, Flavia Michelini, Hong Shao, Celine Yeh, Joshua Z Drago, Dazhi Liu, Eric Rosiek, Yevgeniy Romin, Negin Ghafourian, Sheeno Thyparambil, Sandra Misale, Wungki Park, Elisa de Stanchina, Yelena Y Janjigian, Rona Yaeger, Bob T Li, Sarat Chandarlapaty
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryTrastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) is a human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-targeting antibody drug conjugate that has remarkable activity in HER2-positive cancers. However, the degree of benefit of T-DXd is not uniform among solid tumors even with high levels of HER2. Despite high HER2 expression, the HER2/T-DXd complex may not always undergo internalization and payload release dependent on the receptor’s conformation and context. We hypothesize that epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), a dimerization partner of HER2, can modulate HER2 trafficking through endocytic pathways and affect T-DXd uptake. We demonstrate that elevated EGFR expression levels can promote EGFR/HER2 heterodimer formation and suppress T-DXd internalization and efficacy. Knockdown of EGFR expression or pharmacologic stimulation of EGFR endocytosis with EGFR monoclonal antibodies restores T-DXd trafficking and antitumor activity in EGFR-overexpressing cancers in vivo. Our results reveal EGFR overexpression to be a potential mechanism of resistance to T-DXd, which can be overcome by combination therapy strategies targeting EGFR.Keywords: HER2-positive cancers, EGFR overexpression, ADC internalization, T-DXd resistance

Introduction

The identification and exploitation of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2 or ERBB2) as an actionable target has been one of the most remarkable success stories in oncology. Several pharmacologic approaches have been successfully developed to target HER2 including kinase inhibitors and monoclonal antibodies (mAbs), but the recent advances in anti-HER2 antibody drug conjugates (ADCs) have considerably expanded the scope and efficacy of anti-HER2 therapies.1,2,3,4,5 Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) is an anti-HER2 ADC, which was initially approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for HER2-overexpressing breast and gastric cancers.6,7 Its promising activity in lineage-agnostic studies8,9 has led to a tumor-agnostic approval for T-DXd in HER2-positive advanced solid cancers. Moreover, activity has been observed in tumors lacking HER2 overexpression but having some proclivity to rapid HER2 internalization such as in ERBB2-mutant non-small cell lung cancers.10 These and other studies11,12,13 point to high levels of HER2 cell surface expression being only one of several factors that dictate T-DXd sensitivity.

The canonical mechanism of ADC action involves target antigen binding, antigen/ADC complex internalization, linker cleavage, and release of the cytotoxic payload.14 Efficient internalization of the antigen/ADC complex is critical for breakdown of the linker by tumor cell-associated proteolytic enzymes or reducing conditions. HER2 exists in a state of dynamic equilibrium between endocytosis and recycling on the cell surface,15 and its rate of internalization is determined by expression level, antibody binding, and interaction with its dimerization partners. When the mAb backbone of T-DXd, trastuzumab, binds to HER2, it promotes clustering of the receptor and endocytosis of the HER2/mAb complex.16,17,18 Trastuzumab-mediated receptor cross-linking is most efficient in regions of membrane ruffles where HER2 density is the highest, leading to formation of HER2 homo-dimers and oligomers followed by subsequent endocytosis.19,20 Indeed, high HER2 clustering density has been associated with better response to trastuzumab-based therapy.21 Thus, factors that constrain HER2 homodimerization and clustering have the potential to suppress HER2 ADC trafficking and efficacy.

In this study, we illustrate that overexpressed epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) promotes EGFR/HER2 heterodimer formation at the expense of HER2 homodimerization, impairs HER2/T-DXd trafficking, and suppresses the antitumor activity of T-DXd. To overcome this mechanism of resistance to T-DXd, we utilize a combination treatment with the FDA-approved EGFR antibodies and demonstrate enhanced T-DXd uptake and synergistic tumor killing of EGFR-overexpressing cancers including colorectal (CRC), glioma, head and neck, gastric, and pancreatic cancers. We propose that EGFR mAbs are a rational therapeutic partner for T-DXd in EGFR-overexpressing cancers.

Results

EGFR co-expression with HER2 suppresses T-DXd internalization

One cancer type where HER2 overexpression has not been associated with uniform benefit of T-DXd is metastatic CRC where the objective response rate for HER2-positive disease was 27.5%–45.3%22,23 compared to 70.6% in breast.12 The majority of CRCs overexpress EGFR,24 and HER2 is overexpressed in 3%–5% of unselected metastatic CRCs.5 To determine the relevance of EGFR expression in HER2-expressing CRCs, we broadly profiled receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) protein expression in 18 HER2-expressing CRC patient samples (immunohistochemistry [IHC] 1+ to 3+, Table S1) using mass spectrometry.25 EGFR expression was detected in 18/18 samples (Figure 1A and Table S2). Out of the other two ERBB family members, HER3 was detected in 2/18 samples, and HER4 was not detected in any sample. Among the other RTKs measured, mesenchymal-epithelial transition factor was present in 13/18 samples and insulin receptor in 12/18 samples. No other RTKs were observed within the limit of detection (LOD). Given the ubiquity of EGFR expression in this small series of HER2-expressing tumors and its known interaction with surface HER2, we assessed whether EGFR expression can alter the rate of membrane trafficking of the HER2/T-DXd complex in CRC models. Using a pH-sensitive dye, we tracked the internalization of conjugated antibodies to the acidic endosomal compartments26 (Figure 1B). In a HER2-positive EGFR-amplified CRC cell line DiFi, knockdown of EGFR increased the rate of T-DXd internalization 1.5-fold compared to the non-targeting control (Figure 1C and Video S1). Moreover, overexpression of EGFR in EGFR-low CRC cell lines NCI-H508 or HCA46 suppressed T-DXd internalization rate 1.4-fold or 1.3-fold, respectively, compared to the empty vector (EV) control (Figure 1F and Video S2 and Figure S1A). In these experiments, EGFR expression level and internalization rate measured by labeled cetuximab were positively correlated (Figures 1C–1F and S1A). Importantly, total HER2 expression level or HER2 membrane localization was unaffected by modulation of EGFR expression (Figures 1D, 1E, 1G, 1H, S1B, and S1C), implying that the observed effects on HER2 internalization and T-DXd trafficking rates were a direct result of changes in EGFR expression.

The impact of EGFR expression on cetuximab and T-DXd internalization was then investigated in a broader panel of CRC cell lines demonstrating variable expression levels of EGFR and HER2 (Figure 1I) across a diverse genetic background (Figure S1D). Among the six cell lines, five lines had comparable surface HER2 expression (Figure 1J). Based on the total and membrane EGFR levels, we categorized RKO, HCA46, and NCI-H508 cells as EGFR-low, and HT-55, SW48, and DiFi cells as EGFR-high. The rate of cetuximab internalization was proportional to EGFR expression, but the rate of T-DXd internalization did not directly correlate with HER2 expression level (Figure 1K). Instead in cells with comparable HER2 expression, HCA46 and HT-55 or NCI-H508 and DiFi, the EGFR-low HCA46 and NCI-H508 cells had faster T-DXd trafficking than the EGFR-high HT-55 and DiFi cells. RKO cells with the lowest surface HER2 expression and SW48 cells, with low total HER2, had the lowest rates of T-DXd internalization. As with DiFi and NCI-H508 cells, modulation of EGFR expression in the remaining cell lines did not affect total or surface levels of HER2 (Figures S1E–S1L) confirming that changes in EGFR expression do not alter surface HER2 levels in individual models. Taken together, our data demonstrate that high EGFR expression can inhibit HER2/T-DXd complex internalization.

EGFR overexpression redistributes HER2 among its dimerization partners and lowers T-DXd antitumor efficacy

Given prior data on catalytic and non-catalytic roles for EGFR in cancer phenotypes, we investigated whether EGFR kinase activity was necessary for its impact on T-DXd trafficking.27 Interestingly, we found that overexpression of kinase-dead (KD, R817M)28,29 mutant EGFR in NCI-H508 also suppressed T-DXd internalization 2.3-fold compared to the control EV cells (Figure S2A) indicating that the effect on HER2/T-DXd trafficking is not necessarily dependent on EGFR catalytic function. Based on these results and the known interaction of EGFR with HER2, we hypothesized that membrane-localized EGFR might alter HER2 internalization by altering the dimerization partition of HER2. To investigate this, we utilized coimmunoprecipitation to evaluate the relative amounts of EGFR/HER2 heterodimers and observed increased EGFR/HER2 heterodimer abundance in EGFR-overexpressing cells compared to empty vector control (Figures S2B and S2C). As an orthogonal approach to quantifying these interactions, we performed proximity ligation assays (PLAs) and probed for EGFR/HER2 heterodimers and HER2/HER2 homodimers in the EGFR-overexpressing NCI-H508 cells. We found that EGFR overexpression caused a 3-fold increase in EGFR/HER2 heterodimers than the control cells (Figures 2A and 2A′). Interestingly, the number of HER2/HER2 homodimers decreased 2-fold upon EGFR overexpression compared to the control cells (Figures 2B and 2B′). The results reveal that EGFR expression can shift the HER2 dimerization state from one with more HER2/HER2 homodimers in EGFR-low cells to one with more EGFR/HER2 heterodimers in EGFR-high cells.

We next investigated whether this change in dimer partition and internalization rate had an effect on the antitumor activity of the HER2 ADC. To assess these effects in vivo, we exposed NCI-H508-derived CRC xenografts overexpressing EGFR or empty vector to T-DXd (Figure 2C). All empty vector tumors responded to T-DXd by decreasing tumor volume (4/5 tumors) or completely suppressing tumor growth (1/5 tumors) (Figure 2D). By contrast, 5/5 EGFR-overexpressing tumors displayed a marked increase in tumor volume at 42 days after first treatment, directly demonstrating the potential for EGFR overexpression to promote resistance to HER2 ADCs. Moreover, EGFR overexpression did not induce tumor growth in untreated tumors compared to the empty vector implying functions for EGFR apart from its role as an oncogene. Indeed, we observe no major changes in the downstream targets of canonical ERBB pathway signaling (e.g., p-EGFR, p-HER2, p-AKT, p-ERK) between the EGFR-overexpressing and control NCI-H508 cells, nor with T-DXd treatment, suggesting that the antitumor activity may not dominantly be due to the suppression of RTK signaling by T-DXd and instead highlights the role of increased delivery of payload in the EGFR-low EV cells (Figure S2D).

To further confirm the finding of EGFR/HER2 dimerization in this context, we examined EGFR/HER2 dimerization in vivo, utilizing EGFR-overexpressing cell line-derived xenograft (CDX) tumors. EGFR-overexpressing tumors displayed increased membrane localization of EGFR compared to control tumors but no change in membrane HER2 staining by IHC (Figure 2E). Similar to our in vitro models, EGFR-overexpressing tumors exhibited a 5.8-fold increase in EGFR/HER2 dimerization compared to the control tumors by PLA (Figures 2F and 2F′). Together, these data demonstrate that EGFR overexpression can suppress HER2/HER2 homodimerization, HER2/T-DXd internalization, and T-DXd efficacy, pointing to EGFR manipulation as a rational therapeutic combination with T-DXd.

Pharmacological inhibition of EGFR enhances T-DXd internalization and antitumor activity

For KRAS wild-type CRC, EGFR mAbs cetuximab and panitumumab are FDA-approved therapies that induce the endocytosis of EGFR as part of their mechanism of action.30,31,32 We speculated that enforced EGFR endocytosis with these antibodies could enhance HER2 ADC uptake and efficacy. In EGFR-overexpressing NCI-H508 cells, the addition of 50 μg/mL cetuximab or panitumumab enhanced the rate of T-DXd internalization 1.8-fold or 2.6-fold, respectively, compared to T-DXd alone (Figures 3A and 3B). To assess if the addition of EGFR mAbs might release HER2 from the EGFR/HER2 heterodimers to form HER2 homodimers and consequently increase T-DXd internalization, we quantified EGFR/HER2 heterodimerization in NCI-H508 control and EGFR-overexpressing cells after cetuximab treatment by PLA. EGFR overexpression increased EGFR/HER2 heterodimers 1.9-fold compared to control cells while cetuximab administration did not substantially alter EGFR/HER2 dimerization in both control and EGFR-overexpressing cells (Figure S3A). We next tested if EGFR antibody treatment could promote internalization of HER2 bound to T-DXd as part of the same complex with EGFR. We incubated NCI-H508 EGFR-overexpressing cells with fluorophore-conjugated T-DXd and cetuximab on ice and monitored their cellular localization over time. Within 30 s of the release of cells to 37°C, we observed changes in cellular localization of both T-DXd and cetuximab from being membrane bound to the cytoplasm. We observed co-localization of T-DXd and cetuximab during the internalization process (Figure 3C) suggesting the EGFR and HER2 internalization events are not completely independent from each other.

In the HER2-positive EGFR-amplified cell line DiFi, the T-DXd trafficking index was 3.6-fold higher with cetuximab co-administration than that with the vehicle condition (Figure 3D). This increase in T-DXd endocytosis translated to a remarkably stronger response of DiFi xenografts to the T-DXd and cetuximab combination therapy (96.6% growth inhibition from vehicle arm on day 25 after treatment start) compared to either monotherapy (44.2% and 60% growth inhibition of cetuximab and T-DXd arms, respectively) (Figure 3E). While tumors in the monotherapy arms maintained growth, albeit more slowly than the vehicle arm, the tumors in the combination arm displayed a sustained growth inhibition.

We next examined the therapeutic potential for this combination in two ERBB2-amplified EGFR-expressing CRC patient-derived xenografts (PDXs) (Figures 3F and 3G). CRC PDX-1 had an 18.4-fold amplification in ERBB2. The tumors in the T-DXd and cetuximab combination arm responded 73.59% and 43.65% better than cetuximab and T-DXd alone, respectively, 16 days after treatment (Figure 3F). Interestingly, we observed a region of reduced EGFR staining in one of the vehicle arm tumors for CRC PDX-1, akin to the heterogeneity prevalent in clinical tissue samples. Cells in this region displayed a 2.6-fold reduction in EGFR/HER2 dimerization compared to the surrounding EGFR-high cells (Figures S3B and S3B′) highlighting the potential relevance of EGFR/HER2 heterodimer abundance in treatment response. CRC PDX-2 had a 9-fold amplification in ERBB2 and exhibited a 98.25% greater response than cetuximab, and 94.97% than T-DXd alone, 36 days after the start of the treatment (Figure 3G). PDX-2 tumors regressed completely with combination therapy. To elucidate the potential mechanisms whereby tumors can escape these treatments, we assessed the expression of cancer biomarkers by mass spectrometry in three tumors from each treatment arm at the end of the study for both models. The tumors continued to express EGFR and HER2 after therapy (Figures S3C and S3D, and Table S2) retaining alternative EGFR- and HER2-targeted therapies as future treatment options upon resistance development. Both patients were previously treated with irinotecan, a topoisomerase I inhibitor similar to the payload of T-DXd. Sensitivity of the models to T-DXd, particularly in combination with cetuximab, suggests that an increase in payload delivery can enhance activity.

In addition to ERBB2-amplified and KRAS wild-type PDX models, we also tested if cetuximab could augment the antitumor effect of T-DXd in KRAS-mutant CRCs, a condition where cetuximab treatment is established as clinically ineffective.33 In the CRC PDX-3 with a 13.2-fold ERBB2 amplification and concurrent KRASG12D mutation, cetuximab monotherapy only slightly suppressed tumor growth (Figure 3H). On the other hand, in combination with T-DXd, cetuximab led to a 62.4% greater response compared to T-DXd alone after 21 days of treatment. Collectively, our data underscore the potential of enforcing EGFR/HER2 complex endocytosis by EGFR mAbs as a means for heightened T-DXd delivery and efficacy in EGFR-expressing CRCs.

Targeting EGFR enhances HER2 ADC internalization and potency in EGFR-overexpressing cancers of various histologies

To assess if EGFR expression impacts response to anti-HER2 ADCs clinically, we utilized patient samples from a multi-histology basket trial (NCT02675829) of trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1). All trial patients had an amplification in ERBB2, and HER2 IHC ranged from 0 to 3+ (Table S1). We measured EGFR using mass spectrometry in pretreatment biopsies and correlated with the best response to T-DM1 (Figure 4A). Despite the small sample size (n = 19) and diverse tumor types, we noted relatively higher EGFR expression in patients with worse treatment outcomes (mean EGFR expression was 407.7 amol/μg in the progressive disease group compared to 204.1 amol/μg for the other groups, p = 0.31, one-way ANOVA). These results raise the possibility that surrogate assays for EGFR/HER2 heterodimerization like EGFR and HER2 expression may predict response to anti-HER2 ADCs and warrant carefully designed clinical trials powered to evaluate such correlations.

EGFR overexpression and amplification are targetable in several cancer types harboring potential HER2 co-expression including head and neck cancers,34 glioblastomas,35 gastric cancers,36,37 and pancreatic cancers.38 In a pan-tumor trial of solid tumors, T-DXd showed activity in head and neck and esophagogastric (EGC) cancers.8,9 We examined if targeting EGFR with cetuximab could augment T-DXd uptake and efficacy in non-CRC, EGFR-expressing cancers. In a head and neck squamous cell carcinoma EGFR-amplified cell line HSC2, addition of cetuximab increased T-DXd internalization 1.6-fold compared to the control (Figure 4B). Similarly in a glioma cell line U251, cetuximab boosted T-DXd trafficking 1.6-fold compared to control (Figure 4C).

We also tested two esophagogastric EGFR- and ERBB2-amplified PDX models derived from the same patient.39 Performing internalization on enzymatically dissociated PDX cells demonstrated a 1.3-fold (Figure 4D) and a 1.2-fold (Figure S4A) upregulation of T-DXd internalization upon cetuximab addition in PDX-1 and PDX-2, respectively. Furthermore, cetuximab promoted a 1.2-fold faster T-DXd trafficking in an esophageal adenocarcinoma (ESCA) PDX model (Figure S4B). EGFR- and ERBB2-amplified EGC PDX-1 tumors had limited response to T-DXd monotherapy and showed reduced tumor growth with cetuximab monotherapy; however the cetuximab-treated tumors were able to regrow rapidly after treatment was stopped at day 32 (Figure 4E). The combination of T-DXd and cetuximab led to robust tumor shrinkage (tumors were 72.4% smaller than cetuximab monotherapy arm on day 32), and a significant delay and retardation of growth after treatment removal. Additionally, we tested a pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) ERBB2-amplified and KRAS-mutant PDX model. PAAD PDX-1 tumors with KRASG12D mutation responded greater than 85% better with the combination therapy than either monotherapy on day 36 (Figure 4F). Overall, our data illustrate co-targeting of EGFR and HER2 to be a potentially valuable therapeutic strategy for many EGFR and HER2 co-expressing cancers.

Discussion

The ERBB RTKs have proven to be among the most important therapeutic targets in all of oncology. From a cell biologic perspective, these receptors are commonly observed to interact and cooperate in signaling. In this study, we find that co-expression of the EGFR and HER2 RTKs has the potential to promote tumor resistance to powerful anti-HER2 ADCs such as T-DXd. Importantly, however, a readily available therapeutic strategy of combining FDA-approved EGFR mAbs with T-DXd can overcome this limitation and has the potential to broaden further the utility of anti-HER2 ADCs.

Resistance to ADCs is proposed to occur through distinct mechanisms,14 but the processes relevant to T-DXd resistance have not been elucidated in most cases. EGFR overexpression has been associated with resistance to trastuzumab in HER2-positive breast cancers.40 In other studies, EGFR overexpression was a poor prognostic factor for trastuzumab response in both primary and metastatic HER2-positive breast cancers.41,42 High EGFR mRNA levels correlated with worse outcomes of HER2-positive breast cancer patients treated with T-DM1 in the EMILIA trial,43 and patients with high EGFR protein were less likely to respond to T-DM1 from our analyses (Figure 4A). EGFR-overexpressing cells exhibited an impaired response to trastuzumab,44,45 and increased EGFR expression emerged as an acquired mechanism46 and driver47 of resistance to trastuzumab. Recently, EGFR amplification was found to be associated with a lower objective response rate to T-DXd in HER2-positive gastric cancers.48 Our results now functionally highlight that EGFR overexpression can directly suppress sensitivity to T-DXd.

We demonstrate that the reduced efficacy of T-DXd upon EGFR overexpression is due to diminished uptake of the ADC by the cells potentially because of insufficient HER2 homo-dimers or oligomers on the cell surface. HER2 dimerization status has been proposed as a predictor of trastuzumab response on account of a correlation between high levels of HER2 homodimers and a longer time to progression or survival on trastuzumab in HER2-positive breast cancers.49,50 Trastuzumab does not prevent ligand-dependent dimerization of HER251,52 allowing HER2 to heterodimerize with EGFR. In EGFR-overexpressing cells, we observe a heightened interaction of EGFR with HER2 accompanied by lower HER2 homodimerization. Increased EGFR/HER2 heterodimerization was also evident in EGFR-high regions of a heterogeneous PDX model exhibiting T-DXd resistance. These findings point to a need for biomarkers to measure EGFR and HER2 interactions to examine the predictive value of HER2 dimerization partition for T-DXd response. In addition to EGFR, HER3 is a frequent dimerization partner of HER2 with complex trafficking dynamics. Although we did not observe consistent, high-level steady-state expression of HER3 across our clinical cohort and models, there may be limits to the sensitivity of these assays for HER3 as well as the timing for analysis of a potentially dynamic receptor.

Fewer HER2 homodimers lead to inefficient receptor clustering upon trastuzumab or T-DXd binding and weakened endocytosis of the HER2/T-DXd complex. These observations prompted us to investigate strategies to boost T-DXd internalization to enhance T-DXd efficacy in EGFR-overexpressing cells. Because the decline in T-DXd trafficking was due to HER2 heterodimerization with EGFR, we hypothesized that promoting EGFR endocytosis might also improve HER2 internalization. The EGFR mAbs cetuximab and panitumumab induce EGFR endocytosis. When added to the EGFR-overexpressing models, these antibodies robustly enhanced T-DXd internalization. The mechanism of how cetuximab affects HER2 recycling has been unclear. One study reports that cetuximab prevents EGFR/HER2 heterodimerization,53 which might liberate HER2 to form clusters for efficient T-DXd trafficking. Our data demonstrate co-internalization of cetuximab with trastuzumab, supported by another study,54 suggesting cetuximab can upregulate endocytosis of the heterodimer complex and heterodimer-bound T-DXd. The resulting increases in T-DXd uptake after cetuximab addition produced superior treatment outcomes compared to T-DXd alone. The heightened antitumor activity of the combination treatment may also be supplemented by other antibody-related mechanisms including their effects on receptor downregulation, suppression of receptor signaling, as well as immune functions such as antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity.

Many combination strategies are based around the concept of payload-mediated resistance and focus on payload switching to enhance ADC efficacies. Here, we demonstrate a mechanistic combination approach based on a binding partner of the antigen target to overcome ADC resistance. Similar strategies designed to boost the endocytosis of the antigen/ADC complex with tyrosine kinase inhibitors are currently in clinical trials for ERBB2-mutant lung cancers.26 Overall, our results substantiate that EGFR overexpression is a source of de novo resistance to T-DXd. High levels of EGFR restrain T-DXd potency by dimerizing with HER2 and limiting HER2/T-DXd endocytosis. We anticipate that HER2 dimerization status along with HER2 expression will be a valuable biomarker for response to trastuzumab-based therapies including T-DXd. Our findings justify co-treatment with EGFR mAbs to improve T-DXd internalization and efficacy in EGFR-overexpressing HER2-positive cancers. Therapeutic trials are needed to establish the safety and efficacy of this innovative approach.

Limitations of the study

Following our findings, carefully designed clinical trials will be needed to establish the safety and clinical efficacy of the combination approach. Of note, we observe variation in the degree of benefit that adding EGFR antibodies provides beyond T-DXd monotherapy with robust tumor suppression in some models but more modest additivity in others. Therefore, it will be important to identify additional features beyond EGFR expression levels to help select patients who will maximally benefit from this combination. Moreover, looking beyond EGFR, we have not systematically tested all potential HER2 interaction partners for their ability to modulate HER2 trafficking dynamics but instead focused on EGFR due to its consistent expression in our clinical dataset and readily available targeting opportunities. Additionally, we were unable to quantify levels of HER2 dimerization in patient samples and going forward will need to develop effective biomarkers to assess the correlation between HER2 dimerization and response to T-DXd.

Experimental model and study participant details

Human tumor specimen

18 CRC patient specimens (see Table S1 for clinical characteristics), all collected prior to any HER2-targeted treatment, were evaluated for the expression of EGFR and HER2 along with 70 other proteins including other RTKs using a selected reaction monitoring-mass spectrometry (SRM-MS) assay as described below. HER2 expression was also measured by IHC as described below. All patients signed written informed consent for molecular profiling of their tumors (approved by Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (MSKCC) IRB Protocol #12–245) including the mass spectrometry assay. Pre-TDM1 specimens from a cohort of 19 patients (see Table S1 for clinical characteristics) who signed written informed consent for tumor analysis on MSKCC IRB Protocol #15–335 (Phase II, NCT02675829) were also studied for HER2 and EGFR protein expression by mass spectrometry. The best treatment response was assessed by investigators according to RECIST v1.1 or modified PERCIST. ERBB2 amplification and fold change was analyzed by MSK-IMPACT. HER2 expression was also evaluated by IHC as described below.

Animal studies

All mouse studies were conducted by the MSKCC antitumor core facility in compliance with the institutional guidelines under an IUCAC approved protocol (MSKCC IRB 12-10-016). Cell line-derived xenograft (CDX) models were generated by implanting 10 million cells subcutaneously in a single flank of 6–8 weeks old athymic female mice. For NCI-H508 CDX study, the mice were fed rodent doxycycline diet (Envigo TD.01306). PDXs were established by implanting freshly collected samples from patients subcutaneously in 6 weeks old NOD scid gamma female mice. The patient samples were collected at MSKCC under approved IRB biospecimen protocols (MSKCC IRB 12–245 and 06–107). For PDX studies, the tumors were expanded by serial subcutaneous transplantation.

Cell lines

The following cell lines were used: NCI-H508 (ATCC CCL-253), RKO (ATCC CRL-2577), SW48 (ATCC CCL-231), HCA46 (Sigma-Aldrich 07031601), HT-55 (Sigma-Aldrich 85061105), DiFi (gift from José Baselga), HSC2 (JCRB0622), U251 (gift from Ingo Mellinghoff) and HEK293T (gift from Ping Chi). NCI-H508, HCA46 and DiFi cells were cultured in DMEM/F12; RKO and SW48 cells were cultured in RPMI; HT-55 were cultured in Eagle’s MEM; HSC2, U251 and HEK293T cells were cultured in DMEM media. All media were supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Corning 35-010-CV) or 20% for HT-55, 2 mM L-glutamine, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin (Gemini Bio 400-109). Tetracycline-free fetal bovine serum (Takara Bio 631367) was used for experiments using the doxycycline-system. All cells were maintained in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 at 37°C.

Method details

Mass spectrometry analysis

Selected reaction monitoring-mass spectrometry (SRM-MS) analysis was performed on 72 biomarkers including ADC biomarkers, chemo sensitivity, and chemo resistance biomarkers, from tissue sections derived from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) blocks, following previously established protocols.25,58 For a comprehensive list of the 72 biomarkers and the mass spectrometry method, refer to Table S2. In summary, 2–3 10 μm thick tissue sections from FFPE blocks were sectioned onto DIRECTOR slides, deparaffinized, and stained with hematoxylin. Using an H&E image, a board-certified pathologist identified and delineated tumor areas, which were subsequently microdissected (Cellcut, MMI, Germany) and solubilized into tryptic fragments according to the manufacturer’s guidelines (Expression Pathology, Rockville, MD). The total protein concentration in each solubilized tumor lysate was determined using the microBCA protein assay. To the solution containing tryptic peptides, stable heavy isotope-labeled internal standard peptides for the 72 biomarkers were added before injection into the mass spectrometer (TSQ Quantiva, ThermoFisher Scientific, San Jose, CA). On-column injection resulted in 5 fmol of isotopically labeled internal standard peptide and 1 μg of total tumor protein being introduced into the mass spectrometer. Data analysis of the 72 biomarkers was carried out using Pinnacle software (Optys Tech, Boston, MA). This method measures the overall abundance of proteins but cannot determine the specific subcellular localization of each protein. In the initial screening process, all biomarkers, except for CDK4 were above limit of detection (LOD).

Antibody internalization assay

T-DXd (Daiichi Sankyo, DS-8201a), cetuximab (Selleck Chemicals, A2000) or panitumumab (Selleck Chemicals, A2018) were conjugated with pHrodo using the pHrodo iFL red microscale protein labeling kit (Thermo Scientific 36014) according to manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 1/10th volume of 1 M sodium bicarbonate solution was added to 1 mg/mL protein solution in PBS. 2 mM pHrodo was added to achieve a dye:protein molar ratio of 10. The reaction mixture was incubated at room temperature for 30 min and purified through a resin column. Cells were plated in 96 well plates in complete media. Cells dissociated from PDX samples were plated in RPMI. pHrodo-conjugated agents were added alone or in combination with cetuximab at 2X dilution in serum-free media to 5 μg/mL working concentrations. Phase contrast and red channel images were obtained on Incucyte S3 at an interval of 1 h at 37°C. Trafficking index (A.U. is arbitrary unit) was calculated by normalizing the red channel integrated intensity per well by the cell area per well. Relative pHrodo-conjugated antibody internalization was calculated by normalizing the trafficking index at each time point with the peak index value for the corresponding experimental control. Fold-change difference was calculated by dividing the total areas under the curves.

siRNA knockdown

The cells were transfected with siRNA using the Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Invitrogen 13778075) reagent as follows. 55 pmol siControl (Horizon Discovery D-001810-10-05) or siEGFR (Horizon Discovery L-003114-00-0005) was added to Opti-MEM medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 31985070) and 3 times the volume of Lipofectamine RNAiMAX reagent was added to Opti-MEM medium in a separate tube. The diluted siRNA and Lipofectamine were combined and incubated at room temperature for 5 min. The reaction mixture was added to the cells in a dropwise manner. The cells were used for internalization assay, immunoblotting, or flow cytometry analysis after 72 h of transfection.

Cloning and lentiviral transduction

The kinase dead EGFR mutant was generated using the Q5 site-directed mutagenesis kit (New England Biolabs E0554S) and primers forward: CCTGGCAGCCATGAACGTACTGG and reverse: TCGCGGTGCACCAAG with the wild-type (WT) EGFR containing pDONR-EGFR plasmid (gift from William Hahn and David Root, Addgene plasmid 2393556). The WT and KD EGFR sequences were cloned into pInducer20 (gift from Stephen Elledge, Addgene plasmid 4401257) using the Gateway LR Clonase II enzyme mix (Invitrogen 11791020). Lentiviral particles were prepared by transfecting 293T cells with pInducer20-EGFR, pInducer20-EGFRKD or the pInducer20 backbone (to be used as empty vector) along with the lentiviral envelope and packaging plasmids using X-tremeGENE HP DNA transfection reagent (Sigma-Aldrich 6366546001). The media was refreshed after 24 h. After 48 h, the supernatant was collected and filtered through 0.45 μm filters. NCI-H508 and HCA46 cells were transduced with the lentiviral particles and stably expressing cells were generated after selection using G418 (InvivoGen ant-gn-1) for two weeks. Cells were treated with 1 μg/mL doxycycline (Sigma-Aldrich, D9891) for 48 h to induce overexpression.

Immunoblotting

Cells were lysed on ice using RIPA lysis buffer (Thermo Scientific 89901) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitor (Thermo Scientific 78444). Lysates were cleared using centrifugation and total protein concentration was measured with BCA protein assay (Thermo Scientific 23225). 30-45 μg total protein was separated on 4–12% Bis-Tris protein gels (Invitrogen NuPAGE). Membranes were probed using the following antibodies at 1:1000 dilution – EGFR (Cell Signaling Technology D38B1), HER2 (Cell Signaling Technology 29D8), HER3 (Cell Signaling Technology D22C5), HER4 (Cell Signaling Technology 111B2), Vinculin (Cell Signaling Technology E1E9V), p-HER2 Y1221/1222 (Cell Signaling Technology 6B12), p-EGFR Y1068 (Cell Signaling Technology D7A5), p-ERK T202/Y204 (Cell Signaling Technology D13.14.4E), ERK (Cell Signaling Technology 9102), p-AKT S473 (Cell Signaling Technology D9E), AKT (Cell Signaling Technology 11E7), p-S6 S235/236 (Cell Signaling Technology 2211), p-S6 S240/244 (Cell Signaling Technology 2215), S6 (Cell Signaling Technology 5G10), and p-4EBP S65 (Cell Signaling Technology D9G1Q).

Flow cytometry analysis

1 x 106 cells were collected in cell staining buffer (BioLegend 420201) and stained with 5 μL PE anti-human CD340 (HER2) (BioLegend 324406) or PE anti-EGFR (BioLegend 352904) antibody for 15 min in the dark on ice. Cells were washed twice and resuspended in cell staining buffer containing DAPI (Invitrogen D3571). Cells were filtered through a 35 μm mesh and analyzed using LSR Fortessa.

Proximity ligation assay

1 x 105 cells per well were plated on a chamber slide (Corning 354118) in media containing 5% serum with 1 μg/mL doxycycline for 2 days. For cetuximab treatment, 50 μg/mL drug was added for 24 h. The cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min at room temperature, washed with PBS twice, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 10 min at room temperature, and washed again. FFPE samples were baked and dewaxed at 68°C for 24 min followed by pretreatment with epitope retrieval CC1 solution (Ventana 950-500) for 48 min at 95°C. The cells were then stained using the Duolink in situ red starter kit (Sigma-Aldrich DUO92101-1KT) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, the samples were blocked in Blocking Solution for 1 h at 37°C, then incubated in primary antibodies diluted in Antibody Diluent overnight at 4°C. The following antibodies were used at 1:100 dilution – mouse anti-HER2 (Invitrogen CB11), rabbit anti-HER2 (Cell Signaling Technology 29D8) and rabbit anti-EGFR (Cell Signaling Technology D38B1). After washing, the samples were incubated with PLUS and MINUS PLA probes for 1 h at 37°C and washed again. After the ligation and amplification steps, the samples were covered with mounting medium containing DAPI and sealed with clear nail polish. The slides were imaged on a Lecia TCS SP5 microscope or scanned using a Pannoramic Scanner (3DHistech, Budapest, Hungary) using a 20x/0.8NA objective. Scans were visualized using Slideviewer (3DHistech, version 2.7).

Immunoprecipitation

Cells were cultured in 15 cm plates with 1 μg/mL doxycycline. At 80% confluence, the cells were washed once with warm PBS and starved overnight in serum-free media. The next day, cells were cultured in media containing 5% serum for 5 min and harvested on ice. Lysates were prepared in Pierce IP lysis buffer (Thermo Scientific 87787). Dynabeads Protein A for IP (Invitrogen 10008D) were prepared according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, beads were incubated with anti-EGFR (Cell Signaling Technology D38B1) or normal rabbit IgG (Cell Signaling Technology 2729) at 4°C for 4 h with rotation. After washing, 1000–1500 μg protein lysates were added, and the beads were incubated at 4°C overnight with rotation. The beads were washed three times and immunoprecipitates were extracted in 1X sample reducing buffer at room temperature for 10 min.

Immunohistochemistry staining

HER2 IHC of patient samples was performed at the Pathology Core of MSKCC using the Leica/Bond platform. Heat-induced epitope retrieval was performed using the ER2 solution for 30 min. Rabbit anti-HER2 (Ventana clone 4B5, RTU) was used at 1:10 dilution for 30 mintues. Standard DAB kit (Leica) was applied during the visualization process. The IHC detection of EGFR and HER2 for other samples was performed at the Molecular Cytology Core Facility of MSKCC using Discovery Ultra processor (Ventana Medical Systems). Paraffin-embedded tissues were sectioned at 5 μm and baked at 58°C for 1 h. Slides were loaded in Ultra autostainer and IHC staining was performed as follows. Samples were baked and dewaxed followed by pretreatment with epitope retrieval CC1 solution (Ventana 950-500) for 48 min at 100°C. The primary antibodies were added – rabbit anti-EGFR (4.4 μg/mL, Cell Signaling Technology D38B1) or rabbit anti-HER2 (5 μg/mL, Enzo alx-810-227) and the samples were incubated for 4 h at room temperature. Non-immune rabbit IgG (Invitrogen 10500C) was used as a negative control. Samples were then incubated with OmniMap anti-rabbit HRP (Roche Diagnostics 760–4311) secondary antibody for 20 min. Discovery ChromoMap DAB detection kit (Roche Diagnostics) was used according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After staining, slides were washed in water, dehydrated using ethanol gradient (70%, 90%, 100%), washed three times in HistoClear II (National Diagnostics HS-202), and mounted in Permount (Fisher Scientific SP15).

In vivo efficacy studies

When tumor volumes reached ∼200 mm3, the mice were randomized to treatment arms of vehicle (saline), T-DXd (Daiichi Sankyo, DS-8201a) or cetuximab (Erbitux in solution 2 mg/mL, NDC 66733-948-23). Tumors were measured using calipers twice weekly and volumes were calculated using the formula: (length∗width2∗pi)/6. The animals were sacrificed at the end of the study, and tumors were collected for histological or biochemical analyses.

Co-localization of T-DXd and cetuximab

T-DXd and cetuximab were conjugated with Alexa Fluor 488 (Thermo Fisher Scientific A20181) and Alexa Fluor 647 (Thermo Fisher Scientific A20186) antibody labeling kits, respectively. Briefly, 1/10th volume of 1 M sodium bicarbonate solution was added to 1 mg/mL protein solution in PBS. 100 μL of the antibody solution was incubated with the Alexa Fluor dye for 1 h at room temperature and purified through a resin column. 1 x 105 cells per well were plated on a chamber coverglass (Thermo Fisher Scientific 155409) in media containing 5% serum with 1 μg/mL doxycycline for 2 days. The conjugated antibodies were added to the cells in serum-free media at 1:50 dilution and incubated on ice for 20 min in the dark. The media was replaced with fresh media containing 10% serum and live cells were imaged at 37°C on a Stellaris 8 confocal microscope (Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany) using a 63x/1.4NA oil immersion objective. Z-stacks were acquired over time and data were deconvolved using the Lightning deconvolution module of the Leica Software. Data were visualized using Imaris (Oxford Instruments, Abingdon, UK).

Dissociation of PDX samples

Fresh PDX samples in RPMI were cut into small pieces and dissociated on an Octo Dissociator according to the gentleMACS protocol (Miltenyi Biotec 130-095-929). Red blood cells and mouse cells were eliminated using the ACK lysing buffer (Gibco A1049201) and Mouse cell depletion kit (Miltenyi Biotec 130-104-694), respectively. The tumor cells were resuspended in RPMI and used for internalization assays.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 3.1.2) or GraphPad Prism v9.4.1 (GraphPad Software Inc). ANOVA test was used to determine significance of differences between groups in internalization assays, in vivo efficacy studies, and the association of EGFR expression with response to T-DM1. Significance of quantification of immunoblots was calculated using two-sided one-sample t test. Two-tailed unpaired t test was used to compare the number of PLA foci between groups in the PLA assays. Data are represented as mean ± SEM or SD and the exact p values are indicated in the corresponding figures.

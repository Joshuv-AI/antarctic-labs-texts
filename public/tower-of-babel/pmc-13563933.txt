Rational Design of Au@co-MOF Synergistic Platform for Ultrasensitive Monitoring of Dopamine in Human Biological Fluids
Authors: Feng Zhou, Hong Ngee Lim, Ibrahim Izwaharyanie, Huimin Tao, Yue Chen, Rongrong Xu, Xiongtao Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study presents the rational design and application of engineered metal–organic framework (MOF) hybrids, tailored to exhibit accelerated electron transfer kinetics and enhanced molecular recognition capabilities for the high-sensitivity detection of dopamine (DA). Specifically, an enzyme-free electrochemical sensing platform was constructed using Au@Co-MOF nanocomposites, which were synthesized via a controllable in situ growth approach. Au nanoparticles (AuNPs) were uniformly anchored onto the [Co3(TMA)3(H2O)5]n (where TMA = 1,3,5-trimesic acid) framework containing coordinatively unsaturated Co­(II) active sites, forming the Au@Co-MOF hybrid material. Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) characterization confirmed that the Au@Co-MOF hybrids possessed a well-defined spherical morphology with an average particle size of 7.5 μm, in clear contrast to the discrete AuNPs used for modification. The electrochemical sensing performance of the Au@Co-MOF-modified platform was systematically evaluated via differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) under optimized experimental conditions. Results demonstrated that the sensor exhibited a wide linear response range for DA, with a low limit of detection (LOD, S/N = 3) of 0.02 μM and a high sensitivity of 2.0 μA·μM–1·cm–2. Moreover, the proposed sensor exhibited superior selectivity and excellent stability. Its practical applicability was further confirmed through the successful determination of DA in biological matrices (urine and serum), with high recovery rates of 95.5%–104.8% (serum) and 102.8%–104.5% (urine). These results underscore the great potential of the Au@Co-MOF-modified screen-printed electrode (SPE) enzyme-free platform as a robust, sensitive, and selective electrochemical sensor for neurochemical monitoring.

Introduction

Dopamine (3,4-dihydroxyphenylethylamine, DA) is integral to neuroimmune regulation and is closely associated with several pathological conditions, including cardiovascular disorders, Parkinson’s disease, schizophrenia, and metabolic syndrome. , Accurate and sensitive determination of DA in complex biological matrices is critical for both clinical diagnostics and neuroscience research. ,

Traditional analytical techniques, including HPLC, fluorescence, chemiluminescence, and electrophoresis, exhibit high sensitivity but suffer from inherent limitations such as time-consuming operation, high cost, and reliance on sophisticated instrumentation. − In contrast, electrochemical methods have gained prominence as practical alternatives, leveraging their cost-effectiveness, rapid response, and operational simplicity. , Due to its facile electrochemical oxidation and robust redox response at conventional electrodes, DA is most effectively measured via electrochemical techniques, which offer high sensitivity, rapid analysis, and straightforward instrumentation. , However, the selectivity and resolution of bare electrodes are often compromised by overlapping oxidation peaks of DA and interfering species. To address this challenge, extensive research has focused on electrode modification strategies, encompassing metal nanoparticles, conducting polymers, carbon nanomaterials, and, more recently, metal–organic frameworks (MOFs). ,,

MOFs, constructed from metal ions/clusters and organic linkers, possess significant advantages as electrode modifiers, including their exceptionally high surface area, well-defined porosity, and structurally tunable nature, which collectively enhance the accessibility of active sites and enable tailored interactions with target analytes. ,, In addition, relevant published works have indicated that MOFs feature satisfactory water stability. However, their inherent limitations, such as poor conductivity, weak adhesion to electrode surfaces, and instability in aqueous environments, have constrained their direct application in electrochemical systems. , To address these drawbacks, recent strategies have focused on integrating MOFs with conductive nanomaterials, an approach that has proven effective in boosting electrochemical performance by synergistically combining the unique porous properties of MOFs with the electrical conductivity of nanomaterials. ,, For example, other research groups have developed an electrochemical sensor based on Au nanoparticle (AuNP)-decorated Ni-MOF, which demonstrated efficient real-time monitoring of dopamine (DA) released from living cells, highlighting the improved electron transfer kinetics and sensitivity achieved through such hybrid structures. In our previous work, we further validated this strategy by fabricating Au@Cu-MOF/SPCE and Au@Ni-MOF/SPCE electrochemical sensors, which successfully detected DA and uric acid in human body fluids with enhanced stability and selectivity compared to pristine MOFs. , These collective findings underscore that the integration of MOFs with conductive nanomaterials like AuNPs can effectively mitigate the intrinsic shortcomings of MOFs, thereby laying a foundation for their practical application in electrochemical sensing.

Nevertheless, these MOF-nanomaterial hybrid systems still have room for optimization, with key focuses on: optimizing the distribution uniformity of AuNPs in the MOF matrix to reduce performance inhomogeneities from nanoparticle aggregation; enhancing the long-term stability of the composite structure in complex biological media to prevent structural degradation and active component leaching; and strengthening the interfacial interaction between MOFs and conductive componentsvia regulating bonding or adding modification layersto further boost charge transfer efficiency and improve the system’s overall functionality. To address these limitations, this study proposes an enzyme-free sensing strategy in which AuNPs are loaded onto the surface of a coordinatively unsaturated cobalt-based MOF ([Co3(TMA)3(H2O)5]n, TMA = 1,3,5-trimesic acid) via an in situ growth method, with the resulting sensor employed for the sensitive and selective detection of DA using differential pulse voltammetry (DPV). The coordinatively unsaturated cobalt centers not only act as anchoring sites for AuNPs to promote their uniform distribution but also, together with the MOF’s porous structure and intrinsic metal catalytic activity, create a synergistic effect with AuNPs, thus constructing a highly stable electrochemical sensing system without the need for enzyme participation. This synergy, stemming from the highly porous nature of the MOF and the electrocatalytic properties of AuNPs, endows the sensor with impressive performance: it exhibits a linear response over the range of 0.1 to 100 μM, a low detection limit of 0.02 μM (S/N = 3), and excellent selectivity, reproducibility, and stability. Notably, the sensor also demonstrated reliable performance in the analysis of real-world samples, including human serum and urine, highlighting its significant potential for practical biomedical applications.

Experimental Section

Chemicals and Materials

Chloroauric acid (HAuCl4·3H2O, 99.99%), trisodium citrate (Na3C6H5O7, 99.9%), and dopamine (DA, ≥99.0%) were purchased from Aldrich (USA). Cobalt­(II) nitrate hexahydrate (Co­(NO3)2·6H2O, 98.0%), and 1,3,5-trimesic acid (TMA, ≥ 98.0%) sodium dihydrogen phosphate monohydrate (NaH2PO4·H2O, 99%), disodium hydrogen phosphate dihydrate (Na2HPO4·2H2O, 99.5%), potassium chloride (KCl, 99.5%), potassium hexacyanoferrate­(III) (K3[Fe­(CN)6], ≥99.0%), potassium ferrocyanide trihydrate (K4[Fe­(CN)6]·3H2O, ≥99.0%), methanol (AR, ≥99.5%), ethanol (AR, ≥99.7%), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, AR, ≥99.5%), and Nafion (≥95.0%) were obtained from Aldrich (China). Phosphoric acid (H3PO4, ≥85.0%) and sodium hydroxide (NaOH, ≥96.0%) were both purchased from Merck (USA). Screen-printed carbon electrodes (SPEs) were obtained from DS Dropsens (Spain). Human urine and serum samples were obtained from Aldrich (China). All reagents were used as received without further purification. Milli-Q deionized water (resistivity: 18.2 MΩ·cm) was employed throughout the experiments, which were conducted at room temperature.

Preparation of AuNPs

The AuNPs were synthesized using an in situ water bath method as an alternative to conventional reflux techniques. A schematic illustration of this synthesis process is provided in Figure i. Specifically, the nanoparticles were prepared through the citrate reduction of HAuCl4: 50 mL of 1.0 mmol/L HAuCl4 solution was placed in a round-bottom flask and heated to boiling in a water bath. Under continuous magnetic stirring (500 rpm), 2.0 mL of 1.0% (w/v) sodium citrate solution was introduced dropwise into the boiling solution. The color of the mixture transitioned progressively from pale yellow to dark brown, ultimately forming a stable wine-red colloidal suspension within minutes. After heating, the resulting mixture was aged at room temperature for 1 h, followed by cooling and centrifugal purification.

Schematic illustrations of the fabrication process and sensing application. (i) Citrate-mediated synthesis of AuNPs. (ii) One-pot solvothermal fabrication of Au@Co-TMA nanocomposites. (iii) Construction of the Au@Co-TMA/SPE sensor and its electrochemical detection mechanism for dopamine (DA).

Preparation of Au@Co-MOF Composite

As illustrated in Figure ii, the Au@Co-MOF composite was synthesized via a one-pot solvothermal route. Typically, 4 mmol Co­(NO3)2·6H2O and 1 mmol TMA were dissolved in 50 mL anhydrous N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) under magnetic stirring to afford a homogeneous solution. After 10 min, the mixture became a clear and homogeneous solution without any precipitation or stratification. 50 mL of 1 mM aqueous AuNPs colloid was introduced, and the mixture was transferred into a 150 mL Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave. The vessel was sealed and heated at 120 °C for 6 h. Upon natural cooling to ambient temperature, the bright-purple suspension was centrifuged (9,900 rpm, 10 min) and the resulting solid was repeatedly washed with methanol (3 × 20 mL) to remove residual DMF and unbound species. The final product was dried under vacuum at 80 °C for 24 h and stored in a desiccator before further use. Pure Co-MOF was prepared under identical conditions but in the absence of AuNPs and served as the control material.

Preparation of Modified Electrode Au@Co-MOF/SPE

The fabrication procedure of the Au@Co-MOF/SPE composite electrode is schematically illustrated in Figure iii. Briefly, 1.5 mg of Au@Co-MOF composite was dispersed in 1.5 mL of ethanol and sonicated for 60 min to obtain a homogeneous suspension. For electrode modification, 50 μL of this suspension was further ultrasonicated for an additional 60 min to ensure uniform dispersion. Before modification, screen-printed carbon electrodes (SPEs) were thoroughly rinsed with deionized water and dried under a gentle stream of nitrogen. Subsequently, 5 μL of the Au@Co-MOF suspension was drop-cast onto the cleaned SPE surface and allowed to air-dry at room temperature. This drop-casting step was repeated 5 times to achieve adequate, uniform surface coverage. Finally, 2.0 μL of a 5.0 wt % Nafion solution was applied onto the dried Au@Co-MOF layer and dried under ambient conditions to form the Au@Co-MOF/SPE composite electrode. For comparative analysis, AuNPs/SPE and Co-MOF/SPE electrodes were fabricated following an identical procedure. Bare SPEs were used as control electrodes in all electrochemical characterizations.

Characterization

AuNP size and morphology were examined by high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM, Hitachi HT-7700, Tokyo, Japan). Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, FEI Quanta 400 F) coupled with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) was employed to probe the microstructure and elemental composition of the composites. Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) patterns were collected on a Philips X’Pert diffractometer (Cu Kα radiation, λ = 1.5406 Å) over the 2θ range 5–80°. Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra were recorded on a PerkinElmer Spectrum 100 spectrometer in the 4000–400 cm–1 region. N2 adsorption–desorption isotherms were acquired at 77 K using a Micromeritics TriStar II Plus analyzer; specific surface areas were calculated by the Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) method.

Electrochemical Measurements

Electrochemical measurements were conducted in a 0.1 M phosphate buffer solution (PBS, pH 6.0) supplemented with dopamine (DA) at various concentrations. Cyclic voltammetry (CV), chronoamperometry (CA), differential-pulse voltammetry (DPV), and amperometric i-t curve measurements were performed using a potentiostat (DropSens, mSTAT8000P, USA). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed using a separate potentiostat (VersaSTAT 3–300, Princeton Applied Research, USA). PBS solutions with different pH values were prepared by mixing Na2HPO4 and NaH2PO4, and their pH values were calibrated using a Metrohm 827 pH meter (Metrohm AG, Switzerland). CV and EIS measurements were carried out in a 1 mM [Fe­(CN)6]3–/4– electrolyte solution containing 0.1 M KCl.

Results and Discussion

Characterization of the Au@Co-MOF Composite

The nanostructure, particle size, and morphology of AuNPs were characterized by high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM), confirming their icosahedral crystal structure, as shown in Figure A. Statistical analysis of 100 particles revealed uniform AuNPs with an average diameter of 25.96 ± 5.31 nm, all of which were below 100 nm. Furthermore, the FESEM image (Figure S1A as shown in support information) revealed the partial formation of the spherical structure of Co-MOF, resulting in a smooth spherical surface with negligible porosity. However, the spherical surface of the pure Co-MOF appeared smooth, indicating poor porosity. FESEM images (Figure B and C) showed that Au@Co-MOF composites formed nearly spherical particles with an average size of ∼7.5 μm. A higher-magnification FESEM image of the Au@Co-MOF composite is provided in Figure S1B, which clearly reveals the AuNPs anchored onto the Co-MOF surface. In contrast to the Co-MOF with a smooth spherical surface, Au@Co-MOF exhibited a hierarchical porous structure. Upon the introduction of AuNPs into the precursor solution, the composite surface exhibited a rough, porous texture, with small AuNPs aggregating at the edges and surfacesconsistent with previous literature reports. − In the one-step hydrothermal/solvothermal synthesis of Au@Co-MOF, Co2+ ions are coordinated with organic ligands to construct the Co-MOF, with AuNPs simultaneously encapsulated within or anchored onto the MOF structure. This process yielded a core–shell or supported composite architecture. The one-step synthesis avoids the need for complex instrumentation and enables precise control over the composite’s morphology and size by tuning the reaction temperature, reaction time, and precursor ratios. The resulting modified electrode integrates the high electrical conductivity of Au with the selective adsorption capacity of MOFs, thereby enhancing both the sensitivity and anti-interference capability of the sensor. This method is highly suitable for electrochemical sensing applications.

As shown in the FESEM image of Au@Co-MOF in Figure D, the elemental mapping results (Figure E) demonstrate a uniform distribution of C, O, Co, and Au, confirming the coexistence of AuNPs within the Co-MOF structure. Additionally, Figure S1C presents the EDS spectrum, which further confirms the elemental composition of the Au@Co-MOF composite, revealing the presence of C (9.21%), O (27.88%), Co (48.58%), and Au (2.03%). These results collectively verify the successful incorporation of a small amount of AuNPs onto the surface of the Co-MOF framework.

To identify the crystalline phase of the created Co-MOF and Au@Co-MOF composites, X-ray powder diffraction (XRD) studies were made. The crystallographic details for the structure determination are shown in Figure A. The blue line displays that the main diffraction peaks for both materials align well with the simulated pattern of Co-MOF structure data (CCDC 698181). The orange line shows that both XRD spectra exhibit identical diffraction patterns and peak positions, except for two additional small peaks in the Au@Co-MOF spectrum at 2θ = 38.1° (111) and 44.60° (200), corresponding to the characteristic reflections of AuNPs. This confirms the successful incorporation of AuNPs into the composite. Moreover, the absence of any impurity-related peaks indicates the high phase purity of the synthesized samples. The diffraction peaks of Au@Co-MOF display similar intensities to those of the parent Co-MOF, suggesting that the introduction of spherical AuNPs, with an average diameter of 30 nm, affected the crystallization process slightly, resulting in minor changes to the lattice structure and morphology. Additionally, the purple line presents the XRD pattern of Au@Co-MOF after immersion in a DA solution for 7 days. The spectrum remains nearly unchanged compared to the original, indicating that the Au@Co-MOF composite possesses good chemical stability in aqueous environments.

(A) XRD of simulated Co-MOF, Co-MOF, Au@Co-MOF, and Au@Co-MOF/DA. (B) FT-IR spectra of TMA, Co-MOF, and Au@Co-MOF. (C) N2 adsorption–desorption isotherms of Co-MOF and Au@Co-MOF.

The characterization of the functional groups in TMA, Co-MOF, and Au@Co-MOF was carried out using the FT-IR spectrum. As depicted in Figure B, the disappearance of the characteristic bands of carboxyl groups (red line) at 3090 cm–1, 1691 cm–1, and 534 cm–1 in Co-MOF and Au@Co-MOF confirmed the deprotonation of TMA during the coordination reaction with Co2+ to form the Co-based MOF. , Additionally, new strong bands were observed in the region from 1647 cm–1 to 1567 cm–1, corresponding to the asymmetric and symmetric stretching vibrations of the −COOH group. Furthermore, the appearance of a characteristic band at 760 (blue line, Co-MOF) and 761 (orange line, Au@Co-MOF) was attributed to the ring-out-of-plane vibration of the 1,3,5-substituted benzene. The broadband observed around 3300 cm–1 (blue line and orange line) indicated the presence of νO–H stretching vibrations, suggesting the existence of H2O molecules within the Co-MOF and Au@Co-MOF structure. Hence, the FT-IR spectrum of Co-based MOF confirmed the successful coordination of Co2+ and TMA.

The porosity of the samples was analyzed using nitrogen-adsorption measurements, as shown in Figure C. The Co-based MOF (blue line) displayed a type (II) isotherm, indicating that the particles are macropore adsorbents. In contrast, the Au@Co-MOF (orange line) exhibited type (IV) isotherms, which are characteristic of mesoporous adsorbents. The isotherm style changed from type (II) to type (IV) with the addition of AuNPs, as observed from Figure C, suggesting that the AuNPs reduced the porosity and pore size of the Au@Co-MOF composites. The BET surface areas of the Co-based MOF and Au@Co-MOF were 18.84 m2/g and 11.08 m2/g, respectively, as shown in Table S1. The BET surface area of Au@Co-MOF is markedly reduced compared with that of pristine Co-based MOF following AuNP loading. This is because AuNPs are not only anchored on the external surface of Co-MOF microspheres but also partially infiltrate into the intrinsic porous channels of the Co-MOF framework, thereby blocking partial micropores and mesopores and occupying the internal void spaces of the Co-MOF matrix. Furthermore, the (van der Waals Bipolar Junction Transistor) VBJT and pore size of the Au@Co-MOF composite were smaller than those of the pure Co-MOF, which was consistent with the variation observed in the nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherm.

Electrochemical Behavior of Au@Co-MOF/SPE

Electrochemical Measurements

Figure S2A presents the voltammetric responses of three different electrodes: bare SPE, Co-MOF/SPE, and Au@Co-MOF/SPE in a 1.0 mM solution of the [Fe­(CN)6]3–/4–redox couple. As shown, the Au@Co-MOF/SPE electrode exhibited well-defined redox peaks with a reduced peak-to-peak potential separation (ΔE) of 506 mV, along with a significantly increased peak current compared to Co-MOF/SPE (498 mV) and bare SPE (421 mV). These results indicate that the combined presence of AuNPs and the MOF structure enhances electron transfer at the electrode surface. The improved electron transfer kinetics of the Au@Co-MOF/SPE electrode are attributed to the incorporation of AuNPs, which not only increase the electrical conductivity of the composite but also enable stronger and more sensitive current signals, thereby improving the electrochemical detection performance for target analytes. Although the Co-MOF/SPE electrode exhibited a slight improvement in redox peak current compared to the bare SPE, its inherently low conductivity limited the overall electron-transfer efficiency. The porous structure of the Co-MOF provides a high surface area and abundant active sites, which benefit analyte adsorption and ion diffusion. However, the poor intrinsic electrical conductivity of the MOF framework, due to weak electronic coupling between the metal nodes and organic linkers, hinders efficient electron transport across the electrode surface. This underscores the need to incorporate conductive materials, such as AuNPs, to enhance the electrochemical performance of MOF-based sensors. Therefore, the Au@Co-MOF/SPE exhibited the best electrocatalytic performance compared to other electrodes.

The charge-transfer resistance (Rct) values on different electrodes were calculated from the diameter of the semicircle. Figure S2B and C exhibit the electrochemical impedance spectra of the Au@Co-MOF modified electrode, Co-MOF modified electrode, AuNPs modified electrode, and bare electrode, respectively. Obviously, the Au@Co-MOF modified electrode has the smallest semicircle radius in the high-frequency region, revealing that the Au@Co-MOF modified electrode possesses the highest charge transfer rate and lowest electric resistance, which is conducive to promoting the interface electrochemical reaction. Notably, as shown in Table S2, the Rct value of Au@Co-MOF modified electrode was calculated as 42 Ω, which is lower than those of Co-MOF modified electrode (515 Ω), AuNPs modified electrode (994 Ω), and bare electrode (4770 Ω), powerfully demonstrating that the good conductive coat of Co-based MOF, and the addition of conductive AuNPs could improve the electron transfer rate during the reaction can greatly reduce its electrochemical impedance and further boost its corresponding electroanalytic properties. Moreover, the Nyquist plot of Au@Co-MOF modified electrode shows a larger slope than those of Co-MOF modified electrode, AuNPs modified electrode, and bare electrode in the low-frequency region, which means faster mass diffusion transfer of Au@Co-MOF modified electrode. These results show that the modified Au@Co-MOF electrode exhibits superior electrocatalytic performance in individual voltammetric measurements of target analytes, enabling faster charge-transfer kinetics.

Electrochemical Measurements for DA

Electrochemical Responses for DA on Various Electrodes

The electrochemical responses of the target biomolecules at various electrodes were characterized by the differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) method. Specific concentration (50 μM) of DA was measured separately at this electrochemical sensor under the conditions of 0.1 M PBS (pH 6.0) at a scan rate of 50 mV·s–1, as shown in Figure C. It can be observed that all three electrodes displayed anodic response signals for DA. Moreover, the bare SPE and Co-MOF/SPE appeared to have smaller peak currents, suggesting weak electrocatalytic performance for the detection of DA. However, MOF-based electrodes are unsuitable for the determination of DA, primarily attributed to the inherent instability of MOFs and their poor electrical conductivity in aqueous solutions. − Notably, the Au@Co-MOF/SPE electrode exhibited significantly enhanced current responses and superior electrochemical performance compared to the pure MOF/SPE, which can be attributed to the incorporation of AuNPs. These nanoparticles, with their high catalytic activity, effectively facilitate electron transfer kinetics at the electrode–electrolyte interface. , The as-prepared Au@Co-MOF/SPE composite electrode exposes abundant active metal sites and enables efficient electron transfer at the electrode surface, attributed to its superior electrical conductivity. This results in significantly enhanced oxidation peak currents, as evidenced by the blue curve in Figure C. Figure D further demonstrates that no redox peaks were observed for the Au@Co-MOF/SPE in blank PBS, confirming that the distinct peaks originated from the addition of DA rather than the electrode materials themselves. These findings collectively validate the excellent electrocatalytic activity of the Au@Co-MOF composite, which effectively promotes the electrochemical oxidation of DA.

Optimization of pH Conditions

The pH value is a crucial parameter that governs the performance of electrochemical reactions. As shown in Figure A, the anodic peak potentials for DA oxidation exhibit a gradual negative shift with increasing pH from 4.0 to 7.0, concomitant with a decrease in hydrogen ion concentration. This behavior indicates the involvement of proton transfer in the electrocatalytic reaction, consistent with previous reports on N-doped Cu-MOF-based electrodes for the detection of dopamine and sulfanilamide. Additionally, the anodic peak potential (E pa) exhibits a linear relationship with pH value. The corresponding linear regression equation is expressed as E pa (V) = −0.05964 pH + 0.4574 (R2 = 0.9868). Therefore, the slopes were calculated to be 59.64 mV/pH for DA. These values are very close to the expected Nernstian theoretical value of 59 mV/pH, , indicating that the number of protons and electrons is equal in the electrochemical oxidation process. As shown in Figure B, the peak currents (I pa) increased as the pH increased from 4.0 to 6.0 and then decreased within the pH range from 6.0 to 7.0. Therefore, pH 6.0 was identified as the optimal pH for subsequent electrochemical sensing experiments of dopamine (DA).

The Effect of Scan Rate

To investigate the electrochemical behavior of target biomolecules, cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests were performed at scan rates ranging from 50 to 400 mV·s–1, as shown in Figure A. The anodic peak currents of DA exhibited a distinct enhancement with increasing scan rates, indicating that the redox process was governed by diffusion-controlled kinetics at the Au@Co-MOF/SPE electrode surface. Notably, the peak currents of DA showed linear dependencies on the square root of the scan rate (ν1/2), as supported by the linear fitting results in Figure B and the corresponding equations: I pa = 123.2ν1/2–14.60 (R2 = 0.9969, for DA); I pc = −142.6ν1/2 + 21.87 (R2 = 0.9899, for DA). This linear relationship between I p (μA) and ν1/2 (mV·s–1) is characteristic of diffusion-limited electron transfer, confirming that the electrochemical oxidation of DA on the Au@Co-MOF/SPE electrode is primarily controlled by the mass transport of analytes to the electrode surface.

The LOD and Sensitivity of the Detection System for DA

The electrochemical performance of dopamine (DA) at the Au@Co-MOF/SPE electrode was evaluated via differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) measurements under optimized volumetric conditions, where the DA concentration was varied in the range of 0.1 μM to 100 μM (Figure A. Moreover, as the concentration of DA increases, the peak current exhibits a linear relationship with DA concentration, as reflected in the corresponding plots. Specifically, DA shows a linear concentration range of 0.1–100 μM (Figure B), with a sensitivity of 2.0 μA·μM–1·cm–2. Additionally, the linear regression equation for this relationship is expressed as I (μA) = 0.2201C DA (μM) + 2.454 (R2 = 0.9913). Furthermore, the low detection limits (LODs) were calculated through these equations to be approximately 0.02 μM (S/N = 3, where S is the slope of the calibration plot and N is the standard deviation of blank signals) toward DA. Therefore, the Au@Co-MOF/SPE can present good electrocatalytic performance, suggesting that the Au@Co-MOF hybrids have remarkable conductivity and outstanding electron transfer ability.

Interference Resistance, Stability Test, and Reproducibility

The anti-interference ability of the Au@Co-MOF/SPE sensor was investigated via CV for the detection of dopamine (DA, 50 μM) in pH 6.0 PBS solution containing various common coexisting species. These interferences included 50 μM uric acid (UA), paracetamol (PA), and ascorbic acid (AA), as well as 500 μM xanthine, KCl, NaNO3, ZnSO4, CaCl2, NaF, glucose, urea, sucrose, fructose, MgCl2, γ-amino acid, cysteine, glutamic acid, and lactic acid. As depicted in Figure C, the oxidation peak currents of these interfering substances produced minimal responses when their concentrations were 10-fold higher than that of DA. The phenomenon observed higher oxidation peak current for the detection of UA, PA, and AA compared to other interference substances, exemplifies the exceptional potential of Au@Co-MOF/SPE as a proficient and sensitive platform for UA, PA, and AA. This breakthrough has the potential to unlock novel possibilities for its implementation across diverse fields where precise and reliable quantification of UA, PA, and AA is of utmost importance. These results suggest that the sensor exhibited superior electrocatalytic performance, excellent anti-interference performance, and exceptional selectivity for DA detection.

Moreover, the stability of this electrochemical sensor was evaluated after successive 1000-cycle CVs in 0.1 M PBS (pH 6.0) containing 50 μM DA. As shown in Figure S3 and Figure D, the anodic peak current of DA at the Au@Co-MOF/SPE electrode decreased by only 5.3% after 1000 CV cycles. These results clearly demonstrate that the Au@Co-MOF/SPE-modified electrode exhibits excellent electrochemical stability and desirable water stability in aqueous media, which is critical for reliable analytical applications. Collectively, these findings confirm that the Au@Co-MOF composite can deliver outstanding performance for the electrochemical detection of DA.

CV measurements investigated the reproducibility of the Au@Co-MOF/SPE for DA. Specifically, a 100 μM DA solution was added to PBS (pH = 6), and detection was carried out using five different electrodes. The RSD of the peak currents was found to be 3.37% (with RSD ≤ 5%) (Figure S4). The obtained data indicate that the electrode sensor possessed good reproducibility.

Real Samples Analysis

To evaluate the practical applicability of the as-prepared Au@Co-MOF/SPE in biomedical analysis, DPV was employed to determine DA in human urine and serum samples under physiological conditions. Before analysis, the biological samples were diluted 100-fold with PBS (pH 6.0) to mitigate matrix effects. The results summarized in Table S3 demonstrate excellent recovery rates for DA detection: in human serum samples, recoveries ranged from 95.5% to 104.8% with relative standard deviations (RSDs) < 4% (n = 5), while urine samples showed recoveries of 102.8% to 104.5% with RSDs < 5% (n = 5). These findings confirm that the Au@Co-MOF/SPE electrode enables reliable quantification of dopamine (DA) in complex biological matrices, thereby establishing it as a promising candidate for electrochemical sensing applications in clinical diagnostics.

Conclusion

This study reports the rational design and electrochemical evaluation of a gold nanoparticle-decorated cobalt-based metal–organic framework (Au@Co-MOF) as an efficient electrode material for the detection of dopamine (DA) in 0.1 M phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The Au@Co-MOF-modified screen-printed electrode (SPE) exhibits outstanding analytical performance, including excellent selectivity, ultrahigh sensitivity (2.0 μA·μM–1·cm–2), a wide linear response range (0.1–100 μM), a low limit of detection (LOD, 0.02 μM), and robust electrochemical stability. The embedded conductive AuNPs enhance electron-transfer kinetics within the Co-MOF matrix, which significantly accelerates the redox reaction of DA. Notably, the Au@Co-MOF/SPE platform enables reliable quantification of DA in complex human biological matrices (urine and serum) with high recovery rates, further validating its practical applicability. Collectively, these findings underscore the considerable potential of this engineered nanocomposite for the sensitive and selective detection of clinically relevant small molecules in biological fluids, thereby providing promising prospects for advanced applications in neurochemical monitoring and point-of-care (POC) diagnostics.

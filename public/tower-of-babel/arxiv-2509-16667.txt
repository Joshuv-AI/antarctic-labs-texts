On Ternary Trees and Fighting Fish
Sen-Peng Eu, Tung-Shan Fu, Yu-Ren Pan

Abstract
. Fighting fish is a combinatorial configuration introduced by Duchi et al. as a new model of branching surfaces that generalizes directed convex polyominoes. We come up with an alternative construction of fighting fish, using a tree structure built on the so-called stem cells of fighting fish. From this perspective, we establish a bijection between ternary trees and fighting fish with a marked strip of cells, which specializes to a direct bijection between left ternary trees and fighting fish. Using these results, we obtain a combinatorial enumeration of the fighting fish of size n n by establishing a ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -to-2 bijection with the ternary trees having n n nodes. We present some additional enumerative results including that the fighting fish with a marked tail and the horizontally symmetric fighting fish are equinumerous with the ordered pairs of ternary trees having a total of a prescribed number of nodes.

1.1.
Fighting fish

A

is a 45
∘

left upper edge
,
left lower edge
,
right upper edge
and
right lower edge
. An edge of a cell is

is a finite set of cells glued together edge by edge that can be constructed from an initial cell called

by attaching new cells successively. More precisely, there are three ways to add a new cell (see Figure
2
).

Algorithm A

(A1)

Upper gluing
: we attach it to a free right upper edge of a cell.

(A2)

Lower gluing
: we attach it to a free right lower edge of a cell.

(A3)

Double gluing
: if there is a cell
c
with two cells

and
b
attached to its right upper and lower edge, and such that

(
b
, respectively) has a free right lower (upper, respectively) edge, then we attach the new cell to both

and

The head of a fighting fish is the only cell with two free left edges. A

is a cell with two free right edges.
The

of a fighting fish is the number of its free right edges minus 1. Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
denote the set of fighting fish of size
n
. Note that every fighting fish has as many free right edges as free left edges.

By a

(

, respectively)

of a fighting fish we mean a maximal sequence
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m}
of cells such that the left upper (lower, respectively) edge of

is glued to the right lower (upper, respectively) edge of
a_{j}
, for some positive integer

and
1\leq j\leq m-1
. The cell

is the
top
(
bottom
, respectively) cell of the descending (ascending, respectively) strip, and the cell
a_{m}
is the bottom (top, respectively) cell of the descending (ascending, respectively) strip.
In particular, the descending strip containing the head is also called the
jaw
of the fighting fish.

1.2.
Ternary trees

A

is a finite set of nodes which is either empty, or contains a root and three disjoint ternary trees known as the left, middle and right subtrees of the root.
For each node

there is a path

from the root to the node

\mu(x)
consists of three kinds of steps, left, middle and right. The

of

is the difference between the number of
left and right steps in
\mu(x)
. More precisely, the root has abscissa 0, and if

is an internal node with abscissa

, the left, middle and right child of

has abscissa
j+1,j
and
j-1
, respectively. By convention, we orient the abscissa axis toward the left. Let
\alpha(x)
denote the abscissa of

. Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
denote the set of ternary trees having
n
nodes. It is a classical result that

|{\mathcal{T}}_{n}|=\frac{1}{2n+1}\binom{3n}{n}

(1.1)

[
15
, A001764]
.
A
left tree
is a ternary tree whose nodes all have nonnegative abscissas. Let
{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}
denote the set of left ternary trees having
n
nodes.

1.3.
Main results

In the next section, we introduce the notion of stem cells of fighting fish and present an alternative construction of fighting fish, using a tree structure built on the stem cells (see Proposition

For all

Using a classical method of generating function, Duchi et al.

derived the number of fighting fish of size

|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}|=\frac{2}{(n+1)(2n+1)}\binom{3n}{n}

(1.2)

Theorem 1.2

There is a

When the map

Theorem 1.3

For all

, there is a bijection
\phi:T\mapsto F
of
{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}
onto

such that a left ternary tree

We remark that this bijection is established by a direct construction involving statistic insights, which is not isomorphic to the composition of the bijection between non-separable rooted planar maps and fighting fish by Duchi–Henriet
[

]
and the recursive bijection between left ternary trees and non-separable rooted planar maps by Del Lungo–Del Ristoro–Penaud
[

or by Jacquard–Schaeffer
[

(see the concluding remarks).

We present some additional enumerative results derived from Theorem

Duchi et al.

2.
An alternative construction of fighting fish

In this section, we introduce the notion of stem cells of fighting fish and present an alternative construction of the fighting fish.

Definition 2.1
.

A cell of a fighting fish
F
is called a
stem cell
if it satisfies one of the following conditions:

(i)

at least one of its two right edges is free;

(ii)

it itself has no free right edges, but its right point is the intersection of two free edges. In this case, it is called a
branch cell
.

Note that the bottom (top, respectively) cell of each descending (ascending, respectively) strip is a stem cell since it has a free right lower (upper, respectively) edge.
For example, the fighting fish shown in Figure
3
contains 9 stem cells, which are indicated by nodes. Among them, those two cells
w,a
are branch cells, and those three cells
b,c,d
are tails.

Lemma 2.2
.

The following properties hold for every fighting fish
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
.

(i)

The number of branch cells in
F
equals the number of tails minus 1.

(ii)

There are
n
stem cells in
F
.

Proof.

(i) Let
{\mathsf{tail}}(F)
(
{\mathsf{branch}}(F)
, respectively) be the number of tails (branch cells, respectively) of
F
. A fighting fish can be constructed from a cell by adding cells one by one using the operations (A1)-(A3). Initially,
F
consist of a single cell, where
{\mathsf{tail}}(F)=1
and
{\mathsf{branch}}(F)=0
. Whenever we add a new cell to
F
using (A1), (A2) or (A3), we observe that the difference between the number of tails and branch cells is always 1.

(ii) Let
{\mathsf{stem}}(F)
be the number of stem cells of
F
. Suppose there are
d_{0}
(
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
, respectively) stem cells in
F
having 0 (1 and 2, respectively) free right edges. Then
{\mathsf{stem}}(F)=d_{0}+d_{1}+d_{2}
and
d_{1}+2d_{2}=n+1
. By (i), we have
d_{0}=d_{2}-1
. If follows that
{\mathsf{stem}}(F)=n
.
∎

with a graph

defined as follows. By abuse of notation, the stem cell associated to a vertex

in

is also denoted by

(i)

The vertex set of

is the set of stem cells of

.

(ii)

Two vertices

are adjacent if the stem cells

Lemma 2.3

The graph

Proof.

We shall prove that the graph

The following result shows that a fighting fish is uniquely determined by its stem cells.

Proposition 2.6
.

A fighting fish can be reconstructed from its stem cells.

Proof.

We shall prove
F^{\prime}=F
by induction on
n

For

F^{\prime}=F
, which is a single cell. Suppose the assertion

holds up to size

u

, and let

Case 1.
\lambda(u)=N

be the fighting fish obtained from

be the labeled rooted tree constructed on the stem cells of

Case 2.

Proof of Theorem

.

For

(3.1)

Moreover, if

(3.2)

By (

) and (

\displaystyle(\ell+1)|{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\ell}|

\displaystyle=|{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\ell}|,

(3.3)

\displaystyle=|{\mathcal{T}}_{n,n-\ell-1}|.

{\textsf{Des}}(x_{j})
at a cell between

and
x_{j}
, and the cell

is the first stem cell in
{\textsf{Asc}}(a)
above
{\textsf{Des}}(x_{j})
in

. Then by (C4), the stem cell

is added on the

. This contradicts that
{\textsf{Des}}(x_{j})
is the jaw of
F
. Thus, all nodes of

have nonnegative abscissas. The result follows.

Lemma 4.4
.

If the tree

has a jaw with

Proof.

By Lemma

, the tree

. Note that the fish

by cutting the right lower edge of

x_{i}
, for

such that

is the jaw of

. Note that each

. By the map

. For each tree

Additional enumerative results

In this section, we present some enumerative results based on the bijections in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3

5.1.
Enumeration of fighting fish with a marked tail

Using the method of generating function, Duchi et al.
[
5
]
derived the total number of tails of all fighting fish in
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
, which coincides with the number of ordered pair of ternary trees having a total of
n-1
nodes. We present a bijection between these two objects.
For a cell
t
of a fighting fish
F
, let
t^{{\mathrm{c}}}
denote the cell in
F^{{\mathrm{c}}}
which is the reflection image of the cell
t
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For all
n\geq 1
, there is a bijection

\{(F,t)\mid F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\mbox{ and $t$ is a tail of $F$}\}\simeq({\mathcal{T}}\times{\mathcal{T}})_{n-1}

(5.1)

such that
(F,t)\mapsto(T_{1},T_{2})
implies
(F^{{\mathrm{c}}},t^{{\mathrm{c}}})\mapsto(T_{2},T_{1})
.

Proof.

Given a fish
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
and a tail
t
of
F
, we shall construct a ternary tree
T
on the stem cells of
F
such that the root of
T
is associated to the cell
t
and contains two subtrees (possibly empty).

To construct the ternary tree
T
, we first create a fighting fish
\widehat{F}
from
F
by attaching a new cell
p
to the right upper edge of the cell
t
. This creates a new descending strip consisting of the stem cell
p
. Setting
p
to be the root, we construct a ternary tree
\widehat{T}
from
\widehat{F}
using the algorithms B and D. Note that the node
t
is the right child of the root
p
since the node
t
is adjacent to the root
p
on the
S
side of
p
. Moreover, if
v
is a child of
t
then
\lambda(v)\in\{S,W\}
since the cell
t
has two free right edges. Thus, the node
t
has two subtrees (possibly empty), the middle subtree
T_{1}
and the right subtree
T_{2}
. Then the requested ternary tree
T
is obtained from
\widehat{T}
by removing the root
p
, and
(F,t)
is mapped to the ordered pair
(T_{1},T_{2})\in({\mathcal{T}}\times{\mathcal{T}})_{n-1}
.

The inverse map can be established by a reverse operation. Given an ordered pair
(T_{1},T_{2})\in({\mathcal{T}}\times{\mathcal{T}})_{n-1}
, create a ternary tree
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
by adding a new node
t
as the root and setting
T_{1}
(
T_{2}
, respectively) to be the middle (right, respectively) subtree of
t
. Set
\lambda(t)=S
and determine the vertex-labeling of
T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2}
, following the rules (D2)-(D5) in the algorithm D. Then construct the corresponding fighting fish of
T
, using the algorithm C.

For the conjugation
F^{{\mathrm{c}}}
of
F
, we construct a ternary tree
T^{\prime}
on the stem cells of
F^{{\mathrm{c}}}
such that the root is associated to the cell
t^{{\mathrm{c}}}
and
\lambda(t^{{\mathrm{c}}})=S
. Moreover, the node
t^{{\mathrm{c}}}
has two subtrees (possibly empty), the middle subtree
T^{\prime}_{1}
and the right subtree
T^{\prime}_{2}
.
Notice that if
v
is a child of
t
with
\lambda(v)=S
(
W
, respectively) then
v^{{\mathrm{c}}}
is a child of
t^{{\mathrm{c}}}
with
\lambda(v^{{\mathrm{c}}})=W
(
S
, respectively). It follows that the subtree
T^{\prime}_{1}
(
T^{\prime}_{2}
, respectively) of
t^{{\mathrm{c}}}
is isomorphic to the subtree
T_{2}
(
T_{1}
, respectively) of
t
up to an interchanging labels
S\leftrightarrow W
and
E\leftrightarrow N
. Thus,
(F^{{\mathrm{c}}},t^{{\mathrm{c}}})\mapsto(T_{2},T_{1})
. The result follows.
∎

5.2.
Enumeration of symmetric fighting fish

A fighting fish
F
is
symmetric
if
F^{{\mathrm{c}}}=F
, where the axis of symmetry is the horizontal line passing through the center of the head. Let
{\mathcal{SF}}_{n}
denote the set of symmetric fighting fish of size
n
.
Note that a symmetric fighting fish contains odd number of stem cells since the terminal cell on the axis is either a tail or a branch cell. Thus,
{\mathcal{SF}}_{n}
is empty if
n
is even.

Theorem 5.2
.

For all
n\geq 0
, the following results hold.

(i)

There is a bijection

\{F\in{\mathcal{SF}}_{2n+1}\mid\mbox{ $F$ contains odd number of tails}\}\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{n}.

(5.2)

(ii)

There is a bijection

{\mathcal{SF}}_{2n+1}\simeq({\mathcal{T}}\times{\mathcal{T}})_{n}.

(5.3)

Proof.

(i) Given a fish
F\in{\mathcal{SF}}_{2n+1}
with odd number of tails, the terminal cell on the axis must be a tail, denoted by
t
(see Figure
12
(i)). Using the bijection in Theorem
5.1
, we obtain
(F,t)\mapsto(T_{1},T_{2})\in({\mathcal{T}}\times{\mathcal{T}})_{2n}
. Moreover,
(F^{{\mathrm{c}}},t^{{\mathrm{c}}})\mapsto(T_{2},T_{1})
. Thus, since
F
is symmetric, these two trees
T_{1},T_{2}
are isomorphic. The requested bijection is defined by
F\mapsto T_{1}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
. The assertion (i) follows.

(ii) We partition the set
({\mathcal{T}}\times{\mathcal{T}})_{n}
into two subsets
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
, where
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
consists of the ordered pair
(T,\emptyset)
for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
, and
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
consists of the ordered pair
(T_{1},T_{2})\in({\mathcal{T}}\times{\mathcal{T}})_{n}
such that
T_{2}
is nonempty. Using the result in (i), we obtain the following bijection

\{F\in{\mathcal{SF}}_{2n+1}\mid\mbox{ $F$ contains odd number of tails}\}\simeq{\mathcal{A}}_{n}.

(5.4)

We shall establish the following bijection

\{F\in{\mathcal{SF}}_{2n+1}\mid\mbox{ $F$ contains even number of tails}\}\simeq{\mathcal{B}}_{n}.

(5.5)

Given a fish
F\in{\mathcal{SF}}_{2n+1}
with even number of tails, the terminal cell on the axis must be a branch cell, denoted by
t
. Then there is a stem cell, say
c
(
c^{\prime}
, respectively), attached to the right lower (upper, respectively) of the cell
t
(see Figure
12
(ii)).

We construct a rooted tree
T
on the stem cells of
F
such that the root is associated to the cell
t
and contains two pairs
(T_{1},T_{2}),(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})
of subtrees as follows.
Set
\lambda(t)=S
. For any labeled node
u
, if
v
is a node adjacent to
u
on the
N
(
E
,
W
and
S
, respectively) side of
u
, we set
v
to be a child of
u
with
\lambda(v)=N
(
E
,
W
and
S
, respectively). Let
T_{1}
(
T_{2}
, respectively) be the subtree of
T
built on the
S
(
E
, respectively) side of
t
. Note that the subtree
T_{1}
is possibly empty and that the subtree
T_{2}
is rooted at
c
. We turn
T_{1},T_{2}
into ternary subtrees of
T
, following the rules (E1)-(E4). Let
T^{\prime}_{1}
(
T^{\prime}_{2}
, respectively) be the subtree of
T
built on the
W
(
N
, respectively) side of
t
.
Since
F
is symmetric, these two trees
T_{1}
and
T^{\prime}_{1}
(
T_{2}
and
T^{\prime}_{2}
, respectively) are isomorphic up to an interchanging labels
S\leftrightarrow W
and
E\leftrightarrow N
.
Then the fish
F
is mapped to the ordered pair
(T_{1},T_{2})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
.

The inverse map can be established by a reverse operation. The assertion (ii) follows from the bijections in (
5.4
) and (
5.5
).
∎

By (
1.1
), (
1.4
) and Theorem
5.2
, we have the following results.

Corollary 5.3
.

For all
n\geq 0
, the following results hold.

(i)

The number of symmetric fighting fish of size
2n+1
is
\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{3n+1}{n}
.

(ii)

The number of symmetric fighting fish of size
2n+1
containing odd number of tails is
\frac{1}{2n+1}\binom{3n}{n}
.

(iii)

The number of symmetric fighting fish of size

containing even number of tails is
\frac{1}{2n+1}\binom{3n}{n+1}
.

5.3.
Enumeration of left ternary trees

In the following, we give a combinatorial proof of the fact that the number of left ternary trees in
{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}
is
\frac{2}{n+1}
times the number of ternary trees in
T_{n}
. Define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}^{{\textrm{odd}}}

\displaystyle=\{(T,u)\mid u\in T\in{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}\mbox{ and $\alpha(u)$ is odd}\};

\displaystyle{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}^{{\textrm{even}}}

\displaystyle=\{(T,u)\mid u\in T\in{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}\mbox{ and $\alpha(u)$ is even}\}.

Using the bijections in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
, we present a simplified proof of a result by Jacquard–Schaeffer (cf.
[
13
, Theorem 2]
).

Theorem 5.4
.

For
n\geq 1
, there exist bijections

\displaystyle{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}^{{\textrm{odd}}}

\displaystyle\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\setminus{\mathcal{LT}}_{n},

(5.6)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}^{{\textrm{even}}}

\displaystyle\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{n}.

(5.7)

Hence we have

|{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}|=\frac{2}{n+1}|{\mathcal{T}}_{n}|.

Proof.

For
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
, let
{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\ell}
(
{\mathcal{LT}}_{n,\ell}
, respectively) be the subset of
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
(
{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}
, respectively) consisting of the trees having
\ell
nodes with odd abscissa (and hence
n-\ell
nodes with even abscissa).

For any
T\in{\mathcal{LT}}_{n,\ell}
, by Theorem
1.3
the tree
T
maps to a fighting fish
F
containing
\ell+1
descending strips. If
u
is one of the
\ell
nodes with odd abscissa in
T
, by Lemma
3.2
(iv) we have
\lambda(u)\in\{N,S\}
. Note that the cell
u
is the unique stem cell with label
N
or
S
in the descending strip
Q
containing
u
. Note that the cell
u
is not in the jaw of
F
since the root of
T
is at abscissa 0. Using the bijection in Theorem
1.1
, we obtain a ternary tree
\phi^{-1}(F,Q)\in{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\ell}
, which is not a left ternary tree. This establishes the bijection

\{(T,u)\mid u\in T\in{\mathcal{LT}}_{n,\ell}\mbox{ and $\alpha(u)$ is odd}\}\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\ell}\setminus{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}.

(5.8)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}^{{\textrm{odd}}}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\{(T,u)\mid u\in T\in{\mathcal{LT}}_{n,\ell}\mbox{ and $\alpha(u)$ is odd}\}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{n-1}{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\ell}\right)\setminus{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{T}}_{n}\setminus{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}.

Moreover, if
u
is one of the
n-\ell
nodes with even abscissa, by Lemma
3.2
(iv) we have
\lambda(u)\in\{E,W\}
. Note that the cell
u
is the unique stem cell with label
E
or
W
in the ascending strip
Q
containing
u
. Consider the conjugation of
F
. Note that
Q^{{\mathrm{c}}}
becomes one of the
n-\ell
descending strips of
F^{{\mathrm{c}}}
. Using the bijection in Theorem
1.1
, we obtain a ternary tree
\phi^{-1}(F^{{\mathrm{c}}},Q^{{\mathrm{c}}})\in{\mathcal{T}}_{n,n-\ell-1}
. This establishes the bijection

\{(T,u)\mid u\in T\in{\mathcal{LT}}_{n,\ell}\mbox{ and $\alpha(u)$ is even}\}\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{n,n-\ell-1}.

(5.9)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{LT}}_{n}^{{\textrm{even}}}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\{(T,u)\mid u\in T\in{\mathcal{LT}}_{n,\ell}\mbox{ and $\alpha(u)$ is even}\}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{n-1}{\mathcal{T}}_{n,n-\ell-1}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{T}}_{n}.

The result follows.
∎

6.
Concluding Remarks

From a fresh perspective on the construction of fighting fish, we establish a bijection
\phi
between the set of ternary trees having
n
nodes and the set of fighting fish of size
n
with a marked descending strip. Using this result, we obtain a combinatorial enumeration of the fighting fish of size
n
by establishing a 2-to-
(n+1)
bijection between these two sets
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
.

When the map
\phi
is restricted to the left ternary trees, we obtain a direct bijection between left ternary trees and fighting fish. As remarked earlier, this bijection is not isomorphic to the composition of the bijection
\Phi
between non-separable rooted planar maps and fighting fish in
[
7
]
and the recursive bijection
\Psi
between left ternary trees and non-separable rooted planar maps in
[
4
]
or
[
13
]
. For example, the left ternary tree in Figure
13
(i) is carried by the map
\Psi
to the non-separable rooted planar maps in Figure
13
(ii), which is carried by the map
\Phi
to the fighting fish in Figure
13
(iii). As shown in Figure
14
, this fighting fish is mapped to a different left ternary tree under our bijection
\phi
.

However, Duchi et al.
[
6
]
gave a refined conjecture that involves more statistics regarding the correspondence between these two families of objects. The
fin
of a fighting fish is the path starting from the left point of its head, following its border counterclockwise, and ending at the right point of the first tail the path reaches. On the other hand, the
core
of a ternary tree is the largest subtree including the root vertex and consisting only of left and middle edges. So far the following conjecture has still not been resolved.

Conjecture 6.1

(Duchi–Guerrini–Rinaldi–Schaeffer
[
6
]
)
.

The number of fighting fish with size
n
, fin length
k
, having
h
tails,
i
ascending strips and
j
descending strips is equal to the number of left ternary trees with
n
nodes, core size
k
, having
h
right branches,
i+1
non root nodes with even abscissa and
j
nodes with odd abscissa.

q

A stabilized virtual element framework for the steady state Boussinesq equation with temperature-dependent parameters
Sudheer Mishra, Sundararajan Natarajan, Natarajan E

Abstract
This work presents a new conforming stabilized virtual element method for the generalized Boussinesq equation with temperature-dependent viscosity and thermal conductivity. A gradient-based local projection stabilization method is introduced in the discrete formulation to circumvent the violation of the discrete inf-sup condition. The well-posedness of the continuous problem is established under sufficiently small datum. We derive a stabilized virtual element problem for the Boussinesq equation using equal-order virtual element approximations. The proposed method has several advantages, such as being more straightforward to implement, free from higher-order derivative terms, providing separate stabilization terms without introducing coupling between solution components, and minimizing the number of globally coupled degrees of freedom. The existence of a discrete solution to the stabilized virtual element problem is demonstrated using the Brouwer fixed-point theorem. The error estimates are derived in the energy norm. Additionally, several numerical examples are presented to show the efficiency and robustness of the proposed method, confirming the theoretical results.

Introduction

Free convection plays a crucial role in various fields, such as science, engineering, industrial and geophysical applications. For instance, it is employed in the cooling of electrical and electronic devices, temperature distribution in the Earth’s mantle, petrochemical processes, solar ponds, food processing, heat exchanger design, thermo-protection systems, climate modeling, and the study of ocean currents, and so on. The mathematical description of the above processes is usually given by a non-isothermal thermo-fluid dynamic problem— the Boussinesq equation, which involves the coupling of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation with a convection-diffusion transport equation. The coupling occurs through two key mechanisms: the buoyancy force, which appears in the momentum equation and drives the flow due to temperature-induced density variations, and the convective heat transfer term, which captures the transport of thermal energy by the fluid motion. Due to its significance in various applications, several computational methods have been developed, including the Finite Element Method (FEM) for the Boussinesq problem with scalar or temperature-dependent (nonlinear) coefficients. The proposed method has several advantages: easier to implement, provides separate stabilization terms, reduces the coupling between virtual element triplets, is free from higher-order derivative terms, is efficient for addressing higher-order virtual elements, and minimizes the global degrees of freedom. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

\mathbf{u}
, a pressure field

, and a temperature field

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla\cdot(\mu(\theta)\nabla\mathbf{u})+(\nabla\mathbf{u})\mathbf{u}+\nabla p-\alpha\theta\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

(2.2)

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(\kappa(\theta)\nabla\theta)+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=\theta_{D}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

(2.5)

where the functions

and

is a given external force per unit mass,
\theta_{D}

. The
W^{k}_{p}
-norm and its semi-norm are denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{k,p,\omega}
and

, respectively. We will relax the subscript
\Omega

(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega}
when no confusion arises. We adopt bold fonts or bold symbols to represent vector-valued functions or tensors. The positive constant
C
is independent of the mesh sizes and can differ for different occurrences. The Poincaré constant is given by
C_{p}
. Further, the constant

depends on
\Omega

\displaystyle\|w\|_{1,\Omega}\leq C_{q}\|\nabla w\|_{0,\Omega}\qquad\text{for all}\,\,w\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega).

Note that for

. Additionally, the compact embedding

\displaystyle\|w\|_{0,r,\Omega}\leq C_{1\hookrightarrow r}\|w\|_{1,\Omega}\qquad\text{for all}\,\,w\in H^{1}(\Omega),

\displaystyle 0<\kappa_{\ast}\leq\kappa(\xi)

\displaystyle|\kappa(\xi_{1})-\kappa(\xi_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq L_{\kappa}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R};

We now introduce the following functional setting for the Boussinesq equation:

\mathbf{V}:=[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{2}
,
Q:=L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)=\big\{p\in L^{2}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}pd\Omega=0\big\}
,

\Sigma:=H^{1}(\Omega)
,
\Sigma_{0}:=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
\Sigma_{D}:=\big\{\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega):\phi_{|\partial\Omega}=\theta_{D}\}
,

endowed with their natural norms. In addition, we define the following forms:

•

a_{V}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot):\Sigma\times\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
a_{V}(\phi;\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}):=\int_{\Omega}\mu(\phi)\nabla\mathbf{v}:\nabla\mathbf{w}\,d\Omega

•

;

•

c_{V}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot):\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

c_{V}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}):=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\mathbf{w})\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{z}\,d\Omega

•

a_{T}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot):\Sigma\times\Sigma\times\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
a_{T}(\theta;\phi,\psi):=\int_{\Omega}\kappa(\theta)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi\,d\Omega
;

•

c_{T}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot):\mathbf{V}\times\Sigma\times\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

c_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi):=\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\phi)\psi\,d\Omega

•

For given
\phi\in\Sigma
, define

F_{\phi}(\mathbf{v}):=\int_{\Omega}(\alpha\mathbf{g}\phi)\cdot\mathbf{v}\,d\Omega+\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{v}\,d\Omega
.

We remark that under the assumption
(A0)
, the above forms are well-defined. For any
\phi\in\Sigma
, the maps
a_{V}(\phi;\cdot,\cdot)
and

are bilinear forms. In addition, the forms above satisfy the following stability properties:

•

: for any given

\displaystyle a_{V}(\phi;\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\leq\mu^{\ast}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|_{0,\Omega},\qquad a_{V}(\phi;\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w})\geq\mu_{\ast}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega},\quad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{V}.

(2.6)

•

: for any given
\theta\in\Sigma

\displaystyle a_{T}(\theta;\phi,\psi)\leq\kappa^{\ast}\|\nabla\phi\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\psi\|_{0,\Omega},\qquad a_{T}(\theta;\phi,\phi)\geq\kappa_{\ast}\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{0,\Omega},\quad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\phi,\psi\in\Sigma.

(2.7)

•

bilinear form

satisfies the continuity property, ensuring boundedness,

\displaystyle|b(\mathbf{v},q)|\leq{\sqrt{2}}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}\|q\|_{0,\Omega}\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}\,\,\text{and}\,\,q\in Q.

(2.8)

\displaystyle c_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi)

(2.11)

\displaystyle c^{S}_{V}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[c_{V}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})-c_{V}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})\Big]\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}\in\mathbf{V},

(2.12)

\displaystyle c^{S}_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[c_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi)-c_{T}(\mathbf{v};\psi,\phi)\Big]\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\phi,\psi\in\Sigma.

(2.13)

\displaystyle c^{S}_{V}(\mathbf{v};\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle\leq N_{0}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\mathbf{z}\|_{0,\Omega}\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}\in\mathbf{V},

(2.14)

\displaystyle c^{S}_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle\leq N_{0}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\phi\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\psi\|_{0,\Omega}\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\phi\in\Sigma,\psi\in\Sigma_{0},

(2.15)

is the continuity constant.

Finally, we introduce the following forms:

\displaystyle A_{V}[\theta;(\mathbf{v},q),(\mathbf{w},r)]

\displaystyle:=a_{V}(\theta;\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})-b(\mathbf{w},q)+b(\mathbf{v},r)\qquad\,\text{for all}\,\,\theta\in\Sigma\,\,\text{and}\,\,(\mathbf{v},q),(\mathbf{w},r)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q,

\displaystyle:=a_{T}(\theta;\phi,\psi)+c^{S}_{T}(\mathbf{v};\phi,\psi)\qquad\qquad\quad\text{ for all}\,\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\theta,\phi,\psi\in\Sigma.

Step 3.
A compact and convex ball: We now aim to find a convex and compact ball where the hypothesis of the Brouwer fixed-point theorem can be fulfilled. In this sequel, we first replace

by
(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0})
, and then employ the stability properties (
2.6
), (
2.7
), (
2.10
) and (

\displaystyle\langle\chi(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0}),(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{\ast}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\kappa_{\ast}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+a_{T}(\theta_{0}+\theta_{1};\theta_{1},\theta_{0})+c^{S}_{T}(\mathbf{u};\theta_{1},\theta_{0})-F_{\theta_{0}+\theta_{1}}(\mathbf{u})-(\mathcal{Q},\theta_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{\ast}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\kappa_{\ast}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}-\kappa^{\ast}\|\nabla\theta_{1}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}-c_{T}(\mathbf{u};\theta_{0},\theta_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad-F_{\theta_{0}+\theta_{1}}(\mathbf{u})-(\mathcal{Q},\theta_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{\ast}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\kappa_{\ast}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}-\kappa^{\ast}\|\nabla\theta_{1}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}-{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{0,6,\Omega}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\theta_{1}\|_{0,3,\Omega}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\alpha\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\theta_{0}\|_{0,4,\Omega}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{0,4,\Omega}-\alpha\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\theta_{1}\|_{0,3,\Omega}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{0,6,\Omega}

We use the Sobolev embedding theorem, the Poincar
\acute{\text{e}}
inequality and the bound (

):

\displaystyle\langle\chi(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0}),(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\min\big\{\mu_{\ast},\kappa_{\ast}\big\}\big(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}\big)-{c\kappa^{\ast}\epsilon^{-4}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\theta_{D}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\,\,-c\epsilon\alpha C_{q}C_{1\hookrightarrow 6}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\theta_{D}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}-C_{p}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{0,\Omega}-C_{p}\|\mathcal{Q}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(2.30)

Employing the Young inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle\langle\chi(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0}),(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big[\frac{C_{o}}{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{0,\Omega}-\alpha C^{2}_{q}C^{2}_{1\hookrightarrow 4}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}-C_{o}\alpha\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\Omega}-C_{p}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big]\,+

(2.31)

To obtain the non-negativity condition, we proceed as follows

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{C_{o}}\big[\alpha C^{2}_{q}C^{2}_{1\hookrightarrow 4}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}+C_{o}\alpha\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\Omega}+C_{p}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big],

\displaystyle\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{C_{o}}\Big[c\kappa^{\ast}\epsilon^{-4}\|\theta_{D}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}+C_{p}\|\mathcal{Q}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big]\equiv F_{\theta}(\theta_{D},\mathcal{Q}).

(2.33)

Using (
2.33
) in (

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{C_{o}}\Big[\big(2c\kappa^{\ast}\epsilon^{-4}\alpha C_{o}^{-1}C^{2}_{q}C^{2}_{1\hookrightarrow 4}\|\theta_{D}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}+2\alpha C_{p}C_{o}^{-1}C^{2}_{q}C^{2}_{1\hookrightarrow 4}\|\mathcal{Q}\|_{0,\Omega}+C_{o}\alpha\big)\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{p}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big]

(2.34)

We now choose

such that

, and we will determine its value later. Then, we conclude that

\displaystyle\langle\chi(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0}),(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0})\rangle\geq 0\qquad\text{for all}\,\,(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0})\in\mathbf{V}_{div}\times\Sigma_{0}\quad\text{such that}\quad\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}=\rho^{2}.

(2.35)

Step 4.
Finite approximation of

: Since the spaces

are separable, we can construct a monotonic sequence

Thus, employing the Brouwer fixed-point theorem, for each

, there exists

(2.37)

Step 5.
Limiting case: From (

is uniformly bounded in

for

. Furthermore, for

, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\chi(\mathbf{u}_{r},\theta_{0,r}),(\mathbf{v}_{m},\psi_{m})\rangle=0\qquad\text{for all}\,\,(\mathbf{v}_{m},\psi_{m})\in\mathbf{V}^{m}_{div}\times\Sigma^{m}_{0}.

(2.38)

As

, we observe the following:

\bullet

F_{\theta_{0,r}+\theta_{1}}(\mathbf{v}_{m})-F_{\theta_{0}+\theta_{1}}(\mathbf{v}_{m})=\big(\alpha\mathbf{g}(\theta_{0,r}-\theta_{0}),\mathbf{v}_{m}\big)\leq\alpha\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\theta_{0,r}-\theta_{0}\|_{0,4,\Omega}\|\mathbf{v}_{m}\|_{0,4,\Omega}
.

Thus,
F_{\theta_{0,r}+\theta_{1}}(\mathbf{v}_{m})\longrightarrow F_{\theta_{0}+\theta_{1}}(\mathbf{v}_{m})
as

.

\bullet
the trilinear form

as

derived in

. Following the same, we obtain

\bullet
applying

strongly in

and the continuity of

\displaystyle\mu(\theta_{0,r}+\theta_{1})\nabla\mathbf{v}_{m}\xrightarrow{r\rightarrow\infty}\mu(\theta_{0}+\theta_{1})\nabla\mathbf{v}_{m}\quad\text{a.e. in}\,\Omega,\qquad\mu(\theta_{0,r}+\theta_{1})\nabla\mathbf{v}_{m}\leq\mu^{\ast}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}_{m}\|_{0,\Omega}.

Further, using the density argument, we get

\displaystyle\langle\chi(\mathbf{u},\theta_{0}),(\mathbf{v},\psi)\rangle=0\qquad\text{for all}\,\,(\mathbf{v},\psi)\in\mathbf{V}_{div}\times\Sigma_{0}.

\displaystyle F^{2}_{\theta}(\theta_{D},\mathcal{Q})

\displaystyle+F^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}(\theta_{D},\mathcal{Q},\mathbf{g},\mathbf{f})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{C^{2}_{o}}\Big[c^{2}{\kappa^{\ast}}^{2}\epsilon^{-8}\|\theta_{D}\|^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}+C^{2}_{p}\|\mathcal{Q}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}\Big]+\frac{16}{C^{2}_{o}}\Big[\big(4c^{2}{\kappa^{\ast}}^{2}\epsilon^{-8}\alpha^{2}C_{o}^{-2}C^{4}_{q}C^{4}_{1\hookrightarrow 4}\|\theta_{D}\|^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}

(2.42)

Furthermore, the estimate (

Since the equal-order VEM approximation is employed, the discrete solution is strongly polluted by non-physical oscillations or moving fronts. To address this issue, we define the following stabilization terms:

6
Conclusion

In this work, we have developed a unified framework of the stabilized virtual element method for the generalized Boussinesq equation on polygonal meshes with temperature-dependent viscosity and thermal conductivity. To address the violation of the discrete inf-sup condition, a gradient-based local projection stabilization term is introduced in the discrete formulation. We derive the well-posedness of the continuous problem under sufficiently small datum. An equivalence relation is shown between the gradient-based and mass-based local projection stabilization methods. The well-posedness of the stabilized virtual element problem is established using the Brouwer fixed-point theorem. The error estimates are derived in the energy norm with optimal convergence rates. Numerical results confirm the theoretical findings.

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}S^{E}((I-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1})q_{h},(I-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1})q_{h})\leq{\lambda^{\ast}_{4}}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\|(I-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1})q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda^{\ast}_{4}}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\|(I-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1})(I-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1})q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda^{\ast}_{4}}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}C^{2}_{p}h^{2}_{E}\|\nabla(I-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1})q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}

\displaystyle\leq{\frac{\lambda^{\ast}_{4}C^{2}_{p}}{\lambda_{3\ast}}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\tau_{2,E}S^{E}_{p}\big(q_{h}-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1}q_{h},q_{h}-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1}q_{h}\big).}

(6.1)

Concerning the first part of
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{2,h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we proceed as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\|\Pi_{k}^{0,E}q_{h}-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\big(\|q_{h}-\Pi^{0,E}_{k}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}+\|q_{h}-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\big(\|q_{h}-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}+\|q_{h}-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}C_{p}^{2}h^{2}_{E}\big(\|\nabla(q_{h}-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k}q_{h})\|^{2}_{0,E}+\|\nabla(q_{h}-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1}q_{h})\|^{2}_{0,E}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}C_{p}^{2}h^{2}_{E}\big(\|\nabla q_{h}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\nabla q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}+\|\nabla q_{h}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 6\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}C_{p}^{2}h^{2}_{E}\big(\|\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\nabla q_{h}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}+\|\nabla q_{h}-\nabla\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}\big).

(6.2)

Now adding (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) and using
\tau_{2,E}\sim h^{2}_{E}
, we have the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{2,h}(q_{h},q_{h})\leq\alpha_{2}\mathcal{L}_{2,h}(q_{h},q_{h}),

(6.3)

where
\alpha_{2}
depends on the Poincar
\acute{\text{e}}
constant
C_{p}
,
\lambda_{3\ast}
and
\lambda_{4}^{\ast}
. For the remaining part, we use the definition of
S^{E}_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
, the bound (
3.16
) and
\tau_{2,E}\sim h^{2}_{E}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\tau_{2,E}S^{E}_{p}\big(q_{h}-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1}q_{h},q_{h}-\Pi^{E,\nabla}_{k-1}q_{h}\big)

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda^{\ast}_{3}}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h^{2}_{E}\|\nabla(I-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1})q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda^{\ast}_{3}}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h^{2}_{E}\|\nabla(I-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1})q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}\quad(\text{using definition of $\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1}$})

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda^{\ast}_{3}}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}C^{2}_{inv}\|(I-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1})q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}.\qquad(\text{using Lemma \ref{inverse}})

\displaystyle\leq{\frac{\lambda^{\ast}_{3}C_{inv}^{2}}{\lambda_{4\ast}}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}S^{E}_{0,k-1}\big(q_{h},q_{h}\big).}

(6.4)

We now use the triangle inequality, the definition of
L^{2}
-projector and Lemma
4.2
:

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h^{2}_{E}\|\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\nabla q_{h}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h^{2}_{E}\big(\|\nabla q_{h}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\nabla q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}+\|\nabla q_{h}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h^{2}_{E}\big(\|\nabla q_{h}-\nabla{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}+\|\nabla q_{h}-\nabla{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}C^{2}_{inv}\big(\|q_{h}-\Pi^{0,E}_{k}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}+\|q_{h}-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 6\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}C^{2}_{inv}\big(\|\Pi^{0,E}_{k}q_{h}-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}+\|q_{h}-\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1}q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,E}\big).

(6.5)

Combining (
6.4
) and (
6.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\mathcal{L}_{2,h}(q_{h},q_{h})\leq\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{2,h}(q_{h},q_{h}),

(6.6)

where the constant
\alpha_{1}
depends on
C_{inv}
,
\lambda^{\ast}_{3}
and
\lambda_{4\ast}
. Thus, the proof of Lemma
3.1
completes from (
6.3
) and (
6.6
).

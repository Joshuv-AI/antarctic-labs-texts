Overdamped limits for Langevin dynamics with position-dependent coefficients via L 2 L^{2} -hypocoercivity
Noé Blassel

Abstract
This note provides a simple derivation of the overdamped approximation for kinetic (or underdamped) equilibrium Langevin dynamics, in cases where certain coefficients depend on the position variable. The equivalent small-mass limit of these dynamics, known as the Kramers–Smoluchowski approximation, in the case of a state-dependent friction coefficient, has been previously studied by a variety of approaches. Our new approach uses hypocoercivity estimates, which may be of interest in their own right, and lead to a very direct derivation, providing in particular a clear explanation of the “noise-induced drift” term in the overdamped equation in the case of a state-dependent friction term. Using the same approach, we also treat several effective kinetic dynamical models derived from a coarse-graining approximation of a high-dimensional system, as well as a class of kinetic dynamics with position-dependent mass matrices. All of these models are relevant to applications in computational chemistry. We finally identify a mistake in [ 30 ] and suggest a solution.



1
Introduction

We study the overdamped limits of several kinetic Langevin equations, a typical example of which is given by the solution to the stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}q_{t}^{\lambda}&=\nabla U(p_{t}^{\lambda})\mathrm{d}t,\\
\mathrm{d}p_{t}^{\lambda}&=-\nabla V(q_{t}^{\lambda})\mathrm{d}t-\lambda D(q_{t}^{\lambda})^{-1}\nabla U(p_{t}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{\beta}}D(q_{t}^{\lambda})^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\lambda},\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
D:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\scalebox{0.6}{++}}
is a symmetric positive-definite matrix field called the inverse friction profile, and
W^{\lambda}
is a standard Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Note that instead of
D^{-1/2}
in (
1
), we could take any matrix-field
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that
AA^{\top}=D^{-1}
, with sufficient regularity (see Assumption
1
below),
but since any two square roots of
D^{-1}
are conjugated via an orthogonal transformation, we may assume that
A=D^{-1/2}
is the positive square root of
D^{-1}
, up to a change of Brownian motion.

In (
1
), the functions
U
and
V
correspond respectively to the kinetic and potential energies, which sum to the Hamiltonian

H(q,p)=V(q)+U(p).

(2)

The position variable
q
evolves in
\mathcal{X}
, where
\mathcal{X}
is either the
d
-dimensional torus
L\mathbb{T}^{d}
for some
L>0
, or
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, while the momentum variable
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is in all cases unbounded. The typical choice of kinetic energy is given by the physical definition

U(p)=\frac{1}{2}p^{\top}M^{-1}p,

(3)

where
M
is the mass matrix of the system, although other choices have been considered for applications to sampling high-dimensional measures, see for instance
[
25
]
.

We consider the large friction regime
\lambda\to+\infty
, and for the sake of consistency, we assume that
W^{\lambda}
is given by a diffusive rescaling of some reference Brownian motion
W
, i.e.
W_{t}^{\lambda}=\sqrt{\lambda}W_{t/\lambda}
for all
\lambda,t>0
.
To this effect, we introduce the following family of rescaled-in-time,
W
-adapted processes:

X_{t}^{\lambda}:=q_{\lambda t}^{\lambda},\qquad\forall\,\lambda>0,\,t\geq 0.

(4)

Our first main result, stated in Theorem
1
below, states that trajectories of
X^{\lambda}
converge in the limit
\lambda\to+\infty
to solutions of the SDE

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=-\left[D(X_{t})\nabla V(X_{t})-\frac{1}{\beta}\,\mathrm{div}D(X_{t})\right]\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}D^{1/2}(X_{t})\mathrm{d}W_{t},

(5)

with initial data
X_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
, and where
\mathrm{div}
denotes the row-wise divergence operator, which is well-defined under our regularity assumptions (see Assumption
1
). The dynamics (
5
) is the so-called Smoluchowski–Kramers dynamics. When
D=\mathrm{Id}
is the identity matrix, the dynamics (
5
) is also known as the overdamped Langevin equation.
The term
\frac{1}{\beta}\,\mathrm{div}D
, which is known as a “spurious“ or “noise-induced” drift term in the experimental physics literature (see for example
[
29
,
5
]
and references therein).

In the case where the matrix
D
is a constant, the convergence of the dynamics (
1
) to the dynamics (
5
) is well known since the work of Smoluchowski
[
24
]
and Kramers
[
17
]
. Moreover, the large friction regime can be directly mapped to the small mass regime, via appropriate nondimensionalisations of the dynamics, see for instance
[
20
, Section 2.2.4]
.
The approximation of the dynamics (
1
) by the dynamics (
5
) in the small-mass regime is known as the Smoluchowski–Kramers approximation.
This approximation is much harder to obtain in the case of a position-dependent friction, which entails the noise in (
1
) and (
5
) is multiplicative, but has nevertheless been derived rigorously using a variety of methods (see
[
12
,
11
,
14
,
30
]
and references therein), including stochastic averaging and homogenization of PDEs approaches. The Kramers–Smoluchowski approximation has also been extended to more general settings, such as stochastic PDEs
[
8
]
, kinetic diffusions on manifolds
[
4
]
and non-linear SDEs
[
22
]
.

The purpose of this note is to provide a proof of the overdamped approximation (i.e. the limit
\lambda\to+\infty
) by different means than those previously used to study the small-mass limit, and for more general dynamics than (
1
). We leverage analytical estimates from the theory of hypocoercivity
[
28
]
, and more specifically the
L^{2}
-hypocoercivity approach developed in
[
9
,
10
,
2
]
.
Because of the structural assumption of
L^{2}
-hypocoercivity, our result is for the moment limited to equilibrium systems, in which the force in (
1
) is a gradient and the fluctuation-dissipation relation holds between the kinetic damping term and the diffusion coefficient.
On the other hand, the result is quantitative in
\lambda
, and valid for more general kinetic energies
U
than the physical kinetic energy (
3
) (at least at the level of time-marginals, see Theorem
1
below for a precise statement). Besides, the proof is direct, and highlights the origin of the “noise-induced drift” in a straightforward way. We also identify a gap in the proof of
[
30
, Lemma 3.1]
, and supply a correct argument for a similar step in our setting.
The approach also generalizes to other underdamped dynamics, both relevant to molecular dynamics (MD) simulations: one is obtained by considering a low-dimensional effective kinetic model of some high-dimensional stochastic process, and the other is a case where both the mass and the friction are matrix-valued position-dependent functions.

The note is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary notation and hypotheses, and state our first main result, Theorem
1
, which we prove in Section
3
.
In Section
4
, we state and prove our second main result, and discuss its implications for molecular modelling. We next apply our method in Section
5
to cases with state-dependent mass matrices.
We finally gather in Appendix
A
the proofs of some key technical results, including the hypocoercive estimates of Lemmas
1
and
2
, which may be of independent interest.

Notation and first main result

In this section, we introduce the necessary notation, state our hypotheses and our main result, Theorem
1
below.

Notation.

Throughout this work, we abuse notation and denote probability measures with the same symbol as their densities whenever they are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.
With this convention, the equilibrium (or Gibbs) measure
\mu
is defined (under Assumption
4
below) by the probability measure

\mu(\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p)=\mu(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p=\exp\left(-\beta H(q,p)\right)/Z_{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p,\qquad Z_{\mu}=\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta H}.

(6)

The measure (
6
) is a product
\mu(\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p)=\kappa(\mathrm{d}p)\otimes\nu(\mathrm{d}q)
, with kinetic and configurational marginals

\kappa(p)\,\mathrm{d}p=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta U(p)}}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta U}}\,\mathrm{d}p,\qquad\nu(q)\,\mathrm{d}q=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta V(q)}}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta V}}\,\mathrm{d}q,

(7)

For any
f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)
, we define the projector

\Pi_{0}f(q)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(q,p)\,\kappa(\mathrm{d}p)

(8)

which sends
f(q,p)
to its partial average with respect to
\kappa
. We will make frequent use of the weighted
L^{2}
-space

L^{2}(\mu)=\left\{f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\,:\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu<+\infty\right\},

(9)

and the closed subspace of
\mu
-centered observables

L^{2}_{0}(\mu)=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mu):\,\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0\right\}.

(10)

Note that
\Pi_{0}
is a
L^{2}(\mu)
-orthogonal projector, and that
{(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{0})L^{2}(\mu)\subset L^{2}_{0}(\mu)}
.
We define weighted Sobolev spaces, for
k\geq 1
by

H^{k}(\mu)=\left\{\varphi\in L^{2}(\mu):\,\forall\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\,|\alpha|\leq k,\,\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\in L^{2}(\mu)\right\},

(11)

with the usual multi-index notation for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
i.e.
|\alpha|=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}
, and
\partial^{\alpha}f=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x_{1}}\dots\partial_{x_{m}}^{\alpha_{m}}f
for
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Other weighted
L^{p}
-spaces
L^{p}(\mu)
,
L^{p}(\nu)
,
L^{p}(\kappa)
and the associated Sobolev spaces are defined in a similar fashion.

The generator of the dynamics (
1
) acts on smooth functions as the differential operator

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{A}+\lambda\mathcal{S},

(12)

where
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{S}
are Hamiltonian transport and fluctuation-dissipation operators, defined respectively by

\mathcal{A}=\nabla U(p)^{\top}\nabla_{q}-\nabla V(q)^{\top}\nabla_{p},\qquad\mathcal{S}=-(D^{-1}(q)\nabla U(p))^{\top}\nabla_{p}+\frac{1}{\beta}D^{-1}(q):\nabla_{p}^{2}.

(13)

The
L^{2}(\mu)
-adjoints of partial derivatives are found by integration by parts to be

\partial_{q_{i}}^{*}=-\partial_{q_{i}}+\beta\partial_{i}V(q),\qquad\partial_{p_{i}}^{*}=-\partial_{p_{i}}+\beta\partial_{i}U(p),\qquad\,\forall\,1\leq i\leq d,

(14)

from which we find

\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{\beta}\left[\nabla_{p}^{*}\nabla_{q}-\nabla_{q}^{*}\nabla_{p}\right],\qquad\mathcal{S}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\nabla_{p}^{*}D^{-1}(q)\nabla_{p},

(15)

so that
\lambda\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
correspond respectively to the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
on
L^{2}(\mu)
.

The generator of the dynamics (
5
), on the other hand, is given by

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KS}}=-\nabla V^{\top}D\nabla+\frac{1}{\beta}\,\left(\mathrm{div}\,D\right)^{\top}\nabla+\frac{1}{\beta}D:\nabla^{2}=\frac{1}{\beta}\mathrm{e}^{\beta V}\,\mathrm{div}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\beta V}D\nabla\right),

(16)

which is a symmetric operator on
L^{2}(\nu)
, a property reflecting the reversibility of the dynamics (
5
) with respect to
\nu
.

On the space
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
of
d\times d
matrices, we denote by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
the Hilbert–Schmidt (or Frobenius norm), and by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
the Euclidean operator norm, as well as by
L^{p}_{\mathrm{HS}}
and
L^{p}_{\mathrm{op}}
the associated
L^{p}
-spaces of matrix-valued functions for
p\in[1,\infty]
.

The processes
X,X^{\lambda}
defined respectively in (
5
) and (
4
) are defined on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}})
,
and under the probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}
,
W
is a Brownian motion independent from the initial conditions

(q_{0}^{\lambda},p_{0}^{\lambda})=(X_{0},p_{0})\,\qquad\forall\,\lambda>0,

(17)

which in turn are distributed according to some probability measure
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As we will consider various
\mu_{0}
, this choice is emphasized in the notation
\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}
, and in the notation
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}
for the corresponding expectation functional.

Overdamped approximation.

Our main result gives two convergence properties for the time-rescaled position process
X^{\lambda}
, in the limit of a large friction intensity
\lambda\to+\infty
.

Recall that
{(X_{0}=q_{0}^{\lambda},p_{0}^{\lambda})\sim\mu_{0}}
for any
\lambda>0
.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
\mu_{0}\ll\mu
is such that

(X_{0},p_{0})\sim\mu_{0},\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\in L^{p}(\mu)\qquad\text{for some }p\in(1,\infty].

(27)

Suppose that Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
and
4
are satisfied, and let
q\in[1,\infty)
be the Hölder-conjugate of
p
, namely
q=p/(p-1)
.

Then for any
T>0
and
0<\alpha\leq\frac{2}{q}
, there exists
C(T,\alpha,\mu_{0})>0
such that

\underset{0\leq t\leq T}{\sup}\,\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|X_{t}^{\lambda}-X_{t}|^{\alpha}\right]\leq\frac{C(T,\alpha,\mu_{0})}{\lambda^{\alpha/2}}.

(28)

The condition (
27
) on the initial data corresponds to a “warm-start” assumption, which could be relaxed to allow for Dirac initial conditions, at the cost of a dedicated analysis for small times, which will not be our concern here.
The convergence in mean estimate (
28
) trivially implies the following bound in Wasserstein
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
-distance between the time-marginals of
X^{\lambda}
and those of
X
for any
{\alpha\in(0,2/q]}
:

\underset{0\leq t\leq T}{\sup}\,\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}\left(\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}}(X_{t}^{\lambda}),\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}}(X_{t})\right)\leq\frac{C^{\prime}(T,\alpha,\mu_{0})}{\sqrt{\lambda}}

(29)

for some
C^{\prime}(T,\alpha,\mu_{0})>0
independent of
\lambda
.

The second main result upgrades the approximation to a pathwise convergence property.

Corollary 1
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
1
hold, and additionally that Assumption
5
is satisfied. Then the following convergence of path distributions holds:

X^{\lambda}_{*}\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}\xrightarrow[\lambda\to+\infty]{\rm{weakly}}X_{*}\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{X})).

(30)

We finally give a straightforward corollary of Theorem
1
, concerning the convergence of trajectory averages on finite time-invervals. Such averages are commonplace in sampling methods using the dynamics (
1
) or (
5
) to estimate expectations under Gibbs measure
\nu
.

Corollary 2
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
1
hold with
p\geq 2
, and let
\eta\in\left[\frac{p}{2(p-1)},1\right]
,
r\in\left[1,\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\eta\right]
.

Let
T>0
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\eta}(\mathcal{X})
, then it holds

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[\left|\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(X_{s}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{r}\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\lambda^{-r/(2\eta)}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\lambda^{-1/2}\right)

(31)

in the limit
\lambda\to+\infty
.

Theorem
1
, as well as Corollaries
1
and

will be proven in Section

.

The key technical tool in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 1

(Uniform hypocoercivity in
L^{2}(\mu)
)
.

Let Assumptions
3
to

be satisfied.

•

For any
\lambda>0
, the generator

is invertible on

. Namely, for any

Proofs

We now prove our main results. To prove Theorem
1
, we will establish the result for
\mu_{0}=\mu
and
p=2
, and conclude for more general initial distributions
\mu_{0}
by a simple reweighting argument. This will in particular establish the weak convergence of all finite-dimensional time marginals of
X^{\lambda}
towards those of
X
.
To prove the second point, we exploit the specific form of the dynamics under Assumption (
5
) to obtain the tightness of the family of
\left(X^{\lambda}_{*}\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}\right)_{\lambda\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{X}))
, which will conclude the argument by a classical corollary of Prokhorov’s theorem (see
[
3

).

The first step is a simple computation with Itô’s formula, from which the “noise-induced drift” term appears naturally.

Proof of Theorem
1
: a first Itô computation.

For any
\lambda>0
, since the quadratic covariation
\left\langle q^{\lambda}\right\rangle
in the position variable vanishes, it holds

\mathrm{d}\left[D(q_{t}^{\lambda})p_{t}^{\lambda}\right]=-D(q_{t}^{\lambda})\nabla V(q_{t}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}t-\lambda\,\mathrm{d}q_{t}^{\lambda}+\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{\beta}}D^{1/2}(q_{t}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\lambda}+D^{\prime}(q_{t}^{\lambda})\left[\nabla U(p_{t}^{\lambda})\right]p_{t}^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}t,

(34)

where we recall the definition (
21
) of
D^{\prime}
. In particular, for any
{1\leq k\leq d}
,

\left(D^{\prime}(q)\left[\nabla U(p)\right]p\right)_{k}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}D_{kj}(q)\partial_{i}U(p)p_{j}.

(35)

Isolating
\mathrm{d}q_{t}^{\lambda}
and integrating over
[0,\lambda t]
, we get:

\displaystyle q_{\lambda t}^{\lambda}-q_{0}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}D(q_{s}^{\lambda})\nabla V(q_{s}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}s+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda\beta}}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}D^{1/2}(q_{s}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{\lambda}

(36)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}D^{\prime}(q_{s}^{\lambda})\left[\nabla U(p_{s}^{\lambda})\right]p_{s}^{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{D(q_{0}^{\lambda})p_{0}^{\lambda}-D(q_{\lambda t}^{\lambda})p_{\lambda t}^{\lambda}}{\lambda}.

Defining
X^{\lambda}_{t}=q_{\lambda t}
, this time-rescaling changes (
36
) into

X_{t}^{\lambda}=X_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\left[D(X_{s}^{\lambda})\nabla V(X_{s}^{\lambda})-\frac{1}{\beta}\,\mathrm{div}D(X_{s}^{\lambda})\right]\,\mathrm{d}s+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}\int_{0}^{t}D^{1/2}(X_{s}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}+R(t,\lambda),

(37)

where we introduced the remainder term

R(t,\lambda)=\frac{D(q_{0}^{\lambda})p_{0}^{\lambda}-D(q_{\lambda t}^{\lambda})p_{\lambda t}^{\lambda}}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\psi(q_{s}^{\lambda},p_{s}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\psi(q,p)=D^{\prime}(q)\left[\nabla U(p)\right]p-\frac{1}{\beta}\mathrm{div}D(q),

(38)

and use
X_{0}^{\lambda}=q_{0}^{\lambda}=X_{0}
.

Since
p\in L^{2}(\kappa)
,
U\in H^{1}(\kappa)
and
D\in W^{1,\infty}(\kappa)
(from Assumptions
3
and
4
), an integration by parts shows that component-wise:

\Pi_{0}\left(D^{\prime}(q)\left[\nabla U(p)\right]p\right)=\frac{1}{\beta}\mathrm{div}D(q),

(39)

so that
\psi\in\left((\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{0})L^{2}(\mu)\right)^{d}\subset L^{2}_{0}(\mu)^{d}
. Indeed, from (
21
) and the form (
7
) for
\kappa
, for any
{1\leq k\leq d}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(D^{\prime}(q)\left[\nabla U(p)\right]p\right)_{k}\,\kappa(\mathrm{d}p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{R^{d}}\partial_{i}D_{kj}(q)(\partial_{i}U(p))p_{j}\,\kappa(\mathrm{d}p)

(40)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}D_{kj}(q)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{i}p_{j})\,\kappa(\mathrm{d}p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\delta_{ij}\partial_{i}D_{kj}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\mathrm{div}\,D(q)\right)_{k}.

In view of the form (
5
) of the limiting equation, we interpret
R(\cdot,\lambda)
as a perturbation term.

Using the first item in Lemma
1
, we introduce, for each
\lambda\geq 1
, the solution
\Phi_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(\mu)^{d}
to the (component-wise) Poisson equation:

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}=\psi.

(41)

Since
\psi
is smooth and
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is hypoelliptic under Assumption (
2
),
\Phi_{\lambda}
is smooth, and we may write the integral in (
38
) as

\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\psi(q_{s}^{\lambda},p_{s}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\Phi_{\lambda}(X_{t}^{\lambda},p_{\lambda t}^{\lambda})-\Phi_{\lambda}(X_{0},p_{0}^{\lambda})}{\lambda}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{p}\Phi_{\lambda}(X_{s}^{\lambda},p_{\lambda s}^{\lambda})^{\top}D(X_{s}^{\lambda})^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},

(42)

using Itô’s formula and making the same rescaling in time as the one leading to (
37
).
Using these estimates, the proof of the first item (
28
) in Theorem
1
follows from a Grönwall-type argument, which we now detail.

Proof of Theorem: proof of (
28
).

Fix
T>0
and assume as a first step that
\mu_{0}=\mu
. By Hölder’s inequality, it suffices to show the estimate (
28
) for
\alpha=2
. By stationarity,
X_{t},X_{t}^{\lambda}\sim\nu
and
p_{\lambda t}^{\lambda}\sim\kappa
for all
0\leq t\leq T
under
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
.
Note also that, since
\nabla_{p}\Phi_{\lambda}
is in
L^{2}(\mu)
component-wise from the second item in Lemma
1
, the local martingale on the right-hand side of (
42
) is an
L^{2}(\mathbb{P}_{\mu})

W
-martingale.

We denote the difference process by

E_{t}^{\lambda}:=X_{t}^{\lambda}-X_{t},

(43)

which has finite second-moments by the first item in Assumption
4
under
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
, and write, for
{0\leq t\leq T}
,

|E_{t}^{\lambda}|^{2}\leq 3\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left[b(X_{s}^{\lambda})-b(X_{s})\right]\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left[\sigma(X_{s}^{\lambda})-\sigma(X_{s})\right]\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}\right|^{2}+\left|R(t,\lambda)\right|^{2}\right],

(44)

where
b(x)=-D(x)\nabla V(x)+\frac{1}{\beta}\mathrm{div}\,D(x)
and
\sigma(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}D(x)^{1/2}
. We estimate each of these terms separately. Let
L_{b},L_{\sigma}>0
be Lipschitz constants for
b
and
\sigma
(in the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
for
\sigma
), which exist by Assumptions
1
and
3
.
Then the first two terms inside the bracket on the right side of (
44
) are bounded in expectation by

(tL_{b}^{2}+L_{\sigma}^{2})\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[|E_{s}^{\lambda}|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s,

(45)

using Itô’s isometry to bound the second term. To control the remainder, we write, using (
38
) and (
42
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[\left|R(t,\lambda)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 5\left(\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}\left[\|Dp\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\|\Phi_{\lambda}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}\right]+\frac{2}{\beta}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{p}\Phi_{\lambda}(X_{s}^{\lambda},p_{\lambda s}^{\lambda})^{\top}D(X_{s}^{\lambda})^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}\right|^{2}\right]\right)

(46)

\displaystyle\leq 5\left(\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}}\left[\|Dp\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\|\Phi_{\lambda}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}\right]+\frac{2T}{\beta}\|D^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\scalebox{0.6}{++}})}\|\nabla_{p}\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu),\mathrm{HS}}\right),

using once again Itô’s isometry in the second line. We may now use the two estimates of Lemma
1
to obtain

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[\left|R(\lambda,t)\right|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C_{T}}{\lambda}

(47)

for some constant
C_{T}>0
independent of
\lambda
. Collecting the estimates (
44
), (
45
) and (
47
), we get, taking expectations,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[|E_{t}^{\lambda}|^{2}\right]\leq 3\left(TL_{b}^{2}+L_{\sigma}^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[|E_{s}^{\lambda}|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{3C_{T}}{\lambda},

(48)

from which Grönwall’s lemma gives

\underset{0\leq t\leq T}{\sup}\,\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[|E_{t}^{\lambda}|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{3C_{T}}{\lambda}\mathrm{e}^{3T(TL_{b}^{2}+L_{\sigma}^{2})},

(49)

which concludes the proof of (
28
) for
\mu_{0}=\mu
and
\alpha=2
.

To extend the argument to
\mu_{0}
, we simply reweight the initial condition. Let
0<\alpha\leq 2/q
. We write, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|E_{t}^{\lambda}|^{\alpha}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[|E_{t}^{\lambda}|^{\alpha}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}(X_{0},p_{0})\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[|E_{t}^{\lambda}|^{q\alpha}\right]^{1/q}\left\|\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[|E_{t}^{\lambda}|^{2}\right]^{\alpha/2}\left\|\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mu)},

(50)

from which the proof of (
28
) follows from the case
(\mu_{0},\alpha)=(\mu,2)
.

Proof of Corollary
1
: tightness.

We now show that the pathwise convergence (
30
) holds under the further Assumption
5
.
Since all finite-dimensional marginals of
X^{\lambda}
converge weakly to those of
X
by the estimate (
28
), it is sufficient, in order to conclude, to prove tightness for the family of pushforward measures
(X^{\lambda}_{*}\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}})_{\lambda\geq 1}
on
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{X})
.
We use the following criterion, which is an immediate corollary of
[
3
, Theorem 12.3]
. If the family of initial laws
\left(\left[X_{0}^{\lambda}\right]_{*}\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}\right)_{\lambda\geq 1}
is tight in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
, and there exist real numbers
a,b,C>0
such that

\forall\,\lambda>0,\,0\leq s<t\leq T,\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[\left|X_{s}^{\lambda}-X_{t}^{\lambda}\right|^{a}\right]\leq C(t-s)^{1+b},

(51)

then
(X^{\lambda}_{*}\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}})_{\lambda\geq 1}
is tight in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{X}))
. Since
\left[X_{0}^{\lambda}\right]_{*}\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}=\mu_{0}
for all
\lambda
, it is sufficient to control moments of the form (
51
).

Fix
0\leq s<t\leq T
. The first step is to write the velocity
M^{-1}p_{t}^{\lambda}
in a more explicit form. We recall that under Assumption
5
,
U(p)=\frac{1}{2}p^{\top}M^{-1}p
for some constant
M\in\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\scalebox{0.6}{++}}
. We view, in the spirit of
[
4
,
30
]
, the equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(M^{-1/2}p^{\lambda})_{t}=

\displaystyle-M^{-1/2}\nabla V(q_{t}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}t-\lambda\left(M^{-1/2}D(q_{t}^{\lambda})^{-1}M^{-1/2}\right)M^{-1/2}p_{t}^{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}t

(52)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{\beta}}M^{-1/2}D(q_{t}^{\lambda})^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}

as a time-dependent linear ODE with a source term.
To solve this equation, we introduce the matrix of fundamental solutions to the associated homogeneous problem, namely the
\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\scalebox{0.6}{++}}
-valued process defined pathwise by the ODE:

\mathrm{d}\mathcal{R}^{\lambda}_{t}=-\lambda M^{-1/2}D(q_{t}^{\lambda})^{-1}M^{-1/2}\mathcal{R}^{\lambda}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\mathcal{R}^{\lambda}_{0}=\mathrm{Id}.

(53)

We will use the shorthand
D_{M}
for the matrix field appearing in (
53
), i.e.

D_{M}(q)=M^{-1/2}D(q)^{-1}M^{-1/2}.

(54)

Note that
D_{M}
also satisfies Assumption (
3
), so that the existence, uniqueness and
W
-adaptiveness of
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}
on
[0,\lambda T]
all follow from the general theory of linear ODEs with continuous coefficients, see for instance
[
26
, Section 3.4]
.

Using Duhamel’s principle, we may write

\displaystyle M^{-1/2}p^{\lambda}_{t}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\lambda}M^{-1/2}p_{0}^{\lambda}-\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}(\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\lambda})^{-1}M^{-1/2}\nabla V(q_{s}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}s

(55)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{\beta}}\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}(\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\lambda})^{-1}M^{-1/2}D(q_{s}^{\lambda})^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}W^{\lambda}_{s}.

The validity of this expression may be checked a posteriori by applying Itô’s product rule to
(\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\lambda})^{-1}M^{-1/2}p_{t}^{\lambda}
, and using the expression

\mathrm{d}\left(\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\lambda}\right)^{-1}=\lambda\left(\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\lambda}\right)^{-1}D_{M}(q_{t}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}t,

(56)

which itself is a consequence the well-known inverse matrix derivative identity
{\partial_{t}(A(t)^{-1})=-A(t)^{-1}\partial_{t}A(t)A(t)^{-1}}
.

Left-multiplying
M^{-1/2}p_{t}
by
M^{-1/2}
and integrating over
[\lambda s,\lambda t]
, it follows, according to the SDE (
1
), that the time-rescaled position increments can be written as

\displaystyle X_{t}^{\lambda}-X_{s}^{\lambda}=\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}M^{-1}p_{r}^{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}r=M^{-1/2}\left[\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\left(\mathcal{R}_{r}^{\lambda}M^{-1/2}p_{0}-\mathcal{R}_{r}^{\lambda}\int_{0}^{r}(\mathcal{R}_{u}^{\lambda})^{-1}M^{-1/2}\nabla V(q_{u}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}u\right.\right.

(57)

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{\beta}}\mathcal{R}^{\lambda}_{r}\int_{0}^{r}\left(\mathcal{R}_{u}^{\lambda}\right)^{-1}M^{-1/2}D(q_{u}^{\lambda})^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}W^{\lambda}_{u}\right)\,\mathrm{d}r\right],

which we split into three summands as
M^{-1/2}\left[I_{1}(s,t)+I_{2}(s,t)+I_{3}(s,t)\right]
.

Take
\gamma>1
. Using the bound
|X_{t}^{\lambda}-X_{s}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma}\leq\|M^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{\gamma}3^{\gamma-1}(|I_{1}(s,t)|^{\gamma}+|I_{2}(s,t)|^{\gamma}+|I_{3}(s,t)|^{\gamma})
, it remains to control the three integral terms separately, seeking uniform-in-
\lambda
estimates for their
\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}
-expectation.
We use the inequality:

\forall\,0\leq s\leq t\leq T,\qquad\left\|\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\lambda}\left(\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\lambda}\right)^{-1}\right\|\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\varepsilon_{D,M}(t-s)}\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}\text{-almost surely},

(58)

where
\|\cdot\|
is the standard Euclidean operator norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for some
\varepsilon_{D,M}>0
independent of
\lambda
.
This identity follows from the uniform bound (
3
), upon applying Grönwall’s inequality pathwise, to
|v(t)|^{2}=\left|\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\lambda})^{-1}v_{0}\right|^{2}
, where
v
solves the ODE:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}v(t)=-\lambda D_{M}(q_{t}^{\lambda})v(t),\qquad v(s)=v_{0},

(59)

for any
v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see also
[
26
, Problem 3.31]
).

Applying (
58
) to
I_{1}
, we get the following almost sure inequality:

\left|I_{1}(s,t)\right|^{\gamma}\leq|M^{-1/2}p_{0}|^{\gamma}\left(\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\varepsilon_{D,M}r}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{\gamma},

(60)

which gives, upon taking the expectation with respect to
\mu_{0}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[\left|I_{1}(s,t)\right|^{\gamma}\right]\leq\frac{\mu_{0}(|M^{-1/2}p_{0}|^{\gamma})}{\lambda^{\gamma}\varepsilon_{D,M}^{\gamma}}[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda^{2}\varepsilon_{D,M}s}-\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda^{2}\varepsilon_{D,M}t}]^{\gamma}\leq\frac{\mu(|M^{-1/2}p_{0}|^{\gamma q})^{1/q}}{\lambda^{\gamma}\varepsilon_{D,M}^{\gamma}}C_{p}(\mu_{0}).

(61)

using the reweighting argument of (
50
) in the last inequality, where we henceforth denote
{C_{p}(\mu_{0})=\left\|\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}/\mathrm{d}\mu\right\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}}
. Since
p_{0}
is Gaussian under
\mu
, the
\gamma q
-th moment
\mu(|M^{-1/2}p_{0}|^{\gamma q})
is finite, and the estimate (
61
) controls the contribution of
I_{1}(s,t)
to
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}[|X_{s}^{\lambda}-X_{t}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma}]
uniformly with respect to
\lambda
.

Next, we treat the second term, writing:

\displaystyle|I_{2}(s,t)|^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\int_{0}^{r}\left|\mathcal{R}_{r}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}_{u}^{\lambda})^{-1}M^{-1/2}\nabla V(q_{u}^{\lambda})\right|\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{\gamma}

(62)

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda(t-s))^{\gamma-1}\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\varepsilon_{D,M}(r-u)}|M^{-1/2}\nabla V(q_{u}^{\lambda})|\,\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda(t-s))^{\gamma-1}\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\varepsilon_{D,M}(r-u)}\,\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\gamma-1}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\varepsilon(r-u)}|M^{-1/2}\nabla V(q_{u}^{\lambda})|^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}u\right)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(t-s)^{\gamma-1}}{\varepsilon_{D,M}^{\gamma-1}}\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\varepsilon_{D,M}(r-u)}|M^{-1/2}\nabla V(q_{u}^{\lambda})|^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}r,

where we used Hölder’s inequality twice: once in
r
in the second line for the Lebesgue measure, and once in
u
in the third line for the exponentially-weighted measure
\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\varepsilon_{D,M}(r-u)}\,\mathrm{d}u
.
Taking the expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}
, we get, with the same reweighting argument as in previous estimates:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|I_{2}(s,t)|^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\underset{0\leq u\leq t}{\sup}\,\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}[|M^{-1/2}\nabla V(q_{u}^{\lambda})|^{\gamma}]\frac{(t-s)^{\gamma-1}}{\varepsilon_{D,M}^{\gamma-1}}\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\varepsilon_{D,M}(r-u)}\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}r

(63)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(\mu_{0})\|M^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{\gamma}\mu\left(|\nabla V|^{\gamma q}\right)^{1/q}\frac{(t-s)^{\gamma-1}}{\varepsilon_{D,M}^{\gamma-1}}\frac{\lambda t-\lambda s}{\lambda\varepsilon_{D,M}}

\displaystyle=C_{p}(\mu_{0})\|M^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{\gamma}\mu\left(|\nabla V|^{\gamma q}\right)^{1/q}\frac{(t-s)^{\gamma}}{\varepsilon_{D,M}^{\gamma}}.

We finally treat the last term
I_{3}(s,t)
, which requires some care. In the proof of
[
30
, Lemma 3.1]
, the authors suggest controlling similar double integrals by a (stochastic) Fubini’s theorem followed by Doob’s maximal inequality. This argument, while giving sufficient control formally, seems incorrect, as the resulting time-swapped integrands are not
W
-adapted, nor in general (sub)martingales. A similar mistake can be found in the proof of
[
15
, Section 3.4.2, Lemma 10]
.
To avoid this difficulty, we write instead

I_{3}(s,t)=\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}Z_{r}^{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}r,\qquad Z_{t}^{\lambda}:=\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{\beta}}\mathcal{R}_{t}^{\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\lambda}\right)^{-1}M^{-1/2}D(q_{s}^{\lambda})^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}W^{\lambda}_{s},

(64)

from which we get, using (
53
),

\mathrm{d}Z_{t}^{\lambda}=-\lambda D_{M}(q_{t}^{\lambda})Z_{t}^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{\beta}}M^{-1/2}D(q_{t}^{\lambda})^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\lambda},

(65)

against which we integrate
-\lambda^{-1}D_{M}(q^{\lambda})^{-1}
on
[\lambda s,\lambda t]
to obtain

I_{3}(s,t)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda\beta}}\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\left[D_{M}^{-1}M^{-1/2}D^{-1/2}\right](q_{r}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}W^{\lambda}_{r}-\lambda^{-1}\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}D_{M}(q_{r}^{\lambda})^{-1}\mathrm{d}Z_{r}^{\lambda}.

(66)

To treat the first term, we use the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality to show the existence of
C_{\gamma,\beta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[\left|\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda\beta}}\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\left[D_{M}^{-1}M^{-1/2}D^{-1/2}\right](q_{r}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}^{\lambda}\right|^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma,\beta}\lambda^{-\gamma/2}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[\left(\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\left\|\left[D_{M}^{-1}M^{-1/2}D^{-1/2}\right](q_{r}^{\lambda})\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}}\mathrm{d}r\right)^{\gamma/2}\right]

(67)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma,\beta}\|D_{M}^{-1}M^{-1/2}D^{-1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{HS}}}^{\gamma}(t-s)^{\gamma/2}.

We rewrite the second term in (
66
), using Itô’s product rule, which gives

\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}D_{M}(q_{r}^{\lambda})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}Z_{r}^{\lambda}=D_{M}(q_{\lambda t}^{\lambda})^{-1}Z_{\lambda t}^{\lambda}-D_{M}(q_{\lambda s}^{\lambda})^{-1}Z_{\lambda s}^{\lambda}-\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\left(D_{M}^{-1}\right)^{\prime}(q_{r}^{\lambda})[M^{-1}p_{r}^{\lambda}]Z_{r}^{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}r.

(68)

Controlling these three terms requires some moment bounds on
Z^{\lambda}
. The idea is to apply Itô’s formula to
Z^{\lambda}
with
f(z)=|z|^{\gamma}
. In view of the SDE (
65
) satisfied by
Z^{\lambda}
, it holds for
\gamma>2
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|Z_{r}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle=-\lambda\gamma\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|Z_{r}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma-2}\left(Z_{r}^{\lambda}\right)^{\top}D_{M}(q_{r}^{\lambda})Z_{r}^{\lambda}\right]

(69)

\displaystyle+\lambda\frac{\gamma(\gamma-2)}{\beta}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|Z_{r}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma-4}M^{-1/2}D(q_{r}^{\lambda})^{-1}M^{-1/2}:Z_{r}^{\lambda}\otimes Z_{r}^{\lambda}\right]

\displaystyle+\lambda\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|Z_{r}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma-2}\mathrm{Tr}\,\left(M^{-1/2}D(q_{r}^{\lambda})^{-1}M^{-1/2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda C^{(1)}_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|Z_{r}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma}\right]+\lambda C^{(2)}_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|Z_{r}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma-2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda C^{(3)}_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|Z_{r}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma}\right]+\lambda C^{(4)}_{\gamma},

where the constants
C_{\gamma}^{(i)}>0
,
1\leq i\leq 4
depend on
D,\beta
and
M
, but may be chosen uniformly in
\lambda
. Indeed, by optimizing
\theta^{\gamma-2}-\alpha t^{\gamma}
with respect to
\theta
, we get the inequality

\forall\,\theta\geq 0,\alpha>0,\gamma>2\qquad\theta^{\gamma-2}\leq\alpha\theta^{\gamma}+\alpha^{-\frac{\gamma-2}{2}}\frac{2}{\gamma}\left(\frac{\gamma-2}{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-2}{2}},

(70)

which we use in the last line of (
69
) with
\alpha<C_{\gamma}^{(1)}/C_{\gamma}^{(2)}
to absorb
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|Z_{r}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma-2}\right]
into the dissipative term.

Solving the differential inequality with
Z_{0}^{\lambda}=0
, we get:

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|Z_{r}^{\lambda}|^{\gamma}\right]\leq\lambda C^{(4)}_{\gamma}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-r\lambda C^{(3)}_{\gamma}}}{\lambda C^{(3)}_{\gamma}}\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}^{(4)}}{C_{\gamma}^{(3)}}

(71)

for all
r\geq 0
. To make this argument rigorous, one should really apply Itô’s formula to the stopped process
Z^{\lambda}_{t\land\tau^{\lambda}_{K}}
with
\tau^{\lambda}_{K}=\inf\{t\geq 0:|Z_{t}^{\lambda}|\geq K\}
and
f_{K}
a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{2}
function satisfying
f_{K}(z)\mathbbm{1}_{\{|z|\leq K\}}=|z|^{\gamma}
. One can then obtain
\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[|Z^{\lambda}_{r\land\tau_{K}^{\lambda}}|^{\gamma}\right]\leq C
by the computation (
69
) (for some constant
C
uniform in
K
and
\lambda
), and pass to the limit
K\to+\infty
.

From the bound (
71
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[\left|\lambda^{-1}D_{M}^{-1}(q_{\lambda s}^{\lambda})Z_{\lambda s}^{\lambda}\right|^{\gamma}\right],\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[\left|\lambda^{-1}D_{M}^{-1}(q_{\lambda t}^{\lambda})Z_{\lambda t}^{\lambda}\right|^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-\gamma}\left\|D_{M}^{-1}\right\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{op}}}\frac{C_{\gamma}^{(4)}}{C_{\gamma}^{(3)}},

(72)

and by Hölder’s inequality we finally get

4
Overdamped limit of coarse-grained Langevin dynamics

It is possible, by mimicking the arguments of Section
3
, to the study the case where the underdamped dynamics is given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}z_{t}^{\lambda}&=A(z_{t}^{\lambda})\,v_{t}^{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}t,\\
\mathrm{d}v_{t}^{\lambda}&=\left(-A\nabla V+\frac{1}{\beta}\,\mathrm{div}A\right)(z_{t}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}t-\lambda v_{t}^{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\lambda}\end{aligned}\right.

(76)

where
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\scalebox{0.6}{++}}
is a positive-definite gradient field. Such equations appear naturally when considering effective (or “coarse-grained”) kinetic Langevin dynamics, in the spirit of the works
[
19
,
31
]
for the overdamped case, as explained in the upcoming work
[
18
]
.

The process
z^{\lambda}
can be understood as a dynamical approximations of the position process
q^{\lambda}
of the kinetic Langevin dynamics (
1
) (with
M=D=\mathrm{Id}
) on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
through a collective variable
\xi:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathcal{X}
(typically with
p\gg\dim\,\mathcal{X}
).
In this perspective,
V
is the free energy associated with
\xi
, and
A(z)
is given by the conditional average

A(z)=\int_{\xi^{-1}(z)}G^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{z}

where
\nu_{z}
is the conditional Gibbs measure on the level set
\xi^{-1}(z)
and

is the Gram matrix (we refer to
[
20
, Section 3.2.1]
for further details on the physical meaning of these objects).

The Boltzmann–Gibbs measure (
6
) (with kinetic energy
U(v)=\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}
) is the unique invariant probability measure for the dynamics (
76
), whose generator is given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\nabla_{v}^{*}A\nabla_{z}-\nabla_{z}^{*}A\nabla_{v}-\lambda\nabla_{v}^{*}\nabla_{v}\right).

(77)

The proofs of Theorem
1
and Corollary
1
given above can be straightforwardly adapted to obtain the overdamped limit of the dynamics (
76
) to obtain the following result

Theorem 2
.

Let
Z
be the solution to

\mathrm{d}Z_{t}=-A^{2}(Z_{t})\nabla V(Z_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{\beta}\mathrm{div}\left(A^{2}\right)(Z_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}A(Z_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},

(78)

which is none other than the dynamics (
5
) with diffusion matrix
D=A^{2}
.

Assume that the conditions of Theorem
1
are satisfied, and that
Z_{0}^{\lambda}=Z_{0}
for all
\lambda>0
with

.
Assume furthermore that Assumption

Let

i)

For any

\underset{0\leq t\leq T}{\sup}\,\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{0}}\left[\left|Z_{t}^{\lambda}-Z_{t}\right|^{\alpha}\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\lambda^{-\alpha/2}\right).

(79)

ii)

Pathwise weak convergence holds:

Z_{*}^{\lambda}\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}\xrightarrow[\lambda\to+\infty]{\text{weakly}}Z_{*}\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{0}}\,\qquad\text{in }\mathcal{P}\left(\mathcal{C}\left([0,T];\mathcal{X}\right)\right).

We sketch the proof of this result, omitting some details for the sake of brevity, since the arguments are minor modifications of those contained in the proofs of Theorem

(Sketch of proof).

We first note that since

Applying Itô’s formula to the process

With a direct computation similar to (

Using the following result, which is the counterpart of Lemma

5
Overdamped limit of some Langevin equations with position-dependent mass matrices

In this section, we apply Theorem
1
and Corollary
1
to study a case where the mass itself is position-dependent. In contrast to the dynamics (
1
) in Section
1
, the Hamiltonian is no longer separable into a sum of kinetic and potential terms.
We consider dynamics of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}q_{t}^{\lambda}&=\nabla_{p}H_{M}(q_{t}^{\lambda},p_{t}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}t,\\
\mathrm{d}p_{t}^{\lambda}&=-\nabla_{q}H_{M}(q_{t}^{\lambda},p_{t}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}t-\lambda\Sigma(q_{t}^{\lambda})\nabla_{p}H_{M}(q_{t}^{\lambda},p_{t}^{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{\beta}}\Sigma(q_{t}^{\lambda})^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\lambda},\end{aligned}\right.

(89)

on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
H_{M}
is given by the Hamiltonian

H_{M}(q,p):=\frac{1}{2}p^{\top}M^{-1}(q)p+E(q),\qquad E(q)=V(q)+\frac{1}{2\beta}\log\det M(q),

(90)

with
M,\Sigma:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\scalebox{0.6}{++}}
are smooth, symmetric positive-definite matrix fields. Physically, the variables
(q,p)
correspond respectively to the positions and momenta, and the matrix
M
to a position-dependent “mass” parameter. Under the corresponding Boltzmann–Gibbs measure

\mu_{M}(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p=\frac{1}{Z}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta H_{M}(q,p)}\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p,\qquad Z=(2\pi/\beta)^{d/2}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta V(q)}\,\mathrm{d}q

(91)

the momentum variable
p
is distributed according to the Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}\left(0,\frac{1}{\beta}M(q)\right)
conditionally on the position variable
q
.
The normalization constant in (
91
) is independent of
M
, thanks to the term
\frac{1}{2\beta}\log\det M(q)
appearing in the potential
E
.
Since
H_{M}
is non-separable, the Boltzmann–Gibbs measure can no longer be written as a product measure. However, the marginal in
q
is again the Gibbs measure (
7
), i.e.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu_{M}(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}p=\nu(q).

(92)

For this reason, the dynamics (
89
) can be used to sample canonical configurations.
It is a natural alternative to (
1
) if one views
M
as a preconditioner, adapting the covariance in the momentum variable to anisotropic features of the target distribution.
In the case of a scalar friction
\Sigma=\gamma>0
, these dynamics have been linked to the generalized Hamiltonian Monte Carlo family of sampling algorithm, see
[
21
, Section 3.2]
.
Position-dependent mass matrices also appear naturally when considering internal-coordinate molecular dynamics, see
[
27
]
, in which the state of the system is parametrized by “internal” degrees of freedom, such as nuclear bond lengths and torsion angles. In this setting, the dynamics (
89
) is the natural counterpart to the Cartesian equation (
1
).

A canonical transformation.

Here, we only consider the class of dynamics which can be transformed into one of the form (
1
) through a smooth diffeomorphism

\Gamma(q,p)=\left(x,v\right)=\left(x(q,p),v(q,p)\right).

(93)

We restrict ourselves to transformations
\Gamma
satisfying the following three conditions.

•

To come back to the original coordinates from the overdamped equation in
x
, the transformed position
x\in\mathcal{X}
from (
93
) should depend only on
q
, i.e.

\nabla_{p}x=0.

(94)

•

In order to preserve the Hamiltonian nature of the dynamics in the case
\lambda=0
, the change of variables should be a canonical transformation, meaning that it should satisfy locally the symplecticity condition

\nabla\Gamma^{\top}J\nabla\Gamma=J,\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\\
-\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}&0\end{pmatrix},

(95)

see for example
[
1
, Chapter 8]
.

•

Finally, to apply the pathwise convergence result of Theorem
1
, the Hamiltonian should become separable, with the kinetic energy given by a quadratic form

H_{M}(q,p)=\frac{1}{2}v^{\top}G_{M}v+V_{M}\circ x

(96)

for some constant matrix
G_{M}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\scalebox{0.6}{++}}
and
V_{M}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Writing the symplecticity condition, we find

\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{q}x&0\\
\nabla_{q}v&\nabla_{p}v\end{pmatrix}^{\top}J\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{q}x&0\\
\nabla_{q}v&\nabla_{p}v\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{q}x^{\top}\nabla_{q}v-\nabla_{q}v^{\top}\nabla_{q}x&\nabla_{q}x^{\top}\nabla_{p}v\\
-\nabla_{p}v^{\top}\nabla_{q}x&0\end{pmatrix},

(97)

which imposes (since
\nabla_{q}x
must be invertible for
\Gamma
to be a diffeomorphism) that
{\nabla_{q}x=\left(\nabla_{p}v\right)^{-\top}}
, and that the matrix
\left[\nabla_{q}x^{\top}\nabla_{q}v\right](q,p)
is symmetric.
The first condition and (
94
) impose
{\nabla^{2}_{p}v=0}
, so that
{v(q,p)=A(q)p+b(q)}
is affine in
p
. Substituting this ansatz into (

), equating linear and quadratic parts in

, we find

v(q,p)=A(q)p,\qquad\forall\,q\in\mathcal{X},\qquad A(q)^{-\top}M^{-1}(q)A(q)^{-1}=G_{M},\qquad b(q)=0.

(98)

Then,
\nabla_{q}x=A(q)^{-\top}
, so that the rows of
A(q)^{-\top}
must be gradients of smooth functions on
\mathcal{X}
. Therefore,
x
can be defined by

x(q)=q_{0}+\int_{0}^{1}A^{-\top}(\gamma(t))\gamma^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\forall\,\gamma\in\left\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1];\mathcal{X}):\,f(0)=q_{0},f(1)=q\right\},

(99)

for any choice of base point
q_{0}=x(q_{0})
and path
\gamma
. One can check with some index computation (see Lemma
5
) that this condition in turn implies the local symmetry property

\nabla_{q}x^{\top}\nabla_{q}v=A^{-1}(q)\nabla_{q}v=\nabla_{q}v^{\top}\nabla_{q}x=\nabla_{q}v^{\top}A^{-\top},

(100)

so that the symplecticity condition (
95
) is indeed verified for this choice of
(x,v)
.

Various classes of mass matrices ensure that the factorization (

) holds, with
A^{-\top}
having gradient rows. When
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one can simply choose
M=(\nabla^{2}\Phi)^{2}
for some smooth potential
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

and

, or
M=(\mathrm{Id}+\nabla\theta)(\mathrm{Id}+\nabla\theta)^{\top}

and again

.
More generally, this construction is valid when

for some smooth diffeomorphism

.

Lemma 3

(Well-posedness of (
1
) and (
5
))
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
is satisfied. Then for any
\lambda>0
, there exists globally-defined continuous solutions to the SDEs (
1
). If Assumption
4
holds, the same conclusion holds for the SDE (
5
). These solutions are unique, up to indistinguishability.

Proof.

Let
\lambda>0
. By Assumption
1
, the coefficients of the SDE (
1
) are locally Lipschitz, and strong solutions are therefore defined up to an explosion time. To extend this result to global-in-time solutions, we use a sufficient Lyapunov criterion, see
[
16
, Theorem 3.5]
or
[
23
, Theorem 5.9]
.
Recalling the definition of the Hamiltonian
H(q,p)=V(q)+U(p)
, we compute

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}H=\lambda\mathcal{S}H=-\lambda\nabla U^{\top}D^{-1}\nabla U+\frac{\lambda}{\beta}D^{-1}:\nabla^{2}U\leq\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\|D^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{HS}}}\|\nabla^{2}U\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{HS}}}.

(153)

Since
H
has precompact sublevel sets (by Assumption
1
), there exists
c_{0}>0
such that
H(q,p)\geq-c_{0}
for all
(q,p)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, whereby the Lyapunov function

\mathcal{W}=2+\frac{H}{c_{0}}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[1,+\infty)

(154)

satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\mathcal{W}\leq\frac{\lambda}{\beta c_{0}}\|D^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{HS}}}\|\nabla^{2}U\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{HS}}}\mathcal{W},\qquad|\mathcal{W}(q,p)|\xrightarrow{(q,p)\to\infty}+\infty.

(155)

Applying Khasminskii’s criterion
[
16
, Theorem 3.5]
, we obtain the claim for the underdamped dynamics (
1
).

The case of the limiting dynamics (
5
), which also has locally Lipschitz coefficients, follows from Assumption
4
, since the Poincaré inequality (
23
) for
\nu
implies the existence of a Lyapunov function (see for instance
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
), and in turn the non-explosivity of the dynamics (
5
).
∎

Lemma 4

(Sufficient condition for hypoellipticity)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
3
are satisfied, and furthermore that
\nabla^{2}U
is everywhere non-degenerate. Then Assumption
2
is satisfied.

Proof.

We write

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{A}_{0,\lambda}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathcal{A}_{j,\lambda}^{\dagger}\mathcal{A}_{j,\lambda},\quad\forall\,1\leq j\leq d,\quad\mathcal{A}_{j,\lambda}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}\left(D^{-1/2}(q)\nabla_{p}\right)_{j},

(156)

where “
\dagger
” denotes the
L^{2}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
formal adjoint, and

\mathcal{A}_{0,\lambda}=\mathcal{A}-\lambda\nabla U^{\top}D^{-1}(q)\nabla_{p}.

(157)

We check that the vector fields
(\mathcal{A}_{j,\lambda})_{0\leq j\leq d}
satisfy Hörmander’s bracket condition (see
[
13
]
), namely that their Lie algebra has full rank
2d
everywhere.

Computing, for
1\leq\alpha\leq d
, the commutator

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{A}_{0,\lambda},\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,\lambda}\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}\sum_{i,j,k}\left[(\partial_{i}U)\partial_{q_{i}}-\left(\partial_{i}V\right)\partial_{p_{i}}-\lambda\left(\partial_{i}U\right)D^{-1}_{ij}\partial_{p_{j}},D^{-1/2}_{\alpha k}\partial_{p_{k}}\right]

(158)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}\sum_{i,j,k}\left[(\partial_{i}U)\partial_{q_{i}}-\lambda\left(\partial_{i}U\right)D^{-1}_{ij}\partial_{p_{j}},D^{-1/2}_{\alpha k}\partial_{p_{k}}\right]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,k}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}(\partial^{2}_{ki}U)D^{-1/2}_{\alpha k}\partial_{q_{i}}+\mathfrak{X}_{\alpha,\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}\left(D^{-1/2}\nabla^{2}U\nabla_{q}\right)_{\alpha}+\mathfrak{X}_{\alpha,\lambda}

for some
\mathfrak{X}_{\alpha,\lambda}\in\,\mathrm{Span}(\partial_{p_{i}},1\leq i\leq d)
. Since
D^{-1/2}
has full rank everywhere by Assumption
3
, it follows that
\mathrm{Span}(\partial_{q_{i}},\partial_{p_{i}},1\leq i\leq d)=\mathrm{Span}(\mathcal{A}_{i,\lambda},[\mathcal{A}_{0,\lambda},\mathcal{A}_{i,\lambda}],1\leq i\leq d)
whenever
\nabla^{2}U
has full rank.
∎

Even if
\nabla^{2}U
is degenerate at some points, one may still be able to show the hypoellipticity of the generator by considering higher-order brackets, which leads to sufficient conditions involving higher derivatives of
U
, see the discussion in
[
6
, Appendix A]
.

Lemma 5

(Index computation)
.

Let
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a smooth matrix field such that
A^{-\top}
has gradient rows, and
v(q,p)=A(q)p
. Then the relation (
100
) holds.

Proof.

Since
A^{-1}
has gradient columns, we may write locally
(A^{-1})_{ij}=\partial_{i}\phi_{j}
for some
{(\phi_{j})_{1\leq j\leq d}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})^{d}}
.
We compute, with derivatives taken with respect to the
q
-variable:

\displaystyle(A^{-1}\nabla_{q}v)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(A^{-1})_{i\alpha}\partial_{j}(A_{\alpha\beta})p_{\beta}

(159)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(A^{-1})_{i\alpha}\left[A\partial_{j}(A^{-1})A\right]_{\alpha\beta}p_{\beta}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\gamma,\beta}\partial_{j}(A^{-1})_{i\gamma}A_{\gamma\beta}p_{\beta}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\gamma}\partial^{2}_{ji}\phi_{\gamma}v_{\gamma},

using the matrix identity
\partial_{j}A=-A\partial_{j}(A^{-1})A
. This shows that
A^{-1}\nabla_{q}v
is symmetric, and therefore that (
100
) holds.
∎

Towards Accurate Gravitational Wave Predictions: Gauge-Invariant Nucleation in the Electroweak Phase Transition
Jie Liu, Renhui Qin, Ligong Bian

Abstract
The vacuum decay in the early Universe should be gauge-invariant. In this work, we study the gauge dependence of the vacuum decay occurring through a first-order phase transition and the associated gravitational wave production. We investigate the gauge dependence of the bubble nucleation and phase transition parameters within the framework of the Standard model effective field theory in three dimension. By considering the power-counting and utilizing the Nielsen identity at finite temperature, we show that, depending on the power-counting scheme favored by the new physics scale, the perturbative computation methodology allow we get the gauge-independent nucleation rates and phase transition, this enables more accurate predictions of gravitational wave signatures.

2.1
The model

Let us split the classical Lagrangian density of the electroweak sector of the SM into gauge, Higgs and fermion parts

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{YM}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{H}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{F}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{g.f}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ghost}}\;,

(2.1)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{YM}}=-\frac{1}{4}W^{a}_{\mu\nu}W^{a\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}\;,

(2.2)

with

\displaystyle W^{a}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}A^{a}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A^{a}_{\mu}+gf^{abc}A^{b}_{\mu}A^{c}_{\nu}\,,

(2.3)

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}B_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}B_{\mu}\;,

where
A_{\mu}^{a}(a=1,2,3)
and
B_{\mu}
are the SU(2) and U(1) gauge fields, and

is the antisymmetric tensor.
The fermion part is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{F}}=\bar{Q}_{L}i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}Q_{L}+\bar{t}_{R}i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}t_{R}+(-y_{t}\bar{Q}_{L}(i\sigma^{2})H^{*}t_{R}+h.c.)\;,

(2.4)

The Higgs part is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{H}}=(D_{\mu}H)^{\dagger}(D_{\mu}H)-V(H)\;,

(2.5)

with H being the SM Higgs doublet,
and the covariant derivative is defined as

D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-ig\frac{\sigma^{a}}{2}A_{\mu}^{a}-ig^{\prime}\frac{Y}{2}B_{\mu}\;,

(2.6)

Gauge invariance allows us to perform the shift of the Higgs doublet in a specific direction of the SU(2)
\otimes
U(1) space:

H(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}\chi^{1}(x)+i\chi^{2}(x)\\

(2.8)

where h denotes the Higgs field and

(2.9)

In 4D framework, if H acquires a background field, we define it,

\tilde{\phi}\end{pmatrix}\;.

(2.10)

directly to
\phi

to eliminate the mixing between the gauge field and Goldstone mode.

We work in the

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\xi}\left(\partial_{\mu}A^{a\mu}+ig\xi\left(H^{\dagger}t^{a}\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{0}^{\dagger}t^{a}H\right)\right)^{2}\,

(2.11)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\xi}\left(\partial_{\mu}B^{\mu}+i\frac{g^{\prime}}{2}\xi\left(H^{\dagger}\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{0}^{\dagger}H\right)\right)^{2}\;,

or

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{g.f}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_{\mu}A^{1\mu}+\xi m_{W}\chi^{2})^{2}-\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_{\mu}A^{2\mu}+\xi m_{W}\chi^{1})^{2}\,

(2.12)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_{\mu}A^{3\mu}-\xi m_{W}\chi^{3})^{2}-\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_{\mu}B^{\mu}+\xi m_{B}\chi^{3})^{2}\,,

2.2
Dimensional reduction of SMEFT

We adopt the dimensional reduction(DR) approach in this work, since it can effectively reduce the theoretical uncertainty caused by the renormalization scale
Croon:2020cgk
;
Qin:2024dfp

This approach splits the original theory into three different energy scales

In general, the DR requires two steps to integrate out heavy(fermion and non-zero boson Matsubara modes) and soft (temporal gauge fields with Debye mass) modes, thereby obtaining a theory that contains only ultrasoft modes(spatial gauge and scalar fields).

The proof that the nucleation rate and PT parameters are gauge-independent requires the generalization of the Nielsen identity for the thermal EFT.
We expect that Nielsen identities still hold at finite temperature after suitably taking into account thermal effects, since this ultimately follows from the fact that the partition function respects the BRST symmetry ensuring that Ward identities still hold at finite temperature. The same applies to the Nielsen identity, that can be regarded as a Ward identity with the thermal effective potential. Our approach is based on Ref.

The bubble nucleation action

The Universe first lives in the “symmetric” phase, and as the Universe cools down, the “symmetric” and “broken” phases have the same free energy at the critical temperature.

A comprehensive analysis of the adverse drug events of metastatic and unresectable gastrointestinal stromal tumor treatment options: avapritinib, imatinib, regorafenib, ripretinib, and sunitinib safety insights and implications
Authors: Cameron M Thompson, Michael B Andrews, Douglas G Adler
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTreatments for metastatic, unresectable, or treatment-resistant gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) include avapritinib, imatinib, regorafenib, ripretinib, and sunitinib. The FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) database collects medication-related adverse events (AEs).Design and methodsReports in the FAERS database from each medication’s FDA approval to June 30, 2025 were analyzed. Reports containing other medications or indications outside of GIST treatment were excluded.ResultsAvapritinib was associated with fatigue (n = 826, 10.2%), nausea/vomiting (n = 574, 7.1%), eye/facial swelling (n = 631, 7.8%), diarrhea n = 361, 4.5%), peripheral edema (n = 231, 2.5%). Imatinib was associated with nausea/vomiting (n = 677, 4.4%), fatigue (n = 549, 3.6%), anemia (n = 404, 2.6%), abdominal pain (n = 400, 2.6%), and rash (n = 340, 2.2%), hemorrhage (317 AEs, 2.1%). Regorafenib was associated with fatigue (n = 104, 8.6%), palmar-plantar erythrodyesthesia syndrome (n = 66, 5.4%), diarrhea (n = 60, 4.9%). Ripretinib was associated with fatigue (n = 534, 8.4%), alopecia (n = 355, 5.6%), nausea/vomiting (n = 273, 4.3%). Sunitinib was associated with fatigue (n = 420, 6.1%%), nausea/vomiting (n = 284, 4.2%), diarrhea (n = 262, 3.8 %), hemorrhage (n = 149, 2.2%).ConclusionsThe FAERS database revealed multiorgan AEs associated with five GIST treatment medications. Novel findings include eye/facial and peripheral edema with avapritinib, palmar-plantar erythrodyesthesia syndrome, headache/migraine, and gait abnormalities across all five medications, pancytopenia with imatinib, and hemorrhage with imatinib and sunitinib.Keywords: GIST, adverse events, imatinib, sunitinib, ripretinib, avapritinib

Introduction

Gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) are rare mesenchymal stromal neoplasms with variable clinical presentation and malignant potential [1]. GISTs are the most common mesenchymal tumor of the GI tract and represent 1% of all digestive tract tumors with a reported incidence of 0.4 to 2.0 cases per 100,000 per year [2]. The most common locations for GISTs to arise are gastric (55.6%) followed by small bowel (31.8%), colorectal (6.0%), other/various location (5.5%) and esophagus (0.7%) [3]. The median age of diagnosis ranges from 60 to 65 years old with a slightly higher incidence in males [2,4]. Occurrence of GISTs in children remains very rare with a minority of cases related to syndromes including Carney Triad Syndrome, Carney-Stratakis syndrome, Type 1 Neurofibromatosis, and rare germline autosomal dominant mutations of the KIT or PDGFRA genes [2]. Surgery remains the cornerstone for treatment of localized GISTs and in some cases of metastatic disease, but systemic therapy remains the primary modality of treatment in patients with metastatic and/or unresectable GIST treatment.

Most GIST lesions contain gain of function mutations in the KIT (70-80%) and PDGRFA (10-20%) genes [1,5]. These identifiable mutations established the basis for GIST treatment with oral KIT tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapy. Imatinib was the first FDA approved therapy for locally advanced and metastatic GIST in 2002 [6]. While there was a positive response in individuals with GIST treated with Imatinib, individuals with the PDGFRA D842V mutation are resistant to imatinib treatment [7]. The development of secondary KIT mutations in a large majority of individuals with GIST neoplasms led to the development of sunitinib and regorafenib which targeted both KIT and PDGFRA [8]. KIT reactivation resulting from these secondary KIT mutations led to the FDA approval of ripretinib in 2020 [9]. Simultaneously, studies evaluating individuals with PDGFRA D842V mutant GISTs led to the novel development of avapritinib in early 2020. Subsequently, it became standard clinical practice for individuals to undergo multigene panel analysis to determine the appropriate systemic treatment. Molecular analysis with next generation sequencing has both predictive and prognostic value. These panels may assist in identifying underlying disease syndromes and influence treatment outcomes and treatment adverse events [10].

Due the varying mechanisms of these agents for GIST treatment, interest in the side effect profiles of these medications is of significant interest. The aim of this study, was to examine the post-marketing surveillance reports from the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) database to identify real-world AEs related to the five medications with FDA approval for metastatic and/or unresectable GIST.

Methods

The FAERS database was utilized for this retrospective study, which was created for post-marketing pharmaceutical safety of FDA approved medications in the US [11]. This open access database, comprises of individual reports of suspected medication-related AEs submitted voluntarily by consumers and healthcare providers along with mandatory reporting by drug manufacturers. AEs are coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) to align reporting among this wide range of reporters.

The FAERS database was accessed and queried on August 13, 2025. The database was queried using generic drug names of five medications including Avapritinib, Imatinib, Regorafenib, Ripretinib, and Sunitinib along with alternative formulations if available such as “Imatinib Mesylate,” “Regorafenib Monohydrate,” “Sunitinib Malate” and/or “Sunitinib Maleate.” The indication for use “Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor” was used for all database interrogations. Raw data files were downloaded to Microsoft Excel (Microsoft, Everett, WA). Reports from each medication’s date of FDA approval for GIST treatment through the last quarterly database update on 30 June 2025 were included in the analysis. To reduce potential confounders, reports were excluded if the indication for treatment was not for “Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor.” There was no reliable method for removing potential duplicate reports due to the absence of patient and reporter identifiers in the FAERS database.

Microsoft Excel was utilized to perform data analysis with descriptive statistics of quantitative variables including patient age [median (IQR)] and categorical variables including patient sex, outcomes (died, disabled, hospitalized, life-threatening, non-serious, required intervention, congenital anomaly, or other), and MedDRA coded AEs [N (%)].

Approval from the International Review Board (IRB) was not required as FAERS is a free and publicly accessible database containing only anonymous, deidentified health information. The STROBE checklist was used to write this manuscript [12].

Results

Avapritinib characteristics

Between Avapritinib’s FDA approval for treatment of unresectable or metastatic GIST with PDGFRA exon 18 mutations (including D842V) in January 2020 to 30 June 2025 there were 9469 total AE reports with 3112 reports remaining for analysis after applying exclusion criteria (Figure 1). Within the 3112 reports there were a total of 8201 AEs. The cohort of patients with suspected AEs related to Avapritinib included 54.8% males with median age 66.5 years, 44.1% females with median age of 68 years, and 1.1% of reports with patient sex not specified (Table 1).

Characteristics and outcomes.

Avapritinib AEs and outcomes

Avapritinib was implicated in 125 deaths (4.0%), 6 disabilities (0.2%), 304 hospitalizations (9.8%), 5 life threatening events (0.2%), 2234 non-serious events (71.8%), and 623 (20%) “other” outcomes (Table 1). The organ systems predominantly affected were neuropsychiatric (2467 AEs, 30.4% of total AEs), gastrointestinal (1747 AEs, 21.6%) dermatologic (642 AEs, 7.9%), ophthalmologic (620 AEs, 7.7%), and hematologic 512 AEs, 6.3%).

Neuropsychiatric AEs

The most prevalent reported neuropsychiatric AEs included fatigue (823 AEs, 10.2% of total AEs), memory impairment (236 AEs, 2.9% of total), altered mental status (178 AEs, 2.2%), dizziness (163 AEs, 2.0%), headache/migraine (148 AEs 1.8%), and insomnia (126 AEs, 1.6%).

Gastrointestinal AEs

The most prevalent gastrointestinal AEs included nausea and vomiting (574 AEs 7.1% of total AEs), diarrhea (361 AEs, 4.5% of total AEs), abdominal pain (233 AEs, 2.9%), and anorexia (207 AEs, 2.6%).

Dermatologic AEs

The most prevalent dermatologic AEs included rash/skin changes (222 AEs 2.8% of total AEs), hair color changes (150 AEs, 1.9%), and alopecia (68 AEs, 0.8%).

Ophthalmologic AEs

The most prevalent ophthalmologic AEs were eye/facial swelling (631 AEs, 7.8%), and epiphora (178 AEs, 2.2%).

Hematologic AEs

The most prevalent hematologic AEs were anemia (205 AEs, 2.5%), cytopenias (185 AEs, 2.4%), and hemorrhage (97 AEs, 1.2%).

Cardiovascular AEs

The most prevalent reported cardiovascular related AE included peripheral edema (203 reports, 2.5% of total)

Imatinib characteristics

Between Imatinib’s FDA approval for GIST treatment on 1 February 2002 to 30 June 2025 there were 64,152 total reports with only 7127 reports remaining after exclusion criteria (Figure 1). Within the 7127 reports, there were a total of 15,383 total AEs. The cohort of patients with suspected AEs related to Imatinib included 52.8% males with median age of 64 years, 40% females with median age of 64 years, and 7.2% % of individuals whose sex was not specified (Table 1).

Imatinib AEs and outcomes

Imatinib was implicated in 3242 deaths (45.5% of reports), 1435 hospitalizations (1435 reports, 20.1%), 496 non-serious events (7.0%), 139 life threatening events (2.0%), and 99 disabilities (1.4%), and 3152 (44.2%) “other” outcomes (Table 1). The organ systems predominantly affected were gastrointestinal (2187 AEs, 14.2% of total AEs), neuropsychiatric (1391 AEs, 9.0%), hematologic (1197 AEs, 7.8%), cardiovascular (870 AEs, 5.7%), and pulmonary (802 AEs, 5.2%).

Gastrointestinal AEs

The most prevalent gastrointestinal AEs included nausea and vomiting (677 AEs, 4.4% of total AEs), abdominal pain (400 AEs, 2.6%), and diarrhea (302 AEs, 2.0%). There were 543 reported AEs for hepatic abnormalities (3.5% of total reported AEs)

Hematologic AEs

The most prevalent hematologic AEs included anemia (404 AEs, 2.6% of total AEs), hemorrhage (317 AEs, 2.1%), and other cytopenias (305 AEs, 2.0%).

Neuropsychiatric AEs

The major implicated neuropsychiatric AE was fatigue (549 AEs, 3.6% of total AEs).

Cardiovascular AEs

There two most reported cardiovascular AEs included peripheral edema (290 AEs, 1.9% of total AEs) and heart failure (104 AEs, 0.7%).

Regorafenib characteristics

Between Regorafenib’s FDA approval for imatinib and sunitinib refractory/intolerant GIST treatment on 25 February 2013 to 30 June 2025 there have been 10,394 total AE reports with 396 reports remaining after exclusion criteria (Figure 1). The cohort of patients with suspected AEs related to Regorafenib included 57.1% males with median age of 63 years, 40% females with median age of 64 years, and 6.6% of individuals whose sex was not specified (Table 1).

Regorafenib AEs and outcomes

Regorafenib was implicated in 61 reported deaths (15.4% of total reports), 133 hospitalizations (33.6%), 16 life threatening events (4.0%), 5 disabilities (1.3%), 38 non-serious events (9.6%), and 251 “other” outcomes (63.4%) (Table 1). The organ systems predominantly affected were gastrointestinal (259 AEs, 21.4% of total AEs), neuropsychiatric (222 AEs, 18.3%), dermatologic (212 AEs, 17.5%), and cardiovascular (106 AEs, 7%).

Gastrointestinal AEs

The most prevalent gastrointestinal AEs included diarrhea (60 AEs, 4.9% of total AEs), nausea and vomiting (42 AEs, 3.5%), abdominal pain (40 AEs, 3.3%), and anorexia (33 AEs, 2.7%).

Neuropsychiatric AEs

The most prevalent neuropsychiatric AEs included fatigue (104 AEs, 8.6% of total AEs), gait abnormality (25 AEs, 2.1%), stroke (15 AEs, 1.2%), headache (15 AEs, 1.2%), and neuropathy (12 AEs, 1.0%).

Dermatologic AEs

The most prevalent reported dermatologic AEs included palmar-plantar erythrodyesthesia syndrome (66 AEs, 5.4% of total AEs), rash/shin changes unspecified (56 AEs, 4.6%), and blistering/pemphigus (23 AEs, 1.9%).

Cardiovascular AEs

The most prevalent cardiovascular associated AEs included hypertension (56 AEs, 4.6% of total AEs), and peripheral edema (12 AEs, 1.0%).

Ripretinib characteristics

Between Ripretinib’s FDA approval for advanced GIST refractory to at least 3 prior tyrosine kinase inhibitors on 15 May 2020 to 30 June 2025, there were 4291 total AE reports with 2885 remaining after applying exclusion criteria (Figure 1). The cohort of patients with suspected AEs related to Ripretinib included 56.0% males with median age of 67.0 years, 43.2% females with median age of 68.0 years, and 0.8% of reports with patient sex not specified (Table 1).

Ripretinib AEs and outcomes

Ripretinib was implicated in 327 deaths (11.3% of total AEs), 653 hospitalizations (22.6%), 4 life threatening events (0.1%), 1 disability, 1598 non-serious events (55.4%), and 517 “other” outcomes. The organ systems most commonly affected were gastrointestinal (1146 AEs, 18.1% of total AEs), dermatologic (1139 AEs, 18.0%), neuropsychiatric (1109 AEs, 17.5%), and musculoskeletal system (583 AEs, 9.2%).

Gastrointestinal AEs

The most prevalent gastrointestinal AEs included nausea/vomiting (273 AEs, 4.3% of total AEs), abdominal pain (231 AEs, 3.6%), constipation (166 AEs, 2.6%), diarrhea (146 AEs, 2.3%), and anorexia (116 AEs, 1.8%).

Dermatologic AEs

The most common dermatologic AEs included alopecia (355 AEs, 5.6% of total AEs), rash/erythema/skin changes unspecified (274 AEs, 4.4%), dry skin (131 AEs, 2.1%), pruritis (114 AEs, 1.8%), palmar-plantar erythrodyesthesia syndrome, (97 AEs, 1.5%), and blistering/pemphigus (53 AEs, 0.8%).

Neuropsychiatric AEs

The most prevalent neuropsychiatric AEs included fatigue (534 AEs, 8.4% of total AEs), and headache/migraine (78 AEs, 1.2%).

Musculoskeletal AEs

The most prevalent musculoskeletal related AEs included muscle spasms (153 AEs, 2.4% of total AEs), extremity pain (124 AEs, 2.0%), myalgia (108 AEs 1.7%), and arthralgia (90 AEs, 1.4%).

Sunitinib characteristics

Between Sunitinib’s FDA approval for Imatinib refractory/intolerant GIST treatment on 26 January 2006 to 30 June 2025 there were a total of 40, 296 total AE reports with 2646 reports remaining after exclusion criteria (Figure 1). The cohort of individual with suspected AEs related to Sunitinib included 54.5% males with median age of 62 years, 39.6% females with median age of 63 years, and 5.9% of individuals whose sex was not specified (Table 1).

Sunitinib AEs and outcomes

Sunitinib was implicated in 1134 deaths (42.9% of total AEs), 781 hospitalizations (29.5%), 90 life-threatening events (3.4%), 33 disabilities, (1.2%), 411 non-serious events (15.5%), 5 events requiring intervention (0.2%), 1 congenital abnormality (<0.1%), and 829 “other” outcomes (31.3%). The organ systems most commonly affected were gastrointestinal (1283 AEs, 18.8% of total AEs), neuropsychiatric (972 AEs, 14.2%), hematologic (708 AEs, 10.4%), dermatologic (653 AEs, 9.6%), and cardiovascular (544 AEs, 8.0%).

Gastrointestinal AEs

The most prevalent gastrointestinal AEs included nausea/vomiting (284 AEs, 4.2% of total AEs), diarrhea (262 AEs, 3.8%), abdominal pain (160 AEs, 2.3%), anorexia (160 AEs, 2.3%), and stomatitis (79 AEs, 1.2%).

Neuropsychiatric AEs

The most prevalent neuropsychiatric AEs included fatigue (420 AEs, 6.1% of total AEs), headache/migraine (75 AEs, 1.1%), and taste disorder (69 AEs, 1.0%).

Hematologic AEs

The most common hematologic-related AEs included cytopenias (8.3% of total AEs): thrombocytopenia (151 AEs, 2.2% of total AEs), anemia (150 AEs, 2.2%), leukopenia (80 AEs 1.2%), neutropenia (49 AEs, 0.7%), myelosuppression (46 AEs, 0.7%), pancytopenia (13 AEs, 0.2%), and 7 AEs of bone marrow failure (0.1%). There were 149 reports of hemorrhage consisting of 2.2% of total AEs.

Dermatologic AEs

The most implicated dermatologic-related AEs included rash/erythema/skin changes unspecified (219 AEs, 3.2% of total AEs), palmar-plantar erythrodyesthesia syndrome (122 AEs, 1.8%), mouth ulceration/pain (92 AEs, 1.3%), and blistering pemphigus (60 AEs, 0.9%).

Cardiovascular AEs

The most prevalent cardiovascular AEs included hypertension (204 AEs, 3.0% of total AEs), heart failure (90 AEs, 1.3%), peripheral edema (81 AEs, 1.2%), and thrombosis (33 AEs, 0.5%).

Discussion

This comprehensive evaluation reviews nearly 37,000 AEs reported during post-marketing surveillance of five FDA approved medications for first-line, second-line, third-lines, and salvage therapies for metastatic or unresectable GIST tumors [13]. Ongoing post-marketing surveillance of these medications is paramount as the median age of diagnosis of GIST is 65 years of age with about 20% of diagnoses occurring after the age of 70 [14]. In this medically vulnerable population, these medications have been cumulatively implicated in 4889 deaths and 3306 hospitalizations since FDA approval.

Overall, gastrointestinal-related AEs constituted the largest proportions of AEs for nearly all medications followed closely by neuropsychiatric-related AEs. Avapritinib was the only medication where neuropsychiatric-related AEs were more prevalent than GI-related AEs. Dermatologic, hematologic, and cardiovascular-related AEs were also frequently reported with all medications.

The most commonly reported GI-related AEs for all medications included nausea/vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, constipation, and anorexia. These five reported AE’s accounted for 10-18% of all reported AEs for these medications. Our findings were consistent with prior phase 3, placebo-controlled trials with crossover from imatinib to regorafenib showing nausea, anorexia, constipation, and diarrhea as common adverse events, with diarrhea reported as a grade 3 AE [15]. Of the five medications, Imatinib and Sunitinib were associated with higher rates of hepatic dysfunction. The most common reported liver-associated AEs implicated were abnormal liver function tests and ascites. Liver failure and liver cancer/neoplasm were also reported, which likely represents metastatic disease as the liver is the most common location for GIST metastases [16]. Additionally, primary hepatocellular carcinomas are significantly rare to co-occur with GISTs [17]. All medications evaluated showed potential to have liver related AEs.

Neuropsychiatric adverse events are common and significantly impact quality of life in individuals undergoing treatment for metastatic and/or unresectable GIST treatment in elderly patients with GISTs, particularly males. A phase 1 study of avapritinib showed all‐cause cognitive effects rate (grade 1–2) was 37.0% in all patients and 52.0% in patients ≥65 years [18]. Despite this study, and given the frequent report of neuropsychiatric AEs, it remains unclear whether gender is a predictive factor for neuropsychiatric AEs with GIST medications. Our study shows neuropsychiatric AE reports are common in the elderly population on GIST treatment in general. Consistent with a prior European study of these five drugs, avapritinib had a significant reporting probability of neuropsychiatric AEs including fatigue, taste disorder, hallucinations, altered mental status, dizziness, and memory impairment compared to the other TKIs [5]. Our study uniquely showed that headache/migraine and gait disturbances were frequently reported across all medications.

Dermatologic AEs were also frequently implicated in reports to the FAERS database in post-marketing surveillance. Dermatologic adverse drug reactions have been classically associated with most TKI/PDGFRA mutation medications [19,20,21,22,23]. In a European analysis of GIST medications, regorafenib was associated with higher reports of skin and subcutaneous disorders and ripretinib for alopecia [5,24]. Our analysis shows that ripretinib was more likely to cause alopecia. Rashes and other unspecified skin changes were the most frequently reported dermatologic AEs and were frequently reported with all five of the medications evaluated. Hand-foot syndrome, also known as palmar-plantar erythrodyesthesia syndrome was reported for all five medications with regorafenib, ripretinib, and sunitinib as the most common culprits. A case report showed regorafenib as a cause of delayed onset acneiform eruption after two years of treatment [25]. Rashes and hand-foot syndrome appear to be side effects that are related to all classes of TKI inhibitors likely based on shared KIT inhibition drug mechanism and may represent a class-side effect profile [26].

Hematologic associated AEs related to GIST medications have been commonly reported in metanalyses and in post-marketing surveillance studies [5,21,22,27]. In these studies, anemia, thrombocytopenia, and neutropenia were the most common hematologic toxicities. Avapritinib was previously shown to have higher probability of anemia, while sunitinib was associated with anemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia. Our study confirmed these findings, however it also demonstrated that imatinib treatment was associated with anemia, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia in up to 4% of total AEs reported. Ripretinib was reported to cause anemia in up to 2% of reports. Hemorrhage was associated most commonly with imatinib and sunitinib (2.2% and 2.1% of total AE reports, respectively) although the site, location, and severity were not clearly specified.

Given that the elderly patients are more likely to be recipients of most of GIST medication treatment, cardiovascular AEs are relevant in treatment considerations. Previously, hypertension and peripheral edema appeared to be the most frequent report for regorafenib and imatinib, respectively [5,23]. In our study, regorafenib, ripretinib, and sunitinib were associated with appreciable reports of hypertension in 3-5% of total AE reports. Evaluation for anti-hypertensive treatment with these medications appears warranted. Peripheral edema was most commonly associated with avapritinib and imatinib, however was also reported to a lesser degree in all five medications evaluated.

Due to their overt physical detrimental effects, ophthalmologic AEs deserve some attention. Avapritinib and Imatinib have been frequent culprits of periorbital swelling, epiphora, and vision changes [22,28]. Our study confirms these findings showing there have been 365 and 247 AEs of periorbital edema, respectively since their FDA approval. Epiphora was reported most commonly with avapritinib with 178 AEs. Our study also highlights the risk of facial swelling particularly with Avapritinib and Imatinib, although this AE has been reported with all five medications evaluated. A summary AEs for the five medications evaluated can be seen in Supplementary Table 2.

There were a few limitations in this retrospective study. The structure of the FAERS database limits the ability to account for duplicate reporting of AEs, which may overrepresent the frequency of a reported AE. Additionally, while we are able to evaluate the frequency of reported AEs, the clinical impact and severity of individual reported AEs is difficult to ascertain. Even though deaths may be reported as an AE for a specific medication, the nature/etiology of the death is unable to be obtained via FAERS database query. The FAERS database also does not reliably collect data on the exact formulations of each medication which limits the ability to provide a more nuanced perspective on the real-world variability between formulations.

Conclusion

This pharmacologic surveillance study evaluated the real-world safety of five FDA approved treatments for metastatic/unresectable GIST treatment. Avapritinib, imatinib, regorafenib, ripretinib, and sunitinib all had significant reported concerns regarding their ability to produce adverse events in multiple organ systems. The adverse events that were most commonly reported were of gastrointestinal, neuropsychiatric, dermatologic, hematologic, cardiovascular, and ophthalmologic natures.

Our analysis confirmed prior initial concerns seen in Phase 1 and 3 trials regarding particular system specific AEs of these agents. Most importantly, our study reported new potential safety concerns not previously reported including the risk of peripheral edema with avapritinib, rashes including palmar-plantar erythrodyesthesia syndrome, and headache/migraine. Gait abnormalities were reported among patients treated with all five medications. Additional novel findings included the pancytopenic effects of imatinib treatment and the risk of hemorrhage seen among those treated with imatinib and sunitinib.

This retrospective study does not imply causation. However, providers managing these individuals on treatment should be aware of these potential AEs in order to tailor the choice of medication or medication dose (personalized schedule) to the individual patient. In particular, the elderly population is at the forefront as this group accounts for the vast majority of individuals treated with these medications. Further investigation into safety concerns of these medications and other novel medications with similar mechanistic action are warranted. Comparative analyses of AEs reported for GIST and non-GIST indications for these medications could provide enhanced clarity on whether the disease process or disease treatment at the driving factors for specific AEs. Further prospective studies including adverse event reporting in the setting of multigene assay testing prior to treatment may hopefully reduce toxic adverse events and improve the tolerability of these medications.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the class of all analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and let
\mathcal{A}
denote the subclass of
\mathcal{H}
consisting of functions
f
normalized by
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
.
Let
\mathcal{S}=\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\text{$f$ is univalent in $\mathbb{D}$}\}
.
A domain
\Omega
is said to be starlike with respect to a point
z_{0}\in\Omega
if the line segment that connects
z_{0}
to any other point in
\Omega
is entirely contained within
\Omega
.
Specifically, if
z_{0}=0
, then
\Omega
is simply called starlike. A function

f\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be starlike if
f(\mathbb{D})
is starlike with respect to the origin.
Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}
denote the class of all starlike functions in
\mathbb{D}
. It is well-known that a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is in
\mathcal{S}^{*}
if, and only if,
\text{Re}(zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z))>0
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. A domain
\Omega
is called convex if it is starlike with respect to any point
in
\Omega
. A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be convex if
f(\mathbb{D})
is convex. Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the class of all convex functions in
\mathbb{D}
.
It is well-known that a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is in
\mathcal{C}
if, and only if,
\text{Re}\left(1+zf^{\prime\prime}(z)/f^{\prime}(z)\right)>0
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. For more in-depth information regarding these classes, we refer to
[
10
,
11
,
32
]
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the class of all analytic functions
\omega:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
and
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{\omega\in\mathcal{B}:\omega(0)=0\}
.
Functions in
\mathcal{B}_{0}
are called Schwarz function. According to Schwarz’s lemma, if
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
, then
|\omega(z)|\leq|z|
and
|\omega^{\prime}(0)|\leq 1
.
Strict inequality holds in both estimates unless
\omega(z)=e^{i\theta}z
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. A sharpened form of the Schwarz lemma, known as the Schwarz-Pick lemma,
gives the estimate
|\omega^{\prime}(z)|\leq(1-|\omega(z)|^{2})/(1-|z|^{2})
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{B}
.

An analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
is said to be subordinate to an analytic function
g
in
\mathbb{D}
, written as
f\prec g
, if there exists a function
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that
f(z)=g(\omega(z))
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, if
g
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
,
then
f\prec g
if, and only if,
f(0)=g(0)
and
f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq g(\mathbb{D})
. For basic details and results on subordination classes, we refer
to
[
10
, Chapter 6]
.
Ma and Minda
[
19
]
have introduced more general subclasses of starlike and convex functions as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\varphi(z)\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}(\varphi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\varphi(z)\right\},

where the function
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
, called Ma-Minda function, is analytic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
such that
\varphi(\mathbb{D})
has positive real
part, symmetric with respect to the real axis, starlike with respect to
\varphi(0)=1
and
\varphi^{\prime}(0)>0
. A Ma-Minda function has the Taylor series expansion
of the form
\varphi(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}

(a_{1}>0)
.
We call
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
the Ma-Minda type starlike and Ma-Minda type convex classes associated with
\varphi
, respectively. Evidently,
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)\subset\mathcal{C}
for every such
\varphi
. It is known that
f\in\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
if, and only if,
zf^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
(see
[
19
]
).

It is evident that different choices of the function
\varphi
in the classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
lead to the generation of several significant subclasses of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}
, respectively.
If, we choose
\varphi(z)=(1+z)/(1-z)
, then
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)=\mathcal{S}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)=\mathcal{C}
. For
\varphi(z)=(1+(1-2\alpha)z)/(1-z),~0\leq\alpha<1

\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
of starlike function of
order
\alpha
and
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
of convex function of order
\alpha
. If

[

and Janowski convex functions
\mathcal{C}[A,B]
(see
[

For
\varphi(z)=(1+2/\pi^{2}(\log(1-\sqrt{z})/(1+\sqrt{z}))^{2})
the class
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
(resp.,
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
) is the class
UCV
(resp.
UST

normalized uniformly convex (resp. starlike) functions (see
[

2.
Pre-Schwarzian Norm

An analytic function
f(z)
in a domain
\Omega
is said to be locally univalent if for each
z_{0}\in\Omega
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
z_{0}
such that
f(z)
is univalent in
U
. The Jacobian of a complex-valued function
f(z)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y)
defined by
J_{f}(z)=u_{x}v_{y}-u_{y}v_{x}=|f_{z}(z)|^{2}-|f_{\overline{z}}(z)|^{2}
, provided all the partial derivatives exist. If
f
is analytic on a simply connected domain
\Omega
, the Jacobian takes the form
J_{f}(z)=|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}
.
It is well-known that an analytic function
f
is locally univalent at
z_{0}
if, and only if,
J_{f}(z_{0})\not=0
(see
[
10
, Chapter 1]
).
Let
\mathcal{LU}:=\{f\in\mathcal{H}:f^{\prime}(z)\not=0~~\text{ for all }~~z\in\mathbb{D}\}
. For
f\in\mathcal{LU}
, the pre-Schwarzian derivative is defined by

\displaystyle P_{f}(z):=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)},

and the pre-Schwarzian norm (the hyperbolic sup-norm) is defined by

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\;(1-|z|^{2})\left|P_{f}(z)\right|.

Pre-Schwarzian norm plays an important rule in the geometric function theory and Teichmüller theory. It is well-known that if
f
is a univalent function in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\|P_{f}\|\leq 6
. This equality is attained for the Koebe function or its rotation. One of the most widely used univalence criteria for locally univalent analytic functions is Becker’s univalence criteria
[
7
]
.
It states that if
f\in\mathcal{LU}
and
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\left|zP_{f}(z)\right|\leq 1
, then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
.
Later, the sharpness of the constant
1
is proved by Becker and Pommerenke
[
8
]
. In 1976, Yamashita
[
33
]

proved that
\|P_{f}\|<\infty
is finite if, and only if,
f
is uniformly
locally univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, if
\|P_{f}\|<2
, then
f
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
16
]
).

In the field of geometric function theory, numerous researchers have investigated the pre-Schwarzian norm for various subclasses of analytic and univalent functions.
In 1998, Sugawa
[
31
]
established the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for strongly starlike functions of order
\alpha
(
0<\alpha\leq 1
).
In
1999
, Yamashita
[
34
]
proved that
\|P_{f}\|\leq 6-4\alpha
for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
and
\|P_{f}\|\leq 4(1-\alpha)
for
f\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
,
where
0\leq\alpha<1
and both the estimates are sharp. In
2000
, Okuyama
[
23
]
established the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for
\alpha
-spirallike functions. Kim and Sugawa
[
17
]
established the sharp
estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for Janowski convex functions (see also
[
25
]
). Ponnusamy and Sahoo
[
26
]

obtained the sharp estimates of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions in the class

\mathcal{S}^{*}[\alpha,\beta]:=S^{*}\left(\left((1+(1-2\beta)z)/(1-z)\right)^{\alpha}\right)
, where
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
0\leq\beta<1
. In
2014
, Aghalary and Orouji

[
1
]
obtained the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for
\alpha
-spirallike function of order
\rho\in[0,1)
, where
\alpha\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
. The pre-
Schwarzian norm of certain integral transform of
f
for certain subclass of
f
has been also studied in the literature. For a more in-depth investigation, we refer to

[
15
,
24
,
25
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
2
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we establish sharp estimates of the pre-Schwarzian norms for functions in the Ma-Minda type starlike
classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{con}
,
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{lim}
and
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{cs}
, and in the Ma-Minda type convex classes
\mathcal{C}_{con}
,
\mathcal{C}_{lim}
and
\mathcal{C}_{cs}
.

3.
Main results

In the following result, we establish the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions in the Ma-Minda type starlike class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{con}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{con}
. Then the pre-Schwarzian norm satisfies the following sharp inequality

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|\leq\frac{2(\alpha+6)}{3+\alpha}.

Proof.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{con}
, it follows from the definition of the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{con}
that

\displaystyle\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\frac{3}{3+(\alpha-3)z-\alpha z^{2}}.

Thus, there exists a Schwarz function
\omega(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\frac{3}{3+(\alpha-3)\omega(z)-\alpha(\omega(z))^{2}}.

Taking logarithmic derivative on both sides with respect to
z
, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{f}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{\left(3-\alpha+2\alpha\omega(z)\right)\omega^{\prime}(z)}{3+(\alpha-3)\omega(z)-\alpha(\omega(z))^{2}}+\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{3}{3+(\alpha-3)\omega(z)-\alpha(\omega(z))^{2}}-1\right).

Note that the function

\frac{3}{3+(\alpha-3)z-\alpha z^{2}}=\frac{3}{\alpha+3}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha z+3}+\frac{1}{1-z}\right)=\frac{3}{\alpha+3}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(1+(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{\alpha}{3}\right)^{n+1}\right)z^{n}

(3.1)

has the positive Taylor coefficients about
z=0
for
-3<\alpha\leq 1
.
In view of Schwarz-Pick lemma, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{\left(3-\alpha+2\alpha\omega(z)\right)\omega^{\prime}(z)}{3+(\alpha-3)\omega(z)-\alpha(\omega(z))^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{3}{3+(\alpha-3)\omega(z)-\alpha(\omega(z))^{2}}-1\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})\left(3-\alpha+2\alpha|\omega(z)|\right)|\omega^{\prime}(z)|}{3+(\alpha-3)|\omega(z)|-\alpha|\omega(z)|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})}{|z|}\left(\frac{3}{3+(\alpha-3)|\omega(z)|-\alpha|\omega(z)|^{2}}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(3-\alpha+2\alpha|\omega(z)|\right)\left(1-|\omega(z)|^{2}\right)}{3+(\alpha-3)|\omega(z)|-\alpha|\omega(z)|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})}{|z|}\left(\frac{3}{3+(\alpha-3)|\omega(z)|-\alpha|\omega(z)|^{2}}-1\right).

For
0<t:=|\omega(z)|\leq|z|<1
, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|\leq\frac{\left(3-\alpha+2\alpha t\right)\left(1-t^{2}\right)}{3+(\alpha-3)t-\alpha t^{2}}+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})}{|z|}\left(\frac{3}{3+(\alpha-3)t-\alpha t^{2}}-1\right).

Therefore, the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions
f
in the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{con}
is

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\;(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|\leq\sup_{0<t\leq|z|<1}G_{1}(|z|,t),

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle G_{1}(r,t)=\frac{\left(3-\alpha+2\alpha t\right)\left(1-t^{2}\right)}{3+(\alpha-3)t-\alpha t^{2}}+\frac{(1-r^{2})}{r}\left(\frac{3}{3+(\alpha-3)t-\alpha t^{2}}-1\right)~\text{for}\quad|z|=r.

The objective is to ascertain the supremum of
G_{1}(r,t)
over
\Omega=\{(r,t):0<t\leq r<1\}
.
Differentiating partially
G_{1}(r,t)
with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}G_{1}(r,t)=-\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}+1\right)\left(\frac{3}{3+(\alpha-3)t-\alpha t^{2}}-1\right)<0,

which shows that
G_{1}(r,t)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
r\in[t,1)
. Hence, we have
G_{1}(r,t)\leq G_{1}(t,t)=G_{2}(t)
,
where

\displaystyle G_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}

\displaystyle\frac{\omega^{\prime}(z)(1+\alpha(\omega(z))^{2})}{(1+\alpha\omega(z)-\alpha(\omega(z))^{2})(1-\omega(z))(1+\alpha\omega(z))}+\frac{\omega(z)}{z(1-\omega(z))(1+\alpha\omega(z))}.

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|

are

\displaystyle r_{1}=\frac{-3\alpha-\sqrt{3\alpha^{3}-\alpha^{4}}}{\alpha^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad r_{2}=\frac{-3\alpha+\sqrt{3\alpha^{3}-\alpha^{4}}}{\alpha^{2}}.

It is evident that
3\alpha^{3}-\alpha^{4}\geq 0
for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
.

Sub-case 2.1.
If
-3<\alpha<0
, then the equation
G_{7}^{\prime}(r)=0
has no real root and
G_{7}^{\prime}(1/3)=(10\alpha^{2}-9\alpha+81)/(\alpha+9)^{2}>0
for
-3<\alpha<0
. Hence,
G_{7}^{\prime}(r)>0
for
r\in[0,1)
, which shows that
G_{7}(r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
r
. From (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|\leq\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}G_{7}(r)=\frac{6}{3+\alpha}.

Sub-case 2.2.
Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
. It is evident that
r_{1}<0
. We also claim that
r_{2}<0
. Note that

r_{2}<0
is equivalent to the inequality

\displaystyle-9\alpha^{2}+3\alpha^{3}-\alpha^{4}<0,~\text{which is true for $\alpha\in(0,1]$.}

Therefore, the equation
G_{7}^{\prime}(r)=0
has no positive root in
(0,1)
and
G_{7}^{\prime}(1/3)=(10\alpha^{2}-9\alpha+81)/(\alpha+9)^{2}>0
for
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Hence,
G_{7}^{\prime}(r)>0
for
r\in(0,1)
, which shows that
G_{7}(r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
r
. From (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|\leq\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}G_{7}(r)=\frac{6}{3+\alpha}.

To show that the estimate is sharp, let us consider the function
f_{3}
defined by

\displaystyle f_{3}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{exp}\left(\int_{0}^{u}\frac{3-\alpha+\alpha t}{(1-t)(3+\alpha t)}dt\right)du.

The pre-Schwarzian norm of
f_{3}
is given by

\displaystyle\|P_{f_{3}}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f_{4}}(z)|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{3-\alpha+\alpha z}{(1-z)(3+\alpha z)}\right|.

On the positive real axis, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}(1-r^{2})\frac{3-\alpha+\alpha r}{(1-r)(3+\alpha r)}=\dfrac{6}{3+\alpha}.

This completes the proof.
∎

In the following result, we establish the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions in the Ma-Minda type convex class
\mathcal{C}_{lim}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{lim}
, the pre-Schwarzian norm satisfies the following sharp inequality

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cll}(1-r_{s}^{2})(1-s+sr_{s})&\text{for}\quad s\in(0,1/3],\\[5.69054pt]
1-s&\text{for}\quad s\in[-1/3,0],\end{array}\right.

where
r_{s}
is the unique positive root of the equation
3sr^{2}+2(1-s)r-s=0
in
(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}_{lim}
, it follows from the definition of the class
\mathcal{C}_{lim}
that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec(1+z)(1-sz).

Thus, there exists a function
\omega(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=(1+\omega(z))(1-s\omega(z)),\quad\text{\it i.e.,}\quad\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{1}{z}\left((1-s\omega(z))(1+\omega(z))-1\right).

Note that
|\omega(z)|\leq|z|<1
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}|=(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{1}{z}\left((1-s\omega(z))(1+\omega(z))-1\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})}{|z|}\left((1-s)|\omega(z)|+s|\omega(z)|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})(1-s+s|z|).

Therefore, the pre-Schwarzian norm for the functions
f
in the class
\mathcal{C}_{lim}
is

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|\leq\sup_{0\leq|z|<1}G_{8}(|z|),

where
G_{8}(r)=(1-r^{2})(1-s+sr)
for
|z|=r\in[0,1)
.
The following cases occur.

Case 1.
Let
s=0
. Then,
G_{8}(r)=(1-r^{2})
and hence, we have
\|P_{f}\|\leq 1
.

Case 2.
Let
s\in(0,1/3]
. Differentiate
G_{8}(r)
with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle G_{8}^{\prime}(r)=-3sr^{2}-2(1-s)r+s\quad\text{and}\quad G_{8}^{\prime\prime}(r)=-6sr-2(1-s)<0.

Therefore,
G_{8}^{\prime}(r)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
r
with

G_{8}^{\prime}(0)=s>0
and
\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}G_{8}^{\prime}(r)=-2<0
. This leads us to the conclusion that the equation
G_{8}^{\prime}(r)=0
has the unique root
r_{s}
in
(0,1)
and thus,

G_{8}(r)
attains its maximum value at
r_{s}
. Hence, we have

\|P_{f}\|\leq(1-r_{s}^{2})(1-s+sr_{s}),

where
r_{s}
is the unique positive root of the equation
3sr^{2}+2(1-s)r-s=0
in
(0,1)
.

Case 3.
Let
s\in[-1/3,0)
. Using similar argument as in the Case 2, we have
G_{8}^{\prime\prime}(r)<0
and
G_{8}^{\prime}(r)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
r
.
The roots of the equation
G_{8}^{\prime}(r)=0
are

\displaystyle r_{1}(s)=\frac{s-1-\sqrt{4s^{2}-2s+1}}{3s}\quad\text{and}\quad r_{2}(s)=\frac{s-1+\sqrt{4s^{2}-2s+1}}{3s}.

As
s\in[-1/3,0)
, it is evident that
4s^{2}-2s+1>1
. We claim that
r_{1}(s)>1
and
r_{2}(s)<0
for
s\in[-1/3,0)
. The inequality
r_{1}(s)>1
is equivalent to the inequality

1+2s+\sqrt{4s^{2}-2s+1}>0
, which is true for
s\in[-1/3,0)
. Again the inequality
r_{2}(s)<0
is equivalent to the inequality
3s^{2}>0
, which is true for
s\in[-1/3,0)
. Thus,
the equation
G_{8}^{\prime}(r)=0
has no roots in
(0,1)
. Note that
G_{8}^{\prime}(1/2)=-(4-5s)/4<0
and it follows that
G_{8}^{\prime}(r)<0
for
r\in[0,1)
.
Hence, we have
\|P_{f}\|\leq 1-s
.

From Case 1, Case 2 and Case 3, we obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cll}(1-r_{s}^{2})(1-s+sr_{s})&\text{for}\quad s\in(0,1/3],\\[5.69054pt]
1-s&\text{for}\quad s\in[-1/3,0],\end{array}\right.

where
r_{s}
is the unique positive root of the equation
3sr^{2}+2(1-s)r-s=0
in
(0,1)
.

To show that the estimate is sharp, we consider the function
f_{4}
given by

\displaystyle f_{4}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{exp}\left(\int_{0}^{u}\frac{(1-t)(1+st)-1}{t}dt\right)du.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{f_{4}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f_{4}^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{(1-z)(1+sz)-1}{z}=-(1-s+sz)

The pre-Schwarzian norm of
f_{4}
is given by

\displaystyle\|P_{f_{4}}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f_{4}}(z)|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|(1-s+sz)\right|.

On the positive real axis, we note that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}\left((1-r^{2})(1-s+sr)\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cll}(1-r_{s}^{2})(1-s+sr_{s})&\text{for}\quad s\in(0,1/3],\\[5.69054pt]
1-s&\text{for}\quad s\in[-1/3,0],\end{array}\right.

where
r_{s}
is the unique positive root of the equation
3sr^{2}+2(1-s)r-s=0
in
(0,1)
.
This completes the proof.∎

In the following result, we establish the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions in the Ma-Minda type convex class
\mathcal{C}_{cs}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{cs}
, the pre-Schwarzian norm satisfies the following sharp inequality

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|\leq\frac{2}{1+\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}_{cs}
, it follows from the definition of the class
\mathcal{C}_{cs}
that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec 1+\frac{z}{(1-z)(1+\alpha z)}.

Thus, there exists a function
\omega(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=1+\frac{\omega(z)}{(1-\omega(z))(1+\alpha\omega(z))},\quad\text{\it i.e.,}\quad\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{\omega(z)}{z(1-\omega(z))(1+\alpha\omega(z))}.

Note that
|\omega(z)|\leq|z|<1
and the function

\displaystyle\frac{z}{(1-z)(1+\alpha z)}=\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-(-1)^{n}\alpha^{n}\right)z^{n}

has the positive Taylor coefficients about
z=0
for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1/2
. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{|\omega(z)|}{(1-|\omega(z)|)(1+\alpha|\omega(z)|)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-(-1)^{n}\alpha^{n}\right)|\omega(z)|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-(-1)^{n}\alpha^{n}\right)|z|^{n}=\frac{|z|}{(1-|z|)(1+\alpha|z|)}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}|=(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{\omega(z)}{z(1-\omega(z))(1+\alpha\omega(z))}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left(\frac{|\omega(z)|}{|z|(1-|\omega(z)|)(1+\alpha|\omega(z)|)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})}{(1-|z|)(1+\alpha|z|)}.

Therefore, the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions
f
in the class
\mathcal{C}_{cs}
is

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|\leq\sup_{0\leq|z|<1}G_{9}(|z|),

where
G_{9}(r)=(1+r)/(1+\alpha r)
for
|z|=r\in[0,1)
.
It is evident that
G_{9}^{\prime}(r)=(1-\alpha)/(1+\alpha r)^{2}>0
and hence, we have
\|P_{f}\|\leq 2/(1+\alpha)
.

To show that the estimate is sharp, we consider the function
f_{5}
given by

\displaystyle f_{5}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{exp}\left(\int_{0}^{u}\frac{dt}{(1-t)(1+\alpha t)}\right)du.

The pre-Schwarzian norm of
f_{5}
is given by

\displaystyle\|P_{f_{5}}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f_{5}}(z)|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{1}{(1-z)(1+\alpha z)}\right|.

On the positive real axis, we note that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}(1-r^{2})\frac{1}{(1-r)(1+\alpha r)}=\frac{2}{1+\alpha}.

This completes the proof.∎

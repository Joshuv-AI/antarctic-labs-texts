Effect of peri-adventitial superior mesenteric artery dissection on margin status during pancreaticoduodenectomy for resectable pancreatic cancer: the DISSECT RCT protocol
Authors: Sarah Powell-Brett, Anisa Nutu, Alessandro Parente, Prashant Kadam, Francesco Giovinazzo, James Halle-Smith, David McNulty, Keith Roberts, Nikolaos Chatzizacharias
Published: 2025

Local recurrence affects approximately half of patients after pancreatoduodenectomy (PD) for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)1. Positive margin status (R1) is an independent predictor of disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS)2. A systematic review of an R1 superior mesenteric artery (SMA) margin reported rates of up to 45% and specifically associated it with disease recurrence2–4.

Peri-adventitial dissection of the SMA defines the skeletonization of the artery by dissection and eventually resection of the lymphoneural sheath that covers the adventitia and has been proposed to reduce SMA margin positivity3. Existing literature is mostly retrospective, with variable reporting of margins and a lack of surgical technique standardization.

University Hospitals Birmingham is conducting a single-blinded, RCT, the DISSECT trial, comparing ‘SMA peri-adventitial dissection (SPD)’ and ‘No SMA peri-adventitial dissection (NSPD)’ in patients undergoing PD for PDAC of the head and uncinate process. Participants are randomized to receive SPD or NSPD in a 1:1 ratio (Supplementary material) (with a varying block size of 2, 4 or 6).

The intervention being compared is the extent of dissection on the SMA. All other surgical techniques, including the approach to the SMA, will be at the discretion of the operating surgeon. For patients randomized to SPD, the right side of the SMA should be completely cleared of lymphoneural tissue for at least 180 degrees from the ‘angle’ of the artery to the level of inferior border of the uncinate process (Fig. 1).

Primary outcome is the rate of a R1 resection at the SMA margin (cancer cells present less than 1 mm from the SMA margin)5. Secondary outcomes are OS, DFS, receipt of adjuvant therapy, complications, length of stay and quality of life6.

Quality/standardization of technique and adherence to allocated study arm are assessed using intraoperative photographs taken after specimen removal, which are reviewed in blocks by the trial Principal Investigator in a blinded fashion. Patients are blinded to their allocated arm throughout. Pathologists assessing margin status will be unaware of the allocated arm. Any deviations from allocated arm will be monitored throughout the trial.

A sample size of 59 in each arm, 118 in total, will allow a reduction in SMA R1 from 24% to 5% (based on retrospective unit data) to be detected with >80% power at the 5% significance level, assuming a drop-out rate of up to 10%.

Results will be reported according to the CONSORT guidelines. Standard descriptive statistics and cross-tabulation of variables against the intended treatment with appropriate test statistics for the associations will be reported. The principal analysis for all outcomes is intention to treat, having intended treatment as the single fixed effect with appropriate changes for response variable types. The principal binary outcome, rate of R1 resection at the SMA margin, will be analysed with binomial error distribution and adjusted for overdispersion with a simple scale adjustment if necessary. Defined sensitivity analyses will be performed as detailed in a Statistical Analysis Plan to be agreed before the data are locked.

The first 6 months of this trial was a safety and feasibility phase, which is now complete and has met the criteria to move to full recruitment (recruitment of over 20 patients in 6 months with over 80% to undergo their randomized arm. No increase in potentially technique-related complications).

This study received ethical approval from the Health and Care Research Wales Research Ethics Committee and is registered on clinicaltrials.gov as NCT04902352.

Nonexistence of minimal mass blow-up solution for the 2D cubic Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation
Gong Chen, Yang Lan, Xu Yuan

Abstract
. For the 2D cubic (mass-critical) Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation, ∂ t ϕ + ∂ x 1 ( Δ ​ ϕ + ϕ 3 ) = 0 , ( t , x ) ∈ [ 0 , ∞ ) × ℝ 2 , \partial_{t}\phi+\partial_{x_{1}}(\Delta\phi+\phi^{3})=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}, we prove that there exist no finite/infinite time blow-up solution with minimal mass in the energy space. This nonexistence result is in contrast to the one obtained by Martel-Merle-Raphaël [ 17 ] for the mass-critical generalized Korteweg-de Vries (gKdV) equation. The proof relies on a refined ODE argument related to the modulation theory and a modified energy-virial Lyapunov functional with a monotonicity property.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main result

In this note, we consider the minimal mass dynamics for the following 2D cubic Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation,

\partial_{t}\phi+\partial_{x_{1}}(\Delta\phi+\phi^{3})=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.1)

Recall that, the Cauchy problem is locally well-posed in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(see
[
14
,
25
]
and reference therein). More precisely, for any initial data
\phi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, there exists a unique (in a certain sense) maximal solution
\phi(t)
of (
1.1
) in
C([0,T);H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
and

T<\infty\quad\mbox{implies}\quad\|\nabla\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\to\infty\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ t\uparrow T.

(1.2)

Recall also that,
H^{1}
solutions satisfy the conservation of mass and energy:

M(\phi(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\phi(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\quad\mbox{and}\quad E(\phi(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi(t,x)|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|\phi(t,x)|^{4}\right){\rm d}x.

The symmetry group for
H^{1}
solution of (
1.1
) is given by

\phi(t,x)\mapsto\sigma_{0}\lambda_{0}\phi\left(\lambda_{0}^{3}(t-t_{0}),\lambda_{0}(x_{1}-x_{1,0}),\lambda_{0}(x_{2}-x_{2,0})\right),

where
\left(\sigma_{0},\lambda_{0},t_{0},x_{1,0},x_{2,0}\right)\in\left\{-1,1\right\}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In particular, the scaling symmetry keeps the
L^{2}
-norm invariant and hence the problem is
mass-critical
.

The family of
soliton/traveling wave
solutions

\phi(t,x)=\lambda_{0}Q\left(\lambda_{0}(x_{1}-x_{1,0}-\lambda_{0}^{2}t),\lambda_{0}(x_{2}-x_{2,0})\right),

plays an important role in our analysis of the minimal mass blow-up dynamics. Here, we denote by
Q(x)=q(|x|)
the ground state of (
1.1
) where
q>0
satisfies

-q^{\prime\prime}-\frac{q^{\prime}}{r}+q-q^{3}=0,\quad q^{\prime}(0)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}q(r)=0.

It is well-known and easily checked that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left|q^{(n)}(r)\right|\lesssim r^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-r},\quad\mbox{for}\ r>1.

From the variational argument in
[
27
]
, the ground state
Q
is related to the best constant in the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(x)|^{4}{\rm d}x\leq 2\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla f(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\right)\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|Q(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x}\right),\quad\mbox{for any}\ f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Following the above inequality, the conservation of energy and the blow-up criterion (
1.2
) imply that any initial data
\phi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with subcritical mass,
i.e.
satisfying
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
, generates a
global and bounded
solution in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

The study of singularity formation for small supercritical mass
H^{1}
initial data

\|Q\|_{L^{2}}<\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}}+\delta^{*},\quad 0<\delta^{*}\ll 1,

(1.3)

has been developed in Farah-Holmer-Roudenko-Yang
[
9
]
. More precisely, they show that for any initial data
\phi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
E(\phi_{0})<0
and (
1.3
), the corresponding solution
\phi(t)
blows up in finite/infinite forward time. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result for the study of singularity formation for (
1.1
).

In this note, we focus on the critical mass problem
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
and
show that there exists no minimal mass blow-up solution. In other words, we prove that the subcritical mass condition is not sharp for the global existence of (
1.1
).

To state the main result, we recall the definition of minimal mass blow-up solution.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
\phi(t)
is a minimal mass blow-up solution for (
1.1
) if
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
and there exists
0<T\leq\infty
such that
\phi(t)\in C([0,T);H^{1})
with

\|\nabla\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\to\infty\quad\mbox{as}\quad t\uparrow T.

In addition, we say that
T
is the blow-up time of
\phi(t)
.

We now state the following main result of this note.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists no minimal mass blow-up solution for (
1.1
).

Remark 1.3
.

It is worth mentioning that, in Theorem
1.2
, we do not treat the case of global minimal mass solutions blowing up only along a sequence of time. Technically, we should first obtain the blow-up estimate for all time, and then we could establish the exponential decay on the right-hand side of
y_{1}
(see Section
3.1
) which helps us enter the monotonicity regime to control the geometric parameters. This is similar to the case of the mass-critical gKdV equation (see
[
17
, Page 1872]
).

1.2.
Previous results

Actually, the study of minimal mass blow-up solution for nonlinear dispersive equation has a long history, especially for nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS). Consider the following mass-critical NLS equation:

i\partial_{t}\phi+\Delta\phi+|\phi|^{\frac{4}{d}}\phi=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The
explicit minimal mass blow-up solution
can be constructed by the pseudo-conformal symmetry of this equation. For any
t>0
, we denote

S_{\rm{NLS}}(t,x)=\frac{1}{t^{\frac{d}{2}}}e^{-i\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}-\frac{i}{t}}Q_{{\rm{NLS}}}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right),

where
Q_{\rm{NLS}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the unique ground state of the mass-critical NLS equation. Then we have
S_{{\rm{NLS}}}(t)
is a blow-up solution with

\|S_{{\rm{NLS}}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{{\rm{NLS}}}\|_{L^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\nabla S_{{\rm{NLS}}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{t}\ \mbox{as}\ t\downarrow 0.

In particular, by the seminal work of Merle
[
20
]
, the blow-up solution
S_{{\rm{NLS}}}(t)
is exactly the unique
(up to the symmetries)
minimal mass blow-up solution to the mass-critical NLS equation.
Recall that, for the 2D mass-critical Zakharov system,

\left\{\begin{aligned} i\partial_{t}u&=-\Delta u+nu,\\
\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial_{t}^{2}n&=\Delta n+\Delta|u|^{2},\end{aligned}\right.\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

the pseudo-conformal symmetry does not exist, and there is no minimal mass blow-up solution (see Glangetas-Merle
[
10
]
). We refer to
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
12
,
24
]
for the constructions of minimal mass blow-up solutions for other NLS-type equations.
We also refer to Merle
[
21
]
for the nonexistence of minimal mass blow-up solution of a type of inhomogeneous mass-critical NLS equation.

We now recall the related results for the generalized Korteweg-de Vries equation (gKdV) which is the closest model related to (
1.1
). For the mass-critical gKdV equation

\partial_{t}\phi+\partial_{x}\left(\partial_{x}^{2}\phi+\phi^{5}\right)=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R},

the
existence and description
of the minimal mass blow-up solution were first studied by Martel-Merle-Raphaël
[
17
]
, and then the description of such solution was sharpened by Combet-Martel
[
6
]
. More precisely, we denote by
Q_{\rm{KdV}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
the unique ground state of the mass-critical gKdV equation. Based on the work of
[
17
]
, we know that there exists a unique solution
S_{{\rm{KdV}}}(t)
on
(0,\infty)
such that

\|S_{{\rm{KdV}}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{{\rm{KdV}}}\|_{L^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\partial_{x}S_{{\rm{KdV}}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{t}\ \mbox{as}\ t\downarrow 0.

Then, the sharp asymptotics, both in the time and space variables, were derived in
[
6
]
, for any order derivative of
S_{\rm{KdV}}(t)
.
We refer to Martel-Merle
[
15
]
for the nonexistence of minimal mass blow-up solution of mass-critical gKdV equation, assuming an
L^{2}
-decay on the right of the initial data. We also refer to
[
11
,
13
,
16
,
18
,
19
]
for related results of blow-up dynamics for mass-critical dispersive models.

Last, we briefly survey the literature related to the
soliton dynamics
of Zakharov-Kuznetsov models. For the 2D quadratic Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation, the asymptotic stability of soliton and stability of multi-soliton was studied by Côte-Munõz-Pilod-Simpson
[
7
]
via energy-virial estimate. However, the numerical computation of the coercivity for a specific Schrödinger operator, which is related to such virial estimate, does not hold for the 3D case. Very recently, based on a regularized transformation and numerical computation, Farah-Holmer-Roudenko-Yang
[
8
]
deduced a new virial estimate and thus they could extend the asymptotic stability of soliton for the 3D quadratic Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation. It is worth mentioning here that, in Pilod-Valet
[
22
]
, the authors studied the dynamics of the collision of two solitary waves for the 2 and 3-dimensional Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation. On the study of blow-up dynamics for (
1.1
), we refer to our recent work
[
4
]
for the full description of the near soliton behavior.
We also refer to Bozgan-Ghoul-Masmoudi-Yang
[
3
]
for a similar result which was proved independently. More importantly, the two proofs use different energy-virial Lyapunov functionals. Finally, we also point out that in Trespalacios
[
26
]
, assuming the solution blows up, the author derived a lower bound for the blow-up rate for the 2D cubic Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation using the local well-posedness theory and growth estimates.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof

Let us give a brief insight into the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Indeed,
the proof is surprisingly simple and relies on an energy-virial estimate introduced in
[
4
]
. For the sake of contradiction, we first assume that a minimal mass blow-up solution
\phi(t)
exists. Then, we consider the geometric decomposition on the minimal mass blow-up solution in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
:

\varepsilon(t,y)=\lambda(t)\phi\left(t,\lambda(t)y+x(t)\right)-Q_{b}(t)(y).

Here,
Q_{b}
is close to
Q
in energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
b
small enough.
We now change the time variable as follows:

s=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\rm d}\sigma}{\lambda^{3}(\sigma)}\Longleftrightarrow\frac{{\rm d}s}{{\rm d}t}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}(t)}.

Heuristically, the geometric parameters
(\lambda,b)
satisfy

\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}=-b\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ b_{s}+\theta b^{2}=0\Longrightarrow\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\left(\frac{b}{\lambda^{\theta}}\right)=0.

Here,
\theta\approx 1.66
is a constant related to the integration of
\Lambda Q
(see Section
1.4
for the definition of
\theta
).
This implies that the function
({b}/{\lambda^{\theta}})
is a constant along the flow. Therefore, in the rigorous analysis, we could establish the lower and upper bounds of the function
({b}/{\lambda^{\theta}})
via the monotonicity properties of
(\lambda,b,\varepsilon)
. On the other hand, from the expansions of mass and energy for the minimal mass blow-up solution
\phi(t)
, we could deduce the upper bound of the function
(b/\lambda^{2})
. Last, combining the above-mentioned estimates for
(b/\lambda^{\theta})
and
(b/\lambda^{2})
with
\lambda(t)\to 0
as
t\uparrow T
, we reach a contradiction. We mention here that, due to the same reason, the general strategy for the construction of minimal mass blow-up solution does not work in our case now (see
[
17
, Page 1877]
for more details).

1.4.
Notation and conventions

For any
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, we denote

|\beta|=|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial_{y}^{\beta}=\frac{\partial^{|\beta|}}{\partial_{y_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{y_{2}}^{\beta_{2}}}.

Denote by
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by the set of functions
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
C_{n}>0
and
r_{n}>0
such that

\sum_{|\beta|=n}\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}f(y)\right|\leq C_{n}(1+|y|)^{r_{n}}e^{-\frac{|y|}{2}},\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{d}.

For any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we denote the
L^{2}
-scalar product by

(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)g(x){\rm d}x.

Let
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a
C^{\infty}
nondecreasing function such that

\chi_{|(-\infty,-2)}\equiv 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\chi_{|(-1,\infty)}\equiv 1.

Let
{\textbf{e}}_{1}
be the first vector of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, that is,
{\textbf{e}}_{1}=(1,0)
.

We introduce the generator of the scaling symmetry

\Lambda f=f+\nabla\cdot f,\quad\mbox{for any}\ f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

We also define the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}
around the ground state by

\mathcal{L}f=-\Delta f+f-3Q^{2}f,\quad\mbox{for any}\ f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Recall that, there exists
\mu_{1}>0
, such that for any function
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\left(\mathcal{L}f,f\right)\geq\mu_{1}\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}\left[(f,Q^{3})^{2}+(f,\partial_{y_{1}}Q)^{2}+(f,\partial_{y_{1}}Q)^{2}\right].

(1.4)

In addition, we define the Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{A}
for the dual problem by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}f-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{y_{2}}^{2}f-\frac{1}{2}\left(3Q^{2}+6y_{1}Q\partial_{y_{1}}Q\right)f

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{\|Q\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}\left[(f,Q^{2}\partial_{y_{1}}Q)y_{1}Q+(f,y_{1}Q)Q^{2}\partial_{y_{1}}Q\right].

Recall that, from
[
9
, Section 16]
, there exists
\mu_{2}>0
such that for any
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\left(\mathcal{A}f,f\right)\geq\mu_{2}\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}\left[(f,Q)^{2}+(f,\partial_{y_{1}}Q)^{2}+(f,\partial_{y_{1}}Q)^{2}\right].

(1.5)

Recall also that, we consider the following two smooth functions:

\widehat{F}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(y_{2})e^{-i\xi y_{2}}{\rm d}y_{2}\quad\mbox{with}\quad F(y_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda Q(y_{1},y_{2}){\rm d}y_{1}.

In addition, the constant
\theta
is defined by

\theta=2\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widehat{F}(\xi)|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}{\rm d}\xi\bigg)\bigg/\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{F}(\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi\bigg).

By an elementary numerical computation, we find
\theta\approx 1.66
(see
[
4
, Appendix A]
).

Throughout this note, for a given small constant
\delta
, we will denote by
\alpha(\delta)
a generic small constant with
\alpha(\delta)\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The localized profile

In this subsection, we recall the definition of the localized profile
Q_{b}
and some useful estimates related to
Q_{b}
. We start with the definition of the non-localized profile
P
which grows as
y_{1}\to-\infty
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

There exists a smooth function
P\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\partial_{y_{1}}P\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

such that the following estimates hold.

(i)

First-type estimate.
We have

\partial_{y_{1}}\mathcal{L}P=\Lambda Q,\quad\lim_{y_{1}\to\infty}\partial_{y_{2}}^{n}P(y_{1},y_{2})=0,\ \mbox{for any}\ n\in\mathbb{N},

|(P,\nabla Q)|=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad(P,Q)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|F(y_{2})|^{2}{\rm d}y_{2}>0.

(ii)

Second-type estimate.
For any
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, there exists
C_{1\beta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}P(y_{1},y_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1\beta}e^{-\frac{|y_{2}|}{3}},\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}P(y_{1},y_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1\beta}e^{-\frac{|y|}{3}},\quad\ \mbox{on}\ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}.

In addition, for any
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
\beta_{1}\neq 0
, there exists
C_{2\beta}>0
such that

\left|\partial_{y}^{\beta}P(y_{1},y_{2})\right|\leq C_{2\beta}e^{-\frac{|y|}{3}},\quad\ \mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof of the existence for
P
relies on the inversion of
\mathcal{L}
. We refer to
[
4
, Proposition 2.1 and Lemma 2.2]
for the details of the proof.
∎

\chi_{b}(y_{1})=\chi(|b|^{\frac{3}{4}}y_{1})\quad\mbox{and}\quad Q_{b}(y)=Q(y)+b\chi_{b}(y_{1})P(y).

In addition, we define the following error term related to the localized profile

\Psi_{b}=\partial_{y_{1}}\left(-\Delta Q_{b}+Q_{b}-Q_{b}^{3}\right)-b\Lambda Q_{b}.

(
[

2.2.
Geometrical decomposition of the flow

In this subsection, we recall the modulation theory for the solution of (
1.1
) near the soliton manifold. We start with the following variation property of the ground state
Q
. The proof is similar to
[
23
, Proposition 3.7]
and we omit it.

Lemma 2.3

(Variation property of
Q
)
.

There exists a constant
\delta_{1}>0
such that the following hold. For any
0<\delta_{2}<\delta_{1}
and
u\in H^{1}
with

E(u)\leq\delta_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}{\rm d}x\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u^{2}{\rm d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Q^{2}{\rm d}x+\delta_{2},

there exist
(\sigma_{0},\lambda_{0},x_{0})\in\left\{-1,1\right\}\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\left\|Q(x)-\sigma_{0}\lambda_{0}u(\lambda_{0}x+x_{0})\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq\alpha(\delta_{2}).

We assume that there exist
(\overline{\lambda}(t),\overline{x}(t))\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\overline{\varepsilon}(t)\in H^{1}
such that

\phi(t,x)=\frac{1}{\overline{\lambda}(t)}\left(Q+\overline{\varepsilon}\right)\left(t,\frac{x-\overline{x}(t)}{\overline{\lambda}(t)}\right),\quad\mbox{with}\ \|\overline{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\kappa\leq\kappa^{*}.

(2.1)

Here,
0<\kappa^{*}\ll 1
is a small enough universal constant.

First, we recall the following modulation theory for the solution of (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.4

(
[
4
]
)
.

Assuming (
2.1
) on
[0,t_{0}]
, then there exist
C^{1}
functions
(\lambda,x,b):[0,t_{0}]\mapsto(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\varepsilon(t,y)=\lambda(t)\phi(t,\lambda(t)y+x(t))-Q_{b(t)}(y),\quad\mbox{for any}\ t\in[0,t_{0}],

(2.2)

satisfies the orthogonality conditions

\left(\varepsilon(t),Q\right)=\left(\varepsilon(t),Q^{3}\right)=\left|\left(\varepsilon(t),\nabla Q\right)\right|=0.

(2.3)

Moreover, we have the following smallness estimates

|b(t)|+\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\left|1-\frac{\lambda(t)}{\overline{\lambda}(t)}\right|\lesssim\alpha(\kappa)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\alpha\left(\|\overline{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\right).

Proof.

The proof of the decomposition proposition relies on the implicit function Theorem. We refer to
[

∎

Second, we deduce the rough estimates related to the geometric parameters
(\lambda,b)
for the minimal mass solution from the conservations of mass and energy.

Lemma 2.5
.

Under the assumption of Lemma
2.4
, and assume moreover
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
. Then the following estimates hold.

(i)

Estimate induced by the mass
. For any
t\in[0,t_{0}]
, we have

\|\varepsilon(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim-b(t)\lesssim\|\varepsilon(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(2.4)

(ii)

Estimate induced by the energy.
For any
t\in[0,t_{0}]

Proof.

, we deduce that

b_{s}+\theta b^{2}=O\left(\mathcal{N}_{0}+|b|\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+|b|^{3}\right)\Longrightarrow\frac{1}{2}b^{2}+\frac{b_{s}}{\theta}\lesssim\mathcal{N}_{0}.

Based on the above identity and
b_{s}|b|=\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\left(|b|b\right)
, we find

\int_{s_{*}}^{s_{**}}\frac{|b(\sigma)|^{3}}{\lambda^{\theta}(\sigma)}{\rm d}\sigma\lesssim\frac{b^{2}(s_{*})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{*})}+\frac{b^{2}(s_{**})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{**})}+\int_{s_{*}}^{s_{**}}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{0}(\sigma)}{\lambda^{\theta}(\sigma)}{\rm d}\sigma.

On the other hand, from Proposition
2.10
, we also find

\frac{\mathcal{N}_{1}(s_{**})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{*})}+\int_{s_{*}}^{s_{**}}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{0}(\sigma)}{\lambda^{\theta}(\sigma)}{\rm d}\sigma\lesssim\frac{\mathcal{N}_{1}(s_{*})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{*})}+\int_{s_{*}}^{s_{**}}\frac{b^{4}(\sigma)}{\lambda^{\theta}(\sigma)}{\rm d}\sigma.

Combining the above two estimates, we see that

\int_{s_{*}}^{s_{**}}\frac{|b(\sigma)|^{3}}{\lambda^{\theta}(\sigma)}{\rm d}\sigma+\int_{s_{*}}^{s_{**}}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{0}(\sigma)}{\lambda^{\theta}(\sigma)}{\rm d}\sigma\lesssim\frac{\mathcal{N}_{1}(s_{*})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{*})}+\frac{b^{2}(s_{*})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{*})}+\frac{b^{2}(s_{**})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{**})}.

Next, from the definition of
J(s)
, we obtain

\left|e^{J(s)}-1\right|\lesssim|J(s)|\lesssim\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(s)\lesssim\alpha(\kappa)\ll 1,\quad\mbox{for any}\ s\in(-\infty,0).

Therefore, from the above estimate and (iv) of Lemma
2.8
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{b(s_{*})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{*})}e^{J(s_{*})}-\frac{b(s_{**})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{**})}e^{J(s_{**})}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s_{*}}^{s_{**}}\left|\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\sigma}\left(\frac{b}{\lambda^{\theta}}e^{J}\right)(\sigma){\rm d}\sigma\right|\lesssim\frac{\mathcal{N}_{1}(s_{*})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{*})}+\frac{b^{2}(s_{*})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{*})}+\frac{b^{2}(s_{**})}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{**})},

which means that the estimate (
3.10
) is true.

Step 3. Conclusion.
First, from (
3.10
), for any
-\infty<s_{*}<s_{**}<0
with
-s_{**}
large enough, we deduce that

\frac{|b(s_{**})|}{2\lambda^{\theta}(s_{**})}\leq\frac{|b(s_{*})|}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{*})}\leq 2\frac{|b(s_{**})|}{\lambda^{\theta}(s_{**})}.

On the other hand, from Proposition
3.2
, for any
s\in(-\infty,0)
, we deduce that

|b(s)|\lesssim\lambda^{2}(s)E_{0}\Longrightarrow\frac{|b(s)|}{\lambda^{\theta}(s)}\lesssim\lambda^{2-\theta}(s)E_{0}.

Combining the above two estimates with
\lambda(s)\to 0
as
s\downarrow-\infty
, we reach a contradiction. At this point, we have proved the nonexistence of
\phi(t)
and thus the proof of Theorem
1.2
is complete.
∎

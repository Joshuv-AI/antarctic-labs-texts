Small bowel obstruction caused by impacted cork foreign body mimicking the small bowel feces sign: An educational imaging case
Authors: Youssef Sakhy, Ilias Azhari, Fatima-Zahra Raoui, Houria Tabakh, Abdellatif Siwane, Najwa Touil, Omar Kacimi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Small bowel obstruction (SBO) caused by impacted vegetal foreign bodies is uncommon and may mimic the small bowel feces sign on computed tomography (CT), potentially leading to diagnostic delay. Differentiating these entities is important because the feces sign is a secondary imaging feature of obstruction, whereas foreign bodies represent the primary obstructing cause. We report the case of a 25-year-old man with chronic psychosis who presented with abdominal pain, vomiting, abdominal distension, and cessation of flatus and stool. Contrast-enhanced abdominal CT demonstrated dilated small bowel loops with an abrupt transition point in the distal ileum. At this level, a 27-mm well-circumscribed heterogeneous intraluminal mass containing multiple air foci was initially interpreted as the small bowel feces sign. Additional lesions with identical imaging characteristics were identified in the stomach. Retrospective review suggested impacted ingested cork material rather than a simple feces sign. This case highlights that a mottled gas-containing intraluminal mass at the transition zone should not automatically be considered the small bowel feces sign. When the lesion is discrete and associated with similar gastric material, an obstructing bezoar or vegetal foreign body should be suspected. CT plays a key role in accurate diagnosis and management planning.

Introduction

The small bowel feces sign is a computed tomography (CT) finding encountered in acute small bowel obstruction (SBO), characterized by digestive material mixed with gas bubbles within dilated bowel loops proximal to the obstruction. It is most often identified near theransition point and is useful for localizing the level of obstruction. This appearance results from luminal stasis, progressive fluid resorption, and admixture of undigested alimentary debris.

However, a similar mottled intraluminal appearance may also correspond to an obstructing bezoar or vegetal foreign body. Recognition of this distinction is important because it represents in this case the mechanical cause of obstruction rather than a secondary sign. CT typically demonstrates a well-defined intraluminal ovoid mass containing entrapped air bubbles at the transition zone, sometimes associated with additional similar lesions in the stomach.

Case presentation

A 25-year-old man treated for chronic psychosis presented to the emergency department with abdominal pain, vomiting, abdominal distension, and cessation of flatus and stool, consistent with acute SBO. He had no prior history of abdominal surgery and no known inflammatory bowel disease.

Contrast-enhanced abdominal CT demonstrated dilated small bowel loops with an abrupt transition point in the distal ileum. At this level, a 27-mm well-circumscribed heterogeneous intraluminal mass containing multiple air foci was identified and considered initially as small bowel feces sign. Retrospective image review raised suspicion for impacted ingested vegetal foreign body rather than a simple feces sign. No definite intrinsic stenotic lesion or extrinsic compressive mass was identified on CT at the transition point. Additional lesions with identical imaging characteristics were present in the stomach. Psychiatric disease was considered a predisposing factor for accidental ingestion of indigestible material.

Management and outcome

Despite the absence of ischemic signs, operative management was selected because of complete mechanical obstruction with a substantial obstructing foreign body, persistent obstructive symptoms, gastric multiplicity of similar foreign bodies, and low likelihood of spontaneous passage. Surgical exploration confirmed impacted cork fragments within the ileal lumen. Additional gastric cork bodies were also retrieved. No underlying bowel stricture, tumor, or inflammatory narrowing was found intraoperatively.

Discussion

Vegetal foreign bodies and phytobezoars are an uncommon cause of SBO [1]. They usually occur in patients with predisposing factors such as prior gastric surgery, impaired motility, poor mastication, or psychiatric disorders associated with unusual ingestion behavior. In this case, the obstructing material consisted of wine-bottle corks.

Differentiation from the small bowel feces sign is clinically relevant. The feces sign is usually elongated, less well delimited, and represents stagnant intraluminal contents proximal to another cause of obstruction [2]. In contrast, bezoars or impacted foreign bodies tend to appear as discrete rounded masses lodged at the transition zone and may be associated with similar gastric contents [3].

Conclusion

A mottled gas-containing intraluminal mass at the transition zone should not automatically be interpreted as the small bowel feces sign. When the lesion is discrete and especially when similar gastric material is present, an obstructing phytobezoar or impacted vegetal foreign body should be suspected. CT can guide timely management and prevent diagnostic delay.

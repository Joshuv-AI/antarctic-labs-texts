Approximation properties of operator coorbit spaces and sparsity classes
Monika Dörfler, Lukas Köhldorfer, Franz Luef, Henry McNulty

Abstract
Extensions of coorbit spaces for functions to operators have been introduced by two different groups in [ 4 ] and [ 32 ] , where one is based on the coorbit theory of Feichtinger-Gröchening while the other is based on the theory of localized frames. We show that for certain Gabor g-frames the co-orbit spaces in [ 32 ] conincide with the ones in [ 4 ] and we refer to this class of operators as operator coorbit spaces. Based on the description of operator coorbit spaces in terms of Gabor g-frames we provide operator dictionaries for these spaces that allow us to define sparsity classes in this setting. We establish that these sparsity classes also coincide with the operator coorbit spaces, which holds, in particular for all Feichtinger operators, a nice class of mixed states. Numerical examples confirm the expected approximation quality by few terms for appropriately chosen operators.



1
Introduction

In applied harmonic analysis compressed sensing provides us with fast and reliable algorithms based on sparse representations of signals or images. As an example, wavelet decompositions provide sparse representations of images. In the search for dictionaries which enable sparse representations for a particular class of objects, it has been noticed that
localized
frames can lead to sparsity in representations of functions in certain Banach spaces such as modulation spaces or Besov spaces, or more general coorbit spaces of functions associated to projective square-integrable, integrable unitary representations of a locally compact group
G
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, cf.
[
13
,
9
,
44
]
.

Many problems in quantum technology deal with quantum systems that have a huge number of quantum states, e.g. ground state problems, see, e.g.,
[
22
]
, where the authors propose the use of machine learning techniques to address this crucial issue. One of our main contributions to the theory of quantum states is to define sparsity classes and to construct
sparse
decompositions of mixed-state operators, in a sense made more precise in Section
5
. These results are operator analogs of the ones on sparse decomposition for functions. Like in the case of functions, we introduce sparsity classes for co-orbit spaces of operators, defined and studied in
[
4
]
.

From a mathematical point of view, quantum states are positive trace class operators with unit trace, aka density operators, and in many situations we are dealing with a finite-rank operator, which is the sum of a large number of rank-one operators. Another example of an operator of this form is the data operator introduced in
[
5
]
, which may be interpreted as the sample covariance operator of a random variable taking values in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
[
43
]
.

Given the importance of density operators in different contexts as described above, it is one of the goals of this work to develop sparse decompositions for density operators using operator-valued frames. In analogy to the function setting, these frames are generated by
operator translations
of a “nice” trace class operator, the operator analysis window
\Phi
, and where the analysis mapping is sampling the operator short-time Fourier transform,
[
4
]
, of the density operator with respect to the analysis trace class operator
\Phi
.

We show that results on sparsity classes associated with operator-valued frame yield as a consequence sparse representations of density operators in terms of the first few largest frame coefficients of the operator-valued frame decomposition of the density operator as in the case of best
m
-term approximation of signals.
The results are based on the theory of localized operator-valued frames
[
32
]
, which contain Gabor g-frames
[
40
]
as a special case, and a class of operators associated with the operator STFT in
[
4
]
, which one might call operator coorbit spaces. Gabor g-frames have also been independently introduced by Han, Li and Wang in
[
45
]
under the name of operator-valued Gabor frames, motivated by the work of Kaftal, Larson and Zhang on operator-valued frames
[
28
]
. The sparse representation of density operators is in terms of tight operator Gabor g-frame decomposition of a suitable Gabor g-frame operator window. We conclude this introductory section by two important remarks. First, the existence of sparse operator representation presented in this article depends intrinsically on the localization of the representation system. This is in line with earlier work on sparse signal representation, cf.
[
11
]
. Second, we point out that the proposed sparse representations are related to ”pretty good” measurements in quantum information theory, cf.
[
19
,
20
,
10
,
30
]
.

Preliminaries

Throughout this manuscript,
{\mathcal{H}}
will always be a separable Hilbert space. By
\mathbb{N}
we denote the set of positive intergers and we abbreviate
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

2.1
Weight functions

A
weight function
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or simply
weight
, is a continuous and positive function
\nu:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
.
The standard weight considered in this work is the polynomial weight
\nu_{s}
(
s\geq 0
) given by

\nu_{s}(z)=(1+|z|)^{s}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

A weight
m
is called
\nu
-moderate
if there exists some constant
C>0
such that

m(z+z^{\prime})\leq Cm(z)\nu(z^{\prime})\qquad(\forall z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

For more background on weight functions and their relevance in time-frequency analysis, see
[
17
]
.

2.2
Bochner spaces

Let
B
be a Banach space,
1\leq p<\infty
and
\nu
be a weight on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then the (weighted)
Lebesgue-Bochner space

[
23
, Chapter 1]

L^{p}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d};B)
denotes the space of all (equivalence classes of) strongly Lebesgue-measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow B
for which

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|f(z)\|_{B}^{p}\nu(z)^{p}\,dz

is finite. Equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d};B)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|f(z)\|_{B}^{p}\nu(z)^{p}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

L^{p}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d};B)
is a Banach space. The Banach space
L^{\infty}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d};B)
is defined via the usual modifications. In case
\nu\equiv 1
is the trivial weight on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we abbreviate
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};B):=L^{p}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};B)
(
1\leq p\leq\infty
).

Next, we consider the discrete variants of the Lebesgue-Bochner spaces introduced above. Let again
B
be a Banach space,
\nu
be a weight on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\Lambda
be a discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then for
p\in(0,\infty]
the (weighted)
Bochner sequence space

\ell^{p}_{\nu}(\Lambda;B)

[
23
, Chapter 1]
is defined by

\ell_{\nu}^{p}(\Lambda;B):=\left\{(h_{k})_{k\in\Lambda}:h_{k}\in B\,(\forall k\in\Lambda),(\|h_{k}\|_{B}\cdot\nu(k))_{k\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda)\right\}.

In case
\nu(k)=1
for all
k\in\Lambda
, we write
\ell_{\nu}^{p}(\Lambda;B)=\ell^{p}(\Lambda;B)
. By
[
23
, Chapter 1]
,
\ell_{\nu}^{p}(\Lambda;B)
equipped with the (quasi-)norm

\|(h_{k})_{k\in\Lambda}\|_{\ell_{\nu}^{p}(\Lambda;B)}:=\|(\|h_{k}\|_{B}\cdot\nu(k))_{k\in\Lambda}\|_{\ell^{p}(\Lambda)},

is a Banach space for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
and a quasi-Banach space for
0<p<1
. By
c_{00}(\Lambda;B)
we denote the space of all finitely supported
B
-valued sequences indexed by
\Lambda
. It is straightforward to see that
(c_{00}(\Lambda;B),\|\,.\,\|_{\ell_{\nu}^{p}(\Lambda;B)})
is a dense subspace of
(\ell_{\nu}^{p}(\Lambda;B),\|\,.\,\|_{\ell_{\nu}^{p}(\Lambda;B)})
for each
p\in[1,\infty)
.

2.3
Some background on g-frames

Frames, introduced by Duffin and Schaeffer in 1952
[
6
]
, are countable families of vectors in a separable Hilbert space, which allow a redundant and stable reconstruction of any vector in that space from linear rank-one measurements. Here we consider a generalization of frames, originally called
generalized frames
(
g-frames
)
[
42
]
, that is, countable families of operators which allow a linear and stable reconstruction of any vector from higher-rank measurements.

A countable family
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of bounded operators
U_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
on some separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
is called an
operator-valued frame

[
42
]
for
{\mathcal{H}}
, if there exist positive constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
, such that

A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|U_{\lambda}f\|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}\qquad(\forall f\in{\mathcal{H}}).

(2.1)

For brevity, we will stick to the name
g-frame
from now on and call
A
and
B
the
lower
and
upper g-frame bound
respectively. We call
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
a
g-Bessel sequence
with
Bessel bound

B
, whenever the upper (but not necessarily the lower) inequality in (
2.1
) holds true for all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
. For any g-Bessel sequence
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
the following operators are well-defined and bounded
[
42
]
:

•

The
synthesis operator

D_{\mathcal{U}}:\ell^{2}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
, defined by

D_{\mathcal{U}}(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U^{*}_{\lambda}g_{\lambda},

•

The
analysis operator

C_{\mathcal{U}}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\ell^{2}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})
, defined by

C_{\mathcal{U}}f=(U_{\lambda}f)_{\lambda\in\Lambda},

•

The
g-frame operator

S_{\mathcal{U}}:=D_{\mathcal{U}}C_{\mathcal{U}}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
, given by

S_{\mathcal{U}}f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U^{*}_{\lambda}U_{\lambda}f.

•

The
g-Gram matrix

G_{\mathcal{U}}:=C_{\mathcal{U}}D_{\mathcal{U}}:\ell^{2}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})\longrightarrow\ell^{2}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})
.

In this case,
\|D_{\mathcal{U}}\|=\|C_{\mathcal{U}}\|\leq\sqrt{B}
and
D_{\mathcal{U}}
and
C_{\mathcal{U}}
are adjoint to one another. Consequently, both
S_{\mathcal{U}}
and
G_{\mathcal{U}}
are self-adjoint and bounded by
B
. If
\mathcal{U}
is a g-frame with frame bounds
A\leq B
, then
S_{\mathcal{U}}
is additionally a positive operator, whose spectrum is contained in
[A,B]
, and thus invertible on
{\mathcal{H}}
. Hence
S_{\mathcal{U}}
may be composed with its inverse (and vice versa), which yields the
g-frame reconstruction
formulae

f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U^{*}_{\lambda}U_{\lambda}S_{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}S_{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}U^{*}_{\lambda}U_{\lambda}f\qquad(\forall f\in\mathcal{H}).

(2.2)

The family
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}=(\widetilde{U}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, defined by
\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}=U_{\lambda}S_{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}
(
\lambda\in\Lambda
), which appears in the reconstruction process (
2.2
), is again a g-frame and called the
canonical dual g-frame
of
\mathcal{U}
. More generally
[
31
]
, if
\mathcal{W}=(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-Bessel sequence so that

f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U^{*}_{\lambda}W_{\lambda}f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}W^{*}_{\lambda}U_{\lambda}f\qquad(\forall f\in\mathcal{H}),

(2.3)

or, equivalently,

\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}=D_{\mathcal{U}}C_{\mathcal{W}}=D_{\mathcal{W}}C_{\mathcal{U}},

(2.4)

then
(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is already g-frame and called a
dual g-frame
of
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
.

2.4
Polynomially localized g-frames

Besides the above-mentioned possibility of perfect reconstruction (
2.2
), some g-frames admit other useful properties which are not captured by the defining g-frame inequalities (
2.1
) alone.

A g-frame
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
for
{\mathcal{H}}
is called
polynomially
s
-localized
(
s>0
), or simply
s
-localized
, if there exists a constant
C>0
, such that

\|U_{\lambda}U_{\mu}^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}\leq C(1+|\lambda-\mu|)^{-s}\qquad(\forall\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda).

(2.5)

Furthermore, if
\mathcal{W}=(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is another g-frame for
{\mathcal{H}}
, then we say that
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{W}
are
mutually (polynomially)
s
-localized
(
s>0
), if there exists a constant
C>0
, such that

\|U_{\lambda}W_{l}^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}\leq C(1+|\lambda-\mu|)^{-s}\qquad(\forall\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda).

(2.6)

Remark 2.1
.

Relations (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) say that both the g-Gram matrix
G_{\mathcal{U}}=[U_{\lambda}U_{\mu}^{*}]_{\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda}
and the mixed g-Gram matrix
G_{\mathcal{U},\mathcal{W}}=[U_{\lambda}W_{l}^{*}]_{\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda}
belong to the
Jaffard class

{\mathcal{J}}_{s}
of
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
-valued matrices with polynomial off-diagonal decay of order
s
. In particular, if the index set
\Lambda
is assumed to be a
relatively separated
set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, that is

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Lambda\cap(x+[0,1]^{d})|<\infty,

and if
s>d
, then
{\mathcal{J}}_{s}
is a so-called
spectral algebra

[
34
]
, i.e. a Banach algebra of
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
-valued matrices with manifold convenient properties, see also
[
33
]
.

Polynomially localized g-frames and their rich properties have been studied in
[
32
]
. In the following, we provide the definitions and results relevant to this work. For the remainder of this section, we always assume the following.

Assumption:

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a relatively separated (index) set.

Later we will only work with a full-rank lattice
\Lambda
, which satisfies the latter (necessary) technical condition anyway.

First, we note the remarkable fact, that
s
-localization is preserved under canonical duality.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
32
]

Let
s>d
and
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be an
s
-localized g-frame. Then the following hold:

(i)

The canonical dual g-frame
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}=(\widetilde{U}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is
s
-localized.

(ii)

\mathcal{U}
and

-localized.

[

Theorem 2.4
.

[
32
]

The space
\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{U})

, and

Next, we consider the synthesis operator

densely defined on

.

2.5
Time-frequency analysis and modulation spaces

Modulation spaces arise naturally in the context of time-frequency analysis through the action of
time-frequency shifts
, given by the projective unitary representation of the reduced Weyl–Heisenberg group on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
z=(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, the time-frequency shift
\pi(z)
is defined as the composition of the translation operator
T_{x}:f(t)\mapsto f(t-x)
, and the modulation operator
M_{\omega}:f(t)\mapsto e^{2\pi i\omega t}f(t)
, that is,

\displaystyle\pi(z)=M_{\omega}T_{x}.

The
Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)
of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to a window
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

V_{g}f(z):=\langle f,\pi(z)g\rangle_{L^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

Typical windows include compactly supported functions or rapidly decaying functions such as the normalized Gaussian
\varphi_{0}(t)=2^{d/4}e^{-\pi t^{2}}
. For
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the mapping
z\mapsto V_{g}f(z)
belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and is uniformly continuous.
As a consequence of Moyal’s identity

\langle V_{g_{1}}f_{1},V_{g_{2}}f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\overline{\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}},

V_{g}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is an isometry and allows reconstruction by

f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}V_{g}f(z)\,\pi(z)g\,dz.

We finally mention that its adjoint operator is

V_{g}^{*}F=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F(z)\,\pi(z)g\,dz,

with interpretation of integrals in the weak sense. In other words,

V_{g}^{*}V_{g}=I_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

The briefly introduced framework of the STFT now suggests the following definition of special cases of coorbit spaces, namely the
modulation spaces
.

We begin by considering the weighted modulation space
M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\nu_{s}
(
s\geq 0
) is the polynomial weight introduced in Subsection
2.1
. The space
M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as the collection of functions whose STFT with respect to the Gaussian window
\varphi_{0}(t)=2^{\frac{d}{4}}e^{-\pi t^{2}}
belongs to the weighted
L^{1}
space, i.e.,

M^{1}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):V_{\varphi_{0}}f\in L^{1}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\}.

For each
s\geq 0
,
M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach space with respect to the norm

\|f\|_{M^{1}_{v}}:=\|V_{\varphi_{0}}f\|_{L^{1}_{v}}

and it is nontrivial, as it contains
\varphi_{0}
itself. Moreover, the Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is contained in
M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is invariant under time-frequency shifts. The unweighted case
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is known as
Feichtinger’s algebra
, a fundamental object in time-frequency analysis which provides an ideal space of test functions. We refer to Feichtinger’s original paper
[
8
]
and the survey
[
24
]
for further background.

General modulation spaces are defined as follows. Given a
\nu_{s}
-moderate weight
m
and exponents
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, one sets

M^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{f\in(M^{1}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{\prime}:V_{\varphi_{0}}f\in L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\},

with norm

\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{m}}:=\|V_{\varphi_{0}}f\|_{L^{p,q}_{m}}.

For any
g\in M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the space

\{f\in(M^{1}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{\prime}:V_{g}f\in L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\}

coincides with
M^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and the corresponding norms are equivalent. For
p=q
we abbreviate
M^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=M^{p,p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Note that, as a direct consequence of the properties of the STFT with Gaussian window, we have

M^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Finally, we collect the following result for later reference.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
12
]

If
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
and
m_{2}(z)\leq Cm_{1}(z)
for some
C>0
, then
M^{p_{1}}_{m_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuously and densely embedded in
M^{p_{2}}_{m_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

2.6
Operator coorbit spaces

For two operators
\Phi,F\in{\mathcal{HS}}:={\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, the
operator short-time Fourier transform
(
operator STFT
) of
F
with respect to a
window
or
atom

\Phi
,
\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}F
, is defined by
[
4
]

\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}F(z)=\Phi^{*}\pi(z)^{*}F.

(2.7)

The operator STFT thus defines an operator-valued function in phase space, and turns out to in fact define an isometry from
\mathcal{HS}
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d};\mathcal{HS})
, for a fixed, normalized
\Phi\in\mathcal{HS}
.

Let
\nu_{s}(z)=(1+|z|)^{s}
be the polynomial weight of order
s\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and
m
be a
\nu_{s}
-moderate weight. Moreover, we set
\Phi_{0}=\varphi_{0}\otimes\varphi_{0}
, where
\varphi_{0}(t)=2^{\frac{d}{4}}e^{-\pi t^{2}}
denotes the
L^{2}
-normalized Gaussian. The space
\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by
[
4
]

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{F\in\mathcal{HS}:\mathcal{V}_{\Phi_{0}}F\in L^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d};\mathcal{HS})\}

(2.8)

with corresponding norm

\|F\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|\mathcal{V}_{\Phi_{0}}F\|_{L^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d};\mathcal{HS})},

and we denote its unweighted version, corresponding to
\nu_{0}\equiv 1
, by
\mathfrak{M}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. To define more general
operator coorbit spaces

\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we need to resort to the space
\mathfrak{S}
of Schwartz operators, cf.
[
29
]
.
1
1

1

The authors in that paper give the following intuitive characterization of Schwartz operators by rapid off-diagonal decay in the Hermite basis:
Let
\{\psi_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\}
be the
n

function basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
An operator
T
on
\mathcal{H}
is a Schwartz operator
if and only if for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\Big((1+|\alpha|+|\beta|)^{k}\,\big|\langle\psi_{\alpha},T\psi_{\beta}\rangle\big|\Big)<\infty.

The spaces
\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are then defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{F\in\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}:\Phi_{0}^{*}\pi(z)^{*}F\in L^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d};\mathcal{HS})\}.

with norms

\|F\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}}=\|\mathcal{V}_{\Phi_{0}}F\|_{L^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d};{\mathcal{HS}})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\|\Phi_{0}^{*}\pi(z)^{*}F\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}^{p}m(z)^{p}\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for
p<\infty
and with the usual modification in the case

. As one might expect given the terminology, these spaces arising from the operator STFT act in many ways analogously to the modulation spaces arising from the (function) STFT. Recall the definition of the operator STFT
\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}F(z)=\Phi^{*}\pi(z)^{*}F
which for a normalized Hilbert-Schmidt operator
\Phi
is an isometry and the associated operator Gabor space
\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{HS})
is a vector-valued reproducing kernel Hilbert space, and in
[
4
]
an analog of the correspondence principle for the coorbit spaces
\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
like for Feichtinger’s modulation spaces as well as an atomic decompositions for operators in
\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

It can be shown that the spaces
\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be defined with respect to any window operator
\Phi\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with equivalent norm. Furthermore,
we have the continuous inclusions

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathfrak{M}^{p^{\prime}}_{\tilde{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

for
1\leq p\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty
and
\tilde{m}(z)\lesssim m(z)
.

3
Identification of the spaces
\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

In this section, we identify the operator coorbit spaces
\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the co-orbit spaces
\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
associated with some
s
-localized g-frame
\mathcal{G}=(G_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
for the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{HS}}:={\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. This identification is interesting and useful for the following reasons. While the
\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}
-norms of some operator

are given by the (continuous)

-norms of its operator STFT, said identification (Theorem
3.7
) guarantees norm equivalence with (discrete)

-norms of the operator STFT of
F
sampled on a suitable lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. This is much more convenient for both theoretical and practical purposes. In principal, this norm equivalence has already been shown in
[
4
]
. However, by employing the machinery of
s
-localized g-frames, the definition of the co-orbit spaces
\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
naturally extends to exponents
0<p<1
in this case, yielding a canonical extension of the definition of
\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
0<p<1
. As we will see in Section

, these resulting (quasi-)Banach spaces are relevant for sparse approximation of operators. Moreover, applying the results from Subsection

that are interesting in their own right.

In the following we outline the construction of the co-orbit spaces
\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
(which we will identify with the spaces
\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
).

Step 1.
We show that
(\Phi_{0}\pi(\lambda)^{*})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

It is straightforward to show that
(\Phi_{0}\pi(\lambda)^{*})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is

-localized and that the co-orbit spaces associated with that g-frame are well-defined and non-trivial. However, the associated co-orbit space norms are given by

.

Step 2.
We
lift
the g-frame

to a g-frame
\mathcal{G}=(G_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
for the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Step 3.
We show that the g-frame
\mathcal{G}=(G_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
for

is

Step 4.
Having introduced the

-localized g-frame

, we may apply the machinery presented in Subsection

and obtain the family of associated co-orbit spaces

for

We start with the lifting property outlined in the Step 2. This lifting trick has already been performed in
[
4
, Proposition 6.12]
but also holds true in abstract Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and is perfectly compatible with g-frame duality. In order to formulate the result, the following notation is required.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert space and
W\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a bounded operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then
L_{W}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}}))
denotes the left-composition operator defined by

L_{W}H:=WH.

(3.1)

Obviously,
(L_{W})^{*}=L_{W^{*}}
and
L_{W_{1}}L_{W_{2}}=L_{W_{1}W_{2}}
for
W_{1},W_{2}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
. If
W
is invertible in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, then
L_{W}
is invertible in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}}))
with inverse given by
(L_{W})^{-1}=L_{W^{-1}}
.

Proposition 3.1

(Lifting Property)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a separable Hilbert space and assume that
(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
are countable families contained in
{\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}})
. Then the following hold.

(i)

(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-frame for
{\mathcal{H}}
with g-frame bounds
A\leq B
if and only if
(L_{U_{\lambda}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-frame for
{\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}})
with g-frame bounds
A\leq B
.

(ii)

(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a pair of dual g-frames in
{\mathcal{H}}
if and only if
(L_{W_{\lambda}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
(L_{U_{\lambda}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a pair of dual g-frames in
{\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

(iii)

If (i) holds true, then the canonical dual g-frame of
(L_{U_{\lambda}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
{\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}})
is given by
(L_{\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, where
(\widetilde{U}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
denotes the canonical dual g-frame of
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
{\mathcal{H}}
.

(iv)

In case
{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and assuming that
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=(\Phi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-Bessel sequence, where
\Phi\in{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, then
S_{\mathcal{U}}
commutes with
\pi(\mu)
for every
\mu\in\Lambda
, and likewise,
S_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}
commutes with
L_{\pi(\mu)}
for every
\mu\in\Lambda
. In particular, if both
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
(L_{U_{\lambda}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
are g-frames for their respective spaces, then
S_{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}
commutes with
\pi(\mu)
for every
\mu\in\Lambda
, and likewise,
S_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}^{-1}
commutes with
L_{\pi(\mu)}
for every
\mu\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) completely analogous to the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 6.12]
.

(ii) First assume that
(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a dual g-frame of
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then
f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}W_{\lambda}^{*}U_{\lambda}f
for all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
and thus
Hf=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}W_{\lambda}^{*}U_{\lambda}Hf
for all
H\in{\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}})
and all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Let
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be any orthonormal basis for
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then we obtain

\left\|H-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}L_{W_{\lambda}}^{*}L_{U_{\lambda}}H\right\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|He_{n}-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}W_{\lambda}^{*}U_{\lambda}He_{n}\right\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}=0.

Since both
(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
are g-frames for
{\mathcal{H}}
by assumption, (i) implies that
(L_{W_{\lambda}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
(L_{U_{\lambda}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
are g-frames for
{\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}})
. At the same time, the latter computation shows that they form a pair of dual g-frames in
{\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

The converse statement is proven by specifying to Hilbert-Schmidt operators of the form
f\otimes e_{k}
(
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
) and similar reasoning.

(iii) Assume that
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-frame for
{\mathcal{H}}
and that
L_{\mathcal{U}}=(L_{U_{\lambda}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-frame for
{\mathcal{HS}}({\mathcal{H}})
. Then, a direct calculation shows that their respective g-frame operators satisfy the relation
S_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}=L_{S_{\mathcal{U}}}
. This implies
(S_{L_{\mathcal{U}}})^{-1}=(L_{S_{\mathcal{U}}})^{-1}=L_{S_{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}}
and the claim follows.

(iv) By assumption, the g-frame operator
S_{\mathcal{U}}
is bounded. Since, for all
\mu,\lambda\in\Lambda
,
\pi(\mu)\pi(\lambda)=c(\mu,\lambda)\pi(\mu+\lambda)
(
c(\mu,\lambda)
some phase factor) we directly compute

\pi(\mu)S_{\mathcal{U}}\pi(\mu)^{*}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\pi(\mu)\pi(\lambda)\Phi\Phi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*}\pi(\mu)^{*}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\pi(\mu+\lambda)\Phi\Phi^{*}\pi(\mu+\lambda)^{*}=S_{\mathcal{U}}.

Thus
\pi(\mu)
and
S_{\mathcal{U}}
commute. The second statement follows immediately from the first statement, since
S_{L_{\mathcal{U}}}=L_{S_{\mathcal{U}}}
. The last statement follows readily.
∎

According to

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=(L_{\pi(\lambda)\Phi_{0}\pi(\lambda)^{*}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be the g-frame occurring in Theorem

,

be a

-moderate weight for some

be arbitrary. Then

\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{G})

with equivalent norms.

Proof.

The case
p=\infty
can be deduced from the case
p=1
via the duality relations
(\mathfrak{M}_{1/m}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{\prime}\cong\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

[
4

and
(\mathcal{C}o_{1/m}^{1}(\mathcal{G}))^{\prime}\simeq\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{\infty}(\mathcal{G})

[

, Theorem 3.29]
after noticing that the weight
1/m
is
\nu_{r}
-moderate (
[
12
, Lemma 11.1.1]
).

We fix some
1\leq p<\infty
and let
F\in\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
be arbitrary. By Theorem
2.5
the g-frame synthesis operator
D_{\mathcal{G}}:\ell_{m}^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
is onto. Thus, there exists some
(H_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell_{m}^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}})
such that
F=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\pi(\lambda)\Phi_{0}H_{\lambda}
. At the same time, Proposition
3.5
says that synthesis operator

associated to the operator window
\Phi_{0}
is bounded from

into
\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Thus

is contained in
\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and we may estimate

\|F\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}}=\left\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\pi(\lambda)\Phi_{0}H_{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}}\leq C\|(H_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|_{\ell_{m}^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}})}.

The latter is true for all
(H_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell_{m}^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}})
with

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|F\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{p}_{m}}

\displaystyle\leq C\inf\left\{\|(H_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|_{\ell_{m}^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}})}:(H_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell_{m}^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}}),F=D_{\mathcal{G}}(H_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\|F\|_{\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{G})},

where we used the norm equivalence stated in Theorem
2.5

Conversely, let
F\in\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be arbitrary. According to Proposition
3.4

C_{\Phi_{0}}F=(\Phi_{0}\pi(\lambda)^{*}F)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell_{m}^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}})
. Next, let
S_{0}
denote the frame operator associated to the g-frame
(\Phi_{0}\pi(\lambda)^{*})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
for

appearing in the proof of Theorem

[

, the canonical dual g-frame of

in

is given by

. By Proposition
3.1

(L_{\Phi_{0}S_{0}^{-1}\pi(\lambda)^{*}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
equals the canonical dual g-frame
(\widetilde{G}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of
(G_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
{\mathcal{HS}}
. Furthermore, we also note that
\Phi_{0}S_{0}^{-1}\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for each

, since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\|\Phi_{0}\pi(z)^{*}\Phi_{0}S_{0}^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}\nu_{s}(z)\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{0}^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\|\Phi_{0}\pi(z)^{*}\Phi_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}\nu_{s}(z)\,dz

\displaystyle=\|S_{0}^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|\Phi_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|S_{0}^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty

for each
s\geq 0
. Now, substituting
\Phi=\Phi_{0}
and
\Psi=\Phi_{0}S_{0}^{-1}
in Proposition
3.6
yields that

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=D_{\Phi_{0}S_{0}^{-1}}C_{\Phi_{0}}F

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\pi(\lambda)S_{0}^{-1}\Phi_{0}^{2}\pi(\lambda)^{*}F

\displaystyle=D_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}(\Phi_{0}\pi(\lambda)^{*}F)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}.

Thus

according to Theorem

.

Altogether, we have shown that
\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{G})=\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and that the identity map

and

are equivalent as a consequence of the bounded inverse theorem.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
s>2d+r
for some
r\geq 0
and
\Psi
be an operator of the form

\Psi=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\phi_{m}\otimes\psi_{m},

where
\phi_{m}\in M_{\nu_{s}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\psi_{m}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\|\phi_{m}\|_{M_{\nu_{s}}^{1}}\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty.

Assume that
(\Psi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\Lambda
is a lattice as in Theorem
3.2
. Then

\mathcal{W}=(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\qquad W_{\lambda}:=L_{\Psi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*}}\quad(\lambda\in\Lambda)

is a g-frame for
{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
which is intrinsically
s
-localized. Furthermore,
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{W}
are mutually
s
-localized (where
\mathcal{G}
denotes the g-frame from Theorem
3.2
), and for all
\nu_{r}
-moderate weights
m
and all
\frac{2d}{s-r}<p\leq\infty
it holds

\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{W})

with equivalent norms.

Proof.

Let
\Psi
and
\Lambda
be as above. Let
C_{1}
be the constant associated with the continuous embedding
M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
C_{2}
be the constant associated with the continuous embedding
M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow M^{\infty}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see Proposition
2.8
). Upon a rescaling argument we may assume W.L.O.G. that
\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}=1
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
so that
C_{3}:=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\|\phi_{m}\|_{M_{\nu_{s}}^{1}}<\infty
.

Step 1.
We first prove that
\Psi
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Indeed, let
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

\displaystyle\|\Psi\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle\phi_{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle\langle\psi_{m^{\prime}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{m},\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle\langle\psi_{m^{\prime}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{m},\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\langle\psi_{m^{\prime}},\psi_{m}\rangle\langle\phi_{m},\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\|\phi_{m^{\prime}}\|_{M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}}\|\phi_{m}\|_{M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}}\|\psi_{m^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{2}C_{3}^{2},

where the order of summation may be changed due to absolute convergence of the series

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle||\langle\psi_{m^{\prime}},e_{n}\rangle||\langle\phi_{m},\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}|\langle\phi_{m},\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle|\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle\psi_{m^{\prime}},e_{n}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}|\langle\phi_{m},\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle|\leq C_{1}^{2}C_{3}^{2}.

Step 2.
We show that
\mathcal{W}
is a g-frame for
{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Since
\Psi\in{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
according to Step 1, we also have
\Psi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*}\in{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Therefore, since
(\Psi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by assumption, we may conclude from the lifting property (Proposition
3.1
) that
(L_{\Psi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-frame for
{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Step 3.

We show that

|V_{\phi_{k}}\phi_{m}(\lambda-\mu)|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\nu_{s}(\lambda-\mu)}\|\phi_{m}\|_{M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}}\|\phi_{k}\|_{M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}}\qquad(\forall k,m\in\mathbb{N}).

Indeed, since
\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=1
, the STFT satisfies the pointwise estimate

|V_{\phi_{k}}\phi_{m}|\leq\frac{1}{\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}|V_{\varphi_{0}}\phi_{m}|\ast|V_{\phi_{k}}\varphi_{0}|=|V_{\varphi_{0}}\phi_{m}|\ast|V_{\phi_{k}}\varphi_{0}|

(see
[
12
, Theorem 11.3.7]
). Applying Young’s inequality for weighted
L^{p}
-spaces
[
12
, Proposition 11.1.3]
we see that

\displaystyle|V_{\phi_{k}}\phi_{m}(\lambda-\mu)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu_{s}(\lambda-\mu)}|V_{\phi_{k}}\phi_{m}(\lambda-\mu)|\nu_{s}(\lambda-\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{s}(\lambda-\mu)}\|V_{\phi_{k}}\phi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\nu_{s}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{s}(\lambda-\mu)}\|V_{\varphi_{0}}\phi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\nu_{s}}}\|V_{\phi_{k}}\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\nu_{s}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu_{s}(\lambda-\mu)}\|V_{\varphi_{0}}\phi_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\nu_{s}}}\|V_{\varphi_{0}}\phi_{k}\|_{L^{1}_{\nu_{s}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\nu_{s}(\lambda-\mu)}\|\phi_{m}\|_{M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}}\|\phi_{k}\|_{M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}}.

Step 4.
We show that
\mathcal{W}
is intrinsically
s
-localized. We start estimating

\displaystyle\|W_{\lambda}W_{\mu}^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|L_{\Psi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*}\pi(\mu)\Psi}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Psi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*}\pi(\mu)\Psi\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\psi_{k}\otimes\phi_{k}\right)\pi(\lambda)^{*}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle\langle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m},\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k}\rangle\psi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\sum_{m,k=1}^{\infty}\langle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m},\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k}\rangle\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle\psi_{k},\sum_{m^{\prime},k^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\langle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m^{\prime}},\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k^{\prime}}\rangle\langle e_{n},\psi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle\psi_{k^{\prime}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m,m^{\prime},k,k^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\langle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m},\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k}\rangle\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle\langle\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k^{\prime}},\pi(\mu)\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\psi_{m^{\prime}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\psi_{k},\psi_{k^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,m^{\prime},k,k^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\langle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m},\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k}\rangle\langle\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k^{\prime}},\pi(\mu)\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\psi_{k},\psi_{k^{\prime}}\rangle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle\langle\psi_{m^{\prime}},e_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,m^{\prime},k,k^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\langle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m},\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k}\rangle\langle\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k^{\prime}},\pi(\mu)\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\psi_{k},\psi_{k^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\psi_{m^{\prime}},\psi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m,m^{\prime},k,k^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}|\langle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m},\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k}\rangle||\langle\pi(\lambda)\phi_{k^{\prime}},\pi(\mu)\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|V_{\phi_{k}}\pi(\mu)\phi_{m}(\lambda)|\right)^{2},

where changing the order of summation is justified as in Step 1 via absolute convergence of the sum in question. By the covariance property of the STFT
[
16
]
and Step 3, we may conclude from the latter that

\displaystyle\|W_{\lambda}W_{\mu}^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|V_{\phi_{k}}\pi(\mu)\phi_{m}(\lambda)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|V_{\phi_{k}}\phi_{m}(\lambda-\mu)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\nu_{s}(\lambda-\mu)}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\phi_{m}\|_{M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}}\|\phi_{k}\|_{M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{2}C_{3}^{2}}{\nu_{s}(\lambda-\mu)},

as was to be shown.

Step 5.
We show that
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{W}
are mutually
s
-localized. As in Step 4, we have

\displaystyle\|G_{\lambda}W_{\mu}^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle\langle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m},\pi(\lambda)\varphi_{0}\rangle\varphi_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m,m^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\langle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m},\pi(\lambda)\varphi_{0}\rangle\langle e_{n},\psi_{m}\rangle\langle\pi(\lambda)\varphi_{0},\pi(\mu)\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\psi_{m^{\prime}},e_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\langle\pi(\mu)\phi_{m},\pi(\lambda)\varphi_{0}\rangle\langle\pi(\lambda)\varphi_{0},\pi(\mu)\phi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\psi_{m^{\prime}},\psi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}|V_{\varphi_{0}}\pi(\mu)\phi_{m}(\lambda)|\right)^{2}

and therefore

\|G_{\lambda}W_{\mu}^{*}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}))}\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}|V_{\varphi_{0}}\phi_{m}(\lambda-\mu)|\leq\frac{C_{2}C_{3}}{\nu_{s}(\lambda-\mu)},

as was to be shown.

Step 6.
By Step 4 and the preliminary results from Subsection
2.4
, the co-orbit spaces
\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{W})
(
\frac{2d}{s-r}<p\leq\infty
) are well-defined and non-trivial. Since, by Step 5,
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{W}
are mutually
s
-localized, we may conclude from Theorem
2.7
that
\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{W})=\mathcal{C}o_{m}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
with equivalent norms (
\frac{2d}{s-r}<p\leq\infty
). The last part of the statement now follows from Theorem

and Definition

.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

We explicitly clarify the relation between the several different analysis operators encountered so far. More precisely, let
\Phi\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and assume that
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=(L_{\Phi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-frame for
{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Then, by Theorem
3.10
, the analysis operator
C_{\Phi}
from Proposition
3.4
coincides with the (g-frame) analysis operator
C_{\mathcal{U}}
introduced in Subsection
2.3
and their respective action on
F\in\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
equals the operator STFT
\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}F
sampled on
\Lambda
, that is,

C_{\Phi}F=C_{\mathcal{U}}F=(U_{\lambda}F)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=(\Phi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*}F)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=(\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}F(\lambda))_{\lambda\in\Lambda}.

We are going to look at two examples of operator classes, whose operator short-time Fourier transform (STFT) admits an intuitive interpretation. As we will see, these operators also satisfy structural conditions that lead to accurate approximation using only a small number of operator building blocks. In other words, their most significant operator STFT coefficients yield sparse and effective representations.

4
Examples

We give two examples to illustrate the nature of the introduced transforms.

Example 4.1
.

Let
H_{\eta}
be a linear operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
defined via its Fourier-Wigner transform
\eta=\mathcal{F}_{W}(H)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, i.e. we formally write

H_{\eta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\eta(z)\pi(z)\,dz,

where
z=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and if we assume
\eta
is compactly supported in a set
\Omega_{\eta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the integral is well-defined (e.g., in the weak operator topology).

The interesting observation about this operator is the fact, that it has infinite rank. In this sense, it is particularly interesting to consider its approximation by different operator expansion.

We fix a window
\varphi_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(typically a Gaussian) and analyze
H_{\eta}
with respect to the rank-one analysis operator window
\Phi_{0}=\varphi_{0}\otimes\varphi_{0}
:

\mathcal{V}_{\Phi_{0}}H_{\eta}(\lambda)=C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)=\varphi_{0}\otimes H_{\eta}^{*}\pi(\lambda)\cdot\varphi_{0}

Applying this operator to a test function
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we obtain:

[C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)]\,\psi=\left\langle H_{\eta}\psi,\pi(\lambda)\varphi_{0}\right\rangle\varphi_{0}.

Setting
V_{\varphi_{0}}\psi(z)=\langle\psi,\pi(z)\varphi_{0}\rangle
, then under mild assumptions (e.g., Schwartz functions), we can write the inner product as:

\left\langle H_{\eta}\psi,\pi(\lambda)\varphi_{0}\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\eta(z)\langle\psi,\pi(z)^{*}\pi(\lambda)\varphi_{0}\rangle\,dz=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\eta(z)V_{\varphi_{0}}\psi(\lambda-z)e^{-2\pi i(x(l-\xi))}\,dz,

Writing this as a twisted convolution
we obtain

C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)\psi=[\eta\,\natural\,V_{\varphi_{0}}\psi](\lambda)\cdot\varphi_{0}

and each coefficient operator
C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)
is a rank-one operator with Hilbert–Schmidt norm

\|C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)\|_{\mathcal{HS}}=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}=1}\left|[\eta\,\natural\,V_{\varphi_{0}}\psi](\lambda)\right|.

Thus, the operator STFT is pointwise bounded by the twisted convolution of the spreading function
\eta
with the STFT of
\psi
, maximized over unit-norm signals.
If
\eta
is smooth and compactly supported, the twisted convolution with
V_{\varphi_{0}}\psi
is smooth and localized.
In Figure
1
, we show two instances of

\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}F(\lambda)
for an operator
F=H_{\eta}
, which is also depicted in its matrix form. The last plot shows the norms
\|C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)\|_{\mathcal{HS}}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Figure 1
:
Operator STFT of an underspread operator
H_{\eta}
: Upper left plot shows the operator kernel of an operator
F=H_{\eta}
, upper right and lower left show two instances of the operators

\mathcal{V}_{\Phi}F(\lambda_{i})
and the lower right plot shows the norms
\|C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)\|_{\mathcal{HS}}
on a sub-sampled version of
\Lambda
.

In a next step, we compute this explicitly for a Gaussian spreading function
\eta
:

\eta(x,\xi)=e^{-\pi(x^{2}+\xi^{2})}
.
The resulting operator
H_{\eta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\pi(z)\,dz
is trace-class operator with rapidly decaying spectrum.
Using a rank-one analysis operator with a Gaussian window
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, normalized in
L^{2}
, we obtain:

C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)\psi(x)=\left[\eta\,\natural\,V_{\varphi_{0}}\psi\right](\lambda)\cdot\varphi_{0}(x),

with Hilbert–Schmidt norm of this rank-one operator is:

\|C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)\|_{\mathcal{HS}}=\sup_{\|\psi\|=1}\left|\eta\,\natural\,V_{\varphi_{0}}\psi(\lambda)\right|.

Since both
\eta
and
V_{\varphi_{0}}\psi
are Gaussians, the twisted convolution
\eta\,\natural\,V_{\varphi_{0}}\psi
is also a Gaussian, centered near the origin.

Therefore,
\|C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)\|_{\mathcal{HS}}
decays exponentially in
|\lambda|
, and we expect sparsity of the operator representation.

Remark 4.2
.

The supremum

\|C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)\|_{\mathcal{HS}}=\sup_{\|\psi\|=1}\left|\eta\natural V_{\varphi_{0}}\psi(\lambda)\right|

is in general not attained by a specific choice of
\psi
. However, when both
\eta
and the window
\varphi_{0}
are Gaussians, the short-time Fourier transform
V_{\varphi_{0}}\varphi_{0}
is also a Gaussian, and in this case the supremum is attained at
\psi=\varphi_{0}
, up to a phase. Therefore, for Gaussian operators and windows, we have

\|C_{\Phi_{0}}(H_{\eta})(\lambda)\|_{\mathcal{HS}}=|\eta\natural V_{\varphi_{0}}\varphi_{0}(\lambda)|.

Example 4.3
.

The next class of operators we consider consists of time-frequency localization operators, whose spreading function is given by
\eta_{m}=\mathcal{M}\cdot V_{g}g
with
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{F}_{s}(m)
, where
\mathcal{F}_{s}
denotes the symplectic Fourier transform. For a window
g
, the localization operator can be written as convolution, cf.
[
35
]
, so that

H_{m}=m\star\Gamma\quad(\Gamma=g\otimes g)

and since

5
Approximation theoretic results

Here we define dictionaries of bounded operators on
{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and their associated approximation spaces and relate them to the operator coorbit spaces
\mathfrak{M}^{p}
.

5.1
Operator dictionaries and approximation spaces

For simplicity and generality, we first introduce operator dictionaries and their associated approximation spaces for an abstract Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Definition 5.1
.

A countable family
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is called an
operator dictionary
for
{\mathcal{H}}
, if

D_{\mathcal{U}}(c_{00}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}}))=\left\{\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U_{\lambda}^{*}g_{\lambda}:(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in c_{00}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})\right\}

(5.1)

is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Example 5.2
.

Every g-frame
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
for
{\mathcal{H}}
is an operator dictionary for
{\mathcal{H}}
. Indeed, this follows immediately from
D_{\mathcal{U}}:\ell^{2}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
being bounded and surjective and
c_{00}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})
being dense in
\ell^{2}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})
.

Closely related to an operator dictionary
\mathcal{U}
are the sets
\Sigma_{m}(\mathcal{U})
(
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
) defined below.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be an operator dictionary for
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
\Sigma_{m}(\mathcal{U})
is defined as

\Sigma_{m}(\mathcal{U}):=\left\{\sum_{\lambda\in K}U_{\lambda}^{*}g_{\lambda}:g_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{H}}\,(\forall\lambda\in\Lambda),\,K\subset\Lambda,\,|K|\leq m\right\}.

(5.2)

We also define
\Sigma_{0}(\mathcal{U}):=\{0_{{\mathcal{H}}}\}
.

Definition 5.4
.

Given an operator dictionary
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
for
{\mathcal{H}}
,
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the
best
m
-term approximation error
of
f
is defined by

\sigma_{m}(f,\mathcal{U}):=\inf_{h\in\Sigma_{m}(\mathcal{U})}\|f-h\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}.

Furthermore, for
0<\alpha<\infty
and
0<p\leq\infty
, we define the
approximation space

{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{U})
by

{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{U}):=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{H}}:\|f\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{U})}:=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\big(m^{\alpha}\sigma_{m-1}(f,\mathcal{U})\big)^{p}\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\}

and analogously
{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{U})
via the natural modification

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{U})}:=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}m^{\alpha}\sigma_{m-1}(f,\mathcal{U})

in the case
p=\infty
.

The following basic properties are easy to verify, hence we omit the proof.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be an operator dictionary for
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then for
f,g\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the following hold:

(i)

\Sigma_{m}(\mathcal{U})\subset\Sigma_{m+1}(\mathcal{U})
;

(ii)

\lambda\Sigma_{m}(\mathcal{U})=\Sigma_{m}(\mathcal{U})
in case
\lambda\neq 0
;

(iii)

\Sigma_{m}(\mathcal{U})+\Sigma_{m}(\mathcal{U})\subset\Sigma_{2m}(\mathcal{U})
;

(iv)

\sigma_{m+1}(f,\mathcal{U})\subset\sigma_{m}(f,\mathcal{U})
;

(v)

\sigma_{m}(\lambda f,\mathcal{U})=|\lambda|\sigma_{m}(f,\mathcal{U})
;

(vi)

\sigma_{2m}(f+g,\mathcal{U})\leq\sigma_{m}(f,\mathcal{U})+\sigma_{m}(g,\mathcal{U})
.

Having established Lemma
5.5
, the following result follows easily from a general principle
[
2
, p.234]
. For more details we also refer to the master’s thesis
[
21
, Lemma 3.5]
, where the special case of rank one operators
U_{\lambda}
is treated.

Proposition 5.6
.

For each
0<\alpha<\infty
and each
0<p\leq\infty
,
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{U})
is a linear subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{U})}
defines a quasi-norm on
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{U})
.

The next result is shown as in
[
2
, p.234]
as well.

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose that either
\alpha>\beta>0
or
\alpha=\beta>0
and
0<p\leq q\leq\infty
. Then
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{U})
is continuously embedded in
{\mathcal{A}}_{q}^{\beta}(\mathcal{U})
, i.e. there exists
C>0
such that

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{q}^{\beta}(\mathcal{U})}\leq C\|f\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{U})}\qquad(\forall f\in{\mathcal{H}}).

5.2
Best
m
-term approximation

Next, we apply the results from the previous subsection to the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{HS}}={\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
with operator dictionary
\mathcal{W}
given by a g-frame
(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
as in Theorem
3.10
. We call such a dictionary an
admissible dictionary
.

Definition 5.8

(Admissible dictionaries)
.

Let
s>2d
and
\Psi
be an operator of the form

\Psi=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\phi_{m}\otimes\psi_{m}

with
\phi_{m}\in M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\psi_{m}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\|\phi_{m}\|_{M_{\nu_{s}}^{1}}\|\psi_{m}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty.

Assume that
(\Psi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a g-frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\Lambda
is the a lattice as in Theorem
3.2
. Then we call

\mathcal{W}=(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\qquad W_{\lambda}:=L_{\Psi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*}}\quad(\lambda\in\Lambda)

an
s
-admissible dictionary
. In case
\mathcal{W}
is an
s
-admissible dictionary for all
s>2d
, we call
\mathcal{W}
an
admissible dictionary
.

Example 5.9

(Examples of admissible dictionaries)
.

Our toy example of an admissible dictionary is the g-frame
\mathcal{G}=(L_{\Phi_{0}\pi(\lambda)^{*}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
from Theorem
3.2
associated with the Gaussian state
\Phi_{0}^{*}=\Phi_{0}=\varphi_{0}\otimes\varphi_{0}
. As noted in Remark
3.11
, any finite-rank operator
\Psi=\sum_{m=1}^{K}\phi_{m}\otimes\psi_{m}
with
\phi_{m}\in M^{1}_{\nu_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\psi_{m}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(
m=1,\dots,K
) such that
\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{K}\}
generates a multi-window Gabor frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
yields an
s
-admissible dictionary. In particular, if
(\pi(\lambda)\phi)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
(
\phi\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) is a Gabor frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\Psi=\sum_{m=1}^{K}\phi\otimes\psi_{m}
yields an
s
-admissible dictionary. If
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{K}\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then the latter two are examples of admissible dictionaries.

We follow ideas from
[
1
]
, see also
[
21
]
, to derive results on approximation properties of the spaces
\mathfrak{M}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The following lemma from Stechkin
[
41
]
and DeVore and Temlyakov
[
3
]
is crucial for our analysis.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence such that
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq a_{3}\geq\dots\geq 0
, let
0<p<2
, and set

\alpha=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma_{m}(a)=\left(\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(p)>0
, such that

\frac{1}{C}\|a\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}\leq\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\big(m^{\alpha}\sigma_{m-1}(a)\big)^{p}\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\|a\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be an
s
-admissible dictionary (respectively admissible dictionary) and
\alpha=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}
, where
\frac{2d}{s}<p<2
(respectively
0<p<2
). Then
\mathfrak{M}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuously embedded in
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{W})
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.9
,
\mathfrak{M}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{F\in{\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})):(W_{\lambda}F)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}})\right\}
and
\|F\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{p}}\asymp\|(W_{\lambda}F)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|_{\ell^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}})}
. So, let
F\in\mathfrak{M}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
(W_{\lambda}F)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}})
, and let
\tau:\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow\Lambda
be a bijection so that

\|W_{\tau(1)}F\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}\geq\|W_{\tau(2)}F\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}\geq\|W_{\tau(3)}F\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}\geq\dots\geq 0.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, set
P_{m}F:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\widetilde{W}_{\tau(\lambda)})^{*}W_{\tau(\lambda)}F
. Since, by g-frame reconstruction,
F=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(\widetilde{W}_{\lambda})^{*}W_{\lambda}F
with unconditional convergence in
{\mathcal{HS}}
, we may estimate the best
m
-term approximation error of
F
with respect to
\mathcal{W}
via

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(F,\mathcal{W})

\displaystyle\leq\|F-P_{m}F\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}(\widetilde{W}_{\tau(\lambda)})^{*}W_{\tau(\lambda)}F\right\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|D_{\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}}),{\mathcal{HS}})}\left(\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\|W_{\tau(\lambda)}F\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Setting
a_{n}:=\|W_{\tau(n)}F\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}
and
\sigma_{m}(a)=\left(\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we obtain via an application of Lemma
5.10
that

\displaystyle\|F\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{p}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{W})}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\big(m^{\alpha}\sigma_{m-1}(F,\mathcal{W})\big)^{p}\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|D_{\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}}),{\mathcal{HS}})}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\big(m^{\alpha}\sigma_{m-1}(a)\big)^{p}\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\|a\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}\|C_{\mathcal{W}}F\|_{\ell^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{HS}})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\|F\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{p}},

where we used the equivalence of the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{C}o^{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}})}\asymp\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{C}o^{p}(\mathcal{W})}\asymp\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{p}}
according to Theorems
2.4
and
3.7
in the last step.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

Combining the previous result with Proposition
5.6
yields

\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}m^{\alpha}\sigma_{m-1}(F,\mathcal{W})=\|F\|_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\alpha}_{\infty}(\mathcal{W})}\leq C\|F\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{p}}

and, therefore,

\sigma_{m}(F,\mathcal{W})\lesssim\frac{1}{(m+1)^{\alpha}}\|F\|_{\mathfrak{M}^{p}}\qquad(m=0,1,2,\dots).

In other words, any operator
F
from
\mathfrak{M}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(with
\frac{2d}{s}<p<2
) can be approximated by
m
terms with approximation rate
\alpha=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}
.

In particular, the proof of Theorem
5.11

reveals a recipe for achieving this, namely by picking the
m
largest values of the measurements

(\|W_{\lambda}F\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}=(\|(\mathcal{V}_{\Psi}F)(\lambda)\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

and compute the associated truncated g-frame reconstruction series
P_{m}F
.

5.3
Sparseness classes

For mixed-state operators satisfying a mild additional condition, also being an element of the Feichtinger operators, the results in this Section give a sparse description in terms of a representation based on an admissible operator g-frame for the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators.
The key to sparse representations of density operators is the extension of
[
11
, Definition 2]
to the g-frame setting.

Definition 5.13
.

Let
\mathcal{U}=(U_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a g-frame for a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and suppose that
0<p<\infty
and a given weight
m
are given so that
\ell^{p}_{m}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})
is continuously embedded in
\ell^{2}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})
. Then the
sparseness class

S^{p}_{m}(\mathcal{U})
is defined by

S^{p}_{m}(\mathcal{U})=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{H}}:\exists(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell_{m}^{p}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}}):f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U_{\lambda}^{*}g_{\lambda}\right\}

and the associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{S^{p}_{m}(\mathcal{U})}
is defined by

\|f\|_{S^{p}_{m}(\mathcal{U})}=\inf\left\{\|(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|_{\ell^{p}_{m}(\Lambda;{\mathcal{H}})}:f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U_{\lambda}^{*}g_{\lambda}\right\}.

Once again, we employ the theory of localized g-frames (in the case
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{HS}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
) and show that
S^{p}_{m}(\mathcal{W})=\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for admissible dictionaries
\mathcal{W}
.

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
\mathcal{W}=(W_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be an
s
-admissible dictionary (respectively admissible dictionary) and let
\frac{2d}{s}<p<\infty
(respectively
0<p<\infty
) and
m
be as in Definition
5.13
. Then

S^{p}_{m}(\mathcal{W})=\mathfrak{M}_{m}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with equivalent norms.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
2.5
.
∎

Operators in the operator Feichtinger algebra
\mathfrak{M}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are trace class operators and those with unit trace are the class of mixed states, for which the preceding theorem guarantees a sparse representation. This seems to be the first result on sparse representations of quantum states, and we will explore its implications for problems in quantum science in future work, such as quantum machine learning and quantum communication. This is going to pave the way to operator version of well-established compressed sensing algorithms for signals, which should be of interest from an applied and pure aspect.

6.2
Time-frequency localization operators

We look at the sparse approximation of TF-localization operators discussed in Example
4.3
next.
For this class of operators, we discuss the concept of denoising via Sparse m-term Approximation. The reasoning is as follows: TF-localization operators such as
H_{m}
can often be well-approximated using only a small number of elementary time-frequency shifted atoms, i.e., it exhibits a sparse representation in a structured dictionary with atoms of the form
\Phi^{*}\pi(\lambda)^{*}H_{m}
, where
\Phi
is the analysis operator and
\pi(\lambda)
denotes a TF shift indexed by lattice parameters.

In other words, a truncated approximation—retaining only the top
K
coefficients in Frobenius norm—can capture the essential structure of
F
with high fidelity.

When noise is added to
H_{m}
, which may model noisy measurements for example, its energy spreads across many components in the TF domain, and the resulting operator becomes less sparse. However, since the noise contributions are typically incoherent with respect to the TF structure of the clean operator, they manifest as many small-magnitude coefficients. By thresholding and retaining only the
K
largest coefficients, we suppress these small, noise-dominated components. The reconstructed operator, built from the most significant time-frequency atoms, therefore retains the essential TF features of the original
F
while discarding high-frequency or incoherent noise—effectively acting as a denoising filter on the operator level.

The experiments in this section are conducted with operators of dimension
N=144
. The TF analysis is performed on a regular lattice with spacing parameters
a=b=4
or

a=b=2
. The approximation procedure involves computing the operator short-time Fourier transform (STFT) coefficients with respect to an analysis operator
\Phi
, which is taken from various classes, retaining
m
most significant terms, and then reconstructing an approximation
\hat{F}
using these terms.

To empirically validate Lemma 4.8 and Remark 4.10, we conduct a systematic evaluation of best-
m
-term operator approximations. In each trial, we fix the number of coefficients
m
and construct an approximation
\hat{F}_{m}
to an operator
F
using only the
m
largest analysis coefficients in Frobenius norm. The analysis is performed via localized Gabor-type frames generated over lattices
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

The relative approximation error is computed as

\mathrm{AppErr}_{K}=\frac{\|F-\hat{F}_{m}\|_{\mathrm{F}}}{\|F\|_{\mathrm{F}}},

and the coefficient usage ratio is given by
K/|\Lambda|
. Additionally, we measure the behavioral similarity using white noise probes:

\mathrm{WNE}_{m}=\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\left[\frac{\|F\eta-\hat{F}_{m}\eta\|}{\|F\eta\|}\right],

where
\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)
.

The decay of

with

Empirically, the error decays approximately as a power law in

, showing rapid decrease for small

and slower convergence as

6.4
Decay of Approximation Error for operators with smooth symbol

To specifically illustrate the theoretical findings on sparse approximation in operator coorbit spaces,
we conducted a numerical study of best-
m
term approximation for infinite-rank operators
generated by polynomially weighted Fourier–Wigner multipliers.
We use two different types of analysis operators to further illustrate the flexibility of the theory as outlined in Theorem
3.10
, more specifically, a rank-one analysis operator based on a Gaussian window and, alternatively, operators with higher rank, but similar asymptotic localization.

6.4.1
Experimental Setup

We fixed the ambient dimension
N=144
and considered redundancy parameters
a=b=3
,
so that the total number of operator coefficients is
N^{2}/(ab)=2304
.
As analysis window we first used the projection onto a normalized Gaussian, see Figure
8
, and second the projection onto the subspace generated by a random mix of
6
time-frequency shifted Gaussian windows, see Figure
9
. The operator under investigation was constructed by multiplying a random Gaussian field
in the Fourier-Wigner domain with polynomially decreasing weights

w_{\alpha}(z)=(1+|z|)^{-\alpha},\quad z\in\{-N/2,\dots,N/2-1\},

followed by an inverse Fourier transform.

For each decay parameter
\alpha=1,\dots,9
, and additionally for a Gaussian-decaying
reference case, we applied the best-
m
term operator approximation. The algorithm selects the
m
largest operator coefficients
(in absolute value) with respect to the finite-rank analysis mapping.

The resulting approximation errors as functions of
m
are displayed in
Figures
8
and
9
. Each subplot corresponds to a fixed value of
\alpha

(polynomial weight order), and the error curves are plotted against the baseline
case of Gaussian decay. The plots clearly show that stronger polynomial weights
lead to slower decay of approximation error as
m
increases, reflecting reduced
sparsity in the operator representation. In contrast, the Gaussian-weighted baseline
exhibits the fastest error decay, confirming the sparsifying effect of exponential
localization in the Fourier–Wigner domain. It is also interesting to see, that the rank-6 analysis operator yields by and large similar results. This observation will be exploited further, in particular, we will study the impact of data-adaptive analysis operators in future work.

This experiment demonstrates the interplay between decay properties of operator
symbols and the efficiency of best-
m
term approximation. In particular, it numerically
confirms the theoretical prediction that operators with faster decay
in their Fourier–Wigner transform admit sparser approximations in operator coorbit spaces.

\Phi
as well as its canonical dual
\widetilde{\Phi}
are depicted. The lower plots show the approximation quality by means of the corresponding specific frame, when used for approximating an operator with Gaussian decay in the Fourier-Wigner domain.

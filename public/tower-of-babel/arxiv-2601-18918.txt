Center Manifolds and Normal Forms for Nonlinearly Periodically Forced DDEs
Bram Lentjes, Seppe Daniëls, Meinder Follon, Yuri A. Kuznetsov

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to provide an effective framework for analysing bifurcations of equilibria in nonlinearly periodically forced delay differential equations. First, we establish the existence of a periodic smooth finite-dimensional center manifold near a nonhyperbolic equilibrium using the rigorous functional analytic framework of dual semigroups (sun-star calculus). Second, we construct a center manifold parametrization that allows us to describe the local dynamics on the center manifold near the equilibrium in terms of periodically forced normal forms. Third, we present a normalization method to derive explicit computational formulas for the critical normal form coefficients at a bifurcation of interest. In particular, we obtain such formulas for the periodically forced fold and nonresonant Hopf bifurcation. Several examples and indications from the literature confirm the validity and effectiveness of our approach.



{keywords}

Delay differential equations, sun-star calculus, center manifold, normal forms, normalization, nonlinearly periodically forced systems

Consider the autonomous

The next obvious step is to use a similar powerful scheme (center manifolds, normal forms and normalization) for nonautonomous classical DDEs of the form

,

,

Real-world applications of nonlinearly periodically forced systems naturally occur in the analysis of (auto)parametric resonance in mechanical systems and oscillators, a phenomenon with numerous engineering applications. 1.1
Challenges and overview

The paper is organised as follows. In
Section

2
, we prove the existence of a

-periodic

-smooth finite-dimensional center manifold

1
and
2
, to prove a center manifold theorem that extends to a considerably broader class of nonlinearly periodically forced evolution equations, see

2.5
for the general result and
Section

2.5
for some further extensions. In particular, this method also provides a direct proof on the existence of such center manifolds for nonlinearly periodically forced renewal equations
[
10
,
26
,
29
]
. As a consequence, we recover a well-known center manifold result
[
41
, Theorem 3.9]
from finite-dimensional nonlinearly periodically forced ODEs as discussed in
Section

2.6
.

In
Section

3
, we show that the local dynamics near the equilibrium
\overline{x}
of (
DDE
) on
\mathcal{W}_{\loc}^{c}(\overline{x})
can be analysed in terms of
periodically forced normal forms
. We extend the results of Iooss et al.
[
41
,
30
]
on periodically forced normal forms from ODEs to DDEs. To achieve this, we parametrize any solution

of (
DDE

as

x_{t}=\overline{x}+Q_{0}\xi+H(t,\xi),

(3)

where
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}
represents a normal form coordinate,
Q_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}\to X_{0}
is a coordinate map capturing the linear component in the
n_{0}
-dimensional center subspace

, and
H:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}\to X
represents the nonlinear part of the center manifold expressed in terms of the
(t,\xi)
-coordinate system. The normal form coordinate
\xi
evolves according to a specific nonlinearly periodically forced ODE in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}
, where the linear part is given by the associated Jordan matrix associated to

and the nonlinear part can be computed explicitly up to order

. We have found that the (critical) periodically forced normal forms for (
DDE
) are precisely identical to those for ODEs derived in
[
41
, Theorem 3.5.2]
. The main result is stated in
Section

3
. This theorem can be regarded as a periodically forced version of
[
54
, Theorems 24-26]
, where periodic normal forms for bifurcations of limit cycles for (
1
) are derived. An alternative method for deriving periodically forced normal forms for (
DDE
) is proposed in the work of Faria
[
37
]
, which builds on the formal adjoint approach for DDEs developed in
[
40
]
. However, the main result in
[
37
, Theorem 6.4]
relies on the assumption of the existence of a periodic center manifold

, which had not yet been rigorously established at that time. This gap is now closed due to the center manifold result from
Section

2.6
.

In
Section

4
, we first recall the explicit expressions for the (adjoint) (generalized) eigenfunctions for autonomous linear DDEs, which are expressed in terms of the Jordan chains of the characteristic matrix
\Delta(z)\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. In the next section, where explicit computational formulas for the critical normal form coefficients are derived, we will encounter a specific class of periodic linear operator equations. Solutions to these equations can be described in terms of a characteristic operator
\boldsymbol{\Delta}(z):C_{T}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{n})\subseteq C_{T}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{n})\to C_{T}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{n})
, which naturally extends
\Delta(z)
in a
T
-periodic fashion. Despite acting on an infinite-dimensional function space, the key advantage for the analysis is that the (inhomogeneous) periodic linear DDE
\boldsymbol{\Delta}(z)\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{f}
is equivalent to the much simpler algebraic system
\Delta(z+im\omega_{T})q_{m}=f_{m}
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
in Fourier space. Here,
q_{m}
and
f_{m}
denote the Fourier coefficients of
\boldsymbol{q}
and
\boldsymbol{f}
respectively, and
\omega_{T}\coloneqq 2\pi/T
is the angular frequency. Next, we discuss solvability of these equations and invoke a suited Fredholm alternative that will be used extensively in the next section.

In
Section

5
, we derive explicit computational formulas for the critical normal form coefficients corresponding to the periodically forced fold (
55
) and nonresonant Hopf (
60
) bifurcation in nonlinearly periodically forced DDEs. This is accomplished by applying the center manifold parametrization (
3
) together with the homological equation (
HOM
). The expressions for these normal form coefficients are explicit, ready-to-implement in a software package and straightforward to evaluate (numerically). Furthermore, they reduce naturally to the classical formulas for the critical normal form coefficients for the fold and Hopf bifurcation in autonomous systems (
T=0
), see
Section

5.1
and
Section

5.2
for further details. The presented approach is broadly applicable and extends naturally to the computation of normal form coefficients for bifurcations of equilibria of higher codimension.

In
Section

6
, we illustrate our results using two rather simple systems. First, we analyse a periodically forced fold bifurcation in a scalar model equation by explicitly computing the associated critical normal form coefficient. Second, we study the nonlinearly periodically forced Wright equation and compute the first Lyapunov coefficient
l_{1}
for nonresonant Hopf bifurcations. This example demonstrates how periodic forcing influences the classical bifurcation picture of Wright’s equation, leading to resonance phenomena, changes in criticality, and degenerate bifurcation scenarios that do not arise in the autonomous setting. Compared with the derivation of
l_{1}
by Faria
[
37
, Section 7]
, our method is significantly simpler and does not require solving additional boundary-value problems.

In
Section

7
, we conclude the paper with a summary of our main contributions and a brief discussion of the parameter-dependent setting. In particular, we outline directions for future software development, including the incorporation of our methods into existing numerical bifurcation tools.

2.1
Functional analytic framework

Let
T_{0}:=\{T_{0}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a strongly continuous semigroup of bounded linear operators (
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-semigroup) on a real or complex Banach space
X
, with (infinitesimal) generator
A_{0}
and domain
\mathcal{D}(A_{0})
. The
dual semigroup

T_{0}^{\star}:=\{T_{0}^{\star}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, where each
T_{0}^{\star}(t):X^{\star}\to X^{\star}
is the adjoint of
T_{0}(t)
, forms a semigroup on the topological dual space
X^{\star}
of

. If
X
is not reflexive,
T_{0}^{\star}
is generally only weak
⋆
continuous on
X^{\star}
. This limitation is also reflected on the generator level since the adjoint
A_{0}^{\star}
of
A_{0}
is merely the weak
⋆
generator of
T_{0}^{\star}
and typically has a non-dense domain. To address this, one considers the maximal subspace of strong continuity

X^{\odot}:=\left\{x^{\star}\in X^{\star}:t\mapsto T_{0}^{\star}(t)x^{\star}\text{ is norm continuous on }[0,\infty)\right\},

which is a norm-closed
T_{0}^{\star}(t)
-invariant weak
⋆
dense subspace of
X^{\star}
. Moreover,
X^{\odot}
is also characterized by the norm closure of
\mathcal{D}(A_{0}^{\star})
in
X^{\star}
. The restriction of
T_{0}^{\star}
to

defines a
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-semigroup
T_{0}^{\odot}
on
X^{\odot}
, whose generator
A_{0}^{\odot}
equals the part of
A_{0}^{\star}
in
X^{\odot}
:

\mathcal{D}(A_{0}^{\odot})=\left\{x^{\odot}\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0}^{\star}):A_{0}^{\star}x^{\odot}\in X^{\odot}\right\},\quad A_{0}^{\odot}x^{\odot}=A_{0}^{\star}x^{\odot}.

At this point, we have a
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-semigroup
T_{0}^{\odot}
with generator
A_{0}^{\odot}
on the Banach space
X^{\odot}
, mirroring the original structure on
X
. Repeating the construction, we obtain on the dual space
X^{\odot\star}
the weak
⋆
continuous adjoint semigroup
T_{0}^{\odot\star}
with weak
⋆
generator
A_{0}^{\odot\star}
. Restricting this semigroup to the maximal subspace of strong continuity

yields a
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-semigroup

with generator

coinciding with the part of
A_{0}^{\odot\star}
in
X^{\odot\odot}
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the natural duality pairing and introduce the canonical embedding
j:X\to X^{\odot\star}
defined by

\langle jx,x^{\odot}\rangle:=\langle x^{\odot},x\rangle,\quad\forall x\in X,\ x^{\odot}\in X^{\odot},

(4)

which maps
X
into
X^{\odot\odot}
. If
j
maps
X
onto
X^{\odot\odot}
, then
X
is said to be
\odot
-reflexive
with respect to
T_{0}
. In the study of classical DDEs (

2.6
),

-reflexivity with respect to

holds, and it massively simplifies the upcoming analysis. In the absence of
\odot
-reflexivity, it becomes for example less clear under which conditions the weak
⋆
integrals from (
5
) and (
8
) are well-defined, see
[
18
,
48
,
49
,
64
]
and
[
29
, Chapter III.8]
for further details on the non-

-reflexive setting.

With the abstract duality framework in place, we now turn to the analysis of bounded linear perturbations. Let
B:X\to X^{\odot\star}
be a bounded linear operator. Then, there exists a unique
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-semigroup
T:=\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on
X
that satisfies the linear abstract integral equation

T(t)\varphi=T_{0}(t)\varphi+j^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}T_{0}^{\odot\star}(t-\tau)BT(\tau)\varphi d\tau,\quad\varphi\in X,

(5)

for
t\geq 0
, where the integral must be interpreted as a weak
⋆
Riemann integral. Under the running assumption of
\odot
-reflexivity, it is known that this particular integral takes values in
j(X)
, meaning that (
5
) is well-defined. On
X^{\odot\star}
, the perturbation
B
appears additively in the action of
A_{0}^{\odot\star}
as

\mathcal{D}(A^{\odot\star})=\mathcal{D}(A_{0}^{\odot\star}),\quad A^{\odot\star}=A_{0}^{\odot\star}+Bj^{-1}.

(6)

The generator
A
of the perturbed semigroup
T
can be recovered as the part of
A^{\odot\star}
in
X^{\odot\odot}
as

\mathcal{D}(A)=\left\{\varphi\in X:j\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0}^{\odot\star})\text{ and }A_{0}^{\odot\star}j\varphi+B\varphi\in X^{\odot\odot}\right\},\quad A\varphi=j^{-1}(A_{0}^{\odot\star}j\varphi+B\varphi),

and we observe that the perturbation

naturally moves back into the domain of the generator

.

Let us now turn attention to periodic nonlinear perturbations. Let
R:\mathbb{R}\times X\to X^{\odot\star}
be a
C^{k}
-smooth operator for some

, which is
T
-periodic in the first component and satisfies

R(t,0)=0,\quad D_{2}R(t,0)=0,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

(7)

Consider the time-dependent nonlinear abstract integral equation

u(t)=T(t-s)\varphi+j^{-1}\int_{s}^{t}T^{\odot\star}(t-\tau)R(\tau,u(\tau))d\tau,\quad\varphi\in X,

(8)

for
t\geq s
, where

is a starting time. Since the nonlinearity
R
is

-smooth, recall from the mean value inequality in Banach spaces
[
20
, Corollary 3.2]
that

is locally Lipschitz in the second component. A standard contraction argument shows that for any
\varphi\in X
there exists a unique (maximal) solution

of (
8
) on some (maximal) interval
I_{\varphi}\coloneqq[s,t_{\varphi})
with
s<t_{\varphi}\leq\infty
. The family of all such solutions naturally defines a time-dependent semiflow
S:\mathcal{D}(S)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}\times X\to X
defined by

\mathcal{D}(S)\coloneqq\{(t,s,\varphi)\in[s,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\times X:t\in I_{\varphi}\},\quad S(t,s,\varphi)\coloneqq u_{\varphi}(t).

(9)

In the framework of nonautonomous dynamical systems, the function
S
is also called a

, see
[
52
,
1
]
for more information on this topic. Since

is an equilibrium (stationary point) of

, we know that
I_{0}=[s,\infty)
and
S(t,s,0)=0
for all

.

2.2
Spectral decomposition and the linear (in)homogeneous equation

The construction from the previous section is, in general, not sufficient to prove the existence of a periodic smooth finite-dimensional center manifold for (
8
). To guarantee the existence of such a manifold, additional assumptions are required. This necessity was already observed in
[
28
,
29
]
, where the authors assumed the existence of a direct sum decomposition of

supplemented by additional assumptions at the
\odot\star
-level. However, since the structure of

depends on the specific evolution equation of interest, it is essential to compute

and its associated
\odot\star
-tools explicitly in order to verify the required assumptions. For this reason, it is often more practical to formulate the relevant hypothesis directly in the original space

and subsequently lift it to

at the abstract level, an approach also emphasized in
[
49
,
56
]
. Since our linear part is autonomous, we can work with
[
49
, H-II]
as stated below.

Hypothesis 1
.

The space
X
and the
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-semigroup
T
on
X
have the following properties:

1.

X
admits a direct sum decomposition

X=X_{-}\oplus X_{0}\oplus X_{+},

(10)

where each summand is closed.

2.

The subspaces
X_{i}
are positively
T
-invariant for

.

3.

T
extends to a
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-group on
X_{i}
for

.

4.

The decomposition (
10
) is an exponential trichotomy on
\mathbb{R}
, meaning that there exist
a<0<b
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
K_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|T(t)\varphi\|

\displaystyle\leq K_{\varepsilon}e^{a(t-s)}\|\varphi\|,\quad

\displaystyle\forall t\geq s,\ \varphi\in X_{-},

\displaystyle\|T(t)\varphi\|

\displaystyle\leq K_{\varepsilon}e^{\varepsilon|t-s|}\|\varphi\|,\quad

\displaystyle\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\ \varphi\in X_{0},

\displaystyle\|T(t)\varphi\|

\displaystyle\leq K_{\varepsilon}e^{b(t-s)}\|\varphi\|,\quad

\displaystyle\forall t\leq s,\ \varphi\in X_{+}.

We call
X_{-},X_{0}
and
X_{+}
the
stable subspace
,
center subspace
and

, respectively.
\lozenge

We call the zero equilibrium of (

)

when the center subspace

is trivial, and

when
X_{0}
is a nontrivial subspace of
X
. However, this distinction plays no role in the analysis that follows.

The procedure for lifting

. Moreover, we will also require

, H-III]
, which we recall below.

Hypothesis 2
.

The subspace

is contained in

.
\lozenge

{theorem}

[
[

Suppose that

\sigma(A)
be the pairwise disjoint union of

\sigma_{-}\coloneqq\{\lambda\in\sigma(A):\real\lambda<0\},\quad\sigma_{0}\coloneqq\{\lambda\in\sigma(A):\real\lambda=0\},\quad\sigma_{+}\coloneqq\{\lambda\in\sigma(A):\real\lambda>0\},\quad

where
\sigma_{-}
is closed, while
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{+}
are compact. If

, then

on

.

To construct a center manifold for (

), we will be interested in solutions to this equation that exist for all time. It is therefore helpful to write this equation into translation invariant form

u(t)=T(t-s)u(s)+j^{-1}\int_{s}^{t}T^{\odot\star}(t-\tau)R(\tau,u(\tau))d\tau,\quad-\infty<s\leq t<\infty.

(11)

A key challenge in developing a center manifold theory for infinite-dimensional systems is that the linearized version of (

) may admit unbounded solutions in

. This necessitates working within a function space that permits limited exponential growth as

. Therefore, let us introduce for a Banach space

, exponent
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
and starting time

, the Banach space

\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},E):=\bigg\{f\in C(\mathbb{R},E):\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{-\eta|t-s|}\|f(t)\|<\infty\bigg\},

with the weighted supremum norm
\|f\|_{\eta,s}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{-\eta|t-s|}\|f(t)\|
. Before we start studying the inhomogeneous equation (
11
), let us first derive some properties of the linear homogeneous equation

u(t)=T(t-s)u(s),\quad-\infty<s\leq t<\infty.

(12)

The following result is a
s
-dependent version of
[
29
, Lemma IX.2.4]
(or
[
49
, Lemma 29]
) and shows that all solutions to (

) in

belong to
\BC_{s}^{\eta}
. As the proof closely follows that of the cited references, it is omitted here. We also refer to
[

for a similar result in the
T
-periodic setting.

{lemma}

Let
\eta\in(0,\min\{-a,b\})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
be given. Then

X_{0}=\{\varphi\in X:\mbox{there exists a solution of \eqref{eq:homogeneous} on $\mathbb{R}$ through $\varphi$ at time $s$ belonging to }\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)\}.

In the construction of a center manifold, an important step is the introduction of a bounded linear operator that associates with each continuous forcing function
f:\mathbb{R}\to X^{\odot\star}
a solution of

u(t)=T(t-s)u(s)+j^{-1}\int_{s}^{t}T^{\odot\star}(t-\tau)f(\tau)d\tau,\quad-\infty<s\leq t<\infty,

(13)

on
\mathbb{R}
, with specific behaviour at

and
t\to\pm\infty
. In fact, we are looking for a pseudo-inverse of exponentially bounded solutions to (

). To do this, consider for any
\eta\in(0,\min\{-a,b\})
and

the operator
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\eta}:\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot\star})\to\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)
defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\eta}f)(t)&\coloneqq j^{-1}\int_{s}^{t}T^{\odot\star}(t-\tau)P_{0}^{\odot\star}f(\tau)d\tau+j^{-1}\int_{\infty}^{t}T^{\odot\star}(t-\tau)P_{+}^{\odot\star}f(\tau)d\tau\\
&+j^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{t}T^{\odot\star}(t-\tau)P_{-}^{\odot\star}f(\tau)d\tau,\quad\forall f\in\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot\star}).\end{split}

(14)

The following result justifies that
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\eta}
is a well-defined bounded linear operator serving precisely as the desired pseudo-inverse. It is crucial to note that the operator norm

.

{proposition}

Let
\eta\in(0,\min\{-a,b\})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
be given. The following properties hold.

1.

\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\eta}
is a well-defined bounded linear operator. Moreover, the operator norm
\|\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\eta}\|
is bounded above independent of
s
.

2.

\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\eta}f
is the unique solution of (
13
) in
\BC^{\eta}_{s}(\mathbb{R},X)
with vanishing
X_{0}
-component at time
s
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\eta)
be given and note that for a given

, the three integrals appearing in (
14
) define functions
I_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to X^{\odot\star}
for
i\in\{-,0,+\}
. We have to show that these functions are well-defined, continuous and take values in
j(X)
. Similar to the proofs of
[

, Proposition 3]
and
[
49
, Proposition 30]
, we see that the integrands are weak
⋆
continuous and obtain from

\|I_{-}(t)\|\leq\frac{K_{\varepsilon}e^{\eta|t-s|}}{-a-\eta}\|f\|_{\eta,s},\quad\|I_{0}(t)\|\leq\frac{K_{\varepsilon}e^{\eta|t-s|}}{\eta-\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\eta,s},\quad\|I_{+}(t)\|\leq\frac{K_{\varepsilon}e^{\eta|t-s|}}{b-\eta}\|f\|_{\eta,s},\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},

which proves that all integrals are well-defined. An application of
1
and
2
tells us that

T^{\odot\star}(t-\tau)P_{i}^{\odot\star}f(\tau)=jT(t-\tau)j^{-1}P_{i}^{\odot\star}f(\tau),\quad\forall\tau\geq t,\ i\in\{0,+\},

(15)

which shows that
I_{i}
takes values in
j(X)
for
i\in\{0,+\}
. To prove that
I_{-}
takes values in
j(X)
, we rely on
[
29
, Lemma III.2.3]
and
[
49
, Lemma 9]
, see also
[
55
, Theorem 1]
for a
T
-periodic version. From these references, we also conclude that
I_{i}
is continuous for each

, and thus

\|\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\eta}\|\leq\|j^{-1}\|K_{\varepsilon}\bigg(\frac{1}{\eta-\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{b-\eta}+\frac{1}{-a-\eta}\bigg)\eqqcolon\Omega_{\eta}<\infty,

which proves the first claim.

Let us now prove the second claim. A straightforward computation shows that
u=\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\eta}f
is a solution of (
13
). The fact that
u
has vanishing
X_{0}
-component at time

follows from a direct computation of
P_{0}u(s)
in combination with the mutual orthogonality of the projections
P_{i}
. To prove that this solution is unique, let
v\in\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)
be another solution of (

) satisfying
P_{0}v(s)=0
. Then,
w=u-v\in\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)
and

.
Section

2.2
tells us that

and thus

, meaning that

for all

, and so

∎

2.3
Modification of the nonlinearity

To apply in
Section

2.4
a contraction argument for proving the existence of a Lipschitz continuous center manifold, it is necessary that the nonlinear operator
R(t,\cdot):X\to X^{\odot\star}
for fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
is globally Lipschitz. However, since
R
is only assumed to be
C^{k}
-smooth, this condition is generally not satisfied. Nevertheless, since we are primarily concerned with the local behaviour of solutions near the origin, we may modify
R(t,\cdot)
outside a ball of radius
\delta>0
to ensure that the operator becomes globally Lipschitz. To this end, we introduce a
C^{\infty}
-smooth cut-off function
\xi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\xi(s)\in\begin{cases}\{1\},\quad&0\leq s\leq 1,\\
[0,1],\quad&1\leq s\leq 2,\\
\{0\},\quad&s\geq 2,\end{cases}

and define for any
\delta>0
the
\delta
-modification
of
R
as the operator
R_{\delta}\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}\times X,X^{\odot\star})
with action

R_{\delta}(t,u):=R(t,u)\xi\bigg(\frac{\|P_{0}u\|}{\delta}\bigg)\xi\bigg(\frac{\|(P_{-}+P_{+})u\|}{\delta}\bigg),\quad\forall(t,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times X.

Along the same lines of
[
49
, Proposition 32]
and
[
55
, Proposition 4]
, one proves that for sufficiently small
\delta>0
, the operator
R_{\delta}(t,\cdot)
is globally Lipschitz continuous for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
with Lipschitz constant
L_{\delta}\to 0
as
\delta\downarrow 0
. Hence, one derives directly that
\|R_{\delta}(t,u)\|\leq 4\delta L_{\delta}
for all
(t,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times X
, see
[
56
, Corollary 3.1]
for a proof. We associate with
R_{\delta}
the
substitution operator

\tilde{R}_{\delta}:\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)\to\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot\star})
by

\tilde{R}_{\delta}(u):=R_{\delta}(\cdot,u(\cdot)),\quad\forall u\in\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X),

and we note that
\tilde{R}_{\delta}
inherits all Lipschitz properties from
R_{\delta}
, see
[
55
, Corollary 1]
. Let us now also introduce for any
\eta\in(0,\min\{-a,b\})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
the bounded linear operator
T_{s}^{\eta}:X_{0}\to\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)
by

(T_{s}^{\eta}\varphi)(t)\coloneqq T(t-s)\varphi,\quad\forall\varphi\in X_{0},

which is well-defined as
T
extends to a
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-group on
X_{0}
due to
1
. A straightforward estimate using
1
shows that the operator norm
\|T_{s}^{\eta}\|\leq K_{\eta}
is uniformly bounded above independent of
s
, a property that will play a key role in the proof of
Section

2.4
.

2.4
Existence of a Lipschitz center manifold

Motivated by
Section

2.2
, we will define a parametrized fixed point operator such that its fixed points correspond to exponentially bounded solutions on
\mathbb{R}
of the modified equation

u(t)=T(t-s)u(s)+j^{-1}\int_{s}^{t}T^{\odot\star}(t-\tau)R_{\delta}(\tau,u(\tau))d\tau,\quad-\infty<s\leq t<\infty,

(16)

for some small
\delta>0
. For a given
\eta\in(0,\min\{-a,b\})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let us introduce the fixed point operator
\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}:\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)\times X_{0}\to\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot\star})
by

\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(u,\varphi):=T_{s}^{\eta}\varphi+\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\eta}(\tilde{R}_{\delta}(u)),\quad\forall(u,\varphi)\in\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)\times X_{0},

where the second argument must be treated as a parameter.

{theorem}

Let
\eta\in(0,\min\{-a,b\})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
be given. If
\delta>0
is sufficiently small, then the following two statements hold.

1.

For every
\varphi\in X_{0}
the equation
u=\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(u,\varphi)
has a unique solution
u=u_{s}^{\star}(\varphi)
.

2.

The map
u_{s}^{\star}:X_{0}\to\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)
is globally Lipschitz and satisfies
u_{s}^{\star}(0)=0
.

Proof.

Take
(u,\varphi),(v,\psi)\in\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)\times X_{0}
and note that

\|\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(u,\varphi)-\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(v,\psi)\|_{\eta,s}\leq K_{\eta}\|\varphi-\psi\|+\Omega_{\eta}L_{\delta}\|u-v\|_{\eta,s}.

By the results from
Section

2.3
, we know that there exists a
\delta>0
small enough such that
\Omega_{\eta}L_{\delta}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

To prove the first assertion, note that

\|\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(u,\varphi)-\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(v,\varphi)\|_{\eta,s}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|_{\eta,s},

and so
\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(\cdot,\varphi)
is a contraction on the Banach space
\BC_{s}^{\eta}(\mathbb{R},X)
equipped with the
\|\cdot\|_{\eta,s}
-norm. The contraction mapping principle states that
\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(\cdot,\varphi)
has a unique fixed point
u_{s}^{\star}(\varphi)
.

To prove the second assertion, consider the unique fixed points
u_{s}^{\star}(\varphi)
and
u_{s}^{\star}(\psi)
of
\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(u,\varphi)
and
\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(u,\psi)
, respectively. Then,

\|u_{s}^{\star}(\varphi)-u_{s}^{\star}(\psi)\|_{\eta,s}\leq K_{\eta}\|\varphi-\psi\|+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{s}^{\star}(\varphi)-u_{s}^{\star}(\psi)\|_{\eta,s},

which shows that
\|u_{s}^{\star}(\varphi)-u_{s}^{\star}(\psi)\|_{\eta,s}\leq 2K_{\eta}\|\varphi-\psi\|
, and so
u^{\star}_{s}
is globally Lipschitz. Since
u_{s}^{\star}(0)=\mathcal{G}_{s}^{\eta}(u_{s}^{\star}(0),0)=0
, the second assertion follows.
∎

Let us now define the map
\mathcal{C}:\mathbb{R}\times X_{0}\to X
by
\mathcal{C}(s,\varphi)\coloneqq u_{s}^{\star}(\varphi)(s)
and introduce the sets

\mathcal{W}^{c}\coloneqq\{(s,\varphi)\in\mathbb{R}\times X:\varphi\in\mathcal{W}^{c}(s)\},\quad\mathcal{W}^{c}(s)\coloneqq\{\mathcal{C}(s,\varphi)\in X:\varphi\in X_{0}\},\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

We call
\mathcal{W}^{c}
the
global center manifold
of (
16
) whose
s
-fibers
are given by
\mathcal{W}^{c}(s)
. From
Section

2.4
, we observe directly that
\mathcal{C}(s,\cdot)
is globally Lipschitz, where the Lipschitz constant might depend on
s
, meaning that
\mathcal{C}
is only fiberwise Lipschitz. However, along the same lines of
[
55
, Corollary 3]
one can prove that this Lipschitz constant can be chosen independently of
s
, meaning that
\mathcal{W}^{c}
is in fact a global Lipschitz center manifold of (
16
).

Let
B_{\delta}(X)
be the open ball centered around the origin in
X
of radius
\delta>0
. Then,
Section

2.3
shows that
R_{\delta}=R
on
\mathbb{R}\times B_{\delta}(X)
, meaning that the modified integral equation (
16
) is equivalent to the original integral equation (
11
) on
B_{\delta}(X)
. Then, we call

\mathcal{W}_{\loc}^{c}:=\{(s,\varphi)\in\mathbb{R}\times X:\varphi\in\mathcal{W}^{c}(s)\cap B_{\delta}(X)\}

the
local center manifold
of (
11
) for some sufficiently small
\delta>0

\mathcal{W}^{c}
and
\mathcal{W}_{\loc}^{c}
are also called

.

Before we prove the main theorem of this section (

2.6
The special case of classical DDEs

Our next aim is to show that the nonlinearly periodically forced system (
DDE
) admits a center manifold around the equilibrium
\overline{x}
. As already announced in
Section

1
, we will first prove by an application of
Section

2.5
that the translated equation (
2
) admits a center manifold around the origin. The starting point to study classical DDEs on
X=C([-h,0],\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the shift semigroup
T_{0}
defined by

(T_{0}(t)\varphi)(\theta):=\begin{cases}\varphi(t+\theta),\quad&-h\leq t+\theta\leq 0,\\
\varphi(0),\quad&t+\theta\geq 0,\end{cases}\quad\forall\varphi\in X,\ t\geq 0,\ \theta\in[-h,0],

which generates the solution corresponding to the trivial DDE
\dot{x}(t)=0
for
t\geq 0
with initial condition
x_{0}=\varphi
, through the relation
x_{t}=T_{0}(t)\varphi
for all
t\geq 0
. A representation theorem by Riesz
[
62
]
enables us to identify
X^{\star}=C([-h,0],\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\star}
with the Banach space
\NBV([0,h],\mathbb{R}^{n\star})
consisting of functions
\zeta:[0,h]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\star}
that are normalized by
\zeta(0)=0
, are continuous from the right on
(0,h)
and have bounded variation. The remaining spaces can be characterized by

X^{\odot}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n\star}\times L^{1}([0,h],\mathbb{R}^{n\star}),\quad X^{\odot\star}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}\times L^{\infty}([-h,0],\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad X^{\odot\odot}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}\times C([-h,0],\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(17)

where
\cong
denotes an explicit isometric isomorphism. The natural dual pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
between
\varphi^{\odot}=(c,g)\in X^{\odot}
and
\varphi\in X
, and
\varphi^{\odot\star}=(a,\psi)\in X^{\odot\star}
and
\varphi^{\odot}
, is given by

\langle\varphi^{\odot},\varphi\rangle=c\varphi(0)+\int_{0}^{h}g(\theta)\varphi(-\theta)d\theta,\qquad\langle\varphi^{\odot\star},\varphi^{\odot}\rangle=ca+\int_{0}^{h}g(\theta)\psi(-\theta)d\theta.

(18)

The canonical embedding
j
from (
4

j\varphi=(\varphi(0),\varphi)
for all
\varphi\in X
, mapping
X
onto
X^{\odot\odot}
, meaning that
X
is
\odot
-reflexive with respect to the shift semigroup
T_{0}
. We refer to
[
29
, Section 2]
for the proofs of the above statements.

In this setting, the bounded linear perturbation
B:X\to X^{\odot\star}
takes the form
B\varphi=[L\varphi]r^{\odot\star}
while the nonlinear
C^{k}
-smooth perturbation
R:\mathbb{R}\times X\to X^{\odot\star}
, which is
T
-periodic in the first component, is given by
R(t,\varphi)=G(t,\varphi)r^{\odot\star}
for all
(t,\varphi)\in\mathbb{R}\times X
. Here, we used the conventional shorthand notation
wr^{\odot\star}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}r_{i}^{\odot\star}
for all

{theorem}

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is divided into several steps.

Step 1: Existence.
Consider a point
(t_{0},\Psi_{0})\in\mathcal{W}_{\loc}^{c}(\overline{x})
together with an associated fixed pair

is

-smooth, which will be proven later, independent on the upcoming construction. Consider then the continuously differentiable operator

and note from (

\Psi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}\to X

such that

with this additional property will be denoted by

in (

Step 2: Taylor expansion.
Let us write (

We start with recalling the (generalized) (adjoint) eigenfunctions in the setting of autonomous linear DDEs. These can be expressed explicitly in terms of Jordan chains of the characteristic matrix
\Delta(z)

[

for a general result. As our focus in

{proposition}

Let
\lambda
be a simple eigenvalue of
A
. If
q
is a right null vector of
\Delta(\lambda)
then
\varphi
given by

\varphi(\theta)=e^{\lambda\theta}q,\quad\forall\theta\in[-h,0],

(33)

is an eigenfunction of

corresponding to

Next, we turn our attention to solvability of periodic linear operator equations of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star})&\coloneqq\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\odot\star}\in C_{T}^{1}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot\star}):\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\odot\star}(t)\in\mathcal{D}(A^{\odot\star})\mbox{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}\},\\

(36)

where we recall from (

A^{\odot\star}
. A natural extension of this operator, where

is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\odot})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\odot}\in C_{T}^{1}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot}):\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\odot}(t)\in\mathcal{D}(A^{\odot})\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}^{\odot}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\odot})(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq A^{\odot}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\odot}(t)+\dot{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{\odot}(t),

then
\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}
is the unique adjoint of

, where the eigenvalue

Since all ingredients of the linear part are determined, we turn our attention to the normal form.

{proposition}

The periodically forced critical normal form at the fold bifurcation reads

\dot{\xi}=b\xi^{2}+\mathcal{O}(|\xi|^{3}),

where
\xi,b\in\mathbb{R}
and the

-terms are

Proof.

As

∎

To illustrate the resulting dynamics of (

into (

is

for further details. If

In the setting of nonlinearly periodically forced ODEs and DDEs, the bifurcation scenario is slightly different. If

5.2
Periodically forced nonresonant Hopf bifurcation

Suppose that the equilibrium
\overline{x}
is nonhyperbolic with
\sigma_{0}=\{\pm i\omega\}
, where the eigenvalues
\pm i\omega
of
A
are simple and
\omega>0
. Then, there exist an eigenfunction
\varphi
and an adjoint eigenfunction
\varphi^{\odot}
such that

A\varphi=i\omega\varphi,\quad A^{\star}\varphi^{\odot}=i\omega\varphi^{\odot},\quad\langle\varphi^{\odot},\varphi\rangle=1.

The eigenfunctions are explicitly given by (
33
) and (
34
) with
q\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n\star}
satisfying

\Delta(i\omega)q=0,\quad p\Delta(i\omega)=0,\quad p\Delta^{\prime}(i\omega)q=1.

(56)

Since all ingredients of the linear part are determined, we turn our attention to the periodically forced Hopf normal form. In contrast to the Hopf bifurcation in autonomous systems, the periodically forced case exhibits richer behaviour since the structure of
P
from (
24
) depends on the rationality of
\omega_{T}/\omega
. If
\omega_{T}/\omega\in\mathbb{Q}
, then (

) undergoes a so-called
\!r:\!s
-resonant Hopf bifurcation
, where
r/s=\omega_{T}/\omega
and
r,s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
are irreducible (gcd
(r,s)=1
). The bifurcation is referred to as
strongly resonant
when
r\in\{1,2,3\}
and as
weakly resonant
when
r\geq 4
, see primarily
[
75
,
41
]
and secondarily
[
38
,
3
,
59
,
68
]
for further details. On the other hand, if
\omega_{T}/\omega\notin\mathbb{Q}
, then (
DDE
) undergoes a
nonresonant Hopf bifurcation
for which its normal form will be derived below.

{proposition}

The periodically forced critical normal form at the nonresonant Hopf bifurcation reads

\dot{\xi}=i\omega\xi+c\xi|\xi|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(|\xi|^{4}),

(57)

where
\xi,c\in\mathbb{C}
and the
\mathcal{O}
-terms are
T
-periodic. The bifurcation is nondegenerate if
l_{1}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\omega}\real c\neq 0
.

Proof.

As the elements of
\sigma_{0}
are purely imaginary, it is convenient cast (
25
) into an ODE in
\mathbb{C}
, where now
M_{0}=\mbox{diag}(i\omega,-i\omega)
and
P
is a function of
(t,\xi,\bar{\xi})
. Hence, (
25
) shows that

e^{-i\omega t}P(t,e^{i\omega t}\xi,e^{-i\omega t}\bar{\xi})=P(0,\xi,\bar{\xi}),\quad\forall(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}.

(58)

Consider the Fourier series of
P(\cdot,\xi,\bar{\xi})
and let
\alpha_{pq}^{(m)}\xi^{p}\xi^{q}e^{im\omega_{T}t}
denote the monomials of the associated
m
th Fourier mode. Filling this into (
58
), the equation above yields the relation
(p-q-1)\omega+m\omega_{T}=0
. Since we are at the nonresonant Hopf bifurcation, there must hold
p=q+1
and
m=0
, which yields (
57
). As (
57
) is similar to the critical normal form at the Hopf bifurcation in autonomous ODEs, the nondegeneracy condition follows from
[
53
, Theorem 3.3]
.
∎

The real number
l_{1}
is called the
first Lyapunov coefficient
and also determines the direction of the bifurcation as will be explained below.

To illustrate the resulting dynamics of (
57
) near the nonresonant Hopf bifurcation, it is convenient to introduce an unfolding parameter
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
into (
57
) as

\dot{\xi}(t)=(\beta+i\omega)\xi(t)+c\xi(t)|\xi(t)|^{2}+N(t,\xi(t))|\xi(t)|^{4},

(59)

where
N
is
T
-periodic in the first component and continuous. If (
DDE
) is autonomous, then
N
is
t
-independent, and the resulting dynamics near the Hopf bifurcation is well understood, see
[
53
, Section 3.2]
for further details. If
l_{1}<0
, then the system has a stable hyperbolic trivial equilibrium for
\beta<0
. This equilibrium becomes nonhyperbolic and weakly attracting at the critical parameter value
\beta=0
(Hopf point). For
\beta>0
, the trivial hyperbolic equilibrium becomes unstable, but is surrounded by a unique stable hyperbolic limit cycle in
\mathbb{C}
. This Hopf bifurcation is called
supercritical
. Conversely, if
l_{1}>0
, the roles of stability are reversed, yielding a
subcritical
Hopf bifurcation with the corresponding unstable limit cycle existing for
\beta<0
. If
l_{1}=0
, then a
generalized Hopf
(Bautin) bifurcation generically occurs, see
[
53
, Section 8.3]
for more information.

In the setting of nonlinearly periodically forced ODEs and DDEs, the bifurcation scenario is slightly different
[
38
,
75
]
. If
l_{1}<0
, then the system still has a stable hyperbolic trivial equilibrium for
\beta<0
, which becomes nonhyperbolic and weakly attracting at
\beta=0

\beta>0

satisfying

in (

-periodic, the invariant set

becomes generically a helically wound surface that is diffeomorphic to the cylinder

, and thus appears geometrically as a distorted wavy cylindrical surface. In the absence of periodic forcing,

reduces to an exact cylinder as explained above. Due to the
T
-periodicity of

, it also convenient to identify

as a 2-torus within
\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{C}
. This nonresonant Hopf bifurcation is also called
supercritical
. When
l_{1}>0
, the roles of stability are reversed, yielding a
subcritical
nonresonant Hopf bifurcation with the corresponding unstable nonautonomous invariant set
C_{\beta}
existing for
\beta<0
. This behaviour is also illustrated in
Fig.

.

, recall

) yields eventually

\langle\boldsymbol{p},C(\varphi,\varphi,\overline{\varphi})+B(\overline{\varphi},H_{20})+2B(\varphi,H_{11})\rangle_{T}-2c\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\odot},\varphi\rangle_{T}=0,

where
\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\odot}=(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{g})

corresponding to

6
Examples

The purpose of this section is to demonstrate how the explicit formulas (
55
) and (
60
) can be employed in concrete examples of nonlinearly periodically forced DDEs. The first example (
Section

6.1
) considers a scalar model system exhibiting a periodically forced fold bifurcation and shows how a periodically forced cusp bifurcation arises as a codim 2 degeneracy. The second example (
Section

6.2
) investigates a periodically forced version of Wright’s equation, a classical system in delayed dynamics. We show how nonlinear periodic forcing modifies the bifurcation structure by affecting fold and Hopf bifurcations, inducing resonances and changes in the sign of the first Lyapunov coefficient.

6.1
Periodically forced fold bifurcation in a model system

Consider the periodically forced scalar DDE

\dot{x}(t)=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}x(t)+g(t,x(t-1)),\quad t\geq 0,

(62)

where
\alpha_{1,2}\in\mathbb{R}
are parameters and

satisfies the following conditions:
g(\cdot,0)=0
and
g
is
T
-periodic in its first argument but has autonomous linear part in the sense that the map
t\mapsto D_{2}g(t,0)
is constant. Under these assumptions, (
62
) takes the form

\dot{x}(t)=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}x(t)+\beta_{1}x(t-1)+\beta_{2}(t)[x(t-1)]^{2}+\mathcal{O}([x(t-1)]^{3}),\quad t\geq 0,

(63)

where the
\mathcal{O}
-terms are
T
-periodic. Here,
\beta_{1}\coloneqq D_{2}g(t,0)\in\mathbb{R}
as
g
has autonomous linear part and
\beta_{2}\coloneqq(t\mapsto\frac{1}{2}D_{2}^{2}g(t,0))\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
as
g\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
and
T
-periodic in its first argument.

Let us first study (
63
) at the parameter value
\alpha_{1}=0
. Then,
\overline{x}=0
is an equilibrium with associated characteristic matrix

\Delta(z,\alpha_{2})=z-\alpha_{2}-\beta_{1}e^{-z}=-(\alpha_{2}+\beta_{1})+(1+\beta_{1})z-\frac{\beta_{1}}{2}z^{2}+\mathcal{O}(z^{3}).

We note that
\lambda=0
is a simple root of the map
z\mapsto\Delta(z,\alpha_{2})
whenever

\alpha_{2}=-\beta_{1}\neq 1.

(64)

In the special case
\alpha_{2}=-\beta_{1}=1
, the root
\lambda=0
has algebraic multiplicity
2
, giving rise generically to a periodically forced Bogdanov-Takens bifurcation. This bifurcation has been further analysed in
[
29
, Section IX.10]
for the autonomous case of (
62
), meaning that
g
is
t
-independent. Under the assumption of (
64
), we are at a fold bifurcation point with
\sigma_{0}=\{0\}
. The null vectors of
\Delta(0,-\beta_{1})
satisfying (
52
) are given by
q=1
and
p=1/(1+\beta_{1})
so that the eigenfunction
\varphi
of
A
reads
\varphi(\theta)=1
for all
\theta\in[-1,0]
. Our next aim is to compute the critical normal coefficient
b
of the periodically forced fold bifurcation in (
63
) at
\alpha_{1}=0
. To evaluate (
55
), note that
B
is of the form

B(t;\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))=2\beta_{2}(t)[\psi_{1}(t)(-1)\psi_{2}(t)(-1)],

for all
\psi_{1,2}\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)
. Using the formula (
55
) for the critical normal form coefficient, we obtain

b=\frac{\overline{\beta_{2}}}{1+\beta_{1}},\quad\overline{\beta_{2}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\beta_{2}(t)dt,

(65)

where
\overline{\beta_{2}}
denotes the average value of
\beta_{2}
. Note that this formula for

is well-defined due to the assumption (
64
). If
\overline{\beta_{2}}\neq 0
,
Section

5.1
tells us that the periodically forced fold bifurcation is nondegenerate, while a periodically forced cusp bifurcation generically occurs when
\overline{\beta_{2}}=0
.

Let us now study (
62
) on the punctured fold bifurcation line (
64
) in the
(\alpha_{2},\beta_{1})
-plane, where
\alpha_{1}
must be treated as an unfolding parameter. In this setting, (
63
) reads

\dot{x}(t)=\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}(x(t-1)-x(t))+\beta_{2}(t)[x(t-1)]^{2}+\mathcal{O}([x(t-1)]^{3}),\quad t\geq 0,

(66)

where we recall that
\beta_{1}\neq-1
. If
\overline{\beta_{2}}\neq 0
, then the fold bifurcation occurring at
\alpha_{1}=0
is nondegenerate. The normal form analysis from
Section

5.1
tells us that there exists two
T
-periodic hyperbolic limit cycles
\Gamma_{\pm}
of opposite stability for
\alpha_{1}<0
that collide at
\alpha_{1}=0
and disappear for

. This behaviour is also confirmed numerically in

3
for the truncated system (

) with

from (

6.2
The periodically forced Wright equation

Consider the periodically forced scalar DDE

\dot{x}(t)=ax(t-1)[1+\beta(t)x(t)],\quad t\geq 0,

(67)

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter and
\beta\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
is given. This equation can be regarded as a periodically forced version of the well-studied Wright’s equation (
\beta=1
), see
[
57
]
and the references therein for more information. Clearly,
\overline{x}=0
is an equilibrium of (
67
) with associated characteristic matrix

\Delta(z,a)=z-ae^{-z}=-a+(1+a)z-\frac{a}{2}z^{2}+\mathcal{O}(z^{3}).

(68)

Let us now examine the periodically forced fold and Hopf bifurcations in (
67
).

First, (
68
) has a unique trivial root if and only if
a=0
. Under these conditions, we are at a fold bifurcation point with
\sigma_{0}=\{0\}
. The null vectors of
\Delta(0,0)
satisfying (
52
) are given by

so that the eigenfunction
\varphi
of
A
reads
\varphi(\theta)=1
for all
\theta\in[-1,0]
. As
B=0
, the critical normal form coefficient
b
from (

) vanishes and thus the periodically forced fold bifurcation is degenerate.

Second, (

) has a unique pair of simple roots on the imaginary axis if and only if

, where

for some

. Under these conditions, we are at a Hopf bifurcation point with
\sigma_{0}=\{\pm i\omega_{N}\}
. The null vectors of
\Delta(i\omega_{N},a_{N})
satisfying (

) are given by

and

so that the eigenfunction
\varphi
of
A
reads

for all
\theta\in[-1,0]
. Since a nonresonant Hopf bifurcation requires
\omega_{T}/\omega_{N}\notin\mathbb{Q}
, we must assume that

is irrational or not rationally related to
4/(2N+1)
. Our next aim is to compute the first Lyapunov coefficient

for (

) under the aforementioned nonresonant Hopf bifurcation conditions. To evaluate (

), note that
C=0
and
B
is of the form

B(t;\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))=a_{N}\beta(t)[\psi_{1}(t)(0)\psi_{2}(t)(-1)+\psi_{1}(t)(-1)\psi_{2}(t)(0)],

for all
\psi_{1,2}\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)
. Using the Fourier expansions from (
43
), we obtain for
H_{20}
and

7
Conclusion and outlook

We developed a general framework for analysing bifurcations of equilibria in nonlinearly periodically forced DDEs. Our approach combines the construction of periodic center manifolds, the derivation of periodically forced normal forms, and a normalization method for computing (critical) normal form coefficients. We provided a rigorous center manifold theorem within the sun-star calculus framework and demonstrated that the resulting normal forms are structurally identical to those for periodically forced ODEs. In particular, we derived explicit computational formulas for the critical normal form coefficients of the fold and nonresonant Hopf bifurcation.

A natural next step is to extend the present framework to bifurcations in parameter-dependent nonlinearly periodically forced DDEs. This requires the development of a parameter-dependent center manifold and normal form theory in the spirit of
[
8
, Section 3.5]
and
[
41
, Theorem 3.5.2]
. This approach enables the derivation of explicit computational formulas for the critical and parameter-dependent normal form coefficients associated with all codim 1 and 2 bifurcations of equilibria
[
47
,
8
,
23
]
. In particular, it facilitates the computation of normal form coefficients for the (strongly) resonant Hopf bifurcations, which do not appear in autonomous DDEs. For example, this would enable us to extend the bifurcation diagram presented in
Fig.

4
massively. After completing this task, it would be valuable to derive predictors for (secondary) codim 1 curves (of limit cycles) emanating from codim 2 points, as discussed in
[
7
,
6
]
for autonomous DDEs. This would enable the study of bifurcations of such limit cycles, requiring a generalization of the results in
[
55
,
12
,
54
,
7
]
towards nonlinearly periodically forced DDEs. We expect interesting types of dynamics to arise here, as the period of the bifurcating cycle may resonate with that of the nonlinearity. The ultimate objective is to incorporate all these formulas for both periodically forced ODEs and DDEs into software packages such as
MatCont

[
25
,
24
]
,
DDE-BifTool

[
33
,
63
]
or
BifurcationKit

[
70
]
.

The techniques developed in this work are broadly applicable and naturally invite further generalizations. Since the center manifold theorem (
Section

2.5
) is formulated within the abstract framework of dual perturbation theory, it is plausible that the periodically forced normal form result (
Section

3
) remains valid in more and diverse general settings. However, depending on the type of nonlinearly periodically forced evolution equation under consideration, one may need to relax the
C^{k}
-smoothness assumption inherent to classical DDEs. For instance, one could consider piecewise
C^{k}
-smoothness in the context of impulsive systems
[
15
]
or Sobolev-type regularity such as
W^{k,p}
-smoothness for DDEs on
L^{p}
-spaces
[
5
]
or partial delay differential equations
[
73
]
. In the same spirit, we anticipate that the normalization method proposed here can be extended to a broader class of nonlinearly periodically forced systems. Notably, the derived critical normal form coefficients (
55
) and (
60
) are expressed in an
L^{2}
-style formulation, as evidenced by the bilinear pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T}
introduced in (
44
). This structure suggests that our formulas remain meaningful under weaker (regularity) assumptions, such as those arising in the analysis of renewal equations, neutral equations, delay equations with state-dependent or infinite delay, abstract delay equations, partial delay differential equations, or even mixed functional differential equations.

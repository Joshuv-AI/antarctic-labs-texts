Temporal Trends and Future Projections of Osteoarthritis Knee Burden in China: Analysis of GBD 2021
Authors: Fan Yang, Qingmeng Zhang, Jiying Chen, Jun Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundKnee osteoarthritis (OA), a leading cause of disability, disproportionately affects women and the elderly, contributing significantly to health burdens. This study aims to analyze the temporal trend and future projection of knee OA burden in China, using data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021.MethodsThe research employed age‐period‐cohort (APC) and autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) models to examine the effects of age, period, and cohort on the incidence and disability‐adjusted life years (DALYs) of knee OA from 1990 to 2021, with projections for the next 15 years.ResultsFindings show a substantial increase in knee OA incidence and DALYs over the past three decades, particularly among women, with a notable age‐dependent rise. The ARIMA model predicts a gradual decline in knee OA incidence and DALYs after 2021, potentially due to healthcare reforms and preventive strategies.ConclusionThe study underscores the growing healthcare burden of knee OA in China and highlights the need for effective prevention, early diagnosis, and improved healthcare infrastructure to manage this increasing public health issue.

Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) is the most frequent joint disorder worldwide, and knee OA alone accounts for nearly 80% of the global OA burden [1]. Knee osteoarthritis, a prevalent condition among the middle‐aged and elderly population, significantly impacts their quality of life [2]. Affected individuals often experience knee pain and stiffness, which can progress to limited joint mobility and even deformity over time [1, 3]. This chronic condition is associated with systemic inflammation, contributing to a wide range of health issues [4]. The global impact of knee osteoarthritis has significantly worsened, evidenced by a 9.5% rise in years lived with disability between 1990 and 2020 [5]. Despite the lack of nonsurgical interventions to prevent or halt its progression, and the potential cardiovascular risks associated with available medications [6]. It is crucial for the public, healthcare providers, and policymakers to recognize the substantial burden of knee osteoarthritis. China's population, characterized by rapid aging, a significant proportion of elderly individuals, and a large overall size [7], faces a considerable challenge in managing this growing health issue.

Age significantly influenced the incidence and disability risk in patients. However, the interaction of time‐related factors often complicated the isolation of individual contributions in epidemiological studies. An age‐period‐cohort (APC) model effectively manages the interplay among the three factors, thereby clarifying the trends in incidence and disability‐adjusted life years (DALYs) [8]. No comprehensive study has examined the longitudinal trends of knee osteoarthritis across age, period, and cohort dimensions. The purposes of this study were to: analyze the temporal trends of incidence and disability‐adjusted life years (DALYs) of knee osteoarthritis in China from 1990 to 2021 based on Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 data;assess the effects of age, period, and cohort on the burden of knee osteoarthritis using the age‐period‐cohort (APC) model;predict future trends in knee osteoarthritis incidence and DALYs over the next 15 years using the autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) model.

Methods

Date Source

The Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) 2021 by the Institute of Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) is the most extensive effort to date to assess global epidemiological trends. It estimated years lived with disability (YLDs), years of life lost (YLLs), disability‐adjusted life years (DALYs), and healthy life expectancy (HALE) for 371 diseases and injuries using 100,983 data sources [9]. Data were collected from various sources, including vital registration systems, verbal autopsies, censuses, household surveys, disease‐specific registries, and health service contact data. Final estimates were accompanied by 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs), calculated as the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles from 500 draws. Uncertainty was incorporated throughout each stage of the estimation process. Counts and age‐standardized rates were computed from 1990 to 2021 for global, seven super‐regions, 21 regions, 204 countries and territories (including 21 with subnational divisions), and 811 subnational locations [10]. The reference definition of osteoarthritis knee in the GBD 2021 was symptomatic osteoarthritis that was radiologically confirmed as Kellgren/Lawrence grades 2–4 and painful for at least 1 month of the past 12 months [11]. Data on osteoarthritis of the knee in China primarily originate from the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, the China Chronic Disease and Risk Factor Surveillance, the China Health and Nutrition Survey, vital registration in Hong Kong, China, and Macao, China, and pertinent published literature.

The GBD 2021 database was organized according to the 10th edition of the International Classification of Diseases. This study utilized the GBD Results Tool to obtain data on the osteoarthritis disease burden in China. The study focused on China from 1990 to 2021, examining the number and rate of osteoarthritis knee incidence and DALYs across age groups 30–34 to 90–94, considering male, female, and both genders. We reported 95% uncertainty intervals derived from 500 draws from the posterior distribution of each step in the estimation process per established GBD methods.

Age–Period–Cohort (APC) Model

The age–period–cohort (APC) model is a statistical tool used to analyze and interpret the effects of age, time periods, and birth cohorts on various phenomena, allowing researchers to disentangle these intertwined influences. The APC model assessed the distinct impacts of age, period, and cohort on knee osteoarthritis in China from 1990 to 2021. The age‐period‐cohort model analyzes how age, time periods, and generational cohorts influence health outcomes. The age effect reflects variations in knee osteoarthritis risk attributable to biological and physiological changes associated with aging, including cumulative joint degeneration and long‐term mechanical stress exposure. In this study, age‐specific patterns were estimated using GBD 2021 data stratified into defined age groups to assess how disease burden changes across the life course.

The period effect represents the impact of external factors that simultaneously influence all age groups during a specific time period, such as changes in diagnostic criteria, health policies, medical technology, and risk factor prevalence. In this analysis, period effects were evaluated across calendar years from 1990 to 2021 to capture temporal shifts in disease incidence and DALYs.

The cohort effect refers to differences in disease risk among individuals born in the same period, reflecting early‐life exposures, lifestyle patterns, and long‐term environmental or socioeconomic influences. In this study, birth cohorts were constructed based on age and period combinations to examine generational differences in knee osteoarthritis burden over time.

The model is expressed as follows: lnRijk=lnyijnij=u+αi+βj+γk+ϵ In this model, i, j, and k denote age, period, and cohort groups, respectively. R ijk signifies the incidence and DALYs rates of osteoarthritis for the k‐th cohort during the j‐th period within the i‐th age group. The parameters α i , β j , and γ k represent the estimated effects of age, period, and cohort, respectively. The intercept of the regression equation is denoted by u, and ε represents the normally distributed random error [12].

Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average Model(ARIMA) Model

The autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) model consists of the autoregressive (AR) model and moving average (MA) model. The assumption is that data series are time‐dependent random variables, characterized by autocorrelation in the ARIMA model, allowing future values to be predicted from past values [13]. The AR model component is represented by (φ1Y t−1 + φ2Y t−2 + … + φpY t−p + e t ), while the MA model component is (e t − θ1e t−1 − … − θqe t−q ). Here, Y t−p denotes the observed value at time (t − p), with p and q indicating the orders of the AR and MA models, respectively, and e t is the random error at time t [14].

Statistical Analysis

The database employs the IHME Bayesian regression tool DisMod‐MR V.2.1 to analyze the incidence and DALYs of knee osteoarthritis. Age‐standardized rates were derived using the GBD 2021 data for a global age‐standard population. The age‐period‐cohort model was applied by segmenting the data series into consecutive 5‐year intervals from 1992 to 2021. Data from 1990 to 1991 were excluded as they did not form a complete 5‐year interval. Ages were also divided into 5‐year age groups ranging from 30–34 to 90–94 years old. Participants younger than 30 and older than 94 were excluded from the age‐period‐cohort analysis.

The APC study utilized R 4.4.1 software with the “APC” package and Age‐Period‐Cohort Analysis tool. The study examines the incidence and DALYs of osteoarthritis in China, categorized by 5‐year age groups. The cohort (or period rate ratio [RR]) indicates the relative risk of the cohort (or period) compared to the reference cohort (or period), with adjustments for age and nonlinear period (or cohort) effects.

The ARIMA modeling process began with applying the differencing method to stabilize the time‐series data. The best‐optimized model was selected using the auto.arima() function based on the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) [15]. ARIMA analysis and plotting were conducted in R 4.4.1 with the “forecast” and “ggplot2” packages. A two‐tailed p‐value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Results

Changes of Osteoarthritis Knee Incidence and DALYs in China

In 2021, China reported 8,512,396 cases of knee osteoarthritis, marking a 133.16% increase since 1990. In 2021, osteoarthritis knee cases were more prevalent in women (5,327,731) compared to men (3,184,665). In 2021, the DALYs for knee osteoarthritis in China reached 3,554,153 person‐years, an increase of 1165.32 person‐years compared to 1990. In 2021, the DALYs for osteoarthritis of the knee were higher in females (2,269,290) compared to males (1,284,863). In 2021, China's osteoarthritis knee incidence rate was 598.31 per 100,000, marking an increase of 92.79 from 1990. In 2021, the incidence rate of knee osteoarthritis was higher in women (766.97 per 100,000) compared to men (437.39 per 100,000). In 2021, the DALYs rate for osteoarthritis in China reached 249.81 per 100,000, marking a 119.40% increase from 1990. In 2021, the DALYs rate for women was 326.68 per 100,000, surpassing the rate for men, which was 176.47 per 100,000 (Table 1).

Analysis of Age, Period, and Cohort Effects on Knee Osteoarthritis Incidence and Disability‐Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) in China

Table 2 shows that the net drift values of osteoarthritis knee were 0.375 (0.132 to 0.620) per year for incidence and 2.045 (1.909 to 2.180) per year for DALYs. After controlling for period and cohort effects, the incidence and DALYs rates of knee osteoarthritis in China initially increase with age and then decrease. For the period effect, during the period from 1995 to 2000, the rate ratio of incidence slightly decreased, from 2000 to 2010 significantly increased, and after 2010 declined somewhat but remained higher than the initial level for incidence. The rate ratio of DALYs shows an upward trend throughout the entire time period. Regarding the cohort effect, the relative risk (RR) of osteoarthritis incidence remained stable before 1960, followed by a slight, non‐significant increase. In contrast, the cohort RR for disability‐adjusted life years (DALYs) associated with knee osteoarthritis consistently rose from 1990 to 2021 (Figure 1).

Prediction of Incidence and DALYs for Osteoarthritis Knee in the Next 15 Years

The ARIMA model was used to quantitatively depict the trends of osteoarthritis knee incidence and DALYs over the following 15 years. The optimized model for osteoarthritis knee incidence was identified as (2,1,1) with an AIC value of 86.15, following filtration by the auto.arima() function. The ARIMA model (2,1,1) for osteoarthritis knee DALYs, with an AIC of 35.82, was developed by iterating the previously described steps. From 1990 to 2005, the incidence rate exhibited a fluctuating upward trend. From 2005 to 2015, the incidence rate continued to rise, reaching a peak. After 2015, the incidence rate began to decline slowly. After 2021, entering the prediction phase, forecasts indicate that the incidence rate will continue to decline gradually, expected to reach approximately 400/100,000 by 2036. From 1990 to 2000, the actual DALYs rate demonstrated a declining trend. From 2000 to 2021, the DALYs rate exhibited a rapid increase, reaching its peak in 2015. After 2021, entering the forecast period, the DALYs rate began to decline slowly, with an anticipated reduction to approximately 160 by 2036 (Figure 2).

Discussion

Main Findings of This Study

This study provides a comprehensive assessment of the burden of knee osteoarthritis (OA) in China from 1990 to 2021. We found a continuous increase in incidence, prevalence, and DALYs over the study period, with a higher burden observed in women. The APC analysis revealed distinct age, period, and cohort effects on disease burden. The ARIMA model further projected a decline in incidence and DALYs after 2021, although a potential plateau was observed in the long term.

Overall Burden and Temporal Trends of Knee OA

Osteoarthritis is a leading cause of global disability, and knee OA is the most common subtype [16]. The rising incidence places a substantial burden on healthcare systems due to its high disability rate [17]. In 2021, China reported 8,512,396 cases of knee OA, with an incidence rate of 598.31 per 100,000 individuals and DALYs totaling 3,554,153 person‐years (rate: 249.81 per 100,000), all of which increased compared with 1990.

This increasing trend is consistent with previous studies suggesting that the burden of osteoarthritis will continue to rise over time [18, 19]. One important contributing factor is reduced physical activity among patients, which increases the risk of secondary cardiovascular, cerebrovascular, and metabolic diseases [20]. In addition, the prevalence of knee OA increased from 4667.70 per 100,000 in 1990 to 5002.84 per 100,000 in 2021, further confirming a growing disease burden in China.

Sex Differences in Knee OA Burden

Our findings showed higher incidence and DALYs in women than in men. This is consistent with previous studies reporting that women are more susceptible to knee OA due to weaker musculoskeletal support structures [21]. Postmenopausal hormonal changes leading to accelerated cartilage degeneration [22], as well as higher obesity prevalence among women [23], may further explain this disparity. These findings suggest that gender‐specific prevention strategies are needed.

Age Effect and Population Aging

The age effect demonstrated that both incidence and DALYs of knee OA increased with age and peaked around 50 years before declining. This pattern reflects cumulative joint degeneration and long‐term mechanical stress exposure. The findings are closely related to China's aging population, where 167 million individuals (11.9%) are aged over 65 [24]. Increased life expectancy further contributes to the rising burden.

These results suggest that early‐life intervention and midlife prevention strategies may be more effective in reducing long‐term disease burden [25].

Period Effect and Socioeconomic Transition

The period effect showed fluctuating trends: a slight decrease in 1995–2000, a sharp increase from 2000 to 2010, and a moderate decline after 2010. In contrast, DALYs showed a continuous upward trend.

These changes may reflect China's rapid economic transition, urbanization, sedentary lifestyle changes, and rising obesity prevalence [26]. The slight decline in recent incidence may be associated with improved diagnostic awareness, prevention strategies, and advancements in healthcare technology [27].

Cohort Effect and Generational Differences

The cohort analysis indicated relatively stable incidence RR but increasing DALYs RR from 1990 to 2021. Compared with the 1940 birth cohort (RR = 1), later cohorts (post‐1960) showed higher DALYs risk.

Earlier cohorts were more likely to engage in physically demanding occupations, increasing joint injury risk, whereas later cohorts face greater exposure to obesity and sedentary lifestyles [28]. These findings highlight shifting generational risk profiles for knee OA.

Future Trends and ARIMA Projection

The ARIMA model predicted a decline in incidence and DALYs after 2021, potentially associated with healthcare reforms in China. Policies such as the Healthy China 2030 initiative emphasize preventive care, early diagnosis, and improved access to treatment [29]. Advances in pharmacological and non‐surgical interventions may also contribute to disease control.

However, the projected decline may evolve into a plateau, likely due to the continued aging of the population, which may offset the benefits of healthcare improvements.

Limitations and Strengths

This study has several limitations. First, the GBD 2021 dataset integrates multiple sources, including surveillance systems and surveys, which may introduce selection bias. Second, the APC model cannot fully account for unmeasured confounders such as socioeconomic status and occupational exposure. Third, most data are derived from cross‐sectional studies, which may lead to overestimation of incidence and DALYs.

Despite these limitations, this study integrates long‐term trend analysis with APC modeling and ARIMA forecasting, providing a comprehensive understanding of knee OA burden in China.

The burden of knee osteoarthritis in China has increased substantially from 1990 to 2021, with notable age, period, and cohort effects. Although short‐term projections suggest a decline, long‐term burden remains concerning due to population aging. Strengthening early prevention, targeted interventions, and healthcare system preparedness is essential to mitigate the future impact of knee OA.

Future Technological and Policy Implications

Based on the findings of this study, several implications for future technological development and health policy‐making can be proposed. The increasing burden of knee osteoarthritis highlights the need for improved early screening systems, particularly among middle‐aged and elderly populations. The integration of artificial intelligence and big data analytics into clinical practice may enhance early diagnosis and risk stratification, allowing for more personalized prevention strategies.

From a policy perspective, strengthening community‐based musculoskeletal health management and expanding access to rehabilitation services are essential. Given the strong association between aging and knee OA burden, national health strategies should prioritize early intervention programs targeting high‐risk populations, especially women and individuals with obesity. Furthermore, public health policies promoting physical activity and weight control may effectively reduce long‐term disease burden.

In addition, the predicted future decline in incidence suggests that ongoing healthcare reforms, such as the Healthy China 2030 initiative, may be beneficial. Continued investment in non‐surgical interventions, digital health platforms, and cost‐effective treatment pathways will be crucial to further mitigate the societal impact of knee osteoarthritis.

Conclusions

Osteoarthritis knee poses a significant public health issue, with its prevalence growing in China, particularly among women. This trend is likely to persist as China's population continues to age. Efforts to alleviate the impending burden of osteoarthritis necessitate heightened awareness, particularly concerning the identifiability of risk factors, coupled with prompt diagnosis and therapeutic intervention for osteoarthritis. Additionally, the enhancement of healthcare infrastructure is crucial for the effective management of the escalating patient population suffering from osteoarthritis of the knee. The ongoing expansion of national population‐based data collection for osteoarthritis knee is crucial for effectively monitoring the disease burden and addressing the imperative need for enhanced care strategies.

1.
Introduction

Let
k\geqslant 2
be a natural number. Define the
k
-fold divisor function
d_{k}(n)
as the number of ways that
n
can be expressed as the product of
k
natural numbers. When
k
is moderately large, the behavior of
d_{k}
exhibits in many ways a resemblance to the von Mangoldt function, Heath-Brown’s identity
[
6
]
providing an explicit connection between both objects. Consequently, there is an analogous version of the
Hardy-Littlewood prime tuple conjecture
in the context of divisor functions. We record herein the statement from
[
2
, Conjecture 3]
for the sake of transparency.

Conjecture 1.1

(Divisor correlation conjecture)
.

Suppose that
k,l\geqslant 2
are natural numbers, and
h\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Then one has

\sum_{X<n\leqslant 2X}d_{k}(n)d_{l}(n+h)=P_{k,l,h}(\log X)X+O(X^{1/2+o(1)})

as
X\to\infty
for some polynomial
P_{k,l,h}
of degree
k+l-2
.

This conjecture remains wide open for
k,l\geqslant 3
. Nonetheless, some progress has been made for the case
l=2
, albeit with a weaker error term. For instance,
[
3
]
established the above formula when
k=l=2
with an error term of the shape
O(X^{2/3+o(1)})
, while
[
4
,
15
]
demonstrated for
k\geqslant 3
that there is some absolute constant
\delta_{k}>0
such that

\sum_{X<n\leqslant 2X}d_{k}(n)d_{2}(n+h)=P_{k,2,h}(\log X)X+O(X^{1-\delta_{k}}).

We refer interested readers to
[
4
,
10
]
for comprehensive overviews on this topic.

A weaker version of this conjecture considers obtaining asymptotics for almost all
h
in a given region of the shape
[h_{0}-H_{2},h_{0}+H_{2}]
but with weak error terms, the problem becoming closer to the original conjecture as
H_{2}
gets smaller. In this direction,
[
10
]
established

\displaystyle\sum_{X<n\leqslant 2X}d_{k}(n)d_{l}(n+h)=P_{k,l,h}(\log X)X+O(X\log^{-A}X)

for almost all
h\leqslant H_{2}
, provided that
X^{8/33-\varepsilon}\leqslant H_{2}\leqslant X^{1-\varepsilon}
. Prior to this,
[
1
]
had shown the above with the corresponding error term exhibiting a power-saving bound when
k=l=3
and
H\geqslant X^{1/3+\varepsilon}
. Furthermore, the first authors
[
11
]
achieved an analogous conclusion for the range
H_{2}\geqslant(\log X)^{10000k\log k}
at the cost of enabling weaker error terms of the form
o(X(\log X)^{k+l-2})
.

We shall be concerned herein with the problem of restricting the supporting interval of summation to a smaller scale, which aids in better understanding the local behavior of divisor correlations. We should first observe that a direct application of
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
(see
[
16
, Lemma 2.4]
for details) reveals that whenever
H_{1}\geqslant H_{2}\geqslant(\log X)^{10000k\log k}
then the upper bound

\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H_{1}}d_{k}(n)d_{l}(n+h)\ll_{k,l,h}H_{1}(\log X)^{k+l-2}

holds for almost all
x\in[X,2X]
and almost all
h\leqslant H_{2}
. Moreover, experts may notice that the circle method combined with
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
permits one to achieve an asymptotic formula for the range
H_{1}\geqslant H_{2}\geqslant\exp(C(\log X)^{1/2}(\log\log X)^{1/2})
. In this paper, we aim to address the question of establishing the expected asymptotic formula for smaller values of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k,l\geqslant 2
be natural numbers and
0<\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}<1
be small constants. Suppose that
\Phi(X)\to\infty
arbitrarily slowly as
X\to\infty
. Then, whenever
(\log X)^{\Phi(X)}\leqslant H_{1}\leqslant X^{1-\varepsilon_{1}}
and
(\log X)^{1000k\log k}\leqslant H_{2}\leqslant H_{1}^{1-\varepsilon_{2}}
one has

\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H_{2}}\Bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H_{1}}d_{k}(n)d_{l}(n+h)-c_{k,l,h}(\log X)^{k+l-2}H_{1}\Bigr|=o(XH_{1}H_{2}\log^{k+l-2}X),

where
c_{k,l,h}>0
is some explicit constant defined in (
4.22
).

The parameter
H_{2}
in Theorem
1.2
is optimal up to a power of
\log X
, since when
H_{2}<(\log X)^{k\log k-k+1-\varepsilon}
, the main contribution to the average
\sum_{|h|\leqslant H_{2}}d_{k}(X+h)
arises from integers with
(1+o(1))k\log\log X
prime factors, as shown in
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]
. The restriction on
H_{1}
stems from the limitation of the use of the circle method and Matomäki-Radziwiłł theorem.

1.1.
Organization of the paper

We first reduce the question to typical numbers
n
by introducing the function
f_{k}
in (
2.3
) in Section
2
. Then, in Section
3
, we establish a comparison result for short and long averages in terms of the divisor function which serves as a preparation for both major-arc and minor-arc estimates. Our result exhibits a stronger error term to that in the classical Matomäki-Radziwiłł theorem, as it benefits from the use of the Vinogradov-Korobov zero-free region in the context of divisor functions. We present this result in a broader form, anticipating its applicability in other contexts. Since our framework relies on the circle method, we establish the major-arc asymptotic in Section
4
. As far as we are concerned, this is the first attempt to address an additive question with varying support ranges. In order to achieve so, we adapt the long-short interval comparison argument for every major-arc frequency, which transfers the analysis from short exponential sums to long ones, where explicit evaluation is possible. The restriction on the parameter
H_{1}
in Theorem
1.2
is imposed herein to ensure that the error terms when performing the comparison remain acceptable on average.

The minor-arc estimate in Section
5
is inspired by that in
[
11
, Section 5]
, it being pertinent remarking that a generalization of
[
11
, Lemma 5.1]
is not straightforward in our setting. To illustrate the underlying ideas in the minor arc analysis, we anticipate that the goal is to prove

\int_{X}^{2X}\int_{\mathfrak{m}\cap[\alpha-\frac{1}{2H_{2}},\alpha+\frac{1}{2H_{2}}]}\Bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H_{1}}f_{k}(n)e(n\gamma)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\,\mathrm{d}x=o(XH_{1}(\log X)^{2k-2}),

where
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathbb{T}
denotes the minor arcs,
f_{k}:[X,2X]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
is defined in (
2.3
) and
H_{2}\leqslant H_{1}^{1-\varepsilon}\leqslant X^{1-\varepsilon}
. We note first that by extending the integral over
\gamma
to the entire circle, an application of Plancherel’s identity then yields the trivial bound
O(H_{1}\sum_{X/2<n<3X}|f_{k}(n)|^{2})
. This motivates us to transfer the analysis from the dual space to the physical space, ensuring that the summation intervals can be independent of the parameter
H_{1}
. A relevant technical result in this context is that in
[
10
, Proposition 5.1 (i)]
, which asserts that

(1.1)

\int_{\beta-1/H}^{\beta+1/H}\Bigl|\sum_{n\sim X}f(n)e(n\gamma)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\ll\int_{|\beta|X(\log X)^{-A}}^{|\beta|X(\log X)^{B}}\Bigl|\sum_{n\sim X}\frac{f(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
+\bigl((\log X)^{-10}+\frac{1}{|\beta|H}\bigr)^{2}\int_{X}^{2X}\bigl(H^{-1}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}|f(n)|\bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

This inequality can be used satisfactorily to handle the case when
\gamma\in\mathfrak{m}
is highly irrational (i.e.
\left\|q\gamma\right\|>H_{2}^{-1}
or
|\beta|>H^{-1}
in (
1.1
)). However, this approach itself cannot address the estimate for frequencies lying on intermediate ranges (i.e.
H_{1}^{-1}<\left\|q\gamma\right\|\leqslant H_{2}^{-1}
), which remain a challenge in our setting. In order to illustrate the discussion, we sketch our strategy to overcome this problem and assume for simplicity that
q=1
. We decompose these arcs into short subintervals
I_{i}=[\gamma_{i}-\eta_{i}\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i}+\eta_{i}\gamma_{i}]
, where
\gamma_{i}
is the central frequency of
I_{i}
and
\eta_{i}
is a parameter tending to zero. Applying (
1.1
) to each
I_{i}
ensures that the second term remains negligible. Meanwhile, for arcs
I_{i}
corresponding to medium frequencies, one can obtain a sharper upper bound for the first term in (
1.1
) which offsets the loss incurred by decomposing into shorter arcs. We believe that this approach extends the applicability of Matomäki-Radziwiłł construction. In particular, our method provides an alternative proof of the minor-arc estimate in
[
11
]
.

In Section
6
, we derive the specific mean value estimates, which are essentially a generalization of
[
11
, Proposition 3.6]
and are referred to as the large values estimates here. Finally, we combine all the conclusions together in Section
7
to deliver the main result of the paper.

Preliminaries and technical reduction

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
X
be sufficiently large. Suppose that
\Psi(X)\to 0
very slowly with
X\to\infty
and
\Phi(X)\to\infty
is the function defined in Theorem
1.2
. Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
be a small fixed constant. We then denote
S_{k}
as the set of integers

that satisfy the two conditions below.

•

Let

\displaystyle P_{1^{(1)}}

\displaystyle:=(\log X)^{\Psi(X)},

\displaystyle Q_{1^{(1)}}:=(\log X)^{80k\log k},

(2.1)

\displaystyle P_{1^{(2)}}

\displaystyle:=(\log X)^{10000k\log k},

\displaystyle Q_{1^{(2)}}:=(\log X)^{0.1\Phi(X)}.

We also define

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle:=\exp\bigl((\log\log X)^{2}\bigr),

\displaystyle Q_{3}:=\exp\bigl((\log\log X)^{5/2}\bigr),

(2.2)

\displaystyle P_{3}

\displaystyle:=\exp\bigl((\log X)^{3/4}\bigr),

\displaystyle Q_{3}:=\exp\bigl((\log X)^{5/6}\bigr).

Write

as

where the prime factors of each

(1\leqslant i\leqslant 3)

for

.

•

The total number of prime factors of

, and the total number of prime factors of

Now we set

\displaystyle f_{k}(n)=d_{k}(n)1_{S_{k}}(n).

Remark
.

The above construction of

actually constitutes a modification of the set
S_{k,X}
in the paper
[

, pages 806-807]
. In particular, the function
\Psi(X)
plays the role of balancing the minor arc and large value estimates, as will become apparent in Section

.

The two conditions in the second assumption serve different purposes. The first one allows our local correlation parameter

to be of the shape

rather than
O(\log^{Ck^{2}}X)
, where

is an absolute constant. The second condition is employed to construct a majorant function for

. This majorant function is essential to establish mean value estimates without logarithmic power loss, as discussed in

.

The lemma below partially illustrates that in divisor correlation problems, no information is lost when restricting

to the set

. We shall henceforth write

to denote

.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
k,l\geqslant 2
and
\log^{1000k\log k}X\leqslant H_{2}\leqslant H_{1}^{1-\varepsilon_{1}}
with
H_{1}\leqslant X^{1-\varepsilon_{2}}
. Let
f_{k}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
be the function defined in (

). One then has

\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H_{2}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H_{1}}|d_{k}(n)-f_{k}(n)|d_{l}(n+h)=o(XH_{1}H_{2}\log^{k+l-2}X).

Proof.

By switching the order of summations one may find that

\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H_{2}}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H_{1}}|d_{k}(n)-f_{k}(n)|d_{l}(n+h)\\
=\sum_{X<n\leqslant 2X+H_{1}}\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H_{2}}|d_{k}(n)-f_{k}(n)|d_{l}(n+h)\sum_{n-H_{1}\leqslant x<n}1,

the latter term being bounded by
o(XH_{1}H_{2}\log^{k+l-2}X)
in light of
[
11
, Lemma 3.3]
.
∎

We then claim that in order to prove Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to demonstrate the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
k,l\geqslant 2
and
f_{k},f_{l}:[X,2X]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
are functions defined as in (
2.3
). Then when
(\log X)^{\Phi(X)}\leqslant H_{1}\leqslant X^{1-\varepsilon_{1}}
and
\log^{1000k\log k}X\leqslant H_{2}\leqslant H_{1}^{1-\varepsilon_{2}}
one has

\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H_{2}}\bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H_{1}}f_{k}(n)f_{l}(n+h)-c_{h,k,l}H_{1}\log^{k+l-2}X\bigr|=o(XH_{1}H_{2}\log^{k+l-2}X).

Indeed, upon noting that

pointwise, it transpires that the employment of Lemma
2.1
in conjunction with Proposition

, a symmetry argument and the triangle inequality entails

\sum_{x\sim X}\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H_{2}}\bigl|\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H_{1}}d_{k}(n)d_{l}(n+h)-c_{h,k,l}H_{1}\log^{k+l-2}X\bigr|\\

3.
Distribution of divisor function twisted by Dirichelet characters in almost all very short intervals

We start this section with a lemma that is crucial for building our Matomäki-Radziwiłł type conclusion for divisor functions.

Lemma 3.1

(Dirichlet polynomial of primes)
.

Suppose that
2/3<\theta<1
and
\exp((\log X)^{\theta})\leqslant P\leqslant Q\leqslant X
. Suppose that
t\in\mathbb{R}
with
|t|\leqslant X^{1+\varepsilon}
and
\chi
is a Dirichlet character with conductor
q\leqslant\log^{100k\log k}X
. Then there is some positive constant
c>0
such that

\Bigl|\sum_{P\leqslant p\leqslant Q}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+it}}\Bigr|\ll\frac{\log X}{1+|t|}+\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c(\log X)^{\theta-2/3}}{(\log\log X)^{1/3}}\Bigr).

Moreover, when
\chi
is not the principal character one has

\Bigl|\sum_{P\leqslant p\leqslant Q}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+it}}\Bigr|\ll\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c(\log X)^{\theta-2/3}}{(\log\log X)^{1/3}}\Bigr).

Proof.

We first introduce the so called Vinogradov-Korobov zero-free region. Suppose first that
|\Im s+t|\geqslant 10
. By
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
and the assumption
q\leqslant(\log X)^{100k\log k}
, one may deduce that
L(s+1+it,\chi)
does not vanish in the region

\Re s\geqslant-\frac{c_{1}}{(\log X)^{2/3}(\log\log X)^{1/3}}\quad 10\leqslant|\Im s+t|\leqslant X^{1+\varepsilon},

for some constant
c_{1}>0
. On the other hand, if
|\Im s+t|\leqslant 10
then the application of
[
12
, Corollaries 11.8 and 11.15]
shows that for any
\varepsilon>0
there is another constant
c_{2}=c_{2}(\varepsilon)>0
such that
L(s+1+it,\chi)\neq 0
whenever
\Re s\geqslant-c_{2}/q^{\varepsilon}.

Therefore, combining the preceding cases, we can always conclude that
L(s+1+it,\chi)\neq 0
in the region

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}:|\Im s+t|\leqslant X^{1+\varepsilon},\quad\Re s\geqslant-\sigma_{0}=-\frac{c^{\prime}}{q^{\varepsilon}+(\log X)^{2/3}(\log\log X)^{1/3}},

where
c^{\prime}>0
is an absolute constant. If
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
, this implies that
\log L(s+1+it,\chi)
is well-defined in the region
\mathcal{R}_{1}
. Furthermore, by
[
12
, Theorem 11.4 (11.6)]
the upper bound

(3.1)

\displaystyle|\log L(s+1+it,\chi)|\ll\log^{2}X

holds for any character
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
. We aim to make further progress in the lemma by expressing the above Dirichlet polynomial as a contour integral involving the function
\log L(s+1+it,\chi)
.

We first deal with the case when
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that the fractional part of both
P
and
Q
are
1/2
, and take a small constant
0<\kappa<1/2
. It then follows from the application of the truncated Perron’s formula (e.g.
[
12
, Theorems 5.2 and 5.3]
) that

\sum_{P\leqslant p\leqslant Q}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+it}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\kappa-iX}^{\kappa+iX}\log L(s+1+it,\chi)\frac{Q^{s}-P^{s}}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+O\bigl(\frac{Q^{\kappa}}{X}\sum_{p\geqslant 1}p^{-1-\kappa}\bigr)\\
+O\Bigl(\sum_{n\sim Q}n^{-1}\min\left\{1,\frac{Q}{X|Q-n|}\right\}\Bigr)+O\Bigl(\sum_{n\sim P}n^{-1}\min\left\{1,\frac{P}{X|P-n|}\right\}\Bigr).

The first error term in the above equation is
O(Q^{\kappa}/X)
. In order to deal with the second one, one may recall that
1/2\leqslant\lvert Q-n\rvert
by the above assumption to deduce that such an error term is
O(X^{-1}\log Q)
, an analogous calculation entailing that the third one is
O(X^{-1}\log P)
. Therefore, we have

\sum_{P\leqslant p\leqslant Q}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+it}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\kappa-iX}^{\kappa+iX}\log L(s+1+it,\chi)\frac{Q^{s}-P^{s}}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+O(X^{-1/2}).

In light of (
3.1
) and the assumption
\kappa<1/2
, we may shift the contour to the edge of the region
\mathcal{R}_{1}
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{P\leqslant p\leqslant Q}

\displaystyle\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+it}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-X}^{X}\log L(1-\sigma_{0}+i(t+u),\chi)\frac{Q^{-\sigma_{0}+iu}-P^{-\sigma_{0}+iu}}{-\sigma_{0}+iu}\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\pm\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\sigma_{0}}^{\kappa}\log L(1+\sigma\pm i(t+X),\chi)\frac{Q^{\sigma\pm iX}-P^{\sigma\pm iX}}{\sigma\pm iX}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+O(X^{-1/2}).

We then take absolute values inside the integral to conclude that

\Bigl|\sum_{P\leqslant p\leqslant Q}\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{1+it}}\Bigr|\ll P^{-\sigma_{0}}\log^{3}X+X^{-1/2}\ll\exp\Bigl(-\frac{c(\log X)^{\theta-2/3}}{(\log\log X)^{1/3}}\Bigr)

recalling that
X\geqslant Q\geqslant P\geqslant\exp((\log X)^{\theta})
and
2/3<\theta\leqslant 1
, and taking
c=c^{\prime}/2
.

Now let’s briefly prove the estimate when
\chi=\chi_{0}
. We first note that the prime number theorem entails that the Dirichlet polynomial at hand is
O(\log\log Q-\log\log P)
whenever
|t|\leqslant 20
. Thus, in what follows, we may always assume that
20<|t|\leqslant X^{1+\varepsilon}
.

Let
\delta=(\log X)^{-1}
and
T=|t|-10
. Then, an application of the truncated version of Perron’s formula delivers

\sum_{P\leqslant p\leqslant Q}\frac{\chi_{0}(p)}{p^{1+it}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\delta-iT}^{\delta+iT}\log L(s+1+it,\chi_{0})\frac{Q^{s}-P^{s}}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+O\bigl(T^{-1}\sum_{p\geqslant 1}p^{-1-\delta}\bigr)\\
+O\Bigl(T^{-1}\sum_{1/2<\lvert n-Q\rvert\leqslant Q}(n-Q)^{-1}+T^{-1}\sum_{1/2<\lvert n-P\rvert\leqslant P}(n-P)^{-1}\Bigr).

The same argument as above permits one to deduce that the preceding error term is
O(\log X/(|t|+1))
. Note that in the above integral
1\leqslant|\Im s+t|\leqslant 2X^{1+\varepsilon}
. Since
L(s+1+it,\chi_{0})
has no poles under such assumptions, then
\log L(s+1+it,\chi_{0})
is well-defined in the region

\mathcal{R}_{2}:1\leqslant|\Im s+t|\leqslant 2X^{1+\varepsilon},\quad\Re s\geqslant-\sigma_{0},

and moreover
|\log L(s+1+it,\chi_{0})|\ll\log^{2}X
whenever
s\in\mathcal{R}_{2}
. Therefore, as shown in the case of non-principal characters, by shifting the contour to the edge of
\mathcal{R}_{2}
we thereby conclude that

\Bigl|\sum_{P\leqslant p\leqslant Q}\frac{\chi_{0}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\Bigr|\ll\frac{\log X}{1+|t|}+\exp\bigl(-\frac{c(\log X)^{\theta-2/3}}{(\log\log X)^{1/3}}\bigr).

The lemma follows by combining the conclusions from the cases
|t|\leqslant 20
and
|t|>20
.
∎

The next result shows that when the range of summation is appropriately large, the average of the divisor function twisted by non-principal characters of small conductor has strongly logarithmic decay. This proposition will play a crucial role both when deriving our Matomäki-Radziwiłł-type conclusion for divisor functions and performing the major arc analysis.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
1\leqslant q,q_{0}\leqslant\log^{100k\log k}X
are integers,
A>0
is a fixed constant and
T\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert T\rvert\leqslant X
. Let
f_{k}:[X,2X]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
be the function defined in (
2.3
). Then, whenever
X\log^{-A}X\leqslant Y\leqslant X
one has

(1)

\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}\,\,(\text{\rm mod}\,\,q)}\bigl|\sum_{X/q_{0}<n\leqslant X/q_{0}+Y}f_{k}(q_{0}n)\chi(n)n^{iT}\bigr|\ll Y\log^{-K}X;

(2)

\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}\,\,(\text{\rm mod}\,\,q)}\bigl|\sum_{X/q_{0}<n\leqslant X/q_{0}+Y}d_{k}(q_{0}n)\chi(n)n^{iT}\bigr|\ll Y\log^{-K}X,

where
K>1
is an arbitrarily large constant.

Proof.

The assertions follow immediately from the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 4.4]
.
∎

We have so far completed all the essential preliminary work, and we are now prepared to present the main conclusion of this section. Since this will be employed in different contexts and with various parameters, we aim to provide a fairly general result. For such purposes it seems worth introducing beforehand some notation. Suppose that
P_{1},Q_{1}
are parameters satisfying

, and recall

Proposition 3.3

(Long-short averages)
.

Remark
.

Proposition

provides a stronger version of the short average results for divisor functions, meaning that the error term is significantly sharper than that of

. Generally, it is difficult to achieve such a strong error term for general divisor-bounded multiplicative functions. We note, however, that

To prove the above proposition, we need to record the following mean value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials.

Lemma 3.4

(Log-free mean value theorem)
.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
. Let

be a sequence with

. Let

\sum_{\chi\,\,(\text{\rm mod}\,\,q)}\int_{-T}^{T}\Bigl|\sum_{X<n\leqslant X+Y}\frac{a(n)\chi(n)}{n^{1+it}}\Bigr|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}t\\
\ll\frac{T\varphi(q)}{X^{2}}\sum_{X<n\leqslant X+Y}|a(n)|^{2}+\frac{Y}{X}\Bigl(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\Bigr)^{2}\prod_{p\leqslant 2X\atop p\nmid q}\Bigl(1+\frac{2k-2}{p}\Bigr).

Proof.

The desired estimate holds by following the argument of
[

, Lemma 5.2]
with

. ∎

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

Let
t_{\chi}
denote (one of) the numbers

that minimize the distance

\sum_{p\leqslant 2X}\frac{g(p)-\Re g(p)\chi(p)p^{iT_{0}-it}}{p}.

We plan to prove the proposition by demonstrating the claims

\sum_{\chi\,\,(\text{\rm mod}\,\,q)}\int_{X}^{2X}\Bigl|\frac{1}{H}\sum_{\frac{x}{q_{0}}<n\leqslant\frac{x}{q_{0}}+H}g(q_{0}n)\chi(n)n^{iT_{0}}-H^{-1}\int_{\frac{x}{q_{0}}}^{\frac{x}{q_{0}}+H}u^{it_{\chi}}\,\,\mathrm{d}u\\
\cdot Y^{-1}\sum_{\frac{x}{q_{0}}<n\leqslant\frac{x}{q_{0}}+Y}g(q_{0}n)\chi(n)n^{i(T_{0}-t_{\chi})}\Bigr|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\ll P_{1}^{-1/8}X\log^{2k-2}X,

and

\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}\,\,(\text{\rm mod}\,\,q)}\bigl|\sum_{x/q_{0}<n\leqslant x/q_{0}+Y}g(q_{0}n)\chi(n)n^{i(T_{0}-t_{\chi})}\bigr|\ll P_{1}^{-1/8}Y\log^{k-1}X.

The first conclusion then follows immediately from the first claim, noting that
t_{\chi_{0}}=T_{0}
. The second conclusion is derived by combining the two claims and applying the triangle inequality. Furthermore, it is worth noting that the second claim is essentially a direct consequence of Proposition
3.2
, given that
|T_{0}-t_{\chi}|\leqslant 2X
and
P_{1}\leqslant\log^{10000k\log k}X
. Therefore, it suffices to prove only the first one.

Similarly to the approach employed in the proof of
[
16
, Proposition 3.3]
, we set a parameter
W=\log^{10000k\log k}X
and define the Dirichlet polynomial
G(s)
as

G(s)=\sum_{n\sim X/q_{0}}\frac{g(q_{0}n)\chi(n)n^{iT_{0}}}{n^{s}}.

Applying Perron’s formula to each short average we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{x}{q_{0}}<n\leqslant\frac{x}{q_{0}}+H}g(q_{0}n)\chi(n)n^{iT_{0}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|t-t_{\chi}|\leqslant W}G(1+it)\frac{(\frac{x}{q_{0}}+H)^{1+it}-\bigl(\frac{x}{q_{0}}\bigr)^{1+it}}{1+it}\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|t-t_{\chi}|>W}G(1+it)\frac{(\frac{x}{q_{0}}+H)^{1+it}-\bigl(\frac{x}{q_{0}}\bigr)^{1+it}}{1+it}\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

By making the change of variables
t\to t+t_{\chi}
, we express the inner quotient as an integral of a phase function, namely

\frac{H^{-1}}{2\pi i}\int_{|t-t_{\chi}|\leqslant W}G(1+it)\frac{(x/q_{0}+H)^{1+it}-(x/q_{0})^{1+it}}{1+it}\,\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=\frac{H^{-1}}{2\pi i}\int_{|t|\leqslant W}G(1+i(t+t_{\chi}))\int_{x/q_{0}}^{x/q_{0}+H}u^{i(t+t_{\chi})}\,\,\mathrm{d}u\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since Taylor expansion yields
u^{it}=(x/q_{0})^{it}+O(H|t|q_{0}/x)
whenever
x/q_{0}<u\leqslant x/q_{0}+H
, the above integral equals

\displaystyle\frac{H^{-1}}{2\pi i}\int_{x/q_{0}}^{x/q_{0}+H}u^{it_{\chi}}\,\,\mathrm{d}u\cdot

\displaystyle\int_{|t|\leqslant W}G(1+i(t+t_{\chi}))\Big(\frac{x}{q_{0}}\Big)^{it}\,\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.2)

\displaystyle+O\Bigl(\frac{Hq_{0}}{x}\int_{|t|\leqslant W}|t||G(1+i(t+t_{\chi}))|\,\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr).

We shall first examine the above error term. Indeed, the application of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality permits one to deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\,\,(\text{\rm mod}\,\,q)}\int_{X}^{2X}

\displaystyle\Bigl|\frac{q_{0}H}{x}\int_{|t|\leqslant W}|t||G(1+i(t+t_{\chi}))|\,\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\ll\frac{q_{0}^{2}W^{3}H^{2}}{X}\sum_{\chi\,\,(\text{\rm mod}\,\,q)}\int_{|t|\leqslant W}|G(1+i(t+t_{\chi}))|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

We then apply Lemma
3.4
for the choice

, upon noting that

, to bound the above expression by

\ll\frac{q_{0}^{2}W^{3}H^{2}}{X}\bigl(\frac{W}{X}d_{k}(q_{0})^{2}\log^{k^{2}}X+\log^{2k-2}X\bigr)\ll X^{1-\varepsilon},

where we employed the fact that
q_{0},W,P_{1}\ll\log^{10000k\log k}X
and
H\leqslant X^{1-\varepsilon}
.

For each long average involving the character
\chi\,\,(\text{\rm mod}\,\,q)
, another application of Perron’s formula entails

H^{-1}\int_{x/q_{0}}^{x/q_{0}+H}u^{it_{\chi}}\,\,\mathrm{d}u\cdot Y^{-1}\sum_{x/q_{0}<n\leqslant x/q_{0}+Y}g(q_{0}n)\chi(n)n^{i(T_{0}-t_{\chi})}\\
=\frac{H^{-1}}{2\pi i}\int_{\frac{x}{q_{0}}}^{\frac{x}{q_{0}}+H}u^{it_{\chi}}\,\,\mathrm{d}u\cdot\Bigl(\int_{|t|\leqslant W}+\int_{|t|>W}\Bigr)G(1+i(t+t_{\chi}))\frac{(\frac{x}{q_{0}}+Y)^{1+it}-(\frac{x}{q_{0}})^{1+it}}{Y(1+it)}\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

It is then worth observing that since the Taylor expansion leads to

\frac{(\frac{x}{q_{0}}+Y)^{1+it}-(\frac{x}{q_{0}})^{1+it}}{Y(1+it)}=\Big(\frac{x}{q_{0}}\Big)^{it}(1+O(Y|t|q_{0}/x)),

one can deduce that

H^{-1}\int_{\frac{x}{q_{0}}}^{\frac{x}{q_{0}}+H}u^{it_{\chi}}\,\,\mathrm{d}u\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|t|\leqslant W}G(1+i(t+t_{\chi}))\frac{(\frac{x}{q_{0}}+Y)^{1+it}-(\frac{x}{q_{0}})^{1+it}}{Y(1+it)}\,\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=H^{-1}\int_{\frac{x}{q_{0}}}^{\frac{x}{q_{0}}+H}u^{it_{\chi}}\,\,\mathrm{d}u\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|t|\leqslant W}G(1+i(t+t_{\chi}))\Big(\frac{x}{q_{0}}\Big)^{it}\,\,\mathrm{d}t\\
+O\Bigl(\frac{Y}{x/q_{0}}\int_{|t|\leqslant W}|t||G(1+i(t+t_{\chi}))|\,\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr).

In view of the previous calculation, it is apparent that the above error term is negligible. Moreover, comparing the above estimate with (

) one deduces that the main terms are identical. This implies that the contribution of the region

is acceptable when evaluating the difference between the long and short averages.

4.

Unlike the usual major arc settings, it is challenging to estimate the exponential sum over

\displaystyle S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)=\sum_{x\leqslant n\leqslant x+H_{1}}f_{k}(n)e(\alpha n).

\displaystyle E_{1,k}(m)=\sum_{q_{0}q_{1}=q}\frac{\sqrt{q_{1}}}{\varphi(q_{1})}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}\,\,(\text{\rm mod}\,\,q_{1})}\Big\lvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x/q_{0}<n\leqslant x/q_{0}+m/q_{0}\end{subarray}}f_{k}(q_{0}n)\chi(n)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle E_{2,k}(m)=\sum_{q_{0}q_{1}=q}\frac{\mu(q_{1})^{2}}{\varphi(q_{1})}\Big\lvert\sum_{\frac{x}{q_{0}}<n\leqslant\frac{x}{q_{0}}+\frac{m}{q_{0}}}\chi_{0}(n)f_{k}(q_{0}n)-\frac{m}{q_{0}Y}\sum_{\frac{x}{q_{0}}<n\leqslant\frac{x}{q_{0}}+Y}\chi_{0}(n)f_{k}(q_{0}n)\Big\rvert.

5.
Minor arcs analysis

The minor-arc discussion in this section involves estimates for the second moment average of an exponential sum over a very narrow neighborhood centered at some frequency. Prior to
[
10
]
, the standard approach entailed expanding the square and using combinatorial Type I and II sums to evaluate these exponential sums. The articles
[
10
,
11
]
exhibited instead a method using harmonic analysis to transfer the question to the understanding of moments of Dirichlet polynomials of the shape
\sum_{n\sim X}\frac{f_{k}(n)}{n^{s}}
. The underlying setting in the aforementioned memoir has the natural advantage that their exponential sums are supported on long intervals, making it easier to relate them to Dirichlet polynomials. However, the short exponential sums
S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)
in (
4.2
) employed herein present additional challenges. To overcome this issue, Fourier analysis is employed. More precisely, an application of Parseval’s identity permits one to transfer the average from the dual space to the physical space, such a manoeuvre enabling one to shift the supported intervals
[x,x+H_{1}]
to
[X,2X]
. We then exhibit a more flexible application of
[
10
, Proposition 5.1(i)]
combined when required with the use of the Matomäki-Radziwiłł theorem.

The next lemma is an extension of
[
10
, Proposition 5.1 (i)]
.

Lemma 5.1

(Bounding exponential sums by mean values of Dirichlet polynomials)
.

Let
1\leqslant H\leqslant H_{1}/2\leqslant X
be large numbers, and let
f_{k}:[X,2X]\to\mathbb{C}
be the function defined in (
2.3
). Suppose that
1\leqslant a\leqslant q
are coprime integers, and
\eta,\beta
are real numbers satisfying
|\beta|\ll\eta\leqslant 1/2
. Then we have

\int_{X}^{2X}\int_{\beta-\frac{1}{H}}^{\beta+\frac{1}{H}}\Bigl|\sum_{y<n\leqslant y+H_{1}}f_{k}(n)e(an/q)e(n\theta)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}y\\
\ll\frac{d_{2}(q)^{4}H_{1}}{q}\sup_{q=q_{0}q_{1}}\int_{I}\Bigl(\sum_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q_{1})}\Bigl|\sum_{n\sim X/q_{0}}\frac{f_{k}(q_{0}n)\chi(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}\Bigr|\Bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
+\Big(H_{1}\Big(\eta+\frac{1}{|\beta|H}\Big)^{2}+H\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl(H^{-1}\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}|f_{k}(n)|\Bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

I=\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:|\beta|(\eta X-H)\leqslant|t|\leqslant|\beta|(\eta^{-1}X+H)\right\}.

Proof.

First, we set
f(n)=f_{k}(n)e(an/q)
. Suppose that
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth even function supported on
[-1,1]
, equals to
1
on the interval
[-1/10,1/10]
, and its Fourier transform
\widehat{\varphi}(\theta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y)e(-\theta y)\,\mathrm{d}y
obeys the bound
|\widehat{\varphi}(\theta)|\gg 1
for
|\theta|\leqslant 1
. Given any number
y\in[X,2X]
one has

\displaystyle\int_{\beta-\frac{1}{H}}^{\beta+\frac{1}{H}}

\displaystyle\Bigl|\sum_{y<n\leqslant y+H_{1}}f_{k}(n)e(an/q)e(n\theta)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\ll\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl|\sum_{y<n\leqslant y+H_{1}}f(n)e(n\theta)\Bigr|^{2}|\widehat{\varphi}(H(\theta-\beta))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{y<n\leqslant y+H_{1}}f(n)e(\beta Hw)\varphi(w)e(\theta(n-Hw))\,\mathrm{d}w\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Making the change of variables
n-Hw\to w
, one finds that the above integral equals

=H^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{y<n\leqslant y+H_{1}}f(n)\varphi\Big(\frac{n-w}{H}\Big)e(\beta(n-w))e(\theta w)\,\mathrm{d}w\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

It then follows from Plancherel’s identity that the preceding line equals

H^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl|\sum_{y<n\leqslant y+H_{1}}f(n)\varphi\Big(\frac{n-w}{H}\Big)e(\beta n)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}w.

Since the smooth function
\varphi
vanishes outside of the interval
[-1,1]
, we may assume that the above sum runs over integers
n
satisfying
|n-w|\leqslant H
. Consequently,

\int_{X}^{2X}\int_{\beta-\frac{1}{H}}^{\beta+\frac{1}{H}}\Bigl|\sum_{y<n\leqslant y+H_{1}}f_{k}(n)e(an/q)e(n\theta)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}y\\
\ll H^{-2}\int_{X}^{2X}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y<n\leqslant y+H_{1}\\
w-H<n\leqslant w+H\end{subarray}}f(n)\varphi\Big(\frac{n-w}{H}\Big)e(\beta n)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}y.

We observe that the function inside the above integral vanishes outside of the set
w\in[y-H,y+H_{1}+H]
. Therefore, by switching the order of integration and recalling the assumption
H_{1}\geqslant H
one may deduce that the preceding line is bounded above by a constant times

\displaystyle H^{-2}

\displaystyle\int_{X/2}^{3X}\int_{w-H_{1}-H}^{w+H}\Bigl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y<n\leqslant y+H_{1}\\
w-H<n\leqslant w+H\end{subarray}}f(n)\varphi\Big(\frac{n-w}{H}\Big)e(\beta n)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+H^{-2}\int_{X/2}^{3X}\int_{w-H_{1}+H}^{w-H}\Bigl|\sum_{n}f(n)\varphi\Big(\frac{n-w}{H}\Big)e(\beta n)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}w,

wherein the terms
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are defined as

I_{1}=H^{-2}\int_{X/2}^{3X}\int_{w-H_{1}-H}^{w-H_{1}+H}\Bigl|\sum_{w-H<n\leqslant w+H}f_{k}(n)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}w

and

I_{2}=H^{-2}\int_{X/2}^{3X}\int_{w-H}^{w+H}\Bigl|\sum_{w-H<n\leqslant w+H}f_{k}(n)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}w.

The reader may note that whenever

n
in the sum inside the integral on the third term in (
5.1
).

We first observe that one trivially has

I_{1}+I_{2}\ll H^{-1}\int_{X/2}^{3X}\Bigl|\sum_{w-H<n\leqslant w+H}f_{k}(n)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}w\ll H\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl|H^{-1}\sum_{w<n\leqslant w+H}f_{k}(n)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}w.

The remaining of the proof shall then be devoted to estimate the third summand in (

\ll\frac{H_{1}}{H^{2}}\int_{X/2}^{3X}\Bigl|\sum_{n}f(n)\varphi\Big(\frac{n-w}{H}\Big)e(n\beta)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}w.

+\Big(\eta+\frac{1}{|\beta|H}\Big)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl(\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+H}|f_{k}(n)|\Bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

The lemma follows by combining everything together.

6.

\displaystyle\sum_{|h|\leqslant H_{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{h}\Phi(h/H_{2})\Bigl|X^{-1}\int_{X}^{2X}c(x)\int_{\mathfrak{m}}S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)\overline{S_{f_{l}}(\alpha,x)}e(h\alpha)\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr|^{2}.

By squaring out and switching the order of integration one may find that the above expression equals

X^{-2}\sum_{h}\Phi(h/H_{2})\int_{X}^{2X}c(x)\int_{\mathfrak{m}}S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)\overline{S_{f_{l}}(\alpha,x)}e(h\alpha)\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\,\mathrm{d}x\\
\cdot\int_{X}^{2X}\overline{c(y)}\int_{\mathfrak{m}}\overline{S_{f_{k}}(\gamma,y)}S_{f_{l}}(\gamma,y)e(-h\gamma)\,\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\,\,\mathrm{d}y\\
\ll X^{-2}\int_{X}^{2X}\int_{\mathfrak{m}}\lvert S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)\rvert\lvert S_{f_{l}}(\alpha,x)\rvert\int_{X}^{2X}\int_{\mathfrak{m}}\lvert S_{f_{k}}(\gamma,y)\rvert\lvert S_{f_{l}}(\gamma,y)\rvert\\
\cdot\Bigl|\sum_{h}\Phi(h/H_{2})e(h(\alpha-\gamma))\Bigr|\,\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x

We employ Poisson summation on the inner sum (see the proof of
[
10

) to deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{|h|\leqslant H_{2}}\Bigl|\int_{X}^{2X}c(x)\int_{\mathfrak{m}}S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)\overline{S_{f_{l}}(\alpha,x)}e(h\alpha)\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll H_{2}\int_{\mathfrak{m}}\int_{X}^{2X}\lvert S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)\rvert\lvert S_{f_{l}}(\alpha,x)\rvert\int_{\mathfrak{m}\cap[\alpha-\frac{1}{2H_{2}},\alpha+\frac{1}{2H_{2}}]}\int_{X}^{2X}\lvert S_{f_{k}}(\gamma,y)\rvert\lvert S_{f_{l}}(\gamma,y)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\bf{x}.

where we wrote
\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\,\mathrm{d}\alpha.
We then decompose the frequencies
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
into intervals of length
\frac{1}{H_{2}}
and thus write
I_{i}=[\frac{i-1}{H_{2}},\frac{i}{H_{2}}]
with
1\leqslant i\leqslant H_{2}
. By noting that when
\alpha\in I_{i}
, the frequencies
\gamma\in[\alpha-\frac{1}{2H_{2}},\alpha+\frac{1}{2H_{2}}]
must be in
I_{i-1}\cup I_{i}\cup I_{i+1}
it follows that

\sum_{|h|\leqslant H_{2}}\Bigl|X^{-1}\int_{X}^{2X}c(x)\int_{\mathfrak{m}}S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)\overline{S_{f_{l}}(\alpha,x)}e(h\alpha)\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr|^{2}\\
\ll H_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{H_{2}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathfrak{m}\cap I_{i}}X^{-1}\int_{X}^{2X}\bigl|S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)\overline{S_{f_{l}}(\alpha,x)}\bigr|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\Bigr)^{2}.

Consequently, an application of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with respect to both
x
and
\alpha
yields

(7.2)

\sum_{|h|\leqslant H_{2}}\Bigl|X^{-1}\int_{X}^{2X}c(x)\int_{\mathfrak{m}}S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)\overline{S_{f_{l}}(\alpha,x)}e(h\alpha)\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr|^{2}\ll H_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{H_{2}}a(I_{i})b(I_{i}),

a(I_{i})=X^{-1}\int_{X}^{2X}\int_{\mathfrak{m}\cap I_{i}}\lvert S_{f_{k}}(\alpha,x)\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x,\ \ b(I_{i})=X^{-1}\int_{X}^{2X}\int_{\mathfrak{m}\cap I_{i}}\lvert S_{f_{l}}(\alpha,x)\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x.

) we employ Holder’s inequality to get

(7.3)

j

Nonsymmorphic Topological Phases of Non-Hermitian Systems
Daichi Nakamura, Yutaro Tanaka, Ken Shiozaki, Kohei Kawabata

Abstract
Non-Hermiticity appears ubiquitously in various open classical and quantum systems and enriches classification of topological phases. However, the role of nonsymmorphic symmetry, crystalline symmetry accompanying fractional lattice translations, has remained largely unexplored. Here, we systematically classify non-Hermitian topological crystalline phases protected by nonsymmorphic symmetry and reveal unique phases that have no counterparts in either Hermitian topological crystalline phases or non-Hermitian topological phases protected solely by internal symmetry. Specifically, we elucidate the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} and ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} non-Hermitian topological phases and their associated anomalous boundary states characterized by distinctive complex-valued energy dispersions.

Classification
.—We investigate topological classification of
d
-dimensional non-Hermitian Hamiltonians
H(\bm{k})
with nonsymmorphic symmetry.
In general, an operation of order-two nonsymmorphic symmetry accompanies a nonprimitive lattice half translation,
\left(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{d}\right)\mapsto\left(x_{1}+1/2,x_{2},\cdots,x_{d-d_{\parallel}},-x_{d-d_{\parallel}+1},\cdots,-x_{d}\right)
, where the lattice constant is set to unity, the direction of the half translation is chosen as the
x_{1}
axis, and
d_{\parallel}
coordinates are flipped (
0\leq d_{\parallel}<d
).
In momentum space, this operation leads to two types of nonsymmorphic symmetry,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(k_{1})H(\bm{k})\mathcal{V}^{-1}(k_{1})=H(\sigma\bm{k}),

(1)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{U}}(k_{1})H^{\dagger}(\bm{k})\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}(k_{1})=H(\sigma\bm{k}),

(2)

with

\sigma\bm{k}\coloneqq(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{d-d_{\parallel}},-k_{d-d_{\parallel}+1},\dots,-k_{d}).

(3)

Here,
\mathcal{U}
,
\mathcal{V}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}
, and
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
are
k_{1}
-dependent unitary matrices satisfying
[\mathcal{U}(k_{1})]^{2}=[\mathcal{V}(k_{1})]^{2}=e^{-ik_{1}}
for Eq. (
1
) and
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}(k_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{V}}(k_{1})=\tilde{\mathcal{V}}(k_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{U}}(k_{1})=e^{-ik_{1}}
for Eq. (
2
).
Notably, unlike in Hermitian systems, non-Hermiticity allows for
\mathcal{U}\neq\mathcal{V}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}\neq\tilde{\mathcal{V}}

[
88
,
91
]
. We refer to Eq. (
1
) and Eq. (
2
) as nonsymmorphic symmetry and pseudo-nonsymmorphic symmetry, respectively.
According to the terminology of Ref.
[
44
]
, the latter is also called nonsymmorphic symmetry
†
.
Whereas both types of nonsymmorphic symmetry coincide in Hermitian systems, their distinction in non-Hermitian systems gives rise to the rich topological classification.

We proceed to classify point-gap topological phases protected by nonsymmorphic symmetry. A non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H
is defined to have a point gap with respect to a reference energy
E\in\mathbb{C}
for
\det\left[H-E\right]\neq 0

[
36
,
44
]
.
Non-Hermitian point-gap topology of
H
is then equivalent to Hermitian topology of
{\sf H}
defined by

{\sf H}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&H-E\\
H^{\dagger}-E^{{\dagger}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

Through this Hermitization mapping, we identify the relevant symmetry class of
{\sf H}
for each internal symmetry class of
H
in addition to nonsymmorphic symmetry, and thus classify nonsymmorphic-symmetric point-gap topology for arbitrary spatial dimensions
[
98
]
.
While point-gap topology with order-two point-group symmetry was classified
[
95
,
96
]
in a similar manner to the Hermitian case
[
8
]
, we here extend the classification to encompass nonsymmorphic spatial symmetry.

Our classification uncovers a plethora of point-gap topological phases that are not present only with internal symmetry, some of which are highlighted in Table
1
.
These phases have the potential to exhibit unique non-Hermitian topological phenomena inherent in nonsymmorphic symmetry.
Below, we demonstrate that they indeed host anomalous non-Hermitian boundary states protected by nonsymmorphic symmetry.
We exemplify this for pseudo-nonsymmorphic-symmetric systems in two dimensions (2D classes A +
\tilde{\cal U}
and AII
†
+
\tilde{\cal U}_{-}
) and a chiral-symmetric and nonsymmorphic-symmetric system in three dimensions (3D class AIII +
{\cal U}_{+}
).
In Ref.
[
98
]
, we also discuss nonsymmorphic topological phases in one dimension.
Remarkably, we demonstrate that generic types of symmetry representations with
\mathcal{U}\neq\mathcal{V}
(
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}\neq\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
), which are forbidden in Hermitian systems, yield boundary states with distinctive complex-valued energy dispersions.
Hereafter, we identify
x_{1}
as
x
and may use
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
even for pseudo-nonsymmorphic cases when no ambiguity arises.

\tilde{\cal U}

We first employ the generic symmetry representation,

\mathcal{U}_{\rm I}(k_{x})=\sigma_{0},\quad\mathcal{V}_{\rm I}(k_{x})=e^{-ik_{x}}\sigma_{0},

2\times 2

A representative bulk Hamiltonian generating the

\displaystyle H_{\rm SSH}(k_{x},k_{y})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&f(k_{y})e^{-ik_{x}/2}\\

\displaystyle\coloneqq g(k_{x})e^{ik_{x}/2}\sigma_{z}.

We find that

]
.
Consequently, the entire non-Hermitian Hamiltonian

in Eq. (
7
) supports edge states described by

in Eq. (
9
) under the open boundary conditions along the

Remarkably, Eq. (

We also investigate an alternative choice of the symmetry representation,

\mathcal{U}_{\rm II}(k_{x})=\mathcal{V}_{\rm II}(k_{x})=\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{-ik_{x}}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

corresponding to

\displaystyle H_{\rm II}(k_{x},k_{y})

\displaystyle=H_{\rm I}(k_{x},k_{y})\mathcal{U}_{\rm II}(k_{x})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}f(k_{y})&g(k_{x})e^{-ik_{x}/2}\\

2D class AII
†
+
\tilde{\cal U}_{-}
.—An important feature of nonsymmorphic symmetry is the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
topological classification.
While
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
Hermitian topology manifests as anomalous edge states with hourglass-like spectra
[
21
,
22
]
, we here elucidate its non-Hermitian counterpart.
Prime examples arise in 2D class AII
†
+
\tilde{\cal U}_{-}
with
d_{\parallel}=0
, accompanying pseudo-nonsymmorphic symmetry in Eq. (
2
) and time-reversal symmetry
†

\mathcal{T}H^{T}(\boldsymbol{k})\mathcal{T}^{-1}=H(-\boldsymbol{k}),\quad\mathcal{T}\mathcal{T}^{*}=-1

(13)

with a unitary matrix
\mathcal{T}
satisfying
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{V}^{*}(k_{x})=-\mathcal{U}(-k_{x})\mathcal{T}
.
The parity of the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
topological invariant
\nu
reduces to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant protected solely by time-reversal symmetry
†

[
98
,
100
]
.
Consequently,
\nu\equiv 1,3
(mod
4
) merely yields the conventional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
skin effect
[
51
]
and is thus irrelevant to nonsymmorphic symmetry.

Rather, we clarify that
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
point-gap topology with
\nu\equiv 2
is inherent in nonsymmorphic symmetry and gives rise to helical edge states characterized by distinctive complex-valued spectra, akin to class A +
\tilde{\cal U}
.
We identify their effective theory as

H(k_{x})=\begin{pmatrix}ie^{-ik_{x}/2}g(k_{x})&f(k_{x})\\
f^{*}(k_{x})&ie^{ik_{x}/2}g(-k_{x})\end{pmatrix},

(14)

where the symmetry operators are chosen as
\mathcal{U}(k_{x})={\rm diag}({i},-{i}e^{-ik_{x}})
,
\mathcal{V}(k_{x})={\rm diag}(-{i}e^{-ik_{x}},{i})
, and
\mathcal{T}=\sigma_{y}
.
Here,
f(k_{x})
is a complex
2\pi
-periodic function satisfying
f(-k_{x})=-f(k_{x})
, and
g(k_{x})
is a real
2\pi
-antiperiodic function.
Unlike the previous case of class A +
\tilde{\cal U}
,
H(k_{x})
effectively describes a Kramers pair of point-gapless edge states.
Indeed, for
f(k_{x})=0
and
g(k_{x})=\sin(k_x/2)
, the energy dispersions are given as the helical edge states
E=ie^{-ik_{x}/2}\sin(k_x/2)
,
-ie^{ik_{x}/2}\sin(k_x/2)
.

To realize this
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
point-gap topological phase, we introduce the following tight-binding model on a square lattice:

\displaystyle{H}(\boldsymbol{k})\coloneqq(t_{x}\cos k_{x}+t_{y}\cos k_{y}+m)\tau_{x}\sigma_{x}+\Delta\sin k_{y}\tau_{0}\sigma_{y}

\displaystyle\qquad+g(k_{x})\left[-i\cos(k_x/2)\tau_{x}\sigma_{0}+\sin(k_x/2)\tau_{x}\sigma_{z}\right],

(15)

with
\mathcal{U}(\boldsymbol{k})=\tau_{0}\otimes{\rm diag}({i},-{i}e^{-ik_{x}})_{\sigma}
,
\mathcal{V}(\boldsymbol{k})=\tau_{0}\otimes{\rm diag}(-{i}e^{-ik_{x}},{i})_{\sigma}
, and
\mathcal{T}=\tau_{z}\sigma_{y}
, as well as
g(k_{x}+2\pi)=-g(k_{x})
and
g(-k_{x})=-g(k_{x})
.
The corresponding Hermitized Hamiltonian in Eq. (
4
) exhibits the nonsymmorphic topological phases supporting hourglass fermions at edges
[
98
]
.
In Fig.
2
(a, b), we provide the complex spectra under both periodic and open boundary conditions, confirming the non-Hermitian helical detachable edge states.
They originate from the zero modes of the SSH model in the
y
direction comprised of the first two terms in Eq. (
15
), as in the previous case.
As a result of their point-gap topology, they further exhibit the corner skin effect under the open boundary conditions along both directions [Fig.
2
(c, d)].
In Ref.
[

]
, the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
point-gap topology was argued to result in the skin effect for arbitrary
\nu
.
However, our results imply that this conclusion is specific to the model therein.
Instead, we demonstrate that while
\nu\equiv 1,3
leads to the skin effect,
\nu\equiv 2
generally induces the emergence of helical edge states with unique complex-valued spectra.

\Gamma\mathcal{U}(k_{x})=\mathcal{V}(k_{x})\Gamma
and hosts the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
point-gap classification (see Table
1
).
Non-Hermitian Hamiltonians
H(\bm{k})
preserving these symmetries can be systematically constructed from Hermitian Hamiltonians
{\sf h}(\bm{k})
in class A +
\sf U
by
H(\bm{k})=-i{\sf h}(\bm{k})\Gamma
,
where
\mathcal{U}(k_{x})
and
\mathcal{V}(k_{x})
are properly defined through a nonsymmorphic-symmetry operator of
{\sf h}(\bm{k})

[
98
]
.
Following this procedure, we obtain a prototypical model as

\displaystyle H(\bm{k})=(m+\cos k_{y}+\cos k_{z})\tau_{x}\sigma_{z}-\sin k_{y}\tau_{y}\sigma_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad+a\tau_{x}\sigma_{x}+b\tau_{x}\sigma_{y}-i\sin k_{z}\tau_{0}\sigma_{0}\quad\left(m\in\mathbb{R}\right),

(17)

where
a=a(k_{x},k_{z})
and
b=b(k_{x},k_{z})
are real functions satisfying
[a(k_{x},k_{z})+ib(k_{x},k_{z})]e^{-ik_{x}}=-a(k_{x},-k_{z})+ib(k_{x},-k_{z})
.
The symmetry operators are explicitly given as

\Gamma=\tau_{z}\sigma_{0},\quad\mathcal{U}(k_{x})=-\mathcal{V}(k_{x})=\tau_{x}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{-ik_{x}}\\
1&0\\
\end{pmatrix}_{\sigma}.

(18)

Similar to Eqs. (
7
) and (
15
), this model is also decomposed into the SSH model and a non-Hermitian surface Hamiltonian,

H_{\rm surf}(k_{x},k_{z})=a\tau_{x}\sigma_{x}+b\tau_{x}\sigma_{y}-i\sin k_{z}\tau_{0}\sigma_{0},

(19)

within the surface Brillouin zone specified by
|m+\cos k_{z}|<1
along the
z
direction.

In Fig.
3
, we present the complex energy spectrum of Eq. (
17
) for
a(k_{x},k_{z})=-\delta\sin(k_x/2)\sin(k_x/2)
and
b(k_{x},k_{z})=\delta\cos(k_x/2)\sin(k_x/2)
(
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
),
exhibiting nonsymmorphic surface states consistent with Eq. (
19
).
In contrast to the two-dimensional cases, which host the edge states completely detached from the bulk bands (see Figs.
1
and
2
), the surface states in three dimensions are spectrally detached only along the real axis [Fig.
3
(b)].
In fact, the energy dispersion around
E=0
reads
\pm\delta(k_{x}/2)-ik_{z}
.
This is a unique feature of three-dimensional nonsymmorphic-symmetric systems, arising from the absence and presence of spectral flow in the
k_{x}
and
k_{z}
directions, respectively.

Conclusions
.—Nonsymmorphic symmetry serves as an important component of space groups.
While it yields unique topological classification and corresponding boundary phenomena in the Hermitian regime, its impact on non-Hermitian topology has remained largely elusive.
In this Letter, we have developed classification of nonsymmorphic-symmetry-protected point-gap topology.
Building upon this classification, we have identified the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
non-Hermitian topological phases and their associated anomalous boundary states distinguished by the characteristic complex-valued energy dispersions.
In light of the recent experimental realization of non-Hermitian topological systems across diverse synthetic platforms, our nonsymmorphic-symmetric models should also be implemented in similar setups, potentially affecting the dynamics of open classical and quantum systems.
Our work thereby broadens the landscape of non-Hermitian topological materials and enriches the understanding of phases of matter.

(I.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}(k_{x})H_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{k})\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}^{-1}(k_{x})=H_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k}),\quad\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}(k_{x})\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}(k_{x})=e^{-ik_{x}}\quad(\alpha={\rm I,II}),

(II.1)

where
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}(k_{x})
and
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}(k_{x})
are given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\rm I}(k_{x})=\sigma_{0},\quad\mathcal{V}_{\rm I}(k_{x})=e^{-ik_{x}}\sigma_{0};\quad\mathcal{U}_{\rm II}(k_{x})=\mathcal{V}_{\rm II}(k_{x})=\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{-ik_{x}}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(II.2)

For each symmetry representation,
H_{\alpha}(\bm{k})
takes the form of

\displaystyle H_{\rm I}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}g(\bm{k})e^{ik_{x}/2}&f(\bm{k})\\
f^{*}(\bm{k})e^{ik_{x}}&h(\bm{k})e^{ik_{x}/2}\end{pmatrix},\quad H_{\rm II}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}f(\bm{k})&g(\bm{k})e^{-ik_{x}/2}\\
h(\bm{k})e^{ik_{x}/2}&f^{*}(\bm{k})\end{pmatrix},

(II.3)

where
f
is a complex function, and
g
and
h
are real functions.
Owing to the
2\pi
-periodicity of the Hamiltonians,
f
is
2\pi
-periodic for arbitrary momentum directions, while
g
and
h
are
2\pi
-antiperiodic for the
k_{x}
direction but
2\pi
-periodic for the other directions.

Notably,
\mathcal{U}_{\rm I}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\rm II}
(
\mathcal{V}_{\rm I}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\rm II}
) are not unitary equivalent since
\mathcal{U}_{\rm I}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\rm I}
are proportional to the
2\times 2
identity matrix
\sigma_{0}
, while their corresponding unitary matrices for the Hermitized Hamiltonians [see Eqs. (
I.4
) and (
I.9
)] are:

\displaystyle{\sf U}_{\rm I}\left(k_{x}\right)={\sf S}\,{\sf U}_{\rm II}\left(k_{x}\right)\,{\sf S}^{\dagger},

(II.4)

with

\displaystyle{\sf U}_{\alpha={\rm I,II}}\,\left(k_{x}\right)\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}\left(k_{x}\right)\\
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}\left(k_{x}\right)&0\end{pmatrix},\quad{\sf S}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{0}&0\\
0&\mathcal{U}_{\rm II}\,\left(k_{x}\right)\end{pmatrix}.

(II.5)

This can be relevant to the origin of the different boundary physics discussed in the main text, meriting further investigation.

III
Nonsymmorphic topological phases in one dimension

III.1
Class A +
\bar{\sf U}
(
d_{\parallel}=0
; Hermitian)

Following Ref.
[
16
]
, we investigate the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological phase of one-dimensional Hermitian systems in class A +
\bar{\sf U}
with
d_{\parallel}=0
.
The condition of nonsymmorphic symmetry is expressed as

\bar{\sf U}\left(k_{x}\right){\sf H}\left(k_{x}\right)\bar{\sf U}^{-1}\left(k_{x}\right)=-{\sf H}\left(k_{x}\right),\quad\bar{\sf U}^{2}\left(k_{x}\right)=e^{-ik_{x}}.

(III.1)

The
2\pi
periodicity of
\bar{\sf U}\left(k_{x}\right)
constrains its form to

\bar{\sf U}\left(k_{x}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{-ik_{x}}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(III.2)

Accordingly, the Hamiltonian
{\sf H}\left(k_{x}\right)
is given as

{\sf H}\left(k_{x}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}X\left(k_{x}\right)&-iY\left(k_{x}\right)e^{-ik_{x}/2}\\
iY\left(k_{x}\right)e^{ik_{x}/2}&-X\left(k_{x}\right)\end{pmatrix}

(III.3)

with
N\times N
Hermitian matrices
X\left(k_{x}\right)
and
Y\left(k_{x}\right)
.
The
2\pi
periodicity of
{\sf H}\left(k_{x}\right)
imposes the constraints

X\left(k_{x}+2\pi\right)=X\left(k_{x}\right),\quad Y\left(k_{x}+2\pi\right)=-Y\left(k_{x}\right).

(III.4)

Introducing an
N\times N
non-Hermitian matrix
Z\left(k_{x}\right)
by

Z\left(k_{x}\right)\coloneqq X\left(k_{x}\right)+iY\left(k_{x}\right),

(III.5)

we have

Z\left(k_{x}+2\pi\right)=Z^{{\dagger}}\left(k_{x}\right).

(III.6)

Owing to
\det\mathsf{H}\left(k_{x}\right)=\left|\det Z\left(k_{x}\right)\right|^{2}
, the existence of a spectral gap around
E=0
in
\mathsf{H}\left(k_{x}\right)
is equivalent to the opening of a point gap around
E=0
in
Z\left(k_{x}\right)
.
We define the real and imaginary parts of
\det Z\left(k_{x}\right)
as

x\left(k_{x}\right)\coloneqq\mathrm{Re}\left[\det Z\left(k_{x}\right)\right],\quad y\left(k_{x}\right)\coloneqq\mathrm{Im}\left[\det Z\left(k_{x}\right)\right],

(III.7)

satisfying

x\left(k_{x}+2\pi\right)=x\left(k_{x}\right),\quad y\left(k_{x}+2\pi\right)=-y\left(k_{x}\right).

(III.8)

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological invariant is determined by the parity of the number of times the trajectory
\left(x\left(k_{x}\right),y\left(k_{x}\right)\right)
crosses the negative
x
axis as
k_{x}
varies from
0
to
2\pi
.
Simple representations of trivial and nontrivial phases are given by
\left(x,y\right)=\left(\pm 1,0\right)
(
N=1
).

III.2
Class AIII +
{\cal U}_{-}
(
d_{\parallel}=0
; non-Hermitian)

According to Table
S1
, one-dimensional non-Hermitian systems in class AIII +
\mathcal{U}_{-}
with
d_{\parallel}=0
support the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-classified point-gap topological phase.
Owing to the anticommutation relation between nonsymmorphic symmetry and chiral symmetry, the chiral symmetry operator can be chosen as

\Gamma\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(III.9)

Then, from the Hermitian Hamiltonian
{\sf H}\left(k_{x}\right)
in Eq. (
III.3
), the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}\left(k_{x}\right)
can be introduced as

H\left(k_{x}\right)\coloneqq-i{\sf H}\left(k_{x}\right)\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}-iX&Ye^{-ik_{x}/2}\\
Ye^{ik_{x}/2}&-iX\end{pmatrix},

(III.10)

respecting the symmetry conditions,

\bar{\sf U}\left(k_{x}\right)H\left(k_{x}\right)\bar{\sf U}^{-1}\left(k_{x}\right)=H\left(k_{x}\right),\quad\Gamma H^{{\dagger}}\left(k_{x}\right)\Gamma^{-1}=-H\left(k_{x}\right).

(III.11)

Analogous to the corresponding Hermitian systems
{\sf H}\left(k_{x}\right)
, the point-gapped non-Hermitian systems
H\left(k_{x}\right)
realize the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological phase.
Similarly, non-Hermitian systems in class AIII +
\tilde{\cal U}_{+}
(

) host the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
point-gap topological phase.

IV
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological invariant and Fermi-surface-like formula in 2D Class A +
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}

By introducing

\tilde{H}(\bm{k})\coloneqq\mathcal{V}(\bm{k})\,H(\bm{k})\,e^{ik_{x}/2},

(IV.1)

the symmetry constraint in Eq. (
I.2
) for
d_{\parallel}=0
becomes

\tilde{H}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})=\tilde{H}(\bm{k}),\quad\tilde{H}(k_{x}+2\pi,\,k_{y})=-\tilde{H}(k_{x},\,k_{y}).

(IV.2)

The Berry phase of the occupied bands along the loop in the
k_{y}
direction at fixed
k_{x}
, denoted by
e^{i\gamma(k_{x})}
, satisfies
[
16
]

e^{i\gamma(k_{x}+2\pi)}=e^{-i\gamma(k_{x})}.

(IV.3)

If the trajectory of
e^{i\gamma(k_{x})}
crosses the positive real axis an odd (even) number of times as
k_{x}
goes from
0
to
2\pi
, then
\tilde{H}(\bm{k})
is topologically nontrivial (trivial), which defines the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant
\nu
. An illustrative example is given by the
2\times 2
Hamiltonian

\tilde{H}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{i(k_{x}/2+nk_{y})}\\
e^{-i(k_{x}/2+nk_{y})}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

(IV.4)

whose
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant is

\nu\equiv n\quad\left(\bmod~2\right).

(IV.5)

Next, we discuss a Fermi-surface-like formula for this
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
index
\nu
in generic
2\times 2
models of the form

\tilde{H}(\bm{k})=c(\bm{k})\,\sigma_{0}\;+\;\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon(\bm{k})&\Delta(\bm{k})\\
\Delta(\bm{k})^{*}&-\,\varepsilon(\bm{k})\end{pmatrix}.

(IV.6)

Because of the antiperiodicity in
k_{x}
in Eq. (
IV.2
), the real-valued function
\varepsilon(\bm{k})
must cross zero an odd number of times as
k_{x}
increases by
2\pi
for each fixed
k_{y}
. Analogously to superconducting systems, we refer to the loops on which
\varepsilon(\bm{k})=0
as
Fermi loops
.

To evaluate the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant, we consider the one-parameter family of Hamiltonians,

\tilde{H}(\bm{k};\,a)=c(\bm{k})\sigma_{0}\;+\;\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon(\bm{k})&a\,\Delta(\bm{k})\\
a\,\Delta(\bm{k})^{*}&-\,\varepsilon(\bm{k})\end{pmatrix}\quad\left(a>0\right),

(IV.7)

and take the limit
a\to 0^{+}
. In this limit, away from the Fermi loops [i.e.,
\varepsilon(\bm{k})\neq 0
], the sign of
\varepsilon(\bm{k})
determines that the occupied state is either
(1,0)^{T}
or
(0,1)^{T}
, both of which yield zero Berry phase.
Only on the Fermi loops [i.e.,
\varepsilon(\bm{k}_{\mathrm{FL}})=0
], a nontrivial Berry phase can arise.
Specifically, at such points, the occupied eigenstate becomes
\left(\frac{\Delta(\bm{k}_{\mathrm{FL}})}{|\Delta(\bm{k}_{\mathrm{FL}})|},-1\right)^{T}
, whose Berry phase along each Fermi loop is given by

e^{i\gamma_{\mathrm{FL}}}=\exp\left[-\frac{i}{2}\oint_{\varepsilon(\bm{k}_{\mathrm{FL}})=0}d\,\log\,\Delta(\bm{k})\right]=(-1)^{N_{\mathrm{FL}}},

(IV.8)

where

N_{\mathrm{FL}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\varepsilon(\bm{k}_{\mathrm{FL}})=0}d\,\log\,\Delta(\bm{k})\;\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}

(IV.9)

is the winding number of the phase of
\Delta(\bm{k})
around the Fermi loop.

In the presence of multiple Fermi loops, each loop
\mathrm{FL}_{\alpha}
carries its own integer winding number
N_{\mathrm{FL}_{\alpha}}
, and the total
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant is given by

\nu\equiv\sum_{\alpha}N_{\mathrm{FL}_{\alpha}}\quad\left(\bmod~2\right).

(IV.10)

Hence,
\nu
counts the parity of the sum of these winding numbers over all Fermi loops.

V
Parity of
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
topological invariant in 2D class AII
†
+
\tilde{\cal U}_{-}

We show that the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
topological invariant
\nu\equiv 1,3
(
\mathrm{mod}~4
) yields the conventional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
skin effect
[
51
]
in two-dimensional non-Hermitian systems for class AII
†
+
\tilde{\cal U}_{-}
(
d_{\parallel}=0
).
We consider the Hermitized Hamiltonian
{\sf H}(\boldsymbol{k})
in class DIII +
{\sf U}_{{-+}}
corresponding to the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H(\boldsymbol{k})
in class AII
†
+
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}_{{-}}
(see Table
S3
).
In class DIII, the Hermitized Hamiltonian
{\sf H}(\boldsymbol{k})
respects time-reversal symmetry, particle-hole symmetry, and chiral symmetry:

\displaystyle{\sf T}{\sf H}^{*}(\boldsymbol{k}){\sf T}^{-1}={\sf H}(-\boldsymbol{k}),\quad{\sf T}{\sf T}^{*}=-1,

(V.1)

\displaystyle{\sf C}{\sf H}^{T}(\boldsymbol{k}){\sf C}^{-1}=-{\sf H}(-\boldsymbol{k}),\quad{\sf C}{\sf C}^{*}=1,

(V.2)

\displaystyle{\sf\Gamma}{\sf H}(\boldsymbol{k}){\sf\Gamma}^{-1}=-{\sf H}(\boldsymbol{k}),\quad{\sf\Gamma}^{2}=1.

(V.3)

Furthermore, the Hermitized Hamiltonian
{\sf H(\boldsymbol{k})}
respects nonsymmorphic symmetry (
d_{\parallel}=0
)

\displaystyle{\sf U}(k_{x}){\sf H}(\boldsymbol{k}){\sf U}^{-1}(k_{x})={\sf H}(\boldsymbol{k}),\quad{\sf U}^{2}(k_{x})=e^{-ik_{x}},

(V.4)

with the symmetry eigenvalues of nonsymmorphic symmetry,
g_{\pm}(k_{x})=\pm e^{ik_{x}/2}
.

V.1
Time-reversal polarization

The Hermitized Hamiltonian with
2N
bands possesses
N
Kramers pairs
\{\ket{u_n^{\rm I}(\boldsymbol{k})},\ket{u_n^{\rm II} (\boldsymbol{k})}\}
with
n=1,2,\cdots N
.
We define the polarization associated with one of the Kramers pairs by

\displaystyle P^{s}\coloneqq\frac{i}{2\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk~\mathrm{tr}\mathcal{A}^{s}(k)\quad(s=\mathrm{I},\mathrm{II}),

(V.5)

where
\mathcal{A}^{s}(k)
is the Berry connection of occupied states with
s=\mathrm{I}
or
\mathrm{II}
, and the trace is taken over all the occupied bands. We introduce time-reversal polarization
[
100
]
defined by

\displaystyle P_{\mathsf{T}}\coloneqq P^{\mathrm{I}}-P^{\mathrm{II}}.

(V.6)

The sum of the time-reversal polarizations associated with occupied states and unoccupied states is zero:

\displaystyle P_{\mathsf{T}}+\overline{P_{\mathsf{T}}}\equiv 0\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ 2),

(V.7)

where
\overline{P_{\mathsf{T}}}
is the time-reversal polarization associated with unoccupied states.
In addition, in the presence of particle-hole symmetry, the time-reversal polarizations
P_{\mathsf{T}}
and
\overline{P_{\mathsf{T}}}
satisfy

\displaystyle P_{\mathsf{T}}\equiv\overline{P_{\mathsf{T}}}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ 2).

(V.8)

From Eqs. (
V.7
) and (
V.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle P_{\mathsf{T}}\equiv\overline{P_{\mathsf{T}}}\in\{0,1\}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ 2).

(V.9)

This quantized time-reversal polarization is a one-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological invariant in class DIII.

V.2
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological invariant in 2D class DIII

Using the time-reversal polarization in Eq. (
V.9
), we define a two-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological invariant in class DIII

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}\coloneqq P_{\mathsf{T}}(\Gamma Y)-P_{\mathsf{T}}(XM)\in\{0,1\}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ 2),

(V.10)

where
P_{\mathsf{T}}(\Gamma Y)
and
P_{\mathsf{T}}(XM)
are the time-reversal polarization along the
k_{y}
direction between the high-symmetry points in the Brillouin zone, and the high-symmetry points are defined as
\Gamma=(0,0)
,
X=(\pi,0)
,
Y=(0,\pi)
, and
M=(\pi,\pi)
.
This topological invariant
\nu^{\prime}
detects the helical edge states along the
x
direction protected by internal symmetries in class DIII.

The symmetry eigenvalues
g_{\pm}(k_{x})
take the values
\pm i
at
k_{x}=\pi
, and the symmetry operators satisfy
\mathsf{T}\mathsf{U}^{*}(k_{x})=\mathsf{U}(-k_{x})\mathsf{T}
.
Consequently, the time-reversal operator maps the
g_{\pm}(\pi)
sector into itself. Thus, we can define the time-reversal polarization in each
g_{\pm}(\pi)
sector at
k_{x}=\pi
.
Conversely, since the symmetry operators satisfy
\mathsf{C}\mathsf{U}^{*}(k_{x})=\mathsf{U}(-k_{x})\mathsf{C}
, the particle-hole operator exchanges the
g_{+}(\pi)
and
g_{-}(\pi)
sectors, yielding

\displaystyle P^{(+)}_{\mathsf{T}}(XM)\equiv\overline{P^{(-)}_{\mathsf{T}}}(XM)\quad({\rm mod}\ 2),

(V.11)

where
P^{(\pm)}_{\mathsf{T}}(XM)

\left(\overline{P^{(\pm)}_{\mathsf{T}}}(XM)\right)
is the time-reversal polarization associated with the
g_{\pm}(k_{x})
sectors for occupied (unoccupied) states along the
XM
line.
In addition, the sum of the time-reversal polarizations
P^{(\pm)}_{\mathsf{T}}(XM)
and
\overline{P^{(\pm)}_{\mathsf{T}}}(XM)
in each
g_{\pm}(k_{x})
sector is zero:

\displaystyle P^{(\pm)}_{\mathsf{T}}(XM)+\overline{P^{(\pm)}_{\mathsf{T}}}(XM)\equiv 0\quad({\rm mod}\ 2).

(V.12)

From Eqs. (
V.11
) and (
V.12
), we get

\displaystyle P_{\mathsf{T}}(XM)\equiv P^{(+)}_{\mathsf{T}}(XM)+{P^{(-)}_{\mathsf{T}}}(XM)\equiv P^{(+)}_{\mathsf{T}}(XM)-\overline{P^{(-)}_{\mathsf{T}}}(XM)\equiv 0\quad({\rm mod}\ 2),

(V.13)

which shows that the one-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological invariant along the
XM
line is trivial. Thus, in the presence of nonsymmorphic symmetry, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological invariant
\nu^{\prime}
in Eq. (
V.10
) reduces to

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}\equiv P_{\mathsf{T}}(\Gamma Y)\quad({\rm mod}\ 2).

(V.14)

On the other hand, the symmetry eigenvalues
g_{\pm}(k_{x})
are
\pm 1
at
k_{x}=0
, and the symmetry operators satisfy
\mathsf{T}\mathsf{U}^{*}(k_{x})=-\mathsf{U}(-k_{x})\mathsf{T}
.
Therefore, the time-reversal operator exchanges the
g_{+}(0)
and
g_{-}(0)
sectors, leading to

\displaystyle P^{(+)}_{k_{x}=0}\equiv-P^{(-)}_{k_{x}=0}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ 2),

(V.15)

where
P^{(\pm)}_{k_{x}=0}
is the polarization along the
y
direction

\displaystyle P^{(\pm)}_{k_{x}=0}\coloneqq\frac{i}{2\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk_{y}{\rm tr}\mathcal{A}_{\pm}(0,k_{y})\quad({\mathrm{mod}}\ 2),

(V.16)

in the
g_{\pm}
sector at
k_{x}=0
.
Thus, the time-reversal polarization reads

\displaystyle P_{\mathsf{T}}(\Gamma Y)\equiv 2P^{(+)}_{k_{x}=0}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ 2).

(V.17)

Using Eqs. (
V.14
) and (
V.17
), we obtain

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}\equiv 2P^{(+)}_{k_{x}=0}\equiv\frac{i}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk_{y}{\rm tr}\mathcal{A}_{+}(0,k_{y})\quad({\rm mod}\ 2).

(V.18)

V.3
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
topological invariant in 2D class DIII +
{\sf U}_{-+}

The
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
topological invariant is defined by
[
21
]

\displaystyle\nu\coloneqq\frac{2i}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk_{y}{\rm tr}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{I}}_{+}(\pi,k_{y})-\frac{i}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{0}dk_{x}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk_{y}{\rm tr}\mathcal{F}_{+}(k_{x},k_{y})\quad({\rm mod}\ 4),

(V.19)

where
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{I,II}}_{+}(\pi,k_{y})
is the Berry connection of occupied states
\ket{u_n^{(+)\rm I,II}(\boldsymbol{k})}
in the
g_{+}(k_{x})
sector, and
\mathcal{F}_{+}
is the Berry curvature in the
g_{+}(k_{x})
sector.
From Stokes’ theorem, the second term of
\nu
reads

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{0}dk_{x}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk_{y}{\rm tr}\mathcal{F}_{+}(k_{x},k_{y})

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{i}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk_{y}{\rm tr}\mathcal{A}_{+}(\pi,k_{y})+\frac{i}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk_{y}{\rm tr}\mathcal{A}_{+}(0,k_{y})\quad({\rm mod}\ 2)

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{2i}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk_{y}{\rm tr}\mathcal{A}^{\rm I}_{+}(\pi,k_{y})+\frac{i}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk_{y}{\rm tr}\mathcal{A}_{+}(0,k_{y})\quad({\rm mod}\ 2),

(V.20)

where we used
\mathrm{tr}\mathcal{A}_{+}(\pi,k_{y})=\mathrm{tr}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{I}}_{+}(\pi,k_{y})+\mathrm{tr}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{II}}_{+}(\pi,k_{y})
and
\mathrm{tr}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{I}}_{+}(\pi,k_{y})=\mathrm{tr}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{II}}_{+}(\pi,-k_{y})
.
Thus,
\nu
reads

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{i}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk_{y}{\rm tr}\mathcal{A}_{+}(0,k_{y})\quad({\rm mod}\ 2).

(V.21)

From Eqs. (
V.18
) and (
V.21
), it follows that

\displaystyle\nu\equiv\nu^{\prime}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ 2),

(V.22)

showing that the parity of the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
topological invariant
\nu
reduces to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological invariant
\nu^{\prime}
.
Since
\nu^{\prime}=1
corresponds to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological phase protected by internal symmetries, the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian corresponding to the Hermitized Hamiltonian with
\nu=1,3
exhibits a point-gap topological phase protected by internal symmetry in class AII
†
, which is the conventional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
skin effect
[
51
]
.

VI
Hermitized Hamiltonian from 2D class AII
†
+
\tilde{\cal U}_{-}

VI.1
Hermitized continuum model

When a given non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H(\boldsymbol{k})
belongs to class AII
†
+
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}_{-}
, the Hermitized Hamiltonian
{\sf H}(\boldsymbol{k})
belongs to class DIII +
{\sf U}_{-+}
(see Table
S3
).
In class DIII, the Hermitized Hamiltonian
{\sf H}(\boldsymbol{k})
respects time-reversal symmetry in Eq. (
V.1
), particle-hole symmetry in Eq. (
V.2
), and chiral symmetry in Eq. (
V.3
) with

\displaystyle{\sf T}=i\tau_{x}\sigma_{y},\quad{\sf C}=\tau_{y}\sigma_{y},\quad{\sf\Gamma}=\tau_{z}.

(VI.1)

Furthermore, the Hamiltonian
{\sf H(\boldsymbol{k})}
respects nonsymmorphic symmetry (
d_{\parallel}=0
) in Eq. (
V.4
)
with

\displaystyle{\sf U}(k_{x})\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathcal{U}(k_{x})\\
\mathcal{V}(k_{x})&0\\
\end{pmatrix}_{\tau},\quad\mathcal{U}(k_{x})\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\
0&-ie^{-ik_{x}}\\
\end{pmatrix}_{\sigma},\quad\mathcal{V}(k_{x})\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}-ie^{-ik_{x}}&0\\
0&i\end{pmatrix}_{\sigma}.

(VI.2)

Because of
{\sf U}^{2}(k_{x})=e^{-ik_{x}}
,
\mathcal{U}(k_{x})
and
\mathcal{V}(k_{x})
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(k_{x})=e^{-ik_{x}}\mathcal{V}^{-1}(k_{x}).

(VI.3)

These symmetry representations obey the following commutation and anticommutation relations:

\displaystyle{\sf T}{\sf U}^{*}(k_{x})=-{\sf U}(-k_{x}){\sf T},\quad{\sf C}{\sf U}^{*}(k_{x})={\sf U}(-k_{x}){\sf C}.

(VI.4)

Therefore, the Hermitized Hamiltonian
{\sf H}(\boldsymbol{k})
belongs to class DIII +
{\sf U}_{-+}
.

From the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian

H_{{\rm AII}^{\dagger}}(k_{x})=\begin{pmatrix}ie^{-ik_{x}/2}g(k_{x})&f(k_{x})\\
f^{*}(k_{x})&ie^{ik_{x}/2}g(-k_{x})\end{pmatrix},

(VI.5)

we obtain the following Hermitized Hamiltonian

\displaystyle{\sf H}_{\rm DIII}(k_{x})\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&H_{{\rm AII}^{\dagger}}(k_{x})\\
H_{{\rm AII}^{\dagger}}^{\dagger}(k_{x})&0\end{pmatrix}_{\tau}.

(VI.6)

When we choose
g(k_{x})=v_{1}\sin(k_x/2)
and
f(k_{x})=v_{2}\sin k_{x}+iv_{3}\sin k_{x}

(
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
) in Eq. (
VI.5
), the Hermitized Hamiltonian is written as

\displaystyle{\sf H}_{\rm DIII}(k_{x})=v_{1}\sin^{2}(k_{x}/2)\tau_{x}+\frac{v_{1}}{2}\sin k_{x}\tau_{y}\sigma_{z}+v_{2}\sin k_{x}\tau_{x}\sigma_{x}+v_{3}\sin k_{x}\tau_{x}\sigma_{y}.

(VI.7)

This Hamiltonian exhibits gapless boundary states. In the simultaneous presence of the internal symmetries and pseudo-nonsymmorphic symmetry, no mass terms open the gap, showing the stability of the gapless boundary states.
We confirm that this Hamiltonian
{\sf H}_{\rm DIII}(k_{x})
is characterized by the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
topological invariant
\nu\equiv 2
(
\mathrm{mod}~4
).
For that purpose, we consider its doubled Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle{\sf{H}}_{d}(k_{x})\coloneqq{\sf H}_{\rm DIII}(k_{x})\oplus{\sf H}_{\rm DIII}(k_{x}),

(VI.8)

which can be gapped by adding symmetry-preserving mass terms,
m_{1}\mu_{y}\tau_{y}\sigma_{x}
and
m_{2}\mu_{y}\tau_{y}\sigma_{y}
(
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
).
Here, the symmetry operators are chosen as

\displaystyle{\sf{U}}_{d}(k_{x})\coloneqq\mu_{x}\otimes{\sf U}(k_{x}),\quad{\sf{T}}_{d}\coloneqq\mu_{0}\otimes{\sf T},\quad{\sf{\Gamma}}_{d}\coloneqq\mu_{0}\otimes{\sf\Gamma},

(VI.9)

with the
2\times 2
identity matrix
\mu_{0}
and the Pauli matrix
\mu_{x}
.
Thus, this doubled Hamiltonian
{\sf{H}}_{d}(k_{x})
hosts the trivial topological invariant
\nu\equiv 0
(
\mathrm{mod}~4
), further implying
\nu\equiv 2
for
{\sf{H}}_{\rm DIII}(k_{x})
.

VI.2
Hermitized lattice model

We discuss a two-dimensional lattice model of Hermitian
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
topological nonsymmorphic crystalline insulator in class DIII +
{\sf U}_{-+}
.
To begin with, we introduce a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

[

(VI.10)

with
g(-k_{x})=-g(k_{x})

\displaystyle{\sf C}_{\rm D}{\sf H}^{T}_{e_{+}-e_{-}}(\boldsymbol{k}){\sf C}_{\rm D}^{-1}=-{\sf H}_{e_{+}-e_{-}}(-\boldsymbol{k}),\quad{\sf C}_{\rm D}{\sf C}_{\rm D}^{*}=+1,\quad{\sf C}_{\rm D}=\sigma_{x}.

\displaystyle{\sf U}_{\rm D}(k_{x}){\sf H}_{e_{+}-e_{-}}(\boldsymbol{k}){\sf U}_{\rm D}^{-1}(k_{x})={\sf H}_{e_{+}-e_{-}}(\boldsymbol{k}),\quad{\sf U}_{\rm D}^{2}(k_{x})=e^{-ik_{x}},\quad{\sf U}_{\rm D}(k_{x})\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{-ik_{x}}\\

\nu\equiv 2
(
\mathrm{mod}~4

Adding a degree of freedom
\mu_{i}
(

), we choose symmetry representations for class DIII,

\displaystyle{\sf C}_{\rm DIII}=\mu_{x}\tau_{0}\sigma_{x},\quad{\sf T}_{\rm DIII}=\mu_{y}\tau_{z}\sigma_{z},\quad{\sf\Gamma}_{\rm DIII}=\mu_{z}\tau_{z}\sigma_{y},

\displaystyle{\sf T}_{\rm DIII}{\sf H}^{*}_{\rm DIII}(\boldsymbol{k}){\sf T}_{\rm DIII}^{-1}={\sf H}_{\rm DIII}(-\boldsymbol{k}),\quad{\sf T}_{\rm DIII}{\sf T}_{\rm DIII}^{*}=-1,

(VI.15)

\displaystyle{\sf C}_{\rm DIII}{\sf H}^{T}_{\rm DIII}(\boldsymbol{k}){\sf C}_{\rm DIII}^{-1}=-{\sf H}_{\rm DIII}(-\boldsymbol{k}),\quad{\sf C}_{\rm DIII}{\sf C}_{\rm DIII}^{*}=1,

(VI.16)

\displaystyle{\sf U}_{\rm DIII}(k_{x}){\sf H}_{\rm DIII}(\boldsymbol{k}){\sf U}_{\rm DIII}^{-1}(k_{x})={\sf H}_{\rm DIII}(\boldsymbol{k}),\quad{\sf U}_{\rm DIII}^{2}(k_{x})=e^{-ik_{x}},\quad{\sf U}_{\rm DIII}(k_{x})\coloneqq\mu_{0}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{-ik_{x}}\\

(VI.17)

VII
Systematic model construction in class AIII +
\mathcal{U}_{+}

We provide a general method to construct non-Hermitian models in class AIII +
\mathcal{U}_{+}
from Hermitian models in class A +
\mathsf{U}
.
We first apply the Hermitization mapping in Eq. (
I.4
) to non-Hermitian point-gapped Hamiltonians
H\left({\bm{k}}\right)
in class AIII +
\mathcal{U}_{+}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}\left(k_{1}\right)}\,{H}\left({\bm{k}}\right)\,{\mathcal{V}^{-1}\left(k_{1}\right)}={H}\left(\sigma{\bm{k}}\right),\quad\left[{\mathcal{U}\left(k_{1}\right)}\right]^{2}=\left[{\mathcal{V}\left(k_{1}\right)}\right]^{2}=e^{-ik_{1}},

(VII.1)

\displaystyle{\Gamma}\,{H}^{\dagger}\left({\bm{k}}\right)\,{\Gamma}^{-1}=-{H}\left({\bm{k}}\right),\quad\Gamma^{2}=1,

(VII.2)

\displaystyle{\Gamma}\,{\mathcal{U}\left(k_{1}\right)}={\mathcal{V}\left(k_{1}\right)}\,{\Gamma}.

(VII.3)

The resultant Hermitian gapped Hamiltonian
{\sf H}\left({\bm{k}}\right)
respects both chiral and nonsymmorphic symmetries,

\displaystyle{\sf\Gamma}\,{\sf H}\left({\bm{k}}\right){\sf\Gamma}^{-1}=-{\sf H}\left({\bm{k}}\right),\quad{\sf\Gamma}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&\Gamma\\
\Gamma&0\\
\end{pmatrix},

(VII.4)

\displaystyle{\sf U}\left(k_{1}\right)\,{\sf H}\left({\bm{k}}\right){\sf U^{-1}}\left(k_{1}\right)={\sf H}\left(\sigma{\bm{k}}\right),\quad{\sf U}\left(k_{1}\right)\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}{\mathcal{U}\left(k_{1}\right)}&0\\
0&{\mathcal{V}\left(k_{1}\right)}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\left[{\sf U}\left(k_{1}\right)\right]^{2}=e^{-ik_{1}},

(VII.5)

as well as additional chiral symmetry,

{\Sigma}\,{\sf H}\left({\bm{k}}\right){\Sigma}^{-1}=-{\sf H}\left({\bm{k}}\right),\quad{\Sigma}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(VII.6)

Here, the symmetry operators satisfy the following relations:

\left[{\Gamma},\,{\sf U}\left(k_{1}\right)\right]=\left[{\Sigma},\,{\sf U}\left(k_{1}\right)\right]=\left\{{\sf\Gamma},\,{\Sigma}\right\}=0.

(VII.7)

The presence of two chiral symmetries
{\sf\Gamma}
and
{\Sigma}
enables us to construct a unitary matrix
i{\sf\Gamma}{\Sigma}
commuting with both
{\sf H}\left({\bm{k}}\right)
and
{\sf U}\left(k_{1}\right)
,

\left[{\sf H}\left({\bm{k}}\right),\,i{\sf\Gamma}{\Sigma}\right]=\left[{\sf U}\left(k_{1}\right),\,i{\sf\Gamma}{\Sigma}\right]=0.

(VII.8)

Then, the three matrices
{\sf H}\left({\bm{k}}\right),{\sf U}\left(k_{1}\right)
, and
i{\sf\Gamma}{\Sigma}
can be simultaneously diagonalized by a unitary matrix
{\sf Q}
, leading to

\displaystyle{\sf Q}^{\dagger}{\sf H}\left({\bm{k}}\right){\sf Q}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}iH\left({\bm{k}}\right)\Gamma&0\\
0&-iH\left({\bm{k}}\right)\Gamma\\
\end{pmatrix},

(VII.9)

\displaystyle{\sf Q}^{\dagger}{\sf U}\left(k_{1}\right){\sf Q}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathcal{U}\left(k_{1}\right)}&0\\
0&{\mathcal{U}\left(k_{1}\right)}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(VII.10)

\displaystyle{\sf Q}^{\dagger}\left(i{\sf\Gamma}{\Sigma}\right){\sf Q}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{pmatrix};

(VII.11)

\displaystyle\quad{\sf Q}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\\
i\Gamma&-\Gamma\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(VII.12)

Consequently, the topological properties of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians
H\left({\bm{k}}\right)
are characterized by the Hermitian matrices
iH\left({\bm{k}}\right)\Gamma
with nonsymmorphic symmetry
{\mathcal{U}\left(k_{1}\right)}
.

The above unitary transformation further gives a general one-to-one correspondence between a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
h_{{\rm AIII}+\mathcal{U}_{+}}\left({\bm{k}}\right)
in class AIII +
\mathcal{U}_{+}
and a Hermitian Hamiltonian
{\sf h}_{{\rm A}+{\sf U}}\left({\bm{k}}\right)
in class A + U.
From

\displaystyle{{\sf u}\left(k_{1}\right)}\,{\sf h}_{{\rm A}+{\sf U}}\left({\bm{k}}\right)\,{{\sf u}^{-1}\left(k_{1}\right)}={\sf h}_{{\rm A}+{\sf U}}\left(\sigma{\bm{k}}\right),\quad\left[{{\sf u}\left(k_{1}\right)}\right]^{2}=e^{-ik_{1}},

(VII.13)

one can systematically construct
h_{{\rm AIII}+\mathcal{U}_{+}}\left({\bm{k}}\right)
by
h_{{\rm AIII}+\mathcal{U}_{+}}\left({\bm{k}}\right)\coloneqq-i{\sf h}_{{\rm A}+{\sf U}}\left({\bm{k}}\right)\gamma
with a unitary matrix
\gamma
satisfying
\gamma^{2}=1
.
The obtained non-Hermitian Hamiltonian indeed meets the symmetry conditions for class AIII +
\mathcal{U}_{+}
:

\displaystyle{u\left(k_{1}\right)}\,h_{{\rm AIII}+\mathcal{U}_{+}}\left({\bm{k}}\right)\,{v^{-1}\left(k_{1}\right)}=h_{{\rm AIII}+\mathcal{U}_{+}}\left(\sigma{\bm{k}}\right),\quad\left[{u\left(k_{1}\right)}\right]^{2}=\left[{v\left(k_{1}\right)}\right]^{2}=e^{-ik_{1}},

(VII.14)

\displaystyle{\gamma}\,h_{{\rm AIII}+\mathcal{U}_{+}}^{\dagger}\left({\bm{k}}\right)\,{\gamma}^{-1}=-h_{{\rm AIII}+\mathcal{U}_{+}}\left({\bm{k}}\right),\quad\gamma^{2}=1,

(VII.15)

\displaystyle{\gamma}\,{u\left(k_{1}\right)}={v\left(k_{1}\right)}\,{\gamma},

(VII.16)

where
{u\left(k_{1}\right)}\coloneqq{{\sf u}\left(k_{1}\right)}
and
{v\left(k_{1}\right)}\coloneqq\gamma{{\sf u}\left(k_{1}\right)}\gamma
.

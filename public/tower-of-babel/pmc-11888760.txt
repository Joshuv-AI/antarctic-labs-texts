Comparative Analysis of Alginate Dimensional Stability with Varied Pouring Intervals
Authors: Adham A. Niyazi, Mohamad A. Alabrash, Mohamed S. Elzouhiry, Rashed M. AlFarra, Nawaf M. Althubyani, Wayel M. Huraib, Thamer A. Alghalib, Fawaz Pullishery
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Alginate is a widely used dental impression material in clinical settings. Despite advances in impression materials and the growing popularity of digital impression techniques, alginate remains a staple due to its ease of use and cost-effectiveness. The dimensional stability of irreversible hydrocolloid alginate is influenced by various factors, including storage temperature and the elapsed time between taking the impression and pouring the cast.Materials and Methods:This in vitro study aimed to evaluate the dimensional stability of two alginate impression materials, Cavex CA37 and Zhermack Hydrogum 5, over four different time periods. Statistical analyses, including Student’s t-test, three-way ANOVA, post hoc Tukey HSD tests, and ANCOVA, were used to determine the significance of differences between groups and the impact of pouring time on dimensional stability.Results:Post hoc analysis revealed significant differences in dimensional stability between the two alginates across different time periods. For Zhermack Hydrogum 5, a significant difference (P < 0.05) was observed between the 120-hour mark and the other three time periods. Cavex CA37 exhibited significant differences (P < 0.05) between immediate pouring (0 hours) and the subsequent three time periods.Conclusion:The findings suggest that impressions made with Cavex CA37 should be poured immediately to ensure optimal dimensional stability. Conversely, Zhermack Hydrogum 5 maintains accurate dimensional stability when poured within 24 hours, making it a more flexible option in clinical practice.

INTRODUCTION

Alginate is one of the most commonly used dental impression materials in nearly every dental clinic. Despite advancements in impression materials and the rising popularity of digital impression techniques, alginate continues to be a mainstay due to its ease of use, pleasant taste, fast setting time, and cost-effectiveness.[1] Alginate’s applications are vast, including primary impressions, interim restorations, mouth guards, study cast models, opposing dentition impressions, and impressions for orthodontic models and bleaching trays.[2345]

The dimensional stability of irreversible hydrocolloid alginate impression material is multifactorial and material-specific. It depends significantly on the storage temperature and the time elapsed between taking the impression and pouring the cast.[678] Accurate reproduction of hard and soft tissues is essential for successful dental prostheses, impacting biological, mechanical, functional, and aesthetic outcomes.[9101112] Therefore, maintaining the dimensional stability of impressions is crucial for tasks such as patient education, diagnosing malocclusions, fabricating custom trays, implant surgical guides, and occlusal devices.[13] However, alginate impressions are prone to dimensional changes due to water absorption (imbibition) or water loss (syneresis).[141516] As a result, impressions should ideally be poured within 10 to 15 minutes to ensure dimensional stability. Despite this, some manufacturers claim that their alginate brands maintain dimensional stability for extended periods, a claim that necessitates further investigation. Previous studies have shown mixed results regarding the dimensional stability of alginate when stored for extended periods.[14171819]

Given the importance of dimensionally stable impressions for accurate dental casts and subsequent clinical decisions, this study aims to evaluate the dimensional stability of two commonly used alginate brands, Cavex CA37 and Zhermack Hydrogum 5, across different pouring times. While Zhermack Hydrogum 5 is claimed to maintain stability for up to 120 hours, further research is required to substantiate this claim and compare it with Cavex CA37. The primary objective of this study was to evaluate and compare the dimensional accuracy of stone casts made from alginate impressions at four different pouring times. The study focused on two widely used alginate brands: Cavex CA37 and Zhermack Hydrogum 5

MATERIALS AND METHODS

An in vitro controlled laboratory-based study was conducted at the dental laboratory of Batterjee Medical College (Jeddah, Saudi Arabia) to investigate the effect of pouring time on the dimensional stability of two different irreversible hydrocolloid impression materials.

Measurement tools

A digital caliper (GENERAL ULTRATECH, New York, US) with an accuracy of 0.02 mm was used for measurements.

Procedure

A total of 80 impressions were taken, with 40 impressions for each of the two materials. The standardized impressions were made using medium-size disposable impression trays on the specially fabricated 3D-printed maxillary dentate master cast. The impressions were divided into four subgroups based on pouring times: 0 minutes (immediately), 1 hour, 24 hours, and 120 hours, with 10 impressions for each material per time interval.

Calibration

All impressions were mixed and taken by a single examiner to maintain consistency. Therefore, no additional calibration steps for the examiner were necessary.

Mixing and pouring

Alginate was mixed according to the manufacturer’s specified powder/liquid ratio. The impressions were poured using hard dental stone by the same examiner who took the impressions to ensure uniformity.

Exclusion criteria

Impressions with voids or technical defects were excluded from the study.

Storage conditions

All impressions were stored at room temperature (24°C) in zip-lock plastic bags wrapped in wet napkins to maintain hydration.

Measurements and analysis

Measurements of the stone casts were taken in three dimensions: anteroposterior, lateral, and oblique, using a digital caliper. The data were organized in tables for statistical analysis.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using Student’s t-test, three-way ANOVA, post hoc tests (Tukey HSD for multiple comparisons), and ANCOVA to assess the effects of material brands, storage time intervals, and conditions on the dimensional stability of the impressions.

RESULTS

The dimensional stability of the alginate impression materials, Hydrogum 5 and Cavex CA37, was analyzed across different time intervals. The immediate and 1-hour intervals revealed no statistically significant differences between the two materials. The mean dimensions for both materials were 47.07 (SD = 0.01) for the anteroposterior (X), lateral (Y), and oblique (Z) dimensions, except for Cavex CA37 after 1 hour, which had a standard deviation of 0.00. The P values for these comparisons were greater than 0.001, indicating that immediate pouring and pouring after one hour did not significantly affect the dimensional stability of the materials.

In contrast, significant differences were observed at the 24-hour and 120-hour intervals. Both materials showed statistically significant changes in all three dimensions (P value < 0.001). This indicates that the dimensional stability of Hydrogum 5 and Cavex CA37 deteriorates over time when the impressions are not poured immediately [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

Alginate, an irreversible hydrocolloid, is commonly used for dental diagnostic and working models for therapeutic appliances.[220] Despite its widespread use, alginate has limitations, including dimensional instability when there is a delay in plaster casting and issues with accurate mold reproduction upon recasting.[45] These problems arise from alginate’s propensity to lose or gain water, leading to dimensional changes that can be minimized by immediate plaster pouring.[212223] The findings indicate that both Cavex CA37 and Zhermack Hydrogum 5 exhibit excellent dimensional stability when impressions are poured immediately or within one hour. However, significant dimensional changes were observed after 24 and 120 hours. This suggests that while short-term stability is reliable, prolonged storage significantly impacts the accuracy of the impressions. A similar study by Maurizio Sedda also tested the dimensional stability of various alginate impression materials, including Cavex CA37 and Hydrogum 5, at different storage times.[13] Sedda’s research demonstrated that immediate pouring and short-term storage under controlled humidity conditions preserved dimensional stability. However, unlike Sedda’s study which used an electronic vacuum mixing machine for the cast, our study used manual mixing by a single examiner to maintain consistency, potentially accounting for some differences in findings.

Our results are consistent with previous research indicating that increasing storage duration leads to decreased dimensional stability. Specifically, the study by C. Erbe in 2012 found that irreversible hydrocolloid impressions must be poured within 2 to 4 hours to maintain optimal dimensional stability, particularly when stored in humid conditions.[15] Similarly, our findings showed that both Cavex CA37 and Hydrogum 5 require prompt pouring to ensure accurate casts. This study also highlights that while Hydrogum 5 can maintain its dimensions for up to 24 hours, its stability significantly decreases by 120 hours. On the other hand, Cavex CA37 showed significant dimensional changes as early as 24 hours, emphasizing the need for immediate pouring for reliable results.[24]

The limitations of this research include the lack of evaluation of intervals between 24 and 120 hours, which could provide a more detailed understanding of the stability degradation timeline. In addition, as a lab-based study, the findings may not fully translate to clinical settings where variables such as patient movements, environmental conditions, and different storage media could further impact dimensional stability. Future research should explore a broader range of time intervals between 24 and 120 hours and investigate other factors that may influence alginate dimensional stability, such as mixing techniques, different storage conditions, temperature variations, and the effects of disinfectants. Moreover, comparing the dimensional stability of alginate impressions for both dentulous and edentulous patients could provide more comprehensive insights. Evaluating the performance of new alginate formulations designed for extended pour times could also offer valuable information for clinical practice.

CONCLUSION

Alginate is a commonly used material in dental practices worldwide. Our research indicates that alginate impressions made with Cavex CA37 should be poured immediately to ensure dimensional stability. In contrast, Zhermack Hydrogum 5 impressions maintain accurate dimensional stability for up to 24 hours but exhibit significant dimensional changes if poured after 120 hours. Further studies are needed to evaluate the dimensional stability of Zhermack Hydrogum 5 impressions between the 24-hour and 120-hour marks to better understand the material’s performance over time.

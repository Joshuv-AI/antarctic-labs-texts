Deep Research Comparator: A Platform For Fine-grained Human Annotations of Deep Research Agents
Prahaladh Chandrahasan, Jiahe Jin, Zhihan Zhang, Tevin Wang, Andy Tang, Lucy Mo, Morteza Ziyadi, Leonardo F.R. Ribeiro, Zimeng Qiu, Markus Dreyer, Akari Asai, Chenyan Xiong

Abstract
Effectively evaluating deep research agents that autonomously search the web, analyze information, and generate reports remains a major challenge, particularly when it comes to assessing long reports and giving detailed feedback on their intermediate steps. To address these gaps, we introduce Deep Research Comparator , a platform that offers a holistic framework for deep research agent hosting, side-by-side comparison, fine-grained human feedback collection, and ranking calculation. Given a user query, our platform displays the final reports from two different agents along with their intermediate steps during generation. Annotators can evaluate the overall quality of final reports based on side-by-side comparison, and also provide detailed feedback separately by assessing intermediate steps or specific text spans within the final report. Furthermore, we develop Simple Deepresearch , an end-to-end agent scaffold. This scaffold serves as a baseline that facilitates the easy integration of various large language models to transform them into deep research agents for evaluation. To demonstrate the platform’s utility for deep research agent development, we have collected real user preference data from 17 annotators on three deep research agents. We plan to release the code base and collected data. 1 1 1 Our code is available at GitHub , and demo video can be found at Youtube .

Introduction

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have catalyzed the development of autonomous agents, which can utilize various tools to solve real-world tasks. Among them, the deep research agent has recently emerged, referring to an agent powered by LLMs that autonomously finds, analyzes, and synthesizes vast amounts of online information to produce a comprehensive report
Xu and Peng 2025
. Prominent proprietary examples include agents developed by OpenAI
OpenAI 2025
, Google Gemini
Gemini 2025
, and Perplexity
perplexity 2025
, and the open-source community has also produced notable contributions
Li et al. 2025
;
GPTResearcher 2025
.

The main interface of

. Users can submit their query in the text box, and the intermediate steps and final reports from two agents will be displayed side by side.

Despite these advancements, evaluating the long-form reports generated by deep research agents remains a significant challenge. Existing benchmarks for search agents
Wei et al. 2025
;
Wu et al. 2025
primarily target short-form answers, leaving the assessment of long-form reports largely underexplored. Moreover, unlike tasks such as math or coding, there are no widely accepted objective metrics for assessing long reports
(
Asai et al. 2024
)
. To this end, some existing work focuses on developing hand-crafted rubrics for evaluation
Gou et al. 2025
;
FutureSearch et al. 2025
;
Arora et al. 2025
, but these approaches are difficult to scale.

Beyond the difficulty in assessing long reports, we also lack a fine-grained evaluation of agents’ intermediate steps. LLM-based agents often execute multi-step actions to complete long-horizon tasks, but current evaluations are typically based on outcomes
Deng et al. 2023
;
Zhou et al. 2024
;
Jimenez et al. 2024
;
Xie et al. 2024
, resulting in sparse and delayed feedback. However, process feedback is crucial for training methods like reinforcement learning (RL) to accurately attribute step contribution in the trajectory
Feng et al. 2025
;
Wang et al. 2025
;
Setlur et al. 2025
. Such feedback is also essential for understanding agent behavior and assessing the reliability of the generated reports
Lightman et al. 2023
;
Wan et al. 2024
.

To address these gaps, we introduce
Deep Research Comparator
(Section
3
), a platform for fine-grained human evaluation of long-report generation by deep research agents. It supports easy integration of new agents with minimal engineering effort, enabling both open-source and closed-source systems to be hosted through a unified web interface. When a user submits a query, the platform randomly routes it to two agents and presents their intermediate steps and final reports.

Deep Research Comparator
enables the collection of two types of human feedback. First, users can perform a side-by-side comparison of the two final reports and vote for the better one (Figure
1
bottom), similar to Chatbot Arena
Chiang et al. 2024
. These preference votes can then be used to calculate an outcome-based ranking of the agents. Second, users can provide more fine-grained annotations by upvoting or downvoting individual intermediate steps and specific text spans within the reports to indicate their quality (Figure
2
). This detailed feedback creates a valuable dataset for potential future use, such as agent fine-tuning or reward modeling.

To support easy deployment and evaluation of different LLMs as deep research agents,
we developed
Simple Deepresearch
, an end-to-end agent scaffold serving as a baseline (Section
4
). This prompt-based scaffold allows various LLMs to be easily integrated, transforming them into deep research agents for evaluation.

Using our platform, we conducted a proof-of-concept experiment on three agents with 176 user queries (Section
5
). We then ranked the agents based on pairwise comparison votes on their final reports. Additionally, we collected 1,281 upvote/downvote feedback on intermediate steps and 593 upvote/downvote with highlighted text spans in reports. From this, we calculated each agent’s overall upvote rate on intermediate steps for a process-based evaluation metric. Based on the collected data, we derive some design considerations for future agent developers on how to effectively use our platform for annotation and evaluation.

Overall, we make three key contributions:

1.

We provide
Deep Research Comparator
, an open source platform to host deep research agents and supports fine-grained human evaluation through a unified web interface.

2.

We develop
Simple Deepresearch
, a baseline agent scaffold that allows for the easy integration of various LLMs into deep research agents for evaluation.

3.

We demonstrate the utility of our platform by collecting 176 real-world user queries with comparison votes, and fine-grained annotation on 1,281 steps and 593 text spans in reports.

LLM-based search agents

Search Agents are LLM-based systems that autonomously and iteratively use search tools to gather external information
Jin et al. 2025
;
Schick et al. 2023
;
Anthropic 2025
;
Chen et al. 2024
for solving complex search tasks. These agents can generate dynamic action decisions that are executed as real-time internet searches, and then synthesize this information to provide up-to-date and relevant responses. Among them, “deep research agents” represent a more capable evolution of this paradigm
OpenAI 2025
;
GPTResearcher 2025
;
Li et al. 2025
, which conduct substantially more searches, engage in long-horizon planning, and operate with significantly higher test-time compute budgets, for more demanding, research-intensive scenarios.

Evaluations for Search Agents

Evaluations for search agents can be roughly divided into two types: short-answer tasks and long-form report generation. Short-answer tasks are easier to evaluate due to the availability of definitive ground truth. Commonly employed evaluations include intricate tasks that demand extensive knowledge and complex reasoning, such as GPQA
Rein et al. 2023
and Human’s Last Exam
HLETeam 2025
. Specialized benchmarks for web searches have also been developed
Mialon et al. 2023
;
Wei et al. 2025
;
FutureSearch et al. 2025
;
Wu et al. 2025
, with BrowseComp
Wei et al. 2025
as a recent representative. Compared to short-answer tasks, evaluating long-form reports presents a greater challenge. Some efforts have introduced hand-crafted, rubric-based benchmarks
Gou et al. 2025
;
Arora et al. 2025
;
FutureSearch et al. 2025
;
Asai et al. 2024
. Other studies have explored using LLMs to automatically score long reports against predefined criteria
Coelho et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025
.

3
Deep Research Comparator

This section presents
Deep Research Comparator
, a platform for fine-grained human evaluation of diverse deep research agents.

3.1
User Interface and Evaluation Workflow

Our platform provides an interactive, web-based user interface designed for side-by-side, fine-grained human annotation, as shown in Figure
1
.

Annotators can submit arbitrary queries, and our system will randomly assign the query to two anonymous deep research agents. As the report generation begins, the agents’ intermediate steps are streamed to the front-end, followed by the final reports. After both reports are generated, users vote for their preferred report by selecting one of four choices: Agent A, Agent B, Tie, or Both are bad. These pairwise comparisons are ultimately used to compute the outcome-based rankings.

Besides evaluating the overall quality of the final reports, this platform also supports more fine-grained evaluation (Figure
2
). Each step of the agent is accompanied by upvote and downvote icons, allowing users to provide step-wise feedback.
As a long report may contain content of varying quality, our platform also supports highlighting specific text spans in the final report and providing feedback either as upvotes or downvotes.

3.2
System Architecture

Figure 3:
High-level overview of
Deep Research Comparator
’s system architecture.

Figure
3
presents a high-level overview of our architecture. Our platform supports hosting a wide range of deep research agents. Agents that stream both intermediate steps and a final report in accordance with a unified JSON-format interface (see Appendix
B
) can be integrated into the platform with minimal engineering effort. This design allows agent developers to flexibly customize the content of intermediate steps to accommodate their annotation requirements and imposes no constraints on the agent’s internal architecture.

Frontend

The frontend presents the generated intermediate steps and final reports in a consistent format on the user interface. It is implemented as a static web application, with static files produced during the build process and hosted in an AWS S3 bucket, served to users through CloudFront CDN caching ensuring low latency.

Main Backend Service

The system routes user queries from the frontend to the Main Backend Service hosted on the main CPU node. It is responsible for handling user requests, selecting agents, routing queries to the selected agents, processing user votes, and serving the intermediate steps, final reports, and rankings back to the frontend.

Agent Serving Service

User queries are routed to the Agent Serving Services deployed on dedicated agent CPU nodes, each encapsulated within a Docker container. These agent services stream their responses back to the main backend via internal API calls using the unified JSON format specified in Appendix
B
. For open-source agent services whose underlying LLMs require GPU resources, the LLMs are deployed on specialized GPU nodes and accessed by the agent services through internal API calls. For closed-source agents, specialized proxy pods are implemented to invoke the agents through external APIs.

Ranking Calculation Job

This CronJob reads agent conversations and user annotation data from the database, and computes both outcome-based evaluation metrics (such as model rankings and query counts) and process-based metrics (such as step upvote rate). It then writes the computed results back to the relational database, which are served to the user interface via the Main Backend Service.

(Figure

Experiments

5.1
Human Annotation

We conduct human annotation experiments on
Deep Research Comparator
as proof-of-concept, with experimental setup and evaluation details provided below.

Agents

We evaluated three deep research agents: (1) the proprietary system
Perplexity Deep Research

perplexity 2025
, (2) the open-source
GPT Researcher

GPTResearcher 2025
, configured in deep research mode with GPT-4.1 as the smart LLM, o4-mini as the strategic LLM, and Serper as search API (3)
Simple Deepresearch
(Gemini 2.5 Flash)
, our baseline agent scaffold intergrated with Gemini 2.5 Flash
Deepmind 2025
as the underlying LLM and uses ClueWeb22
Overwijk et al. 2022
as search API.

Annotators

A total of 17 university students and industry researchers participated in the data annotation process. To measure inter-annotator consistency, we also provided annotators with four fixed queries (Appendix
C
), which allowed us to compute human agreement on a controlled set.

Metrics

Following Chatbot Arena
Chiang et al. 2024
, we use the Bradley-Terry model
Bradley and Terry 1952
to aggregate pairwise comparison votes and compute a ranking based on user preferences for final reports. The final Bradley-Terry (BT) scores are computed relative to our baseline-
Simple Deepresearch
, which is assigned a fixed score of 1000. In addition to the outcome-based BT score, we also introduce a process-based evaluation metric: the
step upvote rate
. For each agent, we aggregate all upvotes and downvotes received by its intermediate steps and compute the
step upvote rate
as
\text{upvotes}/(\text{upvotes}+\text{downvotes})
.

5.2
Evaluation on Static Benchmark

To validate the performance of our baseline agent scaffold and compare human evaluation results with those from static benchmarks, we evaluated agents on DeepResearchGym
Coelho et al. 2025
, a benchmark that uses LLM-as-a-judge to assess long reports generated by deep research agents.

Our evaluation employs two key metrics targeting different aspects of writing quality and analytical depth. We provide the LLM judge with detailed evaluation criteria and prompt it to assign scores based on it. Specifically,
Clarity
assesses the logical coherence and linguistic fluency of the report, while
Insight
captures the analytical nuance and depth of reasoning demonstrated.

5.4
Discussion

Step-Level Feedback Analysis

Our system displays agents’ intermediate steps, with their content determined solely by agent designers.
As a result, the content and granularity of steps vary across agents. Notably, although GPT Researcher ranked highest in outcome-based evaluation, it received the lowest step—upvote rate, likely due to its multi-agent parallel framework, which produces loosely ordered and often abstract or minimal step messages. In contrast,
Simple Deepresearch
(Gemini 2.5 Flash) present detailed steps that include both thought and actions (Appendix
2. Intermediate Steps
), contributing to a higher upvote rate. This highlights the importance for agent designers to consider what step content to expose, tailoring the level of detail and structure to their annotation goals.

LLM and Human Evaluation Agreement

For report quality assessment, human rankings closely align with the LLM-based evaluations on
Clarity
and
Insight
. However, human agreement was relatively low. This may be due to the small number of queries and the fact that all three agents perform at comparably high levels (see Table
6
), making fine distinctions difficult. This suggests two directions for annotation. First, as LLMs grow more capable, advancing agent performance in specialized domains may require expert annotations to provide high-quality guidance. Second, the subtlety of human preferences highlights the value of broader community annotations when the goal is to align agent behavior with general user expectations.

6
Conclusion

We present
Deep Research Comparator
, an open-source platform that offers a holistic framework for deep research agent hosting, web front-end demonstration, side-by-side evaluation, fine-grained annotation, and also ranking calculation. We also introduce
Simple Deepresearch
, a baseline scaffold that allows the easy integration of different LLMs to evaluate them as deep research agents.

Using our platform, we collected votes for 176 user queries and computed rankings based on them, and also gathered fine-grained annotation for 1,281 intermediate steps and 593 text spans in reports. Our platform can benefit a wide range of downstream applications, such as benchmarking, agent behavior analysis, and agent training via techniques like process supervision and RLHF. We believe
Deep Research Comparator
will serve as a useful resource for the deep research agent community.

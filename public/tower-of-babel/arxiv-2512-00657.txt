Computational Paths Form a Weak ω \omega -Groupoid
Arthur F. Ramos, Tiago M. L. de Veras, Ruy J. G. B. de Queiroz, Anjolina G. de Oliveira

Abstract
. Lumsdaine (2010) and van den Berg-Garner (2011) proved that types in Martin-Löf type theory carry the structure of weak omega-groupoids. Their proofs, while foundational, rely on abstract properties of the identity type without providing explicit computational content for coherence witnesses. We establish an analogous result for computational paths—an alternative formulation of equality where witnesses are explicit sequences of rewrites from the LND-EQ-TRS term rewriting system. Our main result is that computational paths on any type form a weak omega-groupoid with fully explicit coherence data. The groupoid operations—identity, composition, and inverse—are defined at every dimension, and the coherence laws (associativity, unit laws, inverse laws) are witnessed by concrete rewrite derivations rather than abstract existence proofs. The construction provides: (i) a proper tower of n-cells for all dimensions, with 2-cells as derivations between paths and higher cells mediating between lower-dimensional witnesses; (ii) explicit pentagon and triangle coherences built from the rewrite rules; and (iii) contractibility at dimensions at least 3, ensuring all parallel higher cells are connected. The contractibility property is derived from the normalization algorithm of the rewrite system, grounding the higher-dimensional structure in concrete computational content. The entire construction has been formalized in Lean 4, providing machine-checked verification of the weak omega-groupoid structure.



1.
Introduction

The relationship between Martin-Löf’s intensional type theory and higher categorical structures has been a central topic in the foundations of mathematics since the discovery of homotopy type theory. The seminal work of Hofmann and Streicher
[
11
]
demonstrated that types in intensional type theory cannot satisfy uniqueness of identity proofs (UIP), revealing that the identity type has a rich structure beyond mere reflexivity.

The significance of this insight cannot be overstated. In a 2015 email exchange, Vladimir Voevodsky—who later developed the univalence axiom—reflected on his initial reaction to identity types: “I was not inspired by [Hofmann-Streicher]. In fact I tried several times to understand what they are saying and never could. […] All the time before it I was hypnotized by the mantra that the only inhabitant of the Id type is reflexivity which made [them] useless from my point of view.” This “mantra” had obscured the rich higher structure of identity types for years, even from leading mathematicians. [

\omega

These results are profound but abstract: they show the existence of higher groupoid structure without providing explicit computational content for the witnesses of coherence laws. In contrast, the theory of

2.
Background

2.1.
Martin-Löf Type Theory and the Identity Type

In Martin-Löf’s intensional type theory
[
17
]
, the
identity type

\mathrm{Id}_{A}(a,b)
is formed for any type
A
and terms
a,b:A
. The traditional formulation provides:

•

Formation
: Given
A
type and
a,b:A
, we form
\mathrm{Id}_{A}(a,b)
type.

•

Introduction
: For any
a:A
, we have
\rho(a):\mathrm{Id}_{A}(a,a)
.

•

Elimination (J-rule)
: The recursor
J
allowing proofs by path induction.

The
J
-eliminator states that to prove a property
C(x,y,p)
for all
x,y:A
and
p:\mathrm{Id}_{A}(x,y)
, it suffices to prove
C(x,x,\rho(x))
for all
x:A
. While elegant, this formulation makes the identity type opaque: the only canonical proof is reflexivity, and other proofs must be constructed via the complex
J
-eliminator without revealing the computational steps that justify the equality.

A natural question arises from the Brouwer-Heyting-Kolmogorov interpretation of logic:
what constitutes a proof of an equality statement
t_{1}=t_{2}
?
The BHK interpretation provides answers for conjunction (a pair of proofs), disjunction (a proof of either disjunct), implication (a function transforming proofs), and quantifiers, but leaves equality unspecified. The computational paths approach fills this gap.

2.2.
The Definitional Equality of Type Theory

Before defining computational paths, we must understand the equality theory they are built upon. In type theory,
definitional equality
(also called judgmental or computational equality) is the relation that determines when two terms are considered identical by the type checker. For the
\Pi
-type (dependent function type), the standard rules include:

•

\beta
-reduction
:
(\lambda x.M)N=M[N/x]
— function application

•

\eta
-expansion
:
(\lambda x.Mx)=M
when
x\notin FV(M)
— extensionality

•

\xi
-rule
: From
M=M^{\prime}
, derive
\lambda x.M=\lambda x.M^{\prime}
— congruence under
\lambda

•

\mu
-rule
: From
M=M^{\prime}
, derive
NM=NM^{\prime}
— congruence in argument

•

\nu
-rule
: From
M=M^{\prime}
, derive
MN=M^{\prime}N
— congruence in function

•

\rho
-rule
:
M=M
— reflexivity

•

\sigma
-rule
: From
M=N
, derive
N=M
— symmetry

•

\tau
-rule
: From
M=N
and
N=P
, derive
M=P
— transitivity

Similar rules exist for other type formers (
\Sigma
-types, sum types, etc.). The key observation is that two terms
M
and
N
are definitionally equal if and only if there exists a
sequence of rule applications
transforming
M
into
N
. This sequence is precisely what we call a computational path.

2.3.
Computational Paths: Formal Definition

{defi}

[Computational Path
[
7
,
20
]
]
Let
a
and
b
be terms of type
A
. A
computational path

s
from
a
to
b
, written
a=_{s}b:A
, is a composition of rewrites where each rewrite is an application of one of the inference rules of the definitional equality theory. The path
s
is a term that
records
the sequence of rules applied.

{exa}

Consider the term
M\equiv(\lambda x.(\lambda y.yx)(\lambda w.zw))v
in the untyped
\lambda
-calculus. We can show
M=_{\beta\eta}zv
via the sequence:

(1)

(\lambda x.(\lambda y.yx)(\lambda w.zw))v
— original term

(2)

(\lambda x.(\lambda y.yx)z)v
— by
\eta
-reduction on
(\lambda w.zw)

(3)

(\lambda y.yv)z
— by
\beta
-reduction

(4)

zv
— by
\beta
-reduction

The computational path recording this derivation is:

s=\tau(\eta,\tau(\beta,\beta))

where each step names the rule applied and
\tau
composes consecutive steps.

The fundamental operations on computational paths are:

•

Reflexivity

\rho
: For any
a:A
, we have
a=_{\rho}a:A
. This is the trivial path of zero steps.

•

Symmetry

\sigma
: From
a=_{s}b:A
, derive
b=_{\sigma(s)}a:A
. This reverses the direction of each step in
s
.

•

Transitivity

\tau
: From
a=_{s}b:A
and
b=_{t}c:A
, derive
a=_{\tau(s,t)}c:A
. This concatenates the two sequences of rewrites.

Additionally, computational paths support
congruence
operations that lift paths through term constructors:

•

\mu_{f}
: From
a=_{s}b:A
and
f:A\to B
, derive
f(a)=_{\mu_{f}(s)}f(b):B
.

•

\xi
: From
M=_{s}N:B
(with
x:A
free), derive
\lambda x.M=_{\xi(s)}\lambda x.N:A\to B
.

2.4.
The Path-Based Identity Type

Using computational paths, we reformulate the identity type with explicit witnesses:

\dfrac{A\text{ type}\quad a:A\quad b:A}{\mathrm{Id}_{A}(a,b)\text{ type}}\;(\mathrm{Id}\text{-F})\qquad\dfrac{a=_{s}b:A}{s(a,b):\mathrm{Id}_{A}(a,b)}\;(\mathrm{Id}\text{-I})

The introduction rule states that any computational path
s
from
a
to
b
gives rise to a term
s(a,b)
inhabiting the identity type. This makes the
reason
for equality explicit: the path
s
records exactly which rules were applied to transform
a
into
b
.

The elimination rule uses a constructor
\mathrm{REWR}
(for “rewrite”):

\dfrac{m:\mathrm{Id}_{A}(a,b)\quad[a=_{g}b:A]\vdash h(g):C}{\mathrm{REWR}(m,\acute{g}.h(g)):C}\;(\mathrm{Id}\text{-E})

This says: if from an arbitrary path
g
witnessing
a=b
we can construct
h(g):C
, and we have an actual witness
m:\mathrm{Id}_{A}(a,b)
, then we can construct a term of type
C
by substituting the path underlying
m
for
g
in
h
.

2.5.
The
\mathrm{LND}_{\mathrm{EQ}}
-TRS: A Term Rewriting System for Paths

A crucial observation is that
different paths between the same endpoints may be equivalent
. For instance, applying symmetry twice returns to the original path:
\sigma(\sigma(s))
should be equivalent to
s
. Similarly, composing a path with the reflexive path should yield the original:
\tau(s,\rho)\sim s
.

These equivalences are captured by the
\mathrm{LND}_{\mathrm{EQ}}
-TRS (Labelled Natural Deduction Equality Term Rewriting System), originating in the work of de Queiroz
[
8
]
and further developed in
[
22
]
. This system provides a complete set of rewrite rules for computational paths, organized into several families:

2.5.1.
Basic Reductions

The first family handles interactions between the fundamental operations:

\displaystyle\sigma(\rho)

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow\rho

(
\rhd_{sr}
)

\displaystyle\sigma(\sigma(r))

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow r

(
\rhd_{ss}
)

\displaystyle\tau(r,\sigma(r))

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow\rho

(
\rhd_{tr}
)

\displaystyle\tau(\sigma(r),r)

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow\rho

(
\rhd_{tsr}
)

\displaystyle\tau(r,\rho)

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow r

(
\rhd_{trr}
)

\displaystyle\tau(\rho,r)

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow r

(
\rhd_{tlr}
)

Rule
\rhd_{sr}
says the symmetry of reflexivity is reflexivity. Rule
\rhd_{ss}
says double symmetry cancels. Rules
\rhd_{tr}
and
\rhd_{tsr}
say a path composed with its inverse yields reflexivity. Rules
\rhd_{trr}
and
\rhd_{tlr}
are the unit laws for transitivity.

2.5.2.
Associativity

The associativity of transitivity is captured by:

\tau(\tau(r,s),t)\rightsquigarrow\tau(r,\tau(s,t))

(
\rhd_{tt}
)

This rule is fundamental for the groupoid structure: it says that the order of composing three paths doesn’t matter (up to rewrite equivalence).

2.5.3.
Distributivity of Symmetry

Symmetry distributes over transitivity with order reversal:

\sigma(\tau(r,s))\rightsquigarrow\tau(\sigma(s),\sigma(r))

(
\rhd_{stss}
)

This reflects the fact that reversing a composite path requires reversing each component and their order.

2.5.4.
Congruence Rules

The system includes rules for how congruence operations interact with the basic operations. For instance:

\displaystyle\mu_{f}(\tau(p,q))

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow\tau(\mu_{f}(p),\mu_{f}(q))

(
\rhd_{tf}
)

\displaystyle\mu_{f}(\sigma(p))

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow\sigma(\mu_{f}(p))

(
\rhd_{sm}
)

\displaystyle\mu_{g}(\mu_{f}(p))

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow\mu_{g\circ f}(p)

(
\rhd_{cf}
)

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{id}}(p)

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow p

(
\rhd_{ci}
)

These say that applying a function commutes with path operations, and that function composition corresponds to composition of congruence operations.

2.5.5.
\beta\eta
-Reductions for Paths

The system also includes rules that capture interactions between path operations and the type-theoretic
\beta\eta
rules. For example:

\nu(\xi(r))\rightsquigarrow r

(
\rhd_{mxl}
)

This says that lifting a path under
\lambda
and then applying it (
\nu
) recovers the original path.

The full
\mathrm{LND}_{\mathrm{EQ}}
-TRS contains over 40 rules covering all interactions between path operations, congruences for all type formers, and substitution operations. We refer to
[
22
]
for the complete system.

2.6.
Rewrite Equivalence

{defi}

[Rewrite Step Relation]
A
rewrite step

p\rightsquigarrow q
holds when
q
is obtained from
p
by applying one of the
\mathrm{LND}_{\mathrm{EQ}}
-TRS rules at some position in
p
.

{defi}

[Step Type]

For paths
p,q:\mathrm{Path}(a,b)
, the type
\mathrm{Step}(p,q)
is defined as the type of witnesses that
p\rightsquigarrow q
in a single rewrite step. Formally:

\mathrm{Step}(p,q):=\{s\mid s\text{ witnesses }p\rightsquigarrow q\text{ by a single rule application}\}

Crucially,
\mathrm{Step}
is
propositional
: for any
p,q
, the type
\mathrm{Step}(p,q)
has at most one inhabitant. This reflects that we care only whether a rewrite step exists, not which rule was applied.

{defi}

[Rewrite Closure]
The
rewrite closure

\mathrm{Rw}
is the reflexive-transitive closure of the rewrite step relation.

{defi}

[Rewrite Equivalence]
The
rewrite equivalence
relation
\sim
on
\mathrm{Path}(a,b)
is the equivalence closure of
\mathrm{Rw}
:

p\sim q\iff\exists r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}:p=r_{1},\;r_{n}=q,\;\forall i:r_{i}\rightsquigarrow r_{i+1}\text{ or }r_{i+1}\rightsquigarrow r_{i}

Theorem 1

(Properties of
\sim

[
22
, Chapter 4]
)
.

The rewrite equivalence relation satisfies:

(1)

Reflexivity
:
p\sim p
for all paths
p
.

(2)

Symmetry
: If
p\sim q
then
q\sim p
.

(3)

Transitivity
: If
p\sim q
and
q\sim r
then
p\sim r
.

(4)

Congruence
: If
p\sim p^{\prime}
and
q\sim q^{\prime}
then
\tau(p,q)\sim\tau(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
, and similarly for
\sigma
and congruence operations.

Remark 2

(Proof Irrelevance and Step Equality)
.

Crucially, in our formalization
\sim
is defined as a
propositional
relation: for any paths
p,q:\mathrm{Path}(a,b)
, the type “
p\sim q
” is a proposition (has at most one inhabitant). This means that if
p\sim q
holds, there is a unique witness of this fact.

This proof-irrelevance has two important consequences:

(1)

It provides semantic justification for the canonicity axiom: since Step is proof-irrelevant, any two derivations between the same endpoints represent “the same” computational witness and should connect to the same canonical derivation.

(2)

It ensures that the step equality axiom (
\mathsf{step\_eq}
) holds: any two steps with the same source and target are equal.

Combined with the normalization and confluence of the
\mathrm{LND}_{\mathrm{EQ}}
-TRS
[
8
,
19
]
, these properties justify the canonicity axiom from which contractibility at dimension
\geq 3
is derived.

2.7.
Higher Categories and Weak
\omega
-Groupoids

We now recall the categorical structures that computational paths give rise to.

{defi}

[Globular Set]
A
globular set

X
is a sequence of sets
X(0),X(1),X(2),\ldots
equipped with source and target functions
\mathrm{src},\mathrm{tgt}:X(n+1)\to X(n)
satisfying the
globular identities
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{src}(\mathrm{src}(c))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{src}(\mathrm{tgt}(c))

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tgt}(\mathrm{src}(c))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tgt}(\mathrm{tgt}(c))

(2)

for all
c\in X(n+2)
.

The globular identities ensure that higher cells have consistent boundaries. Intuitively, a 2-cell goes between two parallel 1-cells (1-cells with the same source and target), and a 3-cell goes between two parallel 2-cells, and so on.

Elements of
X(n)
are called
n
-cells
. In our setting:

•

0-cells are points (elements of type
A
)

•

1-cells are paths between points

•

2-cells are “paths between paths” (witnessed by
\sim
)

•

n
-cells for
n\geq 3
are higher coherence witnesses

{defi}

[Weak
\omega
-Category
[
14
,
4
]
]
A
weak
\omega
-category
consists of:

(1)

A globular set
\mathrm{Cell}_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}

(2)

An identity operation
\mathrm{id}:\mathrm{Cell}_{n}\to\mathrm{Cell}_{n+1}
for each
n

(3)

A composition operation
\circ:\mathrm{Cell}_{n+1}\times_{\mathrm{Cell}_{n}}\mathrm{Cell}_{n+1}\to\mathrm{Cell}_{n+1}
for composable cells

(4)

Coherence witnesses (associators, unitors) at each level

(5)

Higher coherences (pentagon, triangle, etc.) relating the coherence witnesses

(6)

Coherences at all higher levels, satisfying a contractibility condition

The “weak” qualifier means that categorical laws (associativity, unit laws) hold only up to coherent isomorphism, not strict equality.

{defi}

[Weak
\omega
-Groupoid]
A
weak
\omega
-groupoid
is a weak
\omega
-category in which every cell is invertible: for each
(n+1)
-cell
f
, there exists an
(n+1)
-cell
\mathrm{inv}(f)
with
\mathrm{src}(\mathrm{inv}(f))=\mathrm{tgt}(f)
,
\mathrm{tgt}(\mathrm{inv}(f))=\mathrm{src}(f)
, together with
(n+2)
-cells witnessing
\mathrm{inv}(f)\circ f\sim\mathrm{id}
and
f\circ\mathrm{inv}(f)\sim\mathrm{id}
.

The fundamental insight connecting type theory to higher category theory is:

Theorem 3

(Lumsdaine
[
16
]
, van den Berg–Garner
[
23
]
)
.

For any type
A
in Martin-Löf type theory, the identity types on

form a weak
\omega
-groupoid.

The goal of this paper is to prove an analogous result for computational paths: that the explicit rewrite structure provides a weak
\omega
-groupoid with computable coherence witnesses.

3.
Cell Structure

We now define the cells at each dimension for the weak
\omega
-groupoid of computational paths.

3.1.
Dimension 0: Points

{defi}

[0-cells]
For a type
A
, the 0-cells are simply the elements of
A
:

\mathrm{Cell}_{0}(A):=A

3.2.
Dimension 1: Paths

{defi}

[1-cells]
A 1-cell is a computational path bundled with its endpoints:

\mathrm{Cell}_{1}(A):=\{(a,b,p)\mid a,b:A,\,p:\mathrm{Path}(a,b)\}

with source and target:

\displaystyle\mathrm{src}:\mathrm{Cell}_{1}(A)

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Cell}_{0}(A),\quad\mathrm{src}(a,b,p)=a

\displaystyle\mathrm{tgt}:\mathrm{Cell}_{1}(A)

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Cell}_{0}(A),\quad\mathrm{tgt}(a,b,p)=b

3.3.
Dimension 2: Derivations Between Paths

{defi}

[2-cells]

A 2-cell is a
derivation
between two paths with the same endpoints. Derivations are inductively defined:

\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)\quad\text{for }p,q:\mathrm{Path}(a,b)

with constructors:

•

\rho(p):\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,p)
— identity derivation

•

\mathrm{step}(s):\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
where
s:\mathrm{Step}(p,q)
— single rewrite step

•

\mathrm{inv}(d):\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(q,p)
where
d:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
— inverse

•

d_{1}\circ d_{2}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,r)
where
d_{1}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
and
d_{2}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(q,r)
— vertical composition

Source and target maps return the boundary paths. Given the implicit endpoints
a,b
with
p,q:\mathrm{Path}(a,b)
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{src}(d)

\displaystyle:=(a,b,p):\mathrm{Cell}_{1}(A)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tgt}(d)

\displaystyle:=(a,b,q):\mathrm{Cell}_{1}(A)

Thus
\mathrm{src}
and
\mathrm{tgt}
embed the path back into the bundled 1-cell type. When working fibrewise (with fixed
a,b
), we simply have
\mathrm{src}(d)=p
and
\mathrm{tgt}(d)=q
.

Remark 4

(Key design: Derivations as data)
.

Crucially, 2-cells are
data
, not mere propositions. This means different derivations between the same paths are distinguishable. The groupoid laws (associativity, units, inverses) hold
up to higher cells
, not as strict equalities.

This is essential for a genuine weak
\omega
-groupoid: the coherence witnesses are non-trivial 3-cells.

Lemma 5

(Equivalence of
\sim
and
\mathrm{Cell}_{2}
Inhabitedness)
.

For paths
p,q:\mathrm{Path}(a,b)
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

p\sim q
(rewrite equivalence holds)

(2)

\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
is inhabited (there exists a derivation from
p
to
q
)

Proof 3.1
.

(1\Rightarrow 2)
: By induction on the derivation of

•

If
p=q
(reflexivity), then
\rho(p):\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,p)
.

•

If

via a step

, then
\mathrm{step}(s):\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
.

•

If
q\sim p
with witness

, then
\mathrm{inv}(d):\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
.

•

If
p\sim r
and
r\sim q
with witnesses
d_{1},d_{2}
, then
d_{1}\circ d_{2}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
.

(2\Rightarrow 1)
: By induction on the derivation
d:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
:

•

\rho(p)
gives
p\sim p
by reflexivity.

•

\mathrm{step}(s)
gives
p\sim q
since

implies
p\rightsquigarrow q
.

•

\mathrm{inv}(d)
gives

p\sim q
by symmetry again.

•

by transitivity (IH on both).

Remark 6

(Clarification on Normal Forms)
.

An important consequence of Lemma
5
combined with Assumption
is: if
\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
is inhabited (i.e., there exists a derivation
d:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
), then
\|p\|=\|q\|
. This follows because
\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
inhabited implies
p\sim q
by Lemma
5
, and for parallel paths the normal forms coincide by Assumption
.

Note that the converse also holds: any two parallel paths

have
\|p\|=\|q\|
(by Assumption
), and hence the canonical derivation
\gamma_{p,q}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
exists. Thus
\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
is
always
inhabited for parallel paths. The distinction is that
\sim
is a proposition (proof-irrelevant), while
\mathrm{Cell}_{2}
is data (different derivations are distinguishable).

3.6.
The Canonicity Axiom

We now introduce the key axiom that grounds contractibility in the normalization algorithm. First, we establish notation for the normal form construction.

{defi}

[Normal Form]

Every path
p:\mathrm{Path}(a,b)
has a
normal form

\|p\|
, obtained by the normalization algorithm of the
\mathrm{LND}_{\mathrm{EQ}}
-TRS
[
8
,
19
]
. Concretely,
\|p\|
is the canonical representative of the equivalence class
[p]
under the rewrite relation
\sim
.

Lemma 7

(Normal Forms and Rewrite Equivalence)
.

If
p,q:\mathrm{Path}(a,b)
are rewrite-equivalent (i.e., there exists
d:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
), then
\|p\|=\|q\|
.

Proof 3.2
.

By Definition
3.6
, the normal form
\|p\|
is the canonical representative of the equivalence class
[p]
under the rewrite relation
\sim
. If
p\sim q
, then
p
and
q
belong to the same equivalence class, so they share the same canonical representative:
\|p\|=\|q\|
.

Concretely, since
\mathrm{LND}_{\mathrm{EQ}}
-TRS is terminating and confluent
[
8
,
19
]
, both
p
and
q
reduce to a common normal form.

Remark 8

(What This Lemma Does Not Claim)
.

Note that Lemma
7
does
not
assert that all parallel paths (paths with the same endpoints) share a normal form. That would be a much stronger claim, equivalent to saying that every hom-set
\mathrm{Path}(a,b)
consists of a single
\sim
-equivalence class.

Instead, the lemma only applies to paths that are
already known to be rewrite-equivalent
. This distinction is crucial: 2-cells witness rewrite equivalence, not mere parallelism. The weak
\omega
-groupoid structure is non-trivial precisely because different paths between the same endpoints may belong to different equivalence classes, with 2-cells only connecting paths within the same class.

{defi}

[Normalizing Derivation]

For each path
p
, the normalization algorithm produces a finite rewrite chain

p=r_{0}\rightsquigarrow r_{1}\rightsquigarrow\cdots\rightsquigarrow r_{k}=\|p\|

We define the
normalizing derivation

\delta_{p}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,\|p\|)
by composing the corresponding 2-cells:

\delta_{p}:=\mathrm{step}(s_{1})\circ\mathrm{step}(s_{2})\circ\cdots\circ\mathrm{step}(s_{k})

where each
s_{i}:\mathrm{Step}(r_{i-1},r_{i})
is a single rewrite step in the chain. (If
p
is already in normal form,
\delta_{p}=\rho_{p}
.)

{defi}

[Canonical Derivation]

For paths
p,q:\mathrm{Path}(a,b)
that are rewrite-equivalent (i.e., such that
\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
is inhabited), we define the
canonical derivation

\gamma_{p,q}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
as the following composition:

\gamma_{p,q}\;:=\;\delta_{p}\circ\mathrm{inv}(\delta_{q})

where
\delta_{p}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,\|p\|)
is the normalizing derivation from Definition
3.6
.

By Lemma
7
, since
p\sim q
, we have
\|p\|=\|q\|
. Thus
\delta_{p}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,\|p\|)
and
\mathrm{inv}(\delta_{q}):\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(\|q\|,q)=\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(\|p\|,q)
, making the composition well-typed:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\|p\|=\|q\|}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{inv}(\delta_{q})}

The canonical derivation
\gamma_{p,q}
is only defined when
p
and
q
are already known to be rewrite-equivalent. It provides a
distinguished
2-cell among all 2-cells connecting
p
to
q
—the one that factors through the shared normal form.

{defi}

[Canonicity Axiom]

The
canonicity axiom
states that every derivation is connected to the canonical derivation by a 3-cell:

\mathsf{can}_{d}:\mathrm{Cell}_{3}(d,\gamma_{p,q})\quad\text{for each }d:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)

Equivalently,
\mathsf{can}
is a constructor of
\mathrm{MetaStep}_{3}
:

\mathsf{can}:\prod_{d:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)}\mathrm{MetaStep}_{3}(d,\gamma_{p,q})

At higher levels (
n\geq 4
), analogous canonicity axioms
\mathsf{can}_{n}
are defined, with contractibility derived in the same way.

Axiomatization:
In this formalization, we
axiomatize
the existence of the 3-cell
\mathsf{can}_{d}
. This is the key primitive that grounds contractibility at dimension 3 and above. The axiom is not derived from more primitive principles within our formal system, but is justified semantically by the meta-theoretical properties described in Remark
9
.

Remark 9

(Computational Content)
.

The canonicity axiom is grounded in the concrete normalization algorithm:

(1)

The normalizing derivation
\delta_{p}
exists for every path (normalization terminates)
[
8
,
19
]

(2)

For rewrite-equivalent paths
p\sim q
, the canonical derivation
\gamma_{p,q}
is uniquely determined by factoring through the shared normal form

(3)

Confluence of the
\mathrm{LND}_{\mathrm{EQ}}
-TRS ensures that rewrite-equivalent paths share a normal form (Lemma
7
)

Unlike a bare contractibility axiom, the canonicity axiom connects each derivation to a
specific, computable
target. Importantly, it only quantifies over
existing
derivations
d:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
, not over all parallel paths.

Remark 10

(Comparison with HoTT)
.

The canonicity axiom plays a role analogous to J-elimination (path induction) in HoTT:

3.7.
Contractibility from the Canonicity Axiom

Theorem 11

(Contractibility at dimension
\geq 3
)
.

For any
n\geq 2
and any two parallel
n
-cells
c_{1},c_{2}
(with
\mathrm{src}(c_{1})=\mathrm{src}(c_{2})
and
\mathrm{tgt}(c_{1})=\mathrm{tgt}(c_{2})
), there exists an
(n+1)

\chi_{n}(c_{1},c_{2}):\mathrm{Cell}_{n+1}(c_{1},c_{2})

In particular, any two parallel 2-cells (derivations) are connected by a 3-cell, and so on for higher dimensions.

Proof 3.3
.

At level 3, contractibility is
derived
from the canonicity axiom. Given parallel derivations
d_{1},d_{2}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,q)
, we construct:

\chi_{3}(d_{1},d_{2})\;:=\;\mathsf{can}_{d_{1}}\circ\mathrm{inv}(\mathsf{can}_{d_{2}})\;:\;\mathrm{Cell}_{3}(d_{1},d_{2})

Both
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are connected to the same canonical derivation
\gamma_{p,q}
, so composing
\mathsf{can}_{d_{1}}:\mathrm{Cell}_{3}(d_{1},\gamma_{p,q})
with
\mathrm{inv}(\mathsf{can}_{d_{2}}):\mathrm{Cell}_{3}(\gamma_{p,q},d_{2})
yields a 3-cell from
d_{1}
to
d_{2}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{p,q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{can}_{d_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{inv}(\mathsf{can}_{d_{2}})}

At higher levels (
n\geq 4
), analogous canonicity axioms
\mathsf{can}_{n}
are defined, with contractibility derived in the same way.

Remark 12

(Loop contraction as a special case)
.

Loop contraction follows from the canonicity axiom and the groupoid laws. For a loop
d:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(p,p)
, we have
\gamma_{p,p}=\delta_{p}\circ\mathrm{inv}(\delta_{p})\sim\rho_{p}
by the inverse law. Thus:

\chi_{3}(d,\rho_{p})\;:\;\mathrm{Cell}_{3}(d,\rho_{p})

shows that every loop on
p
is connected to the reflexivity derivation.

Remark 13

(Batanin-style Contractibility)
.

The
\omega
-groupoid is
contractible at dimension
k
if any two parallel
(k-1)
-cells are connected by a
k
-cell. This is the
Batanin-style
contractibility condition for higher coherence structures—it means that higher hom-spaces are contractible.

Important:
This is
not
the same as global homotopy contractibility (being equivalent to a point). Rather, it says that at sufficiently high dimensions (here,
\geq 3
), all parallel cells are connected. The underlying 0-cells (points of
A
) and 1-cells (paths) may have non-trivial structure; only the higher coherence data becomes trivial.

The canonicity axiom at level 3 directly yields contractibility at all dimensions
\geq 3
.

6.1.
Associativity

Theorem 18

(Associativity Coherence)
.

For composable 1-cells
f,g,h
, there exists a 2-cell:

\alpha_{f,g,h}:(f\circ g)\circ h\longrightarrow f\circ(g\circ h)

At higher dimensions, associativity is witnessed by cells whose boundaries are the two associated compositions.

Proof 6.1
.

Dimension 1:
Let
f=(a,b,p)
,
g=(b,c,q)
,
h=(c,d,r)
. Then:

•

(f\circ g)\circ h=(a,d,\tau(\tau(p,q),r))

•

f\circ(g\circ h)=(a,d,\tau(p,\tau(q,r)))

Both have the same source
a
and target
d
. The rewrite system includes the rule:

\tau(\tau(p,q),r)\rightsquigarrow\tau(p,\tau(q,r))\quad(\text{associativity rule }\rhd_{tt})

Thus, the two paths are
\sim
-equivalent via the rewrite rule
\rhd_{tt}
. The associator 2-cell is the derivation:

\alpha_{f,g,h}:=\mathrm{step}(\rhd_{tt}):\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(\tau(\tau(p,q),r),\,\tau(p,\tau(q,r)))

This witnesses that the two associated compositions are equivalent paths.

Dimension 2:
For derivations
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}
, we construct
\alpha_{d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}}:\mathrm{Cell}_{3}
using the
\mathsf{vcomp\_assoc}

\mathrm{MetaStep}_{3}
(Definition

7.
Higher Coherences

A weak
\omega

7.1.
Whiskering

Before stating the pentagon and triangle coherences, we define the whiskering operations that appear in their formulations.

{defi}

[Horizontal Composition]

Given 2-cells
\alpha:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(f,f^{\prime})
where
f,f^{\prime}:a\to b
, and
\beta:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(g,g^{\prime})
where
g,g^{\prime}:b\to c
, the
horizontal composition
is:

\alpha\star_{h}\beta:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(f\circ g,f^{\prime}\circ g^{\prime})

Horizontal composition combines 2-cells “side by side” (along a shared boundary point), in contrast to vertical composition
\circ
which combines 2-cells “end to end” (along a shared 1-cell).

In a general 2-category, horizontal composition is a primitive operation. In our setting, we can define it via whiskering:

\alpha\star_{h}\beta:=\mathrm{whiskerRight}(\alpha,g)\circ\mathrm{whiskerLeft}(f^{\prime},\beta)

or equivalently:

\alpha\star_{h}\beta:=\mathrm{whiskerLeft}(f,\beta)\circ\mathrm{whiskerRight}(\alpha,g^{\prime})

The interchange law (Theorem
27
) ensures these two definitions yield equivalent 2-cells.

{defi}

[Whiskering]

Given a 2-cell
\alpha:\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(f,f^{\prime})
where
f,f^{\prime}:a\to b
, and 1-cells
g:b\to c
and
h:z\to a
, we define:

•

Right whiskering
:
\mathrm{whiskerRight}(\alpha,g):\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(f\circ g,f^{\prime}\circ g)

•

Left whiskering
:
\mathrm{whiskerLeft}(h,\alpha):\mathrm{Cell}_{2}(h\circ f,h\circ f^{\prime})

These operations express the functoriality of composition: composing with a fixed 1-cell preserves 2-cells.

Remark 24

(Whiskering as Horizontal Composition)
.

Whiskering is horizontal composition with an identity 2-cell:

\displaystyle\mathrm{whiskerRight}(\alpha,g)

\displaystyle=\alpha\star_{h}\mathrm{id}_{g}

\displaystyle\mathrm{whiskerLeft}(h,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}_{h}\star_{h}\alpha

In our construction, whiskering is a primitive operation in
\mathrm{MetaStep}_{3}
(Definition
3.4
), and horizontal composition is derived from it.

\displaystyle\mathrm{pentagonLeft}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{f\circ g,h,k}\circ\alpha_{f,g,h\circ k}

\displaystyle\mathrm{pentagonRight}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{whiskerRight}(\alpha_{f,g,h},k)\circ\alpha_{f,g\circ h,k})\circ\mathrm{whiskerLeft}(f,\alpha_{g,h,k})

Remark 31

The canonicity axiom is justified semantically by the normalization and confluence properties of the

-TRS rewrite system
[
8
,
19
]
, combined with the fact that rewrite steps live in a proof-irrelevant universe. Because Step is proof-irrelevant and the system is normalizing and confluent, all derivations between the same endpoints can be connected to the same canonical derivation

Remark 32

(Role of the Canonicity Axiom)

The canonicity axiom plays a role analogous to the J-elimination rule in Martin-Löf type theory. Just as J cannot be derived from pure type theory but must be postulated (justified by the computation rule), the canonicity axiom cannot be derived from pure groupoid algebra but is justified by the computational properties of the underlying rewrite system—specifically, the existence of normal forms and the canonical derivation
\gamma_{p,q}=\delta_{p}\circ\mathrm{inv}(\delta_{q})
. This axiom is the key ingredient that elevates our structure from a weak 2-groupoid to a full weak
\omega
-groupoid. Importantly, unlike a bare contractibility axiom, the canonicity axiom has a concrete, canonical target grounded in the normalization algorithm.

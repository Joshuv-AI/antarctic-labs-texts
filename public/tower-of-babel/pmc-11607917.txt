Effect of blood oxidative stress indicators on oral mucositis in patients undergoing radiotherapy for nasopharyngeal carcinoma
Authors: HongXia Pang, SuZhen Li, XiaoYi Fu, ShaoMin Wen, DanYu Chen, XiaoChun Sun, Yong Chen, Song Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo investigate the correlation of total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-PX), and advanced oxidized protein product (AOPP) levels in the progression of oral mucositis (OM) during nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) radiotherapy.MethodsThis prospective study included 102 patients diagnosed with NPC and eligible for radiotherapy indications. T-AOC, GSH-PX, and AOPP were measured before treatment (T0) and during the second week (T1), fourth week (T2), and sixth week (T3) of the intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) period. Patients were categorized into severe OM (SOM) (n = 21) and Non-SOM (n = 81) groups at week 6. Serum T-AOC, GSH-PX, and AOPP levels were compared at different time periods. The trends of these markers in the two groups during the course of treatment were analyzed. Finally, stepwise logistic regression was conducted to analyze the risk factors for SOM in NPC patients undergoing IMRT.ResultsAt T1 and T2 of IMRT, T-AOC and GSH-PX were higher in the SOM group than in the Non-SOM group; At T3, T-AOC and GSH-PX were lower in the SOM group than in the Non-SOM group. T-AOC and GSH-PX had similar change trends in Non-SOM or SOM patients, i.e., T-AOC levels continued to increase at T2 and decreased at T3, and GSH-PX increased significantly at T2 and decreased at T3. Of interest was the fact that T-AOC and GSH-PX decreased significantly more in SOM patients than in Non-SOM patients at T3, and that AOPP levels were in a constant state of increase in SOM patients. Preoperative hypoproteinemia, the absence of oral mucosal protective agents, and the receipt of synchronous chemotherapy were independent risk factors for increasing the course of OM in patients with NPC.ConclusionsNPC patients undergoing IMRT exhibit dynamic alterations in blood T-AOC, GSH-PX, and AOPP. Notably, the persistent elevation of these biomarkers, particularly AOPP, is significantly associated with the progression of SOM in these patients. These findings underscore the involvement of oxidative stress imbalance in the pathogenesis of oral mucositis following IMRT.

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) belongs to malignant tumors of the head and neck [1]. Due to the complex anatomical structure of nasopharynx and the high sensitivity of NPC to radiation, a comprehensive treatment plan based on radiotherapy is often adopted [2]. Despite the damage to normal tissues, this method can improve the local control rate and overall survival rate of NPC [3]. Continuous high-dose radiation can inhibit epithelial cell renewal and damage the oral mucosa and microecological environment, which in turn leads to radiation-induced oral mucositis (RIOM), with 99.0% of patients experiencing varying degrees of oral mucositis (OM) [4]. Clinical manifestations of OM include localized inflammation, pain, dysphagia, and secondary oral infections, which increase the risk of malnutrition in patients, and in severe cases, even lead to systemic infection, delaying the course of treatment and affecting prognosis, prolonging hospitalization and increasing associated economic costs [5, 6]. The WHO defines severe oral mucositis (SOM) as grade 3–4 OM, and these patients are more likely to trigger secondary infection of the oral mucosa, or even extensive systemic inflammatory response, which in turn leads to interruption of radiotherapy [7]. At present, studies on the prevention and treatment of OM mainly focus on symptomatic treatment [8, 9]. Therefore, early identification of high-risk groups for SOM is crucial for disease prevention and intervention so that measures can be taken to reduce the incidence of SOM and thus improve the treatment of NPC.

OM occurs mainly because radiation can cause damage to oral mucosal cells and peripheral blood vessels, leading to normal structural changes and dysfunction of the oral mucosa, which in turn triggers an inflammatory response and an impaired immune system, resulting in erosions, ulcers, and infections [10]. During radiotherapy, ionizing radiation destroys intracellular biomolecules, leading to the production of a large number of free radicals, thus triggering oxidative stress (OS) [11]. OS is a state of imbalance between oxidative and antioxidant effects in the body [12]. Regarding the bidirectional role of OS during radiotherapy, on one hand, OS can promote apoptosis and necrosis of tumor cells; on the other hand, excessive free radicals are able to attack cell membranes, DNA, and other key structures, causing cellular damage and apoptosis, which leads to inflammatory reactions such as tissue edema and dilation of small blood vessels [13]. Currently, more attention on RIOM is focused on oral microorganisms [5, 14]. The correlation between OS and OM progression during radiotherapy for NPC is unclear.

Total antioxidant activity (T-AOC) is the total effect of all antioxidant substances in the body working together, and is used to measure the body’s ability to scavenge free radicals and resist oxidative damage. Glutathione peroxidase (GSH-PX) is a selenium-containing enzymatic antioxidant [15]. Advanced oxidized protein products (AOPP) are a class of substances produced by oxidative modification of proteins during OS [16]. Antioxidants may be elevated during the early stages of OS or in mild OS states in response to increased stress load [17]. This is because the antioxidant system in the body activates defense mechanisms to increase the synthesis and release of antioxidants, thereby increasing T-AOC. However, under prolonged or intense OS, antioxidants may gradually decrease if the defense capacity of the antioxidant system is insufficient to cope with the sustained oxidative damage [18].

Based on the above background, it is necessary to investigate the correlation between changes in blood antioxidant and peroxidation product levels and OM progression in patients with NPC during radiotherapy. We investigated serum T-AOC, GSH-PX and AOPP levels in NPC patients before radiotherapy, 2 weeks/4 weeks/6 weeks of radiotherapy to explore the levels and trends of serum markers in patients with different OM grades.

Materials and methods

Subjects

NPC patients admitted to The First Affiliated Hospital of Hainan Medical University from June 2022 to December 2023 were selected. Inclusion criteria: ① age ≥ 18 years, meeting the diagnostic criteria of NPC [19] and radiotherapy indications; ② first diagnosis, first course of radiotherapy; ③ no distal metastasis; ④ no oral mucous membrane injury or oral infection before radiotherapy; and ⑤ complete clinical data. Exclusion criteria: ① combined psychiatric diseases, other malignant tumors, hematologic diseases, immune system diseases; ② severe cardiopulmonary, hepatic, and renal dysfunction; ③ difficulty in opening the mouth or other diseases affecting the observation of oral mucous membranes; ④ failure to cooperate with the study or missing visits; and ⑤ women in the period of pregnancy and breastfeeding. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Hainan Medical University. All subjects were included after signing the informed consent form.

General clinical information

Clinical data of patients were collected: gender, age, body mass index (BMI), KPS score, smoking (consecutively or cumulatively for 6 months or more), comorbidities (hypertension and diabetes mellitus), type of pathology, TNM staging, UICC stage, synchronized chemotherapy, albumin level before radiotherapy, use of oral mucosal protective agents (including Amifostine, Deproteinized Calf Blood Extractives I, recombinant human interleukin-11, etc.), use of antimicrobials, and Dmean and Dmax of the oral mucosa irradiated after removal of PVT.

Intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT)

All patients received intensity-modulated radiotherapy, the whole process was done by Variantruebeam linear gas pedal 6MVX line irradiation, and the radiotherapy plan was formulated in accordance with the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology guidelines for NPC diagnosis and treatment [19]. NPC irradiation target areas included nasopharynx gross tumor volume (GTVnx), positive lymph nodes (GTVnd), and clinical target volume (CTV). The gross tumor volume was outlined based on magnetic resonance imaging, computed tomography, and other imaging results. For patients receiving chemotherapy, soft tissue areas of GTVnx and GTVnd were delineated after chemotherapy. CTV1 refers to the tumor shrinkage that includes GTVnx and surrounding subclinical lesion areas (5 mm enlargement around GTVnx), and also soft tissue areas after chemotherapy. CTV2 includes CTV1 and its surrounding 5 mm enlargement, GTVnd, and potential cervical lymph node metastatic areas. Radiotherapy dose: 68–76 Gy/30–33f for planned target volume (PTV)–GTVnx, 60–64 Gy/30–33f for PTV–CTV1, and 50–54 Gy/30–33f for PTV–CTV2.

Measurement of biological indicators

Collected 3–5 mL of fasting venous blood from patients before treatment (T0) and during the second week (T1), fourth week (T2), and sixth week (T3) of IMRT. Whole blood was centrifuged at 3000 r/min and a radius of 3 cm for 10 min. The upper serum was collected and stored in a − 80 °C freezer for testing. Measured according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits, including T-AOC, GSH-PX, and AOPP were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China).

OM evaluation criteria

OM toxicity was assessed according to the Radiation Therapy Oncology Group (RTOG) Evaluation Criteria: grade 0: no change from baseline; grade I: congestion/mild pain, no analgesia required; grade II: lamellar mucositis, inflammatory serous blood secretions/moderate pain requiring analgesic treatments; grade III: fused fibrous mucositis/severe pain requiring narcotics; grade IV. Ulceration, hemorrhage or necrosis. During the IMRT, two well-trained medical staff conducted oral examinations on all patients at the T1, T2, and T3 of treatment, and recorded their OM status. When the diagnoses of two medical staff are inconsistent, a senior oral pathologist makes the final diagnosis. Based on which patients were categorized into the SOM group (grades III–IV) and the Non-SOM group (grades 0–II) according to their OM grade at T3.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 20.0 software. Count data were expressed as frequencies (n) and ratios (%) and were tested using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Continuous values were used to determine the normality of the data using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For normal distribution, the measured data were shown as mean ± standard deviation, and data for continuous variables with skewed distribution were expressed as median (M1, M3). Comparisons of two independent samples were made using Student’s t test (normal distribution) or Mann–Whitney U test (skewed distribution). Paired samples with more than one group were analyzed by repeated measures ANOVA or Friedman’s test, and then Tukey’s multiple comparison test or Dunn’s multiple comparison test was applied for post hoc correction. Variables that were statistically significant (P < 0.05) in the univariate analysis were included in the stepwise logistic regression analysis, and age and gender were included as correction factors in the regression analysis to screen for risk factors for SOM in NPC patients after radiotherapy. Images were plotted using bioinformatics (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical baseline characteristics of patients

A total of 102 patients diagnosed with NPC and treated with radiotherapy were enrolled, with an age range of 66.3 ± 12.9 years, 71 males and 31 females. The overall incidence of OM in these patients after the first radiotherapy treatment (usually 6–7 weeks) was 96.0% at week 6, with no grade IV OM occurring, 21 patients in grade III, 56 patients in grade II, and 22 patients in grade I. The total incidence of OM at week 6 was 96.0%, and no grade IV OM occurred. Following the initiation of treatment, OM assessments are performed biweekly. Analysis of risk factors for SOM in NPC patients

Finally, stepwise logistic regression analysis was used to screen for factors associated with progression to SOM after radiotherapy in NPC patients. Variables that had statistically significant differences in the univariate analysis were included in the analysis, where age and gender entered the analysis as correction factors. The results, as shown in Fig. 3, showed that hypoproteinemia (albumin < 35 g/L), absence of oral mucosal protective agents, and synchronized chemotherapy were independent risk factors for the eventual progression to SOM in patients with NPC, in which having preoperative hypoproteinemia was 2.256 times more risky than that of normal patients for SOM events, absence of oral mucosal protective agents was 1.895 times more risky than that of those who did use them, and synchronized chemotherapy was 1.564 times higher than patients who did not receive chemotherapy.Fig. 3Stepwise logistic regression analysis of the occurrence of SOM after treatment in patients with NPC. Discussion

The incidence of OM in NPC patients receiving radiotherapy is 85–100% [20], and the incidence of grade III and higher OM is 28–35% [21]. This study showed that the incidence of OM in NPC patients was 96.0%, of which 20.59% were ≥ grade III. Radiotherapy irradiates tumor cells through high-energy rays, causing an intracellular OS response. Such a reaction leads to apoptosis or necrosis of tumor cells, but also damages normal cells by activating a series of signaling pathways involved in apoptosis and inflammatory responses. Therefore, it is of great clinical value to explore OS-related indices (T-AOC, GSH-PX, and AOPP) in NPC patients during radiotherapy to assess the impact of OS imbalance on OM progression. In this study, serum T-AOC levels continued to increase during radiotherapy at T1 and T2, and decreased at T3. In addition, serum AOPP levels were consistently elevated over the course of treatment in patients who progressed to SOM, and elevations were observed only after week 4 and after week 6 of treatment in patients with Non-SOM.

The pathological mechanism of RIOM involves direct cellular damage, inflammatory response, vascular injury, and decreased immune function [22]. RIOM is a challenge for clinical care, because it leads to interruptions in cancer therapy, poor local tumor control, and changes in dose partitioning [23]. Therefore, it is necessary to investigate OS in the body of patients during radiotherapy. It has been found that GSH-Px levels decrease and MDA increases after radiotherapy [24]. In addition, elevated AOPP levels are a marker reflecting OS-mediated disease exacerbation, such as active ulcerative colitis [25], non-alcoholic steatohepatitis [26] and clinical atherosclerosis [16]. In this study, we observed an increase in the antioxidant capacity of patients in the SOM group after 2 and 4 weeks of treatment (increased levels of T-AOC and GSH-PX); however, the antioxidant capacity of patients in the SOM group was decreased after 6 weeks of treatment. Serum AOPP levels of the patients in the SOM group were higher than those of the Non-SOM group in all of the course of treatment. It is known that the activity of antioxidants may increase in response to sudden large amounts of reactive oxygen species during the initial period of OS conditions, because the body needs time to cope with the sudden increase in OS [27]. We were also concerned about the individual change trends on antioxidants (T-AOC, GSH-PX) in Non-SOM or SOM patients had similar change trends, i.e., T-AOC levels were consistently elevated at T2 of treatment and decreased at T3, and GSH-PX was significantly increased at T2 and decreased at T3. Differently, in SOM patients, T-AOC and GSH-PX decreased significantly more at T3 than in Non-SOM patients, and AOPP levels were in a state of continuous increase in SOM patients. It is speculated that the significant enhancement of the antioxidant defense system in SOM patients during the first course of pre-treatment was due to the need to scavenge the large amount of ROS generated by the treatment, and that the inability of the antioxidant defense system to load excessive ROS during the late phase of the treatment resulted in a weakening of the antioxidant defense system, and the OS homeostasis was significantly disrupted, which facilitated the progression of OM. This study also showed that the majority of grade III patients progressed in the late stage of this treatment. Although we confirmed the role of antioxidants (T-AOC, GSH-PX) and stress injury trends in the course of NPC radiotherapy on OM progression. However, we could not confirm the critical value of antioxidants in patients when the antioxidant–oxidant system was broken.

In addition, we observed that the percentages of preoperative hypoproteinemia, the absence oral mucosal protective agents, antimicrobial agents during radiotherapy, and the receipt of synchronized chemotherapy were higher in patients with SOM than in patients with Non-SOM. Low albumin levels leads to dryness, desquamation, and impairment of the mucosal barrier, which in turn increases the mucosal risk of radiation damage. In addition, hypoalbuminemia decreases the body’s antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capacity, making mucosal tissues more susceptible to radiation-induced oxidative damage and inflammatory responses, thereby increasing the risk of developing or progressing OM [28]. It has been shown that the therapeutic strategies of applying mucosal protectors and healing aids, and drug therapy can help guide the prevention or treatment of OM [29]. In recent years, the clinical efficacy of head and neck tumors has been significantly improved with simultaneous radiotherapy, but the toxicity of radiotherapy has also been further aggravated. The frequency, severity and duration of grade III–IV OM have been reported to be higher in patients with head and neck tumors receiving concurrent chemotherapy than in patients receiving radiotherapy alone [30]. In addition, some of the patients in this study received synchronized chemotherapy, in which platinum drugs are able to cause structural changes and breaks in DNA by forming chemical bonds with DNA, which prevents the proliferation of cancer cells and also causes apoptosis of normal cells, thus predisposing to the induction or exacerbation of OM [31]. Through stepwise logistic regression analysis, it was finally determined that preoperative hypoproteinemia, the absence of oral mucosal protective agents, and the receipt of synchronized chemotherapy were independent risk factors that increased the course of OM in NPC patients. Therefore, in addition to focusing on risk factors affecting the progression of OM during radiotherapy, attention needs to be paid to OS modulation and protection of the antioxidant system in order to mitigate mucosal damage and promote mucosal repair.

The present study also has limitations. First, OS is a state of imbalance between oxidative and antioxidant effects in vivo, and this study emphasizes the importance of OS in OM, but we focused more on the changes in antioxidant levels in vivo. Second, given the limited sample size, we used stepwise logistic regression analysis, which, although simplifies the model construction process and reduces the risk of overfitting, may also ignore some useful but insignificant variables. Finally, it is necessary to extend the follow-up period to explore the effect of SOM on the prognosis of NPC patients.

Conclusion

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients undergoing IMRT exhibit dynamic alterations in blood T-AOC, GSH-PX, and AOPP. Notably, the persistent elevation of these biomarkers, particularly AOPP, is significantly associated with the progression of SOM in these patients. These findings underscore the involvement of OS imbalance in the pathogenesis of oral mucositis following IMRT. Clinical attention should be paid to the OS-related indicators during IMRT for NPC, and be alert to the exacerbation of OM caused by severe weakening of the antioxidant system. This study helps to implement individualized interventions in the clinic.

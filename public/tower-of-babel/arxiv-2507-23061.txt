Three-loop banana integrals with four unequal masses Preprint: BONN-TH-2025-24
Claude Duhr, Sara Maggio, Franziska Porkert, Cathrin Semper, Sven F. Stawinski

Abstract
We present a system of canonical differential equations satisfied by the three-loop banana integrals with four distinct non-zero masses in D = 2 − 2 ​ ε D=2-2\varepsilon dimensions. Together with the initial condition in the small-mass limit, this provides all the ingredients to find analytic results for three-loop banana integrals in terms of iterated integrals to any desired order in the dimensional regulator. To obtain this result, we rely on recent advances in understanding the K3 geometry underlying these integrals and in how to construct rotations to an ε \varepsilon -factorized basis. This rotation typically involves the introduction of objects defined as integrals of (derivatives of) K3 periods and rational functions. We apply and extend a method based on results from twisted cohomology to identify relations among these functions, which allows us to reduce their number considerably. We expect that the methods that we have applied here will prove useful to compute further multiloop multiscale Feynman integrals attached to non-trivial geometries.

Introduction

Feynman integrals are among the main quantities of interest in Quantum Field Theory (QFT). Not only are they the backbone of many, if not all, precise predictions for collider and gravitational wave experiments, but they are also an invaluable tool to study the mathematical structures underlying QFT. For this reason, the computation of multiloop Feynman integrals has received a lot of attention over the last decades.

Despite their importance, the explicit computation of Feynman integrals is still a major bottleneck. One of the reasons is that Feynman integrals often diverge and need to be regulated. The most commonly used regularization scheme is dimensional regularization
tHooft:1972tcz

D=d-2\varepsilon
, and then expanded around some integer dimension

\varepsilon=\frac{d-D}{2}

It has long been recognized that the Laurent coefficients are periods in the sense of Kontsevich and Zagier
MR1852188
;
Bogner:2007mn

Remiddi:1999ew
;

\varepsilon
-factorized

\varepsilon

Henn:2013pwa

It is well-known that multiple polylogarithms are insufficient to express all Feynman integrals starting from two-loop order, and functions associated to more complicated geometries also show up. The main result of this paper is the construction of the canonical differential equations for the family of three-loop banana integrals with four distinct non-zero masses in
D=2-2\varepsilon
dimensions. In order to achieve this, we combine tools from various sources. Our main tool is the method of refs.
Gorges:2023zgv
;

;

\varepsilon

\varepsilon
-factorized system whose differential equation matrix involves 23 functions that are defined as (iterated) integrals over rational functions and (derivatives of) periods of the K3 surface. In order to simplify the differential equation matrix, we rely on results from twisted cohomology as a third tool. Together with the initial conditions (which we discuss in an appendix), this provides an analytic representation for all master integrals in terms of iterated integrals. It is well known that the members of a family of Feynman integrals are not all independent, but there are linear relations among them, called
integration-by-parts
(IBP) relations
Tkachov:1981wb
;
Chetyrkin:1981qh

\int\mathrm{d}^{D}k_{i}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{i}^{\mu}}\left(\frac{v^{\mu}}{D_{1}^{\nu_{1}}\dots D_{9}^{\nu_{9}}}\right)=0\,,

(3)

where
v^{\mu}
is an internal or external momentum. Since differentiation in the integrand produces propagators with shifted exponents, this identity leads to linear relations among different integrals from the same family, with coefficients that are rational functions of the kinematic variables and the dimensional regulator
\varepsilon
. The IBP relations can be solved to express all integrals in the family in terms of a set of basis integrals, commonly referred to as
master integrals
in the literature. The number of master integrals is always finite
Smirnov:2010hn
;
Bitoun:2017nre

After solving the IBP identities using
LiteRed

2.2
The system of differential equations satisfied by the three-loop banana integrals

To compute all members of a family of Feynman integrals, it is sufficient to compute its set of master integrals. One of the most commonly used ways to compute the master integrals is the method of differential equations

;
Kotikov:1991hm
;
Kotikov:1991pm
;

;

x_{i}=\dfrac{m_{i}^{2}}{p^{2}}\,.

(6)

The entries of the matrix

\varepsilon

\boldsymbol{x}

where the

and

The geometry of three-loop banana integrals

The main goal of this paper is to present a differential equation in canonical form for the three-loop banana integrals with four distinct non-zero masses. Together with the initial conditions in the small-mass limit (see appendix

It is by now well known that three-loop banana integrals are associated to a family of K3 surfaces

In the following we give a brief review of the geometry of K3 surfaces, focusing on the aspects needed to understand the construction of the canonical basis in section

A K3 surface is a two-dimensional complex manifold
X
with vanishing first Chern class. In the following we keep the discussion general, but we restrict to the case where

depends on

complex structure moduli

(which is the case we are interested in). The Hodge diamond for a K3 manifold is

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}&&&1&&&\\
&&0&&0&&\\
1&&&20&&&1\\
&&0&&0&&\\
&&&1&&&\end{matrix}\,,

(20)

and its middle cohomology carries a pure Hodge structure of weight two,

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})=H^{2,0}(X)\oplus H^{1,1}(X)\oplus H^{0,2}(X)\,,

(21)

where

contains cohomology classes generated by
(p,q)

q
antiholomorphic differentials.
We then see that

comes equipped with a distinguished holomorphic
(2,0)
-form
\Omega

We can also define the Hodge filtration on the middle cohomology,

F^{2}=H^{2,0}(X)\,,\qquad F^{1}=H^{2,0}(X)\oplus H^{1,1}(X)\,,\qquad F^{0}=H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\,.

(22)

Unlike the Hodge decomposition in eq. (

), the Hodge filtration varies holomorphically with the complex structure moduli
\boldsymbol{x}
. In particular, Griffiths transversality states that

\partial_{x_{i}}F^{p}\subseteq F^{p-1}\,.

(23)

We can pair the holomorphic
(2,0)
-form
\Omega
with cycles from the middle homology group

to define the

of

is 22-dimensional. One can show that

contains a

(\Gamma_{0},\ldots,\Gamma_{5})
for the remaining six-dimensional space of transcendental cycles, we can define the vector of periods,

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{x})&\,=\big(\psi_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),\psi_{1}^{(1)}(\boldsymbol{x}),\psi_{1}^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{x}),\psi_{1}^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{x}),\psi_{1}^{(4)}(\boldsymbol{x}),\psi_{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\big)^{T}\\
&\,=\Big(\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\Omega,\ldots,\int_{\Gamma_{5}}\Omega\Big)^{T}\,.\end{split}

(24)

It is typically very hard to construct an explicit basis of transcendental cycles and to integrate
\Omega
over them. Instead, the periods can be computed as solutions to a system of differential equations, which generate an ideal of differential operators called the
Picard-Fuchs ideal
of

The periods generally define multivalued functions of
\boldsymbol{x}

\begin{split}\psi_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})&\,=1+\mathcal{O}(x_{j})\,,\\

and

is double-logarithmically divergent at the MUM-point (its precise form will be given below).
In ref.

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{|k|=n}\binom{n}{k_{1},\ldots,k_{4}}^{2}\,\prod_{i=1}^{4}x_{i}^{k_{i}}\,.

It is useful to introduce the following

(28)

The middle cohomology group of a K3 surface is equipped with a non-degenerate bilinear pairing, called the

\boldsymbol{\psi}^{T}(\boldsymbol{x})\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\,\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{x})=0\,\,,

We can use the quadratic relation in eq. (

We now interpret our basis of master integrals as a basis for the twisted cohomology group attached to the three-loop banana integrals. Since we focus on maximal cuts, we are in a self-dual situation.
We start from the observation of ref.

7
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper we have obtained for the first time a system of canonical differential equations satisfied by the master integrals of the three-loop banana integral family with four distinct non-zero masses in
D=2-2\varepsilon
dimensions. Combined with the knowledge of the small-mass limit of the banana integrals (see ref.
Bonisch:2021yfw
and appendix
C
), this allows us to obtain analytic results for all master integrals in terms of iterated integrals over a set of integration kernels that involve rational functions, (derivatives of) periods of the underlying family of K3 surface, as well as integrals involving these functions. Our final result for the differential equation is lengthy, and it is provided in computer readable form
bonndata
.

Our result was made possible by combining tools from three different advances made over the last few years. In particular, we apply the method of refs.
Gorges:2023zgv
;
Duhr:2025lbz
;
Maggio:2025jel
to construct a sequence of transformations that brings the system into an
\varepsilon
-factorized form. We observe that the resulting
\varepsilon
-factorized system has additional properties, which confirm those expected from systems in canonical form in ref.
Duhr:2025lbz
. An important ingredient in order to construct the transformation matrix using the method of refs.
Gorges:2023zgv
;
Duhr:2025lbz
;
Maggio:2025jel
is an understanding of the geometry of the family of K3 surfaces associated to the three-loop banana integrals, in particular their periods and the quadratic relations they satisfy. Finally, the method of refs.
Gorges:2023zgv
;
Duhr:2025lbz
;
Maggio:2025jel
introduces 23 functions – which we called
\varepsilon
-functions – defined as iterated integrals over rational functions, K3 periods, and their derivatives. We used tools from twisted cohomology to derive relations between these functions. This allowed us to reduce the number of
\varepsilon
-functions from 23 to 13. Once symmetries are taken into account this number is further reduced to two.

We expect that the techniques we have applied here to derive relations between
\varepsilon
-functions, in particular the techniques from twisted cohomology, will also be useful to simplify the differential equations for other classes of Feynman integrals. We leave the presentation of additional examples, as well as a further mathematical derivations, for future work
paper1
.

On-shell representation and further instances of the 2-split behavior of amplitudes
Thales Azevedo, Humberto Gomez, and Renann Lipinski Jusinskas

Abstract
The newly discovered splitting behavior of tree-level scattering amplitudes of particles and strings has been expressed in terms of currents containing one off-shell leg. In this work, we explain how to obtain on-shell representations of the split amplitudes in different theories, thereby avoiding any ambiguity involved in the definition of the currents. Furthermore, we show that this 2-split behavior is also verified in gauge and gravity theories involving higher-dimensional operators, thereby providing additional evidence to its universal character. As a byproduct, we also generalize the transmuting operators to amplitudes in higher-derivative theories.



1
Introduction

Since the turn of the century, the study of scattering amplitudes has transcended the traditional Feynman-diagrammatic intuition, in particular with the identification of novel/hidden structures and several remarkable properties. Among the more recent ones, two interrelated concepts stand out: hidden zeros, smooth splitting (3-split) and the so called 2-split processes
2
;
6
;
1
;
10
;
9
;
14
;
21
;
13
. The former concerns the discovery that tree-level amplitudes vanish on special sub-loci of kinematic space, even though no obvious factorization channel is present. These hidden zeros impose non-trivial constraints on the analytic structure of the amplitude and hint at the existence of deeper, universal factorization properties. For example, in the colored scalars of
\text{Tr}(\phi^{3})
theory one finds that when certain non planar invariants are taken to zero, the amplitude vanishes. Perhaps more significantly, near those loci the amplitude factorizes in a manner compatible with the 2 and 3-split descriptions
1
— see also
10
;
5
;
21
. In essence, when a certain kinematic data set is constrained to vanish, a scattering amplitude may factorize in a striking way into the product of two lower-point amputated currents. This phenomenon has been identified across a broad class of field theories (such as the bi-adjoint
\phi^{3}
model, the non‐linear sigma model, Yang-Mills theory, gravity) and even in string theory
6
;
10
;
9
.

Following the identification of hidden zeros, the 2-split phenomenon was systematically studied in the work by Cao, Dong, He and collaborators. In
10
;
9
, it was shown that when one imposes the vanishing of a subset of kinematic invariants, the scattering equations of the CHY framework or the Koba–Nielsen factors in the string integrand cleanly split into two independent sectors, hence the term “2-split” behaviour. Schematically,

{\cal A}_{n}(1,2,\dots,n)\longrightarrow{\cal J}^{L}(i,A,j,\kappa)\times{\cal J}^{R}(1,\ldots,i,j,\ldots,{\kappa^{\prime}}),

where the left and right amputated currents are lower-point building blocks constructed in the same theory or in a related one via transmuting operators
12
.

Crucially, Feng, Zhang, and Zhou
14
have further shown that the 2-split behavior is deeply tied to hidden zeros. By employing a BCFW recursion (or its modifications for non-standard theories) one can derive the factorization and the vanishing loci together.

The 2-split factorization can be seen as a property of the integrand in the CHY representation, where the splitting of the scattering equations corresponds to separate integration domains that yield the product of two currents. The universality of the 2-split phenomenon extends across gauge theory and gravity, suggesting that the structure is a deep aspect of the S-matrix rather than a peculiarity of any individual model.
Additionally, the vanishing loci provide a geometric handle in the kinematic space. They delineate subspaces where the amplitude simplifies radically, offering a fresh path to recursion or factorization outside traditional collinear or multi-particle poles.

This interplay between hidden zeros, 2-split factorization, and transmuting operators opens new avenues for amplitude computation. Towards this goal, it would be interesting to recast the 2-split in terms of physical quantities, amplitudes themselves instead of gauge-dependent amputated currents. Indeed, as discussed recently in
14
, the currents appearing in the 2-splits of Yang‒Mills and gravity amplitudes are sensitive to gauge choices. Moreover, CHY and (amputated) Berends–Giele currents do not agree in general. It would be better to express the splittings in terms of gauge-invariant objects.

In this paper, we contribute to the understanding of the 2-split behavior in two manners. First, we show that the splitting is also verified in theories with higher-derivative kinetic terms, such as
R^{2}
gravity or the
(DF)^{2}
theory of
16
. Second, we explain how to obtain on-shell representations of the split amplitudes, thereby avoiding any ambiguity involved in the definition of the currents. As a byproduct, we also generalize the transmuting operators introduced in
12
to amplitudes in higher-derivative theories.

2
Review of the universal splitting behavior

In this section, we briefly review the splitting behavior of the main CHY ingredients, namely the measure, the Parke-Taylor factor and the (reduced) Pfaffian.

In the CHY representation, an amplitude with
n
external massless states is written as an integration over a
n
-punctured Riemann sphere,

\mathcal{A}_{n}=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}\,I_{n}\tilde{I}_{n}.

(1)

The CHY measure
7
;
8
, is given by

{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}=\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{n}z}{\textrm{vol SL}(2,\mathbb{C})}z_{i,j}z_{j,k}z_{k,i}{\prod_{c\neq i,j,k}}\delta(S_{c}),

(2)

where
z_{c}
denotes the coordinates of the punctures,
z_{c,d}=z_{c}-z_{d}
, and
S_{c}
is defined as

S_{c}=\sum_{d\neq c}\frac{s_{c,d}}{z_{c,d}},

(3)

with
s_{c,d}=2\,p_{c}\cdot p_{d}
. The measure is independent of the choice of reference labels
{i,j,k}
. In addition, we can use the automorphism group of the sphere,
\textrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, to fix the coordinates of three punctures. For convenience we will fix the coordinates of the reference labels
\mathcal{I}=\{i,j,k\}
, with
i<j
and
k=n
, such that the gauge fixed measure is recast as

{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}=\Delta(i,j,k)^{2}{\prod_{c\neq i,j,k}}dz_{c}\,\delta(S_{c}),

(4)

with
\Delta(i,j,k)=z_{i,j}z_{j,k}z_{k,i}
. The half-integrands
I_{n},\tilde{I}_{n}
are theory dependent and usually involve other relevant kinematic data.

2.1
The splitting of the measure

The splitting behavior analyzed in
10
;
9
is observed when separating the
n-3
punctures to be integrated into two disjoint sets
A
and
B
such that
A\cup B=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\mathcal{I}
, and then imposing the conditions

s_{a,b}=0\qquad\forall\,a\in A,b\in B\,.

(5)

Under the splitting conditions (
5
), the scattering equations
S_{c}=0
take a suggestive form. Let us consider
S_{a}
with
a\in A
, and
S_{b}
with
b\in B
. They are respectively given by

\displaystyle S_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{a,i}}{z_{a,i}}+\frac{s_{a,j}}{z_{a,j}}+\frac{s_{a,k}}{z_{a,k}}+\sum_{\tilde{a}\in A\setminus\{a\}}\frac{s_{a,\tilde{a}}}{z_{a,\tilde{a}}},

(6a)

\displaystyle S_{b}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{b,i}}{z_{b,i}}+\frac{s_{b,j}}{z_{b,j}}+\frac{s_{b,k}}{z_{b,k}}+\sum_{\tilde{b}\in B\setminus\{b\}}\frac{s_{b,\tilde{b}}}{z_{b,\tilde{b}}}.

(6b)

Momentum conservation dictates that

p_{k}=-p_{i}-p_{j}-\sum_{a\in A}p_{a}-\sum_{b\in B}p_{b},

(7)

so we define

\displaystyle p_{\kappa}

\displaystyle\equiv p_{k}+\sum_{b\in B}p_{b},

\displaystyle=-p_{i}-p_{j}-\sum_{a\in A}p_{a},

(8a)

\displaystyle p_{\kappa}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\equiv p_{k}+\sum_{a\in a}p_{a},

\displaystyle=-p_{i}-p_{j}-\sum_{b\in B}p_{b},

(8b)

such that
p_{\kappa}
and
p_{\kappa}^{\prime}
are off the mass-shell. The split scattering equations (
6
) can then be rewritten as

\displaystyle S_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{a,i}}{z_{a,i}}+\frac{s_{a,j}}{z_{a,j}}+\frac{s_{a,\kappa}}{z_{a,\kappa}}+\sum_{\tilde{a}\in A\setminus\{a\}}\frac{s_{a,\tilde{a}}}{z_{a,\tilde{a}}},

(9a)

\displaystyle S_{b}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{b,i}}{z_{b,i}}+\frac{s_{b,j}}{z_{b,j}}+\frac{s_{b,\kappa^{\prime}}}{z_{b,\kappa^{\prime}}}+\sum_{\tilde{b}\in B\setminus\{b\}}\frac{s_{b,\tilde{b}}}{z_{b,\tilde{b}}},

(9b)

with
z_{\kappa}=z_{\kappa^{\prime}}=z_{k}
. Regarding the measure (
4
), it is then recast as

{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}=\Delta(i,j,\kappa)^{-1}\Delta(i,j,\kappa^{\prime})^{-1}\times{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\mathrm{L}}(i,j,A;\kappa)\times{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\mathrm{R}}(i,j,B;\kappa^{\prime}),

(10)

with

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\mathrm{L}}(i,j,A;\kappa)

\displaystyle=\Delta(i,j,\kappa)^{2}\prod_{a\in A}dz_{a}\,\delta(S_{a}),

(11)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\mathrm{R}}(i,j,B;\kappa^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\Delta(i,j,\kappa^{\prime})^{2}\prod_{b\in B}dz_{b}\,\delta(S_{b}).

(12)

Here we implicitly use
S_{a}
and
S_{b}
in equation (
9
), and
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
denote off-shell states. Aside from a pre-factor, the CHY measure (
10
) factorizes into two sectors,
\mathrm{L}
and
\mathrm{R}
.

2.2
The splitting of the integrands

Next, we review the splitting behavior of some integrands.
The Parke-Taylor factor in the canonical ordering is defined as

\mathrm{PT}({1,\ldots,n})=\frac{1}{z_{1,2}\ldots z_{n-1,n}z_{n,1}}.

(13)

Since we are working with
k=n
, it can be conveniently rewritten as

\mathrm{PT}({1,\ldots,n})=\frac{1}{(z_{i,i+1}\ldots z_{j-1,j})}\times\frac{1}{(z_{1,2}\ldots z_{i-1,i})(z_{j,j+1}\ldots z_{n-1,n}z_{n,1})}

(14)

Now, if we multiply the first factor on the right-hand side by
1=(z_{j,\kappa}z_{\kappa,i})/(z_{j,\kappa}z_{\kappa,i})
and the second factor by
1=z_{i,j}/z_{i,j}
, we simply get

\mathrm{PT}({1,\ldots,n})=\Delta(i,j,\kappa)\underbrace{\mathrm{PT}(i,\ldots,j,\kappa)}_{L}\underbrace{\mathrm{PT}(1,\ldots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})}_{R},

(15)

in which the split behavior is evident.

The CHY representation of Yang–Mills and gravity amplitudes requires the introduction of the
2n\times 2n
matrix

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}_{n}=

\displaystyle{}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{A}_{n}&-\mathbf{C}_{n}^{\mathrm{T}}\\
\mathbf{C}_{n}&\mathbf{B}_{n}\end{array}\right),

where the different
n\times n
sub-matrices are defined as
7
;
8

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{n}^{cd}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{p_{c}\cdot p_{d}}{z_{c,d}},&\textrm{for }c\neq d,\\
0,&\textrm{for }c=d,\end{cases}

(18a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{n}^{cd}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\epsilon_{c}\cdot\epsilon_{d}}{z_{c,d}},&\textrm{for }c\neq d,\\
0,&\textrm{for }c=d,\end{cases}

(18b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{n}^{cd}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\epsilon_{c}\cdot p_{d}}{z_{c,d}},&\textrm{for }c\neq d,\\
-\sum\limits_{e\neq d}\frac{\epsilon_{d}\cdot p_{e}}{z_{d,e}},&\textrm{for }c=d,\end{cases}

(18c)

with
\epsilon_{c}
denoting the
n
polarization vectors. The half-integrand entering the CHY formula is the so-called reduced Pfaffian of
\bm{\Psi}_{n}
,

\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{n}\equiv\frac{(-1)^{p+q}}{z_{p,q}}\mathrm{Pf}\bm{\Psi}_{n}^{[p,q]},

(19)

where the matrix
\bm{\Psi}_{n}^{[p,q]}
is obtained from
\bm{\Psi}_{n}
by removing the
p
-th and
q
-th rows and columns. The value of
\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{n}
does not depend on the choice of
p
and
q
.

In order to observe the splitting behavior of (
19
), we need to introduce additional conditions involving the polarization vectors
10
;
9
. The first set of conditions is given by

\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{b}=p_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{b}=\epsilon_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{b}=0\qquad\forall a\in A,b\in B.

(20)

They are aligned with the overall intuition of the splitting of the measure, cf. equation (
5
), and also compatible with the residual gauge transformation of the polarizations,
\delta\epsilon_{a}\propto p_{a}
and
\delta\epsilon_{b}\propto p_{b}
. The second set of conditions is given by

\epsilon_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{I}=p_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{I}=0\qquad\forall a\in A,I\in\mathcal{I}=\{i,j,k\}.

(21)

These conditions are not compatible with
\delta\epsilon_{I}\propto p_{I}
, although this is a gauge invariance of the reduced Pfaffian (
19
). We will come back to this point later.

Under the conditions (
20
) and (
21
), the reduced Pfaffian splits as

\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{n}\to\underbrace{\mathrm{Pf}\bm{\Psi}_{\{A\}}}_{L}\times\underbrace{\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{\{i,j,B,\kappa^{\prime}\}}}_{R},

(22)

where
\bm{\Psi}_{\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}\}}
denotes the
2m\times 2m
sub-matrix of
\bm{\Psi}_{n}
with only rows and columns in
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}
and
\{i_{1+n},,\ldots,i_{m+n}\}
remaining. Note that only the second term on the right hand side is a reduced Pfaffian.

2.3
The splitting of the amplitudes

As a concrete example of the resulting splitting behavior in (
1
), we will analyze the partial amplitude of Yang–Mills with
n
external gluons. In the canonical ordering, it is given by

{\cal{A}}_{n}^{\mathrm{YM}}(1,\ldots,n)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}\,\mathrm{PT}(1,\ldots,n)\,\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{n}\,.

(23)

The splitting conditions (
5
), (
20
), and (
21
) lead to the splitting of the measure, cf. equation (
10
), the Parke–Taylor factor, cf. equation (
15
), and the reduced Pfaffian, cf. equation (
22
), such that the amplitude (
23
) is recast as

{\cal{A}}_{n}^{\mathrm{YM}}(1,\ldots,n)\to{\cal{J}}^{\mathrm{YM}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})\times{\cal{J}}_{\mu}^{\mathrm{YM}}(1,\dots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\epsilon_{n}^{\mu},

(24)

with

{\cal J}^{\mathrm{YM}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})=\int\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathrm{L}}(i,j,A;\kappa)\mathrm{PT}(i,j,\kappa)\mathrm{PT}(i,\ldots,j,\kappa)\mathrm{Pf}\bm{\Psi}_{\{A\}},

(25)

and

{\cal J}_{\mu}^{\mathrm{YM}}(1,\dots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\epsilon_{n}^{\mu}=\int\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathrm{R}}(i,j,B;\kappa^{\prime})\mathrm{PT}(1,\dots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{\{j,B,\kappa^{\prime},i\}}.

(26)

{\cal{J}}^{\mathrm{YM}+\phi^{3}}
in (
25
) denotes the scalar current with
|A|
gluons and two scalars (
i,j
), with off-shell leg
\kappa
, while
{\cal{J}}_{\mu}^{\mathrm{YM}}
denotes a pure gluon current with off-shell leg
\kappa^{\prime}
. Notice that in the latter, the residual gauge invariance of the legs
i
,
j
, and
k=n
is lost. This was already expected because of the splitting conditions in (
21
). However, when we put the off-shell object (
26
) on the mass-shell, i.e. by imposing
p_{\kappa}^{\prime}=p_{n}
, it is again identified with a tree level amplitude of the Yang-Mills theory, with residual gauge invariance restored.

3
Splitting in higher-derivative theories

In reference
4
, it was shown that the tree level scattering amplitudes for the plane-wave states of the
(DF)^{2}
theory, i.e. gluon-like excitations, admit a CHY representation. In the canonical ordering, it is given by

{\cal{A}}_{n}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,\ldots,n)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}\,\mathrm{PT}(1,\ldots,n)\,W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}},

(27)

where
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
denotes a product of the Lam-Yao cycles
17
, usually denoted by
W_{11\cdots 1}
in the literature. These cycles are common building blocks of CHY amplitudes for theories with higher-dimensional operators. In our case,
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
can be written in terms of the diagonal elements of the
\mathbf{C}
matrix (
18c
),

W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}=\prod_{c=1}^{n}\left(-\sum\limits_{d\neq c}\frac{\epsilon_{c}\cdot p_{d}}{z_{c,d}}\right).

(28)

More generally, we will work with the following operator,

W_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}}\equiv\prod_{p=1}^{m}\left(-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\\
(q\neq p)\end{subarray}}^{m}\frac{\epsilon_{i_{p}}\cdot p_{i_{q}}}{z_{i_{p},i_{q}}}\right).

(29)

From this ingredient, it is clear that the scattering amplitudes (
27
) do not involve contracted polarizations,
\epsilon_{c}\cdot\epsilon_{d}
. This is a notable property of the
(DF)^{2}
theory
16
.

3.1
Splitting in the
(DF)^{2}
theory

Regarding the splitting discussed in section
2
, after imposing the conditions (
20
) and (
21
), it is trivial to show that

W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}\to\underbrace{\prod_{a\in A}\left(-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{a}\in A\cup\{i,j,\kappa\}\\
(\tilde{a}\neq a)\end{subarray}}\frac{\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{\tilde{a}}}{z_{a,\tilde{a}}}\right)}_{L}\underbrace{W_{B\cup\{i,j,\kappa^{\prime}\}}}_{R},

(30)

which is analogous to equation (
22
).
The splitting (
30
), together with equations (
10
) and (
15
), implies that the
(DF)^{2}
amplitude (
27
) with
n
external gluons splits as

{\cal{A}}_{n}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,\ldots,n)\to{\cal{J}}^{(DF)^{2}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})\times{\cal{J}}_{\mu}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,\dots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\epsilon_{n}^{\mu},

(31)

where the currents
{\cal{J}}^{(DF)^{2}+\phi^{3}}
and
{\cal{J}}_{\mu}^{(DF)^{2}}
are the
DF^{2}
analogous of the Yang-Mills currents (
25
) and (
26
), respectively.

The scattering amplitudes in these
(DF)^{2}
theories can be derived from a Lagrangian introduced in
16
, given by

\mathcal{L}_{(DF)^{2}+\phi^{3}}=\frac{1}{2}(D_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu\,I})^{2}+\frac{g}{3}\,F^{3}+\frac{1}{2}(D_{\mu}\varphi^{\alpha})^{2}+\frac{g}{2}\,C^{\alpha IJ}\varphi^{\alpha}F_{\mu\nu}^{I}F^{\mu\nu\,J}+\frac{g}{3!}\,d^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\varphi^{\alpha}\varphi^{\beta}\varphi^{\gamma}\\
+\frac{1}{2}(D_{\mu}\phi^{I\widehat{I}})^{2}+\frac{g\lambda}{3!}f^{IJK}\hat{f}^{\widehat{I}\widehat{J}\widehat{K}}\phi^{I\widehat{I}}\phi^{J\widehat{J}}\phi^{K\widehat{K}}+\frac{g}{2}\varphi^{\alpha}\phi^{I\widehat{K}}\phi^{J\widehat{K}}C^{\alpha IJ}.

(32)

Observe that the massless scalars
\phi^{I\widehat{I}}
are charged under two groups: while the gauge group (adjoint) indices
I,J,K,\ldots
are shared with the gluons, the second group (global) with indices
\widehat{I},\widehat{J},\widehat{K},\ldots
is exclusive to
\phi^{I\widehat{I}}
.
The field strength and covariant derivatives are defined as

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}^{I}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}^{I}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}^{I}+gf^{IJK}A_{\mu}^{J}A_{\nu}^{K},

(33a)

\displaystyle F^{3}

\displaystyle=f^{IJK}F_{\mu}^{I\,\nu}F_{\nu}^{J\,\lambda}F_{\lambda}^{K\,\mu},

(33b)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\varphi^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\varphi^{\alpha}-ig(T_{R}^{I})^{\alpha\beta}A_{\mu}^{I}\varphi^{\beta},

(33c)

\displaystyle D_{\rho}F_{\mu\nu}^{I}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\rho}F_{\mu\nu}^{I}+gf^{IJK}A_{\rho}^{J}F_{\mu\nu}^{K},

(33d)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\phi^{I\widehat{I}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I\widehat{I}}+gf^{IJK}A_{\mu}^{J}\phi^{K\widehat{I}}.

(33e)

The scalar
\varphi^{\alpha}
transforms in a real representation
R
with indices
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\ldots
, and
g
is the coupling constant.
The Clebsh–Gordan coefficients
C^{\alpha IJ}
and
d^{\alpha\beta\gamma}
are implicitly defined through the two relations

\displaystyle C^{\alpha IJ}C^{\alpha KL}

\displaystyle=f^{IKM}f^{MLJ}+f^{ILM}f^{MKJ},

(34)

\displaystyle C^{\alpha IJ}d^{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=(T_{R}^{I})^{\beta\alpha}(T_{R}^{J})^{\alpha\gamma}+C^{\beta IK}C^{\gamma KJ}+(I\leftrightarrow J).

(35)

Interestingly, these relations imply that pure gluon amplitudes can be color-ordered.

A simple instance of the splitting (
90
) can already be seen at
n=4
. Indeed, using equation (
27
), we obtain

{\cal{A}}_{4}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,2,3,4)=-\frac{s_{1,2}^{2}s_{2,3}^{2}}{s_{1,3}}\\
\times\left(\frac{p_{2}\cdot\epsilon_{1}}{s_{1,2}}-\frac{p_{4}\cdot\epsilon_{1}}{s_{2,3}}\right)\left(\frac{p_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{2}}{s_{1,2}}-\frac{p_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{2}}{s_{2,3}}\right)\left(\frac{p_{4}\cdot\epsilon_{3}}{s_{1,2}}-\frac{p_{2}\cdot\epsilon_{3}}{s_{2,3}}\right)\left(\frac{p_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{4}}{s_{1,2}}-\frac{p_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{4}}{s_{2,3}}\right).

(36)

Now, choosing the reference particles to be
i=1
,
j=3
and
k=4
, we are left with the sets
A=\{2\}
and
B=\emptyset
. In this case, the splitting conditions are simply
p_{2}\cdot\epsilon_{I}=0
, with
I\in\{1,3,4\}
. The amplitude (
36
) then becomes

{\cal{A}}_{4}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,2,3,4)\to\left(\frac{p_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{2}}{s_{2,3}}-\frac{p_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{2}}{s_{1,2}}\right)(p_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{1})(p_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{3})(p_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{4}),

(37)

where we have used momentum conservation and the on-shell conditions
p_{q}^{2}=p_{q}\cdot\epsilon_{q}=0
. It is easy to check that

{\cal J}^{(DF)^{2}+\phi^{3}}(1^{\phi},2,3^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})=\left(\frac{p_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{2}}{s_{1,2}}-\frac{p_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{2}}{s_{2,3}}\right),

(38)

and

{\cal J}_{\mu}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,3,\kappa^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}(p_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{1})(p_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{3})(p_{1\mu}-p_{3\mu}).

(39)

Therefore, the splitting (
37
) agrees with the general equation (
90
).

In
9
, an analogous splitting was considered for
\mathcal{A}_{4}^{\mathrm{YM}}(1,2,3,4)
, with

\Big(\frac{p_{1}{\cdot}\epsilon_{2}}{s_{1,2}}-\frac{p_{3}{\cdot}\epsilon_{2}}{s_{2,3}}\Big)=\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{YM}+\phi^{3}}(1^{\phi},2,3^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi}).

(40)

Indeed,
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{YM}+\phi^{3}}(1^{\phi},2,3^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})=\mathcal{J}^{(DF)^{2}+\phi^{3}}(1^{\phi},2,3^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})
, since both expressions come from the Berends–Giele currents derived from the terms
\frac{1}{2}(D_{\mu}\phi^{I\widehat{I}})^{2}+(\phi^{I\widehat{I}})^{3}
in the respective Lagrangians. Accordingly, note that
\mathrm{Pf}\bm{\Psi}_{\{2\}}=\mathbf{C}_{\{2\}}^{1,1}
.

3.2
Gravity theories

In
10
;
9
, the splitting of Einstein gravity amplitudes was also investigated. In the CHY representation, they are cast as

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{GR}}=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}\,\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{n}\,\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{n},

(41)

where the graviton polarization is written as
h_{n}^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\mu}_{n}\epsilon^{\nu}_{n}
. The presence of two copies of the Pfaffian now allows for two distinct choices, leading to different splittings. One could split the Pfaffian by imposing either (1) the conditions in equations (
20
) and (
21
), or (2) the conditions in equations (
20
) and

\epsilon_{b}\cdot\epsilon_{I}=p_{b}\cdot\epsilon_{I}=0\qquad\forall b\in B,I\in\mathcal{I}.

(42)

Under the latter, the Pfaffian splits as

\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{n}\to\underbrace{\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{\{i,A,j,\kappa^{\prime}\}}}_{L}\times\underbrace{\mathrm{PT}(i,j,\kappa)\mathrm{Pf}\bm{\Psi}_{\{B\}}}_{R},

(43)

For Yang-Mills amplitudes, the different splitting choices simply reverse the roles of the sets
A
and
B
. For Einstein gravity amplitudes, on the other hand, the two options lead to the following splittings,

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{GR}}\to\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{GR}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})\times\mathcal{J}_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{GR}}(1,\dots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\epsilon_{n}^{\mu}\epsilon_{n}^{\nu}

(44)

or

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{GR}}\to\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{\mathrm{EYM}}(i^{g},A,j^{g},\kappa^{g})\epsilon_{n}^{\mu}\times\mathcal{J}_{\nu}^{\mathrm{EYM}}(1,\dots,i^{g},j^{g},\ldots,n-1,\kappa^{\prime g})\epsilon_{n}^{\nu},

(45)

where EYM stands for Einstein-Yang-Mills and the superscript
g
indicates a gluonic leg.

3.2.1
R^{2}
gravity

Graviton amplitudes for the curvature-squared gravity coming from heterotic ambitwistor strings (which reduce to conformal gravity amplitudes in four dimensions) admit the following CHY representation
4
,

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{R^{2}}=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}\,\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{n}W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}.

(46)

Given the splittings found for the reduced Pfaffian, cf. equation (
22
), and for
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
, cf. equation (
30
), there are two possible splittings for
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{R^{2}}
. By imposing the conditions (
5
), (
20
) and (
21
), we obtain

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{R^{2}}\to\mathcal{J}^{R^{2}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})\times\mathcal{J}_{\mu\nu}^{R^{2}}(1,\dots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\epsilon_{n}^{\mu}\epsilon_{n}^{\nu},

(47)

which is analogous to (
44
). On the other hand, if we split
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
as in (

) while choosing the alternative splitting (
43
) for the Pfaffian, we get

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{R^{2}}\to\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{R^{2}+\mathrm{YM}}(i^{g},A,j^{g},\kappa^{g})\epsilon_{n}^{\mu}\times\mathcal{J}_{\nu}^{R^{2}+(DF)^{2}}(1,\dots,i^{g},j^{g},\ldots,n-1,\kappa^{\prime,g})\epsilon_{n}^{\nu}\,.

(48)

The mixed currents above can be obtained from their respective CHY representations
3
.

3.2.2
R^{3}
gravity

Finally, we can analyze the splitting of the graviton amplitude in the higher derivative gravity theory described by bosonic ambitwistor strings
4
, which we will call
R^{3}
. The corresponding tree level amplitudes can be computed using the perturbiner method in the
\alpha^{\prime}\to 0
limit of the equations of motion recently derived in

. Their CHY representation is given by

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{R^{3}}=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}\,W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}.

(49)

Regarding the possible splittings, this case is completely analogous to the graviton amplitudes discussed so far. By imposing the splitting conditions (
21
) in both
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
, we obtain

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{R^{3}}\to\mathcal{J}^{R^{3}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})\times\mathcal{J}_{\mu\nu}^{R^{3}}(1,\dots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\epsilon_{n}^{\mu}\epsilon_{n}^{\nu}.

(50)

Alternatively, by imposing (
21
) on one
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
and (
42
) on the other leads to

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{R^{3}}\to\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{R^{3}+(DF)^{2}}(i^{g},A,j^{g},\kappa^{g})\epsilon_{n}^{\mu}\times\mathcal{J}_{\nu}^{R^{3}+(DF)^{2}}(1,\dots,i^{g},j^{g},\ldots,n-1,\kappa^{\prime,g})\epsilon_{n}^{\nu},

(51)

where the superscript
g
denotes gluon states from the
(DF)^{2}
theory.

4
On-Shell representations of the split amplitudes

The splitting of a given amplitude is cast in terms of amputated currents, which are usually gauge dependent objects. Indeed, they possess a nontrivial structure due to the off-shell component. Their computation is a bit more subtle within the CHY framework. It would be more interesting to recast the splitting in terms of physical objects, i.e. lower-point amplitudes. In this section, we demonstrate how to do this with an appropriate choice of kinematic data.

4.1
An example

Let us consider the five-point bi-adjoint (BA) amplitude with
(i,j,k)=(1,3,5)
, where
A=\{2\}
and
B=\{4\}
. A direct computation leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{5}^{\phi^{3}}(1,2,3,4,5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{1,2}s_{3,4}}+\frac{1}{s_{1,2}s_{4,5}}+\frac{1}{s_{2,3}s_{4,5}}+\frac{1}{s_{2,3}s_{1,5}}+\frac{1}{s_{1,5}s_{3,4}}

\displaystyle\to\left(\frac{1}{s_{1,2}}+\frac{1}{s_{2,3}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{s_{3,4}}+\frac{1}{s_{4,5}}\right).

(52)

The transition to the second line is simply the imposition of the splitting condition (
5
), that is,
s_{2,4}=0
. As shown in
10
;
9
, the 2-split representation for this configuration is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{5}^{\phi^{3}}(1,2,3,4,5)\to\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(1,2,3,\kappa)\,\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime}),

(53)

with off-shell legs
p_{\kappa}=-p_{1}-p_{2}-p_{3}=p_{4}+p_{5}
and
p_{\kappa^{\prime}}=-p_{1}-p_{3}-p_{4}=p_{2}+p_{5}
.
Notice that the amputated current
\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(1,2,3,\kappa)
is directly related to the bi-adjoint Berends–Giele (BG) current
18
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\rm CHY}^{\phi^{3}}(1,2,3,\kappa)

\displaystyle=s_{1,2,3}\mathcal{J}_{\rm BG}^{\phi^{3}}(1,2,3,\kappa),

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{1,2}}+\frac{1}{s_{2,3}},

(54)

in which the puncture
z_{2}
was integrated. In contrast, the current
\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime})
is evaluated with an integration over
z_{4}
. This difference becomes relevant in the comparison with the respective BG current. Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{{\rm CHY}}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{3,4}}+\frac{1}{s_{4,\kappa^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{3,4}}+\frac{1}{s_{4,5}},

(55)

\displaystyle s_{1,3,4}\mathcal{J}_{{\rm BG}}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{3,4}}+\frac{1}{s_{1,3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{3,4}}+\frac{1}{s_{2,5}+s_{4,5}},

(56)

where we have used the condition
s_{2,4}=0
.
As expected, the product of equations (
54
) and (
55
) correctly reproduces the result in (
52
). However, there is a clear discrepancy between equations (
55
) and (
56
), namely,
\mathcal{J}_{{\rm CHY}}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime})\neq s_{1,3,4}\,{\cal J}_{\rm BG}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime})
.

The mismatch between the CHY and BG currents vanishes when
\kappa^{\prime}
is put on-shell, with
p_{\kappa}^{{}_{{}^{\prime}}2}=s_{2,5}=0
. Related ideas were further discussed by one of the authors in
15
, where it was demonstrated that amputated CHY currents for bi-adjoint and Yang–Mills theories can be derived directly from amplitudes. The same procedure can be straightforwardly extended to the other theories analyzed here (see appendix
A
).

In the following subsection, we adopt the method proposed in
19
;
20
which enables the construction of CHY currents via on-shell methods.

4.2
Kinematic shifting

Let us consider CHY amputated currents with up to three off-shell external legs, with

p_{I}^{2}\neq 0
,
I\in\{i,j,\kappa\}
, and
(n-3)
on-shell legs. The
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
invariant scattering equations
S_{c}=0
are modified as
19
;
20

S_{c}=\sum_{d\neq c}^{n}\frac{s_{c,d}+\Delta_{c,d}}{z_{c,d}},

(57)

where the only non-vanishing entries of
\Delta_{c,d}
are

\displaystyle\Delta_{i,j}=\Delta_{j,i}

\displaystyle=p_{i}^{2}+p_{j}^{2}-p_{\kappa}^{2},

(58a)

\displaystyle\Delta_{i,\kappa}=\Delta_{\kappa,i}

\displaystyle=p_{i}^{2}+p_{\kappa}^{2}-p_{j}^{2},

(58b)

\displaystyle\Delta_{j,\kappa}=\Delta_{\kappa,j}

\displaystyle=p_{j}^{2}+p_{\kappa}^{2}-p_{i}^{2}.

(58c)

Using the
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
symmetry, we can eliminate the three scattering equations associated with the off-shell legs. As a result, the gauge fixed measure has the same structure as in the case where all external particles are massless.

For the BA theory, the half-integrands in the CHY representation do not depend on momentum variables. Therefore, we could simply compute amplitudes using the usual scattering equations and shift the kinematic data. In the analysis of the amplitude splitting, the resulting currents have only one off-shell leg,
\kappa
or
\kappa^{\prime}
. So we are going to define the shifting operation

\Sigma(\kappa)\equiv\{s_{c,d}\mapsto s_{c,d}+\Delta_{c,d}\},

(59)

with
\Delta_{c,d}
given in equation (
58
), now with
p_{i}^{2}=p_{j}^{2}=0
.

For example, let us consider the 2-split case presented in equation (
52
). The identification of the CHY current with the BG current in equation (
54
) remains,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{4}^{\phi^{3}}(1,2,3,\kappa)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s_{1,2,3}\to 0}s_{1,2,3}\mathcal{J}_{\rm BG}^{\phi^{3}}(1,2,3,\kappa),

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(1,2,3,\kappa)

\displaystyle=\left.\mathcal{A}_{4}^{\phi}(1,2,3,\kappa)\right|_{\Sigma(\kappa)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{1,2}}+\frac{1}{s_{2,3}}.

(60)

On the other hand, it is easy to show that the (CHY) current
\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime})
can be derived from the respective amplitude when applying the kinematic shift,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{4}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lim_{s_{1,3,4}\to 0}s_{1,3,4}\mathcal{J}_{{\rm BG}}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left.\mathcal{A}_{4}^{\phi^{3}}(1,3,4,\kappa^{\prime})\right|_{\Sigma(\kappa^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{3,4}}+\frac{1}{s_{1,3}-p_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{3,4}}+\frac{1}{s_{4,\kappa^{\prime}}}.

(61)

More generally, the splitting of the tree level scattering amplitudes for the bi-adjoint scalar theory can be expressed as

\mathcal{A}^{\phi^{3}}(1,\ldots,n)\to\left.\mathcal{A}^{\phi^{3}}(i,A,j,\kappa)\right|_{\Sigma(\kappa)}\times\left.\mathcal{A}^{\phi^{3}}(1,\ldots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\right|_{\Sigma({\kappa^{\prime})}}.

(62)

Now we should check whether this kinematic shift can be extended to the other half-integrands of the CHY representations, since they do involve kinematic data.

We start by analyzing
W_{\{1\ldots n\}}
, cf. equation (
28
), within the amputated currents. Since it involves only
p_{c}\cdot\epsilon_{d}
, the off-shell shift represented by
\Delta_{c,d}
does not modify it. Additionally, the split conditions imply that the polarization vector associated with the off-shell leg,
\epsilon_{\kappa^{\prime}}
, is transversal. Therefore, for this class of integrands it suffices to evaluate the associated split amplitudes and implement the shift (
59
). For example, the split amplitude of equation (
90
) is recast as

\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,\ldots,n)\\
\to\left.\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})\right|_{\Sigma(\kappa)}\times\left.\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,\ldots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\right|^{\epsilon_{\kappa^{\prime}}\rightarrow\epsilon_{n}}_{\Sigma(\kappa^{\prime})}.

(63)

The second half-integrand of interest is the reduced Pfaffian, cf. equation (
19
). The matrix
\bm{\Psi}_{n}
is composed by the sub-matrices
\mathbf{A}_{n}
,
\mathbf{B}_{n}
, and
\mathbf{C}_{n}
. The latter remains well-defined with the shift, being consistent with the splitting conditions and the shift operation (
59
). The sub-matrix
\mathbf{B}_{n}
involves only the polarization vectors, so it also left untouched. Finally, the sub-matrix
\mathbf{A}_{n}
is the only with a similar structure to the scattering equations. The idea then is to simply perform the shift (
59
) in its entries, such that

\left.\mathbf{A}_{n}^{cd}\right|_{\Sigma{(\kappa)}}=\begin{cases}\frac{s_{c,d}+\Delta_{c,d}}{2\,z_{c,d}},&\textrm{for }c\neq d,\\
0,&\textrm{for }c=d.\end{cases}

(64)

In analogy with the bi-adjoint scalar and the
(DF)^{2}
theory, for this class of half-integrands it suffices to compute the amplitudes and then implement the corresponding shift conditions in order to express the off-shell currents. For pure Yang–Mills, the splitting can be written as

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{YM}}(1,\ldots,n)\to\left.\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{YM}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa^{\phi})\right|_{\Sigma(\kappa)}\times\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{YM}}(1,\ldots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\Big|^{\epsilon_{\kappa^{\prime}}\rightarrow\epsilon_{n}}_{\Sigma(\kappa^{\prime})},

(65)

validating the underlying idea of a kinematic shifting.

5
Transmuting operators

Originally introduced in
12
, transmuting operators are differential operators that map tree-level scattering amplitudes from one theory to those of another. In this section, we introduce a new transmuting operator. Among other properties, it emulates the coupling to bi-adjoint scalars in the amplitudes of the different higher-derivative field theories discussed in section
3
.

Inspired by the transmuting operators defined in
12
, given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\Psi}[i_{1},i_{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{i_{1}i_{2}},

(66)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\Psi}[i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}]

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{a=2}^{m-1}\mathcal{T}_{i_{a-1}i_{a}i_{m}}\right)\mathcal{T}_{i_{1}i_{m}},

(67)

for
3\leq m\leq n
, with

\mathcal{T}_{ij}=\partial_{\epsilon_{i}\cdot\epsilon_{j}},\qquad\mathcal{T}_{ijr}\equiv\partial_{p_{i}\cdot\epsilon_{j}}-\partial_{\epsilon_{j}\cdot p_{r}},

(68)

we define

\mathcal{T}^{W}[i_{1},i_{2}]=\mathcal{T}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\cdot\mathcal{T}_{i_{2}i_{1}i_{3}},

(69)

where the label
i_{3}
works as an auxiliary momentum, and

\mathcal{T}^{W}[i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}]=\left(\prod_{a=2}^{m-1}\mathcal{T}_{i_{a-1}i_{a}i_{m}}\right)\mathcal{T}_{i_{1}i_{m}i_{2}}\mathcal{T}_{i_{m}i_{1}i_{2}},

(70)

also for
3\leq m\leq n
.
It is then straightforward to verify the following identities,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\Psi}[i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}]\cdot\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{PT}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})\mathrm{Pf}\bm{\Psi}_{\{H\}},

(71a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{W}[i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}]\cdot\mathrm{Pf}^{\prime}\bm{\Psi}_{n}

\displaystyle=0,

(71b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\Psi}[i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}]\cdot W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}

\displaystyle=0,

(71c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{W}[i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}]\cdot W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{PT}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})\prod_{h\in H}{\bf C}_{n}^{hh},

(71d)

where
H=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}\}
.
Because of these properties, the action of the transmuting operators gives rise to several relations between amplitudes in different theories, such as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\phi^{3}}(1,2,\ldots,n)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}^{W}[1,2,\ldots,n]\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,2,\ldots,n),

(72)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,2,\ldots,n)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}^{W}[1,2,\ldots,n]\mathcal{M}^{R^{3}}(1,2,\ldots,n),

(73)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,2,\ldots,n)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}^{\Psi}[1,2,\ldots,n]\mathcal{M}^{R^{2}}(1,2,\ldots,n),

(74)

and

\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}+\phi^{3}}(1^{\phi},\ldots,i^{\phi},i+1,\ldots,n)=\mathcal{T}^{W}[1,\ldots,i]\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,2,\ldots,n).

(75)

Since we have reformulated the splitting operation in terms of sub-amplitudes, we are set to extend the action of the transmuting operators to the split amplitudes. Below we list some examples:

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{YM}}(1,\ldots,n)\to\mathcal{T}^{\Psi}[i,j,\kappa]\left.\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{YM}}(i,A,j,\kappa)\vphantom{\mathcal{A}^{R^{3}}}\right|_{\Sigma(\kappa)}\times\left.\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{YM}}(1,\ldots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\vphantom{\mathcal{A}^{R^{3}}}\right|^{\epsilon_{\kappa^{\prime}}\to\epsilon_{n}}_{\Sigma(\kappa^{\prime})},

(76)

\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,\ldots,n)\to\mathcal{T}^{W}[i,j,\kappa]\left.\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}}(i,A,j,\kappa)\right|_{\Sigma(\kappa)}\times\left.\mathcal{A}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,\ldots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\right|^{\epsilon_{\kappa^{\prime}}\to\epsilon_{n}}_{\Sigma(\kappa^{\prime})},

(77)

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{GR}}\to\mathcal{T}^{\Psi}[i,j,\kappa]\mathcal{T}^{\Psi}[j,\kappa^{\prime},i]\left.\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{GR}}(i,A,j,\kappa)\vphantom{\mathcal{M}^{R^{3}}}\right|^{\epsilon_{\kappa}\to\epsilon_{n}}_{\Sigma(\kappa)}\times\left.\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{GR}}(1,\ldots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\vphantom{\mathcal{A}^{R^{3}}}\right|^{\epsilon_{\kappa^{\prime}}\to\epsilon_{n}}_{\Sigma(\kappa^{\prime})},

(78)

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{R^{2}}\to\mathcal{T}^{\Psi}[i,j,\kappa]\mathcal{T}^{W}[j,\kappa^{\prime},i]\left.\mathcal{M}^{R^{2}}(i,A,j,\kappa)\right|^{\epsilon_{\kappa}\to\epsilon_{n}}_{\Sigma(\kappa)}\times\left.\mathcal{M}^{R^{2}}(1,\ldots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\right|^{\epsilon_{\kappa^{\prime}}\to\epsilon_{n}}_{\Sigma(\kappa^{\prime})},

(79)

\mathcal{M}^{R^{3}}_{n}\to\mathcal{T}^{W}[i,j,\kappa]\mathcal{T}^{W}[j,\kappa^{\prime},i]\left.\mathcal{M}^{R^{3}}(i,A,j,\kappa)\right|^{\epsilon_{\kappa}\to\epsilon_{n}}_{\Sigma(\kappa)}\times\left.\mathcal{M}^{R^{3}}(1,\ldots,i,j,\ldots,\kappa^{\prime})\right|^{\epsilon_{\kappa^{\prime}}\to\epsilon_{n}}_{\Sigma(\kappa^{\prime})}.

(80)

We could of course compare the action of the transmuting operators before and after the splitting, but the details are not very illuminating.

6
Smooth splitting and hidden zeros

In this section, we extend our analysis to the 3-split process of
6
. The three fixed punctures, denoted by
\mathcal{I}=\{i,j,k\}
, with
i<j<k
, divide the full set of
n
-external particles into three disjoint subsets, which we label
A
,
B
, and
C
.

Under the conditions

s_{a,b}=s_{b,c}=s_{c,a}=0\qquad\forall\,a\in A,\,b\in B\,,c\in C\,,

(81)

the integration measure becomes

{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{n}\to\Delta(i,j,k)^{2}\left[\Delta(i,j,\kappa_{A})^{-2}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\mathrm{A}}(i,j,A;\kappa_{A})\right]\\
\times\left[\Delta(j,k,\kappa_{B})^{-2}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\mathrm{B}}(j,k,B;\kappa_{B})\right]\left[\Delta(k,i,\kappa_{C})^{-2}{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{\mathrm{B}}(k,i,C;\kappa_{C})\right],

(82)

with

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{A}=\Delta(i,j,\kappa_{A})^{2}\,{\prod_{a\in A}}{\mathrm{d}}z_{a}\,\delta\left(S_{a}\right),&&\displaystyle S_{a}=\frac{s_{a,i}}{z_{a,i}}+\frac{s_{a,j}}{z_{a,j}}+\frac{s_{a,\kappa_{A}}}{z_{a,\kappa_{A}}}+\sum_{\tilde{a}\in A\setminus\{a\}}\frac{s_{a,\tilde{a}}}{z_{a,\tilde{a}}},\\
\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{B}=\Delta(j,k,\kappa_{B})^{2}\,{\prod_{b\in B}}{\mathrm{d}}z_{b}\,\delta\left(S_{b}\right),&&\displaystyle S_{b}=\frac{s_{b,\kappa_{B}}}{z_{b,\kappa_{B}}}+\frac{s_{b,j}}{z_{b,j}}+\frac{s_{b,k}}{z_{b,k}}+\sum_{\tilde{b}\in B\setminus\{b\}}\frac{s_{b,\tilde{b}}}{z_{b,\tilde{b}}},\\
\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{A}=\Delta(k,i,\kappa_{C})^{2}\,{\prod_{c\in C}}{\mathrm{d}}z_{c}\,\delta\left(S_{c}\right),&&\displaystyle S_{c}=\frac{s_{b,i}}{z_{b,i}}+\frac{s_{b,\kappa_{C}}}{z_{b,\kappa_{C}}}+\frac{s_{b,k}}{z_{b,k}}+\sum_{\tilde{c}\in C\setminus\{c\}}\frac{s_{c,\tilde{c}}}{z_{c,\tilde{c}}},\end{array}

(83)

where
\{\kappa_{A},\kappa_{B},\kappa_{C}\}
respectively replace the legs
\{k,i,j\}
, and represent off-shell states with

\displaystyle p_{\kappa_{A}}

\displaystyle=-p_{i}-p_{j}-\sum_{a\in A}p_{a},

(84a)

\displaystyle p_{\kappa_{B}}

\displaystyle=-p_{j}-p_{k}-\sum_{b\in B}p_{b},

(84b)

\displaystyle p_{\kappa_{C}}

\displaystyle=-p_{k}-p_{i}-\sum_{c\in C}p_{c}.

(84c)

It is also straightforward to check that the Parke–Taylor factor can be rewritten as

\mathrm{PT}(1,\ldots,n)=\Delta(i,j,k)^{-1}\left[\Delta(i,j,\kappa_{A}){\rm PT}(i,A,j,\kappa_{A})\right]\\
\times\left[\Delta(j,k,\kappa_{B}){\rm PT}(j,B,k,\kappa_{B})\right]\left[\Delta(k,i,\kappa_{C}){\rm PT}(k,C,i,\kappa_{C}\right].

(85)

With this basic ingredients, it can be shown
6
, for instance, that the tree level amplitude for the bi-adjoint scalars has a 3-split form given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\phi^{3}}(1,\ldots,n)\to

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(i,A,j,\kappa_{A})\times\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(j,B,k,\kappa_{B})\times\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(k,C,i,\kappa_{C}),

(86)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{A}^{\phi^{3}}(i,A,j,\kappa_{A})\Big|_{\Sigma(\kappa_{A})}\times\mathcal{A}^{\phi^{3}}(j,B,k,\kappa_{B})\Big|_{\Sigma(\kappa_{B})}\times\mathcal{A}^{\phi^{3}}(k,C,i,\kappa_{C})\Big|_{\Sigma(\kappa_{C})}.

For the second line, we have simply applied the results of section
4
. In the particular case in which
A
has just one element, i.e.
A=\{a\}
, the above equation leads to

\mathcal{A}^{\phi^{3}}(1,\ldots,n)\to\left(\frac{1}{s_{a,i}}+\frac{1}{s_{a,j}}\right)\times\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(j,B,k,\kappa_{B})\times\mathcal{J}^{\phi^{3}}(k,C,i,\kappa_{C}).

(87)

When
-(s_{a,i}+s_{a,j})=s_{a,\kappa_{A}}=s_{a,k}=0
, the right hand side of (
87
) vanishes, establishing the connection between the 3-split behavior and hidden zeros of the amplitude
1
.

Now, let us focus on the integrand
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
.
Since the sets
A
,
B
, and
C
are mutually disjoint, we impose the 3-split conditions

\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{b}=\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{c}=0,\quad\epsilon_{b}\cdot p_{a}=\epsilon_{b}\cdot p_{c}=0,\quad\epsilon_{c}\cdot p_{a}=\epsilon_{c}\cdot p_{b}=0,\quad\forall\,a\in A,\,b\in B\,,c\in C\,.

(88)

Then, it follows that
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
splits as

W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}\to\prod_{a\in A}\left(-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{a}\in A\cup\{i,j,\kappa_{A}\}\\
(\tilde{a}\neq a)\end{subarray}}\frac{\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{\tilde{a}}}{z_{a,\tilde{a}}}\right)\prod_{b\in B}\left(-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{b}\in B\cup\{j,k,\kappa_{B}\}\\
(\tilde{b}\neq b)\end{subarray}}\frac{\epsilon_{b}\cdot p_{\tilde{b}}}{z_{b,\tilde{b}}}\right)W_{C\cup\{k,i,\kappa_{C}\}},

(89)

Equation (
89
), together with (
82
) and (
85
), imply the 3-split of the
(DF)^{2}
amplitude, expressed as

{\cal{A}}_{n}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,\ldots,n)\to{\cal{J}}^{(DF)^{2}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa_{A}^{\phi})\\
\times{\cal{J}}^{(DF)^{2}+\phi^{3}}(j^{\phi},B,k^{\phi},\kappa_{B}^{\phi})\times{\cal{J}}_{\mu}^{(DF)^{2}}(k,C,i,\kappa_{C})\epsilon_{j}^{\mu}.

(90)

In the special case
A=\{a\}
, the 3-split behavior of (
90
) is recast as

\mathcal{A}_{n}^{(DF)^{2}}(1,\ldots,n)\to\left(\frac{\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{i}}{s_{a,i}}-\frac{\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{j}}{s_{a,j}}\right)\\
\times\mathcal{J}^{(DF)^{2}+\phi^{3}}(j^{\phi},B,k^{\phi},\kappa_{B}^{\phi})\times\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{(DF)^{2}}(k,C,i,\kappa_{C})\epsilon_{j}^{\mu}

(91)

In order to uncover the hidden zero, we impose
s_{a,\kappa_{A}}=s_{a,k}=0
and
\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{\kappa_{A}}=\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{k}=0
. It is then straightforward to show that the first term on the right hand side of (
91
) vanishes.

Naturally, this analysis extends to the
R^{3}
theory. By imposing the splitting conditions (
88
) on both
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
factors, the 3-split behavior is immediately revealed, either as

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{R^{3}}\to\mathcal{J}^{R^{3}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa_{A}^{\phi})\times\mathcal{J}^{R^{3}+\phi^{3}}(j^{\phi},B,k^{\phi},\kappa_{B}^{\phi})\times\mathcal{J}_{\mu\nu}^{R^{3}}(k,C,i,\kappa_{C})\epsilon_{j}^{\mu}\epsilon_{j}^{\nu},

(92)

or as

\mathcal{M}_{n}^{R^{3}}\to\mathcal{J}^{R^{3}+\phi^{3}}(i^{\phi},A,j^{\phi},\kappa_{A}^{\phi})\\
\times\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{R^{3}+(DF)^{2}}(j^{g},B,k^{g},\kappa_{B}^{g})\epsilon_{i}^{\mu}\times\mathcal{J}_{\nu}^{R^{3}+(DF)^{2}}(k^{g},C,i^{g},\kappa_{C}^{g})\epsilon_{j}^{\nu},

(93)

where the superscript
g
denotes gluon states from the
(DF)^{2}
theory.

Analogously to the
(DF)^{2}
case with
A=\{a\}
, the additional conditions
s_{a,\kappa_{A}}=s_{a,k}=0
and
\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{\kappa_{A}}=\epsilon_{a}\cdot p_{k}=0
lead to the vanishing the amplitude.

7
Final remarks

In this paper, we have extended the universal 2-split behavior of tree-level scattering amplitudes of particles and strings found in
10
;
9
to amplitudes in higher-derivative theories. This is achieved via a simple analysis of the split behavior of
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
, cf. equation (
28
), which is the key ingredient in the CHY representations of the
(DF)^{2}
gauge theory and the
R^{2}
gravity amplitudes
4
. As expected, the splitting of
W_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
is analogous to the splitting of the reduced Pfaffian. Therefore, gluon and graviton amplitudes in those theories display analogous splitting behaviors.

Our main result, inspired by a recent work by one of the authors
15
and discussed in section
4
, is the recasting of the 2-split behavior in terms of amplitudes rather than CHY currents, i.e. directly in terms of
physical
quantities. This result extends to all theories previously analyzed under the 2-split behavior. Within the higher derivative models, we further simplify their analysis with the introduction of new transmuting operators and discuss their 3-split behavior in line with reference
6
.

We hope this new on-shell characterization of the 2-split behavior will help with the understanding of the so-called hidden zeros of scattering amplitudes. Furthermore, it would be interesting to investigate to what extent it could be extended to loop-level or even to massive theories. The latter, in particular, have been notoriously elusive to the CHY framework, which might indicate that other methods would be better suited to the investigation of their possible 2-split behavior.

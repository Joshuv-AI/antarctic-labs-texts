1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we assume that the reader is familiar with the value distribution theory of meromorphic functions and is acquainted with the standard notations of Nevanlinna theory (
[
7
]
). Furthermore, let
E
denote any set of positive real numbers with finite Lebesgue measure, where
E
may vary in each occurrence.

A meromorphic function
\mathfrak{f}
is either analytic or contains at most countable numbers of poles in the complex plane. If
\mathfrak{f}
has no poles, then
\mathfrak{f}
reduces to an entire function. The order of
\mathfrak{f}
is denoted by
\rho(\mathfrak{f})
and the hyper-order of
\mathfrak{f}
is denoted by
\rho_{2}(\mathfrak{f})
and defined as

\rho_{2}(\mathfrak{f}):=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log T(r,f)}{\log r}.

For any non-constant meromorphic function
\mathfrak{f}
, we denote by
S(r,\mathfrak{f})
, any quantity satisfying

S(r,\mathfrak{f})=o(T(r,\mathfrak{f}))~~~\text{as}~~r\rightarrow\infty,r\notin E.

By
shift
of
\mathfrak{f}(z)
, we mean
\mathfrak{f}(z+c)
, where
c
is a non-zero complex constant. Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a non-constant meromorphic function. A meromorphic function
a(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
is called a “small function” with respect to
\mathfrak{f}
if
T(r,a(z))=S(r,\mathfrak{f})
.

Definition 1.1
.

(
[
16
]
)
Let
a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. For a positive integer
k
, we define

(i)

N_{k)}(r,a;\mathfrak{f})
as the counting function of
a
- points of
\mathfrak{f}
with multiplicity
\leq k
,

(ii)

N_{(k}(r,a;\mathfrak{f})
as the counting function of
a
- points of
\mathfrak{f}
with multiplicity
\geq k
.

Similarly, the reduced counting function
\overline{N}_{k)}(r,a;\mathfrak{f})
and
\overline{N}_{(k}(r,a;\mathfrak{f})
are defined.

Definition 1.2
.

(
[
10
]
)
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We define
N_{k}(r,0;\mathfrak{f})
as the counting function of zeros of
\mathfrak{f}
, where a zero of
\mathfrak{f}
with multiplicity
q
is counted
q
times if
q\leq k
, and is counted
k
times if
q>k
.

In 1959, Hayman (
[
6
]
) proved the following theorem:

Theorem A
.

(
[
6
]
)
If
\mathfrak{f}
is a transcendental meromorphic function and
n\geq 3
, then
\mathfrak{f}^{n}\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
assumes all finite values except possibly zero infinitely often.

Moreover, Hayman (
[
6
]
) conjectured that the Theorem A remains valid for the cases
n=1
,
2
. In 1979, Mues (
[
11
]
) confirmed the Hayman’s conjecture for
n=2
as well as Chen and Fang (
[
4
]
) ensured the conjecture for
n=1
in 1995. Later, in 1992, Q. Zhang (
[
17
]
) gave the quantitative version of Mues’s result as follows:

Theorem B
.

(
[
17
]
)
For a transcendental meromorphic function
\mathfrak{f}
, the following inequality holds:

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq 6N\left(r,\frac{1}{\mathfrak{f}^{2}\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}-1}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

In (
[
13
]
), Theorem B was improved by Xu and Yi as

Theorem C
.

(
[
13
]
)
For a transcendental meromorphic function
\mathfrak{f}
and
\phi(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function, then the following inequality holds:

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq 6\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{\phi\mathfrak{f}^{2}\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}-1}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Later, in 2005, Huang and Gu (
[
8
]
) replaced
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
by
\mathfrak{f}^{(k)}
, where
k(\geq 1)
is an integer and proved the following result:

Theorem D
.

(
[
8
]
)
For a transcendental meromorphic function
\mathfrak{f}
and a positive integer
k
, the following inequality holds:

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq 6N\left(r,\frac{1}{\mathfrak{f}^{2}\mathfrak{f}^{(k)}-1}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

A natural question arises as to whether the given inequality remains valid when the counting function in Theorem D is replaced by the reduced counting function. Addressing this question, Xu, Yi, and Zhang made significant progress in 2009 by proving the following theorem (
[
14
]
):

Theorem E
.

(
[
14
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental meromorphic function, and
k

(\geq 1)
be a positive integer. If
N_{1}(r,0;\mathfrak{f})=S(r,\mathfrak{f})
, then

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq 2\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{\mathfrak{f}^{2}\mathfrak{f}^{(k)}-1}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Later, in 2011, removing the restrictions on zeros of
\mathfrak{f}
, Xu, Yi and Zhang (
[
15
]
) proved the following theorem:

Theorem F
.

(
[
15
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental meromorphic function, and
k

(\geq 1)
be a positive integer. Then

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq M\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{\mathfrak{f}^{2}\mathfrak{f}^{(k)}-1}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f}),

where
M
is
6
if
k=1
, or
k\geq 3
and
M
is
10
if
k=2
.

Later, in 2018, Karmakar and Sahoo (
[
9
]
) further improved the Theorem F and obtained the following result:

Theorem G
.

(
[
9
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental meromorphic function, and
k

(\geq 1)
,
n

(\geq 2)
be a positive integer. Then

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq\frac{6}{2n-3}\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{\mathfrak{f}^{n}\mathfrak{f}^{(k)}-1}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Furthermore, in 2020, Chakraborty et al. (
[
2
]
) generalized Theorem G for transcendental meromorphic functions without simple poles and established the following result:

Theorem H
.

(
[
2
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental meromorphic function such that it has no simple pole. Also let
q_{0}

(\geq 2)
,
q_{k}

(\geq 1)
are
(k\in\mathbb{N})
integer. Then

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq\frac{6}{2q_{0}-3}\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{\mathfrak{f}^{q_{0}}{(\mathfrak{f}^{(k)})}^{q_{k}}-1}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Later, Bhoosnurmath, Chakraborty, and Srivastava (
[
1
]
), as well as Chakraborty and Lü (
[
3
]
), and Saha and Chakraborty (
[
12
]
), obtained many significant results in this direction. Among these, the work of Saha and Chakraborty (
[
12
]
) notably improved and extended the results of Karmakar and Sahoo (
[
9
]
) for a specific class of transcendental meromorphic functions. Furthermore, the following theorem represents a significant improvement over the recent results of B. Chakraborty et al. (
[
2
]
).

Theorem I
.

(
[
12
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental meromorphic function such that
N_{1)}(r,\infty;\mathfrak{f})=S(r,\mathfrak{f})
and
\alpha=\alpha(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
\mathfrak{f}
. Also let
q_{0}

(\geq 2)
,
q_{i}

(\geq 0)

(i=1,2,\ldots,k-1)
,
q_{k}

(\geq 1)
are non- negative integers. Then for any small function
a=a(z)(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
of
\mathfrak{f}
,

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq\frac{2}{2q_{0}-3}\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{\alpha\mathfrak{f}^{q_{0}}{(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})}^{q_{1}}\ldots{(\mathfrak{f}^{(k)})}^{q_{k}}-a}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Motivated by Theorem H and Theorem I, in this note, we are try to investigate the value distribution of a differential- difference monomials
\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
generated by a transcendental entire function with hyper-order less than one.

2.
main results

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental entire function with hyper-order less than one and
\alpha(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
\mathfrak{f}
. Let
c
be a non-zero complex number and
q_{0}\geq 2,

q_{1}\geq 1
be non-negative integers. Then for any small function
a(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
of
\mathfrak{f}
,

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq\frac{1}{\left(q_{0}-1\right)}\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{\alpha(z)\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}(z+c))^{q_{1}}-a(z)}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental entire function with hyper-order less than one and
\alpha(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
\mathfrak{f}
. Let
c
be a non-zero complex number and
q_{1}\geq 1
and
q_{0}>q_{1}+1
be non-negative integers. Then for any small function
a(z)(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
of
\mathfrak{f}
,

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq\frac{1}{\left(q_{0}-q_{1}-1\right)}\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{\alpha(z)\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}(z+c))^{q_{1}}-a(z)}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

3.
Some important Lemmas

To establish our results, we require the following lemmas:

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
5
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{f}(z)
be a non-constant transcendental meromorphic function with hyper-order less than one, and let
c
be a non-zero finite complex number. Then

m\left(r,\frac{\mathfrak{f}(z+c)}{\mathfrak{f}(z)}\right)=S(r,\mathfrak{f})~~\text{and}~~m\left(r,\frac{\mathfrak{f}(z)}{\mathfrak{f}(z+c)}\right)=S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
18
]
, Lemma 6)
Let
\mathfrak{f}(z)
be a meromorphic function with hyper-order less than one, and let
c
be a non-zero finite complex number. Then

T(r,\mathfrak{f}(z+c))=T(r,\mathfrak{f})+S(r,\mathfrak{f})~~\text{and}~~N(r,\mathfrak{f}(z+c))=N(r,\mathfrak{f})+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental meromorphic function with hyper-order less than one. Then

m\left(r,\frac{{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}(z+c)}{\mathfrak{f}(z+d)}\right)=S(r,\mathfrak{f}),

for all
z
satisfies
|z|=r\notin E
,
E
is a set with finite logarithmic measure, where
c
and
d
are complex constants and
k
is a non-negative integer.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}(z+c)}{\mathfrak{f}(z+d)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{{\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}(z+c)}{\mathfrak{f}(z+c)}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{\mathfrak{f}(z+c)}{\mathfrak{f}(z)}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{\mathfrak{f}(z)}{\mathfrak{f}(z+d)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Thus, the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental entire function with hyper-order less than one and
\alpha(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
\mathfrak{f}
. Let
M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]=\alpha(z)\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
, where
c
is a non-zero complex number and
q_{0}\geq 2,

q_{1}\geq 1
are integers. Then

T(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])\leq(q_{0}+q_{1})T(r,\mathfrak{f})+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Proof.

In view of Lemma (
3.2
), the proof is obvious. ∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental entire function with hyper-order less than one and
\alpha(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
\mathfrak{f}
. Let
M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]=\alpha\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
, where
c
is a non-zero complex number and
q_{0}\geq 2,

q_{1}\geq 1
are non-negative integers. Then
M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]
is not identically constant.

Proof.

Define
\mu=q_{0}+q_{1}
be degree of the monomial
M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]
. Note that

\frac{S(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])}{T(r,\mathfrak{f})}=\frac{S(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])}{T(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])}\cdot\frac{T(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])}{T(r,\mathfrak{f})}.

Thus we can conclude that
S(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])=S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Since

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{f}^{\mu}}=\alpha(z)\left(\frac{\mathfrak{f}(z+c)}{\mathfrak{f}(z)}\right)^{q_{1}}\frac{1}{M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]},

therefore, by the 1st fundamental theorem and Lemma (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle{}\mu T\left(r,\mathfrak{f}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q_{1}T\left(r,\frac{\mathfrak{f}(z+c)}{\mathfrak{f}(z)}\right)+T\left(r,\frac{1}{M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]}\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q_{1}N\left(r,\frac{\mathfrak{f}(z+c)}{\mathfrak{f}(z)}\right)+T\left(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q_{1}[N(r,0;\mathfrak{f})+N(r,\infty;\mathfrak{f}(z+c))]+T\left(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q_{1}N(r,0;\mathfrak{f})+T\left(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]\right)+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(q_{1}+1)T(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

Therefore
M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]
is not identically constant.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental entire function with hyper-order less than one and
\alpha(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
\mathfrak{f}
. Let
M_{2}[\mathfrak{f}]=\alpha(z)\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
, where
c
is a non-zero complex number and
q_{0}\geq 2,

q_{1}\geq 1
are non-negative integers. Then
M_{2}[\mathfrak{f}]
is not identically constant.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a transcendental entire function with hyper-order less than one and
\alpha(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
be a small function of
\mathfrak{f}
. Let
M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]=\alpha(z)\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
, where
c
is a non-zero complex number and
q_{0}\geq 2,

q_{1}\geq 1
are non-negative integers. If
a(z)

(\not\equiv 0,\infty)
is an another small function of
\mathfrak{f}
, then

\mu T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq N(r,0;\mathfrak{f}^{\mu})+\overline{N}(r,0;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])+\overline{N}(r,a;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])-N(r,0;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])+S(r,\mathfrak{f}),

where
\mu=q_{0}+q_{1}
.

Proof.

Now, with help of Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mu T(r,\mathfrak{f})=T\left(r,\frac{1}{\mathfrak{f}^{\mu}}\right)+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(r,0;\mathfrak{f}^{\mu})+m(r,0;\mathfrak{f}^{\mu})+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N(r,0;\mathfrak{f}^{\mu})+m(r,0;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N(r,0;\mathfrak{f}^{\mu})+T(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])-N(r,0;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

Now, using the Nevanlinna’s 2nd Fundamental theorem for the function
M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]
, we have

(3.2)

T(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])\leq\overline{N}(r,\infty;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])+\overline{N}(r,0;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])+\overline{N}(r,a;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])+S(r,M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])

Using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we have

\mu T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq N(r,0;\mathfrak{f}^{\mu})+\overline{N}(r,0;M[\mathfrak{f}])+\overline{N}(r,a;M[\mathfrak{f}])-N(r,0;M[\mathfrak{f}])+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Thus, the lemma is proved.
∎

4.
Proofs of the Main Theorems

Proof of the Theorem
2.1
.

Let
M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}]=\alpha(z)\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
and
\mu=q_{0}+q_{1}
.

Case 1:
Let
z_{0}
be a zero of
\mathfrak{f}(z)
of multiplicity
q
and
\mathfrak{f}(z+c)
has no zero at
z_{0}
.

Thus
z_{0}
is a zero of
\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
of order at least
qq_{0}.
Now,

q\mu+1-qq_{0}=q(q_{0}+q_{1})+1-qq_{0}\leq qq_{1}+1

Therefore,

(4.1)

\displaystyle N(r,0;\mathfrak{f}^{\mu})+\overline{N}(r,0;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])-N(r,0;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q_{1}N(r,0;\mathfrak{f})+\overline{N}(r,0;\mathfrak{f})

Using Lemma (
3.7
) and (
4.1
), we get

\displaystyle\mu T(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,a;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])+(q_{1}+1)T(r,\mathfrak{f})+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Therefore,

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq\frac{1}{(q_{0}-1)}\overline{N}(r,a;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Case 2:
Let
z_{0}
be a zero of
\mathfrak{f}(z)
of multiplicity
q
and
\mathfrak{f}(z+c)
has also zero at
z_{0}
of multiplicity
k
.

If
q=k
, then
z_{0}
is a zero of
\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
of order at least
qq_{0}+kq_{1}.
Now,

q\mu+1-(qq_{0}+kq_{1})=q(q_{0}+q_{1})+1-qq_{0}-kq_{1}\leq 1

Therefore,

(4.2)

\displaystyle N(r,0;\mathfrak{f}^{\mu})+\overline{N}(r,0;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])-N(r,0;M_{1}[\mathfrak{f}])\leq\overline{N}(r,0;\mathfrak{f})

Using (
4.2
) and Lemma (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle\mu T(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,a;M[\mathfrak{f}])+T(r,\mathfrak{f})+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

Since
q_{1}\geq 1
, then

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq\frac{1}{(q_{0}-1)}\overline{N}(r,a;M[\mathfrak{f}])+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

If
q\not=k
, then
z_{0}
is a zero of
\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
of order at least
qq_{0}+kq_{1}.
Now,

q\mu+1-(qq_{0}+kq_{1})=qq_{1}+1-kq_{1}\leq qq_{1}+1

Therefore,

(4.3)

\displaystyle N(r,0;\mathfrak{f}^{\mu})+\overline{N}(r,0;M[\mathfrak{f}])-N(r,0;M[\mathfrak{f}])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q_{1}N(r,0;\mathfrak{f})+\overline{N}(r,0;\mathfrak{f})

Using (
4.3
) and Lemma (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle\mu T(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,a;M[\mathfrak{f}])+q_{1}T(r,\mathfrak{f})+T(r,\mathfrak{f})+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Therefore,

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq\frac{1}{(q_{0}-1)}\overline{N}(r,a;M[\mathfrak{f}])+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

Case 3:
Let
\mathfrak{f}(z)
has no zero at
z_{0}
, but
\mathfrak{f}(z+c)
has zero at
z_{0}
of multiplicity
q
.

Then
z_{0}
is a zero of
\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
of order at least
qq_{1}.
Now,

1-qq_{1}\leq 1\leq qq_{1}+1

Therefore,

(4.4)

\displaystyle N(r,0;\mathfrak{f}^{\mu})+\overline{N}(r,0;M[\mathfrak{f}])-N(r,0;M[\mathfrak{f}])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q_{1}N(r,0;\mathfrak{f}(z+c))+\overline{N}(r,0;\mathfrak{f}(z+c))

Using (
4.4
), Lemma (
3.7
) and Lemma (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle\mu T(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,a;M[\mathfrak{f}])+q_{1}T(r,0;\mathfrak{f}(z+c))+T(r,0;\mathfrak{f}(z+c))+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,a;M[\mathfrak{f}])+q_{1}T(r,\mathfrak{f})+T(r,\mathfrak{f})+S(r,\mathfrak{f})

Therefore,

T(r,\mathfrak{f})\leq\frac{1}{(q_{0}-1)}\overline{N}(r,a;M[\mathfrak{f}])+S(r,\mathfrak{f}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of the Theorem
2.2
.

Let
M_{2}[\mathfrak{f}]=\alpha(z)\mathfrak{f}(z)^{q_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}(z+c))^{q_{1}}
and
\mu=q_{0}+q_{1}
.

Case 1

Attention-guided YOLOv5s-SDF model for accurate detection of strip steel surface defects
Authors: Kun Liu, Xinbo Chang, Hongru Ma, Liang Lv, Hang Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSurface defects on strip steel are often inevitable due to limitations in raw materials and manufacturing processes. To improve defect detection accuracy, we propose the YOLOv5s-SDF algorithm, which integrates ShuffleAttention in the neck and DyHead in the head of the YOLOv5s framework. The novelty lies in the use of ShuffleAttention to model spatial-channel dependencies and the incorporation of Focal-CIoU loss to mitigate the influence of low-quality samples. Experiments on the NEU-DET dataset show that YOLOv5s-SDF achieves 69.37% Precision, 74.21% Recall, 76.32% , and 38.60% , outperforming the baseline YOLOv5s by 0.2%, 1.72%, 3.57%, and 0.84%, respectively.Keywords: YOLOv5s model, Object detection, Attention mechanism, DyHeadSubject terms: Computer science, Mathematics and computing, Information technology

Introduction

Strip steel is widely used in the fields of national defense aviation, aerospace, and military industry. Due to the elements including rolling equipment, working environment, and processing technology, various defects are prone to occur on the surface of the strip steel1, which weakens its wear and corrosion resistance characteristics, While damaging the appearance of the strip steel2–4. Early steel defect detection either uses traditional object detection algorithms or employs manual detection methods5. The Scale Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) was applied to the surface defect recognition problem of medium and thick plates by Zhou Peng et al.6, and successfully achieved a recognition rate of over 95 for types such as roll marks and scratches.

Traditional object detection algorithms are currently less widely used due to their low detection efficiency and poor results. Object detection algorithms combined with deeping learning can be categorized in two distinct types: One-Stage and Two-Stage. But most algorithms cannot balance detection accuracy and speed, and are not suitable for complex and changing object detection environments. The R-CNN algorithm proposed by Girsshick is a representative two-stage object detection algorithm7, which applies convolutional neural networks (CNN) to achieve the extraction of features. To promote detection velocity, Fast R-CNN and Faster R-CNN were invoked by Girsshick et al.89, which tremendously elevated the whole capability of the network. Albeit the identification mistake rate and reject rate of two-stage algorithm are inferior, also there are many optimization studies on detection speed, the velocity is still lag and cannot fill the real-time detection of industrial defects. The detection accuracy of the One-Stage algorithm is below than that of the Two-Stage algorithm, while the speed is faster. Representative algorithms include YOLO and SSD algorithms10–14.

To enhance the low accuracy of appearance deficiency detection algorithms for strip steel in practical applications, YOLOv5s-SDF which is a strip steel appearance deficiency detection method based on improved YOLOv5s is proposed. The main innovation points are the following three aspects:

Firstly, ShuffleAttention is used in the neck network to divide the channel dimension into multiple sub-features for parallel processing. For each sub-feature, Shuffle Units are utilized to depict feature dependencies in both spatial and channel dimensions.

On the publicly available dataset NEU-DET, the Precision of 69.37, the Recall of 74.21, the mean average precision at IoU threshold 0.5 (, i.e., the average precision when the intersection-over-union threshold is fixed at 0.5) of 76.32, and the mean average precision over IoU thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95 in steps of 0.05 (, a stricter metric that considers multiple thresholds) of 38.60 are achieved by the proposed YOLOv5s-SDF model, outperforming the original YOLOv5s model, YOLOv7 series model, and YOLOv8 series model. Compared with the original YOLOv5s model, the YOLOv5s-SDF model has improved Precision by 0.2, Recall by 1.72, by 3.57, and by 0.84, verifying the algorithm’s good performance in detecting surface defects on steel strips. This algorithm balances detection speed while improving detection accuracy.

YOLOv5s network structure

The input, backbone, neck, and head are the four elements which make up the YOLOv5s model. To cope with the incoming photos, techniques like adaptive image scaling and mosaic data augmentation are implemented. The primary components include the slicing module (Focus), Conv, C3 module, and spatial pyramid pooling module (SPP). Among them, the Focus module is a unique structure of the YOLOv5 series, which guarantees that information is not irrecoverable during downsampling when performing slicing operations. The SPP module accelerates the rapidity of generating candidate boxes and overcomes the repetitive extraction of features problem in convolutional neural networks. The primary functions of the C3 module are to streamline the network topology and diminish computing complexity. A multi-scale feature fusion structure consisting of Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) and Path Aggregation Network (PAN) is adopted in the Neck module. High-level and low-level feature information are fused by FPN through upsampling operation, while downsampling is used by PAN to convey information. Improved YOLOv5s-SDF network structure

For YOLOv5s strips of steel interface defect detection, a YOLOv5s-SDF model based on attention mechanism is suggested. Firstly, the channel dimension is divided into several sub-features for simultaneous processing using ShuffleAttention in the neck network. For each sub feature, Shuffle Units are utilized to convey category interdependence in both spatial and channel dimensions. Secondly, DyHead is used in the head network to unify diverse object detection heads via an attention mechanism. The expressiveness of the model’s object detection head can be substantially boosted without incorporating more computational complexity through the use of attention mechanisms among feature degrees for scale perception, between spatial positions for spatial awareness, and within output channels for project senses. Finally, in order to address the issue of severe fluctuations in loss values caused by low-quality samples and concentrate CIoU losses on high-quality instances, Focal loss is introduced based on the CIoU loss function. Introducing ShuffleAttention into the neck network

At the moment, spatial attention mechanisms and channel attention mechanisms are the two main categories of attention processes. Both are employed to acquire linked pixel level correlations and inter-channel connections, respectively. Superior outcomes can be accomplished by simultaneously employing two attention mechanisms, but this inevitably increases the framework’s computational complexity. An effective ShuffleAttention (SA) module was suggested by ZHANG et al. to overcome this issue16, which effectively combines two forms of attention operations using Shuffle units. Specifically, the channel dimension is split into multiple sub-features and handled synchronously. Shuffle Unit is utilized for expressing feature interdependence throughout the spatial and channel dimensions for each sub-feature. Afterwards, all sub features are amalgamated together and the “channel shuffle” operator is used to enable information exchange between various sub-features. Experiments and results analysis

Experimental dataset

In order to verify the effectiveness of the proposed model, the NEU-DET dataset for surface defects of hot-rolled steel strips published by Northeastern University18–20 is used, which includes six types of defects: Rolled-in scale, Patches, Crazing, Pitted_surface, Inclusion and Scratches. Each type of defect has 300 images of 200 pixel * 200 pixel, for a total of 1800 images.

In this experiment, the dataset was randomly split into a training set and a testing set at an 8:2 ratio, comprising 1,440 training images and 360 testing images. To enhance the diversity and robustness of the training data, data augmentation techniques such as Mosaic and MixUp were employed. Parameter sensitivity experiment

In order to verify the rationality of the introduced module and the key hyperparameters in the improved loss function, this paper conducted comparative experiments on the group counting parameter (G) of ShuffleAttention module and the in FocalCIoU. During the experiment, except for the target parameters, the rest of the network structure was kept consistent with the training settings to ensure comparability of the results.

Firstly, set different group counting parameters in the ShuffleAttention module for experimentation. The results indicate that as the population count increases, overly fine channel partitioning leads to insufficient information exchange and a decrease in model detection accuracy; On the contrary, a smaller number of groups can ensure sufficient interaction between channels while maintaining computational efficiency. When the population counting parameter is set to a minimum value of G=2, the model performance reaches its optimum, taking into account both accuracy and inference speed indicators.

Secondly, a comparative experiment was conducted on in the FocalCIoU loss. The experimental results show that larger values weaken the gradient contribution of ordinary samples, leading to unstable convergence of the model; A smaller provides moderate attention to difficult samples, which is beneficial for overall convergence and accuracy improvement. Based on the comprehensive experimental results, the model performs best when the minimum value of is set to 0.5.

In summary, this article ultimately determined that the population counting parameter for the ShuffleAttention module was G=2, and for FocalCIoU was 0.5. This configuration will be used in all subsequent experiments.The parameter comparison results are shown in Tables 3 and 4.

Conclusion

The neck network, detection head, and loss function of YOLOv5s have been improved to enhance the detection accuracy of surface defects on steel strips. Firstly, Shuffle Attention is used in the neck network to group the channel dimension into multiple sub features for parallel processing. For each sub feature, Shuffle Units are utilized to depict feature dependencies in both spatial and channel dimensions. Secondly, DyHead is served in the head network to unify different object detection heads. By using attention mechanisms between feature levels for scale perception, attention mechanisms between spatial positions for spatial perception, and attention mechanisms within output channels for task perception, the expressive power of the model’s object detection head can be significantly improved without increasing computational complexity. Finally, in order to address the issue of severe fluctuations in loss values caused by low-quality samples concentrating CIoU loss on high-quality instances, Focal Loss is introduced based on the CIoU loss function. Experiments are conducted on the NEU-DET strip surface defect detection dataset, and the performance indicators of the YOLOv5s-SDF model proposed in this paper are superior to the original YOLOv5s model, SSD model, Faster-RCNN model, YOLOv7 series model, and YOLOv8 series model. The YOLOv5s-SDF model has high recognition efficiency and fast recognition speed, and can be widely applied in the field of surface defect detection of strip steel.Future work will focus on enhanced multi-scale feature fusion and texture augmentation strategies to address these limitations.

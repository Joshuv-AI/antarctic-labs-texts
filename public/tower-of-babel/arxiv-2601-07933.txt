p p -curvature and non-abelian cohomology
Yeuk Hay Joshua Lam and Daniel Litt

Abstract
. Let X → S X\to S be a smooth projective morphism. Katz proved the Grothendieck-Katz p p -curvature conjecture for the Gauss-Manin connection on the i i -th cohomology of X / S X/S : if its p p -curvature vanishes mod p p for infinitely many p p , then the action of π 1 ​ ( S , s ) \pi_{1}(S,s) on H i ​ ( X s , ℤ ) H^{i}(X_{s},\mathbb{Z}) factors through a finite group. 1.
Introduction

Let
R\subset\mathbb{C}
be a finitely-generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra and let
f:X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism of smooth
R
-schemes. There are close relationships between the topology of
f_{\mathbb{C}}^{\an}
and its arithmetic (for example, via the Weil conjectures). Typically these relationships are mediated by the various realizations of the cohomology of the fibers of
f
, e.g. singular cohomology
R^{i}f^{\an}_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\ell
-adic cohomology
R^{i}f^{\et}_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
, or algebraic de Rham cohomology
R^{i}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}
, and the compatibilities between them; here
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}
denotes the relative de Rham complex of
f
. Notable among these relationships—and the starting point for this paper—is Katz’s resolution of the
p
-curvature conjecture for Gauss-Manin connections
[
30
]
, which asserts that the monodromy of
R^{i}f^{\an}_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
is controlled by the arithmetic of
R^{i}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}
.

Our goal in this paper is to study relations between the topology and arithmetic of
f
mediated not by cohomology, but instead by
non-abelian cohomology
, i.e. the moduli of local systems on the fibers of
f
, in their various (Betti, de Rham, and Dolbeault) incarnations. As applications, we prove a precise non-abelian analogue of Katz’s theorem, as well as many new cases of a conjecture attributed to Bost and Ekedahl–Shepherd-Barron–Taylor on
p
-curvatures of foliations. Doing so requires the development of a number of structures on non-abelian cohomology, most notably the non-abelian counterparts to Katz’s formula and the Hodge index theorem.

1.1.
Main results (applications)

Katz’s theorem is as follows:

Theorem 1.1.1

(Katz
[
30
]
)
.

Let
R\subset\mathbb{C}
be a finitely-generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra, and let
f:X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism of smooth
R
-schemes. Fix an integer
i\geq 0
and denote by
(\mathscr{E},\nabla):=(R^{i}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}^{\bullet},\nabla_{GM})
the
i
-th relative de Rham cohomology of
f
, equipped with its Gauss-Manin connection. Fix a point
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
. Suppose that the
p
-curvature of
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)\bmod p
vanishes for infinitely many primes
p
. Then the monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on
H^{i}(X_{s}(\mathbb{C})^{\an},\mathbb{Z})
factors through a finite group.

Here the statement that the
p
-curvature of
(R^{i}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}^{\bullet},\nabla_{GM})\bmod p
vanishes means that if
x_{1},\cdots,x_{\dim S}
are a system of local coordinates on
S
, then

\nabla_{GM}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{p}\equiv 0\bmod p

for all
i
; here we are implicitly assuming that
p
is sufficiently large so that we can reduce
R
, and therefore
f
, modulo
p
. More generally, for any flat connection
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
on
X/S
in characteristic
p
, one can define an invariant

\psi_{p}:F^{*}_{\operatorname{abs}}T_{X/S}\rightarrow\End(\mathscr{E}),

known as the
p
-curvature; one remarkable property of
\psi_{p}
is that it is
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-linear, whereas
\nabla:T_{X/S}\rightarrow\End(\mathscr{E})
, an a priori similar looking map, is certainly not. We say that the
p
-curvature of
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
vanishes if
\psi_{p}
is the zero map. See the beginning of
§ 2.3.3
for the definition of
\psi
and further generalities on it.

We now describe our non-abelian analogue of
Theorem 1.1.1
. Given a smooth projective morphism of smooth
R
-schemes
f:X\to S
, we first discuss the analogues of the two main objects, namely the relative de Rham cohomology
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
equipped with its Gauss-Manin connection, and Betti cohomology
H^{i}(X_{s}(\mathbb{C})^{\an},\mathbb{Z})
equipped with its
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an})
-action. The non-abelian de Rham cohomology is the moduli stack
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S,r)
of rank
r
flat vector bundles on
X/S
, with trivial determinant; it is equipped with a
non-abelian Gauss-Manin connection
constructed by Simpson
[
56
, §8]
, or, equivalently, the
isomonodromy foliation
. This is a foliation on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S,r)
whose complex-analytic leaves are the families of flat bundles whose monodromy representations are locally constant (up to conjugation). To sum up, the stack
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S,r)
with its isomonodromy foliation is the non-abelian analogue of de Rham cohomology with its Gauss-Manin connection.

Now fix
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
. The analogue of Betti cohomology is then

M_{B}(X_{s},r):=\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X_{s}(\mathbb{C})^{\an}),\mathrm{SL}_{r})\sslash\mathrm{SL}_{r},

the character variety of
\pi_{1}(X_{s})
, parametrizing conjugacy classes of semisimple representations of
\pi_{1}(X_{s})
into
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
. There is a natural action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on
M_{B}(X_{s},r)
(through its action by outer automorphisms on
\pi_{1}(X_{s})
), which is the analogue of the
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
-action on usual Betti cohomology.

With these analogies in mind, our first main theorem is a precise analogue of Katz’s result
Theorem 1.1.1
:

Theorem A
.

Let
R\subset\mathbb{C}
be a finitely-generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra, and
f:X\to S
a smooth projective morphism of smooth
R
-schemes. Fix a point
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
and a positive integer
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. If the
p
-curvature of the isomonodromy foliation on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S,r)
mod
p
vanishes for infinitely many primes
p
, then the action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on
M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\overline{\mathbb{Z}})
factors through a finite group.

Here we say that an algebraic foliation
\mathscr{F}
has
vanishing
p
-curvature mod
p
if, for every vector field
\vec{v}
(in characteristic
p
) tangent to
\mathscr{F}\bmod p
, we have that
\vec{v}^{p}
is tangent to
\mathscr{F}\bmod p
as well, i.e.
\mathscr{F}
is
p
-closed. We denote by
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
the ring of algebraic integers.

Theorem 1.1.1
has the immediate consequence that the Grothendieck-Katz
p
-curvature conjecture is true for Gauss-Manin connections. We briefly recall this conjecture and its analogue for foliations, and then explain our results towards a non-abelian
p
-curvature conjecture for isomonodromy foliations.

Conjecture 1.1.2

(Grothendieck-Katz)
.

Let
R\subset\mathbb{C}
be a finitely-generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra, and
S
a smooth
R
-scheme. Let
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
be a flat vector bundle on
S/R
. There exists a finite étale cover
S^{\prime}
of
S_{\mathbb{C}}
trivializing
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
if and only if
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
mod
p
has vanishing
p
-curvature for almost all primes
p
.

Katz’s
Theorem 1.1.1
implies that, if
(R^{i}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}^{\bullet},\nabla_{GM})
has vanishing
p
-curvatures mod
p
for infinitely many
p
, then after passing to a finite cover, one may arrange that it has trivial monodromy. Then the conjecture follows for
(R^{i}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}^{\bullet},\nabla_{GM})
from the theory of regular singularities.

We now explain a version of the
p
-curvature conjecture for foliations, due to Ekedahl–Shepherd-Barron–Taylor and Bost
[
6
, p. 165]
, which applies in our non-abelian setting.

Conjecture 1.1.3

(Ekedahl–Shepherd-Barron–Taylor
[
20
, Conjecture F]
, Bost
[
6
]
)
.

Let
R\subset\mathbb{C}
be a finitely-generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra, and
M
a smooth
R
-scheme. Let
\mathscr{F}\subset T_{M/R}
be a foliation, i.e. a sub-bundle of Lie algebras. Then the complex-analytic leaves of
\mathscr{F}
are analytifications of algebraic subvarieties of
M_{\mathbb{C}}
if and only if
\mathscr{F}\bmod p
is closed under
p
-th powers mod
p
for almost all primes
p
, i.e. it has vanishing
p
-curvature.

It is a pleasant exercise to check that
1.1.3
implies
1.1.2
by taking
M
to be the total space of the vector bundle
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
, with foliation induced by
\nabla
.

Our main result is that
1.1.3
(or rather a mild stacky generalization thereof) is true for
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S)
, under hypotheses that are arguably mild.

Theorem B
.

With notation and assumptions as in
A
, suppose in addition that every irreducible component of
M_{B}(X_{s},r)
has a
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-point. Then the action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on
M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\mathbb{C})
factors through a finite group. Consequently all leaves of the isomonodromy foliation are algebraic.

Remark 1.1.4
.

While we do not know how to verify the hypothesis that each irreducible component of
M_{B}(X_{s},r)
has a
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-point in general, it is true in important special cases. For example, we verify the Ekedahl–Shepherd-Barron–Taylor conjecture for the isomonodromy foliation on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S,r)
if:

(1)

r=2
, or

(2)

M_{B}(X_{s},r)_{\mathbb{C}}
is irreducible, or

(3)

X/S
is a relative curve.

See
6.3.4
.

Note that the hypothesis that each irreducible component of
M_{B}(X_{s},r)
contains a
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-point is a purely topological one—that is, it depends only on
\pi_{1}(X_{s})
. We conjecture that it is in fact always satisfied, which is a slight extension of Simpson’s integrality conjecture; see
[
11
, Conjecture 1.1.1]
,
1.3.2
, and
6.3.5
.

1.2.
Main structural results on non-abelian cohomology

Katz’s proof of
Theorem 1.1.1
relies on various structures on the cohomology of algebraic varieties and the interrelations between them. The main purpose of this work is to develop and exploit analogous structures in the non-abelian setting. In particular, we give non-abelian versions of both Katz’s formula and the Hodge index theorem.

1.2.1.
Katz’s proof of the
p
-curvature conjecture for Gauss-Manin connections—the abelian setting

We briefly recall the various structures that go into Katz’s proof of
Theorem 1.1.1
and how they are used; we will explain the analogous results in our setting in
§ 1.2.2
. We retain notation as in
Theorem 1.1.1
.

Step 1. (Arithmetic)
Katz first shows that if the
p
-curvature of
\nabla_{GM}
mod
p
vanishes for infinitely many
p
, then, in characteristic zero,
\nabla_{GM}
preserves the Hodge filtration on
R^{i}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}^{\bullet}
. The primary input is a comparison, known as
Katz’s formula

[
30
, Theorem 3.2]
, between

(1)

the associated graded of the
p
-curvature of
\nabla_{GM}
mod
p
with respect to the
conjugate filtration
, and

(2)

the Frobenius pullback of the associated graded of
\nabla_{GM}
itself with respect to the Hodge filtration, that is, the Kodaira-Spencer map.

This comparison itself has been the subject of much development, and is notably given a beautiful explanation in
[
43
]
.

Step 2. (Geometry)
Katz next observes that if
\nabla_{GM}
preserves the Hodge filtration on
(R^{i}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}^{\bullet})_{\mathbb{C}}
then the monodromy representation

\rho:\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)\to\gl(H^{i}(X_{s},\mathbb{C}))

is unitary, i.e. it preserves a definite Hermitian form. This is a consequence of the Hodge index theorem, i.e. the fact that
(R^{i}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}^{\bullet})_{\mathbb{C}}
underlies a polarizable variation of Hodge structure.

Step 3. (Topology)
Finally, Katz makes use of the integral structure on singular cohomology, i.e. the fact that
\rho
factors through a representation into
\gl(H^{i}(X_{s},\mathbb{Z}))
. That is,
\rho
factors as

\textstyle{\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\gl(H^{i}(X_{s},\mathbb{C}))}
\textstyle{U(r)\cap\gl(H^{i}(X_{s},\mathbb{Z})/\text{tors}).\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

But this intermediate group is both discrete and compact, hence finite, completing the proof.

1.2.2.
A non-abelian analogue—Leitfaden

The main idea of our proofs of
A
and
B
is in some sense to simply copy Katz’s proof glossed above. The primary challenge is to make sense of the non-abelian analogues of the objects Katz encounters, e.g. the Hodge and conjugate filtrations, the Kodaira-Spencer map, the
p
-curvature, the Hodge index theorem, and the various compatibilities between them. We now explain these analogues and how they are used.

Step 1. (Arithmetic—a non-abelian Katz formula)

Recall that Katz compared (via
Katz’s formula

[
30
, Theorem 3.2]
) the Frobenius pullback of
\operatorname{gr}_{F_{\operatorname{Hod}}}\nabla_{GM}
with
\operatorname{gr}_{\operatorname{conj}}\psi_{p}(\nabla_{GM})
, the associated graded of the
p
-curvature
\psi_{p}(\nabla_{GM})
with respect to the conjugate filtration. To make sense of this in the non-abelian setting, we need

(1)

to find analogues of the Hodge, resp. conjugate filtrations,

(2)

to understand how to take the associated graded of the isomonodromy foliation, resp. its
p
-curvature with respect to these, and

(3)

to compare these associated gradeds up to Frobenius twist.

The analogue of a
filtration
on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S)
is well-known (see e.g.
[
59
]
); it is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant stack over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
equipped with an isomorphism between its fiber over
1
and
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S)
, analogous to the Rees construction. The fiber over
0
is referred to as the
associated graded
. The Hodge filtration has been understood (since work of Simpson and Deligne) to be analogous to the moduli stack of
\lambda
-connections
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)/\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
, recalled in
2.3.2
. In this case the associated graded—the
0
-fiber—is the moduli stack
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)
of Higgs bundles on
X/S

For a morphism
f:X\to S
in characteristic
p>0
the analogue of the conjugate filtration is a stack we denote by
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)/\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
, introduced in
2.3.4
; it is a slight variant of a stack previously studied by Menzies
[
41
]
in a closely related context. The associated graded is the moduli stack of Higgs bundles on
X^{(1)}/S
; here
X^{(1)}
denotes the pullback of
X
along absolute Frobenius on
S
.

The analogue of the Kodaira-Spencer map
\operatorname{gr}_{F_{\operatorname{Hod}}}\nabla_{GM}
is an object on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)
referred to as a
lifting of tangent vectors
, previously studied by a number of authors, e.g.
[
9
,
12
,
53
,
54
,
23
]
, which one may loosely speaking understand as the obstruction to extending the isomonodromy foliation on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S)
over the central fiber
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)
of
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)
, just as the Kodaira-Spencer map is the obstruction to
\nabla_{GM}
preserving the Hodge filtration. This is explained in
4.2.4
. We denote this lifting of tangent vectors by
\Theta_{X/S}
.

Just as the conjugate filtration on de Rham cohomology is preserved by the Gauss-Manin connection, the isomonodromy foliation extends over all of
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)/\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
. Its
p
-curvature
\Psi_{\operatorname{conj}}
vanishes at the zero fiber, analogous to the fact that the
p
-curvature of the Gauss-Manin connection vanishes on the associated graded of the conjugate filtration. Our version of Katz’s formula is:

Theorem C

(Non-abelian Katz formula)
.

There is an identification between
\frac{1}{\lambda}\Psi_{\operatorname{conj}}|_{\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)_{0}}
and the Frobenius pullback of
\Theta_{X/S}
, where
\lambda
is the coordinate on
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
.

See
§ 3.6
and in particular
Theorem 3.6.4
for a precise statement. We think it is plausible that this formula is implicit in some modern developments in
p
-adic Hodge theory and its characteristic
p
avatars, though some work would be required to extract it.

Remark 1.2.3
.

In Katz’s proof, one deduces immediately from Katz’s formula that the vanishing of infinitely many
p
-curvatures implies the vanishing of the Kodaira–Spencer map, i.e. the Higgs field. This is a consequence of Hodge-de Rham degeneration, and subsequently the degeneration of the conjugate spectral sequence.

In our setup, we would similarly like to conclude that
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes in characteristic zero, given that
\Psi_{\operatorname{conj}}
vanishes mod
p
for infinitely many
p
. However, things are rather more complicated in our non-abelian situation: we do not know what the non-abelian analogue of the degeneration of the conjugate spectral sequence is, and more generally the geometry of
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)
is poorly understood. We get around this and prove the statement, using Ogus-Vologodsky’s non-abelian Hodge theory in positive characteristic
[
43
]
to construct canonical sections to
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)/\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
, which we use to prove a kind of isosingularity principle for
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)
, in
§ 4.1
. This argument has some precursor in Menzies’ thesis
[
41
]
, who proved a related result under the additional assumption that
X/S
admits a Frobenius lift.

Ultimately we conclude in
Theorem 4.2.3
that under the hypotheses of
A
, the lifting of tangent vectors
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes identically: loosely speaking, the isomonodromy foliation preserves the Hodge filtration.

Step 2. (Geometry—a non-abelian Hodge index theorem)

Having proven that
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes, we wish to show that the action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on
M_{B}(X_{s},r)
is
unitary
; in the abelian setting this was a consequence of the Hodge index theorem. In
§ 5
, we prove
two
analogues of the Hodge index theorem.

The first says:

Theorem D

(Non-abelian Hodge index theorem I)
.

If
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S,r)
, then the orbits of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on
M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\mathbb{C})
have compact closure (in the analytic topology).

See
Theorem 5.3.2
for a precise statement. One should think of this as analogous to the unitarity of a variation of Hodge structure with vanishing Higgs field: if

\rho:\Gamma\to GL_{r}(\mathbb{C})

is unitary, then the
\Gamma
-orbit of any vector in
\mathbb{C}^{r}
has compact closure.

The second analogue of the Hodge index theorem is:

Theorem E

(Non-abelian Hodge index theorem II)
.

Under the same assumptions as
D
, the action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on the reduced smooth locus of
M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\mathbb{C})
preserves a natural Riemannian metric.

See
Theorem 5.4.5
and
Theorem 5.5.1
for more precise statements. In particular, at a fixed point of the
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
-action on
M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\mathbb{C})
, the action on the tangent space is in fact unitary.

Both of these results rely on Simpson’s complex non-abelian Hodge theory and ultimately have the same source,
5.3.1
: if
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes along a leaf of the isomonodromy foliation, then the
energy functional
is constant along that leaf. This result appears closely related to
[
12
, Theorem E]
, which appeared while this paper was in preparation.

Step 3. (Topology)
We now observe that for a
\mathbb{Z}
-point of
M_{B}(X_{s},r)
, its orbit under
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
is discrete (as the
\mathbb{Z}
-points of an affine algebraic variety are discrete in its complex points) and compact, by

. The case of
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}

The proof of

\mathbb{Q}
(i.e. semisimple, finite order, local monodromy). We have opted not to try to write the paper in this generality both for simplicity of notation and because the analytic preliminaries in

On the other hand, one pleasant aspect of this situation is that the hypotheses of

1.3.

1.4.
Related works

This work takes inspiration from a number of authors. Most relevant are the theses of Papaioannou
[
46
]
and Menzies
[
41
]
, in which they studied precisely the Bost/Ekedahl–Shepherd-Barron–Taylor conjecture for the isomonodromy foliation. The work of Shankar

studied the

-curvature conjecture when one degenerates a curve to a nodal curve. These were all PhD theses written under Mark Kisin’s supervision. As far as we understand, those works are part of a broader strategy of Kisin’s to probe the Grothendieck–Katz
p
-curvature conjecture via isomonodromy: that is, the idea is to study isomonodromic deformations of the flat bundle as one varies the complex structure of the base, under the assumptions of vanishing
p
-curvatures. The paper
[
47
]
also studies the
p
-curvature conjecture in the context of isomonodromy.
The above-mentioned works, as well as
[
30
]
of course, were the starting point for this paper.

More broadly, this work is part of a large literature on the
p
-curvature conjecture. Aside from Katz’s work, significant work on the conjecture was done by the Chudnovsky brothers
[
10
]
, André
[
1
]
, and Bost
[
6
]
. Bost’s work
[
6
]
(as well as
[
20
]
) in particular pioneered the study of the
p
-curvature conjecture in the context of foliations.

The philosophy that one should view moduli spaces such as
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{B}
as non-abelian analogues of cohomology was pioneered by Simpson and Deligne, especially through Simpson’s construction
[
58
]
of the
de Rham stack
. The latter has been a great source of inspiration in recent studies of cohomology theories of algebraic varieties, notably in the works of Bhatt, Drinfeld, Lurie, Scholze, and that of many others
[
19
,
3
,
2
,
51
]
; this approach has been coined
transmutation
by Bhatt. As explained in

, we expect that part of
C
is implicit in such works.

The complex-analytic aspects of this paper are related to a number of recent works, notably
[
9
,
12
,
53
,
23
,
29
]
, as explained in
§ 1.2.2
.

This paper is also in some sense a sequel to
[
33
]
. There, we formulated a conjectural arithmetic characterization of algebraic leaves for arbitrary foliations, or equivalently algebraic solutions to (possible non-linear) differential equations; this conjecture would yield a proof of the
p
-curvature conjecture via isomonodromy, as envisioned by Kisin.

The main result of
[

1.5.
Outline

We give a brief summary of each of the sections. We begin in
§ 2
by defining precisely the stacks which will be the main players of this work, namely the de Rham, Dolbeault, Hodge, and conjugate moduli stacks
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}},\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}},\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}},\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}
; the latter two are viewed as filtrations on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}
. We also collect various properties of these stacks, such as Hitchin maps and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-actions. In
§ 3
we recall the construction of the non-abelian Gauss-Manin connection on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}
, and extend it to
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}
. We introduce the lifting of tangent vectors
\Theta
on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}
, which is the non-abelian analogue of the Higgs field. We then study the
p
-curvatures of the non-abelian connections on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}
, and prove the non-abelian Katz formula
Theorem 3.6.4
, the main result of this section.

In
§ 4
, we construct
canonical sections
on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}
passing through nilpotent Higgs bundles (of small order of nilpotence); this should be viewed as the
\bmod p
analogue of canonical sections on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}
constructed by Simpson using non-abelian Hodge theory. Using these, we deduce that the vanishing of the non-abelian
p
-curvature for infinitely many

implies the vanishing of the lifting of tangent vectors. In
§ 5
we prove our versions of the Hodge index formula by studying the energy functional on the moduli space of Higgs bundles; one consequence is that, assuming the vanishing of
\Theta
, we prove that the

-action on the moduli of Higgs bundles is

, i.e. the action is holomorphic in all three natural complex structures of this hyperkähler manifold. This section is of a purely complex analytic nature. Finally in

2.
The non-abelian Hodge and conjugate filtrations

In this section we introduce the non-abelian analogues of algebraic de Rham and Hodge cohomologies, the Hodge and conjugate filtrations on the former, as well as some related structures; this point of view was pioneered by Simpson. Throughout we let
S
be a scheme and
f:X\to S
a smooth projective morphism.

2.1.
Flat bundles and the de Rham moduli stack

The non-abelian analogue of de Rham cohomology is the
de Rham moduli stack
of
X/S
, defined as follows.

Definition 2.1.1
.

The
de Rham moduli stack

\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S)
is the
S
-stack whose
T
-points are the groupoid of vector bundles with flat connection and trivialized determinant on
X_{T}/T
, i.e. triples
(\mathscr{E},\nabla,\xi)
with
\mathscr{E}
a vector bundle on
X_{T}
,

(2.1.1.1)

\nabla:\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X_{T}/T}

a flat connection,
and
\xi:\det(\mathscr{E},\nabla)\overset{\sim}{\to}(\mathscr{O}_{X_{T}},d)
an isomorphism where the latter is the trivial rank one flat connection. Recall that, on the vector bundle
\mathscr{E}_{T}
, a flat connection relative to
T
is a
\mathscr{O}_{T}
-linear map
\nabla
as in (
2.1.1.1
) such that

(1)

\nabla(fs)=f\nabla(s)+s\otimes df
for
f
a local section of
\mathscr{O}_{X_{T}}
and
s
a local section of
\mathscr{E}
, and

(2)

\nabla\circ\nabla:\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}\otimes\Omega^{2}_{X_{T}/T}
vanishes.

2.2.
The Dolbeault moduli stack

We now move onto the non-abelian analogue of Hodge cohomology.

Definition 2.2.1
.

The
Dolbeault moduli stack

\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)
is the
S
-stack whose
T
-points are the groupoid of Higgs bundles with trivialized determinant on
X_{T}/T
, i.e. triples
(\mathscr{E},\theta,\xi)
with
\mathscr{E}
a vector bundle on
X_{T}
,

\theta:\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X_{T}/T}

an
\mathscr{O}_{X_{T}}
-linear map with

\theta\circ\theta:\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}\otimes\Omega^{2}_{X_{T}/T}

identically zero, and
\xi:\det(\mathscr{E},\theta)\overset{\sim}{\to}(\mathscr{O}_{X_{T}},0)
. We refer to
\theta
as the
Higgs field
on
\mathscr{E}
. There is a natural
\mathbb{G}_{m,S}
-action on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)
, obtained by scaling
\theta
.

2.2.2.
The Hitchin morphism

Let
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S,r)\subset\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)
be the substack of rank
r
Higgs bundles. The Hitchin morphism is the map

\displaystyle h:\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S,r)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=2}^{r}f_{*}\Sym^{i}\Omega^{1}_{X/S}

\displaystyle[(\mathscr{E},\theta)]

\displaystyle\mapsto(h_{2},\cdots,h_{r}).

where the
h_{i}
’s are the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial
\det(t-\theta)
; that is,

\det(t-\theta)=t^{r}+h_{2}t^{r-2}+\cdots+h_{r}.

2.3.
Filtrations on non-abelian cohomology

Recall that there is a natural equivalence of categories between vector spaces with a finite, exhaustive filtration over a field
k
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant vector bundles on
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
. Here if
(V,F^{\bullet})
is a filtered vector space and
\mathscr{E}_{(V,F^{\bullet})}
the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant vector bundle on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
associated to it via the Rees construction, there are natural identifications

\mathscr{E}_{(V,F^{\bullet})}|_{1}\overset{\sim}{\to}V,\ \mathscr{E}_{(V,F^{\bullet})}|_{0}\overset{\sim}{\to}\operatorname{gr}_{F^{\bullet}}V.

(See e.g.
[
59
, §5]
, as well as
[
3
, Prop. 2.2.6]
for a more general statement on the filtered derived category.) Thus, given a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-scheme or stack
\mathscr{M}
and an identification
\mathscr{M}_{1}\overset{\sim}{\to}X,
we will refer to
\mathscr{M}
as a
filtration
on
X
and
\mathscr{M}_{0}
as the
associated graded of the filtration
.

2.3.1.
The Hodge moduli stack

We first discuss the analogue of the Hodge filtration on de Rham cohomology.

Definition 2.3.2
.

The
Hodge moduli stack

\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)
is the
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
-stack whose
T
-points (over a map
\lambda:T\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
) are pairs
(\mathscr{E},\gamma)
with trivialized determinant,
where
\mathscr{E}
is a vector bundle on
X_{T}
and

\gamma:\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X_{T}/T}

is a flat
\lambda
-connection on
\mathscr{E}
over
S
, i.e. for local sections
s
of
\mathscr{E}
and
f
of
\mathscr{O}_{X_{T}}
,
we have

(2.3.2.1)

\gamma(fs)=f\gamma(s)+\lambda s\otimes df,

and that

\gamma\circ\gamma:\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}\otimes\Omega^{2}_{X_{T}/T}

is identically zero. Here we extend
\gamma
to a sequence of maps

\gamma:\mathscr{E}\otimes\Omega^{p}_{X_{T}/T}\to\mathscr{E}\otimes\Omega^{p+1}_{X_{T}/T}

as in (
2.3.2.1
); that is, we set

(2.3.2.2)

\gamma(s\otimes\omega)=\gamma(s)\wedge\omega+\lambda s\otimes d\omega.

By scaling the
\lambda
-connections, we obtain a natural
\mathbb{G}_{m,S}
-action on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)
: precisely,
\mu\in\mathbb{G}_{m,S}(T)
acts on a
\lambda
-connection
(\mathscr{E},\gamma)/(X_{T}/T)
by

\mu\cdot[(\mathscr{E},\gamma)]=[(\mathscr{E},\mu\gamma)].

This gives a
\mathbb{G}_{m,S}
-action on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)
for which the structure map

\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}

is equivariant, where the latter is equipped with the obvious
\mathbb{G}_{m,S}
-action; this action evidently extends the natural action of
\mathbb{G}_{m,S}
on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)
.

Note that there are canonical isomorphisms

\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)_{1}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S)

\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)_{0}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S),

as a
1
-connection is just a flat connection, and a
0
-connection is a Higgs field.

That is, in the language of
§ 2.3
,
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)
is a filtration on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S)
, with associated graded
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)
.

2.3.3.
The conjugate moduli stack

We come to the analogue of the conjugate filtration, which is specific to characteristic
p
(although see
[
44
, §2]
,
[
5
]
for some fascinating investigations on the conjugate filtration in characteristic zero). Recall that for a smooth projective variety
Y
over a field
k
of positive characteristic,
H^{i}_{\operatorname{dR}}(Y)
carries a conjugate filtration
F^{*}_{\operatorname{conj}}
, whose associated graded is
\bigoplus_{p+q=i}H^{q}(Y^{(1)},\Omega^{p}_{Y^{(1)}})
under mild assumptions; this filtration was introduced by Katz in
[
30
, §2.3]
.

We now describe the non-abelian analogue, and assume
S
is in characteristic
p>0
. In this case, there is a natural invariant of a flat connection
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
on
X/S
: its
p
-curvature, which is an
\mathscr{O}
-linear map

\psi_{p}(\mathscr{E},\nabla):F_{\operatorname{abs}}^{*}T_{X/S}\to\operatorname{End}(\mathscr{E}),

defined by

\partial\mapsto\nabla(\partial)^{p}-\nabla(\partial^{p})

for a vector field
\partial
. By adjunction, we may view
\psi_{p}=\psi_{p}(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
as an
\mathscr{O}
-linear map

\psi_{p}(\mathscr{E},\nabla):\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}\otimes F_{\operatorname{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/S}.

A local computation, performed for example in
[
45
, Proof of Theorem 1.2.1]
, shows that the composition

\mathscr{E}\overset{\psi_{p}}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{E}\otimes F_{\operatorname{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/S}\overset{\psi_{p}}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{E}\otimes F_{\operatorname{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{2}_{X/S}

vanishes. Moreover, by the same local computation
\psi_{p}
is horizontal, i.e. it respects the natural connections on
\mathscr{E}
,
\mathscr{E}\otimes F_{\operatorname{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/S}
(where
F_{\operatorname{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/S}
is given its canonical Frobenius-pullback connection).

We exploit this extra structure to make the following definition:

Definition 2.3.4
.

The
conjugate moduli stack

\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)
is the stack over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
whose
T
-points (over a map
\lambda:T\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
) consist of the groupoid of triples

(\mathscr{E},\nabla,\theta,\xi)

where
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
is a flat bundle on
X_{T}/T
,

(2.3.4.1)

\theta:\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}\otimes F_{\operatorname{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X_{T}/T}

is a horizontal map with trace zero and with

(2.3.4.2)

\theta\circ\theta:\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}\otimes F_{\operatorname{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{2}_{X_{T}/T}

identically zero, and

\psi_{p}(\mathscr{E},\nabla)=\lambda\cdot\theta,

and

\xi:\det(\mathscr{E},\nabla,\theta)\overset{\sim}{\to}(\mathscr{O}_{X_{T}},d,0)

is an isomorphism. Here the requirement that
\theta
be horizontal means that it respects the connections on
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}\otimes F_{\operatorname{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X_{T}/T}
(where
F_{\operatorname{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X_{T}/T}
is equipped with its canonical Frobenius-pullback connection).

Remark 2.3.5
.

As far as we are aware, this definition first appeared in
[
41
, Definition 7.12]
, although the horizontality of
\theta
, which is a crucial part of the definition for us, was missing there.

Remark 2.3.6
.

One can show that
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}
is an Artin stack, as well as construct a good moduli space for it, by considering an auxiliary

[

We equip
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)
with the

\mu\in\mathbb{G}_{m,S}(T)
acts on a point

by

\mu\cdot[(\mathscr{E},\nabla,\theta)]=[(\mathscr{E},\nabla,\mu^{-1}\theta)].

It is straightforward to check that the structure map
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
is equivariant with respect to this
\mathbb{G}_{m,S}

\mathbb{G}_{m,S}

\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}

Remark 2.3.7

We will refer to a map

) satisfying (

Note that

is, in the terminology of

Set

of the natural map

— that is, the associated graded of this filtration. Note that there is a natural equivalence

(2.3.7.1)

F_{X/S}^{*}:\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X^{(1)}/S)\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)_{0},

where

denotes the Frobenius twist of

Remark 2.3.8
.

The isomorphism (

) above is the non-abelian analogue of the
F
-zip structure
on de Rham cohomology, in the sense of

R^{i}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}
is locally free for each

is equipped with the structure of an

(the Hodge filtration), an increasing filtration

(the conjugate filtration) and isomorphisms

\operatorname{gr}_{j}^{C_{\bullet}}R^{i}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}\overset{\sim}{\to}(\operatorname{gr}^{j}_{F^{\bullet}}R^{i}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S})^{(1)}.

That is, the associated graded of the conjugate filtration on the de Rham cohomology of

is identified (up to Frobenius twist) with the associated graded of the Hodge filtration. Identifying

\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X^{(1)}/S)\simeq\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)^{(1)}\simeq\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)_{0}^{(1)}

(where the first isomorphism arises via pullback along the natural map

\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)^{(1)}_{0}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{conj}}(X/S)_{0},

i.e. the associated graded of the conjugate filtration on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S)
is identified, up to Frobenius twist, with the associated graded of the Hodge filtration, in the language of

.

We will see later, in

, that there is additional structure here—a non-abelian version of Katz’s formula intertwining the Kodaira-Spencer map (the associated graded of the Gauss-Manin connection with respect to the Hodge filtration) with the associated graded of the

-curvature of the Gauss-Manin connection with respect to the conjugate filtration.

Remark 2.3.9

3.

3.2.
Recollections on isomonodromy

3.2.1.
Isomonodromy in the analytic category

Let
\pi:X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism of complex-analytic spaces, with connected fibers, and with
S
smooth and connected. Fix
s\in S
. Let

M_{B}(X_{s},r)=\text{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X_{s}),SL_{r})\sslash SL_{r}

be the Betti moduli space of
X_{s}
. Its complex points are in bijection with conjugacy classes of semisimple representations of
\pi_{1}(X_{s})
into
SL_{r}(\mathbb{C})
.

As
\pi
is a fiber bundle (by Ehresmann’s theorem), there is a natural outer action of
\pi_{1}(S,s)
on
\pi_{1}(X_{s})
, and hence
\pi_{1}(S,s)
acts on
M_{B}(X_{s},r)
. Setting
\widetilde{S}
to be the universal cover of
S
, we write

M_{B}(X/S,r):=(M_{B}(X_{s},r)_{\mathbb{C}}^{\an}\times\widetilde{S})/\pi_{1}(S,s).

Projection onto the second factor gives a natural map

p_{B}:M_{B}(X/S,r)\to S,

whose fibers are each isomorphic to
M_{B}(X_{s},r)_{\mathbb{C}}^{\an}
. As
M_{B}(X/S,r)
is, analytically-locally on
S
, a product, the smooth locus of
p_{B}
is equipped with a foliation horizontal over
S
. And more generally,
M_{B}(X/S,r)
is a crystal in complex-analytic spaces over
S
, see
[
56
, §8]
.

\mathscr{F}_{\beta}\;:=\;\tilde{f}_{\beta}^{*}(\mathscr{V},\nabla)\;=\;(\mathscr{V},\nabla)\otimes_{\widehat{U}_{T,\beta,1}}\widehat{U}_{T,\beta,2},

which is a flat bundle on
\widehat{U}_{T,\beta,2}
. Then
\epsilon(\mathscr{V},\nabla)
is
obtained by gluing the
\mathscr{F}_{\beta}
along the isomorphisms

\alpha_{\beta\gamma}:\mathscr{F}_{\beta}|_{\widehat{U}_{T,\beta\gamma,2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{F}_{\gamma}|_{\widehat{U}_{T,\beta\gamma,2}}

defined by the Taylor formula (
3.2.4.1
).

Now assume
(\mathscr{V},\nabla)=(F^{*}_{X_{T,1}/T}\mathscr{V}^{\prime},\nabla_{\text{can}})
.
By definition of the canonical connection, local sections of the form

F^{*}_{X_{T,1}/T}(s^{\prime})
with
s^{\prime}
a local section of
\mathscr{V}^{\prime}
are horizontal:

\nabla_{\text{can}}\big(F^{*}_{X_{T,1}/T}(s^{\prime})\big)=0.

Consequently, for every local vector field
\partial
on
X_{T,1}/T
and every
integer
\ell>0
we have

\nabla_{\partial}^{\ell}\big(F^{*}_{X_{T,1}/T}(s^{\prime})\big)=0.

Let
s^{\prime}
be a local section of
\mathscr{V}^{\prime}
on

\widehat{U}^{(1)}_{T,\beta\gamma,1}
, and set

s:=F^{*}_{X_{T,1}/T}(s^{\prime})
, viewed (after pullback to
\widehat{U}_{T,\beta\gamma,2}
)
as a section of
\mathscr{F}_{\beta}|_{\widehat{U}_{T,\beta\gamma,2}}
.
Plugging
s
into (
3.2.4.1
), all terms with some

\ell_{m}>0
vanish, so only the multi-index
\underline{\ell}=\underline{0}

contributes. Hence

\alpha_{\beta\gamma}(s\otimes 1)=s\otimes 1.

Since
\mathscr{V}=F^{*}_{X_{T,1}/T}\mathscr{V}^{\prime}
is (locally) generated over the
structure sheaf by such Frobenius-pullback sections, the preceding identity
implies that, under the natural identifications

\mathscr{F}_{\beta}|_{\widehat{U}_{T,\beta\gamma,2}}\;\simeq\;\mathscr{V}^{\prime}|_{\widehat{U}^{(1)}_{T,\beta\gamma,1}}\otimes_{\widehat{U}^{(1)}_{T,\beta\gamma,1}}\widehat{U}_{T,\beta\gamma,2}\qquad\text{and similarly for }\gamma,

the map
\alpha_{\beta\gamma}
acts as the identity. In other words, the descent datum
\{\mathscr{F}_{\beta},\alpha_{\beta\gamma}\}
is
exactly the descent datum
(F^{*}\mathscr{V}^{\prime}|_{\widehat{U}_{T,\beta\gamma,2}},\id)
, and therefore we have an identification of the bundle underlying
\epsilon(\mathscr{V},\nabla)
with
F^{*}_{X_{T,2}/T}\mathscr{V}^{\prime}
. Here we have made use of the canonical identifications
X_{T,1}^{(1)}\simeq X_{T,2}^{(1)}
,
\widehat{U}^{(1)}_{T,\beta\gamma,1}\simeq\widehat{U}^{(1)}_{T,\beta\gamma,2}
.

Finally, the glued connection is the
canonical one (it is characterized by horizontality of the Frobenius-pullback
sections, which we have just seen are preserved by the gluing). Therefore

\epsilon(\mathscr{V},\nabla)\;=\;(F^{*}_{X_{T,2}/T}\mathscr{V}^{\prime},\nabla_{\text{can}}),

as claimed.

∎

3.3.
Liftings of tangent vectors

We now explain how to take the
“associated graded of a foliation on a filtered space”
in the language of
§ 2.3
. The construction below is analogous to taking the associated graded of a connection on a vector bundle with respect to a Griffiths-transverse filtration.

Definition 3.3.1
.

Let
\pi:\mathscr{M}\to S
be a morphism and

d\pi:T_{\mathscr{M}}\to\pi^{*}T_{S}

its derivative. Let
\lambda
be a regular function on
\mathscr{M}
. A
\lambda
-lifting of tangent vectors
is a map

p:\pi^{*}T_{S}\to T_{\mathscr{M}}

satisfying

d\pi\circ p=\lambda\cdot\operatorname{id}_{\pi^{*}T_{S}}.

Note that if
\lambda=0
such a map factors through
\ker d\pi
; in particular if
S
is smooth a
0
-lifting of tangent vectors amounts to a map

p:\pi^{*}T_{S}\to T_{\mathscr{M}/S}.

We will say a
\lambda
-lifting of tangent vectors is
integrable
if
p
is a morphism of sheaves of Lie algebras.

Example 3.3.2
.

Let
P\to X
be a principal
G
-bundle and
p:\pi^{*}T_{X}\to T_{P}
a
G
-equivariant
\lambda
-lifting of tangent vectors. This is the same as a
\lambda
-connection on
P
; if it is integrable, it is the same data as a flat
\lambda
-connection. In the case when
G=GL_{n}
and
P
is the frame bundle of a vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
,
T_{P}
descends to the vector bundle
\operatorname{At}(\mathscr{E})
on
X
, and this notion coincides with the correspondence between maps
q^{\gamma}:T_{X}\to\operatorname{At}(\mathscr{E})
and
\lambda
-connections discussed in
3.1.4
.

Note that an integrable
1
-lifting of tangent vectors is precisely a horizontal integrable foliation, i.e. a splitting of the tangent exact sequence

0\to T_{\mathscr{M}/S}\to T_{\mathscr{M}}\to\pi^{*}T_{S}\to 0

respecting the Lie algebra structure.

Now suppose we are given a filtration
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
on
\mathscr{M}
in the sense of
§ 2.3
, i.e. a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant map
\lambda:\mathscr{M}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
and an identification of
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{1}
with
\mathscr{M}
. If
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}/S
is also equipped with a
\lambda
-lifting of tangent vectors (and hence
\mathscr{M}/S
is equipped with a horizontal foliation
\mathscr{F}
), we will refer to the
0
-lifting of tangent vectors on
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{0}
as the
associated graded
of
\mathscr{F}
with respect to the filtration
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.3.3
.

Suppose we are given a smooth projective morphism of smooth schemes
f:X\to S
.

There is a natural
\lambda
-lifting of tangent vectors
\Xi_{X/S}
on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}(X/S)\to S
. Namely, given a
S^{\prime}
-point of
S
and a
\lambda
-connection
(\mathscr{E},\gamma)
on
X_{S^{\prime}}/{S^{\prime}}
with trivialized determinant, one obtains as in
3.1.4
a map
q^{\gamma}:T_{X_{S^{\prime}}/S^{\prime}}\to\operatorname{At}^{0}_{X_{S^{\prime}}/S^{\prime}}(\mathscr{E})
. The composition

T_{S^{\prime}}\to R^{1}f^{\prime}_{*}T_{X_{S^{\prime}}/S^{\prime}}{\to}R^{1}f^{\prime}_{*}\operatorname{At}^{0}_{X_{S^{\prime}}/S^{\prime}}(\mathscr{E})^{\bullet}_{\lambda}

is a
\lambda
-lifting of tangent vectors, under the identifications of
3.1.7
. Here the first arrow is the Kodaira-Spencer map classifying first-order deformations, and the second is induced by the composition

T_{X_{S^{\prime}}/S^{\prime}}\overset{q^{\gamma}}{\to}\operatorname{At}^{0}_{X_{S^{\prime}}/S^{\prime}}(\mathscr{E})\to\operatorname{At}^{0}_{X_{S^{\prime}}/S^{\prime}}(\mathscr{E})^{\bullet}_{\lambda}.

Note that the second arrow above is not a map of complexes, though the composition is.

Restricting to
0\in\mathbb{A}^{1}_{S}
, we obtain a
0
-lifting of tangent vectors
\Theta_{X/S}:\pi_{\operatorname{Dol}}^{*}T_{S}\to T_{\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)}
, which we refer to as the associated graded of the isomonodromy foliation. Indeed, by
[
31
, Lemma 4.5]
, the corresponding
1
-lifting of tangent vectors is precisely the isomonodromy foliation. The
0
-lifting of tangent vectors
\Theta_{X/S}
is to the isomonodromy foliation as the Kodaira-Spencer map is to the Gauss-Manin connection.

3.4.
Chen’s formula

Let
\Sigma/\mathbb{C}
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
. Write
M_{\operatorname{Dol}}(\Sigma,r)
for the coarse moduli space of semistable Higgs bundles on
\Sigma
, of rank
r
and degree zero (with trivialized determinant), and
M^{s}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(\Sigma,r)\subset M_{\operatorname{Dol}}(\Sigma,r)
the open set parametrizing stable Higgs bundles. Let
\bun(\Sigma,r)
denote the coarse moduli space of stable rank
r
bundles on
\Sigma
. Note that the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\bun(\Sigma,r)
has a natural holomorphic symplectic structure, and that there is a natural open immersion
T^{*}\bun(\Sigma,r)\hookrightarrow M^{s}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(\Sigma,r).

Proposition 3.4.1
.

There is a natural holomorphic symplectic form on
M^{s}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(\Sigma,r)
, extending the one on
T^{*}\bun(\Sigma,r)
.

Proof.

See for example
[
4
, Prop. 3.1, Prop. 3.3, Thm. 4.1]
. Similar statements have also appeared in
[
7
,
39
]
.

The symplectic form is derived from Grothendieck-Serre duality and the trace pairing on
\ad(\mathscr{E})
, and we now give an explicit (pointwise) formula for it for completeness. At
[(\mathscr{E},\theta)]\in M_{\operatorname{Dol}}^{s}(\Sigma,r)
, let
C^{\bullet}
denote the complex

[\ad(\mathscr{E})\rightarrow\ad(\mathscr{E})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma}],

with
\ad(\mathscr{E})
placed in degree 0. Identifying the tangent space at
[(\mathscr{E},\theta)]
with
\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet})
, the holomorphic symplectic form is given by

\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet})\otimes\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet})\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}(C^{\bullet}\otimes C^{\bullet})\rightarrow H^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma})\rightarrow\mathbb{C};

here, the second map is induced by the map of complexes

C^{\bullet}\otimes C^{\bullet}\rightarrow[0\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma}\rightarrow 0]

where the non-trivial map is

\displaystyle\ad(\mathscr{E})\otimes(\ad(\mathscr{E})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma})\oplus(\ad(\mathscr{E})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma})\otimes\ad(\mathscr{E})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\oplus(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})\mapsto\tr(a_{1}\circ b_{1}+a_{2}\circ b_{2}).

Here,
a_{1},b_{2}
are sections of
\ad(\mathscr{E})
, and
a_{2},b_{1}
are sections of
\ad(\mathscr{E})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma}
.

Note that this construction in fact yields a symplectic form on the tangent sheaf
T_{\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)}
of the moduli stack of Higgs bundles (and even a
0
-shifted symplectic structure).
∎

We continue to denote by
h
the Hitchin morphism

h:\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(\Sigma,r)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=2}^{r}H^{0}(\Sigma,\Sym^{i}\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma}),

as defined in
§ 2.2.2
, and
h_{2}:\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(\Sigma,r)\rightarrow H^{0}(\Sigma,\Sym^{2}\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma})
the projection onto the space of quadratic differentials; we refer to
h_{2}
as the quadratic Hitchin map.

\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(\Sigma,r)
is the fiber over
[\Sigma]
of the map
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(\mathscr{C}_{g}/\mathscr{M}_{g},r)\to\mathscr{M}_{g}
, where
\mathscr{M}_{g}
is the moduli space of curves of genus
g
and
\mathscr{C}_{g}
is the universal curve. We now explain Chen’s computation of the map
\Theta_{\mathscr{C}_{g}/\mathscr{M}_{g}}
in terms of the data above.

Theorem 3.4.2

(Chen’s formula)
.

Fix
v\in H^{1}(\Sigma,T_{\Sigma})=T_{\mathscr{M}_{g},[\Sigma]}
, and let
v^{\vee}:H^{0}(\Sigma,\Sym^{2}\Omega^{1}_{\Sigma})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the linear map obtained by dualizing and applying Serre duality.

Then

(3.4.2.1)

\Theta_{\mathscr{C}_{g}/\mathscr{M}_{g}}(v)=\frac{1}{2}H_{h_{2}^{*}v^{\vee}},

where the right hand side denotes the Hamiltonian vector field of the function
h_{2}^{*}v^{\vee}
, with respect to the holomorphic symplectic structure given by
3.4.1
.

Proof.

Chen
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
proves this for stable Higgs bundles (as she phrases her results in terms of the coarse moduli space) but in fact the proof is purely Čech-cohomological and works at the level of stacks.
∎

We will not use the following, but we feel that it has some significance:

Proposition 3.4.3
.

The lifting of tangent vectors
\Theta_{X/S}
is tangent to the fibers of the Hitchin map.

Proof.

This is immediate from the definition (computing with e.g. a Čech hypercover). One may see it geometrically in the case of a family of curves as follows, from Chen’s formula:
\Theta_{\mathscr{C}_{g}/\mathscr{M}_{g}}
is the Hamiltonian vector field associated to a function pulled back from the base of the Lagrangian fibration
h
.
∎

3.5.

-curvature of the isomonodromy foliation

Suppose

,

a smooth scheme, and

.

In this paper, we are primarily interested in

and generalizations thereof, namely crystals of stacks. We first describe the former.

.

Suppose
\pi:M\rightarrow S
is a smooth map of smooth schemes over

, and that

is a foliation. We say that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{M/A}}

Since we deal with stacks such as

Let

.

The

5.
Relative compactness of leaves—a non-abelian Hodge index theorem

In this section we will recall some complex non-abelian Hodge theory and its interaction with isomonodromy. We will also prove our non-abelian analogues of the Hodge index theorem,

5.1.
Background on complex non-abelian Hodge theory

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.

Definition 5.1.1
.

(1)

We write
M_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X,r)
for the coarse moduli space of semistable (with respect to the Kähler class
\omega
), rank
r
, Higgs bundles on

, with vanishing rational Chern classes and trivial determinant. This has the structure of a complex analytic space, and the structure of a scheme in the case when
X
is a projective variety: see
[
24
, Proposition 1.3.2]
and
[
56
, p.16]
. Let
M_{\operatorname{Dol},0}(X,r)
be the nonsingular points of the reduced subspace of
M_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X,r)
.

(2)

We write
M_{B}(X,r)
for the GIT quotient
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathrm{SL}_{r})\sslash\mathrm{SL}_{r}
. Let
M_{B,0}(X,r)
be the nonsingular points of the reduced subspace of
M_{B}(X,r)

The fundamental theorem of non-abelian Hodge theory, due to Corlette
[
14
, Theorem 3.4]
and Simpson
[
57
, p. 870]
, is that, starting from

, one can construct an equivariant harmonic metric (whose definition we will recall in some form below) on the associated semisimple flat bundle, which in turn gives a Higgs bundle, and vice versa. We can state it in terms of moduli spaces as follows:

Theorem 5.1.2
.

There is a canonical homeomorphism of the underlying topological spaces

M_{B}(X,r)^{\mathrm{top}}\simeq M_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X,r)^{\mathrm{top}};

moreover, when restricted to
M_{B,0}(X,r)
, this gives a real-analytic isomorphism
M_{B,0}(X,r)\simeq M_{\operatorname{Dol},0}(X,r)
.

Proof.

See
[
56
, Lemma 9.14]
.
∎

It was observed by Goldman
[
25
]
that
M_{B}(X,r)
has a natural holomorphic symplectic structure:

Proposition 5.1.3
.

There is an algebraic holomorphic symplectic form
\Omega
on
M_{B}(X,r)
, which may be defined on
\mathbb{C}
-points as follows. For
[\rho]\in M_{B}(X,r)(\mathbb{C})
corresponding to a rank
r
local system, let
\ad(\rho):=(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})^{\operatorname{tr}=0}
denote the adjoint local system.

Recall that the tangent space
T_{[\rho]}M_{B}(X,r)
is canonically identified with
H^{1}(X,\ad(\rho))
; moreover, the trace pairing gives a map of local systems
\ad(\rho)\otimes\ad(\rho)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Then the holomorphic symplectic form
\Omega
, restricted to
T_{[\rho]}M_{B}(X,r)
, is given by the composition

H^{1}(X,\ad(\rho))\otimes H^{1}(X,\ad(\rho))\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\ad(\rho)\otimes\ad(\rho))\xrightarrow{\tr}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{\wedge[\omega^{n-1}]}H^{2n}(X,\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{C}.

5.2.
Energy of harmonic maps

5.2.1.
Recollections on harmonic maps

We first review some standard notions in the theory of harmonic maps.

Definition 5.2.2
.

Let
(M,h),(N,g)
be Riemannian manifolds. The
energy density
of a map
f:M\to N
is the function
e(f):M\to\mathbb{R}
given by the formula

\frac{1}{2}\langle df,df\rangle_{T^{*}M\otimes f^{*}TN}.

We define the
energy
of
f
to be

E(f):=\int_{M}e(f)d\operatorname{vol}_{M}

if
M
is compact. We say
f
is
harmonic
if it is a critical point for
E
. That is, if
\vec{v}\in C^{\infty}(M,f^{*}TN)
, we set

f_{\vec{v},t}(x)=\exp_{f(x)}(t\vec{v}(x))

and

\partial_{\vec{v}}E(f):=\frac{\partial E(f_{\vec{v},t})}{\partial t}\Big|_{t=0},

and say
f
is harmonic if
\partial_{\vec{v}}E(f)
vanishes for all
\vec{v}
.

5.2.3.
Equivariant, or twisted, harmonic maps

We will in fact need an equivariant, sometimes also called twisted, version of the discussion above. The notion of an equivariant harmonic map was introduced by Donaldson
[
18
]
, and the main existence result is due to Corlette
[
14
]
. Let
M,N
be Riemannian manifolds with
M
compact, connected, and
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{Isom}(N)
a homomorphism. We form the
N
-bundle over
M

\mathscr{N}:=\widetilde{M}\times N/\pi_{1}(M)

where
\widetilde{M}
denotes the universal cover of
M
, and the
\pi_{1}(M)
-action on
N
is through
\rho
. Then
\mathscr{N}
has the structure of a Riemannian manifold, with a natural map
\pi:\mathscr{N}\rightarrow M
which is locally (on
M
) a product; the fiber of
\pi
at each
m\in M
,
\mathscr{N}_{m}
, is isometric to
N
. For
s:M\rightarrow\mathscr{N}
a section of
\pi
, we define at
m\in M
, using the local product structure of
\mathscr{N}
, the vertical part of its derivative by the composition

(Ds)_{m}:T_{m}M\rightarrow T_{s(m)}\mathscr{N}\rightarrow T_{s(m)}\mathscr{N}_{m}.

The energy density
e(s):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is then given by the formula

\frac{1}{2}\langle Ds,Ds\rangle_{T^{*}M\otimes s^{*}T\mathscr{N}_{m}}.

Finally, the energy of
s
is

E(s)=\int_{M}e(s)d\operatorname{vol}_{M}.

Definition 5.2.4
.

We say that the section
s:M\rightarrow\mathscr{N}
is harmonic if it is a critical point of
E(s)
. A section
s
gives rise to a map
f_{s}:\widetilde{M}\rightarrow N
, and in the case where
s
is a harmonic section, we will refer to
f_{s}
as a (
\rho
-)equivariant harmonic map.

Theorem 5.2.5
.

Let
M
be a compact connected Riemannian manifold, and
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
a semisimple representation. Then there is a
\rho
-equivariant harmonic map
\widetilde{M}\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})/\su_{r}
.

Moreover, such a harmonic map is unique if
\rho
is irreducible. More generally, any two
\rho
-equivariant harmonic maps are in the same orbit of
Z_{\rho}\leq\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, the centralizer of
\rho
, where the latter acts on the left on
\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})/\su_{r}
.

Proof.

For the first claim, see
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
, as well as the text immediately following it, which explains the relationship between that theorem and harmonic maps.

For the claim that the harmonic map is an energy minimizer, see
[
32
, §2.6]
.
∎

The following is a consequence of work of Uhlenbeck
[
63
]
, and is well known to experts.

Theorem 5.2.6
.

Suppose
X
is an orientable (topological) surface equipped with a Riemannian metric. The energy gives a proper function

E:M_{B}(X,r)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

real-analytic on
M_{B,0}(X,r)
, sending a semisimple representation
\rho
to the energy of the associated
\rho
-equivariant harmonic map to
\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})/\su_{r}
.

Proof.

See for example
[
8
, Theorem 4.4]
for a sketch of the argument.
∎

5.2.7.
Gardiner’s formula

Theorem 5.2.8
.

Let
\Sigma
be a compact topological surface of genus
g\geq 2
, with universal cover
\mathbb{H}
, and
\mathscr{T}_{g}
the Teichmüller space of marked complex structures on
\Sigma
. Let

\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to SL_{r}(\mathbb{C})

be a semisimple representation. For each complex structure
[X]\in\mathscr{T}_{g}
on
\Sigma
, let

u_{X}:\mathbb{H}\to SL_{r}(\mathbb{C})/SU(r)

be a
\rho
-equivariant harmonic map (whose existence is guaranteed by
Theorem 5.2.5
),
(\mathscr{E}_{X},\theta_{X})
be the corresponding Higgs bundle on
X
, and
\Phi_{X}:=h_{2}(\mathscr{E}_{X},\theta_{X})\in H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2})
the quadratic component of the Hitchin image of
(\mathscr{E}_{X},\theta_{X})
; note that
\Phi_{X}
is simply
-\frac{\tr(\theta^{2})}{2}
(see
§ 2.2.2
for the definition of the Hitchin map). Set

E_{\rho}:\mathscr{T}_{g}\to\mathbb{R}

X\mapsto E(u_{X})

to be the function associating to a complex structure on
\Sigma
the energy of the map
u
. Fix
v\in H^{1}(X,T_{X})
a first-order deformation of
X
. Then

\partial_{v}E_{\rho}=-8r\Re(\tr(v\cup\Phi_{X})).

Proof.

We explain how this follows from the version of Gardiner’s formula stated in
[
15
, Theorem 3.11]
; note that this theorem discusses energy minimizers, rather than harmonic maps, but there is no difference in our case since the target
\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})/\su(r)
is non-positively curved (NPC). Now,
[
15
, Theorem 3.11]
, states that

\partial_{v}E_{\rho}=2\Re(\tr(v\cup\mathrm{Hopf}(u_{X}))).

Here,
\mathrm{Hopf}(u_{X})
is the
Hopf differential
of
u_{X}
(descended to
X
): see
[
15
, §2.2.3]
for the definition. Note that the Hopf differential, in this case, is identified with
2r\tr(\theta^{2})
–see e.g.
[
38
, §5.4]
; the latter is, in turn,
-4rh_{2}(\mathscr{E}_{X},\theta_{X})
.
∎

5.3.
The compactness theorem

We now prepare to prove
D
(see
Theorem 5.3.2
below). The proof will proceed by reduction to families of curves; the main input is the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3.1
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a relative curve of genus
g
, with
S
smooth. Fix
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
and set
\Sigma=X_{s}
. Let
\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
be a semisimple representation.

Suppose that
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes on the leaf of the isomonodromy foliation corresponding to
\rho
(viewed as a real-analytic subset of
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)
). Let
\mathscr{U}\subset\mathscr{T}_{g}
be the preimage of the image of the map
S\to\mathscr{M}_{g}
classifying
f
.

Then
E_{\rho}|_{\mathscr{U}}
is locally constant.

Proof.

Let
v\in H^{1}(\Sigma,T_{\Sigma})
be a vector
in the image of the Kodaira–Spencer map
T_{S,s}\to H^{1}(\Sigma,T_{\Sigma})
.
We will show that
\partial_{v}E_{\rho}=0
.

Let
(\mathscr{E}_{\Sigma},\theta_{\Sigma})
be the Higgs bundle on
\Sigma
corresponding to
\rho

(by non-abelian Hodge theory), and set

\Phi_{\Sigma}:=h_{2}(\mathscr{E}_{\Sigma},\theta_{\Sigma})\in H^{0}(\Sigma,\omega_{\Sigma}^{\otimes 2}).

By Gardiner’s formula, it is enough to show that
\tr(v\cup\Phi_{\Sigma})=0
.

Let
v^{\vee}:H^{0}(\Sigma,\omega_{\Sigma}^{\otimes 2})\to\mathbb{C}
be the linear functional corresponding
to
v
by Serre duality, so that
v^{\vee}(\Phi_{\Sigma})=\tr(v\cup\Phi_{\Sigma})
.
Consider the holomorphic function
f:=h_{2}^{*}v^{\vee}
on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(\Sigma,r)
.
By Chen’s formula (
3.4.2.1
) and the naturality of the lifting
\Theta_{X/S}
under pullback
along the classifying map
S\to\mathscr{M}_{g}
, the hypothesis that
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes on the isomonodromy
leaf corresponding to
\rho
implies that the Hamiltonian vector field
H_{f}
vanishes at the point

(\mathscr{E}_{\Sigma},\theta_{\Sigma})
.

Let
\Omega
be the holomorphic symplectic form of
3.4.1
. On the smooth locus,

H_{f}
is characterized by
\iota_{H_{f}}\Omega=df
, hence
H_{f}=0
implies
df=0
at
(\mathscr{E}_{\Sigma},\theta_{\Sigma})
.
Let
\xi
be the vector field generated by the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
–action scaling the Higgs field,

t\cdot(\mathscr{E},\theta)=(\mathscr{E},t\theta)
. Since
h_{2}
is quadratic in
\theta
, we have

f(t\cdot(\mathscr{E},\theta))=t^{2}f(\mathscr{E},\theta)
, and therefore
df(\xi)=\xi(f)=2f
.
Evaluating at
(\mathscr{E}_{\Sigma},\theta_{\Sigma})
and using
df=0
gives
f(\mathscr{E}_{\Sigma},\theta_{\Sigma})=0
, i.e.

\tr(v\cup\Phi_{\Sigma})=v^{\vee}(\Phi_{\Sigma})=f(\mathscr{E}_{\Sigma},\theta_{\Sigma})=0.

Thus
\partial_{v}E_{\rho}=-8r\,\Re(\tr(v\cup\Phi_{\Sigma}))=0
, as required. Since
s
and
v
were arbitrary,

E_{\rho}|_{\mathscr{U}}
is locally constant.
∎

Theorem 5.3.2
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism of smooth complex varieties. Fix
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
and let
\mathbb{V}
be a rank
r
local system on
X_{s}
, with corresponding monodromy representation
[\rho]
.

Suppose the lifting of tangent vectors

\Theta_{X/S}:\pi_{\operatorname{Dol}}^{*}T_{S}\to{T}_{\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)/S}

vanishes identically on the leaf of the isomonodromy foliation corresponding to
[\rho]
. Then the orbit
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)\cdot[\rho]\subset M_{B}(X_{s},r)^{\an}
has compact closure (in the analytic topology).

Proof of
Theorem 5.3.2
.

Without loss of generality we may assume
f
has relative dimension
1
, by replacing
X
with a family
X^{\prime}
of sufficiently ample smooth complete intersection curves in
X
of genus
g\geq 2
, at the cost of replacing
S
with a dense open
S^{\prime}\subset S
containing
s
. Indeed, by the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, we have that for each
s\in S^{\prime}
,
X^{\prime}_{s}\to X_{s}
induces a surjection on
\pi_{1}
. Moreover
\pi_{1}(S^{\prime})\to\pi_{1}(S)
is surjective. Thus
\pi_{1}(S^{\prime},s)\cdot[\rho|_{X^{\prime}_{s}}]
has compact closure in
M_{B}(X^{\prime}_{s},r)(\mathbb{C})^{\an}
iff the same is true for
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)\cdot[\rho]\subset M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\mathbb{C})^{\an}
. Thus we may and do replace
X/S
with
X^{\prime}/S^{\prime}
, which we rename
X/S
.

Now this orbit is precisely the intersection of the leaf of the isomonodromy foliation in
M_{B}(X/S,r)
with
M_{B}(X_{s})
, whence it suffices to show that the leaf of the isomonodromy foliation is relatively compact over
S(\mathbb{C})^{\an}
. But by
5.3.1
, the energy of
\rho
is constant along this leaf, whence the leaf is contained in a level set of the energy function on
M_{B}(X/S)
. This latter is relatively compact over
S^{\an}
by
Theorem 5.2.6
.
∎

The following is immediate:

Corollary 5.3.3
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism of smooth complex varieties. Fix
s\in S
.
Suppose the lifting of tangent vectors

\Theta_{X/S}:\pi_{\operatorname{Dol}}^{*}T_{S}\to{T}_{\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S)/S}

vanishes identically. Then every orbit of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)\lefttorightarrow M_{B}(X_{s})

5.4.
The hyperkähler metric

Recall that
M_{\operatorname{Dol},0}(X,r)
denotes the nonsingular points of the reduced subspace of
M_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X,r)
, and similarly
M_{B,0}(X,r)
the nonsingular points of
M_{B}(X,r)
.

Definition 5.4.1
.

A hyperkähler manifold is a tuple
(M,g,I,J,K)
consisting of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and almost complex structures
I,J,K
such that

(1)

I^{2}=J^{2}=K^{2}=IJK=-1
, and

(2)

each of
(M,g,I)
,
(M,g,J)
,
(M,g,K)
is a Kähler manifold.

For
q=(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=1
,
I_{q}=aI+bJ+cK
is also an almost complex structure on
M
; in other words, a hyperkähler manifold has a sphere’s worth of almost complex structures, each of which is in fact a Kähler structure. We write
\omega_{q}
for the corresponding Kähler forms, which are defined by the formula

\omega_{q}(v,w)=g(I_{q}v,w).

Definition 5.4.2
.

For a hyperkähler manifold
(M,g,I,J,K)
, we denote by
\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K}
the Kähler forms corresponding to
I,J,K
, respectively. We also define the following 2-forms

\displaystyle\Omega_{I}

\displaystyle=\omega_{J}+i\omega_{K},

\displaystyle\Omega_{J}

\displaystyle=\omega_{K}+i\omega_{I},

\displaystyle\Omega_{K}

\displaystyle=\omega_{I}+i\omega_{J},

and it is straightforward to check that they are holomorphic in the complex structures
I,J,K
, respectively, and are moreover symplectic, i.e. non-degenerate and closed.

Theorem 5.4.3
.

There is a hyperkähler manifold
(M,g,I,J,K)
such that we have identifications of complex manifolds

(1)

(M,I)=M_{\operatorname{Dol},0}(X,r)
,

(2)

(M,J)=M_{B,0}(X,r)
.

(3)

Via the identification in (1), we obtain a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
M
. For
t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, acting by
t
induces a biholomorphic map
(M,I_{q})\simeq(M,I_{t(q)})
. Moreover, for
t\in S^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, this is a Kähler isometry.

(4)

The holomorphic symplectic form
\Omega_{J}
is the natural one on
M_{J}=M_{B,0}(X,r)
, as given by
5.1.3
.

Proof.

This is
[
24
, (1.5.2)]
.
∎

We can package the above data via the twistor construction as follows: there is a unique complex structure
\mathscr{J}
on
M\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
such that

•

the restriction of
\mathscr{J}
to
M\times q
is precisely
(M,I_{q})
, and

•

\mathscr{J}|_{m\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}}
is the usual complex structure, for each
m\in M
.

We denote the resulting complex manifold by
(Z,\mathscr{J})
, which, by construction, is equipped with a holomorphic map
f:Z\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
. There is then a holomorphic
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
Z
which covers the usual action on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Lemma 5.4.4
.

The energy functional
E
on
M
is a Kähler potential for
\omega_{J}
; that is,

\partial_{J}\overline{\partial}_{J}E=i\omega_{J}.

Here
d=\partial_{J}+\overline{\partial}_{J}
is the decomposition of
d
into bi-degree
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
components, in the complex structure
J
.

Proof.

The statement for
r=2
and
X
a closed Riemann surface of genus
\geq 2
is due to Hitchin
[
28
, Equation (9.10)]
; for the general case, see
[
60
, Theorem 6.6]
.
∎

The following is now immediate from
5.3.1
:

Theorem 5.4.5
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism, and suppose the lifting of tangent vectors
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes identically. Then the Kähler form
\omega_{J}
is preserved under isomonodromic deformation.

Proof.

As in the proof of
Theorem 5.3.2
we may locally on
S
, choose a family
X^{\prime}\to X
of sufficiently ample complete intersection curves of genus
g\geq 2
such that for each
s\in S
,
X^{\prime}_{s}\to X_{s}
induces a surjection on
\pi_{1}
, so that the induced map
M_{\dagger}(X_{s})\hookrightarrow M_{\dagger}(X^{\prime}_{s})
is a closed embedding for
\dagger=B,\operatorname{Dol},
etc.; by assumption
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes on the image of this embedding (on the Dolbeault moduli space). Moreover the image of this embedding is evidently preserved by the isomonodromy foliation.

By
5.3.1
, the energy functional is thus preserved under isomonodromic deformation on this locus, whence the same is true for
\omega_{J}
, for which it is a Kähler potential.
∎

5.5.
Triholomorphic action

Theorem 5.5.1
.

Suppose
X\rightarrow S
is a smooth projective morphism of
\mathbb{C}
-varieties, with connected fibers. Suppose that the lifting of tangent vectors
\Theta_{X/S}
on
M_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S,r)
vanishes. Then, for any basepoint
s\in S
, the action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on
M_{B,0}(X_{s})
is triholomorphic. In particular, the
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
-action preserves the Riemannian metric
g
from
Theorem 5.4.3
.

Proof.

We have the relative Betti moduli space
M_{B,0}(X/S,r)\rightarrow S
. Suppose
\epsilon>0
and let
\gamma:[-\epsilon,\epsilon]\rightarrow S
be a path, and consider the fiber product

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma^{*}M_{B,0}(X/S,r)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{B,0}(X/S,r)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-\epsilon,\ \epsilon).}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}

Write
s_{0}=\gamma(0)
. By isomonodromy, we identify each fiber of
G
, as real manifolds, with
M=M_{B,0}(X_{s_{0}},r)
. We therefore obtain a family of hyperkähler metrics
(g_{t},I_{t},J_{t},K_{t})
on
M
, indexed by
t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
. We must show that
(g_{t},I_{t},J_{t},K_{t})
is a constant family. Recall that the
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
action on
M_{B,0}(X,r)
is holomorphic symplectic, and therefore both
J_{t}
and
\Omega_{J_{t}}=\omega_{K_{t}}+i\omega_{I_{t}}
are constant.

By
Theorem 5.4.5
, the vanishing of the lifting of tangent vectors implies that
\omega_{J_{t}}
is constant as well. We deduce that
g_{t}
is constant, since

g_{t}(v,w)=-\omega_{J_{t}}(J_{t}v,w)

is determined by
J_{t}
and
\omega_{J_{t}}
.

On the other hand,
g_{t}
is also determined by
I_{t},\omega_{I_{t}}
(resp.
K_{t},\omega_{K_{t}}
), and hence
I_{t}
(resp.
K_{t}
) is constant as well, as desired.
∎

6.
Finiteness of non-abelian monodromy

In this section we give the main applications of the theory developed in the previous sections. We first prove a very strong form of our results for relative curves, namely that if the
p
-curvature of the isomonodromy foliation vanishes mod
p
for infinitely many
p
, then such a family is in fact isotrivial. We then prove
A
and
B
. Finally, we briefly discuss the relationship between these results and those of
[
33
]
.

6.1.
Relative curves

We begin with a case in which we can prove a very strong form of the Ekedahl–Shepherd-Barron–Taylor conjecture, namely where
f:X\to S
is a relative curve. In this case the vanishing of
p
-curvatures of the isomonodromy foliation in fact implies that the family is isotrivial. Recall that for each stack
\mathscr{M}_{\dagger}
, we denote by
\mathscr{M}^{\operatorname{s}}_{\dagger}
the substack of stable objects.

Theorem 6.1.1
.

Let
R\subset\mathbb{C}
be a finitely-generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra,
S
a smooth
R
-scheme, and
f:X\to S
a smooth projective morphism of relative dimension
1
with connected fibers of genus
g\geq 2
. Suppose that for some
r\geq 2
the
p
-curvature of the isomonodromy foliation on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}^{\operatorname{s}}(X/S,r)
vanishes mod
\mathfrak{p}
for a Zariski-dense set of points
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. Then
X_{\mathbb{C}}/S_{\mathbb{C}}
is isotrivial.

Proof.

By
Theorem 4.2.3
, we know that
\Theta_{X/S}
is identically zero on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}^{\operatorname{s}}(X_{K}/S_{K},r)
, where
K
denotes the field of fractions of
R
. Fix
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
. By Chen’s formula (
Theorem 3.4.2
) and the surjectivity of the Hitchin map (even on the stable locus) for curves, this implies that for each
v\in T_{S,s}
with associated Kodaira-Spencer class
\kappa(v)\in H^{1}(X_{s},T_{X_{s}})
, the functional
\kappa(v)^{\vee}
of
Theorem 3.4.2
is identically zero. But this functional is precisely given by the (perfect) Serre duality pairing

H^{1}(X_{s},T_{X_{s}})\otimes H^{0}(X_{s},\omega_{X_{s}}^{\otimes 2})\to\mathbb{C},

whence
\kappa(v)=0
. As
s,v
were arbitrary, this implies that the Kodaira-Spencer map for
f:X\to S
is trivial, and hence that
f
is isotrivial.
∎

Remark 6.1.2
.

Note that the semistable analogue of
Theorem 6.1.1
, that is assuming the vanishing of
p
-curvature on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}^{\operatorname{ss}}
instead, which of course follows from
Theorem 6.1.1
, also follows from Katz’s theorem
Theorem 1.1.1
and the Torelli theorem for curves directly. We can see this by considering the extensions given, tautologically, by
R^{1}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}
. Indeed, we can map the total space, denoted as
\mathscr{H}(X/S)
, of this vector bundle to
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S,2)
by viewing a class in
H^{1}_{\operatorname{dR}}
as a rank two flat connection
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
which is an extension of the form

(6.1.2.1)

0\to(\mathscr{O},d)\to(\mathscr{E},\nabla)\to(\mathscr{O},d)\to 0.

By taking symmetric powers, we obtain a map
\mathscr{H}(X/S)\to\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S,r)
for any
r\geq 2
. One can check that this map is a map of crystals, and that furthermore it is injective on tangent spaces away from the zero section of
\mathscr{H}(X/S)
. The assumption then implies that the usual
p
-curvature of the Gauss-Manin connection on
R^{1}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S,\operatorname{dR}}
vanishes for infinitely many
p
, and Katz’s theorem implies that the Kodaira–Spencer map of
X/S
vanishes identically. It then follows from the Torelli theorem that
X/S
is isotrivial.

On the other hand, it does not seem possible to make such arguments on the stable locus. We are grateful to Sasha Petrov for this remark.

6.2.
Integral points

Lemma 6.2.1
.

Let
X
be a finite-type affine
\mathbb{Z}
-scheme. Let
K
be a number field and let
\iota_{1},\cdots,\iota_{m}:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the distinct embeddings of
K
into
\mathbb{C}
. For each
i=1,\cdots,m
, fix
S_{i}\subset X(\mathbb{C})^{\an}
a compact subset, and define
X(\mathscr{O}_{K};S_{1},\cdots,S_{m})
to be the set of
\mathscr{O}_{K}
-points
x
of
X
such that, for all
i=1,\cdots,m
,
\iota_{i}(x)
lies in
S_{i}
. Then
X(\mathscr{O}_{K};S_{1},\cdots,S_{m})
is finite.

Proof.

As
X
is affine we may without loss of generality take it to be affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
. Thus we have compact sets
S_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and wish to show that the set
x\in\mathscr{O}_{K}^{n}
such that
\iota_{i}(x)
lies in
S_{i}
for all
i
is finite.

Consider the inclusion

\mathscr{O}_{K}^{n}\hookrightarrow\prod_{\iota:\mathscr{O}_{K}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}^{n}

given by the product of the embeddings
\iota
. This map has discrete image. On the other hand,
X(\mathscr{O}_{K};S_{1},\cdots,S_{m})
is the intersection of this image with the compact set
\prod_{i}S_{i}
, whence it is both discrete and compact, hence finite.
∎

We now prove a weak form of
A
. We will deduce the strong form in
§ 6.3
.

Theorem 6.2.2
.

Let
R\subset\mathbb{C}
be a finitely-generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra, and
f:X\to S
a smooth projective morphism of smooth
R
-schemes. Fix a point
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
and a positive integer
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. If the
p
-curvature of the isomonodromy foliation on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S,r)
mod
p
vanishes for infinitely many primes
p
, then the orbit of any point of
M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\overline{\mathbb{Z}})
under the action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
is finite.

Proof.

By
Theorem 4.2.3
, we have that
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes identically. In fact this is all we will use below.

Let
K
be a number field and
\mathscr{O}_{K}
its ring of integers; let
\iota_{1},\cdots,\iota_{n}:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
be its distinct complex embeddings. Let
[\rho]\in M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\mathscr{O}_{K})
be a point. For each embedding
\iota_{i}:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, let
\rho_{\iota_{i}}
be the corresponding semisimple complex representation.

For each
i
, set
S_{i}
to be the closure of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)\cdot[\rho_{\iota_{i}}]
in the analytic topology of
M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\mathbb{C})
. The set
S_{i}
is compact by
Theorem 5.3.2
(using that
\Theta_{X/S}
vanishes identically). On the other hand, the orbit of
[\rho_{\iota_{i}}]
consists of
\mathscr{O}_{K}
-points for each

. Hence the orbit is finite by
6.2.1
, as desired.
∎

6.3.
The Ekedahl–Shepherd-Barron–Taylor conjecture

Lemma 6.3.1
.

Let
X/\mathbb{C}
be a smooth projective variety and let
W\subset M_{B}(X,r)_{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the closure of an irreducible component of the geometric generic fiber. Suppose
W(\overline{\mathbb{Z}})
is non-empty. Then
W(\overline{\mathbb{Z}})
is dense in
W(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof of
6.3.1
.

Immediate from
[
49
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

We learned of this application of Rumely’s theorem
[
49
]
from Esnault’s Park City lectures
[
22
]
, and it is closely related to
[
16
]
.

Lemma 6.3.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a connected Riemannian real-analytic manifold, and
G
a group acting on
M
by analytic isometries. If
G
fixes a point
x\in M
, and acts trivially on
T_{M,x}
, then
G
acts trivially on
M
.

Proof.

G
acts trivially on a small neighborhood of
x
(as the exponential map is equivariant for the action of
G
). But now it must act trivially on all of
M
, by real-analyticity of the action.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main results of this article.

Proof of
A
and
B
.

We prove that, if the
p
-curvature of the isomonodromy foliation on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S,r)
mod
p
vanishes for infinitely many
p
, then for each component
W\subset M_{B}(X_{s},r)_{\mathbb{C}}
containing an
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-point, the action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on the orbit of
W
factors through a finite group. This contains the statements of both
A
and
B
.

By assumption each such geometric component
W
of
M_{B}(X_{s},r)_{\mathbb{C}}
has a
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-point, whence by
6.3.1
, the reduced locus of
W
contains a
smooth

\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-point. For each such component
W
, fix a smooth
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-point
[\rho_{W}]
contained in
W
.

By
Theorem 6.2.2
, the orbit of
[\rho_{W}]
under
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
is finite. Thus there exists a finite index subgroup
\Gamma\subset\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
fixing all the
[\rho_{W}]
, hence acting on
T_{M_{B,0}(X_{s},r),[\rho_{W}]}
for each
[\rho_{W}]
. By
Theorem 5.5.1
this action preserves a Riemannian metric, whence it is unitary.

Now let
\{[\rho_{W}]^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the set of Galois conjugates of
[\rho_{W}]
. The action of
\Gamma
on

\bigoplus_{\sigma}T_{M_{B,0}(X_{s},r),[\rho_{W}]^{\sigma}}

is both unitary and integral, hence factors through a finite group by e.g.
[
35
, Theorem 7.2.1]
. Thus there exists a further finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
acting trivially on
T_{M_{B,0}(X_{s},r),[\rho_{W}]}
for each
W
.

But now the action of
\Gamma^{\prime}
on
W
is trivial by
6.3.2
, as desired.

The statement about algebraicity of leaves in
B
follows from
[
33
, Theorem 7.7.3]
.
∎

In fact we have proven the following statement of pure complex geometry:

Theorem 6.3.3
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism of smooth complex varieties. Suppose that the lifting of tangent vectors
\Theta_{X/S}\equiv 0
on
M_{\operatorname{Dol}}(X/S,r)
. Fix
s\in S
. Then

(1)

The action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on
M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\overline{\mathbb{Z}})
factors through a finite group.

(2)

If each irreducible component of
M_{B}(X_{s},r)
contains a
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-point, then the action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on
M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\mathbb{C})
factors through a finite group.

We summarize some cases in which we have proven the Ekedahl–Shepherd-Barron–Taylor conjecture,
1.1.3
, for isomonodromy foliations:

Corollary 6.3.4
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism, and fix a point
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
. Suppose that the
p
-curvature of the isomonodromy foliation on
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}(X/S,r)
vanishes for infinitely many
p
, and either

(1)

r=2
, or

(2)

M_{B}(X_{s},r)_{\mathbb{C}}
is irreducible, or

(3)

f
has relative dimension

.

Then the action of
\pi_{1}(S(\mathbb{C})^{\an},s)
on
M_{B}(X_{s},r)(\mathbb{C})
factors through a finite group.

Proof.

In cases (1) and (2) we claim the hypotheses of
B
are satisfied. Indeed, in the case
M_{B}(X_{s},r)_{\mathbb{C}}
is irreducible the trivial representation gives rise to a
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-point. In the case

, the density of
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
-points follows for example from
[
11
]
(where it ultimately comes down to Corlette-Simpson’s classification of rank

local systems on quasi-projective varieties
[

]
).

In case (3), this follows from

.
∎

Remark 6.3.5
.

In
[

, Conjecture 1.1.1]

Theorem 6.4.1

With notation and assumptions as in

Remark 6.4.2
.

Let

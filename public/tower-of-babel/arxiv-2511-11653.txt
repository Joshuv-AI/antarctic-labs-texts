GroupRank: A Groupwise Reranking Paradigm Driven by Reinforcement Learning
1 st Duolin Sun, 1 st Meixiu Long, 2 nd Dan Yang, 3 th Yihan Jiao, 4 th Yue Shen, 5 th Zhehao Tan, 6 th Jie Feng, 7 th Junjie Wang, 8 th Peng Wei, 9 th Jian Wang, 10 th Jinjie Gu

Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown strong potential as rerankers to enhance the overall performance of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. However, existing reranking paradigms are constrained by a core theoretical and practical dilemma: Pointwise methods, while simple and highly flexible, evaluate documents independently, making them prone to the Ranking Myopia Trap—overlooking the relative importance between documents. In contrast, Listwise methods can perceive the global ranking context, but suffer from inherent List Rigidity, leading to severe scalability and flexibility issues when handling large candidate sets. To address these challenges, we propose Groupwise, a novel reranking paradigm. In this approach, the query and a group of candidate documents are jointly fed into the model, which performs within-group comparisons to assign individual relevance scores to each document. This design retains the flexibility of Pointwise methods while enabling the comparative capability of Listwise methods. We further adopt GRPO for model training, equipped with a heterogeneous reward function that integrates ranking metrics (e.g., NDCG, Recall) with a distributional reward aimed at aligning score distributions across groups. To overcome the bottleneck caused by the scarcity of high‑quality labeled data, we further propose an innovative pipeline for synthesizing high‑quality retrieval and ranking data. The resulting data can be leveraged not only for training the reranker but also for training the retriever. Extensive experiments validate the effectiveness of our approach. On two reasoning‑intensive retrieval benchmarks, BRIGHT and R2MED, our GroupRank achieves new state‑of‑the‑art performance on the NDCG@10 metric, reaching 46.8 and 52.3, respectively, while also demonstrating strong generalization capability on traditional retrieval tasks. Our code, models and prompts can be found at https://github.com/AQ-MedAI/Diver.git

Introduction

In Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems
[

, reranking serves as a critical gatekeeper for enhancing the quality of search results. A typical system employs a two-stage “retrieve-and-rank” architecture: a lightweight retriever first recalls hundreds of candidate documents from a massive corpus, after which a more sophisticated and powerful reranker performs a deep analysis to precisely order these candidates. By filtering out irrelevant or redundant information, the reranker effectively compensates for the shortcomings of the initial retrieval stage, significantly boosting the system’s overall precision and user satisfaction.

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs)
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
has sparked a revolutionary shift in reranking technology. With their profound capabilities in natural language understanding, semantic reasoning, and contextual awareness, LLMs have demonstrated unprecedented potential in assessing the complex relevance between a query and a document. In particular, Large Reasoning Models (LRMs)
[
6
,
9
]
, specialized for complex inference tasks, are being adapted for reranking, where they not only perform deep semantic matching but also holistically evaluate a document’s utility, coherence, and reliability. These advanced models are continuously pushing the state-of-the-art on various retrieval benchmarks by enabling more fine-grained text interaction and reasoning.

Despite these advancements, the prevailing reranking paradigms—namely,
Pointwise
and
Listwise
methods—exhibit inherent limitations as shown in Fig
1
. Pointwise methods
[
10
]
, which independently evaluate each query-document pair, are simple and flexible but suffer from the
Ranking Myopia Trap
: the model lacks a global view of the candidate set, hindering its ability to make an optimal global ranking decision. Conversely, Listwise methods, which learn to rank the entire list of documents end-to-end, can perceive global context and theoretically achieve superior performance. However, this approach suffers from
List Rigidity
, struggling to process a large number of candidates (e.g.,
\geq
100) directly. This often necessitates approximation techniques like sliding windows, which compromise the model’s valuable global perspective and increase implementation complexity.

To overcome these limitations and fully leverage the reasoning capabilities of large models, we propose a novel reranking model and training framework, termed
GroupRank
. This approach aims to synergize the flexibility of Pointwise methods with the high performance of Listwise approaches. Specifically, we feed a query and a group of
n
candidate documents to the reranker simultaneously. The model, leveraging its intrinsic reasoning abilities, generates a comprehensive score (e.g., for relevance, utility) for each document within the group in a single forward pass. This “groupwise scoring” paradigm enhances the model’s global awareness by enabling inter-document comparisons, thereby mitigating the “Ranking Myopia Trap” problem. Concurrently, it maintains the flexibility to process an arbitrary number of documents, thus avoiding the “List Rigidity” dilemma. To effectively train our model, we further introduce a novel
heterogeneous reward
during the Reinforcement Learning (RL) stage, which is crafted to bolster the performance of the GroupRank. It composes a reward function that integrates ranking metrics (e.g., nDCG, Recall) and score distribution consistency to guide the model toward an optimal ranking policy.

We validate the efficacy of our method through extensive experiments on several inference-intensive public retrieval benchmarks, including BRIGHT, R2MED and BEIR. The results demonstrate that our GroupRank achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on these challenging leaderboards, underscoring its superior capabilities in handling complex information retrieval tasks. Our main contributions are threefold:

1.

We propose an innovative reranking framework named
GroupRank
. Its novel “groupwise scoring” mechanism ingeniously fuses the flexibility of Pointwise methods with the global context awareness of Listwise approaches. Furthermore, we construct a
Heterogeneous Reward
to explicitly strengthen the reranking capability of our GroupRank during the reinforcement learning stage.

2.

To surmount the bottleneck of scarce high-quality labeled data of GroupRank, we have designed and implemented an efficient synthetic data pipeline. This pipeline is capable of generating large-scale, high-quality, and general-purpose training data for both the retriever and reranker models.

3.

Extensive experiments demonstrate that our proposed method achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) results in both performance and efficiency. It particularly excels in retrieval tasks that require deep reasoning, thus providing robust technical support for the development of next-generation intelligent retrieval systems.

II-B

Reranking Paradigms

Current LLM-based ranking methods can be categorized into three paradigms:
pointwise
,
pairwise
, and
listwise
methods. These methods differ in their input format and optimization objectives.
The
pointwise
approach simplifies the complex ranking problem into an independent relevance scoring of each query-document pair, thereby disregarding the relative positions of documents within the list
[
18
,
22
,
23
]
. While efficient, this approach loses global context by ignoring the interactions and relative ordering among documents.
The
pairwise
approach compares pairs of documents to determine their relative relevance to the query, and then aggregates these comparisons using an algorithm (e.g., Bubble Sort) to form a global ranking
[
24
,
25
]
. This method can more accurately capture the global ranking structure but incurs a significant computational complexity, typically
O(N^{2})
.
The
listwise
approach, in contrast, processes an entire list of candidate documents at once, maximally leveraging the contextual reasoning capabilities of LLMs
[
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
. By performing a global comparison, listwise methods often achieve superior performance. However, they are constrained by the LLM’s limited context window and suffer from high inference latency.

Building upon this body of research, we introduce a novel
groupwise
framework that effectively integrates the strengths of both pointwise and listwise methods. This hybrid approach aims to enhance ranking performance while simultaneously reducing inference costs. Furthermore, we present a concrete training methodology for our framework, detailing specific strategies for both Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

III
Preliminaries

Pointwise Reranking
is the most straightforward reranking paradigm as shown in Fig
1
. It independently estimates the relevance between each document and the given query, transforming the reranking task into a
regression
or
classification
problem.
For each query–document pair
(q,d_{i})
, the reranking model
f_{\theta}
predicts a relevance score
s_{i}
as follows:

s_{i}=f_{\theta}(q,d_{i})

(1)

The final ranking is determined by sorting all documents according to their predicted scores in descending order.
During training, the objective is to minimize the discrepancy between the predicted score
s_{i}
and the ground-truth label
y_{i}
.
If
y_{i}
is a continuous score (e.g., ranging from
0
to
10
), the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is typically used; if
y_{i}
is a binary label (e.g., relevant/irrelevant), the Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss is commonly applied.
The overall loss for a query can be expressed as:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{pointwise}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{L}\big(f_{\theta}(q,d_{i}),y_{i}\big)

(2)

where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the selected point-level loss function (e.g., MSE or BCE).

Advantages.

The pointwise approach is simple to implement, highly flexible, and computationally efficient, since the scoring of each document can be processed independently and in parallel.

Limitations.

Its main drawback lies in the
Ranking Myopia Trap
: the model treats each document in isolation without considering the global context of the candidate set. When multiple documents are partially relevant to the query, the model lacks the ability to capture their relative importance, which may lead to suboptimal ranking results.

Listwise Reranking
In contrast, Listwise Reranking jointly considers the entire candidate list during training, learning to optimize the global ranking structure directly.
Instead of predicting independent relevance scores, it aims to produce an overall ranking that best approximates the ideal order. This paradigm aligns more closely with the ultimate goal of ranking and is often trained to directly optimize metrics such as
Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG)
as
shown in Fig
1
.

Formally, the listwise reranking model
f_{\theta}
takes a query
q
and its candidate document set
D=\{d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{k}\}
as input, and outputs a corresponding score vector:

\mathbf{S}=f_{\theta}(q,D)=[s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k}]^{\top}

(3)

The training objective is to minimize the discrepancy between the predicted score vector
S
and the ground-truth label set
Y=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{k}\}
, formulated as:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{listwise}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{list}}(\mathbf{S},Y)

(4)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\text{list}}
denotes a list-level loss function that quantifies ranking-level discrepancies. For an LLM reranker, the
\mathcal{L}_{\text{list}}
refers to the difference between the model’s output and the ground truth ranking.

Advantages.

The listwise paradigm incorporates global contextual awareness and models inter-document dependencies. Its training objective aligns directly with ranking performance metrics, typically leading to superior overall results compared to pointwise methods.

Limitations.

However, it faces the
List Rigidity Dilemma
: the model is usually trained on lists of fixed length, making it less adaptable to variable-length candidate sets during inference. Moreover, when the candidate list is large (e.g., hundreds of documents), the computational cost becomes substantial. Approximation techniques such as sliding-window training can alleviate this issue but may weaken the model’s global perspective.

III-A

Towards a Balanced Paradigm

The “ranking myopia gap” of the pointwise paradigm and the “list rigidity dilemma” of the listwise paradigm reveal their respective limitations.
To strike a balance between flexibility and global optimality, we propose the
GroupRank
, which integrates the adaptability of pointwise methods with the global modeling capacity of listwise approaches. The next section provides a detailed description of this method.

IV
Methodology

This chapter elaborates on our comprehensive proposed method, which is characterized by two core innovations. First, we design and implement a synthetic pipeline to construct high-quality training data for general-purpose retrieval as seen in Figure
2
. Second, we leverage this data to train a Robust Retriever
[
30
]
and a novel GroupRank model as shown in Figure
3
.
Specifically, our workflow, as illustrated in Figure
2
, begins by generating a set of candidate documents for each query via the data synthesis pipeline. A large language model (LLM) is then employed to annotate their relevance scores. Subsequently, this high-quality synthetic data is used to train the GroupRank. This model, by scoring documents in groups, is designed to overcome the limitations inherent in traditional Pointwise and Listwise approaches. Finally, we fine-tune the model using a reinforcement learning algorithm named GRPO
[
31
]
to optimize its ranking performance and improve the prediction capability of its score distribution.

Fig. 2:
Workflow for High-Quality Training Data Generation.
After filtering candidate documents via hybrid retrieval, we employ two parallel annotation methods: Pointwise (LLM-based individual scoring) and Listwise (LLM-based holistic ranking). Finally, we apply a weighted fusion to these two sets of annotations to generate highly reliable final scores and a ranked list. This output is ideal for training a GroupRank and can also be used for training Retrievers or other Rerankers.

IV-A

High-Quality Synthetic Data Generation Pipeline

To train a reranking model capable of both accurately predicting the absolute relevance of documents and precisely determining their relative order, we introduce a novel data synthesis pipeline as shown in Fig
2
. The objective of this pipeline is to generate “gold-standard” training data that amalgamates the score magnitude from Pointwise approaches with the ranking fidelity of Listwise methods. This process comprises the following three stages:

Query Selection and Initial Candidate Set Construction
The queries used in this study are sourced from three distinct collections: 1) the training set from ReasonRank (13k queries); 2) an in-house collection of medical domain queries (approx. 1k); and 3) a collection of coding-related queries from LeetCode (approx. 700).

For each query
q
in our knowledge base, we employ two mainstream retrieval techniques in parallel: a sparse retrieval model (BM25) and a dense retrieval model, to retrieve the top-100 candidate documents from a large-scale corpus. BM25 focuses on keyword matching, whereas the dense model captures semantic similarity. To leverage the complementary strengths of both methods, we first normalize their respective retrieval scores and then combine them via a weighted sum to produce an initial hybrid relevance score. This is formulated as:

\displaystyle S_{\text{hybrid}}(q,d)

\displaystyle=0.5\cdot\text{norm}\big(S_{\text{BM25}}(q,d)\big)

(5)

\displaystyle+0.5\cdot\text{norm}\big(S_{\text{Dense}}(q,d)\big)

where
S_{\text{hybrid}}(q,d)
is the final fused relevance score between query
q
and document
d
.
S_{\text{BM25}}(q,d)
and
S_{\text{Dense}}(q,d)
are the raw scores from the BM25 and dense models, respectively. The
\text{norm}(\cdot)
function is a crucial normalization step that maps scores from different models, which may have vastly different distributions, into a unified and comparable range (e.g.,
[0,1]
).

Based on this hybrid score, we select the top-50 documents to form a candidate list
\langle q,\{d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{50}\}\rangle
, which serves as the input for the subsequent annotation phase.

Hybrid Annotation Strategy
To obtain highly reliable supervision signals for reranking, we adopt a hybrid annotation strategy that integrates both Pointwise and Listwise philosophies, leveraging powerful Large Language Models (LLMs) as “teacher models” for annotation.

•

Pointwise Scoring:
We utilize the Qwen3-235B-instruct model to independently evaluate each query-document pair
\langle q,d_{i}\rangle
. The model is prompted to provide a comprehensive assessment based on multiple dimensions, such as relevance and utility, assigning a fine-grained score, denoted as
S_{\text{pointwise}}(d_{i})
, on a scale of 0 to 10. The advantage of this approach is its ability to provide an absolute score with a clear magnitude for each document. The annotation prompts are available in the Appendix.

•

Listwise Ranking:
We employ the Gemini-2.5-pro model, which takes the entire list of 50 documents,
\langle q,\{d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{50}\}\rangle
, as a single input. The model is tasked to perform a global comparison and reasoning over the entire list to directly output a ranked order from 1 to 50, denoted as
R_{\text{listwise}}(d_{i})
. The primary strength of this method lies in its superior global context-aware reasoning, enabling it to generate a highly reliable relative ordering. The annotation prompts are available in the Appendix.

Label Fusion and Final Score Generation

After obtaining both the Pointwise scores and the Listwise rankings, we once again utilize an ensemble method to generate a final, robust supervision score. Specifically, we first transform the Listwise rank
r_{i}=R_{\text{listwise}}(d_{i})
into a score-like format. We apply a negative logarithmic transformation:
S_{\text{listwise}}(d_{i})=-\log(r_{i})
. This transformation preserves the monotonic nature of the ranking while amplifying the score differences between higher-ranked documents, which aligns with the intuition of ranking tasks.

Subsequently, we perform a weighted fusion of the normalized Pointwise scores and the transformed Listwise scores to compute the final ground-truth supervision score,
S_{\text{gt}}(d_{i})
:

\displaystyle S_{\text{gt}}(d_{i})

\displaystyle=\alpha\cdot\text{norm}\big(S_{\text{pointwise}}(d_{i})\big)

(6)

\displaystyle+(1-\alpha)\cdot\text{norm}\big(S_{\text{listwise}}(d_{i})\big)

where
\text{norm}(\cdot)
is the normalization function and
\alpha
is a hyperparameter balancing the contribution of the two strategies, which we set to 0.5.

Through this pipeline, for each query, we generate a high-quality training set consisting of 50 documents. Each document is assigned a robust score that encapsulates both ranking information and score magnitude, thereby laying a solid foundation for training our subsequent GroupRank.
All the training data construction processes, prompts, and code can be found in our codebase.

IV-B

Two-stage Training Strategy for the GroupRank

In this section, we introduce our proposed training strategy as shown in Figure
3
for the
GroupRank
, which is designed to synthesize the flexibility of pointwise methods with the high performance of listwise approaches.

Our GroupRank overcome the limitations of existing methods by jointly scoring a group of documents in a single pass. Specifically, the model takes a query
q
and a set of
n
candidate documents
\{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}\}
as a unified input. Leveraging the contextual understanding capabilities of a Large Language Model, it performs comprehensive reasoning and comparison across all documents. Ultimately, it outputs a relevance score
s_{i}
for each document
d_{i}
, resulting in a set of scores
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
. Crucially, each score is an integer token generated by the LLM, ranging from 0 to 10, rather than a floating-point value. The final ranking is then determined based on these scores. This approach yields two primary advantages: 1)
Enhanced Global Perspective:
By introducing explicit comparisons among documents during the scoring process, the model gains a superior understanding of their relative merits. This enhances the accuracy of the global ranking. 2)
High Flexibility:
Unlike listwise methods, our GroupRank can process an arbitrary number of documents at inference time without being constrained by a fixed list size or requiring sliding window techniques. This design effectively balances performance and efficiency.

Fig. 3:
The two-stage training paradigm for the Group Wise Reranker is designed to combine the flexibility of pointwise methods with the high performance of listwise approaches. Within the reinforcement learning (RL) component, the reward function incorporates not only format reward and recall reward, but also incorporates our novel ranking reward and distribution reward, specifically designed for the GroupRank.

Cold Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The training process commences with a
Cold Start
phase. Pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) cannot inherently perform relevance scoring according to specific instructions, nor can they guarantee adherence to a required output format. Therefore, we employ Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to instill these foundational abilities into the model. The objective of this stage is twofold:

Heterogeneous reward based Reinforcement Learning
Following the SFT phase, we employ Reinforcement Learning (RL) to further enhance the scoring and ranking capabilities of the GroupRank. We design a novel
heterogeneous reward
function specifically tailored for the GroupRank. This function is designed to effectively guide and enhance the reranking process. Specifically, we utilize the GRPO algorithm for optimization.

During training, for each input
x
, we sample a set of
G
output sequences
\mathcal{G}=\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{G}\}
. Each sequence
y_{i}
receives a reward
r_{i}
, which is subsequently normalized within the group
\mathcal{G}
to compute an advantage value
\hat{A}_{i}
. The optimization objective can be formulated as follows:

1.

To enable the model to comprehend the relevance scoring task itself.

2.

To ensure the model strictly adheres to the specified format for outputting scores.

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{i=1}^{|G|}\frac{1}{|y_{i}|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_{i}|}\min\left(r_{i,t}(\theta)\hat{A}_{i,t},\right.

(7)

\displaystyle\left.\text{clip}\left(r_{i,t}(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon\right)\hat{A}_{i,t}\right)-\beta D_{\text{KL}},

\displaystyle r_{i,t}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{i,t}\mid x,y_{i,<t})},D_{\text{KL}}=D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}\parallel\pi_{\text{ref}})

where
\epsilon
and
\beta
are hyper-parameters.

Our proposed heterogeneous reward is composed of three primary components, designed to create a comprehensive training signal for the reranker.

Recall Reward
(
R_{\text{recall}}
)

This component directly evaluates the reranker from the perspective of retrieval. It ensures the relevant documents are retrieved and ranked as highly as possible within the list.

Groupwise Ranking Reward
(
R_{\text{rank}}
)

This component directly evaluates the quality of the ranked list generated by the model. To ensure a holistic assessment, we employ a weighted combination of several established ranking metrics:

•

NDCG (Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain):
Measures the overall quality of the ranked list, with a particular emphasis on the relevance of top-ranked documents.

•

RBO (Rank-Biased Overlap):
Evaluates the stability and similarity of the top portion of ranked lists, assigning greater weight to head-of-the-list agreement.

The ranking reward,
R_{\text{rank}}
, is a weighted aggregation of these metrics.

Groupwise Distribution Reward
(
R_{\text{dist}}
)

This component is designed to align the distribution of scores predicted by the model with a synthetic ground-truth score distribution, denoted as
S_{\text{gt}}
. The reward is calculated by measuring the discrepancy between the two distributions, specifically using the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. The rationale for introducing this reward is to preserve the magnitude information inherent in the scores. This enables the model not only to perform ranking but also to produce meaningful absolute scores that reflect a document’s relevance.

Let
S_{\text{preds}}
be the score distribution predicted by the model and
S_{\text{gt}}
be the ground-truth score distribution. The distribution reward is formulated as:

\displaystyle R_{\text{dist}}

\displaystyle=1-D_{\text{KL}}(S_{\text{gt}}\parallel S_{\text{preds}})

(8)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{i=1}^{N}S_{\text{gt}}(x_{i})\log\Bigg(\frac{S_{\text{gt}}(x_{i})}{S_{\text{preds}}(x_{i})}\Bigg)

where
x_{i}
represents a set of documents in the candidate set of size
N
.

Heterogeous Reward

The heterogeneous reward signal,
R_{\text{H}}
, is a weighted sum of these three components.

R_{\text{H}}=\alpha\cdot R_{\text{recall}}+\beta\cdot R_{\text{rank}}+\gamma\cdot R_{\text{dist}}

(9)

where
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
are hyperparameters that balance the relative importance of the ranking quality and score distribution alignment. We set the three of them to 0.2, 0.5, and 0.1 respectively.

Final Reward

To ensure proper output structure, we implement format rewards considering two kinds of formats: (1) output format which ensures the presence of both <reason> and <answer> tags, and (2) answer format that validates the content within <answer

tags adheres to the specified groupwise ranking json format (e.g., {“[1]”: 5, “[2]”: 0, …, “[19]”: 3, “[20]”: 7}).

R=\begin{cases}R^{\text{H}},&\text{Both Formats are Good,}\\
0,&\text{Only Output Format is Good,}\\
-1,&\text{Both Formats are Incorrect.}\end{cases}

(10)

By maximizing reward
R
, our heterogeneous reward-based GRPO algorithm guides the GroupRank to learn an optimal policy that excels at both generating high-quality rankings and accurately predicting the distribution of document relevance scores.

VII-C

Contribution of the Training Strategy: Synergy of SFT and RL

To investigate the necessity of our two-stage training paradigm (SFT followed by RL), we conduct experiments by ablating each stage.

w/o RL (SFT Only)

When the model is trained using only Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) without the subsequent Groupwise Ranking Policy Optimization (GRPO), its performance drops from 42.18 to 40.70. This indicates that while SFT on our high-quality synthetic data provides the model with a strong foundation in relevance judgment and score calibration, it is fundamentally a form of imitation learning. It lacks direct optimization for final ranking metrics such as NDCG. GRPO, through reinforcement learning, bridges this gap by fine-tuning the model end-to-end for the specific ranking objectives, thereby unlocking the model’s full reranking potential.

w/o SFT (RL Only)

Conversely, if we bypass the SFT stage and train the model from scratch directly with GRPO, the performance plummets to 38.17, only a 1.24-point improvement over the retriever-only baseline. This highlights the critical role of SFT as a “cold start” mechanism. RL exploration in a vast policy space is notoriously sample-inefficient and unstable. SFT initializes the model in a “favorable” policy region by leveraging high-quality data, which significantly reduces the difficulty of RL exploration and enables stable and efficient optimization from a much-improved starting point.

In summary, SFT and RL fulfill complementary and indispensable roles within our framework: SFT injects foundational knowledge and ensures stability, while RL performs targeted, goal-oriented refinement upon this foundation.

VII-D

Analysis of GRPO Reward Components

Our GRPO algorithm utilizes a composite reward function, which includes a
Ranking Reward
and a
Point Distribution Reward
. We ablate each component to analyze its impact.

w/o Ranking Reward

Removing ranking-based metrics (e.g., NDCG, Recall, RBO) from the reward function causes the model’s performance to decrease to 40.57. This demonstrates that merely incentivizing the model to produce a score distribution similar to the ground truth is insufficient. While the score distribution reward helps maintain a reasonable score scale, it does not explicitly encourage placing the most relevant documents at the top. The ranking reward, being directly tied to final evaluation metrics, is the primary driver for learning the core capability of “how to rank” and is crucial for achieving high ranking performance.

w/o Point Distribution Reward

Ablating the point distribution reward also leads to a performance decline, with the score dropping to 41.00. Although this drop is less severe than when ablating the ranking reward, it still validates the component’s value. With only a ranking reward, the model might resort to “reward hacking.” For instance, to maximize NDCG, it could learn to assign an extremely high score to the most relevant document and extremely low scores to all others, thereby destroying the intrinsic meaning and calibration of the scores. The Point Distribution Reward acts as an effective regularizer, compelling the model to optimize ranking while simultaneously preserving score granularity and scale. This prevents policy collapse and leads to a more robust and generalizable ranking model.

In conclusion, our ablation studies provide compelling evidence for the rationale and effectiveness of each design choice within the
GroupRank
framework. The results confirm that: (1) the two-stage SFT+RL training paradigm is essential for achieving optimal performance, with both stages being indispensable; and (2) the heterogeneous reward design in RL is highly effective, where the ranking reward is the main performance driver and the point distribution reward ensures model stability and score calibration. These components work in concert to achieve state-of-the-art performance on inference-intensive retrieval tasks.

Conclusion

This paper addresses the core challenges faced by rerankers within modern Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems
[
39
]
. We observe that conventional Pointwise methods, which evaluate documents independently, suffer from a “ranking myopia trap” due to the absence of inter-document comparisons. Conversely, Listwise methods, despite their global context awareness, are constrained by a “list rigidity dilemma”, rendering them inflexible for ranking large and variably-sized document sets.
To surmount these challenges, we present three primary contributions. First, we introduce a novel training framework based on reinforcement learning, termed the
GroupRank
. This approach harmonizes the flexibility of Pointwise methods with the performance of Listwise approaches. By enabling the model to jointly evaluate and score a
group
of documents at once, it introduces crucial inter-document comparisons to foster a global perspective while retaining the flexibility to handle an arbitrary number of documents. We employ a Groupwise Ranking Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm for training, guided by a heterogeneous reward function that incorporates recalling reward, ranking reward (e.g., NDCG, RBO), and a measure of distributional divergence.
Second, we design and implement an innovative, high-quality data synthesis pipeline. This pipeline uniquely leverages the capabilities of Large Language Models in both Pointwise (scoring) and Listwise (ranking) annotation modes. By employing a weighted fusion and a
-\log(\text{rank})
transformation, we generate training data that possesses both precise relevance scores and a robust ranking order, laying a solid foundation for end-to-end training of high-performance rerankers.
Finally, extensive experimental results validate the efficacy of our approach. On challenging, reasoning-intensive retrieval benchmarks such as BRIGHT and R2MED, our GroupRank achieves new State-of-the-Art (SOTA) performance, demonstrating significant improvements on key metrics like NDCG@10 over previous leading models. Furthermore, our model exhibits strong competitiveness on traditional retrieval tasks. These results not only affirm the superiority of the Groupwise architecture but also underscore the value of our data synthesis strategy.
In summary, the holistic optimization solution proposed in this study, particularly the GroupRank, presents an effective and scalable new paradigm for constructing more accurate and efficient RAG systems. Looking ahead, we plan to explore more cost-effective data synthesis methods and investigate the generalization of the Groupwise principle to a broader range of information retrieval and recommendation tasks.

IX
Future work

For future work, we plan to explore a progressive reinforcement learning paradigm for RAG systems. First, the GroupRank would be trained incrementally, starting with simpler ranking tasks and gradually tackling more complex, reasoning-intensive document sets to better capture inter-document relationships. Next, the reranker’s outputs would guide the generative model in a collaborative, joint RL framework, aligning retrieval quality with generation quality. This approach aims to create an end-to-end RL-driven system that progressively improves both ranking and generation.

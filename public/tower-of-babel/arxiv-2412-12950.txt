Nonlocal Choquard equations involving critical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev exponent: the effect of the topology of the domain


Abstract
We apply a topological method to prove existence of positive solutions for the nonlineair Choquard equation with upper critical exponent in the sense of Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inquality on bounded domains having nontrivial homology group. Keywords: Choquard equation, Critical nonlinearity, Variational calculus, Singular Homology, Topological method. MSC 2020. 35A16, 35J20, 35J60, 55N10

Introduction

During the last decades, the Choquard equation

\displaystyle-\Delta\,u+V(x)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|u(y)\right|^{q}}{\left|x-y\right|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\right)\left|u\right|^{q-2}u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.1)

where
n\geq 3
,
0<\mu<n
and
\frac{2n-\mu}{n}\leq q\leq\frac{2n-\mu}{n-2}
, has attracted the attention and the interest of a lot of researchers, since it appears in the modeling of various physical phenomena, such as quantum mechanics of a polaron at rest
[
27
]
, Selfgravitatinal collapse of a quantum mechanical wave function
[
26
]
, modeling an electron trapped in its own hole as an approximation to Hartree-Fock theory of one component plasma
[
9
]
and so on.

Following standard variational theory, the solutions of the Choquard equation are the critical points of the Euler-Lagrange functional

\displaystyle I(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|u(y)\right|^{q}\left|u(x)\right|^{q}}{\left|x-y\right|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Recall that by the celebrate Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality see
[
8
]
and
[
10
]
, the second term of
I(u)
is well defined on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if and only if

\displaystyle 2_{\mu}:=\frac{2n-\mu}{n}

\displaystyle\leq q\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2n-\mu}{n-2}:=2_{\mu}^{\ast}.

In this case, the functional
I
is continuously differentiable on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The constants
2_{\mu}
and
{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}
are termed, respectively, as the lower critical exponent and the upper critical exponent in the sense of Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev. It is remarkable that the Choquard equation is a nonlinear problem with superlinear nonlinearity, since
2\mu>1
,
\forall\,\mu\in(0,n)
, and it is a nonlocal problem. The nonlocal term which appears in the nonlinearity makes the problem particularly difficult. For
n=3
,
q=2
,
\mu=1
and
V
is a positive constant, Lieb
[
9
]
obtained the existence of a unique minimizing positive solution (up to translations). Under some restrictions on
V(x)
, Lions
[
25
]
proved the existence of a sequence of radially solutions. For
n\geq 3
,
\mu\in(0,n)
,
V=1
, and suitable range of
q
, Moros-Van Schaftingen
[
21
]
,
[
19
]
proved the existence of a nontrivial solution of (
1.1
), if and only if
2\mu<q<{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}
. Their result has obtained by using a Pohozaev type identity and minimizing arguments. Ma-Zhao
[
23
]
studied the positive and regularity of the minimizing solutions. For
q=2_{\mu}
, the lower critical exponent and a nonconstant potential
V
, Moros-Van Schaftingen
[
20
]
and Cassani-Schaftingen-Zhang
[
5
]
studied the existence problem of positive solutions of equation (
1.1
). they provided sufficient and necessary conditions on the potential
V(x)
to obtain minimizing solutions.

For the upper critical exponent
q={2}_{\mu}^{\ast}
and
V=0
, the Choquard equation reduces to

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|u(y)\right|^{{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{\left|x-y\right|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\right)\left|u\right|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}-2}u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.2)

Using moving plane techniques and analyzes of the associated integral system, Du-Yang
[
7
]
and Guo-Hu-Peng-Shuai
[
15
]
proved a uniqueness classification of the positive solutions of (
1.2
). Precisely, they proved that the positive solutions of (
1.2
) have to be of the form

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{(a,\lambda)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n(n-2))^{\frac{n-2}{4}}C_{n,\mu}^{\frac{2-n}{2(n-\mu+2)}}S^{\frac{(n-\mu)(2-n)}{4(n-\mu+2)}}\delta_{(a,\lambda)}(x),\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where

\displaystyle C_{n,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{\frac{\mu}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-\mu}{2})}{\Gamma(n-\frac{\mu}{2})}(\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})})^{\frac{n-\mu}{n}},\quad\Gamma(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t^{1-s}}\mathrm{d}t,\,s>0,

is the Gamma function,
S
is the best constant of Sobolev and for
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\delta_{(a,\lambda)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{(1+\lambda^{2}\left|x-a\right|^{2})^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\cdot

In this paper we restrict our attention to problem (
1.2
) on bounded domains
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
. we are interested to study the existence problem of positive solutions of the following nonlocal Choquard equation with upper critical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev exponent

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u(y)\right|^{{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{\left|x-y\right|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\right)\left|u\right|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\;\;u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(1.3)

where
\mu\in(0,n)
and
2_{\mu}^{\ast}=\frac{2n-\mu}{n-2}
.

Since
{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}
is critical, a lack of compactness occurs in the sense that the associated Euler Lagrange functional does not satisfy the Palais-Smale condition. This makes the problem of finding positive solutions more challenging. As the celebrate critical elliptic local equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|u\right|^{2^{\ast}-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(1.4)

where

is the critical Sobolev exponent, a concentration compactness principle
[

]
,
[

]
and Bahri-Coron representations
[

]
,
[

]
play an important role in solving nonlinear problems of a non compact nature.

In
[

]

1.3
) where
\Omega
is a bounded annular-type domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n},\;\;n\geq 3
. They proved a global compactness Lemma and used a variational method to show existence of positive solutions when the inner hole of
\Omega
is sufficiently small. Their result is motivated by the celebrate paper of Coron
[

) by adding a linear term
"\lambda u,\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R}"
to the Choquard nonlinearity of (
1.3
). They proved existence results for a suitable range of
\lambda
and

non-existence result in the case of
\lambda\leq 0
and
\Omega
is star shaped domain with respect to the origin. In the papers
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
,
[

]
and
[
18
]

[

Pushing more the resemblance of the local equation (
1.4
) and the nonlocal Choquard equation (
1.3
), we are led to investigate the effect of the domain’s topology on the existence results of problem (
1.3
). In the pioneering paper
[
2
]
, Bahri-Coron introduced a powerful topological argument which leads to prove the existence of positive solutions of equation (
1.4
) when
\Omega
has a nontrivial homology group. In this paper we prove that the topological approach of Bahri-Coron can be successfully adopt to study the existence problem of positive solutions of the Choquard equation (
1.3
). Because of its nonlocal nature, additional difficulties arise in extending such a topological method to the present framework. This requires changes in the construction and novelties in the proof.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer. We denote
H_{k}(\Omega)
the singular homology group of
\Omega
of dimension
k
with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-coefficients. We shall prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1

Let
n\geq 3
and let
\Omega
be a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If there exists a positive integer
k_{0}
such that
H_{k_{0}}(\Omega)
is not null, then problem (
1.3
) has at least a positive solution.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be the subject of Section
3
of this paper. We summarize the idea of the proof in the following three steps.

For a pair of topological spaces
(X,Y)
,
Y\subset X
, we denote
H_{\ell}(X,Y)
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the relative homology of
(X,Y)
.

\text{Step}\;1.

Under the assumption that
H_{k_{0}}(\Omega)
is not trivial, we prove that there exist two sequences of topological pairs
(B_{p},B_{p-1})
and
(E_{p},F_{p})
,
p\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
, a sequence of non-zero homology classes
(\omega_{p})
such that

\omega_{p}\in H_{\ell}(B_{p},B_{p-1})
, for any
p
and a sequence of relative homology homomorphisms

(\phi_{p})_{\ell}\colon H_{\ell}(B_{p},B_{p-1})\to H_{\ell}(E_{p},F_{p})

such that the first homomorphism of the sequence satisfies

\displaystyle(\phi_{1})_{\ell}(\omega_{1})

\displaystyle\neq

\displaystyle 0.

(1.5)

\text{Step}\;2.

If we suppose that problem (
1.3
) has no positive solution, we prove that

for any
p\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
,

\displaystyle(\phi_{p})_{\ell}(\omega_{p})\neq 0\,

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\,(\phi_{p+1})_{\ell}(\omega_{p+1})\neq 0.

(1.6)

\text{Step}\;3.

We prove the existence of a positive large integer
p_{0}
such that

\displaystyle(\phi_{p_{0}})_{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\text{ for any }\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.7)

This concludes the proof.

In order to achieve the above steps, we state in the next Section the variational structure associated to problem (
1.3
) including some preparatory results. We first change the Euler-Lagrange functional
I
and work with a more convenient functional
J
. We study the behavior of sequences failing the Palais-Smale condition and Parametrize the neighborhoods of critical points at infinity
[
3
]
. Finally, we prove asymptotic estimates and expand the Euler-Lagrange functional
J
in these neighborhoods. Particularly, we determine exactly the levels of
J
where the Palais-Smale condition does not hold.

In the following we denote,
\left<u,v\right>=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v\mathrm{d}x
the inner product of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\left|u\right|_{1,\Omega}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
the associated norm and the
\|u\|_{L^{t}}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{t}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}
norm of
L^{t}(\Omega)
.

Variational results and asymptotic expansions

We start this Section by recalling the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality which play a fundamental role in defining the variational approach to critical problems with nonlocal Choquard nonlinearities.

Lemma 2.1

[
8
]
,
[
10
]
. For
t_{1},\;t_{2}>1
, such that
\frac{1}{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{t_{2}}=\frac{2n-\mu}{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)g(x)}{\left|x-y\right|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{t_{1}}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{t_{2}}},\;\forall(f,g)\in L^{t_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{t_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(2.1)

Here

is a positive constant independent of

and

.

Particularly, if

equals to

(up to a multiplicative constant) and

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(\lambda^{2}+\left|x-a\right|^{2})^{\frac{\mu-2n}{2}},

where

,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
and

Let us observe that under the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left|u(y)\right|^{q}\left|u(x)\right|^{q}}{\left|x-y\right|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

is well defined, if

such that
\frac{2}{t}=\frac{2n-\mu}{n}
. Thus, by Sobolev embedding, integral (

In the special case of the upper critical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev exponent

and for an open set

of

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{HL}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u(y)\right|^{{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}}\left|u(x)\right|^{{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{\left|x-y\right|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2\,{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}}},\;\;u\in L^{2^{\ast}}(\Omega),

is a norm on

, where

. In this case the best constant

defined by

\displaystyle S_{HL}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left|u\right|_{1\Omega}}{\left\|u\right\|_{HL}},

is independent of

and it is never achieved except

, see
[

. In addition, the unique minimizers of

are unique and of the form

, where

. Here

\displaystyle\delta_{(a,\lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{(1+\lambda^{2}\left|x-a\right|^{2})^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\;,\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.4)

As a consequence of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, the functional

\displaystyle I(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left|u\right|_{1\Omega}^{2}-\frac{1}{2\,{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}}\left\|u\right\|_{HL}^{2\,{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}},

and it is of class

. It is straightforward to see that for any

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta u-\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u(y)\right|^{{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{\left|x-y\right|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\right)\left|u\right|^{{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}-2}u.

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\geq 0}I(\lambda u).

Lemma 2.2

For any

.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda-\lambda^{2\,{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}-1}\left\|u\right\|_{HL}^{2\,{2}_{\mu}^{\ast}}.

\Box

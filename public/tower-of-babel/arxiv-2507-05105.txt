Tighter Inequalities for A \displaystyle A -Numerical Radii of Operator Matrices and Their Applications
M.H.M. Rashid

Abstract
This paper establishes new upper bounds for the A \displaystyle A -numerical radius of operator matrices in semi-Hilbertian spaces by leveraging the A \displaystyle A -Buzano inequality and developing refined techniques for operator matrices. We present several sharp inequalities that generalize and improve existing results, including novel bounds for 2 × 2 \displaystyle 2\times 2 operator matrices involving A \displaystyle A -absolute value operators and mixed Schwarz-type inequalities, refined power inequalities relating A \displaystyle A -numerical radius to operator norms with optimal parameter selection, and a unified framework extending classical numerical radius inequalities to semi-Hilbertian spaces. The results are supported by detailed examples demonstrating their sharpness, including cases of equality, and we investigate their relationship to classical numerical radius inequalities, showing how our framework provides tighter estimates through A \displaystyle A -operator seminorms and A \displaystyle A -adjoint techniques. These theoretical advances have applications in quantum mechanics (operator bounds for quantum channels), partial differential equations (stability analysis of discretized operators), and control theory (hybrid system energy management). Our work contributes to operator theory in semi-Hilbertian spaces by providing new tools for analyzing operator matrices through A \displaystyle A -numerical radius inequalities, with particular emphasis on the interplay between operator structure and the semi-inner product induced by positive operators.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear operators on a complex Hilbert space
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, equipped with the operator norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|
. Throughout this paper,
\displaystyle I
and
\displaystyle O
represent the identity operator and zero operator on
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
, respectively. For any
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
\displaystyle\mathrm{ran}(T)
denotes the range of
\displaystyle T
, and
\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{ran}(T)}
signifies the norm closure of
\displaystyle\mathrm{ran}(T)
.

We focus on the setting where
\displaystyle A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a positive operator, and
\displaystyle P
is the orthogonal projection onto
\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{ran}(A)}
. Recall that a positive operator
\displaystyle A
(denoted
\displaystyle A\geq 0
) satisfies
\displaystyle\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}
, and is strictly positive (
\displaystyle A>0
) if
\displaystyle\langle Ax,x\rangle>0
for all non-zero
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}
.
The cone of all positive (semidefinite) operators is given by

\mathcal{B}^{+}(\mathcal{H})=\{A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):\left<Ax,x\right>\geq 0\,\mbox{for all $\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}$}\}.

Every
\displaystyle A\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(\mathcal{H})
defines the following positive semidefinite sesquilinear form:

{\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>}_{A}:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C},(x,y)\rightarrowtail{\left<x,y\right>}_{A}={\left<Ax,y\right>}_{A}.

For an
\displaystyle n
-fold direct sum of Hilbert spaces, we consider the block diagonal operator

\mathbb{A}=\begin{bmatrix}A&O&\cdots&O\\
O&A&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&O\\
O&\cdots&O&A\end{bmatrix},

which is positive (strictly positive) when
\displaystyle A
is positive (strictly positive). The operators
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}
will remain fixed throughout our discussion.

The positive operator
\displaystyle A
induces a semi-inner product
\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A}:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
\displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle_{A}=\langle Ax,y\rangle
, which gives rise to the seminorm
\displaystyle\|x\|_{A}=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle_{A}}
. For a semi-Hilbertian space
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
, the following
\displaystyle A
-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality holds:

\left|{\left<x,y\right>}_{A}\right|\leq{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}{\left\|y\right\|}_{A},\,\,\left(x,y\in\mathcal{H}\right).

(1)

It is easy to verify that
\displaystyle{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}=0
if and only if
\displaystyle x\in\ker(A)
. Thus

\displaystyle{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}
is a norm if and only if
\displaystyle A
is injective, and that
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H},{\left\|\cdot\right\|}_{A}
is complete if and only if
the range of
\displaystyle A
is closed in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
.

For
\displaystyle x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}
with
\displaystyle{\left\|z\right\|}_{A}=1
, very recently in
Kittaneh and Zamani 2023

it has been shown that

\left|{\left<x,z\right>}_{A}{\left<z,y\right>}_{A}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\left|\alpha\right|}\left(\max\left\{1,\left|\alpha-1\right|\right\}{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}{\left\|y\right\|}_{A}+\left|{\left<x,y\right>}_{A}\right|\right)

(2)

for any
\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. In particular, by taking
\displaystyle\alpha=2
, we have

\left|{\left<x,z\right>}_{A}{\left<z,y\right>}_{A}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\left\|x\right\|}_{A}{\left\|y\right\|}_{A}+\left|{\left<x,y\right>}_{A}\right|\right).

(3)

We remark that the
\displaystyle A
-Buzano inequality is an extension of the
\displaystyle A
-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in a semi-Hilbertian space

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H},{\left\|\cdot\right\|}_{A})
(see
Kittaneh and Zamani 2023
).

For operators
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we define the
\displaystyle A
-operator seminorm:

\|T\|_{A}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\overline{\mathrm{ran}(A)}\\
x\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|Tx\|_{A}}{\|x\|_{A}}=\inf\{c>0:\|Tx\|_{A}\leq c\|x\|_{A}\text{ for all }x\in\overline{\mathrm{ran}(A)}\}.

The collection of operators with finite
\displaystyle A
-seminorm is denoted by
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{A}(\mathcal{H})
. Notably,
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{A}(\mathcal{H})
is not generally a subalgebra of
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, and
\displaystyle\|T\|_{A}=0
if and only if
\displaystyle ATA=0
.

An operator
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said to admit an
\displaystyle A
-adjoint if there exists
\displaystyle S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
satisfying
\displaystyle\langle Tx,y\rangle_{A}=\langle x,Sy\rangle_{A}
for all
\displaystyle x,y\in\mathcal{H}
, or equivalently,
\displaystyle AS=T^{*}A
. The set of all such operators is denoted by
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
. When it exists, the distinguished
\displaystyle A
-adjoint is given by
\displaystyle T^{\sharp_{A}}=A^{\dagger}T^{*}A
, where
\displaystyle A^{\dagger}
is the Moore-Penrose inverse of
\displaystyle A
.

Recall that the set of all operators admitting
\displaystyle A^{1/2}
-adjoints is denoted by
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A^{1/2}}(\mathcal{H})
.
An application of Douglas’ theorem reveals that this space can be characterized as:

\mathcal{B}_{A^{1/2}}(\mathcal{H})=\{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):\exists\lambda>0\text{ such that }{\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A}\leq\lambda{\left\|x\right\|}_{A},\forall x\in\mathcal{H}\}.

The semi-inner product
\displaystyle{\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>}_{A}
induces a natural semi-norm on
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A^{1/2}}(\mathcal{H})
, which can be expressed in several equivalent ways:

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\overline{ran(A)}\\
x\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{{\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A}}{{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}}=\sup\{{\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A}:x\in\mathcal{H},{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}=1\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sup\{\left|{\left<Tx,y\right>}_{A}\right|:x,y\in\mathcal{H},{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}={\left\|y\right\|}_{A}=1\}<\infty.

The spaces
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A^{1/2}}(\mathcal{H})
form important subalgebras of
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with the inclusion relation
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{A^{1/2}}(\mathcal{H})
(see

Arias et al. 2008b
), satisfying fundamental properties including:
\displaystyle{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}={\left\|\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}

\displaystyle{\left\|\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}T\right\|}_{A}={\left\|T\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}={\left\|\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|}_{A}^{2}={\left\|T\right\|}_{A}^{2}
; and for
\displaystyle T,S\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
,
\displaystyle(TS)^{\sharp_{A}}=\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}
,
\displaystyle{\left\|Tx\right\|}_{A}\leq{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}
,
\displaystyle{\left\|TS\right\|}_{A}\leq{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}{\left\|S\right\|}_{A}
, and
\displaystyle{\left\|T+S\right\|}_{A}\leq{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}+{\left\|S\right\|}_{A}
. An operator
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is called
\displaystyle A
-selfadjoint if
\displaystyle AT
is selfadjoint (in which case
\displaystyle{\left\|T\right\|}_{A}=\sup\{\left|{\left<Tx,x\right>}_{A}\right|:x\in\mathcal{H},{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}=1\}

Feki
), and
\displaystyle A
-positive if
\displaystyle AT
is positive (implying
\displaystyle A
-selfadjointness), with the operators
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}T
and
\displaystyle T\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}
being particularly important as they are always
\displaystyle A
-positive for any
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
.

Thus, in viewing of these
facts, we are able to define the
\displaystyle A
-absolute value operator of
\displaystyle T
, such as
\displaystyle|T|_{A}^{2}=A\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}T
, which is positive
operator, and we write
\displaystyle|T|_{A}=\left(A\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}T\right)^{1/2}
. This property is called the uniqueness of the square root of

\displaystyle A
-positive operators. We note that, the
\displaystyle A
-absolute value operator is self-adjoint if
\displaystyle T
is
\displaystyle A
-self-adjoint and
\displaystyle A

\displaystyle T

The
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius of an operator
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})

w_{A}(T)=\sup\{|\langle Tx,x\rangle_{A}|:x\in\mathcal{H},\|x\|_{A}=1\},

and plays a fundamental role in our investigation. It is well-known that

is equivalent to the
\displaystyle A

\frac{1}{2}\|T\|_{A}\leq w_{A}(T)\leq\|T\|_{A}.

The corresponding version of Schwarz inequality for
\displaystyle A
-positive operators reads that if
\displaystyle T

\displaystyle A
-positive

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})

\left|{\left<Tx,y\right>}_{A}\right|^{2}\leq{\left<Tx,x\right>}_{A}{\left<Ty,y\right>}_{A}.

(4)

for any vectors

For

\left|{\left<x,z\right>}_{A}{\left<z,y\right>}_{A}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\left|\alpha\right|}\left(\max\left\{1,\left|\alpha-1\right|\right\}{\left\|x\right\|}_{A}{\left\|y\right\|}_{A}+\left|{\left<x,y\right>}_{A}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\left\{0\right\}

\left|{\left<x,z\right>}_{A}{\left<z,y\right>}_{A}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\left\|x\right\|}_{A}{\left\|y\right\|}_{A}+\left|{\left<x,y\right>}_{A}\right|\right).

2
Main Results

In this section, we present our main results concerning
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius inequalities for operator matrices. We begin by establishing several key lemmas that will be instrumental in proving our theorems.

2.1
Preliminary Lemmas

The following lemmas provide fundamental inequalities and properties in semi-Hilbertian spaces:

Lemma 2.1

(Mixed Schwarz Inequality)
.

Let
\displaystyle A\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(\mathcal{H})
be a positive operator. If
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
commutes with
\displaystyle A
(i.e.,
\displaystyle TA=AT
), and
\displaystyle f
,
\displaystyle g
are nonnegative continuous functions on
\displaystyle[0,\infty)
satisfying
\displaystyle f(t)g(t)=t
for all
\displaystyle t\geq 0
, then for all
\displaystyle x,y\in\mathcal{H}
:

\left|\langle Tx,y\rangle_{A}\right|\leq\left\|f(|T|_{A})x\right\|_{A}\left\|g(|T^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A})y\right\|_{A}.

Lemma 2.2

(Power Inequality)
.

Under the same assumptions as Lemma
2.1
, for any
\displaystyle\alpha\in[0,1]
, we have:

\left|\langle Tx,y\rangle_{A}\right|\leq\langle|T|_{A}^{2\alpha}x,x\rangle_{A}^{1/2}\langle|T^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}y,y\rangle_{A}^{1/2}.

Lemma 2.3

(Hölder-McCarthy Inequality)
.

For
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
and
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{H}
with
\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|_{A}=1
:

1.

If
\displaystyle T
is
\displaystyle A
-positive and
\displaystyle r\geq 1
, then
\displaystyle\langle Tx,x\rangle_{A}^{r}\leq\langle T^{r}x,x\rangle_{A}
.

2.

If
\displaystyle T
is
\displaystyle A
-positive and
\displaystyle 0\leq r\leq 1
, then
\displaystyle\langle T^{r}x,x\rangle_{A}\leq\langle Tx,x\rangle_{A}^{r}
.

Lemma 2.4

(Jensen-Type Inequality)
.

For
\displaystyle a,b>0
and
\displaystyle\alpha\in[0,1]
, the following inequalities hold for any
\displaystyle r\geq 1
:

a^{\alpha}b^{1-\alpha}\leq\alpha a+(1-\alpha)b\leq\left(\alpha a^{r}+(1-\alpha)b^{r}\right)^{1/r}.

Lemma 2.5

(Bohr’s Inequality)
.

For positive real numbers
\displaystyle a_{i}
(
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,n
) and
\displaystyle r\geq 1
:

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right)^{r}\leq n^{r-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{r}.

2.2
Operator Matrix Results

The following lemma establishes fundamental properties of
\displaystyle 2\times 2
operator matrices in semi-Hilbertian spaces:

Lemma 2.6

(Operator Matrix Properties)
.

Let
\displaystyle X,Y\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
. Then:

1.

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{bmatrix}X&0\\
0&Y\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{\mathbb{A}}=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&X\\
Y&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{\mathbb{A}}=\max\{\left\|X\right\|_{A},\left\|Y\right\|_{A}\}
.

2.

\displaystyle w_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}X&0\\
0&Y\end{bmatrix}\right)=\max\{w_{A}(X),w_{A}(Y)\}
.

3.

\displaystyle w_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&X\\
Y&0\end{bmatrix}\right)=w_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&Y\\
X&0\end{bmatrix}\right)
.

4.

\displaystyle w_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}X&Y\\
Y&X\end{bmatrix}\right)=\max\{w_{A}(X+Y),w_{A}(X-Y)\}
.

5.

For any
\displaystyle\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\displaystyle w_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&X\\
e^{i\theta}Y&0\end{bmatrix}\right)=w_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&X\\
Y&0\end{bmatrix}\right)
.

2.3
Main Theorems

Building upon these lemmas, we now present our main results:

Theorem 2.7
.

For any operators
\displaystyle X,Y\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
and
\displaystyle\alpha\in[0,1]
, we have:

w_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&X\\
Y&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\||X|_{A}^{2\alpha}\right\|+\left\||Y^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}\right\|\right).

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbf{T}=\begin{bmatrix}0&X\\
Y&0\end{bmatrix}
and let
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
be a unit vector with respect to the
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}
-seminorm, i.e.,
\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbb{A}}=1
. This implies:

\|x_{1}\|_{A}^{2}+\|x_{2}\|_{A}^{2}=1.

The
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}
-numerical radius of
\displaystyle\mathbf{T}
is given by:

w_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{T})=\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbb{A}}=1}\left|\left\langle\mathbf{T}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{A}}\right|=\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbb{A}}=1}\left|\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}Xx_{2}\\
Yx_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{A}}\right|.

This simplifies to:

w_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{T})=\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbb{A}}=1}\left|\langle Xx_{2},x_{1}\rangle_{A}+\langle Yx_{1},x_{2}\rangle_{A}\right|.

Applying the triangle inequality and Lemma
2.2
(Power Inequality), we obtain:

\left|\langle Xx_{2},x_{1}\rangle_{A}\right|\leq\langle|X|_{A}^{2\alpha}x_{2},x_{2}\rangle_{A}^{1/2}\langle|X^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}x_{1},x_{1}\rangle_{A}^{1/2},

\left|\langle Yx_{1},x_{2}\rangle_{A}\right|\leq\langle|Y|_{A}^{2\alpha}x_{1},x_{1}\rangle_{A}^{1/2}\langle|Y^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}x_{2},x_{2}\rangle_{A}^{1/2}.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}
-seminorm and the fact that
\displaystyle\|x_{1}\|_{A}^{2}+\|x_{2}\|_{A}^{2}=1
, we have:

w_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{T})\leq\sup_{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbb{A}}=1}\left(\langle|X|_{A}^{2\alpha}x_{2},x_{2}\rangle_{A}^{1/2}\langle|X^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}x_{1},x_{1}\rangle_{A}^{1/2}+\langle|Y|_{A}^{2\alpha}x_{1},x_{1}\rangle_{A}^{1/2}\langle|Y^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}x_{2},x_{2}\rangle_{A}^{1/2}\right).

By the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and the properties of the operator norm, we obtain:

w_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{T})\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\||X|_{A}^{2\alpha}\|_{A}+\||Y^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}\|_{A}\right).

Finally, observing that
\displaystyle\||T^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}\|_{A}=\||T|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}\|_{A}
for any
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
, we conclude:

w_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&X\\
Y&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\||X|_{A}^{2\alpha}\|_{A}+\||Y^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}\|_{A}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Under the same assumptions as Theorem
2.7
, the following sharper inequality holds:

w_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&X\\
Y&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\leq\inf_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\left(\frac{\left\|X\right\|_{A}^{2\alpha}+\left\|Y^{\sharp_{A}}\right\|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}}{2}\right).

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbf{T}=\begin{bmatrix}0&X\\
Y&0\end{bmatrix}
where
\displaystyle X,Y\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
. From Theorem
2.7
, we have for any
\displaystyle\alpha\in[0,1]
:

w_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{T})\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\||X|_{A}^{2\alpha}\|_{A}+\||Y^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}\|_{A}\right).

We aim to show that:

w_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{T})\leq\inf_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\left(\frac{\|X\|_{A}^{2\alpha}+\|Y^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}}{2}\right).

First, observe that for any
\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H})
and
\displaystyle r\geq 0
, we have:

\||T|_{A}^{r}\|_{A}=\|T\|_{A}^{r}.

This follows from the spectral mapping theorem and the definition of the
\displaystyle A
-operator seminorm.

Applying this to our inequality gives:

w_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{T})\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|X\|_{A}^{2\alpha}+\|Y^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}\right).

To show this is indeed a refinement, consider the function:

f(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|X\|_{A}^{2\alpha}+\|Y^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}\right).

The minimum of
\displaystyle f(\alpha)
occurs when the derivative vanishes. Calculating the derivative:

f^{\prime}(\alpha)=\|X\|_{A}^{2\alpha}\ln\|X\|_{A}-\|Y^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}\ln\|Y^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}.

Setting
\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\alpha)=0
yields the critical point:

\alpha_{0}=\frac{\ln\left(\frac{\ln\|Y^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}}{\ln\|X\|_{A}}\right)+2\ln\|Y^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}}{2(\ln\|X\|_{A}+\ln\|Y^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A})}.

Substituting
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}
back into
\displaystyle f(\alpha)
gives the minimal value. However, for our purposes, it suffices to note that the infimum exists and is attained at some
\displaystyle\alpha\in[0,1]
, leading to:

w_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{T})\leq\inf_{\alpha\in[0,1]}f(\alpha)=\inf_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\left(\frac{\|X\|_{A}^{2\alpha}+\|Y^{\sharp_{A}}\|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}}{2}\right).

This completes the proof of the refined inequality. The result is indeed sharper than Theorem
2.7
as it provides the optimal choice of
\displaystyle\alpha
to minimize the upper bound.

∎

These results generalize and improve upon existing numerical radius inequalities in the literature, particularly for operator matrices in semi-Hilbertian spaces.

Example 2.9
.

\|Y\|_{A}=\sup_{\|x\|_{A}=1}\|Yx\|_{A}=\sup_{\|x\|_{A}=1}\sqrt{4|x_{1}|^{2}+|x_{2}|^{2}}=2

Step 3: Apply Theorem 2.8

The refined inequality gives:

w_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&X\\
Y&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\leq\inf_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\frac{2^{2\alpha}+2^{2(1-\alpha)}}{2}

Step 4: Optimize the Bound

f(\alpha)=\frac{4^{\alpha}+4^{1-\alpha}}{2}

The minimum occurs at

Theorem 2.10
.

\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2})

Remark 2.26
.

Corollary

provides a significant generalization and improvement of several known results in the literature concerning
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius inequalities:

1.

unifies and extends several other results including:

•

The
\displaystyle r=1

The

The general form encompasses and refines the inequalities obtained by Rout et al.

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}=\begin{bmatrix}S_{2}^{\sharp_{A}}S_{2}T_{2}^{\sharp_{A}}T_{2}&O\\
O&S_{1}^{\sharp_{A}}S_{1}T_{1}^{\sharp_{A}}T_{1}\\
\end{bmatrix}
.

Now, let
\displaystyle z\in\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
with
\displaystyle{\left\|z\right\|}_{A}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|{\left<\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}\right|^{4}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|{\left<\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}{\left<z,\mathbb{Z}z\right>}_{A}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{4}{\left\|\mathbb{W}z\right\|}_{A}^{2}{\left\|\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}z\right\|}_{A}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}{\left\|\mathbb{W}z\right\|}_{A}{\left\|\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}z\right\|}_{A}\left|{\left<\mathbb{W}z,\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}z\right>}_{A}\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mbox{by Lemma \ref{Buzano}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{4}{\left\|\mathbb{W}z\right\|}_{A}^{2r}{\left\|\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}z\right\|}_{A}^{2r}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}{\left\|\mathbb{W}z\right\|}_{A}^{r}{\left\|\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}z\right\|}_{A}^{r}\left|{\left<\mathbb{W}z,\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}z\right>}_{A}\right|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mbox{by Lemma \ref{lem:jensen}}\right)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|{\left<\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}\right|^{4r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{4}\left(\sqrt{{\left<\mathbb{W}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}^{r}{\left<\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}z,z\right>}_{A}^{r}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}\sqrt{{\left<\mathbb{W}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}^{r}{\left<\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}z,z\right>}_{A}^{r}}\left|{\left<\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}\left({\left<\mathbb{W}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}^{r}+{\left<\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}z,z\right>}_{A}^{r}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}\left({\left<\mathbb{W}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}^{r}+{\left<\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}z,z\right>}_{A}^{r}\right)\left|{\left<\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mbox{by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}\left({\left<\left(\mathbb{W}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{W}\right)^{r}z,z\right>}_{A}+{\left<\left(\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}\right)^{r}z,z\right>}_{A}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}\left({\left<\left(\mathbb{W}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{W}\right)^{r}z,z\right>}_{A}+{\left<\left(\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp_{A}}\right)^{r}z,z\right>}_{A}\right)\left|{\left<\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mbox{by Lemma \ref{lem:holder_mccarthy}}\right).

By replacing
\displaystyle\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}
in the above inequality, and using the fact that
\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}\right)^{\sharp_{A}}=\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}
and

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}\right)^{\sharp_{A}}=\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}
. Thus, it can be deduced that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|{\left<\mathbb{W}z,z\right>}_{A}{\left<z,\mathbb{Z}z\right>}_{A}\right|^{2r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}\left({\left<\left(\left(\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}\right)^{2r}+\left(\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}\right)^{2r}\right)z,z\right>}_{A}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}{\left<{\left<\left(\left(\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}\right)^{2r}+\left(\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}\right)^{2r}\right)z,z\right>}_{A}\right>}_{A}\left|{\left<\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}z,z\right>}_{A}\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}{\left\|\left(\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}\right)^{2r}+\left(\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}\right)^{2r}\right\|}_{A}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}{\left\|\left(\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}\right)^{2r}+\left(\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}\right)^{2r}\right\|}_{A}\omega_{A}^{r}\left(\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}\right).

In addition, by utilizing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|{\left<z,\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}z\right>}_{A}\right|^{4r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|{\left<\mathbb{T}z,\mathbb{S}z\right>}_{A}\right|^{4r}\leq{\left\|\mathbb{T}z\right\|}_{A}^{4r}{\left\|\mathbb{S}z\right\|}_{A}^{4r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\left<\mathbb{T}z,\mathbb{T}z\right>}_{A}^{2r}{\left<\mathbb{S}z,\mathbb{S}z\right>}_{A}^{2r}=\left|{\left<\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}z,z\right>}_{A}{\left<\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{S}z,z\right>}_{A}\right|^{2r}.

Combining above two inequalities, we can obtain

\left|{\left<z,\mathbb{S}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}z\right>}_{A}\right|^{4r}\leq\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}\max\{\phi_{r}^{2},\psi_{r}^{2}\}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}\max\{\phi_{r},\psi_{r}\}\max\{\rho_{r},\theta_{r}\}.

Taking the supremum over all vectors
\displaystyle z\in\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
with
\displaystyle{\left\|z\right\|}_{A}=1
, we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{1}}{16}\left({\left<\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}\,z,\,z\right>}_{A}^{r}+{\left<\mathbb{T}\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\,z,\,z\right>}_{A}^{r}\right)^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{8}\left({\left<\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\mathbb{T}\,z,\,z\right>}_{A}^{r}+{\left<\mathbb{T}\mathbb{T}^{\sharp_{A}}\,z,\,z\right>}_{A}^{r}\right)\left|{\left<\mathbb{T}^{2}\,z,\,z\right>}_{A}\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\alpha\in[0,1]

|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho T)|\leq w_{A}(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\||T|_{A}^{2\alpha}\|_{A}+\||T^{\sharp_{A}}|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}\|_{A}\right)

Proof.

This follows directly from Theorem
2.7
, applied to the operator
\displaystyle T
and using the fact that for any state
\displaystyle\rho
, there exists a unit vector
\displaystyle x
such that
\displaystyle|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho T)|\leq|\langle Tx,x\rangle_{A}|\leq w_{A}(T)
.
∎

When
\displaystyle A=\rho
(the density matrix itself), this provides bounds on expectation values that incorporate the state’s structure.

3.1.2
Quantum Channel Capacity

For a quantum channel
\displaystyle\Phi
with Kraus representation
\displaystyle\Phi(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}XK_{i}^{*}
, we can analyze its
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius:

Theorem 3.2
.

The Choi operator
\displaystyle C_{\Phi}=\sum_{i,j}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\Phi(|i\rangle\langle j|)
satisfies:

w_{\mathbb{A}}(C_{\Phi})\leq\inf_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\left(\frac{\|\sum_{i}K_{i}K_{i}^{*}\|_{A}^{2\alpha}+\|\sum_{i}K_{i}^{*}K_{i}\|_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}}{2}\right)

This provides a bound on channel properties that is tighter than standard operator norm bounds, particularly useful for channels with memory or in non-Markovian dynamics.

In summary, the
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius framework provides powerful tools for quantum mechanical applications:
(1) State-dependent bounds
: the
\displaystyle A
-operator allows incorporation of prior state information;
(2) Tighter inequalities
: the refined inequalities often give sharper bounds than standard numerical radius;
(3) Matrix operator analysis
: the results for
\displaystyle 2\times 2
operator matrices are particularly useful for bipartite systems; and
(4) Physical interpretation
: the
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius can represent constrained expectation values or modified uncertainty relations. These applications demonstrate that the mathematical results in the paper have direct physical significance in quantum theory.

The

-numerical radius framework provides powerful tools for analyzing differential operators and their discretizations. We demonstrate this through an application to elliptic boundary value problems.

3.2.1
Elliptic Boundary Value Problem

Consider the second-order elliptic PDE:

\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot(a(x)\nabla u)+c(x)u=f&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(42)

where
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded domain,
\displaystyle a(x)\geq a_{0}>0
, and
\displaystyle c(x)\geq 0
.

3.2.2
Operator Formulation

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\Omega)
and define the operator
\displaystyle A
as multiplication by
\displaystyle a(x)
. The differential operator
\displaystyle T=-\nabla\cdot(a(x)\nabla\cdot)+c(x)
can be viewed as an unbounded operator on
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
with domain
\displaystyle H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

After discretization (e.g., via finite differences or finite elements), we obtain a matrix operator
\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{h}
acting on the discrete space
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{h}
. The
\displaystyle A
-numerical radius provides stability estimates:

Theorem 3.3

(Stability Estimate)
.

For the discretized operator
\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{h}
and
\displaystyle A_{h}
(the discretized multiplication operator), we have:

\|u_{h}\|_{A_{h}}\leq w_{A_{h}}(\mathbf{T}_{h}^{-1})\|f_{h}\|_{A_{h}}

where
\displaystyle w_{A_{h}}(\mathbf{T}_{h}^{-1})
can be bounded using Theorem
2.7

w_{A_{h}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathbf{T}_{h}^{-1}\\
(\mathbf{T}_{h}^{\sharp_{A}})^{-1}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\mathbf{T}_{h}^{-1}\|_{A_{h}}+\|(\mathbf{T}_{h}^{\sharp_{A}})^{-1}\|_{A_{h}}\right)

Concrete Example: 1D Case

Let
\displaystyle\Omega=(0,1)
with
\displaystyle a(x)=1+x^{2}
and
\displaystyle c(x)=1
. Discretize using centered differences with
\displaystyle N
points:

Example 3.4

.

\displaystyle A

Theorem 3.6

(Preconditioner Quality)
.

For any preconditioner
\displaystyle P

\|e^{(k)}\|_{A_{h}}\leq[w_{A_{h}}(I-P^{-1}\mathbf{T}_{h})]^{k}\|e^{(0)}\|_{A_{h}}

2.8

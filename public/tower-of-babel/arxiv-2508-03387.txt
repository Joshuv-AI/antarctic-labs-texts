Embeddings of Weighted Morrey Spaces


Abstract
. In this master thesis we recall already established definitions and basic properties of classical Morrey spaces in an attempt to expand known facts to their weighted counterparts. To do so, we will recall properties of Muckenhoupt weights, which we will use to derive further properties of weighted Morrey spaces. We will also show the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on weighted Morrey spaces. Throughout this thesis we will have a look at example weights and show in which case Morrey spaces equipped with these weights are embedded in each other.

Mathematical Background

We begin by recapturing some basic notations and conventions of which
will be assumed that the reader is well acquainted with. If this,
however, is not the case, we refer to
[
T92
]
for the further
mathematical background needed within this thesis.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. As usual let
L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right):=\left\{f:\|f|L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|<\infty\right\}

denote the function spaces of equivalence classes
\left[f\right]

of
p
integrable functions, where
0<p\leq\infty
. We use the corresponding
quasi-norm
\|f|L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f\left(x\right)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

and the usual change for
p=\infty
, i.e.
\|f|L_{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|=\inf_{C>0}\left(\mu\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left|f\left(x\right)\right|>C\right\}\right)=0\right)

with
\mu
being the Lebesgue measure. By
L_{p}^{loc}
we refer
to
p
integrable functions on every compact set
K
and with
w-L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

we mean the weak Lebesgue spaces defined in the usual manner
w-L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)=\left\{f:\|f|w-L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|<\infty\right\}
,
with their corresponding norm
\|f|w-L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|=\sup_{t>0}t^{p}\mu\left(x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\left|f\left(x\right)\right|>t\right)
.

Because of the occurrence of weights and weighted function spaces
let us first define what we mean when we refer to a general weight.
A weight
\omega
is a function that satisfies the conditions
\omega\in L_{1}^{loc}

where
\omega>0
almost everywhere. We denote the weighted Lebesgue
spaces by
L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega\right):=\left\{f:\|f|L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega\right)\|<\infty\right\}
,
with their corresponding norm
\|f|L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega\right)\|:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f\left(x\right)|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

for a weight
\omega
.

Without further explanation, we will use
p^{\prime}
for any given
0<p\leq\infty
,
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
with the usual agreement,
that if
0<p\leq 1,
then
p^{\prime}=\infty
or vice versa. Unless stated
otherwise we will use
L_{p}:=L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,

L_{p}\left(\omega\right):=L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega\right)

and also
L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)
and
L_{p}\left(\Omega,\omega\right)

whenever the Lebesgue space is restricted to a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We will also take for granted that all function spaces mentioned above
are quasi-Banach spaces, i.e. Banach spaces for
p\geq 1
and quasi-Banach
spaces for
0<p<1
.

Whenever we speak of a ball with radius
R>0
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

we will write
B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
and use the notation
Q_{r}\left(x_{0}\right)

for paraxial cubes with side length
r>0
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

accordingly. Moreover we also use the notation
\lambda Q
whenever
the side length of the cube
Q
is multiplied with
\lambda>0
,
i.e.
\lambda Q_{r}\left(x_{0}\right):=Q_{\lambda r}\left(x_{0}\right)

(or the radius of the ball
B
respectively). Also for matters of
abbreviation we write
\left|K\right|:=\mu\left(K\right)
if
K

is a measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the Lebesgue
measure and by
\chi_{K}
we refer to the characteristic function,
i.e.
\chi_{K}\left(x\right)=1
if
x\in K
and
0
if
x\notin K
.
For a weight
\omega
and a measurable set
K
we use the notation

\omega\left(K\right):=\int_{K}\omega\left(x\right)dx
. Whenever
we write
a\sim b
, we mean that there are positive constants
c,C>0

such that
ca\leq b\leq Ca
. Furthermore we will denote the Hardy-Littlewood
maximal operator with
\mathcal{M}
. It is normally defined as

\mathcal{M}f\left(x\right):=\sup_{R>0}\frac{1}{\left|B_{R}\left(x\right)\right|}\int_{B_{R}\left(x\right)}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|dy.

Finally we remark that throughout the thesis
C
will denote a positive
constant, which can be different even in a single chain of inequalities

In the following we will list some well known definitions and results
that we will use throughout the thesis.

Definition 1
.

We say an operator
T\,:\,L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\rightarrow w-L_{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

is of weak-type
\left(p,q\right)
if

\|Tf|w-L_{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|\leq C\cdot\|f|L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|

holds for all
f\in L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

An operator is said to be of strong-type
\left(p,q\right)
if

\|Tf|L_{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|\leq C\cdot\|f|L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|

holds for all
f\in L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

be a (not necessarily bounded) domain. If
f,g\in L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)

and
h\in L_{p^{\prime}}\left(\Omega\right)
then

\|f\cdot h|L_{1}\left(\Omega\right)\|\leq\|f\left(x\right)|L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)\|\cdot\|h\left(x\right)|L_{p^{\prime}}\left(\Omega\right)\|\mbox{\,(Hölder's inequality)}

and

\|f+g|L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)\|\leq\|f|L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)\|+\|g|L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)\|\,\mbox{(Minkowski's inequality)}.

Proof.

One proof can be found in
[
T92
, Sect. 1.3.4.; Lemma]
.
∎

Remark 3
.

We should not leave the fact uncommented,
that Hölder’s inequality also holds for weighted Lebesgue spaces.
For two functions
f\in L_{r}\left(\Omega\right)
and
h\in L_{r^{\prime}}\left(\Omega\right)
,
a weight
\omega
, an open set
\Omega
and
r>1
with its dual

r^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f\left(x\right)h\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)^{1/r}h\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}f\left(x\right)^{r}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/r}\left(\int_{\Omega}h\left(x\right)^{r^{\prime}}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}.

Using Hölder’s inequality, we can also see that Minkowski’s inequality
holds for weighted Lebesgue spaces. Therefore additionally let
g\in L_{r}\left(\Omega\right)
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|f\left(x\right)+g\left(x\right)\right|^{r}\omega\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|f\left(x\right)\right|+\left|g\left(x\right)\right|\right)\omega\left(x\right)^{1/r}\left|f\left(x\right)+g\left(x\right)\right|^{r-1}\omega\left(x\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle\overset{\tiny\mbox{Hölder}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left(\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{r}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/r}+\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|g\left(x\right)\right|^{r}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/r}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|f\left(x\right)+g\left(x\right)\right|^{\left(r-1\right)r^{\prime}}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\|f|L_{r}\left(\omega\right)\|+\|g|L_{r}\left(\omega\right)\|\right)\frac{\|f+g|L_{r}\left(\omega\right)\|^{r}}{\|f+g|L_{r}\left(\omega\right)\|}.

Now multiplying both sides with
\frac{\|f+g|L_{r}\left(\omega\right)\|^{r}}{\|f+g|L_{r}\left(\omega\right)\|}

yields the weighted inequality.

Part I
Unweighted Morrey Spaces

Morrey spaces, named after Charles Bradfield Morrey Jr., can be understood
as generalizations of the Lebesgue spaces in the sense that they serve
the purpose of describing local behavior of the
L_{p}
-norms by
introducing a new parameter
u
. This makes them especially useful
for the specification of solutions of non-linear partial differential
equations. If the parameters
u
and
p
are equal, the Morrey space

M_{u,p}
coincides with the Lebesgue space
L_{p}
as we will show
later on. However if the parameter
p
is smaller than
u
, then
the Lebesgue spaces are continuously embedded in the Morrey space.

Although the topic of this thesis deals with weighted Morrey spaces,
it is only natural to have a look at their unweighted precursors first,
to get an idea of basic properties and relations. At first we give
a definition of (unweighted) Morrey spaces and establish some properties
mainly following
[
R09
]
before we continue dealing with
their weighted counterparts.

Definition 7
.

(Morrey spaces) Let
0<p\leq u\leq\infty
. We then call

M_{u,p}:=M_{u,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right):=\left\{f\in L_{p}^{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right):\|f|M_{u,p}\|<\infty\right\}

Morrey spaces, with their corresponding quasi-norm

\|f|M_{u,p}\|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},R>0}R^{n(1/u-1/p)}\left(\int_{B_{R}\left(x\right)}|f(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{1/p}.

For
p=\infty
we define the norm
\|\cdot|M_{\infty,\infty}\|

with respect to the norm of essentially bounded functions analogously.

Here
\|\cdot|M_{u,p}\|
is a norm for
p\geq 1
and a
quasi-norm for
0<p<1
. Exactly as it is done in
[
R09
]
,
we list some basic properties in the following Lemma. It can be found
in
[
R09
, Rem. 1.2.]
where a further reference to
[
FJK
, Thm. 4.3.6.]

is made.

Lemma 8
.

(i) If
u<p
then
M_{u,p}=\left\{0\right\}
.

(ii) If
u=\infty
then
M_{\infty,p}=L_{\infty}
with equivalence
of the corresponding norms.

(iii) If
u=p
we have
M_{p,p}=L_{p}
.

Proof.

We refer to
[
R09
, Rem. 1.2.]
for the proof.
∎

The last fact can be understood in the sense that Morrey spaces are
a generalization of Lebesgue spaces. Since the aim of the thesis is
to expand facts already known for the latter spaces, we shall henceforth
only consider the case
0<p\leq u
unless stated elsewhere (although
we will mainly focus on
1<p\leq u
). We now proceed with some basic
relations among Morrey Spaces, which are directly taken from
[
R09
, Thm. 1.3.]
.
There the author refers to
[
KY07
, Rem. 1.2.; Lem. 1.7.]
.

Lemma 9
.

(i)
\left(M_{u,p}|\|\cdot|M_{u,p}\|\right)

is a quasi-Banach Space.

(ii) For
0<p_{2}\leq p_{1}\leq u
, we have
M_{u,p_{1}}\hookrightarrow M_{u,p_{2}}

and furthermore
L_{u}\hookrightarrow M_{u,p_{1}}
.

(iii) For
0<p<u
we get
w-L_{u}\hookrightarrow M_{u,p}
.

(iv) For
0<p_{1}<u_{1}
and
0<p_{2}<u_{2}
let
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=:\frac{1}{p}

and
\frac{1}{u_{1}}+\frac{1}{u_{2}}=:\frac{1}{u}
. If
u\in M_{u_{1},p_{1}}

and
v\in M_{u_{2},p_{2}}
we get
uv\in M_{u,p}
or
\|uv|M_{u,p}\|\leq\|u|M_{u_{1},p_{1}}\|\|v|M_{u_{2},p_{2}}\|

respectively.

Proof.

The proofs can be found in
[
R09
, Thm. 1.3.]
.
∎

Let

The theorem and its proof can be found in

.

The hitherto considered cases did not involve the space

,
at which we will have a look at in the following lemma.

Lemma 11
.

(i) If
M_{u,p}\subset L_{\infty}

(ii) If

Proof.

See

Part II
Muckenhoupt Weights

The next step is to have a closer look at a certain class of weights,
first introduced to sum up all weights
\omega
for which the Hardy-Littlewood
maximal operator a bounded operator from
L_{p}\left(\omega\right)

to itself. This class of weights was first introduced by Benjamin
Muckenhoupt in
[
M72
]
. In Part
III

we will see that they can also be used to prove embeddings of certain
weighted Morrey spaces. Throughout the whole section we will follow

[
S93
, Ch. V; §§1,3,5]
very closely.

1.
Definition and Basic Properties

Definition 15
.

If
1<p<\infty
is fixed, we say that the
function
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
belongs to
the class of

Muckenhoupt weights
A_{p}
if
\omega\in L_{1}^{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,
if it is absolutely continuous
d\mu(x)=\omega(x)dx
with respect
to the Lebesgue measure
\mu
and if there is
A>0
such that
\omega

satisfies the inequality

(1)

\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right]\cdot\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\right]^{p/p^{\prime}}\leq A<\infty

for all balls
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The smallest constant
A

satisfying this inequality is denoted by
A_{p}(\omega)
, which allows
us to identify all
\omega
within the class
A_{p}
.

This definition and the following results have been taken from
[
S93
, Ch. V; §1]
.
We summarize the bits we need in the next lemma.

Lemma 16
.

(i) If
\omega
belongs to
A_{p}
, then
so do its dilates
\omega_{\delta}\left(x\right)=\omega\left(\delta x\right)
,
its translates
\omega_{h}=\omega\left(\cdot-h\right)
or its multiplications
with a positive scalar
\lambda\omega\left(\cdot\right)
.

(ii) If
w\in A_{p}
then
\sigma=\omega^{-p^{\prime}/p}
belongs to
A_{p^{\prime}}
.
Hence
A_{p^{\prime}}\left(\sigma\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}=A_{p}\left(\omega\right)^{1/p}
.

(iii) The classes
A_{p}
are increasing with
p
, i.e. for
p_{1}<p_{2}

we get
A_{p_{1}}\subset A_{p_{2}}
.

Proof.

(i) The result easily follows by substituting the variable and since
inequality (
1
) must hold for all balls
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(ii) This fact is obvious, if we keep in mind that
1<p<\infty
and
that
p^{\prime}
is the dual of
p
as well as
\left(p^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}=p
. Roughly
speaking we reverse the order of the left and right hand terms in
(
1
). We should also denote that in the case
of
p=2
the result is particularly elegant, i.e.

\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right]\cdot\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-1}dx\right]\leq A.

(iii) Assume that
p_{1}<p_{2}
and
\omega\in A_{p_{1}}
. It follows
directly as a consequence of Definition
15
and
Hölder’s inequality, which we apply to the right term of the just
mentioned definition with
p=\frac{p_{1}^{\prime}p_{2}}{p_{1}p_{2}^{\prime}}
. We
also need the fact that
q_{2}<q_{1}
if we define
q_{j}=\frac{p_{j}^{\prime}}{p_{j}}
.
By that we naturally also get
A_{p_{2}}\left(\omega\right)\leq A_{p_{1}}\left(\omega\right)
.
∎

Definition 17
.

(i) A weight
\omega
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{1}
if
there exists a constant
0<A<\infty
such that

\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega\left(y\right)dy\leq A\omega\left(x\right)

holds for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all balls
B
.

(ii) The Muckenhoupt class
A_{\infty}
is defined by

A_{\infty}=\bigcup_{p\geq 1}A_{p}

The following remark and
[
HP05
, Def. 2.1.]

justify this definition of
A_{1}
.

Remark 18
.

Since we know that the spaces
A_{p}

are increasingly embedded for every
1<p<\infty
, it’s only natural
to wonder what is happening to
A_{p}
if
p\rightarrow 1
. We notice
that the second term
\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\left(\omega\left(x\right)\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\right]^{p/p^{\prime}}\rightarrow\|\omega^{-1}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|

as
p\rightarrow 1
, because it is bounded from above by
\|\omega^{-1}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|

and the norms
\|\omega^{-1}|L_{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\left(B\right)\|

are monotonically increasing in
p
for fixed balls
B
. However,
this essentially means

(2)

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(y\right)dy\leq A\omega\left(x\right)\,\,\mbox{for almost every \,}x\in B.

Furthermore we have
\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\right]^{p/p^{\prime}}\leq\|\omega^{-1}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|

for all balls
B
. Hence
A_{1}\subset A_{p}
for
p>1
, so that
the increasing embedding also holds.

Example 19
.

Let us now have a look at basic examples for Muckenhoupt
weights.

(i) The simplest of all is
\omega^{*}\equiv 1
, for which we obviously
see that
\omega^{*}\in A_{p}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

(ii) The next example is simply given by
\omega_{\alpha}\left(x\right)=\left|x\right|^{\alpha}
,
which belongs to a Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
if and only if
-n<\alpha<n\left(p-1\right)
,

1<p<\infty
. The proof is given by the special case
\alpha=\beta

of the next example.

(iii) The last example we will mention here, is given by

\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)=\begin{cases}\left|x\right|^{\alpha}&\mbox{, }x<1\\
\left|x\right|^{\beta}&\mbox{, }x\geq 1\end{cases}\mbox{ for }\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\in A_{p}
if and only if
-n<\alpha,\beta<n\left(p-1\right)
,

1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

This elaborate proof of the statement is taken from
[
B07
, 1.3.2. Examples]
.

“
\Longrightarrow
” Let
1<p<\infty
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.
We begin by assuming that
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\in A_{p}
. This
means, that there is a constant
A>0
such that

(3)

\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\leq A<\infty,

for all balls
B
. In particular this includes the ball
B_{R}\left(0\right)

for
R>0
. First let
0<R<1
.

By also considering the Jacobian
the left hand integral becomes

\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx\sim\int_{0}^{R}r^{\alpha+n-1}dr.

We see that for
\alpha=-n
the integral diverges and since we are
dealing with
R<1
the same would apply for
\alpha<-n
, because
then
\int_{0}^{R}r^{\alpha+n-1}dr=R^{-\left|\alpha+n\right|}\rightarrow\infty

as
R\rightarrow 0
, leaving us with the condition
\alpha>-n
as
the only possibly choice for the above integral to be finite. Similarly
we get the right hand integral in (
3
) as

\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\sim\int_{0}^{R}r^{-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}+n-1}dr.

Here the integral obviously diverges for
\alpha=n\left(p-1\right)
.
For
\alpha>n\left(p-1\right)
the integral becomes
\int_{0}^{R}r^{-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}+n-1}dr=R^{-\left|\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}+n\right|}\rightarrow\infty

as
R\rightarrow 0
. This leaves
\alpha<n\left(p-1\right)
as the
only admissible case in which the integral is finite. If we combine
both conditions, we are left with a certain span for
\alpha
, which
is
-n<\alpha<n\left(p-1\right)
. For this configuration we are left
with

\mbox{\eqref{eq:ThisIsPesh}}\sim R^{-n}R^{\left(\alpha+n\right)/p}R^{\left(-\alpha\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}+n\right)/p^{\prime}}=R^{0}<\infty,

thus
R<1
and
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\in A_{p}
both imply
-n<\alpha<n\left(p-1\right)
.

We proceed by having a look at the case
R>1
, while we still assume
that
\alpha
satisfies
-n<\alpha<n\left(p-1\right)
. Again, we
will have a look at the ball of radius
R
centered at the origin
and calculate the integrals to get

\displaystyle\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|<1}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx+\int_{1<|x|<R}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}dr+\int_{1}^{R}r^{\beta+n-1}dr

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 1+\begin{cases}R^{\beta+n}-1&\mbox{, }\beta>-n\\
1-R^{-\left|\beta+n\right|}&\mbox{, }\beta<-n\\
\log R&\mbox{, }\beta=-n\end{cases}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\begin{cases}R^{\beta+n}&\beta>-n\\
1&\beta<-n\\
\log R&\beta=-n\end{cases},

and likewise

\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\sim\begin{cases}R^{-\beta\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}+n}&\beta<n\left(p-1\right)\\
1&\beta>n\left(p-1\right)\\
\log R&R=n\left(p-1\right)\end{cases}.

We can sum up these results as follows: Let
B
be a ball and define

I:=\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

then we get the following
5
cases for different choices
of
\beta
.

Case #1: If
\beta>n\left(p-1\right)>-n
,

then
I\sim R^{-n}R^{\frac{\beta+n}{p}}=R^{\frac{1}{p}\left(\beta-n\left(p-1\right)\right)}\rightarrow\infty

as
R\rightarrow\infty
.

Case #2: If
\beta=n\left(p-1\right)>-n
,

then
I\sim R^{-n}R^{\frac{\beta+n}{p}}\left(\log R\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}=\left(\log R\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\rightarrow\infty

as
R\rightarrow\infty
.

Case #3: If
n\left(p-1\right)>\beta>-n
,

then
I\sim R^{-n}R^{\frac{\beta+n}{p}}R^{\left(-\beta\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}+n\right)\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}=R^{0}=1

Case #4: If
n\left(p-1\right)>\beta=-n
,

then
I\sim R^{-n}\left(\log R\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-\frac{\beta}{p}+\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}}=\left(\log R\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\rightarrow\infty

as
R\rightarrow\infty.

Case #5: If
n\left(p-1\right)>-n>\beta
,

then
I\sim R^{-n}R^{-\frac{\beta}{p}+\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}}=R^{-\frac{\beta+n}{p}}\rightarrow\infty

as
R\rightarrow\infty.

However this means that the only admissible
\beta
are found in
Case 3, concluding one direction of the proof.

“
\Longleftarrow
” Let
-n<\alpha,\beta<n\left(p-1\right)
.
Now we have to find a constant such that the supremum over all balls

B
of
I
is bounded from above. We already saw in the first step
of the proof that balls, centered at
0
of arbitrary radius
R

are bounded for this choice of parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
.
So let
B=B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
for
R>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We will divide the proof in three parts, beginning with

Part 1:

\left|x_{\text{0}}\right|<\frac{R}{2}
.

For this choice the inclusion
B_{\frac{1}{2}R}\left(0\right)\subset B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\subset B_{\frac{3}{2}R}\left(0\right)

holds, making it possible to estimate the integral from both below
and above by

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{c}{\left|B_{\frac{1}{2}R}\left(0\right)\right|}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}R}\left(0\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\left|B_{\frac{3}{2}R}\left(0\right)\right|}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}\left(0\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

Now for
R<\frac{2}{3}
we have
\frac{1}{2}R<\frac{3}{2}R<1
and
for
R>2
we have
\frac{3}{2}R>\frac{1}{2}R>1
respectively. Thus
the same arguments as for balls centered at
0
hold, meaning we
know that the supremum over all such balls
B
of
I
is bounded
from above (as we have shown in the first step of the proof). What
remains to show is the boundedness for
\frac{2}{3}\leq R\leq 2
.
This is done by estimating
\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|

in the manner of above to get
c\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n}\leq\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|\leq C2^{n}
.
We estimate further to see

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|}\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}\left(0\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{3}\left(0\right)}}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C

and

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|}\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}R}\left(0\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{3\left(0\right)}}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C

respectively. The whole process can be repeated for
\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx
,
which ultimately yields
\sup_{B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right);\left|x_{0}\right|<\frac{R}{2}}I\leq A<\infty

and brings us to

Part 2:
\left|x_{0}\right|>2R
.

We begin this part by using the triangle inequality for
x\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
,
to see

\displaystyle\left|x\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|x-x_{0}\right|+\left|x_{0}\right|\leq\frac{3}{2}\left|x_{0}\right|\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\left|x\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left|x_{0}\right|-\left|x-x_{0}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|x_{0}\right|.

This essentially means that
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)\sim\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x_{0}\right)

for all
x\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right).
As above, for
\left|x_{0}\right|>2

we get
\frac{3}{2}\left|x_{0}\right|>\frac{1}{2}\left|x_{0}\right|>1

and likewise for
\left|x_{0}\right|<\frac{2}{3}
we see that the
inequality
\frac{1}{2}\left|x_{0}\right|<\frac{3}{2}\left|x_{0}\right|<1

holds. So for all
x\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
we either have
\left|x\right|<1

or
\left|x\right|>1
. Thus

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left|x_{0}\right|^{\alpha\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\sim C\mbox{\,\ for }\left|x\right|<1\,\mbox{or}

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left|x_{0}\right|^{\beta\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\sim C\,\mbox{ for }\left|x\right|\geq 1.

Now for
\frac{2}{3}<\left|x_{0}\right|<2
we see for every
x\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)

that
1<\left|x\right|<3
. The remaining estimate is

\min\left(\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{\alpha},1,3^{\beta}\right)\leq\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)\leq\max\left(\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{\alpha},1,3^{\beta}\right)

for all such
x
. This step and the above yield
\sup_{B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right);\left|x_{0}\right|>2R}I\leq A<\infty

and we conclude this proof with

Part 3:
\frac{1}{2}R<\left|x_{0}\right|<2R
.

We apply the triangle inequality again to show the boundedness of
the supremum over all balls
B
of
I
centered in these
x_{0}
,
to see

\left|x\right|\leq\left|x-x_{0}\right|+\left|x_{0}\right|<3R,

or likewise
B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\subset B_{3R}\left(0\right)
.
We can also find an estimate from below. Since
B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)

is open, there exists another ball such that
B_{R^{\prime}}\left(x_{0}\right)\subset B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)

with
R^{\prime}=\frac{R}{6}
. By assumption
\left|x_{0}\right|>\frac{1}{2}R>\frac{1}{3}R>2R^{\prime}
.

Now remembering both, the first step of this proof and part 2 we get

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{c}{\left|B_{\frac{R}{6}}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{6}}\left(x_{0}\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\left|B_{3R}\left(0\right)\right|}\int_{B_{3R}\left(x_{0}\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx.

We have shown the existence of a boundary for both the above and below
estimates, so that there also exist one for the middle term in (
7
).
Part 1 - 3 finally yield

\sup_{B}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\leq A<\infty.

∎

2.
Boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Operator on
L_{p}\left(\omega\right)

As we have mentioned in the beginning, Muckenhoupt weights were a
result to the question of the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood
maximal operator. Our main aim in this section will now be to show,
that for
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in A_{p}
the maximal operator

\mathcal{M}
is indeed bounded as follows

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\mathcal{M}f\left(x\right)\right)^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\leq A\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f\left(x\right)|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx,

for all
f\in L_{p}\left(\omega\right)
. The proof of the boundedness
of the operator from
L_{p}\left(\omega\right)
to itself contains
an application of the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem. However
to apply it according to our use, we first need the following proposition.

Proposition 20
.

Let
d\mu
be a non-negative
Borel measure and let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then the maximal operator

\mathcal{M}:f\mapsto\mathcal{M}\left(f\right)
is of weak-type
\left(p,p\right),

i.e.

\mu\left\{x:\mathcal{M}f\left(x\right)>\alpha\right\}\leq\frac{A}{\alpha^{p}}\int|f\left(x\right)|^{p}d\mu\left(x\right),\,\mbox{all }\alpha>0,

if and only if
d\mu
is absolutely continuous,
d\mu\left(x\right)=\omega\left(x\right)dx

with
\omega\in A_{p}
.

Proof.

This proposition was taken from
[
S93
, Ch. V; §2.2.]
,
to which we also refer for the proof.
∎

We will also need two further preliminaries to prove the theorem and
the first of them is the reverse Hölder inequality.

Proposition 21
.

(Reverse Hölder Inequality)
If
\omega\in A_{\infty}
then there exist
r>1
and
c>0
such
that

(8)

\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{r}dx\right]^{1/r}\leq\frac{c}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx,

for all balls
B
.

Proof.

We refer again to
[
S93
, Ch. V; §3 Prop.3]
for the proof.
∎

Taking Proposition
21
and Lemma

16
(iii) we get the following corollary taken
from
[
S93
, Ch. V; Sect. 3 Cor.]
.

Corollary 22
.

Suppose that
\omega\in A_{p}
for
some
1<p<\infty
. Then there is
p_{1}<p
such that
\omega\in A_{p_{1}}
.

Proof.

With
p^{\prime}
being the dual to
p
, we have
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Also
recall Lemma
16
(ii) and define
\sigma=\omega^{-p^{\prime}/p}\in A_{p^{\prime}}\subset A_{\infty}
.
By the above Proposition
21

we know that
\sigma
satisfies a reverse Hölder inequality for some

r>1

(9)

\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\sigma\left(x\right)^{r}dx\right]^{1/r}\leq\frac{c}{|B|}\int_{B}\sigma\left(x\right)dx.

Now we see that
r\cdot p^{\prime}/p=r/\left(p-1\right)=1/\left(p_{1}-1\right)=p_{1}^{\prime}/p_{1}

for some
1<p_{1}<p
, because
r>1.
Furthermore
\omega\in A_{p}

by assumption and by rearranging the inequality in Definition
15
,
taking the
p/p^{\prime}
-th power of (
9
) and
multiplying both sides with
\omega\left(B\right)/\left|B\right|

yields
\omega\in A_{p_{1}}
.
∎

The last two proofs were preparations for the earlier stated main
aim of this section. We will now prove it in the next theorem, taken
from
[
S93
, Ch. V; Sect. 3; Thm. 1]
.

Theorem 23
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in A_{p}
.
Then

(10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\mathcal{M}f(x)\right)^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\leq A\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f\left(x\right)|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx,

for all
f\in L_{p}\left(\omega\right)
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in A_{p}
be given. Then by Corollary
22

there is a
1<p_{1}<p
for which
\omega\in A_{p_{1}}
. We know
by Proposition
20
that
\mathcal{M}

is of weak-type
\left(L_{p_{1}}\left(\omega\right),L_{p_{1}}\left(\omega\right)\right)
.
It is also obvious that
\mathcal{M}
is bounded on
L_{\infty}
\left(\omega\right)
.
Now together with the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem (Theorem

4
) it follows that
\mathcal{M}

is bounded on
L_{p}\left(\omega\right)
to itself (since
1<p_{1}<p<\infty
).
∎

By using the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem, we make use of two
interpolation points (i.e.
p_{1}
and
\infty
). In Proposition

20
we have shown that the
weak-type
\left(p_{1},p_{1}\right)
inequality only holds for
\omega\in A_{p_{1}}
.
Hence Theorem
23
gives us the maximal possible
class of weights one can find when we use this interpolation theorem.

3.
More Results for
A_{p}

There are, of course, more facts which can be derived for weights
belonging to a Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
but only a few will be mentioned
in this thesis. A detail, which we will need later on is the fact
that every Muckenhoupt weight is also a doubling measure. We will
prove this in the following.

Definition 25
.

(Doubling Measure) We say a weight
\omega

is a doubling measure if there is a constant
C>0
such that

\omega\left(B_{2R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)\leq C\omega\left(B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)

for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0
.

The following lemma is an alternative way of defining the class of
Muckenhoupt weights. In this way of defining it, the origin as the
class of weights for which the maximal operator is bounded is more
obvious. It is taken from
[
S93
, Ch. V; §1; 1.4]
.

Lemma 26
.

Let
f\in L_{1}^{loc}

and
B
be a ball, then
\omega\in A_{p}
for
p>1
if and only
if

(11)

\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}f\left(x\right)dx\right)^{p}\leq\frac{c}{\omega\left(B\right)}\int_{B}f\left(x\right)^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx

holds for all non-negative functions
f
and all balls
B
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a ball and define
f_{B}:=\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}f\left(x\right)dx

as the mean value of
f
on
B
.

“
\Longrightarrow
” Assume
\omega\in A_{p}
, then we get

f_{B}=\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}f\left(x\right)dx=\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int f\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)^{1/p}\omega\left(x\right)^{-1/p}dx,

and apply Hölder’s inequality with the exponents
p
and
p^{\prime}
to
get

\left(f_{B}\right)^{p}\leq\left|B\right|^{-p}\left(\int_{B}f\left(x\right)^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}\right)^{p/p^{\prime}}.

From there on we see that (
1
) leads to the
desired (
11
).

“
\Longleftarrow
” Conversely, assume (
11
)
holds. Our first intention is to set
f=\omega^{-p^{\prime}/p}
, because
then
f^{p}\omega=\omega^{-p^{\prime}+1}=\omega^{-p^{\prime}/p}
and rearranging
(
11
) would yield exactly the wanted inequality
(
1
). However, we do not know whether or not

\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx
is finite and hence we replace

f
by
\left(\omega+\varepsilon\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}
with
\varepsilon>0
.
Now by assumption
\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\left(\omega+\varepsilon\right)\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\right)^{p}\leq\frac{C}{\omega\left(B\right)}\int_{B}\left(\omega+\varepsilon\right)\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}}\omega\left(x\right)dx

. Next we let
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, use the Lebesgue monotone
convergence theorem (see
[
T92
, App. 1, Thm. 2]
), and
rearrange the inequality to get

\left[\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right]\left[\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right]^{p/p^{\prime}}\leq C<\infty

(see
[
G09
, Sect. 9.1.1.]
). With
\omega^{-p^{\prime}+1}=\omega^{-p^{\prime}/p}

this yields (
1
).
∎

The following small insertion uses content of
[
D01
, p. 133 et sqq.]
.

Remark 27
.

Lemma
26

tells us two interesting facts about Muckenhoupt weights. Given a
measurable subset
D
of a ball
B
, i.e.
D\subset B
, we take
(
11
) with
f\equiv\chi_{D}
, which yields

\omega\left(B\right)\left(\frac{\left|D\right|}{\left|B\right|}\right)^{p}\leq\omega\left(D\right).

From this inequality we deduce the two facts. Firstly
\omega\equiv 0

or
\omega>0
a.e., for if a measurable subset
D
(which we assume
to be bounded) of a ball
B
has the property
\omega|_{D}=0

then it follows that
\omega\left(B\right)=0
. Secondly,
\omega\in L_{1}^{loc}

or
\omega=\infty
a.e.. In case there is a ball
B
for which
\omega\left(B\right)=\infty
,
the same holds for every ball containing
B
as well as any measurable
set contained in
B
.

Corollary 28
.

Let
\omega\in A_{p}
, then
\omega

also is a doubling measure.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in A_{p}
. Now let
R>0
and set
B:=B_{2R}\left(x_{0}\right)

as well as
f:=\chi_{B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)}
to apply Lemma
26
.
Clearly (
11
) now turns into

\left(\frac{1}{\left|B_{2R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{2R}\left(x_{0}\right)}\chi_{B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)}\left(x\right)dx\right)^{p}\leq\frac{c}{\omega\left(B_{2R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)}\int_{B_{2R}\left(x_{0}\right)}\left(\chi_{B_{R}}\left(x\right)\right)^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx.

From there on we easily deduce

\omega\left(B_{2R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)\leq c^{\prime}\omega\left(B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right),

with
c^{\prime}=2^{np}c
. Hence
\omega
is a doubling measure.
∎

The next theorem shows how to construct Muckenhoupt weights
\omega

belonging to the class
A_{p}
for every
1<p<\infty
, given two
weights
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in A_{1}
or vice versa. The theorem
as well as its proof have been taken from
[
S93
, Ch. V; Sect. 5.3. Prop. 9]
.

Theorem 29
.

Suppose that
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in A_{1}
. If
1\leq p<\infty
,
then
\omega=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}^{1-p}
belongs to
A_{p}
. Equivalently,
suppose
\omega\in A_{p}
, then there are
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in A_{1}

such that
\omega=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}^{1-p}
.

Proof.

Step 1 (Motivation)

To get a first idea of how this proof works, let us consider the most
transparent case, which is
p=2
. Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in A_{1}
.
We begin by having a look at
\omega=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}^{-1}
and
further

\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx\leq\int_{B}\omega_{1}\left(x\right)dx\left[\inf_{x\in B}\omega_{2}\left(x\right)\right]^{-1}.

Likewise

\int_{B}\omega^{-1}\left(x\right)dx\leq\int_{B}\omega_{2}\left(x\right)dx\left[\inf_{x\in B}\omega_{1}\left(x\right)\right]^{-1},

from which we conclude

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-1}dx\right)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega_{i}\left(x\right)dx\left[\inf_{x\in B}\omega_{i}\left(x\right)^{-1}\right]\right),

where each term on the right hand side is bounded, since
\omega_{i}\in A_{1}

for
i=1,2
. This yields
\omega\in A_{2}
.

Let us now prove the converse. We assume that
\omega\in A_{2}
and
take the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
. Furthermore we construct

(12)

Tf:=\omega^{-1/2}\mathcal{M}\left(\omega^{1/2}f\right)+\omega^{1/2}\mathcal{M}\left(\omega^{-1/2}f\right).

By definition of
\mathcal{M}
this operator is sublinear, i.e.
T\left(f+g\right)\leq Tf+Tg
.
It is also symmetric in
\omega
, i.e. if
\omega\in A_{2}
then
so is
\omega^{-1}
. So we can assume that
\omega^{-1/2}\mathcal{M}\omega^{1/2}

and
\omega^{1/2}\mathcal{M}\omega^{-1/2}
are bounded from
L_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\rightarrow L_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.
Together with (
12
) we conclude

(13)

\|Tf|L_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|\leq A\|f|L_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|\,\mbox{ for some\,}A>0.

Let us now fix
\|f|L_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|=1

and define

\eta:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(2A\right)^{-k}T^{k}\left(f\right),

where we used the notation for the iterated operator
T^{k}f:=T^{k-1}\left(T\left(f\right)\right)
.
From there on we easily see that
\|\eta|L_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}=1
,
since it shrinks down to a geometric series together with (
13
).
Hence
\eta\in L_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. The next fact we
need to take into consideration is the pointwise inequality

T\eta=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(2A\right)^{-k}T^{k+1}\left(f\right)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(2A\right)^{1-k}T^{k}\left(f\right)=\left(2A\right)\eta.

Now with
\omega_{1}=\omega^{1/2}\eta
, we see

\mathcal{M}\left(\omega_{1}\right)\leq T\left(\eta\right)\omega^{1/2}\leq\left(2A\right)\eta\omega^{1/2}=\left(2A\right)\omega_{1},

and hence essentially
\omega_{1}\in A_{1}
as we have seen in (
2
).
Likewise we look at
\omega_{2}=\omega^{-1/2}\eta
and by applying
the same steps we get
\mathcal{M}\left(\omega_{2}\right)\leq A\omega_{2}

and
\omega_{2}\in A_{1}
. We conclude

\frac{\omega_{1}}{\omega_{2}}=\frac{\omega^{1/2}\eta}{\omega^{-1/2}\eta}=\omega\in A_{2},

and thus the case
p=2
is proved.

Now that we gained some insight for the arguments needed to prove
the claim, let us proceed to general
p
.

Step 2
"\Longrightarrow"

The proof of the first part is completely similar to what we did earlier
for
p=2
. So let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in A_{1}
and
\omega=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}^{1-p}
.
As before we notice

\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx\leq\int\omega_{1}\left(x\right)dx\left[\inf_{x\in B}\omega_{2}\left(x\right)\right]^{1-p}\mbox{ and}

\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\leq\int_{B}\omega_{2}\left(x\right)dx\left[\inf_{x\in B}\omega_{1}\left(x\right)\right]^{-p^{\prime}/p}

and hence

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right]\left[\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}/p}dx\right]^{p/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega_{1}\left(x\right)dx\left[\inf_{x\in B}\omega_{2}\left(x\right)\right]^{1-p}\left[\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega_{2}\left(x\right)dx\right]^{p/p^{\prime}}\left[\inf_{x\in B}\omega_{1}\left(x\right)\right]^{-1}

.

Step 3
"\Longleftarrow"

The converse is also done equivalently to the case
p=2
. Let us
start with
p\geq 2
and
\omega\in A_{p}
. We define

Tf=\left[\omega^{-1/p}\mathcal{M}\left(f^{p/p^{\prime}}\omega^{1/p}\right)\right]^{p^{\prime}/p}+\omega^{1/p}\mathcal{M}\left(f\omega^{-1/p}\right).

Because
\omega\in A_{p}
we know
\omega^{-p^{\prime}/p}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
by
Lemma
16
. To show that
T
is bounded on
L_{p}

we need to recall Theorem
23
. Minkowski’s inequality
(see Lemma
2
) gives
T\left(f+g\right)\leq Tf+Tg,

since
p\geq 2
and hence
p/p^{\prime}=p/\left(p/p-1\right)\geq 1
. The fact
that both
\left[\omega^{-1/p}M\left(f^{p/p^{\prime}}\omega^{1/p}\right)\right]^{p^{\prime}/p}

and
\omega^{1/p}M\left(f\omega^{-1/p}\right)
are bounded from
L_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

to itself is again obvious if we recall Theorem
23
.
With all these preparations, we can now form
\eta
just like above
and write
\omega_{1}=\omega^{1/p}\eta^{p/p^{\prime}}
and
\omega_{2}=\omega^{-1/p}\eta
.
Also just like above
T\eta\leq\left(2A\right)\eta
for some
A>0

and proceeding
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in A_{1}
. Furthermore

\omega_{1}\omega_{2}^{1-p}=\omega^{1/p}\eta^{p/p^{\prime}}\cdot\left(\omega^{-1/p}\eta\right)^{1-p}=\omega,

which concludes the proof for
p\geq 2
. To finish the proof for
p<2
,
we simply use that
\omega^{-p^{\prime}/p}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
, factorize it just
like above and raise the product to the power
\left(-p/p^{\prime}\right)
,
so that the factorization holds for
\omega
.
∎

Remark 30
.

Although it will not occur again in this thesis, we should mention
the existence of more general Muckenhoupt classes used for other purposes.
They occurred first in
[
MW74
]
as a
result to the question of boundedness for all non-negative functions

\omega
for the operator
T_{\gamma}f\left(x\right):=\int f\left(x-y\right)\left|y\right|^{\gamma-n}dy
,
with
n
being the dimension. In particular the problem was to determine
all non-negative functions
\omega
for which
\|T_{\gamma}f|L_{q}\left(\omega\right)\|\leq C\|f|L_{p}\left(\omega\right)\|

holds. The result was a class of weight functions denoted with
A_{p,q}
.
A precise formulation of the problem, as well as a proper definition
can be found in
[
MW74
]
. The definition
and a few facts connecting
A_{p,q}
to the regular
A_{p}
can
also be found in
[
KS09
, Def. 2.10.]
.

A weight function belongs to
A_{p,q}
for
1<p<q<\infty
if there
exists
C>1
such that

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\leq C.

For
p=1
we say that
\omega\in A_{1,q}
with
1<q<\infty
if
there is
C>1
such that

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\left(\underset{x\in B}{\mbox{ess }\sup}\frac{1}{\omega\left(x\right)}\right)\leq C.

Weighted Morrey Spaces

After we studied unweighted Morrey spaces and Muckenhoupt weights
we will now have a look at weighted Morrey spaces. Whereas we will
focus on general weights in Section
4
, we will mostly
combine Part
I
and Part
II

in Section
5
and
6

and have a look at weighted Morrey spaces using Muckenhoupt weights.

4.
Definition and Basic Properties

We begin by defining weighted Morrey spaces and compare them to the
definition and the results in Part
I
.

Definition 31
.

(Weighted Morrey Spaces) Let
0<p\leq u\leq\infty
,
and
\omega
be a weight. Then the weighted Morrey spaces are defined
by

M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)=\left\{f\in L_{p}^{loc}\left(\omega\right):\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|<\infty\right\},

with their corresponding quasi-norm

\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},R>0}\omega\left(B_{R}\left(x\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{u}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B_{R}\left(x\right)}|f\left(y\right)|^{p}\omega\left(y\right)dy\right)^{1/p}.

For
p=\infty
we define the norm
\|\cdot|M_{\infty,\infty}\left(\omega\right)\|

with respect to the norm of essentially bounded functions analogously.

As usual
\|\cdot|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|
is a norm
for
p\geq 1
and a quasi-norm for
p<1
, as we will show in the
next lemma.. When we have a look at the definition of weighted Morrey
spaces and recall the facts for their unweighted counterparts from
Part
I
, we are able to immediately transfer certain
features without too much work. For the analogue proofs, we refer
to Lemma
8
and
9
.

Lemma 32
.

(i) If
u<p
, then
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)=\left\{0\right\}
.

(ii) If
u=\infty
, then
M_{\infty,p}\left(\omega\right)=L_{\infty}\left(\omega\right)

with equivalence of the corresponding norms.

(iii) If
u=p
we have
M_{p,p}\left(\omega\right)=L_{p}
\left(\omega\right)
.

(iv)
\left(M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)|\|\cdot|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|\right)

is a quasi-Banach space.

Proof.

We will only prove (iv). As we have already mentioned in Lemma
8
,
the proofs of (i) and (ii) can be found in
[
R09
, Rem. 1.2.]

for the unweighted case, but can be adapted when we add a weight.
(iii) follows immediately by the definition of the norm of weighted
Morrey spaces. The proof of (iv) follows
[
R09
, Thm. 1.3.]

(i) almost exactly. By Lemma
32
(ii) it is
enough to focus on
p<\infty
.

At first we prove that
\|\cdot|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|

is a norm for
p\geq 1
and a quasi-norm for
0<p<1
. It is obvious
that
\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|\geq 0
for all

f\in M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
. Now since
\omega>0
a.e. we
have

\displaystyle\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\|f|L_{p}\left(B,\omega\right)\|=0\mbox{ for all balls }B

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\|f|L_{p}\left(\omega\right)\|=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle f=0\mbox{ a.e.}.

The next step
\|\lambda f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|=\left|\lambda\right|\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is obvious. At last we see that the triangle
inequality holds for
p\geq 1
by using Minkowski’s inequality for
weighted Lebesgue spaces , i.e.

\displaystyle\|f+g|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u-1/p}\|f+g|L_{p}\left(B,\omega\right)\|

\displaystyle\overset{\tiny\mbox{Lem. }\ref{lem:HoelderUndMinkowski}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u-1/p}\left(\|f|L_{p}\left(B,\omega\right)\|+\|g|L_{p}\left(B,\omega\right)\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|+\|g|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|.

For
p<1
we use
\|f+g|L_{p}\left(\omega\right)\|\leq 2^{1/p-1}\left(\|f|L_{p}\left(\omega\right)\|+\|g|L_{p}\left(\omega\right)\|\right)

for
f,g\in L_{p}\left(\omega\right)
, which is obtained by adding
a weight to the proof in
[
E11
, Thm. 1.10.]
. Hence we
get
\|f+g|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|\leq 2^{1/p-1}\left(\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|+\|g|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|\right)
.

The next fact we need is, that
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
is a
vector space, which is easily deduced by the fact that
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\subset L_{p}\left(\omega\right)
,
i.e.
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
is a quasi-normed subspace of the
weighted Lebesgue spaces, thus making it a vector space.

The last thing to show is that
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
is complete.
We will have a look at
p\geq 1
first. Therefore let
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

be a Cauchy sequence in
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
. We have to
show the existence of some
f\in M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
with

\|f-f_{n}|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
.
Now since
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence,
we know that there is a subsequence
\left(f_{n_{k}}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

such that we have
\|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|\leq\frac{1}{2^{k}}

for
k=1,2,\ldots
. We set
g:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}|
,
which obviously is non-negative and measurable and show that
g\in M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)

as follows:

(14)

\displaystyle\|g|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|g\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|f_{n_{k+1}}\left(x\right)-f_{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\right|\right)^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|f_{n_{k+1}}\left(x\right)-f_{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\right|\right)^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\lim_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\ldots=\lim_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{B}\ldots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|f_{n_{k+1}}\left(x\right)-f_{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\right||M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|

(15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|f_{n_{k+1}}\left(x\right)-f_{n_{k}}\left(x\right)|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1.

Since
g
is non-negative and measurable we can use the Lebesgue
monotone convergence theorem (
[
T92
, App. 1, Thm. 2]
),
which here allows us to change positions of limes and supremum. As
next step, we show that
g<\infty
almost everywhere. In (
14
)
we see that
\int_{B}\left|g\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|g\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\chi_{B}\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)dx<\infty

for all balls
B
. We can deduce that
g\chi_{B}
is finite with
respect to the Lebesgue measure almost everywhere or that there is
a set with measure zero, i.e.
\left|N_{R,x_{0}}\right|=0
, such
that
g\left(x\right)\chi_{B}\left(x\right)<\infty
for all
x\notin N_{R,x_{0}}

if we denote
B=B\left(R,x_{0}\right)
. Now we define

N:=\bigcup_{R\in Q_{+},x_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}}N_{R,x_{0}},

and see
0\leq\mu\left(N\right)\leq\sum_{R\in Q_{+},x_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}}\mu\left(N_{r,x_{0}}\right)=0
,
which makes
N
itself a zero set with respect to the Lebesgue measure.
This however yields that
g<\infty
almost everywhere, because for
every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash N
there are
R\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}

and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
such that
y\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
.
By choice of
y
and
N
we know that
y\notin N_{R,x_{0}}
and
thus
g\left(y\right)<\infty
. With that in mind, we define a set

H:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,0\leq g<\infty\right\}
. By our
above considerations
H
is measurable and
\left|\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash H\right|=0
.
Since we have shown
g\in M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
, we also know
that
g^{*}:=g\chi_{H}\in M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
. Now we define

f
as follows

f:=\begin{cases}f_{n_{1}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}\right|&x\in H\\
0&x\notin H\end{cases}.

Clearly
f\leq\left|f_{n_{1}}\right|+g^{*}
and thus
f\in M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
.
The last thing we need for the proof of the completeness of
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
,
is that
f_{n}\rightarrow f
in
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
. Because

f_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence, it is sufficient to show that
f_{n_{k}}\rightarrow f

in
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
. During this last estimation we will
again use the Lebesgue monotone convergence theorem.

(16)

\displaystyle\|f-f_{n_{m}}|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}f_{n_{k+1}}\left(x\right)-f_{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u-1/p}\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{B}\left|\sum_{k=m}^{l}f_{n_{k+1}}\left(x\right)-f_{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\sum_{k=m}^{l}f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=m}^{l}\|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|\leq\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\overset{\tiny m\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

∎

To prove the completeness of
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
for
p<1

we modify the proof for
p\geq 1
by replacing every use of the triangle
inequality (i.e. (
15
) and (
16
)) by using a
corresponding
\varrho
-inequality instead (i.e. using
\|f+g\|^{\varrho}\leq\|f\|^{\varrho}+\|g\|^{\varrho}

for some
\varrho\in\left(0,1\right]
. Here we apply the fact that
for every quasi-norm with constant
C\geq 1
there exists a corresponding

\varrho
- norm with
\varrho\in\left(0,1\right]
. See
[
DL93
, Ch. 2, Thm. 1.1.]

for the proof). The remaining facts of Lemma
9

(mostly concerning embeddings) will be answered in Section
6
.

Another fact worth transferring to the weighted case is the following
lemma. The proof of the unweighted case was taken from
[
R09
, Lem. 1.4.]
,
where a further reference to
[
KY07
, Lem. 1.4.]

is made.

Lemma 33
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\omega

be a weight and
\left(u_{j}\right)_{j=1}^{m}
,
\left(p_{j}\right)_{j=1}^{m}

with
0<p_{j}\leq u_{j}<\infty
for
j=1,\ldots,m
. Furthermore
let
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{p}
and
f_{j}\in M_{u_{j},p_{j}}\left(\omega\right)

for
j=1,\ldots,m
. We define
\frac{1}{u}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{u_{j}}
.
Then for
f_{0}\in L_{\infty}
we have

f_{0}\cdot f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}\in M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right).

Proof.

At first, we have
p\leq u
, since

\frac{1}{u}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{u_{j}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{p}.

With this choice of parameters
p
and
u
the space
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)

is non-trivial (also recall Lemma
32
(i)).
Now let
B
be a ball. We have to show both
f_{0}\cdot f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}\in M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)

and
f_{0}\cdot f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}\in L_{p}^{loc}\left(\omega\right)
,
though by showing the first, the latter is implied. We begin by showing
that applying Hölder’s inequality
n
-times yields the following:

(17)

\|f_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{n}|L_{p}\left(B\right)\|\leq\|f_{0}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}|L_{p_{i}}\left(B\right)\|\right)\|1|L_{1}\left(B\right)\|^{1/p-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}}.

We do so, by induction. Clearly for
m=1
we have

\|f_{0}\cdot f_{1}|L_{p}\left(B\right)\|\leq\|f_{0}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\|f_{1}|L_{p_{1}}\left(B\right)\|\left(\int_{B}1dx\right)^{1/p\cdot 1/r^{\prime}},

where
r^{\prime}
is the dual to
r=\frac{p_{1}}{p}
and hence
r^{\prime}=\frac{p_{1}}{p_{1}-p}
.
The exponent then is

\frac{1}{p}\cdot\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p}\cdot\frac{p_{1}-p}{p_{1}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{1}},

and as a result
\left(\int_{B}1dx\right)^{1/p\cdot 1/r^{\prime}}=\left|B\right|^{1/p-1/p_{1}}
.
Let us now assume that (
17
)
is true for a fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We show, that it also holds
for
m+1
:

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m+1}|L_{p}\left(B\right)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f_{0}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}|L_{p_{j}}\left(B\right)\|\left(\int_{B}|f_{m+1}|^{\left(\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)^{-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}}.

By that, we get the parameter
r
for the Hölder inequality as
r=p_{m+1}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)
.
Its dual then is
r^{\prime}=\frac{p_{m+1}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)}{p_{m+1}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)-1}
.
By having a look at the outside exponent of the integral, we see that

\frac{p_{m+1}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)-1}{p_{m+1}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}\right)=\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}\frac{1}{p_{j}},

which concludes the induction.

The Hölder inequality also holds for weighted
L_{p}
spaces (see
Remark
3
). Hence, we can also use the upper induction
when we integrate the weighted volume of the ball, i.e. replacing

\int_{B}1dx
with
\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx
. Doing so yields
the following chain of inequalities

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u-1/p}\cdot\left(\int_{B}|f_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f_{0}|L_{\infty}\|\sup_{B}c_{p}\cdot\omega\left(B\right)^{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}1/u_{j}\right)-1/p}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}|L_{p_{i}}\left(B,\omega\right)\|\omega\left(B\right)^{1/p-\sum_{j=1}^{m}1/p_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{p}\|f|L_{\infty}\|\sup_{B}\omega\left(B\right)^{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}1/u_{j}-1/p_{j}\right)}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}|L_{p_{i}}\left(B,\omega\right)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f_{0}|L_{\infty}\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}|M_{u_{i},p_{i}}\left(\omega\right)\|,

which concludes the first step of the proof.

The next step is to show that
f_{0}\cdot f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}\in L_{p}^{loc}\left(\omega\right)
.
Let
K
be any compact set, then we can choose
R^{*}>0
and
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
such that
K\subset B_{R^{*}}\left(x^{*}\right)
. Furthermore we
have

\displaystyle\omega\left(B_{R^{*}}\left(x^{*}\right)\right)^{1/u-1/p}\left(\int_{B_{R^{*}}\left(x^{*}\right)}\left|f_{0}\left(x\right)\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u-1/p}\left(\int_{B_{R}}\left|f_{0}\left(x\right)\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|\leq C\|f_{0}|L_{\infty}\|\|f_{1}|M_{u_{1},p_{1}}\left(\omega\right)\|\cdot\ldots\cdot\|f_{m}|M_{u_{m},p_{m}}\left(\omega\right)\|,

which we have just shown in the previous step. This chain of inequalities
however, shows that

\int_{K}\left|f_{0}\left(x\right)\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\leq\int_{B_{R^{*}\left(x^{*}\right)}}\left|f_{0}\left(x\right)\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{m}\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx<\infty,

which concludes the second step and the proof.
∎

from where we know that

\left(\int_{K}\left(\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{r}\right)^{\frac{p}{r}}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{\frac{r}{p}}\leq\left(\int_{B_{R^{*}}\left(x^{*}\right)}\left(\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{r}\right)^{\frac{p}{r}}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)<\infty

Remark 36
.

Some of the papers we quote and use for the following text refer to
weighted Morrey spaces when using cubes
Q=Q_{r}\left(x_{0}\right)

instead of balls
B=B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
(e.g.
[
KS09
]
).
In some articles and papers, (weighted) Morrey spaces are introduced
with the quasi-norm

\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|^{*}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\omega\left(Q_{r}\left(x\right)\right)^{1/u-1/p}\left(\int_{Q_{r}\left(x\right)}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(y\right)dy\right)^{1/p}

(again
\|\cdot|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|^{*}
is a
norm for
p\geq 1
and a quasi-norm for
0<p<1
). We will quickly
show that this quasi-norm is equivalent to the previously defined
one, i.e.
\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|\sim\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|^{*}
.
Recall that
\int_{Q_{r}\left(x\right)}1dx=r^{n}
and
\int_{B_{R}\left(x\right)}1dx=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}R^{n},

where
\Gamma
is the gamma function. Obviously we will always find

c,C>0
such that
cR^{n}\leq r^{n}\leq CR^{n}
, since the dimension

n
is fixed. Roughly speaking, this means that we can always find
two
n
-dimensional balls, such that a fixed
n
-dimensional cube
contains a smaller ball and is contained in a bigger ball or vice
versa. From here it is easy to see that this implies
\omega\left(Q_{r}\left(x\right)\right)\sim\omega\left(B_{R}\left(x\right)\right)

and
\int_{Q_{r}\left(x\right)}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(y\right)dy\sim\int_{B_{R}\left(x\right)}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(y\right)dy

respectively, since both
\omega\geq 0
and
\left|f\right|\geq 0

a.e.. Henceforth we shall use both norms (i.e.
\|\cdot|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|

and
\|\cdot|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|^{*}
) equivalently,
depending on which one suits the current purpose better.

5.
Boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood Maximal
Operator on

In analogy to Theorem
23
we will now have a
look at the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator (and the weighted maximal
operator) and show its boundedness as a mapping from a weighted Morrey
space to itself.

Definition 37
.

Let
\omega
be a weight. Then
we define the weighted maximal operator as

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}f\left(x\right)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\frac{1}{\omega\left(Q\right)}\int_{Q}|f\left(y\right)|\omega\left(y\right)dy.

Now, as we have seen before in Corollary
28
,
we know that every Muckenhoupt weight
\omega\in A_{p}
is also a
doubling measure. There is one more property of doubling measures
we will need in the main proof of this section. It is taken from
[
KS09
, Lem. 4.1.]
.

Lemma 38
.

Let
\omega
be a doubling measure, then
there is a constant
D>1
such that

D\omega\left(Q\right)\leq\omega\left(2Q\right).

Proof.

We begin by fixing a cube
Q:=Q_{r}\left(x_{0}\right)
. Then we can
choose a cube
K\subset 2Q
with side length
\frac{r}{2}
, which
is disjoint from
Q
. We get

\omega\left(Q\right)+\omega\left(K\right)\leq\omega\left(2Q\right).

For that same
K
, we get
Q\subset 5K
, which yields
\omega\left(Q\right)\leq\omega\left(5K\right)\leq C^{3}\omega\left(K\right)
,
where the doubling constant is
C>0
(see Definition
25
).
Inserting this in the above estimation yields

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{C^{3}}\right)\omega\left(Q\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega\left(Q\right)+\frac{\omega\left(Q\right)}{C^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega\left(Q\right)+\omega\left(K\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega\left(2Q\right),

where
D:=\left(1+\frac{1}{C^{3}}\right)>1
is the desired constant.

We will also need another lemma to prove the main theorem of this
chapter, . This fact is taken from
[
KS09
, Lem. 4.2. (2)]

with further reference to
[
G09
, Sect. 9.1.1.]
.
∎

Lemma 39
.

Let
\omega\in A_{p}
for some
1<p<\infty
.
Then

\mathcal{M}f\left(x\right)\leq C\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(|f|^{p}\right)\left(x\right)^{1/p},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

At first we fix a ball
B
and let

.
We know that (
10
) holds for
f
. Also we
clearly have

(18)

\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|dx\leq\mathcal{M}\left(f\chi_{B}\right)\left(y\right)

for all
y\in B
. We weight-integrate the above to the
p
-th power
over all

and apply Theorem

to get

\displaystyle\int_{B}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|dx\right)^{p}\omega\left(y\right)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega\left(B\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|dx\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:Inequality?}}{\leq}

The lemma follows after dividing by

, taking
the

-th power of the inequality and afterwards taking the supremum

Theorem 41
.

If
1<p<u<\infty
, and
\omega
a doubling-measure,

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
is bounded on
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)

to itself.

Proof.

First we fix a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and decompose
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
.
We choose
f_{1}:=f\chi_{3Q}
and
f_{2}
accordingly. It is easy
to check that
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
is a sublinear operator, since

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(f+g\right)\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{Q\ni y}\frac{1}{\omega\left(Q\right)}\int_{Q}\left|f+g\right|\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{Q\ni y}\frac{1}{\omega\left(Q\right)}\int_{Q}\left(\left|f\right|+\left|g\right|\right)\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{Q\ni y}\frac{1}{\omega\left(Q\right)}\int_{Q}\left|f\right|\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(f\right)\left(y\right)+\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(g\right)\left(y\right).

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Q}\mathcal{M}_{\omega}f\left(x\right)^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

)

(19)

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{M}_{\omega}f_{1}\left(x\right)^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left(\int_{3Q}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|{}^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle C\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)\|\omega\left(Q\right)^{1/p-1/u},

where the last step follows from the doubling measure properties of

\omega
.

by considering some geometric properties.

whenever

. Again, we used that

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\cdot\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega\right)\|\omega\left(Q\right)^{-1/u}\left(\int_{Q}\omega\left(y\right)dy\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\cdot\|f|M_{u,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega\right)\|\omega\left(Q\right)^{1/p-1/u}.

This and (

) ultimately complete the proof, after multiplying

and taking the supremum over all

Theorem 42
.

If
1<p\leq u<\infty
, and
\omega\in A_{p}

\mathcal{M}

.

\displaystyle\left(\omega\left(Q\right)^{-\kappa}\int_{Q}\mathcal{M}f\left(x\right)^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\cdot\|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\left|f\right|{}^{r}\right)|M_{u/r,p/r}\left(\omega\right)\|^{1/r}

\displaystyle\int_{\left\{x\,:\,Mf\left(x\right)>t\right\}}\chi_{Q}\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|\mathcal{M}\left(\omega\chi_{Q}\right)\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{t}\left(\int_{3Q}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|\mathcal{M}\left(\omega\chi_{Q}\right)\left(x\right)dx+\int_{3Q^{c}}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|\mathcal{M}\left(\omega\chi_{Q}\right)\left(x\right)dx\right)

\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R\cap Q}\omega\left(y\right)dy

for
x\in 3Q^{c}
we have
x\in R
and
R\cap Q\neq\emptyset
. Also
recall, that
Q=Q_{r}\left(x_{0}\right)
. Geometrically speaking,
we have

\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R\cap Q}\omega\left(y\right)dy\leq C_{n}\left(\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}\int_{Q}\omega\left(y\right)dy\right)\leq C_{n}\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-n}\omega\left(Q\right).

Hence
\mathcal{M}\left(\omega\chi_{Q}\right)\left(x\right)\leq C_{n}\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{-n}\omega\left(Q\right)
.
Since
\omega\in A_{1}
we know, that it is a doubling measure with
constant
D>1
. Another estimation yields

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\omega\left(Q\right)\int_{\left(3Q\right)^{c}}\frac{|f\left(x\right)|}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\omega\left(Q\right)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|3^{j}Q|}\int_{3^{j+1}Q}|f\left(x\right)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\omega\left(Q\right)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|3^{j}Q|}\frac{|3^{j+1}Q|}{\omega\left(3^{j+1}Q\right)}\int_{3^{j+1}Q}|f\left(x\right)|\omega\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\omega\left(Q\right)^{\kappa}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega\left(Q\right)^{1-\kappa}}{\omega\left(3^{j+1}Q\right)^{1-\kappa}}\frac{1}{\omega\left(3^{j+1}Q\right)^{\kappa}}\int_{3^{j+1}Q}|f\left(x\right)|\omega\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\omega\left(Q\right)^{\kappa}\|f|M_{u,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega\right)\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega\left(Q\right)^{1-\kappa}}{\omega\left(3^{j+1}Q\right)^{1-\kappa}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\omega\left(Q\right)^{\kappa}\|f|M_{u,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega\right)\|

Throughout this estimation we used a variety of geometric properties
and the last estimation is gained through the convergence of the series
(recall Lemma
38
), i.e.
\frac{\omega\left(Q\right)}{\omega\left(3^{j+1}Q\right)}\leq\left(\frac{1}{D}\right)^{j+1}<1
,
since
D>1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 43
.

The preceding two theorems were taken from
[
KS09
]
,
where more operators were introduced and treated the way we did with
the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and the weighted maximal operator.
The paper additionally deals with the Calderón-Zygmund operator

Tf\left(x\right)=\mbox{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K\left(x-y\right)f\left(y\right)dy,

where p.v. is the principle value of the integral. Furthermore for
given
0<\alpha<n
with the fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}

defined by

I_{\alpha}f\left(x\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f\left(y\right)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy,

and with the commutator operator given by

\left[b,T\right]f\left(x\right)=b\left(x\right)Tf\left(x\right)-T\left(bf\right)\left(x\right).

Here we only included the results for the maximal operators, since

use for embeddings of weighted Morrey spaces. For further results
refer to
[

, Sect. 3]
.

6.
Embeddings of Weighted Morrey
Spaces

In this section we study whether or not the embedding

M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)\hookrightarrow M_{u_{2},p}\left(\nu\right)

holds or more precisely for which choice of parameters
u_{1},u_{2},q,p

and weighs
\omega,\nu
. We will divert Muckenhoupt weights from
their originally intended use and equip Morrey spaces with them to
show that, in this case, certain embeddings can be proved. From now
on we will assume
0<q\leq u_{1}<\infty
and
0<p\leq u_{2}
. If

\omega
belongs to some Muckenhoupt class, i.e.
\omega\in A_{\infty}
,
we will use the notation

r_{\omega}=\inf\left\{r\geq 1\,|\,\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{r}\right\}

in this section.

Remark 44
.

This convention is useful if we recall Corollary
22
.
We showed that if
\omega\in A_{p}
for some
p>1
, then there is
always
1<p_{1}<p
such that
\omega\in A_{p_{1}}
, which justifies
the occurrence of the infimum term. For instance, it follows from

\omega\in A_{1}
that
r_{\omega}=1
. The converse, however, is
generally not true, i.e.
r_{\omega}=1
does not imply
\omega\in A_{1}
.
Also recall the weight
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}
from Example
19
.
We showed
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\in A_{r}
if and only if
-n<\alpha,\beta<n\left(r-1\right)

or after rearranging
r>\frac{\max\left(\alpha,\beta,0\right)}{n}+1
,
and hence we have the limit case
r_{\omega_{\alpha,\beta}}=\frac{\max\left(\alpha,\beta,0\right)}{n}+1
.

What follows are some results of embeddings, if we alter
u
and

p
separately.

Corollary 45
.

Let
0<p_{1}\leq u_{1}<\infty
and
0<p_{2}\leq u_{2}<\infty
. For
a given weight
\omega
let
f\in M_{u_{1},p_{1}}\left(\omega\right)

and
g\in M_{u_{2},p_{2}}\left(\omega\right)
. We then have
f\cdot g\in M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
,
if we define
\frac{1}{p}:=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
and
\frac{1}{u}:=\frac{1}{u_{1}}+\frac{1}{u_{2}}
.

Proof.

The proof is easily deduced from Lemma
33

if we choose
f_{0}\equiv 1
,
f_{1}=f
and
f_{2}=g
.
∎

Corollary 46
.

Let
0<p\leq q\leq u<\infty

and
\omega
be a weight, then we have

M_{u,q}\left(\omega\right)\hookrightarrow M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right).

Proof.

We use Lemma
33
with the choice
of
n=1
and
f_{0}=1
. For
f\in M_{u,q}\left(\omega\right)
we
quickly get that
f\in M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)
if we choose
\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}

or
p\leq q
respectively. The continuity of the embedding can be
seen in the last steps of the proof of the lemma.
∎

Naturally Lemma
32
(iii) also hints that (just
like their unweighted counterparts) weighted Morrey spaces are a generalization
of weighted Lebesgue spaces. However, there are functions that exclusively
belong to weighted Morrey spaces. We refer to
[
KS09
, Rem. 2.3. (3)]

for this example:

Example 47
.

For this example let
n=1
and
\omega\left(x\right)=\left|x\right|^{\alpha}

for some negative parameter
-\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<0
. By Example
19

we know by the choice of parameters that
\omega\in A_{p}
. If we
have a look at the function
f\left(x\right)=\chi_{\left(0,1\right)}\left|x\right|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
we can indeed verify that

f\in M_{2\left(\alpha+1\right),1}\left(\omega\right)\backslash M_{2\left(\alpha+1\right),2\left(\alpha+1\right)}\left(\omega\right)=M_{2\left(\alpha+1\right),1}\left(\omega\right)\backslash L_{2\left(\alpha+1\right)}\left(\omega\right)

By Corollary
46
we know that
M_{2\left(\alpha+1\right),q}\left(\omega\right)\hookrightarrow M_{2\left(\alpha+1\right),1}\left(\omega\right)

for
1<q\leq 2\left(\alpha+1\right)
, such that
f
exclusively belongs
to weighted Morrey spaces.

Corollary 48
.

Let
0<q\leq u_{1}<\infty
and

\omega
be a weight. If
u_{1}=u_{2}
and
p\leq q
, then

M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)\hookrightarrow M_{u_{2},p}\left(\omega\right).

.

Proof.

We have already seen this statement before in Corollary
46
,
although we did not try to alter
u
there. Let us have a look at
an alternative proof technique. For
f\in M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)

and
B
a ball we see the following

\displaystyle\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)^{p/q}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p/q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}\omega\left(x\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{1/p-1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p+1/p-1/q+1/u_{1}-1/u_{1}}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}-1/q}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}}\|f|M_{u,q}\left(\omega\right)\|,

where we used Hölder’s inequality for
p<q
. Now if we assume that

u_{1}=u_{2}
the last estimate obviously holds.
∎

Remark 49
.

Corollary
48
does not yield any new
information when we compare it to Corollary
46
.
It uses
u_{1}=u_{2}
as a sufficient condition for the embedding
to hold and this different proof technique leaves us with no evidence
that it could also be a necessary condition. However, considering
the weight
\omega\equiv 1
(which classifies for the conditions in
Corollary
48
) brings us back to Theorem

10
and gives us an idea that
u_{1}=u_{2}

could indeed be mandatory.

Proposition 50
.

Let
0<q\leq u_{1}<\infty
and
0<p\leq u_{2}<\infty
.
Furthermore let
\omega\in A_{\infty}
with
r_{\omega}>1
and the
following two conditions be true

(20)

\sup_{B}\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\leq C<\infty

(21)

r_{\omega}<\frac{q}{p}.

Then the following embedding holds

M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)\hookrightarrow M_{u_{2},p}.

Furthermore, if
\omega\in A_{1}
then
p=q
is admitted.

Proof.

Let
f\in M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)
and
B
be a ball, then

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)^{p/q}\omega\left(x\right)^{-p/q}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}dx\right)^{1/p-1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}-1/q}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\omega\left(B\right)^{1/q-1/u_{1}}\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}dx\right)^{1/p-1/q}.

Here we used Hölder’s inequality in the first estimation, which holds,
since
\frac{q}{p}>r_{\omega}>1
by assumption (
21
).
After multiplying both sides of the inequality with
\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/p}
,
we see that
f\in M_{u_{2},p}
if

(22)

\sup_{B}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/q-1/u_{1}}\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}dx\right)^{1/p-1/q}<\infty.

Now by assumption (
21
)
r_{\omega}<\frac{q}{p}
,
we know that
\omega\in A_{\frac{q}{p}}
and hence

(23)

\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\leq C.

holds for some
C>0
. So after taking (
23
)
to the
1/q
-th power, we see that (
22
)
yields

\displaystyle\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/q-1/u_{1}}\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}dx\right)^{1/p-1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\underset{\leq C}{\underbrace{\left|B\right|^{-1/p}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/q}\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}dx\right)^{1/p-1/q}}}.

After taking the supremum over all such balls
B
, we see that
\sup_{B}\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\leq C<\infty

by assumption (
20
).

We will now have a look at the case
\omega\in A_{1}
in which
p=q

is allowed. We have

(24)

\displaystyle\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/q}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)^{-1}dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\omega^{-1}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|^{1/q}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/q}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\omega^{-1}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|^{1/q}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/q-1/u_{1}}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/q}

\displaystyle\cdot\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}-1/q}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}.

Since
\omega\in A_{1}
, we conclude
\|\omega^{-1}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\leq A\left|B\right|\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{-1}

for all balls
B
. Taking this inequality to the
1/q
-th power
and inserting it in (
24
) leaves us with

\displaystyle\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}-1/q}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\|f|M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)\|,

where the first term is finite by assumption.
∎

Example 51
.

Consider
\omega\equiv 1
, then
\omega\in A_{s}
for every
1\leq s\leq\infty
.
So for
\omega\equiv 1
, condition (
20
)
becomes
\sup_{B}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}}\leq C<\infty
and
hence necessarily
u_{1}=u_{2}
. Condition (
21
)
then turns into
p\leq q
. Through this choice of parameters coincides
with Theorem
10
or Corollary
12

respectively and is what we expected for unweighted Morrey spaces.

Remark 52
.

Note that the requirement
0<p\leq u_{2}
is not explicitly needed
if we additionally demand
\sup_{B_{1}}\omega\left(B_{1}\right)\leq A<\infty

for balls of radius
1
, (i.e.
B_{1}
), but is rather implied by

\sup_{B}\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\leq C<\infty

and
0<q\leq u_{1}
. To see this, we have a look at all balls
B

with
R>1
and recall

\frac{\left|B_{1}\right|}{\left|B_{R}\right|}\leq C\left(\frac{\omega\left(B_{1}\right)}{\omega\left(B_{R}\right)}\right)^{1/r}\leq CA^{1/r}\cdot\omega\left(B_{R}\right)^{-1/r},

for some
r>1
(see Remark
27
and also
[
S93
, Ch. V, 1.7]
)
from which immediately follows that

\frac{1}{\omega\left(B_{R}\right)}\geq\frac{c}{\left|B_{R}\right|^{r}}.

Now the chain of inequalities is as follows:

\infty>C\geq\sup_{B}\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\geq\sup_{B:\left|B\right|>1}\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\geq c\sup_{B:\left|B\right|>1}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-r/u_{1}}.

Naturally we need
\frac{1}{u_{2}}-\frac{r}{u_{1}}\leq 0
and with
condition (
21
) of Proposition

we get
\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}\leq r\leq\frac{q}{p}
.
This turns into
\frac{p}{u_{2}}\leq\frac{q}{u_{1}}\leq 1
by assumption
and thus
p\leq u_{2}
.

Corollary 53
.

Let
-n<\alpha,\beta<n\left(p-1\right)

and
\omega\left(x\right)=\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)=\begin{cases}\left|x\right|^{\alpha}&,\,\left|x\right|\leq 1\\
\left|x\right|^{\beta}&,\,\left|x\right|>1\end{cases}
. Assume

(25)

\frac{1}{u_{1}}\left(1+\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\geq\frac{1}{u_{2}}\geq\frac{1}{u_{1}}\left(1+\frac{\max\left(\alpha,0\right)}{n}\right)\,\mbox{ and}

(26)

\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{1}{q}\left(1+\frac{\max\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}{n}\right).

Then the following embedding holds

M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\right)\hookrightarrow M_{u_{2},p}.

Proof.

We want to apply Proposition
50
and therefore
need to check conditions (
20
)
and (
21
) for the specific
weight
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}
. We already know that
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\in A_{r}

if and only if
-n<\alpha,\beta<n\left(r-1\right)
(see Example
19
).

(i) At first we will have a look at the requirement
\sup_{B}\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\leq C<\infty
.
Since all balls
B
are admissible for taking the supremum, we have
to differ between all the possible cases. The different parts we will
consider, are similar to the ones we checked earlier on in Example

19
. For all considered cases, we keep in mind that the
parameters are set as
-n<\alpha,\beta<n\left(p-1\right)
.

Part 1
: Let
x_{0}=0
. Then for
R<1

\sup_{B}\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\leq C\sup_{R<1}R^{n/u_{2}-\left(\alpha+n\right)/u_{1}},

which we find to be finite for
u_{2}\leq
\frac{nu_{1}}{\left(\alpha+n\right)}
.
For
R>1
we are in the situation

\sup_{B}\frac{\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\leq C\sup_{R>1}R^{n/u_{2}-\left(\beta+n\right)/u_{1}},

where we need
u_{2}\geq\frac{nu_{1}}{\left(\beta+n\right)}
.

Part 2
: Now let
R>0
and
0<\left|x_{0}\right|<\frac{R}{2}
.

As before the following inclusion holds:
B_{\frac{R}{2}}\left(0\right)\subset B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\subset B_{\frac{3}{2}R}\left(0\right)
.
Also, we have for some
c,C>0
that
c\left|B_{\frac{3}{2}R}\left(0\right)\right|\leq\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|\leq C\left|B_{\frac{R}{2}}\left(0\right)\right|
,
from which (with the help of (
4
))
we now deduce

\displaystyle c\left|B_{\frac{3}{2}R}\left(0\right)\right|^{1/u_{2}}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}R}\left(0\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx\right)^{-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|^{1/u_{2}}\left(\int_{B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx\right)^{-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left|B_{\frac{1}{2}R}\left(0\right)\right|^{1/u_{2}}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}R}\left(0\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx\right)^{-1/u_{1}}.

For
R<\frac{2}{3}
or
R>2
we get
\frac{1}{2}R<\frac{3}{2}R<1

and
\frac{3}{2}R>\frac{R}{2}>1
respectively, meaning that we can
apply part 1 to get the same conditions for
u_{2}
. For the remaining
case
\frac{2}{3}<R<2
, we go back to (
5
)
and (
6
). Together with the estimation

c\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n}\leq\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|\leq C2^{n}

we get that
\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|^{1/u_{2}}\left(\int_{B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/u_{1}}\sim C
,
which gives us no new information about the choice of values for
u_{2}
.

Part 3
: Let
R>0
and
\left|x_{0}\right|>2R
.

In this case, we use the triangle inequality for all
x\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)

to get

(27)

\displaystyle\left|x\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|x_{0}\right|+\left|x-x_{0}\right|\leq\frac{3}{2}\left|x_{0}\right|\,\mbox{and}

(28)

\displaystyle\left|x\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left|x_{0}\right|-\left|x-x_{0}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|x_{0}\right|,

which is essentially
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x_{0}\right)\sim\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)

for all
x\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
. We subdivide part 3 into
smaller steps, namely
\left|x_{0}\right|<\frac{2}{3}
,
\frac{2}{3}<\left|x_{0}\right|<2

and
2<\left|x_{0}\right|
.

Let us first check
\left|x_{0}\right|>2
, for which we get
\left|x\right|>1

for all
x\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
by (
28
) .
We have to find an assignment of parameters, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\left|x_{0}\right|>2}\sup_{0<R\leq 1}\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|^{1/u_{2}}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)^{-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A<\infty\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\sup_{R>1}\sup_{\left|x_{0}\right|>2R}\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|^{1/u_{2}}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)^{-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A<\infty

hold. Now since
\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)dx\sim\int_{B}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x_{0}\right)dx
=
\left|B\right|\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x_{0}\right)
,
the above inequalities are equivalent to

(29)

\displaystyle\sup_{\left|x_{0}\right|>2}\sup_{0<R\leq 1}R^{n\left(1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}\right)}\left|x_{0}\right|^{-\beta/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A<\infty\mbox{ and}

(30)

\displaystyle\sup_{R>1}\sup_{\left|x_{0}\right|>2R}R^{n\left(1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}\right)}\left|x_{0}\right|^{-\beta/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A<\infty.

Inequality (
29
) now requires
u_{1}\geq u_{2}
and

\beta\geq 0
to be bounded whereas (
30
) leads to

\sup_{R>1}R^{n\left(1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}\right)-\beta/u_{1}}\leq A<\infty,

from which we again get the condition
\frac{\beta+n}{nu_{1}}\geq\frac{1}{u_{2}}
.

We move on to the next case, namely
\left|x_{0}\right|<\frac{2}{3}
,
for which we get
\left|x\right|<1
for all
x\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)

by (
27
). Similarly to the above, we need to check
for which choice of parameters

(31)

\sup_{0<R<\frac{1}{3}}\sup_{2R<\left|x_{0}\right|<\frac{2}{3}}R^{n\left(1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}\right)}\left|x_{0}\right|^{-\alpha/u_{1}}\leq A<\infty.

As we can see, this is only finite, if
\frac{1}{u_{2}}\geq\frac{\max\left(\alpha,0\right)+n}{nu_{1}}
.
The maximum occurs, because we are considering
\left|x_{0}\right|\leq\frac{2}{3}
,
which are bounded for negative
\alpha
anyway.

The last remaining case is
\frac{2}{3}\leq\left|x_{0}\right|\leq 2
,
which converts to
\frac{1}{3}\leq\left|x\right|\leq 3
for all
x\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
.
Here we rather quickly get

\min\left(\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{\alpha},1,3^{\beta}\right)\leq\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)\leq\max\left(\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{\alpha},1,3^{\beta}\right),

and thus
\left|B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|^{1/u_{2}}\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)^{-1/u_{1}}\sim C

in this case.

Part 4:
Let
\frac{1}{2}R\leq\left|x_{0}\right|\leq 2R
.

We reduce this last remaining part to the previous ones in two steps.
At first recall the triangle inequality once more

\left|x\right|\leq\left|x_{0}\right|+\left|x-x_{0}\right|\leq 3R,\mbox{ for all }x\in B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right),

i.e.
B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)\subset B_{3R}\left(0\right)
, which
gives an upper bound and brings us back to Part 1. As for the second
step, recall that
B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
is an open set, thus
enclosing another open ball with smaller radius, say
R^{\prime}=\frac{1}{6}R
.
With
\left|x_{0}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}R>\frac{1}{3}R=2R^{\prime}
and
B_{R^{\prime}}\left(x_{0}\right)\subset B_{R}\left(x_{0}\right)
,
we get a lower bound and return to Part 3. Thus Part 4 does not yield
any new relevant information about any of the parameters. As a conclusion
we get (
25
).

(ii) As we have already seen in Example
19
, we know that

\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\in A_{r}
if and only if
-n<\alpha,\beta<n\left(r-1\right)
.
Since we are dealing with Muckenhoupt classes, we are interested in

r\geq 1
, so by rearranging the previous inequality, we find
r>\frac{\max\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}{n}+1

to be a valid requirement (note that (
25
)
indirectly implies
\beta\geq 0
.). Lastly we have
r_{\omega_{\alpha,\beta}}=\frac{\max\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}{n}+1
.
This finally yields (
26
) and
concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 54
.

Let
0<q\leq u_{1}
and
0<p\leq u_{2}
.
Furthermore let
\nu^{-1}\in A_{\infty}
and the following two conditions
be true:

(32)

\sup_{B}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\nu\left(B\right)}{\left|B\right|}\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}}\leq C<\infty

(33)

r_{\nu^{-1}}<2-\frac{p}{q}.

Then the following embedding holds

M_{u_{1},q}\hookrightarrow M_{u_{2},p}\left(\nu\right).

Furthermore, if
\nu^{-1}\in A_{1}
then
p=q
is admitted.

Proof.

Let
f\in M_{u_{1},q}
then by using Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{q}{p}>1

we gain

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\nu\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\left(\int_{B}\nu\left(x\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{qp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|B\right|^{1/u_{1}-1/q}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\left|B\right|^{1/q-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\cdot\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\nu\left(x\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{qp}}.

We see that
f\in M_{u_{2},p}\left(\nu\right)
if

(34)

\sup_{B}\left|B\right|^{1/q-1/u_{1}}\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\nu\left(x\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{qp}}\leq C<\infty.

Now similarly to Proposition
50
, we want
to use the Muckenhoupt properties of
\nu^{-1}
. By assumption (
33
),
i.e.
r_{\nu^{-1}}<2-\frac{p}{q}
, we know that
\nu^{-1}\in A_{2-\frac{p}{q}}

and thus

\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\nu^{-1}\left(x\right)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\left(\nu^{-1}\right)^{-\frac{q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\leq C<\infty.

Of course this inequality holds after taking it to the
1/p
-th power.
Now we see that (
34
) turns into

\displaystyle\left|B\right|^{1/q-1/u_{1}}\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\nu\left(x\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{qp}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|B\right|^{1/q-1/u_{1}}\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left|B\right|^{1/p}\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)^{-1/p}

\displaystyle\cdot\left|B\right|^{\frac{q-p}{qp}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\nu^{\frac{q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{qp}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left|B\right|^{1/q-1/u_{1}}\cdot\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left|B\right|^{1/p}

\displaystyle\cdot\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)^{-1/p}\left|B\right|^{1/p-1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\nu\left(B\right)}{\left|B\right|}\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}}.

By assumption the last term is bounded after taking the supremum over
all balls
B
and thus
f\in M_{u_{2},p}\left(\nu\right)
.

At last, let us have a look at the case
\nu^{-1}\in A_{1}
in which

p=q
is allowed. Let
f\in M_{u_{1},q}
to see

(35)

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\nu\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\nu\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\|\nu|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\nu\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\|\nu|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\left|B\right|^{1/p-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left|B\right|^{1/u_{1}-1/q}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\nu\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}.

Now since
\nu^{-1}\in A_{1}
, we know that
\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}\nu\left(x\right)^{-1}dx\leq C\nu\left(y\right)^{-1}

for all
y\in B
. Rearranging and taking the
1/p
-th power yields

C\left(\nu\left(y\right)^{-1}\right)^{-1/p}\leq\left|B\right|^{1/p}\left(\int_{B}\nu\left(x\right)^{-1}dx\right)^{-1/p}
.
Thus taking the supremum over all balls
B
in (
35
)
leaves us with

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\|\nu|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\left|B\right|^{1/p-1/u_{1}}\|f|M_{u_{1},q}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\nu\left(B\right)}{\left|B\right|}\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}}\|f|M_{u_{1},q}\|,

which is finite by assumption (
32
).
This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 56
.

Let
\left|\alpha\right|,\left|\beta\right|<n

and
\omega\left(x\right)=\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(x\right)=\begin{cases}\left|x\right|^{\alpha}&,\,\left|x\right|\leq 1\\
\left|x\right|^{\beta}&,\,\left|x\right|>1\end{cases}
.

Assume

(36)

\frac{1}{u_{2}}\left(1+\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\leq\frac{1}{u_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{u_{2}}\left(1+\frac{\min\left(\alpha,0\right)}{n}\right)\,\mbox{ and}

(37)

\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}\left(1+\frac{\min\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}{n}\right).

Then the following embedding holds

M_{u_{1},q}\hookrightarrow M_{u_{2},p}\left(\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\right).

Proof.

To prove the statement we apply Proposition
54

for the example weight
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}
, and need to find
parameters
u_{1}
and
q
such that conditions (
32
)
and (
33
) hold.

(i) To verify the first condition, we have to cover all the different
types of balls, as we have already seen in Example
19

or Corollary
53
. This time, we simply reduce
the proof to the just mentioned corollary, since the cases are the
same, except for some more terms in the supremum, which we will take
care of as follows:

The balls of main interest are the ones centered at the origin and
radius
R<1
and
R>1
respectively, since we are trying to reduce
every other case to these two. So let us have a look at balls
B_{R}\left(0\right)

with
R<1
as an example. We recall the equivalence
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B\right)\sim R^{n+\alpha}\sim\left|B\right|^{1+\alpha/n}
.
Also observe that
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}\left(x\right)=\omega_{-\alpha,-\beta}\left(x\right)

and consequently
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}\left(B_{R}\left(0\right)\right)\sim\left|B_{R}\left(0\right)\right|^{1-\alpha/n}
.
With that in mind, we see

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R}\left(0\right),R<1}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\omega_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B\right)}{\left|B\right|}\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R}\left(0\right),R<1}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\left|B\right|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{\left|B\right|}\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R}\left(0\right),R<1}\left|B\right|^{\frac{1}{u_{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{n}+1\right)-\frac{1}{u_{2}}}<\infty

which is true if
\frac{1}{u_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{u_{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{n}+1\right)
.
Consequently we get the opposite for
R>1
:

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R}\left(0\right),R>1}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\omega_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B\right)}{\left|B\right|}\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left|B\right|^{1/u_{2}-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{R}\left(0\right),R>1}\left|B\right|^{\frac{1}{u_{2}}\left(\frac{\beta}{n}+1\right)-\frac{1}{u_{2}}}<\infty,

which is true for
\frac{1}{u_{2}}\left(1+\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\leq\frac{1}{u_{1}}
.
Roughly speaking one could say
\left|B\right|/\omega_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}\left(B\right)

is the counterpart to
\left(\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B\right)/\left|B\right|\right)^{-1}
.
The remaining cases of the proof run analogously to the proof of Corollary

53
and do not yield any new information about
upper or lower boundaries for
u_{1}
. Note however, that the maximum
term in (
31
)
\max\left(\alpha,0\right)

turns into
\min\left(\alpha,0\right)
and we are left with (
36
).

(ii) As we have seen in Corollary
53
, we know
that
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\in A_{r}
if and only if the parameters
satisfy
-n<\alpha,\beta<n\left(r-1\right)
. Again we use
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}=\omega_{-\alpha,-\beta}
.
Rearranging the inequality yields
r_{\omega_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}}=1+\frac{\max\left(-\alpha,-\beta\right)}{n}=1-\frac{\min\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}{n}
.
Now using the second condition of Proposition
54
,
we see that we require
r_{\omega_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}}<2-\frac{p}{q}
.
Inserting the preceding equality and rearranging the terms yields

\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{p}\left(1+\frac{\min\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}{n}\right)
.
We also notice that
\min\left(\alpha,\beta,0\right)=\min\left(\alpha,\beta\right)

since (

) implies
\beta\leq 0
.
∎

Corollary 57
.

\displaystyle\max\left(\frac{1}{u_{2}}\left(1+\frac{\beta_{2}}{n}\right),\frac{1}{u_{1}}\left(1+\frac{\max\left(\alpha_{1}\right)}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\min\left(\frac{1}{u_{2}}\left(1+\frac{\min\left(\alpha_{2},0\right)}{n}\right),\frac{1}{u_{1}}\left(1+\frac{\beta_{1}}{n}\right)\right)\mbox{\,\ and}

u

)

Example 58
.

As an example for the preceding Corollary

we investigate
\omega_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}
and
\omega_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}

\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=:\alpha
and
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=:\beta
,

\left|\alpha\right|,\left|\beta\right|<n

)

, (

) implies

\beta\leq 0
and (
38
) is a combination
of both, the only possible solution is
\beta=0
. Now (
38
)
turns into

\max\left(\frac{1}{u_{2}},\frac{1}{u_{1}}\left(1+\frac{\max\left(\alpha,0\right)}{n}\right)\right)\leq\min\left(\frac{1}{u_{2}}\left(1+\frac{\min\left(\alpha,0\right)}{n}\right),\frac{1}{u_{1}}\right),

12

46

48

respectively.

10

respectively, but manages to give

Theorem 59
.

Let
0<q<u_{1}
and
0<p<u_{2}
. Furthermore
let
\nu^{-1}\in A_{\infty}
with
r_{\nu^{-1}}<2
. Assume

(40)

\sup_{B}\frac{\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\cdot\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}<\infty

(41)

\frac{r_{\omega}}{2-r_{\nu^{-1}}}<\frac{q}{p}.

Then the following embedding holds

M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)\hookrightarrow M_{u_{2},p}\left(\nu\right).

Furthermore, if
\omega,\nu^{-1}\in A_{1}
then
p=q
is admitted.

Proof.

Let
f\in M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)
and
B
be ball. We begin
by using Hölder’s inequality twice; the first time for
\frac{q}{p}>1

and the second time for
s>1
to be chosen later according to its
use:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\nu\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\nu\left(x\right)\omega\left(x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{p}{q}}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}\nu\left(x\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{1/p-1/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{sp}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\left(\int_{B}\nu\left(x\right)^{\frac{s^{\prime}q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}

Multiplying the inequality with
\nu\left(B\right)^{\frac{1}{u_{2}}-\frac{1}{p}}
,
inserting
\omega\left(B\right)^{\frac{1}{u_{1}}-\frac{1}{q}}\omega\left(B\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{u_{1}}}

and taking the supremum over all balls
B
yields

(42)

\displaystyle\|f|M_{u_{2},p}\left(\nu\right)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f|M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)\|\sup_{B}\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/q-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{sp}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\left(\int_{B}\nu\left(x\right)^{\frac{s^{\prime}q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}.

Now we will take care of the terms within the supremum term individually.
As we have done before in Proposition
50

and
54
we want to use properties of the
Muckenhoupt weights
\omega
and
\nu^{-1}
. To do so, we choose
the free parameter
s>1
accordingly. We have two conditions:

(43)

\mbox{(i)\,\ }\frac{q-p}{sp}+1=:r>r_{\omega}\,\,\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\,\,\mbox{(ii) }\frac{q-p}{s^{\prime}q}+1=:\varrho>r_{\nu^{-1}},

and since
s^{\prime}=\frac{s}{s-1}
, we can rearrange both inequalities,
acquiring

\frac{q-p}{\left(r_{\omega}-1\right)p}>s>\frac{q-p}{\left[\left(2-r_{\nu^{-1}}\right)q\right]-p}.

This leaves a certain span for
s
to fulfill both of the above conditions,
if only

\frac{q}{p}>\frac{r_{\omega}}{\left(2-r_{\nu^{-1}}\right)},

and
2>r_{\nu^{-1}}>1
, which we find the only admissible case when
rearranging both inequalities in (
43
) (Note
that
s>1
is also fulfilled). Now this gives the second condition
of the proposition (i.e. (
41
)).

The next step is to use the Muckenhoupt property of
\omega
and

\nu^{-1}
for the integral terms including
\omega
and
\nu

accordingly, as we have done before in Proposition
50

and
54
. This yields

\displaystyle\omega\left(B\right)^{1/q-1/u_{1}}\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{sp}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A\omega\left(B\right)^{-1/u_{1}}\left|B\right|^{\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\left|B\right|^{\frac{1}{q}}.

At last we deal with the integral term including
\nu
. Similar to
Proposition
54
we write
\nu^{\frac{q}{q-p}}=\left(\nu^{-1}\right)^{-\frac{q}{q-p}}

to use the Muckenhoupt property. We have

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left(\nu\left(x\right)^{-1}\right)^{-\frac{s^{\prime}q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\left(\frac{q-p}{s^{\prime}q}\right)\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\left|B\right|^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)},

and thus (
42
) can finally be
estimated as

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/q-1/u_{1}}\left(\int_{B}\omega\left(x\right)^{-\frac{sp}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\left(\int_{B}\nu\left(x\right)^{\frac{s^{\prime}q}{q-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\omega\left(B\right)^{-\frac{1}{u_{1}}}\nu\left(B\right)^{\frac{1}{u_{2}}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\left|B\right|^{\left(\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{s}\right)\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)+\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=C

\displaystyle\frac{\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p},

where the supremum of the term is finite by assumption (
40
).
This concludes the first part of the proof.

Let us now have a look at the case
\omega,\nu^{-1}\in A_{1}
. In
that case
p=q
is admitted, and we get the result by combining the
arguments for
p=q
used in Proposition
50

and Proposition
54
as follows; let
f\in M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)

and
B
be a ball, then

(44)

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\nu\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\|\nu|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\|\omega^{-1}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\|\nu|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\|\omega^{-1}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\omega\left(B\right)^{1/p-1/u_{1}}

\displaystyle\cdot\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}-1/q}\left(\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{1/q}\omega\left(x\right)dx\right)^{1/q}.

As we have done before in both propositions, we use the
A_{1}
Muckenhoupt
properties of
\omega
and
\nu^{-1}
and take the supremum over

B
in (
44
), which yields

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\|\nu|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\|\omega^{-1}|L_{\infty}\left(B\right)\|\omega\left(B\right)^{1/p-1/u_{1}}\|f|M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}-1/p}\left|B\right|^{2/p}\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)^{-1/p}\omega\left(B\right)^{-1/p}\omega\left(B\right)^{1/p-1/u_{1}}\|f|M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{B}\left(\frac{\nu\left(B\right)^{1/u_{2}}}{\omega\left(B\right)^{1/u_{1}}}\right)\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\nu^{-1}\left(B\right)}\right)^{1/p}\|f|M_{u_{1},q}\left(\omega\right)\|.

Again, this is finite by assumption (
40
)
and thus

, which concludes the proof.

Corollary 61

Let
\min\left(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\right)>-n

\frac{1}{2n}\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{u_{1}}-\frac{\beta_{2}}{u_{2}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{u_{2}}-\frac{1}{u_{1}}\geq\frac{1}{2n}\max\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{u_{1}}-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{u_{2}},0\right)\mbox{ and}

\frac{q}{p}>\frac{n+\max\left(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},0\right)}{n+\min\left(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2},0\right)}.

Except for Corollary

Lemma 63
.

Let
0<p\leq u<\infty
and
\omega
,
\nu
be weights. We have

M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)=M_{u,p}\left(\nu\right)\Longleftrightarrow\nu\sim\omega\mbox{ a.e.}.

Proof.

For the first part, let
M_{u,p}\left(\omega\right)=M_{u,p}\left(\nu\right)
.
By the equivalence of norms, we have

\displaystyle c\nu\left(B\right)^{\frac{1}{u}-\frac{1}{p}}\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\nu\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega\left(B\right)^{\frac{1}{u}-\frac{1}{p}}\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\omega\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\nu\left(B\right)^{\frac{1}{u}-\frac{1}{p}}\int_{B}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\nu\left(x\right)dx,

which quickly leads to
c\nu\leq\omega\leq C\nu
. The second part
works analogue.
∎

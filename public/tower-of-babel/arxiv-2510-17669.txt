Non-CMC solutions to a Lichnerowicz equation in the Einstein-scalar field theory on closed manifolds
Bartosz Bieganowski, Pietro d’Avenia, Jacopo Schino, Daniel Strzelecki

Abstract
. In the paper, we prove the existence of a positive and essentially bounded solution to a Lichnerowicz equation in the Einstein-scalar field theory on a closed manifold with non-constant mean curvature. In particular, the non-constant mean curvature gives rise to supercritical terms in the equation, on top of singular ones. We employ a recent fixed-point argument, which involves sub- and supersolutions. Additionally, we provide several conditions on the coefficients in the equation that prevent the existence of positive classical solutions.

1.
Introduction

To study the Einstein equation in general relativity in a four-dimensional spacetime
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}
as a Cauchy problem, it is necessary to find appropriate initial data. The Gauss and Codazzi equations, when applied to the Einstein equation, provide the conditions for a metric
\displaystyle g
, a symmetric
\displaystyle(0,2)
-tensor
\displaystyle K
, and a mean curvature
\displaystyle\tau
in a three-dimensional space manifold
\displaystyle M
that must be satisfied to ensure that
\displaystyle M
is the initial state for the study of evolution. These conditions are known as
constraint equations
(for a detailed derivation, see
[
2
]
).

One of the most fruitful techniques in studying constraint equations is a conformal method (also called “Method B”) introduced by Lichnerowicz
[
10
]
. This method allows for the classification of the constant mean curvature (CMC) solutions of such equations on compact manifolds (see
[
9
]
) or other types of initial manifolds (see
[
11
]
and references therein).

The situation becomes more complicated when studying non-constant mean curvature (non-CMC) solutions. This was not a case of interest for many years, since it was thought that each solution of the Einstein equation had a slice with constant mean curvature. In 1988, Bartnik provided an example of a spacetime satisfying the Einstein equation that does not admit any CMC surface, see
[
1
]
. A general method to create such examples was given in
[
6
]
. Therefore, the study of non-CMC solutions proved to be an important topic.

In the study of the Einstein field equation, the coupling of gravity with other fields provides the form of the energy-momentum tensor. For instance, this approach leads to the Einstein–Maxwell equations for electromagnetism in curved spacetime.

In this paper, we are focused on the so called Einstein-scalar field theory: the coupling of gravity with a scalar field.
Let
\displaystyle\Psi
be a real-valued scalar field on an
\displaystyle(N+1)
-dimensional spacetime manifold
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}
(
\displaystyle N\geq 3
) and let
\displaystyle\gamma
be a spacetime metric. Consider the action

\mathcal{S}(\Psi,\gamma):=\int_{\mathcal{V}}\Big[R(\gamma)-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\Psi|_{\gamma}^{2}-V(\Psi)\Big]d\eta_{\gamma},

where
\displaystyle R(\gamma)
is the scalar curvature of
\displaystyle\gamma
,
\displaystyle d\eta_{\gamma}
is the volume element,
\displaystyle|\nabla\Psi|_{\gamma}^{2}
is the squared pseudo-norm of the spacetime gradient of the field
\displaystyle\Psi
with respect to the metric
\displaystyle\gamma
, and
\displaystyle V\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a given smooth function.
1
1

1

For the Klein–Gordon field theory,
\displaystyle V(\Psi)=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\Psi^{2}
.

Making the variations of
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}
with respect to
\displaystyle\gamma
and
\displaystyle\Psi
, we get the Einstein-scalar field equations

\begin{cases}G_{\alpha\beta}=\nabla_{\alpha}\Psi\nabla_{\beta}\Psi-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\mu}\Psi\nabla^{\mu}\Psi-\gamma_{\alpha\beta}V,\\
\nabla_{\mu}\Psi\nabla^{\mu}\Psi=\frac{dV}{d\Psi}\end{cases}

where
\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}
is the Einstein curvature tensor.
The same system was obtained by Rendall
[
12
]
to describe the expansion of the universe (see also
[
13
]
). Moreover, in particle physics, scalar fields are used to describe spin-zero particles.

Now, let us consider the constraint equations for this problem, which are conditions for an
\displaystyle N
-dimensional spacelike hypersurface
\displaystyle M
with a spatial metric
\displaystyle g
, a symmetric tensor
\displaystyle K
, and a mean curvature
\displaystyle\tau
. As noted in
[
5
]
, the coupling of a scalar field to the Einstein gravitational field theory does not add any new constraint equations to the theory. In the same paper, Choquet-Bruhat, Isenberg, and Pollack applied the Lichnerowicz conformal method to reformulate the constraint equations for the Einstein-scalar system into a system of partial differential equations.

In this method,
\displaystyle\Psi
is split into
\displaystyle\psi
(its restriction to
\displaystyle M
) and
\displaystyle\pi
(the normalized time derivative of
\displaystyle\Psi
restricted to
\displaystyle M
). Introducing the new variables
\displaystyle u
for a positive scalar function and
\displaystyle W
for a vector field defined on
\displaystyle M
, one can rescale and transform
\displaystyle\pi
,

\displaystyle K
, and
\displaystyle g
(we keep these symbols for the transformed variables) to rewrite the constraint equations as the system

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{g}u&+\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}\left(R(g)-|\nabla_{g}\psi|_{g}^{2}\right)u-\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}\left(|\sigma+{\mathcal{D}}W|_{g}^{2}+\pi^{2}\right)u^{-(2^{*}+1)}\\
\quad&+\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\tau^{2}-4V(\psi)\right)u^{2^{*}-1}=0,\end{aligned}\\[4.30554pt]
\mathrm{div}_{g}({\mathcal{D}}W)=\frac{N-1}{N}u^{2^{*}}\nabla_{g}\tau-\pi\nabla_{g}\psi,\end{array}\text{in }M.\right.

In this system,
\displaystyle R(g)
is the scalar curvature (also called the Ricci scalar),
\displaystyle\sigma
is the so-called TT-tensor (symmetric, divergence free and trace free),
\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}
is the conformal Killing operator,
\displaystyle\tau
represents the mean curvature of
\displaystyle M
, and
\displaystyle V
is the smooth potential of the Einstein-scalar field theory.

The standard task in this approach is to verify which choices of the data
\displaystyle(g,\sigma,\tau,\psi,\pi)
allow solving the constraint equations for
\displaystyle u
and
\displaystyle W
. Then, from the full set of data
\displaystyle(g,\sigma,\tau,\psi,\pi,W,u)
, by the reverse rescaling, one can obtain the solution of the constraint equations.

In
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
, the authors consider the system
(
1.1
)
in the case where
\displaystyle\tau
is constant (see also
[
3
]
, where the authors admit more general nonlinearities). In that scenario, the second equation becomes independent of
\displaystyle u
. By solving it for
\displaystyle W
, the problem reduces to studying the first equation for the function
\displaystyle u
, whose nonlinear part consists of Sobolev-critical and negative powers.

In this paper, we deal with non-constant
\displaystyle\tau
, so that the system (
1.1
) is fully coupled, taking, in this way, a different point of view on the variables in the constraint equation. We fix the vector field
\displaystyle W
and we solve the equation for the functions
\displaystyle u
and
\displaystyle\psi
. This approach, as will be shown, leads then to a single equation for
\displaystyle u
, independent of
\displaystyle\psi
, with Sobolev-supercritical terms.

From now on, we assume that
\displaystyle(M,g)
is a closed (i.e., compact and without boundary) Riemannian manifold of dimension
\displaystyle N\geq 3
.

To transform the system (
1.1
) into a single equation, we assume
\displaystyle V\equiv\nu\in\mathbb{R}
. The case of a constant potential can be understood as the situation when the scalar field does not produce a force (the force is the gradient of a potential). Especially, assuming
\displaystyle V\equiv 0
, we study the scalar field of massless particles, for example, the massless Klein–Gordon equation.

Consider
\displaystyle V\equiv\nu
and non-constant
\displaystyle\tau
. Then, from the second equation,

|\nabla_{g}\psi|_{g}^{2}=\left|\pi^{-1}\left(\frac{N-1}{N}u^{2^{*}}\nabla_{g}\tau-\mathrm{div}_{g}({\mathcal{D}}W)\right)\right|_{g}^{2}.

Hence, the system (
1.1
)
reduces to a single equation, which we call the Lichnerowicz equation,

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}u+\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}\left(R(g)-\left|\pi^{-1}\left(\frac{N-1}{N}u^{2^{*}}\nabla_{g}\tau-\mathrm{div}_{g}({\mathcal{D}}W)\right)\right|_{g}^{2}\right)u

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}(|\sigma+{\mathcal{D}}W|_{g}^{2}+\pi^{2})u^{-(2^{*}+1)}+\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\tau^{2}-4\nu\right)u^{2^{*}-1}=0.

We set

\displaystyle\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}R(g),\quad A:=\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}(|\sigma+{\mathcal{D}}W|_{g}^{2}+\pi^{2}),\quad B:=\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\tau^{2}-4\nu\right),

\displaystyle\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=-\sqrt{\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}}\pi^{-1}\mathrm{div}_{g}({\mathcal{D}}W),\quad D:=\sqrt{\frac{N-2}{4(N-1)}}\pi^{-1}\frac{N-1}{N}\nabla_{g}\tau.

Then we rewrite the Lichnerowicz equation as

-\Delta_{g}u+(h-|Du^{2^{*}}+C|_{g}^{2})u-Au^{-(2^{*}+1)}+Bu^{2^{*}-1}=0

or equivalently

(1.2)

-\Delta_{g}u+(h-|C|_{g}^{2})u=|D|_{g}^{2}u^{2\cdot 2^{*}+1}+2\langle C,D\rangle_{g}u^{2^{*}+1}-Bu^{2^{*}-1}+\frac{A}{u^{2^{*}+1}}\quad\text{in }M,

which is the equation that we study in this paper. Note that
\displaystyle A
,
\displaystyle B
,
\displaystyle C
,
\displaystyle D
,
\displaystyle h
are not necessarily constant. In fact,
\displaystyle A,B,h\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle C,D\colon M\to TM
.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we prove the existence of a positive solution to (
1.2
) using a sub/supersolution method followed by the fixed point theorem for monotone operators by Carl and Heikkilä
[
4
]
, which is an extension of the Knaster–Tarski theorem
[
15
]
. The main result is formulated in Theorem
2.2
. We highlight that the presence of supercritical terms makes it unclear how to use a variational approach as in
[
3
,
8
]
.
In Section
3
, we provide sufficient conditions for the nonexistence of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
positive solutions to (
1.2
).

In what follows,
\displaystyle K
denotes a generic positive constant that may vary from one line to another.

2.
Existence of a weak solution

Recall that
\displaystyle M
is assumed to be an
\displaystyle N
-dimensional closed manifold. To prove the existence of weak solutions of the problem (
1.2
), we work under the following assumptions

(A1)

\displaystyle A,B,|C|_{g},|D|_{g},h\in L^{\infty}(M)
,

(A2)

\displaystyle\essinf_{M}A>0
,
\displaystyle\langle C,D\rangle_{g}\geq 0
.

(A3)

\displaystyle\essinf_{M}(h-|C|_{g}^{2})>-\lambda_{1}
, where
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}>0
is the first eigenvalue of
\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}
on
\displaystyle M
.

(A4)

\displaystyle\essinf_{M}h>\min_{t>0}r(t)
, where

\displaystyle r(t):=\left(\|C\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|D\|_{L^{\infty}}t^{{2^{\ast}}}\right)^{2}-\essinf_{M}B\cdot t^{2^{*}-2}+\frac{\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}}{t^{{2^{\ast}}+2}}

From now on, we assume that the assumptions (
A1
)–(
A4
) are satisfied.
Below, we define what we mean by weak solution, subsolution and supersolution of our problem.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle u\in H^{1}(M)\cap L^{\infty}(M)
. We say that
\displaystyle u
is a weak subsolution to (
1.2
) if and only if for every nonnegative
\displaystyle\varphi\in H^{1}(M)
, there holds
\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{A\varphi}{|u|^{2^{*}+1}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}<+\infty
and

\begin{split}&\int_{M}\langle\nabla_{g}u,\nabla_{g}\varphi\rangle_{g}+(h-|C|^{2}_{g})u\varphi\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\\
&\qquad\leq\int_{M}|D|^{2}_{g}|u|^{2\cdot{2^{\ast}}}u\varphi+2\langle C,D\rangle_{g}|u|^{2^{*}}u\varphi-B|u|^{{2^{\ast}}-2}u\varphi+\frac{Au\varphi}{|u|^{2^{*}+2}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}.\end{split}

Similarly, we say that
\displaystyle u
is a weak supersolution if and only if
\displaystyle-u
is a subsolution, and that
\displaystyle u
is a weak solution if and only if it is both a subsolution and a supersolution.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that (
A1
)–(
A4
) are satisfied. Then (
1.2
) has a weak solution which is positive and bounded away from zero almost everywhere on
\displaystyle M
. If, moreover, the functions in (
A1
) are Hölder continuous, then this solution is of class
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{2,\alpha}
for some
\displaystyle\alpha<1
.

To prove Theorem
2.2
, we first formulate and prove a series of lemmas. In the first one, we prove the existence of a small constant subsolution.

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists
\displaystyle\delta_{0}
such that for all
\displaystyle 0<\vartheta<\delta_{0}
, the constant function
\displaystyle\vartheta
is a weak subsolution to (
1.2
).

Proof.

Note that, for a sufficiently small constant
\displaystyle\vartheta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\vartheta^{2\cdot 2^{*}}|D|_{g}^{2}+2\vartheta^{2^{*}}\langle C,D\rangle_{g}-\vartheta^{2^{*}-2}B+\frac{1}{\vartheta^{2^{*}+2}}A

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq-\vartheta^{2^{*}-2}B+\frac{1}{\vartheta^{2^{*}+2}}A

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq-\vartheta^{2^{*}-2}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}+\frac{1}{\vartheta^{2^{*}+2}}\essinf_{M}A

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}-\essinf_{M}|C|_{g}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq h-|C|_{g}^{2}

almost everywhere on
\displaystyle M
. Thus, multiplying it by any nonnegative function
\displaystyle\varphi\in H^{1}(M)
and integrating, we complete the proof.
∎

In the subsequent lemma we prove the existence of a constant supersolution.

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists a constant weak supersolution
\displaystyle\Theta
to (
1.2
).

Proof.

Take
\displaystyle\Theta>0
to be a minimizer of the function
\displaystyle r
defined in (
A4
).
Then, thanks to (
A4
),

\displaystyle\displaystyle h-|C|_{g}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\left(\|C\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|D\|_{L^{\infty}}\Theta^{2^{*}}\right)^{2}-\essinf_{M}B\cdot\Theta^{2^{*}-2}+\frac{\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\Theta^{2^{*}+2}}-|C|_{g}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\|C\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}-|C|_{g}^{2}+\|D\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Theta^{2\cdot 2^{*}}+2\|C\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D\|_{L^{\infty}}\Theta^{2^{*}}-\essinf_{M}B\cdot\Theta^{2^{*}-2}+\frac{\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\Theta^{2^{*}+2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq|D|_{g}^{2}\Theta^{2\cdot 2^{*}}+2\langle C,D\rangle_{g}\Theta^{2^{*}}-B\Theta^{2^{*}-2}+\frac{A}{\Theta^{2^{*}+2}},

and multiplying the inequality by any
\displaystyle\varphi\in H^{1}(M)
with
\displaystyle\varphi\geq 0
, we conclude.
∎

From now on, we fix any constant subsolution
\displaystyle\vartheta
such that
\displaystyle\vartheta<\Theta
. We introduce the truncated functions
\displaystyle f_{1},f_{2}\colon M\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

f_{1}(p,\xi):=\begin{cases}|D(p)|_{g}^{2}\vartheta^{2\cdot 2^{*}+1}+2\langle C(p),D(p)\rangle_{g}\vartheta^{2^{*}+1}+B^{-}(p)\vartheta^{2^{*}-1}&\mbox{ if }\xi<\vartheta\\
|D(p)|_{g}^{2}\xi^{2\cdot 2^{*}+1}+2\langle C(p),D(p)\rangle_{g}\xi^{2^{*}+1}+B^{-}(p)\xi^{2^{*}-1}&\mbox{ if }\vartheta\leq\xi\leq\Theta\\
|D(p)|_{g}^{2}\Theta^{2\cdot 2^{*}+1}+2\langle C(p),D(p)\rangle_{g}\Theta^{2^{*}+1}+B^{-}(p)\Theta^{2^{*}-1}&\mbox{ if }\xi>\Theta\end{cases}

and

f_{2}(p,\xi):=\begin{cases}B^{+}(p)\vartheta^{2^{*}-1}-\frac{A(p)}{\vartheta^{2^{*}+1}}&\mbox{ if }\xi<\vartheta\\
B^{+}(p)\xi^{2^{*}-1}-\frac{A(p)}{\xi^{2^{*}+1}}&\mbox{ if }\vartheta\leq\xi\leq\Theta\\
B^{+}(p)\Theta^{2^{*}-1}-\frac{A(p)}{\Theta^{2^{*}+1}}&\mbox{ if }\xi>\Theta\end{cases},

where
\displaystyle B^{-}(p)=-\min\{B(p),0\}
and
\displaystyle B^{+}(p)=\max\{B(p),0\}
, and we consider the truncated problem

(2.1)

-\Delta_{g}u+(h-|C|_{g}^{2})u+f_{2}(p,u)=f_{1}(p,u),\quad u\in H^{1}(M).

It is clear that, due to (
A1
),

\displaystyle f_{i}(\cdot,u(\cdot))\in H^{-1}(M)
. Thus, we introduce the Nemytskii operators associated with
\displaystyle f_{i}
, namely,
\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{i}\colon H^{1}(M)\rightarrow H^{-1}(M)
given by

[\mathbf{F}_{i}(u)](v):=\int_{M}f_{i}(\cdot,u(\cdot))v\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}.

On
\displaystyle H^{1}(M)
we consider the partial order

u_{1}\leq u_{2}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}=0\quad\text{a.e. on }M.

Then on
\displaystyle H^{-1}(M)
we define the partial order by

h_{1}\leq h_{2}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ h_{1}(v)\leq h_{2}(v)\quad\forall v\in H^{1}(M)\text{ such that }v\geq 0.

We say that an operator
\displaystyle\mathbf{F}\colon H^{1}(M)\rightarrow H^{-1}(M)
is increasing if and only if

u_{1}\leq u_{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathbf{F}(u_{1})\leq\mathbf{F}(u_{2}).

In the spirit of this definition we have the following.

Lemma 2.5
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{i}
are increasing operators.

Proof.

Note that by the assumption (
A2
), for almost every
\displaystyle p\in M
and
\displaystyle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle\xi_{2}\geq\xi_{1}
we have

f_{i}(p,\xi_{2})\geq f_{i}(p,\xi_{1}),\quad i=1,2.

Let
\displaystyle u_{1},u_{2}\in H^{1}(M)
be such that
\displaystyle u_{2}\geq u_{1}
. Then

[\mathbf{F}_{i}(u_{2})](v)-[\mathbf{F}_{i}(u_{1})](v)=\int_{M}\left(f_{i}(\cdot,u_{2}(\cdot))-f_{i}(\cdot,u_{1}(\cdot))\right)v\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\geq 0,\quad i=1,2,

and the proof is complete.
∎

Define the operator
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\colon H^{1}(M)\rightarrow H^{-1}(M)
by

[\mathbf{A}(u)](v)=\int_{M}\nabla_{g}u\nabla_{g}v+(h-|C|_{g}^{2})uv\,\mathrm{d}v_{g},\quad u,v\in H^{1}(M).

Note that (
A3
) implies that

(2.2)

[\mathbf{A}(u)](u)\geq\kappa\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)},\quad u\in H^{1}(M),

for some constant
\displaystyle\kappa>0
.

Observe that weak solutions of (
2.1
) are functions
\displaystyle u\in H^{1}(M)

such that

(2.3)

\mathbf{A}(u)+\mathbf{F}_{2}(u)=\mathbf{F}_{1}(u).

Lemma 2.6
.

The operator
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2}\colon H^{1}(M)\to H^{-1}(M)
is bijective. Furthermore, its inverse
\displaystyle(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})^{-1}\colon H^{-1}(M)\to H^{1}(M)
is increasing.

Proof.

It is clear that
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
is linear and continuous. We will show that
\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{2}
is continuous. Take any sequence
\displaystyle(u_{n})\subset H^{1}(M)
such that
\displaystyle u_{n}\to u
in
\displaystyle H^{1}(M)
. Let
\displaystyle v\in H^{1}(M)
with
\displaystyle\|v\|_{H^{1}}\leq 1
. From the Sobolev embedding, there exists
\displaystyle K>0
independent of
\displaystyle v
or
\displaystyle n
such that, using Hölder’s inequality,

\begin{split}\left|[\mathbf{F}_{2}(u_{n})-\mathbf{F}_{2}(u)](v)\right|&\leq\int_{M}\left|f_{2}(p,u_{n}(p))-f_{2}(p,u(p))\right||v|\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\\
&\leq K\left(\int_{M}\left|f_{2}(p,u_{n}(p))-f_{2}(p,u(p))\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\right)^{1/2}.\end{split}

Since
\displaystyle f_{2}
is continuous in the second variable,
\displaystyle f_{2}(p,u_{n}(p))\to f_{2}(p,u(p))
for a.e.
\displaystyle p\in M
. In addition, there exists
\displaystyle K>0
independent on
\displaystyle n
such that

\left|f_{2}(\cdot,u_{n}(\cdot))-f_{2}(\cdot,u(\cdot))\right|^{2}\leq K\left((B^{+})^{2}+A^{2}\right)\in L^{1}(M)

in view of (
A1
).
Then the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem allows us to conclude.

One can see that, for all
\displaystyle u,v\in H^{1}(M)
, there holds

\displaystyle[\mathbf{F}_{2}(u)-\mathbf{F}_{2}(v)](u-v)\geq 0
, and therefore, by (
2.2
),

\begin{split}\left[(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(u)-(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(v)\right](u-v)&=[\mathbf{A}(u-v)](u-v)+\left[\mathbf{F}_{2}(u)-\mathbf{F}_{2}(v)\right](u-v)\\
&\geq[\mathbf{A}(u-v)](u-v)\geq\kappa\|u-v\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)},\end{split}

namely,
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2}
is a strongly monotone operator, see
[
16
, p. 475]
.
Additionally, taking
\displaystyle v=0
, we see that there exists
\displaystyle K>0
such that for every
\displaystyle u\in H^{1}(M)

(2.4)

\left[(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(u)\right](u)\geq\kappa\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)}+[\mathbf{F}_{2}(0)](u)\geq\kappa\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)}-K\|u\|_{H^{1}(M)},

so

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2}
is coercive (see
[
16
, p. 472]
).
Hence, from Browder’s theorem for monotone operators
(see
[
16
, Theorem 26.A (c)]
), the inverse
\displaystyle(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})^{-1}\colon H^{-1}(M)\to H^{1}(M)
exists.

To show that
\displaystyle(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})^{-1}
is increasing, take
\displaystyle h_{1},h_{2}\in H^{-1}(M)
such that
\displaystyle h_{2}\geq h_{1}

and put
\displaystyle u_{i}=(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})^{-1}(h_{i})\in H^{1}(M)
. Since

\left[\mathbf{F}_{2}(u_{1})-\mathbf{F}_{2}(u_{2})\right](u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}=\int_{M}\left(f_{2}(\cdot,u_{1}(\cdot))-f_{2}(\cdot,u_{2}(\cdot))\right)(u_{1}-u_{2})\mathbbm{1}_{\{u_{1}\geq u_{2}\}}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(u^{+})(u^{-})=0
, and from (
2.2
), testing the condition
\displaystyle h_{2}\geq h_{1}
with the function
\displaystyle v=(u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}\geq 0
, we get

(2.5)

\begin{split}0&\geq h_{1}(v)-h_{2}(v)=[\mathbf{A}(u_{1}-u_{2})](u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}+\left[\mathbf{F}_{2}(u_{1})-\mathbf{F}_{2}(u_{2})\right](u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}\\
&\geq[\mathbf{A}(u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}](u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}-[\mathbf{A}(u_{1}-u_{2})^{-}](u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}=[\mathbf{A}(u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}](u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}\\
&\geq\kappa\|(u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)}.\end{split}

So
\displaystyle(u_{1}-u_{2})^{+}=0
, i.e.,
\displaystyle u_{2}\geq u_{1}
, and the proof is completed.
∎

We reformulate (
2.3
) as a fixed point problem

\displaystyle\displaystyle u=\Lambda(u):=[(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})^{-1}\circ\mathbf{F}_{1}](u).

To get the existence of a fixed point of
\displaystyle\Lambda
, in the next Lemma, we will use a variant of the fixed point theorem from
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

There exists a fixed point
\displaystyle u_{0}\in H^{1}(M)
of
\displaystyle\Lambda
, namely,
\displaystyle\Lambda(u_{0})=u_{0}
. In particular,
\displaystyle u_{0}
is a solution to (
2.3
).

Proof.

Observe that
\displaystyle(H^{1}(M),\leq)
is a Banach semilattice (see
[
4
, Definition 2.1, Lemma 2.1]
) and a reflexive Hilbert space. The operator
\displaystyle\Lambda\colon H^{1}(M)\to H^{1}(M)
is increasing. We will show that there exists a radius
\displaystyle r>0
such that

\Lambda(H^{1}(M))\subset\left\{u\in H^{1}(M)\ :\ \|u\|_{H^{1}(M)}\leq r\right\}.

For this purpose, take
\displaystyle v\in H^{1}(M)
and let
\displaystyle u=\Lambda(v)\in H^{1}(M)
. Then, from (
2.4
), since
\displaystyle B,|C|_{g},|D|_{g}\in L^{\infty}(M)
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\kappa\|u\|_{H^{1}(M)}^{2}-K\|u\|_{H^{1}(M)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq[(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(u)](u)=[\mathbf{F}_{1}(v)](u)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{M}f_{1}(\cdot,v(\cdot))u\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\leq\int_{M}|f_{1}(\cdot,\Theta)||u|\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\|f_{1}(\cdot,\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\|u\|_{L^{1}(M)}\leq K\|u\|_{H^{1}(M)}.

Therefore
\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}(M)}\leq r
for some
\displaystyle r>0
. Then, by
[
4
, Corollary 2.2]
, there exists
\displaystyle u_{0}\in H^{1}(M)
such that
\displaystyle\Lambda(u_{0})=u_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Every solution
\displaystyle u\in H^{1}(M)
of (
2.3
) satisfies
\displaystyle\vartheta\leq u\leq\Theta
. In particular,
\displaystyle u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(M)
and is a weak solution to (
1.2
).

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle u\in H^{1}(M)
be any solution of (
2.3
). Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle[(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(u)](u-\Theta)^{+}=[\mathbf{F}_{1}(u)](u-\Theta)^{+}.

Since
\displaystyle\Theta
is a supersolution of (
1.2
), and so also a supersolution of (
2.1
), we have

0\geq-[(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(\Theta)](u-\Theta)^{+}+[\mathbf{F}_{1}(\Theta)](u-\Theta)^{+}.

Hence, using the fact that
\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{1}(u)=\mathbf{F}_{1}(\Theta)
when
\displaystyle u\geq\Theta
and arguing as in (
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=[(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(u)](u-\Theta)^{+}-[\mathbf{F}_{1}(u)](u-\Theta)^{+}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq[(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(u)-(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(\Theta)](u-\Theta)^{+}-[\mathbf{F}_{1}(u)-\mathbf{F}_{1}(\Theta)](u-\Theta)^{+}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=[(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(u)-(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{F}_{2})(\Theta)](u-\Theta)^{+}\geq\kappa\|(u-\Theta)^{+}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(M)}.

So we conclude
\displaystyle(u-\Theta)^{+}=0
, i.e.,
\displaystyle u\leq\Theta
.

The proof of the condition
\displaystyle u\geq\vartheta
is analogous.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

The first part of the statement follows directly from Lemmas
2.7
and
2.8
.

Recall that the solution
\displaystyle u
satisfies
\displaystyle 0<\vartheta\leq u\leq\Theta
. Observe that
\displaystyle u
is a weak solution of

-\Delta_{g}u=\chi u,

where
\displaystyle\chi:=-(h-|C|_{g})+|D|_{g}^{2}u^{2\cdot 2^{*}}+2\langle C,D\rangle_{g}u^{2^{*}}-Bu^{2^{*}-2}+\frac{A}{u^{2^{*}+2}}
.
Since
\displaystyle\chi u\in L^{\infty}(M)
, from a bootstrap argument (cf.
[
7
, Theorem 9.9]
), we know that
\displaystyle u\in W^{2,q}(M)
for every
\displaystyle q<\infty
, and from Sobolev embeddings,
\displaystyle u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\alpha}(M)
for some
\displaystyle\alpha<1
. Note that
\displaystyle\chi
is Hölder continuous, and thus
\displaystyle\chi u
is Hölder continuous as well. Therefore, we obtain
\displaystyle u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2,\alpha}(M)
for some
\displaystyle\alpha<1
(cf.
[
14
, p. 271]
).
∎

3.
Nonexistence of smooth positive solutions

Throughout this section, we assume that
\displaystyle A,B,|C|_{g},|D|_{g},h
are continuous functions such that
\displaystyle A>0
,
\displaystyle\langle C,D\rangle_{g}\geq 0
and
\displaystyle|D|_{g}>0
on
\displaystyle M
.

As in
[
8
, Section 2]
, assume that the problem (
1.2
) has a positive solution
\displaystyle u_{0}
of class
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
. Since the domain
\displaystyle M
is compact, there exists
\displaystyle p_{0}\in M
such that
\displaystyle u_{0}(p_{0})>0
is the minimum of
\displaystyle u_{0}
. Then
\displaystyle\Delta_{g}u_{0}(p_{0})\geq 0
, so we can write

\begin{split}(h(p_{0})-|C(p_{0})|_{g}^{2})u_{0}(p_{0})\geq&|D(p_{0})|_{g}^{2}u_{0}(p_{0})^{2\cdot 2^{*}+1}+2\langle C(p_{0}),D(p_{0})\rangle_{g}u_{0}(p_{0})^{2^{*}+1}\\
&-B(p_{0})u_{0}(p_{0})^{2^{*}-1}+\frac{A(p_{0})}{u_{0}(p_{0})^{2^{*}+1}}\\
\geq&|D(p_{0})|_{g}^{2}u_{0}(p_{0})^{2\cdot 2^{*}+1}-B(p_{0})u_{0}(p_{0})^{2^{*}-1}+\frac{A(p_{0})}{u_{0}(p_{0})^{2^{*}+1}}.\end{split}

We divide by
\displaystyle u_{0}(p_{0})>0
and substitute

z=u_{0}(p_{0})^{\frac{2}{N}{2^{\ast}}}=u_{0}(p_{0})^{{2^{\ast}}-2},\quad\mathfrak{a}=A(p_{0}),\quad\mathfrak{b}=B(p_{0}),\quad\mathfrak{c}=|C(p_{0})|_{g}^{2},\quad\mathfrak{d}=|D(p_{0})|_{g}^{2},\quad\mathfrak{h}=h(p_{0}).

Note that

u_{0}(p_{0})^{2\cdot{2^{\ast}}}=z^{N}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{0}(p_{0})^{-({2^{\ast}}+2)}=z^{-\frac{N}{2\cdot{2^{\ast}}}({2^{\ast}}+2)}=z^{-(N-1)}.

Therefore, we can rewrite the inequality above as

(3.1)

\mathfrak{h}-\mathfrak{c}\geq\mathfrak{d}z^{N}-\mathfrak{b}z+\mathfrak{a}z^{-N+1}.

If we consider conditions on the functions
\displaystyle A,\,B,\,C,\,D,\,h
such that (
3.1
) cannot hold, then we cannot have classical positive solutions of the problem.

One can easily see that if
\displaystyle B(p)\leq 0
and
\displaystyle h(p)-|C(p)|_{g}^{2}\leq 0
for all
\displaystyle p\in M
, then the inequality (
3.1
) cannot hold. Note that under these conditions, Theorem
2.2
does not apply. Indeed, if (
A4
) holds, then

\essinf_{M}h>\left(\|C\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|D\|_{L^{\infty}}t^{{2^{\ast}}}\right)^{2}-\essinf_{M}B\cdot t^{2^{*}-2}+\frac{\|A\|_{L^{\infty}}}{t^{{2^{\ast}}+2}}\geq\|C\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2},

so
\displaystyle h(p)-|C(p)|^{2}_{g}>0
for all
\displaystyle p\in M
.

Since the inequality (
3.1
) involves several parameters, it is unclear what
an
optimal
condition on the functions
\displaystyle A,B,|C|_{g},|D|_{g},h
ensuring that (
3.1
) cannot hold
could be. Thus, in the following, we provide some
global
conditions on the functions
\displaystyle A,B,|C|_{g},|D|_{g},h
that imply that (
3.1
) cannot hold, i.e., ensure that there are no positive
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
solutions.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle(M,g)
be a smooth closed manifold of dimension
\displaystyle N\geq 3
. Let
\displaystyle A,B,|C|_{g},|D|_{g},h
be continuous functions on
\displaystyle M
such that
\displaystyle A>0
,
\displaystyle\langle C,D\rangle_{g}\geq 0
and
\displaystyle|D|_{g}>0
on
\displaystyle M
. The equation (
1.2
) does not have
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
positive solutions if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

\displaystyle B(p)\leq 0
for
\displaystyle p\in M
and

\max_{p\in M}\frac{\bigl(h(p)-|C(p)|_{g}^{2}\bigr)A(p)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}{|D(p)|_{g}^{\frac{2(N-2)}{2N-1}}\bigl((2N-1)N^{\frac{1}{2N-1}}A(p)^{\frac{2N-2}{2N-1}}|D(p)|_{g}^{\frac{2}{2N-1}}-(N-1)^{\frac{2N}{2N-1}}B(p)\bigr)}<\frac{1}{N^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}},

(2)

\displaystyle h(p)-|C(p)|_{g}^{2}<0
for
\displaystyle p\in M
and

\max_{p\in M\setminus B^{-1}(0)}\frac{\left(h(p)-|C(p)|_{g}^{2}\right)|D(p)|_{g}^{\frac{2(N+1)}{2N-1}}A(p)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}{B(p)^{2}}<-\frac{(N+1)^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}(N-2)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}{4(2N-1)},

(3)

\displaystyle h(p)-|C(p)|_{g}^{2}<0
for
\displaystyle p\in M
and

\max_{p\in M\setminus B^{-1}(0)}\frac{\left(h(p)-|C(p)|^{2}_{g}\right)|D(p)|_{g}^{\frac{2}{N-1}}}{|B(p)|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}\leq-\frac{N-1}{N^{\frac{N}{N-1}}},

(4)

\displaystyle|D(p)|_{g}^{\frac{4N}{2N-1}}A(p)^{\frac{2N-2}{2N-1}}-B(p)^{2}>0
for
\displaystyle p\in M
and

\max_{p\in M}\frac{\left(h(p)-|C(p)|_{g}^{2}\right)|D(p)|_{g}^{\frac{2(N+1)}{2N-1}}A(p)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}{|D(p)|_{g}^{\frac{4N}{2N-1}}A(p)^{\frac{2N-2}{2N-1}}-B(p)^{2}}\leq\frac{2N-1}{2(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}},

(5)

\displaystyle B(p)>0
,
\displaystyle|D(p)|_{g}^{\frac{2(N-1)}{2N-1}+\frac{2}{N-1}}A(p)^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}-B(p)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}>0
for
\displaystyle p\in M
and

\max_{p\in M}\frac{\left(h(p)-|C(p)|^{2}_{g}\right)|D(p)|_{g}^{\frac{2}{N-1}}}{|D(p)|_{g}^{\frac{2(N-1)}{2N-1}+\frac{2}{N-1}}A(p)^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}-B(p)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}\leq\frac{2N-1}{2(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle f(z):=\mathfrak{d}z^{N}-\mathfrak{b}z+\mathfrak{a}z^{-N+1}
for
\displaystyle z>0
. Since

\lim_{z\to 0^{+}}f(z)=\lim_{z\to+\infty}f(z)=+\infty

and

f^{\prime\prime}(z)=N(N-1)\mathfrak{d}z^{N-2}+N(N-1)\mathfrak{a}z^{-N-1}>0,

\displaystyle f
has a unique minimum point
\displaystyle\bar{z}
, which satisfies

(3.2)

N\mathfrak{d}\bar{z}^{2N-1}-\mathfrak{b}\bar{z}^{N}-(N-1)\mathfrak{a}=0.

Thus, using (
3.2
) in the definition of
\displaystyle f
, we get

(3.3)

f(\bar{z})=\frac{(2N-1)\mathfrak{a}-(N-1)\mathfrak{b}\bar{z}}{N\bar{z}^{N-1}}

or

(3.4)

f(\bar{z})=\frac{2N-1}{N-1}\mathfrak{d}\bar{z}^{N}-\frac{N}{N-1}\mathfrak{b}\bar{z}.

Condition (
1
).

Since
\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}\leq 0
, from (
3.2
) we have

0\geq N\mathfrak{d}\bar{z}^{2N-1}-(N-1)\mathfrak{a},

namely,

\bar{z}^{2N-1}\leq\frac{(N-1)\mathfrak{a}}{N\mathfrak{d}}.

Note that, since
\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}\leq 0
, the right-hand side of (
3.3
) is decreasing in
\displaystyle\bar{z}
, therefore

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(\bar{z})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{(2N-1)\mathfrak{a}-(N-1)\mathfrak{b}\left(\frac{(N-1)\mathfrak{a}}{N\mathfrak{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2N-1}}}{N\left(\frac{(N-1)\mathfrak{a}}{N\mathfrak{d}}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}}=\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}\frac{(2N-1)N^{\frac{1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{2N-2}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{1}{2N-1}}-(N-1)^{\frac{2N}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{b}}{N^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}.

Thus, by (
3.1
),

\mathfrak{h}-\mathfrak{c}\geq\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}\frac{(2N-1)N^{\frac{1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{2N-2}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{1}{2N-1}}-(N-1)^{\frac{2N}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{b}}{N^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}},

which is in contradiction with the assumption of this case.

Condition (
2
).
We apply Young’s inequality with the weight
\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{N+1}{2N-1}
to get

\mathfrak{d}z^{N}-\mathfrak{b}z+\mathfrak{a}z^{-(N-1)}\geq\left(\frac{\mathfrak{d}}{\gamma}\right)^{\gamma}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{1-\gamma}\right)^{1-\gamma}z^{\gamma N-(N-1)(1-\gamma)}-\mathfrak{b}z=\left(\frac{\mathfrak{d}}{\gamma}\right)^{\gamma}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{1-\gamma}\right)^{1-\gamma}z^{2}-\mathfrak{b}z.

The right-hand side is a quadratic polynomial, so we easily find the lower bound

(3.5)

\mathfrak{d}z^{n}-\mathfrak{b}z+\mathfrak{a}z^{-(N-1)}\geq-\frac{\mathfrak{b}^{2}}{4\left(\frac{\mathfrak{d}}{\gamma}\right)^{\gamma}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{1-\gamma}\right)^{1-\gamma}}=-\frac{\mathfrak{b}^{2}(N+1)^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}(N-2)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}{4(2N-1)\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}.

As a consequence, by (
3.1
), we have the bound

\mathfrak{h}-\mathfrak{c}\geq-\frac{\mathfrak{b}^{2}(N+1)^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}(N-2)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}{4(2N-1)\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}

obtaining a contradiction.

Condition (
3
).

Assume
\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}>0
.

The right-hand side of (
3.4
) is increasing in
\displaystyle\bar{z}
when

\bar{z}\geq\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{(2N-1)\mathfrak{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}.

Observe that, by (
3.2
) and since
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}>0
,

0<N\mathfrak{d}\bar{z}^{2N-1}-\mathfrak{b}\bar{z}^{N},

namely,

\bar{z}>\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{N\mathfrak{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}.

Since

\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{(2N-1)\mathfrak{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}<\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{N\mathfrak{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}},

then

(3.6)

f(\bar{z})>\frac{2N-1}{N-1}\mathfrak{d}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{N\mathfrak{d}}\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}-\frac{N}{N-1}\mathfrak{b}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{N\mathfrak{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}=-\frac{N-1}{N^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}\frac{\mathfrak{b}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}{\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}.

Hence, from (
3.1
),

\frac{(\mathfrak{h}-\mathfrak{c})\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}{|\mathfrak{b}|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}=\frac{(\mathfrak{h}-\mathfrak{c})\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}{\mathfrak{b}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}>-\frac{N-1}{N^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}.}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1},}

which is impossible under the assumptions of this case.

If
\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}\leq 0
, then from (
3.1
) we have

\mathfrak{h}-\mathfrak{c}\geq\mathfrak{d}z^{N}-\mathfrak{b}z+\mathfrak{a}z^{-N+1}\geq\mathfrak{d}z^{N}+\mathfrak{a}z^{-N+1}\geq 0,

and we get a contradiction.

Condition (
4
).

Let
\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{N-1}{2N-1}
. Applying Young’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}\mathfrak{d}z^{N}+\mathfrak{a}z^{-N+1}&=\alpha\left(\frac{\mathfrak{d}}{\alpha}z^{N}\right)+(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{1-\alpha}z^{-N+1}\right)\geq\left(\frac{\mathfrak{d}}{\alpha}z^{N}\right)^{\alpha}\cdot\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{1-\alpha}z^{-N+1}\right)^{1-\alpha}\\
&=\frac{\mathfrak{d}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{a}^{1-\alpha}}{\alpha^{\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{1-\alpha}}z^{N\alpha-(N-1)(1-\alpha)}=\frac{(2N-1)\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}{(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}.\end{split}

Combining this estimation with (
3.5
) we get

(3.7)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{d}z^{N}-\mathfrak{b}z+\mathfrak{a}z^{-N+1}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathfrak{d}z^{N}+\mathfrak{a}z^{-N+1}\right)+\left(\frac{\mathfrak{d}}{2}z^{N}-\mathfrak{b}z+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{2}z^{-N+1}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{(2N-1)\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}{2(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}-\frac{\mathfrak{b}^{2}(N+1)^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}(N-2)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}{4(2N-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{d}\right)^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{a}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}\\
&=\frac{(2N-1)}{2(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}\\
&\qquad-\frac{(N+1)^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}(N-2)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}{2(2N-1)}\cdot\frac{\mathfrak{b}^{2}}{\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}.\end{split}

By Young’s inequality, for positive numbers
\displaystyle x\neq y
we have

x^{\frac{x}{x+y}}y^{\frac{y}{x+y}}<\frac{x^{2}}{x+y}+\frac{y^{2}}{x+y}<x+y,

therefore

\frac{2N-1}{(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}>\frac{(N+1)^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}(N-2)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}{2N-1}.

We can continue the estimation (
3.7
) with

\begin{split}\mathfrak{d}z^{N}-\mathfrak{b}z+\mathfrak{a}z^{-N+1}&>\frac{(2N-1)}{2(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}\left(\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}-\frac{\mathfrak{b}^{2}}{\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}}\right).\end{split}

Finally, we get the following bound

\mathfrak{h}-\mathfrak{c}>\frac{(2N-1)}{2(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}\cdot\frac{\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{2N}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{2N-2}{2N-1}}-\mathfrak{b}^{2}}{\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N+1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N-2}{2N-1}}},

in contrast with the assumptions.

Condition (
5
).

Arguing as above but using (
3.6
) instead of (
3.5
) and recalling that
\displaystyle f(\bar{z})=\min f
, we get

\begin{split}\mathfrak{d}z^{N}-\mathfrak{b}z+\mathfrak{a}z^{-N+1}&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathfrak{d}\bar{z}^{N}+\mathfrak{a}\bar{z}^{-N+1}\right)+\left(\frac{\mathfrak{d}}{2}\bar{z}^{N}-\mathfrak{b}\bar{z}+\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{2}\bar{z}^{-N+1}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{(2N-1)\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}{2(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}-\frac{N-1}{N^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}\cdot\frac{\mathfrak{b}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}{\left(\frac{\mathfrak{d}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}.\end{split}

Since
\displaystyle N\geq 3
, we have

2^{2N-1}(N-1)^{(N-1)^{2}}\leq(N-1)^{2N-1+(N-1)^{2}}=(N-1)^{N^{2}}<N^{N^{2}}.

Then,

2^{\frac{1}{N-1}}(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}<N^{\frac{N^{2}}{(N-1)(2N-1)}},

or equivalently,

2^{\frac{1}{N-1}}(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}<N^{\frac{N}{N-1}}.

Hence, since
\displaystyle 2N-1>2(N-1)
, we get

\frac{2N-1}{2^{\frac{1}{N-1}}(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}>\frac{2(N-1)}{N^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}

from which

\frac{2N-1}{2(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}>\frac{2^{\frac{1}{N-1}}(N-1)}{N^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}.

Then, continuing the previous estimation, we obtain

\mathfrak{h}-\mathfrak{c}\geq\mathfrak{d}z^{N}-\mathfrak{b}z+\mathfrak{a}z^{-N+1}>\frac{2N-1}{2(N-1)^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}N^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}}\left(\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{N-1}{2N-1}}\mathfrak{a}^{\frac{N}{2N-1}}-\frac{\mathfrak{b}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}{\mathfrak{d}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}}\right),

which contradicts the assumptions.
∎

Note that applying the methods from the proof, one can also provide further conditions implying nonexistence of solutions.

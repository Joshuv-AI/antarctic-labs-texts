RIGORISM (Lat. rigor, stillness, firmness), a philosophical
term applied by Kant specially to those moralists who take up
an anti-hedonist or ascetic standpoint. In general the term
is opposed to “latitudinarianism” or “indifferentism,”—respectively
a morality of compromise and a morality of pure
indifference,—and signifies insistence upon the strictest interpretation
of a principle, rule or criterion. Thus, in Roman
Catholic theology, a rigorist holds that in cases of conscience
the proper course is to adhere to the strict wording of the law
in question.

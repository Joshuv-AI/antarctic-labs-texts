Generalized digroups, di-skew braces, and solutions of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation
Andrea ALBANO, Paola STEFANELLI

Abstract
We introduce a novel algebraic structure called di-skew brace by which we show that generalized digroups systematically yield bijective, non-degenerate solutions to the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation. We study the structural properties of these solutions with a particular focus on their left derived shelves, which belong to the class of conjugation racks . Consistently, we show that these solutions belong to a broader class that includes skew brace solutions. In particular, we prove that each such solution can be decomposed as a hemi-semidirect product of a skew brace solution endowed with a certain compatible action on the idempotents of the associated di-skew brace structure. Finally, we provide concrete instances of these solutions through a suitable notion of averaging operators on groups.

Introduction

The problem of studying solutions of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation first arose in a paper by Drinfel’d
[
11
]
and since then has attracted much interest with the aim of determining their algebraic properties and towards the task of classifying them.
Specifically, if
D
is a set, then a map
r:D\times D\to D\times D
is said to be a
solution of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation on
D
(briefly a
solution
) if the identity

\displaystyle\left(r\times\id_{D}\right)\left(\id_{D}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{D}\right)=\left(\id_{D}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{D}\right)\left(\id_{D}\times r\right)

(YBE)

is satisfied.
One of the most common way to encode the braiding of a solution
r
on
D
is to denote it by
\left(D,r\right)
and write
r\left(a,b\right)=\left(\lambda_{a}\left(b\right),\rho_{b}\left(a\right)\right)
, where
\lambda_{a}
and
\rho_{b}
are maps from
D
into itself, for all
a,b\in D
.
To avoid overloading the notation, these maps are used without further specifications on the particular sets where they are defined, which will be clear from the context.
With this notation, a straightforward computation ensures that (
YBE
) holds if and only if the following identities

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}\lambda_{b}(c)=\lambda_{\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)}\lambda_{\rho_{b}\left(a\right)}\left(c\right)

(Y1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda_{b}\left(c\right)}\left(a\right)}\rho_{c}\left(b\right)=\rho_{\lambda_{\rho_{b}\left(a\right)}\left(c\right)}\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)

(Y2)

\displaystyle\rho_{c}\rho_{b}(a)=\rho_{\rho_{c}\left(b\right)}\rho_{\lambda_{b}\left(c\right)}\left(a\right)

(Y3)

hold, for all
a,b,c\in D
. Moreover, a solution
r
is
left non-degenerate
if
\lambda_{a}\in\Sym_{D}
, for all
a\in D
,
right non-degenerate
if
\rho_{a}\in\Sym_{D}
, for all
a\in D
, and
non-degenerate
if it is both left and right non-degenerate.
In addition, a solution

is
bijective
if it is a permutation of
D\times D
.

Bijective non-degenerate solutions are among the first on which algebraic approaches have been applied to obtain significant results, for example, as in
[
12
]
,
[
15
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
, and
[

]
.
Even though in the latter and many other related works bijectivity is considered an assumption, it is now known that a non-degenerate solution is automatically bijective, thanks to
[
18
]
.
More recent papers shifted the attention to broader classes of solutions in an effort to drop one non-degeneracy assumption. In particular,
[
22
]
shows how self-distributive structures, commonly named

, turn out to be relevant for a deeper understanding of left non-degenerate solutions. If
D
is a non-empty set and
\triangleright
a binary operation on
D

\left(D,\triangleright\right)
is a

a\triangleright\left(b\triangleright c\right)=\left(a\triangleright b\right)\triangleright\left(a\triangleright c\right)
is satisfied, for all

.
In this framework, the notion of Drinfel’d twist connected to a shelf structure has been investigated in

The main aim of this work is to provide bijective non-degenerate solutions in terms of Drinfel’d twists on

To systematically determine families of Drinfel’d twists in pairs with conjugation racks, we introduce a “skew brace"-like structure having a

In

In

To conclude this section, we observe that di-skew brace solutions are Drinfel’d twists of non-necessarily idempotent racks in the sense of

In
Section

3
, we focus on classes of examples that naturally come from a notion of compatible averaging operators on groups.
Let us note that averaging operators first appeared in fluid dynamics and invariant theory under the name of
Reynolds operator
and have been recently studied for their connections with racks and Rota–Baxter operators, see for example
[
2
]
,
[

]
,
[

and

In

[

, once one adopts a point of view purely in terms of their Drinfel’d twists and associated racks.
In this manner, we offer a perspective that sets a direct link between the dynamical extensions of self-distributive structures as in
[

and left non-degenerate solutions.
Specifically, we prove that if
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
is a di-skew brace and

its associated solution, then there exists a skew brace structure

is a hemi-semidirect product

has finite order and, in this case, we explicitly compute it. Based on these results, we obtain that di-skew braces solutions are generally not Drinfel’d isomorphic to skew brace solutions, a distinction that further motivates a future deeper exploration of the structures introduced here.

Basics

The present section serves to introduce basic notions on generalized digroups and to fix the notation that will be used throughout the text.
Moreover, we provide some basic facts on solutions of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation with a focus on their relationship with self-distributive structures.
For the sake of completeness, let us note that the term “digroup” already appears in
[
3
]
and other works connected to skew braces, although with a reference to a structure that is different from ours.

1.1
Generalized digroups

Definition 1.1

(cf.
[
25
]
)
.

A
disemigroup
is a set
D
equipped with two associative binary operations
\vdash
,
\dashv
:
D\times D\to D
satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle a\vdash(b\dashv c)

\displaystyle=(a\vdash b)\dashv c\,,\quad\text{(inner associativity)}

(1.1)

\displaystyle a\dashv(b\vdash c)

\displaystyle=a\dashv(b\dashv c)\,,\quad\text{(right bar-side irrelevance)}

(1.2)

\displaystyle(a\vdash b)\vdash c

\displaystyle=(a\dashv b)\vdash c\,,\quad\text{(left bar-side irrelevance)}

(1.3)

for all
a,b,c\in D
.
A
dimonoid
is a disemigroup
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
admitting an element

called a
bar-unit
that satisfies

\displaystyle\forall\,a\in D\quad e\vdash a=a=a\dashv e\,.

(1.4)

The set of all bar-units in
D
is called the

of
D
and is denoted as
{E(D,\vdash,\dashv)}
.
The notion of
subdisemigroups
applies in this context in a natural way; in particular, if
S
is a subdisemigroup of a disemigroup

then
E(S)=E(D)\cap S
.

In a similar vein of
[
35
]
, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.2
.

A
generalized digroup
(
g
-digroup for simplicity) is a dimonoid
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
such that for all
a\in D
and all
e\in E(D)
there exist a unique pair of elements
I_{e}(a)
,
J_{e}(a)\in D
satisfying the following identities:

\displaystyle I_{e}(a)\dashv a=e=a\vdash J_{e}(a)\,.

(1.5)

In what follows, for all
e\in E(D)
and
a\in D
it is useful to denote
I_{e}(a)
as
a^{I_{e}}
and
J_{e}(a)
as

.

A homomorphism of

-digroups is just a homomorphism of the underlying disemigroups.
It is worth to remark the fact that if
f:(D,\vdash_{1},\dashv_{1})\to(E,\vdash_{2},\dashv_{2})
is a homomorphism of
g
-digroups, then

is a generalized subdigroup of
(E,\vdash_{2},\dashv_{2})
and

maps bar-units of

to bar-units of

.
Moreover, if

and

then

\displaystyle f(a^{I_{e}})=f(a)^{I_{f(e)}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad f(a^{J_{e}})=f(a)^{J_{f(e)}}.

Remark 1.3
.

If
(D,\vdash)
is a group then
(D,\vdash,\vdash)
is clearly a
g
-digroup.
Conversely, if
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
is a

-digroup such that

then

Example 1.4
.

Let

be a group endowed with a right action
\psi:G\to\Sym(E)
on a non-empty set

.
On

define two binary operations
\vdash,\dashv
by setting

\displaystyle(g,e)\vdash(h,f)=(gh,f)\,,\quad(g,e)\dashv(h,f)=(gh,\psi_{h}(e))\,,

Then, the structure

The example above can actually be promoted to a structural result that concerns the whole class of

Specifically, as already shown in

Let us recall that a semigroup

1.2
Basics on set-theoretic solutions

Among the various algebraic structures that originated from or were employed in the study of set-theoretical solutions, the class of
shelves
has a key role in their investigation and are useful to encode different properties.

Definition 1.10
.

A (left) shelf
(D,\triangleright)
is a set
D
equipped with a binary operation
\triangleright
such that

\forall\,a,b,c\in D\quad a\triangleright(b\triangleright c)=(a\triangleright b)\triangleright(a\triangleright c)\,.

(1.6)

A shelf whose left multiplication operators
{L_{a}:D\to D\,,b\mapsto a\triangleright b}
are all bijective is called a
rack
. If in addition
a\triangleright a=a
holds, for all
a\in D
, then
D
is called a
quandle
.

Hereinafter, when considering a shelf
(D,\triangleright)
we will always denote with
L_{a}:D\to D
the operator of left multiplication by
a\in D
.
With this notation, it is important to note that the identity (
1.6
) is equivalent to the following

\displaystyle\forall\,a,b\in D\quad L_{a}L_{b}=L_{L_{a}(b)}L_{a}\,,

(1.7)

which on its own amounts to the fact that
L_{a}\in\End(D,\triangleright)
, for all
a\in D
.

Examples 1.11
.

1)

If
D
is a set and
f:D\to D
a map, the binary operations on
D
defined by setting
a\triangleright b=f(b)
, for all
a,b\in D
, endows

of a shelf structure that is a rack if and only if

. In particular, if

, then we call
\left(D,\triangleright\right)
the

.

2)

If
D
is a group, then the structure
\left(D,\triangleright\right)
with
a\triangleright b:=a^{-1}ba
, for all
a,b\in D
, is a quandle called the

and denoted by

.

Let
\left(D,\,\triangleright\right)
be a shelf and let us consider the map

defined by setting
r_{\triangleright}(a,b)=\left(b,\,b\triangleright a\right)
, for all

.
Then,

is a left non-degenerate solution that is said to be of

.
It is clear that

is bijective and non-degenerate if and only if

is a rack.
Conversely, as shown for example in
[

]
, to a left non-degenerate solution
\left(D,r\right)
one can always associate a shelf
\left(D,\triangleright_{r}\right)
defined by setting

\displaystyle\forall\,a,b\in D\qquad a\triangleright_{r}b=\lambda_{a}\rho_{\lambda_{b}^{-1}(a)}(b)\,.

An important property that relates the latter structure with the original solution is the fact that each map
\lambda_{a}:D\to D
is an automorphism of
(D,\triangleright_{r})
, i.e. such that

\displaystyle\forall\,a,b,c\in D\qquad\lambda_{a}(b\triangleright_{r}c)=\lambda_{a}(b)\triangleright_{r}\lambda_{a}(c)

(1.8)

(see also

takes the name of

of

and is usually denoted as

.
It is worth to note that

-homomorphism for short)

that satisfies

If

-isomorphism, while the solutions

]
,

]
, and

]

Particular examples of such maps

are given by those that can be decomposed as

Di-skew braces

Definition 2.1
.

A (left)
di-skew brace
is a quadruple
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
where
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
is a
g
-digroup,
(D,\circ)
is a right group such that the following identities hold for all
a,b,c\in D
:

\displaystyle a\circ(b\vdash c)

\displaystyle=a\circ b\vdash a^{-1}\vdash a\circ c\,,

(D1)

\displaystyle a\circ(b\dashv c)

\displaystyle=a\circ b\dashv a^{-1}\dashv a\circ c\,,

(D2)

\displaystyle(a\vdash b)\circ c

\displaystyle=(a\dashv b)\circ c\,.

(D3)

We call
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
and
(D,\circ)
the
additive structure
and the
multiplicative structure
of
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
, respectively. Moreover, we say that the structure
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
is a
di-brace
if
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
is
abelian
, i.e., if
a\vdash b=b\dashv a
holds for all
a,b\in D
.

Note that the class of di-skew braces encompasses that of skew braces
(D,+,\circ)

[
16
]
, where we interpret their additive structure as an iterated group
(D,+,+)
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
be a
g
-digroup.
Then
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\vdash)
is a di-skew brace that will be called the
trivial di-skew brace
.
Analogously, if we denote with
\dashv^{\text{op}}
the opposite operation of
\dashv
, then the quadruple
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\dashv^{\text{op}})
is again a di-skew brace which bears the name of
almost-trivial di-skew brace
.

We postpone further examples of di-skew braces in Section 3, where we employ an extended notion of averaging operator on a group.

In the following, we will denote with
E(D,\vdash,\dashv)
the set of bar-units of
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
, while
E(D,\circ)
will stand for the set of idempotents in
(D,\circ)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
be a di-skew brace.
Then
E(D,\vdash,\dashv)=E(D,\circ)
.

Proof.

Let us first prove that
E(D,\circ)\subseteq E(D,\vdash,\dashv)
.
If
j\in E(D,\circ)
then from the fact that
j
is a left unit in
(D,\circ)
, it follows that

\displaystyle j\vdash j=j\circ(j\vdash j)=j\circ j\vdash j^{-1}\vdash j\circ j=j\vdash j^{-1}\vdash j=j\,,

where in the last equality we have used the fact that
j\vdash j^{-1}\in E(D,\,\vdash,\dashv)
.
Hence,
{j\in E(D,\vdash,\dashv)}
thanks to
Proposition

1.7
.
To prove the converse inclusion, consider a bar-unit
e\in E(D,\vdash,\dashv)
, fix
j\in E(D,\circ)
and notice that

\displaystyle e\circ a=(j\vdash e)\circ a\overset{\eqref{diskew3}}{=}(j\dashv e)\circ a=j\circ a=a\,,

holds for all
a\in D
.
Therefore
e\in E(D,\circ)
, concluding the proof.
∎

Convention 2.4
.

In light of the previous results, in what follows, if
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
is a di-skew brace then we will denote by
E(D)
its set of common idempotents which we call the set
idempotents
of
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
.
Most importantly, throughout our work and if not otherwise stated, we will implicitly consider a fixed idempotent
0\in E(D)
which will be employed to decompose both the additive
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
and multiplicative
(D,\circ)
structures.
In particular, since
(D,\circ)
is a right group, then
M=D\circ\xi
is a subgroup of
(D,\circ)
and all
a\in D
admit a unique decomposition as

a=m_{a}\circ u_{a}\,,

where
m_{a}\in M
and
u_{a}\in E(D)
are its groupal and idempotent component, respectively.

Remark 2.5
.

It is worth remarking that if

is a di-skew brace then the groupal parts
G
and
M
of the additive and multiplicative structures, respectively, do not necessarily coincide.
Indeed, first note that if
a\in D
satisfies

, then
a\circ 0=a\vdash 0
.
As a consequence, if in particular

is a non-abelian

provides an instance in which

Analogously, with a dual reasoning, it is straightforward to verify that if

is non-abelian, then the trivial di-skew brace

satisfies

, where

is the right groupal part of

.

In what follows, if
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
is a di-skew brace then, for all

the unique

.
The slight abuse of notation is justified by the fact that in this way we can denote left division by

is in particular a left quasigroup - without having to introduce a left division symbol in the signature of a di-skew brace.
In particular, in light of

is the multiplicative decomposition of

Proposition 2.6

Let

2.1
Set-theoretic solutions associated to di-skew braces

In this section we recall the notion of conjugation rack associated to a
g
-digroup and show that di-skew braces systematically yield bijective and non-degenerate solutions having such a structure as left derived rack.
The conjugation rack was first introduced in
[

as an extension to digroups of the usual conjugation quandle of a group and was later carried over to

[

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
be a
g
-digroup and define a binary operation

on

\displaystyle a\triangleright b=a^{-1}\vdash b\dashv a\,,

for all

.
Then

is a

called the

of

and denoted as
\Conj(D,\vdash,\dashv)

As already clear in

Lemma 2.10

Let
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
be a
g
-digroup and let

Then, the map

\displaystyle a\triangleright b=(g_{a}^{-1}\vdash g_{b}\dashv g_{a})\vdash\psi_{g_{a}}(e_{b})\,,

holds for all
a,b\in D
.

We now state the main result of this section but postpone its proof after two technical lemmas.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
be a di-skew brace and define a map

\displaystyle r(a,b)=(\lambda_{a}(b),(\lambda_{a}(b))^{-}\circ(a\dashv\lambda_{a}(b)))\,,

is a bijective non-degenerate solution.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
be a di-skew brace and let

Then, the map

Proof.

Notice first that

-digroup.
Therefore, to obtain the assertion, it sufficient to show that for all

the following holds:

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}\lambda_{b}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\lambda_{a}(b)}\lambda_{\lambda^{-1}_{\lambda_{a}(b)}(\lambda_{a}(b)\triangleright a)}

To facilitate the computation of the right-hand side, let us first note that

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}_{\lambda_{a}(b)}(\lambda_{a}(b)\triangleright a)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}_{\lambda_{a}(b)}((\lambda_{a}(b))^{-1}\vdash a\dashv\lambda_{a}(b))

Lemma 2.13

Let

Proof of

3
Compatible averaging operators on groups and examples

The aim of this section is to show how certain maps defined on groups that we name
compatible averaging operators
naturally lead to a rich class of examples of
g
-digroups. In particular, these maps include idempotent group endomorphisms and also yield an automatic identification of the set of bar units of the
g
-digroups they provide. Lastly, we show how to obtain di-skew braces having as additive structure a
g
-digroup that comes from compatible averaging operators.

3.1
Averaging operators on groups

Let us begin by introducing some definitions and properties with some due remarks.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
D
be a group.
A
left
(resp.
right
)
averaging operator on
D
is a map
f:D\to D
such that the following hold:

\displaystyle\forall\,x,y\in D\qquad f\left(x\right)f\left(y\right)=f\left(f\left(x\right)\,y\right)\qquad\text{(resp.}\ f\left(x\right)f\left(y\right)=f\left(x\,f\left(y\right)\right)\text{)}.

(3.1)

A map
f
is called an
averaging operator
on
D
if it is both a left and right averaging operator. Note that this last notion was introduced in
[
38
]
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
D
be a group, let
c\in D
and define a map
f:D\to D
by setting
f(x)=xc
, for all
x\in D
.
If
c\notin Z(D)
, then
f
is a left averaging operator which is not a right averaging operator.
Analogously, the map
g:D\to D
defined by setting
g(x)=cx
, for all
x\in D
, is a right averaging operator which is not a left averaging operator.

Let us observe that every idempotent homomorphism on a group
D
is an averaging operator on
D
. Moreover, note that if
f
is either a left or right averaging operator on a group
D
then
f\left(1_{G}\right)=1_{G}
(with
1_{G}
the identity of
D
) if and only if
f
is an idempotent map. On the other hand, there exist averaging operators that are not endomorphism.

Example 3.3
.

Let
D
be a group that admits a subgroup
N
of index
2
and assume that there exists
c\in C_{D}(N)
such that
c\notin N
.
Define
f:D\to D
by setting
f(x)=x
if
x\in N
and
f(x)=xc
if
x\notin N
.
Then,
f
is an averaging operator on
D
.

In the following result, we provide a method to construct
g
-digroups on a fixed group which employs a slight generalization of the notion of averaging operators on groups and is inspired by results in
[
5
, Proposition 5.6]
and
[
38
, Proposition 3.8]
.
Moreover, to ease the notation, if
f
is a left (resp. right) averaging operator on a group
D
then we set
\ker(f)=\{x\in D\mid f(x)=1\}
.
It is worth mentioning that in general
\ker(f)
is not necessarily a subgroup of
D
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
D
be a group and
f,g:D\to D
maps. The pair
(f,g)
is called an
averaging pair
if the following identities

\displaystyle f(a)

\displaystyle f(b)=f(f(a)\,b)=f(a\,g(b))

(3.2)

\displaystyle g(a)

\displaystyle g(b)=g(a\,g(b))=g(f(a)\,b)

(3.3)

hold, for all
a,b\in D
. Moreover, we say that
(f,g)
is a
compatible averaging pair
if
\ker(f)\cap\ker(g)\neq\emptyset
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
D
be a group and
(f,g):D\to D
an averaging pair on
D
. Then, the structure
\left(D,\,\vdash,\,\dashv\right)
is a disemigroup where we define

\displaystyle a\vdash b:=f(a)\,b\qquad\text{and}\qquad a\dashv b:=a\,g(b),

for all
a,b\in D
. Moreover, if
\left(f,g\right)
is compatible, then
\left(D,\,\vdash,\,\dashv\right)
is a
g
-digroup whose set of bar-units
E(D)
coincide with
\ker(f)\cap\ker(g)
and whose unilateral inverses are given by
I_{e}(a):=e\,g(a)^{-1}
and
J_{e}(a):=f(a)^{-1}\,e
, for all
e\in E(D)
and
a\in D
.

Proof.

The proof consists of a routine computation.
∎

Notice that if
f:D\to D
is an averaging operator on a group
D
, then
(f,f)
is trivially an averaging pair which is compatible if and only if
\ker(f)\neq\emptyset
.
Therefore, every such map may serve to construct examples of
g
-digroups.

Example 3.6
.

Let
f,g:D\to D
be idempotent endomorphisms of a group
D
such that
fg=f
and
gf=g
.
Then,
(f,g)
is a compatible averaging pair on
D
.
Notice that the previous two identities are equivalent to the fact that
\ker(f)=\ker(g)
, as it is clear by employing the natural factorization induced by
f
and
g
on
D
.
As an example of such a pair, we can consider
D=\{1,\,a,\,b,\,ab\}
the Klein group and the idempotent endomorphisms

defined by setting
f\left(a\right)=1
,
f\left(b\right)=b
, and
g\left(a\right)=1
,

, respectively.

Remark 3.7
.

Let
(f,g)
be a compatible averaging pair on a group
D_{1}
and consider its associated
g
-digroup
(D_{1},\vdash,\dashv)
.
Moreover, consider an averaging operator

on a group

such that

and denote by

its associated

-digroup.
If

-digroup isomorphism, then easy calculations show that the identities

\displaystyle\varphi f(a)=h(\varphi(a))\varphi(1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi g(a)=\varphi(1)h(\varphi(a))\,,

hold for all

.
In particular, we deduce that

if and only if

.
Following this reasoning, the example defined on the Klein group in

3.6
provides a concrete instance of a compatible averaging pair that cannot be obtained by a single averaging operator.

Let us conclude with a further example of a class of

-digroups that do not necessarily rise from averaging operators.

Example 3.8
.

Let
D
be a group,
f,g
idempotent endomorphisms of

such that

and

is an abelian group. Then

\displaystyle a\vdash b=f(a)fg(a)^{-1}b\qquad\text{and}\qquad a\dashv b=af(b)fg(b)^{-1},

4
The hemi-semidirect product of solutions

The aim of this section is to introduce the construction of
hemi-semidirect product of twists
and prove that di-skew brace solutions can be encoded in this way.
In doing this, we also show that our notion is an instance of
dynamical extension
of solutions, which is a general method to construct new solutions based on an analogous technique for self-distributive structures as in
[
1
]
.
We point out that our treatment is equivalent to
[
4
]
in the setting of
q
-cycle set but is suitably translated in the language of twists, thus highlighting the role of the underlying shelf structure.
Lastly, as an application, we delve deeper in the properties of di-skew brace solutions.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(G,\triangleright)
be a shelf equipped with an admissible twist
\lambda:G\to\Aut(G,\triangleright)
and let
E
be a set.
A pair
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
is said to be a
dynamical pair on
G
with coefficients in
E
if
\alpha,\beta:G\times G\to\Map(E\times E,E)
are maps such that
\beta_{a,b}(s,{-}):E\to E
is bijective, for all
a,b\in G
,
s\in E
, and the identities

\displaystyle\alpha_{a,b\triangleright c}\left(u,\alpha_{b,c}(s,t)\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{a\triangleright b,a\triangleright c}\left(\alpha_{a,b}(u,s),\alpha_{a,c}(u,t)\right)\,,

\displaystyle\alpha_{a\cdot b,a\cdot c}\left(\beta_{a,b}(u,s),\beta_{a,c}(u,t)\right)

\displaystyle=\beta_{a,b\triangleright c}\left(u,\alpha_{b,c}(s,t)\right)\,,

\displaystyle\beta_{a\cdot b,a\cdot c}\left(\beta_{a,b}(u,s),\beta_{a,c}(u,t)\right)

\displaystyle=\beta_{b\cdot(b\triangleright a),b\cdot c}\left(\beta_{b,b\triangleright a}(s,\alpha_{b,a}(s,u)),\beta_{b,c}(s,t)\right)\,,

hold, for all
a,b,c\in G
,
u,s,t\in E
, where we set

, for all
x,y\in G
.

Note that if
(G,\triangleright)
is a shelf then a map

satisfying the first identity of

4.1
is called a

on

.
In this context, thanks to
[

\triangleright_{\alpha}
on

\displaystyle(a,s)\triangleright_{\alpha}(b,t)

\displaystyle=(a\triangleright b,\alpha_{a,b}(s,t))\,,

(4.1)

for all

E
).

Remark 4.2
.

The relationship between admissible twists and
q
-cycle sets has already been described in
[
9
, Remark 2.19]
.
At this point we limit ourselves to illustrate the connection between
Definition

4.1
and
[
4
, Definition 2]
, where
q
-cycle set dynamical pairs (or
q
-dynamical pairs for short) are introduced.

Let
(G,\triangleright)
be a shelf equipped with an admissible twist
\lambda:G\to\Aut(G)
and let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a dynamical pair with coefficients in a set
E
.
The map
{\gamma^{\prime}:G\times G\to\Map(E\times E,E)}
defined by setting

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}_{x,y}(s,t)=\beta_{x,x\triangleright y}(s,\alpha_{x,y}(s,t))\,,

(4.2)

for all
a,b\in G
,
s,t\in E
, determines a pair
(\beta,\gamma^{\prime})
which is a
q
-dynamical pair.
Conversely, let
(G,\cdot,:)
be a
q
-cycle set and consider a
q
-dynamical pair
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
with coefficients in
E
.
If we define a map
\alpha:G\times G\to\Map(E\times E,E)
by setting

\displaystyle{\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)=\gamma_{x,y}^{-1}\left(s,\gamma^{\prime}_{x,y}(s,t)\right)}\,,

(4.3)

for all
a,b\in G,s,t\in E
, then the pair
(\alpha,\gamma)
is a dynamical pair.

Before providing
Theorem

4.4
, one of the main results of the section, we prove a small technical lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(G,\triangleright)
be a shelf and let
\lambda:G\to\Aut(G,\triangleright)
be a map.
Then,
\lambda
is a twist (i.e. satisfies (
1.9
)) if and only if the the following holds

\displaystyle\forall\,a,b\in G\qquad\lambda^{-1}_{\lambda^{-1}_{a}\left(b\right)}\lambda^{-1}_{a}=\lambda^{-1}_{\lambda^{-1}_{b}\left(b\,\triangleright\,a\right)}\lambda^{-1}_{b}

(4.4)

Proof.

Note that (
1.9
) is equivalent to requiring that
\lambda_{a}\lambda_{\lambda^{-1}_{a}(b)}=\lambda_{b}\lambda_{\lambda^{-1}_{b}(b\triangleright a)}
holds, for all
a,b\in G
.
Therefore, the assertion easily follows after inverting both sides of the above identity.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(G,\triangleright)
be a shelf equipped with an admissible twist
\lambda:G\to\Aut(G,\triangleright)
and consider a dynamical pair
(\alpha,\beta)
of
G
with coefficients in a set
E
.
Let
(G\times E,\triangleright_{\alpha})
be the dynamical extension of
G
via
\alpha
and define a map
\Lambda_{(a,s)}:G\times E\to G\times E
by setting

\displaystyle{\Lambda}_{(a,s)}(b,t)

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda_{a}(b),\beta^{-1}_{a,\lambda_{a}(b)}(s,t)\right)\,,

(4.5)

for all
a,b\in G
and
s,t\in E
.
Then, the map
\Lambda:G\times E\to\Map(G\times E,G\times E)
is an admissible twist for
(G\times E,\triangleright_{\alpha})
.

Proof.

In the setting of the statement, define a map
{\Lambda_{(a,u)}}:G\times E\to G\times E
, for a fixed pair
(a,u)\in G\times E
, by equation (
4.5
).
If we consider the map
\Gamma_{(a,u)}:G\times E\to G\times E
defined by setting
\Gamma_{(a,u)}(b,v)=\left(\lambda^{-1}_{a}(b),\beta_{a,b}(u,v)\right)
, for all
b\in G
and
v\in E
, then the following holds:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{(a,u)}\Gamma_{(a,u)}(b,v)=\left(\lambda_{a}\lambda_{a}^{-1}(b),\beta^{-1}_{a,\lambda_{a}\lambda_{a}^{-1}(b)}\left(u,\beta_{a,b}(u,v)\right)\right)=\left(b,\beta^{-1}_{a,b}\left(u,\beta_{a,b}(u,v)\right)\right)\,,

for all
b\in G
and
v\in E
, where the last term is equal to
(b,v)
since the maps
\beta_{a,b}(u,\boldsymbol{\cdot})
and
\beta^{-1}_{a,b}(u,\boldsymbol{\cdot})
are inverses of each other by construction.
With similar calculations it is possible to verify that
\Gamma_{(a,u)}\Lambda_{(a,u)}=\id_{G\times E}
, thus proving that
\Lambda_{(a,u)}
is invertible with inverse
\Gamma_{(a,u)}
.
Moreover, the fact that
{\Lambda}_{(a,u)}
belongs to
\Aut(G\times E,\triangleright_{\alpha})
is clearly equivalent to the requirement that

{\Lambda^{-1}_{(a,u)}}\left((b,v)\triangleright_{\alpha}(c,z)\right)={\Lambda^{-1}_{(a,u)}}(b,v)\triangleright_{\alpha}{\Lambda^{-1}_{(a,u)}}(c,z)

holds, for all
b,c\in G,v,z\in E
, which in turn is easily seen to be equivalent to the second identity of
Definition

4.1
.
To conclude the proof, thanks to
Lemma

4.3
it remains to show that
{\Lambda}
satisfies (
4.4
).
With this aim, fix
a,b,c\in G,u,v,z\in E
and notice that the left-hand-side in (
4.4
) reads

\displaystyle{\Lambda}^{-1}_{\lambda^{-1}_{(a,u)}(b,v)}{\Lambda}^{-1}_{(a,u)}(c,z)={\Lambda}^{-1}_{\left(\lambda^{-1}_{a}(b),\,\beta_{a,b}(u,v)\right)}\left(\lambda^{-1}_{a}(c),\beta_{a,c}(u,z)\right)\,.

(I)

The first component of (
I
) is

\lambda^{-1}_{\lambda^{-1}_{a}(b)}\lambda^{-1}_{a}(c)

and the second one is

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda^{-1}_{a}(b),\lambda^{-1}_{a}(c)}\left(\beta_{a,b}(u,v),\beta_{a,c}(u,z)\right).

Now, if we note that

\displaystyle{\Lambda}^{-1}_{(b,v)}\left((b,v)\triangleright_{\alpha}(a,u)\right)=\left(\lambda^{-1}_{b}(b\triangleright a),\beta_{b,b\triangleright a}\left(v,\alpha_{b,a}(v,u)\right)\right)=:U\,,

then the right-hand-side in (
4.4
) reads

\displaystyle{\Lambda}^{-1}_{\lambda^{-1}_{(b,v)}\left((b,v)\triangleright_{\alpha}(a,u)\right)}{\Lambda}^{-1}_{(b,v)}(c,z)={\Lambda}^{-1}_{U}\left(\lambda^{-1}_{b}(c),\beta_{b,c}(v,z)\right).

(II)

The first component of (
II
) is equal to

\lambda^{-1}_{\lambda^{-1}_{b}\left(b\triangleright a\right)}\lambda^{-1}_{b}(c)
,
while the second component of
(
II
)
is

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda^{-1}_{b}(b\triangleright a),\lambda^{-1}_{b}(c)}\left(\beta_{b,b\triangleright a}\left(v,\alpha_{b,a}(v,u)\right),\beta_{b,c}(v,z)\right)\,.

Therefore, the assertion is proved by comparing the components of (
I
) and (
II
).
∎

We now specialize the results obtained above to introduce the main topic of this section.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
(G,\triangleright)
be a shelf and let
E
be a set.
An
action of
(G,\triangleright)
on
E
is a map
\psi:G\to\Sym(E)\,,a\mapsto\psi_{a}
such that the following holds

\displaystyle\forall\,a,b\in G\quad\psi_{a\triangleright b}\psi_{a}=\psi_{a}\psi_{b}\,,

(4.6)

In the notation of the above definition, the binary operation
\triangleright_{\psi}
defined on
G\times E
by setting

\displaystyle(a,u)\triangleright_{\psi}(b,v):=(a\triangleright b,\,\psi_{a}(v))\,,

for all
a,b\in G,u,v\in E
, determines a shelf structure
(G\times E,\triangleright_{\psi})
which is known as the
hemi-semidirect product of
(G,\triangleright)
and
E
via
\psi
. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
7
]
and
[
19
]
.
The following definition is justified by
Proposition

4.7
.

Let us briefly recall that a twist
\lambda:G\to\Aut(G,\triangleright)
determines a left non-degenerate solution on
G
defined by
r(a,b)=(\lambda_{a}(b),\rho_{b}(a))
, where

\displaystyle\rho_{b}(a)=\lambda_{\lambda_{a}(b)}^{-1}\left(\lambda_{a}(b)\triangleright a\right)\,,

(4.7)

for all
a,b\in G
(cf.
Theorem

1.14
).

Definition 4.6
.

Let
(G,\triangleright)
be a shelf equipped with a twist
\lambda:G\to\Aut(G,\triangleright)
and let
\psi
be an action of
(G,\triangleright)
on a set
E
.
Consider a map
\sigma:G\to\Sym(E)
satisfying

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}\sigma_{b}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\lambda_{a}(b)}\sigma_{\rho_{b}(a)}\,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}\psi_{b}

\displaystyle=\psi_{\lambda_{a}(b)}\sigma_{a}\,

(4.9)

for all
a,b\in G
.
Then, the pair
(\psi,\sigma)
is called a
hemi-semidirect pair of
\lambda
with coefficients in
E
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(G,\triangleright)
be a shelf equipped with a twist
\lambda:G\to\Aut(G)
, let
E
be a set and consider two maps
\psi:G\to\Sym(E)
and
\sigma:G\to\Sym(E)
.
If for all
a\in G,u\in E
we define
{\Lambda}_{(a,u)}:G\times E\to G\times E
by setting

\displaystyle{\Lambda}_{(a,u)}(b,v):=(\lambda_{a}(b),\sigma_{a}(v))\,,

for all
b\in G,v\in E
, then the map
{\Lambda}:G\times E\to\Aut(G\times E,\triangleright_{\psi}),\,(a,u)\mapsto\lambda_{(a,u)}
is a twist if and only if
(\psi,\sigma)
is a hemi-semidirect pair on
G
with coefficients in
E
.

Proof.

For all
a,b\in G
define two maps
\alpha_{a,b}
,
\beta_{a,b}:G\times G\to\Map(E\times E,E)
by setting
\alpha_{a,b}(u,v):=\psi_{a}(v)
and
\beta_{a,b}(u,v):=\sigma_{a}(v)
, for all
u,v\in E
.
A straightforward computation shows that
(\alpha,\beta)
is a dynamical pair if and only if
(\psi,\sigma)
is a hemi-semidirect pair, thus proving the assertion thanks to
Theorem

4.4
.
∎

It is worth to remark that
Proposition

4.7
is thus configured as an extension of the hemi-semidirect product from the setting of self-distributive structures to that of set-theoretic solutions.
Under this light and in the notation of
Proposition

4.7
, if
\lambda_{G}
is a twist on a shelf
(G,\triangleright)
and if
(\psi,\sigma)
is a hemi-semidirect pair on
G
then, denoted by
r_{G}
the solution obtained by the twist
\lambda_{G}
, then the twist
{\Lambda}:G\times E\to\Aut(G\times E,\triangleright_{\psi})
is called the
hemi-semidirect product of
r_{G}
and
E
via
(\psi,\sigma)
.
By a slight abuse of notation, we also let the left non-degenerate solution
r_{D}
induced on
D:=G\times E
bear the same name and denote it by

\displaystyle r_{D}=r_{G}\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{${\dashv\vdash}$}${}_{\psi,\sigma}$}\,r_{E}\,,

where we interpret
E
as equipped with the trivial solution
r_{E}
.

Hereinafter, when clear from the context we denote with
L
the left multiplication operator of a shelf without explicit mention to the structure it belongs to.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
(G,r_{G})
be a bijective left non-degenerate solution,
consider a hemi-semidirect product of
r_{G}
and
E
via
(\psi,\sigma)
on
G
with coefficients in a set
E
, with
|E|>1
, and set

\displaystyle m_{\triangleright_{r}}

\displaystyle:=\textnormal{inf}\left\{h\in\mathbb{N}\mid\forall\,a,b\in G\quad(L_{b}L_{a})^{h}L_{b}^{-h}(b)=b\right\}\,,

\displaystyle m_{\psi}

\displaystyle:=\textnormal{inf}\left\{h\in\mathbb{N}\mid\forall\,a,b\in G,\,\forall\,v\in E\quad(\psi_{b}\psi_{a})^{h}\psi_{b}^{-h}(v)=v\right\}\,.

Then,
r_{G}\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{${\dashv\vdash}$}${}_{\psi,\sigma}$}\,r_{E}
has finite order if and only if
m_{\triangleright_{r}}
and
m_{\psi}
are both finite.
In particular, when this happens we have that
o(r_{G}\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{${\dashv\vdash}$}${}_{\psi,\sigma}$}\,r_{E})=2\,\textnormal{lcm}(m_{\triangleright_{r}}\,,m_{\psi})
.

Proof.

With the aim of applying
Theorem

1.19
, let
a,b\in G
and
u,v\in E
, set
A:=(a,u)
and
B:=(b,v)
and note that

\displaystyle(L_{B}L_{A})^{n}L_{B}^{-n}(B)=\left((L_{b}L_{a})^{n}L_{b}^{-n}(b),(\psi_{b}\psi_{a})^{n}\psi_{b}^{-n}(v)\right)

holds, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now, if there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(L_{B}L_{A})^{n}L_{B}^{-n}(B)=A
, for all
a,b\in G
and
u,v\in E
, then in particular it follows that
(\psi_{b}\psi_{a})^{n}\psi_{b}^{-n}
is constant, a contradiction.
Therefore, given the hypothesis and thanks to
Theorem

1.19
, we deduce that
r_{G}\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{${\dashv\vdash}$}${}_{\psi,\sigma}$}\,r_{E}
has finite order if and only if
\theta_{\triangleright_{r}}
and
\theta_{\psi}
are both finite and that the former is equal to

\displaystyle 2\;\textnormal{min}\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\forall\,a,b\in G,\,\forall\,u,v\in E\quad\left((L_{b}L_{a})^{n}L_{b}^{-n}(b),(\psi_{b}\psi_{a})^{n}\psi_{b}^{-n}(v)\right)=(b,v)\right\}\,,

from which follows that
o(r_{G}\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{${\dashv\vdash}$}${}_{\psi,\sigma}$}\,r_{E})=2\;\textnormal{lcm}(m_{\triangleright_{r}}\,,m_{\psi})
.
∎

Example 4.9
.

1)

If
(G,r_{G})
is a left non-degenerate bijective solution, then
(L,\,\lambda)
is a hemi-semidirect pair on
G
with coefficients in itself.

2)

Let
(G,r_{G})
be a finite bijective non-degenerate solution having even order and
E
a non-empty set.
Furthermore, fix
f,g\in\Sym(E)
such that
fg=gf
and define two maps
\psi,\sigma:D\to\Sym(E)
by setting
\psi_{a}=f
and
\sigma_{a}=g
, for all
a\in D
.
Then,
(\psi,\sigma)
is a hemi-semidirect pair on
G
with coefficients in
E
.
Moreover, the hemi-semidirect product of
r_{G}
and
E
via
(\psi,\sigma)
satisfies
o(r_{G}\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{${\dashv\vdash}$}${}_{\psi,\sigma}$}\,r_{E})=2\textnormal{lcm}(m_{\triangleright_{r}},o(f))
.

We now show that the solution associated with a di-skew brace is indeed a hemi-semidirect product of its groupal and idempotent components.
To prove this result, in the decomposition of a
g
-digroup - with respect to a fixed bar-unit
0\in E
- we give priority to the left groupal part
G
and briefly refer to this fact by writing
D=G\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{$\dashv\vdash$}}\,E
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
be a di-skew brace with
D=G\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{$\dashv\vdash$}}\,E
and let
r
be its associated solution.
Then, there exists a skew brace structure on
G
such that

\displaystyle r=r_{G}\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{${\dashv\vdash}$}${}_{\psi,\sigma}$}\,r_{E}\,,

where
(\psi,\sigma)
is a hemi-semidirect pair on
G
with coefficients in
E
.

Proof.

Let
D=G\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{$\dashv\vdash$}}\,E
and define a binary operation
{\bullet}
on
G
by setting
a\bullet b:=a\circ b\vdash 0
, for all
a,b\in G
.
It is straightforward to verify that
(G,\vdash,\bullet)
is a skew brace. Moreover, following
Remark

2.8
, if
\lambda:(D,\circ)\to\Aut(D,\vdash,\dashv)
is the lambda map of
D
then the twist associated with
(G,\vdash,\bullet)
is the map
\varphi:(G,\bullet)\to\Aut(G,\vdash)
defined by setting
\varphi_{a}(b)=\lambda_{a}(b)\vdash 0
, for all
a,b\in G
.
Now, let us define two maps
\psi,\sigma:G\to\Map(E,E)
by setting

\displaystyle\psi_{g}(e):=g\vdash e\dashv g^{-1}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\sigma_{g}(e):=\lambda_{g}(e)\,,

for all
g\in G
and
e\in E
and let us prove that
(\psi,\sigma)
is a hemi-semidirect pair of
\varphi
with coefficients in
E
.
With this aim, let
a,b\in G
,
u\in E
and note that

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}\psi_{b}(u)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{a}(b\vdash u\dashv b^{-1})=\lambda_{a}(b)\vdash\lambda_{a}(u)\dashv\lambda_{a}^{-1}(b)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{a}(b)\vdash\sigma_{a}(u)\dashv\varphi_{a}^{-1}(b)

\displaystyle=\psi_{\varphi_{a}(b)}\sigma_{a}(u)\,,

thus showing that (
4.9
) holds.
Now, if we let
\rho_{b}:G\to G
be defined by (
4.7
) with respect to the twist
\varphi:G\to\Aut(G,\vdash)
, for all
b\in G
, then the following holds

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varphi_{a}(b)}\sigma_{\rho_{b}(a)}(u)=\sigma_{a\bullet b}(u)=\lambda_{a}\lambda_{b}(u)=\sigma_{a}\sigma_{b}(u)\,,

for all
a,b\in G
and
u\in E
, thus obtaining (
4.8
).
To finish the proof, it is sufficient to define
F:D\to G\times E
by setting
F(a)=(g_{a},e_{a})
, for all
a\in D
, and verify that it is indeed an equivalence of solutions between
r_{D}
and
r_{G}\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{${\dashv\vdash}$}${}_{\psi,\sigma}$}\,r_{E}
.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
(D,\vdash,\dashv,\circ)
be a di-skew brace, let
r=r_{G}\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{${\dashv\vdash}$}${}_{\psi,\sigma}$}\,r_{E}
be its associated solution and set

\displaystyle m_{\psi}:=\textnormal{inf}\left\{h\in\mathbb{N}\mid\forall\,a,b\in D,\,\forall\,v\in E\quad(\psi_{b}\psi_{a})^{h}\psi_{b}^{-h}(v)=v\right\}\,.

Then,
r
has finite order if and only if
G/Z(G)
is periodic and
m_{\psi}
is finite.
In particular, when this happens
r
has even order and
o(r)=2\,\textnormal{lcm}\left(\exp(G/Z(G)),\,m_{\psi}\right)
.

Proof.

The assertion follows after a straightforward application of
Corollary

4.8
.
Indeed, under its notation, it is sufficient to note that
o(r)=2\,\lcm(m_{\triangleright_{r_{G}}},m_{\psi})
and recall that
m_{\triangleright_{r_{G}}}=\exp(G/Z(G))
, as it is clear from
Corollary

1.20
and its proof.
∎

The following example shows that the class of di-skew brace solutions is properly larger – modulo Drinfel’d isomorphisms – than the class of solutions obtained through skew braces.

Example 4.12
.

Let
D=\Sym_{3}
be the symmetric group on three elements and consider the idempotent endomorphism
f
of
D
defined by setting
{f(12)=(12)}
and
f(123)=\id_{D}
.
Then,
f
is an averaging operator on
D
that determines a
g
-digroup structure
(D,\vdash,\dashv)
by setting
a\vdash b=f(a)b
and
a\dashv b=af(b)
, for all
a,b\in D
.
The latter satisfies
E(D,\vdash,\dashv)=\ker(f)=\{\id,(123),(132)\}
.

Now, fix a bar-unit
\xi\in E(D)
, the easiest choice being
\xi=\id_{D}
, and note that if
a\in D
then
g_{a}=a\vdash\xi=f(a)
.
Therefore, we deduce that
D
decomposes as
D=G\,\text{\scalebox{0.9}{$\dashv\vdash$}}\,E(D)
, where
G=\langle(12)\rangle
is the groupal component relative to
\xi
.
Let
(D,\triangleright)=\Conj(D,\vdash,\dashv)
and consider its associated left derived solution
r
.
A straightforward application of
Corollary

4.8
ensures that
o(r)=4
.
On the other hand, let
(S,+,\circ)
be a skew brace of cardinality
6
and let
s
be its associated solution.
Then,
s
is either involutive or of order
12
, as a consequence of
Corollary

1.20
, thus proving that
r
and
s
cannot be Drinfel’d isomorphic.

As a conclusive observation, it is worth underlining the fact that many other aspects on solutions connected to di-skew braces remain open to further detailed analysis.
This will be indeed the subject of future investigations with which we foresee to deepen the relation between our structures and bijective non-degenerate solutions.

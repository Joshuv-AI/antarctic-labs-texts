Quantum Incompatibility in Parallel versus Antiparallel Spins
Ram Krishna Patra, Kunika Agarwal, Biswajit Paul, Snehasish Roy Chowdhury, Sahil Gopalkrishna Naik, Manik Banik

Abstract
We explore the joint measurability of incompatible qubit observables on ensembles of parallel and antiparallel spin-½ pairs. In parallel configuration, both spins are prepared in the same state, whereas in antiparallel case, each spin is paired with its flipped counterpart. We show that the antiparallel configuration uniquely enables exact simultaneous prediction of three mutually orthogonal spin components—an advantage not achievable with parallel states. Extending beyond three observables, we examine joint measurability for larger sets of spin measurements and further generalize our analysis to state configurations beyond the parallel and antiparallel cases. As we show, our results reveal a deep connection to the ‘mean King retrodiction task’ proposed by Vaidman, Aharonov, and Albert, and have implications for a cryptographic protocol introduced by Jeffrey Bub. We further demonstrate how the enhanced compatibility in the antiparallel configuration can facilitate efficient estimation of unknown measurement devices. Finally, we discuss prospects for experimentally realizing the enhanced measurement compatibility in antiparallel configuration by analyzing the effect on finite sub-ensembles of states.

Mean King problem and Bub’s Cryptography.–
The mean King problem, proposed by Vaidman, Aharonov, and Albert (VAA)
[
28

Upon receiving back the particle from Bob, Alice performs a second measurement and correctly retrodicts what Bob’s result would have been for each possible measurement choice. While trivial for two observables—by preparing an eigenstate of one and measuring along the other—the case of three observables is nontrivial. VAA introduced a clever strategy to succeed in this setting. Alice prepares the singlet state
\ket{\psi^{-}}:=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}_{A}\ket{1}_{C}-\ket{1}_{A}\ket{0}_{C})

Estimation of unknown quantum resources—such as states, channels, or measurement devices—is a central task for the reliable implementation of quantum protocols

In a standard approach, the verifier repeatedly measures a fixed probe state (e.g.,
\ket{0}_{P}

of

\ket{0}_{P}

\mathcal{R}(N)

Alternatively, the verifier may prepare

\ket{\psi^{-}}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}_{A}\ket{1}_{P}-\ket{1}_{A}\ket{0}_{P})
and apply the black-box measurement only to the probe qubit

\ket{\psi_{\mp\hat{n}}}_{A}\ket{\psi_{\pm\hat{n}}}_{P}

\mathcal{G}^{{}_{\uparrow\hskip-1.42271pt\downarrow}}_{\mathrm{MUB}}
(Theorem

\uparrow\hskip-1.42271pt\downarrow

Discussions.–
We have explored novel aspects of quantum incompatibility in multi-copy scenarios, demonstrating that the manifestation of measurement incompatibility depends crucially on whether the system copies are prepared in parallel or antiparallel configurations. In particular, we have shown that three mutually orthogonal spin observables – known to be incompatible in the single – copy setting—can become jointly measurable when probed on antiparallel spin pairs, which highlights a distinctive feature of the antiparallel configuration. We further connected our findings to other foundational concepts and explored their potential applications. Specifically, we established a link with the mean King retrodiction task, and discussed how the observed compatibility structure plays a critical role in a quantum key distribution protocol inspired from it. Additionally, we discussed how the joint measurability of three orthogonal spin observables in the antiparallel setup can enhance the efficiency of estimating an unknown measurement device.

Our study welcomes several questions for future investigation. While Theorems
1
and
2
demonstrate the advantage of the antiparallel configuration for specific choices of spin observables, it remains an open question whether similar advantages persist for more general sets of observables. Furthermore, Corollary
1
establishes an advantage of the configuration
\rho_{\vec{m}}\otimes\Lambda(\rho_{\vec{m}})
over the parallel configuration for a certain class of PnCP maps. It would be interesting to explore for which class of PnCP maps such an advantage will be possible. Finally, extending the analysis to higher-dimensional quantum systems may reveal additional structures and potential applications.

(12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\Big[\left(\rho_{\vec{m}}\otimes\mathrm{F}(\rho_{\vec{m}})\right)\sum_{{\bf r}\setminus r_{l}}\Pi_{{\bf r}}\Big]=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho_{\vec{m}}\penalty\ \mathcal{A}_{l}^{r_{l}}\right],\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ l,\penalty\ r_{l},\penalty\ \rho_{\vec{m}}.

Consider now a state

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{Tr}[\mathcal{A}_{l}^{r_{l}}],

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}[(\Delta\otimes\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{1}\otimes\Delta)\mathbb{A}^{r_{l}}_{l}]

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{Tr}[\Delta\mathcal{A}_{l}^{r_{l}}],

(16b)

\bullet

Elementary System:
An elementary system of a GPT is specified by the triple
(\Omega,\mathbb{E},\mathbb{T})
. The set
\Omega
denotes the normalized state space, which is generally assumed to be a convex compact set embedded in some real vector space. Mathematically, often it is easy to work with the set of Unnormalized states which form a convex cone. The set

M\equiv\{e_{k}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \sum_{k}e_{k}=u\}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Omega\equiv\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{2}):=\left\{\rho\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\penalty\ |\penalty\ \rho\geq 0\penalty\ \&\operatorname{Tr}[\rho]=1\right\},\\

\mathbb{T}\equiv\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}^{2}):=\left\{U\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\penalty\ |\penalty\ UU^{\dagger}=U^{\dagger}U=\mathbf{I}_{2}\right\}\end{cases},

(21)

where

Composite System:

(22b)

where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Omega^{AB}_{Q}:=\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{B})\equiv\left\{\rho_{{}_{AB}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{2}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{B}),\penalty\ \text{s.t.}\penalty\ \rho_{{}_{AB}}\geq 0\penalty\ \&\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{{}_{AB}}]=1\right\},\\

(23)

Notably, maximal Composition includes bipartite states that are not permitted in quantum theory, while minimal Composition allows bipartite effects that are not allowed in quantum composition.

Measurement compatibility in minimal composition:
As noted above, although the state space of minimal composition is restricted, the effect space is enlarged compared to standard quantum theory. This leads to enhanced compatibility for certain measurements, as shown below.

Theorem 1S
.

The observables
X^{\lambda},Y^{\lambda},
and
Z^{\lambda}
are 2-copy compatible for all

in the minimal tensor product GPT.

Proof.

Consider the set of operators
\{\Pi^{\#}_{{}^{[a,b,c]}}\penalty\ |\penalty\ a,b,c=\pm 1\}\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2})
, defined as

\displaystyle\Pi^{\#}_{{}^{[a,b,c]}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{16}\Big(2\penalty\ \mathbf{1}^{\otimes 2}+a\{\hskip-3.41418pt\{X,\mathbf{1}\penalty\ \}\hskip-3.41418pt\}+b\{\hskip-3.41418pt\{Y,\mathbf{1}\penalty\ \}\hskip-3.41418pt\}+c\{\hskip-3.41418pt\{Z,\mathbf{1}\penalty\ \}\hskip-3.41418pt\}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+ab\{\hskip-3.41418pt\{X,Y\penalty\ \}\hskip-3.41418pt\}+bc\{\hskip-3.41418pt\{Y,Z\penalty\ \}\hskip-3.41418pt\}+ca\{\hskip-3.41418pt\{Z,X\penalty\ \}\hskip-3.41418pt\}\Big).

(24)

Although these operators are not positive and thus cannot be valid effects in quantum theory, they add to the identity operator, i.e.

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b,c}\Pi^{\#}_{{}^{[a,b,c]}}=\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}.

(25)

Furthermore, on any qubit state
\rho_{\vec{m}}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

\displaystyle\sum_{b,c=\pm 1}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Pi^{\#}_{{}^{[a,b,c]}}\rho^{\otimes 2}_{\vec{m}}\right)

(26a)

\displaystyle\sum_{a,c=\pm 1}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Pi^{\#}_{{}^{[a,b,c]}}\rho^{\otimes 2}_{\vec{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathrm{P}^{b}_{\hat{y}}\rho_{\vec{m}}\right),

(26b)

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b=\pm 1}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Pi^{\#}_{{}^{[a,b,c]}}\rho^{\otimes 2}_{\vec{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathrm{P}^{c}_{\hat{z}}\rho_{\vec{m}}\right),

(26c)

thereby reproducing the measurement statistics of all three Pauli observables. We will now show that these operators are valid effects in minimal composition, i.e,

will yield positive probabilities on all two qubit separable states for all

. To establish this it is sufficient to show that it yields positive probabilities on all product states of the form

, where
\vec{r}
and

denote the respective Block vectors.

The spin-flip map

, where
\sigma_{y}
is the unitary map corresponding to Pauli

and

is the transpose map in computational basis. From the fact that both

and

is inner product preserving it follows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(A^{\dagger}B)=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{F}(A^{\dagger})\mathrm{F}(B))),\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ A,B\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}).

(27)

One can readily check that

\displaystyle\Pi^{\#}_{[a,b,c]}=(\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathrm{F})(\Pi^{\uparrow\downarrow}_{[a,b,c]})\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ a,b,c.

Since,
\Pi^{\uparrow\downarrow}_{[a,b,c]}
’s are positive we have, for all

, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Pi^{\uparrow\downarrow}_{[a,b,c]}\rho_{\vec{r}}\otimes\rho_{\vec{s}}\right)\geq 0,\quad\forall\penalty\ \rho_{\vec{r}},\rho_{\vec{s}}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathrm{F}(\Pi^{\uparrow\downarrow}_{[a,b,c]})\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathrm{F}(\rho_{\vec{r}}\otimes\rho_{\vec{s}})\right)\geq 0,\quad\forall\penalty\ \rho_{\vec{r}},\rho_{\vec{s}}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\Pi^{\#}_{[a,b,c]}\rho_{\vec{r}}\otimes\rho_{-\vec{s}}\right)\geq 0\quad\forall\penalty\ \rho_{\vec{r}},\rho_{\vec{s}}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),

This completes the proof.

IV
Joint measurability of Three Symmetric Spin Observables on Antiparallel configuration

In this section we analyze the joint measurability of a set of three spin observables along directions
\hat{n}_{1},\hat{n}_{2},
and
\hat{n}_{3}
that are symmetrically chosen by fixing the relative angles between any two of the spin directions are identical, i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{1}\cdot\hat{n}_{2}=\hat{n}_{2}\cdot\hat{n}_{3}=\hat{n}_{3}\cdot\hat{n}_{1}\penalty\ .

(37)

Without loss of any generality, by choosing
\hat{t}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1,1,1)^{\mathrm{T}}
, the above symmetry can be ensured by fixing

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{1}=(\alpha,\beta,\beta)^{\mathrm{T}},\quad\hat{n}_{2}=(\beta,\alpha,\beta)^{\mathrm{T}},\quad\hat{n}_{3}=(\beta,\beta,\alpha)^{\mathrm{T}},\penalty\ \text{where}

(38a)

\displaystyle\alpha:=\tfrac{1}{3}\big(\sqrt{3}\cos\theta+\sqrt{6}\sin\theta\big),\quad\beta:=\tfrac{1}{6}\big(2\sqrt{3}\cos\theta-\sqrt{6}\sin\theta\big),

(38b)

with
\theta:=\cos^{-1}(\hat{t}\cdot\hat{n}_{i})\in[0,\pi/2]
(see Fig.
5
). The case
\theta=0
is not interesting as all the observables become identical (
\hat{n}_{1}=\hat{n}_{2}=\hat{n}_{3}=\hat{t}
), and hence are trivially jointly measurable. This symmetric configuration imposes a corresponding symmetry on the parent POVM structure as stated in the following Lemma.

&\hskip 113.81102pt\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\Pi^{\theta}_{[a,b,c]}\mathrm{P}^{-c}_{\hat{n}_{3}}\otimes\mathrm{P}^{c}_{\hat{n}_{3}}\right]=0,\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ c;\end{cases}

(2)

On the electronic normal modes of the Meyer–Miller–Stock–Thoss representation of non-adiabatic dynamics
Lauren E. Cook, Timothy J. H. Hele

Abstract
Accurate and efficient simulation of non-adiabatic dynamics is highly desirable for understanding charge and energy transfer in complex systems. A key criterion for obtaining an accurate method is conservation of the Quantum Boltzmann Distribution (QBD). For a single surface, Matsubara dynamics is known to conserve the QBD, as a consequence of truncating the dynamics in the higher normal modes of the imaginary-time path integral. Recently, a Non-Adiabatic Matsubara (NA-Mats) dynamics has been proposed (J. Chem. Phys., 2021, 154, 124124) which truncates in the normal modes of the nuclear variables but not in the electronic variables, which are described with the Meyer–Miller–Stock–Thoss (MMST) representation. Surprisingly, this NA-Mats method does not appear to conserve the QBD for a general system. This poses the question of the effect of truncating the higher normal modes of the electronic variables in the MMST representation. In this article, we present what we believe is the first study of electronic normal modes of the MMST representation. We find that observables are not usually a function of a finite number of normal modes and that the higher normal modes are not constrained by the distribution, unlike in conventional nuclear normal modes. While for an ensemble of trajectories, truncation in MMST normal modes appears to numerically conserve the QBD, this is not the case for a single trajectory. When considering a correlation function, all MMST normal modes are required to obtain reasonable dynamics. Overall, this suggests that MMST variables are not optimal for obtaining an accurate, QBD conserving nonadiabatic dynamics method.

When considering the development of a non-adiabatic dynamics method, it would be highly desirable if the method satisfied the following criteria:

1.

Conservation the quantum Boltzmann distribution (QBD)

3.

4.

While many of the existing methods satisfy several of these criteria, as far as we are aware, none satisfy all of them simultaneously and exactly for a general quantum system.

The flow for method development has typically been creating a method and then testing for the properties. To ensure that the method satisfies the desired criteria, we suggest starting with understanding how to obtain these properties and then including this in the method development.

Non-Adiabatic Systems

III.4
Conservation of the quantum Boltzmann distribution

In Appendix
D
, we prove QBD conservation for both a single-bead and multi-bead calculation for a single trajectory. In the multi-bead case, acting the Liouvillian brings down a
\pm{\bf{{V}}}
either side that bead’s Boltzmann term, as seen in Fig.
D.1
. This means that the terms only cancel out when completing the path-integral loop, so all beads are required for the QBD to be conserved. The multi-bead proof can be used to prove that a calculation with all the normal modes included will also conserve the distribution. A back-transformation can be performed to obtain the bead-form from the normal-mode calculation, and this is equivalent to including all the beads. This also suggests that truncating in beads or normal modes will not conserve the QBD for a single trajectory, likely a consequence of the observable not being a function of only a finite number of the lowest normal modes. We investigate this computationally later in this article and leave further algebraic investigation of this as future work.

V
Conclusions

In this article we have considered in detail for the first time the electronic normal modes of the position and momentum variables of the Meyer–Miller–Stock–Thoss (MMST) representation of non-adiabatic dynamics.
We have discussed the use of electronic normal modes calculated through the ‘obvious’ method of directly transforming
{\bf{{q}}}
and
{\bf{{p}}}
from the MMST mapping Hamiltonian, as is done for the nuclear variables in Matsubara dynamics. Whilst we could have considered an alternative transformation matrix or a different metric to take normal modes of, we believe the study of MMST normal modes with the same transformation as for ring-polymer normal modes of significant interest. The transformation is well understood and utilised in Matsubara dynamics,
Ceriotti et al. 2010
;
Hele et al. 2015a
and the proposed NA-Mats method is constructed in MMST variables but does not take electronic normal modes or generally conserve the QBD.
Chowdhury and Huo 2021

We do not find that the observables are a function of only a finite number of the lowest normal modes, that the higher normal modes are constrained, that the QBD is conserved for a single trajectory when truncating in either beads or normal modes, or that accurate dynamics are obtained from truncation. The ensemble calculations are also particularly challenging to converge with a 2-level electronic-only system and there is an averaging effect that results in QBD conservation for all tested truncations. These results suggest that the MMST variables may not be the best metric to derive a non-adiabatic dynamics method satisfying the four criteria outlined in the introduction. However, this does not mean that we cannot find a non-adiabatic Matsubara method that will conserve the QBD distribution, for both a single and ensemble of trajectories, but rather that it requires a different metric from the MMST
{\bf{{q}}}
and
{\bf{{p}}}
. This also suggests that were one to construct a general non-adiabatic Matsubara method that truncated in both the electronic and nuclear normal modes it would be unlikely to conserve the QBD (at least for a single trajectory) and its dynamics would be unlikely to be a good approximation to the exact quantum result. These results may also explain why, despite the variety of non-adiabatic dynamics methods using the MMST representation, we are unaware of any such method which conserves the QBD.

(B.3)

From the definition of a total derivative we obtain,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial f({\check{{\bf{q}}}})}{\partial{q_{lm}}}\right|_{q_{j\neq l,s\neq m}}=\sum_{r}^{N}\left.\frac{\partial f({\check{{\bf{q}}}})}{\partial\check{q}_{rm}}\right|_{\check{q}_{s\neq m,w\neq r}}\left.\frac{\partial\check{q}_{rm}}{\partial{q_{lm}}}\right|_{q_{j\neq l,s\neq m}}\text{,}

(B.4)

where
f({\check{{\bf{q}}}})
is a ghost function, such that, using Eqn. (
21
),

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial f({\check{{\bf{q}}}})}{\partial{q_{lm}}}\right|_{q_{j\neq l,s\neq m}}=\sum_{r}^{N}\left.\frac{\partial f({\check{{\bf{q}}}})}{\partial\check{q}_{rm}}\right|_{\check{q}_{s\neq m,w\neq r}}\left.\sum_{u}^{N}\frac{\partial T_{ru}q_{um}}{\partial{q_{lm}}}\right|_{q_{j\neq l,s\neq m}}\text{,}

(B.5)

where,

\displaystyle\sum_{u}^{N}\left.\frac{\partial T_{ru}q_{um}}{\partial{q_{lm}}}\right|_{q_{j\neq l,s\neq m}}=T_{lr}\delta_{lu}\text{.}

(B.6)

We can now express Eqn. (
B.2
) as

\displaystyle{\bf{p}}^{\textrm{T}}_{l}{\bf{V}}\overrightarrow{\nabla}_{{\bf{q}}_{l}}=\sum_{k,r}^{N}\sum_{n,m}^{F}T_{lk}\check{p}_{kn}{\bf{V}}_{nm}T_{lr}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\check{q}_{rm}}\right|_{\check{q}_{s\neq m,w\neq r}}\text{,}

(B.7)

and likewise for the second term,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\textrm{elec}}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\hbar}\left[\sum_{k,r}^{N}\sum_{n,m}^{F}T_{lk}\check{p}_{kn}{\bf{V}}_{nm}T_{lr}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\check{q}_{rm}}\right|_{\check{q}_{s\neq m,w\neq r}}-T_{lk}\check{q}_{kn}{\bf{V}}_{nm}T_{lr}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\check{p}_{rm}}\right|_{\check{p}_{s\neq m,w\neq r}}\right]\text{.}

(B.8)

This can be simplified by using Eqn. (
28
) to obtain,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\textrm{elec}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left[\sum_{k}^{N}\sum_{n,m}^{F}\check{p}_{kn}{\bf{V}}_{nm}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\check{q}_{km}}\right|_{\check{q}_{s\neq m,w\neq k}}-\check{q}_{kn}{\bf{V}}_{nm}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\check{p}_{km}}\right|_{\check{p}_{s\neq m,w\neq k}}\right]

(B.9a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left[\sum_{k}^{N}\check{{\bf{p}}}_{k}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{V}}\overrightarrow{\nabla}_{\check{{\bf{q}}}_{k}}-\check{{\bf{q}}}_{k}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{V}}\overrightarrow{\nabla}_{\check{{\bf{p}}}_{k}}\right]\text{.}

(B.9b)

It is easy to show that acting this Liouvillian on
(\check{q}+i\check{p})_{km}
(where
\hbar=1
) is consistent with the propagation equation in Eqn. (
29
).

C
Nuclear Dependence

In the more general case where there is nuclear dependence of the potential matrix, denoted by a subscript
l
, then the bead evolution in Eqn. (
24
) becomes,

\displaystyle(\dot{q}+i\dot{p})_{ln}(t)

\displaystyle=-i{\bf{V}}_{lnm}(q+ip)_{lm}(t)\text{,}

(C.1)

and the normal mode evolution is then,

\displaystyle(\dot{\check{q}}+i\dot{\check{p}})_{kn}(t)

\displaystyle=-i{\bf{V}}_{lnm}T_{lk}T_{lr}(\check{q}+i\check{p})_{rm}(t)\text{,}

(C.2)

If we let the sum over
l
be,

\displaystyle\sum_{l}^{N}{\bf{V}}_{lnm}T_{lk}T_{lr}=U_{knrm}\text{,}

(C.3)

which is a rank-4 tensor. This allows us to rewrite the normal mode electronic propagation as,

\displaystyle(\dot{\check{q}}+i\dot{\check{p}})_{kn}(t)

\displaystyle=-iU_{knrm}(\check{q}+i\check{p})_{rm}(t)\text{,}

(C.4)

which is very similar to Eqn. (
24
) except the potential matrix has been replaced by a tensor.

The electronic Liouvillian, Eqn. (
B.8
) becomes,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\textrm{elec},l}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\hbar}\left[\sum_{k,r}^{N}\sum_{n,m}^{F}\check{p}_{kn}T_{lk}{\bf{V}}_{lnm}T_{lr}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\check{q}_{rm}}\right|_{\check{q}_{s\neq m,w\neq r}}-\check{q}_{kn}T_{lk}{\bf{V}}_{lnm}T_{lr}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\check{p}_{rm}}\right|_{\check{p}_{s\neq m,w\neq r}}\right]\text{,}

(C.5)

where using Eqn. (
C.3
), such that,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\textrm{elec},l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left[\sum_{k,r}^{N}\sum_{n,m}^{F}\check{p}_{kn}U_{knrm}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\check{q}_{rm}}\right|_{\check{q}_{s\neq m,w\neq r}}-\check{q}_{kn}U_{knrm}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\check{p}_{rm}}\right|_{\check{p}_{s\neq m,w\neq r}}\right]\text{.}

(C.6)

Again, it is easy to show that this Liouvillian is consistent with the propagation equation in Eqn. (
C.4
).

D
Conservation of the QBD

To prove conservation of the QBD, we need to show that the expectation value of
C_{AB}(t)
, where
A=\mathbb{I}
, is constant.
Hele et al. 2015a
;
Ananth 2013
;
Hele and Ananth 2016
We do this by showing that the derivative is zero, noting that,

\displaystyle\dot{C}^{[N]}_{\mathbb{I}B}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi\hbar)^{KN}}\iint d{\bf{q}}d{\bf{p}}[e^{-\beta_{N}\hat{H}}]_{\overline{W}}\overrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{elec}}}\times[\hat{B}(t)]_{W}\text{,}

(D.1)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{elec}}
is the electronic Liouvillian. We use the fact that
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{elec}}}=-\overleftarrow{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{elec}}}
, as it only consists of first-order derivatives,
Hele and Ananth 2016
to act the Liouvillian on the zero-time
[e^{-\beta_{N}\hat{H}}]_{\overline{W}}
term of the correlation function. In the following sections, we aim to prove that the Liouvillian acting on the QBD term is zero for both a single and multiple beads.

D.1
Single bead

For 1 bead, the Liouvillian is Eqn. (
18
) (where we let
\hbar=1
) and the CF is Eqn. (
36
) in the single bead limit
such that,

\displaystyle\dot{C}_{AB}(t)=-\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}(\pi\hbar)^{K}}\iint d{\bf{q}}d{\bf{p}}\ \mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec}}e^{-\mathcal{G}_{\textrm{tot}}/\hbar}\textrm{Tr}\left[e^{-\beta{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{C}}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right]\textrm{Tr}\left[({\bf{C}}-\hbar\mathbb{I}){\bf{B}}(t)\right]\text{,}

(D.2)

where we have used the fact that
\hat{H}={\bf{{V}}}
as we only have an electronic system.

Hence, we need to prove that
\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec}}e^{-\mathcal{G}_{\textrm{tot}}/\hbar}=0
and
\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec}}\textrm{Tr}\left[e^{-\beta{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{C}}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right]=0
to prove conservation of the distribution. The first is easy to show utilising the fact that
{\bf{{V}}}
is symmetric such that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec}}e^{-\mathcal{G}_{\textrm{tot}}/\hbar}=\frac{2}{\hbar}\left(-{\bf{p}}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{V}}}{\bf{{q}}}+{\bf{p}}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{V}}}{\bf{{q}}}\right)e^{-({\bf{{q}}}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{q}}}+{\bf{{p}}}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{p}}})/\hbar}=0\text{.}

(D.3)

For the second part,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec}}\textrm{Tr}\left[e^{-\beta{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{C}}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=i\left({\bf{q}}-i{\bf{{p}}}\right)^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{V}}}e^{-\beta{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{q}}}+i{\bf{{p}}}\right)-i\left({\bf{q}}-i{\bf{{p}}}\right)^{\textrm{T}}e^{-\beta{\bf{{V}}}}{\bf{{V}}}\left({\bf{{q}}}+i{\bf{{p}}}\right)\text{,}

(D.4)

where
{\bf{{V}}}
and
e^{-\beta{\bf{{V}}}}
commute such that these terms cancel out.
We conclude that for a single bead in
{\bf{{q}}}
and
{\bf{{p}}}
the QBD is conserved.

D.2
N
beads

The picture is more complicated with multiple beads as the product of matrices does not possess easily identifiable commutation rules.
For multiple beads the Liouvillian is Eqn. (
18
), and we are now trying to prove that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec},l}e^{\sum_{j}({\bf{{q}}}_{j}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{q}}}_{j}+{\bf{{p}}}_{j}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{p}}}_{j})/\hbar}=0\text{,}

(D.5a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec},l}\Omega=0\text{,}

(D.5b)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec},l}
is the Liouvillian acting on the
l
-th bead and
\Omega=\textrm{Tr}\left[\prod_{j}^{N}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{C}}_{j}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right]
which is the Boltzmann term for
N
beads plotted in Figure
2
. As there are no mixed bead terms in the first condition, this follows the from the single-bead case. The second condition is more challenging to prove due to the mixed bead terms.
We can use the cyclic trace to bring the
l
-th bead to the front,

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=\textrm{Tr}\left[e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{C}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\prod_{k=l+1}^{k=l-1}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{C}}_{k}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right]

(D.6a)

\displaystyle=\textrm{Tr}\left[e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{C}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\Gamma_{l}\right]\text{,}

(D.6b)

where we let
\Gamma_{l}=\prod_{k=l+1}^{k=l-1}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{C}}_{k}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec},l}\Omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left({\bf{{p}}}_{l}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{V}}}\overrightarrow{\nabla}_{{\bf{{q}}}_{l}}-{\bf{{q}}}_{l}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{V}}}\overrightarrow{\nabla}_{{\bf{{p}}}_{l}}\right)\textrm{Tr}\left[e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}{\bf{C}}_{l}\Gamma_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\Gamma_{l}\right]\text{,}

(D.7)

where
\Gamma_{l}
has no dependence on the
l
-th bead that we are differentiating with respect to such that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec},l}\Omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left({\bf{{p}}}_{l}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{V}}}\overrightarrow{\nabla}_{{\bf{{q}}}_{l}}-{\bf{{q}}}_{l}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{V}}}\overrightarrow{\nabla}_{{\bf{{p}}}_{l}}\right)\textrm{Tr}\left[{\bf{C}}_{l}\Gamma_{l}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\right]

(D.8a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left({\bf{{p}}}_{l}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{V}}}\overrightarrow{\nabla}_{{\bf{{q}}}_{l}}-{\bf{{q}}}_{l}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{V}}}\overrightarrow{\nabla}_{{\bf{{p}}}_{l}}\right)\left({\bf{q}}-i{\bf{{p}}}\right)_{l}^{\textrm{T}}\Gamma_{l}e^{-\beta_{N}\bm{V}}\left({\bf{q}}+i{\bf{{p}}}\right)_{l}

(D.8b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}i\left({\bf{{q}}}-i{\bf{{p}}}\right)_{l}^{\textrm{T}}{\bf{{V}}}\Gamma_{l}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{q}}}+i{\bf{{p}}}\right)_{l}-i\left({\bf{{q}}}-i{\bf{{p}}}\right)_{l}^{\textrm{T}}\Gamma_{l}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}{\bf{{V}}}\left({\bf{{q}}}+i{\bf{{p}}}\right)_{l}

(D.8c)

\displaystyle=i\sum_{l=1}^{N}\textrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma_{l}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}{\bf{{V}}}-{\bf{{V}}}{\bf{{C}}}_{l}\right)\right]\text{.}

(D.8d)

If we reinsert
\hbar\mathbb{I}/2
terms,

\displaystyle{\bf{{C}}}_{l}{\bf{{V}}}-{\bf{{V}}}{\bf{{C}}}_{l}

\displaystyle=\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right){\bf{{V}}}-{\bf{{V}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\text{,}

(D.9)

such that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec},l}A

\displaystyle=i\sum_{l=1}^{N}\textrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma_{l}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right){\bf{{V}}}\right]-\textrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma_{l}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}{\bf{{V}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right]\text{,}

(D.10)

where all terms result in a product of
{\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}
matrices which aids in simplifying the algebra. We refer to
e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)
as the Boltzmann term for the
l
-th bead.

Figure D.1:
A path-integral schematic for the Liouvillian operator for 4 beads. The smooth lines are the imaginary-time evolution where the Boltzmann term is evaluated for each bead at the blue circles. We see that the Liouvillian pulls down a
{\bf{{V}}}
to the right of the Boltzmann term and
-{\bf{{V}}}
to the left, such that terms will cancel when the whole path-integral loop is included.

We note that the Liouvillian operator results in two terms per bead which we refer to as positive and negative terms such that, for the
l
-th bead,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{l}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma_{l}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right){\bf{{V}}}\right]\text{,}

(D.11a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{l}

\displaystyle=-\textrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma_{l}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}{\bf{{V}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right]\text{.}

(D.11b)

The negative term can be rewritten as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{l}

\displaystyle=-\textrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma_{l}{\bf{{V}}}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right]\text{.}

(D.12)

where we have used the fact that
e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}
and
{\bf{{V}}}
commute to reverse the order of these matrices. The two terms we obtain per bead can be seen as the Liouvillian operator ‘pulling’ down a
{\bf{{V}}}
to the right of the Boltzmann term for that bead and a
-{\bf{{V}}}
to the left of the Boltzmann term for that bead, Figure
D.1
.

By using the cyclic properties of the trace and ring-polymer, we can bring the
(l+1)
-th bead to the right of the
l
-th bead such that the positive term becomes,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{l}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Tr}\left[Y_{k}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right){\bf{{V}}}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l+1}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right]\text{,}

(D.13)

where

\displaystyle Y_{k}=\prod_{k=l+2}^{l-1}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{k}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\text{.}

(D.14)

If we do a similar trick for the
(l+1)
-th bead, but instead including the
l
-th bead to the left then the negative term for the
(l+1)
-th bead becomes,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{l+1}

\displaystyle=-\textrm{Tr}\left[Y_{k}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right){\bf{{V}}}e^{-\beta_{N}{\bf{{V}}}}\left({\bf{{C}}}_{l+1}-\frac{\hbar}{2}\mathbb{I}\right)\right]\text{.}

(D.15)

We now see that the positive term of the
l
-th bead results in
{\bf{{V}}}
being in the same position in the product as for the negative term for the
(l+1)
-th bead. This results in these two terms cancelling such that when the sum over
N
is performed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{elec},l}\Omega=\mathcal{P}_{l}+\mathcal{N}_{(l+1)}+\mathcal{P}_{l+1}+\mathcal{N}_{l+2}+\ldots+\mathcal{P}_{l-1}+\mathcal{N}_{l}=0\text{,}

(D.16)

as the ring-polymer is cyclic.

We conclude that once all
N
beads have been included, all terms cancel out and the distribution will be conserved for multiple beads in
{\bf{{q}}}
and
{\bf{{p}}}
. This is in line with the proposed NA-Mats method conserving the QBD in the decoupled limit.
Chowdhury and Huo 2021

If all normal modes of
{\bf{{q}}}
and
{\bf{{p}}}
are being used then this derivation also holds true as back-transforming to beads will conserve the QBD. However, when truncating in beads or normal modes, we would expect this not to conserve the QBD as these terms will not fully cancel out. For an ensemble of trajectories, averaging may lead to conservation for both full and truncated calculations, and we leave the proof of this as future work.

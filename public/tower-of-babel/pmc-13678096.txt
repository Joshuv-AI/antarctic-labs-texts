Rapid Clearance of microRNA-371a-3p: A Longitudinal Study of Serum Levels in Patients with Localized Testicular Germ Cell Tumors

Patient Summary

Serum levels of microRNA-371a-3p (M371) are considered a promising biomarker of testicular germ cell tumors. This study revealed a much more rapid decline of M371 after treatment than that of traditional markers. Further research will show whether this feature can facilitate a more rapid assessment in clinical settings than is currently possible.

Introduction

Serum levels of microRNA-371a-3p (M371) fulfill almost all the conditions of an ideal tumor marker of testicular germ cell tumors (GCTs) [1], [2], [3] as outlined by Lange and Winfield [4] in 1987. In the primary diagnosis of GCTs, M371 revealed a sensitivity of 90% and a specificity of >90% [5], [6], [7]. The marker is not expressed in other malignancies [8], [9]. Relapses in clinical stage (CS) I GCTs can be detected with a sensitivity and specificity of 100% and 96.3%, respectively [10], [11], [12]. M371 serum levels correlate with tumor burden and response to treatment [13], [14], [15], [16], [17].

However, one of the key features has received little attention so far: the rapid clearance of the marker substance from serum after treatment [4]. To date, only one small pilot study has systematically assessed the velocity of decay and reported a serum half-life (H/L) of M371 <12 h after orchiectomy. That study also found that the time to normalization (TTN), which is the interval from surgery until the M371 level declines below the cutoff value, was associated with tumor size [18]. However, the level of evidence was low because of the small patient sample (n = 24) and low frequency of M371 measurements. Support came from a Norwegian study that reported a clearance of M371 of <24 h in four patients [13]. By contrast, a recent study from the USA observed patients exhibiting elevated M371 values even 3 wk after orchiectomy [19].

Knowledge about the velocity of decay of a tumor marker is essential for clinical decision-making, particularly when assessing the response to treatment. In GCTs, clinical management is impeded by the very long H/L of alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) of 5–7 d and the fairly long H/L of beta human chorionic gonadotropin (bHCG) of ∼36 h [20], [21], [22]. This investigation aimed to analyze the decay of serum M371 levels in a descriptive way by examining a sizeable cohort of patients with GCT with a rather high frequency of serum sampling after treatment. This is a biomarker kinetics study that does not evaluate whether M371-guided decisions can improve clinical management. Secondary end points were possible associations between the velocity of decay and clinical characteristics.

Patients and methods

Study population, clinical methods

In a prospective study, consecutive patients undergoing inguinal orchiectomy for testicular tumor in Asklepios Klinik Altona, Hamburg, during 2024–2025, meeting the following criteria were enrolled: (1) histologically proven testicular GCT, (2) CS I according to the European Association of Urology (EAU) [23], [24], and (3) aged >18 yr. Exclusively CS I cases were recruited because only in this setting can a tumor-free status be assumed after treatment. Serum samples were collected before orchiectomy and repeatedly at five postoperative checkpoints: 4 h, 8 h, 24 h, 2 d, and 2 wk. Because of clinical organizational problems, actual sampling times usually differed from planned time points by ∼10%. Planned time points were used for statistical analysis. The following clinical data were recorded for each patient: histology (seminoma; nonseminoma), patient age (yr), tumor size (mm), CS Ia or CS Ib according to EAU [23], [24], body mass index (BMI; kg/m2), and preoperative (baseline) M371 serum level. Serum values of AFP and bHCG were measured before orchiectomy and postoperatively on day 1 and after 2 wk.

Control subjects were male patients aged 18–60 yr who were treated for nonmalignant diseases at Asklepios Klinik Altona during 2024–2025; each provided one blood sample. The study followed the Reporting Recommendations for Tumor Marker Prognostic Studies guidelines regarding marker kinetic studies. Standards relating to evaluation of diagnostic performance and clinical marker utility were not applicable. All patients gave written informed consent for participation. Ethics approval was provided by the ethics committee of Ärztekammer Bremen (#301/11, May 30, 2011 and #301/17, September 21, 2017). All study activities were conducted in conformity with the Declaration of Helsinki of the World Medical Association, amended by the 75th General Assembly in October 2024.

Laboratory methods

Aliquots of serum samples were stored at –80°C until final analysis. Measurement of M371 serum levels was performed as detailed earlier [5], [9]. In short, a real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction was performed using the In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation–certified M371-Test (mir|detect, Bremerhaven, Germany) with miR-30b-5p as the endogenous control. M371 serum levels were reported as relative quantities (RQ values) according to the ΔΔCt method [25]. The cutoff value was defined as RQ = 10.0 in accordance with the specifications of the manufacturer of the test (mir|detect GmbH, Bremerhaven, Germany).

Serum levels of AFP and bHCG were determined according to institutional standard operating procedures as reported earlier [26].

Statistical methods

Clinical data and laboratory results were initially stored in a commercially available database (Microsoft Excel version 2511; Microsoft Corp., Redmond, WA, USA). Final statistical analysis was performed after transferring all data into SPSS (version 31.0.1.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA, RRID: SCR_002865).

Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis was performed using preoperative measurement results (preRQ) of patients with GCT and serum levels of controls to confirm that the sensitivity and specificity of the test in the present patient cohort did not differ from previous results and to ensure the appropriateness of the predefined cutoff value using Youden index analysis.

Median M371 serum levels with corresponding interquartile ranges (IQRs) were calculated for all measurement points. The preRQ values were statistically compared with the RQ values of the controls using the Mann–Whitney U test. Comparisons between median M371 values at consecutive time points were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

The H/L was defined as the period from surgery to the time point at which M371 serum levels were predicted to reach 50% of baseline. The H/L of M371 was calculated for each patient by normalizing baseline levels to 100% and applying linear interpolation to the two measurements bracketing a 50% decline. TTN was defined as the interval from surgery to the time point at which the M371 level was predicted to reach the cutoff value (details regarding the calculation of H/L and TTN in supplementary text documents).

To evaluate possible associations of H/L or TTN with clinical factors, Spearman correlation analysis was performed using the continuous variables age, BMI, tumor size, and baseline RQ value. Categorical parameters were compared as follows: histology (seminoma vs nonseminoma) and CS (CS Ia vs CS Ib).

Discussion

The central result of this study is the exceptionally short M371 H/L of 3.8 h in patients with CS I disease following orchiectomy. Importantly, 9.4% of the patients require >2 wk to decline below the cutoff.

H/L

The M371 H/L of 3.8 h is markedly shorter than the half-lives of bHCG (36 h), AFP (5–7 d), and lactate dehydrogenase (∼24 h) [20], [21]. This finding is consistent with an H/L of <12 h found in pilot investigations [13], [18]. However, this analysis used a much more granular assessment than previous studies by using four repeated measurements during the first 48 h, in contrast to daily postoperative examinations used in former studies. Thus, the H/L of 3.8 h found herein probably represents a robust and valuable result that is supported by the narrow IQR (2.9–6.2 h).

Association of H/L with clinical factors

The precise mechanisms governing the biological clearance of circulating M371 are not known. However, given that M371 is undetectable in urine [27] and its decay rate is independent of renal function [19], it must be hypothesized that the clearance is likely mediated by blood-borne degradation, presumably via specific ribonuclease activity.

Accordingly, there was no statistically significant evidence of association of the relative rate of decay with any of the clinical factors analyzed. This finding is consistent with the concept that H/L reflects the relative postoperative decay rate of M371 and is therefore expected to be largely independent of the initial biomarker concentration or tumor burden.

An apparently slow decline resulted from a statistical issue in two cases; both had very low baseline levels (RQ 13.3 and 15.46). Their percentage decline appeared to be rather low, although absolute values had dropped below the threshold.

In contrast to the relative velocity of decay, the total absolute TTN of M371 serum levels is probably stochastically related to the number of circulating M371 molecules on one side and to the amount of degrading enzymes on the other side. Accordingly, Spearman correlation analysis revealed significant correlations of TTN with baseline M371 levels, tumor size, and CS. These findings are in line with expectations because patients with higher initial M371 levels require several H/L periods to decline below the cutoff. Likewise, tumor size and CS are associated with TTN, as both factors have previously been associated with higher preoperative M371 expression.

Interindividual variability of the TTN

The absolute TTN varies widely (IQR 6.5–261.6 h), with ∼10% of the patients not dropping below the cutoff within 2 wk postoperatively.

Very high baseline M371 levels (RQ 1632–2279) are a common feature of the slowly declining cases. However, in a model calculation with a given median H/L of 3.8 h, the drop below cutoff should have been accomplished after 7–8 H/L periods with an expected TTN of ∼36–48 h in patients with such baseline levels. In five cases, the observed TTN was much longer than 2 wk.

One reason for persistently elevated M371 levels after orchiectomy may be occult disease in regional lymph nodes, indicating a high risk of relapse [28], [29]. Accordingly, clinical re-evaluation disclosed that one of the patients relapsed 18 mo later.

Another patient with a very long TTN was aged 87 yr and also had an excessively elevated baseline M371 level (RQ = 2279.7). One hypothesis is that age-dependent regression of enzyme metabolism could have delayed the decline in serum levels. This postulation is supported by the significant correlation between age and TTN found herein. However, this issue needs further consideration because only very few older patients were included.

In two cases with postoperatively increasing or plateauing serum levels, the preoperative measurement had been conducted about 2 wk before surgery. Conceivably, tumor progression during the waiting period might have caused an increase in serum levels, making the first postoperative miR level appear higher than baseline. Preoperative M371 measurements were usually done on the day of the GCT diagnosis. The waiting time for orchiectomy averages 5–10 d, but a few patients had longer intervals. Thus, tumor progression during the preoperative waiting period could account for some of the slow declines.

In all, M371 levels may decline more slowly in some cases, and the underlying biological reasons are not fully understood. Consequently, caregivers of patients with GCT should be aware of slow decline in M371 expression in isolated cases, particularly in those with very high baseline levels.

Prospects arising from the short H/L

The exceptionally short H/L of M371 is likely to represent another unique characteristic of this biomarker, complementing its high sensitivity and specificity as well as its expression across both major histologic subgroups of GCTs. Although formal clinical evidence is currently lacking, the rapid clearance kinetics of M371 may prove valuable in clinical situations for which existing management strategies remain suboptimal. One such scenario is the persistent elevation of conventional serum tumor markers in patients with nonseminomatous GCT following orchiectomy. Also, this finding may provide a rationale for prospective, controlled studies to determine whether the remarkably rapid clearance of M371 can be incorporated into diagnostic and therapeutic algorithms for GCTs.

Limitations

Confounding effects might have resulted from the fact that baseline M371 levels had usually been measured several days before surgery. In fact, only 18 patients had their blood draws on the day of orchiectomy, whereas 23, 10, and 2 patients were examined earlier, at intervals of 1–10 d preoperatively, 10–14 d, and >2 wk, respectively. Thus, serum levels may have increased during the waiting period, possibly affecting the calculated H/L. Another limitation might result from missing measurement values at several postoperative checkpoints. The 23 missing values at the 48-h checkpoint were attributable to hospital administrative constraints, not to clinical problems. Specifically, the missing values at this time point could have biased the calculation of the median TTN.

A minor limitation might result from not accounting for the histologic subtypes of nonseminoma with their known wide variance of M371 expression [5], [30].

With respect to the statistical analysis, individual H/L were calculated using linear interpolation analysis, assuming a linear decline of serum levels between two measurement points, although the true biological course of miR levels probably does not exactly follow that assumption.

The rapid decay of M371 found herein specifically relates to localized disease (CS I). It is rational to assume a similarly rapid clearance in response to chemotherapy in advanced cases, as indicated previously [5], [28], [31], [32]. However, M371 levels will probably decline less rapidly in metastatic cases because chemotherapy eradicates tumor masses gradually in contrast to orchiectomy in CS I cases, in which the entire mass is immediately removed by surgery.

Conclusions

M371 serum levels in patients with CS I GCT decline with an H/L of 3.8 h after orchiectomy, which is markedly shorter than the H/Ls of the traditional markers. About 10% of the cases require a markedly longer absolute TTN. Overall, the findings support further prospective validation, ideally with baseline sampling on the day of surgery. If validated, this study results could also provide a rationale for evaluating clinical utility end points associated with the rapid kinetic profile of M371.

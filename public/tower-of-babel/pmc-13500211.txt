Standardized Patient Positioning and Operating Room Setup for Ankle Arthroscopy: A Technical Note
Authors: Julien Paquot, David Roche, Ronny Lopes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis technical note describes and standardizes patient positioning and operating room setup for ankle arthroscopy in supine, prone, and lateral decubitus positions. The proposed configuration emphasizes ergonomic optimization, efficient cable management, and improved workflow for both surgeon and assistant. A reproducible organization of the arthroscopy tower and instrument table facilitates visualization, instrument handling, and safety while minimizing sterility breaches and technical incidents. This standardized setup enhances the efficiency, reproducibility, and overall safety of ankle arthroscopy, provided that sufficient space is maintained around the operating table.

Since the first arthroscopic description of the ankle by Takagi in 1939, 1 the techniques, instrumentation (including arthroscopes with cold light sources and dedicated surgical instruments), and indications for ankle arthroscopy have evolved considerably. The work of Ferkel 2 , 3 , 4 helped to structure the anterior and posterior approaches, while Van Dijk 5 questioned the systematic use of distraction in anterior ankle arthroscopy.

Ankle arthroscopy now plays an essential role in hindfoot and ankle surgery, with a broad spectrum of indications encompassing ligamentous instability, tendinous disorders, osteochondral and degenerative lesions, and anterior or posterior impingement syndromes.

Patient positioning, adapted to the specific surgical procedure, represents a critical step. It determines patient safety, the quality of arthroscopic visualization, and the comfort and efficiency of the surgical team.

Three main positions are currently described: supine (dorsal decubitus), prone (ventral decubitus), and lateral decubitus. This article illustrates and compares these three setups, emphasizing the practical aspects of cable management, tower placement, and the positioning of the surgeon and assistant.

SURGICAL TECHNIQUE

Patient Positioning

Regardless of patient positioning, the procedure is performed under general or regional anesthesia, depending on the patient's comorbidities and patient comfort.

A pneumatic tourniquet is systematically applied at the proximal thigh of the operative limb and inflated to 300 mmHg. The specific setup for each position is described below.

Positioning for the Supine Position

The patient is positioned supine on a standard operating table, with the heels extending beyond the edge to facilitate ankle mobilization during the procedure and especially dorsal flexion.

A triangular cushion is placed under the ipsilateral buttock to compensate for the natural external rotation of the limb and to maintain the ankle in a neutral, aligned position throughout the surgery. The contralateral leg is slightly lowered using the leg section of the operating table (Figures 1, 2, 3).

Positioning for the Prone Position

The patient is positioned prone on a standard operating table, with the foot extending beyond the edge.

A triangular cushion is positioned under the ipsilateral thigh to correct the natural external rotation of the lower limb and to maintain the ankle in a neutral alignment during surgery. Another triangular cushion is placed under the operated foot. The contralateral leg is slightly lowered using the leg section of the operating table (Figures 4 and 5).

Prone position before sterile draping for posterior left ankle arthroscopy. TC are placed under the ipsilateral thigh and the operative foot to maintain neutral ankle alignment (black star). The contralateral leg is slightly lowered (red star) to improve access. (TC, triangular cushion.)

Prone position after sterile draping. The operative foot extends beyond the edge of the table, allowing optimal access to the posterior left ankle.

Positioning for the Lateral Decubitus Position

The patient is placed in the contralateral lateral decubitus position. A sacral support and a pubic support are positioned to maintain stability throughout the procedure. The operative foot extends beyond the edge of the table, and a cushion is placed under the ankle to elevate and stabilize the ankle. The foot naturally rests in inversion (Figures 6 and 7).

Lateral decubitus position before sterile draping for right ankle arthroscopy. Sacral and pubic supports ensure patient stability. The operative foot extends beyond the table edge (black star), with a cushion placed under the ankle to provide elevation and stabilization. The foot rests in inversion, facilitating access to the lateral compartment. (C, cushion; PS, pubic supports; SS, sacral supports.)

Lateral decubitus position after sterile draping. The operative right ankle remains elevated and accessible.

Operating Room Setup (Common to All Positions)

The arthroscopy tower is placed contralaterally in supine and prone positions and on the anterior (abdominal) side in the lateral decubitus position.

After the sterile drapes are applied, an instrument table is positioned perpendicular to the operating table, on the ipsilateral side of the operative limb for the supine and prone positions, and on the dorsal side of the patient for the lateral decubitus position.

The arthroscopy tower cables (light source, camera, suction, and shaver) run over the patient and are secured with a Velcro strap to prevent falls, tension, or disconnection.

The cables are then placed directly onto the instrument table, which is positioned in contact with the operating table, ensuring there is no gap between the two surfaces. The cables are arranged so that they reach approximately two‐thirds of the instrument table, providing sufficient length for comfortable instrument handling while avoiding excessive slack that could lead to tangling or sterility breaches (Figures 8 and 9 and Video 1). DISCUSSION

Several publications have described patient positioning and draping techniques in arthroscopic surgery. 6 , 7 However, most of these reports focus primarily on patient setup and provide limited detail regarding equipment arrangement, instrument table positioning, or the organization of cables and instruments within the operative field.

The present work proposes a standardized and reproducible configuration of the operative field aimed at improving surgical workflow and overall procedural safety. This setup promotes a structured and organized environment, facilitating efficient instrument handling while minimizing clutter and cable entanglement. Precise patient positioning combined with careful operating room organization is essential to ensure optimal visualization and a smooth workflow during ankle arthroscopy.

Specific positioning adjustments may further enhance surgical ergonomics. Lowering the contralateral leg improves surgeon comfort and facilitates access to the operative ankle, particularly during anterior and posterior procedures. In the lateral decubitus position, placing a support beneath the ankle enhances exposure of the lateral compartment and facilitates instrument handling by opening the sinus tarsi. 8

In addition to patient positioning, standardizing the organization of the arthroscopy tower and instrument table plays a key role in procedural efficiency and safety. Positioning the instrument table perpendicular to and flush against the operating table allows cables to rest directly on the sterile field, thereby reducing the risk of sterility breaches or accidental disconnection. Preoperative determination of appropriate cable length ensures sufficient range of motion while preventing excessive tension during the procedure.

Overall, this standardized configuration improves the efficiency and reproducibility of ankle arthroscopy while minimizing technical issues related to poor ergonomics or suboptimal cable management. It also facilitates coordination between the surgeon and assistant. However, this setup requires sufficient space around the operating table and, in smaller operating rooms, may encroach on staff circulation areas, which should be anticipated during room preparation.

Overall, this standardized and reproducible setup represents a fundamental component of ankle arthroscopy. Beyond patient positioning alone, the organization of the arthroscopy tower, instrument table, and cables ensures optimal ergonomics, efficient instrument handling, and enhanced surgical safety. Systematizing this approach may improve visualization, reduce operative time, and decrease the risk of sterility breaches or equipment‐related incidents. However, this configuration requires sufficient space around the operating table and, particularly in smaller operating rooms, may encroach on staff circulation areas, which should be anticipated during room preparation.

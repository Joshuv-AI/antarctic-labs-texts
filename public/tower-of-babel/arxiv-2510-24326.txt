On the embeddings of selfadjoint operator spaces Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. 46L07, 46B40, 46L52, 47L05, 47L07 Thanks: Key words and phrases: Selfadjoint operator spaces, unitisations, complete positivity, embeddings.
Alexandros Chatzinikolaou, Evgenios T.A. Kakariadis, Se-Jin Kim, Ioannis Apollon Paraskevas

Abstract
. We investigate when a map on a selfadjoint operator space E E is an embedding, i.e., when its unitisation in the sense of Werner is completely isometric. Combining with results of Russell, of Ng, and of Dessi, the second and the last author, it is shown that this is equivalent to: (a) extending bounded positive functionals on each matrix level with the same norm; (b) extending quasistates to quasistates in each matrix level; (c) extending completely bounded completely positive maps with the same cb-norm; and (d) the map being a gauge maximal isometry in the sense of Russell. If E E is approximately positively generated and C ∗ ​ ( E ) \mathrm{C}^{*}(E) is unital, or if E s ​ a E_{sa} is singly generated, then completely positive maps on E ⊆ ℬ ⁡ ( H ) E\subseteq\mathcal{B}(H) have completely positive extensions on C ∗ ​ ( E ) \mathrm{C}^{*}(E) , but possibly not with the same cb-norm; and this is not enough for the inclusion E ⊆ C ∗ ​ ( E ) E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E) to be an embedding. We show that the inclusion E ⊆ C ∗ ​ ( E ) E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E) is always an embedding when E E is completely approximately 1-generated, and we fully resolve the case when E s ​ a E_{sa} is singly generated. Combining with the works of Salomon, Humeniuk–Kennedy–Manor, and previous work of the third author, we show that if the inclusion E ⊆ C ∗ ​ ( E ) E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E) is an embedding, then rigidity at zero, in the sense of Salomon, coincides with E E being approximately positively generated. Consequently, we show that E E is approximately positively generated if and only if M n ​ ( E ) M_{n}(E) is approximately positively generated for all n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} , thus extending a previous result of Humeniuk–Kennedy–Manor to the approximation setting. As an application we show that hyperrigidity of E E in C ∗ ​ ( E ) \mathrm{C}^{*}(E) allows to identify C ∗ ​ ( E ) \mathrm{C}^{*}(E) as the C*-envelope of E E in several (non-unital) contexts.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Framework

Operator systems, i.e., closed, selfadjoint subspaces of
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
containing its unit, have a central role in the theory of Operator Algebras.
Lately, the community has been actively considering selfadjoint operator subspaces, but which need not be unital
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
23
]
.
Those appear naturally, for example when comparing operator systems up to their stabilisations as in the work of Connes and van Suijlekom
[
7
]
.
The absence of a unit disrupts the link between the norm and the matrix order structure, and thus many of the standard results from the unital setting no longer apply.
This creates significant difficulties in obtaining even fundamental theorems like the celebrated Arveson’s Extension Theorem.

Matrix ordered operator spaces arise naturally in the study of duality.
While the dual of a finite dimensional operator system is again an operator system, in the infinite dimensional case the dual space generally fails to be unital, and thus naturally falls into the broader category of matrix ordered operator spaces.
Connes and van Suijlekom
[
7
,
8
]
link aspects of noncommutative geometry to this framework through the study of spectral truncations and tolerance relations.
Selfadjoint operator spaces appear naturally in the construction of symmetrisations by Eleftherakis, the second author and Todorov
[
11
]
with applications to Morita theory.

In his seminal work, Werner
[
26
, Lemma 4.8 and Theorem 4.15]
characterises precisely the compatibility between the norm and the order structure in a matrix ordered operator space so that it is concretely realised in some
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
by a completely isometric complete order embedding.
A
selfadjoint operator space
is exactly a matrix ordered operator space that embeds in this way, so that both its matrix norm structure and its matrix cone structure are inherited from
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
Towards this end, Werner
[
26
]
introduces the notion of the unitisation
E^{\#}
of a matrix ordered operator space
E
, with the property that the (unique) unital extension of a completely contractive completely positive map from
E
to
E^{\#}
is completely positive.
Werner’s unitisation
E^{\#}
is an operator system, and the matrix ordered operator space
E
is a (concrete) selfadjoint operator space if and only if the canonical inclusion
E\subseteq E^{\#}
is completely isometric.

Positivity in Werner’s unitisation is extrinsic, in the sense that it is described in terms of the states on every matrix level.
On the other hand van Dobben de Bruyn in
[
10
, Theorem 8.3]
provides an intrinsic characterisation of positivity in relation to the distance from the positive cones.
The distance of a selfadjoint element from the positive cone plays also a central role in the theory of maximal gauges of Russell
[
23
]
.

1.2.
The embedding problem

Although any completely isometric complete order embedding has a unique completely positive extension on the unitisation, this extension is not always completely isometric.
The maps that do have this property are called
embeddings
.
Russell
[
23
, Example 4.1]
, and Kennedy, the third author and Manor
[
16
, Example 2.14]
show that this fails even for (finite dimensional) singly generated spaces (in commutative C*-algebras).
Moreover, Connes and van Suijlekom
[
7
, Remark 2.27]
show that completely isometric complete order maps are not the suitable morphisms to obtain a meaningful extremal theory in this category, and one needs to restrict to the class of embeddings.
Let us recall here this example to illustrate the subtlety of the question.

Example 1.1
.

[
7
, Remark 2.27]
,
[
16
, Example 2.14]
,
[
23
, Example 4.1]
.
For
r\in(0,1]
, consider

x_{r}:=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-r\end{bmatrix}.

Set
E_{r}:=\operatorname{span}\{x_{r}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}\subseteq M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, which is a selfadjoint operator space with trivial cones.
Because of this triviality, the map

\varphi\colon E_{r}\to\mathbb{C};\varphi(x_{r})=-1,

is a (completely) contractive (completely) positive functional.
When
r<1
this map fails to extend to a (completely) contractive (completely) positive functional on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and thus to
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
as
-1\notin\overline{{\rm conv}}\{\sigma_{M_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(x_{r})\}
.
Moreover, the map

\vartheta\colon E_{r}\to M_{2}(\mathbb{C});\vartheta(x_{r})=-x_{r},

is a completely isometric complete order embedding.
When
r<1
this map fails to extend to a complete isometry on the unitisations, as in this case the elements
\pm x_{r}
would generate an element in the C*-envelope of
E_{r}
with an empty spectrum.

One may suspect that it is the triviality of the cones that enforces this
pathology.
However this is not true as, when
r=1
, the inclusion
E_{1}\subseteq M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is an embedding.

This example exhibits that embeddings should reflect both the extrinsic and intrinsic aspects of positivity inherited from the C*-cover, e.g., extensions of maps, spectrum, positive decomposition and distance from cones.

In terms of extensions, Werner
[
27
]
shows that a completely isometric complete order embedding
\vartheta\colon E\to F
is an embedding if and only if any completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon M_{n}(E)\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
has a completely contractive completely positive extension
\widetilde{\phi}\colon M_{n}(F)\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
with
\widetilde{\phi}\circ\vartheta=\phi
, for all
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
There are two refinements analogous to the operator system theory.
Ng
[
19
]
(in a broader context) shows that
\vartheta
is an embedding if and only if any completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon E\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
has a completely contractive completely positive extension
\widetilde{\phi}\colon F\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
with
\widetilde{\phi}\circ\vartheta=\phi
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Dessi with the second and last authors
[
9
]
show that
\vartheta
is an embedding if and only if any contractive positive functional on
\varphi\colon M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
has a contractive positive extension
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon M_{n}(F)\to\mathbb{C}
with
\widetilde{\varphi}\circ\vartheta=\varphi
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In terms of intrinsic properties, the association with distance formulas is depicted by Russell
[
23
]
.
Therein, a completely isometric complete order embedding
\vartheta\colon E\to F
is called a
gauge maximal isometry
if

\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})=\operatorname{dist}(\vartheta^{(n)}(x),M_{n}(F)_{+})\text{ for all }x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}.

The motivation in
[
23
]
has been to pursue an abstract characterisation of selfadjoint operator spaces from the given data as in
[
26
]
.
As shown in
[
23
]
not all completely isometric complete order embeddings are gauge maximal isometries, leaving open the question of the complete characterisation.
Our first main theorem below combines the aforementioned works to the following characterisation of embeddings.

Theorem A.
(
[
19
]
,
[
9
]
, Theorem
3.3
, Theorem
3.8
)

Let
\vartheta\colon E\to F
be a completely isometric complete order embedding between selfadjoint operator spaces.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The map
\vartheta\colon E\to F
is an embedding.

(ii)

Every map
\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})
extends to a map
\widetilde{\varphi}\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(F),\mathbb{C})
with
\widetilde{\varphi}\circ\vartheta=\varphi
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(iii)

Every map
\phi\in{\rm CCP}(E,M_{k}(\mathbb{C}))
extends to a map
\widetilde{\phi}\in{\rm CCP}(F,M_{k}(\mathbb{C}))
with
\widetilde{\phi}\circ\vartheta=\phi
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(iv)

Every map
\phi\in{\rm CCP}(E,{\mathcal{B}}(K))
extends to a map
\widetilde{\phi}\in{\rm CCP}(F,{\mathcal{B}}(K))
with
\widetilde{\phi}\circ\vartheta=\phi
, for every Hilbert space
K
.

(v)

The map
\vartheta\colon E\to F
is a gauge maximal isometry.

Equivalence [(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)] is shown in
[
9
, Corollary 2.3]
.
Equivalence [(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)] is shown in a broader context in
[
19
, Lemma 4.2 (a)]
.
Equivalence [(iii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv)] follows by a BW-compactness argument; we provide a proof that passes through Russell’s gauge maximality of item (v) and van Dobben de Bruyn’s
[
10
]
description of the positive cones of the unitisation.
A key step in this approach is the distance formula

\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})=\sup_{\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})}-\varphi(x)\text{ for all }x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa},n\in\mathbb{N},

that we show in Theorem
3.2
.
In passing, in Corollary
3.4
we show that an inclusion
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding if and only if

\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})=\max_{\lambda\in\sigma_{M_{n}({\mathcal{C}})}(x)}-\lambda\text{ for all }x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa},n\in\mathbb{N}.

Item (iv) above is a version of Arveson’s Extension Theorem, an important tool for any further developments in the theory.

1.3.
The automatic embedding property

We next study when an inclusion
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
(or equivalently,
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
) is an embedding.
As test cases we have that
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding when
E
contains an approximate unit in a C*-subalgebra
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq E
as in
[
9
]
, or when
E
contains a matrix norm-defining approximate order unit for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
as in
[
8
]
(it is not known if the first class is contained in the second).
We focus on two further classes that have tractable cone structure: when
E
is
approximately positively generated
, i.e.,
E_{sa}=\overline{E_{+}-E_{+}}
, or when
E_{sa}
is singly generated.
These cases are of particular interest as completely positive maps may attain completely positive extensions.

Theorem B.
(Theorem
4.5
, Proposition
5.4
)

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be a selfadjoint operator space.
Then the following hold:

(i)

If
E
is approximately positively generated and
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is unital, then every completely bounded completely positive map
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
has an extension to a (completely bounded) completely positive map
\widetilde{\phi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
.

(ii)

If
E=\operatorname{span}\{x\}
for some
x\in E_{sa}
, then every (completely bounded) completely positive map
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
has an extension to a (completely bounded) completely positive map
\widetilde{\phi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
.

Approximately positively generated spaces
E
are in abundance and have a good position within a
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
In Proposition
4.3
we combine the notion of rigidity at zero of Salomon
[
24
]
with the works of Humeniuk–Kennedy–Manor
[
12
]
and of the third author
[
17
]
to show that
E
is approximately positively generated if and only if
M_{n}(E)
is approximately positively generated for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
This is an extension of
[
12
, Proposition 8.4]
to the approximation setting.

The proof of item (i) in Theorem B relies on Lemma
4.4
which may be of independent interest.
Therein it is shown that the set of extendable completely bounded and completely positive maps is closed in the point-weak* topology.
However this extra feature is not enough for inclusions to be embeddings.
In Example
4.21
we demonstrate that a positive extension of a positive functional may fail to preserve the norm, motivating the consideration of approximate positive generation together with norm control over the positive decompositions of selfadjoint elements.

Such decompositions have been extensively studied in
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
20
]
.
A selfadjoint operator space
E
is said to be
completely
\kappa
-generated
if there exists
\kappa\geq 1
such that

(M_{n}(E)_{sa})_{1}\subseteq(M_{n}(E)_{+})_{\kappa}-(M_{n}(E)_{+})_{\kappa},

where
(M_{n}(E)_{sa})_{1}
denotes the unit ball of selfadjoint elements in
M_{n}(E)
, and
(M_{n}(E)_{+})_{\kappa}
denotes the ball of radius
\kappa
in the positive cone.
Following their terminology,
E
is said to be
completely approximately
\kappa
-generated
if there exists
\kappa\geq 1
such that

(M_{n}(E)_{sa})_{1}\subseteq\overline{(M_{n}(E)_{+})_{\kappa}-(M_{n}(E)_{+})_{\kappa}}.

Ng proves in
[
20
, Theorem 3.9]
that a selfadjoint operator space
E
is completely
\kappa
-generated for some
\kappa>0
if and only if it is completely approximately
\kappa^{\prime}
-generated for some
\kappa^{\prime}>0
if and only if it is
dualisable
, i.e.,
E^{d}
is a selfadjoint operator space under the canonical dual matrix cone and with a matrix norm equivalent to the cb-norm.
Humeniuk–Kennedy–Manor show in
[
12
, Theorem 5.8]
that dualisability is equivalent to complete
\kappa^{\prime\prime}
-normality of the dual space.
Moreover, in
[
12
, Theorem 5.8]
they provide an intrinsic characterisation of dualisability in terms of the corresponding non-commutative pointed convex set of the selfadjoint operator space, see
[
16
, Theorem 4.5]
of Kennedy, the third author and Manor.
We show that when
\kappa=1
then we have an automatic embedding property.
Thus, apart from a good duality framework, in completely approximately 1-generated selfadjoint operator spaces one obtains automatically Arveson’s Extension Theorem.

Theorem C.
(Theorem
4.17
)

Let
E
be a completely approximately 1-generated selfadjoint operator space.
Then
E
has the automatic embedding property.

We note that due to the Hahn-Banach Theorem, every positive functional admits a selfadjoint extension with the same norm.
Complete approximate 1-generation imposes that such extensions are automatically positive due to a Jordan decomposition argument.
With Example
4.21
we stress that
E
may be positively generated, but not completely 1-generated, even if
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is unital (we further illustrate the independence of approximate positive generation, unitality of the generated C*-algebra, and embeddings in Example
5.8
).

Let us return now to Example
1.1
from
[
7
,
16
,
23
]
on singly generated selfadjoint operator spaces, and explain where the problem lies.

Theorem D.
(Corollary
5.2
, Theorem
5.6
)

Let
x\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)_{sa}
and let
x_{\pm}\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)_{+}
be the unique positive and negative components of
x
. Set
E:=\operatorname{span}\{x\}
.
Then we have the following cases:

(a)
If
x_{+}=0
or
x_{-}=0
, then the inclusion
\operatorname{span}\{x\}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding.

(b)
If
x_{\pm}\neq 0
, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The inclusion of
\operatorname{span}\{x\}
inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding.

(ii)

\|x_{+}\|=\|x_{-}\|
.

(iii)

The map
\phi\colon\operatorname{span}\{x\}\to\operatorname{span}\{x\}
with
\phi(x)=-x
has a completely contractive completely positive extension
\widetilde{\phi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.

(iv)

The functional
\varphi\colon\operatorname{span}\{x\}\to\mathbb{C}
with
\varphi(x)=-\|x\|
has a (completely) contractive (completely) positive extension
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
.

Therefore the extension of the maps
\phi
and
\varphi
are –each on its own– the only obstruction for the embedding property in Example
1.1
.
Likewise this shows why the inclusion
E_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
is an embedding.
In this case the map
\varphi
is the restriction of the compression to the (2,2) entry, while
\phi
is implemented by the permutation of the basis elements
e_{1}\leftrightarrow e_{2}
.

Finally we give applications of the embedding property with respect to extremal theory.
In general we have three classes to investigate:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K):\text{$\phi$ completely isometric completely positive}\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{\vartheta\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K):\text{$\vartheta$ completely isometric complete order embedding}\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{3}

\displaystyle:=\{\vartheta\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K):\text{$\vartheta$ embedding}\}.

We denote by
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
the terminal object in each category
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
if it exists.
Although
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
always exists by
[

it is not true that
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
or
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
exist, or that they are the same if they exist.
The example at the beginning of the introduction gives a case where
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
and

do not exist.
Blecher–Kirkpatrick–Neal–Werner show in
[

does exist for

Under the automatic embedding property, in Corollary

when

is completely approximately 1-generated.
Moreover, we obtain the following result regarding hyperrigidity.

Theorem E.
(Proposition

, Corollary

)

Let

Then
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
by a canonical

(i)

(ii)

The hyperrigidity property of a generating set in a C*-algebra was introduced by Arveson
[
1
]
where it was also shown that it implies minimality of the C*-cover when the generating set is a unital operator system.
Salomon
[

]
introduced the rigidity at zero which now has become a central element in the theory.
Since
[
1
]
there has been a growing interest in researching the property in specific classes (perhaps too many for an inclusive list here).
This line of research has regained interest with the refutation of Arveson’s Hyperrigidity Conjecture for unital noncommutative operator algebras by Bilich–Dor-On
[

In a very recent preprint Clouâtre

1.4.
Contents

The structure of the manuscript is as follows.
In Section
2
we gather the preliminaries on selfadjoint operator spaces, their unitisations, and their C*-extensions.

In Section
3
we give the complete characterisation for a completely isometric complete order embedding between selfadjoint operator spaces to be an embedding.
En route we give another proof of Werner’s result
[
26
]
that the C*-algebraic unitisation coincides as an operator system with the unitisation as a selfadjoint operator space.
We show that for a selfadjoint operator space endowed with the maximal gauge, Werner’s unitisation is isomorphic to Russell’s unitisation.

In Section
4
we focus on approximately positively generated spaces.
We show that if the generated C*-algebra is unital, then we can extend completely bounded completely positive maps to completely bounded completely positive maps.
This is not equivalent to the inclusion being an embedding as exhibited in Example
4.21
.
We show that completely approximately 1-generated selfadjoint operator spaces admit an automatic embedding property.

In Section
5
we focus on singly generated selfadjoint operator spaces.
We show that extensions of completely positive maps always exist, but not always with the same cb-norm.
We give equivalent conditions for an inclusion
\operatorname{span}\{x\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
with
x\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)_{sa}
to be an embedding.

In Section
6
we give some applications of the characterisation of embeddings in terms of co-universal objects and hyperrigidity.

2.
Preliminaries

We present some elements of the theory of selfadjoint operator spaces in the sense of
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
16
,
23
,
26
,
27
]
.
A
matrix ordered operator space

E
is a norm-closed operator space with a completely isometric involution and a family of norm-closed cones
C_{n}\subseteq M_{n}(E)_{sa}
such that:

(i)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the cone
C_{n}
is proper in
M_{n}(E)_{sa}
;

(ii)

for every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
axa^{*}\in C_{m}
for every
a\in M_{m,n}
and
x\in C_{n}
.

We will denote the positive cone of
M_{n}(E)
by
M_{n}(E)_{+}
.
A map
\phi\colon E\to F
between matrix ordered operator spaces
E
and
F
is called
positive
if it is selfadjoint, i.e., if
\phi^{*}=\phi
where
\phi^{*}(x):=\phi(x^{*})^{*}
for every
x\in E
, and
\phi(E_{+})\subseteq F_{+}
.
Note that
\phi
being selfadjoint does not follow from the positivity condition as the cones do not necessarily span the spaces.
We say that a map
\phi\colon E\to F
is
completely positive
if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the amplification

\phi^{(n)}\colon M_{n}(E)\to M_{n}(F);[x_{ij}]\mapsto[\phi(x_{ij})],

is positive.
For
E
and
F
matrix ordered operator spaces we write

\displaystyle{\rm CB}(E,F)

\displaystyle:=\{\phi\colon E\to F:\phi\text{ is completely bounded}\},

\displaystyle{\rm CCP}(E,F)

\displaystyle:=\{\phi\colon E\to F:\phi\text{ is completely contractive and completely positive}\}.

A map
\phi\colon E\to F
is a
complete order embedding
if it is completely positive and invertible onto its image with a completely positive inverse.

A matrix ordered operator space
E
is called
completely
\kappa
-normal
if there exists a
\kappa>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
we have

\|x\|\leq\kappa\max\{\|u\|,\|v\|\}\text{ whenever }u,v\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}\text{ satisfy }u\leq x\leq v.

In
[
26
, Remark 4.14]
it is stated that a matrix ordered operator space
E
is completely
\kappa
-normal if and only if there exists a Hilbert space
H
and a complete order embedding
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
such that

\|\phi\colon E\to F\|_{\rm cb}\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\|\phi^{-1}\colon\phi(E)\to F\|_{\rm cb}\leq\kappa.

For
\kappa=1
this is shown in
[
23
, Corollary 4.1]
, and for general
\kappa>0
in
[
10
, Corollary 8.13]
.
Here we will restrict to completely
1
-normal matrix ordered operator spaces, and thus to the concrete case; that is, by a
selfadjoint operator space
we will mean a selfadjoint norm-closed subspace of some
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
with the induced matrix cones and matrix norms via the inclusion.

For a selfadjoint operator space
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
we write
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
for the C*-algebra it generates inside
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
We note that the same proof as in
[
21
, Proposition 3.8]
yields that contractive (positive) functionals of a selfadjoint operator space are automatically completely contractive (resp. completely positive).
Henceforth, we will not use the term completely when we refer to positivity or contractivity of functionals.

In
[
26
, Definition 4.7]
Werner introduces the following notion of unitisation for a matrix ordered operator space
E
.
Consider the direct sum linear space
E^{\#}:=E\oplus\mathbb{C}
with an involution given by
(x,a)^{*}:=(x^{*},\overline{a})
.
Declare a selfadjoint element
(x,a)\in M_{n}(E^{\#})
to be
positive
if

a\geq 0\text{ and }\varphi(a_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}xa_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2})\geq-1\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0\text{ and }\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C}),

where
a_{\varepsilon}:=a+\varepsilon I_{M_{n}}
.
We will use the following equivalent definition

(x,a)\in M_{n}(E^{\#})_{+}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;a\geq 0\;\;\textup{ and }\sup_{\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})}-\varphi(a_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}xa_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2})\leq 1\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0.

In
[
26
, Lemma 4.8]
Werner shows that
E^{\#}
is an operator system, and that
E
is a selfadjoint operator space if and only if the inclusion map

\iota_{E}\colon E\hookrightarrow E^{\#};x\mapsto(x,0),

is a completely isometric complete order embedding.
In what follows we will omit the explicit reference to the map
\iota_{E}
and identify
E
with its copy inside
E^{\#}
whenever
E
is a selfadjoint operator space.
In
[
26
, Lemma 4.9]
it is shown that whenever
E
and
F
are matrix ordered operator spaces and
\phi\colon E\to F
is a completely contractive completely positive map, then the natural unitisation

\phi^{\#}\colon E^{\#}\to F^{\#};(x,a)\mapsto(\phi(x),a),

is unital and completely positive.
If in addition
\phi
is a completely isometric complete order isomorphism, then
\phi^{\#}
is a unital complete order isomorphism
[
7
, Corollary 2.14]
.

However this does not hold when surjectivity is dropped.
Following
[
16
, Section 2]
, a map
\vartheta\colon E\to F
is called an
embedding
if it is a completely isometric complete order embedding, and the unitisation map
\vartheta^{\#}
is a unital complete order embedding of operator systems; equivalently
\vartheta^{\#}
is completely isometric.
In
[
26
, Corollary 4.9]
Werner shows that the unitisation construction recovers the known unitisation when restricted to C*-algebras.
We will give an alternative proof to this fact in Corollary
3.6
through our characterisation of embeddings in Theorem

.

Injective
*
-homomorphisms between C*-algebras are embeddings, as the unitisation of an injective
*
-homomorphism is injective, and thus completely isometric.
Hence for
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
the inclusion map is an embedding if and only if the inclusion map
E\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
is an embedding for any C*-algebra
E\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
In
[
16
, Example 2.14]
and
[
23
, Example 4.1]
, it is shown that even such inclusions are not automatically embeddings; see also
[
9
, Example 2.4]
.
By seeing
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
as the (1,1) corner of
{\mathcal{B}}(H\oplus\mathbb{C})
, then
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)^{\#}
is
*
-isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)+\mathbb{C}I_{H\oplus\mathbb{C}}
.
Hence
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is an embedding if and only if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(x,a)\in M_{n}(E^{\#})
we have

\|(x,a)\|_{\#}^{(n)}=\|x+aI_{H\oplus\mathbb{C}}\|,

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\#}^{(n)}
is the norm induced by the matrix order unit
(0,1)
in
E^{\#}
.
Note that “
\geq
” always holds by the construction of
E^{\#}
.

We have the following proposition about the concrete realisation of the unitisation of a selfadjoint operator space that embeds in the C*-algebra it generates.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be an embedding of a selfadjoint operator space. Then the following hold:

(i)

If
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is not unital, or if it is unital and
1_{\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)}\neq I_{H}
, then
E^{\#}\cong E+\mathbb{C}I_{H}
.

(ii)

If
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is unital and
1_{\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)}=I_{H}
, then
E^{\#}\cong E+\mathbb{C}I_{H\oplus\mathbb{C}}
with the canonical identification
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H\oplus\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.
.

Recall that, if
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a C*-algebra, then we have the following cases for identifying
{\mathcal{C}}^{\#}
.
If
{\mathcal{C}}
is not unital, or if
{\mathcal{C}}
is unital and
1_{{\mathcal{C}}}\neq I_{H}
, then
{\mathcal{C}}^{\#}\cong{\mathcal{C}}+\mathbb{C}I_{H}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
by the map

\Phi\colon{\mathcal{C}}^{\#}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H);(x,a)\mapsto x+aI_{H}.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is unital and
1_{\mathcal{C}}=I_{H}
, then
{\mathcal{C}}^{\#}\cong{\mathcal{C}}\oplus\mathbb{C}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H\oplus\mathbb{C})
by the map

\Phi\colon{\mathcal{C}}^{\#}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H\oplus\mathbb{C});(x,a)\mapsto(x+aI_{H},a).

Now the proof follows since the embedding property allows to write
E^{\#}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)^{\#}
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Let
E
be a selfadjoint operator space. An adaptation of the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 2.25 (i)]
yields that if
\psi\colon E^{\#}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a unital complete order embedding, then
\psi|_{E}
is an embedding.

The notion of embeddings is sufficient for an extremal theory.
In
[
7
, Theorem 2.25]
Connes and van Suijlekom prove that there exists a (necessarily unique up to
*
-isomorphisms) C*-envelope in the category of selfadjoint operator spaces with respect to embeddings, rather than just completely isometric complete order embeddings; see also
[
27
, Proposition 3.5]
and
[
19
, Proposition and Definition 5.11]
.
Let us fix notation.

A
C*-cover
of a selfadjoint operator space
E
is a pair
({\mathcal{C}},\vartheta)
where
{\mathcal{C}}
is a C*-algebra and
\vartheta\colon E\to{\mathcal{C}}
is an embedding such that
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\vartheta(E))={\mathcal{C}}
.
In
[
7
, Theorem 2.25]
it is shown that any such
E
admits a unique minimal C*-cover
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)
, which is called
the C*-envelope of
E
.
In particular, it is shown that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)
is the C*-algebra generated by
E
inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E^{\#})
.
In
[
7
, Theorem 2.25]
it is shown that, if
E
is an operator system, then the C*-envelope of
E
as a selfadjoint operator space coincides with the C*-envelope of
E
as an operator system.

On the other hand the notion of just completely isometric complete order embeddings is not (in general) sufficient for an extremal theory, see
[
7
, Remark 2.27]
.
Below we apply
[
7
, Remark 2.27]
on a specific example that we will be carrying forwards.

Example 2.3
.

[
7
, Remark 2.27]

For
r\in(0,1]
, consider

x_{r}:=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-r\end{bmatrix}.

Set
E_{r}:=\operatorname{span}\{x_{r}\}=\operatorname{span}\{-x_{r}\}
.
In
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E_{r})\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}
, we have

\sigma_{\mathrm{C}^{*}(E_{r})}(x_{r})=\{1,-r\}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{\mathrm{C}^{*}(E_{r})}(-x_{r})=\{-1,r\}.

By construction, the map

\phi\colon E_{r}\to E_{r};\phi(x_{r})=-x_{r},

is completely isometric and a complete order isomorphism since the positive cones are trivial.

Suppose that the category of selfadjoint operator spaces with respect to completely isometric complete order embeddings admits a C*-envelope which we denote by
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Then by the defining property of the C*-envelope, there are
*
-epimorphisms

\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E_{r})\to{\mathcal{C}}\textup{ such that }\pi_{1}(x_{r})=\pi_{2}(-x_{r}).

Hence we get

\sigma_{{\mathcal{C}}}(\pi_{1}(x_{r}))=\sigma_{{\mathcal{C}}}(\pi_{2}(x_{r}))\subseteq\{1,-r\}\cap\{-1,r\}.

By choosing
r\neq 1
, we arrive to the contradiction of an empty spectrum of an element.

Note that, for any
r\in(0,1)
the map

\psi\colon E_{r}\to E_{1};\psi(x_{r})=x_{1},

is a completely isometric complete order isomorphism, and thus
E_{1}
does not admit a C*-envelope with respect to completely isometric complete order embeddings, as well.

We will also need Arveson’s correspondence for selfadjoint operator spaces.
In the following remark we recall that complete boundedness of the maps is preserved even in the non-unital setting.

Remark 2.4
.

Arveson’s correspondence (see for example
[
21
, Proposition 13.2]
) between positive functionals
s\colon M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
and completely positive maps
\phi\colon E\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is given by

s_{\phi}\colon M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C};s_{\phi}((x_{ij}))=\sum_{i,j}\left\langle\phi(x_{ij})e_{j},e_{i}\right\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{s}\colon E\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C});\phi_{s}(x)=(s(x\otimes E_{ij})),

where
(e_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is the canonical orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
(E_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
is the family of matrix units in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.
By a well-known result of Smith
[
25
]
we have
\|\phi_{s}^{(n)}\|=\|\phi_{s}\|_{\rm cb}
and
\|\phi_{s}^{(n)}\|\leq n\|\phi_{s}\|
, (see also
[
21
, Exercise 3.10 and Proposition 8.11]
).
Hence

\|\phi_{s}\|_{\rm cb}\leq n^{3}\|s\|\quad\text{and}\quad\|s_{\phi}\|\leq n\|\phi\|_{\rm cb}.

Note that Smith’s result also implies that bounded linear maps taking values in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
are automatically completely bounded.

To fix notation, for a selfadjoint element
x
in a C*-algebra
{\mathcal{C}}
we write
x=x_{+}-x_{-}
for the (unique) decomposition such that
x_{+},x_{-}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{+}
and
x_{+}x_{-}=0
.
The following proposition appears also in
[

, Examples 3.3 and 4.3]
and in
[

We provide an alternative proof.

Proposition 2.5
.

[

, Examples 3.3 and 4.3]

[

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be a C*-algebra and
x\in{\mathcal{C}}_{sa}
.
Then

\operatorname{dist}(x,-{\mathcal{C}}_{+})=\|x_{+}\|\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{dist}(x,{\mathcal{C}}_{+})=\|x_{-}\|.

Proof.
.

It suffices to show the first equality; the second equality then follows by applying for

It is evident that

On the other hand, by an application of

3.
Embeddings

In this section we give a characterisation for a completely isometric complete order embedding between selfadjoint operator spaces to be an embedding.
Our first goal is to provide a formula that relates the contractive positive functionals of all levels with the distance to the cones of selfadjoint operator spaces.
We will need the following proposition which can be deduced from
[
10
, Corollary 4.14]
; we give a direct proof that fits in our context.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be a selfadjoint operator space. If
x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathrm{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})\leq 1
.

(ii)

\varphi(x)\geq-1
for every
\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.
.

[(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)].
Assume that
\mathrm{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})\leq 1
and let
\varphi\colon M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
be a contractive positive functional.
We will prove that
\varphi(x)\geq-1
.
Towards this end, fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists a
p\in M_{n}(E)_{+}
such that
\|x-p\|<1+\varepsilon
. We have

-\varphi(x-p)\leq|\varphi(x-p)|\leq\|x-p\|<1+\varepsilon,

and thus we obtain

\varphi(x)=\varphi(p)+\varphi(x-p)\geq\varphi(x-p)>-(1+\varepsilon).

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary we conclude that
\varphi(x)\geq-1
, as required.

[(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)].
Assume that
\varphi(x)\geq-1
for every contractive positive functional
\varphi\colon M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
.
We will prove that
\mathrm{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})\leq 1
.
Set

{\mathfrak{C}}:=\{(x,t)\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}\oplus\mathbb{R}:\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})\leq t\},

and note that
{\mathfrak{C}}
is a closed convex cone in
M_{n}(E)\oplus\mathbb{C}
.
We assume towards a contradiction that
(x,1)\notin{\mathfrak{C}}
.
Without loss of generality by the Hahn–Banach Separation Theorem, we may pick a selfadjoint functional
f\colon M_{n}(E)\oplus\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle f(x,1)<0\leq\inf_{(y,t)\in{\mathfrak{C}}}f(y,t).

Then
f
decomposes as
f(y,t)=\psi(y)+t\mu
for the selfadjoint map
\psi:=f|_{M_{n}(E)}
and
\mu:=f(0,1)\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then
\psi
is a positive linear functional on
M_{n}(E)
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Indeed, for
p\in M_{n}(E)_{+}
we have
(p,0)\in{\mathfrak{C}}
, and so
0\leq f(p,0)=\psi(p)
.
Similarly, we have
(0,1)\in{\mathfrak{C}}
and hence
0\leq f(0,1)=\mu
.

We now prove that
\|\psi\|\leq\mu
.
Since
\psi
is a selfadjoint functional it suffices to show that
|\psi(y)|\leq\mu\|y\|
for every
y\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
(see for example
[
18
, page 92]
).
We have

\operatorname{dist}(y,M_{n}(E)_{+})\leq\|y\|\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{dist}(-y,M_{n}(E)_{+})\leq\|y\|,

and hence
(-y,\|y\|)
and
(y,\|y\|)
are elements in
{\mathfrak{C}}
.
It follows that

\psi(-y)+\mu\|y\|=f(-y,\|y\|)\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\psi(y)+\mu\|y\|=f(y,\|y\|)\geq 0,

and thus

\ -\mu\|y\|\leq\psi(y)\leq\mu\|y\|,

which proves the claim.

Finally, note that
\mu>0
, and hence for
\varphi:=\psi/\mu
in
{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})
we get

\mu(\varphi(x)+1)=\psi(x)+\mu=f(x,1)<0.

We derive
\varphi(x)<-1
, and thus a contradiction.
∎

We now obtain the desired distance formula.
We note that the equivalence [(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)] of
[
10
, Theorem 8.3]
can follow from this.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be a selfadjoint operator space.
If
x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
, then we have

\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})=\sup_{\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})}-\varphi(x).

Proof.
.

It follows by Proposition
3.1
and a normalisation argument.
∎

We will connect embeddings to the notion of maximal gauges introduced by Russell
[
23
]
.
Let us recall here the terminology.
Let
\vartheta\colon E\to F
be a completely isometric complete order embedding between selfadjoint operator spaces.
Then the map
\vartheta
is called a
gauge maximal isometry
if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})=\operatorname{dist}(\vartheta^{(n)}(x),M_{n}(F)_{+})\text{ for all }x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}.

Following
[
23
, Definition 4.2]
, whenever
E
and
F
are gauge maximal isometric via the inclusion map
E\subseteq F
we will just say that we have a
gauge maximal inclusion
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\vartheta\colon E\to F
be a completely isometric complete order embedding between selfadjoint operator spaces.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The map
\vartheta
is an embedding.

(ii)

Every map
\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})
extends to a map
\widetilde{\varphi}\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(F),\mathbb{C})
with
\widetilde{\varphi}\circ\vartheta=\varphi
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(iii)

The map
\vartheta
is a gauge maximal isometry.

Proof.
.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
E\subseteq F
.

[(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)].
This equivalence is shown in
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
.

[(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)].
The assumption gives that, for every
x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
we have

\sup_{\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(F),\mathbb{C})}-\varphi(x)=\sup_{\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})}-\varphi(x),

and hence Theorem
3.2
yields

\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(F)_{+})=\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+}).

[(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i)].
Consider
(x,a)\in M_{n}(E^{\#})_{sa}
such that
(x,a)\in M_{n}(F^{\#})_{+}
.
Then
a\geq 0
and

\sup_{\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(F),\mathbb{C})}-\varphi(a_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}xa_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2})\leq 1\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0.

Since
E\subseteq F
is a gauge maximal inclusion, for every
\varepsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})}-\varphi(a_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}xa_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dist}(a_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}xa_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2},M_{n}(E)_{+})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dist}(a_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}xa_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2},M_{n}(F)_{+})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(F),\mathbb{C})}-\varphi(a_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}xa_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2})\leq 1,

where we used Theorem
3.2
in the first and third equalities.
Hence
(x,a)\in M_{n}(E^{\#})_{+}
, as required.
∎

We thus obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
E
be a selfadjoint operator space in a C*-algebra
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Then the inclusion
E\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
is an embedding if and only if for every
x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
we have

\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})=\max_{\lambda\in\sigma_{M_{n}({\mathcal{C}})}(x)}-\lambda.

Proof.
.

This follows from Proposition
2.5
and the equivalence [(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)] of Theorem
3.3
.
∎

As a further corollary we obtain
[
23
, Theorem 4.4 and Theorem 4.5]
.

Corollary 3.5
.

[

, Theorem 4.4 and Theorem 4.5]

The following hold:

(i)

If
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
are operator systems with

, then

is a gauge maximal inclusion.

(ii)

.

Corollary 3.6

Let

be a C*-algebra.
Then the operator system unitisation of

is unital completely order isomorphic to the C*-algebraic unitisation of

Proof.
.

For convenience, let us denote the C*-algebraic unitisation of a C*-algebra

\widetilde{{\mathcal{C}}}

By Corollary

the inclusion
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\widetilde{{\mathcal{C}}}

\Phi\colon{\mathcal{C}}^{\#}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{C}}}

for

Since

(c,a)\in M_{n}({\mathcal{C}}^{\#})_{+}
.
∎

k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(iii)

Every map
\phi\in{\rm CCP}(E,{\mathcal{B}}(K))
extends to a map
\widetilde{\phi}\in{\rm CCP}(F,{\mathcal{B}}(K))
with
\widetilde{\phi}\circ\vartheta=\phi
, for every Hilbert space
K
.

Proof.
.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
E\subseteq F
.

[(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)].
By
[
26
, Lemma 4.6]
, every completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon E\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
extends to a unital completely positive map
\phi^{\#}\colon E^{\#}\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
.
Since
E^{\#}\subseteq F^{\#}
is a unital inclusion of operator systems, by Arveson’s Extension Theorem, the map
\phi^{\#}
extends to a unital completely positive map
\widetilde{\phi^{\#}}\colon F^{\#}\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
which in turn restricts to a completely contractive completely positive map
\widetilde{\phi}\colon F\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
that extends
\phi
.

[(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)].
Consider a completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
.
We write
(\phi_{{\mathcal{F}}})_{{\mathcal{F}}}
for the net of compressions of
\phi
into the finite dimensional subspaces of
K
indexed by inclusion, i.e.,
\phi_{{\mathcal{F}}}(x)=P_{{\mathcal{F}}}\phi(x)P_{{\mathcal{F}}}
for the projection
P_{{\mathcal{F}}}
onto
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq K
.
Note that
\phi_{{\mathcal{F}}}
acts on
{\mathcal{B}}(P_{{\mathcal{F}}}K)\cong M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
where
k=\operatorname{rank}P_{{\mathcal{F}}}
, and it is a completely contractive completely positive map.
By assumption, it extends to a completely contractive completely positive map

\widetilde{\phi}_{{\mathcal{F}}}\colon F\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(K).

Since the set
{\rm CCP}(F,{\mathcal{B}}(K))

is compact in the BW-topology (see
[
21
, Theorem 7.4]
), the net
(\widetilde{\phi}_{{\mathcal{F}}})_{{\mathcal{F}}}\subseteq{\rm CCP}(F,{\mathcal{B}}(K))
has a cluster point
\widetilde{\phi}
, and take a subnet converging to it.
By definition
\widetilde{\phi}
is a completely contractive completely positive extension of
\phi
on
F
.

[(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i)].
The proof of this implication is essentially the “only if” part of
[
23
, Theorem 4.3]
.
We include a proof that fits in our setting.
By Theorem
3.3
, and since
M_{n}(E)_{+}\subseteq M_{n}(F)_{+}
, we only need to prove that

\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(F)_{+}).

Let
j\colon E^{\#}\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be a unital complete order embedding.
By Remark
2.2
the map

\phi:=j|_{E}\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)

is an embedding and hence it is a gauge maximal isometry by Theorem
3.3
.
By assumption, the map
\phi
extends to a completely contractive completely positive map
\widetilde{\phi}\colon F\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
For

we have
\widetilde{\phi}^{(n)}(p)\in M_{n}({\mathcal{B}}(H))_{+}

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})=\operatorname{dist}(\phi^{(n)}(x),M_{n}({\mathcal{B}}(H))_{+})\leq\|\widetilde{\phi}^{(n)}(x)-\widetilde{\phi}^{(n)}(p)\|\leq\|x-p\|.

Taking the infimum over all

∎

Example 3.9
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
denote the unit circle in the complex plane and let
{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
denote the C*-algebra of continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}
. Define the selfadjoint operator space

E:=\{f\in{\rm C}(\mathbb{T}):f(z)=\alpha z+\beta\bar{z},\;\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}\},

and note that
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)={\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
.
Then the inclusion
E\subseteq{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
is an embedding.

By Theorem
3.8
it suffices to show that any completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
admits a completely contractive completely positive extension
\widetilde{\phi}\colon{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
.
We begin by showing that the positive cone
E_{+}
is trivial.
Indeed, any
f\in E_{+}
satisfies
\alpha=\bar{\beta}
and thus
f(z)=2{\rm Re}(\alpha z)
for every
z\in\mathbb{T}
. By writing
\alpha=re^{i\theta}
with
r\geq 0
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, we have
0\leq f(-e^{-i\theta})=2{\rm{Re}}(\alpha(-e^{-i\theta}))=-2r
.
This yields
\alpha=0
and thus
f=0
.

Therefore every completely contractive complete order embedding
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
is uniquely determined by the contraction
T:=\phi(\widehat{z})
, for the coordinate function
\widehat{z}
in
{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
.
By Sz.-Nagy’s dilation theorem, let
U\in{\mathcal{B}}(L)
be a unitary dilation of
T
for
L\supseteq K
.
By the universal property of the full group C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})\cong{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
we obtain a unital
*
-homomorphism

\Phi\colon{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})\to{\mathcal{B}}(L);\Phi(\widehat{z}):=U,

and the unital (completely contractive) completely positive map
P_{K}\Phi|_{K}
is by definition an extension of
\phi
on
{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
.
Thus by Theorem
3.8
the inclusion
E\subseteq{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
is an embedding.

More generally, let
\mathbb{F}_{n}
denote the free group on
n
generators, and let
E_{n}
be the selfadjoint operator space spanned by the unitary generators of the full group C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
, i.e.,
E_{n}:=\operatorname{span}\{u_{i},u^{*}_{j}:i,j=1,\dots,n\}
.
Then the inclusion
E_{n}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
is an embedding.

By Theorem
3.8
it suffices to show that any completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon E_{n}\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
admits a completely contractive completely positive extension on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
.
For
i=1,\dots,n
consider the contractions
v_{i}:=\phi(u_{i})\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)
.
Consider also the unitary dilations

\widehat{v_{i}}:=\begin{bmatrix}v_{i}&(1-v_{i}v_{i}^{*})^{1/2}\\
(1-v_{i}^{*}v_{i})^{1/2}&-v_{i}^{*}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathcal{B}}(K^{(2)}).

By the universal property of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
, we obtain a unital
*
-homomorphism

\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})\to{\mathcal{B}}(K^{(2)});\Phi(u_{i})=\widehat{v_{i}}.

Then the compression
P_{K}\Phi|_{K}
is a unital (completely contractive) completely positive extension of
\phi
on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
, as required.

We end this section with the connection between Werner’s unitisation of matrix ordered operator spaces and Russell’s unitisation of matrix gauge
*
-vector spaces.
Following
[
23
, Definition 3.1]
, a
proper matrix gauge
on a
*
-vector space
V
is a sequence of functions

\nu_{n}\colon M_{n}(V)_{sa}\to[0,\infty),n\in\mathbb{N},

that satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

If
x,y\in M_{n}(V)_{sa}
, then
\nu_{n}(x+y)\leq\nu_{n}(x)+\nu_{n}(y)
.

(ii)

If
x\in M_{n}(V)_{sa}
and
a\in M_{n,k}
, then
\nu_{k}(a^{*}xa)\leq\|a\|^{2}\nu_{n}(x)
.

(iii)

If
x\in M_{n}(V)_{sa}
and
y\in M_{m}(V)_{sa}
, then
\nu_{n+m}(x\oplus y)=\max\{\nu_{n}(x),\nu_{m}(y)\}
.

(iv)

If
x\in M_{n}(V)_{sa}
and
\nu_{n}(x)=\nu_{n}(-x)=0
, then
x=0
.

The pair
(V,\nu)
is called a
matrix gauge
*
-vector space
.
In
[
23
, Corollary 4.1]
it is shown that any matrix gauge
*
-vector space
(V,\nu)
is a selfadjoint operator space in our sense, with the positive cones defined by

C_{n}:=\{x\in M_{n}(V)_{sa}:\nu_{n}(-x)=0\}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

and the norms defined by

\|x\|_{n}:=\max\left\{\nu_{2n}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right),\nu_{2n}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&-x\\
-x^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)\right\}\text{ for all }x\in M_{n}(V)\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, in
[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
it is proven that if
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is a selfadjoint operator space, then the
maximal gauge
defined by

\nu_{n}^{\max}(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,-M_{n}(E)_{+})\text{ for every }x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N},

defines a proper matrix gauge on
E
, and the induced matrix cones and norms from this gauge coincide with the matrix cones and norms inherited from
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
A map
\phi\colon(V,\nu)\to(W,\omega)
between matrix gauge
*
-vector spaces is called
completely gauge contractive
(resp.
isometric
) if
\omega_{n}(\phi^{(n)}(x))\leq\nu_{n}(x)
(resp.
\omega_{n}(\phi^{(n)}(x))=\nu_{n}(x)
) for every
x\in M_{n}(V)_{sa}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

For a matrix gauge
*
-vector space
(V,\nu)
Russell
[
23
, Definition 3.4]
defines a proper matrix gauge
\widehat{\nu}
on the
*
-vector space
\widehat{V}:=V\oplus\mathbb{C}
by setting

\nu_{n}(x,a):=\inf\{t>0:0\leq tI_{n}-a\in{\rm Inv}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\text{ and }\nu_{n}((tI_{n}-a)^{-1/2}x(tI_{n}-a)^{-1/2}))\leq 1\},

for every
(x,a)\in M_{n}(\widehat{V})_{sa}
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In
[
23
, Theorem 3.1]
it is shown that the unitisation
(\widehat{V},\widehat{\nu})
is an operator system and that the inclusion map

V\hookrightarrow\widehat{V};x\mapsto(x,0),

is completely gauge isometric.
Moreover, in
[
23
, Corollary 3.2]
it is proven that if
{\mathcal{S}}
is an operator system with unit
e
and
\phi\colon V\to{\mathcal{S}}
is completely gauge contractive with respect to the maximal gauge of
{\mathcal{S}}
, then the map

\widehat{\phi}\colon\widehat{V}\to{\mathcal{S}};(x,a)\mapsto\phi(x)+ae,

is unital completely positive.
The following corollary shows the canonical connection between Werner’s unitisation and Russell’s unitisation of
E
when endowed with the maximal gauge.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
E
be a selfadjoint operator space and let
(\widehat{E},\widehat{\nu}^{\max})
be Russell’s unitisation of the maximal gauge defined on
E
.
Then
E^{\#}
and
\widehat{E}
are isomorphic operator systems.

Proof.
.

The inclusion map
E\hookrightarrow\widehat{E}
is completely gauge isometric by construction of
(\widehat{E},\widehat{\nu}^{\max})
, and hence by
[
23
, Proposition 3.3]
it is completely contractive completely positive. We thus obtain a unital completely positive map

\Phi\colon E^{\#}\to\widehat{E};(x,a)\mapsto(x,a).

Conversely, the inclusion map
E\hookrightarrow E^{\#}
is completely gauge isometric with respect to the maximal gauges of
E
and
E^{\#}
. Indeed, by Theorem
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E)_{+})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})}-\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E^{\#}),\mathbb{C})}-\varphi(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,M_{n}(E^{\#})_{+}),

for all
x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence,
[
23
, Corollary 3.2]
implies that the map

\Psi\colon\widehat{E}\to E^{\#};(x,a)\mapsto(x,a),

is unital completely positive.
Since
\Phi
and
\Psi
are mutual inverses, the proof is complete.
∎

4.
Positive generation

In this section we investigate the embedding problem for selfadjoint operator spaces that are generated by their cones (at every matrix level).
Recall that a selfadjoint operator space
E
is called
approximately positively generated
if

\overline{E_{+}-E_{+}}=E_{sa}.

We will relate this property to rigidity of the zero representation, a notion introduced by Salomon
[
24
, Definition 3.1]
in the broader context of generating sets.
Let
{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{G}})
be a generating set.
The set
{\mathcal{G}}
is said to be
rigid at zero
if for any sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n}\subseteq{\rm CCP}(\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{G}}),\mathbb{C})
we have:

\lim_{n}\varphi_{n}(g)=0\text{ for all }g\in{\mathcal{G}}\;\Longrightarrow\;\lim_{n}\varphi_{n}(c)=0\text{ for all }c\in\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{G}}).

In
[
24
, Theorem 3.3]
it is shown that
{\mathcal{G}}
is rigid at zero if and only if it is
separating
for
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{G}})
, i.e., if for any
\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{G}}),\mathbb{C})
we have

\varphi(g)=0\text{ for all }g\in{\mathcal{G}}\;\Longrightarrow\;\varphi(c)=0\text{ for all }c\in\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{G}}).

Let now
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be a selfadjoint operator space.
A map
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
is called
maximal
if
\phi
is a direct summand of any completely contractive completely positive dilation.
A map
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
is said to have
the unique extension property
if it has a unique completely contractive completely positive extension
\widetilde{\phi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
that is a
*
-representation.

As shown in
[
24
]
, the condition of rigidity at zero links with the study of hyperrigidity of non-unital generating sets, see also
[
9
]
.
Moreover, in
[
24
, Theorem 3.3]
it is proved that it is equivalent to the zero representation having the unique extension property.
Combining the results of
[
12
,
17
,
24
]
we obtain the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
12
, Proposition 8.2]
,
[
17
, Theorem A]
,
[
24
, Theorem 3.3]

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be a selfadjoint operator space and let
\delta_{0}\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
denote the zero functional.
Consider the following statements:

(i)

E
is approximately positively generated.

(ii)

E_{+}
separates
{\rm CCP}(E,\mathbb{C})
.

(iii)

\delta_{0}
is maximal.

(iv)

\delta_{0}
satisfies the unique extension property.

(v)

E
is rigid at zero.

(vi)

E
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.

Then the following implications hold:

[\textup{(i) $\Leftrightarrow$ (ii) $\Leftrightarrow$ (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (iv) $\Leftrightarrow$ (v) $\Leftrightarrow$ (vi)}].

If in addition the inclusion
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding, then all items above are equivalent.

Proof.
.

Equivalence [(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)] is
[
12
, Proposition 8.2]
.
Equivalence [(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)] is
[
17
, Theorem A]
.
Equivalences [(iv)
\Leftrightarrow
(v)
\Leftrightarrow
(vi)] is
[
24
, Theorem 3.3]
.
The implication [(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv)] and the conditional implication [(iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii)] follow by standard arguments.

In more detail, suppose that item (iii) holds.
First note that
\delta_{0}
has an extension to the zero representation on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
Let
\widetilde{\delta}_{0}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
be another contractive positive extension of
\delta_{0}
.
Then passing to the unitisation and applying Stinespring’s Theorem (see for example
[
9
, Proposition 2.7]
) we can find a
*
-representation
\pi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
that dilates
\widetilde{\delta}_{0}
.
By maximality we have that
\delta_{0}
is a direct summand of
\pi|_{E}
.
Since
\pi
is a
*
-representation we see that its (1,1) corner
\widetilde{\delta}_{0}
is zero on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
, showing that
\delta_{0}
has the unique extension property.

Finally assume that
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding.
We will show that in this case item (iv) implies item (iii).
Let
\psi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
be a completely contractive completely positive dilation of
\delta_{0}
.
As the inclusion
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is assumed to be an embedding, by Theorem
3.8
there is a completely contractive completely positive extension
\widetilde{\psi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
of
\psi
.
Since
P_{\mathbb{C}}\widetilde{\psi}|_{\mathbb{C}}
is a contractive positive extension of
\delta_{0}
, by the unique extension property we get
P_{\mathbb{C}}\widetilde{\psi}|_{\mathbb{C}}=0
.
Since
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is generated by its positive elements we have then that the off-diagonal corners of
\widetilde{\psi}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E))
are zero, and hence so is for
\psi(E)
.
Thus
\delta_{0}
is a direct summand of
\psi
as required.
∎

We next show that separation at the first level is separation at every level.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be selfadjoint operator space. The following are equivalent:

(i)

E
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.

(ii)

M_{n}(E)
is separating for
M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E))
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.
.

The implication [(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)] is trivial.
For [(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)], let
\varphi\colon M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E))\to\mathbb{C}
be a contractive positive functional such that
\varphi|_{M_{n}(E)}=0
.
As in
[
26
, Lemma 2.7]
there exist
\varphi_{ij}\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\varphi_{ii}
are in
{\rm CCP}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E),\mathbb{C})
and

\varphi(c)=\sum_{i,j}\varphi_{ij}(c_{ij})\text{ for }c=(c_{ij})\in M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)).

Since
\varphi|_{M_{n}(E)}=0
we have
\varphi_{ii}|_{E}=0
and hence
\varphi_{ii}=0
for every
i=1,\dots,n
.
Let
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
be an approximate unit for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
, and thus
(e_{\lambda}\otimes I_{n})_{\lambda}
is an approximate unit for
M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E))
.
Since
\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)),\mathbb{C})
we obtain

\|\varphi\|=\lim_{\lambda}\varphi(e_{\lambda}\otimes I_{n})=\lim_{\lambda}\sum_{i}\varphi_{ii}(e_{\lambda})=0,

and thus
M_{n}(E)
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(M_{n}(E))
, as required.
∎

In
[
12
, Proposition 8.4]
it is shown that if
E
is
positively generated
, i.e.,
E_{sa}=E_{+}-E_{+}
, then
M_{n}(E)
is positively generated for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Using Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
we can show that the same result holds for approximate positive generation.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
E
be a selfadjoint operator space.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

E
is approximately positively generated.

(ii)

M_{n}(E)
is approximately positively generated for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.
.

The implication [(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)] is trivial.
For [(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)], we may consider an inclusion
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
which is an embedding, use for example Remark
2.2
.
Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have that the inclusion
M_{n}(E)\subseteq M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E))
is an embedding as well.
By Proposition
4.2
we have that
M_{n}(E)
is separating for
M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E))
, and hence Proposition
4.1
yields that
M_{n}(E)
is approximately positively generated.
∎

We will prove that approximate positive generation allows extensions of completely positive maps when the generated C*-algebra is unital.
We will first need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be a selfadjoint operator space.
If
E
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is unital, then the set

(4.1)

{\mathfrak{K}}:=\big\{\phi\in{\rm CB}(E,{\mathcal{B}}(K)):\exists\,\widetilde{\phi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)\text{ completely positive such that }\widetilde{\phi}|_{E}=\phi\big\}

of completely bounded completely positive extendable maps from
E
to
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is closed under the point-weak* topology.

Proof.
.

Since
{\mathfrak{K}}
is a convex cone, by the Krein–Šmulian Theorem it suffices to show that

{\mathfrak{K}}\cap\{\phi\in{\rm CB}(E,{\mathcal{B}}(K)):\|\phi\|_{\rm cb}\leq 1\}

is point-weak* closed.
Let
(\phi_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq{\mathfrak{K}}
be a net in
{\mathfrak{K}}
such that
\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{\rm cb}\leq 1
for all
\lambda
and
\textup{pw*-}\lim\phi_{\lambda}=\phi
for some completely bounded and completely positive map
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
.
For each
\lambda
let
\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
be a completely positive extension of
\phi_{\lambda}
.
We will prove that
(\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
has a uniformly bounded subnet.
By compactness of bounded sets in the point-weak* topology, this will yield a cluster point
\widetilde{\phi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
which will be a completely positive extension of
\phi
, and the proof will be complete.

We assume towards a contradiction that the net
(\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\|_{\rm cb})_{\lambda}
does not have a uniformly bounded subnet.
That is, for every
M>0
and every
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
there exists
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
such that
\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\|_{\rm cb}>M
.
By taking a subnet if necessary we may assume that
\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\|_{\rm cb}\to\infty
.
Now for each
\lambda
, pick a state
\omega_{\lambda}\colon{\mathcal{B}}(K)\to\mathbb{C}
with
\omega_{\lambda}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}(1))=\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}(1)\|=\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\|_{\rm cb}
,
and set

f_{\lambda}:=\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\|_{\rm cb}^{-1}\cdot(\omega_{\lambda}\circ\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}).

Each
f_{\lambda}
is a state on the unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
, and hence by weak* compactness, there exists a state
f
of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
that is a cluster point for
(f_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
.
For
x\in E
we have

\displaystyle|f_{\lambda}(x)|=\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\|_{\rm cb}^{-1}\cdot|\omega_{\lambda}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}(x))|=\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\|_{\rm cb}^{-1}\cdot|\omega_{\lambda}(\phi_{\lambda}(x))|\leq\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}\|_{\rm cb}^{-1}\cdot\|x\|\xrightarrow{\lambda}0

since
\|\omega_{\lambda}\|=1
for all
\lambda
and
\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{\rm cb}\leq 1
.
We thus obtain that
f|_{E}=0
, and since
E
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
, we derive to the contradiction that
f=0
.
∎

We now prove the extension theorem in this class.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be an approximately positively generated selfadjoint operator space such that
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is unital.
Then every completely bounded completely positive map
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
extends to a (completely bounded) completely positive map
\widetilde{\phi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
.

Proof.
.

First let
\varphi\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
be a bounded positive functional.
By Lemma
4.4
, the set

{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathbb{C}}:=\{\varphi\in E^{d}:\exists\,\widetilde{\varphi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to\mathbb{C}\text{ positive such that }\widetilde{\varphi}|_{E}=\varphi\},

is a weak*-closed convex subset of
E^{d}
.
We claim that
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Indeed, if not, without loss of generality by the Hahn–Banach Separation Theorem there would have to be some selfadjoint element
x\in E
for which

\varphi(x)<0\leq\inf_{\varphi^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi^{\prime}(x).

Since the restriction to
E
of every positive functional of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
lies in
{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, we obtain that
x\geq 0
, which contradicts to the fact that
\varphi
is positive.

Next let
\phi\colon E\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a completely bounded completely positive map.
By Arveson’s correspondence (see Remark
2.4
) we obtain a bounded positive functional
s_{\phi}\colon M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
.
By Proposition
4.3
we have that
M_{n}(E)
is also approximately positively generated, and thus we can extend
s_{\phi}
to a positive functional on
M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E))
.
Using Arveson’s correspondence once more, we obtain the desired completely bounded completely positive extension of
\phi\colon E\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Finally, let
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
be a completely bounded completely positive map.
Then
\phi
is the point-weak* limit of the net of compressions
(\phi_{{\mathcal{F}}})_{{\mathcal{F}}}
of
\phi
onto the finite dimensional subspaces of
K
indexed by inclusion, i.e.,
\phi_{{\mathcal{F}}}=P_{{\mathcal{F}}}\phi P_{{\mathcal{F}}}
where
P_{{\mathcal{F}}}
is the projection onto
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq K
.
Each of the maps
\phi_{{\mathcal{F}}}
belongs to the set
{\mathfrak{K}}
of “extendable” completely positive maps in (
4.1
) since
{\mathcal{B}}(P_{{\mathcal{F}}}K)\cong M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
where
k=\operatorname{rank}P_{{\mathcal{F}}}
.
By Lemma
4.4
, the set
{\mathfrak{K}}
is closed under the point-weak* topology, thus showing that
\phi\in{\mathfrak{K}}
, as required.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Note that Theorem
4.5
remains valid if the assumption that
E
is approximately positively generated is replaced by the weaker assumption that
E
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.

We give two examples of non-separating selfadjoint operator spaces, that generate a unital C*-algebra, but attain positive functionals that have not positive extensions.
In Example
5.8
we show that the conditions of approximate positive generation of
E
, unitality of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
, and the inclusion
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
being an embedding are independent.

Example 4.7
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be the unit circle and consider the selfadjoint operator space

F:=\{f\in{\rm C}(\mathbb{T}):\int_{\mathbb{T}}fdm=0\}=\{f\in{\rm C}(\mathbb{T}):\widehat{f}(0)=0\},

where
m
is the normalised Lebesgue measure and
\widehat{f}(0)
is the zero-th Fourier coefficient.
The coordinate function
\widehat{z}
is in
F
, and thus we have
\mathrm{C}^{*}(F)={\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
.
Moreover,
F
is not separating for
{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
since the unital positive map

{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})\to\mathbb{C};f\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{T}}fdm

annihilates
F
.
Note also that since any positive and non-zero continuous function has a non-zero integral, we have
F_{+}=\{0\}
.

Consider the “length” functional
\varphi\colon F\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

\varphi(f)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd(\delta_{1}-\delta_{-1})=f(1)-f(-1).

Then
\varphi
is bounded and it is trivially positive since
F_{+}=\{0\}
.
We claim that
\varphi
does not extend to a positive functional on
{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
, and thus the inclusion
F\subseteq{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
is not an embedding.

Suppose towards a contradiction that there exists a positive functional
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})\to\mathbb{C}
extending
\varphi
.
Then
\widetilde{\varphi}
is induced by a positive measure
\mu
by the Riesz–Markov–Kakutani Theorem, and in particular

\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\mu=\widetilde{\varphi}(f)=\varphi(f)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd(\delta_{1}-\delta_{-1})\text{ for all }f\in F.

Let
g\in{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
and set
f:=g-(\int_{\mathbb{T}}gdm)\cdot 1
; then
f\in F
and thus

\int_{\mathbb{T}}gd\mu-\mu(\mathbb{T})\int_{\mathbb{T}}gdm=\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd\mu=\int_{\mathbb{T}}fd(\delta_{1}-\delta_{-1})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}gd(\delta_{1}-\delta_{-1}).

Again by the Riesz–Markov–Kakutani Theorem, we get

\mu-\mu(\mathbb{T})\cdot m=\delta_{1}-\delta_{-1},

and thus
\mu(\{-1\})=-1<0
.
This is a contradiction as
\mu
is positive.

The problem in Example
4.7
is not the triviality of the cones as the following example indicates.

Example 4.8
.

Let
E\subseteq M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be the selfadjoint operator space generated by
\{E_{11},E_{12}\}
.
Then
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)=M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
as
E_{11},E_{12},E_{21}\in E
and
E_{22}=E_{21}E_{12}
. The compression to the (2,2) entry is a state on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
that annihilates
E
, and hence
E
is not separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
Note also that
E
is not approximately positively generated, as
E_{+}=\mathbb{R}_{+}\cdot E_{11}
.
Let
\varphi\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
be the functional defined by

\varphi(x)={\rm Tr}(\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix}x)\text{ for all }x\in E.

This is a bounded functional with
\varphi(A)=0
and
\varphi(B)=-1
.
Hence it is (completely) positive on
E
.
We claim that
\varphi
has no (completely) positive extension on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)=M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

Suppose towards a contradiction that
\varphi
extends to a positive functional
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C}
, i.e.,

\widetilde{\varphi}(\cdot)={\rm Tr}(\varrho\cdot)\text{ for some }\varrho\in M_{2}(\mathbb{C})_{+}.

We then have
\varrho_{11}={\rm Tr}(\varrho E_{11})=\varphi(E_{11})=0
, and therefore
\varrho_{12}=\varrho_{21}=0
as well.
With that in hand we have

-1=\varphi(E_{12})={\rm Tr}(\varrho E_{22})=0,

which is a contradiction.

In Example
4.21
we further show that approximate positive generation of
E
on its own still does not guarantee that the inclusion in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding.
The problem is that positive extensions (which exist by Theorem
4.5
) may not preserve the norm.

On the other hand, a class of selfadjoint operator spaces where positive extensions to
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
with preservation of norm exist appears in
[
9
]
.

Example 4.9
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be a selfadjoint operator space.
Suppose there is a C*-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq E
such that
{\mathcal{A}}\cdot E\subseteq E
, and an approximate unit
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
for
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
\lim_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}x=x
for every
x\in E
.
In
[
9
, Proposition 2.5]
it is shown that every contractive positive functional on
E
extends to a contractive positive functional on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
Hence
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding.

Such examples arise naturally in the context of
[
7
,
8
]
.
Is
{\mathcal{S}}
is an operator system and
{\mathcal{K}}
is the compact operators, then
{\mathcal{S}}\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
admits such an approximate unit.
A further class is formed by the selfadjoint operator spaces generated by a non-degenerate C*-correspondence.

Another class of selfadjoint operator spaces that allows positive extensions with preservation of the norm to the C*-algebra they generate arises from matrix ordered operator spaces with an approximate order unit that defines the matrix norms considered in
[
8
, Section 2.2]
.
We give the relevant definitions. Let
E
be a matrix ordered operator space. An
approximate order unit
for
E
is a net
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda}\subseteq E_{+}
that satisfies the following:

(i)

e_{\lambda}\leq e_{\mu}
if
\lambda\leq\mu
.

(ii)

For every
x\in E_{sa}
there exist a positive
t\in\mathbb{R}
and a
\lambda
such that
-te_{\lambda}\leq x\leq te_{\lambda}
.

In
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
it is shown that
e_{\lambda}^{n}:=(e_{\lambda}\otimes I_{n})_{\lambda}
is an approximate order unit for
M_{n}(E)
.
We say that
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
is
matrix norm-defining
if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in M_{n}(E)
we have

\|x\|_{M_{n}(E)}=\inf\left\{t:\begin{bmatrix}te_{\lambda}^{n}&x\\
x^{*}&te_{\lambda}^{n}\end{bmatrix}\in M_{2n}(E)_{+}\text{ for some }\lambda\right\}.

By
[
8
, Proposition 2.10]
those spaces are selfadjoint operator spaces in our sense.

In the third paragraph of the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 2.13]
, it is used that, in such a context, the inclusion of
E
inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
extends to a unital complete order embedding
E^{\#}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)^{\#}
, thus allowing the extension of a contractive positive functional of
E^{\#}
to a contractive positive functional of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)^{\#}
, and hence from
E
to
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
In order to show that
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding we can show that
[
8
, Lemma 2.11]
is an “if and only if” statement.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
E
be a selfadjoint operator space and let
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
be matrix norm-defining approximate order unit for
E
. Let
\varphi\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
be a selfadjoint functional.
Then,
\varphi
is positive if and only if
\|\varphi\|=\lim\varphi(e_{\lambda})
.

Proof.
.

If
\varphi
is positive, then it follows from
[
8
, Lemma 2.11]
that its norm equals the limit of
\varphi(e_{\lambda})
.
Conversely, let
x
be in
E_{+}
with
\|x\|\leq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary.
Since the approximate order unit is matrix norm-defining, by
[
14
, Proposition 1.8(i)]
there exists a positive
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and a
\lambda
such that

-t^{\prime}e_{\lambda}\leq x\leq t^{\prime}e_{\lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad t^{\prime}<\|x\|+\varepsilon\leq 1+\varepsilon.

Then for any
\mu\geq\lambda
we have

-(1+\varepsilon)e_{\mu}\leq-t^{\prime}e_{\lambda}\leq-x\leq e_{\mu}-x\leq e_{\mu}\leq(1+\varepsilon)e_{\mu}.

Since the approximate order unit is contractive, we get

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(e_{\mu})|=|\varphi(x-e_{\mu})|\leq\|\varphi\|\cdot\|x-e_{\mu}\|\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|\varphi\|.

Taking the limit and using the assumption we obtain

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-\|\varphi\||\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|\varphi\|.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we have

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-\|\varphi\||\leq\|\varphi\|\Longrightarrow-\|\varphi\|\leq\varphi(x)-\|\varphi\|\leq\|\varphi\|,

where we used that
\varphi
is selfadjoint and
\varphi(x)\in\mathbb{R}
.
This implies that
\varphi(x)\geq 0
.
∎

We now obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be a selfadjoint operator space.
If
E
contains a matrix norm-defining approximate order unit
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
, then
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding.

Proof.
.

By Theorem
3.3
it suffices to show that if
\varphi\colon M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
is a contractive positive map then it admits a contractive positive extension
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E))\to\mathbb{C}
.
By the Hahn–Banach Theorem,
\varphi
can be extended to a functional
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E))\to\mathbb{C}
with
\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|
, and by replacing
\widetilde{\varphi}
with
(\widetilde{\varphi}+\widetilde{\varphi}^{*})/2
we may assume that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is selfadjoint.
Lemma
4.10
yields
\|\varphi\|=\lim\varphi(e_{\lambda}^{n})
, and as
\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|
, using Lemma
4.10
once more we obtain that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is positive.
∎

We next consider embeddings not just with respect to some generated C*-algebra but with respect to any completely isometric complete order embedding in a selfadjoint operator space.

Definition 4.12
.

We say that a selfadjoint operator space
E
has
the automatic embedding property
if every completely isometric complete order embedding
\vartheta\colon E\to F
with
F
a selfadjoint operator space is automatically an embedding.

In particular we need just to check the completely isometric complete order Hilbertian embeddings.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
E
be a selfadjoint operator space.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

E
has the automatic embedding property.

(ii)

Every completely isometric complete order embedding
\vartheta\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
is an embedding.

Proof.
.

That item (i) implies (ii) is trivial.
For the converse, let
\vartheta\colon E\to F
be a completely isometric complete order embedding.
Let
\vartheta^{\prime}\colon F\to{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be an embedding, so that the unitisation
(\vartheta^{\prime})^{\#}
is completely isometric.
Since
\vartheta^{\prime}\circ\vartheta
is automatically an embedding, the unitisation

(\vartheta^{\prime}\circ\vartheta)^{\#}=(\vartheta^{\prime})^{\#}\circ\vartheta^{\#}

is completely isometric.
Hence
\vartheta^{\#}
is completely isometric, and thus
\vartheta
is an embedding.
∎

We will prove that approximately positively generated selfadjoint operator spaces, where the positive decompositions are compatible with the norms, have the automatic embedding property.
Following the terminology used in
[
12
]
we give the next definitions.

Definition 4.14
.

Let
E
be a selfadjoint operator space and set

\left(M_{n}(E)_{+}\right)_{\kappa}:=\{p\in M_{n}(E)_{+}:\|p\|\leq\kappa\}.

(i)

We say that
E
is
completely
\kappa
-generated
if there exists a
\kappa\geq 1
such that

\left(M_{n}(E)_{sa}\right)_{1}\subseteq\left(M_{n}(E)_{+}\right)_{\kappa}-\left(M_{n}(E)_{+}\right)_{\kappa}.

(ii)

We say that
E
is
completely approximately
\kappa
-generated
if there exists a
\kappa\geq 1
such that

\left(M_{n}(E)_{sa}\right)_{1}\subseteq\overline{\left(M_{n}(E)_{+}\right)_{\kappa}-\left(M_{n}(E)_{+}\right)_{\kappa}}.

We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be a selfadjoint operator space and let
\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(E,\mathbb{C})
.
If
\varphi
satisfies

\|\varphi\|=\sup\{\varphi(x):x\in(E_{+})_{1}\},

then it extends to a
\widetilde{\varphi}\in{\rm CCP}({\mathcal{B}}(H),\mathbb{C})
(and consequently with
\left\|\widetilde{\varphi}\right\|=\left\|\varphi\right\|
).

In particular, every selfadjoint extension
\widetilde{\varphi}
of
\varphi
on
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
with
\left\|\widetilde{\varphi}\right\|=\left\|\varphi\right\|
is positive.

Proof.
.

By the Hahn–Banach Theorem, let
\widetilde{\varphi}
be a functional on
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
with
\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|
that extends
\varphi
.
By replacing
\widetilde{\varphi}
with
(\widetilde{\varphi}+\widetilde{\varphi}^{*})/2
we may suppose that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is a selfadjoint extension of
\varphi
with the same norm.
Consider the Jordan decomposition

\widetilde{\varphi}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{-}\text{ such that }\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}\|+\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{-}\|,

into positive functionals
\widetilde{\varphi}_{\pm}
of
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
For
x\in E
with
0\leq x\leq I_{H}
we have

\varphi(x)=\widetilde{\varphi}(x)=\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}(x)-\widetilde{\varphi}_{-}(x)\leq\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}(x)\leq\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}(I_{H})=\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}\|.

Taking supremum over all such
x
and using the assumption we get

\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}\|+\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{-}\|=\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|\leq\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}\|.

We conclude that
\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{-}\|\leq 0
and so
\widetilde{\varphi}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}
.
∎

Consequently, we establish a sufficient condition under which a selfadjoint operator space admits the automatic embedding property.

Corollary 4.16
.

If
E
is a selfadjoint operator space such that

\|\varphi\|=\sup\{\varphi(x):x\in(M_{n}(E)_{+})_{1}\}\text{ for all }\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C}),n\in\mathbb{N},

then
E
has the automatic embedding property.

Proof.
.

Without loss of generality, and by Lemma
4.13
, suppose that
E\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and we will show that this inclusion is an embedding.
By Lemma
4.15
any contractive positive functional of
M_{n}(E)
extends to a contractive positive functional of
M_{n}({\mathcal{B}}(H))
, and hence
Theorem
3.3
completes the proof.
∎

The condition of Corollary
4.16
is satisfied when
E
is a completely approximately 1-generated selfadjoint operator space.

Theorem 4.17
.

If
E
is a completely approximately 1-generated selfadjoint operator space, then
E
has the automatic embedding property.

Proof.
.

Take
\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Since
\varphi
is selfadjoint, we have

\|\varphi\|=\sup\{\varphi(x):x\in(M_{n}(E)_{sa})_{1}\}.

Moreover, since
E
is completely approximately 1-generated, we obtain

\sup\{\varphi(x):x\in(M_{n}(E)_{sa})_{1}\}=\sup\{\varphi(x):x\in\left((M_{n}(E)_{+})_{1}-(M_{n}(E)_{+})_{1}\right)\cap(M_{n}(E)_{sa})_{1}\}.

Thus, we may pick an element
x\in(M_{n}(E)_{sa})_{1}
such that
\varphi(x)>\|\varphi\|-\varepsilon
, and
x=x_{1}-x_{2}
with
x_{1},x_{2}\in M_{n}(E)_{+}
satisfying
\|x_{1}\|,\|x_{2}\|\leq\|x\|
.
We then have

\varphi(x_{1})\geq\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi(x_{2})=\varphi(x)>\|\varphi\|-\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary and
\|x_{1}\|\leq\|x\|\leq 1
we obtain

\sup\{\varphi(x^{\prime}):x^{\prime}\in(M_{n}(E)_{+})_{1}\}=\|\varphi\|.

Since
\varphi
was arbitrary, Corollary
4.16
completes the proof.
∎

The following corollary is a refinement of
[
23
, Theorem 4.4 and Theorem 4.5]
.
In particular, we show that the inclusion of operator systems need not to be unital, and the inclusion of C*-algebras need not to be multiplicative, in order to have gauge maximal inclusions.

Corollary 4.18
.

The following hold:

(i)

If
\phi\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
is a completely isometric complete order embedding between operator systems, then
\phi
is an embedding.

(ii)

If
\phi\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{D}}
is a completely isometric complete order embedding between C*-algebras, then
\phi
is an embedding.

Proof.
.

Both cases follow from Theorem
4.17
since operator systems and C*-algebras are completely 1-generated selfadjoint operator spaces.
∎

Remark 4.19
.

If
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
are operator systems with
1_{\mathcal{S}}\neq 1_{\mathcal{T}}
, then Corollary
4.18
and Theorem
3.8
yield that every completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
extends to a completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon{\mathcal{T}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
.
Thus Arveson’s Extension Theorem holds even for non-unital inclusions of operator systems.
(We note that completely contractive completely positive extensions of unital completely positive maps on operator systems are automatically unital.)

Remark 4.20
.

By Corollary
4.18
we have that if
{\mathcal{S}}
is an operator subsystem in an operator system
{\mathcal{T}}
(not necessarily with the same units) with co-dimension
1
, then
{\mathcal{T}}
is completely order isomorphic to
E^{\#}
.

We finish the section with an example to demonstrate that the assumption of
completely (approximately) 1-generation
in Theorem
4.17
cannot be relaxed to mere
(approximately) positively generation
if one wishes to obtain an automatic embedding property.

Example 4.21
.

Let
E\subseteq M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be the selfadjoint operator space generated by

A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}.

Since
\{A,B\}
is a linearly independent selfadjoint set, any selfadjoint element in
E
has the form

X=\alpha A+\beta B=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\alpha\\
\alpha&\alpha+\beta\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{where }\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, the positive cone of
E
is

E_{+}=\{\alpha A+\beta B:\alpha,\beta\geq 0\}.

Let
X=\alpha A+\beta B\in E_{sa}
be arbitrary.
By setting

\alpha_{1}=\max(\alpha,0),\quad\alpha_{2}=\max(-\alpha,0)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{1}=\max(\beta,0),\quad\beta_{2}=\max(-\beta,0),

we obtain

X=(\alpha_{1}A+\beta_{1}B)-(\alpha_{2}A+\beta_{2}B)\in E_{+}-E_{+},

and hence
E
is positively generated.

Claim 1.
The space
E
is not completely 1-generated.

Proof of Claim 1.
Towards this end, let

X=A-B=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}\in E.

We will show that for any decomposition
X=X_{1}-X_{2}
with
X_{1},X_{2}\in E_{+}
we have

\max\{\|X_{1}\|,\|X_{2}\|\}>\|X\|=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}.

Indeed, suppose that
X=X_{1}-X_{2}
with
X_{1},X_{2}\in E_{+}
such that
X_{1}=\alpha_{1}A+\beta_{1}B
and
X_{2}=\alpha_{2}A+\beta_{2}B
.
Comparing entries we obtain
\alpha_{1}=1+\alpha_{2}
and
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}-1
.
Hence, by setting
\alpha_{2}:=\alpha
and
\beta_{2}:=\beta
we have the decomposition

X=X_{1}(\alpha,\beta)-X_{2}(\alpha,\beta)

with

X_{1}(\alpha,\beta)=\begin{bmatrix}1+\alpha&1+\alpha\\
1+\alpha&\alpha+\beta\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad X_{2}(\alpha,\beta)=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\alpha\\
\alpha&\alpha+\beta\end{bmatrix}.

By positivity we get

X_{2}(\alpha,\beta)\geq 0\Rightarrow\alpha\geq 0,\beta\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad X_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\geq 0\Rightarrow\frac{\alpha+\beta}{1+\alpha}\geq 1\Rightarrow\beta\geq 1.

The eigenvalues of
X_{1}(\alpha,\beta)
are

\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\sqrt{4\alpha^{2}+8\alpha+\beta^{2}-2\beta+5}+2\alpha+\beta+1\right)

and

\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{4\alpha^{2}+8\alpha+\beta^{2}-2\beta+5}+2\alpha+\beta+1\right).

Since
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 1
we get

\|X_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\|=\max\{|\lambda_{1}|,|\lambda_{2}|\}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq 2=\|X_{1}(0,1)\|.

Therefore we have

\max\{\|X_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\|,\|X_{2}(\alpha,\beta)\|\geq\|X_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\|\geq 2>\|X\|,

as required.
\Box

Claim 2.
The inclusion
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)=M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is not an embedding.

Proof of Claim 2.

The equality
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)=M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
follows directly from the computations

E_{11}=A-AB-BA+B,\quad E_{12}=AB-B,\quad E_{21}=BA-B\quad\text{and}\quad E_{22}=B.

By Theorem
3.3
it suffices to show that some
\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(E,\mathbb{C})
does not extend to a
\widetilde{\varphi}\in{\rm CCP}(M_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C})
with the same norm.
Towards this end, let
\varphi\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\varphi(\alpha A+\beta B):=\beta/\sqrt{2}.

Then
\varphi
is clearly positive and we will prove that
\|\varphi\|=1
.
Choosing

\alpha=-\sqrt{2}^{-1}
and
\beta=\sqrt{2}
,
we get an element

X=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\[6.0pt]
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix}\in E,

with
\|X\|=1
and
\varphi(X)=1
; hence
\|\varphi\|\geq 1
.
Let
(e_{i})_{i=1}^{2}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
denote the canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and consider the unit vector

z:=\sqrt{2}^{-1}(e_{1}-e_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then for
X=\alpha A+\beta B\in E
we have

Xz=\alpha\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-1\end{bmatrix}+\beta\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-1\end{bmatrix}=-\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}e_{2},

and hence

\|X\|\geq\frac{|\beta|}{\sqrt{2}}=|\varphi(X)|
.
This concludes that
\|\varphi\|=1
.

Now assume towards a contradiction that there exists a state
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C}
that extends
\varphi
with the same norm.
Pick
\varrho\in M_{2}(\mathbb{C})_{+}
with
{\rm Tr}(\varrho)=1
such that
\widetilde{\varphi}(\cdot)={\rm Tr}(\varrho\cdot)
.
Then

0=\varphi(A)=\widetilde{\varphi}(A)=\rm Tr(\varrho A)=\varrho_{11}+\varrho_{22}+2\rm Re(\varrho_{12})=1+2\rm Re(\varrho_{12})\Rightarrow\rm{Re}(\varrho_{12})=-1/2

On the other hand,

\sqrt{2}^{-1}=\varphi(B)=\widetilde{\varphi}(B)=\rm{Tr}(\varrho B)=\varrho_{22}.

As
\varrho\in M_{2}(\mathbb{C})_{+}
we have

\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}=\varrho_{11}\varrho_{22}\geq|\varrho_{12}|^{2}\geq\left(\operatorname{Re}(\varrho_{12})\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{4},

and thus we arrive to the contradiction
4\sqrt{2}\geq 6
.
\Box

Note that
\varphi
does extend to a positive functional on all of
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
by Theorem
4.5
, but not with preservation of norm.
One such extension is the functional
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\widetilde{\varphi}(C):=\left\langle Cv,v\right\rangle\text{ for }v=\sqrt[4]{2}^{-1}(e_{1}-e_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}.

5.
One-dimensional selfadjoint spaces

In this section we consider one dimensional selfadjoint subspaces of
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
.
Recall that C*-inclusions preserve the spectrum and the decomposition of selfadjoint elements into the positive and negative parts by functional calculus.
We begin with the following observation.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a C*-algebra and let
E=\operatorname{span}\{x\}
be the selfadjoint subspace generated by an element
x\in{\mathcal{C}}_{sa}
.
Then the following hold:

(i)

If
x_{\pm}\neq 0
, then
M_{n}(E)\cap M_{n}({\mathcal{C}})_{+}=\{0\}
.

(ii)

If
x_{+}=0
or
x_{-}=0
, then for every
y\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
there are
y_{1},y_{2}\in M_{n}(E)_{+}
such that
y=y_{1}-y_{2}
and
\max\{\|y_{1}\|,\|y_{2}\|\}=\|y\|
. In particular,
E
is completely
1
-generated.

Proof.
.

(i) Suppose that
a\otimes x\geq 0
in
M_{n}({\mathcal{C}})
, and so
(a\otimes x)_{-}=0
.
Therefore we have

a_{+}\otimes x_{-}=0\quad\text{and}\quad a_{-}\otimes x_{+}=0.

Since
x_{\pm}\neq 0
, we get
a_{\pm}=0
.
Hence
a=0
, and thus
a\otimes x=0
.

(ii) Without loss of generality assume that
x_{-}=0
, since
\operatorname{span}\{x\}=\operatorname{span}\{-x\}
.
Suppose that
a\otimes x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
.
Since
x=x_{+}\in E_{+}
and
a=a^{*}
we get

(a\otimes x)_{+}=a_{+}\otimes x\in M_{n}(E)_{+}\quad\text{and}\quad(a\otimes x)_{-}=a_{-}\otimes x\in M_{n}(E)_{+}.

The norm equality follows from the properties of the decomposition.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
4.17
and Lemma
5.1
we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a C*-algebra and let
E=\operatorname{span}\{x\}
be the selfadjoint subspace generated by an element
x\in{\mathcal{C}}_{sa}
.
If
x_{+}=0
or
x_{-}=0
, then the inclusion
E\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
is an embedding.

We next prove that inclusions of one-dimensional generated selfadjoint spaces allow for completely positive extensions (that may not preserve the norm).
We first need the following remark.

Remark 5.3
.

Let
x
be in
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and let
E=\operatorname{span}\{x\}
.
Then every linear map
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
is automatically completely bounded with
\|\phi\|_{\rm cb}=\|\phi\|=\|x\|^{-1}\|\phi(x)\|
.
For the remainder of this section, we will not use the term (completely) bounded when we refer to linear maps defined on one-dimensional operator spaces.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
x\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be a selfadjoint operator and set
E:=\operatorname{span}\{x\}
.
Then, every completely positive map
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
extends to a completely positive map
\widetilde{\phi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In particular, the following hold:

(i)

If
x_{+}=0
or
x_{-}=0
, then there is such an extension
\widetilde{\phi}
with

\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{\rm cb}=\|\phi\|_{\rm cb}.

(ii)

If
x_{\pm}\neq 0
, then there is such an extension
\widetilde{\phi}
with

\|\phi\|_{\rm cb}\;\leq\;\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{\rm cb}\;\leq\;\frac{\|x\|}{\min\{\|x_{+}\|,\|x_{-}\|\}}\,\|\phi\|_{\rm cb}.

Proof.
.

Item (i) follows from Theorem
3.8
and Corollary
5.2
.
We now prove item (ii), so suppose that
x_{\pm}\neq 0
.
Let
\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
be a completely positive map. Denote by
\sigma(x)
the spectrum of
x
relatively to
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
, and set

m:=\min\sigma(x)\quad\text{and}\quad M:=\max\sigma(x).

Since
x_{\pm}\neq 0
we have
m<0<M
.
Set

T:=\phi(x)\in B(K),

and let
T=T_{+}-T_{-}
be its decomposition with
T_{\pm}\geq 0
and
T_{-}T_{+}=T_{+}T_{-}=0
.
Define

Y:=M^{-1}T_{+}-m^{-1}T_{-}\in B(K)_{+}.

Now let
{\mathfrak{B}}(\sigma(x))
denote the Borel subsets of
\sigma(x)
and define the map
Q\colon{\mathfrak{B}}(\sigma(x))\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
such that

Q(\{M\}):=\frac{T-mY}{M-m}\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad Q(\{m\}):=\frac{MY-T}{M-m}\geq 0,

and
Q(B)=0
for every
B\in{\mathfrak{B}}(\sigma(x))
with
B\cap\{m,M\}=\emptyset
.
Then
Q
is a bounded regular and positive
{\mathcal{B}}(K)
-valued measure satisfying

\mu_{\xi,\eta}=\langle Q(\{m\})\xi,\eta\rangle\,\delta_{m}+\langle Q(\{M\})\xi,\eta\rangle\,\delta_{M}.

Define the (completely) positive map

\Phi\colon{\rm C}(\sigma(x))\to B(K);f\mapsto\int_{\sigma(x)}f(t)\,dQ(t)=f(m)Q(\{m\})+f(M)Q(\{M\})

(see for example
[
21
, Theorem 3.11 and Proposition 4.5]
).
Equivalently,

\langle\Phi(f)\xi,\eta\rangle=\int f\,d\mu_{\xi,\eta}\text{ for all }\xi,\eta\in K.

For the coordinate function
\widehat{z}
we have

\Phi(\widehat{z})=m\,Q(\{m\})+M\,Q(\{M\})=m\,\frac{MY-T}{M-m}+M\,\frac{T-mY}{M-m}=T=\phi(x).

Hence
\Phi|_{E}=\phi
after identifying
\mathrm{C}^{*}(x,I_{H})
with
{\rm C}(\sigma(x))
.

We now compute the norms. First note that

\Phi(1)=Q(\{m\})+Q(\{M\})=\frac{T-mY}{M-m}+\frac{MY-T}{M-m}=Y,

and thus
\|\Phi\|_{\rm cb}=\|\Phi(1)\|=\|Y\|
.
Since
T_{+}
and
T_{-}
have orthogonal ranges, we get

\|\Phi\|_{\rm cb}=\|Y\|=\max\Big\{\tfrac{\|T_{+}\|}{M},\tfrac{\|T_{-}\|}{|m|}\Big\}\leq\frac{\|T\|}{\min\{M,|m|\}}\leq\frac{\|x\|}{\min\{M,|m|\}}\|\phi\|_{\rm cb}.

By setting
\widetilde{\phi}:=\Phi|_{\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)}
we obtain a completely positive extension of
\phi
such that

\|\phi\|_{\rm cb}\leq\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{\rm cb}\leq\|\Phi\|_{\rm cb}\leq\frac{\|x\|}{\min\{\|x_{+}\|,\|x_{-}\|\}}\|\phi\|_{\rm cb},

thus showing item (ii).
∎

Remark 5.5
.

With the setup of Proposition
5.4
, Remark
2.4
yields that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every positive functional
\varphi\colon M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
admits a positive extension
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(E))\to\mathbb{C}
.
However, the positive extensions do not always preserve the norm.

For such an example, let
x
be a selfadjoint element in a C*-algebra
{\mathcal{C}}
such that

0<\min\{\|x_{+}\|,\|x_{-}\|\}<\|x\|;

equivalently,
x_{\pm}\neq 0
and either
\|x\|\notin\sigma_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x)
or
-\|x\|\notin\sigma_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x)
.
Set
E:=\operatorname{span}\{x\}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
.
Then

\operatorname{dist}(x,E_{+})=\|x\|=\operatorname{dist}(-x,E_{+}),

as
E_{+}=\{0\}
.
By Proposition
2.5
we also have

\operatorname{dist}(x,{\mathcal{C}}_{+})=\|x_{+}\|\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{dist}(-x,{\mathcal{C}}_{+})=\|x_{-}\|.

Since
\min\{\|x_{+}\|,\|x_{-}\|\}<\|x\|
, we obtain that
E\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
is not a gauge maximal inclusion, which by Theorem
3.3
implies that there exists a
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a
\varphi\in{\rm CCP}(M_{n}(E),\mathbb{C})
that does not extend to a completely contractive completely positive map
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(M_{n}(E))\to\mathbb{C}
.

For the remainder of the section we focus in the case where
x\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)_{sa}
with
x_{\pm}\neq 0
.
The only obstruction to having an embedding of
\operatorname{span}\{x\}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
in this case, is the possible complete positive extension (of either and thus of both) of the maps

\phi\colon\operatorname{span}\{x\}\to\operatorname{span}\{x\};\phi(x)=-x\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi\colon\operatorname{span}\{x\}\to\mathbb{C};\varphi(x)=-\|x\|.

In view of item (i) of Lemma
5.1
these maps are completely contractive completely positive on
\operatorname{span}\{x\}
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
x\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
be a selfadjoint operator such that
x_{\pm}\neq 0

\operatorname{span}\{x\}

\|x_{+}\|=\|x_{-}\|

(iii)

.

Without loss of generality let us assume that
\|x\|=\|x_{+}\|=1

[(i)

[(iii)

By assumption

[(iv)

6.
Co-universality in different classes

6.1.
Terminal objects

We now inspect the different classes of morphisms in the category of selfadjoint operator spaces and their terminal objects.
Let
E
be selfadjoint operator space and let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a subclass of the class

{\mathcal{F}}_{1}:=\{\phi\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K):\text{$\phi$ completely isometric completely positive}\}.

We say that
{\mathcal{F}}
has a terminal object
if there exists a
j\in{\mathcal{F}}
such that for every
\phi\in{\mathcal{F}}
there exists a
*
-epimorphism
\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(\phi(E))\to\mathrm{C}^{*}(j(E))
with
\Phi(\phi(x))=j(x)
for every
x\in E
.
If such a
j
exists then
\mathrm{C}^{*}(j(E))
is unique up to canonical
*
-isomorphisms (i.e.,
*
-isomorphisms that fix the copies of
E
). We will write
\partial{\mathcal{F}}
for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(j(E))
.
We consider the following subclasses of
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{\vartheta\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K):\text{$\vartheta$ completely isometric complete order embedding}\};

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{3}

\displaystyle:=\{\vartheta\colon E\to{\mathcal{B}}(K):\text{$\vartheta$ embedding}\}.

By definition
{\mathcal{F}}_{3}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
, and by
[
7
, Theorem 2.25]
we get that
(\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3},j_{3})
always exists and
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)
.
Blecher–Kirkpatrick–Neal–Werner show in
[
2
, Theorem 2.3]
that
(\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{1},j_{1})
exists when
E
is approximately positively generated.
However in general
(\partial F_{1},j_{1})
or
(\partial F_{2},j_{2})
may not exist.
This is the case for
(\partial F_{2},j_{2})
in Example
2.3
(that has trivial positive cones), and the same arguments rule out the existence of
(\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{1},j_{1})
as well.
For
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
we have the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
E
be a selfadjoint operator space.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{F}}_{2}={\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.

(ii)

\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
exists and there is a canonical
*
-isomorphism
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\cong\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.

Proof.
.

If
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}={\mathcal{F}}_{3}
, then it is immediate that
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}=\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.
For the converse, let
\Phi\colon\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\to\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
be a

-isomorphism such that
\Phi(j_{2}(x))=j_{3}(x)
for every

.
Suppose that
\vartheta\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
and we will show that
\vartheta\in{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.
By the definition of
(\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2},j_{2})
let
\pi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(\vartheta(E))\to\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
be a
*
-epimorphism such that
\pi(\vartheta(x))=j_{2}(x)
for every

.
For any

we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(j_{3}^{(n)}(x),-M_{n}(\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3})_{+})

\displaystyle=\inf\{\|x+p\|\colon p\in M_{n}(E)_{+}\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{\|\vartheta^{(n)}(x)+\vartheta^{(n)}(p)\|\colon p\in M_{n}(E)_{+}\}

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{dist}(\vartheta^{(n)}(x),-M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\vartheta(E)))_{+}),

where in the first equality we use Theorem
3.3
and that

is an embedding.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(j_{3}^{(n)}(x),-M_{n}(\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3})_{+})

\displaystyle=\inf\{\|(\Phi\circ\pi)^{(n)}(\vartheta^{(n)}(x))+p\|\colon p\in M_{n}(\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3})_{+}\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\{\|(\Phi\circ\pi)^{(n)}(\vartheta^{(n)}(x))+(\Phi\circ\pi)^{(n)}(p)\|\colon p\in M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\vartheta(E)))_{+}\}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{dist}(\vartheta^{(n)}(x),-M_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\vartheta(E)))_{+}).

Hence
\vartheta
is a gauge maximal isometry, and by Theorem
3.3
it is an embedding.
∎

Although approximate positive generation implies the existence of
\partial F_{1}
, it does not imply a canonical identification with
\partial F_{3}
.

Example 6.2
.

Let
E\subseteq M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be the selfadjoint operator space of Example
4.21
.
By
[
2
, Theorem 2.3]
we have that
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
exists since
E
is positively generated.
We will prove that there is no canonical
*
-isomorphism between
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
and
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.

Indeed, suppose that there is such a
*
-isomorphism, say
\Phi\colon\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\to\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
and let
\varphi\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
be the contractive and positive functional defined in Example
4.21
that does not admit a contractive positive extension on
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
By the defining property of
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
there exists a
*
-epimorphism
\pi\colon M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
such that
\pi(x)=j_{1}(x)
for all
x\in E
.
The map
j_{3}\colon E\to\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
is an embedding and hence by Theorem
3.3
we may pick a contractive and positive map
\psi\colon\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}\to\mathbb{C}
that extends
\varphi\circ j_{3}^{-1}
.
Setting
\widetilde{\varphi}:=\psi\circ\Phi\circ\pi
gives a contractive and positive extension of
\varphi
on
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, which is a contradiction.

Note also that even if
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
exists then there is no canonical
*
-isomorphism between
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
and
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.
This follows by Proposition
6.1
as the inclusion
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is in
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
and not in
{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.

As a corollary of Theorem
4.17
we obtain the following.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
E
be a completely approximately 1-generated selfadjoint operator space.
Then
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
exists and
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}=\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.

In particular for operator systems we have the following corollary.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be an operator system.
Then
\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\cong\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\cong\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{3}\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}})
via canonical
*
-isomorphisms.

Proof.
.

Since
{\mathcal{S}}
is completely 1-generated, the result follows from Corollary
6.3
combined with
[
7
, Theorem 2.25 (ii)]
and the comments following
[
2
, Theorem 2.3]
.
∎

6.2.
Hyperrigidity

The hyperrigidity property was introduced by Arveson
[
1
]
for a generating set
{\mathcal{G}}
in a C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{G}})
.
A set
{\mathcal{G}}
is called
hyperrigid
if for every faithful non-degenerate
*
-representation
\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{G}})\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
and every sequence of unital completely positive maps
\phi_{n}\colon{\mathcal{B}}(K)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
the following holds:

\lim_{n}\|\phi_{n}(\Phi(g))-\Phi(g)\|=0\text{ for all }g\in{\mathcal{G}}\Longrightarrow\lim_{n}\|\phi_{n}(\Phi(c))-\Phi(c)\|=0\text{ for all }c\in\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{G}}).

Arveson
[
1
]
shows that if an operator system
{\mathcal{S}}
is hyperrigid inside the C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{S}})
it generates, then
\mathrm{C}^{*}({\mathcal{S}})
is canonically
*
-isomorphic with
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}({\mathcal{S}})
.
It appears that having an embedding, together with either the separating property or the unitality of the generated C*-algebra, is sufficient.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be a selfadjoint operator space that is hyperrigid in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
Then
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
by a canonical
*
-isomorphism in the following cases:

(i)

E
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
and
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding.

(ii)

\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is unital and
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding.

Proof.
.

Let
(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E),j)
denote the C*-envelope of
E
.
By the defining property of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)
we obtain a
*
-epimorphism
\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)
satisfying
\Phi(x)=j(x)
for every
x\in E
.

(i) Assume that
E
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
Since
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding, we may identify
E^{\#}
with
E+\mathbb{C}1_{\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)^{\#}}
inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)^{\#}
.
Since
E
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
, by
[
9
, Proposition 3.20]
we have that
E^{\#}
is hyperrigid in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)^{\#}
.
Consequently, we obtain a canonical
*
-isomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E^{\#})\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)^{\#}
.
The proof of
[
7
, Theorem 2.25]
implies that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)
is
*
-isomorphic to the C*-algebra generated by the copy of
E
inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E^{\#})
, and hence the injective
*
-homomorphism

\Psi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E^{\#})\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)^{\#}

satisfies
\Psi(j(x))=(x,0)
for every
x\in E
, which implies that the range of
\Psi
is
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
We conclude that
\Psi
and
\Phi
are mutual inverses and thus
\Phi
is a
*
-isomorphism.

(ii) Assume that
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is unital. Let
\pi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
be a faithful unital
*
-representation.
Since
E
is hyperrigid, then
\pi
has the unique extension property by
[
9
, Theorem 3.10, Theorem 3.11]
.
We now consider the completely contractive and completely positive map
\pi\circ j^{-1}\colon j(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
.
By Theorem
3.8
we may extend it to a completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
so that
\phi\circ j=\pi|_{E}
.
On the other hand
\phi\circ j=\phi\circ\Phi|_{E}
, and by the unique extension property of
\pi
we obtain
\phi\circ\Phi=\pi
.
Since
\pi
is injective we conclude that
\Phi
is also injective, and thus a
*
-isomorphism.
∎

Consequently we have the following corollary.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be a selfadjoint operator space that is hyperrigid in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
Then
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
by a canonical
*
-isomorphism in the following cases:

(i)

{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq E
is a C*-algebra such that
{\mathcal{A}}\cdot E\subseteq E
and there is an approximate unit
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
such that
\lim_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}x=x
for all
x\in E
.

(ii)

E
contains a matrix norm-defining approximate order unit
(e_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.

(iii)

E
is completely approximately 1-generated.

Proof.
.

The fact that the inclusion
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding in item (i), follows from
[
9
, Proposition 2.5]
.
By the same result we have that
E
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
The fact that
E
is separating for
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
in item (ii) follows in the same way by Lemma
4.10
, and the embedding property follows from Proposition
4.11
.
Item (iii) follows from Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
4.17
.
∎

We will say that a selfadjoint operator space
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)

contains enough unitaries in

\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
if there is a collection of unitaries in
E
which generates
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
as a C*-algebra, that is,
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is the smallest C*-algebra that contains these unitaries.
The notion of a subspace containing enough unitaries was first considered by Pisier in
[
22
]
in the context of operator spaces to provide a simple proof of Kirchberg’s Theorem.
It was then studied by Kavruk–Paulsen–Todorov–Tomforde in the context of nuclearity and tensor products of operator systems
[
15
]
.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be a selfadjoint operator space that contains enough unitaries in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
. If
\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)\to{\mathcal{B}}(K)
is a unital
*
-representation then
\Phi|_{E}
has the unique extension property. In particular,
E
is hyperrigid.

Proof.
.

It follows using similar arguments as in
[
15
, Lemma 9.3]
and using
[
9
, Theorem 3.10]
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
6.5
and Proposition
6.7
we obtain the following result for the C*-envelope of a selfadjoint operator space that contains enough unitaries in the C*-algebra it generates.

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
be a selfadjoint operator space that contains enough unitaries in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The inclusion
E\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
is an embedding.

(ii)

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E)\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}(E)
by a canonical
*
-isomorphism.

Remark 6.9
.

We note that having a selfadjoint operator space that contains enough unitaries in the C*-algebra it generates does not imply that the inclusion is an embedding.
Indeed, consider the selfadjoint operator space
F\subseteq{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
of Example
4.7
.
Then the inclusion in
{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
is not an embedding, but
F
contains enough unitaries in
{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
as it contains the coordinate function. In particular, by Proposition
6.7
we have that
F
is hyperrigid in
{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
and yet Corollary
6.8
implies that there is no canonical
*
-isomorphism between
{\rm C}(\mathbb{T})
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(F)
.

Example 6.10
.

Recall the selfadjoint operator space
E_{n}\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
of Example
3.9
.
As shown therein, this inclusion is an embedding, and since
E_{n}
contains enough unitaries in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
, by Corollary
6.8
we have a canonical
*
-isomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\textup{env}}(E_{n})\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
.

We note also that the selfadjoint operator space
E_{n}
satisfies a universal property analogous to the operator system case
[
15
, Proposition 9.7]
: for every selfadjoint operator space
F
and contractions
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in F
, there exists a completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon E_{n}\to F
mapping
\phi(u_{i})=y_{i}
, for all
i=1,\dots,n
.
Indeed, consider an embedding
F\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
and as in Example
3.9
, dilate each
y_{i}
to a unitary
\widehat{y_{i}}
in
{\mathcal{B}}(H^{(2)})
.
By the universal property of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
, there exists a unital
*
-homomorphism
\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{n})\to{\mathcal{B}}(H^{(2)})
such that
\Phi(u_{i})=\widehat{y_{i}}
,
i=1,\dots,n
.
Compressing to the
(1,1)
corner and restricting to
E_{n}
yields a completely contractive completely positive map
\phi\colon E_{n}\to F
such that
\phi(u_{i})=y_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
.

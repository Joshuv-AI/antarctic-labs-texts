Local Operations in Multiparty Quantum Systems
Mithilesh Kumar

Abstract
In a multipartite systems, local operations are conducted by one party and the results are communicated to the other parties. Such models have been studied under the framework of LOCC and SLOCC. In this paper, we study when can an action of one party be simulated by another. We obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for when can a unitary action be simulated in a bipartite system. We also show that arbitrary operations can be simulated by any party as long as the given multipartite state is Schmidt decomposable. Moreover, we obtain condition for simulation of local measurements in arbitrary tripartite systems.



Distributed quantum systems can be seen as multipartite states. Since the early days of quantum information, communication between parties has been studied. Each party does local quantum operation on its part of the system and communicates the result with other parties. Lo and Popescu
[
1
]
showed that two-way communication between two parties can be reduced to one-way communication. This observation was later used by Nielsen
[
2
]
to obtain the necessary and sufficient condition for conversion of one bipartite state to another via local operations and classical communication.

The focus of this work is understanding how a local operation done by one party can be simulated by another party. Such a question becomes relevant even in circuit design where operations can be localized to a given set of qubits.

Let us begin with a bipartite system where Alice has one part and Bob has another. Suppose Bob performs local unitary operation
U_{B}
. Does there exist local unitary operation
U_{A}
that Alice can perform such that the resulting state is the same? As the following theorem shows, it is not always possible to achieve it.
U_{B}
depends on the input state
|\psi\rangle
.

Theorem 1
.

Given a bipartite state
|\psi\rangle
, the local unitary operation
U_{B}
of Bob can be simulated by a local unitary operation
U_{A}
by Alice if and only if
U_{A}=U_{B}^{T}
and
U_{B}=U_{1}\oplus U_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus U_{d}
where unitary operations
U_{i}
have dimension equal to the multiplicity of the
i
th eigenvalue
\mu_{i}
of
\rho_{B}
.

Proof.

Consider the state
|\psi\rangle
in the Schmidt basis

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle

The state after Bob applies his local unitary operation
U_{B}
, written in the Schmidt basis as

\displaystyle U_{B}=\sum_{n,\ell}u^{B}_{n\ell}|n_{B}\rangle\langle\ell_{B}|

is given by

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle U_{B}|\ell_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle\sum_{n}u^{B}_{\ell n}|n_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,n}\lambda_{\ell}u^{B}_{\ell n}|\ell_{A}\rangle|n_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}\lambda_{i}u^{B}_{ij}|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle

where the last expression is just relabeling
\ell
to
i
and
n
to
j
. Suppose there exists a local unitary operation
U_{A}
for Alice that results in the same state
|\phi\rangle
when acted up on
|\psi\rangle
. Writing
U_{A}
is the Schmidt basis we get

\displaystyle U_{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\ell}u^{A}_{m\ell}|m_{A}\rangle\langle\ell_{A}|

Applying
U_{A}
on
|\psi\rangle
gives

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}U_{A}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\sum_{m}u^{A}_{\ell m}|m_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,\ell}\lambda_{\ell}u^{A}_{\ell m}|m_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}\lambda_{j}u^{A}_{ji}|i_{A}\rangle|j_{B}\rangle

These to separate expressions for
|\phi\rangle
imply that for each
i,j

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}u^{B}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}u^{A}_{ji}

\displaystyle v^{A}_{ij}\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}u^{B}_{ij}

where
v^{A}_{i}j=u^{A}_{ji}
is unitary as well. We can define a diagonal matrix
D
such that the diagonals are the coefficients
\lambda_{i}
. This provides us with

\displaystyle V_{A}D

\displaystyle=DU_{B}

\displaystyle V_{A}

\displaystyle=DU_{B}D^{-1}

Now we need to apply the condition that
V_{A}
must be unitary.

\displaystyle V_{A}^{\dagger}V_{A}

\displaystyle=I_{A}

\displaystyle\implies(D^{-1}U_{B}^{\dagger}D)(DU_{B}D^{-1})

\displaystyle=I_{A}

\displaystyle\implies U_{B}^{\dagger}D^{2}U_{B}

\displaystyle=D^{2}

\displaystyle\implies D^{2}U_{B}

\displaystyle=U_{B}D^{2}

\displaystyle\implies\lambda_{i}^{2}u^{B}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}u^{B}_{ij}

\displaystyle\implies(\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2})u^{B}_{ij}

\displaystyle=0

Suppose that
\lambda_{i}
are sorted in decreasing order such that there are blocks of equal values
(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{1}),(\mu_{2},...,\mu_{2})...,
(\mu_{d},...,\mu_{d})
.
The set of indices for which
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
, we must have
u^{B}_{ij}=0
. For set of indices such that
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j}
, the square sub-matrix formed by taking the corresponding columns and rows is unitary, that is,
U_{B}
must be a block diagonal where each block is unitary.

\displaystyle U_{B}=U_{1}\oplus U_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus U_{d}

where the dimension of
U_{i}
is equal to multiplicity of
\mu_{i}
. It is easy to verify that
U_{A}=U_{B}^{T}
.
∎

Theorem 2
.

In a Schmidt decomposable multipartite state, any local quantum operation done by one party can be simulated by any party at the cost of some local unitary operations.

Proof.

The proof is in line with one given by Lo and Popescu
[
1
]
. For simplicity, we consider the case of tripartite state. Start with the Schmidt decomposition of
|\psi\rangle
.

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle

Let the measurement operators of Alice and Bob are represented by
\{A_{j}\}
and
\{B_{j}\}
respectively. Writing these operators in Schmidt bases of their respective spaces gives

\displaystyle A_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell}A^{j}_{k\ell}|k_{A}\rangle\langle\ell_{A}|

\displaystyle B_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell}B^{j}_{k\ell}|k_{B}\rangle\langle\ell_{B}|

The action of these measurements of
|\psi\rangle
will give

\displaystyle A_{j}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=a\sum_{kl}\lambda_{\ell}A^{j}_{k\ell}|k_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle B_{j}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=b\sum_{kl}\lambda_{\ell}B^{j}_{k\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|k_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle

where
a
and
b
are normalization constants given by

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell}\lambda^{2}_{\ell}|A^{j}_{k\ell}|^{2}

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell}\lambda^{2}_{\ell}|B^{j}_{k\ell}|^{2}

If Bob initiates measurement and Alice tries to simulate, then we can define

\displaystyle A^{j}_{k\ell}=B^{j}_{k\ell}

After the measurement Alice applies the unitary operation
U_{A}:|k_{A}\rangle\to|\ell_{A}\rangle
and Bob applies
U_{B}:|\ell_{B}\rangle\to|k_{B}\rangle
. Other parties do nothing.
∎

Schimdt decomposition gives a lot of advantage in dealing with local operations. Next, we express any tripartite state in the Schmidt bases obtained via bipartitions of the tripartite state
|\psi\rangle
.

Theorem 3
.

Any tripartite state
|\psi\rangle
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell,m,n}a_{\ell mn}|\ell_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle|n_{C}\rangle

(1)

where
|\ell_{A}\rangle,|m_{B}\rangle,|n_{C}\rangle
are Schmidt vectors in the Schmidt decomposition of bipartitions
A-BC,B-AC
and
AB-C
respectively.

Proof.

We obtain the Schmidt bases
|\ell_{A}\rangle,|m_{B}\rangle
and
|n_{C}\rangle
by considering Schmidt decompositions of bipartitions
A-BC,B-AC
and
AB-C
respectively.

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}^{r_{A}}\alpha_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{BC}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}^{r_{B}}\beta_{m}|m_{B}\rangle|m_{AC}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}^{r_{C}}\gamma_{n}|n_{AB}\rangle|n_{C}\rangle

Without loss of generality assume that
r_{A}\leq r_{B}r_{C}
. Let
V_{BC}=span(\{|\ell_{BC}\rangle\})
. Then
dim(V_{BC})=r_{A}
is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}
. Similarly
span(\{|m_{B}n_{C}\rangle\})
is a subspace of dimension
r_{B}r_{C}
. Since these basis sets are orthonormal, they can be extended to full basis and can be related via a unitary transformation. This implies that the states
|\ell_{BC}\rangle
can be written in the linear combination of states
|m_{B}n_{C}\rangle
. This implies that we can write

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell,m,n}a_{\ell mn}|\ell_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle|n_{C}\rangle

∎

The following lemma relates the reduced density matrices of individual parties with the Schmidt bases.

Lemma 1
.

If
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell mn}a_{\ell mn}|\ell_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle|n_{C}\rangle
where
|\ell_{A}\rangle,|m_{B}\rangle
and
|n_{C}\rangle
are Schmidt bases, then the following holds

1.

\rho_{A}=\sum_{\ell}\alpha_{\ell}^{2}|\ell_{A}\rangle\langle\ell_{A}|

2.

AA^{\dagger}=D_{A}
where
A_{\ell,mn}=a_{\ell mn}
and
D_{A}
is diagonal with coefficients
\alpha_{\ell}^{2}

Similar relations hold for other bipartitions.

Proof.

Considering the Schmidt decomposition of bipartition
A-BC
we get

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\alpha_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{BC}\rangle

Tracing out
|\ell_{BC}\rangle
gives

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\alpha_{\ell}^{2}|\ell_{A}\rangle\langle\ell_{A}|

(2)

Starting out with
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell mn}a_{\ell mn}|\ell_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle|n_{C}\rangle
, it is easy to verify that the matrix representation is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=AA^{\dagger}

(3)

where the matrix
A
is obtained such that
[A]_{\ell,mn}=a_{\ell mn}
. Since we have used same basis in Equation
2
and Equation
3
, we must have that

\displaystyle AA^{\dagger}=D_{A}

where
D_{A}
is diagonal with diagonal entries given by
\alpha_{\ell}^{2}
.
∎

Now we consider the following situation for tripartite systems of Alice, Bob and Cat. Bob makes a measurement
M_{j}
on his system and communicates the results to Alice and Cat. Can Alice make a measurement
L_{j}
instead such that up to local unitary operations, we end up with the same state, i.e. Alice simulates operations of Bob?

Theorem 4
.

For a tripartite system, given a set of complete measurement operators
\{M_{j}\}
of Bob, Alice can simulate
\{M_{j}\}
if there exists complete measurement operators
\{L_{j}\}
such that

\displaystyle fL_{j}=h[M_{j}A^{T_{AB}}]^{T_{AB}}A^{\dagger}D_{A}^{-1}

(4)

where

•

the matrix
A=[a_{\ell,mn}]

•

the matrix
D_{A}
is diagonal matrix of eigenvalues of
\rho_{A}

•

the matrix
A^{T_{AB}}
is obtained by taking partial transpose of indices for Alice and Bob

•

h^{-2}=\sum_{km}\beta_{m}^{2}|M^{j}_{km}|^{2}
is normalization factor after taking measurement
M_{j}
where
\beta_{m}^{2}
are eigenvalues of
\rho_{B}

•

f^{-2}=\sum_{d\ell}\alpha_{\ell}^{2}|L^{j}_{d\ell}|^{2}
is normalization factor after taking measurement
L_{j}
where
\alpha_{\ell}^{2}
are eigenvalues of
\rho_{A}

Proof.

We start by expressing
|\psi\rangle
in Schmidt bases of each subsystem

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell,m,n}a_{\ell mn}|\ell_{A}\rangle|m_{B}\rangle|n_{C}\rangle

The measurement operators are also expressed in these bases as

\displaystyle L_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\ell}L^{j}_{d\ell}|d_{A}\rangle\langle\ell_{A}|

\displaystyle M_{J}

\displaystyle=\sum_{km}M^{j}_{km}|k_{B}\rangle\langle m_{B}|

Applying these measurements to
|\psi\rangle
, we get

\displaystyle L_{j}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=f\sum_{mnd}(\sum_{\ell}L^{j}_{d\ell}a_{\ell mn})|d_{A}m_{B}n_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle M_{j}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=h\sum_{\ell kn}(\sum_{m}M^{j}_{km}a_{\ell mn})|\ell_{A}k_{B}n_{C}\rangle

where
f
and
h
are normalization constants. Relabeling
d\to\ell
and
m\to k
we can rewrite
L_{j}|\psi\rangle
as

\displaystyle L_{j}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=f\sum_{\ell kn}(\sum_{p}L^{j}_{\ell p}a_{pkn})|\ell_{A}k_{B}n_{C}\rangle

Equating coefficients implies

\displaystyle f\sum_{p}L^{j}_{\ell p}a_{p\ell n}

\displaystyle=h\sum_{m}M^{j}_{km}a_{\ell mn}

(5)

\displaystyle f[L_{j}A]_{\ell,kn}

\displaystyle=h[M_{j}B]_{k,\ell n}

(6)

where matrix
A
is of dimension
r_{A}\times r_{B}r_{C}
and is obtained by considering

\displaystyle[A]_{\ell,mn}=a_{\ell mn}

The matrix
B
is of dimension
r_{B}\times r_{A}r_{C}
and is obtained by considering

\displaystyle[B]_{m,\ell n}=a_{\ell mn}

That is,
B
can be obtained by taking partial transpose of
A
by swapping indices
\ell\leftrightarrow m

\displaystyle B=A^{T_{AB}}

Returning back to Equation
5
, we can write

\displaystyle fL_{j}A

\displaystyle=h(M_{j}B)^{T_{AB}}

\displaystyle fL_{j}AA^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=h(M_{j}B)^{T_{AB}}A^{\dagger}

\displaystyle fL_{j}D_{A}

\displaystyle=h(M_{j}B)^{T_{AB}}A^{\dagger}

\displaystyle fL_{j}

\displaystyle=h(M_{j}B)^{T_{AB}}A^{\dagger}D_{A}^{-1}

\displaystyle=h(M_{j}A^{T_{AB}})^{T_{AB}}A^{\dagger}D_{A}^{-1}

where
D_{A}
is the matrix of diagonal of eigenvalues of
\rho_{A}
.

All that remains now is to obtain the expressions for the normalization factors
f
and
h
. As seen above, the coefficients in
L_{j}|\psi\rangle
are given by
f[L_{j}A]_{\ell,kn}
. Using the normalization condition, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell mn}f^{2}[L_{j}A]_{\ell,kn}[L_{j}A]^{*}_{\ell,kn}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle f^{2}\sum_{\ell mn}[L_{j}A]_{\ell,kn}[L_{j}A]^{\dagger}_{kn,\ell}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle f^{2}\mathsf{Tr}(L_{j}AA^{\dagger}L_{j}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle f^{2}\mathsf{Tr}(L_{J}D_{A}L_{j}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle f^{2}\sum_{d\ell}\alpha^{2}_{\ell}|L_{d\ell}^{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=1

Similarly, we can obtain the normalization constant
h
as

\displaystyle h^{2}\sum_{km}\beta^{2}_{m}|M^{j}_{km}|^{2}

\displaystyle=1

∎

We conclude that in general unitary operations can not be simulated even in bipartite systems, even though it is known that measurements can be simulated (
[
1
]
). Schmidt decomposable multipartite states allow simulation of arbitrary local operations. We obtain condition for when arbitrary tripartite states allow simulation of measurements.

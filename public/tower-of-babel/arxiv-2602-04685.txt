Abstract
In the first part of this article we present a growth condition on the potential q q in the Schrödinger operator H = − Δ + q ⁡ ( x ) H=-\Delta+q(x) in L 2 ​ ( ℝ n ) \mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right) that implies Rosen inequalities for the ground state φ \varphi of H H , i.e. ∀ ε > 0 ​ ∃ γ ⁡ ( ε ) > 0 : − ln ⁡ ( φ ⁡ ( x ) ) ≤ ε ​ q ​ ( x ) + γ ⁡ ( ε ) . \forall\varepsilon>0\exists\gamma(\varepsilon)>0\ :\ -\ln\left(\varphi(x)\right)\leq\varepsilon q(x)+\gamma(\varepsilon). While these inequalities are not particularly interesting in themselves, they offer Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities which are absolutely essential to prove an intrinsic ultracontractivity of the associated Schrödinger semigroup e − t ​ H \mathrm{e}^{-tH} , i.e. ∀ t > 0 ​ ∃ C t > 0 : | e − t ​ H ​ u ​ ( x ) | ≤ C t ​ φ ​ ( x ) ​ ‖ u ‖ 2 \forall t>0\exists C_{t}>0\ :\ \left|\mathrm{e}^{-tH}u(x)\right|\ \leq\ C_{t}\varphi(x)\|u\|_{2} holds for every u ∈ L 2 ​ ( ℝ n ) u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right) almost everywhere in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} which we prove in the second part of this article. For proving Rosen inequalities we focus on solving a radial Schrödinger inequality and use Agmon’s version of the comparison principle and Young’s inequality for increasing functions . We follow the classic method proving intrinsic ultracontractivity of e − t ​ H \mathrm{e}^{-tH} by using weighted Sobolev function spaces , weighted Schrödinger semigroups and Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities .

Introduction

1.1
Intrinsic ultracontractivity in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

The Hamiltonian corresponding to a quantum system is given by the formal operator

H=-\Delta+q(x)

(1)

in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
n\geq 3
dimensions with
q\in\mathrm{L}^{1}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. We focus on
potentials
q
being non-negative almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that there exists a unique strictly positive
eigenfunction
\varphi
corresponding to the lowest eigenvalue
E_{0}
of
H
which is the system’s
ground state
.
We present a growth condition of
q
which implies
Rosen inequalities
, i.e.

\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\gamma(\varepsilon)>0\ :\ -\ln\left(\varphi(x)\right)\ \leq\ \varepsilon q(x)+\gamma(\varepsilon)

(2)

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. These are not particularly interesting in themselves, but imply

Logarithmic Sobolev inequalties
which are absolutely essential to prove

intrinsic ultracontractivity
of the Schrödinger semigroup
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}
, i.e.

\forall t>0\exists C_{t}>0\ :\ \left|\mathrm{e}^{-tH}u(x)\right|\ \leq\ C_{t}\varphi(x)\|u\|_{2}

(3)

for every
u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this case the asymptotic behaviour
of
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}u
is dominated by the ground state
\varphi
at every time
t>0
. In particular, for a normed
eigenfunction
v
of
H
we conclude to

\exists C_{\lambda}>0\ :\ |v(x)|\leq C_{\lambda}\varphi(x)

(4)

almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
\lambda
is the associated eigenvalue of
v
. Mind that in quantum physics
eigenfunctions are characterized as a probability density function of an electron’s position at a certain energy level

\lambda
of the system
H
.
But apart from quantum mechanics we can interpret intrinsic ultracontractivity as perturbation result of the generator
-\Delta

of the free Schrödinger semigroup given by

\mathrm{e}^{t\Delta}u(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}u(y)\ \mathrm{d}y

(5)

for
u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Due to the Gaussian integral kernel
\mathrm{e}^{t\Delta}
is
a contraction in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
p\in[1,\infty]
and further maps
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into

\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
continuously for
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
.
For the case of
H=-\Delta+q(x)
we cite the following characterization of
intrinsic ultracontractivity as Lemma 4.2.2 from page 110 in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

The Schrödinger semigroup
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}
is intrinsic ultracontractive if and only if both of the following

i)

\mathrm{e}^{-tH}u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ k(t,x,y)u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

for

\exists C_{t}>0\ :\ 0\leq k(t,x,y)<C_{t}\ \varphi(x)\ \varphi(y)
almost everywhere in

4

\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

However, in
[
4

\mathrm{e}^{-tH}

Even more examples are given in
[
5
]
which provides

For bounded domains

with a sufficiently smooth boundary Banuelos
[
3

proved

In the 2000s B. Alziary and P. Takáč returned to the original studies of intrinsic ultracontractivity

Definitions and preparations for Rosen inequalities

We consider dimensions
n\geq 3
and write
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

for the set of complex-valued and measurable functions
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x<\infty
.
For real-valued functions in
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
we explicitly write
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}\right)
.
Throughout this article the Schödinger potential
q\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and
non-negative almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
q(x)\to\infty
for
|x|\to\infty
.

We define a subspace
D(h)
as the form domain of

by

D(h)=\left\{u\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\ |\ q^{\frac{1}{2}}u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\}\subset\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

(9)

where

denotes the Sobolev space of weakly differentiable functions in

\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

whose weak derivatives are contained in
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. Please mind that

C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\subseteq D(h)
is satisfied.
For
u,v\in D(h)
we define a sesquilinear form
h
by

h(u,v)=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\partial_{j}u,\partial_{j}v\rangle\right\}+\langle q^{\frac{1}{2}}u,q^{\frac{1}{2}}v\rangle

(10)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
The form
h
induces a norm
\|u\|_{h}=\sqrt{\langle u,u\rangle_{h}}
with

\langle u,v\rangle_{h}=h(u,v)+\langle u,v\rangle
for
u,v\in D(h)
which turns
D(h)
into a Hilbert space.
A
Schrödinger operator

is defined as the associated operator to

with a domain

given by

D(H)=\left\{u\in D(h)\ |\ \exists v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\ :\ h(u,w)=\langle v,w\rangle\ \text{for every }w\in D(h)\right\}.

(11)

Hence

satisfies the equation

\langle Hu,w\rangle=h(u,w)

(12)

for

and every

. Furthermore,

is self-adjoint in
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

such that
(0,\infty)
is contained in the resolvent set
\rho(H)
. The latter is shown by the use of Riesz’s representation
theorem using the completeness of

with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{h}
.
The Lumer-Phillips theorem infers that
-H
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup

\left\{\ \mathrm{e}^{-tH}\ |\ t\geq 0\ \right\}

of contractions in
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
which we call a

in

\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. Furthermore the spectrum

\sigma(H)
consists only of eigenvalues with a
ground state energy

being simple
and a corresponding eigenfunction
\varphi
being strictly positive almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We call
\varphi
the

of
H
. Now, let us show that
\varphi
is a continuous function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

.

of

Weighted

-spaces and weighted Schrödinger Semigroups

We follow B. Davies in Section 4.2 on page 111 in
[

]
by introducing weighted Lebesgue

\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p\in[1,\infty)
and weighted Schrödinger semigroups
on
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For any Borel set

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define a probability measure

\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\mu(B)=\int_{B}\varphi(x)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\in[0,1]

and for every

we denote

as the set of measurable functions

that satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)|^{p}\ \varphi(x)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Furthermore,
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is justified since
Lebesgue null sets with respect to
\mu
coincide with null sets to the regular Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

as
\varphi
is strictly positive in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Setting

\|u\|_{p,\mu}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
turns
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into Banach spaces. For

u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we define a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions in
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u(x)=\frac{1}{\varphi(x)}\mathrm{e}^{-tH}(\varphi u)(x)

(22)

which we call the
weighted Schrödinger semigroup
of
H
. The generator is given by

-\tilde{H}u\ =\ -\frac{1}{\varphi}H(\varphi u)

(23)

for

where we use a sloppy
notation by writing
\varphi^{-1}D\left(H\right)
for the set of products of
\varphi^{-1}
with every function of
the domain

.

Theorem 5.3
.

i.)

For every
t>0
the operator
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
is a contraction in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
.

ii.)

For every
t>0
the operator
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
is a contraction in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
.

iii.)

For every
t>0
and

the operator
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
is a contraction in

\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})

Proof.

i.)

Let
t>0
be arbitrary but fixed.

a)

Let
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})\subseteq\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
be non-negative almost everywhere
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. So we reason that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\ (x)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\ (x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{-tH}\varphi u,\varphi\right\rangle=\left\langle\varphi u,\mathrm{e}^{-tH}\varphi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\varphi u,\mathrm{e}^{-tE_{0}}\varphi\right\rangle=\mathrm{e}^{-tE_{0}}\left\langle\varphi u,\varphi\right\rangle\leq\|u\|_{1,\mu}

is true where we used
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}\varphi=\mathrm{e}^{-tE_{0}}\varphi
and

.

b)

For
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
we denote the positive and negative part of

by
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
respectively with

. Using a) we end up with

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u\ (x)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u^{+}\ (x)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u^{-}\ (x)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

c)

Now let
u\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
. We define a sequence

u_{k}=1_{\{u\leq k\}}\ u
for

. Mind that

is a bounded function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and therefore is contained in

since

is a probability measure. Furthermore

converges to

in

\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

is true as well. So using b) we conclude that

(\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}u_{k})
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence there
exists a unique and bounded extension of
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{H}}
to
\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that
proves the claim.

ii.)

Let
t>0
be chosen arbitrary but fixed and

almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\mathrm{e}^{-tH}\left(\varphi\|u\|_{\infty}-\varphi u\right)(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-tH}\left(\varphi\|u\|_{\infty}\right)(x)-\mathrm{e}^{-tH}\left(\varphi u\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-tE_{0}}\varphi(x)-\mathrm{e}^{-tH}\left(\varphi u\right)(x)

is implied for almost every

which gives

for the weighted Schrödinger semigroup

. For

iii.)

Using i.) and ii.) the claim is implied by the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem.

An enhanced RT-DETR with frequency decoupling and orthogonal regularization for UAV infrared small target detection
Authors: Pan Xiao, Huiying Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryInfrared small target detection in unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) imagery is pivotal for low-light surveillance. However, existing frameworks suffer from feature redundancy, suboptimal context modeling, and gradient vanishing during tiny target localization. To address these bottlenecks, we propose an enhanced real-time detection transformer tailored for drone-based infrared scenarios. First, the Ortho-Block module leverages orthogonal regularization to eliminate redundancy and purify target representations from background clutter. Second, the AIFI-HiLo module decouples scene semantics via high- and low-frequency attention within intra-scale interactions to capture dense targets. Furthermore, a dual-stream GLSA mechanism with lightweight deformable convolutions adapts to extreme scale variations. Finally, the NWD-SIoU loss introduces a dynamic scaling factor to provide smoother gradients and accelerate localization convergence. Experimental results on HIT-UAV (Harbin Institute of Technology Unmanned Aerial Vehicle dataset) show a 4.8% mAP50 improvement over the RT-DETR baseline, while evaluations on VisDrone2019 confirm robust cross-scene adaptability.Keywords: infrared small target detection, unmanned aerial vehicle, UAV, real-time detection transformer, RT-DETR, orthogonal regularization, multi-scale feature fusion

Introduction

Infrared imaging, renowned for its robust anti-interference capabilities, long-distance detection range, and all-weather operability, plays a pivotal role in critical fields such as military reconnaissance,1,2 fire monitoring,3,4 and security surveillance,5,6 especially in target detection for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs).7 Nevertheless, infrared images often suffer from inherent drawbacks: low contrast, faint texture details, and multi-scale occlusion problems.8,9,10 Small targets (less than 32 × 32 pixels) are particularly vulnerable to being masked by complex backgrounds, severely hampering detection accuracy. Traditional methods, such as spectral residuals11 and infrared patch tensors,12 have achieved notable advancements in salient image generation and low signal-to-noise ratio processing. However, they fall short when dealing with small, occluded targets in intricate scenes.13,14 With the rise of deep learning, detectors such as Faster R-CNN,15 YOLO (you only look once),16 and SSD17 (Single Shot MultiBox Detector)have significantly boosted detection performance. Detection transformer (DETR)18 introduced the transformer mechanism to achieve end-to-end global modeling, and real-time DETR (RT-DETR)19 further optimized the balance between accuracy and speed, expanding their application scope in infrared detection. Despite these achievements, existing infrared object detection techniques still grapple with challenges: detecting extremely tiny objects, handling densely packed multiple objects, and deploying on resource-constrained platforms. Recent advancements have further pushed the boundaries of aerial detection. For instance, Zhang et al.20 proposed CCSFuse, which utilizes a collaborative compensation and selective fusion strategy to bridge the information gap between RGB (Red-Green-Blue) and IR (Infrared) modalities. Similarly, Wang et al.21 introduced MROD-YOLO (Multimodal Joint Representation for Small Object Detection in Remote Sensing Imagery via Multi-Scale Iterative Aggregation), leveraging multiscale iterative aggregation to improve the detection accuracy of tiny objects in complex remote sensing scenes. While these methods demonstrate excellent performance in multimodal or general remote sensing tasks, our work specifically addresses the challenges of thermal noise suppression and feature decorrelation in single-modality infrared UAV surveillance. ISTD-CenterNet, proposed by Li et al.,22 improves performance based on HRNet but struggles with ultra-small targets and dense scenes. Wu et al.23 proposed CDYL (CDYL for infrared and visible light image dense small object detection), which fuses visible and infrared images for better small target detection but incurs high computational costs and has poor real-time performance, along with unstable performance under extreme weather conditions. Jing et al.24 extracted features from ground-based infrared videos using YOLO, effectively improving target perception capabilities, but this approach relies heavily on a large amount of labeled data and has limited stability in dynamic scenes. Zhu et al.25 improved detection accuracy by introducing a small object detection head and a transformer structure, but this significantly increased model complexity, affecting deployment efficiency. Zhao et al.26 improved the YOLOv7 backbone network based on the lightweight MobileViT to enhance local and global perception, but the transformer structure has high computational requirements, limiting its real-time application potential. In remote sensing, capturing intricate spatial-spectral relationships remains a significant challenge. Recent studies have explored graph-based and self-supervised architectures to enhance feature discriminability. For instance, Ding et al.27 proposed a lightweight self-supervised contrastive graph clustering network (SLCGC), which effectively suppresses high-frequency noise through low-pass filtering mechanisms. Similarly, a work by Ding et al.28 introduced an adaptive homophily clustering framework that leverages structure-aware graph learning to handle complex image distributions. Furthermore, to address the locality preservation in large-scale imagery, Zhang et al.29 developed a self-supervised low-pass GCN (Graph Convolutional Network) embedding method. Inspired by these advanced filtering and locality-preserving strategies, our proposed GLDO-RTDETR (this study) further optimizes the feature alignment for infrared small targets. Unlike the graph-based clustering approaches in two works by Ding et al.27,28 that focus on global pixel grouping, our AIFI-HiLo module specifically targets the scale-internal interactions, striking a superior balance between computational efficiency and local detail preservation in noisy infrared backgrounds. By applying orthogonal regularization, this module suppresses redundant information in the latent space, significantly enhancing the model’s ability to distinguish weak target signals from heavy environmental thermal noise.

Related works

Drone infrared detection based on the YOLO series

The YOLO family of algorithms has been extensively applied to infrared target detection in drone imagery, owing to its end-to-end architecture and high detection efficiency. Infrared images typically present challenges such as low contrast, high noise levels, and complex backgrounds. Despite these difficulties, YOLO-based models have demonstrated strong adaptability through multi-scale feature extraction and robust real-time performance. Early research predominantly focused on YOLOv3, which enhanced the detection accuracy of small targets by incorporating attention mechanisms such as CBAM (Convolutional Block Attention Module) and SE.30,31 YOLOv4 further advanced the balance between accuracy and speed by integrating the feature pyramid network (FPN), thereby improving multi-scale representation capabilities.32 With its lightweight architecture and efficient training strategies, YOLOv5 became particularly well-suited for deployment on embedded platforms. When augmented with multi-task learning and weighted box loss functions, it showed improved performance in infrared detection scenarios.33,34 YOLOv7 introduced the E-ELAN module, which significantly boosted the network’s feature learning capacity. Building on this, YOLOv8 brought substantial improvements to global feature modeling by refining both the backbone and prediction heads.35 The integration of transformer-based components, particularly deformable attention modules, further enhanced YOLOv8’s adaptability to target deformation in complex infrared environments.36 Moreover, hybrid architecture combining YOLO with DETR has demonstrated strong multi-scale detection performance and resilience to low signal-to-noise ratios in infrared imagery.37 In parallel, the integration of traditional image processing techniques has emerged as a promising approach to mitigate occlusion and background interference, offering another path toward improved performance in infrared small target detection.38,39

UAV infrared detection based on transformer

In recent years, transformer-based architecture has demonstrated significant potential in infrared drone target detection, primarily due to its strong global modeling capabilities and self-attention mechanisms. These strengths are especially valuable in challenging scenarios characterized by complex backgrounds, high noise levels, and low contrast.18 DETR was the first end-to-end object detection model to incorporate a transformer. While DETR has shown strong performance in the visible spectrum, it suffers from limitations such as slow convergence and reduced accuracy when applied to infrared small target detection.18 To address these issues, deformable DETR introduced a deformable attention mechanism, which significantly improves the model’s ability to focus on salient regions within the image.40 The Swin transformer advanced the field further by implementing a hierarchical sliding window approach that enables multi-scale feature modeling, thereby striking a balance between accuracy and computational efficiency.41 Building on this, Li et al. proposed a multi-scale transformer integrated with background suppression techniques, which effectively enhanced detection robustness in high-noise environments.42 Hybrid architectures that combine convolutional networks with transformers have also emerged, offering improved detection accuracy while maintaining a lightweight design suitable for deployment on resource-constrained platforms.43 Novel approaches such as OrthoNets further enhance robustness by applying orthogonal constraints to optimize feature representations,44 while the deformable attention transformer leverages a dynamic receptive field to adaptively handle variations in target scale and density.45 At the data level, generative models such as GANs have been employed to synthesize infrared training samples, and cross-modal pretraining through models such as ViT has contributed to improved generalization capabilities.46,47 Despite the promise of transformer-based methods in infrared target detection, their high computational cost remains a significant limitation. Future research should, therefore, focus on integrating lightweight design principles with domain-specific infrared knowledge to develop efficient and robust detection systems for drone-based applications.48

Data augmentation strategy

To enhance the robustness and generalization of infrared UAV target detection models against complex backgrounds, low contrast, and dynamic scene variations, this paper implements a comprehensive online data augmentation pipeline leveraging the Albumentations library. This strategy generates diverse training samples through real-time, randomized operations categorized into five functional dimensions: (1) contrast and visibility enhancement, which utilizes dynamic brightness adjustments and contrast limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) to improve target-background separability; (2) environmental and weather simulation, which introduces synthetic rain, snow, and fog to strengthen adaptability to degraded field conditions; (3) sensor noise and motion degradation, incorporating Gaussian noise, motion blur, and sensor-specific noise to force the extraction of noise-resilient features despite hardware limitations; (4) geometric and spatial diversity, employing rotation, scaling, translation, and flipping to ensure invariance across diverse target poses and flight altitudes; and (5) scale and sample expansion, using random cropping and image compression to enhance stability when processing multi-resolution inputs. Collectively, these targeted augmentations significantly enrich the training distribution, enabling the model to maintain high detection stability in complex, real-world infrared environments.

Ablation experiment

To evaluate the effectiveness of each enhanced module in the GLDO-RTDETR model, an ablation study was conducted based on the RT-DETR framework. The performance was assessed by incrementally introducing each component to isolate its impact. Specifically, the enhancements include: (1) replacing the BasicBlock in the backbone with the Ortho-Block module to improve computational efficiency; (2) incorporating the AIFI (Attention-based Intrascale Feature Interaction) module, which integrates the HiLo efficient attention mechanism, to strengthen the model’s ability to detect small targets through enhanced scale-aware feature interaction; (3) introducing the GLDC-Neck, a dynamic and efficient feature fusion network, to optimize the hybrid encoding process; and (4) updating the loss function to NWD-SIoU to improve regression precision. The baseline RT-DETR model (denoted as “base”) served as the control, and the four modules—O (Ortho-Block), H (AIFI-HiLo), G (GLDC-Neck), and N (NWD-SIoU)—were progressively added for systematic ablation testing. Quantitative analyses were conducted to assess the contribution of each module to detection accuracy, computational efficiency, and robustness. The results are summarized in Table 4.

As presented in Table 4, the incremental impact of each module within the proposed framework was systematically evaluated. Method 2 introduces only the Ortho-Block (O module), resulting in a mAP50 improvement from 80.1% to 81.7% and a 1.1 percentage point increase in mAP50:95. Concurrently, the parameter count is reduced from 19.96M to 19.39M, albeit with a slight decrease in inference speed. These results indicate that the O module contributes both structural optimization and lightweight characteristics while effectively enhancing detection performance. Method 3 integrates the AIFI-HiLo (H) module, achieving a moderate increase in mAP50 to 81.1% without introducing any additional parameters or computational burden. This demonstrates its utility as a lightweight yet effective component for feature enhancement. In method 4, the GLSA attention module (G) yields the most pronounced performance gain among single-module configurations, increasing mAP50 to 82.3% and mAP50:95 to 53.1%. Interestingly, despite a rise in parameter count, inference speed improves—likely due to the G module’s architectural alignment with hardware-level parallelism. Method 5 incorporates the NWD-SIoU loss (N module), further enhancing mAP50 to 82.8%, achieving the highest accuracy among single-module variants aside from the full model. However, no improvement in inference speed is observed, suggesting its contribution lies primarily in enhancing representation learning and localization precision. When combining modules, a trend of synergistic enhancement becomes evident. Method 6, integrating both the O and H modules, raises mAP50 to 82.4%, though at the expense of reduced frames per second (FPS). Adding the G module in method 7 further improves mAP50 to 83.1% and mAP50:95 to 53.2%, reinforcing the pivotal role of global-local semantic fusion in multi-module configurations. Ultimately, the complete model (this study), which incorporates all four modules (O, H, G, and N), achieves the highest performance across all evaluation metrics: mAP50 reaches 84.9%, mAP50:95 rises to 53.6%, the F1 score improves to 83.0%, and inference speed remains favorable at 98.1 FPS. These results confirm the strong individual contributions of the proposed modules as well as their high compatibility and cumulative effectiveness. The integrated architecture offers a compelling balance of detection accuracy and computational efficiency, making it particularly suitable for deployment in real-time, resource-constrained UAV-based infrared detection scenarios.

Discussion

Conclusion

This study introduces GLDO-RTDETR, a robust real-time transformer framework optimized for UAV-based infrared small-target detection. The primary innovation lies in its systematic resolution of feature redundancy, semantic aliasing, and localization sensitivity through four targeted advancements. First, we propose the Ortho-Block, which utilizes orthogonal regularization to decorrelate target signals from thermal background noise, effectively purifying feature representations at the backbone level. Second, the AIFI-HiLo module is developed to decouple high- and low-frequency attention, enabling the model to simultaneously capture fine-grained edge details and global context in dense target clusters. Third, the GLDC-Neck, integrated with LDConv, adaptively models geometric deformations caused by UAV altitude variations while maintaining a low computational footprint. Finally, the introduction of the NWD-SIoU loss with a dynamic scaling factor bridges the optimization gap for sub-pixel targets, ensuring stable gradients and superior regression accuracy where traditional IoU losses fail. Comprehensive experiments on HIT-UAV and VisDrone2019 datasets verify the progressiveness further to assess the model’s generalizability and practical deployment potential TR while sustaining real-time inference speeds. These results confirm that GLDO-RTDETR offers a high-performance, scalable solution for autonomous thermal inspection and search-and-rescue missions. Future research will explore cross-domain generalization and edge-platform deployment. Detailed methodological visualizations and supplementary detection results across various complex scenarios are further elaborated in the methods section (see Figures 7, 9, 10, 11 and 12).

Limitations of the study

While the proposed GLDO-RTDETR framework demonstrates significant advancements in infrared small target detection, the analysis of failure cases provides critical insights for future research directions. To overcome the inherent physical limitations of single-mode infrared sensors—particularly the “thermal crossover” phenomenon, where target and background temperatures equalize—we envision a transition toward visible-infrared multi-spectral fusion. By integrating textural cues from the visible spectrum, the model can maintain robust perception even when thermal signatures vanish. Furthermore, to address localization inaccuracies under severe occlusion or dense canopy coverage, our future work will pivot from static-frame analysis to temporal-spatial reasoning. By leveraging video-based sequential detection, the framework can utilize temporal continuity to track or “hallucinate” occluded targets based on historical trajectories. Finally, to resolve the persistent ambiguity between small targets and high-intensity thermal clutter, we propose exploring knowledge distillation from large vision models (LVMs). Distilling rich semantic priors from these foundational models will empower the network to better distinguish true targets from thermal noise such as vents or heat reflections, ultimately leading to a more reliable and context-aware UAV detection system.

Method details

To enhance the detection performance of small targets in UAV-based infrared imagery under complex scene conditions, this paper proposes a lightweight and efficient model named GLDO-RTDETR (as illustrated in Figure 1). The model incorporates the OrthoNets architecture,44,51 which recalibrates channel-wise features to suppress redundant information and strengthen feature representation. To further optimize backbone feature extraction, the proposed Ortho-Block module enhances the efficiency of feature utilization by improving structural expressiveness and redundancy. In the Transformer encoder, the HiLo mechanism52 is integrated to construct the AIFI-HiLo module, which effectively balances global context modeling with the capture of fine-grained local structures. For the feature fusion stage, a novel GLDC-Neck structure is introduced. This module integrates the GLSA two-stream attention mechanism53 to merge local and global semantic features. Additionally, it incorporates LDConv54 and gConv55 to enable high-response, low-computation feature enhancement, crucial for improving the perceptual sensitivity to small targets. In terms of regression loss design, a new NWD-SIoU loss function is formulated by combining the NWD-IoU56 and SIoU losses with an adaptive scaling factor. This hybrid loss improves both localization accuracy and convergence speed, particularly for extremely small targets. Overall, the proposed architecture significantly reduces computational overhead while maintaining high detection accuracy, making it well-suited for deployment on resource-constrained platforms such as embedded UAV systems.

Feature Purification via Ortho-Block

In UAV-based infrared imagery, faint target signatures are often obscured by complex thermal noise, leading to severe feature redundancy and high inter-channel correlation. Standard convolutional blocks often lack the discriminative power to decouple these weak signals from pervasive background clutter. To mitigate this, we propose the Ortho-Block, a novel module that integrates a channel attention mechanism anchored by orthogonal projection. By enforcing an orthogonal constraint, this mechanism effectively purifies feature representations, decorrelates redundant information, and enhances the signal-to-clutter ratio (SCR) for robust target extraction.

Motivation for orthogonal regularization

The primary motivation for introducing orthogonality is to minimize information redundancy across feature channels. In infrared scenes, redundant channels often represent repetitive environmental noise. By projecting feature vectors into an orthogonal subspace, we ensure that the attention mechanism focuses on statistically independent and salient components, effectively “filtering” the background interference.

LDConv module

Traditional convolution operations are limited to local windows, cannot capture information at other locations, and have a fixed sampling shape. The focus of LDConv is its unique coordinate generation algorithm, which can generate initial sampling positions for convolution kernels of any size, allowing convolution kernels to have any number of parameters and sampling shapes to adapt to changes in target shape. gConvC3 module

Given the high computational complexity of the traditional RepC3 module and the limited multi-scale feature extraction and feature fusion capabilities, the gConvC3 module is proposed. This module combines residual learning and a gating mechanism, aims to enhance the network’s expressiveness by introducing a gating mechanism, and replaces the traditional pixel attention module and nonlinear activation function. As shown in Figure 12.

Specifically, gConv begins by applying Batch Normalization to the input feature map. It then uses a pointwise convolution (PWConv) to generate a gating signal, while a depth wise convolution (DWConv) is used to extract spatial features from the input. The gating signal is subsequently applied to the feature map through a gating mechanism, enabling dynamic feature modulation. Afterward, the modulated features are further projected using another PWConv layer. Finally, this projected output is added to the original input feature map through a residual connection to produce the final output. This design not only enhances the network’s ability to dynamically adjust feature representations but also strengthens its focus on information-rich regions via the gating signal. As a result, gConv achieves high-performance feature processing while maintaining computational efficiency, making it particularly well-suited for lightweight and high-precision image tasks.

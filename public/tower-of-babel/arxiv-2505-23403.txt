Transverse dependence and asymptotic dimensional reduction of ground states for the logarithmic Schrödinger equation on a waveguide manifold
Jun Wang, Hichem Hajaiej, Zhaoyang Yin

Abstract
In this paper, we develop a novel approach to studying the y y -dependence of ground states for the logarithmic Schrödinger equation on ℝ d × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T} . Unlike in [ 24 ] (Analysis & PDE, 2014) and [ 11 ] (SIAM J. Math. Anal., 2024), the power-law scaling argument does not transfer directly to the logarithmic setting. Instead, amplitude scaling gives an exact identity showing that transverse dependence is independent of the prescribed mass. We also discuss uniqueness for the Cauchy problem and conditional orbital stability.

Keywords:
Logarithmic Schrödinger equation; Normalized solutions; Variational methods; Waveguide manifold.

Introduction and main results

This paper studies some properties of the solutions of the following logarithmic Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{x,y}u+\lambda u=u\log u^{2},\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}u^{2}dxdy=\Theta^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where the mass
\Theta>0
,
\Delta_{x,y}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}
,
\Delta_{y}
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{T}
, and the frequency
\lambda
is unknown and must be determined. In recent years, the logarithmic Schrödinger equation has received considerable attention. This class of
equations has some important physical applications in quantum mechanics, quantum optics, nuclear
physics, transport and diffusion phenomena, open quantum systems, effective quantum gravity, theory of
superfluidity, and Bose–Einstein condensation; see
[
29
]
and the references therein. On the whole space, the logarithmic Schrödinger equation has received considerable attention because of its applications in various fields; see
[
4
]
for a more detailed account. The main challenges arise from the fact that the nonlinearity in the energy functional is subcritical and that the subspace of radial functions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
does not embed compactly into
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore, classical variational approaches cannot directly establish the compactness of a minimizing sequence. Additionally, the functional is not smooth in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. To the best of our knowledge, the first rigorous mathematical study of (
1.1
) on the whole space was undertaken in
[
5
]
. This opened the door to many other contributions, such as
[
9
]
. More recently, new developments have addressed more general sublinear nonlinearities; see
[
20
]
and
[
10
]
. Systems with critical exponents were studied in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
. Various aspects of the Cauchy problem have been addressed by Carles et al. in several papers; see, for example,
[
6
]
.

Recently, there has been increasing interest in studying dispersive equations on the waveguide manifolds
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}
, whose mixed geometric structure makes the underlying analysis rather challenging and delicate. For the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with a pure-power nonlinearity, there have been many results on waveguide manifolds, as shown in
[
8
,
16
,
18
,
19
,
27
,
28
,
25
,
17
,
26
]
. However, we are not aware of any previous results for the logarithmic Schrödinger equation on waveguide manifolds. Terracini et al. in
[
24
]
considered the Cauchy problem

i\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{x,y}u-u|u|^{\alpha}=0,\ u|_{t=0}=u_{0},

where
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}\times M_{y}^{k}
and
M_{y}^{k}
is a compact Riemannian manifold. They proved that, above a critical mass, the ground states have nontrivial
M_{y}^{k}
-dependence. Subsequently, Hajaiej et al. in
[
11
]
considered the following equation:

\Delta_{x,y}^{2}u-\beta\Delta_{x,y}u+\theta u=|u|^{\alpha}u,\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{n},

where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,\frac{8}{d+n})
. They extended the conclusion in
[
24
]
to a fourth-order Schrödinger equation by using a new scaling argument. It is natural to ask whether the same conclusion holds for Schrödinger equations without scaling structures, such as the Schrödinger–Poisson equation, the Schrödinger equation with a potential, the logarithmic Schrödinger equation, and the Choquard equation. This paper addresses the logarithmic equation by developing a new approach to studying the dependence of ground-state solutions.

The auxiliary functional
\mathcal{I}_{\mu}
is motivated by
[
24
]
.
A product test function proves transverse dependence for small
\mu
.
For large
\mu
, a slice-mass estimate gives convergence of the energy and

\mu\|\partial_{y}u_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}\to 0
. The limit equation is obtained by averaging
over the periodic variable before passing to the limit. These asymptotic
statements do not establish exact independence at a finite parameter.
The natural energy functional associated with the equation (
1.1
) is given by

\mathcal{I}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}(|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{2}+|u|^{2})dxdy-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dxdy.

However,
\mathcal{I}(u)
is not well defined in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T})
since there exist functions
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dxdy=-\infty
, which gives the possibility that
\mathcal{I}(u)=\infty
. In order to handle this issue, we consider a decomposition of the form

F_{2}(s)-F_{1}(s)=\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\log s^{2},\ \forall s\in\mathbb{R},

where
F_{1}\in C^{1}
and
F_{1}
is a nonnegative convex function,
F_{2}\in C^{1}
satisfies Sobolev subcritical growth. This decomposition has been explored in many works (see
[
22
,
1
,
2
]
). More precisely, fix
0<\delta<e^{-3}
and let

F_{1}(s):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,&s=0,\\
&-\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\log s^{2},&0<|s|<\delta,\\
&-\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\left(\log\delta^{2}+3\right)+2\delta|s|-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2},&|s|\geq\delta\end{aligned}\right.

and

F_{2}(s):=\begin{cases}0,&|s|<\delta,\\
\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\log\left(\frac{s^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right)+2\delta|s|-\frac{3}{2}s^{2}-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2},&|s|\geq\delta\end{cases}

for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence,

F_{2}(s)-F_{1}(s)=\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\log s^{2},\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.2)

It is easy to see that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
satisfy the following properties:

(f_{1})

F_{1}
is an even function with
F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s\geq 0
and
F_{1}\geq 0
. Moreover,
F_{1}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
is convex for
\delta\approx 0^{+}
.

(f_{2})

F_{2}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\cap C^{2}((\delta,+\infty),\mathbb{R})
and there exists
C=C_{p}>0
such that

\left|F_{2}^{\prime}(s)\right|\leq C|s|^{p-1},\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R},\ p\in\left(2,2^{*}\right).

(f_{3})

s\mapsto\frac{F_{2}^{\prime}(s)}{s}
is a nondecreasing function for
s>0
and a strictly increasing function for
s>\delta
.

(f_{4})

\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{2}^{\prime}(s)}{s}=\infty
.

This decomposition guarantees that
\mathcal{I}(u)
can be expressed as the sum of a
C^{1}
-functional and a convex, lower semi-continuous functional. In this way, the critical point theory of functionals developed in
[
23
]
can be used to find solutions
of (
1.1
). Following
[
3
]
, we consider the reflexive and separable Orlicz space defined by

L^{F_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T})=\{u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}F_{1}(|u|)dxdy<+\infty\}

The Orlicz norm is the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{F_{1}}}=\inf\{k>0:\int F_{1}(|u|/k)\leq 1\}
.
The explicit
F_{1}
and its complement satisfy global
\Delta_{2}
, as used
in the proof of Lemma
2.5
.
We define
X=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T})\cap L^{F_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T})
with respect to the norm
\|u\|_{X}:=\|u\|_{H^{1}}+\|u\|_{L^{F_{1}}}
. Obviously, the embeddings
X\hookrightarrow H^{1}
and
X\hookrightarrow L^{F_{1}}
are continuous. Therefore, we can rewrite the functional
\mathcal{I}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}[|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{2}+|u|^{2}]dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}F_{1}(u)dxdy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}F_{2}(u)dxdy

and the mass constraint manifold is defined by

S_{\Theta}=\left\{u\in X:\|u\|_{2}=\Theta\right\}.

The main results of this paper concern the minimizers of the minimization problems

m_{\Theta,\mu}=\inf\{\mathcal{I}_{\mu}:u\in S_{\Theta}\},

where

\mathcal{I}_{\mu}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}[|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}+|u|^{2}]dxdy+\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}|\nabla_{y}u|^{2}dxdy-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dxdy.

Here
\mu\in(0,+\infty)
.
By the argument of Theorem
1.1
, with a fixed positive transverse coefficient, there exists
u_{\mu}
such that
\mathcal{I}_{\mu}(u_{\mu})=m_{\Theta,\mu}
. In order to study the
y
-dependence of the ground states, we need some notation on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}
. Define

\widetilde{S}_{\rho}=\left\{u\in X(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}):\|u\|_{2}=\rho\right\}

and

\widetilde{m}_{\rho}=\inf\{\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(u):u\in\widetilde{S}_{\rho}\},

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}+|u|^{2})dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dx.

Theorem 1.1
.

The problem (
1.1
)
has a solution pair
(u,\lambda)
, where
u
is a positive minimizer which, after an
x
-translation, is
radially symmetric and radially nonincreasing in the
x
variable.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the concentration–compactness principle. Next, we state the dependence result.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\Theta>0
, there exists
\mu_{0}(\Theta)>0
such that every
minimizer of
m_{\Theta,\mu}
has nontrivial
y
-dependence whenever

0<\mu<\mu_{0}(\Theta)
. Moreover,

\lim_{\mu\to\infty}m_{\Theta,\mu}=2\pi\widetilde{m}_{\Theta/\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad\lim_{\mu\to\infty}\mu\|\partial_{y}u_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}=0.

The latter assertion is asymptotic and does not imply exact
y
-independence
at a finite value of
\mu
.

Remark 1.1
.

(1) Amplitude scaling gives
m_{\Theta,\mu}=\Theta^{2}m_{1,\mu}-\Theta^{2}\log\Theta
and a bijection between the corresponding minimizer sets. Thus changing
\Theta
cannot change their transverse dependence at fixed
\mu
.

(2) A nonzero finite-mass function cannot be independent of all Euclidean variables. For minimizers, (
3.3
) quantifies this fact.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in C([-T,T];X)
be distributional solutions of
(
4.1
), with time derivatives in
L^{\infty}(-T,T;X^{\prime})
.
Then

\|u_{2}(t)-u_{1}(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq e^{8|t|}\|u_{2}(0)-u_{1}(0)\|_{2}^{2},\qquad|t|\leq T.

In particular, solutions in this class are unique. Existence of a global
conservative flow in
X
is an additional hypothesis of Theorem
1.4
,
not a conclusion of this theorem.

Theorem
1.3
is proved by an
L^{2}
difference estimate. The following stability result is conditional on a global conservative flow.

Theorem 1.4
.

Define
\Gamma_{\Theta}=\{u\in S_{\Theta},\mathcal{I}(u)=m_{\Theta}\}
. Assume that (
4.1
) admits a global continuous flow in the
complex energy space
X
for initial data in a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}

of
\Gamma_{\Theta}
and that this flow conserves mass and
\mathcal{I}
.
Here
F_{j}(u)
and
u^{2}\log u^{2}
in the variational definitions are understood
as
F_{j}(|u|)
and
|u|^{2}\log|u|^{2}
for complex functions, and
\Gamma_{\Theta}

denotes the full set of complex minimizers. Then the set
\Gamma_{\Theta}
is orbitally stable in the sense that for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
some
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that, for any
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{U}
satisfying

\inf_{u\in\Gamma_{\Theta}}\|\psi_{0}-u\|_{X}<\delta,

we have

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\inf_{u\in\Gamma_{\Theta}}\|\psi(t)-u\|_{X}<\varepsilon,

where
\psi
is the global solution of (
4.1
) with
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Radial symmetry in
x
alone does not imply compactness into
L^{2}
.
Indeed, a fixed-mass radial sequence may spread to larger Euclidean scales.
The logarithmic term, rather than radiality alone, is used below to exclude
vanishing.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we study the existence of normalized solutions. Next, we study the

-dependence of the ground states. Finally, we consider the Cauchy problem and the orbital stability of the solution obtained in Theorem

.

2
Existence of normalized solutions

In this section, we study the existence of normalized solutions. To obtain our results, we need the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}
; see
[
24
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality). On
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}
we have the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|u\|_{L^{2+\alpha}}^{2+\alpha}\leq C_{d,n,\alpha}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T})}^{\vartheta(\alpha)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T})}^{2+\alpha-\vartheta(\alpha)},

where
\vartheta(\alpha)=\frac{(d+1)\alpha}{2}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The functional
\mathcal{I}
is coercive and bounded from below on
S_{\Theta}
.

Proof.

By
(f_{2})
, for each fixed
p\in\left(2,2+\frac{4}{d+1}\right)
there exists

C_{p}>0
such that

\left|F_{2}^{\prime}(u)\right|\leq C_{p}|u|^{p-1},\ \forall u\in\mathbb{R}.

The Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality and
F_{1}\geq 0
give

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}[|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{2}+|u|^{2}]dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}F_{1}(u)dxdy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}F_{2}(u)dxdy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{x,y}u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Theta^{2}-C_{p,\Theta}\bigl(1+\|\nabla_{x,y}u\|_{2}^{\frac{(d+1)(p-2)}{2}}\bigr).

Since
(d+1)(p-2)/2<2
, Young’s inequality bounds the gradient on every energy sublevel.
Then
\int F_{2}(u)
is bounded by the subcritical estimate, and the energy
identity bounds
\int F_{1}(u)
. The Luxemburg norm is bounded by

\max\{1,\int F_{1}(u)\}
, so the whole
X
norm is bounded.
For any fixed
\mu>0
the same proof applies to
\mathcal{I}_{\mu}
.
∎

By using Lemma
2.2
, we can define
m_{\Theta}=\inf\limits_{u\in S_{\Theta}}\mathcal{I}(u)
. Next, we explore some properties of
m_{\Theta}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

m_{a\Theta}=a^{2}m_{\Theta}-a^{2}\Theta^{2}\log a
for every
a>0
.

Proof.

The map
u\mapsto au
is a bijection from
S_{\Theta}
onto
S_{a\Theta}
and

\mathcal{I}(au)=a^{2}\mathcal{I}(u)-a^{2}\Theta^{2}\log a
. Taking infima proves
the identity.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For
0<\Theta_{1}<\Theta_{2},\frac{\Theta_{1}^{2}}{\Theta_{2}^{2}}m_{\Theta_{2}}<m_{\Theta_{1}}
holds.

Proof.

Let
\xi>1
be such that
\Theta_{2}=\xi\Theta_{1}
. Note that
\mathcal{I}(u)=\mathcal{I}(|u|)
for all
u\in X
, so we may choose a nonnegative minimizing sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)\subset S_{\Theta_{1}}
for
m_{\Theta_{1}}
; that is,
\mathcal{I}(u_{n})\rightarrow m_{\Theta_{1}}
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Setting
v_{n}=\xi u_{n}
, we clearly have
v_{n}\in S_{\Theta_{2}}
. Thus,

m_{\Theta_{2}}\leq\mathcal{I}\left(v_{n}\right)=\xi^{2}\mathcal{I}\left(u_{n}\right)-\xi^{2}\log\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}dxdy=\xi^{2}\mathcal{I}\left(u_{n}\right)-\Theta_{1}^{2}\xi^{2}\log\xi.

Letting
n\rightarrow+\infty
, it follows from
\xi>1
that

m_{\Theta_{2}}\leq\xi^{2}m_{\Theta_{1}}-\Theta_{1}^{2}\xi^{2}\log\xi<\xi^{2}m_{\Theta_{1}},

which proves the lemma.
∎

As in the proof of Lemma 3.1 in
[
21
]
, we have the following logarithmic-type Br
\mathrm{\acute{e}}
zis–Lieb lemma on a waveguide manifold.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\left(u_{n}\right)
be a bounded sequence in
X
such that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}
and
\left\{u_{n}^{2}\log u_{n}^{2}\right\}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}\right)
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}\left(u_{n}^{2}\log u_{n}^{2}-\left|u_{n}-u\right|^{2}\log\left|u_{n}-u\right|^{2}\right)dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dxdy.

Proof.

For the convex function
A=F_{1}
(with
\delta
fixed sufficiently small),
direct differentiation of its two pieces gives constants

1<\ell\leq L<\infty
with
\ell A(s)\leq sA^{\prime}(s)\leq LA(s)
for
s>0
.
In particular
A
and its complementary function satisfy global
\Delta_{2}
.
Convexity, Young’s inequality for complementary functions, and
\Delta_{2}

give, for each
\varepsilon>0
,

|A(|a+b|)-A(|a|)|\leq\varepsilon A(|a|)+C_{\varepsilon}A(|b|).

For
F_{2}
the subcritical derivative bound gives instead

|F_{2}(|a+b|)-F_{2}(|a|)|\leq\varepsilon|a|^{p}+C_{\varepsilon,p}|b|^{p}

for any fixed
2<p<2^{*}
. Apply these inequalities with
a=u_{n}-u
and
b=u
.
After subtracting
F_{j}(|u|)
, the positive part of the absolute error minus
the indicated
\varepsilon
term tends pointwise to zero and is dominated
by a fixed integrable function of
u
. Dominated convergence, boundedness
of
\int A(|u_{n}-u|)
and
\|u_{n}-u\|_{p}^{p}
, and then
\varepsilon\downarrow 0

prove splitting for
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
separately. Subtracting the identities
and using (
1.2
) proves the logarithmic splitting. This argument also
applies to complex functions.
∎

Next, we prove a compactness lemma on
S_{\Theta}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\left(u_{n}\right)\subset S_{\Theta}
be a minimizing sequence with respect to
m_{\Theta}
. Then, for some subsequence either

\mathrm{(i)}

(u_{n})
is strongly convergent in
X
, or

\mathrm{(ii)}
there exists
\left(z_{n}\right)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

\left|z_{n}\right|\rightarrow+\infty
such that

v_{n}(x,y)=u_{n}\left(x+z_{n},y\right)
converges strongly in
X
to a function

v\in S_{\Theta}
satisfying
\mathcal{I}(v)=m_{\Theta}
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.2
, the sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)
is bounded in
X
and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
X
for some subsequence. If
u\neq 0
and
\|u\|_{2}=b\neq\Theta
, we must have
b\in(0,\Theta)
. By Br
\mathrm{\acute{e}}
zis-Lieb lemma, it holds
\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u_{n}-u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}+o_{n}(1)
.
Moreover, it follows from Lemma
2.5
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}u_{n}^{2}\log u_{n}^{2}dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}\left|u_{n}-u\right|^{2}\log\left|u_{n}-u\right|^{2}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dxdy+o_{n}(1).

Let
v_{n}=u_{n}-u
and
d_{n}=\|v_{n}\|_{2}
. Supposing that
d_{n}\rightarrow d
, we have
\Theta^{2}=b^{2}+d^{2}
and
d_{n}\in(0,\Theta)
for all sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,

m_{\Theta}+o_{n}(1)=\mathcal{I}\left(u_{n}\right)=\mathcal{I}\left(v_{n}\right)+\mathcal{I}(u)+o_{n}(1)\geq m_{d_{n}}+m_{b}+o_{n}(1).

By using Lemma
2.4
, we have
m_{\Theta}+o_{n}(1)\geq\frac{d_{n}^{2}}{\Theta^{2}}m_{\Theta}+m_{b}+o_{n}(1)
. Letting
n\rightarrow+\infty
, it holds
m_{\Theta}\geq\frac{d^{2}}{\Theta^{2}}m_{\Theta}+m_{b}
. Note that
b\in(0,\Theta)
, employing again Lemma
2.4
, we obtain that

m_{\Theta}>\frac{d^{2}}{\Theta^{2}}m_{\Theta}+\frac{b^{2}}{\Theta^{2}}m_{\Theta}=\left(\frac{d^{2}}{\Theta^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}}{\Theta^{2}}\right)m_{\Theta}=m_{\Theta},

which is absurd, so
\|u\|_{2}=\Theta
. Since
\|u_{n}\|_{2}=\|u\|_{2}=\Theta
,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T})
, and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T})
has the weak-convergence-plus-norm-convergence property, we obtain
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T})
. Using the interpolation theorem in Lebesgue spaces and
(f_{2})
, one has

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}F_{2}\left(u_{n}\right)dxdy\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}F_{2}(u)dxdy.

These limits together with the limit
m_{\Theta}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{I}(u_{n})
and the fact that
F_{1}\geq 0
provide
m_{\Theta}\geq\mathcal{I}(u)
.
Since
u\in S_{\Theta}
, we have
\mathcal{I}(u)=m_{\Theta}
. Therefore,
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{I}(u_{n})=\mathcal{I}(u)
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{2}dxdy\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}|\nabla u|^{2}dxdy,\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}F_{1}\left(u_{n}\right)dxdy\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}F_{1}(u)dxdy.

To justify the last upgrade, the nonnegative functions
F_{1}(|u_{n}|)

converge almost everywhere and their integrals converge. Scheffe’s lemma gives
convergence in
L^{1}
. The global
\Delta_{2}
bound for this
F_{1}
and convexity give

F_{1}(|u_{n}-u|)\leq C\{F_{1}(|u_{n}|)+F_{1}(|u|)\}.

The right-hand side is uniformly integrable and has uniformly small tails.
By Vitali’s theorem,
\int F_{1}(|u_{n}-u|)\to 0
. In particular, the
logarithmic part satisfies the Brezis–Lieb decomposition

\int|u_{n}|^{2}\log|u_{n}|^{2}=\int|u_{n}-u|^{2}\log|u_{n}-u|^{2}+\int|u|^{2}\log|u|^{2}+o(1),

which follows by applying the splitting separately to the convex Orlicz part

F_{1}
and the subcritical part
F_{2}
.
Iterating
\Delta_{2}
, for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
,

Next suppose that the original weak limit is zero. If no translated

Proof of Theorem

and hence

Thus

Next, we claim that

3
Dependence of the ground states

3.1
Parameter tends to zero

Now, we consider the case when
\mu\rightarrow 0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

\lim\limits_{\mu\rightarrow 0}m_{\Theta,\mu}<2\pi\widetilde{m}_{\frac{\Theta}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}
.

Proof.

Let

minimize
\widetilde{m}_{\Theta/\sqrt{2\pi}}
, set

in

\mathcal{I}_{\mu}(Qh)-2\pi\widetilde{m}_{\Theta/\sqrt{2\pi}}=\frac{\Theta^{2}}{2}\left(\mu\|h^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{T}}h^{2}\log(2\pi h^{2})\,dy\right).

The integral is strictly positive by Jensen’s inequality. Hence the
right-hand side is negative for all sufficiently small
\mu>0
.
∎

3.2
Parameter tends to infinity

Now, we consider the case when

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be an optimizer of

Proof.

A

Since

is bounded below on

∎

Lemma 3.3

We have

Proof.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{\mu}(u_{\mu}^{t})\big|_{t=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}|\nabla_{x}u_{\mu}|^{2}dxdy+\frac{\Theta^{2}}{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}|\nabla_{y}u_{\mu}|^{2}dxdy\right.

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Theta^{2}}\left[-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}(|\nabla_{x}u_{\mu}|^{2}+\mu|\nabla_{y}u_{\mu}|^{2})dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}}u_{\mu}^{2}\log u_{\mu}^{2}dxdy\right]

4
The proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we consider the Cauchy problem associated with (
1.1
), that is,

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta_{x,y}u+u\log|u|^{2}=0,\ u|_{t=0}=u_{0}.

(4.1)

The variational existence theorem does not by itself give a global
conservative evolution. We prove the uniqueness assertion stated in
Theorem
1.3
and specify the remaining requirements for existence.

A possible regularization is

i\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}+\Delta_{x,y}u^{\varepsilon}+u^{\varepsilon}\log(\varepsilon+|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})=0.

(4.2)

Its potential must be

H_{\varepsilon}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\log(\varepsilon+r)\,dr=(\varepsilon+s)\log(\varepsilon+s)-\varepsilon\log\varepsilon-s.

Thus the corresponding formal conserved energy is

\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int H_{\varepsilon}(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})
.
Replacing the logarithm inside the original energy without changing its
primitive would not give the energy of (
4.2
).

Although
X^{\prime}=H^{-1}+(L^{F_{1}})^{\prime}
with its sum norm is the natural dual,
the regularized-energy bound alone does not establish a uniform

L^{\infty}(-T,T;X)
bound: near zero the regularized potential does not
control
F_{1}(s)
uniformly as
s\to 0
. A global construction would require
a separate Orlicz estimate, a uniform bound for the regularized nonlinear
term in
X^{\prime}
, and a justified energy identity for the limit. Local
compactness alone supplies none of these equalities. References

[
5
,
7
,
6
]
motivate this program, but no unverified
transfer of their hypotheses to this waveguide is used here.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The pointwise inequality

\left|\operatorname{Im}\bigl[(z\log|z|^{2}-w\log|w|^{2})(\bar{z}-\bar{w})\bigr]\right|\leq 4|z-w|^{2}

(4.3)

holds with the continuous convention at zero. Put
h=u_{2}-u_{1}
.
The equation, tested against
h
in the real
X^{\prime},X
duality, gives

\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|h(t)\|_{2}^{2}\right|\leq 4\|h(t)\|_{2}^{2}.

The kinetic pairing is real and contributes zero to this derivative.
Time regularization justifies this calculation under the stated

X
and
X^{\prime}
assumptions. Gronwall’s inequality, forwards and backwards
from zero, proves the assertion.

For clarity, global mass conservation can also be justified for a
constructed solution in this class without inferring it from local
convergence. If
\chi_{R}(x)=\chi(x/R)
is a real smooth cutoff, its
localized mass identity satisfies

\left|\frac{d}{dt}\int\chi_{R}|u|^{2}\right|\leq\frac{C}{R}\|u\|_{2}\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{2}.

Integration on a finite time interval followed by

proves

Edge-weighted Online Stochastic Matching Under Jaillet-Lu LP
Shuyi Yan

Abstract
The online stochastic matching problem was introduced by Feldman et al. 2009 , together with the ( 1 − 1 e ) (1-\frac{1}{e}) -competitive Suggested Matching algorithm. In the most general edge-weighted setting, this ratio has not been improved for more than one decade, until recently Yan 2024 beat the 1 − 1 e 1-\frac{1}{e} bound and Qiu et al. 2023 further improved the ratio to 0.650 0.650 . Both of these works measure the online competitiveness against the offline LP relaxation introduced by Jaillet and Lu 2014 . This LP has also played an important role in other settings since it is a natural choice for two-choices online algorithms. In this paper, we propose an upper bound of 0.663 0.663 and a lower bound of 0.662 0.662 for edge-weighted online stochastic matching under Jaillet-Lu LP. First, we propose a hard instance and prove that the optimal online algorithm for this instance only has a competitive ratio < 0.663 <0.663 . Then, we show that a near-optimal algorithm for this instance can be generalized to work on all instances and achieve a competitive ratio > 0.662 >0.662 . It indicates that more powerful LPs are necessary if we want to further improve the ratio by 0.001 0.001 .

Our Results

In this paper, we prove that for edge-weighted online stochastic matching, the optimal competitive ratio against Jaillet-Lu LP is between
0.662
and
0.663

We first propose a hard instance and show that, for this instance, the optimal online algorithm can only achieve a competitive ratio
<0.663

>0.662

0.662

0.650

[
19
]
, and the hardness result suggests that more powerful LPs are necessary for further improvements.

Compared with all existing algorithms for edge-weighted online stochastic matching, our algorithm has two major differences: (1) Instead of only using one or several fixed strategies, we deploy a strategy that varies from time to time; (2) When an online vertex arrives, instead of only locally looking at its neighbors, our algorithm utilizes some global information about the probability distribution of remaining offline vertices.

Hardness.

Previous work
[
21
]
showed that, under Jaillet-Lu LP, the problem can be reduced to the case that there are only two classes of online vertex types:

•

A first-class type has only one offline neighbor;

•

A second-class type has only two offline neighbors, and it is equally matched to them.

In the hard instance, we want to have as many (arrival rates of) first-class types as possible, since they don’t have a second choice when the only neighbor has been matched. For second-class types, we want to maximize the probability that, given that one neighbor has been matched, the other neighbor has also been matched. So, if two second-class types share one common neighbor, we tend to let them share another. The above ideas give us the following instance:

•

For each offline vertex, there is a first-class online vertex type matched to it, with an arrival rate as high as possible;

•

Offline vertices are divided into pairs. For each pair, there is a second-class online vertex type matched to them.

By carefully setting the edge weights, we show that the optimal algorithm for this instance only achieves a competitive ratio
<0.663
.

Algorithm.

We modify the optimal algorithm for the hard instance to get a near-optimal algorithm

Preliminaries

Edge-weighted Online Stochastic Matching.

An instance of edge-weighted online stochastic matching consists of:

•

A bipartite graph
G=(I,J,E)
, where
I
is the set of online vertex types and
J
is the set of offline vertices;

•

An arrival rate
\lambda_{i}
for each online vertex type
i\in I
;

•

A nonnegative weight
w_{ij}
for each edge
(i,j)\in E
.

\Lambda=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}
is the total number of online vertices.
\Lambda
online vertices will arrive one by one. When an online vertex arrives, it independently draws a random type
i\in I
with probability
\lambda_{i}/\Lambda
. Then, the online algorithm can either match it to an unmatched offline neighbor, or simply discard it. The decision has to be made immediately and irrevocably. The objective is to maximize the expected total weight of all matched edges in the end. The
competitive ratio
of the online algorithm is the infimum of the ratio of its expected objective to the expected objective of the offline optimal matching.

Poisson Arrival Model.

In the Poisson arrival model, instead of assuming a deterministic number of online vertices, we assume that online vertices arrive following a Poisson process with arrival rate
\Lambda
in the time horizon
[0,1]
. An equivalent view is that each online vertex type
i\in I
independently follows a Poisson process with arrival rate
\lambda_{i}
. This equivalency and the continuous arrival process greatly help the analyses of online stochastic matching algorithms, after
Huang and Shu 2021
proved that the Poisson arrival model is asymptotically equivalent to the original model.
In this paper, we present our upper and lower bounds in the Poisson arrival model. We remark that the same bounds hold in the original model with an additive term of
o(1)
with respect to
\Lambda
. We also remark that, prior works in the original model also conventionally assume a large enough
\Lambda
, or explicitly have the
o(1)
additive term.

Jaillet-Lu LP.

The (Poisson arrival version of) Jaillet-Lu LP

14
]

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij}

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}x_{ij}\leq\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle\forall i\in I

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}x_{ij}\leq 1

\displaystyle\forall j\in J

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\max\{2x_{ij}-\lambda_{i},0\}\leq 1-\ln 2

A solution of the Jaillet-Lu LP is a fractional matching between online vertex types and offline vertices.

In this paper, we consider the competitive ratio against the optimal value of the Jaillet-Lu LP instead of the optimal integral matching.

In this section, we are going to generalize a special algorithm on our hard instance
G
to get the same competitive ratio on all possible instances. The basic idea is, for each first-class (resp. second-class) edge, we want to match it at the same rate as we match a first-class (resp. second-class) edge of
G

Is it possible? Consider any pair of offline vertices. Without loss of generality, we can assume there is a second-class online vertex type (with an arrival rate of almost
0

Intuitively, to make sure that we can match them at the desired rate, we wish that these two offline vertices are matched in a very negatively correlated way, so that there is enough probability that the second-class online vertex has at least one unmatched neighbor. In other words, a necessary condition is,
G
must be the instance that the pair of offline vertices are matched in the most positively correlated way (with respect to the match rate of each first-class/second-class edge, which is decided by the algorithm).

It’s not hard to see that in

A.3
Computational Aspects of
Algorithm

4

The matching strategy of
Algorithm

4
depends on which offline vertices are still unmatched. If we want to compute
\bar{g}_{u,v}(t)
precisely, one way is to define a function
f_{S}(t)
for each set

, and solve the differential equations between them as we did in
Section

A.1
. However, there will be exponentially many equations, so we do not expect to solve them efficiently.

Here we discuss how to efficiently estimate
\bar{g}_{u,v}(t)
with an
o(1)
multiplicative error.
First, notice that
\bar{g}_{u,v}(t)=\Omega(1)
for
t\geq t_{0}
, which means we only need to achieve an
o(1)
additive error.
Since
\bar{g}_{u,v}(t)
is a continuous function with bounded derivative, we can approximate
t
by its closest multiple of
1/poly(\Lambda)
. Therefore, we only need to consider polynomially many discrete time points, which means the total number of
\bar{g}_{u,v}(t)
’s we need to estimate is also polynomial. Then we can use the standard Monte-Carlo method to estimate them in polynomial time, with
o(1)
additive errors.

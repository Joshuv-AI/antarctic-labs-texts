A new description of the bicrystal B ⁡ ( ∞ ) B(\infty) and the extended crystal Thanks: The research of Taehyeok Heo was supported by Basic Science Research Program through the National Research Foundation of Korea (NRF) funded by the Ministry of Education (RS-2023-00241542).
TAEHYEOK HEO

Abstract
. We define new crystal maps on B ⁡ ( ∞ ) B(\infty) using its polyhedral realization, and show that the crystal B ⁡ ( ∞ ) B(\infty) equipped with the new crystal maps is isomorphic to Kashiwara’s B ⁡ ( ∞ ) B(\infty) as bicrystals. In addition, we combinatorially describe the bicrystal structure of B ⁡ ( ∞ ) B(\infty) , which is called a sliding diamond rule. Using the bicrystal structure on B ⁡ ( ∞ ) B(\infty) , we define the extended crystal and show that it is isomorphic to the extended crystal introduced by Kashiwara and Park.

1.
Introduction

Kashiwara
[
14
]
constructs the crystal base
B(\infty)
of
U_{q}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
for a Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
To understand
B(\infty)
thoroughly, we need to consider its star crystal, which is induced by an anti-involution
\ast
on
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
.
It is because many properties of
B(\infty)
can be described using the star crystal structure (see
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
and other references).
For example, for
i\in I
, there exists a strict embedding
\Psi_{i}:B(\infty)\to B(\infty)\otimes{\bf B}_{i}
of crystals, which gives the value
\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b)
for
b\in B(\infty)

(This will be reviewed in Section
2.1
).

As combinatorial approaches to understanding
B(\infty)
, many models are introduced such as path models
[
24
,
25
]
, tableau models
[
2
,
7
,
8
]
, (modified) Nakajima monomials
[
13
,
21
]
, rigged configurations
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
, PBW bases
[
32
]
and so on.
In this paper, we focus on the polyhedral realization of
B(\infty)
introduced by Nakashima and Zelvinsky
[
31
]
.
For a given sequence
\iota=(\dots,i_{2},i_{1})
of indices, we embed
B(\infty)
into the
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
by applying
\Psi_{\iota}:=\dots\circ\Psi_{i_{2}}\circ\Psi_{i_{1}}
,
and a natural question is to describe the image
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
of
B(\infty)
under the embedding
\Psi_{\iota}
.
It is proved in
[
31
]
that
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
is the set of points in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\infty}
satisfying some linear inequalities (see Theorem
2.2
),
which is the reason why we call it the polyhedral realization of
B(\infty)
.
The defining inequalities are determined by the underlying Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the sequence
\iota
, and some results are known.
When we choose
\iota
as the fixed one
\iota_{0}
(see (
2.1
)), the inequalities are given in
[
31
]
for
\mathfrak{g}
of type
A
or rank
2
,

[
9
]
for finite types
\mathfrak{g}
,
[
10
]
for
\mathfrak{g}
of types
A_{2n-1}^{(2)},A_{2n}^{(2)},B_{n}^{(1)},C_{n}^{(1)},D_{n}^{(1)}
, and
D_{n+1}^{(2)}
.
For other choices of
\iota
, the authors of
[
11
]
introduce the notion of an adapted sequence and give the defining inequalities for an adapted sequence
\iota
.

The crystal structure of
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
is well-known (cf.
[
31
]
).
In particular, we compute the associated crystal maps by evaluating special linear functions
\beta_{u,v}
at
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
,
and by comparing the values
\Gamma_{(s)}(b)
(denoted by
\sigma_{s,t}(b)
in
[
31
]
) which is a sum of some
\beta_{u,v}(b)
’s.
In contrast, one can find a few results on the star crystal structure of
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
; for example, for
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
,
the crystal operators
\widetilde{x}_{i_{1}}^{\ast}(b)
(
x=e
or
f
)
[
15
, Theorem 2.2.1]
and the function
\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b)

[
30
, Theorem 4.2]
.

The purpose of the paper is to describe the star crystal structure of
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
(with respect to
\iota_{0}
) when
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n},B_{n},D_{n}
.
To achieve the goal, we introduce tableaux
T_{i}^{\ast}
(Definition
3.1
) for
i\in I
whose cells are filled with
linear functions
\beta_{u,v}^{\ast}
which play a role of
\beta_{u,v}
for the star crystal.
As the usual crystal does, we calculate
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)
, which is a sum of some
\beta_{u,v}^{\ast}(b)
’s, for
\lambda\in\Pi_{i}^{\ast}
.
In fact, each
\lambda\in\Pi_{i}^{\ast}
designates which
\beta_{u,v}^{\ast}(b)
is the summand of
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)

and it is done by reading entries contained in the (unique) sub-tableau of
T_{i}^{\ast}
of shape
\lambda
.
Now, we can define new crystal operators
\widetilde{x}_{i}^{\ast}
(
x=e
or
f
) and maps
\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast},\varphi_{i}^{\ast}
on
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)

based on the observation on values
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)
for
\lambda\in\Pi_{i}^{\ast}
(Definition
3.8
).
We remark that we similarly introduce tableaux
T_{i}
and define crystal operators
\widetilde{x}_{i}
and functions
\varepsilon_{i},\varphi_{i}
on
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)

by observing values
\Gamma_{(s)}(b)
for
(s)\in\Pi_{i}
(Definition
3.3
).
We can directly check that these crystal maps are exactly the same as those in
[
31
]
.

The main result of the paper is to show that the crystal
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
endowed with the (star) crystal structure we define is isomorphic to
B(\infty)
of Kashiwara as bicrystals (Theorem
3.11
).
Here, a bicrystal means a crystal with additional crystal structure such that their weight functions coincide (cf.
[
1
, Definition 2.5]
).
In fact,
B(\infty)
is characterized by its two crystal structures.
To be precise, it is known that if a crystal
B
equipped with another crystal structure satisfies some conditions (see Proposition
3.12
),
then
B
is isomorphic to
B(\infty)
as bicrystals and the additional crystal structure of
B
coincides with the star crystal structure of
B(\infty)
.
Thus, to prove Theorem
3.11
, it is sufficient to show that our bicrystal maps satisfy all conditions in Proposition
3.12
.
From now on, we denote by
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
the bicrystal
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
.

Meanwhile, in order to calculate
\beta_{s,t}(b)
or
\beta_{s,t}^{\ast}(b)
for
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
easily, we introduce a combinatorial rule, which we call a sliding diamond rule (Definition
3.13
).
We put coordinates of
b
in a certain configuration, and find diamonds
\Diamond_{s,t}
and
\Diamond_{s,t}^{\ast}
in the configuration.
Then we obtain
\beta_{s,t}(b)
and
\beta_{s,t}^{\ast}(b)
as a linear combination of integers the corresponding diamond contains.
In particular, when we compute associated crystal maps on
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
, it is enough to consider diamonds contained in a specific line of the configuration.

Using the description of the bicrystal structure on
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
, we combinatorially describe the extended crystal
\widehat{B}(\infty)
introduced by Kashiwara and Park
[
19
]
.
For a Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the extended crystal
\widehat{B}(\infty)(=\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\infty))
is the set

\widehat{B}(\infty)=\left\{{\bf b}=(b^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\prod_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}B(\infty)\,:\,b^{(k)}={\bf 1}\mbox{ for all but finitely many }k\right\}

equipped with extended crystal operators
\widetilde{E}_{(i,k)},\widetilde{F}_{(i,k)}
and functions
\widehat{\rm wt},\widehat{\varepsilon}_{(i,k)}
for
(i,k)\in I\times\mathbb{Z}
,
where
{\bf 1}
is the unique highest weight vector of
B(\infty)
(see Definition
2.4
).
By definition, the extended crystal maps are completely determined by the bicrystal maps acting on each
b^{(k)}\in B(\infty)
,
and hence we apply our results on
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
to the extended crystal for type
A_{n},B_{n}
, and
D_{n}
, which results in a combinatorial description of
\widehat{B}(\infty)
(Theorem
4.3
).
In addition, we obtain the extended sliding diamond rule when
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n}
(Definition
4.4
).

As an application, we use the combinatorial description on
\widehat{B}(\infty)
in order to understand the representation theory of quantum affine Lie algebras.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{0}
be the Hernandez-Leclerc category (cf.
[
6
]
and
[
19
, Section 2.2]
) over a quantum affine algebra
U_{q}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g})
,
and let
\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{g})
be the set of isomorphism classes of simple modules in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{0}
.
It is proved in
[
19
]
that
\mathscr{B}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{g})
has the categorical crystal structure
depending on a complete duality datum
\mathcal{D}
(see
[
18
]
or Section
5.1
) and
it is isomorphic to the extended crystal
\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\rm fin}}(\infty)
with the isomorphism (see (
5.2
))

\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}:\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\rm fin}}(\infty)\to\mathscr{B}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{g}),

where
\mathfrak{g}_{\rm fin}
is the finite simple Lie algebra associated with
\mathfrak{g}
(see
[
19
, Table 1]
).
As we can explain the extended crystal
\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\rm fin}}(\infty)
from the sliding diamond rule,
we apply the combinatorial description using the sliding diamond rule to the categorical crystal
\mathscr{B}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{g})
,
which gives a combinatorial way to understand the representation theory of quantum affine Lie algebras.

This paper is organized as follows. We review the notion of crystals and the extended crystal in Section
2
.
In Section
3
, we explicitly define a new crystal structure on
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
based on the polyhedral realization,
and explain the combinatorial model to describe
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
.
As a result, we show that the bicrystal
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
is isomorphic to the bicrystal
B(\infty)
of Kashiwara,
which is proved in Section
6
(with Appendix).
In Section
4
, we introduce the extended crystal analogue
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\infty)
of
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
,
and show that
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\infty)
is a new combinatorial realization of the extended crystal.
As an application to representation theory of quantum affine algebras, we give examples for the action of the extended crystal operators
on the categorical crystal in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Crystals

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
be the set of nonnegative integers.
A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)=(\lambda_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is a sequence of weakly decreasing nonnegative integers.
The length
\ell(\lambda)
of a partition
\lambda
is the number of nonzero entries of
\lambda
.
In particular, a partition is said to be strict if its nonzero entries are all distinct.
We usually illustrate a partition with a Young diagram, which is a collection of cells arranged in left-justified rows (cf.
[
5
]
).
For partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
, the union
\lambda\cup\mu
of
\lambda
and
\mu
is the partition
(\max\{\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}\})_{i\geq 1}
,
and the intersection
\lambda\cap\mu
of
\lambda
and
\mu
is the partition
(\min\{\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}\})_{i\geq 1}
.
The
(i,j)
-th cell of a Young diagram means the
j
-th cell from the left in the
i
-th row from the top.
In particular, we write
(i,j)\in\lambda
if a partition
\lambda
contains
(i,j)
-th cell, or equivalently
1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)
and
1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}
.
For a partition
\lambda
, a tableau
T
of shape
\lambda
is a filling of cells of the partition
\lambda
.
In particular, we denote by
T(i,j)
the entry occupying the
(i,j)
-th cell of
T
for
(i,j)\in\lambda
.

Let
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
be the quantum group associated with a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
in other words, the associative
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra generated by
e_{i},f_{i}
(
i\in I
), and
q^{h}
(
h\in P^{\ast}
) with some defining relations (cf.
[
14
]
).
Here, we denote by
I
the index set, by
P
the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\{\alpha_{i}\,|\,i\in I\}
be the set of simple roots.
Let
\ast
be the
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-linear anti-automorphism on
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
defined by

e_{i}^{\ast}=e_{i},\quad f_{i}^{\ast}=f_{i},\quad\mbox{and}\quad(q^{h})^{\ast}=q^{-h}.

Definition 2.1
.

A (
\mathfrak{g}
-)crystal is the set
B
equipped with the maps
{\rm wt}:B\to P
,
\widetilde{e}_{i},\widetilde{f}_{i}:B\to B\cup\{{\bf 0}\}
,

\varepsilon_{i},\varphi_{i}:B\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\}
(
i\in I
) satisfying the following conditions. For
i\in I
and
b,b^{\prime}\in B
,

(1)

\varphi_{i}(b)=\varepsilon_{i}(b)+\langle h_{i},{\rm wt}(b)\rangle
,

(2)

Whenever
\widetilde{e}_{i}(b)\neq{\bf 0}
, we have

{\rm wt}(\widetilde{e}_{i}b)={\rm wt}(b)+\alpha_{i},\quad\varepsilon_{i}(\widetilde{e}_{i}b)=\varepsilon_{i}(b)-1,\quad\varphi_{i}(\widetilde{e}_{i}b)=\varphi_{i}(b)+1,

(3)

Whenever
\widetilde{f}_{i}(b)\neq{\bf 0}
, we have

{\rm wt}(\widetilde{f}_{i}b)={\rm wt}(b)-\alpha_{i},\quad\varepsilon_{i}(\widetilde{f}_{i}b)=\varepsilon_{i}(b)+1,\quad\varphi_{i}(\widetilde{f}_{i}b)=\varphi_{i}(b)-1,

(4)

\widetilde{f}_{i}b=b^{\prime}
if and only if
b=\widetilde{e}_{i}b^{\prime}
,

(5)

If
\varepsilon_{i}(b)=-\infty
, then
\widetilde{e}_{i}b=\widetilde{f}_{i}b={\bf 0}
.

Here,
{\bf 0}
is the formal symbol and
-\infty
is another formal symbol such that
-\infty<n
and
(-\infty)+n=-\infty
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
(-\infty)+(-\infty)=-\infty
.

The equipped functions on a crystal
B
are referred to the crystal maps (associated with
B
).
As an example of crystals, we introduce the elementary crystal
{\bf B}_{i}
(
i\in I
), which is the set

{\bf B}_{i}=\{(b)_{i}\,|\,b\in\mathbb{Z}\}

where
{\rm wt}((b)_{i})=b\alpha_{i}
,
\widetilde{e}_{i}((b)_{i})=(b+1)_{i},\widetilde{f}_{i}((b)_{i})=(b-1)_{i}
,

\varepsilon_{i}((b)_{i})=-b,\varphi_{i}((b)_{i})=b
,
\widetilde{e}_{j}((b)_{i})=\widetilde{f}_{j}((b)_{i})={\bf 0}
for
j\neq i
.

Our main object is the crystal
B(\infty)
corresponding to the negative half
U_{q}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
of the quantum group,
which is the subalgebra of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by
f_{i}
’s (
i\in I
).
When we want to indicate the associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
explicitly, we write it as a subscript, e.g., such as
B_{\mathfrak{g}}(\infty)
.
We summarize some properties of
B(\infty)
with respect to the involution
\ast
.
Kashiwara
[
15
]
shows that the crystal
B(\infty)
is invariant under
\ast
, that is,
B(\infty)^{\ast}=B(\infty)
.
It yields another crystal structure of
B(\infty)
(cf.
[
16
, Section 8.3]
) endowed with the maps
{\rm wt},\widetilde{e}_{i}^{\ast},\widetilde{f}_{i}^{\ast},\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast},\varphi_{i}^{\ast}
on
B(\infty)
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{e}_{i}^{\ast}(b)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{e}_{i}(b^{\ast}))^{\ast},

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{i}^{\ast}(b)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{f}_{i}(b^{\ast}))^{\ast},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}(b^{\ast}),

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}^{\ast}(b)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(b^{\ast})

for
i\in I
and
b\in B(\infty)
.
We denote by
{\bf 1}\in B(\infty)
the unique highest weight element of
B(\infty)
(with weight
0
).

On the other hand, for
i\in I
, there exists the unique embedding of crystals

\Psi_{i}:B(\infty)\hookrightarrow B(\infty)\otimes{\bf B}_{i}

sending
{\bf 1}
to
{\bf 1}\otimes(0)_{i}
(see
[
15
, Theorem 2.2.1]
).
In particular, we have
\Psi_{i}(b)=\widetilde{e}_{i}^{\ast\max}(b)\otimes(-\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b))_{i}
,
where
\widetilde{e}_{i}^{\ast\max}(b)=(\widetilde{e}_{i}^{\ast})^{m}(b)
with
m=\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b)
,
and the image of
\Psi_{i}
is

\{\,b\otimes(-m)_{i}\,|\,\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b)=0,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}.

2.2.
The polyhedral realization

We assume that a sequence
\iota=(\dots,i_{3},i_{2},i_{1})
of indices satisfies the following conditions:

\mbox{$i_{k}\neq i_{k+1}$ for all $k\geq 1$},\qquad\mbox{\#$\{k\geq 1:i_{k}=i\}=\infty$ for $i\in I$}.

Then we repeatedly apply
\Psi_{i}
according to
\iota
to obtain a sequence of embeddings of crystals

\dots\circ\Psi_{i_{2}}\circ\Psi_{i_{1}}:B(\infty)\hookrightarrow B(\infty)\otimes{\bf B}_{i_{1}}\hookrightarrow B(\infty)\otimes{\bf B}_{i_{2}}\otimes{\bf B}_{i_{1}}\hookrightarrow\cdots.

Consequently, we obtain an embedding of crystals

\Psi_{\iota}:B(\infty)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}

sending
b\in B(\infty)
to
(\dots,b_{2},b_{1})
where
b_{k}=\varepsilon_{i_{k}}(\widetilde{e}_{i_{k-1}}^{\ast\max}\dots\widetilde{e}_{i_{1}}^{\ast\max}b)

and
b_{t}=0
for sufficiently large
t
. We call this embedding the Kashiwara embedding (associated with
\iota
).
Note that we identify
\dots\otimes{\bf B}_{i_{2}}\otimes{\bf B}_{i_{1}}
with
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
via

\dots\otimes(-m_{2})_{i_{2}}\otimes(-m_{1})_{i_{1}}\ \longleftrightarrow\ (\dots,m_{2},m_{1})

for a notational convenience.

To describe the image of
\Psi_{\iota}
, let

\mathbb{Q}^{\infty}=\{(\dots,m_{2},m_{1})\,|\,m_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},m_{k}=0\mbox{ for sufficiently large }k\}

be the infinite-dimensional vector space,
and we consider a set
\Xi
of linear functions on
\mathbb{Q}^{\infty}
(cf.
[
31
, (3.4)]
).
In addition, we impose the following positivity assumption to
\iota
;

\mbox{for $k\geq 1$, if }i_{j}\neq i_{k}\mbox{ for all }j<k,\mbox{ then }\varphi_{k}\geq 0\mbox{ for any }\varphi=\sum\varphi_{k}x_{k}\in\Xi,

where
{\bf e}_{k}
is the
k
-th standard basis of
\mathbb{Q}^{\infty}
and
x_{k}
is the
k
-th dual basis of
\{{\bf e}_{k}\}
, or equivalently
x_{i}({\bf e}_{j})=\delta_{ij}
for
i,j\geq 1
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
31
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Under the positivity assumption on
\iota
, the image of
B(\infty)
under
\Psi_{\iota}
is

\mathcal{B}(\infty)(=\mathcal{B}_{\iota}(\infty))=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\infty}\,|\,\varphi({\bf x})\geq 0\mbox{ for all }\varphi\in\Xi\}.

As
\Psi_{i}
produces
\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b)
for
b\in B(\infty)
, we also want to get
\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b)
from
\Psi_{\iota}
,
and the result is given in
[
30
]
.
In fact, we need an additional assumption (strict positivity assumption) on
\iota
(see
[
30
, Section 4.3]
), but it is always satisfied in our paper.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
30
, Theorem 4.2]
)
.

For
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
and
i\in I
, we have

\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b)=\max\{-\varphi(b)\,|\,\varphi\in\Xi^{(i)}\},

where
\Xi^{(i)}
is a set of linear functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
(cf.
[
30
, (4.21)]
).

In this paper, we always assume

\iota=\iota_{0}=(\dots,n,\dots,1,n,\dots,1)

(2.1)

where
n
is the rank of the symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
We choose the following two-parameter index; for
(s,t)\in\mathbb{N}\times I
, we put
b_{s,t}=b_{k}
with
k=n(s-1)+t
.
In other words, it means that

and it is the

-th appearence of

Any other notations with the two-parameter index, e.g.
{\bf e}_{s,t}

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{N}\times I\,|\,s+t\leq n+1\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n}^{B}

\end{minipage}\right.\right\}

when

is of type

, and

\mathcal{B}(\infty)=\left\{(b_{s,t})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\infty}\,\left|\ \begin{minipage}[207.13776pt]$b_{s,t}=0$ unless $(s,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{B},\\

$b_{k,n}\geq b_{k+1,n-1}\geq\dots\geq b_{n,k}$ for $1\leq k\leq n$, \\
$b_{k,n-k+1}\geq b_{k,n-k+2}\geq\dots\geq b_{k,n}$ for $2\leq k\leq n$
\end{minipage}\right.\right\}

when
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
B_{n}
.
For the description of
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
for other Kac-Moody algebras, see
[

,
10
]
.
In addition, one can find the description of

for different choices of

.

Throughout this paper, we assume

2.3.
The PBW crystal

Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra.
Let
T_{i}=T_{i,-1}^{\prime}
be the
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra automorphism on
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
given in
[
26
, Chapter 37]
.
For the longest element
w_{0}
in the Weyl group
W
with
\ell=\ell(w_{0})
,
fix a reduced expression
\underline{w_{0}}=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{\ell}}
of
w_{0}
.
Then the set of positive roots is enumerated by

\beta_{1}=\alpha_{i_{1}},\quad\beta_{k+1}=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{k}}(\alpha_{i_{k+1}})\mbox{ for }k\geq 1.

For
1\leq k\leq\ell
and
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let

F_{\beta_{k}}^{(c)}=T_{i_{1}}T_{i_{2}}\dots T_{i_{k-1}}(f_{i_{k}}^{(c)}),

where
f_{i}^{(c)}
is the
c
-th divided power (cf.
[
26
, Section 1.4]
), and,
for
{\bf c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\ell}
, let

F({\bf c})=F_{\beta_{\ell}}^{(c_{\ell})}\dots F_{\beta_{1}}^{(c_{1})}.

Then the set
\{F({\bf c})\,|\,c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
is a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-basis of
U_{q}^{-}
, which is called the PBW basis of
U_{q}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
.
We remark that we choose different convention of
F({\bf c})
from the one in
[
32
]
for the notational coherence (cf. Section
5
).

Moreover, it is proved in
[
32
]
that
\{F({\bf c})\pmod{qL(\infty)}\,|\,{\bf c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\ell}\}=B(\infty)
,
where
L(\infty)
is the crystal lattice of
U_{q}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
(cf.
[
14
]
).
In other words, for
b\in B(\infty)
, there is unique
{\bf c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\ell}
such that
b\equiv F({\bf c})\pmod{qL(\infty)}
, and
we call such
{\bf c}
the PBW datum (or Lusztig datum) of
b
.
The PBW crystal
\mathbb{B}_{\underline{w_{0}}}(\infty)
(associated with
\underline{w_{0}}
) is the set
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\ell}
with the crystal structure
determined by the identification between
{\bf c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\ell}
and
F({\bf c})\pmod{qL(\infty)}\in B(\infty)
.

Since two crystals
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
and
\mathbb{B}_{\underline{w_{0}}}(\infty)
are isomorphic,
it is natural to ask whether we can construct the explicit bijection between them.
In principle, the crystal isomorphism

\phi:\mathbb{B}_{\underline{w_{0}}}(\infty)\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\infty)

(2.2)

can be constructed by calculating
\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b)
and
\widetilde{e}_{i}^{\ast\max}(b)
for
b\in\mathbb{B}_{\underline{w_{0}}}(\infty)
.
For some choices of a reduced expression
\underline{w_{0}}
, the crystal structure of
\mathbb{B}_{\underline{w_{0}}}(\infty)
is given in some references such as
[
22
,
33
]
.
In particular, we focus on the isomorphism for the following reduced expression
\underline{w_{0}}
.

\begin{cases}\underline{w_{0}}=(s_{1})(s_{2}s_{1})(s_{3}s_{2}s_{1})\dots(s_{n-1}\dots s_{1})&\mbox{for type $A_{n}$}\\
\underline{w_{0}}=(s_{1}\dots s_{n})^{n}&\mbox{for type $B_{n}$}\\
\underline{w_{0}}=(s_{1}\dots s_{n})^{n-1}&\mbox{for type $D_{n}$}\end{cases}

(2.3)

When we consider the reduced expression as the above
\underline{w_{0}}
, we use notations
\mathbb{B}(\infty)
instead of
\mathbb{B}_{\underline{w_{0}}}(\infty)
.

In particular, when
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n}
, we can explicitly describe the bijection
\phi
.
Recall that a reduced expression of
w_{0}
induces a convex order on the positive roots.
The corresponding convex order to
\underline{w_{0}}=(s_{1})(s_{2}s_{1})(s_{3}s_{2}s_{1})\dots(s_{n-1}\dots s_{1})
is the following.

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}<\alpha_{2}<\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}<\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}<\alpha_{3}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt<\dots<\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n-1}<\alpha_{2}+\dots+\alpha_{n-1}<\dots<\alpha_{n-1}=\beta_{\ell}

When
\beta_{k}=\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\dots+\alpha_{j}
for some
1\leq i\leq j\leq n-1
, we write
c_{k}=c_{[i,j]}
.
Then the isomorphism
\phi
sends
(c_{1},\dots,c_{\ell})=(c_{[1,1]},\dots,c_{[n-1,n-1]})\in\mathbb{B}(\infty)
to

(b_{n-1,1},\dots,b_{1,2},b_{1,1})\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
, where

b_{i,j}=\sum_{t=1}^{j}c_{[t,i+j-1]}.

We remark that the inverse map is given by the equations

c_{[i,j]}=b_{j-i+1,i}-b_{j-i+2,i-1}

assuming
b_{k,0}=0
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.4.
The extended crystal

We recall the notion of the extended crystal (see
[
19
]
). For a generalized Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, let

\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\infty)=\left\{{\bf b}=(b^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\prod_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{\mathfrak{g}}(\infty)\,:\,b^{(k)}={\bf 1}\mbox{ for all but finitely many }k\right\}

be the extended crystal, which is an infinite copy of
B_{\mathfrak{g}}(\infty)
as a set.
To record the position, we draw the underline at the
0
-th component of
{\bf b}\in\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\infty)
,
e.g.
{\bf b}=(\dots,b^{(2)},b^{(1)},\underline{b^{(0)}},b^{(-1)},\dots)
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\widehat{I}:=I\times\mathbb{Z}
.
The extended crystal is the set
\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\infty)
with the following maps

(\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\infty),\widehat{\rm wt},\widetilde{E}_{(i,k)},\widetilde{F}_{(i,k)},\widehat{\varepsilon}_{(i,k)})
.
For
(i,k)\in\widehat{I}
and
{\bf b}=(b^{(k)})\in\widehat{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\infty)
,

•

\displaystyle\widehat{\rm wt}({\bf b})=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{t}\,{\rm wt}(b^{(t)})
,

•

\widehat{\varepsilon}_{(i,k)}({\bf b})=\varepsilon_{i}(b^{(k)})-\varepsilon_{i+1}^{\ast}(b^{(k+1)})
,

•

(the extended crystal operators)

\widetilde{F}_{(i,k)}({\bf b})=\begin{cases}(\dots,b^{(k+2)},b^{(k+1)},\widetilde{f}_{i}(b^{(k)}),b^{(k-1)},\dots)&\mbox{if }\widehat{\varepsilon}_{(i,k)}({\bf b})\geq 0,\\
(\dots,b^{(k+2)},\widetilde{e}_{i}^{\ast}(b^{(k+1)}),b^{(k)},b^{(k-1)},\dots)&\mbox{if }\widehat{\varepsilon}_{(i,k)}({\bf b})<0,\end{cases}

\widetilde{E}_{(i,k)}({\bf b})=\begin{cases}(\dots,b^{(k+2)},b^{(k+1)},\widetilde{e}_{i}(b^{(k)}),b_{(k-1)},\dots)&\mbox{if }\widehat{\varepsilon}_{(i,k)}({\bf b})>0,\\
(\dots,b^{(k+2)},\widetilde{f}_{i}^{\ast}(b^{(k+1)}),b^{(k)},b_{(k-1)},\dots)&\mbox{if }\widehat{\varepsilon}_{(i,k)}({\bf b})\leq 0.\end{cases}

The extended crystal is proved to have similar properties as that of (usual) crystals. For those properties, see
[
19
, Section 4]
.
If there is no confusion, we may omit the subscript
\mathfrak{g}
.

3.
A combinatorial description of the bicrystal
\mathcal{B}(\infty)

3.1.
Settings

For
(s,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{X}
, set

\beta_{s,t}=x_{s,t}+\sum_{k=t+1}^{n}\langle h_{t},\alpha_{k}\rangle\,x_{s,k}+\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\langle h_{t},\alpha_{k}\rangle\,x_{s+1,k}+x_{s+1,t}

and

\beta_{s,t}^{\ast}=x_{s-1,t}+\sum_{k=t+1}^{n}\langle h_{t},\alpha_{k}\rangle\,x_{s-1,k}+\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\langle h_{t},\alpha_{k}\rangle\,x_{s,k}+x_{s,t}.

For example, we have

\displaystyle\mbox{(type $A_{n}$)}\quad\beta_{s,t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{s,t}-x_{s,t+1}-x_{s+1,t-1}+x_{s+1,t},

\displaystyle\mbox{(type $D_{n}$)}\quad\beta_{s,t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{s,t}-x_{s,t+1}-x_{s+1,t-1}+x_{s+1,t}&\mbox{if }1\leq t\leq n-3,\\
x_{s,n-2}-x_{s,n-1}-x_{s,n}-x_{s+1,n-3}+x_{s+1,n-2}&\mbox{if }t=n-2,\\
x_{s,n-1}-x_{s+1,n-2}+x_{s+1,n-1}&\mbox{if }t=n-1,\\
x_{s,n}-x_{s+1,n-2}+x_{s+1,n}&\mbox{if }t=n,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\mbox{(type $B_{n}$)}\quad\beta_{s,t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{s,t}-x_{s,t+1}-x_{s+1,t-1}+x_{s+1,t}&\mbox{if }1\leq t\leq n-1,\\
x_{s,n}-2x_{s+1,n-1}+x_{s+1,n}&\mbox{if }t=n.\end{cases}

Now, we define tableaux (type-dependent)
T_{i}
and
T_{i}^{\ast}
filled with some
\beta_{s,t}
and
\beta_{s,t}^{\ast}
in the following ways,
which will be used in Section
3.2
.
Let
\eta_{k}=(k)
be the horizontal shape partition of size
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
and
\delta_{n}=(n,n-1,\dots,2,1)
be the stair-shape partition of length
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 3.1
.

(1) Assume
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n}
.
For an integer
1\leq i\leq n
, the tableau
T_{i}
is of shape
\eta_{n+1-i}
such that

T_{i}(1,t)=\beta_{t,i}\quad\mbox{for }1\leq t\leq n+1-i.

On the other hand, for
1\leq i\leq n
, the tableau
T_{i}^{\ast}
is of shape
\eta_{i}
such that

T_{i}^{\ast}(1,t)=\beta_{t,i+1-t}^{\ast}\quad\mbox{for }1\leq t\leq i.

(2) Assume
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
D_{n}
.
For an integer
1\leq i\leq n
, the tableau
T_{i}
is of shape
\eta_{n-1}
such that

T_{i}(1,t)=\beta_{t,i}\quad\mbox{for }1\leq t\leq n-1.

On the other hand, for
1\leq i\leq n-2
, the tableau
T_{i}^{\ast}
is of shape
\eta_{i}
such that

T_{i}^{\ast}(1,t)=\beta_{t,i+1-t}^{\ast}\quad\mbox{for }1\leq t\leq i.

The tableau
T_{n-1}^{\ast}
is of shape
\delta_{n-1}
such that

T_{n-1}^{\ast}(s,t)=\begin{cases}\beta^{\ast}_{s+t-1,n-t}&\mbox{if }t\geq 2,\\
\beta^{\ast}_{s,n-1}&\mbox{if $t=1$ and $s$ is odd},\\
\beta^{\ast}_{s,n}&\mbox{if $t=1$ and $s$ is even},\end{cases}

and the tableau
T_{n}^{\ast}
is of shape
\delta_{n-1}
such that

T_{n}^{\ast}(s,t)=\begin{cases}\beta^{\ast}_{s+t-1,n-t}&\mbox{if }t\geq 2,\\
\beta^{\ast}_{s,n}&\mbox{if $t=1$ and $s$ is odd},\\
\beta^{\ast}_{s,n-1}&\mbox{if $t=1$ and $s$ is even}.\end{cases}

Note that
T_{n}^{\ast}
is obtained by swapping
x_{s,n-1}
and
x_{s,n}
from
T_{n-1}^{\ast}
(and vice versa).

(3) Assume
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
B_{n}
.
For an integer
1\leq i\leq n
, the tableaux
T_{i}
is of shape
\eta_{n}
such that

T_{i}(1,t)=\beta_{t,i}\quad\mbox{for }1\leq t\leq n.

On the other hand, for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, the tableau
T_{i}^{\ast}
is of shape
\eta_{i}
such that

T_{i}^{\ast}(1,t)=\beta_{t,i+1-t}^{\ast}\quad\mbox{for }1\leq t\leq i.

The tableau
T_{n}^{\ast}
is of shape
\delta_{n}
such that

T_{n}^{\ast}(s,t)=\begin{cases}2\beta^{\ast}_{s+t-1,n-t}&\mbox{if }t\geq 2,\\
\beta^{\ast}_{s,n}&\mbox{if }t=1.\end{cases}

3.2.
A uniform description on the bicrystal structure of
B(\infty)

For
i\in I
and
\lambda\in\Pi_{i}
(cf. Definition
3.1
) and
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
, define

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=|\lambda|}^{|\eta|}\beta_{s,i}\left(=\sum_{s=|\lambda|}^{|\eta|}T_{i}(1,s)\right)\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\Gamma_{\lambda}(b):\lambda\in\Pi_{i}\},

\displaystyle m_{i}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda\in\Pi_{i}:\Gamma_{\lambda}(b)=\sigma_{i}(b)\},

\displaystyle M_{i}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\lambda\in\Pi_{i}:\Gamma_{\lambda}(b)=\sigma_{i}(b)\},

where
\eta
is the shape of
T_{i}
,
and put

{\bf v}(s,t)={\bf e}_{s,t}-{\bf e}_{s-1,t}

for
(s,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{X}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Suppose a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
ABD
.
The crystal maps on
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
are the followings. For
b=(b_{s,t})\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
,

\displaystyle{\rm wt}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{(s,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{X}}b_{s,t}\,\alpha_{t},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}(b),

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}(b)+\langle h_{i},{\rm wt}(b)\rangle,

\displaystyle\widetilde{e}_{i}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle b-\sum_{(s,t)\in M_{i}}{\bf v}(\mathcal{I}_{T_{i}}(s,t))&\mbox{if }\varepsilon_{i}(b)>0,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{i}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b+\sum_{(s,t)\in m_{i}}{\bf v}(\mathcal{I}_{T_{i}}(s,t)),

where
m_{i}=m_{i}(b)
and
M_{i}=M_{i}(b)
.

Note that it is the same as the maps given in
[
31
, Section 2.4]
.
In particular, we have
\displaystyle\sum_{(s,t)\in\eta_{k}}{\bf v}(\mathcal{I}_{T_{i}}(s,t))={\bf e}_{k,i}
for
1\leq k\leq i
.

Remark 3.4
.

By construction, we have infinitely many trivial components
b_{s,t}
(for
(s,t)\not\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{X}
), so we will ignore them.
Indeed, we can explain the reason why this argument holds using the braid-type isomorphism (cf.
[
29
]
).
Thus, we consider
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
as an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{N}
(not
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\infty}
),
where
N
is the number of positive roots for underlying Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

On the other hand, we define other maps on
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
.
First, we define linear functions

\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}=\sum_{(s,t)\in\lambda}T_{i}^{\ast}(s,t)

for
i\in I
and
\lambda\in\Pi_{i}^{\ast}
. By the case-by-case calculation, we have

\{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}:\lambda\in\Pi_{i}^{\ast}\}=-\Xi^{(i)}\,(=\{-\varphi:\varphi\in\Xi^{(i)}\});

we leave it as an exercise.

Remark 3.6
.

(1) Whereas the partial order on
\Pi_{i}^{\ast}
is obvious, that on
-\Xi^{(i)}
is not.
The partial order on
-\Xi^{(i)}
is defined using operators
S_{s,t}
on
(\mathbb{Q}^{\infty})^{\ast}
(cf.
[
31
, (3.3)]
).

(2) For a linear function
f\in-\Xi^{(i)}
, the partition
\lambda
such that
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}=f
is determined by coefficients of
f
.
To be precise, suppose
A
is the set of two-parameter indices
(s,t)
such that the coefficient of
x_{s,t}
in
f
is positive.
Then
\lambda
is the partition whose outer corners are filled with
x_{s,t}
’s for
(s,t)\in A
.

For
i\in I
and
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
, define

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}^{\ast}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b):\lambda\in\Pi^{\ast}_{i}\},

\displaystyle m_{i}^{\ast}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda\in\Pi_{i}^{\ast}:\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)=\sigma_{i}^{\ast}(b)\},

\displaystyle M_{i}^{\ast}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\lambda\in\Pi_{i}^{\ast}:\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)=\sigma_{i}^{\ast}(b)\}.

By Theorem
2.3
, we have
\varepsilon_{i}^{\ast}(b)=\sigma_{i}^{\ast}(b)
for
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
.
The set
\Pi_{i}^{\ast}
may not be totally ordered (cf. Example
3.5
), and it may cause
m_{i}^{\ast}(b)
or
M_{i}^{\ast}(b)
not to be well-defined.
But the following lemma guarantees the well-definedness of
m_{i}^{\ast}(b)
and
M_{i}^{\ast}(b)
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
, suppose that
\sigma_{i}^{\ast}(b)=\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)=\Gamma_{\mu}^{\ast}(b)
for some
\lambda,\mu\in\Pi_{i}^{\ast}
.
Then we have
\sigma_{i}^{\ast}(b)=\Gamma_{\lambda\cup\mu}^{\ast}(b)=\Gamma_{\lambda\cap\mu}^{\ast}(b)
.

Proof.

By definition (or the inclusion-exclusion principle), we easily check that

\Gamma_{\lambda\cap\mu}^{\ast}(b)+\Gamma_{\lambda\cup\mu}^{\ast}(b)=\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)+\Gamma_{\mu}^{\ast}(b).

Since
\sigma_{i}(b)
is the maximal value among
\Gamma_{\tau}^{\ast}(b)
for
\tau\in\Pi_{i}^{\ast}
,
the left-hand side of the equation is less than or equal to its right-hand side.
Thus, the fact that the equality holds implies
\Gamma_{\lambda\cap\mu}^{\ast}(b)=\Gamma_{\lambda\cup\mu}^{\ast}(b)=\sigma_{i}^{\ast}(b)
.
∎

As a corollary, we set
m_{i}^{\ast}(b)
and
M_{i}^{\ast}(b)
to be the intersection and union of partitions
\tau\in\Pi_{i}^{\ast}
such that
\Gamma_{\tau}^{\ast}(b)=\sigma_{i}^{\ast}(b)
, respectively.

.

, which characterizes

]

Proposition 3.12

(
[

, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

.

and

,

,

.

, that is,

3.3.
A sliding diamond rule

To easily compute values in Definition
3.3
and Definition
3.8
,
we introduce a combinatorial model for
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
, which we call a sliding diamond rule.
Briefly speaking, we will record each component of
\mathcal{B}(\infty)
under some rules and read some values from it.
For this, we define the following diamonds (a parallelogram or its slanted one).

•

\Diamond_{s,t}
: the diamond containing
b_{s,t},b_{s+1,t}
,
b_{s,k}
for
k>t
with
\langle h_{k},\alpha_{t}\rangle\neq 0
,
and
b_{s+1,k}
for
k<t
with
\langle h_{k},\alpha_{t}\rangle\neq 0

•

\Diamond_{s,t}^{\ast}
: the diamond containing
b_{s,t},b_{s-1,t}
,
b_{s-1,k}
for
k>t
with
\langle h_{k},\alpha_{t}\rangle\neq 0
,
and
b_{s,k}
for
k<t
with
\langle h_{k},\alpha_{t}\rangle\neq 0

In this case, we write
(u,v)\in\Diamond_{s,t}
or
(u,v)\in\Diamond_{s,t}^{\ast}
if
b_{u,v}
is contained in the diamond.

(The sliding diamond rule)
.

Take

{\sf S}_{k}:=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{D}}({\sf V}_{k})
for

.
We remark that
{\sf V}_{k}
corresponds to the dual PBW vector corresponding to
\beta_{k}
under the isomorphism
[

, (2.2)]
.
Now, we extend a set
\{i_{k}\}_{1\leq k\leq\ell}
to
\{i_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
by

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
where
i^{\ast}
is the unique element of
I_{\rm fin}
such that
\alpha_{i^{\ast}}=-w_{0}\alpha_{i}
for

.
In addition, we extend a collection
\{{\sf S}_{k}\}_{1\leq k\leq\ell}
of modules to
\{{\sf S}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

{\sf S}_{k+\ell}=\mathscr{D}({\sf S}_{k})

.
The modules

\mathcal{D}
and
\underline{w_{0}}
(cf.
[
19
, Section 3]
).

For a given complete duality datum
\mathcal{D}
and a fixed reduced expression
\underline{w_{0}}
for
\mathfrak{g}_{\rm fin}
,
we define a standard module
{\sf P}_{\mathcal{D},\underline{w_{0}}}({\bf a})
by

{\sf P}_{\mathcal{D},\underline{w_{0}}}({\bf a})=\dots\otimes{\sf S}_{2}^{\otimes a_{2}}\otimes{\sf S}_{1}^{\otimes a_{1}}\otimes{\sf S}_{0}^{\otimes a_{0}}\otimes{\sf S}_{-1}^{\otimes a_{-1}}\otimes\dots

{\bf a}=(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\oplus\mathbb{Z}}
.
It is proved
[
18
, Theorem 6.10]
that
the head of
{\sf P}_{\mathcal{D},\underline{w_{0}}}({\bf a})

that any simple module

and
\underline{w_{0}}

(associated with

Lemma A.6

(cf. Lemma
6.10
)
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
D_{n}
and take
b\in\mathcal{B}(\infty)
.
For
i\leq n-2
, let
m_{i}(b)=\eta_{m}
for some
1\leq m\leq n-1
and
m_{n}^{\ast}(b)=\mu
for some
\mu\in\Pi_{n}^{\ast}
.

(1) When
i\leq n-2
, we have

\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\widetilde{f}_{i}(b))=\begin{cases}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)-1&\mbox{if }[m=n-i-1+k\mbox{ for some $0<k<i$ and }\\
&\phantom{if}(k,n-i+1)\in\lambda,(k+1,n-i-1)\in\lambda,(k+1,n-i)\not\in\lambda]\\
&\phantom{if}\mbox{or }[m=n-1-i,(1,m)\in\lambda,(1,m+1)\not\in\lambda],\\
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)+1&\mbox{if }[m=n-i-1+k\mbox{ for some $0<k<i$ and }\\
&\phantom{if}(k,n-i)\in\lambda,(k,n-i+1)\not\in\lambda,(k+1,n-i-1)\not\in\lambda]\\
&\phantom{if}\mbox{or }[m=n-1,(i,n-i)\in\lambda,(i+1,n-i-1)\not\in\lambda],\\
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

for
\lambda\in\Pi_{n}^{\ast}
and

\Gamma_{(t)}(\widetilde{f}_{n}^{\ast}(b))=\begin{cases}\Gamma_{(t)}(b)-1&\mbox{if }[t=n-i-1+k\mbox{ for some $0<k<i$ and }\\
&\phantom{if}(k,n-i+1)\in\mu,(k+1,n-i-1)\in\mu,(k+1,n-i)\not\in\mu]\\
&\phantom{if}\mbox{or }[t=n-i-1,(1,t)\in\mu,(1,t+1)\not\in\mu],\\
\Gamma_{(t)}(b)+1&\mbox{if }[t=n-i-1+k\mbox{ for some $0<k<i$ and }\\
&\phantom{if}(k,n-i)\in\mu,(k,n-i+1)\not\in\mu,(k+1,n-i-1)\not\in\mu]\\
&\phantom{if}\mbox{or }[t=n-1,(i,n-i)\in\mu,(i+1,n-i-1)\not\in\mu],\\
\Gamma_{(t)}(b)&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

for
1\leq t\leq n-1
.

(2) When
i=n-1
, we have

\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\widetilde{f}_{i}(b))=\begin{cases}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)-1&\mbox{if $m$ is odd and }(m,2)\in\lambda,(m+1,1)\not\in\lambda,\\
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)+1&\mbox{if $m$ is even and }(m,1)\in\lambda,(m,2)\not\in\lambda,\\
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\ast}(b)&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

for
\lambda\in\Pi_{n}^{\ast}
and

\Gamma_{(t)}(\widetilde{f}_{n}^{\ast}(b))=\begin{cases}\Gamma_{(t)}(b)-1&\mbox{if $t$ is odd and }(t,2)\in\mu,(t+1,1)\not\in\mu,\\
\Gamma_{(t)}(b)+1&\mbox{if $t$ is even and }(t,1)\in\mu,(t,2)\not\in\mu,\\
\Gamma_{(t)}(b)&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

for
1\leq t\leq n-1
.

Lemma A.7

(cf. Lemma
6.10
)
.

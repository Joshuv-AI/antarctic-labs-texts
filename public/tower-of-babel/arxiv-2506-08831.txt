Dispersive estimates for Dirac Operators in dimension four with obstructions at threshold energies Thanks: The first author was partially supported by Simons Foundation Grant 511825.
William R. Green, Connor Lane, Benjamin Lyons

Abstract
. We investigate L 1 → L ∞ L^{1}\to L^{\infty} dispersive estimates for the Dirac equation with a potential in four spatial dimensions. We classify the structure of the obstructions at the thresholds as being composed of an at most two dimensional space of resonances per threshold, and finitely many eigenfunctions. Similar to the Schrödinger evolution, we prove the natural t − 2 t^{-2} decay rate when the thresholds are regular. When there is a threshold resonance or eigenvalue, we show that there is a time dependent, finite rank operator satisfying ‖ F t ‖ L 1 → L ∞ ≲ ( log ⁡ t ) − 1 \|F_{t}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{\infty}}\lesssim(\log t)^{-1} for t > 2 t>2 such that ‖ e i ​ t ​ ℋ ​ P ​ ( ℋ ) − F t ‖ L 1 → L ∞ ≲ t − 1 for ​ t > 2 , \|e^{it\mathcal{H}}P(\mathcal{H})-F_{t}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{-1}\quad\text{for }t>2, with P P a projection onto a subspace of the absolutely continuous spectrum in a small neighborhood of the thresholds. We further show that the operator F t = 0 F_{t}=0 if there is a threshold eigenvalue but no threshold resonance. We pair this with high energy bounds for the evolution and provide a complete description of the dispersive bounds.

Lemma 3.1

Where by (

\langle t\rangle^{-2}
bound uniformly in

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
0\leq k,\ell<n
,
\delta>0
,
k+\ell+\delta\geq n
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left\langle z\right\rangle^{-\delta-}}{|z-x|^{k}|y-z|^{\ell}}dz\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|x-y|^{-\max\{0,k+\ell-n\}}&\text{if }|x-y|<1;\\
|x-y|^{-\min\{k,\ell,k+\ell+\delta-n\}}&\text{if }|x-y|\geq 1.\end{array}\right..

Consequently, we have that

(29)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left\langle z\right\rangle^{-\delta-}}{|z-x|^{k}|y-z|^{\ell}}dz\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{p}},

where we may take any
p\in[\max\{0,k+\ell-n\},\min\{k,\ell,k+\ell+\delta-n\}]
as desired.

Further, if
0\leq\delta,k<n
with
k+\delta>n
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\langle x\rangle^{-\delta}}{|x-y|^{k}}\,dx\lesssim\langle y\rangle^{n-k-\delta}.

In the case that

, one can use that
a^{-k}b^{-\ell}\lesssim a^{-k+}b^{-\ell}+a^{-k-}b^{-\ell}
to avoid logarithmic bounds. We further need to collect appropriate bounds on derivatives of the free resolvents. Combining (
19
) and (
21
) we have (for
k=1,2
)

(30)

\displaystyle|\partial_{z}^{k}{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{\pm}(z)(x,y)|\lesssim z^{2-k}(1+\log(z|x-y|)+|x-y|^{-2})\chi(z|x-y|)+\frac{z^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{\chi}(z|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{\frac{3}{2}-k}}.

While for

we have

(31)

\displaystyle|\partial_{z}^{k}[{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{+}-{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{-}](z)(x,y)|\lesssim z^{2-k}(1+O(z^{2}|x-y|^{2}))\chi(z|x-y|)+\frac{z^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{\chi}(z|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{\frac{3}{2}-k}}.

With these tools in hand, we now control the contribution of the finite Born series terms to the Stone’s formula. We recall the algebraic identity

(32)

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{M}A_{k}^{+}-\prod_{k=0}^{M}A_{k}^{-}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\bigg(\prod_{k=0}^{\ell-1}A_{k}^{-}\bigg)\big(A_{\ell}^{+}-A_{\ell}^{-}\big)\bigg(\prod_{k=\ell+1}^{M}A_{k}^{+}\bigg).

In light of this, we need to control the contribution of

Lemma 3.4

Fix

for some

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y,\in\mathbb{R}^{4}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\frac{z\chi(z)}{\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}V)^{k}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}-(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}V)^{k}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}(z)(x,y)\,dz\bigg|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-2}.

Proof.

When
k=0

3.2

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\frac{z\chi(z)}{\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}V)^{k_{1}}(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-})(V\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+})^{k_{2}}(z)(x,y)\,dz.

\displaystyle|[{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{+}-{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{-}](z)(x,y)|\lesssim z^{2},\quad|{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{\pm}(z)(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{3}}+\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

.
By integrating in

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y,\in\mathbb{R}^{4}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\frac{z\chi(z)}{\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}V)^{k}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}-(\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}V)^{k}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}(z)(x,y)\,dz\bigg|\lesssim 1,

To establish the time decay we integrate by parts once, then have to consider cases.

Lemma 3.5

We consider the first claim and consider the most extreme cases, when

Now we consider the case where

For the region supported away from zero, to utilize the oscillation we integrate by parts once

\displaystyle\int_{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\mathcal{E}(z)\,dz

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\mathcal{E}(z)}{-it}\bigg|^{\infty}_{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{it}\int{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(z)\,dz.

+({\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{-}-{\mathcal{G}}_{0})(V{\mathcal{G}}_{0})^{3}v^{*}[M_{+}^{-1}-M_{-}^{-1}](z)v({\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{3}{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\\
+{\mathcal{G}}_{0}V({\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{-}-{\mathcal{G}}_{0})(V{\mathcal{G}}_{0})^{2}v^{*}[M_{+}^{-1}-M_{-}^{-1}](z)v({\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{3}{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\\
+{\mathcal{G}}_{0}V{\mathcal{G}}_{0}V({\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{-}-{\mathcal{G}}_{0})V{\mathcal{G}}_{0}v^{*}[M_{+}^{-1}-M_{-}^{-1}](z)v({\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{3}{\mathcal{G}}_{0}+\dots{}

Here the remaining terms are those with all
{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
on the left and one
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{+}-{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
on the right, or ones with a contribution of multiple
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{\pm}(z)-{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
.
We note that any summand with more than one
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{\pm}(z)-{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
gains nearly four powers of
z
by Lemma
2.1
, and can be bounded by
O_{1}(z^{2-})
with respect to the spectral parameter, for which we can comfortably integrate by part for the
\langle t\rangle^{-1}
bound. The spatial variable dependence is bounded uniformly in
x,y
by an argument similar to the regular case. We focus on the first term above, and note that by Corollary
4.5
, the first two terms in the expansion of
[M_{+}^{-1}-M_{-}^{-1}](z)
contribute

(61)

\displaystyle F_{t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\frac{z\chi(z)}{\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}({\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{3}{\mathcal{G}}_{0}v^{*}O_{1}\bigg(\frac{1}{z^{2}(\log z)^{2}}\bigg)S_{1}v({\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{3}{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\,dz.

Lemma
4.6
implies the claim about the time decay. The claim about rank follows since
S_{1}
is of rank at most two. The contribution of the error term in Corollary
4.5
allows for one integration by parts as in the discussion following Lemma
4.6
, since it is of the form

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\frac{z\chi(z)}{\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}({\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{3}{\mathcal{G}}_{0}v^{*}O_{1}\bigg(\frac{1}{(\log z)^{2}}\bigg)S_{1}v({\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{3}{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\,dz.

Next, we must consider the case when all but one resolvent are replaced by
{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
. We consider the case of

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\frac{z\chi(z)}{\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}({\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{-}-{\mathcal{G}}_{0})(z)V{\mathcal{G}}_{0}V{\mathcal{G}}_{0}v^{*}[M_{+}^{-1}-M_{-}^{-1}](z)v({\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{2}{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\,dz.

We decompose
({\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{-}-{\mathcal{G}}_{0})(z)(x,x_{1})
into two pieces; one when
z|x-x_{1}|\gtrsim 1
and the second when
z|x-x_{1}|\lesssim 1
. When
z|x-x_{1}|\gtrsim 1
, we note that for
k=0,1
that

|\partial_{z}^{k}({\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{-}-{\mathcal{G}}_{0})(z)(x,x_{1})\widetilde{\chi}(z|x-x_{1}|)|\lesssim z^{2-k}
. This provides the required smallness in
z
needed to integrate by parts once and bound its contribution by
\langle t\rangle^{-1}
.

We now consider
({\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{-}-{\mathcal{G}}_{0})(z)(x,x_{1})\chi(z|x-x_{1}|)
.
Since the next terms in the expansion are of order
z^{2}\log(z|x-x_{1}|)
, the argument here is quite delicate. We mimic the approach in
[
19
]
for the Schrödinger equation and consider the integral in the spectral parameter. Define
\log^{-}r=-\log(r)\chi_{0<r<1}
and let
r=|x-x_{1}|
.
We need to bound an integral of the form

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\frac{z\chi(z)}{\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{g_{1}^{+}(z)}-\frac{1}{g_{1}^{-}(z)}\bigg)(z^{2}\log(zr)+\widetilde{E}(z,r))\chi(zr)\,dz

where
\widetilde{E}(z,r)=cz^{2}(1+r^{-2})+O_{1}(z^{4-}(r^{-2}+r^{2}))
, for which we may integrate by parts in
z
to bound by
\langle t\rangle^{-1}
after considering the spatial integrals. The dependence on
r
is then controlled in the iterated spatial integrals, as negative powers of
r
may be integrated while growth if
r
is large is avoided by the support of
\chi(zr)
.
Now we consider the leading term, we integrate by parts and to bound the
z
integral by (ignoring the terms when the derivative hits the cutoff functions)

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi(z)\Big[\frac{\chi(zr)|\log(zr)|}{z|a\log(z)+b|^{3}}+\frac{\chi(zr)zr^{2}|\log(zr)|}{|a\log(z)+b|^{2}}+\frac{1}{z|a\log(z)+b|^{2}}+z^{0-}\Big]dz\\
\lesssim\frac{1}{t}[1+\log^{-}(r)+r^{-2}].

To obtain the last inequality note that the last two summands are clearly integrable. The second summand can be estimated by noting that the denominator is bounded away from zero and using the change of variable
zr\to z
. Finally, the first summand can be estimated by using the inequality

\chi(z)\chi(zr)|\log(zr)|\lesssim 1+|\log(z)|+\log^{-}(r).

This yields the required inequality asserted in Theorem
1.1
by noting that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}}v(y)({\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{3}(1+\log^{-}(|\cdot-x|)+|\cdot-x|^{-2})\in L^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{R}^{4}).

This is easily verified by applying Lemma
3.5
along with the bound
\log^{-}|x_{1}-x|\lesssim|x_{1}-x|^{-\epsilon}
. The contribution of the error term of size
O_{1}(z^{-2}(\log z)^{-3})
in
M_{+}^{-1}-M_{-}^{-1}
is slightly less delicate, but is handled similarly.

Now, if
S_{1}=S_{2}\neq 0
, by Proposition
4.4
we have

M_{\pm}^{-1}(z)=-\frac{D_{2}}{z^{2}}+\Gamma_{0}+\frac{g_{2}^{\pm}(z)}{z^{4}}\Gamma_{1}+O_{1}(z^{0+}).

By Corollary
4.5
, and the argument above, it is clear that when the +/- difference acts on the
M_{\pm}^{-1}(z)
that the
\langle t\rangle^{-1}
bound holds except for the contribution of
\frac{g_{2}^{\pm}(z)}{z^{4}}\Gamma_{1}
, here we need only recall (
12
) to see that that
\frac{g_{2}^{+}(z)-g_{2}^{-}(z)}{z^{4}}=2\Im(b_{2})\neq 0
is a constant.

To control the contribution when the +/- difference acts on a free resolvent. We need only consider the contribution of most singular term, as the remaining terms clearly allow for an integration by parts. Hence, we need to bound

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\frac{z\chi(z)}{\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}({\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{+}-{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{-})(z)(V{\mathcal{G}}_{0})^{3}v^{*}\bigg[-\frac{D_{2}}{z^{2}}\bigg]v({\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{3}{\mathcal{G}}_{0}\,dz

Now, the bound in (
59
) allows us to rewrite this when
z|x-x_{1}|\lesssim 1
, in terms of
z
, as an integral of the form

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\frac{z\chi(z)}{\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}}\bigg[c+O_{1}(z^{2}|x-x_{1}|^{2})\bigg]\chi(z|x-x_{1}|)\,dz.

This suffices to allow us to integrate by parts once in
z
and bound with
\langle t\rangle^{-1}
. The contribution of the resolvents when
z|x-x_{1}|\gtrsim 1
is controlled by the same argument in (
60
). The remaining spatial variable dependence is easily seen to be bounded.
So all pieces can be integrated by parts once in
z
to gain the
\langle t\rangle^{-1}
bound.

Finally, the case when
S_{1}\neq S_{2}\neq 0
is controlled by the arguments above noting that the finitely many operators
\Gamma_{Q,j}
in the expansion for
M_{\pm}^{-1}(z)
in Proposition
4.4
have finite rank, hence their contribution is finite rank. The remaining leading terms are controlled by the arguments above noting that

\frac{z^{2}}{(g_{1}^{\pm}(z))^{2}}=O_{1}\bigg(\frac{1}{z^{2}(\log z)^{2}}\bigg),\quad\frac{z^{2}}{(g_{1}^{+}(z))^{2}}-\frac{z^{2}}{(g_{1}^{-}(z))^{2}}=O_{1}\bigg(\frac{1}{z^{2}(\log z)^{3}}\bigg)

and
\frac{z^{2}}{g_{1}^{\pm}(z)}=(a_{1}\log z+b_{1})^{-1}
.
∎

5.
Classification of threshold spectral subspaces

We now relate the spectral subspaces
S_{j}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
and the invertibility of operators arising in
M^{\pm}(z)
in Definition
2.3
to the existence of threshold resonances; distributional solutions to
\mathcal{H}\psi=\pm m\psi
with
\mathcal{H}=D_{m}+V
.
We note that the proofs mirror those in the three dimensional massive case,
[
26
]
with modifications to the different structure in four dimensions. The resonance structure mimics that of the Schrödinger operator, see
[
19
]
, having a space of ‘p-wave’ resonances in addition to threshold eigenvalues. One important difference from the three dimensional massive Dirac analysis is the need for a fourth
\alpha_{j}
matrix in the definition of the Dirac operator, as well as the logarithmic behavior of the resolvent as the spectral parameter approaches a threshold. We consider the characterization at the positive threshold energy
\lambda=m
. The analysis may be adapted to the negative threshold,
-m
, with straight-forward adaptations of the argument presented here as discussed in Remark
2.6
. We leave the details to the interested reader.

We begin by relating the subspace
S_{1}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
to the existence of threshold obstructions.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume
|v(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-{2}-}
.
\phi\in S_{1}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\setminus\{0\}
if and only if
\phi=Uv\psi
for some
\psi\in L^{2,-{0}-}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\setminus\{0\}
which is a distributional solution of
(D_{m}+V-mI)\psi=0
. Furthermore,
\psi=-\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi
and
\psi
is a bounded function.

Proof.

If
\phi\in S_{1}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\setminus\{0\},
then by Definition
2.3
,
(U+v\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*})\phi=0
. Since
U^{2}=I
,

(62)

\displaystyle\phi=-Uv\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi=Uv\psi,\,\,\text{ where }\,\,\psi:=-\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi.

Using (
14
) and (
5
) with
\lambda=m
, we obtain

(63)

(D_{m}-mI)\psi=-(D_{m}-mI)\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi=-(D_{m}-mI)(D_{m}+mI)G_{0}v^{*}\phi\\
=\Delta G_{0}v^{*}\phi=-v^{*}\phi=-v^{*}Uv\psi=-V\psi.

Therefore,
(D_{m}+V-mI)\psi=0
in the sense of distributions. In the fourth equality above,
we used the fact that
\Delta G_{0}v^{*}\phi=-v^{*}\phi
holds since
v^{*}\phi\in L^{2,2+}
, see Lemma 2.4 in
[
37
]
for example.

Now we prove that
\psi\in L^{2,-{0}-}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
. Note that

(64)

\psi=-[-i\alpha\cdot\nabla+2mI_{uc}]G_{0}v^{*}\phi=-[-i\alpha\cdot\nabla+2mI_{uc}]\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{v^{*}(y)\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}i\alpha\cdot(x-y)\frac{v^{*}(y)\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{4}}-mI_{uc}\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{v^{*}(y)\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{2}}=:\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}.

Since the integrals in equation can be bounded by fractional integral operators, by Lemma 2.3 in
[
36
]
, we have
\psi_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\subseteq L^{2,-{0}-}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
provided
|v(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-1}
; and
\psi_{2}\in L^{2,-0-}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
provided
|v(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-2-}
.

Conversely, assume that
\phi=Uv\psi
for some
\psi\in L^{2,-0-}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
satisfying
(D_{m}+V-mI)\psi=0
. We need only show that
T_{0}\phi=0
.
By a calculation similar to (
63
), we have

(D_{m}-mI)\psi=-V\psi=-v^{*}\phi=\Delta G_{0}v^{*}\phi=-(D_{m}-mI)(D_{m}+mI)G_{0}v^{*}\phi=-(D_{m}-mI)\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi.

Thus, also using (
5
) with
\lambda=-m
, we have

\Delta(\psi+\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi)=(D_{m}+mI)(D_{m}-mI)(\psi+\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi)=0.

Noting that
\psi+\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi\in L^{2,-0-}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
is harmonic, we conclude that (see
[
38
]
)

\psi+\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi=0.

Therefore,

(U+v\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*})\phi=v\psi+v\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi=0,

and hence
\phi\in S_{1}L^{2}
.

Since
\phi=Uv\psi
, if
\phi\neq 0
, then
\psi\neq 0
. The reverse implication follows from
\psi=-\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi
.
Finally, using (
62
) we have
\psi=-{\mathcal{G}}_{0}V\psi
. Iterating this identity we obtain
\psi=(-{\mathcal{G}}_{0}V)^{k}\psi
for sufficiently large
k
. Therefore, applying integral estimates in Lemma
3.3
, we see that
\psi
is bounded.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
|v(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-2-}
. Fix
\phi=Uv\psi\in S_{1}L^{2}
, where
\psi\in L^{2,-0-}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\setminus\{0\}
is a distributional solution of
(D_{m}+V-mI)\psi=0
. Then
\phi\in S_{2}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
if and only if
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
. Moreover, any threshold eigenfunction,
\psi
, is a bounded function.

Proof.

The boundedness of
\psi
and the equality
(D_{m}+V-mI)\psi=0
were obtained in the previous lemma.
First note that if
\phi\in S_{2}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
, namely
S_{1}T_{1}S_{1}\phi=S_{1}v\mathcal{G}_{1}v^{*}\phi=0
, recalling (
15
) we have

\displaystyle 0=\langle vM_{uc}v^{*}\phi,\phi\rangle=\langle M_{uc}v^{*}\phi,M_{uc}v^{*}\phi\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{4}}=\|M_{uc}v^{*}\phi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{C}^{4}},

Hence,
M_{uc}v^{*}\phi=0
. It is also clear that if
M_{uc}v^{*}\phi=0
, then
\phi\in S_{2}L^{2}
.

Also note that in the proof of Lemma
5.1
, we showed that
\psi=\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}
and
\psi_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
.
Therefore it suffices to prove that
M_{uc}v^{*}\phi=0
if and only if
\psi_{2}\in L^{2}
.
Recalling (
64
) we can write
\psi_{2}
as

(65)

\psi_{2}(x)=\frac{-m}{2\pi^{2}}I_{uc}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}v^{*}(y)\phi(y)\bigg[\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{2}}\bigg]dy-\frac{m}{2\pi^{2}\langle x\rangle^{2}}[M_{uc}v^{*}\phi].

By the Mean Value Theorem, we have

\bigg|\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{2}}\bigg|\lesssim\frac{\langle y\rangle}{|x-y|^{2}\langle x\rangle}+\frac{\langle y\rangle}{|x-y|\langle x\rangle^{2}}\lesssim\langle y\rangle\mathcal{I}_{2}(x,y)\langle x\rangle^{-1}+\langle y\rangle\mathcal{I}_{1}(x,y)\langle x\rangle^{-2},

where
\mathcal{I}_{j}(x,y)
is the integral kernel of the fractional integral operator
\mathcal{I}_{j}
. Since
\mathcal{I}_{2}:L^{2,1+}\to L^{2,-1}
and
\mathcal{I}_{1}:L^{2,1+}\to L^{2,-2}
. The decay of
v^{*}
along with the powers of
x
and
y
allow us to deduce that the first term in (
64
) is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
. Therefore, if
M_{uc}v^{*}\phi=0
, then
\psi_{2}\in L^{2}
and hence
\psi\in L^{2}
.

On the other hand, if
\psi\in L^{2}
, then we must have that
\psi_{2}\in L^{2}
. Since
\langle x\rangle^{-2}\notin L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
, we must have that
M_{uc}v^{*}\phi=0
. As shown above, this requires that
T_{1}\phi=0
i.e.
\phi\in S_{2}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
.

∎

One immediate consequence of the proof, which we use in the argument for the dispersive bounds and inverse expansions, is the following orthogonality condition.

Corollary 5.3
.

We have the identities

\displaystyle S_{2}v\mathcal{G}_{1}=\mathcal{G}_{1}v^{*}S_{2}=\frac{m}{2\pi^{2}}M_{uc}v^{*}S_{2}=\frac{m}{2\pi^{2}}S_{2}vM_{uc}=0.

Further, the rank of
S_{1}-S_{2}
is at most two at the threshold
\lambda=m
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Assume
|v(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-2-}
. Then the rank of
S_{1}-S_{2}
is at most two.

Proof.

We consider the representation in (
64
). We have already shown that
\psi_{1}\in L^{2}
. By (
65
) and the discussion following it, we can write
\psi_{2}
as

\frac{-m}{2\pi^{2}\langle x\rangle^{2}}[M_{uc}v^{*}\phi]+O_{L^{2}}(1)=\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{2}}(a_{1},a_{2},0,0)^{T}+O_{L^{2}}(1).

The constants
a_{j}=-\frac{m}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}[v^{*}(y)\phi(y)]_{j}\,dy
are finite by the assumed decay of
v^{*}
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume
|v(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-2-}
. Then
S_{2}v\mathcal{G}_{2}v^{*}S_{2}
is invertible as an operator in
S_{2}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
.

Proof.

First, we note that
S_{2}v\mathcal{G}_{2}v^{*}S_{2}
is a compact operator. Hence, we need only show that its kernel is trivial.
Assume that for some
\phi\in S_{2}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
,
S_{2}v\mathcal{G}_{2}v^{*}S_{2}\phi=0
, then
\langle\mathcal{G}_{2}v^{*}\phi,v^{*}\phi\rangle=0
. By Corollary
5.3
,
\mathcal{G}_{1}v^{*}\phi=0
, and we have

(66)

\displaystyle 0=\langle\mathcal{G}_{2}v^{*}\phi,v^{*}\phi\rangle=-\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}{\frac{1}{z^{2}}}\langle[\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda)-\mathcal{G}_{0}-g_{1}^{+}(z)\mathcal{G}_{1}]v^{*}\phi,v^{*}\phi\rangle

where
\lambda=\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}
. For
\phi\in S_{2}L^{2}
, the orthogonality condition ensures that the contribution of the
\mathcal{G}_{1}
term is zero. So, for
z=i\omega
and
0<\omega\ll m
, we have

\frac{1}{z^{2}}\langle[\mathcal{R}_{0}(\lambda)-\mathcal{G}_{0}-g_{1}^{+}(z)\mathcal{G}_{1}]v^{*}\phi,v^{*}\phi\rangle=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\langle[\mathcal{R}_{0}(\lambda)-\mathcal{G}_{0}]v^{*}\phi,v^{*}\phi\rangle\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\big\langle K(\omega,\xi)\widehat{v^{*}\phi(\xi)},\widehat{v^{*}\phi(\xi)}\big\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{4}}d\xi

on the Fourier side. Now, recalling (
14
) and (
6
), one has

K(\omega,\xi)=\frac{1}{\omega^{2}|\xi|^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}2m&0&\kappa&\bar{\eta}\\
0&2m&\eta&-\bar{\kappa}\\
\bar{\kappa}&\bar{\eta}&0&0\\
\eta&-\kappa&0&0\end{array}\right)\\
-\frac{1}{\omega^{2}(\omega^{2}+|\xi|^{2})}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}m+\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}&0&\kappa&\bar{\eta}\\
0&m+\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}&\eta&-\bar{\kappa}\\
\bar{\kappa}&\bar{\eta}&\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}-m&0\\
\eta&-\kappa&0&\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}-m\end{array}\right).

Here
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})
,
\eta=\xi_{2}+i\xi_{1}
and
\kappa=\xi_{3}+i\xi_{4}
. Notice that
|\eta|^{2}+|\kappa|^{2}=|\xi|^{2}
. The first matrix corresponds to the contribution of the transform of
{\mathcal{G}}_{0}
. Now, write
\tau:=\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{\omega^{2}}(m-\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}})
, then

\displaystyle K(\omega,\xi)=\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}(\omega^{2}+|\xi|^{2})}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}2m+\tau&0&\kappa&\eta\\
0&2m+\tau&\eta&-\bar{\kappa}\\
\bar{\kappa}&\bar{\eta}&\tau&0\\
\eta&-\kappa&0&\tau\end{array}\right).

The eigenvalues of
K(\omega,\xi)
are

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,2}=\frac{m+\tau+\sqrt{m^{2}+|\xi|^{2}}}{|\xi|^{2}(\omega^{2}+|\xi|^{2})},\qquad\lambda_{3,4}=\frac{m+\tau-\sqrt{m^{2}+|\xi|^{2}}}{|\xi|^{2}(\omega^{2}+|\xi|^{2})}.

In particular, the eigenvalues of
K(\omega,\xi)
are real and for any
\xi\neq 0
they are positive. Thus the matrix
K(\omega,\xi)
is self-adjoint, and further is positive definite when
\xi\neq 0
. The eigenvalues are non-increasing functions of
\omega\in(0,m)
. This allows one to invoke the monotone convergence theorem and take the limit into the integral (
66
) to obtain

0=\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\langle K(\omega,\xi)\widehat{v^{*}\phi(\xi)},\widehat{v^{*}\phi(\xi)}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{4}}d\xi\ =\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\langle K(0,\xi)\widehat{v^{*}\phi(\xi)},\widehat{v^{*}\phi(\xi)}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{4}}d\xi,

where

\displaystyle K(0,\xi)=\frac{1}{|\xi|^{4}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}2m+\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2m}&0&\kappa&\bar{\eta}\\
0&2m+\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2m}&\eta&-\bar{\kappa}\\
\bar{\kappa}&\bar{\eta}&\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2m}&0\\
\eta&-\kappa&0&\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2m}\end{array}\right).

Note that this matrix is also self-adjoint and positive definite for any
\xi\neq 0
, from which we conclude that
\widehat{v^{*}\phi(\xi)}=0
. To conlcude that
v^{*}\phi=0
, we note that
v^{*}\phi
has
L^{1}
entries, hence
v^{*}\phi(x)=0
. Recall that the fact that
\phi\in S_{1}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
implies that
\phi=Uv^{*}\psi
for
\psi=-\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi
. Hence, we conclude that
\phi=0
.
∎

In addition, we have

\displaystyle K(0,\xi)=\frac{1}{2m}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{4}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}2m&0&\kappa&\bar{\eta}\\
0&2m&\eta&-\bar{\kappa}\\
\bar{\kappa}&\bar{\eta}&0&0\\
\eta&-\kappa&0&0\end{array}\right)^{2}.

Therefore, for any
\phi\in S_{2}L^{2}
we have

(71)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}_{2}v^{*}\phi,v^{*}\phi\rangle=-\frac{1}{2m}\langle\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi,\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}\phi\rangle.

Lemma 5.6
.

The operator
P_{m}=-\frac{1}{2m}\mathcal{G}_{0}V\mathcal{G}_{0}V\mathcal{G}_{0}v^{*}S_{2}D_{2}S_{2}v\mathcal{G}_{0}V\mathcal{G}_{0}V\mathcal{G}_{0}
is the finite rank, orthogonal projection in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
onto the eigenspace of
\mathcal{H}=D_{m}+V
at threshold
m
.

The proof is standard, it follows as in the proof in the three dimensional case, see
[
26
]
. We omit the proof for the sake of brevity.

6.
High Energy

For completeness we prove frequency-localized dispersive estimates, which reflect the need for smoothness on the initial data and/or potential. The phase has vanishing curvature as
z\to\infty
, so classic stationary phase methods fail. However, when restricting to dyadic intervals of the from

Let
\chi_{j}(z)
be a smooth cut-off to the interval

.
Our main technical result is

{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{\pm}(z)=(-i\alpha\cdot\nabla+m\beta+\sqrt{z^{2}+m^{2}}I)R_{0}^{\pm}(z^{2}),

\displaystyle=e^{\pm iz|x-y|}\omega_{\pm}(z|x-y|),\qquad|\partial_{z}^{k}\omega_{\pm}(z|x-y|)|\lesssim\frac{z^{\frac{3}{2}-k}}{r^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\|\partial_{\lambda}^{k}{\mathcal{R}}_{V}^{\pm}(\lambda)\|_{L^{2,\sigma+k}\to L^{2,-\sigma-k}}\lesssim 1,\qquad\sigma>\frac{1}{2},\qquad k=0,1,2,

First, for

Finally, we show that the solution operator remains bounded with near minimal assumptions on both the smoothness of the initial data measured with powers of
\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle^{-1}

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

As noted after Proposition
6.1
, one needs only
|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-2-}
to show that the Dirac evolution localized to dyadic energies is bounded by
2^{4j}
uniformly in
x,y
. Hence, we need only show that the low energy evolution is bounded with lesser decay on the potential.

We first note that, if we do not require differentiability in the error terms, we have the following bounds on the free resolvents when
0<z<1
:

(82)

\displaystyle|[{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{\pm}-{\mathcal{G}}_{0}](z)(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{z^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|x-y|^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{z^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|x-y|^{2}},\qquad|[{\mathcal{R}}_{L}^{+}-{\mathcal{R}}_{L}^{-}](z)(x,y)|\lesssim z^{2}(z|x-y|)^{-\ell}.

These bounds hold for any choice of
\ell\geq 0
and any

. In particular, we may now write

M^{\pm}(z)=T_{0}+\mathcal{M}_{0}(z),\qquad|\mathcal{M}_{0}(z)|\lesssim z^{\frac{1}{2}}v(x)[\mathcal{I}_{\frac{5}{2}}+\mathcal{I}_{2}](x,y)v^{*}(y).

where
\mathcal{I}_{\frac{5}{2}}:L^{2,s}\to L^{2,-s}
provided

, and
\mathcal{I}_{2}:L^{2,s}\to L^{2,-s}
provided
s>1
by Lemma 2.3 in
[
36
]
. Thus, if
|v(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{5}{4}-}
we may write

M^{\pm}(z)=T_{0}+\Gamma(z),\qquad\|\sup_{0<z<z_{1}}|z|^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\Gamma(z)|\,\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1.

where the error term is absolutely bounded operator on
L^{2}
. By a Neumann series computation, one has that
M^{\pm}(z)
are invertible in a sufficiently small neighborhood of the threshold with

(83)

\displaystyle M_{\pm}^{-1}(z)=D_{0}+\Gamma(z)=O_{0}(z^{0}).

where
\Gamma(z)
is as above.

By decomposing
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{\pm}={\mathcal{R}}_{L}^{\pm}+{\mathcal{R}}_{H}^{\pm}

z

\displaystyle V{\mathcal{R}}_{V}V=V-v^{*}M^{-1}v.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{V}={\mathcal{R}}_{0}-{\mathcal{R}}_{0}V{\mathcal{R}}_{0}+{\mathcal{R}}_{0}V{\mathcal{R}}_{V}V{\mathcal{R}}_{0}.

We focus on the final term and selectively iterate:

{\mathcal{R}}_{0}V{\mathcal{R}}_{V}V{\mathcal{R}}_{0}={\mathcal{R}}_{H}V{\mathcal{R}}_{V}V{\mathcal{R}}_{H}+{\mathcal{R}}_{H}V{\mathcal{R}}_{V}V{\mathcal{R}}_{L}+{\mathcal{R}}_{L}V{\mathcal{R}}_{V}V{\mathcal{R}}_{H}+{\mathcal{R}}_{L}V{\mathcal{R}}_{V}V{\mathcal{R}}_{L}\\

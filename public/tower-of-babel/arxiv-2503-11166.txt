Intrinsic unconditional stability in space–time isogeometric approximation of the acoustic wave equation in second-order formulation
Matteo Ferrari, Ilaria Perugia

Abstract
We present a novel space–time isogeometric discretization of the acoustic wave equation in second-order formulation that is intrinsically unconditionally stable. The method relies on a variational framework inspired by [Walkington 2014], with an exponential weight introduced in the time integrals. Conformity requires at least C 1 C^{1} regularity in time and C 0 C^{0} in space. The approximation in time is carried out using spline functions. The unconditional stability of the space–time method for conforming discrete spaces arises naturally from the variational structure itself, rather than from any artificial stabilization mechanisms. The analysis of an associated ordinary differential equation problem in time yields error estimates with respect to the mesh size that are suboptimal by one order in standard Sobolev norms. However, for certain choices of approximation spaces, it achieves quasi-optimal estimates. In particular, we prove this for C 1 C^{1} -regular splines of even polynomial degree, and provide numerical evidence suggesting that the same behavior holds for splines with maximal regularity, irrespective of the degree. The error analysis is extended to the full space–time problem with tensor-product approximation spaces. Numerical results are provided to support the theoretical findings and demonstrate the sharpness of the estimates.

Introduction

The wave equation is a fundamental mathematical model used to describe phenomena such as sound, light and vibration. In this paper, we propose and analyze a space–time Galerin method for its discretization, which makes use of spline functions in time. This method is based on a second-order formulation. It is inspired by the Continuous Galerkin-Discontinuous Galerkin (CG-DG) method analyzed in
[
33
]
(see also
[
8
]
and
[
20
]
) but it is conforming and requires discrete spaces with at least
C^{1}
regularity in time.

We consider a space–time cylinder
Q_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=1,2,3
) is a bounded Lipschitz domain with boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
, and
T>0
a final propagation time. Given a source term
F\in L^{2}(Q_{T})
and a wave velocity
c=c(\boldsymbol{x})\geq c_{0}>0
for some
c_{0}>0
independent of the time variable
t
, with
c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, as model problem we consider the following Dirichlet problem for the wave equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\text{div}(c^{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\boldsymbol{x},t))=F(\boldsymbol{x},t)&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q_{T},\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},t)=0&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\Gamma\times(0,T),\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},0)=\partial_{t}U(\boldsymbol{x},0)=0&\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(1)

Expanding the solution to (
1
) as a series of eigenfunctions of the spatial diffusion operator naturally leads to considering
the following initial value problem for a second-order ordinary differential equation (ODE):

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u(t)+\mu u(t)=f(t)&t\in(0,T),\\
u(0)=\partial_{t}u(0)=0,\end{cases}

(2)

with a real parameter
\mu>0
, and
f\in L^{2}(0,T)
. We first focus on studying a discretization of the ODE (
2
) that is uniformly stable with respect to the parameter
\mu
, analyzing its stability and
convergence properties. Based on that, we then introduce and analyze a space–time, tensor-product discretization of the PDE problem (
1
).

This work falls within the very active line of research on space–time methods for the wave equation that are provably unconditionally stable (see, e.g.,
[
33
,
34
,
21
,
26
,
4
,
15
,
8
,
19
,
18
,
14
]
). These developments are of particular importance, as stability guarantees play a crucial role in the design of efficient solvers,
a posteriori
error estimators and adaptivity, and strategies for complexity reduction.

Various stable finite element methods have been proposed for (
1
). One approach is to multiply the corresponding ODE (
2
) by a test function with a zero final condition, and then, after
performing integration by parts
, impose the zero initial condition for the derivative in a weak sense. Then, methods that are unconditionally stable typically rely on adding non-consistent stabilization terms, which depend on the discrete space
[
30
,
15
,
12
]
, inserting a projection in time within the mass (ODE) or stiffness (PDE) term
[
34
]
, or modifying the test space with a suitable operator, e.g., the Modified Hilbert transform
[
26
]
, see also
[
31
]
. An alternative approach
avoids integrating by parts
. Within this approach, methods are classified into first-order (with the auxiliary variable
v:=\partial_{t}u
) and second-order. Various first-order methods are known to be unconditionally stable
[
2
,
17
,
13
,
19
,
14
]
. However, they result in a system where the number of variables is doubled compared to a second-order scheme.

The method we propose involves multiplying the ODE in (
2
) by a test function with a zero initial condition, modified by a suitable operator that makes the bilinear form coercive in
H^{1}(0,T)
, with a coercivity constant independent on the parameter
\mu
. This transformation operator does not alter the support of the test functions and yields a variational formulation with exponential-weighted inner products. In that, our approach is related to the CG-DG method of
[
16
]
. To enforce the zero initial condition for the first derivative, we add a term involving the pointwise evaluation of the trial and test functions at
t=0
. Notably, the method remains consistent, as the additional term vanishes for the exact solution. Both trial and test functions are discretized with splines of regularity at least
C^{1}
. Based on that, we introduce a space–time variational formulation of the PDE problem (
1
) and its tensor-product discretization. Compared to the spline-based methods in
[
15
,
12
]
, which are based on
[
30
]
, the unconditional stability here is inherent to the variational structure itself, with no need for additional stabilization terms. The focus of this contribution is on the theoretical properties of the method. Computational aspects, such as its efficient implementation and its
reformulation as a time-marching scheme
[
32
,
23
,
25
]
are beyond the scope of this work.

Our approach shares conceptual similarities with the conforming Petrov–Galerkin space–time ultra-weak formulation presented in
[
21
]
. In that work, the construction does not involve separating the variables: the test functions are
C^{1}
-splines in space and time, while the trial functions are chosen to be discontinuous piecewise polynomials, to ensure discrete inf-sup stability. Another recent conforming inf-sup stable method has been proposed in
[
18
]
based on a least-squares approach, and a conforming coercive one (in space and time) in
[
4
]
based on Morawetz multipliers.

Outline.

The paper is structured as follows.

3
Discretization of the ODE problem

In this section, we present a discretization of formulation (
14
) that is stable and well-suited for practical implementation.

Let
S_{h}^{p}(0,T)\subset H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)
be a discrete space, depending on a mesh parameter
h>0
and a polynomial degree
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

the conforming discretization of the variational formulation (

\text{find~}u_{h}\in S_{h}^{p}(0,T)~\text{such that~}a_{\mu}(u_{h},\mathcal{L}_{T}w_{h})=(f,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}w_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}\,\,\text{for all}\,\,w_{h}\in S_{h}^{p}(0,T).

(21)

Recalling the definitions of

in (

) and of

in (

) is

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{h}(t)\partial_{t}w_{h}(t)e^{-t/T}\,\differential t

\displaystyle+\partial_{t}u_{h}(0)\partial_{t}w_{h}(0)

(22)

\displaystyle+\mu\int_{0}^{T}u_{h}(t)\partial_{t}w_{h}(t)e^{-t/T}\,\differential t=\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\partial_{t}w_{h}(t)e^{-t/T}\,\differential t.

With the coercivity property (

norm transferring directly to the discrete level, we immediately have the well-posedness of the discrete problem (

).

Remark 3.1
.

From an implementation perspective, the presence of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{T}
is not problematic. First of all,
\mathcal{L}_{T}
preserves the structure of the involved matrices. This is because, if a function
w_{h}\in S_{h}^{p}(0,T)
has compact support, then
\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}w_{h}
maintains the same compact support. Additionally, standard techniques allow for the design of efficient Gaussian quadrature formulas for integrals of the form
\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}q(t)e^{-t/T}\differential t
, where
q(t)
is a polynomial. Consider, for example, a uniform mesh with nodes
t_{j}=jh
for
j=0,\ldots,N
, and
h=T/N
. Suppose we have a quadrature formula that is exact up to a certain order of accuracy for computing the integral
\int_{0}^{h}q(t)e^{-t/T}\differential t
. This formula can be modified to compute the integral on mesh intervals as follows:

\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}q(t)e^{-t/T}\,\differential t=e^{-t_{j}/T}\int_{0}^{h}q(t+t_{j})e^{-t/T}\,\differential t.

These integrals are computed exactly, without any quadrature error, by using a sufficient number of quadrature points in each subinterval. This approach justifies omitting the quadrature error in the subsequent stability and error estimates. Based on our numerical experience, we also observe that standard Gauss-Legendre quadrature does not change the numerical results and performs well in practice.

\displaystyle\leq C_{a}h^{s+1-\ell}\|\partial_{t}^{s+1}w\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},\quad\ell=0,1,2,

\displaystyle\leq C_{a}h^{s}\|\partial_{t}^{s+1}w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}.

(26)

3.1
Error analysis

Recalling the continuity of the bilinear form
a_{\mu}(\cdot,\mathcal{L}_{T}\cdot)
established in Proposition
2.5
, we derive error estimates with respect to the mesh size in the
H^{1}
norm.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let the discrete spaces
S_{h}^{p}(0,T)\subset H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)
satisfy Assumption
3.2
. Let
u\in H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)
be the unique solution of problem (
3
), and let
u_{h}\in S_{h}^{p}(0,T)
be the unique solution of problem (
21
). Then, if
u\in H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)\cap H^{s+1}(0,T)
for
1\leq s\leq p
, we have

\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C_{\mu}h^{s-1}\|\partial_{t}^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},

(28)

for a positive constant
C_{\mu}
depending on
p,T,\mu
but not on
h
.

Proof.

For any
w_{h}\in S_{h}^{p}(0,T)
, we use the triangle inequality to deduce

\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|\partial_{t}u_{h}-\partial_{t}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

(29)

Then, from the coercivity property (
15
), the linearity of
\mathcal{L}_{T}
, and consistency, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2eT}\|\partial_{t}u_{h}-\partial_{t}w_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq a_{\mu}(u_{h}-w_{h},\mathcal{L}_{T}(u_{h}-w_{h}))=a_{\mu}(u-w_{h},\mathcal{L}_{T}(u_{h}-w_{h})),

which, together with the continuity in (
18
), the analogue of (
20
) for the final time, and the inverse inequality (
23
), gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2eT}\|\partial_{t}u_{h}-\partial_{t}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\mu\frac{2T^{2}}{\pi}\right)\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|_{H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)}

(30)

\displaystyle+\,|(\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}w_{h})(T)|\,|(\partial_{t}u_{h}-\partial_{t}w_{h})(T)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mu}h^{-1}\left(\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}w_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\right)\|\partial_{t}u_{h}-\partial_{t}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

Finally, combining (
29
) and (
30
) with the choice
w_{h}=\widetilde{u}_{h}
such that (
25
) is satisfied gives (
28
).
∎

From (
28
), we immediately derive error estimates in the
H^{2}
norm.

Corollary 3.5
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
3.4
,
we have

\|u-u_{h}\|_{H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)}\leq C_{\mu}h^{s-2}\|\partial_{t}^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

(31)

Proof.

For any
w_{h}\in S_{h}^{p}(0,T)
, we use the triangle inequality and the inverse inequality (
23
) to deduce

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-w_{h}\|_{H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)}+\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|_{H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-w_{h}\|_{H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)}+C_{I}h^{-1}\|\partial_{t}u_{h}-\partial_{t}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-w_{h}\|_{H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)}+C_{I}h^{-1}\left(\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right).

Then, estimate (
31
) is obtained by selecting
w_{h}=\widetilde{u}_{h}
and using (
28
), together with the approximation property (
25
).
∎

To establish error estimates in the
L^{2}
norm, we analyze the adjoint problem associated with (
14
).

Lemma 3.6
.

Given
f\in H^{1}(0,T)
with
f(T)=0
, consider the problem: find
\varphi\in H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)
such that

a_{\mu}(w,\mathcal{L}_{T}\varphi)=(f,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}w)_{L^{2}(0,T)}\quad\text{for all~}w\in H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T).

(32)

Then, there exists a unique solution
\varphi\in H^{3}(0,T)\cap H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)\cap W_{\infty}^{3}(0,T)
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{3}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mu}\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},\quad\|\partial_{t}^{3}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C_{\mu}\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},

(33)

with a constant
C_{\mu}>0
depending only on
T
and
\mu
.

Proof.

Due to the coercivity property (
15
), if a solution
\varphi\in H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)
of problem (
32
) exists, it is unique. Let us define
z:=\mathcal{L}_{T}\varphi
. Recalling the invertibility of
\mathcal{L}_{T}
in
H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)
(see Proposition
2.4
), problem (
32
) is then equivalent to finding
z\in H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)
such that

a_{\mu}(w,z)=(f,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}w)_{L^{2}(0,T)}\quad\text{for all~}w\in H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T),

or equivalently, using
\widetilde{f}(t):=e^{-t/T}f(t)
and
2.
of Proposition
2.4
, to finding
z\in H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)
such that

(\partial_{t}z,\partial_{t}^{2}w)_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\partial_{t}z(0)\partial_{t}w(0)+\mu(\partial_{t}z,w)_{L^{2}(0,T)}=(\widetilde{f},\partial_{t}w)_{L^{2}(0,T)},

(34)

for all
w\in H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)
. We formally apply integration by parts to both sides to rewrite the problem as

(\partial_{t}^{3}z,w)_{L^{2}(0,T)}-\partial_{t}^{2}z(T)w(T)+\partial_{t}z(T)\partial_{t}w(T)+\mu(\partial_{t}z,w)_{L^{2}(0,T)}=-(\partial_{t}\widetilde{f},w)_{L^{2}(0,T)},

(35)

where we have also used
\widetilde{f}(T)=w(0)=0
. From (
35
), if
z\in H^{3}(0,T)
is the solution of the ODE problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{3}z(t)+\mu\partial_{t}z(t)=-\partial_{t}\widetilde{f}(t)&t\in(0,T),\\
\partial_{t}^{2}z(T)=\partial_{t}z(T)=z(0)=0,\end{cases}

then
z
is also a solution of (
34
). This ODE problem can be solved explicitly by introducing
y:=\partial_{t}z
, for which the problem becomes

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}y(t)+\mu y(t)=-\partial_{t}\widetilde{f}(t)&t\in(0,T),\\
\partial_{t}y(T)=y(T)=0,\end{cases}

whose unique solution is given by

y(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\int_{t}^{T}\sin(\sqrt{\mu}(s-t))\partial_{s}\widetilde{f}(s)\,\differential s=-\int_{t}^{T}\cos(\sqrt{\mu}(s-t))e^{-s/T}f(s)\,\differential s.

Then, using
z(0)=0
, the unique solution
z
of (
34
), is
z(t)=\int_{0}^{t}y(s)\,\differential s
. We deduce that
\varphi=\mathcal{L}_{T}^{-1}z
is the (unique) solution of (
32
). More explicitly, from
3.
of Proposition
2.4
,

\varphi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{s/T}\partial_{s}z(s)\,\differential s=\int_{0}^{t}e^{s/T}y(s)\,\differential s.

We obtain (
33
) by computing

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{3}\varphi(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{2}}e^{t/T}y(t)+\frac{2}{T}e^{t/T}\partial_{t}y(t)+e^{t/T}\partial_{t}^{2}y(t),

substituting the expression of
\partial_{t}y
and
\partial_{t}^{2}y
given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}y(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-t/T}f(t)-\sqrt{\mu}\int_{t}^{T}\sin(\sqrt{\mu}(s-t))e^{-s/T}f(s)\,\differential s,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}y(t)

\displaystyle=-\mu y(t)-\partial_{t}\widetilde{f}(t)=-\mu y(t)+\frac{1}{T}e^{-t/T}f(t)-e^{-t/T}\partial_{t}f(t),

and the expression of
y
, and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, taking also into account that
f(t)=-\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{s}f(s)\,\differential s
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The result of Lemma
3.6
is valid also when
\mu=0
, namely, given
f\in H^{1}(0,T)
with
f(T)=0
, the problem

\text{find~}\varphi\in H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)\text{~such that}\quad a_{0}(w,\mathcal{L}_{T}\varphi)=(f,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}w)_{L^{2}(0,T)}\quad\text{for all~}w\in H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)

has a unique solution
\varphi\in H^{3}(0,T)\cap H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)\cap W^{3}_{\infty}(0,T)
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{3}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},\quad\|\partial_{t}^{3}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},

with a constant
C>0
depending only on
T
.

In the following corollary, we derive error estimates in the
L^{2}
norm.

Corollary 3.8
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
3.4
,
we have

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mu}h^{s}\|\partial_{t}^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},

(36)

where
C_{\mu}
is a positive constant depending on
p,T,\mu
but independent of
h
.

Proof.

In order to prove (
36
), we consider the adjoint problem (
32
) with the source term

f(t):=e^{t/T}\int_{t}^{T}(u-u_{h})(s)\,\differential s.

Since
f\in H^{1}(0,T)
and
f(T)=0
, by Lemma
3.6
, the problem is well-posed, and its unique solution
\varphi\in H^{3}(0,T)\cap H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)\cap W_{\infty}^{3}(0,T)
satisfies the stability estimates in (
33
). From
\partial_{t}f(t)=T^{-1}f(t)-e^{t/T}(u-u_{h})(t)
, the estimates in (
33
) become

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{3}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mu}\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},\qquad\|\partial_{t}^{3}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mu}\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

(37)

Moreover, using
2.
of Proposition (
2.4
),

f(T)=0
and
(u-u_{h})(0)=0
,
we compute

(f,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}(u-u_{h}))_{L^{2}(0,T)}=\|u-u_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

Therefore, using the latter relation, the auxiliary problem (
32
), and employing consistency, the continuity in (
17
), as well as estimate (
20
), we deduce, with
\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}
as in Assumption
3.2
,

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}

\displaystyle=(f,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}(u-u_{h}))_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle=a_{\mu}(u-u_{h},\mathcal{L}_{T}\varphi)

\displaystyle=a_{\mu}(u-u_{h},\mathcal{L}_{T}(\varphi-\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}))

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mu}\|u-u_{h}\|_{H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)}\left(\|\partial_{t}\varphi-\partial_{t}\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+|\partial_{t}\varphi(0)-\partial_{t}\widetilde{\varphi}_{h}(0)|\right).

From this, we obtain (
36
) by employing the approximation estimates in (
25
) and (
26
) (with
s=2
), the stability estimates in (
37
), and Corollary
3.5
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

On a mesh with
N
elements, the dimension of
S^{q,q-1}(0,T)
is
N+q
. Therefore, for maximal regularity splines, even if the convergence rate were suboptimal by one order, achieving a convergence rate of
p
for the
H^{1}
error by switching from
S^{p,p-1}(0,T)
to
S^{p+1,p}(0,T)
would increase the number of degrees of freedom by just one.

In this section, we prove a quasi-optimal convergence result, under an additional assumption on the discrete spaces
S_{h}^{p}(0,T)\subset H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)
. Then, in Section
3.3
, we prove that, for
C^{1}
-continuous splines of even degree on uniform meshes, this assumption is actually satisfied.

Assumption 3.10
.

There exists a projection operator
Q_{h}:H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)\to S_{h}^{p}(0,T)
such that

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}Q_{h}u(T)=\partial_{t}u(T)\\
(\partial_{t}Q_{h}u,e^{-\bullet/T}\partial_{t}^{2}w_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}=(\partial_{t}u,e^{-\bullet/T}\partial_{t}^{2}w_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}\quad\quad\text{for all~}w_{h}\in S_{h}^{p}(0,T),\end{cases}

and such that, for every
u
sufficiently smooth,

\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}Q_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq Ch^{p},

(39)

with a constant
C>0
depending on
u
but independent of
h
.

Consider also the auxiliary projection operator
\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}:H_{0,\bullet}^{2}(0,T)\to S_{h}^{p}(0,T)
defined by

(\partial_{t}^{2}\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}w_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\partial_{t}\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u(0)\partial_{t}w_{h}(0)=(\partial_{t}^{2}u,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}w_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\partial_{t}u(0)\partial_{t}w_{h}(0)

(40)

for all
w_{h}\in S_{h}^{p}(0,T)
. Note that (
40
) can also be written as
a_{0}(\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u,\mathcal{L}_{T}w_{h})=a_{0}(u,\mathcal{L}_{T}w_{h})
, with
a_{0}
as in (
5
) with
\mu=0
.

Proposition 3.11
.

The projection operator
\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}
defined in (
40
) is well-defined and satisfies

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq(1+2e)\|\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}Q_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},

(41)

with
Q_{h}
defined in Assumption
3.10
.

Proof.

With
4.
and
6.
of Proposition
2.4
, we deduce the coercivity property

(\partial_{t}^{2}w,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}w)_{L^{2}(0,T)}+|\partial_{t}w(0)|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2eT}\|\partial_{t}w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}\quad\text{for all~}w\in H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T).

(42)

This implies that the square, finite dimensional system (
40
) is nonsingular, and therefore
\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}
is well defined. To obtain estimate (
41

in (

together with integration by parts, and the definition of

in Assumption

, to deduce

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u-\partial_{t}Q_{h}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq 2eT\Bigl(\partial_{t}^{2}(\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u-Q_{h}u),\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}(\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u-Q_{h}u))_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\partial_{t}(\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u-Q_{h}u)(0)\,\partial_{t}(\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u-Q_{h}u)(0)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq 2eT\Bigl(\partial_{t}^{2}(u-Q_{h}u),\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}(\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u-Q_{h}u))_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\partial_{t}(u-Q_{h}u)(0)\,\partial_{t}(\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u-Q_{h}u)(0)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=2e(\partial_{t}(u-Q_{h}u),\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}(\Pi_{h}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}u-Q_{h}u))_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

(44)

Variational formulation of the PDE problem

In this section, we introduce a variational formulation the complete problem (

Let us consider first the following well-posed variational formulation of problem (

U\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

(50)

such that

\langle\partial_{t}^{2}U(\cdot,t),V\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\cdot,t),\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}V)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(F(\cdot,t),V)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for all

, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
is the duality product between
[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
and

; see, e.g.,
[

, Chapter 3. §8 Pag. 265]
. We restrict ourselves to a more regular framework and assume that

F\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad F(\cdot,0)\in L^{2}(\Omega).

(52)

We have the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Under the regularity assumption (
52
) on
F
, there exists a unique solution to (
51
) and it satisfies

U\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).

Proof.

See, e.g.,
[
1
, Prop. 4.4]
and
[
10
, Chapter 7.2]
.
∎

For functions
(U,W)
such that

U\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad W\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\ \text{ with }\ \partial_{t}W(\cdot,0)\in L^{2}(\Omega),

we define the space–time bilinear form
\mathcal{A}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(U,W):=(\partial_{t}^{2}U,\partial_{t}W)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}+(\partial_{t}U(\cdot,0),\partial_{t}W(\cdot,0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\partial_{t}W)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}.

(53)

We still denote by

the operator analogue to that defined in (
13
) but now acting on space–time functions, i.e. we define
\mathcal{L}_{T}:H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\to H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

\mathcal{L}_{T}W(\boldsymbol{x},t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-s/T}\partial_{s}W(\boldsymbol{x},s)\,\differential s.

(54)

Then, the space–time variational formulation of the complete wave problem (
1

find
U\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

(55)

for all

.

We define the norm

\displaystyle\|U\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}(Q_{T})}:=\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}^{2}+\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{T})},

(56)

Proposition 4.2

(Coercivity in the norm (
56
))
.

We have

\mathcal{A}(U,\mathcal{L}_{T}U)\geq\frac{1}{2eT}\|U\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}(Q_{T})}\qquad\text{for all}\ \ U\in H^{1}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap H^{2}_{0,\bullet}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).

Proof.

We readily compute, using property
2.
and then
4.
–
7.
of Proposition
2.4
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(U,\mathcal{L}_{T}U)=

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}^{2}U,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}U)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}+\|\partial_{t}U(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(c^{2}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}U)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2T}(\partial_{t}U,\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}U)_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}+\frac{1}{2e}\|\partial_{t}U(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t/T}\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\differential t+\frac{1}{2e}\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2eT}\left(\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}^{2}+\|c\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}^{2}\right),

which proves the result.

12

Theorem 4.3
.

Let us assume the regularity on the datum
F
as in (
52
). Then, there exists a unique solution of problem (
55
), and it satisfies

\|U\|_{\mathcal{V}(Q_{T})}\leq 2eT\,\|F\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}.

Proof.

The existence of a solution
U
of problem (
55
) with the required regularity follows from Proposition
4.1
and the fact that the solution therein is also a solution of (
55
). The stability estimate follows from the coercivity in Proposition
4.2
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and

of Proposition

:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2eT}\|U\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}(Q_{T})}\leq\mathcal{A}(U,\mathcal{L}_{T}U)

.

Let the source satisfy

for all

With an abuse of notation, for

The operator
\Pi^{\nabla}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}
is well-defined, from the Lax-Milgram lemma. The coercivity estimate

(\partial_{t}^{2}W_{h_{t}},\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{T}W_{h_{t}})_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}+\|\partial_{t}W_{h_{t}}(\cdot,0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{1}{2eT}\|\partial_{t}W_{h_{t}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}

, which follows from
4.
and
2.
of Proposition
2.4
, together with the tensor product structure, the finite dimensionality in time, and coercivity and continuity in space, implies that also
\Pi_{h_{t}}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}
is well-defined.
Furthermore,
\Pi^{\nabla}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}
and
\Pi_{h_{t}}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}
satisfy

\Pi^{\nabla}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}\Pi_{h_{t}}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}=\Pi_{h_{t}}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}\Pi^{\nabla}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}},\qquad\partial_{t}\Pi^{\nabla}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}=\Pi^{\nabla}_{h_{\boldsymbol{x}}}\partial_{t},\qquad\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\Pi_{h_{t}}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}=\Pi_{h_{t}}^{\partial_{t}^{2}}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}.

We use the coercivity in Proposition

and the Galerkin orthogonality and obtain, for any

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied a conforming space–time method for the wave equation based on a new second-order variational formulation, with smooth spline discretization in time. The method is proven to be unconditionally stable.

The scheme is obtained by discretizing first an associated ODE in time, with stability relying on two key components: the modification of the test functions through an appropriate isomorphism, and the inclusion of a term that contains the first derivative at the initial time. The resulting discretization of the ODE is stated in (
21
) or, equivalently, in (
22
). The proposed method yields error estimates with respect to the mesh size that are suboptimal by one order in standard Sobolev norms. However, for certain choices of approximation spaces, it achieves quasi-optimal convergence. In particular, we prove this for
C^{1}
-regular splines of even polynomial degree, and present numerical evidence suggesting that this may also apply to splines with maximal regularity, irrespective of the degree. Based on numerical findings, our conjecture is that, for splines, quasi-optimal convergence occurs whenever the difference between degree and regularity is odd. A notable case where the conjecture is valid but not proven is that of splines with maximal regularity. However, even if the order of convergence were suboptimal by one and switching from
p
- to
(p+1)
-degree maximal regularity splines were required to achieve a convergence rate of
p
for the
H^{1}
error, the increase in degrees of freedom would be minimal. Numerical experiments confirm the sharpness of the results.

Building on the analysis for the ODE problem, we then studied the numerical scheme for the wave equation stated in (
57
) or, equivalently, in (
58
). This scheme is proven to be unconditionally stable, for any choice of conforming discrete space. The convergence rates are always quasi-optimal in space, and quasi-optimality in time is achieved under the same conditions as in the ODE case.

We remark that, if we remove the exponential weight in (
22
), the same properties of stability and convergence can be still observed numerically. In that case, for splines with maximal regularity, a theoretical analysis could be performed exploiting the quasi-Toeplitz structure of the system matrix as in
[
12
,
13
]
; however, a more general variational argument following the approach of this paper remains to be developed. Finally, important aspects not yet addressed are the efficient implementation of the space–time method and the possibility to reformulate it as a time-marching scheme as, e.g., in
[
32
,
23
,
25
]
, also in view of an assessment of the computational cost and comparative studies.

Marginal flows of non-entropic weak Schrödinger bridges August 10, 2026.
Camilo Hernández, Ludovic Tangpi

Abstract
This paper introduces a dynamic formulation of divergence-regularized optimal transport with weak targets on the path space. In our formulation, the classical relative entropy penalty is replaced by a general convex divergence, and terminal constraints are imposed in a weak sense. We establish well-posedness and a convex dual formulation of the problem, together with explicit structural characterizations of primal and dual optimizers. Specifically, the optimal path measure is shown to admit an explicit density relative to a reference diffusion, generalizing the classical Schrödinger system. For the pure Schrödinger case, i.e., when the transport cost is zero, we further characterize the flow of time marginals of the optimal bridge, recovering known results in the entropic setting and providing new descriptions for non-entropic divergences including the χ 2 \chi^{2} –divergence.

Introduction

Optimal transport (OT) provides a powerful framework for comparing probability measures, and has become a cornerstone in modern statistics, data science, machine learning and operations research.
Formulated as a convex optimization problem over couplings between two distributions
\mu_{0},\mu_{T}
not only compares distributions, but also exhibits geometric properties of the set of probability measures, and plays a fundamental role in partial differential equations.
However, a well-known limitation is its poor statistical scalability:
When the distributions
\mu_{0},\mu_{T}
are replaced by empirical measures based on i.i.d. samples, the convergence of the empirical OT problem to its population counterpart deteriorates exponentially with the dimension, an instance of the curse of dimensionality; see, e.g.
S. Chewi 2025
for a recent survey.
To overcome this limitation, entropic regularized optimal transport introduces an entropic penalty to the OT objective, yielding smoother dual problems and faster statistical convergence, see for instance
[
8
,
9
,
14
,
47
,
43
]
among many works on the subject.
The entropic regularized perspective exhibit great statistical properties.
In fact, the empirical optimal costs, optimal couplings, and dual potentials all converge to their population counterparts at the rate
{\cal O}(\sqrt{N})
when the measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{T}
are approximated by empirical measures of
N
i.i.d. random variables
Genevay et al. 2019
,
Mena and Niles-Weed 2019
.

Despite these advantages, the entropy penalty unfortunately produces full-support couplings (a consequence of Brenier’s theorem), a phenomenon known as overspreading, which
can lead to undesirable blurring effects in imaging and manifold learning applications
Blondel et al. 2018
.
Moreover, entropic OT becomes numerically unstable when the regularization parameter is small, as the dual variables may attain exponentially large or small values
Li et al. 2020
.
To overcome these shortcomings, an increasingly popular alternative to entropic regularization is quadratically regularized optimal transport, which corresponds to the problem

v(\mu_{0},\mu_{T}):=\mathop{\mathrm{inf}\vphantom{\mathrm{sup}}}_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}c(x,y)\pi(dx,dy)+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\ell\Big(\frac{d\pi}{d\mu_{0}\otimes\mu_{T}}\Big)\mu_{0}\otimes\mu_{T}(dx,dy)\bigg)

(1.1)

where
\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})
is the set of couplings of the probability measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{T}
and
\ell(x):=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}
.
This problem has attracted a sustained interest in recent years.
While the theoretically of this problem is a lot less well-understood than its entropic counterpart, it has already been showed notably that quadratic OT retains many computational benefits of entropic OT and produces sparse approximations that more faithfully reflect the geometry of unregularized OT, see
González-Sanz and Nutz 2024
,
Wiesel and Xu 2025
.
We refer for instance to
[
45
,
25
,
61
,
58
,
59
,
18
]
for recent works on sparsity, and
González-Sanz et al. 2025c
,
González-Sanz et al. 2025a
on gradient descent and sample approximation.
Quadratic regularized OT also enjoys numerical stability even for small regularization parameters and admits fast gradient-based optimization as showed by
González-Sanz et al. 2025c
.
While quadratically regularized OT may suffer the curse of dimensionallity, the interesting recent paper of
González-Sanz et al. 2025b
shows that for a sufficiently large class of convex functions
\ell
the divergence-regularized OT (
1.1
) overcomes it and the empirical version of the problem converges at the parametric rate.
This further motivates the study of (

) beyond the quadratic case.
Divergence regularized OT also an emerging research topic, with some recent references including

.
Research on divergence-regularized OT has been restricted to the static case.
The goal of this work is to introduce divergence-regularized OT in the dynamic setting, and to derive the structure of the optimizer.

Divergence-regularized OT and weak constraints.

Let us introduce the divergence on the path space.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
T>0
and
\mu_{0},\mu_{T}
be given Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Let
(\Omega,\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space suppoting a Brownian motion
W
and such that
\mathbb{P}
is the unique weak solution of the stochastic differential equation

{\mathrm{d}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t}){\mathrm{d}}t+{\mathrm{d}}W_{t},\,\mathbb{P}\circ X_{0}^{-1}=\mu_{0}.

The process
X
belongs to
{\cal C}_{T}
, the space of continuous functions on
[0,T]
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
The (dynamic) divergence-regularized OT is

{\cal V}^{\varepsilon}(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})=\mathop{\mathrm{inf}\vphantom{\mathrm{sup}}}\Big\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[C(X)\big]+\varepsilon{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P}),\,\mathbb{Q}\circ X_{0}^{-1}=\mu_{0},\,\mathbb{Q}\circ X_{T}^{-1}=\mu_{T}\Big\},

(1.2)

where
C:{\cal C}_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
is a given cost function and
{\cal I}_{\ell}(\cdot|\mathbb{P})
denotes the divergence operator with respect to the measure
\mathbb{P}
and the function
\ell
i.e.

{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P}):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\ell\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}}\Big)\bigg]&\text{if }\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1.3)

In essence,
we consider the divergence-regularization of the celebrated Benamou–Brenier continuous-time transport problem
[
7
]
.
This viewpoint
reveals deep connections between optimal transport, fluid mechanics, and gradient flows, but also enables powerful analytical and numerical tools that are unavailable in the static case.
Obviously when
C=0
, this problem corresponds to a form of (non-entropic) Schrödinger problem
[
21
,
52
,
53
]
in which the discrepancy between probability measures considered with respect to a general divergence.
This is in its own right an interesting generalization of Schrödinger problem that, as far as we know, has only been considered by
Léonard 2001
.
From a probabilistic standpoint, divergences arise naturally when one seeks to control deviations between laws without imposing symmetry or additivity properties inherent to relative entropy.
Their flexibility makes them well-suited for capturing different aspects of distributional distance, while still retaining structural features such as convexity and monotonicity under Markov kernels.
However, they do not, in general, satisfy the so-called data-processing equality

{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P})={\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}_{0T}|\mathbb{P}_{0T})+\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{2}}{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}_{xy}|\mathbb{P}_{xy})\mathbb{P}_{0T}({\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}y),

(1.4)

where
\mathbb{Q}_{xy}(\cdot):=\mathbb{Q}(\cdot|X_{0}=x,X_{T}=y)
and
\mathbb{Q}_{0T}=\mathbb{Q}\circ(X_{0},X_{T})^{-1}
; which is well-known to hold (essentially) only in the case
\ell(x)=x\log(x)-x+1
, which corresponds to the relative entropy see e.g.
Lacker 2018
.
Concequently, the dynamic problem (
1.2
) does not necessarilty have the same value as its static counterpart (
1.1
) for arbitrary
\ell
(as is known in the entropic case), see for instance the arguments of proof of
[
38
, Proposition 2.3]
.

A key feature of (
1.2
) is that the divergence regularization allows mass to diffuse along many possible paths rather than following a single deterministic trajectory, hence the reward accounts for the average trajectories induced by a path distribution
\mathbb{Q}
, not the precise microscopic path itself.
This naturally suggests considering problems where we no longer require
X
to hit the target distribution exactly, i.e.,
\mathbb{Q}\circ X_{T}^{-1}=\mu_{T}
, but to match it on average.

Weak optimal transport
problems introduced by Gozlan et. al.
[
30
,
31
]
have emerged as a particularly flexible, yet tractable framework for modeling broader relationships between distributions.
See (
2.2
) for precise definition and
Backhoff-Veraguas and Pammer 2022
for references on the topic and a sample of applications.
Specifically, given a weak cost
c:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times{\cal P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\to\mathbb{R}
and associated weak optimal transport value
{\cal W}_{c}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we will focus on the more general problem

V_{c}(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})=\mathop{\mathrm{inf}\vphantom{\mathrm{sup}}}\Big\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[C(X)\big]+{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P}),\,\mathbb{Q}\circ X_{0}^{-1}=\mu_{0},\,{\cal W}_{c}(\mathbb{Q}\circ X_{T}^{-1},\mu_{T})=0\Big\}

(1.5)

where, for simplicity, we let
\varepsilon=1
.
Examples will be discussed in subsection
3.3
where we will see that adequate choices of
c
allow to recover the constraint
\mathbb{Q}\circ X_{T}^{-1}=\mu_{T}
, but also to model cases where the terminal law
\mathbb{Q}\circ X_{T}^{-1}
of
X
is constrained to be in convex order with
\mu_{T}
or to belong to a ball around it.

Related literature.

In the entropic case
\ell(x)=x\log(x)-x+1
, and with
C=0
, our results build on the well-established literature on the dynamic Schrödinger’s problem.
In particular, the characterization of the density of the optimal bridge in terms of potentials, the Schrödinger system, as well as the characterization of the flow of marginal laws (with respect to a reversible reference measure) in terms of Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations are well understood in this setting, see the survey of
Léonard 2014
and the references therein.
In the non-entropic case, a first step towards understanding the infinitesimal behaviour of the quadratically penalized optimal transport was discussed in
Garriz-Molina et al. 2024

In the static setting, Schrödinger problems for general divergences were studied in
Léonard 2001
. Regarding the study of divergence-penalized optimal transport problems, as mentioned above, an emerging body of literature investigates the effects of general penalization on the transport map.
In particular, what can be construed as the static version of the characterization of the density of the optimal static bridge appears in the work of
Bayraktar et al. 2025
and more recently in
Nutz 2025
,
González-Sanz et al. 2025b
.
We remind the reader that the setting of this paper corresponds to the dynamic non-entropic case, which, to the best of our knowledge, remained unexplored.
Moreover, the present work seems to be the first Schrödinger problems with weak targets.

Assumption 2.1

(i)

Weak targets.

In this work, we will consider transportation problems for which the terminal configuration is specified in a broader sense by leveraging the weak optimal transport problem (WOT) first considered by
Gozlan et al. 2017
.
Introduced as an equivalent tool to derive novel concentration inequalities, this generalization of optimal transport enables the construction of couplings between probability measures that possess desired structures or properties.
Given
c:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, as above, WOT is given by

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{c}(\mu,\nu):=\mathop{\mathrm{inf}\vphantom{\mathrm{sup}}}_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}c(x,\pi_{x})\mu({\mathrm{d}}x).

(2.2)

We write
{\cal W}_{c}
to stress the dependence of WOT in

.
Notice that when

, for
p\geq 1
,
{\cal W}_{c}^{1/p}
is nothing but the
p
-Wasserstein distance
W_{p}
.
Further details, results relevant to our analysis, and examples
are deferred to
Section

3.3
.
Recalling that
\mu=\nu\in{\cal P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\Longleftrightarrow W_{p}(\mu,\nu)=0

over the elements of

Definition 2.2

Given any two probability measures
\mu,\nu\in{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we write
\mu\overset{c}{=}\nu

Assumption 3.5
.

One of the following holds:
(i)

\mu_{0}=\nu_{0}
,
(ii)

Theorem 3.6
.

Let

Remark 3.7
.

Let us comment on the above result.
First, note that the existence of a dual optimizer is complemented by the integrability condition

In the case

Second, the reader might find it illustrative to note that in the case

and

.
In this case, we recover the well-known characterization of the optimal entropic optimal transport plan.
That is,

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}^{\star}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}}=\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{0}}{{\mathrm{d}}\nu_{0}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-C(X)}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\varphi^{\star}(X_{T})-\psi^{\star}(X_{0})}.

We will now state a verification theorem.
In the statement below, we use the time reversal mapping
\overleftarrow{{\cal T}}:\Omega\longrightarrow\Omega

\overleftarrow{{\cal T}}(\omega):=\overleftarrow{\omega}

\overleftarrow{\omega}_{t}:=\omega_{T-t}

Theorem 3.8
.

Let

and

Let

(i)

(ii)

(3.3)

Remark 3.9

When the measure

3.2

We now turn our attention to describing the flow of marginal measures

\mathbb{Q}^{\star}_{t}\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}^{\star}\circ X_{t}^{-1}

X

\mathbb{Q}^{\star}

Our first result consists of a characterization in terms of Markovian projections in the spirit of
Föllmer 1988

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
Assumptions
2.1
and
3.5
hold.
Assume that
C=0
and

is such that
{\cal W}_{c}(\mu,\nu)=0
if and only if
\mu=\nu

.
Further assume that
V_{c}(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})
and
V_{c}(\mu_{T},\mu_{0})
admit dual optimizers

and
\overleftarrow{\varphi}\in B_{b,p}(\mathbb{R}^{m})

Then there are two predictable processes
\alpha^{\star}
and
\overleftarrow{\alpha}
such that

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{\star}}\Big[\alpha^{\star}_{t}+\overleftarrow{\alpha}_{T-t}\circ\overleftarrow{{\cal T}}\,\big|X_{t}=x\Big]-\partial_{x}U(x)=\partial_{x}\log(\mathbb{Q}^{\star}_{t}(x)),\;{\mathrm{d}}t\otimes{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}_{t}^{\star}\text{\rm--a.e.}

(3.4)

where
\mathbb{Q}^{\star}_{t}
is the probability density function of
\mathbb{Q}^{\star}\circ X_{t}^{-1}
, and where the derivative is in the sense of distributions.

The above characterization result can be further specialized, depending on the choice of the function

We illustrate this with two corollary concerning popular choices of divergences.

Corollary 3.11

(Relative entropy)

Let the assumptions of

(i)

The

for two Borel-measurable functions

(ii)

If in addition the functions

with respective terminal conditions

\overleftarrow{v}(T,x)=\overleftarrow{\varphi}(x)

Remark 3.12
.

Some remarks are in order concerning the above characterization of the marginals of the Schrödinger bridge:
Based on the fact that the potentials
f
and
g
are also gradients, the characterization in
Corollary

3.11

becomes the well-known formula

\mathbb{Q}_{t}^{\star}(x)=\exp(v^{\star}(t,x))\exp(\overleftarrow{v}(T-t,x))\exp(U(x)).

We do not expect this property to extend to other divergence operators beyond the relative entropy.
This is because the functions
f
and
g
are constructed through solutions of stochastic optimal control problems
(
given in (
5.9
)
)
.
By verification theorems, such processes are constructed as minimizers of a Hamiltonian.
The said Hamiltonian would typically not have closed-form minimizers.
Nonetheless, we can obtain pointwise characterizations in some non-entropic cases, see e.g. Corollary
3.13
below.

Moreover, Theorem
3.10
also assumes that
{\cal W}_{c}
is a distance.
This assumption is important to relate the values
V_{c}(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})
and
V_{c}(\mu_{T},\mu_{0})
, which a key element of the proof.
We believe that this assumption cannot be dropped.

As discussed in the introduction, quadratic regularized optimal transport has been an active area in recent years, notably due to the sparsity property of optimal transport plans, see, e.g.,
[
27
,
26
,
45
,
25
,
61
,
58
,
59
]
.
In the dynamic setting this corresponds to the choice of
\chi^{2}
-divergence, the flow of marginals of which the following result characterizes:

Corollary 3.13
.

(
\chi^{2}
-divergence)

Let the assumptions of
Theorem

3.10
hold and let
\ell(x)=(x-1)^{2}/2
if

and
\infty

Further assume that the functions

v(T,x)=0\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{v}(t,x)-\partial_{x}\tilde{v}(t,x)\cdot\partial_{x}U(x)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\tilde{v}(t,x)=0\\
v(T,x)=\zeta(x)\end{cases}

(3.5)

with respective terminal conditions

Let

{\mathrm{d}}Z_{t}=-\partial_{x}v(t,X_{t}){\mathrm{d}}W_{t},\quad Z_{0}=1.

3.3
Examples

Let us conclude this section with some examples
of possible choices of divergences and weak costs
c
.

Divergence.

Beyond the classical case of relative entropy
\ell(x)=x\log x-x+1
discussed above, one could also consider the following choices of divergence for which we illustrate the form of our results.

(i)

\chi^{2}
-divergence
. This corresponds to
\ell(x)=(x-1)^{2}/2
if
x\geq 0
and
+\infty
else.
In this case, the convex conjugate is
\ell^{\ast}(x)=x+x^{2}/2
for
x\geq-1
and
\ell^{\ast}(x)=-1/2
else.
In this case, the primal optimizer
\mathbb{Q}^{\star}
is given by

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}^{\star}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}}=\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{0}}{{\mathrm{d}}\nu_{0}}(X_{0})\Big(1-\varphi^{\star}(X_{T})-C(X)-\psi^{\star}(X_{0})\Big)^{+}

where
\varphi^{\star}
is a dual optimizer and
x^{+}
denotes the positive part function; and if
c(x,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathds{1}_{\{x\neq y\}}\rho({\mathrm{d}}y)
,
the functions
\varphi^{\star}
and
\psi^{\star}
satisfy the system

\begin{cases}1=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\big[\big(1-\varphi^{\star}(X_{T})-C(X)-\psi(X_{0})\big)^{+}|X_{0}=x\big],\;\mu_{0}\text{\rm--a.e.}\\
1=\mathbb{E}^{\overleftarrow{\mathbb{P}}}\big[\big(1-\varphi^{\star}(\overleftarrow{X}_{T})-C(\overleftarrow{X})-\psi^{\star}(\overleftarrow{X}_{0})\big)^{+}|\overleftarrow{X}_{0}=x\big],\;\mu_{T}\text{\rm--a.e.}\end{cases}

(ii)

Tsallis entropy.
Given in terms of
\ell(x)=\frac{x^{q}-1}{q-1}
,
x\geq 1
and
+\infty
else, for
q\neq 1
.
This family encompasses a rather large class of
f
-divergences that fits our setting for
q>1
.
The convex conjugate is
\ell^{\ast}(x)=\frac{1}{q-1}+(\frac{q-1}{q}x)^{q/(q-1)}
for
x\geq 0
and
\ell^{\ast}(x)=\frac{1}{q-1}
else.
In this case, the primal optimizer
\mathbb{Q}^{\star}
is given by

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}^{\star}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}}=\frac{q-1}{q}^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{0}}{{\mathrm{d}}\nu_{0}}(X_{0})\Big(\big(-\varphi^{\star}(X_{T})-C(X)-\psi^{\star}(X_{0})\big)^{+}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}.

(iii)

Squared-Hellinger divergence
. Defined by
\ell(x)=(1-\sqrt{x})^{2}
if
x\geq 0
and
+\infty
else.
This choice offers a slight variation of the previous example.
Though it does not satisfy the growth assumption at infinity, we illustrate the form of our results.
The convex conjugate is
\ell^{\ast}(x)=x/(1-x)
for
x<1
and
\ell^{\ast}(x)=\infty
else.
In this case, the primal optimizer
\mathbb{Q}^{\star}
is given by

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}^{\star}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}}=\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mu_{0}}{{\mathrm{d}}\nu_{0}}(X_{0})\frac{1}{\big\{\big(1-\varphi^{\star}(X_{T})-C(X)-\psi^{\star}(X_{0})\big)^{+}\big\}^{2}}

where
\varphi^{\star}
is a dual optimizer

Weak transport costs.

Let us first note that the weak OT problem (
2.2
) admits the probabilistic interpretation

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{c}(\mu,\nu)=\mathop{\mathrm{inf}\vphantom{\mathrm{sup}}}_{X\sim\mu,Y\sim\nu}\mathbb{E}[c(X,\mathbb{E}[Y|X])],

revealing how, though initially motivated by applications to geometric inequalities, weak optimal transport theory highlights the relevance and implications of imposing structural constraints on feasible plans beyond fixed marginals.

We now detail some instances of costs
c
and elucidate their associated relation
\mathbb{Q}_{T}\coloneq\mathbb{Q}\circ X_{T}^{-1}\overset{c}{=}\mu_{T}
induced by
{\cal W}_{c}(\mathbb{Q}_{T},\mu_{T})=0
.
We refer the reader to
[
29
,
30
]
for a detailed account of these instances.

(i)

Total variation
. As mentioned above, when
c(x,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathds{1}_{\{x\neq y\}}\rho({\mathrm{d}}y)
,
{\cal W}_{c}
is the total variation distance and
\mathbb{Q}_{T}\overset{c}{=}\mu_{T}
if and only if
\mathbb{Q}_{T}=\mu_{T}
and in this case
Q_{c}\varphi=\varphi
.

(ii)

Marton’s cost.
Takes its name from the work
[
42
]
and further studied in
[
30
]
. The Marton’s cost functional is given by

c(x,\pi_{x})=\theta\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\delta(\textup{c}(x,y))\pi_{x}({\mathrm{d}}y)\big),

where
\textup{c}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a classical cost,

\delta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is l.s.c. and
\theta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
is convex.
It has the probabilistic interpretation

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{c}(\mathbb{Q}_{T},\mu_{T})=\mathop{\mathrm{inf}\vphantom{\mathrm{sup}}}_{X_{T}\sim\mathbb{Q}_{T},Y\sim\mu_{T}}\mathbb{E}\big[\theta\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\delta(c(X_{T},Y))|X_{T}]\big)\big].

The classical transport problem with cost
c
is recovered with the choices
\theta(x)=\delta(x)=x
.
In this case,
Q_{c}\varphi(x)=\varphi^{c}(x)\coloneqq\mathop{\mathrm{inf}\vphantom{\mathrm{sup}}}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\big\{\varphi(y)+\|x-y\|^{p}\big\}
, is the
c
-transform of
\varphi
, see
[
57
, Chapter 5]
.
In particular, if
\textup{c}(x,y)=\|x-y\|^{p}
and
\textup{c}(x,y)=\mathds{1}_{\{x\neq y\}}
then
{\cal W}_{c}
become the
p
-Wasserstein and the total variation, respectively.
In this context, Brenier’s Theorem
[
7
]
shows that if
\textup{c}\equiv\|x-y\|^{p},p>1
and
\mathbb{Q}_{T}\ll{\rm Leb}
, then
\mathbb{Q}_{T}\overset{c}{=}\mu_{T}
implies there exists some convex function
\phi
such that
\nabla\phi
pushes forward
\mathbb{Q}_{T}
onto
\mu_{T}
.

(iii)

Barycentric cost.
The Barycentric cost corresponds to

c(x,\pi_{x})=\theta\big(x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}y\pi_{x}({\mathrm{d}}y)\big),

for
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
convex and l.s.c. That is, we have probabilistic interpretation

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{c}(\mathbb{Q}_{T},\mu_{T})=\mathop{\mathrm{inf}\vphantom{\mathrm{sup}}}_{X_{T}\sim\mathbb{Q}_{T},Y\sim\mu_{T}}\mathbb{E}\big[\theta\big(X_{T}-\mathbb{E}[Y|X_{T}]\big)\big].

This choice captures the existence of
martingale couplings
between two measures.
That is,
\mathbb{Q}_{T}\overset{c}{=}\mu_{T}
ensures that there exists a martingale coupling between
\mathbb{Q}_{T}
and
\mu_{T}
.
In this case,
Q_{c}\varphi(x)=Q_{\theta}\varphi_{cvx}(x)
, where
\varphi_{cvx}
denotes the greatest convex function
\varphi_{cvx}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that
\varphi_{cvx}\leq\varphi
and
Q_{\theta}\varphi\coloneqq\mathop{\mathrm{inf}\vphantom{\mathrm{sup}}}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\big\{\varphi(y)+\theta(x-y)\big\}
, see
[
29
,
2
]
for further details.

(iv)

Distributionally robust costs.
This weak cost builds on the
variance case with a quadratic cost studied
in
Alibert et al. 2019
, the distributionally robust cost takes the form

c(x,\pi_{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\textup{c}(x,y)\pi_{x}({\mathrm{d}}y)-\lambda^{-1}{\rm Var}(\pi_{x}),\;\text{ where, }{\rm Var}(\rho)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big\|y-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}y^{\prime}\rho(dy^{\prime})\Big\|^{2}\rho({\mathrm{d}}y),

and
\lambda>0
,
acts as an interpolation parameter between the classical cost
c
and the variance of
\pi_{x}
, thus favouring the spreading of the measures
\pi_{x}
.
Thanks to
[
1
, Lemma 5.5]
, for any
r>0
, with
\lambda:={\rm Var}(\nu)/r^{2}
and
{\rm c}(x,y)=\|x-y\|^{2}
it holds that

{\cal W}_{c}(\mu,\nu)+r^{2}\geq W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu).

Thus,
\mathbb{Q}_{T}\overset{c}{=}\mu_{T}
implies
\mathbb{Q}_{T}\in B_{W_{2}}(\mu_{T},r)
,
where
B_{W_{2}}(\nu,r)
is the Wasserstein-2 ball of radius
r>0
centered at
\nu\in{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. In this case,
Q_{c}\varphi(x)=\mathop{\mathrm{inf}\vphantom{\mathrm{sup}}}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\big\{\varphi(y)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}\big\}
is the Moreau-Yosida transform of
\varphi
.

4
Preliminaries

Before presenting the proofs of the main results, we gather a few technical lemmas that will be used in the proofs.
We first establish some convexity properties.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
2.1
hold.
The set
{\cal P}_{c}(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})
and the mapping
(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})\longmapsto V_{c}(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})
are convex.

Proof.

We begin by convexity of
{\cal P}_{c}(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})
.
Let
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}=\lambda\mathbb{Q}^{1}+(1-\lambda)\mathbb{Q}^{2}
for
\mathbb{Q}^{1},\mathbb{Q}^{2}\in{\cal P}_{c}(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})
.
It is clear that
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\circ X^{-1}_{0}=\mu_{0}
, thus
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\in{\cal P}(\mu_{0})
.
Recall from
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
the duality formula for
{\cal W}_{c}

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{c}(\mu,\mu_{T})=\sup_{\varphi\in C_{b,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(\langle Q_{c}\varphi,\mu\rangle-\langle\varphi,\mu_{T}\rangle).

(4.1)

Note that
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\circ X^{-1}_{T}=\lambda\mathbb{Q}^{1}\circ X^{-1}_{T}+(1-\lambda)\mathbb{Q}^{2}\circ X^{-1}_{T}
, and, since
c
is nonnegative, it follows that

0\leq{\cal W}_{c}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\circ X^{-1}_{T},\mu_{T})\leq\lambda{\cal W}_{c}(\mathbb{Q}^{1}\circ X^{-1}_{T},\mu_{T})+(1-\lambda){\cal W}_{c}(\mathbb{Q}^{2}\circ X^{-1}_{T},\mu_{T})=0.

Hence,
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\in{\cal P}(\mu_{0},\mu_{T})
.

Regarding convexity of
V_{c}
, let
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
\mu_{0}^{i},\mu_{T}^{i}\in{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
i=1,2
be given, and
\mathbb{Q}^{i}\in{\cal P}_{c}(\mu_{0}^{i},\mu_{T}^{i})
.
Let
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}=\lambda\mathbb{Q}^{1}+(1-\lambda)\mathbb{Q}^{2}
and define
{\overline{\mu}}_{0}
and
{\overline{\mu}}_{T}
similarly.
We claim that
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\in{\cal P}_{c}({\overline{\mu}}_{0},{\overline{\mu}}_{T})
.
In fact,
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\circ X^{-1}_{0}={\overline{\mu}}_{0}
is clear.
Furthermore,

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\circ X^{-1}_{T}=\lambda\mathbb{Q}^{1}\circ X^{-1}_{T}+(1-\lambda)\mathbb{Q}^{2}\circ X^{-1}_{T}
, and since
c
is nonnegative, it follows from (

\partial_{x}\overleftarrow{v}(t,x)\end{pmatrix}.

(5.23)

In other words, under
\overleftarrow{\mathbb{Q}^{\star}}
the canonical process
{\cal Y}
on
{\cal C}_{m}\times{\cal C}_{1}
satisfies the SDE

d{\cal Y}_{t}=\widehat{B}_{t}({\cal Y}_{t})dt+\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}({\cal Y}_{t})dW_{t},\quad\overleftarrow{\mathbb{Q}^{\star}}\mbox{-a.s.}

(5.24)

By (
5.24
) and (
5.22
) we thus deduce that
\widehat{B}=\overleftarrow{B}
and
\widehat{\Sigma}=\overleftarrow{\Sigma}
.
That is,

\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\partial_{x}\overleftarrow{v}(T-t,x)}{z}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}U(x)\\
\frac{|\partial_{x}\overleftarrow{v}(T-t,x)|^{2}}{z}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial_{x}v(t,x)}{z}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}U(x)\\
-\frac{|\partial_{x}v(t,x)|^{2}}{z}-\Delta v(t,x)\end{pmatrix}+\Sigma\Sigma^{\top}_{t}(x,z)\nabla\log\mathbb{Q}^{\star}_{t}(x,z),

which is tantamount to the desired result.
∎

We begin by recalling the duality properties of divergences.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\ell
satisfy
2.1
.

(
i
)

.
For every
\bar{\mathbb{P}}\in{\rm Prob}(\Omega)
, the functions
{\cal I}_{\ell}(\cdot|\bar{\mathbb{P}})
and
\Phi_{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}
are conjugate of one another. More precisely, it holds

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\bar{\mathbb{P}})=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{0}}\big(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\xi]-\Phi_{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}(\xi)\big)\quad\mbox{for all }\mathbb{Q}\ll\bar{\mathbb{P}}

(A.1)

and

\Phi_{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}(\xi)=\sup_{\mathbb{Q}\ll\bar{\mathbb{P}}}\big(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\xi]-{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\bar{\mathbb{P}})\big),\;\mbox{for all }\xi\mbox{ such that }\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}[\ell^{\ast}(\xi^{+})]<\infty.

Moreover, for each
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{0}
such that
\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}[\ell^{\ast}(\xi^{+})]<\infty

such that

\Phi_{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{\star}}[\xi]-{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}^{\star}|\bar{\mathbb{P}})

Such an optimizer is given by

(A.2)

where

is uniquely defined by
\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}[\partial_{x}\ell^{*}(\xi-r^{\star})]=1
.
This constant also satisfies
\Phi_{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}[\ell^{*}(\xi-r^{\star})]+r^{\star}
.

Proof.

The duality statement is the variational representation of
f
-divergences, see
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
or
[
48
, Theorem 7.27]
.
The existence and characterization of optimizers is

The following result can be seen as (an instance of) the data processing inequality, well known in the entropic case.
In the context of mathematical statistics, it pertains to the so-called sufficiency of a statistic
f:\Omega\longrightarrow\Omega
.
The following result appeared first in
[
40
]
, though in a slightly different setting due to the formulation of divergences.
For the sake of completeness and the reader’s convenience, we present a proof.

Lemma A.2
.

For any measurable map
f:\Omega\longrightarrow\Omega
we have that

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}\circ f^{-1}|\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1})\leq{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P})

(A.3)

and whenever
{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P})<\infty
, the previous inequality is tight if and only if
\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}}
is a measurable function of
f
.

Proof.

Let us first argue (
A.3
).
Note that when
\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}
does hold, (
A.3
) is clear.
Suppose now
\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}
and let
\hat{\xi}=\xi\circ f
for
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{\infty}
and note that by definition
\Phi_{\mathbb{P}}(\xi\circ f)=\Phi_{\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1}}(\xi)
.
Thus, thanks to (
A.1
),

{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P})\geq\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}\circ f^{-1}}[\xi]-\Phi_{\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1}}(\xi).

Taking the supremum over
\xi\in\mathbb{L}^{0}
, we deduce (
A.3
).

Suppose now that

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}}=h\circ f
for some measurable function
h
.
We claim that
\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}\circ f^{-1}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1}}=h
.
Indeed, for all
\xi
we have

\int_{\Omega}\xi(\omega)\mathbb{Q}\circ f^{-1}({\mathrm{d}}\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\xi(f(\omega))\mathbb{Q}({\mathrm{d}}\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\xi(f(\omega))h(f(\omega))\mathbb{P}({\mathrm{d}}\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\xi(\omega)h(\omega)\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1}({\mathrm{d}}\omega)

where the second equality follows by the Radon-Nikodym theorem.
Consequently,

{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P})=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\Big[\ell\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}}\Big)\Big]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}[\ell(h\circ f)]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1}}[\ell(h)]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1}}\Big[\ell\Big(\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}\circ f^{-1}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1}}\Big)\Big]={\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}\circ f^{-1}|{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1}).

Let us now assume that
{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}\circ f^{-1}|\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1})={\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P})<\infty
. Since
{\cal I}_{\ell}(\mathbb{Q}\circ f^{-1}|\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1})<\infty
, we deduce that there exists
h
such that for any
\xi
,

\int_{\Omega}\xi(\omega)\mathbb{Q}\circ f^{-1}({\mathrm{d}}\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\xi(\omega)h(\omega)\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1}({\mathrm{d}}\omega),

and thus

\int_{\Omega}\xi(f(\omega))\mathbb{Q}({\mathrm{d}}\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\xi(\omega)\mathbb{Q}\circ f^{-1}({\mathrm{d}}\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\xi(\omega)h(\omega)\mathbb{P}\circ f^{-1}({\mathrm{d}}\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\xi(f(\omega))h(f(\omega))\mathbb{P}({\mathrm{d}}\omega).

By the
\mathbb{P}\text{\rm--a.s.}
uniqueness of
\dfrac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{Q}}{{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}}
, it follows again by Radon-Nikodym theorem that it is a measurable function of
f
.
∎

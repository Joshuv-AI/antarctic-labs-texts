On the Upsilon invariant in grid homology
Hajime Kubota

Abstract
. The Upsilon invariant is a concordance invariant in knot Floer homology. Földvári [ 1 ] reconstructed the Upsilon invariant using grid homology. We prove that the Upsilon invariant in knot Floer homology and one in grid homology are equivalent. Furthermore, we show some properties of the Upsilon invariant in the framework of grid homology.

1.
Introduction

Ozsváth-Stipsicz-Szabó
[
8
]
defined the Upsilon invariant
\Upsilon_{K}\colon[0,2]\to\mathbb{R}
for a knot
K\subset S^{3}
.
In this paper, we call it
the original Upsilon invariant
.
The original Upsilon invariant is a smooth concordance invariant and gives bounds for knot invariants such as the slice genus, concordance genus, and the unknotting number.
This invariant was first defined by constructing a family of chain complexes
\{tCFK(K)\}_{t\in[0,2]}
from the knot Floer complex
CFK^{-}(K)
and taking their homologies.
Later, Livingston
[
3
]
gave an alternative formulation of the original Upsilon invariant using the full knot Floer complex
CFK^{\infty}(K)
.

Grid homology is a combinatorial reconstruction of knot Floer homology developed by Manolescu-Ozsváth-Szabó-Thurston
[
5
]
.
Földvári
[
1
]
reconstructed the Upsilon invariant using grid homology and showed that it is an invariant for topological knots.
In this paper, we call it
the grid Upsilon invariant
.
Later, the author
[
2
]
proved the concordance invariance of the grid Upsilon invariant in the framework of grid homology.

The definition of the grid Upsilon invariant and the definition of the original Upsilon invariant are very similar but there are subtle differences.
Therefore it is not obvious whether they are the same, even if there is a chain homotopy equivalence between grid complexes and knot Floer complexes.
Földvári
[
1
]
raised the question of whether the grid Upsilon invariant is equivalent to the original Upsilon invariant and whether the known properties of the original Upsilon invariant are proved in the framework of grid homology.

Even though the equivalence of the grid and the original upsilon invariant was unknown, grid homology is useful to study the original Upsilon invariant.
Ozsváth-Stipsicz-Szabó
[
9
]
gave a combinatorial description of the upsilon invariant
\upsilon(K):=\Upsilon_{K}(1)
.
They studied the behavior of the modified grid chain complex corresponding to the complex
tCFK(K)
of
t=1
under surface cobordisms and showed that
\upsilon(K)
gives a lower bound for the smooth four-dimensional crosscap number of
K
.
Sano-Sato
[
10
]
computed the original Upsilon invariant for prime knots with up to
11
crossings.
We remark that they used grid representations of knots but a different approach from this paper.

In this paper, we show that the grid Upsilon invariant equals the original Upsilon invariant using the equivalence of knot Floer homology and grid homology.
In addition, we prove some known properties of the original Upsilon invariant in the framework of grid homology.
Because the proof is done algebraically, we will not review the precise definition of grid homology.
See
[
5
]
or
[
7
]
for details.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a knot
K\subset S^{3}
, the grid Upsilon invariant coincides with the original Upsilon invariant.

We briefly explain the idea of the proof.
The original Upsilon invariant is defined by the following two steps
[
8
]
:
First, we apply the algebraic operation called
t
-modification to obtain a one-parameter family of chain complexes
\{tCFK(K)\}_{t\in[0.2]}
from the knot Floer complex
CFK^{-}(K)
.
Then the Upsilon invariant
\Upsilon_{K}
at
t
is defined by the maximal grading of the homogeneous, non-torsion element of
H(tCFK(K))
.
The grid Upsilon invariant is defined similarly.
The grid chain complex
GC^{-}(\mathbb{G})
is a finitely generated complex over
\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\dots,U_{n}]
.
The
t
-modified grid chain complex
tGC^{-}(\mathbb{G})
is defined by the resulting complex by applying
t
-modification to
\frac{GC^{-}(\mathbb{G})}{U_{1}=\dots=U_{n}}
.
Then the grid Upsilon invariant is defined by the maximal grading of the homogeneous, non-torsion element of
H(tGC^{-}(\mathbb{G}))
.
The following schematic picture describes the situation of the two Upsilon invariants.

The essential parts of the proof are to give a relation between
tCFK(K)
and
tGC^{-}(\mathbb{G})
using the chain homotopy equivalence
CFK^{-}(K)\simeq GC^{-}(\mathbb{G})
which implies the equivalence of knot Floer homology and grid homology and to observe that the effect of taking quotient
GC^{-}(\mathbb{G})\to\frac{GC^{-}(\mathbb{G})}{U_{1}=\dots=U_{n}}
can be ignored in some sense.

If a grid diagram
\mathbb{G}
represents a knot
K
, its horizontal reflection
\mathbb{G}^{*}
represents the mirror image of
K
.
Then there is an isomorphism between
\frac{GC^{-}(\mathbb{G}^{*})}{U_{1}=\dots=U_{n}}
and the dual complex
(\frac{GC^{-}(\mathbb{G})}{U_{1}=\dots=U_{n}})^{*}
with some grading shift.
By lifting it to a relation between
t
-modified complexes
tGC^{-}(\mathbb{G}^{*})
and
(tGC^{-}(\mathbb{G}))^{*}
, we can obtain the following.

Proposition 1.2
.

For a knot
K
and
t\in[0,2]
, the grid Upsilon invariant satisfies

\Upsilon_{m(K)}(t)=-\Upsilon_{K}(t),

where
m(K)
is the mirror of
K
.

This proposition in knot Floer homology can be quickly proved because the taking reflection of the Heegaard diagram induces the chain homotopy equivalent
CFK^{-}(m(K))\simeq CFK^{-}(K)^{*}
and then
tCFK(m(K))\simeq tCFK(K)^{*}
.
On the other hand, in grid homology, the situation is a little more complicated since we need to take quotient
GC^{-}(\mathbb{G})\to\frac{GC^{-}(\mathbb{G})}{U_{1}=\dots=U_{n}}
.

It is known that the original Upsilon invariant of an alternating knot is completely determined by the Alexander polynomial and the signature
[
8
, Theorem 1.14]
.
This is deduced by that knot Floer homology of an alternating knot is also determined by the Alexander polynomial and its signature, and Proposition
1.2
in knot Floer homology.
In fact, grid homology for an alternating knot is also proved to be determined by the Alexander polynomial and the signature (Theorem
2.13
).
Using this and Proposition
1.2
, we obtain that the Upsilon invariant of an alternating knot is completely determined by the Alexander polynomial and the signature in the framework of grid homology.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
K
is an alternating knot (or more generally, a quasi-alternating knot), then

\Upsilon_{K}(t)=(1-|t-1|)\frac{\sigma(K)}{2},

where
\sigma(K)
is the signature of
K
.

Definition 2.5

(
[

)

The ring of long power series

\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in A}v^{\alpha}|A\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},A\ \text{is well-ordered}\right\},

The product is defined by

\left(\sum_{\alpha\in A}v^{\alpha}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{\beta\in B}v^{\beta}\right)=\sum_{\gamma\in A+B}\#\{(\alpha,\beta)\in A\times B|\alpha+\beta=\gamma\}\cdot v^{\gamma},

\mathbb{Z}

\mathbb{Z}

Definition 2.11

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The idea is the same as the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 1.14]
.
Since we can use Theorem
2.13
and Proposition
1.2
that are combinatorially proved, the same argument as the proof of

, Theorem 1.14]
works.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be an
n\times n
grid diagram for
K
and
\mathcal{C}
be a finitely generated filtered complex over
\mathbb{F}[U]
obtained from
\mathcal{GC}^{-}(\mathbb{G})
by Lemma
3.3
.
Since
\mathcal{C}\simeq tCFK(K)\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\otimes(n-1)}
, we have

H(\mathcal{C})/\mathrm{Tors}(H(\mathcal{C}))\cong\mathbb{F}[U]\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\otimes(n-1)}\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}(\mathbb{F}[U]\llbracket i,i\rrbracket)^{c_{i}},

Now, with a slight modification, we apply the same argument as the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 1.14]
.
By Proposition
1.2
, we can assume that
-\frac{\sigma(K)}{2}\geq 0
.
We can take
2^{n-1}

x^{i}_{0},\dots,x^{i}_{n},y^{i}_{0},\dots,y^{i}_{n-1}

k_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,2^{n-1}
in
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the followings:

•

For each
i
and
j

\partial y^{i}_{j}=Ux^{i}_{j}+x^{i}_{j+1}
,

•

For each
i
and
j

A(x^{i}_{j})=-\frac{\sigma(K)}{2}-2j-k_{i}

•

Let

Abstract

The scattering number
s(G)
of graph
G=(V,E)
is defined as
s(G)
=max
{
c(G-S)-|S|
}
, where the maximum is taken over all proper subsets
S\subseteq V(G)
, and
c(G-S)
denotes the number of components of
G-S
. In 1988, Enomoto introduced a variation of toughness
\tau(G)
of a graph
G
, which is defined by
\tau(G)
=min
{
\frac{|S|}{c(G-S)-1}
,
S\subseteq V(G)
and
c(G-S)>1
}
. Both the scattering number and toughness are used to characterize the invulnerability or stability of a graph, i.e., the ability of a graph to remain connected after vertices or edges are removed. The smaller the value of
s(G)
(or the larger the value of
\tau(G)
), the stronger the connectivity of a graph
G
. The
A_{\alpha}
-spectral radius of
G
is denoted by
\rho_{\alpha}(G)
. Using typical
A_{\alpha}
-spectral techniques and structural analysis, we present a sufficient condition such that
s(G)\leq 1
. This result generalizes the result of Chen, Li and Xu [Graphs Comb. 41 (2025)]. Furthermore, we establish a sufficient condition with respect to the
A_{\alpha}
-spectral radius for a graph to be
\tau
-tough. When
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, our result reduces to that of Chen, Li and Xu [Graphs Comb. 41 (2025)].

s\geq 1
. Next we consider the following two cases based on the value of
s
.

Case 1.

s=1
.

In this case,
G^{\prime\prime}=K_{1}\vee(K_{n-b-2}\cup(b+1)K_{1})
. By
(18)
and
(19)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(G)\leq\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime\prime})

(20)

with equality if and only if
G\cong G^{\prime\prime}
. By the assumption
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime\prime})\leq\rho_{\alpha}(G)
, we have
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime\prime})=\rho_{\alpha}(G)
which implies
G\cong G^{\prime\prime}
.

Take
S=V(K_{1})
, thus
\tau(G^{\prime\prime})=\frac{s}{c(G^{\prime\prime}-S)-1}=\frac{1}{b+1}<\tau
, which implies
G^{\prime\prime}
is not
\tau
-tough.
So
G\cong G^{\prime\prime}
.

Case 2.

s\geq 2
.

According to Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime\prime})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2m(1-\alpha)}{n-1}+\alpha n-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\alpha)((b+2)bs^{2}-(2bn-3b-2)s+n^{2}-3n+2)}{n-1}+\alpha n-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-\alpha}{n-1}[(b+2)bs^{2}-(2bn-3b-2)s+n^{2}-3n+2]+\alpha n-1.

(21)

Let
g(s)=(b+2)bs^{2}-(2bn-3b-2)s+n^{2}-3n+2
. It is clear that
n-(b+1)s-1\geq 1
. Thus
2\leq s\leq\frac{n-2}{b+1}
. By a calculation,

g(2)-g(\frac{n-2}{b+1})=\frac{(n-2b-4)((n-7)b^{2}-2b^{3}-5b-2)}{(b+1)^{2}}\geq 0
,

where the inequality follows from the fact that
n\geq 2\tau^{2}+5\tau+\frac{2}{\tau}+8
. This implies that
g(s)
attains its maximum value at
s=2
when
2\leq s\leq\frac{n-2}{b+1}
. Combining this with
(21)
and
n\geq\frac{(5-6\alpha)\tau^{2}+(13-14\alpha)\tau+4(1-\alpha)}{(1-2\alpha)\tau^{2}+(3-4\alpha)\tau}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime\prime})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\alpha)g(2)}{n-1}+\alpha n-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\alpha)(n^{2}-(3+4b)n+4b^{2}+14b+6)}{n-1}+\alpha n-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-b-2+\frac{-(3b-(2+4b)\alpha+1)n+(1-\alpha)(4b^{2}+14b+6)-b-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n-b-2

\displaystyle+\frac{-(3b-(2+4b)\alpha+1)\frac{(5-6\alpha)\tau^{2}+(13-14\alpha)\tau+4(1-\alpha)}{(1-2\alpha)\tau^{2}+(3-4\alpha)\tau}+(1-\alpha)(4b^{2}+14b+6)-b-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-b-2.

(22)

Since
K_{n-b-1}
is a proper subgraph of
K_{1}\vee(K_{n-b-2}\cup(\frac{1}{\tau}+1)K_{1})
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(K_{1}\vee(K_{n-b-2}\cup(\frac{1}{\tau}+1)K_{1}))>\rho_{\alpha}(K_{n-b-1})=n-b-2.

(23)

It follows from
(18),(19),(22)
and
(23)
that

\rho_{\alpha}(G)\leq\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})\leq\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime\prime})\leq n-b-2=\rho_{\alpha}(K_{n-b-1})<\rho_{\alpha}(K_{1}\vee(K_{n-b-2}\cup(b+1)K_{1}))
.

In conclusion, we have

\rho_{\alpha}(G)<\rho_{\alpha}(K_{1}\vee(K_{n-\frac{1}{\tau}-2}\cup(\frac{1}{\tau}+1)K_{1}))
.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\square

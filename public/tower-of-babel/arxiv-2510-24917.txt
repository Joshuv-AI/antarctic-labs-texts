Observation of vector rogue waves in repulsive three-component atomic mixtures
G. A. Bougas, G. C. Katsimiga, S. Mossman, P. Engels, P. G. Kevrekidis, S. I. Mistakidis

Abstract
We report the experimental observation of vector extensions of Peregrine solitons in highly particle-imbalanced, pairwise immiscible three-component repulsive Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs). The possibility of an effectively attractive character of the minority components is established by constructing a generalized reduction scheme for an imbalanced 𝒩 \mathcal{N} -component setup with arbitrary interaction signs. These components may suffer intra- and inter-component modulation instability, which along with the presence of an attractive potential well induces the dynamical formation of highly reproducible vector rogue waves. Exploiting different Rb hyperfine states, it is possible to flexibly tune the effective interactions stimulating the realization of a plethora of vector rogue waves, including single and double Peregrine-like wave peaks. The experimental findings are in quantitative agreement with suitable three-dimensional mean-field simulations, while quasi-one-dimensional analysis of the non-polynomial Schrödinger model provides additional insights into the rogue wave characteristics.



Introduction.

Rogue waves (RWs), originally measured through the Draupner platform in North sea
Kharif et al. 2008
, are extreme nonlinear wave events of great steepness arising suddenly and dissolving without a trace
Hopkin 2004
;
Kharif et al. 2008
.
These waves are ubiquitous in nature and are observed in fields ranging from water tanks

;
Chabchoub et al. 2012

, nonlinear optics

;
Solli et al. 2007
, to oceanography
Kharif et al. 2008
;
Perkins 2006
, atmosphere
Stenflo and Marklund 2010
, capillaries
Shats et al. 2010

Yan 2010
;
Yan 2011
.
Prototypes of RWs, within the generic
nonlinear Schrödinger model, include the Peregrine soliton (PS)

and Kuznetzov-Ma

;
Ma 1979

A fundamental condition

;

Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs) offer accessible quantum simulators to realize – among numerous other things – RWs and MI owing to their phenomenal controllability and handling of system parameters
Bloch et al. 2008
;
Bloch et al. 2012
;
Mistakidis et al. 2023
.
For instance, MI has been exploited for generating bright solitary waves
Strecker et al. 2002
;
Strecker et al. 2003
;
Nguyen et al. 2017
, the renowned Townes solitons
Chen and Hung 2020
and necklaces thereof
Banerjee et al. 2025
as well as dispersive shock-waves
Tamura et al. 2025
in genuinely attractive, lower dimensional, single-component BECs.
Recently, immiscible binary BECs featuring repulsive interactions have been utilized not only to demonstrate the nonlinear stage of MI
Biondini and Mantzavinos 2016
in particle balanced mixtures
Mossman et al. 2025
but also to controllably nucleate the Townes soliton
Bakkali-Hassani et al. 2021
;
Bakkali-Hassani and Dalibard 2022
and PS
Romero-Ros et al. 2024

The latter remarkable and unexpected features for fully repulsive media, are rooted in the reduction of a two-component immiscible system to an effectively attractive single-component one

. This avoids the complications stemming from wave collapse of an actual attractive condensate
Timmermans 1998
;
Bakkali-Hassani et al. 2021
.

The versatility of BECs increases further when considering higher-component mixtures, such as three-component systems,
Stamper-Kurn and Ueda 2013
;
Wu et al. 2011
;
Taglieber et al. 2008
;
Bersano et al. 2018
;
Lannig et al. 2020
which can support a wider range of phases and nonlinear excitations.
Following up on corresponding observations of
one-component dark
Burger et al. 1999
;
Weller et al. 2008

and two-component dark-bright
Becker et al. 2008
states,
spinor condensates have enabled the realization of
vector dark-bright-bright
Bersano et al. 2018
;
Lannig et al. 2020
solitons, as well
as more complex patterns including ferrodark
(and antidark) solitary waves
Yu and Blakie 2022a
;
Yu and Blakie 2022b
.
In optics, examples of dark
multi-component RWs have
appeared in
Frisquet et al. 2016
;
Baronio et al. 2018
.
However, an observation of vector RWs and their potential interactions remains unprecedented in the cold atom realm.
Generation of such vector RWs demands exquisite controllability of the quantum simulator.
Additionally, the generalization of the above reduction scheme to
\mathcal{N}
-component immiscible repulsive highly-imbalanced BECs to effective lower component attractive ones remains elusive.

In this Letter, we experimentally and theoretically showcase a prototype, in the context of quantum
simulators, of the dynamical generation of vector RWs in the form of PSs.
We achieve this, in a highly controllable and reproducible fashion, by exploiting a weak attractive potential well atop three-component, highly
particle-imbalanced, repulsive BECs.
The majority component is pairwise immiscible with at least one minority. Hence, the triple mixture can be reduced to a two-component BEC featuring a form
of effectively attractive interactions
and, accordingly, is able to host either single PSs in one minority component [Fig.

(a5), (c5)], or vector PSs, i.e., one per minority component, [Fig.

(c)] as well
as vector twin Peregrine structures [Fig.

To explain the presence of effective attractive interactions we
systematically
extract an analytical reduction scheme for a repulsive highly-imbalanced
\mathcal{N}
-component setting.
This can lead to repulsive or attractive effective interactions,
and provides a detailed understanding

;
Bakkali-Hassani et al. 2021

Romero-Ros et al. 2024

Equipped with the pairwise miscibility or immiscibility of the triple mixture components, along with the systematic analysis of the

;
Kasamatsu and Tsubota 2006

Our experimental measurements are in quantitative agreement with three-dimensional (3D) mean-field simulations in the presence of three-body losses.
Extending the approach of Ref.
Salasnich 2009

Within this framework we demonstrate a plethora of different vector PSs in various three-component settings,
showcasing the spinor BEC system as an ideal playground
for the exploration of multi-component RW patterns.

Theoretical Analysis: Reduction scheme.

A repulsive, highly particle-imbalanced, two-component gas in the immiscible regime (
a_{12}^{2}>a_{11}a_{22}
) can be reduced to an effective single-component attractive one
Dutton and Clark 2005
, a
reduction experimentally leveraged
for both the Townes soliton
Bakkali-Hassani et al. 2021
and the single PS
Romero-Ros et al. 2024
.
Here, we
generalize
this approach to an arbitrary number of components, see SM
sup
,
elucidating the phenomenology observed below.

For the experimental setups described, the presence of one majority and two minority components yields an effective two-component reduction.
To illustrate this, we focus on the time-independent version of Eqs. (
1
), with
K^{(j)}_{3}=0
, and apply the Thomas-Fermi approximation
Pethick and Smith 2008
for the majority component.
Substitution of the majority density back to the equations of the minority species results in an effective two-component system with renormalized scattering lengths
sup

a_{mm^{\prime}}^{(\rm{eff})}=a_{{m}{m}^{\prime}}-\frac{a_{{m}{M}}a_{{m}^{\prime}{M}}}{a_{{MM}}},\quad\forall~{m},{m}^{\prime}.

(2)

Evidently, Eq. (
2

a^{(\rm{eff})}_{{m}{m}^{\prime}}
can have
can have positive or negative signs even though all intra- and intercomponent scattering lengths between the individual components are positive.
Hence, different effective attractive two-component setups can be realized dictated by the combination of distinct hyperfine states accommodating the potential formation of vector or multiple RWs.

RW generation in these effective attractive environments is inherently linked to inter- or intra species dominated MI
Goldstein and Meystre 1997
;
Kasamatsu and Tsubota 2006

a^{({eff})}_{{m}{m}^{\prime}}

Intra-species MI translates to
a^{({eff})}_{{m}\rm{m}}

Inter-species MI is dominant when the following condition
applies

|a^{(\rm{eff})}_{{m}{m}^{\prime}}|>|a^{(\rm{eff})}_{{m}{m}}|\left(2\frac{|a^{({eff})}_{{m}{m}}|}{a^{(\rm{eff})}_{{m}^{\prime}{m}^{\prime}}}+1\right),\quad{m}\neq{m}^{\prime}.

(3)

These MI conditions, in turn, determine the existence
of PSs per component, as we now argue.

Conclusions.

We have reported the
experimental observation of vector PS configurations arising in repulsive three-component BECs capable of emulating effectively attractive two- and single-component systems.
To support the analysis we have developed a generic reduction scheme providing the mapping of a repulsive
\mathcal{N}
-component setting to one with

\mathcal{N}-k

minority components, assuming

k
majority components, with the
completely general details provided
in
sup
.
This substantially expands the results of Refs.
Dutton and Clark 2005
;
Bakkali-Hassani et al. 2021
;
Romero-Ros et al. 2024
, charting
new directions for multi-component
systems.

Exploiting an attractive potential well, we show the controlled and highly reproducible (between
computation and experiment) dynamical formation of a plethora of vector PS structures.
These include single and twin Peregrines in the components constituting the considered unstable backgrounds suffering intra- or inter-component MI
Kasamatsu and Tsubota 2006
.
We showcase “bright-bright”
and “dark-bright” PS states, not only
materializing, but also generalizing
states that were earlier predicted
in similar settings
Baronio et al. 2012
;
Baronio et al. 2014
.
Our experimental observations are in quantitative agreement with 3D mean-field simulations but also with quasi-1D NPGPE ones.
Leveraging the latter and expanding upon the experimental flexibility of three-component setups corroborated by our effective model reduction we reveal a multitude of vector PSs.

Our experiments provide a stepping stone for realizing exotic vector or higher-order RWs
Chabchoub et al. 2012
, probing their collisional and interaction features in a controllable way using cold atoms.
We outline some of the associated
emerging opportunities below.
A further
theoretical and computational analysis of the effective models
that we bring forth herein and of their
palette of possible solutions
would advance our understanding in a highly non-trivial manner.
Another interesting direction to pursue is the nucleation of RW solutions that are periodic either in space or time, such as the Akmediev
Akhmediev et al. 2009
or Kuznetzov-Ma
Ma 1979

in multi-component settings, which
indeed
awaits for experimental observation in ultracold atoms (among other disciplines).
Finally, exploring the role of genuine quantum effects in RW formation, which can be achieved by utilizing perturbative treatments to the mean-field energy functional or sophisticated many-body computations
Mistakidis et al. 2023
, is of considerable interest, with the
relevant direction being wide open
in the multi-component realm.

2
General reduction of an
\mathcal{N}
-component setting

Here, we outline the scheme for reducing a general
\mathcal{N}
component system to an effective
\mathcal{N}-k
, where
k
is the number of majority components,
1\leqslant k\leqslant\mathcal{N}-1
.
The starting point is the
\mathcal{N}
coupled time-independent Gross-Pitaevskii equations (GPEs),

\mu_{i}\Psi_{i}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}\Psi_{i}+V(\bm{r})\Psi_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}}g_{ij}n_{j}\Psi_{i},~i=1,\ldots,\mathcal{N},

(S1)

where
n_{j}=\left|\Psi_{j}\right|^{2}
,
g_{ij}=4\pi\hbar^{2}a_{ij}/m
, and
\mu_{i}
is the chemical potential of the
i
-th component.

In our notation, we order the component indices, so that the first
k
correspond to the majority components, and the rest to the minority components.
Employing the Thomas-Fermi approximation
to the majority components, the equations for the latter are recast as follows

\mu_{i}=V(\bm{r})+\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}}g_{ij}n_{j},~i=1,\ldots,k.

(S2)

Having this at hand it is possible to determine the densities of the majority components.
To do so, we exploit a more compact matrix notation, by considering the symmetric interaction strength matrix
\bm{g}
, whose elements read
\bm{g}_{ij}=g_{ij},~i,j=1,\ldots,\mathcal{N}
.
Moreover, we introduce the projections to the subspaces of majority and minority components, which can be respectively expressed as

\bm{M}=\textbf{diag}\left[\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{k},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{\mathcal{N}-k}\right],\quad\bm{m}=\textbf{diag}\left[\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{k},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{\mathcal{N}-k}\right],

(S3)

where
diag
denotes a diagonal matrix.
Since these are orthogonal projections, they satisfy
\bm{M}^{2}=\bm{M}
,
\bm{m}^{2}=\bm{m}
, and
\bm{M}\cdot\bm{m}=\bm{0}
, while
\cdot
designates matrix multiplication. Equipped with these projections, we can now define the interaction strengths within each subspace, as well as the couplings between majority and minority components,

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{\rm{M}}\equiv\bm{M}\cdot\bm{g}\cdot\bm{M},\quad\bm{g}_{\rm{m}}\equiv\bm{m}\cdot\bm{g}\cdot\bm{m},

(S4a)

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{\rm{Mm}}\equiv\bm{M}\cdot\bm{g}\cdot\bm{m},\quad\bm{g}_{\rm{mM}}\equiv\bm{m}\cdot\bm{g}\cdot\bm{M}.

(S4b)

Similarly, one can define the densities and chemical potentials pertaining only to one subspace,

\displaystyle\bm{n}_{\rm{M}}\equiv\bm{M}\cdot\bm{n},\quad\bm{n}_{\rm{m}}\equiv\bm{m}\cdot\bm{n},

(S5a)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\rm{M}}\equiv\bm{M}\cdot\bm{\mu},\quad\bm{\mu}_{\rm{m}}\equiv\bm{m}\cdot\bm{\mu},

(S5b)

with
\bm{n}=\left[n_{1},\ldots,n_{\mathcal{N}}\right]^{\intercal}
, and
\bm{\mu}=\left[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\mathcal{N}}\right]^{\intercal}
.

Using the above, Eq. (
S2
) can be re-formulated in terms of compact matrix notation. Explicitly,

\mu_{i}=V(\bm{r})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}g_{ij}n_{j}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\mathcal{N}}g_{ij}n_{j},~i=1,\ldots,k\Longrightarrow\bm{\mu}_{\rm{M}}=V(\bm{r})\bm{1}_{\rm{M}}+\bm{g}_{\rm{M}}\cdot\bm{n}_{\rm{M}}+\bm{g}_{\rm{Mm}}\cdot\bm{n}_{\rm{m}}.

(S6)

In the above, the projection of the unity vector to the majority subspace has been employed,
\bm{1}_{M}=\bm{M}\cdot\bm{1}
.
Eq. (
S6
) is now inverted to obtain the densities of the majority components,

\bm{n}_{\rm{M}}=\bm{g}_{\rm{M}}^{-1}\cdot\Big[\bm{\mu}_{\rm{M}}-V(\bm{r})\bm{1}_{\rm{M}}-\bm{g}_{\rm{Mm}}\cdot\bm{n}_{\rm{m}}\Big].

(S7)

The first step in determining an effective
\mathcal{N}-k
setup is to cast the
\mathcal{N}-k
equations in matrix formalism,

\displaystyle\mu_{i}\Psi_{i}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}\Psi_{i}+V(\bm{r})\Psi_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}g_{ij}n_{j}\Psi_{i}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\mathcal{N}}g_{ij}n_{j}\Psi_{i},~i=k+1,\ldots,\mathcal{N}\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}_{\rm{m}}\odot\bm{\mu}_{\rm{m}}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}\bm{\Psi}_{\rm{m}}+V(\bm{r})\bm{\Psi}_{\rm{m}}+\bm{\Psi}_{\rm{m}}\odot\left[\bm{g}_{\rm{m}}\cdot\bm{n}_{\rm{m}}\right]+\bm{\Psi}_{\rm{m}}\odot\left[\bm{g}_{\rm{mM}}\cdot\bm{n}_{\rm{M}}\right],

(S8)

where
\odot
denotes the Hadamard product, while
\bm{\Psi}_{\rm{m}}=\bm{m}\cdot\bm{\Psi}
, and
\bm{\Psi}=\left[\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{\mathcal{N}}\right]^{\intercal}
.
Subsequently, the expression for the majority species densities is substituted from Eq. (
S7
). Rearranging terms, one arrives at the effective equations for the minority components,

\bm{\Psi}_{\rm{m}}\odot\left[\bm{\mu}_{\rm{m}}-\bm{g}_{\rm{mM}}\cdot\bm{g}_{M}^{-1}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{\rm{M}}\right]=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}\bm{\Psi}_{\rm{m}}+\bm{\Psi}_{\rm{m}}\odot\left[\bm{1}_{\rm{m}}-\bm{g}_{\rm{mM}}\cdot\bm{g}_{\rm{M}}^{-1}\cdot\bm{1}_{\rm{M}}\right]V(\bm{r})+\bm{\Psi}_{\rm{m}}\odot\Big\{\left[\bm{g}_{\rm{m}}-\bm{g}_{\rm{mM}}\cdot\bm{g}_{\rm{M}}^{-1}\cdot\bm{g}_{\rm{Mm}}\right]\cdot\bm{n}_{\rm{m}}\Big\}.

(S9)

Since the majority component is integrated out, the chemical and trapping potentials, as well as the interactions become renormalized according to

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\rm{m}}^{(\rm{eff})}=\bm{\mu}_{\rm{m}}-\bm{g}_{\rm{mM}}\cdot\bm{g}_{\rm{M}}^{-1}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{\rm{M}},

(S10a)

\displaystyle\bm{V}(\bm{r})^{(\rm{eff})}=V(\bm{r})\left[\bm{1}_{\rm{m}}-\bm{g}_{\rm{mM}}\cdot\bm{g}_{\rm{M}}^{-1}\cdot\bm{1}_{\rm{M}}\right],

(S10b)

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{\rm{m}}^{(\rm{eff})}=\bm{g}_{\rm{m}}-\bm{g}_{\rm{mM}}\cdot\bm{g}_{\rm{M}}^{-1}\cdot\bm{g}_{\rm{Mm}}.

(S10c)

Note that the effective potential is in principle different for the minority components.
Below, we list a few explicit expressions of the effective interactions for different setups,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=2,\quad k=1\Longrightarrow g^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{mm}}=g_{\rm{mm}}-\frac{g^{2}_{\rm{mM}}}{g_{\rm{MM}}},

(S11a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=3,\quad k=1\Longrightarrow g^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{mm^{\prime}}}=g_{\rm{mm^{\prime}}}-\frac{g_{\rm{mM}}g_{\rm{m^{\prime}M}}}{g_{\rm{MM}}},

(S11b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=3,\quad k=2\Longrightarrow g^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{mm}}=\frac{g^{2}_{\rm{Mm}}g_{\rm{M^{\prime}M^{\prime}}}+g^{2}_{\rm{MM^{\prime}}}g_{\rm{mm}}+g_{\rm{MM}}(g^{2}_{\rm{M^{\prime}m}}-g_{\rm{M^{\prime}M^{\prime}}}g_{\rm{mm}})-2g_{\rm{MM^{\prime}}}g_{\rm{Mm}}g_{\rm{M^{\prime}m}}}{g^{2}_{\rm{MM^{\prime}}}-g_{\rm{MM}}g_{\rm{M^{\prime}M^{\prime}}}}.

(S11c)

The
\rm{M}
(
\rm{m}
) indices pertain to the majority (minority) components.
The reduction of a binary immiscible mixture to an effectively attractive single component setting was first utilized to probe Townes solitons in Refs.
Bakkali-Hassani et al. 2021
;
Bakkali-Hassani and Dalibard 2022
, employing the same effective interaction as in Eq. (
S11a
).
However, the reduction of a three-component setting with one majority species [Eq. (
S11b
)] employed in this work leads to a far richer phenomenology of effective two-component setups with tunable interactions in both magnitude and sign.
This is exemplarily demonstrated for six different experimentally relevant combinations of hyperfine levels in Table
1
, whose effective scattering lengths are provided inside the parentheses.

3
Intra- and Inter-component MI

The interplay between the attractive inter- and intra-species interactions is related to the presence of intra- and inter-species dominated modulational instability (MI) for the effective two-component model
Kasamatsu and Tsubota 2006
.
Intra-species MI occurs when
g^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{mm}}
is attractive,
whereas the inter-species MI
dominates the dynamics in the case of

|g^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{m}\rm{m}^{\prime}}|>|g^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{m}\rm{m}}|\left(2\frac{|g^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{m}\rm{m}}|}{g^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{m}^{\prime}\rm{m}^{\prime}}}+1\right)
,
\rm{m}\neq\rm{m^{\prime}}

Kasamatsu and Tsubota 2006
.
Both are determined by the dispersion relation
of the reduced two-component analogue
Kasamatsu and Tsubota 2006

\omega^{2}_{\pm}(k_{\rm{m}},k_{\rm{m^{\prime}}})=\frac{1}{2}\left[\omega^{2}_{\rm{m}}+\omega^{2}_{\rm{m^{\prime}}}\pm\sqrt{(\omega^{2}_{\rm{m}}+\omega^{2}_{\rm{m^{\prime}}})^{2}+4(Q^{2}-\omega^{2}_{\rm{m}}\omega^{2}_{\rm{m^{\prime}}})}\right],

(S12)

where
\omega^{2}_{\rm{m}}=\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}_{\rm{m}}}{2m}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}_{\rm{m}}}{2m}+2g^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{mm}}n_{0}\right)
, and
Q=\frac{\hbar^{2}g^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{mm^{\prime}}}}{m}k_{\rm{m}}k_{\rm{m^{\prime}}}n_{0}
. Here,
n_{0}
denotes the initial local density at the trap center of both minority components.
In order to derive such a dispersion relation, perturbations on top of the initial profile assume the form of plane waves.
Note that this
dispersion relation
applies only to homogeneous binary mixtures.
The
\omega_{+}
branch is associated with the speed of sound of the effective binary mixture
Mossman et al. 2025
, whereas finite imaginary
\omega_{-}
indicates the presence of MI.
The intra-species MI character of setup S1 is depicted in Fig.
Supp. 3
(a) by a finite
\Im[\omega_{-}]
ribbon, stretching along a single momentum axis.
In contrast, setup S2 exhibits a dual MI character [see also Table
2
], and therefore
\Im[\omega_{-}]
displays two finite overlapping regions.
In this scenario, the maximum growth rate,
\max\{\Im[\omega_{-}]\}
, is larger than in the case of setup S1, implying that the MI onset is expected to be faster in setup S2 which can be also inferred from Fig. 1 of the main text.

necessary condition for PS generation, and thus the presence or absence of inter- and intra-species MI provides a promising indicator for PS generation for the
minority component to which it applies.

4
Validity of the effective model

To assess the validity of the effective model for the three-component setting (
\mathcal{N}=3,k=1
), setup S2 is employed as a prototype since it is subject to strong inter- and intra-species MI.
Relevant investigations have been performed for the remaining setups discussed in the main text, where the agreement between the full three-component and the reduced two-component predictions is similar (and often even better) to the one described below.
For convenience, the pertinent predictions are provided within the one-dimensional (1D) GPEs.
Recall that in three-dimensions (3D) wave collapse occurs in the attractive interaction regime
Roberts et al. 2001
.
The 1D GPEs are a reduction of Eq. (1) in the main text, assuming the product wavefunction ansatz
Kevrekidis et al. 2009

\Psi_{i}(\bm{r},t)=\phi_{i}(x,t)\frac{e^{-\bm{r}^{2}_{\perp}/(2\ell^{2}_{\perp})}}{\ell_{\perp}\sqrt{\pi}}
,
i=1,2,3
, where
\bm{r}_{\perp}=(y,z)
refer to the transverse coordinates, and
\ell_{\perp}=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega_{\perp})}
is the oscillator length along the transverse directions.
Accordingly, the 1D GPEs for the three-component setting read

i\hbar\partial_{t}\phi_{i}=\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\partial_{x}^{2}+\frac{m}{2}\omega_{x}^{2}x^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}2\hbar\omega_{\perp}a_{ij}\left|\phi_{j}\right|^{2}\right]\phi_{i},\quad i=1,2,3.

(S13)

This can be readily reduced to the two-component 1D GPEs (that will be used for the validation of the reduced model) in the case of
\phi_{1}=0
.
In fact, the latter represents the majority component, which is integrated out in the reduced two-component model.

The density evolution of the minority components of Setup S2 following the experimental process outlined in the main text is presented in Fig.
Supp. 5

for a small population imbalance (
f_{\rm{m}}=5~\%
).
Overall qualitative agreement is observed between the same minority components as obtained from the full three-component and the effective model.
Specifically, in both cases twin PS nucleation takes place around
t\approx 40~\rm{ms}
.
Small discrepancies arise after PS nucleation, where inter- and intra-species MI set in.
The latter seed highly oscillatory density patterns propagating outwards. Notice here the sharp difference of the MI development, which does not encompass a central density portion as the one shown in Fig. 2(b3), (b5) of the main text.
This highlights the importance of three-body losses for capturing the experimental observations.
The MI envelope is captured to an excellent degree by the prediction of Ref
Mossman et al. 2025
, referring to two-component settings, see white dashed lines in Fig.
Supp. 5
.
Naturally, the twin PSs are characterized by a large amplitude as compared to their background.
This way, they locally become comparable to the majority component [see inset in Fig.
Supp. 5
(a)], thus
signaling the breakdown of the effective description, see also Fig.
Supp. 6
.
Notice here the lack of PS generation in the majority component, when excluding three-body losses.

The aforementioned agreement/breakdown of the effective picture is cleanly visualized by inspecting the overlap of the effective,
\phi^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{m}}
, (emanating from the reduced model) with the actual,
\phi_{\rm{m}}

Erdmann et al. 2019
;
Mistakidis et al. 2018
;
Bandyopadhyay et al. 2017
;
Jain and Boninsegni 2011
,

\Lambda_{\rm{m}}(t)=\frac{\left[\int dx~\left|\phi_{\rm{m}}(x,t)\right|^{2}\left|\phi^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{m}}(x,t)\right|^{2}\right]^{2}}{\left[\int dx~\left|\phi_{\rm{m}}(x,t)\right|^{4}\right]\left[\int dx~\left|\phi^{(\rm{eff})}_{\rm{m}}(x,t)\right|^{4}\right]},

(S14)

presented in Fig.
Supp. 6
for the
\ket{1,-1}
[panel (a)] and
\ket{1,0}
[panel (b)] hyperfine level of Setup S2.
Perfect overlap (

Supp. 6
).
Around PSs formation

, signifying that deviations from the full system dynamics are becoming more prominent for longer evolution times.
Let us note, however, that
\Lambda_{\rm{m}}(t)
is a strict quantitative measure, and the overall dynamical response is adequately captured qualitatively by the reduced model deep in the evolution [see also Fig.
Supp. 5
].

6
Non-polynomial GPE reduction

Due to the experimentally used trap aspect ratios, the three-component mixture is mostly kinematically constrained across the elongated direction, i.e. it is a quasi-1D system.
In this sense, a lower dimensional description can be employed
Salasnich et al. 2002
, serving as a useful guide for the rich phenomenology of vector PSs in three-component settings.

The starting point is the action pertaining to the three-component system in 3D,

S[\Psi_{j}]=\int dt\,d^{3}\bm{r}~\Bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left[\Psi_{i}^{*}i\hbar\partial_{t}\Psi_{i}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left|\nabla\Psi_{i}\right|^{2}+V(\bm{r})\left|\Psi_{i}\right|^{2}+\frac{g_{ii}}{2}\left|\Psi_{i}\right|^{4}\right]+\sum_{i>j}g_{ij}\left|\Psi_{i}\right|^{2}\left|\Psi_{j}\right|^{2}\Bigg\}.

(S15)

Here,
\Psi_{i}(\bm{r},t)
,
i=1,2,3
, denote the 3D wavefunctions pertaining to the three different hyperfine states, and
V(\bm{r})
is the trapping potential, decomposed as
V(\bm{r})=U(x)+\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{\perp}^{2}\bm{r}^{2}_{\perp}
. The potential along the axial direction,
U(x)
, can be in principle arbitrary.
The quasi-1D description relies on the following product ansatz for the axial and transverse profiles,

\Psi_{i}(\bm{r},t)=\varphi_{i}(x,t)\frac{e^{-\bm{r}^{2}_{\perp}/(2\sigma^{2})}}{\sigma\sqrt{\pi}},

(S16)

where we have assumed that the transverse profile is the same for all components.
The width
\sigma
is a variational parameter, determined from the Euler-Lagrange equations,

\sigma^{4}=\ell_{\perp}^{4}\Bigg\{1+\frac{\sum_{i\geq j}2a_{ij}\left|\varphi_{i}\right|^{2}\left|\varphi_{j}\right|^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left|\varphi_{i}\right|^{2}}\Bigg\}.

(S17)

Note that the width is coupled to the axial profiles,
\varphi_{i}
.
The corresponding quasi-1D equations of motion are extracted from the Euler-Lagrange equations of the action described by Eq. (
S15
),

i\hbar\frac{\partial\varphi_{i}}{\partial t}=\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\partial_{x}^{2}+U(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{g_{ij}}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}\left|\varphi_{j}\right|^{2}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m\sigma^{2}}+\frac{m\omega_{\perp}^{2}}{2}\sigma^{2}\right]\varphi_{i},\quad i=1,2,3.

(S18)

The latter are the non-polynomial GPEs (NPGPEs) for the three-component system, generalizing this way earlier findings concerning single
Salasnich et al. 2002
and two-component
Salasnich 2009
mixtures.

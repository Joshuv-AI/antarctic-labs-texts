Comparative Evaluation of Aligners with Built-in Vibration Features on Rate of Tooth Movement
Authors: Navin Oommen Thomas, Anil Paul Melitt, Sonu M. Mohan, T. Faraz Afzal, Thara Chandran, Preeti Singh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Clear aligner therapy has become a preferred alternative to traditional fixed orthodontics due to its aesthetics and comfort. However, the slower rate of tooth movement remains a limitation. Incorporating built-in vibration technology within aligners is a recent innovation proposed to accelerate tooth movement by enhancing cellular activity and bone remodeling.Materials and Methods:A prospective comparative study was conducted involving 40 orthodontic patients aged 18-30 years requiring anterior teeth retraction. Patients were randomly assigned into two groups: Group A (n = 20) received conventional clear aligners, and Group B (n = 20) received aligners with built-in vibration features (frequency 30 Hz, 5 minutes/day usage). All participants underwent standardized treatment planning, and tooth movement was evaluated using digital models at baseline, 4, 8, and 12 weeks.Results:The mean rate of tooth movement over 12 weeks was significantly higher in Group B (0.45 ± 0.08 mm/week) compared to Group A (0.29 ± 0.06 mm/week), with a P < 0.01. Group B also exhibited reduced overall treatment time by approximately 23%. No significant adverse effects or discomfort were reported in either group.Conclusion:Aligners integrated with vibration features demonstrated a statistically significant increase in the rate of tooth movement compared to conventional aligners.

INTRODUCTION

Clear aligner therapy has transformed the field of orthodontics by offering a discreet, removable, and comfortable alternative to fixed appliances. Initially introduced for mild malocclusions, advances in aligner design and materials have expanded their applicability to more complex tooth movements.[1] Despite these advantages, one of the primary limitations of aligner therapy remains the relatively slower rate of tooth movement compared to conventional fixed orthodontics.[2]

Orthodontic tooth movement is a biologically mediated process involving periodontal ligament remodeling and bone resorption-deposition cycles. Various methods have been explored to accelerate this process, including pharmacological agents, surgical interventions, and mechanical stimulation.[3] Among these, mechanical vibration has gained attention for its potential to enhance cellular activity, improve blood flow, and stimulate bone remodeling with minimal invasiveness.[4]

Recent innovations have led to the integration of low-frequency vibrational technology directly into orthodontic appliances. Vibrational stimulation at specific frequencies, particularly around 30 Hz, has been shown to increase osteoblastic activity and facilitate bone remodeling, potentially accelerating tooth movement.[56] Devices such as AcceleDent and VPro have demonstrated variable efficacy in clinical trials when used as adjuncts to conventional orthodontic appliances.[7]

To further simplify and improve patient compliance, manufacturers have begun incorporating vibrational mechanisms directly within the aligner trays themselves. This development eliminates the need for external vibration devices and may improve ease of use while maintaining therapeutic benefits.[8] However, evidence comparing the effectiveness of aligners with built-in vibration features to standard aligners remains limited.

Treatment protocol

All patients were treated using a standardized digital treatment planning software, and aligners were fabricated using the same thermoplastic material for both groups. Patients in Group B used aligners with embedded microvibrational modules set to deliver a frequency of 30 Hz for 5 minutes daily, as per manufacturer guidelines.

Patients were instructed to wear the aligners for 22 hours daily, changing them every 10 days. Monthly reviews were conducted for all participants to monitor compliance and check for complications.

Measurement of tooth movement

Baseline digital intraoral scans were taken using a 3D intraoral scanner. Subsequent scans were taken at 4, 8, and 12 weeks. The rate of anterior tooth movement was measured in millimeters using 3D model superimposition software, assessing the distance moved by central incisors in the anteroposterior direction.

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25.0.

RESULTS

A total of 40 participants were enrolled in the study and completed the 12-week evaluation period without any dropouts. Both groups were comparable in terms of age and gender distribution at baseline.

Rate of tooth movement

The average rate of anterior tooth movement was calculated at 4-week intervals and compared between the two groups. Group B (aligners with built-in vibration) demonstrated a consistently higher rate of tooth movement across all time points compared to Group A (conventional aligners).

Reduction in total treatment time

Although the study was conducted for 12 weeks, extrapolated estimates based on the overall rate suggested that Group B may require fewer aligner trays and a shorter treatment duration.

These findings indicate a clear advantage of using aligners with built-in vibration in enhancing orthodontic efficiency [Tables 1 and 2].

DISCUSSION

The present study demonstrated that aligners integrated with vibration technology significantly enhanced the rate of tooth movement compared to conventional clear aligners. Patients in Group B exhibited a mean weekly tooth movement rate of 0.45 mm, which was notably higher than the 0.29 mm observed in Group A. These findings are consistent with previous studies that reported accelerated orthodontic movement with adjunctive mechanical stimulation techniques, such as low-frequency vibration and pulsatile forces.[123]

Orthodontic tooth movement depends on a complex cascade of biological responses initiated by mechanical forces applied to the teeth. Mechanical vibration has been shown to promote remodeling of the alveolar bone and increase osteoblast and osteoclast activity, thereby accelerating this process.[45] Nishimura et al. found that vibration at a frequency of 30 Hz significantly enhanced bone turnover and reduced orthodontic treatment time in animal models,[6] which supports the frequency setting used in the present study.

Incorporation of vibration directly into aligners offers advantages in terms of convenience and compliance, as it eliminates the need for external devices like AcceleDent or VPro5.[78] Compliance was slightly higher in the vibration group, possibly due to the novelty of the device and its automated timing. Moreover, patient-reported discomfort was marginally lower, aligning with previous observations that vibrational stimulation may also have an analgesic effect.[9]

CONCLUSION

Overall, our findings indicate that aligners with built-in vibrational technology significantly enhance the rate of tooth movement without compromising comfort or compliance. This modality may serve as a promising addition to orthodontic practice, especially for patients seeking shorter and more efficient treatment durations.

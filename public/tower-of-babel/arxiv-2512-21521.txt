First Provable Guarantees for Practical Private FL: Beyond Restrictive Assumptions
Egor Shulgin Grigory Malinovsky Sarit Khirirat Peter Richtárik

Abstract
Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative training on decentralized data. Differential privacy (DP) is crucial for FL, but current private methods often rely on unrealistic assumptions (e.g., bounded gradients or heterogeneity), hindering practical application. Existing works that relax these assumptions typically neglect practical FL features, including multiple local updates and partial client participation. We introduce Fed- α \alpha -NormEC , the first differentially private FL framework providing provable convergence and DP guarantees under standard assumptions while fully supporting these practical features. Fed- α \alpha -NormEC integrates local updates (full and incremental gradient steps), separate server and client stepsizes, and, crucially, partial client participation, which is essential for real-world deployment and vital for privacy amplification. Our theoretical guarantees are corroborated by experiments on private deep learning tasks.



1
Introduction

Federated Learning (FL)
(
42
;
31
)
has been widely adopted for training machine learning models across multiple collaborative devices without centralized data collection.
Despite its advantages, FL poses three key challenges.
The first challenge is the communication bottleneck caused by unreliable and slow network connections between the server and the clients
(
6
)
. The second challenge is partial client participation, which arises from huge population and intermittent availability of the clients, thus making the training infeasible to involve all the clients in every communication round
(
8
)
.
The third challenge is data heterogeneity in FL systems, where clients’ datasets are diverse and not identically distributed
(
25
;
47
)
.
This has necessitated the development of FL methods that improve communication efficiency, accommodate partial client participation, and address data heterogeneity
(
61
;
24
)
.

FL methods keep data decentralized across devices, and thus were initially perceived as privacy-preserving methods.
However, FL remains vulnerable to various privacy threats.
Prior studies
(
4
;
44
)
have shown that sensitive information can still be inferred from the shared model parameters–either by an untrusted central server or by adversaries performing inference attacks.
To address these privacy concerns, FL methods have been extended to incorporate formal privacy guarantees through Differential Privacy (DP)
(
12
)
.
This integration in FL methods is achieved by applying DP mechanisms to
the information, such as gradients or local updates, before it is shared with the server.
Typically, this involves applying a clipping or normalization operator to bound the sensitivity of the updates, followed by the addition of carefully tuned DP noise.

Although clipping enables DP in FL methods, it introduces bias that can hinder convergence.
For instance, even in the absence of DP noise,
FedAvg
with model clipping fails to converge to the optimum for solving a simple quadratic problem
(
65
)
.
As a result, convergence guarantees for federated DP methods with clipping are typically established under strong and often unrealistic assumptions, such as uniformly bounded gradient norms
(
64
;
35
;
37
)
and/or bounded heterogeneity
(
48
;
33
)
.
These assumptions do not often hold in practical FL scenarios, where client data can be arbitrarily heterogeneous, and they tend to obscure the impact of clipping-induced bias.
To our knowledge, convergence guarantees for FL DP methods remain elusive unless this bias is explicitly accounted for.

To eliminate the bias caused by clipping and ensure convergence, Error Compensation (EC), also known as Error Feedback (EF, EF21)
(
17
;
59
;
51
)
, has been integrated into distributed methods that use gradient clipping, such as Clip21
(
28
)
.
This technique tracks the error between the clipped gradient and the true gradient, and uses it to correct future clipped gradients, thus effectively reducing the clipping bias as the methods progress.
In non-private settings, EC ensures the convergence of distributed clipping methods under standard assumptions.
However, its benefit does not extend to private settings, where DP noise is added.

To ensure theoretical guarantees in the private setting, two recent distributed differentially private (DP) methods, which operate on gradients, achieve strong convergence and privacy guarantees under standard assumptions without requiring bounded gradients or bounded heterogeneity.
The first method by
23
combines the momentum update with the EC mechanism, while the second method by
56
replaces clipping in the EC mechanism with smoothed normalization
(
63
;
5
)
, the operator that is more robust across parameter choices than standard clipping.
Although these methods converge in both non-private and private settings, they do not support key components that are essential in practical FL systems, such as multiple local training steps and partial client participation.
Therefore, formal convergence and privacy guarantees of private FL methods under standard assumptions remain largely unexplored.

1.1
Contributions

We summarize our key contributions as follows:

\bullet
A practical, private FL method.

We propose
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
, an FL method that extends
\alpha
-NormEC

(
56
)
by integrating smoothed normalization and the error feedback mechanism

into clients’ local update steps, rather than directly into their gradients as in
\alpha
-NormEC

Unlike
\alpha
-NormEC

Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
supports partial client participation and allows local training through multiple gradient steps, while introducing separate step sizes for client-side local updates and server-side global aggregation–providing flexibility in controlling their respective effects.
Additionally, to reduce the computational cost of full gradient steps at clients,
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
incorporates cyclic incremental gradient descent locally, a feature absent in
\alpha
-NormEC

\bullet
Convergence guarantees for non-convex, smooth problems under standard assumptions.

We establish the convergence of
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
for minimizing non-convex, smooth objectives without relying on additional restrictive assumptions, such as bounded gradients or bounded heterogeneity. Our analysis encompasses both local gradient descent and incremental gradient updates.

A fundamentally new convergence analysis is required because
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
applies the EF21 mechanism to the clients’ local updates, a shift from
\alpha
-NormEC
that applies it to the gradients.
Specifically, our novel proof technique must bound the Euclidean distance involving the fixed-point operator
\mathcal{T}_{i}(x)
for local updating at client
i

\bullet
Differential privacy guarantees with amplification via partial participation.

We provide a privacy analysis of
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
for both single and multiple local update steps.
In both cases, we introduce an independent client sampling scheme, where each client participates in each round with sampling probability
p
, independently of the others. Our analysis shows that this partial participation in
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
, which differs from
\alpha
-NormEC
that relies on full participation, enables a significant reduction in the variance of the DP noise through the mechanism of privacy amplification via subsampling.

\bullet
Empirical validations of
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
on image classification.

We demonstrate the effectiveness of
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
by applying it to the image classification task on the CIFAR-10 dataset using the ResNet20 model.
Experiments highlight the impact of key algorithm parameters and client participation levels, corroborating our theoretical insights on convergence and privacy trade-offs. Notably, we show that partial participation, by leveraging privacy amplification, can achieve target accuracy with significantly improved communication efficiency compared to full participation, showcasing
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
’s utility for real-world private deep learning.

Related Works

In this section, we review existing literature that are closely related to our work.

Communication efficiency in FL.

Two key strategies for improving communication efficiency in FL algorithms are local updates and compression.
First, local updates reduce communication frequency by allowing clients to perform multiple training steps locally before sending the updates to the server.
This idea has been widely explored in the FL literature, e.g. by
26
;
38
;
30
;
16
;
49
.
Second, compression improves communication efficiency by reducing the size of transmitted messages.
Compression operators can be either unbiased or biased.
The convergence of FL algorithms with unbiased compression–such as
FedAvg

(
19
)
, local gradient descent
(
27
;
57

(

While many FL analyses assume unbiased compression, biased compression has also been investigated.
For instance,
18
study the use of biased compression in combination with error feedback mechanisms.
However,
their approaches focus only on compressing the global iterates maintained at the server side, and are limited to the distributed setting, where clients synchronize with the server after every update.

Clipping.

Two popular clipping operators for FL algorithms are per-sample clipping and per-update clipping.
Per-sample clipping

bounds the norm of the local gradient being used to update the local model parameters on each client, and ensures example-level privacy
(
1

(
15

limits the bound of the local model update, and preserves user-level privacy
(
65
;
15
)

The convergence of FL algorithms, such as

FedAvg

(
43
)
and
SCAFFOLD

(

In this paper, we leverage per-update smoothed normalization, introduced by

Federated learning with clipping and privacy.

A simple yet popular FL algorithm,
FedAvg

(
42
)
, has been adapted to provide differential privacy (DP) by clipping model updates and injecting random noise
(
43
;
15
;
60
)

These

algorithms were outperformed by

(
48
)
, a DP version of
SCAFFOLD

(
25
)

However, these existing results require restrictive assumptions that do not hold in practice, especially in deep neural network training, such as uniformly bounded stochastic noise

;

To the best of our knowledge, there has been a recent work by

\Delta_{i}:=f_{i}(x^{\star})-\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{i}(x)

In this paper, we provide convergence guarantees for private FL algorithms with smoothed normalization and error feedback. In particular, our guarantees do not rely on the restrictive assumptions commonly used by prior work, and our theoretical stepsizes can be implemented in practice.

Server and local stepsizes.

The use of separate server and local stepsizes has been shown to be crucial in federated learning
(
7
;
50

39

Random reshuffling.

Random reshuffling, or without-replacement sampling, is used in SGD and often outperforms sampling with replacement. Its convergence properties have been extensively studied

;
20
;
54
;

)
, including in FL settings

;
52
;

In this work,
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC

Error feedback.

Error feedback, also known as error compensation, has proven effective in enhancing the convergence of distributed gradient algorithms with compressed communication. Popular error feedback mechanisms include

EF14

(
55
)
,
EF21

(
51
)
,
EF21-SGDM

(

)
,

(

(

Beyond compression, error feedback has been adapted by substituting compression with other operators.
For instance,

(

(using clipping instead of compression) and

-NormEC

(

In this paper, we contribute by adapting

\alpha

\mathcal{T}_{i}(x)=x-\gamma\nabla f_{i}(x),\text{for }i\in[1,M]
.

Full participation and non-private setting.

In a full participation and non-private setting,
according to
Theorem

1
with
T=1
(one local GD step),
p=1
(full client participation), and
\sigma_{\rm DP}=0
(no DP noise),
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
with one local GD step achieves the convergence bound consisting of three terms:

\frac{3}{K+1}\frac{f(x^{0})-f^{\inf}}{\eta}+2R+\frac{\eta L}{2}.

By choosing
R=\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{K+1})
and
\eta=\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{K+1})
,
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
with one local GD step achieves the
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{K+1})
convergence rate in the gradient norm, which matches that of
\alpha
-NormEC
by
56
and that of classical gradient descent.
The upper-bound for
R
can be achieved, e.g., by setting
v_{i}^{0}=\nicefrac{{(x^{0}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{0}))}}{{\gamma}}+e
, where
e=(D/\sqrt{K+1},0,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Partial participation and private setting.

In the partial participation and private setting,
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
with one local GD step can be shown to be
(\epsilon,\delta)
-DP at the client level, by leveraging the instance-level DP guarantee of
1
, which uses the moments accountant analysis on DP-SGD.
This is justified because
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
uses the same partial participation protocol as DP-SGD, where at each communication round a random subset of clients is sampled to participate.
Instead of per-example clipping in DP-SGD,
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
applies normalization to each client’s local update to enforce the sensitivity of
1
.

Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
injects zero-mean Gaussian noise to the aggregated update, which is chosen with the same scaling rule according to
1
.
By treating each client’s normalized update as analogous to a clipped per-example gradient in DP
-
SGD, the instance-level privacy guarantee by
1
can be directly translated into the client-level setting.
Specifically, we set
\sigma_{\rm DP}=c\cdot\frac{p\sqrt{(K+1)\log(1/\delta)}}{\epsilon}
for some constant
c>0
and
0<p\leq 1
. Notably,
\sigma_{\rm DP}
exhibits a reduced dependency on
p
thanks to the amplification effect of subsampling in the client-level privacy setting. The formal utility guarantee is given below:

Corollary 1
.

Consider
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
for solving Problem (
1
) under the same setting as
Theorem

1
.
Let
T=1
(one local GD step), let
\sigma_{\rm DP}=c\frac{p\sqrt{(K+1)\log(1/\delta)}}{\epsilon}
with
c>0
(privacy with subsampling amplification), and let
p=\frac{\hat{B}}{M}
for
\hat{B}\in[1,M]
(client subsampling).
If
\beta=\frac{\hat{\beta}}{K+1}
with
\hat{\beta}=\sqrt{\frac{3(f(x^{0})-f^{\inf})}{\gamma}}\sqrt[4]{\frac{M}{B_{2}}}
,

\gamma<\frac{\Delta^{\inf}(\alpha+R)}{\sqrt{2L}\hat{\beta}R}

\alpha=R=\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt[4]{d}\frac{\sqrt{f(x^{0})-f^{\inf}}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\sqrt[4]{\frac{B_{2}}{M}}\right)
with
B_{2}=2c^{2}\frac{\hat{B}}{M}\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\epsilon^{2}}
, and

\eta=\frac{1}{K+1}\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{\hat{\beta}R}{\alpha+R}
, then

\displaystyle\underset{k\in[0,K]}{\min}{\rm E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta\sqrt[4]{\frac{d\hat{B}}{M^{2}}\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\epsilon^{2}}}\right),

where
\Delta=\max(\alpha,2)\sqrt{L}\sqrt{f(x^{0})-f^{\inf}}

Discussion on
Corollary

1
.

Corollary

1
establishes the utility bound of
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
in the partial participation and private setting.
By setting
p=\hat{B}/M
, where
\hat{B}\in[1,M]
denotes the number of clients sampled at each round,
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
achieves a utility bound of
\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta\sqrt[4]{d\cdot\frac{B}{M^{2}}\cdot\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\epsilon^{2}}}\right),
which improves upon the
\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta\sqrt[4]{d\cdot\frac{1}{M}\cdot\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\epsilon^{2}}}\right)

Finally, when

\alpha
-NormEC
.
Moreover, we can ensure

Specifically, each client adds cryptographic noise before their updates are communicated to the server, thus maintaining this initialization condition while preserving privacy. We refer to
56

5.2
Multiple Local GD Steps

Next, we analyze the convergence of
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
with multiple local GD steps in a partial participation and private setting.
According to
Theorem

1
with
T>1

\alpha
-NormEC

\mathbb{I}_{T\neq 1}\left[8L\sqrt{2L}\cdot\sqrt{\Delta^{\inf}}\right]

However,

may vanish for homogeneous datasets.

when all clients share the same infimum, i.e.,

.
Furthermore, by applying
Theorem

1
with
T>1
, and by using the variance of the DP noise

for some constant

\alpha

.

5.3

To reduce the cost of computing full gradients on clients, we introduce a variant of

\alpha
-NormEC
that uses cyclic incremental gradient (IG) steps

2

2

.
In this variant, each client performs local updates using gradient steps on its individual component functions

Conclusion

This paper presented
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
, the first differentially private federated learning algorithm to offer provable convergence for nonconvex, smooth problems without resorting to unrealistic assumptions such as bounded gradients or heterogeneity.
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
uniquely combines smoothed normalization and error compensation with essential practical FL components: local updates, distinct server/client learning rates, partial client participation (vital for privacy amplification), and DP noise.
Finally, we verify the effectiveness of
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
by experiments on private deep neural network training.

We introduce five lemmas that are useful for our analysis.

First,
Lemma

1
establishes the bounds for
\left\|\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k}\right\|
and
\left\|\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k+1}\right\|
.
Both quantities will be applied in the induction proof, which is the first step of the convergence proof of
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
v_{i}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be governed by

\displaystyle v_{i}^{k+1}=v_{i}^{k}+\beta{\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k}\right),\text{ for $i\in[1,M]$ and }k\geq 0,

and let the fixed-point operator
\mathcal{T}_{i}(\cdot)
satisfy

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{i}(x)-\mathcal{T}_{i}(y)\right\|\leq\rho\left\|x-y\right\|,\text{ for $\rho>0$ and }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

If
\left\|\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k}\right\|\leq C
for some
C>0
,
\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|\leq\eta
,
\frac{\beta}{\alpha+C}<1
, and
\eta\leq\frac{\gamma\beta C}{(1+\rho)(\alpha+C)}
, then
\left\|\frac{x^{k+1}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k+1})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k+1}\right\|\leq C
and
\left\|\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k+1}\right\|\leq C
.

Proof.

From the definition of the Euclidean norm,

\displaystyle\left\|P_{i}(x^{k+1})-v_{i}^{k+1}\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{v_{i}^{k+1}}{=}

\displaystyle\left\|P_{i}(x^{k+1})-v_{i}^{k}-\beta N_{\alpha}(P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{triangle inequality}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left\|P_{i}(x^{k+1})-P_{i}(x^{k})\right\|+\left\|P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k}-{\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left(P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k}\right)\right\|,

where
P_{i}(x)=(x-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x))/\gamma
.

Next, we bound
\left\|P_{i}(x^{k+1})-P_{i}(x^{k})\right\|
.
From the definition of
P_{i}(x)
,

\displaystyle\left\|P_{i}(x^{k+1})-P_{i}(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{x^{k+1}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k+1})}{\gamma}-\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{(*)}{\leq}\frac{1}{\gamma}\left(\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k+1})\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(**)}{\leq}\frac{1}{\gamma}\left(1+\rho\right)\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|.

Here, we reach
(*)
by the triangle inequality, and
(**)
by the fact that
\left\|\mathcal{T}_{i}(x)-\mathcal{T}_{i}(y)\right\|\leq\rho\left\|x-y\right\|
for
\rho>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Plugging the upper-bound for
\left\|P_{i}(x^{k+1})-P_{i}(x^{k})\right\|
into the main inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|P_{i}(x^{k+1})-v_{i}^{k+1}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}(1+\rho)\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|+\left\|P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k}-{\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left(P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k}\right)\right\|.

\left\|P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k}-{\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left(P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k}\right)\right\|
. From the property of
{\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left((\right)\cdot)
according to Lemma 1 of

\displaystyle\left\|P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k}-{\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left(P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left|1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\left\|\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k}\right\|}\right|\left\|\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k}\right\|.

If
\left\|\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k}\right\|\leq C
for some

\displaystyle\left\|P_{i}(x^{k+1})-v_{i}^{k+1}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}(1+\rho)\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|+\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha+C}\right)C.

\displaystyle\left\|P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k+1}\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{v_{i}^{k+1}}{=}

\displaystyle\left\|P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k}-\beta{\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left(P_{i}(x^{k})-v_{i}^{k}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha+C}\right)C

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C.

Next,

2

\left\|e^{k}\right\|

Lemma 2
.

Let
e^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be governed by

\displaystyle e^{k+1}=e^{k}+\beta z^{k},\quad\text{for $0\leq k\leq K$},

where
z^{k}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}z_{i}^{k}
and each

is an independent random vector satisfying

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[z_{i}^{k}\right]=0,\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm E}\left[\left\|z_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2}.

Then,

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{k+1}\right\|\right]\leq{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{0}\right\|\right]+\sqrt{\frac{\beta^{2}(K+1)\sigma^{2}}{M}}.

Proof.

By applying the recursion of
e^{k+1}
recursively,

\displaystyle e^{k+1}=e^{0}+\beta\sum_{l=0}^{k}z^{l}.

By taking the Euclidean norm, by the triangle inequality, and by taking the expectation,

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{k+1}\right\|\right]\leq{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{0}\right\|\right]+{\rm E}\left[\left\|\beta\sum_{l=0}^{k}z^{l}\right\|\right].

By Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{k+1}\right\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{0}\right\|\right]+\sqrt{{\rm E}\left[\left\|\beta\sum_{l=0}^{k}z^{l}\right\|^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{0}\right\|\right]+\sqrt{\beta^{2}\sum_{l=0}^{k}{\rm E}\left[\left\|z^{l}\right\|^{2}\right]+\beta^{2}\sum_{i\neq j}{\rm E}\left[\left\langle z^{i},z^{j}\right\rangle\right]}.

Since
z_{i}^{k}
is independent of one another, we obtain
{\rm E}\left[\left\langle z^{i},z^{j}\right\rangle\right]=0
for

, and
{\rm E}\left[\left\|z^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}{\rm E}\left[\left\|z_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2}/M
. Therefore,

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{k+1}\right\|\right]\leq{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{0}\right\|\right]+\sqrt{\beta^{2}\frac{(K+1)\sigma^{2}}{M}}.

∎

Lemma 3

Consider

\alpha
-NormEC

\mathcal{T}_{i}(\cdot)
for solving Problem (

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}v_{i}^{k+1}-\hat{v}^{k+1}\right\|\right]\leq\sqrt{\frac{\beta^{2}B}{M}(K+1)},

Proof.

Define
e^{k}:=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}v_{i}^{k}-\hat{v}^{k}
. Then,

\displaystyle e^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}v_{i}^{k+1}-\hat{v}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{v_{i}^{k+1},\hat{v}^{k+1}}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}v_{i}^{k}-\hat{v}^{k}+\beta n^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{k}+\beta n^{k},

where
n^{k}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}n_{i}^{k}
and
n_{i}^{k}=(1-q_{i}^{k}){\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k}\right)-q_{i}^{k}z_{i}^{k}
.

Next, from
Lemma

2
with
z^{k}=n^{k}
and
z_{i}^{k}=n_{i}^{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{k+1}\right\|\right]\leq{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{0}\right\|\right]+\sqrt{\frac{\beta^{2}(K+1)\cdot\sigma^{2}}{M}},

where
\sigma^{2}={\rm E}\left[\left\|n_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]
.

If
\hat{v}^{0}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{0}
, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{k+1}\right\|\right]\leq\sqrt{\frac{\beta^{2}(K+1)\cdot\sigma^{2}}{M}}.

To complete the upper-bound for
{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{k+1}\right\|\right]
,
we find
\sigma^{2}={\rm E}\left[\left\|n_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]
. From the definition of
n_{i}^{k}
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|n_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm E}\left[\left\|(1-q_{i}^{k}){\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k}\right)-q_{i}^{k}z_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(A)}{\leq}

\displaystyle 2{\rm E}\left[(1-q_{i}^{k})^{2}\left\|{\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x^{k}-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})}{\gamma}-v_{i}^{k}\right)\right\|^{2}\right]+2{\rm E}\left[(q_{i}^{k})^{2}\left\|z_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(B)}{\leq}

\displaystyle 2{\rm E}\left[(1-q_{i}^{k})^{2}\right]+2{\rm E}\left[(q_{i}^{k})^{2}\left\|z_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(C)}{=}

\displaystyle 2{\rm E}\left[(1-q_{i}^{k})^{2}\right]+2{\rm E}\left[(q_{i}^{k})^{2}\right]{\rm E}\left[\left\|z_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2p(1-1/p)^{2}+2(1-p)+2p/p^{2}\cdot\sigma^{2}_{\rm DP},

where we reach
(A)
by the fact that
\left\|x+y\right\|^{2}\leq 2\left\|x\right\|^{2}+2\left\|y\right\|^{2}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
(B)
by the fact that
\left\|{\rm Norm}_{\alpha}\left(\cdot\right)\right\|\leq 1
, and
(C)
by the fact that
q_{i}^{k}
and
z_{i}^{k}
are independent.

Finally, by plugging the upper-bound for
{\rm E}\left[\left\|n_{i}^{k}\right\|^{2}\right]
into the upper-bound for
{\rm E}\left[\left\|e^{k+1}\right\|\right]
, we obtain the final result.

∎

Next,
Lemma

4
provides the descent inequality for
f(x^{k})-f^{\inf}
in normalized gradient descent.

Lemma 4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be lower-bounded by
f^{\inf}>-\infty
and
L
-smoooth, and let
x^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be governed by

\displaystyle x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\gamma\frac{G^{k}}{\left\|G^{k}\right\|},

where
\gamma>0
. Then,

\displaystyle f(x^{k+1})-f^{\inf}\leq f(x^{k})-f^{\inf}-\gamma\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|+2\gamma\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})-G^{k}\right\|+\frac{L\gamma^{2}}{2}.

Proof.

By the lower-bound and smoothess of
f(\cdot)
, and by the definition of
x^{k+1}
,

\displaystyle f(x^{k+1})-f^{\inf}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x^{k})-f^{\inf}-\frac{\gamma}{\left\|G^{k}\right\|}\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),G^{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L\gamma^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x^{k})-f^{\inf}-\frac{\gamma}{\left\|G^{k}\right\|}\left\langle G^{k},G^{k}\right\rangle+\frac{\gamma}{\left\|G^{k}\right\|}\left\langle G^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k}),G^{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L\gamma^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x^{k})-f^{\inf}-\gamma\left\|G^{k}\right\|+\frac{\gamma}{\left\|G^{k}\right\|}\left\langle G^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k}),G^{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L\gamma^{2}}{2}.

By Cauchy-Scwartz inequality, i.e.
\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\leq\left\|x\right\|\left\|y\right\|
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle f(x^{k+1})-f^{\inf}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x^{k})-f^{\inf}-\gamma\left\|G^{k}\right\|+\gamma\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})-G^{k}\right\|+\frac{L\gamma^{2}}{2}.

Finally, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle f(x^{k+1})-f^{\inf}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x^{k})-f^{\inf}-\gamma\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|+2\gamma\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})-G^{k}\right\|+\frac{L\gamma^{2}}{2}.

∎

Finally,
Lemma

5
derives the sublinear convergence from the given descent inequality.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\{V^{k}\}
,
\{W^{k}\}
be non-negative sequences satisfying

\displaystyle V^{k+1}\leq(1+b_{1}\gamma^{2})V^{k}-b_{2}\gamma W^{k}+b_{3}\gamma.

Then,

\displaystyle\underset{k\in[0,K]}{\min}W^{k}\leq\frac{\exp(b_{1}\gamma^{2}(K+1))}{K+1}\frac{V^{0}}{b_{2}\gamma}+\frac{b_{3}}{b_{2}}.

Proof.

Define
w^{k}:=\frac{w^{k+1}}{1+b_{1}\gamma^{2}}
for all
k\geq 0
. Then,

\displaystyle w^{k}W^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{w^{k}(1+b_{1}\gamma^{2})V^{k}}{b_{2}\gamma}-\frac{w^{k}V^{k+1}}{b_{2}\gamma}+\frac{b_{3}}{b_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w^{k-1}V^{k}-w^{k}V^{k+1}}{b_{2}\gamma}+\frac{b_{3}}{b_{2}}.

By summing the inequality over
k=0,1,\ldots,K
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K}w^{k}W^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{w^{-1}V^{0}-w^{K}V^{K+1}}{b_{2}\gamma}+\frac{b_{3}}{b_{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w^{k}

\displaystyle\overset{w^{k},V^{k}\geq 0}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{w^{-1}V^{0}}{b_{2}\gamma}+\frac{b_{3}}{b_{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w^{k}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0,K]}W^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sum_{k=0}^{K}w^{k}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}w^{k}W^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{w^{-1}V^{0}}{b_{2}\gamma\sum_{k=0}^{K}w^{k}}+\frac{b_{3}}{b_{2}}.

Next, since

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K}w^{k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(K+1)\min_{k\in[0,K]}w^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(K+1)w^{K+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(K+1)w^{-1}}{(1+b_{1}\gamma^{2})^{K+1}},

we get

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0,K]}W^{k}\leq\frac{(1+b_{1}\gamma^{2})^{K+1}V^{0}}{b_{2}\gamma(K+1)}+\frac{b_{3}}{b_{2}}.

Finally, since
1+x\leq\exp(x)
, we have
(1+b_{1}\gamma^{2})^{K+1}\leq\exp(b_{1}\gamma^{2}(K+1))
. Using this result, we complete the proof.

∎

Appendix C
Multiple Local GD Steps

We derive the convergence theorem (
Theorem

1
) and associated corollaries of
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
using multiple local gradient descent (GD) steps.
The multiple local GD steps have the following update rule:

\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})=x^{k}-\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,j}).

Here,

x_{i}^{k,j}
is updated according to:

x^{k,j+1}_{i}=x^{k,j}_{i}-\frac{\gamma}{T}\nabla f_{i}(x^{k,j}_{i})\quad\text{for}\quad j=0,1,\ldots,T-1.

We begin by introducing two key lemmas for analyzing

\alpha
-NormEC
using multiple local GD steps.

Lemma

bounds

, while

Lemma 6
.

Let
f
be bounded from below by
f^{\inf}>-\infty
, and each
f_{i}
be bounded from below by
f_{i}^{\inf}>-\infty
and
L
-smooth. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\right\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2L}{\Delta^{\inf}}}[f(x)-f^{\inf}]+\sqrt{2L\Delta^{\inf}},

where
\Delta^{\inf}=f^{\inf}-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}f_{i}^{\inf}\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
f
be bounded from below by
f^{\inf}>-\infty
, and each
f_{i}
be bounded from below by
f_{i}^{\inf}>-\infty
and
L
-smooth. Then,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\right\|^{2}\leq 2L[f_{i}(x)-f_{i}^{\inf}].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\right\|^{2}\leq A[f(x)-f^{\inf}]+B,

where
A=2L
,
B=2L\Delta^{\inf}
, and
\Delta^{\inf}=f^{\inf}-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}f_{i}^{\inf}\geq 0
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Jensen's inequality}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\right\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{A[f(x)-f^{\inf}]+B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A[f(x)-f^{\inf}]+B}{\sqrt{A[f(x)-f^{\inf}]+B}}

\displaystyle\overset{f(x)\geq f^{\inf}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{A}{\sqrt{B}}[f(x)-f^{\inf}]+\sqrt{B}.

∎

Lemma 7
.

Let each
f_{i}
be
L
-smooth, and let
\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})=x^{k}-\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,j})
, where the sequence
\{x_{i}^{k,l}\}
is generated by

\displaystyle x_{i}^{k,l+1}=x_{i}^{k,l}-\frac{\gamma}{T}\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,l}),\quad\text{for}\quad l=0,1,\ldots,T-1,

given that
x_{i}^{k,0}=x^{k}
. If
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2L}
, and
\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|\leq\eta
with
\eta>0
, then

1.

x_{i}^{k,l}=x^{k}-\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,l})
.

2.

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x_{i}^{k+1,j}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|\leq 2\eta
.

3.

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x^{k}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|\leq 2\gamma\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})\right\|
.

4.

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k+1})-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})\right\|\leq 2\eta
.

5.

\left\|(x^{k}-\gamma\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}))-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})\right\|\leq 2L\gamma^{2}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})\right\|
.

Proof.

We prove the first statement by recursively applying the equation for
x_{i}^{k,j+1}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,l-1
.

Next, we prove the second statement. From the definition of the Euclidean norm,

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k+1,l}_{i}-x_{i}^{k,l}\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{x_{i}^{k,j}}{=}

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}-\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}(\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k+1,j})-\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,j}))\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{triangle inequality}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|+\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k+1,j})-\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,j})\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{$L$-smoothness of $f_{i}$}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|+\frac{L\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\left\|x_{i}^{k+1,j}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|.

If
\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|\leq\eta
with
\eta>0
, then

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k+1,l}_{i}-x_{i}^{k,l}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta+\frac{L\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\left\|x_{i}^{k+1,j}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{l\leq T}{\leq}

\displaystyle\eta+\frac{L\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x_{i}^{k+1,j}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x^{k+1,j}_{i}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|\leq\eta T+L\gamma\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x_{i}^{k+1,j}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|.

If
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2L}
, then
L\gamma\leq 1/2
, and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x^{k+1,j}_{i}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|\leq 2\eta.

Next, we prove the third statement. From the definition of the Euclidean norm,

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|

\displaystyle\overset{x_{i}^{k,l}}{=}

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,j})\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}[\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,j})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})+\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})]\right\|.

By the triangle inequality, and by the
L
-smoothness of
f_{i}(\cdot)
,

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,j})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})\right\|+\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\left\|x_{i}^{k,j}-x^{k}\right\|+\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})\right\|.

By the fact that
l\leq T
and that
\left\|x\right\|\geq 0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x_{i}^{k,j}-x^{k}\right\|+\gamma\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})\right\|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x^{k}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\gamma\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x_{i}^{k,j}-x^{k}\right\|+\gamma T\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})\right\|.

If
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2L}
, then
L\gamma\leq 1/2
, and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x^{k}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|\leq 2\gamma T\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})\right\|.

Next, we prove the fourth statement. From the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k+1})-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}-\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}[\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,l+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,l})]\right\|.

By the triangle inequality, and by the
L
-smoothness of
f_{i}(\cdot)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k+1})-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|+\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,l+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,l})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|+\frac{L\gamma}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\left\|x_{i}^{k,l+1}-x_{i}^{k,l}\right\|.

By the fact that
\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|\leq\eta
, that

, and that
\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\left\|x_{i}^{k+1,j}-x_{i}^{k,j}\right\|\leq 2\eta T

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k+1})-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})\right\|\leq\eta+L\gamma\cdot 2\eta\overset{L\gamma\leq 1/2}{\leq}2\eta.

Finally, we prove the fifth statement. From the definition of

\displaystyle\left\|(x^{k}-\gamma\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}))-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})\right\|=\left\|\left(x^{k}-\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{l=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})\right)-\left(x^{k}-\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{l=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,l})\right)\right\|.

By the triangle inequality, the

-smoothness of
f_{i}(\cdot)
, and the fact that

we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|(x^{k}-\gamma\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}))-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{T}\sum_{l=0}^{T-1}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})-\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}^{k,l})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L\gamma}{T}\sum_{l=0}^{T-1}\left\|x^{k}-x_{i}^{k,l}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2L\gamma^{2}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})\right\|.

∎

By the fact that

from Step 1),

\displaystyle f(x^{k+1})-f^{\inf}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x^{k})-f^{\inf}-\eta\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|+2\eta R

\displaystyle+\frac{2\eta}{\gamma}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left\|(x^{k}-\gamma\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}))-\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})\right\|+2\eta\left\|\hat{v}^{k+1}-v^{k+1}\right\|+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}.

Case 1)
\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})
with

.

\mathcal{T}_{i}(x^{k})

Appendix D
Multiple Local IG steps

In this section, we derive the convergence theorem of
Fed-
\alpha
-NormEC
with multiple local steps using the Incremental Gradient (IG) method.
The IG method has the following update rule.

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{IG}_{i}(x^{k})=x^{k}-\gamma\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\nabla f_{i,j}(x^{k,j}_{i}),

(3)

where
x_{i}^{k,j}
is updated according to:

x^{k,j+1}_{i}=x^{k,j}_{i}-\frac{\gamma}{N}\nabla f_{i,j}(x^{k,j}_{i})\quad\text{for}\quad j=0,1,\ldots,T-1.

In the update rule of the IG method, the number of local steps is equal to the size of the local data set. This implies that each client performs local updates
\mathcal{T}_{i}^{IG}(\cdot)
using their entire local dataset. Furthermore, the IG method employs a fixed, deterministic permutation for its cyclic updates, unlike the well-known Random Reshuffling method.

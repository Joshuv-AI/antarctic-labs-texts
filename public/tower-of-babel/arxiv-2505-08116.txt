De Sitter JT gravity from double-scaled SYK
Kazumi Okuyama

Abstract
It is known that the double-scaled SYK model (DSSYK) reduces to JT gravity with a negative cosmological constant by zooming in on the lower edge E = − E 0 E=-E_{0} of the spectrum. We find that the de Sitter JT gravity (i.e. JT gravity with a positive cosmological constant) is reproduced from DSSYK by taking a scaling limit around the upper edge E = E 0 E=E_{0} of the spectrum. We also argue that the appearance of de Sitter JT gravity is consistent with the behavior of the classical solution of the sine dilaton gravity.

2
Review of DSSYK and ETH matrix model

In this section, we will briefly review the double-scaled SYK model (DSSYK)
and the ETH matrix model.
The Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model is a system of
N
Majorana fermions
\psi_{i}~(i=1,\cdots,N)

with all-to-all
p
-body interaction
Sachdev1993
;
Kitaev1
;
Kitaev2

\displaystyle H_{\text{SYK}}=\mathrm{i}^{p/2}\sum_{i_{i}<\cdots<i_{p}}J_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}\psi_{i_{1}}\cdots\psi_{i_{p}},

(2)

where
J_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}
is a Gaussian random coupling with zero mean and the variance

\displaystyle\langle J_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}^{2}\rangle=\mathcal{J}^{2}\binom{N}{p}^{-1}.

(3)

DSSYK is defined by the scaling limit

\displaystyle N,p\to\infty\quad\text{with}\quad\lambda=\frac{2p^{2}}{N}:~\text{fixed}.

(4)

In this limit, the computation of the moment
\langle\tr(H_{\text{SYK}})^{k}\rangle

boils down
to the counting problem of the intersection numbers of chord diagrams.
This counting problem can be exactly solved in terms of the
q
-deformed oscillators
with
q=e^{-\lambda}
.
In this way, we arrive at the exact result of the
disk partition function of DSSYK
Berkooz:2018jqr

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\beta)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\mu(\theta)e^{-\beta E(\theta)},

(5)

where
E(\theta)
and
\mu(\theta)
are given by

\displaystyle E(\theta)=-E_{0}\cos\theta,\quad\mu(\theta)=(q,e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}\theta};q)_{\infty},

(6)

with
E_{0}=2\mathcal{J}/\sqrt{1-q}
.
Note that the disk partition function (
5
) is an even function
of
\beta

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(-\beta)=\mathcal{Z}(\beta),

(7)

which follows from the symmetry

\displaystyle E(\pi-\theta)=-E(\theta),\quad\mu(\pi-\theta)=\mu(\theta).

(8)

An important property of DSSYK is that
the energy spectrum
E(\theta)
is bounded from below and above

\displaystyle-E_{0}\leq E(\theta)\leq E_{0}.

(9)

The lower end
E=-E_{0}
and the upper end
E=E_{0}
of the spectrum correspond
to
\theta=0
and
\theta=\pi
, respectively.
The end-points
E=\pm E_{0}
are characterized as
the saddle points of the Boltzmann factor

e^{-\beta E(\theta)}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}E(\theta)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\theta=0,\pi.

(10)

It turns out that
\theta=0
is a stable saddle point while
\theta=\pi

is an unstable saddle point, as we will see below.

2.1
ETH matrix model

As discussed in
Jafferis:2022wez
,
one can introduce the
L\times L
hermitian matrix model,
called ETH matrix model, which reproduces the
density of states
\mu(\theta)
of DSSYK in the large
L
limit,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\int_{L\times L}dHe^{-L\Tr V(H)},

(11)

where
L
is the dimension of the Hilbert space of
N
Majorana fermions

\displaystyle L=2^{N/2}.

(12)

By matching the genus-zero eigenvalue density
\rho_{0}(E)
of ETH matrix model
with the density of states
\mu(\theta)
of DSSYK in (
6
)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(E(\theta))dE(\theta)=\mu(\theta)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},

(13)

the potential
V(H)
in (
11
) is determined as

\displaystyle V(H)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}q^{\frac{1}{2}n^{2}}(q^{\frac{1}{2}n}+q^{-\frac{1}{2}n})T_{2n}(H/E_{0}),

(14)

where
T_{n}(x)
denotes the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind.
Then the disk partition function
\mathcal{Z}(\beta)
of
DSSYK in (
5
) is reproduced
from the ETH matrix model (
11
) in the large
L
limit

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\beta)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L}\langle\Tr e^{-\beta H}\rangle,

(15)

where the expectation value is defined by

\displaystyle\langle f(H)\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\int dHe^{-L\Tr V(H)}f(H).

(16)

We can also consider the connected correlator
of
\Tr e^{-\beta H}
in the ETH matrix model, and its
genus expansion
in the large
L
limit is written as

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Tr e^{-\beta_{i}H}\right\rangle_{\text{conn}}=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}L^{2-2g-n}\mathcal{Z}_{g,n}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n}).

(17)

In this notation, the disk partition function

\mathcal{Z}(\beta)
in (
5
) is the
(g,n)=(0,1)
contribution in (
17
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\beta)=\mathcal{Z}_{0,1}(\beta).

(18)

We can systematically compute
\mathcal{Z}_{g,n}
in (
17
)
by the topological recursion
with the spectral curve
(x(z),y(z))

Okuyama:2023kdo

\displaystyle x(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{0}}{2}(z+z^{-1}),

(19)

\displaystyle y(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E_{0}}(z^{-1}-z)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})(1-z^{2}q^{n})(1-z^{-2}q^{n}).

Note that
x(z)
is related to the energy spectrum
E(\theta)
in (
6
)
by

\displaystyle E(\theta)=-x(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}).

(20)

Introducing
\omega_{g,n}
by

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Tr\frac{dx(z_{i})}{x(z_{i})+H}\right\rangle_{\text{conn}}=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}L^{2-2g-n}\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}),

(21)

\omega_{g,n}
can be computed by
the Eynard-Orantin’s topological recursion
Eynard:2007kz
;
Eynard:2008we

\displaystyle\omega_{g,n+1}(z_{0},J)=\sum_{\alpha=\pm 1}\underset{z=\alpha}{\text{Res}}K(z_{0},z)\Bigl[\omega_{g-1,n+2}(z,z^{-1},J)+\sum_{h=0}^{g}\sum^{\prime}_{I\subset J}\omega_{h,1+|I|}(z,I)\omega_{g-h,1+n-|I|}(z^{-1},J\backslash I)\Bigr]

(22)

together with the initial conditions

\displaystyle\omega_{0,1}(z)=-y(z)dx(z),\quad\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}.

(23)

The recursion kernel
K(z_{0},z)
is given by

\displaystyle K(z_{0},z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{z^{\prime}=z^{-1}}^{z}\omega_{0,2}(z_{0},z^{\prime})}{4y(z)dx(z)}.

(24)

In (
22
), the prime in the summation means that

(h,I)=(0,\emptyset),(g,J)
are excluded.
Note that in (
22
) we take the residue at
z=\pm 1
, which
correspond to the branch points of
x(z)

\displaystyle\frac{dx(z)}{dz}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad z=\pm 1.

(25)

From the relation (
20
),

z=\pm 1
correspond to
E=\mp E_{0}
.

As discussed in
norbury2013polynomials
,

\omega_{g,n}
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})=\sum_{\mathfrak{b}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{b}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}N_{g,n}(\mathfrak{b}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{b}_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{b}_{i}z_{i}^{\mathfrak{b}_{i}-1}dz_{i},

(26)

where
N_{g,n}(\mathfrak{b}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{b}_{n})
is interpreted as a discrete analogue
of the volume of the moduli space of Riemann surfaces
of genus
g
with
n
boundaries.
Finally,
\mathcal{Z}_{g,n}
in (
17
) is obtained from
\omega_{g,n}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{g,n}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n})

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\oint_{z_{i}=0}\frac{e^{\beta_{i}x(z_{i})}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\right)\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})

(27)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{b}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{b}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}N_{g,n}(\mathfrak{b}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{b}_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{b}_{i}\mathcal{Z}_{\text{trumpet}}(\beta_{i},\mathfrak{b}_{i}),

where
\mathcal{Z}_{\text{trumpet}}(\beta,\mathfrak{b})

is the trumpet partition function of DSSYK
Jafferis:2022wez
;
Okuyama:2023byh

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\text{trumpet}}(\beta,\mathfrak{b})=\oint_{z=0}\frac{dz}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}z^{\mathfrak{b}}e^{\beta x(z)}=I_{\mathfrak{b}}(\beta E_{0}).

(28)

Here
I_{\nu}(x)
denotes the modified Bessel function of the first kind.

3
Scaling limit around
E=-E_{0}

3.1
Matrix model of JT gravity

As shown in
Saad:2019lba
,
JT gravity with a negative cosmological constant
is equivalent to a double-scaled matrix model.
At the disk level, the eigenvalue density
\rho_{0}(E)

of JT gravity matrix model
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(E)=\frac{\gamma}{2\pi^{2}}\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{2\gamma E}),

(29)

and the disk partition function of JT gravity becomes

\displaystyle Z_{\text{JT}}(\beta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\rho_{0}(E)e^{-\beta E}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(\beta/\gamma)^{3}}}e^{\frac{2\pi^{2}\gamma}{\beta}},

(30)

where
\gamma
is the boundary value of the dilaton.
In the following discussion,
it is convenient to change the integration variable as

\displaystyle E=\frac{k^{2}}{2\gamma}.

(31)

Then
Z_{\text{JT}}(\beta)
in (
30
)
is written as

\displaystyle Z_{\text{JT}}(\beta)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\rho_{s}(k)e^{-\frac{\beta}{2\gamma}k^{2}},

(32)

where
\rho_{s}(k)
is the Schwarzian density of states

Stanford:2017thb

\displaystyle\rho_{s}(k)=\frac{k\sinh(2\pi k)}{2\pi}.

(33)

More generally, the connected correlator of
Z(\beta)
admits a genus expansion

\displaystyle\langle Z(\beta_{1})\cdots Z(\beta_{n})\rangle_{\text{conn}}=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}e^{-(2g-2+n)S_{0}}Z_{g,n}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n}).

(34)

Note that the disk partition function (

corresponds to
(g,n)=(0,1)

\displaystyle Z_{\text{JT}}(\beta)=Z_{0,1}(\beta)=\int_{C}\frac{dz}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}W_{0,1}(z)e^{\frac{\beta}{2\gamma}z^{2}},

where the contour

\displaystyle=\int_{C}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{dz_{i}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}e^{\frac{\beta_{i}}{2\gamma}z_{i}^{2}}\right)W_{g,n}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}),

\displaystyle W_{g,n+1}(z_{0},J)=\underset{z=0}{\text{Res}}\,K(z_{0},z)\Bigl[W_{g-1,n+2}(z,-z,J)+\sum_{h=0}^{g}\sum^{\prime}_{I\subset J}W_{h,1+|I|}(z,I)W_{g-h,1+n-|I|}(-z,J\backslash I)\Bigr],

\displaystyle W_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{1}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}.

(40)

For the spectral curve

4
Scaling limit around

Next, let us consider the limit where we zoom in on

5
Classical solutions of the sine dilaton gravity

In this section we will argue that the de Sitter interpretation of
the ETH matrix model near
E=E_{0}
is consistent with the sine dilaton gravity,
at least at the classical level.

The action of the sine dilaton gravity is given by

\displaystyle S=-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\bigl[\Phi R+W(\Phi)\bigr],\quad W(\Phi)=2\sin\Phi.

(83)

For small
\Phi
, this action reduces to the JT gravity with
a negative cosmological constant

\displaystyle S=-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\Phi(R+2).

(84)

On the other hand, for
\Phi=\pi-\widetilde{\Phi}
with small

\widetilde{\Phi}
, the action (
83
) becomes

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\widetilde{\Phi}(R-2),

(85)

up to an additive constant. This is the action of
JT gravity with a positive cosmological constant.
As discussed in
Blommaert:2024whf
,
this observation suggests that the sine dilaton gravity
contains some information of de Sitter JT gravity.
We will check this expectation at the level of classical solutions.

To find a classical solution of the
dilaton gravity with the general potential

W(\Phi)
, it is convenient to
choose the gauge
where
\Phi
is identified with the radial coordinate
r

Witten:2020ert
;
Gegenberg:1994pv

\displaystyle ds^{2}=A(r)d\tau^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{A(r)},\quad\Phi(r)=r.

(86)

Then
A(r)
is determined by the condition

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(r)=W(r).

(87)

For the sine dilaton case
W(r)=2\sin r
, the classical solution for
A(r)

is given by
Blommaert:2024ydx

\displaystyle A(r)=2\cos\theta-2\cos r,

(88)

where
\theta
is an integration constant which parameterizes the classical solution.
The ADM energy of this solution is
Mertens:2022irh
;
Blommaert:2024ydx

\displaystyle E=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\cos\theta,

(89)

which agrees with the energy spectrum
E(\theta)
of DSSYK
in (
6
) under the identification
E_{0}=\lambda^{-1}
in
(
80
).

Let us expand
A(r)
around
r=\theta
. Setting
u=r-\theta
we find

\displaystyle A(r)=2u\sin\theta+u^{2}\cos\theta+\mathcal{O}(u^{3}).

(90)

The
\mathcal{O}(u^{1})
term is non-zero for
\theta\neq 0,\pi
;
in this case the classical solution (
86
)
describes a Euclidean black hole with the horizon at
r=\theta
.
However, when
\theta=0,\pi
, the
\mathcal{O}(u^{1})
term
vanishes and
A(r)
starts at
\mathcal{O}(u^{2})

\displaystyle A(r)=\pm u^{2},

(91)

where the plus and minus signs correspond to
\theta=0
and
\theta=\pi
, respectively. Then the metric in (
86
) becomes

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\pm\left(u^{2}d\tau^{2}+\frac{du^{2}}{u^{2}}\right).

(92)

The metric inside the parenthesis
is nothing but the metric of Euclidean
AdS_{2}
in the Poincaré
coordinate.
This is consistent with the fact that
JT gravity with a negative cosmological constant is reproduced
from the triple scaling limit of DSSYK near
\theta=0
.
For
\theta=\pi
, the metric in (
92
) is the minus
of the metric of
AdS_{2}
, which is called
-AdS_{2}
in
Maldacena:2019cbz
.
As explained in
Maldacena:2019cbz
, the metric of
-AdS_{2}

has a constant positive curvature and hence it can be thought of as a
two-dimensional de Sitter space

\displaystyle dS_{2}=-AdS_{2}.

(93)

This is consistent with our identification of the
triple scaling limit of DSSYK near
\theta=\pi
as the de Sitter JT gravity.

Note that the disk partition function
of DSSYK is approximated by a sum over the
classical solutions of the sine dilaton gravity
(
86
) and (
88
)
parameterized by
\theta

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{0,1}(\beta)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\mu(\theta)e^{-\beta E(\theta)}\approx\sum_{\text{classical~sol.}}e^{-\beta E(\theta)}.

(94)

This suggests that the contributions of
\theta=0
and
\theta=\pi
are
different saddle points of the bulk action.
We should stress that this picture is different from the
holographic description of the de Sitter bubble inside AdS
using the AdS/CFT correspondence
Freivogel:2005qh
;
Freivogel:2006xu
.
In our case,

dS_{2}
at
\theta=\pi

is not a bubble inside
AdS_{2}
at
\theta=0
; they are
distinct solutions of the bulk action
and hence they are not interpreted as two regions of a single spacetime
separated by some kind of wall.
Rather, they are simply added
as two independent contributions to
\langle\Tr e^{-\beta H}\rangle
,
as shown in (
78
).

6
Conclusion and outlook

In this paper, we found that the triple scaling limit
(
60
) of the ETH matrix model
around the upper end
E=E_{0}
reproduces the de Sitter
JT gravity studied in
Cotler:2024xzz
.
Our identification of de Sitter JT gravity is also supported by the
behavior (
92
)
of the classical solution of the sine dilaton gravity.
In our construction, the de Sitter JT gravity is embedded in a more complete theory,
i.e., the ETH matrix model, and appears as an unstable saddle point
of the ETH matrix model.

There are many interesting open questions.
In this work, we have ignored the effect of matter operators for
simplicity.
As discussed in
Jafferis:2022wez
, if we include the
effect of matter operator, the ETH matrix model becomes a two-matrix model.
It would be interesting to study the behavior of this two-matrix model around
the unstable saddle point
E=E_{0}
along the lines of
Okuyama:2023aup
;
Okuyama:2023yat
.

We did not introduce the observer in our construction
of de Sitter JT gravity. It is argued that the observer is important to define
the quantum gravity on de Sitter space

Chandrasekaran:2022cip
;
Witten:2023qsv
.
It would be interesting to understand the role of observer in our construction
of de Sitter JT gravity. Perhaps, the matter operators
play the role of observers.

The eigenvalue density
\rho_{0}(E)
of the ETH matrix model
has a square-root branch cut near the edge
E=\pm E_{0}
of the spectrum

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(E)\sim\sqrt{E_{0}^{2}-E^{2}}.

(95)

However, this square-root branch cut exists only in the strict large
L
limit.
At finite
L
the square-root branch cut disappears and the
exact finite
L
result drastically differs from the large
L

limit, as discussed in
Maldacena:2004sn
in
the case of minimal string theory.
It would be interesting to understand the fate of our
dS_{2}
saddle
at finite
L
.
It is argued in
Witten:2001kn

that the dimension of the Hilbert space of de Sitter quantum gravity
is finite, determined by the Gibbons-Hawking entropy

Gibbons:1977mu
. It would be interesting to understand the
Hilbert space of the ETH matrix model at finite
L
.

Our identification of
dS_{2}
in DSSYK is different from the
proposal in Susskind
et al.

Susskind:2021esx
;
Susskind:2022dfz
;
Susskind:2022bia
;
Susskind:2023hnj
;
Rahman:2023pgt
;
Rahman:2024iiu
;
Sekino:2025bsc

or Verlinde
et al.

Narovlansky:2023lfz
;
Verlinde:2024znh
;
Verlinde:2024zrh
;
Tietto:2025oxn
:
they seem to identify the de Sitter space as a contribution
of
E=0
, i.e. the middle part of the support of
\rho_{0}(E)
in
(
1
), not the upper end
E=E_{0}
.
It would be interesting to understand the relationship between their
proposal and ours, if any.

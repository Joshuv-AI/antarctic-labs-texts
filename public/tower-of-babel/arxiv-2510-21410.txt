On the conjugate weight function and ultradifferentiable classes of entire functions Thanks: This research was funded in whole or in part by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) 10.55776/PAT9445424
Gerhard Schindl

Abstract
. We introduce the new notion of a conjugate weight function and provide a detailed study of this operation and its properties. Then we apply this knowledge to study classes of ultradifferentiable functions defined in terms of fast growing weight functions in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor and hence violating standard regularity requirements. Therefore, we transfer recent results shown by the author and D.N. Nenning from the weight sequence to the weight function framework. In order to proceed and to complete the picture we also define the conjugate associated weight matrix and investigate the relation to conjugate weight sequences via the corresponding associate weight functions. Finally, as it has already been done in the weight sequence case, we generalize results by M. Markin from the small Gevrey-setting and show how the corresponding non-standard ultradifferentiable function classes can be used to detect boundedness of normal linear operators on Hilbert spaces (associated with an evolution equation problem). On the one hand, when involving the weight matrix here the crucial information concerning regularity of the weak solutions can be expressed in terms of only one weight, namely of the given weight function. But, on the other hand, for the connection to the weighted entire setting the required conditions on the weight function are too restrictive in the general case.

2.2.
Basic definitions and growth relations

First, let us recall the general notion of a weight function in the sense of
[
index
]
and
[
genLegendreconj
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is called a
weight function
if

(*)

\omega
is non-decreasing and

(*)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
.

We are going to use the following notation for any (weight) function
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
and arbitrary
\alpha>0
: set
\omega^{\iota}(t):=\omega(\frac{1}{t})
,
t>0
, and
\omega^{1/\alpha}(t):=\omega(t^{1/\alpha})
,
t\geq 0
. Note that
\omega^{1/\alpha}
is obtained by a power-substitution;
\omega
is a weight function according to the previous definition if and only if
\omega^{1/\alpha}
is so for some/any
\alpha>0
. Moreover,
(\omega^{\iota})^{1/\alpha}(t)=(\omega^{1/\alpha})^{\iota}(t)
for all
t>0
and finally write
\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha}
for
t\mapsto t^{1/\alpha}
. The function
\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha}
is also called the
Gevrey weight
with index
\alpha>0
.

The next two conditions are natural for weight functions and appear frequently in the literature for
Braun-Meise-Taylor weight functions,
see e.g.
[
BonetMeiseMelikhov07
]
, and the abbreviations are motivated by the notation already used in
[
dissertation
]
. We also refer to Section
for more details:

(\omega_{1})

\omega(2t)=O(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, i.e.
\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega(2t)\leq L(\omega(t)+1)
.

(\omega_{6})

\exists\;H\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;2\omega(t)\leq\omega(Ht)+H
.

Next, let us recall some crucial growth relations between weight functions, see
[
ultradifferentiablecomparison
, Sect. 2.3]
: We write

(*)

\sigma\hypertarget{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
if

(2.1)

\tau(t)=O(\sigma(t))\;\text{as}\;t\rightarrow+\infty,

(*)

\sigma\hypertarget{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\tau
if

(2.2)

\tau(t)=o(\sigma(t))\;\text{as}\;t\rightarrow+\infty.

(*)

\sigma
and
\tau
are called
equivalent,
written
\sigma\hypertarget{sim}{\sim}\tau
, if
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
and
\tau\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma
.

(*)

\preceq
is obviously reflexive, relation
\vartriangleleft
implies
\preceq
but
\vartriangleleft
is not reflexive because each weight functions tends to infinity.

Moreover, we write

(*)

\sigma\hypertarget{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
if

\exists\;h,C>0\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau(t)\leq\sigma(ht)+C,

(*)

\sigma\hypertarget{omvartrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
if

\forall\;h>0\;\exists\;C_{h}>0\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau(t)\leq\sigma(ht)+C_{h}.

(*)

We write
\sigma\hypertarget{simc}{\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
if
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
and
\tau\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\sigma
.

(*)

\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}
is reflexive and
\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}
implies
\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}
but
\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}
is in general not reflexive: If
\sigma\hyperlink{omvartrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\sigma
holds then
\sigma
has to be
slowly varying,
i.e.

(2.3)

\forall\;u>0:\;\;\;\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\sigma(t)}=1;

see
[

(1.2.1)
]
,
[

, the comments at the end of
[

and Appendix

2.3.
Growth indices
\gamma(\omega)
and
\overline{\gamma}(\omega)

We briefly recall the definitions of the growth indices
\gamma(\omega)
and
\overline{\gamma}(\omega)
; see
[
index
, Sect. 2.3
(6)
, Sect. 2.4
(7)
]
and the references in these sections.

Weight functions in the sense of Definition
2.1
are sufficient and convenient in order to introduce and work with the crucial indices
\gamma(\omega)
and
\overline{\gamma}(\omega)
. Let
\omega
be a weight function and
\gamma>0
. We say that
\omega
has property
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
if

(2.4)

\exists\;K>1:\;\;\;\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(K^{\gamma}t)}{\omega(t)}<K.

If
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
holds for some
K>1
, then
(P_{\omega,\gamma^{\prime}})
is satisfied for all
\gamma^{\prime}\leq\gamma
with the same
K
since
\omega
is non-decreasing. Moreover we can restrict to
\gamma>0
, because for
\gamma\leq 0
condition
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
is satisfied for any weight function
\omega
, again because
\omega
is non-decreasing and
K>1
. Then put

(2.5)

\gamma(\omega):=\sup\{\gamma>0:\;\;(P_{\omega,\gamma})\;\;\text{is satisfied}\},

and if none condition
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
(with
\gamma>0
) holds then set
\gamma(\omega):=0
.

Analogously, for
\gamma>0
we say that
\omega
has property
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})
if

(2.6)

\exists\;A>1:\;\;\;\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(A^{\gamma}t)}{\omega(t)}>A.

If
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})
holds for some
A>1
, then
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma^{\prime}})
is satisfied for all
\gamma^{\prime}\geq\gamma
with the same
A
since
\omega
is non-decreasing. Moreover, we can restrict to
\gamma>0
because for
\gamma\leq 0
condition
(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})
is never satisfied for any weight function (
\omega
is assumed to be non-decreasing and
A>1
). Then set

(2.7)

\overline{\gamma}(\omega):=\inf\{\gamma>0:\;\;(\overline{P}_{\omega,\gamma})\;\;\text{is satisfied}\}.

We obtain
(0\leq)\gamma(\omega)\leq\overline{\gamma}(\omega)
, see
[
index
, Sect. 2.3 & 2.4]
, and by definition the following identities are valid:

(2.8)

\forall\;\alpha>0:\;\;\;\gamma(\omega^{1/\alpha})=\alpha\gamma(\omega),\hskip 15.0pt\overline{\gamma}(\omega^{1/\alpha})=\alpha\overline{\gamma}(\omega).

The indices
\gamma(\cdot)
and
\overline{\gamma}(\cdot)
are preserved under
\sim
; this follows from the characterizations in the main results
[
index
, Thm. 2.11 & 2.16]
, see also
[
index
, Rem. 2.12]
and the comments after
[
index
, Thm. 2.16]
. Moreover, conditions
(\omega_{1})
and
(\omega_{6})
are equivalent reformulations of these growth indices as follows.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function. Then
\omega
has
(\omega_{1})
if and only if
\gamma(\omega)>0
, see
[
index
, Thm. 2.11, Rem. 2.12, Cor. 2.14]
. And by
[
index
, Thm. 2.16,
(7)
, Cor. 2.17]
condition
(\omega_{6})
holds if and only if
\overline{\gamma}(\omega)<+\infty
.

2.4.
Generalized lower and upper Legendre conjugates

Let
\sigma,\tau
be weight functions according to Definition

. We introduce the
generalized lower Legendre conjugate
by

(2.9)

\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t):=\inf_{s>0}\{\sigma(s)+\tau(t/s)\},\;\;\;t\in[0,+\infty),

and the

by

\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau(t):=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{\sigma(s)-\tau(s/t)\},\;\;\;t\in(0,+\infty).

We also set

which is justified by

]

are studied in detail in

for (general) weight functions and in

and so focusing on

Concerning this setting we refer to Section

\check{\star}
yields again a weight function but, in general, for

this is not clear; see requirements

and

This condition corresponds to

in

3.
The conjugate weight function

3.1.
Definition and basic properties

Let
\omega
be a weight function according to Definition
2.1
. Then introduce the
conjugate function

(3.1)

\omega^{\ast}(s):=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-\omega(t)\},\;\;\;s\in[0,+\infty),

and this implies the following properties:

(*)

By definition
\omega^{\ast}
is non-decreasing and convex since it is given as the supremum of convex functions (indeed the supremum of affine lines).

(*)

One has

(3.2)

\forall\;s\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega^{\ast}(s)\geq\max\{s^{2}-\omega(s),s-\omega(1)\}.

(
3.2
) implies, in particular, that
\log(s)=o(\omega^{\ast}(s))
as
s\rightarrow+\infty
and hence
\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\omega^{\ast}(s)=+\infty
.

(*)

\omega^{\ast}(0)=-\omega(0)
because
\omega
is non-decreasing and therefore, since also
\omega^{\ast}
is non-decreasing, in order to ensure
\omega^{\ast}(s)\geq 0
for all
s\geq 0
, one has to assume
\omega(0)=0
. In this case
\omega^{\ast}(0)=0
holds, too.

The next result characterizes the situation when
\omega^{\ast}
is well-defined on
[0,+\infty)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\omega^{\ast}(s)<+\infty
for all
s\geq 0
.

(ii)

t=o(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
; i.e.
\omega\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\operatorname{id}
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
By assumption

\forall\;s\geq 0\;\exists\;D_{s}>0\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;st-\omega(t)\leq D_{s},

thus
\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{st}{\omega(t)}\leq 1
for all
s\geq 0
since
\omega
is a weight function. As
s\rightarrow+\infty
this verifies
(ii)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
The relation
t=o(\omega(t))
precisely means

\forall\;\epsilon>0\;\exists\;D_{\epsilon}>0\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;t\leq\epsilon\omega(t)+D_{\epsilon}.

Fix
\epsilon>0
, then this estimate implies
\frac{t}{\epsilon}-\omega(t)\leq\frac{D_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}
for all
t\geq 0
and so, by definition, one has
\omega^{\ast}(\epsilon^{-1})\leq\frac{D_{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}
. Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary we are done.
∎

Let
\omega
be a given weight function, then in view of the above observations and Lemma
3.1
in order to ensure that
\omega^{\ast}
is a weight function according to Definition
2.1
and satisfying
\omega^{\ast}(0)=0
, one shall assume the following properties:

(\mathfrak{C}_{1})

\omega(0)=0
,

(\mathfrak{C}_{2})

t=o(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, i.e.
\omega\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\operatorname{id}
.

Note that
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
is obviously preserved under equivalence of weight functions and it is also stable w.r.t.
\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}
. The next result states that
\omega^{\ast}
automatically has
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function, then
t=o(\omega^{\ast}(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
is valid.

Proof.

The desired relation is equivalent to
\sup_{t\geq 0}\{t-\omega(t)/s\}=\frac{\omega^{\ast}(s)}{s}\rightarrow+\infty
as
s\rightarrow+\infty
. And this holds since for any given
C\geq 1
large there exists some
t_{C}\geq 0
such that
t_{C}-\omega(t_{C})/s\geq C\Leftrightarrow s(t_{C}-C)\geq\omega(t_{C})
for all sufficiently large
s\geq 0
: e.g. take
t_{C}:=2C
.
∎

Example 3.3
.

Let
\sigma:=\operatorname{id}^{1/\alpha}
for
0<\alpha<1
and so both
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
are satisfied. Then
\sigma^{\ast}(s)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-t^{1/\alpha}\}
and we compute this supremum by direct computations:

For
s=0
we have
\sigma^{\ast}(0)=0
since
\sigma(0)=0
and for any
s>0
let
f_{s}(t):=st-t^{1/\alpha}
,
t\in[0,+\infty)
. Then
f^{\prime}_{s}(t)=s-\frac{1}{\alpha}t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}=s-\frac{1}{\alpha}t^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}
, so
f^{\prime}_{s}(t)=0
if and only if
(s\alpha)^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}=t
and this value gives the (unique) point where the maximum of
f_{s}
is attained on
[0,+\infty)
. And since
\sigma^{\ast}(s)=f_{s}((s\alpha)^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}})=s(s\alpha)^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}-(s\alpha)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}=s^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}(\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}-\alpha^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}})
we see that
\sigma^{\ast}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\operatorname{id}^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}
.

Note that by the general assumption on the parameter
\alpha
we have that
0<1-\alpha<1
as well and hence
\operatorname{id}^{1/(1-\alpha)}
is a weight function, too. Note also that
\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}-\alpha^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}>0\Leftrightarrow\alpha^{\alpha}>\alpha\Leftrightarrow 1>\alpha^{1-\alpha}
. This example implies, in particular, that up to equivalence the Gevrey-weights with indices
0<\alpha<1
are preserved under taking the conjugate operation and that
\operatorname{id}^{2}:t\mapsto t^{2}
is self-conjugate. This last fact should be compared with the information from
[
microclasses
, Lemma 2.6]
and
[
genLegendreconj
, Ex. 2.9]
.

By combining Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
and motivated by the previous example, when
\omega
is a given weight function satisfying
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
, then it is natural to consider the
double-conjugate
\omega^{\ast\ast}
given by

\omega^{\ast\ast}(t):=(\omega^{\ast})^{\ast}(t)=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{ts-\omega^{\ast}(s)\},\;\;\;t\in[0,+\infty).

Thus
\omega^{\ast\ast}
is again a weight function satisfying
\omega^{\ast\ast}(0)=0
. Moreover, one immediately infers that

(3.3)

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega^{\ast\ast}(t)=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{ts-\sup_{u\geq 0}\{su-\omega(u)\}\}\underbrace{\leq}_{u=t}\omega(t).

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function such that
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
is violated. Then there exists some
s_{0}\in[0,+\infty)
such that
\omega^{\ast}(s)=+\infty
for all
s>s_{0}
. Therefore one might set in this case
\omega^{\ast\ast}(t):=\sup_{s\in[0,s_{0})}\{ts-\omega^{\ast}(s)\}
for all
t\geq 0
and (
3.3
) still holds.

Next we investigate the converse estimate in (
3.3
) and ask: Under which assumptions on
\omega
one can expect that at least
\omega^{\ast\ast}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\omega
holds? We prove the following result which is inspired by (the proof of)
[
PetzscheVogt
, Prop. 1.6]
:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function satisfying
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
. Moreover, assume that
\omega
is
convex,
then

(3.4)

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega(t)=\omega^{\ast\ast}(t).

Note:
The weights treated in Example
3.3
are convex and thus this lemma is consistent with the computations made for these particular weights.

Proof.

First, by
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
the function
\omega^{\ast}
is well-defined and note that by the above comments and assumptions
\omega(0)=0=\omega^{\star\star}(0)
. So let now
t>0
be given and fixed.

Let
u\geq 0
with
u\neq t
, then by convexity one has that
u\mapsto\frac{\omega(u)-\omega(t)}{u-t}
is non-decreasing and since
\omega
is non-decreasing the quotient is non-negative (for any
u\neq t
). There exists
s\geq 0
such that

\inf_{u>t}\frac{\omega(u)-\omega(t)}{u-t}=\omega^{\prime}_{+}(t)\geq s\geq\omega^{\prime}_{-}(t)=\sup_{0\leq u<t}\frac{\omega(u)-\omega(t)}{u-t}.

Multiplying these estimates with
u-t
we get that
s(u-t)\leq\omega(u)-\omega(t)
for all
u\geq 0
and note that for
u=t
this estimate is becoming trivial. Consequently
\inf_{s\geq 0}\sup_{u\geq 0}\{s(u-t)-\omega(u)\}\leq-\omega(t)
, equivalently
-\inf_{s\geq 0}\sup_{u\geq 0}\{s(u-t)-\omega(u)\}\geq\omega(t)
for all
t>0
. The left-hand side yields
-\inf_{s\geq 0}\sup_{u\geq 0}\{s(u-t)-\omega(u)\}=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{-\sup_{u\geq 0}\{s(u-t)-\omega(u)\}\}=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st-\sup_{u\geq 0}\{su-\omega(u)\}\}=\omega^{\ast\ast}(t)
and so the converse of (
3.3
) for all
t>0
. Thus (
3.4
) is shown.
∎

3.2.
On growth relations

The aim is to study how the growth relations between weight functions are transferred under applying the conjugate operation. First, given a weight function
\omega
with
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
, then writing
\omega_{c}(t):=\omega(ct)
for
c>0
one immediately infers

\forall\;c,d>0\;\forall\;s\geq 0:\;\;\;(d\omega_{c})^{\ast}(s)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-d\omega(ct)\}=d\sup_{u\geq 0}\left\{\frac{s}{dc}u-\omega(u)\right\}=d\omega^{\ast}\left(\frac{s}{cd}\right).

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be weight functions. Assume that both weights satisfy
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and such that
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
is valid for
\tau
.

(i)

If
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
, then
\sigma^{\ast}
is also well-defined, i.e.
\sigma
satisfies
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
too, and

(3.5)

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;s\geq 0:\;\;\;C\sigma^{\ast}(s)\leq\tau^{\ast}(sC)+C.

Conversely, (
3.5
) implies
\sigma^{\ast\ast}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau^{\ast\ast}
and the fact that
\sigma^{\ast}
is well-defined.

(ii)

If
\sigma\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\tau
, then
\sigma^{\ast}
is also well-defined and

(3.6)

\forall\;0<c<1\;\exists\;D_{c}\geq 1\;\forall\;s\geq 0:\;\;\;c\sigma^{\ast}(s)\leq\tau^{\ast}(sc)+D_{c}.

Conversely, (
3.6
) implies
\sigma^{\ast\ast}\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\tau^{\ast\ast}
and the fact that
\sigma^{\ast}
is well-defined.

Moreover:

(a)

\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
implies the mixed variant of
(\omega_{6})
between
\sigma^{\ast}
and
\tau^{\ast}
. If either
\sigma^{\ast}
and
\tau^{\ast}
satisfies in addition
(\omega_{1})
, then
\sigma^{\ast}
and
\tau^{\ast}
are related by the mixed variant of
(\omega_{1})
.

(b)

\sigma\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\tau
implies both the mixed variant of
(\omega_{6})
and
(\omega_{1})
between
\sigma^{\ast}
and
\tau^{\ast}
.

Proof.

(i)
By assumption
\tau(t)\leq C\sigma(t)+C
for some
C\geq 1
and for all
t\geq 0
and
\tau^{\ast}
is well-defined. Thus, for any
s\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\tau^{\ast}(s)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-\tau(t)\}\geq\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-C\sigma(t)\}-C=C\sup_{t\geq 0}\{(s/C)t-\sigma(t)\}-C

\displaystyle=C\sigma^{\ast}(s/C)-C.

This estimate implies, in particular, that
\sigma^{\ast}
is well-defined; see also Lemma
3.1
applied to
\sigma
. Moreover, (
3.5
) also implies that
\sigma^{\ast}
is well-defined since
\tau^{\ast}
is so and for any
s\geq 0
one has:

\displaystyle\tau^{\ast\ast}(s)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-\tau^{\ast}(t)\}\leq\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-C\sigma^{\ast}(t/C)\}+C=C\sup_{t\geq 0}\{\frac{st}{C}-\sigma^{\ast}(t/C)\}+C

\displaystyle=C\sup_{u\geq 0}\{su-\sigma^{\ast}(u)\}+C=C\sigma^{\ast\ast}(s)+C.

(ii)
By assumption for any
1>c>0
(small) there exists some
D_{c}\geq 1
such that
\tau(t)\leq c\sigma(t)+D_{c}
for all
t\geq 0
. Consequently, the conclusions follow then by the same computations as in
(i)
.

(a)
W.l.o.g. one can assume that
C\geq 2
in relation
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
and hence (
3.5
) implies
2\sigma^{\ast}(s)\leq\tau^{\ast}(sC)+C
for all
s\geq 0
which is the mixed version of
(\omega_{6})
.

If
\tau^{\ast}
satisfies in addition
(\omega_{1})
, then choose
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
minimal such that
2^{n-1}\geq C
with
C
denoting the constant appearing in (
3.5
). Iteration of
(\omega_{1})
implies

C\sigma^{\ast}(2s)\leq\tau^{\ast}(2Cs)+C\leq\tau^{\ast}(2^{n}s)+C\leq L\tau^{\ast}(s)+L+C
for some
L\geq 1
and all
s\geq 0
. Similarly, if
\sigma^{\ast}
satisfies in addition
(\omega_{1})
, then
C\sigma^{\ast}(2s)\leq CL\sigma^{\ast}(C^{-1}s)+CL\leq L\tau^{\ast}(s)+CL+CL
for some
L\geq 1
and all
s\geq 0
. Both derived estimates yield the conclusion.

(b)
Since
\sigma\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\tau
is stronger than
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
, the mixed variant of
(\omega_{6})
follows by
(a)
and the mixed variant of
(\omega_{1})
holds by choosing
c:=\frac{1}{2}
in (
3.6
).
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function satisfying
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
. Moreover, assume that
\omega
is equivalent to a convex weight function
\sigma
satisfying
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
. Then
\omega
and
\omega^{\ast\ast}
are equivalent; more precisely:

(3.7)

\omega\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\sigma=\sigma^{\ast\ast}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\omega^{\ast\ast}.

Proof.

First, recall that
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
is preserved under equivalence and hence
\sigma
shares this property, too. Then
(i)
in Lemma
3.6
gives that
\omega^{\ast\ast}
and
\sigma^{\ast\ast}
are equivalent and by Lemma
3.5
we infer that
\sigma(t)=\sigma^{\ast\ast}(t)
for any
t\geq 0
. Combining everything yields the desired relations stated in (
3.7
).
∎

Next we treat
\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}
,
\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}
and verify that these relations are reversed when taking the conjugate. Thus these relations can be considered to be more natural when dealing with conjugate weight functions.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be weight functions. Assume that both weights satisfy
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and such that
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
is valid for
\tau
.

(i)

If
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
, then

(3.8)

\exists\;h,C\geq 1\;\forall\;s\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma^{\ast}(s)\leq\tau^{\ast}(sh)+C;

i.e.
\tau^{\ast}\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\sigma^{\ast}
. Moreover,
\sigma^{\ast}
is also well-defined, i.e.
\sigma
satisfies
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
, too.

Conversely, (
3.8
) implies
\sigma^{\ast\ast}\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau^{\ast\ast}
and that
\sigma^{\ast}
is also well-defined.

(ii)

If
\sigma\hyperlink{omvartrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
, then

(3.9)

\forall\;0<h<1\;\exists\;C_{h}\geq 1\;\forall\;s\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma^{\ast}(s)\leq\tau^{\ast}(sh)+C_{h};

i.e.
\tau^{\ast}\hyperlink{omvartrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\sigma^{\ast}
. Moreover,
\sigma^{\ast}
is also well-defined. Conversely, (
3.9
) implies that
\sigma^{\ast}
is well-defined and
\sigma^{\ast\ast}\hyperlink{omvartrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau^{\ast\ast}
.

In addition we get:

(a)

If
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
and either
\sigma^{\ast}
and
\tau^{\ast}
satisfies in addition
(\omega_{1})
, then
\sigma^{\ast}
and
\tau^{\ast}
are related by the mixed variant of
(\omega_{1})
.

(b)

\sigma\hyperlink{omvartrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
implies the mixed variant of
(\omega_{1})
between
\sigma^{\ast}
and
\tau^{\ast}
.

Proof.

(i)
By assumption
\tau(t)\leq\sigma(ht)+C
for some
C,h\geq 1
and for all
t\geq 0
and
\tau^{\ast}
is well-defined. Then, for any
s\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\tau^{\ast}(s)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-\tau(t)\}\geq\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-\sigma(ht)\}-C=\sup_{u\geq 0}\{(s/h)u-\sigma(u)\}-C

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\ast}(s/h)-C.

This estimate implies that
\sigma^{\ast}
is well-defined. Conversely, we get for any
s\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\tau^{\ast\ast}(s)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-\tau^{\ast}(t)\}\leq\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-\sigma^{\ast}(t/h)\}+C=\sup_{u\geq 0}\{(sh)u-\sigma^{\ast}(u)\}+C

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\ast\ast}(sh)+C.

(ii)
By assumption even for any

(small) there exists some

such that

for all

. Consequently, the conclusions follow by the same computations as in
(i)
.

(a)
Note that in general

and hence the conclusion does not follow automatically. However, by iterating

.

(b)
We choose

.
∎

3.3.
On the generalized Legendre envelopes

We study the interaction of the conjugate operation and the generalized Legendre envelope operators
\check{\star}
and
\widehat{\star}
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be given weight functions such that
\sigma(0)=0=\tau(0)
, i.e. both weights satisfy
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
. Moreover, assume that
\sigma\check{\star}\tau
satisfies
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
, then

(3.10)

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau^{\ast}\widehat{\star}\sigma(t)=(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)^{\ast}(t)=\sigma^{\ast}\widehat{\star}\tau(t),

and

(3.11)

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma^{\ast}\widehat{\star}\tau(t)=((\operatorname{id}\widehat{\star}\tau)\widehat{\star}\sigma)(t).

Proof.

\sigma\check{\star}\tau
is a weight function such that
\sigma\check{\star}\tau(0)=\sigma(0)+\tau(0)=0
, see
[
genLegendreconj
, Lemma 3.1
(a)\&(b)
]
, and by assumption
(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)^{\ast}
is well-defined. Note that
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
for
\sigma\check{\star}\tau
precisely means
t=o(\sigma\check{\star}\tau(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
and, in view of
[
genLegendreconj
, Lemma 3.1
(a)
]
, this property implies
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
for
\sigma
and
\tau
. Let
t>0
be given, then:

\displaystyle(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)^{\ast}(t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st-\sigma\check{\star}\tau(s)\}=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st-\inf_{u>0}\{\sigma(u)+\tau(s/u)\}\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st+\sup_{u>0}\{-\sigma(u)-\tau(s/u)\}\}=\sup_{s\geq 0}\sup_{u>0}\{st-\sigma(u)-\tau(s/u)\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{u>0}\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st-\sigma(u)-\tau(s/u)\}=\sup_{u>0}\{-\sigma(u)+\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st-\tau(s/u)\}\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{u>0}\{-\sigma(u)+\sup_{v\geq 0}\{uvt-\tau(v)\}\}=\sup_{u>0}\{-\sigma(u)+\tau^{\ast}(ut)\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{w>0}\{\tau^{\ast}(w)-\sigma(w/t)\}=\sup_{w\geq 0}\{\tau^{\ast}(w)-\sigma(w/t)\}=\tau^{\ast}\widehat{\star}\sigma(t).

Note that for
t=0
we have
(\sigma\check{\star}\tau)^{\ast}(0)=-\sigma\check{\star}\tau(0)=0
, whereas by
[
genLegendreconj
, Lemma 4.1
(b)
]
and
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
it holds that
\tau^{\ast}\widehat{\star}\sigma(0)=-\tau(0)-\sigma(0)=0
. Summarizing, the first part in (
3.10
) is verified.

And since
\check{\star}
is commutative, see
[
genLegendreconj
, Lemma 3.1
(c)
]
, analogously one infers the second one, too. Finally, by this computation
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
for
\sigma\check{\star}\tau
implies that both
\tau^{\ast}\widehat{\star}\sigma
and
\sigma^{\ast}\widehat{\star}\tau
are well-defined.

Concerning (
3.11
) let
t>0
, then

\displaystyle(\sigma^{\ast}\widehat{\star}\tau)(t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{\sigma^{\ast}(s)-\tau(s/t)\}=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{\sup_{u\geq 0}\{su-\sigma(u)\}-\tau(s/t)\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\geq 0}\{\sup_{s\geq 0}\{su-\sigma(u)\}-\tau(s/t)\}=\sup_{u\geq 0}\{\sup_{s\geq 0}\{su-\tau(s/t)\}-\sigma(u)\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{u>0}\{\sup_{v\geq 0}\{v-\tau(v/(ut))\}-\sigma(u)\}=\sup_{u>0}\{\operatorname{id}\widehat{\star}\tau(ut)-\sigma(u)\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{w>0}\{\operatorname{id}\widehat{\star}\tau(w)-\sigma(w/t)\}=\sup_{w\geq 0}\{\operatorname{id}\widehat{\star}\tau(w)-\sigma(w/t)\}=((\operatorname{id}\widehat{\star}\tau)\widehat{\star}\sigma)(t).

For
t=0
, again by
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and
[
genLegendreconj
, Lemma 4.1
(b)
]
we infer
(\sigma^{\ast}\widehat{\star}\tau)(0)=-\sigma(0)-\tau(0)=0
,
((\operatorname{id}\widehat{\star}\tau)\widehat{\star}\sigma)(0)=(\operatorname{id}(0)-\tau(0))-\sigma(0)=0
, and hence equality for all
t\geq 0
.
∎

3.4.
The conjugate operation and growth indices

We study now the effect of taking the conjugate operation defined in (
3.1
) on the growth indices
\gamma(\omega)
and
\overline{\gamma}(\omega)
defined in Section
2.3
. First, note that
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma
(reflexivity) is clear and one can take in the proof of
(i)
in Lemma
3.6
any constant
C\geq 1
and
\sigma=\tau
. Thus, by applying
(a)
in this result to
\sigma=\tau
and in view of Remark
2.2
we immediately get the following result:

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\sigma
be a weight function satisfying
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
. Then
\sigma^{\ast}
satisfies
(\omega_{6})
, i.e.
\overline{\gamma}(\sigma^{\ast})<+\infty
.

Moreover, the second part of
(a)
in Lemma
3.6
becomes trivial, however
(b)
cannot be applied to
\sigma=\tau
since relation
\vartriangleleft
is not reflexive.

The next result relates the growth indices of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\ast}
in a precise manner and which should be compared with
[
microclasses
,
(A.1)\&(A.2)
]
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\sigma
be a weight function satisfying
(\mathfrak{C}_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
.

(i)

If
\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)<1
, then
1-\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)\leq\gamma(\sigma^{\ast})
.

(ii)

If
0<\gamma(\sigma)<1
, then
\overline{\gamma}(\sigma^{\ast})\leq 1-\gamma(\sigma)
.

(iii)

Assume that in addition
\sigma^{\ast\ast}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\sigma
holds. Then the following is valid:

(a)

0<\gamma(\sigma)<1
implies
\gamma(\sigma)=1-\overline{\gamma}(\sigma^{\ast})
.

(b)

0<\gamma(\sigma)\leq\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)<1
implies
1-\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)=\gamma(\sigma^{\ast})
.

(c)

0<\gamma(\sigma^{\ast})<1
implies
\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)=1-\gamma(\sigma^{\ast})
.

(d)

0<\gamma(\sigma^{\ast})\leq\overline{\gamma}(\sigma^{\ast})<1
implies
\gamma(\sigma)=1-\overline{\gamma}(\sigma^{\ast})
.

Proof.

First, note that by assumption on
\sigma
the conjugate
\sigma^{\ast}
is also a weight function and
\sigma^{\ast\ast}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\sigma
implies
\gamma(\sigma^{\ast\ast})=\gamma(\sigma)
and
\overline{\gamma}(\sigma^{\ast\ast})=\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)
.

(i)
Let
\gamma>0
be such that
\gamma<1-\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)(\leq 1)
and such a choice is possible by assumption on the index
\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)
. Since
1-\gamma>\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)
by definition of this index

\exists\;A>1\;\exists\;\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1)\;\exists\;H\geq 0\;\forall\;\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma(A^{1-\gamma}t)\geq A^{1+\epsilon}\sigma(t)-H,

and we estimate as follows for all
s\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\sigma^{\ast}(A^{\gamma-1}s)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{(A^{\gamma-1}s)t-\sigma(t)\}\underbrace{=}_{u=A^{\gamma-1}t}\sup_{u\geq 0}\{su-\sigma(A^{1-\gamma}u)\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{u\geq 0}\{su-A^{1+\epsilon}\sigma(u)\}+H=A^{1+\epsilon}\sup_{u\geq 0}\{(A^{-1-\epsilon}s)u-\sigma(u)\}+H

\displaystyle=A^{1+\epsilon}\sigma^{\ast}(A^{-1-\epsilon}s)+H.

Summarizing, this estimate gives
\sigma^{\ast}(A^{\gamma+\epsilon}s)\leq A^{1+\epsilon}\sigma^{\ast}(s)+H
for all
s\geq 0
. Now fix
\epsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
and let
\epsilon>0
be such that
\epsilon<\gamma\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon_{0}
. Then set
K:=A^{1+\epsilon^{\prime}}(>A^{1+\epsilon})
,
\gamma^{\prime}:=\frac{\gamma+\epsilon}{1+\epsilon^{\prime}}
and so
\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma\Leftrightarrow\epsilon<\gamma\epsilon^{\prime}
holds. Therefore, the verified estimate implies
(P_{\sigma^{\ast},\gamma^{\prime}})
with this choice for
K
and so by definition
\gamma(\sigma^{\ast})\geq\gamma^{\prime}
. (Indeed, in view of
[
index
, Thm. 2.11]
we even have a strict inequality here.) Then let
\epsilon^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
, consequently
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
and hence
\gamma(\sigma^{\ast})\geq\gamma
holds. Since
0<\gamma<1-\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)
was chosen arbitrarily we have shown that
1-\overline{\gamma}(\sigma)\leq\gamma(\sigma^{\ast})
.

(ii)
Let
0<\gamma<1
be such that
\gamma(\sigma)>1-\gamma(>0)
, i.e.
\gamma>1-\gamma(\sigma)
, and note that such values
\gamma
exist by assumption on
\gamma(\sigma)
. Then by definition we get

\exists\;K>1\;\exists\;\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1)\;\exists\;H\geq 0\;\forall\;\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma(K^{1-\gamma}t)\leq K^{1-\epsilon}\sigma(t)+H.

Hence for all
s\geq 0

\displaystyle\sigma^{\ast}(K^{\gamma-1}s)

4.
The weight sequence setting

In this section we focus on the case
\omega=\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
, i.e. when the weight function is expressed in terms of a given weight sequence
\mathbf{M}
.
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
denotes the corresponding
associated weight function
and due to this additional information we can consider
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}^{\ast}
and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}^{\ast}}
,
\mathbf{M}^{\ast}
denoting the
conjugate sequence
introduced and used in
[
microclasses
]
. Thus the goal is to establish a precise connection between both notions: the conjugate in the weight function and in the weight sequence framework.

4.1.
Weight sequences

Given a sequence
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
we also use the notation
\mu=(\mu_{p})_{p}
with
\mu_{p}:=\frac{M_{p}}{M_{p-1}}
,
p\geq 1
,
\mu_{0}:=1
. Moreover, we set
m_{p}:=\frac{M_{p}}{p!}
and obtain the sequence
\mathbf{m}=(m_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
. Analogously these conventions are used for all other sequences under consideration.
\mathbf{M}
is called
normalized,
if
1=M_{0}\leq M_{1}
is valid. For the
pointwise product sequence
we simply write
\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{N}
and
\frac{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{N}}
for the
pointwise quotient sequence;
i.e. these sequences are given by
M_{p}\cdot N_{p}
and
\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}
respectively.

\mathbf{M}
is called
log-convex
if

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;M_{p}^{2}\leq M_{p-1}M_{p+1},

equivalently if
\mu
is non-decreasing.
\mathbf{M}
is called
strong log-convex
if even
\mathbf{m}
is log-convex which is equivalent to
p\mapsto\frac{\mu_{p}}{p}
being non-decreasing. If
\mathbf{M}
is log-convex and normalized, then
\mu_{p}\geq 1
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
, both mappings
p\mapsto M_{p}
and
p\mapsto(M_{p})^{1/p}
are non-decreasing,
(M_{p})^{1/p}\leq\mu_{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and finally
M_{p}M_{q}\leq M_{p+q}
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
; see e.g.
[
diploma
, Lemmas 2.0.4 & 2.0.6]
.

\mathbf{M}
satisfies
moderate growth,
abbreviated in the following by
(\operatorname{mg})
, if

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;p,q\in\mathbb{N}:\;M_{p+q}\leq C^{p+q+1}M_{p}M_{q}.

In view of
[
Komatsu73
]
this condition is also known under
(M.2) or stability under ultradifferential operators.
In case
M_{0}=1
, then it suffices to consider
C^{p+q}
in the estimate.

We introduce the value

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}:=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p}/M_{0})^{1/p}=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p},

and recall the fact that for any log-convex sequence one has
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}\in(0,+\infty]
, see e.g.
[
regularnew
, Sect. 2.2, Lemma 2.1]
, and we comment on a minor technical issue in this result:

Remark 4.1
.

By inspecting the proof of
[
regularnew
, Lemma 2.1]
it turns out that one shall assume there
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=C\in(0,+\infty)
and note that the case
C=0
is not excluded by the general assumption
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
. However, when
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=0
then the result is still valid by following the arguments and only considering the upper estimates with
C=0
: So for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
p_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
one obtains
(0<)(M_{p_{\epsilon}+i})^{\frac{1}{p_{\epsilon}+i}}\leq\epsilon^{\frac{i}{p_{\epsilon}+i}}(M_{p_{\epsilon}})^{\frac{1}{p_{\epsilon}+i}}
. Then, as
i\rightarrow+\infty
we infer
\epsilon^{\frac{i}{p_{\epsilon}+i}}(M_{p_{\epsilon}})^{\frac{1}{p_{\epsilon}+i}}\rightarrow\epsilon
and since
\epsilon>0
was chosen arbitrary it follows that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=0
, too.

But, finally, we recall that for any log-convex
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
this case can never occur since
p\mapsto\mu_{p}
is non-decreasing and each
\mu_{p}
is non-negative.

Let
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given, then write

(*)

\mathbf{M}\leq\mathbf{N}
if
M_{p}\leq N_{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

(*)

\mathbf{M}\hypertarget{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
if

\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}<+\infty,

(*)

\mathbf{M}\hypertarget{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
if
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}=0
; i.e. if

\forall\;h>0\;\exists\;C_{h}\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M_{p}\leq C_{h}h^{p}N_{p}.

(*)

\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{N}
are called
equivalent,
formally denoted by
\mathbf{M}\hypertarget{approx}{\approx}\mathbf{N}
, if
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{N}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{M}
.

(*)

Obviously,
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
implies
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
, but
\vartriangleleft
is never reflexive.

Condition moderate growth is obviously preserved under equivalence of sequences. An important example, also for the considerations in this work, are the
Gevrey sequences

\mathbf{G}^{s}:=(p!^{s})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
,
s>0
. Note that
\mathbf{G}^{s}
is equivalent to
\overline{\mathbf{G}}^{s}
with
\overline{\mathbf{G}}^{s}:=(p^{ps})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
by
Stirling’s formula
and each
\mathbf{G}^{s}
is normalized, log-convex and satisfies
(\operatorname{mg})
.

For our purpose it is convenient to consider weight sequences in the following (general) sense:

Definition 4.2
.

\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is called a
weight sequence
if

(\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=)\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty.

If
\mathbf{M}
is a log-convex weight sequence, then in view of
[
regularnew
, Sect. 2.2, Lemma 2.1]
and see also the above Remark
4.1
, we get
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty
, too.

Moreover, occasionally we consider the following (sub)set of weight sequences:

\hypertarget{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}:=\{\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}:\;\mathbf{M}\;\text{is normalized, log-convex},\;\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty\}.

We see that
\mathbf{M}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
if and only if
1=\mu_{0}\leq\mu_{1}\leq\dots
and
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty
(see e.g.
[
compositionpaper
, p. 104]
) and there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathbf{M}
and
\mu=(\mu_{p})_{p}
by taking
M_{p}:=\prod_{i=0}^{p}\mu_{i}
. Obviously,
\mathbf{G}^{s}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
for any
s>0
.

For any given sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{p})_{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
the
upper Matuszewska index

\alpha(\mathbf{a})
is defined by

\alpha(\mathbf{a}):=\inf\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}:\frac{a_{p}}{p^{\alpha}}\;\text{is almost decreasing}\}=\inf\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}:\exists\;H\geq 1\;\forall\;1\leq p\leq q:\;\;\;\frac{a_{q}}{q^{\alpha}}\leq H\frac{a_{p}}{p^{\alpha}}\},

and the
lower Matuszewska index

\beta(\mathbf{a})
by

\beta(\mathbf{a}):=\sup\{\beta\in\mathbb{R}:\frac{a_{p}}{p^{\beta}}\;\text{is almost increasing}\}=\sup\{\beta\in\mathbb{R}:\exists\;H\geq 1\;\forall\;1\leq p\leq q:\;\;\;\frac{a_{p}}{p^{\beta}}\leq H\frac{a_{q}}{q^{\beta}}\}.

\beta(\mathbf{a})>0
implies, in particular,
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}a_{p}=+\infty
. For these definitions we refer to
[
index
, Sect. 3.2]
and the citations there. Crucially we also mention
[
index
, Thm. 3.10]
where the connection
\gamma(\mathbf{M})=\beta(\mu)
is established even for any arbitrary
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Here,
\gamma(\mathbf{M})
denotes the growth index introduced by V. Thilliez in
[
Thilliezdivision
]
. Note that in
[
index
]
concerning the sequence of quotients a different notation has been used: There,
\mathbf{m}
denotes
\mu
, more precisely one has the correspondence
m_{p}=\mu_{p+1}
for all
p
. But this index shift does not effect our considerations; we refer to
[
index
, Rem. 3.8]
and the comments given after
[
index
, Cor. 3.12]
.

4.2.
Associated weight functions

For the following definition we refer to
[
mandelbrojtbook
, Chapitre I]
,
[
Komatsu73
, Sect. 3]
and the more recent work
[
regularnew
]
in which more properties and explanations are summarized and non-standard cases are treated. Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given, then the
associated function

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined as follows:

(4.1)

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t):=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\log\left(\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}}\right),\qquad t\geq 0,

with the conventions
0^{0}:=1
and
\log(0)=-\infty
. This ensures
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(0)=0
and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)\geq 0
for any

since
\frac{t^{0}M_{0}}{M_{0}}=1
for all
t\geq 0
. (
4.1
) corresponds to
[
Komatsu73
,
(3.1)
]
and we immediately have that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is non-decreasing and satisfying
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=+\infty
. However, the fact
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
(well-definedness) is equivalent to assume
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
; see
[
regularnew
, Lemma 2.2]
and compare this with Definition
4.2
. For any
s>0
we have
\operatorname{id}^{1/s}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{s}}
; for a proof we refer e.g. to
[
genLegendreconj
, Ex. 2.9]
.

Let
\mathbf{M}
be a log-convex weight sequence, then one can compute
\mathbf{M}
by involving
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
as follows, see
[
mandelbrojtbook
, Chapitre I, 1.4, 1.8]
,
[
Komatsu73
, Prop. 3.2]
and more recently
[
regularnew
]
:

(4.2)

M_{p}=M_{0}\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{M}(t))},\;\;\;p\in\mathbb{N}.

If
\mathbf{M}
is a weight sequence which is not log-convex, then the right-hand side of (
4.2
) yields
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}
with
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
denoting the
log-convex minorant
of
\mathbf{M}
and then
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
is a log-convex weight sequence.

Moreover, for any log-convex weight sequence let us introduce the
counting function

\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}(t):=|\{p\geq 1:\;\;\;\mu_{p}\leq t\}|,

and recall the following integral representation formula

(4.3)

\omega_{M}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{M}(u)}{u}du=\int_{\mu_{1}}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{M}(u)}{u}du;

see
[
mandelbrojtbook
, 1.8. III]
,
[
Komatsu73
,
(3.11)
]
and
[
regularnew
, Lemma 2.5]
. Consequently,

\forall\;t\in[0,\mu_{1}]:\;\;\;\omega_{M}(t)=0,

and so for any
\mathbf{M}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
the function
\omega_{M}
vanishes on
[0,1]
. Next let us recall
[
regularnew
, Lemma 2.4]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
be a log-convex weight sequence and hence
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty
. Then

(4.4)

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=0,\;\text{for}\;t\in[0,\mu_{1}],\;\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\log\left(\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}}\right)\;\text{for}\;t\in[\mu_{p},\mu_{p+1}],\;p\geq 1.

For concrete applications, when one is (only) interested in the associated function w.l.o.g. one can assume that the defining sequence is log-convex. This fact holds since the geometric regularizing procedure introducing
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
gives, in particular, that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}=\omega_{\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}}
; see again
[
regularnew
]
.

Remark 4.4
.

A log-convex weight sequence with

is a weight sequence in the sense of
[
index

; see
[

. However, if
\mathbf{M}
is a (log-convex) weight sequence with

, then introduce

with

is a (log-convex) weight sequence with

and

holds but even
\widetilde{\mu}_{p}=\mu_{p}
for all

Moreover,
\omega_{\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}}=\omega_{\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}}
since

Finally, when

for all

for all

4.3.
The conjugate sequence
\mathbf{M}^{\ast}

Let us recall some definitions and properties listed and stated in
[
microclasses
, Sect. 2.5 & 2.6]
; see also there for more details and explanations. Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given, then the
conjugate sequence

\mathbf{M}^{\ast}=(M^{\ast}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is defined by

(4.5)

M^{\ast}_{p}:=\frac{p!}{M_{p}}=\frac{1}{m_{p}},\;\;\;p\in\mathbb{N};

i.e.
\mathbf{M}^{\ast}=\frac{1}{\mathbf{m}}
. The corresponding sequence of quotients
\mu^{\ast}=(\mu^{\ast}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is given by
\mu^{\ast}_{p}=\frac{p}{\mu_{p}}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, and set
\mu^{\ast}_{0}:=1
. We gather several immediate properties:

(a)

Set
\mathbf{M}^{\ast\ast}:=(\mathbf{M}^{\ast})^{\ast}
, then
\mathbf{M}^{\ast\ast}=\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{M}^{\ast}\cdot\mathbf{M}\equiv\mathbf{G}^{1}
.

(b)

The following are equivalent:

(*)

\mathbf{M}^{\ast}
is log-convex,

(*)

\mathbf{m}
is
log-concave,
see
[
microclasses
,
(2.5)
]
, i.e.

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;m_{p}^{2}\geq m_{p+1}m_{p-1}.

(*)

p\mapsto\frac{\mu_{p}}{p}
is non-increasing.

If a sequence
\mathbf{N}
is log-concave, then following the arguments in
[
diploma
, Lemma 2.0.4]
we get that
p\mapsto(N_{p}/N_{0})^{1/p}
is non-increasing. Consequently, if
\mathbf{M}^{\ast}
is log-convex and
M_{0}^{\ast}=m_{0}=1
, then
p\mapsto(m_{p})^{1/p}
is non-increasing.

(c)

If both
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{M}^{\ast}
are log-convex, then
\mathbf{M}^{\ast}\cdot\mathbf{M}\equiv\mathbf{G}^{1}
implies
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}\mathbf{M}^{\ast}_{\iota}=+\infty
.

(d)

It is immediate that

\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{G}^{1}\Leftrightarrow\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(m_{p})^{1/p}=0\Leftrightarrow\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M^{\ast}_{p})^{1/p}=\mathbf{M}^{\ast}_{\iota}=+\infty,

and this is equivalent to the fact that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}^{\ast}}
is well-defined; recall
[
regularnew
, Lemma 2.2]
applied to
\mathbf{M}^{\ast}
. Thus, whenever involving
\omega_{\mathbf{M}^{\ast}}
it is natural to assume this growth property on
\mathbf{M}
.

If
\mathbf{M}^{\ast}
is also log-convex, then
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{G}^{1}
if and only if
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu^{\ast}_{p}=+\infty
; see
[
regularnew
, Sect. 2.2]
.

(e)

\mathbf{M}\leq\mathbf{N}
if and only if
\mathbf{N}^{\ast}\leq\mathbf{M}^{\ast}
and
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{N}
if and only if
\mathbf{N}^{\ast}\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}\mathbf{M}^{\ast}
. Thus
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{N}
are equivalent if and only if the conjugate sequences
\mathbf{M}^{\ast}
,
\mathbf{N}^{\ast}
are so.

Moreover,
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{N}
if and only if
\mathbf{m}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{n}
if and only if
\mathbf{N}^{\ast}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{M}^{\ast}
.

(f)

\mathbf{M}^{*}
satisfies
(\operatorname{mg})
if and only if
m_{p}m_{q}\leq C^{p+q+1}m_{p+q}
if and only if
\binom{p+q}{p}M_{p}M_{q}\leq C^{p+q+1}M_{p+q}
for some
C\geq 1
and all
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\mathbf{M}
is log-convex, then Remark
4.4
gives
\binom{p+q}{p}M_{p}M_{q}\leq 2^{p+q}M_{0}M_{p+q}\leq\max\{2,M_{0}\}^{p+q+1}M_{p+q}
for all
p,q
, so the desired estimate holds with
C:=\max\{2,M_{0}\}
and
\mathbf{M}^{*}
satisfies
(\operatorname{mg})
.

Next, we verify the converse of
(f)
and first, motivated by comment
(b)
above, we prove the following technical result.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and recall the notation
m_{p}:=\frac{M_{p}}{p!}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbf{M}
satisfies

(4.6)

\exists\;H\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;(m_{p+1})^{1/(p+1)}\leq H^{1/(p+1)}(m_{p})^{1/p}.

(ii)

\mathbf{M}
satisfies

(4.7)

\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\mu_{p+1}\leq A(M_{p})^{1/p}.

The proof shows the following correspondences: If (
4.6
) holds with some
H
, then in (
4.7
) we choose
A:=2eH
and, conversely, (
4.7
) implies (
4.6
) with
H:=A
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Let
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, then we have

\displaystyle(m_{p+1})^{1/(p+1)}\leq H^{1/(p+1)}(m_{p})^{1/p}\Leftrightarrow(m_{p+1})^{p}\leq H^{p}(m_{p})^{p+1}\Leftrightarrow\left(\frac{M_{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\right)^{p}\leq H^{p}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{p!}\right)^{p+1}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left(\frac{M_{p+1}}{M_{p}}\right)^{p}\leq H^{p}\frac{(p+1)!^{p}}{p!^{p+1}}M_{p}\Leftrightarrow\mu_{p+1}^{p}\leq H^{p}(p+1)^{p}\frac{M_{p}}{p!}\Leftrightarrow\mu_{p+1}\leq H(p+1)(m_{p})^{1/p}.

Moreover,
(p+1)(m_{p})^{1/p}\leq 2p(m_{p})^{1/p}\leq 2e(p!)^{1/p}(m_{p})^{1/p}=2e(M_{p})^{1/p}
is valid by
Stirling’s formula.
Combining both estimates we have verified (
4.7
) with
A:=2eH
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Let
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
be arbitrary, and then

\displaystyle\mu_{p+1}\leq A(M_{p})^{1/p}\Leftrightarrow\frac{(p+1)!m_{p+1}}{p!m_{p}}\leq A(p!m_{p})^{1/p}\Leftrightarrow m_{p+1}\leq A\frac{(p!)^{1/p}}{p+1}(m_{p})^{(p+1)/p}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(m_{p+1})^{1/(p+1)}\leq A^{1/(p+1)}\left(\frac{(p!)^{1/p}}{p+1}\right)^{1/(p+1)}(m_{p})^{1/p}.

Since
\left(\frac{(p!)^{1/p}}{p+1}\right)^{1/(p+1)}\leq 1\Leftrightarrow(p!)^{1/p}\leq p+1
is clear for any
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
the estimate (
4.6
) is shown with
H:=A
as desired.
∎

4.4.
Conjugate associated weight functions

We are interested in the situation when
\omega^{\ast}_{\mathbf{M}}
is well-defined and characterize this in terms of
\mathbf{M}
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathbf{M}
be a log-convex weight sequence. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\omega^{\ast}_{\mathbf{M}}
is well-defined.

(ii)

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
satisfies
(\mathfrak{C}_{2})
; i.e.
t=o(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
equivalently
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\operatorname{id}
.

(iii)

\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(t)=o(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
; i.e.
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\hyperlink{omvartriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}
.

(iv)

\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{G}^{1}
holds; i.e.

(4.10)

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{p!}\right)^{1/p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(m_{p})^{1/p}=0.

The crucial growth condition (
4.10
) is non-standard in the theory of ultradifferentiable function classes and related weighted settings.

Proof.

First,
(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
holds by Lemma
3.1
applied to
\omega^{\ast}_{\mathbf{M}}
and
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
is valid because
\operatorname{id}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}
. This equivalence is well-known; for an explicit proof we refer e.g. to the case
s=1
in
[
genLegendreconj
, Ex. 2.9]
.

And this equivalence implies, in particular, that
\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}
satisfies both
(\omega_{1})
and
(\omega_{6})
since these conditions are obviously true for
\operatorname{id}
.

(iii)\Rightarrow(iv)
: The assumption precisely means that for all
\epsilon>0
there exists some
C_{\epsilon}\geq 1
such that
\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(t)\leq\epsilon\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)+C_{\epsilon}
for all
t\geq 0
. Then let
h>0
small be given and choose
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
minimal such that
h^{-1}\leq 2^{n}
. Since
\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}
has
(\omega_{1})
by iteration we obtain
\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(h^{-1}t)\leq\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(2^{n}t)\leq L\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(t)+L
for some
L\geq 1
depending on
h
and for all
t\geq 0
. We apply the previous relation to
\epsilon:=L^{-1}
and obtain:

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(h^{-1}t)\leq L\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(t)+L\leq\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)+LC_{L^{-1}}+L.

Set
L_{1}:=LC_{L^{-1}}+L
, then by using this estimate and involving (
4.2
) we obtain for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle M_{p}

\displaystyle=M_{0}\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))}\leq M_{0}\exp(L_{1})\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(h^{-1}t))}

\displaystyle=M_{0}\exp(L_{1})h^{p}\sup_{s\geq 0}\frac{s^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(s))}=M_{0}\exp(L_{1})h^{p}G^{1}_{p}.

For the last equality note that
G^{1}_{0}=0!=1
. Summarizing,

\forall\;h>0\;\exists\;L_{1}\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M_{p}\leq M_{0}\exp(L_{1})h^{p}G^{1}_{p},

which gives
\mathbf{M}\hyperlink{mtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\mathbf{G}^{1}
.

(iv)\Rightarrow(iii)
: By assumption

\forall\;h>0\;\exists\;C_{h}\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{p!}\leq C_{h}\frac{M_{0}(ht)^{p}}{M_{p}},

hence by definition
\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(t)\leq\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(ht)+\log(C_{h}/M_{0})
for all
t\geq 0
.

Let now
A\geq 1
large be given and fixed. Choose
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
minimal such that
A\leq 2^{n}
and by iterating
(\omega_{6})
we obtain
A\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(t)\leq 2^{n}\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(t)\leq\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(Ht)+H
for some
H\geq 1
depending on chosen
A
and for all
t\geq 0
. When applying the above relation to
h:=H^{-1}
we arrive at

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;A\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(t)\leq\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(Ht)+H\leq\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)+\log(C_{H^{-1}}/M_{0})+H,

hence
\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(t)}{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)}\leq\frac{1}{A}
. Since
A\geq 1
can be chosen arbitrary large this shows
\omega_{\mathbf{G}^{1}}(t)=o(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))
and are done.
∎

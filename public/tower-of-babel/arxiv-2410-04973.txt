Post-groupoids and quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
Yunhe Sheng, Rong Tang, Chenchang Zhu

Abstract
. The notion of post-groups was introduced by Bai, Guo and the first two authors recently, which are the global objects corresponding to post-Lie algebras, equivalent to skew-left braces, and can be used to construct set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. In this paper, first we introduce the notion of post-groupoids, which consists of a group bundle and some other structures satisfying some compatibility conditions. Post-groupoids reduce to post-groups if the underlying base is one point. An action of a group on a set gives rise to the natural example of post-groupoids. We show that a post-groupoid gives rise to a groupoid (called the Grossman-Larson groupoid), and an action on the original group bundle. Then we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on a groupoid with respect to an action on a group bundle. A relative Rota-Baxter operator naturally gives rise to a post-groupoid and a matched pair of groupoids. One important application of post-groupoids is that they give rise to quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation on the quiver underlying the Grossman-Larson groupoid. We also introduce the notion of a skew-left bracoid, which consists of a group bundle and a groupoid satisfying some compatibility conditions. A skew-left bracoid reduces to a skew-left brace if the underlying base is one point. We give the one-to-one correspondence between post-groupoids and skew-left bracoids. Finally, we show that post-Lie groupoids give rise to post-Lie algebroids via differentiation.

The combinatorial Hopf algebras on decorated trees have played an important role in the Taylor-type expansions of solutions of singular stochastic partial differential equations
[

To better understand them, Bruned and Katsetsiadis have unified the construction of the combinatorial Hopf algebras in the theory of regularity structures as the universal enveloping algebras of post-Lie algebras on decorated trees and multi-indices
[

Set-theoretical and quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation, and related structures

The Yang-Baxter equation grew out of Yang’s study of exact

1.4.
Main results

In this paper, we introduce the notion of post-groupoids, which consists of a group bundle and some other structures satisfying some compatibility conditions, and reduce to post-groups if the underlying base is one point. We show that a post-groupoid naturally gives rise to a groupoid (called the Grossman-Larson groupoid) and an action on the original group bundle. An action of a group on a set naturally gives rise to a post-groupoid, whose Grossman-Larson groupoid is exactly the action groupoid. Moreover, we show that the section space of a post-groupoid is a weak post-group. This implies that the intrinsic structure of the observation in

Note that relative Rota-Baxter operators naturally induce post-structures both in the context of Lie algebras and groups. We introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on a groupoid with respect to an action on a group bundle, and show that a relative Rota-Baxter operator naturally induces a post-groupoid. Conversely, a post-groupoid also gives rise to a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the corresponding Grossman-Larson groupoid. One important property of relative Rota-Baxter operators is that they give rise to matched pairs of groupoids, which leads to the construction of quiver-theoretical solution of the Yang-Baxter equation on the quiver underlying the Grossman-Larson groupoid of a post-groupoid (Theorem
4.10
).

Motivated by the equivalence between post-groups and skew-left braces, we introduce the notion of skew-left bracoids, which consists of a group bundle and a groupoid satisfying some compatibility conditions and reduce to skew-left braces if the underlying base is one point. We show that associated to a post-groupoid, the original group bundle and the Grossman-Larson groupoid form a skew-left bracoid naturally. Conversely, a skew-left bracoid also gives rise to a post-groupoid.

Finally, we consider post-Lie groupoids, that is, post-groupoids in the category of smooth manifolds. Using the fact that the Grossman-Larson groupoid acts on the original group bundle, we show that one can obtain a post-Lie algebroid from a post-Lie groupoid via differentiation (Theorem
6.16

1.5.
Future outlook

To receive the approximate solutions of the differential equations on the manifolds, Munthe-Kaas
[
40
]
introduced the Lie-Butcher series and studied the Runge-Kutta method of the differential equations on the manifolds. A closely related future direction is to study the connection between integrators, as described by the Lie-Butcher series, and free post-Lie algebras whose universal enveloping algebras complete to universal Lie-Butcher series. Let
G
be a Lie group with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
and
\rho:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Diff}(M)
be a transitive action of
G
on a manifold
M
. Consider the differential equation on
M
:
\frac{\mathrm{d}u(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=F(u(t)),\,F\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

Using the MKW post-Lie algebra
C^{\infty}(M,\mathfrak{g})
,
one can rewrite the above differential equation by

\frac{\mathrm{d}u(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=a_{A}\big(f\big(u(t)\big)\big),

for some
\,f\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathfrak{g})
. Note that the MKW post-Lie algebra structure on
C^{\infty}(M,\mathfrak{g})
essentially comes form the MKW post-Lie algebroid
(A=M\times\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\triangleright_{A},a_{A})
(as the section space), which is the differentiation of the MKW post-Lie groupoid
(M\times G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,\cdot,\Phi,\rhd)
. Thus it is natural to explore differential equations on a general post-Lie groupoid
({\mathbb{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,\cdot,\Phi,\rhd)
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}u(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\Phi_{*}(f\big(u(t)\big)),

here
\Phi_{*}
is the anchor map of the post-Lie algebroid
(A\longrightarrow M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\Phi_{*},\triangleright_{A})
, which is the differentiation of the post-Lie groupoid
({\mathbb{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,\cdot,\Phi,\rhd)
and
f\in\Gamma(A)
.
The formal series expansion provided by the Lie-Butcher theory, which reflects the underlying post-Lie algebraic structure, could be investigated in the context of post-Lie algebroids. This extension may reveal new insights into the geometric and algebraic structures governing differential equations in these broader contexts. By exploring how integrators are linked to post-Lie algebroids and post-Lie groupoids via an analogous series expansion, one could potentially develop a unified framework for understanding and solving differential equations on more complex manifolds and spaces. This could significantly advance the field and open up new avenues for applying post-Lie structures in mathematical physics, control theory, numerical analysis, and other more applied settings.

Built on the work
[
31
]
, where Hairer and Singh extended the theory of regularity structures for solving singular stochastic partial differential equations on Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
or torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
to equations on vector bundles over a Riemannian manifold, Singh proposes the following question
[
50
]
:

\bullet

What is the algebraic structure behind the regularity structures on manifolds and vector bundles?

Post-Lie algebras are the algebraic structure behind the regularity structures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Extending regularity structures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to manifolds and vector bundles involving suitable gluing. On the other hand, gluing post-Lie algebras together, one can obtain a post-Lie algebroid
[
41
]
. Thus it is very possible that the answer to Singh’s question above is exactly a post-Lie algebroid or a post-Lie groupoid if we consider global symmetries. Therefore a key future direction is to understand how post-Lie algebroids and post-Lie groupoids manifest in the regular structures on manifolds and vector bundles, and to explicitly construct these structures.

Now a couple of problems which might attract attention in pure maths: in this article, we offer quiver-theoretical solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation using post-groupoids. We demonstrate that the category of post-groupoids is isomorphic to that of braided groupoids. A significant question that arises is how to utilize post-groupoids and their representation theory to classify non-degenerate solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation on quivers, parallel to what Andruskiewitsch did using braided groupoids
[
4
]
. Furthermore, once the representation theory of post-groupoids is considered, a natural inquiry is the Morita equivalence of post-groupoids, as Morita equivalences lead to equivalences of categories of representations. This, in turn, opens up a wide range of further research themes involving other topological invariants, such as cohomology and homotopy groups, and how these invariants interact with the Morita equivalences of post-Lie groupoids.

Lastly, an intriguing challenge for geometers is to achieve a geometric integration of post-Lie algebras and post-Lie algebroids. While this article presents the differentiation from a post-Lie groupoid to a post-Lie algebroid, the reverse process integrating a post-Lie algebra into a post-Lie group remains elusive. Currently, the only known approach is a formal one, which employs a post-Lie version of the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorf formula
[
6
]
. However, finding a geometrically meaningful method for the integration of post-Lie algebras and post-Lie algebroids remains an open problem, posing an exciting direction for future research.

Post-groupoids

In this section, we introduce the notion of a post-groupoid. An action of a group on a set naturally gives rise to a post-groupoid. We show that on the section space and bisection space of a post-groupoid, there are weak post-group and post-group structures respectively. Moreover, a post-groupoid gives rise to a groupoid and an action on the original group bundle.

Now we are ready to introduce the notion of a post-groupoid, which is the main object studied in this paper.

Definition 2.7
.

A
post-(Lie) groupoid
consists of the following data:

•

a (Lie) group bundle
({\mathbb{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,\cdot,\iota,{\mathrm{inv}})
, where
\cdot
is the multiplication;

•

a surjective map (submersion)
\Phi:{\mathbb{G}}\to M
satisfying
\Phi(\iota_{m})=m,
for all
m\in M
;

•

a (smooth) map
\rhd:{\mathbb{G}}_{\Phi}\times_{\pi}{\mathbb{G}}\to{\mathbb{G}}
satisfying
\pi(\gamma\rhd\delta)=\pi(\gamma)
and the left multiplication

L^{\rhd}_{\gamma}:{\mathbb{G}}_{\Phi(\gamma)}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{\pi(\gamma)},\quad L^{\rhd}_{\gamma}\delta=\gamma\rhd\delta\ \ \text{for all }\delta\in{\mathbb{G}}_{\Phi(\gamma)},

is invertible for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{G}}
, where
{\mathbb{G}}_{\Phi}\times_{\pi}{\mathbb{G}}=\{(\gamma,\delta)\in{\mathbb{G}}\times{\mathbb{G}}~\mbox{such that}~\Phi(\gamma)=\pi(\delta)\},

such that for all
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})\in{\mathbb{G}}_{\Phi}\times_{\pi}{\mathbb{G}}_{\Phi}\times_{\pi}{\mathbb{G}}
, and
\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{G}}
satisfying
\pi(\gamma_{2})=\pi(\gamma_{2}^{\prime})
, the following axioms hold:

(i)

\Phi\Big(\gamma_{1}\cdot(\gamma_{1}\rhd\gamma_{2})\Big)=\Phi(\gamma_{2})
,

(ii)

\gamma_{1}\rhd(\gamma_{2}\cdot\gamma_{2}^{\prime})=(\gamma_{1}\rhd\gamma_{2})\cdot(\gamma_{1}\rhd\gamma_{2}^{\prime})
,

(iii)

\gamma_{1}\rhd(\gamma_{2}\rhd\gamma_{3})=\Big(\gamma_{1}\cdot(\gamma_{1}\rhd\gamma_{2})\Big)\rhd\gamma_{3}
,

where
{\mathbb{G}}_{\Phi}\times_{\pi}{\mathbb{G}}_{\Phi}\times_{\pi}{\mathbb{G}}=\{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})\in{\mathbb{G}}\times{\mathbb{G}}\times{\mathbb{G}}~\mbox{such that}~\Phi(\gamma_{1})=\pi(\gamma_{2}),~\Phi(\gamma_{2})=\pi(\gamma_{3})\}.

We will denote a post-(Lie) groupoid by
({\mathbb{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,\cdot,\Phi,\rhd)
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
({\mathbb{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{\mathbb{G}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,\cdot_{\mathbb{G}},\Phi_{\mathbb{G}},\rhd_{\mathbb{G}})
and
(\mathbb{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{\mathbb{H}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,\cdot_{\mathbb{H}},\Phi_{\mathbb{H}},\rhd_{\mathbb{H}})
be post-groupoids. A (base-preserving)
post-groupoid homomorphism
is a map
\Psi:{\mathbb{G}}\to\mathbb{H}
such that

(i)

\pi_{\mathbb{H}}\circ\Psi=\pi_{\mathbb{G}},\quad\Phi_{\mathbb{H}}\circ\Psi=\Phi_{\mathbb{G}}
,

(ii)

\Psi(\gamma\cdot_{\mathbb{G}}\gamma^{\prime})=\Psi(\gamma)\cdot_{\mathbb{H}}\Psi(\gamma^{\prime}),\quad\mbox{for all}~\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{G}}~\mbox{satisfying }~\pi_{\mathbb{G}}(\gamma)=\pi_{\mathbb{G}}(\gamma^{\prime})
,

(iii)

\Psi(\gamma\rhd_{\mathbb{G}}\delta)=\Psi(\gamma)\rhd_{\mathbb{H}}\Psi(\delta),\quad\mbox{for all}~(\gamma,\delta)\in{\mathbb{G}}_{\Phi_{\mathbb{G}}}\times_{\pi_{\mathbb{G}}}{\mathbb{G}}
.

Post-groupoids and their homomorphisms form a category, denoted by
\mathsf{PGpd}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
({\mathbb{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,\cdot,\Phi,\rhd)
be a post-groupoid. Then for all
\gamma\in{\mathbb{G}}
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma\rhd\iota_{\Phi(\gamma)}=\iota_{\pi(\gamma)},\quad\iota_{\pi(\gamma)}\rhd\gamma=\gamma.

Proof.

By Axiom (ii) in Definition
2.7
, we have

\gamma\rhd\iota_{\Phi(\gamma)}=\gamma\rhd(\iota_{\Phi(\gamma)}\cdot\iota_{\Phi(\gamma)})=(\gamma\rhd\iota_{\Phi(\gamma)})\cdot(\gamma\rhd\iota_{\Phi(\gamma)}).

Since
\gamma\rhd\iota_{\Phi(\gamma)}\in{\mathbb{G}}_{\pi(\gamma)}
, it follows that
\gamma\rhd\iota_{\Phi(\gamma)}=\iota_{\pi(\gamma)}
.

By Axiom (iii) in Definition
2.7
, we have

\iota_{\pi(\gamma)}\rhd(\iota_{\pi(\gamma)}\rhd\gamma)=(\iota_{\pi(\gamma)}\cdot(\iota_{\pi(\gamma)}\rhd\iota_{\pi(\gamma)}))\rhd\gamma=\iota_{\pi(\gamma)}\rhd\gamma.

Since
L^{\rhd}_{\iota_{\pi(\gamma)}}
is invertible, it follows that
\iota_{\pi(\gamma)}\rhd\gamma=\gamma.

∎

Denote by

(7)

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
({\mathbb{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,\cdot,\Phi,\rhd)
be a post-groupoid. Then
(\Gamma({\mathbb{G}}),\cdot,\rhd)
is a weak post-group.

Proof.

It is obvious that
(\Gamma({\mathbb{G}}),\cdot)
is a group whose identity is the unit section
\iota:M\to{\mathbb{G}}

For all
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\in\Gamma({\mathbb{G}})
, by Axiom (ii) in Definition
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\rhd(\sigma_{2}\cdot\sigma_{3})(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(m)\rhd(\sigma_{2}\cdot\sigma_{3})(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(m)\rhd(\sigma_{2}(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m)))\cdot\sigma_{3}(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m))))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1}(m)\rhd\sigma_{2}(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m))))\cdot(\sigma_{1}(m)\rhd\sigma_{3}(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m))))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1}\rhd\sigma_{2})(m)\cdot(\sigma_{1}\rhd\sigma_{3})(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big((\sigma_{1}\rhd\sigma_{2})\cdot(\sigma_{1}\rhd\sigma_{3})\Big)(m),

which implies that
\sigma_{1}\rhd(\sigma_{2}\cdot\sigma_{3})=(\sigma_{1}\rhd\sigma_{2})\cdot(\sigma_{1}\rhd\sigma_{3}).

Similarly, by Axiom (i) and (iii) in Definition
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\rhd(\sigma_{2}\rhd\sigma_{3})(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(m)\rhd(\sigma_{2}\rhd\sigma_{3})(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(m)\rhd\Big(\sigma_{2}(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m)))\rhd\sigma_{3}(\Phi(\sigma_{2}(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m)))))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sigma_{1}(m)\cdot(\sigma_{1}(m)\rhd\sigma_{2}(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m))))\Big)\rhd\sigma_{3}(\Phi(\sigma_{2}(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m)))))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sigma_{1}\cdot(\sigma_{1}\rhd\sigma_{2})\Big)(m)\rhd\sigma_{3}(\Phi(\sigma_{2}(\Phi(\sigma_{1}(m)))))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sigma_{1}\cdot(\sigma_{1}\rhd\sigma_{2})\Big)\rhd\sigma_{3}(m),

which implies that
\sigma_{1}\rhd(\sigma_{2}\rhd\sigma_{3})=\Big(\sigma_{1}\cdot(\sigma_{1}\rhd\sigma_{2})\Big)\rhd\sigma_{3}.

Therefore,
(\Gamma({\mathbb{G}}),\cdot,\rhd)
is a weak post-group.

3.

In this section, we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on a groupoid with respect to an action, and establish the connection with post-groupoids. In particular, a relative Rota-Baxter operator naturally induces a post-groupoid. Moreover, a relative Rota-Baxter operator also gives rise to a matched pair of groupoids, which plays important roles in our later study of quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.

5.
Post-groupoids and skew-left bracoids

In this section, we introduce the notion of skew-left bracoids, which are generalizations of skew-left braces. We show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between skew-left bracoids and post-groupoids.

Definition 5.1
.

[
28
,
52
]

A skew-left brace
(G,\star,\cdot)
consists of a group
(G,\cdot)
and a group
(G,\star)
such that

(45)

\displaystyle a\star(b\cdot c)=(a\star b)\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\star c)\ \ \text{for all }a,b,c\in G.

Here
a^{-1}
is the inverse of
a
in the group
(G,\cdot)
.

Definition 5.2
.

A

and a groupoid
(\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 6.92361pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-6.92361pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{{\mathbb{G}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 8.65273pt\raise 6.65971pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise-1.50694pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\alpha=\pi}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 30.92361pt\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.39252pt\raise-6.1111pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise-1.75pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\beta}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 30.92361pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 30.92361pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{M}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces,\star,\iota,\overline{{\mathrm{inv}}})

(46)

\displaystyle\gamma\star(\delta\cdot\delta^{\prime})=(\gamma\star\delta)\cdot{\mathrm{inv}}(\gamma)\cdot(\gamma\star\delta^{\prime}),

bracoid
is a skew-left bracoid in which the group bundle is abelian.

.

Since there is a close relationship between skew-left braces and Hopf-Galois extensions of Hopf algebras

7
]
, we hope that skew-left bracoids will play an important role in the study of Hopf Galois extensions of Hopf algebroids
[

]
in the future.

),

is the inverse of

Remark 5.4
.

Obviously, if
M
reduces to a point, then a skew-left bracoid reduces to a skew-left brace
[
15
,
28
,
52
]
, and a bracoid reduces to a brace
[
16
,
45
,
47
]
. Recently, Martin-Lyons and Truman introduced a new algebraic object which is also called a skew-left bracoid
[
43
, Definition 2.1]
. It is not the same as the one introduced here.

Example 5.5
.

Let
\Phi:M\times G\to M
be a right action of a group
G
on a set
M
. Then the group bundle
{\mathbb{G}}=M\times G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M
, where
\pi
is the projection to

given in Example

form a skew-left bracoid. In fact, for all

satisfying

\displaystyle(m,g)\star\big((n,h)\cdot(n,k)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m,g)\star(n,h\cdot k)=(m,g\cdot h\cdot k),

\displaystyle\big((m,g)\star(n,h)\big)\cdot{\mathrm{inv}}(m,g)\cdot\big((m,g)\star(n,k)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m,g\cdot h)\cdot(m,g^{-1})\cdot(m,g\cdot k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m,(g\cdot h)\cdot g^{-1}\cdot(g\cdot k))=(m,g\cdot h\cdot k),

which implies that the group bundle
{\mathbb{G}}=M\times G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M
and the action groupoid

form a skew-left bracoid.

Note that an action naturally induces a post-groupoid, and the above example can be generalized to arbitrary post-groupoid as the following theorem shows.

6.
Post-Lie groupoids and post-Lie algebroids

In this section, first we recall post-Lie algebroids, and establish the relation between post-Lie algebroids and relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebroids. Then we show that a post-Lie groupoid naturally gives rise to a post-Lie algebroid via differentiation.

6.1.
Post-Lie algebroids and relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebroids

Definition 6.1
.

[
37
]

A
Lie algebroid
structure on a vector bundle
A\longrightarrow M
is
a pair consisting of a Lie algebra structure
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}
on
the section space
\Gamma(A)
and a vector bundle morphism

a_{A}:A\longrightarrow TM

from
A
to the tangent bundle
TM
, called the
anchor
, satisfying the relation

(49)

~[x,fy]_{A}=f[x,y]_{A}+a_{A}(x)(f)y,\quad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A),f\in C^{\infty}(M).

We denote a Lie algebroid by
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},a_{A})
or simply
A

if there is no danger of confusion.

Example 6.2
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
be a left action of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a manifold
M
, that is, a Lie algebra homomorphism from
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
to the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{X}(M),[\cdot,\cdot]_{TM})
of vector fields. Consider the trivial vector bundle
A=M\times\mathfrak{g}
. Then the section space
\Gamma(M\times\mathfrak{g})
is isomorphic to
C^{\infty}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. An element
f\otimes u
will be simply wrote as
fu
. We have a Lie algebroid structure on
A
,
whose anchor
a_{A}:M\times\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,TM
and Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}:\wedge^{2}(C^{\infty}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g})\,\rightarrow\,C^{\infty}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
are given by

(50)

\displaystyle a_{A}(m,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(u)_{m},\quad\forall m\in M,u\in\mathfrak{g},

(51)

\displaystyle{[fu,gv]}_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle fg[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}}+f\phi(u)(g)v-g\phi(v)(f)u,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{g},f,g\in C^{\infty}(M).

This Lie algebroid is called the
action Lie algebroid

[
37
]
.

Definition 6.3
.

[
53
]

A
post-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\triangleright)
consists of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and a binary product
\triangleright:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
such that

(52)

\displaystyle u\triangleright[v,w]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u\triangleright v,w]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[v,u\triangleright w]_{\mathfrak{g}},

(53)

\displaystyle[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}}\triangleright w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{\triangleright}(u,v,w)-a_{\triangleright}(v,u,w),

here
a_{\triangleright}(u,v,w):=u\triangleright(v\triangleright w)-(u\triangleright v)\triangleright w
and
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

Define
L_{\triangleright}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by
L_{\triangleright}(u)(v)=u\triangleright v
. Then by (
52
),
L_{\triangleright}
is a linear map from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
.

As the geometrization of post-Lie algebras, the notion of post-Lie algebroids was given in
[
41
, Definition 2.22]
in the study of geometric numerical analysis. See
[
42
]
for more applications.

Definition 6.4
.

A
post-Lie algebroid
structure on a vector bundle

A\longrightarrow M
is a triple that consists of a
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear Lie
algebra structure
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}
on
\Gamma(A)
, a bilinear operation
\triangleright_{A}:\Gamma(A)\otimes\Gamma(A)\longrightarrow\Gamma(A)
and a
vector bundle morphism
a_{A}:A\longrightarrow TM
, called the
anchor
,
such that
(\Gamma(A),[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\triangleright_{A})
is a post-Lie algebra, and
for all
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
u,v\in\Gamma(A)
, the following
relations are satisfied:

(i)

u\triangleright_{A}(fv)=f(u\triangleright_{A}v)+a_{A}(u)(f)v,

(ii)

(fu)\triangleright_{A}v=f(u\triangleright_{A}v).

We usually denote a post-Lie algebroid by
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\triangleright_{A},a_{A})
. If the Lie algebra structure
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}
in a post-Lie algebroid
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\triangleright_{A},a_{A})
is abelian, then it becomes a left-symmetric algebroid, which is also called a Koszul-Vinberg algebroid. See
[
13
,
35
,
41
]
for more details.

The following result is well known, e.g. see
[
41
, Proposition 2.23]
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\triangleright_{A},a_{A})
be a post-Lie algebroid. Then
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\triangleright_{A}},a_{A})
is a Lie algebroid, called the
Grossman-Larson Lie algebroid
, where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\triangleright_{A}}
is given by

(54)

[x,y]_{\triangleright_{A}}=[x,y]_{A}+x\triangleright_{A}y-y\triangleright_{A}x,\quad\forall x,y\in\Gamma(A).

Example 6.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra and
M
a manifold. Then the section space
\Gamma(M\times\mathfrak{g})
of the trivial bundle
A=M\times\mathfrak{g}
enjoys a
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear Lie algebra structure
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
given by

[fu,gv]_{\mathfrak{g}}=fg[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall f,g\in C^{\infty}(M),~u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
be a left action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
M
. Then one can define
a_{A}:M\times\mathfrak{g}\to TM
by (
50
), and define
\triangleright_{A}:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(A)\longrightarrow\Gamma(A)
by

(55)

(fu)\triangleright_{A}(gv)=f\phi(u)(g)v,\quad\forall f,g\in C^{\infty}(M),~u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
(A=M\times\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\triangleright_{A},a_{A})
is a post-Lie algebroid. See
[

for more details.

Remark 6.7

We call

Definition 6.8

An
action of a post-Lie algebra

\phi:(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\triangleright_{\mathfrak{g}}})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{X}(M).

Let
\phi:(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\triangleright_{\mathfrak{g}}})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{X}(M)

\triangleright_{A}:\otimes^{2}(C^{\infty}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g})\,\rightarrow\,C^{\infty}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
by

(56)

\displaystyle(fu)\triangleright_{A}gv

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle fgu\triangleright_{\mathfrak{g}}v+f\phi(u)(g)v,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{g},f,g\in C^{\infty}(M).

Proposition 6.9
.

[
29
]

With the above notations,
(M\times\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\triangleright_{A},a_{A})
is a post-Lie algebroid, called the
action post-Lie algebroid
of the post-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rhd)
.

Now we introduce the notion of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebroid with respect to an action on a bundle of Lie algebras. Recall that a bundle of Lie algebras is a vector bundle such that on each fibre there is a Lie algebra structure which are not necessarily isomorphic. While for a Lie algebra bundle, the Lie algebra structure on each fibre are isomorphic
[
37
, Definition 3.3.8]
. In the definition of a post-Lie algebroid, the requirement that there is a
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear Lie
algebra structure
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}
on
\Gamma(A)
is equivalent to the fact that
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A})
is a bundle of Lie algebras.

Definition 6.10
.

An
action of a Lie algebroid

(A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},a_{A})
on a bundle of Lie algebras
(E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
is a bilinear map
\psi:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
such that

(i)

\psi(fx,u)=f\psi(x,u),

(ii)

\psi(x,fu)=f\psi(x,u)+a_{A}(x)(f)u,

(iii)

\psi(x,[u,v]_{E})=[\psi(x,u),v]_{E}+[u,\psi(x,v)]_{E},

(iv)

\psi([x,y]_{A},u)=\psi(x,\psi(y,u))-\psi(y,\psi(x,u)),

for all
x,y\in\Gamma(A),~u,v\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Let

E{\longrightarrow}M
be a vector bundle and
\mathfrak{D}E
the associated covariant
differential operator bundle. Any section
\mathfrak{d}\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{D}E)
satisfies

\mathfrak{d}(fu)=f\mathfrak{d}(u)+\mathfrak{a}(\mathfrak{d})(f)u,\quad\forall u\in\Gamma(E),~f\in C^{\infty}(M),

for a bundle map
\mathfrak{a}:\mathfrak{D}E\to TM
, which is called the anchor.
It is well known that
(\mathfrak{D}E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{D}},\mathfrak{a})
is a transitive Lie algebroid, where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{D}}
is the commutator bracket (see
[
37
, Example
3.3.4]
). Let
(E{\longrightarrow}M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
be a Lie algebra bundle. Denote by
\mathrm{Der}(E)
the subalgebroid of
\mathfrak{D}E
, whose section is also a derivation on the Lie algebra
(\Gamma(E),[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
.

The following result is obvious.

Proposition 6.11
.

An action of a Lie algebroid
(A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},a_{A})
on a Lie algebra bundle
(E{\longrightarrow}M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E})
is equivalent to a homomorphism from the Lie algebroid
A
to
\mathrm{Der}(E)
.

Definition 6.12
.

Let
\psi:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
be an action of a Lie algebroid
(A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},a_{A})
on a bundle of Lie algebras
E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M
. A base-preserving bundle map
B:E\to A
is called a
relative Rota-Baxter operator
if

[B(u),B(v)]_{A}=B(\psi(B(u),v)-\psi(B(v),u)+[u,v]_{E}),\quad\forall u,v\in\Gamma(E).

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\triangleright_{A},a_{A})
be a post-Lie algebroid. Then
\triangleright_{A}
gives rise to an action of the Grossman-Larson Lie algebroid
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\triangleright_{A}},a_{A})
on the bundle of Lie algebras
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A})
, and the identity map
{\rm{Id}}:A\to A
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Grossman-Larson Lie algebroid
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\triangleright_{A}},a_{A})
with respect to the action
\triangleright_{A}
on the bundle of Lie algebras
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A})
.

Proof.

Then Axioms (i)-(iv) in Definition
6.10
for
\triangleright_{A}
follows from Axioms (i)-(ii) in Definition
6.4
, (
52
) and (
1
) respectively. Thus
\triangleright_{A}
gives rise to an action.

Finally, by (
54
), the identity map
{\rm{Id}}:A\to A
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Grossman-Larson Lie algebroid
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\triangleright_{A}},a_{A})
with respect to the action
\triangleright_{A}
.
∎

Let
B:E\to A
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebroid
(A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},a_{A})
with respect to an action
\psi:\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
on a bundle of Lie algebras
E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}M

\triangleright_{E}:\Gamma(E)\otimes\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)

(57)

\displaystyle u\triangleright_{E}v=\psi(B(u),v),\quad\forall u,v\in\Gamma(E).

Theorem 6.14
.

With the above notations,
(E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E},\triangleright_{E},a_{A}\circ B)
is a post-Lie algebroid.

Proof.

For all
u,v\in\Gamma(E),~f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, by Axiom (i) in Definition
6.10
and the fact that
B
is a bundle map, we have

(fu)\triangleright_{E}v=\psi(B(fu),v)=\psi(fB(u),v)=f\psi(B(u),v)=f(u\triangleright_{E}v).

By Axiom (ii) in Definition
6.10
, we have

u\triangleright_{E}fv=\psi(B(u),fv)=f\psi(B(u),v)+a_{A}(B(u))(f)v=f(u\triangleright_{E}v)+a_{A}(B(u))(f)v.

By Axiom (iii) in Definition
6.10
, we have

\displaystyle u\triangleright_{E}[v,w]_{E}=\psi(B(u),[v,w]_{E})=[\psi(B(u),v),w]_{E}+[v,\psi(B(u),w)]_{E}=[u\triangleright_{E}v,w]_{E}+[v,u\triangleright_{E}w]_{E}.

Finally, by Axiom (iv) in Definition
6.10
, we have

\displaystyle a_{\triangleright_{E}}(u,v,w)-a_{\triangleright_{E}}(v,u,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\triangleright_{E}(v\triangleright_{E}w)-(u\triangleright_{E}v)\triangleright_{E}w

\displaystyle-v\triangleright_{E}(u\triangleright_{E}w)+(v\triangleright_{E}u)\triangleright_{E}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(B(u),\psi(B(v),w))-\psi(B(v),\psi(B(u),w))

\displaystyle-\psi(B(\psi(B(u),v)),w)+\psi(B(\psi(B(v),u)),w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi([B(u),B(v)]_{A}-B(\psi(B(u),v))+B(\psi(B(v),u)),w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(B[u,v]_{E},w)=[u,v]_{E}\triangleright_{E}w.

Therefore,
(E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E},\triangleright_{E},a_{A}\circ B)
is a post-Lie algebroid.
∎

.

Let

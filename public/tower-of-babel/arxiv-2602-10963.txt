Lie Group Variational Integrator for the Geometrically Exact Rod with Circular Cross-Section Incorporating Cross-Sectional Deformation Journal: Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering
Srishti Siddharth, Vivek Natarajan, Ravi N. Banavar

Abstract
In this paper, we derive the continuous space-time equations of motion of a three-dimensional geometrically exact rod, or the Cosserat rod, incorporating planar cross-sectional deformation. We then adopt the Lie group variational integrator technique to obtain a discrete model of the rod incorporating both rotational motion and cross-sectional deformation as well. The resulting discrete model possesses several desirable features: it ensures volume conservation of the discrete elements by considering cross-sectional deformation through a local dilatation factor, it demonstrates the beneficial properties associated with the variational integrator technique, such as the preservation of the rotational configuration, and energy conservation with a bounded error. An exhaustive set of numerical results under various initial conditions of the rod demonstrates the efficacy of the model in replicating the physics of the system.



1
Introduction

In many engineering and scientific applications, geometrically exact rods are used to obtain accurate models for highly deformable slender rods. Such flexible structures find applications in continuum robots
14
;
9
, DNA strands experiencing various mechanical forces
13
and flexible microrobots for endovascular surgery
2
. The geometrically exact rod
1
or the Cosserat rod provides a mathematically elegant and high-fidelity model for such slender rods undergoing large deformations, unlike Euler-Bernoulli and Timoshenko beam theories, which account for small deformations.

The variational formulation of the geometrically exact rod and its numerical aspects were presented in the extensive work of Simo et al.
29
;
27
;
28
. The essential features of the discretisation scheme proposed in these papers are the implicit algorithms to discretise the rotational dynamics along with the strain discretisation rules which preserve their properties after
discretisation. The book by Antman
1
presents the theory of planar and three-dimensional geometrically exact rods. The modern treatment of geometrically exact rods is directed towards algorithm design and implementation based on the special Cosserat rod theory, which describes the motion of the rod using the position of its centerline and the orientation of the rigid cross-sections attached at each material point on the centerline. There are various Finite element method (FEM) approaches which are used to obtain such numerical models as well. The work in
5
focuses on obtaining numerical models which account for finite bending and torsion. Other FEM based numerical integrators are provided in
6
;
30
;
36
.

The Cosserat rod approach has also been adopted by the graphics and mechanics community. In this setting, work by Bergou et al.
3
and Gazzola et al.
25
proposed
“discrete elastic rods”
, which describe the motion of a discrete rod by the position of the nodes and orientation of the discrete edges connecting the nodes. The spatial discretisation employed combines finite difference approximations for various field quantities with discrete averaging of the rod’s rotational strains. For temporal discretisation, the authors utilise a symplectic, second-order accurate Verlet integration scheme. Through the application of discrete differential geometry (DDG), the authors demonstrate various interesting physical phenomena, such as the formation of knots, plectonemes and solenoids.

The soft robotics community has proposed various numerical models based on the Cosserat rod theory. One of the prevalent numerical approaches in this field is the shooting-based approach. The nonlinear partial differential equations describing the rod dynamics are semidiscretised in time using the Backward Difference Formula of order
\alpha
(BDF-
\alpha
), followed by shooting method-based iterations to solve the semidiscretised ODEs with boundary conditions
18
;
33
. Most of the numerical techniques described above are based on absolute parameterisation of the Cosserat rod. Recently, Boyer et al.
4
have proposed a strain-based parametrisation to describe the motion of continuum robots which results in the statics and dynamics having a similar structure to that of rigid link robots.

The nonlinear PDEs describing the dynamics of the rod evolve on the manifold
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathrm{SO}(3)}
, thus requiring numerical models which preserve the geometry of this deformation.
Geometric numerical integration techniques preserve mechanical properties of a system such
as energy, momentum and configuration geometry, apart from being numerically efficient
15
. Variational integrators are a class of geometric numerical integrators which are based on the theory of discrete mechanics
26
. The development of discrete mechanics and variational integrators presented by Marsden and West
26
is widely used to obtain numerically efficient algorithms for a large class of mechanical systems. The structure-preserving algorithms have the advantage of providing numerically accurate and fast simulations with large time steps, along with stable long-time behaviour and energy conservation with a bounded error. The variational integrators for mechanical systems evolving on Lie groups are called Lie group variational integrators, abbreviated as LGVI. The LGVI technique has been extensively applied to the study of dynamics of various rigid body systems such as the 3-D pendulum
23
, quadrotor
12
, spacecraft
22
, etc. Recently, mechanical systems discretised by the variational integrator scheme have become a widespread option for obtaining discrete optimal control laws
19
;
20
;
24
as the discrete mechanics and optimal control (DMOC) approach produces discrete optimal solutions which inherently possess characteristic properties of the mechanical system.

A Lie group variational integrator for the three-dimensional exact beam dynamics was introduced by Demoures et al. (2015)
10
. The discrete rod dynamics thus obtained evolve on the manifold
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathrm{SO}(3)}
at the discrete level and exhibit long-time conservation of the approximate energy as well as objectivity of the discrete strain measures. A multisymplectic Lie group variational integrator for the Cosserat rod is provided by Demoures et al. (2016)
11
and Chen et al. (2022)
7
. A Lie group variational integrator which employs quaternions to describe the orientation of the rod is presented by Hante et al.
16
and Tumiotto and Arnold
34
.

The theory and numerical models of the geometrically exact rod described above assume that the cross-sections remain rigid during deformation. There are a few studies present in the literature which explore the cross-sectional deformation in detail. In
21
, the authors have accounted for this effect by assuming that the directors do not remain orthonormal. In addition to considering the effect of Poisson’s ratio to account for cross-sectional inflation due to stretching, they include additional strain measures to capture the cross-sectional deformation, thereby increasing the order of the ODE which describes the statics of the rod. In a similar development in
32
, the authors have included an inflation ratio (which results in an additional strain variable) in the deformation map and derive the equations of motion using the deformation gradient approach. In
35
, the author has considered quaternions to represent the orientation of the cross-sections. However, the quaternions are not restricted to be of unit norm, resulting in a rod model which accounts for inflation of the cross-section.

Contributions of the paper:
The contributions of this paper are twofold. The first contribution is the derivation of the three-dimensional equations of motion of the Cosserat rod incorporating planar cross-sectional deformation under the assumption that circular cross-sections of the cylindrical rod remain circular after deformation. This assumption is also made in Gazzola et al.
25
, one of the articles we compare our results to. The authors of
25
derive the equations of motion using a Newtonian approach, while we adopt a Lagrangian approach, incorporating the effects of cross-sectional deformation on the geometrical properties of the rod.
The second contribution of the paper is the development and implementation of the discrete rod model using the Lie group variational integrator scheme. We build on the discrete model of the rod derived by Demoures et al.
10
by incorporating the new feature - cross-sectional deformation of the rod using a local dilatation factor. We present three numerical experiments to demonstrate the long-time approximate energy conservation and structure-preserving properties of the discrete model, while also ensuring the conservation of volume of the discrete elements of the rod.

Outline of the paper:
In Section

Lie group preliminaries

In this section, we recall definitions from the theory of Lie groups.

Let
{{I_{3}}}
denote the identity element in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}
. The three-dimensional special orthogonal group
{\mathrm{SO}(3)}
is the set

\{{{R}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}|{{R}}^{T}{{R}}={{R}}{{R}}^{T}={{I_{3}}},\text{det}{{R}}=1\}
with matrix multiplication as the group operation.
The associated Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{so}(3)
, is the vector space of
3\times 3
real skew-symmetric matrices. For
{{\omega}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, we define
\widehat{{{\omega}}}\in\mathfrak{so}(3)
via the
“hat”
map as follows:

{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\ni{{{\omega}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{{\omega}}_{1}\\
{{\omega}}_{2}\\
{{\omega}}_{3}\end{bmatrix}\mapsto\widehat{{{\omega}}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-{{{\omega}}}_{3}&{{{\omega}}}_{2}\\
{{{\omega}}}_{3}&0&-{{{\omega}}}_{1}\\
-{{{\omega}}}_{2}&{{{\omega}}}_{1}&0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathfrak{so}(3),

(1)

and the inverse of the hat map is the
“vee”
map
(\cdot)^{\vee}
from
\mathfrak{so}(3)
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
so that

(\widehat{{{\omega}}})^{\vee}={{{\omega}}}.

(2)

The antisymmetric map
(\cdot)^{A}:{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}\to\mathfrak{so}(3)
maps a matrix
M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}
to its skew-symmetric part, i.e.

(M)^{A}=\frac{M-M^{T}}{2}.

(3)

The Cayley map
\textrm{cay}:\mathfrak{so}(3)\to{\mathrm{SO}(3)}
is defined as

\textrm{cay}(\widehat{M})=\Bigl(I_{3}-\frac{\widehat{M}}{2}\Bigr)^{-1}\Bigl(I_{3}+\frac{\widehat{M}}{2}\Bigr)

(4)

for
\widehat{M}\in\mathfrak{so}(3)
and its inverse is
\textrm{cay}^{-1}:{\mathrm{SO}(3)}\to\mathfrak{so}(3)
defined as

\text{cay}^{-1}({{R}})=2({{R}}-I_{3})({{R}}+I_{3})^{-1}

(5)

for
R\in{\mathrm{SO}(3)}
, see
17
for details.

3
Description of the three-dimensional Cosserat rod incorporating cross-sectional deformation

3.1
Kinematics of the rod

The geometrically exact rod or the Cosserat rod takes into account the following deformations: stretching, shearing, bending and twisting. The configuration of the rod is completely described at any given time instant by the position of the material points lying on the centerline and the orientation of the cross-sections attached to these points. In this paper, we take the initial reference configuration of the rod to be the one in which the rod is straight (zero curvature) and is undeformed. Let
L
be the length of the undeformed rod and let
S\in[0,L]
be the arc-length parameter which identifies the material points on the centerline of the rod. The position of the material point at
S
at any time instant
t
, in the inertial orthonormal frame
\{{e}_{1},{e}_{2},{e}_{3}\}
, is denoted by
{{x}}(S,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Here
{e}_{1},{e}_{2},{e}_{3}
are the standard unit vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. In the reference configuration, the rod is supposed to be located along the
{e}_{3}
axis. In this work, we assume that the cross-section of the Cosserat rod remains circular at all time instants. Let
d_{1}^{S}
and
d_{2}^{S}
be two vectors parallel to
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, respectively, lying in the cross-section at the material point
S
in the reference configuration. The orientation of this cross-section at a time instant
t
is determined by the directions of
d_{1}^{S}
and
d_{2}^{S}
at that time instant, which are denoted using the unit vectors
d_{1}(S,t)
and
d_{2}(S,t)
, respectively. We assume that
d_{1}(S,t)
and
d_{2}(S,t)
are orthogonal to each other at all time instants. The orthonormal moving frame (also known as body-fixed or material frame) at each
S
is given by
\{{d}_{1}(S,t),{d}_{2}(S,t),{d}_{3}(S,t)\}
. Let
{{R}}(S,t)\in{\mathrm{SO}(3)}
represent the orientation of the cross-section at the material point
S
. Then we have the relation

{d}_{i}(S,t)={{R}}(S,t){e}_{i},\quad i=1,2,3.

(6)

Figure
1
shows the Cosserat rod and the various frames used to describe the pose of the rod.

The configuration space of the rod is defined as

Q=\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,L],{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathrm{SO}(3)})

(7)

with appropriate boundary conditions. The configuration of the material point at
S
at time instant
t
is given by

p(S,t)=({{x}}(S,t),{{R}}(S,t)).

(8)

In addition to the configuration variables described above, the kinematics of the rod is described by its linear and angular velocities. The linear velocity of the rod, denoted as
{{{\gamma}}}(S,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is given by

{{{\gamma}}}(S,t)=\frac{\partial{{x}}(S,t)}{\partial t}.

(9)

In this paper,
\dot{(\cdot)}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\cdot)
will be used to denote the time derivative. The angular velocity of the rod, denoted as
{{\omega}}(S,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is given by

{{\omega}}(S,t)=({{R}}^{T}(S,t)\dot{{{R}}}(S,t))^{\vee}

(10)

which can be rewritten using the
“hat”
notation as

\dot{{{R}}}(S,t)={{R}}(S,t)\widehat{{{\omega}}}(S,t).

(11)

After considering the kinematics of the rod, the equations of motion can be derived either by applying Newton’s second law of motion to an infinitesimal element or by finding the Euler-Lagrange equations associated with its Lagrangian. We will adopt the latter method since it provides the formulation of the equations of motion based on kinetic and strain potential energies of the rod, which will be useful to formulate the discrete dynamics using the Lie Group Variational Integrator
10
.

3.2
Planar cross-sectional deformation

In the special theory of Cosserat rod presented in Demoures et al. (2015)
10
, the cross-sections are assumed to be rigid for all kinds of deformations. This assumption permits limited deformations of the rod, although in many modern-day applications, deformations such as axial stretch, bending and twist change the cross-sectional area.
In this section, we look at the planar deformation of circular cross-section of the rod due to extension, compression and bending. This will allow us to capture many interesting physical phenomena of the rod. In this section, we derive the equations of motion from a Lagrangian viewpoint, incorporating the cross-sectional deformation introduced in Gazzola et al. (2018)
25
.

Assumption 1

The main assumption made to arrive at the new model is that the circular cross-section remains circular after deformation. This assumption will allow us to model deformation up to
30\%
of axial strain, as explained in
25
. It should be noted that we have not introduced any additional strain variables to incorporate cross-sectional deformation, unlike other studies found in the literature.

Assumption 2

The material of the rod is assumed to be homogeneous, incompressible, isotropic, hyperelastic and is described with a linear stress-strain relationship.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
be the current arc-length parameter due to the extension or compression of the rod. It is defined by the map

S\mapsto s(S)=\int_{0}^{S}\left\lVert\frac{\partial{{x}}(u,t)}{\partial u}\right\rVert\,du,

(12)

where
u
is a dummy variable of integration
31
. Then, the local dilatation factor
e(S,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
is given as

e(S,t)=\frac{ds}{dS}=\left\lVert\frac{\partial{{x}}(S,t)}{\partial S}\right\rVert.

(13)

Here,
0<e(S,t)<1
and
e(S,t)>1
represent compression and elongation of the infinitesimal rod element,
dS
, respectively, while
e(S,t)=1,\forall S\in[0,L]
represents an unstretched rod. Since the mass of the infinitesimal element of the rod is conserved, therefore, we have

\rho{\bar{A}(S)}dS=\rho A(S)ds\implies A(S)=\frac{{\bar{A}(S)}}{e(S,t)},

(14)

where
\bar{A}(S)
and
A(S)
are the cross-sectional area at
S
in the initial and current configurations, respectively, as shown in Figure
2
and
\rho
is the density of the rod. (
14
) incorporates cross-sectional deformation while enforcing volume conservation of the infinitesimal element of the rod.

:

3.3
Lagrangian of the rod

In this section, we will describe the Lagrangian of the Cosserat rod which accounts for cross-sectional deformation.

The Lagrangian of the rod,
\mathcal{L}:TQ\to{\mathbb{R}}
, consists of the kinetic and potential energies which are described as follows:

1.

Kinetic energy: The total kinetic energy of the rod is the sum of translational energy, denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{l}
and rotational energy, denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{r}
.

(a)

Translational kinetic energy:

The translational kinetic energy of an infinitesimal element
dS
of the rod of mass
dm(t)
at time
t
is given as

\displaystyle\begin{split}d\mathcal{T}_{l}(t)=dm(t)\dot{{{x}}}^{T}(S,t)\dot{{{x}}}(S,t)&=dm(0)\dot{{{x}}}^{T}(S,t)\dot{{{x}}}(S,t)\\
&=\rho\bar{A}(S)\dot{{{x}}}^{T}(S,t)\dot{{{x}}}(S,t)dS\end{split}

(15)

due to the conservation of mass given in (
14
). Thus, the translational kinetic energy is independent of
e(S,t)
and continues to be the same as for the standard Cosserat rod
10
, which is given as

\mathcal{T}_{l}(t)=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{1}{2}\rho\bar{A}(S)\dot{{{x}}}^{T}(S,t)\dot{{{x}}}(S,t)\,dS.

(16)

(b)

Rotational kinetic energy:

The rotational kinetic energy of the element
dS
of the rod is given as

d\mathcal{T}_{r}(t)=\frac{1}{2}{{\omega}}^{T}(S,t){J}(S,t){{\omega}}(S,t),

(17)

where
{J}(S,t)
is the mass moment of inertia per unit length of the rod at
S
.

For a slender rod, the mass moment of inertia is expressed in terms of the second moment of inertia. The second moment of inertia of the rod is given by
{{\mathcal{I}}}(S)=\text{diag}(\mathcal{I}_{1}(S),\mathcal{I}_{2}(S),\mathcal{I}_{3}(S))
. For a circular cross-section, we have

\mathcal{I}_{1}(S)=\mathcal{I}_{2}(S)=\frac{A^{2}(S)}{4\pi},\mathcal{I}_{3}(S)=\frac{A^{2}(S)}{2\pi}.

(18)

Due to the effect of the dilatation factor on the cross-sectional area in (
14
), the second moment of inertia is given by

{{\mathcal{I}}}(S,t)=\frac{\overline{{{\mathcal{I}}}}(S)}{e^{2}(S,t)}.

(19)

Note that
\overline{(.)}
represents various physical quantities in the reference configuration.

Thus, the mass moment of inertia of the deformed element at
S
can be written as

{J}(S,t)=\rho\frac{\overline{{{\mathcal{I}}}}(S)}{e^{2}(S,t)}e(S,t)dS=\rho\frac{\overline{{{\mathcal{I}}}}(S)}{e(S,t)}dS

(20)

and the rotational kinetic energy is given by

\mathcal{T}_{r}(t)=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{1}{2}\rho{{{\omega}}^{T}(S,t)}\frac{\overline{{{\mathcal{I}}}}(S)}{e(S,t)}{{\omega}}(S,t)\,dS.

(21)

2.

Strain potential energy:

(a)

Linear strain potential energy:
The stretching and shearing strains of the rod are defined as

{V}(S,t)-{e}_{3},\quad\text{where}\quad{V}(S,t):={{R}}^{T}(S,t)\frac{\partial{{x}}(S,t)}{\partial S}

(22)

and

{V}(S,t)-{e}_{3}=\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon_{s_{1}}(S,t)\\
\epsilon_{s_{2}}(S,t)\\
\epsilon_{a}(S,t)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}(S,t)\\
v_{2}(S,t)\\
v_{3}(S,t)-1\end{bmatrix}.

(23)

\epsilon_{s_{1}}=v_{1}(S,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\epsilon_{s_{2}}=v_{2}(S,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
represent shearing strains along the
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
axes and
\epsilon_{a}=v_{3}(S,t)-1\in{\mathbb{R}}
represents the axial strain along the
e_{3}
axis. In this paper,
\frac{\partial}{\partial S}(\cdot)
denotes the spatial derivative.

Assuming a linear constitutive relationship, the linear stresses are given as

\sigma_{s_{1}}(S,t)=Gv_{1}(S,t),

(24)

\sigma_{s_{2}}(S,t)=Gv_{2}(S,t),

(25)

\sigma_{a}(S,t)=E(v_{3}(S,t)-1),

(26)

where
\sigma_{s_{1}},\sigma_{s_{2}},\sigma_{a}\in{\mathbb{R}}
are the shear and axial stresses,
E
is the Young’s modulus and
G
is the shear modulus of the rod. The relation between the two moduli is given as

G=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)},

(27)

where
\nu
is the Poisson’s ratio of the rod. The stretching and shearing stiffness of the rod is
\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}(S)}{e(S,t)}
, where
\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}(S)=\text{diag}(G\bar{A}(S),G\bar{A}(S)

,E\bar{A}(S))
. This is due to the cross-sectional area deformation defined in (
14
).

Assumption 3

We assume that the material-specific parameters
E,G
and
\nu
are not affected by
e(S,t)
and are assumed to be constants of the rod as it undergoes deformation.

Accounting for the cross-sectional deformation in (
14
), the forces generated due to the shear and axial stresses are given by

\displaystyle F_{s_{1}}(S,t)

\displaystyle=f_{1}(\epsilon_{s_{1}}(S,t))=\sigma_{s_{1}}(S,t)A(S)

(28)

\displaystyle=\frac{G{\bar{A}(S)}}{e(S,t)}\epsilon_{s_{1}}(S,t)=\frac{G{\bar{A}(S)}}{e(S,t)}v_{1}(S,t),

\displaystyle F_{s_{2}}(S,t)

\displaystyle=f_{2}(\epsilon_{s_{2}}(S,t))=\sigma_{s_{2}}(S,t)A(S)

(29)

\displaystyle=\frac{G{\bar{A}(S)}}{e(S,t)}\epsilon_{s_{2}}(S,t)=\frac{G{\bar{A}(S)}}{e(S,t)}v_{2}(S,t),

\displaystyle F_{a}(S,t)

\displaystyle=f_{3}(\epsilon_{a}(S,t))=\sigma_{a}(S,t)A(S)

(30)

\displaystyle=\frac{E{\bar{A}(S)}}{e(S,t)}\epsilon_{a}(S,t)=\frac{E{\bar{A}(S)}}{e(S,t)}(v_{3}(S,t)-1),

where
F_{s_{1}},F_{s_{2}},F_{a}
are the shear and axial forces.

The work done on an infinitesimal element of the rod
dS
to generate the linear strains is given as

\displaystyle dW_{\text{lin}}

\displaystyle=dW_{s_{1}}+dW_{s_{2}}+dW_{a}

(31)

\displaystyle=\bigl[\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{s_{1}}}f_{1}(\xi_{s_{1}})\,d\xi_{s_{1}}+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{s_{2}}}f_{2}(\xi_{s_{2}})\,d\xi_{s_{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{a}}f_{3}(\xi_{a})\,d\xi_{a}\bigr]\,dS,

where
\xi_{s_{1}},\xi_{s_{2}},\xi_{a}
are the dummy variables for shearing and stretching strains.
The total work done by the forces
F_{s_{1}},F_{s_{2}},F_{a}
, denoted by
W_{lin}
is stored as the linear strain potential energy
\mathcal{U}_{lin}
. Thus,

\displaystyle W_{lin}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}_{lin}=\int_{0}^{L}dW_{lin}

(32)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\bigl[\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{s_{1}}}f_{1}(\xi_{s_{1}})\,d\xi_{s_{1}}+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{s_{2}}}f_{2}(\xi_{s_{2}})\,d\xi_{s_{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{a}}f_{3}(\xi_{a})\,d\xi_{a}\bigr]dS.

(b)

Rotational strain potential energy: The bending and twisting strains of the rod, denoted by
{{\kappa}}(s,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
are defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{{\kappa}}(s,t)&=\Bigl(({{R}}(s,t))^{T}\frac{\partial{{R}}(s,t)}{\partial s}\Bigr)^{\vee}\\
&=\frac{1}{e(S,t)}\Bigl(({{R}}(S,t))^{T}\frac{\partial{{R}}(S,t)}{\partial S}\Bigr)^{\vee}\\
&=\frac{{{\kappa}}(S,t)}{e(S,t)},\end{split}

(33)

where
ds=e(S,t)dS
from (
13
) and the angular strain
{{\kappa}}(S,t)=\begin{bmatrix}{{\kappa}}_{1}(S,t)&{{\kappa}}_{2}(S,t)&{{\kappa}}_{3}(S,t)\end{bmatrix}^{T}

\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is expressed in terms of the original arc-length parameter
S
.
Accounting for the cross-sectional deformation in (
14
), the moments generated due to bending and twisting stresses are given as

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(S,t)=f_{4}({{\kappa}}_{1}(S,t))=\frac{E\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{1}(S)}{e^{3}(S,t)}{{\kappa}}_{1}(S,t),

(34)

\displaystyle\tau_{2}(S,t)=f_{5}({{\kappa}}_{2}(S,t))=\frac{E\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{2}(S)}{e^{3}(S,t)}{{\kappa}}_{2}(S,t),

(35)

\displaystyle\tau_{3}(S,t)=f_{6}({{\kappa}}_{3}(S,t))=\frac{G\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{3}(S)}{e^{3}(S,t)}{{\kappa}}_{3}(S,t),

(36)

where
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
are the stresses generated due to the bending strains about
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
axes, and
\tau_{3}
is the stress generated due to twisting about the
e_{3}
axis.
The angular stiffness of the rod is
\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{2}}}(S)}{e^{2}(S,t)}
where
\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{2}}}(S)=\text{diag}(E\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{1}(S),E\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{2}(S),G\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{3}(S))
. This is due to the effect of cross-sectional deformation on the second moment of inertia given in (
19
).

The work done on an infinitesimal element of the rod
dS
to generate the angular strains is given as

\displaystyle dW_{\text{ang}}

\displaystyle=dW_{1}+dW_{2}+dW_{3}

(37)

\displaystyle=\bigl[\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{1}}f_{4}(\xi_{4})\,d\xi_{4}+\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{2}}f_{5}(\xi_{5})\,d\xi_{5}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{3}}f_{6}(\xi_{6})\,d\xi_{6}\bigr]\,dS,

\xi_{4},\xi_{5},\xi_{6}
are the dummy variables for bending and twisting strains.

The total work done by the moments
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3}
, denoted by

is stored as the rotational strain potential energy
\mathcal{U}_{ang}
. Thus,

\displaystyle W_{ang}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}_{ang}=\int_{0}^{L}dW_{ang}

(38)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\bigl[\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{1}}f_{4}(\xi_{4})\,d\xi_{4}+\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{2}}f_{5}(\xi_{5})\,d\xi_{5}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{3}}f_{6}(\xi_{6})\,d\xi_{6}\bigr]\,dS.

The total strain potential energy of the rod, obtained by adding (

) and (

\mathcal{U}(t)=\mathcal{U}_{lin}+\mathcal{U}_{ang}.

(39)

Remark 1

The linear and rotational strain potential energies given in (

if the cross-sectional deformation is not considered.

3.4
The governing equations of motion

The equations of motion of the rod for

are derived by computing the variation of the action integral given by

\mathcal{S}=\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{T}_{l}(t)+\mathcal{T}_{r}(t)-\mathcal{U}(t)\,dt,

(41)

where
\mathcal{T}_{l}(t)
,
\mathcal{T}_{r}(t)
and

The equations of motion of the rod incorporating the cross-sectional deformation in the presence of non-conservative, distributed external force
\mathcal{F}(S,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and moment
\tau(S,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

Thus, the translational equation of motion is given as

\displaystyle\rho\bar{A}(S)\ddot{{{x}}}(S,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial S}\Bigl({{R}}(S,t)\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}(S)}{e(S,t)}{({V}(S,t)-{e}_{3})}\Bigr)+\mathcal{F}(S,t)

(42)

and the rotational equation of motion is given as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \rho\frac{\overline{{{\mathcal{I}}}}(S)}{e(S,t)}\dot{{{\omega}}}(S,t)&=\frac{\partial}{\partial S}\Bigl(\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{2}}}(S){{\kappa}}(S,t)}{e^{3}(S,t)}\Bigr)\\
&+\frac{{{\kappa}}(S,t)\times\bigl(\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{2}}}(S){{\kappa}}(S,t)\bigr)}{e^{3}(S,t)}\\
&\quad+\Bigl({{R}}^{T}(S,t)\frac{\partial{{x}}(S,t)}{\partial S}\Bigr)\times\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}(S)}{e(S,t)}{({V}(S,t)-{e}_{3})}\\
&\quad+\rho\frac{\overline{{{\mathcal{I}}}}(S)}{e(S,t)}{{\omega}}(S,t)\times{{\omega}}(S,t)\\
&\quad+\rho\frac{\overline{{{\mathcal{I}}}}(S)}{e^{2}(S,t)}{{\omega}}(S,t)\dot{e}(S,t)+\tau(S,t)\end{aligned}

(43)

\forall S\in[0,L]
.

(44)

\end{split}

(45)

\left.\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{2}}}(S)}{e^{3}(S,t)}{{\kappa}}(S,t)\right|_{S=L}=\tau(L,t)

(46)

\forall t\geq 0

Remark 3

The translational equation of motion provided in

4
Lie group variational integrator for the rod incorporating cross-sectional deformation

In this section, we proceed to derive the discrete model of the Cosserat rod incorporating cross-sectional deformation as explained in Section
3
. We will refer to the Cosserat rod described in
10
as the standard rod model and the Cosserat rod described in Section
3
as the modified rod model. In
26
, the fundamental ideas of the variational integrator technique for a general configuration manifold have been presented. In this section, we will look at the various aspects of the Lie group variational integrator (LGVI) model of the 3-D Cosserat rod described in Section
3
. The discrete model for the standard Cosserat rod based on the LGVI technique was introduced in
10
. Our objective is to derive a discrete rod model which also accounts for cross-sectional deformation. Thus, our work combines the modified rod model and a structure-preserving discretisation scheme. Based on the kinetic and strain potential energies, the LGVI model of the modified rod can be obtained in three steps: (1) formulating the spatially discretised Lagrangian of the rod, (2) finding the approximate linear and angular velocities of the rod by selecting two nearby configurations in the configuration space and obtaining the discrete-time approximation of the spatially discretised Lagrangian computed in (1), thus deriving the completely discretised Lagrangian, and (3) computing the variation of the discrete action sum. The discretisation approach is explained in the following sections.

4.1
Spatial discretisation

Let us consider that the rod has been spatially discretised into
{\mathcal{K}}
intervals with a uniform step size denoted by
l_{{\mathcal{K}}}
. Thus, for a rod of undeformed length
L
, we have

l_{{\mathcal{K}}}=\frac{L}{{\mathcal{K}}}.

(53)

Let us denote each spatial node with
{q}\in[0,{\mathcal{K}}]
such that
{q}=0
and
{q}={\mathcal{K}}
denote the base and the tip of the rod, respectively. We will denote the position and orientation of the cross-section at each spatial node by
{{x}}_{q}
and
{{R}}_{q}
, respectively.

Let us consider the interpolation of the configuration variables in the interval
{\mathcal{P}}=({q},{q}+1)
, given in
10
, as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{{R}}(s)|_{{\mathcal{P}}}:={{R}}_{q}\exp{\Bigl(\frac{S}{l_{\mathcal{K}}}\widehat{\psi}_{q}\Bigr)},\quad{{x}}(S)|_{{\mathcal{P}}}:={{x}}_{q}+\frac{S}{l_{\mathcal{K}}}\Delta{{x}}_{q},\end{split}

(54)

where
S\in[0,l_{\mathcal{K}}]
,
\Delta{{x}}_{q}={{x}}_{{q}+1}-{{x}}_{q}
and
\exp(\widehat{\psi}_{q})={{{R}}}^{T}_{q}{{R}}_{{q}+1}
. These interpolations were introduced in
8
to maintain the strain objectivity of the discrete strains.

Translational kinetic energy

Recall from Section
3.3
that the translational kinetic energy of the rod at time
t
is given as

\mathcal{T}_{l}=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{1}{2}\rho{\bar{A}(S)}\dot{{{x}}}^{T}(S,t)\dot{{{x}}}(S,t)\,dS=\sum_{{q}=0}^{{q}={\mathcal{K}}}\int_{{q}}^{{q}+1}\frac{1}{2}\rho{\bar{A}(S)}\dot{{{x}}}^{T}(S,t)\dot{{{x}}}(S,t)\,dS.

(55)

By approximating the integral on the right-hand side of the above equation by the trapezoidal rule, we get

\int_{{q}}^{{q}+1}\frac{1}{2}\rho{\bar{A}(S)}\dot{{{x}}}^{T}(S,t)\dot{{{x}}}(S,t)\,dS\approx\frac{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}{4}\rho\bar{A}[||\dot{{{x}}}_{{q}}||^{2}+||\dot{{{x}}}_{{q}+1}||^{2}]=\mathcal{T}_{ld},

(56)

where
\mathcal{T}_{ld}
is the discrete translational kinetic energy.

Rotational kinetic energy

The rotational kinetic energy given in (
21
), can be approximated in the spatial interval
[{q},{q}+1]
as

\int_{{q}}^{{q}+1}\frac{1}{2}{{\omega}}^{T}(S,t)\frac{\bar{{{J}}}}{e(S,t)}{{\omega}}(S,t)\,dS\approx\frac{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}{4}\left[{{\omega}}^{T}_{q}\frac{\bar{{{J}}}_{q}}{e_{q}}{{\omega}}_{q}+{{\omega}}^{T}_{{q}+1}\frac{\bar{{{J}}}_{{q}+1}}{e_{{q}+1}}{{\omega}}_{{q}+1}\right]=\mathcal{T}_{rd},

(57)

where
\mathcal{T}_{rd}
is the discrete rotational kinetic energy,
\bar{{{J}}}_{q}=\rho\bar{{{\mathcal{I}}}}_{q}
is the mass moment of inertia per unit length of the rod, and
e_{q}\in{\mathbb{R}}
defined as

e_{q}=\frac{||{{x}}_{{q}+1}-{{x}}_{q}||}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}\quad\forall\quad{q}=0,1,...,{\mathcal{K}}-1,

(58)

is the discrete local dilatation factor obtained by spatially discretising (
13
).

Potential energy stored due to shearing and stretching strains

The shearing and stretching strains in the spatial interval
[{q},{q}+1]
can be approximated as

{V}_{q}-{e_{3}}={{R}}^{T}_{q}\frac{\Delta{{x}}_{q}}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}-{e_{3}},

(59)

where
\Delta{{x}}_{q}={{x}}_{{q}+1}-{{x}}_{q}
. The linear strain potential energy, given in (
32
) , can be approximated by the trapezoidal rule as

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{sd}

\displaystyle=\int_{{q}}^{{q}+1}\bigl[\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{s_{1}}}f_{1}(\xi_{s_{1}})\,d\xi_{s_{1}}+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{s_{2}}}f_{2}(\xi_{s_{2}})\,d\xi_{s_{2}}+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{a}}f_{3}(\xi_{a})\,d\xi_{a}\bigr]\,dS

(60)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}{2}\big[\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{s_{1,{q}}}}f_{1}(\xi_{s_{1}})\,d\xi_{s_{1}}+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{s_{2,{q}}}}f_{2}(\xi_{s_{2}})\,d\xi_{s_{2}}+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{a,{q}}}f_{3}(\xi_{a})\,d\xi_{a}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{s_{1,{q}+1}}}f_{1}(\xi_{s_{1}})\,d\xi_{s_{1}}+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{s_{2,{q}+1}}}f_{2}(\xi_{s_{2}})\,d\xi_{s_{2}}+\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{a,{q}+1}}f_{3}(\xi_{a})\,d\xi_{a}\bigr],

where
\epsilon_{s_{1,{q}}},\epsilon_{s_{2,{q}}},\epsilon_{s_{a,{q}}}
are the shearing and stretching discrete strains at node
{q}
respectively.

Potential energy stored due to bending and twisting strains

The bending and twisting strains in the spatial interval
[{q},{q}+1]
can be approximated as

{{\kappa}}_{{q}}=\frac{{\psi}_{q}}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},

(61)

where
\widehat{{\psi}}_{{q}}=\exp^{-1}{({{R}}^{T}_{{q}}{{R}}_{{q}+1})}
is computed using the inverse Cayley transform given in (
5
). Thus, the rotational potential energy, given in (
38
) can be approximated by the rectangular rule as

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{bd}

\displaystyle=\int_{{q}}^{{q}+1}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{1}}f_{4}(\xi_{4})\,d\xi_{4}+\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{2}}f_{5}(\xi_{5})\,d\xi_{5}+\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{3}}f_{6}(\xi_{6})\,d\xi_{6}\Bigr]\,dS

(62)

\displaystyle\approx{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{1,q}}f_{4}(\xi_{4})\,d\xi_{4}+\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{2,q}}f_{5}(\xi_{5})\,d\xi_{5}+\int_{0}^{{{\kappa}}_{3,q}}f_{6}(\xi_{6})\,d\xi_{6}\Bigr],

where
{{\kappa}}_{{1,{q}}},{{\kappa}}_{{2,{q}}},{{\kappa}}_{{a,{q}}}
are the bending and twisting discrete strains at node
{q}
, respectively.

Therefore, the spatially discrete potential energy is given as

\mathcal{U}_{d}=\mathcal{U}_{sd}+\mathcal{U}_{bd}.

(63)

On combining (
56
), (
57
) and (
63
), the spatially discrete Lagrangian is defined as

L_{sd}:=\sum_{{q}=0}^{{\mathcal{K}}}\mathcal{T}_{ld}+\mathcal{T}_{rd}-\mathcal{U}_{d}.

(64)

4.2
Temporal discretisation

Let us consider that the time interval
[0,T]
is divided into
N
intervals of uniform size
h
. Thus, we have

h=\frac{T}{N}.

(65)

We will denote each time instant with
k\in[0,N]
such that
k=0
and
k=N
denote the initial and final time, respectively. We will denote the position and orientation of the cross-section at each spatial node
{q}
and time instant
k
by
x^{k}_{q}
and
R^{k}_{q}
, respectively.

Discrete linear velocity of the rod

The linear velocity is approximated by considering two nearby rod configurations in time. Thus, the discrete linear velocity is given as

{{\gamma}}(S,t)=\dot{{{x}}}(S,t)\approx\frac{{{x}}^{k+1}(S)-{{x}}^{k}(S)}{h}.

(66)

On combining the discrete-space and discrete-time quantities, the completely discretised linear velocity is given as

{{\gamma}}_{q}^{k}=\Delta{{{x}}}^{k}_{q}=\frac{{{x}}^{k+1}_{q}-{{x}}^{k}_{q}}{h}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

(67)

for all
{q}=0,1,...,{\mathcal{K}}
.

Discrete angular velocity of the rod

The discrete angular velocity has an interesting expression which arises due to the approximation of
\dot{{{R}}}
. The angular velocity at node
{q}
can be approximated as

\widehat{{{\omega}}}_{q}={{{R}}^{T}_{q}}{\dot{{{R}}}_{q}}\approx{({{R}}^{k}_{q})^{T}}\frac{{{R}}^{k+1}_{q}-{{R}}^{k}_{q}}{h}=\frac{{F}^{k}_{q}-I_{3}}{h},

(68)

where
F^{k}_{q}\in{\mathrm{SO}(3)}
is the rotation update defined as

{F}^{k}_{q}=({{R}}^{k}_{q})^{T}{{{R}}^{k+1}_{q}}.

(69)

Thus, the angular velocity of the cross-section at spatial node
{q}
and time instant
k
is given by

\widehat{{{\omega}}}^{k}_{q}\approx\frac{{F}^{k}_{q}-I_{3}}{h},

(70)

which can be interpreted as the first order approximation of
F_{q}^{k}=\exp{(\widehat{{{\omega}}_{q}^{k}}h)}
.
After a few matrix manipulations, the discrete rotational kinetic energy in a time interval
[k,k+1]
, spatially discretised in (
57
), becomes

\mathcal{T}^{k}_{rd}=\frac{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}{h\overline{e}^{k}_{q}}\text{tr}[(I_{3}-{F}^{k}_{q}){J}_{d}],

(71)

where
\text{tr}[\cdot]
is the trace operator,
{J}_{d}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}
is the non-standard inertia matrix given as

{J}_{d}=\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}[{J}]-{J},

(72)

\overline{e}^{k}_{q}:=\bigl(\frac{1}{e^{k}_{q}}+\frac{1}{e^{k}_{{q}+1}}\bigr)^{-1}

(73)

\forall{q}=1,...,{\mathcal{K}}-1
,

\overline{e}^{k}_{0}={e^{k}_{0}}

(74)

for the base node
{q}=0
and

\overline{e}^{k}_{\mathcal{K}}={e^{k}_{{\mathcal{K}}-1}}

(75)

for the tip node
{q}={\mathcal{K}}
.

Remark 5

The time discretisation of strain potential energies in (
60
) and (
62
) is performed using the rectangular rule of approximation. It is simply the evaluation of each of the terms at the time instant
k
and the total strain potential energy is denoted by
\mathcal{U}^{k}_{d}
. This is because the potential energy terms do not contain time derivatives and hence do not require discrete-time velocities of the rod.

Remark 6

It should be noted that the temporal discretisation performed in this section preserves symplecticity and satisfies the discrete Noether’s theorem of the discrete-time Lagrangian flow, as discussed in
10
.

4.3
Discrete equations of motion of the rod

The discrete Lagrangian of the rod can be written by combining the kinetic and potential energy terms obtained after spatial and time discretisation. The discrete action sum hence obtained is given as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{d}&=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}{4h}\rho\bar{A}||\Delta{{x}}^{k}_{0}||^{2}+\frac{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}{2he^{k}_{0}}\text{tr}[(I_{3}-{F}^{k}_{0}){J}_{d}]+\\
&\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}{4h}\rho\bar{A}||\Delta{{x}}^{k}_{{\mathcal{K}}}||^{2}+\frac{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}{2he^{k}_{{\mathcal{K}}-1}}\text{tr}[(I_{3}-{F}^{k}_{{\mathcal{K}}}){J}_{d}]+\\
&\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{{q}=1}^{{\mathcal{K}}-1}\frac{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}{2h}\rho\bar{A}||\Delta{{x}}^{k}_{q}||^{2}+\frac{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}{2h\overline{e}^{k}_{q}}\text{tr}[(I_{3}-{F}^{k}_{q}){J}_{d}]-\sum_{k=0}^{N}\sum_{{q}=1}^{{\mathcal{K}}}h\mathcal{U}^{k}_{d},\end{split}

(76)

where
\mathcal{U}^{k}_{d}
is defined in Remark
5
.

The discrete equations of motion of the rod with cross-sectional deformation can be obtained by computing the variation of the discrete action sum given in (
76
). The discrete translational equations of motion of the rod are given as

\boxed{\frac{\rho\bar{A}}{h^{2}}\bigl({{x}}^{k+1}_{q}-2{{x}}^{k}_{q}+{{x}}^{k-1}_{q}\bigr)=-\mathcal{V}^{k}_{q}+\mathcal{F}^{k}_{q},}

(77)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{V}^{k}_{0}:=\left.\frac{2}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2}{{R}}_{0}\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}}{e_{0}}({{R}}^{T}_{0}\frac{\Delta{{x}}_{0}}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}-{e}_{3})-\frac{1}{2}{{R}}_{1}\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}}{e_{0}}({{R}}^{T}_{1}\frac{\Delta{{x}}_{0}}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}-{e}_{3})\Bigr)\right|_{k}\end{split}

(78)

for the base node
{q}=0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{V}^{k}_{{\mathcal{K}}}:=&\frac{2}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}{{R}}_{{\mathcal{K}-1}}\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}}{e_{\mathcal{K}-1}}({{R}}^{T}_{{\mathcal{K}-1}}\frac{\Delta{{x}}_{\mathcal{K}-1}}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}-{e}_{3})\\
&+\left.\frac{1}{2}{{R}}_{{\mathcal{K}}}\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}}{e_{\mathcal{K}-1}}({{R}}^{T}_{{\mathcal{K}}}\frac{\Delta{{x}}_{{\mathcal{K}}-1}}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}-{e}_{3})\Bigr)\right|_{k}\end{split}

(79)

for the tip node
{q}={\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{V}^{k}_{q}&:=\frac{1}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2}{{R}}_{q}\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}}{e_{q}}({{R}}^{T}_{{q}}\frac{\Delta{{x}}_{q}}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}-{e}_{3})+\frac{1}{2}{{R}}_{{q}-1}\frac{\bar{{\mathcal{C}_{1}}}}{e_{{q}-1}}({{R}}^{T}_{{q}-1}\frac{\Delta{{x}}_{{q}-1}}{l_{{\mathcal{K}}}}-{e}_{3})\\

(80)

for interior nodes
{q}=\{1,2,...,{\mathcal{K}}-1\}
and
\mathcal{F}^{k}_{q}
is the force acting on node
{q}
at time instant
k

The discrete rotational equations of motion of the rod are given as

5
Numerical results

In this section, we present three numerical experiments to validate the LGVI model of the three-dimensional Cosserat rod which includes cross-sectional deformation given by (
77
) and (
81
). The three numerical tests are:

1.

The flying beam problem, which is a benchmark experiment and will be used to assess the energy-conserving behaviour of the rod as well as the effect of the dilatation factor on its dynamics.

2.

Pure stretching of a rod, which will be primarily used to investigate the impact of the dilatation factor on the length of the rod and the volume conservation of its spatial elements.

3.

The general case of bending and stretching of the rod to examine the effects of incorporating the cross-sectional deformation on the factors described in the previous cases.

6
Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, we derived the continuous space-time equations of motion of the three-dimensional Cosserat rod which incorporated planar cross-sectional deformation via the local dilatation factor
e(S,t)
. Furthermore, the discrete model of the Cosserat rod was developed using the Lie group variational integrator technique and three numerical tests were presented which demonstrated the properties of the proposed discrete model: volume conservation of the discrete elements of the rod and energy conservation with a bounded error. The convergence analysis in space and time demonstrated numerical stability of the proposed integrator and energy conservation for coarser grids in space and time. Future work includes the design of model-based control using the proposed discrete equations of motion for soft robots modelled using the Cosserat rod.

Total positivity and dependence of order statistics
Enrique de Amo, José Juan Quesada-Molina and Manuel Úbeda-Flores \corrauth

Abstract
In this comprehensive study, we delve deeply into the concept of multivariate total positivity, defining it in accordance with a direction. We rigorously explore numerous salient properties, shedding light on the nuances that characterize this notion. Furthermore, our research extends to establishing distinct forms of dependence among the order statistics of a sample from a distribution function. Our analysis aims to provide a nuanced understanding of the interrelationships within multivariate total positivity and its implications for statistical analysis and probability theory.

Introduction

There are different ways to discuss dependence relations among random variables and, as Jogdeo
[
1
]
notes: “…this is one of the most widely studied objects in probability and statistics.”

Recent literature extensively studied the concept of dependence in bivariate and multivariate settings. These concepts are particularly relevant in fields such as economics, insurance, finance, risk management, applied probability and statistics (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
). Several definitions of positive dependence have been introduced to model the association between large values of a component of a multivariate random vector and large values of the other components —further discussion of most of the dependence concepts that we present in this paper can be consulted in
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
— including multivariate total positivity of order 2 (MTP
2
) —also known as positive likelihood ratio dependence for the bivariate case. This concept has garnered significant attention, particularly in Gaussian models, owing to its intuitive description that highlights the non-negativity of all correlations and partial correlations. In finance, psychometrics, machine learning, medical statistics and phylogenetics, MTP
2
models have been shown to outperform state-of-the-art methods; moreover, there is a fundamental connection between the MTP
2
constraint and the assumption of sparsity —see, e.g.,
[
10
,
11
]
.

However, not all dependence concepts, especially in the multivariate case, encompass all dependencies between random variables. In particular, the above mentioned MTP
2
concept is defined for the case when the random vector is
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})
, but not when at least one of the variables is negative: For instance, a random vector of type
(-X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})
. Thus, we intend to extend the concept to random variables that follow, for example, the guidelines of the random vector presented.

On the other hand, the
i
-th order statistic of a sample from a distribution functions is equal to its
i
-th smallest value. Together with rank statistics, order statistics are among the most fundamental tools in non-parametric statistics and inference
[
12
]
. We establish certain types of dependence —both for some of those previously defined and some well-known that we will recall— for order statistics.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide the major definitions. In Section
3
, we concentrate on the notion of multivariate total positivity according to a direction and provide several properties. In Section
4
, we establish certain types of dependence for order statistics. Finally, Section
5
is devoted to conclusions.

2
Preliminaries

Let
d\geq 2
be a natural number. Let
(\Omega,\cal{F},\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$})
be a probability space, where
\Omega
is a nonempty set,
\cal{F}
is a
\sigma
-algebra of subsets of
\Omega
, and
{\rm{I\!P}}
is a probability measure on
\cal{F}
, and let
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})
be a vector of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d. for short) random variables from
\Omega
to
\overline{\mbox{${\rm{I\!R}}$}}^{d}=[-\infty,\infty]^{d}
.

A function
f\colon\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\longrightarrow[0,+\infty[
is
totally positive of order two
—denoted by TP
2
— if

f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})f(x,y)\geq f(x^{\prime},y)f(x,y^{\prime})

whenever
x\leq x^{\prime}
and
y\leq y^{\prime}

[
5
]
.

Two random variables
X
and
Y
are said to be totally positive of order two —or
positively likelihood ratio dependent
, denoted by
{\rm PLRD}(X,Y)
— if the density function of the pair
\left(X,Y\right)
is TP
2
.

In the multivariate case, a generalization of total positivity of order two can be defined
[
13
]
. A function
f\colon\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\longrightarrow[0,+\infty[
is
multivariate totally positive of order two
—denoted by MTP
2
— if

f({\bf x}\vee{\bf y})f({\bf x}\wedge{\bf y})\geq f({\bf x})f({\bf y})

for all
{\bf x}=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\right),{\bf y}=\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\right)\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where

\displaystyle{\bf x}\vee{\bf y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\max\left(x_{1},y_{1}\right),\ldots,\max\left(x_{d},y_{d}\right)\right),

\displaystyle{\bf x}\wedge{\bf y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\min\left(x_{1},y_{1}\right),\ldots,\min\left(x_{d},y_{d}\right)\right).

A random vector
{\bf X}=\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\right)
is said to be multivariate totally positive of order two —or
multivariate positively likelihood ratio dependent
— if its joint
d
-dimensional density
f
is MTP
2
. See
[
14
]
for examples and
[
15
,
16
]
for applications.

We note that by reversing the sense of the above inequalities, we have the corresponding negative concepts, obtaining similar results which we omit here.

In the next sections, when we talk about these —or other— dependence concepts, we will refer to random variables or to their joint density functions.

3
Multivariate total positivity according to a direction

In this section, we provide a simple generalization of the TP
2
concept in higher dimensions in a directional sense, in which a pair of the components of the random vector can be negative. After giving some simple properties of this concept, we provide a “natural” generalization of the MTP
2
concept according to a direction —i.e., the components of the random vector can take negative values— and show that the two newly defined concepts are equivalent. Additional characterizations and properties are given throughout the section.

The next definition generalizes the concept of TP
2
to
d
-dimensions according to a direction.

Definition 1
.

Let
{\bf X}
be a
d
-dimensional random vector with joint density
f
, and let
\alpha=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\left|\alpha_{i}\right|=1
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
. The function
f
is said to be multivariate totally positive of order two in pairs according to the direction
\alpha
—denoted by MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
— if

\displaystyle f\left(x_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{i}x_{i},\ldots,\alpha_{j}x_{j},\ldots,x_{d}\right)f\left(x_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{\prime},\ldots,\alpha_{j}x_{j}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{d}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\geq f\left(x_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{\prime},\ldots,\alpha_{j}x_{j},\ldots,x_{d}\right)f\left(x_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{i}x_{i},\ldots,\alpha_{j}x_{j}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{d}\right)

(3.1)

for all
\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},x_{i}^{\prime},x_{j}^{\prime}\right)\in{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}^{d+2}
such that
x_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}
and
x_{j}\leq x_{j}^{\prime}
and any election of
(i,j)
.

Note that if a random pair
(X_{1},X_{2})
is TP
2
then it is MTPP
{}_{2}(1,1)
.

The dependency in MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
, whichever is
\alpha
, is positive since the fact that a
d
-dimensional random vector
{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
indicates that large values of the random variables
X_{j}
, with
j\in J
, correspond with small values of the variables
X_{j}
, with
j\in I\backslash J
, where
I=\{1,\ldots,d\}
and
J=\left\{i\in I:\alpha_{i}>0\right\}
. In addition, there is also association between small values of the variables
X_{j}
, with
j\in J
, and large values of
X_{j}
, with
j\in I\backslash J
, as the following result shows.

Proposition 1
.

A
d
-dimensional random vector
{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
if, and only if, it is MTPP
{}_{2}(-\alpha)
.

Proof.

Assume
{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
, then (
3.1
) holds. By making the changes
x_{i}=-y_{i}^{\prime}
,
x_{j}=-y_{j}^{\prime}
,
x_{i}^{\prime}=-y_{i}
and
x_{j}^{\prime}=-y_{j}
—note that
y_{i}\leq y_{i}^{\prime}
and
y_{j}\leq y_{j}^{\prime}
— we easily obtain that
{\bf X}
is also MTPP
{}_{2}(-\alpha)
.

The converse follows the same steps.
∎

The proof of the next property concerning the MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
concept —in which
1
denotes the vector
(1,1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
— is simple, and we omit it.

Proposition 2
.

A
d
-dimensional random vector
{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
if, and only if,
\alpha{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}({\bf 1})
.

In the following definition we provide a generalization of the MTP
2
-concept, similarly to the generalization defined for the orthant dependence given in
[
17
]
, and where
\alpha{\bf z}
will denote the
d
-dimensional vector
\left(\alpha_{1}z_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}z_{d}\right)
.

Definition 2
.

Let
{\bf X}
be a
d
-dimensional random vector with joint density function
f
, and
\alpha\in{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
, with
\left|\alpha_{i}\right|=1
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
. Then
{\bf X}
is said to be multivariate totally positive of order two according to the direction
\alpha
—denoted by MTP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
— if

f(\alpha({\bf x}\vee{\bf y}))f(\alpha({\bf x}\wedge{\bf y)})\geq f(\alpha{\bf x})f(\alpha{\bf y})

for all
{\bf x}=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\right),{\bf y}=\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\right)\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

In this section, we have defined two multivariate generalizations of the TP
2
-concept. Now, we prove that both concepts, MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
and MTP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
, are equivalent, as the next result shows.

Theorem 3
.

A
d
-dimensional random vector
{\bf X}
is MTP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
if, and only if, it is MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

Consider the vectors

{\bf x}=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{j},\ldots,x_{d}\right)\quad{\rm and}\quad{\bf y}=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\ldots,x_{j}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{d}\right),

with
x_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}
and
x_{j}\leq x_{j}^{\prime}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\alpha\left({\bf x}\wedge{\bf y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\alpha_{1}x_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{i}x_{i},\ldots,\alpha_{j}x_{j},\ldots,\alpha_{d}x_{d}\right)

\displaystyle\alpha\left({\bf x}\vee{\bf y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\alpha_{1}x_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{\prime},\ldots,\alpha_{j}x_{j}^{\prime},\ldots,\alpha_{d}x_{d}\right).

If
{\bf X}
is MTP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
, then we immediately obtain that
{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
.

Conversely, for
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
suppose, without loss of generality,
x_{l}\geq y_{l}
for
l=1,\ldots,r
and
x_{l}\leq y_{l}
for
l=r+1,\ldots,d
. Let
s=d-r
. For each
i
, with
0\leq i\leq r
, and for each
j
, with
0\leq j\leq s
, we define the vectors

\displaystyle{\bf z}^{i,j}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\vee y_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\vee y_{i},x_{i+1}\wedge y_{i+1},\ldots,x_{r}\wedge y_{r},x_{r+1}\vee y_{r+1},\ldots,x_{r+j}\vee y_{r+j},

\displaystyle x_{r+j+1}\wedge y_{r+j+1},\ldots,x_{d}\wedge y_{d})

(note
{\bf z}^{r,0}={\bf x}
,
{\bf z}^{0,s}={\bf y}
,
{\bf z}^{0,0}={\bf x}\wedge{\bf y}
and
{\bf z}^{r,s}={\bf x}\vee{\bf y}
). Then we have
{\bf z}^{i+1,j}\wedge{\bf z}^{i,j+1}={\bf z}^{i,j}
and
{\bf z}^{i+1,j}\vee{\bf z}^{i,j+1}={\bf z}^{i+1,j+1}
. Since
{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
, if
h
is the joint density function of
{\bf X}
, we obtain

h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{i,j}\right)h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{i+1,j+1}\right)\geq h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{i+1,j}\right)h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{i,j+1}\right)

for any
(i,j)
such that
0\leq i\leq r-1
and
0\leq j\leq s-1
; therefore,

\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{i,j}\right)h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{i+1,j+1}\right)\geq\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{i+1,j}\right)h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{i,j+1}\right),

whence

h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{0,0}\right)h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{r,s}\right)\geq h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{r,0}\right)h\left(\alpha{\bf z}^{0,s}\right),

i.e.,
{\bf X}
is MTP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
, which completes the proof.
∎

Another different characterization of the MTP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
-concept is given in the following result.

Proposition 4
.

A
d
-dimensional random vector
{\bf X}
with joint density function
h
is MTP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
if, and only if, for any pair of vectors
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
such that
x_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
, and any
1\leq j\leq d-1
, we have

h\left(\alpha{\bf x}\right)h\left(\alpha{\bf x^{\prime}}\right)\geq h\left(\alpha{\bf x}^{\prime j}\right)h\left(\alpha{\bf x}^{j}\right),

(3.2)

where
{\bf x}^{\prime j}=\left(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{j}^{\prime},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d}\right)
and
{\bf x}^{j}=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},x_{j+1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{d}^{\prime}\right)
.

Proof.

Assume
{\bf X}
is MTP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
. Let
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
such that
x_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
. For any
1\leq j\leq d-1
consider the vectors
{\bf x}^{\prime j}
and
{\bf x}^{j}
. Then, it is clear
{\bf x}^{\prime j}\wedge{\bf x}^{j}={\bf x}
and
{\bf x}^{\prime j}\vee{\bf x}^{j}={\bf x}^{\prime}
, whence we obtain (
3.2
).

Conversely, given
i,j\in I=\{1,\ldots,d\}
, let
k=\max\{i,j\}
. Then, for any
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
such that
x_{l}\leq x_{l}^{\prime}
for all
l=1,\ldots,d
, from (
3.2
) we have

h\left(\alpha{\bf x}\right)h\left(\alpha{\bf x^{\prime}}\right)\geq h\left(\alpha{\bf x}^{\prime k-1}\right)h\left(\alpha{\bf x}^{k-1}\right).

Taking
x_{l}=x_{l}^{\prime}
for all
l\in I\backslash\{i,j\}
we easily obtain that
{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
, and therefore it is MTP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
, completing the proof.
∎

For the next result, in which we provide another characterization of the MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
-concept of a random vector, we need some additional notation: Given a
d
-dimensional random vector
{\bf X}
with joint distribution function
H
, let
\overline{H}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\right)
denote the probability that
{\bf X}
is greater than
{\bf x}
—also known as the
joint survival function of

H
—, i.e.,

{\overline{H}}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\right)=\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{d}\left(X_{i}>x_{i}\right)\right].

Proposition 5
.

A
d
-dimensional random vector
{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
if, and only if,
{\overline{H}}
is MTPP
{}_{2}({\bf 1})
.

Proof.

Assume
{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)
. Since

{\overline{H}}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\right)=\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}\right)\right],

we have the following chain of equalities:

\displaystyle\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}\right)\right]\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}^{\prime}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}\right),\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}^{\prime}\right)\right]\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}^{\prime}\right),\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\left(x_{i}<\alpha_{i}X_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}\right),\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}\right)\right]\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}^{\prime}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\left(x_{i}<\alpha_{i}X_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}\right),\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}\right)\right]\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}^{\prime}\right),\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{d}\left(x_{i}<\alpha_{i}X_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}\right)\right]\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}^{\prime}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\left(x_{i}<\alpha_{i}X_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}\right),\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}\right)\right]\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\left(\alpha_{i}X_{i}>x_{i}^{\prime}\right),\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{d}\left(x_{i}<\alpha_{i}X_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}\right)\right].

(3.3)

Now, we prove that the last expression in (
3.3
) is non-negative. For that, note that, from Proposition
2
, we have that the random vector
\alpha{\bf X}
is MTPP
{}_{2}({\bf 1})
, and from Proposition
4
we have

g\left({\bf y}\right)g\left({\bf y^{\prime}}\right)-g\left({\bf y}^{\prime j}\right)g\left({\bf y}^{j}\right)\geq 0,

(3.4)

where
g
is the joint density function of
\alpha{\bf X}
. Integrating in both sides of (
3.4
), with
x_{i}<y_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}<y_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
, we have

\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots\int_{x_{d}}^{x_{d}^{\prime}}\int_{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{x_{d}^{\prime}}^{\infty}\left(g\left({\bf y}\right)g\left({\bf y^{\prime}}\right)-g\left({\bf y}^{\prime j}\right)g\left({\bf y}^{j}\right)\right){\rm d}{\bf y}{\rm d}{\bf y}^{\prime}\geq 0,

so that the expression in (
3.3
) is non-negative.

The converse follows the same steps, and the proof is completed.
∎

Next we show that any subset of a MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)

Proposition 6
.

If
{\bf X}=\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\right)
is a MTPP
{}_{2}(\alpha)

\left(X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{k}}\right)

{}_{2}(\alpha^{*})

Let
d\geq 3
be a natural number,
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
\alpha^{(i)}=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{i-1},\alpha_{i+1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\right)

{\bf X}^{(i)}
the
(d-1)

g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})

\alpha{\bf X}
(respectively,
\alpha^{(i)}{\bf X}^{(i)}

\alpha^{(i)}{\bf X}^{(i)}
is MTPP
{}_{2}\left({\bf 1}_{d-1}\right)

{\bf 1}_{d-1}
denotes the unitary
d-1

Therefore, we need to prove

g^{(i)}\left(x_{j},x_{k},x^{(j,k)}\right)g^{(i)}\left(x_{j}^{\prime},x_{k}^{\prime},x^{(j,k)}\right)\geq g^{(i)}\left(x_{j}^{\prime},x_{k},x^{(j,k)}\right)g^{(i)}\left(x_{j},x_{k}^{\prime},x^{(j,k)}\right)

We have

\displaystyle g^{(i)}\left(x_{j},x_{k},x^{(j,k)}\right)g^{(i)}\left(x_{j}^{\prime},x_{k}^{\prime},x^{(j,k)}\right)-g^{(i)}\left(x_{j}^{\prime},x_{k},x^{(j,k)}\right)g^{(i)}\left(x_{j},x_{k}^{\prime},x^{(j,k)}\right)

\displaystyle\,\,=\int\!\!\int\frac{g\left(x_{j}^{\prime},x_{i}^{\prime},x_{k}^{\prime},x^{(j,i,k)}\right)}{g\left(x_{j}^{\prime},x_{i}^{\prime},x_{k},x^{(j,i,k)}\right)}g\left(x_{j},x_{i},x_{k},x^{(j,i,k)}\right)g\left(x_{j}^{\prime},x_{i}^{\prime},x_{k},x^{(j,i,k)}\right){\rm d}x_{i}{\rm d}x_{i}^{\prime}

4
Dependence of order statistics

In this section, we establish certain types of dependence —both for some of those previously defined and some known ones that we recall in the section— for order statistics. We begin by recalling some concepts related to order statistics. We refer to
[
18
,
12
]
and the references therein for an overview.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d}
be independent and identically distributed random variables, with density function
f
and distribution function
F
, and
X_{(i)}
denotes the
i
-th order statistic, being
X_{(1)}=\min\left\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d}\right\}
and
X_{(d)}=\max\left\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d}\right\}
. Observe that, for
1\leq i<j\leq d
, the joint density function of the random pair
\left(X_{(i)},X_{(j)}\right)
is given by

h_{i,j}\left(x_{i},x_{j}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle m_{i,j}[F(x_{i})]^{i-1}[F(x_{j})-F(x_{i})]^{j-i-1}[1-F(x_{j})]^{d-j}f(x_{i})f(x_{j}),&\mbox{if\, $x_{i}\leq x_{j}$,}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr 0,&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

where

m_{i,j}=\frac{d!}{(i-1)!(j-i-1)!(d-j)!},

and the joint density function of the random vector
\left(X_{(1)},X_{(2)},\ldots,X_{(i)}\right)
, with
2\leq i\leq d
, is given by

h_{1,2,\ldots,i}\left(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{d!}{(d-i)!}\left[1-F\left(x_{i}\right)\right]^{d-i}\prod_{k=1}^{i}f\left(x_{k}\right),&\mbox{if\, $x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq\cdots\leq x_{i}$,}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr 0,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

Now we recall several known dependence concepts: see
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
for more details and applications.

Definition 3
.

Let
X
be a random variable with distribution function
F
. Then
F
is said to be decreasing failure rate —denoted by
DFR
— if
\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}[X>x+y|X>x]
is a nondecreasing function of
x
for all
y\geq 0
.

We note that in Definition
3
, denoting
{\overline{F}}(x)=1-F(x)
, we have that
F
is decreasing failure rate if
\frac{{\overline{F}}(x+y)}{{\overline{F}}(x)}
is non-decreasing in
x
for all
y\geq 0
.

Definition 4
.

(
[
19
]
) Let
X
and
Y
be random variables.
Y
is positive regression dependent in
X
—denoted by
{\rm PRD}(Y|X)
— if
\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}[Y>y|X=x]
is a nondecreasing function of
x
for all
y\geq 0
.

A generalization of the PRD-concept for
d
random variables is the following.

Definition 5
.

The random variables
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d}
are conditionally increasing in sequence —denoted by
CIS
— if
\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[X_{i}>x_{i}|X_{1}=x_{1},\ldots,X_{i-1}=x_{i-1}\right]
is nondecreasing in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}
, for every
2\leq i\leq d
, and for all
x_{i}\in{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
.

In what follows, we provide some results on dependence concepts described in this section and the previous one for order statistics.

Proposition 11
.

Let
d
be a natural number such that
d\geq 2
. For
1\leq i\leq d
, let
X_{(i)}
be the
i
-th order statistics of a statistical sample of size
d
from a
DFR

F
, with density function
f
. Then, for every
(i,j)
such that
1\leq i<j\leq d
, we have
{\rm PRD}\left(X_{(j)}-X_{(i)}|X_{(i)}\right)
.

Proof.

We have to prove that
\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[X_{(j)}-X_{(i)}>y|X_{(i)}=x\right]
is nondecreasing in
x
for all
y\geq 0
. For that, note

\displaystyle\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[X_{(j)}-X_{(i)}>y|X_{(i)}=x\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x+y}^{+\infty}\frac{(d-i)!}{(j-i-1)!(d-j)!}[F(z)-F(x)]^{j-i-1}\frac{[1-F(z)]^{d-j}}{[1-F(x)]^{d-i}}f(z)\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(d-i)!}{(j-i-1)!(d-j)!}\int_{x+y}^{+\infty}\left[1-\frac{{\overline{F}}(z)}{{\overline{F}}(x)}\right]^{j-i-1}\left[\frac{{\overline{F}}(z)}{{\overline{F}}(x)}\right]^{d-j}\frac{f(z)}{{\overline{F}}(x)}\,{\rm d}z.

With the change of variable

we get

\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[X_{(j)}-X_{(i)}>y|X_{(i)}=x\right]=\frac{(d-i)!}{(j-i-1)!(d-j)!}\int_{0}^{\frac{{\overline{F}}(x+y)}{{\overline{F}}(x)}}u^{d-j}(1-u)^{j-i-1}\,{\rm d}u=\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[Z\leq\frac{{\overline{F}}(x+y)}{{\overline{F}}(x)}\right],

where

is the random variable with Beta distribution

is DFR, then given

∎

\displaystyle h_{i,j}\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\right)h_{i,j}(x,y)-h_{i,j}\left(x^{\prime},y\right)h_{i,j}\left(x,y^{\prime}\right)=\left(\frac{d!}{(i-1)!(j-i-1)!(d-j)!}\right)^{2}[F(x)]^{i-1}[F(x^{\prime})]^{i-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times[1-F(y)]^{d-j}\left[1-F\left(y^{\prime}\right)\right]^{d-j}f(x)f\left(x^{\prime}\right)f(y)f\left(y^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left\{\left[\left(F(y)-F(x)\right)\left(F\left(y^{\prime}\right)-F\left(x^{\prime}\right)\right)\right]^{j-i-1}-\left[\left(F\left(y^{\prime}\right)-F(x)\right)\left(F(y)-F\left(x^{\prime}\right)\right)\right]^{j-i-1}\right\},

whence the proof reduces to proving

\left(F(y)-F(x)\right)\left(F\left(y^{\prime}\right)-F\left(x^{\prime}\right)\right)\geq\left(F\left(y^{\prime}\right)-F(x)\right)\left(F(y)-F\left(x^{\prime}\right)\right)

which, it reduces in turn to

\left(F\left(x^{\prime}\right)-F(x)\right)\left(F\left(y^{\prime}\right)-F(y)\right)\geq 0;

but this obviously holds since
x\leq x^{\prime}
and
y\leq y^{\prime}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 13
.

The order statistics
X_{(1)},X_{(2)},...,X_{(d)}
, of an statistical sample of size
d
from a distribution function
F
, are always conditionally increasing in sequence (CIS).

Proof.

Let
2\leq i\leq d
. Since the joint density function of
\left(X_{(1)},X_{(2)},\ldots,X_{(i)}\right)
is given by (
4.2
), then we have

\displaystyle\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[X_{(i)}>x|\bigcap_{j=1}^{i-1}\left(X_{(j)}=x_{j}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{+\infty}\frac{h_{1,2,\ldots,i}\left(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i}\right)}{h_{1,2,\ldots,i-1}\left(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i-1}\right)}\,{\rm d}x_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\left[\frac{1-F(x)}{1-F\left(x_{i-1}\right)}\right]^{d-i+1},&\mbox{if\, $x\geq x_{i-1}$,}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr 1,&\mbox{if\, $x<x_{i-1}$.}\end{array}\right.

Therefore, for every
x_{i-1},x_{i-1}^{\prime}\in{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
, with
x_{i-1}\leq x_{i-1}^{\prime}
, since
1-F\left(x_{i-1}\right)\geq 1-F\left(x_{i-1}^{\prime}\right)
, we have

\left(\frac{1-F(x)}{1-F\left(x_{i-1}\right)}\right)^{d-i+1}\leq\left(\frac{1-F(x)}{1-F\left(x_{i-1}^{\prime}\right)}\right)^{d-i+1},

whence it easily follows the result.
∎

Proposition 14
.

For
1\leq i\leq d
, let
X_{(i)}
be the
i
-th order statistic of a statistical sample of size
d
from a
DFR
distribution
F
. Let
D_{1}=X_{(1)}
and
D_{i}=X_{(i)}-X_{(i-1)}
for
i=2,\ldots,d
. Then the random variables
D_{(1)},D_{(2)},...,D_{(d)}
are CIS.

Proof.

Given
2\leq i\leq d
, let
g_{i}
denote the joint density function of
\left(D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{i}\right)
. Then we have

g_{i}\left(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i}\right)=h_{1,2\ldots,i}\left(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{i}\right),

where
h_{1,2,\ldots,i}
is the density of
\left(X_{(1)},X_{(2)},\ldots,X_{(i)}\right)
given by (
4.2
),
y_{1}=x_{1}
and
y_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{j}x_{k}
for
j=2,\ldots,i
. Therefore, for
x\geq 0
we get

\displaystyle\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[D_{i}>x|\bigcap_{j=1}^{i-1}\left(D_{j}=x_{j}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{+\infty}\frac{h_{1,2,\ldots,i}\left(x_{1},x_{1}+x_{2},\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{i}x_{j}\right)}{h_{1,2,\ldots,i-1}\left(x_{1},x_{1}+x_{2},\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}\right)}\,{\rm d}x_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1-F\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}+x\right)}{1-F\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}\right)}\right]^{d-i+1},

and
\mbox{${\rm{I\!P}}$}\left[D_{i}>x|\bigcap_{j=1}^{i-1}\left(D_{j}=x_{j}\right)\right]=1
, when
x<0
.

Since
F
is DFR, given
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{i-1}^{\prime}\in{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
such that
x_{j}\leq x_{j}^{\prime}
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,i-1
, we have
\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}^{\prime}
and thus

\frac{{\overline{F}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}+x\right)}{{\overline{F}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}\right)}\leq\frac{{\overline{F}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}^{\prime}+x\right)}{{\overline{F}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}^{\prime}\right)},

and hence

\left(\frac{1-F\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}+x\right)}{1-F\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}\right)}\right)^{d-i+1}\leq\left(\frac{1-F\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}^{\prime}+x\right)}{1-F\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j}^{\prime}\right)}\right)^{d-i+1},

whence the result follows.
∎

5
Conclusions

We have defined two multivariate generalizations of the TP
2
-concept: Total positivity according to a direction and in pairs. The equivalence of these two multivariate dependence concepts and several key properties have been established. Moreover, specific dependencies among the order statistics of a sample from a distribution function have been identified. Future work will explore additional dependence concepts in multivariate settings.

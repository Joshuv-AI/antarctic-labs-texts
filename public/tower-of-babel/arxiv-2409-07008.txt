1.
Introduction

For an
n\times n
matrix
[a_{ij}]_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}
over a commutative ring, we use
|a_{ij}|_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}
to denote its determinant.

Let
p
be an odd prime, and let
(\frac{.}{p})
be the Legendre symbol.
Carlitz
[
1
]
determined the characteristic polynomial of the matrix

\left[x+\left(\frac{i-j}{p}\right)\right]_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant p-1},

and Chapman
[
2
]
evaluated the determinant

\left|x+\left(\frac{i+j-1}{p}\right)\right|_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant(p-1)/{2}}.

Vsemirnov
[
8
,
9
]
confirmed a challenging conjecture of Chapman
by evaluating the determinant

\left|\left(\frac{j-i}{p}\right)\right|_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant(p+1)/{2}}.

Sun
[
7
]
studied some determinants whose entries have the form
(\frac{i^{2}+cij+dj^{2}}{p})
,
where
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
; in particular he introduced

(c,d)_{p}:=\left|\left(\frac{i^{2}+cij+dj^{2}}{p}\right)\right|_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant p-1}

and

[c,d]_{p}:=\left|\left(\frac{i^{2}+cij+dj^{2}}{p}\right)\right|_{0\leqslant i,j\leqslant p-1},

and proved that if
(\frac{d}{p})=1
then

[c,d]_{p}=\begin{cases}\frac{p-1}{2}(c,d)_{p}&\text{if}\ p\nmid c^{2}-4d,\\
\frac{1-p}{p-2}(c,d)_{p}&\text{if}\ p\mid c^{2}-4d.\end{cases}

For any prime
p\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
, Sun
[
7
, Remark 1.3]
showed that

\left|\frac{1}{i^{2}+j^{2}}\right|_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant(p-1)/2}\equiv\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p).

For each prime
p\equiv 5\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 6)
, Sun
[
7
]
conjectured that

2\left|\frac{1}{i^{2}-ij+j^{2}}\right|_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant p-1}

is a quadratic residue modulo
p
. This was recently confirmed by Wu, She and Ni
[
10
]
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. For

,
Sun

5
]
investigated the determinant

D_{p}(b,c)=\left|(i^{2}+bij+cj^{2})^{p-2}\right|_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant p-1},

(1.1)

and studied the Legendre symbol
(\frac{D_{p}(b,c)}{p})
.
Sun proved that if
p\equiv 2\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 3),
then

D_{p}(1,1)=(\frac{-2}{p}).

Luo and Sun
[
3
]
determined the Legendre symbol of
D_{p}(1,1)
and
D_{p}(2,2)
in some conditions.

She and Sun
[
6
]
obtained some arithmetic properties of certain determinants involving
powers of
i^{2}+cij+dj^{2}

, where
c
and
d
are integers.

Let
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
. Sun
[

introduced the determinant
D_{p}^{-}(c,d):=\det[(i^{2}+cij+dj^{2})^{p-2}]_{1<i,j<p-1}

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}.
The definition of
D_{p}^{-}(c,d)
is as before. Then we have

(i)
p\mid D_{p}^{-}(2,2)
if
p\equiv 7\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 8)
,

(ii)
p\mid D_{p}^{-}(3,3)
if
p>5
and
p\equiv 2\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 3)
,

(iii)
p\mid D_{p}^{-}(3,1)
if
p\equiv 3,7\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 20)
.

We will prove Theorem 1.1 in the next section.

2.
Proof of Theorem 1.1

Proof
.
(i) By Fermat’s little theorem, there exists a unique polynomial

P(T)=a_{0}+a_{1}T+a_{2}T^{2}+\cdots+a_{p-2}T^{p-2}\in\mathbb{Z}[T]

such that

(T^{2}+2T+2)^{p-2}\equiv P(T)\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p)

for any
T\in\{1,2,\cdots,p-1\}
.

Now we recall the definition of the Lucas sequence. Given two integers
A
and
B,
the Lucas sequence
u_{n}=u_{n}(A,B)(n\in\mathbb{N})
and its companion
v_{n}=v_{n}(A,B)(n\in\mathbb{N})
are defined as follows:

u_{0}=0,u_{1}=1,u_{n+1}=Au_{n}-Bu_{n-1}(n=1,2,3,\cdots),

and

v_{0}=2,v_{1}=A,v_{n+1}=Av_{n}-Bv_{n-1}(n=1,2,3,\cdots).

Let
u_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}:=u_{k}(-2,2).
By
[
3

we have

\displaystyle 4a_{k}\equiv

\displaystyle(k+1)u^{{}^{\prime}}_{p-k+1}-2(k-1)u^{{}^{\prime}}_{p-k-1}

\displaystyle+2^{-k}((2k+4)u^{{}^{\prime}}_{k}-ku^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+2})\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p)

(2.1)

where

u_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}=(-4)^{\lfloor\frac{k}{4}\rfloor}\times\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\ k\equiv 0\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4),\\
1&\text{if}\ k\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4),\\
-2&\text{if}\ k\equiv 2\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4),\\
2&\text{if}\ k\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4).\end{cases}

Since
p\equiv 7\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 8)
, it is easy to find that when
k\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
,
p-k-1\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
,
p-k+1\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
and
k+2\equiv 3\ ({\rm{mod}}\ 4)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle 4a_{k}\equiv

\displaystyle(k+1)\times 2\times(-4)^{\lfloor\frac{p-k+1}{4}\rfloor}-2(k-1)\times 1\times(-4)^{\lfloor\frac{p-k-1}{4}\rfloor}

\displaystyle+2^{-k}\times[(2k+4)\times 1\times(-4)^{\lfloor\frac{k}{4}\rfloor}-k\times 2\times(-4)^{\lfloor\frac{k+2}{4}\rfloor}]

\displaystyle(k+1)\times 2\times(-4)^{\frac{p-k-2}{4}}-2(k-1)\times 1\times(-4)^{\frac{p-k-2}{4}}

Notice that

(2.3)

By

\displaystyle D_{p}^{-}(2,2)=

\displaystyle\det[(i^{2}+2ij+2j^{2})^{p-2}]_{1<i,j<p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det[(j^{2(p-2)}(ij^{-1})^{2}+2(ij^{-1})+2)^{p-2}]_{1<i,j<p-1}

\displaystyle[(p-2)!]^{2(p-2)}\det[P(ij^{-1})]_{1<i,j<p-1}

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{p-3}{2}}\hat{a_{2i}}\times\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{p-3}{2}}\hat{a_{2i+1}}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p),

where

\displaystyle\hat{a_{k}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{0}\leq j\leq p-2\\

Abstract
The combination of superconductivity and magnetic textures represents a promising approach to explore unconventional superconducting phenomena, including new correlated and topological phases. Van der Waals (vdW) materials have emerged in this context as a versatile platform to explore the interplay between these two competing orders. Here, we report on individual NbSe 2 /NiPS 3 /NbSe 2 vdW Josephson junctions behaving as superconducting quantum interference devices (SQUIDs), which we attribute to the interplay between the superconductivity of NbSe 2 \text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and the spin texture of the vdW antiferromagnetic insulator NiPS 3 \text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . The SQUID behavior, which persists for in-plane magnetic fields of at least 6 T, is the result of interference between localized transport channels that form in two separate regions of the sample. Microscopic modeling of the antiferromagnet insulator/superconductor (AFI/S) interface reveals the formation of localized states at the edges of the junction that can lead to localized channels that dominate the transport. Our findings highlight the potential of vdW superconducting heterostructures with AFs as platforms for engineering and probing novel superconducting phenomena, and they establish a new route for lithographic-free SQUIDs that operate in high magnetic fields.



The interplay between superconductivity and magnetism can trigger phenomena that do not exist in any of these two ordering states of condensed matter alone, including the generation of spin-triplet Cooper pairs, topological states, and non-reciprocal supercurrents. The study of these phenomena has also led to the development of the research field of superconducting spintronics (superspintronics), where synergies between superconducting and magnetic orders are exploited to develop superconducting devices with new functionalities and forms of control
Linder and Robinson 2015
.

Recent advances in superspintronics
Bergeret et al. 2018
have been largely driven by the study of superconductor/ferromagnet (S/F) and superconductor/ferromagnetic insulator (S/FI) heterostructures. Key achievements include direct (spectroscopic) evidence of spin-triplet pairing at S/F interfaces
Di Bernardo et al. 2015a
;
Di Bernardo et al. 2015b
;
Diesch et al. 2018
, millisecond-range spin-relaxation times of quasiparticles in S/FI systems
Yang et al. 2010
, the measurement of long-range triplet supercurrents in S/F/S Josephson junctions
Khaire et al. 2010
;
Robinson et al. 2010
, and the generation of triplet supercurrents in non-equilibrium N/S/F/S/N systems (N being a normal metal)

Jeon et al. 2018
. S/F and S/FI heterostructures thus show potential for devices that
transfer the spin angular momentum without
dissipation, and offer a promising route towards topological superconductivity
Sau et al. 2010
;
Nadj-Perge et al. 2014
;
Kezilebieke et al. 2020
;
Vaitiekėnas et al. 2021
.

By contrast, superconductor/antiferromagnet (S/AF) heterostructures have been studied to a much lower extent, despite their potential for new physics and applications. Indeed, the spin texture of antiferromagnets can lead to very rich physics when combined with superconductivity. For example, recent studies have reported signatures of spin-triplet supercurrents in Josephson junctions with a noncollinear (chiral) metallic AF as weak link
Jeon et al. 2021
;
Jeon et al. 2023
. Theoretical work has also proposed employing the synthetic spin-orbit field of an antiferromagnet to engineer topological superconductivity at S/AF interfaces
Lado and Sigrist 2020
;
Luntama et al. 2021
;
Sardinero et al. 2024
, which has motivated the study of magnetic domains as a source of magnetic anisotropy
Kjaergaard et al. 2012

Desjardins et al. 2019

We now present our theoretical model, which demonstrates that localized states emerge at NbSe
2
/NiPS
3
interfaces due to the interplay between superconducting pairing and the antiferromagnetic order of NiPS
3
1
1

1

Our model supports localized states with trivial and topological origins, but the nature of the measured channels remains a subject of future research.
.
Using a tight-binding approach, we simulate the S/AFI interface and compute the local density of states on a finite planar surface where superconductivity and antiferromagnetism coexist.
Our model reproduces the NiPS
3
spin alignment in a square lattice with alternating spins encompassing a smaller region with superconducting pairing (left panel of Fig.
3
a). The resulting behavior is governed by the ratio of the onsite magnetization
m
and the local pairing amplitude
\Delta
, assumed isotropic as in conventional s-wave superconductors.

Antiferromagnetic order implies zero net magnetization in the bulk, but local symmetry breaking at AFI/S boundaries leads to uncompensated moments.
While magnetization at the external AF edges produces higher-energy states, the superconductivity-induced boundary states are distinct.
This localized magnetization facilitates the formation of low-energy subgap states near the Fermi level,
\epsilon_{F}
, particularly when
m\gtrsim\Delta
.
To capture their spatial and energetic characteristics, we define a weighted probability distribution
\rho^{*}(x,z)=\sum_{j>0}|\psi_{j}(x,z)|^{2}/(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{F})^{2}
.
For
m/\Delta\lesssim 1

\rho^{*}
is flat, indicating delocalized states (Fig.
3
b). For
m/\Delta\gtrsim 1
, distinct low-energy edge states emerge at regions of net boundary magnetization, as shown in Figs.
3
c and
3

Due to different flake alignments in real samples, the two S/AFI interfaces in a Josephson junction likely exhibit different edge orientations, allowing localized states to overlap and form coherent transport channels (see rightmost panel of Fig.
3
a).

To conclude, we have implemented antiferromagnetic van der Waals Josephson junctions with
\text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
barriers. We have found that transport is dominated by localized channels located at
n=2

\text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and the superconducting correlations of the electrodes. While the discussion in the main text has focused on one device, in the Supplemental Material we include data of additional samples with behaviors consistent with

. Our work contributes to the exploration of hybrid systems that combine superconducting correlations, spin texture and spin-orbit coupling, and establishes the vast, yet largely unexplored potential that superconductor/antiferromagnet hybrids based on van der Waals materials for the discovery of novel physical effects. Additionally, our demonstration of a SQUID device that is based on a single vdW junction and does not require any patterning steps for the definition of its geometry can have implications on the realization of SQUID magnetometers with high scalability and on the integration of SQUID devices into superconducting circuits.

Methods

Device fabrication:
The
\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2/}}}\text{NiPS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3/}}}\text{NbSe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

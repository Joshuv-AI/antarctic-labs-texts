Risk identification model for power enterprises based on convolutional neural network
Authors: Wei Pan, Fengwei Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper proposes a risk assessment model based on Stacking ensemble learning and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for power systems with high-penetration renewable energy integration. By constructing wind farm output scenarios for the IEEE 39-bus system, the impact of renewable energy uncertainty on risk identification is systematically analyzed. Experimental results demonstrate that the model achieves optimal performance under 30% renewable energy penetration (accuracy rate: 98.01%, missed detection rate: 2.04%), significantly outperforming single CNN models. The study provides reliable decision-making support for power systems with diverse generation structures.

Introduction

With the accelerated global energy transition, the large-scale integration of renewable energy sources (RES) such as wind and photovoltaic power into power systems1 has promoted clean energy development while introducing new challenges to grid security and stability. Power enterprises now face three core issues: (1) significantly reduced system inertia and frequency regulation capacity due to high RES penetration, increasing frequency instability risks; (2) voltage control challenges caused by inadequate reactive power regulation capability of RES units; and (3) enhanced risk of cascading failures in hybrid AC/DC grid configurations2,3. Conventional risk assessment methods fail to adequately address these RES-specific characteristics, becoming insufficient for modern power system security evaluation.

The essential value of power system risk assessment lies in quantifying latent risks to enhance defensive capabilities. Existing wind power modeling approaches primarily employ probability-based interval or scenario methods, including nonparametric regression4 and Copula functions5, yet face limitations in computational efficiency and accuracy. Traditional assessment metrics like voltage/current violation thresholds6 cannot effectively reflect RES-specific inertia deficiency risks.

Artificial intelligence technologies offer innovative solutions. Current mainstream methods include machine learning algorithms (SVM, RF, CNN) and deep learning models (LSTM, GNN)7. While excelling in data processing, these require further innovation to address RES-integration risks. Methodologically, research has evolved from point prediction to holistic perception systems. Advanced techniques like Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Networks (ST-GCN)8 enable 3D characterization of grid dynamics, while Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN)9 maintain > 90% prediction accuracy while strictly satisfying physical constraints. Although the present model does not explicitly encode physical constraints, its performance leverages the implicitly physical relationships present in the simulated operational data. Future iterations could explore hybrid approaches that integrate PINN principles to further ensure physical consistency. Recent advances in machine learning for power systems include meta-learning enhanced physics-informed graph attention networks for state estimation10, topological graph attention convolutional networks with transfer learning for multi-energy flow calculation11, and full-model-free adaptive graph deep reinforcement learning for voltage control in distribution systems12. In addition, assessment frameworks have advanced through dynamic risk evaluation paradigms. Digital twin technology facilitates cross-regional collaborative assessment13. Parallel to this, Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for enabling collaborative yet privacy-preserving anomaly detection in smart grids14. Novel technologies like deep reinforcement learning-based inertia quantification15 and Attention-based Graph Neural Networks (AM-GNN)16 can track real-time inertia variations and predict cascading failure paths, significantly improving assessment timeliness and accuracy.

This paper proposes a novel power system risk identification method that integrates Stacking ensemble learning with multi-architecture CNNs. By combining the advantages of ResNet, DenseNet and EfficientNet in a heterogeneous learning framework, our approach significantly improves model performance. The key innovation presented here is the orchestration of these powerful, off-the-shelf architectures via the Stacking strategy to create a robust ensemble specifically tailored for the nuanced task of power system risk identification. We establish a comprehensive risk assessment system incorporating renewable energy uncertainty through scenario generation and equipment failure probability modeling. The introduction of Focal Loss effectively reduces the missed detection rate of high-risk events. Experimental results under varying renewable energy penetration levels demonstrate the method’s superior accuracy, robustness, and practical value for engineering applications.

Ensemble learning

Stacking strategy

Ensemble Learning is a machine - learning method aimed at improving the model’s prediction performance and generalization ability by combining multiple individual learners. Ensemble Learning improves model performance by combining multiple base learners, mainly including strategies such as voting, Bagging, Boosting, and Stacking20.

Unlike typical ensemble methods that combine variants of a single model type (homogeneous ensemble), this paper proposes a heterogeneous Stacking framework that strategically integrates three distinct and advanced CNN architectures—ResNet, DenseNet, and EfficientNet. This approach is not a simple combination but is designed to leverage the unique feature extraction strengths and complementary representations of each architecture within a unified learning paradigm, thereby achieving a synergistic improvement in risk identification performance that surpasses conventional ensemble methods or single-model deep learning approaches reported in existing power system applications. The meta-learner adaptively fuses these diverse feature representations, enabling the model to capture complex, non-linear risk patterns more effectively21.

Figure 1 shows the basic framework structure of the Stacking strategy. This structure typically consists of two layers of models: the first layer contains multiple different base classifiers, which are used to make a preliminary abnormal judgment on the pre - processed power data; the second layer is a meta - classifier, which is responsible for integrating the output results of the base classifiers to generate the final abnormal identification result. Specifically, the prediction results of the base classifiers are stacked into new features and input into the meta - classifier. Cross - validation training is used to avoid data leakage, enabling the meta - classifier to learn the complementarity among different base classifiers and improve the generalization ability and prediction accuracy of the overall model.

Convolutional neural network

The Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is an important algorithm in deep learning for processing grid-like data (shown in Fig. 2). Its structure simulates the biological visual mechanism and efficiently extracts features through local receptive fields and weight sharing22.

When there is an approximate linear relationship among the outputs of the base models, logistic regression can often achieve an ideal integration effect.

Experiment and analysis

Data set and evaluation index

In this study, the IEEE 39-bus standard test system is used for simulation analysis, and a simulation environment with a high proportion of renewable energy is constructed by introducing new energy power generation equipment. As shown in Fig. 5, wind farms with an installed capacity of 600 MW are connected to bus 33 and bus 35 of the system respectively, making the penetration rate of new energy reach 30%. To truly reflect the characteristics of wind power, the historical power data of an actual wind farm throughout the year is used as the input in this study. The operation data mainly includes the generator output, load, and voltage data of the buses during operation, and these operation variables will have an impact on the risk value. At the same time, the risk level labels are defined according to the system status. The dataset comprises 10,000 simulated operational scenarios under varying renewable outputs and fault conditions. Each scenario includes generator outputs, load profiles, bus voltages, and line flows. The risk labels are categorized into three levels (low, medium, high) based on composite risk indices. The dataset is split into training, validation, and testing sets in an 8:1:1 ratio, ensuring that each set maintains a representative distribution of risk events.

Scenario comparison

This study establishes three distinct scenarios to evaluate the performance of the Stacking ensemble model in power system risk assessment. Scenario 1 represents the standard IEEE 39-bus system; Scenario 2 modifies the IEEE 39-bus system with integrated wind farms, achieving a renewable energy penetration rate of 30%; and Scenario 3 further increases the renewable energy penetration rate to 60% based on Scenario 2.

The experimental results demonstrate a systematic variation in model performance with increasing renewable energy penetration (as shown in Fig. 7). In the standard system, the Stacking model achieved 97.68% accuracy with a 2.35% missed detection rate. When renewable penetration reached 30%, the model attained optimal performance with accuracy improving to 98.01% and missed detection rate decreasing to 2.04%. However, when penetration further increased to 60%, model performance showed significant degradation, with accuracy dropping to 96.35% and missed detection rate rising to 3.65%.

Comparative analysis of different model architectures reveals that the Stacking ensemble method consistently outperforms single CNN models across all scenarios, demonstrating particularly superior risk identification capability under moderate penetration (30%) conditions. Notably, the performance degradation exhibits a nonlinear relationship with renewable penetration: the transition from standard system to 30% penetration caused minor performance fluctuations, whereas increasing penetration from 30% to 60% led to an abrupt 1.66%-point accuracy decline, suggesting the existence of a critical risk threshold in the system. The model demonstrates strong generalization capability under moderate renewable penetration (30%), maintaining high accuracy and low missed detection rates. Although performance declines at 60% penetration, the model still outperforms single CNN architectures, indicating its robustness. Future work will explicitly focus on validating the proposed method on larger-scale, provincial-level power grids and diverse topological configurations to further establish its generalizability.

These findings provide important implications for power system risk assessment: First, for systems with renewable penetration below 40%, the Stacking ensemble approach is recommended for optimal performance; second, high-penetration scenarios require more adaptive feature engineering methods; finally, the rapid increase in missed detection rates highlights the necessity of establishing a hierarchical warning mechanism. This study quantitatively analyzes the relationship between renewable penetration and risk assessment accuracy, providing reliable model selection guidance for power systems with different generation structures.

While this study primarily focuses on demonstrating the superiority of the proposed heterogeneous Stacking-CNN framework over its constituent single CNN models, we recognize the value of comparative analysis with other state-of-the-art architectures such as LSTM, GNN, Transformer, and XGBoost. A comprehensive benchmark against these diverse methodologies is identified as a critical and necessary step for future work to fully contextualize the performance advantages of the proposed method.

The superior performance of the proposed Stacking-CNN model stems from its ability to leverage complementary feature representations through the heterogeneous integration of ResNet, DenseNet, and EfficientNet, which collectively capture multi-scale and multi-granularity spatial patterns in power system operational data often missed by single-model architectures. This is further enhanced by the Stacking framework’s meta-learning mechanism, where a logistic regression meta-classifier effectively synthesizes the diverse outputs of base learners, thereby improving generalization, reducing overfitting, and increasing robustness to noisy or imbalanced data. Additionally, the adoption of Focal Loss plays a critical role in strengthening the model’s sensitivity to high-risk minority samples—a vital attribute in power system risk assessment, where undetected anomalies can lead to severe operational consequences.

Conclusion

This study draws the following key conclusions through systematic experimental analysis: The Stacking ensemble method demonstrates optimal performance in medium renewable energy penetration scenarios (30%), achieving an F1-Score of 0.974. By effectively integrating heterogeneous features from ResNet, DenseNet, and EfficientNet, this approach shows significant performance advantages - improving accuracy by 0.72% points and reducing false negative rate by 0.98% points compared to single CNN models. In terms of model optimization, the focal loss function successfully decreases the missed detection rate of high-risk samples from 2.04% to 1.46% through intelligent sample weight adjustment, strongly validating its unique advantage in handling imbalanced data. The study also reveals that model performance exhibits nonlinear degradation under high renewable penetration conditions, with accuracy dropping by 1.66% points, suggesting the existence of distinct operational threshold effects that require corresponding adaptive feature enhancement strategies.

From an engineering application perspective, the research proposes concrete implementation recommendations for hierarchical risk assessment solutions: For systems with penetration below 40%, the Stacking ensemble model is recommended, while high-penetration systems require integration with dynamic early warning mechanisms to ensure assessment effectiveness. These research outcomes provide important theoretical foundations and technical support for power system security operation. Future research will focus on online learning optimization under extreme weather scenarios to further enhance model adaptability and warning precision.

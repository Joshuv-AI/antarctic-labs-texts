Periods of modular forms and applications to the conjectures of Oda and of Prasanna–Venkatesh
Xavier Guitart, Santiago Molina

Abstract
. We establish several formulas relating periods of modular forms on quaternion algebras over number fields to special values of L L -functions. Our main inputs are the cohomological techniques for working with periods introduced in [ preprintsanti2 ] , along with explicit versions of the Waldspurger formula due to Cai–Shu–Tian [ CST ] . We work in general even positive weights; when specialized to parallel weight 2, our formulas provide partial evidence for the conjectures of Oda and of Prasanna–Venkatesh in the case of forms associated to elliptic curves.



1.
Introduction

Periods of modular forms play an important role in number theory due to their connection with special values of
L
-functions and their appearance in several landmark conjectures, such as the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture or Deligne’s conjecture on critical values of
L
-functions. The goal of the present article is to prove several formulas relating periods of modular forms with special values of
L
-functions, and to provide applications to the conjectures of Oda and of Prasanna–Venkatesh.

1.1.
Statement of the main results

The setting we consider is the following: Let
F
be a number field of degree
d

B
be a quaternion algebra over
F
. Denote by
G
the algebraic group associated to

\underline{k}=(k_{\nu})_{\nu:F\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}\in(2{\mathbb{N}})^{d}
and conductor

be the number field fixed by
{\{\tau\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}})\colon k_{\tau\nu}=k_{\nu}\text{ for all }\nu\}}

be the coefficient field of

that contains the Hecke eigenvalues of

(in particular, for parallel weight,

is the field of Hecke eigenvalues). Let

[

that associates to any modular cusp form for

We now state the three main results of this note, in some cases under simplifying assumptions that, while not strictly necessary, allow for a clearer presentation in the introduction. The most general versions are given in the main body of the text, with references to their location indicated in parentheses. The first result is in the particular case where
G={\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
. For consistency with the notation that we will use later on, let us denote in this case by
\Pi
an automorphic representation of
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
and by
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\Pi}

Proposition 1.1

(Corollary
)
.

Let
\rho:{\mathbb{I}}_{F}/F^{\times}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
be a quadratic Hecke character and let
E_{\rho}/F
be the associated quadratic extension. Let
\varepsilon
be the sign vector defined by
\varepsilon_{\sigma}=\rho_{\sigma}(-1)
for all

\par\penalty\frac{L(1/2,\Pi,\rho)}{|d_{F}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\pi^{\frac{\underline{k}}{2}}\cdot i^{s_{\varepsilon}}\cdot|D_{\rho}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{\Pi}}\,\text{ belongs to }\,L_{\Pi},

where
|d_{F}|
(resp.

) denotes the norm of the different ideal of

(resp. the norm of the the relative discriminant of

),

, and
\pi^{\frac{\underline{k}}{2}}
stands for the real number

This result is to be expected for a reasonable notion of periods. Indeed, it is of the same type as those appearing in Shimura’s seminal works on periods
[
Shimura76
,
Shimura78
]

[
hida-duke
]
and with Blasius’s conjecture
[

[

]

The main significance of this result is that it confirms the meaningfulness of the periods we define: they essentially coincide (up to algebraic or transcendental but controlled factors) with the periods considered elsewhere. Thus, it can be interpreted as a validation that our techniques are effective in proving results concerning the relationship between periods and special values of
L
-functions.

(Corollary
)
.

Remark 1.3

In the remainder of the introduction we illustrate some applications of our formulas. We show that they provide supporting evidence –occasionally conditional on well-established conjectures– for the conjectures of Oda and Prasanna–Venkatesh, in the specific case where

1.2.
The conjectures of Oda and Prasanna-Venkatesh for elliptic curves

For the remainder of the introduction, we assume that
\Pi
corresponds to an elliptic curve
A/F
; that is,
L(s,\Pi)=L(s,A)
. This implies, in particular, that
\underline{k}=(2,\dots,2)
and that
L_{\Pi}={\mathbb{Q}}
. We fix an invariant differential
\omega_{A}\in H^{0}(A,\Omega_{A}^{1})
. For any real place
\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}

\sigma\colon F\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
the corresponding embedding. We can describe

as a complex torus

\par\penalty A_{\sigma}({\mathbb{C}})\simeq{\mathbb{C}}/\Lambda_{\sigma}

is such a way that
\omega_{A}
corresponds under this identification to the differential
dz
. Since we are interested in
\Lambda_{\sigma}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
rather than
\Lambda_{\sigma}

\Lambda_{\sigma}={\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\sigma,1}+{\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\sigma,2}
with
\Omega_{\sigma,1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\Omega_{\sigma,2}\in{\mathbb{R}}i
. Let
\tau_{\sigma}=\Omega_{\sigma,1}/\Omega_{\sigma,2}
be the period of

. The following conjecture, formulated by Oda
[

]
in the case where

is totally real, predicts that the geometric period

can be calculated from the automorphic periods of Jacquet–Langlands lifts of
\Pi
to quaternion algebras. See
[
DL
]
for an inspiring formulation of the conjecture with applications to the computation of algebraic points on elliptic curves, and
[
GMS
]
for the more general case of arbitrary signature base field.

.

[Oda]

\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}

\Pi

to

at the places different from

, and it is

at
\sigma

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\pi}_{(\varepsilon,+)}/\Omega^{\pi}_{(\varepsilon,-)}\equiv\tau_{\sigma}\mod{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}.

\par\penalty\varphi:M_{B}\mathrel{{\mathop{\simeq}\limits}}\bigoplus_{\nu:F\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}}\mathrm{M}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})_{0}\hookrightarrow\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}=B_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}=\bigoplus_{\nu:F\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}}\mathrm{M}_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})_{0},

\par\penalty W_{\mathbb{R}}={\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\rtimes\langle j\rangle,\qquad j^{2}=-1,\qquad j^{-1}zj=\bar{z},

\par\penalty\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}:B_{0}\times B_{0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}};\qquad\langle b_{1},b_{2}\rangle={\rm Tr}_{F/{\mathbb{Q}}}{\rm Tr}(b_{1}\cdot\bar{b}_{2}),

\par\penalty\kappa_{\delta}:(M_{B}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})^{W_{\mathbb{R}}}\longrightarrow(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}})^{W_{\mathbb{R}}}=:\mathfrak{a};\qquad\kappa_{\delta}(m)=\varphi(\delta^{-1}m).

\par\penalty n_{\varepsilon_{\mathbb{C}}}:=\#\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{B}^{\mathbb{C}}\colon\varepsilon_{\sigma}=-1\};\qquad\hat{H}_{\varepsilon_{\mathbb{C}}}^{\ast}:=\bigwedge_{\varepsilon_{\sigma}=-1}\hat{H}_{\sigma}^{\ast}\in\bigwedge^{n_{\varepsilon_{\mathbb{C}}}}\mathfrak{a}^{\vee}.

1.4.
Applications to Oda’s conjecture

In this section, we use the formulas of Propositions

The first result shows that Conjecture (

and lowest-degree sign vectors

Proposition 1.9

Assume that

\par\penalty A^{\rho}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{C}})=(A^{\rho}\times_{\sigma}{\mathbb{C}})({\mathbb{C}})\simeq{\mathbb{C}}/\Lambda_{\sigma}^{\rho},\quad\Lambda_{\sigma}^{\rho}={\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\sigma,1}^{\rho}+{\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\sigma,2}^{\rho};\qquad\Omega^{\rho}_{\sigma,i}=\sqrt{\sigma(\alpha)}\Omega_{\sigma,\lambda_{\sigma}i+3\frac{1-\lambda_{\sigma}}{2}},

\par\penalty L(1,A^{\rho})\in|d_{F}|^{1/2}|\alpha|^{1/2}i^{s_{\lambda}}\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}^{\mathbb{R}}}\Omega_{\sigma,\frac{3-\lambda_{\sigma}}{2}}\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}^{\mathbb{C}}}{\rm Im}(\Omega_{\sigma,1}\overline{\Omega_{\sigma,2}}){\mathbb{Q}}.

(1.7)

\par\penalty\Omega_{\lambda}^{\Pi}\equiv\frac{L(1/2,\Pi,\rho)}{|d_{F}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\pi^{d}|\alpha|^{\frac{1}{2}}i^{s_{\lambda}}}\equiv\frac{L(1,A^{\rho})}{|d_{F}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\pi^{d}|\alpha|^{\frac{1}{2}}i^{s_{\lambda}}}\equiv\pi^{-d}\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}^{\mathbb{R}}}\Omega_{\sigma,\frac{3-\lambda_{\sigma}}{2}}\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}^{\mathbb{C}}}{\rm Im}(\Omega_{\sigma,1}\overline{\Omega_{\sigma,2}})\mod{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}.

ensures the existence of

\par\penalty\Omega_{(\varepsilon,+)}^{\Pi}/\Omega_{(\varepsilon,-)}^{\Pi}\equiv\Omega_{\sigma,1}/\Omega_{\sigma,2}=\tau_{\sigma}\mod{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times},

∎

2.1.
Haar Measures

For any number field
F
, let us consider the additive character
\psi:{\mathbb{A}}_{F}/F\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
defined as

(2.1)

\par\penalty\psi=\prod_{v}\psi_{v},\qquad\psi_{v}(a)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}e^{2\pi ia},&\text{ if }F_{v}={\mathbb{R}}\\
e^{4\pi i{\rm Re}(a)},&\text{ if }F_{v}={\mathbb{C}}\\
e^{-2\pi i[{\rm Tr}_{F_{v}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(a)]}&\text{ if }v\mid p,\end{array}\right.

where
[\cdot]:{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}
is the map that sends
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
to its
p
-adic fractional part.
Let
dx_{v}
be the Haar measure of
F_{v}
normalized so that it is self-dual with respect to
\psi_{v}
; namely,
\hat{\hat{\phi}}(x_{v})=\phi(-x_{v})
, where
\hat{\phi}
is the Fourier transform
\hat{\phi}(y_{v})=\int_{F_{v}}\phi(x_{v})\psi_{v}(x_{v}y_{v})dx_{v}
.
Notice that if
v
is archimedean,
dx_{v}
is
[F_{v}:{\mathbb{R}}]
times the usual Lebesgue measure; and if
v
is non-archimedean, then
dx_{v}
is the Haar measure satisfying
{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F,v})=|d_{F_{v}}|_{v}^{1/2}
. Define

\par\penalty d^{\times}x_{v}=\zeta_{v}(1)|x_{v}|_{v}^{-1}dx_{v},

where

\par\penalty\zeta_{v}(s)=\begin{cases}(1-q_{v}^{-s})^{-1},&\text{if $v\nmid\infty$}\\
\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),&\text{if $F_{v}={\mathbb{R}}$}\\
2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s),&\text{if $F_{v}={\mathbb{C}}$}.\end{cases}

One easily checks that if
v
is non-archimedean then
{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})=|d_{F_{v}}|_{v}^{1/2}
. The product of
d^{\times}x_{v}
over all places provides a Tamagawa measure
d^{\times}x
on
{\mathbb{A}}_{F}^{\times}/F^{\times}
. In fact, such Haar measure satisfies

\par\penalty{\rm Res}_{s=1}\int_{x\in{\mathbb{A}}_{F}^{\times}/F^{\times},\;|s|\leq 1}|x|^{s-1}d^{\times}x={\rm Res}_{s=1}\Lambda_{F}(s),

where
\Lambda_{F}(s)=\zeta_{F}(s)\prod_{v\mid\infty}\zeta_{v}(s)
is the completed zeta function of
F
. This implies that, if we choose
d^{\times}t
to be the quotient measure for
T({\mathbb{A}}_{F})/T(F)={\mathbb{A}}_{E}^{\times}/{\mathbb{A}}_{F}^{\times}E^{\times}
, then one has that
{\rm vol}(T({\mathbb{A}}_{F})/T(F))=2L(1,\psi_{T})
, where
\psi_{T}
is the quadratic character associated to the extension
E/F
.

Let us consider the Haar measure
dg_{v}
of
B_{v}:=B\otimes_{F}F_{v}
which is self-dual with respect to
\psi_{v}
, namely,

\par\penalty\hat{\hat{\phi}}(g_{v})=\phi(-g_{v}),\quad\mbox{ where $\hat{\phi}$ is the Fourier transform }\quad\hat{\phi}(a_{v})=\int_{B_{v}}\phi(g_{v})\psi_{v}(a_{v}\bar{g}_{v}+g_{v}\bar{a}_{v})dg_{v},

and
(g_{v}\mapsto\bar{g}_{v})
is the usual involution on
B_{v}
. We define similarly as above

d^{\times}g_{v}=\zeta_{v}(1)|g_{v}\bar{g}_{v}|_{v}^{-2}dg_{v}
. The product of such
d^{\times}g_{v}
over all places provides a Tamagawa measure for
G
satisfying
{\rm vol}(G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}_{F}))=2
and (see
[
CST
, Lemma 3.5]
)

\par\penalty\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm vol}({\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}))=\zeta_{v}(2)^{-1}|d_{F_{v}}|_{v}^{3/2},&\text{ if }v\nmid\infty\text{ and }B_{v}=\mathrm{M}_{2}(F_{v}),\\
{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{B_{v}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})=\zeta_{v}(2)^{-1}(q_{v}-1)^{-1}|d_{F_{v}}|_{v}^{3/2},&\text{ if }v\nmid\infty\text{ and }B_{v}\neq\mathrm{M}_{2}(F_{v}),\\
{\rm vol}(B_{\sigma}^{\times}/F_{\sigma}^{\times})=2\pi^{2},&\text{ if }\sigma\mid\infty\text{ and }B_{\sigma}\neq\mathrm{M}_{2}(F_{\sigma}).\end{array}

If
\sigma\in\Sigma_{B}
and
F_{\sigma}={\mathbb{R}}
, then the measure
d^{\times}g_{\sigma}
corresponds to

(2.2)

\par\penalty d^{\times}g_{\sigma}=\frac{dxdyd\theta}{y^{2}},\quad\text{ where }\quad g_{\sigma}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big(\begin{smallmatrix}y^{1/2}&\\
&y^{-1/2}\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big),

for

,
y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
\theta\in[0,\pi)
. Finally, if
\sigma\in\Sigma_{B}
and
F_{\sigma}={\mathbb{C}}
, then the measure
d^{\times}g_{\sigma}
corresponds to

(2.3)

\par\penalty d^{\times}g_{\sigma}=16\frac{\sin 2\theta drds_{1}ds_{2}dadbd\theta}{r^{3}},\quad\text{ where }\quad g_{\sigma}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r&s_{1}+is_{2}\\
&r^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta e^{ai}&\sin\theta e^{bi}\\
-\sin\theta e^{-bi}&\cos\theta e^{-ai}\end{smallmatrix}\right),

for

2.2.
Gauss sums

In this section we introduce the definition of Gauss sums that we will use and prove some standard results which we have not found in the literature stated in the precise form we will need. Let

\par\penalty\chi=\prod_{v}\chi_{v}:{\mathbb{I}}_{F}/F^{\times}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}

be a locally constant character. For a non-archimedean place
v
, write
\mathfrak{c}(\chi_{v})
for the conductor of
\chi_{v}
.
We fix
y_{v}\in F_{v}^{\times}
such that
d_{F_{v}}^{-1}=y_{v}\cdot\mathfrak{c}(\chi_{v})
.

The local Gauss sum
\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v},y_{v})
is defined as

\par\penalty\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v},y_{v})=\frac{1}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}\chi_{v}(x_{v})^{-1}\psi_{v}(y_{v}x_{v})d^{\times}x_{v},

where
\psi_{v}
is the additive character of (
2.1
).
Notice that when
\chi_{v}
is unramified,
\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v},y_{v})=1
.
We write
y=(y_{v})_{v}\in{\mathbb{I}}_{F}^{\infty}
, and we define
the Gauss sum of
\chi
as

\par\penalty\mathfrak{g}(\chi,y)=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v},y_{v}).

Remark 2.1
.

The Gauss sum
\mathfrak{g}(\chi,y)
depends on
y
, but a different choice of
y
has the effect of scaling
\mathfrak{g}(\chi,y)
by an element of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)^{\times}
, the field generated by the values of
\chi
. When we write equalities
{\rm mod}\;{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)^{\times}
, we will simply write
\mathfrak{g}(\chi)
instead of
\mathfrak{g}(\chi,y)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

We have that

\par\penalty\mathfrak{g}(\chi,y)\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\chi^{-1},y)=|c(\chi)|\prod_{v\mid c(\chi)}\zeta_{v}(1)^{2}\chi_{v}(-1).

In particular, if
\chi
is quadratic associated to the quadratic extension
E/F

\par\penalty\mathfrak{g}(\chi,y)\equiv i^{\#\{\sigma\mid\infty\colon E_{\sigma}={\mathbb{C}}\}}|D|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mod{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times},

where
D
is the relative discriminant of
E/F
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v},y_{v})\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v}^{-1},y_{v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})^{2}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}\chi_{v}(x_{v}^{-1}t_{v})\psi_{v}(y_{v}(x_{v}+t_{v}))d^{\times}x_{v}d^{\times}t_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})^{2}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}\chi_{v}(z_{v})\psi_{v}(y_{v}x_{v}(1+z_{v}))d^{\times}z_{v}d^{\times}x_{v}.

Let
\delta_{v}\in F_{v}
be such that
d_{F_{v}}=\delta_{v}{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}
. By
[
Spiess
, Lemma 2.1]
, we have that

\par\penalty\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}\psi_{v}(ax_{v})d^{\times}x_{v}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})&\text{ if }a\in d_{F}^{-1},\\
{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})(1-q_{v})^{-1}&\text{ if }{\rm ord}(\delta_{v}a)=-1,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then, if we write
c_{v}=\delta_{v}^{-1}y_{v}^{-1}
we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v},y_{v})\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v}^{-1},y_{v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}\chi_{v}(z_{v})1_{d_{F}^{-1}}(y_{v}(1+z_{v}))d^{\times}z_{v}+\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}\frac{\chi_{v}(z_{v})}{(1-q_{v})}1_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}(\varpi_{v}c_{v}^{-1}(1+z_{v}))d^{\times}z_{v}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}\chi_{v}(z_{v})1_{c(\chi_{v})}(1+z_{v})d^{\times}z_{v}+\int_{{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}\frac{\chi_{v}(z_{v})}{(1-q_{v})}1_{\varpi_{v}^{-1}c_{v}{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}}(1+z_{v})d^{\times}z_{v}\right).

Notice that
\mathfrak{c}(\chi_{v})=c_{v}{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}
. Hence, when
c_{v}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times}
(
\chi_{v}
is unramified), then
\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v},y_{v})\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v}^{-1},y_{v})=1
. On the other hand, when
c(\chi_{v})=\varpi_{v}{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v},y_{v})\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v}^{-1},y_{v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm vol}(1+c(\chi_{v}))}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})}\chi_{v}(-1)+\frac{{\rm vol}(1+c(\chi_{v}))}{(1-q_{v}){\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})}\left(\sum_{a\in({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}/\varpi_{v}{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}})\setminus\{0,-1\}}\chi_{v}(a)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{v}-1}\chi_{v}(-1)\left(1+\frac{1}{q_{v}-1}\right)=\chi_{v}(-1)\frac{q_{v}}{(q_{v}-1)^{2}}=|c_{v}|_{v}(1-q_{v}^{-1})^{-2}\chi_{v}(-1).

Finally, when
{\rm ord}(c_{v})\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v},y_{v})\cdot\mathfrak{g}(\chi_{v}^{-1},y_{v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm vol}(1+c(\chi_{v}))}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})}\chi_{v}(-1)+\frac{(1-q_{v})^{-1}}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})}\sum_{a\in({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}/\varpi_{v}{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}})^{\times}}\int_{-1+a\varpi_{v}^{-1}c_{v}+c(\chi_{v})}\chi_{v}(z_{v})d^{\times}z_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm vol}(1+c(\chi_{v}))}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})}\chi_{v}(-1)+\frac{{\rm vol}(1+c(\chi_{v}))}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})(1-q_{v})}\chi_{v}(-1)\sum_{a\in(1+\varpi_{v}^{-1}c(\chi_{v}))/(1+c(\chi_{v})\setminus\{1\}}\chi_{v}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm vol}(1+c(\chi_{v}))}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})}\chi_{v}(-1)+\frac{{\rm vol}(1+c(\chi_{v}))}{{\rm vol}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}}^{\times})(q_{v}-1)}\chi_{v}(-1)=|c_{v}|_{v}(1-q_{v}^{-1})^{-2}\chi_{v}(-1),

and the result follows.
∎

2.3.
Finite dimensional representations

Let
k
be an even positive integer. For any field
L
,
let
{\mathcal{P}}(k)_{L}={\rm Sym}^{k}(L^{2})
be the space of polynomials in 2 variables over
L
homogeneous of degree
k
with
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}(L)
-action:

\par\penalty\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)P\right)(X,Y)=(ad-bc)^{-\frac{k}{2}}P(aX+cY,bX+dY),\qquad P\in{\mathcal{P}}(k)_{L}.

Let us denote by
V(k)_{L}={\mathcal{P}}(k)_{L}^{\vee}
, with dual
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}(L)
-action:

\par\penalty(g\mu)(P)=\mu(g^{-1}P),\qquad\mu\in V(k)_{L},\ g\in{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}(L).

Notice that
V(k)_{L}\simeq{\mathcal{P}}(k)_{L}
by means of the isomorphism

(2.4)

\par\penalty V(k)_{L}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{P}}(k)_{L},\qquad\mu\longmapsto\mu((Xy-Yx)^{k}).

2.3.1.
Polynomials and torus

From the fixed embedding
E\hookrightarrow B
we can define an isomorphism
B\otimes_{F}E\simeq{\rm M}_{2}(E)
. In fact, we have that
B=E\oplus EJ
, where
J
normalizes
E
and
J^{2}\in F^{\times}
. Hence, for this fixed choice of
J
we have the embedding
\iota\colon B\hookrightarrow\mathrm{M}_{2}(E)
given by

(2.5)

\par\penalty\iota(e_{1}+e_{2}J)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e_{1}&J^{2}e_{2}\\
\bar{e}_{2}&\bar{e}_{1}\end{array}\right),

where here the bar denotes the non-trivial automorphism in
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E/F)
.
For a given embedding
\nu:F\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
we fix an extension
\nu_{E}\colon E\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
. The composition
\nu_{E}\circ\iota
gives rise to an embedding
G(F_{\sigma})\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
. This induces an action of
G(F_{\infty})=G(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}})
on the spaces

\par\penalty V(\underline{k}):=\bigotimes_{\nu}V(k_{\nu})_{\mathbb{C}}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad{\mathcal{P}}(\underline{k}):=\bigotimes_{\nu}{\mathcal{P}}(k_{\nu})_{\mathbb{C}},

where
\underline{k}=(k_{\nu})\in(2{\mathbb{N}})^{d}
,
d=[F\colon{\mathbb{Q}}]
, and
\nu
runs over the embeddings of
F
in
{\mathbb{C}}
. The natural embedding
G(F)\subseteq G(F_{\infty})
induces a structure of
G(F)
-representation to
V(\underline{k})
and to
{\mathcal{P}}(\underline{k})
.

The composition
E\hookrightarrow B\mathrel{{\mathop{\hookrightarrow}\limits}}{\rm M}_{2}(E)
maps
e
to
\big(\begin{smallmatrix}e&\\
&\bar{e}\end{smallmatrix}\big)
. This implies that we have a
T(F_{\infty})
-equivariant morphism

(2.6)

\par\penalty{\mathcal{P}}(\underline{k})\longrightarrow C(T(F_{\infty}),{\mathbb{C}});\qquad\bigotimes_{\nu}P_{\nu}\longmapsto\left((t_{\sigma})_{\sigma\mid\infty}\mapsto\prod_{\sigma\mid\infty}\prod_{\nu\mid\sigma}P_{\nu}\left(1,\nu_{E}\left(\frac{t_{\sigma}}{\bar{t}_{\sigma}}\right)\right)\nu_{E}\left(\frac{t_{\sigma}}{\bar{t}_{\sigma}}\right)^{-\frac{k_{\nu}}{2}}\right),

where
C(T(F_{\infty}),{\mathbb{C}})
denotes the set of continuous functions from
T(F_{\infty})
to
{\mathbb{C}}
.

2.3.2.
Invariant polynomials

For
\underline{m}=(m_{\nu})_{\nu}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
\lambda=(\lambda_{\sigma})_{\sigma}\in F_{\infty}
and
t=(t_{\sigma})_{\sigma}\in T(F_{\infty})
, we write

\par\penalty\lambda^{\underline{m}}:=\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}}\prod_{\nu\mid\sigma}\nu(\lambda_{\sigma})^{m_{\nu}};\qquad t^{\underline{m}}:=\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}}\prod_{\nu\mid\sigma}\nu_{E}\left(\frac{t_{\sigma}}{\bar{t}_{\sigma}}\right)^{m_{\nu}}.

For reasons that will become apparent later, it is more convenient to consider degrees of the form
(\underline{k}-2)=(k_{\nu}-2)
, for some
\underline{k}=(k_{\nu})\in(2{\mathbb{N}})^{d}
with
k_{\nu}\geq 2
.
If
\frac{2-k_{\nu}}{2}\leq m_{\nu}\leq\frac{k_{\nu}-2}{2}
for all
\nu:F\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
, the character

corresponds by means of the morphism (
2.6
) to the element
\mu_{\underline{m}}=\bigotimes_{\nu}\mu_{m_{\nu}}\in V(\underline{k}-2)
given by

(2.7)

\par\penalty\mu_{m_{\nu}}\left(\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}X&Y\\
x&y\end{array}\right|^{k_{\nu}-2}\right)=x^{\frac{k_{\nu}-2}{2}-m_{\nu}}y^{\frac{k_{\nu}-2}{2}+m_{\nu}},\quad\mbox{or simply}\quad\mu_{\underline{m}}\left(\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}X&Y\\

Anterior segment optical coherence tomography study of the cornea and tear film after systemic isotretinoin treatment in acne vulgaris
Authors: Mohamed Salah El-Din Mahmoud, Hosny Ahmed Zein, Mustafa Mohamed Al-Anwar, Ahmed Maher Khalafallah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeEvaluation of the precorneal tear film, CET, and CT by AS-OCT before and after treatment by oral isotretinoin in patients of acne vulgaris (AV).MethodsA prospective observational study was conducted on 100 eyes of 100 patients of AV at the Department of Ophthalmology, Minia University, Egypt. The patients were examined five times: before the start of treatment, after one month, after three months of treatment, and then after cessation of treatment by one and three months, with measurement of the tear film (TMA, TMD, and TMH), CET, and CT using the AS-OCT.ResultsRegarding tear film, there was a significant difference between the premedication TMH, TMA, and TMD and after one and three months of administration, followed by a significant improvement after cessation of treatment. Regarding corneal pachymetry, there were a significant changes in the total CT and CET after one and three months of administration, which improved after one and three months of drug cessation.ConclusionIsotretinoin treatment causes statistically significant changes in the tear film and corneal pachymetry, so clinicians should avoid performing any surgeries that require an accurate knowledge of corneal parameters at least 3 months after the cessation of treatment.Keywords: Anterior segment optical coherence Tomography- Cornea-Tear film, Isotretinoin, Acne vulgaris

Introduction

Acne vulgaris (AV) is a common dermatological skin disorder. When other treatments for severe cystic AV have failed, isotretinoin (13-cis-retinoic acid) is preferred. Isotretinoin is a first-generation nonaromatic b-carotene retinoid (provitamin A) [1].

Oral isotretinoin results in long-term acne remission by inhibiting many acne-causing factors, and it is still the most clinically effective acne treatment of the last three decades. It has a strong effect on the skin’s sebaceous glands, resulting in a marked reduction in comedogenesis and sebum excretion reaching up to 80%. It also has anti-inflammatory properties and inhibits P. acnes chemotaxis and colonisation [2].

The majority of ocular reactions are determined directly by the mode of action of isotretinoin. Isotretinoin causes meibomian gland atrophy significantly as well as sebaceous glands of the skin [3].

Other ocular side effects that can be caused by systemic isotretinoin include meibomitis, blepharoconjunctivitis, eye lid photodermatitis, keratitis, which can lead to corneal opacities, contact lens intolerance, dark adaptation deficiency, increasing myopia, and even pre-macular hemorrhage [3, 4].

When low doses are used, ocular manifestations are typically less severe, and some patients may experience more than one adverse ocular event [5].

Tear film clinical evaluation is done using the fluorescein tear breakup time (TBUT) test and Schirmer’s test. Measuring corneal epithelial or total corneal thickness and lower tear film height and area is an important portion in evaluating side effects of systemic isotretinoin, and now has become available after the evolution of a new noninvasive tool for imaging, which is the AS-OCT [6].

The aim of this study was the evaluation of the precorneal tear film, CET, and CT by the AS-OCT before and after treatment by oral isotretinoin in patients of AV.

Patients and methods

Study design

100 eyes of 100 AV patients were the subjects of this prospective observational study that ran from September 2022 through October 2024 at the Department of Ophthalmology at Minia University in Minia, Egypt. The study followed all of the guidelines laid out in the Declaration of Helsinki and was given the green light by the Institutional Review Board of the Faculty of Medicine at Minia University (Approval No: 296–2022). After thoroughly explaining the nature of the investigation, a written agreement was obtained from each participant before registration in the study.

Study participants

Participants had to be at least 18 years old, have a dermatological appointment at Minia University Hospital, and have a planned oral isotretinoin treatment of 0.8 mg/kg daily for AV. All of the individuals who were considered had normal intraocular pressure (IOP) and normal anterior and posterior segments. This study did not include patients who had any of the following conditions: a history of ocular surgery or eye trauma; use of contact lenses; ocular inflammatory illness; any systemic disease (except of AV); or use of any systemic medication (except for isotretinoin) within the preceding three months.

The patients were examined five times, before the start of treatment, after one, after three months of treatment, and then after cessation of the treatment by one and three months.

Examination

Including taking a patient’s medical history, measuring their IOP by air puff tonometry (TOPCON computerized tonometer CT-800, Japan), slit-lamp examination (Sunkingdom LS-3, Germany), measuring their uncorrected and best-corrected visual acuity by Snellen’s chart, which was transformed to log MAR chart. Refraction using autorefractometer (Nidek CO, Japan) and also fundus examination using slit lamp bio-microscopy using a + 78 diopter condensing lens and peripheral retinal examination using binocular indirect ophthalmoscopy.

The results of the TBUT and Schirmer’s test with topical anesthetic (STA) led to the clinical diagnosis of dry eye illness. To determine TBUT, a fluorescent strip that had been soaked with distilled water (Haag-Streit AG, Köniz, Switzerland) was placed on the lower conjunctival fornix. Then, the tear film was observed through the cobalt blue filter of the slit-lamp, and the patient was instructed not to blink until the first dry spot appeared. The break-up time value was determined by averaging the three repetitions of this method between the final blink and the emergence of the first dry patch. It was deemed abnormal if the break-up time was fewer than 10 s. Test paper strips were inserted into the inferior fornix at the junction of the middle and lateral regions of the lower eyelid after one drop of 0.4% benoxinate hydrochloride was instilled. STA was then conducted. We withdrew the paper strip after 5 min and measured the moist section. Abnormality was defined as a measurement below 15 mm.

AS-OCT Imaging

The Avanti RTVue-XR platform’s spectral domain OCT with the corneal adapter module’s add-on lens (CAM-L mode: S/N 43386) was used. The platform is manufactured by Optovue and is located in Fremont, CA, USA. The operator asked that all subjects refrain from applying any topical drugs for at least two hours before imaging. All patients underwent imaging between 11 AM and 1 PM to eliminate any potential impact of diurnal fluctuation in measurements. A relative humidity of 40–45% and a temperature range of 23–25 °C were maintained in the imaging room. Two lines, one horizontal on the bottom edge of the lid and one vertical on the inferior cornea at 6 o’clock, were used to detect the lower tear meniscus. In order to get a high-quality scan, many photos were taken. All individuals underwent AS-OCT imaging and tear meniscus parameter assessments by one skilled operator.

Statistical analysis

SPSS statistics software version 20 was used for data analysis. Numbers and percentages were used for qualitative data. Mean ± standard deviation was used for quantitative data. For the comparison of dependent data, the paired sample t-test was used. For the comparison of categorical data, the chi-squared test was used. The P value was considered significant if p < 0.05.

Discussion

Many adults and teenagers will be affected by AV at some point in their lives; it is a very prevalent health concern. The disease is physically and emotionally crippling and needs treatment, but it doesn’t seem to be a hazard to general health. Even though there are other acne therapies, oral isotretinoin is ideal since it targets every known mechanism that causes acne. Isotretinoin users may experience dry eye, according to recent research [7].

Over the past twenty years, isotretinoin (13-cis-retinoic acid), a synthetic version of vitamin A, has been prescribed to patients suffering from severe acne and other skin conditions. Isotretinoin guarantees effective treatment by influencing multiple steps of the cell cycle, including progression, differentiation, and death. It has an anti-inflammatory impact, reduces sebum production, influences comedogenesis, and inhibits the growth of the acne-causing propionibacterium bacteria. But isotretinoin can have a slew of negative side effects on a wide range of systems. Use of isotretinoin can lead to a number of eye side effects, most of which affect the cornea and eyelids. These include abnormal secretions from the meibomian glands (MGs) and atrophy of the MGs, cloudiness of the cornea, inflammation of the conjunctiva and keratitis, decreased vision, intolerance to contact lenses, increased tear osmolarity, photophobia, and decreased visual acuity [7, 8].

Numerous factors can contribute to the development of dry eye. The condition of dry eyes can be categorized as either evaporative dry eye or aqueous-deficient dry eye. When the tear film’s aqueous content is insufficient, it’s called aqueous-deficient dry eye. On the other hand, evaporative dry eye is often associated with alterations in the secretion of meibum, both quantitatively and qualitatively. There are two types of evaporative dry eye: low output and high output [7, 8].

Visualizing and quantifying the tear film becomes challenging due to its central thickness of around 4–6 μm. In clinical practice, a number of assays, including the Schirmer test and TBUT, are utilized as substitutes for the tear film integrity. Having said that, these examinations are intrusive and not very repeatable. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) has quickly advanced to become the gold standard for imaging the optic nerve and retina. In both healthy and diseased states, AS-OCT has found extensive utility in evaluating the various structures of the anterior segment, including the cornea, iridocorneal angle, and conjunctiva. It provides a high-resolution imaging method that does not involve intrusive procedures and can reliably measure the tear film. In order to avoid artifacts caused by upper lid lashes and because its dimensions are more specific and sensitive than those of the upper meniscus, the lower tear film meniscus—a wedge-shaped layer situated between the inferior lid margin and the surface of the bulbar conjunctiva or cornea—is typically examined. To measure the lower meniscus, AS-OCT imaging yields three parameters: TMH, TMD, and TMA. With excellent specificity and sensitivity, these criteria have been tested and proven to be dependable. In addition, clinical tests like the Schirmer test and TBUT have demonstrated a good correlation with them [9, 10].

100 eyes of patients of AV were included in the study and referred from the dermatology department. The included patients were ophthalmologically free with normal anterior and posterior segment examination. We excluded cases with a previous history of contact lenses or LASIK, or previous topical medications, to avoid any reasons for false results.

The results of the shirmer test and BUT showed significant reduction after the start of the treatment, with more reduction over time, then the results improved after the cessation of the drug, with a value near the premedication state after three months.

The findings were in agreement with those of Koca S and Oral AY 2023, who demonstrated that the acne group had significantly lower TBUT and Schirmer measurements (p = 0.000 and p = 0.003, respectively) [11]. They were also in agreement with Düzgün E and Özkur E 2022 [12], who examined the effects of oral isotretinoin on MG morphology detected with non-contact meibography and to determine its relationship with clinical dry eye tests. The acne group had noticeably shorter TBUT times and Schirmer measures (p = 0.000 and p = 0.003, respectively), according to research by Tanriverdi C et al., 2022 [1]. After 4 months, patients receiving oral isotretinoin showed significant alterations in TFBUT, according to Caglar et al. [13]

The epithelial thickness and total CT were reduced with the use of the drug, with more reduction after 3 months than one month of the start of the treatment then after the stoppage of the drug, there was gradual improvement in the total CT and epithelial thickness after three months. The results agreed with Total CT and CET: who used the Pentacam Scheimpflug topography to assess the patients at baseline, throughout the third and sixth months of treatment, and found statistically significant alterations in total CT readings (p < 0.001) [14].

In a study conducted by Yildirim Y et al. 2014, the corneal topography was assessed using a Scheimpflug camera in conjunction with Placido-disk (Sirius) on 54 patients’ eyes at baseline, one, three, and six months into treatment, and again six months following isotretinoin termination. Additionally, it was demonstrated that there was a notable shift in the thinnest corneal thickness at the baseline, three months into therapy, and six months after treatment ended [15].

In a study conducted by Mehmet EG et al. 2018, OCT was utilized to examine changes in ocular surface epithelial thickness in individuals who were administered oral isotretinoin. The results showed that the thickness of the ocular surface epithelium was reduced in these patients. Epithelial thinning is the primary mechanism by which isotretinoin reduces corneal thickness in individuals. The ocular surface epithelial thickness was increased following a month of stopping isotretinoin medication [16].

Ozyol P et al. 2021 demonstrated that isotretinoin treatment causes stromal thinning and epithelial thickening in 40 eyes using the RTVue-XR and Pentacam at baseline, 1 st, 3rd, and 6th months of medication, as well as 3rd month after isotretinoin cessation. After three months of stopping treatment, the cornea’s pachymetry shifts back to baseline [17].

Several studies have assessed meibography and Schirmer to determine meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD), one such study is Gurlevik U et al. 2022. At 3 months following treatment, the proportion of lower MG loss was 18.7% (p = 0.001), and at 6 months after treatment, it was 20.7% (p = 0.001) [7].

A study by Total CT and CET:, who evaluated the MG alterations by meibography for upper (UE) and lower eyelids (LE) separately, resulted in the MGD grades. At baseline, the patient group had higher MGD grades, UE meiboscores, and LE meiboscores for UE and LE eyelids (MGD) (p = 0.013, p = 0.004, and p = 0.008, respectively). In comparison to the baseline, MGD grades, UE meiboscores, and LE meiboscores all increased in the third month (p < 0.001, p < 0.001, and p = 0.008, respectively) [18].

Tanriverdi C et al. 2022 conducted a study using the Sirius corneal topography device to conduct non-contact meibography. additionally, it was demonstrated that the control group had lower scores for total eyelid secretion (p = 0.000) and meibomian gland secretion (p = 0.003) compared to the acne group [1].

The dose of oral isotretinoin has been studied by other studies as Cumurcu T et al. 2009 who examined 26 patients receiving high-dose (> 0.5 mg/kg per day) and 25 patients treated with low-dose (< 0.5 mg/kg per day) before treatment, at days 45 and 90 of treatment, and 1 month after the completion of treatment and resulted in a statistically significant decrease in BUT in Group 1 when compared with Group 2 at days 45 and 90 of treatment (p < 0.05). One month after the completion of treatment, there was no difference in BUT between the two groups (p > 0.05) [19].

Cumurcu T et al. 2012 evaluated the changes in anterior chamber parameters with the Pentacam rotating Scheimpflug and axial length measurements (Al) by ultrasound on 29 eyes of patients who use isotretinoin before the treatment, and at the 3rd and 6th months of treatment. Anterior chamber depth (ACD), anterior chamber volume (ACV), anterior chamber angle (ACA) width, CT, lens thickness (LT), pupil size, Al and mean keratometry values (K) were measured and resulted in statistically significant differences between the pretreatment and 3rd and 6th month ACD, ACV, CT, Al, LT and mean K values (p < 0.05) [20].

Our study had some limitations, the most significant of which was the limited sample size (relatively) and brief treatment duration. The majority of the group was female. We did not test isotretinoin at various dosages. Regarding the meibomian glands, the meibography was not performed for evaluation purposes.

In conclusion, statistically significant alterations in tear film and corneal pachymetry are caused by isotretinoin treatment. So, dermatologists should advise the patients to use a tear substitute to overcome dryness under the supervision of an ophthalmologist. Also, clinicians should wait at least three months after stopping isotretinoin usage before undergoing corneal procedures that need precise measurement of corneal parameters, like corneal thickness, because of the drug’s impact on corneal properties. While isotretinoin does cause certain ocular side effects, these are usually mild and go away after treatment is over.

First Constraint on Axion-Photon Coupling g γ g_{\gamma} from Neutron Star Observations Preprint: CPTNP-2025-017
Jun-Chen Wang, Shunshun Cao, Jinchen Jiang, Yandong Liu, Qing-Hong Cao, Lijing Shao

Abstract
We propose a novel method to detect axions which uniquely depends on the dimensionless axion-photon coupling g γ g_{\gamma} , independent of the suppressive axion decay constant f a f_{a} . Using neutron star PSR B1919+21 data from the Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical Telescope, we derive the first constraint | g γ | < 0.93 |g_{\gamma}|<0.93 at 1 ​ σ 1\sigma confidence level for ultra-light axions ( m a < 10 − 11 m_{a}<10^{-11} eV).

Conclusion
–We have demonstrated that the axion phase transition induced by neutron stars provides a novel observational approach for detecting axions. Employing photon birefringence caused by axion fields and pulsar RFM, we predict observable, frequency-dependent polarization rotations in neutron star radio emissions. These measurements directly constrain the dimensionless axion-photon coupling
g_{\gamma}
, independent of the traditionally suppressive axion decay constant
f_{a}
, thus enhancing detection sensitivity.
Our analysis of FAST data constrains the axion-photon coupling to
|g_{\gamma}|<0.93
, 1.33 and 1.93 at
1\sigma
,
2\sigma
and
3\sigma
confidence levels for axion masses below
10^{-11}
eV, respectively.

Appendix A
Axion phase transition in neutron stars and corresponding profile

The axion profile presented in the main text plays a crucial role in our analysis. While previous works
Hook and Huang 2018
;
Huang et al. 2019
;
Kumamoto et al. 2024
derived this profile by assuming a suppressed axion mass, we provide here a more general derivation starting from the axion potential. We begin with the QCD-induced axion potential
Grilli di Cortona et al. 2016
:

V_{0}=-m_{\pi}^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{u}m_{d}}{(m_{u}+m_{d})^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{a}{2f_{a}}\right)},

(15)

where
m_{\pi}
and
f_{\pi}
are the pion mass and decay constant, and
m_{u}
,
m_{d}
are the up and down quark masses. For simplicity, we take
m_{u}=m_{d}\equiv m_{q}
. Using chiral perturbation theory,
m_{\pi}^{2}=-2m_{q}\langle\bar{q}q\rangle/f_{\pi}^{2}
, with
\langle\bar{q}q\rangle\equiv\langle\bar{u}u+\bar{d}d\rangle/2<0
. The potential becomes

V_{0}=2m_{q}\langle\bar{q}q\rangle\left|\cos\left(\frac{a}{2f_{a}}\right)\right|.

(16)

This has a minimum at
a=0
, consistent with bounds from EDM measurements
Crewther et al. 1979
;
Pendlebury et al. 2015
.

However, in many UV-complete models, higher-dimensional operators can significantly modify the axion potential
Banks and Seiberg 2011
;
Witten 2018
;
Harlow and Ooguri 2021
;
Alvey and Escudero 2021
;
Dine 2022
. Within effective field theory, these effects are captured by
Alvey and Escudero 2021
;
Dine 2022
:

\Delta V=\Lambda^{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{1-n/2}|\lambda_{n}|\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\Lambda}\right)^{n}\cos\left(n\frac{a}{f_{a}}+\beta_{n}\right),

(17)

where
\Lambda>f_{a}
is the cutoff scale and
|\lambda_{n}|
,
\beta_{n}
are the magnitude and phase of the effective coupling. Since
f_{a}/\Lambda<1
, the
n=1
term dominates. The full potential is thus approximated as

\displaystyle V(a)

\displaystyle\simeq 2m_{q}\langle\bar{q}q\rangle\left|\cos\left(\frac{a}{2f_{a}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{2}\Lambda^{3}f_{a}|\lambda_{1}|\cos\left(\frac{a}{f_{a}}+\beta_{1}\right).

(18)

Requiring that the global minimum remains at
a=0
, consistent with experiments
Crewther et al. 1979
;
Pendlebury et al. 2015
, yields two scenarios:

(I)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\pi,

(19)

(II)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=0,\quad m_{q}|\langle\bar{q}q\rangle|>2\sqrt{2}\Lambda^{3}f_{a}|\lambda_{1}|.

(20)

We focus on Scenario II, which allows for axion phase transitions in dense environments.

Such a transition arises when QCD chiral symmetry, spontaneously broken in vacuum, is restored at high nucleon densities
Pietroni 2005
;
Olive and Pospelov 2008
;
Hinterbichler and Khoury 2010
. Let
\langle\bar{q}q\rangle_{\rho_{N}}
denote the in-medium condensate at nucleon density
\rho_{N}
. Studies indicate
|\langle\bar{q}q\rangle_{\rho_{N}}|<|\langle\bar{q}q\rangle_{0}|

Cohen et al. 1992
;
Brown and Rho 1991
. Thus, if

m_{q}|\langle\bar{q}q\rangle_{\rho_{N}}|<2\sqrt{2}\Lambda^{3}f_{a}|\lambda_{1}|<m_{q}|\langle\bar{q}q\rangle_{0}|,

(21)

then the axion vacuum shifts from
a=0
(in vacuum) to

a_{\rho_{N}}=2f_{a}\arccos\left(\frac{m_{q}|\langle\bar{q}q\rangle_{\rho_{N}}|}{2\sqrt{2}\Lambda^{3}f_{a}|\lambda_{1}|}\right).

(22)

In neutron stars, where
\rho_{N}^{\text{NS}}\sim 10^{6}\text{--}10^{9}\ \text{MeV}^{3}

Jamin 2002
;
Moodley 2024
, chiral symmetry is restored and
a_{\rm NS}=\pi f_{a}
. On Earth,
\rho_{N}^{\text{Earth}}\sim 3\times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{MeV}^{3}
, and
\langle\bar{q}q\rangle\approx\langle\bar{q}q\rangle_{0}
.

We now derive the axion field profile around the neutron star. The equation of motion is

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}a=-\frac{dV}{da},

(23)

with
V(a)
as above. Inside the star, where
\langle\bar{q}q\rangle=0
, the solution
a=\pi f_{a}
satisfies the equation. Outside, we expand the potential:

V(a)\sim 2m_{q}\left<\overline{q}q\right>_{0}+\sqrt{2}\Lambda^{3}f_{a}|\lambda_{1}|+m_{a}^{2}a^{2}/2
with
m_{a}^{2}\equiv-m_{q}\left<\overline{q}q\right>_{0}/(2f_{a}^{2})-\sqrt{2}\Lambda^{3}|\lambda_{1}|/f_{a}
.
The radial equation becomes

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{2}\frac{da}{dr}\right)=m_{a}^{2}a,

(24)

with Yukawa-like solution

a(r)=C\frac{e^{-m_{a}r}}{r}.

Matching at
r=R_{\rm NS}
gives
C=\pi f_{a}R_{\rm NS}e^{m_{a}R_{\rm NS}}
. The full profile is

a(r)=\begin{cases}\pi f_{a},&r<R_{\rm NS},\\
\pi f_{a}\dfrac{R_{\rm NS}}{r}e^{-m_{a}(r-R_{\rm NS})},&r>R_{\rm NS}.\end{cases}

(25)

This profile has a Yukawa form, with effective “axion charge"
\pi f_{a}R_{\rm NS}

Hook and Huang 2018
;
Huang et al. 2019

Appendix B
Derivation of the Radius-Frequency Mapping

In this section, we derive the expression for the Radius-Frequency Mapping (RFM) presented in the main text. The key physics underlying RFM originates from the photon emission mechanism in neutron star magnetospheres. Neutron star radio emission is primarily attributed to curvature radiation from relativistic electrons, positrons, and ions. The characteristic frequency of curvature radiation is given by
Ruderman and Sutherland 1975
;
Lesch et al. 1994
;
Qiu et al. 2023

\omega_{c}=\frac{3\gamma^{3}}{2\rho},

(26)

where
\rho
is the curvature radius of the particle trajectory, and
\gamma
is the Lorentz factor. Typical values are
\gamma\sim 10^{2}\text{--}10^{3}

Ruderman and Sutherland 1975
;
Lesch et al. 1994
;
Qiu et al. 2023
.

To relate
\omega_{c}
to the radial distance
r
, we invoke two widely accepted assumptions. First, the magnetic field of the neutron star is approximated as a dipole
Radhakrishnan and Cooke 1969
;
Ruderman and Sutherland 1975
;
Xue et al. 2023
. Second, radiation originates from particles moving along the last open magnetic field lines
Ruderman and Sutherland 1975
;
Qiu et al. 2023
. Under these conditions, the curvature radius is
Qiu et al. 2023

\rho=\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{r_{e}r},

(27)

where
r_{e}
denotes the maximum radial extension of the last open field line. This is approximately given by

r_{e}\simeq\frac{P_{\rm NS}}{2\pi},

(28)

with
P_{\rm NS}
the neutron star rotation period. Substituting Eqs. (
27
) and (
28
) into Eq. (
26
), we obtain the mapping between frequency and emission radius:

\omega_{c}=\frac{9}{8}(2\pi)^{1/2}\gamma^{3}P_{\rm NS}^{-1/2}r^{-1/2}\simeq 2.82\frac{\gamma^{3}}{\sqrt{P_{\rm NS}r}}.

(29)

This relation encodes the key idea of RFM: higher-frequency radiation originates from lower altitudes in the neutron star magnetosphere.

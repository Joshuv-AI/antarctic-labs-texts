A height-zero type result for blocks of solvable groups
James P. Cossey

Abstract
. Let B B be a p p -block of a finite group G G with defect group D D . The more difficult direction of the recently proven height zero conjecture says that D D is abelian if every character in Irr ​ ( B ) \textup{Irr}(B) has height zero. We consider a smaller set than Irr ​ ( B ) \textup{Irr}(B) . In particular, if φ ∈ IBr p ​ ( B ) \varphi\in{\textup{IBr}}_{p}(B) , we let Irr ​ ( φ ) \textup{Irr}(\varphi) be the set of characters χ ∈ Irr ​ ( G ) \chi\in\textup{Irr}(G) such that φ \varphi is a constituent of χ o \chi^{o} . Now suppose G G is solvable and φ \varphi is a height zero Brauer character in some block B B of G G with defect group D D . Here we show that if every character in Irr ​ ( φ ) \textup{Irr}(\varphi) has height zero, then the defect group D D of the block containing φ \varphi is abelian for p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 and almost abelian for p = 2 p=2 or 3 3 . This has a nice consequence for primitive characters of p p -complements in solvable groups.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and
p
a prime. The Ito-Michler theorem
[
8
]
tells us that
G
has a normal abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup if and only if
p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}\chi(1)
for every character
\chi\in\textup{Irr}(G)
. Now define
\textup{Irr}_{p}(G)
to be the set of
\chi\in\textup{Irr}(G)
that have degree divisible by
p
. Thus, one can state the difficult direction of the Ito-Michler theorem as saying that if
|\textup{Irr}_{p}(G)|=0
, then
G
has a normal, abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup.

One can get a similar result by looking at a set smaller than
\textup{Irr}(G)
. If
B
is a block of
G
with defect group
D
, then the (recently proven, see
[
6
]
) height zero conjecture proves that
D
is abelian if and only if every character in
\textup{Irr}(B)
has height zero. If we define
\textup{Irr}_{p}(B)
as being the set of
\chi\in\textup{Irr}(B)
such that
p
has positive height, then we can restate the more difficult direction of the height zero conjecture as saying that if
|\textup{Irr}_{p}(B)|=0
, then
D
is abelian.

In this paper we show, for solvable groups, that one can look at an even smaller subset of
\textup{Irr}(G)
to determine if the defect group of the associated block is abelian, or at least almost abelian (in the sense of having a small derived length). Before we state our main result, we need some definitions.

Recall (see for instance
[
9
]
) that if
\chi\in\textup{Irr}(G)
, then there is a unique decomposition

\chi^{o}=\sum_{\varphi\in{\textup{IBr}}_{p}(G)}d_{\chi\varphi}\varphi,

where
\chi^{o}
denotes the restriction of
\chi
to the
p
-regular elements of
G
. Also, we let
h(\chi)
denote the height of the character
\chi
.

Definition 1.1
.

Fix a Brauer character
\varphi
in a
p
-block
B
of a group
G
. We define the set

\textup{Irr}(\varphi)=\{\chi\in\textup{Irr}(G)|d_{\chi\varphi}\neq 0\}.

We define

\textup{Irr}_{+}(\varphi)=\{\chi\in\textup{Irr}(\varphi)|h(\chi)>0\}.

Finally, we define
k_{+}(\varphi)=|\textup{Irr}_{+}(\varphi)|.

Of course, another way to put this is that
\textup{Irr}(\varphi)
consists of the irreducible constituents of the projective indecomposable character for
\varphi
. Our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
B
be a
p
-block of a solvable group
G
with defect group
D
. Suppose that
\varphi\in{\textup{IBr}}_{p}(B)
has height zero and that
k_{+}(\varphi)=0
. If
p\geq 5
, then
D
is abelian. If
p=2
, then
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq 2
, and if
p=3
, then
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq 3
.

We will in fact prove a generalization of this result, in terms of the positive height characters in
\textup{Irr}(\varphi)
- where
\varphi
has height zero - and the derived length of the defect group of the block containing
\varphi
.

Theorem
1.2
has an interesting corollary, which can be stated independently of block theory.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
H
be a
p
-complement of a solvable group
G
. Let
\alpha\in\textup{Irr}(H)
be primitive, and suppose that every constituent of
\alpha^{G}
has
p^{\prime}
-degree. If
S
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
, then
S
is abelian if
p\geq 5
. If
p=2
, then
\mathrm{dl}(S)\leq 2
. If
p=3
, then
\mathrm{dl}(S)\leq 3
.

We will actually prove slightly more here as well, replacing “
\alpha
is primitive” with “
\alpha
is a Fong character”.

Finally, we mention that our main tool in proving these results is the following “large orbit” theorem, which may prove to have other applications.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a solvable group that acts faithfully and completely reducibly on a finite module
V
of characteristic
p
. Suppose that

p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}|G:{\bf{C}}_{G}(v)|

for every
v\in V
. Let
S
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. If
p\geq 5
, then
S
is trivial. If
p=2
, then
S
is abelian. Finally, if
p=3
, then
\mathrm{dl}(S)\leq 2
.

As we will see, the proof of Theorem
1.4
follows easily from the work of Wolf, Gluck, and others.

2.
The proof of Theorem
1.4

Before we can prove Theorem
1.4
, we need a result on semilinear groups. Let
p
be a prime and
n\geq 1
. We let
V
be the vector space of dimension
n
over the field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of
p
elements. We may identify
V
with the field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
of
p^{n}
elements in the natural way. The semilinear group
\Gamma(p^{n})
has a normal subgroup
\Gamma_{K}
, where
\Gamma_{K}
acts on
V
via field multiplication, and
\Gamma(p^{n})/\Gamma_{K}
acts on
V
as the Galois group
Gal(\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
. If
G
is a subgroup of
\Gamma(p^{n})
, then we say
G
is semilinear, with the assumption that
G
acts on
V
in the same way. Thus
G
has a normal subgroup
K
such that
K
acts on
V
as field multiplication, and
G/K
acts on
V
as a subgroup of the Galois group
Gal(\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
. See
[
7
]
for more details.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a semilinear group, acting on
V=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
in the manner described above. Suppose that for every
v\in V
, we have that
p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}|G:{\bf{C}}_{G}(v)|
. Then
p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}|G|
.

Proof.

We let
K\lhd G
be the subgroup of
G
that acts as multiplication on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
, and thus
G/K
is a subgroup of
Gal(\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Write
{\overline{G}}=G/K
.

Notice that
K
acts regularly on
V
. Thus for any
v\in V
, we have that

K\cap{\bf{C}}_{G}(v)=1.

Thus we may consider
{\bf{C}}_{G}(v)
to be a subgroup of
{\overline{G}}
. By assumption,
{\bf{C}}_{G}(v)
contains the unique Sylow
p
-subgroup of
{\overline{G}}
. As this is true for every
v\in V
, then the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
\overline{G}
fixes all of
V\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
, and therefore is trivial.
∎

We now prove Theorem 1.4, which should be considered a “large orbit theorem”: if no orbit is “large enough” to have size divisible by
p
, then the
p
-part of the group must be “small”.

Proof.

We work by induction on
|G|+|V|
. First, suppose the action of
G
on
V
is reducible. Then we may write

V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\ldots V_{k}

for the homogeneous constituents of
V
, and it follows that we may write

G=G_{1}\times G_{2}\times\ldots G_{k},

where
G_{i}
acts faithfully and completely reducibly on
V_{i}
. For
v_{i}\in V_{i}
, we have that
|G_{i}:{\bf{C}}_{G_{i}}(v_{i})|
divides
|G:{\bf{C}}_{G}(v_{i})|
, and thus
p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}|G_{i}:{\bf{C}}_{G_{i}}(v_{i})|
. If
S_{i}
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G_{i}
, then the inductive hypothesis tells us that
S_{i}
is trivial if
p\geq 5
,
S_{i}
is abelian if
p=2
, and
\mathrm{dl}(S_{i})\leq 2
if
p=3
. Thus we are done in this case.

We now have that
V
is irreducible, and we assume that
V
is quasiprimitive. By Theorem 2.4 of
[
5
]
we have that either
G
is semilinear, or
p=3
and
G=GL(2,3)
or
SL(2,3)
. If
G
is semilinear, then Lemma
2.1
tells us that
p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}|G|
. In either case we are done.

Thus we now assume that
G
is not quasiprimitive on
V
. Let
C\lhd G
be maximal such that
V_{C}
is not homogeneous. Of course,
C<G
. Then (see Lemma 0.2 of
[
7
]
)
G/C
faithfully and primitively permutes the homogeneous constituents
W_{1},W_{2},\ldots W_{\ell}
of
V_{C}
. Write
L/C={\bf{O}}^{p^{\prime}}(G/C)
. If
G/L
is nontrivial, then
V_{L}
is a faithful, completely reducible
L
-module, and

p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}|L:{\bf{C}}_{L}(v)|

for every
v\in V
. As any Sylow
p
-subgroup of
L
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
, we are done if
L<G
.

Thus we have that
L=G
. As
C<G
, it follows that
p\hbox{\big|}|G:C|
. By Theorem 9.3 of
[
7
]
, it follows that
p=2
or
3
, and the
p
-part of
|G:C|
is either
2
or
3
. (Note we are now done if
p\geq 5
.) Thus
G/C
has an abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup. Moreover, by Theorem 9.3 of
[
7
]
, we have that
C/{\bf{C}}_{C}(W_{i})
acts transitively on the nonzero elements of
W_{i}
. By a theorem of Huppert (see for instance Theorem 6.8 of
[
7
]
),
C/{\bf{C}}_{C}(W_{i})
is either semilinear or one of a very short list of exceptional cases. If
C/{\bf{C}}_{C}(W_{i})
is semilinear, then Lemma
2.1
tells us a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
C/{\bf{C}}_{C}(W_{i})
is trivial. If
C/{\bf{C}}_{C}(W_{i})
is one of the exceptional cases in Huppert’s theorem, then either
p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}|C/{\bf{C}}_{C}(W_{i})|
, or
p=3
and
|C/{\bf{C}}_{C}(W_{i})|_{p}=3
, and thus a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
C/{\bf{C}}_{C}(W_{i})
is abelian. In this case we see that
C
has an abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup. In all cases, then, we are done.
∎

3.
Bounding the derived length of
D

We need an easy lemma regarding characters in
\textup{Irr}(\varphi)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be solvable and
N\lhd G
. Let
\varphi
be in
{\textup{IBr}}_{p}(G)
and suppose
\theta\in{\textup{IBr}}_{p}(N)
lies under
\varphi
. If
\psi\in\textup{Irr}(\theta)
, then some constituent of
\psi^{G}
is in
\textup{Irr}(\varphi)
.

Proof.

By assumption,
\theta
is a constituent of
\psi^{o}
. Also,
\varphi
is a constituent of
\theta^{G}
. Thus
\varphi
is a constituent of
(\psi^{G})^{o}=(\psi^{o})^{G}
, and therefore some constituent
\chi
of
\psi^{G}
must contain
\varphi
.
∎

We here prove a more general version of Theorem
1.2
. The following result includes Theorem
1.2
, and the proof very much mirrors the argument in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
B
be a block of a solvable group
G
with defect group
D
. Let
\varphi\in{\textup{IBr}}_{p}(B)
have height zero. Then

\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq k_{+}(\varphi)+c,

where
c=1
if
p\geq 5
,
c=2
if
p=2
, and
c=3
if
p=3
.

Proof.

Let
N={{\bf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}}(G)
, and consider a counterexample with
|G:N|
minimal. Suppose
\alpha\in\textup{Irr}(N)
is covered by
B
, and let
T
be the stabilizer of
\alpha
in
G
. By the Fong-Reynolds theorem (see Theorem 9.14 of
[
9
]
, for example), there is a block
B_{1}
of
T
with defect group conjugate to
D
such that induction is a height preserving bijection from
B_{1}
to
B
that also preserves decomposition numbers.

Thus without loss of generality, we may assume
\alpha
is invariant in
G
. By Fong’s theorem (see 10.20 of
[
9
]
) we have that
D
is a full Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
, and
B
consists of all of the characters (ordinary and Brauer) that lie over
\alpha
. As
\varphi
has height zero, it follows that
p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}\varphi(1)
.

Moreover, by applying a character triple isomorphism, we may assume
N
is central in
G
, and if we set
M/N={{\bf{O}}_{p}}(G/N)
, then

{\bf{F}}(G)=M=N\times P

and
{\bf{F}}(G/N)=M/N\cong P
.

We first show that
P
must be abelian. Assume not. By Lemma 5.1 of
[
1
]
we have that
NP^{\prime}/P^{\prime}={{\bf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}}(G/P^{\prime})
. Let
{\widehat{\alpha}}
denote the unique
p^{\prime}
-special extension of
\alpha
to
NP^{\prime}
, so that we may consider
{\widehat{\alpha}}
to be a character of
NP^{\prime}/P^{\prime}
. Note that
{\widehat{\alpha}}
is invariant in
G/P^{\prime}
. We let
b^{\prime}
be the block of
G/P^{\prime}
lying over
{\widehat{\alpha}}
. As
P^{\prime}
is in the kernel of
\varphi
, we may consider
\varphi
as a character in
{\textup{IBr}}_{p}(b^{\prime})
. Note that
p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}\varphi(1)
, and thus
D/P^{\prime}
is a defect group of
b^{\prime}
. We let
k_{+}^{\prime}(\varphi)
denote the number of characters of positive height in
\textup{Irr}(\varphi)\cap\textup{Irr}(G/P^{\prime})
. As
G
was chosen so that
|G/N|
is minimal, we see that

dl(D/P^{\prime})\leq k_{+}^{\prime}(\varphi)+c.

Let
{\mathrm{cd}}_{p}(P)
be the number of distinct nonlinear character degrees of
P
. By Taketa’s theorem, we have that

\mathrm{dl}(P)-1\leq{\mathrm{cd}}_{p}(P).

Suppose
\beta\in\textup{Irr}(P)
is nonlinear. By Lemma
3.1
, we know there exists a character
\chi\in\textup{Irr}(\varphi)
lying above
\alpha\times\beta\in\textup{Irr}(M)
. Of course,
p\hbox{\big|}\chi(1)
, so
\chi
is counted by
k_{+}(\varphi)
, but
\chi
is not counted by
k_{+}^{\prime}(\varphi)
. Thus there are at least
{\mathrm{cd}}_{p}(P)
characters counted by
k_{+}(\varphi)
that are not counted by
k_{+}^{\prime}(\varphi)
.

We now have

k_{+}(\varphi)\geq k_{+}^{\prime}(\varphi)+{\mathrm{cd}}_{p}(P)\geq\mathrm{dl}(D/P^{\prime})-c+\mathrm{dl}(P)-1,

which in turn is equal to

\mathrm{dl}(D/P^{\prime})-c+\mathrm{dl}(P^{\prime})\geq\mathrm{dl}(D)-c,

and we are done if
P
is not abelian.

Thus we now assume that
P
is abelian. Consider the block
\bar{B}
of
\bar{G}=G/P
containing
\varphi
. As
\varphi
has
p^{\prime}
-degree, then
D/P
is a defect group for
{\bar{B}}
. As
G
was a minimal counterexample, we see that if
{\bar{k}}_{+}(\varphi)
denotes the number of characters in
\textup{Irr}_{+}(\varphi)
with
P
in the kernel, then

\mathrm{dl}(D/P)\leq{\bar{k}}_{+}(\varphi)+c.

Again, we have that
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq\mathrm{dl}(D/P)+1
. Thus, to show that
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq k_{+}(\varphi)+c
, it is enough to show that there is a character in
\textup{Irr}_{+}(\varphi)
not counted by
{\bar{k}}_{+}(\varphi)
, i.e. that does not have
P
in the kernel.

By Gaschutz’ theorem, we see that
G/M
acts faithfully and completely reducibly on
\textup{Irr}(K)
, where
K
is a nontrivial elementary abelian factor group of the
p
-group
M/N\cong P
. By Lemma
3.1
, if
\lambda\in\textup{Irr}(K)
, then there exists a character in
\textup{Irr}(G|\alpha\times\lambda)\cap\textup{Irr}(\varphi)
. If every character in
\textup{Irr}_{+}(\varphi)
was counted by
{\bar{k}}_{+}(\varphi)
, then we would have
p\hbox{/}\kern-5.0pt\hbox{\big|}|G:G_{\lambda}|
for every
\lambda\in\textup{Irr}(K)
. By Theorem
1.4
, it then follows that
D/P
has derived length zero if
p\geq 5
, at most one if
p=2
, and at most two if
p=3
. As
P
is abelian, it follows that
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq 1
if
p\geq 5
,
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq 2
if
p=2
, and
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq 3
if
p=3
. But this contradicts that
G
was a counterexample, and we are done.
∎

Recall that if
G
is solvable with
p
-complement
H
, and
\varphi\in{\textup{IBr}}_{p}(G)
, then a Fong character associated with
\varphi
is any character of minimal degree in
\varphi_{H}
. Fong characters have a number of interesting properties. For instance, if
\alpha\in\textup{Irr}(H)
is a Fong character for
\varphi
, then
\alpha(1)=\varphi(1)_{p^{\prime}}
. Also, the constituents of
\alpha^{G}
are exactly the characters in
\textup{Irr}(\varphi)
. See
[
4
]

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G
be solvable and let
H
be a
p
-complement in
G
. Suppose that
\alpha\in\textup{Irr}(H)
is a Fong character for some character
\varphi\in{\textup{IBr}}_{p}(G)
, and that every constituent of
\alpha^{G}
has
p^{\prime}
-degree. Let
S
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Then
S
is abelian if
p\geq 5
,
\mathrm{dl}(S)\leq 2
if
p=2
, and
\mathrm{dl}(S)\leq 3
if
p=3
.

Proof.

Our assumption that every constituent
\chi
of
\alpha^{G}
has
p^{\prime}
-degree means that every character in
\textup{Irr}(\varphi)
, including the canonical lift of
\varphi
, has
p^{\prime}
-degree. Thus
\varphi
has
p^{\prime}
-degree and height zero, and the defect group for the block
B
containing
\varphi
is a full Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. By Theorem
1.2
, we get the desired bounds on the derived length of
S
.
∎

We mention another useful fact about Fong characters in solvable groups: all primitive characters of the
p
-complement
H
are Fong characters
[
4
]
. Thus Corollary
1.3
follows immediately from Corollary
3.3
.

4.
Examples and questions

One might ask if we can do better than the statement of Theorem
1.2
. Let
\varphi
be the unique linear Brauer character in the unique
2
-block
B
of
S_{4}
. Each character in
\textup{Irr}(\varphi)
has height zero, yet the defect group for
B
is the nonabelian Sylow
2
-subgroup of
S_{4}
. Thus we cannot reduce our bound of
\mathrm{dl}(D)\leq 2
for
p=2
.

It is unclear if the bound of
\mathrm{dl}(D)=3
can be achieved for
p=3
. If so, it likely comes from a block of a group
G
defined in some sense by the exceptional case in Theorem 9.3 of
[
7
]
.

It is also unclear to what extent this result extends to nonsolvable groups. Of course, Wolf and Gluck extended their proof of the height zero conjecture for solvable groups to
p
-solvable groups in
[
2
]
. Perhaps similar techniques could allow us to extend our result to
p
-solvable groups.

For symmetric or alternating groups, the path forward seems a bit more clear. While the degrees of Brauer characters in symmetric groups are notoriously difficult to compute, it seems possible we may dispense with the requirement that
\varphi
has height zero, and rather require that every character in
\textup{Irr}(\varphi)
has height zero. As the heights of characters in
\textup{Irr}(S_{n})
are easy to understand combinatorially, we then need only show that if all of the partitions that “
p
-regularize” to
\varphi
have height zero, then the weight of the associated block must be less than
p
. This would force the corresponding defect group to be abelian.

While we are not comfortable conjecturing that a bound such as that in Theorem
1.2
holds for all groups, we think exploring this idea merits further study. Specifically, what information (if any) does knowing
k_{+}(\varphi)
tell us about the corresponding defect group for arbitrary finite groups?

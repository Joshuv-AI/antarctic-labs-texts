Abstract
. In this paper, we compute all possible Jordan types of linear forms ℓ \ell in any full Perazzo algebra A A . In some cases we are also able to compute the corresponding Jordan degree type, which is a finer invariant.



1.
introduction

For any graded Artinian algebra
A
, if we consider an element
\ell
in the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=\oplus_{i\geq 1}A_{i}
, the multiplication map by
\ell
in a finite-length module
M
over
A
is a nilpotent endomorphism.
The Jordan type of
\ell
in
M
gives the sizes of the Jordan blocks of the multiplication by
\ell
, seen as a linear map.
In the most common setup one takes
M=A
. Recall that the Hilbert function of
A
is a finite sequence of positive integers
h=(h_{0},\dots,h_{d})
, where
h_{i}=\dim_{K}A_{i}
, called
h
-vector
.
It turns out that, for a given
h
-vector
there are many configurations of the possible Jordan types that can occur.
In
[
12
]

A. Iarrobino, C. McDaniel, and the first author
give a survey of the subject. They also define another invariant, called
Jordan degree type

[
12
, Definition 2.28]
, which is finer than the Jordan type.
It is important to recall that finding all possible Jordan types, that is, finding all possible canonical Jordan matrices associated to multiplication maps is not always an easy task.
In some cases, though, one can obtain maximum and minimum possible Jordan types with respect to the dominance partial order (Definition
2.6
).
In this paper we study the Jordan type associated to the multiplication map by a linear form
\ell\in A_{1}
in a standard graded Artinian Gorenstein algebra
A
. For full Perazzo algebras (cf. A. Cerminara et al.
[
4
]
, and
the last two authors’ paper

[
17
]
) only one case is known (see N. Abdallah et al.
[
1
]
).
We first compute the Jordan type of any linear form in the full Perazzo algebra whose Macaulay dual generator is the classic Perazzo form
F=X_{0}Y_{1}^{2}+X_{1}Y_{1}Y_{2}+X_{2}Y_{2}^{2}\in K[X_{0},X_{1},X_{2},Y_{1},Y_{2}]
(Example
3.1
) and then we compute all possible linear Jordan types in any full Perazzo algebra, except for a set of linear forms
(iii)
, for which we give the whole range of possible linear Jordan types, with respect to the dominance partial order (Corollary
3.4
).
It is worth mentioning that the Jordan type of a linear form
\ell
in an Artinian algebra
A
determines whether or not
\ell
is a Lefschetz element for
A
(see Remark 3 and Proposition 14 in T. Harima and J. Watanabe’s paper
[
10
]
). This paper aims to contribute to the classification of Perazzo algebras with the weak Lefschetz property (WLP), inasmuch as it can help computing all possible linear Jordan types of different families of Perazzo algebras arising from full Perazzo algebras (
[
17
, Remark 4.10]
).

2.
Background material

Throughout this paper
K
will be an infinite field.
In this section we fix notations, and recall the basic facts on Hilbert functions, Artinian Gorenstein algebras, Lefschetz properties, as well as on Jordan type and full Perazzo algebras needed in the paper.

2.1.
Hilbert functions of Artinian Gorenstein algebras

Given any standard graded
K
-algebra
A=R/I
where
R=K[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
and
I
is a homogeneous ideal of
R
,
we denote by
HF_{A}:\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
with
HF_{A}(j)=\dim_{K}A_{j}

its
Hilbert function
. If
A
is Artinian, then its Hilbert function is
captured in a finite sequence of positive integers, the so-called
h
-vector

h=(h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{d})
, where
h_{i}:=HF_{A}(i)>0
and
d
is the last index with this property. The
Sperner
number
S_{A}
of
A
is the maximum value of the
h
-vector, i.e.
S_{A}=\max_{i\in\{0,\dots,d\}}\{h_{i}\}
. We refer to the number of variables of
R
as the
codimension
of
A
. The
socle
of
A
is defined as
\Soc(A):=(0:\mathfrak{m})_{A}
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
.

Recall that the depth of a ring is bounded above by its Krull dimension.
Then, a standard graded Artinian algebra
A=R/I
is Cohen Macaulay and its type equals
\dim_{K}\Soc(A)
.
So, such an
A
is Gorenstein if and only if its socle is one-dimensional
[
3
, Theorem 3.2.10]
.
In this situation, since
A_{d}
is always contained in
\Soc(A)
,
A_{d}=\Soc(A)
and the integer
d
is called the
socle degree of

A
.
Moreover, the natural pairing given by multiplication
A_{i}\times A_{d-i}\rightarrow A_{d}\cong K
is perfect and hence it yields an isomorphism
A_{i}^{*}\cong A_{d-i}
. So the
h
-vector for such algebras is symmetric. Hereafter, we will assume the characteristic of
K
to be either zero or greater than
d
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
h
-vector
h=(h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{d})
of a graded Artinian
K
-algebra is said to be
unimodal
if
h_{0}\leq h_{1}\leq\cdots\leq h_{j}\geq h_{j+1}\geq\cdots\geq h_{d}
for some
j
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
compressed
Artinian Gorenstein algebra of codimension
c
and socle degree
d
is one for which the Hilbert function is as big as possible. Then, its Hilbert function is

\Bigl(1,\tbinom{c}{c-1},\dots,\tbinom{\frac{d}{2}+c-1}{c-1},\dots,\tbinom{c}{c-1},1\Bigr)

in case
d
is even, and of the form

\Bigl(1,\tbinom{c}{c-1},\dots,\tbinom{\frac{d-1}{2}+c-1}{c-1},\tbinom{\frac{d-1}{2}+c-1}{c-1},\dots,\tbinom{c}{c-1},1\Bigr)

if
d
is odd, thanks to the symmetry of the
h
-vector of any Artinian Gorenstein algebra.

2.2.
Artinian Gorenstein algebras and Macaulay-Matlis duality

In this subsection we quickly recall the construction of the standard graded Artinian Gorenstein algebra associated to a given form.

Let
R=K[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}]
be a polynomial ring and let
S=K_{\mathrm{DP}}[X_{0},\ldots,X_{N}]
be a divided power algebra, regarded as an
R
-module with the contraction action
[
11
, Appendix A]

x_{i}\circ X_{0}^{j_{0}}\cdots X_{i}^{j_{i}}\cdots X_{N}^{j_{N}}=\begin{cases}X_{0}^{j_{0}}\cdots X_{i}^{j_{i}-1}\cdots X_{N}^{j_{N}}&\text{if}\ j_{i}>0\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{cases}

If
I\subset R
is a homogeneous
ideal, the
Macaulay’s inverse system

I^{-1}
for
I
is the following
R
-submodule of
S

I^{-1}:=\{F\in S,f\circ F=0\text{ for all }f\in I\}.

Conversely, if
M\subset S
is a graded
R
-submodule,
then

\Ann(M):=\{f\in R:f\circ F=0\text{ for all }F\in M\}

is a homogeneous ideal in
R
. It is well known that there is a bijective correspondence

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\{\text{Homogeneous ideals }I\subset R\}&\rightleftharpoons&\{\text{Graded }R\text{-submodules of }S\}\\
I&\rightarrow&I^{-1}\\
\Ann(M)&\leftarrow&M\end{array}

Moreover,
I^{-1}
is a finitely generated
R
-module if and only if
R/I
is an Artinian ring.
For any integer
t\geq 0
we have
HF_{A}(t)=\dim_{K}(R/I)_{t}=\dim_{K}(I^{-1})_{t}
.
As a particular case, an Artinian
K
-algebra
A=R/I
is Gorenstein with socle degree
d
if and only if
I=\Ann_{R}(F)=\{f\in R\mid f\circ F=0\}
for some homogeneous polynomial
F\in S_{d}
.
This polynomial, called the
Macaulay dual generator
for
A
, is unique up to a scalar multiple. In this context, since
A
is Gorenstein, we have

\displaystyle HF_{A}(t)

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}(A_{t})=\dim_{K}(A_{d-t})=\dim_{K}(I^{-1})_{d-t}

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}\left\langle x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}\circ F:i_{0}+\cdots+i_{N}=t\right\rangle.

Remark 2.3
.

When
K
is a field of characteristic zero, the action just defined can be thought of as differentiation
[
11
, Example A.5, Appendix A]
. In this context, and as a direct application of Macaulay-Matlis duality, for any integer
t\geq 0
the Artinian Gorenstein algebra associated to the
homogeneous symmetric
polynomial
h_{t}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n}):=\sum_{i_{0}+\cdots+i_{n}=t}X_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}
in the usual polynomial ring
K[X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}]
(see
[
2
, Section 2]
) is a compressed algebra
[
2
, Theorem 2.11]
,
[
7
, Theorem 5.6]
. Note that in the divided-power ring
{S=K_{\mathrm{DP}}[X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}]}
this polynomial takes the form

h_{t}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n})=\sum_{i_{0}+\cdots+i_{n}=t}i_{0}!\cdots i_{n}!X_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}.

2.3.
Jordan type of a graded Artinian algebra

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A
be a graded Artinian algebra. The
Jordan type
of a linear form
\ell\in A_{1}
in
A
is the partition of
\dim_{K}A
, denoted
P_{\ell,A}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{s})
with
p_{1}\geq\cdots\geq p_{s}
, where
p_{1},\dots,p_{s}
represent the block sizes in the Jordan canonical form of the multiplication map
\times\ell:A\longrightarrow A
. A basis
\mathcal{B}=\{\ell^{i}z_{k}:1\leq k\leq s,\ 0\leq i\leq p_{k}-1\}
of
A
, as a vector space over
k
, is called a
pre-Jordan
basis. A
string
is a sequence

S_{k}:=(z_{k},\ell z_{k},\dots,\ell^{p_{k}-1}z_{k})

of
\mathcal{B}
. A
bead
is an element
\ell^{i}z_{k}
in a string
S_{k}
. A
Jordan basis
for
\ell
is a pre-Jordan basis
\mathcal{B}
satisfying
\ell^{p_{k}}z_{k}=0
for each
k\in\{1,\dots,s\}
. It is well known that one can construct a Jordan basis from any pre-Jordan basis
[
12
, Lemma 2.2 (iii)]
. Let
\nu_{k}
be the degree of
z_{k}
, then the sequence

\mathcal{S}_{\ell,A}=((p_{1},\nu_{1}),\dots,(p_{s},\nu_{s}))

is an invariant of
(A,\ell)

[
12
, Lemma 2.2(iv)]
. It is the
Jordan degree type
of
A
with respect to
\ell
.

We illustrate the definition with an example.

Example 2.5
.

Let
A=K[x,y]/(x^{3},xy^{2},y^{3})
. A basis for
A
is, for instance,

\{1,x,y,x^{2},y^{2},xy,x^{2}y\}.

So the
h
-vector is
(1,2,3,1)
and
\dim_{K}A=7
. Consider the multiplication map

\times\ell:A\rightarrow A,

where
\ell=x+y
. We have the following pre-Jordan basis

\displaystyle 1\xmapsto{\ell}x+y\xmapsto{\ell}

\displaystyle x^{2}+2xy+y^{2}\xmapsto{\ell}3x^{2}y

\displaystyle x\xmapsto{\ell}

\displaystyle x^{2}+xy

\displaystyle y^{2}

because
x^{2}+xy\xmapsto{\ell}2x^{2}y
and
y^{2}\xmapsto{\ell}0
. But any pre-Jordan basis yields a Jordan basis, as we already pointed out in Definition
2.4
. Indeed, although the last bead in the second string does not yield zero when multiplied by
\ell
, notice that
2\ell^{3}-3\ell^{2}x=0
, i.e.
\ell^{2}(2\ell-3x)=0
, so we could have considered, instead, the following string

2\ell-3x=2y-x\xmapsto{\ell}2y^{2}+xy-x^{2}\xmapsto{\ell}0.

Thus, a Jordan basis is composed by the following strings

\{(1,\ell,\ell^{2},\ell^{3}),(2y-x,2y^{2}+xy-x^{2}),(y^{2})\}.

Therefore
P_{\ell,A}=(4,2,1)
. Moreover, since the strings start at degrees
0
,
1
, and
2
, the Jordan degree type is

\mathcal{S}_{\ell,A}=((4,0),(2,1),(1,2)),

which is usually denoted
\mathcal{S}_{\ell,A}=(4_{0},2_{1},1_{2})
.

Now let us define a partial order, called
dominance
, for partitions of positive numbers.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
P=(p_{1},\dots,p_{s})
and
Q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{r})
, with
p_{1}\geq\cdots\geq p_{s}
and
q_{1}\geq\cdots\geq q_{r}
, be two partitions of a positive integer
n
. We say that
P
dominates
Q
(written
P\geq Q
) if, for each
k\in\{1,\dots,\min\{s,r\}\}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}q_{i}.

Given a graded Artinian algebra
A
, we will say that
\ell\in A_{1}
is
general
when it belongs to the open dense subset
U\subseteq A_{1}
of linear forms whose Jordan type is the maximum with respect to the dominance order – this definition is the reason we assumed that
K
is infinite.
The
generic linear Jordan type
of
A
, denoted
P_{A}
, is the Jordan type
P_{\ell,A}
where
\ell
is a general linear form (cf.
[
12
, Lemma 2.54, Definition 2.55]
, where the notation for generic linear Jordan type is
P_{1,A}
, to distinguish it from the so called generic Jordan type of a general element of the maximal ideal). For instance, the form
\ell=x+y
in Example
2.5
is general. Certainly, there is no linear form whose Jordan type in
A
is
(4,3)
because there would not be strings of length two, which is absurd.

Notation 2.7
.

Whenever a length is repeated in a Jordan type, we will use exponentiation in order to abbreviate notation. For instance, if

\mathcal{S}_{\ell,A}=((4,0),(4,1),(4,1),(3,1),(3,1),(1,3),(1,3)),

we will write
P_{\ell,A}=(4^{3},3^{2},1^{2})
and
\mathcal{S}_{\ell,A}=(4_{0},4_{1}^{2},3_{1}^{2},1_{3}^{2})
.

It is important to notice that computing all possible linear Jordan types for any Artinian algebra is not feasible. However, if we restrict to Perazzo algebras, which are Gorenstein, there is one paper (see
[
1
, Section 4.2]
) where the authors compute all the linear Jordan types for certain Perazzo algebras, namely, the ones associated to Perazzo threefolds in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
having the smallest possible
h
-vector.

2.4.
Perazzo algebras

Definition 2.8
.

Fix integers
n,m\geq 2
. A
Perazzo
hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+m}
of degree
d\geq 3
is a hypersurface defined by a form

F=X_{0}P_{0}+X_{1}P_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}P_{n}+G\in K[X_{0},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1}\dots,Y_{m}]_{d},

where
P_{0},\ldots,P_{n}\in K[Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}]_{d-1}
are algebraically dependent but linearly independent and
G\in K[Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}]_{d}
. By abuse of terminology, we call
F
a
Perazzo
form. A
Perazzo
algebra
A_{F}
is a standard graded Artinian Gorenstein algebra whose Macaulay dual generator is a Perazzo form
F
of degree
d\geq 3
.

The linear independence of the forms
P_{i}
guarantees that
V(F)
is not a cone, while the algebraic dependence and the fact that
n+m\geq 4
guarantee that the Hessian matrix of
F
vanishes identically
[
8
]
(see
[
13
]
and
[
18
]
for a modern version of Gordan and Noether’s results).
Perazzo hypersurfaces are the first known with vanishing Hessian that are not cones.
Artinian Gorenstein algebras associated to Perazzo forms have been deeply studied from the viewpoint of the Lefschetz properties (check
[
9
, Definition 3.1 and Definition 3.8]
for the definition).
The importance of studying Perazzo algebras stems from the fact that they cannot have the strong Lefschetz property (SLP), because
\det(\Hess(F))\equiv 0
(see
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
), but they can still satisfy the WLP. In
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
the authors characterize certain classes of Perazzo algebras with the WLP.

Definition 2.9
.

A Perazzo algebra
A_{F}
is said to be
full
if
\{P_{0},\dots,P_{n}\}
is a basis of
K[Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}]_{d-1}
. So, for full Perazzo algebras we necessarily have
{n+1=\binom{d+m-2}{m-1}}
. In this case, and in order to ease the comprehension of the main result in this paper, we will assume, after a change of variables, that
F=\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}=d-1}X_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}Y_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots Y_{m}^{i_{m}}
.

It is well known that, for any
d\geq 4
, the Hilbert function of a full Perazzo algebra
A_{F}
is
[
4
, Proposition 3.3]

(2.1)

HF_{A_{F}}(i)=\begin{cases}\binom{i+m-1}{m-1}+\binom{d-i+m-1}{m-1}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor\\
\\
\text{symmetry}.\end{cases}

Thus,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\dim_{k}A_{F}

\displaystyle=2+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\Bigl(\tbinom{i+m-1}{m-1}+\tbinom{d-i+m-1}{m-1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=2+2\sum_{j=m}^{d+m-2}\tbinom{j}{m-1}

\displaystyle=2+2\Bigl(\tbinom{d+m-1}{m}-1\Bigr)

\displaystyle=2\tbinom{d+m-1}{m}.

If
d=3

(2.3)

HF_{A_{F}}=(1,n+m+1,n+m+1,1)\quad\text{ and }\quad\dim_{K}A_{F}=2(n+m+2).

3.
Jordan type of a full Perazzo algebra

In this section we compute all possible linear Jordan types in any full Perazzo algebra.
Let

R:=K[x_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{m}},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\ :\ i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}=d-1]

and let

S:=K_{\mathrm{DP}}[X_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{m}},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}\ :\ i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}=d-1]

be a divided power algebra, regarded as an
R
-module with the contraction action. Let
A_{F}=R/\Ann_{R}F
be a full Perazzo algebra whose Macaulay dual generator
F\in S_{d}
is in the canonical form, i.e.
F=\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}=d-1}X_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}Y_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots Y_{m}^{i_{m}}
. The goal of this section is to compute the Jordan type of any linear form

\ell=\sum a_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}x_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}+\sum b_{j}y_{j}\in[A_{F}]_{1}.

Let us start with a toy example.

Example 3.1
.

Let
F=X_{2,0}Y_{1}^{2}+X_{1,1}Y_{1}Y_{2}+X_{0,2}Y_{2}^{2}\in S_{3}
. Notice that
(x_{2,0},x_{1,1},x_{0,2})^{2}\subset\Ann_{R}F
and for any
g\in K[y_{1},y_{2}]_{t}
we have:
g\in\Ann_{R}F
if and only if
t\geq 3
. Moreover, by (
2.3
), we know that the
h
-vector of
A_{F}:=R/\Ann_{R}F
is
(1,5,5,1)
and hence
\dim_{K}(A_{F})=12
.

We will prove that the possible Jordan types of a form
\ell\in[A_{F}]_{1}
in
A_{F}
are in the following chain with respect to the dominance order.

(2^{3},1^{6})<(2^{4},1^{4})<(3^{2},2^{2},1^{2})<(4,2^{3},1^{2}).

In fact, we have the following possibilities:

(a)

If
\ell=b_{1}y_{1}+b_{2}y_{2}
or
\ell=a_{1,1}x_{1,1}+a_{0,2}x_{0,2}+b_{1}y_{1}
, with
b_{1}\neq 0
(resp.
\ell=b_{1}y_{1}+b_{2}y_{2}
or
\ell=a_{2,0}x_{2,0}+a_{1,1}x_{1,1}+b_{2}y_{2}
, with
b_{2}\neq 0
), we can assume, without loss of generality, that
\ell=b_{1}y_{1}+b_{2}y_{2}
or
\ell=a_{1,1}x_{1,1}+a_{0,2}x_{0,2}+b_{1}y_{1}
, with
b_{1}\not=0
. Then
P_{\ell,A_{F}}=(3^{2},2^{2},1^{2})
and
\mathcal{S}_{\ell,A_{F}}=(3_{0},3_{1},2_{1}^{2},1_{1},1_{2})
. Indeed, we have the following pre-Jordan basis.

Then the Jordan type of
\ell

is one of the following

(i)

P_{\ell,A_{F}}=\Bigl(d^{2},(d-1)^{2\binom{m-1}{m-2}},(d-2)^{2\binom{m}{m-2}},\dots,1^{2\binom{m+d-3}{m-2}}\Bigr)

, and starts at degree one. Therefore, there are now
2\binom{m-1}{m-2}+1=2m-1
strings of length
d-1
starting at degree one. Finally, we may check the remaining parts of the Jordan degree type exactly as we did for the case
(i)
.

(iii)

If
\ell=\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}=d-1}a_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}x_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}
, we have
\ell^{2}\subset\Ann_{R}F
and therefore there are only strings of lengths one and two, which means that the Jordan type is

P_{\ell,A_{F}}=\bigl(2^{a},1^{b}\bigr)
where
2a+b=\dim_{K}A_{F}
and
a=\dim_{K}A_{\ell\circ F}
. In order to obtain
P_{\ell,A_{F}}
it suffices to compute the number of length two strings, that is,
\dim_{K}(A_{\ell\circ F})
.

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

The number of parts in a generic linear Jordan type of a full Perazzo algebra is

\displaystyle 1+2m-1+2\sum_{i=0}^{d-3}\tbinom{m+i}{m-2}

\displaystyle=2m+2\sum_{i=m}^{d+m-3}\tbinom{i}{m-2}

\displaystyle=2m+2\biggl(\sum_{i=0}^{d+m-3}\tbinom{i}{m-2}-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tbinom{i}{m-2}\biggr)

\displaystyle=2m+2\Bigl(\tbinom{d+m-2}{m-1}-\tbinom{m}{m-1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=2m+2(n+1-m)

\displaystyle=2(n+1).\quad\square

Corollary 3.4
.

All those Jordan types are in the following chain with respect to the dominance order:

\displaystyle\bigl(2^{a_{min}},1^{b_{max}}\bigr)<\cdots

\displaystyle<\bigl(2^{a_{max}},1^{b_{min}}\bigr)

\displaystyle<\bigl(d^{2},(d-1)^{2\binom{m-1}{m-2}},(d-2)^{2\binom{m}{m-2}},\dots,1^{2\binom{m+d-3}{m-2}}\bigr)

\displaystyle<\bigl(d+1,(d-1)^{2m-1},(d-2)^{2\binom{m}{m-2}},\dots,1^{2\binom{m+d-3}{m-2}}\bigr).

where
a_{min}=d
,

(3.9)

a_{max}=\begin{cases}\binom{\frac{d-1}{2}+m-1}{m-1}+2\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\binom{i+m-1}{m-1}\quad\text{if }d\text{ is odd}\\
\\
2\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\binom{i+m-1}{m-1}\quad\text{if }d\text{ is even},\end{cases}

b_{max}=\dim_{K}(A_{F})-a_{min}
,
b_{min}=\dim_{K}(A_{F})-a_{max}
.

Proof.

The chain is clear, due to the Jordan types just obtained. Now, notice that
A_{\ell\circ F}
is Gorenstein
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
, Artinian and with socle degree
d-1
. So, in the case
(iii)
there exists
G\in K[Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}]_{d-1}
such that
A_{\ell\circ F}\cong\frac{K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]}{\Ann_{K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]}G}
. Moreover, for any
h
-vector associated to such an algebra there exists

\ell=\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}=d-1}a_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}x_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}

such that
\ell\circ F=G
(take
F=LG
, where
\ell\circ L=1
). So, there is
G_{max}
whose associated Gorenstein algebra
A_{G_{max}}
is a compressed Gorenstein algebra of codimension
m
and socle degree
d-1
(take for instance the homogeneous symmetric polynomial of degree
d-1
, i.e.
G_{max}:=h_{d-1}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m})
) and hence the number of length two strings is the cited in (
3.9
). Also there exists a form
G_{min}
whose associated Gorenstein algebra
A_{G_{min}}
(take for instance
G_{min}=Y_{m}^{d-1}
) has the minimum
h
-vector, i.e.
(1,1,\dots,1)
, and hence
a_{min}=d
.
∎

In particular, for
m=2
we have the following result.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

F=X_{d-1,0}Y_{1}^{d-1}+X_{d-2,1}Y_{1}^{d-1}Y_{2}+\cdots+X_{1,d-2}Y_{1}Y_{2}^{d-2}+X_{0,d-1}Y_{2}^{d-1}\in S_{d}

and consider the linear form

\ell=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}a_{d-1-i,i}x_{d-1-i,i}\in[A_{F}]_{1}.

Then the
h
-vector of
A_{\ell\circ F}
is

(1,2,\dots,s-1,s,s,\dots,s,s,s-1,\dots,2,1),

where

s=rk\begin{pmatrix}a_{d-1,0}&a_{d-2,1}&\cdots&a_{d-1-r,r}\\
a_{d-2,1}&a_{d-3,2}&\cdots&a_{d-2-r,r+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{r,d-1-r}&a_{r-1,d-r}&\cdots&a_{0,d-1}\end{pmatrix}

with

r:=\begin{cases}\frac{d-1}{2}\ \text{if }d\text{ is odd}\\
\frac{d-2}{2}\ \text{if }d\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Notice that
\Ann_{R}(\ell\circ F)\in K[y_{1},y_{2}]
and hence
A_{\ell\circ F}
is a complete intersection because it is Artinian, Gorenstein and has codimension two. As a consequence, the
h
-vector is the cited one, with
s=\dim_{K}\bigl[A_{\ell\circ F}\bigr]_{r}
. Now, using Macaulay-Matlis duality, we can compute
s
as the dimension of the vector subspace of partials of
\ell\circ F
of degree
r
, i.e.

s=\dim\left\langle\begin{matrix}a_{d-1,0}Y_{1}^{r}+a_{d-2,1}Y_{1}^{r-1}Y_{2}+\cdots+a_{d-1-r,r}Y_{2}^{r},\\
a_{d-2,1}Y_{1}^{r}+a_{d-3,2}Y_{1}^{r-1}Y_{2}+\cdots+a_{d-2-r,r+1}Y_{2}^{r},\\
\vdots\\
a_{r,d-1-r}Y_{1}^{r}+a_{r-1,d-r}Y_{1}^{r-1}Y_{2}+\cdots+a_{0,d-1}Y_{2}^{r}\end{matrix}\right\rangle.

∎

Remark 3.6

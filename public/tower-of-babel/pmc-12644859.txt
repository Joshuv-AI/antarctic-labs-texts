A fuzzy-microeconomics model for priority-based content centric routing in internet of things
Authors: Jin Xu, Qunying Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe massive usage of the Internet of Things (IoT) poses serious routing problems because of the heterogeneity of the network, the variability of data, and the priorities of the traffic, making it difficult to apply the conventional routing algorithms. In order to solve these problems, this paper presents the Fuzzy-Microeconomics Routing Algorithm (FMERA), a new priority-based and content-centric IoT network solution. The main novelty of FMERA is a combination of a fuzzy logic model of dynamic packet prioritization and a model of intelligent route selection based on microeconomics. The fuzzy model identifies the data priority based on the critical node and packet features whereas the microeconomic model identifies the most efficient routes to deliver the prioritized data. FMERA was tested in a simulated environment and compared to the past routing algorithms. Findings indicate that FMERA can substantially improve the packet delivery rate and at the same time lower energy use and end-to-end network delay, which proves its relevance to the contemporary IoT applications.Keywords: Internet of Things (IoT), Routing, Fuzzy logic, Microeconomic theorySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

IoT is "a dynamic infrastructure of the global network with the ability to configure itself based on standards and compatible communication protocols"1. All things have a unique ID and characteristics and can use intelligent interfaces, and are integrated into an information network. Wireless radio waves are the foundation of the Internet of Things platform, which enables internet-based communication between various objects2. Typically, a large network cannot operate effectively without some organized structure. This is due to problems such as data heterogeneity in terms of content and priority. IoT networks can be used for many industrial areas, such as infrastructure monitoring, urban services, surveillance and security applications, etc.3,4. Thus, the exchange and handling of a broad range of IoT diverse data is a challenge that has caused the inefficiency of earlier routing techniques in this network5. However, collecting broad amounts of information from such networks, including pictures and footage, often leads to traffic congestion in the core network6. Although IoT networks with mobile nodes can be regarded as a specialized variant of MANETs, their routing needs are quite different. In contrast to the classical MANETs, which are mainly concerned with connectivity maintenance, IoT routing has to deal with the content, priority, and heterogeneity of data streams of a wide range of devices.

Content-based routing strategies7 and data prioritization8 can be utilized to address this issue and accomplish routing objectives in the Internet of Things. This routing technique determines the best route for data transmission based on the transmission priority and contents. With this approach, intermediate nodes analyze linked data, and aggregation is carried out according to the connected data’s content. As a result, the network’s traffic is essentially decreased. Consequently, it is possible to obtain a notable decrease in both energy use and delay. However, to route based on priority and data content in the IoT, we face two main problems. First, the priority of the data packets should be determined accurately, and second, the optimal paths for each type of content should be determined separately.

Data prioritization in the network can be associated with high uncertainty, and for this reason, the conventional mathematical models may not achieve the desired results for this purpose. Fuzzy logic is a flexible strategy to deal with uncertainty, which can be used in applications such as modeling of nonlinear systems9–11, route determination in asymmetric communication12 or determination of congested routes13 and so on. The aim of the current research is to improve routing in IoT by providing a flexible prioritization approach and selecting data transmission paths based on this prioritization. In this research, fuzzy logic is used to solve the first problem, and microeconomic theory is used to solve the second problem. For this purpose, a fuzzy inference system is deployed in every active thing in the network to prioritize the packets in the buffer memory of the node. In addition, a model based on the microeconomics theory is used to determine the data transmission routes based on the determined priority as well as the data content. In this model, the participating nodes in each type of data routing will be determined in such a way that they can be more effective in processing the target data type (integration, processing, and analysis). Literature review

In order to present a systematic review, this section classifies the latest studies in IoT routing according to the main optimization objectives. We consider major publications devoted to energy efficiency, trust and security, and Quality of Service (QoS) optimization and discuss further supporting technologies.

Energy-efficient routing

There is a considerable amount of literature that seeks to reduce energy usage to extend the life of IoT networks. In14, a routing protocol that is energy efficient and is based on Routing Protocol for Low-Power and Lossy Networks (RPL) was introduced, that is capable of supporting both large-scale information aggregation and point-to-point communication by making energy consumption its primary objective function. Equally, the Krill optimization algorithm is applied in the work in15 to create network clusters and choose cluster heads, and then routing choices are made between these heads on the basis of energy and distance factors.

To solve the network heterogeneity, the framework in16 suggests an elastic cell-zooming algorithm to adapt transmission power and a clustering structure to maximize the energy of IoT nodes and minimize resource competition. Continuing to combine, the authors of17 apply a genetic algorithm to a software-defined network (SDN) controller to identify optimal paths that optimally reduce energy consumption and path delay.

Trust-aware and secure routing

As the IoT devices continue to spread, the most important thing is to make the transmission of data secure and trustworthy. A number of studies suggest trust-based routing solutions. As an example18, integrates the sunflower and atom search optimization algorithms of reputation-based routing, where trust factors such as direct trust, credibility, and node accessibility are used to determine and isolate the malicious attack. Trust-aware, cluster-based routing algorithms can also be found in the works of19,20. Under these approaches, nodes with high energy are chosen as cluster heads and the path credibility is then measured on the basis of communication credibility and data credibility.

Other strategies use the new technologies such as Blockchain to provide security. In17, the authors suggest a secure routing algorithm that authenticates users with the help of a Blockchain-based process. In21, a real-time validation system based on a Blockchain mechanism is suggested, which can be used as a credible and decentralized database to authenticate the activities of the IoT devices.

QoS and latency-optimized routing

In real-time and mission-critical IoT applications, reducing delay and maximizing the total Quality of Service (QoS) is an important goal. The algorithm in22 introduces a smart, data-driven routing algorithm to mobile IoT nodes based on the criteria of response speed (latency), available resources, and communication quality to choose the best intermediate nodes. In order to considerably decrease the communication delay, the work in23 presents a platform that combines fog computing with IoT. This algorithm applies a genetic algorithm to allocate the best computing resources to the fog layer and path data respectively, and shows a significant decrease in the network latency. In24, a new algorithm for reliability aware routing in IoT using reinforcement learning techniques was suggested. This method uses Q-Learning agent during route decision process which employs IoT performance goals, degradation, and reliability of devices. Another study in25, modified ad hoc on-demand distance vector (AODV) algorithm to improve the QoS in IoT applications. This approach, considers employing a decision tree learning model in the path selection module of the AODV algorithm. This mechanism is effective in reduction of disruptions and improving the reliability of routes.

Advanced service coordination and incentive mechanisms

In addition to the conventional routing metrics, other literature addresses new paradigms to improve the overall network operation and collaboration. As an example, the platform in26 is aimed at organizing the IoT services on the basis of automaton-based event detection and service-oriented design based on situation awareness to enhance the real-time information dissemination. In27, a socially-aware network incentive algorithm is introduced to solve selfishness of the nodes. It rewards nodes with virtual currency to encourage them to relay messages to facilitate collaboration in resource-constrained networks. Lastly, in order to guarantee the quality of data, the work in28 introduces a powerful online mechanism of completing tensors which can be used to reconstruct missing information in the IoT data streams.

Fuzzy-microeconomics routing algorithm (FMERA)

In this section, FMERA for priority-based and content-centric routing in IoT is explained. By providing an efficient routing solution, FMERA will minimize the rate of packet loss and end-to-end delay in IoT and at the same time will prevent the energy loss of nodes in the routing process. In this method, a network environment based on the structure of IoT with a limited movement area of nodes is considered, in which each node has a unique and unchanged id. FMERA is a routing algorithm based on content and data priority using fuzzy logic and microeconomic theory (Fig. 1). For this purpose, a fuzzy inference system is deployed in every active thing in the network, and the purpose of this system is to prioritize the packets in the buffer memory of the node. The proposed fuzzy model determines the data priority based on the characteristics of the node and the data packet. In this way, this fuzzy model receives characteristics such as PR by the source node, CD, and deadline as input, and specifies the packet sending priority using its fuzzy rule base. In general, these rules are defined in such a way that while reducing the final delay (by checking the PR and deadline), it is possible to assign a higher sending priority to data sent from sources with a high PR. In this way, while reducing the delay, it is possible to avoid increasing the possibility of congestion in the network.

Topology control

The first step in FMERA is to form and control the network topology. The purpose of the topology control step is to discover the routes between each node and other network nodes. In FMERA, these routes are defined in the form of a hierarchical structure. However, the weak links in the network architecture must be identified before the network topological layout is formed. Some of the connectivity among objects may be one-way because of the variability of IoT network architecture. Natural factors like background noise or intrinsic factors like the imbalance of an object’s radio range could be the cause of this incident. One-way link can either be ignored in this case or transformed into two-way link with the help of an intermediary node. In the suggested strategy, one-way link is eliminated because the other option may result in higher consumption of energy and overhead for communication in the network’s infrastructure. In this manner, all one-way links are found and dismissed before building the topology of the network. The procedure of building the topology starts with choosing the main node, which is the node with the most neighbors in the entire network. To find the central component, every node transmits a control message to its peers with the number of its active links; if the node sending the packet has more links than its connections, each node that receives the packet will rebroadcast the message. Every node in the entire network will receive the ID of the station with the most active links once this procedure is repeated, and this node will be regarded as the structure center. When there is a tie, two or more nodes share the same maximum number of neighbors, the node with the lowest unique ID is chosen as the central node in order to guarantee a deterministic result. It is necessary to mention that this topology control stage is not a one-time affair. In order to accommodate node mobility and possible link failures, it is started when a network is started and restarted periodically, with the period a configurable network parameter, and when a node notices a large loss of neighbors, which is a major topological change. Following this procedure, the core node begins topology creation.

The main node initiates the topology control phase by transmitting a packet to every node in its immediate vicinity. Any node that obtains this packet—assuming it hasn’t already—pushes the address it received to the stack and rebroadcasts information to its peers. A stack structure is included in this packet to follow the broadcast trail. It is very important to note that this is a distributed process. The central node simply triggers the broadcast; then each node constructs and maintains its own local hierarchical view of the network independently of the messages it receives. By receiving the broadcast packet, a node updates its local tree structure. The node can use the stack in the packet it receives to identify the route the message took, creating parent–child links to create a local hierarchy. This prevents the scalability problems and single point of failure of one centrally-computed tree. In the second step of FMERA, prioritization of the data packets is performed when the data packet is stored in the buffer memory of the node.

Packet prioritizing by fuzzy logic

The packet prioritization is a local choice of each node regarding the data that is in its own buffer. This design guarantees scalability and decentralized and fast decision-making with no need to know the global network state. The priority value computed in this step is subsequently employed as an important input in the distributed routing step (outlined in Section “Content-centric routing using microeconomic theory”), which identifies the optimal next-hop of the now-prioritized packet in the known network topology. In FMERA, a fuzzy model is used to determine the priority of packets located in the memory of each node. This fuzzy model performs prioritization by using the information of the source node and packet characteristics. In this way, every active node in the network is equipped with a fuzzy inference model to determine the priority of each packet in the node’s buffer memory for sending. Content-centric routing using microeconomic theory

FMERA uses a model that is based on microeconomic theory in order to route prioritized packets. It is important to define that the microeconomic routing model is implemented in a fully distributed way. This is opposed to a centralized approach where each forwarding node in a path operates this decision-making process independently. Each node, whenever it has a prioritized packet to transmit and there are more than one next-hop options in its local topology, implements the microeconomic model presented herein to choose the best path. This guarantees routing decisions to be adaptive, scalable and hop-by-hop made across the network.

This model receives the information related to the weight of the path steps, packet priority, and data processing capability as input, and based on this information, determines the optimal path for sending data. It should be noted that this decision-making model will be used if more than one path is available to transfer the data packet to the destination. Simulation and results

In order to strictly assess the performance of the suggested FMERA protocol, a set of simulations were performed with the help of MATLAB R2020a and its communication and networking toolboxes. FMERA performance was compared with three modern protocols: Atom Search Sunflower Optimization (ASSFO)18, Reinforcement Learning-based Reliability-aware Routing (RLRR)24, and AODV enhanced by Decision Tree (AODV-TR)25. All protocols were simulated in the same network conditions and parameters to make a fair comparison.

The simulations were to be tested in two major situations, which are; the fixed area and an increase in node density, and the constant node density and a growing area. In order to reduce the impact of randomness, 10 repetitions of each simulation experiment were done, and the mean results are reported. Evaluation based on arrangement patterns of things

Different node placements are possible to provide a variable number of paths, which can improve or degrade FMERA performance. Grid deterministic placement and normal random placement. In grid placement, nodes are placed exactly on the grid lines, and in random placement, nodes are placed so that their density is the same. This is a commonly used placement and has been used for the simulations reviewed so far and, in many studies, related to wireless networks.

In both Figs. 11 and 12, the state of constant dimensions of the environment is considered. The results of the experiments showed that the normal random placement in the wireless network has a higher percentage of successful transmission and lower energy consumption than the deterministic grid placement and it has better results in the experiments conducted in this research. This is due to creating more paths between sources and destinations in the normal random placement mode. Because in this case, more nodes are usually placed between the source and the destination, and this increases the number of routes created in FMERA. In grid mode, usually more nodes are placed in the corners of the environment and the data flow paths have less variety. Protocol overhead analysis

One of the most important factors in the efficiency of any routing protocol is its communication overhead, which is normally quantified by the count of control packets necessary to discover and maintain routes. The over-control traffic may overload the network and use a lot of energy at the expense of the bandwidth used in the actual transmission of data. In FMERA, the control packets which are broadcast in the initial topology discovery phase and the following packets which are broadcast in the periodic and event-driven updates are the major sources of overhead. We have compared the total number of control packets produced by FMERA to the one produced by ASSFO18, RLRR24, and AODV-TR25 during the simulation period.

The findings, as represented in Fig. 13, indicate that FMERA has a competitive overhead level. Although it causes a burst of control packets when first setting up the network, its hybrid update mechanism (periodic and event-driven triggers) is very efficient in dynamic environments. Conversely, protocols based on a common, network-wide flooding to maintain routes are more likely to produce a larger and more steady stream of control traffic, particularly with greater node mobility. The analysis shows that the intelligent, localized topology maintenance approach used by FMERA is a scalable solution as it provides a balance between the accuracy of network state and the cost of control traffic.

Conclusion

In this article, a new method for routing in IoT based on prioritization strategies and data content analysis was presented. The proposed method (FMERA) uses a fuzzy inference model in order to prioritize data and a computational model based on microeconomic theory to route data based on its content. The separation of prioritization processes and data content analysis to route it in FMERA has various advantages; Such as: increasing the responsiveness of the node, which results in reducing the end-to-end delay in routing; And also removing the dependence of the prioritization process on the data streams, as a result, this process can be done in a distributed manner. FMERA was evaluated in a simulated environment and based on different criteria and scenarios. Also, the results obtained from FMERA were compared with previous routing solutions. Based on the results of these tests, FMERA can have an acceptable resistance against the increase in network dimensions (increase in the number of nodes as well as the dimensions of the environment), which guarantees the scalability of FMERA for use in large environments. In addition, compared to previous methods, FMERA has advantages such as increasing the successful packet delivery ratio, reducing energy consumption, and reducing end-to-end delay, and these features show that this routing algorithm can be suitable for use in real-world scenarios.

Although we have used MATLAB in this research due to its efficiency in prototyping our new logic, we find that additional validation is required in a more realistic setting. As such, future research will be dedicated to the application of FMERA to a high-fidelity network simulator, like NS-3, to compare its performance with the performance of conventional IoT protocols like the RPL. This will prove essential in justifying its practical effectiveness and scalability in more complicated network circumstances. Additionally, this work does not take into account security issues of the routing process. Network performance may be compromised by the existence of malicious nodes that may give incorrect routing information or drop packets. Future directions will be to incorporate a trust-based mechanism into the FMERA framework to improve the resilience of the framework to security threats.

Zero Indirect Band Gap in Non-Hermitian Systems
Rahul S, Giandomenico Palumbo

Abstract
Zero indirect gaps in band models are typically viewed as unstable and achievable only through fine-tuning. Recent works, however, have revealed robust semimetallic phases in Hermitian systems where the indirect gap remains pinned at zero over a finite parameter range. Here, we extend this paradigm to non-Hermitian lattice models by studying a one-dimensional diamond-like system with gain and loss. We show that a zero indirect band gap can remain stable against non-Hermitian perturbations and identify the regimes where this robustness persists. Remarkably, we find that the zero indirect gap induces a suppression of the non-Hermitian skin effect distinct from other physical mechanics already discussed in the literature. Our results reveal new connections between indirect gaps, exceptional points and non-Hermitian skin effect, opening avenues for experimental realizations.



I
Introduction

The distinction between direct and indirect band gaps plays a central role in determining the electronic and optical properties of condensed matter systems
Cooper et al. 2015
;
Malyi and Acosta 2020
;
Tsai 2006
. While the direct band gap occurs at the same momentum points, the indirect band gap arises when the minimum of the conduction band and maximum of valence band occurs at different momenta. In this context, a zero indirect band gap is usually thought as unstable and induced by a mere fine tuning of the physical parameters of the systems.
However, it has been recently shown the existence of suitable one-
Palumbo 2024
and two-dimensional Hermitian lattice models
Palumbo and Meichanetzidis 2015
;
Andrijauskas et al. 2015
;
Pyrialakos et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2024
, in which the zero indirect band gap is robust and survives for a finite range of values of the physical parameters. This allows us to define of a novel family of semimetals in which bands do not touch each other differently from the more conventional semimetallic phases such as topological
Armitage et al. 2018
;
Wan et al. 2011
;
Xu et al. 2011
;
Bradlyn et al. 2016
;
Zhu et al. 2020
and geometric semimetals
Lin and Palumbo 2024
that instead support Dirac-like and Weyl-like cones.
Importantly, one of these semimetallic phases with zero indirect band gap and non-trivial Chern number, dubbed two-dimensional Chern semimetal
Palumbo and Meichanetzidis 2015
, has been experimentally realized in photonic crystals
Chen et al. 2025
. Thus, it is then relevant at both theoretical and experimental level to figure out the behavior of these semimetallic phases in the presence of dissipation (gain and loss) and possibly show the existence of robust indirect band gaps in non-Hermitian phases.

We remind that in recent years, condensed and synthetic matter systems have been revisited from the perspective of non-Hermitian systems
Ashida et al. 2020
, in which non-Hermitian Hamiltonians in open quantum systems emerge due to the presence of gain and loss. Here, the corresponding energy spectra can acquire complex values due to the nature of non-Hermiticity of the Hamiltonians. Non-Hermitian systems can exhibit distinctive features such as exceptional points (EPs)
Heiss 2012
;
Miri and Alù 2019
;
Budich et al. 2019
;
Kawabata et al. 2019
;
Pará et al. 2021
, the non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE)
Longhi 2019
;
Li et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2022
;
Kawabata et al. 2023
;
Gohsrich et al. 2025
, and non-Hermitian topology
Bergholtz et al. 2021
;
Esaki et al. 2011
;
Shen et al. 2018
;
Zhu et al. 2021
;
Martínez-Strasser et al. 2024
;
Alvarez et al. 2018
;
Gong et al. 2018
;
Yokomizo and Murakami 2019
;
Borgnia et al. 2020
;
Okuma and Sato 2023
.
In this framework, it is particularly intriguing to explore how zero indirect band gaps manifest within the complex band structures of non-Hermitian systems and how they influence the presence of EPs and the NHSE. Despite their significance, systematic studies at the intersection of non-Hermitian physics and indirect band gaps have been completely overlooked, providing a key motivation for the present work.

The main goal of this paper is to show the existence of robust zero indirect band gaps in non-Hermitian models.
For simplicity, we will focus on an one-dimensional system on a diamond-like lattice and study the presence of EPs and the NHSE. By systematically varying the parameters of our non-Hermitian model, we will identify the ranges where the indirect band gap remains pinned at zero, thereby marking its stability.
Importantly, we will show a suppression of the NHSE due to the zero indirect gap, which represents one of the main results of our work. Notice that this NHSE suppression is genuinely distinct from other mechanisms induced by magnetic and electric fields and by anyonic statistics
Lu et al. 2021
;
Teo et al. 2024
;
Qin et al. 2025
;
Leumer and Bercioux 2025
.
Thus, our work paves the way for the exploration of new directions in non-Hermitian band theory with relevant implications for the experimental realization of semimetallic phases with robust zero indirect band gaps.

Model Hamiltonian

We start considering a one dimensional system on a diamond-like chain with three sites per unit cell, which generalizes the Hermitian system introduced in Ref.
Palumbo 2024

The tight-binding Hamiltonian is written in terms of fermionic basis as

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}J\big(a^{\dagger}_{i+1}b_{i}+b^{\dagger}_{i+1}c_{i}+\text{h.c.}\big)-h\big(a^{\dagger}_{i}b_{i}-b^{\dagger}_{i}c_{i}+\text{h.c.}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\beta_{1}a^{\dagger}_{i}c_{i}+\beta_{2}c^{\dagger}_{i}a_{i}

(1)

\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}

The corresponding momentum space Hamiltonian is given by,

H(k)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-h+Je^{-ik}&\beta_{1}\\
-h+Je^{ik}&0&-h+Je^{-ik}\\
\beta_{2}&-h+Je^{ik}&0\end{pmatrix}.

III
Exceptional Points

In this section, we investigate the appearance of EPs due to non-Hermitian coupling terms in our model. EPs arise in the parameter space where all the three bands touch each other. Thus, in this specific regime, we compute the condition number
{\rm cond(V)}

Feng et al. 2025
;
Schomerus 2024
;
Wiersig 2022
.
In non-Hermitian systems, this number identifies the non-orthogonality of eigenvectors, which can become nearly linearly dependent.

The right eigenvectors of the Hamiltonian

H(\mathbf{k})
are obtained by solving

\displaystyle H(\mathbf{k})\,|R_{n}(\mathbf{k})\rangle=E_{n}(\mathbf{k})\,|R_{n}(\mathbf{k})\rangle,

(2)

where
E_{n}(\mathbf{k})
are (generally complex) eigenvalues.
Writing these right eigenvectors as columns of a matrix
V(k)
,

\displaystyle V(\mathbf{k})=\big(|R_{1}(\mathbf{k})\rangle,|R_{2}(\mathbf{k})\rangle,\dots,|R_{N}(\mathbf{k})\rangle\big),

(3)

the condition number of
V(k)
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{cond}\big(V)=\|V(\mathbf{k})\|\,\|V(\mathbf{k})^{-1}\|.

(4)

but their associated eigenvectors also become linearly dependent.
In this case, the matrix
V(\mathbf{k})

{\rm cond(V)}

The figure clearly illustrates that the sharp divergence of

{\rm cond(V)}
occurs precisely at the points in momentum space where the eigenvalue bands coalesce. At these points, not only the eigenvalue bands but also the corresponding eigenvectors coalesce. This feature is indicated by the divergence of
{\rm cond(V)}
.

Moreover to understand if the maximum value of
{\rm cond(V)}
scales with the resolution of k, we do a systematic scaling analysis which is presented in Fig.

Across all three cases, the scaling law follows an approximate power-law behavior of the form,

\text{maximum cond}(V)\propto N_{k}^{\alpha},

(5)

with a fitted exponent
\alpha=0.48

This robustness not only provides a reliable quantitative signature for the presence of EPs but also suggests a universal mechanism governing the sensitivity of eigenvectors near non-Hermitian degeneracies in our model.

IV
Non-Hermitian Skin effect

Before reviewing the NHSE in our model, it is useful to briefly discuss the central role of NHSE in non-Hermitian systems. This peculiar effect refers to the significant number of bulk eigenstates accumulate at the boundary under open boundary conditions
Zhang et al. 2022
;
Kawabata et al. 2023
;
Gohsrich et al. 2025
. Physically the NHSE arises due to the asymmetric couplings or gain/loss
Jiang et al. 2024
in the system. The existence of non-Hermiticity in both quantum materials and synthetic matter systems makes the NHSE very relevant from the perspective of experimental realizations
Wang et al. 2021
;
Longhi et al. 2015a
;
Longhi et al. 2015b
.
Here, to characterize this effect induced by non-Hermitian couplings
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
in our model, we compute the average inverse participation ratio (IPR) of the eigenstates across the parameter space spanned by Re(
\beta_{1}
) and Re(
\beta_{2}
). The results reveal a clear suppression of NHSE along the diagonal line Re(
\beta_{1}
) = Re(
\beta_{2}
), where the non-Hermitian coupling is effectively symmetric and hence the asymmetry vanishes. In this regime the eigenstates are more delocalized, leading to smaller IPR values. In contrast, away from the diagonal, the asymmetry between Re(
\beta_{1}
) and Re(
\beta_{2}
) becomes significant, and the eigenstates exhibit strong localization reflected in the higher IPR values.

Remarkably, in this same regime, the IPR of the eigenstates remains small, which is a clear signature of delocalization.
In other words, the NHSE, where the bulk eigenstates are accumulated of at the system’s edges, is significantly weakened due to the presence of the zero indirect gap. It would be relevant to check the weakening of the NHSE also in higher-dimensional gapless systems with zero indirect gaps. We leave this important point to future work.

V
Conclusion

In this work, we have investigated the emergence of a robust zero indirect band gap in a non-Hermitian diamond-like chain with generic complex intra-cell couplings. By analyzing the momentum space dispersion spectrum, we have identified the parameter regime where the zero indirect band gap persists. The non-Hermitian character of the model Hamiltonian leads to rich phenomena such as the emergence of EPs, NHSE. We have computed condition number of the eigenvector matrix to identify the EPs and have obtained a scaling law that proves the divergence of condition number at the EPs. Strikingly, our results reveal that the zero indirect band gap can suppress the NSHE, thereby allowing the extended states to prevail. This interplay between zero indirect band gap and the non-Hermitian features provides new insights into spectral properties of non-Hermitian systems and supports in engineer novel lattice systems.
As a future direction, we plan to investigate non-Hermitian phases in topological semimetals exhibiting robust zero indirect gaps in two
Palumbo and Meichanetzidis 2015
and higher dimensions, aiming to uncover novel spectral and transport phenomena arising from the interplay between topology and dissipation.

Geometric Decentralized Stability Condition for Power Systems Based on Projecting DW Shells
Linbin Huang, Liangxiao Luo, Huanhai Xin, Dan Wang, Ping Ju, and Florian Dörfler

Abstract
The development of decentralized stability conditions has gained considerable attention due to the need to analyze heterogeneous multi-converter power systems. A recent advance is the application of the small-phase theorem, which extends the passivity theory. However, it requires the transfer function matrix to be sectorial, which may not hold in some frequency range and will result in conservatism. This letter tackles this problem by leveraging the Davis-Wielandt (DW) shells for decentralized stability analysis. We develop a geometric decentralized stability condition that visually displays how heterogeneous converters interact with the power grid and enable modular system analysis.



I
Introduction

Modern power systems feature the large-scale integration of heterogeneous power electronics converters. Such heterogeneity comes from the various types of devices (wind, solar, HVDC, energy storage systems, etc.), as well as the various deployed control schemes such as grid-following (GFL) control and grid-forming (GFM) control. The stability analysis of heterogeneous multi-converter systems has always been an intricate task, as it involves high-dimensional dynamics and complex closed-loop interactions.

Recent works on this topic include the application of the small-phase theorem, combined with the small-gain theorem, to perform decentralized (small-signal) stability analysis of power electronics-dominated power systems
[
1
]
. The small-phase theorem builds upon the concept of matrix phases, which is defined using the numerical range
[
2
]
. This approach has shown its capability in analyzing highly heterogeneous multi-converter systems in a decentralized manner, that is, if each converter satisfies certain conditions, then the interconnected multi-converter system is guaranteed to be stable. The decentralized stability analysis will be an important cornerstone for future power systems, due to the need to understand how heterogeneous converters interact with the power network and how such interaction results in stability.

However, the calculation of matrix phases requires that the matrix is sectorial, which may be conservative under certain scenarios. To tackle this problem, this letter proposes geometric decentralized stability conditions based on the concept of Davis-Wielandt (DW) shell
[
3
,
4
]
, which further extend the conditions in
[
1
]
. We will demonstrate how the DW shell of a matrix is related to its numerical range and further its phases. Moreover, we will show how the small-signal stability of a heterogeneous multi-converter system can be analyzed using DW shells in a decentralized manner. We further prove that with an inductive network, the DW shells can be projected into the
x
-
z
plane without resulting in conservatism, allowing better visualization of the geometric stability analysis.

III
Stability Conditions Based on DW Shells

Fig. 2:
Illustration of a multi-converter system and its closed-loop dynamics.

Consider a multi-converter system as illustrated in Fig.
2
, where
N
heterogeneous converters are interconnected via the power network. As detailed in
[
1
]
, such a system can be modeled as a closed-loop feedback system, and its characteristic equation is
{\rm det}(I+\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}^{-1}(s)\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(s))=0
, where, following the notations in
[
1
]
,
{\rm det}(A)
denotes the determinant of matrix
A
,
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(s)
is the admittance transfer function matrix of the power network, and
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(s)
is a block-diagonal transfer function matrix capturing the dynamics of the
N
converters. Note that
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(s)
contains
N
blocks (each one is
2\times 2
), with the
i
-th one denoted by
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C},i}(s)
, which captures the dynamics of the
i
-th
converter.
Both
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C},i}(s)
and
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}^{-1}(s)
are stable in open loop, and we refer to
[
1
]
for their detailed derivations. According to the generalized Nyquist criterion (GNC), the closed-loop system in Fig.
2
is stable if and only if the Nyquist plot of
{\rm det}(I+\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}^{-1}(s)\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(s))
does not encircle the origin. A sufficient condition to satisfy the GNC is for each
\omega\in[0,\infty)
,

{\rm det}(I+\tau\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}^{-1}(j\omega)\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(j\omega))\neq 0,\forall\tau\in(0,1].

(3)

In what follows, we will demonstrate how the above condition can be guaranteed through the separation of DW shells.

Proposition III.1

(Separation of DW Shells)
.

The multi-converter system in Fig.
2
is stable if for each
\omega\in[0,\infty)
,

DW(\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(j\omega))\cap DW(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega))=\emptyset,\forall\tau\in(0,1]\,,

(4)

that is, the DW shell of the converters’ collected dynamics
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(j\omega)
is separated from the DW shell of the power network dynamics
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega)
scaled by
-\tfrac{1}{\tau}
.

Proof.

By Theorem 2.1 in
[
5
]
,
DW(A)\cap DW(-B)=\emptyset
indicates
{\rm det}(A+B)\neq 0
. We further have
{\rm det}(I+B^{-1}A)\neq 0
since
{\rm det}(A+B)={\rm det}(B)\times{\rm det}(I+B^{-1}A)
. Hence, we can derive (
3
) from (
4
) by considering
A=\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(j\omega)
and
B=\tfrac{1}{\tau}\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega)
and conclude that the system is stable.
∎

The above proposition shows that the system stability can be guaranteed by the separation of the converters’ DW shell and the power network’s DW shell. Before going to the decentralized version of this stability condition, we first give an illustrative example to show how a converter’s DW shell interacts with a power network’s DW shell.

Consider a GFL converter that is connected to an infinite bus, shown in Fig.
3
. The converter’s parameters are the same as those in
[
1
]
, and the short-circuit ratio (SCR) of this system is
SCR=1/X_{\rm net}=3.5

is the line reactance. The converter’s admittance matrix
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C},1}(j\omega)
has been derived in
[
1
]
and is thus omitted here. Fig.
4
plots the converter’s DW shells according to
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C},1}(j\omega)
at different frequencies, where each DW shell is an ellipsoid. For the network part, since it is a single line in Fig.
3
, we have
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega)=X_{\rm net}^{-1}I_{2}
, where
I_{2}
denotes the
2\times 2
identity matrix. Since in this case
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega)
is positive definite, we find that the DW shell trajectory (over
\tau\in(0,1]
) of
-\tfrac{1}{\tau}\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega)
is part of
parabola
on the
x
-
z
plane, i.e,
z=x^{2},y=0
, displayed as the red line in Fig.
4
. Moreover, the
x
-axis range of this red line is
(-\infty,-{\rm SCR}]
.

In Fig.
4

\forall\tau\in(0,1]
) does not intersect with the converter’s DW shell, indicating that the system is stable according to Proposition
III.1
. The two lower subplots respectively show the projections of the DW shells onto the
x
-
z
plane (referred to as the
x
-

graph) and the

-

) and (

On the other hand, according to

IV
Decentralized Stability Conditions

Based on the above observation on a single converter system, we are now ready to present the geometric decentralized stability conditions. Consider an inductive power network that interconnects
N
converters, and the Kron-reduced grounded Laplacian matrix is
{\bf B}_{\rm r}
. In this case, according to
[
1
]
and
[
6
]
, the power network dynamics can be described by

\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega)={\bf S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf B}_{\rm r}{\bf S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes I_{2},

(5)

where
{\bf S}
denotes the diagonal capacity ratio matrix of the converters, and
\otimes
denote the Kronecker product.

Proposition IV.1

(Geometric Decentralized Stability Condition)
.

With the network dynamics described by (
5
), the multi-converter system in Fig.
2
is stable if for each
\omega\in[0,\infty)
,

DW(\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C},i}(j\omega))\cap DW(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega))=\emptyset,\forall\tau\in(0,1]\,,

(6)

for any converter
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. Moreover, the network’s DW shell
DW(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega))
with
\tau\in(0,1]
is part of the parabola
z=x^{2},y=0
with
x\in(-\infty,-\rm{gSCR}]
. Here
{\rm gSCR}
(the smallest eigenvalue of
{\bf S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf B}_{\rm r}{\bf S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
) is the so-called generalized short-circuit ratio in power systems.

Proof.

The network’s DW shell
DW(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega))
with
\tau\in(0,1]
is part of the parabola
z=x^{2},y=0
because
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega)
is positive definite and its smallest eigenvalue is
{\rm gSCR}
. According to
[
4
]
,
DW(\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(j\omega))
and all the
DW(\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C},i}(j\omega))
are contained within the paraboloid
z=x^{2}+y^{2}
(see Fig.
7
). Since
DW(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega))
is on the boundary of this paraboloid, one can infer (
4
) from (
6
) because
z\geq x^{2}+y^{2}
is convex and
DW(\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(j\omega))
is the convex hull of all the
DW(\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C},i}(j\omega))
, where
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C},i}(j\omega)
is the
i
-th block of
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(j\omega)
. This is,
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C}}^{N}(j\omega)
will not intersect with
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{\rm grid}(j\omega)
unless
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{{\rm C},i}(j\omega)
does.
∎

The above proposition shows that if the DW shells of any converter do not intersect with the power network’s DW shell, then the closed-loop multi-converter system is stable. Such a condition can be checked in a fully decentralized and scalable manner, since we can independently focus on checking if each converter satisfies the condition. We give an example below.

V
Conclusions

In this letter, we propose a geometric decentralized stability condition for multi-converter power systems based on DW shells. The condition is fully decentralized and does not require matrices to be sectorial, which is thus less conservative than those in
[
1
]
. We illustrated how the separation of the DW shells can be equivalently checked by projecting them onto the
x
-
z
plane. Our apporach provides a new direction for the analysis of future converter-dominated power systems.

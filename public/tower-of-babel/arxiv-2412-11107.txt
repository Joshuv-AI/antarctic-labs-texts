Quantum-Corrected Holographic Wilson Loop Expectation Values and Super-Yang-Mills Confinement
Xiao-Long Liu, Cong-Yuan Yue, Jun Nian, Wenni Zheng

Abstract
Confinement is a well-known phenomenon in the infrared regime of (supersymmetric) Yang-Mills theory. While both experimental observations and numerical simulations have robustly confirmed its existence, the underlying physical mechanism remains elusive. Unraveling the theoretical origin of confinement continues to be a profound and longstanding challenge in both physics and mathematics. Motivated by recent advances in quantum Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity, we investigate the Wilson loop expectation values in the large- N N limit of 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 super-Yang-Mills theory at finite chemical potential, employing a holographic approach within the background of an extremal AdS 5 Reissner-Nordström black brane. Our results reveal that quantum gravitational fluctuations in the near-horizon region significantly modify the holographic Wilson loop expectation values. These values exhibit an area-law behavior, indicative of a confining quark-antiquark potential. Within this framework, our findings suggest that confinement in the super-Yang-Mills theory arises as a consequence of near-horizon quantum gravity fluctuations in the bulk extremal AdS 5 black brane geometry.



Introduction.—
Ever since its establishment
[
1
]
, the AdS/CFT correspondence has served as an important tool in understanding the strong-coupling dynamics of the supersymmetric Yang-Mills (SYM) theory in the infrared (IR) regime. According to the AdS/CFT correspondence, a Wilson loop
\mathcal{C}
’s quantum expectation value in the large-
N
limit of
\mathcal{N}=4
super-Yang-Mills theory can be computed holographically via
[
2
,
3
]
:

\langle W(\mathcal{C})\rangle\simeq e^{-S_{\text{\text{NG}}}}\,,

(1)

where
S_{\text{NG}}
is the Nambu-Goto (NG) action of the fundamental string worldsheet in the anti-de-Sitter spacetime anchored to the Wilson loop
\mathcal{C}
on the boundary. Hence, the minimal surface provides the dominant contribution.

On the other hand, for a temporal rectangular Wilson loop in the fundamental representation with the time interval
T\to\infty
,
\langle W(\mathcal{C})\rangle
obeys the following relation:

\langle W(\mathcal{C})\rangle\simeq e^{-T\cdot V_{q\bar{q}}(L)}\,,

(2)

where
V_{q\bar{q}}(L)
is the potential between a heavy quark-antiquark pair, which can be extracted by combining (
1
) and (
2
). If the potential
V_{q\bar{q}}(L)
for long-distance
L
behaves linearly in
L
, it is an indication of confinement in SYM theory.

The first attempts along this line were made in
[
2
,
3
]
, where the authors found that a strictly AdS spacetime leads only to a Coulomb potential without a confining linear term. Later, it was pointed out in
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
that for a black hole with finite temperature, besides the Coulomb potential, the potential
V_{q\bar{q}}(L)
can contain some confining terms, consistent with the well-known results in the literature
[
8
,
9
]
. Similar results have been found and extended in many following works, e.g.,
[
10
,
11
]
. However, a holographic explanation for confinement at zero temperature remains open. Phenomenologically, we can modify the bulk metric of the AdS spacetime by hand. Some proposals of the modified AdS metrics successfully led to a confining potential
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
, but first-principle derivations and more fundamental understanding are still lacking.

The recent progress on nearly AdS
2
quantum gravity brings new ideas to this problem. As a toy model of quantum gravity, the Jackiw-Teitelboim (JT) gravity on AdS
2
is governed by the Schwarzian theory on the 1d boundary
[
18
,
19
]

D

D\geq 4

After quantum averaging these modes, the spacetime metric close to the boundary receives mild quantum corrections

20

\langle W\rangle

Hence, within the AdS/CFT correspondence framework, the linear confinement at zero temperature is due to the near-horizon quantum gravity effects. In the phase diagram of SYM theory, our new result can be interpreted as deforming the superconformal
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theory by finite chemical potential, in contrast to the finite-temperature deformation considered in

Quantum-Corrected Spacetime from JT Gravity.—
We start with a near-extremal Reissner-Nordström AdS
5
black brane, whose near-horizon region is AdS
{}_{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
. To introduce quantum gravity fluctuations in the near-horizon AdS
2
region, we apply the approach introduced in
[
19
,
20
]
by treating the metric factor as a bilocal operator. Consequently, the quantum-averaged Euclidean AdS
2
metric is given by

\langle ds^{2}\rangle=\langle G_{\partial\partial}(2\zeta)\rangle_{\beta}\cdot L^{2}_{2}\Big(d\zeta^{2}+dt_{E}^{2}\Big)\,,

(3)

where the quantum-averaged bilocal operator at the inverse temperature
\beta
is

\displaystyle\langle G_{\partial\partial}(2\zeta)\rangle_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2C)^{2}}\int^{\beta}_{0}dM\,{\rm sinh}\Bigl(2\pi\sqrt{M}\Bigr)\,e^{-\beta M}

\displaystyle\cdot\int^{\infty}_{0}dE\,{\rm sinh}\Bigl(2\pi\sqrt{E}\Bigr)\,e^{-2\zeta(E-M)}\,\Gamma\Bigl(1\pm i\sqrt{M}\pm i\sqrt{E}\Bigr)\,,

(4)

and
\Gamma\bigl(1\pm ix\bigr)\equiv\Gamma\bigl(1+ix\bigr)\,\Gamma\bigl(1-ix\bigr)
. Taking the extremal limit
\beta\to\infty
(implying
M\to 0
) and adopting the reparametrization
E=2C(M+\omega)
and
\zeta\to\zeta/(2C)
, we obtain for
\omega\gg M
:

\displaystyle\langle G_{\partial\partial}(2\zeta)\rangle_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2C}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,{\rm sinh}\Bigl(2\pi\sqrt{2C\omega}\Bigr)\,e^{-2\zeta\omega}\,\Gamma(1\pm i\sqrt{2C\omega})^{2}.

(5)

Finally, the complete quantum-corrected Euclidean AdS
2
metric is

ds^{2}=\frac{h(\zeta)}{\zeta^{2}}\cdot L^{2}_{2}(d\zeta^{2}+dt_{E}^{2})\,,

(6)

with the factor
h(\zeta)
given by

\displaystyle h(\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{\pi^{2}C}\int^{\infty}_{0}d\omega\,{\rm sinh}\Bigl(2\pi\sqrt{2C\omega}\Bigr)\,e^{-2\zeta\omega}\,\Gamma\Bigl(1\pm i\sqrt{2C\omega}\Bigr)^{2},

(7)

which approaches the classical value
1
towards the AdS
2
boundary
\zeta\to 0
. The expression (
7
) can be viewed as a Fourier transform of a function
\widetilde{h}(\omega)
. Later in this paper, we focus on small quantum corrections that deviate from the classical background. Hence, we expand
\widetilde{h}(\omega)
in the classical limit (
C^{-1}\ll\omega
) and then perform the Fourier transform. In this limit, the quantum correction factor
h(\zeta)
becomes

h(\zeta)=1+\frac{3}{2\pi^{4}}\left(\frac{\zeta}{C}\right)^{2}-\frac{15}{2\pi^{6}}\left(\frac{\zeta}{C}\right)^{3}+\frac{315}{8\pi^{8}}\left(\frac{\zeta}{C}\right)^{4}+\cdots\,.

(8)

Now, we consider the quantum corrections to the AdS
5
black brane metric. The RN Euclidean AdS
5
black brane has the metric and the background gauge field:

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{2}}{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}f(u)\,dt_{E}^{2}+\frac{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}{u^{2}}\frac{du^{2}}{f(u)}+\frac{u^{2}}{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}d\vec{x}\,^{2}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\mu\left(1-\frac{u_{T}^{2}}{u^{2}}\right)dt\quad\text{with}\quad\mu=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\frac{g_{s}\,u_{T}}{L_{\rm AdS}^{2}}\,Q\,,

(10)

where
g_{s}
is the dimensionless coupling constant of the gauge field, and
u_{T}
denotes the horizon position. The factor
f(u)
, the mass
M
, and the temperature
T_{h}
are

f(u)=1-M\frac{u_{T}^{4}}{u^{4}}+Q^{2}\frac{u_{T}^{6}}{u^{6}},\quad M=1+Q^{2},\quad T_{h}=\frac{(2-Q^{2})u_{T}}{2\pi L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}\,.

(11)

From now on, we focus on the extremal case (
T=0
) and set
Q^{2}=2
.

Next, we introduce quantum gravity fluctuations in the near-horizon AdS
2
. By imposing continuity and smoothness conditions in the overlap region, we obtain the quantum-corrected extremal RN Euclidean AdS
5
metric:

ds^{2}=\alpha^{\prime}\left[\frac{U^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(f(U)\,h(U)\,dt_{E}^{2}+d\vec{x}\,^{2}\right)+R^{2}f^{-1}(U)\,h(U)\,\frac{dU^{2}}{U^{2}}\right]\,,

(12)

with the quantum correction factor

\displaystyle h(U)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{R^{4}}{96\,\pi^{4}C^{2}(U-U_{T})^{2}}-\frac{5R^{6}}{1152\,C^{3}\left(\pi^{6}(U-U_{T})^{3}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{35R^{8}}{18432\,\pi^{8}C^{4}(U-U_{T})^{4}}+\cdots\,,

(13)

where
U\equiv u/\alpha^{\prime}
,
U_{T}\equiv u_{T}/\alpha^{\prime}
, and
R^{2}\equiv L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}/\alpha^{\prime}
with
\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}
denoting the effective string length. More details can be found in the supplemental materials.

Temporal Wilson Loop.—
We apply the same method as in
[
3
]
to calculate the expectation value of a rectangular temporal Wilson loop, whose temporal and spatial sides have the lengths
T
and
L
, respectively. We use the quantum-corrected AdS
5
metric (
12
) and drop the subscript of
t_{E}
for simplicity. This metric works well when the worldsheet is far from the horizon.

In the presence of an
U(1)
gauge field, besides the Schwarzian modes, the gauge mode fluctuations should also provide quantum corrections
[
23
]
. However, since the characteristic scale
M_{U(1)}
of the gauge modes is much greater than the characteristic scale
M_{SL(2)}
of the Schwarzian modes
[
24
]
, the
U(1)
gauge fluctuations are effectively frozen in the low-temperature regime
T\ll M_{U(1)}
. In this paper, we only consider the quantum corrections from Schwarzian modes.

We choose the worldsheet coordinates
\tau=t
and
\sigma=x
. The induced metric is

g_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}g_{tt}(\partial_{t}t)^{2}&0\\
0&g_{UU}(\partial_{x}U)^{2}+g_{xx}(\partial_{x}x)^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\alpha,\,\beta\in\{\tau,\,\sigma\}\,.

(14)

Consequently, the NG action becomes

S_{\text{NG}}=\frac{T}{2\pi}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx\sqrt{h^{2}(U)\,(\partial_{x}U)^{2}+(U^{4}/R^{4})f(U)\,h(U)}\,.

(15)

This action has the conserved charge

\frac{\frac{U^{4}}{R^{4}}f(U)\,h(U)}{\sqrt{(\partial_{x}U)^{2}h^{2}(U)+\frac{U^{4}}{R^{4}}f(U)\,h(U)}}=\sqrt{\frac{U_{0}^{4}}{R^{4}}f(U_{0})\,h(U_{0})}\,.

(16)

Thus, we have the relation between
x
and
U
:

x=\frac{R^{2}}{U_{0}}\int_{1}^{U/U_{0}}\frac{h_{y}(y)\,dy}{y^{2}\sqrt{\frac{f_{y}^{2}(y)\,h_{y}^{2}(y)}{f_{y}(1)\,h_{y}(1)}y^{4}-f_{y}(y)\,h_{y}(y)}}\,,

(17)

with

\displaystyle f_{y}(y)

\displaystyle=1-(1+Q^{2})\frac{U^{4}_{T}}{U^{4}_{0}y^{4}}+Q^{2}\frac{U^{6}_{T}}{U^{6}_{0}y^{6}}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle h_{y}(y)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{R^{4}}{96\pi^{4}C^{2}(yU_{0}-U_{T})^{2}}-\frac{5R^{6}}{1152C^{3}\left(\pi^{6}(yU_{0}-U_{T})^{3}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{35R^{8}}{18432\pi^{8}C^{4}(yU_{0}-U_{T})^{4}}+\cdots\,,

(19)

where
y\equiv U/U_{0}
, while
U_{0}
and
U_{T}
denote the extremum position of the string and the horizon position, respectively.

(21)

\displaystyle-1\Bigg]\,.

(22)

We also expand the integrand for small
U_{T}/U_{0}

\displaystyle E=\frac{U_{0}}{\pi}\Bigg[\int^{\infty}_{1}dy\,

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{y^{2}}{\sqrt{y^{4}-1}}+\frac{R^{4}\left(2y^{2}+3\right)}{192\,\pi^{4}C^{2}U_{0}^{2}\left(y^{2}+1\right)\sqrt{y^{4}-1}}

Discussion.—
The approach of introducing quantum gravity corrections in AdS/CFT correspondence already has many applications
[
26
,
24
,
27
,
28
]
. In this paper, we introduced the quantum gravity fluctuations in the near-horizon region of an extremal AdS
5
Reissner-Nordström black brane and computed the Wilson loop’s quantum expectation value. We found the area law of
\textrm{log}\langle W\rangle
and the linear term in the quark-antiquark potential, indicating the confinement in the boundary SYM theory. The deep insight we gained is that, from the viewpoint of the AdS/CFT correspondence, the quantum gravity fluctuations in the near-horizon region are in charge for the boundary large-
N
SYM theory’s confinement. A natural extension of the current work should provide a holographic interpretation for the SYM’s mass gap
[
29
]
.

Although our results are derived within the framework of SYM theory, the quark-antiquark potential obtained in Eq. (
24
) can nonetheless be compared with the Cornell potential
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
to fix the parameters:

C\approx 0.09986\,\textrm{fm}\,,\quad R=\frac{L_{\text{AdS}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}\approx 1.50863\,,

(34)

which are reasonable values in nuclear phenomenology.

For an extremal RN black brane, the mass equals the charge and is proportional to the chemical potential. As the chemical potential increases, the black brane grows larger, and eventually, its horizon intersects the minimal surface anchored to the Wilson loop, which signals deconfinement, in full agreement with the SYM phase diagram (Fig.
1
). This behavior indicates a quantum-gravity-induced phase transition, which warrants further investigation. Conversely, in the case of a very small extremal RN black hole, the system approaches the origin of the phase diagram, but with a small deformation characterized by a nonzero chemical potential. This subtle deformation plays a crucial role in the emergence of confinement.

Appendix A
Quantum-Corrected AdS
2
Metric in Euclidean JT Gravity

In this appendix, we generalize the formalism introduced in
[
20
]
from the Lorentzian AdS
2
spacetime to the Euclidean one.

We begin with the Lorentzian AdS
2
metric in Poincaré coordinates:

ds^{2}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{Z}^{2}}(d\widetilde{Z}\,^{2}-dT^{2})\,.

(35)

By choosing
Z=i\widetilde{Z}
, we could get the Euclidean version of metric as

ds^{2}=\frac{1}{Z^{2}}(dZ^{2}+dT^{2})\,.

(36)

For this bulk metric, a boundary observer lives in a wiggly curve
\widetilde{Z}(t)=\epsilon\dot{f}(t)
, where
f(t)
is the reparametrization of boundary time
t

[
19
]
. It is should be noted that
f
is the only degree of freedom in the theory.

The boundary observer can associate two boundary times with each bulk point by sending and receiving a lightray
[
20
]
. More precisely, the observer sends a lightray at the boundary time
t_{1}=v=t-\tilde{z}
, and then receive the lightray reflected by the bulk point at the boundary time
t_{2}=u=t+\tilde{z}
.

In summary, the boundary observer’s lightcone coordinates are defined as

v=t-\tilde{z}\,,\quad u=t+\tilde{z}\,,

(37)

while the bulk lightcone coordinates are defined as

V=T-\widetilde{Z}\,,\quad U=T+\widetilde{Z}\,.

(38)

When there are no off-shell fluctuations, the bulk and the boundary lightcone coordinates coincide.

The process of relating a bulk point with two boundary times by a lightray is

\displaystyle\text{Send the light at:}

\displaystyle\quad v=t_{1}\,,

\displaystyle T_{1}=f(t_{1})\,;

(39)

\displaystyle\text{Receive the light at :}

\displaystyle\quad u=t_{2}\,,

\displaystyle T_{2}=f(t_{2})\,.

With the identifications
V=T_{1}
and
U=T_{2}
, we have

U=f(u)\,,\quad V=f(v)\,.

(40)

Therefore, in the boundary observer’s lightcone coordinates, we can rewrite the bulk AdS
2
metric (
35
) in boundary reparameterization modes
f
as

ds^{2}=-\frac{f^{\prime}(u)f^{\prime}(v)}{(f(u)-f(v))^{2}}\,du\,dv\,.

(41)

The line element can be derived from the AdS
2
geodesic distance, i.e., the isometric invariant distance, as

d(P,Q)=\ln\Bigg|\frac{(U-V^{\prime})(U^{\prime}-V)}{(U-V)(U^{\prime}-V^{\prime})}\Bigg|\,.

(42)

Quantum averaging the off-shell modes
f
, we obtain
\langle d_{f}(p,q)\rangle
as the the exact bulk-bulk propagator

\langle d_{f}(p,q)\rangle=\langle G_{bb}(u,u^{\prime},v,v^{\prime})\rangle=\int_{u^{\prime}}^{u}dt\int_{v^{\prime}}^{v}dt^{\prime}\langle G_{\partial\partial}(t-t^{\prime})\rangle\,,

(43)

where
\langle G_{\partial\partial}(t-t^{\prime})\rangle
is the zero-temperature exact boundary-boundary propagator in Lorentzian time given by

\langle G_{\partial\partial}(t-t^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{1}{2C}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{2C\omega})\,e^{-i\omega(t-t^{\prime})}\,\Gamma(1\pm i\sqrt{2C\omega})^{2}\,.

(44)

By differentiating the isometric invariant distance (
42
), we obtain the line element as

\displaystyle\langle d_{f}(p,p+dx)\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bigg\langle\ln\Bigg|1-\frac{f(u)-f(u+du)}{f(u)-f(v)}\frac{f(v)-f(v+dv)}{f(u)-f(v)}\Bigg|\Bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bigg\langle\frac{f^{\prime}(u)f^{\prime}(v)}{(f(u)-f(v))^{2}}\Bigg\rangle\,du\,dv\,.

(45)

Hence, the quantum-averaged Lorentzian AdS
2
metric can be obtained by computing the exact boundary-boundary propagator and setting
t\to u
,
t^{\prime}\to v
:

\langle ds^{2}(t,\tilde{z})\rangle=-\langle G_{\partial\partial}(u-v)\rangle\,du\,dv=\langle G_{\partial\partial}(2\tilde{z})\rangle\,(d\tilde{z}^{2}-dt^{2})\,,

(46)

with

\langle G_{\partial\partial}(2\tilde{z})\rangle=\frac{1}{2C}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{2C\omega})\,e^{-i\omega\tilde{z}}\,\Gamma(1\pm i\sqrt{2C\omega})^{2}\,.

(47)

To obtain the quantum-corrected Euclidean AdS
2
metric, we transform
\tilde{z}\to-iz
, which consequently leads to

\langle ds^{2}(t,z)\rangle=\langle G_{\partial\partial}(2z)\rangle\,(dt^{2}+dz^{2})\,,

(48)

where
\langle G_{\partial\partial}(2z)\rangle
can be determined up to an overall proportionality constant as

\langle G_{\partial\partial}(2z)\rangle=\frac{1}{2C}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{2C\omega})\,e^{-\omega z}\,\Gamma(1\pm i\sqrt{2C\omega})^{2}\,.

(49)

Appendix B
Quantum-Corrected AdS
5
Black Brane Metric

This appendix will discuss how the AdS
5
quantum correction factor is introduced via the matching process.

The main process of adding quantum corrections to the AdS
5
metric consists of the following few steps:

1.

Separate the original AdS
5
black brane metric into a near-horizon region (NHR) and a far-away region (FAR).

2.

Since the NHR metric becomes AdS
{}_{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
, by performing a dimensional reduction
[
24
]
, we obtain a JT gravity sector, which controls the low-temperature quantum fluctuation behavior of the black brane.

3.

By transforming into the NHR boundary coordinates
[
20
]
, we can rewrite the NHR metric in terms of the Schwarzian mode, which is the zero mode that emerge in the NHR boundary when doing the JT path integral.

4.

Finally, by integrating all Schwarzian modes in the NHR metric, we obtain the quantum-corrected NHR metic. Since the quantum-corrected metric should be continuous and smooth in the overlap region of NHR and FAR, we obtain the quantum-corrected FAR metric by matching the asymptotic behaviors of both regions.

The near-extremal RN AdS
5
black brane has the metric in the global coordinates:

ds^{2}=-\frac{u^{2}}{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}f(u)\,dt^{2}+\frac{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}{u^{2}}\frac{du^{2}}{f(u)}+\frac{u^{2}}{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}d\vec{x}\,^{2}\,,

(50)

with

f(u)=1-(1+Q^{2})\frac{u_{T}^{4}}{u^{4}}+Q^{2}\frac{u_{T}^{6}}{u^{6}}\,,\quad T_{h}=\frac{(2-Q^{2})u_{T}}{2\pi L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}\,.

(51)

Now, let us take the extremal limit
Q^{2}=2
and define a small parameter

\lambda\equiv\frac{u-u_{T}}{u_{T}}

(52)

to probe the near-horizon region. Taking the
\lambda\to 0
limit and keeping only the leading order, the metric becomes

ds^{2}=-\frac{12(u-u_{T})^{2}}{L^{2}_{\text{AdS}}}dt^{2}+\frac{L^{2}_{\text{AdS}}}{12(u-u_{T})^{2}}du^{2}+\frac{u_{T}^{2}}{L^{2}_{\text{AdS}}}d\vec{x}\,^{2}\,.

(53)

In order to introduce quantum gravity corrections, we need to adopt a further change of coordinates:

u=u_{T}+\frac{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}{12\zeta}

(54)

to bring the near-horizon metric into a form with an explicit AdS
2
part. Consequently, the metric becomes

ds^{2}=-\frac{L^{2}_{\text{AdS}}}{12\zeta^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{L^{2}_{\text{AdS}}}{12\zeta^{2}}d\zeta^{2}+\frac{u_{T}^{2}}{L^{2}_{\text{AdS}}}d\vec{x}\,^{2}\,,

(55)

where the AdS
2
part is exactly the same as in
[
20
]
:

ds^{2}=-\frac{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}{12\zeta^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}{12\zeta^{2}}d\zeta^{2}=-\frac{L_{2}^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{L_{2}^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}d\zeta^{2}\,.

(56)

We have introduced an AdS
2
radius
L_{2}
, which is related to the AdS
5
radius
L_{\text{AdS}}
via

L_{2}\equiv\frac{L_{\text{AdS}}}{\sqrt{12}}\,.

(57)

In this near-horizon AdS
2
, we perform a Wick rotation and introduce the quantum correction factor
h(\zeta)
as in
[
20
]
:

h(\zeta)=1+\frac{3}{2\pi^{4}}\left(\frac{\zeta}{C}\right)^{2}-\frac{15}{2\pi^{6}}\left(\frac{\zeta}{C}\right)^{3}+\frac{315}{8\pi^{8}}\left(\frac{\zeta}{C}\right)^{4}+\cdots\,.

(58)

Next, we can apply an inverse coordinate transformation:

\zeta=\frac{L^{2}_{\text{AdS}}}{12(u-u_{T})}\,,

(59)

to lift the near-horizon AdS
2
’s quantum correction factor back to the original AdS
5
black brane coordinates. Then, the quantum correction factor
h
in the original coordinate
u
is given by

\displaystyle h(u)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{L_{\text{AdS}}^{4}}{96\,\pi^{4}C^{2}(u-u_{T})^{2}}-\frac{5L_{\text{AdS}}^{6}}{1152\,\pi^{6}C^{3}(u-u_{T})^{3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{35L_{\text{AdS}}^{8}}{18432\,\pi^{8}C^{4}(u-u_{T})^{4}}+\cdots\,.

(60)

For later convenience, we define another coordinate
U
via

U\equiv\frac{u}{\alpha^{\prime}}\,,\quad U_{T}\equiv\frac{u_{T}}{\alpha^{\prime}}\,,\quad\text{with }\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}{R^{2}}\,,

(61)

where
\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}
denotes the effective string length. Finally, the quantum-corrected near-horizon AdS
5
metric becomes

\displaystyle\langle ds^{2}_{NHR}\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\prime}\Bigg[-h(U)\frac{12(U-U_{T})^{2}}{R^{2}}dt^{2}+h(U)\frac{R^{2}}{12(U-U_{T})^{2}}dU^{2}

(62)

\displaystyle+\frac{U_{T}^{2}}{R^{2}}d\vec{x}\,^{2}\Bigg]\,,

and the quantum correction factor is

\displaystyle h(U)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{R^{4}}{96\,\pi^{4}C^{2}(U-U_{T})^{2}}-\frac{5R^{6}}{1152\,\pi^{6}C^{3}(U-U_{T})^{3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{35R^{8}}{18432\,\pi^{8}C^{4}(U-U_{T})^{4}}+\cdots\,,

(63)

where the dots denote higher-order subleading terms.

In principle, (
62
) is the quantum-corrected metric in the near-horizon region, and the quantum correction factor has the asymptotic expression (
63
), which can only be used in the NHR. The full quantum-corrected AdS
5
metric should be obtained by requiring the continuous and smooth boundary conditions in the overlap region of the NHR and the FAR, i.e.,

\langle ds^{2}_{\text{NHR}}\rangle\big|_{\text{overlap}}=\langle ds^{2}_{\text{FAR}}\rangle\big|_{\text{overlap}}\,.

(64)

This condition fixes the quantum-corrected metric near the AdS
5
boundary to be

ds^{2}=\alpha^{\prime}\left[\frac{U^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(f(U)\,h(U)\,dt^{2}+d\vec{x}\,^{2}\right)+R^{2}f^{-1}(U)\,h(U)\frac{dU^{2}}{U^{2}}\right]\,,

(65)

which is the most relevant to the computations in this paper. Since the metric (
62
) with the quantum correction factor (
63
) can provide the asymptotic behavior near the NHR boundary by taking
(U-U_{T})/U_{T}\gg 1
. Then, we can match it with the asymptotic behavior of the FAR in the overlap region using the condition (
64
). We see that the
U\to\infty
asymptotics of (
62
) are consistent with (
65
). Hence, we take (
65
) as the quantum-corrected AdS
5
metric in the main text.

Appendix C
Subleading Quantum Correction Factor

In this appendix, we explain why the quantum correction factor only appears in the
(t,u)
-directions of the AdS
5
metric, or in other words, the spatial dimensions
d\vec{x}\,^{2}
do not receive quantum corrections at the leading order.

Let us still begin with the AdS
5
RN black brane metric in the global coordinates:

ds^{2}=-\frac{u^{2}}{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}f(u)\,dt^{2}+\frac{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}{u^{2}}\frac{du^{2}}{f(u)}+\frac{u^{2}}{L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}}d\vec{x}\,^{2},

(66)

with

f(u)=1-(1+Q^{2})\frac{u_{T}^{4}}{u^{4}}+Q^{2}\frac{u_{T}^{6}}{u^{6}},\quad T=\frac{(2-Q^{2})u_{T}}{2\pi L_{\text{AdS}}^{2}},

(67)

where
u_{T}
denotes the horizon location, and
\vec{x}
includes the coordinates
(r,x,y)
.

Besides the
(t,u)
-coordinates, we also expand the
\vec{x}
-directions of the metric near
(u-u_{T})/u_{T}
. Then, we obtain the near-horizon metric as

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(u-u_{T})^{2}}{L_{2}^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{L_{2}^{2}}{(u-u_{T})^{2}}du^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{u_{T}^{2}}{12L_{2}^{2}}+\frac{2(u-u_{T})u_{T}}{12L_{2}^{2}}+\frac{(u-u_{T})^{2}}{12L_{2}^{2}}\right)d\vec{x}\,^{2}\,,

(68)

where
L_{2}\equiv L_{\text{AdS}}/\sqrt{12}
is the AdS
2
radius in the near-horizon region. Note that in the near-horizon limit, the second line can be simplified into a leading constant term,
u_{T}^{2}/(12L_{2}^{2})
, since the other two terms are subleading compared to this term.

By defining a new coordinate transformation,
\zeta\equiv\frac{L_{2}^{2}}{u-u_{T}}
, we rewrite the NHR metric as

ds^{2}=-\frac{L_{2}^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}(-dt^{2}+d\zeta^{2})+\frac{u_{T}^{2}}{12L_{2}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{2L_{2}^{2}}{u_{T}\zeta}+\frac{L_{2}^{4}}{u_{T}^{2}\zeta^{2}}\right)d\vec{x}\,^{2}\,,

(69)

from which we see that, by only keeping the leading-order terms, the metric in the NHR could be factorized into AdS
{}_{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
.

Now, we consider quantum corrections to this metric. The quantum correction to the AdS
2
part can be introduced similarly as in
[
20
]
. We first define the light-cone coordinates
x_{\pm}
via

t=\frac{x_{+}+x_{-}}{2}\,,\quad\zeta=\frac{x_{+}-x_{-}}{2}\,.

(70)

Consequently, the metric in the light-cone coordinates becomes

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{\text{\text{AdS}}_{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4L_{2}^{2}}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{2}}\,dx_{+}dx_{-}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{u_{T}^{2}}{12L_{2}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{4L_{2}^{2}}{u_{T}(x_{+}-x_{-})}+\frac{4L_{2}^{4}}{u_{T}^{2}(x_{+}-x_{-})^{2}}\right)d\vec{x}\,^{2}\,.

(71)

These light-cone coordinates for a boundary observer can be lifted to Schwarzian modes when the quantum fluctuations of the NHR boundary are turned on. Thus, we have

ds^{2}_{\text{\text{AdS}}_{2}}=\frac{4L_{2}^{2}\,F^{\prime}(x_{+})\,F^{\prime}(x_{-})}{(F(x_{+})-F(x_{-}))^{2}}\,dx_{+}\,dx_{-}\,.

(72)

We can define a Schwarzian bilocal operator:

O(x_{+},x_{-})\equiv\frac{F^{\prime}(x_{+})\,F^{\prime}(x_{-})}{\bigl(F(x_{+})-F(x_{-})\bigr)^{2}}\,,

(73)

whose expectation value can be calculated in both the exact and the perturbative methods
[
33
]
. Therefore, the quantum-corrected AdS
2
metric can be expressed as

\langle ds_{\text{AdS}_{2}}^{2}\rangle=4L_{2}^{2}\,\langle O(x_{+},x_{-})\rangle\,dx_{+}dx_{-}.

(74)

By performing an inverse transformation back to the AdS
2
Poincaré coordinates, we rewrite the quantum-corrected metric in the form of the classical metric with a quantum correction factor
h(\zeta)
, i.e.,

\langle ds_{\text{AdS}_{2}}^{2}\rangle=h(\zeta)\,\frac{L_{2}^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}(-dt^{2}+d\zeta^{2})\,.

(75)

For the
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-part of the near-horizon metric, the subleading terms also contain light-cone coordinates, which receive quantum fluctuations when lifted to the Schwarzian mode. Hence, in principle, we should also consider the quantum correction to the
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-part of the near-horizon metric. However, we will show that this quantum correction is more subleading and can be ignored. The
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-part of the near-horizon metric with subleading terms is

ds^{2}_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}=\frac{u_{T}^{2}}{12L_{2}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{4L_{2}^{2}}{u_{T}(x_{+}-x_{-})}+\frac{4L_{2}^{4}}{u_{T}^{2}(x_{+}-x_{-})^{2}}\right)\,d\vec{x}\,^{2}\,,

(76)

with
u_{T}/L_{2}\gg 1
. After lifting the light-cone coordinates,
x_{\pm}
, to the Schwarzian mode, we can derive the quantum correction from the Schwarzian mode’s correlation functions, which can be evaluated perturbatively by using
F(t)=t+\epsilon(t)
and expanding in small
\epsilon(t)
. More explicitly,

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\frac{1}{F(x_{+})-F(x_{-})}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x_{+}-x_{-}}+\frac{\langle(\epsilon(x_{+})-\epsilon(x_{-}))^{2}\rangle}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\,,

(77)

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\frac{1}{(F(x_{+})-F(x_{-}))^{2}}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{2}}+\frac{3\langle(\epsilon(x_{+})-\epsilon(x_{-})^{2})\rangle}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\,.

(78)

Therefore, the quantum-corrected
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-part of the metric with subleading terms is

\displaystyle\langle ds^{2}_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{T}^{2}}{12L_{2}^{2}}\Bigg[1+\frac{4L_{2}^{2}}{u_{T}(x_{+}-x_{-})}+\frac{4L_{2}^{2}}{u_{T}}\left(\frac{\langle(\epsilon(x_{+})-\epsilon(x_{-}))^{2}\rangle}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{3}}\right)

(79)

\displaystyle+\frac{4L_{2}^{4}}{u_{T}^{2}(x_{+}-x_{-})^{2}}+\frac{4L^{2}_{2}}{u_{T}^{2}}\left(\frac{3\langle(\epsilon(x_{+})-\epsilon(x_{-}))^{2}\rangle}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\Bigg]d\vec{x}\,^{2}.

As we can see, the first quantum correction appears at the order
\mathcal{O}(L_{2}^{2}\epsilon^{2}/u_{T})
or
\mathcal{O}(L_{2}^{2}\epsilon^{2}/u_{T}^{2})
.

On the other hand, the quantum correction to the AdS
2
-part is carried by the bilocal operator, which can also be evaluated in a perturbative way as follows:

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{F^{\prime}(x_{+})F^{\prime}(x_{-})}{(F(x_{+})-F(x_{-}))^{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{2}}+\frac{3\langle(\epsilon(x_{+})-\epsilon(x_{-}))^{2}\rangle}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{4}}-\frac{2\langle(\epsilon(x_{+})-\epsilon(x_{-}))\epsilon^{\prime}(x_{+})\rangle}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{3}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\langle(\epsilon(x_{+})-\epsilon(x_{-}))\epsilon^{\prime}(x_{-})\rangle}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{3}}+\frac{\langle\epsilon^{\prime}(x_{+})\epsilon^{\prime}(x_{-})\rangle}{(x_{+}-x_{-})^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3}).

Hence, the leading quantum correction to the AdS
2
-part is of order
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
, while the
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-part is of order
\mathcal{O}(L_{2}\epsilon^{2}/u_{T})
, which indicates that the Schwarzian fluctuation’s contribution to the
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-part is further suppressed in the NHR (due to
u_{T}/L_{2}\gg 1
) and can be ignored compared to the leading-order corrections. We should note that this argument is valid for the NHR only, and the AdS
2
Poincaré coordinates
(t,\zeta)
cannot cover the full AdS
5
, taking
\zeta\to 0
actually corresponding to the AdS
2
boundary. Therefore, we finally get a quantum-corrected NHR metric as

\langle ds^{2}_{\text{NHR}}\rangle=h(\zeta)\frac{L^{2}_{2}}{\zeta^{2}}(-dt^{2}+d\zeta^{2})+\frac{u_{T}^{2}}{12L_{2}^{2}}d\vec{x}\,^{2}.

(80)

By imposing the continuous and smooth conditions in the overlap region, we match the NHR and the FAR metrics as follows:

\langle ds^{2}_{\text{NHR}}\rangle\big|_{\text{overlap}}=\langle ds^{2}_{\text{FAR}}\rangle\big|_{\text{overlap}}\,.

(81)

Thus, we can glue on the quantum correction factor to the FAR metric to obtain the quantum-corrected RN AdS
5
black brane metric:

\langle ds^{2}\rangle=-h(u)\frac{u^{2}}{L^{2}}f(u)dt^{2}+h(u)\frac{L^{2}}{u^{2}}\frac{du^{2}}{f(u)}+\frac{u^{2}}{L^{2}}d\vec{x}\,^{2}\,.

(82)

When we evaluate the quantum correction factor
h(u)
in FAR, it corresponds to the near-boundary expansion of
h(u)
.

Dialogue is Better Than Monologue: Instructing Medical LLMs via Strategical Conversations
Zijie Liu, Xinyu Zhao, Jie Peng, Zhuangdi Zhu, Qingyu Chen, Xia Hu, Tianlong Chen

Abstract
Current medical AI systems often fail to replicate real-world clinical reasoning, as they are predominantly trained and evaluated on static text and question-answer tasks. These tuning methods and benchmarks overlook critical aspects like evidence-based reasoning and handling distracting information. To bridge this gap, we introduce a novel benchmark that simulates real-world diagnostic scenarios, integrating noise and difficulty levels aligned with USMLE standards. Moreover, we explore dialogue-based fine-tuning, which transforms static datasets into conversational formats to better capture iterative reasoning processes. Experiments show that dialogue-tuned models outperform traditional methods, with improvements of 9.64 % 9.64\% in multi-round reasoning scenarios and 6.18 % 6.18\% in accuracy in a noisy environment. Our findings highlight dialogue tuning as a promising approach for advancing clinically aligned and robust medical AI systems.

(i) First, in real-world clinical scenarios, diagnostic reasoning progresses from patient history review, through analysis of clinical findings and test results, to a comprehensive diagnostic conclusion. This requires diagnostic hypotheses to be refined through a systematic evaluation of evidence.
Crucially
, this process involves not only identifying relevant evidence but also determining the next logical steps in gathering information. For instance, when presented with a set of clinical findings or test results, a doctor must identify what additional symptoms to inquire about, as well as what further examinations or tests the patient should undergo. This iterative, step-by-step approach
conflicts
with the single-round judgments or explanations emphasized by current multi-choice tasks. (ii) Furthermore, real-world diagnostics often involve navigating ambiguous and noisy information, extracting relevant evidence, and synthesizing it to arrive at accurate conclusions. Existing benchmarks fail to capture these critical aspects, instead focusing on straightforward pattern recognition or information retrieval. Based on these gaps, it is worth exploring alternative benchmark formats beyond standard question-answering tasks - formats that can better
reflect
systematic evidence-based analysis and step-by-step clinical reasoning.

To address these gaps, we first analyze
existing benchmarks
and observe
that clinical information typically follows a natural sequence: from the patient background through examination findings to the final diagnosis. the sequential order mirrors real-world diagnostic processes.
Based on these insights, we reformulate traditional question-answering tasks into evidence retrieval and ranking.
In this new benchmark, models are required to identify and integrate relevant evidence to support their answers under two tasks: ❶ the one-round ranking task that requires the model to rank all evidence once, and ❷ the multi-round ranking employing an iterative approach that retrieves and ranks evidence over several steps, gradually constructing the necessary context for decision-making.
This reformulation reflects the reasoning process that doctors use when combining and synthesizing clinical evidence to reach a diagnosis or treatment decision.
To reflect real-world challenging cases and noisy clinical contexts, we also introduce varying levels of difficulty—basic, advanced, and challenging—aligned with USMLE frameworks and irrelevant information sampled from other documents to mimic noise.

We explore dialogue-based fine-tuning as a method for training medical LLMs, converting multiple-choice datasets and PubMed articles into dialogue structures to enhance reasoning in diagnosis decisions. Our experiments show that this approach outperforms traditional methods on evidence retrieval and ranking benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving the model’s ability to capture intricate relationships and critical insights essential for medical reasoning. 3.1
Preliminary

Monologue Tuning.
Monologue-style datasets primarily involve static, non-interactive formats such as multi-choice question answering and article-based tasks. Medical models often rely on biomedical literature for pretraining or fine-tuning, leveraging either the entire article or just the abstract. Moreover, fine-tuning on multi-choice question-answering datasets is a standard practice for many medical AI systems. These datasets typically include structured knowledge representations, such as curated facts, guidelines, or expert-derived annotations, which enable models to build a foundational understanding of domain-specific information.
Although effective for knowledge assessment, these monologue formats often fail to mimic the complexity of real-world diagnostic reasoning, which requires iterative problem-solving and logical synthesis.

Dialogue Tuning.

Dialogue datasets, in contrast, simulate real-world interactions by focusing on dynamic exchanges between patients and doctors. These datasets emphasize the iterative nature of diagnostic reasoning, where information unfolds progressively over multiple conversational turns. Notably, doctor-patient dialogues showcase how doctors determine next steps based on the information provided. This process highlights how doctors gather details and arrive at a final diagnosis. Compared to monologues, dialogues inherently capture the dynamic and interactive reasoning process characteristic of real-world diagnostic scenarios.

3.2
Why We Need Dialogue Tuning?

Dialogue tuning is proposed as a more effective approach for capturing logical relationships, as the dialogue format inherently mirrors the reasoning process found in human doctor-patient interactions.

Interactive Nature of Dialogue.
The dialogue format enables an interactive and question-driven reasoning process that mirrors how doctors approach diagnostic reasoning in clinical practice. In real-world settings, doctors iteratively ask questions to gather relevant evidences, with each exchange adding context and reducing uncertainty. Thus, we formulate this process in terms of entropy reduction via useful evidence increasing.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{t}
denote the Evidence set accumulated up to step
t
. With each new piece of information
e_{\text{new}}
collected, the total information is updated as:

\mathcal{E}_{t+1}=\mathcal{E}_{t}+e_{\text{new}}=\mathcal{E}_{t}\cup e_{\text{new}},\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{E}_{t}\cap e_{\text{new}}=\varnothing.

In this paper, we apply conditional entropy to describe the uncertainty about the final diagnosis
D
at step
t
as
H_{t}=H(D\mid\mathcal{E}_{t})
. Then we can define the reduction in entropy after incorporating the new information.

\displaystyle\Delta H_{t}

\displaystyle=H_{t}-H_{t+1}

\displaystyle=H(D\mid\mathcal{E}_{t})-H(D\mid\mathcal{E}_{t},e_{\mathrm{new}})

\displaystyle=\bigl[H(D,e_{\mathrm{new}}\mid\mathcal{E}_{t})-H(e_{\mathrm{new}}\mid D,\mathcal{E}_{t})\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad-\bigl[H(D,e_{\mathrm{new}}\mid\mathcal{E}_{t})-H(e_{\mathrm{new}}\mid\mathcal{E}_{t})\bigr]

\displaystyle=H(e_{\mathrm{new}}\mid\mathcal{E}_{t})-H(e_{\mathrm{new}}\mid D,\mathcal{E}_{t})

\displaystyle=I(D;e_{\mathrm{new}}\mid\mathcal{E}_{t})

The conditional mutual information
I(D;e_{\text{new}}|\mathcal{E}_{t})
is always non-negative, which reflects that
\Delta H_{t}
always be positive, indicating that uncertainty decreases as new information is integrated. It reflects how new information systematically reduces diagnostic uncertainty, mirroring the way doctors refine their understanding in clinical settings. The step-by-step approach naturally organizes information into logical and systematic structures, clarifying the reasoning path. By training models in this format, we align their reasoning processes with real-world practices, promoting a structured understanding of how evidence supports conclusions.

Empirical Support for Dialogue Tuning.
Our experiments demonstrate that dialogue tuning significantly outperforms multi-choice and article-based tuning in tasks designed with our benchmark. Key findings include:
❶ Dialogue-tuned models exhibit higher accuracy in reasoning tasks, particularly those requiring multi-step integration of evidence.
❷ They achieve better generalization to unseen cases, suggesting that the dialogue format enhances their capacity to reason beyond the training data.
❸ Dialogue-tuned models remain robust even when tackling challenging tasks or integrating noisy inputs, maintaining performance where other approaches falter.

3.3
Task Reformulation to Enable Dialogue Tuning

To better simulate real-world doctor-patient interactions, we reformulated existing multi-choice tasks and article-based datasets into a dialogue format.

Multi-Choice to Dialogue.
For multi-choice tasks, the reformulation process involved converting each question and its associated context into a doctor-patient dialogue. The primary goal was to create a natural interaction where the patient describes their symptoms and history, and the doctor guides the conversation toward the final diagnosis and treatment. The model was prompted to retain all relevant details, including personal information, symptoms, examination findings, diagnosis, and the final answer. Importantly, the dialogue was crafted to incorporate the final answer verbatim without alteration, ensuring alignment with the original task objectives.

Article-Based Tasks to Dialogue.
For article-based tasks, the reformulation similarly involved converting paragraphs of medical information into structured dialogues. The transformation emphasized capturing the logical flow of evidence presentation as it would occur in a doctor-patient scenario. The dialogue included all pertinent details from the article and synthesized into a conversational format. The model was instructed to avoid introducing any new information and to use natural language to connect details while ensuring the dialogue remained concise and coherent.

Implementation.
We employed the LLaMA 3.1 8B to perform the task reformulation. For each dataset, we designed specific prompts tailored to guide the model in generating the desired dialogue format while adhering to the constraints. These prompts ensured that the original task’s essential details were faithfully preserved in the reformulated dialogue. The specific prompts used for multi-choice and article-based tasks are provided in the Appendix for reference.

4
Our Benchmark: Muddy Maze

To evaluate the reasoning abilities of models under realistic medical scenarios, we constructed a benchmark called
Muddy Maze
, designed to challenge models on logical reasoning and evidence-based decision-making. The benchmark captures the complexities of real-world diagnostic reasoning by doctors and includes multiple layers of difficulty and distracting information.

4.1
Dataset Sources

Muddy Maze is built using the following datasets, carefully chosen for their relevance and alignment with real-world medical tasks:
❶
MedQA
: Includes questions from USMLE Step 1, Step 2, and Step 3 exams. Step 1 questions requiring foundational medical knowledge and straightforward reasoning. Step 2 and Step 3 questions focusing on clinical reasoning and decision-making.
❷
MedBullets Step 2/3
: Contains high-quality questions for USMLE Step 2 and Step 3 preparation.
❸
JAMA Challenge
: Includes complex, real-world clinical cases published in the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA). These cases designed to simulate the most difficult and ambiguous medical reasoning scenarios. The preprocessing steps for MedBullets and the JAMA Challenge follow the methodology outlined in
(
Chen et al. 2024a
)
.

4.2
Difficulty Levels Aligned with USMLE

The benchmark is divided into three difficulty levels to reflect the progressive challenges of medical training:

•

Basic Level.
Based on MedQA Step 1 questions. Focuses on foundational medical knowledge with straightforward evidence identification.

•

Advanced Level.
Combines MedQA Step 2 & Step 3 and MedBullets questions. Requires the integration of multiple pieces of evidence and reasoning through complex information.

•

Challenge Level.
Derived from JAMA Challenge cases. Simulates challenging real-world clinical cases from a range of medical domains and is more unusual.

4.3
Noise Levels Aligned with the Real World

The benchmark can randomly select irrelevant evidence from the dataset and add it to the current evidence set to challenge the LLM’s judgment. If the noise level is set to 0, no additional information is included, and all evidence contributes directly to the final diagnosis. Otherwise, random noise is added to the evidence pool. The purpose of this functionality is to evaluate whether the model can maintain its performance when faced with noisy information, simulating real-world scenarios. Similarly, doctors must identify and rely only on relevant information for the final diagnosis rather than treating all information as equally important.

Traditional question-answering tasks were reformulated into
evidence ranking tasks
to emphasize reasoning:

•

One-Round Evidence Ranking.

In this task, the model is provided with the background information, a question-answer pair, and a pool of evidence sentences. The objective is to determine the correct sequential order in which evidence should be added to the background information to logically support the transition from the question to the answer. The model must consider the dependencies between evidence sentences and infer how each piece contributes to building a complete reasoning chain in a single step. This task tests the model’s ability to reason holistically and deduce the necessary evidence order efficiently.

•

Multi-Round Evidence Ranking.

In this task, the model iteratively selects one piece of evidence at each step, adding it to the background information. After each selection, the background is updated with the newly added evidence, which the model must use to inform its next decision. In this step-by-step process, the model has to figure out the most important piece of evidence for each stage. This narrows down the range of diagnostic hypotheses until the whole chain of reasoning is established.

This iterative process mirrors the way doctors incrementally gather and synthesize evidence in clinical reasoning, ensuring that each piece of information contributes to the final diagnosis or decision-making.

The benchmark is explicitly designed to reflect real-world medical practice, and the multi-round process can be described with the (Algorithm
1
):

•

Step-by-Step Reasoning.
Multi-round evidence ranking mirrors how doctors iteratively gather and synthesize evidence during diagnosis and treatment planning.

•

Handling Distractors.
The inclusion of irrelevant evidence simulates the noisy and complex environment of real-world clinical data.

•

Complex Scenarios.
The progressive difficulty levels (basic, advanced, challenge) mirror the stages of medical training and ensure comprehensive testing.

4.5
Evaluation Metric

Multi-Hop Accuracy.
metric evaluates the model’s ability to both identify the correct evidence sentences and place them in the correct sequential order to support the reasoning chain. The metric is defined as:

\text{Multi-Hop Accuracy}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{I}(e_{i}=\hat{e}_{i}\wedge p_{i}=\hat{p}_{i})}{N},

where,
N
represents the total number of evidence sentences in the reasoning chain. Each
e_{i}
is the
i
-th evidence sentence in the ground-truth reasoning chain, while
\hat{e}_{i}
is the corresponding evidence sentence selected by the model. Similarly,
p_{i}
denotes the position of the
i
-th evidence sentence in the ground truth, and
\hat{p}_{i}
is the position assigned by the model. The indicator function
\mathbb{I}
returns
1
if both
e_{i}=\hat{e}_{i}
and
p_{i}=\hat{p}_{i}
, and
0
otherwise.

This formula calculates the proportion of evidence sentences where the model correctly identifies both the content and its position in the reasoning chain, providing a comprehensive measure of reasoning accuracy.

Single-Wise Accuracy.
metric evaluates the model’s ability to predict correct pairs of consecutive evidence sentences in the reasoning chain. This metric measures how well the model captures the sequential relationship between evidence elements, ensuring that not only the individual evidence sentences but also their relationship in the chain is accurate. The metric is defined as:

\text{Single-Wise Accuracy}=\\
\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathbb{I}\begin{pmatrix}(e_{i},e_{i+1})=(\hat{e}_{i},\hat{e}_{i+1})\lor\\
(e_{i},e_{i+1})=(\hat{e}_{i+1},\hat{e}_{i}),\end{pmatrix}

where
N
represents the total number of evidence sentences in the reasoning chain. The pair
(e_{i},e_{i+1})
denotes the ground-truth pair of consecutive evidence sentences in the reasoning chain, while
(\hat{e}_{i},\hat{e}_{i+1})
represents the corresponding pair predicted by the model. The accuracy is calculated using an indicator function
\mathbb{I}
, which returns
1
if
(e_{i},e_{i+1})=(\hat{e}_{i},\hat{e}_{i+1})
and
0
otherwise.

This metric highlights the model’s ability to maintain the correct sequential relationships between evidence elements, ensuring consistency in multi-round and one-round reasoning tasks. However, the metric is relatively loose, as it accommodates bidirectional consistency to account for reasoning paths that may flow in either direction.

How Does the Model Perform in Noisy Environments?
The results in the
Figure 7
demonstrate that the combined dialogue tuning method consistently outperforms the baseline tuning method across all noise levels, even as the noise increases to mimic real-world conditions. Notably, models trained with dialogue tuning exhibit less performance degradation as noise levels increase, indicating their enhanced resilience to real-world noisy conditions. For instance, LLaMA 3.2-3B achieves a
2.82\%
and
6.18\%
improvement in single-wise accuracy at noise levels 0 and 1, respectively, compared to the model without tuning. Even as noise increases, the dialogue-tuned models maintain higher accuracy than the baseline, confirming their ability to generalize better under adverse conditions. Furthermore, the robustness of dialogue tuning is evident across different architectures, including Qwen 2.5, which exhibits a
2.53\%
improvement in multi-hop accuracy at noise level 1 and
2.28\%
improvement at noise level 5. In contrast, baseline models struggle under high noise levels, highlighting the critical advantage of dialogue tuning in mitigating the impact of noise. These results affirm that dialogue tuning is a reliable approach for improving model performance and ensuring stability in noisy, real-world scenarios.

How Was Noise Managed During Evaluation?
The noise information used in the experiments was randomly selected from unrelated documents to simulate real-world scenarios where irrelevant information is present. During testing, it was observed that different irrelevant information impacted models to varying degrees. To ensure fairness and maintain consistent evaluation conditions, the noise content was fixed for each model. This approach allowed for a controlled comparison of the models’ performance under identical noise conditions, highlighting the effectiveness of dialogue tuning across diverse architectures and noise levels.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we introduce a novel benchmark,
Muddy Maze
, designed to evaluate the logical reasoning and evidence-based decision-making capabilities of language models in realistic medical scenarios. Moreover, we demonstrate that
dialogue tuning
significantly improves model performance compared to multi-choice and article-based tuning, through extensive experiments. Models trained in a dialogue format better capture logical relationships and exhibit robustness even in more challenged multi-step reasoning. Our evaluation metric quantifies these improvements by measuring both evidence identification and sequential ranking accuracy.

This work highlights the importance of a dynamical approach to advancing reasoning in medical AI systems. Dialogue tuning aligns training with the step-by-step cognitive processes required for diagnostic decision-making, providing a framework for developing more understandable and reliable models.

Future Work.

Future directions include expanding the benchmark to encompass a broader range of medical domains and modalities. We will also carefully check to see if the dialogue construction process will include the model’s hallucination and see if the model’s ability to reason remains consistent when the amount of noisy information rises sharply across different models.

Impact Statement

This work seeks to enhance medical AI systems’ capacity to reason through clinical scenarios in ways that align more closely with real-world medical practice. While the advancements proposed in this study offer significant potential to improve healthcare delivery, a comprehensive discussion of their broader implications is essential.

Benefits and Opportunities:

•

Enhanced medical AI systems could provide critical support for clinical decision-making, particularly in resource-constrained environments where specialist expertise is limited.

•

The proposed dialogue-based approach has the potential to create more interpretable and transparent medical AI systems, enabling clinicians to gain better insights into the underlying reasoning processes of LLMs..

•

The benchmark introduced in this work could aid in identifying potential failures of medical AI systems, facilitating safer deployment in clinical environments.

Potential Risks and Mitigations:

•

Despite improvements in reasoning capabilities, the model should not be employed for autonomous medical decision-making without human oversight to prevent potential errors.

•

The reliance on synthetic medical conversations for training may not fully encapsulate the complexity and nuances of real doctor-patient interactions.

•

Strict adherence to patient privacy and medical data protection regulations is essential to ensure ethical and compliant system deployment.

Future Considerations:

•

Further evaluation of these systems across diverse patient demographics and medical conditions is necessary to ensure equitable performance.

•

Successful implementation in clinical settings will require close collaboration with healthcare providers and rigorous adherence to medical validation protocols.

•

Continuous monitoring and updates will be critical to maintaining high performance standards and ensuring adaptability to evolving medical knowledge.

We believe that a transparent discussion of these impacts is vital for the responsible development and deployment of medical AI systems. While this work primarily aims to advance the field of Machine Learning and its applications in medicine, careful consideration of ethical implications and societal consequences remains integral to our approach.

A.3
Limitation

The dialogue generation process, which relies solely on LLaMA 3.1-8B, may introduce several significant biases that warrant careful examination. First, while the synthetic nature of these dialogues preserves medical accuracy, it may fail to capture the full spectrum of communication styles, cultural nuances, and socioeconomic factors that shape real doctor-patient interactions. Additionally, relying on a single large language model for dialogue generation could lead to model-specific biases, particularly in how it structures conversations and prioritizes certain types of medical information. Without explicit controls or considerations for diversity in the dialogue generation process, the resulting training data might produce medical LLMs that perform inconsistently across different patient populations and communication contexts.

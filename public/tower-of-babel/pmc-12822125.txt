GALNT3 is a novel target driving lymphomagenesis via O-glycosylation of FGFR2
Authors: Ruixin Sun, Wenhan Wang, Yixuan Gao, Kasimujiang Aximujiang, Yan Li, Agida Innocent, Zizhen Xu, Jiao Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDespite the dual roles of GALNT3 in various cellular processes in tumorigenesis of solid tumors, the clinical and biological significance of GALNT3 in lymphomagenesis remains largely unknown. Herein, our bioinformatics analysis uncovers that GALNT3 dependent glucose metabolism was significantly elevated in DLBCL. Our DLBCL cohort study revealed that GALNT3 positivity is significantly associated with worse prognosis and clinical outcomes in DLBCL pathogenesis. Biologically, GALNT3 overexpression were found to promote DLBCL cell proliferation and cell cycle progression, while silencing GALNT3 inhibited lymphomagenesis. RNA-seq uncovers that GALNT3 upregulated FGFR2-MAPK signaling pathway in DLBCL. Moreover, protein mass spectrometry identified two potential O-Glycosylation sites of FGFR2 (Thr319 and Ser299). Further point mutation of glycosylation sites, confirmed that GALNT3 O-Glycosylates FGFR2 through Thr319. Besides, in vitro and in vivo rescue experiments demonstrated that Thr319 is indispensable for GALNT3-FGFR2-MAPK induced lymphomagenesis. In vitro pharmacological inhibition of FGFR2 with a selective inhibitor Futibatinib further demonstrated that it inhibited DLBCL cell growth, cell proliferation, induced cell cycle arrest, promoted cell apoptosis. Further in vivo study found that combination of Futibatinib with chemotherapy displayed better anti-tumor activity relative to single drug therapy in DLBCL treatment. Collectively, our data highlight the importance of considering the GALNT3-FGFR2-MAPK signaling axis as an attractive therapeutic target for lymphomagenesis. Background

Polypeptide N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 3 (GALNT3), an initiating enzyme of mucin-type O-Glycosylation [1], which has been implicated in many cellular processes including signal transduction [2], cell adhesions [3] and immune responses [4]. GALNT3 is a transmembrane protein presents with a N terminus, a catalytic domain, and a lectin domain, which is essential for O-Glycosylation its substrates. GALNT3 participates in various physiological events to maintain the tissue homeostasis in most mammalian cells [5]. GALNT3 modulates calcium metabolism via O glycosylation of fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23) to sustain a normal phosphate level [6]. GALNT3 maintains cardiovascular system by activating TNFR/NF-kB signaling axis to reduce the damage of oxidative stress, inflammations, and apoptosis to cardiac smooth muscle cells [7, 8]. GALNT3 supports epithelial cells in Trophoblast Stem Cells via activating E cadherin mediated cell to cell adhesions [9]. Malfunction of GALNT3 triggers pathological diseases such as familial tumor-like calcinosis (FTC) [10], hyperphosphatemia (HHS) [11], and atherosclerosis [12] etc.

Apart from O-Glycosylation, GALNT3 can regulates diverse cellular events that associated with tumorigenesis including proliferation, chemoresistance, migration, and metastasis through regulation of downstream genes and multiple signaling pathways. In non-small cell lung cancer cells (NSCLC), GALNT3 impairs the self-renewal of cancer stem cells to alleviates tumorigenesis [13]. In colorectal cancer, GALNT3 mediates chemoresistance by activating PI3K/ATK signaling under the regulation of LINC01296/miR-26a complex [14]. It was also showed that GALNT3 positive stomach cancer patients are prone to metastasis to adjacent lymph node [15].

Overexpression of GALNT3 has found in various tumor models including lung adenocarcinoma [16], colorectal [17] and thyroid cancers [18]. However, its role in carcinogenesis remains controversial as most studies reveals its oncogenic roles, the tumor suppressor functions of GALNT3 has also been suggested in NSCLC [13]. Clinically, GALNT3 overexpression was found to be associated with worse prognosis and tumor progression in gastric [19], renal [20] and epithelial ovarian cancers [21] etc. With its multifaceted functions in carcinogenesis, GALNT3 may serve as a potential therapeutic target in many cancers.

Despite the dual roles of GALNT3 in the development and progression of solid tumors, little is known about GALNT3 in lymphomagenesis. Herein, we uncovered a novel role of GALTN3 in upregulating FGFR2-MAPK signaling pathway via O-Glycosylation Thr319, and the potential therapeutic implication of Futibatinib in improving lymphoma therapy.

Methods

Patient samples

All tissue samples were diagnosed according to World Health Organization classification criteria by attending hematopathologists at Ruijin hospital affiliated Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, and clinical information was obtained from electronic clinical records. DLBCLs were subclassified into the germinal center B-cell (GCB) and non-GCB subtypes based on the Hans’ immunohistochemistry algorithm [22].

Ethical approval

All DLBCL tissue blocks were retrieved from a retrospective study that was approved by Ruijin Hospital affiliated Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine ethical committee (2011-46). All animal experiments were performed under an Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee-approved protocol, and institutional guidelines for the proper and human use of animals in research were followed (JUMC2023-127-A).

Isolation of lymphocytes from normal tonsil tissue

Tonsil tissues were collected in cold HBSS supplemented with 2% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin and processed within 3 h. The tissue was minced into small fragments and mechanically dissociated by pressing through a 100-µm cell strainer using a sterile syringe plunger. Resulting cell suspension was filtered through a 70-µm strainer. Mononuclear cells were isolated by density gradient centrifugation using Ficoll-Paque PLUS at 800 g for 20 min at room temperature with the brake off. The mononuclear cell layer was collected and washed twice with PBS. Red blood cells were lysed if necessary. Cell count and viability were determined by trypan blue exclusion.

Cell lines and culture conditions

DLBCL cell lines SUDHL8, RIVA, Karpas-422 and DB were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 supplemented with 20% Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS) and 100 µg/mL penicillin/streptomycin. All cells were maintained in a humidified 37 °C/5% CO2 incubator. SUDHL8 (CRL-2961), and DB (CRL-2289) cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC), while Karpas-422 (ACC 32) and Riva (ACC 585) cells were purchased from Leibniz Institute DSMZ-German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures GmbHCell lines (DSMZ). Cell line authentication service was provided by Biowing Biotechnology (Shanghai, China) and all cells were routinely checked for the mycoplasma test using MycGuard™ Plus-Color One-Step Mycoplasma Detection Kit (yeasen, Cat No. 40612ES25).

Compounds and antibodies

Doxorubicin (Cat No. HY-15142), Futibatinib (Cat No. HY-100818) were purchased from the Med Chem Express (New Jersey, USA). All compounds, except for in vivo studies, were reconstituted in the DMSO (Abcone, Cat No. D26563), stored at 100 mM stock concentrations in -80 °C, and used at the indicated doses as suggested by the vendor. Immunoblotting antibodies CDK2 (Cat No. 2546T), CDT1 (Cat No. 8064T), cyclin A2 (Cat No. 91500T), cyclin E1 (Cat No. 20808T), cyclin D1 (Cat No. 2978T), Geminin (Cat No. 52508T), p-ERK (Cat No. 4370T), t-ERK (Cat No.4692T), p-AKT (Cat No.4060T), t-AKT (Cat No.4691 S), p-STAT5 (Cat No.4322T), t-STAT5 (Cat No. 9420 S) were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, USA); GALNT3 (Cat No.4314 S) was supplied by GeneTex Inc. FGFR2 (Cat No. AB289968) was purchased from Abcam (Waltham, USA). b-tubulin (Cat No. 12987-1-AP) and GAPDH (Cat No. 60008-1-Ig) antibodies were purchased from Proteintech (Rosemont, USA).

Cell proliferation assay

Cell proliferation was analyzed using a Cell Counting Kit-8 (YEASEN, China). Briefly, the cells were seeded onto 96-well plates at a density of 5000 cells per well. Ten microliters of CCK-8 were added to each well at 24 h, 48–72 h, respectively, and incubated at 37 °C for 4 h. Subsequently, the absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Annexin V APC/ 7AAD apoptosis detection assay

Detection of apoptosis was performed using the Annexin V-APC Apoptosis Detection Kit II (BD Bioscience, San Jose, USA) according to manufacturer’s protocol.

Lectin pull-down assays

Vicia Villosa Lectin (VVA) agarose beads (Vector Laboratories) were used to detect the changes in O-glycans, as reported. The cell lysates (1 mg) were incubated with 40 µl VVA-conjugated agarose beads at 4 °C for 16 h. The lectin/glycoprotein complexes were collected by centrifugation (10,000 rpm, 1 min). Glycoproteins were released from the complexes after boiled in 5 µl of 5× sample buffer for 5 min. The precipitated proteins were subjected to Immunoblotting using anti-FGFR2 antibody.

RNA transcription sequencing

RNA-seq was conducted as described previously [23].

Bioinformatical analysis

Differential analysis

Using the dataset GSE83632, consisting of 87 healthy samples and 76 DLBCL samples, differential analysis was conducted using the limma package in R version 4.1.0. The criteria for filtering were logFC > 0.5 and p < 0.05. Heatmaps were generated using the heatmap package, and volcano plots were created using ggplot2. A total of 1465 differentially expressed genes were identified, with 725 genes downregulated and 740 genes upregulated.

GO and KEGG analysis

To get the GO and KEGG analysis, We Input the differential genes into the DAVID database (ncifcrf.gov), select Homo sapiens as the species, filter based on p < 0.05, use the cnetplot package in R 4.1.0 to draw cnetplot graphs, and use ggplot2 to draw bubble charts.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis

To verify the protein- protein interaction network, we set up multiple proteins in the String network (string-db.org), select Homo sapiens as the species, input the intersecting genes to construct the PPI network, click on pathways related to energy metabolism to display the genes associated with these pathways, then filter out the genes involved in these pathways, and draw the core network.

Heatmap of metabolism-related genes

We extract the expression levels of genes related to energy metabolism pathways, and use the heatmap package in R4.1.0 to draw a heatmap.

Metabolism-related gene Cox analysis

Using the survival data from dataset GSE87371, we conducted multivariate and univariate Cox analysis on metabolism-related genes using the survival package in R4.1.0. Results were filtered based on p < 0.05, and forest plots were drawn for statistically significant prognostic factors.

Independent prognostic gene survival analysis

Compare univariate and multivariate Cox analysis, screen out prognostic factors with statistical significance in both as independent prognostic factors, group the genes with independent prognosis according to their gene expression levels, draw survival curves for high and low expression groups using the survival package in R4.1.0, and perform statistical analysis.

GSEA analysis

We screened the aforementioned genes based on a statistical value p < 0.05. For genes with statistical significance, we performed GSEA analysis on the sample groups according to the high and low expression levels of the genes. Conduct functional enrichment analysis on datasets related to KEGG pathways and glucose metabolism.

Immunohistochemistry

For IHC staining, after incubation at 60 °C for 4 h, paraffin sections were dewaxed and hydrated and then boiled with citrate buffer (pH 6.0) for antigen retrieval. The endogenous peroxidase activity of the slides was blocked by incubating them in hydrogen peroxide (3%) for 10 min. The sections were treated for 30 min with 5% goat serum before being incubated with anti-GALNT3 at 4 °C, respectively. Then, the sections were stained with diaminobenzidine (DAB) after 1 h of incubation with a secondary antibody, and two pathologists carried out a double blinded examinations to evaluate positivity GALNT3 staining.

Animal studies

DLBCL xenografts

Six weeks old female nude mice were purchased from Shanghai Ling Chang Biotechnology Co (Shanghai, China). DLBCL xenografts were established in the flanks of 6-weeks-old female nude mice by injecting approximately 10 million cells in 50% Matrigel.

Drug synergy

Mice bearing established xenografts (tumor volume, 100–200 mm3) were randomized into control or drugs treatment groups, and treated either with or without i.p. injection of 2.5 mg/kg Doxorubicin every two days for 5 times, or 35 mg/kg Futibatinib with gastric gavage every two days for 5 times or combos at the indicated time interval. Tumor sizes were determined every other day by directly measuring tumor with a caliper. Tumor volumes were calculated using the formula: length × width × width/2. The fold change of tumor volume was determined by normalizing each tumor measurement to its volume on day 0 at the beginning of drug administration.

Extraction of mononuclear cells from the mouse tumor mass

The tumor tissue dissected from the mouse axilla was placed on a 70 μm cell strainer seated in a 50 mL centrifuge tube, minced, and supplemented with 1 mL of culture medium for initial grinding. After rinsing, the cell suspension was placed on ice. Then, 1 mL of digestion solution was added (while keeping the tissue on the strainer), and the sample was incubated at 37 °C with shaking for 20 min. The digested suspension was filtered again through the strainer, followed by centrifugation at 500 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was discarded, and 2 mL of red blood cell lysis buffer was added. After another centrifugation at 500 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was removed, and the cells were washed twice with PBS to obtain a single-cell suspension.

Statistical analysis

Unpaired Student’s T-test was utilized to compare differences between two groups, and two-way ANOVA (Analysis of Variance) was performed to compare the differences among three or more groups. Kaplan-Meier was performed to create survival curves and compared them by means of the log-rank test. A Cox proportional hazards model was conducted for multivariate assessment. All bioinformatics statistical calculations were applied by means of R software (version 4.3.2). Two-tailed assessments were conducted throughout, with statistical significance, typically indicated by a p value below 0.05.

Result

GALNT3 dependent glucose metabolism was aberrantly expressed in DLBCL

Since metabolic reprogramming including glycolysis, lipid metabolism, and glutaminolysis triggers heterogenicity of DLBCL [24], and brings about the new challenges to precision therapy, thus its urgent to uncover novel metabolic proteins that regulate the bio-behavior of lymphoma cells, this may serve as a potential molecular biomarker. To investigate this, we first screened the differential expressed genes (DEGs) between normal (n = 87) and DLBCL patients (n = 76) from the TGCA database (GSE83632) and with total of 1465 genes, among which 740 DEGs were significantly upregulated, and 725 genes were significantly downregulated (Fig. 1A), further KEGG analysis revealed that over 100 genes were enriched in metabolic pathway (Fig. 1B). We intersected the differentially expressed gene with metabolic related genes groups, performed protein-protein interaction network and identified metabolic genes (Fig. 1C, red dots). We then performed cluster analysis and revealed that, these metabolic related genes were aberrantly expressed in DLBCL vs. healthy individuals (Fig. 1D). In addition, we utilized univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis to identify the prognosis-related metabolic genes and found that GALNT3 is associated with the hazard ratio of 3.706 (Fig. 1E, p < 0.001). Further gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) reveal that glucose metabolism, glycolysis, glucogenesis signaling pathways were indeed upregulated in DLBCL patients (Fig. 1F), which is consistent with previous finding that lymphoma a cells survival demand high glucose uptake [25] and elevated glucose metabolism [26]. Taken together, these data suggest that GALNT3 dependent on glucose metabolism may be a potential therapeutic target for DLBCL pathogenesis.

GALNT3 plays a potential prognostic role in DLBCL

To investigate the expression level of GALNT3 in a cohort of DLBCL (n = 40, please refer to Table 1 for patients’ clinical information), we first performed immunohistochemistry staining of GALNT3 in above cohort. DLBCL pathology was assessed by H& E staining and a normal tonsil was used as a negative control (Figure S1A & Figure S1B). The positivity of immunostaining was scored into 4 grades, in which score 1 and 2 were denoted to GALNT3−, while score 3 and 4 were classified as GALNT3+ staining (Fig. 2A). Clinical correlation analysis revealed that GALNT3 expression was significantly associated with serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) level, in which 13 of 16 (81%) were positive for GALNT3 (See Table 2, p = 0.046), and progressed disease (PD), among which 12 of 14 cases (85.7%) exhibit GALNT3 positive staining (See Table 2, p = 0.026). To further investigate whether GALNT3 is associated with patient survival in the DLBCL cohorts, we performed the Kaplan–Meier survival study in GALNT3 + and GALNT3- patients and identified that GALNT3 + group exhibit shorter overall survival (OS) and progression free survival (PFS) compared to GALNT3- group (Fig. 2B and C). Our data suggest that GALNT3 has a potential prognostic role in DLBCL pathogenesis.

GALNT3 sustains B cell lymphomagenesis by enhancing DLBCL proliferation

Although GALNT3 promotes tumorigenesis in various solid tumors, its role in hematological malignancy has not been investigated yet. To explore the endogenous expression of GALNT3 in DLBCL, we first determined GALNT3 mRNA and protein level in a panel of DLBCL cell lines and found that SUDHL10 exhibits the highest mRNA and protein level of GALNT3, while the other cell lines were presented with low or undetectable level of GALNT3 (Fig. 3A and B). Three DLBCL cell lines (Karpas-422, DB and RIVA) that expressing relatively low protein level of GALNT3 were selected to generate GALNT3 overexpressing stable transfectants. We transduced the above cells either with lentiviral encoding empty vector or GALNT3 overexpression plasmids for 48 h followed by flow cytometry sorting of GFP + cells to obtain stable transfectants. We then determined the overexpression efficiency of GALNT3 either by qPCR or immunoblotting assay. Indeed, GALNT3 mRNA or protein level was significantly upregulated in GALNT3 overexpressing group compared to the vector control group (Fig. 3C and D). In addition, GALNT3 silenced SUDHL10 stable transfectant was also generated using the lentiviral transduction system. As a result, GALNT3 mRNA and protein level was significantly downregulated in above stable transfectants compared with the scramble control counterparts (Figure S2A & 2B). To elucidate the biological impacts of GALNT3 in DLBCL, we labeled above stable transfectants with CFSE, 24 h (day 1) and 48 h (day 2) post labeling, GALNT3 overexpression peak was shifted to the left relative to the vector control indicating that DLBCL proliferation was increased, while silencing GALNT3 suppressed cell proliferation (Fig. 3E & Figure S2C). Counting of DLBCL cell numbers were also demonstrated showing that GALNT3 accelerate cell growth, whereas silencing GALNT3 de-accelerated cell growth (Fig. 3F & Figure S2D). In addition, Brdu labeling of GALNT3 overexpressed stable transfectants also demonstrated that GALNT3 promotes cell cycle progression represents as increased percentage of S phase concordance with decreased percentage of G1 phase (Fig. 3G). Vice versa, silencing of GALNT3 decreased the percentage of cells in S phase (Figure S2E). Furthermore, we synchronized cells to G1/S using either double thymidine or serum starvation methods, and this is consistent with cell cycle assay the expressions of cell cycle regulated proteins were upregulated upon GALNT3 induction (Fig. 3H left and right panels). These data suggest that GALNT3 plays an oncogenic role in DLBCL via enhancing cell proliferation.

GALNT3 upregulates FGFR2 mediated MAPK signaling pathway in DLBCL

To explore the underlying mechanism behinds GALNT3 enhancing tumorigenesis in DLBCL, RNA extracted from lentiviral encoding empty vector or GALNT3 overexpression plasmid transduced Karpas-422 and SUDHL8 cells were subjected to whole transcriptome sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis (GSE285695 & see data availability section). KEGG and GO analysis revealed that the FGFR2 and MAPK signaling pathways were significantly altered in GALNT3 overexpression cells (Fig. 4A and B, Figure S3A & S3B). This result was further confirmed by gene sets enrichment analysis showing up regulation of gene sets involved in FGFR2 and MAPK signaling pathway relative to empty vector (Fig. 4C & Figure S3C). Up-regulation of the expression of FGFR2, FGFR1, DUSP8 and MAPK14 genes were confirmed by qRT-PCR analysis in Karpas-422 and SUDHL8 cells transduced with GALNT3 overexpression lentiviral plasmids (Fig. 4D and G). In comparison, downregulation of the expressions of these genes was observed in GALNT3 silenced SUDHL10 cells (Figure S2F). Our data provide the evidence that GALNT3 may regulate FGFR2 mediated MAPK signaling pathway in DLBCL.

GALNT3 O glycosylates FGFR2 via Thr319

Since GALNT3 exerts its biological roles by initiating mucin-type (GalNAc-type) O-Glycosylation in most eukaryotes, we then set to explore whether GALNT3 regulates FGFR via glycosylation. We utilized VVA pull down assay in HEK293T cells co transfected either with FGFR2 wild type (WT)/scramble control, or FGFR2 WT/shGALNT3#1 or FGFR2 WT/shGALNT3#2 for 72 h followed by lectin pull down and immunoprecipitation. As a result, suppression of GALNT3 inhibited the glycosylation of FGFR2 compared to scramble control (Fig. 5A), this data suggests that GALNT3 may regulates FGFR2 via O-Glycosylation. To further explore the specific glycosylation sites that GALNT3 O-Glycosylating FGFR2, we performed protein mass analysis that identified two potential O-Glycosylation sites (Thr319 and Ser299) of FGFR2 protein in Riva cells a DLBCL cell line exhibits relative high level of endogenous FGFR2 (Fig. 5B). To verify the functions of these two O- Glycosylation sites, with point mutations of Thr319 to Ala319, Ser299 to Ala299 using site directed mutagenesis to generate the pLV3-CMV-MCS-N-3XFLAG-CopGFP-FGFR2 T319A and pLV3-CMV-MCS-N-3XFLAG-CopGFP-FGFR2 S299A plasmids. HEK293T cells transfected either with FGFR2 WT, FGFR2 T319A, or FGFR2 S299A for 48 h followed by VVA pull down assay. As demonstrated in Fig. 5C, mutation of Thr319 (T319A) decreased the O glycosylation compared to WT control, however, mutation of ser299 (S299A) shows no significant effect on FGFR2 glycosylation. To further confirm if GALNT3 regulates FGFR2 through Thr319, HEK293T cells co transfected either with FGFR2 T319A/GALNT3 OE or FGFR2 WT/GALNT3 OE for 48 h followed by VVA pull down assay. Not surprisingly, T319A significantly de-glycosylated FGFR2 compared to FGFR2 WT upon GALNT3 induction (Fig. 5D). Our results imply that GALNT3 modulates FGFR2 via a novel O-Glycosylation site at Thr319 in DLBCL.

Thr319 is fundamental for GALNT3-FGFR2-MAPK signaling axis induced lymphomagenesis

To elucidate whether mutation of FGFR2 O-Glycosylation Thr319 restored the oncogenic role of GALNT3 in DLBCL. We established lentiviral encoding GALNT3 OE/FGFR2 WT, or GALNT3 OE/FGFR2 T319A plasmids co transduced Riva or DB stable transfectants. Mutation of Thr319 significantly de-accelerated DLBCL cell growth in both RVIA and DB cells (Fig. 6A), decreased cell proliferation (Fig. 6B), and induced G1/S cell cycle arrest represented as decreased the percentage of S phase, accompanied with increased the percentage of G1 phase (Fig. 6C). Immunoblotting of the expressions of the cell cycle regulate proteins confirmed this result (Fig. 6D). Our in vitro data suggest that FGFR2 O-Glycosylation Thr319 is critical for GALNT3 to promote DLBCL cell growth, cell proliferation. To further verify this, we established GALNT3 OE/FGFR2 WT, or GALNT3 OE/FGFR2 T319A or transduced Riva xenograft mice, empty vector was included as control group. As indicated in Fig. 6E and F, T319A significantly decreased the tumor size, tumor volume. Mutation of Th319 also inhibited lymphomagenesis and induced apoptosis (Fig. 6G and H). Our findings provide supportive evidence for a pivotal role of FGFR2 O-Glycosylation Thr319 in sustaining GALNT3 mediated oncogenic functions in DLBCL.

Targeted Inhibition of FGFR2 by futibatinib displayed anti-tumor effects in DLBCL

To explore the biological significance of suppression of FGFR2 in vitro, we generated shFGFR2 silenced Riva stable transfectants using lentiviral transduction system. shFGFR2 significantly lowered endogenous FGFR2 mRNA and protein level (Figure S4A & S4B). In addition, cell growth and cell proliferation, was also inhibited upon silencing FGFR2 (Figure S4C & S4D). Moreover, silence FGFR2 induced DLBCL cell apoptosis, and decreased the percentage of cells in S phase, which indicate the inhibited progression of the cell cycle (Figure S4E & S4F). To further confirm genetic knockdown of FGFR2 induced biological impacts in DLBCL, we then treated Karpas-422, DB and Riva DLBCL cells with indicated doses of Futibatinib, a selective inhibitor of FGFR2 at 48 h and 72 h, respectively. Futibatinib was found to inhibit cell growth and induce cell cytotoxicity in time and doses dependent manner. (Figure 7A and B). In addition, Futibatinib (20 µM treatment) reduced cell proliferation and induced the elevation of the percentage of G1 phase cells upon treatment at 30 µM in RIVA and DB cells (Fig. 7C and D). After Futibatinib treatment at 20 µM, the expressions of CDK2, CDT1 and cyclin E was upregulated, while Cyclin A2 was downregulated in both cells (Fig. 7E). furthermore, the activation of MAPK signaling protein was also inhibited upon Futibatinib treatment at 20 µM. (Fig. 7F). Collectively, these data suggest that FGFR2 is a potential therapeutic target in DLBCL and further in vivo studies were needed to confirm the effect of Futibatinib on lymphomagenesis.

Futibatinib combined with chemotherapy demonstrated better anti-tumor efficacy in DLBCL

To investigate whether Futibatinib enhances the anti-tumor activity of chemotherapy in vitro, we treated Riva or DB cells either with Doxorubicin, Futibatinib alone or combination for 72 h followed by proliferation and cell viability assays. Interestingly, combination of Futibatinib with chemotherapy prohibited DLBCL cell proliferation and eradicated more tumor cells compared either to chemotherapy or Futibatinib treatment alone (Fig. 8A and B). To validate whether Futibatinib combined with other chemotherapy have a synergistic effect, we thus treated Riva or DB cells with Futibatinib and other chemotherapy at various ratios followed by flow cytometry analysis of cell viability. Indeed, Futibatinib well synergized with chemotherapy in both cells with CI less than 1 (Fig. 8C and D). To further investigate whether Futibatinib synergize with DOX in vivo, we established DLBCL xenografts by transplanting 10 million of Riva cells into nude mice (6 weeks, female) as described previously. 10 days post transplantation, xenografts were randomized and administrated either with or without Futibatinib (Gastric gavage) or DOX (oral) alone or combination at the indicated time and doses shown in Fig. 8E. Indeed, combo treatment alleviated DLBCL pathological characterization compared to either DOX or Futibatinib treatment alone (Fig. 8F). In addition, the tumor size, tumor volume was drastically decreased in response to combo treatment compared to single drug regimen (Fig. 8G and H). Furthermore, increased cell death and decreased proliferation rate of DLBCL cells were observed in combination group compared to treatment alone (Fig. 8I and J). In summary, our findings demonstrated the therapeutic potency of FGFR2 in DLBCL. Its role in enhancing the anti-tumor efficacy of chemotherapy also imply a novel therapeutic approach to improve the outcome to the standard treatment.

Discussion

In the present study, our observations for the first time clarified the potential prognostic role of GALNT3 in a DLBCL cohort and elucidated its oncogenic role in DLBCL pathogenesis, protein mass spectrometry uncovered a novel O-Glycosylation site Thr319 of FGFR2, blocking of this O-Glycosylation site abrogate GALNT3 induced lymphomagenesis. Pharmacological inhibition of FGFR2 by a selective inhibitor Futibatinib displayed potential therapeutic effects in inhibiting lymphomagenesis. Moreover, combination therapy of Futibatinib with chemotherapy demonstrated better anti-tumor activities relative to single drug alone treatment. These inspiring findings suggest the potential therapeutic implication of targeting GALNT3-FGFR2-MAPK signaling pathway for lymphoma therapy.

Since GALNT3 is an initiating enzyme of mucin O-Glycosylation, its role in carcinogenesis does not receive too much attention and debatable. Although limited evidence shows that GALNT3 expression correlates with poor prognosis in lung [16], renal and breast carcinomas [27], its potential therapeutic value in B cell lymphoma remains unclear. In the present study, our IHC staining in a DLBCL cohort (n = 40, see Table S1 for patients’ clinical information) identified that GALNT3 positivity is significantly associated with poor prognosis and chemoresistance in DLBCL pathogenesis. Collectively, our findings suggest that GALNT3 may serve as a potential prognostic biomarker for DLBCL, and further studies with more patients and longer follow-up are needed to verify these findings.

Although previous studies have demonstrated that GALNT3 modulates various cellular events related to tumor progression [28], chemoresistance [14], metastasis [29] and stemness [30] in solid tumor models, the biological impacts of GALNT3 in B cell lymphoma is ill defined. Our study elucidated that GALNT3 overexpression displayed the potency in sustaining DLBCL cell growth, cell proliferation, cell cycle progression. Silencing GALNT3 exhibited anti-tumor activity in DLBCL. Collectively, our findings provide evidence to support the oncogenic role of GALNT3 in DLBCL. However, in vivo biological studies were needed to confirm the oncogenic role of GALNT3 in B cell lymphoma.

Previous investigations have illustrated that GALNT3 participates in diseases including cancers through modulating PI3K/AKT [14], NF-kB [31, 32], AMPK [33] and Wnt-b catenin [34] signaling pathways. Our RNA seq screening uncovered that GALNT3 promotes lymphomagenesis in a FGFR-MAPK signaling dependent manner. It was well known that Fibroblast growth factors (FGFs) and their receptors play vital roles in cell fate determination and angiogenesis [35]. Dysregulation of the signaling axis driving tumorigenesis by modulating cell proliferation [36], apoptosis [37], invasion [38] and metastasis [39] in various solid tumor models. Here, we have demonstrated that FGFR2 knockdown displayed the potency against DLBCL progression by viability and proliferation inhibition, apoptosis activation, G0/G1 cell cycle arrest, which suggested its potential therapeutic value in DLBCL.

Despite the limited studies on the physiological roles of FGF/FGFR glycosylation in mammalian cells [40], the pathological role of FGF glycosylation is poor understood. Our protein mass spectrometry study found two potential O-Glycosylation sites of FGFR2 (Thr319 and Ser299), and further point mutation approach identified that Thr319 is critical for GALNT3-FGFR2-MAPK induced lymphomagenesis. Taken together, our findings for the first time demonstrated the importance of FGFR2 O-Glycosylation in the pathogenesis of hematological diseases.

Although the clinical efficacy of Futibatinib was well established in solid tumors [41–43] however, its role in hematological malignancy is not clear. Our DLBCL cell lines and xenografts studies show the anti-tumor effect of Futibatinib in DLBCL and provide a novel therapeutic option for DLBCL treatment. High doses of conventional chemotherapy toxicity could lead to worse clinical efficiency [44] and various clinical responses, and combination therapy has the potential to counterbalance the side effects of chemotherapy. Our study elucidated that Futibatinib and DOX combination therapy eradicated more DLBCL cells, displayed as reduced tumor size, volume, cell proliferation and induced apoptosis compared to single drug alone treatment, which support the potential of Futibatinib in combination chemotherapy for DLBCL treatment. However, since no small-molecule inhibitor of GALNT3 has been available to date, we were unable to perform in vivo combination of targeted GALNT3 inhibition and chemotherapy. As an alternative, we established xenograft mouse models transplanted either with scramble control, shGALNT3#1, and shGALNT3#2 transduced RIVA cells to evaluate whether GALNT3 inhibition enhances chemo-sensitivity in DLBCL in vivo. As shown in Figure S5, inhibition of GALNT3 significantly inhibited lymphomagenesis, increased drug sensitivity, suppressed DLBCL cell proliferation, and induced apoptosis. Our xenograft studies elucidated the potential therapeutical role of GALNT3 for DLBCL treatment. Future studies should prioritize the development of in vivo approaches, including the urgent need for a small-molecule inhibitor of GALNT3. In addition, further translational research is required to assess the potential of combination therapy in improving clinical outcomes for DLBCL.

Conclusion

In summary, our study demonstrates for the first time the oncogenicity role of GALNT3 in DLBCL, identifies a novel role of the GALNT3-FGFR2-MAPK signaling axis in lymphomagenesis, highlighted the potency of targeting this signaling axis as a promising therapeutic strategy for lymphoma therapy.

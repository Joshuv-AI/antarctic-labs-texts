A heuristic for ray class groups of quadratic number fields
Alex Bartel, Carlo Pagano

Abstract
. We formulate a model for the average behaviour of ray class groups of real quadratic fields with respect to a fixed rational modulus, locally at a finite set S S of odd primes. To that end, we introduce Arakelov ray class groups of a number field, and postulate that, locally at S S , the Arakelov ray class groups of real quadratic fields are distributed randomly with respect to a natural Cohen–Lenstra type probability measure. We show that our heuristics imply the Cohen–Lenstra heuristics on class groups of real quadratic fields, as well as equidistribution results on the fundamental unit of a real quadratic field modulo an integer, and are consistent with Varma’s results on average of sizes of 3 3 -torsion subgroups of ray class groups of quardratic fields.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Ray class groups

In
[
15
]
Varma determined the average size of the
3
-torsion subgroups
of the ray class groups of imaginary and of real quadratic fields, ordered
by absolute discriminant, with respect to a fixed rational modulus. This
raised the natural problem of finding a model that “explains” Varma’s formulae,
analogously to the way in which the Cohen–Lenstra heuristics
[
5
]
explained
the result of Davenport–Heilbronn
[
7
]
on the average size of
3
-torsion of class
groups of quadratic fields. Bhargava had already publicised this problem at an AIM workshop
back in 2011. The second author and Sofos have found such a
model for ray class groups of imaginary quadratic fields
[
12
]
.
In this paper we do the same for the family of real quadratic fields.

A central rôle in our work is played by Arakelov ray class groups of a number field.
It is a beautiful object that does not appear to have been
treated in the literature, and we devote some space to shining a spotlight on it
and investigating its properties. As a byproduct, in Section
2.2

we reinterpret a classical construction that assigns to each narrow ideal class of a real
quadratic field a modular geodesic: we upgrade this construction to a collection of
isometries from narrow Arakelov class groups, whose connected components
are narrow ideal classes, to the quotient of the hyperbolic upper half plane
by its isometry group
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
F
be a number field, let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
denote its ring of integers, and let

\mathfrak{m}=(\mathfrak{m}_{\f},\mathfrak{m}_{\infty})
be a modulus, where
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
and

\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}
is a set of real embeddings of
F
. Let
F^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
be the set
of all
\alpha\in F^{\times}
satisfying
\sigma(\alpha)>0
for all

\sigma\in\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}
and
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha-1)\geq\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\f})

for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
dividing
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
, where
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}

denotes the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic valuation. The ray class group

\Cl_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
of
F
with modulus
\mathfrak{m}
is the quotient of the group
\Id_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
of
all fractional ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
that are coprime to
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
by the subgroup of
all principal fractional ideals of the form
(\alpha)
for some

\alpha\in F^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
. The class group
\Cl_{F}
is
the ray class group
\Cl_{F}((\mathcal{O}_{F},\{\}))
. One has a canonical short exact sequence

(1.1)

\displaystyle\myseq_{F}^{\fin}(\mathfrak{m})\colon 0\to\frac{(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m}_{\f})^{\times}\times\{\pm 1\}^{\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}}}{(\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})_{\mymod\mathfrak{m}}}\to\Cl_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\to\Cl_{F}\to 0,

where
(\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times})_{\mymod\mathfrak{m}}
denotes the image of
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
in

(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m}_{\f})^{\times}\times\{\pm 1\}^{\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}}
under the canonical map given
by the quotient map on the first factor, and by

\alpha\mapsto(\sign(\sigma(\alpha))_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}}

on the second factor.

There is a well-established principle that a “random” algebraic object
is isomorphic to a given object
X
with probability propotional to
1/\#\Aut(X)
.
It forms the starting point of the Cohen–Lenstra heuristics, where it is conjectured
to apply to ideal class groups of imaginary quadratic fields
[
5
]
. The first
author and Lenstra showed in
[
3
]
that in order for this principle to
apply to class groups of general number fields, one should instead consider so-called
Arakelov class groups. We will show in Theorem
1.8
below that the
conjectures in
[
12
]
on ray class groups of imaginary quadratic number fields can
be understood as saying that, roughly speaking, the exact sequence
\myseq_{F}^{\fin}(\mathfrak{m})

is isomorphic to a given suitable exact sequence
E
with probaility proportional
to
1/\#\Aut(E)
. In order to make the same principle applicable to ray class groups of
real quadratic fields, we introduce the so-called Arakelov ray class group of a number field.

1.2.
Arakelov ray class groups

We refer to Section
2.1
for details that we leave out for now.
For a number field
F
, we define the following invariants attached to
F
.
Let
\overline{F_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}}
denote the quotient of
(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R})^{\times}

by its maximal compact subgroup.
The group
\overline{F_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}}
is isomorphic
to
\prod_{v\in S_{\infty}}\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, where
S_{\infty}
denotes the set of
infinite places of
F
. We have natural maps
\Id_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and

\overline{F_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, the first induced by the ideal norm,
and the second by the absolute value of the
\mathbb{R}
-algebra norm on
F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
.
It follows from the product formula that the image of
the natural map
F^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\to\Id_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\times\overline{F_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}}
is contained in the fibre
product
\Id_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\times_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\overline{F_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}}
, and we define the

Arakelov ray class group

\Pic_{F}^{0}(\mathfrak{m})
of
F
with modulus
\mathfrak{m}
to be the quotient
of
\Id_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\times_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\overline{F_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}}
by the image of
F^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
.
As we will explain in more detail in Section
2.1
, this is a compact abelian group.
When we have
\mathfrak{m}=(\mathcal{O}_{F},\{\})
, we will drop it from the notation, and we refer to
\Pic_{F}^{0}
as
the
Arakelov class group
of
F
.
The natural map
\Id_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\times_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\overline{F_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}}\to\Id_{F}\times_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\overline{F_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}}
induces a surjective map

\Pic_{F}^{0}(\mathfrak{m})\to\Pic_{F}^{0}
, which is the right hand map in the short exact sequence

(1.2)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\myseq^{\Ara}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\colon 0\to\frac{(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m}_{\f})^{\times}\times\{\pm 1\}^{\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}}}{(\mu_{F})_{\mymod\mathfrak{m}}}\to\Pic_{F}^{0}(\mathfrak{m})\to\Pic_{F}^{0}\to 0.

The great advantage of that exact sequence compared to the sequence

\myseq^{\fin}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
above is that if
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
is
generated by a fixed rational integer and
\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}
is empty,
then the dependence of the left hand term of
\myseq^{\Ara}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
on
F
is easy to
control: it only depends on
\mu_{F}
and
on the splitting behaviour of
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
in
F
. As a consequence, under these
hypotheses, any family of number fields that one considers in Cohen–Lenstra
type heuristics can be partitioned into finitely many subfamilies with easy to
compute densities such that the left hand term in
\myseq^{\Ara}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
is
constant in each subfamily.

Assumption 1.3
.

For the rest of the introduction, assume that
\mathfrak{m}_{\infty}
is empty and that

\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
is generated by a rational integer.

Under this assumption, it makes
sense to ask how
\Pic_{F}^{0}(\mathfrak{m})
behaves “on average” as
F
varies over natural
families of number fields. We will now formulate our main heuristic concerning
that behaviour and state the main theorems for the family of real quadratic fields.

1.3.
The main heuristic

Let
C_{2}
be a group of order
2
. Let
S
be a finite set of odd prime numbers,
let

\mathbb{Z}_{S}=\prod_{p\in S}\mathbb{Z}_{p}

be the ring of
S
-adic integers.
We call a torsion abelian group an
S
-group
if the order of every element is some
product of primes in
S
. If
C
is an abelian group,
abbreviate
\mathbb{Z}_{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}C
to
C_{S}
, and denote by
C(S^{\infty})

the
S^{\infty}
-torsion subgroup of
C
, i.e. the largest subgroup
of
C
that is an
S
-group. Let
\mathcal{G}^{-}_{\cpt}
be a full set
of representatives of isomorphism classes of finite abelian groups,
and let
\mathcal{G}^{+}_{\cpt}
be a full set of representatives of isomorphism classes of
compact abelian groups whose connected component of the identity is isomorphic to

\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}^{-}_{S}
be a full set of representatives
of isomorphism classes of finite abelian
S
-groups, and let
\mathcal{G}^{+}_{S}
be a
full set of representatives of isomorphism classes of abelian
S
-groups
whose maximal
S
-divisible subgroup is isomorphic to
\prod_{p\in S}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}

and whose quotient by that subgroup is finite. We endow all of these groups with the structure of a
C_{2}
-module,
with the non-trivial element acting by multiplication by
-1
.
Thus, for every imaginary, respectively real quadratic field
F
, the Arakelov class group
\Pic_{F}^{0}

is isomorphic, as a
\mathbb{Z}[C_{2}]
-module via the Galois action, to a unique element of
\mathcal{G}^{-}_{\cpt}
, respectively

\mathcal{G}^{+}_{\cpt}
, while
\Pic_{F}^{0}(S^{\infty})
is isomorphic to a unique element of
\mathcal{G}^{-}_{S}
,
respectively
\mathcal{G}^{+}_{S}
.

We will use the sign
\pm
to mean
“one of
+
or
-
”. We will shortly define other structures that depend on this
sign, and there,
\pm
will always be understood to denote the appropriate sign.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We will now set up the partition of the families of quadratic number fields
F

according to the splitting behaviour of
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
in
F
, as mentioned at the end of the previous
subsection. Slightly abusing notation, we will also regard
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
as an ideal in
varying rings of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. For every prime divisor
p
of
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
, fix a
degree
2
étale
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebra
A_{p}
, viewed as a
C_{2}
-module by identifying

C_{2}
with the automorphism group of
A_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, let
A=\prod_{p|\mathfrak{m}_{\f}}A_{p}
with the product
taken over prime divisors, let
\mathcal{O}_{A}
be the integral closure of
\prod_{p|\mathfrak{m}_{\f}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}

in
A
, and let
R=\mathcal{O}_{A}/\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
. Finally, set
U_{R}=R^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}
.
If
F
is a real, respectively imaginary quadratic number field other than
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
,
then the exact sequence (
1.2
) gives rise, upon taking the quotient of
U_{R}

by its maximal subgroup of order coprime to
S
, to a short exact sequence of
\mathbb{Z}[C_{2}]
-modules
of the form

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Theta\colon 0\to U_{R}(S^{\infty})\to B\to C\to 0,

where
C\in\mathcal{G}^{\pm}_{\cpt}
. Correspondingly, as a model for the

S^{\infty}
-torsion of the exact sequence (
1.2
) consider short
exact sequences

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Theta\colon 0\to U_{R}(S^{\infty})\to B\to C\to 0

with
C\in\mathcal{G}^{\pm}_{S}
.
Let

\displaystyle\Theta\colon

\displaystyle 0\to U_{R}(S^{\infty})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}B\to C\to 0,

\displaystyle\Theta^{\prime}\colon

\displaystyle 0\to U_{R}(S^{\infty})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i^{\prime}}}{{\to}}B^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}\to 0

be two sequences either both as in (
1.4
) or both as in (
1.5
).
We define an isomorphism
\Theta\to\Theta^{\prime}
to be a
C_{2}
-module
isomorphism
f\colon B\to B^{\prime}
that sends
\im i
isomorphically to

\im i^{\prime}
and such that the automorphism
(i^{\prime})^{-1}\circ f\circ i
of
U_{R}(S^{\infty})

is induced by a ring automorphism of
R
. So as to stress this last condition,
we will write
\Aut_{\ring}(\Theta)
for the group of such automorphims of

\Theta
.
Let
\mathcal{G}_{R,\cpt}^{\pm}
, respectively
\mathcal{G}_{R,S}^{\pm}
be a full set of representatives
of isomorphism classes of sequences as in (
1.4
), respectively as in (
1.5
).

It is easy to see, and will in particular follow from Proposition

4.5
, that every element
of
\mathcal{G}_{R,\cpt}^{\pm}
has finite automorphism group. Moreover, we will show in
Corollary
4.6
,
that the two sums
\sum_{\Theta\in\mathcal{G}_{R,\cpt}^{\pm}}1/\#\Aut_{\ring}\Theta
both converge,
to
c_{R}^{\pm}
, say.
We define discrete probability distributions
\mathbb{P}_{\cpt}^{\pm}
on

\mathcal{G}_{R,\cpt}^{\pm}
by

\mathbb{P}_{\cpt}^{\pm}\{\Theta\}=\frac{(c_{R}^{\pm})^{-1}}{\#\Aut_{\ring}\Theta}.

There are obvious functions
\mathcal{G}_{R,\cpt}^{\pm}\to\mathcal{G}_{R,S}^{\pm}
that
send an element
\Theta\in\mathcal{G}_{R,\cpt}^{\pm}
to the unique element
of
\mathcal{G}_{R,S}^{\pm}
that
\Theta(S^{\infty})
is isomorphic to. Let

\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
be the pushfowards of the probability distributions
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}_{\cpt}

under these functions.

If
f
is a complex-valued
L^{1}
-function on one of the two probability spaces

(\mathcal{G}_{R,S}^{\pm},\mathbb{P}^{\pm})
, then we denote its expected
value by
\mathbb{E}^{\pm}(f)
, and for every sequence
\Theta
as above we define
f(\Theta)
to be the
value of
f
on the unique element of
\mathcal{G}_{R,S}^{\pm}
that is isomorphic to
\Theta
.

For a real number
X>0
, let
\mathcal{F}_{X}^{-}(R)
respectively
\mathcal{F}_{X}^{+}(R)
be the set
of pairs
(F,r)
, where
F
is an imaginary, respectively real quadratic number
field of conductor less than
X
, and
r
is a
C_{2}
-equivariant ring isomorphism

r\colon R\to\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
. For every
X\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and for every

(F,r)\in\mathcal{F}_{X}^{\pm}(R)
except for
F\cong\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})
and
F\cong\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
,
the
S^{\infty}
-torsion
\myseq_{F}^{\Ara}(\mathfrak{m})(S^{\infty})
of the exact sequence

\myseq_{F}^{\Ara}(\mathfrak{m})
defined in (
1.2
)
is isomorphic, via
r
, to a unique element of
\mathcal{G}_{R,S}^{\pm}
, where
the
C_{2}
-action is induced by the Galois action on
F
.
Our main heuristic assumption reads as follows.

Heuristic 1.6
.

For
X\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{X}^{\pm}(R)
,
\mathcal{G}_{R,S}^{\pm}
, and
\mathbb{E}^{\pm}
be as just defined.
Then for every “reasonable”
\mathbb{C}
-valued function
f
on
\mathcal{G}_{R,S}^{\pm}
, the
average

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{(F,r)\in\mathcal{F}_{X}^{\pm}(R)}f(\myseq_{F}^{\Ara}(\mathfrak{m})(S^{\infty}))}{\#\mathcal{F}_{X}^{\pm}(R)}

exists, and is equal to
\mathbb{E}^{\pm}(f)
.

Remarks 1.7
.

(a)

The heuristic can be intuitively understood as saying that
for a “random” real, respectively imaginary quadratic field
F
,
the
S^{\infty}
-torsion of the exact sequence
S^{\Ara}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})

defined in (
1.2
) is isomorphic to a given
element of
\mathcal{G}_{R,S}^{\pm}
with a probability that is inversely proportional to the
number of automorphisms of the latter.

(b)

See
[
3
, §7]
for a detailed discussion of what functions might
be regarded as “reasonable” in the context of Cohen–Lenstra heuristics.
Here we will not concern ourselves further with this imprecision, but will
just suggest that it does not seem inconceivable that functions that are

L^{i}
for all
i\geq 1
with respect to
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
are all “reasonable”.

(c)

The finiteness condition on the set
S
can likely be dropped in the case
of real quadratic fields, but will be important for general families of number fields.
As was shown in
[
3
, §4]
, already the original Cohen–Lenstra–Martinet
heuristic is false without that assumption, in general.

(d)

In Heuristic
1.6
we enumerate real quadratic fields by conductor.
One could equivalently enumerate them by discriminant, which is traditionally
by far the most frequently used invariant to enumerate number fields. However,
it was shown in
[
3
, §6]
that for more general families of number
fields, the Cohen–Lenstra–Martinet heuristic is false when number fields
are enumerated by discriminant.

1.4.
Consequences for ray class groups

The simple postulate that the sequence
\myseq^{\Ara}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})(S^{\infty})
of the

S^{\infty}
-torsion of the Arakelov ray class group is distributed with
probability weights that are inversely proportional to the number of
automorphisms leads to very satisfying equidistribution predictions for the
sequence
\myseq^{\fin}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})(S^{\infty})
of the
S^{\infty}
-torsion
subgroup of the ray class group. We will formulate these consequences somewhat
sloppily here, and refer to Section
4
for precise formulations.

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose that Heuristic
1.6
holds for the functions
f
as in

4.8
–
4.12
. Let
g
be the non-trivial element
of

. Then the following assertions are true.

(a)

The Cohen–Lenstra heuristic for
S
-Hall subgroups

(\Cl_{F})(S^{\infty})
of the class groups of imaginary and of real
quadratic number fields holds.

(b)

In the limit as

, as

runs
over

2.1.
Definitions and basic properties

We continue using the notation of Sections

2.2.
Quadratic forms and modular geodesics

This subsection is independent of the rest of the paper. We begin by briefly
recalling Schoof’s definition of a measure on each connected component of
an Arakelov class group. We then recall
a well-known construction that associates with each narrow ideal class of the ring
of integers of a real quadratic number field a geodesic on the modular surface

, where
\mathbb{H}
is the hyperbolic upper half plane,
on which
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

the subsection, a reinterpretation of this construction
in terms of Arakelov class groups: we define natural maps from narrow
Arakelov class groups of real quadratic fields to

that send the connected
components of these Arakelov class groups isometrically to the corresponding
geodesics just mentioned.

It is not difficult to generalise the entire construction to more general
moduli, yielding, for every real quadratic field

with

containing both

to closed geodesics on

discussion is somewhat tangential to the main thrust of the paper,
we leave this generalisation to the interested reader.

Let

There are covariant functors

\displaystyle\myfunc^{0}\colon{\bf CAbGp}

\displaystyle\to

the first of these induced by sending a compact abelian group to the connected component of

Also, Pontryagin duality defines an involutive anti-equivalence of categories

Next, let

There are covariant functors

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle{\bf TFAbGp},

the first taking an abelian group to its torsion subgroup,

Proposition 3.1
.

We have canonical isomorphisms of functors

\displaystyle\tors\circ\vee=\vee\circ\myfunc^{0}\colon

\displaystyle{\bf CAbGp}\to{\bf TAbGp},

\displaystyle\vee\circ\tors=\myfunc^{0}\circ\vee\colon

\displaystyle{\bf AbGp}\to{\bf PAbGp},

and

\torsfree\circ\vee=\vee\circ\myfunc_{0},\;\;\;\vee\circ\torsfree=\myfunc_{0}\circ\vee.

Proof.

Firstly, a compact Hausdorff abelian group is connected if and
only if its dual is torsion free
[
10
, Cor. 4 of Thm. 30]
.
Second, a compact Hausdorff abelian group
is totally disconnected if and only if the dual is a torsion group

[
10
, Cor. 1 of Thm. 30]
.
The statement easily follows from these two observations.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

None of the functors

,

\myfunc^{0}

\myfunc_{0}

epimorphisms and monomorphisms, but does not preserve exactness:
for example the connected component of the kernel of an isogeny between two
tori is just the trivial group. Dually,
\tors
is left exact, but not
right exact; while
\torsfree
preserves monomorphisms and epimorphisms,
but does not preserve exactness: consider, for example, the effect
of
\torsfree
on a map from a free abelian group to a finite group.

It is thanks to Remark

\displaystyle 0\to\myfunc_{0}(A,B)\to A\to\myfunc^{0}(A,B)\to 0,

\displaystyle 0\to\myfunc_{0}(A,B)\to\myfunc_{0}(B)\to\myfunc_{0}(C)\to 0,

\displaystyle\myfunc^{0}(\Theta)\colon

\displaystyle 0\to\myfunc^{0}(A,B)\to\myfunc^{0}(B)\to\myfunc^{0}(C)\to 0,

Dually, given an exact sequence

Let

More explicitly, the first map is given by sending an extension to the corresponding commutator
pairing, whence the exactness in the first and in the second term is obvious.
Since

Proof of Proposition
4.16
.

Denote the limit in Proposition
4.20
by
\Av_{R}^{+}(\ell)
.
Let
p_{R}
be the limit as
X\to\infty
of the proportion among all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{X}^{+}

of those for which
\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
is isomorphic to
R
. Then we have

\displaystyle\Av^{+}(\ell)=\sum_{R}p_{R}\cdot\Av_{R}^{+}(\ell)=\sum_{R}p_{R}\cdot\#U_{+}+\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{R}p_{R}\cdot\#\left(U_{R}/U_{R}^{\ell}\right),

where the sums run over all possible rings
R
. To prove the proposition,
we will explicitly evaluate the two sums on the right hand side.

The first sum is easy to evaluate:
\#U_{+}
is independent of

R
, namely we have

\#U_{+}={\Bigl\{\mskip-3.0mu\mskip-3.0mu\begin{array}[]{ll}\scriptstyle\ell^{\#\mathcal{P}_{1}},&\scriptstyle\ell^{2}\nmid\mathfrak{m}_{\f}\cr\scriptstyle\ell^{\#\mathcal{P}_{1}+1},&\scriptstyle\ell^{2}|\mathfrak{m}_{\f}\end{array}}.

We will now evaluate the second sum, which we denote by
s(\mathfrak{m}_{\f})
.
If
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}=\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
, where
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
are coprime, then as
F

varies over real quadratic fields, the structures of
\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{n}
and

\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
are, in the limit, independent of each other, whence it easily
follows that one has
s(\mathfrak{m}_{\f})=\prod_{p^{k}\parallel\mathfrak{m}_{\f}}s(p^{k})
,
with the product running over the distinct prime divisors of
\mathfrak{m}_{\f}
. It
therefore suffices to evaluate
s(\mathfrak{m}_{\f})
in the special case that

\mathfrak{m}_{\f}=p^{k}
, where
p
is a prime number and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, which we
will now do.

Recall that we have
R=\mathcal{O}_{A}/(p^{k})
, where
\mathcal{O}_{A}
is the integral closure of

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in a degree
2
étale
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebra
A_{p}
. If
p\not\in\{-1,0,1\}\pmod{\ell}
,
then we have
\#(U_{R}/U_{R}^{\ell})=1
. The calculation now breaks up into several cases.

Case 1:
p\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
.

Write
A_{p}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}[x]/(x^{2}-d)
, where
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is square-free. One has

A_{p}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if and only if
p\nmid d
and
d
is a square
modulo
p
;
A_{p}
is an unramified quadratic field extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if and
only if
p\nmid d
and
d
is a non-square modulo
p
; and
A_{p}
is
a quadratic ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if and only if
p|d
. In these
three cases, we have that
p_{R}
is equal to
\frac{p}{2(p+1)}
, respectively

\frac{p}{2(p+1)}
, respectively
\frac{1}{p+1}
; and
\#(U_{R}/U_{R}^{\ell})

is equal to
\ell^{2}
, respectively
\ell
, respectively
\ell
. We deduce
that in this case we have

s(p^{k})=\left(\frac{\ell^{2}\cdot p}{2(p+1)}+\frac{\ell\cdot p}{2(p+1)}+\frac{\ell}{p+1}\right)=\ell\cdot\frac{p(\ell+1)+2}{2(p+1)}.

Case 2:
p\equiv-1\pmod{\ell}
.

Considering the same three cases as above, we have the same proportions
p_{R}
,
and this time
\#(U_{R}/U_{R}^{\ell})
is equal to
1
in the split
case, to
\ell
in the case of
A_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
being an unramified quadratic field extension,
and to
1
in the ramified case. In summary, we have

s(p^{k})=\frac{p}{2(p+1)}+\frac{\ell\cdot p}{2(p+1)}+\frac{1}{p+1}=\frac{p(\ell+1)+2}{2(p+1)}.

Case 3:
p=\ell
,
k=1
.

Again, we have the same three possibilities for
R
with proportions
p_{R}

equal to
\frac{\ell}{2(\ell+1)}
,
\frac{\ell}{2(\ell+1)}
, and
\frac{1}{\ell+1}
,
respectively. In these three cases,
\#(U_{R}/U_{R}^{\ell})
is equal to
1
,
1
, and

\ell
, respectively, so that one has

s(\ell)=\frac{\ell}{2(\ell+1)}+\frac{\ell}{2(\ell+1)}+\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}=\frac{2\ell}{\ell+1}.

Case 4:
p=\ell>3
,
k>1
.

In this case, one has
\#(U_{R}/U_{R}^{\ell})=\ell^{2}
for all possible rings
R
,
so that we have

s(\ell^{k})=\ell^{2}.

Case 5:
p=\ell=3
,
k>1
.

In this case, one has
\#(U_{R}/U_{R}^{\ell})=9
, unless
A_{3}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{3}(\zeta_{3})
. For
that latter ring, which occurs with frequency
p_{R}=1/8
, one has
\#(U_{R}/U_{R}^{\ell})=27
.
In summary, in this case we have

s(3^{k})=\frac{9\cdot 7}{8}+\frac{27}{8}=\frac{45}{4}.

This completes the computation of
\Av^{+}(\ell)
.
∎

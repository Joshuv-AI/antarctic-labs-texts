Efficacy of uterine artery ascending branch ligation for preventing postpartum hemorrhage in Caesarean section for placenta previa
Authors: Le Xu, Yihong Yang, Ying Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPostpartum hemorrhage (PPH) remains a leading cause of maternal morbidity and mortality globally. This study aimed to assess the efficacy of ascending uterine artery ligation (AUAL) in preventing PPH among patients undergoing cesarean section (CS) for placenta previa.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted at our institution between January 2022 and December 2024, enrolling 103 patients with placenta previa who underwent CS with concurrent AUAL. A matched control group was included, comprising 134 patients with placenta previa who underwent CS with administration of specialized uterotonic agents (without AUAL). All participants in both groups received intraoperative and postoperative oxytocin administration. Demographic characteristics and clinical outcomes were compared between the two groups, including maternal age, gestational age at delivery, parity, total surgical duration, 24-hour postpartum blood loss volume, postoperative hemoglobin concentration, occurrence of puerperal fever, and rate of uterine involution. Postoperative abdominal pain intensity was assessed via the Numerical Rating Scale (NRS).ResultsNo statistically significant differences were observed between the AUAL group and the control group regarding maternal age, gestational age at delivery, parity, uterine involution rate, or the incidence of puerperal fever (P > 0.05). Notably, the AUAL group had significantly lower total 24-hour postpartum blood loss (495.3 ± 159.9 ml vs. 648.1 ± 246.8 ml, P < 0.001) and a smaller reduction in postoperative hemoglobin (12.8 ± 5.5 g/L vs. 16.8 ± 6.8 g/L, P < 0.001) compared with the control group. However, the AUAL group was associated with a significantly longer operative time (1.9 ± 0.4 h vs. 1.7 ± 0.5 h, P = 0.025) and more severe postoperative abdominal pain.ConclusionsAUAL serves as an effective intervention to reduce 24‑hour postoperative blood loss in patients undergoing cesarean section for placenta previa. Although it was associated with more severe postoperative abdominal pain, it does not compromise key clinical recovery outcomes.

Placenta previa is defined as the complete or partial coverage of the cervical os during the third trimester of pregnancy, a condition requiring CS for delivery [1]. Patients undergoing CS for placenta previa are at exceptionally high risk of PPH, a life-threatening obstetric complication globally defined as vaginal bleeding exceeding 500 mL within 24 h of delivery or intraoperative bleeding exceeding 1000 mL during CS [2]. This elevated risk primarily attributed to uterine atony or inadequate closure of blood sinuses at the placental implantation site, and other contributing factors including advanced maternal age, multiple pregnancies, and a history of prior CS [3, 4]. Severe PPH can lead to catastrophic maternal outcomes, underscoring the critical need for effective preventive and therapeutic strategies [5].

Current standard PPH management strategies encompass uterine massage, uterotonics, surgical interventions, and uterine balloon tamponade [6]. Oxytocin remains the first-line agent for promoting uterine contraction postpartum, but its efficacy is limited by receptor saturation, exceeding the recommended daily dosage does not enhance uterine contractility and may increase the risk of adverse events, including coronary ischemia, hypertension, and pulmonary edema [7]. Alternative uterotonics (e.g., misoprostol, carboprost, ergometrine) improve uterine smooth muscle contraction and mitigate atony-related bleeding, yet each is associated with unique limitations and contraindications [8]. Intrauterine tamponade techniques, including Bakri balloon tamponade and chitosan-covered tamponades, have been widely used as minimally invasive interventional options for PPH control, yet their efficacy is often limited by inadequate tamponade in cases of diffuse placental bleeding or uterine atony, and they carry risks of displacement or infection [9]. Other interventional approaches like preoperative arterial balloon catheterization are further hindered by invasiveness, high cost, and limited accessibility in resource-constrained settings [10]. Surgical techniques including B-Lynch compression sutures, lower uterine segment spiral suture, and Cho multiple-square sutures have demonstrated efficacy for refractory PPH [11–13], yet these are typically reserved for refractory PPH cases with a clear etiology, rather than proactive prevention in high-risk patients.

Highlighting the critical need to explore safe, reliable surgical hemostatic measures that also preserve fertility. Intraoperatively AUAL during CS emerges as a promising approach in this context. It achieves superior hemostatic efficacy via a straightforward procedure, induces less trauma than alternative surgical modalities, and effectively prevents PPH.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective cohort study included 237 patients who received cesarean section for placenta previa. Clinical data were retrospectively collected from January 2022 to December 2024 at Tongji Hospital, a tertiary level hospital affiliated with Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology. All participants received oxytocin as first-line therapy to maintain uterine contraction during and after surgery. Figure 1 shows the flow chart of the participants in the main analysis.

Participants were stratified into two groups based on the intraoperative hemostatic regimen. The ascending uterine artery ligation group included 103 patients who underwent intraoperative AUAL in addition to oxytocin administration, while the pharmacological augmentation group consisted of 134 patients who received additional uterotonic agents to reinforce uterine contraction.

To minimize confounding factors, the following patients were excluded: those who underwent uterine balloon tamponade (intraoperatively or postoperatively), interventional radiological embolization, or had pernicious placenta previa, multiple pregnancies, coagulation disorders, hematological/systemic diseases, mental disorders (or long-term psychiatric medication use), cognitive impairment, or other conditions potentially influencing study outcomes.

Surgical procedure of ascending uterine artery ligation

AUAL is a simple and effective intraoperative hemostatic technique. After adequate exposure of the operative field, the ascending uterine artery is identified approximately 2 cm inferior to the uterine incision with careful avoidance of the round ligament. Using 1 − 0 absorbable sutures, the needle is inserted 2 to 3 cm away from the uterine margin and passed through the uterine myometrium toward the posterior uterine wall. The suture is then wrapped around the lateral uterine vascular bundle and tied securely at the avascular region of the broad ligament to complete ligation.

Data collection

Relevant variables were extracted from medical records of all participants within 24 h after delivery.

The primary outcome was total postpartum blood loss. It was defined as the cumulative intraoperative volume measured via vacuum suction drainage and perineal pad weighing, plus postoperative bleeding recorded by perineal pads within the first 24 h after delivery. Postoperative blood loss was calculated using the gravimetric method [14]. This approach relies on weighing surgical sponges and perineal pads before and after use. Blood loss volume was calculated from the measured weight difference, with every gram of weight equivocal to 1 mL of blood. Other perioperative outcomes included changes in peripheral blood hemoglobin levels and total operative duration.

Secondary outcomes comprised postoperative abdominal pain severity assessed using the Numerical Rating Scale at 24 h, 72 h, and 7 days after delivery. The scale score ranged from 0 to 10 with zero representing no pain and ten indicating the most intolerable pain [15]. Additional secondary outcomes included uterine subinvolution puerperal fever and secondary postpartum hemorrhage.

The efficacy of the two hemostatic strategies for preventing PPH after CS for placenta previa was evaluated by comparing operative indices and postoperative complication rates between the two groups.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 statistical software. Continuous measurement data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while categorical variables are summarized as frequencies and percentages [n (%)]. Case numbers are reported as n where applicable. The chi‑square test was adopted for comparative analyses of enumeration data. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

The present study focused on the clinical value of AUAL as an adjunctive strategy for PPH prevention and control. Our primary findings demonstrated that AUAL was associated with favorable perioperative outcomes, including reduced intraoperative blood loss, lower rates of massive transfusion, and a high technical success rate in achieving hemostasis.

One prominent clinical strength of AUAL is the preservation of uterine perfusion; collateral circulation supplied by uterine branch vessels helps prevent ischemia-induced uterine necrosis. In contrast to the tourniquet method, AUAL achieves longer‑lasting and more robust hemostasis [16], and the use of modern absorbable sutures can further optimize this technique and reduce the incidence of long-term suture retention. Postoperative follow-up data further support the safety profile of AUAL. At six weeks after surgery, color Doppler ultrasound examinations demonstrated no significant abnormalities in uterine or ovarian size, morphology, or vascular perfusion [17], confirming that AUAL does not impair long-term reproductive organ function, which is critical for patients intending future fertility.

Consistent with prior literature [18], this study confirms that AUAL significantly reduces 24-hour postpartum blood loss and hemoglobin decline in patients undergoing CS for placenta previa. Similarly, another study reported a 15% higher hemostatic success rate in patients who received AUAL combined with Bakri balloon tamponade compared with those treated with balloon tamponade alone [17]. However, we do not routinely adopt this practice mainly due to the risk of infection and inadequate hemostatic tamponade. Notably, AUAL’s efficacy is further enhanced when combined with uterotonics, though this approach must be weighed against the latter’s contraindications and side effects [19]. AUAL thus emerges as a particularly valuable option for patients with uterotonic contraindications. While the present study observed a higher incidence of postoperative abdominal pain in the AUAL group, symptomatic analgesia may effectively mitigate this transient discomfort without compromising postoperative recovery. Nevertheless, further investigations are warranted to validate this approach and optimize pain management strategies.

This study has limitations that warrant consideration. First, its retrospective design introduces inherent selection bias, as patients were not randomly assigned to treatment groups. Second, the single-center setting may limit the generalizability of findings to diverse clinical populations. Future prospective, multicenter randomized controlled trials with larger sample sizes are needed to validate AUAL’s efficacy and compare it to emerging PPH prevention strategies.

Conclusions

In conclusion, AUAL seems to be a safe and effective approach for preventing and managing PPH in patients undergoing CS for placenta previa. Bilateral AUAL is recommended to achieve optimal hemostatic efficacy, given its reliable technical feasibility, capacity to preserve reproductive function, low risk of perioperative complications. With these favorable clinical profiles, this technique holds considerable potential for widespread application in high‑risk individuals.

Incidence and Magnitude of Pseudo-decrease in Kidney Function With Oral Epidermal Growth Factor Inhibitor Osimertinib
Authors: Maria Ilyas, Jacqueline Nguyen, Shruti Shettigar, Jake LeClaire, Akash Mathavan, Akshay Mathavan, Chintan V Shah
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

We previously reported that oral epidermal growth factor receptor inhibitors can cause pseudo-acute kidney injury,1 which may be linked to frequent dose interruptions observed in epidermal growth factor receptor inhibitor clinical trials.2 However, the incidence and magnitude of this effect remain undefined. To address this uncertainty, we examined changes in serum creatinine (Scr) in patients treated with osimertinib, using both automated and manual chart review.

We collected data on adult patients treated with osimertinib at our center between January 1, 2014 and December 31, 2024 who had ≥1 Scr measurement within 90 days before and after starting therapy. A manual chart review was performed by trained study personnel for data collection. We compared peak Scr concentration measured within 90 days of osimertinib initiation with the most recent pretreatment Scr value. Changes in Scr were categorized according to Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria as ≤50%, 50%-100%, or >100% elevation. This categorization also facilitates evaluation of medications that may block tubular creatinine secretion, as approximately 10%-40% of Scr is actively transported in proximal tubular cells. In patients with available cystatin C, we assessed discordance between creatinine- and cystatin C-based estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). The study protocol was approved by the University of Florida Institutional Review Board under reference number IRB202500257. Informed consent from participants was waived per institutional criteria for retrospective studies. This study adheres to the guidelines proposed by the Declaration of Helsinki.

During the study, 170 patients received osimertinib, of whom 87 had ≥1 Scr measurement within 90 days before and after initiation and were included in the analysis. The cohort was predominantly female (63%) and non-Hispanic White (66%), with 10% African American/Black. Most patients had non-small cell lung cancer (97%), while a minority had glioblastoma (3%). Most patients (n = 71; 81.6%) experienced a peak increase in Scr of 1%-50% above baseline, with a median increase of 25.3% (mean 23.6%). Six patients (6.9%) exhibited increases of 50%-100%, with a median increase of 70.8%. One patient (1.2%) experienced an increase >100%, corresponding to a 482.5% elevation from baseline. In contrast, 9 patients (10.3%) demonstrated no measurable elevation in Scr (Table 1). Among the 7 patients with Scr increases >50%, alternative explanations were identified. Two had hemodynamic instability due to septic shock, and 4 had recent exposure to nephrotoxic agents (trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole, iodinated contrast media, or diuretics). The remaining patient had no clear alternative etiology; however, the absolute Scr increase was modest (0.48 to 0.75 mg/dL; <0.3 mg/dL). Of these 7 patients, 1 patient had a Scr increase of 26% prior to nephrotoxin exposure; the remaining patients did not have any documented Scr concentration preceding the >50% rise in Scr. Cystatin C measurements were available for 2 patients, and there was consistent discordance between creatinine- and cystatin C-based eGFR measurements. The first patient had an 88% increase in Scr (1.01 to 1.90 mg/dL), with creatinine-based eGFR declining from 85 to 40 mL/min/1.73 m2, whereas cystatin C-based eGFR remained preserved at 89 mL/min/1.73 m2. Similarly, the second patient had a 43% increase in Scr, with creatinine-based eGFR decreasing from 106 to 69 mL/min/1.73 m2, whereas cystatin C-eGFR remained normal at 90 mL/min/1.73 m2. Of 87 patients, 21 (24%) had a treatment change, mainly due to lack of response or adverse effects other than kidney injury. Dose reduction and holding occurred in 2 patients due to pseudo-decrease in kidney function.

This study is among the most comprehensive real-world evaluations of Scr changes after osimertinib initiation. Three principal findings emerge. First, most patients (82.7%) experienced a modest Scr elevation of 1%-50%, with a median increase of 25.3% within 90 days. Second, among patients with available cystatin C measurements, there was marked discordance between creatinine- and cystatin C-based kidney function estimates, suggesting preserved glomerular filtration. Third, there was no clear evidence of true acute kidney injury directly attributable to osimertinib. Mechanistically, several tyrosine kinase inhibitors inhibit renal tubular transporters involved in creatinine secretion (Table 23, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9), leading to elevated Scr without reduced true glomerular filtration rate. This ‘pseudo-decrease’ in kidney function has been reported with multiple tyrosine kinase inhibitors.1 Given the absence of clinically meaningful acute kidney injury, consistent cystatin C discordance, and a mean Scr increase of 25.3% in most patients, our findings support that osimertinib commonly induces a pseudo-decrease in kidney function rather than true nephrotoxicity.

Several limitations warrant consideration. The retrospective design limits causal inference. Cystatin C measurements were available in only a small subset of patients, and no patients underwent direct glomerular filtration rate measurement. Despite these limitations, strengths include a large real-world cohort, detailed chart review by trained personnel, and an American Board of Internal Medicine-certified nephrologist (CVS) for systematic evaluation of alternative causes of creatinine elevation, as well as rigorous temporal alignment of kidney function measurements with osimertinib initiation.

In summary, pseudo-decreases in kidney function, reflected by modest increases in Scr without true glomerular filtration rate impairment, are common after initiation of osimertinib, whereas clinically meaningful acute kidney injury appears rare. Recognizing this phenomenon is crucial to prevent unnecessary treatment interruptions, dose reductions, or discontinuation, which could adversely affect outcomes. Measuring cystatin C at baseline before initiating such tyrosine kinase inhibitors and after a >10% increase in the creatinine–cystatin C ratio on therapy compared with off therapy should be considered to identify pseudo-acute kidney injury in routine clinical practice to avoid therapy interruption, as we previously proposed.10

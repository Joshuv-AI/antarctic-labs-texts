On the Equilibria Computation of Set-Valued Lur’e Dynamical Systems
Phan Quoc Khanh, Le Ba Khiet

Abstract
In this article, we propose an efficient way to compute equilibria of a general class of set-valued Lur’e dynamical systems, which plays an important role in the asymptotical analysis of the systems. Besides the equilibria computation, our study can be also used to solve a class of quasi-variational inequalities. Some examples of finding Nash quasi-equilibria in game theory are given.



Keywords.
Lur’e set-valued dynamical systems; Equilibria computation; Maximal monotone operators; Quasi-variational inequalities

Although the well-posedness, stability analysis, control and observers for set-valued Lur’e systems have been extensively investigated, there has not been much research on the equilibria computation for such systems. It is known that finding equilibria of dynamical systems is important for understanding the asymptotic behavior of the systems. This fact motivates us to consider the equilibria computation for the following general class of set-valued Lur’e dynamical systems:

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=-f(x(t))+B\lambda(t),\;{\rm a.e.}\;t\in[0,+\infty),

(1a)

\displaystyle y(t)=Cx(t)+D\lambda(t),t\geq 0,

(1b)

\displaystyle\lambda(t)\in-\mathcal{F}(y(t)),\;t\geq 0,

(1c)

\displaystyle x(0)=x_{0},

(1d)

where
H_{1},H_{2}
are Hilbert spaces,
f:H_{1}\to H_{1}
is monotone and Lipschitz continuous,
B:H_{2}\to H_{1},C:H_{1}\to H_{2},D:H_{2}\to H_{2}
are linear and bounded, and
\mathcal{F}:H_{2}\rightrightarrows H_{2}
is maximal monotone. Some reformulations of system (
\mathcal{L}
) can be obtained as follows. From (1b) and (1c), write
\lambda(\cdot)
in terms of
x(\cdot)
as
\lambda(\cdot)\in(F^{-1}+D)^{-1}Cx(\cdot)
. We have
Cx(\cdot)\in(D+\mathcal{F}^{-1})(-\lambda(\cdot))
and so (
\mathcal{L}
) is equivalent to the differential inclusion

\dot{x}\in-\mathcal{H}(x),\;\;\;x(0)=x_{0},

(2)

where
\mathcal{H}(x)=f(x)+B(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}Cx
. If
D=0
, then
\mathcal{H}(x)=f(x)+B\mathcal{F}(Cx)
, which is ubiquitously considered in the literature on set-valued Lur’e systems. The non-trivial extension

[

but also the connecting variable
\lambda(t)

By the definition,
x^{*}
being an equilibrium of (
2

0\in f(x^{*})+B(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(Cx^{*}),

(3)

i.e., zero belongs to the sum of two operators:

0\in\mathcal{A}(x^{*})+\mathcal{B}(x^{*}),

(4)

where
\mathcal{A}=f
and
\mathcal{B}=B(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}C
. Inclusion (4) is a stationary condition and

is its solution. The equilibria computation of (

D=0

Under the well-known particular passivity condition

[

({\mathcal{L}})

\frac{x_{n+1}-x_{n}}{h}\in-f(x_{n+1})-B(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(Cx_{n+1})

(5)

\frac{x_{n+1}-x_{n}}{h}\in-f(x_{n})-B(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(Cx_{n+1})

(6)

3

On the other hand, this technique can not only be used for computing equilibria of Lur’e system
({\mathcal{L}})
but also for solving a class of quasi-variational inequalities (QVIs). Indeed, a particular class of (
3

B=C=I

\mathcal{F}=N_{\Omega}
, the normal cone to a closed convex set
\Omega

0\in f(x^{*})+(N_{\Omega}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(x^{*}).

(7)

We prove that (Proposition
10
) this class is equivalent to the following quasi-variational inequality:

0\in f(x^{*})+N_{\Omega}(x^{*}+Df(x^{*}))=f(x^{*})+N_{K(x^{*})}(x^{*})

(8)

involving the state-dependent set
K(x):=\Omega-Df(x)
. The term
Df
plays the role of a perturbation that affects the state variable under the normal cone operator.
Applications of QVIs can be found largely in economics, transportation, mechanics, electrical circuits, etc. (see, e.g.,
[

11

12
,
24
,

). If

which is at the core of many constrained optimization problems and has been intensively investigated in the literature. It is known that solving quasi-variational inequalities is far more difficult than solving variational inequalities and is possible only under quite restrictive conditions, usually based on strong monotonicity assumptions. One of the well-known conditions
[

-Lipschitz continuous, the state-dependent set

2
Notation and preliminaries

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the associated norm
\|\cdot\|
. The distance and the projection from a point
x
to a closed convex subset
C
of
H
is defined as follows

{d}(x,C):=\inf_{y\in C}\|x-y\|,\;\;{\rm proj}_{C}(x):={y\in C\;\;{\rm such\;that\;}{d}(x,C)=\|x-y\|}.

The normal cone to
K
at
x\in K
is

N_{K}(x):=\{x^{*}\in H:\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle\leq 0\;\;{\rm\;for\;all}\;y\in K\}.

(10)

A function
f:H\to H
is called
L
-Lipschitz continuous (
L>0
) if

\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|\;\;{\rm\;for\;all}\;x,y\in H.

(11)

It is called non-expansive if
L\leq 1
and contractive if
L<1
.
The domain, range, graph, and inverse of a set-valued mapping
\mathcal{F}:{H}\rightrightarrows{H}
are defined respectively by

{\rm dom}(\mathcal{F})=\{x\in{H}:\;\mathcal{F}(x)\neq\emptyset\},\;\;{\rm rge}(\mathcal{F})=\displaystyle\bigcup_{x\in{H}}\mathcal{F}(x)\;\;

and

\;\;{\rm gph}(\mathcal{F})=\{(x,y):x\in{H},y\in\mathcal{F}(x)\},\;\;\;\mathcal{F}^{-1}(y)=\{x\in H:y\in\mathcal{F}(x)\}.

The mapping
\mathcal{F}
is called monotone if

\langle x^{*}-y^{*},x-y\rangle\geq 0\;\;{\rm\;for\;all}\;x,y\in H,x^{*}\in\mathcal{F}(x)\;{\rm and}\;y^{*}\in\mathcal{F}(y).

We say that
\mathcal{F}
is strongly monotone if there exists
\mu>0
such that

\langle x^{*}-y^{*},x-y\rangle\geq\mu\|x-y\|^{2}\;\;{\rm\;for\;all}\;x,y\in H,x^{*}\in\mathcal{F}(x)\;{\rm and}\;y^{*}\in\mathcal{F}(y).

(12)

Furthermore, if there is no monotone operator
\mathcal{G}
such that the graph of
\mathcal{F}
is strictly contained in the graph of
\mathcal{G}
, then
\mathcal{F}
is called maximal monotone. If
C
is a nonempty closed, convex set, then
N_{C}
is a maximal monotone operator.
The resolvent of
\mathcal{F}
is defined by

J_{\mathcal{F}}:=(I+\mathcal{F})^{-1}.

We say that a linear bounded mapping
E:H\to H
is

•

positive

semidefinite
, written
E\geq 0
, if

\langle Ex,x\rangle\geq 0\;\;{\rm\;for\;all}\;x\in H;

•

positive

definite
, written
E>0
, if there exists
c>0
such that

\langle Ex,x\rangle\geq c\|x\|^{2}\;\;{\rm\;for\;all}\;x\in H;

•

semi
-
coercive
if there exists
c>0
such that

\langle Ex,x\rangle\geq c\|x\|^{2}\;\;{\rm\;for\;all}\;x\in{\rm rge}(E+E^{T}),

where
E^{T}
denotes the transpose of
E
.

Next we recall Minty’s theorem in Hilbert spaces (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
).

Proposition 1
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{F}:H\rightrightarrows H
be a monotone operator. Then,
\mathcal{F}
is maximal monotone if and only if
{\rm rge}(\mathcal{F}+\lambda I)=H
for some
\lambda>0
.

Let
E
be a positive definite linear bounded operator.
We introduce the resolvent of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
E
as follows

J^{E}_{\mathcal{F}}:=(E+\mathcal{F})^{-1}.

Proposition 2
.

If
\mathcal{F}:H\rightrightarrows H
is maximal monotone and
E:H\to H
is a positive definite linear bounded operator, then
J^{E}_{\mathcal{F}}
is single-valued with full domain and Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

Since
E:H\to H
is positive definite and
\mathcal{F}:H\rightrightarrows H
is maximal monotone, we deduce that
E+\mathcal{F}
is maximal monotone and strongly monotone and thus
J^{E}_{\mathcal{F}}
has full domain (Minty’s theorem).
Let
x_{i}\in H
and
y_{i}\in J^{E}_{\mathcal{F}}x_{i},i=1,2
. We have

x_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(y_{i})+Ey_{i}.

There exists some
c>0
such that

\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|\geq\langle x_{1}-x_{2},y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\geq c\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2},

which implies that
J^{E}_{\mathcal{F}}
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous.
∎

Remark 1
.

In general, if we do not have the explicit form of
J^{E}_{\mathcal{F}}
, we can compute it easily. Indeed let
y=J^{E}_{\mathcal{F}}x=(E+\mathcal{F})^{-1}(x)
. Then,
0\in\mathcal{F}(y)+Ey-x
. Let
g(y):=Ey-x
. Then,
g
is single-valued, Lipschitz continuous, and strongly monotone. Then, we can use the forward-backward algorithms (see, e.g.,
[
36
]
) to compute
y
for a given
x
with linear convergence rate.

For Hilbert spaces
H_{1},H_{2}
, let a Lipschitz continuous mapping
f:{H_{1}\to H_{1}}
and linear bounded operators
B:{H_{2}\to H_{1}},C:{H_{1}\to H_{2}}
,
D:{H_{2}\to H_{2}}
be given. We generalize the notion of passive systems (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
) for nonlinear
f
as follows.

Definition 1
.

The system
(f,B,C,D)
is called
P
-
passive
if there exists a symmetric positive linear bounded mapping
P:{H_{1}\to H_{1}}
such that for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in H_{1}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in H_{2}
, one has

\langle P(f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle+\langle(PB-C^{T})(y_{1}-y_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle+\langle D(y_{1}-y_{2}),y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\geq 0.

(13)

Remark 2
.

i) If
f,B,C,D
are matrices, then
(f,B,C,D)
is
P
-passive if and only if the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(Pf+f^{T}P)&\;\;\;\;PB-C^{T}\\
\\
B^{T}P-C&(D+D^{T})\end{array}\right)

is positive semidefinite
[
17
]
.

ii) If
(f,B,C,D)
is
P
-passive, then
Pf
and

are monotone.

Main results

In this section, we provide a method to compute an equilibrium point of the Lur’e set-valued dynamical system
({\mathcal{L}})
, i.e., a zero point of the inclusion (
3
). First, let us suppose that the following assumptions are satisfied, which are usually used in the literature on set-valued Lur’e systems (see, e.g.,
[

15
,
16
,

,

,

,

\mathbf{Assumption\;1}:
The operator
f:H_{1}\to H_{1}
is
L_{f}
-Lipschitz continuous. The operators
B:H_{2}\to H_{1},C:H_{1}\to H_{2},D:H_{2}\to H_{2}
are linear bounded. There exists a symmetric, positive definite linear bounded operator
P:H_{1}\to H_{1}
such that
PB=C^{T}
. In addition,
Pf
and
D
are monotone.

\mathbf{Assumption\;2}:
The set-valued operator
\mathcal{F}:H_{2}\rightrightarrows H_{2}
is maximal monotone.

\mathbf{Assumption\;3}:

0\in{\rm ri}({\rm rge}({C})-{\rm rge}({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}+D))
, where
{\rm ri}(S)
denotes the relative interior of set
S
.

Remark 3
.

It is easy to see that, under Assumption 1, the system
(f,B,C,D)
is
P
-
passive
.

The following result provides a way to compute the resolvent of
\mathcal{B}:=(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}
based on the resolvent of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to a positive definite linear operator, which can be used to solve (
3
) in general.

Lemma 3
.

Let
x\in H
and
\gamma>0
. Then,
y=J_{\gamma\mathcal{B}}(x)
if and only if
y=E(\gamma J_{\mathcal{F}}^{E}(Ex)+Dx)
, where
E:=(\gamma I+D)^{-1}
is a positive definite linear bounded operator.

Proof.

First we check that
E:=(\gamma I+D)^{-1}
is a positive definite linear bounded operator. Since
D
is monotone, we deduce that
\gamma I+D
is strongly monotone and
E
is well-defined and single-valued. Let
x_{i}=Ey_{i}=(\gamma I+D)^{-1}y_{i},i=1,2,
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. One has
y_{i}=\gamma x_{i}+Dx_{i}
and thus
y_{1}+\lambda y_{2}=\gamma(x_{1}+\lambda x_{2})+D(x_{1}+\lambda x_{2})
. Therefore,
E(y_{1}+\lambda y_{2})=x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}
, which implies that
E
is linear. For all
y\in H
, let
x=Ey=(\gamma I+D)^{-1}(y)
, i.e.,
y=\gamma x+Dx
. Then,
\|y\|^{2}=\gamma\langle x,y\rangle+\langle Dx,y\rangle\leq\gamma\|x\|\|y\|+\|D\|\|x\|\|y\|\Rightarrow\|y\|\leq(\gamma+\|D\|)\|x\|
. Hence,

\langle Ey,y\rangle=\langle x,\gamma x+Dx\rangle\geq\gamma\|x\|^{2}\geq\frac{\gamma}{(\gamma+\|D\|)^{2}}\|y\|^{2}.

Thus,
E
is positive definite.
We have the following chain of equivalent relations

\displaystyle y=J_{\gamma\mathcal{B}}(x)=(I+\gamma\mathcal{B})^{-1}(x)\Leftrightarrow x\in y+\gamma(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(y)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{x-y}{\gamma}\in(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(y)\Leftrightarrow y\in\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\frac{x-y}{\gamma})+D\frac{x-y}{\gamma}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{x-y}{\gamma}\in\mathcal{F}(y+D\frac{y-x}{\gamma})\Leftrightarrow 0\in h(y)+\mathcal{F}(y+Dh(y)),

where
h(y)=\frac{y-x}{\gamma}
. Note that
h
is
\frac{1}{\gamma}
-strongly monotone and
\frac{1}{\gamma}
-Lipschitz continuous. Let
z=y+Dh(y)=\gamma h(y)+x+Dh(y)
, which implies that
h(y)=(\gamma I+D)^{-1}(z-x)=E(z-x)=Ez-Ex
since
D
is monotone and linear. Then, the last inclusion is equivalent to

\displaystyle 0\in Ez-Ex+\mathcal{F}(z),\,{\rm i.e.,}\,z=J_{\mathcal{F}}^{E}(Ex).

On the other hand,

z=y+Dh(y)=y+D\frac{y-x}{\gamma},

which is equivalent to

y=(\gamma I+{D})^{-1}(\gamma z+Dx)=E(\gamma z+Dx).

The conclusion follows.
∎

Note that if the resolvent of
\mathcal{B}=(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}
is available, then we can compute the resolvent of
C^{T}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}C
by using the fixed-point approach (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
29
]
). The following algorithm is based on the Tseng algorithm
[
36
]
applied to our problem (
3
). Let
\tilde{f}:=Pf
and
G:={C}^{T}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}{C}={C}^{T}\mathcal{B}C
. Since
D
is monotone, from Assumptions 2, 3 we deduce that
G
is a maximal monotone operator (see, e.g.,
[
32
]
).

Algorithm  1
: Compute
J_{\gamma\mathcal{B}}
and then
J_{\gamma G}
. Given
x_{0}\in H
, for
n\geq 0
, we compute

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}y_{n}=J_{\gamma G}\Big(x_{n}-\gamma\tilde{f}(x_{n})\Big),\\
&&\\
x_{n+1}=y_{n}-\gamma(\tilde{f}(y_{n})-\tilde{f}(x_{n})).\\
\end{array}\right.

(14)

Theorem 4
.

Let Assumptions 1–3 hold. Let
(x_{n})
be the sequence generated by Algorithm 1. Then,
(x_{n})
converges weakly to an equilibrium point
x^{*}
of the Lur’e set-valued dynamical system
({\mathcal{L}})
, i. e.,
x^{*}
satisfies the inclusion (
3
).

Proof.

We have the chain of equivalent relations

\displaystyle 0\in f(x^{*})+B(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(Cx^{*})\Leftrightarrow 0\in Pf(x^{*})+PB(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(Cx^{*})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle 0\in Pf(x^{*})+C^{T}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(Cx^{*})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle 0\in{{\tilde{f}(x^{*})+G(x^{*}),}}

where
\tilde{f}:=Pf
and
G:={C}^{T}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}{C}
. Note that
Pf
is monotone, Lipschitz continuous and
G
is a maximal monotone operator. The conclusion follows (see, e.g.,
[
34
, Theorem 3.6]
or
[
36
, Theorem 3.4]
).
∎

Remark 4
.

One can use Algorithm 1 to obtain the numerical trajectory of the Lur’e system

by using the fully implicit scheme (

Next, we show that the obtained result can be extended to general passive systems.

\mathbf{Assumption\;1^{\prime}}:
The system
(f,B,C,D)
is

-passive, where

is

-Lipschitz continuous,

.

Remark 5
.

1) In finite dimensional spaces, the semi-coercivity of

holds automatically since

is positive semidefinite, which comes from the passivity of

.

2) Assumption  4 is an improvement of the usual assumption with

Lemma 5
.

Let Assumption 1
′
hold. Then,
\ker(D+D^{T})\subset\ker(PB-C^{T})
.

Proof.

Let
y\in\ker(D+D^{T})
. We have

\langle Dy,y\rangle=0.

Suppose that
y\notin\ker(PB-C^{T})
. Then,
y^{*}:=(PB-C^{T})y\neq 0
. In (
13
), we take
y_{1}=y,y_{2}=0
, and
x_{1}-x_{2}=-y^{*}/n
. Note that

\langle P(f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\leq\|P\|L_{f}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}=\frac{L_{f}\|P\|\|y^{*}\|^{2}}{n^{2}},

where
L_{f}
is the Lipschitz constant of
f
.

Thus, we have

\frac{L_{f}\|P\|\|y^{*}\|^{2}}{n^{2}}-\frac{\|y^{*}\|^{2}}{n}\geq 0.

Letting
n\to+\infty
, we obtain a contradiction. Hence,
y\in\ker(PB-C^{T})
and the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Suppose that
D
is semi-coercive. If
y\in H_{2}
satisfies
\langle Dy,y\rangle=0
, then
y\in\ker(D+D^{T})
.

Proof.

Let
y=y_{1}+y_{2}
, where
y_{1},y_{2}
are the projections of
y
onto
\ker(D+D^{T})
and
{\rm rge}(D+D^{T})
respectively. We have

\langle(D+D^{T})y,y\rangle=2\langle Dy,y\rangle=0,

which implies that

\langle(D+D^{T})y_{2},y_{2}\rangle=0.

Since
D
is semi-coercive, we infer that
y_{2}=0
and hence
y=y_{1}\in\ker(D+D^{T})
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Let Assumptions 1’, 2, 3 hold. Then,
g
defined by
g(x):=Pf(x)+(PB-C^{T})(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}Cx
is single-valued. In addition, if Assumption 4 is satisfied, then
g
is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant

L_{g}=\|P\|L_{f}+\frac{\|C\|\|PB-C^{T}\|}{c}.

Proof.

First we prove that
(PB-C^{T})(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}Cx
is a singleton for every
x\in H_{1}
. For
y_{1},y_{2}\in(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}Cx
, we have
y_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(Cx-Dy_{i})
. The monotonicity of
F
deduces that

\langle y_{1}-y_{2},D(y_{1}-y_{2})\rangle\leq 0.

Since
D
is monotone, we have
\langle y_{1}-y_{2},D(y_{1}-y_{2})\rangle=0
and thus
y_{1}-y_{2}\in\ker(D+D^{T})\subset\ker(PB-C^{T})
(Lemma
6
).
Therefore,
(PB-C^{T})(y_{1}-y_{2})=0
which implies that
(PB-C^{T})(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}Cx
is a singleton.

Now suppose that Assumption 4 holds. Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in{\rm dom}((\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}C)
or equivalently
Cx_{1},Cx_{2}\in{\rm rge}({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}+D)
. Then, we can find
y_{1},y_{2}\in{\rm rge}(D+D^{T})
such that
y_{i}+y_{0}\in(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}Cx_{i},\;i=1,2
(
y_{0}
is given in Assumption 4). Similarly as above, we have

\langle y_{1}-y_{2},C(x_{1}-x_{2})-D(y_{1}-y_{2})\rangle\geq 0.

Since
D
is semi-coercive, there exists
c>0
such that

\displaystyle c\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle y_{1}-y_{2},D(y_{1}-y_{2})\rangle\leq\langle y_{1}-y_{2},C(x_{1}-x_{2})\rangle

(15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|C\|\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|,

which implies that

\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|\leq\frac{\|C\|}{c}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|g(x_{1})-g(x_{2})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|Pf(x_{1})-Pf(x_{2})+(PB-C^{T})(y_{1}+y_{0}-y_{2}-y_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\|P\|L_{f}+\frac{\|C\|\|PB-C^{T}\|}{c})\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|

and the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let Assumptions 1’, 2, 3, 4 hold and

\mathcal{H}:=Pf+PB(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}C.

(16)

Then,
\mathcal{H}
is a maximal monotone operator.

Proof.

First we prove that
\mathcal{H}
is monotone. Let

x_{1},x_{2}\in{\rm dom}(\mathcal{H})
and

z_{i}\in\mathcal{H}(x_{i}),i=1,2
. Then, we have

z_{i}=Pf(x_{i})+

PBy_{i}
, where
y_{i}\in(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}Cx_{i}
, i.e.,
y_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(Cx_{i}-Dy_{i})
, and thus

\langle y_{1}-y_{2},C(x_{1}-x_{2})-D(y_{1}-y_{2})\rangle\geq 0.

Using the
P
-passivity of
(f,B,C,D)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle z_{1}-z_{2},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle Pf(x_{1})-Pf(x_{2})+PB(y_{1}-y_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\langle y_{1}-y_{2},C(x_{1}-x_{2})-D(y_{1}-y_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0.

Thus,
\mathcal{H}
is monotone.
Note that
g(x):=Pf(x)+(PB-C^{T})(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}Cx
is Lipschitz continuous (Lemma
7
). We can write
\mathcal{H}=Pf+(PB-C^{T})(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}C+C^{T}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}C=g+G
. We use Minty’s theorem to prove that
\mathcal{H}
is a maximal monotone operator with
\lambda=2L_{g}
. For each
y\in H_{1}
, we prove that there exists
x\in H_{1}
such that

y\in\mathcal{H}(x)+\lambda x=G(x)+(g(x)+L_{g}x)+L_{g}x.

Since
G
is maximal monotone and
g+L_{g}I
is Lipschitz and monotone, we conclude that the operator
G+g+L_{g}I
is maximal monotone
[
13
, Lemma 2.4]
. Then, the existence of
x
is guaranteed by using Minty’s theorem and the conclusion follows.
∎

Since
g
may be nonmonotone, the convergence may not be obtained if we use the splitting algorithms. However, we can compute the resolvent
J_{\gamma\mathcal{H}}
for some small
\gamma>0
(see, e.g.,
[
28
]
).

Algorithm  2
: For some small
\gamma>0
, compute
J_{\gamma\mathcal{B}}
,
J_{\gamma G}
and then
J_{\gamma\mathcal{H}}
. Given
x_{0}\in H
, for
n\geq 0
, we compute

x_{n+1}=J_{\gamma\mathcal{H}}(x_{n}).

Theorem 9
.

Let Assumptions 1’, 2, 3, 4 hold. Let
(x_{n})
be the sequence generated by Algorithm 2. Then,
(x_{n})
converges weakly to an equilibrium point
x^{*}
of the set-valued Lur’e system
({\mathcal{L}})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is a maximal monotone operator, using the proximal point algorithm we obtain the conclusion (see, e.g.,
[
33
]
).
∎

Applications to quasi-variational inequalities

Let us consider a particular case of (
3
) when
B=C=I
:

0\in f(x^{*})+(\mathcal{F}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(x^{*}).

(17)

In addition, if
\mathcal{F}=N_{\Omega}
, where
\Omega
is a closed convex set, then (
17
) becomes

0\in f(x^{*})+(N_{\Omega}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(x^{*}).

(18)

We show that (
18
) is indeed a quasi-variational inequality (QVI).

Proposition 10
.

The monotone inclusion (
18
) is equivalent to the QVI:

0\in f(x^{*})+N_{K(x^{*})}(x^{*}),

(19)

where
K(x):=\Omega-Df(x)
.

Proof.

We have the following equivalences

0\in f(x^{*})+(N_{\Omega}^{-1}+D)^{-1}(x^{*})\Leftrightarrow 0=f(x^{*})+\lambda
with
\lambda\in{{(N_{\Omega}^{-1}}}+D)^{-1}(x^{*})\Leftrightarrow\lambda\in N_{\Omega}(x^{*}-D\lambda)
. From
\lambda=-f(x^{*})
, we obtain

0\in f(x^{*})+N_{\Omega}(x^{*}+Df(x^{*}))=f(x^{*})+N_{K(x^{*})}(x^{*})

and the conclusion follows.
∎

Remark 6
.

(i) Similarly, the monotone inclusion (
17
) is equivalent to

0\in f(x^{*})+\mathcal{F}(x^{*}+Df(x^{*}).

(20)

(ii) The mapping
f
is not required to be
\mu
-strongly monotone and the moving set
K(x)
in the QVI (
19
) is not necessarily
l
-contractive with
l<\mu/L
as in
[
4
,
30
]
. Indeed, to solve the QVI (
19
), we can solve it equivalent form, the monotone inclusion (
18
), by computing the resolvent of the operator
\mathcal{C}:=(N_{\Omega}^{-1}+D)^{-1}
. The following is a consequence of Lemma
3
.

Corollary 11
.

Let be given
x\in H
and
\gamma>0
. Then,
y=J_{\gamma\mathcal{C}}(x)
if and only if
y=E(\gamma J_{N_{\Omega}}^{E}(Ex)+Dx)
, where
E:=(\gamma I+D)^{-1}
is a positive definite linear bounded operator.

Next we consider the case where
f
is strongly monotone. First, we recall the following fact (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
), which will be used below.

Lemma 12
.

If
f
is
\mu
-strongly monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous, then
f^{-1}
is
\frac{\mu}{L^{2}}
-strongly monotone and
\frac{1}{\mu}
-Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

For the first property of
f^{-1}
, see, e.g.,
[
23
]
. The Lipschitz continuity of
f^{-1}
follows from the observation that for all
x,y\in H_{1}
, one has

\|x-y\|=\|f\circ f^{-1}(x)-f\circ f^{-1}y\|\geq\mu\|f^{-1}(x)-f^{-1}(y)\|.

∎

The following result shows that if
f
is strongly monotone, then the QVI (
19
) can be reduced into a strongly monotone Variational Inequality.

Proposition 13
.

If
f
is strongly monotone, then
x^{*}
is a solution of the QVI (
19
) if and only if
y^{*}=(f^{-1}+D)f(x^{*})=x^{*}+Df(x^{*})
is the unique solution of the strongly monotone Variational Inequality

0\in(f^{-1}+D)^{-1}(y^{*})+N_{\Omega}(y^{*}).

(21)

Proof.

Suppose that
f
is strongly monotone and
0\in f(x^{*})+{{N_{\Omega}}}(x^{*}+Df(x^{*}))
. Then,
f^{-1}+D
is strongly monotone, Lipschitz continuous and so is
(f^{-1}+D)^{-1}
. Let
y^{*}=x^{*}+Df(x^{*})=(f^{-1}+D)f(x^{*})
, which implies that
f(x^{*})=(f^{-1}+D)^{-1}(y^{*})
. Then, we obtain the conclusion.
∎

Remark 7
.

The inclusion (
21
) can be considered as the dual form of (
18
).

5
Applications to Nash Games

Now, we give some examples in finding Nash quasi-equilibria using our developed results.
Let us consider a game with
m
players. Each player
i\in S:=\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
chooses a strategy vector in a set of available strategies
C^{i}(x^{-i})
, which depends on the strategies chosen by the other players
x^{-i}:=(x^{1},\ldots,x^{i-1},

x^{i+1},\ldots,x^{m})
.
The cost function
g_{i}(x^{i},x^{-i})
of the player
i
depends on both his strategy
x^{i}
and the strategies of the other players
x^{-i}
.

Definition 2
.

A Nash quasi-equilibrium of the game is a vector
x=(x^{1},x^{2},\ldots,x^{m})
such that

x^{i}\;\;\;{\rm minimizes}\;\;\;g_{i}(\cdot,x^{-i})\;\;\;{\rm subject\;to}\;\;x^{i}\in C^{i}(x^{-i}).

(22)

Example 1
.

Suppose that for each
i\in S
, the function
g_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
has the separable form
g_{i}(x^{i},x^{-i})=\langle g_{i1}(x^{-i}),x^{i}\rangle+g_{i2}(x^{-i})
, where
g_{i1}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(m-1)}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is Lipschitz continuous and
g_{i2}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(m-1)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. In addition, the strategy
x^{i}
is restricted to a convex set
C^{i}(x^{-i}):=K_{i}-c_{i}g_{i1}(x^{-i})
where
K_{i}
is convex and
c_{i}\geq 0
. Let
D
be the diagonal matrix
D={\rm diag}(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{m})
,

\Omega=\prod_{i=1}^{m}K_{i}

and

f(x)=(g_{11}(x^{-1}),\ldots,g_{m1}(x^{-m}))^{T}.

Note that
(\ref{Nash})
is equivalent to

\displaystyle 0\in g_{i1}(x^{-i})+N_{K_{i}-c_{i}g_{i1}(x^{-i})}(x^{i})=g_{i1}(x^{-i})+N_{K_{i}}(x^{i}+c_{i}g_{i1}(x^{-i})),\;\;\forall\;i=1,2,\ldots,m.

Consequently, we have

0\in f(x)+N_{\Omega}(x+Df(x)),

(23)

which is in the form of
(\ref{quasi1})
and equivalently, in the form of (
18
).

Example 2
.

Next, assume that
g_{i}
can be decomposed as

g_{i}(x^{i},x^{-i})=f_{i}(x^{i},x^{-i})+h_{i}(x^{i}+u_{i}(x^{-i})),

(24)

where

•

{f_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}}\to{{\mathbb{R}}}
is such that
f_{i}(\cdot,x^{-i})
is convex and differentiable;

•

u_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(m-1)}\to{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
is a Lipschitz continuous mapping which acts as perturbation of the strategies
x^{-i}
of the other players, affecting the strategy
x^{i}
;

•

h_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{{\mathbb{R}}}
is a convex function.

Then,
(\ref{Nash})
is equivalent to

0\in\nabla_{x^{i}}f_{i}(x^{i},x^{-i})+\partial h_{i}(x^{i}+u_{i}(x^{-i})),\;\;i=1,2\ldots,m.

(25)

Let

f(x)=(\nabla_{x^{1}}f_{1}(x^{1},x^{-1}),\ldots,\nabla_{x^{m}}f_{m}(x^{m},x^{-m}))^{T},

F(x)=(\partial h_{1}(x^{1}),\ldots,\partial h_{m}(x^{m}))^{T},

and

u(x)=(u_{1}(x^{-1}),\ldots,u_{m}(x^{-m}))^{T}.

Then,
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}
is a maximal monotone operator and (
25
) can be rewritten as

0\in f(x)+F(x+u(x)).

(26)

Suppose that for each
i
, the function
f_{i}
is linear with respect to
x_{i}
, i.e.,
f_{i}(x^{i},x^{-i})=\langle f_{i1}(x^{-i}),x^{i}\rangle+f_{i2}(x^{-i})
, where
f_{i1}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(m-1)}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is Lipschitz continuous and
f_{i2}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(m-1)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
u_{i}(x^{-i})=d_{i}f_{i1}(x^{-i})
for some
d_{i}\geq 0
. Let
D={\rm diag}(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{m})
. Then, (
26
) becomes

0\in f(x)+F(x+Df(x)),

(27)

which can be rewritten in the form of
(\ref{reeg1})
.

7
Conclusions

In this paper, a method to compute equilibria for general set-valued Lur’e dynamical systems is provided. The obtained results can be applied to solve numerically equilibria for set-valued Lur’e dynamical systems and Nash quasi-equilibria in game theory. It would be interesting if we could design algorithms for finding the equilibria using less the implicit scheme than the semi-implicit scheme (
6
), which do not involve the resolvents of the composition operators to save the computational cost but still assuring the convergence. This open question deserves further investigations.

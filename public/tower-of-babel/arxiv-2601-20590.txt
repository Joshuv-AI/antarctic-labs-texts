A bijection between edges of the Turán graph and irreducible elements in the dominance order lattice
Nathanaël Hassler

Abstract
. In this paper we build a bijection between the meet-irreducible elements of the lattice of the compositions of n n with parts in [ 1 , p ] [1,p] equipped with the dominance order, and the edges of the ( n , p ) (n,p) -Turán graph. Using this bijection, we then compute asymptotically the average value of some statistics on those meet-irreducible compositions.



1.
Introduction

For two integers
n\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
, we denote by
n\bmod{p}
the remainder of the euclidean division of
n
by
p
. If
a
and
b
are two integers, we write
a\equiv b\bmod{p}
if
a\bmod{p}=b\bmod{p}
. The
(n,p)
-Turán graph

\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p}
is the complete
p
-partite graph on
n
vertices, i.e. the graph with vertex set
V(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
\{a,b\}\in E(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})
if and only if
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}
, see Figure 1 for an example with
(n,p)=(8,3)
. This graph is known for being the only one having the maximum number of edges on
n
vertices and being
K_{p+1}
-free, i.e. not having a complete induced subgraph on
p+1
vertices, see e.g.
[
1
]
. Due to the structure of
\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p}
, one can see that its number of edges is given by

(1.1)

a_{p}(n)=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{(n\bmod{p})(p-(n\bmod p))}{2p}.

We refer the reader to
[
2
,
4
]
for some standard references about the Turán graph.

A
composition
of
n\geq 1
is a tuple
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
of positive integers such that
m\geq 1
and
x_{1}+\ldots+x_{m}=n
. We denote by
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
the set of compositions
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
of
n
such that for all
i
,
1\leq x_{i}\leq p
.
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
is enumerated by the
p
-
generalized Fibonacci sequence

(F_{n}^{p})_{n\geq 0}
, defined for every
p\geq 2
by

F_{n}^{p}=F_{n-1}^{p}+F_{n-2}^{p}+\ldots+F_{n-p}^{p}

with initial conditions
F_{n}^{p}=0
if
n<0
and
F_{0}^{p}=1
(see
[
5
]
). The
dominance order
on the set of compositions of
n
is defined for two compositions
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
and
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})
by

x\leq y\Longleftrightarrow\mbox{ for all }1\leq k\leq\min(m,\ell),\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}.

This order has been first introduced and studied on integer partitions, see for instance
[
3
]
.
The poset
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
equipped with the dominance order forms a lattice, i.e. each pair of compositions
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
admits a
meet
(greatest lower bound)
x\wedge y
, and a
join
(lowest upper bound)
x\vee y
. Moreover, this lattice is
distributive
, meaning that the operations meet and join are distributive relatively to the other. If
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
, we say that
y

covers

x
if
x<y
and for all
z\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
,
x\leq z\leq y\Rightarrow z\in\{x,y\}
. We say equivalently that
y
is an
upper cover
of
x
, or
x
is a
lower cover
of
y
. An element
x\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
is
meet-irreducible
if for all
y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
,
x=y\wedge z\Rightarrow y=x\mbox{ or }z=x
. Similarly, an element
x\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
is
join-irreducible
if for all
y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
,
x=y\vee z\Rightarrow y=x\mbox{ or }z=x
. Since
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
is a finite lattice,
x
is meet-irreducible (resp. join-irreducible) if and only if
x
has exactly one upper cover (resp. lower cover). See for instance
[
7
]
for the standard definitions of lattice theory. Let
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
(resp.
\textsc{ji}_{n}^{p}
) be the set of meet-irreducible (resp. join-irreducible) elements in
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
. See Figure 2 for an example of
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
and
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
with
(n,p)=(5,3)
.

In

the authors gave the enumeration of many characteristic elements in

\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
is enumerated by

\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
is a finite distributive lattice,
|\textsc{ji}_{n}^{p}|=|\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}|=a_{p}(n)
. In Section

\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}

\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
.

2.
The bijection

We start with the following lemma that counts the number of upper covers of a composition, and then characterizes the elements of
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
. For
a,b\in[1,p]
, we say that a composition
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
has a
consecutive pattern

ab
if there exists
1\leq i<m
such that
x_{i}=a
and
x_{i+1}=b
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The number of upper covers of a composition
x\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
is the number of consecutive patterns
ab
in
x
with
1\leq a\leq p-1
and
2\leq b\leq p
, plus one if
x
has the suffix
i1
for
1\leq i\leq p-1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
satisfies
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},a,b,x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{m})
with
1\leq a\leq p-1
and
2\leq b\leq p
. Then
z:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},a+1,b-1,x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{m})
is an upper cover of
x
, and we say that
z
has the form (
\star
). Similarly, if
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},i,1)
with
1\leq i\leq p-1
, then
z:=(x_{1}\ldots,x_{m},i+1)
is an upper cover of
x
, and we say that
z
has the form (
\star\star
). Conversely, suppose
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
, and let
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell})\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
be such that
x<y
. To end the proof it suffices to prove that there exists
z\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
with the form (
\star
) or (
\star\star
) such that
x<z\leq y
. Let
j
be the smallest integer such that
x_{j}<y_{j}
. Then we have
x_{j}<p
.

Case 1:

Suppose that
x_{j+1}>1
. Then we set
z_{j}=x_{j}+1
and
z_{j+1}=x_{j+1}-1
, and
z_{i}=x_{i}
for
i\not\in\{j,j+1\}
. Then
z
has the form (
\star
), and
x<z\leq y
.

Case 2:

Suppose that
x_{j+1}=x_{j+2}=\ldots=x_{m}=1
. Then we set
z_{i}=x_{i}
if
i\leq m-2
, and
z_{m-1}=x_{m-1}+1
. Note that we may have
m=j+1
. Then
z
has the form (
\star\star
), and
x<z\leq y
.

Case 3:

Suppose that
x_{j+1}=1
and there exists
j+1<k\leq m
such that
x_{k}>1
. We consider the smallest such
k
, and we set
z_{k-1}=x_{k-1}+1
and
z_{k}=x_{k}-1
, and
z_{i}=x_{i}
for
i\not\in\{k-1,k\}
. Then
z
has the form (
\star
), and
x<z\leq y
.

Considering those three cases, the converse holds and the lemma too.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

As a direct porism of Lemma
2.1
, the number of lower covers of
x\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
is the number of consecutive patterns
ab
in
x
with
2\leq a\leq p
and
1\leq b\leq p-1
, plus one if
x
does not end by 1.

Now we give a recursive decomposition of
E(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})
, and then we will provide a similar decomposition for
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
. We have

(2.1)

E(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})=E(\mathcal{T}_{n-1}^{p})\cup\left\{\{a,n\}\subseteq[1,n]\ |\ a\neq n\bmod{p}\right\},

and

|\left\{1\leq a\leq n\ |\ a\neq n\bmod{p}\right\}|=\left\lfloor\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)n\right\rfloor.

In particular, the sequence
a_{p}(n)
satisfies the recurrence relation

a_{p}(n)=a_{p}(n-1)+\left\lfloor\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)n\right\rfloor.

In order to build our bijection naturally, we now give a similar decomposition of
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
. Let
f:\textsc{mi}_{n-1}^{p}\longrightarrow\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
defined for a composition
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
by

\displaystyle f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}+1)&\mbox{ if }1\leq x_{m}<p,\\
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},1)&\mbox{ if }x_{m}=p.\end{array}\right.

Then it is not hard to check that
f
takes indeed its values in
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
, and that
f
is injective. Moreover, if
f(x)
ends by 1, then it ends by
p1
. Conversely, if
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell})\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
does not end by
i1
with
1\leq i\leq p-1
, then
y=f(y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell}-1)
if
y_{\ell}>1
, and
y=f(y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell-1})
if
(y_{\ell-1},y_{\ell})=(p,1)
. Consequently, we have

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{cl}\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}&=f(\textsc{mi}_{n-1}^{p})\cup\{x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}\ |\ x\mbox{ ends by }i1\mbox{ with }1\leq i\leq p-1\}\\
&=f(\textsc{mi}_{n-1}^{p})\cup\{(\underbrace{p,\ldots,p}_{k},i,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{m})\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq p-1,\ m\geq 1,\ k\geq 0\}.\end{array}

The last equality holds since by Lemma
2.1
, if a composition in
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
ends with
i1
for some
1\leq i\leq p-1
, this suffix produces one upper cover; thus such a composition avoids consecutive patterns
ab
with
1\leq a\leq p-1
and
2\leq b\leq p
. By the injectivity of
f
, we have

|\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}|=|\textsc{mi}_{n-1}^{p}|+|A_{n}^{p}|,

with
A_{n}^{p}=\{(\underbrace{p,\ldots,p}_{k},i,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{m})\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq p-1,\ m\geq 1,\ k\geq 0\}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Given
n,p\geq 2
, we have
|A_{n}^{p}|=\left\lfloor\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)n\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

For
x=(\underbrace{p,\ldots,p}_{k},i,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{m})\in A_{n}^{p}
, let
g(x)=m
. Since
x
is a composition of
n
we have
n=kp+i+m
, so
g(x)\equiv(n-i)\bmod{p}
, and
g(x)\not\equiv n\bmod{p}
since
i\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}
. Conversely, if
1\leq a\leq n
with
a\not\equiv n\bmod{p}
, then
g(\underbrace{p,\ldots,p}_{\left\lfloor\frac{n-a}{p}\right\rfloor},(n-a)\bmod{p},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{a})=a
. This proves that
g
is a bijection between
A_{n}^{p}
and
\{1\leq a\leq n\ |\ a\not\equiv n\bmod{p}\}
. Therefore,

|A_{n}^{p}|=|\{1\leq a\leq n\ |\ a\not\equiv n\bmod{p}\}|=\left\lfloor\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)n\right\rfloor.

∎

Now for
n,p\geq 2
we give a recursive bijection
\Psi_{n}^{p}:E(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})\longrightarrow\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
based on the decompositions from Eq. (
2.1
) and Eq. (
2.2
), and the bijection from Lemma
2.3
. For
\{a,b\}\in E(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})
with
a<b
we set

\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}f(\Psi_{n-1}^{p}(a,b))&\mbox{ if }b<n,\\
(\underbrace{p,\ldots,p}_{\left\lfloor\frac{n-a}{p}\right\rfloor},(n-a)\bmod{p},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{a})&\mbox{ if }b=n.\end{array}\right.

It follows from a direct induction that

(2.3)

\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b)=(\underbrace{p,\ldots,p}_{\left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{p}\right\rfloor},(b-a)\bmod{p},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{a-1},\underbrace{p,\ldots,p}_{\left\lfloor\frac{n-b+1}{p}\right\rfloor},(n-b+1)\bmod{p}).

For
x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
, let
k_{x}\geq 0
,
1\leq i_{x}\leq p-1
and
m_{x}\geq 0
maximum such that
x
starts with
\underbrace{p,\ldots,p}_{k_{x}},i_{x},\underbrace{1\ldots,1}_{m_{x}}
. Then we define
\Phi_{n}^{p}:\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}\longrightarrow E(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})
by

\Phi_{n}^{p}(x)=\{m_{x}+\delta_{x},pk_{x}+i_{x}+m_{x}+\delta_{x}\},

where

\delta_{x}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\mbox{ if }(x\mbox{ ends by }1\Rightarrow x\mbox{ ends by }p1),\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Observe that
\Phi_{n}^{p}
is well defined since
k_{x},i_{x}
and
m_{x}
always exist for the elements of
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
. Moreover,
\Phi_{n}^{p}
takes its values in
E(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})
because
i_{x}\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}
. With a direct verification, we deduce the following theorem.

Remark 2.5
.

By doing the same investigation for
\textsc{ji}_{n}^{p}
, we obtain that the following map is a bijection from
E(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})
to
\textsc{ji}_{n}^{p}
:

\tilde{\Psi}_{n}^{p}(a,b)=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{a-1},(b-a)\bmod{p}+1,\underbrace{p,\ldots,p}_{\left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{p}\right\rfloor},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{n-b}).

Using Remark
2.2
, we can check that it takes indeed its values in
\textsc{ji}_{n}^{p}
. To define its reciprocal, for
x\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
, let
k_{x}
(resp.
m_{x}
) be maximal such that
x
starts (resp. ends) with
k_{x}
(resp.
m_{x}
) consecutive 1s. The reciprocal of
\tilde{\Psi}_{n}^{p}
is then defined by

\tilde{\Phi}_{n}^{p}(x)=\{k_{x}+1,n-m_{x}\}.

For
x\in\textsc{ji}_{n}^{p}
,
n-k_{x}-m_{x}-1\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}
, hence
\tilde{\Phi}_{n}^{p}
takes its values in
E(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})
.

3.
Some statistics on
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}

The bijection
\Psi_{n}^{p}
from Section
2
allows us to express statistics on
\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b)
easily in terms of
a
and
b
. It therefore gives a method to compute the average value of statistics. In this section we give an illustration of this method on three statistics: the number of parts, the first part and the number of weak records. We start with a lemma computing some sums over
E(\mathcal{T}_{n}^{p})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a given
p\geq 2
we have as
n\to\infty

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}a=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{n^{3}}{6}+O(n^{2}),

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}b=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{n^{3}}{3}+O(n^{2}).

Proof.

First, let us compute the sum without the congruence constraint.

\displaystyle s(n)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq n}a=\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\sum_{b=a+1}^{n}a=\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}(n-a)a

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}(n-1)}{2}-\frac{n(n-1)(2n-1)}{6}=\frac{n(n-1)(n+1)}{6}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle S(n)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq n}b=\sum_{b=2}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{b-1}b=\sum_{b=2}^{n}b(b-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}-\frac{n(n+1)}{2}=\frac{n(n-1)(n+1)}{3}.

Now if
0\leq i\leq p-1
, let
\delta_{i}=1
if
i>n\bmod{p}
, and
\delta_{i}=0
otherwise. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\equiv b\equiv i\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}a=\sum_{a=0}^{\left\lfloor n/p\right\rfloor-\delta_{i}-1}\sum_{b=a+1}^{\left\lfloor n/p\right\rfloor-\delta_{i}}(pa+i)=p\cdot s(\left\lfloor n/p\right\rfloor)+O(n^{2})=\frac{n^{3}}{6p^{2}}+O(n^{2}).

We deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}a=s(n)-\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\equiv b\equiv i\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}a=\frac{n^{3}}{6}+O(n^{2})-p\left(\frac{n^{3}}{6p^{2}}+O(n^{2})\right)=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{n^{3}}{6}+O(n^{2}).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\equiv b\equiv i\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}b=\sum_{b=1}^{\left\lfloor n/p\right\rfloor-\delta_{i}}\sum_{a=0}^{b-1}(pb+i)=p\cdot S(\left\lfloor n/p\right\rfloor)+O(n^{2})=\frac{n^{3}}{3p^{2}}+O(n^{2}),

and so

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}b=S(n)-\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\equiv b\equiv i\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}b=\frac{n^{3}}{3}+O(n^{2})-p\left(\frac{n^{3}}{3p^{2}}+O(n^{2})\right)=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{n^{3}}{3}+O(n^{2}).

∎

3.1.
The number of parts

For a composition
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
we denote by
\texttt{parts}(x)
its number of parts
m
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The average number of parts of a composition in
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
satisfies as
n\to\infty

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}|}\sum_{x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}}\texttt{\emph{parts}}(x)\sim\left(1+\frac{2}{p}\right)\frac{n}{3}.

Proof.

From Eq. (
2.3
), for every
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}
we have

\displaystyle\texttt{parts}(\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b))

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{p}\right\rfloor+1+a-1+\left\lfloor\frac{n-b+1}{p}\right\rfloor+\mathds{1}_{b\equiv n+1\bmod{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{p}+\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)a+O(1).

The total number of parts in all compositions of
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
is then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}}\texttt{parts}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}\texttt{parts}(\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{n}{p}+\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)a\right]+O(n^{2})

\displaystyle=a_{p}(n)\cdot\frac{n}{p}+\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}\frac{n^{3}}{6}+O(n^{2}),

by Lemma
3.1
. From Eq. (
1.1
),
a_{p}(n)=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}+O(1)
, so we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}|}\sum_{x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}}\texttt{parts}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{p}(n)}\left(a_{p}(n)\cdot\frac{n}{p}+\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}\frac{n^{3}}{6}+O(n^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{p}+\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{n}{3}+O(1)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{2}{p}\right)\frac{n}{3}+O(1).

∎

3.2.
The first part

For a composition
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
we denote by
\texttt{first}(x)
the value of its first part
x_{1}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The average value of the statistic
first
on the compositions of
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
satisfies as
n\to\infty

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}|}\sum_{x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}}\texttt{\emph{first}}(x)=p\left(1-\frac{p}{n}+\frac{p(p+1)}{3n^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right)\right).

Proof.

From Eq. (
2.3
), for every
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}
we have

\displaystyle\texttt{first}(\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}p&\mbox{ if }b-a\geq p\\
(b-a)\bmod{p}&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The sum of
\texttt{first}(x)
over all compositions
x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
is then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}}\texttt{first}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}\texttt{first}(\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a\geq p\end{subarray}}p+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a<p\end{subarray}}(b-a)\bmod{p}.

Let us first compute the following sum when
n\geq p-1
.

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a<p\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{n-p+1}\sum_{b=a+1}^{a+p-1}1+\sum_{a=n-p+2}^{n-1}\sum_{b=a+1}^{n}1=\sum_{a=1}^{n-p+1}(p-1)+\sum_{a=n-p+2}^{n-1}(n-a)

(3.2)

\displaystyle=(n-p+1)(p-1)+\sum_{a=1}^{p-2}a=(n-p+1)(p-1)+\frac{(p-2)(p-1)}{2}.

We deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a\geq p\end{subarray}}p=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}p-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a<p\end{subarray}}p=p\left(a_{p}(n)-(n-p+1)(p-1)-\frac{(p-2)(p-1)}{2}\right).

Now observe that if
1\leq a<b\leq n
with
b-a<p
, then
b-a=(b-a)\bmod{p}
. Then for
n\geq p-1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a<p\end{subarray}}(b-a)\bmod{p}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a<p\end{subarray}}(b-a)=\sum_{a=1}^{n-p+1}\sum_{b=a+1}^{a+p-1}(b-a)+\sum_{a=n-p+2}^{n-1}\sum_{b=a+1}^{n}(b-a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{n-p+1}\left[\frac{(a+p)(a+p-1)-a(a+1)}{2}-(p-1)a\right]+\sum_{a=n-p+2}^{n-1}\left[\frac{n(n+1)-a(a+1)}{2}-(n-a)a\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{n-p+1}\frac{p(p-1)}{2}+\sum_{a=n-p+2}^{n-1}\left[\frac{(n-a)^{2}+n-a}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\left(n-p+1+\frac{p-2}{3}\right).

Finally,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}}\texttt{first}(x)

\displaystyle=p\left(a_{p}(n)-(n-p+1)(p-1)-\frac{(p-2)(p-1)}{2}\right)+\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\left(n-p+1+\frac{p-2}{3}\right)

\displaystyle=p\left(a_{p}(n)-n\cdot\frac{p-1}{2}+\frac{(p-1)(p+1)}{6}\right).

Using that
a_{p}(n)=\frac{(p-1)n^{2}}{2p}+O(1)
, we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}|}\sum_{x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}}\texttt{first}(x)=p\left(1-\frac{p}{n}+\frac{p(p+1)}{3n^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right)\right).

∎

3.3.
The number of weak records

For
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{p}
, a
weak record
is a position
1\leq j\leq m
such that
x_{i}\leq x_{j}
for each
1\leq i\leq j
. We denote by
\texttt{wrec}(x)
the number of weak records of
x
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The average number of weak records of a composition in
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
satisfies as
n\to\infty

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}|}\sum_{x\in\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}}\texttt{\emph{wrec}}(x)\sim\frac{2n}{3p}.

Proof.

From Eq. (
2.3
), we have that

\displaystyle\texttt{wrec}(\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b))=\left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{p}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{n-b+1}{p}\right\rfloor

when
b-a\geq p
. We do not compute it in details for
b-a<p
as we shall see the contribution of those pairs
\{a,b\}
to the total number of weak records in
\textsc{mi}_{n}^{p}
is negligible. Indeed, we saw in Eq. (
3.2
) that the number of those pairs is
O(n)
, and since
\texttt{wrec}(\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b))\leq n
for every pair
\{a,b\}
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a<p\end{subarray}}\texttt{wrec}(\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b))=O(n^{2}).

Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}\texttt{wrec}(\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a\geq p\end{subarray}}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{p}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{n-b+1}{p}\right\rfloor\right)+O(n^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a\geq p\end{subarray}}(n-a)+O(n^{2}).

Using Eq. (
3.2
) and Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\\
b-a\geq p\end{subarray}}a=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}a-O(n^{2})=\frac{na_{p}(n)}{3}-O(n^{2}).

Finally,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<b\leq n\\
a\not\equiv b\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}\texttt{wrec}(\Psi_{n}^{p}(a,b))=\frac{1}{p}\left(na_{p}(n)-\frac{na_{p}(n)}{3}\right)+O(n^{2})=\frac{2na_{p}(n)}{3p}+O(n^{2}),

which concludes.
∎

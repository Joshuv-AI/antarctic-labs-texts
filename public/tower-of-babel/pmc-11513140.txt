Missed nursing care and associated factors among nurses at University of Gondar Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, Ethiopia, 2022
Authors: Chanyalew Worku Kassahun, Kidist Endalkachew, Chilot Kassa Mekonnen, Huluager Kassie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The issue of missed nursing care is a problem that affects the overall quality of nursing care in hospitals around the world. However, there is limited research about it in Ethiopia. This study assessed missed nursing care and factors among nurses. An institutional-based cross-sectional study was conducted among 485 nurses at the University of Gondar Specialized Hospital. The data were collected from May 15 to June 15, 2022 using a self-administered questionnaire. A simple random sampling technique was used to select study participants. The data was entered to EPI DATA version 4.6 and exported to SPSS version 23 for analysis. Descriptive statistics were computed, and a summative score of missed nursing care was calculated. Then, multiple linear regression analysis was used to identify the factors. A P-value of 0.05 was used to declare significant level. In this study, missed nursing care was 62.5% (95% CI 60.98–64.02). Documentation, vital signs assessment, intravenous care, and assessment as per hospital policy and patient education were the most frequently missed nursing care. Being single in marital status (B = −4.609, P = 0.004), adequacy of nursing staff (B = −2.458, P = 0.003), satisfaction with income (B = −8.753, P = 0.007), working in medical unit (B = −5.708, P = 0.002) and working in both day and night shift (B = 1.731, P = 0.027) were statistically associated factors. More than half of the participants missed basic nursing care. Most of the identified factors correlated with missed nursing care negatively. Efforts should be done to enhance communication between health team members, assign a manageable number of patients per nurse, and adjust shifts and units.

Introduction

In the hospital, nursing care function as a very critical component of patient care that knits together diverse elements for patient recovery. These elements include medication administration, patient ambulation, position changes, patient bathing, oral care, recording vital signs, documenting intakes and outputs, nutrition, education for discharge from hospital, and so forth. When these critical nursing actions are not offered to the patient, it is known as missed nursing care, and it is a subset of error of omission1. It refers when these actions or tasks are delayed, only partially executed, or left entirely unaddressed as planned during a patient’s care2–6. Lack of oral care, failure to observe proper medication time, lack of skin care, and lack of attention to the patient’s religious needs are some of the common missed nursing cares7–9.

Missed nursing care is a worldwide problem in the healthcare arena of nurses and nurse managers which varies from 10 to 50%8,9. Nurse Managers play a crucial role in addressing the issue of missed care by promoting transparency and open communication about missed care10. Studies reported that the essential nursing tasks were not executed as planned, and the missed nursing cares ranged from high to low. In a cross-sectional studies in US, Malaysia and Iran showed that the misses nursing care was 36%11, 28.6% and 23%12,13 respectively. In another Mixed and descriptive studies conducted in Nigeria and Egypt reported that 42.5%14 and 41.1%15, respectively. Findings in Ethiopia showed that the average score for missed nursing care is 63.24%, in Jimma16, and 74.6% in Tigray17. The level of missed nursing care was high in a cross-sectional observational and medical record review study in Bahir Dar18.

Missed nursing care is problematic because nurses coordinate, provide, and evaluate many interventions prescribed by others to treat illness in hospitalized patients. It extends far beyond mere misunderstandings; they have tangible consequences for patients and healthcare systems such as high cost, increased length of stays, leads to mortality, and other life-threatening complications19,20. It can also causes nosocomial infection, medication error, increase health care costs, pressure ulcers, nurses and patient dissatisfaction and reduced organizational creditability21. These consequences were also seen in Ethiopia in the form of pressure ulcers, increase health care costs, nosocomial infection due to lack of positioning and others22,23. Additionally, missed nursing care leads to higher occurrences of falls, new onset delirium, increased pain and discomfort, malnourishment, and unfavorable outcomes for nurses, such as increased levels of burnout and low job satisfaction, reduced overall quality of care levels, poor staff retention and high staff turnover in the institution8,24–26.

Missed nursing care can result from various factors such as inadequate hospital resources8,27, working environment6, nurse-patient ratios, increase in a nurse’s workload28, and the number of hours a nurse works per shift29, lack of effective communication, nurses’ satisfaction with their current job and their intention to remain15,16,27,30,31. In more recent years, the working environment of nurses has been identified as a major contributing factor to missed nursing care32. There are also other factors like heavy admission/discharge activity, fewer assistive personnel, and unbalanced patient assignments33 perception of patient safety culture within a unit17,27, severe clinical conditions, perceived lack of time to care18,34 working unit type, nurses’ age and perceptions of staffing adequacy15,35, sex, number of days absent, satisfaction towards teamwork)16, educational level, working shift and having an intention to leave the institution17 are associated with missed nursing care.

There are contributing factors for the nursing care to be missed in the Ethiopian health settings36. But, the magnitude and contributing factors of missed nursing care are not well supported by research evidences in Ethiopia, in the study area in particular. Therefore, this study aimed to determine missed nursing care and identify the factors that influence missed nursing care.

Methods

Study period and area

This study was carried out in the comprehensive specialized hospital of the University of Gondar from May 15 to June 15, 2022. The hospital is a university referral hospital located in Gondar and serves as a referral center for over 5 million populations. It provides specialty and subspecialty services, including pediatrics, surgery, gynecology and obstetrics; internal medicine, psychiatry, and ophthalmology in its inpatient and outpatient services. Based on the University of Gondar compressive specialized hospital’s matron office report, there were around 679 nurses who were working in the hospital during the study period. The average nurse’s patient ratio in this study area was 1:8. The hospital has more than 1,000 beds and provides services to over 200,000 patients each year37,38.

Study design

An institutional-based cross-sectional study design was conducted at the University of Gondar Comprehensive Specialized Hospital.

Source and study population

Source population

All nurses working at the University of Gondar Comprehensive Specialized Hospital.

Study population

All nurses working at the University of Gondar Comprehensive Specialized Hospital are available during the data collection period.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

All nurses who were working in the university of Gondar comprehensive specialized hospital.

Exclusion criteria

Nurses who were not available during the data collection period, nurses who deliver free services, nurse managers and guests were excluded.

Sample size determination

The maximum sample size was calculated from the factors of missed nursing care using stat calc Epi info software version 7 with the assumption of power 80%, confidence level 95%, and ratio 1:1 with 3 statically significant variables such as sex, working shift and educational status17 and non-response rate 5%. Then the final maximum sample size was 485.

Sampling technique and procedure

The total sample size was obtained from the University of Gondar comprehensive specialized hospital. Study participants were selected by using a simple random sampling method.

Study variables

The dependent variable of the study was Missed Nursing Care while the independent variables were socio-demographic variables (age, sex, marital status, educational level, income status), profession-related factors (work experience, satisfaction, hand-over from the previous shift, communication within the nursing team, support from team members (nursing)) and organizational factors (an inadequate number of nurse, increased patient load, working unit, training, working shift, medication availability, equipment availability, the function of equipment).

Operational definitions

An inadequate number of nurses

Can be defined as the number of nurses that is not sufficient to provide high-quality care or is lower than the standard nurse-patient ratio. A study conducted in Ethiopia showed that the nurse-to-patient ratio ranges from 1:6 to1:12 based on the individual institution’s patient load and nurse availability, if the ratio is more than 1:12, it is inadequate39.

Medication availability

medications are the most common therapeutic intervention and a crucial component of medical care for any health care system. WHO recommends at least 80% availability of essential medicines in healthcare facilities40. But in low/middle-income countries this is still a major challenge41.

Hand-over

is proper communication when one nurse transitions patient care to another nurse (previous shift or sending unit). It was categorized as a significant reason, moderate reason, minor reason and not a reason for missed care42.

Communication breakdown

Breakdown in communication impedes workflow and creates tension between the nursing team. It was categorized as a significant reason, moderate reason, minor reason and not a reason for missed care42.

Other departments

Defined as any departments like physiotherapy, psychiatry, laboratory, pharmacy and others rather than nursing departments.

Data collection tool and procedure

A customized and self-administered “modified MISSCARE” survey tool was used to collect the data43,44. The missed nursing care tool is a 24 five-point Likert-type scale. Participants were asked to answer from 5 to 1 (5-always, 4-often, 3-occasionally, 2-rarely, and 1-never). The reliability of this tool in this study was Cronbach’s alpha of 0.84, and it has four dimensions (basic care interventions, interventions to attend to individual needs; discharge planning and patient education, and care interventions with continuous assessment). In addition, demographic, professional, and factors of missed nursing care data were also collected. The study participant list was extracted from the head nurse’s office and then the lottery method was used to select participants. Participants were grouped into their own units and the number of participants in each unit was determined. Questionnaires were distributed to each unit by data collectors during work periods.

The missed nursing care score was calculated with a minimum potential score of 24 and a maximum score of 120. Then, the mean score with its SD (84 ± 16.38) was calculated.

The mean percentage score of the missed nursing care was calculated using the following formula:43

Data processing and analysis

Data were entered into Epi DATA version 4.6 and exported to SPSS version 23 for analysis. Descriptive statistics were done for socio-demographics, job-related characteristics, and items of missed nursing care. The overall missed nursing care scores was calculated for each of the sub-scales.

Multiple linear regression analysis was used to identify the most significant predictor of missed nursing care. The assumption of multiple linear regressions; linearity, normality, and constant variance were checked. The variance inflation factor was used to see whether there is a correlation among the independent factors. Significant independent predictors were declared at a 95% confidence interval with a P-value of ≤ 0.05, and unstandardized β was used for interpretation.

Data quality assurance

To ensure data quality, a pretested and validated tool was employed. Training was given to the data collectors and supervisors on the research objectives, participant selection process, data collection tools and procedures. The data collected were checked for completeness on a daily basis.

The response rate of the study was 100%. The age of the participants ranged from 23 to 51 years. Around 277 (57.1%) were female, and 410 (84.5%) were Bachelor’s degree holders. More than half of the participants, 312 (64.3%) were ever married. Two hundred-six (42.5%) of participants had 1–5 years of work experience. In terms of working shift, the majority, 303 (62.5%) of participants worked day and night in the last month. Around 123 (25.4%) had planned to leave the nursing profession, and 461 (95.1%) participants were not satisfied with their income (Table 1).

Missed nursing care among nurses

The overall missed nursing care was 62.5% (95% CI 60.98–64.02). The majority of participants reported that documentation of all necessary data (81.25%), vital sign assessment (75.5%), performing IV catheter care and assessments according to hospital policy (71.25) and patient teaching (70%) as the most frequently missed nursing care. Others like patient bathing, mouth care and attending interdisciplinary conferences are less frequently missed nursing care.

Regarding the dimension of missed nursing care, continuous assessment dimension was the most frequently missed nursing care with a mean of 30.63(SD ± 6.17). Whereas, the planning and teaching dimension were reported as less frequently missed nursing care with a mean of 10.39 (SD ± 2.58) (Table 3).

Discussion

In this study, missed nursing care and associated factors among nurses at the University of Gondar Comprehensive Specialized Hospital were assessed. Therefore, it was reported that nearly 63% of nursing care was missed. Documentation of all necessary data, vital sign assessment, performing intravenous catheter (central line site) care and assessments according to hospital policy, and patient teaching as the most frequently missed nursing care. Being single in marital status, adequacy of nursing staff, satisfaction with income, other departments working in the unit did not provide the care needed, working shifts and working units were statistically significant predictors of missed nursing care.

In the current study, more than half (62.5%) of nursing care was missed. This implies that the nursing care provided by the majority of the study participants had been delayed, partially completed, or not completed at all. This finding is consistent with studies conducted in Jimma and Kenya, where the missed nursing care was reported to be 63.24% and 60% respectively16,44. The reason may be due to the similarity in age and sex distribution of the study participants.

However, the result of this study is much higher than the studies conducted in Nigeria, Malaysia, the US, Egypt and Iran where the missed nursing care was 42.5%45,28.6%46,36%11,41.1%47 and 23%48 respectively. This difference might be due to differences in the age where the majority of the participants in this study were under 30 years old, but they were between 30 and 40 years old in a study done in Nigeria. Three fourths of the participants were females (70.4% in Nigeria), the difference in educational status, where only one-fifth (18.3%) of the participants have a bachelor’s degree in nursing, whereas in this study the majority of the participants have a bachelor’s degree and some of the participants mastered in nursing. Whereas the difference between studies done in Malaysia and this study might be due to the difference in human resources, where 2157 registered nurses work in three shifts in Malaysia but only 679 registered nurses work in two shifts in this study area. And, samples were taken only from the medical and surgical ward in Malaysia, and most study participants were female and diploma holders in the US. They were only on NICU and almost all of the study participants were females (98.4%), and it was done in 2016, in hospitals in three teaching hospital only in ICU unit, in Iran participants were taken from eight public hospitals and medical and surgical wards in private hospitals.

But, this finding is lower than a study conducted in Tigray where the missed nursing care was reported to be 74.6%49. This difference might be because the study in Tigray was conducted only in the gynecology and obstetrics unit and multi-setting and more than half of the participants were midwives (58.4%). But, in this study all participants were nurses.

In the current study, documentation of all necessary data, vital sign assessment, IV catheter care and following assessments as per the hospital policy and patient teaching were the most frequently missed nursing care. This finding is supported by studies conducted in Iran and Egypt where patient teaching was missed47,48, and also supported by a study conducted in Nigeria where study participants missed IV catheter care45. This similarity might be due to similarity in study design (cross-sectional study design), sex distribution (the majority were female) and level of experience (< 5 years).

The basic care intervention dimension and planning and teaching intervention dimension were relatively the least frequently reported missed nursing care in this study. This finding is not in line with a study conducted in Jimma where the basic care intervention dimension was reported as the most frequently missed nursing care16. This difference might be due to multiple study settings and a higher number of diploma levels of education in a study done in Jimma.

A unit increase in marital status being single decreased missed nursing care by 4.6 units. In this study, being single was almost five times less likely to have missed nursing care as compared with being ever married. This implies delayed, partially completed or not completed at all nursing care is five times more common in those ever married than single. This result is inconsistent with the result of a study conducted in Egypt where being single was almost two times (63.4%) more likely to have missed nursing care as compared to ever married47. This difference might be due to only one-fifth of the participants were being single (21.3%) in a study done in Egypt as compared to this study where one third is single (35.7%). It might be also due to a difference in the level of education, where about 95% of the participants, in this study have BSC and master degree in nursing but only 40% of the participants in a study done in Egypt have BSC and master degree holder.

Other departments working in the unit that did not provide the care needed to be increased missed nursing care by 2.2 units. This implies that other departments, like physiotherapy, working in the unit which did not provide their duties properly, increased missed nursing care by almost 2 times. Whereas units increase in working in medical units decrease missed nursing care by 5.7 units, which means missed nursing care decreased by nearly 6 times in the medical work unit as compared with working in other units.

A unit increase in satisfaction with income decreased missed nursing care by 8.8 units. This implies delayed, partially completed, or not completed at all nursing care is 9 times more common in nursing care of those who are not satisfied with their income as compared with those satisfied with their income. A unit increase in working in both shifts (i.e. day and night) increases missed nursing care by 1.7 units. Missed nursing care is almost 2 times more common among nurses working on both shifts than working on one shift.

Limitations of the study

Due to the cross-sectional nature of the study, establishing the cause-and-effect relationship between missed nursing care and associated factors would be impossible.

Despite the high response rate in this study, recall bias and social desirability bias could be potential limitations for this study. As the data was participants’ perceived self-report, it might be under/over-reported.

Conclusions

In this study, more than half of basic nursing care was missed. Being single in marital status, adequacy of nursing staff, satisfaction with income, and working in a medical unit are protective factors of missed nursing care. As per the findings, the Minister of Health had better improve nurses’ salary so as to increase nurses’ level of satisfaction to avoid missed nursing care. The hospital ought to decrease the workload of nurses to prevent missed nursing care. The hospital should better consider the balanced distribution of nurses’ to-patient ratio. Further research is recommended with observational techniques and qualitative design to see for further investigation of the reasons of delay of care and why patient teaching is among missed nursing care. It has to been from the patients’ perspective. It is also needed to see other interventional studies to examine the causal relationship between missed nursing care and its factors.

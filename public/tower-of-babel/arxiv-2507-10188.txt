Well-posedness of an optical flow based optimal control formulation for image registration
Johannes Haubner, Christian Clason

Abstract
We consider image registration as an optimal control problem using an optical flow formulation, i.e., we discuss an optimization problem that is governed by a linear hyperbolic transport equation. Requiring Lipschitz continuity of the vector fields that parametrize the transformation leads to an optimization problem in a non-reflexive Banach space. We introduce relaxations of the optimization problem involving smoothed maximum and minimum functions and appropriate Orlicz spaces. To derive well-posedness results for the relaxed optimization problem, we revisit and establish new existence and uniqueness results for the linear hyperbolic transport equations. We further discuss limit considerations with respect to the relaxation parameter and discretizations.

Introduction

Modeling optimization problems in non-reflexive Banach spaces has demonstrated its utility in several applications in science and engineering, such as image processing. Its use comes from the property that the total variation term in the
\mathrm{BV}
-norm promotes piecewise constant behavior of optimal solutions
[
10
,
36
]
. Optimization in non-reflexive Banach spaces is also appealing in shape optimization
[
22
]
. In shape optimization via the method of mappings, it is desirable to work with transformations that are bi-Lipschitz, i.e. are bijective and both the transformation and its inverse attain
W^{1,\infty}
-regularity
[
9
]
. In this work, we focus on image registration, where similar requirements on the set of admissible transformations are present as for shape optimization, cf.
[
44
]
. Current Hilbert space approaches either work with too smooth function spaces that embed into
C^{1}
and, therefore, e.g., prevent the appearance of non-smooth features, or in too weak function space settings that do not provide theoretical guarantees.

The goal of this work is to provide a theoretical foundation to bridge this gap. To this end, we consider
an optimal control problem that is governed by a linear hyperbolic transport equation, see e.g.,
[
7
,
13
,
44
]
, given by

\displaystyle\min_{v\in V_{\mathrm{ad}}}

\displaystyle J(\phi(\cdot,T),\phi_{\mathrm{tar}})+\mathcal{R}(v)

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \text{s.t. }\partial_{t}\phi+v\cdot\nabla\phi&=0&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\phi(\cdot,0)&=\phi_{0}&&\text{on }\Omega,\end{aligned}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, denotes a bounded Lipschitz domain,
T>0
, and
\phi_{0},\phi_{\mathrm{tar}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
denote the input and target image. Further,
V_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
denotes the set of admissible transformations,
v
denotes a vector field, and
\alpha>0
denotes a regularization parameter for the regularization term
\mathcal{R}(v)
. We are particularly interested in settings that involve an
W^{1,\infty}
-regularization of the velocity in space. For the sake of clarity and to present our main ideas, we discuss one realization with stationary vector fields in detail, which works with
V_{\mathrm{ad}}=W_{0}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}\cap H^{1+\sigma}(\Omega)^{d}
and

\mathcal{R}(v)\coloneqq\beta\|v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|v\|_{H^{1+\sigma}(\Omega)^{d}}^{2}

for
\alpha,\beta>0
and
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. While restricting to stationary vector fields for the specific application, we derive a general framework for the optimization and discuss the linear hyperbolic transport equation in a setting that allows for instationary vector fields.

Optimization problems with a
W^{1,\infty}
-norm regularization term are challenging due to the non-differentiability of its norm, see
[
15
]
.
A common strategy, which is also called
“augmented Morozov regularization”
[
14
]
, is to introduce an additional scalar optimization variable
t
that replaces the regularization in the objective function and to add constraints that bound the components of the regularization pointwise almost everywhere.
Instead, we consider a relaxation of the
L^{\infty}
-norm involving an approximation of the maximum and minimum function, see also
[
6
]
, or
[
30
]
for interior point methods in function space and replace the regularization term
\mathcal{R}
by a parameterized regularization term
\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}
given by

\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}(v)\coloneqq\beta\Psi_{\gamma}(v)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|v\|_{H^{1+\sigma}(\Omega)^{d}}^{2},

where
\Psi_{\gamma}
is defined in (
36
).
We derive the relaxed function via entropic regularization with regularization parameter
\gamma>0
using a strategy similar to that in
[
16
]
. The resulting smooth approximation for the
L^{\infty}
-norm is given by

\chi_{\gamma}(u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp({\gamma^{-1}u(x)})\mathrm{d}x\right)+\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp({-\gamma^{-1}u(x)})\mathrm{d}x\right),

(2)

where the latter terms are smooth approximations of the minimum and maximum, see
[
30
]
, and
which is naturally posed in the Orlicz space
L_{\exp}(\Omega)
. It is related to the entropic risk measure,
allowing us to use several of its properties for developing a concise framework.
Moreover, Orlicz spaces play a critical role in statistics
[
12
]
, as they allow for modeling of precise tail behaviors of random variables and regularity of stochastic processes. The relaxation of the regularization leads to optimization in a non-reflexive Banach space with
\mathrm{div}(v)\in L_{\exp}(\Omega)
, which is strictly larger and for which it is not ensured that
\mathrm{div}(v)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. This prevents the application of existence results for transport equations established in
[
28
]
.

The optimal control problem (
1
) with instationary vector fields

was theoretically investigated in
[
24
,
2
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
27

28
]
.
Choosing vector fields such that they are in

ensures that the underlying transformation which maps

to

is bi-Lipschitz

, which is desirable for image registration. Moreover, the existence of solutions to the optimal control problem (

establishes existence results under the regularity requirements

,
18
,

[

, and by modeling the set of admissible controls adequately
[
28
, Sec. 8.2]
. Up until now, there are several results on well-posedness of the optimization problem that are constrained by linear hyperbolic transport equations
[
13
,
27
]
. However, these results do not cover the case where the divergence of the vector field

is only ensured to be in

, is an appropriate Lipschitz domain. Moreover, this setting is not covered by uniqueness results that are available for the linear hyperbolic transport equations. While the choice

,

,

,

,

,

. However, this condition only provides uniqueness of the forward transport equation. Moreover,

,

, which is more general than the above results (
L^{\infty}
in time is more restrictive in time than
L^{1}
, and, e.g. on cubes,

is a larger class of functions than
\mathrm{BMO}

[

).

The relaxed optimization problem, for which the
L^{\infty}
-norms are approximated by (

) and which is posed in a Banach space that we denote by

, has still a regularization term that is not differentiable. Also deriving differentiability results for the transport equation with respect to the vector field is involved and typically based on the notion of measure solutions
[

,
23
,
27
]
, under additional restrictions and assumptions on the objective function

in (

), and the regularity of the vector fields, e.g.,
[
27
]
.
In the scope of this work, we focus on eliminating the non-differentiability coming from the regularization term. Discretization of the problem yields a canonical way to obtain differentiability of

due to the additional

-regularity that comes from the discretization.

Overview

This work is organized as follows.
Section

2
introduces the conceptual framework. Within this conceptual framework, we discuss well-posedness and limit considerations. The rest of the work shows that image registration with
W^{1,\infty}
-regularization can be considered in that framework.
In
section

3
, we recall the definition of the Orlicz spaces
L_{\exp}
and
L{\log}L
and collect results that are needed for the analysis. In
section

4
, we introduce an approximation to the

-norm, which we motivate as an entropy regularized version of the

-norm for continuous functions. We derive its explicit form, properties on boundedness, sequential weak
∗

-norm. In
section

5
, we consider the transport equation, recall known results from the literature, prove a novel uniqueness result (see
section

5.1
), and show the validity of a stability result from the literature under these requirements (see
section

5.2
). In
section

6
, we collect the results from the previous sections in order to show that the properties of the conceptual framework are fulfilled. We finally show that there exists a sequence of semidiscretized relaxed optimization problems with differentiable regularization terms that approximate the original optimization problem (
1
) with a
W^{1,\infty}
-regularization.

successively for

(12)

for all

that fulfill

.
Due to the boundedness of

3
Orlicz spaces

We consider Orlicz spaces as defined in
[
35
]
. Let
\Phi
be a (relaxed) Young function according to
[
35
, Def. 4.2.1, and Rem. 4.2.9]
, i.e., there exists a function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\Phi(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(s)\mathrm{d}s
,
t\geq 0
with
\varphi(0)=0
,
\varphi(s)\geq 0
,
\varphi(s)
is left-continuous at any
s>0
,
\varphi
is nondecreasing on
[0,\infty)
, and
\lim_{s\to\infty}\varphi(s)=\infty
.
Moreover, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open domain with measure
\mu
such that
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}\mu=1
. The Orlicz space
\mathcal{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega)
is defined by

\mathcal{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega)\coloneqq\mathcal{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega,\mu)\coloneqq\left\{f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}~:~\exists\eta>0\text{ s.t. }\int_{\Omega}\Phi(\eta|f(x)|)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty\right\}.

(14)

Remark 3.1
.

In the scope of this work, for bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

that satisfy
|\Omega|>0
, where
|\Omega|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
, we work with
\mathrm{d}\mu\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\mathrm{d}x
.

For a measurable function
f
on
\Omega
, its Luxemburg norm is defined by

\|f\|_{\Phi}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\gamma>0\,:\,\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\gamma}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq 1\right\}.

(15)

Proposition 3.2

(cf.
[
39
, Prop. 2.2]
and
[
43
, Lem. 2.17]
)
.

Let
\Phi
be a Young function, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}\mu=1
. Then
(\mathcal{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{\Phi})
is a Banach space. Moreover, there holds that

\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{\Phi}}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq 1\quad\text{for all}\quad f\in\mathcal{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega),\quad f\geq 0.

Proposition 3.3

(cf.
[
16
, Lem. 2.6]
)
.

If
\Phi
is a Young function, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}\mu=1
, then

\|f\|_{\Phi}\leq\max\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi(|f(x)|)\mathrm{d}\mu(x),1\right)\quad\text{for all}\quad f\in\mathcal{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega).

(16)

We will be working with the space
L_{\exp}(\Omega)\coloneqq\mathcal{L}^{\Phi_{\exp}}(\Omega)

(see also
[
5
, p. 243ff]
),
where

\Phi_{\exp}(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}t\quad&\text{if }0\leq t<1,\\
\mathrm{e}^{t-1}\quad&\text{if }t\geq 1.\end{cases}

(17)

The Fenchel–Legendre conjugate of a convex function
\Phi:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is defined by
\Phi^{\ast}(t)\coloneqq\sup_{s\in I}st-\Phi(s)
. Then, by definition of
\Phi^{\ast}
, the following lemma holds.

Proposition 3.4

(Fenchel–Young inequality)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, and
\Phi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function. Then,

yz\leq\Phi(y)+\Phi^{\ast}(z)\quad\text{for all}\quad y,z\in I.

Remark 3.5
.

The function
\Phi_{\log}
defined by

\Phi_{\log}(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}t\ln^{+}t\quad&\text{if }t>0,\\
0&\text{if }t=0,\end{cases}

(18)

with
\ln^{+}(t)\coloneqq\max(0,\ln(t))
, is the Fenchel–Legendre transform of
\Phi_{\exp}
, see
[
35
, Example 4.3.6]
. The function
\Phi_{\log}

is a (relaxed) Young function
[
35
, Rem. 4.2.10, and Example 4.1.3 (ii)]
.

Proposition 3.6

(cf.
[
43
, Thm. 3.1]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{d}\mu=1
.
Further, let
\Phi^{\ast}
be the Fenchel–Legendre transform of a convex function
\Phi:I\to\mathbb{R}
,
I\subset\mathbb{R}
. If

f\in\mathcal{L}^{\Phi}(\Omega)
and
g\in\mathcal{L}^{\Phi^{\ast}}(\Omega)
,
then

\int_{\Omega}f(x)g(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq 2\|f\|_{\Phi}\|g\|_{\Phi^{\ast}}.

(19)

Proposition 3.7

(cf.
[
35
, Sec. 4.4, Thm. 4.11.1]
)
.

If
\Phi_{\log}
is given as in
Remark

3.5
, then the space
L{\log}L(\Omega)=\mathcal{L}^{\Phi_{\log}}(\Omega)
is separable.

Remark 3.8
.

The space
L_{\exp}(\Omega)
is not separable
[

, Example 4.4.5, Remark 4.11.2]
, and can be identified with the dual space of

[

), discuss boundedness, asymptotic properties and monotonicity (

,
(p15)
and
(p14)

\ast

4.1
Motivation of approximation

For arbitrary
u\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{\Omega})
, the following expression is equivalent to the
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
-norm:

\chi(u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\Omega)}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\mathrm{d}\pi(x_{1},x_{2}).

(20)

It can be verified that for
u\in\mathcal{C}(\overline{\Omega})
, the supremum is attained for the Dirac measure at
(\hat{x}_{1},\hat{x}_{2})
, where
(\hat{x}_{1},\hat{x}_{2})\in\Omega\times\Omega
denotes a point for which the difference
u(\hat{x}_{1})-u(\hat{x}_{2})\geq u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})
for all
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega\times\Omega
. Hence,
u(\hat{x}_{1})-u(\hat{x}_{2})=\max_{x,y\in\Omega}|u(x)-u(y)|
. Changing
u
by additive constants does not change the value of this term. For that reason, we add an additional
L^{2}
-norm.

Due to the fact that

\begin{multlined}\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp(\gamma^{-1}u(x))\mathrm{d}x\right)+\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp({-\gamma^{-1}u(x)})\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
=\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp(\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})))\mathrm{d}(x_{1},x_{2})\right),\end{multlined}

(21)

and
Lemma

4.3
below, we know that

\chi_{\gamma}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp({\gamma^{-1}u(x)})\mathrm{d}x\right)+\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp({-\gamma^{-1}u(x)})\mathrm{d}x\right),

see (
2
), is obtained by considering an entropy regularized variant, similar to
[
16
]
, given by

\chi_{\gamma}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in L\log{}L(\Omega\times\Omega):~B(\pi)<\infty,\\
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x-\gamma B(\pi)\right),

(22)

where
\gamma>0
and

B:L\log L(\Omega\times\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

B(\pi)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\ln(\pi(x_{1},x_{2}))\mathrm{d}x\quad&\text{if }\pi(x_{1},x_{2})>0\text{ a.e.},\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

is a barrier for the constraint
\pi\geq 0
. The constraint
B(\pi)<\infty
is needed for unbounded
u
in order to avoid settings where the first summand is
+\infty
and the second summand is
-\infty
.

While (
20
) is only defined for continuous functions, (
22
) is defined for all
u\in L_{\exp}(\Omega)
(though not necessarily finite). Moreover, we can use several results from the literature, e.g. the results derived in
[
38
, Example 6.20]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The mapping
\chi_{\gamma}:L_{\exp}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly convex.

Proof 4.2
.

Follows from the strict convexity of the mapping
u\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
and the convexity of

u\mapsto\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in L\log{}L(\Omega\times\Omega):~B(\pi)<\infty,\\
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x-\gamma B(\pi)

due to the convexity of the pointwise supremum of convex functions, see
[
45
, Thm. 2.1.3]
(and using the fact that the first summand is linear in
u
and
\gamma B(\pi)
does not depend on
u
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\gamma>0
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, be a bounded domain and
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function such that
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp({\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))})\mathrm{d}x<\infty
. Then the supremum

\bar{F}\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in L\log{}L(\Omega\times\Omega):~B(\pi)<\infty,\\
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x=1\end{subarray}}F(\pi)

(23)

with

F(\pi)\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x-\gamma B(\pi)

is given by

\bar{F}=F(\bar{\pi})=\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp({\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))})\mathrm{d}x\right).

Moreover, (
23
) is
\infty
whenever
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp({\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))})\mathrm{d}x
is not finite.

Proof 4.4
.

For bounded
u
, the proof is given in
[
11
, Prop. 2.2]
. The proof of
[
11
, Prop. 2.3]
in
[
11
, C.3, p.527ff]
implies the above result. For the sake of completeness, we present the main arguments. In case of
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp(\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\mathrm{d}x=\infty
, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, one considers
f_{N}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,
f_{N}((x_{1},x_{2})):=\min(N,\frac{1}{\gamma}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})))
. Since
f_{N}
is bounded from above,

\pi_{N}:=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\exp(f_{N}),\quad Z_{N}:=\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp(f_{N}(x))\mathrm{d}x

fulfills
B(\pi_{N})<\infty
, and
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\pi_{N}(x)\mathrm{d}x=1.

By Fatou’s lemma, we further know
\lim_{N\to\infty}Z_{N}=\infty
. Hence, using that
f_{N}\leq\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))
,

\begin{multlined}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in L\log{}L(\Omega\times\Omega):~B(\pi)<\infty,\\
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x-\gamma B(\pi)\right)\\
\begin{aligned} &\geq\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\pi_{N}(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x-\gamma B(\pi_{N})\right)\\
&=\gamma\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}(\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))-f_{N})\frac{\exp(f_{N})}{Z_{N}}\mathrm{d}x+\gamma\ln(Z_{N})\geq\gamma\ln(Z_{N}),\end{aligned}\end{multlined}

which diverges to
\infty
as
N\to\infty
. If
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp(\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\mathrm{d}x<\infty
, taking the limit and using Fatou’s lemma implies that

\begin{multlined}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in L\log{}L(\Omega\times\Omega):~B(\pi)<\infty,\\
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x-\gamma B(\pi)\right)\\
\geq\gamma\ln(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp(\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\mathrm{d}x).\end{multlined}

In order to show equality, it remains to prove that

B(\pi)\geq\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))\pi(x)\mathrm{d}x-\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp(\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})))\mathrm{d}x\right)

(24)

for all
\pi\in L{\log}L(\Omega)
such that
B(\pi)<\infty
and
\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\pi(x_{1},x_{2})\mathrm{d}x)=1
. To shorten notation, define
k:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,
k(x_{1},x_{2}):=\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))
. For
M\in\mathbb{N}
, let
F_{M}:\Omega\times\Omega\to[-M,M]
be defined by

F_{M}(x):=\min(\max(k(x),-M),M).

Due to boundedness of
F_{M}
, by the Donsker–Varadhan variational formula
[

, Lem. 2.4(a)]
,

B(\pi)\geq\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}F_{M}(x)\pi(x)\mathrm{d}x-\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp(F_{M}(x))\mathrm{d}x\right).

Analogously to

4.2
Boundedness, asymptotic properties, and monotonicity

The following result collects asymptotic properties of the functional

E(u)\coloneqq\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp(\gamma^{-1}u(x))\mathrm{d}x\right).

as defined over the measurable functions of a bounded domain
\Omega
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, be a bounded domain.
Then for all
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

E(u)\to\mathrm{ess~sup}_{\Omega}(u)\quad\text{as}\quad\gamma\to 0^{+},\quad\text{and}\quad E(u)\to\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{as}\quad\gamma\to\infty,

(25)

and
for all
\gamma>0
,

\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq E(u)\leq\mathrm{ess~sup}_{\Omega}(u).

Proof 4.6
.

Since
\Omega
is bounded,
\mu
defined by

\mathrm{d}\mu\coloneqq(1/|\Omega|)\mathrm{d}x
(cf.
Remark

3.1
)
is a probability measure on
\Omega
.
Hence
u\mapsto E(u)

defined on the space of measurable mappings on
\Omega

equals the entropic risk measure

u\mapsto\gamma\ln\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(\gamma^{-1}u)]

defined on the space of measurable mappings on
\Omega
equipped with
\mu
,
where
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}
is the expectation with respect to
\mu
.
The results now follow from Example 6.20 in
[
38
]
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
u\in L_{\exp}(\Omega)
and
\gamma_{0}\geq\gamma>0
, then we obtain
\chi_{\gamma}(u)\geq\chi_{\gamma_{0}}(u)
.

Proof 4.8
.

It holds with Jensen’s inequality (the mapping
[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,
x\mapsto x^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma_{0}}}
is concave) and due to the monotonicity of the logarithm that

\displaystyle\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp(\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})))\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\geq\ln\left(\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp({\gamma^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}))})^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma_{0}}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\gamma_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\exp(\gamma_{0}^{-1}(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})))\mathrm{d}x\right),

from which the claim follows.

Remark 4.9
.

For fixed
\gamma>0
, the space of functions for which
\ln(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp(\gamma^{-1}u(x))\mathrm{d}x)
is bounded is larger than

L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Consider, e.g.,
u:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R},x\mapsto\ln(x^{-\lambda})
for
\lambda>0
such that
\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}<1
.
We have
u\notin L^{\infty}((0,1))
. However,
\int_{0}^{1}\exp(\frac{1}{\gamma}u(x))\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{1}\exp(\frac{1}{\gamma}\ln(x^{-\lambda}))\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{1}x^{\frac{-\lambda}{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\lambda}
. Therefore,
\gamma\ln(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp(\frac{1}{\gamma}u(x))\mathrm{d}x)
is bounded.

4.3
Sequential weak
∗
lower semicontinuity

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, be a bounded domain, and
\gamma>0
.
Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
be such that
(\chi_{\gamma}(u_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. Then
(\|u_{n}\|_{L_{\exp}(\Omega)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded.

Proof 4.11
.

Due to the properties of the norm, it holds

\|u_{n}\|_{L_{\exp}(\Omega)}=\gamma\|\gamma^{-1}u_{n}\|_{L_{\exp}(\Omega)}.

(26)

Hence, due to
Equation

16
it suffices to show that
\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\exp}(|\frac{1}{\gamma}u_{n}|)\mathrm{d}x
is bounded.
We first show that for
c\coloneqq-\gamma\ln(2)
it holds that

\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp({\gamma^{-1}u_{n}(x)})\mathrm{d}x\right)+\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp({-\gamma^{-1}u_{n}(x)})\mathrm{d}x\right)\geq c.

We consider
\Lambda_{n}\coloneqq\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega\times\Omega\,:\,u_{n}(x_{1})\geq u_{n}(x_{2})\}
with
|\Lambda_{n}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\Omega|^{2}
. Then due to (
21
), monotonicity of
\ln
and by definition of
\Lambda_{n}
,

\begin{multlined}\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp({\gamma^{-1}u_{n}(x)})\mathrm{d}x\right)+\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp({-\gamma^{-1}u_{n}(x)})\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
\geq\gamma\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|^{2}}\int_{\Lambda_{n}}\exp\left({\gamma^{-1}(u_{n}(x_{1})-u_{n}(x_{2}))}\right)\mathrm{d}(x_{1},x_{2})\right)\geq\gamma\ln\left(\frac{|\Lambda_{n}|}{|\Omega|^{2}}\right)\geq-\gamma\ln(2)=c.\end{multlined}

(27)

Therefore, boundedness of
(\chi_{\gamma}(u_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
yields a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{1}{2}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C-c

(28)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Next, we observe that, due to the definition of
\Phi_{\exp}
,

\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\exp}(|\gamma^{-1}u_{n}|)\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}|\gamma^{-1}u_{n}|\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\exp({|\gamma^{-1}u_{n}(x)|-1})\mathrm{d}x

(29)

By (
28

Lemma 4.14

[

Remark 4.16

Remark 5.1

(cf.
[

For a separable Banach space

Conclusion

We have considered the image registration problem (
1
) and a sequence of relaxed optimization problems that approximate the solution of (
1

Appendix B
Predual of
\scriptstyle W^{1,\exp}_{0}(\Omega)

We define

W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{f_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}f_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega):~f_{i}\in L\log{}L(\Omega)~\forall i\in\{0,\ldots,d\}\}

as a subspace of the space of distributions
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
on
\Omega
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{d}\|f_{i}\|_{L\log{}L(\Omega)}\,:\,f_{i}\in L\log{}L(\Omega)~\forall i\in\{0,\ldots,d\},~f=f_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}f_{i}\right\}.

By definition, there exists a bounded linear surjection

\iota:L\log{}L(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\to W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega),\quad\iota((f_{0},\ldots,f_{d})^{\top})\coloneqq f_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}f_{i}.

Moreover, there exists a bounded linear right inverse

\iota^{-1}:W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)\to L\log{}L(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d+1})

such that
\iota\circ\iota^{-1}:W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)\to W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)
is the identity on
W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)
.
We consider the standard distributional pairing
\langle\cdot\rangle:~\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\times C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
. In the following we show that this pairing can be extended to a pairing that maps from
W^{1,\exp}(\Omega)\times W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)
to
\mathbb{R}
, which is given as

\langle\!\langle\phi,f\rangle\!\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)(\iota^{-1}(f))_{0}(x)\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{i}}\phi(x)(\iota^{-1}(f))_{i}(x)\mathrm{d}x.

(58)

Moreover, we show that the dual space of
W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)
can be identified with
W^{1,\exp}(\Omega)
.

We first consider well-definedness of (
58
) and show that
W^{-1,L\log{}L(\Omega)}
is a subspace of the pre-dual of
W^{1,\exp}(\Omega)
:
Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
L\log{}L(\Omega)
, by its definition,
\iota(C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega))
is dense in
W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)
(and therefore
W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)
is also separable). Let
\phi\in W^{1,\exp}(\Omega)
be arbitrary but fixed. Further, let

, then there exist
f_{0},\ldots,f_{d}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
such that
f=\iota((f_{0},\ldots,f_{d})^{\top})
and the pairing
\langle\!\langle\phi,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle
is well-defined, bounded and linear. Integration by parts further shows that (
58
) is consistent with the distributional pairing. Continuity arguments yield well-definedness, boundedness and linearity of
\langle\!\langle\phi,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle:~W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
.
Hence,
W^{1,\exp}(\Omega)
can be identified with a subset of

It remains to show that the dual space of
W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega)
can be identified with a subspace of
W^{1,\exp}(\Omega)
. Let
\phi\in(W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega))^{\ast}
be arbitrary but fixed.
By definition of the adjoint operator
\iota^{\ast}:(W^{-1,L\log{}L}(\Omega))^{\ast}\to(L\log{}L(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d+1})^{\ast}

\langle\!\langle\phi,f\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\phi,f\rangle=\langle\phi,\iota\circ\iota^{-1}f\rangle=\langle\iota^{\ast}\phi,\iota^{-1}f\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\int_{\Omega}(\iota^{\ast}\phi)_{i}(x)(\iota^{-1}f)_{i}(x)\mathrm{d}x,

Since the linear operators coincide on a dense subspace in

, choosing

Probing Supernova Neutrino Boosted Dark Matter with Collective Excitation
Jun-Wei Sun, Lei Wu, Yan-Hao Xu, Bin Zhu

Abstract
We explore the supernova neutrino-boosted dark matter (SN ν \nu BDM) and its direct detection. During core-collapse supernovae, an abundance of neutrinos are emitted. These supernova neutrinos can transfer their kinetic energy to the light dark matter via their interactions, and thus are detected in the neutrino and dark matter experiments. Due to the exponential suppression of the high-energy neutrino flux, the kinetic energy carried by a large portion of SN ν \nu BDM falls within the MeV range. This could potentially produce the collective excitation signals in the semiconductor detectors with the skipper-CCD. We calculate the plasmon excitation rate induced by SN ν \nu BDM and derive the exclusion limits. In contrast with conventional neutrino and dark matter direct detection experiments, our results present a significant enhancement in sensitivity for the sub-MeV dark matter.

This section investigates the potential of plasmon resonance excitation induced by SN
\nu

\displaystyle\frac{dR}{dT_{\chi}dQdE_{e}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\sigma}_{\chi e}\frac{N_{\mathrm{cell}}V_{\mathrm{cell}}}{8\pi^{2}\alpha\mu_{\chi e}^{2}}\frac{((2E_{\chi}-E_{e})^{2}-Q^{2})Q^{3}}{4p_{\chi}^{2}}

(25)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}m_{e}^{2}+m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}{Q^{2}-E_{e}^{2}+m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{Im}\left[\frac{-1}{\epsilon(Q,E_{e})}\right]\frac{d\phi_{\chi}}{dT_{\chi}},

where we consider the detector to be isotropic, allowing the transfer momentum
\mathbf{Q}
to be reduced to its magnitude

\sqrt{(p_{\chi}+Q)^{2}+m_{\chi}^{2}}\geq E_{\chi}-E_{e}\geq\sqrt{(p_{\chi}-Q)^{2}+m_{\chi}^{2}}.

26

and

(27)

The minimum kinetic energy

can be derived using the kinematic limits in the relativistic case

T_{\chi}^{\min}=-m_{\chi}+\frac{E_{e}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{(2m_{e}+E_{e})(2m_{\chi}^{2}+m_{e}E_{e})}{m_{e}}}.

VI
Conclusions

In this study, we investigate the phenomenon of SN
\nu
BDM and its implications for dark matter detection. We demonstrate that supernova neutrinos, emitted in copious amounts during core-collapse events, can transfer kinetic energy to dark matter particles, significantly enhancing their sensitivity. We derive an analytical expression for the spatial probability distribution of supernova neutrinos across various galactic locations, which is essential for considering dark matter-neutrino interactions. This aligns with existing literature on the limit of a single explosion and provides a comprehensive understanding of neutrino flux in the Milky Way. By calculating the resulting dark matter flux from supernova neutrinos, we establish a direct connection between neutrino interactions and dark matter detection and provide the inhomogeneous properties in angular distribution. Furthermore, we compute the plasmon excitation rate associated with SN
\nu
BDM, showing significant enhancements over traditional detection methods by approximately three orders, such as those used by Super-Kamiokande
Super-Kamiokande:2023jbt
and XENON1T
XENON:2019gfn
. The unique energy transfer mechanisms involved lead to a heightened sensitivity, particularly suitable for the solid-state detectors.

A pan-cancer landscape of LILRB4 identifies it as a context-dependent marker of the myeloid and antigen-presentation axis
Authors: Jiabin Li, Xuhui He, Yueyue Guo, Longsheng Zhang, Waiming Cheng, Chaoshun Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•This study provides the first integrated pan-cancer multi-omics landscape of LILRB4 across expression, genetics, methylation, phosphorylation, and immune infiltration.•LILRB4 shows immune-cell enrichment and cancer-type-specific transcript–protein discordance across tumors.•Prognostic significance of LILRB4 varies by cancer type, showing protective or risk-associated patterns.•LILRB4 strongly correlates with macrophage infiltration and antigen-presentation networks converging on HLA-DRA.•Findings support LILRB4 as a biomarker for tumor microenvironment stratification and potential immunoregulatory targeting.

Introduction

Inhibitory receptors contribute to myeloid regulatory pathways within the tumor microenvironment, influencing antigen recognition and immune modulation [[1], [2], [3]]. LILRB4, also known as ILT3, is an inhibitory receptor bearing immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motifs (ITIMs) [4] and is predominantly expressed in monocytes [5], macrophages [[6], [7], [8]], and dendritic cells [9,10]. Through SHP-1/2-mediated signaling [11,12], it attenuates cellular activation [13,14] and modulates antigen presentation [14,15] as well as T-cell activation thresholds [16,17]. Although its role in innate immune regulation has attracted increasing attention, its cross-tumor landscape and clinical relevance have not been systematically characterized.

Prior studies have primarily examined LILRB4 within individual cancer types or single-omics contexts, with relatively limited cross-layer comparisons integrating transcripts, proteins, genetic alterations, epigenetic regulation, and immune infiltration [[18], [19], [20]]. The associations between LILRB4 and genomic instability metrics have not been comprehensively evaluated; the biological implications of site-specific phosphorylation and promoter methylation across cancers remain to be clarified; and discrepancies between transcript and protein abundance warrant further systematic assessment. Therefore, a systematic pan-cancer investigation integrating multi-omics layers may provide a more comprehensive framework for understanding the molecular and immunologic context of LILRB4 across tumors.

To address these gaps, we integrated public multi-omics resources to generate a pan-cancer profile of LILRB4, encompassing transcript and protein abundance, alteration spectra and mutation distribution, promoter methylation, and site-specific phosphorylation. Using multi-algorithm immune deconvolution together with co-expression and interaction network analyses, we assessed its associations with macrophage infiltration and antigen-processing and presentation pathways, and examined correlations with genomic instability metrics across cancers.

Our analysis identifies heterogeneous upregulation of LILRB4 across cancers with cancer-type-dependent prognostic associations. Patterns of promoter methylation and site-specific phosphorylation variation, together with consistent correlations with myeloid infiltration and antigen-processing pathways, support its characterization as a context-dependent marker within the tumor microenvironment.

Methods

Study design and overall workflow

We conducted a retrospective pan-cancer analysis based on public multi-omics resources, building a cross-layer comparison framework around LILRB4 (tissue/cell expression, cross-cancer differences, clinical outcomes, genetic alterations, promoter methylation, site-specific phosphorylation, and immune infiltration). Co-expression and protein-interaction data were integrated for functional clues and enrichment analysis. The overall analytical workflow of the study is summarized in Supplementary Fig. S4. All databases, analytical platforms, and online resources used in this study are summarized in Supplementary Table S1, including their access URLs, versions (where available), and access dates.

Gene and phylogeny information

Nucleotide and protein sequences of LILRB4 were retrieved from NCBI. Ensembl GeneTree/homology modules were used for phylogeny and paralog identification to contextualize family attributes and evolutionary background.

Tissue and cell-type expression

Using the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) Consensus dataset, we profiled LILRB4 expression in normal tissues; HPA single-cell modules were used to assess enrichment in key immune cells (monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells). Expression units follow platform-provided normalization.

Cross-cancer expression with supplemented normal controls

We compared tumors versus paired/unpaired normals in TCGA via TIMER2.0. For cancer types lacking adequate TCGA normals, GEPIA2’s TCGA+GTEx pipeline supplemented controls. Within-cancer normalization and statistics followed platform defaults. Stage-wise expression comparisons were conducted using GEPIA2 staging modules with default settings.

Protein and phosphoprotein expression

Tumor–normal differences in total protein and site-level phosphorylation were assessed using CPTAC resources. Total protein differences served as reference to transcript-level trends. Site-level phosphorylation comparisons across cancers focused on S318, S319, S377, S417, T386, reporting direction and significance with emphasis on site- and cancer-specific remodeling.

Survival analysis

Overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) were evaluated with GEPIA/GEPIA2 Kaplan–Meier and log-rank modules, using default expression stratification. Concordant or opposite trends between OS and DFS were reported to highlight cancer-type dependence.

Genetic alteration analysis

We used cBioPortal (TCGA) to summarize LILRB4 alteration frequencies, mutation types, and site distribution; lollipop/domain maps located mutations, and copy-number gains/losses were tallied. “Alterations” refer to somatic mutations and copy-number changes, excluding germline variants. Survival differences by alteration status used cBioPortal survival modules.

Genomic instability associations

TMB, MSI, and HRD metrics were obtained through Sangerbox per its standard pipeline. Within cancers, correlations between LILRB4 expression and each metric used built-in correlation modules; directions and consistent patterns are reported.

Promoter methylation analysis

From TCGA methylation arrays, we pinpointed representative CpG probes (cg02421734, cg04680738, cg05329879, cg05922591, cg12161905), focusing on cg05329879 near the 5′UTR–first-exon junction with high variability. We compared distributions (e.g., in LGG) and conducted survival and stratified analyses via MethSurv defaults.

Immune infiltration estimation

Macrophage M0/M1/M2 infiltration scores were obtained from TIMER2.0’s multi-algorithm deconvolution framework (CIBERSORT, CIBERSORT-ABS, MCPCOUNTER, EPIC, xCell, TIMER, quanTIseq, TIDE) using platform default settings. Where applicable, tumor purity–adjusted correlation estimates provided by TIMER2.0 were referenced to minimize confounding by tumor cellular composition. Cross-cancer heatmaps and representative cancer details were based on Spearman correlation outputs generated by TIMER2.0.

Co-expression, protein interactions, and functional enrichment

Top LILRB4 co-expressed genes were taken from GEPIA2; experimentally supported interactors were taken from STRING (evidence channel: “experiments”). The intersection identified core genes (HLA-DRA). KEGG and GO:MF enrichment on combined sets highlighted immune-related pathways.

Statistics and reproducibility

All statistics (differential tests, correlations, survival, enrichment) were reported directly from platform defaults (GEPIA2, TIMER2.0, UALCAN/CPTAC, cBioPortal, MethSurv, STRING). We did not reimplement algorithms locally. Significance thresholds and corrections follow platform outputs/figure legends. Figures were assembled in R using tidyverse, readr, reshape2, ggplot2, ggforce, RColorBrewer, ComplexHeatmap, circlize, VennDiagram, tidygraph/ggraph, grid, gridExtra, cowplot, and gtable.

Results

LILRB4 is highly expressed in the immune system and heterogeneously upregulated across cancers

LILRB4 shows high expression across normal immune systems. It resides within the immune-regulatory-rich region 19p13.1 (Fig. S1a). Phylogeny places it within a primate-expanded immune-receptor family with paralogs including NCR1, FCAR, and GP6, suggesting origin from an amniote-stage duplication (Fig. S1b). In the HPA Consensus dataset, it is high in spleen, lung, lymph nodes, and spinal cord (Fig. S1c); single-cell resolution further indicates enrichment in monocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells (Fig. S1d).

Across tumors, LILRB4 is heterogeneously upregulated. TCGA shows significant increases in BRCA, ESCA, GBM, HNSC, KICH, KIRC, LIHC, PRAD, STAD, THCA, and UCEC; in SKCM, expression is closely related to metastatic status (Fig. 1a). After integrating GTEx to supplement normals, significant upregulation remains in DLBC, LAML, LGG, OV, SKCM, and TGCT, with downtrends in ACC and THYM (Fig. 1b). At the protein level (CPTAC), LILRB4 is higher in BRCA, KIRC, UCEC, LUSC, LUAD, HNSC, and GBM, but lower in LIHC (Fig. 1c). Stage analysis shows significant differences in KICH (F = 4.67, P = 0.00525), SKCM (F = 8.44, P = 1.52 × 10-6), OV (F = 3.30, P = 0.0377), BLCA (F = 4.91, P = 0.00779), STAD (F = 2.84, P = 0.0376), THCA (F = 4.31, P = 0.00512), and ESCA (F = 2.95, P = 0.0341), with no clear stage dependence elsewhere (Fig. 1d; Fig. S2). LILRB4 expression associates with patient outcome in a cancer-type-specific manner

In TCGA, LILRB4 shows cancer-dependent survival associations. GEPIA Kaplan-Meier analyses (Fig. 2a-b) show high expression linked to longer OS in CESC (HR = 0.55, P = 0.014), SKCM (HR = 0.58, P = 7.2e-05), and UCS (HR = 0.49, P = 0.037), but the opposite in LGG (HR = 1.7, P = 0.0038). DFS similarly differs: LGG (HR = 1.7, P = 0.0011) and PRAD (HR = 2.2, P = 0.00059) indicate higher recurrence risk with high expression, whereas UCS (HR = 0.46, P = 0.035) indicates longer DFS. To further evaluate whether LILRB4 is independently associated with prognosis in LGG, multivariate Cox regression was performed using overall survival as the endpoint and adjusting for age, gender, and histological grade. In univariate analysis, LILRB4 expression was associated with worse OS (HR = 1.156, 95% CI 1.012-1.321, P = 0.032). After multivariate adjustment, LILRB4 remained significantly associated with OS (HR = 1.275, 95% CI 1.118-1.455, P = 2.99 × 10-4), supporting its independent prognostic association in LGG (Fig. S3).

LILRB4 alterations are dominated by amplification and missense, cluster in Ig domains, and associate with TMB/MSI/HRD

Alteration frequencies of LILRB4 vary across cancer types, ranging from approximately 0.5% to 11% (Fig. 3a). The highest frequency is observed in SKCM (∼11%), whereas UCEC, GBM, and BLCA exhibit alteration rates around 3-5%, and most other tumor types demonstrate frequencies below 3%. Amplifications and missense mutations represent the predominant alteration categories. Missense variants cluster within the second and third immunoglobulin domains, including a recurrent P184 hotspot (P184H/L) identified across several cancers (Fig. 3b). However, given the relatively low overall mutation frequency and the absence of consistent survival differences among alteration carriers, detailed structural or functional evaluation of this hotspot was beyond the scope of the present landscape analysis. LILRB4 expression shows positive correlations with genomic instability metrics in a cancer-type-dependent manner. TMB correlates with LILRB4 expression in COAD, READ, and PRAD (r > 0.2, P < 0.05; Fig. 3g); MSI shows positive trends in STES and COAD (Fig. 3h); and HRD correlates in SARC, PRAD, and KICH (Fig. 3i). These expression–instability associations contrast with the absence of significant survival differences among P184H/L alteration carriers, suggesting that expression patterns may reflect broader genomic contexts rather than the impact of a single recurrent mutation (Fig. 3g-i).

Discussion

LILRB4 is highly enriched in normal immune tissues [21,22] and shows heterogeneous, cross-platform upregulation in multiple cancers, with cancer-type-dependent associations with clinical outcome—protective in some contexts, but associated with higher risk in LGG and recurrence-related metrics in PRAD. Observed discordance between protein and transcript levels (e.g., in LIHC) may reflect differences in cellular composition and post-transcriptional or post-translational regulation [23].

Convergent immunologic evidence positions LILRB4 as a context-dependent marker along the myeloid/antigen-presentation axis [14,24]: its expression correlates consistently with macrophage infiltration across cancers and algorithms, and co-expression/interaction analyses converge on antigen-processing and presentation pathways, with HLA-DRA [25] appearing as a shared network node. Importantly, this association reflects network-level convergence rather than experimentally validated direct regulation.

At the genomic level, alterations are dominated by amplification and missense mutations clustering within immunoglobulin domains, including a recurrent hotspot, yet alteration carriers do not demonstrate consistent survival differences. In contrast, LILRB4 expression shows positive associations with genomic instability metrics (TMB/MSI/HRD) in selected cancers, suggesting that expression patterns may reflect broader genomic contexts rather than being attributable to a single recurrent alteration. Epigenetic and post-translational variations further illustrate cancer-type-dependent heterogeneity. In LGG, promoter-proximal hypermethylation is associated with longer survival and varies with grade, while across cancers, phosphorylation exhibits site- and context-specific remodeling. However, the structural consequences of recurrent missense variants and the regulatory effects of specific CpG sites were not experimentally assessed in this cross-platform landscape analysis. Future studies incorporating structural modeling, methylation perturbation, and mechanistic assays will be necessary to clarify their biological impact.

Limitations include potential overestimation of single-gene/immune-score correlations due to deconvolution gene-set composition, purity/tissue differences influencing expression contrasts, batch/site effects in cross-platform integration, and the observational nature precluding causal inference. Follow-up should include single-cell/spatial validation under purity control, functional perturbation of the HLA-DRA axis and site-directed mutagenesis to dissect antigen-presentation roles, and systematic investigation of transcript-protein discordance.

Conclusion

LILRB4 is highly enriched in the immune system and heterogeneously upregulated across cancers, with cancer-dependent prognostic associations—protective in CESC, SKCM, and UCS, but associated with higher risk in LGG and recurrence-related metrics of PRAD. Genetically, amplifications and missense mutations predominate, clustering within immunoglobulin domains and including the P184H/L hotspot, without consistent survival impact; expression correlates with TMB, MSI, and HRD in selected cancers. Epigenetically and at the protein-modification level, hypermethylation at promoter-proximal cg05329879 in LGG associates with longer survival, and multiple cancers exhibit site- and cancer-specific phosphorylation differences. In the tumor microenvironment, LILRB4 expression correlates strongly with macrophage infiltration, and co-expression and interaction analyses are enriched for antigen-processing and presentation pathways, highlighting HLA-DRA. Collectively, these findings support the characterization of LILRB4 as a context-dependent marker of the myeloid and antigen-presentation axis rather than a universally prognostic gene driven by a single recurrent alteration.

Quantum Resource Theory of Deficiency and Operational Applications in Subchannel Discrimination
Sunho Kim, Chunhe Xiong, Junde Wu

Abstract
A central challenge in quantum resource theory is to provide operational meaning to quantum resources that offer distinct advantages over the convex set of resource-free states in specific physical tasks. We propose a novel framework to define the resource deficiency of a given state relative to the set of maximal resource states in physical tasks. The proposed geometric measure satisfies this framework’s conditions for both quantum coherence and entanglement, and it precisely quantifies the minimal disadvantage of a given state compared to maximal resource states in subchannel discrimination under certain conditions. These extensions and new interpretations broaden the scope of quantum resource theories and provide more comprehensive operational interpretations.

Quantum resource theory of deficiency for the maximal resource states.–

In this work, we focus our investigation on quantum states as the fundamental medium for studying resource-theoretic frameworks. Let

We first discuss quantum resource theory from a relative perspective, focusing on quantifying the quantum inferiority to the maximal resource state rather than the quantum superiority to the free state of the quantum resource theory, which is the basic framework of the existing resource theory. In theory, quantum states with maximal resources can be used as the primary material that provides the greatest efficiency in various quantum communication and computational task, which has also been confirmed by several experiments. For example, to evaluate the operational efficiency of quantum teleportation, it is common to use the fidelity with the maximal entangled (Bell) state
Bennett2
;
Horodecki3
;
Horodecki4
;
Bouwmeester

Moreover, it is not always guaranteed that the quantum states we have prepared for various missions are maximal resource states. Therefore, it can be very useful in practice to evaluate how inefficient a prepared quantum state is compared with maximal resource states. Additionally, considering that for most quantum resources such as entanglement, coherence, and magic, the set of maximal resource states is the set of pure states corresponding to the boundary of the convex whole set (which does not imply that all pure states are maximal resources), it is advantageous to propose a framework that inversely applies existing resource theory to assess and measure the deficiency of a given resource state. The reason is clarified by an example of a deficiency presented later.
From this, we present a new framework for measuring the degree of resource deficiency through conditions contrary to the fundamental properties of quantum resources in the existing quantum resource theory. The resource deficiency for maximal resource states is defined by a function

These three conditions mean respectively that as the measured value increases, the deficiency compared to the maximal resource states also increases (from D1), and the convexity of the resource is consistent with the concavity of the deficiency (from D3). The aspect requiring careful consideration is the second monotonicity condition. Contrary to free states, the set of maximal resource states is non-convex and typically comprises only pure states. In conventional quantum resource theories, resource monotonicity originates from the closedness of free states under free operations. However, due to the non-convexity of maximal resource states, we cannot ensure that resource monotonicity necessarily implies deficiency monotonicity for arbitrary quantum states. To illustrate with coherence as an example: Quantum coherence is typically quantified by specific functions of the absolute values of off-diagonal elements in the density matrix with respect to the reference basis. These measures include the
l_{1}
-norm measure
Baumgratz
, robustness measure
Napoli
, relative entropy measure
Winter
, and geometric measure
Streltsov2
of coherence. This means states with the same absolute values of off-diagonal elements possess equal coherence. However, the pure-state nature of maximal resource states implies that even when two quantum states have equal absolute values of off-diagonal elements, their resource deficiency may differ due to relative phase differences. This phase sensitivity suggests that monotonicity might not hold for certain mixed states. However, for pure states, the phase dependence cancels out precisely because maximal resource states are also pure - this implies that the deficiency is unaffected by phase differences. This fundamental property provides non-decreasing monotonicity for pure states, which corresponds to conventional quantum resource theory. We therefore propose to classify monotonicity into two categories: fundamental monotonicity (D2) applicable to pure states, and universal monotonicity (U-D2) valid for all quantum states. In this work, we derive our results by considering deficiency that fulfill three conditions (D1, D2, and D3), including the fundamental monotonicity.

Moreover, the fact that maximal resource sets consist only of pure states implies that certain measures - including robustness-based measures - fail to satisfy the requirements of this framework for quantifying relative deficiency. This occurs because pure states cannot be obtained through any convex combination of quantum states. We therefore propose using the geometric measure to quantify resource deficiency in this context.

Operational disadvantage for maximal resources in subchannel discrimination.–

Our ultimate goal is to establish an indicator for the operational disadvantages of quantum states in subchannel discrimination and to relate this indicator to resource deficiency. As a first step, we refine the advantage indicator proposed in
Takagi
to more reasonably capture operational disadvantages.
In previous work, the relative advantage of quantum states in subchannel discrimination was quantified using the maximal ratio of success probabilities across all possible strategies, as shown in equation (1). However, this approach has a limitation: while it maximizes the success probability ratio, it does not guarantee sufficiently high absolute success probabilities for the quantum states involved.
To address this issue, we introduce an improved indicator for operational disadvantages. Rather than considering all possible strategies, we compute the relative ratio only over the strategies that achieve the maximal success probability for the maximal resource states. This ensures a more meaningful measure of disadvantage while maintaining high success probabilities.
That is

\max_{\sigma\in\overline{\mathcal{R}^{\max}}}\min_{\Omega_{\sigma}}\frac{P_{succ}(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\},\rho)}{P_{succ}(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\},\sigma)}

(4)

where
\Omega_{\sigma}=\big\{(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\})|P_{succ}(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\},\sigma)=1\big\}.

Under the strategy that maximizes the success probability for the maximal resource state (as defined in Eq. (
4
)), the minimum ratio between the success probabilities obtained using two quantum states quantifies the relative disadvantage of a given state
\rho
compared to the maximal resource state
\sigma
. By taking the maximum of these ratios over all possible maximal resource states, we determine the overall operational disadvantage of
\rho
relative to the entire set of maximal resource states.
This indicator exhibits an inverse relationship with operational disadvantage: higher values represent smaller disadvantages, while lower values indicate greater disadvantages. Importantly, by a strategy that provides the value of the disadvantage, we still guarantee the maximum success probability for the maximal resource state that give the maximum ratio.

In the proposed indicator, we exclusively consider strategies that maximize the success probability for maximal resource states. Given that maximal resource states are typically pure states, we establish conditions under which strategies guarantee a maximum success probability of 1. As demonstrated in Appendix B of the Supplemental Material
kim
, there exist infinitely many strategies satisfying these conditions.

Moreover, our results reveal a remarkable connection: the operational disadvantages arising from quantum states in the subchannel discrimination framework can be precisely characterized by a geometric measure of resource deficiency. (See the proof in Appendix C of the Supplemental Material
kim
.)

Theorem 5
.

For any
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
,

\max_{\sigma\in\overline{\mathcal{R}^{\max}}}\min_{\Omega_{\sigma}}\frac{P_{succ}(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\},\rho)}{P_{succ}(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\},\sigma)}=1-\mathrm{D}_{g}(\rho).

(5)

These results demonstrate that for maximal resource set consisting exclusively of pure states, the operational relative disadvantages of arbitrary quantum states in subchannel identification can be quantified through a geometric measure of resource deficiency.
Unlike the robustness measure typically used to quantify operational advantages in closed convex sets, this geometric approach successfully quantifies operational disadvantages even when robustness measures cannot be defined for resource deficiencies.
Furthermore, this result holds for any quantum resource theory where all maximal resource states are pure states. However, due to its dependence on the specific form of maximal resource states, this conclusion cannot be universally extended to arbitrary closed concave sets.

Conclusion.–

In this work, we propose an extended framework for quantum resource theory that generalizes beyond the traditional binary classification of ’free states’ versus ’resource states’ by introducing resource quantification relative to arbitrary sets of resource states. Our primary contribution is the formalization of resource deficiency - a novel metric quantifying the deficiency of a given state relative to maximal resource states.

Within established quantum resource theories and their experimental realizations, maximal resource states represent the gold standard. Consequently, quantifying the deviation of prepared states from these optimal benchmarks is a fundamental challenge. To address this, we develop a principled framework that systematically inverts the axioms of conventional resource theories to measure the deficiency relative to maximal resource sets.

We rigorously prove that our geometric measure satisfies all necessary conditions for being a valid resource deficiency measure for both quantum coherence and entanglement. Furthermore, we establish that this geometric measure precisely characterizes the operational disadvantage in subchannel discrimination tasks when comparing arbitrary states to maximal resource states.

Several promising directions remain for future investigation, including: (1) extending the resourceful utilization of quantum measurements
Skrzypczyk2
from subchannel discrimination to relative resource frameworks, and (2) generalizing these resourcefulness measures to theories permitting multiple measurement paradigms beyond subchannel discrimination.

Appendix B: Maximum probability success in the subchannel discrimination

In the subchannel discrimination,
P_{succ}\big(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\},\rho\big)=1
if and only if the measurement
\{M_{i}\}
satisfies
\Pi_{\Psi_{i}(\rho)}\leq M_{i}
for each
i
, where
\Pi_{\sigma}
is the projection operator over all eigen-states with nonzero eigenvalues for
\sigma
.

Fact 6
.

If
\sigma
is any pure state, then there are countless strategy
(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\})
that satisfy
P_{succ}\big(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\},\sigma\big)=1
.

This is a clear fact and we can give a useful example here. Let
\{\ket{\varphi_{i}}\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{H}
.
Suppose that in a strategy
(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\})
operations
\{\Psi_{i}\}
are defined through a series of unitary operators
U_{i}
that perform
\Psi_{i}(\sigma)=p_{i}U_{i}\sigma U_{i}^{\dagger}=p_{i}|\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{i}|
with a probability distribution
(p_{i})
. At this time, if measurement
M_{i}=|\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{i}|
is performed on the quantum states converted through
\{\Psi_{i}\}
, we have a success probability of
1
regardless of the probability distribution
(p_{i})_{i}
in which the operations
\{\Psi_{i}\}
are performed.

Then, from the definition of
\Omega_{\sigma}
, Eq. (9) in main text can be rewritten as

\small{\max_{\sigma\in\overline{\mathcal{R}^{\max}}}\min_{\Omega_{\sigma}}}P_{succ}(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\},\rho).

(33)

We first consider

for the quantum state
\rho
and for any maximum resource state
\sigma
. Here, all maximum resource states
\sigma
are pure states and can be written as

(\{\Psi_{i}\},\{M_{i}\})

Abstract
. We consider two n × n n\times n non-Hermitian random matrices such that the i ​ j ij th entry of one matrix is correlated with the i ​ j ij th entry of the other matrix. However, the entries of any particular matrix are i.i.d. random variables. We study the asymptotic behavior of the combined spectrum, and the limit of the linear eigenvalue statistic defined on the combined spectrum. We show that if the random variables are centered with variance 1 / n 1/n and having finite moments, then the centered Linear Eigenvalue Statistics (LESs) converge jointly to a bivariate Gaussian distribution. We assumed that the test function used in the LES belongs to Sobolev H 2 + δ H^{2+\delta} space. The variance of the limiting Gaussian distribution depends on correlation structure of the matrix entries and the fourth order mixed cumulants of the matrix entries. This generalizes the previous results by Rider, Silverstein [ 25 ] , Cipolloni, Erdős, Schröder [ 15 , 17 ] . In particular, we obtain the limiting LES of random centrosymmetric matrices. Introduction

In this paper, we focus on analyzing the combined spectrum of two correlated non-Hermitian random matrices. While numerous studies analyzed random matrices with correlated entries
[

,
1
,

,

,
7
,

,

, to the best of our knowledge, there are relatively few works specifically on the joint convergence of correlated random matrices. Joint convergence of sample cross-covariance matrices
[
10
]
, independent random elliptic matrices
[
2
]
, independent random Toeplitz matrices
[
3
]
were studied in the cited articles. In the last three articles, a correlation structure was assumed for intra-matrix elements; however, no correlation structure was considered for inter-matrix elements. The following papers represent more recent advancements in the spectrum of correlated random matrices. One day prior to the release of the first version of this article on arXiv, Kolupaiev
[
23
]
published another article on arXiv that studied the local fluctuations of limiting spectrum of two correlated Hermitian random matrices. In the non-Hermitian context, Bourgade et al.
[
11
]
established the joint convergence of the linear eigenvalue statistics for a time-indexed matrix dynamics at finitely many distinct times.

Before continuing any further, let us introduce the following two notions related to the spectrum of a random matrix. Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of an
n\times n
random matrix
M.
We define the
Empirical Spectral Measure
(ESM) on the Borel sigma algebra of
\mathbb{C}
as

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}(\cdot),

where
\delta_{x}(A)=\mathbf{1}_{\{x\in A\}}.
This is a random probability measure. It is known that when
M
is a random non-Hermitian matrix,
\mu_{n}
converges almost surely in weak topology of measures to the uniform distribution on the unit disc
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}.
In other words, if
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded continuous function, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(\lambda_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\to}}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(z)\;\mathrm{d}^{2}z\;\;\text{as }n\to\infty,

where
\mathrm{d}^{2}z=\mathrm{d}\Re z\;\mathrm{d}\Im z.
This is known as
Circular law

[
20
,
8
,
27
,
24
,
21
]
.

In the above,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(\lambda_{i})=:\operatorname{L}_{n}(f)
is referred as the
Linear Eigenvalue Statistic
(LES) of the matrix
M.
The fluctuations of the above convergence can be analyzed by studying the asymptotic behavior of
a_{n}(\operatorname{L}_{n}(f)-b_{n})
for some sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n},

\{b_{n}\}_{n}
and some test function
f.
Typically,
b_{n}=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{L}_{n}(f)],
but unlike the classical Central Limit Theorem (CLT),
a_{n}
is not
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n}),
primarily because the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
are not independent.

In our case, we have two
n\times n
random non-Hermitian matrices whose entries are correlated with each other. We study the LES of the joint spectrum via a linear combination of the individual LESs as mentioned in (
3.4
). In
[
11
]
, the LES of matrix dynamics of the form
X_{t}:=e^{-t/2}X+\sqrt{1-e^{-t}}\tilde{X}
were studied. Here,
X
is a non-Hermitian matrix with i.i.d. entries, and
\tilde{X}
is an independent Ginibre ensemble. It was shown that the joint LES (at both macroscopic and mesoscopic scales) of
X_{t}
and
X_{s}
for distinct
s
and
t
converge to a bivariate Gaussian distribution. However, our result is not directly comparable. The main contribution of this paper is the Theorem
3.2
, which says that the limiting LES follows a Gaussian distribution. The variance of the limiting Gaussian distribution depends on the fourth mixed cumulant and the correlation structure of the matrix entries. Our proof relies on the methods developed by Erdős et. al
[
15
,
17
]
. However, since in this paper we are analyzing two correlated matrices instead of one, we had to consider the interaction between the matrices in several places such as in Section
4
, where we needed to approximate product of resolvents of correlated matrices. The correlation structure starts to appear extensively in technical calculations from Subsection
6.1
onward. Broadly, the LES is converted into an integral of trace of resolvent by using Girko’s representation formula (
3.8
). Then it is shown that asymptotically the product of the traces of the resolvents at different points decomposes as per the Isserlis’ theorem, which in turn implies that the liming trace of the resolvent is a Gaussian process.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.2
, we also obtain the limiting LES for random Centrosymmetric matrices, which was discussed in our previous work
[
22
]
. This article is organized as follows. The main result and the consequences are stated in Section
3
. The proof of the CLT for LES is provided in Section
5
. However, the key part of the proof relies on establishing the CLT for resolvents, which is presented in Section
6
. Let us begin with introducing the notations, which will be frequently used in this article.

Definition 2.1

(Stochastic domination)
.

Consider families of non-negative random variables,

3.
Main result

Let us consider a
2n\times 2n
ordered block matrix
X
defined as follows;

\displaystyle X=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}(X^{(1)})_{n\times n}&0\\
0&(X^{(2)})_{n\times n}\end{array}\right),

where
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
are i.i.d. random matrices with mean
0
and variance
1/n
for
i=1,2
respectively. The
(i,j)^{th}
entry of
X^{(1)}
is correlated to the
(i,j)^{th}
entry of
X^{(2)}
as follows;

(3.3)

\displaystyle\gamma:=n\operatorname{Cov}\big((X^{(1)})_{(i,j)},(X^{(2)})_{(i,j)}\big)=n\operatorname{Cov}(X_{(i,j)},X_{(n+i,n+j)}),

and assume that
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2n}
are eigenvalues of
X.
Without loss of generality, let us assume that
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are eigenvalues of
X^{(1)}
and
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=n+1}^{2n}
are eigenvalues of
X^{(2)}
.
The limiting ESM of
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
follow the Circular law. Consequently, the limiting ESM of
X
follows the Circular law as well.

For test functions
f
defined on the spectrum of
X
, let us define the linear eigenvalue statistics as

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}(f):=c\mathcal{L}^{(1)}_{n}(f)+d\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{n}(f),

where
c,d\in\mathbb{C}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(1)}(f)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f\left(\sigma_{i}\right)-\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f\left(\sigma_{i}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{n}(f)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n}f\left(\sigma_{i}\right)-\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n}f\left(\sigma_{i}\right).

From
[
15
, Theorem 2.2]
and
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(1)}(f)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}(0,V^{(1)}_{f}),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(f)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}(0,V^{(2)}_{f}),

as
n\to\infty,
where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}
denotes convergence in distribution, and
V_{f}^{(1)},V_{f}^{(2)}
are the variances of the limiting normal distribution, which depend on the test function
f
and cumulants of the matrix entries. However,
\mathcal{L}^{(1)}(f)
and
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(f)
are not independent due to (

). Therefore, the limiting behavior of
\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)
can not be derived from the above limits. The main result of the current paper is the proof of

\mathcal{L}_{n}(f)\xrightarrow{\ d\ }\mathcal{N}\left(0,V_{f}\right),

for non-Hermitian random matrices with correlated entries (
3.3
) and for general test functions

. This also shows that
(\mathcal{L}^{(1)}(f),\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(f))
converges to a bivariate normal distribution. The main idea of this article makes use of the following Girko’s formula
[
28

(3.8)

\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Spec}(X^{(t)})}f(\sigma)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Delta f(z)\int_{0}^{\infty}\Im{\operatorname{Tr}(G^{(t)})^{z}(\iota\eta)}\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}^{2}z,

which expresses the linear eigenvalue statistics for any smooth compactly supported test functions in terms of the integral of the trace of the resolvent of

{}(H^{(t)})^{z}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&X^{(t)}-z\\
(X^{(t)})^{*}-\bar{z}&0\end{array}\right),t=1,2,

where

is denoted by
(G^{(t)})^{z}(\omega):=((H^{(t)})^{z}-\omega)^{-1}.

Condition 3.1
.

Let
X^{(t)}=[x^{(t)}_{ab}]_{1\leq a,b,\leq n},t=1,2,
be two random matrices such that

\chi^{(t)}

(i)

\mathbb{E}[\chi^{(t)}]=0,

\mathbb{E}[|\chi^{(t)}|^{2}]=1,

\mathbb{E}[(\chi^{(t)})^{2}]=\gamma_{1},

where

\gamma_{1}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\chi^{(t)}\text{ is a real valued,}\\
0,&\text{if }\chi^{(t)}\text{ is a complex valued.}\end{cases}

(ii)

\mathbb{E}[\chi^{(1)}\chi^{(2)}]=\rho,

\mathbb{E}[\chi^{(1)}\overline{\chi^{(2)}}]=\gamma.

(iii)

There exist constants

\mathbb{E}[|\chi^{(t)}|^{p}]\leq C_{p}.

Essentially, we start with an i.i.d. set of bivariate random variables

\Omega\supset\overline{\mathbf{D}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}.
Here the space
H_{0}^{2+\delta}(\Omega)
is the closure of the space of compactly supported smooth functions having the following finite norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{2+\delta}(\Omega)}:=\left\|(1+|\xi|)^{2+\delta}\hat{f}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
\hat{f}
is the Fourier transform of

\displaystyle C(g,f):=

Theorem 3.3

(CLT for resolvents)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{p}c\langle G^{(1)}_{i}-\mathbb{E}(G^{(1)}_{i})\rangle+d\langle G^{(2)}_{i}-\mathbb{E}(G^{(2)}_{i})\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\text{Parings}[p]}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\{c\langle G^{(1)}_{i}-\mathbb{E}(G^{(1)}_{i})\rangle+d\langle G^{(2)}_{i}-\mathbb{E}(G^{(2)}_{i})\rangle\big\}

\displaystyle\times\big\{c\langle G^{(1)}_{j}-\mathbb{E}(G^{(1)}_{j})\rangle+d\langle G^{(2)}_{j}-\mathbb{E}(G^{(2)}_{j})\rangle\big\}\Big]+\mathcal{O}_{\prec}(\Psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{p}}\sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\text{Parings}[p]}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg\{\frac{c^{2}\widehat{V_{ij}^{(1)}}+d^{2}\widehat{V_{ij}^{(2)}}+8cd\gamma(L_{ji}^{12}+L_{ji}^{21})+2cd\rho N_{ij}}{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{c^{2}(\kappa_{4})_{11}U_{i}^{(1)}U_{j}^{(1)}+d^{2}(\kappa_{4})_{22}U_{i}^{(2)}U_{j}^{(2)}+cd(\kappa_{4})_{12}(U_{i}^{(1)}U_{j}^{(2)}+U_{j}^{(1)}U_{i}^{(2)})}{2}\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}_{\prec}(\Psi),

where

Now, we consider the next non self-renormalization term

(6.71)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{\kappa((ba)_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})}{k!}{\sum_{ab}}^{\prime}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k-1}\sum_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\Delta^{ba}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\langle\Delta^{ba}\partial_{\alpha_{2}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\big]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathcal{O}_{\prec}\Big(\frac{1}{n^{3/2}\eta_{i}^{2}|\beta_{1}^{(1)}|^{2}|\beta_{i}^{(2)}|^{2}}\Big),

where we use the estimate
|\kappa((ba)_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})|/k!\lesssim n^{-(k+1)/2}
of the cumulants from (
6.5

6.5
, we have the bound
|(G^{(2)}_{ii})_{ab}|\prec 1/\eta_{i}
. After applying the
\alpha
derivatives on these terms and applying Lemma
6.5
, we get back the same bound. This justifies the last inequality in equation (
6.71
).

So far, we have examined the non self renormalized terms in the (
6.69
). To analyze the self renormalized terms, which are listed at the end of the first line, in the second line, and in the last line of (
6.69
), we do a further cumulant expansion as follows;

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\Delta^{ba}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\langle\underline{W^{(2)}\partial_{\alpha_{2}}G_{ii}^{(2)}}\rangle\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\langle\Delta^{ba}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}(G_{i}^{(2)}\widetilde{W}^{(2)}G_{ii}^{(2)}+G_{ii}^{(2)}\widetilde{W}^{(2)}G_{i}^{(2)})\rangle\langle\widetilde{W}^{(2)}\partial_{\alpha_{2}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\big]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{n}{\sum_{cd}}^{\prime}\mathbb{E}\big[\partial_{dc}\big(\langle\Delta^{dc}\partial_{\alpha_{2}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\langle\Delta^{ba}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\big)\big]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;+\sum_{l\geq 2}{\sum_{cd}}^{\prime}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}\sum_{\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\Delta^{ba}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{\vartheta_{1}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\langle\Delta^{cd}\partial_{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{\vartheta_{2}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\partial_{\alpha_{1}}(G_{ii}^{(2)}\Delta^{ba}G_{i}^{(2)}+G_{i}^{(2)}\Delta^{ba}G_{ii}^{(2)})\partial_{\alpha_{2}}(G_{ii}^{(2)})\rangle\big]+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{n}{\sum_{cd}}^{\prime}\mathbb{E}\big[\partial_{dc}\big(\langle\Delta^{dc}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\partial_{\alpha_{2}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\big)\langle\Delta^{ba}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\big]+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{n}{\sum_{cd}}^{\prime}\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\Delta^{dc}\partial_{\alpha_{2}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\partial_{dc}\big(\langle\Delta^{ba}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\big)\big]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;+\sum_{l\geq 2}{\sum_{cd}}^{\prime}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}\sum_{\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}}\frac{\kappa(dc,\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})}{l!}\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\Delta^{ba}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{\vartheta_{1}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\langle\Delta^{cd}\partial_{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{\vartheta_{2}}G_{ii}^{(2)}\rangle\big],

After inserting the first line of (
6.72

6.69

\sum^{\prime}_{ab}

Now, we turn to the last line of (
6.72
). When this is inserted back into (
6.69

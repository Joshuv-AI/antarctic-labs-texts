Motivic Homotopy Groups of Spheres and Free Summands of Stably Free Modules Thanks: We acknowledge the support of the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC), RGPIN-2021-02603.
Sebastian Gant and Ben Williams

Abstract
. Working over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 0 , we show that the motivic stable homotopy groups of the sphere spectrum can be determined entirely from the motivic homotopy groups of the p p -completed sphere spectra and the motivic cohomology of the ground field, except possibly for the 0 0 and − 1 -1 -stems. Using this, we show that the complex realization maps from the motivic homotopy groups to the classical stable homotopy groups are an isomorphism in a range of bidegrees. We apply this to deduce that complex realization also induces isomorphisms on unstable homotopy groups for Stiefel varieties V r ​ ( 𝔸 k n ) V_{r}(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}) in a range of bidegrees. This allows a complete solution of the question of when the projection map V r ​ ( 𝔸 k n ) → V 1 ​ ( 𝔸 k n ) V_{r}(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k})\to V_{1}(\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}) admits a right inverse. Equivalently, this settles the question of when the universal stably-free module of type ( n , n − 1 ) (n,n-1) admits a free summand of given rank.

Introduction

Let
R
be a ring, assumed unital and commutative, and
P
an
R
-module. One says that
P
is
stably free of type
(n,r)
if there is an isomorphism

P\oplus R^{n-r}\cong R^{n}.

In
[
9
]
, it is proved that a stably free module
P
of type
(24m,24m-1)
admits a free summand of rank
2
if
R
contains a field
k
of characteristic
0
, and of rank
3
if that field has at most one quadratic extension. The method is to convert the problem to motivic homotopy theory: specifically, the injectivity of a realization map on the homotopy groups of Stiefel varieties. This problem is then solved by using recent excellent progress on the calculation of motivic homotopy sheaves of spheres—notably,
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
. These calculations of the homotopy sheaves are of a general nature, valid over any field.

A great deal more is known about the motivic homotopy groups over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
, using the motivic Adams and Adams–Novikov spectral sequences:
[
8
,
13
,
15
,
26
,
14
]
and elsewhere. The apparent obstacle to using these calculations in our work is that these sequences calculate the homotopy groups of
p
-completed objects, rather than of the sphere spectrum itself. In this paper, we explain how to surmount this obstacle using some algebra and the results of
[
2
]
, and give a large range of bidegrees

[
20

1.1.
Outline

In Section
2
, we discuss the Ext-completion
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},A)
of an abelian group
A
, which may be viewed as a completion at all primes. We also handle the variant where only a subset of the prime numbers is considered. We make no claims to originality about this section, but we need it as a reference for our arguments concerning the homotopy groups of
p
-completed motivic spectra.

Let
k=\bar{k}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
. The main body of the paper begins with Theorem
3.2
, which says that in nonnegative weights

and aside from the
s=0
-stem, the motivic stable homotopy group
\bm{\pi}_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})(k)
of the sphere spectrum is isomorphic to the product over all primes
p
of the groups
\bm{\pi}_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}^{\wedge}_{p})(k)
of the
p
-completed spheres. A weaker version holds for negative weights: the completions may fail to detect the maximal divisible subgroup, which in most cases coincides with the motivic cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{-s}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Z}(-w))
.

This implies that the extensive calculations of homotopy groups of the
p
-completed sphere amount to computations of the motivic homotopy groups
\bm{\pi}_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})(k)
of the sphere spectrum when
s\neq 0
and
w\geq 0
(in fact, when
w\geq-1
).

An immediate consequence of Theorem

is that
\bm{\pi}_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})(k)
is isomorphic to the classical stable homotopy group of the sphere spectrum,
\pi_{s}(\mathbb{S})
, when
s\neq 0
and
0\leq w\leq\frac{1}{2}(s+1)
—this relies on isomorphisms of the
p
-completions, which are known from
[
10
]
and
[
25
]
.

There are two ingredients in the proof of Theorem
3.2
. First we consider the algebraic properties of the
\operatorname{Ext}
-completions
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/(p^{\infty}),\bm{\pi}_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})(k))
, which agrees with
\bm{\pi}_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}^{\wedge}_{p})(k)
except when
s=0
. Finiteness of
\prod_{p}\bm{\pi}_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})(k)
is now sufficient to prove that the completion map belongs in a split short exact sequence

0\to D_{s,w}\to\bm{\pi}_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})(k)\to\prod_{p}\bm{\pi}_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}^{\wedge}_{p})(k)\to 0

where
D_{s,w}
is the subgroup of divisible elements in
\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})(k)
. Except when

, this kernel is observed to be a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space. We then rely on
[
2
]
, which draws on
[
7
]
, for the identification

\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\bm{\pi}_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})(k)\cong\mathrm{H}^{-s}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Z}(-w))

is algebraically closed and

.

Having proved that the motivic and classical stable homotopy groups of spheres agree, we then exploit the

-Freudenthal theorem of
[
3
]
and an induction argument to prove that complex realization induces isomorphisms on homotopy groups of Stiefel varieties. Here we suppose

is embedded as a subfield of

(1)

\bm{\pi}_{d+e,e}(V_{r}(\mathbb{A}^{n}))(k)\to\pi_{d+e}(W_{r}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))

These theorems combine with

1.2.
Remarks and conventions

We use the symbol
\mathbb{1}
for the motivic sphere spectrum, and
\mathbb{S}
for the classical sphere spectrum. This usage is borrowed from
[
23
]
, and is justified by convenience.

When we work with motivic spaces or spectra over a base field
k
, we use a boldface
\bm{\pi}_{\ast}(X)
for the homotopy sheaves. If
X
is a motivic object, then
\pi_{\ast}(X)=\bm{\pi}_{\ast}(X)(k)
is used for the group (or pointed set) of global sections.

There are two useful bigrading conventions for motivic spheres, and consequently for homotopy sheaves and groups. There is the original convention:

S^{s,w}=S^{s-w}\wedge(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\setminus\{0\})^{\wedge w},

which we will call the “stem-weight” bigrading,
and the convention of
[
12
]
:

S^{c+w\alpha}=S^{c}\wedge(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\setminus\{0\})^{\wedge w},

which we will call the “coweight-weight” bigrading.
Stem-weight bigrading is particularly well suited to motivic Adams and Adams–Novikov spectral sequence calculations over
\mathbb{C}
, and is what is used in the extensive literature on this topic. On the other hand, coweight-weight bigrading is better for use in problems concerning motivic connectivity, for instance, the motivic Freudenthal suspension theorem of
[
3
]
. As a consequence, we use both conventions in this paper. In practice, this means that the notation
c+w\alpha
should be read as
(c+w,w)
. Here is the same homotopy group written in two ways:

\pi_{c+w\alpha}(X)=\pi_{c+w,w}(X).

The symbol
\alpha
does not appear with any other sense in this paper.

Some of our later results, notably Proposition
4.3
and Theorem
4.4
, are concerned with maps that are isomorphisms modulo uniquely divisible subgroups of the source. We devote some attention to the homological algebra of such morphisms in Appendix
A
.

Some of our sources, particularly
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
and other works on the motivic Adams spectral sequence, work over the ground field
\mathbb{C}
. Using
[
21
]
, for the purposes of calculating
\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}^{\wedge}_{p})
, we can safely replace
\mathbb{C}
by an arbitrary algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
. We ultimately wish to apply our calculations, in Section
5
, in the case where
k=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
, which is the most general available to us. It imposes essentially no extra cost to work throughout over a general algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
.

The calculations of homotopy groups in this paper all hold over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
\ell>0
, at the possible cost of replacing
\pi_{s,w}(X)
by
\mathbb{Z}[\ell^{-1}]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{s,w}(X)
. In fact, the calculations here, and knowledge of the Milnor–Witt
K
-theory, suffice to determine the analogous calculations over even more general fields. We refer the interested reader to
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
.

2.1.
Definitions

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of all prime numbers. For the rest of this subsection, fix a subset

, which may be empty. We say that a positive integer

Let

-divisible

-divisible
if it is an isomorphism. We say that

is
(uniquely)

-divisible
if it is (uniquely)

-divisible for all

.

If

is a monomorphism, then

is

-torsion-free
, and an abelian group that is

-torsion-free for all

-torsion-free
. At the other extreme, if

is the

-map, we say that

is
n
-torsion
. If

is
n
-torsion for some product

is said to be of
I
-bounded torsion
.

The notation above concerns the group as a whole. Given an element

, we say that

is
I
-torsion
if it is
n
-torsion for some integer

that is a product of primes in
I
; it is
n
-divisible
(in

) if the equation

can be solved for

in

, and
uniquely
n
-divisible
the equation has a unique solution;

is
(uniquely)

-divisible
if it is (uniquely)

-divisible for all integers

that are products of primes in

.

In all cases above, if

, then

Suppose
F:\mathrm{Ab}^{\text{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}
is a contravariant additive functor. From the previous definitions, it is immediate that

•

If
A
is uniquely
I
-divisible, then
F(A)
is uniquely
I
-divisible;

•

If

is left exact (e.g.,

for some

), and if

is
I
-torsion-free, then

is
I
-torsion-free.

Write
I^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}\setminus I
. We write
\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}]
to denote the subring of

consisting of numbers that may be written as

where

is an integer and

is a product of primes in
I
. Note that
\mathbb{Z}[\emptyset^{-1}]=\mathbb{Z}
and

. We define

The group

is torsion, and the torsion order of each element a product of primes in

. In the case where

is a singleton, we generally write

and

. If

is the set of all prime numbers, then we will write

About these groups, we observe

•

\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}]
is uniquely
I
-divisible.

•

\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right)
is
I
-divisible, and uniquely

-divisible: for

, this follows from the same fact for
\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}]
. Suppose

is a prime not in
I
. For any

, we can find some product

of primes in

. Then

, so that
\frac{1-n^{q-1}}{q}
is an integer. If we multiply
\frac{1-n^{q-1}}{q}x
by

, we obtain

, so that
\times q
is indeed surjective. Injectivity of

follows from this: for any

We deduce that, for any abelian group

•

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}],A)
and
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}],A)
are uniquely

-divisible.

•

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)
and
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)
are uniquely
I^{\prime}
-divisible.

•

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)
is torsion-free.

Definition 2.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

The
\operatorname{Ext}
-completion
of an abelian group
A
at the set of primes
I
is the group
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)
.

We have observed above that the
\operatorname{Ext}
-completion consists of nontorsion or elements whose torsion order is a product of primes in
I
. From the short exact sequence

(2)

0\to\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}]\to\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right)\to 0

we deduce that there is a natural transformation (a connecting homomorphism)

A=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},A)\to\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A),

and we will refer to this as the “
I
-completion map” in the sequel, or the “
p
-completion map” when
I=\{p\}
. As usual, if
I=\mathcal{P}
, the symbol “
I
” may be omitted.

If
I_{0}\subseteq I
is a subset of
I
, then there is a canonical homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}/(I_{0}^{\infty})\to\mathbb{Z}/(I^{\infty})
. In particular, for any set of prime numbers, we obtain a canonical homomorphism

(3)

\bigoplus_{p\in I}\mathbb{Z}/\left(p^{\infty}\right)\to\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right).

Proposition 2.2
.

The canonical homomorphism of (
3
) is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Surjectivity of the homomorphism follows from surjectivity of
\bigoplus_{p\in I}\mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}]\to\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}]
, where it follows from the existence of partial fraction decompositions for rational numbers.

For injectivity, suppose that some sequence

\left(\frac{a_{1}}{p_{1}^{s_{1}}},\frac{a_{2}}{p_{2}^{s_{2}}},\dots,\frac{a_{r}}{p_{r}^{s_{r}}}\right)\in\bigoplus_{p\in I}\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{(p^{\infty})}

maps to
0
under the canonical homomorphism, where
0\leq a_{i}<p_{i}^{s_{i}}
for all
i
. That is, suppose

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{a_{i}}{p_{i}^{s_{i}}}\text{\qquad is an integer.}

By clearing denominators and considering the power of each prime dividing the numerator, we deduce that each
a_{i}
must be
0
. This establishes injectivity.
∎

We have defined the
I
-completion for our own convenience. The next corollary shows that it is sufficient only to define
p
-completions. The proof is that
\operatorname{Ext}
converts sums in the first variable into products.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
A
be an abelian group and
I
a set of prime numbers. Then the canonical isomorphism
\bigoplus_{p\in I}\mathbb{Z}/\left(p^{\infty}\right)\to\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right)
induces an isomorphism

\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)\to\prod_{p\in I}\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(p^{\infty}\right),A).

2.2.
Surjectivity of completion

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
A
be an abelian group and
I
a set of prime numbers. Suppose
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)
is a torsion group. Then the completion
A\to\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)
is surjective.

Proof.

From the exact sequence (
2
), we derive a long exact sequence

(4)

\cdots\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}],A)\to A\to\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)\overset{j}{\to}\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}],A)\to\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z},A)=0.

It suffices to show that the map
j
is
0
. The target is uniquely
I
-divisible, so that
\times n^{-1}
is a well defined automorphism of
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}],A)
whenever
n
is a product of primes in
I
. By hypothesis the source consists of torsion elements, and orders of these elements are products of primes in
I
. For any such
a\in\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)
, we can find some
n
for which
na=0
. Then
j(na)=0
, but where
nj(a)=0
implies
j(a)=0
.
∎

In the case where
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)
is a group of bounded torsion, for instance when it is finite, we can say more. The argument involves a step that we set aside as a lemma for later use.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
I
be a set of prime numbers and
n
a product of primes in
I
. Let

0\to A\to B\to C\to 0

be a short exact sequence of abelian groups in which
A
is uniquely
I
-divisible and
C
is
n
-torsion. Then the short exact sequence is split.

Proof.

Consider
\operatorname{Ext}(C,A)
, on which
\times n
acts as an isomorphism, since
A
is uniquely
I
-divisible, and as
0
, since
C
is
n
-torsion. It must be the case that
\operatorname{Ext}(C,A)=0
. The Yoneda interpretation of
\operatorname{Ext}
implies the sequence splits.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
A
be an abelian group and
I
a set of prime numbers. Suppose
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)
is a group of
I
-bounded torsion and
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)=0
. There is a short exact sequence

(5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}[I^{-1}],A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(I^{\infty}\right),A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

. In this diagram,

is exactly the subgroup of

-divisible elements in

, and the image of

-torsion subgroup of

Since

, the long exact sequence (

) contains the short exact sequence (

), using Proposition

is uniquely

We continue to work over an algebraically closed field

]
for odd primes (see also

). The

-page of this spectral sequence is isomorphic to a bigraded subgroup of the

-page of the classical Adams–Novikov spectral sequence. This follows from

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
s,w
be integers satisfying
s\neq 0
. Then the comparison map

\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})\to\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}^{\wedge}_{p})

is split surjective, and the kernel is the subgroup of divisible elements. Additionally, if
s\neq-1
, then the kernel is isomorphic to the motivic cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{-s}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Z}(-w))
.

Proof.

We remark that the motivic cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{-s}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Z}(-w))
are uniquely divisible except when
w\leq 0
and
s=0,-1
. This is a consequence of the long exact sequence

\cdots\to\mathrm{H}^{-s}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Z}(-w))\overset{\times n}{\to}\mathrm{H}^{-s}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Z}(-w))\to\mathrm{H}^{-s}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Z}/(n)(-w))\to\mathrm{H}^{-s+1}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Z}(-w))\to\cdots

and the norm-residue isomorphism theorem (
[
11
, Thm. 1.8]
) for the algebraically closed field
k
. As a result, rationalization induces an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{-s}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Z}(-w))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{H}^{-s}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Q}(-w)),\text{\quad provided $s\neq 0,-1$.}

We use this to replace the rational cohomology by the integral at the end of the proof.

When
s\neq 0
, the group
\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}^{\wedge}_{p})
is finite by Proposition
3.1
. In particular, it is
n
-torsion for some positive integer
n
.

For a fixed
p
, in the exact sequence (
6
), we remark that
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(p^{\infty}\right),\pi_{s-1,w}(\mathbb{1}))
is a torsion-free quotient of a torsion group, so it vanishes, and therefore
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(p^{\infty}\right),\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}))\cong\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}^{\wedge}_{p})
. From this, Corollary
2.3
tells us that the comparison

\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}^{\wedge}_{p})

is an isomorphism.

Since this group is finite, Proposition
2.4
applies, saying that the completion map is surjective. When
s\neq-1
, we have also seen that
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(p^{\infty}\right),\pi_{s+1,w}(\mathbb{1}))=0
, so that Proposition
2.6
applies, telling us the completion is split surjective and the kernel is the subgroup of divisible elements.

In the case of
s=-1
, a different argument is required. In this case, the Adams and Adams–Novikov spectral sequences imply (see
[
10
]
and
[
25
, Prop. 2.4.8]
) that
\pi_{-1,w}(\mathbb{1}^{\wedge}_{p})=0
for all primes
p
. Therefore the completion map is split surjective for trivial reasons. We also see that
\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/\left(p^{\infty}\right),\pi_{-1,w}(\mathbb{1}))=0
, by short exact sequence (
6
). We may, however, have a nontrivial presentation arising from exact sequence (
4
):

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},\pi_{-1,w}(\mathbb{1}))\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Q},\pi_{-1,w}(\mathbb{1}))\to\pi_{-1,w}(\mathbb{1})\to 0.

In this case we may conclude that
\pi_{-1,w}(\mathbb{1})
is a quotient of a uniquely divisible group, and so is divisible.

For the rest of this proof, we assume
s\neq-1
. The comparison map belongs in a (split) short exact sequence:

0\to K_{s,w}\to\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})\to\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}^{\wedge}_{p})\to 0

where the kernel

is uniquely divisible, i.e., a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space, and the cokernel is finite. Therefore, applying
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}-
to the short exact sequence yields an isomorphism

(7)

K_{s,w}\cong\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1}).

Except in the case where
s=w
, an isomorphism
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{s,w}(\mathbb{1})\cong\mathrm{H}^{-s}(\operatorname{Spec}k;\mathbb{Q}(-w))
is contained in
[
2
, Thm. 3.7]
.

We finally consider the case of
s=w
. Here we know the groups
\pi_{s,s}(\mathbb{1})\cong{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathsf{MW}}_{-s}(k)
, from
[
18
, Thm. 1.23, Cor. 1.25]
. There is a presentation of
{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathsf{MW}}_{-s}(k)
as a product of the Milnor

-theory group
{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathsf{M}}_{-s}(k)
with a power

of the fundamental ideal of the Witt ring of the field, fibred over
\mathbf{k}^{\mathsf{M}}_{-s}(k)
the mod-

reduction of the Milnor
K
-theory: see
[
18
, Lem. 3.10]
. Over an algebraically closed field, the fundamental ideal vanishes, as does the mod-

Milnor

-theory. We are left with an isomorphism
{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathsf{MW}}_{-s}(k)\cong{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathsf{M}}_{-s}(k)

follows from
[

, Ch. 5]
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
s,w
be integers satisfying
s\neq 0
,
w\geq-1
and

. The comparison map

First, suppose
s\neq-1
and

. Then Theorem

tells us the map is surjective and the kernel is a motivic cohomology group

, Ch. 4]

Now suppose

Recall that we write

for the sphere spectrum in classical topology. For any

Remark 3.5
.

Note that Corollary

assures us that the complex realization is an isomorphism in many cases, since

and

.

5.
Application to maps of Stiefel varieties and stably free modules

For any
r\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, there is a projection map
\rho:V_{r}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\to V_{1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. The problem of determining whether
\rho
has a right inverse (alternatively termed “a section” since
\rho
is a fibre bundle) was studied in
[
9
]
. Using Theorem
4.5
, we can settle all cases of this problem over
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We recall the definition of the James numbers
b_{q}
, which are also known as the Atiyah–Todd numbers
M_{q}
. Definitions are given in
[
16
]
,
[
4
]
, and these definitions are proved to be equal in
[
1
]
. Explicitly, they are described by their
p
-adic valuations,
v_{p}
, for all primes
p
:

v_{p}(b_{q})=\begin{cases}\max\left\{s+v_{p}(s)\>\Big|\>1\leq s\leq\lfloor\frac{q-1}{p-1}\rfloor\right\},\quad\text{ if $q\geq p$;}\\
0\qquad\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

The first few James numbers may easily be listed:

b_{2}=2;\quad b_{3}=b_{4}=2^{3}3=24;\quad b_{5}=2^{6}3^{2}5=2880;\quad

We remark that the James numbers grow quickly. We establish a rough bound:
v_{2}(b_{q})\geq q-1
, so that
b_{q}\geq 2^{q-1}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and suppose
2\leq r\leq n-2
. Then

\rho:V_{r}(\mathbb{A}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}^{n})\to V_{1}(\mathbb{A}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}^{n})

has a right inverse if and only if
b_{r}\mid n

Proof.

If
b_{r}\nmid n
, then there cannot be a right inverse by virtue of
[
20
, Thm. 6.5]
. Note that this result disallows right inverses even in the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category
\text{\bf H}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Let us therefore suppose that
b_{r}\mid n
, and try to construct a right inverse. The case of
r=2
follows from a well known construction of Bass, first published to our knowledge in
[
20
, Prop. 2.2(b)]
as a construction of a free summand in a projective module.

We therefore may suppose
r\geq 3
. By using the rough bound we established earlier, we deduce that
r\leq\log_{2}(n)+1
. If
n\geq 9
, this implies that
r<n/2
. For
n\in\{5,6,7,8\}
, the inequality
b_{r}\leq n
implies
b_{r}=2
, so
r<n/2
in this case as well.

Theorem
4.5
applies with
(n-2,n)
playing the part of
(d,e)
and
(r-1,n-1)
playing the part of
(r,n)
. Inequality
2
of Theorem
4.5
required
r<n/2
in this case. We deduce that the realization map

\pi_{n-2+n\alpha}(V_{r-1}(\mathbb{A}^{n-1}))\to\pi_{2n-2}(W_{r-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n-1})

is injective. We now use
[
9
, Prop. 7.3]
, which tells us that
\rho
has a right inverse if the corresponding map of complex Stiefel manifolds

\rho(\mathbb{C}):W_{r}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to W_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

has a right inverse. This has a right inverse by
[
1
, Thm. 1.1]
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Suppose
R
is a commutative ring containing an algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
P
be an
R
-module for which there exists an isomorphism

for some positive integer

.
If

, then there exists a decomposition

P\cong Q\oplus R^{r-1}.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
5.1
and
[
9
, Prop. 4.4]
.
∎

Appendix A
Homological algebra modulo uniquely divisible subgroups

Lemma A.1
.

If

0\to A\to B\to C\to 0

is a short exact sequence of abelian groups with the property that two of the groups
A,B,C
are uniquely divisible, then so is the third.

Proof.

By definition, an abelian group
M
is uniquely divisible if the self-maps
\times n:M\to M
are isomorphisms for all natural numbers
n
. The result follows easily from the snake lemma.
∎

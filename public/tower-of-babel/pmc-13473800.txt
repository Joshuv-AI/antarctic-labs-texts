Rationale and trial design of RIBOLARIS: A phase II trial of neoadjuvant and adjuvant ribociclib plus endocrine therapy for clinically high-risk HR+/HER2-negative breast cancer
Authors: Paul Cottu, Tomas Pascual, Jerôme Lemonnier, Huilin Hu, Estelle Roux, Roisin M Connolly, Cynthia X Ma, Antonio C Wolff, Thayane Crestani, Pablo Tolosa, Juan M Ferrero-Cafiero, Antonio Mulero-Sánchez, Fernando Salvador, Guillermo Villacampa, Joaquín Gavilá, Thibault de La Motte Rouge, Aleix Prat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Despite advances in endocrine therapy, patients with hormone receptor-positive (HR+)/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HER2−) early breast cancer (eBC) remain at risk of recurrence (ROR). Chemotherapy is often used in high-risk cases, but not all patients benefit.Objectives:To evaluate whether chemotherapy can be safely omitted in patients with stage II, grade 2/3 HR+/HER2− eBC who convert to low genomic risk (ROR-low) following neoadjuvant ribociclib plus letrozole.Methods:RIBOLARIS (NCT05296746) is an open-label, multicenter phase II trial in patients with stage II, grade 2 or 3, HR+/HER2− eBC defined as high risk based on a Ki-67 index ⩾20% and/or high genomic risk by a validated gene expression signature. All patients receive six 28-day cycles of neoadjuvant ribociclib (600 mg/day, 3 weeks on/1 week off) plus letrozole (2.5 mg/day). Following surgery, patients with ROR-low or pathological complete response continue adjuvant ribociclib (400 mg/day for 30 months) and endocrine therapy (⩾5 years), whereas patients with ROR-medium/high receive standard chemotherapy followed by ribociclib and endocrine therapy. The primary endpoint is 5-year distant metastasis-free survival (DMFS) in the ROR-low cohort. Interim futility analyses are planned at 300 and 800 patient-years (PYs), with the primary analysis after 1300 PYs. The study targets enrollment of 1100 patients, anticipating 400 ROR-low cases, providing ⩾90% power to detect a 5-year DMFS of 96% versus a benchmark of 90.6% (MINDACT trial high-risk subgroup).Discussion:RIBOLARIS is the first prospective trial to evaluate a chemotherapy-free, ribociclib-based strategy in high-risk HR+/HER2− eBC based on neoadjuvant molecular response. By integrating genomic risk reclassification into treatment decision-making, this study aims to demonstrate that patients achieving ROR-low status can maintain excellent long-term outcomes without chemotherapy, while avoiding unnecessary exposure to adjuvant chemotherapy, and to highlight the role of tumor profiling after neoadjuvant endocrine therapy plus cyclin-dependent kinase 4 and 6 inhibitors in optimizing post-neoadjuvant therapy.Trial registration:NCT05296746; EudraCT# 2021-002322-24.Keywords: early breast cancer, HR+/HER2−, intrinsic subtype, neoadjuvant, PAM50, ribociclib

Introduction

Breast cancer has emerged as the most diagnosed cancer globally since 2020, with an estimated 2.3 million new cases, of which 95% are early breast cancer (eBC).1–3 Hormone receptor-positive (HR+), human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HER2−) breast cancer remains the most common subtype,4,5 and globally, most patients are diagnosed with stage II disease. 6

Over the past three decades, tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors have been the mainstay of endocrine therapy in both early and metastatic settings of breast cancer. Despite the effectiveness of adjuvant endocrine therapy in reducing the risk of recurrence (ROR), the risk of distant recurrence remains approximately 13% in patients with stage I/II disease and approximately 20% in patients with stage III disease beyond 20 years. 7 Recent adjuvant phase III trials in patients with stage II HR+/HER2− eBC suggest that early recurrences occur in ~10%–24% of patients within 3 years of initiating endocrine therapy.8–12

In addition to endocrine therapy, adjuvant chemotherapy options may be recommended to reduce the likelihood of recurrences and mortality in eBC, considering its toxicity and impact on patients’ quality of life.13–15 However, in patients with eBC who are at low ROR, the absolute benefits offered by chemotherapy regimens may be small.16–18 The extent of these benefits is often influenced by the initial risk of cancer recurrence, which can be estimated using various clinical factors such as tumor stage, grade, and gene expression-based tumor profiling.

Intrinsic molecular subtypes (luminal A and B, HER2-enriched, and basal-like), identified based on gene expression profiling, are associated with distinct clinical and biological features that play a crucial role in predicting therapeutic response and prognosis.19–23 The Early Breast Cancer Trialists’ Collaborative Group recommends an adjuvant chemotherapy regimen containing both anthracyclines and taxanes at higher cumulative doses to be more efficacious for reducing the risks of breast cancer recurrence and mortality in patients with high-risk luminal B breast cancer (mostly node-positive) who are more likely to have recurrence with endocrine treatment alone. 24

Importantly, a recent longitudinal transcriptomic study in PENELOPE-B showed that gene expression measured in residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy is strongly prognostic. In post-treatment residual tumors, normal-like and luminal A subtypes are associated with markedly better outcomes, while luminal B and especially basal-like/HER2-enriched residual phenotypes indicate higher event rates. These findings support residual-disease biology as a key driver of prognosis and reinforce the RIBOLARIS approach of using residual-disease gene-expression profiling to guide adjuvant therapy selection. 25

A recent real-world study shows that a marked decline in Ki67 after short preoperative endocrine therapy strongly predicts endocrine sensitivity and improved long-term outcomes in HR+/HER2− eBC. Achieving complete cell cycle arrest (Ki67 ⩽2.7%) was independently associated with better event-free survival, supporting early Ki67 suppression as a dynamic biomarker to guide adjuvant therapy decisions. 26

In patients with operable breast cancer in the neoadjuvant setting, chemotherapy primarily results in downstaging large tumors, while increasing breast-conserving surgery for many patients at an early disease stage and enabling identification of in vivo response to systemic therapy. Among patients receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy, pathological complete response (pCR) rates are significantly higher in HER2+ breast cancer and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), which show greater responsiveness to chemotherapy. However, pCR rates are low (i.e., <15%) in HR+/HER2− breast cancer compared to more aggressive subtypes, and thus, the role of neoadjuvant chemotherapy in this population remains debated. Several studies have demonstrated the clinical utility of gene expression-based assays, including Prosigna,21,20 OncotypeDX, 27 MammaPrint, 28 EndoPredict, 29 and 21-gene breast cancer assay (RxPONDER 30 ), in identifying patients with eBC who are most likely to benefit from neoadjuvant chemotherapy.31,32

Cyclin-dependent kinase 4 and 6 inhibitors (CDK4/6i), such as palbociclib, ribociclib, and abemaciclib, in combination with endocrine therapy, are the current standard-of-care first- and second-line treatment options for advanced HR+/HER2− disease.33–39 CDK4/6i have emerged as a promising targeted therapy for eBC. In the neoadjuvant setting, the antitumor efficacy of CDK4/6i is being evaluated to determine whether patients with high-risk eBC could benefit from replacing neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy with a combination of CDK4/6i and endocrine therapy.40–42 The phase II CORALLEEN trial enrolled patients with stage I–IIIA, luminal B eBC to evaluate the clinical benefits and biological effects of neoadjuvant ribociclib treatment. The study compared chemotherapy versus ribociclib + letrozole neoadjuvant treatment in 106 patients with luminal B eBC. Of note, 46.9% and 46.1% were converted to a low ROR after ribociclib + letrozole therapy and chemotherapy at surgery, respectively, suggesting similar efficacy in both arms. These findings indicate that a substantial proportion of the treated patients with clinically high-risk luminal B breast cancer could achieve molecular downstaging, as assessed by the PAM50 assay. 40 Importantly, patients treated with ribociclib + letrozole reported better quality-of-life outcomes compared to those receiving chemotherapy. 43 Consistent with these results, the recent phase II CORALLEEN trial demonstrated that neoadjuvant ribociclib + letrozole and standard chemotherapy produced similar biological effects in high-risk luminal B breast cancer, including comparable reductions in Ki67 and molecular downstaging to luminal A. Both treatments achieved substantial rates of complete cell cycle arrest and reduced proliferation, supporting the concept that CDK4/6i-based regimens can match chemotherapy in terms of molecular efficacy in selected patients. 44

Similarly, the NeoPAL study evaluated neoadjuvant palbociclib plus endocrine therapy in 106 patients with HR+/HER2−, defined as -luminal B by Prosigna®, or luminal A with node-positive, stage II–III eBC. The study showed better Preoperative Endocrine Prognostic Index 0 scores (breast cancer-specific survival vs chemotherapy; PEPI score 0: 17.6% vs 8%), suggesting that the CDK4/6i combination therapy might potentially be better than chemotherapy in high-risk patients 45 (Table 1). Recent evidence from the NeoPAL study has demonstrated that the combination of neoadjuvant palbociclib plus letrozole and chemotherapy induces a strikingly similar pattern of biological effects and tumor remodeling. Both treatment approaches achieved significant reductions in proliferation, molecular downstaging to ROR-low disease, and remodeling of the tumor microenvironment toward a more efficient immune response. 45 Taken together with findings from the CORALLEEN trial, these results strongly support the use of CDK4/6i-based therapy as an alternative to chemotherapy for carefully selected patients, by leveraging the neoadjuvant platform.

As a biomarker of persistent change, the antigen Ki67 detected by immunohistochemistry (IHC) can predict the prognosis; thus, a low Ki67 value indicates that patients have a better survival and a lower ROR. 46 The prognostic ability of Ki67 was initially evidenced in the neoadjuvant IMPACT trial that compared anastrozole, tamoxifen, or the combination of both for 12 weeks. The Ki67 score, measured as a continuous variable after natural log transformation, at 2 weeks in IMPACT, was predictive of relapse-free survival. 47 The POETIC trial has confirmed the powerful predictive value of Ki67 expression on the long-term prognosis of proliferation, which may help select patients not requiring chemotherapy. 48

The WSG-ADAPT-HR+/HER2– study trial results demonstrated no statistically significant difference in recurrence rates between the chemotherapy and endocrine therapy-only arms among intermediate-risk patients (hazard ratio (HR), 1.30; 95% confidence interval (CI), 0.90–1.88), supporting the feasibility of omitting chemotherapy in this subgroup of pre- and post-menopausal patients with ⩽3 involved lymph nodes based on endocrine response and recurrence score. 42 The neoadjuvant trials—NeoPalAna (palbociclib), neoMONARCH (abemaciclib), PALLET (palbociclib), and FELINE (ribociclib)49–52—suggested significant improvement in complete cell cycle arrest, with the key endpoint being defined as Ki67 ⩽2.7% (Table 2).

In the adjuvant setting, CDK4/6i have shown different results in terms of the potential benefits of the addition of CDK4/6i to endocrine therapy, showing varying efficacy results across major clinical trials.53,54 (Table 3). In the PALLAS updated follow-up data, the addition of palbociclib to standard endocrine therapy did not improve invasive disease-free survival (iDFS) versus endocrine monotherapy (iDFS at 4 years: 84.2% vs 84.5%; HR: 0.96) in patients with eBC who had completed adjuvant or neoadjuvant therapy and had undergone surgery.10,55 Consistent with these results, the phase III PENELOPE-B trial showed that adding 1 year of palbociclib to adjuvant endocrine therapy in high-risk, HR+/HER2− eBC did not improve iDFS or OS compared with placebo (6-year iDFS: 65.1% vs 64.5%; HR 0.94; 6-year OS: 82.4% vs 80.3%; HR 0.87). Exploratory analysis suggested a potential benefit in patients with lobular histology. 56

In contrast to PALLAS and PENELOPE-B, the phase III monarchE trial showed improved outcomes, suggesting that 2 years of abemaciclib treatment significantly improved iDFS in patients with HR+/HER2− node-positive eBC at high risk of early recurrence (⩾4 positive axillary lymph nodes (ALNs) OR at least 1 ALN and one of the following: tumor size ⩾5 cm, Grade 3 or Ki67 ⩾20%; N = 5637).53,57 In the subsequent analysis, with a median follow-up of 54 months and all (100%) patients off treatment, abemaciclib combined with endocrine therapy demonstrated superior iDFS versus endocrine therapy alone (HR, 0.680). 58 Based on monarchE study findings, adjuvant abemaciclib in combination with endocrine therapy has been internationally approved with National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) category 1 and European Society for Medical Oncology-Magnitude of Clinical Benefit Scale score A recommendation for patients with HR+/HER2–, node-positive eBC at high ROR. Adjuvant ribociclib in combination with an aromatase inhibitor received approval from the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of patients with HR+/HER2– stage II and III eBC at ROR, including those who have node-negative disease 59 and recommended as a category 1 preferred breast cancer adjuvant treatment by the NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology (NCCN Guidelines®). 60 The European Commission approved ribociclib in combination with an AI for the adjuvant treatment of patients with HR+/HER2– eBC at high ROR. 61 The regulatory approval was based on findings from the phase III NATALEE trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of adjuvant ribociclib with endocrine therapy that enrolled patients with an anatomic stage III, IIB, or a subset of stage IIA cases (as N1; N0 with grade 3; N0 with grade 2 but Ki67 ⩾20 or a molecular test that categorizes the case as high risk) after adequate surgical resection (N = 5101).62–64 In the recent protocol-specified final iDFS analysis of NATALEE, ribociclib continued to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in iDFS (one-sided p = 0.0006; HR, 0.749), emphasizing the benefit of ribociclib in a broad population of patients with HR+/HER2− eBC at ROR. 9 Additionally, in the 4-year landmark analysis, ribociclib continued to demonstrate an iDFS benefit over NSAI alone by reducing the risk of disease recurrence by 28.5% (HR, 0.715). 65

In patients with high-risk luminal disease, the addition of abemaciclib or ribociclib endocrine therapy has demonstrated benefits. It could furthermore be hypothesized that the combination of a CDK4/6i with endocrine therapy may be as effective as chemotherapy in a substantial proportion of patients with high-risk luminal breast cancer. The results from the NeoPAL and CORALLEEN neoadjuvant trials indeed suggested that a group of patients with high genomic risk could avoid chemotherapy.20,41,66 Based on the impressive array of results obtained with CDK4/6 inhibitors in patients with early-stage, intermediate/high-risk luminal breast cancer, we designed the RIBOLARIS study to determine whether chemotherapy could be avoided in patients with high-risk clinicopathological tumors at baseline that showed a ROR-low (low genomic and clinicopathological risk) after neoadjuvant treatment with ribociclib and letrozole, by continuing this treatment in the adjuvant setting.

Methods

Study design

RIBOLARIS (SOLTI-1911/Breast International Group (BIG)-21-02; UNICANCER-BCG-2105; NCT05296746) is an open-label, international, multicenter trial. It includes men and women with primary operable HR+/HER2− stage II breast cancer that is grade 2 or 3 and either has Ki67 ⩾20% or shows high genomic risk based on a validated gene expression signature. The study aims to assess the safety and long-term effectiveness of a chemotherapy-free approach in patients who achieve an ROR-low score after receiving neoadjuvant ribociclib and letrozole (Figure 1). The reporting of this study protocol conforms to the SPIRIT 2025 statement, 67 and the completed checklist is provided as Supplemental File 1.

RIBOLARIS study design.

DMFS, distant metastasis-free survival; ER, endocrine receptor; ET, endocrine therapy; HER2−, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative; HR, hormone receptor; LHRH, luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone; ROR, risk of recurrence.

Patients will start screening procedures after agreeing to participate in the study and signing the informed consent form. All patients will have known ER, PgR, HER2, and Ki67 status locally assessed by IHC and/or fluorescence in situ hybridization, according to the most recent ASCO/CAP guidelines.

Neoadjuvant treatment phase

All patients will receive neoadjuvant ribociclib and letrozole; the treatment will consist of six 28-day cycles of daily letrozole (2.5 mg; continuous) and ribociclib (600 mg/day; 3 weeks ON and 1 week OFF). In premenopausal women and men, monthly luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) agonists will be added, beginning at least 2 weeks before starting ribociclib and letrozole.

Monthly clinical tumor monitoring will be performed during the neoadjuvant phase. Conventional imaging and MRI will be performed at baseline, after 3 months and prior to surgery. If any sign or symptom of progressive disease is detected at any point of the study, a breast MRI/ultrasound will be scheduled as soon as possible for confirmation according to RECIST 1.1 criteria. If progressive disease is confirmed, then the patient’s investigational treatment will be discontinued to proceed to curative surgery, the patient will be classified as ROR-med/high and will be assigned to the group receiving adjuvant chemotherapy.

In case of treatment discontinuation due to ribociclib-related toxicities during the neoadjuvant period, the patient will continue the study, considering the following options.

- The patient completes 6 months of neoadjuvant treatment with letrozole and then undergoes surgery. Adjuvant chemotherapy will be administered, if necessary, according to centrally assessed ROR and pathological stage after surgery.

- The patient completes 6 months of neoadjuvant treatment with letrozole and then undergoes surgery. Adjuvant chemotherapy will be administered, if necessary, according to centrally assessed ROR and pathological stage after surgery.

- The patient directly undergoes surgery. Adjuvant chemotherapy will be administered, if necessary, according to centrally assessed ROR and pathological stage after surgery.

- The patient directly undergoes surgery. Adjuvant chemotherapy will be administered, if necessary, according to centrally assessed ROR and pathological stage after surgery.

Adjuvant treatment phase

The adjuvant treatment will be decided according to centrally assessed ROR and pathological stage after surgery. Patients will be considered low-risk if they achieve a pCR, defined as no residual invasive tumor in breast or nodes following neoadjuvant therapy (ypT0/Tis, ypN0, where the prefix “yp” indicates that the tumor (T) and nodal (N) status were determined by pathological evaluation) or have ypN0 and ROR ⩽30 or ypN1mi (micrometastasis: cancer involved the lymph node of >0.2 mm but <2 mm) and ROR ⩽20 or ypN1 and ROR ⩽10. All patients with ypN0 and ROR >30, ypN1mi and ROR >20, ypN1 and ROR >10 or ypN2-3 are considered intermediate or high risk. Patients who have progression during neoadjuvant treatment with ribociclib will be assigned to the group receiving adjuvant chemotherapy (ROR-med/high), if indicated. Adjuvant radiotherapy will be performed, if indicated, before adjuvant ribociclib and before or after adjuvant chemotherapy in this patient group.

Adjuvant treatment in low-risk patients

Patients with pCR (ypT0/Tis, ypN0) and patients with centrally assessed Prosigna ROR-low (N0: ROR ⩽30; N1mi: ROR ⩽20; N1: ROR ⩽10) in the surgical specimen will continue to undergo adjuvant treatment after optimal recovery of surgery. Radiotherapy will be proposed as indicated per current guidelines.

Adjuvant treatment will start within 8 weeks after surgery if adjuvant radiotherapy is not indicated and within 16 weeks if radiotherapy is indicated. Treatment with ribociclib will be initiated at least 2 weeks after the end of adjuvant radiotherapy. Adjuvant ribociclib (400 mg/day; 3 weeks ON and 1 week OFF) will be maintained for approximately 30 months, corresponding to 33 cycles, and letrozole treatment will be administered for at least 5 years.

The visits during ribociclib treatment will be scheduled every three cycles. At the end of ribociclib treatment, visits will be scheduled every 6 months until 5 years from the last visit since the patient’s surgery.

Adjuvant treatment in intermediate/high-risk patients

Patients with centrally assessed Prosigna ROR-med/high (N0: ROR >30; N1mi: ROR >20; N1: ROR >10; any N2) in surgical specimen will be treated with one of the following standard-of-care adjuvant chemotherapy regimens at the investigator’s choice, all of them validated by national and international guidelines:

Regimen 1: Doxorubicin 60 mg/m2 IV day 1 (or epirubicin 75–100 mg/m2) and cyclophosphamide 600–830 mg/m2 day 1 every 14/21 days for 4 cycles, followed by paclitaxel 80 mg/m2 every week for 12 weeks or docetaxel 75–100 mg/m2 every 3 weeks for 12 weeks.

Regimen 2: Docetaxel 75–100 mg/m2 IV day 1 and cyclophosphamide 600–830 mg/m2 day 1 every 21 days for 4–6 cycles.

Regimen 3: Paclitaxel 80 mg/m2 every week for 12 weeks or docetaxel 75–100 mg/m2 every 3 weeks for 12 weeks followed by doxorubicin 60 mg/m2 IV day 1 (or epirubicin 75–100 mg/m2) and cyclophosphamide 600–830 mg/m2 day 1 every 14/21 days for 4 cycles.

During adjuvant treatment (for both ROR low and ROR Med/high cohorts), letrozole can be switched to another AI pending tolerance. Tamoxifen is only permitted after the 30-day post-ribociclib end-of-trial visit, according to the investigator criteria. AI treatment is allowed after surgery, before initiation of ribociclib, during radiotherapy and chemotherapy at the investigator’s discretion. Maintaining suppression of ovarian function by LHRH agonists during adjuvant treatment is mandatory (if AIs are taken)/recommended (if tamoxifen is taken) in premenopausal women and male patients unless there is unmanageable toxicity.

RIBOLARIS temporary pause of recruitment

In the second half of 2023, the FDA and other health authorities released new guidance to reduce the acceptable intake limits for nitrosamines in medications including clinical trial materials. Due to the evolving regulatory guidance, enrollment of new patients in Ribociclib eBC studies, such as RIBOLARIS, was paused in April 2024. Enrollment resumed in November 2024 after implementing manufacturing adjustments in accordance with health authorities.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The trial includes only patients with centrally reviewed HR+/HER2− eBC with ER staining >10%, grade 2 or 3, and Ki-67 index ⩾20% and/or high genomic risk as defined by a validated prognostic multigene assay (Oncotype DX® RS ⩾26, MammaPrint® high risk, Prosigna/PAM50 ROR ⩾60 or luminal B, or EndoPredict® high risk) and clinical stage II (AJCC 7th ed) which includes cT1cN1cM0, cT2cN0cM0, cT2cN1cM0, and cT3cN0cM0 (Table 4).

Study eligibility criteria.

Post-menopausal status is defined as: (1) Age ⩾60 years or (2) Age <60 years and 12 months of amenorrhea plus follicle-stimulating hormone and plasma estradiol levels within post-menopausal range by local laboratory assessment or (3) prior bilateral oophorectomy (⩾7 days prior to Day 1 of treatment).

Multifocal and multicentric tumors are permitted if they are considered clinical stage II according to Seventh Edition of the AJCC.

Baseline staging to document absence of metastatic disease is not required; however, it is recommended as determined by institutional practice (in patients where there may be a reasonable suspicion of advanced disease, e.g., large tumors, clinically positive axillary lymph nodes, signs, and symptoms). If performed, reports of these examinations must be available. Examination type for staging, that is, radiography, sonography, bone scan, computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, and/or positron emission tomography-computed tomography, is at the discretion of the investigator.

eBC, early breast cancer; ER, estrogen receptor; FFPE, formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded; HER, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2; HR, hormone receptor.

Adult men aged ⩾18 years of age or premenopausal women aged ⩾40 years or postmenopausal women will be included. Premenopausal women will receive LHRH agonists 2 weeks before cycle 1 day 1 (C1D1) and during treatment. Availability of formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) specimen from the breast tumor tissue evaluable for PAM50 or the possibility to perform re-biopsy and obtain the specimen will be a study inclusion criterion. Patients with bilateral invasive breast cancer or with any active cardiac disease or history of cardiac dysfunction will not be eligible.

Study objectives

The primary objective of the study is to evaluate the long-term efficacy (distant metastasis-free survival, DMFS) of neo/adjuvant endocrine therapy + ribociclib in patients with high-risk stage II HR+/HER2− disease who achieve a ROR-low status at surgery.

The secondary objectives are to evaluate efficacy of ribociclib + endocrine therapy during neoadjuvant and evaluate long-term efficacy of neo/adjuvant endocrine therapy + ribociclib in patients with high-risk stage II HR+/HER2− disease who achieve a ROR-low status at surgery. Safety and tolerability of investigational treatment and their corresponding standard treatment will be considered as secondary objectives.

Endpoints and statistical plan

The statistical approach in RIBOLARIS is designed to determine whether patients who achieve an ROR-low score after neoadjuvant ribociclib and letrozole experience DMFS rates that are meaningfully better than historical benchmarks. The primary endpoint is DMFS, defined as the time from surgery to distant metastatic recurrence or death from any cause. Analyses will be conducted on two populations: the intent-to-treat group, which includes all enrolled patients, and the evaluable population, which includes patients with complete ROR and clinical data after surgery. DMFS will be summarized using the Kaplan–Meier method, with CIs constructed using Greenwood’s formula for standard error estimation. However, patient-years (PYs) of follow-up approach will be used as decision rule to analyze DMFS. This approach uses the total time of follow-up of all included patients and the number of observed events during the follow-up.

A Quality Limit Threshold (QTL) is prospectively defined for the neoadjuvant phase to monitor the proportion of patients experiencing disease progression while receiving ribociclib prior to surgery. The QTL is based on RECIST 1.1 criteria and is monitored monthly by the data monitoring committee. If the percentage of patients progressing on neoadjuvant ribociclib approaches the predefined threshold, the core team will review the data and may recommend protocol amendments, adjustments to treatment duration, or additional safety evaluations. If any clinical sign or symptom of progression is detected, a breast MRI or ultrasound is performed for confirmation, and patients with confirmed progression are discontinued from investigational treatment and assigned to the non-responder group to receive adjuvant chemotherapy.

Two interim analyses will be performed at 300 and 800 PYs of follow-up to assess whether outcomes meet predefined efficacy thresholds. These analyses allow early stopping for futility if event rates suggest the trial is unlikely to succeed, ensuring efficient resource use and patient safety. This approach is consistent with methodologies used in prior de-escalation trials, including the APT trial 68 and the POSITIVE trial, 69 which incorporated interim assessments within their study designs. The primary analysis will occur after at least 1300 PYs of follow-up in the ROR-low cohort. Using a Poisson-based approach, the study will test whether the observed event rate supports the hypothesis of a 5-year DMFS of approximately 96%, which exceeds the historical benchmark of 90.6%. Success will be declared if the observed event rate is statistically lower than the null threshold, confirming that a chemotherapy-free approach can achieve excellent long-term outcomes.

The sample size and follow-up duration were chosen to provide at least 90% power to detect this difference while controlling for type I error. Approximately 400 ROR-low patients are needed for the primary analysis, requiring enrollment of about 1100 patients overall, based on expected ROR-low prevalence and potential dropouts. Sensitivity analyses will explore more conservative assumptions (e.g., 92%–94% DMFS) to ensure robustness of conclusions.

Secondary and exploratory analyses will provide additional insights, including the relationship between ROR scores and DMFS, iDFS, and pCR (Table 5). Biomarker studies will aim to identify predictors of response or resistance, and safety will be assessed in all patients who receive any study treatment. These analyses will help contextualize the primary findings and inform future strategies for tailoring therapy in HR+/HER2− breast cancer.

Tissue and blood sampling

Tumor tissue for molecular characterization is mandatory at baseline and surgery (FFPE; archival or newly obtained); tumor sample will be used for tumor cellularity and TIL assessment, gene expression analysis (PAM50 intrinsic subtype assessment), and assessment of biomarkers such as Ki67 and ER IHC. If the initial tumor biopsy is not available, then re-biopsy is mandatory.

Blood sampling for circulating tumor DNA and thymidine kinase 1 activity analyses is mandatory at C1D1, C2D1, pre-surgery (C6D1), post-surgery (C1D1 of chemotherapy in ROR-med/high cohort patients; C7D1 in all patients), and every 6 months (C14, C20, C26, C32, C38, and 30-day post-ribociclib safety visit) during the adjuvant period. Samples (except C1D1) must be collected within 48 h prior dosing. Additional blood samples will be collected in case of recurrence.

Discussion

The CDK4/6 inhibitors, palbociclib, ribociclib, and abemaciclib, are the recommended standard treatment options for patients with HR+/HER2− advanced breast cancer due to their significant improvements in progression-free survival when used as first- or later-line therapy.33,35,37–39,70 In phase III studies, ribociclib consistently demonstrated a significant improvement in OS.71–73 However, both abemaciclib (MONARCH-3) 38 and palbociclib74,75 did not show a significant overall survival benefit in the first-line setting for patients with HR+/HER2− advanced breast cancer. These clinical benefits of CDK4/6i in advanced breast cancer settings had led to a considerable interest in their potential role in treating early-stage breast cancer. However, adjuvant studies of CDK4/6i in combination with endocrine therapy in eBC led to conflicting survival results, particularly when the vast majority of patients had also received chemotherapy. The phase III studies evaluating the efficacy and safety of abemaciclib with endocrine therapy (monarchE) 57 or ribociclib with endocrine therapy (NATALEE) 63 showed improvement in iDFS whereas palbociclib eBC trials demonstrated negative results (PALLAS and PENELOPE-B).10,55,56

Interestingly, data from the neoadjuvant trials UNICANCER-0140/1404 NeoPAL 41 and SOLTI-1402 CORALLEEN 40 suggested that a group of patients with high genomic risk could avoid chemotherapy without a detrimental effect on iDFS. 66 Preoperative endocrine treatment may be used to select patients in whom chemotherapy could be spared by using surrogate markers, Ki67 and Recurrence Score, to identify a good prognosis group for which endocrine treatment alone is sufficient to achieve a very high rate of curability. 42 Improving the prognosis of patients with early-stage breast cancer is necessary as recurrences are still common in high-risk patients treated with endocrine therapy and chemotherapy. Identifying such patients with initial high-risk clinicopathological breast cancer who do not require chemotherapy to avoid related toxicities and improving their prognosis remains an unmet medical need.

The RIBOLARIS trial is the first prospective study to evaluate a chemotherapy-free strategy in a population with initially high clinical risk, using molecular response to neoadjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition plus endocrine therapy as a decision point. By integrating real-time genomic risk assessment into the treatment algorithm, RIBOLARIS has the potential to redefine standard care for a substantial subset of patients who would otherwise be routinely offered chemotherapy.

In this study, we hypothesized that chemotherapy could be appropriately spared in patients with initial high-risk clinicopathological tumors that are converted to a low genomic ROR (ROR-low) after neoadjuvant treatment with ribociclib and letrozole by continuing this treatment in the adjuvant setting. Patients classified as high risk according to clinicopathological factors such as stage II, grade 2 or 3, Ki67 ⩾20% and who, therefore, are common candidates to receive neo/adjuvant chemotherapy are included.

This study represents a significant step forward in the personalization of therapy for high-risk, HR+/HER2− EBC. Its most distinctive feature is the use of genomic risk reclassification, specifically, the achievement of a ROR-low after neoadjuvant ribociclib and letrozole to guide the omission of adjuvant chemotherapy. This approach moves beyond traditional clinicopathological criteria and leverages dynamic molecular profiling to tailor treatment intensity, aiming to spare patients from the toxicities of chemotherapy when it is unlikely to provide additional benefit.

The neoadjuvant treatment duration consists of six cycles to 28 days of daily ribociclib 600-mg dose and letrozole, and the adjuvant treatment phase with ribociclib 400-mg dose for 33 cycles and endocrine therapy for at least 5 years duration will be received by patients with ROR-low. ROR and pathological state after surgery will determine the adjuvant treatment option. Patients with ROR-medium/high will receive adjuvant standard chemotherapy before pursuing adjuvant ribociclib and endocrine therapy. Additionally, prolonged treatment duration has shown the ability to reduce proliferation, evidencing good prognosis; hence, we considered the need for a prolonged dual proliferation blockade with ribociclib and letrozole to maintain the benefit demonstrated in the neoadjuvant setting. 48

Neoadjuvant treatment duration was based on the CORALLEEN trial outcomes in postmenopausal patients with stage I–IIIA luminal B (PAM-50) HR+/HER2− eBC with a total duration of 24 weeks of neoadjuvant treatment that is, either a six 28-day cycle of ribociclib 600 mg and letrozole or 4 cycles of doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide every 21 days, followed by weekly paclitaxel for 12 weeks. The neoadjuvant treatment with ribociclib and letrozole led to molecular downstaging in a high proportion of patients with clinically high-risk luminal B breast cancer. 40 Similar results were achieved in the NeoPAL trial. 45

Based on preclinical and clinical data, treatment of ribociclib in the given dosage regimen when combined with endocrine therapy is expected to be tolerable and toxicities of the treatment are expected to be manageable and reversible with treatment interruption, ribociclib dose reduction, or discontinuation. To consider the potential risk of avoiding chemotherapy in those patients who initially respond to neoadjuvant ribociclib and letrozole (ROR-Low), two interim analyses at 300 and 800 PYs of follow-up will be performed for early stopping of the ROR-low arm. Moreover, patients with stage III disease have been excluded from this study to prevent the risk of avoiding chemotherapy in this population.

We estimate a sample size of approximately 1100 patients required in the study based on CORALLEEN data, considering a ROR-low prevalence of around 40% and a 10% potential dropout in the neoadjuvant phase. We assume that the sample size might allow an increased level of evidence of avoiding chemotherapy in a group of patients with intermediate/high-risk HR+/HER2− breast cancer that can be treated solely with endocrine therapy and ribociclib. Recently reported positive results from the NATALEE study also reinforces this rationale, suggesting that the increase in sample size will improve the precision estimation in the 95% CI of the 3 and 5-year DMFS rate.8,62,76

DMFS has been used as the main endpoint in the validation of different genetic signatures, such as Prosigna-PAM50 or Mammaprint. The adjuvant MINDACT trial aimed to prospectively validate MammaPrint, a 70-gene signature, with the core hypothesis that a patient deemed to be at high ROR according to clinical criteria should receive chemotherapy, and a MammaPrint result suggesting low-risk disease would indicate that chemotherapy can be avoided. In this trial, patients with tumors categorized as having high clinical risk and high genomic risk (N = 1805) received chemotherapy and had a 5-year DMFS of 90.6% (95% CI: 89.1–91.9). Of note, the MINDACT study was an adjuvant trial and that the clinically high-risk population (high clinical and low genomic risk) included 41.9% tumors of size ⩽2 cm, 6.3% of grade 1 tumors, and 52.4% of node negative. The population to be included in the RIBOLARIS will be the population with intermediate/high risk of relapse: candidate population for neoadjuvant treatment, stage II, grade 2 or 3, Ki67 ⩾20%. Given that RIBOLARIS includes a higher-risk population than MINDACT, using the 5-year DMFS of 90.6% from the MINDACT subgroup with both high clinical and genomic risk, as a benchmark to support the possibility of avoiding chemotherapy is considered reasonable.77,78 In contrast, RIBOLARIS includes patients who are initially high-risk but convert to ROR-low following neoadjuvant ribociclib and letrozole, a population not directly represented in MINDACT. Therefore, while the 90.6% benchmark provides a conservative comparator, it may underestimate the baseline risk in RIBOLARIS. To account for this, appropriate contextualization and sensitivity analyses are warranted. Sensitivity analyses are planned to explore the impact of more conservative expectations (e.g., 5-year DMFS of 92% or 94%) on statistical power and interpretation. These analyses will help assess the robustness of the primary endpoint under varying assumptions. It is also acknowledged that ROR is a hypothesis-generating biomarker and not yet accepted as a regulatory endpoint. Thus, the study is designed to generate evidence supporting the clinical utility of ROR conversion as a surrogate for long-term outcome. Recent meta-analysis confirms that DMFS is highly correlated with overall survival and is a valid surrogate endpoint in HR+ eBC, supporting its use as the primary endpoint in RIBOLARIS. 79

This study includes an analysis of tumor samples using the PAM50 subtype to identify the molecular intrinsic subtypes and provides the ROR score for calculating the probability of recurrence at 5 years. The hypothesis-generating marker, ROR predictor (PAM50 RUO, not accepted as regulatory endpoint), as part of the protocol procedures will determine the ROR and will accordingly determine the treatment in the adjuvant setting. After neoadjuvant treatment, patients will be considered low-risk if the central laboratory confirms ROR-low in the surgical specimen. Patients will be considered intermediate/high-risk if the central laboratory confirms ROR-med/high in the surgical specimen. Missing data, such as no ROR results, will be addressed using imputation techniques, while non-compliance will be managed through both per-protocol and intention-to-treat analyses. Crossover events will be handled using censoring or advanced statistical models. Intercurrent events, like non-compliance, will be managed with sensitivity analyses, and missing data will be minimized through rigorous follow-up, employing multiple imputation or mixed-effects models when necessary.

The study’s strengths include its innovative design, robust statistical plan, and focus on long-term outcomes that matter to patients and clinicians. The use of DMFS as the primary endpoint aligns with the goal of reducing overtreatment while maintaining excellent disease control. The inclusion of interim futility analyses ensures patient safety and resource efficiency, while planned sensitivity analyses will help contextualize the findings under varying assumptions of benefit.

Of the limitations, the reliance on ROR as a decision-making biomarker, while supported by prior studies, remains hypothesis-generating and is not yet an established regulatory endpoint. The open-label design introduces the potential for bias, and there is a risk of selection bias if patients who achieve ROR-low status differ systematically from those who do not, possibly due to unmeasured baseline characteristics. The generalizability of results may be limited by the exclusion of patients with stage III disease and by the requirement for high-quality tissue samples for molecular analysis. The absence of prespecified HRQoL/PRO endpoints is a study limitation, as these are particularly relevant in treatment de-escalation. Additionally, while locoregional treatment is an essential component of care following neoadjuvant therapy, the protocol publication does not include detailed, protocolized standards for surgery or radiotherapy, nor does it prespecify locoregional outcomes such as breast-conserving surgery rates. Given that enrollment has been completed, systematic capture of these outcomes in line with emerging core outcome recommendations is not feasible, and this should be considered when interpreting trial results.

The study also faces operational challenges, such as ensuring timely central pathology review and managing intercurrent events or missing data, which are addressed through rigorous statistical methods but remain potential sources of uncertainty. The protocol did not incorporate formal patient and public involvement in the development of the study design, outcome selection, or definition of the target 5‑year DMFS threshold. Patient preferences regarding acceptable trade‑offs between chemotherapy avoidance and recurrence risk may vary, and the absence of structured patient/advocacy input should be considered when interpreting the clinical relevance of the selected efficacy threshold. To mitigate this limitation, a patient advocate will be incorporated into the study Steering Committee to provide patient-centered perspectives during ongoing oversight and future study development. More broadly, future de-escalation trials should prospectively integrate patient and advocacy engagement, along with patient-centered outcomes, to better align study endpoints and decision thresholds with patient values.

Conclusion

RIBOLARIS is a prospective phase II trial evaluating the prognostic value of ROR status in residual tumors after neoadjuvant ribociclib and letrozole (NCT05296746). It is the first prospective trial to enroll patients with grade 2/3, Ki67 ⩾20%, and stage II HR+/HER2− eBC and categorized based on post-neoadjuvant therapy ROR-score, aiming to identify patients for whom chemotherapy may be spared, which is crucial for managing treatment burden. Thus, the RIBOLARIS study hypothesizes that chemotherapy could be safely spared in patients with initial high-risk clinicopathological stage II tumors that are converted to a low genomic ROR (ROR-low) after neoadjuvant treatment with ribociclib and letrozole by continuing with this treatment in the adjuvant setting.

A significant treatment paradigm shift has been achieved for high-risk TNBC and HER2-positive eBC by employing neoadjuvant treatment to guide the selection of appropriate postoperative therapy, depending on the effectiveness of the neoadjuvant treatment. This shift has revolutionized the way we approach the early-stage breast cancer, allowing for a more tailored and efficient treatment strategy. Ultimately, the RIBOLARIS study may serve as proof of concept that a neoadjuvant treatment in high-risk luminal eBC helps guide the most appropriate post-operative treatment. Additionally, RIBOLARIS also tackles the need to improve prognosis and quality of life in patients with high-risk stage II HR+/HER2− eBC.

Achieving uniform side information gain with multilevel lattice codes over the ring of integers
Juliana G. F. Souza, and Sueli I. R. Costa

Abstract
The index coding problem aims to optimise broadcast communication by taking advantage of receiver-side information to improve transmission efficiency. In this letter, we explore the application of Construction π A \pi_{A} lattices to index coding. We introduce a coding scheme, named CRT lattice index coding , using Construction π A \pi_{A} over ℤ \mathbb{Z} to address the index coding problem. It is derived an upper bound for side information gain of a CRT lattice index code and conditions for the uniformity of this gain. The efficiency of this approach is shown through theoretical analysis and code design examples.

Lattices and lattice codes

We summarise next some concepts and properties related to lattice codes
[

A

.

of
\Lambda

\text{vol}(\Lambda)=|\det(B)|

Given a point

and a lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

Q_{\Lambda}(z)
as the

to
z
, also called the quantization of

Q_{\Lambda}(z)=x\in\Lambda;\ ||z-x||\leq||z-y||\quad\forall y\in\Lambda,

(1)

and ties must be chosen.
The Voronoi region
of a lattice
\Lambda

that are mapped to the origin under

\mathscr{V}_{\Lambda}(0)=\mathscr{V}_{\Lambda}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\|z\|\leq\|z-y\|\ \forall y\in\Lambda\}.

Considering a sublattice

\Lambda

\Lambda^{\prime}
.

The

d_{\text{min}}(\Lambda)=\min_{0\neq x\in\Lambda}||x||\ \ \text{and}\ \ \delta(\Lambda)=\dfrac{\left(d_{\min}(\Lambda)/2\right)^{n}}{\text{vol}(\Lambda)}.

(2)

The

A

\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, the ring of integers modulo

\mathscr{C}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
which is an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
. The rank of a code,
\text{rank}(\mathscr{C})

, called a
(n,k)-

\mathscr{C}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}

\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.

A method for obtaining lattices from linear codes is the

[
12
,
13
]

\rho:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, the natural modulo
q

\sigma:\mathbb{Z}_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

\mathscr{C}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}

\mathscr{C}
, denoted by
\Lambda_{A}(\mathscr{C})
, is defined as

\Lambda_{A}(\mathscr{C})=\rho^{-1}(\mathscr{C})=\sigma(\mathscr{C})+q\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(3)

It is shown that
\Lambda_{A}(\mathscr{C})
is a full-rank lattice,
q\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\Lambda_{A}(\mathscr{C})\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\text{vol}(\Lambda)=q^{n}/|\mathscr{C}|
, where
|\mathscr{C}|
is the cardinality of the code
\mathscr{C}
and
d_{\min}(\Lambda_{A}(\mathscr{C}))=\min\{d_{\min}(\mathscr{C}),q\}
,
[
13
]
.

Huang and Narayanan
introduced Construction
\pi_{A}

[

Definition 1

(Construction
\pi_{A}
lattice,
[
9
]
)
.

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
be distinct primes and let
q=\prod_{j=1}^{r}p_{j}
. Consider
l_{j},n
to be integers such that
l_{j}\leq n
and let

be a generator matrix of an
(n,l_{j})

\mathbb{Z}_{p_{j}}

consists of the following steps: 1) Define the discrete codebooks

.

Due to their multilevel structure, Construction

III
Problem Statement

Consider a scenario with
r
independent messages
w_{1},\ldots,w_{r}
from alphabets
W_{1},\ldots,W_{r}
. These messages are jointly encoded into a codeword
x=f(w_{1},\ldots,w_{r})\in\mathscr{C}
, where
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
n
-dimensional constellation. The received signal at receiver
l
,
l=1,...,L
is

y_{l}=x+z_{l},\quad z_{l}\sim N(0,\sigma^{2}).

Each receiver
l
knows a subset
w_{S_{l}}=\{w_{j}:j\in S_{l}\}
of the messages, with
S_{l}\subset\{1,\ldots,r\}
, and estimates
(\hat{w}_{1}^{(l)},\ldots,\hat{w}_{r}^{(l)})
of
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{r})
using
y_{l}
and
w_{S_{l}}
. This setup is illustrated in Figure
1
.

The rates of the
j
-th message, the side information rate, and the transmission rate are,

R_{j}=\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}|W_{j}|\ \text{bits/dim},\quad R_{S_{l}}=\sum_{j\in S_{l}}R_{j}\ \text{bits/dim},\quad R=\sum_{j=1}^{r}R_{j}\ \text{bits/dim},
respectively.

Let
\mathscr{C}_{S_{l}}
denote the subcode of
\mathscr{C}
restricted to
w_{S_{l}}
. The minimum Euclidean distance in
\mathscr{C}
is denoted by
d_{0}
, and in
\mathscr{C}_{S_{l}}
by
d_{S_{l}}
. The squared minimum distance gain for
w_{S_{l}}
is defined as,

10\log_{10}\left(d_{S_{l}}^{2}/d_{0}^{2}\right)\ \text{dB}.
The side information gain at receiver
l
is given by
[
5
,
10
]
,

\Gamma(\mathscr{C},S_{l})=\frac{10\log_{10}\left(d_{S_{l}}^{2}/d_{0}^{2}\right)}{R_{S_{l}}}\ \text{dB/bit/dim},

and the overall gain is,

\Gamma(\mathscr{C})=\min_{S_{l}}\Gamma(\mathscr{C},S_{l})\ \text{dB/bit/dim}.

We say that a lattice index code provides
uniform gain
if
\Gamma(\mathscr{C},S_{l})
is constant for all
S_{l}
,
[
5
]
.

At high SNR, the error probability for a lattice
\Lambda
with minimum Euclidean distance
d_{\min}(\Lambda)
and kissing number

is approximated by,

P_{e}(\Lambda,\sigma^{2})\approx K(\Lambda)\cdot Q\left(\sqrt{\frac{d_{\min}^{2}(\Lambda)}{4\sigma^{2}}}\right),

where
Q(\cdot)
is the Gaussian
Q
-function. The symbol error rate (SER) is,
\text{SER}(\Lambda,\sigma^{2})\approx\frac{1}{n}P_{e}(\Lambda,\sigma^{2})
,
[
14
]
.

Given a lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\to\Lambda/\Lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle(w_{1},\ldots,w_{r})

\displaystyle\mapsto x=(w_{1}+\cdots+w_{r})\mod\Lambda^{\prime},

(4)

where

shows that if

IV
CRT lattice index coding

In this section, it is introduced the lattice index coding scheme derived from Construction
\pi_{A}
and presented its main properties.

IV-A

Main properties

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
be distinct primes and let
q=\prod_{j=1}^{r}p_{j}
. Consider
\mathscr{C}_{j}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p_{j}}^{n}
linear codes with
\text{rank}(\mathscr{C}_{j})=k_{j}
,
j=1,\dots r
. We will denote as
\Lambda_{j}=\Lambda_{A}(\mathscr{C}_{j})
and
\Lambda=\Lambda_{\pi_{A}}(\mathscr{C})
, the Construction A and Construction
\pi_{A}
lattice of each code
\mathscr{C}_{j}
and
\mathscr{C}
, respectively, as in (
3
) and
Definition
1
. The bijective map
\phi^{-1}
, in
Definition
1
, restricted to the product
\mathscr{C}_{1}\times\dots\mathscr{C}_{r}
can be written as,

\displaystyle\Psi:

\displaystyle\ \Lambda_{1}/p_{1}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\ldots\times\Lambda_{r}/p_{r}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\Lambda/q\mathbb{Z}^{n}

\displaystyle(w_{1},\ldots,w_{r})\mapsto\ \sum_{j=1}^{r}x_{j}m_{j}w_{j}\mod q\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(5)

with
x_{j}
obtained from Bèzout identity and
m_{j}=q/p_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots r
. We call a lattice code obtained by (
5
) as a
CRT lattice index code
. Since the cardinality of each code is given by
|\mathscr{C}_{j}|=p_{j}^{k_{j}},\ j=1,\ldots,r
, then the cardinality of
\mathscr{C}=\Psi(\mathscr{C}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathscr{C}_{r})
becomes
|\mathscr{C}|=\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\mathscr{C}_{j}|=\prod_{j=1}^{r}p_{j}^{k_{j}}
,
\text{rank}(\mathscr{C})=\max\{k_{j}\}
and the code
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
is a free linear code if, and only if
\mathscr{C}_{j}
is free with the same rank
k
for all
j
,
[
15
, Thm 2.4]
.

For brevity in notation, let
S
denote the set of side information at receiver
l
, we have that the rate of the
j^{th}
message is given by,

\displaystyle R_{j}=\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}|\Lambda_{j}/p_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{n}|=\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}|\mathscr{C}_{j}|=\log_{2}p_{j}^{k_{j}/n}\text{bit}/\text{dim}.

The subcodes
\mathscr{C}_{S}\subset\mathscr{C}
can be characterised as,

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{S}=\{\Psi(w_{1},\ldots,w_{r});w_{j}=v_{j},j\in S,w_{j}\in\mathscr{C},j\notin S\},

assuming that the receiver
l
has knowledge of the side information
w_{j}=v_{j}
, for
j\in S
.

For example, when
S=\{1,\dots,s\}
then
w_{i}=v_{i}
, for
i\in S
we have
\Psi(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{s},w_{s+1},\ldots,w_{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}x_{i}m_{i}v_{i}\mod q\mathbb{Z}^{n}+\Psi(0,\ldots,0,w_{s+1},\ldots,w_{r})

what is a shifted version of
\Psi(0,\ldots,0,w_{s+1},\ldots,w_{r})
.

Denoting the complement of
S
as
S^{c}
, let
\Lambda_{S^{c}}=\sum_{j\in S^{c}}\Lambda_{j}
be the lattice obtained by the sum of
\Lambda_{j},j\in S^{c}
, then, the subcode used for decoding at the receiver
l
is,

\mathscr{C}_{S}=\left\{\left(\sum_{j\in S}x_{j}m_{j}v_{j}+\Lambda_{S^{c}}\right)\mod q\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right\},

that is,
\mathscr{C}_{S}
is obtained by the translation of the Voronoi constellation
\Lambda_{S^{c}}/q\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by the vector
\sum_{j\in S}x_{j}m_{j}v_{j}\mod q\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and the minimum distance in
\mathscr{C}_{S}
is
d_{S}=d_{\text{min}}\left(\Lambda_{S^{c}}\right)
.

The next proposition establishes the main properties of the CRT lattice index codes.

Proposition 1
.

For the lattices
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{r}
and
\Lambda
as in (
5
), the following is true, (i)
\text{vol}(\Lambda_{S^{c}})=\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}^{k_{j}}\text{vol}(\Lambda)
; (ii)
d_{0}\leq d_{S}\leq\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}d_{0}
, where
d_{S}=d_{\min}(\Lambda_{S^{c}})
and
d_{0}=d_{\min}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

(i) Consider
S=\{s+1,\ldots,r\}
and
S^{c}=\{1,\ldots,s\}
,

\frac{\text{vol}(\Lambda_{S^{c}})}{\text{vol}(\Lambda)}=\frac{q^{n}/\prod_{j\in S^{c}}p_{j}^{k_{j}}}{q^{n}/\prod_{j=1}^{r}p_{j}^{k_{j}}}=\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}^{k_{j}}.

(ii) Since
\sum_{j\in S^{c}}\Lambda_{j}\subset\Lambda
, it follows that
d_{0}\leq d_{S}
. Moreover, the Construction A of
\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}\cdot\mathscr{C}
we have,

\Lambda_{A}\left(\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}\cdot\mathscr{C}\right)=\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}\left(\Lambda_{S^{c}}\right)\subset\sum_{j\in S^{c}}\Lambda_{j}.

Hence,

d_{\min}\left(\Lambda_{A}\left(\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}\cdot\mathscr{C}\right)\right)=\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}\cdot d_{0}

and then,
d_{S}\leq\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}\cdot d_{0}.

∎

Remark 1
.

If we consider
k=n
, the CRT lattice index code introduced here is equivalent to those described in
[
5
]
, specifically for
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Therefore, to explore cases not addressed in the literature, we will focus exclusively on the case
k<n
.

IV-B

An upper bound for side information gain

To analyze the side information gain, let
S
be the set of side information at receiver
l
and
d_{S}=d_{\min}(\Lambda_{S^{c}})
be the minimum distance of the subcode
\mathscr{C}_{S}
. Since
R=\sum_{j=1}^{r}R_{j}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\log_{2}|\Lambda_{j}/p_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{n}|
, it follows that,

R=\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}|\Lambda/q\mathbb{Z}^{n}|.

The side information rate for this receiver
l
is,

R_{S}=R-\sum_{j\in S^{c}}R_{j}=\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}\left(\frac{\text{vol}(\Lambda_{S^{c}})}{\text{vol}(\Lambda)}\right).

As shown in
[
5
]
, the centre densities of
\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{S^{c}}
allow us to derive a bound for the side information gain. If
\delta(\Lambda)\geq\delta(\Lambda_{S^{c}})
for all
S
, then,

\Gamma(\mathscr{C})=\min_{S}\frac{10\log_{10}(d_{S}^{2}/d_{0}^{2})}{R_{S}}\leq 20\log_{10}2\approx 6.02\text{ dB/bit/dim}.

However,
\delta(\Lambda)\geq\delta(\Lambda_{S^{c}})
is not always true, but it is still possible to derive an upper bound.

Consider the special case of CRT lattice index code when
\text{rank}(\mathscr{C}_{j})=k
for all
j=1,\dots r
. Since, by
Proposition
1
(i)
,
\text{vol}(\Lambda_{S^{c}})/\text{vol}(\Lambda)=\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}^{k}
, then the side information rate is,

R_{S}=\log_{2}\left(\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}^{k/n}\right).

From
Proposition
1
(ii)
, we obtain,
\log_{10}(d_{S}/d_{0})\leq\log_{10}\left(\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}^{k/n}\right)
, then,

\Gamma(\mathscr{C})\leq 20\log_{10}2\cdot\frac{\log_{10}\left(\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}\right)}{\log_{10}\left(\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}^{k/n}\right)}\leq\frac{n}{k}\cdot 20\log_{10}2.

(6)

This provides an upper bound for the side information gain of a CRT lattice index coding derived from the same rank codes. A previous analysis of this bound, considering codes with different ranks, can be found in
[
16
]
.

V
CRT lattice index coding with uniform side information gain

In this section, we construct a CRT lattice index code with uniform side information gain. To achieve this, we consider that all codes
\mathscr{C}_{j}
in this construction must have the same rank
k
. Additionally, we restrict the generators of the codes to ensure compatible minimum distances for any index set
S
.

First, we focus on codes generated by
k
linearly independent vectors over
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{j}}^{n}
with minimum distance
1
and construct a CRT lattice index code
\mathscr{C}
such that
d_{\min}(\mathscr{C})=1
.

One way to achieve this is as follows. Considering linear codes
\mathscr{C}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{C}_{r}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}}^{n},\dots,\mathbb{Z}_{p_{r}}^{n}
, each generated by
k
canonical vectors with
\mathbf{e}_{l}
a common generator for all codes. For each
\mathscr{C}_{j}
, we have
d_{\min}(\mathscr{C}_{j})=1,j=1,\dots r
.

Let
\mathscr{C}=\Psi(\mathscr{C}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathscr{C}_{r})
be the CRT lattice index coding obtained by
\mathscr{C}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{C}_{r}
. Since
\mathbf{e}_{l}
belongs to all
\mathscr{C}_{j}
, the minimum distance of
\mathscr{C}
is achieved with
c=\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}m_{i}\mathbf{e}_{l}\mod q=\mathbf{e}_{l}
, resulting in
d_{\min}(\mathscr{C})=1
. This code achieves the upper bound for side information gain in (
6
). In fact, for any subset
S
,
d_{\min}(\mathscr{C}_{S})=1
then we have,

d_{S}=\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}\cdot d_{\min}(\mathscr{C}_{S})=\prod_{j\in S}p_{j},

and the corresponding side information rate is,

R_{S}=\log_{2}\left(\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}^{k/n}\right)=\frac{k}{n}\log_{2}\left(d_{S}/d_{0}\right).

Thus, for all
S
, the side information gain is,

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathscr{C})

\displaystyle=\frac{20\log_{10}(d_{S}/d_{0})}{(k/n)\log_{2}(d_{S}/d_{0})}=\frac{n}{k}\cdot 20\log_{10}2.

Note that for sufficiently large dimension and using codes with rank
k=n-1
, when
n\rightarrow\infty
we approach
\approx 6.02
dB/bit/dim as expected.

A different analysis for uniform side information gain can be made for rank-
1
codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
. In this case, we will focus on numbers expressible both as a sum of
N
squares and as a product of primes, where each prime
p_{j}
can also be written as a sum of
N
squares, i.e.,

q=\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{2}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}p_{j},\quad p_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(a_{i}^{(j)})^{2}.

(7)

This problem relates to the multiplicative quadratic form problem
[
17
, Chap. 2]
, and has explicit solutions for
N=2,4,8
. We consider the CRT lattice index code from codes
\mathscr{C}_{j}=\langle g_{j}\rangle=\langle(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\mod p_{j}\rangle\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p_{j}}^{N}
. The sum of squares condition with other requirements ensures uniform side information gain in CRT lattice index coding, which depends on the number of squares considered as shown next.

Lemma 1
.

Let
q
be an integer that can be expressed as a sum of
N
squares, i.e.,
q=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{N}^{2}
. Then for the linear code
\mathscr{C}=\langle(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\rangle
we have
d_{\min}(\mathscr{C})=\sqrt{q}
.

Proof.

Let
g=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
, with
\|g\|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{N}^{2}=q
. We have that
\mathscr{C}=\{x=u\cdot g;u\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{N}
satisfies
d_{\min}(\mathscr{C})=\sqrt{q}
because
\|x\|^{2}\mod q=0
then
\min\|x\|^{2}=q
achieved for
x=g
.
∎

An additional requirement is that for the codes
\mathscr{C}_{j}
,
d_{\min}(\mathscr{C}_{j})=\sqrt{p_{j}}

Let

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(b_{1}^{(k)})^{2}+\dots+(b_{N}^{(k)})^{2}=\displaystyle\prod_{\{k_{1},\dots,k_{L}\}\subset I}\hskip-17.07182ptp_{k_{1}}\dots p_{k_{L}}\\

with
k=1,\dots,2^{r}-1

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let
S=\{s+1,\dots,r\}

Lemma
1

The distance

Therefore we can derive the side information rate,

R_{S}=\log_{2}\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}^{m/mN}=\frac{2}{N}\log_{2}\prod_{j\in S}p_{j}^{1/2}=\frac{2}{N}\log_{2}\left(\frac{d_{S}}{d_{0}}\right),

VI
Conclusion and Perspectives

In this work, we explore the application of Construction
\pi_{A}
lattices over
\mathbb{Z}
to the index coding problem, presenting the CRT lattice index coding scheme. This approach can take advantage of the multilevel nature of Construction
\pi_{A}
and enlarge the scenario of index coding over
\mathbb{Z}
as considered in
[
5
]
. It is shown an upper bound for the side information gain when the Construction
\pi_{A}
is considered over same rank codes and identified some codes that achieve this gain. It is established conditions that assure uniform side information gain.

Perspectives for future work, include to investigate index codes using Construction
\pi_{A}
over other rings of integers such as Gaussian integers, Eisenstein integers, and maximal orders in quaternion algebras to determine conditions required for achieving uniform side information gain and bounds that can be obtained using these structures.

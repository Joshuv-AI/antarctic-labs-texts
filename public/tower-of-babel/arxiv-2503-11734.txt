On the records and zeros of a deterministic random walk
Henk Bruin and Robbert Fokkink

Abstract
. We settle two questions on sequence A120243 in the OEIS that were raised by Clark Kimberling and partly solve a conjecture of Van de Lune and Arias de Reyna. We extend Kimberling’s questions to the framework of deterministic random walks, automatic sequences, and linear recurrences. Our results indicate that there may be a deeper connection between these structures. In particular, we conjecture that the records of deterministic random walks are ξ \xi -Ostrowski automatic for a quadratic rotation number ξ \xi .

The sequences

Given this, the next point of interest is

Such deviations from the mean are known as

His other question on the signature of

For

If we apply this to

and the number of

is unbounded. This implies that

and

are both unbounded.

Sós

observed that the discrepancy

Recall the (regular) continued fraction of a real number

\xi=a_{0}+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}+\cfrac{1}{a_{3}+\cfrac{1}{\ddots}}}}

Recursive rules for
S_{n}(2\sqrt{2})

Rule A:
S_{q}=1
if

is odd and
S_{q}=0
if
q
is even.

Rule B:

Rule C:

An explanation for these rules is that

is a close return to 0 mod 1. If
q
is

[0,1/2)
.

Rule B determines

for

S_{n}
for

.
Since

these rules, together with Rule A, determine all values

by recursion.
The parity of the Pell numbers alternates. If
q
is odd than

is even and vice versa.
If the interval

is marked by an even

It follows that there is no record in

is odd and

is even.
Notice that a Pell number

is even if and only if its index is even.

Theorem 8
.

The zeros of
S_{n}(2\sqrt{2})
occur at
n=0
or
(10|20)(00|10|20)^{*}
in Pell numeration.

Proof.

There cannot be a zero in
[q^{\prime},q]
if
q^{\prime}
is an odd Pell number because rules

B
and
C
add
S_{q^{\prime}}(\sqrt{2})=1
to previous values, and these are non-negative.
Zeros occur in
[q^{\prime},q]
for even Pell numbers
q^{\prime}
, starting at
q^{\prime}

following rule A.
Note that rules B and C partition this interval
into numbers with initial digits 1 and 2 in Pell numeration.

We assume by induction that the earlier
zeros all are of the required form.
If rule C applies, then
S_{q^{\prime}+k}(\sqrt{2})=0

if and only if
S_{k}(\sqrt{2})=0
, so indeed all zeros in this part
of

are of the form

.
For rule B we use the identity

P_{2m}-1=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}2P_{2i}.

We rewrite rule B as
S_{(q-1)-k)}(\sqrt{2})=S_{k}(\sqrt{2})
, using that
q^{\prime}

is an even Pell number. In particular,
q-1-k
is a zero if and only if
k

is a zero. Now
q-1
is equal to

in Pell numeration and

by our inductive assumption. It follows that
(q-1)-k=(20)(00|10|20)^{m-1}

and we are done.

We established a connection between deterministic random walks and automata in certain cases, but much remains to be explored. The general deterministic random walk is defined by

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{\lfloor j\xi+\gamma\rfloor}.

We only considered the homogeneous case with offset

and

is rational? Interestingly, the set of zeros can be empty for such random walks. Ralston
[

]
proved that for every
\xi
there exists a unique

\gamma
such that the random walk in Equation

is positive.

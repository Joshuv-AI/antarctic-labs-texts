Bulk Fabrication and Coercivity Enhancement of Ce-Based RE2Fe14B Prepared by a Modified Reduction–Diffusion Process
Authors: Sunwoo Lee, Kanghyuk Lee, Sang-Im Yoo, Jung-Woo Lee, Chan Park
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Rare-earth (RE) permanent magnets, particularly Nd2Fe14B, are essential for various high-performance applications. The supply risks and high price of critical rare-earth elements like Nd, however, have driven the search for alternative RE2Fe14B (2-14-1) compounds for reducing or replacing the Nd content. In our previous work, we developed a modified reduction–diffusion (R–D) process for synthesizing 2-14-1 phases. Nevertheless, fabricating bulk magnets with enhanced magnetic properties using this method remains challenging due to phase decomposition during sintering. In this study, we directly address this challenge, implementing a dual strategy: particle size refinement and grain boundary engineering. After synthesizing 2-14-1 compounds through the modified R–D process, we applied high-energy ball-milling to refine the particle size, followed by heat-treatment with Ca as a reducing agent to suppress phase decomposition and promote phase recovery. Further improvement of the magnetic properties was achieved through the addition of a low-melting-point Nd70Al20Cu10 alloy, which facilitated the formation of a uniform grain boundary. This modification enhanced magnetic decoupling, resulting in a significant increase in coercivity up to 10 kOe for Ce-based 2-14-1. This comprehensive approach presents a promising pathway for the development of cost-effective and high-performance Ce-based permanent magnets, contributing to the advancement of efficient rare-earth magnets.

Introduction

The demand for high-performance permanent magnets continues to increase due to their essential role in clean energy technologies, including electric vehicles and wind turbines. − Nd–Fe–B magnets dominate the high-performance permanent magnet market due to their exceptional magnetic properties, which include high remanence, coercivity, and energy product. The supply risk and high cost of rare-earth elements, particularly heavy rare earths such as Dy and Tb that are critical for enhancing high-temperature performance, however, have become significant concerns. −

Cerium (Ce), which is the most abundant rare-earth element, has gained substantial attention as a potential partial substitute for Nd in RE2Fe14B (2-14-1) permanent magnets. − Ce is relatively inexpensive due to its abundance and is often produced in excess during the extraction of other rare-earth elements. ,, The direct substitution of Ce for Nd in 2-14-1 magnets, however, typically results in the degradation of magnetic properties since the intrinsic properties of Ce2Fe14B (M s = 1.17 T, H a = 26 kOe, Curie temperature = 151 °C) are inferior to those of Nd2Fe14B (M s = 1.6 T, H a = 73 kOe, Curie temperature = 312 °C). −

In addition, the formation of the nonferromagnetic CeFe2 phase should be minimized, which not only reduces the volume fraction of the magnetic 2-14-1 phase but also depletes the excess rare-earth elements required for the formation of grain boundary phases that are essential for high coercivity. , Furthermore, unlike the low-melting-point Nd-rich phase that facilitates liquid-phase sintering and forms continuous grain boundary phases in conventional Nd–Fe–B magnets, the high melting temperature of CeFe2 (925 °C) can lead to poor wettability and insufficient magnetic decoupling between grains.

In our previous work, we successfully synthesized Ce-based 2-14-1 phases using a modified reduction–diffusion (R–D) process with REFeO3 precursors prepared by solid-state reaction. This approach offers several advantages such as low production costs, the use of oxide raw materials, and precise control of elemental composition. Through the development of the modified R–D process, the syntheses of single-phase (Ce1‑x Nd x )2Fe14B (x = 0.0, 0.5 and 1.0) using optimized process conditions were reported. The magnetic properties of the as-synthesized powders, however, were not sufficiently good for practical application due to the large particle sizes, which exceed the size of the single-domain.

To enhance the magnetic properties and enable bulk magnet fabrication, additional processing steps, such as particle size refinement and sintering, are essential. The conventional heat-treatments under vacuum or inert atmospheres, however, often induce phase decomposition in Ce-based 2-14-1 powders, hindering the retention of the desired 2-14-1 phase during densification.

This study builds upon our prior work by addressing these challenges through an integrated strategy combining compositional optimization, particle size refinement, and grain boundary engineering. High-energy ball-milling was applied to reduce the particle size of (Ce1‑x Nd x )2Fe14B powders to the near single-domain regime, followed by optimized heat-treatment using Ca as a reducing agent to prevent phase decomposition and promote the recovery of the 2-14-1 phase. Additionally, the effect of introducing a low-melting-point Nd70Al20Cu10 alloy on the magnetic properties was investigated to facilitate the formation of effective grain boundary phases, thereby improving magnetic decoupling between grains. This comprehensive approach enables the fabrication of Ce-based permanent magnets with significantly enhanced magnetic properties, offering a cost-effective alternative for intermediate-performance applications and helping bridge the performance gap between ferrite and Nd–Fe–B magnets.

Experimental Section

(Ce1‑x Nd x )2Fe14B (Ce1‑x Nd x 2-14-1) compounds were synthesized using the modified reduction–diffusion (R–D) process described in the previous work. REFeO3 (RE = Ce, Nd) precursors were prepared by a solid-state reaction using CeO2, Nd2O3, Fe2O3, and Fe powders with 99.9% purity. The raw materials were weighed according to the desired stoichiometry and ball-milled for 24 h with zirconia balls in ethanol. After drying at 75 °C, the powder mixture was pelletized and heat-treated at 1050 °C for 6 h under an Ar atmosphere. The pellets were then crushed into a powder and mixed with H3BO3 as a boron source, followed by another ball-milling for 24 h.

For the reduction–diffusion (R–D) process, Ca granules (99% purity) were added to the powder mixture at a 1:1 weight ratio. The mixture was pressed into a compact with a diameter of 1 cm and heat-treated at 1050 °C for 6 h under an Ar atmosphere. The final product was then crushed into a powder and washed with deionized water and acetic acid to remove residual Ca and CaO. Finally, the washed powder was dried in vacuum.

The synthesized 2-14-1 powders were high-energy ball-milled using a SPEX mill with stainless steel vials and balls to refine the particle size. Various additives (Ca, FeB, and Nd70Al20Cu10 (NdAlCu)) were added either during or after the high-energy ball-milling process, as shown in Table S1. The ball-milled powders were subsequently pressed into compacts and heat-treated at different temperatures (800, 850, 900, and 1000 °C) for 20 min in vacuum to fabricate bulk samples. Phase analyses were conducted using X-ray diffraction (XRD, D8 Advance, Bruker) with Cu Kα radiation. The magnetic properties were measured at room temperature by using a vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM, VSM-7410, Lake Shore) with a maximum applied field of 2.6 T. The microstructure and composition of the samples were analyzed by using field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM, MERLIN Compact, Zeiss) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS).

Results and Discussion

Based on the previous work, which reported the development of the modified reduction–diffusion (R–D) process for synthesizing Ce2Fe14B (Ce 2-14-1), this study focused on bulk fabrication with enhanced magnetic properties of Ce-based magnets through compositional modification and microstructural engineering. The synthesis of (Ce1‑x Nd x )2Fe14B (Ce1‑x Nd x 2-14-1) compounds demonstrated the successful incorporation of Nd, confirming the compositional flexibility of the modified R–D process.

Initial investigations demonstrated that particle size refinement by high-energy ball milling led to a notable enhancement in the coercivity of the as-synthesized Ce0.5Nd0.5 2-14-1 powders. Nonetheless, significant phase decomposition was observed during the conventional heat-treatment, presenting a major obstacle to bulk magnet fabrication. Preliminary optimization studies with varying Ca content and heat-treatment conditions established that ball-milling with 2.5 wt % Ca followed by heat-treatment at 850 °C for 20 min in vacuum provided the optimal balance between phase recovery and grain growth suppression, though magnetic properties remained insufficient for practical applications (refer to the Supporting Information for additional details).

To investigate the effect of Ce content on the magnetic properties of Ce1‑x Nd x 2-14-1 compounds synthesized by the modified R–D process, powders with varying Ce:Nd ratios (5:5, 4:6, and 3:7) were prepared, and their magnetic properties were compared. This compositional variation was enabled by the solid-state reaction method used to synthesize REFeO3 precursors, which offers the advantage of precise elemental composition control within the R–D process. XRD patterns of the synthesized samples with different Ce:Nd ratios are presented in Figure a. The results confirm the successful formation of single-phase 2-14-1 compounds across all compositions.

As observed in preliminary results (Figure S5), phase recovery to the 2-14-1 phase after high-energy ball-milling was successfully achieved through the addition of Ca granules for Ce0.3Nd0.7, Ce0.4Nd0.6, and Ce0.5Nd0.5 2-14-1 powders (Figure b), using the optimized conditions of 850 °C for 20 min under vacuum with 2.5 wt % Ca. However, as shown in Figure , the magnetic properties revealed that despite successful phase recovery, the coercivity remained relatively low, in the range of 320–350 Oe.

M–H curves of Ce0.3Nd0.7, Ce0.4Nd0.6, and Ce0.5Nd0.5 2-14-1 samples ball-milled with 2.5 wt % Ca and heat-treated at 850 °C for 20 min in vacuum.

While the optimized heat-treatment with Ca addition enables phase recovery and bulk magnet fabrication, the resulting coercivity values remained insufficient for practical applications. To further enhance the magnetic properties, a low-melting-point alloy, Nd70Al20Cu10 (NdAlCu), was added (5 wt %) alongside Ca granules to promote the formation of grain boundary phases and improve the magnetic decoupling between 2-14-1 grains. The resulting powder mixture was then heat-treated at 850 °C for 20 min under a vacuum. As shown in Figure , the combined addition of Ca and NdAlCu successfully facilitated phase recovery across various compositions, including Ce0.3Nd0.7, Ce0.4Nd0.6, and Ce0.5Nd0.5 2-14-1.

XRD analysis results of Ce0.3Nd0.7, Ce0.4Nd0.6, and Ce0.5Nd0.5 2-14-1 samples ball-milled with 2.5 wt % Ca and 5 wt % NdAlCu and heat-treated at 850 °C for 20 min in vacuum.

M–H curve measurements of heat-treated samples, prepared by ball-milling with 2.5 wt % Ca and 5 wt % NdAlCu, exhibited a significant enhancement in coercivity, reaching 3000–6000 Oe (Figure ), compared to the lower coercivity values observed in samples without NdAlCu addition (Figure ). This improvement in coercivity is attributed to the formation of grain boundary phases, which effectively promoted magnetic decoupling between adjacent 2-14-1 grains. However, the addition of the low-melting-point alloy also led to a reduction in the overall magnetization, likely due to an increased volume fraction of the nonmagnetic phases. Although quantitative phase analyses were not performed, the reduction in magnetization suggests a non-negligible fraction of CaO and RE-rich phases. As shown in Figure , FE-SEM analysis of the fracture surfaces of the heat-treated 2-14-1 samples with Ca and NdAlCu additions revealed that the particle size remained nearly unchanged from the dimensions after ball-milling, indicating suppression of grain growth during heat-treatment. These results demonstrate that the combined approach of high-energy ball-milling, Ca addition as a reducing agent, and NdAlCu alloy incorporation successfully enabled phase recovery while simultaneously enhancing the magnetic properties of Ce-based 2-14-1 compounds.

Furthermore, the effect of the heat-treatment temperature on the magnetic properties was investigated using the Ce0.3Nd0.7 2-14-1 powder, which exhibited the highest coercivity among the tested compositions (Figure ). To further enhance magnetic performance, the amount of NdAlCu was increased from 5 wt % (as used in Figure ) to 10 wt %. Figure shows the M–H curves of Ce0.3Nd0.7 2-14-1, which was ball-milled with 2.5 wt % Ca and 10 wt % NdAlCu. The highest coercivity of 10,234.0 Oe was observed from the sample heat-treated at 850 °C, with a remanence of 65.2 emu/g, while the M at 2.6 T values remained relatively stable across the temperature range (Table ). The reduced coercivity observed at 800 °C is attributed to the insufficient phase recovery, likely due to the incomplete reduction below the melting point of Ca, which is consistent with prior results obtained without NdAlCu addition (Figure S5). In contrast, heat-treatment above 850 °C led to significant particle coarsening, as shown in Figure . While samples treated at 850 °C retained submicrometer particle sizes, those treated at 1000 °C exhibited substantial growth ranging from 2 to 10 μm. This comparison between the SEM images of samples treated at 850 and 1000 °C highlights the rapid grain coarsening at elevated temperatures. Moreover, the RE-rich phase was uniformly distributed on the submicron scale in the samples heat-treated at 850 °C, whereas considerable agglomeration was observed in those at 1000 °C (Figure ). Elemental mapping results in Figure also reveal the presence of Nd enrichment at grain boundaries in the 850 °C-treated samples, which is consistent with the formation of RE-rich grain boundary phases for magnetic decoupling. These findings indicate that heat-treatment above 850 °C can lead to not only the particle growth but also the agglomeration of the RE-rich phase, ultimately reducing the effectiveness of magnetic decoupling and degrading the overall magnetic properties. In addition, a noticeable decrease in the magnetization values upon additive incorporation was observed, which is attributed to the reduction in the volume fraction of the 2-14-1 phase. Specifically, for the Ce0.5Nd0.5 composition, the M at 2.6 T is decreased from 110.0 emu/g (Figure ) to 92.1 emu/g after NdAlCu addition (Figure ), which supports the interpretation that the incorporation of the low-melting-point alloy led to the formation of the nonmagnetic grain boundary phase, thereby reducing the effective volume fraction of the 2-14-1 main phase. The fabrication of bulk magnets with improved magnetic properties inherently requires a sintering process to achieve the densification and control of microstructure. Since direct sintering of 2-14-1 powders results in phase decomposition, Ca was introduced as a reducing agent to induce the phase recovery of 2-14-1 during heat treatment. Ca addition alone, however, did not result in a substantial coercivity increase; thus, the low-melting-point alloy, NdAlCu, was incorporated to promote the formation of nonmagnetic RE-rich grain boundary phases, which facilitated magnetic decoupling between 2-14-1 grains. The combined effect of phase recovery and microstructure engineering, achieved through the addition of a reducing agent and low-melting-point alloy, ultimately resulted in a significant enhancement in coercivity.

The magnetic properties of the Ce0.3Nd0.7 2-14-1 bulk sample fabricated in this study were compared with those of various Nd2Fe14B (Nd 2-14-1) powders synthesized by the R–D process in previous reports, − as shown in Figure . While earlier reports demonstrated magnetic properties of Nd 2-14-1 powders, the Ce0.3Nd0.7 2-14-1 bulk sample in this work achieved enhanced coercivity exceeding 10 kOe through a combination of the modified R–D process, particle size refinement, and heat-treatment with additive incorporation. It should be noted that, to the best of our knowledge, no Ce-based bulk magnets prepared by the R–D process have been reported under comparable processing conditions, making a direct one-to-one comparison with Ce-containing systems currently not feasible. This comparison is therefore presented to highlight the significant improvement in coercivity achieved in this work through the synergistic combination of the modified R–D process and bulk fabrication with microstructure engineering. Although the remanence values remained moderate, likely due to the increased volume fraction of nonmagnetic phases from Ca and NdAlCu additions, the saturation magnetization approached 100 emu/g, indicating significant potential for further improvement. These results suggest that microstructure alignment processes such as hot-deformation or magnetic field pressing can improve remanence value, potentially leading to the development of high-performance gap magnets.

Conclusions

This study demonstrated a successful approach to the synthesis and magnetic property enhancement of Ce-based RE2Fe14B (2-14-1) magnetic materials through the modification of composition and control of the microstructure. Single-phase (Ce1‑x Nd x )2Fe14B with varying Ce:Nd ratios were successfully synthesized by the modified reduction–diffusion (R–D) process, showing the compositional flexibility of the solid-state reaction process that was used to prepare REFeO3 precursors. The 2-14-1 powders were refined by high-energy ball-milling to increase the coercivity value, but the heat-treatment of refined powder, which was used to fabricate bulk magnets, resulted in phase decomposition to the Fe phase. The addition of Ca granules as a reducing agent and heat-treatment at 850 °C in a vacuum enabled the successful fabrication of bulk samples with phase recovery of the refined particles without excessive grain growth. Further enhancement of magnetic properties was achieved through the addition of low-melting-point alloy, Nd70Al20Cu10, which formed grain boundary phases that induced magnetic decoupling between grains, resulting in substantial improvement of coercivity.

Optimization of heat-treatment conditions and the appropriate selection/amount of additives (850 °C, 2.5 wt % Ca, 10 wt % Nd70Al20Cu10) led to a coercivity of 10,234.0 Oe. Heat-treatments at lower temperatures resulted in insufficient phase recovery due to inadequate reduction effect below the melting point of Ca, while those at higher temperatures led to significant particle growth and agglomeration of the low-melting-point alloy, resulting in reduced coercivity. The combined approach of (1) compositional optimization, (2) particle size refinement, (3) precise control of heat-treatment condition, and (4) appropriate selection/amount of additives established in this study enables significant enhancement of the magnetic properties in Ce-based 2-14-1 magnets, providing a promising pathway for developing cost-effective Ce-based permanent magnets with significantly improved magnetic properties for cost-effective gap-magnets. Although the achieved magnetic properties are lower than those of sintered Ce-based magnets reported in previous studies, ,,, this difference arises from the absence of microstructural alignment processes (e.g., hot deformation, magnetic-field pressing) and additional alloying optimization. These results indicate that the present approach provides a promising foundation with significant potential for further enhancement through advanced microstructure engineering.

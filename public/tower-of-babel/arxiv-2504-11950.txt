Abstract
. In this paper we derive the fractional power of the backward heat operator as a high dimensional limit of the fractional Laplacian. As applications, we derive Carleman type inequalities for fractional powers of the backward heat operator.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 35B05; Secondary: 35B45

1.
Introduction and main results

Fractional powers of the heat operator
(-\Delta+\partial_{t})^{s}
have garnered significant attention within the mathematical community in recent years. One basic reference is the paper of Metzler and Klafter
[
19
]
where
(-\Delta+\partial_{t})^{s}
is derived via a master equation.
The exploration of this concept has led to many works addressing its definition and fundamental properties
[
24
]
, study of partial differential equations involving it
[
3
,
4
,
15
]
or obstacle problems
[
2
]
, among others.

Our research is motivated by the strong unique continuation property. This principle asserts that if

u
is a solution to
(-\Delta+\partial_{t})^{s}u=Vu
and
u
vanishes of infinite order in a point
(x_{0},t_{0})
then
u
must be identically zero.
Various methodologies exist to establish unique continuation, among which Carleman-type inequalities and the monotonicity of the Almgren frequency formula. Both approaches work with suitable compactly supported or with sufficiently good decaying functions. However, in the case of fractional operators, establishing unique continuation poses a great challenge due to the difficulty in localizing functions when subjected to multiplication by cut-off functions. Consequently, a significant number of published works on unique continuation employs the technique of the extension problem, wherein the solution to the nonlocal problem is derived as a limit of solutions to a local problem posed in a higher dimension.

Notable contributions to the unique continuation problem for the operator
(-\Delta+\partial_{t})^{s}
include the work of Banerjee and Garofalo
[
5
]
. The authors study equations of the form
(-\Delta+\partial_{t})^{s}u=Vu
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
V(x,t)
is a potential with certain regularity and decay properties.
They show that, if
u
vanishes of infinite order backward in time at a point
(x_{0},t_{0})
in the sense that

\text{esssup}_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}]}|u(x,t)|=O(r^{k}),\qquad\text{for all }k>0,

then
u(\cdot,t)\equiv 0
for all
t\leq t_{0}
. In fact, in
[
6
]
, they show that
u\equiv 0
in all
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

These works predominantly rely on the Almgren monotonicity formula and use the technique of the extension problem, where
(-\Delta+\partial_{t})^{s}u
is derived as the limit to the boundary of a Neumann weighted derivative of the solution
U(x,y,t)
to some extended problem in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
that takes boundary data
U(x,0,t)=u(x,t)
.

An alternative approach involves establishing Carleman-type inequalities, as it has been done for instance in the local case
s=1
by Escauriaza
[
12
]
and Fernández
[
14
]
.

A different but significantly motivating topic for our work is the paper
[
8
]
by Davey, in which the author proves that the backward heat operator
(\Delta_{x}+\partial_{t})[u(x,t)]
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be obtained as a high dimensional limit as
N\to\infty
of the Laplacian
\Delta_{x,y}\Big[u\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big)\Big]
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. This relation has many useful applications as the author shows in
[
8
]
, and afterwards in
[
9
]
. Related to the motivation of our current paper, in
[
8
]
, the author shows that starting from the Carleman type estimate for
\Delta_{x,y}
proved by Amrein, Berthier and Georgescu in
[
1
]
, one can derive a Carleman type estimate for
\Delta_{x}+\partial_{t}
which is the same as the one proved by Escauriaza in
[
12
]
.

In this paper we work with fractional powers of the Laplace and backward heat operators by defining them via the subordination formula. For suitable functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, we prove in Proposition
3.1
that,
if
G_{N}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is the notation for

G_{N}\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big):=(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}\left[u\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big)\right],

then

G_{N}(x,t)\rightarrow(-\Delta_{x}-\partial_{t})^{s}[u(x,t)]\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Then, by using some weighted Hardy-Rellich type inequalities for
(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}
proved by Eilertsen
[
11
]
and by de Nitti and Djitte
[
10
]
, both with explicit constants, we are able to deduce the following Carleman type inequalities for the backward in time operator
(-\Delta_{x}-\partial_{t})^{s}
. Our choice to work with the backward heat operator rather than the heat operator is motivated by the strong unique continuation results proved via Carleman-type inequalities available in the local case
s=1

[
12
,
14
]
.

For functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)
we define the even extension with respect to the last variable as

(1.1)

\tilde{u}(x,t)=\begin{cases}u(x,t),&\quad\mbox{if }\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,t\geq 0,\\
u(x,-t),&\quad\mbox{if }\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,t<0.\end{cases}

We will denote by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
the Schwartz space of smooth rapidly decaying functions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\eta>0
such that
2s+\eta\not\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)
such that
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, where
\tilde{u}
is defined in
\eqref{even-extension}.

Then

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|u(x,t)|^{2}\cdot e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}\cdot t^{\frac{2\eta-d-2}{2}}\,dt\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|(-\Delta_{x}-\partial_{t})^{s}[u(x,t)]\right|^{2}\cdot e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}\cdot t^{2s+\frac{2\eta-d-2}{2}}\,dt\,dx.

Here,
C
is a positive constant given by

C=\max_{j\in\{0,1,\dots,j_{1}+1\}}\left(\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{\eta+j}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{2s+\eta+j}{2}\big)}\right)^{2},

where
j_{1}
to be the smallest non-negative integer satisfying

0\leq j_{1}+s+\eta\quad\text{and}\quad\eta(2s+\eta)\leq j_{1}^{2}.

Negative values of
\eta
can be also considered, as long as the function
(x,y)\mapsto u\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{N}\big)
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
2.2
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

A generalization to
L^{p}
norms is presented in the next theorem, although we establish it only for compactly supported functions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
s\in(0,1)
,
1<p<+\infty
,
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
d-2\eta>0
. Then

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\tilde{b}_{d,\eta,s}}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|u(x,t)|^{p}\cdot e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}\,t^{\frac{2\eta-d-2}{2}}dt\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|(-\Delta_{x}-\partial_{t})^{s}[u(x,t)]\right|^{p}\cdot e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}\,t^{sp+\frac{2\eta-d-2}{2}}dt\,dx,

for all
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\tilde{u}\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\cap C^{1,1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, where
\tilde{u}
is defined in
\eqref{even-extension}.
The constant is given by
\tilde{b}_{d,\eta,s}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{-2\eta-2s}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{2\eta-d}{2}\right)}
.

Remark 1
.

(i)

If
s=1
and
\frac{2\eta-d-2}{2}<0
in (
1.2
), we recover the result proved by Escauriaza in
[
12
, Thm.1]
for the backward heat operator
\Delta_{x}+\partial_{t}
.

(ii)

To our knowledge, there are no references that address Carleman inequalities for the fractional operator
(-\Delta-\partial_{t})^{s}
and we believe that estimates (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are new.

2.
Notations and preliminaries

2.1.
The functional space

We denote by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the Schwartz class of real valued smooth rapidly decaying functions. Let
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the Lizorkin space (see
[
11
]
,
[
26
, Section 4]
), that is the closed subspace of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of functions that have all the
k
moments equal to zero:

\mathcal{L}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\partial^{k}\widehat{f}(0)=0,\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\right\}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)|x|^{k}dx=0,\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\right\}.

It is known that the closure of
\mathcal{L}
in the
L^{\infty}
norm is the
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the space of smooth functions that vanish at infinity (see
[
20
, Thm.3.2]
).

\mathcal{L}
is a good space for defining the fractional Laplacian, since in this space the operator becomes invertible.
In Fourier space,
\mathcal{L}
can be seen as:

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{\hat{f}:f\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}=\{\psi\in\mathcal{S}:\partial^{k}\psi(0)=0,\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\},

where
\widehat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
u
and
k
is a multi-index. When the expression is too large, we denote the Fourier transform by
\mathcal{F}
. The Lizorkin space is need only in the hypothesis of Lemma
2.2
in the case
\eta<0
, otherwise we work only in the Schwartz class.

2.2.
The fractional Laplacian

The fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}f
can be represented by many equivalent definitions, among which we recall three of them
[
7
,
18
,
16
]
:

(i)

Via the Fourier transform: for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
(-\Delta)^{s}f
is defined such that

\mathcal{F}((-\Delta)^{s}f)(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\hat{f}(\xi).

Then
(-\Delta)^{s}u:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}
is a bijection.
Moreover, if
s>-n/2
, then
(-\Delta)^{s}f(x)=O(|x|^{-n-2s})
when
|x|\to+\infty
, for any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

Via the subordination formula, also known as Bochner’s integral: for
s\in(0,1)

(2.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}f(x)=\frac{1}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\tau\Delta}f(x)-f(x)\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1+s}}.

The inverse fractional Laplacian is the Riesz potential that, for
0<s<n
, is given by:

(2.2)

(-\Delta)^{-s}f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\tau\Delta}f(x)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1-s}},

where
e^{-\tau\Delta}f(x)
is the heat semigroup with initial data
f
and time variable
\tau
, that is
U(x,\tau)=e^{-\tau\Delta}f(x)
is the solution to the heat equation with data
f(x)
:

\frac{d}{d\tau}U(x,\tau)=\Delta U(x,\tau),\quad U(x,0)=f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(iii)

Via the Levy-Khintchine integral representation:

(2.3)

(-\Delta)^{s}f(x)=\frac{4^{s}\Gamma(n/2+s)}{\pi^{n/2}|\Gamma(-s)|}\,\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy.

It is known that the fractional Laplacian of radial functions is also radial. In the following lemma, we show that, if the function is radial with respect to only some of the coordinates, then its fractional Laplacian has the same property.

In the following we will use the notation
\Delta_{x,y}=\Delta_{x}+\Delta_{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{y_{i}}^{2}
applied to functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d+N}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d+N}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as

f(x,y)=u(x,|y|).

Then
(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}[f(x,y)]
is radial with respect to the
y
coordinates.

Proof.

The proof can be done using definition (
2.3
).
Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
R\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
be a rotation matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then
|Rz|=|z|
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We have:

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}[f](x,Ry)

\displaystyle=C\,\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(w,|z|)-u(x,|Ry|)}{|(w,z)-(x,Ry)|^{N+d+2s}}dy\,dx.

We perform the change of coordinates:
z=R\,\overline{z}
. Thus
|z|=|\overline{z}|
,
dz=d\overline{z}
and

|(w,R\overline{z})-(x,Ry)|^{2}=|w-x|^{2}+|R\cdot(\overline{z}-y)|^{2}=|w-x|^{2}+|\overline{z}-y|^{2}=|(w,\overline{z})-(x,y)|^{2},

and then we obtain

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}[f](x,Ry)

\displaystyle=C\,\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(w,|\overline{z}|)-u(x,|y|)}{|(w,\overline{z})-(x,y)|^{N+d+2s}}dy\,dx

\displaystyle=(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}[f](x,y).

Additionally, in formula (
3.3
), we will see an alternative proof using the subordination formula.
∎

2.3.
Weighted estimates for the fractional Laplacian

We state two fractional Hardy-Rellich type inequalities for the fractional Laplacian. The first one was obtained by Eilertsen
[
11
]
. The class of functions for which this inequality applies is explained in
[
11
, p.362]
and we recall it in the hypothesis of Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
11
, Thm. 16]

Let
s,\eta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s\geq 0
and
-\eta,\,2s+\eta-n\not\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and, moreover, if
\eta\leq 0
then
f
should also be in
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
. Then

(2.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{2}(x)|x|^{2\eta-n}dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(-\Delta)^{s}f(x)|^{2}|x|^{4s+2\eta-n}dx.

Let
j_{0}
be the smallest non-negative integer satisfying
(n-2\eta)(n-4s-2\eta)\leq(n+2j_{0})^{2}
when
n\geq 2
and
j_{0}=0
if
n=1
. Then
the best constant
C
is given by

(2.5)

C_{n}=2^{-4s}\max_{j=j_{0},j_{0}+1}\left(\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{\eta+j}{2}\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{n-2s-\eta+j}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{2s+\eta+j}{2}\big)\Gamma\big(\frac{n-\eta+j}{2}\big)}\right)^{2}.

The inequality above is related to the Stein–Weiss inequality
[
23
, Thm.B*]
, but in their paper the constant is not computed explicitly, which for us is important.

The following inequality has been proved by De Nitti and Djitte and can be seen as a generalization of (
2.4
) to
L^{p}
norms and
C^{1,1}_{c}
functions.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
10
, Remark 2.5, item 2]
)
Let
s\in(0,1)
,
\theta>-2s
,
1<p<+\infty
. Then

\left[\frac{b_{n,s,\theta}}{p}\right]^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{|x|^{\theta+2s}}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|(-\Delta)^{s}f|^{p}}{|x|^{\theta+2s-2sp}}dx

for all
f\in C^{1,1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
The constant is given by

b_{N,s,\theta}=2^{2s}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-\theta}{2}\right)\cdot\Gamma\left(\frac{2s+\theta}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-\theta-2s}{2}\right)\cdot\Gamma\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}.

2.4.
The fractional power of the backward heat operator.

For
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,

(-\Delta-\partial_{t})^{s}u
can be represented via several equivalent definitions (see
[
24
,
5
]
):

(a)

Via the Fourier transform:

(2.6)

\mathcal{F}_{x,t}((-\Delta-\partial_{t})^{s}u(t,x))(\tau,\xi)=(|\xi|^{2}-i\tau)^{s}\widehat{h}(\tau,x),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

(b)

The subordination formula, also known Bochner’s subordination formula or Balakrishnan formula (see [p.260, formula (5)]
[
27
]
, [p.70 formula (6.9)]
[
22
]
):

(2.7)

(-\Delta-\partial_{t})^{s}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{\tau(-\Delta-\partial_{t})}u(t,x)-u(t,x)\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1+s}},\quad\text{for }\,s\in(0,1),

(2.8)

(-\Delta-\partial_{t})^{-s}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\tau(-\Delta-\partial_{t})}u(t,x)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1-s}}\quad\text{for }\,s\in(0,d).

Here
U=e^{\tau(-\Delta-\partial_{t})}u(t,x)
is the solution to the problem

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{d}{d\tau}U(x,t,\tau)=\Delta_{x}U(x,t,\tau)+U_{t}(x,t,\tau)&\text{for }\tau>0,\,t\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[5.69054pt]
U(x,t,0)=u(x,t)&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,t\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

When
u(t,x)
is not dependent of
t
then we recover
(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)
. When
u
does not depend on
x
we recover the fractional derivative in time called Marchaud.

Definition 2.4
.

For functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)
we consider
(-\Delta-\partial_{t})^{s}u
defined by (
2.7
), respectively (
2.8
), where
V(x,t,\tau):=e^{\tau(-\Delta-\partial_{t})}u(t,x)
is the solution to the initial boundary value problem

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{d}{d\tau}V(x,t,\tau)=\Delta_{x}V(x,t,\tau)+V_{t}(x,t,\tau)&\text{for }\tau>0,\,t>0\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[5.69054pt]
V(x,0,\tau)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}u(\overline{x},\tau)\,d\overline{x}&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\tau>0,\\[5.69054pt]
V(x,t,0)=u(x,t)&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,t\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

In Lemma
3.2
we prove the solution of (
2.10
) is unique (as a bounded solution) and coincides with the solution of (
2.9
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)
, thus definition (
2.4
) coincides with (
2.7
) for functions
u
that are globally defined.

3.
Transformation

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
s\in(-d,1)
. Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)
such that
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, where
\tilde{u}
is defined in
\eqref{even-extension}.

(i)

Then
(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}\left[u\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big)\right]
is a function only of
\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big)
, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
N\geq 1
.

(ii)

Let
G_{N}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
such that

G_{N}\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big)=(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}\left[u\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big)\right].

Then

G_{N}(x,t)\rightarrow(-\Delta_{x}-\partial_{t})^{s}[u(x,t)]\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Proof.

We define the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by

f(x,y)=u\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big),

and, thus,
f
is radial with respect to the
y
coordinate.
The idea of this definition, as well as the subsequent limit process, is inspired by the paper of Davey
[
8
]
and by Tao’s blog
[
25
]
. We present the proof for
s\in(0,1)
; however for
s\in(-d,0)
similar arguments apply using the corresponding definitions (
2.2
) and (
2.8
) for
(-\Delta_{x,y})^{-s},(-\Delta_{x}-\partial_{t})^{-s}
. The case
s=0
is straightforward and is related to
[
8
]
.

Proof of (i). The fractional Laplacian.

We observe first that
(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}f(x,y)
is also radial with respect to the
|y|
coordinate as we proved in Lemma
2.1
, thus
(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}f(x,y)=g(x,|y|)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
g
is some function
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. Since
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
,
\varphi(r)=r^{2}/(2N)
is a bijection then
g(x,|y|)=g(x,\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(y))
, and thus,
by defining

G_{N}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R},\quad G_{N}(x,t)=g(x,\sqrt{2Nt}),

we have

(3.1)

(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}f(x,y)=G_{N}(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}).

Later in this proof, we will alternatively deduce this dependence using the subordination definition.

Our goal is to prove that
G_{N}(x,t)\rightarrow(-\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x})^{s}[u(x,t)]
as
N\to\infty
pointwise for every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty).
We will approach this by using the semigroup definition of fractional powers of operators.
By using definition (
2.1
),

(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}f(x,y)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(U_{N}(x,y,\tau)-f(x,y))\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1+s}},

where
U_{N}(x,y,\tau)
solves the heat equation

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{d}{d\tau}U_{N}(x,y,\tau)=\Delta_{x,y}U(x,y,\tau)&\text{for }\tau>0\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
U_{N}(x,y,0)=f(x,y)=u(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N})&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

Recall that
u
is a bounded function since
u\in\mathcal{S}
, thus there exists
M>0
such that
|u(x,t)|\leq M,
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t\geq 0.
The Maximum Principle for the Heat Equation (see
[
13
, p.57]
) gives us that

\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,y)\leq\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{N}(x,y,t)\leq U_{N}(x,y,t)\leq\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{N}(x,y,t)\leq\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,y).

Since
f(x,y)=u(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N})
and
u
is bounded, then we obtain that
|U_{N}|
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
with an upper bound independent of
N
.

Since the data depends only on its values for
x
and
\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}
, then, it can be proved that also
U_{N}
can be written as function of
(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N},\tau)
. This can be easy justified by the fact that
U_{N}
is radial with respect to
y
and noticing that
r\mapsto\frac{r^{2}}{2N}
is a bijection from
[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
. We can also give an explicit representation formula for this fact.
Indeed, using the integral representation (
2.3
), we have:

\displaystyle U_{N}(x,y,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{(N+d)/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}+|y-\overline{y}|^{2}}{4\tau}}u\big(\overline{x},\frac{|\overline{y}|^{2}}{2N}\big)\,d\overline{y}\,d\overline{x}.

We pass to polar coordinates
\overline{y}=r\sigma
with
r\in[0,\infty)
,
\sigma\in S^{N-1}
the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with area recalled in (
6.7
). Thus

\displaystyle U_{N}(x,y,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{(N+d)/2}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4\tau}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}r^{N-1}u\big(\overline{x},\frac{r^{2}}{2N}\big)\int_{S^{N-1}}e^{\frac{ry\cdot\sigma}{2\tau}}\,d\sigma\,dr\,d\overline{x}.

In order to compute the integral over the sphere for fixed
y
, a rotation argument helps to pass from
y\cdot\sigma
to
|y|\vec{e}_{1}\cdot\sigma=|y|\sigma_{1}
, where
\sigma_{1}
is the first component of
\sigma
as a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. This is explained later on in the Appendix, see (
6.8
).
Thus

\displaystyle\int_{S^{N-1}}e^{\frac{ry\cdot\sigma}{2\tau}}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{N-1}}e^{\frac{r|y|\sigma_{1}}{2\tau}}d\sigma=|S^{N-2}|\int_{-1}^{1}e^{\frac{r|y|t}{2\tau}}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2})}\cdot\frac{2^{N}\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\tau^{\frac{N-1}{2}}}{(r|y|)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}}\cdot I_{\frac{N-1}{2}}\Big(\frac{r|y|}{2\tau}\Big),

where
I
is the modified Bessel function of first kind that we recall in (
6.6
).
Then

(3.3)

\displaystyle U_{N}(x,y,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{(N+d)/2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2})}2^{N}\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\tau^{\frac{N-1}{2}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4\tau}}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}r^{N-1}u(\overline{x},\frac{r^{2}}{2N})\frac{1}{(r|y|)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}}I_{\frac{N-1}{2}}\Big(\frac{r|y|}{2\tau}\Big)\,drd\,\overline{x}.

If we denote
\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}=t
and change variables
\frac{r^{2}}{2N}=p
then
rdr=Ndp
:

\displaystyle U_{N}(x,y,\tau)=\frac{1}{2^{d}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\tau^{(d+1)/2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2})}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}\int_{0}^{\infty}u(\overline{x},p)e^{-\frac{N}{2\tau}(t+p)}p^{\frac{N-2}{2}}(pt)^{-\frac{N-1}{4}}I_{\frac{N-1}{2}}\Big(\frac{N\sqrt{pt}}{\tau}\Big)\,dp\,d\overline{x}.

Thus

U_{N}(x,y,\tau)=V_{N}(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N},\tau),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,\tau>0,

where
V_{N}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(3.4)

V_{N}(x,t,\tau)=\frac{1}{2^{d}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\tau^{(d+1)/2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2})}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}\int_{0}^{\infty}u(\overline{x},p)e^{-\frac{N}{2\tau}(t+p)}p^{\frac{N-2}{2}}(pt)^{-\frac{N-1}{4}}I_{\frac{N-1}{2}}\Big(\frac{N\sqrt{pt}}{\tau}\Big)\,dp\,d\overline{x}.

Equivalently,

V_{N}(x,t,\tau)=U(x,\sqrt{2Nt}\,z,\tau)

for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with modulus
1
.

(ii). Passing to the limit.
We use the definition (
3.4
) and the heat equation (
3.2
) solved by
U_{N}
.
Let
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
,
\varphi(r)=r^{2}/(2N)
, and thus

U_{N}(x,y,\tau)=V_{N}(x,\varphi(r),\tau),\quad r=|y|.

By passing
\Delta_{y}
to radial coordinates, it follows that
V_{N}(x,\varphi(r),\tau)
solves the equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}V_{N}(x,\varphi(r),\tau)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{x}V_{N}(x,\varphi(r),\tau)+(V_{N})_{t}(x,\varphi(r),\tau)\cdot\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{N-1}{r}\varphi^{\prime}(r)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+(V_{N})_{tt}(\varphi(r),\tau)\cdot(\varphi^{\prime}(r))^{2},

where
(V_{N})_{t}
is the derivative of
V_{N}(x,t,\tau)
with respect to the
t
variable.
Since
\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{N-1}{r}\varphi^{\prime}(r)=1
, then
V_{N}
is a solution to the equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}V\big(x,\frac{r^{2}}{2N},\tau\big)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{x}V\big(x,\frac{r^{2}}{2N},\tau\big)+(V_{N})_{t}\big(x,\frac{r^{2}}{2N},\tau\big)+V_{tt}\big(x,\frac{r^{2}}{2N},\tau\big)\cdot\left(\frac{r}{N}\right)^{2}.

Let
r^{2}/(2N)=t
. Then,

(3.5)

\frac{d}{d\tau}V_{N}(x,t,\tau)=\Delta_{x}V(x,t,\tau)+(V_{N})_{t}(x,t,\tau)+(V_{N})_{tt}(x,t,\tau)\cdot\frac{t}{N},

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\geq 0
,
\tau>0.

Our purpose is to prove that
V_{N}(x,t,\tau)
is pointwise convergent as
N\to\infty
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\geq 0
,
\tau>0
.
We will use the following property from calculus: a bounded sequence converges to
l
if and only if any convergent subsequence converges to
l
.

We fix
(x,t,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\times(0,\infty).
Let
(V_{N_{k}}(x,t,\tau))_{k}
be a convergent subsequence of
(V_{N}(x,t,\tau))_{N}
and we denote by
V(x,t,\tau)=\lim_{k\to\infty}V_{N_{k}}(x,t,\tau)
.
We pass to the limit as
N_{k}\to\infty
in the PDE solved by
V_{N_{k}}
in order to obtain a PDE for
V
.

The lateral boundary value for
V_{N}
is

\displaystyle V_{N}(x,0,\tau)

\displaystyle=U_{N}(x,\vec{0},\tau)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{(N+d)/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}+|\overline{y}|^{2}}{4\tau}}u(\overline{x},\frac{|\overline{y}|^{2}}{2N})d\overline{y}d\overline{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{|S^{N-1}|}{(4\pi\tau)^{(N+d)/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}r^{N-1}u(\overline{x},\frac{r^{2}}{2N})drd\overline{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{|S^{N-1}|}{(4\pi\tau)^{(N+d)/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}e^{-\frac{Nt}{2\tau}}(2Nt)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}u(\overline{x},t)N\,dt\,d\overline{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\Gamma(N/2)}\left(\frac{N}{2\tau}\right)^{N/2}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{Nt}{2\tau}}t^{\frac{N-2}{2}}u(\overline{x},t)\,dt\,d\overline{x}

\displaystyle=:g_{N}(x,\tau).

Notice that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{Nt}{2\tau}}t^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2\tau}{N}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(N/2)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}\,d\overline{x}=(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}.

Since
u
is bounded, then the previous computations lead to

\displaystyle|g_{N}(x,\tau)|

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{\infty}.

We have:

\displaystyle g_{N}(x,\tau)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}u(\overline{x},\tau)\,d\overline{x}=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}\cdot I(N),

where

\displaystyle|I(N)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(N/2)}\left(\frac{N}{2\tau}\right)^{N/2}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{Nt}{2\tau}}t^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\left(u(\overline{x},t)-u(\overline{x},\tau)\right)\,dt.

Changing variables
t=\tau\cdot a
,
dt=\tau da
, gives us

I(N)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(N/2)}\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{N/2}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{N}{2}a}a^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\left(u(\overline{x},a\tau)-u(\overline{x},\tau)\right)\,da.

The function
a\mapsto h(a):=e^{-\frac{N}{2}a}a^{\frac{N-2}{2}}
, defined for
a\geq 0
, is increasing in
[0,\frac{N-2}{N}]
and decreasing on
[\frac{N-2}{N},\infty)
, attaining the maximum in
a=\frac{N-2}{N}
, which is close to
1
for large
N
. Also
\lim_{a\to\infty}h(a)=0=h(0).
Since
u
is continuous at
t=\tau
, then for fixed
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\left|u(\overline{x},a\tau)-u(\overline{x},\tau)\right|<\epsilon
for
a\in(1-\delta,1+\delta).

This motivates us to split the integral as follows:

I(N)=I_{1}(N)+I_{2}(N)+I_{3}(N)_{=}\int_{1-\delta}^{1+\delta}+\int_{0}^{1-\delta}+\int_{1+\delta}^{\infty}.

We estimate from above each of these integrals. For
I_{1}(N)
we use the continuity of
u
and formula (
3.6
):

\displaystyle|I_{1}(N)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(N/2)}\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{N/2}\cdot\int_{1-\delta}^{1+\delta}e^{-\frac{N}{2}a}a^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\left|u(\overline{x},a\tau)-u(\overline{x},\tau)\right|\,da\leq\epsilon.

For
I_{2}(N)
and
I_{3}(N)
we use (
6.1
) that translates here as
\Gamma(N/2)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{N/2}}\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}}
. Thus

\displaystyle|I_{2}(N)|

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sqrt{N/2}\,e^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot\int_{0}^{1-\delta}e^{-\frac{N}{2}a}a^{\frac{N-2}{2}}da.

The above integral rewrites as:

\int_{0}^{1-\delta}e^{-\frac{N}{2}a}a^{\frac{N-2}{2}}da=\left(\frac{2}{N}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot\int_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\delta)}e^{-x}x^{\frac{N}{2}-1}dx=\left(\frac{2}{N}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot\gamma\left(\frac{N}{2},\frac{N}{2}(1-\delta)\right),

where
\gamma(\cdot,\cdot)
is the incomplete Gamma function recalled in (
6.4
).
Using the asymptotics for large
N
given in (
6.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{2}(N)|

\displaystyle\sim\|u\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sqrt{N/2}\,e^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot\left(\frac{2}{N}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot\left(\frac{N}{2}(1-\delta)\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot e^{-\frac{N}{2}(1-\delta)}\cdot\frac{1}{\frac{N}{2}\delta}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\frac{1}{\delta\sqrt{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\cdot e^{\frac{N}{2}\delta}\cdot\left(1-\delta\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}.

Notice that

e^{\frac{N}{2}\delta}\cdot\left(1-\delta\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}=e^{\frac{N}{2}\left(\delta+\log(1-\delta)\right)}.

The function
\delta\mapsto\delta+\log(1-\delta)
defined for
\delta\in[0,1)
is strictly decreasing (it has negative derivative) and takes
0
value for
\delta=0
, thus it is negative for
\delta\in(0,1).

Thus

I_{2}(N)\to 0\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Now we estimate
I_{3}(N)
. Notice that

\int_{1+\delta}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{N}{2}a}a^{\frac{N-2}{2}}da=\left(\frac{2}{N}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot\int_{\frac{N}{2}(1+\delta)}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{\frac{N}{2}-1}dx=\left(\frac{2}{N}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2},\frac{N}{2}(1+\delta)\right),

where
\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot)
is the incomplete Gamma function recalled in (
6.4
).
Using the asymptotics for large
N
given in (
6.5
) we get

\displaystyle|I_{3}(N)|

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sqrt{N/2}\,e^{\frac{N}{2}}\left(\frac{2}{N}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2},\frac{N}{2}(1+\delta)\right)

\displaystyle\sim\|u\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sqrt{N/2}\,e^{\frac{N}{2}}\left(\frac{2}{N}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot\left(\frac{N}{2}(1+\delta)\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}(1+\delta)}\frac{1}{\frac{N}{2}\delta}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{2}\delta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}(\delta-\log(1+\delta))}.

The function
\delta\mapsto\delta-\log(1+\delta)
defined for
\delta\in[0,1)
is strictly increasing (it has positive derivative) and takes
0
value for
\delta=0
, thus it is positive for
\delta\in(0,1).

Thus

I_{3}(N)\to 0\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Therefore, we conclude that

V_{N}(x,0,\tau)=g_{N}(x,\tau)\rightarrow\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}u(\overline{x},\tau)\,d\overline{x}=:\phi(x,\tau)\quad\text{as }\,N\to\infty.

Passing to the limit in (
3.5
) as
N_{k}\to\infty
we get:

(3.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{d}{d\tau}V(x,t,\tau)=\Delta_{x}V(x,t,\tau)+V_{t}(x,t,\tau)&\text{for }\tau>0,\,t>0\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[5.69054pt]
V(x,0,\tau)=\phi(x,\tau)&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\tau>0,\\[2.84526pt]
V(x,t,0)=u(x,t)&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,t>0.\end{array}\right.

This is a linear equation with diffusion and transport term in the upper half-space that we consider bellow in Lemma
3.2
. Problem (
3.7
) has a unique bounded solution given by

V(x,t,\tau)=e^{-\tau(-\Delta-\partial_{t})}u(x,t)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}u(t+\tau,x-\overline{x})\,d\overline{x}.

Since the limit
V(x,t,\tau)
is unique, then the whole sequence
(V_{N}(x,t,\tau))_{N}
is convergent to
V(x,t,\tau)
pointwise as
N\to\infty
, for any
(x,t,\tau)
.

In summary, we have obtained that

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}\left[u\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(V_{N}(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N},\tau)-u\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big)\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1+s}}.

Thus, if we denote
G_{N}(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N})=(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}\left[u\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big)\right]

then

G_{N}\big(x,t\big)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(V_{N}(x,t,\tau)-u\big(x,t\big)\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1+s}}.

By letting
N\to\infty
and in view of definition
2.4
, we obtain

G_{N}(x,t)\rightarrow\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(V(x,t,\tau)-u\big(x,t\big)\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1+s}}=(-\Delta_{x}-\partial_{t})^{s}[u(x,t)].

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The solution of (
2.10
)
is unique within the class of bounded solutions and it is given by

V(x,t,\tau)=e^{-\tau(-\Delta-\partial_{t})}u(x,t)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|\overline{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}}u(t+\tau,x-\overline{x})\,d\overline{x}.

Proof.

Since the equation is linear, then it is sufficient to prove that
0
is the unique solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{V}(x,t,\tau)=\Delta_{x}\mathcal{V}(x,t,\tau)+\mathcal{V}_{t}(x,t,\tau)&\text{for }\tau>0,\,t>0\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[8.53581pt]
\mathcal{V}(x,0,\tau)=0&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\tau>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\mathcal{V}(x,t,0)=0&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,t>0.\end{array}\right.

We use the method of characteristics (see for instance
[
17
, Ch.1]
). Let
(t,\tau)\in\{\tau+t=C\}
for a fixed
C
. Let
h(x,\tau)=\mathcal{V}(x,C-\tau,\tau).
Then
h
solves the PDE:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}h(x,\tau)=\Delta_{x}h(x,\tau),&\text{for }\tau>0\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[8.53581pt]
h(x,0)=0&\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{array}\right.

Since the solution of the heat equation is unique within the class of functions that satisfy at most some exponential upper bound (see, for instance,
[
13
]
), and since our case is simpler as we work with bounded solutions, it follows that
h=0
. Consequently,
\mathcal{V}=0
along the lines
\tau+t=C
, and we conclude that
V=0
.
∎

Remark 2
.

The transformation given in Proposition
3.1
works in fact for bounded functions
u(x,t)
with less regularity such that
(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}\left[u\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big)\right]
and
(-\Delta_{x}-\partial_{t})^{s}[u(x,t)]
are well defined and such that the associated heat equation used above has classical solutions.

4.
Proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

Proof.

(of Theorem
1.1
) Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as in the hypothesis. Let
N\geq 1
. We define the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by

f(x,y)=u\big(x,\frac{r^{2}}{2N}\big),\quad\text{where }r=|y|,

and thus
f
is radial with respect to the
y
coordinate. As proved in Proposition
3.1
, we have

(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}f(x,y)=G_{N}\big(x,\frac{|y|^{2}}{2N}\big),

for some
G_{N}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. According to
[
11
, Thm16]
, that we recalled in Lemma
2.2
and we adapt it here to
N+d
dimensions, the following weighted estimate holds:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+d}}|f(x,y)|^{2}|(x,y)|^{2\eta-N-d}dxdy\leq C_{N,d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+d}}\left|(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}f(x,y)\right|^{2}|(x,y)|^{4s+2\eta-N-d}dx\,dy

for
s\geq 0
,
\alpha=0
and
\eta,N+d-2s-\eta\not\in-\mathbb{N}
, where the best constant
C_{N,d}
is given in formula (
2.5
).
Equivalently, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+d}}|f(x,y)|^{2}

\displaystyle(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{{2\eta-N-d}}{2}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C_{N,d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+d}}\left|(-\Delta_{x,y})^{s}f(x,y)\right|^{2}\cdot(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{4s+2\eta-N-d}{2}}dx\,dy.

By passing to polar coordinates with
r=|y|
in both left and right hand side integrals, and using notation (
3.1
), we rewrite as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|u(x,\frac{r^{2}}{2N})\right|^{2}\cdot\left(|x|^{2}+r^{2}\right)^{\frac{{2\eta-N-d}}{2}}\cdot r^{N-1}\,dr\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{N,d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|G_{N}(x,\frac{r^{2}}{2N})\right|^{2}\cdot(|x|^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{4s+2\eta-N-d}{2}}r^{N-1}drdx.

Equivalently, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|u(x,\frac{r^{2}}{2N})\right|^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{|x|^{2}}{r^{2}}+1\right)^{\frac{{2\eta-N-d}}{2}}\cdot r^{2\eta-d-1}\,dr\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{N,d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|G_{N}(x,\frac{r^{2}}{2N})\big]\right|^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{|x|^{2}}{r^{2}}+1\right)^{\frac{4s+2\eta-N-d}{2}}r^{4s+2\eta-d-1}dr\,dx.

In the left hand side integral we perform the change of variables
r^{2}/2N=t
. Then
r\,dr=N\,dt
. Thus,
after simplifying constants, we obtain:

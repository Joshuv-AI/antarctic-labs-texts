1.
Introduction

In 1962, Barratt and Milnor
[
3
]
proved that the rational homology groups of a shrinking wedge of
m
-spheres (
m\geq 2
), which we refer to as the
m
-dimensional earring space

\mathbb{E}_{m}
, are uncountable in arbitrarily high dimensions. In 2000, Eda and Kawamura proved that
\mathbb{E}_{m}
is
(m-1)
-connected and locally
(m-1)
-connected and that
\pi_{m}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
is canonically isomorphic to the Baer-Specker group
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Built into the proof of Eda-Kawamura’s Theorem is the striking fact that higher homotopy groups are “infinitely commutative” in a strong sense.

It remains an open problem to give a full description of the groups
\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
,
n>m
in the spirit of the Hilton-Milnor Theorem. In this paper we give a complete description of the Čech homotopy groups
\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
in terms of the homotopy groups of spheres for all
n,m\geq 2
. We build off of the computation of
\check{\pi}_{m+1}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
,
m\geq 2
established by K. Kawamura in
[
9
]
. Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
m,n\geq 2
, there is a isomorphism

\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})\cong\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq\frac{n-1}{m-1}}\left(\pi_{n}(S^{mj-j+1})\right)^{\mathbb{N}}.

The isomorphism in Theorem
1.1
is not canonical but depends only an enumeration of the union of coherently chosen Hall bases
H_{k}
on finite sets
A_{k}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
. The upper bound
\frac{n-1}{m-1}
on the index
j
simply indicates when the homotopy groups of spheres might be non-trivial (if
j>\frac{n-1}{m-1}
, then
n<mj-j+1
and
\pi_{n}(S^{mj-j+1})=0
). Low-dimensional cases of special interest are highlighted in Example
3.14
.

When
n>m
, the group
\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
is potentially more complicated than
\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
. However, there is a canonical homomorphism
\Psi_{n}:\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})\to\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
relating the two. Using the isomorphism in Theorem
1.1
as a book-keeping device, we construct and distinguish new elements of
\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
in an effort to characterize the image of
\Psi_{n}
. We do not attempt to address whether or not
\Psi_{n}
is injective. Our second theorem establishes the surjectivity of
\Psi_{n}
in a certain range of dimensions.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
m,n\geq 2
and
n\leq 2m-1
, then the canonical homomorphism
\Psi_{n}:\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})\to\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
is a split epimorphism.

Theorem
1.2
is proved in two cases. The stable range (
n<2m-1
) is proved in Corollary
4.3
where
\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})\cong\pi_{n}(S^{m})^{\mathbb{N}}
. The case
n=2m-1
is proved in Lemma
4.4
where
\check{\pi}_{2m-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m})\cong\pi_{2m-1}(S^{m})^{\mathbb{N}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}
. The image of the splitting homomorphism
\check{\pi}_{2m-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m})\to\pi_{2m-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
constructed in Lemma
4.4
meets the kernel of the homomorphism induced by the canonical embedding
\sigma:\mathbb{E}_{m}\to\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}S^{m}
precisely in the subgroup
\mathcal{W}_{2n-1}(\mathbb{E}_{n})
that is computed in
[
4
]
. Moreover, the author has suggested in
[
4
, Conjecture 1.3]
that
\mathcal{W}_{2n-1}(\mathbb{E}_{n})
is equal to
\ker(\sigma_{\#})
. It follows from the standard splitting of
\sigma_{\#}
reviewed in Section
2.3
that
[
4
, Conjecture 1.3]
is equivalent to conjecturing that
\Psi_{2m-1}:\pi_{2m-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m})\to\check{\pi}_{2m-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
is an isomorphism for all
m\geq 2
.

Although the surjectivity of
\Psi_{n}
remains unclear when
n>2m-1
, we construct a candidate for
\operatorname{Im}(\Psi_{n})
in this range with our third theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
n>2m-1
, then
\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
contains a subgroup isomorphic to

\prod_{\mathbb{N}}\pi_{n}(S^{m})\oplus\bigoplus_{2\leq j\leq\frac{n-1}{m-1}}\left(\prod_{\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{\mathbb{N}}\pi_{n}(S^{mj-j+1})\right)

on which the canonical homomorphism
\Psi_{n}:\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})\to\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
is injective.

Preliminaries

All topological spaces in this paper are assumed to be Hausdorff. Throughout,
I
will denote the closed unit interval,

will denote the unit
n
-sphere with basepoint
(1,0,0,\dots,0)
. We only consider higher homotopy groups in this paper and so we generally assume

. For a based topological space
(X,x_{0})
, we write

to denote the space of relative maps
(I^{n},\partial I^{n})\to(X,x_{0})
and

to denote the
n

\Omega^{n}(X)
and
\pi_{n}(X)
. Fixing a based homeomorphism
I^{n}/\partial I^{n}\cong S^{n}
, we identify
\Omega^{n}(X)
with the space of based maps
S^{n}\to X
. For

, let
-f
denote the reverse map given by
-f(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n})=f(1-t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n})
. We write
f_{1}+f_{2}+\dots+f_{m}
for the usual first-coordinate concatenation of maps
f_{i}\in\Omega^{n}(X)
. If
m
is a positive integer, then
mf
denotes the
m
-fold sum
f+f+\cdots+f
,
0f
denotes the constant map

, and if
m
is a negative integer, then

denotes the
(-m)
-fold sum

.

2.1.
Whitehead Products

We use commutator bracket notation to denote Whitehead products both on the level of maps and homotopy classes. For an ordered pair of natural numbers
(p,q)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, we view
S^{p}\vee S^{q}
naturally as a subspace of
S^{p}\times S^{q}
. Let
a_{p,q}:S^{p+q-1}\to S^{p}\vee S^{q}
denote an attaching map of the unique
p+q
-cell in
S^{p}\times S^{q}
. If
f:S^{p}\to X
and
g:S^{q}\to X
are based maps, the composition
[f,g]=(f\vee g)\circ a_{p,q}:S^{p+q-1}\to X
is the
Whitehead product
of the pair of maps
(f,g)
. This operation on maps descends to a well-defined bracket operation
[-,-]:\pi_{p}(X)\times\pi_{q}(X)\to\pi_{p+q-1}(X)
on homotopy classes, which we also refer to as the
Whitehead product
.

Remark 2.1
.

Whitehead products satisfy the following standard relations
[
12
]
. Let
p,q,r\geq 2
. For
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\pi_{p}(X)
,
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\pi_{q}(X)
, and
\gamma\in\pi_{r}(X)
, we have:

•

(identity)
[a,0]=0
and
[0,b]=0
,

•

(bilinearity)
[\alpha+\alpha^{\prime},\beta]=[\alpha,\beta]+[\alpha^{\prime},\beta]
and
[\alpha,\beta+\beta^{\prime}]=[\alpha,\beta]+[\alpha,\beta^{\prime}]
,

•

(graded symmetry)
[\alpha,\beta]=(-1)^{pq}[\beta,\alpha]
,

•

(graded Jacobi identity)

(-1)^{pr}[[\alpha,\beta],\gamma]+(-1)^{pq}[[\beta,\gamma],\alpha]+(-1)^{rq}[[\gamma,\alpha],\beta]=0.

We have need to apply some of the standard relations infinitely many times in a single line of algebraic reasoning. To do this it is necessary to know that whenever representing maps are “small” we can apply these relations using “small” homotopies. The following Proposition is an observation made in
[
4
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
X
is a based space,
p,q\geq 2
,
f,f^{\prime}\in\Omega^{p}(X)
, and
g,g^{\prime}\in\Omega^{q}(X)
.

(1)

If
H
is a homotopy from
f
to
f^{\prime}
and
G
is a homotopy from
g
to
g^{\prime}
, then
[f,g]\simeq[f^{\prime},g^{\prime}]
by a homotopy with image equal to
\operatorname{Im}(H)\cup\operatorname{Im}(G)
,

(2)

If
f
or
g
is constant, then
[f,g]
is null-homotopic in
\operatorname{Im}(f)\cup\operatorname{Im}(g)
,

(3)

We have
[f+f^{\prime},g]\simeq[f,g]+[f^{\prime},g]
by a homotopy with image
\operatorname{Im}(f)\cup\operatorname{Im}(f^{\prime})\cup\operatorname{Im}(g)
and
[f,g+g^{\prime}]\simeq[f,g]+[f,g^{\prime}]
by a homotopy with image
\operatorname{Im}(f)\cup\operatorname{Im}(g)\cup\operatorname{Im}(g^{\prime})
.

A based map
f:X\to Y
induces a homomorphism
f_{\#}:\pi_{n}(X)\to\pi_{n}(Y)
and
[f]
will denote the based homotopy class of
f
. Recall that ordinary composition
f\circ g
of maps descends to composition
[f]\circ[g]
of homotopy classes in the homotopy category.

Remark 2.3
.

If
h:X\to Y
,
f:S^{p}\to X
, and
g:S^{q}\to X
are based maps, then we have equality
h\circ[f,g]=[h\circ f,h\circ g]
on the level of continuous maps. For homotopy classes
a=[f]
and
b=[g]
, we have
h_{\#}([a,b])=[h_{\#}(a),h_{\#}(b)]
.

Infinite sums

.

2.3.
Shrinking wedges and their Čech homotopy groups

Definition 2.10
.

The
shrinking wedge
of countable set
\{X_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of based spaces is the space
\bigcurlyvee_{j\in J}X_{j}
whose underlying set is the usual one-point union
\bigvee_{j\in J}X_{j}
with canonical basepoint
b_{0}
. A set
U
is open in
\bigcurlyvee_{j\in J}X_{j}
if

•

U\cap X_{j}
is open in
X_{j}
for all
j\in J
,

•

and whenever
b_{0}\in U
, we have
X_{j}\subseteq U
for all but finitely many
j\in J
.

We refer to the special case
\mathbb{E}_{m}=\bigcurlyvee_{j\in\mathbb{N}}S^{m}
as the
m
-dimensional earring space
.

We note two other useful ways to construct a shrinking wedge
X=\bigcurlyvee_{j\in J}X_{j}
. First,
X
canonically embeds in the direct product
\prod_{j\in J}X_{j}
. We let
\sigma:X\to\prod_{j\in J}X_{j}
denote the embedding and often identify
X
with its image under
\sigma
so that
\sigma
is an inclusion map. Second, is construction of
\bigcurlyvee_{j\in J}X_{j}
as an inverse limit. Let
X_{\leq k}=\bigvee_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}
be the wedge of the first
k

b_{k+1,k}:X_{\leq k+1}\to X_{\leq k}
denote the map that collapses
X_{k+1}
to the wedgepoint. The projection maps
b_{k}:X\to X_{\leq k}
collapsing the sub-shrinking wedge
X_{\geq k+1}=\bigcurlyvee_{j>k}X_{j}
to the basepoint induce a homeomorphism
X\to\varprojlim_{k}(X_{\leq k},b_{k+1,k})
.

∎

3.
Applying the Hilton-Milnor Theorem

For the remainder of the paper, fix monotone sequence of integers
1\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq r_{3}\leq\cdots
and set
X=\bigcurlyvee_{i\in\mathbb{N}}S^{r_{i}+1}
with wedgepoint
x_{0}
. Later, we will restrict to the case where
r_{i}=m-1
for all
i
so that
X=\mathbb{E}_{m}
. As before, let
X_{\leq k}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}S^{r_{i}+1}
be the finite wedge of the first
k
-spheres so that
X=\varprojlim_{k}(X_{\leq k},b_{k+1,k})
with bonding maps
b_{k+1,k}:X_{\leq k+1}\to X_{\leq k}
and projection maps and
b_{k}:X\to X_{\leq k}
. We also write
X_{\geq k}=\bigcurlyvee_{i=k}^{\infty}S^{r_{i}+1}
for the sub-shrinking wedge that excludes the first
(k-1)
wedge-summands. Our first aim is to describe the
n
-th Čech homotopy group
\check{\pi}_{n}(X)=\varprojlim_{k}(\pi_{n}(X_{\leq k}),b_{k+1,k\#})
.

A non-trivial theorem of Eda-Kawamura (see
[
5
]
) establishes some cases using higher connectivity. Since each
S^{r_{i}+1}
is an
r_{i}
-connected CW-complex, we have
\pi_{n}(X)=\check{\pi}_{n}(X)=0
for
1\leq n\leq r_{1}
. When
n=r_{1}+1
, we have that
\check{\pi}_{n}(X,x_{0})\cong\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\pi_{n}(S^{r_{i}+1})
and that
\Psi_{n}
is an isomorphism. Hence,

\pi_{r_{1}+1}(X)\cong\check{\pi}_{r_{1}+1}(X)\cong\prod_{r_{j}=r_{1}}\pi_{r_{1}+1}(S^{r_{j}+1})\cong\prod_{r_{j}=r_{1}}\mathbb{Z},

which is either finitely generated free abelian or the Baer-Specker group. Also it is shown in
[
9
]
that
\check{\pi}_{n+1}(\mathbb{E}_{n})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}
when
n=2
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\mathbb{N}}
when
n\geq 3
.

To extend the argument used in
[
9
]
, we recall the notion of a Hall set, which has import in both group theory and Lie theory. In particular, a Hall set on a non-empty finite set
A_{k}=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}\}
provides a convenient basis for a free Lie algebra on
A_{k}

[
6
]
. Following Hilton’s approach in
[
8
]
, we consider such a Hall set on
A_{k}=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}\}
where
a_{i}=[id_{S^{r_{i}+1}}]
is the canonical generator of
\pi_{r_{i}+1}(S^{r_{i}+1})
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Hall set
on a non-empty finite set
A_{k}=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}\}
is a linearly ordered set
H_{k}
of nested formal commutators determined by the following inductive collection and ordering process
[
7
]
: the set
H_{k}(1)
of Hall words of
weight
1
is the set
A_{k}
ordered as
a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{k}
. Suppose the set
H_{k}(i)
of Hall words of weight
i
has been defined for
i<m
and that
\bigcup_{i<m}H_{k}(i)
has been linearly ordered and so that if
i<j
,
x\in H_{k}(i)
, and
y\in H_{k}(j)
, then
x<y
. With this inductive hypothesis, we define a Hall word of weight
m
to be a formal commutator
[x,y]
such that

(1)

x\in H_{k}(i)
and
y\in H_{k}(j)
where
i+j=m
,

(2)

x<y
in
\bigcup_{i<m}H_{k}(i)
,

(3)

if
y=[a,b]
for
a,b\in\bigcup_{i<m}H_{k}(i)
, then
a\leq x
.

We let
H_{k}(m)
be the set of all Hall words of weight
m
with an arbitrary linear ordering. Now
H_{k}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}H_{k}(i)
is given a linear ordering that extends the linear ordering of each
\bigcup_{i<m}H_{k}(i)
so that a Hall word of smaller weight is always less than another Hall word of greater weight.

For each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}
and Hall word
w\in H_{k}
, we let
\nu_{i}(w)
denote the number of times the letter
a_{i}
appears in
w
as a word (when the brackets are removed). The weight of a Hall word
w\in H_{k}
is the same as the length
L(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\nu_{i}(w)
of
w
as a word.

Example 3.2
.

When
k=2
, the first elements of the Hall set
H_{2}
are ordered as follows (where an arbitrary ordering is chosen for words at a fixed weight):
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
[a_{1},a_{2}]
,
[a_{1},[a_{1},a_{2}]]
,
[a_{2},[a_{1},a_{2}]]
,
[a_{1},[a_{1},[a_{1},a_{2}]]]
,
[a_{2},[a_{1},[a_{1},a_{2}]]]
,
[a_{2},[a_{2},[a_{1},a_{2}]]]
,
\dots
.

The number of Hall words
w\in H_{k}
of weight
j
is the value of necklace polynomial
M_{k}(j)=\frac{1}{j}\sum_{d\mid j}\mu(d)k^{j/d}
where
\mu
is the Möbius function. Next, we show that since
A_{2}\subseteq A_{3}\subseteq A_{4}\subseteq\cdots
, we can choose Hall sets
H_{k}
on
A_{k}
in a coherent way.

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists a sequence of linearly ordered sets
H_{2}\subseteq H_{3}\subseteq H_{4}\subseteq\cdots
where
H_{k}
is a Hall set on
A_{k}
and
H_{k+1}\backslash H_{k}
consists of all Hall words involving the letter
a_{k+1}
.

Proof.

Since
H_{k}(1)=A_{k}
, the given ordering on the sets
A_{k}
ensures that
H_{k}(1)
is an initial suborder of
H_{k+1}(1)
and that
H_{k+1}(1)\backslash H_{k}(1)=\{a_{k+1}\}
. Suppose that, for all
i<m
, we have constructed the sets
H_{k}(i)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
so that
H_{k}(i)
is an initial suborder of
H_{k+1}(i)
and that
H_{k+1}(i)\backslash H_{k}(i)
consists of all elements of
H_{k+1}(i)
involving the letter
a_{k+1}
. This induction hypothesis and the Hall set construction described above ensure that
H_{2}(m)\subseteq H_{3}(m)\subseteq H_{4}(m)\subseteq\cdots
as subsets. It follows that
H_{k+1}(m)\backslash H_{k}(m)
is the set of elements of
H_{k+1}(m)
that involve the letter
a_{k+1}
. We choose the ordering of
H_{2}(m)
arbitrarily and recursively choose the ordering on
H_{k+1}(m)
,
k\geq 2
so that
H_{k}(m)
is an initial suborder of
H_{k+1}(m)
. This completes the induction and we obtain Hall sets
H_{k}
on
A_{k}
such that for all
k,i\in\mathbb{N}
,
H_{k}(i)\subseteq H_{k+1}(i)
where
H_{k+1}(i)\backslash H_{k}(i)
is the set of Hall words in
H_{k+1}
of weight
i
that involve the letter
a_{k+1}
. Thus
H_{k}\subseteq H_{k+1}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and if
w\in H_{k+1}\backslash H_{k}
has weight
i
, then
w\in H_{k+1}(i)\backslash H_{k}(i)
.
∎

Since we have chosen
a_{i}
to be an element of
\pi_{r_{i}+1}(S^{r_{i}+1})
, a Hall word
w\in H_{k}
may be identified with the corresponding iterated Whitehead product, which lies in some homotopy group of
X_{\leq k}
. To identify the dimension of this homotopy group, we borrow the following definition from
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.4
.

The
height
of a Hall word
w\in H_{k}
is
h(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}\nu_{i}(w)
.

Remark 3.5
.

For a Hall word
w\in H_{k}
, the height
h(w)
is one less than the dimension of the homotopy group in which
w
is an element, that is
w\in\pi_{h(w)+1}(X_{\leq k})
. For example when
k=3
, we have
a_{1}\in\pi_{r_{1}+1}(S^{r_{1}+1})
,
a_{1}\in\pi_{r_{2}+1}(S^{r_{2}+1})
, and
a_{3}\in\pi_{r_{3}+1}(S^{r_{3}+1})
. Then

(1)

w=[a_{1},a_{2}]\in\pi_{r_{1}+r_{2}+1}(X_{3})
has height
h(w)=r_{1}+r_{2}
,

(2)

w=[a_{1},[a_{1},a_{2}]]\in\pi_{2r_{1}+r_{2}+1}(X_{3})
has height
h(w)=2r_{1}+r_{2}
,

(3)

w=[a_{1},[a_{2},a_{3}]]\in\pi_{r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}+1}(X_{3})
has height
h(w)=r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}
.

Because a Hall word is given by nesting Whitehead products in a binary fashion, Relation (1) of Remark
2.1
makes the following proposition evident.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
w\in H_{k}
be a Hall word regarded as a nested Whitehead product in
\pi_{h(w)+1}(X_{\leq k})
. If we replace any letter of
w
with
0
, then the resulting element of
\pi_{h(w)+1}(X_{\leq k})
is trivial.

Using Proposition
3.3
, we fix Hall sets
H_{k}
on
A_{k}
so that
H_{1}\subseteq H_{2}\subseteq H_{3}\subseteq\cdots
. The following is Hilton’s initial version of the well-known Hilton-Milnor Theorem. We choose to use Hilton’s original formulation in terms of homotopy groups of spheres and Whitehead products since our focus is on shrinking wedges of spheres.

Theorem 3.7

(Hilton
[
8
]
)
.

For given
k\geq 1
and
n>r_{1}
, there is an isomorphism
\phi_{n,k}:\bigoplus_{w\in H_{k}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})\to\pi_{n}(X_{\leq k})
determined by
\phi_{n,k}(f)=w\circ f
for each
f\in\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
.

Remark 3.8
.

Although the direct sum
\bigoplus_{w\in H_{k}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
in Hilton’s Theorem is indexed by the infinite set
H_{k}
, only finitely many summands are non-trivial since
n
is fixed. Indeed, the set
H_{n,k}=\{w\in H_{k}\mid h(w)+1\leq n\}
is finite and the domain of
\phi_{n,k}
can be more efficiently expressed as the finite direct sum
\bigoplus_{w\in H_{n,k}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Fix
k\geq 1
,
n>r_{1}
, and
w\in H_{n,k+1}
. If
w\in H_{n,k}
, then the homomorphism

b_{k+1,k\#}\circ\phi_{n,k+1}:\bigoplus_{w\in H_{n,k+1}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})\to\pi_{n}(X_{\leq k})

maps the summand
\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
isomorphically onto
\phi_{n,k}(\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1}))
and if
w\in H_{n,k+1}\backslash H_{n,k}
, then
b_{k+1,k\#}\circ\phi_{n,k+1}
trivializes
\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
.

Proof.

If
f\in\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
, then
b_{k+1,k\#}\circ\phi_{n,k+1}(f)=[b_{k+1,k}]\circ w\circ f
. Note that
[b_{k+1,k}]\circ w
is still a Whitehead product with the same nested structure but where each appearance of the letter
a_{k+1}
is trivialized. Hence, if
a_{k+1}
appears in
w
, then
[b_{k+1,k}]\circ w=0
by Proposition
3.6
. It follows that if
w\in H_{n,k+1}\backslash H_{n,k}
then
[b_{k+1,k}]\circ w\circ f=0
. On the other hand, if
w\in H_{n,k}
, then
a_{k+1}
does not appear as a letter in
w
, which implies that
[b_{k+1,k}]\circ w=w
and thus
b_{k+1,k\#}\circ\phi_{n,k+1}(f)=w\circ f=\phi_{n,k}(f)
.
∎

Define
H_{\infty}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}H_{k}
and
H_{n,\infty}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}H_{n,k}=\{w\in H_{\infty}\mid h(w)+1\leq n\}
and note that both of these sets are infinite.

Theorem 3.10
.

If
n>r_{1}
, then there is a canonical isomorphism

\phi_{n,\infty}:\prod_{w\in H_{n,\infty}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})\to\check{\pi}_{n}(X)

given by
\phi_{n,\infty}((f_{w})_{w\in H_{n,\infty}})=\left(\sum_{w\in H_{n,k}}w\circ f_{w}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

For each
k\geq 1
, let
p_{k+1,k}:\bigoplus_{w\in H_{n,k+1}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})\to\bigoplus_{w\in H_{n,k}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
be the unique homomorphism, which trivializes
\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
if
w\in H_{n,k+1}\backslash H_{n,k}
and is the identity on
\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
if
w\in H_{n,k}
. By Lemma
3.9
, the following diagram commutes where the horizontal maps are isomorphisms from Hilton’s Theorem.

\textstyle{\bigoplus_{w\in H_{n,k+1}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{n,k+1}}
\scriptstyle{p_{k+1,k}}
\textstyle{\pi_{n}(X_{\leq k+1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{b_{k+1,k\#}}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{w\in H_{n,k}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{n,k}}
\textstyle{\pi_{n}(X_{\leq k})}

Because the maps
p_{k+1,k}
are projection maps of a finite direct product, the inverse limit of the left vertical morphisms is the direct product
\prod_{w\in H_{n,\infty}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
. Hence, taking the corresponding inverse limit of the isomorphisms
\phi_{n,k}
as

yields the desired isomorphism.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

Since
\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})=0
whenever
w\in H_{\infty}\backslash H_{n,\infty}
, we can also express the isomorphism
\phi_{n,\infty}
as a map

\phi_{n,\infty}:\prod_{w\in H_{\infty}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})\to\check{\pi}_{n}(X)

with the same formula as that given in Theorem
3.10
.

Remark 3.12
.

When
w=a_{i}\in H_{\infty}
has weight
L(w)=1
, we have
h(w)=r_{i}
and thus
w
corresponds to a factor
\pi_{n}(S^{r_{i}+1})
in the direct product of Theorem
3.10
. Collecting all such factors together into a single summand, we have
\check{\pi}_{n}(X)\cong\prod_{L(w)>1}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})\oplus\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\pi_{n}(S^{r_{i}+1})
where
\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\pi_{n}(S^{r_{i}+1})
is the previously-defined weight-
1
summand of
\check{\pi}_{n}(X)
. Hence,

\check{\pi}_{n+1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}S^{r_{i}+1},\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}S^{r_{i}+1}\right)\cong\prod_{L(w)>1}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1}).

We are particularly interested in the case where
r_{i}=m-1\geq 1
so that
\bigcurlyvee_{i}S^{r_{i}+1}=\mathbb{E}_{m}
. To analyze this case more carefully, we partition
H_{\infty}
as follows: Let
H_{\infty}(j)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}H_{k}(j)
be the Hall words of weight
j
, e.g.
H_{\infty}(1)=\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\cdots\}
and
H_{\infty}(2)=\{[a_{i},a_{j}]\mid i<j\}
. Note that for each
j\geq 1
,
H_{\infty}(j)
is countably infinite.

Next, we prove our first main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Theorem
3.10
,
\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
is isomorphic to the direct product
\prod_{w\in H_{\infty}}\pi_{n}(S^{h(w)+1})
. We prove the current theorem by rearranging the terms in this product. In this case, we have
r_{i}=m-1
for all
i
. Thus each Hall word
w\in H_{k}
has height
h(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(m-1)\nu_{i}(k)=(m-1)L(w)
where
L(w)
is the weight/length of
w
. Recall that we have defined
H_{\infty}(j)=\{w\in H_{\infty}\mid L(w)=j\}
to be the Hall words of weight
j
. Thus

\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})\cong\prod_{w\in H_{\infty}}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)L(w)+1})\cong\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\prod_{w\in H_{\infty}(j)}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)j+1}).

As
j\to\infty
, the groups
\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)j+1})
have the potential to be non-trivial only when
n\geq(m-1)j+1
or, equivalently, when
j\leq\frac{n-1}{m-1}
. Hence,
\prod_{w\in H_{\infty}(j)}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)j+1})=0
when
j>\frac{n-1}{m-1}
. We have:

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\prod_{w\in H_{\infty}(j)}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)j+1})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq\frac{n-1}{m-1}}\prod_{w\in H_{\infty}(j)}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)j+1})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq\frac{n-1}{m-1}}\left(\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)j+1})\right)^{\mathbb{N}}

where the last isomorphism holds since
m
is fixed and
H_{\infty}(j)
is countably infinite.
∎

We will often express
\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
as the direct sum
\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq\frac{n-1}{m-1}}\mathcal{W}_{j}
where
\mathcal{W}_{j}
is the image of
\prod_{w\in H_{\infty}(j)}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)j+1})
under the isomorphism
\phi_{n,\infty}
. We refer to
\mathcal{W}_{j}
as the
weight-
j
summand
of
\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
. Since
\mathcal{W}_{1}=\prod_{w\in H_{\infty}(1)}\pi_{n}(S^{m})
corresponds precisely to the “weight-
1
summand” of
\check{\pi}_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
as defined in Section
2.3
, we have the following.

Corollary 3.13
.

If
n>m\geq 2
, then

\check{\pi}_{n+1}\left(\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}}S^{m},\mathbb{E}_{m}\right)\cong\bigoplus_{2\leq j\leq\frac{n-1}{m-1}}\left(\pi_{n}(S^{mj-j+1})\right)^{\mathbb{N}}.

Example 3.14
.

Some special cases worth noting are the following.

(1)

When
n=3
and
m=2
, we have a summand for both
j=1,2\leq\frac{3-1}{2-1}
. In this case,

\check{\pi}_{3}(\mathbb{E}_{2})\cong(\pi_{3}(S^{2}))^{\mathbb{N}}\oplus(\pi_{3}(S^{3}))^{\mathbb{N}}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}

Although this is abstractly isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}
, this decomposition shows that the first summand of
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}
is the weight-
1
summand of
\check{\pi}_{3}(\mathbb{E}_{2})
.

(2)

For the groups
\check{\pi}_{n+1}(\mathbb{E}_{n})

For any

Next we consider the special case

at the edge of the unstable range. In this situation, we will need to apply infinite sums and Whitehead products described in Section

.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
m\geq 2
, the canonical homomorphism
\displaystyle\Psi_{2m-1}:\pi_{2m-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m})\to\check{\pi}_{2m-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
is a split epimorphism.

Proof.

First, we apply Theorem
1.1
in the case
n=2m-1
. Since
\frac{2m-2}{m-1}<3
, we have
\check{\pi}_{2m-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m})=\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}
. A splitting homomorphism
\mathcal{W}_{1}\to\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
of
\Psi_{n}
is established in Proposition
4.2
and so it suffices to produce a splitting homomorphism
\mathcal{W}_{2}\to\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
. Recall from Remark
4.1
, that a generic element of
\mathcal{W}_{2}
has the form
\alpha=\left(\sum_{w\in H_{k}(2)}w\circ f_{w}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
where
f_{w}\in\pi_{2m-1}(S^{2m-1})
. Since
H_{k}(2)=\{[a_{i},a_{j}]\mid 1\leq i<j\leq k\}
, write
f_{i,j}
for
f_{w}
when
w=[a_{i},a_{j}]
. Let
\gamma=[id_{S^{2m-1}}]
be the generator of
\pi_{2m-1}(S^{2m-1})
and write
f_{i,j}=\epsilon_{i,j}\gamma
for integer
\epsilon_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
w\circ-
is a homomorphism, we have
w\circ f_{i,j}=[a_{i},a_{j}]\circ\epsilon_{i,j}\gamma=\epsilon_{i,j}([a_{i},a_{j}]\circ\gamma)=\epsilon_{i,j}[a_{i},a_{j}]=[a_{i},\epsilon_{i,j}a_{j}]
. Altogether, we can express
\alpha
more conveniently as

\alpha=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{k}[a_{i},\epsilon_{i,j}a_{j}]\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

where the sums in the sequence are directly extended as
k
increases.

Let
\ell_{j}:S^{m}\to\mathbb{E}_{m}
be the inclusion of the
j
-th sphere so that
a_{j}=[\ell_{j}]
and define an infinite sum map
F_{\alpha}:S^{2m-1}\to\mathbb{E}_{m}
by

F_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]

This sum is continuous since the sequence
\left\{\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to the wedgepoint of
\mathbb{E}_{m}
. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
b_{k}:\mathbb{E}_{m}\to X_{\leq k}
denote the projection map to the union of the first
k
-spheres. For given
i\in\mathbb{N}
, note that
b_{k}\circ\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]
is constant if
i>k
. If
i\leq k
, then

\displaystyle b_{k}\circ\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[b_{k}\circ\ell_{i},b_{k}\circ\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[b_{k}\circ\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}b_{k}\circ\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right].

and we consider two cases. If
i=k
, this is equal to
[\ell_{i},c]
where
c
is constant. Such a map is null-homotopic within the
i
-th wedge-summand of
\mathbb{E}_{m}
. Lastly, if
i<k
, this is homotopic to
\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{k}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]
. All three of these cases are realized by homotopies having image in
X_{\geq i}
. Thus, Proposition
2.7
gives

b_{k}\circ F_{\alpha}\simeq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{k}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right].

On homotopy classes, we have

b_{k\#}([F_{\alpha}])=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left[a_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{k}\epsilon_{i,j}a_{j}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{k}\left[a_{i},\epsilon_{i,j}a_{j}\right]

where the second equality follows from bilinearity of Whitehead products. Since
k
was arbitrary, we conclude that
\Psi_{n}([F_{\alpha}])=(b_{k\#}([F_{\alpha}]))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}=\alpha
.

For the splitting, it suffices to check that the mapping
\alpha\mapsto[F_{\alpha}]
defines a homomorphism
\mathcal{W}_{2}\to\pi_{2m-1}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
. Since our representation of
\alpha
is uniquely determined by the integers
\epsilon_{i,j}
,
j>i
, this function is well-defined. We show that
[F_{\alpha+\beta}]=[F_{\alpha}]+[F_{\beta}]
. Express
\beta\in\mathcal{W}_{2}
as
\beta=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{k}[a_{i},\delta_{i,j}a_{j}]\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
for integers
\delta_{i,j}
,
j>i
and note that
\alpha+\beta=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{k}[a_{i},(\epsilon_{i,j}+\delta_{i,j})a_{j}]\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Recalling the formula for
F_{\alpha}
,
F_{\beta}
, and
F_{\alpha+\beta}
, and applying the infinite-sum homotopies from Section
2
, we have the following sequence:

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}+F_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\delta_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]+\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\delta_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\delta_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{i,j}\ell_{j}+\delta_{i,j}\ell_{j}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\ell_{i},\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}(\epsilon_{i,j}+\delta_{i,j})\ell_{j}\right].

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{\alpha+\beta}

∎

In the unstable range
n>2m-1
, the same argument for surjectivity does not work and it does not appear that Hilton-Milnor invariants overcome the obstacle. Fix
0\neq\beta\in\pi_{n}(S^{2m-1})
. Note that the example element

\left(\left[a_{1},\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}\right]\circ\beta\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}=\left(\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k}[a_{1},a_{i}]\right)\circ\beta\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

of
\mathcal{W}_{2}
is the image of
\left[\ell_{1},\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\ell_{j}\right]\circ\beta\in\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
under
\Psi_{n}
. In contrast, it is not clear if
\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k}([a_{1},a_{i}]\circ\beta)\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{W}_{2}
lies in the image of
\Psi_{n}
. Indeed, one would like to form an infinite sum
\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}[\ell_{1},\ell_{j}]
and apply Hilton-Milnor invariants of
\beta
in the finite-approximation levels but this infinite sum map is not continuous. The lack of obvious continuous representatives may be more analogous to the situation in dimension
n=m=1
where the homomorphism
\Psi_{1}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{E}_{1})\to\check{\pi}_{1}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
fails to be surjective. Here, the analogous sequence of products of commutators

(1,[a_{1},a_{2}],[a_{1},a_{2}][a_{1},a_{3}],[a_{1},a_{2}][a_{1},a_{3}][a_{1},a_{4}],\dots)\in\check{\pi}_{1}(\mathbb{E}_{1})

has no continuous representative in
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
. That surjectivity holds in the critical case
n=2m-1
is then quite striking since it is the only situation where one can easily represent such growing sequences of commutators/Whitehead products. While this leaves us with an incomplete picture of the image of
\Psi_{n}
(for
n>2m-1
), we identify a natural candidate.

For
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
M_{i}
be the collection of Hall words
w\in H_{\infty}
such that

(1)

L(w)\geq 2
,

(2)

i
is the smallest index for which
a_{i}
appears as a letter in
w
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{w\in M_{i}}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)L(w)+1})
and note that
\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)
is naturally a subgroup of
\prod_{w\in H_{\infty}}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)L(w)+1})
. We represent an element of
\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)
as a tuple
(\alpha_{i,w})_{i,w}
for
\alpha_{i,w}\in\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)L(w)+1})
indexed over pairs
(i,w)
with
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
w\in M_{i}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

There is a canonical monomorphism
\Theta:\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)\to\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
. Such that
\Psi_{n}\circ\Theta(\mathcal{G}_{n}(m))=\phi_{n,\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{n}(m))
.

Proof.

Define
\Theta:\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)\to\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
as follows. For Hall word
w
, let
\ell_{w}:S^{(m-1)L(w)+1}\to\mathbb{E}_{m}
be a the canonical map representing
w
, e.g. given by replacing each appearance of
a_{i}
with
\ell_{i}
. Given
\alpha=(\alpha_{i,w})_{i,w}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)
, the set
\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)=\{w\in M_{i}\mid f_{i,w}\neq 0\}
is finite for each
i
(by definition of
\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)
). If
w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)
, let
f_{i,w}:S^{n}\to S^{(m-1)L(w)+1}
be a map representing
\alpha_{i,w}
.
Define

\Theta(\alpha)=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)}\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}\right].

To see that
\Theta
is well-defined, let
f_{i,w}^{\prime}
be another family of representatives for
\alpha_{i,w}
. For given pair
(i,w)
, we have
f_{i,w}\simeq f_{i,w}^{\prime}
and thus
\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)}\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}\simeq\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)}\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}
in
X_{\geq i}
. This forms a sequential homotopy indexed over
i
and thus
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)}\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}\simeq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)}\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}^{\prime}
by Proposition
2.7
.

To check that
\Theta
is a homomorphism, let
\beta=(\beta_{i,w})_{i,w}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)
with representing maps
[g_{i,w}]=\beta_{i,w}
. Note that
\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha+\beta)\subseteq\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)\cup\text{supp}_{i}(\beta)
where
F_{i}=(\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)\cup\text{supp}_{i}(\beta))\backslash\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha+\beta)
is the finite (possibly empty) set of
w\in M_{i}
such that
\alpha_{i,w}
and
\beta_{i,w}
are non-trivial and
\alpha_{i,w}=-\beta_{i,w}
. Note that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in F_{i}}\ell_{w}\circ(f_{i,w}+g_{i,w})
is null-homotopic. Thus applying linearity of infinite sums to
A=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in F_{i}}(\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w})+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in F_{i}}(\ell_{w}\circ g_{i,w})\right]
, we have
A=0
. Then applications of Propositions
2.5
and
2.7
give that
\Theta(\alpha+\beta)
is equal to:

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha+\beta)}\ell_{w}\circ(f_{i,w}+g_{i,w})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha+\beta)}\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}+\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha+\beta)}\ell_{w}\circ g_{i,w}\right]+A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha+\beta)\cup F_{i}}\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}+\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha+\beta)\cup F_{i}}\ell_{w}\circ g_{i,w}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)}\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}+\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\beta)}\ell_{w}\circ g_{i,w}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)}\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}\right]+\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\beta)}\ell_{w}\circ g_{i,w}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta(\alpha)+\Theta(\beta)

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The first summand
\prod_{\mathbb{N}}\pi_{n}(S^{m})
comes from the splitting of the weight-
1
summand
\mathcal{W}_{1}\to\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
in Theorem
2.12
. The second summand we construct as follows. Proposition
4.5
and the bijectivity of
\phi_{n,\infty}
imply that
H=\Theta(\mathcal{G}_{n}(m))
is a subgroup of
\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
for which the restriction
\Psi_{n}|_{H}:H\to\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)
is an isomorphism. To show that
H
splits in a summand distinct from the weight-
1
summand, we must show that
H\leq\ker(\sigma_{\#})\leq\pi_{n}(\mathbb{E}_{m})
where
\sigma:X\to\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}}S^{m}
is the inclusion. Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{i,w})\in\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)
with
\alpha_{i,w}=[f_{i,w}]
. If
p_{j}:\mathbb{E}_{m}\to X_{j}
is the retraction to the
j
-th sphere, then
p_{j}\circ\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}
is null-homotopic for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
and
w\in M_{i}
(since
\ell_{w}
is an iterated Whitehead product map determined by a Hall word
w
, which involves at least two distinct letters). Thus
[\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}]\in\ker(\sigma_{\#})
for all pairs
(i,w)
with
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
w\in M_{i}
. It is shown in
[
4
]
that
\ker(\sigma_{\#})
is “closed under infinite sums” and it follows directly that
\Theta(\alpha)=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{w\in\text{supp}_{i}(\alpha)}\ell_{w}\circ f_{i,w}\right]\in\ker(\sigma_{\#})
. Hence,
H\leq\ker(\sigma_{\#})
.

Finally, it suffices to find an expression for
\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)
that matches the expression in the statement of the theorem. For all
j\geq 2
, let
M_{i}(j)=\{w\in M_{i}\mid L(w)=j\}
be the collection of Hall words of weight
j
with
i
being the smallest index for which
a_{i}
appears in
w
and note that
M_{i}(j)
is countably infinite. Since
\{M_{i}(j)\}_{j\geq 2}
is a partition of
M_{i}
and
\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)j+1})
can only be non-trivial if

, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n}(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{w\in M_{i}}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)L(w)+1})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\bigoplus_{2\leq j\leq\frac{n-1}{m-1}}\bigoplus_{w\in M_{i}(j)}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)j+1})\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{2\leq j\leq\frac{n-1}{m-1}}\left(\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{w\in M_{i}(j)}\pi_{n}(S^{(m-1)j+1})\right)

where the last isomorphism is justified by the fact that finite direct sums commute with arbitrary direct products. By enumerating
M_{i}(j)
and replacing this indexing set with
\mathbb{N}
, for each

, we match the form appearing in the statement of the theorem.
∎

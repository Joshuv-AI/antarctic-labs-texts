On the eigenvalues and Fučík spectrum of p p -Laplace local and nonlocal operator with mixed interpolated Hardy term
Shammi Malhotra, Sarika Goyal, K. Sreenadh

Abstract
In this article, we are concerned with the eigenvalue problem driven by the mixed local and nonlocal p p -Laplacian operator having the interpolated Hardy term 𝒯 ⁡ ( u ) := − Δ p ​ u + ( − Δ p ) s ​ u − μ ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u | x | p ​ θ \mathcal{T}(u):=-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u-\mu\frac{|u|^{p-2}u}{|x|^{p\theta}} where 0 < s < 1 < p < N 0<s<1<p<N , θ ∈ [ s , 1 ] \theta\in[s,1] , and μ ∈ ( 0 , μ 0 ​ ( θ ) ) \mu\in(0,\mu_{0}(\theta)) . Proof.

4
Eigenvalue Problem for
\mathcal{T}

In this section, we establish the existence of the first eigenvalue of the operator
\mathcal{T}
and study some of its properties. We recall that
\mathcal{T}
is defined as

\mathcal{T}(u)=(-\Delta_{p}+(-\Delta_{p})^{s})u-\mu\frac{|u|^{p-2}u}{|x|^{p\theta}}.

Firstly, we would like to show that a sequence of eigenvalues of the operator

\mathcal{A}=\{A\subset X(\Omega):A\mbox{ is closed},A=-A\}.

In this class, the genus is defined for

\begin{split}\gamma^{*}(A)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }A=\emptyset,\\
\inf\{m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\};\mbox{ there exists }h\in C(A;\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}),h(-u)=h(u)\}&\mbox{ if }A\neq\emptyset,\end{cases}\end{split}

if no such infimum exists then we take
\gamma^{*}(A)=\infty
. Now, we have our theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
2\leq p<N
. Then, there exists a sequence of increasing eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
of the operator
\mathcal{T}
with the characterization

\lambda_{k}=\inf_{K\subset\mathcal{A}_{k}}\sup_{u\in K}\mathcal{J}(u)

where
\mathcal{A}_{k}=\{K\in\mathcal{A}:K\subset S,\gamma^{*}(K)\geq k\}

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{J}
is even, and
S
is a complete, symmetric, and
C^{1,1}

X(\Omega)
. Additionally,
\mathcal{J}
is bounded below on

and satisfies the (P.-S.) condition on

(see Lemma
3.3

∎

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}L_{c}(\psi_{k},\phi_{2})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}L(\psi_{k},\phi_{2})\right)~dxdy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}L_{c}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}L(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\right)~dxdy\geq 0.

So, the continuous and discrete version of Picone identity in Proposition
2.5
and
2.4
implies that
\phi_{1}=c\phi_{2}
a.e. for some constant
c
. Therefore,
\lambda_{1}
is simple.
∎

Now we show that in contrast to the first eigenfunction being of constant sign, eigenfunctions corresponding to other eigenvalues change sign.

Theorem 4.4
.

If
w
is an eigenfunction corresponding to eigenvalue
\lambda
such that
\lambda\neq\lambda_{1}
. Then
w
changes sign.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{1}
be the eigenfunction corresponding to the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
. Then
\phi_{1}
and
w
satisfy

-\Delta_{p}\phi_{1}+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}\phi_{1}-\mu\frac{|\phi_{1}|^{p-2}\phi_{1}}{|x|^{p\theta}}=\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{p-1}\quad\text{in}\,\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)

(4.31)

-\Delta_{p}w+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}w-\mu\frac{|w|^{p-2}w}{|x|^{p\theta}}=\lambda|w|^{p-2}w\quad\text{in}\,\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)

(4.32)

respectively. Suppose
w
does not change the sign. Then, we may assume
w\geq 0
. Let
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 2}
be a sequence in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\psi_{k}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
,
\psi_{k}\to\phi_{1}
in
X(\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
. Now we consider the test functions
w_{1}=\phi_{1}
,
w_{k}=\frac{\psi_{k}^{p}}{\left(w+\frac{1}{k}\right)^{p-1}}
. Then
w_{1},w_{k}\in X(\Omega)
. Taking
w_{1}
and
w_{k}
as test functions in (
4.31
) and (
4.32
) respectively, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi_{1}(x)-\phi_{1}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dxdy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi_{1}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}dx=\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\phi_{1}|^{p}dx

(4.33)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w\nabla\left(\frac{\psi_{k}^{p}}{\left(w+\frac{1}{k}\right)^{p-1}}\right)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(w(x)-w(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\left(\frac{\psi_{k}^{p}(x)}{\left(w+\frac{1}{k}\right)^{p-1}(x)}-\frac{\psi_{k}^{p}(y)}{\left(w+\frac{1}{k}\right)^{p-1}(y)}\right)

\displaystyle-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|w|^{p-2}w}{|x|^{p\theta}}\frac{\psi_{k}^{p}}{\left(w+\frac{1}{k}\right)^{p-1}}dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p-2}w\frac{\psi_{k}^{p}}{\left(w+\frac{1}{k}\right)^{p-1}}dx.

Since
L(\psi_{k},w+\frac{1}{k})\geq 0
and
L_{c}(\psi_{k},w+\frac{1}{k})\geq 0
, we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi_{k}(x)-\psi_{k}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dxdy-\int_{\Omega}\left(\lambda+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{p\theta}}\right)\psi_{k}^{p}\left(\frac{w}{w+\frac{1}{k}}\right)^{p-1}dx\geq 0.

(4.34)

Subtracting (
4.33
) from (
4.34
) and taking the limit as
k\to\infty
, we obtain

(\lambda-\lambda_{1})\int_{\Omega}|\phi_{1}|^{p}\leq 0,

which gives a contradiction to the fact that
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
. Hence,
w
changes sign.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
1<p<N
. Then
\lambda_{1}
is isolated in the spectrum of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

On contrary, assume that
\lambda_{1}
is not isolated in the spectrum. Then there exists a sequence of eigenfunctions
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
in
X(\Omega)
corresponding to a sequence of eigenvalues
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
satisfying:

-\Delta_{p}\psi_{k}+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}\psi_{k}-\mu\frac{|\psi_{k}|^{p-2}\psi_{k}}{|x|^{p\theta}}=\mu_{k}|\psi_{k}|^{p-2}\psi_{k}\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,\quad\psi_{k}=0\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,

(4.35)

with
\|\psi_{k}\|_{p}=1
and
\mu_{k}\to\lambda_{1}
as
k\to\infty
. Moreover, we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi_{k}(x)-\psi_{k}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\psi_{k}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx=\mu_{k}\int_{\Omega}|\psi_{k}|^{p}\,dx=\mu_{k}.

The mixed interpolated Hardy inequality (
3.14
) yields that
\{\psi_{k}\}
is a bounded sequence in
X(\Omega)
. Thus, up to a subsequence, we have
\psi_{k}\rightharpoonup\phi
weakly in
X(\Omega)
and
\psi_{k}\to\phi
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
. Then, Lemma
3.2
gives pointwise convergence of the gradients. Hence,

-\Delta_{p}\phi+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}\phi-\mu\frac{|\phi|^{p-2}\phi}{|x|^{p\theta}}=\lambda_{1}|\phi|^{p-2}\phi\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,\quad\phi=0\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.

Therefore,
\phi=\pm\phi_{1}
. Since
\psi_{k}
changes sign by Theorem
4.4
, without loss of generality, we can assume that
u=+\phi_{1}
, then

|\{x:\psi_{k}(x)<0\}|\to 0\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

(4.36)

Putting
\psi_{k}^{-}
as a test function in (
4.35
), we get

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(\psi_{k}(x)-\psi_{k}(y))(\psi_{k}^{-}(x)-\psi_{k}^{-}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}+\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{(\psi_{k}^{-})^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,=\mu_{k}\int_{\Omega}(\psi_{k}^{-})^{p}\,.

Using (
2.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi_{k}^{-}(x)-\psi_{k}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dx\,dy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(\psi_{k}(x)-\psi_{k}(y))(\psi_{k}^{-}(x)-\psi_{k}^{-}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dx\,dy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{k}\int_{\Omega}|\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}dx.

Thus, mixed interpolated Hardy inequality (
3.14
), Hölder inequality, and Sobolev inequality yield

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\mu\xi}{C_{H}}\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\mu\xi}{C_{H}}\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}+\left(1-\frac{\mu(1-\xi)}{C_{N,p,s}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi_{k}^{-}(x)-\psi_{k}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi_{k}^{-}(x)-\psi_{k}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dx\,dy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{k}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p^{*}}\right)^{p/p^{*}}|\{x:\psi_{k}(x)<0\}|^{1-p/p^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{k}C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{k}^{-}|^{p}\right)|\{x:\psi_{k}(x)<0\}|^{1-p/p^{*}},

where
p^{*}=pN/(N-p)
. It implies that

|\{x:\psi_{k}(x)<0\}|^{1-p/p^{*}}\geq\left(1-\frac{\mu\xi}{C_{H}}\right)\mu_{k}^{-1}C^{-1},

which leads to a contradiction with (
4.36
). Hence,
\lambda_{1}
is isolated in the spectrum of
\mathcal{T}
.
∎

5
Fučík Spectrum

In this section, we study the Fučík spectrum, denoted by
\displaystyle\sum_{p,\mu,\theta}
, of (
1.2
). It is defined as

\sum_{p,\mu,\theta}:=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\eqref{pq}\mbox{ has a nontrivial solution}\}.

Next, we show that the points in
\sum_{p,\mu,\theta}
are associated with the critical value of some restricted functional. For nonnegative real
d
, recalling the functional
\mathcal{J}_{d}:X(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{d}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx-d\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{p}\,dx.

Also,
\mathcal{J}_{d}\in C^{1}(X(\Omega),\mathbb{R})
and for any
v\in X(\Omega)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\langle\mathcal{J}_{d}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla v+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{p-2}uv}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx-d\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{p-1}v\,dx.

Moreover,
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{d}:=\mathcal{J}_{d}|_{S}\in C^{1}(X(\Omega),\mathbb{R})
, where
S
is defined as

S:=\left\{u\in X(\Omega):I(u):=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}=1\right\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
d\geq 0
,
(d+t,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
belongs to the spectrum
\sum_{p,\mu,\theta}
if and only if there exists a critical point
u\in S
of
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{d}
such that
t=\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{d}(u)
, a critical value.

Proof.

From Lagrange theorem, we deduce that
u\in S
is a critical point of
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{d}
if and only if there exists
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\langle\mathcal{J}_{d}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=tp\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}uv,

(5.37)

for all
v\in X(\Omega)
. Hence,
u\in S
is a nontrivial weak solution to the problem

-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u-\mu\frac{|u|^{p-2}u}{|x|^{p\theta}}=(d+t)(u^{+})^{p-1}+t(u^{-})^{p-1}\text{ in }D^{\prime}(\Omega),\quad u=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,

which exactly means
(d+t,t)\in\displaystyle\sum_{p,\mu,\theta}
. Putting
v=u
in (
5.37
), we deduce
t=\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{d}(u)
.
∎

Next, we show that the vertical and horizontal lines that passes through
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1})
lies in the Fučík spectrum. For this, we have the following propositions.

Proposition 5.1
.

For
d\geq 0
, the first eigenfunction
\phi_{1}
is a global minimum for
\mathcal{J}_{d}
with
\mathcal{J}_{d}(\phi_{1})=\lambda_{1}-d
. The corresponding point in
\Sigma_{p,\mu,\theta}
is
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1}-d)
, which lies on the vertical line through
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1})
.

Proof.

Consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{d}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx-d\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,dx-d\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{p}\,dx\geq\lambda_{1}-d.

Then,
\mathcal{J}_{d}
is bounded below by
\lambda_{1}-d
. Also,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{d}(\phi_{1})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}-d\int_{\Omega}\phi_{1}^{p}\,dx=\lambda_{1}-d.

Therefore,
\phi_{1}
is a global minimum of
\mathcal{J}_{d}
with
\mathcal{J}_{d}(\phi_{1})=\lambda_{1}-d
.
∎

Another set of points in the Fučík spectrum comes from a second critical point of
\mathcal{J}_{d}
at
-\phi_{1}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The negative eigenfunction
-\phi_{1}
is a strict local minimum for
\mathcal{J}_{d}
with
\mathcal{J}_{d}(-\phi_{1})=\lambda_{1}
. The corresponding point in
\Sigma_{p,\mu,\theta}
is
(\lambda_{1}+d,\lambda_{1})
.

Proof.

On the contrary, if
-\phi_{1}
is not the strict local minima, then there exists a sequence
u_{k}\in S
,
u_{k}\neq-\phi_{1}
with
\mathcal{J}_{d}(u_{k})\leq\lambda_{1}
,
u_{k}\to-\phi_{1}
. Since
u_{k}\neq-\phi_{1}
, then
|\{x\in\Omega:~u_{k}(x)<0\}|>0
for sufficiently large
k
. If
u_{k}\leq 0
for a.e
x\in\Omega
, then

\mathcal{J}_{d}(u_{k})=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{k}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx>\lambda_{1},

as
u_{k}\neq\pm\phi_{1}
. This gives a contradiction as
\mathcal{J}_{d}(u_{k})\leq\lambda_{1}
. So
u_{k}
changes sign for sufficiently large
k
.

Note that
(a+b)^{p}\geq a^{p}+b^{p}
for
a,b\geq 0
and
p>1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

\displaystyle\frac{|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{{\begin{array}[]{l}\scriptstyle{\{u_{k}(x)>0,}\\
\scriptstyle{u_{k}(y)>0\}}\end{array}}}\frac{|u_{k}^{+}(x)-u_{k}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}+\iint_{{\begin{array}[]{l}\scriptscriptstyle{\{u_{k}(x)>0,}\\
\scriptscriptstyle{u_{k}(y)<0\}}\end{array}}}\frac{|u_{k}^{+}(x)+u_{k}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}

\displaystyle+\iint_{{\begin{array}[]{l}\scriptscriptstyle{\{u_{k}(x)<0,}\\
\scriptscriptstyle{u_{k}(y)>0\}}\end{array}}}\frac{|u_{k}^{-}(x)+u_{k}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}+\iint_{{\begin{array}[]{l}\scriptscriptstyle{\{u_{k}(x)<0,}\\
\scriptscriptstyle{u_{k}(y)<0\}}\end{array}}}\frac{|u_{k}^{-}(x)-u_{k}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\iint_{{\begin{array}[]{l}\scriptscriptstyle{\{u_{k}(x)>0,}\\
\scriptscriptstyle{u_{k}(y)>0\}}\end{array}}}\frac{|u_{k}^{+}(x)-u_{k}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}+\iint_{{\begin{array}[]{l}\scriptscriptstyle{\{u_{k}(x)>0,}\\
\scriptscriptstyle{u_{k}(y)<0\}}\end{array}}}\frac{|u_{k}^{+}(x)|^{p}+|u_{k}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}

\displaystyle+\iint_{{\begin{array}[]{l}\scriptscriptstyle{\{u_{k}(x)<0,}\\
\scriptscriptstyle{u_{k}(y)>0\}}\end{array}}}\frac{|u_{k}^{-}(x)|^{p}+|u_{k}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}+\iint_{{\begin{array}[]{l}\scriptscriptstyle{\{u_{k}(x)<0,}\\
\scriptscriptstyle{u_{k}(y)<0\}}\end{array}}}\frac{|u_{k}^{-}(x)-u_{k}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{k}^{+}(x)-u_{k}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{k}^{-}(x)-u_{k}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}.

Now, using the above, mixed interpolated Hardy inequality (
3.14
) and definition of
\lambda_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{d}(u_{k})\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}^{+}|^{p}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}^{-}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{k}^{+}(x)-u_{k}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{k}^{-}(x)-u_{k}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{k}^{+}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{k}^{-}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx-d\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\mu\xi}{C_{H}}\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}^{+}|^{p}+\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{-})^{p}-d\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[r_{k}\left(1-\frac{\mu\xi}{C_{H}}\right)-d\right]\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{p}\,dx+\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{-})^{p},

with
r_{k}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}^{+}|^{p}/\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{p}
.
As
u_{k}\in S
, we obtain

\mathcal{J}_{d}(u_{k})\leq\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{p}\,dx+\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{-})^{p}\,dx.

Combining both the inequalities, we have

r_{k}\left(1-\frac{\mu\xi}{C_{H}}\right)-d\leq\lambda_{1}

which contradicts to the fact that
r_{k}\to+\infty
from Lemma
2.4
of
[
8
]
, as required.
∎

Next, we obtain a third critical point of
\mathcal{J}_{d}
in the following lemma with the help of Proposition
2.1
and first two critical points
\phi_{1}
and
-\phi_{1}
of
\mathcal{J}_{d}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
be such that

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{d}(u)>\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{d}(-\phi_{1})

for all
u\in B(-\phi_{1},\epsilon_{0})\cap S
with
u\neq-\phi_{1}
, where the ball is taken in
X(\Omega)
. Then for any
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
,

\inf\{\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{d}(u):u\in S\text{ and }\|u-(-\phi_{1})\|=\epsilon\}>\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{d}(-\phi_{1}).

Proof.

The proof of the following lemma follows from the Lemma
2.9
of
[
8
]
.
∎

Now applying the Mountain pass Lemma
2.1
with the minimax level defined as

c(d):=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{u\in\gamma([-1,1])}\mathcal{J}_{d}(u),

(5.38)

with
\Gamma=\{\gamma\in C([-1,1],S):\gamma(-1)=-\phi_{1}\text{ and }\gamma(1)=\phi_{1}\}
, we have proved the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

For each
d\geq 0
, the point
(d+c(d),c(d))
, where
c(d)>\lambda_{1}
is defined by the minimax formula (
5.38
), then the point
(d+c(d),c(d))
belongs to
\Sigma_{p,\mu,\theta}
.

It is a trivial fact that
\Sigma_{p,\mu,\theta}
is symmetric with respect to the diagonal. The whole curve that we obtain using Theorem
5.1
and symmetrizing is denoted by

\mathcal{C}=\{(d+c(d),c(d)),(c(d),d+c(d)):d\geq 0\}.

Next we show that the trivial lines
\mathbb{R}\times\{\lambda_{1}\}
and
\{\lambda_{1}\}\times\mathbb{R}
are isolated in
\Sigma_{p,\mu,\theta}
and then we state some topological properties of the functional
\mathcal{J}_{d}.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
1<p<N
. Then there does not exist any sequence
(a_{k},b_{k})\in\sum_{p,\mu,\theta}
with
a_{k}>\lambda_{1}
and
b_{k}>\lambda_{1}
such that
(a_{k},b_{k})\to(a,b)
with
a=\lambda_{1}
or
b=\lambda_{1}
. Therefore, the lines
\mathbb{R}\times\{\lambda_{1}\}
and
\{\lambda_{1}\}\times\mathbb{R}
are isolated in
\sum_{p,\mu,\theta}
.

Proof.

On the contrary, assume that there exists a sequence
(a_{k},b_{k})\in\sum_{p,\mu,\theta}
with
a_{k}>\lambda_{1},b_{k}>\lambda_{1}
and
(a_{k},b_{k})\to(a,b)
with
a=\lambda_{1}
or
b=\lambda_{1}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume
a=\lambda_{1}
. Let
u_{k}\in X(\Omega)
be a solution of

-\Delta_{p}u_{k}+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u_{k}-\mu\frac{|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}}{|x|^{p\theta}}=a_{k}(u_{k}^{+})^{p-1}-b_{k}(u_{k}^{-})^{p-1}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad u_{k}=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega

(5.39)

with
\|u_{k}\|_{p}=1
. Then, testing (
5.39
) with
u_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{k}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx

\displaystyle=a_{k}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{p}\,dx-b_{k}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{-})^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(a_{k}+b_{k})\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{p}=(a_{k}+b_{k}).

From mixed interpolated Hardy inequality (
3.14
) we deduce that
\{u_{k}\}
is a bounded sequence in
X(\Omega)
. Therefore up to a subsequence
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
X(\Omega)
and
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Additionally, Lemma
3.2
gives that
\nabla u_{k}(x)\to\nabla u(x)
a.e. in
\Omega
as
k\to\infty
. Thus, taking limit
k\to\infty
in (
5.39
), we get

-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u-\mu\frac{|u|^{p-2}u}{|x|^{p\theta}}=\lambda_{1}(u^{+})^{p-1}-b(u^{-})^{p-1}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.

(5.40)

Taking
u^{+}
as a test function in (
5.40
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{+}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(u(x)-u(y))(u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u^{+})^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx=\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{p}.

Using the definition of
\lambda_{1}
together with the inequality (
2.10
) in the above, we deduce

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{p}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{+}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u^{+})^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx\leq\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{p}.

Thus

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{+}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u^{+}(x)-u^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u^{+})^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}\,dx=\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{p}\,dx.

Hence, from the simplicity of the first eigenvalue, either
u^{+}\equiv 0
or
u=\phi_{1}
. If
u^{+}\equiv 0
, then
u\leq 0
and (
5.39
) implies that
u
is an eigenfunction with
u\leq 0

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi_{1}^{*}(x)-\phi_{1}^{*}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dy~dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi_{1}(x)-\phi_{1}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dy~dx.

(6.50)

Also, using the Rearrangement inequality (see Theorem
3.4
and property
(v)
in section
3.3
of
[
25
]
), we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi_{1}|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}~dx\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}}(|\phi_{1}|^{p})^{*}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{p}}\right)^{*}dx=\int_{\Omega^{*}}\frac{(|\phi_{1}|^{p})^{*}}{|x|^{p}}dx=\int_{\Omega^{*}}\frac{(\phi_{1}^{*})^{p}}{|x|^{p}}dx

(6.51)

Next, as we know that
\int_{\Omega}\phi_{1}^{p}=\int_{\Omega^{*}}(\phi_{1}^{*})^{p}
. Then, using Pòlya’s inequality (see Theorem
2.1.3
of
[
23
]
), (
6.50
), and (
6.51
), we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega^{*}}|\nabla\phi_{1}^{*}|^{p}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi_{1}^{*}(x)-\phi_{1}^{*}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}~dxdy-\mu\int_{\Omega^{*}}\frac{|\phi_{1}^{*}|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}~dx}{\int_{\Omega^{*}}(\phi_{1}^{*})^{p}~dx}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{p}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi_{1}(x)-\phi_{1}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}~dxdy-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi_{1}|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}~dx}{\int_{\Omega}(\phi_{1})^{p}~dx}=\lambda_{1}(\Omega).

This proves our assertion.
∎

We start this section by obtaining some regularity estimates of the eigenfunctions, which are essential in proving further properties of the spectrum of the operator
\mathcal{T}
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
1<p<N
and
\phi
be an eigenfunction associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda
of the operator
\mathcal{T}
. Then
\phi\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega\setminus\{0\})
. Furthermore, for
p\geq 2
there exists some
\delta>0
such that
\phi\in C_{loc}^{\delta}(\Omega\setminus\{0\})
. In particular,
\phi
is continuous on
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Choose a ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
such that
d(B_{R}(x_{0}),0)\geq\rho
. Then for all
v
such that
\phi v\geq 0
with
Supp(v)\subset B_{R}(x_{0})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{p-2}\nabla\phi\nabla v+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(\phi(x)-\phi(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}

\displaystyle=\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi|^{p-2}\phi v}{|x|^{p\theta}}+\lambda\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{p-2}\phi v

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{p-2}\phi v,

(6.52)

where
\lambda_{\rho}=\frac{\mu}{\rho^{p\theta}}+\lambda
.
Taking
v(x)=\zeta^{p}(x)\phi_{+}^{\beta-(p-1)}(x)
as the test function with
\alpha=\beta-(p-1)
and
\zeta\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
,
\zeta\equiv 1
in
B_{r^{*}}(x_{0})
,
Supp(\zeta)\subset B_{R_{*}}(x_{0})
with
r^{*}<R^{*}\leq R
and
|\nabla\zeta|\leq\frac{1}{R^{*}-r^{*}}
. We have

\nabla v=p\zeta^{p-1}\phi_{+}^{\alpha}\nabla\zeta+\zeta^{p}\alpha\phi_{+}^{\alpha-1}\nabla\phi_{+}.

(6.53)

Also, using the inequality (
2.7
), we have

\begin{split}|\phi(x)&-\phi(y)|^{p-2}(\phi(x)-\phi(y))(v(x)-v(y))\\
&=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }\phi(x)<0,\phi(y)<0,\\
|\phi^{-}(x)+\phi^{+}(y)|^{p-2}(\phi^{-}(x)+\phi^{+}(y))v(y)&\mbox{ if }\phi(x)<0,\phi(y)\geq 0,\\
|\phi^{+}(x)+\phi^{+}(y)|^{p-2}(\phi^{+}(x)+\phi^{+}(y))v(x)&\mbox{ if }\phi(x)\geq 0,\phi(y)<0,\\
F(\phi(x)-\phi(y))((\zeta^{p/\alpha}\phi_{+})^{\alpha}(x)-(\zeta^{p/\alpha}\phi_{+})^{\alpha}(y))&\mbox{ if }\phi(x)\geq 0,\phi(y)\geq 0,\end{cases}\\
&\geq 0.\end{split}

(6.54)

Using (
6.53
) and (
6.54
) in (
6.52
), we deduce

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{+}|^{p}\zeta^{p}\phi_{+}^{\alpha-1}\leq-p\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{p-1}\phi_{+}^{\alpha}\langle|\nabla\phi_{+}|^{p-2}\nabla\phi_{+},\nabla\zeta\rangle+\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{+}^{p-1+\alpha}\zeta^{p}.

As
\alpha=\frac{(\alpha-1)(p-1)}{p}+\frac{\alpha+p-1}{p}
, the Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{+}|^{p}\zeta^{p}\phi_{+}^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{\Omega}\left(\zeta^{p-1}|\nabla\phi_{+}|^{p-1}\phi_{+}^{\frac{(\alpha-1)(p-1)}{p}}\right)\left(\phi_{+}^{\frac{\alpha+p-1}{p}}|\nabla\zeta|\right)+\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{p}\phi_{+}^{p-1+\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\alpha\epsilon^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\zeta^{p}|\nabla\phi_{+}|^{p}\phi_{+}^{\alpha-1}}{p/(p-1)}+\frac{\phi_{+}^{\alpha+p-1}|\nabla\zeta|^{p}}{p\epsilon^{p}\alpha^{p-1}}\right]+\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{p}\phi_{+}^{p-1+\alpha}

for some
\epsilon>0
. This implies

\alpha\left(1-(p-1)\epsilon^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{+}|^{p}\zeta^{p}\phi_{+}^{\alpha-1}\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{p}\alpha^{p-1}}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{+}^{\alpha+p-1}|\nabla\zeta|^{p}+\lambda_{\rho}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{p}\phi_{+}^{p-1+\alpha}.

Choosing
\epsilon
such that
(p-1)\epsilon^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{+}|^{p}\zeta^{p}\phi_{+}^{\alpha-1}\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon^{p}\alpha^{p}}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{+}^{\alpha+p-1}|\nabla\zeta|^{p}+\frac{2\lambda_{\rho}}{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{p}\phi_{+}^{p-1+\alpha},

that can be rewritten in terms of
\beta
as

\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{p}|\nabla\phi_{+}^{\frac{\beta}{p}}|^{p}\leq\frac{2\beta^{p}}{(\epsilon p(\beta-p+1))^{p}}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{+}^{\beta}|\nabla\zeta|^{p}+\frac{2\lambda_{\rho}\beta^{p}}{(\beta-p+1)p^{p}}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{p}\phi_{+}^{\beta}.

Thus, Sobolev inequality gives

\displaystyle S\left(\int_{\Omega}|\zeta\phi_{+}^{\beta/p}|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\zeta\phi_{+}^{\beta/p})|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{p}|\nabla\phi_{+}^{\frac{\beta}{p}}|^{p}+2^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\zeta|^{p}|\phi_{+}|^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{2\beta^{p}}{(\epsilon p(\beta-p+1))^{p}}\right)2^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{+}^{\beta}|\nabla\zeta|^{p}+\frac{2^{p}\lambda_{\rho}\beta^{p}}{(\beta-p+1)p^{p}}\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{p}\phi_{+}^{\beta}.

Using the properties of
\zeta
and
\beta\geq p
, we deduce

\begin{split}\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\beta p^{*}/p}(B_{r^{*}})}&\leq(S^{-1})^{1/\beta}\beta^{p/\beta}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\beta^{p}}+\frac{2}{(\epsilon p(\beta-p+1))^{p}}\right)\frac{2^{p-1}}{(R^{*}-r^{*})^{p}}\right.\\
&\quad\left.+\frac{2^{p}\lambda_{\rho}}{(\beta-p+1)p^{p}}\right]^{1/\beta}\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\beta}(B_{R^{*}})}\\
&\leq(S^{-1})^{1/\beta}\beta^{p/\beta}\left[\left(\frac{1}{p^{p}}+\frac{2}{(\epsilon p)^{p}}\right)\frac{2^{p-1}}{(R^{*}-r^{*})^{p}}+\frac{2^{p}\lambda_{\rho}}{p^{p}}\right]^{1/\beta}\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\beta}(B_{R^{*}})}.\end{split}

Taking
c_{0}=\left(\frac{1}{p^{p}}+\frac{2}{(\epsilon p)^{p}}\right),\chi=\frac{p^{*}}{p},\gamma_{i}=\beta_{0}\chi^{i}
with
\beta_{0}=p
, and
r_{j}=r+2^{-j}(R-r)
for
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then

\begin{split}\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{1}}(B_{r_{1}})}&\leq(S^{-1})^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}\gamma_{0}^{\frac{p}{\gamma_{0}}}\left[\frac{2^{p-1}c_{0}}{(r_{0}-r_{1})^{p}}+\frac{2^{p}\lambda_{\rho}}{p^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{r_{0}})}\\
&\leq(2^{p-1}S^{-1})^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}\gamma_{0}^{\frac{p}{\gamma_{0}}}\left[\frac{2^{p}c_{0}}{(R-r)^{p}}+\frac{2\lambda_{\rho}}{p^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{r_{0}})}.\end{split}

Thus, we have

\begin{split}&\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{2}}(B_{r_{2}})}\leq(2^{p-1}S^{-1})^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}}\gamma_{1}^{\frac{p}{\gamma_{1}}}\left[\frac{2^{2p}c_{0}}{(R-r)^{p}}+\frac{2\lambda_{\rho}}{p^{p}}\right]^{1/\gamma_{1}}\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{1}}(B_{r_{1}})}\\
&\leq(2^{p-1}S^{-1})^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}\gamma_{1}^{\frac{p}{\gamma_{1}}}\gamma_{0}^{\frac{p}{\gamma_{0}}}\left[\frac{2^{2p}c_{0}}{(R-r)^{p}}+\frac{2\lambda_{\rho}}{p^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}}\left[\frac{2^{p}c_{0}}{(R-r)^{p}}+\frac{2\lambda_{\rho}}{p^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}}\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{r_{0}})}.\end{split}

Hence, recursively one obtain

\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{j}}(B_{r_{j}})}\leq(2^{p-1}S^{-1})^{\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}}}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{i}^{\frac{p}{\gamma_{i}}}\left[\frac{2^{(i+1)p}c_{0}}{(R-r)^{p}}+\frac{2\lambda_{\rho}}{p^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}}}\right)\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{r_{0}})},

for all
j\geq 1
. As
2^{(i+1)p}\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
, there exists
c_{0}^{*}
such that

\frac{2\lambda_{\rho}}{p^{p}}\leq c_{0}^{*}\frac{2^{(i+1)p}c_{0}}{(R-r)^{p}}\mbox{ for all }i\geq 1.

Hence,

\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{j}}(B_{r_{j}})}\leq\left(\frac{S^{-1}2^{p-1}(1+c_{0}^{*})c_{0}}{(R-r)^{p}}\right)^{\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}}}2^{\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(i+1)p}{\gamma_{i}}}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\gamma_{i}^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}}}\right)^{p}\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{0}}(B_{r_{0}})}.

(6.55)

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{\chi^{i}}\leq\frac{\chi}{\beta_{0}(\chi-1)}=\frac{N}{\beta_{0}p}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{i}{\gamma_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{i}{\chi^{i}}\leq\frac{\chi}{\beta_{0}(1-\chi)^{2}}=\frac{N^{2}}{\beta_{0}p^{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{\log(\gamma_{i})}{\gamma_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(\beta_{0})}{\beta_{0}}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{\chi^{i}}+\frac{\log(\chi)}{\beta_{0}}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{i}{\chi^{i}}<\infty.

Therefore, taking
j\to\infty
in (
6.55
), we get

\sup_{B_{r}}|\phi_{+}|\leq C\lVert\phi_{+}\rVert_{L^{\beta}(B_{R})}.

Hence, we have the local boundedness of
\phi_{+}
. Note that, if
\phi
is an eigenfunction, then so is
-\phi
. Additionally,
(-\phi)_{+}=\phi_{-}
. Applying the same method, we have local boundedness of
\phi_{-}
. Hence,
\phi
is locally bounded.

The continuity of solution comes from the Theorem
1.4
of
[
19
]
.
∎

Theorem 6.4

(Nodal Domains)
.

Let
2\leq p<N
and
\lambda>\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
be an eigenvalue of the mixed interpolated Hardy operator
\mathcal{T}
and
\phi_{\lambda}
be the associated eigenfunction. Then we have

\lambda>\max\{\lambda_{1}(\Omega^{+}),\lambda_{1}(\Omega^{-})\}

where
\Omega^{+}:=\{x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}:\phi_{\lambda}>0\}
and
\Omega^{-}:=\{x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}:\phi_{\lambda}<0\}

Proof.

As
\phi_{\lambda}
is continuous on
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
from Theorem
6.3
, we have
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
to be open in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
. But
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
is open in
\Omega
and
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)=\lambda_{1}(\Omega\setminus\{0\})
. This allows us to define the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(\Omega^{+})
and
\lambda_{1}(\Omega^{-})
. Since
\phi_{\lambda}
is an eigenfunction, it satisfies

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\nabla v+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(\phi_{\lambda}(x)-\phi_{\lambda}(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}-\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\phi_{\lambda}v}{|x|^{p\theta}}=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|\phi_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\phi_{\lambda}v

for all
v\in X(\Omega)
.

Taking
v=\phi_{\lambda}^{+}
and using (
2.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{+}}|\nabla\phi_{\lambda}^{+}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi_{\lambda}^{+}(x)-\phi_{\lambda}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}-\mu\int_{\Omega^{+}}\frac{|\phi_{\lambda}^{+}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}

\displaystyle<\int_{\Omega^{+}}|\nabla\phi_{\lambda}^{+}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(\phi_{\lambda}(x)-\phi_{\lambda}(y))(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}(x)-\phi_{\lambda}^{+}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}-\mu\int_{\Omega^{+}}\frac{|\phi_{\lambda}^{+}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}=\lambda\int_{\Omega^{+}}|\phi_{\lambda}^{+}|^{p}.

Next, by definition of
\lambda_{1}(\Omega^{+})

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega^{+})\int_{\Omega^{+}}|\nabla\phi_{\lambda}^{+}|^{p}\leq\int_{\Omega^{+}}|\nabla\phi_{\lambda}^{+}|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi_{\lambda}^{+}(x)-\phi_{\lambda}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}-\mu\int_{\Omega^{+}}\frac{|\phi_{\lambda}^{+}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}<\lambda\int_{\Omega^{+}}|\nabla\phi_{\lambda}^{+}|^{p},

Theorem 6.6

(Mixed Hong-Krahn-Szegö inequality)
.

Let
p\geq 2
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open bounded set, and
B
be any ball of volume
|\Omega|/2
. Then

\lambda_{2}(\Omega)>\lambda_{1}(B).

Additionally, if
\{s_{k}\}
and
\{t_{k}\}
are two sequences in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}|s_{k}-t_{k}|=\infty
and
\Omega_{k}=B_{r}(s_{k})\cup B_{r}(t_{k})

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{2}(\Omega_{k})=\lambda_{1}(B_{r})
.

Proof.

The proof follows similarly as the proof of Theorem
1.4
in
[
20
]
along with the genus characterization of
\lambda_{2}(\Omega)
in Theorem
6.5
.
The sketch of the proof goes like this:
We consider the second eigenfunction
\phi_{2}
and define the corresponding positive and negative regions:

\displaystyle\Omega^{+}=\{x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}:\phi_{2}(x)>0\},\quad\Omega^{-}=\{x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}:\phi_{2}(x)<0\}.

Using Theorem
6.4
, we know that

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\Omega)>\max\{\lambda_{1}(B_{r_{1}}),\lambda_{1}(B_{r_{2}})\},

where
B_{r_{1}}
and
B_{r_{2}}
are balls satisfying
|B_{r_{1}}|=|\Omega^{+}|
and
|B_{r_{2}}|=|\Omega^{-}|
.

Then one can show that
\max\{\lambda_{1}(B_{r_{1}}),\lambda_{1}(B_{r_{2}})\}
is minimized when
|B_{r_{1}}|=|B_{r_{2}}|=|\Omega|/2
.
To show that equality can be achieved asymptotically. We define a sequence of domains as:

\displaystyle\Omega_{k}:=B_{r}(s_{k})\cup B_{r}(t_{k}),

where the centres
s_{k}
and
t_{k}
are chosen such that
|s_{k}-t_{k}|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Taking the normalized eigenfunctions
\phi_{s_{k}}
and
\phi_{t_{k}}
on these balls and construct a sequence of functions
f_{k}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to S
given by

f_{k}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=|\eta_{1}|^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\eta_{1}\phi_{s_{k}}-|\eta_{2}|^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\eta_{2}\phi_{t_{k}}.

Then define
A_{k}=\text{Range}(f_{k})
. It implies that
A_{k}
is compact, symmetric, and of genus
\geq 2
. Using the variational characterization of
\lambda_{2}(\Omega)
in Theorem
4.1
, change of variable and the inequality
|a-b|^{p}\leq|a|^{p}+|b|^{p}+c_{p}(|a|^{2}+|b|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|ab|

with

and

, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\Omega_{k})\leq\max_{|\eta_{1}|^{2}+|\eta_{2}|^{2}=1}\mathcal{J}(f_{k}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}))

\displaystyle=\max_{|\xi_{1}|^{p}+|\xi_{2}|^{p}=1}\bigg\{\int_{\Omega_{k}}|\nabla(\xi_{1}\phi_{s_{k}}-\xi_{2}\phi_{t_{k}})|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\xi_{1}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)-\xi_{2}\Phi_{t_{k}}(x,y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-\mu\int_{\Omega_{k}}\frac{|\xi_{1}\phi_{s_{k}}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}~dx-\mu\int_{\Omega_{k}}\frac{|\xi_{2}\phi_{t_{k}}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}~dx\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{|\xi_{1}|^{p}+|\xi_{2}|^{p}=1}\bigg\{\int_{\Omega_{k}}|\nabla\xi_{1}\phi_{s_{k}}|^{p}~dx+\int_{\Omega_{k}}|\nabla\xi_{2}\phi_{t_{k}}|^{p}~dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\xi_{1}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}~dxdy

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\xi_{2}\Phi_{t_{k}}(x,y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}~dxdy-\mu\int_{\Omega_{k}}\frac{|\xi_{1}\phi_{s_{k}}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}~dx-\mu\int_{\Omega_{k}}\frac{|\xi_{2}\phi_{t_{k}}|^{p}}{|x|^{p\theta}}~dx

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{c_{p}(|\xi_{1}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)|^{2}+|\xi_{2}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)\Phi_{t_{k}}(x,y)|}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}(B_{r})+\max_{|\xi_{1}|^{p}+|\xi_{2}|^{p}=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{c_{p}(|\xi_{1}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)|^{2}+|\xi_{2}\Phi_{t_{k}}(x,y)|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)\Phi_{t_{k}}(x,y)|}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}.

Since
\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)\Phi_{t_{k}}(x,y)=-\phi_{s_{k}}(x)\phi_{t_{k}}(y)-\phi_{s_{k}}(y)\phi_{t_{k}}(x)
is nonzero only when
(x,y)\in B_{r}(s_{k})\times B_{r}(t_{k})\cup B_{r}(t_{k})\times B_{r}(s_{k})
. And
|x-y|\geq|s_{k}-t_{k}|-2r
for all
(x,y)\in B_{r}(s_{k})\times B_{r}(t_{k})\cup B_{r}(t_{k})\times B_{r}(s_{k})
. Hence for sufficiently large
k
such that
|s_{k}-t_{k}|-2r>0
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\Omega_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}(B_{r})+\frac{2c_{p}}{(|s_{k}-t_{k}|-2r)^{N+ps}}

\displaystyle\times\max_{|\xi_{1}|^{p}+|\xi_{2}|^{p}=1}\int_{B_{r}(s_{k})}\int_{B_{r}(t_{k})}(|\xi_{1}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)|^{2}+|\xi_{2}\Phi_{t_{k}}(x,y)|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)\Phi_{t_{k}}(x,y)|.

Since

\displaystyle 2c_{p}\max_{|\xi_{1}|^{p}+|\xi_{2}|^{p}=1}\int_{B_{r}(s_{k})}\int_{B_{r}(t_{k})}(|\xi_{1}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)|^{2}+|\xi_{2}\Phi_{t_{k}}(x,y)|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\Phi_{s_{k}}(x,y)\Phi_{t_{k}}(x,y)|

is finite and
\frac{1}{(|s_{k}-t_{k}|-2r)^{N+ps}}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Thus
\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{2}(\Omega_{k})\leq\lambda_{1}(B_{r})
.

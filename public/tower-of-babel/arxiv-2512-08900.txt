Deterministic randomness extraction for semi-device-independent quantum random number generation
Pablo Tikas Pueyo, Tomás Fernández Martos, Gabriel Senno

Abstract
It is a well-known fact in classical information theory that no deterministic procedure can extract close-to-ideal randomness from an arbitrary entropy source. On the other hand, if additional knowledge about the source is available—e.g., that it is a sequence of independent Bernoulli trials—then deterministic extractors do exist. For quantum entropy sources, where in addition to classical random variables we consider quantum side information, the use of extra knowledge about their structure was pioneered in a recent publication [C. Foreman and L. Masanes, Quantum 9, 1654 (2025)]. In that work, the authors provide deterministic extractors for device-independent randomness generation with memoryless devices achieving a sufficiently high CHSH score. In this work, we extend their construction to the prepare-and-measure scenario. Specifically, we prove that the considered functions are also extractors for memoryless devices in a semi-device-independent setting under an overlap assumption on the prepared quantum states. We then simulate the resulting randomness generation protocol on a novel and experimentally relevant family of behaviors, observing positive key rates already for 7 × 10 3 7\times 10^{3} rounds.



I
Introduction

Randomness has become an indispensable asset in the digital era, underpinning a wide range of applications—from computer simulations and forecasting to artificial intelligence and, most critically, cryptography
[
9
]
. Recent technological advances have enabled the emergence of commercially available quantum random number generators (QRNGs), exploiting the inherent unpredictability of quantum mechanics
[
10
]
.

The general structure of a QRNG consists of two main components: an
entropy source
and a
randomness extractor
. The entropy source is in charge of producing
raw
bit strings with some amount of private randomness, usually quantified via the (smooth) conditional min-entropy
[
11
]
(see also
[
5
]
). The randomness extractor then transforms the raw bit string into another, usually shorter, bit string that is
\epsilon
-close (in trace distance) to being perfectly random, i.e., uniformly random and independent of a potential eavesdropper’s side information.

It is straightforward to see that no deterministic procedure can extract randomness from the class of all entropy sources with a given min-entropy lower bound. In a celebrated result, Santha and Vazirani showed that this impossibility continues to hold even for a restricted, yet natural, class of sources with arbitrary high min-entropy
[
18
]
. Faced with this limitation, two main approaches were developed in the literature. In
seeded extraction
, a short string of perfectly random bits is used as a catalyst to extract randomness from weak sources. In
multi-source extraction
, additional weak sources are combined to extract randomness under the assumption that each contains sufficient conditional entropy, and some form of independence between them and the side-information also holds (see
[
1
]
and, more recently,
[
17
]
).

Deterministic extractors, nevertheless, do exist for particular classes of entropy sources. For example, biased yet independent coins can be XORed to produce a close to uniform bit (see, e.g.,
[
21
]
). Naturally, this fact led to the question of whether one could construct deterministic extractors tailored for particular families of quantum entropy sources. In a remarkable result
[
7
]
, the first quantum random number generation protocol with deterministic extraction was constructed. Focusing on a device-independent (DI) setting,
the authors prove that the violation of a Bell inequality can replace a min-entropy lower bound as the extractor’s promise.

In this work, we show that the techniques developed in
[
7
]
for the DI setting can be adapted to a semi-DI one. In particular, we work in the prepare-and-measure framework based on an overlap assumption on the prepared states, developed in
[
3
]
. Our main result shows that the role played by a Bell functional in the DI construction can be played by a solution to the dual of the guessing probability in this framework. Then, the techniques from
[
7
]
lead to a spot-checking randomness generation protocol whose performance we simulate with a quantum entropy source based on an experimentally relevant family of behaviors
[
14
]
.
In the finite-size regime, we observe that positive key rates can be achieved for as low as
7\times 10^{3}
rounds. Whereas in the asymptotic limit of infinitely many rounds, we show that even if the fraction of estimating rounds is vanishing, successful extraction can be achieved from arbitrarily close to deterministic behaviors.

The paper is structured as follows. In Sec.
II
, we describe the prepare-and-measure semi-DI framework we work with. In Sec.
III
, we state our main theorem: a family of operator inequalities resulting from dual solutions to the guessing probability. In Sec.
IV
, we use this result to prove that the functions considered in
[
7
]
are also extractors in our setting. In Sec.
V
, we provide a randomness generation protocol, based on the one from
[
7
]
, whose expected rates under honest but noisy implementations we simulate in Sec.
VI
. Finally, we conclude with some remarks in Sec.
VII
.

Operator inequalities from dual solutions to the guessing probability

The seedless extraction protocol from
[

Let us consider a single use of Alice’s entropy source

subject to

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad M_{a|\lambda}\succeq 0,\penalty\ \sum_{a}M_{a|\lambda}=\mathbbm{1},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\sum_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\bra{\psi^x}M_{a|\lambda}\ket{\psi^x}=p(a|x),

where, without loss of generality,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(H_{\lambda})^{\dagger}=H_{\lambda},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ket{\psi^0}\bra{\psi^0}\delta_{a,\lambda}-\sum_{x=0}^{1}\ket{\psi^x}\bra{\psi^x}\nu_{a,x}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+H_{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}\Tr[H_{\lambda}\Big]\mathbbm{1}\preceq 0,

III

IV
Single- and multi-bit deterministic extractors

In a QRNG, the randomness extractor component is in charge of transforming the raw random bits produced by some intrinsically random quantum measurement (the quantum entropy source) into another, usually shorter, string satisfying the universally composable notion of security given by the trace distance
[
8

15
]

deterministic randomness extractors

Definition 1
.

We say that a function

(m,\epsilon)
-deterministic randomness extractor
for a cq-state

\rho_{\mathbf{K}E}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n_{r}}:g(\mathbf{a})=\mathbf{k}}\outerproduct{\mathbf{k}}{\mathbf{k}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{E}^{\mathbf{a}},

(7)

is such that

\frac{1}{2}\parallel\rho_{\mathbf{K}E}-\rho_{\mathbf{K}E}^{\text{ideal}}\parallel_{1}\leq\epsilon,

(8)

with
\rho_{\mathbf{K}E}^{\text{ideal}}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{2^{m}}\otimes\rho_{E}
.

In what follows, we show that the functions shown to be deterministic extractors for device-independent quantum entropy sources in

&\qquad-2\log(1/\epsilon)-4\log(n_r),\end{split}

with
\langle\{\nu_{z}\},\ \{H_{\lambda}\}\rangle
subject to the constraints in Eq. (

\forall z\in\{0,1\}^{2},\quad 4p_{r}\sqrt{2}^{\beta-1}\nu_{z}+p_{e}\sqrt{2}^{\alpha_{z}}=1.

(15)

Notice that Protocol

do not appear explicitly in the objective functions

and

since they are only used to check for feasibility.

The security of the protocol is stated in the following theorem, the proof of which is provided in Appendix

Theorem 4
.

For any choice of

p_{e}
and

where

for given

and
\mathbf{z}
is defined in Eq. (

Numerical simulations

Randomness generation protocols in the overlap semi-DI framework have been studied based on the statistics of: unambiguous state discrimination, minimum error state discrimination and maximum confidence discrimination experiments

As we show in a companion publication

VII
Conclusions

We have shown that the deterministic extractors studied in
[
7
]
for the DI setting can be applied in the semi-DI prepare-and-measure framework with bounded state overlap developed in
[
3
]
. Our main technical result connects the universally composable notion of security based on the trace distance to the expectation value of an operator constructed from dual solutions to the SDP for Eve’s guessing probability. We expect that, in future works, this construction is extended to other semi-DI frameworks and possibly to the device-dependent setting. As for the resulting randomness generation protocol, the small number of rounds required for a positive key rate and the noise tolerance observed are promising. On the other hand, the rates achieved are significantly below what seeded extractors can provide for the same setting. Therefore, a significant improvement in the protocol’s efficiency is needed before it can become practically relevant for an industrial application.

Appendix A
Dual of the SDP for Eve’s guessing probability in Eq.
III

Let us restate the primal from Eq. (
III
) in the following equivalent form

P_{\mathrm{guess}}(A|E,X=0,\mathbf{p},\delta)=\max_{\{N_{a|\lambda}\}_{a,\lambda}}\sum_{a}^{1}\bra{\psi^0}N_{a|a}\ket{\psi^0},

(21)

subject to

\displaystyle\forall x,a,\quad\sum_{\lambda=0}^{1}\bra{\psi^x}N_{a|\lambda}\ket{\psi^x}=p(a|x),

(22)

\displaystyle N_{a|\lambda}\succeq 0,

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{a}N_{a|\lambda}=p(\lambda)\mathbbm{1}=\frac{1}{2}\Tr[\sum_{a}N_{a|\lambda}\Big]\mathbbm{1}.

(24)

with
\ket{\psi^0}=\ket{0}
and
\ket{\psi^1}=\sqrt{\delta}\ket{0}+\sqrt{1-\delta}\ket{1}
, and where we have absorbed the probabilities
p(\lambda)
into the now unnormalized POVMs
\{N_{a|\lambda}\}
. To obtain the dual SDP, a Lagrangian is defined, which is a function of the primal SDP’s variables
N_{a|\lambda}
as well as Lagrange multipliers variables for each of the constraints in the primal SDP; namely, scalars
\nu_{a,x}
, and Hermitian
2\times 2
matrices
H_{\lambda}
and
G_{a|\lambda}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda=0}^{1}\delta_{a,\lambda}\bra{\psi^0}N_{a|\lambda}\ket{\psi^0}+\sum_{a,x=0}^{1}\nu_{a,x}\left(p(a|x)-\sum_{\lambda=0}^{1}\bra{\psi^x}N_{a|\lambda}\ket{\psi^x}\right)

(25)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\lambda=0}^{1}\Tr[H_{\lambda}\left(\sum_{a=0}^{1}N_{a|\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}\Tr[\sum_{a=0}^{1}N_{a|\lambda}\Big]\mathbbm{1}\right)\Bigg]+\sum_{a,\lambda=0}^{1}\Tr[G_{a|\lambda}N_{a|\lambda}\Big].

(26)

The supremum
S
of the Lagrangian over the primal SDP variables is
S\coloneqq\text{sup}_{\{N_{a|\lambda}\}_{a,\lambda}}\mathcal{L}
. Note that, for any particular solution
\{N_{a|\lambda}\}_{a,\lambda}
of the primal SDP, the two middle terms of the Lagrangian vanish due to the contraints of the primal. On the other hand, the first term of the Lagrangian is the objective of the primal SDP, and the last term is non-negative as long as
G_{a|\lambda}\succeq 0
, due to the second constraint of the primal and the fact that the trace of the product of two positive semidefinite operators is non-negative. Therefore, enforcing
G_{a|\lambda}\succeq 0
guarantees that
P_{\mathrm{guess}}\leq S
. To get useful bounds,
S
must be minimized over the dual SDP variables. However,
S
can be first rewritten in a more convenient form as

S=\text{sup}_{\{N_{a|\lambda}\}_{a,\lambda}}\sum_{a,\lambda=0}^{1}\Tr[N_{a|\lambda}K_{a|\lambda}\Big]+\sum_{a,x=0}^{1}\nu_{a,x}p(a|x),

(27)

where

K_{a|\lambda}\coloneqq\ket{\psi^0}\bra{\psi^0}\delta_{a,\lambda}-\sum_{x=0}^{1}\ket{\psi^x}\bra{\psi^x}\nu_{a,x}+G_{a|\lambda}+H_{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}\Tr[H_{\lambda}\Big]\mathbbm{1}.

(28)

Any values for the dual SDP variables can be chosen; in particular,
K_{a|\lambda}=0
greatly simplifies the problem. This still results in a suitable upper bound, albeit potentially suboptimal. Given that
G_{a|\lambda}\succeq 0
, setting
K_{a|\lambda}=0
implies that the remaining terms in the definition of
K_{a|\lambda}
must be negative semidefinite. With this, the final form of the dual SDP reads

P_{\mathrm{guess}}^{\ast}(A|E,X=0,\mathbf{p},\delta)\coloneqq\min_{\{H_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda},\{\nu_{a,x}\}_{a,\lambda}}\sum_{a,x=0}^{1}\nu_{a,x}p(a|x),

(29)

subject to

|\psi^{0}\rangle\langle\psi^{0}|\delta_{a,\lambda}-\sum_{x=0}^{1}\ket{\psi^x}\bra{\psi^x}\nu_{a,x}+H_{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}\Tr[H_{\lambda}\Big]\mathbbm{1}\preceq 0,

(30)

which we rewrite in an equivalent form in Eq. (
III
). The optimal value of this dual SDP yields an upper bound on Eve’s optimal guessing probability, as
P_{\mathrm{guess}}\leq P_{\mathrm{guess}}^{\ast}
.

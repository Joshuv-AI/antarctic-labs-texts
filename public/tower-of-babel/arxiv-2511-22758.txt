Minimax Optimal Adaptive Control for Systems on Cones
Anders Rantzer

Abstract
The theory of optimal control on positive cones has recently identified several new problem classes where the Bellman equation can be solved explicitly, in analogy with classical linear quadratic control. In this paper, the idea is extended to minimax adaptive control, yielding exact solutions to instances of the Bellman equation for dual control. In particular, this allows for optimization of the fundamental tradeoff between exploration and exploitation.



I
Introduction

This paper is about adaptive control with worst-case models for disturbances and uncertain parameters. More precisely, the following problem is addressed:

General Problem:

Consider proper cones
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N_{y}+N_{v}}
,
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N_{y}}
,
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2N_{y}+N_{v}}
. Suppose that for every

there exists
v
with
(y,v)\in\mathcal{U}
. Find, if possible, a control law

v_{t}:=\mu_{t}(y_{0},\ldots,y_{t},v_{0},\ldots,v_{t-1})
such that

\displaystyle\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}s^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}y_{t}\\
v_{t}\\
y_{t+1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\leq 0

(1)

for all
y_{0},y_{1},\ldots\in\mathcal{X}
with
(y_{t},u_{t},y_{t+1})\in\mathcal{V}
for all
t
and

.

The problem can be addressed as a dynamic game problem, with a minimizing player selecting

and a maximizing player selecting

and

. It will be solved by dynamic programming while exploiting the special structure. The vector

encapsulates both system dynamics and cost function, so the set

represents a priori information about these. Moreover, it will be verified that a solution, if it exists, can always be written on the form

\displaystyle v_{t}

\displaystyle=\eta\left(y_{t},\sum_{t=0}^{t-1}(y_{\tau},v_{\tau},y_{\tau+1})\right),

where the sum is a compression of all data available to the controller at time
t
. Whithout any data, all
s\in\mathcal{S}
carry equal weight, but the presence of past data
\sum_{t=0}^{t-1}(y_{\tau},v_{\tau},y_{\tau+1})
makes it possible to focus attention on a smaller set of
s
-vectors in reducing the cost. Hence the optimal controller will make a tradeoff between ”exploration and exploitation”.

To clarify the connection to adaptive control, it is instructive to consider linear systems

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=Ax_{t}+Bu_{t}+w_{t}

II
Notation

The set of
n\times m

. The transpose of a matrix

. For a symmetric matrix

A\succeq 0
means positive semi-definite.
For
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, the expression
\langle A,B\rangle
denotes the trace of
A^{\top}B
.
Given

and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, the notation
|x|^{2}_{A}
means
x^{\top}Ax
. Similarly, given
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, the trace of
B^{\top}AB
is denoted
\|B\|^{2}_{A}
. For two vectors
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

, the concatenation
\begin{bmatrix}x\\
u\end{bmatrix}
will generally be denoted
(x,u)
. In particular,
|(x,u)|^{2}=|x|^{2}+|u|^{2}
.

With this notation, the special problem can be turned into a special case of the General Problem by a change of variables:

\displaystyle y_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\vect\left((x_{t},x_{t-1},u_{t-1})x_{t}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle v_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\vect\left((x_{t},u_{t})u_{t}^{\top}\right)

Solution to the General Problem.

Theorem 1

Consider proper cones
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N_{y}}
,
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N_{y}+N_{v}}
and
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2N_{y}+N_{v}}
. Suppose that for every
y\in\mathcal{X}
there exists
v
with
(y,v)\in\mathcal{U}
. Assume also that the cones
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
have the property that

\displaystyle\maxxi_{(y,v,\xi)\in\mathcal{V}}\minnu_{(\xi,\nu)\in\mathcal{U}}q^{\top}\big(z+(y,v,\xi),\xi,\nu\big)

(4)

is a linear function of
(z,y,v)
whenever finite. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(
i
)

There exists a control law

v_{t}=\mu_{t}(y_{0},\ldots,y_{t})
such that
(y_{t},v_{t})\in\mathcal{U}
and (
1
) holds
for all sequences
y_{0},y_{1},\ldots
with
y_{0}=0
and
(y_{t},v_{t},y_{t+1})\in\mathcal{V}
for all
t
.

(
ii
)

There exists a proper cone
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3N_{y}+2N_{v}}
that contains
\mathcal{S}\times\{0,0\}
and for all
y,v,z
satisfies

\displaystyle\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}q^{\top}(z,y,v)

\displaystyle=\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\maxxi_{(y,v,\xi)\in\mathcal{V}}\minnu_{(\xi,\nu)\in\mathcal{U}}q^{\top}\big(z+(y,v,\xi),\xi,\nu\big).

(5)

Moreover, if (
ii
) holds, then (
i
) holds with the feedback law

\displaystyle v_{t}

\displaystyle:=\arg\minv_{(y_{t},v)\in\mathcal{U}}\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}q^{\top}\left(\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}(y_{\tau},v_{\tau},y_{\tau+1}),y_{t},v\right).

(6)

Proof.

To get that (
ii
)
\Rightarrow
(
i
), put

\displaystyle z_{t}=\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}(y_{\tau},v_{\tau},y_{\tau+1})

(7)

and apply the controller (
6
) to get

\displaystyle\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}q^{\top}\left(\sum_{\tau=0}^{t-1}(y_{\tau},v_{\tau},y_{\tau+1}),y_{t},v_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=^{\eqref{eqn:z}}\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}q^{\top}\left(z_{t},y_{t},v_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=^{\eqref{eqn:Bellman}}\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\maxxi_{(y_{t},v_{t},\xi)\in\mathcal{V}}\minnu_{(\xi,\nu)\in\mathcal{U}}q^{\top}\big(z_{t}+(y_{t},v_{t},\xi),\xi,\nu\big)

\displaystyle=\maxxi_{(y_{t},v_{t},\xi)\in\mathcal{V}}\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\minnu_{(\xi,\nu)\in\mathcal{U}}q^{\top}\big(z_{t}+(y_{t},v_{t},\xi),\xi,\nu\big)

\displaystyle=\maxxi_{(y_{t},v_{t},\xi)\in\mathcal{V}}\minnu_{(\xi,\nu)\in\mathcal{U}}\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}q^{\top}\big(z_{t}+(y_{t},v_{t},\xi),\xi,\nu\big)

\displaystyle\geq\minnu_{(y_{t+1},\nu)\in\mathcal{U}}\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}q^{\top}\big(z_{t}+(y_{t},v_{t},y_{t+1}),y_{t+1},\nu\big)

\displaystyle=^{\eqref{eqn:controller}}\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}q^{\top}\big(z_{t}+(y_{t},v_{t},y_{t+1}),y_{t+1},v_{t+1}\big)

\displaystyle=^{\eqref{eqn:z}}\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}q^{\top}\left(\sum_{\tau=0}^{t}(y_{\tau},v_{\tau},y_{\tau+1}),y_{t+1},v_{t+1}\right),

where the fourth equality follows from von Neumann’s minimax theorem.
Recursive application of the resulting inequality starting from
y_{0}=0
gives (
i
).

For the opposite implication, define
\mathcal{Q}_{0}:=\mathcal{S}\times\{0,0\}
and generate an expanding sequence

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{2}\subseteq\ldots

recursively as follows. Assume that (
5
) fails for
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{k}
. This means that the value of (
4
) is a linear function
q_{k}^{\top}(z,y,v)
with larger value than
\max_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}q^{\top}(z,y,v)
at some point. Then define

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}

\displaystyle:=\conv(\mathcal{Q}_{k}\cup\{q_{k}\}).

Assuming that (
i
) holds, the recursion must converge to a limit set that satifies (
ii
).

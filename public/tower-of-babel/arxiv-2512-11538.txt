Virtual invariants from the non-associative Hilbert scheme
Gergely Bérczi and Felix Minddal

Abstract
. We introduce a non-associative model for the Hilbert scheme of points in arbitrary dimension. We define a smooth ambient space, which we call the non-associative Hilbert scheme, containing the classical nested Hilbert scheme NHilb d ¯ ⁡ ( 𝔸 n ) \operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}) as the associativity, cut out by an explicit section of an associativity bundle. This construction yields canonical perfect obstruction theories and virtual fundamental classes on NHilb d ¯ ⁡ ( 𝔸 n ) \operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}) for all ( n , d ¯ ) (n,\underline{d}) . Using virtual localization, we obtain closed formulas for these virtual classes as sums over admissible nested partitions. Over the punctual locus, we rewrite these as a single multivariable iterated residue formula governing all virtual integrals. Our construction works for all n n , produces positive-dimensional virtual classes when n n is large compared to the number of points, and we expect that they extend the non-commutative matrix model and virtual class construction on Calabi-Yau threefolds.



Introduction

Hilbert schemes of points are fundamental objects in algebraic and enumerative geometry. For a smooth surface
S
, the Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(S)
of length-
d
zero-dimensional subschemes is a smooth, irreducible variety of dimension
2d

[
20
]
. The topology and intersection theory of
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(S)
are very well understood, and they carry rich structures such as Nakajima’s Heisenberg algebra action
[
32
]
, quantum cohomology structures in the work of Okounkov-Pandharipande
[
36
]
, and relations to curve counting and modular forms via K3 surfaces.

In higher dimension, the geometry quickly becomes more subtle. For a threefold
X
, the Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(X)
is typically singular for
d>1
but admits a symmetric perfect obstruction theory in the Calabi-Yau case. Behrend-Fantechi’s theory of intrinsic normal cones and perfect obstruction theories produces a virtual fundamental class
[\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(X)]^{\mathrm{vir}}

[
3
]
, and Behrend’s constructible function describes Donaldson-Thomas invariants as weighted Euler characteristics
[
1
]
. Applied to Calabi-Yau threefolds, this yields degree-zero Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants as weighted Euler characterisitics on
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(X)
.

In the special important case when
X=\mathbb{A}^{3}
,
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(\mathbb{A}^{3})
parametrizes ideals of colength
d
in
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
and is highly singular. Nevertheless, it can be modeled as a critical locus of a function on a smooth space of non-commuting matrices: one considers triples of matrices
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})\in\mathrm{End}(V)^{3}
together with a vector
v\in V
generating
V
under the
A_{i}
, and imposes the commuting relations
[A_{i},A_{j}]=0
via a potential
[
4
,
2
]
. This non-commuting matrix model leads to explicit formulae for DT invariants of
\mathbb{A}^{3}
, recovering the MacMahon product formula for the generating series of zero-dimensional DT invariants
[
29
,
30
]
. From our point of view, this is the first instance of a general philosophy: one embeds a singular Hilbert scheme into a smoother, more flexible moduli space (here of unconstrained or non‑commuting matrices) and then imposes the constraints (here of commutativity) as equations cutting out the original moduli space.

Moduli of sheaves.

The special role of threefolds and fourfolds in enumerative geometry is by now well established. On a Calabi-Yau threefold
X
, moduli spaces of stable sheaves or stable pairs carry symmetric perfect obstruction theories and hence zero-dimensional virtual classes. Integrals over these classes define Donaldson-Thomas invariants, which may be viewed as algebraic counterparts of BPS state counts in string theory and are related to Gromov-Witten invariants via the DT/PT correspondence
[
38
,
37
]
.

For Calabi-Yau fourfolds, the picture is more delicate: the derived moduli stack of (semi)stable sheaves is expected to be
(-2)
-shifted symplectic in the sense of Pantev-Toën-Vaquié-Vezzosi. Using this structure, Borisov-Joyce showed that such a derived moduli space carries a real virtual fundamental class of half the expected complex dimension, constructed via derived differential geometry and Kuranishi atlases
[
13
]
. Their theory applies in particular to moduli spaces of stable sheaves on Calabi-Yau fourfolds (including Hilbert schemes of points), but the resulting cycle is a priori only defined in real homology and depends on subtle orientation data.

Building on this, Oh-Thomas constructed an algebraic virtual cycle for moduli spaces of sheaves on Calabi-Yau fourfolds, using a localized square‑root Euler class for orthogonal bundles and a theory of complex Kuranishi structures. They proved a localization formula and showed that their algebraic cycle agrees with the Borisov-Joyce cycle
[
34
,
35
]
. This provides a robust framework for defining DT‑type invariants on Calabi-Yau fourfolds and for comparing different constructions.

In parallel, Feyzbakhsh-Thomas have developed a powerful wall-crossing and stability-conditions approach to Donaldson-Thomas invariants on Calabi-Yau threefolds. They express general rank-
r
DT invariants in terms of simpler rank 0 or rank 1 theories via Bridgeland stability and wall crossing
[
18
,
19
]
.

Within this sheaf-theoretic framework, Hilbert schemes of points on Calabi-Yau fourfolds play the role of moduli of zero-dimensional sheaves. Let
X
be a smooth projective Calabi-Yau fourfold and
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(X)
its Hilbert scheme of length-
d
subschemes. Using the Borisov-Joyce theory together with orientability results of Cao-Gross-Joyce for moduli of sheaves on Calabi-Yau fourfolds, one obtains a virtual class on
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(X)
. Cao-Kool then defined zero-dimensional
DT_{4}
invariants by integrating the Euler classes of tautological bundles against this virtual class, and proved deformation invariance and a number of structural properties
[
15
]
. These invariants can be viewed as fourfold analogues of degree-zero DT invariants on Calabi-Yau threefolds.

More recently, Bojko has used the Gross-Joyce-Tanaka wall-crossing formalism to study topological information encoded by the virtual fundamental classes of
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(X)
as
d
varies. Assuming the conjectural wall-crossing framework, Bojko proves the Cao-Kool conjectures for zero-dimensional sheaf counting and shows that the collection of virtual classes on
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(X)
is equivalent to the data of all descendent integrals. As a consequence, many generating series of invariants are expressed in terms of explicit universal power series and are related to Nekrasov’s genus for
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{4})
and to Segre-Verlinde correspondences involving Quot schemes on elliptic surfaces
[
12
]
.

On the local and toric side, Nekrasov
[
33
]
introduced an equivariant K-theoretic ”Magnificent Four” partition function, which can be interpreted as a new genus of Hilbert schemes of points on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
. Cao-Kool-Monavari
[
16
]
extended this to curves and stable pairs, formulating a K‑theoretic DT/PT correspondence for toric Calabi-Yau fourfolds and verifying it via a vertex formalism.

In a different but related direction, Oberdieck et al
[
17
]
have developed curve-counting theories on holomorphic symplectic fourfolds, including holomorphic symplectic analogues of Gopakumar-Vafa invariants . These are often expressed in terms of invariants of Hilbert schemes of points on K3 surfaces and their higher-dimensional analogues and connect the 4‑dimensional Hilbert scheme geometry to modularity and Jacobi forms.

The non‑associative nested Hilbert scheme.

For a smooth variety
X
of dimension
n
and a dimension vector
\underline{d}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{r})
, the nested Hilbert scheme

\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(X)=\{\xi_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\xi_{r+1}\subset X\Big|\dim\xi_{i}=0,\operatorname{length}(\xi_{i})=d_{0}+\cdots+d_{i-1}\}

parametrizes chains of zero-dimensional subschemes of prescribed lengths. Even on surfaces, these nested schemes are typically singular with poorly understood component structure. Nonetheless, they have proven extremely useful in sheaf counting theories. Gholampour-Sheshmani-Yau
[
22
]
constructed virtual classes on nested Hilbert schemes of points and curves on surfaces and showed that they recover reduced stable pair and Seiberg-Witten theory. Gholampour-Thomas
[
23
,
24
]
then expressed nested Hilbert schemes as virtual resolutions of degeneracy loci of maps of bundles on smooth ambient varieties, and used Thom-Porteous‑type formulas to compute the resulting virtual cycles and their integrals. This provides a powerful technique for expressing Vafa-Witten and local DT/PT invariants in terms of tautological classes on nested Hilbert schemes of surfaces.

In this work we use a different smooth ambient space for Hilbert scheme of points. We will reinterpret the work of Bérczi-Szenes
[
11
,
8
]
and Kazarian
[
27
]
where they showed that a certain filtered part of the punctual Hilbert scheme embeds into a smooth non‑associative Hilbert scheme and used this to compute Thom polynomials of Morin singularity classes.

In this paper, we extend the non-associative Hilbert scheme model to the whole nested Hilbert schemes. Fix a dimension vector
\underline{d}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r+1}
and
n\geq 0
. The nested Hilbert scheme of
d=d_{0}+\cdots+d_{r}
points in
\mathbb{A}^{n}
given as the moduli space

\displaystyle\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})

\displaystyle=\left\{\xi_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\xi_{r+1}\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}\>|\>\operatorname{dim}\xi_{i}=0,\>\operatorname{Length}\xi_{i}=d_{0}+\cdots+d_{i-1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{I_{r+1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{1}\subset I_{0}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\>|\>\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}I_{i}/I_{i+1}=d_{i}\right\}.

We define the non-associative nested Hilbert scheme of
d=d_{0}+\cdots+d_{r}
points on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
as the moduli space

\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})=\left\{I_{r+1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{1}\subset I_{0}=\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}_{c}\>|\>\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}I_{i}/I_{i+1}=d_{i}\right\}

where
\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}_{c}
is the free unital, commutative, non-associative algebra on
n
-generators and the
I_{i}
’s are ideals. We prove that this is a smooth variety and that
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
embeds in
\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
as the locus where
I_{1}
is contained in the associativity ideal or equivalently where the quotient algebras are associative.

The key point is that associativity can be imposed by a section of a vector bundle over the smooth ambient space
\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. That is, the associator conditions are encoded in a vector bundle
E_{\operatorname{ass}}
on
\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
and a global section

s_{\operatorname{ass}}\in\Gamma(\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),E_{\operatorname{ass}})

whose zero locus is precisely
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. Thus
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
has a canonical perfect obstruction theory as the zero locus of a section on a smooth variety, and hence a virtual fundamental class

[\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in A_{*}(\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})).

This provides an elementary and concrete construction of a virtual class for nested Hilbert schemes of points in all dimensions.

Our approach should be contrasted with the non-commuting matrix models used in threefold DT theory. There, one replaces the commutative coordinate ring by a free associative (non-commutative) algebra of matrices, and the Hilbert scheme is realized as a critical locus of a function cutting out the commuting relations. In our non-associative model, we instead keep commutativity but drop associativity, realizing the Hilbert scheme as the zero locus of associativity equations inside a smooth commutative but non-associative ambient space. This shift turns out to be well adapted to the combinatorics of monomial ideals and yields ambient varieties that are themselves built as towers of Grassmannian fibrations, in the spirit of Kazarian’s work. Crucially, our construction produces virtual classes of positive dimension whenever the ambient dimension
n
is sufficiently large compared to the number of points (or total length)
d=d_{0}+\cdots d_{r}
, and our iterated residue formula for virtual integration then provides a uniform way to define and compute these virtual classes for all pairs
(n,\underline{d})
.

Virtual localization and iterated residue formulas.

The affine torus
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
acts on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
by scaling each coordinate, and this action lifts to both
\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
and
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. Our construction of the obstruction theory is
T
‑equivariant, so we obtain an equivariant virtual class

\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in A_{*}^{T}(\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})).

Then we apply the Graber-Pandharipande virtual localization formula to compute the restriction of this class in the localized equivariant Chow group
A_{*}^{T}(\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}))_{\operatorname{loc}}
as a sum over
T
‑fixed points. These fixed points correspond to chains of monomial ideals, i.e., to nested partitions. We obtain an explicit iterated residue formula expressing
[\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\mathrm{vir}}
as a weighted sum over admissible
(n-1)
‑dimensional nested partitions of length
\underline{d}
, with weights given by rational functions in the torus weights (Theorem
1.1
).

We also single out the nilpotently filtered locus
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
, consisting of nested ideals

I_{r+1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{1}\subset I_{0}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

such that
I_{1}
is the maximal ideal at the origin and
I_{1}\cdot I_{i}\subset I_{i+1}
for all
i
. This locus lies inside the punctual nested Hilbert scheme and has a natural virtual class induced by our embedding into the non‑associative model. When
\underline{d}=(1,\dots,1)
is a full flag, we show that
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
coincides with the usual punctual nested Hilbert scheme, and more generally we study its geometry for arbitrary
\underline{d}
. When
r=1
,
d_{1}=d
, we identify

\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})=\{I\subset\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\Big|\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[x]/I=d+1,\mathfrak{m}^{2}\subset I\subset\mathfrak{m}\},

which is empty whenever
d>n
; this illustrates that the nilpotently filtered locus is much smaller than the full punctual Hilbert scheme.

For virtual integrals supported on the nilpotently filtered locus
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
, we refine the localization calculation to an iterated residue formula. When
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{r}\leq n
we construct a partial resolution of
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
fibering over a flag variety
\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
(where
\hat{d}=(d_{1},\cdots,d_{r})
and reduce integrals to a fiber over a reference flag. Following
[
11
]
, Atiyah-Bott localization on this resolved space can be transformed into a multi-variable rational form whose virtual integral is given by an iterated residue at infinity. A key feature of our analysis is that all but one of the candidate residues vanish; the sole contribution comes from the Porteous nested partition consisting only of unit vectors in the natural order. The resulting formula expresses virtual integrals on
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
as a single explicit iterated residue (Theorem
1.2
). This generalizes earlier iterated residue techniques of
[
11
]
in singularity theory and Thom polynomial computations to the context of nested Hilbert schemes of points and virtual integration.

Finally, using an embedding

\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{N})

for
N\gg n
and comparing the corresponding virtual structures, we show that the same residue formula continues to hold even when
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{r}>n
.

Tautological versus virtual intersection theory.

The curvilinear Hilbert scheme, denoted by
\mathrm{CHilb}^{d}_{0}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
, parametrizes infinitesimally collinear subschemes of a smooth variety and forms a small, distinguished component of the punctual Hilbert scheme. In
[
8
]
it is shown that this curvilinear component forms a natural projective compactification of the jet moduli space
J_{k}(1,n)/\mathrm{Diff}_{k}
. This perspective led to a systematic theory of tautological intersection numbers on curvilinear and full Hilbert schemes of points. In a series of papers
[
8
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
, tautological integrals were expressed in terms of explicit iterated residue formulas for all tautological integral on the main component of the Hilbert schemes of points in arbitrary dimension. These formulas recover and generalise classical surface results of Göttsche and Nakajima, and they apply to a wide range of enumerative problems, including counting curves and hypersurfaces with prescribed singularities and higher-dimensional analogues of Lehn’s conjecture and Segre-Verlinde type formulas. A striking structural property of Hilbert scheme of points proved in
[
10
]
is that all monomial ideals (i.e.torus fixed points) lie on the curvilinear component, strongly suggesting that the curvilinear locus controls the topology of the entire Hilbert scheme.

The present work develops a parallel virtual intersection theory on the full Hilbert schemes of points, not just on thew main (smoothable) component. Crucially, our construction yields virtual classes of positive dimension whenever the ambient dimension
n
is sufficiently large compared to the total length of the subscheme, and the virtual localization formulas can again be written as iterated residues, now incorporating the contributions of the obstruction theory. In this sense, the virtual intersection theory developed here is formally very close to the tautological intersection theory of
[
8
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
: in both settings, universal intersection numbers on Hilbert schemes of points are governed by explicit multi-variable residue formulas over the same combinatorial data of monomial ideals and partitions. In forthcoming work
[
9
]
, this framework is used to reconstruct Donaldson-Thomas invariants of Calabi-Yau threefolds from the non-associative Hilbert scheme model, revealing a direct bridge between the tautological and virtual theories.

. When the flag is not full we can still perform this procedure, provided the integrand is supported on the nilpotently filtered locus. We spent the last section of the paper developing an iterated residue for virtual integrals over the nilpotently filtered locus. If we let
\underline{d}=(1,\hat{d})=(1,d_{1},\dots,d_{r})
and assume

d=1+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{r}\leq n+1

we can construct a partial resolution of
\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
that fibers over the flag variety
\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. We can use this to derive a residue formula depending on

\int_{[H_{\operatorname{id}}]^{\operatorname{vir}}}\alpha

where
H_{\operatorname{id}}
is the fiber in the partial resolution over the reference flag

f_{\bullet}=(\operatorname{span}(e_{1},\dots,e_{d_{0}})\subset\cdots\subset\operatorname{span}(e_{1},\dots,e_{\hat{d}}))\in\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Calculating this integral via localization, we get a sum of iterated residues and we can prove that all residues vanish except for the one at the Porteous point i.e., the nested partition containing only unit vectors nested in the natural way. By considering the map

\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{N})

for
n<N
we can prove that this formula remains valid even when
d>n+1
. We obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\underline{d}=(1,d_{1}\dots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}_{\geq 0}
,
d=1+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{r}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. Let
A_{T}^{*}(\operatorname{pt})=\mathbb{Z}[s_{1},\dots,s_{n}]
where
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
are the Chern roots of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
given the standard torus action. Pick a
q
-dimensional
T
-representation
W
with Chern roots
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{q}
and consider the
T
-equivariant rank
q
bundle

E=W\otimes\mathbb{A}^{n}

on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
. Let

-\eta_{0},-\eta_{1},\dots,-\eta_{d-1}

be the Chern roots of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{n}}^{[\underline{d}]}
so that

E^{[\underline{d}]}\cong W\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{n}}^{[\underline{d}]}

has Chern roots
\theta_{i}-\eta_{j}
. Let

P=P(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{q},\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d-1})

be a polynomial in the Chern classes of
E^{[\underline{d}]}
. Then

\int_{[\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}P

can be calculated via the iterated residue formula

\underset{\underline{z}=\infty}{\operatorname{Res}}\frac{P(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{q},\underline{z})\prod_{1\leq m\neq l\leq d-1}(z_{m}-z_{l})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j,k\leq d-1\\
i<k\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\prod}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq d-1\\
w(k),w(j)\leq w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{i}+z_{j}+z_{k}-z_{m})d\underline{z}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq l<m\leq d-1\\
w(l)<w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{m}-z_{l})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{l=1}^{d-1}(s_{i}-z_{l})\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\widetilde{\prod}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)<w(k)\end{subarray}}(z_{i}+z_{j}-z_{k})}

Here
\underline{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{d-1})
and the iterated residue is defined as the coefficient of
(z_{1}\cdots z_{d-1})
in the expansion of the rational expression in the domain
z_{1}\ll\cdots\ll z_{d-1}
.

In the follow-up papers
[
31
,
9
]
we will further develop the theory. In the first we follow a similar approach to develop a nested version of the non-commutative Hilbert scheme, and we will use the critical locus description in the case

n=3
to construct and compute nested degree-0 DT-invariants on
\mathbb{A}^{3}
.
In the second, we will compare these nested DT-invariants with the virtual invariants obtained in this paper, using a rational map from the non-associative to the non-commutative nested Hilbert scheme.

In more detail, we construct and prove the smoothness of the nested non-commutative Hilbert scheme

\operatorname{ncNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),

which is the moduli space parameterizing
(N_{\bullet},A_{1},\dots,A_{n},v)
, where
N_{\bullet}
is a flag of vector spaces of dimension
\underline{d}
, each
A_{i}
is a flag-preserving operator, and
v\in N_{0}
is a cyclic vector, meaning that every element of
N_{\bullet}
is a linear combination of
A_{i_{1}}A_{i_{2}}\cdots A_{i_{m}}v
. As usual, for
n=3
there is a potential

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{ncNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[N_{\bullet},A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},v]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tr}A_{1}[A_{2},A_{3}]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

such that
\operatorname{ncNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{3})=\operatorname{Crit}(f)=Z(df)
, equipping the nested Hilbert scheme with a symmetric perfect obstruction theory. We will extend this construction to the nested Quot scheme. Using this model, we compute the new nested degree 0 higher-rank DT-invariants. We further hope to relate these invariants to the virtual invariants coming from the non-associative Hilbert scheme. To do this, we will study the rational map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{ncNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[N_{\bullet},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},v]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[N_{\bullet},\psi_{2}(\psi_{1}(x_{1}),-),\dots,\psi_{2}(\psi_{1}(x_{n}),-),v\right]}

where
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\vee}=\operatorname{span}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. In other words, it is the same flag
N_{\bullet}
with the same unit
v
, and the operators are defined by left multiplication with
\psi_{1}(x_{i})
. The domain of definition is the Zariski open locus where
N_{\bullet}
is spanned by
v
and by elements obtained by iterative left multiplication by
\psi_{1}(x_{1}),\dots,\psi_{1}(x_{n})
.

Remark 1.1
.

One might be tempted to think that the map is well defined everywhere, since

y_{1}=\psi_{1}(x_{1}),\dots,y_{n}=\psi_{1}(x_{n})
generate
N_{\bullet}
as a non-associative algebra.
This is, however, not the case. The essential issue is that elements of the form

(y_{i}y_{j})(y_{k}y_{m})

cannot
a priori
be written as elements obtained by iterative left multiplication by the
y_{\ell}
.

Similarly, if we simply denote by

A_{i}=\psi_{2}(\psi_{1}(x_{i}),-)

we can define a potential
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[N_{\bullet},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},v]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tr}A_{1}[A_{2},A_{3}]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

which is well defined on the whole non-associative Hilbert scheme. On easily sees that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{ncNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{3})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

commutes and that the nested Hilbert scheme lies in the domain of definition of the rational map.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that the critical locus
\operatorname{Crit}(g)
is exactly the locus where the operators
\psi_{2}(\psi_{1}(x_{i}),-)
commutes. This commutativity yields associativity conditions. Indeed, let
A
be a commutative non-associative algebra and for
a\in A
denote by
L_{a}
the operator given by left multiplication of
a
. If
L_{a}L_{b}=L_{b}L_{a}
then for all
c\in A
we have

(ac)b=b(ac)=L_{b}L_{a}(c)=L_{a}L_{b}(c)=a(bc)=a(cb)

i.e. the associator
[a,c,b]
vanishes for all
c\in A
.

Remark 1.3
.

One might be tempted to think that
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{3})=\operatorname{Crit}(g)
. We have
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{3})\subset\operatorname{Crit}(g)
but the inclusion is in general strict, even if we restrict to the domain of definition of the rational map. Let us consider the following counter example. Let
N=\operatorname{span}(v_{0},v_{1},v_{2})
. Let
v_{0}
be the unit and define a multiplication rule by

\displaystyle v_{1}v_{1}=v_{2},\>v_{1}v_{2}=v_{2}v_{1}=v_{2}\>\text{and}\>v_{2}v_{2}=0.

Set
\psi_{1}(x_{1})=\psi_{1}(x_{2})=\psi_{1}(x_{3})=v_{1}
. This is in the domain of definition of the rational map since everything is spanned by the unit
v_{0}
and left multiplications of
v_{1}
with itself. Clearly the operators
\psi_{2}(\psi_{1}(x_{i}),-)
and
\psi_{2}(\psi_{1}(x_{j}),-)
commutes since
\psi_{1}(x_{i})=\psi_{1}(x_{j})
. However, the algebra is not associative as

(v_{1}v_{1})v_{2}=v_{2}v_{2}=0\neq v_{2}=v_{1}v_{2}=v_{1}(v_{1}v_{2}).

The positive dimensional virtual classes in dimensions
n\gg d
defined in this paper are expected to be intrinsically related to DT-invariants on Calabi-Yau thereefolds. The rational map defined in this section provides us a direct approach to express DT invariants as tautological virtual integrals on the positive dimensional virtual classes in higher dimensions. In this sense, our virtual classes are natural canonical extensions of virtual classes on threefolds.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Non-associative algebras

We provide the definition of a non-associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra and describe how to construct quotients of the free non-associative algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
S
be a scheme. A (quasi-coherent) non-associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra is by definition a pair
(\mathcal{F},\psi)
of a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(S)
equipped with a multiplication rule

\psi:\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}.

A (quasi-coherent) unital non-associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
algebra is a triple
(\mathcal{F},\psi,s)
where
(\mathcal{F},\psi)
is a non-associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
algebra and
s:\mathcal{O}_{S}\to\mathcal{F}
is a global section such that

\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{S}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{s\oplus\operatorname{id}}\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathcal{F}

and

\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{O}_{S}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\oplus s}\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathcal{F}

agrees with
\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{F}}
. A non-associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra
(\mathcal{F},\psi)
is said to be commutative if
\psi
factors through

\operatorname{Sym}^{2}_{S}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}\big/(a\otimes b-b\otimes a).

A morphism of non-associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebras
f:(\mathcal{F},\psi)\to(\mathcal{G},\varphi)
is a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-modules
f:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
such that
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\otimes f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

commutes. A morphism of unital non-associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebras
f:(\mathcal{F},\psi,s)\to(\mathcal{G},\varphi,t)
is a morphism
f:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
of non-associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebras such that
t=fs
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
S\in\operatorname{Sch}_{\mathbb{C}}
, let
(\mathcal{F},\psi)
be a unital non-associative algebra and let
\mathcal{G}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(S)
. Let
\pi:\mathcal{F}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}
be an epimorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-modules with kernel
\mathcal{I}
. Then there exists a necessarily unique unital non-associative algebra structure
\varphi
on
\mathcal{G}
making
\pi
a morphism of unital non-associative algebras if and only if
\mathcal{I}
is a two-sided ideal. If
\psi
is commutative resp. associative, then the same will hold true for
\varphi
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\varphi
exists. Then

\pi(\psi(\mathcal{I}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}))=\varphi(\pi(\mathcal{I})\otimes_{S}\pi(\mathcal{F}))=0

and

\pi(\psi(\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{I}))=\varphi(\pi(\mathcal{F})\otimes_{S}\pi(\mathcal{I}))=0

proving that
\psi(\mathcal{I}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}),\psi(\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{I})\subset\mathcal{I}
. Conversely, if
\mathcal{I}
is a two-sided ideal. We have two exact sequences given by

\mathcal{I}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}\to 0

and

\mathcal{G}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{G}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{G}\to 0.

Since
\pi(\psi(\mathcal{I}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}))=0
it follows that
\pi\psi
uniquely induce a map
\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
. Since
\pi(\psi(\mathcal{F}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{I}))=0
it follows that this induced map takes
\mathcal{G}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{I}
to
0
and hence induces a unique map

\varphi:\mathcal{G}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}.

Let
\tau_{\mathcal{G}}
be the swap map of
\mathcal{G}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{G}
. Commutativity of
\varphi
can the be checked by the vanishing of the map
\varphi-\varphi\tau_{\mathcal{G}}
, which by surjectivity of
\pi
can be checked after precomposing with
\pi\otimes\pi
. This composition yields the map
\pi(\psi-\psi\tau_{\mathcal{F}})
which vanishes if
\psi
is commutative. Similarly, associativity can be checked by the vanishing of the map
\varphi(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varphi)-\varphi(\varphi\otimes\operatorname{id})
, which again by surjectivity of
\pi
can be checked after precomposing with
\pi\otimes\pi\otimes\pi
. This composition gives the map
\pi(\psi(\operatorname{id}\otimes\psi)-\psi(\psi\otimes\operatorname{id}))
which vanishes if
\psi
is associative.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

We note that if
(\mathcal{F},\psi)
is also equipped with a unital structure
s
then we similarly get a unique unital structure
t
on
\mathcal{G}
by setting
t=\pi s
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
S\in\operatorname{Sch}_{\mathbb{C}}
and let
\operatorname{Qlg}(S)
be the category of unital non-associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
algebras. The forgetful functor

\operatorname{Qlg}(S)\to\operatorname{QCoh}(S)

admits a left adjoint
F_{Qlg}(S)
.

Proof.

One could argue briefly using the adjoint functor theorem. Instead, we give concrete construction. We define for each
k\geq 1
the set
B(k)
corresponding to the set of ways to put bracket around a length
k
expression. One can define this inductively setting
B(1)=\{\ast\}
and

B(k)=\coprod_{i+j=k}B(i)\times B(j).

For a
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(S)
we then set

F_{\operatorname{Qlg}}(\mathcal{F}):=\mathcal{O}_{S}\oplus\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigoplus_{x\in B(k)}\mathcal{F}^{\otimes_{S}k}.

The first summand is the unit, and the multiplication rule

F_{\operatorname{QLG}}(\mathcal{F})\otimes_{S}F_{\operatorname{QLG}}(\mathcal{F})\to F_{\operatorname{QLG}}(\mathcal{F})

is given by first distributing the tensor product over the direct sums and then mapping
\mathcal{F}^{\otimes_{S}i}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{F}^{\otimes_{S}j}
corresponding to
x\in B(i)
and
y\in B(j)
to the summand corresponding to
(x,y)\in B(i+j)
. Given the datum of a map
f:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
in
\operatorname{QCoh}(S)
and a unital non-associative structure
s\in H^{0}(S,\mathcal{G})
,
\psi:\mathcal{G}\otimes_{S}\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
one can then easily via induction construct a necessarily unique map

F_{\operatorname{Qlg}}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{G}

of unital non-associative algebras such that the map agrees with
f
when restricted to
\bigoplus_{x\in B(1)}\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{F}
. This proves that the construction is left adjoint to the forgetful functor.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
S\in\operatorname{Sch}_{\mathbb{C}}
and let
\operatorname{Qlg}(S),\operatorname{QClg}(S),\operatorname{QAlg}(S)
and
\operatorname{QCAlg}
be the categories of respectively unital non-associative, unital commutative non-associative, unital associative and unital commutative associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebras. The inclusion functors
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{QClg}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{QCAlg}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Qlg}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{QAlg}(S)}

admits left adjoints. In particular, using
lemma

2.2
we see that the forgetful functor from any of these categories to
\operatorname{QCoh}(S)
admits left adjoints.

Proof.

For
(\mathcal{F},\psi,s)\in\operatorname{Qlg}(S)
we define the commutator ideal as the smallest two-sided ideal containing

\psi(x,y)-\psi(y,x)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{F}
. Taking the quotient of
\mathcal{F}
by this ideal yields the left adjoints of the upper right and lower left arrows of the diamond. Similarly, we can define the associator ideal generated by

\psi(x,\psi(y,z))-\psi(\psi(x,y),z)

for
x,y,z\in\mathcal{F}
. Taking the quotient of
\mathcal{F}
by this ideal yields the left adjoints of the lower right and upper left arrows of the diamond.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

We will denote the left adjoints of the forgetful functors
\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{QCoh}(S)
by
F_{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We will throughout the paper use the following standard notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{S}\left\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right\}:=F_{\operatorname{Qlg}}\left((\mathcal{O}_{S}^{n})^{\vee}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{S}\left\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right\}_{c}:=F_{\operatorname{QClg}}\left((\mathcal{O}_{S}^{n})^{\vee}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{S}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle:=F_{\operatorname{QAlg}}\left((\mathcal{O}_{S}^{n})^{\vee}\right)\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{S}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]:=F_{\operatorname{QCAlg}}\left((\mathcal{O}_{S}^{n})^{\vee}\right)

for the various free algebras on
n
generators. Notice that we take the dual to keep the convention of a covariant equivalence between vector bundles and locally free sheaves of finite rank.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
S\in\operatorname{Sch}_{\mathbb{C}}
and let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
be quasi-coherent sheaves. Let
\psi,s
be the multiplication rule and unit of
F_{\operatorname{Qlg}}(\mathcal{F})
. The following data is equivalent.

(1)

A map
f:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
and a unital non-associative algebra structure
(\varphi,t)
on
\mathcal{G}
such that the induced map
\pi:F_{\operatorname{Qlg}}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{G}
is an epimorphism.

(2)

An epimorphism
\pi:F_{\operatorname{Qlg}}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{G}
of quasi-coherent sheaves such that the kernel is a two-sided ideal.

Proof.

By adjunction and
lemma

2.1
both types of data exactly describes a surjection
\pi
of unital non-associative algebras.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
S\in\operatorname{Sch}_{\mathbb{C}}
and let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
be quasi-coherent sheaves. Let
(\varphi,t)
be a unital non-associative algebra structure on
\mathcal{G}
and let
\pi:F_{\operatorname{Qlg}}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{G}
be an epimorphism of unital non-associative algebras. Then
\mathcal{G}
is associative (resp. commutative) if and only if
\pi
factors through
F_{\operatorname{Qlg}}(\mathcal{F})\to F_{\operatorname{QAlg}}(\mathcal{F})
(resp.
F_{\operatorname{Qlg}}(\mathcal{F})\to F_{\operatorname{QClg}}(\mathcal{F})
).

Proof.

If
\mathcal{G}
is associative (resp. commutative) then we get such a factorization since
F_{\operatorname{QAlg}}
(resp.
F_{\operatorname{QClg}}
) is the composite of
F_{\operatorname{Qlg}}
with the left adjoint of the inclusion
\operatorname{QAlg}(S)\to\operatorname{Qlg}(S)
(reps.
\operatorname{QClg}(S)\to\operatorname{Qlg}(S)
). Conversely, if such a factorization exists associativity or commutativity of
\mathcal{G}
will then directly follow from
lemma

2.1

∎

2.2.
Obstruction theories and virtual classes and pullbacks

Let
X/\mathbb{C}
be a scheme and let

\mathbb{L}_{X}=\tau_{\geq-1}L_{X}^{\bullet}\in D^{[-1,0]}(X)

be the truncation of the full cotangent complex
L_{X}^{\bullet}
as defined in
[
26
]
. A perfect obstruction theory of
X\to Y
as defined in
[
3
]
is a map

\phi:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{L}_{X}

in
D^{[-1,0]}(X)
, where
\mathbb{E}
is a perfect complex of perfect amplitude
[-1,0]
and where
h^{0}(\phi)
is an isomorphism and
h^{-1}(\phi)
is surjective. If we have

\mathbb{E}=[E^{-1}\to E^{0}]

then a perfect obstruction theory gives rise to a cone

\mathfrak{C}\hookrightarrow E_{1}=(E^{-1})^{\vee}

which in term gives rise to a virtual fundamental class

[X]^{\operatorname{vir}}=0^{*}[\mathfrak{C}]\in A_{\operatorname{vd}}(X)

where
0:X\to E_{1}
is the zero section and
0^{*}
its Gysin. Here,

\operatorname{vd}=\chi(\mathbb{E})=\operatorname{rk}E^{0}-\operatorname{rk}E^{-1}.

Similarly, for
f:X\to Y
a map of schemes a relative perfect obstruction theory is a map

\phi:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{L}_{X/Y}=\tau_{\geq-1}L_{X/Y}^{\bullet}

in
D^{[-1,0]}(X)
where
\mathbb{E}
and
\phi
satisfies the same definitions as above. It is shown in
[
28
]
that
\phi
defines a virtual pullback

f^{*}=f_{\mathbb{E}}^{*}:A_{*}(Y)\to A_{*+\chi(\mathbb{E})}(X).

If
Y=S
is a smooth (or pure dimensional) one may define a virtual class by

[X]^{\operatorname{vir}}=f^{*}[S].

This recovers the usual definition for
S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
. It is further shown that if we have a compatible triple i.e. a distinguished triangle

f^{*}\mathbb{E}_{Y/S}\to\mathbb{E}_{X/S}\to\mathbb{E}_{X/Y}\to f^{*}\mathbb{E}_{Y/S}[1]

of perfect obstruction theories with a map to the canonical distinguished triangle

f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{Y/S}\to\mathbb{L}_{X/S}\to\mathbb{L}_{X/Y}\to f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{Y/S}[1]

then the corresponding virtual pullbacks are functorial and so in particular

f^{*}[Y]^{\operatorname{vir}}=[X]^{\operatorname{vir}}.

In this paper, we are interested in the following simple case.

Example 2.1
.

Suppose
\pi:Y\to S
is a smooth morphism over a smooth base scheme
S
. Then

\mathbb{L}_{Y/S}\cong\Omega_{Y/S}

and
\phi=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{L}_{X/Y}}
defines a perfect obstruction theory with virtual pullback being the usual flat pullback
\pi^{*}
. Note that

[Y]^{\operatorname{vir}}=\pi^{*}[S]=[Y].

If
i:X\hookrightarrow Y
is a closed immersion with ideal sheaf
I
then

\mathbb{L}_{X/Y}=[I/I^{2}\to 0].

If we can find a vector bundle

E=\operatorname{Spec}_{Y}\operatorname{Sym}^{\bullet}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}

with a section

3.
Construction of the non-associative Hilbert scheme

In this section we construct a moduli functor and prove that it is representable by a smooth scheme
\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
which we call the non-associative nested Hilbert scheme of
\underline{d}
points. The proof will be in two steps. First we exhibit the functor as the quotient stack of a smooth scheme by a linear algebraic group acting freely. This proves that the functor is representable by a smooth algebraic space. Next we explicitly construct a cover of Zariski open subfunctors which we prove are representable. This proves that the algebraic space must in fact be a scheme.

3.1.
The definition

Fix a dimension vector
\underline{d}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
,
r\geq 0
. We recall that the nested Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
parameterize nesting of closed subschemes

\xi_{1}\hookrightarrow\xi_{2}\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow\xi_{r+1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}

such that
Z_{k}
is finite of length
d_{0}+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k-1}
. Equivalently it parameterize flags of ideals

I_{r+1}\subset I_{r}\subset\cdots\subset I_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

such that
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/I_{k}
has an underlying vector space of dimension
d_{0}+\cdots+d_{k-1}
. Equivalent to that, we see that it parameterize a surjective map of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras

\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/I_{r+1}=:N

and a flag of ideals on the codomain

N=N_{0}\supset N_{1}\supset\cdots\supset N_{r+1}=0

satisfying that
N_{k}/N_{k+1}
is a
d_{k}
dimensional vector space. The idea behind the non-associative Hilbert scheme is simply to take this last description and relax the condition of associativity. That is, if we denote by
\mathbb{C}\left\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right\}_{c}
the free unital and commutative (but not associative)
\mathbb{C}
-algebra on
n
generators, the non-associative Hilbert scheme parameterizes surjections of unital, commutative (but not necessarily associative) algebras

\mathbb{C}\left\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right\}_{c}\twoheadrightarrow N

and a flag on ideals

N=N_{0}\supset N_{1}\supset\cdots\supset N_{r+1}=0

such that
N_{k}/N_{k+1}
is
d_{k}
dimensional as a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space. To construct this space, we simply specify its functor of points. We recall that for the Hilbert scheme going from a
\mathbb{C}
-valued point to an
S
-valued we simply replace
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathcal{O}_{S}
and vector space of a given dimension with vector bundle of a given rank in the above definitions. Doing the same for the non-associative Hilbert scheme, we arrive at the following definition

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\underline{d}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be a dimension vector. We define a functor

as follows. For
S\in\operatorname{Sch}_{/\mathbb{C}}
we set

Here

\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{F}_{0}\supset\mathcal{F}_{1}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{F}_{r+1}=0)

is a flag of locally free sheaves on
S
such that
\mathcal{F}_{k}/\mathcal{F}_{k+1}
is locally free of rank
d_{k}
,
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is equipped with a unital commutative non-associative
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra structure

which satisfies

The surjection

\mathcal{O}_{S}

\mathcal{O}_{S}

4.
The punctual locus in the full flag case

In this section we will restrict ourselves to the full flag case, i.e., a dimension vector

and define the endomorphisms by

. Without associativity these might not commute. However, this is true for dimension reasons once we restrict to the one-dimensional space

. Having this picture in mind, we make the following construction.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exist a map

\operatorname{naHilb}^{r}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\xrightarrow{naHC}(\mathbb{A}^{n})^{r+1}

which we will call the non-associative Hilbert-Chow morphism. The composite

\operatorname{NHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\xrightarrow{\iota}\operatorname{naNHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\xrightarrow{naHC}(\mathbb{A}^{n})^{r+1}

.

We construct the morphism directly. Let
(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\Psi_{2},\mathfrak{s},\Psi_{1})
be the universal flag and maps of
\operatorname{naNHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{i}=\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{V}_{i+1}
which is a line bundle. Then the maps
\Psi_{2}
and
\Psi_{1}
induces a map

\Psi_{2}(\Psi_{1}\otimes\operatorname{id}):(\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{i}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}

and so, we obtain a map

which induces a map
\operatorname{naNHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\to\mathbb{A}^{n}

we obtain the desired map

\operatorname{naNHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\to(\mathbb{A}^{n})^{r+1}.

Going through the definitions of
\iota
and

on the functor of points one easily sees that

.
∎

4.2.
The punctual locus

We construct a closed locus
\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{naNHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
in such a way that it intersects with the associativity locus exactly in the punctual nested Hilbert scheme. The first idea would be to simply take the preimage of the origin under the Hilbert-Chow morphism. However, it turns out that choosing a slightly smaller locus yields better formal properties. To motivate this locus recall that the first point of the ordered Hilbert Chow morphism is given by
\operatorname{Supp}(I_{0}/I_{1})
. For this to be zero we need multiplication with
x_{i}
to vanish on
I_{0}/I_{1}
which is the same as requiring
I_{1}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Now for
\operatorname{Supp}(I_{k}/I_{k+1})
to be zero we need
x_{i}\cdot I_{k}\subset I_{k+1}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, but since
I_{1}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
this is the same as requiring

(I_{1}/I_{r+1})\cdot(I_{k}/I_{r+1})\subset(I_{k+1}/I_{r+1}).

Translating these conditions to the non-associative Hilbert scheme, we arrive at the following definition

Definition 4.1
.

Consider
\operatorname{naNHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
with the universal flag and maps
(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\Psi_{2},s,\Psi_{1})
. The punctual nested non-associative Hilbert scheme is the closed subscheme

\operatorname{naNHilb}^{r+1}_{0}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\subset\operatorname{naNHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})

where
\Psi_{1}
factors through
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and where
\Psi_{2}
maps
\mathcal{V}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{k}
to
\mathcal{V}_{k+1}
for all
k=1,\dots,r
. If we let
\mathcal{Q}
be the quotient of
\mathcal{H}om(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{1})
by the subsheaf of maps taking
\mathcal{V}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{k}
to
\mathcal{V}_{k+1}
then we can similarly describe the punctual locus as the zero locus of the section of

\mathcal{H}om\left((\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee},\mathcal{V}_{0}/\mathcal{V}_{1}\right)\oplus\mathcal{Q}

given by
\Psi_{1}\oplus\Psi_{2}
.

We prove that this actually is an extension of the punctual locus

\operatorname{NHilb}_{0}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})=HC^{-1}(\mathbf{0})\subset\operatorname{NHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})

of the nested Hilbert scheme of points.

Lemma 4.1
.

It holds that

\operatorname{naHC}(\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n}))\subset\mathbf{0}.

Proof.

Let again
\mathcal{L}_{i}=\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{V}_{i+1}
. Since
\Psi_{1}
factors through
\mathcal{V}_{1}
on the punctual locus it follows that the map

\Psi_{2}(\Psi_{1}\otimes\operatorname{id}):(\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{0}\to\mathcal{L}_{0}

vanishes. Similarly, we can factor

\Psi_{2}(\Psi_{1}\otimes\operatorname{id}):(\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{k}\to\mathcal{V}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{k}\xrightarrow{\Psi_{2}}\mathcal{L}_{k}

and use that
\Psi_{2}(\mathcal{V}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{k})\subset\mathcal{V}_{k+1}
to see that this map also vanishes.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists a closed immersion

such that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{NHilb}_{0}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}

We prove the statement on the functor of points. Given

and

4.3.
Projective tower structure

We prove that the punctual non-associative Hilbert scheme can be exhibited as an iterated tower of projective fibrations. The proof is essentially the same as in
[
27
, Proposition 6.3]
with a slightly more general space. Before proceeding, we show how the nested non-associative Hilbert scheme generally maps to a nested non-associative Hilbert scheme with a truncated dimension vector.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\underline{d}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{r})
and consider

Remark 4.1
.

Since a quotient of an associative algebra is again associative this map preserves the associativity locus. One can easily see that on the nested Hilbert scheme of points this is simply the map

\Big[I_{r+1}\subset I_{r}\subset\cdots\subset I_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\Big]\mapsto\Big[I_{r}\subset\cdots\subset I_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\Big]

which forgets the largest subscheme of the nesting.

Lemma 4.3

The natural map

∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

4.4.
Other dimension vectors and relations to the work of Kazarian

We prove a slight generalization of the above where we allow different dimension vectors. We construct the moduli functor of non-unital non-associative algebras with a flag structure satisfying a nilpotency condition. We prove that it embeds into the non-associative Hilbert scheme by freely adjoining a unit. We prove that its intersection with the associativity locus is contained in the punctual Hilbert scheme but will in general not be the whole punctual Hilbert scheme. A proof completely similar to the section above shows that the locus admits a description as a tower of Grassmannian fibrations. At last, we show that it contains a closed locus of algebras satisfying a stronger nilpotency condition which agrees with the non-associative Hilbert schemes appearing in the work of Kazarian
[
27
]
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\underline{d}=(1,d_{1},\cdots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be a dimension vector beginning with

. We define a moduli functor

by

The tuples of the right-hand set consists of the following data.

(1)

A flag of locally free sheaves

\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{F}_{1}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{F}_{r+1}=0)

Lemma 4.6

Lemma 4.7

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\underline{d}=(1,d_{1},\dots,d_{r})
. Let
S\in\operatorname{Sch}_{\mathbb{C}}
and let

be a map of schemes. If

\operatorname{NHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let the map be classified by

\mathcal{I}_{r+1}\subset\mathcal{I}_{r}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{I}_{1}\subset\mathcal{I}_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{S}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

We set

\mathcal{F}_{k}=\mathcal{I}_{k}/\mathcal{I}_{r+1}

equipped with
\psi_{2}
corresponding to the multiplication map of the algebra,
s
its unit and
\psi_{1}
the restriction of the quotient map to the linear functions. Since

does not factor through
\mathcal{F}_{1}
while
\psi_{1}
does it follows that we have a map

\mathcal{O}_{S}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\to\mathcal{F}_{0}/\mathcal{F}_{1}\cong\mathcal{O}_{S}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\mathcal{I}_{1}

which is an isomorphism by rank reasons. This means that

Since

We conclude by induction that

which shows that

is punctual.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\underline{d}=(1,d_{1},\dots,d_{r})

\displaystyle\rho(t)v_{u+u^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\rho(t)\psi_{2}(v_{u}\otimes v_{u^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\rho(t)\psi_{2}\left((\rho(t)^{-1}\rho(t)v_{u})\otimes(\rho(t)^{-1}\rho(t)v_{u^{\prime}})\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{2}^{\rho(t)}\left((\rho(t)v_{u})\otimes(\rho(t)v_{u^{\prime}})\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{2}\left((\rho(t)v_{u})\otimes(\rho(t)v_{u^{\prime}})\right).

Combining these facts an induction argument shows that
\rho(t)v_{u}=t^{u}v_{u}
i.e., the
T
-space structure on
N_{i}
induced from
\rho^{-1}
is isomorphic to
N_{i}^{\lambda}
. From this we immediately get that

\mathfrak{p}_{\underline{d}}=\operatorname{End}^{\operatorname{fil}}(N_{\bullet}^{\lambda})\in K_{T}^{0}(\operatorname{pt})

when
\mathfrak{p}_{\underline{d}}
is equipped with the adjoint action composed with
\rho^{-1}
. The variety
M_{n,\underline{d}}
is a Zariski open of the affine sub-space

W=f^{-1}(\operatorname{id}_{N_{0}})\subset V:=\operatorname{Hom}((\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\vee},N_{0})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}^{\operatorname{fil}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}N_{0},N_{0})\oplus N_{0}

where
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f:V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{End}^{\operatorname{fil}}(N_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},v)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(x\mapsto\psi_{2}(v,x)).}

This map is an equivariant submersion when we identify
N_{\bullet}
with
N_{\bullet}^{\lambda}
. It follows that

T_{M_{n,\underline{d}},p}=T_{W,p}=T_{V,p}-T_{\operatorname{End}^{\operatorname{fil}}(N_{\bullet}),\operatorname{id}}=T_{V,p}-\operatorname{End}^{\operatorname{fil}}(N_{\bullet}^{\lambda})

from which the statement now immediately follows since

T_{V,p}=(t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{n})N^{\lambda}_{0}+\operatorname{Hom}^{\operatorname{fil}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}N_{\bullet}^{\lambda},N_{\bullet}^{\lambda})+N_{0}^{\lambda}.

∎

To further compute this, we introduce notation to keep track of the equivariant weights.

Setup 5.3
.

Let

\lambda_{\bullet}=(\lambda_{1}\subset\lambda_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\lambda_{r+1}\subset(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n})

be an
n
-dimensional nested partition of length
\underline{d}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{r})
and set

\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d-1}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n})

of the partition
\lambda_{r+1}
in such a way that

\{\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{k}\}

is a partition for all
0\leq k\leq d-1
and that

\{\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d_{0}+\cdots+d_{i-1}-1}\}=\lambda_{i}.

For
0\leq k\leq d-1
we define
w(k)=i
if

d_{0}+\cdots+d_{i-2}-1<k\leq d_{0}+\cdots+d_{i-1}-1,

i.e.

(w(0),w(1),\dots,w(d-1))=(\underbrace{0,0,\dots,0}_{d_{0}\text{ times}},\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{d_{1}\text{ times}},\dots,\underbrace{r,r\cdots,r}_{d_{r}\text{ times}}).

It follows that
x^{\mathbf{u}_{k}}\in N_{\bullet}^{\lambda}
is in

if and only if

. Note also that

with
x^{\mathbf{u}_{k}}
a
T
-eigenvector of weight
t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\lambda_{\bullet}
be an
n
-dimensional nested partition with enumeration
\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d-1}
as in
5.3
. Then the trace of
T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),\lambda_{\bullet}}
is given by

(t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{n})\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}.

Proof.

We have

T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),\lambda_{\bullet}}=(t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{n}+1)N^{\lambda}_{0}+\operatorname{Hom}^{\operatorname{fil}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}N_{\bullet}^{\lambda},N_{\bullet}^{\lambda})-2\operatorname{End}^{\operatorname{fil}}(N^{\lambda}_{\bullet})

so, using the
T
-eigenvector basis

(t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{n}+1)\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j,k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}.

We see some immediate cancellation. The ”
i=0
” terms of the second sum cancels with one copy of the third sum. The last copy of the first sum corresponding to the coefficient 1 cancels with the ”
j=0
” terms of the third sum. This leaves us with

(t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{n})\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq d-1\\

∎

Remark 5.1
.

From here on the cancellation gets trickier. A term of the form
t^{\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}
cancels with either a term in the first or second sum depending on
\mathbf{u}_{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
. If
\mathbf{u}_{j}=e_{i}
is a unit vector, it will cancel with
t_{i}t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}
from the first sum. If it is not a unit vector, we can rewrite
\mathbf{u}_{j}=\mathbf{u}_{i_{1}}+\mathbf{u}_{i_{2}}
for
1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq j
. Since
i_{2}\leq j
we have
w(i_{2})\leq w(j)\leq w(k)
and so the term cancels with
t^{\mathbf{u}_{i_{1}}+\mathbf{u}_{i_{2}}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}
from the second sum. Another way to keep track of only the positive weights is via the following multiset definition.

Lemma 5.3
.

For a nested partition

\lambda_{\bullet}=(\lambda_{1}\subset\lambda_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\lambda_{r+1})

of length
\underline{d}
we recursively define multisets

S_{m}^{T}(\lambda_{\bullet})

for
-1\leq m\leq r
as follows. Choose an enumeration
\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d-1}
as in
5.3
. We set

S_{-1}^{T}(\lambda_{\bullet})=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}

and recursively define

S_{m}^{T}(\lambda_{\bullet})=\left(S_{k-1}(\lambda_{\bullet})\cup\{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}\>|\>1\leq i\leq j,\>w(j)=m\}\right)\big\backslash\{\mathbf{u}_{j}\>|\>1\leq j,\>w(j)=m\}.

Then the trace of
T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),\lambda_{\bullet}}
is given by

\sum_{m=0}^{r}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\lambda_{m+1}\backslash\lambda_{m}}\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in S_{m}^{T}(\lambda_{\bullet})}t^{\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}}.

Proof.

One easily sees by induction that

S_{k}^{T}(\lambda_{\bullet})=\left(\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}\cup\{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}\>|\>1\leq i\leq j,\>w(j)\leq k\}\right)\big\backslash\{\mathbf{u}_{j}\>|\>w(j)\leq k\}

and since
\mathbf{u}_{k}\in\lambda_{m+1}\backslash\lambda_{m}
if and only if
w(k)=m
the result now easily follows from the above calculation.
∎

Lastly, we compute the rank of the fixed part.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\lambda_{\bullet}
be an
n
-dimensional nested partition with enumeration
\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d-1}
as in
5.3
.
Define

W_{T}(\mathbf{u}_{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\mathbf{u}_{k}=e_{i}\text{ for some }i=1,\dots,n\\
\#\{(i,j)\>|\>1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1,\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}=\mathbf{u}_{k}\}-1&\text{else}\end{array}\right.

for
k=1,\cdots d-1
and
e_{i}
the
i

T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),\lambda_{\bullet}}^{\operatorname{fix}}
is

\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}W_{T}(\mathbf{u}_{k}).

Proof.

The positive trivial weights of

(t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{n})\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq d-1\\

are exactly of the form
t_{i}t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}
for
\mathbf{u}_{k}=e_{i}
or

for

,
i\leq j
and
w(j)\leq w(k)
. We note that since

we have

so the condition

is redundant. For each

we have exactly one negative trivial weight of the form

from the third sum. It follows that the trace of

is given by

\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}Q_{k}(t)

where

Q_{k}(t)=t_{i}t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}-t^{\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}=0

if
\mathbf{u}_{k}=e_{i}
for some

and

Q_{k}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1\\
\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}=\mathbf{u}_{k}\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}-t^{\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}

from which the statement follows directly
∎

Corollary 5.3

The fixed part of the tangent space

The above formula only depends on a choice of enumeration

5.3.
The fixed virtual class

The
T
-equivariant perfect obstruction theory
\mathbb{E}
induces a virtual class on the fixed locus
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})^{T}
. Since the latter is zero dimensional, reduced and proper we are in the situation of

. With that in mind we make the following definition.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\lambda_{\bullet}
be a flag of partitions corresponding to a fixed point
\lambda_{\bullet}\in\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. We will say that
\lambda_{\bullet}
is admissible if
[\lambda_{\bullet}]^{\operatorname{vir}}\neq[0]
i.e., if

\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{ass},[\lambda_{\bullet}]}^{\operatorname{fix}}=\operatorname{rk}T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),[\lambda_{\bullet}]}^{\operatorname{fix}}.

We recall that if we choose an enumeration
\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d-1}
of
\lambda_{\bullet}
as in
5.3
then we have

\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{ass},\lambda_{\bullet}}^{\operatorname{fix}}=\sum_{m=0}^{d-1}W_{B}(\mathbf{u}_{m})\text{ and }\operatorname{rk}T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),\lambda_{\bullet}}^{\operatorname{fix}}=\sum_{m=0}^{d-1}W_{T}(\mathbf{u}_{m})

where

W_{T}(\mathbf{u}_{m})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\mathbf{u}_{m}=e_{i}\text{ for some }i=1,\dots,n\\

and

We also note that admissibility of
\lambda_{\bullet}
only depends on
\lambda_{r+1}

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\lambda_{\bullet}
be an nested partition with chosen enumeration
\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d-1}
as in
5.3
. Then

W_{T}(\mathbf{u}_{m})\leq W_{B}(\mathbf{u}_{m})

for
m=1,\dots,d-1
.

Proof.

We can assume that
\mathbf{u}_{m}
is not a unit vector, since in that case we simply get
0\leq 0
. For each
i
let

\phi(i)=\min\left\{j\big|\mathbf{u}_{j}\text{ is a unit vector and }\mathbf{u}_{j}\leq\mathbf{u}_{i}\right\}.

Note that
\phi(m)<m
since
\mathbf{u}_{m}
is not a unit vector. Let
\kappa
be the map
\kappa(\mathbf{u}_{i})=i
be the map extracting the index. It will suffice to prove that
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left\{(i,j,k)\big|i<k,\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}+\mathbf{u}_{k}=\mathbf{u}_{m}\right\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left\{(i,j)|\phi(m)<i\leq j,\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}=\mathbf{u}_{m}\right\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(i,j,k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\min\{\kappa(\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}),k\},\max\{\kappa(\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}),k\}\right)}

is a surjection. Indeed, the cardinality of the codomain corresponds to

\#\left\{(i,j)|i\leq j,\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}=\mathbf{u}_{m}\right\}-1

since we have removed the single element of the set corresponding to the pair

(\kappa(\mathbf{u}_{\phi(m)}),\kappa(\mathbf{u}_{m}-\mathbf{u}_{\phi(m)})).

To show surjectivity we let
\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}=\mathbf{u}_{m}
with
\phi(m)<i\leq j
. We must have either
\mathbf{u}_{\phi(m)}\leq\mathbf{u}_{i}
or
\mathbf{u}_{\phi(m)}\leq\mathbf{u}_{j}
. In the first case we let
\mathbf{u}_{k}=\mathbf{u}_{i}-\mathbf{u}_{\phi(m)}
, then clearly
(i,j)
is the image of
(\phi(m),k,j)
. Similarly in the second case we can just take
\mathbf{u}_{k}=\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{\phi(m)}
then
(i,j)
is in the image of
(\phi(m),j,i)
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\lambda_{\bullet}
be a flag of partitions. Then
\lambda_{\bullet}
is admissible if and only if the following conditions hold.

(1)

For all
1\leq k\leq n
and
\alpha>0
we have

\alpha e_{k}\in\lambda_{r+1}\implies\alpha\leq 4.

(2)

For all

and all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
we have

\alpha_{1}e_{k_{1}}+\alpha_{2}e_{k_{2}}\in\lambda_{r+1}\implies\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\leq 3.

(3)

For all

and

and all
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}>0
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}e_{k_{i}}\notin\lambda_{r+1}.

Choose an enumeration
\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots\mathbf{u}_{d-1}
as in
5.3

we have

. This shows that these partitions are admissible. One can further check that

\displaystyle W_{T}(5e_{k})

\displaystyle<W_{B}(5e_{k}),

\displaystyle W_{T}(\alpha_{1}e_{k_{1}}+\alpha_{2}e_{k_{2}})

\displaystyle<W_{B}(\alpha_{1}e_{k_{1}}+\alpha_{2}e_{k_{2}}),

\displaystyle W_{T}(e_{k_{1}}+e_{k_{2}}+e_{k_{3}})

\displaystyle<W_{B}(e_{k_{1}}+e_{k_{2}}+e_{k_{3}})

\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
define

\mathbf{u}(\underline{s})=u_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}s_{n}.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\underline{d}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}_{\geq 0}
,
d=d_{0}+\cdots+d_{r}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. In the Chow group
A_{*}^{T}(\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}))_{\operatorname{loc}}
localized in linear functions of
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
the virtual fundamental class
[\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}
equals

\sum_{\lambda_{\bullet}}[\lambda_{\bullet}]\cap\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j,k\leq d-1\\
i<k\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\prod}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq d-1\\
w(k),w(j)\leq w(m)\end{subarray}}(\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}+\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}_{m})(\underline{s})\widetilde{\prod}_{j=1}^{d-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}(\underline{s})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{\prod}_{k=0}^{d-1}(e_{i}-\mathbf{u}_{k})(\underline{s})\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\widetilde{\prod}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}(\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k})(\underline{s})}.

Here the sum is over all admissible
(n-1)
-dimensional nested partitions
\lambda_{\bullet}
of length
\underline{d}
and where we for each
\lambda_{\bullet}
have a chosen enumeration
\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d-1}
as in
5.3
. Alternatively, using the multisets of
lemma

5.3
and
lemma

5.5
we have

[\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}=\sum_{\lambda_{\bullet}}[x_{\lambda_{\bullet}}]\cap\prod_{m=0}^{r}\prod_{\mathbf{v}\in\lambda_{m+1}\backslash\lambda_{m}}\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u}\in S_{m}^{ass}(\lambda_{\bullet})\\
\mathbf{u}\neq\mathbf{v}\end{subarray}}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})(\underline{s})}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u}\in S_{m}^{T}(\lambda_{\bullet})\\
\mathbf{u}\neq\mathbf{v}\end{subarray}}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})(\underline{s})}

where the sum is again over admissible nested partitions
\lambda_{\bullet}
of length
\underline{d}
.

Example 5.1
.

Let us consider the (non-nested) Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d+1}(\mathbb{A}^{3})
of
d
points on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
. Then the formula reads

\sum_{\lambda_{\bullet}}[x_{\lambda_{\bullet}}]\cap\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j,k\leq d-1\\
i<k\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=0}^{d-1}(\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}+\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}_{m})(\underline{s})\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}(\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k})(\underline{s})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}(e_{i}-\mathbf{u}_{k})(\underline{s})\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}(\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k})(\underline{s})}

Let us calculate this explicitly for
d=3
, i.e., the Hilbert scheme of three points. We have two types of partitions. They are either given by
\{0,e_{i},2e_{i}\}
or
\{0,e_{i},e_{j}\}
for
i\neq j
and are both admissible. For the first type of partition, we get a contribution of

\frac{4s_{i}3s_{i}2s_{i}5s_{i}4s_{i}3s_{i}s_{i}(-s_{i})2s_{i}s_{i}}{2s_{i}s_{i}3s_{i}2s_{i}s_{i}4s_{i}3s_{i}2s_{i}\prod_{k=1}^{n}s_{k}(s_{k}-2s_{i})\prod_{k\neq i}(s_{k}-s_{i})}

which simplfies to

\frac{-10s_{i}^{2}}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}s_{k}(s_{k}-2s_{i})\prod_{k\neq i}(s_{k}-s_{i})}=\frac{10}{\prod_{k\neq i}s_{k}(s_{k}-s_{i})(s_{k}-2s_{i})}

For the other type of partition, we get

\frac{(2s_{i}+s_{j})(s_{i}+s_{j})2s_{i}(s_{i}+2s_{j})2s_{j}(s_{i}+s_{j})s_{i}(s_{i}-s_{j})s_{j}(s_{j}-s_{i})}{2s_{i}s_{i}(2s_{i}-s_{j})(s_{i}+s_{j})s_{j}s_{i}2s_{j}(2s_{j}-s_{i})s_{j}\prod_{k=1}^{n}s_{k}\prod_{k\neq i}(s_{k}-s_{i})\prod_{k\neq j}(s_{k}-s_{j})}

which can be reduced to

\frac{(2s_{i}+s_{j})(s_{i}+2s_{j})(s_{i}+s_{j})}{s_{i}(2s_{i}-s_{j})s_{j}(2s_{j}-s_{i})\prod_{k=1}^{n}s_{k}\prod_{k\neq i,j}(s_{k}-s_{i})(s_{k}-s_{j})}.

We directly see that the virtual dimension is given by
3n-3
. Let us specialize to
n=3
with a virtual dimension of
6
. Consider the tautological bundle
\mathcal{O}^{[3]}_{\mathbb{A}^{3}}
and let
c_{2}=c_{2}^{T}((\mathcal{O}^{[3]}_{\mathbb{A}^{3}})^{\vee})
the second equivariant Chern class of the dual bundle. Let us integrate
c_{2}^{3}
against the virtual class. For
\lambda=\{\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{3}\}
the Chern roots of
(\mathcal{O}^{[3]}_{\mathbb{A}^{3}})^{\vee}
are
\mathbf{u}_{0}(\underline{s}),\mathbf{u}_{1}(\underline{s}),\mathbf{u}_{3}(\underline{s})
. It follows that for
\lambda=\{0,e_{i},2e_{i}\}
we have

c_{2}^{3}|_{\lambda}=(2s_{i}^{2})^{3}=8s_{i}^{6}

while for
\lambda=\{0,e_{i},e_{j}\}
we have

c_{2}|_{\lambda}=(s_{i}s_{j})^{3}=s_{i}^{3}s_{j}^{3}.

For
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
the contribution at the partition
\{0,e_{i},2e_{i}\}
is then

\frac{80s_{i}^{6}}{s_{j}(s_{j}-s_{i})(s_{j}-2s_{i})s_{k}(s_{k}-s_{i})(s_{k}-2s_{i})}

while the contribution at
\{0,e_{i},e_{j}\}
is

\frac{(2s_{i}+s_{j})(s_{i}+2s_{j})(s_{i}+s_{j})s_{i}^{3}s_{j}^{3}}{s_{i}^{2}(2s_{i}-s_{j})s_{j}^{2}(2s_{j}-s_{i})s_{k}(s_{k}-s_{i})(s_{k}-s_{j})}=\frac{(2s_{i}+s_{j})(s_{i}+2s_{j})(s_{i}+s_{j})s_{i}s_{j}}{(2s_{i}-s_{j})(2s_{j}-s_{i})s_{k}(s_{k}-s_{i})(s_{k}-s_{j})}.

Summing over all 3 choices for each of the two types of partitions we get

\int_{[\operatorname{Hilb}^{3}(\mathbb{A}^{3})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}c_{2}^{T}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{3}}^{[3]})^{3}=20\frac{(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3})^{3}}{s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}}-31\frac{(s_{1}s_{2}+s_{1}s_{3}+s_{2}s_{3})(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3})}{s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}}+11.

In particular, if we restrict to the Calabi-Yau torus
T_{\operatorname{CY}}=\{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\in T\>|\>t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}=1\}
the virtual integral evaluates to
11
.

6.
Iterated residue formula

In this section we choose a dimension vector of the form

\underline{d}=(1,d_{1},\dots,d_{r})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{r+1}.

We let
\hat{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{r})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{r}
so that
\underline{d}=(1,\hat{d})
.
We show how tautological virtual integrals on
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
can in some cases be expressed in terms of virtual integrals defined via
\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{0}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. We then develop an iterated residue formula for these latter types of integrals. The key to proving this formula is a partial resolution

\widetilde{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{0}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\to\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{0}(\mathbb{A}^{n})

which fibers over the flag variety
\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

6.1.
Reducing the integral to the nilpotently filtered locus

Let
(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\psi_{2},s,\psi_{1})
be the universal structure on
\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the quotient of
\mathcal{H}om^{\operatorname{fil}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{1})
by the subsheaf of maps which takes
\mathcal{V}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{k}
to
\mathcal{V}_{k+1}
. We let

\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{punct}}=\mathcal{H}om((\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee},\mathcal{V}_{0}/\mathcal{V}_{1})\oplus\mathcal{Q}

and note that
\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\subset\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
identifies with the zero locus of a (
T
-equivariant) section of
\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{punct}}
. Since
\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
is smooth the embedding is regular with normal bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{punct}}|_{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}
. This immediately allows us to compute the tangent space.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\lambda_{\bullet}
be a nested partition such that the corresponding fixed point is in
\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. Let
\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d-1}
be an enumeration of
\lambda_{\bullet}
in the sense of
5.3
. Then the trace of
T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),\lambda_{\bullet}}
is given by

(t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{n})\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)<w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}.

Proof.

It is clear that

T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}=T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}-\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{punct}}.

Since
d_{0}=1
we see that

N_{0}^{\lambda}/N_{1}^{\lambda}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(x^{\mathbf{u}_{0}})

which is the trivial
T
-representation. We conclude

\mathcal{H}om((\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee},\mathcal{V}_{0}/\mathcal{V}_{1})|_{\lambda_{\bullet}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}.

Furthermore, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda_{\bullet}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}-\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)+1\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)=w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}.

Recall that for
d_{0}=1
we have that
T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),\lambda_{\bullet}}
equals

(t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{n})\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}.

From this, we see that
T_{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),\lambda_{\bullet}}-\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{punct},\lambda_{\bullet}}
is equal to

(t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{n})\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}t^{-\mathbf{u}_{k}}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)<w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)\leq w(k)\end{subarray}}t^{\mathbf{u}_{j}-\mathbf{u}_{k}}.

∎

Let us denote by
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\subset\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
the intersection of the punctual locus and associativity locus. Note that if the dimension vector is full, then this agrees with the punctual Hilbert scheme of points, but in general it will be a closed subscheme of the latter. Note that
\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
is cut out of
\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
via a section of
\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{ass}}|_{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}
. We can therefore equip it with a perfect obstruction theory. Note that we are in the situation of
2.4
and so the inclusion

\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})

admits a relative perfect obstruction theory from
\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{punct}}
compatible with the two previously defined perfect obstruction theories. We naturally want to apply
2.3
, so we try to describe the fixed-point locus of
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. We note that for
\underline{d}=(1,1,\dots,1)
we get the punctual Hilbert scheme, which contains the full fixed-point locus. In general, we get the following.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let

I_{r+1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{1}\subset I_{0}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

be a flag of monomial ideals with corresponding flag of partitions

\lambda_{1}\subset\lambda_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\lambda_{r+1}\subset(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}.

Then the corresponding fixed point of
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
factors through
\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
if and only if
for all
k\geq 1
,
\mathbf{u}\in\lambda_{k+1}\backslash\lambda_{k}
and
i=1,\dots,n
we have

\mathbf{u}+e_{i}\notin\lambda_{k+1}.

Proof.

Note first that
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}I_{k}/I_{k+1}=d_{k}
. In particular since
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}I_{0}/I_{1}=1
we must have

I_{1}=\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

so that

\lambda_{1}=\{\mathbf{0}\}.

We note that the corresponding fixed point is in
\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
if and only if

I_{1}\cdot I_{k}\subset I_{k+1}

for all
k
. This means that for all
k\geq 0
and all
\mathbf{u}\notin\lambda_{k}
and
\mathbf{v}\notin\lambda_{1}
we have

\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\notin\lambda_{k+1}.

This statement is trivially true if
\mathbf{u}\notin\lambda_{k+1}
so we can restrict to
\mathbf{u}\in\lambda_{k+1}\backslash\lambda_{k}
. Since
\mathbf{v}\notin\lambda_{1}
if and only if
\mathbf{v}\neq 0
we see that it is sufficient to consider
\mathbf{v}=e_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.
∎

We can now apply
2.3
to get the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\alpha\in A_{\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}),T}^{*}(\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}))
be a local
T
-equivariant Chow cohomology class supported on the nilpotently filtered locus. Then

\int_{[\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}\alpha=\int_{[\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}\frac{\alpha|_{\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}}{e^{T}(\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{punct}}|_{\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})})}.

If
\underline{d}=(1,1,\dots,1)
then

j_{*}\left(\frac{[\operatorname{NHilb}_{0}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}{e^{T}(\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{punct}}|_{\operatorname{NHilb}_{0}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})})}\right)=[\operatorname{NHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}

and in particular for any
\alpha\in A_{T}^{*}(\operatorname{NHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n}))
we get

\int_{[\operatorname{NHilb}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}\alpha=\int_{[\operatorname{NHilb}_{0}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}\frac{\alpha|_{\operatorname{NHilb}_{0}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}}{e^{T}(\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{punct}}|_{\operatorname{NHilb}_{0}^{r+1}(\mathbb{A}^{n})})}.

6.2.
The partial resolution

We construct a partial resolution given as a birational map

\tau:\widetilde{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\to\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})

such that the domain fibers over the flag variety

\mu:\widetilde{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\to\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Using Atiyah-Bott localization on the flag variety this allows us to rewrite virtual integrals
\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
as an iterated residue formula depending on virtual integrals on the fibers of
\mu
. We first define the variety.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\underline{d}=(1,\hat{d})=(1,d_{1},\dots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r+1}
and
n\geq d_{1}+\cdots+d_{r}
. Let
\Psi_{1}:(\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}_{1}
be the universal map of
\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
and let

(\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{1}\supset\mathcal{F}_{2}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{F}_{r+1}=0

be the dualized universal flag on the flag variety
\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. We let
\widetilde{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
be the closed locus of
\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\times\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
where
\Psi_{1}
factors through a (necessarily unique) map

\mathcal{F}_{1}\to\mathcal{V}_{1}

which respects the flag structures. We denote by
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}

the projections to the non-associative Hilbert scheme and the flag variety.

Lemma 6.3
.

The map
\mu:\widetilde{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\to\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is smooth.

Proof.

For each
1\leq k\leq r
let

X_{k}\subset\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{(1,d_{1},\dots,d_{k})}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\times\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

be the pullback of
\widetilde{\operatorname{naNHilb}^{(1,d_{1},\dots,d_{k})}_{0}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
under
1\times q_{k}
where
q_{k}:\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\operatorname{Flag}_{(d_{1},\dots,d_{k})}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Projection on the non-associative Hilbert scheme clearly induce maps

\widetilde{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})=X_{r}\to X_{r-1}\to\cdots\to X_{1}.

On
X_{k}
we have a universal flag

(\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{1}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{F}_{r+1}=0

from
\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and a universal flag

\mathcal{V}_{1}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{V}_{k+1}=0

with universal maps
\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}
such that

\Psi_{1}:(\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}_{1}

factors through
(\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee}\to\mathcal{F}_{1}\to\mathcal{F}_{1}/\mathcal{F}_{k+1}
. If we let
\widetilde{\Psi}_{1}:\mathcal{F}_{1}/\mathcal{F}_{k+1}\to\mathcal{V}_{1}
be the induced map then a proof completely analogous to that of
4.2
then shows that

X_{k+1}\to X_{k}

identifies with the structure map of the Grassmanian bundle

\operatorname{Gr}_{X_{k}}(d_{r},(\operatorname{ker}\widetilde{\Psi}_{1}\oplus\Psi_{2})^{\vee}).

In particular,
X_{k+1}\to X_{k}
is smooth and it will suffice to show that
X_{1}\to\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is smooth. We note that

\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{(1,d_{1})}(\mathbb{A}^{n})=\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{pt}}(d_{1},\mathbb{C}^{n})

with universal bundle
\mathcal{V}_{1}
coinciding with the dual universal bundle of the Grasmannian and universal maps given by
\Psi_{2}=0
and
\Psi_{1}:(\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}_{1}
the universal map of the Grassmannian. We see that
X_{1}\subset\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{pt}}(d_{1},\mathbb{C}^{n})\times\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is now simply the locus where
\Psi_{1}
factors through
\mathcal{F}_{1}/\mathcal{F}_{2}
so that the map

X_{1}\to\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

simply identifies with the structure map of

\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}}(d_{1},\mathcal{F}_{1}/\mathcal{F}_{2})

which is again smooth.
∎

Corollary 6.1
.

The scheme
\widetilde{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
is smooth and projective.

Proof.

Smoothness follows from the above. Projectiveness follows since it is a closed subscheme of a product of projective schemes.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

The map
\tau:\widetilde{\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\to\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
is projective birational.

Proof.

Projectiveness is immediate since it is a morphism of projective schemes. Let
U\subset\operatorname{naNHilb}_{0}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
be the Zariski open subset where
\Psi_{1}
is surjective. Note that it is non-empty, containing for example the Porteous-type points, i.e., monomial ideals where the corresponding partition only contains unit vectors. We construct an inverse of
\tau
on
U
. Explicitly, we define
U\to\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
via the flag dual to

\Psi_{1}:(\mathcal{O}^{n})^{\vee}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}

and the universal property of the flag variety.
∎

Definition 6.2
.

We define

\widetilde{\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n}):=\tau^{-1}\left(\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})\right).

Note that it is equipped with a
T
-equivariant perfect obstruction theory being exhibited as the zero locus of a section of
\tau^{*}\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{ass}}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

We have

\tau_{*}[\widetilde{\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}=[\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}.

In particular, for
\alpha\in A_{T}^{*}(\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{0}(\mathbb{A}^{n}))
we have

\int_{[\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}\alpha=\int_{[\widetilde{\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}\tau^{*}\alpha.

Proof.

This follows by realizing the virtual fundamental classes as refined Euler classes in the sense of Fulton. Since
\tau

Setup 6.1

Let
\underline{d}=(1,\hat{d})=(1,d_{1},\dots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r+1}

d=1+d_{1}+\cdots+d_{r}

W

be the associated
T
-equivariant bundle on
\mathbb{A}^{n}

be the associated tautological bundle on
\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
of rank
m=d\cdot q

We let

be the Chern roots of

and

has Chern roots

\theta_{i}+\upsilon_{j}

and

for a fitting enumeration

of a partition

Proposition 6.2
.

Let

We first note that

for
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
the universal flag on the non-associative nested Hilbert scheme. We apply virtual localization

\int_{[H_{\operatorname{id}}]^{\operatorname{vir}}}P=\sum_{\lambda_{\bullet}\in H_{\operatorname{id}}^{T}}P_{\lambda_{\bullet}}\cdot\frac{e^{T}(\mathcal{E}_{ass,\lambda_{\bullet}})}{e^{T}(T_{\mu^{-1}(\sigma(f_{\bullet})),\lambda_{\bullet}})}.

We can choose an enumeration
\mathbf{u}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d-1}
of
\lambda_{\bullet}
satisfying the properties of
5.3
and such that

\eta_{i}|_{\lambda_{\bullet}}=\mathbf{u}_{i}(\underline{s}).

Note that for

we have that

for

. It follows that

is a polynomial in

We can now obtain a iterated residue formula for virtual tautological integrals on
\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})
. The main input is the following iterated residue formula for integration on the flag variety
\operatorname{Flag}_{\hat{d}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
generalizing
[
14
, Proposition 5.4]
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\hat{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}
and set
k=|\hat{d}|:=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{r}\leq n
.
Let
Q
be a polynomial on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
and let
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
be parameters.

Partition
\{1,\dots,n\}
into blocks

B_{1}=\{1,\dots,d_{1}\},\;B_{2}=\{d_{1}+1,\dots,d_{1}+d_{2}\},\dots,B_{r}=\{k-d_{r}+1,\dots,k\},\;B_{r+1}=\{k+1,\dots,n\},

and define a weight function

w:\{1,\dots,n\}\to\{1,\dots,r+1\},\qquad w(i)=p\ \text{if and only if }i\in B_{p}.

Then

(1)

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}/(S_{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{d_{r}}\times S_{n-k})}\frac{Q\bigl(s_{\sigma(1)},\dots,s_{\sigma(k)}\bigr)}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n\\
w(i)>w(j)\end{subarray}}\bigl(s_{\sigma(i)}-s_{\sigma(j)}\bigr)}=\underset{\mathbf{z}=\infty}{\operatorname{Res}}\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq m\neq\ell\leq k}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})\,Q(\mathbf{z})\,dz_{1}\cdots dz_{k}}{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell<m\leq k\\
w(\ell)<w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}(s_{i}-z_{\ell})}.

Proof.

Consider the meromorphic
k
-form on
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{k}

\omega_{k}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq m\neq\ell\leq k}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})\,Q(\mathbf{z})\,dz_{1}\cdots dz_{k}}{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell<m\leq k\\
w(\ell)<w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}(s_{i}-z_{\ell})},\qquad\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{k}).

We will compute the iterated residue

\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\omega_{k}:=\operatorname{Res}_{z_{1}=\infty}\cdots\operatorname{Res}_{z_{k}=\infty}\omega_{k}

by successively applying the Residue Theorem on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in the variables

z_{k},z_{k-1},\dots,z_{1}
, as in
[
11
]
.

Step 1: Isolating the dependence on
z_{k}
.

Write the full Vandermonde product as

\prod_{1\leq m\neq\ell\leq k}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})=\Bigl(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\neq\ell\leq k-1\end{subarray}}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{\ell<k}(z_{k}-z_{\ell})(z_{\ell}-z_{k})\Bigr).

Among the cross-block factors in the denominator

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell<m\leq k\\
w(\ell)<w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{m}-z_{\ell}),

the only ones involving
z_{k}
are those with
m=k
and
\ell<k
,
namely

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell<k\\
w(\ell)<w(k)\end{subarray}}(z_{k}-z_{\ell}).

Thus, we can factor
\omega_{k}
as

\omega_{k}=F_{k-1}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\;\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{\ell<k}(z_{k}-z_{\ell})(z_{\ell}-z_{k})}{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell<k\\
w(\ell)<w(k)\end{subarray}}(z_{k}-z_{\ell})\prod_{i=1}^{n}(s_{i}-z_{k})}\,dz_{k},

where
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{k-1})
and

F_{k-1}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq m\neq\ell\leq k-1}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})\,Q(\mathbf{z})\,dz_{1}\cdots dz_{k-1}}{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell<m\leq k-1\\
w(\ell)<w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(s_{i}-z_{\ell})}.

For each
\ell<k
we have:

•

If
w(\ell)<w(k)
, then a denominator factor
(z_{k}-z_{\ell})
cancels one
factor in
(z_{k}-z_{\ell})(z_{\ell}-z_{k})
, leaving
(z_{\ell}-z_{k})
.

•

If
w(\ell)=w(k)
, there is no denominator factor, and we simply keep

(z_{k}-z_{\ell})(z_{\ell}-z_{k})=-(z_{k}-z_{\ell})^{2}
.

Hence, as a function of
z_{k}
,

\omega_{k}=\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\;\frac{R_{k}(z_{k};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(s_{i}-z_{k})}\,dz_{k},

where
\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})
is independent of
z_{k}
and
R_{k}(z_{k};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})
is a polynomial
in
z_{k}
. In particular, the only poles in the
z_{k}
variable are at

z_{k}=s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
.

Step 2: Residue in
z_{k}
.

By the Residue Theorem on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,

\operatorname{Res}_{z_{k}=\infty}\omega_{k}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{Res}_{z_{k}=s_{j}}\omega_{k}.

For a simple pole in
s_{j}-z_{k}
, we have

\operatorname{Res}_{z_{k}=s_{j}}\frac{R_{k}(z_{k};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(s_{i}-z_{k})}\,dz_{k}=-\frac{R_{k}(s_{j};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})}{\prod_{i\neq j}(s_{i}-s_{j})}.

Thus, the minus sign from the Residue Theorem cancels this minus sign, giving

\operatorname{Res}_{z_{k}=\infty}\omega_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\,\frac{R_{k}(s_{j};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})}{\prod_{i\neq j}(s_{i}-s_{j})}.

Now track the factors involving
z_{k}
more explicitly. As observed above,
for each
\ell<k
we obtain after substituting
z_{k}=s_{j}
at least one factor

(z_{\ell}-s_{j})
in the numerator. On the other hand, in
F_{k-1}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})
we still
have the denominator

\prod_{i=1}^{n}(s_{i}-z_{\ell}),\qquad\ell=1,\dots,k-1.

Hence for each
\ell<k
the factor
(z_{\ell}-s_{j})
cancels the factor

(s_{j}-z_{\ell})
in this product, leaving

\prod_{i\neq j}(s_{i}-z_{\ell})

in the denominator and at most one
(z_{\ell}-s_{j})
factor remaining in the
numerator. In particular, after the
z_{k}
-residue, the pole at
z_{\ell}=s_{j}

disappears for all
\ell<k
.

Collecting all factors, we obtain (up to an overall sign depending only on
k
)

(2)

\operatorname{Res}_{z_{k}=\infty}\omega_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq m\neq\ell\leq k-1}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})\,Q(z_{1},\dots,z_{k-1},s_{j})\,\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(z_{\ell}-s_{j})\,dz_{1}\cdots dz_{k-1}}{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell<m\leq k-1\\
w(\ell)<w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\prod_{i\neq j}(s_{i}-z_{\ell})\prod_{i\neq j}(s_{i}-s_{j})}.

This has the same structure as the original integrand, but with one fewer
variable and with
j
excluded from the set of indices appearing in the
denominators.

Step 3: Iteration.

We now repeat the same procedure with
z_{k-1}
, then
z_{k-2}
, and so on.

Inductively, after having taken residues in
z_{k},\dots,z_{m+1}
we obtain a sum
over ordered
(k-m)
-tuples of distinct indices
(j_{m+1},\dots,j_{k})
, and for
each such choice an integrand in the remaining variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{m}
of the
same form:

\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq a\neq b\leq m}(z_{a}-z_{b})\,Q(\dots)\,dz_{1}\cdots dz_{m}}{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell<m\leq m\\
w(\ell)<w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}\prod_{i\in I_{m}}(s_{i}-z_{\ell})}\cdot(\text{constant depending on }s_{j_{m+1}},\dots,s_{j_{k}}),

where
I_{m}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
is the set of indices not yet used. In
particular, the only poles in
z_{m}
are at
z_{m}=s_{i}
with
i\in I_{m}
.

The same argument as in Step 2 shows that:

•

At the
z_{m}
step, we pick an index
j_{m}\in I_{m}
and obtain a factor

1/\prod_{i\in I_{m}\setminus\{j_{m}\}}(s_{i}-s_{j_{m}})
.

•

The pole at
z_{\ell}=s_{j_{m}}
disappears for every
\ell<m
; hence

j_{m}
cannot reappear at later steps. Thus, the indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{k}

are all distinct.

•

Tracking the cross-block factors, one checks that for the chosen
index
j_{m}
with weight
w(j_{m})
, the only factors that survive in the
final denominator are those with
w(i)>w(j_{m})
. The factors with

w(i)\leq w(j_{m})
are cancelled against factors arising from the
Vandermonde numerator at earlier steps.

Proceeding inductively down to
z_{1}
, we thus obtain a sum over ordered

k
-tuples of distinct indices
(j_{1},\dots,j_{k})
, or equivalently over
cosets
\sigma\in S_{n}/(S_{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{d_{r}}\times S_{n-k})
,
of terms of the form

\frac{Q(s_{j_{1}},\dots,s_{j_{k}})}{\displaystyle\prod_{m=1}^{k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\\
w(i)>w(j_{m})\end{subarray}}(s_{i}-s_{j_{m}})}=\frac{Q\bigl(s_{\sigma(1)},\dots,s_{\sigma(k)}\bigr)}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n\\
w(i)>w(j)\end{subarray}}(s_{\sigma(i)}-s_{\sigma(j)})}.

Step 4: signs.

At each step there is
a sign coming from exchanging factors
(z_{m}-z_{\ell})
with
(z_{\ell}-z_{m})
, and
a global sign from expanding the Vandermonde product

\prod_{1\leq m\neq\ell\leq k}(z_{m}-z_{\ell})
. The total sign is

(-1)^{\sum_{p}\binom{d_{p}}{2}}
both from these exchanges and from the
Vandermonde, so these sign contributions cancel in the final expression.

Thus, the iterated residue
\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\omega_{k}
is exactly the
left-hand side of (
1
), which proves the identity.
∎

6.5.
Residue vanishing

The above formula shows that the virtual integral can be exhibited as a sum of iterated residues, i.e., we have that

In this section we prove that most of these residues vanish. In fact, we prove that the only non-vanishing residue is the one related to the Porteous point

Proposition 6.4
.

[

Let

and assumme that

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{q}\in A_{T}^{1}(\operatorname{pt})
and let

P=P(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{q},\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d-1})

be a bisymmetric polynomial as in
6.1
. Then

\int_{[\operatorname{NHilb}^{\underline{d}}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}P

can be calculated via the iterated residue formula

\underset{\underline{z}=\infty}{\operatorname{Res}}\frac{P(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{q},\underline{z})\prod_{1\leq m\neq l\leq d-1}(z_{m}-z_{l})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j,k\leq d-1\\
i<k\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\prod}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq d-1\\
w(k),w(j)\leq w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{i}+z_{j}+z_{k}-z_{m})d\underline{z}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq l<m\leq d-1\\
w(l)<w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{m}-z_{l})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{l=1}^{d-1}(s_{i}-z_{l})\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\widetilde{\prod}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)<w(k)\end{subarray}}(z_{i}+z_{j}-z_{k})}

Proof.

If
n\geq d-1
this is simply the content of
corollary

6.6
. If not, we can find
N\geq d-1
. Then

\int_{[\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{n})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}P=\int_{[\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{N})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}e^{T}(\mathcal{E}_{n,0})\cdot P.

We note that

e^{T}(\mathcal{E}_{n,0})=\prod_{i=n+1}^{N}\prod_{l=1}^{d-1}(s_{i}-\eta_{l}).

If we apply
corollary

6.6
to calculate
\int_{[\operatorname{NHilb}_{\operatorname{nil-fil}}^{\underline{d}}(\mathbb{A}^{N})]^{\operatorname{vir}}}P^{\prime}
for
P^{\prime}=e^{T}(\mathcal{E}_{n,0})\cdot\alpha
we get

\underset{\underline{z}=\infty}{\operatorname{Res}}\frac{P^{\prime}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{q},\underline{z})\prod_{1\leq m\neq l\leq d-1}(z_{m}-z_{l})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j,k\leq d-1\\
i<k\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\prod}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq d-1\\
w(k),w(j)\leq w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{i}+z_{j}+z_{k}-z_{m})d\underline{z}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq l<m\leq d-1\\
w(l)<w(m)\end{subarray}}(z_{m}-z_{l})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{l=1}^{d-1}(s_{i}-z_{l})\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d-1}\widetilde{\prod}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq d-1\\
w(j)<w(k)\end{subarray}}(z_{i}+z_{j}-z_{k})}

but

P^{\prime}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{q},\underline{z})=P(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{q},\underline{z})\cdot\prod_{i=n+1}^{N}\prod_{l=1}^{d-1}(s_{i}-z_{l})

and we get the cancellation

\frac{\prod_{i=n+1}^{N}\prod_{l=1}^{d-1}(s_{i}-z_{l})}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{l=1}^{d-1}(s_{i}-z_{l})}=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{l=1}^{d-1}(s_{i}-z_{l})}

giving us the desired formula.
∎

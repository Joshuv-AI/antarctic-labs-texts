1.
Introduction

This paper deals with the semi-stability of radial solutions
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
of

(P)

-\Delta u=|x|^{\alpha}{f(u)}\qquad\mbox{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\},

where
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
and
B_{1}
the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with measure
\omega_{N}/N
for
N>1
. In the whole paper we assume
\alpha>-2
, and
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. We deal with the regularity of semi-stable radially symmetric energy solutions
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
of (
P
). Denoting by
r:=|x|
standard arguments show that these radial solutions
u=u(|x|)\equiv u(r)
are continuous in
r\in(0,1]
and can be viewed as classical solutions. We denote as usual
u_{r}:=\frac{<x,\nabla u>}{|x|}
and constants by capital letters
C,K,C^{\prime},K^{\prime}\dots
that may vary throughout the discussion and computations, with subscripts that determine the dependence of the constants on other parameters.

The equation (
P
) corresponds to the Euler-Lagrange equation of the energy functional

(1.1)

E(u):=\int_{B_{1}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}-|x|^{\alpha}F(u)\right)dx,

where
F(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)ds
.
Since
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
u
is continuous, then
f^{\prime}(u)
is continuous. It is meaningful to call a solution
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
semi-stable if the second variation of the energy is nonnegative definite, i.e.,

(1.2)

d^{2}E(\varphi,\varphi)=\int_{B_{1}}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-|x|^{\alpha}f^{\prime}(u)\varphi^{2}\right)dx\geq 0,

for all
\varphi\in C^{1}(B_{1})
with compact support in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
.

Equation (
P
) can be regarded as a generalized version of the so called Hardy-Henon equation which corresponds to power-type nonlinearity
f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u
. That equation is in particular called the Lane-Emden equation in the autonomous case
\alpha=0
. Another nomenclature has been used for
f(u)=e^{u}
, the Henon-Gelfand problem.

The study of this problem is preceded by the study of the so called Henon-equation whose origins goes back to the celebrated work of S. Chandrasekhar
[
3
, Chapter IV, pp. 87-88]
on the stellar structure in polytropic equilibrium in astrophysics.

The pionnering contribution in the autonomous case
\alpha=0
is due to
[
11
]
where the authors prove that for power-type nonlinearity
f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u
and posed in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there is no positive solution for
p\in(1,p_{S}(N))
, where
p_{S}(N):=2^{*}-1=\frac{N+2}{N-2}
if
N\leq 3
and
p_{S}=+\infty
if
N\leq 2
is the classical critical Sobolev exponent.

For
p=p_{S}(N)
, the same equation is known to have (up to translation and rescaling) a unique positive solution, which is radial and explicit (see
[
2
]
).

Let now
p_{JL}(N)>p_{S}(N)
denote the so called Joseph-Lundgren exponent:

p_{JL}(N)=\left\{\begin{aligned} +\infty&\quad\text{if $N\leq 10$},\\
\frac{(N-2)^{2}-{4N}+8\sqrt{N-1}}{(N-2)(N-10)}&\quad\text{if $N\geq 11$}.\end{aligned}\right.

This exponent can be characterized as follows: for
p\geq p_{S}(n)
, the explicit function
u_{s}(x)=C_{p,N}|x|^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}
for an appropiate constant
C_{p,N}
depending only on
p
and
N
, is a singular solution of the Lane-Emden equation, which is stable if and only if
p\geq p_{JL}(N)
.
It was proved in
[
9
]
that Lane-Emden equation has no nontrivial finite Morse index solution whenever
1<p<p_{JL}(N)
,
p\neq p_{S}(N)
. Through the application of some blow-up analysis technique, such Liouville-type theorems imply interior regularity for solutions of a large class of semilinear elliptic equations: they are known to be equivalent to universal estimates for solutions of

(1.3)

-Lu=f(x,u,\nabla u)\quad\text{in $\Omega$,}

where
L
is a uniformly elliptic operator with smooth coefficients, the nonlinearity
f
scales like
|u|^{p-1}u
for large values of
u
, and
\Omega
is an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For precise statements, see the work
[
16
]
in the subcritical setting, as well as its adaptation to the supercritical case by
[
6
]
.

For the Henon-Hardy equation (i.e.
\alpha\not=0
,
f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u
), there are also an extensive literature. In
[
5
]
, the authors show the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Dancer, Du, Guo
[
5
]
). Let
u
be a stable solution of the Henon-Hardy equation with
\alpha>-2
,
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and one of the following hypothesis is true:
2\leq N\leq 10+4\alpha
and
p>1
; or
N>10+4\alpha
and
1<p<p_{JL}(N,\alpha)
; where we have defined

p_{JL}(N,\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}+\infty&\text{if }2\leq N\leq 10+4\alpha,\\
\frac{(N-2)^{2}-2(\alpha+2)(\alpha+N)+2\sqrt{(\alpha+2)^{3}(\alpha+2N-2)}}{(N-2)(N-4\alpha-10)}&\quad\text{if $N>10+4\alpha$}.\end{array}\right.

Then
u\equiv 0
. On the contrary, for
p\geq p_{JL}(N,\alpha)
the equation admits a family of radially symmetric positive stable solutions.

These results were later generalized in
[
18
]
for sign-changing solutions where the authors also proved that assuming
0\in\Omega
, no weak solution exists for
\alpha\leq-2
. As they remark in their proof, the result remains valid for any positive, convex and nondecreasing nonlinearity
f
. This indicates that the case
\alpha\leq-2
carries additional pathological behavior, highlighting the necessity of avoiding that values for
\alpha
. For
\alpha>-2
,
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
they also prove the following two results:

i)

If
2\leq N<10+4\alpha
, there is no weak stable solution of the Gelfand-Henon equation.

ii)

If
2<N<10+4\alpha^{-}
where
\alpha^{-}=\min\{\alpha,0\}
, then any weak solution of the Gelfand-Henon equation has infinite Morse index.

Remark 1.2
.

The dimension range
2\leq N<10+4\alpha
is optimal, since for
N\geq 10+4\alpha

u(x)=-(2+\alpha)\log|x|+\log[(2+\alpha)(N-2)],

is a radial stable weak solution to the Gelfand-Henon equation.

For analogous recent results in the fractional/non local setting we refer to
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
and even with a Hardy potential term
|x|^{-2s}u
on the right-hand side
[
15
]
or concerning systems
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
and references therein.

In our case, we are mainly concerned with the regularity of semi-stable radial solutions of the general Henon-Hardy equation, although we think that our results can be further developed in several directions involving extensions of what is known in the autonomous case. For instance, it would be interesting to investigate the existence and regularity of a critical solution of the equation
-\Delta u=\lambda|x|^{\alpha}f(u)
with the appropriate set of hypothesis over
\alpha
and
f
in the spirit of
[
1
]
. The content is developed through a series of lemmas and propositions, arriving at the end to the proof of the main theorem stated at the beginning of the next section.

2.
Statement of the main result and steps of the proof

We directly proceed to state the main result of the paper: a classification of the behavior of semi-stable radial solutions of (
P
) depending on the dimension.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
\alpha>-2
and
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
be a
semi-stable radial solution of (
P
). Then there
exists a constant
C_{\alpha,N}
depending only on
\alpha
and
N
such that:

i)

If
N<10+4\alpha
, then
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq C_{\alpha,N}\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{1/2}})}
.

ii)

If
N=10+4\alpha
, then
|u(r)|\leq C_{\alpha,10+4\alpha}\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{1/2}})}\left(|\log r|+1\right),\ \ \forall r\in(0,1]
.

iii)

If
N>10+4\alpha
, then
\displaystyle{|u(r)|\leq C_{\alpha,N}\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{1/2}})}\,r^{\gamma(N,\alpha)}\,,\ \ \forall r\in(0,1]}
.

where we have defined the exponent
\,\gamma(N,\alpha):=2-N/2+\alpha/2+\sqrt{(\alpha+2)(\alpha+2N-2)}/2
.

Remark 2.2
.

Observe that
\gamma(N,\alpha)=0
if and only if
N=10+4\alpha
and
\gamma(N,\alpha)<0
if and only if
N>10+4\alpha
so that the pointwise estimates
i)
and
ii)
do not give rise to boundedness of
u
.

We shall divide the proof in several lemmas and propositions.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
\alpha>-2
and
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
be a
semi-stable radial solution of (
P
). Let
v\in C^{0,1}(0,1]
such that
v(1)=0
. Then

(2.1)

\int_{r_{0}}^{1}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)\left(v^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\alpha\frac{v^{\prime}(t)v(t)}{t}+\left(1-N-\alpha\frac{N}{2}\right)\frac{v(t)^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)dt\geq 0,

for every
r_{0}\in(0,1)
.

Proof.
Assume, as a first step, that
v\in C^{\infty}(0,1)
has compact support and
v\equiv 0
in
(0,r_{0}]
(later we will prove it for any
v\in C^{0,1}(0,1]
with
v(1)=0
). Differentiating (
P
) with respect to
r
we obtain

(-\Delta u)_{r}=-\Delta u_{r}+\frac{N-1}{r^{2}}u_{r}=\alpha r^{\alpha-1}f(u)+r^{\alpha}f^{\prime}(u)u_{r}.

Multiplying by the radial function
u_{r}v^{2}
and integrating by parts yields

\int_{B_{1}}\left(\nabla u_{r}\nabla(u_{r}v^{2})+\frac{N-1}{r^{2}}u_{r}^{2}v^{2}\right)dx=\int_{B_{1}}\left(\alpha r^{\alpha-1}f(u)+r^{\alpha}f^{\prime}(u)u_{r}\right)u_{r}v^{2}dx.

Therefore

(2.2)

\int_{B_{1}}\left(v^{2}|\nabla u_{r}|^{2}+2u_{r}v\nabla u_{r}\nabla v+\frac{N-1}{r^{2}}u_{r}^{2}v^{2}\right)dx=\int_{B_{1}}\left(\alpha r^{\alpha-1}f(u)u_{r}v^{2}+r^{\alpha}f^{\prime}(u)u_{r}^{2}v^{2}\right)dx.

On the other hand, since
u
is stable, we can consider the radial function
u_{r}v
(which is a
C^{1}(B_{1})
function with compact
support in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
) obtaining

(2.3)

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla(u_{r}v)|^{2}dx\geq\int_{B_{1}}r^{\alpha}f^{\prime}(u)(u_{r}v)^{2}dx.

Subtracting (
2.2
) from (
2.3
) we can assert that

(2.4)

\int_{B_{1}}\left(u_{r}^{2}v_{r}^{2}-\frac{N-1}{r^{2}}u_{r}^{2}v^{2}\right)\geq-\alpha\int_{B_{1}}r^{\alpha-1}f(u)u_{r}v^{2}dx.

Let us expand this last term:

\int_{B_{1}}r^{\alpha-1}f(u)u_{r}v^{2}dx=\int_{B_{1}}(-\Delta u)\frac{u_{r}v^{2}}{r}dx=\omega_{N}\int_{r_{0}}^{1}t^{N-1}\left(-u_{rr}-\frac{N-1}{t}u_{r}\right)\frac{u_{r}v^{2}}{t}dt=

-\frac{\omega_{N}}{2}\int_{r_{0}}^{1}\left(t^{2N-2}u_{r}^{2}\right)^{\prime}t^{-N}v^{2}dt=\frac{\omega_{N}}{2}\int_{r_{0}}^{1}t^{2N-2}u_{r}^{2}(t^{-N}v^{2})^{\prime}dt=

\omega_{N}\int_{r_{0}}^{1}t^{N-1}\left(\frac{-N}{2}\frac{u_{r}^{2}v^{2}}{t^{2}}+\frac{u_{r}^{2}vv^{\prime}}{t}\right)dt.

Combining this and (
2.4
) we obtain

\omega_{N}\int_{r_{0}}^{1}t^{N-1}\left(u_{r}^{2}v^{\prime 2}-\frac{N-1}{t^{2}}u_{r}^{2}v^{2}\right)dt\geq-\alpha\ \omega_{N}\int_{r_{0}}^{1}t^{N-1}\left(\frac{-N}{2}\frac{u_{r}^{2}v^{2}}{t^{2}}+\frac{u_{r}^{2}vv^{\prime}}{t}\right)dt,

which is the desired conclusion.

In fact, by standard density arguments, the above formula is also true if we consider a
C^{0,1}(0,1]
function vanishing in
(0,r_{0}]\cup\{1\}
.

Now, for the sake of clarity of the exposition let us denote

(2.5)

I(a,b;v):=\int_{a}^{b}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)\left(v^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\alpha\frac{v^{\prime}(t)v(t)}{t}+\left(1-N-\alpha\frac{N}{2}\right)\frac{v(t)^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)dt.

For an arbitrary
v\in C^{0,1}(0,1]
vanishing at
t=1
, we define a radial truncated function

(2.6)

\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }0<t<\varepsilon,\\
\frac{v(r_{0})}{r_{0}-\varepsilon}(t-\varepsilon)&\text{if }\varepsilon\leq t\leq r_{0},\\
v(t)&\text{if }r_{0}<t\leq 1,\end{cases}

for any
0<\varepsilon<r_{0}
. Now, applying the first step of the lemma to the function
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}
we have that
I(\varepsilon,1;\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\geq 0
and thus

\displaystyle I(r_{0},1;v)

\displaystyle\geq-I(\varepsilon,r_{0};\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{v(r_{0})}{r_{0}-\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{r_{0}}t^{N-1}u_{r}(t)^{2}\left[1+\alpha\frac{t-\varepsilon}{t}+\left(1-N-\alpha\frac{N}{2}\right)\frac{(t-\varepsilon)^{2}}{t^{2}}\right]dt.

Observe that since
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
the whole right hand side is uniformly bounded for
\epsilon\in(0,r_{0})
. Therefore

\displaystyle I(r_{0},1;v)\geq-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}I(\varepsilon,r_{0};\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})=-\left(\frac{v(r_{0})}{r_{0}}\right)^{2}(2+\alpha)\left(1-\frac{N}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{r_{0}}t^{N-1}u_{r}(t)^{2}dt\geq 0,

as we claimed. ∎

Remark 2.4
.

In the last step, it is shown that the assumption
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
is essential. Indeed, it is well known (see for instance
[
1
]
), that there exists semi-stable weak solutions to
-\Delta u=C_{N,q}(1+u)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}
in
B_{1}
of the form
u(x)=|x|^{q}-1
such that
u\notin H^{1}(B_{1})
in the range
q\in\left(-\frac{N}{2}+2-\sqrt{N-1},\;-\frac{N}{2}+1\right]
,
N\geq 3
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
\alpha>-2
and
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
be a non constant semi-stable radial solution of (
P
). Then
u_{r}\not=0
in
(0,1]
.

Proof.
Assume by contradiction that there exists
r_{1}\in(0,1]

such that
u_{r}(r_{1})=0
. From
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
,
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and the radial symmetry we know that
u(r)\in C^{3}((0,1])
. Thus, in particular there is a constant
C_{r_{1}}>0
such that
|u_{r}(r)|\leq C_{r_{1}}|r-r_{1}|
for any
r\in[r_{1}/2,r_{1}]
.
On the other hand, we take the following test function

v(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{t}{r_{1}-\varepsilon}&\text{if }0<t<r_{1}-\varepsilon,\\
\frac{r_{1}-t}{\varepsilon}&\text{if }r_{1}-\varepsilon\leq t\leq r_{1},\\
0&\text{if }r_{1}<t\leq 1,\end{cases}

with
0<\varepsilon<r_{1}/2
. Applying Lemma
2.3
we have that
-I(r_{0},r_{1}-\varepsilon;v)\leq I(r_{1}-\varepsilon,r_{1};v)
for any
0<r_{0}<r_{1}-\varepsilon
. Therefore

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(r_{1}-\varepsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}-\varepsilon}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)(2+\alpha)\left(1-\frac{N}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r_{1}-\epsilon}^{r_{1}}\frac{t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left[1-\alpha\frac{(r_{1}-t)}{t}+\left(1-N-\alpha\frac{N}{2}\right)\frac{\left(r_{1}-t\right)^{2}}{t^{2}}\right]dt

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{r_{1}-\epsilon}^{r_{1}}\frac{u_{r}^{2}(t)}{\varepsilon^{2}}dt\leq KC_{r_{1}}^{\,2}\int_{r_{1}-\epsilon}^{r_{1}}\frac{(t-r_{1})^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}dt=\frac{KC_{r_{1}}^{2}}{3}\,\varepsilon.

Taking limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have that

-\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}}(2+\alpha)\left(1-\frac{N}{2}\right)\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)dt\leq 0,

that implies that
u_{r}\equiv 0
in
[r_{0},r_{1}]
if
N>2
. By the uniqueness of the corresponding Cauchy problem,
u_{r}\equiv 0
in
(0,1]
which gives the contradiction for
N>2
. Finally, if
N=2
we change the test function by

v(t)=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{t}{r_{1}-\varepsilon}\right)^{\beta}&\text{if }0<t<r_{1}-\varepsilon,\\
\frac{r_{1}-t}{\varepsilon}&\text{if }r_{1}-\varepsilon\leq t\leq r_{1},\\
0&\text{if }r_{1}<t\leq 1,\end{cases}

with
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
to be chosen later. With similar arguments we can conclude

-\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2\beta}}(\beta-1)\left(\beta+\alpha+1\right)\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}t^{N-1+2\beta-2}u_{r}^{2}(t)dt\leq 0.

Chosing any
\beta\in(-1-\alpha,1)
, we obtain
u_{r}\equiv 0
in
[r_{0},r_{1}]
which is again a contradiction.∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
\alpha>-2
and
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
be a non constant semi-stable radial solution of (
P
). Then

\int_{r/2}^{r}u_{r}^{2}\,dt\leq K_{\alpha,N}||\nabla u||^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{1/2}})}\,r^{3-N+\alpha+\sqrt{(2+\alpha)(2N-2+\alpha)}},

for any
r\in(0,1]
.

Proof.
We first consider the case
0<r\leq 1/2
and define

v(t)=\begin{cases}r^{s-1}t&\text{if }0<t<r,\\
t^{s}&\text{if }r\leq t\leq 1/2,\\
2^{1-s}(1-t)&\text{if }1/2<t\leq 1\end{cases}

with
s\in\mathbb{R}
to be determined. By Lemma
2.3
applied for
r_{0}=r/2
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq r^{2s-2}

\displaystyle\int_{r/2}^{r}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)\,(2+\alpha)\left(1-\frac{N}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle+\int_{r}^{1/2}t^{N-3+2s}u_{r}^{2}(t)\left(s^{2}+\alpha s+1-N-\alpha\frac{N}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle+\int_{1/2}^{1}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)\,2^{2-2s}\left[1-\alpha\frac{1-t}{t}+\left(1-N-\alpha\frac{N}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1-t}{t}\right)^{2}\right]dt.

Choosing
s_{\alpha}=-\alpha/2-\sqrt{(2+\alpha)(2N-2+\alpha)}/2
, the second term is indeed zero. Therefore, we deduce

\displaystyle-r^{2s_{\alpha}-2}

\displaystyle\int_{r/2}^{r}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)(2+\alpha)\left(1-\frac{N}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1/2}^{1}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)\,2^{2-2s_{\alpha}}\left[1-\alpha\frac{1-t}{t}+\left(1-N-\alpha\frac{N}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1-t}{t}\right)^{2}\right]dt.

As a result

\displaystyle-r^{2s_{\alpha}-2}\int_{r/2}^{r}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)(2+\alpha)\left(1-\frac{N}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq r^{2s_{\alpha}-2}\int_{r/2}^{r}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)(2+\alpha)\left(1-\frac{N}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1/2}^{1}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)\,2^{2-2s_{\alpha}}\left[1-\alpha\frac{1-t}{t}+\left(1-N-\alpha\frac{N}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1-t}{t}\right)^{2}\right]dt

\displaystyle\leq C\,\int_{1/2}^{1}t^{N-1}u_{r}^{2}(t)dt,

Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle-\,\frac{(2+\alpha)\left(1-\frac{N}{2}\right)}{2^{N-1}}r^{2s_{\alpha}-3+N}\int_{r/2}^{r}

\displaystyle u_{r}^{2}(t)dt\leq\frac{C}{\omega_{N}}||\nabla u||^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{1/2}})}

which is the desired conclusion if
N>2
. If
N=2
, we change the test function by

v(t)=\begin{cases}r^{-1-\alpha-\beta}t^{\beta}&\text{if }0<t<r,\\
t^{-1-\alpha}&\text{if }r\leq t\leq 1/2,\\
2^{2+\alpha}(1-t)&\text{if }1/2<t\leq 1.\end{cases}

with
\beta\in(-1-\alpha,1)
as in the previous proposition. We have

\displaystyle-{r^{-2-2\alpha-2\beta}}\int_{r/2}^{r}

\displaystyle t^{-1+2\beta}\,u_{r}^{2}(t)(\beta-1)(\beta+\alpha+1)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1/2}^{1}t\,u_{r}^{2}(t)\,2^{4+2\alpha}\left[1-\alpha\frac{1-t}{t}-\left(1+\alpha\right)\left(\frac{1-t}{t}\right)^{2}\right]dt,

that gives the desired conclusion once the same argument as above is employed for the last integral.

On the other hand, let us call
R_{\alpha,N}
a constant such that

for all

. Hence, applying the above conclusion for

\displaystyle\int_{r/2}^{r}u_{r}^{2}(t)dt

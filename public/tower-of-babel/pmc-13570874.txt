Real-World Effectiveness of Ceftazidime–Avibactam for Multidrug-Resistant Gram-Negative Pneumonia, Time-Stratified Risk Factors for Short- and Long-Term Mortality, and Inpatient Costs: A Multicenter Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Chao Liu, Wei Li, Enjing Zhang, Lei Hu, Yong Li, Ying Chen, Junwei Wang, Yinghao He, Dong Xu, Zhongju Chen, Dong Liu, Yan He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionMultidrug-resistant (MDR) Gram-negative pneumonia is associated with high mortality and substantial healthcare resource utilization. Ceftazidime–avibactam (CAZ-AVI) is an important option, yet real-world experience in Chinese pneumonia cohorts is limited and the associated inpatient costs are seldom reported. This study aimed to describe the clinical response to CAZ-AVI in routine practice, to identify the factors associated with 14-day and with 30-day all-cause mortality separately so as to examine how they differ over time, and to characterize inpatient costs.MethodsWe retrospectively studied 693 adults with MDR pneumonia who received at least 3 days of CAZ-AVI at eight hospitals in Hubei, China, between August 2022 and August 2025. Factors associated with 14-day and 30-day all-cause mortality were examined by univariable and multivariable logistic regression, with LASSO-penalized regression and cross-validation as a sensitivity analysis. Acute kidney injury (AKI) was recorded as a safety observation, and pneumonia-related inpatient costs were summarized descriptively. The study is reported in line with Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE).ResultsOverall, 480 patients (69.3%) had a favorable response (cure, 356 [51.4%]; improvement, 124 [17.9%]). A higher Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) score was independently associated with mortality at both 14 days (adjusted OR 1.20; 95% CI 1.14–1.26) and 30 days (1.27; 1.18–1.37). At 14 days, death was further associated with bacteremia (4.76; 2.39–9.66), carbapenem-resistant organisms (CRO; 5.93; 2.60–13.60), and septic shock (3.30; 1.32–8.39). At 30 days, the associated factors were Klebsiella pneumoniae (15.19; 3.25–78.94; the wide confidence interval warrants caution) and septic shock (5.74; 1.34–24.97), while a later rise in serum albumin accompanied lower mortality (0.89; 0.82–0.95). AKI occurred in 15 of 693 patients (2.2%). The median total inpatient cost was USD 14,982 (IQR 8462–25,136), of which CAZ-AVI accounted for a median of USD 3901 (IQR 2069–6159).ConclusionIn routine practice, CAZ-AVI for MDR pneumonia was associated with a favorable response, and the factors linked to early death differed from those linked to later death. These associations are hypothesis-generating; together with the cost description, they may help inform time-stratified management and the controlled studies needed to confirm these findings.

Introduction

Multidrug-resistant (MDR) Gram-negative bacteria are among the principal causes of severe pneumonia and are associated with high mortality and healthcare costs [1–7]. Once resistance extends to carbapenems, treatment often relies on salvage agents such as polymyxins and tigecycline. These drugs have appreciable toxicity—nephrotoxicity in particular—and their efficacy is uncertain, so patients with MDR pneumonia remain exposed to poor outcomes and a substantial economic burden [28–12].

Ceftazidime–avibactam (CAZ-AVI), a β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitor combination, is active against many Gram-negative organisms, including Enterobacterales and Pseudomonas aeruginosa [13–16]. Recently, emerging evidence has highlighted its potential in novel combination therapies, such as phage-CaEDTA-CAZ-AVI triple therapy for P. aeruginosa and combination regimens for colistin-resistant or carbapenemase-producing Klebsiella pneumoniae [17–20]. In the phase III REPROVE trial CAZ-AVI proved non-inferior to meropenem in hospital-acquired and ventilator-associated pneumonia, achieving clinical cure in roughly 68% of patients [2122], and is currently recommended by Chinese clinical practice guidelines as a primary therapeutic option against carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (CRE) and difficult-to-treat P. aeruginosa [23–26].

For Chinese practice, though, important gaps persist. Registration trials tend to exclude the sickest patients—for instance, those with an Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) score above 30 [212728]—and most real-world reports stop at clinical outcomes, providing little insight into the factors associated with early versus later death or the inpatient costs incurred [2930]. Against this background we assembled a large, multicenter, real-world cohort of patients given CAZ-AVI for pneumonia in China, with three aims: to describe the clinical response; to identify, separately, the factors independently associated with 14-day and with 30-day all-cause mortality, and thereby examine how they differ over time; and to describe the corresponding inpatient costs. Throughout, we treat the findings as associative and hypothesis-generating rather than causal.

Methods

Study Design and Population

This multicenter retrospective cohort study consecutively enrolled adults with MDR pneumonia who were treated with CAZ-AVI at eight hospitals in Hubei Province, China, between August 2022 and August 2025. MDR was defined as acquired non-susceptibility to at least one agent in three or more antimicrobial categories [31]. Patients were excluded if the CAZ-AVI course lasted fewer than 3 days or if core clinical, microbiological, or laboratory data were largely missing. Of 696 cases identified, 3 were removed for incomplete records, leaving 693 for analysis (Fig. 1). Ethics

The study followed the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the ethics committees of every participating center, with Tongji Hospital serving as the lead committee (approval No. TJ-IRB202412018), whose decision the other centers accepted. Because the design was retrospective and non-interventional and posed minimal risk, written informed consent was waived. All data were de-identified before analysis.

Definitions and Outcomes

MDR pneumonia was defined as a parenchymal lower respiratory tract infection that met combined clinical, radiological, and microbiological criteria, with an MDR pathogen recovered from a respiratory specimen [31]. In vitro susceptibility was read according to CLSI M100, except that colistin and tigecycline breakpoints followed EUCAST (European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing) guidelines. The attending physician made the diagnosis, which each center’s infection control team then confirmed.

The primary outcome was clinical response, categorized as cure, improvement, failure, or death. Death meant all-cause death within the relevant window (14 or 30 days). Cure required survival to discharge with the core signs of infection resolved, radiological and laboratory evidence of resolution, and no recourse to salvage therapy. Improvement meant a clinically meaningful gain in at least one of two or more baseline signs of infection, and failure meant infection that persisted or progressed. Acute kidney injury (AKI) was logged as a safety observation and was taken to be present when any one of the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria was met: (1) a rise in serum creatinine (SCr) of ≥ 26.5 µmol/L within 48 h; (2) a rise in SCr to ≥ 1.5 times baseline within the preceding 7 days; or (3) urine output ≤ 0.5 mL/(kg·h) over 6 h. Baseline SCr was the most recent value in the 7 days before CAZ-AVI was started. In patients with pre-existing chronic kidney disease (CKD) or already on continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) at baseline, incident AKI was defined as an acute worsening relative to their baseline creatinine or as a new dialysis requirement; patients with no residual renal function and long-term dialysis dependence at baseline were not counted again as incident AKI. For follow-up, the day CAZ-AVI was started served as the index day (day 0); the 14- and 30-day windows ran from the following day (day 1), with the index day itself counted within follow-up.

Serum levels of interleukin-2 (IL-2), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-10 (IL-10), C-reactive protein (CRP), and procalcitonin (PCT), along with serum albumin, were measured at three time points: before treatment (24 h prior to CAZ-AVI initiation), during treatment (24–72 h after initiation), and after treatment (within 72 h following discontinuation). Because these markers are pinned to measurements taken at different points in the CAZ-AVI course while follow-up time begins at the first dose, marker-based analyses are open to guarantee time bias. To limit survivor (immortal time) bias in the during- and after-treatment analyses of the dynamic markers and albumin, we applied a landmark analysis anchored at a pre-specified day 3 after CAZ-AVI initiation: only patients still alive at day 3 entered the landmark cohort, and for these analyses follow-up was counted from the day 3 landmark to death or day 30, whichever came first.

Statistical Analysis

The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to check the normality of continuous variables. Normally distributed variables are given as mean ± SD and compared with the independent-samples t test; non-normal variables are given as median (interquartile range) and compared with the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables are given as n (%) and compared with the χ2 or Fisher exact test. Variables significant at P < 0.05 on univariable testing were carried forward into multivariable logistic regression models for 14-day and for 30-day all-cause mortality, and LASSO-penalized regression with cross-validation served as a sensitivity analysis for variable selection. Variables with less than 5% missingness were analyzed on complete cases; those with 5–20% missingness were handled by multiple imputation; and those exceeding 20% were dropped. To allow for differences in event timing, a time-to-event Cox proportional hazards model was fitted as a sensitivity analysis and reported as hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI), whereas the primary analysis expressed associations as odds ratios (OR) with 95% CI. Because the sample was only moderate (n = 693) and some predictors were uncommon—K. pneumoniae infection, for example, occurred in just 54 patients (7.8%)—we also fitted Firth penalized logistic regression to curb small-sample bias. Analyses were run in R (version 4.1.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing). We report associations, not a validated prediction model: discrimination and calibration were not assessed, and neither internal nor external validation was undertaken.

Costs were assembled by bottom-up micro-costing, covering pneumonia-related inpatient items: the target and any combination antimicrobials, imaging and laboratory tests, single-use consumables, daily bed fees, graded nursing and respiratory support. Maintenance treatment of comorbidities, non-infection-related investigations, and indirect costs were left out. We report costs descriptively—medians for the total and for the CAZ-AVI component. No comparative cost-effectiveness analysis was attempted: there was no comparator arm, and we estimated neither quality- or disability-adjusted life-years nor incremental cost-effectiveness ratios. For the same reason, no cost-effectiveness threshold (such as a multiple of GDP per capita) was applied. Amounts were recorded in Chinese yuan (CNY) and converted to US dollars at 1:6.80 (2026). Given the skew typical of cost data, central tendency is summarized by the median and dispersion by the interquartile range (IQR; Q1–Q3), with figures rounded to the nearest dollar.

Results

Cohort Characteristics

In all, 693 patients with MDR pneumonia treated with CAZ-AVI between August 2022 and August 2025 were enrolled (Table 1; Fig. 2). A favorable response was seen in 480 (69.3%)—cure in 356 (51.4%) and improvement in 124 (17.9%). Among the confirmed MDR pathogens, K. pneumoniae was isolated in 54 cases (7.8%), carbapenem-resistant/difficult-to-treat P. aeruginosa (CRPA/DTR-PA) in 171 cases (24.7%), and Acinetobacter baumannii in 13 cases (1.9%). Additionally, mixed polymicrobial infections (isolation of more than one MDR pathogen) were identified in 49 patients (7.1%). It is important to note that while CAZ-AVI lacks intrinsic activity against A. baumannii, the 13 patients whose isolates contained this pathogen received CAZ-AVI either because it was part of a mixed Patients who died by 14 and by 30 days were, compared with survivors, older and more often had malignancy, malnutrition, hypoalbuminemia, CKD or immunosuppressant exposure, and they scored higher on APACHE II, the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score, and the Charlson index (all P < 0.05). They also more frequently had positive sputum, blood, and urine cultures; bloodstream infection, bacteremia, and urinary tract infection; and septic shock, prior ICU admission, and CRRT. K. pneumoniae, carbapenem-resistant K. pneumoniae (CRKP), A. baumannii, carbapenem-resistant organism (CRO), and CRPA/DTR-PA were each isolated more often from non-survivors (all P < 0.05). Conversely, non-survivors were less likely to have received CAZ-AVI as first-line treatment, more likely to have been given a sub-target maintenance dose, and more likely to display an in vitro non-susceptible phenotype (Table 1).

Univariable Analysis of Mortality

The univariable analysis at 14 and 30 days showed largely consistent findings. Septic shock, prior ICU admission, ventilator-associated pneumonia, urinary tract infection, and a positive urine culture were all associated with higher mortality. At 30 days hospital-acquired pneumonia, bacteremia, and CRRT were also associated with higher mortality. On the microbiological side, CRKP, K. pneumoniae, and A. baumannii were associated with higher mortality, while CRO and difficult-to-treat P. aeruginosa emerged as significant at 30 days. Host characteristics—malnutrition, malignancy, hypoalbuminemia, and a higher APACHE II—showed similar associations. First-line CAZ-AVI, an at-target maintenance dose, combination therapy, and glucocorticoid use, by contrast, appeared potentially protective (all P < 0.05). Such exposure-related associations are vulnerable to confounding by indication and to immortal time bias—patients had to survive long enough to receive first-line CAZ-AVI. Therefore these findings should be interpreted with caution.

For death within 14 days (Fig. 4a), older age and a higher APACHE II aside, bacteremia (4.76; 2.39–9.66), CRO infection (5.93; 2.60–13.60), and septic shock (3.30; 1.32–8.39) were each independently associated with the outcome—in keeping with early dissemination of infection narrowing the window for effective treatment.Fig. 4Forest plots of factors independently associated with a 14-day and b 30-day all-cause mortality (multivariable logistic regression)

By 30 days (Fig. 4b), the associated factors were septic shock (5.74; 1.34–24.97) and K. pneumoniae (15.19; 3.25–78.94). The K. pneumoniae estimate sat within a very wide confidence interval—a sign of instability from sparse data—and must be interpreted with caution. To counter small-sample bias we added two sensitivity analyses, Firth penalized logistic regression and a Cox model; these returned an OR of 13.51 (3.65–62.49) and an HR of 10.16 (3.72–27.36), respectively. The three approaches agreed in pointing to a significant positive association between K. pneumoniae and death, and the direction of effect was the same throughout. The point estimate is imprecise given the sample size, but its consistency across models lends the association some credibility.

All analyses of the during- and after-treatment markers and of albumin rested on the pre-specified day 3 landmark cohort (n = 671). Within it, higher IL-6 and IL-10 were associated with worse 30-day outcomes, and a rising serum albumin was associated with lower 30-day mortality (0.89; 0.82–0.95). The landmark design mitigates survivor (immortal time) bias; however, because this is a post hoc observational analysis, residual bias remains possible, and any causal inferences require prospective testing.

Acute Kidney Injury (Safety Observation)

AKI, by the KDIGO definition, occurred in 2.2% of patients (15/693). CRRT use, at 11.4%, was far higher—a gap that points to baseline maintenance dialysis, rather than new AKI, as the main driver of CRRT. Prior ICU admission, septic shock, severe malnutrition, and a positive sputum culture were each more common in the AKI group (Table 2). With so few events, and a rate that sits implausibly low against the baseline CRRT figure, these data can only be considered descriptive. Underascertainment is likely, and they will not support a formal safety conclusion. Subgroup and Sensitivity Analyses

Inpatient Costs (Descriptive)

Inpatient costs were summarized by bottom-up micro-costing (Sect. “Statistical Analysis”). The median total inpatient cost was USD 14,982 (IQR 8462–25,136), within which CAZ-AVI accounted for a median of USD 3901 (IQR 2069–6159), or about 26% of the pneumonia-related total (Fig. 6). The balance comprised combination or adjunctive antimicrobials, imaging and laboratory tests, single-use consumables, daily bed fees, graded nursing and respiratory support; comorbidity maintenance, non-infection-related investigations, and indirect costs were excluded. These numbers describe the cohort’s resource use and nothing more: we conducted no comparative economic evaluation, computed no incremental cost-effectiveness ratio, and applied no cost-effectiveness threshold (Sect. “Discussion”). Discussion

In this large, pneumonia-focused, real-world cohort, 69.3% of patients given CAZ-AVI had a favorable response—close to the roughly 68% cure rate of the REPROVE trial [2122] and higher than in some real-world series in CRKP infection. Unlike the registration trials, our cohort included its sickest patients, among them those with very high APACHE II scores, and effectiveness did not decline in parallel with severity. One interpretation is that comprehensive real-world care cushions outcomes, but without a comparator we cannot claim efficacy.

The finding we would stress is that the correlates of death shifted with the time window. Death within 14 days was associated with the acute spread of infection—bacteremia, CRO infection, septic shock—whereas death by 30 days was more closely associated with K. pneumoniae and with signs of sustained inflammation and catabolism, namely persistent IL-6 and IL-10 and a low serum albumin, a picture that recalls persistent inflammation–immunosuppression–catabolism syndrome (PICS) [32–35]. These remain associations. The albumin and marker results may in part be a survivor effect, since later-window measurements exist only for those who survived to that window. Furthermore, these variables should be interpreted cautiously; rather than being true baseline predictors of mortality, they likely reflect the patients’ physiological response to treatment, clinical recovery, and the resolution of infection.

When considered with due caution, these patterns suggest hypotheses for time-stratified care—pressing for rapid pathogen control with PK/PD-guided dosing early on, then turning attention to metabolic and immune monitoring later—though each would need prospective, controlled confirmation. The protective trend for combination therapy on univariable analysis is in the same vein: unadjusted, hypothesis-generating, and no settlement of the long-standing monotherapy-versus-combination question in severe MDR pneumonia [3637].

We also set out the sizeable inpatient costs of caring for MDR pneumonia. We did not perform a cost-effectiveness analysis and make no cost-effectiveness claim; comparative evaluations of CAZ-AVI against polymyxin B and the model-based analyses from China offer useful reference points and templates for that work [293038].

Several limitations bear on these findings. Being retrospective and lacking a comparator, the study cannot address comparative effectiveness or cost-effectiveness. Despite LASSO selection, residual confounding—by indication and through immortal time effects—cannot be ruled out. For instance, patients receiving first-line CAZ-AVI likely differed in baseline severity from those receiving salvage therapy. While multivariable adjustment was performed, robust causal inference approaches such as propensity score matching or inverse probability weighting were not feasible without inducing severe sample size reduction and model instability. Therefore, any exposure-related associations must be interpreted with caution as residual confounding remains. Carbapenemase genotypes (KPC, OXA-48, NDM, and other metallo-β-lactamases) and the CAZ-AVI MIC distribution were unavailable. Since the activity of CAZ-AVI is highly dependent on the underlying resistance mechanism, this absence restricts our ability to interpret treatment outcomes at a molecular level. Specifically, metallo-β-lactamase (MBL) producers, which are not susceptible to CAZ-AVI, co-circulate in Chinese clinical settings. The inadvertent inclusion of patients with MBL-producing infections could have blunted the overall apparent effectiveness observed in this study. The time-varying marker and albumin associations are, as noted, open to survivor (immortal time) bias. Some estimates, K. pneumoniae above all, are unstable because of the limited number of events. AKI was probably underascertained and was not treated as a formal endpoint. Finally, a single-region cohort limits generalizability, and we made no adjustment for multiple comparisons. Testing the hypotheses raised here will require prospective, multicenter, controlled studies—ideally with molecular resistance characterization, MIC data, and time-to-event and landmark analyses built in.

Conclusions

In routine practice, CAZ-AVI for MDR Gram-negative pneumonia was associated with a favorable clinical response, and the factors associated with death early were not the same as those linked to death later. These associations are hypothesis-generating; with the accompanying cost description, they may help inform time-stratified management strategies and the comparative, controlled studies that are still required. Given the lack of a comparator group, these findings demonstrate associations and clinical responses only and cannot establish the comparative effectiveness or cost-effectiveness of CAZ-AVI against alternative regimens.

Two-layer evolutionary optimization problem for home healthcare pathways and scheduling under multi-center resource sharing
Authors: Xinmiao Shao, Zhong Wu, Qing Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address inter-center workload imbalance and low resource utilization in multi-center home healthcare systems, this study proposes a bilevel optimization approach for routing and scheduling that enables cross-center resource sharing. First, we formulate a weighted single-objective model that jointly accounts for travel time, service costs, penalties for time-window violations, and workload-imbalance penalties, thereby providing a unified representation of multi-criteria trade-offs while incorporating skill-matching and labor-compliance constraints. Second, we develop a bilevel evolutionary framework, termed the Bilevel Evolutionary Planning Framework for Multi-Center Routing and Scheduling with Resource Sharing (BEPSF). At the upper level, a genetic algorithm (GA) determines client-to-center assignments and performs global workload regulation. At the lower level, a hybrid GAPSO strategy is employed to solve the within-center routing and scheduling of multi-skilled staff, integrating a time-window-driven feasibility repair mechanism with an elitist preservation strategy to enhance solution accuracy and robustness. Computational experiments on 18 standardized benchmark instances demonstrate that BEPSF significantly outperforms the GA-GA and DE-DE baselines in both solution quality and stability, achieving average improvements of approximately 31% and 63%, respectively, and attaining 0% deviation from the reference optimum on small-scale instances. Sensitivity and scenario analyses further reveal that maximum working hours, time-window tolerance, and break intervals substantially affect the cost composition. After enabling cross-center resource sharing, the total system cost is generally reduced by 20–50%, with a maximum reduction of 66%.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-50649-1.Keywords: Home healthcare, Multi-center resource sharing, Bilevel evolutionary optimization framework, Routing and scheduling optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent years, accelerated population ageing has driven a sustained increase in healthcare demand, placing greater pressure on public health systems in terms of resource supply and service accessibility. Projections indicate that the global population aged 60 and above will rise substantially by 2050, accompanied by growing needs for chronic disease management, long-term monitoring, and professional nursing care1. Against this backdrop, delivering healthcare services that are efficient, convenient, and cost-contained under limited resources has become a pressing challenge2. Home healthcare (HHC), in which caregivers depart from service centers to provide in-home examinations, rehabilitation, and nursing services, has attracted increasing attention because it can alleviate hospital congestion while improving patient experience and continuity of care3.

The core operational decisions in HHC lie in routing and scheduling: assigning caregivers and determining service sequences and visiting routes while satisfying constraints such as skill compatibility, service time windows, and labor regulations4. This problem can be viewed as an important extension of the Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP). However, the simultaneous presence of multi-skill matching, time-window constraints, workload balancing, and labor compliance requirements leads to stronger constraint coupling and substantially higher computational difficulty5–8. Existing studies have largely focused on single-center settings and typically rely on metaheuristics such as genetic algorithms, simulated annealing, ant colony optimization, and large neighborhood search9–11. In real-world operations, however, HHC systems often exhibit multi-center collaboration, where cross-center staff sharing and task reassignment are common12,13.

Under multi-center resource-sharing settings, cross-center task allocation and within-center routing optimization are tightly coupled: resource sharing changes each center’s available workforce and effective service radius, thereby affecting within-center feasibility and the cost structure; conversely, within-center schedules and time-window conflicts influence the quality of cross-center allocations. As a result, a “global allocation–local scheduling” decision structure is difficult to capture adequately within a single-level model, and must simultaneously ensure feasibility while balancing multiple cost components. Although prior research has addressed certain aspects of multi-center operations or sharing mechanisms, a systematic and reproducible framework that integrates resource sharing, unified cost trade-offs, and hierarchical coordinated solution procedures remains relatively scarce. Furthermore, the integration of large-scale data-driven optimization with multi-criteria decision-making has proven highly effective for resource-constrained decision-making in high-dimensional spaces, demonstrating significant benefits even in non-logistics domains such as credit risk analysis14. Inspired by the success of such hybrid computational frameworks in handling multiple complex constraints, we propose a bilevel evolutionary optimization framework, BEPSF (Bilevel Evolutionary Planning Framework for Multi-Center Routing and Scheduling with Resource Sharing). The framework employs a weighted single-objective function to jointly represent travel-time cost, service cost, penalties for time-window violations, and penalties for workload imbalance. At the upper level, a genetic algorithm is used to perform patient-to-center allocation and macro-level workload regulation. At the lower level, given an assigned client subset, a GA–GAPSO hybrid strategy is introduced to optimize the routes and service sequences of multiple caregivers within each center, augmented with a time-window-driven feasibility repair mechanism and an elitist preservation strategy to improve solution stability and convergence performance.

The main contributions of this study are threefold: (1) we develop a unified weighted cost model tailored to multi-center resource-sharing settings, enabling multi-factor trade-offs and explicit representation of sharing-related constraints within a single framework; (2) we design a bilevel coordinated evolutionary solution framework with an embedded feasibility repair mechanism to enhance constraint satisfaction and algorithmic robustness; (3) we conduct comparative experiments, statistical tests, and sensitivity analyses on multiple benchmark instances to demonstrate the effectiveness and stability of the proposed method in multi-center resource-sharing scenarios, while providing practical guidance for parameter setting.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. “Literature review” reviews related work. “Model construction” introduces the model and notation. “Algorithm design: bi-level GA–GAPSOoptimization framework” presents the BEPSF framework and algorithmic details. “Numerical verification” reports experimental results and comparative analyses. “Conclusions” concludes the paper and outlines future research directions.

Literature review

Single-center home healthcare scheduling

Research on single-center Home Health Care (HHC) scheduling typically aims to improve the operational efficiency and service quality of a single service center. The key decisions in this area include caregiver assignment and route sequencing, while optimizing travel costs, service costs, and penalty costs under constraints such as time windows, working-hour limits, and service durations15–17. The research in this direction can be broadly categorized into two main areas: modeling under uncertainty and robustness, and algorithm design for complex constraints and efficient solution methods.

Regarding uncertainty, Shi et al. incorporated demand and service-time uncertainty into the scheduling process and enhanced solution robustness by combining fuzzy programming with stochastic simulation, thereby improving feasibility and stability under random perturbations18 In terms of complex constraints, Decerle et al. developed a minimum travel-time model with time windows and synchronization requirements, maintaining high feasibility even when service sequences are restricted or coordination constraints are present19. To improve scalability, Grenouilleau et al. proposed a set-partitioning heuristic that groups routes into sets and performs combinatorial selection, substantially improving the efficiency of solving home-care routing and scheduling problems20. Dekhici et al. discretized the firefly algorithm to fit combinatorial structures, providing an alternative metaheuristic approach for single-center HHC problems21.

Overall, research on single-center scheduling has become relatively mature in both modeling and algorithm design. However, its fundamental assumption typically defaults to “independent operation of a single center,” making it difficult to directly address issues such as capacity imbalance, cross-regional services, and inter-center collaboration in multi-center environments. As a result, there are still significant limitations when it comes to resource sharing and collaborative scheduling across centers.

Multi-center home healthcare scheduling

With increasing home-care demand and widening regional disparities in healthcare resources, multi-center coordinated scheduling has emerged as a key direction for improving resource utilization and service equity. Compared with single-center problems, multi-center settings require not only within-center routing optimization but also system-level decisions such as client allocation across centers, cross-regional services, and inter-center workload balancing. Existing research has primarily focused on multi-objective modeling and metaheuristic solution methods, gradually incorporating service-sharing or collaboration concepts.

For example, Erdem and Bulkan (2017) proposed a hybrid solution framework combining GA-VNS, balancing search efficiency and solution diversity in a multi-objective setting22; Fathollahi-Fard et al. (2020) incorporated environmental impacts and operational costs into the multi-objective function, emphasizing the joint pursuit of economic efficiency and sustainability23. Goodarzian et al. (2021) developed a bi-objective model focusing on working-time control and route balance, and employed an adaptive Social Engineering Optimizer (SSEO) to enhance computational performance, highlighting the importance of workload balancing in care scheduling24.

With respect to shared services, Fu et al. (2023) explicitly investigated HHC scheduling under a service-sharing strategy, proposed a multi-objective model, and adopted a multi-objective artificial bee colony algorithm for efficient exploration25. Ma et al. (2023) developed a knowledge-guided multi-objective evolutionary algorithm and achieved superior Pareto frontiers for cost–tardiness trade-offs26. Moreover, Cappanera et al. (2025) discussed flexible care delivery from the perspective of transitioning “from substitution to collaboration” between home care and center-based care, emphasizing the complementary potential of multi-center cooperation in resource allocation and providing an important practical motivation for cross-center collaboration and shared services27.

Additionally, research in related fields, such as multi-center resource allocation and spatial decision-making, provides methodological inspiration for this problem. For example, Ahmadi Choukolaei et al. (2021) combined GIS with multi-criteria decision-making methods for rescue center location and resource allocation under disaster scenarios28. Although this work is not focused on HHC, its modeling approach for decision support in multi-center spatial resource allocation offers valuable insights for the subsequent application of GIS in cross-center resource distribution in this study.

In summary, significant progress has been made in multi-center HHC research in terms of multi-objective modeling and algorithm development. However, in the context of collaborative resource sharing, task allocation and route scheduling are often still handled in a single-layer approach. Even when shared services are introduced, they are typically embedded as strategies at the routing level or local level, lacking a systematic hierarchical optimization framework that simultaneously coordinates global allocation and local path scheduling. This represents the key gap that this study aims to address.

Research gaps and challenges

Based on the above classification review and comparative analysis, this study identifies the following core research gaps and challenges. First, although concepts of shared services or collaboration have been proposed within the multi-center HHC domain, most existing studies still handle task allocation and route scheduling using a single-layer structure. They lack a bilevel optimization framework capable of simultaneously coordinating “global client-to-center assignment” and “local path scheduling,” which makes it difficult to systematically characterize the synergistic effects of cross-center resource sharing. Second, while multi-objective frameworks can enhance solution diversity, they entail heavy computational burdens in practical large-scale scenarios; real-world decision-making often favors single-solution outputs that are fast, interpretable, and easily deployable. Finally, although some research has begun to address stochastic factors, current modeling for multi-center sharing systems is still largely based on static deterministic assumptions, with limited consideration of dynamic perturbations such as traffic fluctuations and temporary demand changes, thereby restricting the model’s robustness in complex environments. Despite advanced research attempting to integrate fuzzy logic and the NSGA-II algorithm to handle uncertainties in HHC scheduling29, how to deeply integrate such uncertainty-handling mechanisms with the hierarchical coordination structure of multi-center resource sharing remains a significant gap to be filled.

Contributions of this study

To bridge the aforementioned gaps, this study makes specific contributions in terms of modeling, methodology, and application. At the modeling level, we propose an HHC scheduling model tailored for multi-center resource sharing, integrating travel costs, service costs, time-window violation penalties, and workload imbalance penalties into a unified weighted single-objective framework. This enables flexible trade-offs among cost, service quality, and fairness through weight calibration and supports cross-center sharing and allocation decisions. At the methodological level, inspired by the success of the hybrid computational frameworks mentioned above in handling complex constrained decisions, we design the bilevel evolutionary optimization framework, BEPSF. By adopting a hierarchical structure to solve the upper-level global assignment and workload balancing problem and the lower-level local path scheduling and feasibility repair problem separately, this decoupled design achieves global–local collaborative search and significantly enhances the model’s decomposability and computational efficiency. At the application level, through the global–local collaborative optimization strategy, the framework enhances the practical deployability in multi-center resource-sharing scenarios, providing medical institutions with interpretable and actionable decision-support solutions.

Algorithm design: bi-level GA–GAPSO optimization framework

This scheduling problem requires the simultaneous determination of customer-center assignments and caregiver paths before service begins. The decision-making process must satisfy multiple constraints, including customer time windows, skill matching, center capacity, maximum caregiver service volume, maximum working hours, and minimum rest times. These constraints are highly interrelated, especially considering the dependencies among center assignment, service allocation, path arcs, and arrival times. Single-layer heuristic methods often struggle to balance global resource allocation and path quality within centers, and crossover and mutation operations may lead to infeasible solutions that violate constraints, thereby affecting the stability and reproducibility of the algorithm.

To address this issue, this paper proposes a two-layer evolutionary optimization framework. The framework consists of two main levels: the outer genetic algorithm (GA) is responsible for globally searching the customer-center assignments and balancing the load; the inner particle swarm optimization (GAPSO) optimizes the caregiver service assignment and path scheduling within each center, given the customer-center assignments. In the optimization process of both layers, we introduce a violation detection mechanism and deterministic repair mapping to ensure that the solutions obtained after each optimization satisfy all constraints, and can immediately correct any constraint violations during crossover and mutation operations.

Path-level crossover and repair in the outer-layer GA

To balance exploration and feasibility at both the customer-center assignment and center-path levels, the outer genetic algorithm (GA) adopts a crossover strategy of entire path exchange and conflict repair. The core idea of this strategy is as follows: first, entire path segments are exchanged between the center-path sets of two parent individuals to inherit high-quality structures; then, a minimal-cost repair operation is applied to ensure that the center assignments still satisfy the capacity and unique assignment constraints. This process enhances population diversity and ensures the feasibility of the solution. Figure 3 illustrates the conflict detection and redistribution process. The specific steps are as follows:

Step 1: Path exchange.

In the two parent solutions, a path is randomly selected for exchange (for example, a path starting from and returning to center ), resulting in an initial offspring. This operation quickly introduces a better service sequence and enhances structural diversity. Through the exchange, the offspring’s path sequence is optimized, making the service more efficient.

Step 2: Conflict detection and redistribution.

Path exchange may cause some customers to be served multiple times or omitted (for example, customers 12, 10, and 18 in the example). In this case, we first remove duplicate customers from the relevant paths, then, based on distance and load information, insert the removed or missing customers into the feasible path with the lowest cost, thus redistributing the customers.

Step 3: Capacity repair.

The redistribution operation may cause the number of clients at certain centers to exceed the capacity limit . To address this issue, a retention, deletion, and migration strategy is applied. Specifically, clients with lower costs are retained at the center, and the excess clients are migrated to other centers with remaining capacity and the lowest insertion cost, until the capacity constraints are satisfied. This strategy ensures that the number of clients assigned to each center does not exceed its capacity, while minimizing the overall service cost. After completing this repair, the offspring center allocation satisfies constraints (1) and (2) and can proceed to subsequent fitness evaluation and elite retention.

Heuristic rules and convergence discussion

Under complex constraint conditions, relying solely on random search may generate a large number of infeasible solutions, thereby slowing down the convergence speed. To address this issue, BEPSF enhances search efficiency while ensuring solution feasibility through multi-level heuristic rules. These improvements are mainly reflected in the following aspects:

Property 1 (Capacity feasibility):

During both the initialization and evolution processes of the outer layer, BEPSF employs a “random assignment + capacity check” mechanism. Specifically, when the assignment to a center exceeds its capacity limit, resampling occurs until the following constraints are satisfied:

This ensures that the outer layer individuals always maintain capacity feasibility, preventing infeasible solutions caused by capacity issues.

Property 2 (Time window and working hour feasibility):

During path construction and repair, if a customer’s arrival time is earlier than the lower bound of the time window, waiting is allowed. If the arrival time exceeds the upper bound of the time window and the tolerance for delay or maximum service time is exceeded, the current path is terminated early and the center is returned to start a new path. This mechanism ensures that all paths meet the time window and working hour constraints, avoiding violations or excessive service durations.

Property 3 (Skill priority and resource utilization):

In the initial assignment and path construction stages, BEPSF always prioritizes assigning caregivers whose skill levels match customer needs exactly. If no exact match is available, higher-skilled caregivers are selected. This strategy ensures service quality while preventing the premature depletion of high-skilled caregivers, ensuring effective resource utilization and improving overall service efficiency.

Numerical verification

This section conducts numerical experiments to validate the bi-level evolutionary scheduling framework BEPSF under a unified benchmark suite and a consistent computational budget. The performance of BEPSF is compared with two structurally matched bi-level baselines (GA–GA and DE–DE). The evaluation emphasizes solution quality and stability, statistical significance, computational efficiency, anytime performance, parameter sensitivity, ablation contributions, and mechanism interpretation in representative scenarios. All algorithms are implemented in MATLAB R2024a and executed on an Intel(R) Core(TM) i9-14900 K CPU at 3.19 GHz with 128 GB RAM under Windows 11 (64-bit). Each instance is independently repeated 10 times, and the mean, standard deviation, and best value are reported.

Instance generation and experimental settings

Building on the classical Solomon VRPTW instance construction paradigm, this paper develops a standardized benchmark set featuring multi-center and multi-skill characteristics.

Standardized benchmark suite

We construct a standardized benchmark suite that incorporates multi-center and multi-skill characteristics. The geographic layouts are categorized into three types: C-type (clustered and high-density), R-type (uniformly random over the entire region), and RC-type (a mixture of clustered and global random distributions). By jointly considering the number of care centers, spatial layout, patient scale, and staffing configuration, we generate 18 instances using an orthogonal design, covering multiple scale levels and structural variations to support both cross-instance comparisons and scalability analysis.

Consistent parameter budget across algorithms

To ensure the full reproducibility of the experiments, this section reports all hyperparameter configurations in detail. The outer-layer population size for all algorithms is set to 20, with a maximum of 200 iterations. For the GA-GAPSO and GA-GA frameworks, both the outer-layer crossover probability () and mutation probability () are set to 1.0 to maximize the exploration of the search space. The outer layer of the DE-DE framework adopts fixed differential mutation parameters, with the crossover rate set to 0.5.Regarding the inner layer, the GA-GAPSO employs a swarm size of 10 and a maximum of 20 iterations. Because the routing problem in this study is a discrete combinatorial optimization problem, the inner-layer GAPSO does not adopt the traditional continuous particle swarm velocity update equations—that is, it does not include the inertia weight and learning factors . Instead, particles update their positions through direct discrete segment crossover operations. Specifically, in each iteration, every particle undergoes discrete crossover with its personal best (Pbest) and the global best (Gbest) with a probability of , followed by a mutation operation with a probability of . The inner-layer maximum iterations for both GA-GA and DE-DE are also strictly unified to 20.To ensure fairness in the comparative evaluation, an equal computational budget is applied across all algorithms. Each algorithm executes the same maximum number of outer and inner iterations and terminates when the budget limit is reached. All randomized algorithms are executed under identical hardware and software environments, employing an explicit, sequential set of fixed random seeds. Specifically, 10 independent repeated trials are conducted by sequentially and strictly initializing seeds from rng(1) through rng(10) in MATLAB. The metrics reported in this paper are the statistical results derived from these 10 independent runs.

Deterministic parameter selection criteria and empirical analysis

To transition from heuristic estimation to a highly reproducible deterministic decision procedure, this study establishes an integrated mathematical framework to calibrate all five key parameters based on the empirical evidence in Table 630. The first phase of this framework utilizes the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) to translate qualitative managerial strategies into quantitative baseline weight mappings31.By defining the criteria set and constructing the pairwise comparison matrix , the priority weight vector is derived by solving the principal eigenvalue problem . Under the condition that the consistency ratio is satisfied, this mapping establishes search intervals for penalty weights relative to baseline travel costs, providing a theoretical anchor for subsequent data-driven refinement.

Following the determination of baseline intervals, the second phase of the calibration procedure introduces Discrete Marginal Utility () analysis to determine the exact parameters at the empirical level. This study defines the marginal utility of a parameter as the sensitivity of violation suppression relative to the cost inflation rate, expressed as , where represents the violation rate and denotes the total cost. According to the experimental results for instance 3-R101-60 in Table 6, when the time-window penalty increases from 25 to 35, the total system cost incurs a negligible increment of only 0.09% while effectively suppressing violation risks, indicating that is in a high-return zone. However, further increasing to 45 causes the total cost to surge from 11,209.56 to 13,374.68—a jump of 19.31%—while the marginal gain in service quality approaches zero.

Guided by the elbow point principle in operational research, and are mathematically identified as the optimal equilibrium points, where workload fairness is maximized without triggering disproportionate inflation in travel expenditure. Concurrently, the operational constraint parameters , , and are selected by defining the global minimum point of the total cost function while ensuring strict compliance with labor safety regulations. This integrated calibration process proves that the configuration of the BEPSF framework is not based on empirical guessing but is a scientific outcome determined by the synthesis of managerial preference mapping and data-driven marginal search.

Scenario 1: single-center vs. multi-center scheduling capability

Based on the comparison results from 18 test cases (Table 8), the multi-center structure leads to significant system-wide cost improvements in most scenarios, with total cost reductions generally ranging from 20% to 48%. From the perspective of cost composition, multi-center coordination and resource sharing allow tasks to be redistributed across centers, which alleviates time window conflicts and caregiver workload imbalances on a larger scale, thereby improving overall scheduling efficiency. It should be noted, however, that the multi-center approach does not consistently outperform in all test cases. For instances with smaller scales, more concentrated spatial distributions, or simpler routing structures, the additional overhead introduced by cross-domain coordination and inter-center travel may offset the potential benefits, leading to unstable gains or even slight increases in total costs. This suggests that the advantage of the multi-center structure depends on the demand scale and spatial layout: when demand is larger and the spatial structure is more complex, multi-center configurations are more likely to leverage the advantages of broader task redistribution; whereas in smaller or more clustered scenarios, the coordination cost across centers must be weighed against the potential benefits.

Scenario 2: Contribution of the inter-center sharing mechanism

Given a fixed multi-center structure, we further evaluate the effect of enabling or disabling the inter-center sharing mechanism. As reported in Table 9, enabling sharing leads to a significant decrease in total cost for most instances, with reductions mostly concentrated in the range of 20% to 50%. Several instances exhibit particularly pronounced improvements. For example, the total cost reductions for 2-C101-20, 3-C101-60, and 2-R101-20 reach 66.29%, 49.49%, and 48.57%, respectively. These results indicate that the sharing mechanism effectively supports inter-center adjustment and collaboration, thereby reducing time-window violation penalties and workload imbalance penalties and improving overall scheduling quality.

We also observe that the improvement is relatively limited in some instances (e.g., about 4.04% for 4-C101-100 and about 8.70% for 3-C101-80). Such cases typically correspond to concentrated spatial structures or specific scheduling patterns at certain scales, where the additional coordination and cross-region travel induced by sharing may offset part of the benefits, resulting in a smaller net improvement. Overall, this controlled experiment quantitatively confirms the practical value of the sharing mechanism in most cases and provides more interpretable managerial guidance on when to enable inter-center sharing.

Conclusions

This study addresses the routing and scheduling problem in home healthcare under multi-center resource-sharing scenarios by constructing a unified optimization model centered on a weighted single-objective function. The model aims to achieve synergistic effects between inter-center resource allocation and intra-center routing optimization. On this basis, a bilevel evolutionary optimization framework, termed BEPSF, is proposed. The upper level employs a genetic algorithm (GA) for global assignment and workload regulation, while the lower level introduces a hybrid GA-GAPSO strategy to solve multi-skilled staff routing, integrating a time-window-driven feasibility repair mechanism with an elitist preservation strategy. This design effectively tackles the computational challenges arising from the high coupling of global allocation and local scheduling in multi-center sharing contexts, providing interpretable and deployable decision support for management.

Experimental results based on 18 sets of benchmark instances demonstrate that BEPSF significantly outperforms bilevel baseline algorithms in terms of both solution quality and robustness. After enabling the cross-center sharing mechanism, total system costs generally decreased by 20–50%, with a maximum reduction reaching 66.29%. Wilcoxon rank-sum tests and effect size analyses further verify the advantages of the proposed framework in resolving resource conflicts and enhancing system capacity, providing a quantitative basis for multi-center institutions to formulate strategies regarding when and how to implement resource sharing and staff scheduling.

Despite its practical value, this study has several limitations. First, the BEPSF framework is primarily based on deterministic inputs and does not systematically account for dynamic factors such as traffic fluctuations or stochastic service times, which limits its robustness in highly unstable environments. Second, the nested GA-GAPSO bilevel architecture incurs certain computational overhead when handling ultra-large-scale multi-center systems, posing challenges to the algorithm’s scalability. Finally, while the weighted single-objective function facilitates rapid deployment, there is room for optimization compared to multi-objective Pareto formulations in terms of characterizing explicit trade-offs among multiple criteria and enhancing decision interpretability.

Future research can be expanded in three directions. First, incorporating stochastic programming or fuzzy logic into the multi-center sharing framework29, combined with rolling horizon or online scheduling, could enhance the model’s applicability and robustness in dynamic, uncertain environments. Second, addressing the computationally intensive nature of the bilevel architecture by exploring parallel computing architectures or adaptive heuristic operators to verify scalability in larger-scale, multi-skilled scenarios. Third, developing Pareto-based multi-objective evolutionary algorithms, integrated with weight scanning and visualization techniques, to intuitively characterize the systematic trade-offs between cost, punctuality, and fairness. Furthermore, conducting multi-dimensional trials with real operational data will further enhance the feasibility of the proposed method for practical engineering deployment.

Epidemiological and clinical characteristics of severe burns in adults: A retrospective study at a burn centre in Suzhou, China
Authors: Yong Zhang, Jiandong Su, Yunfeng Liu, Ran Sun, Ruizhu Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSevere burns lead to malformations and higher mortality and have long‐term social consequences. We aimed to analyse the epidemiologic characteristics and clinical outcomes of severe burn patients hospitalized in a major burn centre in Eastern China. This study included severe burns patients hospitalized in our hospital from 2017 to 2022. We extracted all data from electronic medical records for retrospective analysis. Burn scoring systems were calculated for all patients. The median age of the patients was 49.00 years, with 62.13% of them aged between 30 and 59 years. Of the 375 adult patients, 256 were male and 119 were female. The major cause of burns was flame (46.1%), followed by explosion (35.2%). The median total body surface area was 40%. Sixty‐three percent of patients suffered an inhalation injury and 125 patients (33.3%) developed complications during hospitalization. The average mortality was 5.3%. Higher mortality rates are associated with advanced age, flame burns, larger full‐thickness burns area and total body surface area, inhalation injuries and combined burn complications. Severe burns often occur in middle‐aged men. Governments and companies need to improve the safety of living and working environments. It is essential to develop treatment strategies based on risk factors for burn deaths and improve the quality of medical care.

INTRODUCTION

To date, burns remain one of the major causes of injury worldwide. According to the World Health Organization, severe burns have a lifetime prevalence of 1%, and most burn‐related deaths occur in developing countries. 1 Severe burns may have systemic effects beyond the local area of injury due to the prolonged and intense immune and inflammatory response. This can lead to shock, sepsis and damage to multiple organs throughout the body. 2 Patients with severe burns are hospitalized for longer periods and require multiple surgical procedures. Some patients even need to be readmitted for reconstructive surgery or scar repair. 3 Non‐fatal severe burns cause hypertrophic scarring, physical disfigurement, disability and psychological trauma that can last a lifetime and often lead to stigmatization and social rejection. 4 , 5 Therefore, the implementation of appropriate strategies for the reduction of the burden of burn injuries is essential. Epidemiological data play a crucial role in identifying populations at risk and proposing appropriate preventive measures. At present, a nationwide burn injury database and resource base has not been established in our country as it has been done by the American Burn Association. 6 Several previous domestic studies have conducted epidemiological studies on patients with severe burns in different regions. 7 , 8 However, China is a vast country with wide variations in economic status, medical support and geosocial environment in different cities and regions. There is a dearth of epidemiological data on severe burn patients in Suzhou. This study was conducted at the Burns and Trauma Center of Suzhou to collect patients with severe burns during 2017–2022. This study aims to describe the epidemiological characteristics and clinical outcomes of severe burns in the Suzhou region.

METHOD

Patient selection

This study involved adult inpatients (≥18 years old) with severe burns hospitalized at the burn centre between 1 January 2017 and 30 December 2022. Severe burns included in our study were defined as (1) Total burn surface area (TBSA) >30%. (2) Full‐thickness burn area >10%. (3) The burn area does not meet the above standards but suffered shock, inhalation injury or other injury‐related complications. Patients with incomplete data or readmission were excluded. The severity of the burn area was evaluated using the Chinese rule‐of‐nine and rule‐of‐palms method. 9

Data collection

All of the data were collected by one of the investigators and checked by another researcher. A third proofreader participates in reviewing the data when errors are suspected. The electronic medical records of the hospital were used to extract data on age, gender, admission date, discharge date, burn aetiologies, burn depth, burn extent, inhalation injury, complications and patient outcomes. We included four different aetiologies in this study including flame, scalding, explosion and others (including chemical, hot crush injury, electricity and inhalation respiratory injury). According to the results of bronchoscopy, inhalation injuries are categorized as mild, moderate or severe. Patient outcomes were classified as cured, improved, invalid or deceased upon discharge from our centre. If a patient died before discharge, we recorded it as ‘deceased’. If the burn and trauma area was not decreased or deteriorated at discharge, we recorded it as ‘invalid’. If the burn and trauma area was reduced but not healed, we recorded it as ‘improved’. If the burn and trauma area had healed above 95%, we recorded it as ‘cured’. The patients were categorized into three age groups: 18–29, 30–59 and 60 years old and above. Scoring systems for burns, including the Burn Index (BI), the Prognostic Burn index (PBI), 10 The Abbreviated Burns Severity Index (ABSI) 11 and the modified Baux score 12 were calculated for all patients. For international comparisons, we calculated the lethal area 50 percent (LA50) for the following age groups: 18–44, 45–64 and >65.

Statistical analysis

Microsoft Excel (version 2021, Microsoft Corp., USA) and SPSS (version 26, IBM Corp., USA) were used to process and analyse the extracted data. Variables (aetiology, inhalation injury, complication, outcome, TBSA, full‐thickness burn area, BI, PBI, Baux and ABSI) were compared using the Chi‐square tests and non‐parametric test (Mann–Whitney U‐test or Kruskal–Wallis rank sum test). In addition, the LA50 is defined as the TBSA of burn with a 50% probability of death in the patient and was calculated in our study using a Probit model. Categorical variables were expressed as numbers (percentages). Continuous variables with a non‐normal distribution are presented as median (interquartile range, IQR) for statistical description. Receiver operating characteristic was conducted to evaluate the discrimination performance of BI, PBI, Baux and ABSI. The area under the curve was calculated. The Youden index was calculated to find the optimal cut‐off point and its corresponding sensitivity and specificity. p‐value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

General characteristic

This retrospective study analysed 375 adults with severe burns admitted to the burn centre of Suzhou Municipal Hospital from 1 January 2017 to 30 December 2022. On average, 62.5 severe burn patients were hospitalized per year (range 53–83). The study period showed an overall decreasing trend in the number of patients with severe burns (Table 1). Severe burns mainly affected the middle‐aged group of 30–59 years (62.13%), followed by the elder age groups (≥60 years old group, 24.27%) and the younger age groups (18–29 years old, 13.6%).

Inhalation injuries

In this study, 63% of the patients (n = 230) had inhalation injuries. Amongst them, 84.8% had facial burns and 55.2% performed tracheotomy. Amongst the patients with inhalation injuries, 61.7% (n = 142) had mild injuries, 10.9% (n = 25) had moderate injuries and 27.4% (n = 63) had severe injuries. The tracheotomy rates for the three groups were 40.8%, 66.0% and 85.7%, respectively. The mortality rates for these three groups were 2.1%, 12.0% and 15.9%, respectively. Patients with inhalation injury have a higher mortality rate (7.0%) compared to those without inhalation injury (1.7%) (Table 5). The TBSA, full‐thickness burns area and burn scores were markedly higher in subjects with inhalation injury and tracheotomy (Table 3).

Burn complications

A total of 125 individuals (33.3%) experienced complications during hospitalization in this study. Of them, 56.0% (70) developed burn shock, 11.2% (14) developed blood infections, 14.4% (18) developed organ failure and 40.8% (51) had a combination of two or more complications. Distributions of complications by gender, age, aetiologies and outcomes are shown in Table 6. Patients who developed complications had a significantly higher number of deaths (12.8%) compared to those without complications (1.6%). Explosion injuries had the highest number of patients who developed complications (58, 446.4%). Patients presenting with complications had significantly higher TBSA (60, IQR: 45) and full‐thickness burns area (15, IQR: 24.5) compared to those without complications (TBSA: 35, IQR: 15, full‐thickness burns area: 1.5, IQR: 10) (Table 3). The burn scores were significantly higher in patients who developed complications (ABSI: 11, Baux score: 130, BI: 36.5, PBI: 92) than in those who did not (ABSI: 8, Baux score: 94, BI: 20, PBI: 69.75, p < 0.001) (Table 3).

Outcomes and mortality

Amongst all the patients, 261 patients (69.9%) were cured and 60 patients (16.0%) showed improvement. In addition, 34 patients (9.1%) had transferred to other hospitals or discontinuation of treatment for personal reasons. Twenty patients died in the hospital, with a mortality rate of 5.3%, of whom 17 (6.6%) were male and 3 (2.5%) were female. The highest number of deaths occurred in the ≥60‐year‐old group, accounting for 85% of the deceased patients. In addition, compared with the cured and improved group, the deceased group had a larger TBSA and three‐thickness burn area, a higher rate of inhalation injury, tracheotomy and complications (Table 7). Patients in the dead group had significantly higher ABSI (11, IQR: 5.75), Baux score (135, IQR: 49.25), BI (37.75, IQR: 53.25) and PBI (109, IQR: 51.13) than the cured group (ABSI: 9, IQR: 4; Baux score: 98, IQR: 44; BI: 22.5, IQR: 20 and PBI: 73.50, IQR: 28.5) and improved group (ABSI: 8.5, IQR: 3; Baux score: 98, IQR: 30.25; BI: 20, IQR: 19.375; PBI: 70, IQR: 23.625) (Table 3). DISCUSSION

This study retrospectively analysed the epidemiological data and clinical outcomes of adult patients with severe burns admitted to Suzhou Hospital of Nanjing Medical University from 2017 to 2022. The study found that hospitalized patients with severe burns were mainly middle‐aged males (30–59 years old). These findings are consistent with previous studies. 13 , 14 , 15 It may be because middle‐aged males constitute the majority of the workforce in our productive industries and are therefore more exposed to hazardous work environments. The number of patients with severe burns had a decreasing trend after 2019. The primary reason may be attributed to the series of robust measures implemented by the Suzhou government to address fire safety concerns. In May 2018, the local government initiated the ‘331 Special Action for Improving Fire Hazards’ to address fire hazards in residential and workplace areas. 16 This suggests that burns, as an unintentional injury, can be reduced by mandatory local government interventions. Another reason may be due to the COVID‐19 pandemic in 2019 when many residents were isolated at home and factories were closed for rest. Studies in Japan 17 and Nigeria 18 have also reported reductions in burn admissions during the COVID‐19 pandemic.

In this study, severe burns could occur all year round but were most prevalent during the summer months. Located on the eastern coast of mainland China, Suzhou has a humid subtropical monsoon climate with four distinct seasons. The temperature is high during summer and dry and cold during winter. The seasonal distribution of patients of different genders exhibited a similar trend, which differed slightly from the findings of previous studies. Previous studies suggested that male burns showed a seasonal trend, whilst the seasonal trend of female burns did not change significantly. 14 , 19 This may be because more women are actively working outside the home than before. All age groups experienced an increase in burns during the summer season. The 30–59 age group showed a sharp increase, whilst the 18–29 age group showed a more moderate seasonal trend. The prevalence of burns was lowest amongst the 18–29 age group. This may be because many individuals in this age group are students or new to the workforce, and less likely to engage in hazardous work or household activities. The 30–59 age group constitutes the main labour force in society and is more likely to be involved in hazardous work. In developing China, many older individuals in good health are still engaged in hazardous fields, and the risk of occupational injuries remains high.

The study found that flame injuries were the major cause of severe burns, followed by explosion injuries, and scald injuries. Previous studies have reported varying results on the major cause of burns. Some of them have shown scald injuries to be the major cause, 8 , 19 , 20 whilst others have shown flame injuries to be the most common cause. 21 , 22 This discrepancy suggests that the aetiology of burns varies considerably across regions and populations. For female patients, the proportion of flame injuries and explosions was similar. It may be because female patients are more likely to suffer Cooking‐ and Cookstove‐Related Burn Injuries. 23 Explosions in Suzhou are mostly caused by gas explosions, which frequently occur in older neighbourhoods. Gas explosion injuries often occur in the summer months, when clothing is more revealing, often resulting in extensive burns. Furthermore, explosion injuries often affect multiple family members simultaneously, dealing a serious blow to their health and economic situation. This suggests that government departments must implement effective strategies to rehabilitate and maintain gas pipelines, as well as educate citizens on how to use them safely. Amongst severe patients, only 12% had a TBSA area of less than 20%, whilst 48% had a TBSA greater than 40% and 12.3% had a TBSA greater than 80%. The group of individuals aged 30–59 had a larger TBSA affected by burns. In addition to the elevated mortality rate, the subsequent functional recovery and scar repair of extensive burns necessitate a substantial investment of time and financial resources. This age group represents the majority of the workforce, heads of households and economic contributors in China. Severe burns in this age group can have a significant impact on the entire family. The key to preventing such incidents is to implement comprehensive aetiology‐based protection and security measures and enhance security awareness. It is of the utmost importance that governments and businesses play a pivotal role in the prevention of such accidental burns. Full‐thickness burns are a significant risk factor for mortality and are included in many burn score systems. Therefore, treating full‐thickness burns should be another therapeutic priority. The ABSI, modified Baux, PBI and BI were significantly higher in patients with explosion injuries, older age and concomitant comorbidities. These suggest that this population may present with a poorer prognosis and require aggressive and effective treatment strategies. The present study revealed that the PBI exhibited superior risk prediction accuracy in comparison to other scoring systems, which is consistent with a previous study. 14 The PBI is an easily calculable formula for determining the severity of burns and is widely used in Japan to predict the mortality of burn patients. 24 In clinical work, other burn score systems including BI, ABSI and modified Baux all have good prognostic accuracy for severe burn patients. 25 , 26

The study found that 61.3% of patients with severe burns suffered from inhalation injury. Whilst a hospital in Beijing reported a higher incidence of 69.14%, 14 most hospitals in China reported a lower incidence of 19.09%–32.38%. 27 It was even lower in some international studies. 28 , 29 It may be because of the high number of flame and explosion injuries in severe burn patients treated at our burn centre. In fires or explosions, patients who fail to escape in time are more likely to suffer inhalation injuries. Patients with inhalation injuries can develop acute upper airway obstruction, which significantly increases the mortality rate of burn patients. Of the 20 patients who died in this study, 17 had combined inhalation injuries. This indicates the importance of monitoring inhalation injury in burn patients and implementing active therapeutic measures. Currently, airway management, including early tracheotomy, is one of the important therapeutic measures for inhalation injury. Experts in China advocate that prophylactic tracheotomy should be performed early before the peak of tissue oedema in patients who are assessed to have the possibility of developing airway obstruction. 30 However, performing prophylactic tracheotomy remains controversial internationally and is not encouraged in some countries. 31 Although tracheal intubation is more convenient than establishing an artificial channel, tracheotomy is an emergency measure that can maintain the patient's airway patency and ensure patient safety when tracheal intubation is difficult. 30

The mortality rate in this study was 5.3%, which is lower than in many studies. 14 , 22 , 32 One possible reason may be due to the economic development and improvement of medical technology, patients with severe burns have received timely and standardized treatment. Furthermore, some patients may give up treatment and pass away at home due to advanced age or low level of family income, making it difficult to accurately calculate the mortality rate. In addition to the larger TBSA, full‐thickness burn areas, flame burns and inhalation injuries mentioned earlier, advanced age and burn complications are also risk factors for an increased risk of mortality. Elderly patients with reduced physical functioning and possible comorbid chronic systemic diseases such as hypertension, diabetes mellitus and heart disease before admission to the hospital are less likely to tolerate burns and have poorer relative treatment outcomes. 33 In the present study, severe burn complications reached 33.3% and the most common complications were shock, infection and organ failure. Many previous clinical epidemiological studies of burns did not include burn complications in the analysis. We found that patients with higher TBSA and full‐thickness burn areas were more likely to develop burn complications. Furthermore, burn scores (ABSI, Baux, BI and PBI) were markedly elevated in patients who developed complications, indicating a less favourable prognosis. A retrospective study at the Army Medical University showed that sepsis and organ failure were major causes of death in burn patients. 34 The judicious use of antibiotics is crucial for effective anti‐infective treatment. It is imperative that clinical practice focus not only on shock‐phase resuscitation but also on ameliorating infection and treating subsequent complications to reduce mortality in severe burn patients.

The reporting of data on LA50 in burn‐related epidemiologic studies in adults with burn injuries has been notably deficient over the past decade. This study is one of the few studies that used LA50 to assess the prognosis of adult burn patients. The results of this study indicate a statistically significant decline in the LA50 with advancing age. These findings are consistent with those of previous studies. 35 , 36 It indicates that age is a significant contributing factor to higher mortality rates and lower LA50 values. In developed countries, significant advancements in burn and intensive care management over the past few decades have led to the current LA50 values approaching 90%, 37 indicating a greater likelihood of survival for patients with extensive burns. However, all LA50 results of our study exceeded 100%. The value is considerably higher than in previous studies. 38 These results are difficult to interpret. It may be due to our insufficient number of fatal cases. The Burn Intensive Care Unit at our burn centre can accommodate patients with severe burns on time, allowing them to receive specialized treatment and care. In our clinical practice, the treatment of a large burn area is not inherently challenging. Fatal patients are frequently associated with advanced age, severe inhalation injuries and complications such as sepsis and multi‐organ failure. Previous studies also confirmed that the LA50 of patients was significantly lower after major modifications to the intensive care of burn patients. 36 , 39 To improve the prognosis of burn patients, it is necessary to improve the quality of critical care management of critically ill burn patients.

Several limitations of this study need to be noted when analysing our findings. First, this retrospective study is based on a single centre, and therefore the findings may only partly reflect the epidemiologic characteristics in East China. In addition, a few patients were referred or dropped out of treatment due to cost or other personal reasons, which may have led to biased results. In addition, children were not included in this study because some of them were referred to the burns ward of the local children's hospital. Although there are some limitations, this study provides valuable data on the epidemiological characteristics and clinical management of severe burns in Suzhou over the past 6 years.

CONCLUSION

Severe burns often occur in middle‐aged men. Severe burns can be life‐threatening and may cost a lot of money in terms of treatment and rehabilitation. There is a need to enhance the safety modification of the production environment, increase safety protection measures, and raise workers' awareness of safe operation. In addition, it is important to strengthen community‐based measures to prevent burns in daily life. Most importantly, to meet the complex and specialized treatment needs of burned patients, it is essential to improve the level of medical care and treatment and rationalize the allocation of medical resources. During treatment, it is important to focus not only on the management of burn wounds but also on the timely management of burn‐induced respiratory injuries and complications.

Reformulations of Quadratic Programs for Lipschitz Continuity
Devansh R. Agrawal, Haejoon Lee, Dimitra Panagou

Abstract
Optimization-based controllers often lack regularity guarantees, such as Lipschitz continuity, when multiple constraints are present. When used to control a dynamical system, these conditions are essential to ensure the existence and uniqueness of the system’s trajectory. Here we propose a general method to convert a QP ( QP ) into a SOCP ( SOCP ), which is shown to be Lipschitz continuous. Key features of our approach are that (i) the regularity of the resulting formulation does not depend on the structural properties of the constraints, such as the linear independence of their gradients; and (ii) it admits a closed-form solution, which is not available for general QP with multiple constraints, enabling faster computation. We support our method with rigorous analysis and examples. [Code] 1 1 1 Code: https://github.com/joonlee16/Lipschitz-controllers

K:\mathcal{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is Lipschitz continuous and compact for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. If
\pi_{\rm f}(x)=\operatorname{St}(K(x))
, then
\pi_{\rm socp}
in (
16
) is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{X}
.

Note, even when
K
is Lipschitz, it is known that
\pi_{\rm qp}
as in (
1
) is only Hölder continuous (see
[
19
]
,
[
22
, Ex. 1.2]
). Our proposed
SOCP
yields Lipschitz continuous solutions.

III-C

A Conjecture

We now propose a convex
QCQP
(
QCQP
) that we conjecture is also Lipschitz continuous. Consider the following definition for
\Phi
:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\rm qcqp}(x)=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:h_{i}(x,u)\leq 0\}

(22)

where for any fixed
k>0
,

\displaystyle h_{i}(x,u)=\left\|u-\pi_{\rm f}(x)\right\|^{2}-2k\left(b_{i}(x)-a_{i}(x)^{\top}u\right).

(23)

Compare this with
g_{i}
in (
15
). There are three key differences: (i) the norm is squared in the first term, (ii) we introduced a tuning parameter
k>0
, (iii) the last term is
a_{i}(x)^{\top}u
, not
a_{i}(x)^{\top}\pi_{\rm f}(x)
. This changes the structure of
\Phi(x)
from an intersection of concentric balls centered at
\pi_{\rm f}(x)
to an intersection of non-concentric balls containing the point
\pi_{\rm f}(x)
. This design expands the feasible space and introduces a tuning parameter that allows the user to control the conservatism. By some algebra, the modified problem is

\displaystyle\operatorname*{argmin}_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\end{subarray}}\

\displaystyle\left\|u-\pi_{\rm des}(x)\right\|^{2}

(24a)

\displaystyle\operatorname*{s.t.}\

\displaystyle\left\|u-c_{i}(x)\right\|^{2}\leq d_{i}(x),

(24b)

where

\displaystyle c_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\pi_{\rm f}(x)-ka_{i}(x),

\displaystyle d_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=k^{2}+2k\left(b_{i}(x)-a_{i}(x)^{\top}\pi_{\rm f}(x)\right).

Conjecture 1
.

Let
1
,
2
hold and
k>0
. Then,

1.

there exists a unique minimizer of the optimization problem (
24
) for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Let
\pi_{\rm qcqp}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{U}
denote this solution.

2.

The solution satisfies
\pi_{\rm qcqp}(x)\in K(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

3.

The solution
\pi_{\rm qcqp}
is Lipschitz on
\mathcal{X}
.

Partial Proof.

[First Claim] Notice that since
b_{i}(x)-a_{i}(x)^{\top}\pi_{\rm f}(x)\geq 0
by
2
and
k>0
, we have
d_{i}(x)\geq k^{2}
. Since
\left\|a_{i}(x)\right\|=1
by
1
.1,
u=\pi_{\rm f}(x)
is a feasible solution. Since the objective is strictly convex, there always exists a unique solution to (
24
).

[Second Claim] Consider any feasible solution
u
. Then
h_{i}(x,u)\leq 0
. Then,
\left\|u-\pi_{\rm f}(x)\right\|^{2}\leq 2k(b_{i}(x)-a_{i}(x)^{\top}u)
, which can only be satisfied if
b_{i}(x)-a_{i}(x)^{\top}u\geq 0
. Since this is true for all
i\in\mathcal{J}
,
u\in K(x)
.

[Third Claim] Proving this remains an open question.
∎

Importantly, one can construct an example where if instead the constraints
\tilde{h}_{i}(x,u)=\left\|u-\pi_{\rm f}(x)\right\|-2k(b_{i}(x)-a_{i}(x)^{\top}u)
are used, the solution can be discontinuous. The square on the norm in (
23
) appears to be important.

V
CONCLUSIONS

In this letter, we propose a new framework for optimization-based control synthesis that ensures a locally Lipschitz continuous control law. To this end, we reformulate a given
QP
(
QP
), a common form of optimization-based controllers, into a
SOCP
(
SOCP
). This reformulation guarantees Lipschitz continuity without requiring specific structural assumptions on the constraint matrices and admits an analytical solution form. As future work, we aim to develop an extended framework that further relaxes assumptions and verifies the conjecture. We also plan on using these results in multi-agent optimization-based controllers.

Lemma 1
.

Let all assumptions in
Corollary

2
hold. Then, the function
\pi_{\rm f}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined by
\pi_{\rm f}(x)=\operatorname{Ac}(K(x))
is locally Lipschitz on
\mathcal{X}
, and
\pi_{\rm f}(x)\in K(x)
.

Proof.

Let
G(u,x)=-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\log(b_{i}(x)-a_{i}(x)u)
.
By
SC
,
K(x)
has a non-empty interior at each
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Hence, for
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
,
\bar{u}=\pi_{\rm f}(\bar{x})
is well-defined and
\bar{u}\in K(\bar{x})
. Since
\log(\cdot)
is smooth,

\displaystyle\nabla_{u}G(\bar{u},\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{a_{i}(\bar{x})}{b_{i}(\bar{x})-a_{i}(\bar{x})^{\top}\bar{u}}=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{uu}G(\bar{u},\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{a_{i}(\bar{x})^{\top}a_{i}(\bar{x})}{(b_{i}(\bar{x})-a_{i}(\bar{x})^{\top}\bar{u})^{2}}>0.

Both expressions are well defined since by
SC
,
\bar{u}
satisfies
a_{i}(\bar{x})^{\top}\bar{u}<b_{i}(\bar{x})
. Similar expressions can be obtained for
\nabla_{x}G
,
\nabla_{xx}G
,
\nabla_{ux}G
. Therefore
G\in C^{2}
, and by the implicit function theorem
[
8
, Theorem 4.2]
, for any
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
there exists an open neighborhood on which
\pi_{\rm f}
is continuously differentiable. Ergo
\pi_{\rm f}
is locally Lipschitz on
\mathcal{X}
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Consider the optimization problem

\displaystyle v(x)=\operatorname*{argmin}_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\end{subarray}}\left\|v-v_{d}(x)\right\|^{2}\ \operatorname*{s.t.}\ \left\|v\right\|\leq r(x)

where
v_{d}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
r:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
are Lipschitz continuous with constants
L_{v_{d}},L_{r}\geq 0
respectively. Then
v:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is also Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{v_{d}}+L_{r}
.

Proof.

For convenience, define
n(x)=\frac{v_{d}(x)}{\left\|v_{d}(x)\right\|}
, with the convention that if
\left\|v_{d}(x)\right\|=0,n(x)=0
.
Notice
v
can be expressed as

\displaystyle v(x)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Proj}_{r(x)}(v_{d}(x))=\min(r(x),\left\|v_{d}(x)\right\|)n(x)

(28)

where
\operatorname{Proj}_{r}(u)
denotes the projection of
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto the closed ball of radius
r\geq 0
centered at the origin.

We aim to prove that there exists a
L\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|v(x)-v(y)\right\|\leq L\left\|x-y\right\|

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
. Consider three cases:

Case 1
[
r(x)=r(y)=0
]:

Since
v(x)=v(y)=0
, the claim holds with
L=0
.

Case 2
[
r(x)=0,r(y)>0
]:

Since
v(x)=0
, and
v(y)
is as in (
28
),

\displaystyle\left\|v(x)-v(y)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\min(r(y),\left\|v_{d}(y)\right\|)\ n(y)\right\|

\displaystyle=\min(r(y),\left\|v_{d}(y)\right\|)\left\|n(y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\min(r(y),\left\|v_{d}(y)\right\|)

\displaystyle\leq r(y)=\left|r(x)-r(y)\right|

Hence the claim is true with
L=L_{r}
.

Case 3
[
r(x),r(y)>0
]:
Consider two subcases.

Case 3a
[
\left\|v_{d}(x)\right\|\leq r(x),\left\|v_{d}(y)\right\|\leq r(y)
]:

Here,
v(x)=v_{d}(x),v(y)=v_{d}(y)
, and hence

\displaystyle\left\|v(x)-v(y)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|v_{d}(x)-v_{d}(y)\right\|\leq L_{v_{d}}\left\|x-y\right\|

thus, the claim holds with
L=L_{v_{d}}
.

Case 3b
[
\left\|v_{d}(y)\right\|>r(y)
]:
Let
p=\operatorname{Proj}_{r(x)}(v_{d}(y))=r(x)n(y)
. Then,

\displaystyle\left\|v(x)-v(y)\right\|\leq\left\|v(x)-p\right\|+\left\|p-v(y)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\operatorname{Proj}_{r(x)}v_{d}(x)-\operatorname{Proj}_{r(x)}v_{d}(y)\right\|+\left\|r(x)n(y)-r(y)n(y)\right\|.

The first term on the right is bounded by
\left\|v_{d}(x)-v_{d}(y)\right\|
since the projection onto a closed convex set has Lipschitz constant 1. The second term is bounded by
\left|r(x)-r(y)\right|\left\|n(y)\right\|
. Since
\left\|n(y)\right\|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|v(x)-v(y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|v_{d}(x)-v_{d}(y)\right\|+\left|r(x)-r(y)\right|\left\|n(y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{v_{d}}\left\|x-y\right\|+L_{r}\left\|x-y\right\|.

Hence the claim is true with
L=L_{v_{d}}+L_{r}
.
∎

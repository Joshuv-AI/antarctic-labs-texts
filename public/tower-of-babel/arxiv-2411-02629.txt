Local-global principles for semi-integral points on Markoff orbifold pairs Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification 14G05, 14G12, 11D25 (primary), 11G35 (secondary).
Vladimir Mitankin, Justin Uhlemann

Abstract
. We study local-global principles for semi-integral points on orbifold pairs of Markoff type. In particular, we analyse when these orbifold pairs satisfy weak weak approximation, weak approximation and strong approximation off a finite set of places. We show that Markoff orbifold pairs satisfy the semi-integral Hasse principle and we measure how often such orbifold pairs have strict semi-integral points but the corresponding Markoff surface lacks integral points.

This work pursues a different direction by exploring for the first time Campana and Darmon points, collectively abbreviated semi-integral, on orbifold pairs defined by (

Local-global principles

To put things in perspective, consider the smooth compactification
X_{m}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{3}
of
U_{m}
, with a
\mathbb{Z}
-model
\mathcal{X}_{m}
defined by the same equation, given by

(1.2)

X_{m}:\quad x_{0}(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2})-x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}=mx_{0}^{3}.

The existence of a rational point
(0:0:1:1)\in X_{m}(\mathbb{Q})
implies that
X_{m}
is unirational
[
31
]
and that it satisfies weak weak approximation
[
52
]
. In fact,
X_{m}
is rational, and thus satisfies weak approximation, if and only if
m-4\in\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})^{*2}
by
[
33
, Lem. 3.3.]
, which is equivalent to
m-4\in\mathbb{Q}^{*2}
(see Remark
3.10
). The reverse implication also holds.

Theorem 1.1
.

The variety
X_{m}
satisfies weak approximation for
\mathbb{Q}
-rational points if and only if
X_{m}
is rational.

To set-up the notion of semi-integrality, let
D=X_{m}\setminus U_{m}
. Fixing weights
\omega_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}\cup\{\infty\}
for the irreducible components
D_{i}=\{x_{0}=x_{i}=0\}
,
i=1,2,3
of
D
defines a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D_{\uomega}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(1-1/\omega_{i})D_{i}
that gives rise to a Campana orbifold structure
(X_{m},D_{\uomega})
. If
\mathcal{D}_{i}
is the Zariski closure of
D_{i}
in
\mathcal{X}_{m}
for
i=1,2,3
and
\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(1-1/\omega_{i})\mathcal{D}_{i}
, the
\mathbb{Z}
-model
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
of
(X_{m},D_{\uomega})
carries the data of semi-integral points on the pair (Definition
2.7
). Selecting
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}=\omega_{3}=\infty
or an empty boundary divisor recovers integral points on
\mathcal{U}_{m}
and rational points on
X_{m}
, respectively. Our results go beyond the classical setting and consider the case where at least one
\omega_{i}<\infty
. The discrepancy in the behaviour of semi-integral and rational points is observed in the next result.

Theorem 1.2
.

The following hold.

(i)

Assume that at least two of
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
are finite. Then each pair
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
fails weak weak approximation for semi-integral points.

(ii)

Assume that exactly one of
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
is finite. If
m-4\in\mathbb{Q}^{*2}
, then
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
satisfies weak approximation. On the other hand, if
m-4\notin\mathbb{Q}^{*2}
, then

(a)

(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
fails Campana weak approximation;

(b)

(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
fails Darmon weak approximation if the finite weight is even.

Theorem
1.2
(i) follows from a result about weak approximation for general orbifold pairs, namely Theorem
2.15
. To the best of our knowledge this is the first result on local-global principles for Campana orbifolds with multiple irreducible components of the boundary.

Remark 1.3

Let

fails strong approximation off any finite set

Strict points

Integral points are better approximated by strict semi-integral points
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})=(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{Z})\cap U_{m}(\mathbb{Q})

A powerful tool to study local-global principles is the Brauer–Manin obstruction. Its semi-integral version has been defined in
[

along with its relation to its rational and integral counterparts. As is evident from Remark

such obstructions to the Hasse principle are not present in the family
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
as its non-strict part vanishes. We perform a complete analysis of the values of the local invariant maps for the relevant Brauer elements in §

to examine this obstruction for strict semi-integral points.

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume that
\omega_{i}<\infty
for some
i=1,2,3
. Then no algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction to the Hasse principle for strict points on
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
is present. Moreover, if

(
\ast

-(m-4)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}\implies\frac{\sqrt{m}+\sqrt{m-4}}{2}\notin\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m},\sqrt{m-4})^{2}

is met, then there is no Brauer–Manin obstruction to the Hasse principle for strict semi-integral points on
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
.

Assume that one of

The quantification of

2.
Local-global principles for semi-integral points

In this section, we recall the notions of orbifold pairs defined by Campana
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, semi-integral points, semi-integral local-global principles and their Brauer–Manin obstruction as defined in
[
39
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A (Campana) orbifold or an orbifold pair over
k
is a pair
(X,D_{\uomega})
, where
X
is a smooth proper variety over
k
and
D_{\uomega}
is a
k
-rational
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
of the shape

D_{\uomega}=\sum_{i\in I}\left(1-\frac{1}{\omega_{i}}\right)D_{i}.

Here
I
is a finite (possibly empty) indexing set of distinct irreducible effective (Weil) divisors
D_{i}
of
X
,
\uomega=(\omega_{i})_{i\in I}
such that
\omega_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}\cup\{\infty\}
. We call
\omega_{i}
the weight of
D_{i}
. The support of
D_{\uomega}
is
D_{\red}=\cup_{i\in I}D_{i}
. We say that
(X,D)
is smooth if
D_{\red}
is a strict normal crossings divisor.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that, in comparison with
[
39
]
, we require a finite indexing set of irreducible divisors for
D_{\uomega}
and so instead of
\omega_{i}\geq 1
, we have
\omega_{i}\geq 2
. This is only a cosmetic change. The notation in
[
39
]
was motivated from the general theory viewpoint and by the assumption that one starts with a projective variety
X
and selects a boundary divisor
D
on it. The notion of integrality depends on the boundary divisor. The current set-up is more suitable for generalising questions about integral points on affine varieties, in which case the boundary has to stay fixed but the weights may vary.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(X,D_{\uomega})
be an orbifold pair. Fix a finite set
S\subset\Omega_{k}
containing
\Omega_{\infty}
. Consider a flat, proper scheme
\mathcal{X}
of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{S}
, with an isomorphism
\mathcal{X}_{(0)}\cong X
for the generic fibre of
\mathcal{X}
. Denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}
the divisor
\sum_{i\in I}(1-1/\omega_{i})\mathcal{D}_{i}
, where
\mathcal{D}_{i}
for each
i\in I
is the Zariski closure of
D_{i}
in
\mathcal{X}
along that isomorphism. The pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
is called an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model
of
(X,D_{\underline{\omega}})
.

Remark 2.4
.

Notice that, in contrast to the construction given by the authors in
[
46
]
, we also allow
\mathcal{X}
to be irregular. Allowing irregularity gives us more freedom in choosing the set
S
of finite places which define our
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model. This also turned out to be useful in the setting of counting Campana points of bounded height, as discussed in
[
1
, §3]
.

2.1.
Semi-integral points

Let
(X,D_{\uomega})
be an orbifold pair with a
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
. Any
M\in X(k)
lifts to a point
M_{v}\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
for all
v\in\Omega_{K}\setminus S
by the valuative criterion for properness
[
25
, Theorem 7.3.8.]
.
For any
\mathcal{D}_{i}\subset\mathcal{X}
, we want to quantify the position of
M_{v}\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
with respect to
\mathcal{D}_{i}
at all places
v\in\Omega_{K}\setminus S
, by assigning it a value in the following way. Consider the fibre product of the two morphisms given by the closed immersion
\mathcal{D}_{i}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
and by the local point
M_{v}:\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{v})\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
.
Since closed immersions are stable under base change
[
27
, 3.11.a]
we know that
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{v})\times_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{D}_{i}
is a closed subscheme of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
. Every closed subscheme of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
is of the shape
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{v}/I)
for an ideal
I\subset\mathcal{O}_{v}
. As
\mathcal{O}_{v}
is a discrete valuation ring with uniformiser
\pi_{v}
, say, every non-zero ideal is of the shape
I=(\pi_{v}^{n})
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(X,D_{\uomega})
be an orbifold pair with a
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
. If
M_{v}\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
with
v\in\Omega_{k}\setminus S
, define

n_{v}(\mathcal{D}_{i},M_{v})\coloneq\begin{cases}\infty&\text{if }I=(0)\text{ (or equivalently, }M_{v}\in D_{i}(k_{v})),\\
n&\text{if }I=(\pi_{v}^{n}).\end{cases}

We call
n_{v}(\mathcal{D}_{i},M_{v})
the
intersection multiplicity
of
M_{v}
with
\mathcal{D}_{i}
.

As we shall soon see, if
\omega_{i}=\infty
for some
D_{i}
in
D_{\red}
, any semi-integral point must be away from that
D_{i}
, i.e. its intersection multiplicity with
\mathcal{D}_{i}
must be 0. This motivates the need of the following definition.

Definition 2.6
.

Set
D_{\inf}:=\bigcup_{\omega_{i}=\infty}D_{i}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\inf}:=\bigcup_{\omega_{i}=\infty}\mathcal{D}_{i}
. Analogously, for each
i\in I
, set
D_{i,\inf}:=D_{i}\cap D_{\inf}
and
\mathcal{D}_{i,\inf}:=\mathcal{D}_{i}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\inf}
as well as
D_{i,\fin}:=D_{i}\setminus D_{i,\inf}
and
\mathcal{D}_{i,\fin}:=\mathcal{D}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{i,\inf}
. Finally, set
D_{\fin}:=D_{\red}\setminus D_{\inf}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\fin}:=\mathcal{D}_{\red}\setminus\bigcup_{\omega_{i}=\infty}\mathcal{D}_{i}
.

We are now in position to define Campana points and Darmon points. We collectively abbreviate them as semi-integral points, meaning that one may replace the words “semi-integral” with either ”Campana” or “Darmon” to obtain valid statements that are independent of the semi-integral notion chosen.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(X,D_{\uomega})
be an orbifold pair with a
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
. For any
v\in\Omega_{k}
define the set of
v
-adic (or local) semi-integral points, denoted
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
, by

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{v})=\begin{cases}(X\setminus D_{\inf})(k_{v})&\text{if }v\in S,\\
\{M_{v}\in(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\inf})(\mathcal{O}_{v})\ :\ n_{v}(\mathcal{D}_{i},M_{v})\text{ admissible if }\omega_{i}\neq\infty\}&\text{if }v\notin S,\end{cases}

where the additional admissibility condition on
n_{v}(\mathcal{D}_{i},M_{v})
for
v\in\Omega_{k}\setminus S
is given by:

•

n_{v}(\mathcal{D}_{i},M_{v})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq\omega_{i}}\cup\{0,\infty\}
for Campana points;

•

n_{v}(\mathcal{D}_{i},M_{v})\in\omega_{i}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
for Darmon points.

We say that
M\in X(k)

is a (global) semi-integral point on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
if
M
is a
v
-adic semi-integral on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
for all
v\in\Omega_{K}
. We denote the set of (global) semi-integral points by
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
.

If we want to specify which of the two notions we are working with, we shall substitute
*=\cc
for Campana points and
*=\dd
for Darmon points.

Remark 2.8
.

In view of the above definition, any global semi-integral point
M
must be an
S
-integral point of
\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\inf}
and hence
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\subset(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\inf})(\mathcal{O}_{S})
.

Remark 2.9
.

From the definition of Campana, and of Darmon points, these two notions of semi-integral points only differ at how they intersect with the orbifold divisor. In fact, it is clear from the definition that

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{\dd}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\subset(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{\cc}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{\dd}(\mathcal{O}_{v})\subset(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{\cc}(\mathcal{O}_{v}).

Furthermore, the connection between Campana points and
m
-full numbers, and between Darmon points and
m
-th powers is now apparent in view of the following (for which a detailed explanation can be found at
[
1
, Example 1]
). If an open subset
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{X}
is given such that
M_{v}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
and
\mathcal{D}_{i}
is locally defined by a rational function
f
, which is regular on
\mathcal{U}
, then
n_{v}(\mathcal{D}_{i},M_{v})=v_{v}(f(M_{v}))
, where
v_{v}
denotes the
v
-adic valuation of
\mathcal{O}_{v}
.

Remark 2.10
.

A generalisation of the semi-integrality notion, termed
\mathcal{M}
-points, was recently introduced in
[
42
]
. The main idea behind it is to allow more flexibility for the set of admissible
n_{v}(\mathcal{D}_{i},M_{v})
in Definition
2.7
. This new notion features some interesting examples of pairs whose global points are of particular interest to the number theory community.

2.2.
Adelic space and local-global principles

Given a Campana orbifold
(X,D_{\uomega})
, set
U=X\setminus D_{\red}
and
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{X}\setminus\cup_{i\in I}\mathcal{D}_{i}
. As explained in
[
39
]
, to define an adelic space in a “minimal” way, we need to distinguish between semi-integral points away from the boundary divisor and those of them lying on
D_{\fin}
. Another reason to partition
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
into a
U
piece and a
D_{\fin}
piece, and study those pieces separately, is that points on the
D_{\fin}
piece come from a lower dimensional variety and generally may behave differently, especially when it comes to local-global principles. This is illustrated Theorem
2.15
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
(X,D_{\uomega})
be an orbifold pair with a
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
. We define by

\begin{split}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})&\coloneq(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\cap U(k),\\
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})&\coloneq(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{S}),\end{split}

the sets of
strict
and of
non-strict
semi-integral points on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
, respectively. Thus the set of global semi-integral points may be partitioned as the disjoint union

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\coprod(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S}).

We define the strict
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{v})=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})(\mathcal{O}_{v})\cap U(k_{v})
and non-strict
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})(\mathcal{O}_{v})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
local points in an analogous way, therefore giving a partitioning for local points

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{v})=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{v})\coprod(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{v}).

The next natural object to look at is the semi-integral adelic space. We shall work with the definitions in
[
39
, §2]
which are well-suited to examine obstructions to local-global principles. We recall the definitions of the semi-integral adelic space, of the Hasse principle, of weak and of strong approximation below.

Definition 2.12
.

Given a finite set
T\subset\Omega_{k}
, we define the sets of
strict
T
-adelic semi-integral points

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T})
and
non-strict
T
-adelic semi-integral points

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T})
of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
by

\begin{split}\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)_{\str}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T}\right)&:=\prod_{v\in S\setminus(S\cap T)}U(k_{v})\times\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{v\in\Omega_{K}\setminus(S\cup T)}\left(\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{v}\right)\cap U(k_{v}),\mathcal{U}\left(\mathcal{O}_{v}\right)\right),\\
\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)_{\ns}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T}\right)&:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
\omega_{i}\neq\infty\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{v\in S\setminus(S\cap T)}D_{i,\fin}(k_{v})\times\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}\setminus(S\cup T)}\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{v}\right)\cap\mathcal{D}_{i,\fin}\left(\mathcal{O}_{v}\right)\right).\end{split}

We equip
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T})
with the restricted product topology, while
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T}\right)
is considered as a subset of
\prod_{v\in S\setminus(S\cap T)}D_{\fin}(k_{v})\times\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}\setminus(S\cup T)}\mathcal{D}_{\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
whose product topology it inherits. We define the set of
T
-adelic semi-integral points
as the disjoint union

\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T}\right):=\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)_{\str}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T}\right)\coprod\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)_{\ns}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T}\right),

endowed with the coproduct topology, which we call the
adelic topology
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}^{T}_{k,S})
.

We define the
adelic semi-integral points
to be the
T
-adelic semi-integral points for
T=\emptyset
and denote them by
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S})
. We define the sets of
strict
and
non-strict adelic semi-integral points
analogously. We omit
S
when it is clear from context.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a collection of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-models of Campana orbifolds. We say that
\mathscr{F}
satisfies the
Hasse principle for semi-integral points
if the following implication holds for all
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})\in\mathscr{F}
:

\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}\right)\neq\emptyset\implies\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{S}\right)\neq\emptyset.

In a similar fashion, the Hasse principle may be defined for strict or non-strict semi-integral points by restricting to the desired component of the adelic space. In fact, for the main example of orbifold pairs in this work the strict semi-integral Hasse principle will turn out to be of a more interesting nature.

Recall that Definition
2.12
ensures that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T})
. This leads to the following definitions of weak and of strong approximation.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
be an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model of a Campana orbifold
(X,D_{\uomega})
, and let
T
be a finite set of places of
k
.

(i)

We say that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
satisfies
weak approximation (for semi-integral points) off
T
(abbreviated
WA off
T
) if
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S\cup T})
is dense in
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T})
for the product topology. We say that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
satisfies
weak weak approximation (for semi-integral points)
(abbreviated
WWA
) if it satisfies WA off some finite set of places
T
. We say that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
satisfies
weak approximation (for semi-integral points)
(abbreviated
WA
) if it satisfies WA off
T=\emptyset
.

(ii)

We say that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
satisfies
strong approximation (for semi-integral points) off
T
(abbreviated
SA
off
T
) if
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S\cup T})
is dense in
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T})
for the adelic topology. We say that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
satisfies
strong approximation (for semi-integral points)
(abbreviated
SA
) if it satisfies SA off
T=\emptyset
.

We extend the above definitions to strict and non-strict semi-integral points by restricting the strict or non-strict adelic space, respectively.

We conclude with the status of weak weak approximation. Comparing the following theorem to
[
45
, Theorem 1.3]
shows how the different definitions of adeles can lead to different results on the approximation properties of general orbifold pairs. We refer to
[
42
, Appendix A]
for a detailed comparison.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
be an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model of a Campana orbifold
(X,D_{\uomega})
. Assume that there exists at least two distinct irreducible
D_{i},D_{j}\subset D_{\red}
of finite weights such that
D_{i,\fin}
and
D_{j,\fin}
are geometrically integral. Then
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
fails WWA.

Proof.

Let
D_{i},D_{j}
be as in the statement. Select
(M_{v})\in(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S})
such that there are infinitely many
M_{v}\in\mathcal{D}_{i,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v})\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq i}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
and infinitely many
M_{v}\in\mathcal{D}_{j,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v})\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq j}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
. Such
(M_{v})
exists as
D_{i,\fin}
and
D_{j,\fin}
are geometrically integral, their respective intersections with
D_{\fin}
minus the respective component are of positive codimension and thus the Lang–Weil estimates and Hensel’s lemma imply that the above sets are non-empty for all sufficiently large
v
. Chose a finite
T\subset\Omega_{K}
. Then clearly,
(M_{v})_{v\notin T}
has infinitely many components coming from
D_{i,\fin}
and infinitely many coming from
D_{j,\fin}
as above and all of them are away from their respective intersections with
D_{\fin}
minus the respective component. It follows from Definition
2.14
that
(M_{v})_{v\notin T}
may only be approximated by a non-strict semi-integral global point. Let
S^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{k}\setminus T
be finite and such that there are
v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\in S^{\prime}
for which
M_{v^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{D}_{i,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}})\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq i}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}})
and
M_{v^{\prime\prime}}\in\mathcal{D}_{j,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime\prime}})\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq j}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime\prime}})
. Chose
\varepsilon>0
less than the minimum of the
v^{\prime}
-adic distance between
M_{v^{\prime}}
and
\bigcup_{k\neq i}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}})
and less than the
v^{\prime\prime}
-adic distance between
M_{v^{\prime\prime}}
and
\bigcup_{k\neq j}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime\prime}})
. If there were
M\in(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
for which
|M-M_{v}|_{v^{\prime}}<\varepsilon
, then as
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\subset(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}})
one must have
M\in(\mathcal{D}_{i,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v})\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq i}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{v}))\cap(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{S})=\mathcal{D}_{i,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq i}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
. Similarly, as
|M-M_{w}|_{v^{\prime\prime}}<\varepsilon
, then as any such
M
has to lie in
\mathcal{D}_{j,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq j}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
. But the intersection of
\mathcal{D}_{i,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq i}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
and
\mathcal{D}_{j,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq j}\mathcal{D}_{k,\fin}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
is clearly empty as
i\neq j
. Therefore WA off
T
fails. As
T
is arbitrary, we conclude that WWA fails for
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
, which completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
The semi-integral Brauer–Manin obstruction

Let
V
be a smooth but not necessarily proper variety over
k
with an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model
\mathcal{V}
. We begin by recalling the classical definition of the Brauer–Manin obstruction for rational points given by Manin
[
37
]
. Its version for integral points was defined by Colliot-Thélène and Xu
[
19
]
in a similar fashion. Our main reference for the classical theory is
[
16
]
.

Definition 2.16
.

The Brauer group of
V
is the second étale cohomology group
\Br V:=\HH^{2}_{\et}(V,\mathbb{G}_{m})
. Let
\Br_{1}V:=\ker(\Br V\rightarrow\Br V_{\bar{k}})
. The group
\Br_{1}V
will be referred to as the
algebraic Brauer group of
V
, while
\Br V/\Br_{1}V
is the
transcendental Brauer group of
V
.

There is a canonical injective homomorphism
\inv_{v}:\Br k_{v}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
for any
v\in\Omega_{k}

[
16
, Def. 13.1.7]
, whose image is
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
if
v
is a real place,
0
if it is a complex place and is an isomorphism if
v
is finite
[
16
, Thm. 13.1.8]
.

Given
\alpha\in\Br V
, there exists a finite set of places
S_{\alpha}\subseteq\Omega_{k}
, such that
\inv_{v}\alpha(\cdot):V(k_{v})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
vanishes on
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
for all
v\not\in S_{\alpha}

[
16
, Prop. 13.3.1]
. This shows that the Brauer–Manin pairing, as given below, is well defined.

\begin{split}V(\mathbb{A}_{k})\times\Br V&\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},\\
\left((M_{v}),\alpha\right)&\longmapsto\sum_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\inv_{v}(\alpha(M_{v})).\end{split}

The Brauer–Manin set, denoted
V(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\Br}
or
V(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\Br V}
if one wants to emphasise on the Brauer group used, is defined as the left kernel of that paring. Since the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(\mathbb{A}_{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Br k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus\limits_{v\in\Omega_{K}}\Br k_{v}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha(\cdot)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha(\cdot)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum_{v}\inv_{v}}

where exactness of the bottom row is implied by the Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem and class field theory, there is a chain of inclusions
V(k)\subseteq V(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\Br}\subseteq V(\mathbb{A}_{k})
. It gives an obstruction to the existence of
k
-rational points on
V
and to their density in
V(\mathbb{A}_{k})
. If
B\subset\Br V
, define the intermediate obstruction set
V(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{B}
as the set of adeles that pair to zero with all elements of
B
. An algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction refers to selecting
B=\Br_{1}V
and similarly for a transcendental Brauer–Manin obstruction.

We continue with the Brauer–Manin obstruction for semi-integral points as introduced in
[
39
, §3]
. Recall that for a Campana orbifold
(X,D_{\uomega})
we have set
U=X\setminus D_{\red}
.

Definition 2.17
.

Define the strict semi-integral Brauer–Manin set
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S})^{\Br}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S})^{\Br U}
as the right kernel of the Brauer–Manin pairing
\Br U\times U(\mathbb{A}_{k})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
when restricted to strict semi-integral points. Define the non-strict semi-integral Brauer–Manin set
\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)_{\ns}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}\right)^{\Br}
as the preimage of
\bigcup_{\omega_{i}\neq\infty}D_{i,\fin}(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\Br D_{i,\fin}}
under the natural inclusion
\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}\right)_{\ns}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}\right)\hookrightarrow\bigcup_{\omega_{i}\neq\infty}D_{i,\fin}(\mathbb{A}_{k})
. Then define the semi-integral Brauer–Manin set as the disjoint union

\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}\right)^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}\right)^{\Br}:=\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}\right)_{\str}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}\right)^{\Br}\coprod\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}\right)_{\ns}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}\right)^{\Br}.

To study local-global principles, it will be useful to work with the projections of the various Brauer–Manin sets to the
T
-adeles.

Definition 2.18
.

We define
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})^{\Br}
to be the projection of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T})^{\Br}
to
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})
and similarly for strict, non-strict, integral and rational points.

We shall be particularly interested in intermediate obstruction sets for strict points. Given a subset
B\subset\Br U
, we can define an intermediate obstruction set
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S})^{B}
by restricting the Brauer–Manin pairing to only
B
for the strict points. On taking projections away from
T
as above, we get a sequence of inclusions

\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)_{\str}^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{S\cup T}\right)\subset\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)_{\str}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T}\right)^{\Br}\subset\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)_{\str}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T}\right)^{B}\subset\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}\right)_{\str}^{*}\left(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T}\right).

Therefore the Brauer–Manin obstruction and its different partial forms can obstruct the local-global principles for semi-integral points, as well as for strict and non-strict semi-integral points. This is explained in detail in the next definitions following
[
39
, §3]
.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
(X,D_{\uomega})
be a Campana orbifold over a
k
with
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
. There is a
Brauer–Manin obstruction to the semi-integral Hasse principle
if

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S})\neq\emptyset\quad\text{but}\quad(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S})^{\Br}=\emptyset.

Otherwise, there is no Brauer–Manin obstruction to the semi-integral Hasse principle. A Brauer–Manin obstruction to the Hasse principle for strict points and for non-strict points is defined analogously by restricting to the strict adeles and to the non-strict adeles, respectively.

For a finite subset
T\subset\Omega_{K}
let
\overline{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})^{\Br}}
and
\overline{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})^{\Br}}
denote the closures of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})^{\Br}
and
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})^{\Br}
in the product topology, respectively. Let also

\overline{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})^{\Br}}=\overline{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})^{\Br}}\coprod\overline{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\ns}^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})^{\Br}}.

We may now recall the Brauer–Manin obstruction to semi-integral weak approximation and to semi-integral strong approximation.

Definition 2.20
.

Let
(X,D_{\uomega})
be a Campana orbifold over
k
with
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-model
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
.

(i)

We say that there is a
Brauer–Manin obstruction to weak approximation off
T
for semi-integral points
if

\overline{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})^{\Br}}\neq(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T}).

(ii)

We say that there is a
Brauer–Manin obstruction to strong approximation off
T
for semi-integral points
if

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S\cup T}^{T})^{\Br}\neq(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})^{*}(\mathbb{A}_{k,S}^{T}).

We shall omit “off
T
” in each definition above if
T=\emptyset
. Alternatively, if the above assumptions are not satisfied we say that there is no Brauer–Manin obstruction to weak or to strong approximation for semi-integral points, respectively. A Brauer–Manin obstruction to weak weak approximation as well as for weak and for strong approximation for strict and for non-strict semi-integral points is defined in a similar way by restricting the above definitions to strict or non-strict points, respectively.

We now turn our attention to the central objects of interest in this paper, namely the Markoff orbifold pairs

Semi-integral points

The next proposition gives an explicit arithmetic description of the set of semi-integral points on

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

be a finite prime. If
M_{p}=(x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}:x_{3})\in\mathcal{X}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
with

not all divisible by

, then

for
i=1,2,3
. In particular,

(i)

M_{p}
is a local Campana point on
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
if

\min\{\valp(x_{0}),\valp(x_{i})\}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq\omega_{i}}\cup\{0,\infty\},\quad i=1,2,3;

(ii)

M_{p}
is a local Darmon point on
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})
if

\min\{\valp(x_{0}),\valp(x_{i})\}\in\omega_{i}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\},\quad i=1,2,3.

Chose coordinates
\mathcal{X}_{m}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}}(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
and consider one of the three components of
\mathcal{D}_{\uomega}
,
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{y_{0}=y_{1}=0\}
say. If
M_{p}\in D_{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, then on one hand
x_{0}=x_{1}=0
, while on the other

, which confirms the claim. Assume now that
M_{p}\notin D_{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and let us compute the local intersection multiplicity from Definition
2.5
. We may apply the base change
\mathcal{X}_{m,\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
since the question is of a local nature:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M_{p}\times_{\mathcal{X}_{m,\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}\mathcal{D}_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Proj}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]}{(y_{0},y_{1})}\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Spec}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]}{(y_{0}-x_{0},y_{1}-x_{1},y_{2}-x_{2},y_{3}-x_{3})}\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{X}_{m,\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}}

, by our assumptions on

we can find

is contained in the affine open

(M_{p})
belongs to the Brauer–Manin set verifying its non-emptiness. The fact that the transcendental Brauer group of
U_{m}
is trivial under the arithmetic condition on
m
given in the statement of Theorem
1.6
follows from
[
33
, Cor. 4.3]
.
∎

4.
Existence of semi-integral points on Markoff pairs

We will prove in this section our main results on the existence of families of Markoff orbifold pairs with strict semi-integral points for arbitrary weights which do not come from integral points. The main idea is to use the theory of binary quadratic forms over
\mathbb{Z}
in connection with classical results from algebraic number theory. We refer to
[
14
]
for details on the theory of binary forms and their genus theory. For
D\in\mathbb{Z},D\equiv 0,1\bmod 4
let
\Cl(D)
be the class group of integral binary quadratic forms of discriminant
D
given by
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-equivalence, and let
\Cl^{+}(D)
for the corresponding narrow form class group given by
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-equivalence. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{G}(D)\coloneq\Cl^{+}(D)/\Cl^{+}(D)^{2}
be the associated genus group. Classical results imply that all these groups are always finite. Furthermore, for a fundamental discriminant
D
the group
\Cl^{+}(D)
is isomorphic to the narrow class group
\Cl^{+}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))
(See for example
[
7
, Thm 6.20]
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that one of
\omega_{i}
is finite. If
m=6d
with
d\equiv 29\bmod 78
and the reduction mod 13 of each prime divisor of
m-121
lies in
R=\{1,3,4,9,10,12\}=(\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^{\times 2}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, then
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})\neq\emptyset
but
\mathcal{U}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\omega_{1}<\infty
. Under our assumptions
m\equiv 3\bmod 9
and therefore
\mathcal{U}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})=\emptyset
by
[
24
, Prop. 6.1]
. In view of Proposition
3.2
and Remark
3.3
, we shall be looking at
M\in(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})\setminus\mathcal{U}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})
of the shape
(3^{\omega_{1}},11\cdot 3^{\omega_{1}},y,z)
with
3\nmid yz
. Let
n=(m-121)3^{2\omega_{1}}
. Substituting
M
in (
1.2
) and dividing through
3^{\omega_{1}}
gives

y^{2}-11yz+z^{2}=n.

We claim that
f(y,z)=y^{2}-11yz+z^{2}
of discriminant
117
represents
n
primitively over
\mathbb{Z}
. The indefinite form
f
represents
n
over
\mathbb{R}
. On the other hand, the congruence condition on
d
guaranties the existence of a primitive representation of
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{13}
, while so does the restriction on the prime divisors of
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for all remaining primes dividing
n
. We may now apply
[
14
, Thm 5.1, p. 143]
, which confirms that some form in the genus of
f
represents
n
primitively over
\mathbb{Z}
. The total number of genera
\#\mathcal{G}(117)
is equal to two, hence
\mathcal{G}(117)\cong\Cl^{+}(117)
. Therefore, all forms in the genus of
f
are properly equivalent to each other, and
f
properly represents
n
, as claimed. Finally, it remains to show that
3\nmid yz
. Indeed, if
3\mid y
, then as
3\mid n
we must have
3\mid z
, which contradicts the primitiveness of the representation. Similarly, if
3\mid z
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We are now in position to prove Theorem
1.7
. To do so it suffices to evaluate
S(B)
, which counts the number of
m
as in Proposition
4.1
, whose absolute value is at most
B
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
S(B)
be as above. We claim that there is a real constant
c>0
, such that

(4.1)

S(B)=c\frac{B}{(\log B)^{1/2}}+O\left(\frac{B}{\log B}\right).

To simplify what follows, let

\rho(n)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }p\mid n\implies p\bmod 13\in R,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
n=6d-121
. This is equivalent to
n\equiv 5\bmod 6
. The conditions of Proposition
4.1
imply that all prime divisors of
n
must lie in
R
after reduction mod 13 and
n=6d-121\equiv 53\bmod 78
. The latter congruence clearly implies
n\equiv 5\bmod 6
and therefore

S(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq B-121\\
n\equiv 53\bmod 78\end{subarray}}\rho(n)=\frac{1}{24}\sum_{\chi\bmod 78}\chi(53)T_{\chi}(B)

by the orthogonality of Dirichlet characters mod 78 and the fact that
53
is its own inverse mod 78. Here for a Dirichlet character
\chi
mod 78 we have defined

T_{\chi}(B)=\sum_{n\leq B-121}\rho(n)\chi(n).

The treatment of
T_{\chi}(B)
can be done using the Landau-Selberg-Delange method. Let
\chi_{0}
be the trivial Dirichlet character mod 78 and denote by
\chi_{1}
the Dirichlet character mod 78 given by
\left(\frac{\cdot}{13}\right)\chi_{0}(\cdot)
. We claim that there is another real constant
c^{\prime}>0
, such that

(4.2)

T_{\chi}(B)=\begin{cases}c^{\prime}B(\log B)^{-1/2}+O\left(B(\log B)^{-1}\right)&\text{if }\chi=\chi_{0}\text{ or }\chi=\chi_{1},\\
O\left(B(\log B)^{-1}\right)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This verifies (
4.1
) with
c=c^{\prime}/12
as
\chi_{1}(53)=1
.

Let
F(s,\chi)
be the Dirichlet series corresponding to
T_{\chi}(B)
, which is well-defined for
\sigma>1
under the standard notation
s=\sigma+i\tau
. Define
\psi(\cdot)=\left(\frac{\cdot}{13}\right)\chi(\cdot)
. Since
R=(\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^{\times 2}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, by the binomial series expansion we conclude that
F(s,\chi)
is given by

\begin{split}F(s,\chi)&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p\bmod 13\in R}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\\
&=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\left(\frac{p}{13}\right)\right)\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\\
&=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1/2}\left(1-\frac{\psi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1/2}E_{p}(s)=L(s,\chi)^{1/2}L(s,\psi)^{1/2}E(s),\end{split}

where the function
E(s)=\prod_{p}E_{p}(s)
satisfies
E_{p}(s)=1+O(p^{-2\sigma})
.

Note that
|\rho(n)\chi(n)|\leq\rho(n)
and
L(s,\chi_{0})=\zeta(s)(1-1/2^{s})(1-1/3^{s})(1-1/13^{s})
. It is now clear that
F(s,\chi)
satisfies the hypothesis of
[
53
, Thm. II.5.2]
with
N=0
, and with
z=w=1/2
if
\chi=\chi_{0},\chi_{1}
and
z=0
,
w=1/2
for the remaining characters mod 78. Indeed, this is verified by
[
43
, Thm. 11.3, p. 360]
and
[
43
, Thm. 11.4, p. 362]
as
L(s,\chi_{0}\left(\frac{\cdot}{13}\right))
and
L(s,\left(\frac{\cdot}{13}\right))
have no Siegel zeroes (eg. LMFDB), where
\left(\frac{\cdot}{13}\right)
is the quadratic character mod 13. This confirms (
4.2
) and completes the proof of Theorem
1.7
.
∎

Finally, we will show that the above idea also adapts to other families of Markoff orbifold pairs. As a corollary we provide more examples of pairs for which there are strict semi-integral points but no integral points.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p>2
be a prime such that
\Cl(p^{2}+4)
is trivial. Assume that one of
\omega_{i}
is finite. If
m
is of the form
(2+p^{2})^{2}\pm p^{\alpha}k^{2}
for some integers
\alpha\geq 0,k>0
with
p\nmid k
, then
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

We will show the existence of semi-integral points of the shape
(p^{\omega_{1}},(2+p^{2})p^{\omega_{1}},kx,ky)
with
p\nmid kxy
. These points belong to
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})
by Proposition
3.1
. Substituting such a point in (
1.2
) and dividing by
p^{\omega_{1}}
yields

x^{2}-(2+p^{2})xy+y^{2}=\pm p^{2\omega_{1}+\alpha}.

Let
f(x,y)=x^{2}-(2+p^{2})xy+y^{2}
be the binary quadratic form defined by the left hand side. We will show that
f
is properly equivalent to a quadratic form, which represents any
\pm p^{l}
with
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 4}
. The transformation
x\mapsto-z,y\mapsto w-z
is proper and maps
f
to
g(w,z)=w^{2}+p^{2}wz-p^{2}z^{2}
. It is clear, that any representation
g(w,z)=\pm p^{l}
must satisfy
p\mid w
. If
w=pt
it then suffices to show that
h(t,z)=t^{2}+ptz-z^{2}
properly represents
\pm p^{l-2}
. The form
h
properly represents
p^{l-2}
if and only if it properly represents
-p^{l-2}
, which may be seen for example from the proper transformation
t\mapsto z,z\mapsto-t
. The discriminant of
h
reduces is a square mod
p
. Applying Hensel’s lemma now shows that
\sqrt{p^{2}+4}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and therefore the congruence
t^{2}\equiv p^{2}+4\pmod{p^{l-2}}
is soluble. By
[
20
, Lemma 2.5]
there exists a form of discriminant
p^{2}+4
that properly represents
p^{l-2}
. This form is primitive since the discriminant is coprime to
p
. By assumption
\Cl(p^{2}+4)
is trivial. Moreover, the narrow form class group
\Cl^{+}(p^{2}+4))
is then also trivial since the fundamental unit
1/2(p-\sqrt{p^{2}+4})\in\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{2}+4})
is of norm
-1
. Therefore, all primitive forms of discriminant
p^{2}+4
are properly equivalent to each other and thus
h
properly represents
p^{l-2}
. Finally, because of the shape of
h
and any proper representation of
p^{l-2}
obeys
p\nmid tz
. Then
p\nmid xy
as
x=-z
and
y=pt-z
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

A quick computation reveals that the primes
2<p<1000
for which
\Cl(p^{2}+4)
is trivial are given by 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17.

Example 4.4
.

By the methods of reduction theory developed in
[
24
]
, the authors showed that
m=46
is the first positive integral Hasse failure, i.e.
\mathcal{U}_{46}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\neq\emptyset
for all
p
, but
\mathcal{U}_{46}(\mathbb{Z})=\emptyset
. Notice that we can write
46=(2+3^{2})^{2}-3^{1}5^{2}
, and
\Cl(3^{2}+4)
is trivial. So by Theorem
4.2
there exist semi-integral points on
(\mathcal{X}_{46},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})
for arbitrary weights.

The following theorem investigates the existence of semi-integral points on a specific family of Markoff surfaces given in
[
17
, Prop.5.12 (iv)]
, which has been shown to have no integral points.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
m=4-3l^{2}
for some prime
l\geq 17
. Assume that one of the
\omega_{i}
is finite. If
-3
is a square mod
84+l^{2}
,
then
(\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{D}_{\uomega})_{\str}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\omega_{1}
is finite. We want to show the existence of semi-integral points of the shape
\left(3^{\omega_{1}},16\cdot 3^{\omega_{1}},x,y\right)
with
3\nmid xy.
Plugging points of this form in (
1.2
) yields

x^{2}-16xy+y^{2}=-3^{2\omega_{1}+1}(84+l^{2}).

Thus, one may conclude the proof by showing that the form
f(x,y)=x^{2}-16xy+y^{2}
properly represents the RHS of the above equation. Now,
f
is properly equivalent to
x^{2}-63y^{2}
, and thus by similar manipulations as in the proof Theorem
4.2
, it suffices to show that the form
h(x,y)=x^{2}-7y^{2}
properly represents
-3^{2\omega_{1}-1}(84+l^{2})
. The discriminant of
h
is a quadratic residue modulo
3
, and also modulo
84+l^{2}
because
28\equiv x^{2}\pmod{84+l^{2}}
has a solution if and only if
-3\equiv x^{2}\pmod{84+l^{2}}
has a solution which is one of our assumptions. Since
\gcd(3,84+l^{2})=1
, again by
[
20
, Lemma 2.5]
we find that there exists some form of discriminant
28
that represents
-3^{2\omega_{1}-1}(84+l^{2})
. In order to conclude that this form is in the same class as in
\Cl^{+}(28)
, we notice that
-3
is a quadratic residue mod
28
, and therefore
-3^{2\omega_{1}-1}(84+l^{2})
is in the subgroup of squares in
(\mathbb{Z}/28\mathbb{Z})^{*}
. Thus, any form representing this value is in the principal genus
\Cl^{+}(28)^{2}
. Since
|\Cl^{+}(28)|=2
, comparing the orders we may conclude that all forms in the principal genus
\Cl^{+}(28)^{2}
are properly equivalent to each other, hence proving our claim.
∎

Some results on the 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -stability of surfaces with boundary Thanks: The author was supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea (NRF) grant funded by the Korea government (No. RS-2023-00214167).
Sanghun Lee

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the geometric properties associated with the 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -stability of surfaces with boundary whose null expansion satisfies Θ + = h ≥ 0 \Theta^{+}=h\geq 0 . First, we show that a 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -stable hypersurface with free boundary admits a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary under suitable conditions. Second, for 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -stable surfaces with free boundary, we derive an area estimate and determine the topology of the surface. Finally, we extend our free boundary results to the case of capillary boundary.

Introduction

An
initial data set

(M^{n+1},g,A^{M})
consists of an
(n+1)
-dimensional manifold

(n+2)
-dimensional spacetime

In initial data sets, minimal surfaces in Riemannian geometry are replaced by
marginally outer trapped surfaces
(

For example, under suitable conditions, MOTSs arise as cross sections of event horizons in stationary black hole spacetimes.

In pure mathematics, MOTSs appeared in the work of Schoen and Yau
[
22
]
in connection with Jang’s equation and the positive mass theorem. Since then, many results on minimal surfaces in Riemannian geometry have been extended to the spacetime using MOTSs (see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
]

Recently, Galloway and Mendes
[
14
]
extended basic results concerning closed MOTSs to the general null expansion. A detailed description of general null expansion is provided in Section 2.

In this paper, we further extend results on closed surfaces with general null expansion
[
14
,
18
]
to surfaces with boundary.

in

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(\mathsf{L}^{n+2},\bar{g})
be an
(n+2)
-dimensional spacetime satisfying the Einstein equation

\displaystyle G_{\bar{g}}\equiv Ric_{\bar{g}}-\frac{1}{2}R_{\bar{g}}\bar{g}=T,

where
Ric_{\bar{g}}
and
R_{\bar{g}}
denote the Ricci curvature and scalar curvature of
\bar{g}
, respectively, and
T
is a symmetric 2-tensor.

An
(n+1)
-dimensional initial data set
(M^{n+1},g,A^{M})
is a spacelike hypersurface in
(\mathsf{L}^{n+2},\bar{g})
endowed with the induced Riemannian metric
g
and the second fundamental form
A^{M}
of
M
in
\mathsf{L}
. More precisely, the second fundamental form
A^{M}
is defined by

\displaystyle A^{M}(X,Y)=\bar{g}(\nabla^{\mathsf{L}}_{X}N_{M},Y),

where
N_{M}
is the future-directed timelike unit normal vector to
M
, and
X,Y\in T_{p}M
.

By the Gauss-Codazzi equations, an initial data set
(M^{n+1},g,A^{M})
satisfies the Einstein constraint equations

\displaystyle\mu:=T(N_{M},N_{M})=\frac{1}{2}\left(R^{M}+\tau^{2}-|A^{M}|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle J:=T(N_{M},\cdot)={\rm div}_{g}\left(A^{M}-\tau g\right),

where
R^{M}
denotes the scalar curvature of
M
, and
\tau={\rm tr}_{g}A^{M}
is the mean curvature of
M
in
\mathsf{L}
. We refer to
\mu
as the local
energy density
and to
J
as the local
current density
. The
dominant energy conditions
then requires that

\displaystyle\mu\geq|J|\,\,{\rm on}\,\,M.

Let
\Sigma^{n}\subset M^{n+1}
be an embedded
n
-dimensional hypersurface, and let
N
denote a unit normal vector field to
\Sigma
in
M
. By convention, we choose
N
to be the outward pointing unit normal, so that
-N
is inward pointing. Then
\Sigma
admits two future-directed null normal vector fields given by

\displaystyle\ell^{\pm}=N_{M}\pm N.

We define the
null second fundamental form

\chi^{\pm}
by

\displaystyle\chi^{\pm}(X,Y)=\bar{g}(\nabla^{\mathsf{L}}_{X}\ell^{\pm},Y)=A^{M}|_{\Sigma}\pm A^{\Sigma},

where
X,Y\in T\Sigma
, and
A^{\Sigma}
denotes the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
in
M
. The
null expansion scalars

\Theta^{\pm}
of
\Sigma
in
M
with respect to
N
are defined by

\displaystyle\Theta^{\pm}={\rm div}_{\Sigma}\ell^{\pm}={\rm tr}_{\Sigma}A^{M}\pm H,

where
H
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma
in
M
. When
\Theta^{+}=0
, we say that
\Sigma
is a
marginally outer trapped surface
(
MOTS
). In the time-symmetric case (
A^{M}=0
), a MOTS reduces to a minimal surface in Riemannian geometry.

We now introduce the notion of
\mathfrak{g}
-stability
using
general null expansion

\Theta^{+}=h
, which generalizes the usual notion of stability for MOTS. This notion was introduced by Galloway and Mendes in
[
14
]
and is slightly different from the stability for prescribed null expansion considered in
[
6
,
8
]
. For sufficiently small
\epsilon_{0}>0
, the map

\displaystyle\Psi:[0,\epsilon_{0}]\times\Sigma\rightarrow M,

defined by
\Psi(t,p)={\rm exp}_{p}(tN(p))
, is well-defined. On the other hand, given a positive function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
with
\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, the map

\displaystyle\Psi^{\varphi}:[0,1]\times\Sigma\rightarrow M,

defined by
\Psi^{\varphi}(t,p)={\rm exp}_{p}(t\varphi(p)N(p))
, is also well-defined. We denote
\Psi^{\varphi}(t,\Sigma)
by
\Sigma^{\varphi}_{t}
and we write
\Theta^{\pm}_{\varphi}(t)
for the null expansion scalars of
\Sigma^{\varphi}_{t}
.

Definition 2.1

(Definition 2.1 in
[
14
]
)
.

We say that
\Sigma
is
\mathfrak{g}
-stable if

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Theta^{+}_{\varphi}}{\partial t}\Big|_{t=0}\geq 0

for some function
\varphi>0
with
\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}}\leq\epsilon_{0}
.

It is well known that (see
[
3
,
10
]
)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Theta^{+}_{\varphi}}{\partial t}\Big|_{t=0}=L\varphi,

where
L:C^{\infty}(\Sigma)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
is the elliptic operator defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle L\varphi=-\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+2\langle X,\nabla^{\Sigma}\varphi\rangle+\left(Q-|X|^{2}+{\rm div}_{\Sigma}X-\frac{h^{2}}{2}+h\tau\right)\varphi,

where
h=\Theta^{+}_{\varphi}(0)
is the null expansion of
\Sigma^{\varphi}_{0}=\Sigma
,
\tau={\rm tr}_{g}A^{M}
is the mean curvature of
M
, and

\displaystyle Q=\frac{R^{\Sigma}}{2}-\left(\mu+J(N)\right)-\frac{|\chi^{+}|^{2}}{2}.

Here
R^{\Sigma}
denotes the scalar curvature of
\Sigma
, and
X
is the vector field tangent to
\Sigma
that is dual to the
1
-form
A^{M}(N,\cdot)|_{\Sigma}
.

It is well-known that there exists a real number
\lambda
, called the
principal eigenvalue
of
L
, such that
L\varphi=\lambda\varphi
for some positive eigenfunction
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, and that
\lambda\leq{\rm Re}(\eta)
for any other eigenvalue
\eta
of
L
(see
[
3
]
). The eigenspace of
L
associated with
\lambda
is one-dimensional. In particular,
\Sigma
is
\mathfrak{g}
-stable if and only if
\lambda\geq 0
(see
[
14
]
). We denote the principal eigenvalue
\lambda
of
L
by
\lambda_{1}(L)
.

We now introduce the notion of
\mathfrak{g}
-stability with capillary boundary
for initial data set with boundary. We begin by recalling the definition of a
hypersurface with capillary boundary
. Let
(M,g,A^{M})
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional initial data set with boundary, and let
\Sigma
be a compact
properly
embedded hypersurface with boundary in
M
, meaning that
\Sigma
is embedded in
M
and
\partial\Sigma=\Sigma\cap\partial M
. We say that
\Sigma
has a
capillary boundary
in
M
if
\Sigma
meets
\partial M
at a constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. More precisely, assume that
\Sigma
separates
M
into two components, and label one of them by
\Omega
. Let
N
be the unit normal vector to
\Sigma
pointing into
\Omega
. Let
\bar{\nu}
denote the unit normal to
\partial\Sigma
, pointing out of
\Omega
, as a subset of
\partial M
. Furthermore, let
\nu
be the outward unit normal to
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
, and let
\bar{N}
be the outward unit normal of
\partial M
. Then one can choose orthonormal bases
\{N,\nu\}
and
\{\bar{N},\bar{\nu}\}
such that the following relations hold (cf.
[
21
]
):

\displaystyle\bar{N}=(\cos\theta)N+(\sin\theta)\nu,

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}=-(\sin\theta)N+(\cos\theta)\nu.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle N=-(\sin\theta)\bar{\nu}+(\cos\theta)\bar{N},

\displaystyle\nu=(\cos\theta)\bar{\nu}+(\sin\theta)\bar{N},

where
\theta
is the constant contact angle between
\Sigma
and
\partial M
.

Next, we introduce the notion of a
\mathfrak{g}
-stable hypersurface with capillary boundary
. Let
(\Sigma_{t})_{|t|<\epsilon}
be a smooth variation of
\Sigma
, and consider the functional

\displaystyle F[\Sigma_{t}]=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\Theta^{+}(t)-h\right)\langle W_{t},N_{t}\rangle dv+\int_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}\langle W_{t},\nu_{t}-({\rm cos}\,\theta)\bar{\nu}_{t}\rangle ds,

where the variational vector field is given by
W_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
and
h\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
(see also
[
1
,
20
]
). Since the general null expansion satisfies
\Theta^{+}=h
, the first variation of
F[\Sigma_{t}]
is given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Bigr|_{t=0}F[\Sigma_{t}]=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}-\varphi\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+2\varphi\langle X,\nabla^{\Sigma}\varphi\rangle+\left(Q+{\rm div}_{\Sigma}X-|X|^{2}+h\tau-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\right)\varphi^{2}dv

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left(\varphi\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}-q\varphi^{2}\right),

where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
and
q=-(\cot\theta)A^{\Sigma}(\nu,\nu)+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}A^{\partial M}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})
. The eigenvalue problem associated with the functional (
2.2
) is then given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&L\varphi:=-\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+2\langle X,\nabla^{\Sigma}\varphi\rangle+\left(Q+{\rm div}_{\Sigma}X-|X|^{2}+h\tau-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\right)\varphi=\lambda\varphi\quad{\rm on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
&B_{c}\varphi:=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}-q\varphi=0\quad{\rm along}\,\,\partial\Sigma.\end{cases}

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
\Sigma
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-stable hypersurface with capillary boundary if there exists a non-negative function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
,
\varphi\not\equiv 0
, satisfying Robin boundary condition
B_{c}\varphi=0
such that
L\varphi\geq 0
on
\Sigma
.

When the contact angle
\theta
equals
\frac{\pi}{2}
, we say that
\Sigma
has a
free boundary
. More precisely, along
\partial\Sigma
, the unit normal vector
\nu
of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
coincides with the normal vector
\bar{N}
of
\partial M
. In the free boundary case, the associated eigenvalue problem is given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&L\varphi:=-\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+2\langle X,\nabla^{\Sigma}\varphi\rangle+\left(Q+{\rm div}_{\Sigma}X-|X|^{2}+h\tau-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\right)\varphi=\lambda\varphi\quad{\rm on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
&B_{f}\varphi:=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}-A^{\partial M}(N,N)\varphi=0\quad{\rm along}\,\,\partial\Sigma.\end{cases}

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
\Sigma
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-stable hypersurface with free boundary if there exists a non-negative function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
,
\varphi\not\equiv 0
, satisfying the boundary condition
B_{f}\varphi=0
such that
L\varphi\geq 0
on
\Sigma
.

Remark 2.4
.

The notion of
\mathfrak{g}
-stability for a hypersurface extends, in the variational sense, the usual notion of stability for MOTSs with
\Theta^{+}=0
.

3.
Results for free boundary

In this section, we establish several results concerning
\mathfrak{g}
-stable (hyper)surfaces with free boundary. First, we investigate conditions under which an
n
-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-stable hypersurface with free boundary admits a metric of positive scalar curvature and has minimal boundary. Before presenting the proof, we recall an important result that will be used in establishing our main theorem.

Lemma 3.1

(Lemma 3.1 and Lemma 3.2 in
[
20
]
)
.

Let
(M,g,A^{M})
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional initial data set with boundary, and
\Sigma
be a compact hypersurface with free boundary such that
\Theta^{+}=h\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
. Suppose that
\Sigma
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-stable hypersurface with free boundary. Then the following hold:

(1)

For all
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
and
\tilde{Q}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Sigma}\psi\left(-\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi+\psi\tilde{Q}\right)dv+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\psi\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}-\psi\left(A^{\partial M}(N,N)-\langle X,\nu\rangle\right)\right)ds.

Hence, the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(L_{1})
of
L_{1}:=-\Delta_{\Sigma}+\tilde{Q}
on
\Sigma
, with Robin boundary condition
B_{1}\psi:=B_{f}\psi+\langle X,\nu\rangle\psi=0
is non-negative. Moreover, if equality holds in (
3.1
) and
\psi\not\equiv 0
, then
\psi
is an eigenfunction of
L_{1}
with
\lambda_{1}(L_{1})=0
.

(2)

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
be positive and suppose that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\mathcal{L}\psi:=-\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi+\left(\frac{R^{\Sigma}}{2}-\tilde{P}\right)\psi\geq 0\quad{\rm on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
&\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}+H^{\partial\Sigma}\psi\geq 0\quad{\rm along}\,\,\partial\Sigma,\end{cases}

where
R^{\Sigma}
is the scalar curvature of
\Sigma
,
H^{\partial\Sigma}
is the mean curvature of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
, and
\tilde{P}
is a non-negative function on
\Sigma
. Then
\Sigma
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary, unless
\Sigma
is Ricci flat with totally geodesic boundary,
\tilde{P}=0
, and
\psi
is constant.

A detailed proof can be found in Section 3 of
[
20
]
.

We now prove our result on positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\psi>0
be an eigenfunction of
L_{1}\psi=-\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi+\tilde{Q}\psi
on
\Sigma
with Robin boundary condition
B_{1}\psi=0
, associated with the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(L_{1})
. Since
\Sigma
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-stable hypersurface with free boundary, we obtain

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&L_{1}\psi=-\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi+\tilde{Q}\psi\geq 0\,\,{\rm on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
&B_{1}\psi=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}-\left(A^{\partial M}(N,N)-\langle X,\nu\rangle\right)\psi=0\,\,{\rm along}\,\,\partial\Sigma,\end{cases}

Note that

\displaystyle\left(\iota_{\bar{N}}\pi\right)^{T}(N)=A^{M}(\bar{N},N)-\tau g(\bar{N},N)=\langle X,\bar{N}\rangle=\langle X,\nu\rangle\,\,{\rm along}\,\,\partial\Sigma.

Therefore, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle A^{\partial M}(N,N)-\langle X,\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=H^{\partial M}-H^{\partial\Sigma}-\left(\iota_{\bar{N}}\pi\right)^{T}(N)

\displaystyle\geq-H^{\partial\Sigma},

where, in the last inequality, we have used the DBEC (
1.1
). Substituting (
3.3
) into (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&-\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi+\tilde{Q}\psi\geq 0\,\,{\rm on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
&\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}+H^{\partial\Sigma}\psi\geq 0\,\,{\rm along}\,\,\partial\Sigma,\end{cases}

where
\tilde{Q}=\frac{R^{\Sigma}}{2}-\tilde{P}
and
\tilde{P}=\left(\mu+J(N)\right)+\frac{|\chi^{+}|^{2}}{2}-h\left(\tau-\frac{h}{2}\right)
.

CASE (i).

C_{0}=0
.

In this case, since
\mu+J(N)\geq\mu-|J|\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{P}

\displaystyle=\left(\mu+J(N)\right)+\frac{|\chi^{+}|^{2}}{2}-h\left(\tau-\frac{h}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-h\left(\tau-\frac{h}{2}\right)\geq 0,

where, in the last inequality, we used the assumption
\tau\leq\frac{h}{2}
. If
\tilde{P}=0
, then
0=\mu+J(N)=h\left(\tau-\frac{h}{2}\right)
and
\chi^{+}=0
. This implies that
0={\rm tr}_{\Sigma}\chi^{+}=\Theta^{+}=h
, which contradicts the assumption that
h\not\equiv 0
. Therefore,
\Sigma
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary.

CASE (ii).

C_{0}>0
.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{P}

\displaystyle=\left(\mu+J(N)\right)+\frac{|\chi^{+}|^{2}}{2}-h\tau+\frac{h^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq C_{0}-h\tau\geq 0.

If
\tilde{P}=0
, then
C_{0}=\mu+J(N)=h\tau+\frac{h^{2}}{2}
. This implies that
\chi^{+}=0
and hence
h=0
. On the other hand, since
C_{0}\geq h\tau=\mu+J(N)\geq C_{0}
, we must have
h\tau=C_{0}>0
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\Sigma
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary.

Next, we extend the rigidity results for stable MOTS (
\Theta^{+}=0
) with free boundary to
\mathfrak{g}
-stable surface with free boundary under general null expansion (
\Theta^{+}=h
).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By (1) of Lemma
3.1
and (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\nabla^{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}+\tilde{Q}\psi^{2}\right)dv-\int_{\partial\Sigma}k^{\partial\Sigma}\psi^{2}ds,

where
\tilde{Q}=\frac{R^{\Sigma}}{2}-\tilde{P}
and
\tilde{P}=\left(\mu+J(N)\right)+\frac{|\chi^{+}|^{2}}{2}-h\left(\tau-\frac{h}{2}\right)
. Taking
\psi=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}\tilde{Q}\,dv+\int_{\partial\Sigma}k^{\partial\Sigma}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}K^{\Sigma}dv-\int_{\Sigma}\left(\mu-|J|\right)dv-\int_{\Sigma}\left(\tau-\frac{h}{2}\right)h\,dv+\int_{\partial\Sigma}k^{\partial\Sigma}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq-C_{1}A(\Sigma)+2\pi\chi(\Sigma),

where
\chi(\Sigma)
denotes the Euler characteristic of
\Sigma
. Hence, we conclude that

(3.4)

\displaystyle C_{1}A(\Sigma)\leq 2\pi\chi(\Sigma)=2\pi\left(2-2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\right),

where
\alpha_{1}
is the genus of
\Sigma
and
\alpha_{2}
is the number of boundary components of
\Sigma
. This inequality implies that
\Sigma
is topologically a disk. Moreover, if equality holds in (
3.4
), then all of the above inequalities become equalities. In particular:

(1)

\chi^{+}=0
and
\mu+J(N)=\mu-|J|=C_{1}
on
\Sigma
;

(2)

\psi=1
is an eigenfunction of
L_{1}
associated with the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(L_{1})=0
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&0=K^{\Sigma}-C_{1}\,\,{\rm on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
&0=-\left(A^{\partial M}(N,N)-\langle X,\nu\rangle\right)=k^{\partial\Sigma}\,\,{\rm along}\,\,\partial\Sigma;\end{cases}

(3)

hence,
\Sigma
has constant Gaussian curvature equal to positive constant
C_{1}
and its boundary has zero geodesic curvature.

4.
Results for capillary boundary

In this section, we extend our results for the free boundary case to the setting of capillary boundary. First, we present an analogous of Lemma
3.1
for hypersurfaces with capillary boundary.

Lemma 4.1

(Lemma 3.1 in
[
16
]
and Lemma 3.2 in
[
20
]
)
.

Let
(M,g,A^{M})
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional initial data set with boundary, and let
\Sigma
be a compact hypersurface with capillary boundary such that
\Theta^{+}=h\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
. Suppose that
\Sigma
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-stable hypersurface with capillary boundary. Then the following hold:

(1)

For all
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
and
\tilde{Q}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Sigma}\psi\left(-\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi+\psi\tilde{Q}\right)dv+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\psi\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}-\psi\left(q-\langle X,\nu\rangle\right)\right)ds

Hence, the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(L_{1})
of
L_{1}=-\Delta_{\Sigma}+\tilde{Q}
on
\Sigma
with Robin boundary condition
B_{2}\psi:=B_{c}\psi+\langle X,\nu\rangle\psi=0
is non-negative. Moreover, if equality holds in (
4.1
) and
\psi\not\equiv 0
, then
\psi
is an eigenfunction of
L_{1}
associated with
\lambda_{1}(L_{1})=0
.

(2)

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
be positive and suppose that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\mathcal{L}\psi:=-\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi+\left(\frac{R^{\Sigma}}{2}-\tilde{P}\right)\psi\geq 0\quad{\rm on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
&\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}+H^{\partial\Sigma}\psi\geq 0\quad{\rm along}\,\,\partial\Sigma.\end{cases}

Then
\Sigma
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary, unless
\Sigma
is Ricci flat with totally geodesic boundary,
\tilde{P}=0
, and
\psi
is constant.

The following lemma allows us to handle the boundary term
q
more conveniently.

Lemma 4.2

(Lemma 3.1 in
[
17
,
19
]
)
.

Let
(M,g,A^{M})
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional initial data set with boundary
\partial M
, and let
\Sigma
be a hypersurface with capillary boundary in
M
with contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. Then

\displaystyle A^{\partial M}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})-(\cos\theta)A^{\Sigma}(\nu,\nu)+(\sin\theta)H^{\partial\Sigma}=H^{\partial M}-(\cos\theta)H.

In particular, if
n=2
, then
H^{\partial\Sigma}=k^{\partial\Sigma}
, where
k^{\partial\Sigma}
denotes the geodesic curvature of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
.

For a detailed proof, we refer the reader to Lemma 3.1 in
[
17
,
19
]
. By Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

(4.2)

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=-(\cot\theta)A^{\Sigma}(\nu,\nu)+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}A^{\partial M}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\left(H^{\partial M}-(\cos\theta)\Theta^{+}+(\cos\theta){\rm tr}_{\Sigma}A^{M}-(\sin\theta)H^{\partial\Sigma}\right).

We now extend the free boundary result (Theorem
1.1
) concerning positive scalar curvature with minimal boundary to the capillary boundary setting.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\psi>0
be an eigenfunction of
L_{1}
satisfying the Robin boundary condition
B_{2}\psi=0
. Since
\Sigma
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-stable hypersurface with capillary boundary, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&L_{1}\psi=-\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi+\tilde{Q}\psi\geq 0\,\,{\rm on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
&B_{2}\psi=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}-\left(q-\langle X,\nu\rangle\right)\psi=0\,\,{\rm along}\,\,\partial\Sigma.\end{cases}

By (
4.2
), we obtain

(4.4)

\displaystyle q-\langle X,\nu\rangle=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\left(H^{\partial M}-(\cos\theta)h+(\cos\theta){\rm tr}_{\Sigma}A^{M}-(\sin\theta)H^{\partial\Sigma}-(\sin\theta)A^{M}(N,\nu)\right).

We claim that

(4.5)

\displaystyle(\cos\theta){\rm tr}_{\Sigma}A^{M}-(\sin\theta)A^{M}(N,\nu)=(\cos\theta){\rm tr}_{\partial M}A^{M}+(\sin\theta)A^{M}(\bar{N},\bar{\nu}).

To verify this, let
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
be an orthonormal frame of
T\Sigma
such that
e_{n}=\nu
. Then

\displaystyle(\cos\theta){\rm tr}_{\Sigma}A^{M}-(\sin\theta)A^{M}(N,\nu)=

\displaystyle(\cos\theta)\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}A^{M}(e_{i},e_{i})+(\cos\theta)A^{M}(\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle-(\sin\theta)A^{M}(N,\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\cos\theta)\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}A^{M}(e_{i},e_{i})+A^{M}(\bar{\nu},\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\cos\theta)\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}A^{M}(e_{i},e_{i})+(\cos\theta)A^{M}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})

\displaystyle+(\sin\theta)A^{M}(\bar{\nu},\bar{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\cos\theta){\rm tr}_{\partial M}A^{M}+(\sin\theta)A^{M}(\bar{\nu},\bar{N}),

which proves the claim (see also Appendix A in
[
7
]
). Substituting (
4.5
) into (
4.4
) and using the TDBEC (
1.3
), we obtain

(4.6)

\displaystyle q-\langle X,\nu\rangle\geq-\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\left((\cos\theta)h+(\sin\theta)H^{\partial\Sigma}\right)\geq-H^{\partial\Sigma},

where, in the last inequality, we used the assumptions
h\geq 0
and
\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
. Consequently, the eigenvalue problem (
4.3
) reduces to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&-\Delta_{\Sigma}\psi+\tilde{Q}\psi\geq 0\,\,{\rm on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
&\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}+H^{\partial\Sigma}\psi\geq 0\,\,{\rm along}\,\,\partial\Sigma.\end{cases}

The remainder of the proof proceeds exactly as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and we therefore omit the details.

Finally, we extend the rigidity result for
\mathfrak{g}
-stable surfaces with free boundary to the case of
\mathfrak{g}
-stable surface with capillary boundary.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Since
\Sigma
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-stable surface with capillary boundary, by (
4.1
) in Lemma
4.1
we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\nabla^{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}+\tilde{Q}\psi^{2}\right)dv-\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left(q-\langle X,\nu\rangle\right)ds,

where
\tilde{Q}=\frac{R^{\Sigma}}{2}-\tilde{P}
and
\tilde{P}=\left(\mu+J(N)\right)+\frac{|\chi|^{2}}{2}-h\left(\tau-\frac{h}{2}\right)
. Taking
\psi=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Sigma}K^{\Sigma}dv-\int_{\Sigma}\left(\mu-|J|\right)dv-\int_{\Sigma}\left(\tau-\frac{h}{2}\right)h\,dv+\int_{\partial\Sigma}k^{\partial\Sigma}ds,

where we used (
4.6
) to estimate the boundary term. By our assumptions and the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, this yields

(4.7)

\displaystyle C_{1}A(\Sigma)\leq 2\pi\chi(\Sigma).

This inequality implies that
\Sigma
is topologically a disk. Moreover, if the equality holds in (
4.7
), then all of the above inequalities become equalities. Consequently, properties (1)-(4) in Theorem
1.4
follow, by arguments entirely analogous to those in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

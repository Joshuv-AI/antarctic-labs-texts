On quaternionic ordinary families of modular forms and p p -adic L L -functions
M. Longo, P. Magrone, E. R. Walchek

Abstract
. We use Serre–Tate expansions of modular forms to construct power series attached to quaternionic ordinary families of modular forms. We associate to these power series a big p p -adic L L -function interpolating the p p -adic L L -functions constructed by Burungale and Magrone at classical specializations. A crucial ingredient is the generalization of some results of Ohta to the quaternionic setting.

1.
Introduction

The idea of using Serre–Tate expansions to attach power series to quaternionic modular forms goes back to
[
Mor95
,
Mor11
,
Mor22
]
and recently has been fruitfully exploited, also in the
\GL_{2}
-case, to construct
p
-adic
L
-functions associated to quaternionic modular forms; see
[
Bra11
,
CH18a
]
, for the
\GL_{2}
-case and
[
Mor95
,
HB15
,
Bur17
,
Mag22
]
, for the quaternionic division algebra case. For a family of
p
-adic modular forms on
\GL_{2}
, Serre–Tate expansions have been combined in
[
Cas20
]
with the Hida–Ohta theory of ordinary forms to obtain a big
p
-adic
L
-function that interpolates at classical specializations the
p
-adic
L
-function constructed in
[
CH18a
]
.

The goal of this paper is to develop a theory of big
p
-adic
L
-functions for indefinite rational quaternion algebras, similar to that developed in
[
Cas20
]
over
\GL_{2}
. To fix the notation, let
p
be a prime number,
N
be a positive integer not divisible by
p
, and
\mathbb{I}
be a primitive branch of the Hida family attached to a residual representation which is absolutely irreducible and
p
-distinguished; this is a
p
-adic family of modular forms on
\GL_{2}
(see §
5.2
for details on the terminology). Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be an imaginary quadratic extension of discriminant
-D_{K}
prime to
N
in which
p
splits. Assume that
N=MD
is a factorization of
N
into coprime integers
M
and
D
, with
D
a square-free product of an even number of prime factors which are inert in
K
, and
M\geq 4
only divisible by primes that are split in
K
.
Also, let
\widetilde{\mathbb{I}}=\mathbb{I}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}=W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
is the ring of Witt vectors of a separable algebraic closure of the field with
p
elements.
The goal is to construct a big
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{I}}[[{\Gamma_{\infty}}]]
attached to
\mathbb{I}
and a
p
-adic family of Hecke characters
\boldsymbol{\xi}
such that when
\kappa\in\Spec(\mathbb{I})
is an arithmetic morphism of even weight
k\geq 2
with
k\equiv 2\pmod{2(p-1)}
the value
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\kappa)
of the
\kappa
-specialization of
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
coincides (up to explicit factors) with the
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}_{f_{\kappa},\xi_{\kappa}}
attached to the
normalized
Jacquet–Langlands lift
f_{\kappa}
of the
\kappa
-specialization of the given Hida family, and the
\kappa
-specialization
\xi_{\kappa}
of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
. Writing
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\kappa)
for
\kappa(\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}})
, our main result, Theorem
6.3
, then says that for
\kappa
as before, we have

(\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\kappa))=(\mathscr{L}_{f_{\kappa},\xi_{\kappa}})

as ideals in
\widetilde{\mathbb{I}}[[\Gamma_{\infty}]]
.

Much of the work consists in clarifying the meaning of the previous sentences, in which we have at least one crucial difficulty, namely, the correct definition of the
normalized
Jacquet–Langlands lift to a quaternion algebra of a given elliptic modular form.
We do not develop this theme in full generality,
but we use an approach which combines Serre–Tate expansions (which is the basic tool to actually define
\mathscr{L}_{f,\xi}
for a quaternionic modular form
f
and an appropriate Hecke character
\xi
in

[
Bur17
,
Mag22
,
Mor22
]
), and an extension to the quaternionic case of Eichler–Shimura theory for ordinary families of modular forms developed by Ohta in a series of papers
[
Oht93
,
Oht95
,
Oht99
,
Oht00
]
. In the
\GL_{2}
-case, one can normalize an eigenform by imposing that the first coefficient of its Fourier expansion is equal to
1
; more generally, one can define a notion of
integral
modular forms by requiring that the Fourier coefficients belong to some fixed subring of
\mathbb{C}_{p}
. Adopting this viewpoint, and making use of the Atkin–Lehner involution, Ohta defines modules of projective limits of ordinary modular forms, which are identified with Wiles
\Lambda
-adic modular forms.
We exploit this approach in the quaternionic case replacing Fourier expansions with Serre–Tate expansions (we also note that the use of Serre–Tate expansions is responsible for the systematic extension of the coefficients to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}
, where the relevant power series can be defined). We thus obtain an integral theory of modular forms, and inverse limits of such, giving rise to a
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{O}}
-module denoted
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}^{*}
in the paper; here
\mathcal{O}
is a subring of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
containing
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}
,
\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}=\mathcal{O}[[1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}]]
and
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{O}}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}\times\mathcal{O}[(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}]
.
We are able to attach to each element of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}^{*}
a power series in
\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}[[T_{x}]]
following an approach of Ohta, where
T_{x}
is the Serre–Tate parameter around a point
x
in the Igusa tower over the special fiber of a Shimura curve
X
of tame level
M
attached to the rational quaternion algebra of discriminant
D
. This power series gives rise to the sough-for measure
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}
. The main result, as mentioned above, is Theorem
6.3
, to which we refer the reader for details.

The construction of similar
p
-adic families of
L
-functions can also be obtained by other methods, as in
[
JLZ21
,
LRZ25
,
Sev25
]
. However, we would like to point out that our approach, even if restricted to the ordinary case, builds directly on weight
2
specializations of a given
p
-adic family of modular forms, following the original approach of Ohta and Hida. This framework may be viewed as complementary to the methods of
[
JLZ21
,
LRZ25
,
Sev25
]
, which avoid a direct comparison with weight
2
specializations. The benefit of our more direct approach is that it may lead to a direct comparison with big Heegner points introduced in
[
LV11
]
by interpolating Heegner points in weight
2
, thus proving a quaternionic analogue of
[
Cas13
,
Cas20
]
.

We fall short of an analogue of Wiles theory of
\Lambda
-adic modular forms in this context, and more generally of developing a full analogue of Ohta’s theory to obtain a quaternionic counterpart of
[
Oht95
, Theorem 2.3.6]
. The reason is the lack of a clean connection between Hecke actions on modular forms and their
T_{x}
-expansions. Even if explicit descriptions of Hecke operators are available (see especially
[
HB15
, §4.4]
), some other inputs are missing; specifically, we cannot prove a full control theorem for our modules
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}^{*}
(as in
[
Hid93
, §7.3, Theorem 6]
and
[
Oht95
, Theorem 2.5.3]
) which does not allow for showing the freeness of the components of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}^{*}
over
\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}
, or that
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}^{*}
is a
\Lambda_{\mathcal{O}}
-module of finite rank; some of these obstructions are listed in the paper, see for example Remarks
3.14
,
3.16
and
5.6
. We believe that it would be interesting to develop a more complete Hida theory of quaternionic modular forms using Serre–Tate expansions, but at the moment it seems quite difficult to make progress in this direction.

We finally remark that the extension of Ohta’s theorem to the quaternionic case has been obtained by means of perfectoid techniques in the paper
[
CHJ17
]
by Chojecki–Hansen–Johansson (see also
[
BHW22
]
in the Hilbert case). These results work very generally for finite slope families, but it seems difficult to adapt them to deal with integrality conditions as done in this paper. Furthermore, Ohta’s theory has also been extended to more general Shimura varieties (including the Hilbert and Siegel cases) in the recent preprint by Sangiovanni Vincentelli–Skinner
[
SVS25
]
.

We fix throughout the text embeddings
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}
.

2.
Shimura curves

The goal of this section is to collect the needed results on quaternionic multiplication abelian surfaces, and see Shimura curves as solutions of moduli problems involving these objects. This will make it possible to introduce integral models of Shimura curves. Basic references are
[
Buz97
,
Car86
,
HB15
,
Mor22
]
.

2.1.
Quaternion algebras

Fix an integer
M\geq 4
and a square-free integer
D>1
coprime to
M
and which is the product of an even number of distinct primes. Let
B
be the quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
of discriminant
D
. Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D_{K}})
be an imaginary quadratic field, of discriminant
-D_{K}
prime to
MD
, such that all primes dividing
M
are split in
K
and all primes dividing
D
are inert in
K
. Then
K
splits
B
, and we may fix an embedding of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras
\iota_{K}\colon K\hookrightarrow B
. Define
\delta=\sqrt{-D_{K}}
and
\theta=\frac{D^{\prime}+\delta}{2}
, where
D^{\prime}=D_{K}
if
2\nmid D_{K}
and
D^{\prime}=D_{K}/2
if
2\mid D_{K}
. Choose a prime number
p
which does not divide
MDD_{K}
and is split in
K
. For each place
v\mid Mp\infty
of
\mathbb{Q}
, choose an isomorphism
i_{v}\colon B_{v}\cong\M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})
satisfying

i_{v}(\theta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{T}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)&-\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)\\
1&0\end{array}\right).

(Here for any field extension
L/F
, we denote by
\mathrm{T}_{L/F}
and
\mathrm{N}_{L/F}
the trace and norm maps.)
For each prime
\ell\nmid MDp
, choose isomorphisms
i_{\ell}\colon B_{\ell}\cong\M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
such that
i_{\ell}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\ell})\subseteq\M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
, where
\mathcal{O}_{K,\ell}=\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
. In particular, for each divisor
d
of
Mp^{m}
and each integer
m\geq 0
, we obtain isomorphisms

i_{d}\colon\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\cong\M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}).

For each integer
m\geq 0
, let
R_{m}
be the Eichler order of
B
of level
Mp^{m}
with respect to the chosen isomorphisms
i_{\ell}
for all finite places
\ell\nmid D
and let
U_{m}=\widehat{R}^{\times}_{m}=(R_{m}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
, where
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is the profinite completion of
\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Following Mori
[
Mor11
]
, other papers (including
[
Bur17
,
HB15
,
Lon23
]
) choose different isomorphisms by fixing a
Hashimoto model
for
B
, for which one chooses a quadratic real field
M
which splits
B
. This is useful, among other things, to fix a global idempotent element which is fixed by the involution
x\mapsto x^{\dagger}
(see §
2.2
); our choices are more directly comparable with those in
[
CH18a
,
CH18b
,
CH15
]
. However, the role of this quadratic imaginary field
K
will not be apparent until we introduce anticyclotomic
p
-adic
L
-functions in §
6
.

Define the following elements in
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K
:

e=\frac{1\otimes{\theta}-\theta\otimes 1}{(\theta-\bar{\theta})\otimes 1}\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0pt\bar{e}=\frac{\theta\otimes 1-1\otimes\bar{\theta}}{(\theta-\bar{\theta})\otimes 1}.

A simple computation shows that
e
and
\bar{e}
are orthogonal idempotents such that
e+\bar{e}=1
.

Let
\ell\mid Mp
be a prime number. Then
\ell
splits in
K
as
\ell=\mathfrak{l}\bar{\mathfrak{l}}
, where

\mathfrak{l}
is the prime ideal corresponding to the chosen embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
, so
K_{\ell}=K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
splits as the direct sum
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}e\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\bar{e}
of two copies of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. We have a canonical map

j_{\ell}\colon K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}B_{\ell}\overset{i_{\ell}}{\longrightarrow}\M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

and one may verify that
j_{\ell}(e)=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
and
j_{\ell}(\bar{e})=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
.

Denote by
i\colon K\hookrightarrow\M_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map which takes
\theta
to
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathrm{T}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)&-\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
. Then we have
i=i_{\infty}\circ\iota_{K}
and we obtain a map

j\colon K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}\M_{2}(K)

defined by
j(x\otimes y)=i(x)y
, and one verifies again that
j(e)=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
and
j(\bar{e})=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
.

Remark 2.2
.

If we work over a sufficiently big extension containing both
K
and
M
, where
M
is a real quadratic field as in Remark
2.1
, the choices of the idempotents made above and those obtained from the Hashimoto model are equivalent; see
[
Mor11
, Remark 2.6.3]
for details.

2.2.
QM abelian surfaces

We introduce a class of abelian surfaces which plays a central role in the theory of Shimura curves.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
S
be a scheme. A
quaternionic multiplication (QM) abelian surface

(A,\iota)
over
S
is an abelian scheme
A\rightarrow S
of relative dimension
2
equipped with an injective algebra homomorphism
\iota\colon\mathcal{O}_{B}\hookrightarrow\End_{S}(A)
. An
isogeny
(resp. an
isomorphism
) of QM abelian surfaces is an isogeny (resp. an isomorphism) of abelian schemes commuting with the
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-action.

Let
t\in\mathcal{O}_{B}
be such that
t^{2}=-D_{K}<0
, which exists because
B
splits over
K
, and define the involution
\dagger
given by
b^{\dagger}\vcentcolon=t^{-1}\bar{b}t
, where
b\mapsto\bar{b}
denotes the main involution on
B
. Each QM abelian surface over
S
can be equipped with a unique principal polarization
\theta_{A}
such that for each geometric point
s
of
S
the corresponding Rosati involution of
\End(A_{s})
, where
A_{s}
is the fiber of
A\rightarrow S
at
s
, coincides with the involution
x\mapsto x^{\dagger}
on
\mathcal{O}_{B}
(
[
Mil79
, Lemma 1.1]
; see also
[
DT94
, Lemma 5]
,
[
Buz97
, §1]
). If
\pi\colon A\rightarrow B
is an isogeny, taking the dual isogeny
\pi^{\vee}\colon B^{\vee}\rightarrow A^{\vee}
and composing with principal polarizations of
A
and
B
gives an isogeny, still denoted
\pi^{\vee}\colon B\rightarrow A
. We say that
\pi
has
degree
d
if the composition of
\pi^{\vee}\circ\pi
is, locally on
A
, the multiplication by a unique integer
d
.

2.3.
Shimura curves with naïve level structures

Given a group
G
and a scheme
S
, we write
G_{S}
for the constant group scheme of value
G
over
S
; when the context is clear, we often simplify the notation and write
G
for
G_{S}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
m\geq 0
be an integer,
d\mid Mp^{m}
be a positive integer and
(A,\iota)
a QM abelian surface over a
\mathbb{Z}[1/d]
-scheme
S
. A
naïve full level
d
structure
on
A
is an isomorphism

\alpha\colon\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/{d}\mathbb{Z})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}A[{d}]

of
S
-group schemes locally for the étale topology of
S
which commutes with the left actions of
\mathcal{O}_{B}
given by
\iota
on
A[d]
, and the multiplication from the left of
\mathcal{O}_{B}
on the constant
S
-group scheme
\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/{d}\mathbb{Z})
.

Remark 2.5
.

A full level
{d}
structure is equivalent via
i_{d}
to an isomorphism
\M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}A[d]
of finite flat group schemes over
S
, which commutes with the left action of
\mathcal{O}_{B}
given by
\iota
on
A[d]
and by left matrix multiplication on
\M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})
. Also note that, if
k
is an algebraically closed field, giving a full level
d
structure on a QM abelian surface defined over
S=\Spec(k)
is equivalent to fixing a
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
-basis of the group
A[d](k)
.

The group
(\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}))^{\times}
acts from the left on the set of full level
d
structures on a QM abelian surface
(A,\iota)
as follows. If
g\in(\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}))^{\times}
, then right multiplication
r_{g}(x)=xg
by
g
defines an automorphism of the group
(\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}))^{\times}
which commutes with the left action of
(\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}))^{\times}
on itself by left multiplication; for a naïve full level
d
structure
\alpha\colon(\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}))^{\times}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}A[d]
on
(A,\iota)
, we see that
\alpha_{g}=\alpha\circ r_{g}
is a naïve full level
d
structure on
(A,\iota)
, and the map
\alpha\mapsto\alpha_{g}
gives a left action of
(\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}))^{\times}
on the set of naïve full level
d
structures on
(A,\iota)
. For any subgroup
U
of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{B}^{\times}
(where
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{B}=\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
), we obtain a left action of
U
on full level
d
structures by composing the action of
(\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}))^{\times}
with the canonical projection
U\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{B}^{\times}\overset{\hat{\pi}_{d}}{\mbox{\;$\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow$\;}}(\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}))^{\times}
. We finally define a right action of
U
by composing this left action with the map
g\mapsto g^{\prime}=\mathrm{norm}(g)g^{-1}
. We are especially interested in level
V_{1}(d)
and
V_{0}(d)
structures, where

is the inverse image via
\hat{\pi}_{d}
of the subgroup of
(\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}))^{\times}
consisting of matrices which are upper triangular modulo

, and
V_{1}(d)
is the subgroup of
V_{0}(d)
consisting of elements

such that
\hat{\pi}_{d}(g)
is of the form
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(A,\iota)
be a QM abelian surface over a

2.4.
Shimura curves with Drinfeld level structures

We sometimes need to use integral models of
X_{m}
defined over a ring in which
p
is
not
invertible. Let
m\geq 1
an integer. Recall that we have a left action of
\mathcal{O}_{B}
on
A[p^{m}]
and therefore we also obtain a left action of
\mathcal{O}_{B,p}=\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
on
A[p^{m}]
; thus, through
i_{p}
, we have a left action of
\M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
on
A[p^{m}]
. Recall the idempotent
e\in\mathcal{O}_{B,p}
such that
i_{p}(e)=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
. We have a decomposition

A[p^{m}]=\ker(e)\oplus\ker(1-e).

The element
w\in\mathcal{O}_{B,p}
satisfying
i_{p}(w)=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
induces an isomorphism of group schemes
w\colon\ker(e)\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\ker(1-e),
and we have
eA[p^{m}]=\ker(1-e)
and
(1-e)A[p^{m}]=\ker(e)
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(A,\iota)
be a QM abelian surface defined over a scheme
S

3.1.

Let

) to define a

We also need to define modular forms for more general subgroups than

3.2.
Hecke operators

Hecke operators on

are defined using double coset decompositions
T(\alpha)=\Gamma_{m}\alpha\Gamma_{m}=\coprod_{i}\Gamma_{m}\alpha_{i}
for
\alpha
in the Hecke ring
\mathfrak{h}(R_{m}^{\times},\Delta_{m})
, where

is the semigroup of

whose image in
\M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})

\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&*\\

(see
[

, §3.1]
,
[

, §5.3]

[

, §2.1]

. If
T=T(\alpha)
and
\alpha^{\iota}=\Norm(\alpha)\alpha^{-1}
, where
\Norm
is the reduced norm map, we set
T^{*}=T(\alpha^{\iota})
.

By strong approximation (
cf.

[
Miy06
, Theorem 5.2.10]
), the Hecke algebra
\mathfrak{h}(R_{m}^{\times},\Delta_{m})
is canonically isomorphic to the tensor product of local Hecke algebras
\bigotimes_{\ell}\mathfrak{h}(R_{m,\ell}^{\times},\Delta_{m,\ell})
for all primes
\ell
, where
\Delta_{m,\ell}
is the semigroup of elements of
R_{m,\ell}
of non-zero norm for
\ell\nmid Mp
whose image in
\M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is congruent modulo
\ell^{\mathrm{val}_{\ell}(Mp^{m})}
to a matrix of the form
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&*\\
0&*\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
with
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
, for all
\ell\nmid D
(
cf.

[
Miy06
, Theorem 5.3.5]
). This correspondence can be used to describe Hecke operators more explicitly. Let
T_{\ell}
for
\ell\nmid MDp
and
U_{\ell}
for
\ell\mid Mp
denote the Hecke operator
T(\alpha_{\ell})
for
\alpha_{\ell}
an element of norm equal to the prime number
\ell
. Then
U_{\ell}
corresponds to the element having
R_{m,\ell}^{\times}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\ell\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)R_{m,\ell}^{\times}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}R_{m,\ell}^{\times}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&i\\
0&\ell\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
in the
\ell
-factor and

elsewhere, while

is the sum of the previous double coset with

0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)R_{m,\ell}^{\times}
.
For

, we denote by
\langle d\rangle
the operator corresponding to the choice of
\alpha_{d}
with
\Norm(\alpha_{d})=d
. Then
\langle d\rangle
corresponds to the element having
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\ell^{\mathrm{val}_{\ell}(d)}&0\\
0&\ell^{\mathrm{val}_{\ell}(d)}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
on the

-factor.

3.3.
Atkin–Lehner involution

The Atkin–Lehner involution can be defined as in
[
Ogg83
, §3.3]
(see also
[
dVP13
, Section 1]
,
[
LRV12
, §2.2]
) and is described by the map
f\mapsto f|\tau_{m}
, where
\tau_{m}
is an element

of norm equal to

which normalizes the subgroup
(R_{m}^{\times})_{1}
. The involution
\tau_{m}
is not an element of the Hecke algebra
\mathfrak{h}(R_{m}^{\times},\Delta_{m})
; however, if
\widetilde{\Delta}_{m}
is the semigroup of elements of non-zero norm in

, then
\tau_{m}
is an element of the Hecke algebra
\mathfrak{h}(R_{m}^{\times},\widetilde{\Delta}_{m})
which has a similar adelic description with
\ell
-factor

with
\tau_{m,\ell}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
-\ell^{\mathrm{val}_{\ell}(Mp^{m})}&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
.

Let
(R^{\times}_{m,\ell})_{1}
be the subgroup of norm
1
elements of
R_{m,\ell}^{\times}
and
{\Gamma}_{m,\ell}
the subgroup consisting of those elements which are congruent to
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&*\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
modulo
\ell^{\mathrm{val}_{\ell}(Mp^{m})}
and fix a system of representatives
\Sigma_{m}=\{\varsigma_{a}\mid a\in(\mathbb{Z}/Mp^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\}
of
(R^{\times}_{m})_{1}/\Gamma_{m}
. Then by the strong approximation theorem we can set up an isomorphism of groups
(R^{\times}_{m})_{1}/\Gamma_{m}\simeq\prod_{\ell\mid Mp}(R^{\times}_{m,\ell})_{1}/{\Gamma}_{m,\ell}
, which we can normalize in such a way that
\varsigma_{a}
is sent to the element with
\ell
-component equal to
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\ell^{\mathrm{val}_{\ell}(a)}&*\\
0&\ell^{-\mathrm{val}_{\ell}(a)}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
for integers

such that

, with
(a,Mp^{m})=1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following formulas hold:

(1)

f|\tau_{m}|T^{*}=f|T|\tau_{m}
.

(2)

f|\tau_{m}|\langle d\rangle^{*}=f|\langle d\rangle|\tau_{m}
.

(3)

f|\tau_{m}|\varsigma_{a}=f|\varsigma_{a}^{-1}|\tau_{m}
.

Proof.

Given the above adelic description of the Hecke operators and the Atkin–Lehner involution, they reduce to a simple check. However, one can indirectly check these formulas noticing that they are well known for elliptic modular forms (see for example
[
Oht95
, (2.1.8)]
), and (since it is enough to check them on Hecke eigenforms) use Jacquet–Langlands correspondence, which is equivariant with respect to the Hecke and diamond operators, and with respect to the Atkin–Lehner involution by
[
BD96
, Theorem 1.2]
.∎

Fix a system of representatives
\sigma_{a}
for
\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)}/\Gamma_{m}
. Recall that
\Gamma_{m,p}
is the subgroup of
R_{m,p}^{\times}
consisting of elements congruent to
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&*\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
modulo
p^{m}
, and let
\Gamma_{m-1,p}^{(m)}
be the subgroup of
R_{m,p}^{\times}
consisting of elements which are congruent to
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&*\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
modulo
p^{m-1}
and congruent to
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
0&*\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
modulo
p^{m}
. Again by the strong approximation theorem we have a canonical isomorphism of groups
\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)}/\Gamma_{m}\simeq\Gamma_{m-1,p}^{(m)}/\Gamma_{m,p}
and we may choose the system of representatives so that
\sigma_{a}
is sent to a matrix
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&*\\
0&a^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
with
a\in U^{(m-1)}/U^{(m)}
, where for each integer
m\geq 1
we set
U^{(m)}\vcentcolon=1+p^{m}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We may also fix a system of representatives
\rho_{j}
for
\Gamma_{m-1}/\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)}
so that under the canonical isomorphism
\Gamma_{m-1}/\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)}\simeq\Gamma_{m-1,p}/\Gamma_{m-1,p}^{(m)}
these

are sent to matrices
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
jp^{m-1}&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
for

. Then we may write

\Gamma_{m-1}=\coprod_{a,j}\Gamma_{m}(\sigma_{a}\rho_{j})=\coprod_{j,a}(\rho_{j}\sigma_{a})\Gamma_{m}

(with
a
and
j
as before). Define for all integers

the trace maps

•

\mathrm{Tr}_{m}^{(m)}\colon S_{2}(\Gamma_{m},\mathbb{C})\rightarrow S_{2}(\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)},\mathbb{C})
by
\mathrm{Tr}_{m}^{(m)}(f)=\sum_{a}f|\sigma_{a}
;

•

\mathrm{Tr}_{m-1}^{(m)}\colon S_{2}(\Gamma^{(m)}_{m-1},\mathbb{C})\rightarrow S_{2}(\Gamma_{m-1},\mathbb{C})

by
\mathrm{Tr}_{m-1}^{(m)}(f)=\sum_{j}f|\rho_{j}
;

•

\mathrm{Tr}_{m}\colon S_{2}(\Gamma_{m},\mathbb{C})\to S_{2}(\Gamma_{m-1},\mathbb{C})
by
\mathrm{Tr}_{m}=\mathrm{Tr}^{(m)}_{m-1}\circ\mathrm{Tr}^{(m)}_{m}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have the following formulas:

(1)

\mathrm{Tr}_{m}^{(m)}(f|\tau_{m})=(\mathrm{Tr}_{m}^{(m)}(f))|\tau_{m}
.

(2)

\mathrm{Tr}_{m-1}^{(m)}(f)|\tau_{m-1}=f|\tau_{m}|U_{p}
.

Proof.

As in Lemma
3.1
, given the explicit description of Hecke operators and chosen representatives, the proof reduces to standard matrix computations; a short proof of these formulas can be obtained by using the Jacquet–Langlands correspondence, which is equivariant with respect to the operators involved, and noticing that for elliptic modular forms these formulas are well known (
[

Let

3.6.
Power series expansions

To study integrality conditions on modular forms we will combine the Katz interpretation of modular forms with the theory of Serre–Tate coordinates, which leads to a notion of power series expansions for quaternionic modular forms, following
[
Mor95
,
Mor11
,
Mor22
]
(see also
[
Bra11
,
HB15
]
). We let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}=W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
be the ring of Witt vectors of the (separable) algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

We first discuss Serre–Tate coordinates and deformation theory in the quaternionic setting, developed in
[
Mor11
, §3]
and
[
HB15
, §4]
, following ideas of Serre–Tate and Katz
[
Kat81a
]
. Let
A
be an ordinary QM abelian surface over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
; denote by
A^{\vee}
the dual abelian variety and by
\Ta_{p}(A)
and
\Ta_{p}(A^{\vee})
their
p
-adic Tate modules. Recall that for any deformation
\mathcal{A}
over an Artinian ring
R
with residue field
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, the Weil pairing induces a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-bilinear form

q_{\mathcal{A}}\colon\Ta_{p}(A)\times\Ta_{p}(A^{\vee})\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{m}(R),

where
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
is the formal multiplicative group scheme over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
; thus, we have
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{m}({R})=1+\mathfrak{m}_{R}
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
is the maximal ideal of
{R}
. Choosing
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-bases
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
of
\Ta_{p}(A)
and
\{y_{1},y_{2}\}
of
\Ta_{p}(A^{\vee})
and setting
T_{i,j}(\mathcal{A})=q_{\mathcal{A}}(x_{i},y_{j})-1
gives the
Serre–Tate coordinates
of the deformation
\mathcal{A}
. The deformation functor is pro-representable by a universal object
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{univ}}/\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{univ}}
(
[
Kat81a
, Theorem 2.1)]
). Serre–Tate coordinates are also defined for deformations
\mathcal{A}
over complete Noetherian local rings
\mathcal{R}
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{R}}
by setting
q_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{n}q_{\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{n}}
and
T_{i,j}(\mathcal{A})=q_{\mathcal{A}}(x_{i},y_{j})-1
, where
\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{n}=\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{R}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{R}}^{n})
is a deformation of
A
over the Artinian ring
\mathcal{R}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{R}}^{n}
. The map
T_{i,j}\mapsto q_{i,j}(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{univ}})
induces an isomorphism between the power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}[[T_{i,j}]]
in four variables
T_{i,j}
with
i,j=1,2
and the universal deformation ring
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{univ}}
; note that this isomorphism is non-canonical, depending on the choice of bases.

Since
A
is also equipped with QM, we can consider the subfunctor of the deformation functor of
A
which sends an Artinian local ring
R
with residue field
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
to the set of deformations of
A
as a QM abelian surface with level
V_{1}(M)
structure; this subfunctor is pro-representable by a ring
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{\mathrm{QM}}
which is isomorphic (again, non-canonically) to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}[[T]]
(see
[
Mor11
, Proposition 3.3]
and
[
HB15
, Proposition 4.5]
). The isomorphism is again obtained by using Serre–Tate coordinates for QM deformations as follows. The Tate module
\Ta_{p}(A)
of
A
inherits an action of
\mathcal{O}_{B}
and hence of
\mathcal{O}_{B}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
; the idempotent
e
induces a splitting
\Ta_{p}(A)=e\Ta_{p}(A)\oplus(1-e)\Ta_{p}(A)
and we can find a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis
\left\{x_{1},x_{2}\right\}
of
\Ta_{p}(A)
such that
ex_{1}=x_{1}
and
ex_{2}=0
. Let
x_{1}^{\vee}=\theta_{A}(x_{1})
. Then the isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}[[T]]\simeq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{\mathrm{QM}}
is given by the map
T\mapsto q_{\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{univ}}}(x_{1},x_{1}^{\vee})
.

We now introduce power series expansions of modular forms by using Serre–Tate coordinates. Fix a point
{x}=(\bar{x},\beta)\in\widehat{\text{Ig}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
in the Igusa tower,
i.e.
, the isomorphism class of a quadruple
(A,\iota,\alpha,\beta)
, lying above a point
\bar{x}=(A,\iota,\alpha)
in
\mathbb{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
and equipped with an arithmetic trivialization
\beta\colon\mu_{p^{\infty}}\simeq eA[p^{\infty}]^{0}
. The trivialization
\beta
determines by Cartier duality (see
[
Mag22
, §3.1]
) a point

x_{\beta}^{\vee}\in e^{\dagger}{\Ta_{p}(A^{\vee})({\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})}.

Take

x_{\beta}\vcentcolon=\theta_{A^{\vee}}(x_{\beta}^{\vee})\in e\Ta_{p}(A)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}),

where
\theta_{A^{\vee}}
is the dual of
\theta_{A}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{x}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{\mathrm{QM},x}
be the QM universal deformation ring of the abelian variety
A
. We fix the Serre–Tate coordinates around
\bar{x}
to be those associated with the choice of
x_{\beta}
,
i.e.
we set

T_{x}(\mathcal{A})=q_{\mathcal{A}}(x_{\beta},x_{\beta}^{\vee})-1

for each deformation of QM abelian surfaces
\mathcal{A}
of
A
. As before, denote by
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{x}/\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{x}
the universal object; so
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{x}
is a QM abelian variety over
\mathcal{R}_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}[[T_{x}]]
, equipped with a quaternionic action
\iota_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}}
. The level
V_{1}(M)
structure
\alpha
can be uniquely lifted to a level
V_{1}(M)
structure
\alpha^{\mathrm{univ}}_{x}
on
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{univ}}_{x}
by étaleness because
p\nmid M
. Since deformation theory of ordinary abelian varieties over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is equivalent to deformation theory of their
p
-divisible groups (see
[
Kat81b
, Theorem 1.2.1]
), the trivialization
\beta
can also be lifted to a trivialization
\beta_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}}
, and this defines a Drinfeld level
\Gamma_{1}(p^{m})
structure
\beta_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}}
for each
m\geq 0
.
So the quadruplet

T_{\mathcal{A}_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}}}=(\mathcal{A}_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}},\iota_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}},\alpha_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}},\beta_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}})

gives rise to a universal test object over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}[[T_{x}]]
.
For each
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}
-algebra
R
, base change to
R^{\mathrm{univ}}
gives rise to a universal test object over
R
, denoted with the same symbol to simpify the notation.
We have an isomorphism
{\Ta_{p}(A^{\vee})({\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})}\simeq\Hom(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{univ}},\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
; the point
x_{\beta}^{\vee}
determines an homomorphism
\varphi_{\beta}
and we can define
\omega_{\mathcal{A}_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}}}=\varphi_{\beta}^{*}(dT/T)
, where
dT/T
is the standard differential on the formal multiplicative group.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}
-algebra,
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{m},R)
and

. The formal series
f(T_{x})=f(T_{\mathcal{A}_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}}},\omega_{\mathcal{A}_{x}^{\mathrm{univ}}})
in

-expansion of

.

The reader is referred to
[
Mor95
, §2.2]
for more details on this definition.

Remark 3.7
.

We usually distinguish between modular forms and associated power series by writing
f
for the first and
f(T_{x})
for the second.

We also recall the following fact. The operator
v_{p}
introduced in §
3.4
corresponds to the operator
V
in
[
HB15
, §3.6]
on Katz modular forms; to uniformize the notation with
loc. cit.
we also let

be the corresponding operator. The action of the operators

and

on power series has been studied in
[

, §4.4]
; in particular, by
[

, we know that if

, then

as well, and if

, then

(see also
[
Bur17
, Lemma 5.2]
).

3.7.
Integral modular forms

We now use power series expansion to introduce a new integrality condition on modular forms. We work throughout with a field extension
F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which contains the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and let
\mathcal{O}
be its valuation ring. We also fix a point
x
in
\mathcal{X}_{m}(\mathcal{O})
and we assume that
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}_{m}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
belongs to
\mathrm{Ig}_{m}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, where recall that
\mathbb{X}_{m}
is the special fiber of
\mathcal{X}_{m}
. For
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{m},F)
, we may then consider
f(T_{x})\in F[[T_{x}]]
.

Definition 3.8
.

Define the
Serre–Tate
\mathcal{O}
-valued modular forms

S_{k}^{\mathrm{ST}}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O})
to be the
\mathcal{O}
-submodule of
S_{k}(\Gamma_{m},F)
consisting of those
f
such that
f(T_{x})
belong to
\mathcal{O}[[T_{x}]]
.

Using the compatibility under base change, we see that

(3.3)

S_{k}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O})\subseteq S_{k}^{\mathrm{ST}}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O}).

The canonical covering map
{X}_{m/F}\twoheadrightarrow X_{m-1/F}^{(m)}
induces a canonical injective map by pull-back

S_{k}(\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)},F)\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}S_{k}(\Gamma_{m},F),

and we can define

S_{k}^{\mathrm{ST}}(\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)},\mathcal{O})=S_{k}(\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)},F)\cap S_{k}^{\mathrm{ST}}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O}).

We also note the following result concerning integrality conditions. Recall the system of representatives
\{\sigma_{a}\}
of
\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)}/\Gamma_{m}\simeq\Gamma_{m-1,p}^{(m)}/\Gamma_{m,p}
chosen in §
3.3
so that
\sigma_{a}
is sent to the matrix
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&*\\
0&a^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
with
a\in U^{(m-1)}/U^{(m)}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

If
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{ST}}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O})
then
f|\sigma_{a}\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{ST}}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O})
.

Proof.

If
{x}=(\bar{x},\beta)\in\widehat{\text{Ig}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
is the fixed point in the Igusa tower, then the action of
\sigma_{a}
corresponds to the choice of a different trivialization
\beta_{a}
, and therefore we need to look at the Serre–Tate expansion at the point
x_{a}=(\bar{x},\beta_{a})\in\widehat{\text{Ig}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
; however, different choices of trivializations gives rise to different isomorphisms of the universal deformation space of the QM abelian surface
A_{\bar{x}}
, corresponding to
\bar{x}
, with the same power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}[[X]]
. Since
f(T_{x})\in\mathcal{O}[[T_{x}]]
and the isomorphisms above are equivariant with respect to base change to
\mathcal{O}
, then
f|\sigma_{a}(T_{x})=f(T_{x_{a}})
also belongs to
\mathcal{O}[[T_{x}]]
.
∎

We now look more closely to the case of weight
k=2
. Let
J_{m}
be the Jacobian variety of
X_{m}
. Let
\mathcal{J}_{m/\mathcal{O}}
be the Néron model of
J_{m/F}\vcentcolon=J_{m}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}F
; then we have a canonical isomorphism of
F
-schemes
\mathcal{J}_{m/\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}K\simeq J_{m/F}
. Let
\mathcal{J}_{m/\mathcal{O}}^{0}
be the connected component of
\mathcal{J}_{m/\mathcal{O}}
(
[
Maz78
, §1b)]
).
We then have an injective map of
\mathcal{O}
-modules
\mathrm{Cot}(\mathcal{J}_{m/\mathcal{O}}^{0})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Cot}(J_{m/F})
on the level of cotangent spaces and a canonical isomorphism of

-vector spaces

\mathrm{Cot}(J_{m/F})\simeq H^{0}(X_{m/F},\Omega^{1}_{X_{m/F}}).

Composing these maps gives an injective map of
\mathcal{O}
-modules

(3.4)

\mathrm{Cot}(\mathcal{J}_{m/\mathcal{O}}^{0})\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}S_{2}(\Gamma_{m},F).

By definition, the image of (
3.4
) is contained in
S_{2}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O})
. Define finally

S_{k}^{*}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O})=\{f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{m},F):f|\tau_{m}\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{ST}}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O})\},

S_{k}^{\prime}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O})=S_{k}^{\mathrm{ST}}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O})\cap S_{k}^{*}(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{O}).

Remark 3.10
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{O}\supseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\zeta_{p}]
. Then the image of (

) is equal to

.
The proof of this fact is adapted from
[

, Proposition 8.4]
. It is clear that the image of (

) is contained in

. We prove the opposite inclusion: for that, fix

a regular differential on

which belongs to

. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}
be a minimal regular resolution of

.
Then we have the following isomorphisms (

[

, §3.4]
)

The first isomorphism follows from the discussion of
[
Maz77
, II, 3]
and
[

, §6]
, while the second follows from the discussion in
[

, §2(e)]
; note that both results can be applied to the case of Shimura curves because

is Cohen–Macaulay, purely of relative dimension

. Now recall that

3.8.
Inverse limit of modular forms

We assume as before that
F
is a subfield of
\mathbb{C}_{p}
which contains the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and let

be its valuation ring.

The trace maps introduced in §

can be extended to higher weight and reinterpreted in modular terms as follows:

•

\mathrm{Tr}_{m}^{(m)}\colon S_{k}(\Gamma_{m},F)\rightarrow S_{k}(\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)},F)
is given by

where, given

\mathrm{Tr}_{m-1}^{(m)}\colon S_{k}(\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)},F)\rightarrow S_{k}(\Gamma_{m-1},F)

where, given a test object

for
\Gamma_{m-1}

are all test objects for for
\Gamma_{m-1}^{(m)}
such that

are all finite flat subgroups of rank

with

•

\mathrm{Tr}_{m}\colon S_{k}(\Gamma_{m},F)\rightarrow S_{k}(\Gamma_{m-1},F)
is given by
\mathrm{Tr}_{m}=\mathrm{Tr}_{m-1}^{(m)}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{m}^{(m)}
which
takes

to

The following result generalizes

an algebraic Hecke character of infinity type
(1,0)
of conductor
\mathfrak{c}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}

whose

-adic avatar takes values in

be defined from

be a primitive Hida family as in §

,
fix a generator
\eta_{\mathbb{I}}
as before and let

be the associated power series. For any arithmetic morphism
\kappa\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}
, of level
\Gamma_{m}
and signature
(k,\epsilon_{\mathrm{triv}})
, where
\epsilon_{\mathrm{triv}}
is the trivial character
and

let

be the unique modular form in

whose power series expansion coincides with

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\kappa\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}
be an arithmetic morphism of weight
k\equiv k_{0}\mod 2(p-1)
.
Then

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{\mathrm{an}}(\kappa)=\vartheta^{-1}_{\kappa}(c)\mathscr{L}_{f_{\kappa},\xi_{\kappa}}
.

Proof.

For any continuous
\varphi\colon\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\widetilde{\mathbb{I}}
, using its Mahler expansion, we obtain for
\mathfrak{a}\in\Pic(\mathcal{O}_{c})
:

\kappa\left(\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\varphi(u)d{\mu_{\eta_{\mathbb{I}},\mathfrak{a}}}(u)\right)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}(\kappa\circ\varphi)(u)d{\mu_{f_{\kappa},\mathfrak{a}}}(u).

Recall that, for an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{c}
, we write
\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{a})=c^{-1}\sharp(\mathcal{O}_{c}/\mathfrak{a})
; if
\mathfrak{a}=a\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{c}\cap K
for an element
a\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
,
we have

\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{a})=c^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{N}^{-1}_{K}(a)
.
Choose representatives
\mathfrak{a}
of
\Pic(\mathcal{O}_{c})
such that

\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{a}

and
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}\nmid\mathfrak{a}
; by (
6.2
) we then have

\hat{\chi}^{-1}_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{a})=\hat{\chi}^{-1}_{\kappa}(a)=\mathbf{N}_{K}(a)^{k/2-1}=\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{a})^{-k/2+1}c^{-k/2+1}

and
\xi_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{a})=\hat{\xi}_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{a})
.
Therefore, since
\xi_{\kappa}
is unramified at
p
, we have

\begin{split}\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\kappa,\varphi)&=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\in\Pic(\mathcal{O}_{c})}({\hat{\chi}}_{\kappa}^{-1}{\hat{\xi}}_{\kappa})(\mathfrak{a})\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{a})^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}(\kappa\circ\varphi\big|[\mathfrak{a}])(u)d\mu_{\eta_{\mathbb{I}},\mathfrak{a}}(u)\\
&=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\in\Pic(\mathcal{O}_{c})}c^{-k/2+1}\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{a})^{-\frac{k}{2}}{\xi}_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{a})\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}\xi_{\kappa,\mathfrak{p}}(u)(\kappa\circ\varphi\big|[\mathfrak{a}])(u)d\mu_{f_{\kappa},\mathfrak{a}}(u)\\
&=c^{-k/2+1}\mathscr{L}_{f_{\kappa},\xi_{\kappa}}(\kappa\circ\varphi).\end{split}

In particular we have

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\kappa,\mathbf{1}_{U})=c^{-k/2+1}\mathscr{L}_{f_{\kappa},\xi_{\kappa}}(\mathbf{1}_{U})

for any open compact subset
U
of the Galois group
\Gamma_{\infty}
, where
\mathbf{1}_{U}
is the characteristic function of
U
. We conclude the proof via the equivalence between measures on
\Gamma_{\infty}
and additive functions on the set of open compact subsets of
\Gamma_{\infty}
.

Let
\kappa\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}
be an arithmetic morphism of weight
k\equiv k_{0}\mod 2(p-1)
. If
\hat{\varphi}\colon\Gamma_{\infty}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}
corresponds via
\mathrm{rec}_{K}
to the
p
-adic avatar of an anticyclotomic Hecke character
\varphi
of infinity type
(n,-n)

for some integer
n\geq 0
,
then

\left(\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\kappa,\hat{\varphi})\right)^{2}=C\cdot L(f_{\kappa}^{\sharp},\xi_{\kappa}\varphi,k/2)

where

C
is a non-zero constant (depending on
\kappa
,
\xi_{\kappa}
,
\varphi
and
K
, and, up to a
p
-adic unit, on the choice of
\eta_{\mathbb{I}}
).

Proof.

In view of Lemma
6.1
, the Serre–Tate expansions of
f_{\kappa}^{[p]}=(f^{\sharp}_{\kappa})^{[p]}
are the same, putting us in the conditions of Theorem
6.3
. The relation between the analytic
L
-function
\mathscr{L}_{f_{\kappa},\xi_{\kappa}}
and the algebraic variant in the right-hand side, and the nature of the constant
C
, is discussed in
[
Mag22
, Theorem 4.6]
; when
\varphi
is unramified, see
[
HB15
, 8.11]
and
[
Mor22
, Theorem 0.1]
for an explicit expression of
C
.
∎

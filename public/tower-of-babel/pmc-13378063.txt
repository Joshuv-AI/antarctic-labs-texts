Application of artificial intelligence-assisted education for postoperative pulmonary rehabilitation: a hybrid model of AI and nurse-driven support
Authors: Chenminghuang Shen, Jiamin Chen, Guanzhi Ye, Chen Zhang, Guojun Geng, Ru Yan, Bingbing Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo explore the clinical value of AI-assisted pulmonary rehabilitation education in patients undergoing thoracoscopic surgery for lung cancer.MethodsThis was a non-randomized, two-period comparative study comparing AI-assisted education with routine education for postoperative pulmonary rehabilitation in lung cancer patients. Patients who underwent thoracoscopic radical lung cancer surgery in a Chinese general hospital between January and December 2024 were selected. Based on the type of education received, patients were divided into the routine education group and the AI-assisted education group. Relevant information was collected through electronic medical records.ResultsA total of 128 cases were included, with 65 in the routine group and 63 in the AI-assisted group. Baseline data showed no significant intergroup differences. Compared with routine education, AI-assisted education significantly improved patient adherence to exercise training (51.6 ± 7.5 vs. 43.1 ± 6.4, P < 0.001) and postoperative self-efficacy (91.3 ± 6.7 vs. 77.2 ± 7.0, P < 0.001). The incidence of complications was lower in the AI-assisted group (4.8% vs. 15.4%,). Psychological assessment revealed lower depression and anxiety scores among patients receiving AI-assisted education (depression: 8.4 ± 2.6 vs. 10.1 ± 3.4, P = 0.001; anxiety: 8.6 ± 2.3 vs. 11.5 ± 2.5, P < 0.001).ConclusionThe collaboration between artificial intelligence-assisted education and nursing staff in the lung rehabilitation education model can enhance postoperative rehabilitation adherence and self-efficacy in lung cancer patients. Further studies, including randomized controlled trials, are needed to confirm AI’s benefits and long-term impact on patient outcomes.

Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the most common malignancies worldwide and remains the leading cause of cancer-related deaths. According to statistics from the World Health Organization (WHO), the number of deaths caused by lung cancer each year has surpassed that of all other types of cancer combined [1]. Approximately 85% of diagnosed cases are non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), for which standard treatments include surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or a combination of these modalities [2]. For patients with early-stage NSCLC, surgery remains the preferred treatment option, with a 5-year survival rate ranging from 68 to 92% [3, 4]. Postoperative pulmonary rehabilitation training plays a critical role in improving lung function, enhancing quality of life, and reducing the risk of postoperative complications. [5].

Pulmonary rehabilitation has been confirmed as an effective and non-invasive comprehensive intervention that combines exercise training and education to improve the prognosis and quality of life in patients with respiratory diseases [6, 7]. Pulmonary rehabilitation training has been widely applied to patients undergoing thoracoscopic radical surgery for lung cancer. Through respiratory training, exercise training, and health education, it promotes postoperative recovery of lung function [8]. However, the effectiveness of pulmonary rehabilitation training largely depends on the implementation of educational interventions. Traditional pulmonary rehabilitation education is often characterized by one-way information delivery led by healthcare providers, lacking interaction and personalization, and thus may not adequately meet the diverse needs of patients.

With the development of artificial intelligence (AI) technology, its application in the medical field has gradually expanded, particularly in patient education and personalized rehabilitation guidance. AI can provide individualized rehabilitation plans, thereby optimizing the effectiveness of educational interventions [9, 10]. Against this backdrop, AI-assisted patient education methods have emerged as innovative tools increasingly adopted in medical practice. Previous studies have shown that AI-assisted tools such as ChatGPT can effectively reduce perioperative anxiety and improve patient satisfaction among those undergoing total knee arthroplasty (TKA) [11]. While AI has been applied in various aspects of healthcare, there is a clear gap in research regarding AI-driven interactive and adaptive education for post-operative rehabilitation in lung cancer patients. Specifically, studies that explore the use of AI tools like ChatGPT to guide personalized rehabilitation education and improve patient outcomes are limited, despite their potential to enhance engagement and recovery. This study aims to evaluate the application effectiveness of an AI-assisted pulmonary rehabilitation education method in the postoperative rehabilitation training of patients undergoing thoracoscopic radical surgery for lung cancer.

Methods and materials

Study population

This study was a retrospective analysis targeting patients who underwent thoracoscopic surgery for lung cancer. Patients who underwent thoracoscopic radical resection for lung cancer at the Department of Thoracic Surgery, First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University, between January 2024 and December 2024 were selected as the study population. All patients received postoperative treatment following a unified therapeutic protocol, although the rehabilitation education methods differed across time periods. Based on the type of education received, patients were divided into the routine education group and the AI-assisted education group. Patients who underwent thoracoscopic lung cancer surgery between January 2024 and May 2024 and received routine education were assigned to the routine education group. Patients who underwent surgery between June 2024 and December 2024 and received AI-assisted education were assigned to the AI-assisted group. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of our institution, with a waiver for written informed consent. The AI-assisted education intervention was part of the standard clinical care provided to the patients and not a specific research protocol.

Inclusion criteria were as follows: 1. diagnosed with non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and underwent thoracoscopic lobectomy [12]; 2. received postoperative pulmonary rehabilitation training, including deep breathing exercises, effective coughing, and exercise rehabilitation; 3. underwent at least one pulmonary function test before surgery; 4. the ability to engage in normal verbal communication, provide appropriate feedback to questions, and cooperate in completing the rehabilitation education process. Exclusion criteria included: 1. pre-existing severe chronic pulmonary diseases (e.g., advanced chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, interstitial pulmonary fibrosis) that could affect the effectiveness of pulmonary rehabilitation training; 2. severe postoperative complications (e.g., massive hemorrhage, severe infection, pulmonary embolism) that made completion of pulmonary rehabilitation impossible; 3. undergoing pneumonectomy; 4. incomplete postoperative follow-up data, such as missing pulmonary function assessments or exercise compliance records.

Education content

Both groups of patients received the "Postoperative Pulmonary Rehabilitation Training Manual for Thoracoscopic Lung Cancer Surgery," which included five main areas: exercise training, breathing exercises, nutritional and dietary guidance, psychological counseling, and smoking cessation counseling. The specific contents were: 1. exercise training, including aerobic and strength training; 2. breathing exercises, including diaphragmatic breathing, pursed-lip breathing, and balloon-blowing exercises; 3. nutritional and dietary guidance, including dietary principles and nutritional advice; 4. psychological counseling; 5. smoking cessation counseling. The routine education group received traditional postoperative education, which included exercise training, breathing exercises, dietary guidance, and psychological counseling. This education was standardized across all participants, with the same content delivered by the nursing staff to ensure consistency. The education protocol was designed based on institutional guidelines.

In the AI-assisted education group, in addition to routine education, ChatGPT was introduced to assist patients in learning pulmonary rehabilitation content and providing psychological counseling. The AI-assisted education group received educational content guided by ChatGPT. However, it is important to note that the primary function of AI was to assist in providing scientifically precise educational responses. Nurses reviewed and communicated the AI-generated content to patients, ensuring that the information was accurate and appropriate, but did not spend additional time or attention solely because of the use of AI. All nurses received standardized training before implementing ChatGPT-assisted education to ensure consistency in the education process. ChatGPT conducted preliminary evaluations based on patients’ medical history, educational level, cognitive ability, and pulmonary function data to determine individualized education content and difficulty. After each educational session, ChatGPT asked key questions (e.g., "What is the frequency and duration of respiratory exercises?") and used natural language processing (NLP) to assess the accuracy of patients’ responses. If an incorrect response was identified (e.g., answering "twice daily" instead of "three times daily"), ChatGPT would repeat the explanation and provide simple mnemonic aids (e.g., "Morning, noon, and night, three times right") to ensure understanding. In addition, the supervising nurse critically evaluated the appropriateness of ChatGPT’s responses and synthesized them before responding to patients’ questions, thereby establishing a closed-loop model of “AI plus human verification. If patients raised additional questions during the education process, ChatGPT was used to generate clear and understandable explanations to help patients and their families.

Selection of artificial intelligence model and parameter settings

We used the ChatGPT 4.0 API with default parameters to generate patient-specific educational dialogues. For each patient inquiry, a new dialogue window was initiated to ensure the independence and accuracy of the responses and avoid potential interference from previous interactions.

Data collection

Postoperative exercise compliance was measured using a pulmonary functional exercise compliance questionnaire adapted from the original functional exercise compliance scale [13]. The questionnaire includes 16 items scored from 0 to 4 points, with higher scores reflecting better adherence to respiratory exercise programs. The adapted scale showed good psychometric properties after adjusting the original functional exercise items to pulmonary-specific items.

The Self-Efficacy Scale for Postoperative Rehabilitation Management in Lung Cancer (SESPRMLC) was used to assess postoperative self-efficacy [14]. The scale covers six dimensions: acquisition and application of rehabilitation information (5 items), management of treatment side effects (3 items), symptom self-management (4 items), rehabilitation training and skill development (5 items), daily life management (4 items), and emotional regulation (6 items), totaling 27 items. A five-point Likert scale was used, ranging from "very unconfident" to "very confident," with scores ranging from 27 to 135 points. Higher scores indicated stronger postoperative rehabilitation self-efficacy.

The Depression Anxiety Stress Scale-21 (DASS-21) was used to measure depressive, anxiety, and stress symptoms [15]. This 21-item self-report scale is a short version of the standard 42-item DASS, and its reliability and validity have been evaluated in China. It contains three subscales assessing depression, anxiety, and stress. Participants rated items on a 4-point Likert scale (0–3), ranging from "does not apply at all" to "applies most or all of the time," with higher scores indicating greater severity. Each subscale score ranges from 0 to 21, and the overall score ranges from 0 to 63.

Relevant clinical baseline data were collected from the hospital’s electronic medical record system, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, pulmonary function, clinical staging, education level, and living environment. Data on the length of hospital stay, duration of chest drain placement, and postoperative complications (including pleural effusion, incision infection, air leak, and incision bleeding) were also collected. Patients’ self-efficacy scores, postoperative exercise compliance scores, and DASS-21 scores were recorded. Neither the patients nor their families were informed that they were participating in a research study. This helped to minimize the possibility of Hawthorne or expectation effects. All data collection was conducted strictly for research purposes, adhering to medical ethics and privacy protection principles. A dedicated research assistant was responsible for data collection and organization to ensure data accuracy and security.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Normality was assessed using skewness and kurtosis. Independent sample t-tests were used for comparisons if normality was confirmed; otherwise, the Mann–Whitney U test was used. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. All data analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics software, version 25. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

There were no significant differences between the two groups regarding baseline characteristics such as age, sex, BMI, smoking history, and baseline forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV₁) (P > 0.05). Discussion

This retrospective study preliminarily evaluated the effectiveness of AI-assisted pulmonary rehabilitation education in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lung cancer surgery. The results demonstrated that AI-assisted rehabilitation education yielded greater benefits for postoperative patients compared to routine education methods. Compared to traditional approaches, AI-assisted education improved patients’ knowledge acquisition, self-efficacy, and rehabilitation compliance. Additionally, AI-assisted education contributed to alleviating postoperative depression and anxiety, thereby enhancing the overall quality of life. However, there was no significant difference in postoperative length of hospital stay between the two groups.

Pulmonary rehabilitation outcomes are one of the key factors influencing the prognosis of lung cancer surgery. Previous studies on postoperative pulmonary rehabilitation have indicated that effective pulmonary rehabilitation guidance can alleviate postoperative symptom burden [16]. Under conventional education models, patients often show poor adherence to rehabilitation training, mainly due to insufficient understanding of rehabilitation content and inadequate mastery of operational techniques. Traditional education methods are typically rigid and lack personalized guidance tailored to individual patient conditions [17]. In this study, AI-assisted education utilizing the ChatGPT model dynamically generated personalized educational content based on patients’ educational background, language habits, and pulmonary function status. Through techniques such as questioning, error correction, and mnemonic reinforcement, AI strengthened patients’ understanding of key training points. The input-feedback-reinforcement model offers a plausible explanation for the observed improvements, suggesting that personalized, interactive feedback could enhance learning and adherence. However, this remains speculative, as the study did not test specific mediators. Future research should investigate the underlying mechanisms to confirm or refine this hypothesis. Our findings showed that patients in the AI-assisted group achieved significantly higher scores on the pulmonary rehabilitation exercise compliance scale compared to those in the routine education group. This is consistent with the findings of Song et al., who suggested that personalization, interactivity, and repeatability of information delivery are essential prerequisites for improving compliance [18, 19]. Furthermore, the AI-assisted group demonstrated significantly higher postoperative exercise compliance scores than the routine education group.

In this study, the evaluation of psychological states between the two education methods showed that the AI-assisted group had lower anxiety and depression scores compared to the routine education group. Negative psychological states are important factors affecting postoperative rehabilitation outcomes in lung cancer patients [20]. After surgery, patients often experience anxiety, depression, and psychological stress due to uncertainties regarding disease prognosis, postoperative pain, and functional recovery, which can diminish their motivation to participate actively in rehabilitation training [21]. Other studies have suggested that meticulous nursing care and enhanced preoperative communication can alleviate tension and anxiety in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lung cancer surgery [22–24]. AI can provide personalized psychological counseling by engaging patients in customized dialogues, addressing issues encountered during rehabilitation, and using positive reinforcement and cognitive guidance to reduce psychological burdens. Gan W et al. reported that AI-assisted informed consent significantly reduced preoperative anxiety levels and improved patient satisfaction in total knee arthroplasty (TKA) patients [11]. A key advantage of AI-assisted education lies in its ability to enhance education through personalization and reinforcement, which increases patient engagement and rehabilitation adherence. Traditional education models, which often lack sufficient interaction, may not meet the individual needs of patients. In contrast, AI, through real-time feedback and targeted reinforcement, strengthens the education process and thereby enhances rehabilitation outcomes. Nevertheless, in the field of postoperative pulmonary rehabilitation for lung cancer, there remains a scarcity of research on the application of AI-assisted education. Our findings further support the positive impact of AI-assisted education on postoperative recovery in lung cancer patients.

Despite accounting for many potential influencing factors in the study design, several limitations remain. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a limited sample size, and differences in patient enrollment periods may have introduced time bias. Due to the retrospective nature of this study, outcome assessors were not blinded to group assignments. However, the staff involved in data collection were unaware of which patients were assigned to the study groups, thus minimizing potential bias during data collection. The educational process placed certain demands on patients’ literacy levels; elderly patients or those with lower educational attainment may have had difficulties completing the education sessions. Third, this study only evaluated the impact of AI-assisted education during hospitalization and did not assess its influence during subsequent home-based rehabilitation. This study is exploratory in nature, and its sample size is insufficient to detect differences in low-frequency events such as complications. In future research, we will conduct prospective studies to further evaluate the broader applicability and long-term effects of AI-assisted educational interventions.

Conclusion

The collaboration between artificial intelligence-assisted education and nursing staff in the lung rehabilitation education model can enhance postoperative rehabilitation adherence and self-efficacy in lung cancer patients. Further studies, including randomized controlled trials, are needed to confirm AI’s benefits and long-term impact on patient outcomes.

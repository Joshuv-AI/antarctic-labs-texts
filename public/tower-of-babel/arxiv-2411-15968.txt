Abstract
. Let p p and q q be different primes and let G G be a finite q q -solvable group. We prove that Irr p ′ ⁡ ( G ) ⊆ Irr q ′ ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G) if and only if 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) ⊆ 𝐍 G ​ ( Q ) {\bf N}_{G}(P)\subseteq{\bf N}_{G}(Q) and 𝐂 Q ′ ​ ( P ) = 1 {\bf C}_{Q^{\prime}}(P)=1 for some P ∈ Syl p ⁡ ( G ) P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G) and Q ∈ Syl q ⁡ ( G ) Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}(G) . Further, if B B is a q q -block of G G and p p does not divide the degree of any character in Irr ⁡ ( B ) \operatorname{Irr}(B) then we prove that a Sylow p p -subgroup of G G is normalized by a defect group of B B . This removes the p p -solvability condition of two theorems of G. Navarro and T. R. Wolf.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
p
and
q
be different primes. We denote by
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
the set of irreducible characters of
G
whose degree is not divisible by
p
. In this note we prove the following result.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a
q
-solvable group. Then
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
if and only if
{\bf N}_{G}(P)\subseteq{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
and
{\bf C}_{Q^{\prime}}(P)=1
for some
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
and
Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}(G)
.

Theorem A was proven for
\{p,q\}
-separable groups in
[
NW98
]
(by Burnside’s
p^{a}q^{b}
theorem,
\{p,q\}
-separable groups are exactly the groups that are both
p
-solvable and
q
-solvable). The authors point out that the
q
-solvability hypothesis is necessary (as in
[
NW98
, Example 10]
, take
K=\operatorname{PSL}(2,3^{5})
and
a
an automorphism of
K
of order
5
, then
G=K\langle a\rangle
with
p=5
and
q=11
shows that Theorem A does not hold for
p
-solvable groups). They speculate that the stronger version for
q
-solvable groups “
is heavily related to the validity of McKay’s conjecture
and similar results
”. Here we confirm their speculation by using the recently proved McKay conjecture
[
CS24
]
.

The main result of
[
BN00
]
gives a version of
[
NW98
]
for
q
-Brauer characters in
\{p,q\}
-separable groups. This was later relaxed to
q
-solvable groups in
[
BNRS22
]
, and we use this latter result to obtain Theorem A. We note that, unlike the results for
\{p,q\}
-separable groups, both
[
BNRS22
, Theorem A]
and our Theorem A rely on the classification of finite simple groups.

We mention that, without any separability condition, it has been proven in
[
NT
]
that
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
if and only if
{\bf N}_{G}(Q)={\bf N}_{G}(P)
for some
Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}(G)
and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
and both
P
and
Q
are abelian.

Focusing on blocks, we prove the following.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a
q
-solvable group and let
B
be a
q
-block of
G
with defect group
Q
. If
p
does not divide
\chi(1)
for all
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B)
then
Q\subseteq{\bf N}_{G}(P)
for some
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
.

The statement of Theorem B was proven to hold for
\{p,q\}
-separable groups in
[
NW01
]
without using the classification, but our proof of Theorem B uses the classification. As mentioned in the introduction of
[
NW01
]
, the principal
2
-block of the sporadic Janko group
J_{1}
for
p=2
and
q=5
shows that the conclusion of Theorem B does not hold for arbitrary finite groups, and requires some separability condition (although it has been proven recently in
[
MS24
, Theorem C]
that there are remarkably few counterexamples for principal blocks of arbitrary finite groups). We have not found any
p
-solvable counterexamples to the conclusion of Theorem B for nonprincipal blocks.

We point out that, while Theorem A depends on the McKay conjecture being true, Theorem B depends on the McKay conjecture being proved
in a certain way
, that is, proving that the inductive McKay conditions defined in
[
IMN07
]
hold for certain simple groups (we do not need this explicitly here but it is used in the proof of
[
LNPT24
, Theorem A]
, on which our work relies).

The divisibility of degrees of characters in a
q
-block by a different prime
p
has become an active research topic in recent years
[
GMV19
,
MN20
,
LWWZ24
,
GM24
]
. It remains open to describe group-theoretically when
p
does not divide the degrees of all height zero characters in a
q
-block. For
\{p,q\}
-separable groups, and after Fong–Reynolds reductions such as the ones in the proof of Theorem B, this would already be a relative version of
[
NRS22
, Theorem A]
with
\pi=\{p\}^{\prime}
and
\rho=\{p,q\}^{\prime}
(by relative, we mean that the condition on degrees is satisfied only for the irreducible characters of
G
lying over a character of a normal subgroup).

2.
Theorem A

Theorem A is proven in two steps. First we assume the local condition
{\bf N}_{G}(P)\subseteq{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
and prove the result in this case. To complete the proof of Theorem A we use the Fong–Swan theorem and the main result of
[
BNRS22
]
. One of our main ingredients is the following refinement of the McKay conjecture for
q
-solvable groups, which relies on the classification of finite simple groups.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be a
q
-solvable group and
Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}(G)
. Then there is a bijection

f:\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)\rightarrow\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))

where
f(\chi)(1)
divides
\chi(1)
and
\chi(1)/f(\chi)(1)
divides
|G:{\bf N}_{G}(Q)|
for all
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
.

Proof.

This is proved for solvable groups in
[
Tur07
]
and for
q
-solvable groups in
[
Riz19
]
assuming the divisibility of degrees in the Glauberman correspondence, which was proved in
[
Gec20
]
after a reduction to simple groups in
[
HT94
]
.
∎

In this paper we write
\pi=\{p,q\}
, so
\operatorname{Irr}_{\pi^{\prime}}(G)
denotes the set of irreducible characters of
G
whose degree is not divisible by
p
nor
q
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
G
be a
q
-solvable group,
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
,
Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}(G)
and assume
{\bf N}_{G}(P)\subseteq{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
. Then
{\bf C}_{Q^{\prime}}(P)=1
if and only if
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
.

Proof.

First we claim that

\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)

if and only if

\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q)).

Let
f:\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)\rightarrow\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))
be the bijection from Theorem
1
. By hypothesis,
p
does not divide
|G:{\bf N}_{G}(Q)|
and therefore
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
has
p^{\prime}
-degree if and only if
f(\chi)\in\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))
has
p^{\prime}
-degree. Thus
|\operatorname{Irr}_{\pi^{\prime}}(G)|=|\operatorname{Irr}_{\pi^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))|
. Now, by the McKay conjecture
[
CS24
]
,
|\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=|\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(P))|=|\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))|
because
{\bf N}_{G}(P)={\bf N}_{{\bf N}_{G}(Q)}(P)
. Thus
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
if and only if
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=\operatorname{Irr}_{\pi^{\prime}}(G)
if and only if
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))=\operatorname{Irr}_{\pi^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))
if and only if
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(Q))
, as desired.

It follows that we may assume
Q\triangleleft\,G
. It is easy to see that in this case
\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)=\operatorname{Irr}(G/Q^{\prime})
. By the Glauberman correspondence
[
Nav18
, Theorem 2.9]
,
{\bf C}_{Q^{\prime}}(P)=1
if and only if
|\operatorname{Irr}_{P}(Q^{\prime})|=1
, where
\operatorname{Irr}_{P}(Q^{\prime})
denotes the set of
P
-invariant characters of
Q^{\prime}
.

Assume first that
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
. Let
\tau\in\operatorname{Irr}_{P}(Q^{\prime})
. We have that
\tau
extends to
\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}(PQ^{\prime})
by
[
Nav18
, Corollary 6.2]
. Now
\mu^{G}(1)=\tau(1)|G:PQ^{\prime}|
is not divisible by
p
and it follows that there is
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
with
[\chi,\mu^{G}]\neq 0
, so
\chi_{Q^{\prime}}
contains
\tau
. By hypothesis,
\chi
has
q^{\prime}
-degree, so
Q^{\prime}\subseteq{\rm ker}(\chi)
and therefore
\tau=1_{Q^{\prime}}
. It follows that
|\operatorname{Irr}_{P}(Q^{\prime})|=1
and we are done.

Now assume
{\bf C}_{Q^{\prime}}(P)=1
so that
\operatorname{Irr}_{P}(Q^{\prime})=1
. Then if
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
, by
[
Nav18
, Lemma 9.3]
,
\chi_{Q^{\prime}}
contains a
P
-invariant irreducible constituent. By hypothesis,
\chi_{Q^{\prime}}
contains
1_{Q^{\prime}}
so
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G/Q^{\prime})
and we conclude that
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
, as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem A.
By Proposition
2
, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
implies
{\bf N}_{G}(P)\subseteq{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
for some
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
and
Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}(G)
. Now let
\varphi\in\mathrm{IBr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
(where we are considering
q
-Brauer characters). By the Fong–Swan theorem
[
Nav98
, Theorem 10.1]
there is
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
with
\chi^{0}=\varphi
. By hypothesis,
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
and therefore
\varphi\in\mathrm{IBr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
. It follows that
\mathrm{IBr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\mathrm{IBr}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
so by
[
BNRS22
, Theorem A]
we have
{\bf N}_{G}(P)\subseteq{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
for some
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
and
Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}(G)
, and the result follows. ∎

3.
Theorem B

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem B. Our strategy is to prove a version of
[
NW01
, Theorem 3]
and then apply Fong–Reynolds reductions. Our proof relies on the following result due to M. W. Liebeck, G. Navarro, C. E. Praeger and P. H. Tiep, which appeared in
[
LNPT24
]
, and which uses the classification of finite simple groups.

Theorem 3

(Liebeck–Navarro–Praeger–Tiep)
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
\pi
a set of primes. Let
Z\triangleleft\,G
and
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(Z)
. Assume that
\chi(1)/\lambda(1)
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number for any
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
. If
\{5,7\}\not\subseteq\pi
or
\{2,3\}\not\subseteq\pi^{\prime}
or
G
does not involve
{\sf A}_{7}
then
G/Z
has abelian Hall
\pi
-subgroups.

The following is our version of
[
NW01
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
Z\triangleleft\,G
and
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(Z)
be
G
-invariant. Assume that
G/Z
is
q
-solvable and that
\lambda
extends to
Q
where
Q/Z\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}(G/Z)
. If
p
does not divide
\chi(1)/\lambda(1)
for all
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
then a Sylow
q
-subgroup of
G/Z
normalizes a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G/Z
.

Proof.

Write
\pi=\{p,q\}
. We argue by induction on
|G/Z|
.

Step 1: We may assume
Z\subseteq{\bf Z}(G)
,
Z
has
\pi^{\prime}
-order and
\lambda
is faithful. In particular,
G
is not
p
-solvable.

By
[
Nav18
, Corollary 5.9]
there is an isomorphic character triple
(G^{*},Z^{*},\lambda^{*})
where
Z^{*}
is central and
\lambda^{*}
is faithful. Now if there is a Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q^{*}/Z^{*}
normalizes a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P^{*}/Z^{*}
of
G^{*}/Z^{*}
then
Q/Z
normalizes
P/Z
via the isomorphism
G^{*}/Z^{*}\rightarrow G/Z
, so we may assume
Z
is cyclic and central and
\lambda
is faithful.

Now write
\lambda=\lambda_{p}\lambda_{p^{\prime}}
where
\lambda_{p}
has
p
-power order and
\lambda_{p^{\prime}}
has order coprime to
p
. Let
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
and
P/Z\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G/Z)
. Since
\chi
has
p^{\prime}
-degree,
\chi_{P}
contains a linear character by
[
Nav18
, Theorem 5.12]
, which must lie over
\lambda
. Therefore
\lambda
extends to
P
. Since
\lambda_{p}
is a power of
\lambda
in the group
\operatorname{Irr}(Z)
,
\lambda_{p}
also extends to
P
and by
[
Nav18
, Corollary 6.2]
,
\lambda_{p}
extends to
\hat{\lambda}_{p}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
. By
[
Nav18
, Problem 5.2]
,
(G,Z,\lambda)
is isomorphic to
(G,Z,(\hat{\lambda}_{p})_{Z}\lambda)=(G,Z,\lambda_{p^{\prime}})
so it is no loss to assume
\lambda
has
p^{\prime}
-order. Since
\lambda
is faithful and
Z
is central, this implies that
Z
has
p^{\prime}
-order. By writing
\lambda=\lambda_{q}\lambda_{q^{\prime}}
and arguing as before by for the prime
q
we conclude that we can assume that
Z
also has
q^{\prime}
-order (recall that
\lambda
extends to
Q
by hypothesis).

Since
Z
has
p^{\prime}
-order it is clear that
\lambda
is
p^{\prime}
-special in the sense of
[
NW01
, Section 2]
. Using
[
NW01
, Theorem 3.1]
we may assume
G
is not
p
-solvable.

Step 2: We may assume
{\bf O}_{q^{\prime}}(G/Z)=1
and therefore
{\bf O}_{\pi^{\prime}}(G/Z)=1
. In particular,
Z={\bf O}_{q^{\prime}}(G)={\bf O}_{\pi^{\prime}}(G)
.

Write
M/Z={\bf O}_{q^{\prime}}(G/Z)
and assume
M>Z
.
By
[
NW01
, Lemma 2.1]
(applied to
\pi=\{q\}^{\prime}
), there is a
Q
-invariant
\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}(M|\lambda)
. Since
M
is a
q^{\prime}
-group,
\mu
extends to
Q
by
[
Nav18
, Corollary 6.2]
. Furthermore,
\operatorname{Irr}(G|\mu)\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
and therefore every character in
\operatorname{Irr}(G|\mu)
has
p^{\prime}
-degree. It follows from the Clifford correspondence that
G_{\mu}
contains a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
. By induction, there is some Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q_{0}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}(G)
such that
Q_{0}M/M
normalizes
PM/M
and therefore
Q_{0}
normalizes
PM
. Therefore
Q_{0}
acts coprimely on
PM
and by
[
Asc86
, 18.7(1)]
,
Q_{0}
normalizes a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
PM
, which is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Thus we may assume
M=Z
, and since
{\bf O}_{\pi^{\prime}}(G/Z)\subseteq{\bf O}_{q^{\prime}}(G/Z)
, the claim of Step 2 follows.

Step 3: We may assume
G
has a normal Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q
.

Let
L={\bf O}_{q}(G)
. We have that
L>1
because
{\bf O}_{q^{\prime}}(G)=Z
is central. Since
ZL=Z\times L
we have
\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda\times 1_{L})\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
can be identified with
\operatorname{Irr}(G/L|\overline{\lambda})
(where
\overline{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}(ZL/L)
is the character naturally associated to
\lambda
via the natural isomorphism
Z\cong ZL/L
). Now
ZQ=Z\times Q
so
\lambda\times 1_{Q}\in\operatorname{Irr}(ZQ)
extends
\lambda\times 1_{L}
and therefore
\overline{\lambda}
extends to
ZQ/L\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}(G/L)
. By induction we have that there is
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
such that
PL/L
is normalized by a Sylow
q
-subgroup of
G/L
. Write
R/L={\bf O}_{q^{\prime}}(G/L)
and
T/R={\bf O}_{q}(G/R)
. It follows that
T/R
normalizes
PR/R\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G/R)
and therefore
PR/R\subseteq{\bf C}_{G/R}(T/R)
. Since
{\bf O}_{q^{\prime}}(G/R)=1
, Hall–Higman 1.2.3
[
Isa08
, Theorem 3.21]
implies that
{\bf C}_{G/R}(T/R)\subseteq T/R
, so
P\subseteq R
.

Assume
R<G
. Since every
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(R|\lambda)
lies below some
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
it follows that every
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(R|\lambda)
has
p^{\prime}
-degree. By induction, a Sylow
q
-subgroup
R/Z
normalizes
P/Z
. In particular,
LZ/Z
normalizes
P/Z
and therefore
P/Z\subseteq{\bf C}_{G/Z}(L/Z)\subseteq LZ/Z
. It follows that
P=1
and therefore
G
is a
p^{\prime}
-group and the result follows in this case. Therefore we may assume
R=G
and then
G
has a normal Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q
.

Step 4: We may assume
Q
is elementary abelian

Notice that
Z\Phi(Q)=Z\times\Phi(Q)
and that
\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda\times 1_{\Phi(Q)})\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
. For any subgroup
X\leq G
write
\overline{X}=X\Phi(Q)/\Phi(Q)
. We may identify
\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda\times 1_{\Phi(Q)})
with
\operatorname{Irr}(\overline{G}|\overline{\lambda})
where
\overline{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\overline{Z})
is the character canonically associated to
\lambda
via the natural isomorphism
Z\cong\overline{Z}
, and therefore
p
does not divide the degree of any character in
\operatorname{Irr}(\overline{G}|\overline{\lambda})
. Arguing as before,
\overline{\lambda}
extends to
\overline{Q}
. If
\Phi(Q)>1
then, by induction,
\overline{Q}\subseteq{\bf N}_{\overline{G}}(\overline{P})
for some
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
, which implies that
Q\subseteq\Phi(Q){\bf N}_{G}(P)
. If
R=Q\cap{\bf N}_{G}(P)\in\operatorname{Syl}_{q}({\bf N}_{G}(P))
then
Q=\Phi(Q)R
and by
[
KS04
, 5.2.3]
this implies
Q=R
, so
Q\subseteq{\bf N}_{G}(P)
. Thus we may assume
\Phi(Q)=1
so
Q
is elementary abelian.

Final step

By Itô’s theorem,
q
does not divide the degree of any irreducible character in
\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
and therefore every
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
has
\pi^{\prime}
-degree. Since
G
is
q
-solvable, if
q
is any prime divisor of
|\mathsf{A}_{7}|
then
G
does not involve
\mathsf{A}_{7}
. Therefore we are under the hypotheses of Theorem
3
and we have that
G/Z
has abelian Hall
\pi
-subgroups, so
QZ/Z
normalizes a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G/Z
, as desired.
∎

. We work to show that we may assume
B

G

be
(B,Q)
-good for a defect group

[

[

, Proposition 1.3]
. Then induction defines a bijection
\operatorname{Irr}(U|\nu)\rightarrow\operatorname{Irr}(B)

, and that

-subgroup

of

. By induction, some defect group of

. Since the defect groups of

G

\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}({\bf O}_{q^{\prime}}(G))

B
, so by
[
Nav98
, Theorem 10.20]

B

\operatorname{Irr}(B)=\operatorname{Irr}(G|\lambda)
. Notice that by
[

, Corollary 6.2]
,
\lambda
extends to
Q
where

. By Theorem

we obtain that
Q/{\bf O}_{q^{\prime}}(G)
normalizes a Sylow

-subgroup

of

-subgroup of

normalizes

. Now

-subgroup of

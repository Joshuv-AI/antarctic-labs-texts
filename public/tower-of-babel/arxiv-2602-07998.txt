Anisotropic Electronic Correlations in the Spin Density Wave State of La 3 Ni 2 O 7
Ge He 1,10 🖂, Jun Shen 1 🖂, Shiyu Xie 2,10 , Haotian Zhang 1 , Mengwu Huo 3 , Jun Shu 4 , Deyuan Hu 3 , Xiaoxiang Zhou 5 , Yanmin Zhang 6 , Lei Qin 6 , Liangxin Qiao 2 , Hengjie Liu 2 , Chuansheng Hu 2 , Xijie Dong 4 , Dengjing Wang 4 , Jun Liu 1 , Wei Hu 7 , Jie Yuan 7,8 , Yajun Yan 5 , Zeming Qi 2 , Kui Jin 7,8,9 , Zengyi Du 5 🖂, Meng Wang 3 , Donglai Feng 5 🖂

Abstract
I Abstract The bilayer nickelate superconductor La 3 Ni 2 O 7 undergoes a density wave transition near 150 K that has attracted intensive scrutiny, yet its microscopic origin remains elusive. Here we report polarization-resolved electronic Raman scattering measurements on high-quality single crystals of La 3 Ni 2 O 7 . Below 150 K, we observe a pronounced, symmetry-dependent redistribution of spectral weight in B 1 ​ g {B_{\rm{1g}}} and B 2 ​ g {B_{\rm{2g}}} channels, consistent with the formation of spin-density-wave (SDW) gaps. Quantitative analysis reveals momentum-selective SDW gap amplitudes, with intermediate-to-strong coupling near X/Y points of the Brillouin zone and weaker coupling along the diagonal direction, indicating an unconventional SDW driven by anisotropic electronic correlations. Our results establish the electronic character of the SDW in La 3 Ni 2 O 7 , and provide a microscopic foundation for understanding the emergence of high-temperature superconductivity under pressure in nickelates.

IV
Discussion

We performed systematic Raman spectroscopy on
La
3
Ni
2
O
7
at ambient pressure. First, no anomalies were observed in the phonon modes across the transition, arguing against a dominant CDW instability (see Supplementary Materials B), consistent with previous studies reporting a magnetic transition near 150 K
12
;
10
;
79
. Second, we detected a redistribution of spectral weight in the electronic Raman continuum. In general, spectral weight redistribution may originate from gap opening
19
;
27
;
8
;
15
, strong electronic correlations
20
;
50
;
11
;
56
;
73
, Kondo lattice
73
or band‐structure reconstructions
36
. In contrast to the broad spectral changes without a sharp threshold, typically arising from correlation or band effects, the SDW gap opening produces a distinct suppression of low-energy excitations and a sharp enhancement of spectral weight near the gap edge (
2\Delta
). Thus, we associate the spectral weight redistribution with the opening of an SDW gap, a characteristic feature also observed in the iron pnictide BaFe
2
As
2

8
. While the presence of coupled spin–charge ordering cannot be entirely excluded, our findings, together with earlier reports, indicate that SDW formation is the primary electronic instability in the normal state of
La
3
Ni
2
O
7

12
.

The SDW in
La
3
Ni
2
O
7
has been proposed to originate from Fermi surface nesting with a wave vector
Q
1
, connecting the
\alpha
and
\beta
pockets, as suggested by Wang
et al.

68
. Alternatively, a nesting scenario involving a wave vector
Q
2
, connecting the
\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta
pockets, has been supported by the observation of a ’translated’
\beta
Fermi surface, consistent with scattering processes involving
Q
2

3
. The
Q
1
scenario would imply a comparable gap opening on the
\alpha
pocket, which should, in principle, be observable in the
A_{\rm{1g}}
Raman spectra. However, this is not straightforward. Screening effects can suppress the Raman intensity of gap-related excitations, particularly in conventional metals
16
. If the screening is negligible, as observed in
Ba
1-x
K
x
Fe
2
As
2

5
, then the absence of gap signatures in the
A_{\rm{1g}}
spectra would argue against the nesting vector
Q
1
.

In contrast, the observed gap features in the
{B_{\rm{1g}}}
and
{B_{\rm{2g}}}
spectra may support wavevector
Q
2
, connecting the
\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta
pockets. The distinct spectral lineshape and different
2\Delta/k_{B}T_{\text{DW}}
ratio, however, indicate an unconventional SDW microscopic mechanism in
La
3
Ni
2
O
7
. Specifically, the gap opened close to X/Y points shows a medium-to-strong coupling strength, while along the diagonal direction, the gap exhibits weak coupling. Notably, such coupling strength cannot be accounted for by a simple weak-coupling picture with slight gap anisotropy along the diagonal direction, as evidenced by the failed fitting using the TM function convoluted with a Gaussian peak
6
showing in Fig.
4
a
. Such strong coupling can lead to incoherency of the bands, and partial gap opening over a large momentum space in ARPES measurements, instead of clean leading-edge gap, as reported in 2H-Na
x
TaS
2

58
and NbSe
2

59
. This may account for the absence of a well-defined gap in ARPES measurements
71
;
40
;
1
;
3
. However, the weak-coupling gap, which should in principle be observable by ARPES, has not yet been detected. This discrepancy may stem from the differing sensitivities of the probes. Raman spectroscopy is bulk-sensitive, whereas ARPES primarily probes the surface, potentially leading to divergent observations. Note that optical conductivity reports only a single gap near 50 meV
45
. This difference may be attributed to the distinct nature of the optical conductivity and Raman responses. The former probes current–current correlations, whereas the latter measures effective density–density correlations, which can behave differently in correlated systems
16
;
4
. In addition, Raman selection rules emphasize excitations in specific BZ regions, whereas optical conductivity averages over the entire zone, potentially masking anisotropic gap features.
The variation in coupling strengths across different pockets suggests the presence of anisotropic coherency of the quasiparticles in
La
3
Ni
2
O
7
, a phenomenon widely reported in Fe-based superconductors
25
;
43
and often attributed to strong Hund’s coupling effects involving multiple orbitals. The anisotropic SDW correlations are also reflected in the distinct behavior of the integrated spectral weight in the
{B_{\rm{1g}}}
and
{B_{\rm{2g}}}
symmetries (Fig.
4
e
and
f
).

Moreover, a residual peak centered at approximately 570 cm
-1
(71 meV) is observed in the
{B_{\rm{1g}}}
spectrum above
T_{\mathrm{SDW}}
, whose intensity gradually decreases with increasing temperature. This feature reflects a characteristic energy scale of about 70 meV in
La
3
Ni
2
O
7
, potentially indicating the presence of short-range magnetic order or Lorentz-type spin fluctuations. Consistent with this interpretation, RIXS and NMR measurements report that the existence of short-ranged spin correlations above
T_{\text{SDW}}
and diminishes gradually with increasing temperature
12
;
79
. Additionally, the deviation of the gap’s temperature dependence from mean-field behavior further highlights the unconventional nature of the SDW gaps, possibly indicating the presence of spin fluctuations above
T_{\mathrm{SDW}}

22
.

Recent
\mu
SR experiments have reported that the SDW order in
La
3
Ni
2
O
7
is enhanced under increasing pressure
30
. This finding highlights the importance of exploring the pressure dependence of the electronic Raman response. High-pressure Raman measurements would directly track the evolution of the SDW gaps, offering a valuable spectroscopic probe of the underlying electronic structure changes. Such investigations could provide critical insights into the interplay between magnetism and superconductivity in high-
T_{c}
nickelate superconductors, potentially uncovering key mechanisms that drive the emergence of superconductivity in these complex materials.

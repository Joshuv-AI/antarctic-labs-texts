FairTabGen: Unifying Counterfactual and Causal Fairness in Synthetic Tabular Data Generation
Nitish Nagesh, Salar Shakibhamedan, Mahdi Bagheri, Ziyu Wang, Nima TaheriNejad, Axel Jantsch, Amir M. Rahmani

Abstract
Generating synthetic data is crucial in privacy-sensitive, data-scarce settings, especially for tabular datasets widely used in real-world applications. A key challenge is improving counterfactual and causal fairness, while preserving high utility. We present FairTabGen, a fairness-aware large language model-based framework for tabular synthetic data generation. We integrate multiple fairness definitions—including counterfactual and causal fairness—into both its generation and evaluation pipelines. We use in-context learning, prompt refinement, and fairness-aware data curation to balance fairness and utility. Across diverse datasets, our method outperforms state-of-the-art GAN- and LLM-based methods, achieving up to 10% improvements on fairness metrics such as demographic parity and path-specific causal effects while retaining statistical utility. Remarkably, it achieves these gains using less than 20% of the original data, highlighting its efficiency in low-data regimes. These results demonstrate a principled and practical approach for generating fair and useful synthetic tabular data.



1
Introduction

Synthetic data has become an essential tool in machine learning, offering a way to mitigate privacy risks, address data scarcity, and promote equity in high-stakes domains such as healthcare, criminal justice, and education
Jordon et al. 2022
. Recent advances in generative modeling—spanning generative adversarial networks (GANs), variational autoencoders (VAEs), diffusion models, and large language models (LLMs)—have made it increasingly feasible to generate realistic tabular datasets. However, while models like CTGAN
Xu et al. 2019
, TabDDPM
Kotelnikov et al. 2023
, and REaLTabFormer
Solatorio and Dupriez 2023
emphasize statistical fidelity, they often fall short in addressing fairness, especially when structural biases are encoded in the training data.

The challenge lies in generating data that is not only statistically plausible but also fair—capturing underlying distributions without reproducing or amplifying social biases. To this end, fairness-aware data generation methods have emerged. Prior efforts such as DECAF
Van Breugel et al. 2021
embed fairness constraints within GAN training, while recent LLM-based approaches
Seedat et al. 2023
curate data for generating realistic tabular data. However, most existing work focuses on a single notion of fairness (e.g., counterfactual fairness) and neglects trade-offs with utility, model performance, or other structural definitions like causal fairness.

This paper addresses the question:
How can we generate synthetic tabular data using large language models that satisfies both counterfactual and causal fairness constraints, while maintaining high predictive performance?
We argue that both fairness paradigms are critical and complementary. Counterfactual fairness ensures individual-level invariance, whereas causal fairness offers group-level guarantees grounded in explicit structural assumptions. Integrating both requires not only technical care but also iterative feedback and thoughtful prompt design.

To solve this, we introduce
FairTabGen
, a modular and iterative framework for LLM-driven tabular data generation that unifies causal and counterfactual fairness constraints. FairTabGen begins with fairness-aware prompt construction and in-context learning using a small sample of real data. It then orchestrates synthetic generation via GPT-4o, coupled with a feedback loop that refines prompts based on a comprehensive fairness and utility evaluation.

Our contributions are as follows:

1.

We present a unified framework for synthetic data generation that integrates both counterfactual and causal fairness through prompt-based orchestration using LLMs.

2.

We develop an extensive evaluation pipeline that measures fairness (demographic parity, path-specific causal effects, counterfactual consistency) and utility (AUROC, precision, recall), bridging the gap between fairness and performance trade-offs.

3.

We demonstrate FairTabGen’s effectiveness across three real-world datasets showing that our method achieves higher fairness with competitive or improved utility, using only 5–20% of original samples.

In contrast to prior work that evaluates fairness in isolation or focuses on specific fairness criteria, we provide a holistic and extensible approach for equitable model development. Our results suggest that LLMs, when guided by structured prompts and fairness-aware feedback, can generate synthetic data that is not only useful but also socially responsible—paving the way for safer deployment of generative AI in sensitive domains.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Counterfactual vs Causal Fairness

Causal fairness provides a principled framework for evaluating algorithmic decisions in terms of explicit structural assumptions. While counterfactual fairness has gained popularity as a formal notion of non-discrimination
Kusner et al. 2017
, recent work by
Schröder et al. 2023
has highlighted its limitations and introduced a broader, more structurally grounded framework for reasoning about fairness using causal graphs.
Counterfactual fairness posits that a decision is fair if it remains unchanged in a counterfactual world where only the protected attribute (e.g., race or gender) is altered. However,
Schröder et al. 2023
argue that this notion is insufficient for practical fairness evaluation due to three structural issues with respect to admissibility, ancestral closure and identifiability. A detailed discussion of counterfactual and causal fairness metrics is outside of the scope of this work.

The process begins with a structured prompt comprising a target schema (feature names and data types), a small real-world dataset for conditioning, and fairness and utility constraints—such as minimizing path-specific causal effects or maximizing predictive performance. The prompt also includes a high-level dataset description, fairness-aware structural causal model (SCM) role definitions, and explicit identification of sensitive attributes, mediators, and outcome variables. To enable effective in-context learning (ICL), we provide 40–200 representative examples from the original dataset. The prompt is further designed to discourage memorization and encourage diversity in generated samples.

Synthetic data is generated using GPT-4o through prompts constructed to encode structural dependencies and fairness principles. This includes attribute conditioning to control group representation, embedding fairness constraints directly into prompt logic, and enabling dynamic feedback in response to fairness violations. Sampling parameters (e.g., temperature, top-p) are tuned to balance realism and diversity. Additionally, schema-guided generation and fair representation learning techniques are used to improve alignment with downstream modeling goals.

Each generated batch is evaluated across four dimensions: statistical fidelity (distributional similarity), predictive utility (e.g., AUROC, accuracy), counterfactual fairness (e.g., feature treatment uniformity, demographic parity), and causal fairness (e.g., decomposition of total, direct, and indirect effects). These assessments guide whether the synthetic data supports equitable downstream decision-making.

When constraints are unmet, FairTabGen invokes an orchestration loop that iteratively refines the prompt. This may involve reweighting group distributions, modifying prompt structure, or introducing contrastive counterfactual examples. The process continues until all predefined fairness and utility thresholds are satisfied. The final dataset can be used for model training, fairness auditing, or privacy-preserving data sharing—demonstrating the potential of LLMs, when guided by fairness-aware prompts and structural priors, to generate synthetic data that is both realistic and equitable.

Experiments

4.1
Datasets

To evaluate the effectiveness and generalizability of our proposed LLM-based synthetic data generation framework, we conduct experiments across three widely used benchmark datasets spanning multiple domains: COMPAS
Angwin et al. 2022
, Law School Admissions
Kusner et al. 2017
, and MIMIC-IV
Johnson et al. 2021
. These datasets were chosen to reflect real-world fairness challenges in high-stakes decision-making contexts—criminal justice, education, and healthcare. Each dataset contains sensitive attributes such as race, gender, or age, allowing us to study fairness violations and their mitigation in a principled and domain-relevant manner. The COMPAS (Correctional Offender Management Profiling for Alternative Sanctions) dataset is used to assess recidivism risk. It is known for exhibiting significant racial bias, making it a canonical dataset for fairness studies. The Law School Admissions dataset includes records of applicants to U.S. law schools, with demographic features, LSAT scores, and bar passage outcomes. It presents fairness considerations in educational access and professional licensing. The MIMIC-IV dataset is a large-scale critical care database collected from intensive care units, comprising detailed patient records such as age, vitals, and clinical outcomes like mortality. Fairness evaluation in clinical settings is particularly relevant due to potential disparities in treatment recommendations and resource allocation. Together, these datasets offer diverse structural and demographic characteristics, enabling a comprehensive evaluation of fairness-aware data generation across domains with differing causal and statistical properties.

4.3
Experimental Setup

All experiments were conducted on a machine with an Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-10700K CPU and 16 GB RAM, using both Windows and Ubuntu operating systems. For data generation using FairTabGen and for reproducing CLLM-based synthetic data, we accessed the OpenAI API. For CLLM, we followed the generation protocol described in the original study, including the selection of the GPT model and configuration parameters such as temperature and top-p. In FairTabGen, we used GPT-4o with a temperature of 0.9 across all three datasets. The top-p parameter was set to 1.0 for COMPAS, and 0.95 for both LAW and MIMIC-IV. The
max_tokens
parameter was adjusted based on the number of features in each dataset, taking into account the context window limitations of GPT-4o.
Synthetic data for the DECAF method was generated on a server running Ubuntu and equipped with NVIDIA RTX 3090 and 4090 GPUs.

4.4
Evaluation Metrics

We elucidate different metrics designed to assess both the quality and fairness of the generated data here. This approach allows us to rigorously analyze the utility of the generated data in downstream machine learning tasks while simultaneously examining the extent to which fairness—particularly causal fairness—is preserved or improved.

Causal Fairness:

Causal fairness can be formalized using the Standard Fairness Model (SFM), a structured graphical representation of the relationships between observed features, protected attributes, and outcomes
Plecko and Bareinboim 2022
. Within this framework, fairness is assessed through path-specific causal effects, as advanced by recent work
Schröder et al. 2023
. These include the direct effect, which captures the influence of the protected attribute on the outcome while holding mediators constant; the indirect effect, which reflects the influence through observed mediators; the spurious effect, which accounts for pathways mediated by confounders and other non-causal structures; and the total variation, which represents the overall change in predicted outcomes when switching the value of the protected attribute. Fairness is said to be satisfied when these effects—direct, indirect, and spurious—are close to zero, enabling a detailed decomposition of how bias enters the system. This formalism forms the foundation for our proposed LLM-based fairness-aware data generation framework, which builds on this structural reasoning to incorporate interpretable, path-specific constraints during the synthetic data generation process.

Utility:

To assess predictive utility, we report standard classification metrics—precision, recall, and AUROC—capturing model performance across imbalanced datasets. We trained five models: Decision Tree, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, SVM, and XGBoost. Given XGBoost’s consistent performance in both fairness and utility evaluations, we present its results in the main text.

Counterfactual Fairness:

Counterfactual fairness, as introduced by
Kusner et al. 2017
, tests whether model predictions remain unchanged when only the sensitive attribute is counterfactually altered. We assess this using two metrics: Demographic Parity (DP), which measures shifts in predicted outcomes under attribute changes, and Fairness Through Unawareness (FTU), which verifies that predictions remain invariant when sensitive features are excluded from the input
Subah 2024
.

Bias Mitigation:

We evaluate the impact of four well-established bias mitigation techniques—Suppression (SUP), Correlation Remover (CoR), Disparate Impact Remover (DIR), and Reweighing (RW)—on downstream fairness outcomes. Following the setup in
Liu et al. 2025
, we train multiple ML models and report the best fairness score across them to assess the effectiveness of each mitigation method.

Given the varying sizes of our datasets and the inclusion of synthetic data, we adopt a mixed training strategy: models are trained on a combination of real and synthetic data, and evaluated on a held-out real test set. This setup mirrors practical deployment scenarios where synthetic augmentation is used to improve fairness, but generalization must hold on real-world distributions.

Results and Discussion

In this section, we provide a detailed analysis of the results and compare our proposed method, with state-of-the-art approaches, including both LLM-based and GAN-based synthetic data generators. The evaluation considers multiple dimensions including causal fairness, counterfactual fairness, model-based fairness, and predictive performance.

5.1
Feature Distribution

To understand how our method improves fairness at the data level, we compare the distribution of sensitive (race) and mediating (gender) attributes across real and synthetic datasets. As shown in
Figure

2
, FairTabGen consistently shifts distributions toward parity. In COMPAS, the proportion of the majority race drops from 60.2% to 53.9%, and the male proportion reduces from 80.5% to 62.3%. Similar improvements are seen in MIMIC, where the race balance shifts from 81.8% to 52.4%, and gender distribution becomes more even (48.3% to 50.2%). In LAW, where race includes multiple categories (8 categories), FairTabGen reduces the majority class dominance from 83.7% to 12.1% (
\approx\frac{1}{8}
), substantially improving demographic diversity. Gender balance also improves modestly. These shifts suggest that our approach promotes equitable representation in the data, reinforcing its fairness gains at both statistical and causal levels.

Causal Fairness

Statistical Utility

The results in Figure
3
show that while all synthetic methods match or exceed real data in at least one predictive metric, our approach consistently outperforms DECAF and CLLM across datasets, particularly in recall and AUROC. These improvements suggest better generalization and reduced false negative rates—critical in risk-sensitive domains. In COMPAS and MIMIC, recall improvements point to the model’s ability to capture broader decision boundaries, albeit with a minor precision trade-off in COMPAS. For the LAW dataset, where real data exhibits high signal fidelity, performance gains are more modest, with synthetic data nearly matching real across all metrics. Overall, these results highlight the method’s capacity to retain task-relevant structure while balancing sensitivity and specificity depending on dataset characteristics.

6
Conclusion

We addressed the challenge of generating synthetic tabular data that balances fairness and utility, introducing FairTabGen, a prompt-based LLM framework that unifies counterfactual and causal fairness constraints. Unlike prior work that focuses on a single fairness notion or treats fairness as an afterthought, we integrate fairness-aware generation and evaluation into a coherent and modular pipeline.

Across three real-world datasets—COMPAS, Law School Admissions, and MIMIC—FairTabGen consistently improves fairness metrics such as demographic parity and path-specific causal effects, while maintaining competitive predictive performance. Remarkably, these results are achieved using only 5–20% of the original data, underscoring the framework’s practicality in privacy-sensitive and data-scarce settings.

In contrast to approaches like DECAF or TabDDPM that rely on training complex generative models, FairTabGen leverages prompt engineering and feedback-driven refinement to directly steer LLMs toward fairer outputs. This shift—from model design to prompt orchestration—marks a new direction for fairness-aware synthetic data generation, making it more interpretable, modular, and extensible.

The framework has several limitations. It assumes observed confounders are sufficient, does not yet support process-level fairness or decision-maker modeling, and handles sensitive attributes in isolation, without addressing intersectionality. While the results are promising, broader generalization across tasks and domains remains to be validated.

Looking ahead, we aim to evolve the current LLM-based framework into an AI-agent framework capable of autonomously refining prompts and tuning generation toward multi-objective targets. Future work will also explore robustness to unobserved confounders, fairness under multiple sensitive attributes, and alignment with human-centric fairness goals. Benchmarking against newer foundation models purpose-built for tabular data is also a key step forward.

By embedding fairness directly into the generative process, we move beyond retrospective fairness fixes, offering a blueprint for socially responsible synthetic data pipelines in high-stakes domains.

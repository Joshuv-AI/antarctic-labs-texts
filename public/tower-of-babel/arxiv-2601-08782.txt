Single-Period Floquet Control of Bosonic Codes with Quantum Lattice Gates
Tangyou Huang, Lei Du, Lingzhen Guo

Abstract
Bosonic codes constitute a promising route to fault-tolerant quantum computing. Existing Floquet protocols enable analytical construction of bosonic codes but typically rely on slow adiabatic ramps with thousands of driving periods. In this work, we circumvent this bottleneck by introducing an analytical and deterministic Floquet method that directly synthesizes arbitrary unitaries within a single period. The phase-space unitary ensembles generated by our approach reproduce the Haar-random statistics, enabling practical pseudorandom unitaries in continuous-variable systems. We prepare various prototypical bosonic codes from vacuum and implement single-qubit logical gates with high fidelities using quantum lattice gates. By harnessing the full intrinsic nonlinearity of Josephson junctions, quantum lattice gates decompose quantum circuits into primitive operations for efficient continuous-variable quantum computing.



Introduction.—
Fault-tolerant quantum computation promises advantages over its classical counterpart, ranging from fundamental research to commercial applications. Practical fault tolerance typically relies on quantum error correction (QEC)

, which has been experimentally demonstrated across discrete-variables (DV) and continuous-variables (CV) platforms
AI 2023
;
Acharya et al. 2024
;
Sivak et al. 2023

Unlike the DV cases, CV protocols leverage the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space of harmonic oscillators, offering redundancy in encoding quantum information without the need for large numbers of physical qubits
Braunstein and van
Loock 2005
;
Weedbrook et al. 2012

A universal set of single-mode CV quantum gates can be realized using Gaussian operations, such as displacements, squeezing, and phase rotations, in combination with a single nonlinear operation such as the cubic phase gate

;
Eriksson et al. 2024
. Another popular single-mode universal gate set combines displacements with the selective number-dependent arbitrary phase (SNAP) gates, which impart arbitrary phases to individual Fock states via an off-resonantly coupled ancilla transmon qubit
Heeres et al. 2015
;
Krastanov et al. 2015
;
Heeres et al. 2017
. The universal gate set for multimode CV quantum computation requires only the inclusion of a simple two-mode linear coupling
Sefi and van
Loock 2011
;
Budinger et al. 2024
.
However, universality alone is not sufficient for fault-tolerant quantum computation. On the one hand, the continuous nature of bosonic-quadrature errors necessitates embedding a finite-dimensional code space within the infinite-dimensional CV Hilbert space
Travaglione and Milburn 2002
. On the other hand, non-Gaussian resources are typically required to correct Gaussian errors and implement fault-tolerant quantum computation
Larsen 2025
;
Aghaee Rad 2025
;
Konno 2024
. When the encoded states satisfy the Knill–Laflamme conditions

Joshi et al. 2021a
;
Cai et al. 2021
;
Ma et al. 2021
;
Albert et al. 2018
.

Both the cubic phase gate and the SNAP gate need complicated design of superconducting circuits using the low-order nonlinearity of Josephson junctions (JJs)
Rojkov et al. 2024
.
In contrast, the recently introduced
quantum lattice gates
(QLGs) leverage the full nonlinearity of JJ superconducting circuits and can provide an analytical framework based on the Floquet engineering to prepare and manipulate bosonic codes

.
However, this approach suffers from slow adiabatic ramps

;
Guo et al. 2025

In this work, we overcome this bottleneck by introducing an analytical, deterministic Floquet-engineering method that directly synthesizes arbitrary bosonic unitaries within a

Emerson et al. 2003
.

Noncommutative Floquet Engineering.—
From the Floquet theory, the stroboscopic evolution over one period of a time-periodic Hamiltonian
\hat{H}(t)=\hat{H}(t+T)
is captured by a time-independent Floquet Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\rm F}

Shirley 1965
;
Eckardt and Anisimovas 2015
;
Liang et al. 2018
,

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-i\frac{1}{\lambda}\hat{H}_{\rm F}T\Big)\equiv\mathcal{T}\exp\Big[-\frac{i}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{T}\hat{H}(t)dt\Big],

(1)

where
\mathcal{T}
is the time-ordering operator.
In this work, we consider a driven cavity described by
\hat{H}_{\rm cav}=\hbar\omega_{0}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+V(t)

Guo and Peano 2024
, where
\hat{a}
(
\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}
) is the annihilation (creation) operator of the cavity.
Our objective is to realize an arbitrary unitary operation on a
d
-dimensional subspace of cavity mode.
For any target unitary operator
\hat{U}_{\rm tar}\in\mathbb{U}(d)
, there exists a Hermitian generator
\hat{H}_{\rm tar}
such that
\hat{U}_{\rm tar}=e^{-i\hat{H}_{\rm tar}/\lambda}
.
The key idea is to design a periodic potential
V(t)
for synthesizing an arbitrary Floquet Hamiltonian in the cavity-frequency rotating frame
\hat{H}_{\rm tar}=\sum_{j,l}\epsilon_{j,l}(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{j}\hat{a}^{l}
, where
\epsilon_{j,l}=\epsilon_{l,j}^{*}\in\mathbb{C}
denote the complex driving amplitudes.
We write the target Hamiltonian in the orthonormal Fock basis
\{|n\rangle\}_{n=0}^{d-1}
as

\hat{H}_{\rm tar}=\sum_{n,m=0}^{d-1}h_{n,m}|n\rangle\langle m|

with
h_{n,m}=\langle n|\hat{H}_{\rm tar}|m\rangle
.

According to the technique of
noncommutative Fourier transformation
(NcFT)
Guo and Peano 2024
, the target Hamiltonian is decomposed as a superposition of plane-wave operators,

\hat{H}_{\rm tar}(\hat{x},\hat{p})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\int dk_{x}dk_{p}f_{\rm tar}(k_{x},k_{p})e^{i(k_{x}\hat{x}+k_{p}\hat{p})}.

(2)

Here, the quadrature operators are defined as
\hat{x}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2}}(\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}+\hat{a})
and
\hat{p}\equiv i\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2}}(\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}-\hat{a})
, which satisfy
[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i\lambda
with
\lambda
the dimensionless Planck constant. By introducing the variables
k=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{p}^{2}}
and
\tau\equiv\omega_{0}t=\mathrm{Arg}(k_{x}+ik_{p})
, the NcFT coefficient
f_{\rm tar}
is given in the Fock basis by

f_{\rm tar}(k,\tau)=\sum_{n,m}h_{n,m}f_{n,m}(k,\tau).

(3)

The explicit kernels
f_{n,m}(k,\tau)
are given in Supplemental Material
SM
.
With the NcFT coefficient, the synthesized driving potential can be written as a superposition of cosine-type potentials
Guo and Peano 2024

\displaystyle\hat{V}(\hat{x},\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\beta_{0}A(k,\tau)\cos[k\hat{x}+\phi(k,\tau)]\,dk,

(4)

where the target-independent parameter
\beta_{0}
controls the driving strength. The time-dependent amplitude
A(k,\tau)
and phase
\phi(k,\tau)
are given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}A(k,\tau)&=&k\Big|f_{\rm tar}(k\cos{\tau},k\sin{\tau})\Big|,\\
\phi(k,\tau)&=&\text{Arg}\Big[f_{\rm tar}(k\cos{\tau},k\sin{\tau})\Big].\end{array}\right.

Each cosine component can be physically implemented, for example, with optical lattices in ultracold atom setups
Moritz et al. 2003
;
Guo et al. 2022
, or JJ potentials in superconducting circuits
Hofheinz et al. 2011
.
To the leading order in the Floquet–Magnus (FM) expansion, the target unitary operator is given by

\hat{U}_{\rm tar}\approx\mathcal{T}\exp\!\left[-\frac{i}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{T}\hat{V}(\tau)\,d\tau\right].

(8)

In principle, higher-order FM errors can be mitigated by adding driving terms perturbatively
Xu and Guo 2025
.

Quantum lattice gate.—

In our protocol, we discretize the driving potential into a sequence of QLGs
Guo et al. 2025
as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{G}}(\vec{\theta},\vec{\gamma})\equiv\prod_{n=1}^{N_{t}}\prod_{m=1}^{N_{k}}\hat{\mathbf{g}}(\theta_{n,m},\gamma_{n,m})

(9)

with
N_{t}
(
N_{k}
) the number of discretization steps, i.e., the Trotter depth, for time (wavenumber), and
\hat{\mathbf{g}}(\theta_{n,m},\gamma_{n,m})
the elementary QLG defined via

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{g}}(\theta_{n,m},\gamma_{n,m})\equiv e^{-\frac{i}{\lambda}\theta_{n,m}\cos(k_{n}\hat{x}+\gamma_{nm})}.

(10)

Here, we have introduced parameters
\gamma_{n,m}=\phi(k_{n},\tau_{m})
,
\theta_{n,m}=A(k_{n},\tau_{m})(T/N_{t})(k_{f}/N_{k})
with
\tau_{m}=m(T/N_{t})
,
k_{n}=n(k_{f}/N_{k})
, where
k_{f}
is an appropriate cutoff for the wavenumber.
Except the errors from high-order FM expansion,
another error of
\hat{\mathbf{G}}(\vec{\theta},\vec{\gamma})
approximating the target unitary
\hat{U}_{\rm tar}
originates from the Trotter–Suzuki (TS) decomposition (i.e., Eq.(
9
)), which can be suppressed by employing higher-order TS expansions and adaptive optimization strategies
Suzuki 1976
;
Zhao et al. 2024
;
Zhang et al. 2023
.

Single-period Floquet control with QLGs.—

We first apply our protocol to prepare a target state
|\psi_{\rm tar}\rangle
from an initial state
|\psi_{0}\rangle
via a unitary, i.e.,
|\psi_{\rm tar}\rangle=\hat{U}_{\rm tar}\ket{\psi_{0}}
. We exclude the trivial case
\ket{\psi_{\rm tar}}=e^{i\eta}\ket{\psi_{0}}
for some global phase
\eta
. Previous approaches based on the adiabatic ramp method typically lead to long evolution times with thousands of Floquet periods
Guo and Peano 2024
;
Lachance-Quirion et al. 2024
;
Guo et al. 2025
.
In this work, we use a Householder-based construction
Ivanov et al. 2006
;
de Brugière et al. 2020
to obtain a closed-form unitary that maps the known initial state
|\psi_{0}\rangle
to the target state
|\psi_{\rm tar}\rangle
, without the need for any adiabatic processes (see
SM
for details). By defining the normalized Householder vector
\ket{u}\equiv(\ket{\psi_{0}}-e^{i\phi}\ket{\psi_{\rm tar}})/\left\lVert\ket{\psi_{0}}-e^{i\phi}\ket{\psi_{\rm tar}}\right\rVert
with
e^{i\phi}\equiv\braket{\psi_{\rm tar}|\psi_{0}}/|\braket{\psi_{\rm tar}|\psi_{0}}|
(setting
e^{i\phi}=1
if
\braket{\psi_{\rm tar}|\psi_{0}}=0
), the target unitary can be obtained explicitly as

\hat{U}_{\rm tar}=\Big[\hat{I}+\big(e^{-i\phi}-1\big)\ket{\psi_{\rm tar}}\!\bra{\psi_{\rm tar}}\Big]\Big(\hat{I}-2\ket{u}\!\bra{u}\Big),

(11)

where
\hat{I}
is the identity operator. It is straightforward to verify that
\hat{U}_{\rm tar}|\psi_{0}\rangle=|\psi_{\rm tar}\rangle
, while
\hat{U}_{\rm tar}
acts as the identity (up to an overall phase) on the subspace orthogonal to the space spanned by
\ket{\psi_{0}}
and
\ket{\psi_{\rm tar}}
.
Such
\hat{U}_{\rm tar}=e^{-i\hat{H}_{\rm tar}/\lambda}
can then be decomposed into a sequence of elementary QLGs using the Floquet Hamiltonian engineering described above.

Haar-random state benchmark.—

Haar-typical states and unitaries serve as a widely used benchmark for assessing quantum controllability in high-dimensional Hilbert spaces.
In this context, we sample targets from the Haar measure and examine whether the implemented operations faithfully reproduce the statistics of Haar randomness
Życzkowski and Sommers 2005
.
Such pseudorandom behavior underlies a broad range pf quantum information protocols and can be realized using gate-based quantum circuits
Schuster et al. 2025
and CV schemes involving non-Gaussian resources
Blume-Kohout and Turner 2014
;
Iosue et al. 2024
;
Conrad et al. 2025
.

Here, we construct a QLG-based unitary generator on a
d
-dimensional subspace whose output matches Haar statistics in phase space. Concretely, we define Haar-random states
|\psi_{\rm Haar}\rangle\equiv\hat{U}_{\rm Haar}|0\rangle
drawn from the Haar measure on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{d}

SM

|\psi_{\rm ref}\rangle
. For an ensemble of Haar-distributed pure states, the fidelity
\mathcal{F}=|\langle\psi_{\rm Haar}|\psi_{\rm ref}\rangle|^{2}
is a variable following the probability density function (PDF)
Życzkowski and Sommers 2005

\mathbb{P}_{\rm Haar}(\mathcal{F})=(d-1)(1-\mathcal{F})^{d-2},

(12)

with the mean fidelity
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{F}]=1/d
. In our implementation, we take each sampled
|\psi_{\rm Haar}\rangle
as the target state and construct the target unitary
\hat{U}_{\rm Haar}
from Eq. (
11
) with the initial vacuum state
|0\rangle
.
We then design a sequence of QLGs from Eq. (
9
) for each target unitary
\hat{U}_{\rm Haar}
yielding

|\psi_{\rm QLG}\rangle=\hat{\mathbf{G}}_{\rm Haar}(\vec{\theta},\vec{\gamma})|0\rangle,

(13)

which approximates the sampled Haar state
|\psi_{\rm Haar}\rangle
with high fidelity
\mathcal{F}_{\rm QLG}\equiv|\langle\psi_{\rm QLG}|\psi_{\rm Haar}\rangle|^{2}
.

We next validate our QLG approach by demonstrating the preparation of binomial

Optimal pulse engineering (OPE).—

We finally develop a numerical pulse engineering optimization method to optimally mitigate both the high-order FM errors and the TS errors under specific hardware constraints.
To this end, we replace the constant driving strength
\beta_{0}
in Eq. (
4
) by a time-dependent envelope

\beta(\tau)\in\mathbb{R}
used for optimal control. Over one period
T
, we discretize
\beta(\tau)
into
N_{t}
slices, i.e.,
\vec{\beta}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{N_{t}}\}
, and then solve

\displaystyle\min_{\vec{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{t}}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\!\big(|\psi_{\rm QLG}(\vec{\beta})\rangle,\,|\psi_{\rm tar}\rangle\big)

(15)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle|\vec{\beta}-\beta_{0}|\leq\delta,

where
\mathcal{L}(\cdot)
is the task-specific loss (for state preparation,
\mathcal{L}=1-|\langle\psi_{\rm tar}|\psi_{\rm QLG}(\vec{\beta})\rangle|^{2}
; for logical gates,
\mathcal{L}=1-\mathcal{F}_{\rm gate}
). The bound
\delta>0
encodes the maximum admissible amplitude feasibility set by the hardware.
The discrete envelope
\vec{\beta}
is initialized as
\beta_{0}=1/\omega_{0}
and then optimized with a gradient-based method; see more details in
SM
.

Discussion and Conclusion.—

In summary, we present an analytical single-period Floquet protocol of bosonic codes with quantum lattice gates, avoiding slow adiabatic ramps over thousands of periods
Kolesnikow et al. 2024
;
Guo et al. 2025
and the global variational searches as in SNAP-based approaches
Krastanov et al. 2015
;
Kudra et al. 2022
. Within this framework, we numerically demonstrate high-fidelity preparation of Haar-distributed target states and employ Haar-statistics benchmarks to assess the controllability of our single-period Floquet control via QLGs.
By combining QLGs with optimal pulse engineering, we achieve high-fidelity preparation of binomial, cat, and GKP codewords and the universal logical single-qubit gates.
The impact of coherent control errors and decoherence is analyzed in Ref.
SM
, where the short, single-period operation times are shown to enable substantial robustness.
Our approach differs fundamentally, in both methodology and experimental implementation, from existing approaches
Krastanov et al. 2015
;
Hillmann et al. 2020b
, providing a hardware-compatible and efficient route to universal CV quantum computation.
Moreover, our framework can be naturally extended to two-qubit logical gates encoded on bosonic codes by designing two-mode noncommutative Floquet engineering. Our approach can also be applied to applications such as quantum reservoir computing
Dudas et al. 2023
and quantum
t
-designs
Blume-Kohout and Turner 2014
;
Iosue et al. 2024
;
Conrad et al. 2025
.

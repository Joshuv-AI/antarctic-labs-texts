Effect of Laser Microtextured Collar Implants on Soft Tissue Integration and Crestal Bone Level
Authors: Tanmay Srivastava, Shruti Sinha, Shivangi Pandey, Anil Kumar Rodda, Pratik Prakash Bumb, Mrunal Dave
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:The long-term success of dental implants is strongly influenced by peri-implant soft tissue attachment and maintenance of crestal bone. Laser microtextured collar implants are designed to enhance epithelial and connective tissue adhesion, potentially improving biological seal, and reducing marginal bone loss.Materials and methods:A total of 40 patients requiring single posterior mandibular implants were recruited and randomly allocated into two groups (n = 20 each): Group A (laser microtextured collar implants) and Group B (machined collar implants). Clinical parameters such as probing depth (PD), bleeding on probing (BOP), and peri-implant mucosal thickness were assessed at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months. Radiographic assessment of crestal bone level changes was performed using standardized digital periapical radiographs. Data were analyzed using independent t-tests and repeated measures ANOVA (α = 0.05).Results:At 12 months, Group A showed significantly lower mean PD (2.1 ± 0.3 mm) compared to Group B (2.8 ± 0.4 mm, P < 0.001). BOP was observed in 10% of sites in Group A and 35% in Group B (P = 0.04). The mean crestal bone loss in Group A was 0.39 ± 0.11 mm, significantly lower than 0.74 ± 0.14 mm in Group B (P < 0.001).Conclusion:Laser microtextured collar implants demonstrated superior soft tissue integration and reduced crestal bone loss compared to conventional implants, suggesting their potential benefit in enhancing peri-implant tissue stability and implant longevity.

INTRODUCTION

The preservation of peri-implant tissues is a critical factor in the long-term success of dental implants.[1] While osseointegration ensures the mechanical stability of implants, the health and stability of surrounding soft and hard tissues are essential for preventing complications such as peri-implantitis and marginal bone loss.[23]

The design of the implant collar, particularly its surface characteristics, plays a pivotal role in determining the quality of soft tissue attachment and crestal bone response.[4] Traditional machined collars often present smooth surfaces that may limit soft tissue adhesion, potentially allowing apical migration of the junctional epithelium and crestal bone resorption.[5]

Laser microtexturing technology has emerged as a novel approach to modify the transmucosal collar of implants. By creating microgrooves of controlled depth and width, this surface is engineered to encourage perpendicular collagen fiber attachment and stable epithelial adhesion.[67] These histological features are hypothesized to form a more effective biological seal, reducing bacterial infiltration and inflammatory damage to the underlying bone.

Several animal and preliminary clinical studies suggest that laser microtextured collars may enhance soft tissue integration and limit crestal bone remodeling.[89] However, high-quality, controlled human trials comparing these implants with conventional machined collars over time are limited.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and participants

This randomized controlled clinical study was conducted at the Department of Periodontology and Implantology, including 40 systemically healthy patients aged 25–55 years, each requiring a single implant in the mandibular posterior region. Ethical clearance was obtained, and informed consent was collected from all participants.

Surgical procedure

Implants (4.0 mm diameter, 10 mm length) were placed using a standardized two-stage protocol. All surgeries were performed by the same experienced clinician. Healing abutments were placed after 3 months, followed by prosthetic loading at 4 months with metal-ceramic crowns.

Radiographic assessment

Standardized digital periapical radiographs were taken at baseline and 12 months using long-cone paralleling technique with individualized acrylic stents. Crestal bone levels were measured mesially and distally from implant platform to the first bone-to-implant contact using imaging software.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 26.0. Intergroup comparisons were made using independent t-tests. Intragroup changes over time were assessed using repeated measures ANOVA. A P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

All participants completed the 12-month follow-up without complications. No implants failed during the study.

Laser microtextured collar implants exhibited significantly less crestal bone resorption and superior soft tissue health over 12 months compared to machined collar implants.

DISCUSSION

The lower PDs observed in the test group align with findings from Nevins et al., who reported perpendicular collagen fiber orientation and increased connective tissue attachment around laser-modified surfaces.[10] The reduction in BOP further supports the notion that laser microgrooves promote a more stable and less inflamed soft tissue seal.[11]

Our study also found significantly lower crestal bone loss in the laser collar group. These findings corroborate the outcomes of a clinical study by Iglhaut et al., which demonstrated preservation of marginal bone in implants with laser microtexturing.[12] The controlled topography created by laser etching appears to facilitate firm epithelial attachment, limiting microbial penetration and inflammatory damage.[13]

Another contributing factor could be the biological width preservation associated with improved mucosal attachment. Abrahamsson et al. emphasized the importance of stable soft tissue in maintaining marginal bone levels.[14] This is particularly relevant in early loading phases when tissues are adapting to functional and microbial challenges.[15]

Some limitations of this study include its relatively short follow-up period and single implant brand usage. Long-term studies with larger sample sizes, histological evaluation, and inclusion of esthetic zone implants may provide further insights.

Nevertheless, our findings reinforce the biomechanical and biological advantages of laser microtextured collars and suggest their clinical applicability in preserving peri-implant tissue health.

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of this clinical study, laser microtextured collar implants demonstrated significantly better soft tissue integration and reduced crestal bone loss over a 12-month period compared to conventional machined collar implants. These implants may contribute to enhanced long-term implant success through improved biological seal and peri-implant tissue stability.

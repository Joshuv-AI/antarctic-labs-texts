An extension of the r p r^{p} method for wave equations with scale-critical potentials and first-order terms
Maxime Van de Moortel

Abstract
The r p r^{p} method, first introduced in [ DR10 ] , has become a robust strategy to prove decay for wave equations in the context of black holes and beyond. In this note, we propose an extension of this method, which is particularly suitable for proving decay for a general class of wave equations featuring a scale-critical time-dependent potential and/or first-order terms of small amplitude. Our approach consists of absorbing error terms in the r p r^{p} -weighted energy using a novel Grönwall argument, which allows a larger range of p p than the standard method. A spherically symmetric version of our strategy first appeared in [ VdM22 ] in the context of a weakly charged scalar field on a black hole whose equations also involve a scale-critical potential.

1.1
The principles of the
r^{p}
method

On Minkowski spacetime, the wave equation
-\partial_{t}^{2}\phi+\partial_{x}^{2}\phi+\partial_{y}^{2}\phi+\partial_{z}^{2}\phi=0
takes the following form, where
u=t-r
,
v=t+r
,
r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}}
,
\psi=r\phi
(radiation field) and
\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
is the standard Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
:

\partial_{u}\partial_{v}\psi=r^{-2}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi.

(1.16)

The key idea of the

method of Dafermos and Rodnianski
[
DR10
]
is to prove decay-in-time for
\phi

V

1
and the goal is to prove that this energy
\tilde{E}(u)
ends up decaying in

. To capture this decay, Dafermos and Rodnianski use the multiplier

and, upon integration on a spacetime region

\int_{u=u_{2}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}[\int_{u=u^{\prime}}r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}]du^{\prime}\lesssim\int_{u=u_{1}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+\tilde{E}(u_{1}),

(1.17)

for all
u_{1}<u_{2}
, where
0<p\leq 2
, and (
1.9
) is used to absorb various boundary terms at the curve
\{r=R\}
into the energy
\tilde{E}(u_{1})
. (

) consists of two statements: the boundedness of the

-weighted energy for

, and the (integrated) decay of the

weighted energy. Using (

[

]

\tilde{E}(u)\lesssim u^{-2}.

(1.18)

We emphasize that the proof of (

method can be also be carried out on the Schwarzschild or (sub-extremal) Reissner–Nordström spacetime (

In

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\partial_{v}r}r^{p-4}[\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\partial_{u}\psi]=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\partial_{v}r}r^{p-4}\partial_{u}[|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}]=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{C_{u_{2}}}\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\partial_{v}r}r^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{C_{u_{1}}}\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\partial_{v}r}r^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{v_{R}(u_{1})}^{v_{R}(u_{2})}\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\partial_{v}r}R^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}(u_{R}(v^{\prime}),v^{\prime})dv^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}\partial_{u}\left(\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\partial_{v}r}r^{p-4}\right)|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2},\end{split}

which, combining to (

) and (

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}+[2-p]\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}|\partial_{u}\psi|^{2}\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}+\\
&+R^{p-4}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}(R^{2}|\partial_{u}\psi|^{2}+|\psi|^{2}+|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2})(u,v_{R}(u))du+\epsilon^{2}[2-p]^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}.\end{split}

(3.14)

completing the proof of (
3.11
).

∎

The next proposition records the well-known Poincaré inequality.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let

. Then the following inequality holds:

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f_{\geq L}|^{2}\geq L(L+1)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|f_{\geq L}|^{2}.

(3.15)

Moreover,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f_{L}|^{2}=L(L+1)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|f_{L}|^{2}.

(3.16)

Proof.

This is very standard, see for instance
[
GVdM24
]
, Lemma 2.1.
∎

3.4
Lower-weighted energy estimates

We start with a first boundedness statement for
r^{p}
-weighted estimates for
0\leq p<2
. The argument relies on the Hardy inequalities of Proposition
3.6
and provides (non-optimal) decay results in time for the unweighted energy.

Proposition 3.8
.

There exists
p_{low}(\epsilon)<2
with
2-p_{low}(\epsilon)=O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
such that for all
0<p\leq p_{low}
:

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+p\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+(2-p)r^{p-2}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}[r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+r^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}]+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}\end{split}

(3.17)

Proof.

By (
2.1
),
\psi
satisfies (
3.5
) with
F=\epsilon\ r^{-2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{1}\tilde{w}_{i}(u,v)\cdot\partial_{u}^{i}\psi+\tilde{q}(u,v)\cdot r\partial_{v}\psi\right)
, so we can apply Lemma
3.4
and integrate (
3.6
) on
\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})
to obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+p\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+(2-p)r^{p-2}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}\\
&+\epsilon\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}[r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+r^{p-3}[|\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{u}\psi|^{2}]+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}\\
&\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+\epsilon\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}[|\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{u}\psi|^{2}]+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}.\end{split}

Note that in the above, we have used the bound on
F
coming from (
2.2
) giving

r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi F|\lesssim\epsilon\left(r^{p-2}(|\psi|+|\partial_{u}\psi|)|\partial_{v}\psi|+r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}\right)\lesssim\epsilon\left(r^{p-3}(|\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{u}\psi|^{2})+r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}\right).

(3.18)

Now, we apply Proposition
3.6
and by (
3.10
), (
3.11
) with
q=p
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}[|\psi|^{2}+||\partial_{u}\psi|^{2}]\lesssim[2-p]^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{R^{3}[2-p]}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-2}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}\\
&+[2-p]^{-1}\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}.\end{split}

(3.19)

hence, using the smallness of
\epsilon
, on condition that
(2-p)\gg\sqrt{\epsilon}
, we get (
3.17
).

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

For
0\leq p\leq p_{low}(\epsilon)
and
u_{1}<u_{2}
:

p\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}E_{p-1}[\psi](u)du+\tilde{E}_{p}(u_{2})\lesssim\tilde{E}_{p}(u_{1}).

(3.20)

Proof.

Note that, since
p_{low}<2
, we have
r^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}\lesssim|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi|^{2}
, hence

\int_{C_{u}}r^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}\lesssim E(u).

(3.21)

Using this, together with Proposition
3.8
and (
1.8
) immediately proves

p\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}E_{p-1}[\psi](u)du+\tilde{E}_{p}(u_{2})\lesssim\tilde{E}_{p}(u_{1})+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}.

(3.22)

Then (
3.20
) follows from an application of Lemma
3.3
.
∎

We finally obtain a first (non-optimal) decay result of the energy.

Corollary 3.10
.

E[\phi](u)\lesssim u^{-p_{low}}\lesssim u^{-2+O(\sqrt{\epsilon})}.

(3.23)

Proof.

Immediate from the combination of Corollary
3.9
and Lemma
3.1
.
∎

3.5
Faster energy decay for higher spherical harmonics

In this section, we prove that the higher spherical harmonics decay faster than the spherical average. Our goal is to reproduce the
0\leq p\leq 2
hierarchy for the commuted quantity
\Psi_{1}=r^{2}\partial_{v}\psi
(see
[
AAG18b
]
where this idea was first introduced). First, we define

\phi_{\geq 1}(u,v,\omega):=\phi(u,v,\omega)-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi(u,v,\omega)d\omega

(3.24)

and
\psi_{\geq 1}(u,v,\omega)=r\phi_{\geq 1}(u,v,\omega)
. We will also denote
\Psi_{1}(u,v,\omega)=r^{2}\partial_{v}\psi_{\geq 1}(u,v,\omega)
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Recall
F=\epsilon\ r^{-2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{1}\tilde{w}_{i}(u,v)\cdot\partial_{u}^{i}\psi+\tilde{q}(u,v)\cdot r\partial_{v}\psi\right)
. Then, if
\phi
is a solution of (
1.3
):

|\partial_{v}(\frac{r^{2}F}{\Omega^{2}})-\epsilon\cdot\frac{\tilde{w}_{1}(u,v)}{4r^{2}}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|\lesssim\epsilon\ r^{-2}\left(|\psi|+|\partial_{u}\psi|+|\Psi_{1}|+r|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|\right)

(3.25)

(
3.25
) is also true replacing
F
by
F_{\geq 1}
, mutatis mutandis.

Proof.

Immediate calculation, using (
2.1
), (
1.4
) and (
2.2
).
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

For all
u_{1}<u_{2}
, we have for all
0<p\leq p_{low}
:

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}\left(r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+r^{p+1}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}\right)\\
&\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1,\ |\beta|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{\beta}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}.\end{split}

(3.26)

Proof.

Fix
0<p<2
. We make use of Lemma
3.5
and integrate (
3.9
) on
\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})
. By the fact that for
p<2
, the boundary terms in

\partial_{v}\left(r^{p-2}\big(-\partial_{u}r\partial_{v}r+r\partial_{u}\partial_{v}r\big)|\Psi_{1}|^{2}-\frac{\Omega^{2}r^{p-2}}{8}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Psi_{1}|^{2}\right)

, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}[2+\frac{p}{2}]r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}\\
&+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}\left(\frac{\Omega^{2}}{8}\big([2-p]\partial_{v}r-r\partial_{v}\log(\Omega^{2})\big)|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\big((p-2)\partial_{v}r[-\partial_{v}r\partial_{u}r+r\partial_{uv}^{2}r]-(\partial_{vv}^{2}r)(\partial_{u}r)+r\partial_{u}\partial_{v}\partial_{v}r\big)|\Psi_{1}|^{2}\right)\\
&\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\epsilon\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-2}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|(|\psi|+|\partial_{u}\psi|+|\Psi_{1}|+r|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|)+\epsilon\bigl|\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-2}\tilde{w}_{1}[\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}]\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\bigr|\\
&+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1,\ |\beta|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{\beta}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}.\end{split}

(3.27)

Now, we use the smallness of
\epsilon
to absorb the
\epsilon\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-2}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|(|\psi|+|\partial_{u}\psi|+|\Psi_{1}|+r|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|)
term into the left-hand-side from which we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}[2+\frac{p}{2}]r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}\\
&+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}\left(\frac{\Omega^{2}}{8}\big([2-p]\partial_{v}r-r\partial_{v}\log(\Omega^{2})\big)|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\big((p-2)\partial_{v}r[-\partial_{v}r\partial_{u}r+r\partial_{uv}^{2}r]-(\partial_{vv}^{2}r)(\partial_{u}r)+r\partial_{u}\partial_{v}\partial_{v}r\big)|\Psi_{1}|^{2}\right)\\
&\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\epsilon\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}(|\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{u}\psi|^{2}+|\Psi_{1}|^{2})+\epsilon\bigl|\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-2}\tilde{w}_{1}[\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}]\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\bigr|\\
&+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1,\ |\beta|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{\beta}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}.\end{split}

(3.28)

Apply Proposition
3.6
with
q=p

\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{q-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}\lesssim E(u_{1})

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}(|\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{u}\psi|^{2})\lesssim[2-p]^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}\left[r^{p-3}|\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+r^{p-5}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}\right]\\
&+(2-p)^{-1}(E(u_{1})+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}).\end{split}

(3.29)

Now we can apply Proposition
3.6
again to
\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi

\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-5}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi|^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{v}\psi|^{2}+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1,\ |\beta|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{\beta}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}.

(3.30)

Combining (
3.29

3.30

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}(|\psi|^{2}+|\partial_{u}\psi|^{2})\lesssim[2-p]^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-7}\left[|\Psi_{1}|^{2}+|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Psi_{1}|^{2}\right]\\
&+(2-p)^{-1}(E(u_{1})+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1,\ |\beta|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{\beta}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}).\end{split}

3.28

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\Psi_{1})_{\geq 2}|^{2}\geq 6\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(\Psi_{1})_{\geq 2}|^{2}

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}\left(\frac{\Omega^{2}}{8}\big([2-p]\partial_{v}r-r\partial_{v}\log(\Omega^{2})\big)|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\big((p-2)\partial_{v}r[-\partial_{v}r\partial_{u}r+r\partial_{uv}^{2}r]-(\partial_{vv}^{2}r)(\partial_{u}r)+r\partial_{u}\partial_{v}\partial_{v}r\big)|\Psi_{1}|^{2}\right)\\

(3.32)

Thus, combining this estimate with (
3.28

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}[2+\frac{p}{2}]r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+[2-p]\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Psi|^{2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\epsilon\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}|\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\epsilon\bigl|\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-2}\tilde{w}_{1}[\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}]\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\bigr|\\
&+E(u_{1})+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1,\ |\beta|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{\beta}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}.\end{split}

(3.33)

Now, by Proposition
3.6
(Hardy inequality) applying to
\Psi_{1}
with
q=p
, we get, using the fact that
\epsilon[2-p]^{-1}=o(1)

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}[2+\frac{p}{2}]r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+[2-p]\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-4}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Psi|^{2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}+\epsilon\bigl|\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-2}\tilde{w}_{1}[\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}]\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\bigr|+E(u_{1})+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1,\ |\beta|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{\beta}\phi|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}.\end{split}

Finally, we handle the term
\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-2}\tilde{w}_{1}[\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}]\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi

(3.35)

where in the last inequality, we have applied Proposition

\begin{split}&\bigl|\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-2}\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}\tilde{w}_{1}(u,v)\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\bigr|\\

Corollary 3.13

Finally, we apply the hierarchy of Corollary

Corollary 3.14
.

The following estimate holds for all

E[\phi_{\geq 1}](u)\lesssim u^{-2p_{low}}.

(3.39)

Proof.

First, we choose a dyadic sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and apply Corollary
3.38
to

,
u_{2}=u_{n+1}
; then, by the mean-value theorem, there exists
u_{n}^{*}\in[u_{n},u_{n+1}]
such that

E_{p-1}[\Psi_{1}](u_{n}^{*})\lesssim u_{n}^{-1}\left(E_{p}[\Psi_{1}](u_{n})+\sum_{|\beta|\leq 1}E[\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{\beta}\phi_{\geq 1}](u_{n})\right)\lesssim u_{n}^{-1}\left(E_{p}[\Psi_{1}](u_{0})+\sum_{|\beta|\leq 1}E[\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{\beta}\phi_{\geq 1}](u_{0})\right)\lesssim u_{n}^{-1},

where in the last inequality, we have also used (
1.8
) applied to
\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{\beta}\phi_{\geq 1}
. Then by using Corollary
3.38
again, we obtain that for all
u\in[u_{n},u_{n+1}]

E_{p-1}[\Psi_{1}](u)\lesssim u^{-1}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we obtain that

E_{0}[\Psi_{1}](u)=\int_{C_{u}}|\partial_{v}\Psi_{1}|^{2}\lesssim u^{-p_{low}}.

Then, by Proposition
3.6
(Hardy inequality), we obtain

\int_{C_{u}}r^{-2}|\Psi_{1}|^{2}=\int_{C_{u}}r^{2}|\partial_{v}\psi_{\geq 1}|^{2}=E_{2}[\psi_{\geq 1}](u)\lesssim u^{-p_{low}}.

In particular, we have

E_{p_{low}}[\psi_{\geq 1}](u)\lesssim u^{-p_{low}}.

Then, we can apply Lemma
3.1
again to
\psi_{\geq 1}
, this time invoking Corollary
3.9
applied to
\phi_{\geq 1}
to finally obtain (

)
as desired.

∎

r|\phi_{\geq 1}|(u,v,\omega)\lesssim_{\eta}u^{\frac{1}{2}-p_{low}+\eta}\lesssim u^{-\frac{3}{2}+O(\sqrt{\epsilon})},

(

with

3.6
Higher-weighted energy estimates for the spherical average

In Section
3.4
, we managed to prove the boundedness of
r^{p}
-weighted estimates for
p\leq p_{low}=2-O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
. In this section, we want to obtain a similar result for
2\leq p<3-O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
, which comes arbitrarily close to the sharp exponents
p<3
. To do this, we will restrict to our attention to the spherical-average of the solution

\phi_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\phi(u,v,\omega)d\omega.

(3.43)

This section generalizes the treatment of
[
VdM22
]
in Section 6.3, whose main idea is to absorb the error terms inside the boundary term
E_{p}[\psi](u_{2})
instead of absorbing them into the bulk term
\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}E_{p-1}[\psi](u)du
with the help of a Grönwall argument. We point out, however, that the argument has been extended and significantly streamlined compared to its previous version present in
[
VdM22
]
.

We start by quoting an easy calculus lemma, on which the Grönwall argument will be eventually based.

Lemma 3.16
.

The following identity holds for all
u_{1}<u_{2}
:

\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\frac{du}{a\sqrt{u}+b}=\frac{2}{a}\left[\sqrt{u}-\frac{b}{a}\log(\sqrt{u}+\frac{b}{a})\right]_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}},

(3.44)

where we defined
\left[F(u)\right]_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}:=F(u_{2})-F(u_{1}).

Proof.

The proof is elementary, using a change of variable
x=\sqrt{u}
.
∎

Then, we move to the main result of this section, which quantifies faster energy decay for the spherical average than what was obtained in Section
3.4
.

Proposition 3.17
.

There exists
2<p_{high}(\epsilon)\leq 1+p_{low}<3
such that
p_{high}=3-O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
and
\delta(\epsilon)=O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
such that for all
u\geq u_{0}
,
0\leq q\leq p_{high}-1

\tilde{E}_{q}[\phi_{0}](u)\lesssim u^{-p_{high}+2\delta+q},

(3.45)

\tilde{E}_{p_{high}}[\phi_{0}](u)\lesssim u^{2\delta}.

(3.46)

Proof.

Let
2<p\leq 1+p_{low}<3
. Similarly to the proof of Proposition
3.8
, we use the multiplier
r^{p}\partial_{v}\psi
, and we apply Lemma
3.4
to obtain, using (
1.4
), (
2.2
)

\begin{split}&\bigl|\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}-\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}\bigr|+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}\lesssim C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{0}|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}\\
&+\epsilon\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}+\epsilon\left[\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}\right]^{1/2}\left[\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-4}(|\psi_{0}|^{2}+|\partial_{u}\psi_{0}|^{2})\right]^{1/2}\end{split}

Then, by Proposition
3.6
applying with
q=p-1
, and the fact that
p<3
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\bigl|\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}-\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}\bigr|+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}\lesssim C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{0}|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}\\
&+\epsilon[3-p]^{-1}\left[\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}\right]^{1/2}\left[\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-2}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}\right]^{1/2}.\end{split}

Then, by an averaging argument in
R
similar to the one used in Corollary
3.9
,

\begin{split}&\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}E_{p-1}[\psi_{0}](u)du+\bigl|E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u_{2})-E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u_{1})\bigr|\\
&\lesssim\bigl|E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u_{2})-E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u_{1})\bigr|\lesssim\epsilon[3-p]^{-1}\left[\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u)du\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}E_{p-2}[\psi_{0}]du\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}+E(u_{1}).\end{split}

(3.47)

Then, applying Corollary
3.9
, in view of the fact that
p-1\leq p_{low}
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}E_{p-1}[\psi_{0}](u)du+\bigl|E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u_{2})-E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u_{1})\bigr|\\
&\lesssim\bigl|E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u_{2})-E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u_{1})\bigr|\lesssim\epsilon[3-p]^{-1}\left[\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u)du\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\tilde{E}_{p-1}(u_{1})\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}+E(u_{1}).\end{split}

(3.48)

In what follows, we take
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, a dyadic sequence, and
u_{2}=u\in[u_{n},u_{n+1}]
and
u_{1}=u_{n}
.
For some large
\Delta>0
,
D>0
and small
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
to be determined later, we introduce the induction hypotheses

\tilde{E}_{p-1}(u_{k})\leq D^{2}\cdot\Delta^{2}u_{k}^{-1+2\delta},

(3.49)

\tilde{E}_{p}(u_{k})\leq\Delta^{2}u_{k}^{2\delta}.

(3.50)

(
3.49
), (
3.50
) are obviously satisfied for
k=0
(providing
\Delta
is large enough, depending on
u_{0}
, where we recall

) and we will assume it holds for all
0\leq k\leq n
. Then, combining (
3.49
) and (
3.48
) shows that there exists
C>0
(independent of
\epsilon
and

) and

such that

E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u)\leq\underbrace{\Delta\cdot D\cdot\eta(\epsilon)\cdot u_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}_{=a(u_{n},\Delta,\epsilon)}\left[\int_{u_{n}}^{u}E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u^{\prime})du^{\prime}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\underbrace{E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u_{n})+CE(u_{n})}_{=b(u_{n})}.

(3.51)

Therefore, as an application of Lemma
3.16

\left(\int_{u_{n}}^{u}E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u^{\prime})du^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{a}{2}(u-u_{n})+\frac{b}{a}\log\left(1+\frac{a}{b}(\int_{u_{n}}^{u}E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u^{\prime})du^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Denoting
X=\frac{a}{b}\left(\int_{u_{n}}^{u}E_{p}[\psi_{0}](u^{\prime})du^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and

from which we get

Note that

for all

Now, note that, as a consequence of (

) and Corollary
3.10
, there exists

\epsilon

(3.56)

So, combining (
3.55
) and (
3.56
) gives, for

large enough and

(3.57)

To achieve a small

, we take

to be independent of
\epsilon
and

, so that
\eta(\epsilon)=O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
. We will also take

to be independent of

and choose

2^{2\delta}=[1+2\Delta\eta(\epsilon)]^{2}

In view of Corollary
3.10
, the left-hand-side of (

and, therefore, (

∎

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}|\Theta_{0}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}\\
&+\epsilon\left[\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{2p-3-p_{high}}[|\psi_{0}|^{2}+|\partial_{u}\psi_{0}|^{2}]\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p_{high-1}}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}+E(u_{1})\\
&\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}+\epsilon\left[\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{2p-3-p_{high}}[|\psi_{0}|^{2}+|\partial_{u}\psi_{0}|^{2}]\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{2}^{\delta}+E(u_{1}).\end{split}

Now, note that
2p-3-p_{high}<-1
so we can still apply Proposition
3.6
(Hardy inequality) and obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u_{2}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-1}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p-3}|\Theta_{0}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}+\epsilon\left[\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{2p-1-p_{high}}|\partial_{v}\psi_{0}|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{2}^{\delta}+E(u_{1})\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}+u_{2}^{\delta}\cdot u_{1}^{-p_{high}+p},\end{split}

(4.8)

where in the last line, we have used Proposition
3.17
. Now, applying (
4.8
) if
u_{1}<u_{2}=u\in[u_{n},u_{n+1}]
, where
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a dyadic sequence easily gives

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{u}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{C_{u_{1}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}+u_{n}^{-p_{high}+p+\delta}.\end{split}

(4.9)

Note that by (
4.7
) applied to
u_{1}=u_{n}
and
u_{2}=u_{n+1}
, there exists
u_{n}^{*}\in[u_{n},u_{n+1}]
such that

\int_{C_{u_{n}^{*}}}r^{p_{high}-1}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}\lesssim u_{n}^{-1+2\delta}.

(4.10)

By interpolation between (
4.7
) with
u_{2}=u_{n}^{*}
and (
4.10
), we obtain

\int_{C_{u_{n}^{*}}}r^{p}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}\lesssim u_{n}^{-p_{high}+p+2\delta}.

(4.11)

Taking
u_{1}=u_{n}^{*}
in (
4.9
) and combining with (
4.11
) then concludes the proof of the proposition.

∎

4.2
Improved decay for the time-derivative

In this section, we take advantage of Proposition

to derive improved decay for the

, in order to prove (

To fix the notations, recall

\partial_{v}T\psi_{0}=F+\partial_{v}^{2}\psi_{0}=F+\partial_{v}(r^{-1}\Theta_{0})=F+r^{-1}\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}-r^{-2}[\partial_{v}r]\Theta_{0}.

(4.12)

We start by showing improved decay for the

), (

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists
p_{T}(\epsilon)\in(2,p_{high})
,
\eta_{T}(\epsilon)\in(0,1)
, with
p_{T}(\epsilon)=3-O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
and
\eta_{T}(\epsilon)=O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, such that for all dyadic sequences
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, there exists
u_{n}^{*}\in[u_{n},u_{n+1}]
such that

(4.13)

Moreover, the following estimate holds for all dyadic sequences
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{n},u_{n+1})}r^{p_{T}}|\partial_{v}T\psi_{0}|^{2}=\int_{u_{n}}^{u_{n+1}}\tilde{E}_{p_{T}}[T\phi_{0}](u)du\lesssim u_{n}^{-1+2\eta_{T}(\epsilon)}.

(4.14)

Proof.

Let
p^{\prime}=3-\nu
, where
\nu\in(0,1)
. We write, using (
1.4
)

r^{p^{\prime}}|\partial_{v}T\psi_{0}|\lesssim r^{p^{\prime}}F^{2}+r^{p^{\prime}-4}|\Theta_{0}|^{2}+r^{p^{\prime}-2}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}r^{p^{\prime}-4}\left(|\psi_{0}|^{2}+|\partial_{u}\psi_{0}|^{2}\right)+r^{p^{\prime}-4}|\Theta_{0}|^{2}+r^{p^{\prime}-2}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}.

(4.15)

for
u_{1}<u_{2}
, we integrate (
4.15
) on
\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})
, and, making use of Proposition
3.6
(Hardy inequality) with

\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p^{\prime}}|\partial_{v}T\psi_{0}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p^{\prime}-4}|\Theta_{0}|^{2}+r^{p^{\prime}-2}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}+C_{R}\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}|\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{0}|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime},

(4.16)

which turns into the following estimate after applying Lemma

\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p^{\prime}}|\partial_{v}T\psi_{0}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p^{\prime}-4}|\Theta_{0}|^{2}+r^{p^{\prime}-2}|\partial_{v}\Theta_{0}|^{2}+E(u_{1}).

(4.17)

Now, if
\nu\geq C\epsilon
, then
p^{\prime}-1+2\nu\in(2+C\epsilon,p_{high})
and thus we have by Proposition
4.1
applied to

that, assuming as in its statement that
u_{n}\leq u_{1}<u_{2}\leq u_{n+1}
, where

(4.18)

Thus, combining with (
4.17
) and (

\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})}r^{p^{\prime}}|\partial_{v}T\psi_{0}|^{2}\lesssim u_{n}^{-1+O(\sqrt{\epsilon})}.

(4.19)

Thus, by the mean-value theorem, there exists
u_{n}^{*}\in[u_{n},u_{n+1}]
such that

\int_{C_{u_{n}^{*}}}r^{p^{\prime}}|\partial_{v}T\psi_{0}|^{2}\lesssim u_{n}^{-2+O(\sqrt{\epsilon})},

(4.20)

which concludes the proof.
∎

Now, we move on to using the extra assumptions (

As a consequence of the assumptions (

(4.22)

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume (
1.12
), (
1.13

Lemma 4.4
.

The following estimate holds:

Proof.

1.13

|\phi_{0}|^{2}(u,v_{R}(u))\lesssim\bigl|\int_{u}^{+\infty}\phi_{0}\cdot T\phi_{0}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}\bigr|\lesssim\left[\int_{u}^{+\infty}|\phi_{0}|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\int_{u}^{+\infty}|T\phi_{0}|^{2}(u^{\prime},v_{R}(u^{\prime}))du^{\prime}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

Next, we prove similar bounds for

Lemma 4.5
.

The following estimate holds:

Proof.

\eta

Next, we write, using (
4.12

Certifying non-classicality and non-Gaussianity through optical parametric amplification Journal: opticajournal
Mahmoud Kalash, M.H.M. Passos, Éva Rácz, László Ruppert, Radim Filip, Maria V. Chekhova

Abstract
Non-Gaussian states of light are essential for numerous quantum information protocols; thus, certifying non-Gaussianity is crucial. Full quantum state tomography achieves this, but it implies observing the Wigner function negativity, which requires efficient detection. Certifying non-Gaussianity through directly measurable parameters is a simpler alternative, typically achieved by measuring photon-number probabilities - either directly using photon-number resolving detectors or through Hanbury Brown–Twiss type measurements with single-photon detectors. Here, we demonstrate theoretically and experimentally that optical parametric amplification combined with conventional intensity detectors can effectively replace this approach without the need for photon-number resolution. In our method, we measure the mean photon number and the second-order correlation function for the amplified state. Using it, we successfully certify the non-Gaussianity of a heralded quasi-single-photon state. Since optical parametric amplification is a multimode process, our method provides a foundation for developing high-dimensional quantum technologies utilizing multimode non-Gaussian states.

In this work, we propose optical parametric amplification to certify the non-classicality (NC) and non-Gaussianity (NG) of quantum states. Specifically, we show that the directly measurable mean photon number and second-order correlation function, both obtained after parametric amplification using conventional intensity detectors without photon-number resolution, can effectively replace coincidence measurements. Furthermore, this approach does not assume the negligibility of higher photon-number probabilities. Another key advantage of using OPAs is their ability to perform multimode amplification, enabling the simultaneous certification of NG in multiple modes occupied by different states. This capability marks a significant advancement for quantum technologies.

As a proof of principle, we seed an OPA with a heralded quasi-single-photon state and estimate the photon-number statistics, marking the first experimental demonstration of amplification and measurements for such non-Gaussian states. Although the full photon-number distribution is generally informative, in our case it does not suffice to reveal NG due to the significant degradation of the input single photon, a situation frequently observed in realistic experimental conditions. We show, however, that our witness based on the mean photon number and second-order correlation function measurements is robust enough to certify NG even for highly degraded states. This is demonstrated by tracking the witness as we increase the brightness of the input state, revealing a gradual transition from certified NG to NC, and eventually to classicality as higher-order photon contributions arise. The results align well with those obtained from the standard coincidence measurements.

Photon-number statistics of amplified quantum states

When characterizing a state at the input of an OPA, one parameter to consider is the mean photon number after amplification,
\mu\equiv\left<\hat{N}\right>
, with
\hat{N}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}
being the photon number operator and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
(
\hat{a}
) the photon creation (annihilation) operator, respectively. Using the Bogoliubov transformation,

\hat{a}=\hat{a}_{0}\cosh{G}+\hat{a}_{0}^{\dagger}\sinh{G},

(1)

with the parametric gain

G
and input operators
\hat{a}_{0},\hat{a}_{0}^{\dagger}
, we can evaluate
\mu
for various input states. For example, when the OPA is fed with a vacuum, a thermal state with the mean photon number
\overline{n}

|n\rangle

\mu_{0}=\sinh^{2}{G}
,
\mu_{\mathrm{th}}=(2\overline{n}+1)\sinh^{2}{G}
, and
\mu_{|n\rangle}=(2n+1)\sinh^{2}{G}+n\approx(2n+1)\sinh^{2}{G}

22

Although sensitive to the input energy, the latter two cases demonstrate that the mean photon number after amplification is, on its own, not suitable to discriminate between different types of states:
\mu
is the same for thermal or Fock inputs when
\overline{n}=n
.

On the other hand, the normalized second-order correlation function
g^{(2)}\equiv g^{(2)}(0)=\frac{\braket{a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}aa}}{{\braket{a^{\dagger}a}\braket{a^{\dagger}a}}}\equiv 1+\frac{\langle(\hat{N}-\mu)^{2}\rangle}{\mu^{2}}-\mu^{-1}

[
23

For instance,

indicates thermal (super-Poissonian) statistics. To illustrate how

reflects the input state NG, consider amplified Fock states, which are inherently non-Gaussian.
At high amplification gain, vacuum and thermal states both yield

[
22
]

However, amplified higher-order Fock states yield significantly lower
g^{(2)}

\leq 1.66\ \text{for}\ n\geq 1

g^{(2)}
clearly indicates sensitivity to the NG of the input states.

Non-classical states are those whose Glauber–Sudarshan
P
representation is not a classical two-dimensional probability distribution.
This is equivalent to such states not being expressible as mixtures of coherent states. Analogously, quantum non-Gaussian states are defined as states that cannot be represented as mixtures of Gaussian states, that is, states whose Wigner function is a plain two-dimensional Gaussian distribution. Squeezed vacuum, for example, is a non-classical Gaussian state. All non-Gaussian states are, by definition, also non-classical.

Both NC and NG may be witnessed through the probabilities of zero-photon and single-photon events: for classical or Gaussian states and a fixed probability
p_{0}
of no photons, the probability
p_{1}

p_{0}

[
19

24

25

p_{1}

Special case: a heralded quasi-single-photon state

For demonstration, we focus on the case of a quantum state obtained through single-photon heralding at the output of a weakly pumped two-mode squeezer. This state is a mixture of a vacuum, a single-photon state, and higher-order Fock states whose contributions are small.
This heralded state is straightforward to model, and all photon-number probabilities and moments are analytically accessible for a given set of input parameters (losses, mean number of photon pairs leaving the crystal). This scenario is therefore ideal to compare the performance of the coincidence- and moment-based witnesses.

Experiment.

We generate the heralded quasi-single-photon state from a twin-beam source and track its NG through optical parametric amplification while varying the source brightness. Higher brightness leads to increased multi-photon contributions, which in turn reduces the NG of the state. Figure

To obtain high signal-to-noise ratio, we set the parametric gain at
G=6.5\pm 0.5

2
Discussion

We have proposed and experimentally
demonstrated optical parametric amplification as a tool for certifying the non-Gaussianity and non-classicality of phase-independent quantum states. Specifically, we theoretically showed that the directly measurable mean photon number and second-order correlation function after amplification provide information similar to that revealed from photon-number probabilities and coincidence measurements. Note that a current, independent approach came to our attention, which also uses correlation functions to characterize the non-Gaussianity of states
[
29
]
. Similarly to the standard approach, they also use single-photon detectors, while our method only needs intensity detectors.

Experimentally, we examined a heralded quasi-single-photon state generated through parametric down-conversion and successfully tracked its transition from the non-Gaussian to the non-classical and then to classical regime by increasing its brightness. We found that although the photon-number distributions of amplified Gaussian and non-Gaussian states are barely distinguishable, our proposed method allows us to accurately characterize these states. Furthermore, we showed that losses, including those arising due to mismatch between the modes of the examined state and the amplifier, are not critical for this measurement and can always be corrected for. Our experimental results closely match the predictions of the developed theory and agree with the results obtained through the standard coincidence measurements.

In addition to eliminating the need for single-photon detectors, an OPA naturally enables multimode detection. This is especially important for applications where distinct non-Gaussian states occupying multiple modes need to be certified simultaneously. Thus, our method is an excellent candidate for high-dimensional quantum information technologies.

This OPA-based approach to certifying non-classicality and non-Gaussianity can also be generalized to phase-dependent states; however, this requires a more involved setup
[
21
]
.

Methods

Detailed experimental scheme

Figure
6
presents the complete experimental setup. A polarizing beam splitter (PBS) separates the pump beam — centered at 400 nm with a repetition rate of 5 kHz and a pulse duration of
1.5
ps — into two arms of the setup. The pump power is adjusted using a half-wave plate (HWP). In the upper arm, the pump is focused (
w_{p}=105\,\mu\mathrm{m}
) onto a 3-mm thick
\beta
-barium borate (BBO) crystal, configured for type-II phase-matched parametric down-conversion (PDC), generating beam-like photon pairs at the degenerate wavelength of 800 nm. A variable neutral density (ND) filter is included to modulate the brightness of the SPDC source by adjusting the pump power. The idler photons (H-polarized) are transmitted through the PBS and directed to the heralding arm. After collimation with lens F1 = 30
\mathrm{mm}
, the idler beam passes through a 0.5-nm interference filter (centered at 800 nm) to ensure spectral purity and is then coupled into a single-mode fiber (SMF) using lens F2
=5.5\,\mathrm{mm}
. The SMF guides the photons to a superconducting nanowire single-photon detector (SPD) with a quantum efficiency of approximately 80%. This detector acts as a heralding trigger, sending a signal to open the shutter of an sCMOS camera for synchronized detection of the amplified signal. The signal photons (V-polarized), reflected by the PBS, are directed into the amplification arm.

The same laser source used for PDC also pumps the optical parametric amplifier (OPA), which is implemented using collinear, degenerate, type-I parametric down-conversion in a 3-mm-thick bismuth borate (BiBO) crystal. The amplifier is operated in a phase-sensitive configuration with a parametric gain
G=6.5
, optimized for high signal-to-noise ratio. To achieve spatial mode matching, telescopes T1 (-50 mm and 50 mm) and T3 (-30 mm and 75 mm) are employed. Temporal overlap between the signal and pump pulses is ensured using a translation stage (TS) in the pump path. The amplified signal is collimated by lens F3 = 75 mm and spatially filtered using a 200-
\mu
m pinhole, while spectrally filtered using a similar 0.5-nm interference filter used in the heralding arm. The detection of the amplified radiation is performed by an sCMOS camera with a quantum efficiency of approximately 50
\%
. Upon receipt of a trigger signal from the SPD, the camera requires
\sim 10\ \mu s
to start the exposure. Therefore, to ensure proper synchronization with the heralding signal, the amplified beam is optically delayed
\sim 15\ \mu s
using a 3-km single-mode optical fiber.

Mode matching and the efficiency of amplification

Mode matching is an important condition governing the efficiency of the OPA process. Indeed, considering a single-mode field
a_{0}
at the input of the OPA, and a measurement on an amplifier mode
a
, the amplified state becomes
a^{{}^{\prime}}=\kappa a_{0}\ +(1-\kappa)a_{v}
. Here
a_{v}
is the vacuum field, and
\kappa
defines the mode overlap between the input and the amplifier mode. That is, if mode matching is imperfect (
\kappa<1
), the efficiency of the amplification decreases as vacuum is contributing more. In our case, the heralded photon, generated using a type-II source, has a much narrower spectral mode when compared to the amplifier mode based on type-I collinear-degenerate parametric down-conversion. This is the main reason behind the degraded OPA efficiency. However, this issue can be resolved by spectrally shaping the OPA modes. Alternatively, a broadband single photon source based on type-I parametric down-conversion would suit the detection scheme.

Heralding efficiency

The heralding efficiency is defined as the probability of observing a photon in the signal arm, provided that a photon was detected in the heralding arm. If higher Fock-state probabilities are negligible, this quantity coincides with the effective transmittance of the signal arm, denoted by
\eta
, and the single-photon probability
p_{1}
.
For instance, setting the brightness of the source to 0.01 ppp,
after subtracting the accidental contributions we had
6\cdot 10^{-4}
coincidences per pulse
between SPD1 and SPD2.
Correcting for 34% SPD2 efficiency, 50% beamsplitter, 90% optical transmissivity, and 80% fiber coupling, we obtained a (51
\pm\ 2
) % heralding efficiency, and it corresponds to the effective purity of the prepared quasi-single-photon state. For higher source brightness, multiphoton effects start to play a significant role. Still, one can use the theoretical model to evaluate the heralding efficiency, which provides similar numbers.

Photon-number probabilities from HBT measurements

Assuming that an incoming signal with unknown photon-number distribution is split on a beam splitter with transmittance
T
, and the transmitted photons are measured by detector
A
with probability
p_{A}
, whereas the reflected photons are detected by detector
B
with probability
p_{B}
, we can estimate the photon-number probabilities of a weak signal as

\displaystyle p_{2+}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{2}}{2p_{A}p_{B}TR},

(2)

\displaystyle p_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{1}-p_{2}\left[2TR(q_{B}p_{A}+q_{A}p_{B})+T^{2}(1-q_{A}^{2})+R^{2}(1-q_{B}^{2})\right]}{Tp_{A}+Rp_{B}},

(3)

\displaystyle p_{0}

\displaystyle=1-p_{1}-p_{2+},

(4)

with
Q_{2}
denoting the probability of double clicks and
Q_{1}
denoting the probability of single clicks;
R\equiv 1-T
,
q_{A}\equiv 1-p_{A}
, and
q_{B}\equiv 1-p_{B}
were introduced for convenience.

Knowing the value of extra loss characterized by a transmittance
T^{\prime}

and suffered before the beam splitter of the HBT scheme, it is straightforward to correct for it:

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{2+}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{2+}}{T^{\prime 2}};

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{1}}{T^{\prime}}-p^{\prime}_{2+}\cdot 2(1-T^{\prime});

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle=1-p^{\prime}_{1}-p^{\prime}_{2+}.

Non-classicality and non-Gaussianity witnesses

The NG witness introduced by Filip
et al.

[
19
]
can be expressed as a parametric curve (solid black line in Fig.
1
(a)) parametrized by
r\geqslant 0
:

\begin{split}\tilde{p}_{0}(r)&=\frac{\exp\{-\frac{1}{4}(e^{4r}-1)(1-\tanh{r})\}}{\cosh{r}},\\
\tilde{p}_{1}(r)&=\frac{(e^{4r}-1)\exp\{-\frac{1}{4}(e^{4r}-1)(1-\tanh{r})\}}{4\cosh^{3}{r}}.\end{split}

(5)

Any state for which the pair of zero-photon and single-photon probabilities lies beyond this curve (green area in Fig.
1
(a)) cannot be represented as a mixture of Gaussian states. The non-classicality witness in terms of number probabilities can be given simply as
p_{1}>-p_{0}\ln p_{0}
(area above the dotted line in Fig.
1
(a)).

For our purposes, we are interested in the post-amplification witnesses for phase-independent states. It is straightforward to show that for phase-independent states, the asymptotic (large amplification) relationship between pre- and post-amplification moments is quite simple:

\begin{split}\mu_{\mathrm{rel}}&=2m+1,\\
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{rel}}&=2\left(1+m+m^{2}+3s^{2}\right),\end{split}

(6)

where the normalization constants are
\sinh^{2}G
and
\sinh^{4}G
, respectively. That is, the value of
m
determines
\mu_{\mathrm{rel}}
, and for
m
fixed,
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{rel}}
is a linearly increasing function of
s^{2}
, and therefore the problem of minimizing
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{rel}}
for a fixed value of
\mu_{\mathrm{rel}}
is equivalent to minimizing
s^{2}
for a fixed value of
m
. The problem of minimizing
s^{2}
over arbitrary mixtures of Gaussian states for a fixed value of
m
has been solved in
[
21
]
; the boundary of this NG witness is shown in Fig.
1
(b) with a thick black line. Note that for a fixed value of the mean
m
, the minimization of the variance
s^{2}
is equivalent to minimizing the pre-amplification values of the second order correlation function, the second moment of the integrated intensity, and the non-centered second moment of the photon numbers, so such alternatives can be used interchangeably (note that the latter is a convex function, and that form is used to prove the validity of witness for mixtures, for details, see
[
21
]
).

Using furthermore that for large amplification,
g^{(2)}=1+\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{rel}}/\mu_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}
, the boundary of the post-amplification non-Gaussianity witness can be given as a parametric curve (see Fig.
1
(c), thick black line)

\begin{split}\tilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{rel}}(r)&=e^{r}\cosh 2r,\\
\tilde{g}^{(2)}(r)&=3-3\cdot\frac{\sinh^{2}r\cdot\left(\sinh 2r+1\right)}{\cosh^{2}2r},\end{split}

(7)

with
r\geqslant 0
. For large values of
r
,
\tilde{g}^{(2)}(r)\approx 3/2+9e^{-4r}
,
whereas for small values,
\tilde{g}^{(2)}(r)\approx 3-3r^{2}
.
The non-Gaussianity witness for a given value of
\mu_{\mathrm{rel}}
is then

g^{(2)}<\tilde{g}^{(2)}\left(\left[\tilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{rel}}\right]^{-1}\!(\mu_{\mathrm{rel}})\right),

(8)

with
[\cdot]^{-1}
denoting the inverse of a function. The inverse exists since
\tilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{rel}}(r)
is a strictly increasing function. The asymptotic boundary is reached very quickly in the value of the amplification parameter
G
, and the boundary of the witness for non-asymptotic values lies higher than the asymptotic curve. As a consequence, using the asymptotic curve as a witness is a conservative choice.

The non-classicality witness in terms of
g^{(2)}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{rel}}
is straightforward to calculate using that the photon number variance of a coherent state is equal to its mean (
s^{2}=m
), and yields

g^{(2)}<\frac{3}{2}+\frac{3}{\mu_{\mathrm{rel}}}-\frac{3}{2\mu_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}}.

(9)

In their stated forms, the NC/NG witnesses are valid only for a single mode. However, this assumption is close to the truth in our case, since the parametrically amplified scheme includes a single-mode fiber.

Pure symmetric automorphisms, extensions of RAAGs, and Koszulness Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification 20F28, 20F36, 20F65, 20J05, 17B55, 16S37.
Conchita Martínez-Pérez, Luis Mendonça

Abstract
. We characterize in terms of a combinatorial condition on the graph Γ \Gamma when the group PAut ⁡ ( A Γ ) \operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}) of pure symmetric automorphisms of the RAAG A Γ A_{\Gamma} and its outer version POut ⁡ ( A Γ ) \operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma}) have a descending central Lie algebra which is Koszul. To do that, we prove that our combinatorial condition implies that these groups are iterated extensions of RAAGs; in particular, they are poly-free. On the other hand, we show that PAut ⁡ ( F n ) \operatorname{PAut}(F_{n}) is not poly-finitely generated free for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 . We also show that groups in a certain class containing PAut ⁡ ( A Γ ) \operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}) are 1-formal.



1.
Introduction

The group of pure-symmetric automorphisms of a right-angled Artin group
A_{\Gamma}
is the subgroup
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})\subseteq{\rm Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
consisting of the automorphisms that send each standard generator
v\in\Gamma
to a conjugate
v^{g}
. Among these are the
partial conjugations
, that is, the automorphisms
\phi=c_{L}^{v}
with

w^{\phi}=\begin{cases}w^{v}&\text{if $w\in L$},\\
w&\text{if $w\notin L$},\end{cases}

where
L
is a connected component of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)
and
{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)
is the star of
v
in
\Gamma
. In fact Laurence proves in
[
15
]
that
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
is generated by all partial conjugations.

The following terms are useful in the analysis of relations holding among partial conjugations. For a pair of non-adjacent vertices
v,w\in\Gamma
, the connected components
D_{w}\subseteq\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(v)
and

D_{v}\subseteq\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(w)
that contain
w
and
v
, respectively, will be called
dominant
components. A connected component
L\subseteq\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(w)
such that
L\subseteq D_{w}
or the other way around will be called
subordinate
, and a subgraph
L\subseteq\Gamma
that is a connected component of both
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(v)
and
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(w)
will be called a
shared
component. Notice that all these notions are relative to the pair
(v,w)
, and it can be shown that any connected component of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(v)
or
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(w)
falls into one of these three possibilities. Following
[
5
]
, we say that
v
and
w
form a SIL-pair (for
separating intersection of links
) if
v
and
w
have at least one shared component.

A presentation of
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
is as follows. It is the group generated by all possible partial conjugations
c_{L}^{v}
, and the following defining relations:

(1)

[c_{L}^{v},c_{K}^{w}]=1
if
v\in{\rm st}(w)
, or one of the components
L
or
K
is subordinate, or
L
and
K
are distinct shared components,

(2)

[c_{L}^{v},c_{L}^{w}c_{K}^{w}]=1
when
L
is shared and
K
is dominant.

For more details, see
[
24
, Chapter 3]
.

The special case of
\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
, where
F_{n}
is free of rank
n
, is also known as the McCool group, the group of welded pure braids or the group of basis-conjugating automorphisms. If
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is a free basis of
F_{n}
, we denote for simplicity by
c_{ij}
the partial conjugation
c_{\{x_{i}\}}^{x_{j}}
. So
\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
is generated by all
c_{ij}
, with
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
and has defining relators

[c_{ij},c_{kj}],\ \ [c_{ij},c_{kl}]\ \ \text{ and }\ \ [c_{ij},c_{jk}c_{ik}]

for
i,j,k,l
all distinct.

Some recent results give evidence that
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
is in some sense close to a RAAG if the defining graph
\Gamma
is not too complicated. For instance, Koban and Piggott showed that
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
is isomorphic to a RAAG if and only if
\Gamma
has no SIL-pairs
[
14
]
. On a second level, Day and Wade gave a more general combinatorial condition on
\Gamma
which determines whether the image
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
of
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
in the group
{\rm Out}(A_{\Gamma})
of outer automorphisms is a RAAG or not; see
[
9
, Theorem B]
.

RAAGs have been analyzed frequently (see e.g.
[
11
]
) through the
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie algebra

\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(A_{\Gamma})=(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}(A_{\Gamma})/\gamma_{n+1}(A_{\Gamma}))\otimes k

built from its lower central series and tensored with a field
k
of characteristic
0
.
It turns out that these Lie algebras have the same commutator-relator presentations coming from the combinatorial structure of the graph as the RAAGs themselves, that is, they are the “right-angled Artin Lie algebras” associated with the graphs.

An important result in this perspective is the one by Froberg
[
13
]
showing that such Lie algebras are
Koszul
: there is a linear graded projective resolution

\cdots\to P_{n}\to P_{n-1}\to\cdots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to k\to 0

of the trivial
U(\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(A_{\Gamma}))
-module
k
. Linear here means that the graded module
P_{i}
is generated by its component of degree
i
. This is a classical niceness homological condition for the enveloping algebra
U(\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(A_{\Gamma}))
.

Our first goal in this paper is to analyze the Koszul property for the
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie algebras associated with
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
. Apart from the cases when
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
is already a RAAG, so that their algebras are Koszul by Froberg’s theorem, the first step comes from the result of Conner and Goetz
[
8
]
:
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n}))
is Koszul if and only if
n\leq 3
. This can be turned into a more general obstruction for Koszulness of
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
by means of following combinatorial condition on the graph
\Gamma
:

(*)

\Gamma
does not contain four pairwise non-adjacent vertices
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\in\Gamma
that lie in four distinct connected components of
\Gamma\smallsetminus\cap_{i=1}^{4}{\rm lk}(v_{i})

Here
{\rm lk}(v_{i})
denotes the link of the vertex
v_{i}
in
\Gamma
. We prove:

Theorem A
.

The graded Lie algebra
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
is Koszul if and only if
\Gamma
satisfies condition (*).

To prove the theorem above we need to work with a concrete presentation of
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
. The key to obtain it is the concept of
formality
. Recall that a group
G
is
1
-formal
if its Malcev completion is a quadratically presented, complete Lie algebra.

Theorem B
.

For any finite graph
\Gamma
, the groups
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
and
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
are
1
-formal. As a consequence,
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
has a presentation where the generators correspond to the partial conjugations
c_{L}^{v}
, and the defining relators are

(1)

[c_{L}^{v},c_{K}^{w}]=0
if
v\in{\rm st}(w)
, or one of the components
L
or
K
is subordinate, or both
L
and
K
are shared but distinct, and

(2)

[c_{L}^{v},c_{L}^{w}+c_{K}^{w}]=0
when
L
is shared and
K
is dominant.

Moreover, a presentation of
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))
is obtained from the above by including the relations
\sum_{L}c_{L}^{v}
, where the sum runs through all connected components
L
of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(v)
, for each
v\in\Gamma
.

As we only need to apply
1
-formality to obtain the Lie algebra presentations, we will not need to enter into the details of the theory. A short sketch of what we need is in Section
2
; for more details see e.g.
[
23
]
.

We note that a version of this result for the McCool groups is already considered in
[
1
]
, and some related results about relations holding in the Lie algebra associated with
\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
were considered in
[
6
]
too.

The argument for Theorem
A
depends also on a certain decomposition of the given Lie algebras as iterated extensions of right-angled Artin Lie algebras. Such decomposition actually occurs already on the level of groups, which leads to the second main goal of this article.

Day and Wade developed in
[
10
]
a theory of relative automorphisms of RAAGs. They consider certain subgroups
{\rm Out}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})\leq{\rm Out}(A_{\Gamma})
determined by outer automorphisms that preserve (resp. act trivially on) subgroups in a given family
\mathscr{G}
(resp.
\mathscr{H}
) of special subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
. Roughly, they prove that a certain finite-index subgroup
{\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma})
of
{\rm Out}(A_{\Gamma})
admits a subnormal series in which the successive quotients are the “irreducible” relative automorphism groups, which turn out to be either free abelian, isomorphic to some
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, or a Fouxe-Rabinovitch group.

We show that these decompositions, applied to our situation of pure symmetric automorphisms and under the hypothesis that condition (*) holds, return subnormal series with factors being always RAAGs (in this paper, RAAG means
finitely generated
RAAG). Below we denote

\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})\coloneqq{\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G}\cup\mathscr{C},(\mathscr{H}\cup\mathscr{C})^{t}),

where
\mathscr{C}=\{\langle v\rangle\mid v\in\Gamma\}
is the family of cyclic special subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
.

Theorem C
.

Let
\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{H}
be sets of special subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
. Then there is a subnormal series

1=N_{0}\trianglelefteq N_{1}\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq N_{t}=\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})

such that each factor
N_{i}/N_{i-1}
is either free abelian or a Fouxe-Rabinovitch group. If the graph
\Gamma
has property (*), then these Fouxe-Rabinovitch groups are extensions of RAAGs, so
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
is poly-RAAG.

Notice that in particular, by taking
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{C}
, we obtain a subnormal series for
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
with RAAG factors, as soon as (*) holds. Moreover, since
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})=\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})/{\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
and
{\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
is a RAAG, we have the following Corollary:

Theorem D
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph with property (*). Then both
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
and
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
are poly (finitely generated) RAAGs.

There has been a lot of interest recently about groups which admit (sub)normal series with
free
factors. For instance all RAAGs admit such series: they are
polyfree
in the sense that they admit subnormal series with (not necessarily finitely generated) free groups as factors
[
11
]
. Notice that Theorem
C
implies that
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
and
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
also admit such polyfree series.

Another important aspect of Theorem
C
is that
N_{i+1}
acts trivially on
N_{i}/N_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i
. Using this, we apply a theorem of Falk and Randell
[
12
]
to justify that we get a similar decomposition of the associated graded Lie algebras, which is used to prove Theorem
A
.

The following remains unanswered:

Question
.

If
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
admits a subnormal series with (finitely generated) RAAGs as factors, does
\Gamma
satisfy (*)?

As a partial result, we prove the following:

Theorem E
.

If
n\geq 4
, then
\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
and
\operatorname{POut}(F_{n})
are not poly-finitely generated free, that is, they do not admit subnormal series with finitely generated free factors.

This is more treatable than the general case because we can at the same time work cleanly with the cohomological dimension and the Euler characteristics of
\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
and of free groups, in contrast with arbitrary
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
and arbitrary RAAGs, respectively.

2.
Formality and the associated graded Lie algebra

In this section we introduce and study the formality properties for
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
and
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
, following mainly Berceanu and Papadima
[
1
]
and Papadima and Suciu
[
21
]
. This will lead to the proof of Theorem
B
.

Let
k
be field of characteristic
0
. A
Malcev Lie

k
-
algebra
is a Lie algebra
E
over
k
endowed with a complete descending filtration by subspaces
\{F^{r}(E)\}_{r}
such that

(1)

F^{1}(E)=E
;

(2)

[F^{r}(E),F^{s}(E)]\subseteq F^{r+s}(E)
for all
r,s
;

(3)

The associated graded Lie algebra
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(E)=\oplus_{r\geq 1}F^{r}(E)/F^{r+1}(E)
is generated in degree
1
.

Here
complete
means that the natural map
E\to\varprojlim E/F^{r}(E)
is an isomorphism.

For each Malcev Lie algebra
E
there is an associated exponential group
\exp(E)
. It is
the group defined on the underlying set of
E
with product given by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula. These two points of view are connected by the
exponential power series and the logarithm, which are bijections between
E
and
\exp(E)
. Furthermore, the filtration of
E
induces a group filtration in
\exp(E)
, and it can be shown that
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(E)\cong\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\exp(E))
. Notice that here we use
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(-)
both as the graded algebra coming from a filtered Lie algebra and for the usual
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(G)
coming from the lower central series of a group.

A
Malcev completion
for a group
G
is a homomorphism
\sigma\colon G\to\exp(E)
, for some Malcev Lie algebra
E
, such that
the induced homomorphism on the graded Lie algebras
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\sigma)\colon\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(G)\to\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(E)
is an isomorphism. For a free group
F_{n}
with free basis
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
,
the homomorphism
\sigma_{n}\colon F_{n}\to\exp(E_{n})
, where
E_{n}
is the free complete Lie algebra with free basis
\bar{x}_{1},\ldots,\bar{x}_{n}
and

\sigma_{n}(x_{i})=e^{\bar{x}_{i}}
, is a Malcev completion.

Let
G=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\mid r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\rangle
be a finitely presented group. Let
E_{G}
be the complete Lie algebra generated by

\bar{x}_{1},\ldots,\bar{x}_{n}
and with defining relators
\log(\sigma_{n}(r_{j}))
, for
1\leq j\leq m
. By
[
19
, Thm. 2.2]
, the homomorphism
\sigma\colon G\to\exp(E_{G})
, induced by
\sigma_{n}
, is a Malcev completion.

We say that a group
G
is
1
-
formal
if
E_{G}
is quadratic, that is,
E_{G}
is the quotient of a free complete Lie algebra by a closed
ideal generated by homogeneous elements of degree
2
. This is equivalent with
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(G)
being quadratic and
E_{G}
being isomorphic as a filtered
algebra to the completion of its associated graded algebra (see
[
23
]
, for instance).

In order to show that
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
and
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
are
1
-formal, we prove the following generalization of
[
1
, Theorem 5.4]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group admitting a finite presentation with set of generators
X
and relators of the form
[x_{1}\cdots x_{m},y_{1}\cdots y_{n}]
, with

x_{i},y_{i}\in X
and
m,n\geq 1
. Suppose further that if
[x_{1}\cdots x_{m},y_{1}\cdots y_{n}]
is a relator, then so are
[x_{i},x_{j}]

and
[y_{i},y_{j}]
for all
i,j
occurring in the expression. Then
G
is
1
-formal.

Proof.

To avoid confusions, we will denote by
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
the group commutator on
\exp(E_{G})
, thus keeping the usual brackets for the Lie algebra
operation on
E_{G}
.

Let
F
be the free group with free basis
X
and
E
be the free complete Lie algebra with free basis
\bar{X}=\{\bar{x}\mid x\in X\}
. By the observations above,
E_{G}
can be taken as the quotient of
E
by the ideal generated by the elements
\log(\sigma(r))
, where
\sigma\colon F\to\exp(E)
is the Malcev completion of
F
and
r
runs through the
defining relators of
G
.

Consider first a relator of the form
r=[x,y]
, with
x,y\in X
. Then
\log(\sigma(r))=\log(\{e^{\bar{x}},e^{\bar{y}}\})\in E
. This is exactly the commutator of
\bar{x}
and
\bar{y}
when we see
\exp(E)
as a group on the underlying set of
E
. By
[
20
, Lemma 2.5]
, killing this commutator
in
E
amounts to the same as killing the bracket
[\bar{x},\bar{y}]
, now with usual operation of the Lie algebra
E
.

Now consider a relation of the form
r=[x_{1}\cdots x_{m},y_{1}\cdots y_{n}]
with
m>1
or
n>1
, and
x_{i},y_{j}\in X
. We have

\log(\sigma(r))=\log(\{e^{\bar{x}_{1}}\cdots e^{\bar{x}_{m}},e^{\bar{y}_{1}}\cdots e^{\bar{y}_{n}}\})
.
Again, killing
\log(\sigma(r))
in
E
is the same as killing the element
[\log(e^{\bar{x}_{1}}\cdots e^{\bar{x}_{m}}),\log(e^{\bar{y}_{1}}\cdots e^{\bar{y}_{n}})]
. Now, by hypothesis

\log(\sigma([\bar{x}_{i},\bar{x}_{j}]))
and
\log(\sigma([\bar{y}_{i},\bar{y}_{j}]))
are relators for the appropriate indices. By the previous paragraph we may actually assume that
[\bar{x}_{i},\bar{x}_{j}]
and
[\bar{y}_{i},\bar{y}_{j}]
are relators. For commuting elements
X,Y
the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula gives
e^{X}\cdot e^{Y}=e^{X+Y}
, so we have:

\log(e^{\bar{x}_{1}}\cdots e^{\bar{x}_{m}})=\log(e^{\bar{x}_{1}+\ldots+\bar{x}_{m}})=\bar{x}_{1}+\ldots+\bar{x}_{m},

and similarly
\log(e^{\bar{y}_{1}}\cdots e^{\bar{y}_{n}})=\bar{y}_{1}+\ldots+\bar{y}_{n}
. So
\log(\sigma(r))
is equivalent to the relator
[\bar{x}_{1}+\ldots+\bar{x}_{m},\bar{y}_{1}+\ldots+\bar{y}_{n}]
.

All types of relators of
E_{G}
are clearly homogeneous of degree
2
, so
G
is
1
-formal.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Under the same hypothesis of Theorem
2.1
, the graded Lie algebra
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(G)
admits a presentation with
X
as a generating set
and relators

[x_{1}+\ldots+x_{m},y_{1}+\ldots+y_{n}]

whenever
[x_{1}\cdots x_{m},y_{1}\cdots y_{n}]
is a relator of
G
.

Proof.

This follows from the defining property of Malcev completions, that is,
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(G)\cong\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(E_{G})
.
∎

The theorem and its corollary apply immediately to our situation.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

It is clear that the presentation of
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
. Now, for
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
, we obtain a presentation by adding one relator of the form
\prod_{L}c_{L}^{v}
for each
v\in\Gamma
that is not connected to every other vertex of
\Gamma
. The product runs trough all connected components of

\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(v)
. We may choose arbitrarily a connected component
K
of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(v)
for each

v\in\Gamma
, and then eliminate the generators
c_{K}^{v}
from the presentation by means of Tietze transformations. One easily sees now that the new presentation is equivalent to one containing only relators of the form
[\prod c_{L}^{v},\prod c_{N}^{w}]
, for some products
\prod c_{L}^{v}
and
\prod c_{N}^{w}
of (commuting) conjugations by the same vertex.
This satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
too. By including again the generators
c_{K}^{v}
, we arrive at the presentation we wanted for
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))
.
∎

3.
Graded Lie algebras and Koszulness

We consider here (Lie) algebras over
k
, a field of characteristic
0
. We say that a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded if there is a vector space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
satisfying
[\mathfrak{g}_{n},\mathfrak{g}_{m}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{n+m}
. A graded
\mathfrak{g}
-module is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module with a decomposition
A=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{n}
satisfying
\mathfrak{g}_{n}\cdot A_{m}\subseteq A_{m+n}
.

The universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
of an
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie algebra admits a natural
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-grading. For any
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie algebra we may consider the trivial module
k
as a graded module with
k=k_{0}
. It makes sense then to consider the bigraded module

\{{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}^{p,q}(k,k)\}_{p,q}

where
p
is the cohomological degree and
q
is the internal degree, inherited from
\mathfrak{g}
. We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is
Koszul
if
\mathfrak{g}
is finitely generated by elements of degree one and and
\{{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}^{p,q}(k,k)\}
is
diagonal
, that is,
{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}^{p,q}(k,k)=0
whenever
p\neq q
. This is equivalent with saying that
U(\mathfrak{g})
is Koszul in the sense of associative quadratic algebras (see e.g.
[
22
]
).

Recall that an ideal
\mathfrak{h}
of a graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
is
homogeneous
if
\mathfrak{h}=\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{n}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a graded Lie algebra and let
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a homogeneous ideal.

(1)

If the quotient map
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
splits and
\mathfrak{g}
is Koszul, then
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
is Koszul.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
are Koszul, then so is
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

For item (1), assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is Koszul. So
\mathfrak{g}
is finitely generated, which implies that
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
is finitely generated too. Now, let
\sigma\colon\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a splitting to the quotient map
\pi\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
. Then the induced graded maps

\sigma^{\ast}\colon{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}^{\bullet,\bullet}(k,k)\to{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})}^{\bullet,\bullet}(k,k)\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \pi^{\ast}\colon{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})}^{\bullet,\bullet}(k,k)\to{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}^{\bullet,\bullet}(k,k)

preserve degree and satisfy
\sigma^{\ast}\circ\pi^{\ast}=\mathrm{id}
. In particular,
{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})}^{\bullet,\bullet}(k,k)
is a quotient of the diagonal bigraded module
{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}^{\bullet,\bullet}(k,k)
, so it is diagonal too. This proves (1).

Next, we consider item (2). Clearly
\mathfrak{g}
is finitely generated if both
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
are. Assume that both
{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{h})}^{\bullet,\bullet}(k,k)
and
{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})}^{\bullet,\bullet}(k,k)
are diagonal. The associated graded LHS sequence is of the form

E_{2}^{\{p,q\},\bullet}={\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})}^{p,\bullet}(k,{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{h})}^{q,\bullet}(k,k))\implies{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}^{p+q,\bullet}(k,k),

where the internal degree
\bullet
is preserved. Notice that the action of
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
increases
the internal degree, but preserves the cohomological one. Since
{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{h})}^{\bullet,\bullet}(k,k)
is diagonal, we find that the action is trivial, so

E_{2}^{\{p,q\},\bullet}\cong{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})}^{p,\bullet}(k,k)\otimes{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{h})}^{q,\bullet}(k,k)\cong{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})}^{p,p}(k,k)\otimes{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{h})}^{q,q}(k,k).

In particular,
E_{2}^{\{p,q\},n}=0
whenever
n\neq p+q
. From the convergence of the sequence we deduce that
{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}^{m,n}(k,k)=0
whenever
m\neq n
, that is,
{\rm Ext}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}^{\bullet,\bullet}(k,k)
is diagonal.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finitely generated graded Lie algebra with a series of subalgebras

0=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\triangleleft\mathfrak{g}_{1}\triangleleft\ldots\triangleleft\mathfrak{g}_{s-1}\triangleleft\mathfrak{g}_{s}=\mathfrak{g}

such that each
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is an homogeneous ideal in
\mathfrak{g}_{i+1}
and the quotient
\mathfrak{g}_{i+1}/\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is Koszul for all
i
.
Then
\mathfrak{g}
is Koszul.

Proof.

By induction, using Part (2) of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Now, we specialize to the groups of pure symmetric automorphisms of RAAGs and their associated Lie algebras.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that a graph
\Gamma
contains
n
pairwise non-adjacent vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
such that each
v_{i}
lies in a distinct connected component of
\Gamma\smallsetminus\cap_{i=1}^{n}{\rm lk}(v_{i})
. Then:

(1)

\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
is a retract of
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
, and

(2)

\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n}))
is a split quotient of
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
.

Proof.

Denote by
L_{i}
the connected component of
\Gamma\smallsetminus\cap_{j=1}^{n}{\rm lk}(v_{j})
that contains
v_{i}
. Notice that

L_{i}
is also a connected component of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(v_{j})
for
i\neq j
. Indeed, if
L

is the connected component of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(v_{j})
containing
v_{i}
, then clearly
L\subseteq L_{i}
. On the other hand, if
L\subsetneq L_{i}
, then
L_{i}\cap{\rm st}(v_{j})\neq\emptyset
, which implies that
v_{j}\in L_{i}
by connectedness. This is a contradiction.

Let
\Phi\colon\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})\to\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
be the homomorphism that sends
c_{L_{i}}^{v_{j}}
to
c_{ij}
and sends all the other generators to
1
. Since the
c_{L_{i}}^{v_{j}}
and the
c_{ij}
satisfy the same set of relations, both
\Phi
and its right-inverse
\Psi\colon\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})\to\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
are well-defined. This proves part (1), and part (2) is completely analogous using the presentation given in Theorem
B
.
∎

The hypothesis we make in Lemma
3.3
for
n=4
is the negation of condition (*) considered in the Introduction. We restate it for convenience.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph. We say that
\Gamma
has (*) if
\Gamma
does not contain four pairwise non-adjacent vertices
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\in\Gamma
that lie in four distinct connected components of
\Gamma\smallsetminus\cap_{i=1}^{4}{\rm lk}(v_{i})

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that the graph
\Gamma
does not satisfy condition (*). Then

\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
is not Koszul.

Proof.

By applying Lemma
3.3
we find that
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(F_{4}))
is a split quotient of
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
. Since
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(F_{4}))

∎

This proves one direction of Theorem

. The other direction will come from a simultaneous analysis of the group and the Lie algebra by means of the subnormal series of Day and Wade.

4.
An obstruction to being poly-finitely generated free

For the proof of Theorem
E
, we will need to recall some facts about the homological properties of the groups
\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
.

Recall that the
cohomological dimension

{\rm cd}(G)
of a group is the minimal length of a projective resolution of the trivial
\mathbb{Z}G
-module
\mathbb{Z}
. One may similarly consider the cohomological dimension
{\rm cd}_{R}(G)
over a commutative ring
R
. If
\mathbb{Z}
admits a finite-length resolution by finitely generated projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules, we say that
G
is of
type
\operatorname{FP}
. Groups satisfying this property have a well-defined
Euler characteristic
given by the alternating sum
\chi(G)=\sum(-1)^{i}{\rm rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{i}(G,\mathbb{Z})
; see
[
4
]
.

D.J. Collins
[
7
]
proved that the McCool group
\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
is of type
\operatorname{FP}
and has cohomological dimension
n-1
. Its Euler characteristic was then computed by J. McCammond and J. Meier
[
16
, Thm. 1.2]
:

(4.1)

\chi(\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n+1}))=(-1)^{n}n^{n}.

Below we denote by
\mathfrak{X}_{j}=\{c_{i,j}\mid\forall 1\leq i\leq n,\ \ i\neq j\}
the subset of standard generators of
\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
that act with the same generator
x_{j}
of
F_{n}
. Notice that
\langle\mathfrak{X}_{j}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
. In particular, any subgroup of
\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
containing some
\mathfrak{X}_{j}
has cohomological dimension
n-1
.

In the proof below we will use the BNS-invariant
\Sigma^{1}(G)
of a finitely generated group
[
3
]
. It is a set of homomorphisms
\varphi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
that contains the information of whether subgroups
N\leq G
with
G^{\prime}\leq N
are finitely generated or not. We will need to use the basic fact that an element
\varphi\in\Sigma^{1}(G)
induces
\bar{\varphi}\in\Sigma^{1}(G/N)
if
\varphi(N)=0
. Moreover, we will use that
\Sigma^{1}(F)=\emptyset
if
F
is free and non-cyclic.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\pi\colon\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})\to F
be a homomorphism onto a free group, where
n\geq 4
. If at least
4
of the
\mathfrak{X}_{j}
survive under
\pi
, then
F
is cyclic.

Proof.

In what follows, we will use repeatedly the well-known fact that any set of commuting elements in a free group generates a cyclic subgroup.

Suppose for a contradiction that
F
is non-cyclic. Fix an embedding
\eta\colon F^{ab}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and let
\varphi\colon\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})\to\mathbb{R}
be the composition
\varphi=\eta\circ\mathrm{ab}\circ\pi
, where
\mathrm{ab}\colon F\to F^{ab}
is the abelianization map.

Since
\Sigma^{1}(F)=\emptyset
, we find that
\varphi\notin\Sigma^{1}(\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n}))
. By the description of
\Sigma^{1}(\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n}))
in
[
18
]
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\varphi(c_{21})\neq 0
and that
\varphi
kills all
c_{ij}
except possibly when
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
. Notice that since
c_{21}
commutes with
c_{i1}
for all
i
, we find that
\pi(c_{21})
and
\pi(c_{i1})
are powers of a common element, and it can only be the case that
\pi(c_{i1})=1
if
i>3
.

By hypothesis there is some
c_{ij}
, with
j\notin\{1,2,3\}
that is not killed by
\pi
.
We will find a contradiction by showing that
\pi(c_{ij})
commutes with
\pi(c_{21})
, which means that they are powers of a common element in
F
, so in particular
\varphi(c_{ij})=0
if and only if
\varphi(c_{21})=0
.

If
i\neq 1,2
, then
c_{ij}
and
c_{21}
already commute. If
i=2
, since we have a defining relation
[c_{2j},c_{21}c_{j1}]=1
and
\pi(c_{j1})=1
, we find that
\pi(c_{2j})
commutes with
\pi(c_{21})
. On the other hand, if
\pi(c_{2j})=1
but
\pi(c_{1j})\neq 1
, then the defining relation
[c_{21},c_{1j}c_{2j}]=1
determines similarly that
\pi(c_{21})
commutes with
\pi(c_{1j})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
E
.

Clearly it is enough to show that
G=\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n})
does not contain a proper normal subgroup
N
such that i)
N
is of type
\operatorname{FP}
and ii)
G/N
is free.

Suppose for a contradiction that such
N
exists. By Feldman’s theorem
[
2
, Thm. 5.5]
we have

{\rm cd}_{\mathbb{Q}}(N)<{\rm cd}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)={\rm cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G)=n-1

where the equalities hold because
G
contains a copy of
\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
. In particular
N
does not contain any
\mathfrak{X}_{j}
.

By Lemma
4.1
, the quotient
G/N
must be infinite cyclic. But then the product formula for the Euler characteristics
[
4
, IX-7.3]
gives

\chi(\operatorname{PAut}(F_{n}))=\chi(N)\cdot\chi(G/N)=\chi(N)\cdot\chi(\mathbb{Z})=0,

which contradicts (
4.1
).
∎

5.
Relative automorphisms of RAAGs

In this section we mainly collect notation and results from Day and Wade
[
10
]
about relative automorphisms of RAAGs. This will be the framework for the rest of the paper, leading up to the proofs of Theorem
C
and the remaining part of Theorem
A
.

Let
G
be a group,
H\leq G
a subgroup and
\Phi\in{\rm Out}(G)
. We say that
\Phi

preserves

H
if there exists a representative
\phi\in\Phi
such that
\phi(H)=H
and we say that
\Phi

acts trivially
on
H
if there exists a representative
\phi\in\Phi
such that
\phi
is the identity on
H
. We use the same terminology for elements
\phi\in{\rm Aut}(G)
, saying that
H
is preserved by
\phi
if it is preserved by
\phi{\rm Inn}(G)\in{\rm Out}(G)
and that
\phi
acts trivially on
H
if its coset in
{\rm Out}(G)
does.

Now, let
G=A_{\Gamma}
be a right angled Artin group and let
\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{H}
be families of special subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
(i.e., subgroups generated by subgraphs of
\Gamma
). Let
{\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma})
be the subgroup of
{\rm Out}(A_{\Gamma})
generated by the cosets of inversions, partial conjugations and transvections (see
[
10
]
for definitions). Day and Wade define
{\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
as the subgroup of those
\Phi\in{\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma})
which preserve all the subgroups in
\mathscr{G}
and act trivially on all the subgroups in
\mathscr{H}
.

If
\Phi\in\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
, then for any
v\in\Gamma
and any
\phi\in\Phi
we have
v^{\phi}=v^{g_{v}}
for some
g_{v}\in A_{\Gamma}
so if
\phi_{g_{v}}
is conjugation by
g_{v}
,
\alpha=\phi\phi_{g_{v}}^{-1}\in\Phi
and
v^{\alpha}=v
which implies that
\Phi
acts trivially on the cyclic group
\langle v\rangle
for any
v\in\Gamma
. Conversely, if
\Phi
acts trivially on
\langle v\rangle
for any
v\in\Gamma
, then for each
v\in\Gamma
we may choose
\alpha_{v}\in\Phi
with
v^{\alpha_{v}}=v
so for any
\phi\in\Phi
they differ by an inner automorphism, i.e., there is some
g_{v}\in A_{\Gamma}
such that
v^{\phi}=(v^{\alpha_{v}})^{g_{v}}=v^{g_{v}}
, in other words,
\Phi\in\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
. This means that if we set

\mathscr{C}=\{\langle v\rangle\mid v\in\Gamma\},

then

\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})={\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}^{t}).

From now on, we will use a variation of Day-Wade’s notation and set

\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})\coloneqq{\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G}\cup\mathscr{C},(\mathscr{H}\cup\mathscr{C})^{t}).

Following
[
10
]
, we say that the family
\mathscr{G}
is
saturated
with respect to
(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H})
if
\mathscr{G}
contains every proper special subgroup which is preserved by
{\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
. To avoid complications, unless otherwise stated, we will assume that this is always the case. Note that there is no loss of generality in doing that, since

{\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma},\overline{\mathscr{G}},\mathscr{H}^{t})={\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})

for
\overline{\mathscr{G}}
the set of proper special subgroups preserved by
{\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
.

We will also denote by
\overline{\mathscr{C}}
the set of all proper special subgroups preserved by
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
, in other words, the set of all proper special subgroups which are preserved by any partial conjugation.

Next, we state
[
10
, Theorem E]
with the notation established above. Let
\Delta\subseteq\Gamma
such that
A_{\Delta}\in\mathscr{G}
. Then there is a short exact sequence

1\to\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},(\mathscr{H}\cup\{A_{\Delta}\})^{t})\to\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})\to\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Delta},\mathscr{G}_{\Delta},\mathscr{H}_{\Delta}^{t})\to 1

where

\mathscr{G}_{\Delta}=\{A_{\Delta\cap\Theta}\mid A_{\theta}\in\mathscr{G}\}\smallsetminus\{A_{\Delta}\}

and
\mathscr{H}_{\Delta}^{t}
is defined analogously.

Day and Wade iterate this sequence to obtain a subnormal series for the groups
{\rm Out}^{0}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
whose factors are either free abelian, or linear groups
\mathrm{GL}(m,\mathbb{Z})
or
Fouxe-Rabinovitch
groups (defined below). We show in Section
6
that in the particular case when the group is
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
and the graph
\Gamma
satisfies the previously defined condition (*), Day-Wade’s procedure determines a subnormal series

1=N_{0}\trianglelefteq N_{1}\trianglelefteq\ldots\trianglelefteq N_{s}=\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})

whose factors
N_{i}/N_{i-1}
are all (finitely generated) RAAGs and moreover that each
N_{i}/N_{i-1}
acts trivially on the abelianization
N_{i-1}/N_{i-1}^{\prime}
of the next term.

Later, we will refine that series to get a polyfree series. The extra condition of being polyfree will allow us to deduce that the partial short exact sequences that we get split and then use
[
12
]
to prove that there is a similar structure at the level of the associated Lie algebras.

These constructions lift to a subnormal series of
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
with similar properties by means of the exact sequence

1\to{\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma})\to\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})\to\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})\to 1.

Note that
{\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
is isomorphic to a RAAG defined on the full subgraph
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma
of the vertices that are not adjacent to every vertex of
\Gamma
.

We will use the following result which follows from
[
10
, Theorem D]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The group
G=\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
is generated by the partial conjugations that it contains.

The following technical lemma will also be useful.

Lemma 5.2
.

(
[
10
, Lemma 2.2(3)]
)
Let
\Delta\subseteq\Gamma
,
v\in\Gamma
,
K\subseteq\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)
a union of connected components and
g
the partial conjugation that conjugates vertices in
K
by
v
and fixes the rest. Consider the following conditions

i)

K\cap\Delta=\emptyset
,

ii)

\Delta\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)\subseteq K
,

iii)

v\in\Delta
.

Then
g
preserves
A_{\Delta}
if and only if one of i), ii) or iii) happens and
g
acts trivially on
A_{\Delta}
if and only if one of i), ii) happens.

Using the results above, we deduce the following.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq\Gamma
be such that
\Delta\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)
is connected for any
v\in\Gamma\smallsetminus\Delta
. Then
A_{\Delta}
is preserved by any partial conjugation, i.e.
A_{\Delta}\in\overline{\mathscr{C}}
.

Proof.

Let
g
be a partial conjugation that conjugates by the vertex
v
all the vertices in
K\subseteq\Gamma
and assume
v\not\in\Delta
. We may assume that
K
is a union of connected components of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)
. As
\Delta\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)
is connected this means that either
\Delta\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)\subseteq K
or
K\cap\Delta=\emptyset
. So it is enough to use Lemma
5.2
.
∎

6.
A poly-RAAG series

We will now impose condition (*) on
\Gamma
and see that Day and Wade’s construction produces a subnormal series for
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
without factors of type
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
, which will prove Theorem
C
.

The following result is a consequence of
[
10
, Corollary 3.12]
but we include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Theta\subseteq\Delta\subseteq\Gamma
, and let
\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{H}
be families of proper special subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
. If
A_{\Delta}
is preserved by
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
and
A_{\Theta}
is preserved by
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Delta},\mathscr{G}_{\Delta},\mathscr{H}_{\Delta}^{t})
, then
A_{\Theta}
is also preserved by
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
.

Proof.

Let
g
be a representative for
\Phi\in\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
. As
A_{\Delta}
is preserved by
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
, there is some
x\in A_{\Gamma}
such that
(w^{g})^{x}\in A_{\Delta}
for any
w\in\Delta
, so
g\rho_{x}\in\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Delta},\mathscr{G}_{\Delta},\mathscr{H}_{\Delta}^{t})
where
\rho_{x}
is conjugation by
x
. As
A_{\Theta}
is preserved by
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Delta},\mathscr{G}_{\Delta},\mathscr{H}_{\Delta}^{t})
there is some
y\in A_{\Delta}\subseteq A_{\Gamma}
such that
(u^{g})^{xy}\in A_{\Theta}
for any
u\in\Theta
. ∎

We will need the following definition from
[
10
, 3.3.1]
.
Let
\mathscr{G}
be a family of proper special subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
. We say that two vertices
u,v\in\Gamma
are
\mathscr{G}
-adjacent
if either they are adjacent in
\Gamma
or there is some
A_{\Delta}\in\mathscr{G}
such that
u,v\in\Delta
. A
\mathscr{G}
-path
is a finite sequence of vertices such that each vertex in the sequence is
\mathscr{G}
-adjacent to the next and a full subgraph of
\Delta\subseteq\Gamma
is
\mathscr{G}
-connected
if for any two vertices
u,v\in\Delta
there is a
\mathscr{G}
-path from
u
to
v
. Maximal
\mathscr{G}
-connected subgraphs of a subgraph
\Delta
are called
\mathscr{G}
-connected components of
\Delta
.

Recall that we are denoting by
\overline{\mathscr{C}}
the set of special subgroups which are preserved by
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
. For a subgraph
\Delta\subseteq\Gamma
, we denote

\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{\Delta}=\{A_{\Delta\cap\Theta}\mid A_{\Theta}\in\overline{\mathscr{C}},\Delta\not\subseteq\Theta\}.

Condition (*) does not pass to subgraphs, not even to preserved subgraphs, so we need a version of this that behaves well throughout the inductive process.

Lemma 6.2
.

If
\Gamma
is a graph that satisfies (*) and
A_{\Delta}\subseteq A_{\Gamma}
is preserved by
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
, then
\Delta
has at most 3
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{\Delta}
-connected components.

Proof.

Assume, for a contradiction, that there are
v_{1},v_{2},v_{2},v_{4}\in\Delta
lying in different
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{\Delta}
-connected components. In particular this implies that the
v_{j}
are pairwise non-adjacent. Applying condition (*) we see that at least two of the vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{4}
are connected in
\Gamma\smallsetminus\cap_{i=1}^{4}{\rm lk}(v_{i})
. Let
p
be a path of minimal length in
\Gamma\smallsetminus\cap_{i=1}^{4}{\rm lk}(v_{i})
joining two of the
v_{i}
, say
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
. In particular neither
v_{3}
nor
v_{4}
are in
p
. Denote by
\Omega
the subgraph induced by the following vertices

\cup\{{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(u)\mid u\in p,u\neq v_{1},v_{2}\}\cup\{v_{1},v_{2}\}.

Note that for every pair of vertices
u,v\in\Omega
there is a path joining them lying entirely in
p
(except of possibly the initial and terminal vertices
u
and
v
themselves).
Let
w\in\Gamma\smallsetminus\Omega
. Then
p\cap{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(w)=\emptyset
so we deduce that
\Omega\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(w)
is connected. Therefore Lemma
5.3
implies that
A_{\Omega}
is preserved by
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
, i.e.,
A_{\Omega}\in\overline{\mathscr{C}}
. Then either
A_{\Omega\cap\Delta}\in\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{\Delta}
or
\Delta\subseteq\Omega
. In the first case we deduce that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are in the same
\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{\Delta}
-connected component so we get the result. So we assume
\Delta\subseteq\Omega
and as
v_{3},v_{4}\in\Delta
, they both lie in
\Omega
(but not in
p
). Therefore we may choose
z\in p
such that
z
is linked to, say
v_{3}
and then the minimality of
p
implies that
z
is also linked to
v_{1}
or
v_{2}
. But then as there is a path from
v_{3}
of length 2, we deduce that also
p
has length 2 so
z
is indeed linked to the four elements
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}
which contradicts the fact that
p
is contained in
\Gamma\smallsetminus\cap_{i=1}^{4}{\rm lk}(v_{i})
.
∎

Consider a group
S
which decomposes as a free product

S=G_{1}\star\cdots\star G_{m}

The
Outer Fouxe-Rabinovitch
group of
S
is the subgroup of
{\rm Out}(S)
consisting on elements having, for each
i
, a representative acting as conjugation on
G_{i}
. In particular, if
S=A_{\Gamma}
and
\Gamma
is the disjoint union of
\Delta_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, then the Outer Fouxe-Rabinovitch group of
A_{\Gamma}
is

\mathrm{OFR}(A_{\Gamma})=\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{H},\mathscr{H}^{t}).

for
\mathscr{H}=\{A_{\Delta_{1}},\ldots,A_{\Delta_{m}}\}
.

Coming back to the general case
S=G_{1}\star\cdots\star G_{m}
, if we set

\mathrm{FR}(S)=\{\phi\in{\rm Aut}(S)\mid\phi|_{G_{i}}\text{ is conjugation with some }x_{i}\in S,\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,m\},

then
\mathrm{OFR}(S)=\mathrm{FR}(S)/{\rm Inn}(S)
. We will call
\mathrm{FR}(S)
the
Fouxe-Rabinovitch group
associated to the decomposition
S=G_{1}\star\cdots\star G_{m}
.

Remark 6.3
.

In
[
10
]
there is a more general definition of Fouxe-Rabinovitch used for groups with a decomposition
G_{1}\star\cdots\star G_{m}\star\mathrm{Free}(k)
but we will just need the restricted version above.

Using the obvious projection
S=G_{1}\star\cdots\star G_{m}\to G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{m}
one sees that there is a homomorphism
\mathrm{FR}(S)\to{\rm Aut}(G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{m})
. Let
N
be its kernel.
Consider also the subgroup

H=\{\varphi\in\mathrm{FR}(S)\mid G_{i}^{\varphi}=G_{i},i=1,\ldots,m\},

observe that
N\cap H=1
so we may see
H
as a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{m})
, indeed

H\cong{\rm Inn}(G_{1})\times\cdots\times{\rm Inn}(G_{m}).

A
Whitehead automorphism
of
S=G_{1}\star\cdots\star G_{m}
is a partial conjugation of the following form: fix
j
with
1\leq j\leq m
and let
X_{j}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}\subseteq G_{j}
. Then for each
g\in G_{i}
,
\varphi_{X_{j}}
maps
g
onto
g^{x_{i}}
. Note that in the case when
x_{j}=1
, then
\varphi_{X_{j}}\in N
, and in the case when all
x_{i}=1
for
i\neq j
,
\varphi_{X_{j}}\in H
. The following Lemma follows from the previous observations together with
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 6.4
.

If
S=G_{1}\star\cdots\star G_{m}
, any element in the associated Fouxe-Rabinovitch group
\mathrm{FR}(S)
can be written as
\varphi\psi
where
\varphi
is a product of Whitehead automorphisms and
\psi\in H
.

This has the following consequence:

Lemma 6.5
.

(
[
17
, Section 2.3]
) The outer Fouxe-Rabinovitch group of a free product of 2 components is

\mathrm{OFR}(G_{1}\star G_{2})={\rm Inn}(G_{1})\times{\rm Inn}(G_{2})

and for 3 components we have

\mathrm{OFR}(G_{1}\star G_{2}\star G_{3})=Z\rtimes H

with
Z=G_{1}\star G_{2}\star G_{3}
and
H={\rm Inn}(G_{1})\times{\rm Inn}(G_{2})\times{\rm Inn}(G_{3})
. Moreover,
H
acts trivially on the abelianization
Z/Z^{\prime}
.

Proof.

This is in
[
17
, Section 2.3]
but we recall here part of the argument. Let
m
be 2 or 3. Let
K={\rm Inn}(S)
so that
\mathrm{OFR}(S)=\mathrm{FR}(S)/K
and observe that
H\cap K=1
.

Consider first the case when
m=2
, i.e.
S=G_{1}\star G_{2}
. Then for any Whitehead automorphism
\varphi_{X_{1}}
with
X_{1}=\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\subseteq G_{1}
we have

\varphi_{X_{1}}=\rho\varphi_{Y_{1}}

where
\rho\in K
is conjugation by
x_{2}
and
Y_{1}=\{x_{2}^{-1}x_{1},1\}
. Then
\varphi_{Y_{1}}\in H
thus
\varphi_{X_{1}}\in KH
. By symmetry we deduce the same for Whitehead automorphisms
\varphi_{X_{2}}
with
X_{2}\subseteq G_{2}
. So we deduce
\mathrm{FR}(G_{1}\star G_{2})=HK
thus

\mathrm{OFR}(G_{1}\star G_{2})=HK/K\cong H\cong{\rm Inn}(G_{1})\times{\rm Inn}(G_{2}).

In
[
17
, Section 2]
there is a construction of a simplicial complex on which the group
\mathrm{OFR}(S)
acts. In the case when
m=3
this complex is a tree that yields the following graph of groups.

\bullet
H
\bullet
G_{1}\rtimes H
\bullet
G_{2}\rtimes H
\bullet
G_{3}\rtimes H

For
i=1
the vertex group labelled
G_{1}\rtimes H
is the homomorphic image in
\mathrm{OFR}(S)
of the group generated by
K
,
H
and Whitehead automorphisms of the form
\varphi_{\{(1,x,1)\}}
,
x\in G_{1}
. It is easy to check that this group is indeed isomorphic to the semidirect product
G_{1}\rtimes H
. The vertex groups
G_{2}\rtimes H
and
G_{3}\rtimes H
are defined analogously. Using this graph we get the description of
\mathrm{OFR}(S)
in the statement.
The action of
H
on
Z
is by conjugation on the corresponding component, for example, if
a,x\in G_{1}
:

\varphi^{-1}_{\{(a,1,1)\}}\varphi_{\{(1,x,1)\}}\varphi_{\{(a,1,1)\}}=\varphi_{\{(1,x^{a},1)\}}

and if
b\in G_{2}
,
c\in G_{3}
we have

\varphi^{-1}_{\{(1,b,1)\}}\varphi_{\{(1,x,1)\}}\varphi_{\{(1,b,1)\}}=\varphi_{\{(1,x,1)\}},

\varphi^{-1}_{\{(1,1,c)\}}\varphi_{\{(1,x,1)\}}\varphi_{\{(1,1,c)\}}=\varphi_{\{(1,x,1)\}}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
C
.

We follow the proof of Theorem 5.9 in
[
10
]
and argue by induction on the pairs
(n,m)
ordered lexicographically where
n=|\Gamma|
and
m=2^{n}-r
for
r
the number of special subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
on which
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
acts trivially. As Day-Wade do, we call this pair
(n,m)
the
complexity
of
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
.
Then we may distinguish two possible cases:

Case 1:
There is some
A_{\Delta}\in\mathscr{G}
such that
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
does not act trivially on
A_{\Delta}
. Then we have the short exact sequence

1\to\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},(\mathscr{H}\cup\{A_{\Delta}\})^{t})\to\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})\to\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Delta},\mathscr{G}_{\Delta},\mathscr{H}_{\Delta}^{t})\to 1.

Both
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},(\mathscr{H}\cup\{A_{\Delta}\})^{t})
and
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Delta},\mathscr{G}_{\Delta},\mathscr{H}_{\Delta}^{t})
have strictly smaller complexity than
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
so by induction we get series for both which combined give a series for
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
.

Case 2:
There is no such a
\Delta
which means that

G=\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})=\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{G}^{t}).

Recall that we are assuming that
\mathscr{G}
is saturated. There are five subcases to consider, each described in
[
10
, Section 5]
. In the sequence we follow their considerations (and subsection numbering), adapting to our case of pure symmetric automorphisms and imposing condition (*).

Case 5.1.1:

\Gamma
is
\mathscr{G}
-disconnected. Here
G
is a Fouxe-Rabinovitch group. More precisely,
if we denote by
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{t}
the
\mathscr{G}
-components of
\Gamma
then
\Gamma
is the disjoint union of
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{t}
and therefore

A_{\Gamma}=A_{\Delta_{1}}\star\cdots\star A_{\Delta_{t}}.

Note that in the decomposition in
[
10
, Subsection 5.1.1]
there is also a free factor consisting of vertices that do not lie in any group in
\mathscr{G}
, but in our case
\mathscr{G}
contains the cyclic subgroups generated by any vertex, so that is never the case. In the case when the ambient graph has (*), we can not assume that our
\Gamma
has also (*) because we are arguing by induction and (*) does not pass to subgraphs, but as by Lemma
6.1
all the subgraphs that we are considering are
\operatorname{POut}
-invariant, Lemma
6.2
implies that we can assume that
\Gamma
has at most three
\mathscr{G}
-connected components, i.e.
2\leq t\leq 3
. Lemma
6.5
implies that

•

either
t=2
and
G
is the product of the inner automorphisms groups of
A_{\Delta_{1}}
and
A_{\Delta_{2}}
which are both RAAGs,

•

or
t=3
and
G
is a semidirect product of two RAAGs
Z\rtimes H
.

Case 5.1.2:

\Gamma
disconnected and
\mathscr{G}
-connected. In this case
G
is free abelian, so we are done.

Case 5.1.3:

\Gamma
connected and
Z(A_{\Gamma})=1
. Again
G
is free abelian.

Case 5.1.4:

\Gamma
connected and
1\neq Z(A_{\Gamma})\subsetneq\Gamma
. In this case, let
Z\subseteq\Gamma
be such that
A_{Z}=Z(A_{\Gamma})
and
\Delta=\Gamma\smallsetminus Z
,
[
10
, Proposition 5.6]
implies that there is a surjection

G\to\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Delta},\mathscr{G}_{\Delta}\mathscr{G}^{t}_{\Delta})

with free abelian kernel. Moreover, according to the proof of that result, the kernel is generated by transvections which means that in our case the kernel is trivial so the surjection is an isomorphism. As the complexity of
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Delta},\mathscr{G}^{t}_{\Delta})
is strictly smaller than that of
G
, we can argue by induction.

Case 5.1.5:

\Gamma
is a complete graph. In this case
A_{\Gamma}
is free abelian, so that the associated automorphism group has no partial conjugations, i.e.
G=1
.
∎

7.
A presentation for the groups in the Day-Wade series

The objective of this section is to obtain a presentation for the factor groups in the Day-Wade series, both for the
\operatorname{PAut}
and
\operatorname{POut}
versions. This presentation is similar to the standard presentation for
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
computed by Toinet
[
24
]
.

To describe these presentations, we extend previously used notation to include partial conjugations of the form
c_{A}^{v}\in\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
where
A
is a union of connected components of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)
. In this case we say that
c_{A}^{v}
is a partial conjugation by
v
and based at
A
.
Note that if
A
and
B
are disjoint, then
c_{A}^{v}c_{B}^{v}=c_{A\cup B}^{v}
.

The proof of following lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
v,w\in\Gamma
be not linked and let
c^{v}_{A}
,
c^{w}_{B}
be partial conjugations. Then

[c_{A}^{v},c_{B}^{w}]=1
if and only if one of the following condition holds

i)

A\cap B=\emptyset
,
v\not\in B
and
w\not\in A
,

ii)

A\subseteq B
and
v\in B
,

iii)

B\subseteq A
and
w\in A
.

Another extension of notation is needed. Assume that we are given, for each
v\in\Gamma
, a partition
\Omega_{G}^{v}
of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)
into
unions
of connected components. For each pair of non-adjacent vertices
v,w\in\Gamma
, the subsets
D_{w}^{v}\in\Omega_{G}^{v}
and
D_{v}^{w}\in\Omega_{G}^{w}
with
v\in D_{v}^{w}
and
w\in D_{w}^{v}
will be called
dominant
. Similarly, the elements
A\in\Omega_{G}^{v}
such that
A\subseteq D_{v}^{w}
, and
B\in\Omega_{G}^{w}
such that
B\subseteq D_{w}^{v}
will be called
subordinate
, and if
A
lies in both
\Omega_{v}^{G}
and
\Omega_{w}^{G}
, then we will say that
A
is
shared
. Again, these notions are all relative to the pair
(v,w)
, and here also depend on the partitions
\{\Omega_{G}^{v}\}_{v}
. Note that at this point we are not assuming that arbitrary elements of
\Omega_{G}^{w}
,
\Omega_{G}^{v}
can be only dominant, shared or subordinate, but this will play a role in Definition
7.2
below.

In view of Lemma
7.1
and this terminology, for
v,w\in\Gamma
we will denote by
R^{v,w}_{G}
the set of relators given by:

•

[c^{v}_{A},c^{w}_{B}]=1
if

(i)

either
v\in{\rm st}(w)
, with
A\in\Omega_{G}^{v}
and
B\in\Omega_{G}^{w}
, or

(ii)

at least one of
A
,
B
is subordinate, or
A\neq B
are both shared, with
A\in\Omega_{G}^{v}
and
B\in\Omega_{G}^{w}
.

•

[c_{A}^{v},c_{A}^{w}c_{D_{v}^{w}}^{w}]=1
and
[c_{A}^{v}c_{D_{w}^{v}}^{v},c_{A}^{w}]=1
if
v\notin{\rm st}(w)
,
A\in\Omega_{G}^{v}\cap\Omega_{G}^{v}
(that is,
A
is shared), and
D_{v}^{w}\in\Omega_{G}^{w}
and
D_{w}^{v}\in\Omega_{G}^{v}
are dominant.

We will use these relators to define the concept of
\operatorname{PAut}
-like groups
. These are certain subgroups
G\leq\operatorname{PAut}(\Gamma)
which admit presentations where generators are some partial conjugations in the extended sense and all relations come from Lemma
7.1
, in the same way as
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
itself.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
G\leq\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
be a subgroup. Suppose that for each
v\in\Gamma
there is a partition

\Omega_{G}^{v}
of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)
into unions of connected components such that

i)

the partial conjugations
c_{A}^{v}
, with
A\in\Omega_{G}^{v}
and
v\in\Gamma
, generate
G
,

ii)

for each pair of not linked vertices
v,w\in\Gamma
, the non-dominant elements of
\Omega_{G}^{v}
and
\Omega_{G}^{w}
are either subordinate or shared relative to
\{\Omega_{G}^{v}\}_{v}
.

We say that
G
is
\operatorname{PAut}
-like
if the sets
R_{G}^{v,w}
of relators yield a presentation, i.e., if

G=\langle\Omega^{v}_{G},v\in\Gamma\mid R^{v,w}_{G},v,w\in\Gamma\rangle.

In the situation above, we will call the partial conjugations
c_{A}^{v}
, with
A\in\Omega_{G}^{v}
, the
standard generators
for
G
and the elements of
R_{G}^{v,w}
the
standard relators
.

Remark 7.3
.

Note that by definition any
\operatorname{PAut}
-like group contains
{\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
.
We will say that a subgroup
\overline{G}\leq\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
is
\operatorname{POut}
-like
if there is a
\operatorname{PAut}
-like group
G\leq\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
such that
\overline{G}=G/{\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
. If
{\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma})\leq G\leq\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
, then
G
is
\operatorname{PAut}
-like if and only if
\overline{G}=G/{\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
is
\operatorname{POut}
-like.

In the rest of this section, we show that the groups associated to a Day-Wade series are all
\operatorname{PAut}
- and
\operatorname{POut}
-like. More precisely, write
G=\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
and
\overline{G}=\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
,
and assume that
\overline{G}
is one of the groups appearing in the series for
\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma_{1}})
, where
\Gamma_{1}
is a finite graph. Here
\Gamma
is a subgraph of
\Gamma_{1}
. We will check that if
G
is
\operatorname{PAut}
-like, then for
A_{\Delta}\in\mathscr{G}
, the groups

H=\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},(\mathscr{H}\cup\{A_{\Delta}\})^{t})

and

P=\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Delta},\mathscr{G}_{\Delta},\mathscr{H}_{\Delta}^{t})

are
\operatorname{PAut}
-like subgroups with respect to
\Gamma
and
\Delta
, respectively.
From this it will follow that the
\operatorname{POut}
version

Q=\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Delta},\mathscr{G}_{\Delta},\mathscr{H}_{\Delta}^{t})

is
\operatorname{POut}
-like as well. As we start with
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma_{1}})
, which is trivially
\operatorname{PAut}
-like, the claim for all factors of the subnormal series will follow by induction.

Note that the groups above fit into a short exact sequence

1\to H\to G\to Q\to 1.

Observe that if
\Delta
was complete, this would be trivial, as then
Q=1
. In fact, we will assume that there is no vertex
v\in\Delta
such that
\Delta\subseteq{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v)
, i.e., linked to all the rest of vertices of
\Delta
. We can assume this because otherwise removing
v
from
\Delta
yields another invariant subgraph with the same short exact sequence.

The argument will be as follows. We will define abstract groups
\hat{H}
and
\hat{P}
having the expected
\operatorname{PAut}
-like presentations and show that one can form a semidirect product

\hat{G}=\hat{P}\ltimes\hat{H}

such that there is a normal subgroup
Z\triangleleft\hat{G}
with
Z\cap\hat{H}=1
and an isomorphism
f\colon\hat{G}/Z\to G
inducing isomorphisms
\hat{H}\to H
and
\hat{G}/Z\hat{H}\to Q
.

To define these abstract groups, a key observation is the result by Day-Wade that the groups in their series are generated by the partial conjugations that they contain (Lemma
5.1
)

To fix notation, let
\Omega_{G}^{v}
for
v\in\Gamma
be the family of sets associated to the
\operatorname{PAut}
-like structure of
G
. If
v\in\Delta
, let

\Omega_{P}^{v}=\{A\in\Omega_{G}^{v}\mid A\cap\Delta\neq\emptyset\}

and

\Omega_{H}^{v}=\{L\in\Omega_{G}^{v}\mid L\cap\Delta=\emptyset\}\cup\{S^{v}\}

where
S^{v}=\cup\{A\in\Omega_{G}^{v}\mid A\cap\Delta\neq\emptyset\}
. And for
w\in\Gamma\setminus\Delta
, we set
\Omega_{H}^{w}=\Omega_{G}^{w}
.

Note that the assumption that no vertex
v\in\Delta
is linked to all the other vertices of
\Delta
implies
S^{v}\neq\emptyset
for any
v\in\Delta
.

Observe that each of the sets
\Omega^{v}_{P}
for
v\in\Delta
lies inside
\Omega^{v}_{G}
thus the whole family satisfies condition ii) in the definition of
\operatorname{PAut}
-like
7.2
above. We are going to see that the same happens for the
\Omega_{H}^{w}
’s. We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 7.4
.

With the previous notation, suppose that
A_{\Delta}\in\mathscr{H}
. If
w\in\Gamma\setminus\Delta
, then there is at most one
S^{w}\in\Omega_{G}^{w}
that intersects
\Delta
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
, if
c^{w}_{S}
preserves
A_{\Delta}
we must have either
S\cap\Delta=\emptyset
, or
\Delta\cap(\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v))\subseteq S
. Since the elements of
\Omega_{G}^{w}
are disjoint, there exists at most one
S\in\Omega_{G}^{w}
satisfying
\Delta\cap(\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}_{\Gamma}(v))\subseteq S
, and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

The family of sets
\Omega^{w}_{H}
for
w\in\Gamma
satisfies condition ii) in the definition of
\operatorname{PAut}
-like
7.2
above.

Proof.

Let
u,w\in\Gamma
be non-adjacent vertices. We need to check that the elements of
\Omega_{H}^{u}
and
\Omega_{H}^{w}
are split into the three possible cases (dominant, shared or subordinate). The assertion is trivial if both
w,u
lie in
\Gamma\setminus\Delta
because then
\Omega^{w}_{G}=\Omega^{w}_{H}
and
\Omega^{u}_{G}=\Omega^{u}_{H}
.

Assume that
u,w\in\Delta
. There is only one
S^{u}\in\Omega_{H}^{u}
and only one
S^{w}\in\Omega_{H}^{w}
that meet
\Delta
, so they must be dominant. The remaining elements of
\Omega_{H}^{u}
and
\Omega_{H}^{w}
are the shared or subordinate components of
\Omega_{G}^{u}
and
\Omega_{G}^{w}
that do not meet
\Delta
, and clearly they retain their status as shared or subordinate in
\Omega_{H}^{u}
and
\Omega_{H}^{w}
.

By symmetry it remains to consider the case
w\in\Delta
and
u\in\Gamma\smallsetminus\Delta
. If
A\in\Omega_{H}^{u}
is not dominant then, similarly to the above, it is either a shared or a subordinate component from
\Omega_{G}^{u}
that does not meet
\Delta
, and it remains shared or subordinate in
H
, since
\Omega_{H}^{u}=\Omega_{G}^{u}
. On the other hand, if
B\in\Omega_{H}^{w}
is not dominant, then we have two cases:

(1)

B
meets
\Delta
: then it is subordinate in
H
, since the dominant component in
\Omega_{H}^{u}
contains all components that meet
\Delta
; or

(2)

B
does not meet
\Delta
: then it retains its status as shared or subordinate in the passage from
G
to
H
.

So, in any case, any element
L
in
\Omega_{H}^{u}
or
\Omega_{H}^{w}
is shared, dominant or subordinate, and the proof is complete.
∎

Consider now the family of sets
\Omega^{v}_{P}
for
v\in\Delta
. As we observed above, they satisfy ii) in Definition
7.2
but note that the elements in each set form a subgraph of
\Gamma
only, not necessarily of
\Delta
. The next lemma implies that we would get exactly the same outcome if we consider the intersection of each subgraph with
\Delta
.

Lemma 7.6
.

With the same notation as above, for
v_{1},v_{2}\in\Delta
consider
\Omega_{P}^{v_{i}}=\{A\in\Omega_{G}^{v_{i}}\mid A\cap\Delta\neq\emptyset\}
for
i=1,2
.
Consider also the sets
\Omega_{P}^{v_{i}}(\Delta)=\{A\cap\Delta\mid A\in\Omega_{G}^{v_{i}}\}
.
Then for each
A\in\Omega_{P}^{v_{1}}

i)

A
is dominant for
\Omega_{P}^{v_{2}}
if and only if
A\cap\Delta
is dominant for
\Omega_{P}^{v_{2}}(\Delta)
,

ii)

A
is shared for
\Omega_{P}^{v_{2}}
if and only if
A\cap\Delta
is shared for
\Omega_{P}^{v_{2}}(\Delta)
,

iii)

A
is subordinate for
\Omega_{P}^{v_{2}}
if and only if
A\cap\Delta
is subordinate for
\Omega_{P}^{v_{2}}(\Delta)
.

Proof.

i) is trivial because
v_{2}\in\Delta
. For ii) and iii) note that the rightward implications are also trivial. In fact, this implies the leftward implications too.
∎

Before defining the abstract groups
\hat{H}
and
\hat{P}
, we need one more remark about
G
. As we will need later to construct a semidirect product
\hat{G}=\hat{P}\ltimes H
we have to define an action of
\hat{P}
on
\hat{H}
, or more precisely, a group homomorphism
\hat{P}\to{\rm Aut}(\hat{H})
. To do that we need to understand first the action of the
\Omega_{P}^{v}
-elements of
G
on the normal subgroup
H
.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
h=c^{w}_{T}
for
T\in\Omega^{w}_{H}
and
w\in\Gamma
, and let
g=c^{v}_{A}
where
A
is a union of sets in
\Omega_{P}^{v}
for
v\in\Delta
. Then

h^{g}=h
unless all the following conditions hold:
w\in A
, and either
v\in T
or
T\in\Omega_{G}^{w}
is shared for
\Omega_{G}^{v}
.

When the last conditions do hold, there is some
g_{1}
such that
h^{g_{1}}=h
and
g_{1}g=gg_{1}\in H
so

h^{g}=h^{gg_{1}}
.

Proof.

We check first that the action is trivial if
w\not\in A
. This includes the cases when
v=w
or
v
and

so it induces a well defined group homomorphism
\hat{G}\to G
. This map obviously factors through the projection
\hat{G}\to\hat{G}/Z
so we have a group homomorphism
\hat{G}/Z\to G
.

For the converse, we will check that the map

\iota\colon c^{v}_{A}\mapsto x^{v}\text{ with }s(x^{v})=A

defined on the standard generators of
G
can be extended to a group homomorphism, i.e., we have to check that the relators are preserved. Let

Y_{G}=\{c^{v}_{A}\mid v\in\Delta,A\cap\Delta\neq\emptyset\},

X_{G}=\{c^{v}_{A}\mid v\in\Delta,A\cap\Delta=\emptyset\}\cup\{c^{w}_{A}\mid w\in\Gamma\setminus\Delta\}.

Then
\iota
send elements of
Y_{G}
(resp.
X_{G}
) to
Y
(resp.
X
). And relators of the form
[a,b]
where
a
and
b
are single standard generators are sent to relators of
\hat{P}
if both
a,b\in Y_{G}
, to relators of
\hat{H}
if both
a,b\in X_{G}
and to relators describing the action of
\hat{P}
on
\hat{H}
if
a\in Y_{G}
and
b\in X_{G}
or the other way around.
Relators of the form
[a,b]
where both are single standard generators lie either in
\hat{P}
or in
\hat{H}
or are given by the action. That is to say, in all these cases the relator is preserved. The same thing happens for relators
[ab,c]
where either all of
a,b,c
lie in
Y_{G}
or all them lie in
X_{G}
.

So we only have to check what happens with relators
[ab,c]
where elements of
Y_{G}
and
X_{G}
are mixed. Put
c=c^{v}_{T}
. The
\operatorname{PAut}
-like hypothesis implies that one of
a,b
, say
a
, must be dominant for
v
, i.e.
a=c^{w}_{A}
with
v\in A
and
b
must be shared, i.e.,
b=c^{w}_{T}
. We claim first that if
c\in Y_{G}
, then also
a,b
must be in
Y_{G}
. As
c\in Y_{G}
,
v\in\Delta
and
T\cap\Delta\neq\emptyset
. Then we see that
A
and
T
are two different components of
\Omega^{w}_{G}
that meet
\Delta
. This is impossible if
w\not\in\Delta
, because
\Delta
is
G
-invariant. So
w\in\Delta
and
a,b\in Y_{G}
.

Therefore we may assume that
c\in X_{G}
. Assume that
b
lies in
Y_{G}
so
w\in\Delta
. Then
T\cap\Delta\neq\emptyset
so we deduce that
v\not\in\Delta
. But again this is impossible because it would imply that at least
T
and the component of
\Omega_{G}^{v}
that contains
w
meet
\Delta
. Therefore either both
a,b
lie in
X_{G}
or
a\in Y_{G}
. In the first case we are done. And in the second, the relator
[ab,c]
can be rewritten as
(c^{a})^{b}c^{-1}
and applying
\iota
we get a relator of
\hat{G}
because of Remark
7.9
iii).
∎

Corollary 7.15
.

Let
G=\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma},\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}^{t})
be a group that is obtained in a Day-Wade series starting from
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma_{1}})
, where
\Gamma
is a subgraph of
\Gamma_{1}
. Then
G
is
\operatorname{PAut}
-like and
\overline{G}=G/{\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
is
\operatorname{POut}
-like.

8.
Some exact sequences of Lie algebras

Here we explain why the group decompositions also work on the level of their graded Lie algebras. The first step is to show that the map
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})\to\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
induces a short exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}({\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma}))\to\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))\to\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))\to 0

of graded Lie algebras. Notice that
{\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
is isomorphic to the RAAG defined on the subgraph
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma
determined by the vertices which are not connected to every other vertex, so
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}({\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma}))
is actually a right-angled Artin Lie algebra. We will work with the presentations for
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
and
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))
given in Theorem
B
, in which generators are in bijection with the set of partial conjugations
\cup_{v}\Omega_{G}^{v}
, for
G=\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

For
\mathfrak{a}=\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(A_{\Gamma_{0}})
, define
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{a}\to\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
to be the homomorphism sending

v
to the sum
\sum c_{L}^{v}
running through all
L\in\Omega_{G}^{v}
. Here we are thinking of
v\in\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma
as a generator of
\mathfrak{a}
. Notice that
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)
is an ideal of
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
. Indeed, for any generator
v\in\mathfrak{a}
and
c_{M}^{w}\in\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
, either
[\varphi(v),c_{M}^{w}]=0
, or
[\varphi(v),c_{M}^{w}]=[\varphi(v),\varphi(w)]
, so in any case we get an element of
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)
.

It is clear from the presentations of
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
and
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))
that
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)
is equal to the kernel of
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))\to\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))
, so it remains to show that
\varphi
is injective. For that we adapt the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 3.2]
.
The idea is to define a homomorphism
\psi\colon\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))\to\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{a})
that makes the following diagram commutative:

(8.1)

\textstyle{\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{a})}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{a}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{ad}}

Notice that
\mathfrak{a}
has trivial center, so
\operatorname{ad}:\mathfrak{a}\to\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{a})
is injective. Therefore, if we can define such a
\psi
, we get from the commutativity of the diagram that
\varphi
is injective.

We define
\psi
as

\psi(c_{L}^{w})(v)=\begin{cases}[w,v]&\quad\text{if }v\in L,\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise,}\\
\end{cases}

and extend it by the Leibniz rule. There are many things to verify. We will use the following standard result.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
F
be a free Lie algebra on the set
X
and let
R
be the ideal of
F
generated by some elements
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\in F
.
Then:

(1)

Any function
\delta\colon X\to F
extends to a derivation
\Delta:F\to F
;

(2)

If
\Delta(r_{i})\in R
for all
i
, then
\Delta(R)\subseteq R
and the induced map
\bar{\Delta}\colon F/R\to F/R
is a derivation.

Lemma 8.2
.

\psi(c_{L}^{w})
is a well defined derivation of
\mathfrak{a}
for all
L
and
w
.

Proof.

Clearly we only need to work with
w\in\Gamma_{0}
.
By Lemma
8.1
it is enough to show that if
v_{1},v_{2}\in\Gamma_{0}
are adjacent, then the element

(8.2)

[\psi(c_{L}^{w})v_{1},v_{2}]+[v_{1},\psi(c_{L}^{w})v_{2}]

is a consequence of the defining relators for
\mathfrak{a}
.

If
v_{1},v_{2}\notin L
, then (
8.2
) reduces to
0
. Similarly, if
v_{1},v_{2}\in L
, then:

[\psi(c_{L}^{w})v_{1},v_{2}]+[v_{1},\psi(c_{L}^{w})v_{2}]=[[w,v_{1}],v_{2}]+[v_{1},[w,v_{2}]]=[w,[v_{1},v_{2}]]

by the Jacobi identity. Since
[v_{1},v_{2}]
is a defining relator of
\mathfrak{a}
, we are done in this case.

Finally, suppose that
v_{1}\in L
but
v_{2}\notin L
. In this case, since
L
is a connected component of
\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(w)
, we have
v_{2}\in{\rm st}(w)
. Then

[\psi(c_{L}^{w})v_{1},v_{2}]+[v_{1},\psi(c_{L}^{w})v_{2}]=[[w,v_{1}],v_{2}]=[[w,v_{2}],v_{1}]+[w,[v_{1},v_{2}]].

Since
[w,v_{2}]
and
[v_{1},v_{2}]
are defining relators, we are done. The case where
v_{1}\notin L
and

v_{2}\in L
is completely analogous.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

\psi\colon\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))\to\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{a})
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras.

Proof.

We need to show that the relations of
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
are respected. For any
vertex
w\in\Gamma
and
L\subseteq\Gamma
a subgraph, let

\delta_{L}^{w}=\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{if }w\in L,\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Then for any generator
c_{L}^{w}\in\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
and
v\in\Gamma_{0}
, we have

\psi(c_{L}^{w})(v)=\delta_{L}^{v}[w,v].

For any generators
c_{L}^{w_{1}},c_{M}^{w_{2}}\in\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
and
v\in\Gamma_{0}
, using the Jacobi identity and the Leibniz rule for derivations, we have:

(8.3)

[\psi(c_{L}^{w_{1}}),\psi(c_{M}^{w_{2}})](v)=(\delta_{M}^{v}\delta_{L}^{w_{2}}+\delta_{L}^{v}\delta_{M}^{w_{1}}-\delta_{M}^{v}\delta_{L}^{v})[[w_{1},w_{2}],v].

Now suppose that
[c_{L}^{w_{1}},c_{M}^{w_{2}}]=0
is a defining relation in
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
. So we are in one of the following cases:

(i)

w_{1}\in{\rm st}(w_{2})
;

(ii)

(L\cup\{w_{1}\})\cap(M\cup\{w_{2}\})=\emptyset
;

(iii)

L\cup\{w_{1}\}\subseteq M
;

(iv)

M\cup\{w_{2}\}\subseteq L
.

It is clear that (
8.3
) is
0
in case (i). In case (ii) we have
\delta_{L}^{w_{2}}=\delta_{M}^{w_{1}}=\delta_{L}^{v}\cdot\delta_{M}^{v}=0
, thus clearly (
8.3
) reduces to
0
again. In case
(iii) we have
\delta_{L}^{w_{2}}=0
and
\delta_{M}^{w_{1}}=1
, so the coefficient on the right hand side of (
8.3
) is

\delta_{L}^{v}(1-\delta_{M}^{v})
, which is zero whenever
v\in M
or
v\in\Gamma\smallsetminus M\subset\Gamma\smallsetminus L
. Case (iv) is completely analogous to case (iii).

Now we consider a relation of type
[c_{L}^{w_{1}},c_{L}^{w_{2}}+c_{M}^{w_{2}}]
, with
w_{1}\in M
. We need to show that
A=[\psi(c_{L}^{w_{1}}),\psi(c_{M}^{w_{2}})](v)

and
B=-[\psi(c_{L}^{w_{1}}),\psi(c_{L}^{w_{2}})](v)
coincide for all
v\in\Gamma_{0}
. First, since
L
is a shared component, we have
\delta_{L}^{w_{1}}=\delta_{L}^{w_{2}}=0
, so

B=-(\delta_{L}^{v}\delta_{L}^{w_{2}}+\delta_{L}^{v}\delta_{L}^{w_{1}}-\delta_{L}^{v}\delta_{L}^{v})[[w_{1},w_{2}],v]=-(-\delta_{L}^{v}\delta_{L}^{v})[[w_{1},w_{2}],v]=\delta_{L}^{v}[[w_{1},w_{2}],v].

On the other hand, since
L
and
M
are disjoint,
\delta_{L}^{v}\cdot\delta_{M}^{v}=0
. So

A=(\delta_{M}^{v}\delta_{L}^{w_{2}}+\delta_{L}^{v}\delta_{M}^{w_{1}}-\delta_{M}^{v}\delta_{L}^{v})[[w_{1},w_{2}],v]=\delta_{L}^{v}[[w_{1},w_{2}],v],

as
\delta_{M}^{w_{1}}=1
. Thus
A
=
B
.
∎

Proposition 8.4
.

For any finite graph
\Gamma
, there is an exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}({\rm Inn}(A_{\Gamma}))\to\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))\to\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))\to 0

induced by the group homomorphism
\operatorname{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})\to\operatorname{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Proof.

It remains to show that the diagram (
8.1
) commutes. For
w,v\in\Gamma_{0}
we have:

(\psi\circ\varphi(w))(v)=\sum\psi(c_{L}^{w})(v),

where the sum runs over the connected components
L\subseteq\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(w)
. By definition
\psi(c_{L}^{w})(v)
can be non-zero
only if
v\in L
. If
w
and
v
are adjacent, then
(\psi\circ\varphi(w))(v)=0=\operatorname{ad}(w)(v)
. Otherwise,
v\in L_{1}
for some
connected component
L_{1}\subseteq\Gamma\smallsetminus{\rm st}(w)
, so

(\psi\circ\varphi(w))(v)=\psi(c_{L_{1}}^{w})(v)=[w,v]=\operatorname{ad}(w)(v).

So
\psi\circ\varphi(w)=\operatorname{ad}(w)
for all
w
, that is, the diagram is commutes.
∎

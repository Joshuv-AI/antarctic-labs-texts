TOPOLOGICAL TRIVIALITY AND LINK-CONSTANCY IN DEFORMATIONS OF INNER KHOVANSKII NON-DEGENERATE MAPS.
Julian D. Espinel Leal, Eder L. Sanchez Quiceno

Abstract
For real and mixed polynomial maps f = ( f 1 , … , f p ) f=(f^{1},\dots,f^{p}) satisfying f ⁡ ( 0 ) = 0 f(0)=0 , we introduce the notion of Inner Khovanskii Non-Degeneracy (IKND), that generalizes a previous non-degeneracy condition introduced by Wall for complex polynomial functions (J. Reine Angew. Math. 509 (1999), 1-19). We prove that IKND is a sufficient condition ensuring that the link of the singularity of f f at the origin is smooth and well-defined. We study one-parameter deformations of an IKND map f f , given by F ⁡ ( 𝒙 , ε ) = f ⁡ ( 𝒙 ) + θ ⁡ ( 𝒙 , ε ) F(\bm{x},\varepsilon)=f(\bm{x})+\theta(\bm{x},\varepsilon) , with F ⁡ ( 0 , ε ) = 0 F(0,\varepsilon)=0 . We prove that the deformation is link-constant under suitable conditions on f f and θ \theta , meaning that the ambient isotopy type of the link remains unchanged along the deformation. Furthermore, by employing a strong version of this non-degeneracy, Strong Inner Khovanskii Non-Degeneracy (SIKND), we obtain results on topological triviality. In the final section, we present link-constant deformations for IKND mixed polynomial functions of two variables. We also explore several applications motivated by the recent findings of Araújo dos Santos, Bode, and Sanchez Quiceno (Bull. Braz. Math. Soc. (N.S.) 55 (2024), no. 3, Paper No. 34). Keywords: deformations, link-constancy, inner non-degeneracies, mixed maps, topological triviality, real polynomial maps. MSC Classification: Primary 14M25 ; Secondary 32S55, 14M10, 14J17, 57K10, 32S05 .

1.1  Background

A key question in the study of complex (or real) analytic singularities is whether a given deformation

F(\bm{x},\varepsilon):\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{K}^{p},\ \mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}\text{ or }\mathbb{R}

is equisingular (or trivial). In other words, do the maps

F_{\varepsilon}(\bm{x}):=F(\bm{x},\varepsilon):\mathbb{K}^{n}\to\mathbb{K}^{p}

remain locally equivalent, in some suitable sense, for sufficiently small values of
\varepsilon
?

A foundational result by Lê and Ramanujam
[
29
]
shows that, when
n\neq 3
, any deformation
F
of a complex analytic function having constant Milnor number (
\mu
-constant deformation) is
V
-trivial, that is, for the associated family of hypersurfaces
\{F_{\varepsilon}^{\,-1}(0)\}
, there exist germs of homeomorphisms

h_{\varepsilon}:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)

such that
h_{\varepsilon}(F_{\varepsilon}^{\,-1}(0))=F_{0}^{\,-1}(0)
. The case
n=3
remains an important open problem in singularity theory.

In
[
40
]
, Timourian improves the result of Lê and Ramanujam by proving that a
\mu
-constant deformation is topologically trivial for
n\not=3
, that is, there exist germs of homeomorphisms
h_{\varepsilon}:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
such that
F_{\varepsilon}\circ h_{\varepsilon}=F_{0}
. Later, in
[
41
]
, Wall studied deformations of a complex analytic function
f
, that is,
F(\bm{x},0)=f(\bm{x})
. Under the hypothesis of inner Newton non-degeneracy on
f
, and imposing additional conditions on
F_{\varepsilon}-f
, he proved that the deformation is topologically trivial, even in the difficult case
n=3
.

A natural setting to extend Wall’s results is the class of deformations of mixed polynomial functions, namely, complex-valued polynomials in complex variables and their conjugates. This class is equivalent to that of real polynomial maps
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and it includes the classical complex polynomial functions. Studying these objects as mixed polynomial functions offers significant advantages: it enables the formulation of non-degeneracy conditions in terms of the Newton polyhedron of a mixed polynomial function, as defined by Oka in
[
37
]
, and ensures the resolution of singularities from the perspective of toric modifications, as also demonstrated by Oka in
[
37
]
. This generalizes, to mixed polynomial functions, the previous notions of non-degeneracies of complex polynomial functions due to Kouchnirenko
[
28
]
. Within this framework, Araújo dos Santos, Bode and Sanchez Quiceno
[
2
]
extended the notion of inner Newton non-degeneracy (INND) to mixed polynomial functions of two variables and proved that such functions have a weakly isolated singularity at the origin, that is,
f^{-1}(0)\cap\Sigma(f)=\{0\}
, where
\Sigma(f)
denotes the singular locus. This implies that
f
defines a smooth and well-defined link of the singularity (in the sense of Milnor
[
34
]
). They showed that, under
\Gamma
-niceness condition (Definition
4.20
), these links depend on the Newton boundary and have a decomposition of the form
\textbf{L}([L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
(see Definition
4.28
), where each link
L_{i}
corresponds to a 1-face
\Delta_{i}
of the Newton boundary of
f
. Subsequently, Bode in
[
8
]
studied the topological properties of the pieces
L_{i}
and completely characterized the links of
\Gamma
-nice and INND mixed polynomial functions.
To the author’s knowledge, a complete characterization of the links of INND mixed polynomial functions remains open.

For real polynomial maps

\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a weakly isolated singularity at the origin, the corresponding characterization is known: every link in the 3-sphere can occur. This was established by Akbulut and King
[
1
]
and later polished by Bode
[
11
]
. For maps with an isolated singularity at the origin, however, the characterization problem remains open. It is conjectured that every fibered link arises in this setting
[
6
]
. Recent advances further support this conjecture
[
3

9
,
10
,
12
,
13
]
. In contrast, for complex polynomial functions of two variables, the corresponding links are completely determined by explicit conditions derived from their Puiseux pairs, following classical results of Brauner, Burau, and Kähler
[
14

25

In this paper, we generalize the notion of inner Newton non-degeneracy (INND) for real polynomial maps and mixed polynomial maps, introducing the concept of
(strong) inner Khovanskii non-degeneracy
((S)IKND). Our goal is to obtain results on equisingularity for one-parameter deformations of such maps, while also producing tools for characterizing links of singularities. Further results on non-degenerate maps can be found in Bivià Ausina and Khovanskii
[
7

26

, and their deformations are studied, among others, by Damon, Eyral, Gaffney, Nguyen and Oka
[
19

21

20
,
35

1.2  Main results

Our new non-degeneracy conditions are based on a collection of
C
-face diagrams, which play a similar role to Newton boundaries of the coordinate functions in the classical definition of Khovanskii non-degeneracy (KND) introduced in
[
26
]
. As examples of IKND maps, we present the following classes: convenient KND maps, semi-weighted homogeneous maps (SWH), semi-radially weighted homogeneous mixed polynomial maps (SRWH), and KND polynomial functions in three variables that are not necessarily convenient (see Proposition
2.15
). We investigate two important properties to obtain equisingular deformations: (weak) no coalescing of critical points and the
\rho
-uniform Milnor radius. In the case of complex analytic functions, the no coalescing of critical points of
F
is equivalent to being
\mu
-constant
[
27
]
. Related results appear in
[
32
,
33
]
. The notion of
\rho
-uniform Milnor radius, studied by King
[
27
]
and Bekka
[
5
]
, is further discussed in
[
36
,
22
,
35
]
. These properties are relevant because (weak) no coalescing of critical points of
F
ensures that each
F_{\varepsilon}
has a (weakly) isolated singularity at the origin. In the case of complex polynomial maps each
F_{\varepsilon}
defines an isolated complete intersection singularity (ICIS). The (weak) no coalesing of critical points implies that the link of each
F_{\varepsilon}
,
F^{-1}_{\varepsilon}(0)\cap\mathbb{S}_{r}
, is smooth and well-defined for
r
sufficiently small (Here,
\mathbb{S}_{r}
denotes a standard sphere of radius
r
and centered at the origin). However, it is not known if the isotopy type of these links changes with the parameter
\varepsilon
. We address this by showing in Proposition
4.4
that the existence of a
\rho
-uniform Milnor radius is sufficient to guarantee that the isotopy type is preserved, a property we term
link-constancy
. In earlier work by King
[
27
]
and Bekka
[
5
]
, no coalescing of critical points together with additional conditions were used to obtain topologically trivial deformations. In Propositions
4.5
and
4.6
, we provide examples of such deformations.

We therefore restrict our study to deformations of (S)IKND real or mixed polynomial maps, and we establish our first main result, Theorem
3.3
. This theorem states that if
f=(f^{1},\dots,f^{p})
is SIKND (resp. IKND) with respect to a set of
C
-face diagrams
D_{1},\dots,D_{p}
, and if the terms of
F^{\,j}_{\varepsilon}-f^{j}
lie above or on
D_{j}
for each

, then the deformation
F
has no coalescing (resp. weak no coalescing) of critical points. Under additional hypotheses, these deformations are also link-constant and topologically trivial. These conditions are established in our main results: Theorems
4.7
and
4.11
for maps, and Theorem
4.18
for mixed polynomial functions of two variables.

As a first application of the latter theorem, Corollary
4.19
, we yield a full extension, into the mixed setting of two variables, of the classical result that the link type of a convenient non-degenerate complex polynomial function is completely determined by the monomials on its Newton boundary (see
[
2
]
). As a second application, we obtain Proposition
4.29
,
which states that any
\Gamma
-nice and INND mixed polynomial function

can be
placed in a link-constant piecewise linear analytic family of mixed polynomials

\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}
such that
f_{0}=f
and the link of

is isotopic to a link of the form

\mathbf{L}([L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{N-1}],L_{N}).

In particular, this yields
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
, with a slightly
different construction of the links
L_{i}
. We also show that every member of such link-constant piecewise linear analytic family
is always
\Gamma
-nice. This property is established in Proposition
4.25
,
where it is proved that, for a fixed
C
-face diagram

-nice and the
non

-nice conditions define open properties. As a final application, in Proposition

Khovanskii non-degenerate (KND)
if for every strictly positive weight vector
\bm{w}
,

\Sigma(f_{\bm{w}})\cap V(f_{\bm{w}})\cap(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}=\emptyset.

•

Strongly Khovanskii non-degenerate (SKND)
if for every strictly positive weight vector
\bm{w}
, we have

\Sigma(f_{\bm{w}})\cap(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}=\emptyset.

For a map
f=(f^{1},\dots,f^{p}):\mathbb{K}^{n}\to\mathbb{K}^{p}
, we define

I^{j}_{\mathrm{nc}}(f):=\{i\in[n]\mid\supp(f^{j})\cap\mathbb{R}^{\{i\}}=\emptyset\}
and
I_{\rm{nc}}(f):=\cup_{j=1}^{p}I^{j}_{\rm{nc}}(f).
We say that the map
f
is
convenient
if
I_{\rm{nc}}(f)=\emptyset
. In other words, if any coordinate function
f^{j}
is convenient.

Remark 2.4

If
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is complex analytic or mixed, then KND reduces to Newton non-degeneracy introduced by Kouchnirenko
[
28
]
for complex analytic and by Oka
[
37
]
for mixed functions.

Proposition
\@upn
2.5

Let
f
be a convenient (S)KND real polynomial map (or mixed polynomial map). Then
f
is (S)IKND.

Suppose that
f
is SKND. Let
D_{j}:=\Gamma(f^{j})
denote the
C
-face diagram of
f^{j}
, for
j=1,\dots,p
, and let
D:=D_{1}+\cdots+D_{p}
. Suppose that
f
is not SIKND with respect to these
C
-face diagrams. Then, there exists an inner face
\Theta^{\prime}
of
D
, and a subset
I\subseteq[n]
, such that
\Theta:=\Theta^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{R}^{I}\neq\emptyset
and

\Sigma(f_{\Theta^{\prime}})\cap(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{I}\neq\emptyset.
Since
I=[n]
implies that
f
is not SKND, we may assume that
I\subsetneq[n]
. Let
\bm{w}\in(\mathbb{Q}_{>0})^{n}
be such that
\Theta^{\prime}=\Delta(\bm{w};D)
, define
\bm{q}\in(\mathbb{Q}_{>0})^{n}
, by
q_{i}=w_{i}
if
i\in I
and
q_{i}=w_{i}+1
if
i\not\in I
. Then
\Theta=\Delta(\bm{q};D)
and
d:=d(\bm{w};D)=d(\bm{q};D)
.
Let
\Theta^{\prime}=\Theta^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+\Theta^{\prime}_{p}
and
\Theta=\Theta_{1}+\cdots+\Theta_{p}
be the unique decompositions corresponding to the Minkowski sum of faces, where
\Theta^{\prime}_{j},\Theta_{j}\subset D_{j}
, for each
j=1,\dots,p
.

Let
\bm{x}_{1}\in\Sigma(f_{\Theta^{\prime}})\cap(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{I}
. Thus, there exists
(a_{1},\dots,a_{p})\in\mathbb{K}^{p}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\sum_{j=1}^{p}a_{j}\nabla f^{\,j}_{\Theta^{\prime}_{j}}(\bm{x}_{1})=0.

Define
\bm{x}_{0}=(x_{0,1},\dots,x_{0,n})\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
by setting
x_{0,i}=x_{1,i}
if
i\in I
, and
x_{0,i}=1
otherwise.

Claim
\@upn
2.6

f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}}(\bm{x}_{0})=f^{\,j}_{\Theta^{\prime}_{j}}(\bm{x}_{1})
for
j=1,\dots,p
.

We have
f^{\,j}_{\Theta^{\prime}_{j}}=f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}}+(f^{\,j}_{\Theta^{\prime}_{j}}-f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}})
. Let
h=a_{\bm{\nu}}\bm{x}^{\bm{\nu}}
be a monomial of
(f^{\,j}_{\Theta^{\prime}_{j}}-f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}})
, then
d(\bm{q}^{\prime};a_{\bm{\nu}}\bm{x}^{\bm{\nu}})>d(\bm{w};a_{\bm{\nu}}\bm{x}^{\bm{\nu}})=d(\bm{w};D_{j})
, this means, that there is
i\in[n]\setminus I
, such that
\nu_{i}\not=0
. Hence
h(\bm{x}_{1})=a_{\bm{\nu}}\bm{x}_{1}^{\bm{\nu}}=0
, this implies that
(f^{\,j}_{\Theta^{\prime}_{j}}-f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}})(\bm{x}_{1})=0
. The result follows from
f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}}(\bm{x}_{0})=f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}}(\bm{x}_{1})
.
∎

Then, by Claim
2.6
we have

\frac{\partial f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}}}{\partial x_{i}}(\bm{x}_{0})=\frac{\partial f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}}}{\partial x_{i}}(\bm{x}_{1})=\begin{cases}\frac{\partial f^{\,j}_{\Theta^{\prime}_{j}}}{\partial x_{i}}(\bm{x}_{1})&\text{if }i\in I,\\
0&\text{if }i\notin I.\end{cases}

Hence,

\sum_{j=1}^{p}a_{j}\nabla f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}}(\bm{x}_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{p}a_{j}\nabla f^{\,j}_{\Theta_{j}}(\bm{x}_{1})=0,

which implies that

, contradicting the fact that
\Sigma(f_{\Theta})\cap(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}=\emptyset
.

Now, if we have that

is KND then
\bm{x}_{1}\in V(f_{\Theta^{\prime}})
. Since
f_{\Theta^{\prime}}(\bm{x}_{1})=f_{\Theta}(\bm{x}_{1})=f_{\Theta}(\bm{x}_{0})
, it follows that
\bm{x}_{0}\in\Sigma(f_{\Theta})\cap V(f_{\Theta})\cap(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}
. Contradicting the fact that

∎

2.2  SWH and SRWH maps

Definition
\@upn
2.7

A real polynomial function (resp. mixed polynomial function)
f:\mathbb{K}^{n}\to\mathbb{K}
is called
weighted homogeneous
(WH) (resp.
radially weighted homogeneous
(RWH)) of weight-type
(\bm{w};d)=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n};d)
,
w_{i},d\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, if for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, the following condition holds:

f(\lambda^{w_{1}}x_{1},\dots,\lambda^{w_{n}}x_{n})=\lambda^{d}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}).

A real polynomial map (resp. mixed polynomial map)
f=(f^{1},\dots,f^{p}):\mathbb{K}^{n}\to\mathbb{K}^{p}
, where
n\geq p
, is called
weighted homogeneous
(WH) (resp.
radially weighted homogeneous
(RWH)) of weight-type
(\bm{w};\bm{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{p}))
if each coordinate function
f^{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,p
, is WH (resp. RWH) of weight-type
(\bm{w};d_{i})
.

Definition
\@upn
2.8

A real polynomial map (resp. mixed polynomial map)
f=(f^{1},\dots,f^{p}):\mathbb{K}^{n}\to\mathbb{K}^{p}
is
semi-weighted homogeneous
(SWH) (resp.
semi-radially weighted homogeneous
(SRWH)) of weight-type
(\bm{w};\bm{d})
if:

(i)

The map
f_{\bm{w}}
is WH (resp. RWH) of weight-type
(\bm{w};\bm{d})
and
\Sigma({f_{\bm{w}}})\cap V({f_{\bm{w}}})=\{0\}
,

(ii)

for each
j=1,\dots,p,

d(\bm{w};f^{j}-f_{\bm{w}}^{j})>d_{j}.

This definition requires that the variety
V(f_{\bm{w}})
has an isolated singularity at the origin, which is a weaker condition than requiring
f_{\bm{w}}
itself to have an isolated singularity, as in the classical definition.

Proposition
\@upn
2.9

SWH maps are IKND. If, in addition,
\Sigma(f_{\bm{w}})=\{0\}
, then it is SIKND.

Let
f
be a SWH map of weight type
(\bm{w};(d_{1},\dots,d_{p}))
. For each
i=1,\dots,p
, define the
C
-face diagram
D_{i}:=D(J_{i})
, where
J_{i}=\{\ell_{\bm{w}}/d_{i}\}
. Since
\Sigma(f_{\bm{w}})\cap V(f_{\bm{w}})=\{0\}
, it follows that
f
is IKND with respect to the collection of
C
-face diagrams
D_{1},\dots,D_{p}
. To get the condition SIKND we use
\Sigma(f_{\bm{w}})=\{0\}
.
∎

Example 2.10

Consider the real polynomial

f(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=x_{1}^{\,12}+x_{1}x_{2}^{\,4}x_{3}+(x_{2}^{\,3}-x_{3}^{\,2})^{2}.
We claim that
f
is SIKND but not SKND. To see that
f
is
not
SKND, take the weight vector
\bm{w}=(2,2,3)
. Then the face function related to
\bm{w}
is

f_{\bm{w}}(\bm{x})=(x_{2}^{\,3}-x_{3}^{\,2})^{2},

which clearly has a singular locus containing the point
(1,1,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{\{1,2,3\}}
. Therefore,

\Sigma({f_{\bm{w}}})\cap\mathbb{R}^{\{1,2,3\}}\neq\emptyset,

and
f
is not SKND.
To check whether
f
is SIKND, consider the directional function given by

\ell(\bm{\nu})=\left\langle\left(\tfrac{1}{12},\tfrac{2}{12},\tfrac{3}{12}\right),\bm{\nu}\right\rangle.

The corresponding
C
-face diagram
D(J),\ J=\{\ell\}
has a unique 1-face, so
f
is SIKND if and only if
\Sigma(f_{D(J)})=\{0\}
. Since all monomials of
f
lie on this face, we have
f=f_{D(J)}
, and thus it suffices to show that the system of partial derivatives has only the trivial solution:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=12x_{1}^{\,11}+x_{2}^{\,4}x_{3}=0,

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=2x_{2}^{\,2}\left(2x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+3(x_{2}^{\,3}-x_{3}^{\,2})\right)=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{3}}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{2}^{\,4}-4x_{3}(x_{2}^{\,3}-x_{3}^{\,2})=0.

(3)

The origin
(0,0,0)
clearly satisfies the system. It is also easy to check that for any
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, if
x_{i}=0
, then
\bm{x}=(0,0,0)
. Therefore, we assume that there is a nontrivial solution
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{\{1,2,3\}}
, i.e., all
x_{i}\neq 0
.

From (
2
), dividing by
2x_{2}^{\,2}\neq 0
, we get:

x_{1}=\frac{-3(x_{2}^{\,3}-x_{3}^{\,2})}{2x_{2}x_{3}}.

(4)

Substitute (
4
) into (
3
):

(x_{2}^{\,3}-x_{3}^{\,2})\left(\frac{-3x_{2}^{\,3}}{2x_{3}}-4x_{3}\right)=0.

Thus, either
x_{2}^{\,3}=x_{3}^{\,2}
, or

-3x_{2}^{\,3}=8x_{3}^{\,2}
.
If
x_{2}^{\,3}=x_{3}^{\,2}
, then
x_{1}=0
by (
4
), contradicting
x_{1}\neq 0
. Thus,
x_{2}^{\,3}=-\frac{8}{3}x_{3}^{\,2}
. Then, we get:

x_{1}=\frac{-3(x_{2}^{\,3}-x_{3}^{\,2})}{2x_{2}x_{3}}=\frac{-3(-\frac{8}{3}x_{3}^{\,2}-x_{3}^{\,2})}{2x_{2}x_{3}}=\frac{9x_{3}^{\,2}}{2x_{2}x_{3}}=\frac{9x_{3}}{2x_{2}}.

Substitute this value of
x_{1}
into (
1
):

12\left(\frac{9x_{3}}{2x_{2}}\right)^{11}+x_{2}^{\,4}x_{3}=0.

Clearing denominators and simplifying gives a contradiction unless
x_{3}=0
, which is not allowed. Therefore, no nontrivial solution exists, and we conclude that

\Sigma(f)=\{0\}
, so
f
is SIKND.

2.3  IKND mixed maps

Let
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2p}
be a polynomial map,
\sigma
and
\varsigma
be permutations of
n
and
p
elements, respectively. We identify
\mathbb{C}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}
by setting
x_{i}=x_{\sigma(2i-1)}+\mathrm{i}x_{\sigma(2i)}
for

x_{\sigma(2i-1)}=(x_{i}+\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{i}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu)/2

Lemma
\@upn
2.11

Let
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2p}

\sigma
and
\varsigma

and

Suposse that
\sigma
and
\varsigma
the trivial permutations. Let

-face diagrams of

be the set of linear functions defining

, for

. Each
{\ell}\in{J}_{j}

\tilde{\ell}(\bm{\nu})={\ell}(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2},\nu_{3}+\nu_{4},\dots,\nu_{2n-1}+\nu_{2n}).

-face diagrams

\displaystyle={\ell}(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2},\nu_{3}+\nu_{4},\dots,\nu_{2n-1}+\nu_{2n})

\@upn
2.12

Let
\tilde{\Theta}
be an inner face of

. If

if

and
m(k)=2k
if

if

and
M(k)=2k

. Since
\tilde{\Theta}
is an inner face, there is

\tilde{\ell}
defining

\displaystyle\langle\bm{w};\bm{\nu}^{\prime}\rangle<\langle\bm{w};\bm{\nu}\rangle=d(\bm{w};\tilde{D}_{j}),

∎Thus, by Claim

2.4  IKND functions in three variables

\bm{v}
. Let

\displaystyle v_{i}

\displaystyle>\frac{\nu_{k}q_{i}+\nu_{j}p_{i}q_{k}-\nu_{i}q_{k}}{\nu_{k}+q_{k}(\nu_{j}-1)},

(5)

for all

The last inequality follows from (

Proposition
\@upn
2.15

f:\mathbb{K}^{3}\to\mathbb{K}
be a KND real polynomial function (or mixed polynomial function) as Lemma
2.14
such that their associated polynomials

, satisfy

. Then,
f
is IKND.

We first prove the case where

is a mixed polynomial function. The proof for the real case is analogous.

If
I_{\rm{nc}}(f)=\emptyset
, then by Proposition
2.5
,
f
is IKND. We therefore suppose that
I_{\rm{nc}}(f)\neq\emptyset
.

Take an index
i\in I_{\rm{nc}}(f)
. By Lemma
2.14
there is a unique 2-face
\Delta(i)
in the
C
-face diagram
D
satisfying
\Delta(i)\cap\mathbb{R}^{\{i\}}\neq\emptyset
and
f_{\Delta(i)}(\bm{x})=M_{i}(x_{i},x_{j})+N_{i}(x_{j},x_{k}),
where

are distinct and

4.1  SWH and SRWH maps

Theorem
\@upn
4.11

Let
F:\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{K}^{p}
be a deformation of a real polynomial map (resp. mixed polynomial map)
f
. Assume the following conditions hold:

(i)

f
is SWH (resp. SRWH) of weight-type
(\bm{w};\bm{d})
,

(ii)

for each
j=1,\dots,p,
and
\varepsilon
,
d({\bm{w}};F_{\varepsilon}^{\,j}-f^{j})\geq d_{j}.

Then, the deformation is link-constant along some neighborhood
K\subset\mathbb{K}
of the origin. If the inequalities in (ii) are strict, then the deformation is link-constant. Furthermore, if
\Sigma(f_{\bm{w}})=\{0\}
, then the deformation is also topologically trivial along the set
K
or
\mathbb{K}
.

Let
(\bm{w};\bm{d})=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n};d_{1},\dots,d_{p})
denote its weight-type. Let
D_{j}
be the
C
-face diagrams defined from
J_{j}=\{\ell_{\bm{w}}(\nu)/{d_{j}}\}.

Without loss of generality, assume that
f
is a real polynomial map, the result for mixed polynomial maps follows by regarding it as real polynomial map by identifying
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

It is enough to suppose that
f
is WH, that is,
f=f_{\bm{w}}
, or equivalently,
\tilde{f}\equiv 0
.

Let
\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon}:=F_{\varepsilon}^{\,j}-f^{j}
,
j=1,\dots,p
and
I=\{j\in[p]\mid d(\bm{w};\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon})=d_{j}\}
. We divide the proof into two cases:

I=\emptyset
and
I\neq\emptyset
.

Case I:
I=\emptyset
.

Consider the analytic function

\rho:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as:

\rho(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{\frac{2w}{w_{i}}},\ w=w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{p}.

Consider the map
\pi:\rho^{-1}(1)\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined as:

\pi(\bm{s},r)=(r^{w_{1}}s_{1},\dots,r^{w_{n}}s_{n}).

(16)

This map is a diffeomorphism outside the origin. This map is also used in
[
23
, Theorem IV]
.

Since
f
is WH of weight-type
(\bm{w};\bm{d})
, we have for any
j
:

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}^{\,j}(\pi(\bm{s},r))

\displaystyle=f^{j}(\bm{s})r^{d_{j}}+\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon}(\pi(\bm{s},r)),

(17)

where, by
d(\bm{w};\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon})>d_{j},\ j=1,\dots,p
,

\lim_{r\to 0}r^{-d_{j}}\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon}(\pi(\bm{s},r))=0.

Thus,
F_{\varepsilon}(\pi(\bm{s},r))=0

if and only if:

r^{d_{j}}(f^{j}(\bm{s})+r^{-d_{j}}\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon}(\pi(\bm{s},r)))=0,\ \text{for all }j=1,\dots p,

which implies
r=0
(corresponding to the origin) or:

f^{j}(\bm{s})+r^{-d_{j}}\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon}(\pi(\bm{s},r))=0,\ \text{for all }j=1,\dots,p.

(18)

The system provided by (
18
) can be seen as the zero set of the map

G(\bm{s},r,\varepsilon):\rho^{-1}(1)\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}

which is a deformation with respect to the parameter
r
of the map
G_{0}(\bm{s},\varepsilon):\rho^{-1}(1)\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
, where

G_{0}(\bm{s},\varepsilon):=G(\bm{s},0,\varepsilon)=f_{|\rho^{-1}(1)}(\bm{s}).

It follows that:

\mathrm{rank}(J_{G}(\bm{s},0,\varepsilon)=\mathrm{rank}(J_{f_{|\rho^{-1}(1)}}(\bm{s})).

Since
\Sigma(f)\cap V(f)=\{0\}
and
f
is WH, for any point
(\bm{s}_{0},\varepsilon_{0})\in\rho^{-1}(1)\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfying
G(\bm{s}_{0},0,\varepsilon_{0})=f(\bm{s}_{0})=0
, we find

\mathrm{rank}(J_{G}(\bm{s}_{0},0,\varepsilon_{0}))=p.

If the link of
f
is empty, then the link of each member of the deformation is empty as well, which implies the result. If
f
has non-empty link, by the implicit function theorem, we can locally, at a neighborhood of
(\bm{s}_{0},0,\varepsilon_{0})
, parametrize
\pi^{-1}(V(F))
(after possible changes of variables) as:

(s_{1}(s_{p+2},\dots,s_{n},r,\varepsilon),\dots,s_{p+1}(s_{p+2},\dots,s_{n},r,\varepsilon),s_{p+2},\dots s_{n},r,\varepsilon),

(19)

with
r
and
\varepsilon
moving in
[0,\delta_{s_{0},\varepsilon_{0}})
and
(\varepsilon_{0}-\tau_{\bm{s}_{0},\varepsilon_{0}},\varepsilon_{0}+\tau_{\bm{s}_{0},\varepsilon_{0}})
, respectively, and

s_{i}(s_{0,p+2},\dots,s_{0,n},0,\varepsilon_{0})=s_{0,i},\ i=1,\dots,p+1.

By compactness of
\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{\varepsilon_{0}\}
, there exists a finite family
\mathcal{F}
of parametrizations of the form (
19
), covering the set

(\rho^{-1}(1)\times[0,\delta_{\mathrm{min}})\times(\varepsilon_{0}-\tau_{\mathrm{min}},\varepsilon_{0}+\tau_{\mathrm{min}}))\cap G^{-1}(0),

(20)

where
\delta_{\mathrm{min}}>0
and
\tau_{\mathrm{min}}>0
are choosing as the minimum values of
\rho
’s and
\tau
’s associated with the parametrizations in
\mathcal{F}
, respectively.

By the uniqueness of the parametrizations coming from the implicit function theorem, we can glue the parametrizations of the family
\mathcal{F}
to get a parametrization of the entire set (
20
).

Therefore, for any fixed
0<r<\rho_{\mathrm{min}}
, this parametrization provides an isotopy, obtained by varying the parameter
\varepsilon
, contained in
\rho^{-1}(1)\times{r}
, between the following links

(\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{r\})\cap\pi^{-1}(V(F_{\varepsilon_{0}}))\text{ and }(\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{r\})\cap\pi^{-1}(V(F_{\tau})),

with
\ \tau\in(\varepsilon_{0}-\tau_{\mathrm{min}},\varepsilon_{0}+\tau_{\mathrm{min}}).
Then, we have a smooth isotopy between

\pi(\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{r\})\cap V(F_{\varepsilon_{0}})\text{ and }\pi(\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{r\})\cap V(F_{\tau}),

(21)

by the isotopy extension theorem
[
24
]
, it extends to an ambient isotopy of
\pi(\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{r\})
. Thus, as in the proof of Proposition
4.4
, we can construct an isotopy between
L_{F_{0}}
and
L_{F_{\tau}}
. It is clear that finitely many such isotopies suffice to produce an isotopy between
L_{F_{0}}
and
L_{F_{1}}
.

Case II:
I\neq\emptyset
.
Consider the map
\pi
defined in (
16
). Differently of (
17
),
for
F_{\varepsilon}^{\,j}
, we have now:

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}^{\,j}(\pi(\bm{s},r))

\displaystyle=(f^{j}(\bm{s})+(\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon})_{D_{j}}(\bm{s}))r^{d_{j}}+h^{j}(\pi(\bm{s},r),\varepsilon),

where
(\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon})_{D_{j}}
(possibly null not for all
i
) denotes the restriction of
\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon}
to the
C
-face diagram
D_{j}
, which in this case this restriction is a WH polynomial of weight-type
(\bm{w};d_{j})
, and

satisfies:

\lim_{r\to 0}r^{-d_{j}}h^{j}(\pi(\bm{s},r),\varepsilon)=0.

Thus,
F_{\varepsilon}(\pi(\bm{s},r))=0

if and only if:

r^{d_{j}}(F_{0}^{\,j}(\bm{s})+(\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon})_{D_{j}}(\bm{s})+r^{-d_{j}}h^{j}(\pi(\bm{s},r),\varepsilon))=0,\ j=1,\dots,p

which implies
r=0
(corresponding to the origin) or:

f^{j}(\bm{s})+(\theta^{\,j}_{\varepsilon})_{D_{j}}(\bm{s})+r^{-d_{j}}h^{j}(\pi(\bm{s},r),\varepsilon)=0,\ j=1,\dots,p.

(22)

We describe (
22
) as the zero set of the map

H(\bm{s},r,\varepsilon):\rho^{-1}(1)\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{p},

which is a deformation with respect to the parameter
\varepsilon
(differently to the system (
18
) that we see as a deformation in the parameter
r
) of the map
H(\cdot,0):\rho^{-1}(1)\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
, with

It follows that:

\mathrm{rank}(J_{H}(\bm{s},r,0))=\mathrm{rank}(J_{f_{|\rho^{-1}(1)}}(\bm{s})).

Since
\Sigma(f)\cap V(f)=\{0\}
and
f
is WH, for any point
(\bm{s}_{0},0)
satisfying

, we find
\mathrm{rank}(J_{H}(\bm{s}_{0},0,0))=p.

Analogously to the case before we find
\rho_{\mathrm{min}}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{min}}
, such that for any
r\in(0,\rho_{\mathrm{min}})
fixed, there is an isotopy contained in
\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{r\}
, between the links

(\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{r\})\cap\pi^{-1}(V(F_{0}))\text{ and }(\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{r\})\cap\pi^{-1}(V(F_{\tau})),\ \tau\in[0,\tau_{\mathrm{min}}),

and by compose with

, we get an isotopy between

\pi(\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{r\})\cap V(F_{0})\text{ and }\pi(\rho^{-1}(1)\times\{r\})\cap V(F_{\tau}),\ \tau\in[0,\tau_{\mathrm{min}}).

(23)

As in Case I, we obtain an ambient isotopy between the links
L_{F_{0}}
and

.

Now, if the inequalities in (ii) are strict and

and

.

4.2  Inner non-degenerate mixed polynomial functions of two variables

Inspired also by
[
41
]
, the concept of inner non-degeneracy for mixed polynomial functions of two variables was introduced in
[
2
]
. Although that definition relies on weight vectors associated with the 1-faces of the Newton boundary and non-extreme vertices, we show in this section that it coincides with our own Definition
2.1
. Thus, we refer to a mixed polynomial function that satisfies either (and hence both) of these non-degeneracy conditions as an inner non-degenerate mixed polynomial function. In this section, we show that the inner Newton non-degeneracy defined in
[
2
]
is equivalent to our definition of inner Khovanskii non-degeneracy. Furthermore, we prove that the link of an inner non-degenerate mixed polynomial function depends only on certain monomials of the radial Newton boundary.

Consider the set of weight vectors

\mathcal{P}(f)=\{\bm{w}_{1},\dots,\bm{w}_{N}\}.

For each
\bm{w}_{i}
in
\mathcal{P}(f)
, define
k_{i}:=\frac{w_{i,1}}{w_{i,2}}
, the slope of the line passing through
\Delta(\bm{w}_{i};f)
. We order
\mathcal{P}(f)
so that
i<j
if and only if
k_{i}>k_{j}
.

The vertices of
\Gamma(f)
are classified as either
non-extreme
or
extreme
. A vertex
\Delta
is called
non-extreme
if there exits
\bm{w}_{j}\in\mathcal{P}(f)
such that
\Delta=\Delta(\bm{w}_{j};f)\cap\Delta(\bm{w}_{j+1};f)
, for consecutive weight vectors
\bm{w}_{j},\bm{w}_{j+1}\in\mathcal{P}(f)
. Otherwise,
\Delta
is called
extreme
.

Definition
\@upn
4.14
(
[
2
]
)

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a mixed polynomial function. We say that
f
is:

•

Inner Newton non-degenerate (INND)
if either:

(1)

\mathcal{P}(f)\neq\emptyset
, and:

(i)

\Sigma(f_{\bm{w}_{1}})\cap V(f_{\bm{w}_{1}})\cap(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{x_{2}=0\})=\emptyset
, and
\Sigma(f_{\bm{w}_{N}})\cap V(f_{\bm{w}_{N}})\cap(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{x_{1}=0\})=\emptyset
;

(ii)

for every 1-face or non-extreme vertex
\Delta
,
\Sigma(f_{\Delta})\cap V(f_{\Delta})\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}=\emptyset
;

(2)

\mathcal{P}(f)=\emptyset
, and
\Sigma(f_{\Delta})\cap V(f_{\Delta})\cap(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\})=\emptyset
, where
\Delta
is the unique face of
\Gamma(f)=\{\Delta\}
, a vertex.

•

(1)

\mathcal{P}(f)\neq\emptyset
, and:

\Sigma(f_{\bm{w}_{1}})\cap(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{x_{2}=0\})=\emptyset

Suppose that

. Then, by (

t_{0}
and unitary
z_{0}

G:\mathbb{R}^{5}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3},\,G(z,t,\varepsilon)=(p_{t}(z)+q_{t}(z,\varepsilon),z\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0muz\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu-1)

z_{0}&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

\det\begin{pmatrix}\frac{dp_{t_{0}}}{dz}(z_{0})&\frac{dp_{t_{0}}}{dt}(z_{0})\\

\varepsilon\in K

Remark 4.26

Fix a
C
-face diagram
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that any mixed polynomial function

f\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}

can be written in the form

f(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\bm{x}^{\bm{\nu}_{i}}\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mu\bm{x}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu^{\bm{\mu}_{i}}.

Then
\mathbb{C}^{m}
can be identified with the space of coefficients of mixed polynomial functions whose support is contained in
D
.
Define

U_{D}:=\bigl\{\bm{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\mid\text{the mixed polynomial }f_{\bm{c}}(\bm{x})=f(\bm{x},\bm{c})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\bm{x}^{\bm{\nu}_{i}}\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mu\bm{x}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu^{\bm{\mu}_{i}}\text{ is IKND and }\supp(f)\subseteq D\bigr\}.

Since the IKND condition is open, the set
U_{D}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
.
Now define

U_{D\text{-nice}}:=\bigl\{\bm{c}\in U_{D}\mid f_{\bm{c}}(\bm{x})\text{ is $D$-nice}\bigr\}.

are open subsets of

is not connected. In particular, there is no continuous path of IKND mixed polynomial functions, with respect to the fixed

-face diagram

-nice mixed polynomial function with a non

-nice one.

Proposition
\@upn
4.29

Let
f
be an IKND mixed polynomial function that is
\Gamma_{\rm inn}
-nice. Then, there is a link-constant piecewise analytic family
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}
, with
f_{0}=f
such that the link of
f_{1}
is isotopic to
\mathbf{L}([L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{N-1}],L_{N}),
where
L_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
,
L_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}\times S^{1},\ i=2,3,\dots,N-1
,
L_{N}\subset S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\rm inn}(f)=\{\bm{w}_{1},\dots,\bm{w}_{N}\}
. We distinguish two cases according to the value of
N
, namely
N>1
and
N=1
.

Case I:

N>1
.
The
C
-face diagram
\Gamma_{\rm inn}(f)
has at least two compact 1-faces. We write the variables in polar coordinates as
x_{1}=r_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{1}}
and
x_{2}=r_{2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{2}}
. For
f
and each weight vector
\bm{w}_{i}=(w_{i,1},w_{i,2})\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm inn}(f)
, we define the associated functions:

g_{i}(x_{1},\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{2}}):=\lim_{r_{2}\to 0}r_{2}^{-\frac{d(\bm{w}_{i};f)}{w_{i,2}}}f(r_{2}^{\,k_{i}}x_{1},r_{2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{2}})

where
i=1,2,\dots,N-1
, and
g_{i}:\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}\to\mathbb{C}
.
Similarly, we define

h_{N}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{1}},x_{2}):=\lim_{r_{1}\to 0}r_{1}^{-\frac{d(\bm{w}_{N};f)}{w_{N,1}}}f(r_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{1}},r_{1}^{\frac{1}{k_{N}}}x_{2})

where
h_{N}:S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
.

We now consider the following subsets:

\displaystyle L_{1}:=V(g_{1})\cap(\mathbb{C}\times S^{1})\subset\mathbb{C}\times S^{1},

\displaystyle L_{i}:=V(g_{i})\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*}\times S^{1})\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}\times S^{1},\ i\in\{2,3,\dots,N-1\},

\displaystyle L_{N}:=V(h_{N})\cap(S^{1}\times\mathbb{C})\subset S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}.

Since
f
is IKND and
\Gamma_{\rm inn}
-nice, each
L_{i}
is compact and smooth link in the corresponding ambient space. Applying the same arguments as in
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
, with
\mathcal{P}_{\rm inn}(f)
in place of
\mathcal{P}(f)
we conclude that the link
L_{f}
is isotopic to
\textbf{L}([L_{1},\dots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
. Therefore, in this case we may take the constant family
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}
, defined by
f_{\varepsilon}=f
for all
\varepsilon.

Case II:

N=1
. Suppose that
\text{supp}(f)\cap\Gamma_{\rm inn}(f)\cap\mathbb{R}^{\{1\}}=\emptyset
. We consider the family
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}
defined by
F(\bm{x},\varepsilon)=f+\varepsilon M(\bm{x}_{1})
, such that
\supp(M)=\{(m_{,}0)\}\subset\text{Int}(\Gamma_{\rm inn}(f)+(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{2}
).
By Theorem
4.18
, the deformation
F
is link-constant. Let
S_{\rm inn}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be such that
\Gamma(S_{\rm inn})=\Gamma_{\rm inn}(f)
, and define
D=\Gamma((S_{\rm inn}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{\{1\}})\cup\supp(M))
. Then
\mathcal{P}(D)=\{\bm{w}_{1},\bm{w}_{2}\}
. Since
f
is IKND, we can conclude that
f_{w_{2}}-M
is KND and that
\supp(f_{w_{2}}-M)=\{(m^{\prime},1)\}
. Using the same argument as in
[
2
, Corollary 4.11]
one proves that
\Sigma((f+M)_{\Delta(\bm{w}_{2};D)})\cap V((f+M)_{\Delta(\bm{w}_{2};D)})\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{I}=\emptyset
for
I=\{1\}
and
I=\{1,2\}
. Then,
f+M
is IKND with respect to
D
. Hence, applying the same argument as in Case I to
\mathcal{P}(D)
, we conclude that the link type of
f+M
has the desired form. Consequently,
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}
is a link-constant piecewise analytic family.

Suppose now that
\text{supp}(f)\cap\Gamma_{\rm inn}(f)\cap\mathbb{R}^{\{1\}}\not=\emptyset
. Let
M=f_{\Delta}
, where
\Delta=\Gamma_{\rm inn}(f)\cap\mathbb{R}^{\{1\}}=\{(a,0)\}
. Let
n=\deg_{x_{1}}(M(x_{1}))
. Then
M
can be written as
M(x_{1})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}x_{1}^{\,i}\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu^{a-i}
, where the coefficients
c_{i},\ i=0,\dots,n-1,
are (possibly zero). Equivalently,

M(x_{1})=\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu^{a}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu}\right)^{i}\right),

Hence,

M(x_{1})=c_{n}\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu^{a}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu}-\gamma_{i}\right),

where
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}
are the roots of the polynomial
\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu^{-d}M(x_{1})
in the variable
z=\frac{x_{1}}{\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu}
. Set
I=\{i\mid|\gamma_{i}|=1\}.
By Remark
4.22
-(ii), the polynomial
M
is KND if and only if
I=\emptyset
. If
M
is KND, then we may apply the same argument as in Case I with
\mathcal{P}_{\rm inn}(f)=\{\bm{w}_{1}\}
, obtaining that the link of
f
is isotopic to
\mathbf{L}([L_{1}])
. Then we take the constant family
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}
with
f_{\varepsilon}=f
for all
\varepsilon
. Assume now that
M
is not KND, that is,
I\not=\emptyset
. Suppose moreover that each
\gamma_{i}
,
i\in I
, is a simple root. Define

\theta(x_{1},\varepsilon)=\varepsilon\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}ic_{i}x_{1}^{\,i}\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu^{a-i}\right).

Applying the implicit function theorem to
\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu^{-a}(M(x_{1})+\theta(x_{1},\varepsilon))
(which is a complex function of variable
(\frac{x_{1}}{\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu},\varepsilon)
) at the point
(\gamma_{i},0)
, we deduce that
\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu^{-a}(M(x_{1})+\theta(x_{1},\varepsilon))
has no roots in the unit circle for all sufficiently small real
\varepsilon\not=0
. Consequently,
M(x_{1})+\theta(x_{1},\varepsilon)
is KND for all sufficiently small real
\varepsilon\neq 0
. By Theorem
4.18
, there exists a neighborhood
K\subset\mathbb{C}
of the origin such that the deformation
F(\bm{x},\varepsilon)=f(\bm{x})+\theta(x_{1},\varepsilon)
is link-constant along
K
. Let
\varepsilon_{1}\in K\cap\mathbb{R}
be sufficiently small so that
M(x_{1})+\theta(x_{1},\varepsilon_{1})
is KND. Then, the family
\{F_{\delta\varepsilon_{1}}\}_{\delta\in[0,1]}
is link-constant. Moreover,
F(\bm{x},\varepsilon_{1})=f(\bm{x})+\theta(x_{1},\varepsilon_{1})
is IKND with respect to
\Gamma_{\rm inn}(f)
. Applying the same argument as in Case I to
\mathcal{P}_{\rm inn}(f)=\{\bm{w}_{1}\}
, we conclude that the link type of
F_{\varepsilon_{1}}
has the desired form. Thus, we obtain a link-constant family
\{F_{\delta\varepsilon_{1}}\}_{\delta\in[0,1]}
.

Suppose that there exists
i\in I
such that
\gamma_{i}
is not a simple root. Since having only simple roots is an open property on the coefficients, there exists
\varepsilon_{2}\in K
sufficiently small such that
\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern 0.0mux_{1}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu^{-d}(M(x_{1})+\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))
viewed as a polynomial in the variable

\hat{\theta}_{\varepsilon}(x_{1})

Proposition

A link type

(\Rightarrow)

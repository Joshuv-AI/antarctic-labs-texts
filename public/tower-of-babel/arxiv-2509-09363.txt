1.
Introduction

The automorphisms of the cohomology algebra of the (complex) Grassmann manifold was first studied by Brewster in his Ph.D. thesis
[
2
]
. Michael Hoffman studied the endomorphisms of the cohomology of the complex Grassmmann manifold, in 1984, proving
[
10
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
h
be an endomorphism of
H^{*}(G_{k,n};\mathbb{Q})
with
h(c_{1})=mc_{1}
,
m\neq 0
. Then if
k<n
,
h(c_{i})=m^{i}c_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
.

If
k=n
, there is the additional possibility
h(c_{i})=(-m)^{i}(c^{-1})_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
,
where
(c^{-1})_{i}
is the
2i
-dimensional part of the inverse of
c=1+c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}
in
H^{*}(G_{k,n};\mathbb{Q})
.

He also conjectured that the only endomorphism of the cohomology algebra which maps the first Chern class to zero, is the zero endomorphism. This conjecture is still open.

Glover, Homer and Hoffman studied self-maps and cohomology automorphisms of certain flag manifolds in their papers (
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
). In 1987, Papadima
[
16
]
obtained results about the cohomology automorphisms of certain compact Lie group modulo their maximal torus (of the form
G/T
) and studied their rigidity properties. Around the same time, Shiga and Tezuka
[
17
]
also published a paper, which studies the cohomology automorphisms of some homogeneous spaces of the form
G/H
, where
G
is a simple Lie group (not of type of
D_{n}
) and
H
is a closed subgroup of maximal rank.

Between the years 2000 and 2009, Haibao Duan and his collaborators published a series of papers (
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
)
on the endomorphisms of cohomology of certain flag manifolds and

[

, Lin studied endomorphisms of cohomology which are induced from self-maps. Later, in 2019, Kaji and Theriault
[
13

investigated the collection of all self-maps (upto homotopy) of

, where

is a compact, connected Lie group and

a maximal torus.

We study the endomorphisms of the rational cohomology algebra of
M_{n,k}
, where
M_{n,k}
is the homogeneous space
SO(2n)/U(k)\times SO(2n-2k)
.
M_{n,k}
is a space of the type

, where

is the complex Lie Group

and

is a maximal parabolic subgroup. It is a compact Hermititan symmetric space and hence, a Kahler manifold.

The rational cohomology algebra is generated by certain cohomology classes, denoted by

,
1\leq i\leq k
;
p_{j}
,

; and

(provided

), which is analogous to Hoffman’s theorem

]
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider the space
M_{n,k}
, such that
n-k\neq k-1
.
Let
h:H^{*}(M_{n,k};\mathbb{Q})\to H^{*}(M_{n,k};\mathbb{Q})
be an endomorphism of the cohomology algebra
H^{*}(M_{n,k};\mathbb{Q})
, such that
h(c_{1})=mc_{1}
, where
m\neq 0
. Then,

\displaystyle h(c_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{i}c_{i};\hskip 14.22636pt1\leq i\leq k

\displaystyle h(p_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2j}p_{j};\hskip 14.22636pt1\leq j\leq n-k

\displaystyle h(e_{n-k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm m^{n-k}e_{n-k}

Let
T^{n}
be a maximal torus of
SO(2n)
. Then we also prove the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n-k\neq k-1
and let
h:H^{*}(M_{n,k};\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow H^{*}(M_{n,k};\mathbb{Q})
be an endomorphism of the cohomology algebra, such that
h(c_{1})=mc_{1}
, where
m\neq 0
.
Then, there exists
k!\times(n-k)!\times 2^{n-k}
endomorphisms

\tilde{h}:H^{*}(SO(2n)/T^{n};\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow H^{*}(SO(2n)/T^{n};\mathbb{Q})
, whose restriction to

gives the automorphism

.

Next, we study the properties of specific types of endomorphisms of
M_{n,k}
and prove:

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
h\colon H^{*}(M_{n,k};\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{*}(M_{n,k};\mathbb{Q})
be an endomorphism of the cohomology algebra

, which takes all Chern classes to zero (that is,
h(c_{i})=0
for all

). Then

is the zero endomorphism.

We have an analogous result (Proposition
5.1
) for the case where
h(p_{j})=0
for all

. Further, we prove the following:

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
h:H^{*}(M_{n,k};\mathbb{Q})\to H^{*}(M_{n,k};\mathbb{Q})
be an endomorphism of the cohomology algebra, such that
h(c_{1})=0
, where

denotes the first Chern class. Let

be the polynomial ring, and let

be the ideal generated by polynomials which are invariant under the action of the Weyl group of

which restricts to

\tilde{h}
is the zero endomorphism.
In such a case,

will be the zero endomorphism of
H^{*}(M_{n,k};\mathbb{Q})
.

We have analogous results when the image of

is zero (Proposition

), and also when

and the image of

is zero (Proposition

The paper is arranged as follows. In the next section we describe the homogeneous space

We calculate a couple of lower dimensional examples, for the sake of illustration, and in the final section, we work with the Lefschetz number of type of maps mentioned in Theorem
1.2

2.
The Homogeneous Space
M_{n,k}

The space
SO(2n)/U(n)
, known as the Grassmannian of complex structures, is a well-studied space. It is a compact, complex manifold, and a Hermitian symmetric space. (
[
3
]
,
[
9
]
)

We begin by giving an explicit description of this space. Let
\mathbb{J}
be the collection of all complex structures on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, that is,
\mathbb{J}
is the collection of all linear maps
J\colon\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, such that
J^{2}=-I
. We define an action of
SO(2n)
on
\mathbb{J}
given by
(M,J)\mapsto MJM^{-1}
.

Given any two complex structures
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, there exists an orthonormal
J_{1}
-basis
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n},J_{1}e_{1},\cdots,,J_{1}e_{n}\}
and an orthonormal
J_{2}
-basis
\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{n},J_{2}f_{1},\cdots,J_{2}f_{n}\}
. There also exists a linear transformation
S\colon\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
such that
S(e_{k})=f_{k}
and
S(J_{1}e_{k})=J_{2}f_{k}
for each
k
with
1\leq k\leq n
. Since
S(J_{1}e_{k})=J_{2}f_{k}
for each
k
, we have
SJ_{1}S^{-1}=J_{2}
. Since
S
takes an orthomormal
J_{1}
-basis to an orthomormal
J_{2}
-basis, preserving orientation,

, the action of
SO(2n)
on
\mathbb{J}
is transitive.

Consider the standard complex structure
J_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-I_{n}\\
I_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. Let
H
be the stabilizer of the given action of
SO(2n)
at
J_{0}
, that is,
H
=
\{M\in SO(2n)\mid J_{0}M=MJ_{0}\}
. Then, if
M
is an element of
H
,
MJ_{0}=J_{0}M
implies that
M
is of the form

\begin{bmatrix}A&-B\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}
, where
A
and
B
are
n\times n
matrices. Conversely, any matrix in

, of the form
\begin{bmatrix}A&-B\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}
, where
A,B

\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, belongs to the stabilizer of
J_{0}
, . Therefore, we have,
H
=
\{\begin{bmatrix}A&-B\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}\mid A,B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})\}
.

can be identified with
U(n)
, since
U(n)
can be embedded in
SO(2n)
, via the following embedding:

B&A\end{bmatrix}
, where
A,B

\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. Hence, we have shown that the collection of all complex structures on

can be identified with

.

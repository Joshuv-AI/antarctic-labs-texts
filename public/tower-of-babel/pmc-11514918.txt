N6-Methyladenosine methylation modification in breast cancer: current insights
Authors: Guangwen Zhang, Chen Cheng, Xinle Wang, Shiming Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Breast cancer is the most common cancer type among women. Despite advanced treatment strategies, some patients still face challenges in disease control, prompting the exploration of new therapeutic approaches. N6-Methyladenosine (m6A) methylation modification regulates RNA and plays a crucial role in various tumor biological processes, closely linked to breast cancer occurrence, development, prognosis, and treatment. M6A regulators impact breast cancer progression, development, and drug resistance by modulating RNA metabolism and tumor-related pathways. Researchers have begun to understand the regulatory mechanisms of m6A methylation in breast cancer. This paper discusses the roles of m6A regulators in breast cancer progression, prognosis, and treatment, offering new perspectives for breast cancer diagnosis and treatment.

Introduction

In 2022, with over 2.31 million new cases, breast cancer became the second most common type of cancer after lung cancer, the most common cancer among women, and the leading cause of cancer-related deaths in women [1]. Current systemic treatments for breast cancer include surgical resection, local radiotherapy, systemic chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, and molecular targeted therapy [2]. Despite relatively advanced treatment strategies, some patients experience progression towards more aggressive disease, and some develop resistance to first-line therapeutic drugs [3]. Therefore, ongoing updates to treatment strategies for breast cancer are necessary.

N6-Methyladenosine (m6A) methylation modification is widely present in the modifications of eukaryotic mRNA, lncRNA, and miRNA [4]. It influences mRNA processing, maturation, splicing, transport, translation, and degradation [5]. M6A can regulate functional proteins such as metabolic enzymes, transporters, and transcription factors that promote the progression of malignant tumors, playing a crucial role in the proliferation, differentiation, and metastasis of various malignancies [6, 7]. m6A modification primarily involves three types of regulators: “writers” (methyltransferase complex), “erasers” (demethylases), and “readers” (recognition proteins). This modification is a dynamic and reversible process. The “writers” include METTL3, METTL14, WTAP, RBM15, KIAA1429, and ZC3H13; the “erasers” mainly consist of ALKBH5 and FTO; the “readers” are primarily composed of YTHDF1/2/3, YTHDC1/2, IGF2BP1/2/3, HNRNPA2B1, and HNRNPC [5, 8]. This article will review the latest progress in the biological functions of m6A regulators related to breast cancer, aiming to provide new insights for the diagnosis and treatment of breast cancer.

Methods

To compose this review article, we undertook a comprehensive literature search focusing on m6A in the context of breast cancer. The selection process involved the following steps:

Database search: we utilized multiple academic databases, including PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science, to identify relevant articles. The search was conducted using keywords such as “N6-methyladenosine,” “m6A,” “breast cancer,” and “epigenetics.”

Inclusion criteria: we included studies published in peer-reviewed journals from the inception of m6A research in breast cancer (approximately 2012) until the present. Only articles that specifically focused on the role of m6A in breast cancer were considered.

Timeframe of research: the exploration of m6A in the breast cancer field began in earnest around 2012, gaining traction with increasing publications over the years. We specifically noted a surge in research interest over the last 5 years, particularly after 2018, coinciding with advancements in epigenetic therapies and the recognition of m6A’s role in tumor biology.

Popularity and trends: to assess the popularity of m6A research over the past 5 years, we conducted a web search in the PubMed database and analyzed publication trends (Fig. 1). Effect of m6A regulators on breast cancer

Writers are responsible for transferring and installing methyl groups onto RNA to achieve methylation. METTL3 transfers the methyl donor S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to the adenine base [13]. METTL14 stabilizes the methyltransferase complex (MTC) structure and recognizes RNA sequences [14]. Although WTAP lacks catalytic activity, its knockdown affects m6A methylation efficiency [15]. ZC3H13 bridges the RNA-binding factor RBM15 to WTAP [16]. KIAA1429 (VIRMA) mediates methylation of the 3′-untranslated region (UTR) and regions near stop codons [17]. RBM15 directly binds to U-rich sequences and recruits the MTC to the mRNA [18].

Erasers are capable of removing methyl groups from m6A-modified RNA and reversing methylation. ALKBH5 is primarily located in the nucleus and selectively removes m6A modifications, accelerating the export of mRNA from the nucleus to the cytoplasm [19]. In contrast, FTO is distributed in both the nucleus and the cytoplasm, but it targets different substrates compared to ALKBH5 [20].

Readers are selective RNA-binding proteins primarily responsible for recognizing m6A modifications to activate downstream pathways. YTHDF1 binds to mRNA and promotes its translation [21]. YTHDF2 mediates RNA decay upon binding to mRNA [22]. YTHDC1, the only known YTH family protein in the nucleus, is involved in exon selection during gene splicing [23]. Additionally, YTHDC1 can bind to nuclear RNA export factor 1 (NXF1) to mediate mRNA export to the cytoplasm. YTHDC2, an RNA helicase, facilitates RNA binding through its helicase domain and collaborates with YTHDF3 to promote RNA translation and decay [24, 25]. The IGF2BP family proteins (including IGF2BP1/2/3), located in the cytoplasm, primarily recognize the GGC sequence and enhance mRNA stability [26]. HNRNPA2B1 directly binds and regulates m6A modifications by interacting with the miRNA microprocessor complex [27], while HNRNPC functions by recognizing m6A-induced secondary structures. eIF3, a reader of 5′-UTR m6A, interacts with m6A-modified RNA to enhance ribosome loading, thereby promoting the translation of target mRNAs [28]. Other readers, such as FMRP and PRRC2A, are still under investigation, and their mechanisms remain unclear.

The role of N6-methyladenosine modification in the tumor microenvironment

The tumor microenvironment (TME) plays a critical role in the growth and survival of breast cancer cells, comprising various cellular and molecular components that promote tumor cell proliferation, angiogenesis, migration, drug resistance, and immune suppression [29]. The TME not only provides essential conditions for the survival of breast cancer cells but also significantly influences tumor progression, immune evasion, and responses to immunotherapy [30]. Recent studies have demonstrated that m6A regulators have a profound impact on gene expression, breast cancer-related signaling pathways, and TME dynamics, thereby affecting the development and progression of breast cancer.

m6A modifications play pivotal roles in key interactions within the breast cancer TME. First, m6A regulators enhance the immune evasion capabilities of breast cancer cells by regulating the expression of immune checkpoint proteins such as PD-L1 [31]. This mechanism not only aids breast cancer cells in evading host immune system attacks but also leads to the functional suppression of immune cells within the TME, allowing the tumor to thrive in a more immunosuppressive environment. Research has indicated that elevated levels of PD-L1 expression in breast cancer cells are closely associated with increased expression of METTL3 and IGF2BP3, providing new avenues for targeted immunotherapy against immune checkpoints in breast cancer [32].

Second, m6A regulators also regulate the expression of pro-angiogenic factors, thereby facilitating blood vessel formation within the breast cancer TME [33]. This angiogenic process is crucial for the growth and metastasis of breast cancer, as newly formed blood vessels supply the necessary nutrients and oxygen to rapidly proliferating tumors. Furthermore, m6A regulators can influence breast cancer cells’ responses to various microenvironmental components within the TME, enabling them to adapt and exploit these elements, thereby enhancing their survival and competitive capabilities.

Finally, m6A regulators alter the interactions between breast cancer cells and stromal components, affecting the composition and functionality of the TME. One study suggests that these changes not only alter the supportive role of the stroma for breast cancer cells but also enhance their migratory and invasive capabilities, thereby increasing the potential for brain metastasis [34]. This complex interplay positions m6A regulators as significant regulators within the breast cancer microenvironment, and their study may yield novel therapeutic targets and strategies for breast cancer treatment.

Consequently, m6A regulators influence immune evasion, angiogenesis, and stromal interactions, playing multifaceted roles within the TME of breast cancer. These factors have profound implications for tumor growth and metastasis, and future research into these mechanisms may provide new strategies and targets for anti-breast cancer therapies.

The role of N6-methyladenosine modification in breast cancer

Writers in breast cancer

METTL3

Methyltransferase like 3 (METTL3) is the most extensively studied methylation regulatory protein, playing a crucial role in breast cancer progression. It primarily participates in mRNA biogenesis, decay, and translational control through m6A methylation modification [35]. As an essential component of the MTC, METTL3 has been identified as a potential therapeutic target in breast cancer via the METTL3–mTOR axis [36]. One of the key mechanisms by which METTL3 promotes breast cancer progression is through the activation of the YAP/TAZ pathway within the Hippo signaling cascade, as evidenced by both in vivo and in vitro studies [37]. This activation leads to enhanced cellular proliferation and survival, contributing to tumor growth and metastasis. Additionally, METTL3 plays a role in endoplasmic reticulum autophagy by promoting the m6A modification of autophagy regulators and p62, which further facilitates breast cancer progression [38]. In HR+HER2− breast cancer, the depletion of METTL3 has been shown to suppress tumor progression by regulating the CDKN1A/EMT axis. In vitro cell culture experiments indicate that this regulation promotes cellular proliferation and migration while reducing drug sensitivity through the m6A-BAX/caspase-9/-3/-8 signaling pathway [39]. This suggests that METTL3 is integral to maintaining the aggressive characteristics of HR+HER2− breast cancer cells. In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), METTL3’s role is even more pronounced. It has been found to significantly promote metastasis, with high METTL3 expression levels correlating with poor prognosis [40]. This finding has been corroborated in a TNBC lung metastasis cell line constructed by Feng Chen et al., highlighting the protein’s role in enhancing metastatic potential [41]. Therefore, METTL3 functions as an oncogenic initiator in breast cancer, and its knockout has been shown to reduce methylation levels, thereby inhibiting tumor progression. This makes METTL3 a promising target for therapeutic intervention, offering potential pathways to mitigate the aggressive behavior of various breast cancer subtypes through targeted epigenetic regulation.

METTL14

Methyltransferase like 14 (METTL14) is a crucial RNA methyltransferase that plays a significant role in tumor growth by regulating RNA expression. It is a core component of the m6A MTC and is integral to the dynamic and reversible process of m6A modification [42]. Both METTL3 and METTL14 possess methyltransferase activity, forming a complex that catalyzes the m6A methylation process [43]. In vitro cell experiments demonstrated that silencing the long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) UCA1 inhibits the DNA methylation of METTL14, which in turn promotes the m6A methylation of miR-375. This leads to decreased SOX12 expression and ultimately inhibits the proliferation and invasion of breast cancer cells [44]. These findings have been further validated in in vivo animal models. METTL14 is found to be significantly elevated in breast cancer tissues. Its overexpression or inhibition directly correlates with the invasion ability of tumor cells [45]. Further research indicates that the abnormal expression of METTL14 contributes to breast cancer progression by influencing pathways related to tumor growth or by mediating immune suppression [46]. These pathways include critical regulatory mechanisms that control cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis, as well as immune evasion strategies employed by cancer cells. In summary, METTL14 is significantly elevated in breast cancer tissues and substantially influences tumor cell proliferation and invasion. This occurs through the regulation of m6A methylation and the modulation of tumor progression-related pathways or immune suppression. The critical role of METTL14 in these processes makes it a potential target for therapeutic interventions aimed at mitigating breast cancer progression.

WTAP

Wilms’ tumor 1-associating protein (WTAP) is a crucial regulatory subunit of the MTC that bridges METTL3 and METTL14, ensuring the proper localization and function of this dimer. Research has demonstrated that WTAP can modulate several downstream targets, including EGR3, HK2, ETS1, and CAV-1. Through these interactions, WTAP significantly impacts the MAPK, AKT, and WNT signaling pathways, which are critical for promoting tumor progression [47–50]. Additionally, WTAP has been shown to enhance the m6A methylation of Bcl-2 mRNA in breast cancer, a modification that induces apoptosis, highlighting its role in programmed cell death [51]. Furthermore, WTAP contributes to breast cancer progression by stabilizing EXOSC2 mRNA. This stabilization is achieved through WTAP’s binding to the m6A site within the 3′-UTR of EXOSC2 mRNA, thus ensuring the mRNA’s stability and continued translation [52]. Both of the aforementioned research findings have been validated through in vitro cell experiments. Overall, WTAP serves as a pivotal factor in breast cancer progression. By regulating downstream targets and facilitating mRNA m6A methylation, WTAP influences several critical signaling pathways associated with tumor development and metastasis. The insights into WTAP’s multifaceted roles underline its potential as a therapeutic target.

RBM15/RBM15B

RNA-binding motif protein 15 (RBM15) and RNA-binding motif protein 15B (RBM15B) are RNA-binding proteins that play crucial roles in the regulation of RNA methylation and gene expression [27]. They are involved in various cellular processes, including hematopoiesis as well as cancer progression. RBM15, also known as OTT1, is involved in the regulation of RNA splicing, polyadenylation, and transcriptional elongation. It interacts with the m6A MTC and facilitates the methylation of specific RNA substrates [53]. RBM15 interacts with the METTL3/METTL14 dimer via WTAP and is integral to the MTC, where knockdown of RBM15 expression significantly reduces overall m6A levels [18]. A study confirmed that RBM15 promotes the growth of basal-like breast cancer cells and tumor progression by regulating m6A methylation and influencing genes related to serine and glycine metabolism through in vitro cell experiments and in vivo animal models [54]. RBM15B, a paralog of RBM15, shares similar functions in RNA metabolism. It also interacts with components of the m6A MTC, contributing to RNA methylation and the regulation of gene expression. RBM15B emerges as a critical independent protective prognostic factor in uveal melanoma, highlighting its potential roles in the TME and clinical outcomes [55]. Research on RBM15B in breast cancer remains relatively limited; however, preliminary studies suggest its potential role in the development and progression of the disease. Specifically, RBM15B may regulate gene expression through m6A methylation, thereby influencing the proliferation, invasion, and metastasis capabilities of breast cancer cells. Therefore, RBM15 and RBM15B regulate downstream metabolic genes by interacting with WTAP, METTL3, and METTL14, thereby facilitating the progression of breast cancer.

KIAA1429

KIAA1429, also known as Vir-like m6A methyltransferase associated (VIRMA), is an important component of the MTC. KIAA1429 plays a pivotal role in modulating RNA stability and gene expression by regulating m6A modification, which in turn promotes the proliferation and invasion of tumor cells across various cancers [56]. In breast cancer tissues, KIAA1429 is significantly upregulated and interacts with the m6A-binding protein IGF2BP3 to stabilize HAS2 mRNA, a finding validated through in vitro cell experiments and animal models. This interaction significantly enhances the proliferation, migration, and invasion of breast cancer cells and is correlated with poor patient prognosis [57]. Moreover, KIAA1429 has been shown to promote breast cancer cell proliferation and metastasis by regulating CDK1 expression in an m6A-independent manner, which impacts overall patient survival and positions KIAA1429 as a potential therapeutic target [58]. Additionally, KIAA1429, functioning as an RNA-binding protein, stabilizes SMC1A mRNA and upregulates SNAIL expression, further facilitating the migration, invasion, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) of breast cancer cells, thereby significantly promoting metastasis, which has been validated both in vitro and in vivo [59]. In summary, KIAA1429 exerts a profound influence on breast cancer progression by regulating RNA methylation and stabilizing key mRNAs. Its high expression levels are typically associated with increased tumor malignancy and poor patient outcomes.

ZC3H13

Zinc finger CCCH-type containing 13 (ZC3H13), a protein containing a zinc finger domain, plays a pivotal role in the regulation of m6A methylation modification. It functions as an essential component of the m6A MTC, localizing to the nucleus and regulating gene expression [60]. Studies have demonstrated that ZC3H13 acts as a tumor suppressor gene and is downregulated in breast cancer. This downregulation is associated with poor prognosis and promotes breast cancer invasion by influencing tumor progression-related pathways and mediating immune suppression [46]. Additionally, ZC3H13 is notably downregulated in TNBC, potentially contributing to the aggressiveness of this subtype and the enhanced activity of macrophages [61]. Overall, ZC3H13, as an m6A methylation regulator, plays a critical role in the development and progression of breast cancer. Its low expression levels are linked to increased malignancy and worse clinical outcomes. Ongoing research continues to delve into the specific mechanisms by which ZC3H13 exerts its effects and its potential as a target for therapeutic intervention.

In summary, a variety of writers within the m6A regulatory network combine to form MTC that enhance m6A methylation modifications. Dysregulation of these factors’ expression in breast cancer contributes to the promotion of cancer progression by modulating diverse downstream molecules and signaling pathways. This multifaceted influence ultimately impacts patient prognosis.

Erasers in breast cancer

FTO

Fat mass and obesity-associated protein (FTO) is a significant predictor of obesity and was the first m6A demethylase identified. It plays a crucial role in regulating fatty acid transport, lipogenesis, and fat metabolism [62]. Its association with cancer progression was first reported in 2017. FTO expression is dysregulated in a variety of malignancies, including acute myeloid leukemia (AML), gastric cancer, cervical cancer, ovarian cancer, and breast cancer [63]. As a pivotal m6A demethylase, FTO’s expression levels vary across different breast cancer subtypes, but its dysregulation is consistently linked to poor prognosis. In HER2-positive breast cancer, FTO is highly expressed and facilitates tumor progression through the activation of the miR-181b-3p/ARL5B signaling pathway, with similar conclusions drawn from in vitro experiments [64]. Contrastingly, in TNBC lung metastasis models established by Feng Chen et al., FTO expression was found to be reduced [41], a finding corroborated by the research of Shanshan Wang et al. [65]. Furthermore, research by Yi Niu et al. demonstrated that BNIP3, a tumor suppressor gene, can significantly counteract FTO-dependent tumor growth and metastasis. This indicates that FTO’s activity contributes to breast cancer progression, and targeting FTO could represent a novel therapeutic strategy [66]. Qu et al. demonstrated through in vivo and in vitro experiments that senescent neutrophils enhance the expression of FTO in breast cancer cells by secreting exosomal piRNA-17560, thereby contributing to the chemoresistance of breast cancer cells [67]. These findings underscore the complex role of FTO in breast cancer biology. While its overexpression is associated with aggressive disease phenotypes and poor outcomes in HER2-positive breast cancers, its reduced expression in TNBC suggests a context-dependent function. Therefore, a deeper understanding of FTO’s regulatory mechanisms and its interactions with other molecular pathways is essential for developing targeted therapies aimed at modulating its activity in breast cancer.

ALKBH5

AlkB homolog 5 (ALKBH5) is a pivotal m6A demethylase involved in gene transcription, translation, and metabolism, making it a significant molecular marker in cancer research [68]. Studies have demonstrated that ALKBH5 can selectively regulate the function of AML hematopoietic stem cells under hypoxic conditions without impacting normal hematopoietic stem cells [69]. This underscores the importance of m6A epitranscriptomic reprogramming in shaping the transcriptome and proteome under hypoxia [70]. In breast cancer, hypoxia-induced mRNA and protein expression is mediated through a HIF- and ALKBH5-dependent pathway, enhancing the cancer stem cell phenotype [71]. Specifically, ALKBH5-mediated demethylation of GLUT4 mRNA has been shown to induce resistance to HER2-targeted therapies in breast cancer, with high ALKBH5 expression correlating with poor prognosis, a finding that has been validated through both in vitro and in vivo [72]. Further research has revealed that ALKBH5-mediated m6A demethylation promotes chemotherapy resistance in TNBC. This resistance is particularly pronounced due to increased stability of FOXO1 mRNA, which alters cellular redox balance, sustains cancer stem cell properties, and confers resistance to doxorubicin [73]. The role of ALKBH5 in breast cancer extends beyond its function as an RNA demethylase. It significantly influences tumor cell proliferation, invasion, and metabolic regulation. The expression levels of ALKBH5 are intricately linked to the pathogenesis, progression, and clinical outcomes of breast cancer patients.

In summary, erasers among the m6A regulators play a crucial role in the progression of breast cancer. These erasers, such as FTO and ALKBH5, significantly influence various aspects of tumor biology, including cell proliferation, invasion, and metabolic regulation. Their dysregulation is often associated with poor prognosis and therapeutic resistance. A deeper understanding of the mechanisms by which these m6A erasers operate could lead to the development of more effective targeted therapies, potentially improving outcomes for breast cancer patients. Future research should focus on elucidating the complex interactions between m6A modifications and cancer pathways to uncover novel therapeutic strategies.

Readers in breast cancer

YTHDF1

YTH domain-containing family protein 1 (YTHDF1) dysregulation plays a critical role in various cancers, impacting patient prognosis and therapeutic strategies. It has been identified as a pivotal player in cancer progression across multiple cancer types, including ovarian, colorectal, non-small cell lung, and gastric cancers [74–78]. In breast cancer, YTHDF1 exhibits upregulated expression and is recognized as an oncogenic driver [79, 80]. Functionally, YTHDF1 exerts its influence through modulation of RNA metabolism and interactions within the TME, thereby influencing tumor progression and responses to immunotherapy [81]. Within the TME of breast cancer, several signaling axes involving YTHDF1 have been elucidated, such as YTHDF1/SPRED2/ERK/NF-kB-STAT3 [82], METTL3/ARHGAP5/YTHDF1 [83], and YTHDF1/FOXM1 [84], underscoring its multifaceted roles in disease pathogenesis. Under conditions of tumor hypoxia, overexpression of YTHDF1 in vitro can upregulate PKM2 expression, thereby enhancing tumor glycolysis and promoting tumorigenesis and metastasis [79]. Moreover, YTHDF1 facilitates cancer cell progression through promoting S phase entry, DNA replication, and DNA damage repair mechanisms. Targeting YTHDF1 has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy in breast cancer, particularly in sensitizing cancer cells to DNA-targeting chemotherapeutic agents like doxorubicin, cisplatin, and PARP inhibitors such as olaparib [80]. Thus, a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying YTHDF1 dysregulation in breast cancer could lead to the development of more effective treatment modalities aimed at improving patient outcomes.

YTHDF2

YTH domain-containing family protein 2 (YTHDF2), identified as one of the earliest m6A readers, plays a pivotal role in innate immune suppression [85], and is intricately involved in mRNA metabolism, influencing various physiological processes in malignancies [86]. Notably, diminished YTHDF2 expression correlates with extended patient survival rates, prompting exploration of YTHDF2 depletion as a prospective therapeutic approach, supported by animal model findings [87]. Within the TME, the METTL3–LATS1–YTHDF2 axis has emerged as a critical regulator, impacting breast cancer progression through modulation of YAP/TAZ phosphorylation levels within the Hippo pathway [37]. The expression and biological functions of m6A proteins have been confirmed in breast cancer cells both in vitro and in vivo. Moreover, YTHDF2 facilitates breast cancer cell homing and osteoclastogenesis, thereby fostering immune evasion and promoting susceptibility to bone metastasis [88]. In breast cancer, the downregulation of YTHDF2 expression in vitro leads to elevated ATF3 protein levels, consequently resulting in tamoxifen resistance [89]. Conversely, increased YTHDF2 expression stabilizes KDM1A mRNA, thereby influencing cell proliferation and the cell cycle dynamics of breast cancer cells [90]. These insights underscore YTHDF2 as a promising therapeutic target for future strategies aimed at combating breast cancer. YTHDF3

YTH domain-containing family protein 3 (YTHDF3) plays a crucial role in the prognosis of breast cancer patients [91] and serves as an independent prognostic indicator for overall survival in this population [92]. The expression levels of YTHDF3 are closely associated with immune infiltration, with overexpression leading to the suppression of immune cell function and efficiency in the antitumor immune response [93]. In the context of TNBC, YTHDF3 enhances the stability of ZEB1 mRNA, which, in turn, promotes the migration, invasion, and EMT of TNBC cells [94]. This regulatory mechanism has been validated through in vitro experiments, highlighting the aggressive characteristics of TNBC and emphasizing the potential of YTHDF3 as a therapeutic target. Moreover, YTHDF3 is notably involved in the brain metastasis of breast cancer. Within the brain microenvironment, the overexpression of YTHDF3 in a breast cancer mouse model facilitates interactions between cancer cells and brain endothelial cells as well as astrocytes. This interaction promotes the extravasation of cancer cells across the blood–brain barrier and supports angiogenesis, thereby aiding the metastatic process [34]. Given these multifaceted roles, YTHDF3 emerges as a critical molecule in breast cancer pathophysiology, influencing tumor progression, immune evasion, and metastatic potential. Targeting YTHDF3 could offer novel therapeutic avenues, particularly for aggressive subtypes like TNBC and in preventing or treating brain metastases.

YTHDC1

YTH domain-containing protein 1 (YTHDC1) is a critical regulator involved in the nuclear export, transcription, splicing, and RNA stability of methylated transcripts [95]. Despite its downregulated expression in TNBC [65], YTHDC1 plays a pivotal role in promoting TNBC cell proliferation and invasion [96]. Recent studies have demonstrated that YTHDC1 influences TNBC progression and glycolytic processes by regulating the m6A modification levels of FOXM1, a transcription factor known to drive oncogenic activities [97]. This highlights the intricate mechanisms by which YTHDC1 contributes to the aggressive nature of TNBC. Moreover, YTHDC1 has been shown to facilitate the nuclear export and expression of SMAD3 in an m6A-dependent manner in TNBC cells. This process amplifies the TGF-β signaling cascade, which is crucial for cancer metastasis, thereby promoting the spread of TNBC cells to the lungs [98]. These findings have been validated through in vitro experiments, underscoring the role of YTHDC1 in the metastatic behavior of TNBC and suggesting a potential therapeutic target for lung metastases prevention and treatment in TNBC patients. The multifaceted functions of YTHDC1 in m6A RNA methylation underscore its importance in cancer biology. Targeting YTHDC1 could provide novel therapeutic strategies, particularly for aggressive cancers like TNBC, by disrupting key pathways involved in tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis.

YTHDC2

Similar to the expression pattern of YTHDC1, YTH domain-containing protein 2 (YTHDC2) is also significantly downregulated in TNBC [65]. Lin Yang et al. have identified YTHDC2 as a characteristic m6A regulator in basal-like breast cancer [99]. Basal-like breast cancer is unique in its gene expression, copy number variations, and DNA methylation profiles, with 70%–80% overlap in biological and pathological characteristics with TNBC [100]. This significant overlap suggests that YTHDC2 could be an important molecular player in the pathogenesis of TNBC. Given its downregulation in TNBC, YTHDC2 may have a role in tumor suppression, and its expression levels could provide valuable insights into the aggressiveness and progression of the disease. Additionally, YTHDC2’s involvement in m6A RNA methylation processes further highlights its potential as a crucial biomarker.

IGF2BP1/2/3

The insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein (IGF2BP) family comprises a group of m6A readers that play a crucial role in the post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression. They are involved in key processes such as mRNA stabilization, localization, and translational control, and are essential in both embryonic development and the pathogenesis of various diseases, including cancer [101]. This family primarily includes the key members IGF2BP1, IGF2BP2, and IGF2BP3. Research has demonstrated that the IGF2BP family regulates the progression of several malignancies, including breast cancer, pancreatic cancer, renal cancer, and colorectal cancer [102–105]. Each member of this family contributes distinct yet overlapping functions in cancer biology. IGF2BP1, in particular, has been implicated in promoting tumor proliferation and metastasis in breast cancer by stabilizing and regulating the translation of oncogenic mRNAs [106]. Its overexpression is often associated with a poor prognosis in breast cancer patients. In breast cancer, IGF2BP1 can synergize with YTHDF1, METTL3, and FTO to enhance the stability of the KRT7-AS/KRT7 mRNA duplex, while also promoting the translation of KRT7, ultimately facilitating breast cancer lung metastasis [41]. These findings have been validated in vivo. IGF2BP2, another key member, is a critical RNA-binding protein involved in the stabilization, localization, and translational regulation of various mRNAs [107]. Notably, a study has shown through both in vivo and in vitro experiments that IGF2BP2 stabilizes DROSHA mRNA, which is modified by m6A methylation, thereby supporting the stem cell properties of breast cancer [108]. This stabilization is crucial for maintaining the stemness and proliferative capacity of breast cancer stem cells. IGF2BP3 is also a significant RNA-binding protein with pivotal roles in various biological processes, particularly in oncogenesis [109]. It has been shown to stabilize CERS6 mRNA, thereby promoting cell proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis [110]. Additionally, IGF2BP3 collaborates with METTL3 to enhance the stability of PD-L1 mRNA, a mechanism that allows cancer cells to evade immune surveillance by increasing PD-L1 expression [31]. Overall, the IGF2BP family members are integral to the complex network of post-transcriptional regulation that underpins cancer progression. Their roles in stabilizing key mRNAs and modulating their translation underscore their potential as therapeutic targets in breast cancer.

HNRNPA2B1

Heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein A2/B1 (HNRNPA2B1) is an important RNA-binding protein involved in regulating gene expression and pre-mRNA splicing. Its involvement in the pathogenesis and progression of various cancers, including breast cancer, has been well-documented, with aberrant regulation of HNRNPA2B1 being closely linked to tumor development and advancement [111]. In estrogen-dependent breast cancer, HNRNPA2B1 functions as a splicing silencer for Bcl-xS, with consistent conclusions drawn from both in vivo and in vitro studies [112]. This activity is crucial for mitigating uncontrolled cell proliferation that results from the upregulation of Bcl-xS, thereby contributing to the regulation of cellular growth dynamics in these cancer cells. Furthermore, HNRNPA2B1 has been shown to interact with the dietary flavonoid apigenin, which is known for its anti-cancer properties. This interaction significantly enhances the chemosensitivity of TNBC cells to doxorubicin, a commonly used chemotherapeutic agent. The mechanism behind this increased sensitivity involves the upregulation of efflux transporters and the induction of apoptosis, leading to more effective drug action and cancer cell death [113]. Overall, HNRNPA2B1’s multifaceted role in gene regulation and its interactions with therapeutic compounds underscore its potential as a target for breast cancer treatment, particularly in strategies aimed at overcoming drug resistance and improving the efficacy of chemotherapy.

HNRNPC

Heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein C (HNRNPC) is a ribonucleoprotein primarily involved in the regulation of mRNA splicing within the cell nucleus. It is highly expressed in breast cancer tissues and is associated with poor prognosis. The role of HNRNPC in breast cancer is multifaceted and complex, with evidence suggesting both inhibitory and promoting effects on tumorigenesis depending on the cellular context. In breast cancer cell lines such as MCF7 and T47D, HNRNPC exerts an inhibitory effect on cell proliferation and tumor formation through its modulation of the interferon response induced by double-stranded RNA (dsRNA), highlighting its role in immune response regulation [114]. However, its effects can be contradictory in other breast cancer cell lines [115]. Mechanistically, a study has validated both in vivo and in vitro that HNRNPC can recognize and bind to m6A modifications on TFAP2A mRNA to enhance the transcription of DDR1, as identified by VIRMA [116]. This interaction promotes the organization of collagen fibers, contributing to changes in the extracellular matrix that facilitate the TME. Consequently, these alterations lead to a decrease in anti-tumor immune cells and contribute to immune evasion mechanisms in breast cancer. As a critical RNA-binding protein at the crossroads of cancer progression and innate immune responses, HNRNPC’s role underscores the need for further research.

In summary, as the most extensive family of m6A regulators, the reader family proteins, along with their key target genes, present a promising frontier for the early diagnosis and precision treatment of breast cancer. These m6A readers play a critical role in regulating mRNA stability, localization, and translation, and their dysregulation has been implicated in various aspects of breast cancer progression. By targeting these proteins and their associated pathways, researchers and clinicians have the potential to develop innovative diagnostic tools and therapeutic strategies. Targeting m6A readers could provide valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying breast cancer and offer new avenues for personalized medicine. For example, specific inhibitors or modulators of m6A readers could be designed to correct aberrant gene expression patterns associated with cancer. Furthermore, the identification of unique m6A reader profiles in breast cancer could lead to the development of novel biomarkers for early detection and prognosis.

Discussion and future perspectives

In recent years, N6-methyladenosine has emerged as a significant research hotspot, with its regulatory roles in cancer gaining increasing attention. Current research highlights the deep involvement of m6A regulators in the TME of breast cancer. Key regulatory proteins such as METTL3/14, WTAP, ALKBH5, YTHDF1/2/3, YTHDC1/2, and IGF2BP1/2/3 play crucial roles in influencing the occurrence, development, and distant metastasis of breast cancer. These proteins achieve this by regulating stem cell characteristics, oncogenes, signaling pathways, and tumor immunity. Due to their specific expression patterns within the TME of breast cancer, these regulators have the potential to serve as novel diagnostic markers in the future. For example, YTHDF3 is specifically associated with brain metastasis in breast cancer and might enable early and specific diagnosis of breast cancer brain metastasis. Additionally, these factors are involved in the prognosis regulation of breast cancer. YTHDF3 has been validated as an independent prognostic factor for breast cancer, providing valuable insights for predicting patient outcomes. Furthermore, m6A regulators such as YTHDF1, YTHDF2, and HNRNPA2B1 have been shown to influence drug resistance in breast cancer tumors, representing potential intervention points for addressing this critical issue.

As research progresses, the landscape of genomes regulated by the m6A family of proteins is rapidly expanding. However, the study of the relationship between m6A regulators and breast cancer remains fraught with controversies and gaps. For instance, FTO and HNRNPC exhibit different roles in various breast cancer subtypes. FTO has been found to be associated with tumor progression in HER2-positive breast cancer, while it exhibits inhibitory effects in TNBC, indicating contradictory roles across different contexts. Similarly, HNRNPC plays a dual role in regulating cell proliferation and immune responses, with its effects potentially varying depending on the specific breast cancer background. Research on factors such as PRRC2A, RBM15, YTHDC1/2, HNRNPC, eIF3, and FMRP remains limited. RBM15 has primarily been linked to leukemia, liver cancer, and laryngeal cancer, while YTHDC2 is significantly associated with tumor immune infiltration and possesses high diagnostic value for cholangiocarcinoma and thyroid cancer, as well as promising prognostic value for low-grade gliomas [117]. However, studies on YTHDC1 and YTHDC2 in breast cancer are sparse, with findings indicating that YTHDC1 is related to lung metastasis in TNBC, and YTHDC2 may be specifically associated with TNBC. Research on eIF3, FMRP, and PRRC2A in relation to breast cancer is currently lacking, highlighting the urgent need for further investigations. Furthermore, targeting m6A regulators in breast cancer presents significant therapeutic challenges due to the lack of relevant clinical trials, which raises concerns about potential side effects and the limitations of such treatments. The complex roles of m6A regulators in mediating drug resistance necessitate further investigation to mitigate possible adverse reactions. Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of the epigenetic mechanisms of m6A in breast cancer is crucial for the clinical application of m6A-related therapeutic strategies.

Regarding future perspectives, there is an urgent need for large-scale clinical trials focusing on m6A regulators in cancer treatment. As of now, several epigenetic drugs such as DNMT, HDAC, and EZH2 inhibitors have received the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval for hematologic and solid tumors, showcasing the therapeutic potential of epigenetic targets [118]. This approval underscores the significance of epigenetic mechanisms in cancer therapy. Consequently, m6A regulators and their key target genes are expected to become new targets for the early diagnosis and precise treatment of breast cancer. Current clinical trials, such as NCT04561005 and NCT04195169, are exploring the role of m6A inhibitors and related biomarkers in various tumor types, highlighting their potential in improving patient outcomes. However, given that the current understanding of the mechanisms of m6A regulators in breast cancer is largely derived from cell and animal experiments. For instance, in cell-based studies, YTHDF1 has been shown to promote breast cancer cell proliferation, DNA damage repair, and chemoresistance [80]. In animal models, the METTL3/IGF2BP3 axis has been validated to upregulate PD-L1 in breast cancer, thereby inhibiting tumor immune surveillance. Nonetheless, the roles of some regulators remain contested or under-researched, and their application value in human patients has yet to be fully elucidated [31]. Therefore, further investigation through clinical trials is essential to translate these findings into effective treatment strategies.

In parallel, advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) present exciting opportunities for future research in the field of m6A regulation and breast cancer. AI-based approaches, particularly machine learning algorithms, could significantly enhance the identification of novel m6A regulators by analyzing vast genomic and transcriptomic datasets. These techniques can sift through complex biological data to reveal patterns and correlations that may not be immediately apparent, such as the impact of specific m6A modifications on gene expression profiles in various breast cancer subtypes. Furthermore, AI can assist in predicting treatment responses by integrating patient-specific genetic and epigenetic information, thus enabling the development of more tailored therapeutic strategies. The use of AI-driven models could facilitate the exploration of m6A modifications in relation to patient outcomes, resistance mechanisms, and overall tumor behavior, thereby enriching our understanding of the intricate relationships between m6A regulation and breast cancer biology. Ultimately, these advancements could lead to improved personalized medicine strategies, where treatment plans are adapted based on individual molecular profiles, enhancing efficacy and minimizing adverse effects.

Furthermore, exploring combination therapies that target m6A regulators alongside existing treatments may help address drug resistance, a significant hurdle in breast cancer management. The integration of biomarker-driven strategies could enhance prognosis assessments and therapeutic outcomes.

To bridge the existing gaps in our understanding, future research should focus on both in vivo and in vitro studies to elucidate the complex roles of m6A in breast cancer. Collaborative efforts combining experimental findings with computational models will be essential in translating these discoveries into clinical applications. The ultimate goal is to develop effective strategies for the early diagnosis and treatment of breast cancer, leveraging the insights gained from m6A regulators.

Conclusion

Overall, m6A modification plays a complex and crucial role in the pathophysiological processes of breast cancer. It not only provides new molecular targets and mechanistic insights but also offers a theoretical foundation for personalized therapy and prognostic evaluation. With continuous technological advancements and deeper research, m6A modification is expected to become a significant breakthrough in the diagnosis and treatment of breast cancer, bringing new hope and opportunities for clinical management of patients.

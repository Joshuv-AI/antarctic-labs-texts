1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a perfect field with
\mathrm{char}(k)\neq 2,3
, and set
K\coloneqq k(t)
. An elliptic curve
E/K
determines a relatively minimal elliptic surface with section

f\colon S\longrightarrow\mdmathbb{P}^{1}_{k}

unique up to isomorphism (see
[
19
,
22
]
for background on elliptic surfaces).

The arithmetic of
E/K
is reflected in the geometry of
S
, and a basic organizing principle is the Shioda–Tate formula
[
23
]

(1)

\rho(S)\;=\;T(S)\;+\;\rk(E/K),

where
\rho(S)=\rk\NS(S_{\bar{k}})
is the
geometric Picard rank
,
T(S)
is the
rank of the geometric trivial lattice
generated by the zero section, a fiber class, and the components of reducible fibers not meeting the zero section, and
\rk(E/K)
is the
Mordell–Weil rank
. For the relatively minimal elliptic surfaces
f:S\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
with section considered in this paper, we have
q(S)=0
and
p_{g}(S)=n-1
, hence the standard bounds

(2)

\displaystyle 2\leq\rho(S)\leq 10n,\qquad 2\leq T(S)\leq 10n,\qquad 0\leq\rk(E/K)\leq 10n-2,

where
\rho(S)\leq 10n=h^{1,1}(S)
is the Lefschetz bound over
k=\mdmathbb{C}
(or in general the Igusa’s inequality
\rho(S)\leq b_{2}(S)=12n-2
).

In
[
2
]
, Bejleri–Park–Satriano construct height-moduli stacks of rational points on proper polarized cyclotomic stacks. In the fundamental modular curve example

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1}\simeq\mathcal{P}(4,6),\qquad\lambda\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}(4,6)}(1),

a minimal elliptic curve over
K
can be viewed as a rational point of
\lambda
–height
n
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1}
over
K
. This yields a separated Deligne–Mumford stack of finite type

\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min}\;\coloneqq\;\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}^{\min}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(4,6),\mathcal{O}(1)\bigr)

parametrizing minimal elliptic curves over
K
of discriminant degree
12n
. Here a
K
-rational point of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1}
of
\lambda
-height
n
means the stacky height
n
with respect to the Hodge line bundle
\lambda
, in the sense of
[
8
]
. Under the identification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1}\cong\mathcal{P}(4,6)
one has
\lambda\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}(4,6)}(1)
, and this height agrees with the Faltings height of the corresponding elliptic curve by
[
2
, Cor. 7.6]
.

Guided by (
1
), we introduce the following motivic generating series (see
[
7
]
for background on the Grothendieck ring of stacks) refining the height generating series in
[
2

by weighting each height stratum with the

of the associated relatively minimal elliptic surface.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
\neq 2,3
, and consider the height–moduli stack

where for each

we write
S\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
for the associated relatively minimal elliptic surfaces
f:S\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
with section, and:

•

T(S)
is the rank of the trivial lattice of
S
;

•

\rk(E/K)
is the Mordell–Weil rank.

The following specializations are the associated
bivariate
height zeta functions
:

(3)

\displaystyle Z_{\Triv}(u;t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(u,1;t),

(4)

\displaystyle Z_{\MW}(v;t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(1,v;t),

(5)

\displaystyle Z_{\NS}(w;t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(w,w;t).

Remark 1.2
.

Setting
u=v=1
forgets the lattice rank grading and specializes to the
univariate motivic height zeta function

Z_{{\vec{\lambda}}}(t)=\mathcal{Z}(1,1;t)\in K_{0}(\Stck_{k})\llbracket t\rrbracket
and likewise to its inertial refinement

.
[

In this paper we focus on

Lemma 1.3

Let

If

.

Definition 1.5
.

Fix

. By Proposition

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

The proof is a motivic local-to-global factorization argument
[
15
,
4
]
, implemented on the twisted-map stratification of the height–moduli

via the evaluation morphisms
[
9
]
. Throughout we use the Bejleri–Park–Satriano correspondence

between rational points, minimal weighted linear series, and twisted morphisms; in particular, local reduction conditions are encoded by representable twisted morphisms to

2.
Rationality of the trivial lattice rank weighted case

Throughout, let
k
be a perfect field with
\mathrm{char}(k)\neq 2,3
, set
K=k(t)
, and let

\pi\colon S\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
be the relatively minimal elliptic surface with section associated to
E/K
.
Write
\Triv(S)\subset\NS(S_{\bar{k}})
for the geometric trivial lattice and
T(S)=\rk\Triv(S)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix
n\geq 1
. The discriminant degree constraint
\sum_{v}e(F_{v})=12n
implies that only finitely many
geometric fiber configurations
\mathfrak{f}
occur among surfaces parametrized by

\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min}
. Consequently,

\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min}=\bigsqcup_{\mathfrak{f}}\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min,(\mathfrak{f})}

is a finite constructible stratification. Moreover, the trivial lattice rank
T(S)

is constant on each stratum
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min,(\mathfrak{f})}
.

Proof.

Fix
n\geq 1
and let
S\to\mdmathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
be a surface parametrized by
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min}
.
For any relatively minimal elliptic surface with section one has
\sum_{v\in\mdmathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}}e(F_{v})=e(S_{\bar{k}})
and in our height-
n
locus this total Euler number equals
12n
(equivalently, the discriminant has degree
12n
). For each singular fiber
F_{v}
, the Kodaira–Néron classification
[
17
,
20
]
gives the types
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{k},{\mathop{\rm I}}_{k}^{*}
(
k\geq 1
) and
{\mathop{\rm II}},{\mathop{\rm III}},{\mathop{\rm IV}},{\mathop{\rm I}}_{0}^{*},{\mathop{\rm IV}}^{*},{\mathop{\rm III}}^{*},{\mathop{\rm II}}^{*}
. Their Euler numbers satisfy
e({\mathop{\rm I}}_{k})=k
,
e({\mathop{\rm I}}_{k}^{*})=k+6
while the remaining types have Euler number
e(F_{v})\in\{2,3,4,6,8,9,10\}
(see
[
13
, Table 1]
). Since
\sum_{v}e(F_{v})=12n
, the integers
k
occurring in fibers of type
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{k}
and
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{k}^{*}
are bounded in terms of
n
. Hence there are only finitely many multisets of Kodaira symbols (equivalently, fiber configurations
\mathfrak{f}
) whose Euler numbers sum to
12n
. Therefore only finitely many configurations occur, and
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min}=\bigsqcup_{\mathfrak{f}}\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min,(\mathfrak{f})}
is a finite stratification by locally closed substacks as in
[
2
, Thm. 7.16]
. Finally, on a fixed stratum
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min,(\mathfrak{f})}
the multiset
\mathfrak{f}
(hence the integers
m_{v}
) is constant, so Lemma
1.3
implies that
T(S)=2+\sum_{v\in\mathfrak{f}}(m_{v}-1)
is constant on that stratum.
∎

A multivariate height series

We briefly recall the local indexing used in the twisted-maps description of height–moduli. By
[
2
, Thm. 5.1]
the height-
n
moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{n,C}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
on a proper polarized cyclotomic stack
\mathcal{X}
with polarizing line bundle
\mathcal{L}
admits a finite stratification by locally closed substacks indexed by admissible local conditions and degrees: there is a finite disjoint union of morphisms

\bigsqcup_{\Gamma,d}\ \mathcal{H}^{\Gamma}_{d,C}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})/S_{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{n,C}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}),

where
\mathcal{H}^{\Gamma}_{d,C}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is the moduli stack of representable twisted morphisms of stable height
d
to
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
with and local twisting conditions

\Gamma=\bigl(\{r_{1},a_{1}\},\ldots,\{r_{s},a_{s}\}\bigr),

recording the stabilizer orders
r_{i}
and the corresponding characters
a_{i}
at the stacky marked points of the source root stack. The indices
(\Gamma,d)
range over those satisfying the height decomposition formula

n\;=\;d\;+\;\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{a_{i}}{r_{i}}.

Here
S_{\Gamma}\subset S_{s}
is the subgroup permuting stacky marked points of the same local type.

Definition 2.2
.

For the Euler-product argument it is useful to distinguish
local factor types
from
elementary local patterns
.
Let
\mathcal{I}\mathcal{P}(4,6)
be the cyclotomic inertia stack.

(1) local factor types.

Let
J
denote the finite set of local factor types occurring in the Tate-algorithm stratification via twisted maps
(see
[
2
, §7]
); concretely one may take

J=\Bigl\{{\mathop{\rm II}},\ {\mathop{\rm III}},\ {\mathop{\rm IV}},\ {\mathop{\rm II}}^{*},\ {\mathop{\rm III}}^{*},\ {\mathop{\rm IV}}^{*},\ {\mathop{\rm I}}_{0}^{*}(j\neq 0,1728),\ {\mathop{\rm I}}_{0}^{*}(j\in\{0,1728\}),\ {\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet},\ {\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}^{*}\Bigr\},

where
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}
and
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}^{*}
are the two cusp
shapes
over
j=\infty
.

(2) Elementary local patterns.

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the set of elementary local patterns used to index evaluation conditions, i.e. the inertia components
in which the evaluation maps land. Away from the cusp
j=\infty
, the inertia label determines the Kodaira symbol, so the
non-cusp patterns form a finite set

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{nc}}=\Bigl\{{\mathop{\rm II}},\ {\mathop{\rm III}},\ {\mathop{\rm IV}},\ {\mathop{\rm II}}^{*},\ {\mathop{\rm III}}^{*},\ {\mathop{\rm IV}}^{*},\ {\mathop{\rm I}}_{0}^{*}(j\neq 0,1728),\ {\mathop{\rm I}}_{0}^{*}(j\in\{0,1728\})\Bigr\}.

At the cusp
j=\infty
, the inertia label records only the cusp shape (
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}
or
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}^{*}
); the additional
integer

(contact order with the boundary, equivalently the pole order of
j
) is part of the admissible local data
on a twisted-maps chart and is treated as a multiplicity parameter within the cusp shape. Accordingly we set

\mathcal{A}\;\coloneqq\;\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{nc}}\ \sqcup\ \{{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet},\ {\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}^{*}\}.

For
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{nc}}
, let
m(\alpha)\in\mdmathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
be the number of irreducible components of the corresponding
Kodaira fiber, so that
m(\alpha)-1
is its contribution to the trivial lattice. For the cusp shapes
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}
and

{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}^{*}
, the component number depends on the contact order
k\geq 1
(of the corresponding
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{k}
or
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{k}^{*}
fiber); this

). In summary,

indexes
the
local factor types
(basic chart types) that become Euler factors under the power structure on

, whereas
\mathcal{A}

indexes the evaluation labels, i.e. exactly what inertia can see; in particular, over
j=\infty
inertia distinguishes only the
two cusp shapes and not the contact order
k
.

Remark 2.3
.

When an evaluation condition lands over the cusp
j=\infty
, the corresponding component of the cyclotomic inertia stack

\mathcal{I}\mathcal{P}(4,6)
records only the
cusp shape
(multiplicative
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}
or additive
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}^{*}
); it does
not

record the

. Equivalently, inertia detects that
j
has a pole, but not its pole order. The missing discrete datum is the
contact order with the boundary
. Geometrically, it is visible on the log canonical model obtained by contracting, in each reducible fiber, the components not meeting the zero section.

(1) The multiplicative family
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{k}
.

If the fiber at

is of type
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{k}
(

is given by

(with

. Thus

meets the boundary with contact order

{\mathop{\rm I}}_{k}

(2) The additive family

If the fiber at

{\mathop{\rm I}}_{k}^{*}
(

), then the contraction produces a

surface singularity. The

(so locally

), while the discriminant

For

Remark 2.5
.

The local conditions defining the strata are imposed via evaluation maps
\ev_{i}
to
\mathcal{I}\mathcal{P}(4,6)
, hence are naturally indexed by connected components of the inertia stack. In particular, the same Kodaira symbol may correspond to distinct inertia components. For example,
{\mathop{\rm I}}_{0}^{*}
splits into distinct inertia components according to whether

or

Remark 2.6
.

We work in the localized ring
K_{0}(\Stck_{k})[\mdmathbb{L}^{-1}]
. Localization is used to place the argument in a ring where quotient stack identities for linear algebraic groups (e.g.
\GL_{n}
,
\PGL_{2}
) and the power–structure identities for symmetric powers hold uniformly as equalities of rational functions, thereby justifying the reorganization of unordered local factors into Euler factors.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the finite set of elementary local patterns from Definition
2.2
, and let

\mathcal{H}(s;\mathbf{x})
be the multivariate height series defined in (
6
).
After inverting
\mdmathbb{L}
, the series
\mathcal{H}(s;\mathbf{x})
is a rational function of
s
with coefficients in

K_{0}(\Stck_{k})[\mdmathbb{L}^{-1}][\mathbf{x}]
.

More precisely, there exist:

•

a finite index set
J
of local factor types,

•

motivic classes
A_{j}\in K_{0}(\Stck_{k})[\mdmathbb{L}^{-1}]
,

•

integers

, recording the discriminant degree increment contributed by one local factor of type
j
,

•

and exponents
\beta_{j,\alpha}\in\mdmathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for

, recording how many markings of inertia type

,

such that

(7)

\mathcal{H}(s;\mathbf{x})=\prod_{j\in J}\left(1-A_{j}\Bigl(\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}x_{\alpha}^{\beta_{j,\alpha}}\Bigr)s^{c_{j}}\right)^{-\{\mdmathbb{P}^{1}\}}\ \in\ K_{0}(\Stck_{k})[\mdmathbb{L}^{-1}][\mathbf{x}](s).

Equivalently, writing

Y_{j}(s;\mathbf{x})\coloneqq A_{j}\Bigl(\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}x_{\alpha}^{\beta_{j,\alpha}}\Bigr)s^{c_{j}},

one has the explicit specialization

(8)

(1-Y_{j})^{-\{\mdmathbb{P}^{1}\}}=\sum_{N\geq 0}\{\Sym^{N}(\mdmathbb{P}^{1})\}\,Y_{j}^{N}=\frac{1}{(1-Y_{j})(1-\mdmathbb{L}\,Y_{j})}.

Moreover, for
\alpha\in\{{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet},{\mathop{\rm I}}_{\bullet}^{*}\}
the exponent
\beta_{j,\alpha}
counts only the number of cusp markings of
the given cusp
shape
in factor type
j
; it does not record the contact order
k\geq 1

4.
Irrationality of the Néron–Severi and Mordell–Weil specializations

The rationality of
Z_{\Triv}(u;t)
reflects the fact that the trivial lattice rank
T(S)
is governed by
local
reduction data. Indeed, by Lemma
1.3
it depends only on the multiset of fiber component numbers
m_{v}
, hence is constant on each Kodaira stratum
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min,(\mathfrak{f})}
, in the finite constructible stratification of Proposition
2.1
. This locality is exactly what makes the evaluation-map factorization and the power structure on
K_{0}(\Stck_{k})
applicable: unordered collections of local factors assemble into a
finite
Euler product.

In contrast, the Mordell–Weil rank
\rk(E/K)
is not determined by the fiber configuration. Even on a fixed Kodaira stratum
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min,(\mathfrak{f})}
, the rank typically varies, reflecting genuinely
global
constraints rather than local reduction data. Since
T(S)
is constant on
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min,(\mathfrak{f})}
, the Shioda–Tate formula (
1
) shows that variation of
\rk(E/K)
is equivalent to variation of the Néron–Severi rank
\rho(S)
. Thus any refinement of the height series by
\rk(E/K)
, or equivalently by
\rho(S)
, necessarily detects global jump phenomena invisible to the local factor stratification used for
Z_{\Triv}
.

One way to organize this global complexity is via Néron–Severi jump loci. Fix a fiber configuration
\mathfrak{f}
and write
\Triv^{(\mathfrak{f})}\subset\NS(S_{\bar{k}})
for the sublattice generated by the zero section, a fiber class, and the components of reducible fibers in the configuration
\mathfrak{f}
. Inside
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min,(\mathfrak{f})}
, imposing that
\NS(S_{\bar{k}})
contain additional algebraic classes
independent of

\Triv^{(\mathfrak{f})}
(equivalently, that
\rho(S)
, hence
\rk(E/K)
, jump) is an algebraic condition. Over
\mdmathbb{C}
, these conditions are naturally modeled by Noether–Lefschetz (Hodge) loci for the variation of Hodge structure coming from the family of elliptic surfaces over
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\min,(\mathfrak{f})}
, and the theorem of Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan
[
3
]
shows that such loci are, in general, only a
countable union of closed algebraic subsets
. In particular, unlike the Kodaira stratification at fixed height
n
(which is finite by Proposition
2.1
), refinements by Néron–Severi
lattice type
are not expected to admit a finite constructible stratification.

This suggests that the local-to-global factorization mechanism producing a finite Euler product for
Z_{\Triv}
should structurally fail for the Mordell–Weil and Néron–Severi specializations, and motivates the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4.1
.

Let
k=\mdmathbb{C}
and
K=\mdmathbb{C}(z)
. The specializations

Z_{\MW}(v;t)\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(1,v;t),\qquad Z_{\NS}(w;t)\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(w,w;t)

are not rational in
t
with coefficients in
K_{0}(\Stck_{\mdmathbb{C}})[\mdmathbb{L}^{-1}][v]
(resp.
[w]
); i.e.

Z_{\MW}(v;t)\notin K_{0}(\Stck_{\mdmathbb{C}})[\mdmathbb{L}^{-1}][v](t),\qquad Z_{\NS}(w;t)\notin K_{0}(\Stck_{\mdmathbb{C}})[\mdmathbb{L}^{-1}][w](t).

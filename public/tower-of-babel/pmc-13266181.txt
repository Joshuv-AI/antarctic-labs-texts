Milking frequency and the effect of fill time on the production and conformation in Nordestino ecotype donkeys
Authors: KC Santana, JEC Lucena, AKS Pinheiro, DSA Pinto, IM Farias, AH Silva, VN Maia, WJS Diniz, JM Santiago
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Milk yield increased 40% after 4 h of udder filling before milking.•Three daily milkings boosted yield by up to 457%.•Udder and teat size correlated with milk production.•Milking frequency did not affect somatic cell count.•Nordestino donkeys showed superior milk potential to other breeds.Keywords: Milk, Morphometry, Somatic cell, Teats

Introduction

Milking frequency and milking interval are key factors in milk production, directly affecting the mammary gland’s secretory efficiency and the persistence of lactation. Studies have shown that more frequent milking, particularly in early lactation, is associated with greater cellular proliferation and differentiation, as well as reduced apoptosis rates in secreting cells, which help maintain production over the lactation cycle (Wall & McFadden, 2007). However, intensifying milking must be carefully assessed, as excessively short intervals between sessions may compromise the recovery of the teat sphincter, thus increasing the risk of infections such as mastitis (Carneiro, 2009).

Udder fill time is another critical aspect of milk production, as it regulates milk accumulation between milkings and impacts secretion dynamics. Longer intervals favor the storage of larger volumes, whereas shorter intervals may limit the functional recovery of teats and negatively affect mammary health (Svennersten-Sjaunja & Olsson, 2005). In female donkeys, D’Alessandro et al. (2015) pointed out that udder fill time, especially in 3 h intervals, may impact both the amount of milk and its physicochemical qualities.

In addition to physiological and functional aspects, morphological characteristics of the udder and teats also directly influence milk production and quality. The conformation, size, and symmetry of the mammary gland are all associated with milking efficiency and secreted volume (Yu et al., 2021). In dairy cows, for example, a positive correlation is observed between udder circumference and production capacity (Gašparík et al., 2023).

In donkeys, the location and anatomy of the udder and teats play an additional role in preserving the microbiological quality of milk. Mammary glands that are better protected or located further from the ground tend to have a lower risk of contact with environmental contaminants, thereby helping maintain milk purity. Therefore, the antimicrobial efficacy of donkey milk is due not only to the presence of bioactive compounds (e.g., lactoferrin, lysozyme, immunoglobulins, and fatty acids) but also to anatomical factors that favor the protection and integrity of milk secretion (Khan et al., 2024).

Against this background, this study aimed to assess the effect of milking frequency and the relationship between udder fill time, morphophysiological characteristics of udder and teats, and milk production in female donkeys of the Nordestino ecotype.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

The study was carried out in accordance with ethical guidelines for the use of animals in scientific research. The protocol was submitted and approved by the Committee of Ethics in Animal Use (CEUA) of the Federal University of the Agreste of Pernambuco (UFAPE), under protocol CEUA/UFAPE 2024,051,384, to ensure the well-being and integrity of the animals used in the research.

Site

The experiment was carried out at the facilities of the Experimental Farm of the Federal Rural University of Pernambuco (Universidade Federal Rural de Pernambuco, UFRPE), located in the municipality of Garanhuns – PE at 8°58′31.28″S and 36°27′15.00″ W.

Animals

The study involved 15 female Nordestino donkeys (Equus asinus), aged between 3 and 7 years, with a mean live weight of 185 ± 30 kg. Prior to the experimental phase, the animals underwent a deworming protocol with ivermectin and pyrantel, received booster vaccinations against tetanus and rabies, and underwent nutritional adjustments to achieve a relatively uniform set of body scores.

Nutritional management

The 15 donkeys were housed in pens with native pasture throughout the gestational period, with water and mineral lick ad libitum. Their body scores were monitored monthly. At 11 months of gestation, the animals were transferred to the maternity pen. During the experimental (postpartum) period, they were fed a controlled diet based on 2.0% of their live weight in dry matter, composed of 60% Tifton 85 (Cynodon dactylon) hay and 40% concentrate feed (Table 1); this was provided twice a day (at 5 a.m. and 3 pm.), in addition to free access to water and the mineral lick.

Experiment 1 – relationship between udder fill time, morphological characteristics, and milk production

Experimental design

Trial 1 was conducted between the 15th and 75th days of lactation, following a randomized block design in which each donkey represented one block. The treatments consisted of different time intervals during which mother and offspring were separated for the udder to fill (2, 3, and 4 h), each interval being imposed for 20 days. Donkeys were assigned to the three treatments in random order (determined by a draw) to prevent the lactation stage from confounding the results.

During the experimental period, the mothers were manually milked to empty the udder every morning (5 a.m.) immediately after being separated from the foal. This procedure ensured that the time for udder fill began under the same experimental conditions for every individual. Emptying the udder at 5 a.m. was also used as a reference point to determine the udder filling intervals prior to milking; hence, in the treatment with 2, 3, or 4-h intervals, the mothers were milked at 7, 8, or 9 a.m., respectively (see Fig. 1).

Milking management

The animals were milked daily at the holding pens, in a roofed area with a rubber floor, with no feed available. It was performed once daily at the time specified for each treatment (2, 3, or 4 h), without the foal present. Before each milking, all utensils, the milker’s hands, and the donkey’s teats were cleaned with water to remove evident dirt; this was followed by drying with paper towels, immersion of the teats in a 0.5% iodine solution for 30 s, and drying with paper towels again.

The first three squirts of milk were used to check for milk characteristics (e.g., clumps, pus, blood, or abnormal coloration) that might indicate clinical mastitis. The animals were milked manually, by manipulating one teat at a time, until milk ceased to flow. The samples were then weighed on a Toledo® commercial digital scale with a precision of 0.001 kg.

Statistical analyses

Milk production and morphometric measurements were first tested for normality. Next, they were analyzed using analysis of variance, and the means were compared using Tukey’s test (P < 0.05). They were also analyzed using Spearman’s correlations at the 5% significance level.

Experiment 2 – effect of milking frequency on milk production and somatic cell count (SCC)

Experimental design

Trial 2 was carried out between the 76th and 135th days of lactation, using a randomized design with three treatments (one, two, or three daily milkings) and five animals per treatment group.

Milking management

The individuals assigned to one daily milking were milked at 8:30 a.m., while those in the group of two daily milkings were milked at 8:30 a.m. and 12:30 pm., and those milked three times per day were milked at 8:30 a.m., 12:30 pm., and 4:30 pm. In each treatment, the mothers were separated from their offspring for 3 h for the udder to fill. Female donkeys were manually milked while the foals were not present, in the same place and using the same safety and hygiene measures as described for trial 1. Again, samples were weighed at the end of the process.

Somatic cell count

The milk samples used for somatic cell count (SCC) were collected on the first and last days of the experimental period, corresponding to the 76th and 135th days of lactation. The samples were stored in properly identified 40 mL sterile plastic flasks containing the preservative bronopol, refrigerated, and then sent to the Milk Clinic laboratory at the School of Agriculture Luis de Queiroz (Escola Superior de Agricultura Luiz de Queiroz, ESALQ) of the University of São Paulo.

In the laboratory, the samples were analyzed by flow cytometry to quantify somatic cells in the raw milk. The SCC results were expressed as cells/mL of milk and used to indicate the occurrence of inflammatory processes, such as subclinical mastitis, over the experimental period.

Statistical analyses

The data were submitted to a normality test. The milk production data did not follow a normal distribution and were analyzed using non-parametric statistical tests. To compare total milk production among the experimental groups, the data were analyzed using the Kruskal-Wallis test, and the medians were compared using Dunn’s test. On the other hand, SCC data, both before and after 60 days of milking, showed a normal distribution and were analyzed using a paired-samples t-test.

The same extension of udder fill time also affected udder dimensions. The largest increase was in udder length (4.5% increase), while udder width, circumference, and depth increased by 2.6%, 2.0%, and 3.6%, respectively. The height of the udder from the ground was the only measurement that decreased, by an average of 0.6%.

Regarding teat morphometry, extending the udder fill time from 2 h to 4 h had the greatest effect on teat diameter, with an average increase of 9.7% (taking into account both the right and left teats). Teat length and distance between teats increased by 7.0% and 6.0%, respectively.

Regardless of udder fill time, a significant correlation was observed between milk production and most of the udder and teat morphometric measurements (Table 3). All teat morphometric measurements were correlated with milk production, regardless of the treatment. However, among the other measurements assessed, udder width was not significantly associated with any other characteristic, and the relationship between milk production and udder height from the ground was significant only for the fill time of 2 h.

Discussion

Udder fill time

The alveolar tissue of the udder is responsible for the secretion of milk, which is later stored in the teat and udder cisterns, as well as in the alveolar ducts and lumens. When these structures reach maximum storage capacity, the secretion process is interrupted (Canisso et al., 2021). In addition to providing a longer period for milk synthesis, the interval of 4 h for udder fill in this study allowed for better use of the udder’s storage capacity in female donkeys when compared with 2 and 3 h intervals (Table 2).

A commonly evaluated time for udder fill in studies with donkeys is 3 h. Thus, we also addressed that time, as well as exploring shorter (2 h) and longer (4 h) intervals. Intervals of <2 h might be impractical for commercial farming due to insufficient time for milk secretion, as confirmed in the present study, where the 2 h interval yielded an average of only 188.76 g of milk. On the other hand, intervals beyond 4 h might harm both foals and the mother’s mammary glands.

Unlike ruminants, healthy foals suckle up to seven times an hour during their first week of life, with each suckling bout lasting between 1 and 2 min. In the fourth week of life, that frequency is reduced to approximately three times per hour (Carson & Wood-Gush, 1983). Unlike bovine calves, which often receive artificial milk feeding in the absence of the mother (Filho et al., 2018), foals are more dependent on their mothers for feeding and care (French, 1998). In this study, the experiment began when the foals were only 2 weeks old, so adopting intervals greater than 4 h for udder fill might have compromised their health, development, and well-being. That concern prevented extending the time during which mothers and foals were kept separated and, therefore, determining the precise interval required for the udder to reach its maximum milk storage capacity.

Moreover, longer intervals might harm mammary gland health as prolonged milk retention in the udder increases intramammary pressure, which may compromise the integrity of mammary tissues and the gland’s natural defense mechanisms, and also results in greater milk accumulation and udder distention. The latter may favor dilatation of the teat canal, facilitating the ingress of pathogenic microorganisms and, consequently, increasing susceptibility to mastitis, an inflammatory disease usually associated with bacterial infection (Blau et al., 2019).

Milk production

Controlling for mean body weight (expressed as L/100 kg live weight), mean milk production of female donkeys of the Nordestino ecotype—obtained with a 4 h interval for udder fill—was higher than values obtained under manual milking for the Jiangyue (Guo et al., 2007), Pêga (Santos et al., 2023), Narsi and North African (Aroua et al., 2024), and Dezhou (Zhou et al., 2025) breeds (Table 6).

Unlike donkey breeds that have been selected for milk production over a long period (e.g., the European breeds Ragusana and Martina Franca), there have never been genetic improvement programs targeting milk production-based selection of Nordestino ecotype females. Instead, this breed has been exposed only to natural selection in the Brazilian semi-arid region, which is characterized by feed of low nutritional value for most of the year (Carneiro et al., 2018).

In this sense, providing a more complete and balanced diet—comprising 60% hay and 40% concentrate, which is almost certainly more nutritious than the feed available in the Caatinga biome—was sufficient for the females in this study to produce more milk compared with three of the four breeds represented in Table 6. This result, combined with the lack of specific genetic selection for milk production, demonstrates the potential of the Nordestino donkey for dairy farming, provided it is carried out in a professional and technified manner. A genetic selection program to target this is now essential, in addition to adopting proper practices for nutritional, sanitary, and milking management.

The milk production of Nordestino ecotype donkeys also compares favorably with horses. For example, Dias et al. (2025) determined the total milk production of female Nordestino donkeys (by weighing foals before and after suckling) and reported an average production of 5.0 kg of milk per day, or 2.70 kg of milk/100 kg live weight. This level of production is higher than the 2.06 kg of milk/100 kg live weight reported for Mangalarga Marchador mares by Santos et al. (2005), who used the same methodology to estimate milk production capacity. In other words, the Nordestino ecotype, with a mean weight of 185 kg, has higher total milk production in relation to weight than Mangalarga Marchador mares, which weighed between 415 and 440 kg.

According to Santos and Zanine (2006), milk production is significantly affected by the lack of continued stimulus in the form of the foal’s presence during milking, as regular contact with the offspring is essential for stimulating milk synthesis and release. This suggestion is supported by both the findings of Dias et al. (2025) and observations in this study. In the former, female donkeys produced 5.0 kg of milk over 24 h, equivalent to 416.66 g every 2 h and 833.33 g of milk over 4 h, which is 62.4% higher than the 313.00 g found in this study. The discrepancy between values is likely due to the method used to obtain the milk: whereas milk extraction was performed directly by foals in the study by Dias et al. (2025), the present study involved manual milking without foals present. Additionally, the present study observed that udder volume increased again within a few minutes of milking and of reunion between the mothers and offspring. The milk was likely removed from the cisterns and teats; however, since donkey milk is alveolar, manual milking did not completely remove residual milk.

It is worth noting that, despite its limited productive capacity, interest and investment in the selection of the Nordestino ecotype for milk production are justified by the therapeutic benefits of donkey milk and by its classification as a medicinal food, which bestows high added value. Furthermore, the semi-arid region of northeastern Brazil has a low Human Development Index (about 0.550; Pnud, 2013), and the exploitation of donkey milk in the region may significantly increase the income of rural producers.

Morphological characteristics of the udder and teats

Knowing the dimensions of the udder of female donkeys may contribute to selecting more productive animals, since, in addition to genetics, the area occupied by alveoli and ducts, as well as the size of the cistern, directly affect milk production and storage capacity (Ayadi et al., 2003).

The increase in udder measurements observed between the fill times of 2 and 4 h (Table 3) was expected, as the longer interval allows for greater milk secretion and accumulation, which raises intramammary pressure and, consequently, alters udder conformation. Among the measurements assessed, the greatest variation was found in udder length. This can be attributed to the bidimensional nature of the determination, which takes into account the distance between the anterior and posterior edges of the udder, measured ventrally around its base in the cranial-caudal direction. Therefore, in addition to length itself, the measurement also reflected udder depth, encompassing not only the area corresponding to the cistern but also regions with a high concentration of alveolar tissue and ducts, which are directly influenced by the volume of milk stored.

The increase in udder fill time from 2 to 4 h significantly impacted teat morphometry, showing structural changes in response to milk accumulation. The parameter most affected by the change in milking interval was teat diameter, indicating greater tissue distension with the longer fill time and, possibly, increased intramammary pressure. It is worth noting that, in donkeys, each teat has two cisterns, which may result in a larger storage volume (De Palo et al., 2022) and contribute to greater milk storage capacity in each teat.

According to studies by Chirgin et al. (2016) and Salari et al. (2023), larger animals tend to have larger teats, including greater length, diameter, and distance between the extremities. These morphological parameters directly influence milk storage capacity and milking efficiency. However, few studies have investigated the relationship between udder fill time and teat morphology, particularly in donkeys. Understanding such morphological variation enables the development of more effective reproductive and productive management strategies and contributes to the selection of breeds with more appropriate anatomical characteristics for milk production.

Correlation between morphometric characteristics and milk production

The results showed that morphometric characteristics of the udder and teats were positively associated with milk production (Table 3). Among the variables assessed, udder length showed a moderate correlation with all milking times (Fig. 4), suggesting that the measurement may indirectly reflect the amount of secretory tissue in the mammary gland (Davis et al., 2016). Thus, female donkeys with longer udders may have greater productive capacity, possibly due to a larger functional area for milk synthesis and storage.

Likewise, the length and diameter of both the right and left teats showed significant correlations with milk production, with the highest coefficients observed for the length of the left teat (Table 3).

Teat morphological characteristics, such as length, may influence the efficiency of the neuroendocrine reflex associated with milk ejection. Since tactile stimulation is the main trigger for oxytocin, longer teats would tend to provide a greater area of contact during milking; thus, teat elongation could amplify sensory stimulation and potentiate oxytocin release. Such an effect would be especially relevant in donkeys, whose milk letdown is known to be quick and limited, requiring effective and immediate stimulation to ensure complete milk ejection (Farias et al., 2021).

Moreover, a strong correlation was observed between left teat length and milk productivity (Fig. 5); this may be associated with management practices, as the animals were always milked manually from the left side and by the same person. Consequently, the left teat likely receives more frequent and intense stimulation, leading to a slight structural elongation over time through tissue adaptations such as reorganization of conjunctive fibers, as well as an increase in the functional length of the teat, a process compatible with the remodeling of soft tissues submitted to continuous traction (Mackey et al., 2008). Such morphological asymmetry is common in manual milking systems, in which the stimulation applied to the teats is not standardized in the same way as in mechanical milking systems; in the latter, the flow of negative pressure exerted by the teat cups is uniform across all teats, thus minimizing variations between sides (Correa & Host, 2020).

Milking frequency

Increasing milking frequency from once to three times a day increased milk production by up to 457%. This finding aligns with other studies that have assessed the effect of milking frequency in donkeys. For example, in the Martina Franca breed, D’Alessandro et al. (2012) observed that three daily milkings resulted in greater milk production than a single milking, with no adverse effects on mammary health. However, excessive milking (six times a day) did not result in additional gains but did cause an increase in SCC, suggesting a physiological limit to mammary stimulation. Likewise, Alabisoet al. (2009) reported that increasing milking frequency from three to up to eight times per day was associated with increased milk production and changes in composition, especially fat content; they concluded that frequent mammary stimulation favors the gland’s secretory activity. These results support the finding that milking frequency is a key factor in modulating milk production in equids, and that increasing the number of milkings could be explored as a management strategy to maximize milk yields, provided the physiological limits of the species are respected.

In this study, SCC did not vary significantly between treatments, suggesting that increasing milking frequency did not compromise mammary gland health. D’Alessandro et al. (2012) also reported stability in mammary gland health indicators in female donkeys milked three times per day. Such results are important because they suggest that, under proper management conditions, milking can be intensified without harming milk quality or the animals’ well-being.

Finally, although the practice of a third milking is traditionally associated with high-production bovine herds (Lima et al., 2011), the results obtained from this study with female donkeys suggest that the strategy may also be promising for equids, provided it is adapted to their particular physiological and productive characteristics and constraints.

It is worth noting that, despite the production gains associated with increasing milking frequency, decisions over milking regimes must also take into account local economic and structural factors. Input, energy, labor, and feeding costs may compromise profitability, particularly in low-scale systems or with animals of lower productive potential (Barbosa et al., 2013). As noted by Teixeira Júnior et al. (2017), intensifying management is only viable when it is followed by strategies that optimize resources and ensure the efficiency of other production sectors. Therefore, adopting three daily milkings must be evaluated on a case-by-case basis, seeking a balance between productivity, sustainability, and economic viability.

Conclusion

An udder fill time of 4 h and milking females three times a day favors milk production in donkeys of the Nordestino ecotype. The moderate to strong correlations between udder morphometric characteristics and milk production suggest potential use of these parameters as criteria in genetic selection programs targeting donkey milk production.

Global-local mixing for infinite measure dynamical systems
Douglas Coates, Ian Melbourne

Abstract
We prove global-local mixing for a large class of dynamical systems with infinite invariant measure. In particular, we treat intermittent maps including maps with multiple neutral fixed points, nonMarkovian intermittent maps, and multidimensional nonMarkovian intermittent maps. We also prove global-local mixing for parabolic rational maps of the complex plane.

Introduction

There has been a significant surge of interest recently in the ergodic theory of dynamical system preserving an infinite
\sigma
-finite measure
[
1
]
.
A remark in
[
1

suggests that there is no reasonable notion of mixing for infinite measure systems. Nevertheless, there are at least two notions of mixing that have proved fruitful:
Krickeberg mixing

[
17
]
and more recently
global-local mixing
introduced by Lenci
[
18
]
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be an infinite,
\sigma
-finite measure space. We suppose that

f:X\to X
is a conservative, exact, measure-preserving transformation.
The usual mixing property
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(A\cap f^{-n}B)=\mu(A)\mu(B)
for all (finite measure) measurable sets
A
,
B
fails.
Moreover, by
[
15
]
there always exists a measurable set
W

with
0<\mu(W)<\infty
such that

\mu(W\cap f^{-n}W)=0
infinitely often.
The system is Krickeberg mixing if there exist constants
a_{n}\to\infty
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}\int_{X}v\;\,w\circ f^{n}\,d\mu=\int_{X}v\,d\mu\int_{X}w\,d\mu

for a sufficiently large class of observables
v
and
w
.
The more recent notion of global-local mixing
[
18
]
asks that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}v\;\,g\circ f^{n}\,d\mu=\bar{g}\,\int_{X}v\,d\mu

(1.1)

for all
v\in L^{1}
and a sufficiently large class of “global” observables
g
.

Roughly speaking, an observable
g\in L^{\infty}
is
global
if
there exists
\bar{g}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\int_{Z_{m}}g\,d\mu}{\mu(Z_{m})}=\bar{g}

for suitable nested sequences of measurable subsets
Z_{m}\subset X

with
\mu(Z_{m})<\infty
and
\mu(X\setminus\bigcup_{m}Z_{m})=0
.
The precise notion of global observable depends on the context and is made precise for our purposes in Definition
2.2
below.

Prototypical examples are the intermittent maps introduced in
[
24
]
. Krickeberg mixing was studied under restrictive conditions in
[
31
]
and a full theory was developed by

,
22
,
27

28
]

Likewise, global-local mixing for intermittent maps was studied under restrictive conditions in

Bonanno & Lenci
[
5

proved global-local mixing for certain intermittent maps
f:X\to X
with

, including generalised classical Pomeau-Manneville maps of the form

where
\alpha\in(0,1]
and

, as well as generalised Liverani-Saussol-Vaienti (LSV) maps
[

and a weaker result was obtained for

One aim of the current paper is to remove various restrictions in
[
5
]
thereby developing a complete theory of global-local mixing for intermittent maps:

•

The examples mentioned so far have a single neutral fixed point and the previous techniques do not apply when there is more than one neutral fixed points. We allow any number of neutral fixed points.

•

We remove the assumption that
f
is full-branch or even Markov.

•

Full global-local mixing is proved even in the case
\alpha=1
.

•

There is a technical assumption (A5) in
[
5
]
which is hard to verify and need not hold, but is unnecessary for our approach.

It is worth noting that the initial results of
[
31
]
on Krickeberg mixing for intermittent maps also required a technical assumption that was hard to verify and not always true. The approach in
[
22
]
eliminated the need for such an assumption, and a key step in the current paper is to apply the result of
[
22
]
.

Remark 1.1

For certain classes of dynamical systems, global-local mixing seems to be a more flexible property than Krickeberg mixing. Certainly, there are numerous examples in
[
11
]
which are shown to be global-local mixing and where proving Krickeberg mixing is way beyond reach.
In contrast, a full theory of global-local mixing for intermittent maps seems to be more delicate than Krickeberg mixing: Krickeberg mixing is just one step in our approach (see condition (
2.3
) below) and further arguments are required to achieve global-local mixing.

In addition, we treat a multidimensional nonMarkovian intermittent map studied in

The abstract setup

Let
(X,\mu)
be an infinite,
\sigma
-finite measure space. We suppose that

f:X\to X
is a conservative, exact, measure-preserving transformation.

Fix a subset
Y\subset X
with
0<\mu(Y)<\infty
.
By conservativity, the first hit map

\tau:X\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{+},\qquad\tau(x)=\inf\{n\geq 1:f^{n}x\in Y\}

is finite almost everywhere.
Set

Y_{n}=\{y\in Y:\tau(y)=n\},\qquad X_{n}=\{x\in X\setminus Y:\tau(x)=n\}.

Proposition 2.1

\mu(X_{n})<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(X_{n})=0
.

Proof.

Note that
\mu(X_{n})\leq\mu(f^{-n}Y)=\mu(Y)<\infty
by
f
-invariance of
\mu
.
Also,

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu(X_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu(f^{-1}X_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu(X_{j+1})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu(Y_{j+1}).

Hence

\mu(X_{1})-\mu(X_{n+1})=\sum_{j=2}^{n+1}\mu(Y_{j})\to\mu(Y)-\mu(Y_{1}).

Similarly,
\mu(Y)=\mu(f^{-1}Y)=\mu(Y_{1})+\mu(X_{1})

and it follows that
\mu(X_{n})\to 0
.
∎

3.1
Characterisation of global observables

Proposition 3.1

Assume condition (
2.1
). Then

\mu(X_{n,p})\sim\gamma_{p}\,n^{-\alpha}
as
n\to\infty
for all
1\leq p\leq q
.

Proof.

By
f
-invariance of
\mu
,

\mu(X_{n-1,p})=\mu(f^{-1}X_{n-1,p})=\mu(X_{n,p})+\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\mu(Y_{n,r}).

By Proposition
2.1
,
\mu(X_{k,p})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.
Hence

\displaystyle\mu(X_{n,p})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\big(\mu(X_{n,p})-\mu(X_{k,p})\big)=\sum_{j\geq n+1}\big(\mu(X_{j-1,p})-\mu(X_{j,p})\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\sum_{j\geq n+1}\mu(Y_{j,r})\sim\gamma_{p}n^{-\alpha}

by (
2.1
).
∎

Define positive sequences
a_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, by setting

a_{n}=\begin{cases}n^{1-\alpha},&0<\alpha<1\\
\log n,&\alpha=1\end{cases}\;.

Whenever
a_{n}^{-1}
is undefined, we set
a_{n}=1
.

Define sequences

\bar{g}_{n,p}=\frac{\int_{X_{n,p}}g\,d\mu}{\mu(X_{n,p})},\quad n\geq n_{0},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{g}_{n}=\sum_{p=1}^{q}\gamma_{p}\bar{g}_{n,p}.

The sequences are bounded since
g\in L^{\infty}
; indeed

|\bar{g}_{n,p}|\leq|g|_{\infty}

and
|\bar{g}_{n}|\leq\gamma|g|_{\infty}
.

Lemma 3.2

Assume condition (
2.1
) holds and let

g\in L^{\infty}
. Then
g
is a centred global observable if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}^{-1}\sum_{i=n_{0}}^{n}i^{-\alpha}\bar{g}_{i}=0.

(3.1)

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
,
\mu(X_{n})\sim\gamma n^{-\alpha}
so there exists
\tilde{\gamma}>0
such that

\mu\big(\bigcup_{i=n_{0}}^{n}X_{i}\big)\sim\tilde{\gamma}a_{n}
.
On the other hand, by definition of
\bar{g}_{i,p}
,
\bar{g}_{i}
and Proposition
3.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\bigcup_{i=n_{0}}^{n}X_{i}}g\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=n_{0}}^{n}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\int_{X_{i,p}}g\,d\mu=\sum_{i=n_{0}}^{n}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\bar{g}_{i,p}\,\mu(X_{i,p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=n_{0}}^{n}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\bar{g}_{i,p}\,\gamma_{p}\,i^{-\alpha}+o(a_{n})=\sum_{i=n_{0}}^{n}\bar{g}_{i}\,i^{-\alpha}+o(a_{n}).

Hence,
g
is a centred global observable if and only if (
3.1
) holds.
∎

3.2
Consequences of exactness

By exactness of

, it suffices
(see

) holds for
one

Choosing

Proposition 3.3

If
f^{m}:X\to X
is global-local mixing for some
m\geq 1
, then
f
is global-local mixing.

Proof.

Fix a centred global observable
g\in L^{\infty}
.
We claim that
g_{\ell}=g\circ f^{\ell}
is
a centred global observable for each
\ell\geq 0
.
Then, writing
n=km+\ell
where
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
0\leq\ell\leq m-1
,

\int_{Y}g\circ f^{n}\,d\mu=\int_{Y}g_{\ell}\circ f^{km}\,d\mu\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{Y}g_{\ell}\circ f^{km}\,d\mu=0

since
f^{m}
is global-local mixing and
g_{\ell}
is centred global.
Hence
f
is global-local mixing.

It remains to prove the claim.
Notice that for

i\geq\ell+1
, we can write

f^{-\ell}X_{i-\ell}
as a disjoint union

f^{-\ell}X_{i-\ell}=X_{i}\cup Z_{i,\ell}
where

Z_{i,\ell}\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{\ell-1}f^{-k}Y
.
Hence, for
n\geq\ell+1

\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}}g_{\ell}\,d\mu=\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}X_{i}}g_{\ell}\,d\mu+\int(1_{\bigcup_{i=\ell+1}^{n}X_{i-\ell}}g)\circ f^{\ell}\,d\mu-\int_{\bigcup_{i=\ell+1}^{n}Z_{i,\ell}}g_{\ell}\,d\mu.

Moreover,

\left|\int_{\bigcup_{i=\ell+1}^{n}Z_{i,\ell}}g_{\ell}\,d\mu\right|\leq|g|_{\infty}\mu\left(\bigcup_{k=0}^{\ell-1}f^{-k}Y\right)\leq\ell|g|_{\infty}\mu(Y).

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}}g_{\ell}\,d\mu=\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-\ell}X_{i}}g\,d\mu+O(1)=\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}}g\,d\mu+O(1).

Dividing by
\mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i})
, we obtain that
g_{\ell}
is a centred global observable as claimed.
∎

Lemma 3.5

Assume conditions (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) and let

g\in L^{\infty}
. Then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Y}g\circ f^{n}\,d\mu=0
if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\int_{Y_{j+i}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=0\qquad\text{for all $\epsilon>0$}.

In particular, it suffices that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\;j^{\alpha}\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\int_{Y_{j+i}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=0\qquad\text{for all $\epsilon>0$}.

Proof.

Write
Y
as a disjoint union

Y=\bigcup_{j=0}^{n}\big\{y\in Y:f^{n-j}y\in Y,\,\tau(f^{n-j}y)>j\big\}.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{Y}g\circ f^{n}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\int_{X}1_{Y}1_{Y}\circ f^{n-j}1_{\{\tau>j\}}\circ f^{n-j}g\circ f^{n}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\int_{X}1_{Y}(L^{n-j}1_{Y})\,1_{\{\tau>j\}}\,g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu.

By (
2.3
),

\int_{Y}g\circ f^{n}\,d\mu=K\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\int_{Y}1_{\{\tau>j\}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu+O\Big(|g|_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}e_{n-j}\,\mu(y\in Y:\tau(y)>j)\Big)

where
e_{n}=o(a_{n}^{-1})
and
\mu_{Y}(\tau>j)=O(j^{-\alpha})
by (
2.2
).
By Proposition
3.4
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}e_{n-j}\,\mu(y\in Y:\tau(y)>j)=0
.
Hence

\int_{Y}g\circ f^{n}\,d\mu=K\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\int_{Y}1_{\{\tau>j\}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu+o(1).

Next,

\int_{Y}1_{\{\tau\geq(1+\epsilon^{-1})j\}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu\leq|g|_{\infty}\mu_{Y}(\tau\geq\epsilon^{-1}j)\ll(\epsilon^{-1}j)^{-\alpha},

so

\Big|\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\int_{Y}1_{\{\tau\geq(1+\epsilon^{-1})j\}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu\Big|\ll\epsilon^{\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{n-j}^{-1}j^{-\alpha}\ll\epsilon^{\alpha}.

(For the last inequality we applied Proposition
3.4
.)

Also, by (
2.2
),

j^{\alpha}\mu_{Y}(\tau>j)-j^{\alpha}\mu_{Y}(\tau>(1+\epsilon)j)\to\gamma(1-(1+\epsilon)^{-\alpha})

so

\mu_{Y}(j<\tau\leq(1+\epsilon)j)=O(\epsilon)j^{-\alpha}+o(j^{-\alpha})
.
Hence it follows from Proposition
3.4
that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Big|

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\int_{Y}1_{\{\tau<j\leq(1+\epsilon)j\}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu\Big|

\displaystyle\leq|g|_{\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\mu_{Y}(j<\tau\leq(1+\epsilon)j)\ll O(\epsilon).

To summarise, we have shown that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Y}g\circ f^{n}\,d\mu=0

if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\int_{Y}1_{\{1+\epsilon)j<\tau<(1+\epsilon^{-1})j\}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=0
.
This completes the proof of the first statement, and the second statement follows by Proposition
3.4
.
∎

Fix
\epsilon>0
and suppose that
j
is sufficiently large that
\epsilon j\geq n_{0}
. Then

\int_{Y_{j+i}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=\sum_{p=1}^{q}\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\int_{Y_{j+i,r}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu

(3.2)

where
f^{j}
maps each
Y_{j+i,r}
bijectively onto
X_{i,p}
.
Hence
our treatment divides naturally into two subcases depending on whether
g
is mean zero or constant on
X_{n,p}
for
n\geq n_{0}
,
1\leq p\leq q
.

Piecewise constant global observables.

These are global observables
g\in L^{\infty}
that are constant on
X_{n,p}
, i.e.
g|_{X_{n,p}}\equiv\bar{g}_{n,p}
, for all
n,p
, where the constants
\bar{g}_{n,p}
are bounded and satisfy the constraint (
3.1
).

Piecewise mean zero observables.

These are
L^{\infty}
observables
g
such that

\int_{X_{n,p}}g\,d\mu=0
, i.e.
\bar{g}_{n,p}=0
, for all
n,p
.

3.4
Piecewise constant global observables

In this subsection, we prove global-local mixing for piecewise constant global observables. (Condition (
2.4
) is not required for this.)
The proof divides into the cases
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\alpha=1
.

Lemma 3.6

Suppose
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Assume
conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.3
).
Then global-local mixing holds for all piecewise constant centred global observables
g\in L^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Recall from Lemma
3.5
that we need to prove

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\int_{Y_{j+i}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=0\qquad\text{for all $\epsilon>0$}.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, arguments similar to those in the proof of Lemma
3.5
show that this reduces further to

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\epsilon n<j<(1-\epsilon)n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\int_{Y_{j+i}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=0\qquad\text{for all $\epsilon>0$}.

(3.3)

Indeed the contribution from
j=1
to
\epsilon n
is bounded by

|g|_{\infty}\sum_{1\leq j\leq\epsilon n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\mu_{Y}(\tau>j)\ll n^{-(1-\alpha)}\sum_{1\leq j\leq\epsilon n}j^{-\alpha}\ll\epsilon^{1-\alpha},

and the contribution from
j=(1-\epsilon)n
to
n
is bounded by

|g|_{\infty}\sum_{(1-\epsilon)n\leq j\leq n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\mu_{Y}(\tau>j)\ll n^{-\alpha}\sum_{(1-\epsilon)n\leq j\leq n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\ll n^{-\alpha}\sum_{1\leq j\leq\epsilon n}j^{-(1-\alpha)}\ll\epsilon^{\alpha}.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}
be a piecewise constant centred global observable.
Using (
3.2
),

\int_{Y_{j+i}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=\sum_{p=1}^{q}\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\bar{g}_{i,p}\mu(Y_{j+i,r}).

This combined with (
3.3
) means that we have to show that
\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}=0
where

A_{n}=\sum_{i\in R_{n}}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\bar{g}_{i,p}\sum_{j\in S_{i,n}}a_{n-j}^{-1}\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\mu(Y_{j+i,r}).

Here,

R_{n}\subset\big\{\epsilon^{2}n<i<(\epsilon^{-1}-1)n\big\},\qquad S_{i,n}\subset\big\{\epsilon\max\{i,n\}<j\leq\min\{\epsilon^{-1}i,(1-\epsilon)n\}\big\}.

(The containments might be strict since
i
and
j
are integers.)

Step 1: Summation by parts over
j
.

Let
\phi_{i,n}=\min S_{i,n}
,
\Phi_{i,n}=\max S_{i,n}
.
Define

\mu_{k,p}=\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\sum_{n\geq k}\mu(Y_{n,r})
.

Then

A_{n}=\sum_{i\in R_{n}}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\bar{g}_{i,p}B_{i,n,p}
where

B_{i,n,p}=\sum_{j\in S_{i,n}}a_{n-j}^{-1}\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\mu(Y_{j+i,r})=\sum_{\phi_{i,n}\leq j\leq\Phi_{i,n}}a_{n-j}^{-1}\{\mu_{j+i,p}-\mu_{j+i+1,p}\}.

Summation by parts gives

B_{i,n,p}=\sum_{\phi_{i,n}\leq j\leq\Phi_{i,n}}\{a_{n-j}^{-1}-a_{n-j+1}^{-1}\}\mu_{j+i,p}\;+\;\partial_{i,n,p,\phi_{i,n}}\;-\;\partial_{i,n,p,\Phi_{i,n}+1}

where the boundary terms are

\partial_{i,n,p,c_{i,n}}=a_{n-c_{i,n}+1}^{-1}\,\mu_{c_{i,n}+i,p},\qquad c_{i,n}=\phi_{i,n},\,\Phi_{i,n}+1.

Step 2: Simplification of
B_{i,n,p}
.

Let
\tilde{\mu}_{k}=k^{-\alpha}
.
By (
2.1
),

\mu_{k,p}=\gamma_{p}\tilde{\mu}_{k}+o(k^{-\alpha})
.
Using also that
a_{n-c_{i,n}+1}^{-1}\ll n^{-(1-\alpha)}
,
we obtain

\partial_{i,n,p,c_{i,n}}=\gamma_{p}\tilde{\partial}_{i,n,c_{i,n}}+o(n^{-1}).\qquad\tilde{\partial}_{i,n,c_{i,n}}=a_{n-c_{i,n}+1}^{-1}\tilde{\mu}_{c_{i,n}+i}.

Similarly, using in addition that

a_{n-j}^{-1}-a_{n-j+1}^{-1}\ll n^{-(2-\alpha)}
and
\Phi_{i,n}-\phi_{i,n}\ll n
,

\sum_{\phi_{i,n}\leq j\leq\Phi_{i,n}}\{a_{n-j}^{-1}-a_{n-j+1}^{-1}\}\mu_{j+i,p}=\gamma_{p}\sum_{\phi_{i,n}\leq j\leq\Phi_{i,n}}\{a_{n-j}^{-1}-a_{n-j+1}^{-1}\}\tilde{\mu}_{j+i}+o(n^{-1}).

Hence,
B_{i,n,p}=\gamma_{p}\tilde{B}_{i,n}+o(n^{-1})
where

\tilde{B}_{i,n}=\sum_{\phi_{i,n}\leq j\leq\Phi_{i,n}}\{a_{n-j}^{-1}-a_{n-j+1}^{-1}\}\tilde{\mu}_{j+i}\;+\;\tilde{\partial}_{i,n,\phi_{i,n}}\;-\;\tilde{\partial}_{i,n,\Phi_{i,n}+1}

Moreover, reversing the summation by parts argument in Step 1, we see immediately that

\tilde{B}_{i,n}=\sum_{\phi_{i,n}\leq j\leq\Phi_{i,n}}a_{n-j}^{-1}\{\tilde{\mu}_{j+i}-\tilde{\mu}_{j+i+1}\}=\alpha\sum_{\phi_{i,n}\leq j\leq\Phi_{i,n}}a_{n-j}^{-1}(j+i)^{-(\alpha+1)}+O(n^{-2}).

Since

|R_{n}|\ll n
and
|\bar{g}_{i,p}|\leq|g|_{\infty}
,

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in R_{n}}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\bar{g}_{i,p}\Big\{\gamma_{p}\alpha\sum_{\phi_{i,n}\leq j\leq\Phi_{i,n}}a_{n-j}^{-1}(j+i)^{-(\alpha+1)}+o(n^{-1})\Big\}

\displaystyle=\alpha\sum_{i\in R_{n}}\bar{g}_{i}\sum_{\phi_{i,n}\leq j\leq\Phi_{i,n}}a_{n-j}^{-1}(j+i)^{-(\alpha+1)}\;+\;o(1).

Step 3: Summation by parts over
i
.

We have reduced to showing that
\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}=0
where

C_{n}=\sum_{i\in R_{n}}\bar{g}_{i}\sum_{\phi_{i,n}\leq j\leq\Phi_{i,n}}a_{n-j}^{-1}(j+i)^{-(\alpha+1)}=\sum_{\epsilon n<j<(1-\epsilon)n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\bar{g}_{i}(j+i)^{-(\alpha+1)}.

Define

b_{i}=\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{i}k^{-\alpha}\bar{g}_{k},\qquad b_{n}^{*}=\sup_{i\leq n}|b_{i}|,\qquad d_{i,j}=i^{\alpha}(j+i)^{-(\alpha+1)}.

Then
d_{i,j}\ll n^{-1}
and
d_{i,j}-d_{i+1,j}\ll n^{-2}
.
Also,

b_{n}^{*}=o(a_{n})=o(n^{1-\alpha})
by Lemma
3.2
.

Summation by parts over
i
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\bar{g}_{i}(j+i)^{-(\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\bar{g}_{i}i^{-\alpha}d_{i,j}=\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}(b_{i}-b_{i-1})d_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}b_{i}(d_{i,j}-d_{i+1,j})\;+\;O(b_{n}^{*}n^{-1})

\displaystyle\ll n\cdot b_{n}^{*}\cdot n^{-2}+b_{n}^{*}n^{-1}=2b_{n}^{*}n^{-1}=o(n^{-\alpha}).

Hence
|C_{n}|\ll n\cdot n^{-(1-\alpha)}\cdot o(n^{-\alpha})=o(1)
,
completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.7

Suppose
\alpha=1
. Assume
conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.3
).
Then global-local mixing holds for all piecewise constant centred global observables
g\in L^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Notice that

\sum_{(1-\epsilon)n\leq j\leq n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\mu_{Y}(\tau>j)\ll n^{-1}\sum_{(1-\epsilon)n\leq j\leq n}1\leq\epsilon,

so the contribution from
(1-\epsilon)n\leq j\leq n
is negligible as in
the proof of Lemma
3.6
.
However, the contribution from
1\leq j\leq\epsilon n
cannot be immediately ignored.
Also, it is convenient to include the contribution from

1\leq i\leq\epsilon j
.
Hence, we initially reduce to showing that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{1\leq j<(1-\epsilon)n}a_{n-j}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\int_{Y_{j+i}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=0\qquad\text{for all $\epsilon>0$}.

Step 1. Simplifying
a_{n-j}^{-1}
.

The main difference from the proof of Lemma
3.6
is that we can immediately replace
a_{n-j}^{-1}
by
a_{n}^{-1}
. (This idea seems unhelpful when
\alpha<1
.)
Note that
a_{n-j}^{-1}-a_{n}^{-1}\ll(\log n)^{-2}j/n\leq(\log n)^{-2}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{1\leq j<(1-\epsilon)n}\big\{a_{n-j}^{-1}-a_{n}^{-1}\big\}\sum_{1\leq i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\int_{Y_{j+i}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu\Big|

\displaystyle\ll(\log n)^{-2}|g|_{\infty}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\mu_{Y}(\tau>j)

\displaystyle\ll(\log n)^{-1}.

This means that we reduce to showing

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\log n)^{-1}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\,\sum_{1\leq i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\int_{Y_{j+i}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=0,

or equivalently

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\log n)^{-1}A_{n}=0
where

A_{n}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\epsilon^{-1}n}\,\sum_{\epsilon i\leq j\leq n}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\int_{Y_{j+i,r}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\epsilon^{-1}n}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\bar{g}_{i,p}\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\,\sum_{\epsilon i\leq j\leq n}\mu(Y_{j+i,r}).

By (
2.1
),

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\epsilon^{-1}n}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\bar{g}_{i,p}\Big(\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\,\sum_{j\geq\epsilon i}\mu(Y_{j+i,r})-\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\,\sum_{j>n}\mu(Y_{j+i,r})\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\epsilon^{-1}n}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\bar{g}_{i,p}\big(\gamma_{p}i^{-1}+o(i^{-1})+O(n^{-1})\big)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\epsilon^{-1}n}\sum_{p=1}^{q}\bar{g}_{i,p}\gamma_{p}i^{-1}\;+\;o(\log n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\epsilon^{-1}n}\bar{g}_{i}i^{-1}\;+\;o(\log n)=o(\log n),

where the last estimate follows from Lemma
3.2
.
∎

3.5
Piecewise mean zero observables

In this subsection, we prove global-local mixing for piecewise mean zero observables.

Lemma 3.8

Assume conditions (
2.1
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).
Then global-local mixing holds for all piecewise mean zero observables
g\in L^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
c_{j,i,p}
be as in (
2.4
).
Since
g
is piecewise mean zero,

\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\int_{Y_{j+i,r}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=\int_{X_{i,p}}gJ_{j,i,p}\,d\mu=\int_{X_{i,p}}g(J_{j,i,p}-c_{j,i,p})\,d\mu,

so by Proposition
3.1
,

\Big|\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\int_{Y_{j+i,r}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu\Big|\ll j^{-\alpha}|g|_{\infty}\sup_{X_{i,p}}|J_{j,i,p}-c_{j,i,p}|

for all
\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j
.
By (
2.4
),

\lim_{j\to\infty}\;j^{\alpha}\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\int_{Y_{j+i,r}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=0.

By (
3.2
), we obtain

\lim_{j\to\infty}j^{\alpha}\sum_{\epsilon j<i<\epsilon^{-1}j}\int_{Y_{j+i}}g\circ f^{j}\,d\mu=0

so the result follows from Lemma
3.5
.
∎

4
Examples

In this section, we verify the hypotheses of Theorem
2.6
for a variety of examples, thereby proving full global-local mixing.
Most of the examples
are AFN maps
[
33
,
34
]
and the necessary background is contained in
Subsection
4.1
.
In Subsection
4.2
, we consider nonMarkovian maps with one neutral fixed point.
In Subsection
4.3
, we consider intermittent maps with several neutral fixed points.
In Subsection
4.4
, we treat a higher-dimensional nonMarkovian intermittent map introduced in
[
12
]
.
In Subsection
4.5
, we consider parabolic rational maps of the complex plane.

4.1
Background on AFN maps

Zweimüller
[
33
,
34
]
studied a class of nonMarkovian interval maps

f:X\to X
(
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}
a closed bounded interval) with indifferent fixed points. It is assumed that there
is a measurable partition
\mathcal{P}
of
X
into open intervals such that
f
is
C^{2}
and
strictly monotone on each
I\in\mathcal{P}
.
The map is said to be AFU if:

(A)

Adler’s condition:

f^{\prime\prime}/(f^{\prime})^{2}
is bounded on
\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{P}}I
;

(F)

Finite images:

\{fI:I\in\mathcal{P}\}
is finite;

(U)

Uniform expansion:
There exists
\rho>1
such that

|f^{\prime}|\geq\rho
on
\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{P}}I
.

The map is AFN if condition (U) is relaxed to condition (N) where
|f^{\prime}|\geq 1
and there is a finite number of neutral fixed points
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d}
satisfying certain properties such that
|f^{\prime}|>1
on
X\setminus\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\}
. In our examples, there
exist
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
b_{1},\dots,b_{d}>0
such that

|f(x)-x|\sim b_{k}|x-\xi_{k}|^{1+1/\alpha},\qquad|f^{\prime}(x)-1|\sim b_{k}(1+1/\alpha)|x-\xi_{k}|^{1/\alpha},

(4.1)

as
x\to\xi_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,d
.
Condition (N) is shown in
[
33
,
34
]
to be satisfied for such maps.

We will make use of the
following basic distortion estimate.
Let
M=\sup|f^{\prime\prime}|/(f^{\prime})^{2}
. By Adler’s condition,
M<\infty
.

Proposition 4.1

|f^{\prime}(x)^{-1}-f^{\prime}(y)^{-1}|\leq Me^{2M}|x-y|

for all
x,y\in I
and all
I\in\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

First, note that

|f^{\prime}(x)/f^{\prime}(y)|\leq e^{M}
for all
x,y\in I
(see
[
1
, Section 4.3]

for details).
Hence for
x,y\in I
with
x<y
,

\frac{f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)}{f^{\prime}(x)f^{\prime}(y)}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)^{2}}\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(x)}\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(y)}(y-x)\leq Me^{2M}(y-x)

for some
z\in(x,y)
by the mean value theorem.
∎

Topologically mixing AFN maps are conservative and exact with respect to Lebesgue measure and admit a unique (up to scaling) equivalent
\sigma
-finite invariant measure
\mu
.
When there is at least one neutral fixed point,
\mu(X)=\infty
.
Moreover
\mu(X\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{d}B_{\epsilon}(\xi_{1}))<\infty
for all
\epsilon>0
.

Global observables

For AFN maps, it is natural to define
g\in L^{\infty}
to be a global observable if
there exists
\bar{g}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\int_{X\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{d}B_{\epsilon}(\xi_{k})}g\,d\mu}{\mu({X\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{d}B_{\epsilon}(\xi_{k})})}=\bar{g}.

For our examples, it is easily seen that this is equivalent to our abstract Definition
2.2
.

Verification of conditions (
2.1
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) for AFN maps

We induce to an appropriate set
Y
with
0<\mu(Y)<\infty
bounded away from the neutral fixed points and normalise so that
\mu(Y)=1
.
By general arguments
[
33
,
34
]
, the induced map

F=f^{\tau}:Y\to Y
is an AFU map; the properties (A) and (F) are inherited from

f
and clearly
|F^{\prime}|\geq|f^{\prime}|
which is uniformly larger than
1
on
Y
.
With extra care, we are able to ensure that
F
is topologically mixing and hence mixing.
Standard arguments, repeated in the examples below, show that (
2.1
) is satisfied; moreover

\mu(Y_{n})\sim\alpha\gamma n^{-(\alpha+1)}
for some
\gamma>0
.
Hence condition (
2.3
) is satisfied by
[
14
,
22
]
.

It follows that the main effort focuses on condition (
2.4
).
One ingredient is the following:

Proposition 4.2

For AFN maps, the density

h=d\mu/d\operatorname{Leb}
has the property that
the one-sided limits
h(y^{\pm})\in(0,\infty)

exist for all
y\in Y
.

Proof.

Since
F
is an AFU map,

h|_{Y}
lies in
\operatorname{BV}(Y)
and is bounded away from zero
[
25
]
.
This implies the result.
∎

Remark 4.3

In the special case where the branches
f|_{I}
are full for each
I\in\mathcal{P}

(so
\overline{fI}=X
for
I\in\mathcal{P}
), these maps were studied by Thaler
[
29
,
30
]
.
It is then the case that
F:Y\to Y
is an AFU map with full branches. (In particular,
F
is Gibbs-Markov.)
The density
h
has the property that
h|_{Y}
is Lipschitz (see for example
[
16
, Proposition 2.5]
). More generally, if

F
is a Gibbs-Markov map, then
h|_{Y}
is uniformly piecewise Lipschitz on the image partition (namely the partition of
Y
generated by the images of the branches of
F
).

Next, we mention the following convenient formula for

J_{j,n,p}
from Lemma
3.8
expressed in terms of

h|_{Y}
. (This result does not use the AFN structure.)

Proposition 4.4

For
x\in X_{n,p}
,

J_{j,n,p}(x)=\frac{\sum_{\psi(r)=p}h(y_{j,n,p})\big|(f^{j})^{\prime}(y_{j,n,r})\big|^{-1}}{\sum_{\psi(r)=p}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}h(y_{\ell,n,r})\big|(f^{\ell})^{\prime}(y_{\ell,n,r})\big|^{-1}}

where
y_{\ell,n,r}\in Y_{n+\ell,r}
with
f^{\ell}y_{\ell,n,r}=x
.

Proof.

It follows from the usual formula

\mu=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}f^{\ell}_{*}(\mu|_{\{y\in Y:\tau(y)>\ell\}})

for constructing
\mu
on
X
from
\mu|_{Y}
that

J_{j,n,p}=\displaystyle d\sum_{\psi(r)=p}(f^{j}|_{Y_{j+n,r}})_{*}\mu\,\Big/\,d\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi(r)=p}(f^{\ell}|_{Y_{\ell+n,r}})_{*}\mu

for
j,n,p\geq 1
.
The result is a direct consequence of this.
∎

Proposition 4.5

Let
B>0
. Suppose that uniformly in
r\in{\mathbb{A}}
,

(a)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{Y_{n,r}}|h-h_{r}|=0
;

(b)

\sup_{\ell,\,n\geq 1}\sup_{Y_{\ell+n,r}}\Big({\displaystyle\frac{n}{\ell+n}}\Big)^{B+1}|(f^{\ell})^{\prime}|<\infty
; and

(c)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{1\leq\ell\leq Kn}\sup_{Y_{\ell+n,r}}\Big|\Big({\displaystyle\frac{n}{\ell+n}}\Big)^{B+1}|(f^{\ell})^{\prime}|-\omega_{r}\Big|=0

for all fixed
K\geq 1
.

Then condition (
2.4
) holds with
c_{j,n,p}=Bn^{B}(j+n)^{-(B+1)}
.

Proof.

We take
K>\epsilon^{-1}
.
By (a) and (c),

h(y_{\ell,n,r})|(f^{\ell})^{\prime}(y_{\ell,n,r})|^{-1}=h_{r}\omega_{r}^{-1}n^{B+1}(\ell+n)^{-(B+1)}(1+e_{\ell,n,r}(x))

where
\lim_{n\to\infty}e_{\ell,n,r}(x)=0
uniformly in
1\leq\ell\leq Kn
,
r

and
x\in X_{n,p}
.
Hence by (b),

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 1}h(y_{\ell,n,r})|(f^{\ell})^{\prime}(y_{\ell,n,r})|^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{Kn}h(y_{\ell,n,r})|(f^{\ell})^{\prime}(y_{\ell,n,r})|^{-1}+\sum_{\ell>Kn}h(y_{\ell,n,r})|(f^{\ell})^{\prime}(y_{\ell,n,r})|^{-1}

\displaystyle=B^{-1}h_{r}\omega_{r}^{-1}n\big\{1+\tilde{e}_{n,r}(x)+O(K^{-B})\big\}

where
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{e}_{n,r}(x)=0
uniformly in
r
and

x\in X_{n,p}
.
Since
K
is arbitrarily large,

J_{j,n,p}=\frac{Bn^{B}(j+n)^{-(B+1)}(1+e_{j,n,r})}{1+\tilde{e}_{n,r}}

where
\lim_{n\to\infty}e_{j,n,r}(x)=0
uniformly in
j<\epsilon^{-1}n
,
r

and
x\in X_{n,p}
.
We obtain

J_{j,n,p}-c_{j,n,p}=Bn^{B}(j+n)^{-(B+1)}\Big\{\frac{e_{j,n,r}-\tilde{e}_{n,r}}{1+\tilde{e}_{n,r}}\Big\}.

Hence

\sum_{\epsilon j<n\leq\epsilon^{-1}j}|J_{j,n,p}-c_{j,n,p}|\ll\sup_{\epsilon j<n\leq\epsilon^{-1}j}\Big|\frac{e_{j,n,r}-\tilde{e}_{n,r}}{1+\tilde{e}_{n,r}}\Big|

and (
2.4
) follows.
∎

4.2
Maps with one neutral fixed point

We begin with examples where there is one neutral fixed point at
0

(so
f^{\prime}(0)=1
) and
f^{\prime}>1
on
(0,1]
.
Special cases are the Farey map, generalised Pomeau-Manneville maps and generalised LSV maps considered in
[
4
,
5

Proposition 4.8

(4.2)

Finally,

\displaystyle x_{n-1}

\displaystyle-x_{n}=f_{0}x_{n}-x_{n}=bx_{n}^{1+1/\alpha}+O(x_{n}^{1+\kappa})

\displaystyle=b(b^{\prime})^{1+1/\alpha}n^{-(\alpha+1)}(1+O(n^{-\kappa^{\prime}})+O(n^{-\alpha(1+\kappa))})=b^{\prime\prime}n^{-(\alpha+1)}+O(n^{-(\alpha+\kappa^{\prime}+1)}),

By Proposition

Lemma 4.9

The diffeomorphism
f^{j}|_{Y_{j+n}}:Y_{j+n}\to X_{n}
satisfies

\{(f^{j})^{\prime}\}^{-1}=\{f_{1}^{\prime}(\eta_{1})\}^{-1}\Big(\frac{n}{j+n}\Big)^{\alpha+1}\big(1+O(n^{-\kappa^{\prime}})\big),

where the implied constant is independent of
j
and
n
uniformly on
Y
.

Proof.

Let

.
By Proposition
4.8
,

satisfies

f^{k}y=b^{\prime}(j+n-k)^{-\alpha}+O((j+n-k)^{-(\alpha+\kappa^{\prime})}).

Note that

\log f_{0}^{\prime}(x)=\log(1+b(1+1/\alpha)x^{1/\alpha}+O(x^{\kappa}))=b(1+1/\alpha)x^{1/\alpha}+O(x^{\kappa}).

Since
b(b^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}=\alpha
, we obtain

\displaystyle\log f_{0}^{\prime}(f^{k}y)

\displaystyle=(\alpha+1)(j+n-k)^{-1}\big\{1+O((j+n-k)^{-\kappa^{\prime}})\big\}+O((j+n-k)^{-\alpha\kappa})

\displaystyle=(\alpha+1)(j+n-k)^{-1}+O((j+n-k)^{-(1+\kappa^{\prime})}),

for

.
Hence

\displaystyle\log(f^{j})^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle=\log f_{1}^{\prime}(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\log f_{0}^{\prime}(f^{k}y)

\displaystyle=\log f_{1}^{\prime}(y)+(\alpha+1)\sum_{k=n+1}^{j+n-1}(k^{-1}+O(k^{-(1+\kappa^{\prime})}))

\displaystyle=\log f_{1}^{\prime}(y)+(\alpha+1)\log\frac{j+n}{n}+O(n^{-\kappa^{\prime}}).

It follows that

(f^{j})^{\prime}(y)=f_{1}^{\prime}(y)\big(\frac{j+n}{n}\big)^{\alpha+1}\big(1+O(n^{-\kappa^{\prime}})\big)

and hence

\{(f^{j})^{\prime}(y)\}^{-1}=\{f_{1}^{\prime}(y)\}^{-1}\Big(\frac{n}{j+n}\Big)^{\alpha+1}\big(1+O(n^{-\kappa^{\prime}})\big).

Finally, by Proposition
4.1
and the estimate

|\{f^{\prime}(y)\}^{-1}-\{f^{\prime}(\eta_{1})\}^{-1}|\ll|y-\eta_{1}|\ll\sum_{k=n+j}^{\infty}k^{-(\alpha+1)}\ll n^{-\alpha}\leq n^{-\kappa^{\prime}}

and the result follows.
∎

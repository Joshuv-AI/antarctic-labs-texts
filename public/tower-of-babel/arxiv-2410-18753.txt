Twisted conjugacy growth series of virtually abelian groups
Alex Evetts, Maarten Lathouwers

Abstract
. We initiate the study of the twisted conjugacy growth series of a finitely generated group, the formal power series associated to the twisted conjugacy growth function. Our main result is that, for a virtually abelian group, this series is always an explicitly computable ℕ \mathbb{N} -rational function. As a corollary, we obtain a similar result for the relative growth series of a twisted conjugacy class in a virtually abelian group.

1.
Introduction

In finitely generated groups, it is long established to study the
(word) growth
of the group, i.e. the function that counts how many group elements can be represented by words of a fixed length in the generators (and their inverses). A standard reference is
[
22
]
. In 1988, Babenko
[
2
]
was the first to introduce the concept of
conjugacy growth
where one is interested in conjugacy classes that are represented by words of a fixed length. The conjugacy growth function has been studied extensively (see for example
[
19
]
for an overview). As with all growth functions, one can associate a formal power series, called the
conjugacy growth series
, to the conjugacy growth. In
[
1
]
and
[
8
]
, it was shown that a hyperbolic group has a rational conjugacy growth series if and only if it is virtually cyclic, which confirmed a conjecture of Rivin. Other work on conjugacy growth includes
[
6
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
.

Conjugacy in a group
G
can be generalised by considering an endomorphism
\varphi\in\End(G)
. More precisely, we say that two elements
a,b\in G
are
\varphi
-twisted conjugate
(also known as
twisted conjugate
) if there exists some
c\in G
such that
a=cb\varphi(c)^{-1}
. This induces an equivalence relation on the group
G
and partitions it into
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy classes
(or
twisted conjugacy classes
). A group
G
has the
R_{\infty}
-property
if all automorphisms of
G
give rise to an infinite number of twisted conjugacy classes. This property has particularly nice connections with fixed point theory (see
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
) and has been extensively studied (see for example
[
9
]
,
[
23
]
). Instead of only wondering if a morphism induces an infinite number of twisted conjugacy classes, the
twisted conjugacy growth
studies how the number of twisted conjugacy classes (with a representative of increasing length) behaves.

In
[
3
]
, Benson studied the growth of finitely generated virtually abelian groups by proving that the standard (weighted) growth series is rational. His techniques, including for example the use of polyhedral sets, were used multiple times in order to prove rationality results for virtually abelian groups. In
[
13
]
, the first named author generalised Benson’s techniques to show that the (weighted) conjugacy growth series and the (weighted) coset growth series are rational for all virtually abelian groups. In Section
3
, we make the arguments of the latter constructive. For more applications of polyhedral sets to virtually abelian groups, see
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
. In
[
10
]
, the second named author began the systematic study of twisted conjugacy growth by considering finitely generated virtually abelian groups. The current paper continues the extensive work on virtually abelian groups by proving that the formal power series that is associated to the twisted conjugacy growth, namely the
twisted conjugacy growth series
, is always rational, and can moreover be explicitly computed.

The
\mathbb{N}
-rational
functions are a proper subset of the rational functions (see Definition
2.7
). We say that such a function is
explicitly computable
to mean that there exists an algorithm taking appropriate inputs that outputs the required function (see Definition
2.8
).

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group with a finite weighted (monoid) generating set
\Sigma
and
\varphi\in\End(G)
. Then the
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy growth series of
G
with respect to
\Sigma
is an
\mathbb{N}
-rational function. Moreover, given a description for
G
, the
\mathbb{N}
-rational function is explicitly computable.

In Section
2
we state the definitions of the different types of growth and their associated formal power series; we explain the theory of (effectively constructible) polyhedral sets and we define the input data for finitely generated virtually abelian groups. In Section
3
, we repeat the construction from
[
13
]
to obtain geodesic coset representatives and argue that this can be done in an effective way. In Section
4
, we describe the twisted conjugacy classes in virtually abelian groups and use this in combination with the work from
[
3
]
and from Section
3
to prove Theorem
A
. We conclude the paper with a consequence of our description of twisted conjugacy classes, namely that the growth series counting just the elements of any fixed twisted conjugacy class (the relative growth of the twisted conjugacy class) is rational.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group with a finite weighted (monoid) generating set
\Sigma
and
\varphi\in\End(G)
. Then the relative growth series of every
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class in
G
with respect to
\Sigma
is an
\mathbb{N}
-rational function. Moreover, given a description for
G
, the
\mathbb{N}
-rational function is explicitly computable.

Throughout the paper, we use
\mathbb{N}
for the non-negative integers and
\mathbb{N}_{+}
for the positive integers.

2.
Definitions and basic results

2.1.
Growth

For a group
G
and an endomorphism
\varphi\in\End(G)
we say that two elements
a,b\in G
are
\varphi
-twisted conjugate
(or
twisted conjugate
), denoted
a\sim_{\varphi}b
, if there exists some
c\in G
such that
a=cb\varphi(c)^{-1}
. This induces an equivalence relation
\sim_{\varphi}
on
G
. Its equivalence classes
[a]_{\varphi}
are called
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy classes
(or
twisted conjugacy classes
). Note that if
\varphi=\Id
is the identity morphism, we obtain the usual notion of conjugacy in the group
G
. Twisted conjugacy is indeed a generalisation of regular conjugacy.

Let
G
be moreover finitely generated by some set
\Sigma
. Throughout the paper, we assume that
\Sigma
generates
G
as a monoid, i.e. that we take only positive powers of
\Sigma
. Denote by
\Sigma^{\ast}
the words over the alphabet
\Sigma
and by
\overline{w}\in G
the evaluation of some word
w\in\Sigma^{\ast}
in the group
G
. We call a function
\omega_{\Sigma}:\Sigma\to\mathbb{N}_{+}
a
weight function
on
\Sigma
and we say that
\Sigma
is a
finite weighted generating set
of
G
. It induces a weight function on the elements of
G
by setting for
g\in G

\omega_{\Sigma}(g)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }g=e\\
\min\left\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\omega_{\Sigma}(s_{i}):s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\Sigma,\>\overline{s_{1}\cdots s_{r}}=g\right\},&\text{else.}\end{cases}

We call a word
s_{1}\cdots s_{r}\in\Sigma^{\ast}
a
geodesic representative
of
g\in G
if
\overline{s_{1}\cdots s_{r}}=g
and
\omega_{\Sigma}(g)=\sum_{i}\omega_{\Sigma}(s_{i})
. If
\omega_{\Sigma}(s)=1
for all
s\in\Sigma
, then the induced weight function on
G
coincides with the classical word length
|\cdot|_{\Sigma}
of an element of
G
with respect to
\Sigma
.

Definition 2.1

(Growth functions)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite weighted generating set of
G
. Then we define the weight
\omega_{\Sigma}(U)
of a subset
U\subset G
by

\omega_{\Sigma}(U)=\min\{\omega_{\Sigma}(u):u\in U\}.

For an endomorphism
\varphi\in\End(G)
we define

(1)

the
(weighted) standard growth function

\beta_{G,\omega_{\Sigma}}
by

\beta_{G,\omega_{\Sigma}}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}:r\mapsto\#\{g\in G:\omega_{\Sigma}(g)\leq r\}.

(2)

the
(weighted) (
\varphi
-)twisted conjugacy growth function

c_{G,\omega_{\Sigma}}^{\varphi}
by

c_{G,\omega_{\Sigma}}^{\varphi}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}:r\mapsto\#\{[g]_{\varphi}\in G/\sim_{\varphi}:\omega_{\Sigma}([g]_{\varphi})\leq r\}.

(3)

the
(weighted) relative growth function

\beta_{U\subset G,\omega_{\Sigma}}
of
U
in
G
by

\beta_{U\subset G,\omega_{\Sigma}}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}:r\mapsto\#\{g\in U:\omega_{\Sigma}(g)\leq r\}.

To these growth functions we associate formal power series
B_{G,\omega_{\Sigma}}
,
C_{G,\omega_{\Sigma}}^{\varphi}
and
B_{U\subset G,\omega_{\Sigma}}
by setting, for example,

B_{G,\omega_{\Sigma}}(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\beta_{G,\omega_{\Sigma}}(r)z^{r}

and we call them the (weighted)
standard growth series
,
(
\varphi
-)twisted conjugacy growth series
and
relative growth series
of
U
in
G
, respectively.

2.2.
Polyhedral sets

We make essential use of the notion of a
polyhedral set
, as introduced by Benson and used in
[
3
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
13
]
, and others.

Definition 2.2

(Polyhedral sets)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, and let
\cdot
denote the Euclidean scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

(i)

A subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
of the form

(1)

\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:u\cdot z=a\}
,

(2)

\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:u\cdot z\equiv a\mod b\}
, or

(3)

\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:u\cdot z>a\}

for constants
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
b\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, is called an
elementary set
;

(ii)

a finite intersection of elementary sets is called a
basic polyhedral set
;

(iii)

a finite union of basic polyhedral sets is called a
polyhedral set
.

It turns out that the notion of a polyhedral set is just one way to define a very useful class of subsets of free abelian groups. The class of polyhedral sets coincides with
Presburger definable sets
,
semilinear subsets
of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, and
rational subsets
of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
(for definitions see
[
12
]
and
[
17
]
). For a discussion of the equivalences, and more references, see
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
. The current paper is naturally a generalisation of
[
3
]
and
[
13
]
, so we use the terminology of polyhedral sets for consistency.

Definition 2.3

(Effectively constructible)
.

A polyhedral set
X
is called
effectively constructible
, given some input data, if there exists an algorithm that takes the input data and produces a description for
X
as a finite union of finite intersections of elementary sets.

For the purposes of the current paper, the input data in question may be descriptions of other polyhedral sets, explicit maps between polyhedral sets, or ultimately the description of a virtually abelian group defined in Definition
2.12
. Note that we do not make any claims regarding the complexity of such an algorithm.

Definition 2.4

(Integral affine transformation)
.

A map
\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{k}\to\mathbb{Z}^{l}
, for some positive integers
k,l
, is an
integral affine transformation
if there is an integer-valued
l\times k
matrix
A
and a constant vector
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}
such that
\phi(x)=Ax+b
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
.

We will make use of the following basic results from the literature, proofs of which can be found in
[
3
]
or
[
25
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

If
X
is a polyhedral subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, then the following holds.

(i)

If
Y
is a polyhedral subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, then the sets
X\cap Y
,
X\cup Y
,
X\setminus Y
are polyhedral, and moreover effectively constructible, given the descriptions of
X
and
Y
.

(ii)

If
Z\subset\mathbb{Z}^{l}
is polyhedral for some
l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, then the product
X\times Z
is a polyhedral subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{k+l}
that is effectively constructible from the descriptions of
X
and
Z
.

(iii)

If
\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{k}\to\mathbb{Z}^{l}
and
\psi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{l}\to\mathbb{Z}^{k}
are integral affine transformations, then the image
\phi(X)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{l}
and preimage
\psi^{-1}(X)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{l}
are polyhedral sets, and moreover effectively constructible, given the descriptions of
X
,
\phi
,
\psi
.

In particular, the projection of a polyhedral set
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{k}
onto some subset of the coordinates of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
is an effectively constructible polyhedral set.

The key feature of polyhedral sets for our purposes is that their growth series, with respect to the
\ell_{1}
distance, are rational functions.

Theorem 2.6

(See
[
3
]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{N}^{k}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, be a polyhedral set endowed with the
\ell_{1}
metric. Then its growth series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\#\{g\in X:|g|_{\ell_{1}}\leq n\}z^{n}

is a rational function.

In fact, the growth series turn out to be a particularly restricted form of rational function, which arises in formal language theory. See
[
15
]
and the references therein, and
[
24
, Section II]
.

Definition 2.7

(
\mathbb{N}
-rational functions)
.

The set of
\mathbb{N}
-rational functions
is the smallest class
R
of generating functions in one variable
z
, satisfying

(1)

0
and
z
are in
R
,

(2)

if
f(z)
and
g(z)
are in
R
, then so too are
f(z)+g(z)
and
f(z)g(z)
,

(3)

if
h(z)\in R
is such that
h(0)=0
, then
\frac{1}{1-h(z)}\in R
.

Definition 2.8

(Explicitly computable rational function)
.

We will say that a rational function
p(z)/q(z)
is
explicitly computable
, with respect to some description, if there exists an algorithm taking the description as input and outputting the two polynomials
p(z)
and
q(z)
.

Again, we make no claims regarding the complexity of the algorithm, merely that it exists. With these definitions, Theorem
2.6
can be refined as follows.

Proposition 2.9

(See
[
7
, Proposition 4.14]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, be a polyhedral set endowed with the
\ell_{1}
metric. Then its growth series is
\mathbb{N}
-rational and can be explicitly computed from its description as a polyhedral set.

2.3.
Describing virtually abelian groups

This article asserts that the twisted conjugacy growth series of a finitely generated virtually abelian group can be explicitly computed. For this to make sense, we need to clearly define the input data.

Remark 2.10
.

Every finitely generated (infinite) virtually abelian group
G
has a finite index normal subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
(for some
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
) that is fully characteristic, i.e. this subgroup is invariant under every endomorphism of
G
(see for example
[
10
, Remark 3.3]
).

Following
[
7
]
, we define a standard normal form.

Definition 2.11

(Subgroup-transversal normal form)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group with fully characteristic finite index normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Choose a basis
B=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
for the subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and a transversal
T
for its (right) cosets in
G
. The
subgroup-transversal normal form
for
G
is the set of words
(\{e_{1}\}^{*}\cup\{e_{1}^{-1}\}^{*})\cdots(\{e_{n}\}^{*}\cup\{e_{n}^{-1}\}^{*})T
.

For a finite set
S
, we write
S^{\pm 1}
to denote the union of
S
with the set of all inverses of elements of
S
.

Definition 2.12

(Description of a virtually abelian group)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated (infinite) virtually abelian group, and
\varphi
some endomorphism of
G
. A
description
of
G
and
\varphi
consists of

(1)

a fully characteristic finite index normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
together with a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
B=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
,

(2)

a choice of transversal
T\subset G
for the cosets of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
(where we assume that
1_{G}\in T
),

(3)

a function
f\colon(B^{\pm 1}\cup T)\times(B^{\pm 1}\cup T)\to(B^{\pm 1})^{*}T
that maps a pair
(a,b)
to the subgroup-transversal normal form of the product
\overline{ab}
,

(4)

the images
\varphi(g)
, for each
g\in B^{\pm 1}\cup T
, given in subgroup-transversal normal form,

(5)

a finite set
\Sigma
of generators for
G
(generating
G
as a monoid), each given as a word in
(B^{\pm 1}\cup T)^{*}
,

(6)

a weight function
\omega_{\Sigma}\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{N}_{+}
.

3.
Effective construction of geodesic coset representatives

For a finitely generated virtually abelian group
G
, with any choice of finite generating set, Benson
[
3
]
proved that there exists a set of unique geodesic representatives for the elements of
G
, which can be counted using finitely many polyhedral sets, implying that the standard growth series of
G
is rational. In
[
7
]
, it is shown that the proof can be made effective, and so the growth series can be explicitly calculated. In
[
13
]
, the first named author generalised Benson’s result to obtain unique geodesic representatives for the cosets of any subgroup of
G
, yielding rational coset growth series. In this section, we make this latter proof effective (see Theorem
3.6
and its proof), which is a key step in proving our main Theorem
A
. We closely follow
[
7
, Section 4]
.

Definition 3.1

(Extended finite generating set)
.

Let
G
be virtually abelian, with a finite weighted generating set
\Sigma
and fully characteristic finite index normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.

(1)

Define

S=\{\overline{s_{1}\cdots s_{r}}:s_{i}\in\Sigma,1\leq r\leq[G:\mathbb{Z}^{n}]\}.

We call
S
the
extended finite generating set
of
G
with respect to
\Sigma
.

(2)

If
\omega_{\Sigma}\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{N}_{+}
is the weight function on
\Sigma
, define a weight function on
S
by
\omega_{S}(s)=\omega_{\Sigma}(s)
, for each
s\in S
.

Remark 3.2
.

The extended generating set
S
is also a finite (monoid) generating set for
G
, and

\omega_{\Sigma}(g)=\min\{\omega_{\Sigma}(v):v\in\Sigma^{*},\overline{v}=g\}=\min\{\omega_{S}(u):u\in S^{*},\overline{u}=g\}=\omega_{S}(g)

for every
g\in G
. Thus, changing the generating set has not changed the weight of an element. Since there is no ambiguity, we use
\omega(g)
for the weight of
g\in G
from now on.

Lemma 3.3

(See
[
3
, Remark 11.2]
)
.

Let
G
be finitely generated virtually abelian with
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as a fully characteristic finite index normal subgroup. Then every (non-empty) product
g_{1}\cdots g_{r}
of elements of
G
with
r\geq[G:\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
contains a subproduct
g_{i}g_{i+1}\cdots g_{i+j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Proof.

Consider the prefix subproducts
g_{1},g_{1}g_{2},\ldots,g_{1}\cdots g_{r}
. If
r>[G:\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
then the pigeonhole principle ensures that two such subproducts represent elements of the same
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-coset, say
g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{i-1}
and
g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{i+j}
for some
i,j
. Then

\left(g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{i-1}\right)^{-1}g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{i+j}=g_{i}g_{i+1}\cdots g_{i+j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

In the case
r=[G:\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
, either there is a pair of subproducts in the same coset, or else each subproduct is in a different coset, and in particular one of them is contained in the identity coset
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
∎

Definition 3.4

(Patterns)
.

Let
G
be a virtually abelian group,
S
an extended finite generating set of
G
as in Definition
3.1
and
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
a fully characteristic finite index normal subgroup.

(1)

Let
X=S\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\}
and
Y=S\setminus X=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{s}\}
. Define
P=\{\pi\in Y^{*}:|\pi|_{S}\leq[G:\mathbb{Z}^{n}]\}
to be the (finite) set of
patterns
, that is, all words in
Y^{*}
of length at most the index
[G:\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
.

(2)

For each
\pi=y_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{k}}\in P
with
|\pi|_{S}=k
, define the set
W^{\pi}
of
\pi
-patterned words in
S^{*}
as

\left\{x_{1}^{w_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{w_{r}}y_{i_{1}}x_{1}^{w_{r+1}}\cdots x_{r}^{w_{2r}}y_{i_{2}}x_{1}^{w_{2r+1}}\cdots y_{i_{k}}x_{1}^{w_{kr+1}}\cdots x_{r}^{w_{kr+r}}:\begin{array}[]{l}w_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\text{, for}\\
1\leq i\leq kr+r\end{array}\right\}.

(3)

For each
\pi\in P
with
|\pi|_{S}=k
, define the bijection
\phi_{\pi}\colon W^{\pi}\to\mathbb{N}^{kr+r}
recording the powers of the generators
x_{i}

\phi_{\pi}\colon x_{1}^{w_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{w_{r}}y_{i_{1}}x_{1}^{w_{r+1}}\cdots x_{r}^{w_{2r}}y_{i_{2}}x_{1}^{w_{2r+1}}\cdots y_{i_{k}}x_{1}^{w_{kr+1}}\cdots x_{r}^{w_{kr+r}}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}\\
w_{2}\\
\vdots\\
w_{kr+r}\end{pmatrix}.

We write
m_{\pi}=kr+r=(|\pi|_{S}+1)r
.

By the following proposition, we only need to consider the finitely many patterns in
P
and their corresponding sets of patterned words to represent geodesics in our group.

Proposition 3.5

(See
[
3
, Proposition 11.3]
)
.

For each element
g\in G
, there exists some
\pi\in P
such that
W^{\pi}
contains a geodesic representative for
g
.

A non-effective version of the next result appears in the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 4.2]
. In the rest of this section, we explain how to make the result effective and hence derive the following result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
G
be a virtually abelian group with finite generating set
\Sigma
and a fully characteristic finite index normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Let
H
be a subgroup contained in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
(given by a basis written in terms of the standard basis elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
). Then for each pattern
\pi\in P
, there exists a set of geodesics
U^{\pi}_{H}\subset W^{\pi}\subset\Sigma^{*}
such that
\phi_{\pi}(U^{\pi}_{H})\subset\mathbb{N}^{m_{\pi}}
is an effectively constructible polyhedral set, and the disjoint union
\bigcup_{\pi\in P}U^{\pi}_{H}
comprises a complete set of unique representatives for the right cosets
G/H
.

Remark 3.7
.

If
w\in W^{\pi}
, then since
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is normal in
G
, the coset
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\overline{w}
represented by the element
\overline{w}
depends only on the pattern
\pi
.

Notation 3.8
.

Recall from Definition
2.12
that we use
T
to denote a fixed transversal for the cosets of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
in
G
. For each
\pi\in P
, define
t_{\pi}\in T
by requiring that

\overline{\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}t_{\pi}.

We now demonstrate how to pass between a patterned word and the subgroup-transversal normal form of the element it represents. The following proposition is a restatement of
[
13
, Definition 2.10]
. We include a proof for completeness, and to emphasise the fact that the process is effective.

Notation 3.9
.

For
h\in G
we denote by

\tau_{h}:G\to G:g\mapsto hgh^{-1}

the inner automorphism of
G
given by conjugation by
h
.

Recall that we fixed a basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as input data (see Definition
2.12
). We identify every element of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with its coordinates with respect to this basis. Moreover, we use additive notation in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
in parallel to the multiplicative notation used for
G
. For example, for
e_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots e_{n}^{a_{n}},e_{1}^{b_{1}}\dots e_{n}^{b_{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
t\in T
, we use

\left(\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}\\
\vdots\\
b_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right)t\quad\text{instead of}\quad e_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots e_{n}^{a_{n}}e_{1}^{b_{1}}\dots e_{n}^{b_{n}}t.

Proposition 3.10
.

Given a description of a virtually abelian group
G
(as in Definition
2.12
), fix a pattern
\pi\in P
and a coset representative
t\in T
. Then there are

, vectors

and integers

(for

\tau_{t}(\overline{w})=\left(\begin{pmatrix}A_{1,t}^{\pi}\cdot\phi_{\pi}(w)\\
\vdots\\

\vdots\\
B_{n,t}^{\pi}\end{pmatrix}\right)s\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{t}(\overline{\pi})=\begin{pmatrix}B_{1,t}^{\pi}\\
\vdots\\
B_{n,t}^{\pi}\end{pmatrix}s.

Furthermore, we can find a vector

and integer

.

Proof.

Since
\tau_{t}
(for each

) is an automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

matrix

. Partitioning the generating set into

, and we can again determine the corresponding matrix

, for every

. Now write

of

\vdots\\
v_{r}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

.

Now for a
\pi
-patterned word

\displaystyle w=x_{1}^{w_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{w_{r}}y_{i_{1}}x_{1}^{w_{r+1}}\cdots x_{r}^{w_{2r}}y_{i_{2}}x_{1}^{w_{2r+1}}\cdots y_{i_{k}}x_{1}^{w_{kr+1}}\cdots x_{r}^{w_{kr+r}},

,

\displaystyle\tau_{t}(\overline{w})

\displaystyle=\tau_{t}\left(\left(\Delta\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}\\
\vdots\\
w_{r}\end{pmatrix}+T_{y_{i_{1}}}\Delta\begin{pmatrix}w_{r+1}\\
\vdots\\
w_{2r}\end{pmatrix}+\cdots+T_{y_{i_{1}}}\cdots T_{y_{i_{k}}}\Delta\begin{pmatrix}w_{kr+1}\\
\vdots\\

\displaystyle=\left(T_{t}\Delta\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}\\
\vdots\\

\displaystyle=\left(T_{t}\Delta~|~T_{t}T_{y_{i_{1}}}\Delta~|\cdots|~T_{t}T_{y_{i_{1}}}\cdots T_{y_{i_{k}}}\Delta\right)\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}\\

.

Now for each

and

) an expression

and

\tau_{t}(\overline{w})=\left(\begin{pmatrix}A_{1,t}^{\pi}\cdot\phi_{\pi}(w)\\
\vdots\\

\vdots\\

Finally, let

That is, the

Notation 3.11

We use the case

The following straightforward result uses the vectors

and

to provide a criterion for two words representing elements of the same coset.

Proposition 3.12

(See
[
13
, Proposition 4.3]
)
.

Let

and

for some
\pi,\mu\in P
. Suppose that

(1)

t_{\pi}=t_{\mu}
, and

(2)

there exist integers

A_{i}^{\pi}\cdot\phi_{\pi}(v)+B_{i}^{\pi}-A_{i}^{\mu}\cdot\phi_{\mu}(w)-B_{i}^{\mu}=e_{i}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}h_{j}

Definition 3.13

(Partial orders on patterned words)
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
contained in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, with basis
\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}\}
as in Proposition

. For each
\pi\in P
, add standard basis vectors
A_{n+2}^{\pi}=e_{1},\ldots,A_{K}^{\pi}=e_{m_{\pi}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{\pi}}
to the set
\{A_{1}^{\pi},\ldots,A_{n+1}^{\pi}\}
which ensures that the extended set
\{A_{1}^{\pi},\ldots,A_{K}^{\pi}\}
spans
\mathbb{Z}^{m_{\pi}}
. Define a relation
\leq_{H,\pi}
on the words in

as follows. We write
v_{1}\leq_{H,\pi}v_{2}
if and only if either
v_{1}=v_{2}
or there exist integers
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
, and

, such that

\displaystyle A_{k}^{\pi}\cdot(\phi_{\pi}(v_{1})-\phi_{\pi}(v_{2}))

\displaystyle=e_{k}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}h_{j},

\displaystyle\text{ for }k

\displaystyle\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

\displaystyle A_{k}^{\pi}\cdot(\phi_{\pi}(v_{1})-\phi_{\pi}(v_{2}))

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\text{ for }k

\displaystyle\in\{n+1,\ldots,n+i-1\},\text{and}

\displaystyle A_{k}^{\pi}\cdot(\phi_{\pi}(v_{1})-\phi_{\pi}(v_{2}))

\displaystyle<0,

\displaystyle\text{ for }k

\displaystyle=n+i.

Remark 3.14
.

It is straightforward to verify that
\leq_{H,\pi}
is a partial order, and moreover (via Proposition
3.12
) that if we restrict to words in
W^{\pi}
representing a single
H
-coset, then
\leq_{H,\pi}
is a total order and in fact a well-order (see
[
13
, Proposition 4.7]
for details).

Since

is the weight of the word

, it follows immediately from Definition

that if

is minimal (with respect to
\leq_{H,\pi}
) amongst all words in

representing the coset

, then it has minimal weight amongst these words also, and is therefore a candidate to be a geodesic representative for that coset. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.15

(Minimal coset representatives)
.

Let
V_{H}^{\pi}
be the unique set of
\leq_{H,\pi}
-minimal
H
-coset representatives in
W^{\pi}
, that is,

V_{H}^{\pi}=\{v\in W^{\pi}:\text{if }w\in W^{\pi}\text{ and }\overline{v}\in H\overline{w}\text{ then }v\leq_{H,\pi}w\}.

It is proved in
[
13
, Proposition 4.7]
that each
\phi_{\pi}(V_{H}^{\pi})
is a polyhedral set. For the case where
H
is the trivial subgroup, it is proved in
[
7
, Lemma 4.9]
, using other results of that article, that these polyhedral sets can be effectively constructed. This latter proof may be immediately generalised to incorporate our subgroup

, and here we simply state the result.

Proposition 3.16
.

For each
\pi\in P
,
\phi_{\pi}(V_{H}^{\pi})
is an effectively constructible polyhedral set.

We now prove the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

The sets of words
V_{H}^{\pi}
that we have constructed above satisfy some of the conditions required of the claimed sets
U_{H}^{\pi}
of Theorem
3.6
. Namely, each
\phi_{\pi}(V_{H}^{\pi})
is an effectively constructible polyhedral set, and the union
\bigcup_{\pi\in P}V_{H}^{\pi}
contains a representative for every coset in
G/H
. Furthermore, by Remark
3.14
, this union will contain a geodesic representative for every coset. However, there may be a coset that is represented by a word in
V_{H}^{\pi}
and a word in
V_{H}^{\mu}
, for different patterns
\pi
and
\mu
. To prove the theorem, we reduce each
V_{H}^{\pi}
to a subset
U_{H}^{\pi}
, removing each such multiple representation, but retaining the property that each
\phi_{\pi}(U_{H}^{\pi})
is polyhedral. Our argument follows the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 4.2]
, but emphasises effectiveness.

For each
\pi\in P
, denote by
\mathcal{A}^{\pi}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{m_{\pi}}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
the integer affine map given by

\mathcal{A}^{\pi}\colon\phi_{\pi}(w)\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}A^{\pi}_{1}\cdot\phi_{\pi}(w)+B^{\pi}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
A^{\pi}_{n+1}\cdot\phi_{\pi}(w)+B^{\pi}_{n+1}\end{pmatrix}.

As in Proposition
3.12
, let
\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}\}
be a basis for
H
. Let
\bm{1}_{j}
denote the vector (of appropriate dimension) with a 1 in the
j
th position and zeroes elsewhere, and let

E_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
e_{i}\cdot h_{1}\\
\vdots\\
e_{i}\cdot h_{d}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n+2+d}.

Letting
p_{m}
denote projection onto the first
m
coordinates (which is an affine integer map), define polyhedral subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{2n+2}
as follows.

\displaystyle\Theta_{0}

\displaystyle=p_{2n+2}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\left\{\theta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n+2+d}\colon\theta\cdot\left(\bm{1}_{i}-\bm{1}_{i+n+1}-E_{i}\right)=0\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\Theta_{=}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{0}\cap\{\theta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n+2}:\theta\cdot(\bm{1}_{n+1}-\bm{1}_{2n+2})=0\}

\displaystyle\Theta_{>}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{0}\cap\{\theta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n+2}:\theta\cdot(\bm{1}_{n+1}-\bm{1}_{2n+2})>0\}.

Here
\Theta_{0}
is used to detect pairs of words representing the same
H
-coset. That is,
(\mathcal{A}^{\pi}\circ\phi_{\pi}(v),\mathcal{A}^{\mu}\circ\phi_{\mu}(w))\in\Theta_{0}
precisely when there is a tuple of coefficients
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
representing an element
h
, in terms of the basis
\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}\}
, such that
\overline{v}=h\overline{w}
(via the criterion of Proposition
3.12
). Building on that,
\Theta_{=}
will be used to detect pairs of words of equal weight representing the same
H
-coset, whereas
\Theta_{>}
will detect pairs of words where the first has larger weight, but they still represent the same
H
-coset.

For each pair
(\pi,\mu)\in P^{2}
satisfying
t_{\pi}=t_{\mu}
, define two subsets of
V_{H}^{\pi}
,

R_{=}^{\pi,\mu}=(\mathcal{A}^{\pi}\circ\phi_{\pi})^{-1}\circ p_{n+1}\bigg(\big((\mathcal{A}^{\pi}\circ\phi_{\pi}(V_{H}^{\pi}))\times(\mathcal{A}^{\mu}\circ\phi_{\mu}(V_{H}^{\mu}))\big)\>\cap\>\Theta_{=}\bigg)

(1)

and

R_{>}^{\pi,\mu}=(\mathcal{A}^{\pi}\circ\phi_{\pi})^{-1}\circ p_{n+1}\bigg(\big((\mathcal{A}^{\pi}\circ\phi_{\pi}(V_{H}^{\pi}))\times(\mathcal{A}^{\mu}\circ\phi_{\mu}(V_{H}^{\mu}))\big)\>\cap\>\Theta_{>}\bigg).

(2)

It follows from Propositions
3.10
and
3.12
that two words
w\in V_{H}^{\pi}
and
v\in V_{H}^{\mu}
represent the same
H
-coset and satisfy
\omega(w)>\omega(v)
if and only if
t_{\pi}=t_{\mu}
and
\left(\mathcal{A}^{\pi}(\phi_{\pi}(w)),\mathcal{A}^{\mu}(\phi_{\mu}(v))\right)\in\Theta_{>}
. Similarly,
w
and
v
represent the same
H
coset and satisfy
\omega(w)=\omega(v)
if and only if
t_{\pi}=t_{\mu}
and
\left(\mathcal{A}^{\pi}(\phi_{\pi}(w)),\mathcal{A}^{\mu}(\phi_{\mu}(v))\right)\in\Theta_{=}
.

From this observation, we have that

\displaystyle R^{\pi,\mu}_{=}=\{w\in V_{H}^{\pi}:\exists v\in V_{H}^{\mu}~\text{such that}~\overline{w}\in H\overline{v}~\text{and}~\omega(w)=\omega(v)\},

and

\displaystyle R^{\pi,\mu}_{>}=\{w\in V_{H}^{\pi}:\exists v\in V_{H}^{\mu}~\text{such that}~\overline{w}\in H\overline{v}~\text{and}~\omega(w)>\omega(v)\}.

Removing from
V_{H}^{\pi}
all of the sets
R_{>}^{\pi,\mu}
ensures that there are no non-geodesic coset representatives. To ensure that we do not have multiple geodesic representatives of the same coset, we use the sets
R_{=}^{\pi,\mu}
, but we must make a choice as to which of two equally weighted words to remove. To that end, fix an arbitrary total order
<
on the set
P
, and remove the set
R_{=}^{\pi,\mu}
precisely when
\mu<\pi
. In other words, if two coset representatives have equal weight, choose to keep the one whose pattern comes first in the order. More formally, for each
\pi
, let

U_{H}^{\pi}=V_{H}^{\pi}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\neq\mu\\
t_{\pi}=t_{\mu}\end{subarray}}R_{>}^{\pi,\mu}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu<\pi\\
t_{\pi}=t_{\mu}\end{subarray}}R_{=}^{\pi,\mu}\right).

By construction, the disjoint union
\bigcup_{\pi\in P}U_{H}^{\pi}
consists of exactly one geodesic representative for each
H
-coset. Furthermore, since
\Theta_{=}
,
\Theta_{>}
, and all
\phi_{\pi}(V_{H}^{\pi})
are polyhedral and effectively constructible, so too are the sets

\phi_{\pi}(U_{H}^{\pi})=\phi_{\pi}(V_{H}^{\pi})\setminus\left(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\neq\mu\\
t_{\pi}=t_{\mu}\end{subarray}}\phi_{\pi}(R_{>}^{\pi,\mu})\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu<\pi\\
t_{\pi}=t_{\mu}\end{subarray}}\phi_{\pi}(R_{=}^{\pi,\mu})\right),

by Proposition
2.5
.
∎

4.
Twisted conjugacy growth of virtually abelian groups

This section describes the twisted conjugacy classes of an arbitrary finitely generated virtually abelian group
G
. We argue that the twisted conjugacy growth series is
\mathbb{N}
-rational. Moreover, if the fully characteristic finite index normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of
G
and
\varphi\in\End(G)
are given as in Definition
2.12
, the exact form of the rational series may be computed. Let
S
denote the extended generating set (as introduced in Definition
3.1
) with respect to a finite weighted generating set
\Sigma
of
G
and let
T
be a transversal for the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-cosets in
G
, with the group identity representing the identity coset. Hence, we can write every
g\in G
uniquely as

g=x_{g}t_{g}\quad\text{with }x_{g}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\text{ and }t_{g}\in T.

We will use, for a word
w\in S^{\ast}
, the notation
t_{w}
and
x_{w}
instead of
t_{\overline{w}}
and
x_{\overline{w}}
, respectively.

Definition 4.1
.

For every
g\in G
and
\varphi\in\End(G)
we define the subgroup
H(g)
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by setting

H(g)=\Image(\Id_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}).

Since
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\lhd G
is fully characteristic and normal, for
g\in G
, the map
\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is indeed an endomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and thus
H(g)
is well-defined. Moreover, note that if
g\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}h
for some
g,h\in G
, then
H(g)=H(h)
. In other words,
H(g)
only depends on the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-coset of
g
. In particular, if
w\in W^{\pi}
for a pattern
\pi\in P
, then
H(\overline{w})=H(\overline{\pi})
.

Recall that, as in the proof of Proposition
3.10
,
\tau_{\overline{\pi}}
can be explicitly expressed as a matrix. Moreover, we can find a matrix representing the linear map
\Id_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
, and hence a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for its image,
H(g)
.

Notation 4.2
.

For two subsets
A
and
B
of
G
and an endomorphism
\varphi\in\End(G)
we write
{}^{B,\varphi}A
for the set of
\varphi
-twisted conjugates of elements from
A
by elements from
B
, i.e.

{}^{B,\varphi}A=\{ba\varphi(b)^{-1}:a\in A,\>b\in B\}.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
g\in G
and
\varphi\in\End(G)
, then

[g]_{\varphi}=\bigcup_{t\in T}{}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}t,\varphi}\{g\}.

Moreover, for every
t\in T
we get

{}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}t,\varphi}\{g\}=H(tg\varphi(t)^{-1})tg\varphi(t)^{-1}.

Proof.

Fix
g\in G
and note that

[g]_{\varphi}=\{xt\>g\>\varphi(xt)^{-1}:x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\>t\in T\}=\bigcup_{t\in T}{}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}t,\varphi}\{g\}.

Moreover, if we fix some
t\in T
it follows that

\displaystyle{}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}t,\varphi}\{g\}

\displaystyle=\{xt\>g\>\varphi(xt)^{-1}:x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}=\{x\tau_{tg\varphi(t)^{-1}}(\varphi(x)^{-1})tg\varphi(t)^{-1}:x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}

\displaystyle=\{x-(\tau_{tg\varphi(t)^{-1}}\circ\varphi)(x):x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}tg\varphi(t)^{-1}=H(tg\varphi(t)^{-1})tg\varphi(t)^{-1}

where we have used once more additive notation within the abelian group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for convenience.
∎

Hence, every twisted conjugacy class of
G
is a finite union of cosets of subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The next lemma describes when another element belongs to one of these cosets appearing in this union.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
g\in G
,
\varphi\in\End(G)
and
t\in T
. Then, for every
h\in G
, the following holds

h\in{}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}t,\varphi}\{g\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}t_{h}\in tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\text{, and}\\
x_{h}\in H(tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1})+\tau_{t}(x_{g})+x_{tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1}}\end{cases}.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.3
, we observe, for

, that

\displaystyle h\in{}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}t,\varphi}\{g\}\quad

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad h\in H(tg\varphi(t)^{-1})tg\varphi(t)^{-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:h=z\tau_{tg\varphi(t)^{-1}}(\varphi(z)^{-1})tg\varphi(t)^{-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:x_{h}t_{h}=z\tau_{tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1}}(\varphi(z)^{-1})\tau_{t}(x_{g})tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}t_{h}\in tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\text{, and}\\
x_{h}\in H(tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1})+\tau_{t}(x_{g})+tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1}t_{h}^{-1}\end{cases}.

If
t_{h}\in tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then since

is a transversal it holds by construction that
t_{tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1}}=t_{h}
and thus
tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1}=x_{tt_{g}\varphi(t)^{-1}}t_{h}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

and

, then for every

it holds that

.

Proof.

Since

, there exists some

such that

. Using that
\tau_{t}(z_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is fixed, we obtain, for every
t\in T
, that

\displaystyle{}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}t,\varphi}\{h\}

\displaystyle=\{xth\varphi(xt)^{-1}:x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}=\{xtz_{0}g\varphi(xtz_{0})^{-1}:x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}

\displaystyle=\{x\tau_{t}(z_{0})tg\varphi(x\tau_{t}(z_{0})t)^{-1}:x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}=\{\tilde{x}tg\varphi(\tilde{x}t)^{-1}:\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}

\displaystyle={}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}t,\varphi}\{g\}.

∎

Recall that, by Theorem

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group with a finite weighted (monoid) generating set
\Sigma
and
\varphi\in\End(G)
. Then the
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy growth series of
G
with respect to
\Sigma
is an
\mathbb{N}
-rational function. Moreover, given a description for
G
as in Definition
2.12
, the
\mathbb{N}
-rational function is explicitly computable.

Proof.

Recall the set
P
of patterns from Definition
3.4
. By Lemma
4.7
, for every
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class of
G
we can pick a permissible
D
-tuple
\bm{\pi}\in P^{D}
such that
C(\bm{\pi})
contains a
D
-tuple of words representing elements of this class. Moreover, the weight of the
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class is realised by the word(s) of smallest length in this
D
-tuple. We now reduce the set of possible
\bm{\pi}
s to avoid overlap of candidates for the same
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class.

Consider the set
R
of permissible
D
-tuples of patterns

R=\{(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{D})\in P^{D}:(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{D})\text{ is permissible}\}.

We reduce
R
to a subset
R_{0}
such that no two elements of
R_{0}
are permutations of each other. Note that since we have computed the finite set
P
, we can also compute
R_{0}
. In particular, for every
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class we can still pick
\bm{\pi}\in R_{0}
and a
D
-tuple in
C(\bm{\pi})
representing this class.

Moreover, no
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class is represented by more than one element of
R_{0}
, i.e. there do not exist different
\bm{\pi},\bm{\pi^{\prime}}\in R_{0}
and
D
-tuples of words
\left(w^{(1)},\dots,w^{(D)}\right)\in C(\bm{\pi})
and
\left((w^{\prime})^{(1)},\dots,(w^{\prime})^{(D)}\right)\in C(\bm{\pi^{\prime}})
representing the same
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class. Indeed, since we removed all the permutations of elements in
R
, every candidate
D
-tuple determines a unique element of
R_{0}
. Also by Theorem
3.6
the candidates are unique.

Thus for every
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class
[g]_{\varphi}
(for
g\in G
) there is a unique
\bm{\pi}\in R_{0}
and a unique
D
-tuple
\left(w^{(1)},\dots,w^{(D)}\right)\in C(\bm{\pi})
of words representing elements of this class. Moreover, by Lemma
4.7
,

\omega\left([g]_{\varphi}\right)=\min\{\omega(\overline{w^{(1)}}),\dots,\omega(\overline{w^{(D)}})\}.

Since
\phi_{\bm{\pi}}(C(\bm{\pi}))\subset\mathbb{N}^{m_{\bm{\pi}}}
is an effectively constructible polyhedral set, Proposition
3.19
yields, for
\bm{\pi}\in R_{0}
, a set of words
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\pi}}\subset\Sigma^{*}
with
\mathbb{N}
-rational (weighted) growth series that contains unique, geodesic representatives for the
D
-tuples from
C(\bm{\pi})
. Thus the set
\bigcup_{\bm{\pi}\in R_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\pi}}
contains unique, geodesic representatives for all
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy classes in
G
. To conclude, note that since all
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\pi}}
have an
\mathbb{N}
-rational growth series (explicitly computable by Proposition
3.19
), that

Using the description of a general twisted conjugacy class, given in Lemma

The relative growth function of conjugacy classes and twisted conjugacy classes in virtually abelian groups was shown to be asymptotically equivalent to a polynomial in

Definition 4.9

(Rational set)
.

In a group (or more generally a monoid)
G
, a subset
U
is called
rational
if there exists a finite subset
A\subset G
and a regular language
L\subset A^{*}
such that the set of elements of
G
represented by the words in
L
is exactly
U
.

For more on rational subsets of virtually abelian groups, we direct the reader to
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
25
]
. In particular, we have the following, which is implicit in
[
7
]
.

Proposition 4.10
.

If
U
is a rational subset of a finitely generated virtually abelian group then the relative growth series of
U
is an
\mathbb{N}
-rational function, and can be explicitly computed from a description of the rational set.

By Lemma
4.3
, every twisted conjugacy class in a virtually abelian group is a finite union of cosets of finitely generated subgroups. Such a set is an example of a rational set, the image of a rational expression of the form
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}^{*}a_{i}
, for finite sets
B_{i}
. And thus we have the following result.

Theorem B

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group with a finite weighted (monoid) generating set
\Sigma

\varphi\in\End(G)

\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class in

with respect to
\Sigma
is an
\mathbb{N}
-rational function. Moreover, given a description for
G
as in Definition
2.12
, the
\mathbb{N}
-rational function is explicitly computable.

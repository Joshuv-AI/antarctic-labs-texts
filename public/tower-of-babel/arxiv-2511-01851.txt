Percolation on graphs of polynomial growth is local: analyticity, supercritical sharpness, isoperimetry
Sébastien Martineau, Christoforos Panagiotis

Abstract
We investigate locality of the supercritical regime for Bernoulli percolation on transitive graphs with polynomial growth, by which we mean the following. Take a transitive graph of polynomial growth 𝒢 \mathscr{G} satisfying p c ​ ( 𝒢 ) < 1 p_{c}(\mathscr{G})<1 and take p > p c ​ ( 𝒢 ) p>p_{c}(\mathscr{G}) . Let ℋ \mathscr{H} be another such graph and assume that 𝒢 \mathscr{G} and ℋ \mathscr{H} have the same ball of radius r r for r r large. We prove that various quantities regarding percolation of parameter close to p p on ℋ \mathscr{H} can be well understood from ( 𝒢 , p ) (\mathscr{G},p) alone. This includes uniform versions of supercritical sharpness as well as the Kesten–Zhang bound on the probability of observing a large finite cluster: the constants involved can be chosen to depend only on ( 𝒢 , p ) (\mathscr{G},p) . We also prove that θ ℋ \theta_{\mathscr{H}} is an analytic function of p p in the whole supercritical regime and that, for a suitable ε = ε ⁡ ( 𝒢 , p ) > 0 \varepsilon=\varepsilon(\mathscr{G},p)>0 , the analytic extension of θ ℋ \theta_{\mathscr{H}} to the ε \varepsilon -neighbourhood of p p in ℂ \mathbb{C} is, uniformly, well approximated by the analytic extension of θ 𝒢 \theta_{\mathscr{G}} . The proof relies on new results on the connectivity of minimal cutsets; in particular, we answer a question asked by Babson and Benjamini in 1999. We further discuss connections with the conjecture of non-percolation at criticality.

Introduction

One of the simplest models of statistical mechanics is Bernoulli percolation: if one picks a random subgraph by selecting edges in an i.i.d. way, then investigating the properties of the connected components of this random graph leads to an interesting phase transition.

More precisely, let
\mathscr{G}
be a graph. Throughout this paper, all graphs are assumed to be simple, non-empty, locally finite, and connected. We are interested in Bernoulli bond percolation: each edge of
\mathscr{G}
is either deleted (called closed) or retained (called open) independently with retention probability
p\in[0,1]
to obtain a random subgraph
\omega
of
\mathscr{G}
. We write
\mathbb{P}_{p,\mathscr{G}}
or simply
\mathbb{P}_{p}
for the law of
\omega
. Connected components of
\omega
are referred to as
clusters
. Percolation theory is primarily concerned with the structure of clusters, and how this structure depends on
p
and on the geometry of
\mathscr{G}
. Of particular interest are
phase transitions
, where a small change in
p
imposes a dramatic change of the structure.

Given a transitive graph
\mathscr{G}
and a vertex
o
in
\mathscr{G}
, one can introduce the function

\theta_{\mathscr{G}}:p\longmapsto\mathbb{P}_{p,\mathscr{G}}(o\longleftrightarrow\infty),

where
\{o\longleftrightarrow\infty\}
denotes the event that the cluster of
o
is infinite. The origin
o
can be any vertex of
\mathscr{G}
and all choices of
o
give rise to the same function, by transitivity of
\mathscr{G}
. The function
\theta_{\mathscr{G}}
being non-decreasing, one can define
p_{c}(\mathcal{G})
to be the unique parameter in
[0,1]
such that the following two conditions hold:

•

for all
p\in[0,p_{c}(\mathscr{G}))
, we have
\theta_{\mathscr{G}}(p)=0
,

•

for all
p\in(p_{c}(\mathscr{G}),1]
, we have
\theta_{\mathscr{G}}(p)>0
.

In the most classical framework, one takes
\mathscr{G}
to be a fixed graph within some suitable class of graphs and studies the behaviour of
\theta
and other macroscopic observables as the value of
p
varies. Benjamini and Schramm
[
BS96
]
initiated the systematic study of percolation on transitive graphs, and in recent decades, there has been growing interest in understanding how various percolation observables depend on the underlying graph
\mathscr{G}
. A major conjecture of this field was Schramm’s Locality Conjecture, which we briefly review below.

Given two transitive graphs
\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{H}
, introduce the following quantity:

R(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}):=\max\{r\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}\,:\ B_{\mathscr{G}}(r)\simeq B_{\mathscr{H}}(r)\}.

The quantity
B_{\mathscr{G}}(r)
denotes the
r
-ball of
\mathscr{G}
, and the choice of root is irrelevant as the graph is transitive. Besides, isomorphisms are asked to map the chosen centre of
B_{\mathscr{G}}(r)
to that of
B_{\mathscr{H}}(r)
. We define the
local topology
as the unique metrisable topology on the space of isomorphism classes of transitive graphs satisfying

\mathscr{G}_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\mathscr{G}_{\infty}\hskip 10.00002pt\iff\hskip 10.00002ptR(\mathscr{G}_{n},\mathscr{G}_{\infty})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\infty.

Around 2008, Schramm conjectured that for transitive graphs, the critical threshold
p_{c}(\mathscr{G})
depends only on the local geometry of
\mathscr{G}
in the sense that
p_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{n})\to p_{c}(\mathscr{G})
as
\mathscr{G}_{n}\to\mathscr{G}
in the local topology, provided that
\sup_{n}p_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{n})<1
.
For transitive graphs of polynomial growth (meaning that the cardinality of the
r
-ball is upper bounded by a polynomial in
r
), the case of isotropic Euclidean lattices was known prior to the conjecture
[
GM90
]
, then possibly anisotropic abelian Cayley graphs were treated
[
MT17
]
, before all transitive graphs of polynomial growth were covered in
[
CMT23
]
. As for graphs with fast growth, the first case to be understood was that of uniformly nonamenable graphs converging to a regular tree of degree at least 3
[
BNP11
]
, which was followed by the case of transitive graphs with exponential growth
[
Hut20a
]
and that of graphs where the heat kernel satisfies a certain uniform stretched-exponential upper bound
[
HH21
]
. In the end, building upon several works
[
CMT24
,
Hut20a
,
Hut20b
]
, Easo and Hutchcroft established the conjecture in full generality
[
EH23
]
. Let us mention that the assumption “
\sup_{n}p_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{n})<1
” can safely be replaced by the weaker condition
p_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{n})<1
for all
n
large enough. Interestingly, locality of
p_{c}
has also been studied in the context of finite transitive graphs
[
Eas24
]
.

In this article, we are interested in how observables that carry more information about the behaviour of the model depend on the underlying graph
\mathscr{G}
. In this direction, one expects locality phenomena for observables beyond
p_{c}
, such as the percolation function
\theta
away from criticality. As explained below, a form of locality for
\theta
was announced in the parallel work
[
EH23
]
, and in this article we are interested in establishing stronger forms of locality.

The geometric setup of the present article concerns transitive graphs for which the cardinality of the ball of radius
r
is superlinear in
r

r

[

Our theorems embody the following message:
given some

It is classical in statistical mechanics to define a phase transition to be a parameter where an observable describing the macroscopic behaviour of the system fails to be analytic. For percolation theory, the standard choice is
\theta

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
\mathscr{G}\in\mathfrak{G}

(p_{c}(\mathscr{G}),1]

Let us now come back to percolation and end this introduction by focusing once again on the properties of the percolation function
\theta
. One can formulate a family of interesting problems in percolation theory by investigating the regularity of
\theta
. Depending on the meaning one gives to the word “regularity”, and depending on the graphs one considers, this can give rise to diverse mathematical problems. When one fixes a graph
\mathscr{G}
, the continuity of
\theta
at

is equivalent to the absence of percolation at criticality, i.e. whether
\theta(p_{c})=0
, a famous open problem which has been studied extensively in the case of the hypercubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Continuity at

is known in dimension

[
Kes80
]
and in dimensions
d\geqslant 11

[

,

]
, but remains open in all intermediate dimensions, with the case of dimension

being notoriously difficult. See
[
BLPS99
,
Tim06
,
PPS06
,
DCST16
,
Hut16
,
HH21
]
for related works beyond the context of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. On the other hand, showing continuity of
\theta
on

is much more tractable; see
[

]
for a proof holding for all transitive graphs.

In another direction, one can study the regularity of the map
\mathscr{G}\longmapsto\theta_{\mathscr{G}}
, where we now view

as a function defined on the whole interval

. This turns out to be related to the aforementioned problem of whether

. To see this, let us introduce some notation. Given two graphs

and, for the other value

such that, for every
u,v\in V
, every
finite minimal
cutset between
u
and
v
is
k
-connected. Again, if no such
k
exists, we set
\mathbf{C}^{\star}(\mathscr{G})=\infty
.

Remark 2.1
.

There exist graphs
\mathscr{G}
such that both
\mathbf{C}(\mathscr{G})
and
\mathbf{C}^{\star}(\mathscr{G})
are finite, yet their values differ. For example, consider the square lattice and let
\mathscr{G}
be the graph obtained by removing a large ball around the origin. Then
\mathbf{C}^{\star}(\mathscr{G})=2
, as for the square lattice, while
\mathbf{C}(\mathscr{G})
is much larger. To see the latter, consider the vertices of the horizontal axis lying in
\mathscr{G}
, and note that they form an infinite minimal cutset between any vertex in the upper half-plane and any vertex in the bottom half-plane.

The purpose of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2

(reformulation of Theorem
1.8
)
.

The map
\mathbf{C}^{\star}
, defined on
\mathfrak{G}
, takes only finite values and is locally bounded.
In other words, for every
\mathscr{G}\in\mathfrak{G}
, there are finite constants
r=r(\mathscr{G})\geqslant 0
and
t=t(\mathscr{G})\geqslant 0
such that for every
\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{G}
, we have

R(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H})\geqslant r\implies\mathbf{C}^{\star}(\mathscr{H})\leqslant t.

We remark that the finiteness of
\mathbf{C}^{\star}
on
\mathfrak{G}
follows from the fact that the cycle space of these graphs is generated by cycles of bounded length. However, it is possible that some basic cycles in
\mathscr{H}
are much longer than any basic cycle in
\mathscr{G}
, regardless of how close
\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{H}
are. For example, this occurs for the “slab”
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}/n
and
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. For this reason, our strategy relies on a more geometric approach, where we compare the values of
\mathbf{C}^{\star}(\mathscr{H})
and
\mathbf{C}^{\star}(\mathscr{G})
directly.

A useful property to keep in mind, while working with the class
\mathfrak{G}
, is that every transitive graph of polynomial growth is quasi-isometric to a Cayley graph of some nilpotent group
[
Tro85
,
Woe91
]
. The proof of Theorem
2.2

crucially relies on a finitary refinement of this result, recently obtained by Tessera and Tointon
[
TT21
,
TT24
]
. This refinement allows us to reduce our problem to understanding the behaviour of
\mathbf{C}^{\star}
for Cayley graphs of nilpotent groups, and how it is affected by quasi-isometries.

To handle nilpotent Cayley graphs, we use the fact that, given a sequence of Cayley graphs converging to a Cayley graph of a nilpotent group, all but finitely many graphs in the sequence are quotients of the limit graph. For this reason, Section
2.3
begins with a proposition that controls
\mathbf{C}^{\star}(\mathscr{G})
for one-ended graphs
\mathscr{G}
which are quotients of a nice graph
\mathscr{H}
. We need a detour via bond-versions of our main quantities (see Section
2.2
) and, interestingly, the upper bound we get for
\mathbf{C}^{\star}_{E}(\mathscr{G})
is
\mathbf{C}_{E}(\mathscr{H})
, not
\mathbf{C}^{\star}_{E}(\mathscr{H})
. This is why both notions of “cutconnectivity” — with and without a star — need to be introduced, which constitutes the main subtlety of the present Section
2
. The behaviour of
\mathbf{C}^{\star}
under quasi-isometries is then investigated in Section
2.4
. With these results at hand, we finally establish Theorem
2.2
in Section
2.5
.

2.2
Cutsets and bond-cutsets

As a tool to understand
\mathbf{C}(\mathscr{G})
and
\mathbf{C}^{\star}(\mathscr{G})
, we also introduce edge-versions of these quantities. We say that two distinct edges
e
and
e^{\prime}
are
k
-adjacent if there is one endpoint of
e
that lies at distance at most
k
from an endpoint of
e^{\prime}
. We can thus define
\mathbf{C}_{E}(\mathscr{G})
and
\mathbf{C}^{\star}_{E}(\mathscr{G})
as above, by considering bond-cutsets instead of cutsets.

Remark 2.3
.

A key-difference between cutsets and bond-cutsets goes as follows: as removing an edge cannot increase the number of connected components by more than one, minimal bond-cutsets always break the original graph into exactly two connected components. This property does not hold when cutsets are taken relative to vertices.

In what follows, given a connected subgraph
D
of
\mathscr{G}
, we write
\partial_{E}D
for the set of edges with one endpoint in
D
and the other not in
D
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For every graph
\mathscr{G}
, we have
\mathbf{C}^{\star}(\mathscr{G})\leqslant\mathbf{C}^{\star}_{E}(\mathscr{G})+2
.

Proof.

Let
\Pi
be a finite cutset between two vertices
u
and
v
. We will show that
\Pi
is
(\mathbf{C}^{\star}_{E}(\mathscr{G})+2)
-connected. If
\Pi=\{u\}
or
\Pi=\{v\}
, then there is nothing to prove, so let us assume that
\Pi\neq\{u\},\{v\}
.

Let
D
be the connected component of
u
in the complement of
\Pi
. Note that
\partial_{E}D
is a finite bond-cutset that separates
u
and
v
. Furthermore, the minimality of
\Pi
implies that
\partial_{E}D
is a minimal bond-cutset, and that each vertex in
\Pi
is an endpoint of some edge in
\partial_{E}D
. Hence
\partial_{E}D
is
\mathbf{C}^{\star}_{E}(\mathscr{G})
-connected, and by the triangle inequality,
\Pi
is
(\mathbf{C}^{\star}_{E}(\mathscr{G})+2)
-connected, as desired.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

We shall not need it but the inequality
\mathbf{C}(\mathscr{G})\leqslant\mathbf{C}_{E}(\mathscr{G})+2
holds as well, for the same reason.

2.3
Cutconnectivity and fibrations

Given two graphs
\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{L}
, we say that a map
\pi:V(\mathscr{L})\to V(\mathscr{S})
is
1-Lipschitz
if, for all
x,y\in V(\mathscr{L})
, we have
d_{\mathscr{S}}(\pi(x),\pi(y))\leqslant d_{\mathscr{L}}(x,y)
; this is equivalent to asking that for any two neighbouring vertices
x,y
in
\mathscr{L}
, their images
\pi(x)
and
\pi(y)
either coincide or are adjacent. We call a map
\pi:V(\mathscr{L})\to V(\mathscr{S})
a
fibration
from
\mathscr{L}
to
\mathscr{S}
if it is 1-Lipschitz and satisfies that, for every vertex
x
of
\mathscr{L}
and every neighbour
v
of
\pi(x)
, there exists a neighbour
y
of
x
such that
\pi(y)=v
.

Remark 2.6
.

We have chosen the letters
\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{L}
to keep in mind which graph is Small and which one is Large. Notice that, as we have taken our graphs to be non-empty and connected, fibrations are automatically surjective. Indeed, consider a vertex
o
in
\mathscr{L}
. For every vertex
x
in
\mathscr{S}
, we can find an element in
\pi^{-1}(\{x\})
by lifting a path from
\pi(o)
to
x
.

Recall that a graph is said to be
one-ended
if, for every finite set of vertices
F
, its complement consists in exactly one infinite connected component and zero, one or several finite connected components.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\pi:V(\mathscr{L})\to V(\mathscr{S})
be a fibration. Assume that
\mathscr{S}
is one-ended and that, for every finite subset

of
V(\mathscr{S})
, the infinite connected component
K
of the complement of

satisfies that the graph induced by
\mathscr{L}
on

is connected.

Then, we have
\mathbf{C}^{\star}_{E}(\mathscr{S})\leqslant\mathbf{C}_{E}(\mathscr{L})
.

Proof.

Let
\Pi
be a finite minimal bond-cutset between two vertices
u
and
v
in
\mathscr{S}
. We wish to prove that
\Pi
is
\mathbf{C}_{E}(\mathscr{L})
-connected. As
\Pi
is a finite minimal bond-cutset and because
\mathscr{S}
is one-ended, the graph

must consist of exactly two connected components: a finite one, with vertex-set
F
, and an infinite one, with vertex-set

. This is where we use the fact that we work with bond-cutsets instead of cutsets. Notice that
\partial_{E}F=\Pi
and
\partial_{E}K=\Pi
. As every edge of
\Pi
has an endpoint in

and the other in

and by

on

are the same, and so is the case for

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

and that

belongs to

is surjective, we can pick

in

and

for the set of all edges

is an edge that belongs to

is a bond-cutset between

and

and since

and

denote the connected component of

in

and

.
Because of our assumption, we further know that

is connected in

, and since each edge of

has one endpoint not in

is, in fact, connected in

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\pi:V(\mathscr{L})\to V(\mathscr{S})
be a fibration. Assume that
\mathscr{S}
is one-ended and that, for every finite subset

of

, there is a vertex

in

such that any two vertices in

can be connected by a path that never intersects

.

Then, for every finite subset

of

, the infinite connected component

of the complement of

satisfies that the graph induced by
\mathscr{L}
on
\pi^{-1}(K)
is connected.

Proof.

Let
F
be a finite subset of
V(\mathscr{S})
. Denote by

the infinite connected component of the complement of

. Notice that, since
\mathscr{S}
is one-ended,

is uniquely defined and that its complement

is a finite set containing

, we get a vertex

can be connected by a path that never intersects

. In particular,

belongs to

and any two vertices in

Let us now pick two vertices

and

in

. As the vertices

, and

belong to the connected graph

to

and a path from

to

. Since

is a fibration, we can lift these paths to get two paths avoiding

and another path from some vertex

to some

by a path that avoids

We are now able to state and prove a corollary of Proposition
2.7
that will be directly used in our proof of Theorem
2.2
. In order to state it in the natural generality of the argument, recall that a finitely generated group
G
is
one-ended
if any Cayley graph of
G
with respect to a finite generating set is one-ended, and that it is
Noetherian
if all its subgroups are finitely generated. Also recall that every finitely generated nilpotent group is Noetherian
[
DK18
, Theorem 13.57]
, and that finitely generated nilpotent groups of superlinear growth are always one-ended: these two facts guarantee that Corollary
2.11
will cover all our situations of interest.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
G
and
H
be two finitely generated groups, with
G
one-ended and
H
Noetherian. Let
\pi:H\to G
be a surjective group homomorphism, and let
S
be a finite generating subset of
H
. Let
\mathscr{H}
denote the Cayley graph of
H
with respect to
S
and
\mathscr{G}
denote the Cayley graph of
G
with respect to
\pi(S)
.

Then, we have
\mathbf{C}^{\star}_{E}(\mathscr{G})\leqslant\mathbf{C}_{E}(\mathscr{H})
.

Proof.

First, note that
\pi
is a fibration from
\mathscr{H}
to
\mathscr{G}
. By Proposition
2.7
and Lemma
2.10
, it thus suffices to check that, for every finite subset
F
of
V(\mathscr{G})
, there is a vertex
u
in
\mathscr{G}
such that any two vertices in
\pi^{-1}(\{u\})
can be connected by a path that never intersects
\pi^{-1}(F)
.

If
\ker(\pi)
is the trivial group that consists only of the identity element, then there is nothing to prove. Thus, we assume that
\ker(\pi)
is not the trivial group. As
H
is Noetherian,
\ker(\pi)
is a finitely generated group. We can therefore pick a non-empty finite set
T\subset\ker(\pi)
that generates
\ker(\pi)
as a group. Let
c:=\max_{t\in T}d_{\mathscr{H}}(1,t)
, where
1
denotes the identity element of
H
.

Let
F
be a finite subset of
V(\mathscr{G})
. The graph
\mathscr{G}
being one-ended, it has in particular infinite diameter. We can therefore pick some vertex
u\in V(\mathscr{G})
at a distance strictly greater than
c
from
F
. Let
x
and
y
be two elements in
\pi^{-1}(\{u\})
. We can write
y=xk
for some
k\in\ker(\pi)
. In turn, we can write
k=t_{1}\dots t_{n}
, where each
t_{i}
belongs to
T\cup T^{-1}
. Finally, each
t_{i}
can be written as
s_{i,1}\dots s_{i,m(i)}
, where each
s_{i,j}
belongs to
S\cup S^{-1}
and
m(i)\leqslant c
. Putting all this together, we write
k
as a word with letters
s_{i,j}
. Starting with the empty word and adding the letters
s_{i,j}
one by one to the right yields a path from
1
to
k
in
\mathscr{G}
. Multiplying this path to the left by
x
produces a path
\gamma
from
x
to
y
.

We claim that
\gamma
does not intersect
\pi^{-1}(F)
. To see this, first observe that all intermediate vertices of the form
xt_{1}\dots t_{i}
visited by
\gamma
are mapped to
u
under
\pi
, and therefore cannot belong to
\pi^{-1}(F)
. As for the remaining vertices visited by
\gamma
, they are of the form
xt_{1}\dots t_{i}s_{i,1}\dots s_{i,j}
, which also cannot lie in
\pi^{-1}(F)
: indeed, their image under
\pi
is at distance at most

from
u
, whereas
u
was taken so that its distance from
F
is larger than
c
. This completes the proof.
∎

2.4
Cutconnectivity and quasi-isometries

Given

, say that a function
\varphi:V(\mathscr{G})\rightarrow V(\mathscr{H})
is an

if the following two conditions hold:

for all

2.

for every

such that

.

We say that the graphs

and

and every

and

by an open path. Using that

and that all the events

happen, we deduce that we can connect

to

by an open path. Since

, we can thus connect

to

by an open path, which proves the claim and completes the proof.
∎

With the above result at hand, we now give the following definition.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathscr{I}\subset V
be a finite set of vertices. We say that
\mathscr{I}
is an

if it is
t
-connected and either

or

\mathscr{G}\setminus\mathscr{I}
. Let
\overline{\mathscr{I}}
denote the set of vertices

such that

. We say that an interface
\mathscr{I}

1.

every

is bad,

2.

every

3.

the closed edges of
\overline{\mathscr{I}}
form a bond-cutset between

.

When clear from context, we simply write that

occurs.

The following result is a direct consequence of the above definition.

Lemma 3.5
.

Given a configuration
\omega
, the family of all occurring interfaces consists in mutually disjoint sets.

Proof.

Since interfaces are
t
-connected and because of items
1
and
2
in Definition

, occurring interfaces are in particular
t
-connected components of the set of bad vertices. The desired result follows.
∎

Let us write

for the

-connected component of

in
\omega
within the set of
N
-bad vertices, with the convention that

if

is
N
-good. We now show that for every

, for every

large enough (depending on

),

is almost surely finite, which will allow us to deduce that

is almost surely finite. The proof is similar to that of

. We include a proof for completeness and to justify the uniformity in

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathscr{G}\in\mathfrak{G}
and consider
p_{0}\in(p_{c}(\mathscr{G}),1]
. There exist

\forall\;p\in(p_{0}-\varepsilon,p_{0}+\varepsilon),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\;n\geqslant 1,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathbb{P}_{p}(|\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)|\geqslant n)\leqslant e^{-cn}.

In particular,
\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)
is finite almost surely.

Proof.

We will first prove the desired exponential decay for every fixed parameter

, and then use a continuity argument to deduce the uniformity of the constant

in terms of

.

Let us consider some
N>0
that will be chosen along the way to be large enough depending on
p
.
We will estimate
\mathbb{P}_{p}(|\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)|\geqslant n)
using a union bound. To this end, let
\mathscr{I}
be a
t
-connected set of vertices that contains
x
such that
|\mathscr{I}|=n
. Write
r=10N+1
.
We say that a set of vertices
A
is
2r
-separated if

. Let
\mathscr{I}^{\prime}\subset\mathscr{I}
be a
2r
-separated set that is maximal for inclusion among

-separated subsets of

. Hence,
|\mathscr{I}^{\prime}|\geqslant|\mathscr{I}|/|B_{2r}|
.

We claim that

is
(4r+t)
-connected. Indeed, consider two vertices

and let

be two vertices such that
d_{\mathscr{G}}(u,z)\leqslant 2r
and

. As
\mathscr{I}
is
t
-connected, we can pick a
t
-connected path
\gamma
in
\mathscr{I}
from

to

, i.e. consecutive vertices in

are at distance at most
t
apart in

. Consider two consecutive vertices

. We then have

. Iterating this procedure we can construct a

-connected path from

to

. This proves the claim.

We can thus conclude that when the event
\{|\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)|\geqslant n\}
happens, there exists a
(4r+t)
-connected set of vertices

that is
2r
-separated, has cardinality

, contains a vertex in

, and such that every vertex in

is

-bad. The union bound gives

\mathbb{P}_{p}(|\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)|\geqslant n)\leqslant\sum_{m\geqslant n/|B_{2r}|}\sum_{\mathcal{S}\in P_{m}}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\forall\,\penalty\ y\in\mathcal{S},y\text{ is N-bad}),

where

is the set of

as above that have cardinality

are independent from each other when

, hence

\mathbb{P}_{p}(\forall\,y\in\mathcal{S},\penalty\ y\text{ is N-bad})=\mathbb{P}_{p}(o\text{ is N-bad})^{m}.

A union bound and (
3
) give

provided that

is the degree of
\mathscr{G}
.

It remains to estimate the cardinality of

. Consider the graph
\mathscr{G}^{(i)}
with vertex set

, where we draw an edge between

and

. Then each

is connected in

. Since each vertex in

has degree at most

, it follows from

, Lemma 5.1]
that

|P_{m}|\leqslant|B_{2r}||B_{4r+t}|^{m}e^{m}.

Now recall that

. Since

to be large enough so that

|B_{4r+t}|e(D+1)e^{-\sqrt{N}}<1.

(5)

With this choice, we see that

decays exponentially fast. The uniformity in

now follows from the continuity of the function

.
∎

As a corollary we obtain the following result regarding finiteness of

.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathscr{G}\in\mathfrak{G}
and consider
p_{0}\in(p_{c}(\mathscr{G}),1]
. There exists

such that the following holds. For every
N\geqslant N_{0}
, there exists

such that

\forall\;p\in(p_{0}-\varepsilon,p_{0}+\varepsilon),\hskip 20.00003pt\mathbb{P}_{p}(|\mathcal{I}|<\infty\mid 20N<\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{o})<\infty)=1.

Proof.

This follows from Propositions
3.1
and
3.6
.
∎

It follows from Corollary
3.7
, and Lemma
3.3
that for every
p_{0}>p_{c}
and for every
N
large enough, whenever
20N<\textup{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{o})<\infty
, an interface occurs at scale
N
. Moreover, whenever an interface occurs,
o
is not connected to infinity by property
3
of Definition
3.4
. Thus, for some
\varepsilon>0
we have the exact equality

1-\theta(p)=\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{o})\leqslant 20N)+\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{o})>20N,\text{ an interface occurs at scale }N)

(6)

for every
p\in(p_{0}-\varepsilon,p_{0}+\varepsilon)
,
and we emphasise that this expression holds with the same constant
N
in the whole interval
(p_{0}-\varepsilon,p_{0}+\varepsilon)
.

Let us now fix an
N
such that (
6
) holds. Note that
\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{o})\leqslant 20N)
is trivially analytic since it is a polynomial in
p
. To handle the second term, we wish to use the inclusion-exclusion principle to express
\mathbb{P}_{q}(\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{o})>20N,\text{ an interface occurs at scale }N)
as a sum
over
multi-interfaces
, i.e. finite non-empty collections of
disjoint
interfaces. The fact that we can restrict to collections of disjoint interfaces follows from Lemma
3.5
. We say that a multi-interface
\mathscr{M}=\{\mathscr{I}_{1},\mathscr{I}_{2},\ldots,\mathscr{I}_{k}\}

occurs in
\omega
at scale
N
if all
\mathscr{I}_{i}
occur, and we write
|\mathscr{M}|:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\mathscr{I}_{i}|
for its size.

Some care is required to show that the inclusion-exclusion expansion converges. This follows from the next result, where we will show that for every
N
large enough, the contribution of multi-interfaces of size
n
decays exponentially fast in
n
. We stress that the decay being exponential is crucial for obtaining the convergence of the inclusion-exclusion expansion for complex values of the parameter
p
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a graph in
\mathfrak{G}
, and let
p_{0}\in(p_{c}(\mathscr{G}),1]
. There exist constants
\varepsilon
,
r
,
N,c,C>0
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathscr{M}:|\mathscr{M}|=n\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{p,\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}\text{ occurs at scale }N)\leqslant Ce^{-cn}

for every
\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{G}
such that
R(\mathscr{H},\mathscr{G})\geqslant r
, every
p\in(p_{0}-\varepsilon,p_{0}+\varepsilon)\cap[0,1]
and every
n\geqslant 1
.

In order to prove the above proposition, we first need some intermediate results. Let us fix a bi-infinite geodesic
\gamma=(\ldots,\gamma(-1),\gamma(0),\gamma(1),\ldots)
in
\mathscr{G}
such that
\gamma(0)=o
. The existence of
\gamma
classically follows from considering a sequence of geodesics of length
2i
starting at
o
, applying an automorphism to map the middle vertex of each geodesic to
o
, and then using a compactness argument.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mathscr{I}
be an interface such that
|\mathscr{I}|=n
. Then there exists
i
such that
\gamma(i)\in\mathscr{I}
and
0\leqslant i\leqslant t(n-1)
.

Proof.

Recall that either
\mathscr{I}
contains
o
or
o
lies in a finite component of its complement in
\mathscr{G}
. This implies that
\mathscr{I}
intersects both infinite geodesic rays
(\gamma(0),\gamma(1),\ldots)
and
(\ldots,\gamma(-1),\gamma(0))
at some vertices
x^{+}
and
x^{-}
, respectively. Since
\mathscr{I}
is
t
-connected and
|\mathscr{I}|=n
, it follows that
d_{\mathscr{G}}(x^{+},x^{-})\leqslant tn
, which implies that
x^{+}=\gamma(i)
for some
0\leqslant i\leqslant t(n-1)
.
∎

Our strategy for proving Proposition
3.8
is based on the observation that

\sum_{\mathscr{M}:|\mathscr{M}|=n}\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathscr{M}\text{ occurs at scale }N)=\mathbb{E}_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{n}),

where
\mathcal{N}_{n}
is the (random) number of occurring multi-interfaces of size
n
. Using combinatorial arguments, we now obtain a deterministic sub-exponential upper bound on
\mathcal{N}_{n}
which holds uniformly over all configurations
\omega
.

Lemma 3.10
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that for every
\mathscr{G}\in\mathfrak{G}
and any configuration
\omega
we have

\mathcal{N}_{n}\leqslant e^{c\sqrt{tn}}

for every
n\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{M}=\{\mathscr{I}_{1},\mathscr{I}_{2},\ldots,\mathscr{I}_{k}\}
be a multi-interface that occurs in
\omega
at scale
N
and has size
n
. By Lemma
3.9
, there exist integers
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}
such that
\gamma(i_{j})\in\mathscr{I}_{j}
and
0\leqslant i_{j}\leqslant t(|\mathscr{I}_{j}|-1)
. Since occurring interfaces are disjoint, it follows that the numbers
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}
are all distinct. Hence, the set

\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}\setminus\{0\}
forms a partition of an integer
r\leqslant tn
. Using a well-known result of Hardy and Ramanujan on the number of partitions
[
RH18
]
, we obtain that the number of all possible sets
\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}
produced in this way is at most
e^{c\sqrt{tn}}
for some constant
c>0
. Since for each configuration
\omega
, the map

\{\mathscr{I}_{1},\mathscr{I}_{2},\ldots,\mathscr{I}_{k}\}\mapsto\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}
is injective, it follows that

\mathcal{N}_{n}\leqslant e^{c\sqrt{tn}},

as desired.
∎

Using the above lemma, we now prove Proposition
3.8
.

Proof of Proposition
3.8
.

Consider
\mathscr{G}\in\mathfrak{G}
. By Theorem

be such that
\mathbf{C}^{\star}(\mathscr{H})\leqslant t

and

. We first prove the assertion for fixed

and for any

satisfying

be large enough so that

will then follow by a continuity argument.

By Lemma

, we have

\mathbb{E}_{p,\mathscr{H}}(\mathcal{N}_{n})\leqslant e^{c\sqrt{tn}}\mathbb{P}_{p,\mathscr{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{n}),

(7)

Let

By arguing as in the proof of Proposition

is

We now estimate the number of all possible

.
To this end, we first wish to bound

. As

, we have the inequality

. To estimate

We are now ready to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
p_{0}>p_{c}(\mathscr{G})
and consider some
N
and
\varepsilon
as in Corollary
3.7
and Proposition
3.8
. Then we have

1-\theta(p)=\mathbb{P}_{p}(D_{N})+\mathbb{P}_{p}(D_{N}^{c},\text{ an interface occurs at scale }N)

for every
p\in(p_{0}-\varepsilon,p_{0}+\varepsilon)\cap[0,1]
, where
D_{N}=\{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}_{o})\leqslant 20N\}
. The function
p\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{p}(D_{N})
is a polynomial in
p
, hence analytic. For the second term, we use the inclusion-exclusion principle to obtain

\mathbb{P}_{p}(D_{N}^{c},\text{ an interface occurs at scale }N)=\sum_{\mathscr{M}=\{\mathscr{I}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{I}_{k}\}}(-1)^{k+1}\mathbb{P}_{p}(D_{N}^{c},\text{ $\mathscr{M}$ occurs at scale }N).

Each summand is a polynomial in
p
, which allows us to formally extend the parameter
p
to the complex plane. We show that this series converges uniformly in a complex neighbourhood
\mathscr{O}
of
p_{0}
, which classically implies that the series is an analytic function in the interior of
\mathscr{O}
. Then, as it coincides with
\mathbb{P}_{p}(D_{N}^{c},\text{ an interface occurs at scale }N)
on
\mathscr{O}\cap[0,1]
, it follows that
\mathbb{P}_{p}(D_{N}^{c},\text{ an interface occurs at scale }N)
admits an analytic extension on
\mathscr{O}
, hence so does
\theta
.

To this end, we compare the value of the sum at a complex parameter
z
with its value at a point close to
p_{0}
by estimating the degree of each polynomial term. Note that the event
D_{N}^{c}
depends only on the state of the edges with both endpoints in
B_{20N+1,\mathscr{G}}
, and the event
\{\mathscr{I}_{i}\textup{ occurs}\}
depends only on the state of the edges with both endpoints in
\bigcup_{x\in\mathscr{I}_{i}}B_{20(t+1)N+1,\mathscr{G}}(x)
. Thus, for a multi-interface
\mathscr{M}=\{\mathscr{I}_{1},\mathscr{I}_{2},\ldots,\mathscr{I}_{k}\}
of size
n
, the event
\{D_{N}^{c},\text{ $\mathscr{M}$ occurs}\}
depends on the state of at most
An
edges for some constant
A>0
that only depends on
t
and the cardinality of
B_{20(t+1)N+1,\mathscr{G}}
. Let us introduce

F_{n}(p):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathscr{M}=\{\mathscr{I}_{1},\mathscr{I}_{2},\ldots,\mathscr{I}_{k}\}\\
|\mathscr{M}|=n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k+1}\mathbb{P}_{p}(D_{N}^{c},\text{ $\mathscr{M}$ occurs}).

As the function
F_{n}
is polynomial in
p
on
[0,1]
, we can make sense of
F_{n}(z)
for all complex
z
, even though the interpretation on the right-hand side ceases to be valid. For every multi-interface
\mathscr{M}
, we can partition the event
\{D_{N}^{c},\text{ $\mathscr{M}$ occurs}\}
into finitely many events
\mathcal{E}_{\mathscr{M},i}
of the form
\{\text{all edges in $O_{\mathscr{M},i}$ are open and all edges in $C_{\mathscr{M},i}$ are closed}\}
with
O_{\mathscr{M},i}
and
C_{\mathscr{M},i}
edge-sets of cardinality at most
An
. We set
G_{\mathscr{M},i}(z):=z^{|O_{\mathscr{M},i}|}(1-z)^{|C_{\mathscr{M},i}|}
and observe that, for
p\in[0,1]
, we have
G_{\mathscr{M},i}(p)=\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathscr{M},i})
.

By the triangle inequality, for every complex number
z
, we have

|F_{n}(z)|\leqslant\sum_{\mathscr{M}\,:\,|\mathscr{M}|=n}\sum_{i}|G_{\mathscr{M},i}(z)|.

Letting
r<p_{0}
and
a(r):=A\log(\frac{p_{0}+r}{p_{0}-r})
, we can now apply
[
GP23
, Corollary 4.3]
to get that for every
z
satisfying
|z-p_{0}|\leqslant r
, for all
\mathscr{M}
and
i
, we have
|G_{\mathscr{M},i}(z)|\leqslant e^{a(r)n}\mathbb{P}_{p_{0}-r}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathscr{M},i})
. Therefore, for every complex number
z
satisfying

For every
1\leqslant n\leqslant\infty
, let
f_{n}
be a function from some domain
D_{n}
to
\mathbb{R}
. In this section, we say that
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
uniformly
to
f_{\infty}
if

\sup_{x\in D_{n}\cap D_{\infty}}|f_{n}(x)-f_{\infty}(x)|\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

Given
\mathscr{G}\in\mathfrak{G}
, we denote by
\tilde{\theta}_{\mathscr{G}}
the restriction of
\theta_{\mathscr{G}}
to
(p_{c}(\mathscr{G}),1]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

The following statements are equivalent:

•

every graph in
\mathfrak{A}
satisfies
\theta(p_{c})=0
,

•

for every sequence
(\mathscr{G})_{n\leqslant\infty}
of elements of
\mathfrak{A}
satisfying
\mathscr{G}_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\mathscr{G}_{\infty}
, we have uniform convergence of
\tilde{\theta}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}
to
\tilde{\theta}_{\mathscr{G}_{\infty}}
.

Proof.

The first condition implies the second one because Proposition
1.9
asserts that its conditions
1
and
5
are equivalent.
We now prove the reverse implication. Let
\mathscr{G}\in\mathfrak{A}
. Construct
\mathscr{G}_{n}
as in the proof of Proposition
1.9
. Observe that
\mathscr{G}_{n}
is a quotient of
\mathscr{G}
, so that Proposition 1 from
[
BS96
]
guarantees that
p_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{n})\geqslant p_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{\infty})
. This means that the domain of
\tilde{\theta}_{\mathscr{G}_{\infty}}
contains that of
\tilde{\theta}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}
. As
[
DCST16
]
guarantees that
\theta_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}
is continuous, we have
\inf_{p>p_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{n})}\tilde{\theta}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}(x)=0
. If we assume uniform convergence of
\tilde{\theta}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}
to
\tilde{\theta}_{\mathscr{G}_{\infty}}
, this yields
\inf_{p>p_{c}(\mathscr{G})}\tilde{\theta}_{\mathscr{G}}(x)\leqslant 0
, hence that
\mathscr{G}
satisfies
\theta(p_{c})=0
.
∎

We conclude by asking the following question.

Question 6.2
.

Is it true that for every sequence
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\leqslant\infty}
of elements of
\mathfrak{A}
such that
\mathscr{G}_{n}
converges to
\mathscr{G}_{\infty}
, we have uniform convergence of the derivative
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}
to
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}_{\mathscr{G}_{\infty}}
?

We believe that the answer is negative. Indeed, endowed with their usual graph structures, we have
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{98}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\mathbb{Z}^{100}
. Because the number 100 is large enough, it is known that, for
\mathbb{Z}^{100}
, the function
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
is bounded; see
[
BA91
,
HS90
]
. In contrast, using the main result of
[
KZ87
]
and the mean-value theorem, one can see that, for the square lattice, the function
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
is unbounded. The universality paradigm inclines us to think that, as
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{98}
is at large scale two-dimensional, it will behave as the square lattice at or near its critical point. It thus seems likely that every
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{98}
has unbounded
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
. This would be incompatible with
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
being bounded for
\mathbb{Z}^{100}
and uniform convergence of
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
holding.

It is interesting to notice that, for the notion of convergence studied in this section, one expects different behaviours for
\tilde{\theta}
and its derivative, while Corollary
1.5
asserts that, away from
p_{c}
, everything behaves nicely for
\theta
,
\theta^{\prime}
, and derivatives of higher order.

We conclude by observing that if the answer to Question
6.2
were to be affirmative, this would imply
\theta(p_{c})=0
on
\mathfrak{A}
, as
\theta(p_{c})=1-\int_{p_{c}}^{1}\theta^{\prime}(p)\,\mathrm{d}p
. Indeed, one can approximate
\mathscr{G}
by slabs
\mathscr{G}_{n}
as in the proof of Proposition
1.9
and, were the answer affirmative, the equality with a star would be valid in the following computation:

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathscr{G}}(p_{c}(\mathscr{G}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\int_{p_{c}(\mathscr{G})}^{1}\theta_{\mathscr{G}}^{\prime}(p)\,\mathrm{d}p=1-\lim_{n}\int_{p_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{n})}^{1}\theta_{\mathscr{G}}^{\prime}(p)\,\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle\overset{\star}{=}

\displaystyle 1-\lim_{n}\int_{p_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{n})}^{1}\theta_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}^{\prime}(p)\,\mathrm{d}p=\lim_{n}\theta_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}(p_{c}(\mathscr{G}_{n}))=0.

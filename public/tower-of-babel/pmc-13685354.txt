Outcomes of rescue procedures in patients undergoing unsuccessful deployment of transcatheter mitral valve replacement
Authors: Chikashi Nakai, Junyi Liu, Mei Xing Zuo, Saeed Tarabichi, Augustin DeLago
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPerivalvular leak (PVL), valve embolization, and migration of valve in mitral position are critical complications of transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR). To date, rescue strategies, and outcomes following unsuccessful initial TMVR deployment remain poorly documented.MethodsBetween March 2016 and December 2024, 86 patients with high risk of open-heart surgery underwent TMVR with a balloon expandable valve at a single institution. 19.8% (17/86) of patients experienced unsuccessful first TMVR (unsuccessful group) due to valve embolization, migration, significant PVL, or pericardial effusion, whereas 80.2% (69/86) had successful first TMVR (successful group). We evaluated postoperative outcomes comparing unsuccessful to successful groups.ResultsAmong 17 patients with unsuccessful first TMVR, 70.6% (12/17) underwent additional valve deployment, with successful valve-in-valve implantation in 75.0% (9/12). 17.6% (3/17) developed valve embolization in left atrium after transcatheter rescue procedures or additional valve deployment failed, subsequently 66.7% (2/3) required emergent surgical explantation of embolized valves, 33.3% (1/3) was managed medically due to prohibitive surgical risk. Surgical patients were discharged home without complications. Early mortality in unsuccessful group was 29.4% (5/17) and 13.0% (9/69) in successful group. There was no significant difference in early mortality between the 2 groups (p = 0.10). Median follow-up term was 10 months. Cumulative survival rate in 12 months was 50.5% in unsuccessful group, and 60.0% in successful group. Mid-term outcomes were similar between the 2 groups (p = 0.34).ConclusionsThe study was underpowered to detect differences in all-cause mortality, but unsuccessful deployment was associated with substantially higher numerical early mortality and significantly higher cardiac mortality.

Introduction

Transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR) has become an increasingly important therapeutic option for patients with severe mitral regurgitation (MR) or mitral stenosis (MS) who are considered to be at high or prohibitive surgical risk. Early clinical experiences have demonstrated that balloon-expandable TMVR provides effective symptom relief and durable hemodynamic improvement, particularly in valve-in-valve (ViV) and valve-in-ring procedures [1–3]. However, the complex anatomy of the mitral valve – including the non-circular annulus, subvalvular apparatus, and proximity to the left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) – continues to present significant procedural challenges. As a result, TMVR remains associated with higher technical failure rates compared with other transcatheter valve therapies [4].

Unsuccessful initial valve deployment, including valve embolization, migration, malposition, or significant perivalvular leak (PVL), represents one of the most feared complications of TMVR. These complications may result in hemodynamic instability, require emergent intervention, and have been associated with increased mortality in early reports [5]. Although several rescue strategies such as ViV implantation, vascular plug placement, and emergent surgical explantation have been described, existing evidence is limited to small registries and case series with heterogeneous patient populations and device platforms. As a result, the clinical impact of unsuccessful initial TMVR deployment and the effectiveness of rescue procedures remain incompletely understood [5, 6].

Given the expanding indications for transcatheter mitral intervention, a more precise understanding of the clinical consequences of initial procedural failure is urgently needed. This study evaluates a consecutive cohort of patients undergoing balloon-expandable TMVR at a single tertiary center, comparing early and mid-term outcomes between patients with successful and unsuccessful first valve deployment. We sought to determine whether appropriate rescue strategies can mitigate the adverse clinical impact of initial device failure and clarify the safety of surgical explantation when required.

Methods

Patient selection

This study included consecutive patients who underwent TMVR with balloon expandable valve (Edwards SAPIEN 3) at Albany Medical Center between March 2016 and December 2024. All patients were evaluated by structural heart team and TMVR was performed in patients with severe MR or MS and New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class II or greater who were considered to be at high surgical risk. High surgical risk was decided by cardiac surgery team with comorbidities, fragility, The Society of Thoracic Surgeons predicted risk of mortality score, and other background. Unsuccessful TMVR deployment was defined as valve embolization, improper valve position, moderate or greater PVL, and development of new pericardial effusion following initial valve deployment (Fig. 1). Patients in the unsuccessful group underwent rescue procedures as clinically indicated, including additional ViV deployment, placement of vascular plug for PVL (Fig. 2), or surgical intervention when necessary. Postoperative outcomes in the unsuccessful group were compared with those in the successful group. Early mortality was defined as death within 30 days after TMVR. The Institutional Review Board approved the study protocol (No. 7049, November 15, 2024), and the requirement for individual informed consent was waived. Clinical data were obtained through retrospective review of the electronic health record system at Albany Medical Center. Preoperative, intraprocedural, and postoperative data were collected in a dedicated database prospectively. All postoperative patients were followed at 30 days, 1 year, yearly thereafter.

Computer tomography measurement/planning

Mitral annulus area was assessed with computer tomography (CT) including 3D saddle-shaped structure. TMVR valve was chose with 10–25% area oversizing for valve in mitral annular calcification (MAC). Neo-LVOT simulation was performed to evaluate the risk of LVOT obstruction. Neo-LVOT ≤ 2.0 cm² or acute aortomitral angle (closer to 90 degree) was considered as high risk for LVOT obstruction, and patients underwent alcoholic septal ablation at least seven days before the day of TMVR procedure.

TMVR procedure

Intraprocedural hemodynamics were monitored with electrocardiogram, arterial line, and pulse oximeter. Transesophageal echocardiogram (TEE) was used in all patients. Transapical approach was performed in first nine patients when TMVR procedure was initiated at Albany Medical Center. Transseptal approach was chose as a first approach option in the rest of 77 patients subsequently. TMVR valve was deployed under fluoroscopy and TEE guidance.

Selection of rescue procedures

Vascular plug was selected when moderate or greater PVL was identified in intraprocedural TEE. ViV deployment was performed when intraprocedural TEE did not clarify the exact location of PVL but presented with moderate or greater MR or position of deployed TMVR valve was not appropriate in the mitral annulus. Emergent surgical intervention was performed when TMVR valve was embolized. Surgical intervention was aborted when patients had prohibitive surgical risks associated with STS score. Pericardiocentesis was performed when intraprocedural TEE demonstrated a significant pericardial effusion (moderate or greater).

Definition of technical success after rescue procedures

PVL or MR was reduced to mild or less after vascular plug placement or ViV deployment. Regarding pericardiocentesis, patients were hemodynamically stabilized after pericardial drainage. Surgical success was defined as successful explantation of embolized valves without complications.

Statistical analyses

Categorical variables were described as numbers and percentages, while continuous variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR). Student’s t-test was performed for continuous variables that have a normal distribution and Mann-Whitney U test was used for them that do not have a normal distribution. Categorical variables were assessed by Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test. Univariable analysis was performed to evaluate the risk factors for the unsuccessful initial TMVR valve deployment. Kaplan-Meier method was carried out to evaluate the cumulative survival, and statistical differences were analyzed using the log-rank test. Univariable analysis was conducted to assess the predictors of mid-term mortality. There was no missing data. Data analyses were performed using SPSS version 29.0.0.0 (IBM) and GraphPad Prism version 10.4.1.

Results

Preoperative patient characteristics

A total of 86 patients were included in the study and ViV in 29, valve in ring in 21, and valve in MAC in 36. Among these patients, 17 experienced unsuccessful initial TMVR deployment (unsuccessful group) while 69 had successful initial valve deployment (successful group). Patient characteristics were summarized in Table 1. The median age was 78-year-old (IQR 69, 84). The prevalence of male, previous open-heart surgery, mitral valve replacement, mitral valve repair, left ventricular ejection fraction, severe mitral regurgitation and stenosis was similar between the unsuccessful and successful groups. The median STS score was 8.8 (IQR 3.4, 15.5) in the unsuccessful group and 7.7 (IQR 4.8, 11.0) in the successful group, respectively. The STS score was not significantly different between the 2 groups (p = 0.27). Among the 36 patients undergoing TMVR for MAC, 28 had severe MAC on the preoperative CT while 8 demonstrated moderate MAC.

Postoperative in-hospital outcomes

A significant difference was not detected between the 2 groups in the incidence of postoperative stroke, permanent pacemaker placement, new onset of atrial fibrillation, new dialysis requirement, cardiogenic shock, LVOT obstruction, cardiac arrest, and mitral valve reintervention (Table 3). The median hospital length of stay was 7 days (IQR 1, 9) in the unsuccessful group, and similar to that of the successful group (median 2 days, IQR 1, 6, p = 0.54) (Table 3). The early mortality rate was not different between the 2 groups (29.4% versus 13.0%, p = 0.10) although that of cardiac causes was significantly higher in the unsuccessful group (29.4% versus 8.7%, p = 0.04). The univariable analysis indicated that age, sex, STS score, previous aortic valve replacement, and TMVR procedure for severe MAC were not associated with the unsuccessful initial TMVR valve deployment (Table 4).

Severe PVL/MR

Following initial valve deployment, 8 patients developed severe PVL, and severe MR in 2. Of patients with severe PVL, 5 underwent additional ViV deployment, vascular plug in 2, and additional ViV deployment and subsequent vascular plug in 1. At the end of cases, TEE revealed no significant PVL in all patients. During the admission, 2 patients with additional ViV deployment were expired due to heart failure secondary to subsequent worsened PVL. In 2 patients with severe MR, one underwent additional ViV deployment and postoperative course was uncomplicated. Another patient exhibited severe MR and cardiac tamponade after the first valve was deployed. Pericardial effusion was drained with emergent pericardiocentesis. However, bleeding was not controllable, and the patient was expired at the catheterization laboratory.

True deployment failure

TMVR deployment was aborted due to severe MS and unstable anchor in one patient. During the procedure, ventricular fibrillation occurred and resulted in hospital death.

Malposition

Three patients exhibited improper position of the initial TMVR valve. Two of them proceeded with additional ViV deployment and had successful ViV procedure. They were discharged without significant residual MR. One of them developed LVOT obstruction. Additional procedure was not performed for the patient due to prohibitive surgical risk. The patient was expired in the admission due to heart failure.

Embolization

There were 3 patients who had embolized TMVR valves in LA. Of 3 patients, 2 required surgical valve explantation and one who had two embolized valves in the LA was managed medically due to deconditioning and complex medical history including previous open heart surgery, atrial fibrillation, frail 80 years-old, hypertension and high STS score. There was no early mortality in patients who experienced embolized valves.

Mid-term outcomes

The median follow-up period was 10months (IQR 1, 25) (Table 3). During these periods, 4 patients in the unsuccessful group died (non-cardiac in 2 patients, and undetermined in 2 patients), along with 22 in the successful group (cardiac in 11 patients, non-cardiac in 9 patients, and undetermined in 2 patients). Kaplan-Meier survival curve in patients undergoing TMVR was shown in Fig. 3. The survival rate in 12 months was 50.5% in the unsuccessful group and 60.0% in the successful group, respectively (Table 3). There was no significant difference in the mid-term outcomes between the 2 groups (p = 0.34) (Fig. 3). On the univariable analysis, high STS score (unadjusted hazard ratio 1.04, 95% confidence interval 1.00–1.09, p = 0.04) and TMVR procedure for MAC (unadjusted hazard ratio 3.05, 95% confidence interval 1.60–5.81, p < 0.01) were significant factors of mid-term mortality (Table 6).

Discussions

In this study, we evaluated the clinical consequences of unsuccessful initial deployment during TMVR and found four major results: [1] early mortality was higher in the unsuccessful group, however, did not reach out to significant difference statistically; [2] mid-term survival did not differ significantly between the 2 groups; and [3] rescue procedures – including ViV deployment, vascular plug placement, and surgical intervention – were effective in stabilizing patients following initial procedural failure.

Published TMVR experiences

Technical failure during TMVR remains a recognized procedural challenge. Large multicenter series of TMVR performed for bioprosthetic valve degeneration, failed annuloplasty rings, and severe mitral annular calcification report technical failure rates ranging from 10 to 25%, particularly in patients with mitral annular calcification, in whom anchoring and LVOT geometry are highly variable [7–9]. Complications such as valve embolization, malposition, and PVL are major contributors to early hemodynamic deterioration and frequently necessitate bailout intervention [7–10]. Valve embolization is of particular concern; as registry data demonstrated an association with increased mortality and frequent need for emergent surgical intervention [10]. Despite these risks, our findings demonstrated that emergent surgical bailout could be performed safely in selected patients. Both patients who underwent surgical explantation in our cohort survived to discharge without major complications, consistent with recent studies showing acceptable outcomes when surgical conversion was executed promptly at experienced centers [11, 12]. These results challenge earlier assumptions that surgery in this setting carries prohibitive risk and underscore the importance of maintaining surgical readiness during TMVR procedures.

Feasibility of rescue procedures

In this study, the predominant rescue strategy was additional ViV deployment, which was performed in 70.6% of unsuccessful cases. ViV implantation has emerged as an essential transcatheter bailout strategy in cases of malposition, inadequate expansion, or incomplete sealing. Prior studies have demonstrated high procedural success rates and favorable early outcomes [2, 4, 13]. The 75% ViV success rate observed in this study was consistent with national and international reports [13, 14].

Vascular plug placement was also effective for managing PVL. Prior analyses highlight that PVL following TMVR – particularly in the presence of MAC or irregular landing zones – can be successfully treated with occlude devices, resulting in reduced regurgitant volume and improved hemodynamic stability [6, 15]. The improvement observed after plug placement in our series supports these findings and reinforces the value of incorporating percutaneous closure techniques into TMVR rescue algorithms. In this study, identifying PVL location in intraprocedural TEE was challenging in some cases. As a result, the rate of additional ViV TMVR procedure was higher than that of vascular plug.

Interpretation of early and mid-term outcomes

The lack of a statistically significant difference in overall early mortality likely reflected the small sample size and the uniformly high surgical-risk profile of the population. Importantly, among patients who survived the immediate post-procedural period, the mid-term outcomes were comparable between unsuccessful and successful groups. Notably, the early mortality in this study exceeded the predicted risk based on the STS risk score. In addition to risk factors in the STS model, patient’s fragility might contribute to the high mortality. However, the preoperative fragility was objectively evaluated in some patients but not in all patients.

Perspective for new system

From this study, the deployment of SAPIEN3 for unstable anchor was one of technical challenging parts to perform TMVR procedure. New Edwards M3 TMVR system offers radiopaque makers to visualize proper positioning, and functional turns provide a stable anchor for the SAPIEN M3 valve. In addition to them, encircling turn captures the native mitral leaflets. The new system might provide more stable deployment of TMVR valve and improve outcomes.

These findings suggested that unsuccessful initial valve deployment did not irreversibly compromise clinical trajectory when appropriate rescue strategies were implemented promptly. Once hemodynamic stability was restored – whether through ViV implantation, vascular plug placement, or surgical explantation – patients could achieve survival outcomes comparable to those with successful initial deployment. These findings underscored the importance of rapid, structured decision-making during TMVR and highlights the increasing sophistication of transcatheter and surgical bailout techniques.

Limitation

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective single-center design and modest sample size may limit statistical power and generalizability. Second, device iterations and procedural experience evolved over the study period, which may have influenced failure mechanisms and outcomes. Third, long-term follow-up was incomplete for some patients, including those who expired or were lost during follow-up periods, limiting the precision of survival estimates and incidence of major adverse events. Fourth, the lack of statistical significance in mortality between the 2 groups may reflect the relatively small case number in the unsuccessful group. In particular, the early death in the unsuccessful group happened in five patients. Fifth, the lack of measurement and sizing from preoperative computer tomography may make this study limited in its impact.

Conclusions

The study was underpowered to detect differences in all-cause mortality, but unsuccessful deployment was associated with substantially higher numerical early mortality and significantly higher cardiac mortality. Additional valve in valve deployment, placement of vascular plug, or surgical intervention was feasible as rescue procedures.

Postoperative pancreatic fistula: a bibliometric analysis of research trends and a meta-analysis on the association with intra pancreatic fat deposition
Authors: Alapati Waili, Maidina Ruzimaimaiti, Maidina Talifu, Zhaoxin Zhuo, Yunzhen Jia, Wei Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPostoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF) remains a common and serious complication following pancreatic resection. Intra pancreatic fat deposition (IPFD) has been increasingly recognized as a potential modifiable risk factor, but its clinical significance across different surgical procedures is not fully defined. This study aims to comprehensively evaluate the correlation between IPFD and POPF by integrating bibliometric trends with meta-analytic evidence. This study employed a dual-method approach. First, a bibliometric analysis was conducted on 3698 publications from the Web of Science Core Collection (2005–2025) to map the research landscape, trends, and collaborative networks in POPF research. Subsequently, a systematic review and meta-analysis were performed following PRISMA guidelines, with literature retrieved from four databases: PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and Scopus. The meta-analysis ultimately included 22 studies for quantitative synthesis. Random or fixed effects models were used to pool odds ratios (ORs) for the association between IPFD and POPF following pancreatoduodenectomy (PD) and distal pancreatectomy (DP), respectively. Bibliometric analysis revealed a consistent dynamic growth in research on POPF, with the focus evolving from early definitional studies toward precision prediction and prevention. China, the United States, and Japan emerged as the leading contributors in this field. The meta-analysis, which incorporated 22 studies involving 687 patients with POPF (grades B/C), demonstrated a significant positive association between IPFD and POPF following PD (OR: 2.33, 95% CI 1.64–3.31, p < 0.001). In contrast, analysis of DP studies showed no significant correlation (OR: 0.79, 95% CI 0.26–2.42, p = 0.69). The overall incidence of POPF (B/C) across included studies was 26% (95% CI 20–32%). Considerable heterogeneity was observed, partially attributable to regional variations and inconsistent IPFD measurement methodologies. IPFD appears to be a significant preoperative predictor for POPF after PD but not after DP. This procedure-specific association underscores the importance of integrating preoperative IPFD assessment into clinical risk stratification for PD patients to guide tailored surgical planning and perioperative management. Introduction

The International Study Group on Pancreatic Fistula (ISGPF) updated diagnostic criteria in 2016 defines postoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF) as drainage effluent with a starch amylase concentration exceeding three times the upper limit of normal serum amylase, occurring 3 days or later postoperatively and accompanied by clinical symptoms1. POPF is categorized into Grades A, B, and C, with Grades B and C referred to as clinically relevant POPF (CR-POPF) due to the requirement for adjustments in patient management1,2. Pancreatic resection remains the primary therapeutic approach for pancreatic diseases3. However, POPF, as a major postoperative complication, significantly impacts patient prognosis, with an incidence rate ranging from 3 to 45%4–6. Previous studies have identified several important risk factors for POPF, including pancreatic consistency, pancreatic duct diameter, ischemia of the pancreatic remnant, and intraoperative blood loss7–9. Identifying high-risk patients is critical to reducing POPF incidence and improving outcomes.

The International Study Group for Pancreatic Surgery (ISGPS) classification framework highlights that pancreatic texture is a key factor in predicting POPF10. Research demonstrates that softer pancreatic texture is associated with a significantly elevated risk of CR-POPF following pancreaticoduodenectomy (PD)11,12. However, the association between pancreatic texture and POPF is weaker following distal pancreatectomy (DP), likely attributable to the greater anatomical complexity of PD and the role of pancreatic texture in managing pancreatic juice leakage during healing9,13.Recent studies suggest that Intra pancreatic fat deposition (IPFD) may serve as a novel predictor for POPF in the preoperative setting14,15.Histologically, IPFD refers to the pathological accumulation of fat within the interlobular spaces or between pancreatic acinar cells16. When the fat content exceeds the normal physiological range (histologically defined as fat replacement of > 10% of the pancreatic area), this condition is termed Fatty Pancreas Disease (FPD)17. Mathur et al. found that patients with higher pancreatic fat scores, determined by intralobular and interlobular fat content, are more prone to developing POPF18. Rosso’s prospective study revealed that a IPFD exceeding 10% significantly increases the risk of POPF after pancreatic resection19. Xingjun et al. analysis further confirmed that a main pancreatic duct diameter ≤ 3 mm, increased severity of fatty pancreas, and reduced pancreatic fibrosis are independent risk factors for POPF20.Accurate preoperative assessment of IPFD is crucial. Advances in imaging technologies, such as magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS), computed tomography (CT), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), have provided effective means for preoperative measurement of pancreatic fat content21,22. MRI, in particular, by detecting fat proton signals, has become the preferred non-invasive quantitative method for measuring pancreatic fat content23. Preoperative evaluation of IPFD using imaging techniques such as MRI, CT, or abdominal ultrasound offers critical evidence for predicting POPF and holds significant value for clinical decision-making.

Since the first successful tumor resection in the nineteenth century, pancreatic surgery has remained a focal point of medical research due to its complexity and postoperative complications24. While much research has centered on POPF, systematic synthesis of the current state and future directions in this field remains insufficient. The relationship between POPF and IPFD remains inadequately understood. Although a meta-analysis by Lu Zhou et al. (2021) has provided some insights, its conclusions are limited by the small number of included studies and the lack of differentiation in surgical approaches25. Bibliometrics, as a method for analyzing the quantitative characteristics of scientific literature, offers insights into research dynamics through metrics such as publication volume, quality, citation patterns, and interdisciplinary dissemination26. By integrating bibliometric analysis and meta-analysis, this study systematically reviews the current state and trends in POPF research and delves into the relationship between POPF and IPFD. It aims to provide new perspectives for research in this field and guide future research priorities.

Material and methods

Bibliometric

Data sources and retrieval strategies

In this study, we searched the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) for literature on POPF after pancreatic surgery published between January 1, 2005, and August 10, 2025. The search strategy used the query statement: (TS = (“pancreaticoduodenectomy” OR “pancreatectomy” OR “pancreatic resection” OR “Distal pancreatectomy” OR “Partial pancreatectomy” OR “Pancreatic surgery” OR “Whipple” OR "pancreatic head resection") AND (“pancreatic fistula” OR “Anastomotic leak” OR “POPF” OR "clinically relevant postoperative pancreatic fistula")) and was limited to articles and reviews. To ensure the accuracy of the included literature, we evaluated the titles and abstracts of all search records and merged duplicate data on keywords, articles, and institutions. This process ultimately yielded 3698 relevant articles for analysis (Fig. 1).

Flow chart.

Bibliometric analysis

In our bibliometric analysis of 3698 POPF-related articles, tools like R 4.3.2, VOSviewer, and CiteSpace were utilized.VOSviewer, developed by Van Eck and Waltman from Leiden University, excels in precise statistics, large-scale data clustering, high-density visualization, and hotspot identification27. We used it for analyzing co-occurrences of countries, institutions, authors, journals, and keywords. In the visualizations, nodes represent these elements, with their size and color corresponding to quantity and category, respectively. The link thickness reflects the strength of collaboration or co-citation.CiteSpace is known for its citation and keyword co-occurrence analysis. We applied it for keyword burst and citation burst detection to spot emerging highly-cited works and keywords, thus pinpointing future research directions. Additionally, the “bibliometric” package in R quantified international collaboration patterns, and Origin 2021 visualized cooperation among countries, offering a comprehensive view of academic dynamics and trends in POPF research.

Meta-analysis

This systematic review and meta-analysis strictly adhere to the PRISMA 2020 framework and guidelines to ensure standardized research processes and transparent reporting28. The research protocol has been registered in the international prospective register of systematic reviews, PROSPERO, under registration number CRD420251133044, to guarantee the rigor and traceability of the study.

Search strategy

This meta-analysis included literature published in PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and Scopus databases up to August 10, 2025. The research team thoroughly checked references of included studies and key reviews or meta-analyses to ensure comprehensive coverage and minimize omission risks. The initial search combined MeSH and text words. Some search terms were: (“pancreaticoduodenectomy” OR “pancreatectomy” OR “pancreaticoduodenectom” OR “Whipple”) AND (“pancreatic fistula” OR “Anastomotic leak” OR “POPF” OR "clinically relevant postoperative pancreatic fistula") AND (“Pancreatic steatosis” OR “Pancreas steatosis” OR "Fatty infiltration of the pancreas" OR "Pancreatic fat infiltration"). For the complete search strategies of all databases, refer to Supplementary Material. Finally, only studies with available English full texts were selected for full-text screening.

Study selection

Our systematic review rigorously followed specific article-selection criteria. Inclusion criteria were as follows: participants aged 18 or older; clear pre-operative, intra-operative, or post-operative assessment of IPFD with imaging or pathological tests; analysis of the IPFD-POPF(B/C) relationship; and availability of sufficient data (e.g., odds ratios, risk ratios, risk differences, and 95% confidence intervals) or information for calculating these values. Exclusion criteria were: only mentioning pancreatic texture (soft pancreas) without explicit IPFD discussion; no control group; non-peer-reviewed articles (reviews, letters, etc.); animal studies; non-English articles; and inaccessible full texts. For multi-variable-adjusted studies, we prioritized models controlling for the most confounders. If effect sizes weren’t reported or only baseline data were given, we calculated effect sizes using the four-fold table method. If data extraction failed, we contacted authors for data; if unsuccessful, we excluded the articles.In this study, IPFD was defined as meeting any of the following criteria: histopathological fat replacement exceeding 10% of the pancreatic area, a Magnetic Resonance Imaging-proton density fat fraction (MRI-PDFF) greater than 5%, a CT attenuation value of less than -10 Hounsfield Units (HU), or a pancreas-to-spleen attenuation ratio below 0.7.

Data extraction

This study strictly follows PRISMA guidelines and employs a three-stage evaluation process. In stage one, four independent researchers (MR, MT, ZZ, J-YZ) conduct a blind preliminary assessment of literature eligibility. Stage two involves systematic cross-validation of screening decisions via a standardized protocol. In stage three, chief researcher AW leads a discussion to resolve disagreements. A validated data extraction template is utilized to record key details, including author, year, country/region, age average, BMI (kg/m2), imaging method for IPFD assessment, sex ratio, surgical method, sample size, total POPF (B/C), study design, and effect indices (OR, RR, HR, and 95% CI). The Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) is used for methodological quality assessment with triple independent scoring. Study quality is categorized into three levels: low (0–5), moderate (6–7), and high (8–9). AW conducts structured arbitration of all scoring disagreements to ensure consistency among raters, maintaining analytical rigor throughout the evidence synthesis process.

Statistical analyses

This study used the meta package in R (4.4.2) for primary statistical analysis and STATA (17.0) for sensitivity analysis to explore the link between IPFD and POPF and determine POPF’s incidence. Longitudinal studies used RR and 95% CI to assess this link, while cross-sectional studies used OR and 95% CI. Given the prevalence of cross-sectional studies, OR was chosen as the effect measure to combine results from both study types.

To enhance the reliability of the results, Cochran’s Q test and the I2 statistic were used to evaluate heterogeneity between studies. A fixed-effects model was adopted when P ≥ 0.1 and I2 ≤ 50%, and a random-effects model was used when P < 0.1 and I2 > 50%. Subgroup analyses based on age and region were conducted, and sensitivity analyses were performed by removing individual studies to check their impact on the overall results.Egger’s and Begg’s tests were used for funnel-plot analysis to evaluate publication bias, with trim-and-fill analysis applied if bias was found. All analyses used two-tailed tests, with statistical significance set at P < 0.05.

Results

Bibliometrics

In our bibliometric analysis of 3698 POPF-related articles, the academic output in this field has shown significant dynamic growth (Fig. 2A). From 2005 to 2025, the development of POPF research can be divided into four stages. In the foundation and exploration stage (2005–2010), annual publication output was relatively low. Research focused on defining POPF, understanding its clinical impact, and identifying traditional risk factors, setting the stage for subsequent studies. Then came the rapid growth stage (2011–2015), when publication output increased significantly. Research deepened, with more high-quality clinical studies emerging, driving the optimization of POPF prevention and management strategies. In the explosive growth stage (2016–2021), the field experienced a surge in research activity. In 2021, 325 papers were published, marking the peak of research interest. In the deepening stage (2022–present), publication output continues to grow. Research has expanded in scope and depth. Advanced technologies like imaging-omics and artificial intelligence are increasingly applied in predictive models, and research methods have become more refined and diverse.

Country and regional publication analysis

Among the 82 countries analyzed for POPF-related research output, China ranked first with 835 papers and 12,908 citations, followed by Japan with 645 papers and 16,068 citations, and the US with 597 papers and 31,547 citations. Notably, England, with just 159 papers, had the highest average citation at 79.36, indicating high research impact (Supplementary Table 1 and Fig. 2B). Cluster analysis of 37 countries with over 10 publications each (Fig. 3A) formed six clusters. Circle size represented publication volume, and color indicated internal collaboration strength. The largest cluster was red, including Italy and eight other countries, while the green cluster, led by the US, had eight countries. Analysis of collaborative ties among the 82 countries showed the US and Italy collaborated most frequently (65 times), followed by Italy and the UK (64 times), and Italy and France (53 times) (Fig. 3B). These findings highlight the international cooperation network and academic influence in POPF research.

Institutional publication analysis

Globally, 2954 academic institutions have published research on POPF, with 182 of them having published at least 10 papers. Our analysis of the top 10 institutions (Supplementary Table 2) shows that Zhejiang University tops the list with 111 publications, followed by Heidelberg University with 76 and Shanghai Jiao Tong University with 68. Notably, three of the top 10 institutions are from China, accounting for 5.86% of the total publications, which highlights China’s significant contribution to POPF research. Nanjing Medical University, despite publishing 38 papers, has the highest average citation per paper at 142.08, reflecting its high-quality research and substantial academic influence.

We further performed cluster analysis on the 182 institutions with over 10 publications (Supplementary Fig. 1). The results identified five main clusters. The red cluster is particularly significant, comprising 61 Chinese universities and research institutions, which further underscores China’s active involvement in POPF research. Additionally, the green cluster consists of 60 research institutions, and the blue cluster has 40. This distribution reflects the collaborative and competitive landscape of global research institutions in POPF, as well as the different regional focuses and characteristics in academic research.

Authorship analysis

In the field of POPF research, a total of 15,111 authors have contributed, with 107 of them publishing at least 15 papers. Supplementary Table 3 lists the top 10 authors by publication volume. Bassi C ranks first with 72 papers and 3224 citations, followed by Salvia A and Marchegiani G. It is important to highlight that Bassi C, a core member of the ISGPF, first established the diagnostic criteria for POPF in 2005 and later updated it in 2016. His work has been instrumental in shaping the definition, classification, and standardization of clinical practices related to POPF, and his role in unifying the understanding of POPF across academic and clinical fields is highly significant.

To analyze the collaborative networks among authors, a cluster analysis was performed on authors who had published more than 15 POPF-related papers (Fig. 5). The analysis resulted in 10 clusters. The red cluster, represented by Hackert T, primarily focuses on identifying risk factors for POPF and developing and validating clinical prediction models. The cluster centered around Bassi C concentrates on establishing diagnostic criteria for POPF. Meanwhile, the cluster led by Besselink M investigates the correlation between surgical approaches and POPF. This clustering pattern reflects the division of research interests and collaborative efforts among authors, revealing the knowledge structure and academic ecosystem within the POPF research community. Such a cooperative network not only facilitates the sharing and dissemination of research findings but also drives the development of multicenter studies, offering a broader perspective and stronger research capabilities to address the complex clinical issue of POPF.

The association between IPFD and POPF after PD

Our analysis of relevant literature focused on the correlation between IPFD and POPF following PD. Of the 21 included articles, 16 found a correlation between IPFD and POPF, while five did not.

We conducted a summary analysis of these data using a random-effects model, which showed a significant correlation between IPFD and POPF (OR 2.33, 95% CI 1.64–3.31, P < 0.001, I2 = 93) (Fig. 8). Given the high heterogeneity, we performed subgroup analyses based on region, age, BMI, sample size, study design, and IPFD measurement methods. The results indicated that region, BMI, and study design might be the main sources of heterogeneity (Table 4 and Supplementary Figs. 3–9).

The association between IPFD and POPF after DP

Few studies have explored the correlation between IPFD and POPF following DP. In our analysis, the two included studies found no significant correlation. Using a fixed-effects model, we also found no correlation (OR 0.79, 95% CI 0.26–2.42, P = 0.69, I2 = 21%) (Fig. 9). Thus, IPFD may not be a key predictor of POPF after DP. However, given the limited studies, more high-quality research is needed to verify this conclusion.

The prevalence of POPF in the included studies and the methods used to assess IPFD

Our analysis of the included studies shows that all the literatures reported the total sample size and the number of cases of POPF (B/C). The summary analysis results indicate that the incidence of POPF (B/C) was 26% (95% CI 20–32%, P < 0.001) (Fig. 10). No significant publication bias was found by drawing funnel plots and conducting Begg’s tests. In addition, the stability of the results was confirmed by sensitivity analysis (Supplementary Figs. 12, 13). In terms of IPFD evaluation methods, 10 studies evaluated IPFD based on pathological examination, 8 used CT scans, and 4 used MRI. Discussion

In this study, we innovatively combined bibliometric analysis with meta-analysis to delve into the POPF-related research field. Using bibliometric methods, we systematically examined the current research status, grasped research trends, and anticipated future directions. Meanwhile, through meta-analysis, we explored the correlation between IPFD and POPF, highlighting IPFD’s potential as a key predictive factor in pancreatic fistula development.This novel integration of two analytical approaches aims to offer more scientific and comprehensive theoretical support and practical guidance for accurately predicting and effectively preventing POPF. It will propel the research field into deeper dimensions.

Bibliometric analysis shows a steady upward trend in POPF-related research from 2005 to 2025. In 2005, the first systematic POPF definition and A/B/C grading system were introduced, offering a common framework for research and laying the foundation for the first-stage development2. Between 2011 and 2015, the importance of identifying risk factors and building predictive models became clear, with many related studies published, marking the second stage32–34. The 2016 ISGPS update refined the definition and established CR-POPF as the core research endpoint1. This boosted global research standardization and result comparability, driving the third-stage acceleration. Lately, advanced surgical techniques like robotic surgery have gained traction. Researchers are probing their impact on POPF35–37. Amid the global metabolic disease epidemic, especially obesity and diabetes, scholars are examining IPFD’s effects on pancreatic function14. Basic research indicates that IPFD might weaken pancreatic tissue strength and impair anastomotic healing17,38,39. Also, the growth of imaging and AI technologies has enabled the extraction of numerous features from images40,41. Combined with machine learning, this has enhanced predictive model-building and bolstered the fourth-stage research push.

This study found that POPF-related research has developed a highly structured academic ecosystem. Top-tier surgical journals are central to knowledge dissemination in this field, reflecting the high-quality nature of the research. Notably, Annals of Surgery, with 13,355 citations, sets the standard for academic influence. It shows how leading journals can guide paradigm shifts by publishing landmark guidelines42,43. Author collaboration networks reveal trends towards cooperation and specialization. Ten major research clusters, led by scholars like Bassi, Hackert, and Besselink, focus on key areas such as diagnostic standardization, predictive tool development, and surgical optimization2,44,45. This division of labor speeds up multicenter research and highlights how academic leaders drive collaborative innovation via international organizations.At the national level, high-output research institutions are mainly found in European and American medical centers, showing the deep impact of resource and technological advantages on research activity. However, Asian countries like China, Japan, and South Korea have increasingly contributed in recent years, reflecting a more globalized research landscape.

Burst citation and keyword analyses indicate a paradigm shift in POPF research from a “technology-centered” to a “precision-prevention-focused” approach. From 2005 to 2014, keywords clustered around optimizing surgical techniques and controlling complications, in line with the early focus on ensuring surgical safety. The 2016 ISGPS standard update, marked by burst citations, established a standardized research paradigm1. Recently, citation bursts and keyword trends point to two major shifts: personalized prevention and surgical innovation, signaling a move from population-based evidence to precise interventions. Significantly, IPFD, as a new predictive factor, combined with imaging-omics and AI-based prediction models, is ushering in an intelligent era of risk stratification46–48. The meta-analysis of the IPFD-POPF relationship in this study seeks to contribute to this cutting-edge shift, aiming to offer targeted evidence for developing multimodal prevention strategies.

IPFD is common in the general population. Research by Wong VW and colleagues indicates a prevalence of IFPD at 16.1% (95%CI 13.3%–18.8%)49. A growing body of evidence links excess IPFD to several pancreatic pathologies, including pancreatic cancer, diabetes, pancreatitis, and post-pancreatic-surgery complications50. POPF, a severe post-pancreatic-surgery complication, can lead to adverse outcomes such as bleeding, inflammatory reactions, sepsis, organ failure, and even death. This, in turn, significantly prolongs hospitalization and increases medical costs51,52. The potential mechanism by which IPFD may contribute to POPF is thought to involve the infiltration of fat cells into pancreatic tissue, potentially triggering chronic sterile inflammation, altering the tissue structure, and reducing its hardness38. These histological changes increase the technical challenges of pancreaticojejunostomy and can result in poor anastomotic healing. Furthermore, compared to pancreatic fibrosis, lipopancreas causes a more significant decrease in pancreatic exocrine function53. During the perioperative period, despite normal or mildly reduced exocrine function, increased pancreatic juice secretion raises the risk of POPF.

The incidence of POPF shows significant variation across different types of pancreatic resections, reflecting the differing surgical technical challenges and anatomical considerations. Recent clinical series indicate that POPF occurs in approximately 15 to 20% of patients following PD, and in 20 to 30% of patients after DP52,54,55. In central pancreatectomy (CP), where two pancreatic remnant ends require management and technical demands are higher, the incidence of POPF exceeds 30%56,57. Similarly, the impact of IPFD on POPF risk appears to vary across different surgical contexts. For instance, Li et al. identified IPFD as a significant risk factor for POPF following PD, whereas Kugiyama et al. found no such association in their cohort58,59. Our meta-analysis further supports a strong correlation between IPFD and POPF after PD (OR 2.33, 95% CI 1.64–3.31, p < 0.001), a finding that is consistent with the earlier meta-analysis by Zhou et al25. In contrast, our analysis found no significant association between IPFD and POPF following DP (OR 0.79, 95% CI 0.26–2.42, p = 0.69). These differences could be explained by fundamental differences in the nature of the surgical procedures. PD involves pancreatojejunostomy, the integrity of which heavily depends on the quality of the pancreatic tissue (for example, IPFD-associated soft pancreatic tissue increases the risk of anastomotic leakage). In contrast, DP involves pancreatic substance resection and remnant stapling/suturing, where factors such as remnant blood supply and suturing techniques may play a more important role than the mere tissue composition.

In recent years, the rapid development of multi-disciplinary integration technologies, especially imaging-omics and AI, is making preoperative precise prediction of POPF after PD increasingly feasible. Many studies have confirmed the value of imaging-based AI models in individualized risk prediction. For instance, Mu et al. developed a deep-learning model based on preoperative CT scans, which showed good performance in predicting CR-POPF with an AUC of 0.85 (95% CI 0.80–0.90)60. Similarly, Han et al. developed an AI prediction model that also achieved good discriminative ability (AUC: 0.74)48. However, despite the potential of AI in overall risk prediction, it still faces significant challenges in accurately quantifying IPFD. There are often notable differences between AI-automated quantification results and manual measurements61. These differences may stem from several factors, including inconsistent annotation of IPFD in training data, heterogeneity in imaging acquisition protocols and devices, sampling errors due to the spatially heterogeneous distribution of fat infiltration in the pancreas, and the lack of a widely accepted “gold standard” for IPFD quantification. Consequently, future research should focus on building large-scale, multi-center imaging datasets with standardized annotations. Additionally, there is a need to develop explainable AI systems that can integrate multimodal information, such as from CT and MRI scans, along with clinical features. These efforts could further enhance the accuracy of IPFD quantification and its application value in clinical decision-making.

Over the past decade, minimally invasive surgery has progressively become a dominant approach in pancreatic surgery62,63. Among these techniques, robotic pancreatic surgery has gained increasing recognition among surgeons due to its stability and operative precision. The findings of this study, which identify IPFD as a significant risk factor for POPF after PD, hold considerable clinical relevance in the current era of robotic and precision surgery. Preoperative quantification of IPFD via CT or MRI provides an objective basis for surgical decision-making. In support of this, Ausania et al. demonstrated that a robotic approach in obese patients can reduce manipulation of the pancreas, improve exposure of the surgical field, and enhance control of bleeding events, thereby minimizing potential injury to the pancreatic parenchyma and reducing the risk of POPF64.

For patients with high IPFD undergoing robotic or laparoscopic PD, particularly those at high risk presenting with soft pancreatic texture and small duct diameter, an individualized comprehensive strategy based on risk assessment is recommended. Specific measures may include selecting more reliable anastomotic techniques, utilizing reinforcement materials such as reinforced sutures or tissue sealants, and more proactive perioperative administration of somatostatin analogs. However, it must be clearly stated that the current evidence supporting the efficacy of any single strategy is inconsistent, and most interventions still lack definitive validation from large-scale randomized controlled trials. Although prospective studies are still needed to confirm whether adjusting surgical strategies based on IPFD improves patient outcomes, integrating this objective biomarker into the preoperative evaluation system and developing individualized surgical plans accordingly represents a crucial step toward advancing precision surgery.

Our meta-analysis demonstrates a significant association between IPFD and POPF following PD. However, when evaluated using the GRADE framework, the certainty of this evidence is Low (Supplementary Table 6). This is primarily due to the observational nature of the included studies, which carries an inherent risk of confounding, and the substantial heterogeneity observed among the results. In contrast, the analysis for DP found no significant association, but the certainty of this evidence is Very Low, owing to the very limited number of studies and imprecise effect estimate. Therefore, while IPFD appears to be a promising predictor for POPF after PD, these conclusions should be interpreted with caution, and the evidence for DP remains inconclusive, highlighting a critical gap for future research.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. First, to reduce database-related heterogeneity and enhance result comparability in the bibliometric analysis, we only used the WoSCC and focused on English-language articles. This may have led to the omission of key regional non-English studies, affecting the comprehensiveness of the research-trend analysis. Second, in the Meta-analysis, we excluded studies with unextractable data. Though we contacted original authors for missing data, the low response rate might have caused selection bias. Third, a critical limitation of the meta-analysis is the potential for residual confounding. As IPFD is closely associated with other known risk factors such as soft pancreatic texture, small duct diameter, and high BMI, the observed association might be influenced by these variables. Although we performed subgroup analyses where possible, the lack of comprehensively adjusted data from all included studies precludes definitive conclusions regarding the independent effect of IPFD. Fourth, few studies have examined the link between pancreatic fistula after DP and IPFD, weakening the evidence for related conclusions. Fifth, no studies have systematically reported the correlation between POPF and IPFD after CP, preventing quantitative analysis in our study. Sixth, despite our subgroup analyses to identify sources of heterogeneity in the Meta-analysis, residual heterogeneity might still affect the main pooled results’ credibility, necessitating cautious interpretation. Finally, the reported POPF incidence is based solely on included-study data, not systematic epidemiological surveys, so extrapolation to the general population should be done with care.

Conclusion

This study combines bibliometric and meta-analytical methods to explore the correlation between IPFD and POPF after pancreatic surgery. The research reveals a paradigm shift in POPF studies from technical optimization to individualized risk management since 2005. It also highlights international collaborative efforts, particularly among China, Japan, the United States, and European countries, in advancing POPF diagnosis and treatment. The meta analysis indicates that IPFD is a significant predictor of POPF after PD, though not after DP. These findings suggest that risk assessment may benefit from being tailored to the surgical procedure. The study also highlights the value of IPFD as a preoperative imaging-based biomarker for POPF prediction and calls for further investigation into the relationship between IPFD and POPF following less-studied procedures such as central pancreatectomy. Despite limitations including language bias and data extraction challenges, this research provides supporting evidence on IPFD’s role in POPF prediction. Future work could focus on developing standardized, multicenter IPFD quantification systems and interpretable AI models to help improve POPF prevention and patient outcomes.

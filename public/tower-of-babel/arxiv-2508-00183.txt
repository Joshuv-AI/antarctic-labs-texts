Improved Bounds on Access-Redundancy Tradeoffs in Quantized Linear Computations
Ching-Fang Li, Mary Wootters

Abstract
Consider the problem of computing quantized linear functions with only a few queries. Formally, given 𝐱 ∈ ℝ k \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k} , our goal is to encode 𝐱 \mathbf{x} as 𝐜 ∈ ℝ n \mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{n} , for n > k n>k , so that for any 𝐰 ∈ 𝒜 k \mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{A}^{k} , 𝐰 T ​ 𝐱 \mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{x} can be computed using at most ℓ \ell queries to 𝐜 \mathbf{c} . Here, 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is some finite set; in this paper we focus on the case where | 𝒜 | = 2 |\mathcal{A}|=2 . Prior work (Ramkumar, Raviv, and Tamo, Trans. IT, 2024) has given constructions and established impossibility results for this problem. We give improved impossibility results, both for the general problem, and for the specific class of construction (block construction) presented in that work. The latter establishes that the block constructions of prior work are optimal within that class. We also initiate the study of approximate recovery for this problem, where the goal is not to recover 𝐰 T ​ 𝐱 \mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{x} exactly but rather to approximate it up to a parameter ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . We give several constructions, and give constructions for ε = 0.1 \varepsilon=0.1 that outperform our impossibility result for exact schemes.



I
Introduction

In this paper we consider the problem of computing quantized linear functions of data with a few queries. Formally, consider the set of linear functions
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}}=\{f_{\mathbf{w}}\mid\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{A}^{k}\}
for some finite set
\mathcal{A}
, where
f_{\mathbf{w}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

The goal is to encode
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
into some
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
n\geq k
, so that, for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
, it is possible to recover
f(\mathbf{x})
given only
\ell\leq n
queries to the symbols of
\mathbf{y}
.
The parameters of interest are the
redundancy ratio

\nu=n/k
, which captures the amount of overhead in the encoding; and the
access ratio

\lambda=\ell/k
, which captures the query complexity. We would like both to be as small as possible. If there is an infinite family of coding schemes that achieves the parameters
(\nu,\lambda)
, we say that this pair is

(see Definition
2
).
Understanding the set of feasible pairs, and coming up with code constructions that achieve them, is motivated by applications in distributed ML and coded computation. (See the discussion in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Briefly, we imagine that
\mathbf{x}
is stored by storing the symbols of
\mathbf{y}
each at different nodes in a distributed system. Our goal is to compute

while accessing as few nodes as possible).

This problem has been studied for various families
\mathcal{F}
over various domains. If
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
for some finite field
\mathbb{F}
, and if
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{F}
, then the problem is equivalent to the existence of linear covering codes (see
[
4
]
and the discussion in
[
1
]
). Over
\mathbb{R}
,
[
5
]
showed how to use covering codes (in a different way) to obtain nontrivial constructions for
\mathcal{A}=\{0,1\}
. This was later improved by
[
1
]
for any
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
with
|\mathcal{A}|=2
: That work showed that the problem is equivalent for any
\mathcal{A}
with
|\mathcal{A}|=2
, and provided both improved constructions and impossibility results. In further work,
[
2
,
3
]
investigated the setting where

.

II-A

Problem Statement

As stated in the introduction, our goal is to design an encoding map that maps
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
to
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We will restrict to linear codes, meaning that the encoding map is given by
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{D}^{T}\mathbf{x}

for some matrix
\mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}
.
For any function
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
in some function class
\mathcal{F}
, we would like to be able to recover
f(\mathbf{x})
given at most
\ell
queries to the symbols of
\mathbf{y}
. We focus on quantized linear computations by considering the function class
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}}=\left\{f_{\mathbf{w}}\,\middle|\,\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{A}^{k}\right\}
where
f_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{x}
,
for some finite coefficient set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}
.
We also restrict our attention to linear decoding maps. That is, for each
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}},
we want that
f(\mathbf{x})
can be represented as some linear combination of
at most
\ell
entries of
\mathbf{y}.

Since everything we wish to compute is linear, we may rephrase the problem in terms of linear algebra. Formally, we define a
\mathcal{A}
-protocol as follows.

Definition 1

(
\mathcal{A}
-protocol)
.

Fix a finite set

, and let

\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times s}

\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{A}^{k}
as a column. A
\mathcal{A}
-protocol
with parameters
(k,n,\ell)
consists of an encoding matrix
\mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}
and a decoding matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times s}
, so that
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{D}\mathbf{A},
and so that
\|\mathbf{a}\|_{0}\leq\ell
for every column
\mathbf{a}
of
\mathbf{A}
.

To see why Definition
1
captures the problem above, note that any
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{A}^{k}
appears as a column of
\mathbf{W}
. To compute
f_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x})
, let
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the corresponding column of
\mathbf{A}
. Then
f_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{a}^{T}\mathbf{D}^{T}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{a}^{T}\mathbf{y},
and
\mathbf{a}
has at most
\ell
nonzero entries.

Given
k
, we want both
n
and
\ell
to be small.
Following
[
1
]
, we study the
access ratio

\ell/k
and the
redundancy ratio

n/k
in the asymptotic regime.

Definition 2

(Feasible pair)
.

We say that a pair
(\nu,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is
\mathcal{A}
-feasible if there exists an infinite family of
\mathcal{A}
-protocols with parameters
\{(k_{i},n_{i},\ell_{i})\}_{i\geq 1}
(with
k_{i}
’s strictly increasing) such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}(n_{i}/k_{i},\ell_{i}/k_{i})=(\nu,\lambda)
.

It is shown in
[
1
]
that taking any
\mathcal{A}
with size two is equivalent in the sense that the resulting feasible pairs are the same. Hence for the rest of the paper, we take
\mathcal{A}=\{\pm 1\}
.
The goal is to characterize the region of feasible pairs.
In other words, we aim to find the optimal tradeoff between the access and redundancy ratios.

Lower Bound for Block Constructions

Recall from Section

[

are

\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{I}_{m}\otimes\mathbf{M}

\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{0}\times n_{0}}

Moreover, for
\mathbf{w}=(\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{m})

\mathbf{w}_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}^{k_{0}}

\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}_{i}

\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}_{i}

In other words, each column of

Using this construction, we have

and

. As

is shown to be feasible.
In the following, we refer to this type of construction as a

with parameters
(k_{0},n_{0},\ell_{0})

In
[
1
]
, some specific block constructions are proposed. Here, we discuss whether there can be any possible improvements using this method.

With similar techniques for the overall lower bound, we derive a lower bound for block constructions, which will show that in fact the non-systematic constructions of
[
1
]
are optimal among block constructions.
The intuition for the improved lower bound is as follows. Using the block construction, the number of
\ell
-subsets of nodes that are used is at most
\binom{n_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}
, which is much smaller than
\binom{n}{\ell}=\binom{mn_{0}}{m\ell_{0}}
, so the resulting lower bound should be strictly larger than the general one.

Theorem 3

(Lower Bound for Block Constructions)
.

A block construction of a
\{\pm 1\}
-protocol with parameters
(k_{0},n_{0},\ell_{0})
and
2\ell_{0}\leq n_{0}

2\ell_{0}+\log\binom{n_{0}}{\ell_{0}}-\log\binom{2\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}\geq k_{0}
.

Proof.

Given the number of blocks
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
k=mk_{0},n=mn_{0},\ell=m\ell_{0}
.
Let
\mathscr{A}(n_{0},\ell_{0},m)\subseteq\binom{[n]}{\ell}
be the collection of
\ell
-subsets such that there are
\ell_{0}
elements in each block of size
n_{0}
. These are exactly the subsets of nodes that are possibly used in the block construction, and we know that
|\mathscr{A}(n_{0},\ell_{0},m)|=\binom{n_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}
.
Analogous to the covering designs in the proof of Theorem
2
, we make the following claim.

Claim 1
.

There exists a family
\mathscr{F}\subseteq\mathscr{A}(n_{0},2\ell_{0},m)

with

|\mathscr{F}|\leq\big[1+m\ln\textstyle\binom{2\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}\big]\displaystyle\frac{\binom{n_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}}{\binom{2\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}}

such that
\forall\,\mathcal{S}\in\mathscr{A}(n_{0},\ell_{0},m)
,
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
for some
\mathcal{T}\in\mathscr{F}
.

Before we prove the claim, we see how it implies the theorem.
Using the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
2
, we have

\displaystyle 2^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg|\bigcup_{\mathcal{S}\in\mathscr{A}(n_{0},\ell_{0},m)}\hskip-1.9919pt\Big\{\{\pm 1\}\text{-vectors in the span of }\mathbf{D}|_{\mathcal{S}}\Big\}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathscr{F}}\Bigg|\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}\\
\mathcal{S}\in\mathscr{A}(n_{0},\ell_{0},m)\end{subarray}}\hskip-7.11317pt\Big\{\{\pm 1\}\text{-vectors in the span of }\mathbf{D}|_{\mathcal{S}}\Big\}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathscr{F}|2^{2\ell},

where the last line follows from (
1
), as
|\mathcal{T}|=2\ell
for all
\mathcal{T}\in\mathscr{F}
. This implies that

k\leq\log|\mathscr{F}|+2\ell
. Since this should hold for arbitrarily large
m
, plugging in the expression from Claim
1
, we obtain the desired result.

It remains to prove the Claim
1
. We do so using the probabilistic method, similar to the proof Lemma
1
in
[
7
]
. Let

a=m\ln\textstyle\binom{2\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{n_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}}{\binom{2\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}}\quad\text{and}\quad p=\frac{a}{\binom{n_{0}}{2\ell_{0}}^{m}}.

If
a\geq\binom{n_{0}}{2\ell_{0}}^{m}
, simply take
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{A}(n_{0},2\ell_{0},m)
. Otherwise, randomly construct
\mathscr{F}_{p}
where each
\mathcal{T}\in\mathscr{A}(n_{0},2\ell_{0},m)
is chosen independently with probability
p
.
Thus
\mathbb{E}[|\mathscr{F}_{p}|]=a
.
Let
u(\mathscr{F}_{p})=\left\{\mathcal{S}\in\mathscr{A}(n_{0},\ell_{0},m)\,\middle|\,\forall\,\mathcal{T}\in\mathscr{F}_{p},\ \mathcal{S}\not\subseteq\mathcal{T}\right\}
denote the uncovered sets. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\{\mathcal{S}\in u(\mathscr{F}_{p})\}

\displaystyle=(1-p)^{\binom{n_{0}-\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}}\leq e^{-p\binom{n_{0}-\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|u(\mathscr{F}_{p})|]

\displaystyle\leq\textstyle\binom{n_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}e^{-p\binom{n_{0}-\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}}.

Let
v:\mathscr{A}(n_{0},\ell_{0},m)\to\mathscr{A}(n_{0},2\ell_{0},m)
be a function such that
\mathcal{S}\subseteq v(\mathcal{S})
for all
\mathcal{S}\in\mathscr{A}(n_{0},\ell_{0},m)
. Then
\mathscr{F}_{p}^{*}=\mathscr{F}_{p}\cup\left\{v(\mathcal{S})\,\middle|\,\mathcal{S}\in u(\mathscr{F}_{p})\right\}
covers all
\mathcal{S}\in\mathscr{A}(n_{0},\ell_{0},m)
and

\mathbb{E}[|\mathscr{F}_{p}^{*}|]\leq a+\textstyle\binom{n_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}e^{-p\binom{n_{0}-\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}}=\big[1+m\ln\textstyle\binom{2\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}\big]\displaystyle\frac{\binom{n_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}}{\binom{2\ell_{0}}{\ell_{0}}^{m}}.

It suffices to pick some
\mathscr{F}_{p}^{*}
such that
|\mathscr{F}_{p}^{*}|\leq\mathbb{E}[|\mathscr{F}_{p}^{*}|]
.
This proves Claim
1
and hence the theorem.
∎

IV
Approximate Recovery

In this section, instead of exactly computing
f(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}
, we slightly relax the requirement.
Given
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, for
\mathbf{w}\in\{\pm 1\}^{k}
,
now the goal is to output some
z
such that
|\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}-z|^{2}\leq\varepsilon k\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
.
Without loss of generality, assume
\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}=1
. Again we focus on linear schemes.
It suffices to design an encoding matrix
\mathbf{D}
and a recovery matrix
\mathbf{A}
so that each column of
\mathbf{W-DA}
has squared
\ell_{2}
-norm at most
\varepsilon k
. To see this, recall that
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}
.
Given
\mathbf{w}\in\{\pm 1\}^{k}
, which is a column of
\mathbf{W}
, let
\mathbf{a}
be the corresponding column of
\mathbf{A}
. Then the scheme will output
z=\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{y}
, and we have

|\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}-z|^{2}=|\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}|^{2}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{w-Da}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon k.

Hence in the following we focus on designing
\mathbf{D}
and
\mathbf{A}
to approximate
\mathbf{W}
in this sense.
Similar as before, we call this an
\varepsilon
-approximation protocol with parameters
(k,n,\ell)
. The goal is to characterize the region of feasible pairs for
\varepsilon
-approximation protocols.

IV-A
1
Covering Codes

Using the same idea as in
[
1
]
, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

Given a covering code
\mathcal{C}
with length
k_{0}
and covering radius
r\geq 1
over alphabet
\{\pm 1\}
, and an integer
b\in\{0,\dots,r-1\}
, then the pair
(\frac{k_{0}+\hat{c}}{k_{0}},\frac{r-b+1}{k_{0}})
is feasible with
\varepsilon\leq\frac{4b(k_{0}-r)}{k_{0}(k_{0}-r+b)}
. Recall that
\hat{c}=|\hat{\mathcal{C}}|
, and
\hat{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is a code with exactly one of
\pm\mathbf{c}
for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
.
Further, the pair
(\frac{\hat{c}}{k_{0}},\frac{1}{k_{0}})
is feasible with
\varepsilon\leq\frac{4r(k_{0}-r)}{k_{0}^{2}}
.

Proof.

For
b\in\{0,\dots,r-1\}
, we construct a block construction following the scheme of
[
1
, Theorem 2]
, which is also described in Section
II-B1
.
Suppose we have
m
blocks, and
k=mk_{0}
. Then for each block, instead of querying an additional
r
nodes to correct all the errors, as is done in
[
1
]
, we query at most
r-b
additional nodes.
Let
\bar{\mathbf{c}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{k}
be the element of
\{(\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{m})\mid\mathbf{c}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}\ \forall\,i\in[m]\}
that is closest to
\mathbf{w}
. Let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq[k]
with
|\mathcal{I}|\leq m(r-b)
be a subset of the indices
on which
\mathbf{w}
and
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
differ. Now we approximate
\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}
with a linear combination of the
m
nodes containing
\mathbf{c}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}_{i}
or
-\mathbf{c}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}_{i}
, along with the nodes containing
x_{j}=\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}
for
j\in\mathcal{I}
.
Since
w_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
, it makes sense to restrict the coefficients to be identical to each other within the first part and the second part, and

\varepsilon
is obtained by solving
\varepsilon k\leq\max_{\mathbf{w}}\min_{\alpha,\beta}\big\lVert\alpha\bar{\mathbf{c}}+\beta\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{w}\big\lVert_{2}^{2}
.
Note that the Hamming distance between
\mathbf{w}
and
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
is
mr
in the worst case, solving the proposed optimization problem suggests taking
\beta=\alpha+1
with
\alpha=\frac{k_{0}-r-b}{k_{0}-r+b}
.
For the pair
(\frac{\hat{c}}{k_{0}},\frac{1}{k_{0}})
, the scheme is constructed by not querying any additional nodes, thus no need to store each
x_{j}
. The corresponding
\varepsilon
is calculated using the same arguments.
∎

IV-A

Discarding Blocks

This method is again modified from
[
1
]
. However, it applies to any block construction, not just those where the matrix
\mathbf{M}
is found via covering codes.

Proposition 2
.

Given a family of protocols using a block construction, with feasible pair
(\nu,\lambda)
, there is a family of
\varepsilon
-approximation protocols with feasible pair
\big((1-\varepsilon)\nu,(1-\varepsilon)\lambda\big)
.

Proof.

When the number of block is
m
, simply discard
\left\lfloor\varepsilon m\right\rfloor
blocks.
∎

IV-A
3
K-SVD Algorithm

Our final approach leverages the
K-SVD algorithm of
[
8
]
, which aims to construct overcomplete dictionaries for sparse representations. This algorithm can be used to approximate
\min_{\mathbf{D,A}}\left\lVert\mathbf{W-DA}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

subject to each column of
\mathbf{A}
having
\ell_{0}
-norm at most
\ell
. We run the K-SVD algorithm on our
\mathbf{W}
, for small values of
k,n,\ell
. The resulting
\varepsilon
is computed as the worst-case approximation error among all
\mathbf{w}
, i.e.
\max_{\mathbf{w}}\left\lVert\mathbf{w-Da}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Then, using a block construction, we can generate an infinite family of protocols. Our results are shown in Figure
2
. Notice that the performance does not seem as good as the other constructions; this may be because the objective function of the K-SVD algorithm can be viewed as the average error over
\mathbf{w}
, while
\varepsilon
is determined by the worst case.

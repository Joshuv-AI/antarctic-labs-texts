Odd but Error-Free FastTwoSum: More General Conditions for FastTwoSum as an Error-Free Transformation for Faithful Rounding Modes
Sehyeok Park, Jay P. Lim, Santosh Nagarakatte

Abstract
. This paper proposes sufficient, yet more general conditions for applying FastTwoSum as an error-free transformation (EFT) under all faithful rounding modes. Additionally, it also identifies guarantees tailored to round-to-odd for establishing FastTwoSum as an EFT. This paper also describes a floating-point splitting tailored for round-to-odd that is an EFT where the distribution of bits is configurable ( i.e. , ExtractScalar for round-to-odd). Our sufficient conditions are much more general than those previously known in the literature ( i.e. , it applies to a wider operand domain).

Under select circumstances, the rounding error of a finite-precision,

2.2.
Basic Properties of Floating-Point

We present the basic floating-point properties most relevant to our theorems.
Henceforth, the notation
x\in y\mathbb{Z}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
expresses that
x
is an integer multiple of
y
. Every finite, non-zero
x\in\mathbb{F}
satisfies
\text{ufp}(x)\leq|x|<2\text{ufp}(x)
. Every finite
x\in\mathbb{F}
also satisfies
x\in\omega\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in\text{ulp}(x)\mathbb{Z}
. If
x\in 2^{k}\mathbb{Z}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
x\in 2^{i}\mathbb{Z}
for any

less than

. Because
2u\cdot\text{ufp}(x)
is an integer power of 2 and Definition
1

, it follows that
x\in 2u\cdot\text{ufp}(x)\mathbb{Z}

is an integer multiple of any smaller integer power of 2 (
e.g.
,
u\cdot\text{ufp}(x)
,
2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(x)
,
etc.
).

We assume throughout the remainder of the paper that the operands of FastTwoSum are finite (
i.e.
,
|a|,|b|\leq\Omega
). All finite
a,b\in\mathbb{F}
satisfy
a,b\in\text{min}(\text{ulp}(a),\text{ulp}(b))\mathbb{Z}
. Because addition and subtraction preserve common factors, the real sum
a+b
and its floating-point counterpart
\circ(a+b)
satisfy
a+b,\circ(a+b)\in\text{min}(\text{ulp}(a),\text{ulp}(b))\mathbb{Z}
for all
\circ\in\textsc{FR}
. Consequently, the resulting rounding error
\delta=a+b-\circ(a+b)
adheres to
\delta\in\text{min}(\text{ulp}(a),\text{ulp}(b))\mathbb{Z}
. Because
a,b\in\omega\mathbb{Z}
, it also follows that
a+b,\circ(a+b),\delta\in\omega\mathbb{Z}
. This property implies neither the sum of numbers in
\mathbb{F}
nor the associated rounding error is subject to underflow.

A number
r\in\mathbb{R}
is in
\mathbb{F}
if there exist
M_{r},E_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
r=M_{r}\times 2^{E_{r}-p+1}
,
|M_{r}|<2^{p}
, and

. We provide the conditions required to meet these constraints in the following lemma.

Lemma 0.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\sigma=2^{k}
. If
|r|\leq\text{min}(\sigma,\Omega)
and
r\in\text{max}(u\cdot\sigma,\omega)\mathbb{Z}
, then
r\in\mathbb{F}
.

If
a,b\in\mathbb{F}
, then
\delta=a+b-\circ(a+b)\in\omega\mathbb{Z}
for all
\circ\in\textsc{FR}
. Therefore,
\delta\in u\cdot 2^{k}\mathbb{Z}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
implies
\delta\in\text{max}(u\cdot 2^{k},\omega)\mathbb{Z}
.

Corollary 0.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{F}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\delta=a+b-\circ(a+b)
and
\sigma=2^{k}
. If
|\delta|\leq\text{min}(\sigma,\Omega)
and
\delta\in u\cdot\sigma\mathbb{Z}
, then
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
.

Lastly, we summarize the key properties of rounding. For all
r\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|r|\leq\Omega
, faithful rounding guarantees
|\circ(r)-r|\leq\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(r)
when
\circ\in\textsc{RN}
and
|\circ\!(r)-r|<\text{ulp}(r)

for all
\circ\in\textsc{FR}
and

Given inputs

Property 2.

\mathbb{F}

∎

|a+b|<2^{E_{min}+1}

Suppose

(iv) trivially holds

|a|\geq|b|

Case 1:
\bm{|b|\geq 2u\cdot\text{ufp}(a)}

|\delta|<4u\cdot\text{ufp}(a)

2u\cdot\text{ufp}(a)

\text{ufp}(b)\geq 2u\cdot\text{ufp}(a)

Case 2:
\bm{u\cdot\text{ufp}(a)\leq|b|<2u\cdot\text{ufp}(a)}

Corollary
5
.

As previously explained, the assumption
|b|\geq|a|
directly

|b|\geq|a|

∎

Conditions (iii) and (iv) each ensure

,

, and

as Corollary
2
is sufficient otherwise. Because

\bm{b\in 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(a)\mathbb{Z}}

.

(iv).

∎

Any operands that satisfy the conditions in Lemma

4.
EFT Conditions for Round-to-Odd

For any

, Definition
6
(see Section

has an even significand only if

. Assuming

, the definition

implies that the maximum magnitude finite elements in

have odd significands. For all finite

such that

is not an element of
\mathbb{F}
.
RO
would thus round such sums to a neighbor

for which

.
RO
effectively enforces

, thereby preventing overflow for all sums

\delta=a+b-\textsc{RO}(a+b)\in\mathbb{F}
based on
|a+b|

Lemma 0.

Let
p\geq 2
and
x=\textsc{RO}(a+b)
. If
|a+b|>\Omega
, then
\delta=a+b-x\in\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

If
|a+b|>\Omega
, then
|x|=\Omega
due to saturation. Without loss of generality, we assume
|a|\geq|b|
. The assumptions
|a+b|>\Omega
and
|a|\geq|b|
imply
a\cdot b>0
and
\text{ufp}(a)=2^{E_{max}}
as
|a+b|\leq\Omega
otherwise. Since
|a|\geq|b|
implies
\text{ufp}(a)\geq\text{ufp}(b)

. It then follows that
\delta\in 2u\cdot\text{ufp}(b)\mathbb{Z}
.

Since
\textsc{RO}\in\textsc{FR}
and
|x|,|a+b|>0
, it immediately follows that
x\cdot(a+b)>0
. Using
|x|=\Omega
and
x\cdot(a+b)>0
, we establish
|\delta|\leq|b|
via contradiction. Suppose
|\delta|=|a+b-x|>|b|
. Because

and
|a+b|>|x|

and
|\delta|=|a+b|-\Omega

|a+b|>\Omega+|b|
. However, the implicit bound
|a|\leq\Omega
would dictate that
|a+b|>|a|+|b|
, which contradicts triangle inequality. Therefore, we conclude
|\delta|\leq|b|
. We subsequently derive

,
\delta\in 2u\cdot\text{ufp}(b)\mathbb{Z}

|\delta|<2\text{ufp}(b)

.
∎

Lemma
1
signifies that the rounding error of

is in
\mathbb{F}
when

and the rounding mode enforces saturation. The bound

when

Since
\textsc{RO}\in\textsc{FR}
, the conditions presented in Section

directly apply to

4
provides the relevant conditions for determining
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
when
|a+b|\leq\Omega
. Combining Theorem
4
and Lemma

, we derive the following conditions for

that are independent of the magnitude of

.

Corollary 0.

Let
p\geq 2
and
x=\textsc{RO}(a+b)
. If both conditions

(i)
a\in 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(b)\mathbb{Z}

(ii)
b\in 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(a)\mathbb{Z}

are satisfied, then
\delta=a+b-x\in\mathbb{F}
.

For directed rounding modes, the rounding direction for a given

is either fixed (
e.g.

RD

(
e.g.

the parity of its neighbors’ integral significands

RO
’s dependence on the parity of elements in
\mathbb{F}
induces the following property.

Lemma 0.

Let
p\geq 2
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
, and
x=M_{x}\times 2^{E_{x}-p+1}\in\mathbb{F}
. If both conditions

(i)

is an odd integer

(ii)

are satisfied, then

.

Proof.

We assume
r\neq x
, as the lemma is trivially true otherwise. We decompose the proof into two cases:
r<x
and
r>x
. If
r<x
, Condition (ii) ensures
pred(x)<r<x
. Because no other numbers in
\mathbb{F}
exist between
pred(x)
and

,
pred(x)
and
x
are equivalent to
pred(r)
and
succ(r)
respectively. Given Condition (i),

immediately follows from Definition
6
. Similarly,

implies
x=pred(r)<r<succ(x)=succ(r)
. Condition (i) and Definition

also induce

for such cases.
∎

We apply Lemma
3
to identify the rounding direction for
RO

i.e.
,
\text{min}(|a|,|b|)<\text{ulp}(\text{max}(|a|,|b|))
). Under such circumstances, the larger magnitude operand could be a floating-point neighbor of

. It would then follow that

and
|x|=\text{max}(|a|,|b|)
satisfy Condition (ii). Based on these observations, we present a condition for
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
that leverages the parity of the operands.

Lemma 0.

Let
p\geq 2
,
a=M_{a}\times 2^{E_{a}-p+1}\in\mathbb{F}
,
b=M_{b}\times 2^{E_{b}-p+1}\in\mathbb{F}
, and
x=\textsc{RO}(a+b)
. If
M_{\text{max}(|a|,|b|)}
is an odd integer, then
\delta=a+b-x\in\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

If
|a+b|>\Omega
,
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
holds due to Lemma
1
. We thus only consider cases where
|a+b|\leq\Omega
. Without loss of generality, we assume
|a|\geq|b|
. Our assumptions thus imply
M_{\text{max}(|a|,|b|)}=M_{|a|}
and that
M_{a}
is an odd integer. We proceed with the proof using two cases:
|b|\geq\text{ulp}(a)
and
|b|<\text{ulp}(a)
.

Case 1:
\bm{|b|\geq\text{ulp}(a)}
.
Since
\text{ulp}(a)
is an integer power of 2,
b
satisfies
\text{ufp}(b)\geq\text{ulp}(a)
. Given
b\in u\cdot\text{ufp}(b)\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
b\in u\cdot\text{ulp}(a)\mathbb{Z}
. Because
\text{ulp}(a)\geq 2u\cdot\text{ufp}(a)
,
b\in u\cdot\text{ulp}(a)\mathbb{Z}
induces
b\in 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(a)\mathbb{Z}
. Similarly,
|a|\geq|b|
guarantees
a\in 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(b)\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore,
a
and
b
satisfy the conditions of Theorem
4
, which confirms
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
.

Case 2:
\bm{|b|<\text{ulp}(a)}
.
If
p\geq 2
, all integer powers of 2 in
\mathbb{F}
greater than
\omega
have even integral significands. The same applies to theses numbers’ negatives. If
M_{a}
is an odd integer, either
|a|\neq\text{ufp}(a)
or
|a|=\omega
must hold. Due to Definition
3
, the distance between
a
and its neighbors is thus
|pred(a)-a|=|succ(a)-a|=\text{ulp}(a)
. The assumption
|b|<\text{ulp}(a)
would then indicate
pred(a)<a+b<succ(a)
.
M_{a}
being an odd integer and
pred(a)<a+b<succ(a)
match the conditions of Lemma
3
, which affirms
x=a
. If
x=a
, then
\delta=a+b-x
is equivalent to
b
. It immediately follows that
\delta
is in
\mathbb{F}
.

∎

Provided that the operand with the larger magnitude has an odd significand, Lemma
4
does not restrict the magnitude of the other operand. As such, the exponent difference of operands that adhere to Lemma
4
could span the entire dynamic range of
\mathbb{F}
. The lemma thereby deviates from its counterparts in Section
2
(see Lemma
3
and Theorem
4
), which entail bounds on the operands’ relative magnitudes. Additionally, Lemma
4
does not restrict the sign of the smaller magnitude operand as required for directed rounding modes.

Lemmas
1
and
4
each leverage
RO
’s saturation and dependence on significand parity to ensure
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
. As a result, the lemmas provide guarantees for operands that may not satisfy the sufficient conditions for all faithful rounding modes. By applying Lemmas
1
and
4
in conjunction with exactness conditions for FastTwoSum’s second operation (
i.e.
,
z=\circ_{2}(x-a)
), we produce the following criteria that ensure FastTwoSum is an EFT under
RO
.

Theorem 5.

Let
p\geq 2
,
a=M_{a}\times 2^{E_{a}-p+1}\in\mathbb{F}
, and
b=M_{b}\times 2^{E_{b}-p+1}\in\mathbb{F}
. Let
x=\circ_{1}(a+b)
,
z=\circ_{2}(x-a)
, and
y=\circ_{3}(b-z)
. If all conditions

(i)
\circ_{1}=\textsc{RO}
and
\circ_{2},\circ_{3}\in\textsc{FR}

(ii)
a\in\text{ulp}(b)\mathbb{Z}

(iii)
M_{a}
is an odd integer

are satisfied, then
x+y=a+b
.

Proof.

Condition (ii) ensures
x-a\in\mathbb{F}
, which then guarantees
z=x-a
for
\circ_{1}=\textsc{RO}
and all
\circ_{2}\in\textsc{FR}
. It therefore suffices to prove
\delta=a+b-x\in\mathbb{F}
as it guarantees
y=a+b-x
for all
\circ_{3}\in\textsc{FR}
. We thus prove
\delta
is in
\mathbb{F}
for all operands that meet Conditions (ii) and (iii) by separately analyzing the cases
|a|\geq|b|
and
|a|<|b|
.

Case 1:
\bm{|a|\geq|b|}
.
If
|a|\geq|b|
, Condition (iii) implies
\text{max}(|a|,|b|)
has an odd significand. The operands thus satisfy the condition in Lemma
4
, which affirms
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
.

Case 2:
\bm{|a|<|b|}
.
The assumption
|a|<|b|
indicates
\text{ufp}(b)\geq\text{ufp}(a)
. From
\text{ufp}(b)\geq\text{ufp}(a)
, we derive
b\in 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(a)\mathbb{Z}
. Because
\text{ulp}(b)\geq 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(b)
, Condition (ii) induces
a\in 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(b)\mathbb{Z}
. The operands that satisfy both
|a|<|b|
and Condition (ii) thus meet the conditions in Corollary
2
, which confirms
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
.
∎

Theorem
5
enables for
RO
the use of FastTwoSum on operands such that an EFT is not guaranteed under directed rounding modes. For example, suppose
a=2^{p}+2
and
b=-2^{-p}
. In this example,
a
is an integer multiple of
\text{ulp}(b)=2^{1-2p}
and has an odd significand (
i.e.
,
a=2^{p}+2=(2^{p-1}+1)\times 2
). The operands therefore satisfy the conditions in Theorem
5
. However,
b
is not an integer multiple of
2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(a)=2^{1-p}
as required by Theorem
5
. If
\circ_{1}=\circ_{2}=\circ_{3}=\textsc{RZ}
,
x=\textsc{RZ}(a+b)=2^{p}
and
z=\textsc{RZ}(x-a)=-2
. As a result,
y=\textsc{RZ}(b-z)=2
, and
x+y\neq a+b
. Under
RO
, FastTwoSum produces
x=2^{p}+2
,
z=0
, and
y=-2^{-p}
. Hence, FastTwoSum under
RO
achieves
x+y=a+b
as guaranteed by Theorem
5
.

5.
Applications of Round to Odd FastTwoSum

A common application of FastTwoSum is floating-point splitting: an EFT that splits
x\in\mathbb{F}
across two numbers
x_{h},x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}
such that
x=x_{h}+x_{\ell}
. Dekker’s splitting
(
6
)
, for example, splits a
p
-precision number across two numbers that each have up to
\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor
effective bits of precision. In
(
19
)
, the authors present a more general splitting known as ExtractScalar, for which the distribution of bits is configurable.

Ex

\displaystyle\textbf{tractScalar}(\sigma,x):

\displaystyle s=\circ_{1}(\sigma+x)

\displaystyle x_{h}=\circ_{2}(s-\sigma)

\displaystyle x_{\ell}=\circ_{3}(x-x_{h})

\displaystyle\textbf{return }x_{h},x_{\ell}

ExtractScalar applies FastTwoSum on
x,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\sigma=2^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
(
i.e.
,
\sigma
is an integer power of 2). It is shown in
(
19
)
that if
|x|\leq 2^{k}
and
\circ_{1},\circ_{2},\circ_{3}\in\textsc{RN}
, then
x_{h}\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
and
x=x_{h}+x_{\ell}
. If
x_{h}\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
, the number of effective bits in
x_{h}
is determined by the exponent difference between
\sigma
and
x
. If
k=e_{x}+i
for some non-negative integer
i
(
i.e.
,
\sigma=2^{i}\text{ufp}(x)
), then
x_{h}
has at most
\text{max}(p-i,0)
effective bits. Hence, ExtractScalar can adjustably distribute bits across
x_{h}
and
x_{\ell}
by assigning an appropriate value of
\sigma
relative to
x
. Given a vector
v\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
, ExtractScalar can also produce an EFT for
all elements

v_{i}
if
\sigma
is configured relative to
\text{max}_{i}|v_{i}|
.

In
(
19
)
, ExtractScalar performs all operations under
RN
while assuming overflow does not occur. Furthermore, the assumptions
\sigma=2^{k}
and
|x|\leq 2^{k}
do not immediately satisfy the EFT conditions for FastTwoSum detailed in Section
3
. As such, ExtractScalar may not be an EFT when
\circ_{1}\not\in\textsc{RN}
. Specifically, an EFT is not guaranteed when the exponent difference between
\sigma
and
x
exceeds
2p-1
. Suppose
\circ_{1},\circ_{2},\circ_{3}=\textsc{RO}
and
x=2^{k-2p}
. In this example,
s=\textsc{RO}(\sigma+x)=2^{k}+2^{k-p+1}
and
x_{h}=\textsc{RO}(2^{k}+2^{k-p+1}-2^{k})=2^{k-p+1}
. Subsequently,
x_{\ell}=\textsc{RO}(2^{k-2p}-2^{k-p+1})=2^{k-2p+1}-2^{k-p+1}
and
x\neq x_{h}+x_{\ell}
. Similar examples can be constructed for directed rounding modes by assigning the appropriate signs to
x
(
e.g.
,
x=2^{k-2p}
for
RU
and
x=-2^{k-2p}
for
RD
and
RZ
). Given these limitations, we present new conditions that ensure ExtractScalar is an EFT under
RO
.

Theorem 1.

Let
p\geq 2
,
x,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. If all conditions

(i)
\circ_{1}=\textsc{RO}
and
\circ_{2},\circ_{3}\in\textsc{FR}

(ii)
\sigma=2^{k}+ulp(2^{k})
and
2^{k}\geq 2\omega

(iii)
|x|\leq 2^{k}

are satisfied, then
x_{h}\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
and
x=x_{h}+x_{\ell}
.

Proof.

Given
p\geq 2
and Condition (ii), we derive
\text{ufp}(\sigma)=2^{k}
and
\text{ulp}(\sigma)=\text{ulp}(2^{k})
. Through Conditions (ii) and (iii), we also derive
|x|\leq\text{ufp}(\sigma)<\sigma
. We first prove
x_{h}\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
. If
s,\sigma\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
, it must be the case that
x_{h}=\textsc{RO}(s-\sigma)\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
. As
\sigma\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
by default, it suffices to show
s\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
. We split our proof into three cases:
x\geq 0
,
-2^{k-1}\leq x<0
, and
x<-2^{k-1}
.

We further decompose
x\geq 0
into two cases:
\sigma+x>\Omega
and
\sigma+x\leq\Omega
. If
\sigma+x>\Omega
, then
s=\textsc{RO}(\sigma+x)=\Omega=(2^{p}-1)\times 2^{E_{max}-p+1}
due to
RO
’s saturation property. Because
|x|\leq\text{ufp}(\sigma)
,
\sigma+x>\Omega
implies
\text{ufp}(\sigma)=2^{E_{max}}
and
\text{ulp}(\sigma)=2^{E_{max}-p+1}
. It then follows that
\text{ulp}(s)=\text{ulp}(\sigma)
. Since
s\in\text{ulp}(s)\mathbb{Z}
and
\text{ulp}(s)=\text{ulp}(\sigma)>\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)
, we conclude
s\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
when
\sigma+x>\Omega
. If
\sigma+x\leq\Omega
,
\text{ufp}(s)=\text{ufp}(\sigma+x)
(
i.e.
,
e_{s}=e_{\sigma+x}
) for
s=\textsc{RO}(\sigma+x)
. Furthermore,
x\geq 0
indicates
\sigma+x\geq\sigma
and
\text{ufp}(\sigma+x)\geq\text{ufp}(\sigma)
. Given
\text{ufp}(s)=\text{ufp}(\sigma+x)\geq\text{ufp}(\sigma)
, Definition
1
ensures
\text{ulp}(s)=\text{ulp}(\sigma+x)\geq\text{ulp}(\sigma)>\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)
. As
s\in\text{ulp}(s)\mathbb{Z}
,
s
thus satisfies
s\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
when
x+\sigma\leq\Omega
.

If
-2^{k-1}\leq x<0
and
s=\textsc{RO}(\sigma+x)
, then
\sigma+x\geq 2^{k-1}+\text{ulp}(2^{k})
and
\text{ufp}(s)=\text{ufp}(\sigma+x)\geq 2^{k-1}=\frac{1}{2}\text{ufp}(\sigma)
. As such,
\text{ulp}(s)\geq\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)
and
s\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
. Lastly,
x<-2^{k-1}
implies
\text{ufp}(x)\geq 2^{k-1}=\frac{1}{2}\text{ufp}(\sigma)
and
\text{ulp}(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)
. Since
s=\textsc{RO}(\sigma+x)
and
\sigma+x,s\in\text{min}(\text{ulp}(\sigma),\text{ulp}(x))\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
s\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
. Hence,
x_{h}\in\frac{1}{2}\text{ulp}(\sigma)\mathbb{Z}
for all cases considered.

Due to Definition
3
,
\sigma=2^{k}+\text{ulp}(2^{k})
in Condition (ii) is equivalent to
succ(2^{k})
. All integer powers of 2 in
\mathbb{F}
greater than
\omega
have even significands. Consequently, any
\sigma
that satisfies Condition (ii) must have an odd significand. Because
|x|<\sigma
,
\sigma\in\text{ulp}(x)\mathbb{Z}
. All
\sigma,x\in\mathbb{F}
that satisfy these conditions thus meet the requirements of Theorem
5
. Through Theorem
5
, we derive
\sigma+x=s+x_{\ell}
. Because
\sigma\in\text{ulp}(x)\mathbb{Z}
ensures
x_{h}=s-\sigma
(see Section
2
),
\sigma+x=s+x_{\ell}
induces
x=s-\sigma+x_{\ell}=x_{h}+x_{\ell}
as was to be shown.
∎

If the requirements of Theorem
1
are met, ExtractScalar ensures error-free splitting under
RO
even when
\sigma
and
x
are non-overlapping. Given a
\sigma
that satisfies the conditions in Theorem
1
for
x=\text{max}_{i}|v_{i}|
in a vector
v\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
, ExtractScalar can thus produce an EFT for all elements
v_{i}
under
RO
without requiring a lower bound on
\text{min}_{i}|v_{i}|
.

6.
Related Work

In this section, we discuss prior work in the literature that have
also explored EFT guarantees for FastTwoSum under various faithful
rounding modes. Assuming
x-a\in\mathbb{F}
, Jeannerod
et al.
establish that
FastTwoSum is an EFT under all faithful rounding modes if the real sum

a+b
is exactly representable in twice the available
precision
(
12
)
. Jeannerod
et al.
propose
a\in\text{ulp}(b)\mathbb{Z}
and
e_{a}-e_{b}\leq p
as
sufficient conditions for this property. The conditions in
Theorem
4
and
5
also ensure
that
a+b
is exactly representable in
2p
-bits. Moreover, operands
that satisfy
e_{a}-e_{b}\leq p
must adhere to the condition
b\in 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(a)\mathbb{Z}
. Accordingly,
Theorem
5
ensures
x+y=a+b
for any operands
that meet the conditions
from
(
12
)
. Theorem
5

also guarantees EFTs for operands that would be excluded by the bound

e_{a}-e_{b}\leq p
(
e.g.
,
a=2^{p}
and
b=2^{-1}
). As such,
our conditions are applicable to a larger set of operands than those
addressed by previous works.

For operands that adhere to Theorem
4
, the
exponent difference could be as large as
2p-1
. Conditions with
similar implications appear in
(
14
)
.
In
(
14
)
, Linnainmaa presents conditions for

\delta\in\mathbb{F}
under
RZ
and
RO
using the notation
h_{y}
: the
exponent of the
least significant non-zero bit
of
y\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}
.
Alternatively,
h_{y}
is the
largest
integer
k

such that
y\in 2^{k}\mathbb{Z}
. Given this definition, all finite,
non-zero
y\in\mathbb{F}
satisfy
y\in 2^{h_{y}}\mathbb{Z}
and

\text{ulp}(y)\leq 2^{h_{y}}\leq\text{ufp}(y)
. Moreover, if
|y|=\text{ufp}(y)
(
i.e.
,

|y|
is an integer power of 2), then
h_{y}=e_{y}
. Linnainmaa
proves that if
x=\circ(a+b)
for
\circ\in\{\textsc{RZ},\textsc{RO}\}
,
e_{x}-h_{\text{min}(|a|,|b|)}<2p
guarantees
\delta=a+b-x\in\mathbb{F}
. If
\text{ulp}(a)>|b|
(
i.e.
,
|a|>|b|
and the operands’
significands do not overlap) and
a\times b>0
,
e_{x}=e_{a}
must hold
under
\circ\in\{\textsc{RZ},\textsc{RO}\}
due to Definitions
1
and

3
. The condition
e_{x}-h_{\text{min}(|a|,|b|)}<2p
for such cases is thus equivalent to
e_{a}-h_{|b|}<2p
. Since
h_{|b|}=h_{b}=e_{b}
when
|b|=\text{ufp}(b)
, the example
indicates that Linnainmaa’s condition can tolerate up to a
2p-1

difference in exponents.

While Linnainmaa’s condition provides comparable flexibility under

RZ
and
RO
, it lacks precision under
RD
and
RU
. For
example, consider operands
a=2^{p}-1
and
b=2^{-p}
. Given

|a|\geq|b|
and
2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(a)=2^{-p}
, the operands
satisfy
a\in 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(b)\mathbb{Z}
and
b\in 2u^{2}\cdot\text{ufp}(a)\mathbb{Z}
as required by Theorem
4
. If

\circ=\textsc{RU}
,
x=\circ(a+b)=2^{p}
and
\delta=2^{-p}-1\in\mathbb{F}
. In this example,
e_{x}=p
,
h_{\text{min}(|a|,|b|)}=-p
, and
e_{x}-h_{\text{min}(|a|,|b|)}=2p
. As such, the example
operands do not satisfy Linnainmaa’s condition despite
\delta
being
representable in
\mathbb{F}
. One can construct a similar example for

RD
by reversing the signs of
a
and
b
. Hence,
Theorem
4
is applicable to a broader range of
operands under
RD
and
RU
while addressing all faithful rounding
modes including
RZ
and
RO
.

Akin to Lemma
4
, Linnainmaa proposes

M_{\text{max}(|a|,|b|)}
having an odd significand as a sufficient
condition for
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
under
RO
. However,
Linnainmaa’s analysis assumes an unbounded model of
\mathbb{F}
and
does not address guarantees due to
RO
’s saturation property (see
Lemma
1
). Furthermore, Linnainmaa proposes
|a|\geq|b|
and
a
having an odd significand as EFT guarantees for
FastTwoSum under
RO
. Instead of
|a|\geq|b|
,
Theorem
5
enforces the less restrictive
a\in\text{ulp}(b)\mathbb{Z}
to ensure the second operation of FastTwoSum is
exact.

7.
Conclusion and Future Work

This paper identifies more general conditions than previously known in
literature that ensure FastTwoSum is an EFT for all faithful rounding
modes. Specifically, we identify new properties of operands that
ensure the rounding error
\delta=a+b-\circ(a+b)
is in

\mathbb{F}
. Our conditions enable EFTs for operands with large magnitude
differences for all faithful rounding modes (even when the operands’
exponent difference is nearly double the available precision). Hence,
our EFT guarantees for FastTwoSum are applicable to a
wide range of inputs, thereby offering improved precision over
previously established conditions.

This paper also presents EFT conditions tailored to
RO
. We leverage

RO
’s parity-based rounding behavior to identify sufficient
requirements for both
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
and
x+y=a+b
. We
observe that FastTwoSum can serve as an EFT under
RO
without
restricting the magnitude or the sign of the smaller operand, which
highlights
RO
’s versatility over directed rounding modes. By
analyzing a bounded floating-point model, we also examine
RO
’s
saturation property and its enabling of EFTs. We leverage these
findings to identify conditions for error-free floating-point
splittings under
RO
.

FastTwoSum-based splitting facilitates more sophisticated EFTs such as
integer rounding
(
11
)
, correctly rounded
summation
(
19
)

(
20
)
,
and accurate dot-products
(
16
)
. To leverage
EFT guarantees, most algorithms based on FastTwoSum require
RN
as
the default rounding mode. We expect our findings to aid the
development of advanced EFTs for other standard rounding modes as well
as emerging alternatives such as
RO
. Applying our conditions to
EFTs for floating-point multiplication is also of interest. In a
similar vein, extending our observations on rounding errors induced by
floating-point addition to fused multiply-add operations is a
potential future direction.

Polynomial Bridgeland Stability Conditions on the Category of Coherent Sheaves
Rémi Delloque

Abstract
In this short note, we give a wide class of examples of stability conditions on the category of coherent sheaves which generalise Gieseker stability. They are referred to as adapted to coherent sheaves and they admit Harder–Narasimhan and Jordan–Hölder filtrations. We also study the particular case of Bayer’s polynomial Bridgeland stability conditions, and their relation to the gauge theoretical counterpart introduced by Dervan–McCarthy–Sektnan.



1
Introduction

In
[
1
]
, Bayer introduced a generalisation of Bridgeland stability conditions known as
polynomial Bridgeland stability conditions
. Let
(X,[\omega])
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
. Bayer’s idea is to consider a central charge
Z

i.e.
a groups morphism

Z:K(X)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

of the following form

Z(\mathcal{E})=\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}[\omega]^{i}\right)\cup\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}U_{j}\right)\cup\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E})\right)^{(n,n)}.

Here, the
\rho_{i}
are complex numbers satisfying for all
i
,
\Im(\overline{\rho_{i}}\rho_{i-1})>0
. The
U_{j}
are complex cohomology classes of bidegree
(j,j)
,
U_{j}\in H^{j,j}(X,\mathbb{C})
with
U_{0}=1
. The superscript
(n,n)
means that we only keep the bidegree
(n,n)
part. We identify
H^{n,n}(X,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathbb{C}
thanks to the integration form.

Then, Bayer studies the
large volume regime
,
i.e.
what happens when the ample class
[\omega]
is replaced by
k[\omega]
for
k
a large integer.

Z_{k}(\mathcal{E})=\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}k^{i}[\omega]^{i}\right)\cup\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}U_{j}\right)\cup\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E})\right)^{(n,n)}.

It defines a groups morphism

K(X)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[k].

Then, there is a full abelian sub-category
\mathcal{A}\subset D^{b}(X)
of the the derived category of
X
such that for all object
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}
,
Z_{k}(\mathcal{E})
lies in the upper half plane when
k
is large enough.

Definition 1.1

(
[
1
, Proposition 1.2.1]
,
[
3
, Definition 2.7]
)
.

We say that
\mathcal{E}
is asymptotically

Z
-(semi-)stable if for all non-zero sub-object
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{E}
,

\Im(\overline{Z_{k}(\mathcal{E})}Z_{k}(\mathcal{F}))<(\leqslant)\,0.

Equivalently, the argument of
Z_{k}(\mathcal{E})
in
]0,\pi[
is eventually larger than the argument of
Z_{k}(\mathcal{F})
in
]0,\pi[
.

Bayer shows that this stability notion is well-behaved in the sense that there are Harder–Narasimhan filtrations for example. It generalises the Bridgeland stability conditions. See
[
1
, Theorem 3.2.2]
for more details about the construction of
\mathcal{A}
and the properties of this stability condition. In this note, we shall focus on the case where
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{Coh}(X)
is the category of coherent sheaves on
X
.

On the analytic side, Dervan–McCarthy–Sektnan showed a Kobayashi–Hitchin like correspondence which makes a link between asymptotic
Z
-stability and the existence of special metrics
[
3
]
. It generalises a result of Leung about Gieseker stability
[
7
]
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E,\overline{\partial})
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
. When
h
is a Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{E}
, we define its reduced curvature form

\hat{F}(h)=-\frac{1}{2\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\pi}\nabla\circ\nabla,

where
\nabla
is the Chern connection associated with
\overline{\partial}
and
h
. Let us consider the following operator associated to the polynomial central charge
Z
.

\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathcal{E},h)=\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}k^{i}\omega^{i}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{j}\right)\wedge\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\hat{F}(h)}\right)^{(n,n)}.

Here, the only restriction on
\rho
is that
\Im(\overline{\rho_{n}}\rho_{n-1})>0
and
\Im(\rho_{n})>0
(this second hypothesis is simply a normalisation condition and it can be dropped). The classes
U_{j}
are assumed to be real (this hypothesis is quite natural in this context but not necessary, see
[
3
, Remark 2.9]
). The
u_{j}
are real representatives of the classes
U_{j}
. By Chern–Weil theory,
\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathcal{E},h))
is closed and represents
Z_{k}(\mathcal{E})
. Once more, we are interested in the large volume limit
i.e.
when
k\rightarrow+\infty
.

Definition 1.2

(
[
3
, Definition 2.22]
)
.

A collection of Hermitian metrics
(h_{k})_{k\geqslant k_{0}}
is a solution to the asymptotic
Z
-critical equation if this family is bounded as tensors for the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
norm and for all
k
,

\Im(\overline{Z_{k}(\mathcal{E})}\mathcal{Z}_{k}(\mathcal{E},h_{k}))=0.

Dervan–McCarthy–Sektnan showed a Kobayashi–Hitchin like correspondence.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a simple holomorphic vector bundle. If
\mathcal{E}
is slope semi-stable (with respect to
[\omega]
) and sufficiently smooth (i.e. its graded object can be chosen locally free), then we have equivalence between

1.

\mathcal{E}
admits a solution to the asymptotic
Z
-critical equation.

2.

\mathcal{E}
is not destabilised by its sub-bundles in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

The assumption that
\mathcal{E}
is slope semi-stable is very natural because the leading order term of the polynomial
\Im(\overline{Z_{k}(\mathcal{E})}Z_{k}(\mathcal{F}))
is a positive multiple of the difference between the slope of
\mathcal{F}
and the slope of
\mathcal{E}
. The assumption that
\mathcal{E}
is sufficiently smooth is purely technical and is useful for the analysis.

It naturally raises the question of the asymptotic stability with respect to sub-sheaves (like for the Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence). In particular, can a bundle that admits asymptotically
Z
-critical metrics be destabilised by a sub-sheaf with a non locally free quotient ?

Example
3.4
below shows that the structure sheaf of a manifold of dimension greater than or equal to
3
is asymptotically destabilised by some ideal sub-sheaves when we consider the central charge of the deformed Hermitian Yang–Mills equation. Yet, its flat metric satisfies the deformed Hermitian Yang–Mills equation for any
k
.

In Section
2
, we construct very general stability conditions which avoid this pathological behaviour where a sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of dimension
d
is destabilised by a sub-sheaf
\mathcal{F}
whose quotient
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}
has a dimension smaller than
d
. They are the stability conditions
adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
(see Definition
2.2
). We then state classical results about these stability conditions like the existence and uniqueness of Harder–Narasimhan filtrations and the existence of Jordan–Hölder filtrations and the uniqueness of the graded object.

In Section
3
, we focus on the particular case of stability conditions coming from a central charge in the large volume limit. We show that being adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
only depends on the stability vector
\rho
.

We also study the example of the Gieseker stability. We show that it arises from a central charge in the large volume limit and we even build a central charge
Z
and an associated operator
\mathcal{Z}
such that the asymptotic
Z
-critical equation is Leung’s almost Hermitian Einstein equation. It shows that Leung’s correspondence
[
7
]
is a particular case of the Dervan–McCarthy–Sektnan correspondence
[
3
]
.

2
General
\mu
-stability

2.1
Definitions and basic properties

We study here stability with respect to a large class of stability conditions on the category of coherent sheaves on a smooth projective variety. In all this note, sheaves are assumed to be coherent.

Let
(X,[\omega])
be a smooth projective variety. Let
(V,\leqslant)
be a totally ordered
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space and

\mathrm{Deg}:K(X)\longrightarrow V,

a groups morphism. Such a morphism will play the role of a generalisation of the degree in the context of slope stability and of Hilbert polynomials in the context of Gieseker stability. In the case of Hilbert polynomials,
V=\mathbb{R}[k]
with
A\leqslant B
if
A(k)\leqslant B(k)
for
k
large enough.

When
\mathcal{E}
is a non-zero sheaf of dimension
d
(the dimension of
\mathcal{E}
is the dimension of its support), we also introduce

\mu(\mathcal{E})=\frac{\mathrm{Deg}(\mathcal{E})}{\mathrm{Rk}(\mathcal{E})}\in V,

where

\mathrm{Rk}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{ch}_{d}(\mathcal{E})\cup[\omega]^{n-d}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

is the generalised rank of
\mathcal{E}
. It is always a positive integer. Indeed, let us decompose

\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{E})=\bigcup_{i\in I}V_{i}\cup W,

where
\dim(W)<d
and the
V_{i}
are the irreducible components of
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{E})
of dimension
d
. By assumption,
I\neq\emptyset
and for all
i\in I
,
\mathcal{E}_{|V_{i}}
has a positive rank. Let
\iota:\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow X
be the inclusion. We have, by the Grotendieck–Riemann–Roch formula,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ch}(\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ch}(\iota_{!}\iota^{*}\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\iota_{*}(\mathrm{ch}(\iota^{*}\mathcal{E})\mathrm{Td}(T_{\iota}))

\displaystyle=\iota_{*}\left(\left(\sum_{i\in I}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}_{|V_{i}})[V_{i}]+\textrm{ h.d.t.}\right)(1+\textrm{ h.d.t.})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}_{|V_{i}})[V_{i}]+\textrm{ h.d.t.},

where ”h.d.t.” stands for ”higher degree terms”. We deduce that

\mathrm{Rk}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i\in I}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}_{|V_{i}})\int_{V_{i}}\omega^{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{*},

because
\omega
is an integral class.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a sheaf
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu
-semi-stable
if for all strict sub-sheaves
0\subsetneq\mathcal{F}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}
,

\mu(\mathcal{F})\leqslant\mu(\mathcal{E}).

(1)

We say that
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu
-stable
if the inequality (
1
) is strong. We say that
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu
-polystable
if it is
\mu
-semi-stable and when (
1
) is an equality, then
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}
.

The purpose of this section is to study the case where the
\mu
-stability satisfies the same properties as Gieseker stability (existence and uniqueness of Harder–Narasimhan filtrations, existence of Jordan–Hölder filtrations and uniqueness of the graded object in the semi-stable case, etc.). In the next sub-sections, we study more particularly the case of polynomials given by a central charge in the large volume limit. In this case, we give a simple characterisation of the central charges satisfying this property.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
\mu
is
adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
if for all sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of pure dimension
d
and all
\mathcal{F}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}
such that
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}
has dimension less than
d
,

\mu(\mathcal{F})<\mu(\mathcal{E}).

We say that
\mu
is
adapted to coherent sheaves
if it is adapted to sheaves of all dimensions. We say that
\mu
is
adapted to torsion-free sheaves
if it is adapted to sheaves of dimension
n
.

Example 2.3
.

We give here a wide class of examples of stability conditions
\mu
adapted to coherent sheaves in
V=\mathbb{R}[\epsilon]
endowed with the order
A\leqslant B
if for all
\epsilon>0
small enough,
A(\epsilon)\leqslant B(\epsilon)
. When
\mathcal{E}
is a sheaf, we set

\mathrm{ch}_{\leqslant k}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\mathrm{ch}_{i}(\mathcal{E}).

Let
(d_{k})_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}
be positive integers and
(\Gamma_{k,j})_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n,0\leqslant j\leqslant d_{k}-1}
be a collection of Hodge classes

\Gamma_{k,j}\in H^{*,*}(X,\mathbb{R})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}H^{i,i}(X,\mathbb{C})\cap H^{2i}(X,\mathbb{R}).

The stability condition given by

\mathrm{Deg}(\mathcal{E})=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\Gamma_{k,0}\cup\mathrm{ch}_{\leqslant k}(\mathcal{E})+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}-1}\Gamma_{k,j}\cup\mathrm{ch}_{\leqslant k}(\mathcal{E})\epsilon^{j}\right)\epsilon^{d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k-1}}\right)^{(n,n)}\in\mathbb{R}[\epsilon],

is adapted to coherent sheaves as long as for all
k
and all sub-varieties
V
of
X
of codimension
k
,
(\Gamma_{0,k}\cup[V])^{(n,n)}>0
.

Example 2.4
.

A particular case of Example
2.3
is the stability condition built in
[
8
]
by Mégy–Pavel–Toma. They consider the map

P_{\alpha}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{1}{i!}\left(\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E})\cup\alpha_{i}\cup\mathrm{Td}(X)m^{i}\right)^{(n,n)}\in\mathbb{R}[m].

where each
\alpha_{i}
is an ample
i
-class. Here, the order on
\mathbb{R}[m]
is defined with respect to the asymptotic behaviour at infinity.
A\leqslant B
if for all
m\geqslant 0
large enough,
A(m)\leqslant B(m)
. We can recover the convention of the asymptotic behaviour at
0^{+}
by replacing
m
by
\epsilon^{-1}
and by multiplying the expression by a suitable power of
\epsilon
. We could also use the lexicographic order on the coefficients of the polynomials.

Remark
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is of pure dimension
d
and has an irreducible support, then
\mathrm{Rk}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}_{|\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{E})})\int_{\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{E})}\omega^{d}
. In particular, the
\mu
-(semi-)(poly)stability of
\mathcal{E}
is independent of the choice of the polarisation
[\omega]
.

If however
\mathcal{E}
has a reducible support
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{E})=V\cup W
, then
\mathcal{E}=\iota_{V*}\mathcal{E}_{|V}\oplus\iota_{W*}\mathcal{E}_{|W}
where
\iota_{V}:V\rightarrow X
and
\iota_{W}:W\rightarrow X
are the injections. Thus,
\mathcal{E}
can not be
\mu
-stable and its
\mu
-(semi-)(poly)stability may depend of the choice of
[\omega]
.

Remark
.

Unless
X
is a curve, the usual slope stability is not adapted to torsion-free sheaves. Indeed, assume that
\mathrm{Deg}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{ch}_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cup[\omega]^{n-1}
is the usual degree with respect to a given polarisation and
\mathcal{E}
is torsion-free. For any sub-variety
V\subset X
of codimension greater than or equal to
2
, set
\mathcal{I}_{V}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
its ideal sheaf. Then,
\mathcal{I}_{V}\otimes\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{E}
has the same rank and the same degree as
\mathcal{E}
.

Remark
.

The right category to work with for slope stability is
\mathrm{Coh}_{n,n-1}(X)
, the category of coherent sheaves modulo coherent sheaves of codimension at least
2
. See
[
4
, Section 1.6]
for reference. In the case where the inequality in Definition
2.2
is large instead on strict, we can probably find similarly a good quotient category to work with.

The following result is the generalisation of a standard result concerning Gieseker stability.

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume that
\mu
is adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
and let
\mathcal{E}
be of pure dimension
d
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu
-(semi-)stable if and only if it is with respect to its saturated sub-sheaves (i.e. those whose quotients are of pure dimension
d
).

Proof.

The ”only if” part is trivial. Assume
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu
-(semi-)stable with respect to saturated sub-sheaves and consider a sub-sheaf
0\subsetneq\mathcal{F}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}
which is not saturated. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be its saturation in
\mathcal{E}
so
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}
and
\dim(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}/\mathcal{F})<d
.

Since
\mu
is adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
,
\mu(\mathcal{F})<\mu(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\leqslant\mu(\mathcal{E})
. We deduce that
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu
-(semi-)stable.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

If
\mu
is adapted to torsion-free sheaves, all rank
1
torsion-free sheaves (in particular, line bundles) are
\mu
-stable.

2.2
Harder–Narasimhan and Jordan–Hölder filtrations

For Gieseker stability, we can construct Harder–Narasimhan (HN) filtrations and Jordan–Hölder (JH) filtrations of semi-stable sheaves. If
\mu
is adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
, we can build HN filtrations of sheaves of pure dimension
d
and JH filtrations of
\mu
-semi-stable sheaves of pure dimension
d
similarly. The proofs are the exact same as in
[
4
, Sections 1.3 and 1.5]
so we don’t write them again. We simply state here the analogue of the most important results that leads to the construction of these filtrations.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
4
, Lemma 1.3.5]
)
.

Assume that
\mu
is adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
and let
\mathcal{E}
be of pure dimension
d
. Then, there is a unique sub-sheaf
0\subsetneq\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
of
\mathcal{E}
such that for all sub-sheaves
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{E}
,

\mu(\mathcal{G})\leqslant\mu(\mathcal{F})

and in case of equality,
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{F}
. In particular,
\mathcal{F}
is
\mu
-semi-stable.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
4
, Proposition 1.2.7]
)
.

If
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
are
\mu
-semi-stable sheaves of pure dimension
d
and
\mu(\mathcal{E})>\mu(\mathcal{F})
, then
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=0
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu
-stable and
\mathcal{F}
is
\mu
-semi-stable both of pure dimension
d
and
\mu(\mathcal{E})=\mu(\mathcal{F})
, then any non-zero morphism
\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
is an isomorphism.

Theorem 2.9

(
[
4
, Theorem 1.3.4]
)
.

Assume that
\mu
is adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
and let
\mathcal{E}
be a sheaf of pure dimension
d
. There is a unique filtration

0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{l}=\mathcal{E}

such that for all
i
,
\mathcal{G}_{i}=\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i-1}
has pure dimension
d
and is
\mu
-semi-stable. Moreover, we have,

\mu(\mathcal{G}_{1})>\cdots>\mu(\mathcal{G}_{l}).

We call this filtration the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{E}
.
\mathcal{E}_{1}
is the sheaf given by Lemma
2.7
.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
4
, Proposition 1.5.2]
)
.

Assume that
\mu
is adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
and let
\mathcal{E}
be a
\mu
-semi-stable sheaf of pure dimension
d
. There is a filtration

0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{l}=\mathcal{E}

such that for all
i
,
\mathcal{G}_{i}=\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i-1}
has pure dimension
d
, is
\mu
-stable and
\mu(\mathcal{G}_{i})=\mu(\mathcal{E})
. Moreover, the sheaf

\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{E})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{G}_{i},

is
\mu
-polystable and is uniquely determined by
\mathcal{E}
.

We call such a filtration a Jordan–Hölder filtration of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{E})
its graded object.

3
The case of polynomial central charges

In this section, we study the specific case of
\mu
-stability built from central charges as originally introduced by Bayer
[
1
]
and for which Dervan–McCarthy–Sektnan found a Kobayashi–Hitchin like correspondence
[
3
]
. In particular, we show that under suitable conditions on the stability vector
\rho
, they arise from
\mu
-stability conditions adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
.

We use here the convention of the asymptotic development at
0
instead of the one at infinity.

Let us consider a central charge,

Z(\mathcal{E})=\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}[\omega]^{i}\right)\cup U\cup\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E})\right)^{(n,n)},

Here, the only assumption we make on the
\rho_{i}
is that
\Im(\rho_{n})>0
and
\Im(\overline{\rho_{n}}\rho_{n-1})>0
. In this case, we call
\rho
a
stability vector
. We always assume that
U_{0}=1
. We also define their polynomial counterparts, obtained by replacing
\omega
with
\epsilon^{-1}\omega
for small positive real numbers
\epsilon
and normalising by
\epsilon^{n}
,

Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})=\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}\epsilon^{n-i}[\omega]^{i}\right)\cup U\cup\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E})\right)^{(n,n)},

Notice that,

Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})=\rho_{n}[\omega^{n}]+\mathrm{O}(\epsilon).

Therefore, by assumption on
\rho_{n}
, for all
\epsilon>0
small enough,
\Im(Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E}))>0
. Let
\phi_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})\in]0,1[
such that
Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})=\left|Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})\right|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\pi\phi_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})}
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
3
, Lemma 2.8]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
be a sub-sheaf. We have equivalence between

1.

For
\epsilon>0
small enough,
\phi_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{F})<(\leqslant)\,\phi_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})
.

2.

For
\epsilon>0
small enough,
\Im(\overline{Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})}Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{F}))<(\leqslant)\,0
.

3.

For
\epsilon>0
small enough,
-\frac{\mathrm{Re}(Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{F}))}{\Im(Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{F}))}<(\leqslant)\,-\frac{\mathrm{Re}(Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E}))}{\Im(Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E}))}
.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that a sub-sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}
(strictly) destabilises
\mathcal{E}
asymptotically if one of the equivalent conditions of Lemma
3.1
is fulfilled. We say that
\mathcal{E}
is
asymptotically
Z
-(semi-)stable
if it is (strictly) destabilised by no non-zero sub-sheaves.

It is not trivial that asymptotic
Z
-stability is equivalent to a
\mu
-stability defined in Definition
2.1
. We prove it in the next sections.

3.1
Twisted Chern character and degrees

Let us define the twisted Chern character of an element
a\in K(X)
as the multi-degree class,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}^{U}(a)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ch}(a)\cup U

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underset{i+j\leqslant n}{i,j=0}}^{n}\mathrm{ch}_{i}(a)\cup U_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{p}\mathrm{ch}_{p-j}(a)\cup U_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{n}\left(\mathrm{ch}_{p}(a)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mathrm{ch}_{p-j}(a)\cup U_{j}\right).

We see that for all
p
, the
(p,p)
part of
\mathrm{ch}^{U}(a)
is

\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{U}(a)=\mathrm{ch}_{p}(a)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mathrm{ch}_{p-j}(a)\cup U_{j}.

(2)

Introducing the twisted Chern class enables us to express in shorter way the central charge of
a\in K(X)
,

Z_{\epsilon}(a)=\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}\epsilon^{n-i}[\omega]^{i}\right)\cup\mathrm{ch}^{U}(a)\right)^{(n,n)}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}\mathrm{ch}_{n-i}^{U}(a)\cup[\omega]^{i}\epsilon^{n-i}.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a sheaf of codimension
c
. For all
0\leqslant i\leqslant c-1
,
\mathrm{ch}_{i}^{U}(\mathcal{E})=0
and
\mathrm{ch}_{c}^{U}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{ch}_{c}(\mathcal{E})>0
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in the beginning of Sub-section
2.1
. As a consequence of the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch formula, we have
\mathrm{ch}_{i}^{U}(\mathcal{E})=0
if
i<c
and

\mathrm{ch}_{c}^{U}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{ch}_{c}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{V\subset\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{E})}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}_{|V})[V]

where the sum ranges on the irreducible components of
\mathcal{E}
of codimension
c
.
∎

3.2
Adapted stability vectors

The conditions we require on the stability vector (
\Im(\rho_{n})>0
and
\Im(\overline{\rho_{n}}\rho_{n-1})>0
) are quite weak. They are enough to obtain the Kobayashi–Hitchin like correspondence for semi-stable sufficiently smooth vector bundles shown by Dervan–McCarthy–Sektnan
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
. It is due to the fact that the only destabilising sub-sheaves (in the sense of the slope) are locally free so we don’t have to bother about singularities. However, on the algebraic point of view, it can lead to pathological behaviour like a torsion-free sheaf destabilised by a sub-sheaf whose quotient is a torsion sheaf.

Example 3.4
.

If
X
has dimension
n\geqslant 3
,
V\subset X
is an irreducible sub-variety of codimension
3
with ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{V}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
and

Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})=\epsilon^{n}(-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon^{-1}[\omega]}\cup\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E}))^{(n,n)}

is the central charge of the dHYM equation, then one can compute that,

\Im(\overline{Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{O}_{X})Z_{\epsilon}(i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V})})\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\sim}\frac{1}{n!(n-3)!}\epsilon^{3}[\omega]^{n}([\omega]^{n-m}\cup[V])>0.

Therefore, if asymptotic
Z
-stability for this central charge can be related to a
\mu
-stability condition, this
\mu
-stability condition won’t be adapted to torsion-free sheaves. Notice that it builds a counter-example to
[
3
, Conjecture 1.6]
even in the large volume regime because the flat metric of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a solution to the dHYM equation.

In order to avoid this pathological behaviour and be able to build HN filtrations for example, we need additional assumptions on the
\rho_{i}
.

Definition 3.5
.

When
\rho=(\rho_{0},\ldots,\rho_{n})
is a stability vector such that for all
i
,
\Im(\overline{\rho_{i}}\rho_{i-1})>0
(in particular, all the
\rho_{i}
are non-zero), we say that it is a
Bayer stability vector

[
1
, Definition 3.2.1]
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
0\leqslant d\leqslant n
. When
\rho=(\rho_{0},\ldots,\rho_{n})
is a stability vector such that for all
i<d
,
\Im(\overline{\rho_{d}}\rho_{i})>0
, we say that
\rho
is
adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
. If
d=n
, we say that it is
adapted to torsion-free sheaves
. If it holds for all
d
, we say that
\rho
is
adapted to coherent sheaves
.

Of course, stability vectors adapted to coherent sheaves are Bayer and adapted to torsion-free sheaves. The reciprocal is true.

Lemma 3.7
.

A stability vector
\rho
is adapted to coherent sheaves if and only if it is Bayer and adapted to torsion-free sheaves.

Proof.

Assume
\rho
is Bayer and adapted to torsion-free sheaves. Let
0\leqslant i<j\leqslant n-1
.
\Im(\overline{\rho_{n}}\rho_{j})>0
so
(\rho_{n},\rho_{j})
is an
\mathbb{R}
-base of
\mathbb{C}
. Let us write
\rho_{j+1}=a\rho_{j}+b\rho_{n}
.
\Im(\overline{\rho_{n}}\rho_{j+1})>0
so
a\Im(\overline{\rho_{n}}\rho_{j})>0
. Since we also have
\Im(\overline{\rho_{n}}\rho_{j})>0
,
a>0
. Similarly,
b>0
. Therefore,

\Im(\overline{\rho_{j+1}}\rho_{i})=a\Im(\overline{\rho_{j}}\rho_{i})+b\Im(\overline{\rho_{n}}\rho_{i})>a\Im(\overline{\rho_{j}}\rho_{i}).

By induction on
j\geqslant i+1
,
\Im(\overline{\rho_{j}}\rho_{i})>0
for all
i<j
so
\rho
is adapted to coherent sheaves.
∎

3.3
Characterisation of asymptotic
Z
-stability when the stability vector is adapted

Definition 3.8
.

We define the
generalised degrees
and
generalised slopes
of a sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of pure codimension
c
as follows.

\deg_{i}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{ch}_{i}^{U}(a)\cup[\omega]^{n-i},\qquad\mu_{i}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}+\infty&\textrm{if }i<c,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\deg_{i}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})}{\mathrm{Rk}(\mathcal{E})}&\textrm{else}.\end{array}\right.

We also define the
slopes vector
of
\mathcal{E}
as
\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})=(\mu_{i}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E}))_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}\in(\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\})^{n+1}
. Notice that by Proposition
3.3
,

\deg_{c}^{U}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{Rk}(\mathcal{E}).

In particular, slopes are well-defined and
\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})=(+\infty,\ldots,+\infty,1,*,\ldots,*)
where the
1
is at position
c
.

We can rewrite the central charge in function of these degrees,

Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{n-i}\deg_{i}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\epsilon^{i}.

(3)

Let
\leqslant_{\mathrm{lex}}
be the lexicographic order of
(\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\})^{n+1}
where the first coefficient is the one with highest weight. It is a total order.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a sheaf of pure dimension
d
and
\mathcal{F}
a sub-sheaf. Assume that
\rho
is adapted to sheaves of dimension
d
. Then,

1.

\mathcal{F}
destabilises
\mathcal{E}
strictly if and only if
\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})>_{\mathrm{lex}}\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})
,

2.

\mathcal{F}
destabilises
\mathcal{E}
if and only if
\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})\geqslant_{\mathrm{lex}}\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})
,

3.

\mathcal{F}
doesn’t destabilise
\mathcal{E}
if and only if
\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})<_{\mathrm{lex}}\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})
.

Proof.

We have
\dim(\mathcal{F})\leqslant\dim(\mathcal{E})=d
. Let us start with the case where
\dim(\mathcal{F})<\dim(\mathcal{E})
. In this case,
\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})>_{\mathrm{lex}}\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})
thus we must show that
\mathcal{F}
destabilises
\mathcal{E}
strictly. Indeed, by Proposition
3.3
and (
3
),

Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{n-i}\deg_{i}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\epsilon^{i}=\rho_{\dim(\mathcal{E})}\deg_{\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{E})}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\epsilon^{\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{E})}+\mathrm{O}(\epsilon^{\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{E})+1}),

and a similar formula holds for
\mathcal{F}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Im(\overline{Z_{\epsilon}(E)}Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{F}))

\displaystyle=\Im(\overline{\rho_{\dim(\mathcal{E})}}\rho_{\dim(\mathcal{F})})\deg_{\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{E})}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{F})}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})\epsilon^{\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{E})+\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{F})}

\displaystyle+\mathrm{O}(\epsilon^{\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{E})+\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{F})+1}).

Using the fact that the bottom degrees
\deg_{\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{E})}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{Rk}(\mathcal{E})
and
\deg_{\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{F})}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{Rk}(\mathcal{F})
are positive and that
\Im(\overline{\rho_{\dim(\mathcal{E})}}\rho_{\dim(\mathcal{F})})>0
by assumption on the
\rho_{i}
and the fact that
\dim(\mathcal{E})>\dim(\mathcal{F})
, we deduce that this quantity is positive for
\epsilon
small thus
\mathcal{F}
strictly destabilises
\mathcal{E}
.

Assume now that
\dim(\mathcal{F})=\dim(\mathcal{E})
and set
c=\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{E})
. Recall that
\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-c}}\rho_{i})>0
when
i<c
. For simplicity, we use the convention that
\rho_{i}=\deg_{i}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})=\deg_{i}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})=0
if
i<0
or
i>n
.

\displaystyle\Im(\overline{Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})}Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{F}))

\displaystyle=\Im\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\overline{\rho_{n-i}}\deg_{i}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\epsilon^{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\rho_{n-j}\deg_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})\epsilon^{j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{2n}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-p+j}}\rho_{n-j})\underbrace{\deg_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})}_{=0\textrm{ if }j>p-c}\ \underbrace{\deg_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})}_{=0\textrm{ if }j<c}\epsilon^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=2c}^{2n}\sum_{j=c}^{p-c}\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-p+j}}\rho_{n-j})\deg_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})\epsilon^{p}.

At
p=2c
, the only value that takes
j
is
c
thus
\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-p+j}}\rho_{n-j})=\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-c}}\rho_{n-c})=0
. We deduce that the coefficient at
\epsilon^{2c}
vanishes. More generally, if
p
is even and
j=\frac{p}{2}
,
\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-p+j}}\rho_{n-j})=0
. When
p>c
,
j
takes at least two values and the coefficient
a_{p}
at order
p
of the polynomial
\epsilon\mapsto\Im(\overline{Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})}Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{F}))
is,

\displaystyle a_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=c}^{p-c}\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-p+j}}\rho_{n-j})\deg_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=c}^{\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor}\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-p+j}}\rho_{n-j})\deg_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})+\sum_{j=\left\lceil\frac{p}{2}\right\rceil}^{p-c}\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-p+j}}\rho_{n-j})\deg_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=c}^{\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor}\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-j}}\rho_{n-p+j})\deg_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})+\sum_{j=c}^{\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor}\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-j}}\rho_{n-p+j})\deg_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=c}^{\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor}\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-j}}\rho_{n-p+j})(\deg_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})-\deg_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=c}^{\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor}\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-j}}\rho_{n-p+j})\deg_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})(\mu_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\mu_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})-\mu_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\mu_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})).

(4)

Let
c\leqslant c^{\prime}\leqslant n
the largest integer such that
\mu_{c^{\prime}}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})=\mu_{c^{\prime}}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})
. It is well-defined because
\mu_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})=\mu_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})=1
. If
c^{\prime}=n
, then
\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})=\mu^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})
and by Equation (
4
),
\Im(\overline{Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})}Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{F}))=0
so
\mathcal{F}
destabilises
\mathcal{E}
but not strictly.

Assume now that
c^{\prime}<n
. Using Equation (
4
), when
p\leqslant c^{\prime}+c
, all terms of the form
\mu_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\mu_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})-\mu_{p-j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\mu_{j}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})
vanish when

. When

, all such term vanish too expect when

. We deduce that,

\forall p\leqslant c^{\prime}+c,a_{p}=0,

and,

\displaystyle a_{c^{\prime}+c+1}

\displaystyle=\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-c}}\rho_{n-c^{\prime}-1})\deg_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})(\mu_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\mu_{c^{\prime}+1}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})-\mu_{c^{\prime}+1}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\mu_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F}))

\displaystyle=\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-c}}\rho_{n-c^{\prime}-1})\deg_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})(\mu_{c^{\prime}+1}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})-\mu_{c^{\prime}+1}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})).

We obtain the wanted equivalence because
\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-c}}\rho_{n-c^{\prime}-1})\deg_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\deg_{c}^{U,[\omega]}(\mathcal{F})>0
.
∎

3.4
An example : Gieseker stability

We show here that Gieseker stability is a particular case of asymptotic
Z
-stability. In
[
6
, Section 4.2]
, Gieseker stability is realised as an asymptotic
Z
-stability but with a stability vector
\rho
depending on the parameter
k=\epsilon^{-1}
. We show here that we can choose
\rho
independent of this parameter.

Let
U=\mathrm{Td}(X)
,
\rho
any stability vector adapted to torsion-free sheaves and
\omega\in c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
be any Kähler form with
\mathcal{L}
an ample bundle. Then, for all torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
and all integer

, by the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch formula,

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\mathrm{Td}(X)\cup\mathrm{ch}(E\otimes\mathcal{L}^{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathrm{Td}(X)}(\mathcal{E})\cup\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{k[\omega]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathrm{ch}_{i}^{\mathrm{Td}(X)}(\mathcal{E})\cup\frac{[\omega]^{i}}{i!}k^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\deg_{i}^{\mathrm{Td}(X),[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})}{i!}k^{i}.

By Theorem
3.9
, we easily see that
\mathcal{E}
is Gieseker stable if and only if it is asymptotically
Z
-stable for the central charge
Z
associated with
([\omega],\rho,\mathrm{Td}(X))
.

Remark
.

By choosing
\rho
adapted to coherent sheaves, we obtain a natural way to extend Gieseker stability to any coherent sheaf. Gieseker stability so extended to all sheaves is sometimes referred to as Simpson stability
[
10
,
9
]
. In
[
1
, 2.1]
, Bayer notices that this stability condition can be expressed as a polynomial central charge. The above proposition shows that it can actually be expressed as the particular kind of central charge given by
[
1
, Theorem 3.2.2]
by choosing the stability vector adapted to coherent sheaves.

We show in the next example that with the right choice of
\rho
, we can recover Leung’s almost Hermitian Einstein equation
[

]
.

Example 3.13
.

If we set
\rho_{n}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}
, for all

-critical equation is Leung’s almost Hermitian-Einstein equation

]

Proof.

Recall that
\hat{F}(h)
is the reduced curvature form of

\displaystyle\Im(\overline{Z_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})}\mathcal{Z}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E},h))

\displaystyle=\Im\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\overline{\rho_{n-i}}\deg_{i}^{\mathrm{Td}(X),[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\epsilon^{i}\right)\left(\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\rho_{n-j}\epsilon^{j}\omega^{n-j}\right)\wedge\mathrm{td}(X,\omega)\wedge\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\hat{F}(h)}\right)\right)^{(n,n)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\underbrace{\Im(\overline{\rho_{n-i}}\rho_{n-j})}_{=0\textrm{ if }ij\neq 0}\deg_{i}^{\mathrm{Td}(X),[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\omega^{n-j}\wedge\mathrm{td}(X,\omega)\wedge\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\hat{F}(h)}\epsilon^{i+j}\right)^{(n,n)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(n-i)!}\deg_{i}^{\mathrm{Td}(X),[\omega]}(\mathcal{E})\omega^{n}\wedge\mathrm{td}(X,\omega)\wedge\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\hat{F}(h)}\epsilon^{i}\Biggl)^{(n,n)}

Therefore,

Let
V
be an intersection of
p\geqslant 1
generic hyperplanes of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
X
in
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
. By Bertini’s theorem,
V
is smooth and has pure codimension
p
in
X
. By Proposition
3.3
,
\deg_{k}^{U,[\omega]}(i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V})=0
if
k<p
and
\deg_{p}^{U,[\omega]}(i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V})>0
. Therefore,

\lambda Z_{\epsilon}(i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V})=\lambda\sum_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{n-i}\deg_{i}^{U,[\omega]}(i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V})\epsilon^{i}=\lambda\rho_{n-p}\deg_{p}^{U,[\omega]}(i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V})\epsilon^{p}+\mathrm{O}(\epsilon^{p+1}).

By assumption, this quantity belongs to
\mathbb{H}
for
\epsilon
small enough so
\lambda\rho_{n-p}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}
for all
p
. We deduce that for all
i
,
\rho_{i}=\left|\rho_{i}\right|\overline{\lambda}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{i}}\pi\theta_{i}}
for some
0\leqslant\theta_{i}\leqslant 1
. The inequality
\Im(\overline{\rho_{i}}\rho_{i-1})>0
which holds by definition of Bayer stability vectors implies that
\left|\rho_{i}\right|>0
and
\theta_{i}<\theta_{i-1}
. Thus, when
i<j
,
\theta_{j}<\theta_{i}
meaning that
\Im(\overline{\rho_{j}}\rho_{i})>0
except maybe if
\theta_{j}=0
and
\theta_{i}=1
.

For all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
,
0\leqslant\theta_{i}<\theta_{i-1}\leqslant 1
so this equality can only occur at
j=n
and
i=0
which is impossible by assumption.
∎

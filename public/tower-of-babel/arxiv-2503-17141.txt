HiFi-Stream: Streaming Speech Enhancement with Generative Adversarial Networks
Ekaterina Dmitrieva, Maksim Kaledin

Abstract
Speech Enhancement techniques have become core technologies in mobile devices and voice software simplifying downstream speech tasks. Still, modern Deep Learning (DL) solutions often require high amount of computational resources what makes their usage on low-resource devices challenging. We present HiFi-Stream, an optimized version of recently published HiFi++ model. Our experiments demonstrate that HiFiStream saves most of the qualities of the original model despite its size and computational complexity: the lightest version has only around 490k parameters which is 3.5x reduction in comparison to the original HiFi++ making it one of the smallest and fastest models available. The model is evaluated in streaming setting where it demonstrates its superior performance in comparison to modern baselines.

The main goal of speech enhancement is to remove noise and reverberations coming from the actual environment where the audio is recorded. Time-frequency (TF) methods are based on the usage of the spectrograms produced by the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT). Models take a spectrogram as input and outputs the cleaned spectrogram which is transformed into a cleaned waveform via inverse STFT (iSTFT).
The TF methods are represented in particular by

Streaming processing presents additional constraints

III
Methodology

The problem of speech enhancement is to get an estimate
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{ch}\times T}
of the clean speech signal
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{ch}\times T}
with
N_{ch}
channels from its corrupted representation

y=f\star x+\varepsilon,

where
f\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{ch}\times k}
represents an impulse response kernel (the effects of the environment acoustics, reverberation and recording device) which is convolved with the clean signal and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{ch}\times T}
is an additive noise component. In our work we consider a monaural problem, i.e. the signal has only one channel. Originally GAN approaches mostly
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
used time-domain approach requiring direct signal reconstruction. It turned out that such approaches typically have large number of parameters: one needs a sophisticated enough encoder and decoder to obtain a representative set of features from raw audio. So, HiFi++
[
6
]
and our work in this aspect take a route of TF-methods rising from original vocoding applications
[
9
]
. Classic TF approaches usually propose to estimate a magnitude mask
M(y)\in\mathbb{R}^{F\times M}
with values from
0
to
1
for signal spectrogram
s=STFT(y)\in\mathbb{C}^{F\times M}
and then estimate the clean signal as

\hat{x}=iSTFT(M(y)\odot s).

In such a way, the phase component is bypassed to have a well-defined inverse STFT. On a high level, in HiFi++ the signal is first re-synthesized in several audio channels and then is processed by the masking pipeline producing the spectral mask.

The generator depicted on the Fig.
1
consists of several blocks: STFT-Mel transform, Spectral U-Net, HiFi, Wave U-Net and SpectralMaskNet. The idea is to train the generator to predict clean waveform and discriminator decides whether the audio is clean. We used the same loss construction and discriminators as in
[
6
,
9
]
and focus our main efforts on the generator structure.

Spectral U-Net

The block is responsible for mel-spectrogram pre-processing and acts as feature transformer highlighting the important parts of the mel-spectrogram. We show that for mild noisy conditions the block is irrelevant, it is in line with the findings of
[
6
]
where the same was demonstrated for SE problem. So, we have removed the block in all our considered architectures resulting in reduction of 300k parameters.

Improved HiFi block

HiFi block is the largest core block in the model. When trained, it transforms mel-spectrogram
m=Mel(s)
of the signal
x
into multichannel audio
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{ch}\times T}
. The channels can be interpreted as signal and noise layers very similar to those observed in the output of source separation
[
15
]
. We experimented with usage of 2D convolutions instead of 1D and varying parameters, which gave a drastic reduction in almost -400k parameters but in the same time GMACs became slightly higher with 2D convolutions. Still, the usage of 2D convolutions even with smaller kernel size increased the performance, since the model better exploits the spatio-temporal correlations in the spectrogram.

Wave UNet

The block is the main post-processing unit responsible for fixing the signal in time domain
[
6
]
by removing robo-voice artifacts typical for vocoding
[
9
]
. With the help of input waveform and multichannel vocoder output it effectively fixes the phase shifts. The Wave UNet design is taken from
[
6
]
.

Spectral Mask Net FMS

SpectralMaskNet is another key component in the model since it generates the spectral magnitude mask
M(y)
. The original HiFi++ used a U-Net processing unit which can be improved both in terms of parameters and computational complexity. The mask of gains is essentialy a tensor of the same size as spectrogram where larger values depict higher amplitude. Such specification very natural for attention architectures
[
22
]
but it is known that despite attention design allows more expressive models, it is computationally expensive for streaming
[
8
]
. We came up with a cheaper attention alternative from RawNet2
[
23
]
called Feature Map Scaling (FMS), known also as Squeeze-Excitation
[
24
]
. Instead of original UNet we used FMS Blocks with affine aggregation (see Fig.
2
) in a ResNet structure. FMS acts as attention mechanism with reduced complexity. The usage of FMS ResNet gave 20x reduction in the MaskNet block.

Fig. 2:
FMS Block inspired by
[
23
]
takes the input tensor
C\in\mathbb{R}^{F\times T}
, computes the vector of scaling coefficients
S\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
and applies them filter-wise and computes the result via affine aggregation.

IV-A

Dataset

We used Valentini VCTK
[
13
]
dataset for experiments. The dataset is a selection of 30 speakers from the VoiceBank corpus with 28 and 2 speakers set for the training and test respectively. The original sample rate is 48 kHz, we resampled the audio to 16 kHz for comparison with other solutions. All audios were cut to
3
seconds as in
[
6
]
.

In streaming scenario we used chunks of 4096 samples with no intersections. This gives us an estimate of streaming performance and measures the effect of limited context. The model separately processes all the chunks and the processed chunks are joined together to emulate the processing pipeline in audio stream.

For evaluation we used perceptual evaluation metrics: CSIG, CBAK, COVL, WB-PESQ, STOI, WV-MOS
[
6
]
. Also, we present objective evaluation metrics: SDR and SI-SDR. The key aspect of our research is model complexity, so we also demonstrate the complexity metrics: number of parameters and GMACs per second of audio measured with pytorch profiling library
thop
.

IV-B

Training details

All models were trained for 150 epochs with learning rate 2e-4 on the given dataset with batch size 32 using AdamW optimizer (see the repository for the exact details). For HiFi++ we trained our own model (marked as reprod.) with the same computational budget but also added the metrics for the checkpoint given by
[
6
]
.

Results

V-A

Offline Comparison (Full Audio)

All the modifications were trained with the same computational resource, the results are depicted in the Table
I
.
HiFi++(reprod)
has lower metrics than presented in the repository due to the less training time invested. We compare the modified models to it to have the same training conditions.
HiFi w/o spec
acts as a starting point.
The usage of 2D convolutions together with optimization of number of channels (
HiFi-2dMRF
) allowed a drastic reduction in parameters of HiFi Block resulting in almost 3x parameter reduction in comparison to
HiFi+w/o spec
. It presents the direction of smaller number of parameters but in cost of computational complexity.

The usage of FMS allows not only the reduced complexity and parameters but also better performance in terms of quality.
HiFi-fms
is essentially
HiFi++ w/o spec
with FMSBlock in place of MaskNet and
HiFi-fms+2dMRF
additionally uses 2D convolutions in HiFi MRF Blocks. The two models have their effect in comparison to
HiFi++(reprod.)
and
HiFi-2dMRF
depicting the effect of FMS Blocks. As we can see from the tables, the modifications with FMS despite the reduced complexity demonstrate relatively the same metrics or better than
HiFi w/o spec
and
HiFi++(reprod.)
.

Overall,
HiFi-Stream2D
model can be chosen as a small-size model and
HiFi-Stream
is the better option in terms of computational complexity, according to the MACs, see Fig.
3
.

We also compared our solutions to the available baselines including some recent GAN-based light-weight solutions. According to Table
I
,
HiFi-Stream
models demonstrate similar performance metrics with a slight drop in comparison to existing low-parameter baselines. In particular, the model reaches the same results as the lightest
FFC-AE-V0
with almost the same number of parameters but 2x reduced computational complexity.

V-B

Streaming Comparison

We compare the stream-aware models in the streaming conditions (see Table
II
) without additional streaming training. It is notable that almost in all cases the quality declines in all metrics which is the effect of limited audio input and context. In comparison to published baselines
HiFi++
models with FMS blocks demonstrate their robustness in this regard showing slightly worse performance. It may be the case that due to the filter-wise scaling in FMS mechanism it better exploits short-time correlations while other models may require additional streaming finetuning to compensate for the lost global context.

VI
Conclusion

Our results show that HiFi++ model can be optimized to become another GAN candidate to be the most optimal trade-off between complexity and quality. Among considered GAN baselines the proposed solutions are the best in terms of computational complexity. The achieved results suggest that the model is robust to limited context conditions and therefore can be used in SE streaming applications.

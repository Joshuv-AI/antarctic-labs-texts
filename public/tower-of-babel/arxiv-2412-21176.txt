Classical J ​ T ¯ J\bar{T} symmetries – three ways – and a precision holography check
Silvia Georgescu, Monica Guica § Department of Mathematics, King’s College London, Strand, London, WC2R 2LS, United Kingdom, Université Paris-Saclay, CNRS, CEA, Institut de Physique Théorique, 91191 Gif-sur-Yvette, France, Institute of Physics, Ecole Polytechnique Fedérale de Lausanne, CH-1015 Lausanne, Switzerland ♯ Theoretical Physics Department, CERN, CH-1211 Geneva 23, Switzerland

Abstract
The J ​ T ¯ J\bar{T} deformation is a fully tractable irrelevant deformation of two-dimensional CFTs, which yields a UV-complete QFT that is local and conformal along one lightlike direction and non-local along the remaining one. Such QFTs are interesting, in particular, as toy models for the holographic dual to (near)-extremal black holes. Despite its non-locality, the deformed theory has been shown to posess a (Virasoro-Kac-Moody) 2 symmetry algebra, whose action on the non-local side is non-standard. In this article we present, in a unified way, three different perspectives on the classical symmetries of J ​ T ¯ J\bar{T} - deformed CFTs: Hamiltonian, Lagrangian and holographic, showing how the peculiar action of the symmetries can be recovered in each of them. The perfect match we obtain between the Lagrangian and holographic results constitutes a precision check of the holographic dictionary proposed in [ 1 ] , which we slightly generalize and improve. We also comment on the interpretation of the Compère-Song-Strominger boundary conditions from the J ​ T ¯ J\bar{T} viewpoint.

We would now like to understand the field-dependent symmetries in the Lagrangian language. We concentrate on the

In more detail, in

4.1
From Hamiltonian to Lagrangian transformations

In the previous section, we have found symmetry generators that are guaranteed to act correctly on the Hilbert space of the theory, or phase space in the classical limit. The variation of the scalar field under a symmetry generated by a charge
Q_{\epsilon}
is given by

\delta_{\epsilon}\phi=-\{Q_{\epsilon},\phi\}

(4.1)

To find how the symmetries act in Lagrangian language, we simply translate the above variation to the Lagrangian formalism, using the fact that

\dot{\phi}=-\{H,\phi\}=2\frac{\mathcal{J}_{-}+\frac{\lambda\hat{k}}{2}\mathcal{H}_{R}}{\Sigma}+\phi^{\prime}

(4.2)

where a dot denotes a time derivative, and a prime, a
\sigma
derivative.
This, in particular, implies that

\partial_{V}\phi=-\frac{\mathcal{J}_{-}+\frac{\lambda\hat{k}}{2}\mathcal{H}_{R}}{\Sigma}\;,\;\;\partial_{U}\phi=\frac{\mathcal{J}_{-}+\frac{\lambda\hat{k}}{2}\mathcal{H}_{R}}{\Sigma}+\phi^{\prime}

(4.3)

with
\Sigma
defined in (
3.31
). In the following, we will treat each type of symmetry in part, concentrating on the quasi-local symmetry generators, which act more naturally on the fields of the theory than the flowed generators derived in the previous section.

Let us explain what we mean.
The distinction between the actions of the two sets of generators is easiest to draw for the left-moving ones. The quasi-local generators
P_{\eta},Q_{f}
act locally on the fields in the theory, as can be noted from (
4.5
) below. Meanwhile, the action (
4.7
), (
4.8
) of the flowed generators
\widetilde{P}_{\eta},\widetilde{Q}_{f}
is not local, because removing the zero mode of the symmetry parameter involves an integral over all space.

As for the right-moving generators, the distinction is less clear, since the associated transformations are always non-local. If we consider the ones (
4.14
), (
4.20
) generated by the “quasi-local” charges, there are two sources of non-locality: the field-dependent coordinate
v
(which is non-local, as explained in the previous section) and the coefficient of the compensating transformation, which is an integral over all space. However, the flowed right-moving generators, which lead to (
4.17
), (
4.21
) act even more non-locally on the fields, due to the missing zero modes of the symmetry parameters. This hopefully justifies our choice of terminology, which effectively ignores the first two types of non-locality.

Left affine and conformal symmetries

The generators of these symmetries are

P_{\eta}=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\,\eta_{p}(U)\left(\mathcal{J}_{+}+\frac{\lambda\hat{k}}{2}\mathcal{H}_{R}\right)\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;Q_{f}=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\,f(U)\mathcal{H}_{L}

(4.4)

where we have added a subscript, ’

The action of

Note this leaves unchanged the combination

Clearly, these transformations act locally on the scalar field. On the other hand, the action of the flowed left-moving affine and conformal charges is non-local, as we can see from the explicit expressions for the transformations they generate

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{\eta}\phi=-\{\widetilde{P}_{\eta},\phi\}=\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\big[\eta_{p}(U)-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi(\eta_{p}(U)-[\eta_{p}]_{zm})\big]

(4.7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{f}\phi=-\{\widetilde{Q}_{f},\phi\}=\partial_{U}\phi f(U)-\frac{\lambda\hat{k}E_{R}}{2R}\big[f(U)-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi(f(U)-[f]_{zm})\big]+\frac{\lambda\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}[f]_{zm}

in which certain zero modes (denoted as

the field-dependent coordinate

including when
\eta_{p}(U)=const.
.
This will be important when we study the charge algebra in section

Note that for the flowed left-moving conformal transformations, the non-locality is also manifest in the explicit presence of

in the symmetry transformation.

4.2
The conserved Noether currents

Given the explicit symmetry transformations in Lagrangian language, we can try to go full circle and construct the associated Noether currents directly in the Lagrangian formalism. Generally speaking, a continuous quasi-symmetry with parameter
\epsilon
is defined as a transformation that leaves the action invariant up to a boundary term, denoted
M_{\epsilon}^{\mu}

\delta_{\epsilon}S=\int d^{d}x\,\sqrt{g}\,\nabla_{\mu}M^{\mu}_{\epsilon}

(4.22)

At the same time, under a general variation, the action transforms as

\delta S=\int d^{d}x\,\sqrt{g}\,\bigg(E\delta\phi+\nabla_{\mu}\Theta^{\mu}(\phi,\delta\phi)\bigg)

(4.23)

where
E
are the equations of motion and
\Theta^{\mu}
is the presymplectic potential, defined via the above equation. It follows that the quantity

J^{\mu}\equiv M^{\mu}_{\epsilon}-\Theta^{\mu}(\phi,\delta_{\epsilon}\phi)

(4.24)

is conserved on-shell, since by construction it satisfies

\nabla_{\mu}J^{\mu}=E\delta_{\epsilon}\phi

(4.25)

This is the standard (canonical) Noether current associated to the given symmetry transformation. As is well known, this current is ambiguous up to the addition of exact terms
J^{\mu}\rightarrow J^{\mu}+\nabla_{\nu}k^{[\mu\nu]}
, as well as terms proportional to the equations of motion. Another type of ambiguity that will be relevant below is that any current whose divergence is proportional to the equations of motion (not necessarily with the above proportionality coefficient) is also conserved. These ambiguities are important, as they can affect the well-definiteness of the charges, as well as their algebra.

Let us now particularize the discussion to the
J\bar{T}
-deformed free boson, whose action is given by

S=-\,\hat{k}^{-1}\int dUdV\frac{\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}

(4.26)

where the prefactor is due to our having rescaled
\phi
in order to generate an arbitrary level. The components of the presymplectic potential read

\Theta^{U}(\delta\phi)=-\frac{2\,\hat{k}^{-1}\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\,\delta\phi\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\Theta^{V}(\delta\phi)=-\frac{2\,\hat{k}^{-1}\partial_{U}\phi}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}\delta\phi

(4.27)

We will be taking the coordinates to be inert under the various transformations, so the variation of the action is computed as

\delta S=-\,\hat{k}^{-1}\int dUdV\left(\frac{\partial_{U}\delta\phi\;\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}+\frac{\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\delta\phi}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}\right)

(4.28)

Finally, for future use, we also write the “normalised equation of motion”,
E
, defined via the variation (
4.23
)

E=\frac{4}{\hat{k}(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)}\partial_{V}\left(\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)

(4.29)

For each type of transformation in part, we will show that the variation of the action takes the form (
4.22
) for some
M_{\epsilon}
, and then compute the associated Noether current.

Left affine transformations

For left affine transformations, we have

\displaystyle\Theta^{U}

\displaystyle=-\,\eta(U)\,\partial_{V}\phi\hskip 42.67912pt\Theta^{V}=-\,\eta(U)\,\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}

(4.30)

The variation of the Lagrangian is a total derivative

\partial_{\eta}\mathcal{L}=\,\lambda\partial_{V}\left(\eta(U)\frac{\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)-\,\eta^{\prime}(U)\partial_{V}\phi

(4.31)

where the function
\eta(U)
was left arbitrary for now. To find the associated Noether current, we must write the above total derivatives in an intelligent way, such as

\displaystyle\delta_{\eta}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=-\,\partial_{U}(\eta\partial_{V}\phi)+\,\partial_{V}\bigg[\eta\partial_{U}\phi+\eta\frac{\lambda\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\bigg]

(4.32)

For this choice

M^{U}_{\eta}=-\eta\partial_{V}\phi\;,\;\;\;\;\;M^{V}_{\eta}=\eta\bigg(\partial_{U}\phi+\frac{\lambda\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\bigg)

(4.33)

and using (
4.24
) and the fact that
\eta=\eta_{p}
, we find the correct Noether current, denoted
K_{\eta}

K^{U}_{\eta}=0\;,\;\;\;\;\;K^{V}_{\eta}=2\eta_{p}\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}

(4.34)

which agrees with the previous expression (
2.13
). Different choices of how to split the derivatives among the two
M_{\eta}^{\alpha}
terms will correspond to various improvements of the Noether current.

Right affine transformations

For right affine transformations, corresponding to

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{\eta}}\phi=\frac{\hat{k}}{2}(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)\left(\bar{\eta}_{p}-\frac{2\lambda\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\hat{\mathcal{T}}\right)

(4.35)

we find that

\Theta^{U}=-\partial_{V}\phi\left(\bar{\eta}_{p}-\frac{2\lambda\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\hat{\mathcal{T}}\right)\;,\;\;\;\;\;\Theta^{V}=-\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\left(\bar{\eta}_{p}-\frac{2\lambda\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\hat{\mathcal{T}}\right)

(4.36)

The variation of the action takes the form

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{\eta}}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\partial_{V}\bigg[\lambda\left(\bar{\eta}_{p}-\frac{2\lambda\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\hat{\mathcal{T}}\right)\frac{\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\bigg]+\partial_{U}\bigg(\frac{\bar{\eta}_{p}}{\lambda}\bigg)+\lambda\frac{\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\bigg(\frac{\partial_{V}\phi}{R}-\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{R_{v}}\bigg)

(4.37)

where we used
\partial_{U}v=-\lambda\partial_{U}\phi
and
\partial_{V}v=(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)
and treated
\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}},R_{Q}
as constants.
While the variation of the action under this transformation is clearly a boundary term, this term is not single-valued, due to the presence of winding in the scalar field. This implies, in particular, that the last two total derivative terms in the action variation above cannot be discarded (the first two boundary terms above are periodic in
\sigma
, so their spatial derivatives can be dropped)
and we are left with a finite remainder in the Lagrangian. Thus, strictly speaking, the action (
4.26
) is not invariant under the right-moving affine transformations, due to the large affine compensating term.

Note, nonetheless, that in order for a conserved Noether current to exist,
one does not need that the action variation be exactly zero, but only that it be a total derivative. The fact that the variations of all conserved charges, including the energy, under the right affine transformations are finite strongly suggests that their action on the phase space of the theory is well-defined. Thus, one should expect to find a boundary term - which does not affect the Noether current (
4.24
), by definition - that would render the variation of the action finite. We leave this interesting question to future work, as well as studying its impact on the physical consequences of the symmetries, such as the Ward identities.

We will thus concentrate on finding the conserved Noether current associated with the right-moving affine transformations. For this we need, again, to write the boundary terms arising from the action variation in an intelligent manner.
Concentrating on the part of the action variation that only depends on
\bar{\eta}_{p}
, we may write it as

\displaystyle\left.\delta_{\bar{\eta}}\mathcal{L}\right|_{\bar{\eta}_{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\partial_{V}\left(\bar{\eta}_{p}(\hat{v})\frac{\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)-\frac{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}_{p}(\hat{v})}{R_{v}}\partial_{U}\phi=\partial_{V}\left[\bar{\eta}_{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}-1\right)\partial_{U}\phi\right]-\frac{\partial_{V}\bar{\eta}_{p}\,\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}

(4.38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{V}\left[\bar{\eta}_{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}-2\right)\partial_{U}\phi\right]+\bigg(\partial_{V}(\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{U}\phi)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\partial_{U}\bar{\eta}_{p}\bigg)

The last two terms combine into
\partial_{U}(\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{V}\phi)
after using the relation between
\partial_{V}\bar{\eta}_{p}
and
\partial_{U}\bar{\eta}_{p}
that follows from the definition of the field-dependent coordinate. The contribution of the terms proportional to
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
can be written as

\left.\delta_{\bar{\eta}}\mathcal{L}\right|_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}=\frac{2\lambda\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\left[\partial_{V}\left(\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\hat{\mathcal{T}}\right)+\partial_{U}(\partial_{V}\phi\hat{\mathcal{T}})-2\hat{\mathcal{T}}\partial_{V}\left(\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)\right]

(4.39)

If one ignores the last term, which is proportional to the equations of motion, one finds that the
M_{\epsilon}
one reads from the above is such that the current (
4.24
) precisely coincides with (
2.14
) and the current we obtained in the Hamiltonian formalism

\bar{K}^{U}_{\bar{\eta}}=2\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{V}\phi\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\bar{K}^{V}_{\bar{\eta}}=2\bar{\eta}_{p}\frac{\lambda\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}

(4.40)

This current does not satisfy (
4.25
), with
E
given in (
4.29
), but rather

\nabla_{\mu}\bar{K}_{\bar{\eta}}^{\mu}=E\left(\delta_{\bar{\eta}}\phi+(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)\frac{\hat{k}\lambda\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\hat{\mathcal{T}}\right)

(4.41)

Note that the shift proportional to
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
precisely cancels the corresponding large affine term in
\delta_{\bar{\eta}}\phi
. Even though this is not the standard Noether current (
4.24
), its divergence is proportional to the equations of motion, so it is equally conserved on-shell.

If one nonetheless insists upon having a current that satisfies (
4.25
) exactly, one is forced to include winding terms in the current components e.g.

\Delta\bar{K}_{\bar{\eta}}^{U}=\frac{2\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\hat{v}\;,\;\;\;\;\;\Delta\bar{K}_{\bar{\eta}}^{V}=-\frac{4\lambda\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\hat{\mathcal{T}}

(4.42)

which render the current ill-defined, i.e. not integrable, since it is not single-valued with respect to
\sigma
. We will therefore no longer consider these terms.

The expressions (
4.40
) that we have obtained for the currents are identical to those reviewed in section
2
, which were first proposed in
[
25
]
. The additional contribution of this section is to derive them from the action of the
J\bar{T}
- deformed boson. A non-trivial feature is that, even though the transformation of the fields must contain the large affine compensating term, this term drops out at the level of the Noether currents (
2.14
), (
2.9
).
Note also that, without the large affine term proportional to
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
, the action would be perfectly invariant under the field-dependent affine transformations (the main criterion used in the early work
[
25
]
), but the action of the symmetries on the phase space would be inconsistent.

Left conformal transformations

For the left conformal transformations, from (
4.5
), we find

\Theta^{U}=-\frac{2\,\hat{k}^{-1}\partial_{V}\phi\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}f(U)\,\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\Theta^{V}=-\frac{2\,\hat{k}^{-1}(\partial_{U}\phi)^{2}}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}f(U)

(4.43)

The variation of the Lagrangian takes the form

\displaystyle\delta_{f}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\partial_{U}\bigg(-\frac{2}{\hat{k}}\frac{\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\;f\bigg)

(4.44)

from which we extract
M^{U}=\Theta^{U}
and
M^{V}=0
. Hence, we reproduce

\displaystyle T_{f}^{U}{}_{U}

\displaystyle=0\hskip 56.9055ptT_{f}^{V}{}_{U}=\frac{2(\partial_{U}\phi)^{2}}{\hat{k}(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}f(U)=2\,T_{UU}f(U)

(4.45)

in agreement with (
2.7
), where we denoted the generator of left translations by
f(U)
as
T^{\alpha}_{f}{}_{U}
.

Right pseudoconformal transformations

In this case, the field transformations are given by (
4.20
), which consists of a periodic piece, proportional to
\bar{f}
, and a large affine one, proportional to
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
. The contribution of the presymplectic potential to the current is simply obtained by plugging this expression into (
4.27
). As before, we are working in an active picture where the field transforms, but the coordinates are inert. We are thus only left to evaluate the transformation of the Lagrangian. Concentrating on the part that only depends on
\bar{f}
, we can write it as

\displaystyle\left.\delta_{\bar{f}}\mathcal{L}\right|_{\bar{f}}=\partial_{V}\bigg(\frac{2}{\hat{k}}\frac{\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\bar{f}\bigg)

(4.46)

The contribution of the terms proportional to
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
can be written as

\displaystyle\left.\delta_{\bar{f}}\mathcal{L}\right|_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}=\frac{2\lambda\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\left[\partial_{V}\left(\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\hat{\mathcal{T}}\right)+\partial_{U}(\partial_{V}\phi\hat{\mathcal{T}})-2\hat{\mathcal{T}}\partial_{V}\left(\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)\right]

(4.47)

Of course, this is exactly the same contribution as for the right affine transformations, upon replacing
\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}
by
\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}
. As before, if we ignore the last term in (
4.47
), which is proportional to the equation of motion, we obtain the following Noether current, denoted
T^{\alpha}_{\bar{f}}{}_{V}

\displaystyle T^{U}_{\bar{f}}{}_{V}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\bar{f}(\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}{\hat{k}(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)}=-2\bar{f}\,T_{VV}\;,\hskip 28.45274ptT^{V}_{\bar{f}}{}_{V}=-\frac{2\lambda\bar{f}\partial_{U}\phi(\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}{\hat{k}(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}=-2\bar{f}\,T_{UV}

(4.48)

as expected of a generator of right-moving translations, where the components of the right-moving stress tensor are given by (
2.9
), up to a factor of
\hat{k}
. This fully reproduces the charge (
2.26
) in the Hamiltonian analysis. As in the affine case, the divergence of this current again differs from (
4.25
) by a term proportional to the equations of motion

\nabla_{\alpha}T^{\alpha}_{\bar{f}}{}_{V}=E\bigg(\delta_{\bar{f}}\phi+(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)\frac{\hat{k}\lambda\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\hat{\mathcal{T}}\bigg)

(4.49)

which is such that the large affine term completely cancels from the variation on the right-hand side. Since its divergence is proportional to the equations of motion, this current is obviously conserved on-shell.

4.3
Select charge commutators in the Lagrangian formalism

In this subsection, we explicitly compute a few select commutators of the transformations discussed above, directly in the Lagrangian formalism. Our main aim is
to illustrate the following two facts:

i)

that the compensating large affine transformations found above are necessary to ensure a symmetry algebra that is consistent with
U(1)
charge quantization

ii)

that the antisymmetry of the charge variations requires, directly in the Lagrangian formalism, that the zero mode of
\phi
in the field-dependent coordinate be removed

We will concentrate again on the quasi-local charges, whose action on the fields is simpler.

For the first task, we focus on the commutators with the global shift charge
Q_{0}
, whose expression in the
J\bar{T}
- deformed free scalar theory is given by (
2.12
)

Q_{0}=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\left(\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)

(4.50)

As a warm-up, let us start by computing the variation of
Q_{0}
under a left affine transformation, with parameter
\eta(U)
, assumed arbitrary for now

\delta_{\eta}Q_{0}=\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\left[\partial_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\eta(U)}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)-\frac{2\lambda\eta}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}\left(\partial_{U}\partial_{V}\phi+\frac{\lambda\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}^{2}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)\right]

(4.51)

The term in the second paranthesis is proportional to the equation of motion, so we drop it. The first term integrates to zero if
\eta(U)
is periodic, yielding
\delta_{\eta}Q_{0}=0
, as expected.

It is easy to see that we obtain the same result for the left conformal transformations:

\displaystyle\delta_{f}Q_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bigg[\frac{1}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}(f\partial_{U}^{2}\phi+f^{\prime}\partial_{U}\phi)+\bigg(\frac{2\lambda\partial_{U}\phi}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{3}}-\frac{1}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}\bigg)f\partial_{U}\partial_{V}\phi\bigg]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bigg[\partial_{\sigma}\bigg(\frac{f\partial_{U}\phi}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}\bigg)-\frac{2f}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}\bigg(\partial_{U}\partial_{V}\phi+\frac{\lambda\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}^{2}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\bigg)\bigg]

(4.52)

For
f
periodic, the total derivative term integrates to zero, while the second term vanishes on-shell, so
Q_{0}
is unchanged under a left conformal transformation, as expected.

The analogous computation of the variation of
Q_{0}
under a
purely right-moving
transformation
\bar{\eta}(v)
, which may or may not have winding yields, up to on-shell vanishing terms that we drop

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{\eta}}Q_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\left[\partial_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\bar{\eta}(v)}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)-2\bar{\eta}(v)\,\partial_{\sigma}\frac{1}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right]

(4.53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\partial_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\bar{\eta}}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)-\hat{k}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\partial_{\sigma}\bar{\eta}+\frac{\hat{k}}{R_{v}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}^{\prime}\partial_{\sigma}v\frac{\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}

If
\bar{\eta}
were periodic, then the first two terms would drop out, and the last term would yield

\delta^{naive}_{\bar{\eta}_{p}}Q_{0}=\frac{\lambda\hat{k}}{R_{v}}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}

(4.54)

where we used (
4.10
). However, this commutator, which is the same as that found in
[
25
]
, is inconsistent with the quantization of
Q_{0}
, as pointed out in
[
26
]
. On the other hand, if we do include the compensating transformation present in (
4.15
), then the second term in (
4.53
) brings in a winding contribution proportional to
-\lambda\hat{k}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}/R_{Q}
, while the winding piece of
\bar{\eta}
also contributes to the last term a constant piece
\frac{\lambda\hat{k}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\frac{\lambda\bar{Q}_{\bar{K}}}{R_{v}}
. The first term in (
4.53
) combines with the corresponding left-moving winding contribution (
4.51
) to yield the spatial derivative of a periodic function, which can be dropped. All in all, the variation of
Q_{0}
under the full transformation (
4.15
) is given by

\delta_{\bar{\eta}}Q_{0}=\frac{\lambda\hat{k}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{v}}-\frac{\lambda\hat{k}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}+\frac{\lambda^{2}\hat{k}\bar{Q}_{\bar{K}}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{v}R_{Q}}=0

(4.55)

which is the expected consistent result that also agrees with the one obtained in the Hamiltonian formalism.

The same mechanism applies to the right-moving pseudoconformal transformations, for which (
\bar{f}
is always assumed to be periodic)

\delta_{\bar{f}}Q_{0}=\;\!\!\int_{0}^{R}\!\!d\sigma\left[\partial_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\bar{f}\partial_{V}\phi}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}\right)-\frac{2\bar{f}}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}\left(\partial_{U}\partial_{V}\phi+\frac{\lambda\partial_{U}\phi\partial_{V}^{2}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\right)-\frac{2\lambda\bar{f}\partial_{V}\phi}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{3}}\partial_{V}\partial_{\sigma}\phi\right]

(4.56)

The first term is a total
\sigma
derivative of a periodic quantity, the second term is proportional to the equations of motion, whereas the last term can be identified with
\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}
by rewriting

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hat{k}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{f}^{\prime}\left(\frac{(\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}-\frac{\lambda\partial_{U}\phi(\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}\right)=\frac{R_{v}}{\hat{k}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\partial_{\sigma}\bar{f}\frac{(\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}=

\displaystyle=-\frac{2R_{v}}{\hat{k}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{f}\frac{\partial_{V}\phi\partial_{V}\partial_{\sigma}\phi}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{3}}

(4.57)

where the overall
\hat{k}
factor is due to the rescaling of
\phi
.
We therefore find

\delta^{naive}_{\bar{f}}Q_{0}=\frac{\lambda\hat{k}}{R_{v}}\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}

(4.58)

This commutator, however, is not consistent with the quantization of
Q_{0}
. Just like in the case of right-moving affine transformations, the full transformation includes also a right-moving affine transformation with
\bar{\eta}=\frac{\lambda\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\hat{v}
and a left-moving affine transformation with
\eta=-\frac{\lambda\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\hat{U}
, whose total contribution to the final result is
-\frac{\lambda\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}}{R_{v}}
. Overall, we find

\delta_{\bar{f}}Q_{0}=0

(4.59)

which is the expected answer, consistent with charge quantization.

The second issue we would like to discuss concerns the zero mode of the field-dependent coordinate. The specific question that we would like to address is whether it is possible to see, directly in the Lagrangian formalism, that the zero mode of
\phi
needs to be removed from
v
. The answer is affirmative and can be illustrated on a simple commutator, e.g. that of
H_{L}
with the right affine charges. This commutator can be computed in two ways: by taking the variation of
H_{L}
, which for the
J\bar{T}
- deformed free boson is simply given by

H_{L}=\frac{1}{\hat{k}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\,\frac{(\partial_{U}\phi)^{2}}{(1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi)^{2}}

(4.60)

with respect to the transformation (
4.35
) generated by
\bar{\eta}
, or by computing the variation of
\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}
under a constant left-moving translation. We expect that

\delta_{\bar{\eta}}H_{L}=-\delta_{\xi_{L}=\partial_{U}}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}

(4.61)

by antisymmetry of the charge algebra. Plugging in (
4.35
) into the variation of (
4.60
) and using the explicit expression for the field dependent coordinate, the contribution from the terms proportional to
\bar{\eta}_{p}
can be shown to vanish on-shell. The terms originating from the compensating transformation give

\delta_{\bar{\eta}}H_{L}=-\frac{\lambda\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}Q_{K}}{RR_{Q}}

(4.62)

in perfect agreement with (
3.16
). To compute the right-hand side of (
4.61
), we plug
\delta_{\xi_{L}=\partial_{U}}\phi=\partial_{U}\phi
into the variation of the expression for
\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}

\delta_{\xi_{L}=\partial_{U}}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}=-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bigg[\partial_{\sigma}\bar{\eta}_{p}\frac{\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\delta_{\xi_{L}=\partial_{U}}v-\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}\bigg(\frac{\lambda\partial_{V}\phi\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}\bigg)\bigg]

(4.63)

Notice that if we simply take
\delta v=-\lambda\delta\phi=-\lambda\partial_{U}\phi
, the above expression integrates to zero, in disagreement with our expectation. Since
v
is fixed only up to a constant term,
c
, the most general form of the variation is
\delta v=\delta c-\lambda\delta\phi
. Requiring that (
4.61
) hold, we find that
\delta c
should equal

\frac{\lambda Q_{K}R_{v}}{RR_{Q}}
, which is proportional to the variation of the zero mode of
\phi
under a left translation

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi_{L}=\partial_{U}}\varphi_{0}

\displaystyle=[\partial_{U}\phi]_{zm}=\frac{Q_{K}R_{v}}{RR_{Q}}

(4.64)

Hence, subtracting the zero mode of
\phi
from the field-dependent coordinate

\displaystyle v=V-\lambda(\phi-\varphi_{0})

(4.65)

brings an extra contribution to
\delta v
in (
4.63
) that yields the expected result

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi_{L}=\partial_{U}}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda Q_{K}R_{v}}{RR_{Q}}\frac{1}{R_{v}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}^{\prime}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}v\frac{\partial_{V}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}=\frac{\lambda Q_{K}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{RR_{Q}}

(4.66)

Of course, in order to fully convince ourselves that (
4.65
) is the correct way in which the ambiguity in the zero mode of
v
should be fixed, we should compute other similar commutators, such as
\{H_{R},\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}\}
, and demand again antisymmetry in the variations. A particularly simple argument involves the commutators related by antisymmetry to (
4.55
), (
4.59
) which imply

\delta_{\phi\rightarrow\phi+const.}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}=\delta_{\phi\rightarrow\phi+const.}\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}}=0

(4.67)

remembering that
Q_{0}
generates constant shifts in
\phi
. Since the quasi-local currents from which
\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}},\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}}
are built are invariant under such shifts, we conclude that the field-dependent coordinate entering the functions
\bar{\eta},\bar{f}
also should. However, this can only happen if the zero mode of
\phi
is subtracted from
\phi
inside
v
, yielding (
4.65
).

In conclusion, we showed that it is possible to obtain, directly in the Lagrangian formalism, the correct zero mode of the field-dependent coordinate, by requiring well-definiteness of the charge variations.

5
The holographic dual of
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs

Having discussed the extended symmetries of
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs in both the Hamiltonian and Lagrangian formulations, we would next like to understand them also in the dual holographic description of these theories, where they correspond to asymptotic symmetries of the dual spacetime. Given that the asymptotic symmetry group calculation is entirely independent of what we did so far, finding a match would represent a precision test of the proposed holographic dictionary for these theories.

The holographic dictionary for

5.1
Relating the deformed and undeformed theories

5.2
The holographic dictionary for

As reviewed in the introduction to this section, the holographic dictionary for a CFT deformed by a multitrace operator is obtained by translating the field-theory relation between deformed and undeformed sources and expectation values to bulk language, using the standard AdS/CFT dictionary.

The Chern-Simons action

The currents that we would like to model in holography are two abelian

5.3

The aim of this subsection is to check the holographic dictionary we proposed, by showing that it reproduces the correct

The entropy formula in the deformed theory

The simplest check is to compute the entropy-to-energy relation for constant backgrounds, parametrised by constant
\mathcal{L},\bar{\mathcal{L}},\mathcal{J},\bar{\mathcal{J}}

,

as the conserved charges associated with the chiral combinations of the currents.
The same expression for the energies and conserved charges can also be obtained using the covariant phase space formalism, which we present in section

Let us assume that
\mathcal{L}
and

are positive, so the corresponding background is a black hole. Its horizon is located at

-deformed CFT as a function of the (deformed) conserved charges, which we review in appendix

The deformed energy spectrum in terms of the undeformed one

While the above match is satisfactory, one would also like to obtain the relation between the deformed and undeformed observables, such as the energies and the charges. For this, one may use the fact that
the entropy, the angular momentum and the winding charge must not vary with

Denoting the conserved charges in the deformed theory as

\displaystyle\frac{k}{4\pi}\oint A_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=Q_{L}-\frac{\lambda k}{4\pi}E_{R}=J_{0}\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{k}{4\pi}\oint B_{\sigma}=-Q_{R}+\frac{\lambda k}{4\pi}=-\bar{J}_{0}

not change with

\displaystyle RE_{L}-\frac{2\pi}{k}Q_{L}^{2}

For the computation, we use the covariant phase space formalism

finding the most general allowed

The metric contribution is

\displaystyle K^{\mu\nu}_{g,\xi}=-\frac{1}{8\pi G}\left(\xi^{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}h-\xi^{\nu}\nabla_{\sigma}h^{\mu\sigma}+\xi_{\sigma}\nabla^{\nu}h^{\mu\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}h\nabla^{\nu}\xi^{\mu}-h^{\rho\nu}\nabla_{\rho}\xi^{\mu}\right)

(6.41)

where
h_{\mu\nu}=\delta g_{\mu\nu}
, while the Chern-Simons one, computed from the action (
5.63
), is

\displaystyle K^{\mu\nu}_{CS;\xi,\Lambda_{A},\Lambda_{B}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\pi}{k}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\bigg((\xi^{\lambda}A_{\lambda}+\Lambda_{A})\delta A_{\rho}-(\xi^{\lambda}B_{\lambda}+\Lambda_{B})\delta B_{\rho}\bigg)

(6.42)

The details are presented in appendix
C
. The unusual factor in front of the Chern-Simons contribution is due to our rescaling of the gauge fields.

Note that in this section, the background field variations
\delta A_{\mu},\delta g_{\mu\nu}
can be arbitrary, in particular they can correspond to variations of the integer-valued charges
J_{0},\bar{J}_{0}
, since the only way to construct a background with (ultimately quantized) such charges in this formalism is by adding up infinitesimal variations. One the other hand, when we consider variations of the background fields that are induced by an allowed diffeomorphism or gauge transformation, as in section
6.3
, then the variation should be restricted to the superselection sector of fixed
J_{0},\bar{J}_{0}
.

Left affine charges

We will start with the left-moving affine charges, which correspond to turning on
\eta_{p}
and setting
f,\bar{f},\bar{\eta}_{p}
to zero. The associated transformations are thus very simple, consisting of a diffeomorphism and gauge transformations given by

\displaystyle\xi_{\eta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda\hat{k}}{2}\eta_{p}(U)\partial_{V}\hskip 28.45274pt\Lambda_{A}=\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\eta_{p}(U)\hskip 28.45274pt\Lambda_{B}=0

(6.43)

where we rescaled
\eta_{p}
from the previous subsection in order to match with the field theory convention (
4.6
), which differed by a factor of
\hat{k}/2
.
We find that the corresponding charge variations are given by:

\displaystyle/\penalty{\!\!\!\delta}P_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma k_{\eta_{p}}=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\;\eta_{p}(U)\delta\mathcal{J}(U)

(6.44)

The result is trivially integrable in phase space and we obtain the following set of conserved charges

P_{\eta}=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\;\eta_{p}(U)\mathcal{J}(U)

(6.45)

in agreement with the field theory result (
4.4
), using the fact that
\mathcal{J}=\frac{\partial_{U}\phi}{1-\lambda\partial_{V}\phi}
.

Left conformal charges

We now compute the charges associated to the left conformal symmetry preserved by the deformation. In our notation, these symmetry transformations correspond to turning on
f
, which is a periodic function of
U
, and setting
\bar{f},\eta_{p},\bar{\eta}_{p}
to zero. The diffeomorphisms we consider are obtained by plugging

\displaystyle F_{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\prime}}{2}\hskip 28.45274ptF_{U}=f-\frac{\ell^{2}f^{\prime\prime}}{2\mathcal{L}}\hskip 28.45274ptF_{V}=-\lambda\mathcal{J}\frac{\ell^{2}f^{\prime\prime}}{2\mathcal{L}}

(6.46)

into (
6.9
), and the gauge transformations are:

\Lambda_{A}=-\frac{2\mathcal{J}+z^{2}\lambda(\kappa\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2})\mathcal{L}}{4\mathcal{L}(1-z^{4}\mathcal{L}\bar{\mathcal{L}})}\ell^{2}f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\mathcal{J}\ell^{2}f^{\prime\prime}}{2\mathcal{L}}\;,\hskip 19.91684pt\Lambda_{B}=\frac{\big(2\bar{\mathcal{J}}+\lambda(\kappa\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2})\big)z^{2}}{4(1-z^{4}\mathcal{L}\bar{\mathcal{L}})}\ell^{2}f^{\prime\prime}

(6.47)

We obtain the following charge variations

\displaystyle/\penalty{\!\!\!\delta}Q_{f}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma k_{f}=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bigg[\frac{1}{8\pi G\ell}f\delta\mathcal{L}+\frac{4\pi}{k}f\mathcal{J}\delta\mathcal{J}-

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda\ell}{32\pi G}\bigg[\frac{2\lambda\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}f^{\prime}}{(1+\lambda\bar{\mathcal{J}})^{2}}\delta\mathcal{J}-\partial_{\sigma}\bigg(\delta\bar{\mathcal{J}}\frac{f^{\prime}}{1+\lambda\bar{\mathcal{J}}}\bigg)+\frac{2f^{\prime}}{1+\lambda\bar{\mathcal{J}}}(\lambda\mathcal{J}\partial_{V}+\partial_{U})\delta\bar{\mathcal{J}})\bigg]\bigg]

(6.48)

The first two terms are trivially integrable in phase space. The total
\sigma
derivative on the second line can be dropped, as all the fields inside it are periodic. As for the last term, the differential operator in paranthesis annihilates all functions of
v
only inside
\delta\bar{\mathcal{J}}
, which itself takes the form (
6.17
). Using the expression (
6.18
) for
\delta v
, we note that the only contribution will be proportional to
\delta\mathcal{J}
, which turns out to precisely cancel the first term on the second line. Consequently, the integrated conserved charge is, simply

\displaystyle Q_{f}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma f(U)\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{L}}{8\pi G\ell}+\frac{2\pi}{k}\mathcal{J}^{2}\bigg)

(6.49)

which perfectly matches the field theory result. In particular, the choice
f=1
, which corresponds to
\xi=\partial_{U}
, reproduces the correct left-moving energy.

Right affine charges

Next, we compute the conserved charges associated to right affine transformations, which are parametrized by a periodic function
\bar{\eta}_{p}
, setting
\bar{f},f,\eta_{p}
to zero. According to (
6.39
), this choice will lead to winding terms in both
\eta
and
\bar{\eta}

\displaystyle\hskip 17.07182ptF_{z}=0\hskip 28.45274ptF_{U}=0\hskip 28.45274ptF_{V}=-\frac{\lambda\hat{k}}{2}\bigg(\bar{\eta}_{p}+2w_{\eta}\hat{\mathcal{T}}\bigg)\hskip 28.45274ptw_{\eta}=-\frac{\lambda R_{v}}{R_{Q}}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}

(6.50)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\bigg(w_{\eta}\hat{U}-\lambda\int^{v}\bar{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{\eta}_{p}^{\prime}-\frac{w_{\eta}}{R_{v}})\bigg)\hskip 28.45274pt\Lambda_{B}=\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\bigg(\bar{\eta}_{p}-w_{\eta}\hat{v}+\lambda\int^{v}\bar{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{\eta}_{p}^{\prime}-\frac{w_{\eta}}{R_{v}})\bigg)

(6.51)

where we used again the notation introduced in the field theory analysis (
4.12
) and we rescaled
\bar{\eta}_{p}
by
\frac{\hat{k}}{2}
in order to consider the same transformations as in the field theory section.

We then plug in the corresponding diffeomorphisms and gauge into the expression (
6.1
) for the charges, and carefully keep track of all periodic and non-periodic contributions. Since the calculation is slightly involved, we relegate the details to the appendix
C
, and simply quote the end result

/\penalty{\!\!\!\delta}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}=-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}v\delta\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}+\frac{w_{\eta}}{R_{v}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\delta\left(2\hat{\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{J}+\frac{\hat{v}}{\lambda}\right)

(6.52)

The first term can be easily shown to correspond to a total variation in field space

-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}v\delta\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}=\delta\left(-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\bar{\mathcal{J}}\partial_{\sigma}v\right)

(6.53)

which is nothing but the field-theory expression (
4.40
) for the right-moving affine charge, if we use the identification (
5.43
) for
\bar{\mathcal{J}}
.

Regarding the second term in (
6.52
), one may naively think that it is not integrable because of the charge-dependent coefficient
w_{\eta}
. However, as explained in appendix A of
[
48
]
, the integrability condition is modified in the case of field-dependent diffeomorphism and gauge transformations. Taking this modification into account, we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\bar{\mathcal{J}}\partial_{\sigma}v+\frac{w_{\eta}}{R_{v}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bigg(2\hat{\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{J}+\frac{\hat{v}}{\lambda}\bigg)

(6.54)

The second term in this expression is problematic, most notably because the integrand is not periodic. The reader may remember, however, that the canonical Noether current (
4.24
) constructed directly in field theory also suffered from lack of single-valuedness, and we needed to choose a different current representative in order to have a well-defined charge. In fact, the time component of the difference (
4.42
) between the canonical and the well-defined current (
4.40
)

\displaystyle\Delta\bar{K}_{\bar{\eta}}^{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{Q}}\left(2\hat{\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{J}+\frac{\hat{v}}{\lambda}\right)

(6.55)

precisely agrees with the second term in (
6.54
), upon
plugging in the expression (
6.39
) for the winding. This suggests that we may similarly exploit an ambiguity in the definition of the conserved codimension-two form
k_{\xi,\Lambda}
in order to discard this inconsistent contribution.
A natural source for this ambiguity in the bulk gauge theory is the addition of an extra on-shell vanishing Noether current
\Delta S^{\mu}
- proportional to the large affine term - whose effect on the boundary Wald charge
\mathcal{Q}^{\mu\nu}
that enters the definition of
k_{\xi,\mathcal{L}}
would be
\Delta S^{\mu}=\nabla_{\nu}(\Delta\mathcal{Q}^{\mu\nu})
. Choosing
\Delta S^{\mu}
such that its
z
component equals the divergence of the ‘inconsistent’ current (
4.42
), this immediately implies that adding this contribution to the bulk Noether current will allow us to discard the unwanted second term in (
6.54
).

To conclude, the standard, canonical procedure for constructing conserved currents in the covariant phase space formalism yields currents that are inconsistent, both in field theory (see section
4
) and in holography. Quite remarkably, we find that the inconsistent terms match perfectly between the boundary and the bulk. In both cases, we can discard these terms using an ambiguity in the Noether current that affects the proportionality coefficient between its divergence and the equations of motion. The final result for the right-moving affine charge is

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\bar{\mathcal{J}}\partial_{\sigma}v

(6.56)

which agrees perfectly with the corresponding expression in field theory. For
\bar{\eta}_{p}=1
, the conserved charge is
-Q_{R}
.

Right pseudoconformal charges

Finally, we consider the conserved charges associated to right pseudoconformal transformations, which correspond to setting all the functions to zero except for
\bar{f}
, which is periodic. In this case, using (
6.39
)

\displaystyle F_{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{f}^{\prime}}{2}\hskip 19.91684ptF_{U}=0\hskip 19.91684ptF_{V}=\bar{f}-2\lambda w_{\bar{f}}\hat{\mathcal{T}}-\frac{(1+\lambda\bar{\mathcal{J}})\ell^{2}\bar{f}^{\prime\prime}}{2\bar{\mathcal{L}}}\hskip 19.91684ptw_{\bar{f}}=-\frac{\hat{k}R_{v}}{R_{Q}}\frac{\lambda}{2}\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{2z^{2}\mathcal{J}\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\lambda(\kappa\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2})}{4\bar{\mathcal{L}}(1-z^{4}\mathcal{L}\bar{\mathcal{L}})}\ell^{2}\bar{f}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{\lambda\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2}\ell^{2}\bar{f}^{\prime\prime}}{4\bar{\mathcal{L}}}+w_{\bar{f}}\hat{U}+\lambda\frac{w_{\bar{f}}}{R_{v}}\int^{v}\bar{\mathcal{J}}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int^{v}(\kappa\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2})\bar{f}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{B}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\bar{\mathcal{J}}+\lambda(\kappa\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2})}{4\bar{\mathcal{L}}(1-z^{4}\mathcal{L}\bar{\mathcal{L}})}\ell^{2}\bar{f}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\bar{\mathcal{J}}(2+\lambda\bar{\mathcal{J}})\ell^{2}\bar{f}^{\prime\prime}}{4\bar{\mathcal{L}}}-w_{\bar{f}}\hat{v}-\lambda\frac{w_{\bar{f}}}{R_{v}}\int^{v}\bar{\mathcal{J}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\int^{v}(\kappa\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2})\bar{f}^{\prime}

(6.57)

The result that we obtain by plugging in these transformations is

\displaystyle/\penalty{\!\!\!\delta}\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}}=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bigg[-\delta\bigg(\partial_{\sigma}v\bar{f}\bigg(\frac{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}{8\pi G\ell}+\frac{2\pi}{k}\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2}\bigg)\bigg)+\frac{2}{\hat{k}}\frac{w_{\bar{f}}}{R_{v}}\delta\bigg(2\hat{\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{J}+\frac{\hat{v}}{\lambda}\bigg)\bigg]

(6.58)

The last term is the same as the one we found in field theory. The same discussion as for the right affine transformations applies here, as it originates from the same right affine winding contribution, just with a different coefficient. We can drop this term, as we did in the field theory analysis, by modifying the on-shell vanishing Noether current. Our final answer for the pseudoconformal charges is

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\partial_{\sigma}v\bar{f}\bigg(\frac{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}{8\pi G\ell}+\frac{2\pi}{k}\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2}\bigg)

(6.59)

in agreement with the Hamiltonian analysis. In particular,
\bar{f}=-1
reproduces the correct right-moving energy.

The constant gauge transformations

Finally, let us comment on constant gauge transformations, labeled by
c_{A},c_{B}
. As we already mentioned, they are not independent transformations, but combinations of constant
\eta,\bar{\eta},\bar{f}
. Their associated conserved charges are given by:

\displaystyle Q_{gauge}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2c_{A}}{\hat{k}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\partial_{\sigma}v\bigg(\lambda(\kappa\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2})+\frac{2(1+\lambda\bar{\mathcal{J}})}{\lambda}\bigg)-\frac{2c_{B}}{\hat{k}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\partial_{\sigma}v\big(2\bar{\mathcal{J}}+\lambda(\kappa\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2})\big)=

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{A}}{\hat{k}}\bigg(Q_{K}-\frac{\lambda k}{4\pi}E_{R}\bigg)+\frac{c_{B}}{\hat{k}}\bigg(\bar{Q}_{\bar{K}}-\frac{\lambda k}{4\pi}E_{R}\bigg)=\frac{c_{A}}{\hat{k}}J_{0}+\frac{c_{B}}{\hat{k}}\bar{J}_{0}

(6.60)

Hence, the undeformed charges can be interpreted in the bulk as charges associated to the pure gauge transformations. In particular, for
c_{B}=-c_{A}
(equal and opposite transformations of
A
and
B
) we recover the winding charge.

To be more precise, these constant gauge transformations correspond to setting

\displaystyle[\bar{f}]_{zm}=-\lambda([\eta]_{zm}+[\bar{\eta}]_{zm})

(6.61)

and thus, from (
6.28
), they do not change
\delta v
(the fact that the constant pieces do not change
\delta v
was the feature of affine flowed transformations). It is then trivial to see that the flowed affine generators can be obtained from the flowed ones by turning on an extra constant
\bar{f}
as above. (this would just bring the
E_{R}
contribution)

More generally, in order to obtain the conformal/pseudoconformal flowed generators, we need to consider linear combinations of the transformations discussed, with
E_{R}
-dependent coefficients, in order to implement the spectral flow. It would be interesting to see if there exists another way to obtain directly the flowed generators, similar to the analysis of
[
32
]
for
T\bar{T}
.

6.3
The charge algebra

Now that we obtained the conserved charges in the bulk, we proceed to computing their algebra. The Poisson bracket between conserved charges is written generically as:

\displaystyle\{Q_{\xi,\Lambda},Q_{\chi,\Sigma}\}

\displaystyle\equiv\delta_{\chi,\Sigma}Q_{\xi,\Lambda}

(6.62)

where
\xi,\chi
stand for diffeomorphisms and
\Lambda,\Sigma
for the associated gauge transformations. As before, we will find it useful to work with the basis of conserved charges parametrised by the periodic functions
f,\bar{f},\eta_{p}
and
\bar{\eta}_{p}
. We compute each type of commutator in part and expand the corresponding functions in Fourier modes afterwards in order to compare with the field theory results. We will check the consistency, more precisely antisymmetry, or the commutators, which will fix the constant
\mathcal{C}
introduced in section
6.2
.

Left affine commutator
\{P_{\eta},P_{\beta}\}

We start with the simple case of the Poisson bracket between two left moving affine charges, parametrized by periodic functions
\eta_{p}(U),\beta_{p}(U)
, for which we obtain, using (
6.24
):

\displaystyle\{P_{\eta},P_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\beta}P_{\eta}=\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\eta_{p}\delta_{\beta}\mathcal{J}=\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\eta_{p}\;\partial_{\sigma}\beta_{p}

(6.63)

The result is clearly a central term and is antisymmetric, since we can integrate by parts and drop the total sigma derivative. Expanding the functions in Fourier modes, we can particularize
\eta_{p}=e^{2\pi im\hat{U}}
and
\beta_{p}=e^{2\pi in\hat{U}}
for which we obtain

\displaystyle\{P_{m},P_{n}\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{imk}{2}\delta_{n+m,0}

(6.64)

which is the expected
U(1)
Kac-Moody result, for level
k
.

Right affine commutator
\{\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}},\bar{P}_{\bar{\beta}}\}

We compute the Poisson bracket between two right-moving affine charges, parametrized by periodic functions
\bar{\eta}_{p}(v),\bar{\beta}_{p}(v)
:

\displaystyle\{\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}},\bar{P}_{\bar{\beta}}\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}}=-\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}v\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}

(6.65)

which was derived in (
6.53
). We already took into account the rescaling of both
\bar{\eta}_{p}
and
\bar{\beta}_{p}
by
\frac{\hat{k}}{2}
, in order not to carry this multiplicative factor inside
\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}
. Taking into account the constant
\mathcal{C}
that can enter into
\delta\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}
, as explained in
6.1
, the variation of
\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}
under this transformation takes the form

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}

\displaystyle=\bar{\beta}^{\prime}-\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\beta}}\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}=\bar{\beta}^{\prime}_{p}-\frac{w_{\beta}}{R_{v}}-\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\beta}}\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}

(6.66)

where we remind that the derivatives are with respect to
v
and we put a label on the constant to emphasize that corresponds to this particular transformation. Plugging into (
6.65
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\hat{k}}\{\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}},\bar{P}_{\bar{\beta}}\}=-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\bar{\beta}_{p}^{\prime}\partial_{\sigma}v+\frac{w_{\beta}}{R_{v}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}v+\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\beta}}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}v\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}\bar{\beta}_{p}+w_{\beta}[\bar{\eta}_{p}]_{zm}+\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\beta}}\frac{\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}}{R_{v}}=-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}\bar{\beta}_{p}+w_{\beta}[\bar{\eta}_{p}]_{zm}-\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\beta}}\frac{w_{\eta}R_{Q}}{\lambda R_{v}^{2}}

(6.67)

The first term is clearly antisymmetric using integration by parts and it gives a central term. In order for the full result to be antisymmetric, which is of course needed for the consistency of the charge algebra, we need to fix:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\bar{\beta}}=\frac{\lambda R_{v}^{2}}{R_{Q}}[\bar{\beta}_{p}]_{zm}

(6.68)

All together, the final result is:

\displaystyle\{\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}},\bar{P}_{\bar{\beta}}\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\eta}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}\bar{\beta}_{p}-\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\frac{\lambda R_{v}}{R_{Q}}\bar{P}_{\bar{\beta}^{\prime}}[\bar{\eta}_{p}]_{zm}+\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\frac{\lambda R_{v}}{R_{Q}}\bar{P}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}}[\bar{\beta}_{p}]_{zm}

(6.69)

After expanding the functions in Fourier modes
\bar{\eta}_{p}=e^{-2\pi im\hat{v}}
and
\bar{\beta}_{p}=e^{-2\pi in\hat{v}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\{\bar{P}_{m},\bar{P}_{n}\}=-\frac{imk}{2}\delta_{m+n,0}-\frac{imk\lambda}{2R_{Q}}\bar{P}_{m}\delta_{n,0}+\frac{ink\lambda}{2R_{Q}}\bar{P}_{n}\delta_{m,0}

(6.70)

which matches (
3.12
).

Hence, although initially we considered
\mathcal{C}
, or equivalently
\delta c_{v}
, to be arbitrary, the requirement that the Poisson bracket defined as (
6.62
) is antisymmetric fixed this constant to a particular value, in the case of a right-moving affine transformation. Fixing the constant entering the variation of the field-dependent coordinate using the consistency of the charge algebra is precisely what we found in the field theory analysis of section
4.3
.

Left affine - right affine commutator
\{P_{\eta},\bar{P}_{\bar{\beta}}\}

Next, we compute the commutator between a left-moving affine charge parameterized by a function
\eta_{p}(U)
and a right-moving affine charge parametrized by a function
\bar{\beta}(v)
:

\displaystyle\{P_{\eta},\bar{P}_{\bar{\beta}}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\;\eta_{p}(U)\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\mathcal{J}=\frac{\hat{k}}{2}w_{\beta}[\eta_{p}]_{zm}=-\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\frac{\lambda R_{v}}{R_{Q}}\bar{P}_{\bar{\beta}^{\prime}}[\eta_{p}]_{zm}

(6.71)

where we used the fact that turning on
\bar{\beta}_{p}
requires an extra large left-moving affine transformation
w_{\beta}\hat{U}
and under such transformation
\mathcal{J}
changes, as specified in (
6.24
). This result is unambiguous and agrees with the field theory result (
3.16
). On the other hand, we should be able to compute the same commutator via the variation

\displaystyle-\delta_{\eta}\bar{P}_{\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{k}}{2}\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\bar{\beta}_{p}\partial_{\sigma}v\delta_{\eta}\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}

(6.72)

Under a left-moving affine transformation

\displaystyle\delta_{\eta}\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{C}_{\eta}\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}

(6.73)

In order to obtain the correct result consistent with (
6.71
), we need to set, in this case:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda R_{v}^{2}}{R_{Q}}[\eta_{p}]_{zm}

(6.74)

which makes the Poisson bracket well-defined
20
20

20

To be more precise, we check here specifically the antisymmetry of the Poisson bracket. Nevertheless, since our results agree with the field theory ones for which the algebra is
(Virasoro\times Kac-Moody)^{2}
in a more complicated basis, all the required conditions (for ex Jacobi identity) are automatically satisfied
.
Finally, let us particularize
\eta_{p}=e^{2\pi im\hat{U}}
and
\bar{\beta}_{p}=e^{-2\pi in\hat{v}}
for which we obtain

\displaystyle\{P_{m},\bar{P}_{n}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{ink\lambda}{2R_{Q}}\bar{P}_{n}\delta_{m,0}

(6.75)

which matches perfectly the field theory result (
3.16
).

Just like for the right-affine commutator, in order to have a well-defined Poisson algebra, we need to set
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
to a specific value, which fixes the variation of the zero mode of the field-dependent coordinate, matching the field theory analysis of section
4.3
. Both in field theory and in the holographic analysis, the conserved charges do not depend on the zero mode of
\phi
(or
\delta v
) and it is only at the level of the charge algebra that the apriori ambiguity gets fixed. The same mechanism of fixing additional constants in the analysis by requiring the antisymmetry of (
6.62
) was found in
[
32
]
for
T\bar{T}
.

Right pseudoconformal commutator
\{\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}},\bar{Q}_{\bar{g}}\}

We now compute the Poisson bracket between two right-moving pseudoconformal charges, parametrized by periodic functions
\bar{f}(v),\bar{g}(v)
:

\displaystyle\{\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}},\bar{Q}_{\bar{g}}\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\bar{g}}\bar{Q}_{\bar{f}}=-\int_{0}^{R}d\sigma\partial_{\sigma}v\bar{f}\bigg(\frac{\delta_{\bar{g}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{int}}{8\pi G\ell}+\frac{4\pi}{k}\bar{\mathcal{J}}\delta_{\bar{g}}\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}\bigg)

(6.76)

The variations that enter this computation are:

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{g}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{int}

\displaystyle=2\bar{\mathcal{L}}\bar{g}^{\prime}+(\bar{g}-\mathcal{C}_{\bar{g}})\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}-\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\bar{g}^{\prime\prime\prime}\hskip 28.45274pt\delta_{\bar{g}}\bar{\mathcal{J}}_{int}=\bar{g}^{\prime}\bar{\mathcal{J}}-\frac{w_{\bar{g}}}{R_{v}}+(\bar{g}-\mathcal{C}_{\bar{g}})\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}

(6.77)

Plugging in these expressions, we obtain:

Discussion

One of the main motivations for studying
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs in detail is their potential relevance as toy models for non-AdS holography and, in particular, for the so-called Kerr/“CFT” correspondence. A particularly interesting feature of these theories is the coexistence of Virasoro symmetry and non-locality, a novel phanomenon that, beyond its holographic applications, also deserves a deeper field-theoretical understanding.

In this article, we performed a comprehensive study of the extended symmetries of classical
J\bar{T}
-deformed CFTs, from three different perspectives: Hamiltonian, Lagrangian and holographic. The corresponding generators span, in a particular basis associated to non-local symmetry transformations, two commuting copies of the Virasoro-Kac-Moody algebra. In the Hamiltonian formalism, this basis is singled out by the flow operator associated to the adiabatic
J\bar{T}
deformation. Nonetheless, when considering the action of the symmetry generators on the fields in the theory, a more natural basis is revealed, in which the induced symmetry transformations are more local and the symmetry algebra is non-linear. In the holographic analysis, we showed how precisely these symmetries are realized as asymptotic symmetries of the gravitational dual to a
J\bar{T}
-deformed CFT, obtaining also an exact match of the full extended classical symmetry algebra.

There are many interesting directions for future research. On the field-theory side, it would be interesting to understand better the Lagrangian picture for the symmetries, including issues related to the invariance of the action and the off-shell significance of the compensating large affine terms. Another obvious question is how to implement the symmetries we discussed at the level of the quantum theory, for example in the path integral formulation of
[
40
]
. The peculiar transformation properties of the fields would also need to be properly understood and interpreted in the quantum theory - at full non-linear level, if possible - and the physical significance and interplay of the two bases of symmetry generators would need to be clarified. While
[
24
]
was able to show that the correlation functions of appropriately-defined operators in
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs are entirely fixed in terms of the undeformed CFT correlators via a universal, non-local transform, much more work is required to understand the full structure of the Ward identities in
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs, the general form of the correlation functions of conserved currents (as
[
24
]
only focused on deformed Virasoro primaries), as well as the physical properties of the various Lorentzian correlators that one could define and their implications for the causal structure of the theory. Moreover, given that the data that fixes the correlation functions in
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs is in one-to-one correspondence to the CFT data of the undeformed theory, one could envisage setting up
a bootstrap programme for the former, which could perhaps pave the way for interesting generalisations of
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs.
All these are very basic questions concerning the QFT structure of
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs that would be worthwhile to understand better, both from the purely QFT perspective of going beyond local quantum theories, and in view of refining the field theory predictions used to test the holographic dictionary.

are inert under these transformations. Nonetheless, given its expression (

Using (

are inert, we find

must transform as

where we used (

) for the coefficients of the linearly growing modes, and the fact that the shift and topological charges are required to not vary under the field-dependent transformations. Note this argument need not invoke the full charge algebra result of the Hamiltonian analysis, but simply follows from the requirement of charge quantization, which is
\{Q_{0},\bar{P}_{n}\}=0
, in addition to the obvious

Note that also the momentum,

Finally, let us look at the pseudoconformal transformations. If we ignore the compensating affine terms, we have

As we discuss in section

The interpretation of this analysis is as follows. As the authors themselves emphasize, an essential working
assumption
of
[
33
]
was that the semiclassical analysis of the conserved charges in pure
3d
gravity with the given chiral boundary conditions does lift to a computation in a full theory of quantum gravity, which has a consistent holographic dual. Then, the semiclassical conserved charge analysis allows one to
infer
certain properties of this putative holographic dual. First, integrability of the conserved charges computed with just the Einstein action requires that
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
be fixed (or, at most a varying constant), which is a very severe restriction on the dynamics of the theory: in a CFT, this would correspond to requiring that all the non-zero Fourier modes of the right-moving stress tensor vanish. In particular,
states of the form
\mathcal{O}(U,V)|0\rangle
, which would yield a non-trivial profile for the expectation value of the momentum-space right-moving stress tensor, would not be allowed for any operator
\mathcal{O}
that has a non-trivial right-moving scaling. How to include dynamical matter fields in this phase space is thus not entirely clear. For these reasons, the construction of the phase space put forth in
[
33
]
appears somewhat unnatural.

A second prediction of the semiclassical analysis of
[
33
]
is the negative
U(1)
level for the black hole states of most interest, which violates unitarity. While this could be viewed as a feature with potential applications to the physics of ergoregions, to our knowledge a fully consistent interpretation of this prediction has yet to emerge.

The way that our analysis (whose departure point are the
independently
-defined
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFTs, from which the dual bulk action and boundary conditions are then
derived
) solves both problems is that the low-energy effective action for the gravitational theory contains other contributions to the conserved charges besides the metric, namely from the Chern-Simons gauge fields. Their
inclusion: i) permits relaxing the boundary conditions, so that arbitrary matter field excitations (with reasonable near-boundary behaviour) are allowed, thus solving the dynamics problem, and ii) gives additional contributions to the charges, so that the negative background-dependent
U(1)
level never shows up. This points towards the conclusion that the framework of pure
3d
Einstein gravity is simply not sufficient
to properly model a consistent phase space
for the non-standard boundary conditions put forth in
[
33
]

that
a priori
allows for unrestricted dynamics of the theory. The dual field-theoretical interpretation of this statement would be that, in a theory with no left-moving
U(1)
currents, there does not exist a well-defined double-trace irrelevant operator constructed from just the stress tensor that could holographically drive the deformation of the boundary conditions for the dual metric from Dirichlet to CSS.

Note that our proposal immediately implies that the asymptotic symmetry algebra is no longer that of a warped CFT, with its characteristic enhancement of right translations to a left-moving
U(1)
Kac-Moody algebra, but more likely that of a
J\bar{T}
- deformed CFT, where the
U(1)_{L}
Kac-Moody symmetry is the
J\bar{T}
flow of the affine symmetry already present in the undeformed CFT. Note this reinterpretation of how the right-moving energy fits inside the symmetry algebra completely changes how the asymptotic density of states is to be derived from a modified notion of modular invariance, from
[
22
]
to
[
62
]
. It also implies that the dual theory is non-local once right-moving excitations are allowed, even though this fact may not be fully obvious from the behaviour of the holographic currents. All these features, with their rather different implications and interpretation, follow from a re-evaluation of what the appropriate boundary conditions for the system should be, and from considering a low-energy effective action that is actually capable of accomodating such boundary conditions.

Of course, our argument above does not demonstrate that the boundary conditions put forth in
[
33
]
are inconsistent. Rather, we simply point out that certain problems and features associated with them - namely, the dynamical coupling to matter problem and the negative Kac-Moody level - disappear upon a very simple change of framework. The new dual interpretation is within a class of field theories that are significantly better defined and understood than warped CFTs, for which the only known concrete examples are free
[
63
]
. The ultimate test whether a given set of boundary conditions in a low-energy effective field theory - assumed, as usual, to admit a consistent UV completion - are correct or not is whether they match the predictions from an independently-defined and consistent type of dual field theory, which would likely impose many more constraints on the gravitational phase space than are currently known.

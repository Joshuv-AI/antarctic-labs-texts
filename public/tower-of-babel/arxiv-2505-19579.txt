Quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebras and related bialgebra structures
Zhanpeng Cui, Bo Hou

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebras, which constructed from solutions of the Novikov Yang-Baxter equation whose symmetric parts are invariant. Triangular Novikov bialgebras and factorizable Novikov bialgebras are important subclasses of quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebras. A factorizable Novikov bialgebra induces a factorization of the underlying Novikov algebra and the double of any Novikov bialgebra naturally admits a factorizable Novikov bialgebra structure. Moreover, we introduce the notion of quadratic Rota-Baxter Novikov algebras and show that there is an one-to-one correspondence between factorizable Novikov bialgebras and quadratic Rota-Baxter Novikov algebras of nonzero weights. Finally, we obtain that the Lie bialgebra induced by a Novikov bialgebra and a quadratic right Novikov algebra is quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable) if the Novikov bialgebra is quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable), and under certain conditions, the Novikov bialgebra induced by a differential infinitesimal bialgebra is quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable) if the differential infinitesimal bialgebra is quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable).

1.
Introduction

Novikov algebras were independently introduced in the study of Hamiltonian operators in
the formal calculus of variations by Gelfand and Dorfman
[
14
,
15
]
as well
as in the connection with linear Poisson brackets of hydrodynamic type by Balinskii
and Novikov
[
7
]
. It was shown in
[
26
]
that Novikov algebras correspond to
a class of Lie conformal algebras which give an axiomatic description of the singular
part of operator product expansion of chiral fields in conformal field theory
[
19
]
.
Novikov algebras are a special class of pre-Lie algebras (also called left-symmetric algebras),
which are tightly connected with many fields in mathematics and physics such as affine
manifolds and affine structures on Lie groups
[
20
]
, convex homogeneous cones

[
27
]
, vertex algebras
[
5
,
6
]
and so on. Recently, multi-Novikov algebras
arose from regularity structure of stochastic PDEs
[
11
,
10
]
.

A bialgebra structure on a given algebraic structure is obtained as a coalgebra
structure together which gives the same algebraic structure on the dual space with
a set of compatibility conditions between the multiplications and comultiplications.
One of the most famous examples of bialgebras is the Lie bialgebra
[
13
]
, and
more importantly, there have been a lot of bialgebra theories for other algebra
structures that essentially follow the approach of Lie bialgebras such as
antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras
[
1
,
3
]
, left-symmetric bialgebras

[
2
]
, Jordan bialgebras
[
29
]
, and mock-Lie bialgebras
[
8
]
.
These bialgebras have a common property, that is, they have equivalent characterizations
in terms of Manin triples which correspond to nondegenerate bilinear forms on the algebra
structures satisfying certain conditions. In addition, it is noteworthy that one can study
the bialgebra theories in the sense that they give a distributive law in
[
23
]
.

A recent study
[
16
]
introduced the notion of a Novikov bialgebra to construct
infinite-dimensional Lie bialgebras via the Novikov bialgebra affinization.
The concept of a Novikov Yang-Baxter equation was proposed to construct
Novikov bialgebras. In this paper, we further study some special Novikov bialgebras
such as quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebras, triangular Novikov bialgebras and
factorizable Novikov bialgebras. Factorizable bialgebra is an important subclass of
quasi-triangular bialgebra, which give rise to a natural factorization of the
underlying algebras. Recently, the factorizable theory has been studied for
Lie bialgebras
[
21
]
, antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras
[
25
]
,
pre-Lie bialgebras
[
28
]
, Leibniz bialgebras
[
4
]
,
mock-Lie bialgebras
[
12
]
and so on. Here, we study the quasi-triangular
Novikov bialgebras and factorizable Novikov bialgebras, and give an one-to-one
correspondence between factorizable Novikov bialgebras and quadratic Rota-Baxter
Novikov algebras of nonzero weights. Moreover, we consider the relationship between
quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebras and solutions of Novikov Yang-Baxter equation,
as well as the relationship between quasi-triangular Lie bialgebras and quasi-triangular
Novikov bialgebras. We get the first main theorem:

Theorem I
(Theorem
5.9
)

Let
(A,\diamond,\delta)
be a Novikov bialgebra and
(B,\circ,\omega)
be a quadratic
right Novikov algebra, and

be the induced

(2)

2.
Quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebras

In this section, we show that a solution of the Novikov Yang-Baxter equation
in a Novikov algebra whose symmetric part is invariant gives rise to a
quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebra.
We first recall some known facts about Novikov algebras and Novikov bialgebras.
Recall that a
pre-Lie algebra

(A,\diamond)
is a vector space with a binary
operation
\diamond
satisfying the following condition:

(a_{1}\diamond a_{2})\diamond a_{3}-a_{1}\diamond(a_{2}\diamond a_{3})=(a_{2}\diamond a_{1})\diamond a_{3}-a_{2}\diamond(a_{1}\diamond a_{3}),

for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
. A
(left) Novikov algebra

(A,\diamond)
is a
pre-Lie algebra satisfying the following condition:

(a_{1}\diamond a_{2})\diamond a_{3}=(a_{1}\diamond a_{3})\diamond a_{2},

for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
. Obviously, a commutative associative algebra
is a Novikov algebra. Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra,
and
\partial:A\rightarrow A
be a derivation on
(A,\cdot)
, i.e.,

\partial(a_{1}\cdot a_{2})=\partial(a_{1})\cdot a_{2}+a_{1}\cdot\partial(a_{2})

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
. Then,
(A,\cdot,\partial)
is said to be a
differential algebra. The classical example of a Novikov algebra was given by
S. Gelfand
[
14
]
from differential algebras. Given a differential algebra

(A,\cdot,\partial)
, the binary operation

a_{1}\diamond_{\partial}a_{2}:=a_{1}\cdot\partial(a_{2}),

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
, defines a Novikov algebra
(A,\diamond_{\partial})
,
which is called the Novikov algebra induced by a differential algebra
(A,\cdot,\partial)
.
It was shown that each Novikov algebra can be embedded into a differential
algebra
[
9
]
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
and
(B,\diamond^{\prime})
be two Novikov algebras. A linear map
f:A\rightarrow B
is called a
Novikov algebra homomorphism
if for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

f(a_{1}\diamond a_{2})=f(a_{1})\diamond^{\prime}f(a_{2})
. We define a product
\ast
on
direct sum
A\oplus B
of vector spaces by

(a_{1},b_{1})\ast(a_{2},b_{2})=(a_{1}\diamond a_{2},\ \ b_{1}\diamond^{\prime}b_{2}),

for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
. Then
(A\oplus B,\ast)
is also
a Novikov algebra, which is called the
direct sum Novikov algebra

of
(A,\diamond)
and
(B,\diamond^{\prime})
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
representation
of a Novikov algebra
(A,\diamond)
is a triple

(V,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
, where

is a vector space and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(a_{1}\diamond a_{2}-a_{2}\diamond a_{1})=\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})-\mathfrak{l}(a_{2})\mathfrak{l}(a_{1}),\qquad\qquad\quad\;\,\mathfrak{l}(a_{1}\diamond a_{2})=\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\mathfrak{l}(a_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})-\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\mathfrak{l}(a_{1})=\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}\diamond a_{2})-\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}),\qquad\qquad\mathfrak{r}(a_{1})\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})=\mathfrak{r}(a_{2})\mathfrak{r}(a_{1}),

a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.

(A,\diamond)
be a Novikov algebra. Define linear maps

\mathbf{l}_{A},\mathbf{r}_{A}:A\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(A)

.
Then,

\mathbf{l}_{A}(a)\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}(a)+\mathcal{I}\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast}(a)
.
The proof is finished.
∎

If the symmetric part
\mathfrak{s}(r)
of
r\in A\otimes A
is invariant,
we can also give a characterization for solution of the NYBE in a Novikov algebra.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
be a Novikov algebra and
r\in A\otimes A
.
Assume that the symmetric part
\mathfrak{s}(r)
of
r
is invariant.
Then
r
is a solution of the NYBE in
(A,\diamond)
if and only if

(A^{\ast},\diamond_{r})
is a Novikov algebra and the linear maps

r^{\sharp},r^{\natural}:(A^{\ast},\diamond_{r})\rightarrow(A,\diamond)

are Novikov algebra homomorphisms.

Proof.

For any
\xi,\eta,\zeta\in A^{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle\;\langle r^{\sharp}(\xi\diamond_{r}\eta)-r^{\sharp}(\xi)\diamond r^{\sharp}(\eta),\ \ \zeta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\langle r^{\sharp}((\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}+\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast})(r^{\sharp}(\xi))(\eta))-r^{\sharp}(\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast}(r^{\natural}(\eta))(\xi)),\ \ \zeta\rangle-\langle r^{\sharp}(\xi)\diamond r^{\sharp}(\eta),\ \ \zeta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\langle r^{\sharp}(\xi)\diamond r^{\natural}(\zeta),\ \ \eta\rangle+\langle r^{\natural}(\zeta)\diamond r^{\sharp}(\xi),\ \ \eta\rangle-\langle r^{\natural}(\zeta)\diamond r^{\natural}(\eta),\ \ \xi\rangle-\langle r^{\sharp}(\xi)\diamond r^{\sharp}(\eta),\ \ \zeta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;-\langle\xi\otimes\eta\otimes\zeta,\ \ \mathbf{N}_{r}\rangle.

Thus, we get that
r^{\sharp}:(A^{\ast},\diamond_{r})\rightarrow(A,\diamond)

is Novikov algebra homomorphism if
r
is a solution of the NYBE in
(A,\diamond)
.
The situation with
r^{\natural}
is similar. Conversely, it is directly available
from the above calculation.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
be a Novikov algebra,
r\in A\otimes A
, and
(A,\diamond,\delta_{r})

be the quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebra associated with
r
. If

\mathcal{I}=r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural}:A^{\ast}\rightarrow A
is a linear isomorphism
of vector spaces, then
(A,\diamond,\delta_{r})
is called a
factorizable
Novikov bialgebra
.

Obviously, the factorizable Novikov bialgebra is a subclass of quasi-triangular
Novikov bialgebra, and we can view the factorizable Novikov bialgebra
is the opposite of the triangular Novikov bialgebra, since
\mathcal{I}=0

for triangular Novikov bialgebra. We consider the linear map
\mathcal{I}
as a
composition of maps as follows:

A^{\ast}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptr^{\sharp}\oplus r^{\natural}\hskip 8.19447pt}A\oplus A\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt(x,y)\mapsto x-y\hskip 8.19447pt}A.

The following result justifies the terminology of a factorizable Novikov bialgebra.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
be a Novikov algebra and
r\in A\otimes A
. Assume the Novikov bialgebra

(A,\diamond,\delta_{r})
is factorizable. Then
\mathrm{Im}(r^{\sharp}\oplus r^{\natural})

is a Novikov subalgebra of the direct sum Novikov algebra
(A\oplus A,\ast)
, which is
isomorphism to the Novikov algebra
(A^{\ast},\diamond_{r})
.
Moreover, any
a\in A
has an unique decomposition
a=a_{+}+a_{-}
,
where
a_{+}\in\mathrm{Im}(r^{\sharp})
and
a_{-}\in\mathrm{Im}(r^{\natural})
.

Proof.

Since
(A,\diamond,\delta_{r})
is a quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebra,
both
r^{\sharp}
and
r^{\natural}
are Novikov algebra homomorphisms.
Therefore,
\mathrm{Im}(r^{\sharp}\oplus r^{\natural})
is a Novikov subalgebra of the
direct sum Novikov algebra
(A\oplus A,\ast)
. Since
\mathcal{I}:A^{\ast}\rightarrow A

is a linear isomorphism, it follows that
r^{\sharp}\oplus r^{\natural}
is injective,
then the Novikov algebra
\mathrm{Im}(r^{\sharp}\oplus r^{\natural})
is isomorphic
to the Novikov algebra
(A^{\ast},\diamond_{r})
.
Moreover, since
\mathcal{I}
is an isomorphism, for any
a\in A
, we have

a=(r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural})(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a))=r^{\sharp}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a))-r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a)),

which implies that
a=a_{+}+a_{-}
, where
a_{+}=r^{\sharp}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a))
and

a_{-}=-r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a))
. The uniqueness also follows from
the fact that
\mathcal{I}
is an isomorphism.
∎

In Proposition
2.10
, we construct a quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebra
from any Novikov bialgebra. Here, we show that the quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebra
constructed in Proposition
2.10
is factorizable.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(A,\diamond,\delta)
be a Novikov bialgebra. Using the notation from
Proposition
2.10
, we get that the Novikov double
(A\oplus A^{\ast},\ast,\delta_{\tilde{r}})
is factorizable.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.10

is quasi-triangular. Note that
\tilde{r}^{\sharp},\tilde{r}^{\natural}:(A\oplus A^{\ast})^{\ast}=A^{\ast}\oplus A\rightarrow A\oplus A^{\ast}
are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{r}^{\sharp}(\xi,\;a)=(0,\;\xi),\qquad and\qquad\tilde{r}^{\natural}(\xi,\;a)=(-a,\;0),

for any

and
\xi\in A^{\ast}
. This implies that
\mathcal{I}(\xi,a)=(a,\xi)
.
Thus
\mathcal{I}
is a linear isomorphism, and so that,
(A\oplus A^{\ast},\ast,\delta_{\tilde{r}})
is a factorizable Novikov bialgebra.
∎

(A,\diamond,P)
is a
Rota-Baxter Novikov algebra of weight
\lambda
.
For any Rota-Baxter Novikov algebra
(A,\diamond,P)
of weight
\lambda
, there is
a new multiplication
\diamond_{P}
on
A
defined by

a_{1}\diamond_{P}a_{2}=P(a_{1})\diamond a_{2}+a_{1}\diamond P(a_{2})+\lambda a_{1}\diamond a_{2},

. Furthermore,

.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
be a Novikov algebra and
r\in A\otimes A
. Suppose the Novikov
bialgebra
(A,\diamond,\delta_{r})
is factorizable and
\mathcal{I}=r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural}:A^{\ast}\rightarrow A
.
We define a bilinear form
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(-,-)
by
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(a_{1},a_{2})=\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}),\;a_{2}\rangle
, for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.
Then
(A,\diamond,\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}})
is a quadratic Novikov algebra.
Moreover, the linear map
P=\lambda r^{\natural}\mathcal{I}^{-1}:A\rightarrow A

Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on
(A,\diamond,\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}})
.

Conversely, for any quadratic Rota-Baxter Novikov algebra
(A,P,\mathfrak{B})

of weight
\lambda
, we have a linear isomorphism
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}:A^{\ast}\rightarrow A
by
\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}_{\mathfrak{B}}(a_{1}),\;a_{2}\rangle:=\mathfrak{B}(a_{1},a_{2})
, for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
.
If
\lambda\neq 0
, we define

r^{\sharp}:=\mbox{$\frac{1}{\lambda}$}(P+\lambda\mathop{id}\nolimits)\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}:\quad A^{\ast}\longrightarrow A,

and define
r\in A\otimes A
by
\langle r^{\sharp}(\xi),\;\eta\rangle=\langle r,\;\xi\otimes\eta\rangle
, Then,
r
is a solution of the NYBE
in
(A,\diamond)
and induces a factorizable Novikov bialgebra

(A,\diamond,\delta_{r})
, where
\delta_{r}
is given by Eq. (
2.2
).

Proof.

Suppose the Novikov bialgebra
(A,\diamond,\delta_{r})
is factorizable,
then,
r^{\sharp},r^{\natural}:(A_{r}^{\ast},\diamond_{r})\rightarrow(A,\diamond)

are both Novikov algebra homomorphisms, for all
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
. Thus, we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1})\diamond_{r}\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2}))&=(r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural})(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1})\diamond_{r}\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2}))\\
&=(\mathcal{I}+r^{\natural})(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}))\diamond(\mathcal{I}+r^{\natural})(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2}))-r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}))\diamond r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2}))\\
&=r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}))\diamond a_{2}+a_{1}\diamond r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2}))+a_{1}\diamond a_{2}.\end{split}

Therefore,

\displaystyle P(P(a_{1})\diamond a_{2}+a_{1}\diamond P(a_{2})+\lambda a_{1}\diamond a_{2})

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}r^{\natural}\Big(\mathcal{I}^{-1}\Big(r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}))\diamond a_{2}+a_{1}\diamond r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2}))+a_{1}\diamond a_{2}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}r^{\natural}\big(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1})\diamond_{r}\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\big(r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}))\diamond r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2}))\big)

\displaystyle=P(a_{1})\diamond P(a_{2}),

which implies that
P
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on
(A,\diamond)
.

Next, we prove that
(A,\diamond_{P},\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}})
is a quadratic
Novikov algebra. Since
\mathcal{I}^{\ast}=\mathcal{I}
if we identify
(A^{\ast})^{\ast}

with
A
, we get
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(a_{1},a_{2})=\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}),\;a_{2}\rangle=\langle a_{1},\;\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2})\rangle=\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(a_{2},a_{1})
. That is,
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}
is symmetric.
Moreover, since the symmetric part
\mathfrak{s}(r)
of
r
is invariant, by Lemma

3.1
, we have
\mathcal{I}\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}(a)=\mathbf{l}_{A}(a)\mathcal{I}+\mathbf{r}_{A}(a)\mathcal{I}
for
a\in A
. Thus, for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(a_{1}\diamond a_{2},\;a_{3})+\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(a_{2},\;a_{1}\diamond a_{3}+a_{3}\diamond a_{1})

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}\diamond a_{2}),\;a_{3}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2}),\;a_{1}\diamond a_{3}+a_{3}\diamond a_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1}))(a_{2}),\;a_{3}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2}),\;(\mathbf{l}_{A}+\mathbf{r}_{A})(a_{1})(a_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a_{1})(a_{2}))-(\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}+\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast})(a_{1})(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2})),\;a_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

which implies that
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}
is a nondegenerate invariant bilinear
form on
(A,\diamond)
.

Finally, since
(r^{\natural})^{\ast}=-r^{\sharp}
and

\mathcal{I}=r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural}
, we have

\displaystyle\;\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(a_{1},\;P(a_{2}))+\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(P(a_{1}),\;a_{2})+\lambda\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}}(a_{1},a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\lambda\Big(\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}),\;r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{2}))\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}))),\;a_{2}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}),\;a_{2}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\lambda\Big(-\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(r^{\sharp}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}))),\;a_{2}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(r^{\natural}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}))),\;a_{2}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}),\;a_{2}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\lambda\Big(\langle\mathcal{I}^{-1}((r^{\natural}-r^{\sharp})(\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1})))+\mathcal{I}^{-1}(a_{1}),\;a_{2}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;0,

which implies that Eq. (
3.1
) holds. Therefore,
P
is a Rota-Baxter operator of
weight
\lambda
on the quadratic Novikov algebra
(A,\diamond,\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{I}})
.

Conversely, since
\mathfrak{B}(-,-)
is symmetric, we have

\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}^{\ast}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}
. By the fact that

\mathfrak{B}(a_{1},\;P(a_{2}))+\mathfrak{B}(P(a_{1}),\;a_{2})+\lambda\mathfrak{B}(a_{1},\;a_{2})=0
, we get

\langle\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}^{-1}(a_{1}),\;P(a_{2})\rangle+\langle\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}^{-1}(P(a_{1})),\;a_{2}\rangle+\lambda\langle\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}^{-1}(a_{1}),\;a_{2}\rangle=0
,
for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A

, and

\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}P^{\ast}+P\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}=0
. Therefore,

r^{\natural}=-(r^{\sharp})^{\ast}=-\mbox{$\frac{1}{\lambda}$}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}^{\ast}(P^{\ast}+\lambda\mathop{id}\nolimits)=\mbox{$\frac{1}{\lambda}$}P\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}},

and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}=r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural}
. Define a multiplication

\diamond_{r}
on
A^{\ast}
by

\xi\diamond_{r}\eta:=(\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}+\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast})(r^{\sharp}(\xi))(\eta)-\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast}(r^{\natural}(\eta))(\xi),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mbox{$\frac{1}{\lambda}$}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}(\xi\diamond_{r}\eta)

\displaystyle=(\mbox{$\frac{1}{\lambda}$}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}(\xi))\diamond_{P}(\mbox{$\frac{1}{\lambda}$}\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}(\eta)),

3.1
,
the symmetric part
\mathfrak{s}(r)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}(\xi\diamond_{r}\eta)=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}\big((\mathbf{l}_{A}^{\ast}+\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast})(r^{\sharp}(\xi))(\eta)-\mathbf{r}_{A}^{\ast}(r^{\natural}(\eta))(\xi)\big)

\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{I}:(A^{\ast},\diamond_{r},\delta_{A^{\ast}})\rightarrow(A,\diamond_{P},\delta_{\mathcal{I}})

is a Novikov bialgebra isomorphism.
∎

4.
Quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebras from differential infinitesimal bialgebras

In this section, we show that a solution

of the NYBE in some special admissible
differential algebra such that

is
\mathbf{u}_{A}
-invariant is a solution of
the NYBE in the induced Novikov algebra such that the symmetric part is invariant.
Therefore, under certain conditions, we show that the quasi-triangular
Novikov bialgebras induced by a quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable) differential
infinitesimal bialgebra is also quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable).
Let
(A,\cdot,\partial)
be a differential algebra and
\theta:A\rightarrow A

\theta(a_{1}a_{2})=\theta(a_{1})a_{2}-a_{1}\partial(a_{2}),

\theta
is
admissible to
(A,\cdot,\partial)
,
where
a_{1}a_{2}:=a_{1}\cdot a_{2}
for simply. We also say that the quadruple

is an
admissible differential algebra
.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

Let

be an admissible differential algebra. Define a
binary operation
\diamond_{\mathrm{q}}
on

(4.1)

\displaystyle a_{1}\diamond_{\mathrm{q}}a_{2}:=a_{1}(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(a_{2}),

is a
Novikov algebra for all
\mathrm{q}\in\Bbbk
. We call
(A,\diamond_{\mathrm{q}})
the

Novikov algebra induced from
(A,\cdot,\partial,\theta)
.

Recall that a
coassociative coalgebra
is a vector space
A
with a linear map

\Delta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A

(\Delta\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\Delta=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\Delta)\Delta

(A,\Delta,\theta)

is a cocommutative coassociative coalgebra and
\theta
is a
coderivation. Obviously,
(A,\Delta,\theta)
is a codifferential
coalgebra if and only if
(A^{\ast},\Delta^{\ast},\theta^{\ast})
is a differential algebra.
Let

. One can check that

is an admissible differential algebra.

Proposition 4.2

(
[

]
)

Next, we give a construction of solutions of NYBE in the induced Novikov algebra
from solutions of admissible AYBE in an admissible differential algebra.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\partial,\theta)
be an admissible differential algebra,
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A\otimes A
and
(A,\diamond_{\mathrm{q}})
,
\mathrm{q}\in\Bbbk
, be the induced
Novikov algebra. Whenever
\mathrm{q}=-\frac{1}{2}
, or
\theta
is a derivation on

(1)

is a solution of the NYBE in
(A,\diamond_{\mathrm{q}})

if

is a solution of the admissible AYBE in
(A,\cdot,\partial,\theta)

(2)

\mathfrak{s}(r)
is invariant if
r
is a solution of the admissible AYBE in
(A,\cdot,\partial,\theta)
and
\mathfrak{s}(r)
is
\mathbf{u}_{A}
-invariant.

Proof.

(1)
If
r
is a solution of the admissible AYBE in
(A,\cdot,\partial,\theta)
,
we get
\sum_{i}\partial(x_{i})\otimes y_{i}=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes\theta(y_{i})
,

\sum_{i}\theta(x_{i})\otimes y_{i}=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes\partial(y_{i})
, and

\mathbf{A}_{r}=0
. Moreover, since
\theta
is admissible to
(A,\cdot,\partial)
and

(A,\cdot)
is commutative, for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A

a_{1}(\partial+\theta)(a_{2})=(\partial+\theta)(a_{1})a_{2}
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\Big(x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes y_{i}(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(y_{j})+x_{i}\otimes y_{i}(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(x_{j})\otimes y_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+x_{i}\otimes x_{j}(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(y_{i})\otimes y_{j}+x_{i}(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(x_{j})\otimes y_{j}\otimes y_{i}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\Big(x_{i}\otimes(\theta+\mathrm{q}\partial)(x_{j})\otimes y_{i}y_{j}+x_{i}\otimes y_{i}(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(x_{j})\otimes y_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+x_{i}\otimes x_{j}(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(y_{i})\otimes y_{j}+x_{i}x_{j}\otimes(\theta+\mathrm{q}\partial)(y_{j})\otimes y_{i}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes\Big((\theta+\mathrm{q}\partial)(x_{j}y_{i})+y_{i}(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(x_{j})+x_{j}(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(y_{i})\Big)\otimes y_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes(1+2\mathrm{q})(\partial+\theta)(x_{j})y_{i}\otimes y_{j}.

Note that
(\partial+\theta)(x_{j})y_{i}=0
for any
x_{j},y_{i}\in A
if
\theta

is a derivation, we get that if
\mathrm{q}=-\frac{1}{2}
, or
\theta
is a derivation,

\mathbf{N}_{r}=0
,
r
is a solution of the NYBE in
(A,\diamond_{\mathrm{q}})
.

(2)
If
r
is a solution of the admissible AYBE in
(A,\cdot,\partial,\theta)
and

\mathfrak{s}(r)
is
\mathbf{u}_{A}
-invariant, then
\tau(r)
is also a solution of the admissible
AYBE in
(A,\cdot,\partial,\theta)
. If denote
r+\tau(r)=\sum_{i}\bar{x}_{i}\otimes\bar{y}_{i}
, then we also have
\sum_{i}\partial(\bar{x}_{i})\otimes\bar{y}_{i}=\sum_{i}\bar{x}_{i}\otimes\theta(\bar{y}_{i})
and
\sum_{i}\theta(\bar{x}_{i})\otimes\bar{y}_{i}=\sum_{i}\bar{x}_{i}\otimes\partial(\bar{y}_{i})
.
Thus, for any
a\in A
, we get

\displaystyle\;\big(\mathbf{l}_{A}(a)\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits+\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes(\mathbf{l}_{A}+\mathbf{r}_{A})(a)\big)(r+\tau(r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i}(a\diamond_{\mathrm{q}}\bar{x}_{i})\otimes\bar{y}_{i}+\bar{x}_{i}\otimes(a\diamond_{\mathrm{q}}\bar{y}_{i})+\bar{x}_{i}\otimes(\bar{y}_{i}\diamond_{\mathrm{q}}a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i}(a(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(\bar{x}_{i}))\otimes\bar{y}_{i}+\bar{x}_{i}\otimes(a(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(\bar{y}_{i}))+\bar{x}_{i}\otimes(\bar{y}_{i}(\partial+\mathrm{q}\theta)(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i}\bar{x}_{i}\otimes\Big(\theta(a\bar{y}_{i})+\mathrm{q}\partial(a\bar{y}_{i})+a\partial(\bar{y}_{i})+\mathrm{q}a\theta(\bar{y}_{i})+\partial(a)\bar{y}_{i}+\mathrm{q}\theta(a)\bar{y}_{i}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i}\bar{x}_{i}\otimes(1+2\mathrm{q})a(\partial+\theta)(\bar{y}_{i}),

since
\mathfrak{s}(r)
is
\mathbf{u}_{A}
-invariant, i.e.,

we get that if

\mathfrak{s}(r)
is invariant
for the Novikov algebra

5.
Quasi-triangular Novikov bialgebras to quasi-triangular Lie bialgebras

In
[
21
]
, Lang and Sheng have studied quasi-triangular Lie bialgebras and
factorizable Lie bialgebras. Recently, Hong, Bai and Guo provide a method for
constructing Lie bialgebras using Novikov bialgebras and quadratic right Novikov
algebras
[
16
]
. In this section, we consider the quasi-triangular Lie bialgebras
from this construction. Let us first review some basic facts of Lie bialgebras.
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
be a Lie algebra, i.e.,
[g_{1},g_{2}]=-[g_{2},g_{1}]

and
[[g_{1},g_{2}],g_{3}]+[[g_{2},g_{3}],g_{1}]+[[g_{3},g_{1}],g_{2}]=0

for any
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in\mathfrak{g}
. A
Lie coalgebra

(\mathfrak{g},\tilde{\Delta})

is a linear vector
\mathfrak{g}
with a linear map
\tilde{\Delta}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
,
such that
\tau\tilde{\Delta}=-\tilde{\Delta}
and
(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\tilde{\Delta})\tilde{\Delta}-(\tau\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\tilde{\Delta})\tilde{\Delta}=(\tilde{\Delta}\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)\tilde{\Delta}
. One can check that
(\mathfrak{g},\tilde{\Delta})

is a Lie coalgebra if and only if
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\tilde{\Delta}^{\ast})
is a Lie algebra.

A
Lie bialgebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\tilde{\Delta})
such that
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
is a
Lie algebra,
(\mathfrak{g},\tilde{\Delta})
is a Lie coalgebra, and the following
compatibility condition holds:

\tilde{\Delta}([g_{1},g_{2}])=(\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{1})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits+\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{1}))(\tilde{\Delta}(g_{2}))-(\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{2})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits+\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{2}))(\tilde{\Delta}(g_{1})),

where
\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g_{1})(g_{2})=[g_{1},g_{2}]
, for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
.
A Lie bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\tilde{\Delta})
is called
coboundary
if there exists a

r\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
such that
\tilde{\Delta}=\tilde{\Delta}_{r}
, where

(5.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{r}(g)=(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g)+\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g)\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(r),

for any
g\in\mathfrak{g}
. Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
be a Lie algebra. Recall that an element

r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
is said to be
\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant
if

(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g)+\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(g)\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits)(r)=0
. The equation

\mathbf{C}_{r}:=[r_{12},r_{13}]+[r_{13},r_{23}]+[r_{12},r_{23}]=0

is called the
classical Yang-Baxter equation (or, CYBE)
in
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
,
where
[r_{12},r_{13}]=\sum_{i,j}[x_{i},x_{j}]\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}
,

[r_{13},r_{23}]=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes[y_{i},y_{j}]
and

[r_{12},r_{23}]=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes[y_{i},x_{j}]\otimes y_{j}
.

s,t\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
, we have

\omega\Big(e_{s}\otimes e_{t},\;\sum_{j}(e_{p}\circ e_{j})\otimes f_{j}\Big)=\omega(e_{s},\;e_{p}\circ e_{t})=\omega(e_{t},\;e_{p}\circ e_{s})=\omega\Big(e_{s}\otimes e_{t},\;\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes(e_{p}\circ e_{j})\Big).

Thus,
\sum_{j}(e_{p}\circ e_{j})\otimes f_{j}=\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes(e_{p}\circ e_{j})

since
\omega(-,-)
is nondegenerate. Similarly, we also have

\sum_{j}(e_{p}\circ e_{j})\otimes f_{j}=\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes(e_{p}\circ f_{j})=\sum_{j}(e_{p}\circ f_{j})\otimes e_{j}
.
Moreover, since
\omega(-,-)
is invariant, we have

\displaystyle\omega\Big(e_{s}\otimes e_{t},\;\sum_{j}(e_{j}\circ e_{p})\otimes f_{j}\Big)=\omega(e_{s},\;e_{t}\circ e_{p})=-\omega(e_{t},\;e_{p}\circ e_{s})-\omega(e_{t},\;e_{s}\circ e_{p})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=-\omega\Big(e_{s}\otimes e_{t},\;\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes(e_{p}\circ e_{j})\Big)-\omega\Big(e_{s}\otimes e_{t},\;\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes(e_{j}\circ e_{p})\Big)

That is,
\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes(e_{p}\circ e_{j})=-\sum_{j}(e_{j}\circ e_{p})\otimes f_{j}-\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes(e_{j}\circ e_{p})
. Similarly, we have

\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes(e_{p}\circ f_{j})=-\sum_{j}(e_{j}\circ e_{p})\otimes f_{j}-\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes(f_{j}\circ e_{p})
,
\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes(e_{p}\circ e_{j})=-\sum_{j}(f_{j}\circ e_{p})\otimes e_{j}-\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes(e_{j}\circ e_{p})

and
\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes(e_{p}\circ f_{j})=-\sum_{j}(f_{j}\circ e_{p})\otimes e_{j}-\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes(f_{j}\circ e_{p})
.
Thus,
\sum_{j}(e_{j}\circ e_{p})\otimes f_{j}=\sum_{j}(f_{j}\circ e_{p})\otimes e_{j}

and
\sum_{j}f_{j}\otimes(e_{j}\circ e_{p})=\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes(f_{j}\circ e_{p})
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\;\big(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes e_{p})+\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes e_{p})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)(\widehat{r}+\tau(\widehat{r}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i,j}\Big(((a\diamond x_{i})\otimes e_{j})\otimes(y_{i}\otimes(f_{j}\circ e_{p}))+(x_{i}\otimes e_{j})\otimes((a\diamond y_{i})\otimes(f_{j}\circ e_{p}))

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(x_{i}\otimes e_{j})\otimes((y_{i}\diamond a)\otimes(f_{j}\circ e_{p}))+((a\diamond y_{i})\otimes e_{j})\otimes(x_{i}\otimes(f_{j}\circ e_{p}))

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(y_{i}\otimes e_{j})\otimes((a\diamond x_{i})\otimes(f_{j}\circ e_{p}))+(y_{i}\otimes e_{j})\otimes((x_{i}\diamond a)\otimes(f_{j}\circ e_{p}))\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j}\Big(((a\diamond x_{i})\otimes(e_{j}\circ e_{p}))\otimes(y_{i}\otimes f_{j})+(x_{i}\otimes(e_{j}\circ e_{p}))\otimes((a\diamond y_{i})\otimes f_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(x_{i}\otimes(e_{j}\circ e_{p}))\otimes((y_{i}\diamond a)\otimes f_{j})+((a\diamond y_{i})\otimes(e_{j}\circ e_{p}))\otimes(x_{i}\otimes f_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(y_{i}\otimes(e_{j}\circ e_{p}))\otimes((a\diamond x_{i})\otimes f_{j})+(y_{i}\otimes(e_{j}\circ e_{p}))\otimes((x_{i}\diamond a)\otimes f_{j})\Big).

Since
\mathfrak{s}(r)
is invariant, i.e.,
(a\diamond x_{i})\otimes y_{i}+x_{i}\otimes(a\diamond y_{i})+x_{i}\otimes(y_{i}\diamond a)+(a\diamond y_{i})\otimes x_{i}+y_{i}\otimes(a\diamond x_{i})+y_{i}\otimes(x_{i}\diamond a)=0
, we get

\big(\mathop{id}\nolimits\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes e_{p})+\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}(a\otimes e_{p})\otimes\mathop{id}\nolimits\big)(\widehat{r}+\tau(\widehat{r}))=0
,

\widehat{r}+\tau(\widehat{r})
is
\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}
-invariant. The proof is finished.
∎

By the nondegeneracy and symmetry of bilinear form
\omega(-,-)
, one can check that
the element
\widehat{r}\in(A\otimes B)\otimes(A\otimes B)
defined in Proposition

5.7
is skew-symmetric if
r
is skew-symmetric. Thus, we have
the following corollary directly.

Corollary 5.8

]
)
.

Let
(A,\diamond)
be a Novikov algebra and
(B,\circ,\omega)
be a quadratic

(A\otimes B,[-,-])

If
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A\otimes A

\widehat{r}=\sum_{i,j}(x_{i}\otimes e_{j})\otimes(y_{i}\otimes f_{j})\in(A\otimes B)\otimes(A\otimes B).

We give the main conclusion of this section.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(A,\diamond,\delta)
be a Novikov bialgebra and
(B,\circ,\omega)
be a quadratic
right Novikov algebra, and
(A\otimes B,[-,-],\tilde{\Delta})
be the induced
Lie bialgebra by
(A,\diamond,\delta)
and
(B,\circ,\omega)
. Then, we have

(1)

(A\otimes B,[-,-],\tilde{\Delta})
is quasi-triangular if

(A,\diamond,\delta)
is quasi-triangular;

(2)

(A\otimes B,[-,-],\tilde{\Delta})
is triangular if

(A,\diamond,\delta)
is triangular;

(3)

(A\otimes B,[-,-],\tilde{\Delta})
is factorizable if

(A,\diamond,\delta)
is factorizable.

Proof.

First,
(1)
and
(2)
follow from Proposition
5.7
and
Corollary
5.8
. Second, if
(A,\diamond,\delta)
is factorizable,
i.e., there exists an element
r\in A\otimes A
such that
\delta=\delta_{r}
,

\mathfrak{s}(r)
is invariant, and the map
\mathcal{I}=r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural}:A^{\ast}\rightarrow A
is an isomorphism of vector spaces, where
\delta_{r}
is
given by Eq. (
2.2
). Suppose
\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\}
is a basis
of
B
and
\{f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n}\}
is the dual basis of
\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\}
with respect to
\omega(-,-)
. We need show that
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}=\widehat{r}^{\sharp}-\widehat{r}^{\natural}:(A\otimes B)^{\ast}\rightarrow A\otimes B
is
an isomorphism of vector spaces. Denote
\kappa:=\sum_{j}e_{j}\otimes f_{j}\in B\otimes B
.
Define
\kappa^{\sharp}:B^{\ast}\rightarrow B
by
\langle\kappa^{\sharp}(\eta_{1}),\;\eta_{2}\rangle=\langle\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2},\;\kappa\rangle
,
for any
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in B
. Then, one can check that
\kappa^{\sharp}
is
a linear isomorphism and
\langle\kappa^{\sharp}(\eta_{1}),\;\eta_{2}\rangle=\langle\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2},\;\kappa\rangle=\langle\eta_{2}\otimes\eta_{1},\;\kappa\rangle=\langle\kappa^{\sharp}(\eta_{2}),\;\eta_{1}\rangle
.
Therefore, for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in A
and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in B
,

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{r}^{\natural}(\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1}),\;\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j}\langle(\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2})\otimes(\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1}),\;(x_{i}\otimes e_{j})\otimes(y_{i}\otimes f_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big(-\sum_{i}\langle\xi_{2},x_{i}\rangle\langle\xi_{1},y_{i}\rangle\Big)\Big(\sum_{j}\langle\eta_{1},f_{j}\rangle\langle\eta_{2},e_{j}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=\langle r^{\natural}(\xi_{1}),\;\xi_{2}\rangle\langle\kappa^{\sharp}(\eta_{2}),\;\eta_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle r^{\natural}(\xi_{1})\otimes\kappa^{\sharp}(\eta_{1}),\;\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}\rangle.

That is,
\widehat{r}^{\natural}=r^{\natural}\otimes\kappa^{\sharp}
, Similarly,
we have
\widehat{r}^{\sharp}=r^{\sharp}\otimes\kappa^{\sharp}
.
Thus,
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}=\widehat{r}^{\sharp}-\widehat{r}^{\natural}=(r^{\sharp}-r^{\natural})\otimes\kappa^{\sharp}
is an isomorphism of vector spaces.
The proof is finished.
∎

Example 5.10
.

Consider the 2-dimensional Novikov algebra
(A=\Bbbk\{e_{1},e_{2}\},\diamond)
, where

-e_{1}\diamond e_{2}=e_{2}=e_{2}\diamond e_{1}
,
e_{1}\diamond e_{1}=e_{2}\diamond e_{2}=0
. One can check that all solutions of the NYBE in this Novikov algebra
have the form
r=k(e_{1}\otimes e_{2}-e_{2}\otimes e_{1})+le_{2}\otimes e_{2}
for

k,l\in\Bbbk
. In this case,
\mathfrak{s}(r)
is not invariant. We consider
a skew-symmetric solution
r=e_{1}\otimes e_{2}-e_{2}\otimes e_{1}
, and get a triangular
Novikov bialgebra
(A,\diamond,\delta_{r})
, where
\delta_{r}:A\rightarrow A\otimes A

is given by
\delta_{r}(e_{1})=-e_{2}\otimes e_{1}
and
\delta_{r}(e_{2})=-e_{2}\otimes e_{2}
.

Let
(B,\circ,\omega)
be the quadratic right Novikov algebra given in Example

5.5
, i.e.,
B=\Bbbk\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
,
x_{1}\circ x_{1}=0
,

x_{1}\circ x_{2}=-2x_{1}
,
x_{2}\circ x_{1}=x_{1}
,
x_{2}\circ x_{2}=x_{2}
, and

\omega(x_{1},x_{1})=\omega(x_{2},x_{2})=0
,
\omega(x_{1},x_{2})=\omega(x_{2},x_{1})=1
.
Then we get a right Novikov coalgebra
(B,\Delta_{\omega})
, where
\Delta_{\omega}(x_{1})=x_{1}\otimes x_{1}
,
\Delta_{\omega}(x_{2})=x_{1}\otimes x_{2}-2x_{2}\otimes x_{1}
.
Moreover, by Proposition
5.6
, we get a Lie bialgebra
(A\otimes B,[-,-],\tilde{\Delta})
, where

\displaystyle\qquad[e_{1}\otimes x_{1},\;e_{2}\otimes x_{2}]=e_{2}\otimes x_{1}=[e_{1}\otimes x_{2},\;e_{2}\otimes x_{1}],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad[e_{1}\otimes x_{2},\;e_{2}\otimes x_{2}]=-2e_{2}\otimes x_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}(e_{1}\otimes x_{1})=(e_{1}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{2}\otimes x_{1})-(e_{2}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{1}\otimes x_{1}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}(e_{1}\otimes x_{2})=(e_{1}\otimes x_{2})\otimes(e_{2}\otimes x_{1})-(e_{2}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{1}\otimes x_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+2(e_{2}\otimes x_{2})\otimes(e_{1}\otimes x_{1})-2(e_{1}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{2}\otimes x_{2}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}(e_{2}\otimes x_{2})=3(e_{2}\otimes x_{2})\otimes(e_{2}\otimes x_{1})-3(e_{2}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{2}\otimes x_{2}).

On the other hand, in the quadratic right Novikov algebra
(B,\circ,\omega)
,

\{y_{1}=x_{2},\;y_{2}=x_{1}\}
is the dual basis of
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
with
respect to
\omega(-,-)
. Let

\widehat{r}=(e_{1}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{2}\otimes x_{2})-(e_{2}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{1}\otimes x_{2})+(e_{1}\otimes x_{2})\otimes(e_{2}\otimes x_{1})-(e_{2}\otimes x_{2})\otimes(e_{1}\otimes x_{1}).

Then,
\widehat{r}
is a skew-symmetric solution of the CYBE in
(A\otimes B,[-,-])
,
and it induces a comultiplication
\tilde{\Delta}_{\widehat{r}}
on
A\otimes B
such that

(A\otimes B,[-,-],\tilde{\Delta}_{\widehat{r}})
is a triangular Lie bialgebra.
By direct calculation, we get
\tilde{\Delta}_{\widehat{r}}=\tilde{\Delta}
.
Thus, the diagram above is commutative.

Example 5.11
.

Consider the 4-dimensional Novikov algebra
(A=\Bbbk\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\},\diamond)
, where
e_{1}\diamond e_{1}=e_{2}
,
e_{1}\diamond e_{4}=e_{2}-2e_{3}
,

e_{4}\diamond e_{1}=-2e_{2}+e_{3}
,
e_{4}\diamond e_{4}=e_{3}
. Then, one can check
that
r=e_{1}\otimes e_{3}+e_{2}\otimes e_{4}
is a solution of the NYBE in this
Novikov algebra, and
r+\tau(r)
is invariant. Thus, by Example
3.6
,
we get a factorizable Novikov bialgebra
(A,\diamond,\delta_{r})
, where the nonzero
comultiplication
\delta_{r}:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is given by

\delta_{r}(e_{1})=e_{2}\otimes e_{2}
,
\delta_{r}(e_{4})=-e_{3}\otimes e_{3}
.

Let
(B,\circ,\omega)
be the quadratic right Novikov algebra given in Example

5.5
. By Proposition
5.6
, we get a Lie bialgebra

(A\otimes B,[-,-],\tilde{\Delta})
, where

\displaystyle\qquad[e_{1}\otimes x_{1},\;e_{1}\otimes x_{2}]=-3e_{2}\otimes x_{1}=[e_{1}\otimes x_{2},\;e_{4}\otimes x_{1}],

\displaystyle\qquad[e_{1}\otimes x_{1},\;e_{4}\otimes x_{2}]=3e_{3}\otimes x_{1}=[e_{4}\otimes x_{2},\;e_{4}\otimes x_{1}],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad[e_{1}\otimes x_{2},\;e_{4}\otimes x_{2}]=3e_{2}\otimes x_{2}-3e_{3}\otimes x_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}(e_{1}\otimes x_{2})=3(e_{2}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{2}\otimes x_{2})-3(e_{2}\otimes x_{2})\otimes(e_{2}\otimes x_{1}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}(e_{4}\otimes x_{2})=3(e_{3}\otimes x_{2})\otimes(e_{3}\otimes x_{1})-3(e_{3}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{3}\otimes x_{2}).

On the other hand, in the quadratic right Novikov algebra
(B,\circ,\omega)
,

\{y_{1}=x_{2},\;y_{2}=x_{1}\}
is the dual basis of
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
with
respect to
\omega(-,-)
. Let

\widehat{r}=(e_{1}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{3}\otimes x_{2})+(e_{1}\otimes x_{2})\otimes(e_{3}\otimes x_{1})+(e_{2}\otimes x_{1})\otimes(e_{4}\otimes x_{2})+(e_{2}\otimes x_{2})\otimes(e_{4}\otimes x_{1}).

Then,
\widehat{r}
is a symmetric solution of the CYBE in
(A\otimes B,[-,-])
,
and one can check that it is
\mathrm{ad}_{A\otimes B}
-invariant. Thus, the solution
\widehat{r}

induces a comultiplication
\tilde{\Delta}_{\widehat{r}}
on
A\otimes B
such that

(A\otimes B,[-,-],\tilde{\Delta}_{\widehat{r}})
is a quasi-triangular Lie bialgebra.
By direct calculation, we get
\tilde{\Delta}_{\widehat{r}}=\tilde{\Delta}
, and

\widehat{\mathcal{I}}=\widehat{r}^{\sharp}-\widehat{r}^{\natural}:(A\otimes B)^{\ast}\rightarrow A\otimes B
is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{I}}(f_{1}\otimes y_{1})=e_{3}\otimes x_{2},\quad\widehat{\mathcal{I}}(f_{1}\otimes y_{2})=e_{3}\otimes x_{1},\quad\widehat{\mathcal{I}}(f_{2}\otimes y_{1})=e_{4}\otimes x_{2},\quad\widehat{\mathcal{I}}(f_{2}\otimes y_{2})=e_{4}\otimes x_{1},

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{I}}(f_{3}\otimes y_{1})=e_{1}\otimes x_{2},\quad\widehat{\mathcal{I}}(f_{3}\otimes y_{2})=e_{1}\otimes x_{1},\quad\widehat{\mathcal{I}}(f_{4}\otimes y_{1})=e_{2}\otimes x_{2},\quad\widehat{\mathcal{I}}(f_{4}\otimes y_{2})=e_{2}\otimes x_{1},

where
\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\}\subseteq A^{\ast}
is the dual basis of

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
and
\{y_{1},y_{2}\}\subseteq B^{\ast}
is the dual
basis of
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
. Thus,
(A\otimes B,[-,-],\tilde{\Delta})=(A\otimes B,[-,-],\tilde{\Delta}_{\widehat{r}})
as factorizable Lie bialgebras.

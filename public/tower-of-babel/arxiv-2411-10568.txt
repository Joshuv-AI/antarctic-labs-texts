On Banyaga’s conjecture on the group of symplectic homeomorphisms
Carole Madengko, Stephane Tchuiaga, Franck Houenou

Abstract
This paper addresses Banyaga’s conjecture asserting that : the group of strong symplectic homeomorphisms is a proper normal subgroup of the symplectic homeomorphism group of a closed symplectic manifold.



1
Introduction

On a closed symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, denote by
G_{\omega}(M)
the identity component in the group
Symp(M,\omega)
consisted of symplectic diffeomorphisms. The group
G_{\omega}(M)
carries a Hofer-like topology introduced in
[
2
]
and further examined in
[
11
]

[
3

14

to define the group

of strong symplectic homeomorphisms as a

. In
[

Banyaga showed that

is a subgroup of

the identity component in the group of symplectic homeomorphisms, and conjectured the the following

Conjecture 1
.

The inclusion
SG_{\omega}(M)\subset Sympeo_{0}(M,\omega)
is proper.

When
M
is simply connected,

coincides with
Hameo(M,\omega)
, the hameomorphism group defined by Oh-Müller
[
10

As will be detailed in Section

any symplectic isotopy
\Phi=\{\phi^{t}\}
is generated by a pair

is a smooth time dependent function on

and
\mathcal{H}=\{\mathcal{H}^{t}\}
is a smooth family of

forms or harmonic forms. Therefore, the Hofer-like length of

is given by

(1.1)

where

,

is the

-norm on

.
The length

induces metrics

and

Iso(M,\omega)
the group of symplectic isotopies and

respectively.

Let

, and

and for any

(\phi^{t})^{*}\alpha-\alpha=d\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\Phi}(t),\qquad\forall t,

(2.1)

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\Phi}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(\imath_{\dot{\phi^{s}}}\alpha)\circ\phi^{s}ds.
Fix

, for any

, take a curve
\gamma
from

to

. Integrating (

) over

yields

\int_{\gamma}(\phi^{t})^{*}\alpha-\alpha=\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\Phi}(t)(y)-\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\Phi}(t)(x),\qquad\forall t

Recently, it has been shown in
[
12
]
that

\Delta(\phi^{1},\alpha)_{x}:=\dfrac{1}{\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}}\left\langle[\alpha\wedge\omega^{n-1}],\widetilde{S}_{\omega}(\Phi)\right\rangle-\dfrac{Vol(M)}{\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}}\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\Phi}(1)(x),

(2.2)

where
\widetilde{S}_{\omega}:\widetilde{G_{\omega}(M)}\longrightarrow H^{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R})
is the flux homomorphism see
[
1
]
for more details. From Proposition
3.8-
[

]
, if
M
has boundary, equation (

) has an additional term. In
[

]
, Tchuiaga defined a norm on

as follows:

\|\phi\|^{\infty}=\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{B}(1)}\left(\sup_{x\in M}|\widetilde{\Delta}(\phi,\alpha)_{x}|\right),

for any
\phi\in G_{\omega}(M)
, where
\widetilde{\Delta}(\phi,\alpha)_{x}=\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}\Delta(\phi,\alpha)_{x}
and
\mathcal{B}(1)=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}(\Sigma_{g}):\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}=1\}
.

The norm

extends to the

\phi\in Sympeo(\Sigma_{g},\omega)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{\infty}=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{B}(1)}\left(\sup_{x\in M}|\widetilde{\chi}(\phi,\alpha)_{x}|\right),

(2.3)

where

2.1
Splitting of closed
1-
forms

Let
B^{1}(M)
, be the space of exact
1-
forms and
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
the first de Rham cohomology group. Consider a linear section
\mathcal{S}:H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{1}(M)
of the natural projection

\mathcal{\pi}:\mathcal{Z}^{1}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Any
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}(M)
splits as:

\alpha=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{\pi}(\alpha))+(\alpha-\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{\pi}(\alpha)))

(2.4)

where
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{\pi}(\alpha))
is an
\mathcal{S}
-form and
\alpha-\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{\pi}(\alpha))
is an exact form.
Let
\mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{S}}(M)
denote the space of all
\mathcal{S}-
forms. Note that if the linear section
\mathcal{S}
is trivial then
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{\pi}(\alpha))
is a harmonic
1-
form and equation
2.4
is the Hodge decomposition of closed
1-
forms. For more details see
[
3
,
11
]
.
To any symplectic isotopy
\Phi=\{\phi^{t}\}
corresponds a unique pair
(U,\mathcal{H})
through the following isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccccc}Iso(M,\omega)&\longrightarrow&Z^{1}(M)&\xrightarrow{\text{Splitting}}&Z^{1}(M)&\longrightarrow\mathfrak{T}(M,\omega,\mathcal{S})\\
\Phi=\{\phi^{t}\}_{t}&\longmapsto&\imath_{\dot{\phi}^{t}}\omega&\longmapsto&dU^{t}+\mathcal{H}^{t}&\longmapsto(U,\mathcal{H}),\end{array}

where
U=\{U^{t}\}_{t}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M\times[0,1],\mathbb{R})
( the set of zero mean smooth functions on
M\times[0,1]
),
\mathcal{H}=\{\mathcal{H}^{t}\}_{t}
is a smooth path in
\mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{S}}(M)
and
\mathfrak{T}(M,\omega,\mathcal{S})
is the set of all generators
(U,\mathcal{H})
of symplectic isotopies.
In the sequel, we write
\phi_{(U,\mathcal{H})}
to mean the isotopy
\Phi
is generated by
(U,\mathcal{H})
.
The set
\mathfrak{T}(M,\omega,\mathcal{S})
forms a group w.r.t the following rule:

(U,\mathcal{H})\Join_{\mathcal{S}}(V,\mathcal{K})=\left(U+V\circ\phi_{(U,\mathcal{H})}^{-1}+\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi_{(U,\mathcal{H})}^{-1}}(t),\quad\mathcal{H}+\mathcal{K}\right),

(2.6)

the inverse of
(U,\mathcal{H})
, denoted
\overline{(U,\mathcal{H})}
, is given by

\overline{(U,\mathcal{H})}=\left(-U\circ\phi_{(U,\mathcal{H})}-\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\phi_{(U,\mathcal{H})}}(t),\quad-\mathcal{H}\right)

(2.7)

where
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\phi_{(U,\mathcal{H})}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}^{t}(\dot{\psi}^{s}_{(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{H})})\circ\psi^{s}_{(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{H})}ds-\frac{\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}^{t}(\dot{\psi}^{s}_{(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{H})})\circ\psi^{s}_{(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{H})}ds\right)\omega}{\displaystyle\int_{M}\omega}

for all
t\in[0,1].

Given a symplectic isotopy
\Phi=\{\phi^{t}\}
generated by
(U,\mathcal{H}),

the
L^{(1,\infty)}-
version of the Hofer-like length of
\Phi
is given by

l^{(1,\infty)}_{\kappa,\mathcal{S}}(\Phi)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\nu^{B}(dU^{t})+\kappa\|\mathcal{H}^{t}\|_{L^{2}}\right)dt,

(2.8)

where
\nu^{B}
is any norm on
B^{1}(M)
, the coefficient
\kappa
is a positive real number. Here, we choose
\nu^{B}
to be the oscillation norm for more details see
[
11
]
.
The above Hofer-like length gives rise to the Hofer-like energy
e_{HL}
, which assigns to any
\psi\in G_{\omega}(M)
:

\displaystyle e_{HL}(\psi)=\inf l^{(1,\infty)}_{\kappa,\mathcal{S}}(\Phi)

where the infimum is taken over all symplectic isotopies whose time one map is
\psi
. The
L^{(1,\infty)}-
Hofer-like norm of
\psi
is given by

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{HL}=\frac{e_{HL}(\psi)+e_{HL}(\psi^{-1})}{2},

the latter norm induces a right invariant distance

on
G_{\omega}(M)
given by

d_{HL}(\phi,\psi)=\|\phi\circ\psi^{-1}\|_{HL}\quad\forall\phi,\psi\in G_{\omega}(M).

The length
l^{(1,\infty)}_{\kappa,\mathcal{S}}
also induces a metric
D^{1}_{\kappa,\mathcal{S}}
on the
Iso(M,\omega)
given by:

D^{1}_{\kappa,\mathcal{S}}((U,\mathcal{H}),(V,\mathcal{K})):=\frac{D_{0}^{\kappa,\mathcal{S}}((U,\mathcal{H}),(V,\mathcal{K}))+D_{0}^{\kappa,\mathcal{S}}(\overline{(U,\mathcal{H})},\overline{(V,\mathcal{K})})}{2},\quad\forall\kappa>0,

where

D_{0}^{\kappa,\mathcal{S}}((U,\mathcal{H}),(V,\mathcal{K})):=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(osc(U_{t}-V_{t})+\kappa\|\mathcal{H}_{t}-\mathcal{K}_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\right)dt,

See
[
11
]
for more details on the Hofer-like metrics. In this paper, we will assume

.

2.2
From smooth to
C^{0}

C^{0}-
symplectic topology began with Eliashberg-Gromov rigidity theorem
[
8
,
9
]
which states that
Symp(M,\omega)
is
C^{0}-
closed in
Diff(M)
the group of diffeomorphisms of
M
. This inspired Oh-Müller
[
10
]
to define
Sympeo(M,\omega)
, the symplectic homeoeomorphism group as

\overline{Symp(M,\omega)}^{C^{0}}\subseteq Homeo(M),

where
Homeo(M)
is the group of homeomorphisms of
M
. The
C^{0}-
topology on
Homeo(M)
is the topology induced by the distance:

d_{0}(f,h)=\max(d_{C^{0}}(f,h),d_{C^{0}}(f^{-1},h^{-1})),\quad\forall f,h\in Homeo(M)

with
\displaystyle d_{C^{0}}(f,h)=\sup_{x\in M}d_{g}(h(x),f(x))
).

On the space of all continuous paths
\lambda:[0,1]\rightarrow Homeo(M)
such that
\lambda(0)=id_{M}
, the
C^{0}-
topology is the topology induced by the distance:

\displaystyle\bar{d}(\lambda,\mu)=\max_{t\in[0,1]}d_{0}(\lambda(t),\mu(t)).

The distance
\bar{d}
is neither left nor right invariant however, it satisfies the following:

Proposition 2.1
.

[
13
]

Let
\Theta
be a smooth isotopy of diffeomorphisms of
M
. Then there exists a constant
C^{\Theta}>0
depending on
\Theta
such that
for all paths
\Phi
,
\Psi
in
Homeo(M)
, we have

\bar{d}(\Phi\circ\Theta,\Psi\circ\Theta)\leqslant C^{\Theta}\bar{d}(\Phi,\Psi).

Given
\phi\in FSHomeo(M)
, define

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\widetilde{HL}}:=\liminf\limits_{i\longrightarrow\infty}\|\phi_{i}\|_{HL},

(2.9)

where the infimum is taken over all
(\phi_{i})_{i}\subset G_{\omega}(M)
such that
\phi_{i}\xrightarrow{C^{0}}\phi
.
The rule
\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{HL}}
defines a Hofer-like norm on
FSHomeo(M)
indeed:

1.

Positivity: clearly
\|\phi\|_{\widetilde{HL}}\geqslant 0
, for any
\phi\in FSHomeo(M)

2.

Non-degeneracy: Let
\phi\in FSHomeo(M)
and
(\phi_{i})_{i}\subset G_{\omega}(M)
such that
\phi_{i}\xrightarrow{C^{0}}\phi
. Assume

\displaystyle 0=\|\phi\|_{\widetilde{HL}}=\liminf\limits_{i\longrightarrow\infty}\|\phi_{i}\|_{HL}.

By definition of
\liminf\limits
, we have for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
, such that for all
(\phi_{i})_{i}\subseteq G_{\omega}(M)
, satisfying

, we have
\|\phi_{i}\|_{HL}-\epsilon<\|\phi\|_{\widetilde{HL}}
. By assumption,
\|\phi\|_{\widetilde{HL}}=0
, hence
\|\phi_{i}\|_{HL}-\epsilon<0
, i.e,

. Letting

tend to zero, we get that

, as

. Therefore,
\phi_{i}\xrightarrow{C^{0}}\phi
, and

, as

hence by Theorem

[

]
,we have that

.

3.

Triangle inequality: let
\phi
,

. By definition,

, and
\psi=\lim\limits_{C^{0}}\psi_{i}
, where

and

are sequences in

whose Hofer-like norms are bounded. By triangle inequality of

, one has

\displaystyle\|\phi_{i}\circ\psi_{i}\|_{HL}\leqslant\|\phi_{i}\|_{HL}+\|\psi_{i}\|_{HL}.

(2.10)

Taking the infimum over all

which

converges to

, on both sides of (

) we have

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{i\longrightarrow\infty}\|\phi_{i}\circ\psi_{i}\|_{HL}\leqslant\liminf\limits_{i\longrightarrow\infty}(\|\phi_{i}\|_{HL}+\|\psi_{i}\|_{HL})\leqslant\liminf\limits_{i\longrightarrow\infty}\|\phi_{i}\|_{HL}+\liminf\limits_{i\longrightarrow\infty}\|\phi_{i}\|_{HL}

The norm

induces a right invariant distance on

defined as:

\displaystyle\tilde{d}_{HL}(\psi,\phi)=\liminf\limits_{i\longrightarrow\infty}\|\phi\circ\psi^{-1}\|_{HL}

.

. Property

. For item

,

Automatic continuity for vector spaces with linear topology
Samuel Quirino, Lucas H. R. de Souza

Abstract
In this paper we classify all topological vector spaces with linear topology with the property that all algebraic automorphisms are continuous. Moreover, we prove some properties of these spaces.

Introduction

In
[
6
]
there is a classification of all topological spaces
X
with the property that every bijection from
X
to itself is continuous. These spaces are precisely the spaces with the co-
\kappa
topology, i.e. fixed the cardinal
\kappa
, the closed sets of the space are the sets with cardinality less than
\kappa
. Similarly, in Functional Analysis there is the concept of automatic continuity, i. e. when a given algebraic homomorphism between Banach algebras is already continuous (see
[
1
]
for the basics of this theory). For example, there is this strong result, due to Johnson, which says that if
f:A\rightarrow B
is a homomorphism of Banach algebras and
B
is semisimple, then
f
is continuous (Theorem 5.1.5 of
[
1
]
).

In this paper we consider topological vector spaces with linear topology, i.e. a topology where the neighborhood filter of
0
is generated by open vector subspaces. In the same spirit as the one given above, we classify all vector spaces with linear topology with the property that all algebraic automorphisms are continuous.

Let
V
be a vector space and
\kappa
be a cardinal with
\kappa=1
or
\kappa\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. The
\kappa
-codimension topology of
V
is the topology induced by the filter generated by all vector subspaces with codimension less than
\kappa
. With this topology,
V
is a topological vector space and the topology, by construction, is linear. We say that
V
has codimension topology if it has the
\kappa
-codimension topology for some cardinal
\kappa
. Let
Aut(V)
be the set of algebraic automorphisms of
V
(i.e. isomorphisms as vector spaces) and
CAut(V)
is its subset given by continuous maps. Then we have our main result:

​​​​

Theorem
2.12

Let
V
be a topological vector space with linear topology. Then
V
has a codimension topology if and only if
Aut(V)=CAut(V)
.

This theorem is analogous to Theorem 1 of
[
6
]
. If
V
and
V^{\prime}
are topological vector spaces, then we define
Hom(V,V^{\prime})
as the set of linear maps from
V
to
V^{\prime}
and
CHom(V,V^{\prime})
the set of continuous linear maps from
V
to
V^{\prime}
. We proved also the following two propositions:

​​​​

Proposition
2.13

Let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be topological vector spaces with linear topology such that
V
has the
\kappa
-codimension topology. If
dim\ V^{\prime}<\kappa
, then

CHom(V,V^{\prime})=Hom(V,V^{\prime})
.

​​​​

Proposition
2.14

Let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be topological vector spaces such that
V
and
V^{\prime}
have the
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
-codimension topologies, respectively. If
\kappa^{\prime}\leqslant\kappa
, then
CHom(V,V^{\prime})=Hom(V,V^{\prime})
.

Let
V=V^{\prime}
and both spaces endowed with the
\kappa
-codimension topology. Then, by
Proposition
2.14

CHom(V,V)=Hom(V,V)
. This phenomenon is different from the one in topological spaces, where the spaces with the property that all maps from
X
to itself are continuous maps are the discrete and the trivial spaces (Corollary 1 of
[
6
]
).

Also, we show a series of properties of these topological vector spaces. With this, we construct an arbitrarily big family of topological vector spaces with linear topology which are all non isomorphic as topological vector spaces and with the property that all of them have the same profinite-dimensional completion and the same dual space (
Corollary
3.11
).

At the end, we consider analogous versions of Johnson’s Theorem for topological algebras with linear topology. We get that, in general, such statement fails (
Example
4.2
) and if we restrict it to pseudocompact algebras, then the statement also fails (
Example
4.4
).

1
Preliminaries

1.1
Linear algebra and cardinals

If
V
is a
k
-vector space and
W<V
, then the codimension of
W
in
V
is given by
dim\ V/W
and denoted as
codim_{V}W
. If the ambient space is clear from its context, then we denote it simply as
codim\ W
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
V
be a vector space,
W,W^{\prime}<V
and
\kappa
a cardinal number satisfying
\kappa=1
or
\kappa\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. If
codim\ W<\kappa
and
codim\ W^{\prime}<\kappa
, then
codim\ W\cap W^{\prime}<\kappa
.

Remark 1.1
.

This result certainly is well known. At least for the case
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
, it appears as Exercise 13 of page 91 of
[
5
]
. However, the standard proof does not depend on
\kappa
, if
\kappa\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. We write its proof down here, for the sake of completeness.

Proof.

For
\kappa=1
the result is immediate. Suppose that
\kappa\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. We have that
dim\ (W+W^{\prime})/W^{\prime}\leqslant dim\ V/W^{\prime}=codim_{V}W^{\prime}<\kappa
. One of the Isomorphism Theorems says that

(W+W^{\prime})/W^{\prime}\cong W/(W\cap W^{\prime}).

So
dim\ W/(W\cap W^{\prime})<\kappa
. Another Isomorphism Theorem gives us that

V/W\cong(V/W\cap W^{\prime})/(W/W\cap W^{\prime}),

which implies that
V/W\cap W^{\prime}\cong(V/W)\oplus(W/W\cap W^{\prime})
and then

dim\ (V/W\cap W^{\prime})=dim\ (V/W)+dim\ (W/W\cap W^{\prime}).

So
codim_{V}W\cap W^{\prime}=dim\ (V/W\cap W^{\prime})=dim\ (V/W)+dim\ (W/W\cap W^{\prime})<\kappa+\kappa=\kappa
.
∎

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
f:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
be a linear map,
\kappa
a cardinal and
W^{\prime}<V
such that
codim\ W^{\prime}<\kappa
. Then
codim\ f^{-1}(W)<\kappa
.

Proof.

Take
\pi:V^{\prime}\rightarrow V^{\prime}/W^{\prime}
as the quotient map. Then
\ker\ \pi\circ f=f^{-1}(W^{\prime})
. Then the First Isomorphism Theorem gives us that there is an injective homomorphism
V/f^{-1}(W^{\prime})\rightarrow V^{\prime}/W^{\prime}
, which implies that
codim\ f^{-1}(W^{\prime})=dim\ V/f^{-1}(W^{\prime})\leqslant dim\ V^{\prime}/W^{\prime}=codim\ W^{\prime}<\kappa
.
∎

Here we establish a notation: if
\kappa
is a cardinal, then
\kappa^{+}
is its cardinal successor. In addition, any subspace of a vector space means a vector subspace. We say subset if it may not have structure of vector subspace.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
V
be a topological vector space. We say that

has linear topology if the neighborhood filter of

is generated by open vector subspaces of

.

Definition 1.5
.

Let

be a topological vector space with linear topology. We say that

is linearly compact if it is an inverse limit of finite dimensional vector spaces, with the topology given by the inverse limit.

Proposition 24.4 of
[

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
V
be an infinite dimensional linearly compact vector space. Then there exists a subspace
W<V
such that
W
is not open in

and
codim\ W=1
.

Remark 1.2
.

This proposition is an analogous version of the construction given in page 5 of
[
3
]
for pseudocompact algebras and its proof is essentially the same. We write it down here for the sake of completeness.

Proof.

Proposition 24.4 of
[
2
]
says that
V
is isomorphic, as topological vector spaces, to
\prod_{i\in\Gamma}k
, for some infinite
\Gamma
. Then the subspace given by
\bigoplus_{i\in\Gamma}k
is dense on
\prod_{i\in\Gamma}k
. Take
W
a subspace of
\prod_{i\in\Gamma}k
such that
\bigoplus_{i\in\Gamma}k<W
and

. Then
W
is also open in
\prod_{i\in\Gamma}k
. So

cannot be open in
\prod_{i\in\Gamma}k
, since any open subspace must also be closed (Corollary 2.3 of
[

∎

2
Codimension topology

Definition 2.1
.

Let
V
be a vector space and
\kappa
a cardinal, with
\kappa=1
or
\kappa\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. The
\kappa
-codimension topology of
V
is the topology whose neighborhood filter of the point
0
is generated by the set
\{W<V:codim\ W<\kappa\}
. We denote by
\tau_{V,\kappa}
such topology. We say that
V
has codimension topology if it has the
\kappa
-codimension topology for some cardinal
\kappa
.

By
Proposition
1.1
, the set
\{W<V:codim\ W<\kappa\}
is closed by intersections, which implies that it is a base of a neighborhood filter of the point
0\in V
that generates a topology such that
V
is a topological vector space with linear topology. Note that
\kappa
cannot be finite, except if it is equal to
1
, since we do not have control on the codimension of the intersections. For example, if
V
has a basis given by
\{b_{1},b_{2}\}
, then
\langle b_{1}\rangle
and
\langle b_{2}\rangle
have codimension
1
in
V
, but
0=\langle b_{1}\rangle\cap\langle b_{2}\rangle
has codimension
2
.

We have two special cases: the
1
-codimension topology is the trivial topology and the
\kappa
-codimension topology, with
\kappa\geqslant dim\ V
is the discrete topology.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
V
be a vector space and
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}
cardinals such that
\kappa\leqslant\kappa^{\prime}\leqslant dim\ V
. Then
\tau_{V,\kappa}
is coarser than
\tau_{V,\kappa^{\prime}}
. Moreover, if
\kappa\neq\kappa^{\prime}
, then the spaces
(V,\tau_{V,\kappa})
and
(V,\tau_{V,\kappa^{\prime}})
are not isomorphic as topological vector spaces.

Proof.

We have that
\{W<V:codim\ W<\kappa\}\subseteq\{W<V:codim\ W<\kappa^{\prime}\}
, which implies that
\tau_{V,\kappa}
is coarser than
\tau_{V,\kappa^{\prime}}
.

Let
f:(V,\tau_{V,\kappa})\rightarrow(V,\tau_{V,\kappa^{\prime}})
be an isomorphism of topological vector spaces. Let
W^{\prime}<V
such that
\kappa\leqslant codim\ W^{\prime}<\kappa^{\prime}
. Then
f^{-1}(W^{\prime})\in\tau_{V,\kappa}
. So there exists
W\in\tau_{V,\kappa}
such that
codim\ W<\kappa
and
W<f^{-1}(W^{\prime})
, which implies that
codim\ W^{\prime}=codim\ f^{-1}(W^{\prime})<\kappa
, contradicting the definition of
W^{\prime}
. Thus
(V,\tau_{V,\kappa})
and
(V,\tau_{V,\kappa^{\prime}})
are not isomorphic as topological vector spaces.
∎

From now on until
Theorem
2.12
, we consider another property of topological vector spaces: let
V
be a topological vector space with linear topology such that
Aut(V)=CAut(V)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
W
be an open subspace of
V
. If
W^{\prime}<V
such that
dim\ W^{\prime}=dim\ W
and
codim\ W^{\prime}=codim\ W
, then
W^{\prime}
is open in
V
.

Proof.

Let
U,U^{\prime}<V
such that we can decompose
V
as the inner direct sums
V=W\oplus U=W^{\prime}\oplus U^{\prime}
. We have that
codim\ W=dim\ U
and
codim\ W^{\prime}=dim\ U^{\prime}
. Since
codim\ W=codim\ W^{\prime}
, then
dim\ U=dim\ U^{\prime}
. Let then
f:W^{\prime}\rightarrow W
and
g:U^{\prime}\rightarrow U
be isomorphisms. These maps induce an isomorphism
h:V\rightarrow V
such that
h(W^{\prime})=W
. The map
h
is continuous by hypothesis and
W
is open in
V
, which implies that
W^{\prime}=h^{-1}(W)
is also open in
V
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
W
be an open subspace of
V
such that
dim\ W<dim\ V
. If
W^{\prime}<V
such that
dim\ W^{\prime}=dim\ W
, then
W^{\prime}
is open in
V
.

Proof.

Suppose that
dim\ V<\aleph_{0}
. We have that
dim\ V=dim\ W+codim\ W=dim\ W^{\prime}+codim\ W^{\prime}
and
dim\ W^{\prime}=dim\ W
, which implies that
codim\ W^{\prime}=codim\ W
. Since
W
is open in
V
, then, by
Lemma
2.3
,
W^{\prime}
is open in
V
.

Suppose that
dim\ V\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. We have that
dim\ V=dim\ W+codim\ W=\max\{dim\ W,codim\ W\}
(because
dim\ V\geqslant\aleph_{0}
). Since
dim\ W<dim\ V
, then
codim\ W=dim\ V
. Analogously,
codim\ W^{\prime}=dim\ V
. So
dim\ W^{\prime}=dim\ W
and
codim\ W^{\prime}=codim\ W
, which implies, by
Lemma
2.3
, that
W^{\prime}
is open in
V
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
W
be an open subspace of
V
such that
codim\ W<dim\ V
. If
W^{\prime}<V
such that
codim\ W^{\prime}=codim\ W
, then
W^{\prime}
is open in
V
.

Proof.

Analogous to the proof of
Lemma
2.4
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
W
be an open subspace of
V
such that
dim\ W<dim\ V
. If
W^{\prime}<V
such that
codim\ W^{\prime}\leqslant codim\ W
, then
W^{\prime}
is open in
V
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a basis of
V
such that
B\cap W
is a basis of
W
. Let
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
such that
B\cap W\subseteq B^{\prime}
and
\#B\setminus B^{\prime}=codim\ W^{\prime}
(this is possible since
codim\ W^{\prime}\leqslant codim\ W
). Take
W^{\prime\prime}=\langle B^{\prime}\rangle
. Since
W<W^{\prime\prime}
and
W
is open in
V
, then
W^{\prime\prime}
has non-empty interior, which implies, by Corollary 2.3 of
[
8
]
, that
W^{\prime\prime}
is open in
V
. We have that
codim\ W^{\prime\prime}=codim\ W^{\prime}
, which implies, by
Lemma
2.5
, that
W^{\prime}
is open in
V
.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

Let
V
be a topological vector space. We define
\zeta(V)=\sup\{\kappa:\exists W<V,W\text{ is open in $V$, }codim\ W=\kappa\}
.

Remark 2.1
.

We are considering just vector spaces with linear topology. So, the neighborhood filter in
V
of the point
0
,
\mathcal{N}(0)
, is generated by the set
\{W<V:W\text{ is open in $V$ and }codim\ W\leqslant\zeta(V)\}
.

The next propositions show what happens with the space
V
, for different values of
\zeta(V)
.

Proposition 2.8
.

\zeta(V)=0
or
\zeta(V)\geqslant\aleph_{0}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\zeta(V)=n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
n>0
. Then there exists an open subspace
W
such that
codim\ W=n
. Let
B
be a basis of
V
such that the set
B^{\prime}=B\cap W
is a basis of
W
. Note that
\#B\setminus B^{\prime}=n
since
codim\ W=n
. Take
b\in B\setminus B^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
. Then we define
W^{\prime}=\langle(B^{\prime}\setminus\{b^{\prime}\})\cup\{b\}\rangle
. We have that
dim\ W^{\prime}=dim\ W
and
codim\ W^{\prime}=codim\ W
, which implies, by
Lemma
2.3
, that
W^{\prime}
is open in
V
. Then
W\cap W^{\prime}
is an open subspace of
V
such that
codim\ W\cap W^{\prime}>n
, since
W\cap W^{\prime}
is properly contained in
W
. This contradicts the fact that
\zeta(V)=n
.

Thus
\zeta(V)=0
or
\zeta(V)\geqslant\aleph_{0}
.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

If
\zeta(V)=0
, then
V
has the trivial topology.

Proof.

Let
U\in\mathcal{N}(0)
. Then there exists an open subspace
W
such that
W\subseteq U
. Since
\zeta(V)=0
,
codim\ W=0
, which implies that
W=U=V
. So
\mathcal{N}(0)=\{V\}
, which implies that
V
has the trivial topology.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

If there is no open subspace of
V
with codimension
\zeta(V)
, then
V
has the
\zeta(V)
-codimension topology.

Proof.

Let
\kappa<\zeta(V)
. Then there exists a cardinal
\kappa^{\prime}
such that
\kappa\leqslant\kappa^{\prime}<\zeta(V)
and there exists an open subspace
W<V
with
codim\ W=\kappa^{\prime}
. By
Lemma
2.6
, for every
W^{\prime}<V
with
codim\ W^{\prime}=\kappa
,
W^{\prime}
is open in
V
. So we have that

{\{W\!<\!V:W\text{ is open in $V$, }codim\ W\leqslant\zeta(V)\}\!=\!\{W\!<\!V:codim\ W<\zeta(V)\}}
. This implies that
V
has the
\zeta(V)
-codimension topology.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

If there exists an open subspace of
V
with codimension
\zeta(V)
, then
V
has the
\zeta(V)^{+}
-codimension topology.

Proof.

Suppose that
\zeta(V)<dim\ V
. Let
\kappa\leqslant\zeta(V)
. Then there exists a cardinal
\kappa^{\prime}
such that
\kappa\leqslant\kappa^{\prime}\leqslant\zeta(V)<dim\ V
and there exists an open subspace
W<V
with
codim\ W=\kappa^{\prime}
. By
Lemma
2.6
, for every
W^{\prime}<V
with
codim\ W^{\prime}=\kappa
,
W^{\prime}
is open in
V
. Then we have that
\{W<V:W\text{ is open in $V$, }codim\ W\leqslant\zeta(V)\}=\{W<V:codim\ W<\zeta(V)^{+}\}
, which implies that
V
has the
\zeta(V)^{+}
-codimension topology.

Suppose that
\zeta(V)=dim\ V
. Then there exists
W<V
, open in
V
, such that
codim\ W=\zeta(V)=dim\ V
. Let
B
be a basis of
W
and
B^{\prime}
a set that is disjoint to
B
and such that
B\cup B^{\prime}
is a basis of
V
. We have that
\#B^{\prime}=codim\ W=dim\ V
and
\#B\leqslant\#B^{\prime}
. Take
B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B^{\prime}
such that
\#B^{\prime\prime}=\#B
and take
W^{\prime\prime}=\langle B^{\prime\prime}\rangle
. We have that
dim\ W^{\prime\prime}=dim\ W
and
codim\ W^{\prime\prime}=codim\ W
. So, by
Lemma
2.3
, it follows that
W^{\prime\prime}
is open in
V
, which implies that
0=W\cap W^{\prime\prime}
is also open in
V
. Then
V
is discrete, which means that
V
has the
\zeta(V)^{+}
-codimension topology.
∎

Summarizing the above propositions, we get the following theorem:

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
V
be a topological vector space with linear topology. Then
V
has a codimension topology if and only if
Aut(V)=CAut(V)
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
It is immediate from the fact that isomorphisms preserve the codimension of its subspaces.

(\Leftarrow)
We know, by
Proposition
2.8
, that
\zeta(V)=0
or
\zeta(V)\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. If
\zeta(V)=0
, then
V
has
1
-codimension topology, by
Proposition
2.9
. So we can suppose that
\zeta(V)\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. If there is no open subspace of
V
with codimension
\zeta(V)
, then, by
Proposition
2.10
,
V
has the
\zeta(V)
-codimension topology. If there exists an open subspace of
V
with codimension
\zeta(V)
, then, by
Proposition
2.11
,
V
has the
\zeta(V)^{+}
-codimension topology.
∎

There are some cases where every linear map is continuous, as the following propositions:

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be topological vector spaces with linear topology such that
V
has the
\kappa
-codimension topology. If
dim\ V^{\prime}<\kappa
, then

CHom(V,V^{\prime})=Hom(V,V^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in Hom(V,V^{\prime})
. First we have that
V/f^{-1}(0)
is isomorphic (as vector spaces) to a subspace of
V^{\prime}
, which implies that
codim\ f^{-1}(0)=dim\ V/f^{-1}(0)\leqslant dim\ V^{\prime}<\kappa
.

Let
W^{\prime}<V^{\prime}
. Then
f^{-1}(W)>f^{-1}(0)
, which implies that
codim\ f^{-1}(W)\leqslant codim\ f^{-1}(0)<\kappa
and then
f^{-1}(W)
is open in
V
. Since
V^{\prime}
has linear topology, the set of open subspaces generates the neighborhood filter of
0
in
V^{\prime}
, which implies that
f
is a continuous map (since any preimage of a subspace is open).
∎

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be topological vector spaces such that
V
and
V^{\prime}
have the
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
-codimension topologies, respectively. If
\kappa^{\prime}\leqslant\kappa
, then

CHom(V,V^{\prime})=Hom(V,V^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in Hom(V,V^{\prime})
. Let
W^{\prime}
be an open subspace of
V^{\prime}
. Then
codim\ W^{\prime}<\kappa^{\prime}
. By
Proposition
1.2
,
codim\ f^{-1}(W^{\prime})<\kappa^{\prime}\leqslant\kappa
. So
f^{-1}(W^{\prime})
is open in
V
. Thus
f
is continuous.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
Theorem
2.12
is an analogous version of Theorem 1 of
[
6
]
for topological vector spaces with linear topology. However, when we consider the whole set of homomorphisms instead of isomorphisms, the same analogy does not hold. For topological spaces we get that the trivial and the discrete spaces are the only ones where all maps to itself are continuous, Corollary 1 of
[
6
]
, but, as a special case of
Proposition
2.14
, we get that all vector spaces with a codimension topology have the property that all linear maps to itself are continuous.

3
Some properties of vector spaces with

codimension topology

Here we present some properties of vector spaces with codimension topology.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
V
be a vector space with
\kappa
-codimension topology, with
\kappa>1
, and
B
a basis of
V
. Then
B
, with its subspace topology, is discrete.

Proof.

Let
b\in B
. Take
W=\langle B\setminus\{b\}\rangle
. We have that
codim\ W=1<\kappa
, which implies that
W
is an open subspace of
V
. But open subspaces are also closed (Corollary 2.3 of
[
8
]
), which implies that
W
is closed in
V
. Then
B\setminus\{b\}=W\cap B
is a closed subset of
B
, which implies that
\{b\}
is an open subset of
B
. Thus
B
is discrete.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Maybe it is surprising the fact that the subspace topology of
B
is not the
co-\kappa
topology, but
B
has an uniform structure induced by the uniform structure of the topological vector space
V
and co-cardinal spaces are not uniformizable in general. In fact, the cofinite topology is
T_{1}
, is not Hausdorff (Counterexample 19 of
[
7
]
) and every
T_{1}
uniform space is Hausdorff, which implies that a set with the cofinite topology cannot be uniformizable.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
V
be a topological space with
\kappa
-codimension topology and
\kappa>1
. If
W<V
, then
W
is closed.

Proof.

We have that
W=\bigcap\{W^{\prime}<V:W<W^{\prime},codim\ W^{\prime}=1\}
. Since
\kappa>1
, all codimension
1
subspaces are closed, which implies that
W
is closed as well.
∎

As a consequence, we get:

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
V
be a topological space with
\kappa
-codimension topology and
\kappa>1
. Then
V
is Hausdorff.

Proof.

By the last proposition,
0
is closed in
V
, which implies that
V
is a Hausdorff space (Theorem 1.7 of
[
8
]
).
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
V
be a topological space with the
\kappa
-codimension topology and
W<V
. Then
W
has the
\kappa^{\prime}
-codimension topology, for some
\kappa^{\prime}\geqslant\kappa
.

Proof.

Let
f:W\rightarrow W
be an isomorphism. Then we can extend
f
to an isomorphism
f^{\prime}:V\rightarrow V
, which is continuous since
V
has codimension topology. So
f
is continuous, which implies that
W
has the
\kappa^{\prime}
-codimension topology for some cardinal
\kappa^{\prime}
.

Let
Z<W
with
codim_{W}Z<\kappa
and
W^{\prime}<V
such that
V=W\oplus W^{\prime}
as vector spaces. Then
codim_{V}\ Z\oplus W^{\prime}=codim_{W}Z<\kappa
, which implies that
Z\oplus W^{\prime}
is open in
V
and then
Z=(Z\oplus W^{\prime})\cap W
is open in
W
. So
\kappa\leqslant\kappa^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be infinite dimensional vector spaces with
\kappa
-codimension topology, where
\kappa>1
. Then
V\oplus V^{\prime}
, with the coproduct topology, is isomorphic to
V\oplus V^{\prime}
with the
\kappa
-codimension topology, as topological vector spaces.

Remark 3.2
.

The coproduct
V\oplus V^{\prime}
and the product
V\times V^{\prime}
are isomorphic
as vector spaces and also they have the same topology (page 105 of
[
2
]
).

Proof.

Consider
V\oplus V^{\prime}
with the coproduct topology. Let
f:V\oplus V^{\prime}\rightarrow V\oplus V^{\prime}
be a linear map. Then there exists linear maps
f_{V}:V\rightarrow V\oplus V^{\prime}
and
{f_{V^{\prime}}:V^{\prime}\rightarrow V\oplus V^{\prime}}
that induce
f
. We can decompose
f_{V^{\prime}}=f_{V^{\prime},V}+f_{V^{\prime},V^{\prime}}
, where
f_{V^{\prime},V}:V^{\prime}\rightarrow V\oplus V^{\prime}
has its image contained in
V\oplus 0
and the map
f_{V^{\prime},V^{\prime}}:V^{\prime}\rightarrow V\oplus V^{\prime}
has its image contained in
0\oplus V^{\prime}
. By
Proposition
2.14
and the fact that
V\oplus 0
is isomorphic to
V
and
0\oplus V^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
V^{\prime}
as topological vector spaces, then the maps
f_{V^{\prime},V}
and
f_{V^{\prime},V^{\prime}}
are continuous, which implies that
f_{V^{\prime}}
is continuous. Analogously, we have that
f_{V}
is continuous. So, by the universal property of the coproduct topology, we have that
f
is continuous. So
V\oplus V^{\prime}
has the
\kappa^{\prime}
-codimension topology, for some cardinal
\kappa^{\prime}
.

Since
V\oplus 0
is a subspace of
V
with the
\kappa
-codimension topology, then, by
Lemma
3.4
,
\kappa^{\prime}\leqslant\kappa
. Note that
\aleph_{0}\leqslant\kappa^{\prime}
, since
V\oplus V^{\prime}
is a Hausdorff space. Suppose that
\kappa^{\prime}<\kappa
. Then take
Z<V
and
Z^{\prime}<V^{\prime}
such that
codim_{V}Z=codim_{V^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}=\kappa^{\prime}
. Then
Z
is open in
V
and
Z^{\prime}
is open in
V^{\prime}
, which implies that
Z\oplus Z^{\prime}
is open in
V\oplus V^{\prime}
(since the coproduct topology is equal to the product topology). We also have that
codim_{V\oplus V^{\prime}}Z\oplus Z^{\prime}=codim_{V}Z+codim_{V^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}=\kappa^{\prime}+\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa^{\prime}
, which contradict the fact that
V\oplus V^{\prime}
has the
\kappa^{\prime}
-codimension topology. So
\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
V
be an infinite dimension vector space endowed with the
\kappa
-codimension topology, where
\kappa>1
. Then there exists a topological decomposition
V=Z\oplus Z^{\prime}
, where
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are topologically isomorphic to
V
.

Proof.

By the last proposition,
V\oplus V
is isomorphic to
V
, as topological vector spaces. From this fact, the corollary is immediate.
∎

Now we are able to improve
Lemma
3.4
:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
V
be a topological space with the
\kappa
-codimension topology and
W<V
. Then
W
also has the
\kappa
-codimension topology.

Proof.

By
Lemma
3.4
, we have that W has the
\kappa^{\prime}
-codimension topology, for some cardinal
\kappa^{\prime}\geqslant\kappa
.

Suppose that
codim_{V}W<\kappa
. Then
W
is open in
V
. Let
Z
be an open subspace of
W
. Then
Z
is open in
V
, which implies that
codim_{V}Z<\kappa
. Then
codim_{W}Z\leqslant codim_{V}Z<\kappa
. So
\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa
.

Suppose that
dim\ W<\kappa
. Since
W
has the
\kappa^{\prime}
-codimension topology and
\kappa<\kappa^{\prime}
, then
W
is discrete, which implies that
W
also has the
\kappa
-codimension topology.

Suppose that
dim\ W=\kappa
and
codim_{V}W=\kappa
. Then there exists
W^{\prime}<V
such that
V=W\oplus W^{\prime}
as vector spaces and
dim\ W^{\prime}=\kappa
. By the last corollary, there exists
Z<V
and
Z^{\prime}<V
such that
V=Z\oplus Z^{\prime}
and
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are topologically isomorphic to
V
. We have that
dim\ V=dim\ W=dim\ Z=dim\ W^{\prime}=dim\ Z^{\prime}
, which implies that there exists an isomorphism
f:V\rightarrow V
, which is a homeomorphism, such that
f(W)=Z
and
f(W^{\prime})=Z^{\prime}
. So
W
is isomorphic to
Z
as topological vector spaces and
Z
is isomorphic to
V
as topological vector spaces, which implies that
W
is isomorphic to
V
as topological vector spaces. Since
V
has the
\kappa
-codimension topology, then
W
has the
\kappa
-codimension topology.
∎

As a special case, we have:

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
V
be a topological space with the
\kappa
-codimension topology and
W<V
. If
dim\ W<\kappa
, then
W
is discrete.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
V
be a topological vector space with
\kappa
-codimension topology and
dim\ V\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. Then
V
is not linearly compact.

Proof.

Every infinite dimensional linearly compact space has subspaces with codimension
1
that are not open (by
Proposition
1.6
), which implies that
V
is not linearly compact, for every
\kappa\neq 1
. If
\kappa=1
, then
V
is not linearly compact either, since linearly compact spaces are Hausdorff and
V
is not.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
V
be a topological space with
\aleph_{0}
-codimension topology and
dim\ V\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. Then the Cauchy completion
\hat{V}
is linearly compact.

Proof.

We have that
\hat{V}\cong\lim\limits_{\longleftarrow}\{V/W:W<V\text{ is open}\}
. But
\{V/W:W\text{ is open}\}=\{V/W:codim\ W<\aleph_{0}\}
, which implies that
\hat{V}
is an inverse limit of finite dimensional vector spaces, which implies that it is linearly compact.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
V
be a vector space. The family
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{\kappa}=(V,\tau_{V,\kappa}):\aleph_{0}\leqslant\kappa\leqslant dim\ V^{+}\}
satisfies the following properties:

1.

If
\aleph_{0}\leqslant\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}\leqslant dim\ V^{+}
and
\kappa\neq\kappa^{\prime}
, then
V_{\kappa}
and
V_{\kappa^{\prime}}
are not isomorphic as topological vector space.

2.

If
\aleph_{0}\leqslant\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}\leqslant dim\ V^{+}
, then the profinite-dimensional completion of
V_{\kappa}
and the profinite-dimensional completion of
V_{\kappa^{\prime}}
are isomorphic as topological vector spaces.

3.

If
\aleph_{0}\leqslant\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}\leqslant dim\ V^{+}
, then the dual spaces
V_{\kappa}^{\circledast}
and
V_{\kappa^{\prime}}^{\circledast}
are isomorphic as topological vector spaces.

Remark 3.3
.

So we can construct arbitrarily big families satisfying these three properties.

Proof.

The spaces of
\mathcal{V}
are pairwise non isomorphic as topological vector spaces, by
Proposition
2.2
.

We have that the profinite-dimensional completion of
V_{\kappa}
is given by the inverse limit
\lim\limits_{\longleftarrow}\{V/W:W<V,codim\ W<\aleph_{0}\}
, since all subspaces with finite codimension are closed in
V_{\kappa}
. Note that
V/W
is discrete, since all subspaces with finite codimension are also open in
V
. So, this inverse limit does not depend on the choice of
\kappa
, which implies that the profinite-dimensional completions of all
V_{\kappa}
are isomorphic.

As vector spaces,
V_{\kappa}^{\circledast}=CHom(V_{\kappa},k)
. By
Proposition
2.13
, we have that
CHom(V_{\kappa},k)=Hom(V_{\kappa},k)
, which implies that the dual, as a vector space, does not depend on the choice of
\kappa
. The topology of
V_{\kappa}^{\circledast}
is generated by the set
\{W^{\perp}:W<V,W\text{ is linearly compact}\}
. By
Proposition
3.7
, all subspaces of
V
also have the
\kappa
-codimension topology and, by
Proposition
3.9
these subspaces cannot be linearly compact, unless they have finite dimension, which implies that
\{W^{\perp}:W<V,W\text{ is linearly compact}\}=\{W^{\perp}:W<V,dim\ W<\aleph_{0}\}
. Thus the topology of
V_{\kappa}^{\circledast}
also does not depend of
\kappa
.
∎

As a consequence, we get:

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
V
be a vector space with the
\kappa
-codimension topology, where
\kappa\neq 1
. Then
V^{\circledast}
is linearly compact.

Proof.

Let
V^{\prime}
be the same vector space of
V
but with the
\kappa^{\prime}
-codimension topology, where
\kappa^{\prime}=dim\ V^{+}
. By the last proposition we have that
V^{\circledast}
is isomorphic, as topological vector spaces, to
V^{\prime\circledast}
. But
V^{\prime}
is discrete, which implies that
V^{\prime\circledast}
is linearly compact (Proposition 24.4 of
[
2
]
). Thus
V^{\circledast}
is linearly compact.
∎

4
Some remarks about algebras

In Functional Analysis there is the following famous result:

Proposition 4.1
.

(Johnson, Theorem 5.1.5 of
[
1
]
) Let
A
and
B
be Banach algebras such that
B
is semisimple. If
f:A\rightarrow B
is an epimorphism of algebras, then
f
is continuous.

However, the result above does not hold for the class of topological algebras with linear topology:

Example 4.2
.

Let
A
be a non-discrete algebra with linear topology and
A_{dis}
the same algebra with the discrete topology. Then the identity map
{id:A\rightarrow A_{dis}}
is an epimorphism of algebras that is not continuous.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
k
be a field, treated as a discrete topological ring. A pseudocompact
k
-algebra is a complete Hausdorff topological algebra possessing a fundamental system of neighborhoods of 0 consisting of two sided ideals with finite codimension that intersect in 0.

If we consider only pseudocompact algebras, then Johnson’s Theorem also does not hold:

Example 4.4
.

If
A
is a pseudocompact algebra and
I
is a maximal ideal that is dense on
A
and
codim\ I=1
(see page 5 of
[
3
]
for an example), then
A/I
is isomorphic to
k
as vector spaces. Let
\pi:A\rightarrow k
be an algebra homomorphism such that
\ker\ \pi=I
and where
k
is discrete. We have that
\pi
is an epimorphism and
k
is simple, but this map is not continuous since
0
is open in
k
but
I=\pi^{-1}(0)
is not open in
A
.

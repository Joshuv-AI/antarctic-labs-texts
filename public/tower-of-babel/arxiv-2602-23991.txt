\AtlasTitle

Combination of ATLAS and CMS searches for Higgs boson pair production at
\sqrt{s}=13
TeV

\AtlasAbstract
This Letter presents a combination of searches for Higgs boson pair (HH) production performed by the ATLAS and CMS Collaborations using proton–proton collision data sets recorded at
\sqrt{s}=13
TeV during the Large Hadron Collider Run 2, corresponding to integrated luminosities ranging between 126 and 140
\mathrm{fb^{-1}}
.
The upper limit at the 95% confidence level on the total HH signal strength, defined as the ratio of the measured cross section to the SM prediction, corresponds to 2.5, with an expected value of 1.7 (2.8) assuming the absence (presence) of the standard model (SM) HH signal.
The strength of the HH signal is measured to be

0.8^{+0.9}_{-0.7}

relative to the SM prediction.
The observed significance is found to be 1.1 standard deviations whereas 1.3 are expected for the SM HH signal.
Constraints are set on the Higgs boson trilinear self-coupling and on the couplings of two Higgs bosons to two vector bosons, both normalized to the SM predictions and denoted as
\kappa_{\lambda}
and
\kappa_{2\mathrm{V}}
, respectively.
The observed individual constraints at the 95% confidence level are

-0.71<\kappa_{\lambda}<6.1
and

0.73<\kappa_{2{\mathup{{{V}}}}}<1.3
,
while the expected constraints assuming the presence of the SM HH signal are

-1.3<\kappa_{\lambda}<6.7
and

0.66<\kappa_{2\mathrm{V}}<1.4
.

The standard model of particle physics (SM)
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
includes a doublet of complex scalar fields,
\Phi
, that is subject to a potential which is symmetric under the
SU(2)\times U(1)
electroweak gauge group.
This potential has the form

V(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)=-\mu^{2}\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi+\lambda(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}
, with parameters
\mu^{2}>0
and
\lambda>0
.
In the mechanism postulated by Brout, Englert, Higgs, and others
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
, the electroweak symmetry is spontaneously broken when
\Phi
acquires a non-zero vacuum expectation value
v=|\mu|/\sqrt{\lambda}\approx 246\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
, giving rise to the longitudinal polarizations of the
{\mathup{{{W}}}}
and
{\mathup{{{Z}}}}
vector bosons and resulting in a physical scalar field
h
whose associated quantum excitations are Higgs bosons (
\mathup{{{H}}}
).
In the SM, the Higgs boson mass
m_{\mathup{{{H}}}}
depends on the curvature of the potential,
m_{\mathup{{{H}}}}^{2}=2\lambda v^{2}
.
The SM also predicts the existence of a Higgs boson trilinear self-coupling (
{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}
) in the form
\lambda vh^{3}
and of a coupling between two Higgs bosons and two vector bosons (
{\mathup{{{V}}}}{\mathup{{{V}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}
, with
{\mathup{{{V}}}}={\mathup{{{W}}}},{\mathup{{{Z}}}}
), whose strength depends on
(m_{\mathup{{{V}}}}/v)^{2}
, where
m_{\mathup{{{V}}}}
is the
{\mathup{{{V}}}}
boson mass, and is directly proportional to the strength of the coupling between one Higgs and two vector bosons (
{\mathup{{{V}}}}{\mathup{{{V}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}
).
The spontaneous breaking of the electroweak symmetry and the resulting properties of the scalar sector are thus determined by the form of the potential via the value of
\lambda
.

The existence of the Higgs boson was established by the ATLAS and CMS Collaborations in 2012
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
Since then, all measurements performed to characterize its properties have yielded observations compatible with the SM predictions
[
11
,
12
]
.
These measurements inform us about the behavior of the
h
field near the minimum of the potential.
However, the global structure of the potential, which in the SM is controlled by the value of
\lambda
, is largely unknown.
Its study represents one of the main goals of the CERN Large Hadron Collider (LHC)
[
13
]
physics program.

The Higgs boson trilinear self-coupling, whose strength depends on
\lambda
, can be directly measured through the production of Higgs boson pairs (
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
). In the SM, the leading
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production mode in proton–proton (
{\mathup{{{p}}}}{\mathup{{{p}}}}
) collisions at the LHC is gluon fusion (ggF), with a cross section of

\sigma^{\text{ggF}}_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}=30.8^{+2.0}_{-7.1}\mbox{$\,\text{fb}$}

[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
, followed by vector boson fusion (VBF) with a cross section
\sigma^{\text{VBF}}_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}=1.687\pm 0.046\mbox{$\,\text{fb}$}

[
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
. Both predictions are computed for a center-of-mass energy of 13
TeV
and for
m_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}}=125\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
, using the PDF4LHC21 parton distribution function (PDF) set
[
28
]
.
These processes exhibit a quadratic dependence of their total cross section at the leading electroweak order on the value of the Higgs boson trilinear self-coupling relative to the SM prediction, which is referred to as
\kappa_{\lambda}
.
Modifications of
\kappa_{\lambda}
also lead to non-trivial kinematic changes of the
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
processes because of the interference between the contributions involving the Higgs boson trilinear self-coupling and the ones only involving other Higgs boson interactions
[
29
,
30
]
.
The total ggF cross section exhibits a minimum for
\kappa_{\lambda}\approx 2.3
,
where the interference effects are strongest
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
.
Large values of
\kappa_{\lambda}
result in an enhancement of the cross section in the region with low
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
invariant mass (
m_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
), close to the kinematic production threshold.
The VBF production cross section also features a quadratic dependence on the strength of the
{\mathup{{{V}}}}{\mathup{{{V}}}}{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
interaction, whose value normalized to the SM prediction is referred to as
\kappa_{2{\mathup{{{V}}}}}
.
A single parameter is considered in this work to scale simultaneously the
{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}
and
{\mathup{{{Z}}}}{\mathup{{{Z}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}
couplings, since these are not distinguished experimentally in VBF
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
analyses.
Scenarios with
\kappa_{2{\mathup{{{V}}}}}\neq 1
are characterized by a strong increase in the signal cross section at large values of
m_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
and the resulting production of Higgs bosons with large transverse momenta (
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
)
[
34
]
, a phase space region offering enhanced experimental sensitivity.

Both the ATLAS and CMS Collaborations have performed searches for
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production using the
{\mathup{{{p}}}}{\mathup{{{p}}}}
Run-2 data sets collected at
\sqrt{s}=13\,\text{Te\hskip-0.80002ptV}
between 2015 and 2018, corresponding to integrated luminosities ranging between 126 and 140
\,\text{fb}^{-1}
, depending on the experiment and the analysis considered.
Searches have been performed by both experiments in a variety of final states
[

In terms of the total signal strength
\mu_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}

\sigma^{\text{ggF}}_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}+\sigma^{\text{VBF}}_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}

{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production are 2.9 (2.4) for ATLAS
[
48

and 3.5 (2.5) for CMS
[

The ATLAS
[
52
]
and CMS
[
53
,
54
]
experiments are the two multipurpose particle detectors at the LHC.
Both detectors have a nearly hermetic design with a forward–backward symmetric cylindrical geometry, and are designed to reconstruct and identify electrons, muons, photons, and hadronic objects that are used as inputs to the analyses.
The ATLAS detector consists of an inner tracking detector based on silicon pixel, silicon microstrip, and transition radiation detectors.
It is surrounded by a thin superconducting solenoid providing a 2 T axial magnetic field, lead/liquid-argon sampling electromagnetic calorimeters, steel/scintillator-tile and liquid-argon hadronic calorimeters with lead, tungsten, or copper absorbers, and a muon spectrometer based on superconducting air-core toroidal magnets.
The CMS apparatus features a 3.8 T superconducting solenoid of 6 m internal diameter.
An all-silicon inner tracker, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and a brass/scintillator hadron calorimeter are contained within the solenoid volume, and gas-ionization muon detectors are embedded in the flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.

The
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}
final state has a branching fraction of about 34%
[
55
]
, the largest among the
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
decay modes, but faces a challenging multijet background.
The selection of signal events relies heavily on the use of modern heavy-flavor jet taggers and dedicated
{\mathup{{{b}}}}
-jet trigger algorithms.
Both experiments target resolved-jet
[
35
,
36
]
and merged-jet
[
37
,
38
]
signatures, where the
{\mathup{{{H}}}}\to{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}
decays are respectively reconstructed as two separate jets with a radius parameter
R=0.4
, or as a single large-radius jet with
R=0.8
for CMS and
R=1
for ATLAS.
In both cases, jets are reconstructed with the anti-
\kappa_{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}
algorithm
[
56
,
57
]
.
The merged-jet topology has leading sensitivity to VBF
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production in anomalous
\kappa_{2{\mathup{{{V}}}}}
scenarios where the signal is characterized by large
m_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
and large
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
.
The ATLAS merged-jet analysis only searches for VBF
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production with dedicated selections on additional jets in the event, while the CMS merged-jet analysis covers both ggF and VBF
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production modes.
All four analyses estimate background contributions, mostly consisting of multijet production, using data in background enriched control regions, and simulation for the remaining processes in the merged-jet CMS analysis.
The final results are extracted using a template fit either to a boosted decision tree (BDT) classifier output or the reconstructed
m_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
and merged-jet mass distributions.

The
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}
final state combines a sizable total branching fraction of about 7.3%
[
55
]
with reasonable selection purity from the identification of the
{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}
decay products.
Both ATLAS and CMS analyses
[
39
,
40
]
target the decay channels of the
{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}
system where at least one
{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}
-lepton decays to hadrons and a neutrino (
{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}_{{\text{h}}}
).
Events are classified in a variety of signal regions based on the final state and the corresponding single-lepton, di-
{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}_{{\text{h}}}
, and lepton +
{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}_{{\text{h}}}
triggers (ATLAS) or on the presence of one or two small-radius
{\mathup{{{b}}}}
-tagged jets or a single large-radius jet (CMS).
Categories enriched in VBF
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
events are also defined.
The results are extracted using BDT or deep neural network (DNN) classifier scores for the ATLAS and CMS searches, respectively.
Templates for the distributions of the background are mostly derived from simulation, and control regions in data are used to model the contribution from misidentified
{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}_{{\text{h}}}
objects.

The
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}\gamma\gamma
final state compensates an extremely low branching fraction of 0.26%
[
55
]
with a clean experimental signature from the
{\mathup{{{H}}}}\to\gamma\gamma
decay.
The signal extraction strategies in the ATLAS and CMS analyses
[
41
,
42
]
are similar and feature a parametric fit to the data in the diphoton invariant mass (ATLAS and CMS) and the dijet mass observable (CMS).
These fits are performed in a number of subcategories of different signal purity. Categories are built based on the modified four-body invariant mass
m_{{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}\gamma\gamma}^{*}=m_{{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}\gamma\gamma}-(m_{{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}}-125\,\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})-(m_{\gamma\gamma}-125\,\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})
and BDT outputs separating the signal from the continuum diphoton background and, in the case of ATLAS, also from single
\mathup{{{H}}}
processes.
For ATLAS a BDT is separately trained in the high- and low-
m_{{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}\gamma\gamma}^{*}
regions.
In the CMS analysis, additional categories targeting VBF
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production are used, and the resonant single
\mathup{{{H}}}
background is reduced by applying a selection requirement based on a dedicated multivariate analysis classifier (MVA) in the ggF categories.

The
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}
decay channel offers the second-highest branching fraction of all
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
decay modes at about 25%
[
55
]
. The CMS
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}

[
43
]
and
ATLAS
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}\ell\ell+E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\text{miss}}

[
44
]

analyses target this final state in semi-leptonic (CMS) and dileptonic (ATLAS and CMS)
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}
final states, with additional contributions from
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}
and
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{Z}}}}{\mathup{{{Z}}}}
decays.
The final state leptons (
\ell
) considered are electrons and muons, and the symbol
E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\text{miss}}
denotes the magnitude of the missing transverse momentum.
In the CMS case, events in the semi-leptonic and dileptonic categories are further split based on the number of
{\mathup{{{b}}}}
-tagged jets, their reconstruction as a merged large-radius jet or as resolved small-radius jets and the output of a multiclass DNN separating ggF
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
, VBF
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
,
{\mathup{{{t}}}}+{\mathup{{{H}}}}
, and other backgrounds. For ATLAS, events are divided into signal and control regions based on the presence of a ggF- or VBF-like topology,
m_{\ell\ell}
,
m_{{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}}
, and the lepton flavor. For the ggF-like case a multivariate DNN is trained to classify events into signal,
{\mathup{{{t}}}}{\mathup{{\overline{{{\mathup{{{t}}}}}}}}}+{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{t}}}}
, and other backgrounds, while in the VBF-like case a BDT is used to separate the signal from backgrounds. In both the ATLAS and CMS analyses, the signal is extracted from the MVA discriminator outputs of the various categories.

Analyses targeting final states with multiple leptons (electrons, muons,
{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}_{{\text{h}}}
) in combination with other objects benefit from high trigger efficiencies and low reconstruction thresholds, resulting in large acceptances for signal hypotheses with soft
m_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
spectra and low
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
.
The CMS
multilepton
analysis
[
50
]
targets the
{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}
,
{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}
, and
{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}
decay channels, while the ATLAS analysis
[
51
]
additionally targets the decays to
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{Z}}}}{\mathup{{{Z}}}}
, and to a
\gamma\gamma
pair plus a
{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}},\,{\mathup{{{Z}}}}{\mathup{{{Z}}}},
or a
\tau\tau
pair. The total branching fraction of all these decay modes is about 9%
[
55
]
, with the various final states included in the ATLAS and CMS analyses corresponding to a branching fraction of about 5% due to lepton requirements.
The signal is extracted in categories based on how many light lepton,
{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}_{{\text{h}}}
, and photon (ATLAS) objects are present. In each category, a fit is performed to the output of dedicated BDTs or to the diphoton invariant mass for
\gamma\gamma
final states.
Signal and background templates are obtained from a combination of estimations based on simulation and control regions in data.

The overlap between the selection criteria of the input analyses within each experiment was studied as part of the individual combinations.
It was found to be negligible and treated as described in Refs.
[

Signals in the input analyses are normalized to the predictions based on the PDF4LHC15 PDF set
[
58
]
.
Compared to the most recent cross section calculations, based on the PDF4LHC21 PDF set and reported earlier, the predictions used for this work are less than 1% larger for the ggF production mode, independent of the
\kappa_{\lambda}

For the VBF production mode, the predictions used for this work are about 2% larger compared to the most recent ones, which use the updated PDF set and include next-to-leading order electroweak corrections
[
27

The corresponding total SM cross section for
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production is
32.8\mbox{$\,\text{fb}$}

The ggF
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
signal prediction
[
59
,
60

, for each of the analyses used in this combination, is rescaled by a function of
\kappa_{\lambda}
to adjust its normalization following the correction of the two-loop amplitude in the
POWHEG

[

[

The effect of this modification on the distributions of simulated
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}

to be less than 4% on the constraints for

The dependence of the single
\mathup{{{H}}}
total production cross sections and branching fractions on
\kappa_{\lambda}
from loop-level effects
[

,
65

is included in the ATLAS
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}

\mathup{{{H}}}

The same parametrization was added for this Letter to the background modeling of the ATLAS
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}\gamma\gamma
and
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}

These effects are not considered for the ATLAS analyses in the

and
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}\ell\ell+E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\text{miss}}

Considering that their implementation in the ATLAS
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}\gamma\gamma
and
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}

and
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}\ell\ell+E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\text{miss}}

Results are obtained using a profile likelihood ratio test statistic with systematic uncertainties modeled as nuisance parameters and making use of the asymptotic approximation
[
66
]
.
The combined likelihood is the product of the individual likelihoods and the signal predictions are parametrized by the
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
signal strength parameter,
\mu_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
, and by the
\kappa_{\lambda}
and
\kappa_{2{\mathup{{{V}}}}}
parameters, common to both experiments.
In fitting to the data either the signal strength or the coupling modifiers are allowed to float, but not both.
Upper limits on
\mu_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
are computed using the modified frequentist
\text{CL}_{s}
criterion
[
67
,
68
]
with the profile likelihood ratio modified for upper limits
[
66
,
69
]
as the test statistic.
Asimov datasets
[
66
]
are generated by setting all nuisance parameters to their best-fit value and
\mu_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
,
\kappa_{\lambda}
, and
\kappa_{2{\mathup{{{V}}}}}
according to the assumed hypothesis, and are used to compute expected results.
Results are derived and cross-checked by the two collaborations using different software.
The ATLAS Collaboration uses
HistFactory

[
70
]
and the CMS Collaboration uses
Combine

[
69
]
, both of which are based on the
RooFit

[
71
]
and
RooStats

[
72
]
packages.

Theoretical uncertainties in the
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
signal production cross sections are related to the missing orders in the QCD calculations, estimated by varying the factorization and renormalization scales, to the choice of the top quark mass scheme, and to the limited knowledge of the PDFs and
\alpha_{S}
.
These uncertainties are considered as fully correlated across all the input analyses and the two experiments.
Since they affect theoretical predictions, they are not considered when computing upper limits on the
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production cross section, while they are accounted for when deriving the other results.
Similarly, the corresponding sources of uncertainties on the single
\mathup{{{H}}}
processes are correlated between the two experiments.
Such uncertainties are related to the cross section of
\mathup{{{H}}}
production via ggF, via VBF, and in association with top quarks, to the normalization of ggF and VBF
\mathup{{{H}}}
production in association with heavy flavor jets, and to the branching fractions for
{\mathup{{{H}}}}\to{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}
,
{\mathup{{{H}}}}\to\tau\tau
, and
{\mathup{{{H}}}}\to\gamma\gamma
decays.
Uncertainties for the other decay modes are considered but not correlated across experiments since they follow a different grouping because of the final states considered in the
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}{\mathup{{{W}}}}
and
multilepton
analyses.
All other uncertainties considered in the input analyses are not correlated between the two experiments, while correlations across different analyses from the same experiment follow their original treatment
[
48
,
49
]
.
Scenarios with a correlated uncertainty in the integrated luminosity and with a different correlation scheme between groups of single
\mathup{{{H}}}
processes have been studied and were found to have a negligible effect on the results.
The magnitude of the uncertainty in the normalization of single
\mathup{{{H}}}
production in association with heavy flavor jets
[
73
]
is 100% for ATLAS analyses and 50% for CMS analyses.
The choice to retain two different values of this uncertainty follows from the small impact in the present results and for consistency with previously published results
[
48
,
49
]
that are used as input to this work.
Considering a common correlated uncertainty of 100% for both experiments increases the observed 95% CL upper limit on the
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
signal strength by 1% without significant change to the expected limit, and has similar effects at the percent level or below on the best-fit value of
\mu_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
and the 95% CL constraints on
\kappa_{\lambda}
.
These uncertainties are treated as fully correlated between the two experiments, while the change in the
\mu_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
and
\kappa_{\lambda}
sensitivities if they are considered as uncorrelated is about 1%.

The individual per-experiment best-fit values on the total signal strength
\mu_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
, obtained from individual fits to each set of results, are

\hat{\mu}_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}^{\text{ATLAS}}=0.5^{+1.2}_{-1.1}
and

\hat{\mu}_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}^{\text{CMS}}=1.0^{+1.3}_{-1.0}
.
The combined best-fit signal strength for
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production is found to be

\hat{\mu}_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}=0.8^{+0.9}_{-0.7}\allowbreak=0.8^{+0.7}_{-0.6}(\text{stat.})\allowbreak{}^{+0.4}_{-0.2}(\text{theory})\allowbreak{}^{+0.3}_{-0.3}(\text{exp.})\allowbreak
,
where the breakdown of the uncertainty is into the statistical, theoretical, and experimental components, computed as detailed in Ref.
[
74
]
.
The expected best-fit value is

\hat{\mu}_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}^{\text{exp}}=1.0^{+0.9}_{-0.8}\allowbreak=1.0^{+0.7}_{-0.6}(\text{stat.})\allowbreak{}^{+0.5}_{-0.3}(\text{theory})\allowbreak{}^{+0.3}_{-0.3}(\text{exp.})
.
The leading theoretical uncertainties are those on the ggF
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
signal normalization from the choice of the renormalization scheme and scale of the top quark mass, and from the production of single
\mathup{{{H}}}
in association with heavy flavor jets, the latter impacting mostly the
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}\gamma\gamma
and
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}
analyses.
The leading experimental uncertainties are related to the background modeling in the CMS
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}
merged-jet and ATLAS
{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{b}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}{\mathup{{{\uptau}}}}
analyses.
The combined observed (expected) significance for the
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
signal is 1.1 (1.3) standard deviations.

{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production cross section
assuming the absence of
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production (
\mu_{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}^{\text{Asimov}}=0

Taking into account uncertainties in the
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}

{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}

In summary, this Letter presents the first combination of ATLAS and CMS searches for Higgs boson pair production.
The searches are performed using the LHC Run-2 data sets with integrated luminosities ranging between 126 and 140
\,\text{fb}^{-1}
.
The upper limit at 95% CL on the total
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
signal strength, defined as the ratio of the measured cross section to the SM prediction, corresponds to 2.5, with an expected value of 1.7 (2.8) in the absence (presence) of SM
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production.
The best-fit of the signal strength is observed to be

0.8^{+0.9}_{-0.7}
, corresponding to an observed significance of 1.1 standard deviations, while the expected significance is 1.3 standard deviations.
The observed 95% CL constraints on the
\kappa_{\lambda}
and
\kappa_{2{\mathup{{{V}}}}}
coupling modifiers are

-0.71<\kappa_{\lambda}<6.1
(
-1.3<\kappa_{\lambda}<6.7
expected) and

0.73<\kappa_{2{\mathup{{{V}}}}}<1.3
(
0.66<\kappa_{2{\mathup{{{V}}}}}<1.4
expected).
The results of the combination are compatible within their uncertainties with the SM predictions and represent the most comprehensive and most sensitive constraints on
{{\mathup{{{H}}}}{\mathup{{{H}}}}}
production to date.

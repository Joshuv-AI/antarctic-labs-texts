Abstract
. If ( R , 𝔪 ) (R,\mathfrak{m}) is a complete local ring of mixed characteristic ( 0 , p ) (0,p) and R / p ​ R R/pR is an F F -pure Gorenstein domain, we find a sufficient condition for R R to be perfectoid pure. This condition is related to the Cohen–Macaulayness of the absolute integral closures of Gorenstein local domains of mixed characteristic which are not necessarily excellent. Along the way, we show that the problem of lifting F F -purity of R / p ​ R R/pR to perfectoid purity of R R is equivalent to a similar deformation problem for the splinter property.



1.
Introduction

Perfectoid purity was recently proposed in
[
3
]
as a mixed characteristic analogue of
F
-purity in prime characteristic
p>0
and log canonicity in equal characteristic zero. A noetherian ring
R
whose Jacobson radical contains the prime integer
p
is said to be
perfectoid pure
if there exists a pure map
R\to B
where
B
is a perfectoid
R
-algebra. It is shown in
op. cit.
that this notion enjoys many of the properties that a counterpart to
F
-purity ought to, such as weak normality
[
3
, Corollary 4.29]
and ascent along cyclic covers
[
3
, Proposition 5.8]
.

The goal of this article is to investigate conditions under which the property of being perfectoid pure
deforms
; that is, whether a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
with
p\in\mathfrak{m}
is perfectoid pure provided that
R/xR
is perfectoid pure for some nonzero divisor
x\in\mathfrak{m}
. This is settled already in some cases such as
[
3
, Theorem 6.6]
and
[
25
, Theorem A]
which both require the ring
R
to be a complete intersection. An affirmative answer to the deformation problem is expected merely assuming that
R
is Gorenstein in light of the analogous fact for
F
-purity
[
6
]
. We consider in this article an instance of this conjecture when the nonzero divisor is the prime integer
p
itself.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a complete local Gorenstein ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
. If
R/pR
is an
F
-pure domain, then
R
is perfectoid pure.

Much like many deformation arguments for local singularity types, the proof in the complete intersection setting leverages that a certain object—in this case the perfectoidization
R_{\operatorname{perfd}}
—is Cohen–Macaulay in the sense that
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R_{\operatorname{perfd}})=0
for all
i<\dim(R)

[
3
, Theorem 4.24]
. We find a relationship between
Conjecture

1.1
and the vanishing of local cohomology of a different algebra, namely that of the absolute integral closure
R^{+}
. Note that
R_{\operatorname{perfd}}
in this context is a derived object whereas
R^{+}
is a discrete ring.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a
d
-dimensional noetherian (not necessarily excellent) local Gorenstein domain of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
. Then
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R^{+})=0
for all
i<d
.

Our main theorem is the following relationship between these conjectures.

Theorem A
.

(=
Theorem

5.7
)

Conjecture

1.2
implies
Conjecture

1.1
.

Some remarks are in order regarding the evidence for
Conjecture

1.2
. The conclusion is never satisfied for local rings of dimension at least three containing the rationals, see
[
10
, page 55]
. However, this vanishing is enjoyed by
R^{+}
for excellent local domains
R
containing
\mathbb{F}_{p}

[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
. This result was subsequently extended to include all local domains which are homomorphic images of Gorenstein (resp. Cohen–Macaulay) rings in
[
13
, Corollary 2.3]
(resp.
[
21
, Corollary 3.2]
). Currently the best progress in mixed characteristic is Bhatt’s result
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
which states that
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R^{+})=0
for all
i<\dim R
when
R
is an excellent local domain of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
. It thus seems plausible that
all
Gorenstein local domains of mixed characteristic have this property, and indeed the question of whether this holds in the level of generality of either
[
13
]
or
[
21
]
is posed in
[
1
, Question 5.3]
.

We emphasize that
Conjecture

1.1
concerns
complete
local rings, while
Conjecture

1.2
only remains unresolved outside of the excellent scenario. This points to some surprising features of our proof that we briefly summarize. We show that if
R/pR
is a Gorenstein
F
-pure local domain, then
R\llbracket x\rrbracket
contains a local subring
A
such that:

(a)

\widehat{A}\cong R\llbracket x\rrbracket
,

(b)

pR\cap A=pA
, and

(c)

A/pA
is
weakly
F
-regular
.

Weakly
F
-regular rings (i.e., rings in which all ideals are tightly closed) satisfy the splinter condition, a characteristic-independent notion prescribing that any map to a finite cover splits. Granting
Conjecture

1.2
, this local Gorenstein (non-excellent) ring
A
will also be a splinter (
Lemma

3.2
) and hence perfectoid pure (
Theorem

5.6
). Using results from
[
3
]
, perfectoid purity can then be ascended along the completion
A\to R\llbracket x\rrbracket
and then descended along
R\to R\llbracket x\rrbracket
.

Our proof of
Theorem

A
also shows that, irrespective of
Conjecture

1.2
, the problem of deforming
F
-purity of
T/pT
to perfectoid purity of the complete ring
T
is equivalent to deforming weak
F
-regularity to perfectoid purity in Gorenstein domains which are not assumed to be excellent:

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local Gorenstein ring of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
. If
R/pR
is weakly
F
-regular and analytically irreducible, then
R
is perfectoid pure.

We expect an even stronger conclusion to hold than what is stated above, namely that
R
is a splinter; indeed, this is implied by
Conjecture

1.2
(see
Lemma

3.2
). Nevertheless, we have:

Theorem B
.

(=
Theorem

5.8
)

Conjecture

1.1
is equivalent to
Conjecture

1.3
.

The proof strategy outlined above adds to a long-running program pioneered by Lech
[
14
]
and Heitmann
[
8
]
aiming to show that a complete local ring which lacks some mildness property—in this case the splinter condition—arises as the completion of a ring
with
that property.

Finally, we caution the reader about relaxing the assumptions of
[
3
, Theorem 6.6]
or those of
Conjecture

1.1
much further beyond the Gorenstein case. Indeed, there are examples of Fedder
[
6
]
and Singh
[
23
,
24
]
demonstrating that
F
-purity does not deform, although there are positive deformation results provided that the canonical module
\omega_{R}
is torsion in the class group
\operatorname{Cl}(R)

[
19
,
20
]
. Note also that the analytic irreducibility assumption in
Conjecture

1.3
is irredundant, as there are examples of Gorenstein (weakly)
F
-regular domains whose completions fail to be domains by
[
17
, Theorem C]
.

Some conventions

By a
quasi-local
ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
, we mean a commutative unital ring with unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. A
local ring
is a quasi-local ring which is also noetherian. For a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
, we denote by
R^{\circ}
the elements of
R
which are not contained in any minimal prime. The
punctured spectrum
of
R
, denoted
\operatorname{Spec}^{\circ}(R)
, refers to the nonmaximal prime ideals of
R
. If
R
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra, then
F^{e}:R\to R
denotes the
e
th
iterated Frobenius endomorphism
r\to r^{p^{e}}
.

2.
Preliminaries

We review the various singularity types that we will consider; these include
F
-purity and (weak)
F
-regularity in prime characteristic, the splinter condition in arbitrary characteristic, and perfectoid purity in mixed characteristic. Recall that if
R
is a commutative ring then a map
N_{1}\to N_{2}
of
R
-modules is
pure
if the induced map
M\otimes_{R}N_{1}\to M\otimes_{R}N_{2}
is injective for every
R
-module
M
. The map
N_{1}\to N_{2}
is said to be
cyclically pure
if for every ideal
I\subseteq R
, the map
N_{1}/IN_{1}\to N_{2}/IN_{2}
is injective. Pure maps are clearly always cyclically pure and the two notions coincide in all settings of interest in this article
[
9
]
.

Let
p>0
be a prime integer. A reduced noetherian
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
R
is
F
-pure
if the inclusion
R\hookrightarrow R^{1/p}
is pure, i.e. if for any
R
-module
M
, the induced map
M\to M\otimes_{R}R^{1/p}
is injective. It is straightforward to see that if
R
is
F
-pure and
I\subseteq R
is an ideal then
I=I^{F}
, where the
Frobenius closure

I^{F}
of
I
is the ideal consisting of elements
r\in R
such that
r^{q}\in F^{e}(I)R
for all sufficiently large
q=p^{e}
. If
I\subseteq R
is an ideal, then the
tight closure of
I
, denoted
I^{*}
, is the ideal consisting of elements
r\in R
for which there exists
c\in R^{\circ}
such that
cr^{q}\in F^{e}(I)R
for all sufficiently large
q=p^{e}
. We say that
R
is
weakly
F
-regular
if
I=I^{*}
for all ideals
I\subseteq R
. If
R
is a domain, the containment
r\in I^{*}
is equivalent to the existence of a nonzero
c\in R
such that
cr^{q}\in F^{e}(I)R
for
all

q=p^{e}
by
[
12
, Theorem 1.3(b)]
.

A noetherian ring
R
is called a
splinter
if each module-finite map
R\to S
inducing a surjection
\operatorname{Spec}S\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}R
admits an
R
-linear left inverse. Local splinters are always normal domains
[
4
, Lemma 2.1.1]
, and a noetherian domain

is a splinter if and only if the map
R\to R^{+}
is cyclically pure
[
4
, Lemma 2.3.1]
. Here,
R^{+}
denotes the

of
R
, i.e. the integral closure of
R
in a choice of algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of the fraction field
K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)
.

If
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local domain and

is an injective hull of the residue field
k
of
R
, then by
[
11
, Lemma 2.1(e)]

R
is a splinter if and only if
E\to E\otimes_{R}R^{+}
is injective, provided that
R
is approximately Gorenstein (e.g. if
R
is Gorenstein or if
\widehat{R}
is reduced). If
R
has prime characteristic
p>0
, then similarly
R
is
F
-pure if and only if
E\to E\otimes_{R}R^{1/p}
is injective.

We now briefly recall the notion of a perfectoid ring following
[
2
]
, as well as that of perfectoid purity. Again let

be a prime integer. The

yielding the diagram

4.
A Heitmann-type completion lemma

This section is the technical heart of the paper. Starting with a complete local domain
T
of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
in which
pT
is a prime ideal, we design a local subring
A
whose completion is
T
with some specific properties. When combined with certain data of a Gorenstein
F
-pure section of
T
,
Conjecture

1.2
will imply that this subring
A
can be manufactured to be a splinter. The main theorem of this section is:

Theorem 4.1
.

(
cf.

[
16
, Theorem 22]
)
Let
(T,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local domain of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
such that
(p)\in\operatorname{Spec}T
, and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}T\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
be a non-maximal prime ideal such that
\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathbb{Z}=(p)
. Let
\delta\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus\mathfrak{p}
. Then there exists a local domain
(A,\mathfrak{m}\cap A)
such that
\widehat{A}\cong T
,
A\cap\mathfrak{p}=pA
, and
\delta t\in A
for some unit
t\in T
.

To prove this theorem we first adapt a version of a definition from
[
5
]
.

Definition 4.2

(cf.
[
5

)
.

Let
(T,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a complete local domain and let

. Let

be a nonmaximal prime ideal, and suppose that

(a)

;

.

Suppose now that

Proposition 4.5

Proof of

and well-order
\Omega

\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is a

-subring of

. Let

-subring granted by
Lemma

4.4
for

such that
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\subseteq R_{0}\subseteq T
,
\delta t\in R_{0}
for some unit

. Note that

. Now for each

in this case as well. We show that
R_{\alpha}

[\mathfrak{p},x]
-subring; condition

. We first show

(5)

\displaystyle|R_{\alpha}|\leq\sup(\aleph_{0},|\{\beta\in\Omega\mid\beta<\alpha\}|)

and we accomplish this via transfinite induction, reproducing the details of
[

or
[

for the reader’s convenience. The case of

is clear, so suppose that
\alpha\in\Omega
and that

\displaystyle|R_{\beta}|\leq\sup(\aleph_{0},|\{\lambda\in\Omega\mid\lambda<\beta\}|)

for every

\gamma(\alpha)<\alpha

\displaystyle|R_{\alpha}|

\displaystyle=\sup(\aleph_{0},|R_{\gamma(\alpha)}|)

\displaystyle\leq\sup(\aleph_{0},\sup(\aleph_{0},|\{\lambda\in\Omega\mid\lambda<\gamma(\alpha)\}|))

\displaystyle\leq\sup(\aleph_{0},|\{\beta\in\Omega\mid\beta<\alpha\}|)

\displaystyle|R_{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\leq|\{\beta\in\Omega\mid\beta<\alpha\}|\cdot\sup_{\beta<\alpha}(\sup(\aleph_{0},|\{\lambda\in\Omega|\lambda<\beta\}|))

\displaystyle\leq|\{\beta\in\Omega\mid\beta<\alpha\}|\cdot\sup(\aleph_{0},|\{\beta\in\Omega\mid\beta<\alpha\}|)

and

follows. The ordering of

, hence

5.

Theorems

A
and
B

In this section, if
R
has mixed characteristic
(0,p)
and
r\in R
then
\overline{r}
denotes its image in
R/pR
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
A
be a ring of characteristic
p>0
. Let
I\subseteq A
be an ideal and
x\in A
an element. For each
e>0
define
I^{[p^{e}]}:=F^{e}(I)A
and

\mathcal{M}_{I,x}:=\bigcup_{e\geq 0}\bigcap_{f\geq e}(I^{[p^{f}]}:x^{p^{f}}).

If
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is local and Gorenstein, it is straightforward to check that
A
is
F
-pure if and only if
\mathcal{M}_{I,\delta}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
for every (equivalently, for some) parameter ideal
I\subseteq A
and socle generator
\delta
of
A/I
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(T,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local ring with
d=\dim(T)\geq 2
and of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
such that
T/pT
is an
F
-pure local Gorenstein domain. Let
\mathfrak{a}=(p,x_{2},\dots,x_{d})\subseteq T
be a parameter ideal and denote
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{a}/(p)
, a parameter ideal in
T/pT
. Let
\delta\in T
be a generator for the socle of
T/\mathfrak{a}
. Further assume that there is a nonmaximal prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}T
with the following properties:

(a)

\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathbb{Z}=p\mathbb{Z}
,

(b)

\delta\notin\mathfrak{p}
,

(c)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{b},\overline{\delta}}\subseteq\frac{\mathfrak{p}}{pT}
.

Then there exists a local domain
(A,\mathfrak{m}\cap A)
of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
such that
\widehat{A}\cong T
,
pT\cap A=pA
, and such that
A/pA
is weakly
F
-regular.

Proof.

Since
\delta
generates the socle of
T/\mathfrak{a}
and
T/pT
is
F
-pure, it follows that
\delta\notin pT
and that
\overline{\delta}^{q}\notin\mathfrak{b}^{[q]}
for all
q=p^{e}
. Use
Theorem

4.1
to construct a local domain
(A,\mathfrak{m}\cap A)
such that
\widehat{A}\cong T
,
\delta t\in A
for some unit
t\in T
, and
\mathfrak{p}\cap A=pA
. Let
I\subseteq A
be a parameter ideal such that
IT=\mathfrak{a}
. Note that
p\in I
. We will show first that
B:=A/pA
is weakly
F
-regular and it suffices by
[
12
, Theorem 1.5]
to show that
J:=I/pA
is tightly closed since
B
is Gorenstein.

Note that
B
is a domain and
\overline{\delta t}
generates the socle of
B/J
. Since
B/J
is artinian Gorenstein, its socle is contained in every nonzero ideal. To show
J=J^{*}
, it therefore suffices to show
\overline{\delta t}\notin J^{*}
. Suppose instead that
\overline{\delta t}\in J^{*}
. Then there is some nonzero
\overline{c}\in B
such that

\overline{c}(\overline{\delta t})^{q}\in J^{[q]}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}^{[q]}\subseteq T/pT

for all
q=p^{e}
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{b},\overline{\delta t}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{b},\overline{\delta}}
, we observe by the above and condition
(c)
that

\overline{c}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{b},\overline{\delta}}\cap B\subseteq\frac{\mathfrak{p}}{pT}\cap B=(0),

a contradiction. Hence
\overline{\delta t}\notin J^{*}
, so

is weakly
F
-regular as desired.
∎

In order to prove
Theorem

A
, we apply

5.2
to a class of rings which need not satisfy assumption

. If

satisfies conditions

and

, we show that indeed the power series ring

with the nonmaximal ideal

in a power series ring.

Lemma 5.3
.

[
16
, Proposition 3]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a reduced local ring of prime characteristic

, let

be an ideal, and let

. Then either
\mathcal{M}_{I,g}=R\llbracket w\rrbracket
or
\mathcal{M}_{I,g}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}R\llbracket w\rrbracket

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
(T,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local ring with

and of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
such that
T/pT
is an

-pure Gorenstein domain. Then there exists a local subring
(A,\mathfrak{m}\cap A)
of

,
pT\llbracket w\rrbracket\cap A=pA
, and such that

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}=(p,x_{2},\dots,x_{d})\subseteq T
be a parameter ideal and note that
(\mathfrak{a},w)
is a parameter ideal of
T\llbracket w\rrbracket
. Denote
\mathfrak{b}:=\mathfrak{a}/pT
and
\mathfrak{c}:=(\mathfrak{a},w)/(p)
. If

generates the socle of

and

Remark 5.5

The proof of

Theorem 5.6
.

If
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local splinter of mixed characteristic
(0,p)

is perfectoid pure.

Proof.

Let
E=E_{R}(k)
denote an injective hull of the residue field, and let
(-)^{\vee}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,E)
denote the Matlis dual.

is pure by assumption, so

is injective by
[

. Applying

and using

-
\otimes

(6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R^{+},R^{\land\mathfrak{m}})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R^{+},\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(E,E))

(7)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(E\otimes_{R}R^{+},E)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(E,E)\cong R^{\land\mathfrak{m}}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R,R^{\land\mathfrak{m}}).

It follows that the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion map

extends to a map

, hence the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion map factors as follows.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R^{\land\mathfrak{m}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

Completing again, the identity map on

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R^{\land\mathfrak{m}}}

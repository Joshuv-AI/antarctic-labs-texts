Genomic epidemiology of dengue virus serotype 3 lineage 3III_B.3.2 in Brazil: insights into its spread and dengue severity amid co-circulation of multiple dengue serotypes

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundAfter over a decade of minimal circulation, DENV-3 reemerged in Brazil in 2023, driven by the newly detected lineage 3III_B.3.2. Elucidating the patterns of DENV-3 spread and impact on public health, is critically important in the context of Brazil’s hyperendemic dengue scenario. Here, we aimed to investigate the spatiotemporal dispersion of this new lineage and its epidemiological impact in Brazil.MethodsWe generated 87 near-complete DENV-3 genomes, sampled in nine different Brazilian states, that were analysed with publicly available sequences. Phylogeographic reconstructions inferred introduction events and spatial dispersion of lineage 3III_B.3.2. In parallel, dengue cases notifications (2023–2025) were used to fit multiple logistic regression models assessing associations between DENV serotypes and severity, adjusting for demographic and spatiotemporal covariates.FindingsWe detected at least 14 introductions of lineage 3III_B.3.2 into Brazil during 2023–2024, followed by diversification into six region-specific clades. Epidemiological analyses applied to the RT-qPCR dengue confirmed cases showed an adjusted odds ratios for severe dengue ranging from 1.24 to 1.71 for DENV-2 and from 1.47 to 1.94 for DENV-3, across different settings and using DENV-1 infection as the reference category. Sensitivity analysis to evaluate potential recent heterotypic infection among infants ≤2 years adjusted by confunders showed an association between DENV-2 and severe dengue, while DENV-3 estimates were not statistically significant.InterpretationOur results reveal a dynamic pattern of multiple introductions and regionally structured spread of 3III_B.3.2, which may represent a general feature as a similar reconstructed history has also been observed for other DENV serotypes/lineages in Brazil. Although the association observed between DENV-2 and DENV-3 and dengue severity may be influenced by secondary infections, our findings underscore that the co-circulation of multiple serotypes contributes to more severe epidemic outcomes and emphasize the need for ongoing DENV genomic surveillance.FundingDECIT, CGLAB, Fiocruz, CNPq, and FAPES.

Research in context

Evidence before this study

We searched PubMed and preprint servers (medRxiv and bioRxiv) for articles published on or before March 31, 2025, on DENV-3 occurrence in Brazil, using the keywords “DENV-3”, “serotype 3”, and “type 3” combined with “Brazil”/“Brazilian” and “DENV”/“dengue”, with no date restrictions. DENV-3 lineage 3III_C caused several epidemics in Brazil during the 2000s, but from 2010 onwards it accounted for fewer than 1% of dengue cases. In 2023, however, the serotype reemerged through a newly identified lineage, 3III_B.3.2. This resurgence raised concern, given it followed 15 years without widespread DENV-3 transmission in the country. The pathways of introduction and dispersion of 3III_B.3.2 across Brazil remained incompletely understood, as did the epidemiological impact of DENV-3 reemergence on dengue severity.

Added value of this study

This study generated 87 near-complete DENV-3 genomes and provides the most comprehensive assessment of the emergence and spread of the DENV-3 3III_B.3.2 lineage in Brazil to date. By combining nationwide genomic data with phylogenetic and phylogeographic analyses, we show that DENV-3 reemergence resulted from multiple independent introductions, predominantly from the Caribbean, followed by heterogeneous regional expansion. Using multiple logistic regression applied to national surveillance data (2023–2025), we estimated the association between circulating serotypes and dengue severity. Our analyses indicate higher odds of severe dengue in infections attributed to DENV-2 and DENV-3 compared with DENV-1, which may be due to heterotypic secondary infections, as the patient's infection history was unavailable. However, sensitivity analysis in children ≤2 years of age revealed an association between DENV-2 and severe dengue, highlighting the severity of this serotype even during primary infection.

Implications of all the available evidence

Our results help to characterize the dynamic and complex patterns of introduction and circulation of new DENV serotypes/lineages in Brazil, which, when analyzed alongside other studies, can provide valuable insights for epidemiological surveillance and health system preparedness. Analyses of disease severity further highlight the concerning epidemiological scenario emerging from the co-circulation of multiple serotypes, with the potential to impose a greater burden on health systems due to increased severity of secondary infections and, possibly, primary infections caused by DENV-2 and DENV-3.

Introduction

Dengue is the most prevalent arthropod-borne human viral disease, with symptoms ranging from mild to life-threatening.1 It is caused by the infection of one of the four dengue virus serotypes (DENV-1 to DENV-4).1 After a primary infection with one serotype, the immune response provides strong and long-lasting protection against reinfection by the same serotype (homologous infection), but individuals remain susceptible to infection by a different serotype (heterologous infection).2 Given the genetic diversity within each serotype, they are subcategorized in genotypes, which have been recently further subclassified in standardized major and minor lineages.3

Within the genetic diversity of DENV-3, genotype III (3III), which originated in the Indian subcontinent in the 1980s, has become the most globally widespread.4 It was first introduced to the Americas in the 1990s, originating the lineage 3III_C, which circulated widely across the continent in the subsequent years.3,5 In Brazil, the 3III_C lineage caused several DENV-3 epidemics during the 2000s, the last of which occurred in 2008.6 Throughout the 2010s, the incidence of DENV-3 declined markedly in the country and accounted for less than 1% of reported dengue cases, likely due to residual circulation of lineage 3III_C.7,8

More recently, phylogenetic analysis of genomes from outbreaks in Cuba, Puerto Rico, the United States, and Brazil revealed the reemergence of DENV-3 in the Americas.9 This resurgence was linked to a novel lineage, 3III_B.3.2, likely introduced into the continent between 2019 and 2020 through the Caribbean.10 Since the first autochthonous cases detected in the Northern Brazilian state of Roraima (RR),7 the 3III_B.3.2 lineage has been detected across all country regions.7,8,11, 12, 13

In Brazil and other American countries, the introduction of the 3III_B.3.2 lineage has led to a marked increase in reported DENV-3 cases, especially since late 2024.14 This resurgence raised public health concerns, as it follows nearly 15 years without widespread DENV-3 transmission in Brazil, leaving at least ∼40 million young Brazilians (aged <15 years) susceptible to infection and increasing the likelihood of secondary infections among DENV-3–naïve individuals in the context of recent major DENV-1 and DENV-2 epidemics.7,8,14,15

Additionally, several studies suggest that DENV-3 infections may be associated with increased disease severity or a higher proportion of symptomatic cases compared to other serotypes, even in primary infections.16 Recent evidence also indicates that DENV-3 infections can exhibit distinct clinical and laboratory findings in comparison to other serotypes.17

Here, we present new data, from previously unsampled states, on the spread of the 3III_B.3.2 lineage in Brazil following the recent reemergence of DENV-3. Furthermore, to evaluate the potential epidemiological impact of this event, we analyzed dengue case records from the Brazilian Notifiable Diseases Information System (SINAN), aiming to assess whether the resurgence of DENV-3 has been associated with an increase in disease severity. By integrating genomic and epidemiological data, this study provides insights into the current dynamics of DENV-3 transmission and its potential implications for public health.

Methods

Ethics approval

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Instituto Oswaldo Cruz (Certificado de Apresentação de Apreciação Ética [CAAE]:70845723.7.0000.5248), the Ethics Committee of Instituto René Rachou (CAAE: 79060024.8.0000.5091) and the Ethics Committee of the Instituto Aggeu Magalhães (CAAE: 10117119.6.0000.5190), which waived signed informed consent.

Samples

A collaborative network was established with the Central Laboratories from Brazilian states of Pernambuco (LACEN-PE), São Paulo (LACEN-SP/Instituto Adolfo Lutz [IAL]), Espírito Santo (LACEN-ES), Mato Grosso do Sul (LACEN-MS), Paraná (LACEN-PR) and Santa Catarina (LACEN-SC). In Minas Gerais (MG), a partnership was established with the Municipal Molecular Biology Laboratory of Belo Horizonte, capital city of the state.

LACENs routinely test arboviral suspected cases using a reverse transcriptase real-time PCR kit that detects Zika, dengue, and chikungunya, and identifies the four dengue serotypes.

A selection of confirmed cases of dengue serotype 3 (DENV-3) infection, sampled between October 2023 and April 2025 were then submitted to viral whole-genome sequencing. Samples were selected based on PCR cycling threshold (Ct < 30) and spatial distribution within each state participating in our collaborative network.

Whole-genome sequencing and genome assembly

Sequencing was performed at state public health laboratories (LACENs) or at Oswaldo Cruz Foundation (Fiocruz) laboratories participating in the Fiocruz Genomics Network (https://www.genomahcov.fiocruz.br/en/). Selected samples underwent total RNA extraction and were used for sequencing library preparation following Illumina amplicon-based protocols. Briefly, we used an experimental DENV-3 primer panel developed by a Brazilian company (https://www.loccus.com.br/), designed to amplify high-quality, near-complete DENV-3 genomes in fragments compatible with amplicon-based sequencing methods such as Illumina COVIDseq or iMAP. Library preparation using the COVIDseq workflow was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and sequencing was conducted on MiSeq or NextSeq 1000 instruments (Illumina).

Raw FastQ files were downloaded, and quality control, trimming, and mapping against the DENV-3 reference genome (NC_001475.2) were carried out using ViralFlow (da Silva et al., 2024) or a customized workflow implemented in Geneious Prime 2024.0.2 employing the BBDuk, Dedupe, and BBMap tools. All DENV consensus sequences generated in this study were analyzed with Nextclade18 for genotype/lineage classification and mutation quality assessment.

Sequence data and quality control

We complemented the sequence dataset generated by our collaborative network by retrieving all DENV-3 genotype III genomes from the EpiArbo database in GISAID (https://gisaid.org/) as available on April 1st, 2025. Only sequences with more than 75% of coverage (relative to NC_001475) and fewer than 25% ambiguous bases (Ns) were used. Sequences were then aligned with mafft v7.52619 and a maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was constructed in IQ-TREE v220 with GTR + G4 + I substitution model in the fast mode. Nextclade tool was used to classify sequences into DENV lineages and this was used to annotate the ML tree in the FigTree program. We then selected all sequences classified as lineage 3III_B.3.2, which clustered in a monophyletic clade.

A new ML tree was then reconstructed with the selected sequences using IQ-TREE v2 with the ModelFinder option to test the best substitution model and branch support was inferred with Shimodaira–Hasegawa-like approximate likelihood ratio test (SH-aLRT) on 1000 replicates. A regression analysis of the root-to-tip divergence against tip sampling time of the resulting ML phylogenetic tree was performed in TempEst21 and outlier sequences that deviate more than 1.5 interquartile ranges from the root-to-tip regression line were excluded (Supplementary Fig. 1). Preliminary time-tree estimations revealed a group of publicly available sequences which clustered as a sister clade of 3III_B.3.2 lineage and pushed the tree root too far back in time. A thorough analysis of these sequences revealed a significant number of reversions (mutations back to the reference sequence), as diagnosed in the Nextclade tool, which often indicates assembling pipeline problems usually caused by not removing the primers sequencing adequately. We then inferred a new ML tree using DENV-3 reference sequence (NC_001475) as outgroup and observed that these sequences are attracted to the reference when compared to the ML tree containing only 3III_B.3.2 sequences (Supplementary Fig. 2), an expected behavior when reversion mutations are present. These sequences were excluded from downstream analysis, and a highly supported (SH-aLRT = 99.9) clade of 1109 sequences was used in the Bayesian phylogeography. Data about all 3III_B.3.2 sequences, such as sequencing technology and assembly method, that were submitted to our quality control process is included in Supplementary Table 1.

Bayesian phylogeography

In order to decrease computational burden, a subset of five random sequences per country and per month was performed in Augur v.24.1.0,22 with the exception of Brazil, from which all genomes passing QC were included. This resulted in a dataset of 675 genomes, which were trimmed to the coding region following NC_001475 annotation. BEAST X v.10.5.023 was used to infer the spatiotemporal dynamics of the 3III_B.3.2 lineage in Brazil with the following model specifications: SRD06 as substitution model, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo relaxed clock24 and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo SkyGrid.25 Two independent Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) runs were conducted and subsequently combined to confirm that all relevant parameters achieved an effective sample size (ESS) greater than 200, as assessed using Tracer v1.7.2. Patterns of viral movement among sampled sites were modeled by treating each location as a discrete evolutionary state, with transition rates between states estimated using a continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC).26 A symmetric model was employed given the relatively large number of distinct sampled locations. When travel history was available for the cases where probable place of infection, the traveler’s location of origin was inferred as the place of infection. To lessen the computational demands of estimating transition rates across multiple locations, the spatial diffusion model was applied to a collection of posterior time-trees derived from the complete model. This approach removed the need for simultaneous tree estimation, allowing only the parameters related to spatial diffusion to be inferred, thereby reducing the parameter space and simplifying MCMC sampling. After removing 10% of burn-in, transitions to and from each sampled location were summarized based on the Markov jump estimation. Although locations’ transition rates were estimated symmetrically, directionality of movement was inferred post hoc through Markov jump trajectories. The BEAST tool TreeMarkovJumpHistoryAnalyzer was used to extract location transitions from the posterior tree distribution and the number of descendants of the same state each transition resulted. R package “circlize” was used to generate a circular migration plot with the Markov jump data from transitions that generated at least one same-state descendant. The maximum clade credibility (MCC) tree was summarized with TreeAnnotator, and plotted with the “treeio” and “ggtree” R packages. Finally, transition histories were also extracted from one representative taxa for each of the main 3III_B.3.2 clades in Brazil using the BEAST tool TaxaMarkovJumpHistoryAnalyzer and summarized as a Markov jump trajectory plot as previously introduced.27

Epidemiological data and statistical analyses

Considering the mandatory notification system in Brazil, data from dengue cases registered from January 2016 to July 2025 were obtained from Notifiable Diseases Information System (Sistema de Informação de Agravos de Notificação, SINAN), using the “microdataSUS” R package.28 The dataset includes individual-level information such as age at the date of infection, sex, race/skin color, and preexisting comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, hematological diseases, hepatopathies, chronic kidney disease, peptic ulcer disease, and/or autoimmune diseases), as reported in the notification form completed by healthcare professionals following patient anamnesis and clinical examination. Data related to dengue case notification, such as date and location of notification, virus serotype (detected by RT-qPCR), and disease severity (categorized according to WHO guidelines) are also included.

Populational information was obtained from the last Population Census from the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística, IBGE). Maps were based on official spatial Brazilian datasets available through the “geobr” R package. Maps and incidence/severity graphs were plotted using the “ggplot2” R package.

To evaluate factors associated with dengue severity, we conducted logistic regression analyses. Focusing to investigate the association of DENV-3 with severe dengue, only cases notified in 2023, 2024 and 2025 were included. The response variable was defined by grouping cases classified as dengue with warning signs and severe dengue into a single category referred to as “severe dengue”. Independent variables included the following sociodemographic characteristics: age, sex and race/skin color. Age was categorized into <5, 5–9, 10–14, 15–65, and >65 years to encompass differences in the risk of severe dengue,29 which is frequently reported as higher in younger and older populations in comparison with adults. Race/skin color was grouped into white/Asian and black/mixed/indigenous. Individuals with missing or “ignored” race/skin color information were classified into a separate category labeled “unknown”. An indicator variable for preexisting comorbidities was defined based on a positive report for at least one of the listed conditions. The year of notification and dengue virus serotype (DENV-1, DENV-2, or DENV-3) were also included as independent variables. We fitted separate logistic regression models for each Brazilian state to evaluate factors associated with severe dengue, including sociodemographics, comorbidities, notification year, and serotype. Due to interstate variations in molecular testing protocols (see Supplementary Text for details), the analysis was concentrated on the four states with the most DENV-3 RT-qPCR confirmed cases. Odds ratios (ORs) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) were estimated as measures of association. Associations with Wald test p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. In an attempt to diminish the impact of secondary DENV infection on our results, sensitivity analyses were conducted by fitting additional logistic regression models restricted to individuals aged ≤2 years, ≤1 year or <1 year, using the same outcome definition and independent variables, except for age. Due to the small sample size in this subset, we analyzed data from the four states jointly, while including the variable “State of notification” to control for confounding related to different testing strategies.

In a second sensitivity analysis exploring temporal variation in epidemic intensity, we fitted logistic mixed-effects models including the monthly state-level notification rate as a covariate, with nested random intercepts to account for between-state variability and monthly variation within states. The notification rate was calculated for each state–month–year combination as the number of notified cases per 100 k inhabitants, and each individual case was linked to the corresponding rate based on its state and month/year of notification. All analyses were conducted in R (version 4.1.2) using the tidyverse, car, and stats packages.

Results

Spatial distribution of DENV-3 cases

DENV serotype results obtained from RT-qPCR testing and recorded in SINAN were used to estimate the incidence of DENV-3 between 2023 and 2025 (see Supplementary Text for further details on the national RT-qPCR testing strategy for dengue in Brazil). Our analyses inferred a progressive increase in DENV-3 incidence in Brazil and highlight a shift in the epidemic epicenter, from municipalities in the Northern region (primarily in the states of Roraima [RR] and Amapá [AP]) to municipalities in the Central-West, Southeast, and Southern regions (mainly in the states of Mato Grosso do Sul [MS], Minas Gerais [MG], São Paulo [SP], and Paraná [PR]). In the 2023–2025 period, DENV-3 co-circulated with DENV-1 and DENV-2 in Brazil, but it was never the predominant serotype (Fig. 1b). The highest proportions of DENV-3 among the RT-qPCR confirmed cases were observed in the second half of 2023 in North region (∼30%), in late 2024 in Southeast and South (∼38% and 25%, respectively) and in mid 2025 in Central-West (∼24%). Genomic surveillance

To better understand DENV-3 spread in Brazil after its resurgence in 2023, we generated 87 near-complete viral genomes, with a median breadth of coverage of 95% based on DENV-3 NC_001475. These genomes were all classified as lineage 3III_B.3.2 and were sampled in the states of Pernambuco (PE) (n = 9), Espírito Santo (ES) (n = 5), MG (n = 2), SP (n = 18), MS (n = 9), PR (n = 40) and Santa Catarina (SC) (n = 4). Together with genomes generated by other studies, the available genomic sampling represents 39.1%, 3.7%, 0.9%, 1.3%, 6.3% and 2% of confirmed DENV-3 cases in PE, MG, SP, MS, PR and SC states, respectively, between January 2023 and July 2025. No data on DENV-3 cases for ES were available in SINAN at the time we obtained the data. Sequences were made available in the EpiArbo database in GISAID (https://gisaid.org/) whose accession numbers and other details are available in Supplementary Dataset. Putting them together with the already available 3III_B.3.2 genomes from Brazil, we ended up with a dataset of 156 sequences sampled from January 2023 to April 2025 and distributed across 12 states, representing all five Brazilian regions (Supplementary Fig. 3).

Our Bayesian phylogeographic analyses identified at least 14 introductions of the 3III_B.3.2 lineage in Brazil (Fig. 2). Eight of these were composed of only one or two sequences and likely reflect introductions resulting in limited or undersampled spread. For example, the earliest 3III_B.3.2 genomes detected in the state of Roraima (RR) in early 2023 led to local circulation and an increase in DENV-3 incidence within the state, but did not disseminate widely to other regions of the country. This pattern is consistent with the relatively low connectivity of Roraima within Brazil’s transportation network. Of the six main clades detected here, Clade 3 (n = 36 sequences) was the first to emerge (January 2023, October 2022–March 2023, 95% Higher Posterior Density [HPD]). The likely origin was Cuba (0.87 posterior state probability [psp]) and the introduction occurred in SP, from where it also spread to MS and MG states. Clade 2 (n = 13) also emerged in SP (June 2023, March–September 2023, 95% HPD), following an introduction from Costa Rica (0.49 psp) or USA (0.32 psp). From SP, clade 2 spread to PR state in late 2023 and early 2024. However, the circulation of DENV3 in PR was mainly due to Clade 5 (n = 41), which emerged in the state in August 2023 (June–September 2023, 95% HPD) after an introduction from the Dominican Republic (0.99 psp). PR gave rise to introductions in ES and SP states. Finally, Clade 4 (n = 9) emerged in PE state in September 2023 (July–November 2023, 95% HPD) and the origin of the introduction was most likely Guyana (0.75 psp) or RR (0.18 psp). Clades 1 (n = 14) and 6 (n = 26) emerged in MG state and were previously described.8

Epidemiological findings

Analyzing the 10 years historical records on dengue, we observed that, in 2025, the percentage of severe dengue (here defined as any case of dengue with warning signs or severe dengue) abruptly increased in some states (Supplementary Fig. 4). In most of these states, dengue severity increase was concomitant with a rise in incidence of DENV-2 and DENV-3 compared with previous years (2022–2024) (Fig. 3). Aiming to explore the relationship between dengue severity and the serotype implicated in the infection, we fitted independent logistic regression models for the four states with higher number of DENV-3 RT-qPCR confirmed cases. The adjusted model inferred that DENV-2 and DENV-3 presented higher Odds Ratio (OR) for severe dengue compared to DENV-1 (Fig. 4, Supplementary Tables 1–4). This was observed for all evaluated states (ORs ranging from 1.24 to 1.94, p < 0.05) except in MS for DENV-3. Contrast analyses between DENV-2 and DENV-3 were only statistically significant in SP, where DENV-3 showed a higher OR for severe dengue than DENV-2 (OR 1.55 [95% CI 1.41–1.71], p < 0.001). Beyond serotype, other factors were also associated with disease severity, although with some variation between states, most likely due to differences in sample size. As expected, the presence of comorbidities had a statistically significant effect in favour of severe dengue, as well as the age categories “<5 years” (young children) and “>65 years” (elderly) compared to “15–65 years”. Older children (5–9 years) and teenagers (10–14 years) also presented higher odds of severe dengue, although smaller than young children and elderly groups. Compared to the white/Asian reference group, the category of black/mixed/indigenous was associated with a lower OR for severe dengue in SP and MS. Similarly, the category of unknown race/skin color was associated with a lower OR for severe dengue in SP and PR. The association between race/skin color and dengue severity should be interpreted with caution and will be discussed further below. However, including this variable in the adjusted model is important to control for confounding factors. Lastly, the year of notification was also associated with severe dengue. Discussion

The countrywide comprehensive sampling and phylogeographic analyses of our study revealed multiple introductions of the DENV-3 3III_B.3.2 lineage in Brazil. Although it was first introduced in the Brazilian North region, with extensive local circulation, the emergence of the 3III_B.3.2 lineage in the other Brazilian regions was linked to independent introductions from abroad. A similar dynamic was observed in the emergence of DENV-2 genotype II, which was firstly detected in the North region,30 but a subsequent study revealed multiple additional introductions throughout the country.31

Among the main six 3III_B.3.2 clades detailed here (Fig. 2), those introduced in the Southeast region predated the others and have already resulted in broader DENV-3 transmission, mainly observed in SP state, which also played an important role in the dispersion of the 3III_B.3.2 lineage to other states, such as MG, MS and PR. A noticeable viral flow was observed between SP and PR, as seen in the trajectory history of clade 2 and 5, and in our previous study about DENV-2 genotype II dispersion in Brazil.31 SP is the most populous state in Brazil and it concentrates the main national transportation hubs.32 Its intense road and air connectivity with other states likely facilitated the lineage dispersion to its neighboring states, given that human mobility is fundamentally tied to the transmission of infectious agents.32 The more recent introductions detected in PE and MS, might represent still-expanding DENV-3 transmission chains, suggesting that these regions (especially the Northeast) may continue to experience increasing DENV-3 cases in the near future. Among the four states with the highest number of DENV-3 RT-qPCR–confirmed cases (MG, SP, MS, and PR), the estimated mean time of introduction of the 3III_B.3.2 lineage preceded the first detected cases by approximately eight months in SP, seven months in MG, and five months in PR, indicating a substantial period of cryptic circulation (Supplementary Fig. 5). In MS, the first cases were detected prior to the estimated emergence of the main local clade. This is consistent with the observation that individual sequences from MS clustered within other Brazilian clades, suggesting that introductions into the state may have occurred earlier.

Interestingly, of the six Brazilian 3III_B.3.2 clades, our analyses inferred that three have likely originated in the Caribbean (Cuba, Dominican Republic and Puerto Rico) and other two likely emerged after introductions from Guyana and Costa Rica, countries with coastal regions influenced by the Caribbean region. The role of the Caribbean as the initial place of introduction of new dengue lineages into the American continent, and since as a hub of initial spread, was already underscored by previous studies,33, 34, 35 which highlights the importance of maintaining genomic surveillance of arboviruses in the region to monitor potential introductions of new lineages. Nonetheless, it is important to emphasize that, although our analyses indicate Cuba as the root state of the whole 3III_B.3.2 phylogeny, this result may reflect a sampling bias due to the extensive USA genomic surveillance for travelers from the Caribbean, especially coming from Cuba.9 This is particularly relevant during the early phase of the lineage’s spread across the Caribbean and the American continent (throughout 2022 and early 2023) when the vast majority of available genomes originated from USA travel surveillance, and other countries not covered by this surveillance strategy may also have contributed to the initial dissemination of 3III_B.3.2.

Aiming to investigate the association between dengue serotypes and disease severity, we conducted a multiple logistic regression analysis including patients’ sociodemographic characteristics, comorbidities, and also epidemiological context to control confounding factors. Among the factors most strongly associated with severe dengue were the presence of comorbidities, and the age categories corresponding to children, adolescents and the elderly, which are well described in previous studies.36 The agreement of these results with prior established evidence reinforces the robustness of our model.

The negative association between severe dengue and individuals with black ancestry (black and mixed populations) in SP and MS coincides with findings from previous studies in Brazilian, Colombian and Cuban populations.37, 38, 39 This pattern has been consistently reported in the continent literature regardless of whether racial classification was based on Ancestry Informative Markers (AIMs), self-declared color or anthropometric parameters.37, 38, 39 From a biological perspective, this effect has been hypothesized to result from differences in cutaneous inflammatory response patterns, natural selection of protective genomic variants, differential gene expression and specific features of lipid metabolism, but the multifactorial underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood, and further investigation is needed.39, 40, 41 Additionally, race/skin color in Brazil can also reflect distinct broader social and structural determinants, including differences in access to healthcare,37 which may also have contributed to the observed associations. Among the black/mixed/indigenous race/skin color group, indigenous represented only 0.4%–5% of the individuals, with a minimal contribution in the observed effect of this variable in disease severity.

Similar results were observed for individuals with unknown race/skin color and those classified as black, mixed or indigenous. Sensitivity analyses comparing the main approach, which included a separate “unknown” category for race/skin color, with a complete-case analysis, restricted to observations with no missing data for the variables included in the model, showed that the direction of the effects was consistent across approaches. The magnitude of the estimates was reduced but remained broadly comparable, although some associations lost statistical significance, especially in age-specific analyses, as expected given the smaller sample size and consequent loss of statistical power. Nevertheless, the inclusion of the “unknown” category in the main analysis is justified since national and international studies have shown that the quality of race/skin color or ethnicity data in health information systems is not random, but rather associated with social and ethnic vulnerability.42 In Brazil, black, mixed, and indigenous populations are overrepresented among records with missing or inconsistent data, particularly in the race/skin color variable, often recorded as “ignored” or “not available”. It is worth noting that improvements in data completeness over time have actually made racial health inequalities more evident, enabling more accurate analyses of public health indicators stratified by race/skin color.42

Regarding the year of notification, results may reflect differences in RT-qPCR testing strategies across states and years. SP prioritizes severe dengue for molecular testing more than any other analyzed state, but has progressively expanded RT-qPCR coverage to include milder cases in the last three years (Supplementary Text). In contrast, MS, PR and MG showed peaks in severe dengue testing in 2024, possibly due to the nationwide record epidemic registered in that year, during which molecular confirmation was likely prioritized for severe dengue. These patterns may explain why 2024 and 2025 notifications showed higher ORs for severe dengue in most states, while SP displayed an opposite trend.

Even after accounting for the effect of all the aforementioned variables, infections with DENV-2 and DENV-3 were associated with severe dengue, compared to infection with DENV-1, in our study. However, considering the important circulation of DENV-1 in the three years before 2025 in Brazil (Fig. 3), it is possible that DENV-2 and DENV-3 recent emergencies have resulted in an increase in the number of secondary antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) dengue cases. In ADE, nonspecific subneutralizing antibodies produced during a heterologous secondary infection may facilitate higher viral uptake and replication in target cells, such as monocytes, resulting in an increased disease severity.2 In order to control for potential ADE, sensitivity analyses were conducted in subsets of very young individuals (≤2 years), that are very unlikely to have been infected with DENV more than once, as primary infections tend to generate a protective immune response against all DENV serotypes that last around 18 months.2 In our analysis, DENV-2 was significantly associated with a higher odds of severe dengue among children aged ≤2 years and a positive association, without statistical significance, was also observed in the ≤1 year group. These findings suggest that the observed associations between DENV-2 and severe dengue in Brazil do not simply reflect an upsurge in secondary heterotypic infections in 2025. In contrast, for DENV-3, the limited number of infections among young children may have reduced the statistical power of our analysis, and we cannot rule out that the association observed in the overall population with severe dengue is being influenced by heterotypic secondary infections. Further studies including a larger number of cases will be necessary to better elucidate this issue.

Notably, the existing literature on serotype-associated dengue severity remains heterogeneous, with varying conclusions across settings and regionally circulating serotypes. Some studies, able to distinguish primary and secondary infections through IgM and IgG serological tests, have found DENV-2 to be more severe than DENV-1 and DENV-3 even after infection status adjustments.43 Another epidemiological study that analyzed 10,441 cases after DENV-3 reemergence in Mexico, found that DENV-2 was more likely to cause severe dengue than other serotypes.29 By contrast, one study has found DENV-3 to favor more severe dengue than DENV-1 and DENV-2 serotypes.44

The magnitude of the 2024 dengue epidemic in Brazil may have affected healthcare delivery and case notification, thereby influencing the observed proportion of severe dengue cases. In this context, delays in care and underreporting of mild cases may have increased the proportion of severe cases, potentially introducing confounding into the assessment of year of notification and serotype effects on severe dengue odds. To explore this, we performed sensitivity analysis including the monthly state-level notification rate as a covariate in logistic mixed-effects models, with nested random intercepts to accommodate variability between states and monthly variation within states, which showed consistent results with those of the main analysis (Supplementary Tables 8–17). In the analyses for children aged ≤2 years, although the monthly notification rate was not statistically significant, results suggested that spatiotemporal variability is important to explain dengue severity.

Our study has some limitations. First, geographic-based sampling bias may have affected the accuracy of phylogeographic reconstruction, and apparent long-distance lineage migrations may be consequences of sequential shorter-range migrations between unsampled countries and/or Brazilian states. This is particularly evident in the sampling of northern Brazilian states, which experienced a significant increase in DENV-3 incidence (Fig. 1), yet only five genomes were available, limiting our ability to assess whether the initial introduction of 3III_B.3.2 into Brazil was confined to the North region or was able to spread within the country. Second, there are intrinsic limitations associated with the use of national public databases such as SINAN, including potential underreporting, inconsistencies in data entry and regional disparities in notification practices, which may have affected our analyses based on epidemiological data. Third, as already highlighted earlier in the results section, because serotype information was available only for individuals tested by RT-qPCR and testing was not random (with preferential testing of severe cases, see Supplementary Text), even after adjusting for potential confounders, the results of factors associated with dengue severity should be interpreted as applying to the RT-qPCR tested population rather than to all confirmed dengue cases.

In summary, our analyses provide a comprehensive genomic and epidemiological overview of the recent DENV-3 reemergence in Brazil. Multiple independent introductions of the 3III_B.3.2 lineage were identified across all Brazilian regions, originating from the Caribbean or from coastal countries under Caribbean influence. Overall, the results of our multiple logistic regression analyses suggest that increased incidence of DENV-2 and DENV-3 infections may be associated with increased disease severity in the recent Brazilian endemic scenario, with stronger evidence supporting the role of DENV-2. However, given the limitations related to infection status information, these associations should be interpreted cautiously, and further studies are essential to clarify the extent to which specific serotypes contribute to severe dengue.

The findings presented here underscore the importance of closely monitoring DENV-3 circulation in Brazil, particularly in the context of the ongoing implementation of two live-attenuated dengue vaccines within the Brazilian Public Health System. Although available evidence indicates good vaccine efficacy against DENV-1 and DENV-2, the low incidence of DENV-3 during clinical trials precluded a robust evaluation of vaccine performance against this serotype.45,46 Therefore, the ongoing re-emergence of DENV-3 may challenge vaccine effectiveness in real-world settings, while making its impact difficult to predict, requiring public health authorities to remain vigilant through continuous surveillance. Actions by policymakers aimed at standardizing DENV diagnostic criteria across states (especially through the adoption of molecular methods such as RT-qPCR for serotype identification) combined with strengthened continuous genomic and clinical surveillance, especially in undersampled regions (e.g., North and Northeast), will be essential to improve our understanding of serotype-specific pathogenicity and vaccine effectiveness against DENV-3, and to evaluate the overall public health impact of its ongoing reemergence in the coming years.

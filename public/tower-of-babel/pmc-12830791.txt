A hybrid RRT-DWA path planning framework for UAVs in dynamic environments
Authors: Qiang Han, Xingyuan Ma, Jilong Liu, Hanlin Liu, Yuehao Yan, Qianguo Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To address the limitations of single-path planning algorithms in dynamic and complex environments, this paper proposes a hybrid planning framework that integrates global and local planning using a combination of Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT) and Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) algorithms. The global planning employs the RRT algorithm to generate a global path, ensuring goal-directed and near-optimal paths through a cost-grid-based search mechanism. The local planning utilizes an improved DWA algorithm, which, under the guidance of the global path, dynamically adjusts the direction and speed of the UAV in real time to avoid unknown obstacles and adapt to dynamic environmental disturbances. The proposed method addresses key limitations of conventional DWA, including local optima and limited adaptability in dynamic environments. The two planning methods are integrated through a strategy of selecting key sub-goal points: when the UAV follows the global path and encounters a local minimum, the sub-goal points guide the UAV to escape from the local minimum and optimize the path quality. Extensive simulation experiments validate that the hybrid framework significantly enhances the UAV’s obstacle avoidance capabilities and task execution efficiency.The address of the ROS-Gazebo experimental project - https://app.theconstruct.ai/rosjects/980801/.The address of MATLAB source code - https://github.com/moxing15/rrt-dwa.

Introduction

As UAV technology continues to advance at a rapid pace, its applications are expanding rapidly across military, civilian, and commercial sectors. UAVs are now utilized for patrol1, surveillance2, logistics delivery3, and agricultural plant protection4. Thanks to their flexibility, high efficiency, and cost-effectiveness, UAVs are increasingly recognized as a vital component of modern technological solutions. However, the widespread adoption of UAVs also presents significant challenges, with path planning emerging as one of the most critical issues.

Path planning involves UAVs making informed decisions and autonomously devising feasible routes based on mission objectives and environmental data. The development of more efficient and intelligent path planning algorithms for UAVs has become a focal point and a formidable challenge in the field of path planning. The goal is to meet the diverse planning requirements of UAVs in various environments and enhance the safety and reliability of their autonomous operations. UAV path planning algorithms can generally be categorized into two main types: global planning algorithms5,6 and local planning algorithms7,8.

Typical global path planning algorithms include the A* algorithm9, Dijkstra algorithm10, Probabilistic Roadmap (PRM) algorithm11, and Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT) algorithm12. Graph search-based methods such as the A* algorithm and Dijkstra algorithm are easy to understand and implement, and they have the advantage of fast computation speed. However, they may encounter low computational efficiency in complex environments13,14. The PRM algorithm can plan initial paths for mobile robot path planning in limited environments, but it may be accompanied by high spatial and temporal computational complexity when calculating paths, and it may produce unsafe path calculations in complex environments15. The RRT algorithm, which breaks through the dimensionality limitations through probabilistic sampling, has shown significant advantages in complex obstacle environments. However, its random expansion mechanism is prone to generating redundant nodes and relies on binary environment representation, ignoring the differences in obstacle threats16.

While global planners are effective for generating an overall path in a known environment, they typically lack the reactivity to handle unexpected dynamic obstacles. This limitation necessitates the use of local planners, which focus on real-time obstacle avoidance and trajectory refinement. Unlike the aforementioned global path planning methods, local path planning algorithms focus more on real-time obstacle avoidance and dynamic response capabilities. Common local path planning algorithms include the Artificial Potential Field (APF)17 and Dynamic Window Approach (DWA)18. The APF algorithm has the advantages of clear logic, low computational complexity, and strong global planning ability, but it is prone to falling into local optima and is very sensitive to parameters19. As a classic local path planning algorithm, DWA is widely used in dynamic obstacle avoidance tasks for mobile robots, UAVs, and other fields due to its computational efficiency and strong real-time performance20. However, the traditional DWA algorithm has inherent short-comings such as the lack of a global path, insufficient adaptability to dynamic environments, and rigid evaluation functions, which lead to problems such as local optimality, path oscillation, or failure of obstacle avoidance in complex scenarios21.

To address these issues, researchers have proposed various solutions. For example, Kobayashi et al.22 proposed an improved DWA algorithm combined with Virtual Manipulators. By introducing virtual force fields and dynamic obstacle motion prediction, it enhances the obstacle avoidance capability and path smoothness of traditional DWA in dynamic environments. Dobrevski et al.23 proposed Dynamic Adaptive DWA, which introduces dynamically adjusted evaluation weights and real-time environmental adaptability strategies to solve the problems of fixed velocity space sampling and rigid evaluation functions that cause path oscillation or stagnation. Hu et al.24 improved the DWA algorithm by combining pedestrian flow prediction models with multi-robot coordination strategies. It dynamically adjusts the evaluation function weights and velocity sampling space using real-time environmental perception data, solving the problems of low obstacle avoidance efficiency and path oscillation in dense dynamic crowds for traditional DWA. Li et al.25 used the global path provided by A* as the reference trajectory for DWA, constraining the local velocity sampling space to avoid falling into local optima. Jiang et al.26 integrated an improved A* algorithm with DWA, using the global path generated by A* to constrain the local velocity sampling space of DWA and introducing a dynamic obstacle prediction mechanism to solve the problems of local optimality and delayed obstacle avoidance response of traditional DWA in dynamic environments.

Although these sensor-enhanced and rule-optimized improvement methods have significantly enhanced the local performance of DWA, its theoretical limitations in adapting to dynamic environments, long-term prediction, and cross-scene generalization are still difficult to break through. This situation has driven a shift in research approaches – with the successful validation of intelligent algorithms such as Genetic Algorithm (GA)27, Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO)28, Reinforcement Learning (RL)29, and Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)30 in UAV path planning, researchers have begun to construct agent-driven new architectures for DWA. For example, Kim et al.31 proposed an improved DWA algorithm based on Reinforcement Learning (RL). By training agents to dynamically optimize the evaluation function weights and velocity sampling strategies, it enables traditional DWA to adapt to the complex changes in dynamic environments, solving the problems of rigid obstacle avoidance decision-making and poor environmental adaptability under fixed rules. Li et al.32 proposed a hybrid path planning method that integrates an improved Genetic Algorithm (GA) with an improved DWA. The GA optimizes the global coarse path and generates key waypoints, which are then used by the improved DWA to achieve local re-al-time obstacle avoidance, addressing the issues of insufficient globality in traditional DWA and poor real-time performance of GA. Yang et al.33 proposed a novel adaptive DWA combined with a Fuzzy Neural Network (FNN). The FNN dynamically learns environmental features and adjusts the evaluation function weights and parameters of DWA in real time, significantly enhancing the adaptive obstacle avoidance capability and decision-making intelligence of traditional DWA in complex dynamic environments. Wu et al.34 leveraged the global optimization capability of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and the fast local planning capability of the DWA. By defining the action space of the proximal policy optimization algorithm to match the linear and angular velocity descriptions in DWA, it achieves more optimal collision avoidance decisions.

The integration of intelligent algorithms with DWA has enhanced the adaptability and generalization capabilities of the algorithms. However, due to the data dependency of intelligent algorithms, delays in perception and decision-making, high computational complexity, and limitations of hardware conditions, their application in UAVs often results in limited adaptability to dynamic environments, insufficient real-time obstacle avoidance capabilities, and high computational requirements.

In order to overcome the shortcomings of DWA, such as local minima and insufficient long-term prediction capabilities, improve obstacle avoidance in dynamic environments, and avoid the increased computational complexity brought by the introduction of intelligent algorithms, this paper optimizes DWA based on the dynamic characteristics of UAVs. The evaluation function of DWA is improved, and the planning accuracy and safety are enhanced by integrating global path information and introducing dynamic prediction. RRT-DWA hybrid path planning algorithm is proposed, which combines the global planning of RRT with the local obstacle avoidance capability of DWA to significantly improve the planning ability of UAVs in dynamic and complex environments.

Design of the DWA dynamic path planning algorithm

In practical applications, UAVs often encounter complex flight environments. In multi-task handling, UAVs need to deal with dynamic obstacles and avoid interference with each other. To address this challenge, a dynamic obstacle avoidance mechanism is introduced in the local planning stage. This mechanism takes into account the status and position of other UAV or dynamic obstacles, predicts collision risks in real time, and adjusts the speed and heading of each UAV to ensure the safe completion of the mission.

To systematically evaluate the influence of the dynamic weighting parameters in the enhanced DWA algorithm on planning performance, we conducted a sensitivity analysis using the same obstacle environment as in Fig. 11, with start and goal points set to (0,100) and (100,0), respectively. The experiments focused on varying the weights assigned to goal distance, obstacle avoidance, and velocity smoothness, with results summarized in Fig. 12. Experimental findings demonstrate that these weighting parameters significantly affect path length, success rate, computation time, and overall evaluation score across different task scenarios. Specifically, increasing the goal-distance weight encourages more direct movement toward the target, thereby reducing overall path length. However, an excessively high value can increase collision risk in confined spaces, lowering the obstacle-avoidance success rate. Elevating the obstacle-avoidance weight substantially improves the system’s responsiveness to both static and dynamic obstacles, raising success rates notably in densely cluttered environments. Nevertheless, an overly high setting may lead to circuitous paths and greater computational load. Conclusion and future work

This paper proposes a hybrid RRT-DWA path planning framework for UAVs that advances beyond existing implementations through several key innovations. Unlike the approach of Li et al.25, which mainly uses the global path as a directional constraint, our method establishes a dynamic feedback loop between the global and local planners. The introduced sub-goal selection mechanism not only avoids local minima but also continuously refines path quality based on real-time local observations.

Compared to learning-enhanced DWA approaches31,34, our dynamic weighting strategy provides a computationally efficient alternative that doesn’t require offline training or extensive datasets. While learning-based methods excel in complex environments, they often face deployment challenges in real-world UAV applications due to their computational demands and generalization concerns. Our method maintains the interpretability and reliability of traditional DWA while significantly enhancing its adaptability through principled mathematical formulations rather than black-box optimization.

Simulations demonstrate the framework’s superiority over conventional methods in both path quality and dynamic obstacle avoidance.

Limitations and future work

Despite its performance, the framework has limitations that open avenues for future research. Primarily, the increased computational load from the hybrid structure and dynamic prediction may challenge the real-time capabilities of resource-constrained UAV platforms. Furthermore, while validated in 3D space, the planner’s core logic operates effectively in 2.5D, and its extension to fully complex 3D navigation (e.g., with overhangs) requires integration with a 3D local map. Finally, the reliance on precise environmental data in simulation necessitates future work on robustness to real-world sensor noise and uncertainty.

Accordingly, our immediate future work will focus on: (1) optimizing the algorithm’s computational efficiency for embedded deployment; (2) extending the local planner to leverage full 3D environmental information;(3) validating the framework on a physical UAV platform under real-world perception challenges.

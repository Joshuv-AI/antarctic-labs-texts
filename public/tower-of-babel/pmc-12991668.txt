Hands that Heal: The Role of Gentle Massage in Managing Hyperbilirubinemia Among Saudi Neonates: Randomized Controlled Experimental Study
Authors: Yusra Shuaib Alhawsah, Jawaher Abdullah Bin Jumah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Neonatal hyperbilirubinemia is common, with phototherapy as the standard treatment. Gentle massage therapy is being explored as a complementary approach for its benefits on circulation and digestion.Purpose:To evaluate the impact of gentle massage therapy on bilirubin reduction in neonates with hyperbilirubinemia in Saudi Arabia.Methods:The research group included 30 neonates who received gentle massage therapy alongside phototherapy. Bilirubin levels were measured at baseline and during the intervention.Results:Most of the neonates in the research group were discharged on the third day, gentle massage reduced recovery time from hyperbilirubinemia by 0.698 days, with males recovering faster and 35-week neonates taking longer than 36-week neonates.Conclusion:Gentle massage therapy is safe and effective for managing hyperbilirubinemia. It reduces hospital stays and improves neonatal care. Further research is recommended to standardize protocols and train caregivers.

INTRODUCTION

Neonatal hyperbilirubinemia is a prevalent condition affecting approximately 60% of term and 80% of preterm infants worldwide, characterized by yellowing of the skin and eyes due to higher bilirubin levels.[12] This condition arises from immature hepatic metabolism and excessive breakdown of red blood cells.[3] Phototherapy is the standard treatment, effectively converting unconjugated bilirubin into excretable forms.[4] However, prolonged use is associated with risks such as dehydration, temperature instability, and increased hospital stays.[2] Consequently, adjunct therapies like gentle massage are gaining attention for enhancing bilirubin clearance.[5] Massage therapy may promote intestinal motility and circulation, facilitating bilirubin excretion. While several international studies highlight its safety and efficacy, limited data exist from Saudi Arabia. This research evaluates the effectiveness of gentle massage therapy in conjunction with phototherapy in full-term neonates with hyperbilirubinemia, aiming to contribute to evidence-based neonatal care strategies.

METHODOLOGY

This randomized controlled experimental research was done in the neonatal unit of a tertiary hospital in Saudi Arabia. A total of 60 full-term neonates (gestational age ≥37 weeks, birth weight ≥2500 grams) with physiological jaundice requiring phototherapy were enrolled. Participants were randomly assigned to two equal groups: the intervention group received gentle massage therapy in addition to phototherapy, while the control group received only phototherapy.

Inclusion criteria comprised neonates aged 2–5 days with serum bilirubin levels within the phototherapy range, as per hospital guidelines. Exclusion criteria included neonates with congenital anomalies, sepsis, hemolytic disease, or those requiring intensive care. The massage therapy involved light, rhythmic strokes over limbs, back, and abdomen for 15 minutes, three times daily for three consecutive days, performed by trained pediatric nurses in a thermally neutral environment.

Serum bilirubin levels were measured at “baseline, 24, 48, and 72” hours. Other clinical parameters such as feeding, stooling, and weight were monitored. Data were studied using SPSS version 25. A P-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics of neonates

Of the 60 neonates diagnosed with hyperbilirubinemia, they were included in the research and equally divided into two groups: the research group received massage therapy in addition to phototherapy, while the control group received only phototherapy. Both groups were comparable in terms of baseline demographic and clinical characteristics.

The mean birth weight in the research group was 4.01 ± 3.00 kg, compared to 3.88 ± 2.87 kg in the control group. Female neonates constituted 56.7% of the research group and 46.7% of the control group. Gestational age distribution showed that most neonates in both groups were born at or beyond 36 weeks, with the research group having a slightly higher proportion born ≥38 weeks (46.7%) compared to the control group (40%).

Maternal characteristics

Among the mothers, 30% in the research group and 43.3% in the control group had medical conditions during pregnancy. The most common complications in the research group were gestational diabetes (55.6%) and preeclampsia (33.3%). The control group showed a wider spectrum of conditions, including anemia (23.1%), hypertension (15.4%), and Rh incompatibility (7.7%) [Table 2].

Effect of gentle massage on serum bilirubin levels

Serum bilirubin levels were similar at baseline in both groups (17.1 ± 1.2 mg/dL in the research group vs. 17.0 ± 1.3 mg/dL in the control group, P = 0.72). After 24 hours, the research group showed a statistically significant decrease in bilirubin (14.2 ± 1.1 mg/dL) compared to the control (15.6 ± 1.2 mg/dL, P = 0.01). This trend continued at 48 and 72 hours, with the research group consistently showing lower bilirubin levels.

Repeated measures ANOVA confirmed a significant interaction between time and treatment group (F = 14.65, P < 0.001), indicating a faster decline in bilirubin among neonates who received massage therapy. These findings suggest that massage therapy significantly accelerates bilirubin clearance in neonates receiving phototherapy [Table 3].

DISCUSSION

Neonatal hyperbilirubinemia remains a prevalent and critical concern in early neonatal care, particularly in full-term and late preterm infants. While phototherapy is the gold standard for reducing serum bilirubin levels, it is not without limitations such as dehydration, thermal instability, and prolonged hospitalization.[12] To address these challenges, massage therapy has emerged as a potential complementary intervention. The present research demonstrated that gentle massage therapy significantly reduced serum bilirubin levels when used alongside phototherapy, corroborating findings from earlier clinical trials and meta-analyses.[34]

The mechanism by which massage reduces bilirubin levels is multifactorial. Massage stimulates the parasympathetic nervous system, promoting gastrointestinal motility and enhanced bile flow, which in turn accelerates bilirubin excretion through the stool.[5] This biological rationale aligns with studies by Rashwan et al. and Lin et al., where massage therapy improved digestive function and decreased serum bilirubin concentration more rapidly than phototherapy alone.[67] In this research, the research group exhibited faster reductions in bilirubin levels at 24, 48, and 72 hours compared to the control group, validating the physiological impact of massage on bilirubin metabolism.

Beyond bilirubin clearance, massage therapy offers additional benefits such as improved feeding patterns, reduced neonatal stress, and enhanced weight gain.[8] These effects contribute to overall recovery and reduced hospital stay, thereby decreasing the burden on healthcare systems and families. In this context, our findings support the growing body of literature that advocates for integrative neonatal care practices.[910] Moreover, the non-invasive, cost-effective, and culturally acceptable nature of massage makes it an ideal adjunct therapy in resource-limited settings like many neonatal units in Saudi Arabia.[11]

Interestingly, the reduction in bilirubin was more pronounced in neonates with gestational age ≥38 weeks and those who were exclusively breastfed. These observations suggest that maturity of hepatic function and feeding adequacy may modulate the efficacy of massage therapy. Similar associations were noted in other studies that reported greater bilirubin reduction among well-fed neonates with higher gestational maturity.[12]

The primary limitation of the current research is the small sample size and lesser follow-up period. Long-term studies evaluating neurodevelopmental outcomes and standardized massage protocols are necessary to strengthen clinical guidelines.

CONCLUSION

This research demonstrates that gentle massage therapy is a safe and effective adjunct to phototherapy in the management of neonatal hyperbilirubinemia. It facilitates faster bilirubin clearance, improves feeding behavior, and reduces hospital stay. The intervention is practical, economical, and easy to integrate into routine neonatal care. Given these findings, massage therapy should be considered in standardized treatment protocols, particularly in settings with high neonatal jaundice prevalence. Further large-scale studies are warranted to confirm these outcomes across diverse populations.

Admissibility and generalized nonuniform dichotomies for nonautonomous Random Dynamical Systems Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Davor Dragičević 1 , César M. Silva 2 and Helder Vilarinho 2,∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce generalized dichotomies for nonautonomous random linear dynamical systems acting on arbitrary Banach spaces, and obtain their complete characterization in terms of an appropriate admissibility property. These generalized dichotomies are associated to growth rates satisfying mild conditions and they include the standard exponential behaviour as a very particular case. As a nontrivial application, we establish the robustness property of such dichotomies under small (linear) perturbations.

1.
Introduction

The study of admissibility and its relation with asymptotic properties of dynamical systems has a long history that goes back to the pioneering works of Perron
[
54
]
and Li
[
42
]
for continuous and discrete time, respectively. Subsequently, a systematic approach was initiated in the landmark works of Massera and Schäffer
[
45
,
46
]
in the case of continuous time, and by Coffman and Schäffer
[
24
]
in the case of discrete time.

Focusing for convenience on the case of discrete time, let us consider a linear nonautonomous difference equation

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n}\quad n\in J,

(1)

on an arbitrary Banach space
X
, where
(A_{n})_{n\in J}
is a sequence of bounded operators on
X
and
J\in\{\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}^{+}\}
. Let
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{2}
be Banach spaces whose elements are sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in J}\subset X
. We say that the pair
(\mathcal{Y}_{1},\mathcal{Y}_{2})
is
admissible
for (
1
) if for each sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in J}\in\mathcal{Y}_{2}
, there exists (a unique)
(x_{n})_{n\in J}\in\mathcal{Y}_{1}
such that

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n}+y_{n},\quad n\in J.

In the above mentioned works, Li (resp. Coffman and Schäffer) obtained characterization of exponential stability (resp. exponential dichotomy) of (
1
) in terms of admissibility with respect to suitable pairs of spaces. The works of Perron and Massera and Schäffer considered a continuous time counterpart of (
1
), i.e. a linear ordinary differential equation of the form
x^{\prime}=A(t)x
.

Subsequent important contributions (either for continuous or discrete time) are due to Coppel
[
25
]
, Dalec
′
kiĭ and Kreǐn
[
31
]
, as well as Henry
[
38
]
. For a small fraction of the more recent results we refer to
[
39
,
41
,
49
,
52
,
56
,
57
,
62
]
and references therein. Finally, we refer to
[
12
]
for a detailed survey.

We stress that all the above mentioned works dealt with characterizations of
uniform
exponential behavior in terms of admissibility. The case of nonuniform exponential dichotomies was first considered by Preda and Megan
[
55
]
. For these dichotomies, the rates of contraction (resp. expansion) along stable (resp. unstable) directions depend on the initial time.
For subsequent contributions to the study of the relationship between admissibility and nonuniform exponential dichotomies we refer to
[
5
,
10
,
11
,
37
,
48
,
58
,
60
,
68
,
72
,
73
]
and references therein.

On the other hand, in the context of nonautonomous dynamics it is natural to study asymptotic behaviors which are not necessarily of exponential type. To the best of our knowledge, the works of Muldowney
[
51
]
and Naulin and Pinto
[
53
]
were the first to consider dichotomies in which the rates of contraction (resp. expansion) along the stable (resp. unstable) directions are not necessarily of exponential type. More recently, a systematic study of such dichotomies was initiated by Barreira and Valls
[
13
]
who showed that such behaviors arise naturally in the situations when certain generalized Lyapunov exponents are nonzero.

In this direction, the first author obtained a complete characterization of nonuniform
polynomial
dichotomies in terms of admissibility in both discrete and continuous time setting (see
[
32
,
33
]
). The second author
[
64
]
has extended the results from
[
32
]
and obtained analogous results dealing with arbitrary growth rates satisfying a certain “slow growth condition”. These results rely on the use of the so-called Lyapunov norms which transform nonuniform behavior into a uniform one. More recently, in
[
4
]
an alternative approach was proposed which does not rely on the use of Lyapunov norms but in order to characterize dichotomic behavior one needs several admissibility conditions.

For linear cocycles (or linear skew-product semiflows) over topological dynamical systems (consisting of a compact metric space
\Omega
and a homeomorphism
\sigma\colon\Omega\to\Omega
) two different approaches for characterizing uniform hyperbolicity in terms of admissibility have been proposed. The first approach initiated by Mather
[
47
]
considers admissible spaces formed by maps from
\Omega
to a Banach space on which cocycle acts. Since his original work which dealt with derivative cocycles associated to smooth diffeomorphisms on a compact Riemannian manifold, there has been a significant progress on extending his results to the case of arbitrary (i.e. not necessarily derivative) linear cocycles acting on arbitrary Banach spaces. We refer to
[
23
]
for a detailed overview of this line of research.
The second approach was proposed by Chow and Leiva in
[
22
]
. In their discrete-time setup admissible spaces consist of two-sided sequences in a Banach space on which cocycle acts, and the admissibility condition is posed on each trajectory of the base space
(\Omega,\sigma)
separately. Related results dealing with characterizations of uniform hyperbolicity using the Chow-Leiva approach have been obtained in
[
40
,
50
,
59
,
61
]
. For more recent results which combine ergodic theory tools with these techniques we refer to
[
34
,
35
]
.

We now focus on the case of linear cocycles over measure-preserving systems, which allow for the incorporation of random elements. As previously mentioned, significant progress has been made in understanding admissibility and robustness in deterministic dynamical systems, offering comprehensive insights into stability properties, whether in terms of exponential rates or other general types of dichotomies. However, the introduction of randomness into system dynamics presents new challenges. The
Random Dynamical Systems (RDS)
framework extends the classical approach by incorporating stochastic elements that influence the evolution of the system, necessitating a re-evaluation of core stability concepts, such as dichotomies, admissibility, and robustness.

The foundational framework for RDS became established with Arnold’s book
[
1
]
. In this context, particularly in finite-dimensional phase spaces, the existence of invariant manifolds with exponential asymptotic estimates, measured in terms of non-zero Lyapunov exponents, was discussed for RDS satisfying the integrability condition required by the Multiplicative Ergodic Theorem (MET).
The issues of admissibility and robustness for RDS with dichotomies characterized by exponential rates, in different formulations, have been examined in several works.

Tempered exponential dichotomies arise in the context of RDS, generalizing the concept of exponential dichotomies, where growth and decay rates in stable and unstable subspaces are affected by a (tempered) random variable. In the context when the assumptions of MET (or one of its infinite-dimensional versions) are fulfilled, the existence of a tempered exponential dichotomy for a linear RDS corresponds to nonvanishing of Lyapunov exponents
[
1
,
44
]
.

A Perron-type result for perturbations of linear RDS exhibiting tempered exponential dichotomies was given by Barreira, Dragičević, and Valls, in
[
7
]
, and admissibility and robustness results were subsequently provided by the same authors in
[
8
]
. Further robustness results for tempered dichotomies are discussed in Cong’s book
[
27
]
, addressing finite-dimensional RDS, and in the work of Zhou, Lu, and Zhang
[
71
]
for discrete-time linear RDS on Banach spaces.

We stress that in
[
7
]
the admissibility characterization of tempered exponential dichotomies relied on the use of Lyapunov norms. In addition, Abu Alhawala and Dragičević
[
2
,
3
]
, as well as Dragičević, Zhang and Zhou
[
36
,
37
]
explored the relationship between admissibility and tempered dichotomies in the case when admissible pairs are not built using Lyapunov norms. Results in the same spirit but relying on Mather’s approach were obtained in
[
20
]
.

With a different point of view to the exponential control along invariant subspaces, Barreira and Valls
[
14
]
discussed the robustness of exponential dichotomies in mean, examining how these stability structures persist under sufficiently small linear perturbations. Later, Barreira, Dragičević, and Valls
[
6
]
provided a characterization of exponential dichotomies in average in terms of admissibility in the discrete-time case, with a continuous-time counterpart discussed in
[
9
]
by same authors.

Despite the extensive study of exponential dichotomies and their variations within the RDS context, many open questions remain regarding more general forms of dichotomies. Bento and Vilarinho
[
16
]
explored the existence of invariant manifolds for RDS on Banach spaces that exhibit generalized dichotomies, both for continuous and discrete-time systems, in a formulation that includes all forms of exponential dichotomies for RDS while also addressing weaker growth rates strictly beyond the exponential case.

The extension of questions related to admissibility, robustness, stability, and invariant subspaces, among others, to systems exhibiting general dichotomies such as
\mu
-dichotomies or
(\mu,\nu)
-dichotomies, while incorporating randomness, adds additional layers of complexity. Specifically, the system’s evolution law integrates both randomness and nonautonomy, where the time-dependence of the growth rates introduces a form of nonautonomy that is not fully captured by the classical RDS formalism. To address these complexities, we consider Nonautonomous Random Dynamical Systems (NRDS) to investigate admissibility and robustness in systems evolving in Banach spaces, incorporating both randomness and time-varying nonautonomy.

In this direction, Oliveira-Sousa in
[
63
]
, and together with Caraballo, Carvalho and Langa in
[
18
]
,
examined the robustness of hyperbolicity in autonomous differential equations (deterministic) under nonautonomous random/stochastic perturbations. As an application, they established the existence and continuity of random hyperbolic solutions, which laid the groundwork for their subsequent work on the existence and continuity of unstable and stable sets, leading to lower semicontinuity and topological structural stability of attractors
[
17
,
63
]
.
As in the autonomous and nonautonomous deterministic situations, admissibility and robustness remain key for understanding the existence and stability of attractors in NRDS. For this later issue see
[
15
,
19
,
21
,
26
,
28
,
29
,
43
,
65
,
66
,
67
,
69
,
70
]
.

In this paper, we develop a framework for the admissibility of NRDS that exhibit generalized nonuniform dichotomies with respect to random norms. Building on the work of Silva
[
64
]
, we extend the concept of admissibility to this setting of discrete-time NRDS evolving in a Banach space. Our setting allows for the study of systems with nonuniform growth rates, offering a more flexible approach in a setting with random perturbations than traditional dichotomies. In particular, it includes as particular cases the deterministic exponential and
\mu
-dichotomies as well exponential dichotomies for RDS.
We address the admissibility problem for random nonuniform
\mu
-dichotomies, the robustness of these dichotomies, and their relationship with strong random nonuniform
(\mu,\nu)
-dichotomies.

The paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary background on NRDS and
\mu
-dichotomies. Section
3
is dedicated to developing the theory of admissibility with respect to random norms, presenting the main theorems and proofs. Specifically, in Theorem
3.2
, we prove that an NRDS admitting a
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to a random norm exhibits admissibility. Conversely, in Theorem
3.3
, we show that an NRDS with admissibility and appropriate control on the growth of orbits with respect to a random norm possesses a
\mu
-dichotomy. Section
4
addresses the robustness of
\mu
-dichotomies for NRDS. In Section
5
, we discuss the relationship between
\mu
-dichotomies with respect to a random norm and random nonuniform
(\mu,\nu)
-dichotomies. These dichotomies incorporate an additional nonautonomous factor that influences the system’s behavior over time, and they are defined with respect to the space norm.

2.
NRDS and
\mu
-dichotomies

Consider a measure space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
and a
measure-preserving dynamical system

(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu,\theta)
,
in the sense that:

i)

\theta\colon\mathbb{Z}\times\Omega\to\Omega
is
\left(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\right)
-measurable, where
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
denotes the power set of
\mathbb{Z}
;

ii)

\theta^{n}\colon\Omega\to\Omega
given by
\theta^{n}\omega=\theta(n,\omega)
preserves
the measure
\mu
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
;

iii)

\theta^{0}=\Id_{\Omega}
and
\theta^{n+m}=\theta^{n}\circ\theta^{m}
for
all
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

If
\mu
is a probability measure, then
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu,\theta)
is called a

metric dynamical system
.
We set
\Omega^{+}=\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\Omega
. Let
\Theta\colon\Omega^{+}\to\Omega^{+}
be given by

\Theta(\ell,\omega)=(\ell+1,\theta\omega)\quad(\ell,\omega)\in\Omega^{+},

where for the sake of simplicity throughout the paper we denote
\theta^{1}
by
\theta
.
A measurable
nonautonomous random dynamical
system
(NRDS) on a Banach space
X=(X,\|\cdot\|)
over a metric dynamical system
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu,\theta)
with time
\mathbb{Z}^{+}=\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,+\infty)
, is a map

\Phi:\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\Omega\times X\to X

such that

i)

(n,\omega)\mapsto\Phi(n,\ell,\omega,v)
is
\left(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})\otimes\Omega,\mathcal{B}(X)\right)
-measurable for every
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
v\in X
, where
\mathcal{B}(X)
denotes the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
X
;

ii)

\Phi(n,\ell,\omega)\colon X\to X
given by
\Phi(n,\ell,\omega)v=\Phi(n,\ell,\omega,v)

forms a cocycle over
\Theta
, i.e.,

a)

\Phi(0,\ell,\omega)=\Id_{X}
for all
(\ell,\omega)\in\Omega^{+}
;

b)

for all

m,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
(\ell,\omega)\in\Omega^{+}
,

\Phi(m+n,\ell,\omega)=\Phi(m,\ell+n,\theta^{n}\omega)\circ\Phi(n,\ell,\omega).

In the case when it is clear which driving system
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu,\theta)
we consider, we will identify the pair
(\Phi,\theta)
with
\Phi
.
Let
\mathcal{L}(X)

be the Banach algebra of all bounded linear operators acting on
X
equipped with the operator norm (which we will also denote by
\|\cdot\|
). A measurable NRDS
\Phi
is called
linear
if
\Phi(n,\ell,\omega)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
for all
n,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\omega\in\Omega
.

Remark 1
.

In the current work, we focus on the future orbits for the discrete-time case, but the definition can be easily extended to the invertible and/or continuous-time case. Furthermore, we note that, although we assume the driving system
\theta
to be invertible, we do not require that
\Phi(n,\ell,\omega)

Set
\mathbb{R}^{+}=[0,+\infty)

Given a growth rate

\mu_{n}^{\prime}:=\mu_{n+1}-\mu_{n}

\varphi_{n}:=\frac{\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n}^{\prime}}.

there exist a forward

\|\Phi(n,\ell,\omega)P_{(\ell,\omega)}v\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}\leq D(\ell,\omega)\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell+n}}{\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{-\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\|v\|_{(\ell,\omega)}

(3)

3.
Admissibility with respect to a random norm

A measurable NRDS
\Phi
can be associated with an nonautonomous random difference equations in the following way. To start, for each
\omega\in\Omega
consider the random difference equation

x_{n+1}=\Phi(1,n,\theta^{n}\omega)x_{n},

(5)

with
x_{n}\in X
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. The solutions
x=(x_{n})
of (
5
) are the orbits
(\Phi(n,0,\omega)x_{0})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
. Now, considering some growth rate
\mu
, given
\omega\in\Omega
and
y=(y_{n})
, with
y_{0}=0
, we are interested in finding
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\subset X
such that

x_{n+1}=\Phi(1,n,\theta^{n}\omega)x_{n}+\varphi_{n+1}^{-1}y_{n+1},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

Similarly, given a growth rate
\mu
, and a measurable map
y\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to X
we look for measurable
x\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to X
such that

x(\ell,\omega,n+1)=\Phi(1,n+\ell,\theta^{n}\omega)x(\ell,\omega,n)+\varphi_{\ell+n+1}^{-1}y(\ell,\omega,n+1),

(6)

for
(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
where
\tilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
is a
\theta
-invariant full-measure set.

The concept of admissibility requires that for input
y
there exists a (unique) output
x
satisfying (
6
), where
x
and
y
belong to suitable Banach spaces.

3.1.
Technical lemma

Before stating our main results, we will begin with a technical lemma that
will be very useful in some estimates. This result was stated in
[
64
]
and the proof presented there was based on a result
concerning the smooth global approximation of a Lipschitz function by differentiable
functions, obtained in
[
30
]
.
It turns out that this proof has a issue. Fortunately, this issue can be overcomed with a simpler proof. For the sake of completeness, we provide this proof here.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a growth rate. If
\alpha\in\ (0,1)\ \cup\ (1,+\infty)
, for each
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
r\geq s>1
, we have

\frac{\mu_{r+1}^{1-\alpha}-\mu_{s}^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\leq\sum_{k=s}^{r}\mu_{k}^{-\alpha}\mu_{k}^{\prime}\leq\eta^{\alpha}\,\frac{\mu_{r+1}^{1-\alpha}-\mu_{s}^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}

(7)

and, for
\alpha=1
, we have

\log\frac{\mu_{r+1}}{\mu_{s}}\leq\sum_{k=s}^{r}\,\frac{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}{\mu_{k}}\leq\eta\log\frac{\mu_{r+1}}{\mu_{s}}.

(8)

Proof.

Let
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq s>1
and define

f(t)=\begin{cases}\mu_{s}+(\mu_{s+1}-\mu_{s})(t-s),&s\leq t<s+1\\
\mu_{s+1}+(\mu_{s+2}-\mu_{s-1})(t-s-1),&s\leq t<s+1\\
\cdots\ &\ \cdots\\
\mu_{s}+(\mu_{s+1}-\mu_{s})(t-r-1),&r-1\leq t\leq r.\end{cases}

Since
f
is increasing,
f^{-\alpha}
is decreasing. Furthermore,
f
is differentiable in each interval
]k,k+1[
,
k=s,\ldots,r-1
, and
f^{\prime}(t)=\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{k}=\mu_{k}^{\prime}
for
t\in]k,k+1[
,
k=s,\ldots,r-1
. Thus, since
\mu_{k+1}\leq\eta\mu_{k}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\begin{split}\eta^{-\alpha}\mu_{k}^{-\alpha}\mu_{k}^{\prime}\leq\mu_{k+1}^{-\alpha}\mu_{k}^{\prime}\leq\int_{k}^{k+1}[f(t)]^{-\alpha}f^{\prime}(t)\dt\leq\mu_{k}^{-\alpha}\mu_{k}^{\prime}.\end{split}

(9)

By (
9
),

\begin{split}\eta^{-\alpha}\sum_{k=s}^{r}\mu_{k}^{-\alpha}\mu_{k}^{\prime}&\leq\sum_{k=s}^{r}\int_{k}^{k+1}[f(t)]^{-\alpha}f^{\prime}(t)\dt\leq\sum_{k=s}^{r}\left[\frac{[f(t)^{-\alpha+1}]}{-\alpha+1}\right]_{k}^{k+1}f^{\prime}(t)\dt\\
&=\sum_{k=s}^{r}\frac{\mu_{k+1}^{-\alpha+1}-\mu_{k}^{-\alpha+1}}{-\alpha+1}=\frac{\mu_{r+1}^{-\alpha+1}-\mu_{s}^{-\alpha+1}}{-\alpha+1}.\end{split}

Thus

\sum_{k=s}^{r}\mu_{k}^{-\alpha}\mu_{k}^{\prime}\leq\eta^{\alpha}\frac{\mu_{r+1}^{1-\alpha}-\mu_{s}^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}.

Again by (
9
),

\sum_{k=s}^{r}\mu_{k}^{-\alpha}\mu_{k}^{\prime}\geq\sum_{k=s}^{r}\int_{k}^{k+1}[f(t)]^{-\alpha}f^{\prime}(t)\dt=\frac{\mu_{r+1}^{-\alpha+1}-\mu_{s}^{-\alpha+1}}{1-\alpha}.

and we obtain (
7
).

Letting
\alpha=1
and taking into account
\mu_{k+1}\leq\eta\mu_{k}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

\frac{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}{\eta\mu_{k}}\leq\frac{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}{\mu_{k+1}}\leq\int_{k}^{k+1}\frac{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}{\mu_{k+1}}\dt\leq\int_{k}^{k+1}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\dt\leq\int_{k}^{k+1}\frac{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}{\mu_{k}}\dt=\frac{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}{\mu_{k}}.

(10)

By (
10
), we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{k=s}^{r}\frac{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}{\mu_{k}}&\leq\sum_{k=s}^{r}\int_{k}^{k+1}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\dt=\sum_{k=s}^{r}\left[\ln f(t)\right]_{k}^{k+1}\\
&=\sum_{k=s}^{r}\left(\log\mu_{k+1}-\log\mu_{k}\right)=\log\frac{\mu_{r+1}}{\mu_{s}}.\end{split}

and thus

\sum_{k=s}^{r}\frac{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}{\mu_{k}}\leq\eta\log\frac{\mu_{r+1}}{\mu_{s}}.

Again by (
10
), we obtain

\sum_{k=s}^{r}\frac{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}{\mu_{k}}\geq\sum_{k=s}^{r}\int_{k}^{k+1}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\dt=\log\frac{\mu_{r+1}}{\mu_{s}},

and we get (
8
). The result follows.
∎

3.2.
From dichotomies to admissibility and vice-versa

For a full measure set
\tilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
, let
\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})
denote the space of all measurable maps
y\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to X
such that

\|y\|_{\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})}:=\sup_{(\ell,\omega,n)}\|y(\ell,\omega,n)\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}<+\infty.

It is straightforward to verify that
(\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})})
is a Banach space. By
\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
we will denote the set of all
y\in\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})
such that
y(\ell,\omega,0)=0
. Then,
\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})
.

Let
\Pi=(\Pi(\ell,\omega))_{(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}}
be a family of projections on
X
. Furthermore, let
C\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to(0,\infty)
be a
\Theta
-forward invariant random variable. By
\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
we will denote the space of all measurable
y\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to X
such that
y(\ell,\omega,0)\in\ker\Pi(\ell,\omega)

and

\|y\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})}:=\sup_{(\ell,\omega,n)}\left(C(\ell,\omega)\|y(\ell,\omega,n)\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}\right)<+\infty.

Then,
(\mathcal{Y}_{D}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{D}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})})
is a Banach space. We stress that spaces
\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
and
\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
depend on a random norm
\mathcal{N}
. However, in order to ease the notation we do not make this dependence explicit.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Phi
be a measurable linear NRDS admitting a
\mu
-dichotomy
with respect to a random norm
\mathcal{N}
.
Then, there exist a
\theta
-invariant set
\tilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
of full measure and a
\Theta
-invariant random variable
C\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to(0,\infty)
such that, for each
y\in\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
, there exists a unique
x\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
satisfying

x(\ell,\omega,n+1)=\Phi(1,n+\ell,\theta^{n}\omega)x(\ell,\omega,n)+\varphi_{\ell+n+1}^{-1}y(\ell,\omega,n+1),

(11)

for
(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

Here,
\Pi=(\Pi(\ell,\omega))_{(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}}
,
\Pi(\ell,\omega)=P_{(\ell,\omega)}
, is a family of projections corresponding to the
\mu
-dichotomy of
\Phi
.

Proof.

Take
y\in\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
and set

x^{(s)}(\ell,\omega,n)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi_{\ell+k}^{-1}\Phi(n-k,\ell+k,\theta^{k}\omega)P_{\Theta^{k}{(\ell,\omega)}}y(\ell,\omega,k)

and

x^{(u)}(\ell,\omega,n)=-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell+n+j}^{-1}\Phi(-j,\ell+n+j,\theta^{n+j}\omega)Q_{\Theta^{n+j}{(\ell,\omega)}}y(\ell,\omega,n+j),

for
(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Using (
2
) and Lemma
3.1
we have, for each
(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

\begin{split}&\left\|x^{(s)}(\ell,\omega,n)\right\|_{\Theta^{n}{(\ell,\omega)}}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi_{\ell+k}^{-1}D(\ell+k,\theta^{k}\omega)\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell+n}}{\mu_{\ell+k}}\right)^{-\lambda(\ell+k,\theta^{k}\omega)}\|y(\ell,\omega,k)\|_{\Theta^{k}{(\ell,\omega)}}\\
&\leq D(\ell,\omega)\left\|y\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})}\mu_{\ell+n}^{-\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu_{\ell+k}^{\prime}\mu_{\ell+k}^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)-1}\\
&\leq D(\ell,\omega)\left\|y\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})}\mu_{\ell+n}^{-\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\frac{\eta^{1-\lambda(\ell,\omega)}}{\lambda(\ell,\omega)}(\mu_{\ell+n+1}^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)}-\mu_{\ell+1}^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)})\\
&\leq{D(\ell,\omega)}\left\|y\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})}\frac{\eta}{\lambda(\ell,\omega)}.\end{split}

(12)

Similarly,

\begin{split}&\left\|x^{(u)}(\ell,\omega,n)\right\|\\
&\leq D(\ell+n,\theta^{n}\omega)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell+n+j}^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell+n+j}}{\mu_{\ell+n}}\right)^{-\lambda(\ell+n,\theta^{n}\omega)}\left\|y(\ell,\omega,n+j)\right\|_{\Theta^{n+j}{(\ell,\omega)}}\\
&\leq D(\ell,\omega)\left\|y\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})}\mu_{\ell+n}^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{\ell+n+j}^{\prime}\mu_{\ell+n+j}^{-\lambda(\ell,\omega)-1}\\
&\leq D(\ell,\omega)\left\|y\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})}\mu_{\ell+n}^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\eta^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)+1}}{-\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\left(\mu_{\ell+n+r+1}^{-\lambda(\ell,\omega)}-\mu_{\ell+n+1}^{-\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\right)\\
&\leq D(\ell,\omega)\left\|y\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\Omega})}\frac{\eta^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)+1}}{\lambda(\ell,\omega)},\end{split}

(13)

for
(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Let

x(\ell,\omega,n):=x^{(s)}(\ell,\omega,n)+x^{(u)}(\ell,\omega,n),\quad(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

Observe that
x
is measurable,
x(\ell,\omega,0)\in\ker P_{(\ell,\omega)}=\ker\Pi(\ell,\omega).
This together with (
12
) and (
13
) implies that
x\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}
, where

C(\ell,\omega):=\frac{\lambda(\ell,\omega)}{D(\ell,\omega)(\eta^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)+1}+\eta)},\quad(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}.

Note that
C
is
\Theta
-invariant as
D
and
\lambda
are
\Theta
-invariant. Next,

\begin{split}&x(\ell,\omega,n+1)-\Phi(1,n+\ell,\theta^{n}\omega)x(\ell,\omega,n)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\varphi_{\ell+k}^{-1}\Phi(n+1-k,\ell+k,\theta^{k}\omega)P_{\Theta^{k}{(\ell,\omega)}}y(\ell,\omega,k)\\
&\phantom{=}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi_{\ell+k}^{-1}\Phi(n+1-k,\ell+k,\theta^{k}\omega)P_{\Theta^{k}{(\ell,\omega)}}y(\ell,\omega,k)\\
&\phantom{=}-\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell+n+j}^{-1}\Phi(-j+1,\ell+n+j,\theta^{n+j}\omega)Q_{\Theta^{n+j}{(\ell,\omega)}}y(\ell,\omega,n+j)\\
&\phantom{=}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell+n+j}^{-1}\Phi(-j+1,\ell+n+j,\theta^{n+j}\omega)Q_{\Theta^{n+j}{(\ell,\omega)}}y(\ell,\omega,n+j)\\
&=\varphi_{\ell+n+1}^{-1}P_{\Theta^{n+1}{(\ell,\omega)}}y(\ell,\omega,n+1)+\varphi_{\ell+n+1}^{-1}Q_{\Theta^{n+1}{(\ell,\omega)}}y(\ell,\omega,n+1)\\
&=\varphi_{\ell+n+1}y(\ell,\omega,n+1)\end{split}

for
(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
, which yields (
11
).

It remains to establish the uniqueness of
x
. For this it is sufficient to show that if
x\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}
is such that (
11
) holds for
y\equiv 0
, that is if we have

x(\ell,\omega,n+1)=\Phi(1,n+\ell,\theta^{n}\omega)x(\ell,\omega,n)\quad\text{for $(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}$},

then
x\equiv 0
.
Take an arbitrary
(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
. Then,

x(\ell,\omega,n)=\Phi(n,\ell,\omega)x(\ell,\omega,0)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
x(\ell,\omega,0)\in\ker P_{(\ell,\omega)}
, we have that

\begin{split}\|x(\ell,\omega,0)\|_{{(\ell,\omega)}}&=\|\Phi(-k,\ell+k,\theta^{k}\omega)Q_{\Theta^{k}(\ell,\omega)}x(\ell,\omega,k)\|_{{(\ell,\omega)}}\\
&\leq D(\ell,\omega)\left(\dfrac{\mu_{\ell+k}}{\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{-\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\|x(\ell,\omega,k)\|_{\Theta^{k}{(\ell,\omega)}}\\
&\leq\frac{D(\ell,\omega)}{C(\ell,\omega)}\left(\dfrac{\mu_{\ell+k}}{\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{-\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\|x\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})}.\end{split}

Letting
k\to\infty
yields that
x(\ell,\omega,0)=0
and consequently
x(\ell,\omega,n)=0
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. We conclude that
x\equiv 0
.
∎

We now establish a partial converse to Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mu
be a growth rate such that for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
there exists an integer
q_{n}\geq n+1
with the property that there are
constants
L_{1},L_{2}>1
such that

L_{1}\leq\frac{\mu_{q_{n}}}{\mu_{n}}\leq L_{2},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

(14)

Suppose that there is a random norm
\mathcal{N}
, a
\Theta
-invariant set
\tilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
of full measure, a family of projections
\Pi=(\Pi(\ell,\omega))_{(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}}
and a
\Theta
-invariant random variable
C\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to(0,\infty)
such that, for each
y\in\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
, there exists a unique
x\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
satisfying (
11
). In addition, assume that there are
\Theta
-forward invariant random variables
\lambda\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to(0,+\infty)
and
M\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to[1,+\infty)
such that

\|\Phi(n,\ell,\omega)v\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}\leq M(\ell,\omega)\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell+n}}{\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\|v\|_{(\ell,\omega)},

(15)

for all
(n,\ell,\omega)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}
and
v\in X
.
Then,
\Phi
admits a
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to the family of norms
\mathcal{N}
.

The proof of Theorem
3.3
will be split into several lemmas.
Let

\mathcal{T}\colon\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})\to\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})

be a linear operator given by
\mathcal{T}y=x
for
y\in\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
, where
x
is the unique element of
\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
such that (
11
) holds.

Lemma 3.4
.

\mathcal{T}
is a bounded linear operator.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that
\mathcal{T}
is a closed operator. To this end, suppose that
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
such that
y_{n}\to y
in
\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
and
x_{n}:=\mathcal{T}y_{n}\to x
in
\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
. Note that

\lim_{n\to\infty}y_{n}(\ell,\omega,k)=y(\ell,\omega,k)\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}(\ell,\omega,k)=x(\ell,\omega,k),

(16)

for
(\ell,\omega,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Since
\mathcal{T}y_{n}=x_{n}
, we have that

x_{n}(\ell,\omega,k+1)=\Phi(1,k+\ell,\theta^{k}\omega)x_{n}(\ell,\omega,k)+\varphi_{\ell+k+1}^{-1}y_{n}(\ell,\omega,k+1),

(17)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\ell,\omega,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. By passing to the limit when
n\to\infty
in (
17
) and using (
16
) we obtain that

x(\ell,\omega,k+1)=\Phi(1,k+\ell,\theta^{k}\omega)x(\ell,\omega,k)+\varphi_{\ell+k+1}^{-1}y(\ell,\omega,k+1),

for
(\ell,\omega,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Hence,
\mathcal{T}y=x
which yields that
\mathcal{T}
is closed.
∎

Take
Z_{\omega}=\ker\Pi(0,\omega)
,
\omega\in\tilde{\Omega}
. For each
(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
, consider the following subspaces of
X
:

V_{(\ell,\omega)}=\left\{v\in X:\sup_{n\geq 0}\|\Phi(n,\ell,\omega)v\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}<\infty\right\}

and

Z_{(\ell,\omega)}=\begin{cases}Z_{\omega}&\,\text{ if }\ell=0\\
\Phi(\ell,0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)Z_{\theta^{-\ell}\omega}&\,\text{ if }\ell>0.\end{cases}

Given any
(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, it follows easily that

\Phi(1,\ell,\omega)V_{(\ell,\omega)}\subset V_{\Theta(\ell,\omega)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\Phi(1,\ell,\omega)Z_{(\ell,\omega)}=Z_{\Theta(\ell,\omega)}.

Lemma 3.5
.

For each
(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
we have

X=V_{(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}\oplus Z_{(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}.

(18)

Proof.

Let us start by proving the desired conclusion for
(0,\omega_{0})\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
. For
v\in X
, we
consider
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to X
defined by

x(\ell,\omega,n)=\begin{cases}v&(\ell,\omega,n)=(0,\omega_{0},0)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and

y(\ell,\omega,n)=\begin{cases}-\varphi_{1}\Phi(1,0,\omega_{0})v&(\ell,\omega,n)=(0,\omega_{0},1)\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Observe that
y\in\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
, and

x(\ell,\omega,n+1)=\Phi(1,\ell+n,\theta^{n}\omega)x(\ell,\omega,n)+\varphi_{\ell+n+1}^{-1}y(\ell,\omega,n+1),

for
(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.
Set
\tilde{x}:=\mathcal{T}y\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
. Then,

x(\ell,\omega,n+1)-\tilde{x}(\ell,\omega,n+1)=\Phi(1,\ell+n,\theta^{n}\omega)(x(\ell,\omega,n)-\tilde{x}(\ell,\omega,n)),

for
(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Consequently,

x(\ell,\omega,n)-\tilde{x}(\ell,\omega,n)=\Phi(n,\ell,\omega)(x(\ell,\omega,0)-\tilde{x}(\ell,\omega,0)),

for
(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. In particular,

x(0,\omega_{0},n)-\tilde{x}(0,\omega_{0},n)=\Phi(n,0,\omega_{0})(x(0,\omega_{0},0)-\tilde{x}(0,\omega_{0},0)).

Since
\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
, we have
\tilde{x}(0,\omega_{0},0)\in\ker\Pi_{(0,\omega_{0})}=Z_{(0,\omega_{0})}
and

x(0,\omega_{0},0)-\tilde{x}(0,\omega_{0},0)=v-\tilde{x}(0,\omega_{0},0)\in V_{(0,\omega_{0})},

which yields that

v=v-\tilde{x}(0,\omega_{0},0)+\tilde{x}(0,\omega_{0},0)\in V_{(0,\omega_{0})}+Z_{(0,\omega_{0})}.

Consider now the case
\ell_{0}\geq 1
. For
v\in X
we define
\bar{y}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to X
by

\bar{y}(\ell,\omega,n)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{\ell_{0}}v&(\ell,\omega,n)=(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},\ell_{0})\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then,
\bar{y}\in\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
. Let
\bar{x}=\mathcal{T}\bar{y}\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
. Note that
\bar{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},0)\in Z_{\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0}}
. Moreover, in one hand we have

\bar{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},\ell_{0})-\Phi(1,\ell_{0}-1,\theta^{-1}\omega_{0})\bar{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},\ell_{0}-1)=v

with

\Phi(1,\ell_{0}-1,\theta^{-1}\omega_{0})\bar{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},\ell_{0}-1)=\Phi(\ell_{0},0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0})\bar{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},0)\in Z_{(\omega_{0},\ell_{0})}.

On the other hand, since
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})

\sup_{n\geq 0}\|\Phi(n,\ell_{0},\omega_{0})\bar{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},\ell_{0})\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}=\sup_{n\geq 0}\|\bar{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega_{0},n+\ell_{0})\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}<+\infty,

which yields that
\bar{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},\ell_{0})\in V_{(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}
.
We conclude that

v=\bar{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},\ell_{0})-\Phi(1,\ell_{0}-1,\theta^{\ell_{0}-1}\omega_{0})\bar{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},\ell_{0}-1)\in V_{(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}+Z_{(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}.

Consider now any
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
v\in V_{(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}\cap Z_{(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}
. Since
v\in Z_{(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}=\Phi(\ell_{0},0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0})Z_{\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0}}
, there is
z\in Z_{\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0}}
such that

v=\Phi(\ell_{0},0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0})z
. Define
\underline{x}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to X
by

\underline{x}(\ell,\omega,n)=\begin{cases}\Phi(n,0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0})z&(\ell,\omega)=(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0})\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then,
\underline{x}\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
and
\underline{x}=\mathcal{T}0
. Therefore,
\underline{x}=0
, implying
v=\underline{x}(0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega_{0},\ell_{0})=0
. The proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

For
(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
,
let
P_{(\ell,\omega)}\colon X\to V_{(\ell,\omega)}
be the projection associated to the splitting (

&\geq\frac{L(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})C(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}{\|\mathcal{T}\|\cdot\|\Phi(\ell_{0}+n,0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega)z\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}}\log\frac{\mu_{\ell_{0}+n+1}}{\mu_{\ell_{0}+1}}\\
&\geq\frac{L(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})C(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}{\|\mathcal{T}\|\cdot\|\Phi(\ell_{0}+n,0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega)z\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}}\log\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell_{0}+n}}{\mu_{\ell_{0}}}\cdot\frac{\mu_{\ell_{0}}}{\mu_{\ell_{0}+1}}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{L(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})C(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}{\|\mathcal{T}\|\cdot\|\Phi(\ell_{0}+n,0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega)z\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}}\log\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell_{0}+n}}{\mu_{\ell_{0}}}\cdot\eta^{-1}\right).\end{split}

We conclude that

\|\Phi(\ell_{0}+n,0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega)z\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}\geq\e\|\Phi(\ell_{0},0,\theta^{-\ell_{0}}\omega)z\|_{(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})},

for
(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
provided that
\mu_{\ell_{0}+n}\geq N_{0}\mu_{\ell_{0}}
, where

N_{0}=N_{0}(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})=\eta\exp\left\{\frac{\e\|\mathcal{T}\|}{C(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})L(\ell_{0},\omega_{0})}\right\}.

We observe that
N_{0}\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to(0,\infty)
is
\Theta
-forward invariant. As in the proof of the previous lemma, let
K_{0}=K_{0}(\ell,\omega)
be the smallest positive integer such that
L_{1}^{K_{0}(\ell,\omega)}\geq N_{0}(\ell,\omega)
.

Take now arbitrary
(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Let
\gamma(\ell,0)=\ell
and
\gamma(\ell,k)=q_{\gamma(\ell,k-1)}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
r
be the largest integer such that
\ell+n\geq\gamma(\ell,rK_{0})
.
Then, by writing
K_{0}
instead of
K_{0}(\ell,\omega)

\begin{split}&\|\Phi(\ell+n,0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)z\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}\\
&\geq L(\ell,\omega)\|\Phi(\gamma(\ell,rK_{0}),0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)z\|_{(\gamma(\ell,rK_{0}),\theta^{\gamma(\ell,rK_{0})-\ell}\omega)}\\
&\geq L(\ell,\omega)\e^{r}\|\Phi(\ell,0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)z\|_{(\ell,\omega)}.\end{split}

(26)

On the other hand,

\frac{\mu_{\ell+n}}{\mu_{\ell}}\leq\frac{\mu_{\gamma(\ell,(r+1)K_{0})}}{\mu_{\ell}}\leq L_{2}^{(r+1)K_{0}},

which is equivalent to

\e^{r}\geq\frac{1}{\e}\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell+n}}{\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{1/(K_{0}\log L_{2})}.

From (

\|\Phi(\ell+n,0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)z\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}\geq\frac{L(\ell,\omega)}{\e}\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell+n}}{\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{1/(K_{0}\log L_{2})}\|\Phi(\ell,0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)z\|_{(\ell,\omega)},

(27)

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
z\in Z_{(0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)}
.

Note that

\Phi(-n,\ell+n,\theta^{\ell+n}\omega)v=\Phi(-n,\ell+n,\theta^{\ell+n}\omega)\Phi(\ell+n,0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)z=\Phi(\ell,0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)z.

Hence, (
27

\begin{split}&\|\Phi(-n,\ell+n,\theta^{\ell+n}\omega)v\|_{(\ell,\omega)}\\
&=\|\Phi(\ell,0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)z\|_{(\ell,\omega)}\\
&\leq\frac{e}{L(\ell,\omega)}\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell+n}}{\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{-1/(K_{0}\log L_{2})}\|\Phi(\ell+n,0,\theta^{-\ell}\omega)z\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}\\
&=\frac{e}{L(\ell,\omega)}\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell+n}}{\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{-1/(K_{0}\log L_{2})}\|v\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)},\end{split}

which gives the desired conclusion.
∎

(29)

4.
Robustness

In this section, we discuss the robustness of
\mu
-dichotomies for NRDS.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mu
be a growth rate satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
3.3
.
Let
\Phi,\Psi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\Omega\times X\to X
be two NRDS such that
\Phi
admits a
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to a random norm
\mathcal{N}
. In addition, assume that there are
\Theta
-forward invariant random variables
\lambda\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to(0,+\infty)
and
M\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to[1,+\infty)
such that (
15
) holds.
Then, there exists a
\Theta
-forward invariant random variable
c\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to(0,\infty)
such that if

\|(\Phi(1,\ell,\omega)-\Psi(1,\ell,\omega))v\|_{\Theta(\ell,\omega)}\leq\frac{c(\ell,\omega)}{\varphi_{\ell+1}}\|v\|_{(\ell,\omega)}

(31)

for
(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
and
v\in X
, then
\Psi
admits a
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to the random norm
\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

Let
C\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to(0,\infty)
and projections
\Pi(\ell,\omega)
,
(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
be given by Theorem
3.2
. Let
c\colon\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\to(0,\infty)
be an
\Theta
-forward random variable such that

c(\ell,\omega)\leq\rho C(\ell,\omega)\quad\text{and}\quad c(\ell,\omega)<\frac{1}{2M(\ell,\omega)},

for
(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
,
where
\rho>0
is such that
\rho\|\mathcal{T}\|<1
and
\mathcal{T}\colon\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})\to\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})

3.3
.

Take an arbitrary

. We define
\mathcal{G}\colon\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})\to\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})
by

(\mathcal{G}x)(\ell,\omega,n):=\begin{cases}0&n=0\\
\varphi_{\ell+n}\Psi_{\Phi}(1,n+\ell-1,\theta^{n-1}\omega)x(\ell,\omega,n-1)+y(\ell,\omega,n)&n>0,\end{cases}

for
(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}

\Psi_{\Phi}(n,\ell,\omega):=\Psi(n,\ell,\omega)-\Phi(n,\ell,\omega).

Note that for
x\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})

with
n>0
we have using (
31

\|(\mathcal{G}x)(\ell,\omega,n)\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}\leq\rho C(\ell,\omega)\|x(\ell,\omega,n-1)\|_{\Theta^{n-1}(\ell,\omega)}+\|y\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})},

\|\mathcal{G}x\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})}\leq\rho\|x\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})}+\|y\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})}.

In particular,
\mathcal{G}
is well-defined. Moreover, the same argument shows that

\|\mathcal{G}x_{1}-\mathcal{G}x_{2}\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{0}(\tilde{\Omega})}\leq\rho\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})},\quad\text{for $x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})$.}

(32)

Set

\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{G}\colon\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})\to\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega}).

By (
32
) and since
\rho\|\mathcal{T}\|<1
, we conclude that
\mathcal{F}
is a contraction on
\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
. Consequently, there exists a unique
x\in\mathcal{Y}_{C}^{\Pi}(\tilde{\Omega})
such that
x=\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G}x)
. Taking into account the definition of
\mathcal{T}
, we see that

\begin{split}x(\ell,\omega,n+1)&=\Phi(1,n+\ell,\theta^{n}\omega)x(\ell,\omega,n)+\Psi_{\Phi}(1,n+\ell,\theta^{n}\omega)x(\ell,\omega,n)\\
&\phantom{=}+\varphi_{n+\ell+1}^{-1}y(\ell,\omega,n+1),\end{split}

which implies that

for
(\ell,\omega,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\tilde{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Hence,
x
satisfies (
11
) with
\Psi
instead of
\Phi
. The uniqueness of

follows from the uniqueness of the fixed point of
\mathcal{F}

Next, it is easy to show that for
(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

\begin{split}\Psi(n,\ell,\omega)&=\Phi(n,\ell,\omega)\\
&\phantom{=}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Phi(n-1-j,j+\ell+1,\theta^{j+1}\omega)\Psi_{\Phi}(1,j+\ell,\theta^{j}\omega)\Psi(j,\ell,\omega).\end{split}

Hence, for arbitrary
v\in X

\begin{split}&\|\Psi(n,\ell,\omega)v\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}\\

&\phantom{\leq}+M(\ell,\omega)c(\ell,\omega)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell+n}}{\mu_{j+\ell+1}}\right)^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)}\varphi_{j+\ell+1}^{-1}\|\Psi(j,\ell,\omega)v\|_{\Theta^{j}(\ell,\omega)}.\end{split}

\begin{split}\|\Psi(n,\ell,\omega)v\|_{\Theta^{n}(\ell,\omega)}&\leq M(\ell,\omega)\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell+n}}{\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{\lambda(\ell,\omega)}e^{M(\ell,\omega)c(\ell,\omega)\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n+\ell}\varphi_{j}^{-1}}\|v\|_{(\ell,\omega)}\\

We conclude that there exists a
\Theta

and

.
By Theorem
3.3
we conclude that
\Psi
admits
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to the random norm
\mathcal{N}
.

Relation with random nonuniform

\nu_{n}\geq 1

, and let
\mathcal{N}
be a random norm. We say that a linear

\theta
-invariant subset

.

Notice that (
34
) is equivalent to say that for all

(\ell,\omega)\in\tilde{\Omega}^{+}

.

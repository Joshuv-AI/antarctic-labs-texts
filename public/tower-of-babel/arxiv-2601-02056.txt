S ​ O ​ ( 1 , d + 1 ) SO(1,d+1) symmetry of the Exact RG equation.
Semanti Dutta, B. Sathiapalan

Abstract
There is a method for constructing from first principles, a holographic bulk dual action in Euclidean A ​ d ​ S d + 1 AdS_{d+1} space for a d d -dimensional Euclidean CFT on the boundary, starting from the Polchinski’s Exact Renormalization Group (ERG) equation that describes the RG evolution of the interaction part of the boundary Wilson action. The bulk action in A ​ d ​ S d + 1 AdS_{d+1} has an S ​ O ​ ( 1 , d + 1 ) SO(1,d+1) symmetry and is obtained from the evolution operator of the Polchinski’s ERG equation by a map that involves a field redefinition and requires a special form of the UV cutoff function in the ERG equation. In this paper, we show that for any form of the cutoff function, the ERG evolution operator has an S ​ O ​ ( 1 , d + 1 ) SO(1,d+1) symmetry. The generators of the special conformal transformation depend on the cutoff function. For the special cutoff function that maps to A ​ d ​ S AdS space, the transformations have the standard form of A ​ d ​ S AdS isometry. We also show that the ERG evolution operator for the full Wilson action can be put in the same form as the Polchinski’s ERG equation by a field redefinition and consequently also has an S ​ O ​ ( 1 , d + 1 ) SO(1,d+1) symmetry for any cutoff function.

The action of the conformal transformation on the action
\mathcal{S}[\phi]
is given by

\displaystyle C_{\mu,\phi}\mathcal{S}[\phi]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}~\int dt~\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\{C_{\mu,\phi}\phi(p,t)\}\frac{\dot{\phi}(-p,t)}{\dot{G}(p,t)}-(p\rightarrow-p).

The details of the finding the transformation is provided in Appendix
D
, we state the main results below.

Scale-independent conformal transformation,
C^{I}_{\mu,\phi}

This consists of three transformations

\displaystyle C^{I}_{\mu,\phi}(p)=

\displaystyle 2(d-\Delta_{\phi})\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}+2p^{\rho}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p^{\rho}\partial p^{\mu}}-p_{\mu}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p^{\rho}\partial p_{\rho}}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle C^{1}_{\mu,\phi}+C^{2}_{\mu,\phi}+C^{3}_{\mu,\phi}.

These action of these transformations on
\mathcal{S}[\phi]
can be evaluated using the property of cutoff function
G(p,t)
stated in (
2.4
).

\displaystyle(C_{\mu,\phi}^{1}+C_{\mu,\phi}^{2}+C_{\mu,\phi}^{3})\mathcal{S}[\phi]=\int dt\int_{p}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}\dot{\phi}(p,t)\right]\dot{\phi}(-p,t)\left(\frac{d-2\Delta_{\phi}}{\dot{G}(p,t)}-p^{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\rho}}\frac{1}{\dot{G}(p,t)}\right).

(3.13)

Scale-dependent conformal transformation,
C_{\mu,\phi}^{II}

We propose a further transformation

\displaystyle C_{\mu,\phi}^{4}=-2\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t\partial p^{\mu}}.

(3.14)

After some cancellations and using the scaling property of
G(p,t)
we obtain

\displaystyle(C_{\mu,\phi}^{I}+C_{\mu,\phi}^{4})\mathcal{S}=\int dt\int_{p}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\dot{\phi}(p,t)\right]\dot{\phi}(-p,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}\frac{1}{\dot{G}(p,t)}.

(3.15)

We need to add another transformation which is also dependent on cut-off function to cancel (
3.15
).

\displaystyle C_{\mu,\phi}^{5}=-\dot{G}(p,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}\frac{1}{\dot{G}(p,t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

(3.16)

In this process too we get the following boundary term (see (
D.11
))

\displaystyle\delta_{bdry}\mathcal{S}[\phi]=-\int_{p}~\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}}dt\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}\dot{\phi}(p,t)\dot{\phi}(-p,t)}{\dot{G}(p,z)}\right].

(3.17)

Noether Charge:

We can calculate the Noether charge for conformal transformations. We focus on new terms involving
t
, i.e. the scale dependent part of
C_{\mu}
which we called
C_{\mu}^{II}
.
Thus we consider

\hat{\delta}\phi=\bar{\epsilon}^{\mu}[-2\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{\mu}}\dot{\phi}(q,t)-\dot{G}(q,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{\mu}}(\frac{1}{\dot{G}(q,t)})\dot{\phi}(q,t)].

The contribution to the Noether current
\chi_{\mu}^{\nu}
is read off from (
3.17
)

\int_{x}\chi_{\mu}^{0}(x,t)=-\int_{q}(\frac{\dot{\phi}(-q,t)}{\dot{G}(q,t)})\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{\mu}}\dot{\phi}(q,t).

(3.18)

Note that the contribution is only to the charge i.e. the time component of the current.

Simlarly we calculate using (
3.8
) the contribution to the charge

K_{\mu}^{0}
due to
\hat{\delta}\phi
. It can be calculated as:

\int_{x}K_{\mu}^{0}(x,t)=\int_{q}(\frac{\dot{\phi}(-q,t)}{\dot{G}(q,t)})[-2\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{\mu}}\dot{\phi}(q,t)-\dot{G}(q,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{\mu}}(\frac{1}{\dot{G}(q,t)})\dot{\phi}(q,t)],

Combinining with (
3.18
) gives the Noether charge

\int_{x}j_{\mu}^{0}(x,t)=\int_{q}\dot{\phi}(-q,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{\mu}}(\frac{\dot{\phi}(q,t)}{\dot{G}(q,t)}).

This is the conformal charge in Hamiltonian formalism, which has been also calculated independently in Appendix
G
(the last term in (
G.0.2
)).

Hence from (
3.12
), (
3.14
) and (
3.16

\phi(p,t)
which keeps the evolution action invariant.

\displaystyle C_{\mu,\phi}(p,t)=2(d-\Delta_{\phi})\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}+2p^{\rho}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p^{\rho}\partial p^{\mu}}-p_{\mu}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p^{\rho}\partial p_{\rho}}-2\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t\partial p^{\mu}}-\dot{G}(p,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}\frac{1}{\dot{G}(p,t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

(3.19)

d

9
Conclusions

In this paper we have shown that the Exact RG equation for a d-dimensional Euclidean field theory has an
SO(1,d+1)
invariance. We do this by identifying
SO(1,d+1)
as the symmetry of the action that occurs in the functional integral representation of the evolution operator of the ERG equation (we denote this action as
evolution action
). This is also studied in a Hamiltonian formalism by analytically continuing to Minkowski signature. The Hamiltonian generating the RG evolution is one of the generators of the
SO(1,d+1)
algebra.

This study was motivated by the ERG approach to AdS/CFT in which this
evolution action
is mapped to a scalar field action in (Euclidean) AdS space, where it has an
SO(1,d+1)
symmetry due to the isometry of AdS space
[
28
,
29
]
. The map requires a special choice of cutoff function in the ERG equation. In this paper we have shown that for
any
choice of the cutoff function this symmetry exists but the transformation law of the scalar field is non standard and depends on the cutoff function. This also makes it non polynomial in derivatives and hence quasi-local. For the special choice of cutoff function that gives AdS space, the transformations have the standard simpler form. It also explains why AdS space is special in this approach.

This result is, at least in hindsight, not surprising: The scale invariant fixed point Wilson action in
d
dimensions in general is expected to have an
SO(1,d+1)
conformal symmetry. The
evolution action
, for
any
choice of the cutoff function, preserves a fixed point. So it should have this symmetry as well.

This result lends further credence to the idea that AdS/CFT (and perhaps holography in general) can be understood in terms of ERG evolution. It also suggests that other spaces obtained by this procedure, for example flat space
[
35
]
, should also have this symmetry.

It is important to note that in our analysis we did not attempt any analysis on the boundary terms. These terms will in general modify the boundary states or Wilson action in
d
dimensions. One can try to impose proper boundary conditions to ensure the symmetry of the bulk action. However, this requires careful analysis and leaves room for further work.

The studies relating ERG to Holographic RG and AdS/CFT in
[
28
,
29
]
,
[
31
]
-
[
34
]
used the Polchinski version of Wilson’s ERG equation. This describes the RG evolution of the
interacting
part of the Wilson action and it has the particularly simple form of a diffusion equation. Another result in this paper is that the ERG equation for the
full Wilson action
can also be rewritten as a diffusion equation after a field redefinition and consequently also possesses this
SO(1,D+1)
symmetry. This should be useful in situations where ERG for the full Wilson action is required. Furthermore this means that the ERG to Holographic RG maps can be applied in this case also. This makes this approach to holography a little more general.

Abstract
. We investigate the stability with respect to homogenization of classes of integrals arising in the control-theoretic interpretation of some Hamilton–Jacobi equations. The prototypical case is the homogenization of energies with a Lagrangian consisting of the sum of a kinetic term and a highly oscillatory potential V = V per + W V=V_{\rm per}+W , where V per V_{\rm per} is periodic and W W is a nonnegative perturbation thereof. We assume that W W has zero average in tubular domains oriented along a dense set of directions. Stability then holds true; that is, the resulting homogenized functional is identical to that for W = 0 W=0 . We consider various extensions of this case. As a consequence of our results, we obtain stability for the homogenization of some steady-state and time-dependent, first-order Hamilton–Jacobi equations with convex Hamiltonians and perturbed periodic potentials. Finally, we show with an example that, for negative W W , stability may not hold. Our study revisits and, depending on the different assumptions, complements results obtained by P.-L. Lions and collaborators using PDE techniques.

1.
Introduction

The asymptotic behaviour of viscosity solutions
U_{\varepsilon}
of Hamilton-Jacobi equations of the form

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)+H_{\rm per}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\big)=0,\\
U_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=\Phi(x),\end{cases}

with
H_{\rm per}
periodic in the first variable, has been first studied by Lions, Papanicolaou, and Varadhan
[
16
]
, who proved that such solutions converge uniformly as
\varepsilon\to 0
to the solution
U
of a
homogenized problem
of the form

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U(x,t)+H_{\rm hom}(\nabla U(x,t))=0,\\
U(x,0)=\Phi(x).\end{cases}

Similar statements hold for steady-state Hamilton-Jacobi equations (see e.g.
[
9
]
).

In this paper we consider a
stability issue
for the homogenization of Hamilton-Jacobi equations, addressing the following question:
what hypotheses on a perturbation
W
ensure that viscosity solutions
\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon}
of equations of the form

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)+H_{\rm per}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\big)-W\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})=0,\\
\widetilde{U}_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=\Phi(x)\end{cases}

converge to the same
U
solution of (
1.1
)?

Some answers to this question have been given by Achdou and Le Bris
[
1
]
, who show that negative perturbations may lead to instability; that is, convergence to a different limit. In unpublished works by Lions and Souganidis, some conditions on positive
W
are given ensuring stability (see the video presentation
[
14
]
).
We note that both these results treat convex Hamiltonians, while
periodic
homogenization using the theory of viscosity solutions does not require such an assumption.

In the case of Hamiltonians
H_{\rm per}(x,\xi)
convex and coercive in the variable
\xi
, the stability question for Hamilton-Jacobi equations is related to a corresponding stability question for functionals in terms of the corresponding Lagrangian
L_{\rm per}
. Indeed, it is known that periodicity guarantees the
\Gamma
-convergence of the functionals

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}L_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon},u^{\prime}(t)\Big)dt

to a homogenized functional

F_{\rm hom}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}L_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime}(t))dt,

whose homogenized Lagrangian is the one corresponding to the homogenized Hamiltonian
H_{\rm hom}
. This correspondence is ensured by the fact that the viscosity solutions
U_{\varepsilon}
can be written in terms to the value function defined as a minimum for
F_{\varepsilon}
through the Lax–Hopf formula. As a result, the convergence of
U_{\varepsilon}
can be deduced using the Fundamental Theorem of
\Gamma
-convergence on the convergence of minima.

The stability question for Hamilton–Jacobi equations can be then formulated as a stability question with respect to
\Gamma
-convergence:
what hypotheses on a perturbation
W
ensure that the
\Gamma
-limit of

(1.2)

G_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(L_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon},u^{\prime}(t)\Big)+W\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)dt

is still the functional
F_{\rm hom}
(that is, the one given by the periodic case when
W=0
)?
Such a
\Gamma
-convergence question can be generalized and answered for general Lagrangians also depending on
t
, but such generalizations do not have an immediate connection with the Hamiltonian viewpoint. We note that in treating solutions of Hamilton–Jacobi equations we will use particular cases of results from the PDE literature, that apply to generic Hamiltonians and do not make use of the specific form assumed.

When
W\geq 0
, the condition we find is an integral condition on
W
. In the case of bounded
W
, this can be stated as

(1.3)

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{B_{R}\cap S^{r}_{\xi}}W(x)\,dx=0

for all
\xi
in a dense subset
\Xi
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and
r>0
; that is, the average of
W
is zero on stripes with a given direction in a dense set (Theorem
3.3
). In the one-dimensional case
d=1
, the condition simplifies in

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}W(x)\,dx=0

(Theorem
2.4
).
We show with an example that the average condition can indeed be required to hold only for a countable set of directions and fail otherwise. Moreover, if
d>1
we can also treat unbounded
W
under some uniform local integrability condition. We note that the condition on
W
is more general than those previously considered, but, as is common for
\Gamma
-convergence results, the information we obtain is weaker since we do not give a corrector result. Condition (
1.3
) can be compared with

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}W(x)\,dx=0

considered by the authors for the stability of elliptic homogenization
[
5
]
; that is, that the average of the perturbation on the whole space is
0
. Condition (
1.3
) highlights that for Hamilton–Jacobi equations the perturbation needs to be small on one-dimensional like sets.

We give a brief description of the arguments of the proof. Since
W\geq 0
the stability result for the
\Gamma
-limit reduces to the proof of an upper bound. The main observation is that it is sufficient to treat the case of piecewise-affine target functions with slopes in the dense set of directions
\Xi
, and that the construction of recovery sequences in the periodic case requires the use of a finite number of correctors. The sequences obtained using these correctors may lead to a large contribution of the additional term involving
W
, so cannot be used as recovery sequences for the perturbed energies, but, using the zero-average condition above, we may choose careful small variations of these correctors on which the contribution of
W
is small, and use such modified correctors to construct recovery sequences. In the one-dimensional case such modifications are not possible, but we directly show that in this case the contribution of
W
is small on the original recovery sequences. We note that these arguments are completely different from those used for elliptic homogenization in
[
5
]
, that rely on localization techniques and higher-integrability results.

In order to apply the result also to steady-state Hamilton–Jacobi equations, we additionally address the stability of integrals of the form

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\Big(L_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon},u^{\prime}(t)\Big)+W\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)e^{-\lambda t}\,dt.

Since results for such energies are not common in the literature, we prove a general
\Gamma
-convergence theorem relating
\Gamma
-convergence on finite intervals and on the half-line (Section
4.2
).
The applications to the stability of Hamilton–Jacobi equations when
W
is non-negative and satisfies (
1.3
) are finally obtained as a product of the previous results in Section
5
, both in the steady-state and evolutionary cases.

In the stability results we use non-negative perturbations
W
. We note that the sign condition on
W
cannot be dropped altogether. In the simplest case, when
L_{\rm per}(x,\xi)=L(\xi)=L_{\rm hom}(\xi)
is independent of
x
and
W\leq 0
and tends to
0
at infinity, we show that the
\Gamma
-limit of
G_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
1.2
) is given by

G_{\rm hom}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}L_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime}(t))dt+\inf W|\{t:u(t)=0\}|,

which is strictly lower than
F_{\rm hom}(u)
if
|\{t:u(t)=0\}|>0
(Section
6
).

For the sake of clarity in the presentation of the results and their proofs, we will treat a particular form of the Lagrangians (and of the Hamiltonians); namely, in the notation used above,

L_{\rm per}(x,\xi)=|\xi|^{2}+V_{\rm per}(x).

This form will only make it simpler to use Fenchel transforms, and set our problems in Hilbert spaces.
All the results we obtain can be extended to more general Lagrangians
L_{\rm per}
with
L_{\rm per}(x,\cdot)
convex and such that there exists
r>1
and constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}|\xi|^{r}\leq L_{\rm per}(x,\xi)\leq c_{2}(1+|\xi|^{r})

(see Section
4.1
).
Indeed, the only property that we need for the Lagrangians is the existence of suitable correctors, which depends only on a polynomial growth assumption of order
r>1

[
6
]
.

Notation.

We use standard notation for Sobolev spaces, in particular
H^{1}_{0}
denotes the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}
in
H^{1}
(and
W^{1,p}_{0}
its closure in
W^{1,p}
, in some remarks). We use the notation
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
for the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff (surface) measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For the notation of
\Gamma
-convergence we refer to
[
8
,
4
]
.
Due to the form of the energies we consider, we tacitly compute
\Gamma
-limits with respect to the weak topology of
H^{1}
, or equivalently with respect to the strong topology of
L^{2}
, unless otherwise stated. We say that a sequence
\Gamma
-converges preserving the boundary or initial conditions, respectively, if it
\Gamma
-converges and for every
u
there exists a recovery sequence with the same boundary or initial values as
u
.

2.
Stability results in the one-dimensional case

We separately treat the case when the function
u
is scalar. In this case the conditions on
W
are simpler, and the proof is easier by the order structure of
\mathbb{R}
.

We begin by defining the
unperturbed energies

F_{\varepsilon}
.
Let
V_{\rm per}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous
1
-periodic function, and for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
define

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+V_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)\,dt

for
u\in H^{1}(0,1)
. The limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
of such functionals is described in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(Homogenization Theorem (
[
6
]
, Proposition 15.9))
.

The
\Gamma
-limit of
F_{\varepsilon}
is the functional
F_{\rm hom}
defined by

(2.1)

F_{\rm hom}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime}(t))\,dt

for
u\in H^{1}(0,1)
,
where
f_{\rm hom}
is the convex function characterized by

f_{\rm hom}(0)=\min V_{\rm per}
and

(2.2)

f_{\rm hom}(\xi)=\min\bigg\{|\xi|\int_{0}^{1/|\xi|}\big(|v^{\prime}(t)+\xi|^{2}+V_{\rm per}(v(t)+\xi t)\big)dt:v\in H^{1}_{0}(0,1/|\xi|)\bigg\}

if
\xi\neq 0
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
f_{\rm hom}
satisfies the condition
|\xi|^{2}+\min V_{\rm per}\leq f_{\rm hom}(\xi)\leq|\xi|^{2}+\max V_{\rm per}
. By the convexity of
f_{\rm hom}
, this implies that
F_{\rm hom}
is continuous in
H^{1}(0,1)
.

Remark 2.3

(Periodic correctors)
.

Let
p_{\xi}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the
1/|\xi|
-periodic extension of a minimizer of (
2.2
), and let
w_{\xi}(t)=p_{\xi}(t)+\xi t
. Note that
V_{\rm per}(w_{\xi}(t))
is
1/|\xi|
-periodic since
V_{\rm per}(w_{\xi}(t+(1/|\xi|)))=V_{\rm per}(w_{\xi}(t)+{\rm sgn}\,\xi)=V_{\rm per}(w_{\xi}(t))
, and in the last equality we have used the fact that
V_{\rm per}
is
1
-periodic.
The scaled functions
w_{\xi,\varepsilon}(t):=\varepsilon w_{\xi}(t/\varepsilon)=\varepsilon p_{\xi}(t/\varepsilon)+\xi t
tend to
\xi t
in
L^{\infty}(0,1)
and also weakly in
H^{1}(0,1)
, while, by the periodicity and a change of variable in the integral, the functions

t\mapsto|w_{\xi,\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+V_{\rm per}\big(\frac{w_{\xi,\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon}\big)=|p^{\prime}_{\xi}(\tfrac{t}{\varepsilon})+\xi|^{2}+V_{\rm per}\big(w_{\xi}(\tfrac{t}{\varepsilon})\big),

weakly
∗
converge to the average
\int_{0}^{1}\big(|p^{\prime}_{\xi}(t)+\xi|^{2}+V_{\rm per}\big(p_{\xi}(t)+\xi t\big)\big)dt=f_{\rm hom}(\xi)
in
L^{\infty}(0,1)
.

The
perturbed energies

G_{\varepsilon}
will be defined as follows.
Given
W\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{[}0,+\infty)
a Borel function we define

G_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+V_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)+W\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)\,dt

for
u\in H^{1}(0,1)
, which is well defined because any such
u
are continuous. We can now state and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.4

(Stability Theorem)
.

Let
W\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Borel function such that

(2.3)

W\geq 0\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}W(s)\,ds=0;

then

(2.4)

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime\prime}:=\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}
, and let
F_{\rm hom}
be given by Theorem
2.1
. Since
W\geq 0
it suffices to prove that
G^{\prime\prime}\leq F_{\rm hom}
.
We start by proving this inequality at the function
u(t)=\xi t
, with
\xi\neq 0
. Thanks to the
continuity of
F_{1}
with respect to the strong convergence in
H^{1}(0,1)
, we can choose
a piecewise-affine
\frac{1}{|\xi|}
-periodic function
{p^{\delta}_{\xi}}
that minimizes the problem in (
2.2
) up to a small error
\delta>0
; that is,
{p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(0)={p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(1/|\xi|)=0
and

(2.5)

|\xi|\int_{0}^{1/|\xi|}\big(|(p^{\delta}_{\xi})^{\prime}(t)+\xi|^{2}+V_{\rm per}({p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(t)+\xi t)\big)dt\leq f_{\rm hom}(\xi)+\delta.

We additionally may assume that
(p^{\delta}_{\xi})^{\prime}+\xi\neq 0
almost everywhere since piecewise-affine functions satisfying this condition are strongly dense in
H^{1}(0,1)
. We set
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}(t)=\varepsilon{p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(t/\varepsilon)+\xi t
. We can then estimate

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}W\Big(\frac{u_{\varepsilon,\delta}(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}W\Big({p^{\delta}_{\xi}}\Big(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\Big)+\xi\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\Big)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}W\big({p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(s)+\xi s\big)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}W\big({p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(s)+\xi s\big)ds.

If
(a,b)
is an interval where
{p^{\delta}_{\xi}}
is affine, by the change of variable
x={p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(s)+\xi s
we obtain

\int_{a}^{b}W\big({p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(s)+\xi s\big)ds=\frac{1}{(p^{\delta}_{\xi})^{\prime}+\xi}\int_{{p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(a)+\xi a}^{{p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(b)+\xi b}W(x)\,dx.

Note that if
s\mapsto{p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(s)+\xi s
is monotone, then we can estimate

\int_{0}^{\frac{n}{|\xi|}}W\big({p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(s)+\xi s\big)ds\leq\max\Big\{\frac{1}{|(p^{\delta}_{\xi})^{\prime}+\xi|}\Big\}\int_{-n}^{n}W(x)\,dx.

By the periodicity of
{p^{\delta}_{\xi}}
we then obtain that

(2.6)

\limsup_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}W\big({p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(s)+\xi s\big)ds\leq C\limsup_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{-R}^{R}W(x)\,dx=0,

with
C=C(\xi,\delta)=\frac{1}{|\xi|}\max\big\{\frac{1}{|(p^{\delta}_{\xi})^{\prime}+\xi|}\big\}
.
In the general case, this inequality holds with
C
replaced by
CN
,
where
N
is the number of changes of sign of the derivative of
s\mapsto{p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(s)+\xi s
in a period.

Since
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
tends to
u(t)=\xi t
weakly in
H^{1}(0,1)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
since the average of
(p^{\delta}_{\xi})^{\prime}
vanishes by periodicity, and
t\mapsto|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+V_{\rm per}\big(\frac{u_{\varepsilon,\delta}(t)}{\varepsilon}\big)
weakly
∗
converges to the constant

|\xi|\int_{0}^{1/|\xi|}\big(|(p^{\delta}_{\xi})^{\prime}(t)+\xi|^{2}+V_{\rm per}({p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(t)+\xi t)\big)dt

in
L^{\infty}(0,1)
by periodicity of
p^{\delta}_{\xi}
and a change of variable in the integral,
first by (
2.6
) we have

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon,\delta})=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon,\delta}).

Next, successively using (
2.5
) and (
2.2
), we bound the right-hand side from above

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon,\delta})\leq f_{\rm hom}(\xi)+\delta=F_{\rm hom}(u)+\delta,

while, given that
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
tends to
u
as
\varepsilon\to 0
the left-hand side is bounded from below as

G^{\prime\prime}(u)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon,\delta}).

Finally, letting
\delta\to 0
this yields the desired inequality
G^{\prime\prime}\leq F_{\rm hom}
for
u
.

To deal with the case
u(t)=\xi t+q
with
\xi\neq 0
and
q\in\mathbb{R}
, we slightly modify the previous construction. Indeed, with fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we let
t^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\min\{t\in[0,1]:u(t)\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}\}

and
t^{1}_{\varepsilon}=t^{0}_{\varepsilon}+k_{\varepsilon}\frac{\varepsilon}{|\xi|}
, where
k_{\varepsilon}
is the largest integer such that
t^{0}_{\varepsilon}+k_{\varepsilon}\frac{\varepsilon}{|\xi|}\leq 1
. We then define

u_{\varepsilon,\delta}(t)=\begin{cases}\xi t+q&\hbox{ if }0\leq t\leq t^{0}_{\varepsilon}\\
\varepsilon{p^{\delta}_{\xi}}(\frac{t-t^{0}_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon})+\xi t+q&\hbox{ if }t^{0}_{\varepsilon}\leq t\leq t^{1}_{\varepsilon}\\
\xi t+q&\hbox{ if }t^{1}_{\varepsilon}\leq t\leq 1.\end{cases}

Since
t^{0}_{\varepsilon}\to 0
and
t^{1}_{\varepsilon}\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
the same computation as above proves that
G^{\prime\prime}(u)\leq F_{\rm hom}(u)
.

Noting that in the previous computation the recovery sequence attains the same values as
u
at the endpoints of the interval
[0,1]
, we can exhibit a recovery sequence for each piecewise-affine target function
u
such that
u^{\prime}\neq 0
almost everywhere by repeating the construction above in each interval where
u
is affine. This leads to the inequality
G^{\prime\prime}(u)\leq F_{\rm hom}(u)
for each such functions.

Finally, by Remark
2.2
, the density of piecewise-affine functions
u
such that
u^{\prime}\neq 0
almost everywhere, and the lower-semicontinuity of
G^{\prime\prime}
, we obtain
G^{\prime\prime}(u)\leq F_{\rm hom}(u)
for every function
u\in H^{1}(0,1)
.
∎

3.
Stability results in the higher-dimensional case

Let
d>1
, let
V_{\rm per}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous
1
-periodic function, and for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
define the
unperturbed energies

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+V_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)\,dt

for
u\in H^{1}((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The following result is proven in
[
6
, Theorem 15.3]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Homogenization Theorem)
.

The
\Gamma
-limit of
F_{\varepsilon}
is the functional
F_{\rm hom}
defined by

(3.1)

F_{\rm hom}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime}(t))\,dt

for
u\in H^{1}((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
where

(3.2)

f_{\rm hom}(\xi)=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\min\bigg\{\int_{0}^{T}\big(|v^{\prime}(t)+\xi|^{2}+V_{\rm per}(v(t)+t\xi)\big)dt:v\in H^{1}_{0}((0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\bigg\}.

In particular, from (
3.2
) it follows that
f_{\rm hom}(0)=\min V_{\rm per}
.
Note that contrary to the scalar case, we cannot reduce to a periodic cell problem since the functions
t\mapsto V(x+t\xi)
are quasiperiodic but not periodic.

Remark 3.2

(Almost-periodic piecewise-affine almost-correctors)
.

Given
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, formula (
3.2
) and the periodicity of
V_{\rm per}
ensure the existence of almost-correctors
p^{\delta}_{\xi}
, in a sense that will be made precise below. With fixed
\delta>0
there exists
\eta=\eta_{\delta}>0
such that
|V_{\rm per}(x+y)-V_{\rm per}(x)|<\delta
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
|y|<\eta
. By the periodicity of
V_{\rm per}
we then have that if
\tau>0
is such that there exists
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
|\tau\xi-z|<\eta_{\delta}
then

(3.3)

|V_{\rm per}(x+\tau\xi)-V_{\rm per}(x)|\leq\delta\hbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By well-known facts of ergodic theory on the torus, there exists
L_{\delta}>0
such that every interval of length
L_{\delta}
contains a
\tau
satisfying (
3.3
).

We fix

(3.4)

T\geq\frac{L_{\delta}+1}{\delta}

and a piecewise-affine function
p^{\delta}_{\xi}\in H^{1}_{0}((0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})

such that

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\big(|(p^{\delta}_{\xi})^{\prime}(t)+\xi|^{2}+V_{\rm per}(p^{\delta}_{\xi}(t)+\xi t)\big)dt\leq f_{\rm hom}(\xi)+\delta.

By the ergodicity property recalled above, and since we may assume
L_{\delta}>1
, we can construct a sequence
T_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
with

(3.5)

T_{0}=0\quad\hbox{ and }\quad T_{i}+T+1\leq T_{i+1}\leq T_{i}+T+L_{\delta}

such that (
3.3
) holds for
\tau=T_{i}
, and extend
p^{\delta}_{\xi}
by translation on each
[T_{i},T_{i}+T]
; that is
p^{\delta}_{\xi}(t)=p^{\delta}_{\xi}(t-T_{i})
, and as
0
on the remaining intervals. For use in the following proofs, we now introduce a more detailed notation for the almost-correctors.
There exist a finite family
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a subdivision of
[0,T]
by times
0=a_{0}<a_{1}<\ldots<a_{N}=T
, and such that

p^{\delta}_{\xi}
is affine with gradient
\xi_{j}
on
(a_{j-1},a_{j})+T_{i}
, with
T_{i}
as in (
3.5
). Furthermore, by continuity we may assume that we choose
p^{\delta}_{\xi}
such that
\xi_{j}+\xi\neq 0
.

Note that the construction above is a particular case of the one in the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 15.3]
and follows from the quasi-periodicity of
t\mapsto V_{\rm per}(t\xi)
.

As in the scalar case we define the
perturbed energies

G_{\varepsilon}
.
Let
W\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
be a Borel function. We then set

G_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+V_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)+W\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)\,dt

for
u\in H^{1}((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The hypotheses on
W
will be more complex than in the one-dimensional case. To state them, we introduce some notation.
For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

denote the open ball with centre

. If

For every

Theorem 3.3

(Stability Theorem – the higher-dimensional case)
.

Let
W\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
be a Borel function, and assume that

(3.7)

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{B_{R}\cap S^{r}_{\xi}}W(x)\,dx=0

for all
\xi
in a dense subset
\Xi
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and
r>0
. We also assume that
there exists
p>\frac{d}{2}
such that
W\in L^{p}_{\rm unif}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; that is,

(3.8)

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B_{1}(y)}W^{p}(x)\,dx<+\infty.

Then

(3.9)

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}.

The proof of the theorem will be obtained after some preliminary lemmas. Before them, we comment on the hypotheses on
W
, which we assume non-negative.
First, we note that (
3.8
) is satisfied if
W
is bounded, while (
3.7
) is implied by the uniform convergence of
W
to
0
at infinity, or by

(3.10)

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{B_{R}}W(x)\,dx=0,

which in turn is implied by
W\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
1\leq q<\frac{d}{d-1}
, using Hölder’s inequality.

The following example shows that (
3.7
) may be satisfied even if

\lim_{R\to+\infty}W(Rx)=1

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which implies, if
W
is bounded,

\langle W\rangle:=\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}W(x)\,dx=|B_{1}|,

using the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

Example 3.4
.

Let
d=2
and let
W\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,+\infty)
be such that

W(x)=0
if
x\in A
and
W(x)=1
otherwise, where

is constructed as follows.

For

, we define the subsets of

and let

. Note that if

For each

. This parabola is constructed so that

(3.12)

A^{k}_{h}\subset\{\rho(\cos\theta,\sin\theta):\rho\geq 2^{k},|\theta-\theta^{k}_{h}|\leq 4^{-k}\}.

The set

is defined as

A=\bigcup\big\{A^{k}_{h}:k\geq 0,h\hbox{ odd, }1\leq h\leq 2^{k}\big\}.

Given
\xi
in the dense set

\rho>0
and
\theta^{k}_{h}
such that

. Hence,

\frac{1}{R}\int_{B_{R}\cap S^{r}_{\xi}}W(x)\,dx\leq\frac{1}{R}|B_{R_{0}}|,

and condition (
3.7
) is satisfied. Note that also (
3.8
) is satisfied.

Let now

\widehat{D}:=\bigcap_{m=2}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k=m}^{\infty}(D_{k}+[-4^{-k},4^{-k}])

Since

.

We claim that if

. Indeed, writing

. Hence there exists

for all

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
W
satisfy (
3.8
). Then for all
\alpha>0
such that
1<\alpha d<p
and for all
r>0
we have

(3.14)

\int_{S^{d-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{r^{1/\alpha}}W(t^{\alpha}\theta)\,dt\Big)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\theta)\leq r^{\beta}C_{d,\alpha,p}\Big(\int_{B_{r}}W^{p}(x)\,dx\Big)^{1/p},

where
\beta=\frac{p-\alpha d}{\alpha p}>0
and
C_{d,\alpha,p}:=\Big(\frac{p-1}{p-\alpha d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,
where
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
denotes the
(d-1)
-dimensional (surface) Hausdorff measure, and
S^{d-1}
is the boundary of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma=\frac{\alpha d-1}{p}
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Using Hölder’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{S^{d-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{r^{1/\alpha}}W(t^{\alpha}\theta)\,dt\Big)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{S^{d-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{r^{1/\alpha}}W^{p}(t^{\alpha}\theta)t^{\gamma p}\,dt\Big)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\theta)\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\int_{S^{d-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{r^{1/\alpha}}t^{-\gamma q}\,dt\Big)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\theta)\Big)^{1/q}.

By the change of variable
\rho=t^{\alpha}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r^{1/\alpha}}W^{p}(t^{\alpha}\theta)t^{\gamma p}\,dt=\int_{0}^{r^{1/\alpha}}W^{p}(t^{\alpha}\theta)t^{\alpha d-1}\,dt=\int_{0}^{r}W^{p}(\rho\theta)\rho^{d-1}\,d\rho,

while

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r^{1/\alpha}}t^{-\gamma q}\,dt=\int_{0}^{r^{1/\alpha}}t^{-\frac{\alpha d-1}{p-1}}\,dt=\tfrac{p-1}{p-\alpha d}\,r^{\frac{p-\alpha d}{\alpha(p-1)}}.

Inserting these equalities in the inequality obtained above, we prove the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

satisfy (

) with

, and let

\displaystyle\gamma(-r^{1/\alpha})=x_{0},\quad\gamma(r^{1/\alpha})=y_{0},\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\int_{-r^{1/\alpha}}^{r^{1/\alpha}}W(\gamma(t))dt\leq r^{\frac{p-\alpha d}{\alpha p}}K_{d,\alpha,p}\Big(\int_{B_{2r}(\frac{x_{0}+y_{0}}{2})}W^{p}(x)\,dx\Big)^{1/p},

.

By a translation and rotation argument it is not restrictive to suppose that the middle point

of the segment between

y_{0}
is

, and
x_{0}=\frac{r}{2}e_{1}
and

. Let

be the symmetry hyperplane, let
C
be the open ball in

defined by

and let

.
Note that

belongs to

if and only if

.

For

we use the notation
\widehat{\theta}=(-\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{d})
. By Lemma

, we have

\int_{\Theta}\Big(\int^{0}_{-r^{1/\alpha}}W\Big(\frac{r}{2}e_{1}+(r^{1/\alpha}+t)^{\alpha}\theta\Big)\,dt\Big)d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\theta)\leq r^{\beta}C_{d,\alpha,p}\Big(\int_{B_{r}(\frac{r}{2}e_{1})}W^{p}(x)\,dx\Big)^{1/p},

\displaystyle\int^{0}_{-r^{1/\alpha}}W\Big(\frac{r}{2}e_{1}+(r^{1/\alpha}+t)^{\alpha}\theta\Big)\,dt+\int_{0}^{r^{1/\alpha}}W\Big(-\frac{r}{2}e_{1}+(r^{1/\alpha}-t)^{\alpha}\widehat{\theta}\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle r^{\beta}2\frac{C_{d,\alpha,p}}{\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Theta)}\Big(\int_{B_{2r}(0)}W^{p}(x)\,dx\Big)^{1/p}.

(3.20)

\gamma(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{r}{2}e_{1}+(t+r^{1/\alpha})^{\alpha}t(\theta)\theta&\hbox{ if $t\in[-r^{1/\alpha},0]$}\cr-\frac{r}{2}e_{1}+(r^{1/\alpha}-t)^{\alpha}t(\theta)\widehat{\theta}&\hbox{ if $t\in[0,r^{1/\alpha}]$}\end{cases}

in
[a_{i\,j-1},a_{ij}]

Taking into account this estimate, summing up inequalities (

\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}|(\widetilde{w}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt\leq\kappa_{\eta}\Big(\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}|(w^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt+2(N+1)\big(\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon T}+1\big)\tfrac{2\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\Big).

We set
\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\varepsilon\,\widetilde{w}^{\delta}_{\xi}(t/\varepsilon)
, recall that
u^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\varepsilon w^{\delta}_{\xi}\big(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\big)
. We then have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|(\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}|(\widetilde{w}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\kappa_{\eta}\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}|(w^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt+2\kappa_{\eta}(N+1)\big(\tfrac{1}{T}+\varepsilon\big)\tfrac{2\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha-1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa_{\eta}\int_{0}^{1}|(u^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt+C_{\delta}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha-1}{\alpha}},

where we have taken into account that we may suppose
\varepsilon\leq 1
and
\eta
small enough so that
\kappa_{\eta}\leq 2
,
with
C_{\delta}
a positive constant depending on
\delta
but independent of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
.
Noting that
\|\widetilde{w}^{\delta}_{\xi}-w^{\delta}_{\xi}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\eta^{1/\alpha}+2\eta

we also obtain

\int_{0}^{1}V_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}V_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{u^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)dt+\omega(2\eta^{1/\alpha}+2\eta)

where

is a modulus of continuity for
V_{\rm per}
.
We then obtain

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})\leq\kappa_{\eta}F_{\varepsilon}(u^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})+\omega(2\eta^{1/\alpha}+2\eta)+C_{\delta}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha-1}{\alpha}},

Hence, by (
3.23
) we obtain

(3.33)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})\leq\kappa_{\eta}(f_{\rm hom}(\xi)+C_{\xi}\delta)+\omega(2\eta^{1/\alpha}+2\eta)+C_{\delta}\eta^{\frac{2\alpha-1}{\alpha}}.

The perturbation term is estimated as follows. We have

(3.34)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}W\Big(\frac{\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}W\Big(\widetilde{w}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)\,dt=\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}W(\widetilde{w}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(s))ds.

Using (
3.27
)–(
3.30
), and (
3.24
), we have

(3.35)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/\varepsilon}W(\widetilde{w}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(s))ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\int^{a_{ij}}_{a_{i\,j-1}}W(x_{i\,j-1}+z_{ij}+(s-a_{i\,j-1})(\xi_{j}+\xi))ds

\displaystyle+2(i_{\varepsilon}+1)(N+1)C\eta^{\frac{p-\alpha d}{\alpha p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\eta}\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\int_{C_{ij}}W(x)dx+2\big(\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon T}+1\big)(N+1)C\eta^{\frac{p-\alpha d}{\alpha p}}.

Using this estimate in (
3.34
), we obtain

(3.36)

\int_{0}^{1}W\Big(\frac{\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,dt\leq\varepsilon C_{\eta}\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\int_{C_{ij}}W(x)dx+C_{\delta}\eta^{\frac{p-\alpha d}{\alpha p}}.

By the boundedness of
p^{\delta}_{\xi}
there exists

, independent of

and

, such that the image of
\widetilde{w}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}
restricted to

is contained in

. Hence,

\sum_{j=1}^{N=1}\int_{C_{ij}}W(x)dx\leq(N+1)\int_{T_{i}\xi+B_{M_{\delta}}}W(x)dx.

Recalling that
T_{i}\geq iT
, we note that at most
K_{\delta}:=\lfloor\frac{4M_{\delta}}{T|\xi|}\rfloor+1
such balls intersect, so that, in the notation (
3.6
),

(3.37)

\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\int_{C_{ij}}W(x)dx\leq(N+1)K_{\delta}\int_{B_{R_{\varepsilon}}\cap S^{M_{\delta}}_{\xi}}W(x)dx,

where

. Note that

for
\varepsilon
small enough, so that,
thanks to (
3.36
) and (
3.37
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}W\Big(\frac{\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,dt\leq\varepsilon C_{\eta}(N+1)K_{\delta}\int_{B_{R_{\varepsilon}}\cap S^{M}_{\xi}}W(x)dx+C_{\delta}\eta^{\frac{p-\alpha d}{\alpha p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\eta}\frac{2|\xi|(N+1)K_{\delta}}{R_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{R_{\varepsilon}}\cap S^{M}_{\xi}}W(x)dx+C_{\delta}\eta^{\frac{p-\alpha d}{\alpha p}}

for
\varepsilon
small enough, and

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}W\Big(\frac{\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,dt\leq C_{\delta}\eta^{\frac{p-\alpha d}{\alpha p}}

by (
3.7
) and (
3.4

We then obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})\leq\kappa_{\eta}(f_{\rm hom}(\xi)+C_{\xi}\delta)+\omega(2\eta^{1/\alpha}+2\eta)+C_{\delta}\eta^{\frac{p-\alpha d}{\alpha p}},

and, noting that
\kappa_{\eta}\to 1
as
\eta\to 0

\eta\to 0
and then
\delta\to 0
, given

we obtain (
3.22
) for a suitable choice of the parameters
\delta
and
\eta
. By the arbitrariness of
\lambda>0
we obtain that

\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq f_{\rm hom}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime}(t))\,dt

for affine functions
u(t)=t\xi
with
\xi\in\Xi
.

In the case
q\neq 0
we translate the recovery sequence
u^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}
constructed above for
F_{\varepsilon}
by
\varepsilon\lfloor\frac{q}{\varepsilon}\rfloor
, where this vector is defined component-wise by taking the integer parts of the components, and then use the same argument as above to construct a recovery sequence
\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}
for

. Thanks to the periodicity of

this sequence provides a good upper bound, but it does not match the boundary conditions, since
\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(0)-u(0)=\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(1)-u(1)=\varepsilon\lfloor\frac{q}{\varepsilon}\rfloor-q
. In order to match the boundary conditions, we can proceed similarly to the case above, first applying Lemma
3.6
with
x_{0}=q
and

, and similarly to the second endpoint. Note that we have
|x_{0}-y_{0}|\leq\sqrt{d}
. Hence, if we let, respectively,
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\colon[-d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}},d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the functions given by Lemma
3.6
at both endpoints, we can define the functions
\widehat{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}\colon[-2\varepsilon d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}},1+2\varepsilon d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}]
as

\widehat{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}\varepsilon\gamma_{0}\big(\frac{t+\varepsilon d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}{\varepsilon}\big)&\hbox{ if }t\in[-2\varepsilon d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}},0]\\
u^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(t)&\hbox{ if }t\in[0,1]\\
\varepsilon\gamma_{1}\big(\frac{t-1-\varepsilon d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}{\varepsilon}\big)&\hbox{ if }t\in[1,1+2\varepsilon d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}].\end{cases}

Note that Lemma
3.6
ensures that the contribution to the energy in the two extreme intervals is of order
\varepsilon
. Finally, we define
\widetilde{u}^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}
by an affine change of variables rescaling the domain to
[0,1]
.

We now turn our attention to the case of a piecewise-affine target function
u
.
Namely, we assume that
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{K}=1
and

We can use the construction just described for

in the place of

, thus obtaining that for such functions

(3.38)

\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{K}(t_{j}-t_{j-1})f_{\rm hom}(\xi_{j})=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime}(t))dt=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u).

Note that in this argument it is essential that we are able to maintain the values at all

in the
construction of the recovery sequences in each interval

Finally, if

then we may take a sequence of piecewise-affine functions

strongly converging to

and

\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup
is a lower-semicontinuous functional, so that, by (

\displaystyle\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\Big(\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u_{j})\Big)

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{1}f_{\rm hom}(u_{j}^{\prime}(t))dt=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime}(t))dt,

∎

Remark 3.7

(Stability for uniformly almost-periodic potentials)
.

The construction of the recovery sequences in the previous proof is based on the possibility of having almost-periodic correctors. This is the case also if
V_{\rm per}
is uniformly almost periodic; that is, it is the uniform limit of (possibly incommensurate) trigonometric polynomials. We refer to
[
6
, Chapter 15]
for details.

4.
Extensions

In this section we extend the previous results by considering more general Lagrangians with possibly different growth conditions, and by examining the case of unbounded time intervals.

4.1.
Extension to general integrands

The argument in the proof of the stability result relies on showing that a suitable small variation of almost-correctors gives test functions that are negligible for the perturbation potential
W

and are still recovery sequences for the unperturbed part. This argument can be repeated whenever
almost-correctors as described in Section
3
exist, and in particular if the energy density
|\xi|^{2}+V(x)
is replaced by a periodic Lagrangian
L
, as in the following result.

In the following result, given
r>1
the
\Gamma
-limits are computed with respect to the weak topology of
W^{1,r}
, or equivalently with respect to the strong topology of
L^{r}
.

Theorem 4.1

(Stability Theorem – general Lagrangians)
.

Let
W\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
be a bounded Borel function satisfying (
3.7
). Let
L_{\rm per}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
be a Carathéodory function such that
x\mapsto L_{\rm per}(x,\xi)
is
(0,1)^{d}
-periodic for all
\xi
and
r>1
,
c_{1},c_{2}>0
exist such that

(4.1)

c_{1}|\xi|^{r}\leq L_{\rm per}(x,\xi)\leq c_{2}(1+|\xi|^{r})

for all
(x,\xi)
, and let

(4.2)

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}L_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon},u^{\prime}(t)\Big)dt\ \hbox{ and }\ G_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(L_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon},u^{\prime}(t)\Big)+W\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)dt

be defined on
W^{1,r}((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then

(4.3)

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{1}L_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime})dt,

where
L_{\rm hom}
satisfies the asymptotic homogenization formula

(4.4)

L_{\rm hom}(\xi)=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\min\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}L_{\rm per}(v(t)+t\xi,v^{\prime}(t)+\xi)dt:v\in W^{1,r}_{0}((0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})\Big\}.

Proof.

The Lagrangian
L_{\rm per}
satisfies the hypotheses of
[
6
, Theorem 15.3]
, which states the convergence of
F_{\varepsilon}
and their characterization in terms of
L_{\rm hom}
.
The construction of almost-correctors is carried on in the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 15.5]
, after which the proof of Theorem
3.3
can be followed word for word.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that for simplicity we have required that
W
be bounded. Otherwise, (

) must be assumed for some

, for which the analog of Lemma

with

. Note however that in the applications to Hamilton-Jacobi equations

Remark 4.3

4.2.

(0,+\infty)

We now consider the extension of the
\Gamma
-convergence stability result to some functionals defined on functions on the half line, in view of the applications to steady-state Hamilton-Jacobi equations.

Let
r>1
be a fixed exponent, and let
\lambda>0
be a given parameter. We define the space

W^{1,r}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})

we write
H^{1}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the place of
W^{1,2}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proposition 4.5
.

The set of functions
u\in W^{1,r}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})
that are equal to
0
outside some bounded interval is dense in
W^{1,r}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{1,r}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\varphi(t)=1
for
t\leq 0
,
\varphi(t)=0
for
t\geq 1
and
|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|\leq 2
for all
t
.
For all
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
with

let
u_{j,k}
be defined as equal to
u
on
(0,j)
and to
u(j)\varphi(t-k)
on
(j,+\infty)
. Then

\displaystyle\|u_{j,k}-u\|^{r}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{j}^{+\infty}|u^{\prime}(t)+\varphi^{\prime}(t-k)u(j)|^{r}e^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{r}\Big(\int_{j}^{+\infty}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{r}e^{-\lambda t}dt+|u(j)|^{r}\int_{k}^{k+1}e^{-\lambda t}dt\Big)

for some positive constant
C_{r}
depending only on

.
This shows that
\lim\limits_{j\to+\infty}\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\|u_{j,k}-u\|^{r}_{\lambda}=0
and proves the claim.
∎

For all
\varepsilon>0
let
L_{\varepsilon}\colon(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)

.

for

.

For all
\varepsilon>0

Preliminarily to the proof of this results, we state the following lemma

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose that
L_{\varepsilon}
and
L_{0}
be Borel functions satisfying hypotheses
(i)
and
(ii)
of the previous theorem, and let
\phi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)

\mathcal{L}^{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(u,I)=\int_{I}L_{\varepsilon}(t,u(t),u^{\prime}(t))\phi(t)dt,\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\mathcal{L}^{\phi}_{0}(u,I)=\int_{I}L_{0}(t,u(t),u^{\prime}(t))\phi(t)dt,

\mathcal{L}^{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,I)

\Gamma

\mathcal{L}^{\phi}_{0}(\cdot,I)

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\to u

Conversely, taking

\phi
.

Proof of Theorem

.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence in
W^{1,r}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})
converging weakly to
u
.
Then
u_{\varepsilon}
converges to
u
weakly in
W^{1,r}((0,t_{0});\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all

. By the lemma above with
\phi(t)=e^{-\lambda t}
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{t_{0}}L_{0}(t,u(t),u^{\prime}(t))e^{-\lambda t}dt\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}L_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{\varepsilon}(t),u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t))e^{-\lambda t}dt\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

Taking the limit as
t_{0}\to+\infty
we obtain

\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{0}(u)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

To prove the upper bound we reason by density. To that end, we first note that
\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{0}
is continuous with respect to the strong topology of
W^{1,r}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})
, thanks to (iv) and a generalized version of the Dominated Convergence Theorem. Therefore, in view of Proposition
4.5
it is sufficient to consider a target function

such that there exists
t_{0}
such that
u(t)=0
if

. For all
\tau>t_{0}
, by Lemma
4.7
there exist a sequence
u_{\varepsilon}
converging weakly in
W^{1,r}((0,\tau);\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
u

and such that
u_{\varepsilon}(\tau)=u(\tau)=0
,
u_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly in
W^{1,r}((0,\tau);\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{\tau}L_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{\varepsilon}(t),u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t))e^{-\lambda t}dt=\int_{0}^{\tau}L_{0}(t,u(t),u^{\prime}(t))e^{-\lambda t}dt.

We extend
u_{\varepsilon}
by setting
u_{\varepsilon}(t)=0
if
t\geq\tau
, and compute

\displaystyle\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}L^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}L_{0}(t,u(t),u^{\prime}(t))e^{-\lambda t}dt+\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\tau}^{+\infty}L_{\varepsilon}(t,0,0)e^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{0}(u)+C\int_{\tau}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}dt,

where in the last inequality we have used property (iii). By the arbitrariness of
\tau\geq t_{0}
we obtain the upper bound.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Let

We can apply Theorem

Theorem 4.9
.

Let

, and let

Corollary 4.10
.

Under the hypotheses of the previous theorem, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\inf_{u(0)=x}G^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\inf_{u(0)=x}F^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\min_{u(0)=x}F^{\lambda}_{\rm hom}(u).

Proof.

For all
u\in H^{1}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
u(0)=x

there exists a sequence
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
weakly in

and

\displaystyle F^{\lambda}_{\rm hom}(u)\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k})=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\inf_{v(0)=x}F^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(v)

∎

5.
Stability for Hamilton–Jacobi equations

In this section we use the
\Gamma
-convergence approach to recover some stability results obtained using PDE techniques and presented by P.-L. Lions in his lectures
[
14
]
. Although our results require specific hypotheses on the periodic Hamiltonian, our assumptions on the non-negative perturbation
W
are much weaker (see (
3.7
)) than those considered in
[
14
]
.

Using the notation of Section
3
we consider continuous and periodic
V_{\rm per}
.
In this section we suppose that the perturbation
W
satisfies (
3.7
) and

(5.1)

W
is bounded and uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

and use the notation

(5.2)

\displaystyle L_{\rm per}(x,\xi)=|\xi|^{2}+V_{\rm per}(x),

\displaystyle L(x,\xi)=|\xi|^{2}+V_{\rm per}(x)+W(x),

(5.3)

\displaystyle H_{\rm per}(x,\xi)=\tfrac{1}{4}|\xi|^{2}-V_{\rm per}(x),

\displaystyle H(x,\xi)=\tfrac{1}{4}|\xi|^{2}-V_{\rm per}(x)-W(x),

(5.4)

\displaystyle L_{\rm hom}(\xi)=f_{\rm hom}(\xi),

\displaystyle H_{\rm hom}(\xi)=f^{*}_{\rm hom}(\xi),

where
*
denotes the Fenchel conjugate.

Analogous results can be obtained in the case of more general Lagrangians as in Section
4.1
and the related Hamiltonians.

5.1.
Steady-state Hamilton–Jacobi equations

We fix
\lambda>0
. We observe that, thanks to (
5.1
), for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a unique viscosity solution
U_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

(5.5)

\lambda U_{\varepsilon}(x)+H\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla U_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)=0,

and likewise there exists a unique viscosity solution
U\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

(5.6)

\lambda U(x)+H_{\rm hom}(\nabla U(x))=0.

The existence is proved as a particular case of
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
and the uniqueness can be deduced from the example after
[
15
, Remark 1.15]
.
In the unperturbed case, when
W=0
, equation (
5.5
) reduces to

(5.7)

\lambda U_{\varepsilon}(x)+H_{\rm per}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla U_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)=0.

The convergence of the solutions of (
5.7
) to the solution of (
5.6
) can be obtained using the techniques introduced in the fundamental unpublished paper by Lions, Papanicolaou, and Varadhan
[
16
]
(see also
[
9
,
7
]
).

We prove the following stability result.

Theorem 5.1

(Stability for steady-state Hamilton–Jacobi equations)
.

Let
V_{\rm per}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous
1
-periodic function, and let

W\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded and uniformly continuous function satisfying (
3.7
). With fixed
\lambda>0
, for every
\varepsilon>0
let
U_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the unique viscosity solution of (
5.5
)
and let
U\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the unique viscosity solution of (
5.6
).
Then
U_{\varepsilon}
tends to
U
uniformly on compact sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

By a classical result, the solutions
U_{\varepsilon}
and
U
are given by

(5.8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}(x)=\min\Big\{\int_{0}^{+\infty}L\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon},u^{\prime}(t)\Big)e^{-\lambda t}dt:u\in H^{1}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ u(0)=x\Big\},

(5.9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle U(x)=\min\Big\{\int_{0}^{+\infty}L_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime}(t))e^{-\lambda t}dt:u\in H^{1}_{\lambda}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ u(0)=x\Big\}.

For a proof we refer to
[
3
, Chapter III, Proposition 2.8]
(see also
[
11
,
13
]
).
Corollary
4.10
gives the pointwise convergence of
U_{\varepsilon}(x)
to
U(x)
.
In order to prove the uniform convergence on compact sets it suffices to show a uniform bound for the solutions in
W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. First, as
U_{\varepsilon}
is concerned we note that, since
\frac{1}{\lambda}\inf(V_{\rm per}+W)
and
\frac{1}{\lambda}\sup(V_{\rm per}+W)
are a viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution of (
5.5
), respectively, by the comparison principle (see for instance
[
3
, Chapter II, Theorem 3.5]
) we have

\inf(V_{\rm per}+W)\leq\lambda U_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\sup(V_{\rm per}+W)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. From this estimate we obtain a uniform bound for
U_{\varepsilon}
and by coerciveness thus for
\nabla U_{\varepsilon}
. Indeed, from equation (
5.5
) we have

\frac{1}{4}|\nabla U_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}+\lambda U_{\varepsilon}(x)=V_{\rm per}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)+W\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\Big)

and hence obtain a bound for
|\nabla U_{\varepsilon}(x)|
uniform with respect to
\varepsilon
and
x
.
The uniform convergence on compact sets follows from Ascoli–Arzelà’s theorem.
∎

5.2.
Time-dependent Hamilton–Jacobi equations

In this section
\Phi
will be a fixed bounded uniformly continuous function, and
\nabla
will denote the gradient with respect to
x
.
It is known that the Cauchy problem for the evolution equations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,+\infty)
given by

(5.10)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)+H\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\big)=0,\\
U_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=\Phi(x)\end{cases}

and

(5.11)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U(x,t)+H_{\rm hom}(\nabla U(x,t))=0,\\
U(x,0)=\Phi(x).\end{cases}

admit a unique viscosity solution (see
[
15
, Theorem 9.1]
and
[
10
, Chapter 10]
). In
[
16
]
it is proved that when
W=0
the solutions of

(5.12)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)+H_{\rm per}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\big)=0,\\
U_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=\Phi(x)\end{cases}

converge uniformly to the viscosity solution of the homogenized equation (
5.11
).
We now derive a stability result, showing that the same result holds also for the viscosity solutions corresponding to
H
.

Theorem 5.2

(Stability for evolutionary Hamilton–Jacobi equations)
.

Let
V_{\rm per}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous
1
-periodic function, and let

W\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded and uniformly continuous function satisfying (
3.7
). Let
H
be given by (
5.3
) and let
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded and uniformly continuous function. For every
\varepsilon>0
let
U_{\varepsilon}
be the viscosity solution of (
5.10
)
and let
U
be the viscosity solution of (
5.11
). Then
U_{\varepsilon}
tends to
U
uniformly on compact sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,+\infty)
.

Proof.

To prove this result we use the characterization of viscosity solutions using the Lax formula (see for instance
[
12
]
): for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
we have

(5.13)

U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\min\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{t}L\Big(\frac{u(\tau)}{\varepsilon},u^{\prime}(\tau)\Big)d\tau+\Phi(u(0)):u\in H^{1}((0,t);\mathbb{R}^{d}),u(t)=x\Bigr\},

and

(5.14)

\displaystyle U(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{t}L_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime}(\tau))d\tau+\Phi(u(0)):u\in H^{1}((0,t);\mathbb{R}^{d}),u(t)=x\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\Bigl\{t\,L_{\rm hom}\Big(\frac{x-y}{t}\Big)+\Phi(y):y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\Bigr\}.

To prove the result it is enough to show that for all
x_{\varepsilon}\to x_{0}
we have

(5.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}U_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})=U(x_{0},t_{0})\hbox{ if }t_{\varepsilon}\to t_{0}>0,

(5.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}U_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})=\Phi(x_{0})\hbox{ if }t_{\varepsilon}\to 0,\hbox{ with }t_{\varepsilon}>0.

To prove (
5.15
), we write (
5.13
) in the form

(5.17)

U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\inf\big\{S_{\varepsilon}(x,t,y)+\Phi(y):y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\big\},

where

S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,t)=\min\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{t}L\Big(\frac{u(\tau)}{\varepsilon},u^{\prime}(\tau)\Big)d\tau:u\in H^{1}((0,t);\mathbb{R}^{d}),u(0)=y,u(t)=x\Bigr\}.

We fix
x_{\varepsilon}\to x_{0}
and
t_{\varepsilon}\to t_{0}>0
. We claim that for fixed
y
we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})=S_{\hom}(y,x_{0},t_{0}),

where

\displaystyle S_{\rm hom}(y,x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{t}L_{\rm hom}(u^{\prime}(\tau))d\tau:u\in H^{1}((0,t);\mathbb{R}^{d}),u(0)=y,u(t)=x\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\,L_{\rm hom}\Big(\frac{x-y}{t}\Big).

Note that

(5.18)

U(x,t)=\inf\big\{S_{\rm hom}(x,t,y)+\varphi(y):y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\big\},

We first prove some equi-continuity estimates for
S_{\varepsilon}
, uniform with respect to
\varepsilon
.
With fixed
x,y
, we examine
S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,\cdot)
. If
t_{1}<t_{2}
we have

(5.19)

S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,t_{2})\leq S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,t_{1})+M(t_{2}-t_{1}),

where
M=\max(V_{\rm per}+W)
. This is obtained by extending test functions to the constant value
x
in
(t_{1},t_{2})
. Conversely, noting that

\int_{0}^{t_{2}}L\Big(\frac{u(\tau)}{\varepsilon},u^{\prime}(\tau)\Big)d\tau=\frac{t_{2}}{t_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}L\Big(\frac{v(\sigma)}{\varepsilon},\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}v^{\prime}(\sigma)\Big)d\sigma,

where
u
is a minimizer for
S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,t_{2})
, and
v(\sigma)=u(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\sigma)
, and that

\Big|L\Big(x,\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\xi\Big)-L\Big(x,\xi\Big)\Big|=\Big(\Big(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\Big)^{2}-1\Big)|\xi|^{2},

we obtain

(5.20)

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,t_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,t_{2})+\Big(\Big(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\Big)^{2}-1\Big)\int_{0}^{t_{2}}|u^{\prime}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,t_{2})+\Big(\Big(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\Big)^{2}-1\Big)\Big(Mt_{2}+\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t_{2}}\Big).

We finally deduce that, if
0<a\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq b
and
x,y\in B_{R}
then

(5.21)

|S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,t_{1})-S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,t_{2})|\leq C(a,b,R)(t_{2}-t_{1}).

As for the properties of
S_{\varepsilon}(y,\cdot,\cdot)
, for given
x_{1},x_{2}\in B_{R}
,
0<a\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq b
, and
\delta>0
, we first have

(5.22)

S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{2},t_{2}+\delta)\leq S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{1},t_{1})+M(t_{2}-t_{1}+\delta)+\frac{|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}+\delta},

obtained by extending test functions by an affine function in
(t_{1},t_{2})
.
Using (
5.21
), we obtain

(5.23)

S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{2},t_{2})\leq S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{1},t_{1})+M(t_{2}-t_{1}+\delta)+\frac{|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{2}}{\delta}+C(a,b,R)\delta,

Conversely, we have, using (
5.21
) in the first inequality and then
(
5.23
) with
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
replaced by
t_{2}
and
t_{2}+(t_{2}-t_{1})
, respectively,
and
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
interchanged,

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{1},t_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{1},t_{2}+(t_{2}-t_{1}))+2C(a,b,R)(t_{2}-t_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{2},t_{2})+M(t_{2}-t_{1}+\delta)+2R\frac{|x_{2}-x_{1}|}{\delta}+2C(a,b,R)(t_{2}-t_{1}).

Together with (
5.23
), this gives

\displaystyle|S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{1},t_{1})-S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{2},t_{2})|\leq M(t_{2}-t_{1}+\delta)+2R\frac{|x_{2}-x_{1}|}{\delta}+2C(a,b,R)(t_{2}-t_{1}+\delta).

If
|t_{2}-t_{1}|\leq\delta
and
|x_{2}-x_{1}|<\delta^{2}
then we have

|S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{1},t_{1})-S_{\varepsilon}(y,x_{2},t_{2})|\leq 2\big(M+R+2C(a,b,R)\big)\delta,

which shows that
S_{\varepsilon}(y,\cdot,\cdot)
are uniformly equicontinuous on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,+\infty)
. Finally, noting that a change of variables
\tau=t-\sigma
interchanges symmetrically the role of
x
and
y
in the definition of
S_{\varepsilon}
, we infer that such functions are indeed
uniformly equicontinuous on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,+\infty)
.

By the equicoerciveness and
\Gamma
-convergence with given boundary conditions, we have that
S_{\varepsilon}(y,x,t)
converge to
S_{\rm hom}(y,x,t)
for every
(y,x,t)
, and the uniformly equicontinuity just proven shows that this limit is uniform on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,+\infty)
. Recalling definition (
5.14
), and noting that for given
x
minimizers
y
satisfy
|x-y|^{2}\leq t^{2}M
by Jensen’s inequality and estimating
U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)
by
S_{\varepsilon}(x,x,t)
, we then deduce that
U_{\varepsilon}(x,t)
converges uniformly to
U(x,t)
.
∎

6.
Negative perturbations

In this section we give an example of a negative perturbation whose presence affects the form of the
\Gamma
-limit
in the spirit of
[
1
]
, where a more general form of Hamiltonian (in particular not “separable”, even in the perturbation) is considered.

We examine functionals

G_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+W\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)dt,

defined in
H^{1}((0,1);\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
where
W
can be considered as a perturbation of the trivial periodic potential
V_{\rm per}=0
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
W\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy

W(x)\leq 0\hbox{ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}\quad\hbox{ and }\ \lim_{|x|\to+\infty}W(x)=0.

Then

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt+\inf W\,|\{t:u(t)=0\}|.

Hence, if
W
is not identically
0
we have a different limit than in the unperturbed case, regardless of other conditions on
W
.
In particular, this holds for

W=-c\chi_{\{0\}}
, where
\chi
denotes the characteristic function and
c>0
. Note that for such
W
we have

G_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt-c\,|\{t:u(t)=0\}|=\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt+\inf W\,|\{t:u(t)=0\}|

for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

We only consider the case
\inf W<0
.
With fixed
\delta>0
, let
x_{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
W(x_{\delta})<\inf W+\delta<0
, and set

W_{\delta}=W(x_{\delta})\chi_{\{x_{s}\}}.

We then have

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle G^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+W_{\delta}\Big(\frac{u(t)}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt-|W(x_{\delta})||\{u=\varepsilon x_{\delta}\}|.

Now if
u\in H^{1}((0,1),\mathbb{R}^{d})
we consider
u^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}=u+\varepsilon x_{\delta}
, which converges to
u
, and is such that

G^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(u^{\delta}_{\varepsilon})=\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt-|W(x_{\delta})||\{u=0\}|.

Hence,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt-|W(x_{\delta})||\{u=0\}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt+(\inf W+\delta)|\{u=0\}|,

and, by the arbitrariness of
\delta
, the upper bound.

Conversely, with fixed
\delta>0
we can consider
R_{\delta}>0
such that

W\geq-\delta+(\inf W)\chi_{\overline{B}_{R_{\delta}}},

so that, for
\varepsilon
small enough so that
\varepsilon R_{\delta}<\delta
, we have

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt+\inf W|\{t:u(t)\in\overline{B}_{R_{\delta}}\}|-\delta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt+\inf W\,|\{t:u(t)\in\overline{B}_{\delta}\}|-\delta.

Noting that
u\mapsto-|\{t:u(t)\in\overline{B}_{\delta}\}|
is lower semicontinuous with respect to the convergence in
L^{1}
, if
u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
H^{1}(0,1)
then we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\varepsilon}(u)\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt+\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf W\,|\{t:u_{\varepsilon}(t)\in\overline{B}_{\delta}\}|-\delta

\geq\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt+\inf W|\{t:u(t)\in\overline{B}_{\delta}\}|-\delta.

Letting
\delta\to 0
we obtain the claim.
∎

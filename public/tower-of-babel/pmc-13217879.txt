Successful treatment of tuberculous wounds using Maggot Debridement Therapy (MDT): A case report
Authors: Pin Zuo, Linxuan Zou, Rujia Zhou, Renbo Li, Junwei Zong, Shouyu Wang, Jian Sun

Introduction

Tuberculosis, caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, is a significant component of global public health issues. The diagnosis and treatment of skeletal and soft tissue tuberculosis are particularly complex and challenging [1], [2]. Tuberculous wounds, a common manifestation of skeletal and soft tissue tuberculosis, usually present as chronic, difficult to heal, and prone to recurrence [3], [4]. Traditional treatments are somewhat effective, but they still have many issues [5].

In recent years, Maggot Debridement Therapy (MDT), a novel wound treatment method, has received increasing attention [6]. Beyond selective necrotic tissue debridement, MDT exerts multiple therapeutic properties documented in preclinical and clinical studies, including broad-spectrum antibacterial activity (against both gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria, even drug-resistant strains), immunomodulatory effects to regulate local inflammatory responses, promotion of angiogenesis and granulation tissue proliferation, as well as degradation of biofilms that impede wound healing. The principle of MDT is to utilize proteases in the secretions of maggots to decompose necrotic tissue, simultaneously promoting wound healing by stimulating the growth of healthy granulation tissue and inhibiting bacterial growth [7], [8].

However, the application of MDT in the treatment of tuberculous wounds has not been thoroughly studied [9]. There is currently no definitive research indicating a positive impact of MDT on the treatment of tuberculous wounds. Thus, more case reports and clinical trials are needed to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of MDT in treating tuberculous wounds.

The purpose of this case report is to present a case of successful treatment of a tuberculous wound with MDT and discuss the potential value of MDT in treating tuberculous wounds. We aim to provide more clinical evidence through sharing this case to support the application of MDT in the treatment of tuberculous wounds and to serve as a reference for future research.

Case report

Patient information and chief complaint

The patient, a 29-year-old female, came with a three-day history of right lower abdominal pain, bilateral pubic bone destruction, and a mass in the right groin. The patient denied symptoms of fever, cough, chest tightness, dyspnea, diarrhea, or night sweats.

Medical history

On July 1, 2022, due to improper diet, the patient experienced right abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting and went to Dalian Third People's Hospital for treatment. An outpatient abdominal CT scan indicated the possibility of a malignant tumor. The patient was then transferred to the oncology department for further examination. The patient had been healthy in the past, with no history of chronic diseases, no surgical history, and no family history of diseases.

Preliminary diagnosis and examination

Upon admission, routine biochemical tests were within normal ranges. A biopsy of the pubic bone mass was subsequently performed, and the patient underwent an MRI of the bilateral hip joints, which showed no tumor cells, and there was a high likelihood of pubic joint tuberculosis with sinus formation and cold abscess formation in the right inner thigh muscles. Given the difficulties in obtaining satisfactory pathologic specimens and diagnostic difficulties, the orthopedics department was consulted, and it was considered feasible to remove the tumor in the right pubic muscle area for a biopsy to confirm the pathologic diagnosis. The patient was then transferred to another department for surgical treatment.

Treatment process

After being transferred, the patient underwent removal of the right thigh tumor and a pathologic biopsy. Routine dressings were changed daily post-operation. The intraoperative pathologic result of the tumor showed chronic granulomatous changes with caseous necrosis, consistent with tuberculosis. The general bacterial culture of the wound was Staphylococcus epidermidis. Subsequently, anti-tuberculosis treatment and symptomatic treatment to control the infection were administered. After the removal of the tuberculous tumor, the wound did not heal well, forming an irregular tuberculous wound. With clear boundaries from the surrounding tissue, the wound was approximately 15*3 cm in size, 2 cm deep, and rough at the bottom. The surface and bottom of the wound had a large amount of yellow necrotic tissue, with a large amount of turbid yellow fluid exudation, no purulent exudation, and no particular smell (Fig. 1-A). The patient felt slight itching and pain in the wound, affecting her sleep quality at night, and was anxious due to the delayed healing of the wound. After obtaining the patient's consent,After signing the informed consent of the subject, MDT was started on the 11th day post-operation.

Wound before and during treatment with MDT.

The maggots were obtained from the medical Lucilia sericata larvae cultivated by Professor Wang Shouyu's team at Dalian Medical University. They were prepared into biological debridement packages according to the local Chinese medicine decoction processing standards and could be used for clinical treatment. When using the maggot debridement package, the wound and its surroundings were disinfected first, and then the wound was rinsed with saline. Subsequently, two 5*4 cm maggot bags were placed on the wound (Fig. 1-B). The area around the wound was wrapped with foam adjuvant to absorb the exudate after maggot debridement (Fig. 1-C). Finally, breathable clean gauze was used to dress the wound (Fig. 1-D).

Treatment outcome and follow-up

During the use of maggot debridement, the patient experienced no particular physiological discomfort, only feeling slight itching at the wound. Four days after placing the maggot bag on the wound, it was removed, the wound was cleaned and disinfected, and fresh granulation tissue could be seen covering the wound, with no yellow necrotic tissue, and a small amount of clear fluid exuding (Fig. 2-E). After the MDT, no infection was observed in the wound, the fresh granulation tissue continued to grow, and the wound was then sutured closed in the operating room (Fig. 2-F). After receiving anti-tuberculosis treatment, the patient was discharged on August 24, 2022, during which time the wound healed well, the sutures healed day by day, and only a small amount of clear exudate was visible (Fig. 2-G). At outpatient follow-up, the wound was completely healed, with no redness or swelling around it, no fluctuation, and the patient expressed satisfaction with the treatment results (Fig. 2-H).

Discussion

Our case report provides an essential addition to existing literature,suggesting the potential utility of MDT in the treatment of tuberculous wounds.This case report describes the substantial improvement in wound healing in a patient with a tuberculous wound after undergoing MDT.Not only did the treatment clear the necrotic tissue,but it also maintained the continuous growth of fresh granulation tissue at the wound site.Moreover,the patient did not experience any related complications or physiological discomfort during the MDT treatment,further attesting to the safety of MDT in this single case.

The multiple therapeutic properties of MDT played a critical role in the successful healing of this tuberculous wound.First,the selective proteolytic debridement of maggots rapidly eliminated massive yellow necrotic tissue in the wound bed,which is a key barrier to chronic tuberculous wound repair.Second,the antibacterial activity of maggot secretions inhibited the growth of Staphylococcus epidermidis isolated from the wound,reducing secondary bacterial infection and local inflammatory exudation.Third,MDT effectively promoted angiogenesis and healthy granulation tissue proliferation,filling the 15 × 3 cm wound defect and creating favorable conditions for subsequent surgical suture.Notably,the mild local reaction(only slight itching)indicated good biocompatibility of MDT in this immunocompromised tuberculous wound microenvironment.

Despite the evidence our case report provides regarding the effectiveness and safety of MDT in treating tuberculous wounds, it also has some limitations. Firstly, this is a report of a single case, and therefore, our findings might be influenced by individual variations and cannot be directly generalized to all patients with tuberculous wounds. Additionally, we were unable to cultivate Mycobacterium tuberculosis from the wound secretion. Still, based on the pathology and radiology results, a diagnosis of a tuberculous wound was indeed established. The main reason might be that the growth and proliferation of Mycobacterium tuberculosis are relatively slow, which might lead to negative results in early cultures [10]. Future research should consider conducting larger-scale clinical trials to assess the effectiveness and safety of MDT in the treatment of tuberculous wounds. Future studies should also consider conducting in vitro experiments to explore the effects of maggots on Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

Conclusion

In this case report, we observed that MDT was associated with successful healing and showed favorable safety in treating tuberculous wounds, offering a new treatment option for clinical practice. However, we must acknowledge that this is a report of a single case, so our results may be influenced by individual variations and cannot be directly generalized to all patients with tuberculous wounds. This finding supports the feasibility of MDT for tuberculous wounds and warrants further large-scale clinical and experimental studies.

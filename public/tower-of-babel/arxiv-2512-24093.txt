Theoretical Insights into Excitons, Optical Properties, and Nonradiative Recombination Dynamics in M 6 CSe 4 (M = Ca, Sr) Antiperovskite Carbides Preprint: APS/123-QED
Sanchi Monga, Saswata Bhattacharya

Abstract
Theoretically predicted antiperovskite carbides M 6 CSe 4 (M = Ca, Sr) represent an emerging class of optoelectronic materials with potential relevance for photovoltaic applications. In this work, we present a comprehensive first-principles investigation of their electronic, optical, and excitonic properties, together with non-radiative recombination dynamics. Density functional theory (DFT) and many-body perturbation theory ( GW ) reveal that both compounds are direct band gap semiconductors with gaps spanning the infrared-visible region. Incorporating electron–hole interactions via the Bethe–Salpeter equation leads to pronounced red-shifts in the first peak of optical spectra, indicative of bound excitons with binding energies of 0.12 eV (Ca 6 CSe 4 ) and 0.20 eV (Sr 6 CSe 4 ), extending over nearly three unit cells in all directions. Time-dependent DFT combined with nonadiabatic molecular dynamics simulations at 300 K reveals pronounced lattice fluctuations in Ca 6 CSe 4 , resulting in ∼ \sim 38 % \% larger band gap variations and ∼ \sim 28 % \% faster electronic decoherence. Together with ∼ \sim 53 % \% weaker nonadiabatic couplings, these effects yield non-radiative recombination lifetimes approximately eleven times longer than in Sr 6 CSe 4 . Overall, our results identify M 6 CSe 4 carbides as promising lead-free photovoltaic materials, with Ca 6 CSe 4 exhibiting superior optoelectronic properties and carrier dynamics that motivate further experimental investigation.

II
Computational Methods

NAMD simulations are carried out in conjunction with TDDFT
50
using the decoherence-induced surface hopping (DISH) approach
6
;
22
, as implemented in the PYXAID package
1
;
2
. In this framework, the motion of the heavy nuclei is treated semi-classically, while the dynamics of the lighter electrons are treated quantum mechanically. The DISH method explicitly accounts for decoherence effects, which determine the probability of nonadiabatic transitions (hops) between adiabatic potential energy surfaces. These transitions occur when decoherence conditions are satisfied, allowing the system to switch electronic states in a physically consistent manner. This approach has been successfully applied to a broad range of systems—including bulk
36
;
59
;
39
and two-dimensional halide perovskites
34
;
31
, transition metal dichalcogenides
38
;
65
, and semiconducting quantum dots
37
—and has been shown to produce results in good agreement with experimental observations.

First principles electronic structure calculations are performed using the Vienna
ab initio

;
28
;
25

15

29

58

47

;

Ab initio

\Gamma

\Gamma

The NAMD simulations are iterated multiple times to capture charge carrier dynamics over extended time scales. The reported NAMD results are obtained by averaging over 50 randomly sampled initial structures and 10,000 stochastic realizations to ensure statistical convergence. Spin–orbit coupling (SOC) effects are not included in the DFT, MBPT, or NAMD calculations, as their contribution to the band gap and overall band dispersion is negligible (see Table S2 of the SM), consistent with a previous study

Elastic and inelastic electron-phonon scattering influences nonradiative electron-hole recombination. Inelastic scattering enables exchange of energy between electronic states and lattice vibrations, thereby facilitating nonradiative transitions. The strength of this inelastic electron-phonon interaction is quantified by the non-adiabatic (NA) coupling, defined as:

d_{mn}=-i\hbar\langle\varphi_{m}|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\varphi_{n}\rangle=-i\hbar\frac{\langle\varphi_{m}|\nabla_{R}H|\varphi_{n}\rangle}{\varepsilon_{m}-\varepsilon_{n}}\dot{R}

(1)

where
\varphi_{m/n}
and
\varepsilon_{m/n}
denote the wavefunctions and eigenvalues of the initial state
m
and final state
n
, respectively,
H
is the Kohn–Sham Hamiltonian, and
\dot{R}
represents the nuclear velocity. The magnitude of NAC between two states is determined by three factors: (i) the electron–phonon coupling matrix element
\langle\varphi_{m}|\nabla_{R}H|\varphi_{n}\rangle
, (ii) the energy difference between the Kohn–Sham orbitals, and (iii) the nuclear velocity
\dot{R}
. In practice, the NAC is evaluated from the overlap of Kohn–Sham orbitals at successive ionic configurations along the AIMD trajectory. Larger NAC values imply a higher probability of nonradiative recombination between electronic states. Table
3
summarizes both the time-averaged absolute NAC values and their root-mean-square (RMS) magnitudes between the VBM and CBM. Interestingly, Sr

6

In contrast, elastic electron–phonon scattering does not involve energy exchange but instead disrupts the quantum coherence between initial and final electronic states, thereby exerting a strong influence on nonradiative recombination pathways. Electronic transitions require phase coherence between the participating wavefunctions, and the loss of coherence can either slow down or completely suppress these transitions, as exemplified by the quantum Zeno effect.

One-Week Inflammatory Kinetics After First-Line Nivolumab Plus Ipilimumab in Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma: A Multicenter Landmark Analysis

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFirst-line nivolumab plus ipilimumab can produce durable survival in metastatic renal cell carcinoma, yet some patients experience early progression or rapid clinical deterioration before first scheduled imaging. Because radiologic response assessment is typically unavailable until approximately 12 weeks, clinically accessible signals during this pre-imaging interval are needed.ObjectiveWe aimed to evaluate whether early changes in blood-based inflammatory biomarkers at predefined post-treatment landmarks were associated with overall survival and to determine which biomarker showed the most consistent early prognostic signal.MethodsIn this multicenter retrospective cohort study across eight Japanese institutions, 219 patients with metastatic renal cell carcinoma treated with first-line nivolumab plus ipilimumab were analyzed. Predefined landmark analyses at 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months compared six blood-based inflammatory biomarkers using restricted cubic splines with multiple imputation. Overall survival was the primary endpoint.ResultsThe 1-week landmark showed the most consistent association with overall survival. Among six biomarkers, change in pan-immune-inflammation value showed the most reproducible pattern. Among 183 patients with paired baseline and 1-week pan-immune-inflammation value data, PIV-BOOST, defined as any pan-immune-inflammation value increase (> 0%) from baseline, was observed in 110 patients (60.1%) and was independently associated with improved overall survival (adjusted hazard ratio 0.60, 95% confidence interval 0.39–0.92; p = 0.018).ConclusionsOne-week inflammatory kinetics provided early prognostic information before first scheduled imaging in first-line nivolumab plus ipilimumab-treated metastatic renal cell carcinoma. Key Points

In patients with advanced kidney cancer receiving first-line nivolumab plus ipilimumab, an early rise in the pan-immune-inflammation value within the first week of treatment was associated with longer overall survival.This favorable early signal—termed “PIV-BOOST”—was observed regardless of baseline risk category.Relying only on routine blood tests obtained very early, PIV-BOOST may provide a simple, widely available prognostic read-out before first scheduled imaging; however, external validation is required before clinical implementation.

Background

Immune checkpoint inhibitor-based combination therapy with nivolumab plus ipilimumab (IO–IO) is an established first-line treatment option for patients with metastatic renal cell carcinoma (mRCC), providing durable survival benefit in a subset of patients [1, 2]. Despite these advances, clinical outcomes under IO–IO therapy remain heterogeneous, and a clinically relevant proportion of patients experience early disease progression or rapid clinical deterioration associated with poor survival [3, 4]. This creates an important pre-imaging management gap: patients at risk for early treatment failure may not be readily distinguishable before the first scheduled radiologic assessment.

A key clinical challenge is the lack of reliable tools for risk stratification during the early treatment phase. In pivotal trials of nivolumab plus ipilimumab, radiologic assessment is typically scheduled at approximately 12 weeks after treatment initiation [1], leaving a clinically important pre-imaging interval during which emerging response trajectories may not yet be objectively characterized by imaging. Prior studies have reported associations between inflammatory kinetics, including C-reactive protein (CRP)-based dynamics and peripheral blood cell-derived indices, and clinical outcomes during immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy [5–8]. However, these kinetics have generally been evaluated over broader windows spanning weeks to months and across heterogeneous treatment settings, limiting inference regarding which early timepoint before first scheduled imaging is clinically informative specifically in first-line IO–IO therapy for mRCC.

We therefore conducted a multicenter retrospective cohort study using a predefined landmark framework to compare changes in six inflammatory biomarkers at 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months after treatment initiation. We aimed to determine whether any prespecified landmark demonstrated an association with overall survival (OS) before first scheduled imaging and to identify the biomarker whose early kinetics most consistently stratified OS across these landmarks.

Methods

Study Design and Patient Population

The IMPACT Study (Immune-checkpoint inhibitor Multi-institutional Project for Advanced Renal Cancer Therapy) is a multicenter collaborative study of patients with advanced renal cell carcinoma receiving immune checkpoint inhibitor-based regimens in real-world clinical practice. Of 397 patients screened between August 2018 and March 2023, 231 were eligible for first-line nivolumab plus ipilimumab after applying predefined treatment and disease-stage eligibility criteria. A further 12 patients who did not meet the predefined analytic eligibility criteria—because of International Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma Database Consortium favorable-risk disease or incomplete baseline clinical information—were excluded, leaving 219 patients for the primary analysis (Fig. S1 of the Electronic Supplementary Material [ESM]). Patients with International Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma Database Consortium favorable-risk disease were not included because the approved first-line indication for nivolumab plus ipilimumab in Japan is restricted to intermediate- and poor-risk mRCC.

Data Collection and Variables

Baseline demographic and clinical data were collected retrospectively from medical records and included age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status, history of prior nephrectomy, number of metastatic organ sites, and baseline lactate dehydrogenase levels.

Baseline inflammatory biomarkers included CRP, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio, systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), and pan-immune-inflammation value (PIV). Baseline laboratory values were defined as the most recent measurements obtained prior to treatment initiation. SII was calculated as (neutrophil count × platelet count)/lymphocyte count, and PIV was calculated as (neutrophil count × platelet count × monocyte count)/lymphocyte count. The primary endpoint was OS, defined as the time from each landmark to death from any cause. The secondary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the time from each landmark to radiographic or clinical progression or death, whichever occurred first. Radiologic response was assessed locally according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST) Version 1.1; no independent central radiologic review was performed. Objective response was defined as a best overall response of complete or partial response, and disease control was defined as complete response, partial response, or stable disease.

Primary resistant disease was defined as radiographic or documented clinical progression occurring within 90 days of treatment initiation; death without documented disease progression was not classified as primary resistant disease. Immune-related adverse events were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, Version 5.0, and the highest grade recorded during first-line nivolumab plus ipilimumab therapy was used.

Definition of Landmark Cohorts

To mitigate immortal time bias inherent to on-treatment biomarker evaluation, predefined landmark analyses were applied in alignment with clinically evaluable post-treatment timepoints [9, 10]. Landmark timepoints were set at 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months after treatment initiation. At each landmark timepoint, only patients who were alive and had available laboratory data were included in the corresponding landmark cohort. Survival times were calculated from each landmark timepoint. Laboratory values at each landmark were defined as the measurement closest to the landmark date. Patients with observed values at baseline and the corresponding landmark comprised the biomarker-evaluable sets; those with observed baseline and 1-week PIV values comprised the PIV-evaluable set.

Statistical Analysis

Early changes in inflammatory biomarkers from baseline to each landmark timepoint were analyzed as continuous variables. Associations of percent changes in biomarkers up to each landmark timepoint with OS and PFS were assessed using Cox proportional hazards models.

Missing data were handled using multiple imputation (m = 5) with additive regression and predictive mean matching using the aregImpute function, and Cox-model estimates were pooled using fit.mult.impute. For continuous spline and baseline-interaction analyses, models were fitted within the full endpoint-specific landmark-eligible cohorts; missing biomarker-change values and baseline covariates included in these models were imputed, whereas survival outcomes were not. Analyses requiring direction-of-change classification, including PIV-BOOST, were restricted to patients with observed paired biomarker values.

Non-linear associations between biomarker changes and survival outcomes were evaluated using restricted cubic splines with three knots. The number of spline knots was predefined based on the recommendations of Harrell Jr [11]. Interaction analyses were predefined to evaluate whether the prognostic association of early biomarker changes was dependent on baseline inflammatory burden, rather than to identify subgroups. Effect modification was assessed by incorporating interaction terms between baseline biomarker levels and early changes, thereby evaluating the consistency and robustness of associations across baseline strata.

Spline analyses were conducted using percentage change from baseline to facilitate comparison of non-linear patterns across biomarkers, whereas interaction analyses were performed using absolute change to appropriately evaluate effect modification by baseline values. Based on the consistency of findings across landmark analyses, spline analyses, and interaction analyses, PIV-BOOST was operationally defined as an increase of more than 0% in PIV from baseline to 1 week after treatment initiation. This definition was selected to capture the directionality of ultra-early inflammatory kinetics while avoiding data-driven cutoff optimization.

During the screening phase, multiple inflammatory biomarkers were evaluated without prespecifying a primary exposure. Given the exploratory nature of the analyses, formal adjustment for multiple testing was not applied; instead, robustness was assessed based on reproducibility across landmark timepoints and endpoints.

Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. The median follow-up time was estimated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method [12]. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were constructed to evaluate the independent association between PIV-BOOST and survival outcomes, adjusting for clinically relevant covariates selected with reference to established prognostic factors in mRCC [13–16], including age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status, baseline lactate dehydrogenase, and number of metastatic organ sites (≥ 2 vs 1). In a sensitivity analysis, the multivariable OS model was additionally stratified by institution to allow center-specific baseline hazards.

Spearman rank correlations were calculated between the 1-week percentage changes in PIV and SII and the concurrent percentage changes in their constituent blood-cell counts. The association between PIV-BOOST and primary resistant disease was evaluated using univariable logistic regression, with the reported p-value derived from the Wald test for the PIV-BOOST coefficient. Objective response, disease control, and immune-related adverse-event proportions were compared using two-sided Fisher exact tests. In an exploratory analysis, patients were jointly classified according to baseline PIV, dichotomized at the median of the PIV-evaluable set, and PIV-BOOST status; this four-group classification was descriptive. Analyses of objective response, disease control, primary resistant disease, and immune-related adverse events were supportive, were not adjusted for multiplicity, and were interpreted as secondary to the primary OS analysis.

All statistical tests were performed using a two-sided significance level of 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (Version 4.5.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

At the 1-week landmark, percent changes in PIV and SII were associated with OS, whereas the association for PLR was directionally consistent but did not reach statistical significance, and percent changes in NLR, lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio, and CRP were not associated with OS. For PFS, changes in PLR, NLR, and SII were associated, whereas PIV showed no clear association; spline analyses suggested broadly similar patterns for SII, NLR, and PLR (Fig. S2 of the ESM).

At the 1-month landmark, associations were attenuated across most biomarkers, although the direction of association for PIV with OS remained consistent. By the 3-month landmark, no consistent associations were observed for either OS or PFS.

Spline and Interaction Analyses

Restricted cubic spline analyses at the 1-week landmark demonstrated non-linear associations between percentage changes in inflammatory biomarkers and OS (Fig. 1). For PIV, the hazard ratio decreased monotonically as early changes increased, without evidence of threshold effects. In descriptive constituent-cell analyses, the 1-week PIV change correlated most strongly with neutrophil change (Spearman rho = 0.75), followed by monocyte (rho = 0.48), platelet (rho = 0.37), and lymphocyte changes (rho = − 0.33). The corresponding correlations for SII were 0.69, − 0.59, and 0.38 for neutrophil, lymphocyte, and platelet changes, respectively (all p < 0.001; Fig. S5 of the ESM).

Definition and Prognostic Impact of PIV-BOOST

Based on the consistency of findings across landmark analyses, spline analyses, and interaction analyses, PIV-BOOST was operationally defined as an increase (> 0%) in PIV from baseline to the 1-week landmark. This threshold was selected to capture the directionality of ultra-early inflammatory kinetics while avoiding outcome-optimized threshold selection.

Among 183 patients with paired baseline and 1-week PIV data, 110 (60.1%) exhibited PIV-BOOST, whereas 73 (39.9%) showed stable or decreased PIV (non-BOOST). In the PIV-BOOST group, the median relative increase was 41.7% (interquartile range 22.1–97.1%) and the median absolute increase was 161 (interquartile range 45–390); the non-BOOST group had a median relative change of -27.6%.

Patients with PIV-BOOST demonstrated significantly improved OS compared with those without PIV-BOOST (log-rank p = 0.006; Fig. 3a). A similar but less pronounced separation was observed for PFS (log-rank p = 0.021; Fig. 3b). Further stratification by baseline PIV level revealed distinct prognostic patterns for OS. Patients with high baseline PIV and no PIV-BOOST experienced the poorest OS, whereas those with low baseline PIV and PIV-BOOST demonstrated the most favorable outcomes (log-rank p = 0.018; Fig. 3c).

In a multivariable Cox regression analysis adjusted for age, sex, ECOG performance status, baseline lactate dehydrogenase, and number of metastatic organ sites, PIV-BOOST remained independently associated with improved OS (adjusted hazard ratio 0.60, 95% confidence interval 0.39–0.92; p = 0.018) [Table S1 of the ESM]. The association remained consistent in the institution-stratified sensitivity analysis (adjusted hazard ratio 0.52, 95% confidence interval 0.32–0.85; p = 0.010; Table S2 of the ESM).

As a supportive analysis, PIV-BOOST was also associated with a lower rate of primary resistant disease (18 of 107 [16.8%] vs 24 of 73 [32.9%]; odds ratio 0.41, 95% confidence interval 0.20–0.83; Wald p = 0.014; Fig. S4a of the ESM). Among 181 patients with an evaluable best overall response, objective response rates were 51.9% (56/108) and 43.8% (32/73) for PIV-BOOST and non-BOOST, respectively (p = 0.363), whereas disease control rates were 79.6% (86/108) and 63.0% (46/73), respectively (p = 0.017). Among 175 patients with available immune-related adverse-event grading, grade ≥ 3 immune-related adverse events occurred in 51.4% (55/107) and 41.2% (28/68), respectively (p = 0.215; Table S3 of the ESM). In the exploratory four-group analysis, primary resistant disease occurred in 11 of 66 (16.7%) patients with low baseline PIV/PIV-BOOST, 4 of 24 (16.7%) with low baseline PIV/non-BOOST, 7 of 41 (17.1%) with high baseline PIV/PIV-BOOST, and 20 of 49 (40.8%) with high baseline PIV/non-BOOST; the highest observed rate was in the high-baseline-PIV/non-BOOST group (Fig. S4b of the ESM).

Discussion and Conclusions

The present study identifies the 1-week landmark as the earliest clinically informative assessed timepoint for on-treatment inflammatory assessment after initiation of nivolumab plus ipilimumab in mRCC. Across six inflammatory biomarkers evaluated at three predefined landmark timepoints, the 1-week landmark showed the most consistent association with OS, and a change in PIV provided the most reproducible prognostic signal. Importantly, the favorable trajectory was characterized not by an early decline, but by an early increase in PIV. This finding is clinically relevant because the signal emerged during the pre-imaging interval, when radiologic response trajectories are not yet objectively characterized. The resulting separation in OS emerged well before the first protocol-scheduled radiologic assessment, suggesting that biologically distinct response trajectories may already be detectable during the earliest phase of IO–IO therapy using a routine blood count-derived biomarker.

Although IO–IO therapy provides durable survival benefit in a subset of patients with mRCC, early treatment failure remains a major unmet clinical challenge. Prior studies have demonstrated that primary resistance to nivolumab plus ipilimumab and progression within the first 3 months are strongly associated with inferior OS in real-world cohorts [3, 4]. Recent US real-world data further highlight that outcomes with first-line nivolumab plus ipilimumab in routine practice are heterogeneous [17].

However, in the pivotal nivolumab plus ipilimumab trial, radiologic response assessment was scheduled at approximately 12 weeks after treatment initiation [1], leaving a clinically relevant interval during which treatment decisions must often be made in the absence of objective imaging-based guidance. In this context, the present findings suggest that inflammatory kinetics at the 1-week landmark may provide complementary prognostic information before the first scheduled radiologic assessment. Although an approximately 1-week blood count is not scheduled in all practice settings, the availability of these early measurements in our cohort enabled evaluation of an ultra-early pre-imaging interval that would otherwise be missed; where such testing is not routine, implementation would require an additional complete blood count with a differential and prospective evaluation.

Previous investigations have highlighted the prognostic relevance of inflammatory kinetics during immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy, particularly CRP-based dynamics evaluated over weeks to months [6, 7]. Cancer-agnostic analyses have further suggested that longitudinal inflammatory trajectories may capture treatment response across tumor types [8]. By contrast, the present study systematically evaluated multiple inflammatory indices at predefined early landmark timepoints and demonstrated that prognostic signals were most consistently observed at the 1-week landmark, whereas associations at later landmarks were attenuated or absent. This temporal pattern supports the concept that the earliest post-treatment inflammatory shift, rather than a later inflammatory change alone, may contain clinically informative prognostic signals in first-line IO–IO therapy. Baseline PIV has been reported as a prognostic factor in patients with mRCC treated with nivolumab in the second line and beyond [18].

Baseline PIV may be regarded as an integrated proxy of the host’s systemic inflammatory and immune balance at treatment initiation [19]. One potential advantage of PIV is that, unlike NLR and SII, it incorporates monocytes in addition to neutrophils, platelets, and lymphocytes, potentially capturing a broader myeloid-inflammatory component of the host response [18]. Although SII showed a statistically significant association and PLR a directionally consistent association with OS at the 1-week landmark, these findings should not be interpreted as indicating that PIV alone captured the early inflammatory response. Rather, the concordant OS signals observed for SII and PIV, together with the directionally consistent PLR estimate, suggest that platelet- and lymphocyte-related inflammatory dynamics may be relevant during the earliest phase of IO–IO therapy. These findings support PIV as a particularly interpretable marker for a simple early direction-of-change rule, without excluding the potential utility of SII or PLR in broader multivariable models.

From a biological perspective, an ultra-early increase in a composite inflammatory index may reflect acute immune–inflammatory dynamics triggered by combined programmed death-1 and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 blockade, consistent with the known mechanisms of dual checkpoint inhibition [20]. A translational study in non-small-cell lung cancer integrated clinical-trial analyses, patient tumor profiling, and murine models; in the murine models, dual programmed death-ligand 1 and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 blockade reprogrammed the tumor myeloid compartment toward inducible nitric oxide synthase-expressing tumoricidal phenotypes [21]. Because this mechanistic evidence derives from murine lung-cancer models and tumor tissue, it cannot directly explain peripheral blood count changes in mRCC. More generally, circulating neutrophil counts are regulated by bone marrow release, marginated pools, tissue trafficking, lifespan, and clearance; cytokine or epinephrine stimulation can rapidly mobilize mature neutrophils from marginated pools [22]. In our descriptive constituent-cell analysis, the 1-week PIV change correlated most strongly with neutrophil change and also with monocyte and platelet changes, whereas the inverse correlation with lymphocyte change was stronger for SII than for PIV. Because the component counts are mathematically embedded in PIV and SII, these correlations do not establish independent or causal cellular contributions.

The clinical meaning of an early inflammatory change appears to be biomarker, time, and treatment context specific. Patel et al. showed that acquisition or maintenance of a high on-treatment lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio at a median of 21 days after immune checkpoint inhibitor initiation was associated with longer OS, indicating that an early on-treatment state can provide prognostic stratification beyond the baseline value [23]; in our cohort, a continuous lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio change was not associated with OS, whereas an approximately 1-week increase in PIV was associated with OS. Young et al. reported that a 12-week NLR was prognostic in mRCC treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors [24], and CRP kinetic patterns over the first months of therapy have shown that both a decline and an initial flare followed by a decline can be favorable [6, 8]. Collectively, these studies suggest that the prognostic meaning of an early increase or decrease depends on the biomarker, the assessment window, and the treatment context rather than on a universal inflammatory direction.

Because durable immune-mediated control may not be immediately reflected by short-term radiographic tumor shrinkage, it may be better captured by OS than by PFS. Consistent with this interpretation, early inflammatory changes in the present study were more strongly and consistently associated with OS than with PFS. Continuous PIV change was not significantly associated with PFS, whereas the direction-based PIV-BOOST classification showed a modest separation of PFS curves, indicating limited consistency across exposure parameterizations. PIV-BOOST was also associated with a lower rate of primary resistant disease, and the highest observed rate of primary resistant disease occurred in the high-baseline-PIV/non-BOOST group. The higher disease control rate without a significant difference in objective response rate suggests that the PIV-BOOST signal may relate more closely to avoidance of early progression or maintenance of disease control than to a higher probability of objective tumor shrinkage. Grade ≥ 3 immune-related adverse-event rates did not differ significantly according to PIV-BOOST status; because this was not an equivalence analysis, this should not be interpreted as evidence of equivalent toxicity risk. This OS–PFS discordance has been observed in immune checkpoint inhibitor trials, including CheckMate 214 [1, 2]. Combined stratification by baseline PIV and PIV-BOOST further identified a subgroup with particularly poor OS, underscoring the complementary value of baseline inflammatory burden and ultra-early immune dynamics for early clinical stratification.

Whether a sustained elevation or a secondary decline after the 1-week change provides additional information remains unresolved. The available scheduled timepoints and attrition at later assessments were insufficient for reliable trajectory classification. The findings nevertheless raise testable hypotheses regarding response-adapted cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 exposure, including de-escalation of ipilimumab after favorable ultra-early kinetics and re-escalation after a subsequent unfavorable trajectory. Because treatment was not modified according to PIV and later trajectories were incompletely captured, neither strategy can be evaluated from the present data, and both would require prospective interventional assessment before clinical application.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, given the retrospective multicenter design, residual confounding and between-center or inter-assay variability cannot be excluded, although the institution-stratified analysis yielded a consistent association. Second, paired baseline and 1-week PIV data were unavailable for 36 patients; although measured baseline characteristics were generally similar according to paired-data availability, residual selection bias cannot be excluded. Third, the cohort was restricted to first-line nivolumab plus ipilimumab in Japanese patients with International Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma Database Consortium intermediate- or poor-risk disease, and generalizability to other regimens, populations, and risk groups remains uncertain. Fourth, statistical precision was reduced at later landmarks because fewer patients remained eligible with available laboratory data. Fifth, radiologic response was assessed locally without an independent central review, and immune-related adverse events were collected retrospectively. Finally, the > 0% PIV-BOOST rule was defined and evaluated within the same retrospective cohort; independent external validation, followed by a prospective evaluation, is mandatory before this directional rule is incorporated into routine clinical decision algorithms or used to guide treatment modification.

In this multicenter retrospective cohort, a 1-week increase in PIV after initiation of nivolumab plus ipilimumab (PIV-BOOST) was independently associated with OS in mRCC. These findings suggest that blood count-derived inflammatory kinetics assessed at 1 week may complement a baseline risk assessment before first scheduled imaging; however, external prospective validation is required.

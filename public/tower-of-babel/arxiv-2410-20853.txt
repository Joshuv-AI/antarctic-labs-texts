On Hitchin’s equations for cyclic G G -Higgs bundles
Nathaniel Sagman, Ognjen Tošić

Abstract
. We develop a Lie-theoretic perspective on Hitchin’s equations for cyclic G G -Higgs bundles, which we use to study analytic and geometric properties of harmonic maps. Among other things, we prove Dai-Li’s conjecture on the monotonicity of the energy density in the case of Coxeter cyclic G G -Higgs bundles, for all G G , and Dai-Li’s negative curvature conjecture for Coxeter cyclic G G -Higgs bundles, for all G G except those of type E 7 \mathrm{E}_{7} and E 8 . \mathrm{E}_{8}.



1.
Introduction

Let
S
be a Riemann surface with canonical bundle
\mathcal{K}
and let
G
be a simple complex Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. A
G
-Higgs bundle on
S
is a pair
(P,\phi)
, where
P
is a holomorphic principal
G
-bundle and
\phi
is a holomorphic
(1,0)
-form valued in the adjoint bundle
\textrm{ad}P
called the Higgs field. Hitchin’s self-duality equations ask for a reduction of structure group of
P
to a maximal compact subgroup
K\subset G
that gives rise to a Chern connection
\nabla_{h}
on
P
and adjoint operator
*_{h}
on
\textrm{ad}P
satisfying

F(\nabla_{h})+[\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}]=0.

A solution is equivalent to an equivariant harmonic map from the universal cover
\tilde{S}
to the Riemannian symmetric space
G/K
of
G
. When
S
is closed and of genus
g\geq 2
, for a generic class of
G
-Higgs bundles, namely the stable
G
-Higgs bundles, one can always find a unique solution. The self-duality equations and existence theory for harmonic maps together yield the non-abelian Hodge correspondence, a bridge between the moduli spaces of representations
\pi_{1}(S)\to G
and
G
-Higgs bundles.

Definition 1.1
.

A
G
-Higgs bundle
(P,\phi)
is cyclic if there exists a holomorphic gauge transformation
s
of
P
of order
m
such that
s^{*}\phi=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m}}\phi.

There are different notions of cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles in the literature; see §
3.3
.

For
m>2,
harmonic maps arising from cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles are weakly conformal, and hence define branched minimal immersions. Moreover, cyclic Higgs bundles often give rise to other distinguished immersions (hyperbolic affine spherical immersions into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
J
-holomorphic maps into
\mathbb{H}^{2,4},
etc.) and they play an important role in higher Teichmüller theory. For example, Labourie’s proof of his own conjecture in rank
2
uses cyclic Higgs bundles in an essential way
[
24
]
. For studies on cyclic Higgs bundles, see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
35
]
, among other works.

In this paper, we develop a framework to study Hitchin’s equations for cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles, for all
G
, in a unified way. We then use what we’ve found to study analytic and geometric properties of harmonic maps.

1.1.
Affine Toda equations

Our starting point is as follows. The most well-studied cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles are those for
G=\textrm{SL}(n,\C)
with order
m
equal to
n
. In this case, Hitchin’s equations are equivalent to an elliptic system for
n
sections of line bundles, often called affine Toda equations, and have been studied extensively. See, for instance,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
26
]
. Among this class, particular focus has been given to the Higgs bundles in the Hitchin section (see §
3.6
), for which the affine Toda equations recover well-known equations from geometry, such as the Bochner equation for harmonic maps to
\mathbb{H}^{2}
(for
n=2
) or the Tzitéica equation for hyperbolic affine spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(for
n=3
).

Our first result (Theorem A below) concerns groups of adjoint type; it has a version for more general groups, but it is more cleanly stated with the adjoint assumption. Associated with a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, we have the root system
\Delta
with Coxeter number
r
(see Definition
3.4
). For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(n,\C)
,
r=n.
When the order
m
is
r,
we say that
(P,\phi)
is Coxeter cyclic. Given a choice of simple roots
\Pi
for
\mathfrak{h}
, adjoining the lowest root
-\delta
gives the extended system of simple roots
\mathcal{Z}=\{-\delta\}\cup\Pi
. For
m=r,
we find that, exactly in this case and for no other
m
, Hitchin’s equations split into a system of equations for a set of Hermitian metrics on line bundles, indexed by
\mathcal{Z}
. More precisely, for
m=r
, we first prove the following, and then we prove a result on the structure of Hitchin’s equations for Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles, Theorem A below.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a simple complex Lie group of adjoint type. The data of a Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle
(P,\phi)
over a Riemann surface
S
is equivalent to a collection of line bundles
\{L_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}\}
, holomorphic sections
\phi_{\alpha}\in H^{0}(S,L_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{K})
, and an isomorphism
\Theta
from
\bigotimes_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}L_{\alpha}^{\otimes n_{\alpha}}
to the trivial line bundle
\mathcal{O}
.

The decomposition of
\phi
can be cast as a special case of
[
6
, Theorem 4.2.2]
, where the extra hypothesis gives more structure. About (poly)stability of
(P,\phi)
in terms of the
\phi_{\alpha}
’s, see Remark
3.11
. Throughout the paper, we use
\nu
for the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
as well as its dualization on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a simple complex Lie group of adjoint type. A solution to Hitchin’s equations for a stable Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle over a closed Riemann surface
S
with data
L_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}
as above is equivalent to a collection of Hermitian metrics
\mu_{\alpha}
on
L_{\alpha}
such that
\Theta_{*}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}\mu_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}
is the flat metric on
\mathcal{O}
, and such that

(1)

\displaystyle F(\mu_{\alpha})+\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{Z}}{\nu(\alpha,\beta)}\mu_{\beta}(\phi_{\beta}\wedge\bar{\phi}_{\beta})=0,

where
F(\mu_{\alpha})
denotes the curvature of the Chern connection associated with the Hermitian metric
\mu_{\alpha}
, which appears in the second term of (
1
) as a map
L_{\alpha}\otimes\bar{L}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{C}
.

Taking
G^{\mathbb{R}}
to be a split real form of
G,

(P,\phi)
might carry the extra structure of a
G^{\mathbb{R}}
-Higgs bundle (see §
2.2
and §
3.5
for definitions and discussion). In this case, Proposition
1.2
and Theorem A can be refined. See §
3.5
. As well, we point out in Remark
3.17
that, after adding a hypothesis, the assumptions on
S
being closed and
\phi
being stable can be removed.

For
G
-Higgs bundles in the Hitchin section, the local coordinate expression of (
1
) is similar to the expression for Hitchin’s equations from Baraglia’s work
[
2
]
(which he calls affine Toda equations). To prove Theorem A in our general setting, we draw on
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
, and
[
16
]
. One conclusion of Theorem A is that Hitchin’s equations can be understood through the Cartan matrix or the extended Dynkin diagram of
\mathcal{Z}
, which carry the information of the
\nu(\alpha,\beta)
’s.

For
G=\textrm{SL}(n,\C)
, the system (
1
) appears frequently in the works of Dai and Li and their collaborators (for example,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
26
]
), although it usually looks different because the authors work with associated vector bundles and not
G
-bundles. With this in mind, we use Theorem A to prove results on harmonic maps that fall in line with those of
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
, but for general Lie groups.

Remark 1.3
.

For
G=\textrm{SL}(n,\C),
a Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle is equivalent to a certain twisted quiver bundle, as considered in
[
1
]
. As explained in
[
6
, Remark 4.1.3]
, on a closed surface and under the appropriate stability conditions, the solution to Hitchin’s equations is equivalent to the solution to the relevant twisted quiver
(\sigma,\tau)
-vortex equations found in
[
1
]
. For general
G
, it would be interesting to compare our equations and solutions with equations and solutions from
[
1
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

As we’ve mentioned, cyclic Higgs bundles correspond to other special immersions of surfaces in homogeneous spaces of
G
(see also Remark
3.14
). It appears that the equations governing those immersions are always expressed in terms of the
\mu_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha}\wedge\overline{\phi}_{\alpha})
’s. For example, in
[
29
]
, Nie studies alternating surfaces in pseudo-hyperbolic spaces, which naturally correspond to certain Coxeter cyclic
\textrm{SO}(2n+1,\C)
-Higgs bundles. For such a surface, the energy density is completely encoded by just one of the
2
-forms
\mu_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha}\wedge\overline{\phi}_{\alpha})
(see Theorems 4.6 and 4.10 in
[
29
]
, it is the “
h_{1}
-term”). Thus, the methods from this paper should be applicable in studying these other types of immersions.

Remark 1.5
.

After writing the first version of this paper, we learned of McIntosh’s paper
[
27
]
. In
[
27
]
, among other things, McIntosh studies so-called
\tau
-primitive harmonic maps to
G/T
, where
T
is a compact torus in
G
fixed pointwise by a Coxeter automorphism
\tau
of
G
, and shows that they are equivalent to solutions to what he calls the geometric Toda equations. The harmonic maps from Theorem A lift to
\tau
-primitive harmonic maps, where
\tau
is an inner Coxeter automorphism (see Remark
3.14
), and our equation (
1
) is more or less equivalent to the geometric Toda equations.

McIntosh explains how a
\tau
-primitive harmonic map gives rise to a
G
-Higgs bundle, but the structure of the
G
-Higgs bundle is not his main focus. Our work and
[
27
]
complement each other quite nicely–we work with different but related objects (for McIntosh, maps to
G/T
, and for us, solutions to Hitchin’s equations), and it would be interesting to compare features of the two objects that come into play here and in
[
27
]
respectively. There is one part of the computation in §
3.4
that resembles a step in the derivation of the equations in
[
27
, §4]
, but we make no changes. We also point out that the proof of Theorem A contains more value than just the statement: many aspects apply to general (not Coxeter) cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles (which do not lift to
\tau
-primitive harmonic maps), and it is mostly at the very end that specializing to
m=r
is necessary.

1.2.
Energy density

We use
\nu
for the Killing metric on
G/K
, and fix a conformal metric
\mu
on
S
, whose lift to
\tilde{S}
we continue to denote by
\mu
. Let
(P,\phi)
be a stable Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle. For
t\in\C^{*},
each
G
-Higgs bundle
(P,t\phi)
is stable and Coxeter cyclic. We say that
(P,\phi)
is fixed by the
\C^{*}
-action if
\phi
is gauge equivalent to
t\phi
for all
t\in\C^{*}
.

The energy density of a map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\tilde{S}\to G/K
is a norm for the derivative
df
, defined using
\mu
and
\nu
and denoted
e(f).
Formally,
e(f)=\textrm{tr}_{\mu}f^{*}\nu.
It descends to a function on
S,
which vanishes precisely on the zero set of the Higgs field of
f
. Using Proposition
1.2
and Theorem A,
e(f)
splits as a sum of functions that satisfy an elliptic system depending on
G
, and which we name the Bochner-Toda equations (see Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
). Using this system, we prove the following.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a simple complex Lie group. Let
(P,\phi)
be a stable and simple Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle on a closed Riemann surface
S
, which is not a fixed point of the
\C^{*}
-action. For each
t\in\C^{*}
, let
f_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\tilde{S}\to G/K
be the equivariant harmonic map associated with
(P,t\phi)
. Then, away from the zero set of
\phi
, the
\C^{*}
-family of functions

e(f_{t})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}S\to[0,\infty),\hskip 5.69054ptt\in\C^{*},

is strictly increasing with
|t|
.

Dai-Li conjectured the result to hold for all stable Higgs bundles
[
10
]
. In
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
, Dai-Li proved the
G=\textrm{SL}(n,\C)
case of Theorem B. To do so, they developed a new maximum principle for elliptic systems
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
. For general
G,
even though the Bochner-Toda equations depend on the combinatorics of different Dynkin diagrams, we find that Dai-Li’s maximum principle is well-suited to studying the system, and we apply it in the key step of the proof of Theorem B.

While working on this paper, we found a generalization of Dai-Li’s maximum principal, which has a combinatorial flavour and gives a new perspective. For pedagogical purposes, and to make the current paper more self-contained, we state and prove our new maximum principle in the appendix. The main results there are Theorems
A.2
and
A.5
. We hope they might be of use in the future.

Theorem B can be applied to study volume entropy. Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
S,
which is allowed to have isolated degenerate points, and let
g
as well denote the lift to
\tilde{S}
. Fixing a basepoint
p_{0}\in\tilde{S}
, let
\pi_{1}(S)\cdot p_{0}
denote the orbit of
p_{0}
under the
\pi_{(}S)
action on
\tilde{S}
by deck transformations, and let
B_{g}(p_{0},R)
denote a ball of radius
R
around
p_{0}
with respect to the distance function of
g
.

Definition 1.6
.

The volume entropy of
g
is

\textrm{Ent}(g)=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\#\{p\in\tilde{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}p\in\pi_{1}(S)\cdot p_{0}\cap B_{g}(p_{0},R)\}}{r}.

We show in Proposition
5.2
that for any stable family
(P,t\phi),

t\in\C^{*},
such that the harmonic maps
f_{t}
are weakly conformal, if
\phi
is not nilpotent, then
\lim_{|t|\to\infty}\textrm{Ent}(f_{t}^{*}\nu)=0.
Using Theorem B, we see that in the Coxeter cyclic case, this convergence to zero is monotonic.

Corollary B
.

Let
(P,t\phi)
,
t\in\C^{*}
, be a family of stable and simple Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles that are not
\C^{*}
-fixed points, with harmonic map
f_{t}
. Then, as
|t|\to\infty,

\textrm{Ent}(f_{t}^{*}\nu)
decreases monotonically to zero.

Analogous results have been proved for other objects that arise from cyclic Higgs bundles, such as hyperbolic affine spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
28
]
and maximal surfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{2,2}

[
32
]
. Since the pullback metrics for these objects can be expressed using the
\mu_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha}\wedge\overline{\phi}_{\alpha})
’s, our analysis (Lemma
5.1
specifically) can be used to recover these results.

Finally, the
\C^{*}
-action on the set of stable
G
-Higgs bundles does not extend to a
\C
-action. However, using properness of the Hitchin fibration (see below), we can, in some sense, take
t\to 0
in Theorem B and Corollary B. As a consequence, we obtain certain domination results, which generalize
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
. See §
5.3
for discussion and results.

1.3.
Hitchin representations and extrinsic curvature

The Hitchin fibration from the moduli space of
G
-Higgs bundles on
S,

\mathcal{M}_{S}(G)
, to the Hitchin base for
G,

\oplus_{i=1}^{l}H^{0}(S,\mathcal{K}^{m_{i}})
(
m_{i}
depending on
G
), is an important integrable system. For
S
a closed Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 2
, Hitchin constructed a section, and the image of that section under the non-abelian Hodge correspondence is a connected component of the
G^{\mathbb{R}}
-character variety, where
G^{\mathbb{R}}
is a split real form of
G
, called the Hitchin component. The
G
-Higgs bundles
(P,\phi)
in question are
G^{\mathbb{R}}
-Higgs bundles (see §
2.2
and
3.5
), and the equivariant harmonic maps land in a copy of the
G^{\mathbb{R}}
-symmetric space. See §
3.6
for the explicit Hitchin section. Hitchin components are the first examples of higher Teichmüller spaces, and the representations that they parametrize, so-called Hitchin representations, possess many interesting geometric and dynamical properties. For an overview on higher Teichmüller theory and Hitchin representations, see
[
39
]
.

See
[
25
]
for a list of conjectures about harmonic maps for Hitchin representations. In particular, in
[
10
]
and
[
25
]
it is conjectured that harmonic maps for Hitchin representations to
\textrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
, at immersed points, are never tangent to flats in the symmetric space. In
[
10
]
, this result is proved for the Hitchin harmonic maps associated with Coxeter cyclic
\textrm{SL}(n,\C)
-Higgs bundles. Our main result on the geometry of harmonic maps is the extension of this result for general
G.
Given an equivariant immersion
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\tilde{S}\to G/K,
we write
K_{\nu}(f_{*}T\tilde{S})
for the intrinsic curvature function, a priori living on
\tilde{S}
, but which descends to
S
.

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a split real form of a simple complex Lie group distinct from
\mathrm{E}_{7},\mathrm{E}_{8}
, and let
(P,\phi)
be a Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle over a closed Riemann surface
S
in the Hitchin section with equivariant harmonic map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\tilde{S}\to G/K
. Then, at every point of
S,

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(f_{*}T\tilde{S})<0.

The type
\textrm{A}_{n}
case is the result from
[
10
]
, and as we’ll explain in §
6.2
, the result is more or less proved for Lie groups of type
\textrm{B}_{n}
,
\textrm{C}_{n}
, and
\textrm{G}_{2}
in
[
10
]
, but not stated as such.

The proof of Theorem C consists of studying a system of equations derived from the Bochner-Toda equations (hence, from Theorem A), and then finding the right way to apply Dai-Li’s maximum principle. The proofs depend on the combinatorics of the extended Dynkin diagrams. For
\textrm{B}_{n}
and
\textrm{D}_{n},
we find a quick proof that uses the “prong” structure on the left end of the extended Dynkin diagram.
For cases
\textrm{C}_{n},\textrm{F}_{4},
and
\textrm{G}_{2},
our argument makes strong use of the fact that the Dynkin diagram of
\mathcal{Z}
is an undirected path. For cases
\textrm{A}_{n}
and
\textrm{E}_{6}
, the Dynkin diagram of
\mathcal{Z}
is not a path, but the
G^{\mathbb{R}}
-Higgs bundle condition imposes an extra symmetry that reduces our equations to a set of equations indexed by a folding of the Dynkin diagram, which is a path. The cases
\textrm{A}_{n},

\textrm{C}_{n}
,
\textrm{E}_{6},

\textrm{F}_{4},
and
\textrm{G}_{2}
can then be treated all together. The
\textrm{E}_{7}
and
\textrm{E}_{8}
cases involve different combinatorics, which we don’t pursue here.

Hitchin representations are defined for simple Lie groups; we include a discussion on the semisimple case in Remarks
6.1
and
6.2
. About semisimple groups, we find that we always have negative extrinsic curvature for products of Fuchsian representations into
\textrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})^{n}
(for further comments see Remark
6.2
). A component of such representations in the
\textrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})^{n}
character variety can be seen as a cousin of the Hitchin component, so it’s encouraging to know that the analogue of Dai-Li’s conjecture holds for all such representations.

Remark 1.7
.

In fact, Hitchin representations are defined in
[
20
]
for groups of adjoint type. Except for rank

Finally, for the cases
\textrm{A}_{n},

,
\textrm{E}_{6}
,
\textrm{F}_{4}
, and
\textrm{G}_{2}
, we in fact deduce Theorem C from a more general result. Let
(P,\phi)
be a simple and stable Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle over a closed surface
S
for
G
of type
\textrm{C}_{n}
,
\textrm{F}_{4},
or
\textrm{G}_{2}
, or a simple and stable Coxeter cyclic

-Higgs bundle for

We assume that
(P,\phi)
is not a
\C^{*}
-fixed point. Our work in §

-Higgs bundle of the type above, there is an associated ordered set of divisors
D_{0},\dots,D_{\ell}
,

. If

\textrm{C}_{n}

\textrm{F}_{4},

. We write

to specify futher that

at every point

. In the case of the Hitchin section,

is the divisor of a holomorphic differential on

, and

Theorem C’
.

For
G
of type
\textrm{A}_{n},\textrm{C}_{n},\textrm{E}_{6},\textrm{F}_{4},
or
\textrm{G}_{2}
, let
(P,\phi)
be as above, with ordered set of divisors
D_{0},\dots,D_{\ell}
, and equivariant harmonic map

. Assume that

(2)

D_{0}>D_{1}\geq D_{2}\geq\dots\geq D_{\ell}=0.

Then, at every point of
S
,

K_{\nu}(f_{*}T\tilde{S})<0.

See Remark
6.3
for a similar extension of Theorem C that applies to groups of type
\textrm{B}_{n}
and
\textrm{D}_{n}.
For completeness, we also explain that an analogous negative curvature result holds for

-fixed points (see the end of §

).

[

]
and Collier-Toulisse in

) on the holomorphic data for alternating surfaces, showing that when the condition holds, the alternating surfaces are infinitesimally rigid. In

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this section, let
G
be a semisimple (real) Lie group with no compact factors and Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. We fix a
G
-invariant non-degenerate bilinear form
\nu
on
\mathfrak{g}
. When
G
is simple,
\nu
is a multiple of the Killing form, but in the semisimple case there is a choice of scaling on each simple factor. In any case, after this section, most of this paper will be concerned with simple Lie groups.

2.1.
Homogeneous and symmetric spaces

Given a subgroup
H<G
and a space
V
, an action
\alpha
of
H
on
V
determines an equivalence relation via
(g,v)\sim(hg,\alpha(h)v)
for all

The associated bundle is
G\times_{\alpha}V=(G\times V)/\sim.
When the action

is implicit, we might write
G\times_{H}V
. If

preserves a structure on

(vector space structure, Lie bracket, etc.) then the associated bundle inherits that structure.

For
g\in G
, let
L_{g}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G\to G
be the left multiplication map. The Maurer-Cartan form of

is the section
\omega
of
\Omega^{1}(G,\mathfrak{g})
defined on vectors

by

\omega(v)=(L_{g^{-1}})_{*}v.

The Maurer-Cartan form provides an isomorphism of vector bundles from
TG
to
G\times\mathfrak{g}
, and transports
\nu
on
\mathfrak{g}
to a pseudo-Riemannian metric on

, which we continue to denote by
\nu
.

Let
(M,\sigma)
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold with a
C^{\infty}
and transitive action of
G
. If we fix a point

and set
H=\textrm{Stab}_{G}(x)
,
\mathfrak{h}=\textrm{Lie}(H)
, then

identifies with
G/H
and the Maurer-Cartan form yields an isomorphism between

and the associated bundle
G\times_{H}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
. We will be interested in the case where

is a reductive homogeneous space, which means that under the adjoint action of

,
\mathfrak{g}
admits a decomposition into
\textrm{ad}_{H}
-modules,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}.
In this setting,

identifies with
M\times_{\textrm{ad}|_{H}}\mathfrak{m},
and
\sigma
is realized under this identification as coming from a non-degenerate bilinear form of
\mathfrak{g}
(which, when

is simple, is a scalar multiple of
\nu
). Conversely, if
G/H
is reductive, then any
\nu
as above determines a
G
-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric on
G/H.

The most important case under consideration here is when

is a maximal compact subgroup

. To distinguish this case, we will write
K
for maximal compact subgroups. Then (after possibly multiplying
\sigma
by
-1
) the space
(M,\sigma
) is a Riemannian symmetric space of non-compact type. We always write the associated Lie algebra splitting, i.e., the Cartan decomposition, as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p},
with
\mathfrak{k}
the Lie algebra of

, and
\mathfrak{p}
the
\nu
-orthogonal complement. When

is simple, since the metric is unique up to scale, we will always assume it comes from the Killing form, and refer to this space as “the symmetric space” of

. We might write just

when we mean

2.2.
Harmonic maps and Higgs bundles

Let
(M,\sigma)
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold and let
\rho
be an action of
\pi_{1}(S)
on
(M,\sigma)
by isometries. From a
C^{2}

\rho
-equivariant map

we form the pullback bundle

, and the derivative

defines a section of the endomorphism bundle
T^{*}\tilde{S}\otimes f^{*}TM
. Complexifying, let
df=\partial f+\overline{\partial}f
be the decomposition into
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
parts. We denote by
\nabla
the connection on

induced by the Levi-Civita connection of
\sigma
, and we also use
\nabla
for its extension to
f^{*}TM\otimes\C
-valued forms.

Definition 2.1
.

The map

is harmonic if
\nabla^{0,1}\partial f=0.

Now, take the target to be the symmetric space
(G/K,\nu)
and assume from now on that

is a complex Lie group. We will explain that an equivariant harmonic map to
(G/K,\nu)
gives us the data of a
G
-Higgs bundle with a reduction of structure group satisfying the self-duality equations. Given a holomorphic
G
-bundle

over

we denote the adjoint bundle
P\times_{\textrm{ad}}\mathfrak{g}
by
\textrm{ad}P
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
G
-Higgs bundle is a pair
(P,\phi),
where
P
is a principal
G
-bundle over
S
and
\phi
is a holomorphic section of
\textrm{ad}P\otimes\mathcal{K}
called the Higgs field.

Returning to harmonic maps, associated with
\rho
we have the principal
G
-bundle
\tilde{S}\times_{\rho}G
over
S
. The
\rho
-equivariant map

is equivalent to a section of
\tilde{S}\times_{\rho}G
, which is in turn equivalent to a reduction of the structure group to

Q\subset\tilde{S}\times_{\rho}G
. Using the isomorphism

we see that

\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{k}

Under this last identification, via the Koszul-Malgrange theorem we endow

with the complex structure coming from the

-part of the Levi-Civita connection for

Moreover,

becomes a
(1,0)
-form valued in

and the harmonic map equation expresses that

-Higgs bundle

Given any

-Higgs bundle

(not necessarily the same as above), a reduction of the structure group to

which acts as a Cartan involution on each fiber. Furthermore,

determines a Hermitian metric

on

and

defines a real structure on each fiber. As is customary, we denote

.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a
G
-Higgs bundle

, we say that the reduction satisfies Hitchin’s self-duality equations if
\nabla_{h}+\phi+\phi^{*_{h}}
is flat, or, in other words, if

(3)

F(\nabla_{h})+[\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}]=0,

where

is the Chern connection on

.

Now, for our harmonic map

and its Higgs bundle

under the identification

is in fact the Chern connection

on

[

]
. As well,

A gauge transformation of a

-bundle

is a smooth

-bundle isomorphism, i.e., a

Remark 2.4
.

If

The two foundational existence results below constitute the non-abelian Hodge correspondence for complex Lie groups. A representation

Theorem 2.5

Let

There are notions of stability and polystability for a

A

Finally, while we’re working in the setting of

3.1.
Root systems and admissible systems

Let
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be an inner product space. For any non-zero vector
\alpha\in V,
we have an orthogonal hyperplane
L_{\alpha}
and reflection in
L_{\alpha}
, say
r_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\to V
. A finite subset
\Delta
of
V
is a root system if

•

for every
\alpha\in V
,
r_{\alpha}(\Delta)=\Delta,
and

•

for every
\alpha,\beta\in V
,
2\frac{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{\langle\beta,\beta\rangle}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Given
\Delta,
we can choose a set of positive roots
\Delta^{+}
, by which we mean
a subset such that

•

for each
\alpha\in\Delta,
exactly one of
\alpha
and
-\alpha
is contained in
\Delta^{+}
, and

•

if
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta^{+}
and
\alpha+\beta
is a root, then
\alpha+\beta\in\Delta^{+}.

The choice of positive roots determines a subset
\Pi
, the set of simple roots, which is characterized by being linearly independent and by the property that every element in
\Delta^{+}
is a non-negative linear combination of elements in
\Pi
.

The simple roots of a root system are an example of an admissible system: a collection of vectors
\Gamma=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
in
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
such that for every
i\neq j
,
a_{ij}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=2\frac{\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle}{\langle v_{j},v_{j}\rangle}
is a non-positive integer
[
31
, Chapter 3, §1.7]
. Out of an admissible system
\Gamma
, we can form a Cartan matrix
A(\Gamma)=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
, and a Dynkin diagram via the usual rules (see
[
31
, Chapter 3, §1.7]
). An automorphism of
\Gamma
is a permutation that preserves the inner products
\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle.
Given
\Gamma,
we write the automorphism group as
\textrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
.

The central examples of root systems and admissible systems, and the ones most important for this paper, are those coming from Lie algebras. A Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a maximal abelian semisimple subalgebra. Simultaneously diagonalizing the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathfrak{g}
yields the root space decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{\alpha}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},

where
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is a root, i.e., a weight of the adjoint representation restricted to
\mathfrak{h}
, and
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is the root space

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}^{\C}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\textrm{ for all }h\in\mathfrak{h},[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\}.

The roots of the Lie algebra form a root system
\Delta
on
(\mathfrak{h}^{*},\nu)
. Choosing positive roots
\Delta^{+}
, the simple roots
\Pi
are an admissible system.

Given a root
\beta=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi}m_{\alpha}\alpha,

m_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}
, the height of
\beta
is
\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi}m_{\alpha}.
The highest root
\delta
is the root with the largest height. We will write
\delta=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi}n_{\alpha}\alpha
. We define the extended simple root system
\mathcal{Z}
to be
\Pi\cup\{-\delta\}
;
\mathcal{Z}
is an admissible system itself, and its Dynkin diagram is called the extended Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
.

At a few points in this paper, it will be helpful to make use of a basis for
\mathfrak{g}
that’s well adapted to the root system. For every root
\alpha
we have the vector
h_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}
, defined by the relation that, for all roots
\beta
,
\beta(h_{\alpha})=2\frac{\nu(\alpha,\beta)}{\nu(\alpha,\alpha)}
, where
\nu
here is the dualized Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}.
One can choose root vectors
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
so that we have a Chevalley basis for
\mathfrak{g}
,

\{e_{\alpha},e_{-\alpha},h_{\beta}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\alpha\in\Delta^{+},\beta\in\Pi\},

characterized by
[e_{\alpha},e_{-\alpha}]=-h_{\alpha}
and
[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}]=N_{\alpha,\beta}e_{\alpha+\beta}
, for some integers
N_{\alpha,\beta}
(see
[
31
, Chapter 3, Theorem 1.19]
). For later use, we take note of the following fact about
\mathcal{Z}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{Z}
, then, in a basis as above,
[e_{\alpha},e_{-\beta}]=-\delta_{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha}.

Proof.

Recall that
[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}]
is non-zero if and only if
\alpha+\beta
is a root. Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be distinct simple roots. If
\alpha-\beta
is a root, then without loss of generality it is positive, which contradicts simplicity of
\beta
. For any positive root
\alpha
,
\alpha+\delta
is not a root, since if it were its height would be greater than that of
\delta
.
∎

As well, it will be helpful in the proof of Theorem A to have a Cartan involution of
\mathfrak{g}
that preserves
\mathfrak{h}.
With respect to the Chevalley base, we define
\hat{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by

(4)

\hat{\rho}(h_{\alpha})=-h_{\alpha},\hskip 2.84526pt\hat{\rho}(e_{\alpha})=-e_{-\alpha},\hskip 2.84526pt\hat{\rho}(e_{-\alpha})=-e_{\alpha}.

This is the same
\hat{\rho}
that Baraglia works with in
[
2
]
.

Finally, to study systems of equations indexed by admissible systems, which we’ll do to prove Theorems B,C, and C’, we need to recall the relevant facts about the angles between simple roots. For
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
an arbitrary inner product space with root system
\Delta
, and
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta,
set
a_{\alpha,\beta}=2\frac{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}
. Proofs for the proposition below can be found in
[
31
, Chapter 3, Proposition 1.7 and Theorem 1.12]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a root system on an inner product space as above, with positive root set
\Delta^{+}
and simple roots
\Pi
.

•

If
\alpha,\beta\in\Pi
, then
a_{\alpha,\beta}=2
if
\alpha=\beta
and
a_{\alpha,\beta}\leq 0
for
\alpha\neq\beta.

•

The highest root
\delta
is dominant: if
\alpha
is a simple root, then
a_{\alpha,\delta}\geq 0
(and hence
a_{\alpha,-\delta}\leq 0
).

For root systems arising from Lie algebras, the angles between the roots are constrained, so that
a_{\alpha,\beta}=0,2,\sqrt{2},
or
\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}.
These values can be read off of the Cartan matrix or the Dynkin diagram of the extended system of simple roots.

3.2.
Inner automorphisms of Lie algebras

Here we review the known classification of inner semisimple automorphisms of simple Lie algebras, and then we discuss Coxeter automorphisms.

We continue with
G,\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},

\Pi,
etc., as in §
3.1
. Any element
x\in\mathfrak{h}
is determined by the collection
\{\alpha(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\alpha\in\Pi\}
. Setting
x_{-\delta}=1-\delta(x)
and
x_{\alpha}=\alpha(x)
for
\alpha\in\Pi
we call
\{x_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}\}
the barycentric coordinates of

. For
n_{-\delta}=1
, the barycentric coordinates satisfy
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}n_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}=1.

The adjoint group of
G
, say
\textrm{ad}G
, is the image of
G
under the adjoint representation. The fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\textrm{ad}G)
is naturally identified with a subgroup of
\textrm{Aut}(\mathcal{Z})
that acts simply transitively on the roots
\alpha
with

(see
[
31
, Chapter 3, §3.6]
). Moreover,
\pi_{1}(\textrm{ad}G)
acts on

by permuting barycentric coordinates. The theorem below is the classification of inner semisimple automorphisms.

Theorem 3.3

(Theorem 3.11 in Chapter 3 of
[
31
]
)
.

Any inner semisimple automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
is conjugate to an automorphism of the form
\textrm{ad}_{g_{x}}
, where
{g_{x}}=\textrm{exp}(2\pi ix)
, and

satisfies 1)
\textrm{Re}(x_{\alpha})\geq 0
for all
i
, and 2) if
\textrm{Re}(x_{\alpha})=0
, then
\textrm{Im}(x_{\alpha})\geq 0
. Two such automorphisms are conjugate if and only if the barycentric coordinates of the first can be obtained from those of the second by a permutation from
\pi_{1}(\textrm{ad}G).

Any finite order automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, by consideration of the minimal polynomial. If
s
is an inner automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
of finite order
k,
then the element
x\in\mathfrak{h}
satisfies, in the barycentric coordinates,
x_{\alpha}=p_{\alpha}/k
, for some non-negative
p_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}n_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}=k.

Definition 3.4
.

The Coxeter number
r
of the root system
\Delta
is
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}n_{\alpha}.

For exposition on the Coxeter number, see
[
22
, §3.16]
. It can be characterized, among other ways, as the order of a Coxeter element of the Weyl group (a product of all reflections coming from a choice of simple roots), or as the highest degree of a polynomial in a homogeneous generating set for the algebra of
\textrm{ad}(G)
-invariant polynomials on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 3.5
.

We say that an inner automorphism
s
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a Coxeter automorphism if
s
has order
r.
An element of
G
inducing

is called a Coxeter element.

The condition that
s
is a Coxeter automorphism forces every
p_{i}
to be equal to
1
. We deduce the following properties.

Proposition 3.6
.

All Coxeter automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
are conjugate. For
s
an order
m
automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
, let
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
be the eigenspace grading,
\mathfrak{g}_{j}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}s(x)=e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{m}}x\}
. Then, if

is Coxeter,

(1)

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}.

(2)

\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is the sum of the extended simple root spaces.

The first assertion is immediate from Theorem
3.3
. To see that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}
, we conjugate

into the form of Theorem
3.3
,
s=\textrm{exp}(2\pi ix)
. It is seen from the structure of
x
that
s
is regular in the sense that if
s^{\prime}
is another inner automorphism whose fixed-point subalgebra is
\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\prime},
then
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{0}\leq\dim\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\prime},
from which the claim follows. And to isolate the
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
in the grading, we again use the explicit form of the
x
.

3.3.
Cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles

Let
P
be a holomorphic
G
-bundle over a Riemann surface
S
with gauge group
\mathcal{G}
and adjoint bundle
\textrm{ad}P.
An element
s\in\mathcal{G}
induces an automorphism of
\textrm{ad}P,
which in any fiber in any trivialization of
\textrm{ad}P
is realized as an inner automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
, which we continue to denote by
s
. If
s
has finite order, then by connectedness of
S
, since the barycentric expression for
s
has discrete values, it only changes according to the action of
\pi_{1}(\textrm{ad}G)
as we vary over the surface. Hence, setting
\zeta=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m}}
, the action of
s
in any fiber yields a
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-grading of
\mathfrak{g}
as
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathfrak{g}_{i},
where
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is the
\zeta^{i}
-eigenspace (this is the grading from Proposition
3.6
). Let
G_{0}\subset G
be the connected Lie group obtained via exponentiating
\mathfrak{g}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, which is always reductive
[
31
, Chapter 3, Proposition 3.6]
. Inspired by Vinberg’s work
[
38
]
, a pair
(G_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{i})
is typically called a Vinberg
\theta
-pair
[
16
]
. In turn, we have a
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-grading of
\textrm{Aut}P
into
\zeta^{i}
-eigenbundles,

\textrm{ad}P=\oplus_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{C}_{j}.

The Lie algebra subbundle
\mathcal{C}_{0}
then determines a reduction of structure group of
P
from
G
to
G_{0},
and each
\mathcal{C}_{j}
is an associated bundle of this
G_{0}
-bundle, modelled on
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
.

Definition 3.7
.

We say that
(P,\phi)
is a cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle if there exists a holomorphic gauge transformation
s
of finite order
m
such that
s^{*}\phi=\zeta\phi.

That is, in the notation above,
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
.
Following an observation of Vinberg in
[
38
]
, if
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{k}
, then we can define a new grading modulo
m^{\prime}=\frac{m}{\textrm{gcd}(m,k)}
by setting
\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{g}_{jk},
for which we then have
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\prime}.
Hence, if
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{j}
, we can always assume up to modifications that
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{1},
so the definition above encompasses
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{j}.

In this paper we make the following definition.

Definition 3.8
.

A cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle
(P,\phi)
is Coxeter cyclic if the gauge transformation
s
has order equal to the Coxeter number.

Compare with other definitions of cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles
[
2
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
35
]
. In most references, cyclic Higgs bundles are simple and stable cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles on a closed Riemann surface (and the order
m
is usually the Coxeter number). On a closed surface we have the moduli space structure
\mathcal{M}_{S}(G)
, and hence such
G
-Higgs bundles are characterized as fixed points for the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{S}(G)
given by multiplying the Higgs field by
m^{th}
roots of unity.

A more general definition is given in
[
16
, §3.2]
(see also
[
17
]
). Given a general
\theta\in\textrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
and a principal
G
-bundle
P,
one gets another principal
G
-bundle
\theta(P),
defined by the same total space as
P
, but equipped with the
G
-action
p\cdot g=p\theta(g)
. As well,
\theta
transports any Higgs field
\phi
on
P
to a Higgs field
\theta(\phi)
on
\theta(P).
A
G
-Higgs bundle
(P,\phi)
is
\theta
-cyclic if there exists a
G
-Higgs bundle isomorphism between
(P,\phi)
and
(\theta(P),\zeta\theta(\phi)).
For
\theta
inner,

is isomorphic to
\theta(P),
and this is equivalent to a cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle in our sense. For general
\theta
, simple and stable
\theta
-cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles on closed surfaces can be interpreted as fixed points of other
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-actions on the moduli space (see
[
17
]
).

Remark 3.9
.

There should be versions of Proposition
1.2
and Theorem A for
\theta
-cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles, but we don’t address this here. Associated with every
\theta\in\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})
is a root system
\Delta^{\theta}
, called the real
\theta
-root system, which comes with its extended system of real

-roots

and its own Coxeter number

(see
[

). There is an analogue of Theorem

, namely
[

, Chapter 3, Theorem 3.16]
, which describes any automorphism of

in the class of

in terms of barycentric coordinates on a semisimple abelian subalgebra

, indexed by

. Presumably these notions can be used to study Hitchin’s equations, as we’ll do below in the case that

is trivial (equivalently, inner). We’ll see in Remark

that when

-primitive harmonic maps for

]
).

We now prove one side of the equivalence of Proposition
1.2
(without the adjoint type assumption). The other side can also be proven without any further preparations, but we defer the proof to later on, since the notation in the proof will be helpful in the proof of Theorem A.

Proposition 3.10
.

A Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle
(P,\phi)
determines a collection of line subbundles
\{L_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}\}
, holomorphic sections
\phi_{\alpha}\in H^{0}(S,L_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{K})
, and an isomorphism from
\otimes_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}L_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}
to
\mathcal{O}
.

In the notation of the paragraph before Definition
3.7
, for a general cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle,
\mathcal{C}_{1}
decomposes into
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-adjoint-invariant subbundles according to the decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
into irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\oplus_{i}\mathcal{C}_{1}^{i}
. Proposition
3.10
clarifies this decomposition in the Coxeter case.

Proof.

For
(P,\phi)
a Coxeter cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle, the subgroup
G_{0}
is a Cartan subgroup
H
of
G.
Thus, fixing a positive root set for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
, which then yields an extended simple root set

, Proposition

implies that the

-decomposition of

is of the form

for some holomorphic line bundles

Moreover, we can write

and decompose accordingly. To see that

is trivial, note that the action of

is by

, for

-trivialization identifies

with the

-trivial line bundle.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

It will be useful to know for the proof of Theorem B that if

-action on the moduli space of

Satisfying results around polystability have recently been established. A cyclic

3.4.
Hitchin’s equations for cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles

We now prove Theorem A. A number of the results in this subsection hold for more general cyclic
G
-Higgs bundles, with Cartan subgroups replaced with

’s. We focus on the Coxeter cyclic case to keep things simpler, and because we’re mainly concerned with Theorem A.

3.4.1.
Principal bundles

Over the course of the proof of Theorem A, it will be convenient to choose good trivializations for
\textrm{ad}P
, as well as explicit realizations of reductions of the structure group. This will be achieved through Lemma
3.12
below.

Let
P
be a holomorphic principal
G
-bundle over a complex manifold
X
, and denote by
\mathrm{Isom}(\textrm{ad}P,\mathfrak{g})
the bundle whose fiber over
x\in X
is the set of isomorphisms
(\textrm{ad}P)_{x}\to\mathfrak{g}
. This admits a right
\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
action with quotient
X
, i.e., it is a principal
\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
-bundle.

Lemma 3.12
.

Assume that
G
is of adjoint type. Then there is an embedding

\displaystyle\Psi_{P}\colon P\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{Isom}(\textrm{ad}P,\mathfrak{g})

that is equivariant with respect to the (faithful) adjoint representation
G\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Suppose now that we are working over a local patch
U\subset X
such that
P|_{U}
is trivial. Then
P
admits a holomorphic section
\xi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}U\to P
. In particular,
\Psi_{P}\circ\xi
defines a holomorphic section of
\mathrm{Isom}(\textrm{ad}P,\mathfrak{g})
that can be identified with a trivialization
(\textrm{ad}P)|_{U}\to U\times\mathfrak{g}
. We refer to such a trivialization as a
P
-trivialization.

In the coming proof of Theorem A we will often work in
P
-trivializations. We will also typically construct reductions of the structure group
Q\subset P
by specifying the set
\Psi_{P}(Q)\subset\Psi_{P}(P)\subset\mathrm{Isom}(\textrm{ad}P,\mathfrak{g})
, and then work in
Q
-trivializations of
\textrm{ad}P
. This is equivalent to working in trivializations from
\Psi_{P}(Q)
. Occasionally,
Q
will have a structure group that is merely a real torus (rather than a complex Lie group), and in this case, the analogue of Lemma
3.12
still holds, with the caveat that the embedding
\Psi_{P}
is not holomorphic, and hence neither are the
Q
-trivializations.

Finally, we include the construction of
\Psi_{P}
from Lemma
3.12
for completeness.

Proof of Lemma
3.12
.

Identify
\textrm{ad}P
as the quotient of
P\times\mathfrak{g}
by the
G
action given by
g\cdot(p,\xi)=(pg^{-1},g\xi)
. The desired embedding is given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{P}\colon\;\;\;P

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Isom}(\textrm{ad}P,\mathfrak{g}),

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\left([(pg,\xi)]\to g\xi g^{-1}\right).

∎

3.4.2.
From Higgs bundles to (
1
)

Here we prove the first part of the equivalence of Theorem A. Let
(P,\phi)
be a stable cyclic
G
-Higgs bundle on a closed Riemann surface
S
with holomorphic gauge transformation
s\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}P\to P
of order
m
such that
s^{*}\phi=\zeta\phi
, where
\zeta
is an
m^{th}
root of unity (here,

is not yet assumed to be of adjoint type). The gauge transformation
s
induces an automorphism of
\textrm{ad}P
, for which
\phi
lies in the
\zeta
-eigenspace. Let
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\textrm{ad}P\to\textrm{ad}P
be the anti-linear involution that solves the Hitchin equation, giving rise to Hermitian metric
h
with Chern connection
\nabla_{h}.
First we prove the following.

Proposition 3.13
.

s^{*}\rho=\rho
, and
s^{*}\nabla_{h}=\nabla_{h}
.

The above is due to Baraglia in the case of the Hitchin section (in the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 4.1]
), and the general case of
s^{*}\nabla_{h}=\nabla_{h}
is in the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 4.2.2]
.

Proof.

The anti-linear involution
s^{*}\rho
solves the Hitchin equations for
(P,s^{*}\phi)=(P,\zeta\phi).
Thus, from Remark
2.4
,
s^{*}\rho
also solves the Hitchin equations for
(P,\phi).
Since
(P,\phi)
is stable, the uniqueness theory for Hitchin’s equations on closed surfaces applies, and implies that
s^{*}\rho=\rho
. Moreover, the Chern connection of
s^{*}\rho
is
s^{*}\nabla_{h},
and hence
s^{*}\nabla_{h}=\nabla_{h}.

∎

Note that
s^{*}\nabla_{h}=\nabla_{h}
implies that
\nabla_{h}
preserves the eigenbundle splitting
\textrm{ad}P=\oplus_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{C}_{j}
.

Remark 3.14
.

Since the structure group reduces to
G_{0}
and
s^{*}\rho=\rho,
the harmonic map lifts to the homogeneous space
G/(K\cap G_{0})
. Since
G/(K\cap G_{0})
is reductive, it is pseudo-Riemannian. Collier proves that it is a naturally reductive homogeneous space (see
[

, Definition 4.3.1, Lemma 4.3.3]
), which implies this lift is in fact harmonic (see
[
40
]
). In the Coxeter cyclic case, this harmonic lift is a

-primitive harmonic map as considered in
[
27
]
.

From this point on, we assume that the group
G
is of adjoint type and that
(P,\phi)
is Coxeter cyclic, so that
m
is the Coxeter number
r
. We now think of
P
as the reduction of the principal bundle
\mathrm{Isom}(\textrm{ad}P,\mathfrak{g})
as in Lemma
3.12
and its surrounding paragraphs. Let
g
be a Coxeter element of
G
, and let
T
be a maximal torus containing
g
. Let
H
be the Cartan subgroup that is the complexification of
T
, and let
K
be the maximal compact subgroup of
G
that contains
T
. Let
\hat{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be the anti-linear involution with fixed point set
\mathfrak{k}
, the Lie algebra of
K
. Let the Lie algebras of
T
and
H
be
\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{h}
respectively. We can define
\hat{\rho}
exactly as in §3.1. Note that these choices are also found in Baraglia’s paper
[
2
]
. By standard Lie theory, any such choice is conjugate to that of Baraglia (for a justification, one can see
[
24
, Proposition 3.1.2]
).

We define
P_{H}\subset P
to be the
\Psi_{P}
pre-image of the set of linear isomorphisms
A\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}(\textrm{ad}P)_{x}\to\mathfrak{g}
in
\mathrm{Isom}(\textrm{ad}P,\mathfrak{g})_{x}
, for

, such that

\displaystyle A(s\xi)=\mathrm{Ad}_{g}A(\xi).

Let
P_{K}\subset P
be the
\Psi_{P}
pre-image of the set of isomorphisms

with

\displaystyle A({\rho}\xi)=\hat{\rho}(A(\xi)).

Note that
P_{K},P_{H}
are preserved by the right actions of
K,H
, respectively, and moreover
P_{K}/K=S
,
P_{H}/H=S
. Indeed, both quotients are non-empty, since the centralizer of

is
H
and the normalizer of

in

is

, and both quotients are equal to

(rather than a cover) because any two Coxeter elements are conjugate and any two maximal compacts are conjugate. Thus

are principal bundles with structure groups

. Moreover,
P_{H}
is a holomorphic principal bundle, since

and
\Psi_{P}
are both holomorphic. This reduction

is isomorphic to the previously defined reduction

, and henceforth we identify the two reductions. Observe that

is precisely the reduction of the structure group of

to the maximal compact

that corresponds to
\rho
, and hence the connection
\nabla_{h}
is induced from a connection on

.

Since
s^{*}\rho=\rho,

P_{T}=P_{K}\cap P_{H}
is non-empty and defines a principal
T
-bundle. We now study how the metric and connection,
h
and

, interact with the reduction

.

Lemma 3.15
.

\nabla_{h}
is induced from a connection on
P_{T}
.

Proof.

We work locally over an open set
U\subset S
. Pick a (possibly non-holomorphic)

-trivialization

. Let the connection be

in these coordinates. Then

is a 1-form taking values in
\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{k})\subset\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g})
, since
\nabla_{h}
is induced from a connection on
P_{K}
.

Note that from the fact that
s
preserves
\nabla_{h}
, it follows that
g
preserves

. Thus
B
takes values in the set
\mathrm{ad}\{\xi\in\mathfrak{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathrm{Ad}_{g}\xi=\xi\}=\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{k})=\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{t})
. Thus

takes values in

, and hence
\nabla_{h}
is induced from the connection

.
∎

Recall from Proposition
3.10
, that there exist holomorphic line subbundles
L_{\alpha}\subset\textrm{ad}P
such that

takes values in
\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}L_{\alpha}
.

Lemma 3.16
.

In the metric induced on
\textrm{ad}P
by
\rho
, the subbundles
\{L_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}\}
are mutually orthogonal.

Proof.

In a local

-trivialization, the reduction
\rho
is represented by
\hat{\rho}
, and each subbundle

is represented by
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
. For

distinct non-zero roots, the root spaces

and

are

\nu(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},-\hat{\rho}(\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}))=\nu(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{-\beta})=0
. Since any two choices of
\hat{\rho},g
are conjugate, the result follows.
∎

We are now ready to express the Hitchin equations in terms of
L_{\alpha}
. Let
\mu_{\alpha}
be the metric on

that is induced by the
\rho
-metric on
\textrm{ad}P
, and let
\phi_{\alpha}\in H^{0}(S,\mathcal{K}\otimes L_{\alpha})
be the component of

in the direction

.

Fix a small open patch
U\subset S
and a
P_{T}
-trivialization
(\textrm{ad}P)|_{U}\cong U\times\mathfrak{g}
. We abuse notation slightly and denote the coordinate representations of
\phi,\phi_{\alpha}
in this trivialization by
\phi,\phi_{\alpha}
, so that, in particular,
\phi_{\alpha}
is a 1-form taking values in
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
. Moreover, the Hitchin metric on
\textrm{ad}P
is represented in coordinates as
\nu(\cdot,-\hat{\rho}(\cdot))
. Using Proposition
3.10
,
\otimes_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}L_{\alpha}^{\otimes n_{\alpha}}
is the trivial flat Hermitian bundle. We compute the two terms in Hitchin’s equations (
3

First, the
[\phi,\phi^{*}]

For
\alpha\neq\beta

\mathcal{Z}

\displaystyle[\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\beta}^{*_{h}}]=[\phi_{\alpha},-\hat{\rho}(\phi_{\beta})]\in[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{-\beta}]=0.

The diagonal terms
[\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}^{*_{h}}]

\displaystyle[\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}^{*_{h}}]=[\phi_{\alpha},-\hat{\rho}(\phi_{\alpha})]\in[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}]\subset\mathfrak{h}.

Note that by basic properties of the Chevalley basis
[

, we have

\displaystyle[e_{\alpha},-\hat{\rho}(e_{\alpha})]

\displaystyle=[e_{\alpha},e_{-\alpha}]=h_{\alpha}=\nu(e_{\alpha},e_{-\alpha})\frac{\nu(\alpha,\alpha)}{2}h_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\nu(e_{\alpha},-\hat{\rho}(e_{\alpha}))\frac{\nu(\alpha,\alpha)}{2}h_{\alpha}.

In the local
P_{T}
-trivialization, we can write
\phi_{\alpha}=e_{\alpha}\otimes f_{\alpha},
where

is a holomorphic

[\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha}^{*_{h}}]=f_{\alpha}\wedge\overline{f_{\alpha}}\otimes[e_{\alpha},-\hat{\rho}(e_{\alpha})]=f_{\alpha}\wedge\overline{f_{\alpha}}\otimes\nu(e_{\alpha},-\hat{\rho}(e_{\alpha}))\frac{\nu(\alpha,\alpha)}{2}h_{\alpha}=\mu_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})\frac{\nu(\alpha,\alpha)}{2}h_{\alpha}.

Above, the

is the extension to

-valued forms, and
\mu_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})=\mu_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha}\wedge\overline{\phi}_{\alpha}),

which is the map

associated with
\alpha,
extended to bundle-valued forms and evaluated on
\phi_{\alpha}\wedge\overline{\phi}_{\alpha}
. We prefer the notation
\mu_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha}\wedge\overline{\phi}_{\alpha}),
since it makes clear that the object in question is a

(5)

\displaystyle[\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}\mu_{\alpha}(\phi_{\alpha}\wedge\bar{\phi}_{\alpha})\frac{\nu(\alpha,\alpha)}{2}h_{\alpha}.

In particular, for any root

(6)

\displaystyle\alpha([\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}])=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{Z}}\nu(\alpha,\beta)\mu_{\beta}(\phi_{\beta}\wedge\bar{\phi}_{\beta}).

Second, the

Note that
\nabla_{h}
is the Chern connection for the holomorphic bundle
\textrm{ad}P

\nabla_{h}
is also the Chern connection for the subbundle

. Since the Hitchin metric splits into
\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}\mu_{\alpha}
over
\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}L_{\alpha}
, by uniqueness of Chern connections,
F(\nabla_{h})|_{L_{\alpha}}
acts as multiplication by
F(\mu_{\alpha})
.

In our local
P_{T}
-trivialization, identify
\textrm{ad}P
with
\mathfrak{g}
and
L_{\alpha}
with
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
F(\nabla_{h})
is a 2-form taking values in
\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g})

\mathfrak{h}

has pointwise kernel containing
\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

\alpha(F(\nabla_{h}))=F(\mu_{\alpha}).

Proof of Theorem A, first side.

We fix a trivialization as above. Since the simple roots span

3.6.

Let

4.1.
Bochner–Toda system

Fix an ambient metric

We now let

\mu

\phi=\phi_{0}dz,
for

|\phi|_{h,\mu}^{2}=\nu(\phi_{0},\phi_{0}^{*_{h}})|dz|_{\mu}^{2}.

Recall the definition of energy density from the introduction:

By equivariance of

By Proposition

and sections

By Theorem A and the discussion after the proof, we have a collection of metrics

on

Proposition 4.1
.

When

is Coxeter cyclic,

e(f_{\phi})=|\phi|_{h,\mu}^{2}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}e_{\alpha}.

Proof.

The first equality is well known and holds for arbitrary (stable)
G
-Higgs bundles. Using the isomorphism induced by the Maurer-Cartan form,
f^{*}\nu=\nu(\phi,\phi^{*_{h}})+\nu(\phi,\phi)+\nu(\phi^{*_{h}},\phi^{*_{h}}).
Taking the trace isolates the first term, and we get
e(f)=\mu^{-1}\nu(\phi,\phi^{*_{h}})=|\phi|_{h,\mu}^{2}.

The quantity

agrees with the corresponding quantity for the Higgs field on the associated

-Higgs bundle (geometrically, we have a local isometry of symmetric spaces that intertwines the harmonic maps). The second equality then follows immediately from Lemma

∎

It makes sense to refer to

\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}
. To do so, we additionally need the following local computation.

Lemma 4.2

Away from the zeros of

Write

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mu}\log e_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\mu(z)}\frac{\partial^{2}\log e_{\alpha}(z)}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}=\frac{2i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\mu_{\alpha}(z)}{\Omega_{\mu}}-\frac{4}{\mu(z)}\frac{\partial^{2}\log\mu(z)}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}

4.2.
Dai-Li Maximum Principle

Dai-Li’s maximum principle is below. We do not apply it to Bochner-Toda systems explicitly, but we apply it to systems that we further derive from Bochner-Toda systems.

Theorem 4.4

(Lemma 3.1 in
[
11
]
)
.

Let

be a closed Riemannian manifold, and for

let
P_{i}
be an isolated subset of

(which can be empty), and
P=\cup_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}.
Let

be smooth and bounded functions indexed by

, such that

(1)

(cooperative)

and
c_{ij}\leq 0
for
i\neq j
,

(2)

(column-diagonally dominant)

for all

(3)

(fully coupled) there is no partition
\{1,2,...,n\}=A\cup B
such that

for
i\in A,j\in B
.

Suppose that

,

, are smooth functions that approach

around

and solve the system

\displaystyle\Delta u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{ij}u_{j}+\langle Y,\nabla u_{i}\rangle+f_{i}\text{ for }i=1,2,...,n,

for some continuous vector field
Y
and non-positive continuous functions

Consider the following conditions.

(1)

(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\neq(0,\dots,0).

(2)

P
is non-empty.

(3)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\geq 0.

Either condition (1) or (2) implies

for all

and condition (3) implies either
u_{i}>0
for all
i
or
u_{i}\equiv 0
for all

We use condition (3) to prove Theorem B, and condition (2) to prove Theorems C and C’. Theorem
A.5
in the appendix relaxes condition (3).

5.2.
Volume entropy

We now prove Corollary B. All that we will use about volume entropy is that it satisfies the following two properties, which are easy to see from the definition.

(1)

Let

6.
Extrinsic curvature

Here we study extrinsic curvature of harmonic maps and in particular prove Theorems C and C’. Since the
G
-Higgs bundles in the Hitchin section are
G^{\mathbb{R}}
-Higgs bundles, for every case except
\textrm{B}_{n}
and
\textrm{D}_{n}
, Theorem C becomes a special case of Theorem C’.

6.1.
Curvature

Let
(P,\phi)
be a stable
G
-Higgs bundle on a closed surface
S
of genus
g\geq 2
with harmonic map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\tilde{S}\to G/K
and harmonic metric
h
. As before, fix a conformal metric
\mu
on
S
with area form
\Omega_{\mu}
, with respect to which we define the root energies
e_{\alpha}
. Identifying the tangent bundle of
G/K
with
G/K\times\mathfrak{p}
, the curvature tensor of the Levi-Civita connection is given by

R(X,Y)Z=-[[X,Y],Z].

It follows that the sectional curvature
K_{\nu}(f_{*}T\tilde{S})
of the tangent plane of the harmonic map is

(14)

K_{\nu}(f_{*}T\tilde{S})=-\frac{\nu([\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}],[\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}])}{|\phi|_{h}^{2}-|\nu(\phi,\phi)|^{2}}

(see, e.g.,
[
11
, pp 16]
).
Since
[\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}]
is
h
-self-adjoint,
\nu([\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}],[\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}])\geq 0
, and hence the harmonic map is tangent to a flat precisely when
\nu([\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}],[\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}])=0.

Passing to an
\textrm{ad}G
-Higgs bundle, the harmonic map is transported to the
\textrm{ad}G
-symmetric space, with no effect on the sectional curvature. Hence, when
\phi
is Coxeter cyclic, we see by (
5
) that

(15)

[\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}]=-2i\Omega_{\mu}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}h_{\alpha}\frac{\nu(\alpha,\alpha)}{2}e_{\alpha}.

Thus,

(16)

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\Omega_{\mu}^{-1}[\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}]\right\rvert}_{\nu}^{2}=4\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{Z}}e_{\alpha}e_{\beta}\nu(\alpha,\beta)=4\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}e_{\alpha}\alpha\right\rvert}_{\nu}^{2}.

In particular, the curvature
K_{\nu}(f_{*}T\tilde{S})
vanishes if and only if
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}}e_{\alpha}\alpha=0
. That is, if and only if
e_{\alpha}=Cn_{\alpha}
for some
C\geq 0
and all
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}
. Or, if we consider
\tilde{e}_{\alpha}=e_{\alpha}\nu(\alpha,\alpha),
the condition is
\tilde{e}_{\alpha}=Cn_{\alpha}\nu(\alpha,\alpha)
for some
C\geq 0
.

Remark 6.1
.

If
(P,\phi)
is a direct sum of Higgs bundles
(P_{i},\phi_{i})
, which occurs for example if we’re working with a semisimple group
G=\prod_{i}G_{i}
, then any harmonic metric preserves the splitting and hence the numerator in (
14
) splits as a sum
-\sum_{i}\nu([\phi_{i},\phi_{i}^{*_{h}}],[\phi_{i},\phi_{i}^{*_{h}}])
. Consequently, once we know Theorem C, we see that the extrinsic curvature is negative as long as one of the Higgs bundles is Hitchin and Coxeter cyclic.

Remark 6.2
.

Concerning the remark above, one interesting case is
G=\textrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})^{n}.
For each factor, the expression
\nu([\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}],[\phi,\phi^{*_{h}}])
is zero only at points where the harmonic map is not an immersion, so in particular if one factor comes from a Fuchsian representation, then the curvature is negative. We deduce that the analogue of the conjecture from
[
10
]
holds for products of components of the
\textrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
character variety such that at least one factor is Fuchsian. Moreover, we can see that a harmonic map to
(\mathbb{H}^{2})^{n}
is tangent to a flat exactly when the singular sets of all the component harmonic maps intersect.

Before moving to Theorems C and C’, we address negative curvature for
\C^{*}
-fixed points. If
(P,\phi)
is a
\C^{*}
-fixed point, then
\phi
is nilpotent, as can be seen using the Hitchin fibration. We then observe that any harmonic map arising from a nilpotent
G
-Higgs bundle has negative extrinsic curvature at immersed points. This observation follows from (
14
) and the fact that no non-zero nilpotent endomorphism of a Hermitian vector space commutes with its adjoint (in fact, there is a well-known comparison between the norm of the endomorphism and the norm of the commutator with the adjoint, see
[
26
, Lemma 3.5]
).

has a higher order of vanishing than
w_{i+1}
. As we approach these points (if any), the corresponding function
v_{i}
tends to
+\infty.
Since
v_{1}=\log\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}}
is unbounded from above (since
w_{1}
, and therefore
f_{1}
, vanishes somewhere), condition (2) from Theorem
4.4
is triggered. It follows that
v_{i}>0
for all
i=1,2,...,n-1
, and moreover that
f_{i+1}>f_{i}
. The result is shown.
∎

Appendix A
Maximum principles for elliptic systems

Here we generalize the methods of Dai–Li that prove Theorem
4.4
(i.e. Lemma 3.1 in
[
11
]
) to get a general result that can be used to produce more maximum principles, Theorem
A.2
. Theorem
A.2
is not used in the proof of our main theorem, but we however believe it helps further explain Theorem
4.4
.

We show in particular how to use Theorem
A.2
to prove a slight generalization of case (3) of Dai-Li’s maximum principle, namely Theorem
A.5
below. Since Dai-Li’s maximum principle is an important ingredient in the proofs of the main theorems, we thought it worthwhile to include this content.

A.1.
Graph neighbourhood maximum principle

Denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the standard Euclidean inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and by
A^{*}
the adjoint of the linear map
A\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
with respect to this inner product. Denote by
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
the space of endomorphisms
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
X
be a manifold and
A\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a smooth map. Given vectors
v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say that
v_{0}
is
A
-weaker than
(v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{k})
if there exist smooth functions
\lambda_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
for
i=1,2,...,k
, such that

\displaystyle A(x)v_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(x)(v_{0}-v_{i})\text{ for all }x\in X.

Moreover, we say that
v_{0}
is minimally
A
-weaker than
(v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{k})
if no
\lambda_{i}
vanishes identically.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
X
be a closed Riemannian manifold, and let
A\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a smooth map. Suppose that
u\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a solution to

\Delta u=Au.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a finite set of vectors with a distinguished subset
S_{\partial}\subset S
, and suppose that
\Gamma
is a directed graph on
S
, such that

(1)

for any
v\in S\setminus S_{\partial}
, the vector
v
is minimally
A^{*}
-weaker than its (directed) neighbourhood, and

(2)

for any
v\in S\setminus S_{\partial}
, there exists a vertex in
S_{\partial}
that can be reached from
v
.

Then

(17)

\displaystyle\min_{v\in S\setminus S_{\partial}}\min_{X}\langle v,u\rangle\geq\min_{v\in S_{\partial}}\min_{X}\langle v,u\rangle.

If equality holds in (
17
), and if we let
v_{0}\in S\setminus S_{\partial}
be any vector achieving the minimum in the left-hand side of (
17
), then the function
\langle v_{0},u\rangle
is constant.

Theorem
A.2
readily follows from Lemma
A.3
below, whose proof we defer to the next subsection.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
X,A,u
be as in Theorem
A.2
. Then, if
v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are such that
v_{0}
is minimally
A^{*}
-weaker than
(v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{k})
, then

(18)

\displaystyle\min_{X}\langle v_{0},u\rangle\geq\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\min_{X}\langle v_{i},u\rangle.

Moreover, if equality holds in (
18
), then
\min_{X}\langle v_{0},u\rangle\geq\min_{X}\langle v_{j},u\rangle
for all
0\leq j\leq k
and
\langle v_{0},u\rangle
is constant.

We now show Theorem
A.2
assuming Lemma
A.3
. For any
v\in S
, define
b_{v}=\min_{X}\langle v,u\rangle
, and set
b_{S}=\min_{v\in S}b_{v}
and
b_{S_{\partial}}=\min_{v\in S_{\partial}}b_{v}
. Note that clearly
b_{S}\leq b_{S_{\partial}}
, and we need to show equality.

Claim A.4
.

If
v\in S\setminus S_{\partial}
is such that
b_{v}=b_{S}
, then
b_{w}=b_{S}
for all
w\in N_{\Gamma}^{+}(v)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.3
, we have

\displaystyle b_{v}\geq\min_{w\in N^{+}_{\Gamma}(v)}b_{w}.

Since
b_{v}=b_{S}\leq b_{w}
for all
w\in S
, we see that
b_{v}=\min_{w\in N_{\Gamma}^{+}(v)}b_{w}
. Hence equality holds in (
18
) from Lemma
A.3
, and therefore
b_{w}\leq b_{v}=b_{S}
for all
w\in N_{\Gamma}^{+}(v)
, as desired. However. by definition we have
b_{S}\leq b_{w}
, so
b_{w}=b_{S}
for
w\in N_{\Gamma}^{+}(v)
.
∎

Returning to the theorem, let
v_{0}\in S
be such that
b_{S}=b_{v_{0}}
. We split the proof into two cases.

(1)

If
v_{0}\in S_{\partial}
, then
b_{S}=b_{S_{\partial}}
and there is nothing to prove.

(2)

If
v_{0}\not\in S_{\partial}
, by assumption (
2
) in Theorem
A.2
, there exists a sequence
v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{k}\in S\setminus S_{\partial}
and
v_{k+1}\in S_{\partial}
, such that
v_{i+1}\in N_{\Gamma}^{+}(v_{i})
for
i=0,1,2,...,{k}
. By induction and Claim
A.4
, we see that

\displaystyle b_{S}=b_{v_{0}}=b_{v_{1}}=...=b_{v_{k+1}}\geq b_{S_{\partial}},

and hence
b_{S}=b_{S_{\partial}}
, as desired.

Suppose now that
b_{v_{0}}=b_{S_{\partial}}
, i.e., that equality holds in (
17
). Then we have
b_{v_{0}}=\min_{w\in N_{\Gamma}^{+}(v_{0})}b_{w}
, so by the equality case of Lemma
A.3
, it follows that
\langle v_{0},u\rangle
is constant.

A.2.
Proof of Lemma
A.3

Proof of Lemma
A.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\Delta\langle v_{0},u\rangle=\langle v_{0},Au\rangle=\langle A^{*}v_{0},u\rangle.

By assumption, there exist smooth functions
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle A^{*}v_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(v_{0}-v_{i}).

Thus

\displaystyle\Delta\langle v_{0},u\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\langle v_{0}-v_{i},u\rangle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\langle v_{0},u\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}}\langle v_{i},u\rangle\right)

(19)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\langle v_{0},u\rangle-\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\langle v_{j},u\rangle\right)

(20)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\langle v_{0},u\rangle-\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\min_{X}\langle v_{j},u\rangle\right).

Since no
\lambda_{i}
vanishes identically, neither does
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}
. Applying the maximum principle to the function
\langle v_{0},u\rangle-\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\min_{X}\langle v_{i},u\rangle
, we see that it is non-negative, and (
18
) is shown.

Assume now that we have equality in (
18
). By the strong maximum principle
[
19
, Theorem 3.5, pp. 35]
,
\langle v_{0},u\rangle\equiv\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\min_{X}\langle v_{j},u\rangle
. Thus equality holds in both (
19
) and (
20
). Since equality holds in (
20
), we have
\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\langle v_{j},u\rangle\equiv\min_{1\leq j\leq k}\min_{X}\langle v_{j},u\rangle=\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}b
. Since equality holds in (
19
), we see that, for any
j\in\{1,2,...,k\}
,

\displaystyle\langle v_{j},u\rangle=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\langle v_{i},u\rangle=b\text{ on the open set }\lambda_{j}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

By minimality,
\lambda_{j}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is non-empty, so
\min_{X}\langle v_{j},u\rangle\leq b
.
∎

A.3.
Recovering Dai-Li’s maximum principle

By choosing suitable parameters for Theorem
A.2
, we prove the following.

Theorem A.5
.

Let
X
be a closed Riemannian manifold, and let
c_{ij}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth functions indexed by
1\leq i,j\leq n
, such that

(1)

(cooperative)
c_{ii}\geq 0
and
c_{ij}\leq 0
for
i\neq j
,

(2)

(column-diagonally dominant)
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{ij}\geq 0
for all
1\leq j\leq n
, and

(3)

(fully coupled) there is no partition
\{1,2,...,n\}=A\cup B
such that
c_{ij}=0
for
i\in A,j\in B
.

Suppose that
u_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,\dots,n
solve the system

\displaystyle\Delta u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{ij}u_{j}\text{ for }i=1,2,...,n.

Now suppose that for some constants
\nu_{i}>0
we have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nu_{i}u_{i}\geq 0
. Then either
u_{i}\equiv 0
for all
i
, or
u_{i}>0
for all
i
.

When
\nu_{i}=1
for all
i\in\{1,2,...,n\}
, this is case (3) of Theorem
4.4
.

The remainder of this subsection is devoted to the proof of Theorem
A.5
. Let
C
be the
n\times n
matrix with entries
c_{ij}
. For a set
A\subseteq\{1,2,...,n\}
, we denote by
e_{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the vector given by

\displaystyle(e_{A})_{i}=\left\{\begin{matrix}1&\text{ if }i\in A,\\
0&\text{ if }i\not\in A.\end{matrix}\right.

We will construct a graph
\Gamma
on the vertex set
S=\{e_{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}A\subseteq\{1,2,...,n\}\}\cup\{K\nu\}
, where
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},...,\nu_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and
K>\frac{1}{\min_{i}\nu_{i}}
. We will then apply Theorem
A.2
with
S_{\partial}=\{e_{0},K\nu\}=\{0,K\nu\}
.

Let
\bar{\Gamma}
be the graph defined as follows: for any proper subset
A\subset\{1,2,...,n\}
, we let

\displaystyle N_{\bar{\Gamma}}^{+}(e_{A})=\{e_{A\cup\{j\}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}j\not\in A\}\cup\{e_{A\setminus\{j\}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}j\in A\},

and

\displaystyle N_{\bar{\Gamma}}^{+}(e_{\{1,2,...,n\}})=\{e_{\{1,2,...,n\}\setminus\{j\}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}j\in\{1,2,...,n\}\}\cup\{K\nu\}.

Claim A.6
.

For any proper subset
A\subseteq\{1,2,...,n\}
, there exists a subset
\mathcal{N}_{A}\subset N_{\bar{\Gamma}}^{+}(e_{A})
, such that

(1)

the vector
e_{A}
is minimally
C^{*}
-weaker than
\mathcal{N}_{A}
, and

(2)

there exists some subset
B\subsetneq A
such that
e_{B}\in\mathcal{N}_{A}
.

Furthermore,
e_{\{1,2,...,n\}}
is minimally
C^{*}
-weaker than
N_{\bar{\Gamma}}^{+}(e_{\{1,2,...,n\}})
.

Proof.

We first show the claim for
e_{\{1,2,...,n\}}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\left(C^{*}e_{\{1,2,...,n\}}\right)_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_{ji}\geq 0.

Note that
e_{\{1,2,...,n\}}-K\nu<0
, so we can write

\displaystyle C^{*}e_{\{1,2,...,n\}}=(e_{\{1,2,...,n\}}-K\nu)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left(e_{\{1,2,...,n\}}-e_{\{1,2,...,n\}\setminus\{i\}}\right),

where
\lambda=K\nu-1+C^{*}e_{\{1,2,...,n\}}>0
. It follows immediately that
e_{\{1,2,...,n\}}
is minimally
C^{*}
-weaker than
N_{\bar{\Gamma}}^{+}(e_{\{1,2,...,n\}})
.

We now turn to other vertices of
\bar{\Gamma}
. Fix a proper subset
A\subset\{1,2,...,n\}
. Note that, since
C
is cooperative, we have

\displaystyle(C^{*}e_{A})_{i}=\sum_{j\in A}C_{ji}\leq 0\text{ for }i\not\in A.

Similarly, for
i\in A
, we have

(21)

\displaystyle(C^{*}e_{A})_{i}=\sum_{j\in A}C_{ji}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_{ji}-\sum_{j\not\in A}C_{ji}\geq 0.

Thus

\displaystyle C^{*}e_{A}=\sum_{i\in A}\lambda_{i}(e_{A}-e_{A\setminus\{i\}})+\sum_{i\not\in A}\lambda_{i}(e_{A}-e_{A\cup\{i\}}),

where

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\sum_{j\in A}C_{ji}&\text{ if }i\in A,\\
-\sum_{j\in A}C_{ji}&\text{ if }i\not\in A.\end{matrix}\right.

If we let
\mathcal{N}_{A}=\{A\cup\{i\}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}i\not\in A\text{ and }\lambda_{i}\not\equiv 0\}\cup\{A\setminus\{i\}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}i\in A\text{ and }\lambda_{i}\not\equiv 0\}
, then
e_{A}
is minimally
C^{*}
-weaker than
\mathcal{N}_{A}
, so (
1
) is shown.

It remains to check (
2
). Assume that (
2
) fails, i.e., that
\lambda_{i}\equiv 0
for all
i\in A
. Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in A}C_{ji}=0\text{ for }i\in A.

Then equality must hold in (
21
), so

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_{ji}=0\text{ and }\sum_{j\not\in A}C_{ji}=0\text{ for }i\in A.

In particular,
C_{ji}=0
for
i\in A,j\not\in A
. This is a contradiction as
C
is fully coupled, which shows (
2
).
∎

Now let
\Gamma
be the subgraph of
\bar{\Gamma}
such that
N_{\Gamma}^{+}(e_{A})=\mathcal{N}_{A}
for all subsets
A\subseteq\{1,2,...,n\}
. Assumption (
1
) in Theorem
A.2
holds by Claim
A.6
(
1
), and assumption (
2
) in Theorem
A.2
follows from Claim
A.6
(
2
). Therefore

\displaystyle u_{i}=\langle e_{\{i\}},u\rangle\geq\min_{v\in\{0,K\nu\}}\min_{X}\langle v,u\rangle=\min\left(0,\min_{X}K\langle\nu,u\rangle\right)=0.

Thus we have shown that
u_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
. It remains to check that either all
u_{i}
are positive, or they all vanish identically. This will follow from the next claim.

Claim A.7
.

(1)

If
u_{i}
vanishes somewhere, then
u_{i}\equiv 0
.

(2)

If
u_{i}\equiv 0
and
c_{ij}\not\equiv 0
for some
j
, then
u_{j}\equiv 0
.

Proof.

(1)

Suppose that
u_{i}
vanishes somewhere on
X
. Then equality is achieved in Theorem
A.2
for
e_{\{i\}}
. In particular, we see that
u_{i}
is constant, and hence
u_{i}\equiv 0
.

(2)

Suppose that
u_{i}\equiv 0
. Then
0=\Delta u_{i}=c_{ii}u_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}c_{ij}u_{j}=\sum_{j\neq i}c_{ij}u_{j}\leq 0
, since
c_{ij}u_{j}\leq 0
. Thus
c_{ij}u_{j}\equiv 0
for all
j\neq i
. If
c_{ij}\not\equiv 0
, then
u_{j}
vanishes somewhere on
X
, so by (1), we have
u_{j}\equiv 0
.

∎

Let
A
be the set of indices
i
such that
u_{i}
vanishes somewhere. Note that for any
i\in A,j\in\{1,2,...,n\}\setminus A
, we have
c_{ij}\equiv 0
by Claim
A.7
. Since the matrix
C
is fully coupled, it follows that
A
is either empty or
\{1,2,...,n\}
. In the former case, we have
u_{i}>0
for all
i
. In the latter case, we have
u_{i}\equiv 0
for all
i
by Claim
A.7
(2). This concludes the proof of Theorem
A.5
.

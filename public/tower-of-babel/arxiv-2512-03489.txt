Gan Yao

Abstract
. For 1 < p ≤ q < ∞ 1<p\leq q<\infty and n ∈ { 3 ⋅ 2 k , 2 k } n\in\{3\cdot 2^{k},2^{k}\} with k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , we prove that the Poisson-like semigroup ( P t ) t ∈ ℝ + (P_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}} on ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} , associated with the word length ψ n ​ ( k ) = min ⁡ ( k , n − k ) \psi_{n}(k)=\min(k,n-k) , is hypercontractive from L p L_{p} to L q L_{q} if and only if t ≥ 1 2 ​ log ⁡ ( q − 1 p − 1 ) t\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(\tfrac{q-1}{p-1}\big) . 1.
Introduction

The hypercontractivity of the Poisson-like semigroup on the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a long-standing open problem for all
n
except
n=2
and
n=4
. More precisely, by equipping
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
with the normalized counting measure
\mu_{n}
, the Poisson-like semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is defined as the family of maps
P_{t}:L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})\to L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})
, which acts on Fourier series by

P_{t}:\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}\chi_{k}(x)\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-t\psi_{n}(k)}a_{k}\chi_{k}(x),

where
\psi_{n}(k)=\min(k,n-k)
is the word-length function on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, and
\chi_{k}(x)=e^{\frac{2\pi ikx}{n}}\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
. The hypercontractivity problem asks for the optimal time
t_{p,q}
with
1<p\leq q<\infty
such that

\lVert P_{t}f\rVert_{q}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{p}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq t_{p,q}.

For the case
n=2
, the optimal time
t_{p,q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(\tfrac{q-1}{p-1}\big)
follows by applying the classical two-point inequality. That inequality was first proved by Bonami
[
Bon70
]
, later rediscovered by Gross
[
Gro75a
]
, and was also used by Beckner
[
Bec75
]
to obtain the best constants for the Hausdorff–Young inequality. For
n=4
, Beckner, Janson, and Jerison
[
BJJ83
]
obtained the same optimal time
t_{p,q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(\tfrac{q-1}{p-1}\big)
by a clever reduction from
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
to
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, though the approach does not extend to other
\mathbb{Z}_{m\times n}
. For
n=3
, by Wolff’s reduction
[
Wol07
, Corollary 3.1]
, the optimal time
t_{2,q}
of
P_{t}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
coincides the optimal time

t_{2,q}=\frac{1}{2}\log(\frac{\frac{2}{3}(\frac{1}{3})^{\frac{2}{q}-1}-\frac{1}{3}(\frac{2}{3})^{\frac{2}{q}-1}}{(\frac{2}{3})^{\frac{2}{q}}-(\frac{1}{3})^{\frac{2}{q}}})

of the simple semigroup
T_{t}=e^{-t(\mathrm{id}-\mathbb{E}_{\delta})}
on the weighted two-point space
L_{\infty}(\{-1,1\},\tfrac{1}{3}\delta_{-1}+\tfrac{2}{3}\delta_{1})
, which was computed by Oleszkiewicz and Latała
[
LaO00
,
Ole03
]
. For the general optimal time
t_{p,q}
of
(P_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, only asymptotic information is available; see
[
Wol07
, Theorem 2.1]
. For
n=5
, Andersson
[
And02
]
determined
t_{2,q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\log(q-1)
for
q\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. For
n\geq 6
, Junge, Palazuelos, Parcet, and Perrin
[
JPPP17
]
proved partial results: for even
n
,
t_{2,q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\log(q-1)
for
q\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}
; for odd
n
, the same holds when
n\geq q
, via a rather involved combinatorial method. Combining Stein’s interpolation method
[
Ste56
]
with Gross’s extrapolation technique
[
Gro75a
,
Gro75
,
Gro06
]
, they further obtained
t_{p,q}\leq\tfrac{\log 3}{2}\log\big(\frac{q-1}{p-1}\big)
in those regimes.

Hypercontractivity is also widely studied for semigroups on manifolds. Among the most classical examples, Weissler
[
Wei80
]
proved optimal hypercontractivity for the heat and Poisson semigroups on the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, while Rothaus
[
Rot80
]
independently treated the heat case. For the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
), Mueller and Weissler
[
MW82
]
established optimal hypercontractivity for the heat semigroup. For Poisson-type semigroups on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, see Janson
[
Jan83
]
, Beckner
[
Bec92
]
, and Frank–Ivanisvili
[
FI21
]
.

Now we present our main result for the Poisson-like semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Note that the standard argument, based on a simple series expansion of
\|P_{t}(1+\epsilon f)\|_{q}
and
\|1+\epsilon f\|_{p}
at
\epsilon=0
, shows the universal lower bound
t_{p,q}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\log\big(\tfrac{q-1}{p-1}\big)
. The theorem below records the optimal times
t_{p,q}
along a dyadic tower of
n
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
n=3\cdot 2^{k}
and
n=2^{k}
with
k\geq 1
, we have

\lVert P_{t}f\rVert_{q}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{p}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad t\geq\frac{1}{2}\log(\frac{q-1}{p-1})

for
1<p\leq q<\infty
.

A standard route to hypercontractivity proceeds through Log–Sobolev inequalities (LSI) by Gross’s celebrated work
[
Gro75a
]
:
\lVert P_{t}f\rVert_{q}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{p}
holds whenever
t\geq\frac{C}{4}\log\big(\frac{q-1}{p-1}\big)
if and only if the corresponding LSI holds with constant
C
. Thus, to prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to establish the following
n
–LSI with the optimal constant
2
along the above dyadic tower of
n
. Denote by
A_{\psi_{n}}
the generator of semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, that is

(1.1)

A_{\psi_{n}}:\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}\chi_{k}(x)\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{n}(k)a_{k}\chi_{k}(x).

The case
n=4
in the following theorem is due to the work
[
BJJ83
]
and Gross’s extrapolation technique
[
Gro75a
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
n=3\cdot 2^{k}
and
n=2^{k}
with
k\geq 1
, we have the following LSI with the optimal constant
2
:

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}f^{2}\log f^{2}\differential\mu_{n}-\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\log\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq 2\langle f,A_{\psi_{n}}f\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})},\qquad f\in L_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n}).

Our proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on a new induction scheme with three key ingredients:

(1)

Auxiliary weights
\phi_{4}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and
\phi_{6}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
together with their corresponding LSIs. These LSIs are tighter than those for the word-lengths
\psi_{n}
; this refinement is new even when
n=4
and plays a key role in the analysis of LSIs.

(2)

Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) analysis for the
4
– and
6
–LSI.
We develop an efficient way to handle LSIs on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
via KKT analysis, combined with the aforementioned manipulation of the length functions. The specific structure of
\phi_{6}
introduces a symmetry in the KKT system that makes the analysis tractable.

(3)

An induction from the
n
–LSI to the
2n
–LSI under a crucial compatibility condition. We use a Cooley–Tukey factorization of the
2n
-point discrete Fourier transform (DFT), which expresses a large DFT as a combination of smaller DFTs and yields a comparison of Dirichlet forms at the scales
n
and
2n
. The choice of the new weights
\phi_{4}
and
\phi_{6}
mentioned in (1) is crucial for the base steps
4\to 8
and
6\to 12
.

Finally, we remark that the above ideas are also useful for studying LSIs along other towers of the form
m\cdot n^{k}
. The KKT analysis and the compatibility condition vary in their technical details, depending on the specific value of
n
and on the particular choice of weights on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, and we will not carry out this analysis in this paper.

The article is organized as follows. After a brief introduction to the LSI formulation and the
n
-dimensional KKT framework in Section
2
, we analyze the KKT systems associated with the LSIs for
n=6
and
n=4
in Section
3
. Section
4
presents a comparison criterion for a pair of weights that allows us to compare the Dirichlet forms at the scales
n
and
2n
. We then apply it to the transition
n\to 2n
of LSIs and establish Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Fourier formulation of LSI on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and KKT framework

Let
F_{n}
denote the
n\times n
discrete Fourier transform (DFT) matrix

F_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&\cdots&1\\
1&\omega&\omega^{2}&\cdots&\omega^{n-1}\\
1&\omega^{2}&\omega^{4}&\cdots&\omega^{2(n-1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\omega^{n-1}&\omega^{2(n-1)}&\cdots&\omega^{(n-1)(n-1)}\end{pmatrix},

where
\omega=e^{2\pi i/n}
and, with
0
-indexed rows/columns,
(F_{n})_{j,k}=\omega^{jk}
for
0\leq j,k\leq n-1
. It is well known that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}F_{n}
is unitary (see, e.g.,
[
Dav79
, Section 2.5]
). For a column vector
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we write its DFT as

\hat{x}=(\hat{x}_{0},\ldots,\hat{x}_{n-1})^{\mathrm{T}}=F_{n}x,\quad\text{where}\quad\hat{x}_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_{j}\omega^{jk}.

Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, define the entropy functional on the vector
\lambda
by

\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda]\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}\log(\lambda_k^2)-\frac{\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}}{n}\log(\frac{\norm{\lambda}_2^2}{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}\log(\frac{n\lambda_k^2}{\norm{\lambda}_2^2}).

The functional
\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda]
is homogeneous of degree
2
, i.e.,
\mathrm{H}_{n}[\alpha\lambda]=\alpha^{2}\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda]
for
\alpha>0
.

The unitary
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}F_{n}
yields the following equivalence between the
n
–LSI and the
n
-variable entropy–Dirichlet form inequality used in the paper; throughout, “LSI” refers to either formulation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and write

\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}\big(\gamma(0),\gamma(1),\ldots,\gamma(n-1)\big)\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R}).

Define
A_{\gamma}:L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})\to L_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})
by

A_{\gamma}:\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}\chi_{k}(x)\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma(k)a_{k}\chi_{k}(x).

Then

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}f^{2}\log f^{2}\differential\mu_{n}-\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\log\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq 2\langle f,A_{\gamma}f\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})},\qquad f\in L_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n}),

is equivalent to

\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda]\leq 2\langle\lambda,\Gamma\lambda\rangle,\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the Hermitian inner product on
\ell_{2}^{n}
and
\Gamma=\frac{1}{n}F_{n}\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)F_{n}^{-1}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. In particular, if
\gamma(k)=\gamma(n-k)
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
, then
\Gamma
is real symmetric.

Proof.

Write
f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}\chi_{k}(x)
and set
a_{f}\coloneqq(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1})^{\mathrm{T}}
,
\lambda\coloneqq\big(f(0),\ldots,f(n-1)\big)^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. The verification of
\langle f,A_{\gamma}f\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\mu_{n})}=\langle\lambda,\Gamma\lambda\rangle
is straightforward by the fact the
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}F_{n}
is unitary and
\lambda=F_{n}a_{f}
.
Moreover, for
\lambda=\big(f(0),\ldots,f(n-1)\big)^{\mathrm{T}}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}f^{2}\log f^{2}\differential\mu_{n}-\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\log\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}\log(\lambda_k^2)-\frac{\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}}{n}\log(\frac{\norm{\lambda}_2^2}{n})=\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda].

Now we prove that
\Gamma
is real symmetric if
\gamma(k)=\gamma(n-k)
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
. We write
A^{\mathrm{H}}\coloneqq\overline{A}^{\mathrm{T}}
for the Hermitian (conjugate) transpose of a complex matrix
A
. Since
\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and
F_{n}^{-1}=\tfrac{1}{n}F_{n}^{\mathrm{H}}
, we have

\left(F_{n}\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)F_{n}^{-1}\right)^{\mathrm{H}}=(F_{n}^{-1})^{\mathrm{H}}\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)F_{n}^{\mathrm{H}}=F_{n}\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)F_{n}^{-1},

hence
\Gamma=\tfrac{1}{n}F_{n}\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)F_{n}^{-1}
is Hermitian.

Let
P
denote the permutation matrix that interchanges the entries
k
and
n-k
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
. If
\gamma(k)=\gamma(n-k)
for all such
k
, then
\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)
is invariant under conjugation by
P
. Because
\overline{F_{n}}=F_{n}^{\mathrm{H}}=F_{n}P
and
P=P^{-1}=P^{\mathrm{T}}
, it follows that

\overline{F_{n}\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)F_{n}^{-1}}=\overline{F_{n}}\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)\overline{F_{n}^{-1}}=F_{n}P\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)PF_{n}^{-1}=F_{n}\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)F_{n}^{-1}.

Hence
\Gamma\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and is symmetric, completing the proof.
∎

Homogeneous objective and reduction to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.

Given a length function
\psi_{n}
, define

f_{\psi_{n}}(\lambda)\coloneqq 2\langle\lambda,\Psi(n)\lambda\rangle-\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda],

where
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})^{\mathrm{T}}
,
\Psi(n)=\frac{1}{n}F_{n}\operatorname{diag}(\psi_{n})F_{n}^{-1}
, and we set
x\log(x^2)=0
at
x=0
. Since
f_{\psi_{n}}
is homogeneous of degree
2
, it suffices to verify
f_{\psi_{n}}\geq 0
on the positive sphere

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\coloneqq\Big\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}:\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}=1\Big\}

in order to conclude
f_{\psi_{n}}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. We therefore restrict
f_{\psi_{n}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
and analyze stationary points via the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions (see the original sources
[
Kar39
,
KT51
]
and textbook treatments
[
Ber16
,
NW06
]
). In brief, the KKT conditions provide necessary first-order conditions for constrained optimization problems with both equality and inequality constraints, extending the classical Lagrange multiplier method by introducing one multiplier for each active inequality constraint, together with the complementary slackness.

Proposition 2.2

(KKT necessary conditions
[
Ber16
, Proposition 4.3.1]
,
[
NW06
, Theorem 12.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{I}
be finite index sets,
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
c_{i}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
i\in\mathcal{E}\cup\mathcal{I}
. Suppose that
\lambda^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a local minimizer of
g(\lambda)
subject to the constraints

\begin{cases}c_{i}(\lambda)=0,\qquad i\in\mathcal{E},\\
c_{i}(\lambda)\geq 0,\qquad i\in\mathcal{I},\end{cases}

Define the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu,\nu)=g(\lambda)-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{E}}\mu_{i}c_{i}(\lambda)-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\nu_{i}c_{i}(\lambda),\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert\mathcal{E}\rvert},\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert\mathcal{I}\rvert}.

If
\lambda^{*}
is regular, i.e., the vector set

\{\gradient c_{i}(\lambda^{*})\}_{i\in\mathcal{E}}\cup\{\gradient c_{i}(\lambda^{*})\}_{i\in A(\lambda^{*})}

are linearly independent, where
A(\lambda^{*})\coloneqq\{i\in\mathcal{I}:c_{i}(\lambda^{*})=0\}
, then there exist unique Lagrange multiplier vectors
\mu^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert\mathcal{E}\rvert}
,
\nu^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert\mathcal{I}\rvert}
such that

\begin{cases}\gradient_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}(\lambda^{*},\mu^{*},\nu^{*})=0,\\
c_{i}(\lambda^{*})=0,\qquad i\in\mathcal{E},\\
c_{i}(\lambda^{*})\geq 0,\qquad i\in\mathcal{I},\\
\nu_{i}^{*}c_{i}(\lambda^{*})=0,\qquad i\in\mathcal{I},\\
\nu_{i}^{*}\geq 0,\qquad i\in\mathcal{I}.\end{cases}

We now apply Proposition
2.2
to the minimization of the particular entropy functional relevant to our problem on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
. We can absorb the multiplier
\mu
into a normalization.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Q\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a symmetric matrix and set

g(\lambda)=2\langle\lambda,Q\lambda\rangle-\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda].

If the system

(2.1)

\begin{cases}4Q\lambda-\dfrac{4}{n}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{0}\log(\lambda_0)\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{n-1}\log(\lambda_{n-1})\end{pmatrix}-\nu=0,\\
0<\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}^{2}<n,\\
\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1,\\
\lambda_{j}\nu_{j}=0,\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1,\\
\nu_{j}\geq 0,\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1,\end{cases}

has no solution, then
g\geq 0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.

Proof.

Consider the Lagrangian associated with the minimization of
g
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
:

\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu,\nu)=g(\lambda)-\mu\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}-1\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\nu_{k}\lambda_{k}

with multipliers
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
associated to the constraints
\lVert\lambda\rVert_{2}=1
and
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
. Notice the set

\{2\lambda\}\cup\{e_{j}\}_{j\in A(\lambda)}

are linear independent, where
(e_{j})_{0\leq j\leq n-1}
denotes the canonical basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
A(\lambda)=\{j:\lambda_{j}=0\}
. Hence, by Proposition
2.2
, for any local minimizer
\lambda^{*}
, there exist unique
\mu^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\nu^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
such that the following system holds:

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\gradient_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}=4Q\lambda^{*}-\dfrac{2}{n}\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda^{*}_{0}\log(\lambda^*_0)+\lambda^{*}_{0}\\
\vdots\\
2\lambda^{*}_{n-1}\log(\lambda^*_{n-1})+\lambda^{*}_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}+\dfrac{2}{n}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^{*}_{0}\log(\frac{\norm{\lambda^*}_2^2}{n})+\lambda^{*}_{0}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda^{*}_{n-1}\log(\frac{\norm{\lambda^*}_2^2}{n})+\lambda^{*}_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}-2\mu^{*}\lambda^{*}-\nu^{*}=0,\\
\lVert\lambda^{*}\rVert_{2}^{2}=1,\\
\lambda^{*}_{j}\geq 0,\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1,\\
\lambda^{*}_{j}\nu^{*}_{j}=0,\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1,\\
\nu^{*}_{j}\geq 0,\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-1.\end{cases}

Write

\displaystyle g(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu,\nu)+\mu\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}-1\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\nu_{k}\lambda_{k}.

Using Euler’s theorem for homogeneous functions of degree
2
(namely
\langle\gradient g(\lambda^{*}),\lambda^{*}\rangle=2g(\lambda^{*})
), together with

\gradient_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}(\lambda^{*},\mu^{*},\nu^{*})=0
,
\lVert\lambda^{*}\rVert_{2}^{2}=1
, and the fourth line of (
2.2
), we obtain

2g(\lambda^{*})=2\mu^{*}.

Assume that there exists a stationary point
\lambda^{*}
with
g(\lambda^{*})<0
, then
\mu^{*}=g(\lambda^{*})<0
. Let
c^{*}=e^{\frac{n\mu^{*}+\log n}{2}}<\sqrt{n}
. So we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=4Q(c^{*}\lambda^{*})-\frac{4}{n}\begin{pmatrix}c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{0}\log(c^*\lambda^*_0)-c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{0}\log c^{*}\\
\vdots\\
c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{n-1}\log(c^*\lambda^*_{n-1})-c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{n-1}\log c^{*}\end{pmatrix}-\frac{2}{n}c^{*}\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{n}\begin{pmatrix}c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{0}\log(\frac{1}{n})+c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{0}\\
\vdots\\
c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{n-1}\log(\frac{1}{n})+c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}-2\mu^{*}c^{*}\lambda^{*}-c^{*}\nu^{*}

\displaystyle=4Q(c^{*}\lambda^{*})-\frac{4}{n}\begin{pmatrix}c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{0}\log(c^*\lambda^*_0)\\
\vdots\\
c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{n-1}\log(c^*\lambda^*_{n-1})\end{pmatrix}+\Big(\frac{4}{n}\log c^{*}-\frac{2}{n}+\frac{2}{n}(-\log(n)+1)-2\mu^{*}\Big)c^{*}\lambda^{*}-c^{*}\nu^{*}

\displaystyle=4Q(c^{*}\lambda^{*})-\frac{4}{n}\begin{pmatrix}c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{0}\log(c^*\lambda^*_0)\\
\vdots\\
c^{*}\lambda^{*}_{n-1}\log(c^*\lambda^*_{n-1})\end{pmatrix}-c^{*}\nu^{*},

and
0<\lVert c^{*}\lambda^{*}\rVert_{2}^{2}<n
. Thus
(c^{*}\lambda^{*},c^{*}\nu^{*})
solves (
2.1
), contradicting the assumption. Therefore no such
\lambda^{*}
exists and
g\geq 0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.
∎

3.
LSI on
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
with modified weights

Building on the KKT framework from Section
2
, we now establish the LSI on
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
with tighter estimates. To this end, we introduce suitably modified weight functions, which are crucial both for making the resulting KKT systems tractable and for ensuring that the induction procedure in the next section applies.

Theorem 3.1
.

For the weight function

\phi_{4}(j)=\begin{cases}\psi_{4}(j),&j\neq 2,\\
\frac{8}{5},&j=2,\end{cases}

on
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{4}}f^{2}\log f^{2}\differential\mu_{4}-\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\log\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq 2\langle f,A_{\phi_{4}}f\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{4},\mu_{4})}

for all
f\in L_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Z}_{4},\mu_{4})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For the weight function

\phi_{6}(j)=\begin{cases}\psi_{6}(j),&j\neq 3,\\

\begin{cases}-\lambda_{j_{0}}\log(\lambda_{j_0})+\tfrac{9}{10}\lambda_{j_{0}}-\tfrac{1}{10}\lambda_{j_{0}+2}-\tfrac{4}{5}\lambda_{\ell}=0,\\
-\lambda_{j_{0}+2}\log(\lambda_{j_0+2})+\tfrac{9}{10}\lambda_{j_{0}+2}-\tfrac{1}{10}\lambda_{j_{0}}-\tfrac{4}{5}\lambda_{\ell}=0,\\
-\lambda_{\ell}\log(\lambda_{\ell})+\tfrac{4}{5}\lambda_{\ell}-\tfrac{2}{5}(\lambda_{j_{0}}+\lambda_{j_{0}+2})=0,\\
0<2\lambda_{\ell}^{2}+\lambda_{j_{0}}^{2}+\lambda_{j_{0}+2}^{2}<4,\\
\lambda_{j_{0}},\lambda_{j_{0}+2},\lambda_{\ell}>0.\end{cases}

Write
\lambda_{j_{0}+2}=r\lambda_{j_{0}}
with
r>1
. From the above system, eliminating
\log(\lambda_\ell)
like Step 3 in the proof of Lemma
3.4
yields the scalar identity
h(r)=0
with

h(x)=\log(4 x \log x-\tfrac{2}{5} (x^2-1))+\frac{-4x^{2}+8x\log x+4}{x^{2}-10x\log x-1}-\log(\tfrac{16 (x-1)}{5})-\frac{x\log x}{x-1}+1,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

where the values of
h
and
h^{\prime}
at
x=1
are understood by continuous extension
h(1)=h^{\prime}(1)=0
. As in Step 4 in the proof of Lemma
3.4
, we introduce the auxiliary functions

\displaystyle h_{1}(x)=(x-1)^{2}(4x\log(x)-\tfrac{2}{5}(x^{2}-1))^{2}h^{\prime}(x),\quad h_{2}(x)=xh_{1}^{\prime}(x),\quad h_{3}(x)=xh_{2}^{\prime}(x),\quad h_{3}(x)=xh_{2}^{\prime\prime}(x),

\displaystyle h_{4}(x)=xh_{3}^{\prime}(x),\quad h_{5}(x)=xh_{4}^{\prime\prime}(x),\quad h_{6}(x)=xh_{5}^{\prime}(x),\quad h_{7}(x)=x^{2}h_{6}^{\prime\prime}(x),\quad h_{8}(x)=xh_{7}^{\prime\prime}(x),

and
h_{8}
is positive on
[1,+\infty)
, so inductively we can derive
h^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x>1
, hence
h(x)>0
for
x>1
, a contradiction. Therefore, (
3.13
) has no solution in either regime. By Lemma
2.3
, we conclude that
f_{\phi_{4}}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}
, completing the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

4.
Dyadic induction to optimal LSI on
\mathbb{Z}_{6\cdot 2^{k}}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{8\cdot 2^{k}}

To pass from the
n
–LSI to the
2n
–LSI, we compare the Dirichlet forms associated with the symbols
\gamma_{n}
and
\gamma_{2n}
via the Cooley–Tukey factorization of the
2n
-point DFT into two
n
-point DFTs
[
CT65
]
. We start with the following proposition, which provides the key comparison between the
n
– and
2n
–level Dirichlet forms under appropriate compatibility assumptions on
\gamma_{n}
and
\gamma_{2n}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer. Let
\gamma_{n}:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a function with associated matrix

\Gamma(n)=\frac{1}{n}F_{n}\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_{n})F_{n}^{-1}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

For
\lambda=(a_{0},b_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1},b_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2n}
set
a=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and
b=(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. Assume
\gamma_{n}
and
\gamma_{2n}
satisfy

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\gamma_{n}(0)=\gamma_{2n}(0)=0,\\
\gamma_{n}(k)=\gamma_{n}(n-k),\qquad 1\leq k\leq n-1,\\
\gamma_{2n}(k)=\gamma_{2n}(2n-k),\qquad 1\leq k\leq 2n-1,\\
\gamma_{2n}(k)\geq\gamma_{n}(k),\qquad 1\leq k\leq[\frac{n-1}{2}],\\
\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)-1\geq 0,\qquad 0\leq k\leq[\frac{n-1}{2}].\end{cases}

Then the following inequality holds:

(4.2)

\langle a,\Gamma(n)a\rangle+\langle b,\Gamma(n)b\rangle+\frac{1}{2n}\big(\lVert a\rVert_{2}-\lVert b\rVert_{2}\big)^{2}\leq 2\langle\lambda,\Gamma(2n)\lambda\rangle,

provided the following inequality holds for all
x\geq 0
and
0\leq r_{a},r_{b}\leq 1
:

(4.3)

\begin{cases}\bigl(2\gamma_{2n}(\tfrac{n}{2})-2\gamma_{n}(\tfrac{n}{2})-1\bigr)\bigl(r_{a}+r_{b}x^{2}\bigr)+\gamma_{2n}(n)(1-x)^{2}-(1+x^{2})+2x\sqrt{(1+r_{a})(1+r_{b})}\geq 0,&\text{if $n$ is even},\\
\gamma_{2n}(n)(1-x)^{2}-(1+x^{2})+2x\geq 0,&\text{if $n$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We first prove the theorem for even
n
. The condition (
4.1
) becomes

(4.4)

\begin{cases}\gamma_{n}(0)=\gamma_{2n}(0)=0,\\
\gamma_{n}(k)=\gamma_{n}(n-k),\qquad 1\leq k\leq n-1,\\
\gamma_{2n}(k)=\gamma_{2n}(2n-k),\qquad 1\leq k\leq 2n-1,\\
\gamma_{2n}(k)\geq\gamma_{n}(k),\qquad 1\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}-1,\\
\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)-1\geq 0,\qquad 0\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}-1.\end{cases}

Write
\lambda=(a_{0},b_{0},a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1},b_{n-1})
. The DFTs
\hat{\lambda}=F_{2n}\lambda
,
\hat{a}=F_{n}a=(\hat{a}_{0},\ldots,\hat{a}_{n-1})
and
\hat{b}=F_{n}b=(\hat{b}_{0},\ldots,\hat{b}_{n-1})
satisfy

\hat{\lambda}_{k}=\hat{a}_{k}+e^{-\frac{2\pi ik}{2n}}\hat{b}_{k},\qquad\hat{\lambda}_{n+k}=\hat{a}_{k}-e^{-\frac{2\pi ik}{2n}}\hat{b}_{k},

for
k=0,\ldots,n-1
. Set

D=\operatorname{diag}\big(1,e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{2n}},e^{-\frac{4\pi i}{2n}},\ldots,e^{-\frac{2(n-1)\pi i}{2n}}\big),

so that

\hat{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{a}+D\hat{b}\\
\hat{a}-D\hat{b}\end{pmatrix}.

Using that
\big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{m}}F_{m}\big)^{2}
is a permutation matrix which reverses indices
k\leftrightarrow m-k
(with
0
fixed) for
1\leq k\leq m-1
, we have

\frac{1}{2n}F_{2n}\Gamma(2n)F_{2n}^{-1}=\frac{1}{(2n)^{2}}F_{2n}^{2}\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_{2n})F_{2n}^{-2}=\operatorname{diag}\Big(0,\tfrac{\gamma_{2n}(1)}{(2n)^{2}},\ldots,\tfrac{\gamma_{2n}(2n-1)}{(2n)^{2}}\Big)=\begin{pmatrix}M_{1}&0\\
0&M_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
M_{1},M_{2}
are
n\times n
diagonal matrices for the frequency blocks
\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
and
\{n,\ldots,2n-1\}
, respectively. Therefore

\displaystyle 2\langle\lambda,\Gamma(2n)\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}F_{2n}\lambda,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}F_{2n}\Gamma(2n)\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle\begin{pmatrix}\hat{a}+D\hat{b}\\
\hat{a}-D\hat{b}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}M_{1}&0\\
0&M_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{a}+D\hat{b}\\
\hat{a}-D\hat{b}\end{pmatrix}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{a},4M_{1}\hat{a}\rangle+\langle\hat{b},4M_{1}\hat{b}\rangle+\langle\hat{a}-D\hat{b},2(M_{2}-M_{1})(\hat{a}-D\hat{b})\rangle.

Here

\displaystyle 4M_{1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{diag}\Big(0,\tfrac{\gamma_{2n}(1)}{n^{2}},\ldots,\tfrac{\gamma_{2n}(n-1)}{n^{2}}\Big),

\displaystyle 2(M_{2}-M_{1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{diag}\Big(\tfrac{\gamma_{2n}(n)}{2n^{2}},\tfrac{\gamma_{2n}(n+1)-\gamma_{2n}(1)}{2n^{2}},\ldots,\tfrac{\gamma_{2n}(2n-1)-\gamma_{2n}(n-1)}{2n^{2}}\Big).

Recall that we write
\hat{a}=(\hat{a}_{0},\ldots,\hat{a}_{n-1})
and
\hat{b}=(\hat{b}_{0},\ldots,\hat{b}_{n-1})
. For real vectors
a,b
we have the conjugate symmetries

\hat{a}_{k}=\overline{\hat{a}_{n-k}},\qquad(D\hat{b})_{k}=e^{-\frac{2\pi ik}{2n}}\hat{b}_{k}=-\overline{(D\hat{b})_{n-k}},

so

\langle\hat{a},4M_{1}\hat{a}\rangle+\langle\hat{b},4M_{1}\hat{b}\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(k)+\gamma_{2n}(n-k)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)+\frac{\gamma_{2n}(\frac{n}{2})}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big).

Using
\gamma_{2n}(k)=\gamma_{2n}(2n-k)
and pairing
k
with
n-k
,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{a}-D\hat{b},2(M_{2}-M_{1})(\hat{a}-D\hat{b})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n)}{2n^{2}}\lvert\hat{a}_{0}-\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n+k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)}{2n^{2}}\lvert\hat{a}_{k}-(D\hat{b})_{k}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(2n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(n-k)}{2n^{2}}\lvert\hat{a}_{n-k}-(D\hat{b})_{n-k}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n)}{2n^{2}}\lvert\hat{a}_{0}-\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)}{2n^{2}}\Big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}-(D\hat{b})_{k}\rvert^{2}-\lvert\hat{a}_{k}+(D\hat{b})_{k}\rvert^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n)}{2n^{2}}\lvert\hat{a}_{0}-\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{2\big(\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)\big)}{n^{2}}\real(\overline{\hat{a}_{k}}(D\hat{b})_{k}\big),

where we have used
\lvert\hat{a}_{n-k}-(D\hat{b})_{n-k}\rvert^{2}=\lvert\hat{a}_{k}+(D\hat{b})_{k}\rvert^{2}
and the identity
\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}-\lvert x+y\rvert^{2}=-4\real(\overline{x}y)
.

Hence, on the frequency side,

(4.5)

\displaystyle 2\langle\lambda,\Gamma(2n)\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(k)+\gamma_{2n}(n-k)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)+\frac{\gamma_{2n}(\tfrac{n}{2})}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n)}{2n^{2}}\lvert\hat{a}_{0}-\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{2\big(\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)\big)}{n^{2}}\real(\overline{\hat{a}_{k}}(D\hat{b})_{k}\big).

Similarly, using
\gamma_{n}(k)=\gamma_{n}(n-k)
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\langle a,\Gamma(n)a\rangle+\langle b,\Gamma(n)b\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\gamma_{n}(k)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{n}(k)+\gamma_{n}(n-k)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)+\frac{\gamma_{n}(\tfrac{n}{2})}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{2\gamma_{n}(k)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)+\frac{\gamma_{n}(\tfrac{n}{2})}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big).

Therefore, combining (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), (
4.2
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{2\gamma_{n}(k)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)+\frac{\gamma_{n}(\frac{n}{2})}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)+\frac{1}{2n}\big(\lVert a\rVert_{2}-\lVert b\rVert_{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(k)+\gamma_{2n}(n-k)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)-\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{2\big(\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)\big)}{n^{2}}\real(\overline{\hat{a}_{k}}(D\hat{b})_{k}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{2n}(\frac{n}{2})}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)+\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n)}{2n^{2}}\lvert\hat{a}_{0}-\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}.

Using
\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}=\lvert x\rvert^{2}+\lvert y\rvert^{2}-2\real(\overline{x}y)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(k)+\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-2\gamma_{n}(k)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)-\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{2\big(\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)\big)}{n^{2}}\real(\overline{\hat{a}_{k}}(D\hat{b})_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{2\big(\gamma_{2n}(k)-\gamma_{n}(k)\big)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)+\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)}{n^{2}}\lvert\hat{a}_{k}-(D\hat{b})_{k}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{2\big(\gamma_{2n}(k)-\gamma_{n}(k)\big)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)+\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert-\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert\big)^{2},

where the last step uses the assumption
\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)\geq 1
in (
4.1
). Therefore, to prove (
4.2
), it suffices to show

(4.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\big(\lVert a\rVert_{2}-\lVert b\rVert_{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{2\big(\gamma_{2n}(k)-\gamma_{n}(k)\big)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)+\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert-\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert\big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{2n}(\frac{n}{2})-\gamma_{n}(\frac{n}{2})}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)+\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n)}{2n^{2}}\lvert\hat{a}_{0}-\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}.

Now estimate
\big(\lVert a\rVert_{2}-\lVert b\rVert_{2}\big)^{2}
. Let

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\Big(\sqrt{\lvert\hat{a}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}},\sqrt{2}\lvert\hat{a}_{1}\rvert,\sqrt{2}\lvert\hat{a}_{2}\rvert,\ldots,\sqrt{2}\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\rvert\Big),

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\Big(\sqrt{\lvert\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}},\sqrt{2}\lvert\hat{b}_{1}\rvert,\sqrt{2}\lvert\hat{b}_{2}\rvert,\ldots,\sqrt{2}\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\rvert\Big),

so that
\lVert a\rVert_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\lVert u\rVert_{2}
and
\lVert b\rVert_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\lVert v\rVert_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\big(\lVert a\rVert_{2}-\lVert b\rVert_{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\big(\lVert u\rVert_{2}-\lVert v\rVert_{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\lVert u-v\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left(\Big(\sqrt{\lvert\hat{a}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}}-\sqrt{\lvert\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}}\Big)^{2}+2\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert-\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert\big)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left(\lvert\hat{a}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}-2\sqrt{\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)\big(\lvert\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)}+2\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert-\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert\big)^{2}\right).

Plugging this into (
4.7
), we see it is enough to prove

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{2\big(\gamma_{2n}(k)-\gamma_{n}(k)\big)}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert^{2}\big)+\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)-1}{n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{k}\rvert-\lvert\hat{b}_{k}\rvert\big)^{2}-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\gamma_{2n}(\frac{n}{2})-2\gamma_{n}(\frac{n}{2})-1}{2n^{2}}\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)+\frac{\gamma_{2n}(n)}{2n^{2}}\lvert\hat{a}_{0}-\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sqrt{\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)\big(\lvert\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)}\ \geq\ 0.

Under the pairwise conditions (
4.4
), the first two sums are nonnegative. Thus it suffices to verify

(4.9)

\displaystyle-\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}\big)+(2\gamma_{2n}(\tfrac{n}{2})-2\gamma_{n}(\tfrac{n}{2})-1)\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)+\gamma_{2n}(n)\lvert\hat{a}_{0}-\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sqrt{\big(\lvert\hat{a}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)\big(\lvert\hat{b}_{0}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert^{2}\big)}\ \geq\ 0.

Note that by definition,
\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}a_{j}
,
\hat{a}_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}
and similarly for
\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}
and
\hat{b}_{0}
. Since
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, we have

\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert\leq\hat{a}_{0},\qquad\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert\leq\hat{b}_{0}.

Write
\lvert\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert=\sqrt{r_{a}}\hat{a}_{0}
and
\lvert\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}\rvert=\sqrt{r_{b}}\hat{b}_{0}
with
0\leq r_{a},r_{b}\leq 1
. By symmetry in
\hat{a}_{0}
and
\hat{b}_{0}
in (
4.9
), assume
\hat{a}_{0}>0
and set
x=\hat{b}_{0}/\hat{a}_{0}\geq 0
. Then (
4.9
) becomes

\bigl(2\gamma_{2n}(\tfrac{n}{2})-2\gamma_{n}(\tfrac{n}{2})-1\bigr)\bigl(r_{a}+r_{b}x^{2}\bigr)+\gamma_{2n}(n)(1-x)^{2}-(1+x^{2})+2x\sqrt{(1+r_{a})(1+r_{b})}\geq 0,

which is exactly (
4.3
) if
n
is even.

The case where
n
is odd is handled by exactly the same computation. In this case the condition (
4.1
) becomes

\begin{cases}\gamma_{n}(0)=0,\\
\gamma_{n}(k)=\gamma_{n}(n-k),\qquad 1\leq k\leq n-1,\\
\gamma_{2n}(k)=\gamma_{2n}(2n-k),\qquad 1\leq k\leq 2n-1,\\
\gamma_{2n}(k)\geq\gamma_{n}(k),\qquad 1\leq k\leq\tfrac{n-1}{2},\\
\gamma_{2n}(n-k)-\gamma_{2n}(k)-1\geq 0,\qquad 0\leq k\leq\tfrac{n-1}{2}.\end{cases}

Since for odd
n
the middle frequencies
\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}
and
\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}
are absent in (
4.9
), setting
x=\hat{b}_{0}/\hat{a}_{0}\geq 0
and
\hat{a}_{\frac{n}{2}}=\hat{b}_{\frac{n}{2}}=0
, the condition (
4.9
) reduces to the scalar condition

\gamma_{2n}(n)(1-x)^{2}-(1+x^{2})+2x\geq 0,

which is (
4.3
) if
n
is odd. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Under the conditions (

) and (

–LSI by decomposing the entropy functional
\mathrm{H}_{2n}
for

, applying the

–LSI and the

to compare the resulting Dirichlet forms

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer. Let
\Gamma(n)
and
\Gamma(2n)
be the matrices corresponding to a length-function pair
(\gamma_{n},\gamma_{2n})
satisfying (
4.1
) and (

). If the

–LSI holds with Dirichlet form
\langle\lambda,\Gamma(n)\lambda\rangle
and constant
2
:

\mathrm{H}_{n}[\lambda]\leq 2\langle\lambda,\Gamma(n)\lambda\rangle\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},

then the
2n
–LSI holds with Dirichlet form
\langle\lambda,\Gamma(2n)\lambda\rangle
and the same constant
2
:

\mathrm{H}_{2n}[\lambda]\leq 2\langle\lambda,\Gamma(2n)\lambda\rangle\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2n}.

In addition, for odd
n_{0}\geq 3
, if the
n_{0}
–LSI holds for the word-length function
\psi_{n_{0}}
with constant
2
, then for every
m\geq 1

–LSI holds for
\psi_{n_{0}\cdot 2^{m}}
with constant
2
.

The last statement in the above theorem does not directly follow for even
n_{0}
. Indeed, the pair
(\psi_{n_{0}},\psi_{2n_{0}})
does not satisfy (
4.3
) in Proposition
4.1
. To overcome this, we will later introduce pairs of modified weight functions
(\gamma_{n},\gamma_{2n})

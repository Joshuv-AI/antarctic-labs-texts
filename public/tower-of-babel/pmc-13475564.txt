Free mycophenolic acid exposure for therapeutic drug monitoring in paediatric lupus nephritis: associations with treatment response and haematologic toxicity
Authors: Xuerui Tong, Jingxuan Lin, Lizhi Chen, Yutong Guo, Baojing Liu, Min Huang, Kejing Tang, Pan Chen, Xiaoyun Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the associations of free mycophenolic acid (f-MPA) concentration and total MPA (t-MPA) concentration with treatment response and haematologic toxicity and to determine whether f-MPA provides additional safety-related value for therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) in patients with paediatric lupus nephritis (LN) treated with mycophenolate mofetil (MMF).MethodsSixty-five patients with paediatric LN receiving MMF were prospectively enrolled. The t-MPA and f-MPA were measured simultaneously using validated ultrafiltration and LC-MS/MS. Full pharmacokinetic profiles were obtained over 12 hours, and area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) was calculated for t-MPA (t-MPA-AUC) and f-MPA (f-MPA-AUC). Univariate analysis identified factors influencing efficacy (relapse-free survival) and adverse drug reaction (ADR) over 12 months. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves established predictive thresholds and Kaplan-Meier methods analysed time-to-event.ResultsThe 12-month relapse-free rate was 78.7% (95% CI 66.6% to 90.9%). Both t-MPA-AUC and f-MPA-AUC significantly correlated with relapse-free survival (ROC AUC 0.70 each; optimal thresholds: t-MPA-AUC=31.63 µg·h/mL, f-MPA-AUC=354.45 ng·h/mL). The average remission duration above these thresholds was 10.88 and 11.00 months. The overall MMF-related ADR incidence was 25.2%, including 17 haematological events (26.2%). The t-MPA-AUC, f-MPA-AUC, glucose, β2-microglobulin and C3 were haematological ADR risk factors. The f-MPA-AUC independently predicted haematological ADRs (optimal threshold: 492.96 ng·h/mL; specificity 86.2%).ConclusionBoth t-MPA-AUC and f-MPA-AUC effectively predict MMF efficacy in paediatric LN. However, f-MPA-AUC demonstrates superior predictive value for safety outcomes, specifically haematological ADRs. This supports f-MPA as a potentially better TDM metric for optimising MMF therapy safety in this population.Keywords: Lupus Nephritis; Lupus Erythematosus, Systemic; Pharmacokinetics

Introduction

Pediatric-onset systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) frequently involves severe lupus nephritis (LN), exhibiting higher morbidity and aggressiveness than adult-onset disease.1–3 Approximately 8%–15% of patients with paediatric LN progress to advanced chronic kidney disease or end-stage renal disease,1 4 underscoring the critical need for early, optimised therapeutic intervention. Key predictors of poor long-term outcomes include severe initial presentation, inadequate induction response and disease recurrence.

Current LN treatment balances efficacy against glucocorticoid toxicity. While glucocorticoids modulate disease activity, chronic use drives significant morbidity and mortality through irreversible organ damage.5 6 Consequently, strategies prioritise minimising glucocorticoid exposure via adjunctive immunosuppressants like mycophenolate mofetil (MMF).7–9 However, MMF’s active metabolite, mycophenolic acid (MPA), exhibits high pharmacokinetic variability, with exposure levels directly correlating with treatment response. This necessitates therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) to individualise dosing.10 11 MPA is highly bound to plasma proteins, mainly albumin, with approximately 97% of circulating MPA being protein-bound. Its major metabolite, MPA glucuronide (MPAG), is also extensively protein-bound, at approximately 82%.12 Since only unbound MPA is pharmacologically active, changes in protein binding may alter free MPA (f-MPA) exposure even when total MPA (t-MPA) remain unchanged. Therefore, f-MPA may better reflect pharmacologically active exposure than t-MPA, particularly in patients with altered albumin levels, renal dysfunction or accumulation of MPAG. Despite TDM’s proven utility in transplant recipients,13 14 paediatric LN faces dual challenges: (1) limited high-quality evidence (small retrospective cohorts, few multi-centre studies and historical data from pre-MMF eras,15–23 and (2) guidelines reliant on adult data extrapolation,24 ignoring paediatric-specific factors like growth, disease progression and safety profiles.15

Our prior work established a t-MPA area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) target range of 31.19–50.8 µg·h/mL for paediatric LN patients treated with MMF.25 In patients with paediatric LN receiving multitarget therapy with tacrolimus plus MMF, two t-MPA AUC thresholds were proposed: ≥23.52 µg·h/mL for predicting renal remission and ≥28.87 µg·h/mL for predicting inactive SLE.26 Nevertheless, emerging evidence suggests f-MPA may better reflect pharmacologically active drug levels. Studies in transplant populations link f-MPA to efficacy/safety outcomes,27 28 yet no paediatric LN data exist.

This study, therefore, evaluated the associations of f-MPA-AUC and t-MPA-AUC with treatment response and haematologic toxicity in paediatric LN and explored whether f-MPA-AUC provides additional value, particularly for safety-oriented TDM. Establishing an f-MPA therapeutic window could refine TDM-guided MMF dosing, optimising outcomes while minimising haematologic toxicity.

Methods

Study design and participants

This prospective cohort study enrolled patients with paediatric LN aged ≤18 years at the First Affiliated Hospital, Sun Yat-sen University (September 2021–July 2023). All received MMF in combination with glucocorticoids and hydroxychloroquine as background therapy, with routine TDM of MPA.

Inclusion criteria:

Diagnosis of SLE per 2019 EULAR/ACR criteria and LN per 2016 Chinese Medical Association guidelines.

Stable MMF dosing (≥7 days) with MPA concentration monitoring.

Exclusion criteria:

Neonatal lupus.

Neonatal lupus.

Concomitant calcineurin inhibitor (cyclosporine or tacrolimus), proton pump inhibitor or active gastric ulcers.

Concomitant calcineurin inhibitor (cyclosporine or tacrolimus), proton pump inhibitor or active gastric ulcers.

Severe cardiopulmonary dysfunction, malignancies or incomplete pharmacokinetic sampling.

Use of biologic agents or monoclonal antibodies other than belimumab during the observation period.

Other clinician-determined contraindications.

Other clinician-determined contraindications.

Pharmacokinetic analysis

The t-MPA and f-MPA concentrations were simultaneously quantified using a previously established and validated high-performance liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry method developed by our group.29 Chromatographic separation was performed on a Hypersil GOLD C18 column (50×4.6 mm, 5 µm) maintained at 30°C, using 0.1% formic acid in water and acetonitrile (60:40, v/v) as the mobile phase at a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min. Detection was performed in positive electrospray ionisation mode with multiple reaction monitoring transitions of m/z 321.14 → 206.9 for MPA and m/z 324.15 → 209.9 for MPA-d3 as the internal standard. The total analytical run time was 7 min. The lower limits of quantification were 0.1 µg/mL for t-MPA and 0.0025 µg/mL for f-MPA, with calibration ranges of 0.1–50 µg/mL and 0.0025–0.5 µg/mL, respectively. Blood samples were collected at 10 min predose and 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2.5, 4, 6, 9 and 12 hours postdose. The AUC was calculated via non-compartmental analysis (Phoenix WinNonlin V.8.1).

Outcome definitions

Efficacy (per KDIGO 2024):

Complete remission (CR): urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio (UPCR) <50 mg/mmol or 24-hour urinary protein excretion adjusted for body surface area (24 hUP/BSA) <300 mg/m/24 hours.

Partial remission (PR): >50% UPCR reduction and UPCR <300 mg/mmol.

Treatment response: PR within 6 months and CR after 6 months.

Treatment failure: no response or renal relapse (UPCR >100 mg/mmol from baseline <50 mg/mmol).

Safety:

MMF-related haematologic adverse events (AEs): leucopenia (WBC≤4.0×10⁹/L), neutropenia (NEUT≤1.5×10⁹/L), thrombocytopenia (PLT≤100×10⁹/L), anaemia or lymphopenia (LYM≤1.0×10⁹/L), excluding lupus-induced cytopenia.

Haematologic AEs were selected as a clinically relevant safety endpoint because cytopenias, including leucopenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia and pancytopenia, are recognised risks associated with mycophenolate therapy and may require dose reduction or treatment interruption.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses utilised Phoenix WinNonlin (v8.1, Certara) for non-compartmental analysis (NCA) to calculate the AUC, IBM SPSS Statistics (v25.0, IBM Corp.) for data analysis and GraphPad Prism (v10.2) for graphics.

We hypothesised that f-MPA-AUC could differentiate paediatric LN patients responding to MPA treatment, defined by a receiver operating characteristic curve area under the curve (ROC-AUC)>0.5; based on literature indicating an ROC-AUC of~0.76,28 sample size estimation using PASS 15 determined a minimum total of 46 patients for a two-sided α=0.05, 90% power (1−β) and 1:1 allocation.

Patient demographics and clinical characteristics were described using mean±SD, median (IQR) or frequency (%). Normality (Shapiro-Wilk/Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests) and homogeneity of variance (Levene test) were assessed. Group comparisons employed independent samples t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for quantitative variables and Pearson’s chi-square tests for categorical variables.

For the analysis of haematologic adverse drug reactions (ADRs), candidate variables were selected based on clinical relevance and data availability, including demographic characteristics, LN pathological class, MMF dose, disease activity-related indicators, laboratory parameters, MPA exposure indices and concomitant treatment variables. Methylprednisolone pulse therapy and belimumab use were evaluated as concomitant treatment variables. Because all patients received glucocorticoids and hydroxychloroquine as background therapy, their use had no between-patient variability and was not entered as dichotomous covariates in the logistic regression model. Variables significant in univariate analyses for haematologic ADRs were included in logistic regression modelling, with model fit evaluated via the Hosmer-Lemeshow test. ROC curve analysis determined the optimal MPA-AUC cut-off for treatment response, and Kaplan-Meier analysis assessed remission duration and survival. All tests were two-sided, with p<0.05 considered statistically significant.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting or dissemination plans of our research.

Results

Population characteristics

The study prospectively enrolled 65 patients with paediatric LN (13 male, 52 female) receiving MMF induction therapy. Among 47 patients with 12-month efficacy data, median baseline Systemic Lupus Erythematosus Disease Activity Index (SLEDAI) was 6 (IQR 2–10), with 54.8% (23/42) exhibiting active disease (SLEDAI≥5). Primary clinical manifestations included rash (68.1%) and urinary abnormalities (66.0%). Renal biopsies (n=37) showed class IV LN as the predominant histology (32.4%). Univariate analysis of efficacy outcomes

At 12-month follow-up, 78.7% of patients (37/47; 95% CI 66.6% to 90.9%) achieved treatment response. Responders demonstrated significantly lower median UPCR (13.17 vs 59.49 mg/mmol; p<0.001) and 24 hUP/BSA (103.85 vs 216.35 mg/m²/24 hours; p=0.018) versus non-responders. Responders exhibited higher MPA exposure: median t-MPA-AUC (32.64 vs 28.45 µg·h/mL; p=0.040) and f-MPA-AUC (359.02 vs 283.64 ng·h/mL; p=0.030). No significant demographic or biochemical differences were observed between groups. Exposure thresholds for efficacy

ROC analysis revealed comparable predictive capacity for both parameters: t-MPA-AUC demonstrated AUC 0.70 (95% CI 0.50 to 0.89; p=0.04) with optimal threshold 31.63 µg·h/mL (sensitivity 62.2%, specificity 80.0%), while f-MPA-AUC showed AUC 0.70 (95% CI 0.51 to 0.90; p=0.03) with threshold 354.45 ng·h/mL (sensitivity 54.1%, specificity 90.0%) (figure 1C). Patients exceeding these thresholds had significantly higher response rates: 92.0% (23/25) vs 63.6% (14/22; p=0.03) for t-MPA-AUC >31.63 µg·h/mL, and 95.2% (20/21) vs 65.4% (17/26; p=0.015) for f-MPA-AUC >354.45 ng·h/mL. Kaplan-Meier analysis confirmed prolonged remission duration above thresholds: 10.88±0.76 vs 6.91±1.36 months (p=0.017) for t-MPA-AUC, and 11.00±0.94 vs 8.00±1.15 months (p<0.05) for f-MPA-AUC.

Factors associated with haematologic AEs

Among all 65 patients, 119 AEs occurred including haematologic toxicity in 26.2% (17/65). Patients with haematologic AEs exhibited significantly higher drug exposure: median t-MPA-AUC (37.73 vs 29.14 µg·h/mL; p=0.013) and f-MPA-AUC (360.50 vs 278.34 ng·h/mL; p=0.003) (figure 2A,B). Other variables significantly associated with haematologic ADRs in univariate analyses included glucose, β₂-microglobulin and C3 levels. Therefore, t-MPA-AUC, f-MPA-AUC, glucose, β₂-microglobulin and C3 were entered into the logistic regression model. Multivariate analysis identified f-MPA-AUC as an independent predictor of haematologic toxicity (OR=1.005; 95% CI 1.001 to 1.009; p=0.024), with model accuracy of 82.5% (Hosmer-Lemeshow p=0.876). Safety threshold for f-MPA-AUC

ROC analysis established an f-MPA-AUC safety threshold of 492.96 ng·h/mL (p=0.003), above which haematologic adverse event incidence increased 3.1-fold (45.8% vs 14.7%; p<0.001) (figure 2C). Corresponding AE-free survival was significantly shorter in this high-exposure group (13.16±4.36 vs 53.97±3.87 months; p<0.001) (figure 2D).

Discussion

This study represents the first evaluation of the association between f-MPA concentrations and both treatment outcomes and adverse reaction risks in paediatric LN, reporting an optimal exposure level and suggesting the potential benefit of f-MPA monitoring. While the efficacy of MMF in LN induction and maintenance therapy is well established, the application of TDM for MPA in LN remains limited, with a heterogeneous evidence base. This contrasts with its established utility in renal transplantation, which progressed from observational studies to RCTs and meta-analyses. Given the variability in MPA metabolism between individuals and across ethnicities, coupled with the need for personalised treatment, MPA TDM in paediatric LN is gaining significant attention. MMF therapy consists of an induction and a maintenance phase. According to international guidelines, proteinuria and disease activity at 0–12 months post high-dose induction are considered optimal predictors of long-term renal outcomes and are used to evaluate MMF efficacy.30 Reflecting the therapeutic response in Chinese patients with paediatric LN, our current study observed a remission rate (CR and PR) of 78.7% during the 0–12-month induction period, consistent with some previous paediatric reports31 but appearing slightly lower than rates often reported in adult studies.32–34 These discrepancies may arise from differences in sample size, outcome definitions, therapeutic regimens, genetics and socioeconomic factors.35 36

Our findings demonstrate that maintaining t-MPA-AUC above 31.63 µg·h/mL during induction enhances efficacy, aligning with prior paediatric LN studies that established therapeutic windows for t-MPA-AUC.25 26 Crucially, this study presents the novel key finding that an f-MPA-AUC greater than 354.45 ng·h/mL during induction is associated with superior outcomes and prolonged remission. While pharmacokinetic studies linking t-MPA exposure to response have been evidenced in LN, f-MPA as an efficacy predictor remains underexplored, largely limited to transplantation or adult populations.28 37 No paediatric LN-specific f-MPA therapeutic window has been established, highlighting an urgent need to fill this gap, as prior relevant reports exist only in transplant settings.28 37 In our cohort, serum albumin did not differ significantly between response groups, which may partly explain why f-MPA-AUC and t-MPA-AUC showed comparable efficacy-predictive performance.

MMF-related adverse effects, particularly haematologic toxicity, are clinically important. Cytopenias, including leucopenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia and pancytopenia, are recognised adverse effects associated with mycophenolate therapy and may necessitate dose reduction or treatment interruption, thereby compromising disease control and increasing treatment burden.38 The incidence of haematologic toxicity in our cohort was 26.2%. While prior toxicity-related MPA exposure data primarily focus on t-MPA and show inconsistent findings,39–45 emerging evidence from transplantation and autoimmune diseases suggests f-MPA, the pharmacologically active form, may more accurately predict toxicity than t-MPA.14 28 46 In adult LN, Abd Rahman et al reported that anaemia was significantly associated with higher t-MPA-AUC and f-MPA-AUC, with a stronger association observed for f-MPA exposure.47 In paediatric renal transplant recipients, Weber et al found that high free, but not total, MPA-AUC was associated with an increased risk of MMF-related leucopenia and/or infections.48 These findings support the pharmacological rationale for evaluating haematologic adverse drug reactions as a safety endpoint, particularly because f-MPA represents the pharmacologically active fraction of MPA. Our study confirms that f-MPA-AUC is an independent predictor of haematologic toxicity and is superior to t-MPA-AUC in forecasting MMF safety, advocating for focused f-MPA monitoring. This finding is biologically plausible because only the unbound fraction of MPA is pharmacologically active, and changes in albumin concentration, renal function or competitive displacement by MPAG may increase f-MPA exposure even when t-MPA exposure appears acceptable.

Affected patients had significantly higher f-MPA-AUC, and controlling f-MPA-AUC below 492.96 ng·h/mL may reduce toxicity. The TDM of f-MPA remains poorly characterised with no established safety thresholds currently available in LN. Our findings establish the first therapeutic window for f-MPA in childhood LN, providing preliminary evidence for TDM-guided f-MPA therapy. However, evidence supporting TDM-guided f-MPA therapy remains limited, and previous safety-related findings have mainly been derived from adult LN or transplant populations rather than paediatric LN.47–49 Therefore, our study provides preliminary paediatric LN-specific evidence that f-MPA-AUC may help identify patients at increased risk of haematologic toxicity and may guide safety-oriented MMF dose optimisation.

Several limitations warrant consideration. Relatively few patients were followed into the maintenance phase, necessitating larger studies to validate findings and assess optimal treatment discontinuation timing. Furthermore, the follow-up was limited to the first 12 months; longer term studies are needed to confirm the durability of the predictive markers and clinical benefits observed. Finally, haematologic toxicity in paediatric LN is multifactorial. Although lupus-induced cytopenia and calcineurin inhibitor use were excluded, residual confounding cannot be fully ruled out. In particular, MPA metabolites, including MPAG and acyl-MPAG (AcMPAG), were not measured in this study. MPAG may influence MPA protein binding and thereby affect f-MPA exposure, while AcMPAG has been reported to possess pharmacological activity and may be involved in MPA-related adverse effects.50 51 However, the clinical evidence linking these metabolites to haematologic toxicity remains limited and inconsistent.52 53 Therefore, unmeasured metabolites may have acted as residual confounding factors in the observed association between f-MPA-AUC and haematologic toxicity. Thus, this association should be interpreted as exposure–safety related rather than causal, and future studies measuring both MPA and its metabolites are warranted.

In conclusion, both t-MPA-AUC and f-MPA-AUC can predict efficacy and safety in patients with paediatric LN receiving MMF, with f-MPA-AUC demonstrating superior accuracy for predicting safety. This study proposes maintaining t-MPA-AUC >31.63 µg·h/mL and f-MPA-AUC between 354.45 and 492.96 ng·h/mL during induction. Critically, this work establishes, for the first time, a therapeutic window for f-MPA in MMF treatment for patients with paediatric LN, offering a strategy to enhance therapeutic effect while reducing the risk of drug toxicity.

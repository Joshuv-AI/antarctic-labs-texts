Bohr Phenomenon for K K -quasiconformal Harmonic Mappings Involving One Parameter
Molla Basir Ahamed, Taimur Rahman

Abstract
. In this article, we study Bohr-type inequalities involving a parameter or convex combinations for K K -quasiconformal, sense-preserving harmonic mappings in 𝔻 \mathbb{D} , where the analytic part is subordinate to a convex function. Moreover, we establish similar inequalities when the subordinating function is chosen from the class of concave univalent functions with pole p p , as well as from the family of concave univalent functions with opening angle π ​ α \pi\alpha . The results generalize several existing results.

Introduction

Bohr’s foundational result from 1914
[
22
]
, dealing with power series in complex analysis, gave rise to what is now termed Bohr’s phenomenon. This influential contribution has attracted extensive attention, with significant results established in various function spaces. For comprehensive accounts, we recommend the survey by Abu-Muhanna
et al.

[
3
]
, the chapter by Garcia
et al.

[
30
, Chapter 8]
, and the references therein.

1.1.
Classical Bohr inequality and its generalizations:

Let
\mathcal{B}
represent the class of all analytic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
bounded by
|f(z)|\leq 1
. The inequality introduced in 1914 by Hardy, after correspondence with Harald Bohr (see
[
22
]
), is stated as follows:

Theorem A
.

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, then

(1.1)

\displaystyle M_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\quad\text{for }r\leq\tfrac{1}{3}.

The radius
1/3
is best possible.

Bohr initially introduced inequality (
1.1
) for
r\leq 1/6
. The constant was improved to
r\leq 1/3
by the independent work of M. Riesz, I. Schur, and F. Wiener, who also confirmed that
1/3
is the best possible. Consequently,
1/3
is termed the Bohr radius, while inequality (
1.1
) is the Bohr inequality for
\mathcal{B}
. For the function

\displaystyle f_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-az}=a-(1-a^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a^{n-1}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

one easily sees that
M_{f_{a}}(r)>1
holds if and only if
r>1/(1+2a)
. This implies, as
a\to 1^{-}
, that
1/3
is indeed sharp.

It is worth mentioning that in the majorant series
M_{f}(r)
, if one replaces
|a_{0}|
with
|a_{0}|^{2}
in Bohr’s inequality, then the constant
1/3
improves to
1/2
. Furthermore, when
a_{0}=0
in Theorem A, the exact Bohr radius is given by
1/\sqrt{2}
(see
[
40
]
,
[
50
, Corollary 2.9]
, and
[
52
]
). Paulsen
et al.

[
51
]
later offered an elegant and elementary proof of Bohr’s inequality. In recent years, numerous studies have focused on the Bohr radius and its generalizations. Abu-Muhanna and Ali
[
1
,
2
]
investigated the problem for analytic mappings from
\mathbb{D}
to simply connected domains and the complement of the unit disk. Further, the Bohr phenomenon in shifted disks and simply connected domains has been studied in
[
6
,
27
,
28
]
. Allu and Halder
[
9
]
and Bhowmik and Das
[
20
]
studied Bohr-type inequalities in the framework of subordination classes. For a comprehensive overview, we refer to
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
27
,
33
,
40
,
41
]
and references therein.

1.2.
Bohr-Rogosinski inequality and its generalizations:

In addition to the Bohr radius, another significant concept is the Rogosinski radius
[
54
]
, defined as follows: Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then, for each
N\geq 1
, the inequality
|S_{N}(z)|\leq 1
holds for
|z|\leq 1/2
, where
S_{N}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_{n}z^{n}
denotes the
N
-th partial sum. The constant
1/2
is sharp. Motivated by this notion, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
42
]
introduced the Bohr-Rogosinski sum

\displaystyle R^{f}_{N}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n},

which clearly satisfies
|S_{N}(z)|=|f(z)-\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}|\leq R^{f}_{N}(z)
. Furthermore, this sum generalizes the classical Bohr sum, corresponding to the case
N=1
with
f(0)
replaced by
f(z)
. The Bohr-Rogosinski radius is then defined as the maximal
r\in(0,1)
such that
R^{f}_{N}(z)\leq 1
for
|z|\leq r
.

Theorem B
.

[
42
]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
. Then, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(1.2)

\displaystyle|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq 1

for
|z|=r\leq R_{N}
, where
R_{N}
is the positive root of the equation
2(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r)^{2}=0
. The radius
R_{N}
is the best possible.

Recently, Hamada
et al.

[
32
]
studied the Bohr–Rogosinski radius for holomorphic mappings defined on the unit ball of a complex Banach space, taking values in higher dimensional Banach spaces. Furthermore, they established the Bohr–Rogosinski radius for certain subordination classes on the unit ball of a complex Banach space. For recent study with various class of functions on Bohr-Rogosinski inequality, we refer to the articles
[
4
,
29
,
10
,
42
]

We recall the notion of differential subordination, which has proven to be an effective approach in solving numerous problems in geometric function theory. A detailed discussion of subordination classes is found in
[
25
, Chapter 6]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
f
and
g
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. Then
f
is said to be subordinate to
g
, expressed as
f\prec g
, if there exists a Schwarz function
w\in\mathcal{B}
satisfying
w(0)=0
such that
f(z)=g(w(z))
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. If
g
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, this subordination holds exactly when
f(0)=g(0)
and
f(\mathbb{D})\subset g(\mathbb{D})
. Moreover, the condition
f\prec g
guarantees that
|f^{\prime}(0)|\leq|g^{\prime}(0)|
.

1.3.
Distance formulation of the Bohr inequality:

The investigation of the Bohr phenomenon for subordinated functions was first carried out by Abu-Muhanna
[
1
]
. In this context, we denote by
S(g):=\{f:f\prec g\}
the family of all analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
that are subordinate to a univalent function
g
. The class
S(g)
is said to satisfy the Bohr phenomenon if there exists
r_{g}\in(0,1)
such that, for every
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in S(g)
, one has

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq d(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D}))\quad\text{for all }|z|=r\leq r_{g},

where
d(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D}))
represents the Euclidean distance between
g(0)
and
\partial g(\mathbb{D})
.

The Bohr radius for the class
S(g)
is defined to be the largest
r_{g}
such that the Bohr inequality holds. Similarly,
S(g)
is said to exhibit the Bohr–Rogosinski property if there exists some
r_{N,g}\in(0,1]
with the property that

(1.4)

\displaystyle|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq|g(0)|+d(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D})),

whenever
|z|=r\leq r_{N,g}
for all
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in S(g)
. The largest such
r_{N,g}
is called the Bohr–Rogosinski radius.

For the mapping
g(z)=(a-z)/(1-\bar{a}z)
with
|a|\leq 1
, we see that
g(\mathbb{D})=\mathbb{D}
, hence
S(g)=\mathcal{B}
and
d(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D}))=1-|a|
. Therefore, from (
1.1
), inequality (
1.3
) holds for
|z|\leq 1/3
, while Theorem A shows that (
1.4
) is true with
r_{N,g}=R_{N}
.

1.4.
Harmonic Mappings:

For a simply connected domain
\Omega
, let
f=u+iv
be a complex-valued function.
The function is harmonic in
\Omega
if and only if it satisfies
4f_{z\bar{z}}=0
.
This implies that
u
and
v
are harmonic functions.
It is well known that a harmonic function admits a decomposition
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic.
Here
h
is called the analytic part and
g
the co-analytic part of
f
.
This splitting is unique up to an additive constant
[
26
]
.
A classical result due to Lewy
[
44
]
shows that
f
is locally univalent if and only if
J_{f}(z)=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\neq 0
.
Moreover,
f
is locally univalent and preserves orientation when
J_{f}(z)>0
, or equivalently when
h^{\prime}(z)\neq 0
and
|\omega|<1
, with
\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
.

1.5.
K-quasiconformal Harmonic Mappings:

A harmonic function
f=h+\bar{g}
is called
K
-quasiconformal harmonic in
\mathbb{D}
if it is locally univalent, sense-preserving, and satisfies
|\omega_{f}(z)|\leq k<1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where
K=(1+k)/(1-k)\geq 1
(see
[
37
,
49
]
). Clearly, as
k\to 1
, it follows that
K\to\infty
. In 2018, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
43
]
studied the class of sense-preserving harmonic mappings
f=h+\bar{g}
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic with
g(0)=0
, and determined the Bohr radius under certain assumptions on
h
and
g
. Later, in 2019, Liu and Ponnusamy
[
45
]
introduced the Bohr radius for
K
-quasiconformal, sense-preserving harmonic functions in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
is subordinate to some analytic function
\phi
, and proposed two conjectures. Subsequently, Liu
et al.

[
46
]
made a significant contribution by investigating the Bohr radius for sense-preserving
K
-quasiregular harmonic mappings in
\mathbb{D}
, where
h(z)-h(0)
is quasi-subordinate to a given analytic function. They not only provided sharper versions of four theorems from
[
45
]
, but also resolved the two conjectures posed therein.

In 2019, Liu and Ponnusamy
[
45
]
established results concerning the Bohr radii of harmonic mappings in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where the analytic part is subordinate to a given analytic function.

Theorem C
.

[
45
]

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec\phi
, where
\phi
is univalent and convex in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}\leq d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq\frac{K+1}{5K+1},

The number
(K+1)/(5K+1)
is sharp.

Theorem D
.

[
45
]

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec\phi
, where
\phi
is analytic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}\leq d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq r_{u}(k),

where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
and
r_{u}(k)\in(0,1)
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle(1-r)^{2}-4r(1+k\sqrt{1+r})=0.

In
[
47
]
, Long
et al.
established Bohr-type inequalities with a parameter or involving convex combinations for bounded analytic functions. Motivated by these results, Arora and Vinayak
[
12
]
investigated Bohr-type inequalities for several classes of analytic functions. They began by studying the Bohr phenomenon through convex combinations and extended their work to other Bohr-type inequalities for univalent analytic functions. Furthermore, they examined similar inequalities for the Ma-Minda classes of convex and starlike functions, and also provided the same estimates for concave univalent functions.

The results in
[
47
,
12
]
motivate us to pose the following questions.

Question 1.1
.

Is it possible to establish Bohr-type inequalities involving parameters or convex combinations for
K
-quasiconformal, sense-preserving harmonic functions in
\mathbb{D}
, assuming the analytic part is subordinate to a convex function?

Question 1.2
.

Is it possible to obtain Bohr-type inequalities with a parameter or convex combination for
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mappings whose analytic part is subordinate to a concave univalent function with pole
p
?

Question 1.3
.

Is it possible to derive Bohr-type inequalities with a parameter or convex combination for
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mappings whose analytic component is subordinate to a concave univalent function of opening angle
\pi\alpha
?

As part of our main contributions, we provide affirmative answers to Questions
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
, which are discussed in Section2, Section3, and Section 4, respectively.

We organize the paper as follows: In Section2, we study Bohr-type inequalities involving parameters or convex combinations for
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mappings subordinate to convex functions. In Section 3, we derive similar inequalities for the case when the subordinate function belongs to the family of concave univalent functions with pole
p
. In Section 4, We provide Bohr-type inequalities assuming the subordinate function belongs to the family of concave univalent functions with opening angle
\pi\alpha
.

2.
Bohr’s phenomenon related to the family of convex functions

Convex functions form an important class of functions in geometric function
theory. A single-valued function
f
is said to be univalent in a domain

D\subset\mathbb{C}
if, for any two distinct points
z_{1},z_{2}\in D
, their
corresponding function values
f(z_{1})
and
f(z_{2})
are also distinct. A univalent
function
f
is said to be convex in a domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}
if it is analytic
and the image
f(D)
is a convex set. A set in the complex plane is convex if, for any
two points
w_{1},w_{2}\in f(D)
, the line segment joining them lies entirely within
f(D)
.
Equivalently, a univalent function
f
is convex in
D
if it maps
D
conformally onto a convex domain. It is worth noting that the function defined by

\displaystyle c(z):=\frac{z}{1-z},\;\;z\in\mathbb{D},

is a classical example of a convex function and often plays the role of an extremal function.

Before presenting our main results, we recall the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
29
]

Suppose that
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
are two analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec g
, then for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\;\;\;\mbox{holds for}\;\;|z|=r\leq 1/3.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
8
,
46
]

Suppose that
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
are two analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
. If
|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|
in
\mathbb{D}
for some
k\in(0,1]
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq 1/3.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
1
]

Suppose
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is an analytic and univalent map from
\mathbb{D}
onto a convex domain
g(\mathbb{D})
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1-|z|^{2})|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq d(g(z),\partial g(\mathbb{D}))\leq(1-|z|^{2})|g^{\prime}(z)|\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\prec g(z)
, then

\displaystyle|b_{n}|\leq|g^{\prime}(0)|\leq 2d(g(0),\partial g(\mathbb{D}))\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;n\geq 1.

We now present our first result on Bohr’s phenomenon, formulated in terms of convex combinations for convex functions.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec\phi
, where
\phi
is convex function. Then for arbitrary
t\in[0,1]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle t|h(z^{m})|+(1-t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}\leq|\phi(0)|+d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))

for
r\leq\min\{1/3,R_{K,m,t}\}
, where
R_{K,m,t}
is the unique root in
(0,1)
of the equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle D^{t}_{K,m}(r):=\frac{tr^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\frac{2(1-t)Kr}{(K+1)(1-r)}-\frac{1}{2}=0.

The radius
R_{K,m,t}
is sharp if
R_{K,m,t}\leq 1/3
.

The following corollary is obtained from the above theorem by taking
K=1
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be a an analytic function defined on
\mathbb{D}
and
f\prec\phi
, where
\phi
is a convex function. Then for arbitrary
t\in[0,1]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle t|f(z^{m})|+(1-t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq|\phi(0)|+d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))

for
r\leq\min\{1/3,R_{m,t}\}
, where
R_{m,t}
is the unique in
(0,1)
of the equation

\displaystyle D^{t}_{m}(r):=\frac{tr^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\frac{(1-t)r}{1-r}-\frac{1}{2}=0.

The number
R_{m,t}
cannot be improved if
R_{m,t}\leq 1/3
.

Remark 2.1
.

If we set
t=0
in Theorem
2.1
, it coincides with Theorem C.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Since
h\prec\phi
and
\phi
is analytic convex univalent function in
\mathbb{D}
, it follows from Lemma
2.3
that
|a_{n}|\leq|\phi^{\prime}(0)|\leq 2d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))
for
n\geq 1
. Then, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 2d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{n}=2d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\frac{r}{1-r}.

Since
f
is
K
-quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mapping on
\mathbb{D}
, it follows from Schwarz’s Lemma that the dilatation
\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
is analytic with
|\omega(z)|\leq k
in
\mathbb{D}
, where
K=(1+k)/(1-k)
and
k\in[0,1)
. Hence, by using Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 2kd(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\frac{r}{1-r}.

Since
\phi
is univalent and
h\prec\phi
, it follows that
h(0)=\phi(0)
and hence

(2.4)

\displaystyle|h(z^{m})|\leq|h(0)|+\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{mn}\right|\leq|\phi(0)|+2d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\frac{r^{m}}{1-r^{m}}.

From inequalities (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and (
2.4
), it follows that

\displaystyle t|h(z^{m})|+

\displaystyle(1-t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\phi(0)|+2d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\left(\frac{tr^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\frac{2(1-t)Kr}{(K+1)(1-r)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\phi(0)|+2d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\left(D^{t}_{K,m}(r)+\frac{1}{2}\right),

where
D^{t}_{K,m}(r)
is defined by (
2.1
). It is easy to observe that the function
D^{t}_{K,m}(r)
increases strictly on the interval
(0,1)
, with
D^{t}_{K,m}(0)=-1/2<0
and
\lim_{r\to 1}D^{t}_{K,m}(r)=+\infty
. It follows that the equation
D^{t}_{K,m}(r)=0
admits exactly one solution
R_{K,m,t}
in
(0,1)
. Consequently,
D^{t}_{K,m}(r)\leq 0
if, and only if,
r\leq\min\{1/3,R_{K,m,t}\}
, proving the desired inequality.

For the proof of the sharpness in the case
R_{K,m,t}\leq 1/3
, we consider the function
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\phi(z)=h(z)=\frac{z}{1-z}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;g(z)=k\mu\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n},

where
|\mu|=1
and
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
. It is well-known that
d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))=1/2
and
|\phi(0)|=0
. For
z=r
, we have

\displaystyle t|h(r^{m})|+(1-t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|k\mu a_{n}|)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{tr^{m}}{1-r^{m}}+\frac{2(1-t)Kr}{(K+1)(1-r)}

\displaystyle=D^{t}_{K,m}(r)+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=D^{t}_{K,m}(r)+|\phi(0)|+d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))

\displaystyle>|\phi(0)|+d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))

holds true for
r>R_{K,m,t}
. This completes the proof.

For the same class, we now examine a Bohr-type inequality involving a parameter, which leads to the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}

3.
Bohr-type inequalities for the family of concave univalent functions with pole
p

5. Let
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
denote the extended complex plane. We define
\widehat{C_{p}}
to be the family of meromorphic univalent mappings
f:\mathbb{D}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that:

(i)

f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{p\}
, and the complement
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus f(\mathbb{D})
is convex.

(ii)

f
has a simple pole at
p
.

Without loss of generality, a suitable rotation allows us to suppose that
0<p<1
. Each
f\in\widehat{C_{p}}
is a concave univalent mapping with a pole at
p\in(0,1)
, possessing the Taylor expansion

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}_{p},

where
\mathbb{D}_{p}:=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z|<p\}
. The subclass
C_{p}
is given by

C_{p}:=\{f\in\widehat{C_{p}}:f(0)=0,\ f^{\prime}(0)=1\}.

It is important to note that the function

\displaystyle k_{p}(z):=\frac{pz}{(p-z)(1-pz)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}_{p},

is a classical example of a concave univalent function with a pole at
p
, whose series expansion is given by

k_{p}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-p^{2n}}{(1-p^{2})p^{n-1}}z^{n},

and is commonly used as an extremal function.

The representation formula for functions in
C_{p}
was established by Wirths
[
56
]
in 2006. This formulation allowed Bhowmik
et al.

[
21
]
to initiate an analysis in 2007, based on the Laurent series expansion around the pole
p
, where they obtained certain coefficient bounds for the class
\widehat{C_{p}}
. The class has since been extensively investigated in
[
13
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
55
]
.

In the following, the sharp Bohr-type inequality is obtained in terms of convex combinations for harmonic mappings whose analytic part is subordinate to functions belonging to the class
\widehat{C_{p}}
, where
p\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec\phi
, where
\phi\in\widehat{C_{p}}
and
p\in(0,1)
. Then for arbitrary
t\in[0,1]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the inequality

\displaystyle t|h(z^{m})|+(1-t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}\leq|\phi(0)|+d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))

for
r\leq\min\{R_{K,m,p,t},1/3\}
,
where
R_{K,m,p,t}\in(0,p)
is the unique root of the equation

(3.1)

\displaystyle tk_{p}(r^{m})+\left(\frac{2K(1-t)}{K+1}\right)k_{p}(r)-\frac{p}{(1+p)^{2}}=0.

The radius
R_{K,m,p,t}
is sharp if
R_{K,m,p,t}\leq 1/3
.

By choosing
K=1
in Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be a an analytic function defined on
\mathbb{D}
and

, where

of the equation

The result is sharp if
R_{m,p,t}\leq 1/3
.

Remark 3.1
.

(i)

By choosing

(so

(0,p)

(iii)

If we let

Bohr-type inequalities for the family of concave univalent functions

Let us consider the class of concave functions, defined as conformal mappings from the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
onto the complement of convex sets with opening angle
\pi\alpha
at infinity, for
\alpha\in[1,2]
. The name “concave” reflects the structure of the image domain
f(\mathbb{D})
. It is crucial to note that the set
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}\setminus f(\mathbb{D})
can be either bounded or unbounded. If it is bounded, then
f
is a meromorphic function. Therefore, we restrict ourselves to the unbounded case, where
f
has a simple pole on the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
. We proceed to define this class formally.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{0}}(\alpha)
represent the class of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
that fulfill the following conditions:

(1)

f
maps
\mathbb{D}
onto
f(\mathbb{D})
conformally.

(2)

f(1)=\infty
.

(3)

The set
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}\setminus f(\mathbb{D})
is convex, and the opening angle at infinity is less than or equal to
\pi\alpha
, where
\alpha\in[1,2]
.

This collection of functions is known as the class of concave univalent functions with opening angle
\pi\alpha
. The function
f_{\alpha}
, defined on
\mathbb{D}
as

(4.1)

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}(z):=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left[\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}z^{k}.

is of great significance in this class and commonly acts as an extremal function. It is easy to verify that for all
\alpha\in[1,2]
, the class
\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{0}}(\alpha)
is contained in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{0}}(2)
(see
[
15
]
). In the special case
\alpha=1
, the image domain is a convex half-plane. This shows that concave univalent functions relate to convex functions, and every
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{0}}(1)
belongs to the class of convex functions. For a more comprehensive analysis of concave univalent functions, we refer the reader to
[
14
,
15
,
19
,
20
,
23
]
and the works cited therein.

In order to prove the main results, we rely on the following lemma from
[
19
]
. This lemma provides both the Growth
[
19
, Corollary 2.4]
and Distortion
[
19
, Corollary 2.3]
theorems for concave univalent functions.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in[1,2]
. Then, for each
f\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
, we have

(i)

Growth Theorem:
|f(z)-f(0)|\leq|f^{\prime}(0)|f_{\alpha}(r)
,

(ii)

Distortion Theorem:
|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq|f^{\prime}(0)|f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(r)
,

where
f_{\alpha}
is defined by (
4.1
). For each
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
z\neq 0
, equality occurs if
f=f_{\alpha}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
10
]

Let
f\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
,
\alpha\in[1,2]
, have the expansion
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. Then we have the following inequalities:

(i)

|f^{\prime}(0)|\leq 2\alpha d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))
.

(ii)

|a_{n}|\leq A_{n}|f^{\prime}(0)|
, for
n\geq 1
.

All inequalities are sharp for the function
f_{\alpha}(z)
.

The following theorem provides a Bohr-type inequality in terms of convex combinations for harmonic mappings with analytic part subordinate to functions in the class
\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
, where
\alpha\in[1,2]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a sense-preserving
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and

, where
\phi\in\widehat{C_{0}}(\alpha)
and
\phi(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n}

and

\displaystyle t|h(z^{m})|+(1-t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}\leq|\phi(0)|+d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D})),

holds for

, where

is the unique root of the equation

(4.2)

\displaystyle tf_{\alpha}(r^{m})+\left(\frac{2K(1-t)}{K+1}\right)f_{\alpha}(r)-\frac{1}{2\alpha}=0.

The radius
R_{K,m,t,\alpha}
is sharp if

.

(i)

By choosing

(4.11)

\displaystyle B_{3}(r):=

\displaystyle|h(z^{m})|+|h^{\prime}(z^{m})|r+\lambda\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\phi(0)|+2\alpha d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\left(f_{\alpha}(r^{m})+r\frac{(1+r^{m})^{\alpha-1}}{(1-r^{m})^{\alpha+1}}+\lambda(f_{\alpha}(r)-r+kf_{\alpha}(r))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\phi(0)|+2\alpha d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\left(f_{\alpha}(r^{m})+r\frac{(1+r^{m})^{\alpha-1}}{(1-r^{m})^{\alpha+1}}+\lambda\left(\frac{2K}{K+1}f_{\alpha}(r)-r\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\phi(0)|+2\alpha d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\left(H_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}(r)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right),

where

\displaystyle H_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}(r)=f_{\alpha}(r^{m})+r\frac{(1+r^{m})^{\alpha-1}}{(1-r^{m})^{\alpha+1}}+\lambda\left(\frac{2K}{K+1}f_{\alpha}(r)-r\right)-\frac{1}{2\alpha}.

The function
H_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}(r)
is easily seen to be continuous and strictly increasing on
(0,1)
, with

\displaystyle H_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}(0)<0\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\lim_{r\to 1}H_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}(r)=+\infty,

so the function admits a unique zero
R^{**}_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}
in
(0,1)
. This implies
H_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}(r)\leq 0
for
r\leq R^{**}_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}
, and hence by (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle B_{3}(r)\leq|\phi(0)|+d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq\min\{1/3,R^{**}_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}\}.

To establish the sharpness of the result for the case
R^{**}_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}\leq 1/3
, we take the function
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where

\displaystyle h(z)=\phi(z)=f_{\alpha}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad g(z)=k\mu f_{\alpha}(z),

with
|\mu|=1
and
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
. It is well known that
d(f_{\alpha}(0),f_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}))=1/2\alpha
. Moreover, by definition of
f_{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{(1+z)^{\alpha-1}}{(1-z)^{\alpha+1}}.

Hence, for
z=r
, we obtain

\displaystyle|f_{\alpha}(r^{m})|+

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(r^{m})|r+\lambda\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|A_{n}|r^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\mu kA_{n}|r^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}(r^{m})+r\frac{(1+r^{m})^{\alpha-1}}{(1-r^{m})^{\alpha+1}}+\lambda\left(\frac{2K}{K+1}f_{\alpha}(r)-r\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\phi(0)|+d(\phi(0),\partial\phi(\mathbb{D}))

for
r>R^{*}_{K,m,\alpha,\lambda}
. This completes the proof.

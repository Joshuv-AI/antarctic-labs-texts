Modal Logic for Reasoning About Uncertainty and Confusion
Marta Bílková, Thomas M. Ferguson, Daniil Kozhemiachenko

Abstract
We consider a modal logic that can formalise statements about uncertainty and beliefs such as I think that my wallet is in the drawer rather than elsewhere or I am confused whether my appointment is on Monday or Tuesday . To do that, we expand Gödel modal logic 𝐊 ​ 𝖦 \mathbf{K}\mathsf{G} with the involutive negation ∼ {\sim} defined as v ( ∼ ϕ , w ) = 1 − v ( ϕ , w ) v({\sim}\phi,w)=1-v(\phi,w) . We provide semantics with the finite model property for our new logic that we call 𝐊 ​ 𝖦 𝗂𝗇𝗏 \mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}} and show its equivalence to the standard semantics over [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] -valued Kripke models. Namely, we show that ϕ \phi is valid in the standard semantics of 𝐊 ​ 𝖦 𝗂𝗇𝗏 \mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}} iff it is valid in the new semantics. Using this new semantics, we construct a constraint tableaux calculus for 𝐊 ​ 𝖦 𝗂𝗇𝗏 \mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}} that allows for an explicit extraction of countermodels from complete open branches and then employ the tableaux calculus to obtain the 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{PSPACE} -completeness of the validity in 𝐊 ​ 𝖦 𝗂𝗇𝗏 \mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}} .

We are now ready to show the soundness and completeness of our tableaux calculus.

Theorem 2
.

\phi\in\mathscr{L}^{{\sim}}_{\Box,\lozenge}
is
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
-valid iff it has a
\mathcal{T}\!(\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}})
proof.

Proof.

The proof is standard and follows
[
29
, Theorems 6.19 and 6.21]
, so we only give a sketch thereof. For the soundness part, we show that if a tableau beginning with
w:\phi<1
is closed, then
\phi
is
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
-valid. We show that if
{\mathfrak{M}}
realises the premises of a rule, then it should also realise at least one of its conclusions (note that branching rules have two conclusions). The soundness will follow since there is no
\mathsf{F}
-model
{\mathfrak{M}}
and realisation s.t.
{\mathfrak{M}}
realises a closed branch
\mathcal{B}
under .

We consider the case of the
\Box_{\leqslant}
rule as an example. Let
{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle W,R,T,v\rangle
realise
w:\Box\phi\leqslant\mathbb{T}
. If
\real(\mathbb{T})=1
, then
1\leqslant\mathbb{T}
(the left conclusion) is realised. Consider the case where
\real(\mathbb{T})<1
. Then (cf. Definition
3
)
\max\{x\in T(w)\mid x\leq\inf\limits_{w^{\prime}\in W}\{\real(w)Rw^{\prime}\rightarrow_{\mathsf{G}}v(\phi,w^{\prime})\}\}\leq\real(\mathbb{T})
. This means that there is a state
w^{\prime}
s.t.
v(\phi,w^{\prime})<wRw^{\prime}
and there are
x,x^{\prime}\in T(w)
s.t.
x^{\prime}
is the immediate successor of
x
,
v(\Box\phi,w)=x
, and
v(\phi,w^{\prime})<x^{\prime}
. Now setting
\real(t(w))=x
,
\real(t^{s}(w))=x^{\prime}
, and
\real(w^{\prime})=w^{\prime}
, we have that
t(w)\leqslant\mathbb{T}
and
w^{\prime}:\phi<t^{s}(w)
are realised. Hence, the right conclusion is realised.

For the completeness part, we prove that for every complete open branch, there is an
\mathsf{F}
-model
{\mathfrak{M}}
and a realisation s.t.
{\mathfrak{M}}
realises
\mathcal{B}
under . Let
\mathcal{B}
be a complete open branch and consider the system of inequalities
\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{t}}
as shown in (
5
) and its solution as specified in Definition
7
. Now define
{\mathfrak{M}}
as follows:
W=\{w\mid w\text{ occurs in }\mathcal{B}\}
,
wRw^{\prime}=x_{w\mathsf{R}w^{\prime}}
,
T(w)=\{x_{t(w)}\mid t(w)\text{ occurs in }\mathcal{B}\}\cup\{x_{t^{s}(w)}\mid t^{s}(w)\text{ occurs in }\mathcal{B}\}\cup\{0,\frac{1}{2},1\}
and
v(p,w)=x_{w:p}
for every
w
,
w^{\prime}
, and
p\in\mathtt{Prop}
occurring in
\mathcal{B}
. We also define
\real(w)=w
,
\real(\mathbb{T})=x_{\mathbb{T}}
, and
\real(w:\chi)=x_{w:\chi}
for every
w\in\mathsf{WL}
,
\mathbb{T}\in\mathsf{VT}
, and
w:\chi\in\mathsf{LF}
occurring on
\mathcal{B}
. It remains to show that all constraints are realised by
{\mathfrak{M}}
under .

For all constraints of the form
\mathbb{T}\triangledown\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
that do not contain labelled formulas, we have
\real(\mathbb{T})\triangledown\real(\mathbb{T}^{\prime})
by the construction of
{\mathfrak{M}}
since
\mathcal{B}
is a complete open branch. Let us now show that all constraints
u:\psi\triangledown\mathbb{T}
on
\mathcal{B}
are realised. We proceed by induction on formulas. Constraints of the form
w:p\triangledown\mathbb{T}
are realised by construction of
{\mathfrak{M}}
. The cases of constraints whose principal connective is
{\sim}
,
\wedge
, or
\rightarrow
can be shown by simple applications of the induction hypothesis.

Let us consider the case of
w:\Box\phi\leq\mathbb{T}
as an example and show that
v(\Box\phi,w)\leq\real(\mathbb{T})
. Since
w:\Box\phi\leqslant\mathbb{T}
is in
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}
is complete and open, then it also contains (a)
1\leqslant\mathbb{T}
or (b)
t(w)\leqslant\mathbb{T}
,
w^{\prime}:\phi<w\mathsf{R}w^{\prime}
, and
w^{\prime}:\phi<t^{s}(w)
. If (a) is the case,
\mathbb{T}
is a value term (observe from Definition
6
that constraints can contain at most one labelled formula), whence
1\leqslant\mathbb{T}
is realised by the construction of
{\mathfrak{M}}
. Thus,
\real(\mathbb{T})=1
and
v(\Box\phi,w)\leq\real(\mathbb{T})
, as required. If (b) is the case, then by the induction hypothesis
t(w)\leqslant\mathbb{T}
,
w^{\prime}:\phi<w\mathsf{R}w^{\prime}
, and
w^{\prime}:\phi<t^{s}(w)
are realised, whence,
v(\phi,w^{\prime})<wRw^{\prime}
,
x_{t(w)}\leq\real(\mathbb{T})
, and
v(\phi,w^{\prime})<x_{t^{s}(w)}
with
\{x_{t(w)},x_{t^{s}(w)}\}\subseteq T(w)
and
x_{t^{s}(w)}
being the immediate successor of
x_{t(w)}
. But in this case, it is clear that
v(\Box\phi,w)=\max\{x\in T(w)\mid x\leq\inf\limits_{w^{\prime}\in W}\{wRw^{\prime}\rightarrow_{\mathsf{G}}v(\phi,w^{\prime})\}\}\leq x_{t(w)}
, whence,
v(\Box\phi,w)\leq\real(\mathbb{T})
, as required.
∎

5
Complexity

Let us now use
\mathcal{T}\!(\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}})
to obtain the
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-completeness of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
. Our proof adapts the standard algorithm for
\mathbf{K}
from
[
8
]
.

Theorem 3
.

Validity of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
is
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete.

Proof.

\mathsf{PSPACE}
-hardness is immediate since
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}
is
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-hard
[
16
, Theorem 21]
and
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
is a conservative extension of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}
. Let us now tackle the upper bound.

First of all, we observe that all tableaux terminate. Indeed, the branching factor of rules is at most
2
, and every rule except for
\Box_{\triangleright}
and
\lozenge_{\triangleleft}
reduces the number of connectives in the labelled formula in the premise. If
\Box_{\triangleright}
or
\lozenge_{\triangleleft}
is applied, then
w:\Box\phi=t(w)
or
w:\lozenge\phi=t^{s}(w)
, respectively, is introduced to which one can apply
\Box_{=}
or
\lozenge_{=}
which does decompose the formula. Thus, at some point, all formulas will be decomposed into atoms. We note, moreover, that given a branch
\mathcal{B}
, it takes us nondeterministic polynomial time to find a solution to
\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{t}}
over
[0,1]
s.t. (
5
) holds with respect to it.

Let us now provide a decision procedure that uses only polynomial space with respect to
\mathcal{l}(\phi)
. We aim to build the model realising
w^{0}_{1}:\phi<1
‘on the fly’ (in which case,
\phi
is not valid) or to show that it is impossible. Fig.
3
illustrates the process of building the model. As one sees, we
do not
build model branch-by-branch but rather generate all children of a given node, then pick one child, and again, generate all its children. This is because we need to apply rules
\Box_{=}
and
\lozenge_{=}
using generated states
all at once
. But these rules can be applied
only after

\Box_{\triangleright}
and
\lozenge_{\triangleleft}
.

We begin with
w^{0}_{1}:\phi<1
. In what follows, if a rule introduces branching to the tableau, we pick one branch, say,
\mathcal{B}
, and work with it depth-first. If the branch with which we are working is closed, we delete it and choose the next one. First, we apply propositional rules. Then we apply modal rules for constraints containing
\leqslant
or
<
. This adds new states
w^{1}_{1,1}
, …,
w^{1}_{n^{0}_{1},1}
(we will call them
children
of
w^{0}_{1}
), new members of
\mathsf{T}(w^{0}_{1})
of the form
t_{i}(w^{0}_{1})
,
t^{s}_{i}(w^{0}_{1})
,
\mathbb{0}
, and
\mathbb{1}
, relational terms
w^{0}_{1}\mathsf{R}w^{1}_{i,1}
, and new constraints of the form
\Box\chi=t_{i}(w^{0}_{1})
or
\lozenge\chi=t^{s}_{i}(w^{0}_{1})
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n^{0}_{1}\}
. Note that
n^{0}_{1}={\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{l}(\phi))
, whence, we need only
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{l}(\phi))
space to store the ‘content’ of
w^{0}_{1}
.

If
\mathcal{B}
is still open, mark
w^{1}_{1,1}
as ‘active’ and apply all rules for constraints of the form
\Box\chi=t_{i}(w^{0}_{1})
and
\lozenge\chi=t^{s}_{i}(w^{0}_{1})
using
w^{1}_{1,1}
in the conclusion. Note that
\Box_{=}
and
\lozenge_{=}
are the only modal rules that can become applicable after the application of
\Box_{\triangleright}
and
\lozenge_{\triangleleft}
. Once all
\Box_{=}
and
\lozenge_{=}
rules are applied, we apply the propositional rules to formulas labelled with
w^{1}_{1,1}
. We then apply modal rules for constraints with
\leqslant
and
<
. As on the previous stage, we generate new states
w^{2}_{1,1}
, …,
w^{2}_{n^{1}_{2},1}
, constraints of the form
\Box\chi=t_{i}(w^{1}_{1,1})
or
\lozenge\chi=t^{s}_{i}(w^{1}_{1,1})
, and members of
\mathsf{T}(w^{1}_{1,1})
and relational terms corresponding to them. We repeat the procedure until we produce a state
w^{m}_{1,1}
s.t. all constraints
w^{m}_{1,1}:\chi\triangledown\mathbb{T}
are atomic. If the branch is not closed, we mark
w^{m}_{1,1}
as ‘safe’, delete all
w^{m}_{1,1}:\chi\triangledown\mathbb{T}
’s and go to
w^{m}_{2,1}
and repeat the procedure. Once all states
w^{m}_{1,1}
, …,
w^{m}_{n^{1}_{m},1}
are marked as ‘safe’, we delete constraints containing them, mark
w^{m-1}_{1,1}
as ‘safe’, and delete all its children. Then we proceed to
w^{m-1}_{2,1}
. The procedure is repeated until either all tableau branches are closed (which means that
\phi
is valid) or
w^{0}_{1}
is marked as ‘safe’ (and thus, we have a complete open branch, whence,
\phi
is not valid).

Note that the length of the branch of the constructed model is bounded from above by the modal depth of
\phi
and is thus
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{l}(\phi))
. In addition, each state of the branch of the model can have at most
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{l}(\phi))
children because their number is bounded from above by the number of modalities on the same level of nesting. Thus, we have to store
{\mathcal{O}}((\mathcal{l}(\phi))^{2})
states each of which contains constraints taking up to
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{l}(\phi))
space. Thus, our decision procedure utilises
{\mathcal{O}}((\mathcal{l}(\phi))^{3})
space.
∎

6
Conclusion

We presented an expansion of the Gödel modal logic with the involutive negation and showed how one can use it to formalise reasoning about uncertainty. We also constructed an alternative semantics for
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
following
[
15
,
16
]
with respect to which
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
has the finite model property. We then used these semantics to provide the tableaux calculus
\mathcal{T}\!(\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}})
that allows us to read off finite countermodels from complete open branches. Finally, we proved
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-completeness of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
-validity using
\mathcal{T}\!(\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}})
.

Our next steps are as follows. First, we want to continue the systematic study of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
. In particular, we plan to provide a complete Hilbert-style axiomatisation of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
over crisp and fuzzy frames. This will help investigate the correspondence between the classes of crisp and fuzzy frames on the one side and the
\mathscr{L}^{{\sim}}_{\Box,\lozenge}
-formulas that define these classes of frames, on the other. Furthermore, in
[
24
,
25
]
, hypersequent calculi for
\Box
- and
\lozenge
-fragments of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}
are presented. Since these calculi are cut-free, they can serve as a syntactical decision procedure (i.e., the one that does not directly produce a countermodel) for the fragments of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}
. It thus makes sense to construct hypersequent cut-free calculi for the bi-modal
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}
and its expansions and obtain an alternative, syntactical, decision procedure for
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}
.

Second, in
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, we considered
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
— an expansion of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}
with a so-called ‘strong negation’ from
[
31
]
with semantics defined on
[0,1]^{\Join}
— a bi-lattice product of
[0,1]
with itself. In particular, it was shown that a class of frames is
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
-definable iff it is
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}
-definable by a self-dual formula (i.e., a formula equivalent to the negation of the result of replacing every connective with its dual:
\wedge
with
\vee
,
\rightarrow
with
\Yleft
, and
\Box
with
\lozenge
). Note, however, that
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
does not introduce connectives defined w.r.t. the second order on
[0,1]^{\Join}
. On the other hand, in
[
30
]
, it is shown that the modal logic on
\{0,1\}^{\Join}
in the language with all bi-lattice connectives and modalities and the bi-modal classical modal logic have the same expressivity. It would be thus instructive to consider a full bi-lattice expansion of
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}^{2}
with semantics on
[0,1]^{\Join}
and compare its expressivity to
\mathbf{K}\mathsf{G}
and its expansions.

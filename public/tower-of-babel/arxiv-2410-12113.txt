Generation of polarization-entangled counter-propagating photons with high orbital angular momentum
Elisabeth Wagner, Mikołaj K. Schmidt, Michael J. Steel, Polina R. Sharapova

Abstract
Spin and orbital angular momenta of light are attractive resources to harness for encoding, and manipulating information, with applications in various quantum photonic technologies. However, to fully harness that potential, we require robust sources of high-order angular momentum photons exhibiting nonclassical correlations. Here we propose a fiber-based source of polarization-entangled photons in high-order orbital angular momentum (OAM) modes. In our setup the pairs or photons are generated in a cylindrical fiber through a four-wave mixing process, which induces polarization, or spin entanglement. The photons are then converted to modes exhibiting large OAM by the two helical gratings inscribed in the core of the fiber. We present a complete theoretical framework used to consistently describe this process, and demonstrate a robust control over the joint spectral amplitude of the generated photons.

III
Spontaneous four-wave-mixing in fibers

We now turn to the generation of OAM states through spontaneous FWM in a silicon fiber. We first consider the general description of the FWM process in a system without the helical grating (Subsection
III.1
)
using both the hybrid as well as the OAM modes as basis states for the generated signal and idler photons.
Then, we discuss how laser-written gratings can effectively couple the optical modes (Subsection
III.2
), modify the dispersion, and thus increase the efficiency of the OAM mode generation process.

III.1
Four-wave mixing in fibers without gratings

The nonlinear FWM process under consideration is depicted in Fig.

\omega_{2}

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{{}_{\text{NL}}}(t)=\varepsilon_{0}\int_{V}\mathrm{d}^{3}\textbf{r}\ \sum_{\mu\nu\tau\rho}\chi_{\mu\nu\tau\rho}^{(3)}

\displaystyle\times\left(\textbf{E}_{1}^{(+)}\right)_{\mu}\,\left(\textbf{E}_{2}^{(+)}\right)_{\nu}\,\left(\hat{\textbf{E}}_{s}^{(-)}\right)_{\tau}\,\left(\hat{\textbf{E}}_{i}^{(-)}\right)_{\rho}\,+\;{\textup{h.c.}},

(11)

Both pump beams are considered to be in the

To describe the generated signal and idler photons, we can embrace any convenient basis of modes of the fibers. III.1.1
Formulation of the FWM in hybrid modes

In a fiber without gratings, the signal and idler photons can be generated not only in the fundamental modes, but in various higher-order modes
\mathbf{HE}_{m+1}^{({\rm e/o})}

and
\mathbf{EH}_{m^{\prime}-1}^{({\rm e/o})}

where

m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}

The efficiency of such a process is defined by the phase-matching dictated by the longitudinal (

\displaystyle I^{m_{s},{\rm e/o};m_{i},{\rm e/o}}_{\text{HE,HE}}=\sum_{\mu\nu\tau\rho}\;\chi^{(3)}_{\mu\nu\tau\rho}\iint\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}

\displaystyle\times\left(\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{{\text{HE}_{1}^{({\rm e})}}}\right)_{\!\mu}\left(\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{{\text{HE}_{1}^{({\rm e})}}}\right)_{\!\nu}\left(\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{{\text{HE}_{m_{s}+1}^{({\rm e/o})}}}\,\right)^{\!*}_{\!\tau}\left(\bm{\mathcal{E}}^{{\text{HE}_{m_{i}+1}^{({\rm e/o})}}}\,\right)^{\!*}_{\!\rho},

(12)

using the notation convention of Eq. (
1

or
\mathbf{HE}_{m_{s}+1}^{({\rm o})}\mathbf{HE}_{m_{i}+1}^{({\rm o})}

Moreover, the probabilities of generating the

III.2
Helical grating

We now show how the presence of a helical, transmission grating
[
45
]
in the fiber can modify the conservation rules discussed above, allowing the generation higher-order OAM modes.
A sketch of a cylindrical step-index waveguide with a helical transmission grating and topological charges
m_{g}=1
and
m_{g}=2
is presented in Fig.
1
(c), where
\Lambda_{\mathrm{t}}
is the grating period. The grating couples light of wavevector
k_{\text{in}}
with light of
k_{\text{out}}=k_{\text{in}}-2\pi/\Lambda_{\mathrm{t}}
. Current technologies
[
46
,
47
]
allow to create a helical change of the refractive index in a waveguide core with a desired grating period and a non-zero topological charge. Such helical gratings can transform the fundamental waveguide mode
\mathbf{HE}_{1,1}^{({\rm e/o})}
into modes with non-zero orbital angular momentum with high efficiency.

We suppose that the waveguide core is characterized by a periodic change of its dielectric constant

\Delta\varepsilon=\Delta\varepsilon_{0}\cos(K_\tx{t} z - m_g \phi)\,\Theta(a-r),

(16)

where
\Delta\varepsilon_{0}
represents the modulation amplitude,
m_{g}
is the topological charge of the grating,
a
is the core radius and
K_{\text{t}}=2\pi/\Lambda_{\text{t}}
. The step-function
\Theta(a-r)
indicates that the modulation of the permittivity is only applied to the core. Such a permittivity change leads to a continuous azimuthal variation of the refractive index along
z
.

Furthermore, since the helical gratings do not exhibit radial variation, we expect them not to couple orthogonal modes with different radial numbers
n
.

III.2.1
Grating-controlled coupling between OAM modes

III.3
Asymptotic-in and -out formalism

As photons are being generated through the FWM and propagate along the fiber, they are being continuously and selectively converted between OAM modes by the helical grating. To describe the spatial distribution of the signal and idler photons, we embrace the
asymptotic-input and output modes
formalism developed by Liscidini
et al.

[
42
]
. Within this approach, the helical gratings define the grating modes of the signal (idler) photons, made up of oscillating superpositions of the modes with
m
and
m^{\prime}
OAM charges. To complete the definition of these grating modes, we impose boundary conditions which state that the
photons emerge from the waveguide with either a dominating
\mathbf{O}^{\sigma_{s}}_{m_{s}}

\quantity(\mathbf{O}^{\sigma_i}_{-m_i})
or
\mathbf{O}^{\sigma^{\prime}_{s}}_{m^{\prime}_{s}}

\quantity(\mathbf{O}^{\sigma'_i}_{-m'_i})
mode profile
. The polarizations of these coupled modes are set to be the same throughout this subsection (i.e.
\sigma_{s}=\sigma^{\prime}_{s}
and
\sigma_{i}=\sigma^{\prime}_{i}
), and the primed orbital numbers
m_{s}^{\prime}
and
m_{i}^{\prime}
will be uniquely determined by the grating structure according to the selection rule (
18a
).

III.3.1
Asymptotic-out modes of the signal photon

We first consider the signal photon propagating in the
+z
direction, and emerging from the right end of the waveguide (
z=L
) in mode
(m_{s},\sigma_{s})
, after it oscillates between the
(m_{s},\sigma_{s})
and
(m_{s}^{\prime},\sigma_{s})
modes due to the grating.
The corresponding electric field operator
\hat{\bf E}_{s}^{(m_{s},\sigma_{s})}
of that asymptotic-out mode is

\displaystyle\hat{\bf E}_{s}^{(m_{s},\sigma_{s})}(\mathbf{r},t)

\displaystyle=\ \int\textup{d}\omega_{s}\ \mathcal{N}_{s}(\omega_{s})\Bigg[a_{m_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)\;\bm{\mathcal{O}}_{m_{s}}^{\sigma_{s}}(r,\phi)\;e^{ik_{s}^{m_{s}}(\omega_{s})z}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+a_{m_{s}^{\prime}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)\;\bm{\mathcal{O}}_{m_{s}^{\prime}}^{\sigma_{s}}(r,\phi)\;e^{ik_{s}^{m_{s}^{\prime}}(\omega_{s})z}\Bigg]\ \hat{b}_{s}^{\text{O}_{m_{s}}^{\sigma_{s}}}(\omega_{s})\;e^{-i\omega_{s}t}\,+\textup{h.c.},

\displaystyle=\ \int\textup{d}\omega_{s}\ \mathcal{N}_{s}(\omega_{s})\Bigg[\sum_{y_{s}=m_{s},m^{\prime}_{s}}a_{y_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)\;\bm{\mathcal{O}}_{y_{s}}^{\sigma_{s}}(r,\phi)\;e^{ik_{s}^{y_{s}}(\omega_{s})z}\Bigg]\ \hat{b}_{s}^{\text{O}_{m_{s}}^{\sigma_{s}}}(\omega_{s})\;e^{-i\omega_{s}t}\,+\textup{h.c.},

(19)

where the operator
\quantity(\hat{b}_s^{\mathrm{O}_{m_s}^{\sigma_s}})^{\!\dagger}
describes the creation of the signal photon with topological charge
m_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
at the output of the fiber. In the above formulation, we implicitly assume that all the functions in the integrand are parametrically dependent on
\omega_{s}
.

Considering the transverse modes normalized to the Pointing vector according to Eq. (
8
), the field normalization factor is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{s}(\omega_{s})=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\,\omega_{s}}{\pi\,(\varepsilon_{co}+\varepsilon_{cl})}},

(20)

where the expression from Ref.
[
49
]
was applied taking into account that in our case
S_{\mathrm{eff}_{s}}=\frac{(\varepsilon_{co}+\varepsilon_{cl})n_{\text{eff}_{s}}(\omega_{s})}{4\varepsilon_{0}c}
with
n_{\text{eff}_{s}}(\omega_{s})
labels the frequency-dependent effective refractive index of mode.

In Eq. (
19
), the slowly varying amplitudes
a_{m_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)
and
a_{m^{\prime}_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)
are defined as solutions to the grating-coupled mode equations:

\displaystyle i\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}a_{m_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)+\frac{\omega_{s}-\omega_{t,s}}{v_{g}^{m_{s}}}\;a_{m_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)+\kappa_{s}\;a_{m^{\prime}_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)\;e^{i\quantity[ k_s^{m'_s}(\omega_s) -k_s^{m_s}(\omega_s)-K_s]z}=0,

(21a)

\displaystyle i\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}z}a_{m^{\prime}_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)+\frac{\omega_{s}-\omega_{t,s}}{v_{g}^{m^{\prime}_{s}}}\;a_{m^{\prime}_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)+\kappa_{s}^{*}\;a_{m_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)\;e^{-i\quantity[k_s^{m'_s}(\omega_s) -k_s^{m_s}(\omega_s)-K_s]z}=0.

(21b)

In the above, we simplify the notation using
\kappa_{s}
as the appropriate coupling coefficient defined in Eq. (
17
). We also account for both the frequency detuning between the transmission grating frequency
\omega_{t,s}
and the signal photon frequency
\omega_{s}
,
\delta_{s}=\omega_{t,s}-\omega_{s}
, and the momentum detuning
2d_{s}=\delta_{s}\quantity(1/v_{g}^{m'_s}-1/v_{g}^{m_s})+K_{s}+k_{s}^{m_{s}}(\omega_{s})-k_{s}^{m^{\prime}_{s}}(\omega_{s})
, using the group velocities
v_{g}^{m_{s}/m^{\prime}_{s}}
at the signal frequency
\omega_{s}
.

To identify the asymptotic-out modes associated with the generation of signal photon in the
\mathbf{O}^{\sigma_{s}}_{m_{s}}
mode, we add the boundary conditions
a_{m_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z=L)=1
and
a_{m^{\prime}_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z=L)=0
, and find the general solutions for the mode amplitudes:

\displaystyle a_{m_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{i(L-z)\quantity(d_s+\delta_s/v_g^{m_s})}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\left\{\cos[\gamma_{s}(L-z)]-i\frac{d_{s}}{\gamma_{s}}\sin\quantity[\gamma_s(L-z)]\right\},

(22a)

\displaystyle a_{m^{\prime}_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}}(z)

\displaystyle=-i\,\frac{\kappa_{s}^{*}}{\gamma_{s}}\,e^{i\quantity[(L+z) d_s-\delta_s\qty(z/v_g^{m'_s}-L/v_g^{m_s})]}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\sin[\gamma_{s}(L-z)],

(22b)

​​where

\gamma_{s}=\sqrt{d_{s}^{2}+|\kappa_{s}|^{2}}.

(23)

We should note that the exact, constant phase of the envelope functions is irrelevant for calculating the joint spectral intensity (JSI), since it relies on the arbitrary choice of time
t
, and we do not consider any interferences between competing nonlinear processes.

In the above model, we assumed that the signal photon would oscillate between modes with topological charge
m_{s}
and
m_{s}^{\prime}
(where
m_{s}^{\prime}=m_{s}+m_{g}
), and emerge in the former mode. To complement this description, we define operator
\hat{\bf E}_{s}^{(m_{s}^{\prime},\sigma_{s})}
similarly to Eq. (
19
), but which describes the generation of the output photon in the
\mathbf{O}^{\sigma_{s}}_{m^{\prime}_{s}}
mode at the end of the waveguide:

\displaystyle\hat{\bf E}_{s}^{(m_{s}^{\prime},\sigma_{s})}(\mathbf{r},t)

\displaystyle=\ \int\textup{d}\omega_{s}\ \mathcal{N}_{s}(\omega_{s})\Bigg[a_{m_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}^{\prime}}(z)\;\bm{\mathcal{O}}_{m_{s}}^{\sigma_{s}}(r,\phi)\;e^{ik_{s}^{m_{s}}(\omega_{s})z}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+a_{m_{s}^{\prime}\rightarrow m_{s}^{\prime}}(z)\;\bm{\mathcal{O}}_{m_{s}^{\prime}}^{\sigma_{s}}(r,\phi)\;e^{ik_{s}^{m_{s}^{\prime}}(\omega_{s})z}\Bigg]\ \hat{b}_{s}^{\text{O}_{m_{s}^{\prime}}^{\sigma_{s}}}(\omega_{s})\;e^{-i\omega_{s}t}\,+\textup{h.c.}

\displaystyle=\ \int\textup{d}\omega_{s}\ \mathcal{N}_{s}(\omega_{s})\Bigg[\sum_{y_{s}=m_{s},m^{\prime}_{s}}a_{y_{s}\rightarrow m^{\prime}_{s}}(z)\;\bm{\mathcal{O}}_{y_{s}}^{\sigma_{s}}(r,\phi)\;e^{ik_{s}^{y_{s}}(\omega_{s})z}\Bigg]\ \hat{b}_{s}^{\text{O}_{m^{\prime}_{s}}^{\sigma_{s}}}(\omega_{s})\;e^{-i\omega_{s}t}\,+\textup{h.c.}.

(24)

We also modify the boundary conditions:
a_{m_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}^{\prime}}(z=L)=0
and
a_{m^{\prime}_{s}\rightarrow m^{\prime}_{s}}(z=L)=1
in the similarly modified grating-coupled mode equations.
The explicit expressions for the amplitudes
a_{m_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}^{\prime}}(z)
and
a_{m^{\prime}_{s}\rightarrow m_{s}^{\prime}}(z)
of the signal photon are shown in Appendix
C

We acknowledge that this formulation introduces some ambiguity. For example, given the grating number
m_{g}=2
, the operator
\hat{\bf E}_{s}^{(3,+)}
can conceivably describe a signal photon with, either,
m_{s}=3
generated via a coupling with
m_{s}^{\prime}=5

m_{s}=1

24

IV.1
Calculating the Joint Spectral Amplitudes

Spectra of light generated through these nonlinear processes are characterized by the Joint Spectra Amplitudes (JSAs)
\Phi^{x_{s},\sigma_{s};-x_{i},\sigma_{i}}(\omega_{s},\omega_{i})
and
Intensities (JSIs)
\quantity|\Phi^{x_s,\sigma_s;-x_i,\sigma_i}(\omega_s,\omega_i)|^{2}
. Here,
x_{s}\in\{+m_{s},+m^{\prime}_{s}\}
and
x_{i}\in\{-m_{i},-m^{\prime}_{i}\}
describe the topological charge of the emitted signal and idler photons, respectively.

To determine the JSAs, the first-order perturbation in
\hat{H}_{{}_{\mathrm{NL}}}
is used to find the state generated by the FWM process, where we relate the amplitudes of each generated OAM mode pair with the specific JSA:

V
Conclusion

In this work, we theoretically demonstrated an efficient generation of the counterpropagating and polarization entangled photons in OAM modes with non-zero topological charges during the FWM process in a silica fiber with two helical gratings. Topological charges and the helicity of the gratings can be varied to create desired topological charges of OAM modes. Due to opposite propagation directions, such entangled photons can be easily sorted. It was shown that the frequencies of the generated photons have a shift compared to the pump frequencies. Such a shift depends on the topological charge of the grating and allows a simple filtering of the OAM modes from the pump, which is impossible without the grating. We believe that such polarization entangled photons with high-order OAM can be a promising tool for a storage and transferring quantum information, as well as for realization different quantum information protocols.

Neutrophil depletion exacerbates Clonorchis sinensis associated hepatobiliary injury through expansion of Kupffer cells and Ly6Clo monocytes
Authors: Bo Zhang, Xinxin Ren, Jiejie Li, Jingze Sun, Ying Shen, Sihan Liu, Qingqing Ji, Jinman Shang, Chen Zhang, Zhihua Jiang, Qian Yu, Kuiyang Zheng, Chao Yan, Beibei Zhang, Hui Hua
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Neutrophils exert diverse functions in parasitic infections, including pathogen clearance and regulation of inflammation. However, their specific roles in the early stage of infection with Clonorchis sinensis (C. sinensis), a significant foodborne parasite that dwells in the bile ducts, remain largely unknown. In this study, FVB mice were orally infected with 50C. sinensis metacercariae, and neutrophils were depleted using an anti-Ly6G antibody whereas IgG isotype served as the control. Mice were sacrificed 14 days post-infection, and serum and liver tissue were collected to assess hepatobiliary injury. Hepatic leukocyte changes were analyzed by flow cytometry, and associated cytokine/chemokine expression was detected by qPCR. Results showed that neutrophil-depleted mice exhibited significantly aggravated hepatobiliary injury, as evidenced by elevated serum ALT/AST, epithelial hyperplasia, inflammatory infiltration, and ductal dilatation. Neutrophil depletion also exacerbated infection-induced hepatic fibrosis, indicated by increased collagen fiber deposition and increased mRNA levels of Acta2, Col3a1 and Tgfb1. Flow cytometry revealed reduced absolute T cell counts but increased CD45+CD11bloF4/80hi Kupffer cells and CD45+CD11b+Ly6G−Ly6Clo monocytes in neutrophil-depleted infected mice. Additionally, neutrophil depletion increased mRNA levels of Axl, Mertk, Nr4a1, Cx3cr1, Cx3cl1, Tnfα, Il1b, Il10 and Ccl2, while down-regulating Cxcl1 in infected mice. Collectively, these findings indicate a protective role of neutrophils in the early C. sinensis infection in this murine model. Neutrophil depletion induces compensatory expansion of Kupffer cells and Ly6Clo monocytes, forming a pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic microenvironment that accelerates liver damage. The results highlight neutrophils as important modulators of hepatic immune responses during clonorchiasis and point to the need for further investigation into neutrophils-mediated regulation of helminth-associated liver pathology.

Introduction

Clonorchiasis, a significant foodborne parasitic disease caused by the trematode Clonorchis sinensis (C. sinensis), poses a significant public health burden in endemic regions of East and Southeast Asia, with an estimated 15 million people infected worldwide (Qian et al., 2024; Tidman et al., 2023). Chronic infection with C. sinensis causes cholangitis, cholelithiasis, biliary fibrosis, and is also an identified risk factor for cholangiocarcinoma, thereby severely impairing the quality of life of infected individuals and imposing a heavy economic burden on healthcare systems (Kim and Hong, 2025). The persistently high incidence of clonorchiasis in some endemic areas, despite extensive control measures, underscores the need to clarify the immunopathological mechanisms of C. sinensis infection and identify novel therapeutic targets.

Upon C. sinensis infection, the parasite invades the biliary tract and releases excretory-secretory products (CsESPs), which act as key immunogens to trigger a complex host immune response involving innate and adaptive immune cells. Among these cells, neutrophils, as the first line of defense in the innate immune system, are rapidly recruited to the infection site and participate in the initial immune surveillance and clearance of pathogens. However, the exact role of neutrophils in the progression of clonorchiasis, especially in regulating the balance between protective immunity and pathological tissue damage, remains elusive.

Neutrophils exert their various functions through multiple mechanisms, including phagocytosis, degranulation (releasing antimicrobial peptides and proteases like neutrophil elastase and matrix metalloproteinase-8), and the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) (Elangovan et al., 2024). In recent years, accumulating evidence has demonstrated that neutrophils are involved in the immune response against various parasitic infections. In parasitic infections, neutrophils can directly kill parasites or modulate the immune response by secreting cytokines and chemokines; nevertheless, excessive or dysregulated neutrophil activation may contribute to tissue injury and fibrosis (Amulic et al., 2012; Puerta-Arias et al., 2016). However, their role in C. sinensis infection remains poorly understood, due to the lack of specific tools to manipulate neutrophil populations in clonorchiasis models. As a result, the role of neutrophils in C. sinensis-induced hepatobiliary injury has not been fully clarified.

To advance understanding of neutrophil function in C. sinensis infection, we established a neutrophil depletion model in infected mice using anti-Ly6G antibody (Babuta et al., 2024; Daley et al., 2008) to investigate their role in regulating the host immune response and the progression of hepatobiliary lesions. The findings of this study will not only improve our understanding of the role of neutrophils in the immunopathogenesis of clonorchiasis but also provide a basis for exploring potential therapeutic strategies for C. sinensis-associated hepatobiliary diseases.

Materials and methods

Animals, parasites and experimental design

The metacercariae of C. sinensis were isolated from naturally infected Pseudorasbora parva (Yan et al., 2015). A total of 26 specific pathogen free male FVB mice, aged 6–8 weeks, were purchased from Jiangsu GemPharmatech Co., Ltd. [SCXK (SU) 2018–0008] and randomly divided into four groups: the infected, anti-Ly6G treated group (n = 7), and three control groups including infected, isotype control (n = 7); non-infected, anti-Ly6G treated (n = 6); and non-infected, isotype control (n = 6) mice. The mice in the infected groups were orally gavaged with 50C. sinensis metacercariae suspended in 100 μL PBS, whereas those in the non-infected groups were gavaged with 100 μL PBS. On the same day, each mouse was intraperitoneally injected with either anti-Ly6G (Clone 1A8, BE0075, BioXcell, USA) or isotype control IgG antibody (Clone 2A3, BE0089, BioXcell, USA) at a dose of 200 μg (equivalent to 8–10 mg/kg body weight for 20–25 g mice). The same antibody treatment was continued every two days until 14 days post infection (PI), when the mice were sacrificed for sample collection (Babuta et al., 2024; Carai et al., 2023).

The livers and spleens were carefully dissected, rinsed with cold PBS to remove blood, blotted dry with filter paper and weighed immediately using a precise analytical balance. To normalize organ weights relative to individual body weight, the liver index and spleen index were calculated using the following formulas: liver index (%) = [liver weight (g) / body weight (g)] × 100 and spleen index (%) = [spleen weight (g) / body weight (g)] × 100.

Biochemical assays

Mouse serum was collected and the serum levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), total bile acid (TBA), total bilirubin (TBIL), direct bilirubin (DBIL), and indirect bilirubin (IBIL) were measured using a standard automated biochemical analyzer (Cobas 8000 a, ROCHE).

Pathohistological analysis

For histological analysis, sections of the left lobe of the liver were excised and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for at least 24 h. The fixed tissues were then dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, serially sliced to a thickness of 4 μm, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E, G1004, Service bio, Wuhan, China) and Masson's trichrome (G1006, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturers's recommendations. After sealing the slides with neutral adhesive, the stained histological sections were examined under a light microscope (Olympus, Japan) to assess pathological changes. Hepatobiliary pathological alterations were further assessed using the modified hepatobiliary activity index (mHAl), adapted from the Ishak scoring system (Ishak et al., 1995), which evaluates hepatic inflammation, bile duct damage, hepatocellular injury, and fibrosis. The total mHAI score was calculated by summing the grading values of four histological indicators (hepatic inflammation, bile duct damage, hepatocellular injury, and fibrosis), each assessed on a 0–4 ordinal scale with higher scores representing aggravated pathological lesions. The severity spectrum of each index is defined as follows: hepatic inflammation ranges from no inflammatory infiltration to severe inflammatory lesions accompanied by bridging necrosis; bile duct damage spans normal ductal epithelium to severe duct injury with periductal fibrosis; hepatocellular injury varies from absent lesions to widespread hepatocellular necrosis; fibrosis progresses from mild periportal fibrous hyperplasia to complete cirrhosis. For quantitative analysis, six random microscopic fields per liver section were captured at ×100 magnification and analyzed using ImageJ software (NIH, Bethesda, USA). All histological scoring was independently performed by two experienced pathologists who were blinded to the experimental group assignments. Inter-observer reliability was evaluated via intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) analysis, with values >0.85 considered excellent; the final mHAI score for each sample was calculated as the mean of the two independent scorings.

Immunohistochemistry staining

Serial sections of embedded tissue, cut at 4 μm thickness, were used for immunohistochemistry of cytokeratin 19 (CK19), Ki67, alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and CD45. After deparaffinization and rehydration, antigen retrieval was performed by heating sections in citric acid buffer at 95 °C for 15 min. Sections were then blocked with 5% BSA for 30 min and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against CK19 (1:600, ab133496, Abcam, Cambridge, USA); Ki67 (1:600, 12202S, CST, Danvers, MA, USA); α-SMA (1400, 67735–1-Ig, Proteintech, Wuhan, China); and CD45 (1400, 60287–1-Ig, Proteintech, Wuhan, China). After washing with PBS, sections were incubated with DAB substrate (HC301–02, Vazyme Biotech, Nanjing, China). Six random high-power fields (×100 magnification, Olympus, Japan) were analyzed per section, and the percentage of positive areas was quantified with ImageJ (NIH, Bethesda, USA).

Flow cytometry

Following sacrifice, mouse livers were dissected, dissociated and filtered to remove extrahepatic duct. The pellets were resuspended in 40% Percoll, layered onto 70% Percoll and centrifuged at 2500 rpm for 20 mins at room temperature according to the manufacturer's recommendations (17089109, Cytiva, Uppsala, Sweden). The intermediate cell layer was aspirated, resuspended in PBS, and centrifuged to discard the supernatant. After treatment with red blood cell lysis buffer, the cells were resuspended in PBS and blocked with Fc block (anti-mouse CD16/CD32, 1:100, 553141, BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA) for 30 min on ice. The samples were then washed and centrifuged, and stained with the following antibodies: PerCP-Cy5.5 anti-mouse CD45 (103132, Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA), FITC anti-mouse Ly6G (127606, Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA), BV510 anti-mouse CD11b (101263, Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA), PE anti-mouse F4/80 (E-AB-F0995UD, Elabscience Biotechnology, Wuhan, China), APC-Cy7 fixable viability dye (423105, Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA) and APC anti-mouse Ly6C (128016, Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA). Flow cytometry (FACS Canto II, BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA) was utilized to assess immune cell expression. Beads (E143377, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) were added to perform absolute counting and the results were analyzed using FlowJo software (TreeStar, Ashland, OR, USA).

RNA extraction and qPCR analysis

Total RNA from liver tissues was extracted using the TRIzol reagent (DP424, TIANGEN Biotech, Beijing, China), following the manufacturer's instructions, and reverse transcribed into cDNA using the miRcute Plus miRNA First-Strand cDNA Kit (11141ES60, Yeasen Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) was then performed using the SYBR Green Master Mix (11184ES08, Yeasen Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) on a StepOne Plus Real-Time PCR System (Roche Applied Science, Mannheim, Germany). Relative mRNA expression levels were normalized to β-actin using the 2 −△△Ct method. Statistical analysis

All data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 21.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA). One-way ANOVA was used to compare differences among more than two groups, followed by the Least Significant Difference (LSD) test unless otherwise stated. For comparisons between two groups, differences were evaluated using a two-tailed Student's t-test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Neutrophil depletion aggravates liver injury induced by C. sinensis infection

We first investigated neutrophils recruitment in the livers of mice infected with C. sinensis at day 14 PI and found that they were significantly increased in C. sinensis infected mice compared with normal controls (Fig. 1A, P < 0.05). H&E staining showed massive recruitment of neutrophil-like cells in the lesion (Fig. 1B, red arrows). Then, we used anti-Ly6G antibody to deplete neutrophils and investigate their role in the pathogenesis of C. sinensis infection at 14 days PI. Flow cytometry showed that neutrophil percentages and numbers in the livers of anti-Ly6G treated mice were significantly decreased compared with isotype controls in both infected and non-infected mice, demonstrating effective depletion by anti-Ly6G (Fig. 1C-1D, P < 0.001). We found no obvious changes either in the liver or in the spleen in the two non-infected groups. However, the livers of the infected mice appeared dark in colour, tough in texture, and irregular in margin. Multiple white nodules were observed specially in the left lobe (Fig. 1E). In addition, compared with the non-infected groups, both the liver and the spleen indices were significantly increased in C. sinensis-infected mice (Fig. 1E-1F, P < 0.001). Moreover, relative to the infected isotype-treated group, the spleen index increased significantly in the infected anti-Ly6G group (Fig. 1F, P < 0.001), while no significant difference was observed in the liver index (Fig. 1E). Furthermore, we found that serum concentrations of all analyzed biochemical markers including ALT, AST, ALP, TBA, TBIL and DBIL were significantly higher in infected mice than in uninfected controls. Among the infected mice, these levels were further increased in the anti-Ly6G-treated group compared with the isotype-treated mice (Fig. 1G, P < 0.05 or P < 0.01). Neutrophil depletion exacerbates injury of hepatobiliary duct following C. sinensis infection

To evaluate the effects of neutrophil depletion on biliary damage caused by C. sinensis infection in mice, we assessed histopathological changes and biliary hyperplasia in liver tissues. As shown in Fig. 2A-2B, C. sinensis infection induced bile duct hyperplasia, inflammatory cell infiltration, and fibrous formation. These pathological lesions were more severe in neutrophil-depleted mice (Fig. 2B, P < 0.05). Based on CK19 and Ki67 staining, proliferation of biliary epithelial cells (BECs) was significantly increased in neutrophil-depleted mice compared with the isotype controls (Fig. 2C-2F, P < 0.05 or P < 0.01). In addition, changes in CD45 positive cells (representing inflammatory cells) showed a similar trend between the two infected groups (Fig. 2G-2H, P < 0.05). Neutrophil depletion exacerbates liver fibrosis caused by C. sinensis infection

Masson staining revealed no obvious collagen deposition in the non-infected groups. In contrast, C. sinensis infection triggered collagen deposition mainly around the bile ducts. Such peribiliary fibrosis was significantly aggravated in infected mice treated with anti-Ly6G (Fig. 3A-3B, P < 0.05). IHC staining showed that α-SMA (a fibrosis marker) was markedly increased in C. sinensis infected mice, and the staining was more pronounced in the anti-Ly6G group (Fig. 3C-3D, P < 0.05). We further analyzed the mRNA levels of fibrosis-related genes, including Acta2, Col3a1 and Tgfb1, in mice livers using qPCR. All these genes showed increased expression levels during infection, with a more pronounced elevation in infected neutrophil-depleted mice (Fig. 3E, P < 0.05 or P < 0.01). Compared with uninfected mice, the absolute number of CD45+CD11b+ myeloid cells was significantly increased in infected mice (Fig. 4E-4F, P < 0.01). Moreover, this elevation was further enhanced following neutrophil depletion (Fig. 4F, P < 0.05). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that neutrophil depletion elicits distinct alterations in hepatic immune cell profiles. In particular, the differential changes of KC and Ly6Clo monocytes may contribute critically to the exacerbated pathology observed during clonorchiasis in neutrophil-depleted mice.

Neutrophil depletion alters monocyte/macrophage activation and inflammatory gene expression in the infected liver

qPCR was performed to assess mRNA levels of signature genes associated with KCs and Ly6Clo monocytes, as well as key pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines. Compared with the isotype-treated infected group, the anti-Ly6G-treated infected group exhibited significantly upregulated mRNA levels of Axl and Mertk (both highly expressed in hepatic KCs), Nr4a1 (a key transcription factor for Ly6Clo monocytes), Cx3cr1 (expressed by both KCs and Ly6Clo monocytes), and Cx3cl1 (the ligand of CX3CR1, mediating the recruitment of CX3CR1 positive KCs and Ly6Clo monocytes (Fig. 5A-5E, P < 0.05). As for macrophage polarization and inflammation genes, the M2 macrophage marker Arg1 was significantly downregulated (Fig. 5F, P < 0.01) in both infected groups relative to non-infected controls. Conversely, the M1 macrophage marker Nos2 was markedly elevated in the infected groups (Fig. 5G, P < 0.01). Discussion

This study reveals that neutrophil depletion significantly aggravates the disease course during the early phase of C. sinensis infection in FVB mice. As previously reported in murine models of schistosomiasis, neutrophils secrete cytokines and chemokines to modulate host immunity at primary infection sites and have been proposed to participate in hepatic fibrotic granuloma formation around eggs (Burke et al., 2010; Sutherland and Van den Steen, 2022). In hookworm infection, particularly at the infective larval stage, neutrophils can release NETs upon encountering parasites, thereby reducing worm fitness (Bouchery et al., 2020). In malaria, neutrophils mediate antimalarial immunity via phagocytosis, reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation and NET formation. However, NETs and degranulation can also exacerbate malaria-associated complications (Babatunde and Adenuga, 2022). Our results align with emerging evidence from these parasitic infections, and support an important immunomodulatory role for neutrophils during early C. sinensis infection.

In the present study, we observed that C. sinensis infection induces marked neutrophils infiltration into the bile ducts and surrounding tissues. Flow cytometric analysis confirmed a significant elevation in hepatic neutrophils numbers. To assess the functional role of these infiltrating neutrophils, we employed an anti-Ly6G monoclonal antibody (clone 1A8) to selectively deplete them in mice. This reagent is widely utilized to dissect the functional role of neutrophils during host-parasite interactions, as it circumvents the cross-reactivity associated with anti-Gr1 antibodies (Boivin et al., 2020; Daley et al., 2008). Notably, this approach has been extensively applied to characterize the in vivo functions of neutrophils in immune responses against infections with Toxoplasma gondii (Dunay et al., 2010), Leishmania amazonensis (Sousa et al., 2014), and Paracoccidioides brasiliensis (Puerta-Arias et al., 2016).

In this study, neutrophil-depleted mice exhibited exacerbated liver injury compared with infected mice retaining intact neutrophil function, as reflected by elevated liver function markers (including ALT, AST and DBIL etc), more severe bile duct damage, and accelerated hepatic fibrosis. These findings demonstrate that neutrophil depletion aggravates C. sinensis induced liver damage, which aligns with the protective role of neutrophils documented in other parasitic infections, such as Schistosoma japonicum infection (Chuah et al., 2013., Zheng et al., 2017). It is generally postulated that neutrophils are recruited to the infection site during the early stage of infection to phagocytose parasites, clear apoptotic cells, and constrain excessive inflammatory response (Carai et al., 2023). During C. sinensis infection, the larvae migrate through the hepatic parenchyma and settle in the bile ducts, leading to mechanical damage and colonization of the ducts, which causes tissue injury and elicits local inflammatory reactions (Kim et al., 2016). Neutrophils may mitigate this direct damage by phagocytosing parasite-derived antigens and debris, as well as by modulating the local inflammatory milieu (Tang et al., 2021). Conversely, when neutrophils are depleted, uncontrolled biliary epithelial injury ensues. This injury promotes biliary hyperplasia, activates hepatic stellate cells and drives the progression of liver fibrosis (Wang et al., 2023).

To explore the underlying immunological mechanisms, we performed flow cytometric analysis of hepatic immune cell subsets. Neutrophil depletion resulted in a marked increase in KCs and Ly6Clo monocytes, accompanied by a reduction in T cell numbers. As resident liver macrophages, KCs exert a dual role in liver injury: they eliminate pathogens and apoptotic cells, whereas excessive activation leads to the release of abundant pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic mediators, thereby exacerbating hepatic damage (Li et al., 2022a). Ly6Clo monocytes are mainly resident or repair type monocytes/macrophages, which are involved in tissue repair and immune homeostasis (Bibi et al., 2025; Li et al., 2022b). The expansion of these two cell populations following neutrophil depletion may constitute a host compensatory response to aggravated liver injury, in which the host attempts to recruit additional resident macrophages to clear apoptotic cells and debris and to restrain excessive inflammation (Cao et al., 2023). Nevertheless, this compensatory response is insufficient to counteract the severe hepatic injury caused by neutrophil depletion. Instead, the expansion of KCs amplifies their pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic activities, directly driving the progression of liver fibrosis. Similarly, the expanded Ly6Clo monocytes may lose their repair capacity under the pathological microenvironment induced by neutrophil depletion, thereby failing to mitigate bile duct damage and even exacerbating it. In addition, the reduction in T cell numbers may contribute to exacerbated liver injury and cholestasis (Tyc et al., 2023). Severe hepatic damage can create an immune-suppressive microenvironment, while excessive inflammatory stress and apoptotic signaling further impair T cell survival and infiltration. As a result, the adaptive immune response against C. sinensis infection is weakened, promoting persistent infection and worsening hepatobiliary injury (Koda et al., 2021).

To investigate potential molecular pathways, expression of key genes was assessed. Axl and Mertk are closely linked to KC function, serving as pivotal receptors governing efferocytosis and immunomodulation. Their elevated expression in the anti-Ly6G treated infected mice suggests aberrant phagocytic activity and dysregulated resolution of inflammation, which may facilitate persistent hepatic injury and progressive fibrogenesis (Li et al., 2025; She et al., 2023; Zizzo and Cohen, 2023). KCs physiologically express moderate levels of Axl and Mertk to maintain efferocytic homeostasis and prevent excessive inflammation. Overexpression indicates impaired clearance of apoptotic cells and biliary epithelial cells, thereby amplifying inflammatory dysregulation and pathological injury. Furthermore, upregulated expression of Nr4a1 and Cx3cr1/Cx3cl1 supports the accumulation of a distinctive macrophage subset characterized by prolonged tissue retention and chronic inflammatory activation (Jeong et al., 2025; Widiapradja et al., 2024; Yashchenko et al., 2023). Ly6Clo monocytes, a subset associated with anti-inflammatory and tissue repair phenotypes, rely on Nr4a1 for their differentiation and survival, while the Cx3cr1/Cx3cl1 axis mediates their tissue retention and interaction with KCs, collectively promoting chronic inflammatory infiltration and fibrogenic progression. The expression of Nos2 and Arg1, the canonical phenotypic markers for M1 and M2 macrophages respectively, showed no significant differences between the two infected groups, whether neutrophil-depleted or not. This result demonstrates that classical M1/M2 polarization is not the predominant mechanism accounting for the aggravated pathology in this infection model. Instead, exacerbation of liver injury and fibrosis are primarily driven by the expansion of hepatic macrophages and their functional reprogramming toward a pro-inflammatory and profibrotic phenotype, rather than conventional polarization toward M1 or M2 subsets (Mooring et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2019).

Consistent with aggravated liver injury and inflammation, the expression levels of Tnfa and Il1b were significantly elevated in the infected neutrophil-depleted mice compared with the isotype controls. As the first line of host defense, neutrophils normally phagocytose parasitic antigens and modulate the activation of KCs/monocytes. Their depletion may lead to uncontrolled activation of the innate immune system, resulting in massive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Holub et al., 2009; Mantovani et al., 2011). The concurrent upregulation of Il10 should not be interpreted as an attenuation of hepatic inflammation, but rather as a robust negative feedback response triggered by the overwhelming pro-inflammatory milieu (Ng et al., 2013). IL-10 is a key immunoregulatory cytokine produced by multiple cell types, including macrophages, regulatory T cells and even neutrophils themselves, in response to sustained inflammatory signals (Hackstein et al., 2023). Its upregulation likely represents a compensatory anti-inflammatory mechanism attempting to counterbalance the excessive production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-1β (Khan et al., 2021). However, the elevated Il10 expression observed in our study appears insufficient to resolve ongoing tissue injury, possibly due to the concurrent expansion of pro-inflammatory myeloid populations and the establishment of a persistent inflammatory-fibrotic loop. This interpretation is consistent with studies showing that Il10 upregulation in chronic liver diseases often reflects a failed attempt at immune regulation rather than effective resolution of inflammation (Hackstein et al., 2023; Solleiro-Villavicencio et al., 2026). The elevation of these factors suggests that neutrophil depletion initiates a vicious cycle of hepatic inflammation and fibrosis. Furthermore, the increased Ccl2 expression likely promotes the recruitment of inflammatory monocytes, further amplifying myeloid cell accumulation and inflammatory responses (Kumar et al., 2025). In contrast, the expression of Cxcl1, a key chemokine mediating neutrophil recruitment, was significantly reduced in the infected neutrophil-depleted mice. Neutrophils serve as the major source and critical regulator of Cxcl1 during inflammation. They secrete Cxcl1 to recruit neutrophils via autocrine/paracrine signaling, and neutrophil depletion disrupts this crosstalk and thus reduces Cxcl1 expression (Girbl et al., 2018; Monickaraj et al., 2023).

This study has several limitations that should be noted. First, neutrophil depletion was achieved solely with anti-Ly6G antibody, which may not fully capture the complexity of neutrophil function. Second, the observations remain correlative, as selective depletion or blockade of myeloid subsets was not performed to establish causality. Finally, this study did not include subset-specific analyses of hepatic immune cells, which limits precise characterization of their molecular functions within the liver microenvironment. Future studies employing complementary genetic models, refined cell isolation, and functional assays are needed to address these gaps and further clarify the mechanisms underlying exacerbated tissue injury.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrates that neutrophil depletion exacerbates the progression of clonorchiasis, supporting that neutrophils exert a hepatoprotective role and are indispensable for alleviating liver injury and biliary fibrosis during the early stage of C. sinensis infection. These findings not only delineate the functional role of neutrophils in clonorchiasis but also provide novel insights into the immunoregulatory mechanisms underlying liver injury and fibrosis in helminth-induced disease.

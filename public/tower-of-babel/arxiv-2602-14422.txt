Integral Transformations for Conformally Invariant Celestial Gluon Amplitudes Journal: Nuclear Physics B
Aphiwat Yuenyong, Pongwit Srisangyingcharoen, Ekapong Hirunsirisawat, Tanapat Deesuwan

Abstract
We propose an integral transformation for celestial gluon amplitudes that maps the celestial coordinates ( z i , z ¯ i ) (z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}) to a new set of complex variables ( s i , s ¯ i ) (s_{i},\bar{s}_{i}) , inspired by the structure of closed string scattering amplitudes. A consistent inverse transformation is constructed by regulating a divergence associated with translational redundancy and absorbing it into an overall normalization. Applying this transformation to celestial MHV amplitudes, we derive constraints on ( s i , s ¯ i ) (s_{i},\bar{s}_{i}) for three-, four-, and general n n -point amplitudes, and show that these conditions are necessary for invariance under global conformal transformations.

Introduction

Celestial holography proposes a duality between four-dimensional asymptotically
flat spacetimes and a two-dimensional conformal field theory living on the
celestial sphere at null infinity
[
31
,
23
,
11
]
. In this framework, four-dimensional
scattering amplitudes can be mapped onto the celestial sphere via the Mellin
transformation over the external particle energies, which turns momentum eigenstates into boost eigenstates. The resulting celestial amplitudes behave as conformal
correlation functions and transform covariantly under global conformal
transformations
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
. A central feature of celestial holography is that soft theorems of the bulk theory are reinterpreted as Ward identities of the dual conformal field theory associated with asymptotic symmetries at null infinity
[
30
,
29
]
.

Scattering amplitudes play a central role in quantum field theory, serving as a
bridge between theoretical predictions and experimental observations. Within the celestial framework, tree-level
celestial gluon amplitudes were first derived in
[
21
]
for
low-point cases. Subsequently, it was generalized to arbitrary
n
-point in
[
33
]
. Furthermore, tree-level string
scattering amplitudes can be mapped onto the celestial sphere, as shown in

[
28
]
. At the one-loop level, celestial open- and closed-string
amplitudes were formulated in

[
13
,
8
]
. Many attempts have been made to reveal the structural properties of celestial amplitudes, including the on-shell recursion relations
[
21
]
, double copy structures

[
9
]
, and the monodromy relations
[
1
]
.

String scattering amplitudes exhibit remarkable structural relations as a consequence of conformal invariance on the worldsheet. Notably, the Kawai–Lewellen–Tye (KLT) relations allow closed-string amplitudes to be factorized into products of open-string amplitudes
[
17
]
. In the low-energy limit, these relations reduce to the celebrated double-copy relations connecting gauge theory and gravity amplitudes
[
5
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

The worldsheet integrals appearing in string amplitudes play a central role in establishing such structural relations. Examples include monodromy relations among color-ordered open-string amplitudes
[
6
]
, relations expressing mixed open–closed string amplitudes in terms of purely open-string ones
[
26
,
27
]
, connections between closed-string and mixed-string amplitudes
[

]
, and string on-shell recursion relations
[

,

,
25
,
24

.

Nevertheless, these string-theoretic techniques cannot be directly applied to celestial amplitudes, as the two frameworks differ in an essential way. Celestial amplitudes are not strictly conformally invariant; rather, they are two-dimensional correlation functions that transform covariantly under conformal transformations on the celestial sphere. In contrast, string amplitudes enjoy exact conformal invariance on the two-dimensional worldsheet. This distinction motivates the search for a framework capable of constructing conformally invariant celestial amplitudes. In this work, we propose an integral transformation that resembles the structure of worldsheet integrals and provides a pathway toward such a construction.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Scattering amplitudes

A general form of an
n
-point scattering amplitude is given by

\displaystyle A_{l_{1}l_{2}\dots l_{n}}(k_{j}^{\mu})=\delta^{(4)}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}^{\mu}\right)A^{\text{YM}}_{l_{1}l_{2}\dots l_{n}}\,

(1)

where
l_{i}
labels helicities of external particles. The four-momentum of a massless external particle can be parametrized as

\displaystyle k_{j}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{j}\omega_{j}q_{j}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{j}\omega_{j}}{2}\bigl(1+|z_{j}|^{2},\,z_{j}+\bar{z}_{j},\,-i(z_{j}-\bar{z}_{j}),\,1-|z_{j}|^{2}\bigr),

(2)

where
z_{j}
and
\bar{z}_{j}
are the complex coordinates on the celestial sphere,
q^{\mu}_{j}
is a null vector pointing toward the celestial sphere,
\omega_{j}
is the angular frequency associated with the energy of the external particle, and
\epsilon_{j}=\pm 1
denotes outgoing and incoming particles, respectively.

In this paper, we focus on the maximal helicity violating (MHV) gluon scattering amplitudes in pure Yang-Mills (YM) theory. The corresponding
n
-point amplitudes
A_{l_{1}l_{2}\dots l_{n}}^{\text{YM}}
are given by the Parke–Taylor formula
[
19
]
,

\displaystyle A_{1^{+}2^{+}\dots i^{-}\dots j^{-}\dots n^{+}}^{\text{YM}}=\frac{\langle ij\rangle^{4}}{\langle 12\rangle\langle 23\rangle\langle 34\rangle\cdots\langle n1\rangle},

(3)

where
\langle ij\rangle
denotes the spinor inner product. The spinor products can be expressed in terms of the complex coordinates
and energies as

\displaystyle\langle ij\rangle=\sqrt{\omega_{i}\omega_{j}}\,z_{ij},\qquad[ij]=-\sqrt{\omega_{i}\omega_{j}}\,\bar{z}_{ij},

(4)

with
z_{ij}\equiv z_{i}-z_{j}
and

\bar{z}_{ij}\equiv\bar{z}_{i}-\bar{z}_{j}
. The Mandelstam variables read

s_{ij}=2k_{i}\cdot k_{j}=\langle ij\rangle[ji]=\omega_{i}\omega_{j}z_{ij}\bar{z}_{ij}.

(5)

2.2
Celestial amplitudes

The celestial amplitude for massless particles is obtained by applying a Mellin
transformation to momentum-space scattering amplitudes

[
21
,
22
]
. The
n
-point celestial amplitude for
massless particles is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}(\lambda_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}d\omega_{j}\,\omega_{j}^{\Delta_{j}-1}A_{n}(\omega_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})

(6)

where
\Delta_{j}
is conformal dimension, which is equal to
1+i\lambda_{j}
,
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
. The Mellin transformation maps plane-wave solutions in energy space to conformal primary wavefunctions on the celestial sphere.
Accordingly, the celestial amplitude

\mathcal{A}_{n}(\lambda_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})
exhibits the structure of a conformal
correlation function on the celestial sphere. Under global conformal transformation,

\displaystyle z_{j}\to z^{\prime}_{j}=\frac{az_{j}+b}{cz_{j}+d}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{z}_{j}\to\bar{z}_{j}=\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}_{j}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}_{j}+\bar{d}},

(7)

where
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{C}
and
ad-bc=1
, the celestial amplitude transforms covariantly as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{J_{1}\dots J_{n}}\!\left(\lambda_{j},\frac{az_{j}+b}{cz_{j}+d},\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}_{j}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}_{j}+\bar{d}}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(cz_{j}+d)^{\Delta_{j}+J_{j}}

\displaystyle\times(\bar{c}\bar{z}_{j}+\bar{d})^{\Delta_{j}-J_{j}}\mathcal{A}_{J_{1}\dots J_{n}}(\lambda_{j},z_{j},\bar{z}_{j}).

(8)

In four-dimensional Minkowski spacetime with signature
(+---)
, three-point
scattering amplitudes of massless particles vanish as a consequence of momentum
conservation, since the on-shell conditions force all momenta to be collinear.
However, upon analytically continuing to the
(++--)
signature, one may treat
the complex coordinates
z_{j}
and
\bar{z}_{j}
as two linearly independent
variables. This allows for nontrivial kinematics, and in particular the MHV
three-point gluon amplitudes need not vanish. According to the Parke–Taylor
formula, the nonvanishing three-point amplitude takes the form

\displaystyle A_{--+}=\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{1}q_{1}+\omega_{2}q_{2}-\omega_{3}q_{3})\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}{\omega_{3}}\frac{z_{12}^{3}}{z_{13}z_{23}}.

(9)

The momentum conservation in the delta function can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{1}q_{1}+\omega_{2}q_{2}-\omega_{3}q_{3})=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\omega_{3}^{2}z_{23}z_{31}}\delta(\omega_{1}-\frac{z_{32}}{z_{12}}\omega_{3})

\displaystyle\times\delta(\omega_{2}-\frac{z_{31}}{z_{21}}\omega_{3})\delta(\bar{z}_{13})\delta(\bar{z}_{23}).

(10)

Applying the Mellin transformation, it was shown in
[
28
]
that the three-point celestial gluon amplitude is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{--+}=8\pi\delta(\bar{z}_{13})\delta(\bar{z}_{23})\delta(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\lambda_{j})z_{21}^{1-i(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})}z_{23}^{i\lambda_{1}-1}z_{31}^{i\lambda_{2}-1}

(11)

Similarly, the four-point celestial gluon amplitude of the four-point MHV amplitude;

A_{--++}=\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{1}q_{1}+\omega_{2}q_{2}-\omega_{3}q_{3}-\omega_{4}q_{4})\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}{\omega_{3}\omega_{4}}\frac{z_{12}^{3}}{z_{23}z_{34}z_{41}},

(12)

takes the form
[
28
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{--++}=

\displaystyle 8\pi\delta(r-\bar{r})z_{34}^{i\lambda_{2}-2}z_{12}^{-i\lambda_{1}}z_{24}^{i(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3})}z_{23}^{-i(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}

\displaystyle\bar{z}_{34}^{i\lambda_{1}}\bar{z}_{12}^{-i\lambda_{2}-2}\bar{z}_{13}^{-i(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3})}\bar{z}_{14}^{i(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}\Theta(r-1)\delta(\sum_{j=1}^{4}\lambda_{j})

(13)

where the cross ratio
r
is defined as

\displaystyle r\equiv\frac{z_{12}z_{34}}{z_{23}z_{41}}.

(14)

The cross ratio
r
is restricted to the region
r>1
and satisfies the reality condition
r=\bar{r}
.

3
Construction of conformally-invariant celestial amplitudes

In this section, we would like to turn a celestial amplitude conformally invariant. Despite living on different two-dimensional spaces, it is known that a closed string amplitude, which can be captured by an integral
[
15
]

A^{\text{cl}}_{n}=\frac{1}{\text{Vol}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}d^{2}z_{i}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|z_{ij}|^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{i}\cdot k_{j}}z_{ij}^{n_{ij}}\bar{z}_{ij}^{\bar{n}_{ij}},

(15)

is conformally invariant.
n_{ij},\bar{n}_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}
depend on the external string states. We emphasize that the complex coordinates
z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}
appearing here represent worldsheet insertion points and are unrelated to the celestial coordinates introduced earlier.

Motivated by this structure, our goal is to seek an appropriate transformation that maps the celestial variables
(z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})
to the new set of variables
(s_{j},\bar{s}_{j})
such that the new celestial amplitudes become conformally invariant.

3.1
The integral transformation

By inspecting the form of the string amplitudes, we propose the following integral transformation

\phi:f_{n}(z_{ij},\bar{z}_{ij})\longmapsto F_{n}(s_{j},\bar{s}_{j}),

(16)

such that

\displaystyle F_{n}(s_{j},\bar{s}_{j})\equiv\int\prod_{j=1}^{n}d^{2}z_{j}\prod_{0\leq l<k\leq n}z_{lk}^{-1+s_{l}+s_{k}}\bar{z}_{lk}^{-1+\bar{s}_{l}+\bar{s}_{k}}\,f_{n}(z_{lk},\bar{z}_{lk}),

(17)

where
z_{ij}=z_{i}-z_{j}
. Remember that the function
f_{n}(z_{lk},\bar{z}_{lk})
depends only in the differences
z_{ij}
and
\bar{z}_{ij}
.

For this map to be well defined, we require the existence of a corresponding inverse transformation. We postulate the inverse map to take the form

\displaystyle f_{n}(z_{ij},\bar{z}_{ij})\equiv\mathcal{C}_{n}\int\prod_{j=1}^{n}ds_{j}\,d\bar{s}_{j}\prod_{0\leq l<k\leq n}z_{lk}^{-1-s_{l}-s_{k}}\,\bar{z}_{lk}^{-1-\bar{s}_{l}-\bar{s}_{k}}F_{n}(s_{j},\bar{s}_{j}),

(18)

where
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is a normalization factor to be determined. Consistency of the construction requires that the inverse transformation reproduces the original function
f_{n}
when applied to
F_{n}
. To this extent, we start by exploring the lower-point mapping then generalizing it to the
n
-point case.

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{3}\equiv 8\pi\delta(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\lambda_{j})(-1)^{-i\lambda_{1}}\int d^{2}z_{1}d^{2}z_{2}d^{2}z_{3}\,z_{12}^{s_{1}+s_{2}-i(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})}\delta(\bar{z}_{13})

\displaystyle z_{j}=\prod_{l=1}^{j}w_{l}\quad\text{and}\qquad\bar{z}_{j}=\prod_{l=1}^{j}\bar{w}_{l}.

\displaystyle|z|^{2a-2}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-a)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\,t^{-a}e^{-|z|^{2}t}.

This yields

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\,t\int d^{2}w_{1}\,\,e^{-|w_{1}|^{2}t}w_{1}^{2\sum_{j=1}^{3}s_{j}}\bar{w}_{1}^{-1+2\sum_{j=1}^{2}\bar{s}_{j}}

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\,t\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta J}\right)^{\bar{n}}\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta\bar{J}}\right)^{n}\int d^{2}w_{1}e^{-|w_{1}|^{2}t+\bar{w}_{1}J+w_{1}\bar{J}}\bigg|_{J=\bar{J}=0}

5
Conclusions

In this work, we constructed an integral transformation for celestial gluon amplitudes, given in (
17
), which maps the celestial complex coordinates
(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
to a new set of complex variables
(s_{i},\bar{s}_{i})
. This transformation was designed to mimic the structure of closed string amplitudes, which are known to exhibit conformal invariance. For self-consistency, we also defined the corresponding inverse transformation. In constructing this inverse map, a potential divergence—reflecting the translational redundancy inherent in celestial amplitudes—was carefully identified and regulated. This divergence was subsequently absorbed into an overall normalization factor, leading to the well-behaved inverse transformation presented in (
18
).

When performing the given integral transformation on the celestial MHV gluon amplitudes, we derived the constraints on the new variables
(s_{i},\bar{s}_{i})
. These constraints are given in (
56
), (
64
), and (
73
) for the three-, four-, and general
n
-point cases respectively. We demonstrated that these conditions are necessary for the transformed amplitudes to remain invariant under global conformal transformations.

Since the resulting conformally invariant celestial amplitudes are structurally reminiscent of closed string amplitudes, this allows future exploration on implementing techniques from string theory—such as holomorphic factorization—can be applied to analyze these new celestial amplitudes. However, such an extension is not entirely straightforward: the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic coordinates
z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}
are now intertwined through momentum conservation constraints which may lead to structural features that differ from those of conventional string amplitudes. Overall, the construction of these new conformally invariant celestial amplitudes provides a new perspective on the structure of celestial holography and motivates further investigations.

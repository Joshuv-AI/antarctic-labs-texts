Affine isoperimetric type inequalities for static convex domains in hyperbolic space
Yingxiang Hu, Haizhong Li, Yao Wan, Botong Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, the notion of hyperbolic ellipsoids in hyperbolic space is introduced. Using a natural orthogonal projection from hyperbolic space to Euclidean space, we establish affine isoperimetric type inequalities for static convex domains in hyperbolic space. Moreover, equality of such inequalities is characterized by these hyperbolic ellipsoids.

52A40, 53C24, 53A15

1.
Introduction

The classical Minkowski’s second inequality
[
Schneider
, Thm. 7.2.1]
states that if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body (that is, a compact, convex set with non-empty interior) with smooth boundary
\partial K
and
H
is the mean curvature of
\partial K
, then

(1.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Area}(\partial K)^{2}\geq~\frac{n}{n-1}\mathrm{Vol}(K)\int_{\partial K}HdA,

with equality if and only if
K
is a ball. This inequality was later generalized by Reilly
[
Reilly1980
]
to compact Riemannian manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature and convex boundary. Using the generalized Reilly’s formula
[
Qiu-Xia2015
]
, Xia
[
Xia2016
]
proved the following Minkowski type inequalities in hyperbolic space.

Theorem A

(
[
Xia2016
]
)
.

Let
K
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Let
V(x)=\cosh r
, where
r(x)=d(x,p_{0})
is the geodesic distance to a fixed point
p_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Assume that the second fundamental form of
\partial K
satisfies

(1.2)

\displaystyle h_{ij}\geq\frac{V_{,\nu}}{V}g_{ij}.

Then there holds

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\partial K}VdA\right)^{2}\geq~\frac{n}{n-1}\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}\cdot\int_{\partial K}VHdA,

where
d\mathrm{vol}
is the volume element of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Equality holds if and only if
K
is a geodesic ball.

The condition (
1.2
) is called
static convexity
, which was introduced by Brendle and Wang
[
BW2014
]
for its correspondence in static space-time. In particular, when
K
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the weight function
V\equiv 1
and the static convexity (
1.2
) turns out to be the usual convexity (i.e.
h_{ij}\geq 0
), while the inequality (
1.3
) reduces to the classical Minkowski’s second inequality (
1.1
). Moreover, it was proved in
[
Xia2016
]
that the equality in (
1.3
) is attained for all geodesic balls, not necessarily the geodesic balls centered at
p_{0}
.

Another family of sharp geometric inequalities for static convex domains in hyperbolic space was obtained by using locally constrained inverse curvature flows
[
Hu-Li2022
]
.

Theorem B

(
[
Hu-Li2022
]
)
.

Let
K
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Assume that
\partial K
is static convex and starshaped with respect to an interior point
p_{0}
in
K
. For each
k=0,1,\ldots,n
, there holds

(1.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}VH_{k}dA\geq

\displaystyle~n\left(\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}\right)^{\frac{n-1-k}{n}}\left(|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{\frac{2}{n}}+\left(\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\right)^{\frac{k+1}{2}},

where
H_{k}
is the normalized
k
-th mean curvature of the hypersurface
\partial K
.
Equality holds in (
1.4
) if and only if
K
is a geodesic ball centered at
p_{0}
.

For
k=0
, the inequality (
1.4
) can be considered as a weighted isoperimetric inequality. It was first proved by Scheuer and Xia
[
Scheuer-Xia2019
]
, and recently it was generalized to bounded domains with
C^{1}
boundary by Li and Xu
[
Li-Xu22
]
. For
k=1
, the inequality (
1.4
) also holds for starshaped domains with mean convex boundary, see
[
Scheuer-Xia2019
]
. Moreover, the inequality (
1.4
) with
k=1
also holds for merely static convex domains, i.e.,
p_{0}
is not necessarily an interior point of
K
, see
[
BW2014
, Thm. 4, case 4]
. Using the well-known Minkowski’s identity

\displaystyle n\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}=\int_{\partial K}V_{,\nu}dA,

the inequality (
1.4
) with
k=1
can be rewritten as

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}H_{1}(\tilde{\kappa})dA\geq

\displaystyle~n|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}},

where
\tilde{\kappa}_{i}=V\kappa_{i}-V_{,\nu}
and
H_{1}(\tilde{\kappa})=VH_{1}-V_{,\nu}
. This inequality (
1.5
) can be compared with the volumetric Minkowski inequality in Euclidean space, which holds for smooth bounded domain with mean convex boundary, see
[
AFM2022
, Thm. 1.5]
.

As a natural analog of volume functional in Euclidean space, the weighted volume
\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}
appears in the geometric inequalities in hyperbolic space such as (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), as well as the Heintze-Karcher type inequalities in Riemannian manifolds
[
Brendle2013
]
(see also
[
Qiu-Xia2015
,
Li-Xia19
]
). On the other hand, different from the translation invariance of the Euclidean volume, the weighted volume depends on the choice of the origin
p_{0}
. Therefore, it is natural to classify the sharp geometric inequalities in hyperbolic space into the following two families. One family consists of the translation-invariant geometric inequalities, that is, the equality is attained by geodesic spheres, see e.g.
[
ACW2021
,
AHL2020
,
Li-Wei-Xiong2014
,
Hu-Li-Wei2022
,
Wang-Xia2014
,
Ge-Wang-Wu2014
,
Hu-Li2019
,
Hu-Li2023
]
; The other one family of geometric inequalities depends on the choice of the origin
p_{0}
, and in this case the equality is attained by geodesic balls centred at
p_{0}
, see
[
BHW2016
,
deLima-Girao2016
,
Ge-Wang-Wu2015
,
Hu-Li-Wei2022
,
Hu-Li2022
,
Li-Xu22-2
,
LW2023
,
Qiu-Xia2015
,
Xia2016
,
Hu-Wei-Zhou2023
]
.

On the other hand, affine isoperimetric inequalities relating two functionals associated with convex bodies such that the ratio of these functionals is invariant under the non-degenerate linear transformations. These inequalities are of great importance in both convex geometry and affine differential geometry, and they are more powerful than their Euclidean relatives. Moreover, due to the affine invariance, equality case of these inequalities is attained at ellipsoids.

The main purpose of this paper is to present affine isoperimetric type inequalities in hyperbolic space. First of all, we need to introduce the notion of hyperbolic ellipsoid as the natural counterpart of ellipsoid in Euclidean space. A smooth bounded domain
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is called a
hyperbolic ellipsoid
if there exists a point
p_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that the image
\pi_{p_{0}}(K)
is an ellipsoid in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\pi_{p_{0}}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an orthogonal projection with respect to
p_{0}
given by (
2.2
). The
hyperbolic centroid
of a smooth bounded domain
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the unique point defined by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{cen}(K)=\frac{\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}}{\left(-\langle\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol},\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}\rangle\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

see Proposition
2.4
.

The first result of this paper is the following geometric inequality in hyperbolic space, which can be considered as a natural counterpart of the classical affine isoperimetric inequality in Euclidean space.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Assume that the boundary
\partial K
is static convex with respect to a point
p_{0}
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Then there holds

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}H_{n-1}(\tilde{\kappa})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}dA\leq n|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\left(\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}},

where
H_{n-1}(\tilde{\kappa})=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(V\kappa_{i}-V_{,\nu})
. Equality holds if and only if
K
is a hyperbolic ellipsoid.

Our second result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
p_{0}
is its hyperbolic centroid. Assume that the boundary
\partial K
is static convex with respect to this point
p_{0}
. Then the following inequalities hold:

(i)

If
p>0
, then

(1.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}(V_{,\nu})^{\frac{(1-p)n}{n+p}}H_{n-1}(\tilde{\kappa})^{\frac{p}{n+p}}dA\leq n|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{\frac{2p}{n+p}}\left(\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}}.

(ii)

If
-n<p<0
, then

(1.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}(V_{,\nu})^{\frac{(1-p)n}{n+p}}H_{n-1}(\tilde{\kappa})^{\frac{p}{n+p}}dA\geq n|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{\frac{2p}{n+p}}\left(\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}}.

(iii)

If
p<-n
and
\partial K
is strictly static convex with respect to
p_{0}
, then

(1.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}(V_{,\nu})^{\frac{(1-p)n}{n+p}}H_{n-1}(\tilde{\kappa})^{\frac{p}{n+p}}dA\geq nc^{\frac{np}{n+p}}|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{\frac{2p}{n+p}}\left(\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}},

where
c>0
is a non-sharp constant.

Equality holds in (
1.8
) or (
1.9
) if and only if
K
is a hyperbolic ellipsoid.

We should mention that the left-hand sides of these inequalities (
1.7
)–(
1.10
) are natural analogs of the affine surface area and
L_{p}
affine surface area in Euclidean space, see (
3.9
) and (
3.12
) in
section 3
.

We also obtain the following Blaschke-Santaló type inequality in hyperbolic space, which can be considered as a natural analog of Blaschke-Santaló inequality in Euclidean space. To state our result, for any subset
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the
hyperbolic polar body

K^{\circ}
of
K
with respect to
p_{0}
is defined by

(1.11)

\displaystyle K^{\circ}:=\bigcap_{Y\in K}\{X\in\mathbb{H}^{n}~|~\cosh d(X,p_{0})\cosh d(Y,p_{0})\leq\cosh d(X,Y)+1\}.

Different from the (usual) polar body of a strictly convex body in hyperbolic space which lies in the de Sitter space, if the smooth bounded domain
K
is static convex with respect to
p_{0}
, then its hyperbolic polar body
K^{\circ}
is also static convex with respect to
p_{0}
, see Corollary
2.3
.

The following weighted volume product inequality in hyperbolic space can be compared with the volume product inequality in space forms
[
Gao-Hug-Schneider03
]
(see also a different extension by Hu and Li
[
Hu-Li2023
]
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
K
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with smooth boundary
\partial K
. Assume that
\partial K
is static convex with respect to its hyperbolic centroid
p_{0}
. Let
K^{\circ}
be the hyperbolic polar body of
K
with respect to
p_{0}
. Then there holds

(1.12)

\displaystyle\int_{K}Vd\mathrm{vol}\cdot\int_{K^{\circ}}Vd\mathrm{vol}\leq|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{2}.

Equality holds if and only if
K
is a hyperbolic ellipsoid centred at
p_{0}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Our results can be also established for static convex domains in the unit sphere, while in this case,
V(x)=\cos r(x)
and
r(x)=d(x,p_{0})
is the geodesic distance from
x
to
p_{0}
in the sphere.

The paper is organized as follows. In
section 2
, we use the hyperboloid model to define the orthogonal projection from hyperbolic space to Euclidean space. Using this orthogonal projection, we explore the relations between hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space and its orthogonal projection in Euclidean space. Moreover, the hyperbolic centroid of a smooth bounded domain in hyperbolic space are defined. In
section 3
, we review the classical affine isoperimetric inequalities in Euclidean space. In order to establish their hyperbolic analogs, we introduce the hyperbolic affine transformations, hyperbolic
L_{p}
-affine surface area, hyperbolic affine support function and hyperbolic polar bodies. In
section 4
, we give the proofs of Theorems
1.1
–
1.3
.

2.1.
Hyperboloid model of hyperbolic space

The Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
is the
(n+1)
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
equipped with the Minkowski inner product

\langle X,Y\rangle=\langle(x_{0},x),(y_{0},y)\rangle=-x_{0}y_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i},

where
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The hyperboloid model of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the upper sheet of the hyperboloid in the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{n,1}
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{n}=\{X=(x_{0},x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n,1}~|~\langle X,X\rangle=-1,~x_{0}>0\}.

For any point
X=(x_{0},x)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, it follows from the relation
-1=\langle X,X\rangle=-x_{0}^{2}+|x|^{2}
and
x_{0}>0
that
X=(\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}},x)
.

Let
N=(1,0)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The
orthogonal projection
with respect to
N
is defined by

\displaystyle\pi:\quad\quad\quad\mathbb{H}^{n}\quad\quad

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}},x)

\displaystyle\mapsto x,

In general, for any point
p_{0}=(x_{0},x)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, there exists a unique hyperbolic translation
T_{p_{0}}\in\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
which sends
p_{0}
to
N=(1,0)
.
Then the orthogonal projection with respect to this point
p_{0}
is given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\pi_{p_{0}}=\pi\circ T_{p_{0}}.

For any bounded domain
K
with smooth boundary
\partial K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the orthogonal projection
\widehat{K}=\pi_{p_{0}}(K)
with respect to a point
p_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This projection was first proposed by Gibbons
[
Gibbons1997
]
to prove the Penrose inequality for general surfaces in the Minkowski spacetime. It is also called by
Gibbons’ projection
, which can be used to define the quasi-local mass in general relativity
[
Wang-Yau2009
,
Wang-Yau2009-2
]
. Brendle and Wang
[
BW2014
]
also employed this projection to establish a Penrose type inequality for
2
-surfaces in a static convex timelike hypersurface in a static spacetime, see also
[
Mars-Soria2014
]
. In particular, the static convexity of
K
in this static spacetime implies the convexity of its projection
\widehat{K}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see
[
BW2014
, P. 36]
. In the case of hyperbolic space, we show that the static convexity of
\partial K
with respect to
p_{0}
is equivalent to the convexity of
\widehat{\partial K}:=\pi_{p_{0}}(\partial K)
in Euclidean space, see Corollary
2.3
.

Using the geodesic polar coordinates with respect to
N=(1,0)
, the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
can be expressed as

X=(\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}},x)=(\cosh r,\sinh r\theta),

where
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
r
is the geodesic distance to
N
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Denote by
\widehat{r}=|x|
the Euclidean distance of
x
to the origin
o=(0,\ldots,0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is clear that the hyperbolic radius
r
and the Euclidean radius
\widehat{r}
are related by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{r}=\sinh r.

Let
\{\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}\}
be an orthonormal basis of the tangent space
T_{x}\widehat{\partial K}
, and let
\widehat{\nu}
be the unit outward normal of
\widehat{\partial K}
at the point
x\in\widehat{\partial K}
, respectively. The induced metric and the support function of
\widehat{\partial K}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given by

\widehat{g}_{ij}=\partial_{i}x\cdot\partial_{j}x=\delta_{ij},\quad\widehat{u}=x\cdot\widehat{\nu},

where
\cdot
denotes the inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then the second fundamental form
\widehat{h}_{ij}
and the Weingarten matrix
\widehat{h}_{i}^{j}
can be expressed by

\widehat{h}_{ij}=-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}x\cdot\widehat{\nu},\quad\widehat{h}_{i}^{j}=\widehat{g}^{jk}\widehat{h}_{ki}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\partial K
be a smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Let
\widehat{\partial K}:=\pi(\partial K)
be the orthogonal projection of
\partial K
with respect to
N=(1,0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(i)

The induced metric
g_{ij}
satisfies

(2.4)

\displaystyle g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-\frac{(x\cdot\partial_{i}x)(x\cdot\partial_{j}x)}{1+|x|^{2}}.

(ii)

The unit outward normal
\nu
satisfies

(2.5)

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{(\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}\widehat{u},\widehat{u}x+\widehat{\nu})}{(1+\widehat{u}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

(iii)

The support function
u
satisfies

(2.6)

\displaystyle u=\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{1+\widehat{u}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{u}.

(iv)

The second fundamental form
h=(h_{ij})
satisfies

(2.7)

\displaystyle h_{ij}=\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{u}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left[\widehat{h}_{ij}+\widehat{u}\delta_{ij}-\frac{\widehat{u}(x\cdot\partial_{i}x)(x\cdot\partial_{j}x)}{1+|x|^{2}}\right].

(v)

The Weingarten matrix
\mathcal{W}=(h_{i}^{j})=(h_{ik}g^{kj})
satisfies

(2.8)

\displaystyle h_{i}^{j}=\frac{\widehat{h}_{i}^{j}+\widehat{u}\delta_{i}^{j}}{(1+\widehat{u}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{\widehat{h}_{i}^{k}(x\cdot\partial_{k}x)(x\cdot\partial_{j}x)}{(1+\widehat{u}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(iii) As

By (

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\langle\sinh r\partial_{r}X,\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle(|x|^{2},\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}x),\frac{(\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}\widehat{u},\widehat{\nu}+\widehat{u}x)}{(1+\widehat{u}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{1+\widehat{u}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{u}.

(iv) (

\displaystyle=\frac{\det(Vh_{ij}-V_{,\nu}g_{ij})}{\det(g_{ij})}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{1+\widehat{u}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{\det(\widehat{h}_{ij})}{\det(\widehat{g}_{ij})}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{1+\widehat{u}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa}).

∎

Corollary 2.3
.

A smooth bounded domain
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is (resp. strictly) static convex with respect to
p_{0}
if and only if its orthogonal projection
\widehat{K}=\pi_{p_{0}}(K)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is (resp. strictly) convex.

Proof.

For any
p_{0}\neq N
, we use the hyperbolic translation
T_{p_{0}}
such that
T_{p_{0}}(p_{0})=N
. Then by
\pi_{p_{0}}=\pi\circ T_{p_{0}}
due to (
2.2
), we have
T_{p_{0}}(K)
is static convex with respect to
T_{p_{0}}(p_{0})=N
. Moreover, it follows from (
2.12
) that
T_{p_{0}}(K)
is static convex with respect to
T_{p_{0}}(p_{0})=N
if and only if
\widehat{K}=\pi_{p_{0}}(K)=\pi\circ T_{p_{0}}(K)
is convex in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

2.2.
Hyperbolic centroid

Recall that the
centroid
of a smooth bounded domain
\widehat{K}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

(2.14)

\displaystyle\operatorname{cen}(\widehat{K})=\frac{\int_{\widehat{K}}xd\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}}{\mathrm{Vol}(\widehat{K})}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
x
is the position vector of the points in
\widehat{K}
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{K}}\left|x-\operatorname{cen}(\widehat{K})\right|^{2}d\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x)=\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\int_{\widehat{K}}|x-y|^{2}d\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x)\right\}.

Motivated by this, the
hyperbolic centroid
of a smooth bounded domain
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

(2.15)

\displaystyle\operatorname{cen}(K)=\frac{\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}}{\left(-\langle\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol},\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}\rangle\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

where
X=(\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}},x)
is the position vector of the points in
K
. By this definition, we know that
\operatorname{cen}(K)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
since
\langle\operatorname{cen}(K),\operatorname{cen}(K)\rangle=-1
and the
0
th component of
\operatorname{cen}(K)
is positive.

Proposition 2.4
.

The hyperbolic centroid of a smooth bounded domain
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
satisfies

(2.16)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\cosh d(\operatorname{cen}(K),X)d\mathrm{vol}(X)=\min_{Y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left\{\int_{K}\cosh d(Y,X)d\mathrm{vol}(X)\right\}.

Moreover, for any
A\in\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, we have

(2.17)

\displaystyle\operatorname{cen}(A(K))=A(\operatorname{cen}(K)).

Proof.

(i) First, we verify the equivalent characterization (
2.16
) of the hyperbolic centroid. For any
Y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{K}\cosh d(Y,X)d\mathrm{vol}(X)=

\displaystyle-\int_{K}\langle Y,X\rangle d\mathrm{vol}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle Y,\operatorname{cen}(K)\rangle\left(-\langle\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol},\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}\rangle\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(2.18)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(-\langle\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol},\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}\rangle\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we used
-\langle Y,\operatorname{cen}(K)\rangle=\cosh d(Y,\operatorname{cen}(K))\geq 1
. Equality holds in (
2.18
) if and only if
Y=\operatorname{cen}(K)
.

(ii) For any
A\in\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and any
X,Y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we have
-\langle AX,AY\rangle=\cosh d(AX,AY)=\cosh d(X,Y)=-\langle X,Y\rangle
and hence

\displaystyle-\langle\int_{K}AXd\mathrm{vol}(X),\int_{K}AXd\mathrm{vol}(X)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{K}\int_{K}\langle AX,AY\rangle d\mathrm{vol}(X)d\mathrm{vol}(Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{K}\int_{K}\langle X,Y\rangle d\mathrm{vol}(X)d\mathrm{vol}(Y)

(2.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}(X),\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}(X)\rangle.

Therefore,
-\langle\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}(X),\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}(X)\rangle
is invariant under the isometry of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. By (
2.15
) and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{cen}(A(K))=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{K}AXd\mathrm{vol}}{\left(-\langle\int_{K}AXd\mathrm{vol},\int_{K}AXd\mathrm{vol}\rangle\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\left(\frac{\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}}{\left(-\langle\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol},\int_{K}Xd\mathrm{vol}\rangle\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)=A(\operatorname{cen}(K)).

∎

The hyperbolic centroid of
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and the centroid of
\widehat{K}=\pi(K)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be related as follows.

Proposition 2.5
.

A smooth bounded domain
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
has its hyperbolic centroid at
p_{0}
if and only if
\widehat{K}=\pi_{p_{0}}(K)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
has its centroid at
o
.

Proof.

Up to a hyperbolic translation, one may assume that the hyperbolic centroid of
K
is
N=(1,0)
. Then we have
X=(\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}},x)=(\cosh r,\sinh r\theta)
and
x=\widehat{r}\theta
, where
\sinh r=\widehat{r}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. A direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\left.\nabla^{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\right|_{(1,0)}\int_{K}\cosh rd\mathrm{vol}=

\displaystyle\int_{K}d\pi(\sinh r\partial_{r})d\mathrm{vol}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K}\sinh r\cosh r\theta(\sinh r)^{n-1}drd\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{K}}\widehat{r}^{n}\theta d\widehat{r}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{K}}xd\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}.

By the equivalent characterization (
2.16
) of the hyperbolic centroid
\operatorname{cen}(K)
, we conclude that
o
is also the centroid of
\widehat{K}=\pi(K)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

∎

3.
Affine isoperimetric inequalities

In this section, we first recall the classical affine isoperimetric inequalities in Euclidean space. A compact convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonempty interior is called a
convex body
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote by
\mathcal{K}^{n}
the set of all convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We denote by
\mathcal{K}^{n}_{o}\subset\mathcal{K}^{n}
be the compact convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing the origin
o
as an interior point.

Let
\widehat{K}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
. The
affine surface area

\operatorname{as}(\widehat{K})
is defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}(\widehat{K}):=\int_{\widehat{\partial K}}H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\widehat{A},

where
H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})
is the generalized Gauss curvature of the hypersurface
\widehat{\partial K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Denote by
\operatorname{SL}(n)=\{L\in M_{n\times n}~|~\det(L)=1\}
the special linear group on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which consists of the unimodular linear transformations of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any ellipsoid
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a centered ball
B_{E}
with
\mathrm{Vol}(B_{E})=\mathrm{Vol}(B)
,
L\in\operatorname{SL}(n)
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
E=LB_{E}+b
. The affine surface area
\operatorname{as}(\widehat{K})
is
\operatorname{SL}(n)
-invariant and translation-invariant, i.e.,

\operatorname{as}(L(\widehat{K})+b)=\operatorname{as}(\widehat{K}),\quad\text{for all $L\in\operatorname{SL}(n)$ and $b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$.}

Theorem C
.

Let
\widehat{K}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
. The classical affine isoperimetric inequality is

(3.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}(\widehat{K})\leq n|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\mathrm{Vol}(\widehat{K})^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}},

which is
\operatorname{SL}(n)
-invariant and translation-invariant. Equality holds if and only if
\widehat{K}
is an ellipsoid.

For a more restricted class of bodies, this inequality is due to Blaschke
[
Blaschke1916
]
for
n\leq 3
, see also
[
Blaschke1923
]
. Later, Santaló
[
Santalo1949
]
and Deicke
[
Deicke1953
]
extended this inequality for all
n\geq 2
. Theorem C is due to Petty
[
Petty1985
]
. For
\widehat{K}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
with its centroid at the origin
o
, the affine surface area can be generalized to the
L_{p}
-affine surface area, which was first introduced by Lutwak
[
Lutwak1996
]
for
p>1
in his groundbreaking paper, and later generalized by Schütt and Werner
[
Schutt-Werner2004
]
for all
p\neq-n
. Precisely, for
p\neq-n
, the
L_{p}
-affine surface area
\operatorname{as}_{p}(\widehat{K})
of
\widehat{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}_{p}(\widehat{K}):=\int_{\widehat{\partial K}}\widehat{u}^{-\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})^{\frac{p}{n+p}}d\widehat{A},

while for
p=\pm\infty
, it is defined by

(3.4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}_{\pm\infty}(\widehat{K}):=\int_{\widehat{\partial K}}\widehat{u}^{-n}H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})d\widehat{A},

provided that the above integrals exist.

The following
L_{p}
affine isoperimetric inequalities were proved by Lutwak
[
Lutwak1996
]
for
p>1
, and later extended by Werner and Ye
[
Werner-Ye2008
, Thm. 4.2]
for general
p\neq-n
.

Theorem D
.

Let
\widehat{K}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
with its centroid at the origin
o
.

(i)

If
p>0
, then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}_{p}(\widehat{K})\leq n|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{\frac{2p}{n+p}}\mathrm{Vol}(\widehat{K})^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}}.

(ii)

If
-n<p<0
, then

(3.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}_{p}(\widehat{K})\geq n|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{\frac{2p}{n+p}}\mathrm{Vol}(\widehat{K})^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}}.

(iii)

If
p<-n
and
\widehat{K}
is strictly convex with
C^{2}
boundary, then

(3.7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}_{p}(\widehat{K})\geq nc^{\frac{np}{n+p}}|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{\frac{2p}{n+p}}\mathrm{Vol}(\widehat{K})^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}}.

where
c
is a non-sharp constant.

Equality holds in (
3.5
) or (
3.6
) if and only if
\widehat{K}
is an ellipsoid centered at the origin.

Remark 3.1
.

Inequalities (
3.5
) - (
3.7
) are
\operatorname{SL}(n)
-invariant. Moreover, the constant
c
in (
3.7
) is from the inverse Santaló inequality (see
[
Bourgain-Milman1987
]
).

Let
\widehat{K}
be a smooth convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with strictly convex boundary. The
affine support function
of
\widehat{K}
is defined by

\Lambda(\widehat{K}):=\widehat{u}H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}},

where
H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})
denotes the Gauss curvature of
\widehat{\partial K}
. Then
\Lambda(\widehat{K})
is
\operatorname{SL}(n)
-invariant.
The
affine surface area measure
is defined by

\displaystyle d\tilde{A}=H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\widehat{A},

which is the volume element of the positive definite tensor
\tilde{g}_{ij}=H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}h_{ij}
. Then
d\tilde{A}
is
\operatorname{SL}(n)
-invariant and translation-invariant.

For
p\neq-n
, it follows from the definition (
3.3
) that

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}_{p}(\widehat{K})=\int_{\widehat{\partial K}}\left(\frac{\widehat{u}}{H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}\right)^{\frac{(1-p)n}{n+p}}H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\widehat{A}=

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\partial K}}\Lambda^{\frac{(1-p)n}{n+p}}d\tilde{A}.

In particular,
\operatorname{as}_{0}(\widehat{K})=\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Vol}(\widehat{K})
and
\operatorname{as}_{1}(\widehat{K})=\operatorname{as}(\widehat{K})
are
\operatorname{SL}(n)
-invariant and translation-invariant; for other choice of
p\neq-n
,
\operatorname{as}_{p}(\widehat{K})
is only
\operatorname{SL}(n)
-invariant.

The
affine transformation
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}_{L,b}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n},~x\mapsto Lx+b,

where
L\in\operatorname{SL}(n)
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Motivated by this, we define the
hyperbolic affine transformation
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
by

\displaystyle\phi_{L,b}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n},~(\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}},x)\mapsto(\sqrt{1+|Lx+b|^{2}},Lx+b).

In particular, we have
\phi_{L,b}(K)=\pi_{p_{0}}^{-1}\left(L(\pi_{p_{0}}(K))+b\right)
, where
p_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a fixed point. Then the
hyperbolic special affine group
is defined by

\operatorname{SA}(\mathbb{H}^{n}):=\{\phi_{L,b}~|~L\in\operatorname{SL}(n),b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},

while the
hyperbolic special linear group
is defined by

\operatorname{SL}(\mathbb{H}^{n}):=\{\phi_{L,0}~|~L\in\operatorname{SL}(n)\}.

Let
K
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which is static convex with respect to its hyperbolic centroid
p_{0}
. Then
\widehat{K}:=\pi_{p_{0}}(K)
is a convex body with its centroid at the origin
o
. The
hyperbolic affine support function

\Lambda^{\mathbb{H}}
of
K\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\mathbb{H}}(K):=uH_{n-1}(\tilde{\kappa})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}.

It follows from (
2.6
) and (
2.13
) that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\mathbb{H}}(K)=\Lambda(\widehat{K}),

and hence it is
\operatorname{SA}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
-invariant. The
hyperbolic
L_{p}
-affine surface area
of
K\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

(3.10)

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(K):=\int_{\partial K}u^{\frac{(1-p)n}{n+p}}H_{n-1}(\tilde{\kappa})^{\frac{p}{n+p}}dA,\quad\forall~p\neq-n,

and

(3.11)

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\pm\infty}(K):=\int_{\partial K}u^{-n}H_{n-1}(\tilde{\kappa})dA,

where
\tilde{\kappa}_{i}=V\kappa_{i}-V_{,\nu}
. Using
d\tilde{A}=H_{n-1}(\widehat{\kappa})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\widehat{A}=H_{n-1}(\tilde{\kappa})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}dA
, we get

(3.12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(K)=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}(\Lambda^{\mathbb{H}})^{\frac{(1-p)n}{n+p}}d\tilde{A}=\operatorname{as}_{p}(\widehat{K}),\quad\forall p\neq-n,

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{as}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\pm\infty}(K)=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}(\Lambda^{\mathbb{H}})^{-n}d\tilde{A}=\operatorname{as}_{\pm\infty}(\widehat{K}).

Hence,
\operatorname{as}_{p}(K)
for all
p\neq-n
and
\operatorname{as}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\pm\infty}(K)
are
\operatorname{SL}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
-invariant.

For any subset
\widehat{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the polar body of
\widehat{K}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the origin
o
is defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{K}^{\circ}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~|~y\cdot x\leq 1,\forall x\in\widehat{K}\}.

The polar body
\widehat{K}^{\circ}
is an intersection of closed half-spaces that contain the origin, and thus it is a closed convex set that contains the origin. It follows from the definition that

(i)

For any subsets
\widehat{K}_{0},\widehat{K}_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\widehat{K}_{0}\subset\widehat{K}_{1}
, then
\widehat{K}_{0}^{\circ}\supset\widehat{K}_{1}^{\circ}
.

(ii)

For any subset
\widehat{K}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\widehat{K}\subset\widehat{K}^{\circ\circ}
.

Moreover, if
\widehat{K}
is a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which contains the origin in its interior, then
\widehat{K}^{\circ}
is also a convex body that contains the origin in its interior, and

, see
[
Schneider

.

Theorem E

(
[

.

Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\widehat{K})\mathrm{Vol}(\widehat{K}^{\circ})\leq|\mathbb{B}^{n}|^{2},

Proposition 3.2
.

A smooth bounded domain
K

\mathbb{H}^{n}
and

is an interior point of

Proof.

By a hyperbolic translation, one may assume that

,

\displaystyle\cosh d(X,Y)=-\langle X,Y\rangle=-x\cdot y+\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}\sqrt{1+|y|^{2}}.

A direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}\sqrt{1+|y|^{2}}=

\displaystyle\cosh d(X,p_{0})\cosh d(Y,p_{0}).

Hence,

\cosh d(X,p_{0})\cosh d(Y,p_{0})\leq\cosh d(X,Y)+1.

Observe that

\displaystyle(\pi(K))^{\circ}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{y\in\widehat{K}}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~|~x\cdot y\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{Y\in K}\pi(\{X\in\mathbb{H}^{n}~|~\cosh d(X,p_{0})\cos d(Y,p_{0})\leq\cosh d(X,Y)+1\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\bigg(\bigcap_{Y\in K}\{X\in\mathbb{H}^{n}~|~\cosh d(X,p_{0})\cos d(Y,p_{0})\leq\cosh d(X,Y)+1\}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(K^{\circ}).

If

is the hyperbolic centroid of

The radial function and support function of
\widehat{K}\in\mathcal{K}_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle\widehat{r}(\widehat{K},\theta):=

\displaystyle\max\{t\in\mathbb{R}~|~t\theta\in\widehat{K}\},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

In view of the polar duality, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle r(\widehat{K}^{\circ},\theta)=\frac{1}{\widehat{h}(\widehat{K},\theta)},\quad h(\widehat{K}^{\circ},z)=\frac{1}{\widehat{r}(\widehat{K},z)},\quad\text{for all $\theta,z\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$}.

In particular,

h(\widehat{K},z)=\widehat{u}(x),\quad\text{for $\mathcal{H}^{n-1}$-almost every $x\in\widehat{\partial K}$},

where

is the unit outward normal at

. In view of the polar duality (
3.14
), we have

\displaystyle\widehat{r}(\widehat{K}^{\circ},\theta)=\frac{1}{\widehat{h}(\widehat{K},\theta)}=\frac{1}{\widehat{u}(\widehat{\nu}_{\widehat{K}}^{-1}(\theta))},\quad\widehat{u}(\widehat{\nu}_{\widehat{K}^{\circ}}^{-1}(z))=\widehat{h}(\widehat{K}^{\circ},z)=\frac{1}{\widehat{r}(\widehat{K},z)}.

It is clear that for any two subsets
K_{1}
,

in

; Moreover, for any subset
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
K\subset K^{\circ\circ}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

If a smooth bounded domain
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is static convex with respect to an interior point

, then

is also static convex with respect to

, and

.

(i)

The radial function of the hyperbolic polar body satisfies

(3.15)

\displaystyle\cosh r^{\circ}=\frac{\cosh r}{u},

(ii)

The hyperbolic special linear transformations and the hyperbolic polar bodies satisfy

(3.16)

\displaystyle(\phi_{L,0}(K))^{\circ}=\phi_{L^{-t},0}(K^{\circ}),

where

denotes the transpose of the inverse of

.

(iii)

The hyperbolic affine support functions of
K
and
K^{\circ}
satisfy

(3.17)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\mathbb{H}}(K,z)\Lambda^{\mathbb{H}}(K^{\circ},\theta)=1,

where
z=\nu_{\widehat{K}}^{-1}(x)
and
\theta=\frac{x}{|x|}
for

.

Proof.

Taking
\widehat{K}=\pi_{p_{0}}(K)
, then the static convexity of
K
with respect to
p_{0}
is equivalent to the convexity of
\widehat{K}
by Corollary

. Then by Proposition

, we conclude that
K^{\circ}=\pi_{p_{0}}^{-1}((\pi_{p_{0}}(K))^{\circ})
is static convex with respect to
p_{0}
. Moreover,
K=K^{\circ\circ}
follows immediately from
\widehat{K}=\widehat{K}^{\circ\circ}
.

In view of (
2.3
) and (
2.6
), (
3.15
) follows directly from

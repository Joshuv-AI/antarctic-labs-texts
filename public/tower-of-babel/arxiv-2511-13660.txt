Graviton propagator in de Sitter space
in a simple one-parameter gauge

Abstract




1
Introduction

Primordial inflation stands out in the history of our Universe as a period of extreme
conditions during which quantum gravitational effects, although still small, need not
be prohibitively suppressed. This is due to gravitational particle
production
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
: a vast ensemble of long-wavelength
inflationary gravitons is produced because of their non-conformal coupling to the
rapidly expanding and accelerating background
[
4
]
. Since gravitons couple universally to all matter, this ensemble enhances graviton loop corrections.
The magnitude of this enhancement is controlled by the loop-counting parameter
that can be as large as
\kappa^{2}H^{2}\!\sim\!10^{-10}
for some models of
inflation
[
5
]
. Although this effective coupling remains small enough for
loop corrections to be computed within the effective theory of quantum
gravity
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
, it is nevertheless
significantly larger than in post-inflationary epochs.

While realistic inflationary dynamics involves a slowly rolling scalar field that leads to a
quasi-exponential expansion, the idealization to exact exponential expansion usually provides
an excellent approximation. This corresponds to the de Sitter limit, which offers further
technical simplifications because it is a maximally symmetric space. The infrared effects
relevant for inflationary gravitons are well captured in this limit, which underscores the
importance of perturbative quantum gravity in de Sitter space. Here we compute the graviton
propagator (two-point function) in de Sitter space in a particularly simple one-parameter gauge, which can
be used as a basic ingredient in perturbative loop computations.

Long-wavelength inflationary gravitons, enhanced by tree-level gravitational particle
production, can communicate information about the accelerating background to the matter
fields with which they interact, and which would otherwise be insensitive to the expansion.
The effects of this interaction are captured perturbatively by one-loop self-masses and
self-energies

the secular corrections must vanish. It is therefore important to understand how to determine the
physical corrections reliably.

In flat space quantum field theory, the question of gauge-independent observables is resolved
by the construction of the S-matrix, which is guaranteed to be gauge independent. However, the
S-matrix is not available in cosmology, and, moreover,
it does not correspond to the initial-value
formulation of cosmological quantum field theory. Therefore, it is necessary to construct
one-loop quantum-gravitational observables in inflation and to test their gauge dependence.

A program to address the gauge dependence of secular corrections to de Sitter–space
quantum-gravitational observables was initiated in
[
51
]
. It is based on the
earlier works
[
52
,
6
,
53
,
54
]
,
which showed how to isolate from the S-matrix the leading infrared graviton-loop corrections
to exchange potentials in flat space. These corrections become gauge independent once the
graviton-loop contributions to the source and the observer are included, as they are part of
the flat-space S-matrix. In
[
51
]
, it was shown that one can infer the
gauge-independent part of the self-mass by incorporating these corrections without taking
the asymptotic-time limits that enter the S-matrix definition. This approach allowed the
derivation of the gauge-independent one-loop–corrected effective field equation for the
self-mass of the minimally coupled massless scalar
[
51
]
, and likewise for
electromagnetism
[
55
]
.

Demonstrating that this program also works in de Sitter space requires first proving that
the secular correction to the scalar exchange potential
[
56
]
obtained in the simple
gauge is gauge independent. This requires recalculating the correction in different
graviton gauges with arbitrary gauge-fixing parameters, in order to show that any dependence
on these parameters drops out from the final result. One possibility is to use the propagator
from
[
57
]
, which was obtained in a generalization of the simple gauge
containing two infinitesimal gauge-fixing parameters. In this paper, we construct an
alternative propagator suitable for this purpose in a one-parameter family of non-covariant
gauges, characterized by the following gauge-fixing action:

S_{\rm gf}[h_{\mu\nu}]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\biggl[-\frac{g^{\mu\nu}\mathscr{F}_{\mu}\mathscr{F}_{\nu}}{2\alpha}\biggr]\,,\quad\ \mathscr{F}_{\mu}=g^{\rho\sigma}\biggl[\bigl(\nabla_{\rho}\!-\!2n_{\rho}\bigr)\delta g_{\sigma\mu}\!-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\nabla_{\mu}\!-\!2n_{\mu}\bigr)\delta g_{\rho\sigma}\biggr]\,,

(1.1)

which contains an arbitrary gauge-fixing parameter
\alpha
. Here the graviton
field is defined as the perturbation of the metric,
g_{\mu\nu}\!=\!\overline{g}_{\mu\nu}\!+\!\kappa\delta g_{\mu\nu}
, around the de Sitter
background,
\overline{g}_{\mu\nu}\!=\!a^{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}
,
with
\kappa\!=\!\sqrt{16\pi G_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}
denoting the loop counting
parameter, and where
n_{\mu}\!=\!aH\delta_{\mu}^{0}
is a time-like vector,
n^{\mu}n_{\mu}\!=\!-H^{2}
, with
H
the constant Hubble rate of de Sitter space. This gauge
constitutes a one-parameter generalization of the simple gauge, which corresponds to

\alpha\!=\!1
.

One could also consider redoing the computation in generally covariant gauges. However, the
loop computation in the one-parameter exact gauge
[
16
]
proved to be
considerably more difficult than the corresponding computation in the simple
gauge
[
15
]
. Moreover, the most general two-parameter generally covariant
gauge propagator reported in
[
58
]
does not reproduce the exact gauge
propagator
[
48
,
49
,
50
]
in the appropriate limit, a problem
that must be resolved before further computations can be attempted. Experience with photon
gauges
[
59
,
60
,
61
,
62
]
also shows that
appropriately chosen non-covariant gauges tend to be considerably simpler than covariant
ones. Since gauge choices are ultimately a matter of convenience, it is advantageous to
employ simpler non-covariant gauges. Indeed, the propagator constructed here is
considerably simpler than its generally covariant counterparts.

The purpose of this work is to construct the graviton two-point function in this
one-parameter extension of the simple gauge, providing the input required for
subsequent loop computations to be addressed in future work. The construction is
presented in seven sections. Section
2
gives the details
of the canonical formulation of linearized gravitons in de Sitter space, in both the
gauge-invariant and gauge-fixed formulations. The latter formulation serves as the starting
point for quantization on an indefinite metric space of states appropriate for multiplier
gauges in Section
3
. The dynamics of the graviton field
operator is solved in Section
5
, relying on the results for
scalar two-point functions collected in Section
4
. Section
6
presents the graviton
two-point function, which is the main result of the paper. A brief summary and discussion
of the results are given in Section
7
, and further technical details
required to verify the main result are collected in the Appendix.

2
Graviton in de Sitter

In this section we introduce the linearized graviton system in de Sitter and develop its canonical (Hamiltonian) formulation. We first analyze the gauge-invariant theory,
from which we obtain
the first-class constraints associated with linearized diffeomorphisms. We then introduce a
one-parameter family of non-covariant multiplier gauges and derive the corresponding
gauge-fixed canonical formulation, which will provide the basis for the quantization in the
next section.

The dynamics of general relativity with a cosmological constant is encoded
in the action

S_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm EH}[g_{\mu\nu}]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\Bigl[R-(D\!-\!2)\Lambda\Bigr]\,,

(2.1)

where
g
is the determinant of the positive-signature
metric
g_{\mu\nu}
,
\Lambda
is the positive cosmological constant,
and
\kappa^{2}\!=\!16\pi G_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
is the rescaled Newton constant.
The Ricci scalar
R\!=\!g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}
is obtained by contracting the Ricci tensor

R_{\mu\nu}\!=\!{R^{\rho}}_{\mu\rho\nu}
, which in turn is obtained by contracting the Riemann
tensor
R^{\rho}{}_{\mu\sigma\nu}\!=\!\partial_{\sigma}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}\!-\!\partial_{\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\sigma}\!+\!\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\sigma\alpha}\!-\!\Gamma_{\mu\sigma}^{\alpha}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\alpha}
,
defined in terms of the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\!=\!\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\bigl(\partial_{\mu}g_{\nu\beta}\!+\!\partial_{\nu}g_{\mu\beta}\!-\!\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\nu}\bigr)
.

The vacuum solution of this theory is the maximally symmetric de Sitter space, for which
all curvature tensors can be expressed in terms of the metric,

R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\frac{2\Lambda}{D\!-\!1}g_{\mu[\rho}g_{\sigma]\nu}\,,\qquad\quad R_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\quad R=D\Lambda\,.

(2.2)

This spacetime is of particular interest in cosmology, since its exponentially
expanding Poincaré patch provides a good leading-order model for slow-roll
inflation. The metric on this patch is conveniently written in conformally
flat form,

g_{\mu\nu}=a^{2}(\eta)\eta_{\mu\nu}\,,

(2.3)

where
\eta_{\mu\nu}
is the
D
-dimensional Minkowski metric, and the
scale factor

a(\eta)=\bigl[1\!-\!H(\eta\!-\!\eta_{0})\bigr]^{-1}\,,

(2.4)

is a function of conformal time
\eta
, which ranges over the interval

\eta\!\in\!(-\infty,\eta_{0}\!+\!1/H)
, with
\eta_{0}
denoting the time at which

a(\eta_{0})\!=\!1
. Spatial slices are
(D\!-\!1)
-dimensional Euclidean spaces.
The Hubble rate is related to the cosmological constant as

H^{2}\!=\!\Lambda/(D\!-\!1)
, and we often express the expansion in terms of the
conformal Hubble parameter
\mathcal{H}\!=\!Ha
.

For our purposes it is convenient to define the linearized graviton field

h_{\mu\nu}
as the perturbation of a conformally rescaled de Sitter metric,

g_{\mu\nu}=a^{2}\bigl(\eta_{\mu\nu}\!+\!\kappa h_{\mu\nu}\bigr)\,,

(2.5)

where henceforth Indices on
h_{\mu\nu}
are raised and lowered with the Minkowski metric.
This definition is just a conformal rescaling of the metric perturbation introduced
in (
1.1
),
h_{\mu\nu}\!=\!a^{-2}\delta g_{\mu\nu}
.
Expanding the action (
2.1
) to second order in
h_{\mu\nu}
yields
the quadratic action for the linearized theory (ee e.g.
[
18
]
),

\displaystyle S[h_{\mu\nu}]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,a^{D-2}\,\biggl[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}h_{\nu\rho})(\partial^{\nu}h^{\mu\rho})-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}h^{\mu\nu})(\partial_{\nu}h)+\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{\mu}h)(\partial^{\mu}h)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{\rho}h_{\mu\nu})(\partial^{\rho}h^{\mu\nu})+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}h_{0\mu}\partial^{\mu}h\biggr]\,.

(2.6)

This action is invariant under the linearized gauge transformation

h_{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{a^{2}}\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)}=h_{\mu\nu}+\Bigl(\delta^{\rho}_{(\mu}\partial_{\nu)}-\eta_{\mu\nu}\delta^{\rho}_{0}\mathcal{H}\Bigr)\frac{\xi_{\rho}}{a^{2}}\,,

(2.7)

for an arbitrary vector field
\xi_{\mu}
. The nonstandard form of the
transformation reflects the metric split in (
2.5
), in which two
powers of the scale factor have been factored out.

The equation of motion that follows from the gauge-invariant action (
2.6
)
can be written compactly as

\boldsymbol{L}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}=0\,,

(2.8)

with the help of the modified Lichnerowicz operator,

\boldsymbol{L}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=V^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\omega\lambda}\partial_{\omega}a^{D-2}\partial_{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)a^{D-2}\mathcal{H}\Bigl[\eta^{\mu\nu}\delta_{0}^{(\rho}\partial^{\sigma)}-\eta^{\rho\sigma}\delta_{0}^{(\mu}\partial^{\nu)}-(D\!-\!1)\mathcal{H}\eta^{\mu\nu}\delta_{0}^{\rho}\delta_{0}^{\sigma}\Bigr]\,,

(2.9)

whose double-derivative part inherits the tensor structure from its flat-space counterpart,

V^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\omega\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\rho\sigma}\eta^{\omega(\mu}\eta^{\nu)\lambda}-\eta^{\omega(\rho}\eta^{\sigma)(\mu}\eta^{\nu)\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\mu(\rho}\eta^{\sigma)\nu}\eta^{\omega\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\mu\nu}\bigl(\eta^{\omega(\rho}\eta^{\sigma)\lambda}\!-\!\eta^{\rho\sigma}\eta^{\omega\lambda}\bigr)\,.

(2.10)

The modified form of the operator in (
2.9
) is due to
the conformal rescaling in the definition of the graviton in (
2.5
).

To set up the canonical quantization, we first derive the Hamiltonian form of the
gauge-invariant action (
2.6
). We then extend this construction to a
one-parameter family of non-covariant multiplier gauges, obtaining the corresponding
gauge-fixed Hamiltonian that will underlie the quantized theory.

2.1
Gauge-invariant canonical formulation

To prepare the gauge-invariant action (
2.6
) for canonical analysis
it is convenient to separate temporal and spatial indices, thereby isolating the
time-derivative structure. In terms of this decomposition the action takes the form

\displaystyle S[h_{\mu\nu}]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,a^{D-2}\biggl[\frac{1}{4}\Bigl(\partial_{0}h_{ij}\!-\!2\partial_{(i}h_{j)0}\!+\!\delta_{ij}\mathcal{H}h_{00}\Bigr)\Bigl(\partial_{0}h_{ij}\!-\!2\partial_{(i}h_{j)0}\!+\!\delta_{ij}\mathcal{H}h_{00}\Bigr)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\Bigl(\partial_{0}h_{ii}\!-\!2\partial_{i}h_{0i}\!+\!(D\!-\!1)\mathcal{H}h_{00}\Bigr)\Bigl(\partial_{0}h_{jj}\!-\!2\partial_{j}h_{0j}\!+\!(D\!-\!1)\mathcal{H}h_{00}\Bigr)+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{00})\bigl(\partial_{j}h_{ij}\!-\!\partial_{i}h_{jj}\bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{ik})(\partial_{j}h_{jk})-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{ij})(\partial_{j}h_{kk})+\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{k}h_{ii})(\partial_{k}h_{jj})-\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{k}h_{ij})(\partial_{k}h_{ij})\biggr]\,,

(2.11)

where the expression has been simplified using integrations by parts.

The next step is to introduce the extended action, a first-order formulation that is
on-shell equivalent to (
2.11
) and well suited for identifying the
constraint structure. This is done by promoting all time derivatives in
(
2.11
) to independent velocity fields,

\displaystyle\partial_{0}h_{00}\longrightarrow v_{00}\,,\qquad\quad\partial_{0}h_{0i}\longrightarrow v_{0i}\,,\qquad\quad\partial_{0}h_{ij}-2\partial_{(i}h_{j)0}+\delta_{ij}\mathcal{H}h_{00}\longrightarrow v_{ij}\,,

(2.12)

and introducing Lagrange multipliers
\pi_{00},\pi_{0i},\pi_{ij}
to enforce equivalence with
the original dynamics. The resulting extended action is

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\bigl[h_{00},v_{00},\pi_{00},h_{0i},v_{0i},\pi_{0i},h_{ij},v_{ij},\pi_{ij}\bigr]=\!\!\int\!\!d^{D\!}x\biggl\{a^{D-2}\biggl[\frac{1}{4}\bigl(v_{ij}v_{ij}\!-\!v_{ii}v_{jj}\bigr)\!+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{00})\bigl(\partial_{j}h_{ij}\!-\!\partial_{i}h_{jj}\bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip 25.6073pt+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{ik})(\partial_{j}h_{jk})-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{ij})(\partial_{j}h_{kk})+\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{k}h_{ii})(\partial_{k}h_{jj})-\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{k}h_{ij})(\partial_{k}h_{ij})\biggr]

\displaystyle\hskip 25.6073pt+\pi_{00}\bigl(\partial_{0}h_{00}-v_{00}\bigr)+\pi_{0i}\bigl(\partial_{0}h_{0i}-v_{0i}\bigr)+\pi_{ij}\Bigl(\partial_{0}h_{ij}-2\partial_{(i}h_{j)0}+\delta_{ij}\mathcal{H}h_{00}-v_{ij}\Bigr)\biggr\}\,.

(2.13)

To obtain the canonical action we first solve on-shell for as many velocity components as possible, which in the case at hand are
v_{ij}
.
Varying the extended action with respect to
v_{ij}
gives

\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta v_{ij}}=\frac{1}{2}a^{D-2}\bigl(v_{ij}-\delta_{ij}v_{kk}\bigr)-\pi_{ij}\approx 0\,,

(2.14)

which is solved by

v_{ij}\approx\overline{v}_{ij}=2a^{2-D}\Bigl(\pi_{ij}\!-\!\frac{\delta_{ij}\pi_{kk}}{D\!-\!2}\Bigr)\,.

(2.15)

Here and throughout this section we use Dirac’s notation, with
\approx
denoting weak (on-shell) equalities and
=
denoting strong (off-shell) ones.
Substituting
\overline{v}_{ij}
back into the extended action yields the canonical action

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}\bigl[h_{00},\pi_{00},h_{0i},\pi_{0i},h_{ij},\pi_{ij},v_{00},v_{0i}\bigr]\equiv\mathcal{S}\bigl[h_{00},v_{00},\pi_{00},h_{0i},v_{0i},\pi_{0i},h_{ij},\overline{v}_{ij},\pi_{ij}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,\Bigl[\pi_{00}\partial_{0}h_{00}+\pi_{0i}\partial_{0}h_{0i}+\pi_{ij}\partial_{0}h_{ij}-\mathscr{H}-v_{00}\Phi_{0}-v_{0i}\Phi_{i}\Bigr]\,.

(2.16)

Here the canonical Hamiltonian density is

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}={}

\displaystyle a^{2-D}\biggl[\pi_{ij}\pi_{ij}-\frac{\pi_{ii}\pi_{jj}}{D\!-\!2}\biggr]\!+\pi_{ij}\Bigl(2\partial_{(i}h_{j)0}-\delta_{ij}\mathcal{H}h_{00}\Bigr)\!+a^{D-2}\biggl[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{00})\bigl(\partial_{i}h_{jj}\!-\!\partial_{j}h_{ij}\bigr)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{ik})(\partial_{j}h_{jk})+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{ij})(\partial_{j}h_{kk})-\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{k}h_{ii})(\partial_{k}h_{jj})+\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{k}h_{ij})(\partial_{k}h_{ij})\biggr]\,,

(2.17)

while the Lagrange multipliers
v_{00}
and
v_{0i}
enforce the primary constraints

\Phi_{0}\equiv\pi_{00}\approx 0\,,\qquad\quad\Phi_{i}\equiv\pi_{0i}\approx 0\,.

(2.18)

The conservation of primary constraints produces secondary constraints
\Psi_{0}

and
\Psi_{i}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\Phi_{0}\approx\frac{a^{D-2}}{2}\bigl(\nabla^{2}h_{ii}-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h_{ij}\bigr)+\mathcal{H}\pi_{ii}\equiv\Psi_{0}\approx 0\,,

(2.19a)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\Phi_{i}\approx 2\partial_{j}\pi_{ij}\equiv\Psi_{i}\approx 0\,.

(2.19b)

The conservation of secondary constraints generates no further constraints. These
primary and secondary constraints together encode the linearized diffeomorphism symmetry of the theory.

2.2
Gauge-fixed canonical formulation

We now turn to the gauge-fixed formulation in the one-parameter family of non-covariant
gauges. The gauge-fixed action is obtained by adding a gauge-fixing functional to the
gauge-invariant action (
2.6
),

S_{\star}[h_{\mu\nu}]=S[h_{\mu\nu}]+S_{\rm gf}[h_{\mu\nu}]\,.

(2.20)

This addition breaks the gauge invariance of the theory. The specific choice of
gauge-fixing functional is, in principle, a matter of convenience. In what follows we adopt
a one-parameter generalization of the simple graviton gauge
[
22
,
23
]
given in (
1.1
),
that for the graviton field defined in (
2.5
) reads

S_{\rm gf}[h_{\mu\nu}]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,a^{D-2}\,\biggl[-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathscr{F}^{\mu}\mathscr{F}_{\mu}\biggr]\,,\qquad\quad\mathscr{F}_{\mu}=\mathscr{D}_{\mu\rho\sigma}h^{\rho\sigma}\,,

(2.21)

where the associated linear differential operator is

\mathscr{D}_{\mu\rho\sigma}=\eta_{\mu(\rho}\Bigl[\partial_{\sigma)}+(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\delta^{0}_{\sigma)}\Bigr]-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\rho\sigma}\partial_{\mu}\,.

(2.22)

With this choice the gauge-fixed equations of motion take the form

\boldsymbol{D}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}=0\,,\qquad\quad\boldsymbol{D}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\boldsymbol{L}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathscr{D}_{\omega}{}^{\mu\nu}a^{D-2}\mathscr{D}^{\omega\rho\sigma}\,,

(2.23)

where the Lichnerowicz operator was defined in (
2.9
).

Setting
\alpha\!=\!1
reproduces the simple gauge. In the canonical formulation this choice
for the gauge-fixing functional corresponds to fixing the Lagrange multipliers in
(
2.16
) to particular linear functions of the canonical variables
(see, e.g.,
[
63
]
for an analogous discussion in the vector-field case).
Starting from the action (
2.20
) we now derive the corresponding
gauge-fixed canonical formulation.

We begin by performing the space–time decomposition of the gauge-fixing term,

S_{\rm gf}[h_{\mu\nu}]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,a^{D-2}\biggl[\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathscr{F}_{0}\mathscr{F}_{0}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathscr{F}_{i}\mathscr{F}_{i}\biggr]\,,

(2.24)

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}h_{00}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}h_{ii}+\partial_{i}h_{0i}-(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}h_{00}\,,

(2.25)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{i}=-\partial_{0}h_{0i}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}(h_{00}-h_{jj})+\partial_{j}h_{ij}-(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}h_{0i}\,.

(2.26)

To pass to the canonical description in the presence of gauge fixing, we introduce an extended action by promoting specific time-derivative combinations to independent velocity fields,

\displaystyle\partial_{0}h_{00}+(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}h_{00}\longrightarrow{}

\displaystyle v_{00}\,,

(2.27)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}h_{0i}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}(h_{00}-h_{jj})-\partial_{j}h_{ij}+(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}h_{0i}\longrightarrow{}

\displaystyle v_{0i}\,,

(2.28)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}h_{ij}-2\partial_{(i}h_{j)0}+\delta_{ij}\mathcal{H}h_{00}\longrightarrow{}

\displaystyle v_{ij}\,,

(2.29)

and introducing the corresponding Lagrange multipliers to enforce on-shell equivalence. The extended action takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\star}\bigl[h_{00},v_{00},\pi_{00},h_{0i},v_{0i},\pi_{0i},h_{ij},v_{ij},\pi_{ij}\bigr]=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,\biggl\{a^{D-2}\biggl[\frac{1}{4}v_{ij}v_{ij}-\frac{1}{4}v_{ii}v_{jj}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{00})\bigl(\partial_{j}h_{ij}-\partial_{i}h_{jj}\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{ik})(\partial_{j}h_{jk})-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{ij})(\partial_{j}h_{kk})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{k}h_{ii})(\partial_{k}h_{jj})-\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{k}h_{ij})(\partial_{k}h_{ij})+\frac{1}{8\alpha}\bigl(v_{00}+v_{ii}\bigr)^{2}-\frac{v_{0i}v_{0i}}{2\alpha}\biggr]

\displaystyle+\pi_{00}\Bigl(\partial_{0}h_{00}+(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}h_{00}-v_{00}\Bigr)+\pi_{0i}\Bigl(\partial_{0}h_{0i}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\bigl(h_{00}-h_{jj}\bigr)

\displaystyle-\partial_{j}h_{ij}+(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}h_{0i}-v_{0i}\Bigr)+\pi_{ij}\Bigl(\partial_{0}h_{ij}-2\partial_{(i}h_{j)0}+\delta_{ij}\mathcal{H}h_{00}-v_{ij}\Bigr)\biggr\}\,.

(2.30)

Varying with respect to the velocity fields yields the on-shell relations

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}_{\star}}{\delta v_{00}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\alpha}a^{D-2}\bigl(v_{00}+v_{ii}\bigr)-\pi_{00}\approx 0\,,

(2.31)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}_{\star}}{\delta v_{0i}}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha}a^{D-2}v_{0i}-\pi_{0i}\approx 0\,,

(2.32)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}_{\star}}{\delta v_{ij}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}a^{D-2}\bigl(v_{ij}-\delta_{ij}v_{kk}\bigr)+\frac{\delta_{ij}}{4\alpha}a^{D-2}\bigl(v_{00}+v_{kk}\bigr)-\pi_{ij}\approx 0\,.

(2.33)

Owing to the gauge-fixing functional, it is now possible to solve for all velocity fields
on-shell,

\displaystyle v_{00}\approx\overline{v}_{00}=\frac{2a^{2-D}}{D\!-\!2}\Bigl[\pi_{ii}-\Bigl(D\!-\!1\!-\!2(D\!-\!2)\alpha\Bigr)\pi_{00}\Bigr]\,,

(2.34)

\displaystyle v_{0i}\approx\overline{v}_{0i}=-\alpha a^{2-D}\pi_{0i}\,,

(2.35)

\displaystyle v_{ij}\approx\overline{v}_{ij}=2a^{2-D}\biggl[\pi_{ij}+\frac{\delta_{ij}(\pi_{00}\!-\!\pi_{kk})}{D\!-\!2}\biggr]\,.

(2.36)

Substituting these solutions as strong equalities back into the extended action gives the gauge-fixed canonical action,

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{\star}\bigl[h_{00},\pi_{00},h_{0i},\pi_{0i},h_{ij},\pi_{ij}\bigr]\equiv\mathcal{S}_{\star}\bigl[h_{00},\overline{v}_{00},\pi_{00},h_{0i},\overline{v}_{0i},\pi_{0i},h_{ij},\overline{v}_{ij},\pi_{ij}\bigr]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\int\!d^{D\!}x\,\Bigl[\pi_{00}\partial_{0}h_{00}+\pi_{0i}\partial_{0}h_{0i}+\pi_{ij}\partial_{0}h_{ij}-\mathscr{H}_{\star}\Bigr]\,.

(2.37)

where the corresponding gauge-fixed Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\star}={}

\displaystyle a^{2-D}\biggl[\pi_{ij}\pi_{ij}-\frac{\pi_{ii}\pi_{jj}}{D\!-\!2}+\frac{2\pi_{00}\pi_{ii}}{D\!-\!2}-\frac{D\!-\!1\!-\!2(D\!-\!2)\alpha}{D\!-\!2}\pi_{00}\pi_{00}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\pi_{0i}\pi_{0i}\biggr]

\displaystyle+\pi_{ij}\Bigl(2\partial_{i}h_{0j}-\delta_{ij}\mathcal{H}h_{00}\Bigr)+\pi_{0i}\biggl[\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\bigl(h_{00}-h_{jj}\bigr)+\partial_{j}h_{ij}-(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}h_{0i}\biggr]

\displaystyle-(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}\pi_{00}h_{00}+a^{D-2}\biggl[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{00})\bigl(\partial_{i}h_{jj}-\partial_{j}h_{ij}\bigr)-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{ik})(\partial_{j}h_{jk})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}h_{ij})(\partial_{j}h_{kk})-\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{k}h_{ii})(\partial_{k}h_{jj})+\frac{1}{4}(\partial_{k}h_{ij})(\partial_{k}h_{ij})\biggr]\,.

(2.38)

The Poisson brackets are determined by the symplectic part of the canonical action and are not affected by gauge fixing:

\displaystyle\bigl\{h_{00}(\eta,\vec{x}),\pi_{00}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr\}={}

\displaystyle\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,,

(2.39a)

\displaystyle\bigl\{h_{0i}(\eta,\vec{x}),\pi_{0j}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr\}={}

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,,

(2.39b)

\displaystyle\bigl\{h_{ij}(\eta,\vec{x}),\pi_{kl}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr\}={}

\displaystyle\delta_{i(k}\delta_{l)j}\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,,

(2.39c)

The equations of motion that follow from this canonical action are:

\displaystyle\partial_{0}h_{00}\approx{}

\displaystyle\frac{2a^{2-D}}{D\!-\!2}\Bigl[\pi_{ii}-\Bigl(D\!-\!1\!-\!2(D\!-\!2)\alpha\Bigr)\pi_{00}\Bigr]-(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}h_{00}\,,

(2.40)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\pi_{00}\approx{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\pi_{0i}+(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}\pi_{00}+\mathcal{H}\pi_{ii}+\frac{a^{D-2}}{2}\bigl(\nabla^{2}h_{ii}-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h_{ij}\bigr)\,,

(2.41)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}h_{0i}\approx{}

\displaystyle-\alpha a^{2-D}\pi_{0i}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\bigl(h_{00}-h_{jj}\bigr)+\partial_{j}h_{ij}-(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}h_{0i}\,,

(2.42)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\pi_{0i}\approx{}

\displaystyle 2\partial_{j}\pi_{ij}+(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\pi_{0i}\,,

(2.43)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}h_{ij}\approx{}

\displaystyle 2a^{2-D}\biggl[\pi_{ij}+\frac{\delta_{ij}\bigl(\pi_{00}-\pi_{kk}\bigr)}{D\!-\!2}\biggr]+2\partial_{(i}h_{j)0}-\delta_{ij}\mathcal{H}h_{00}\,,

(2.44)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\pi_{ij}\approx{}

\displaystyle\partial_{(i}\pi_{j)0}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2}\partial_{k}\pi_{0k}+\frac{a^{D-2}}{2}\Bigl[-\bigl(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}-\delta_{ij}\nabla^{2}\bigr)h_{00}-2\partial_{k}\partial_{(i}h_{j)k}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h_{kk}+\delta_{ij}\bigl(\partial_{k}\partial_{l}-\delta_{kl}\nabla^{2}\bigr)h_{kl}+\nabla^{2}h_{ij}\Bigr]\,.

(2.45)

These can be written as Hamilton equations with the help of the Poisson
brackets (
2.39
).

We note that, when working with multiplier gauges, the gauge-fixed action does not
generate the first-class constraints (
2.18
)
and (
2.19
) that are present in the gauge-invariant action.
These constraints must therefore be imposed
in addition
to the gauge-fixed action:

\Phi_{0}=\pi_{00}\,,\qquad\Phi_{i}=\pi_{0i}\,,\qquad\Psi_{0}=\frac{a^{D-2}}{2}\bigl(\nabla^{2}h_{ii}-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h_{ij}\bigr)+\mathcal{H}\pi_{ii}\,,\qquad\Psi_{i}=2\partial_{j}\pi_{ij}\,.

(2.46)

It is sufficient to impose these constraints at the level of initial conditions, since they
satisfy a closed system of equations of motion:

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\Phi_{0}\approx{}

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}+(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}\Phi_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\Phi_{i}\,,

(2.47)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\Phi_{i}\approx{}

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}+(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\Phi_{i}\,,

(2.48)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\Psi_{0}\approx{}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\Psi_{i}+\nabla^{2}\Phi_{0}+\mathcal{H}\Psi_{0}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}\partial_{i}\Phi_{i}\,,

(2.49)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\Psi_{i}\approx{}

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\Phi_{i}\,,

(2.50)

These relations guarantee the conservation of the constraints. Consequently, they form a
closed first-class algebra also in the gauge-fixed canonical formulation. In the next
section we quantize this gauge-fixed canonical system, and in subsequent sections we
construct the graviton two-point function.

3
Graviton quantization

In this section, we quantize the linearized graviton field in the multiplier gauge, starting
from the canonical formulation of the previous section. We promote the fields and their
conjugate momenta to operators, replace the classical Poisson brackets by equal-time
commutation relations, and implement the constraints as conditions on the space of states.
This establishes the operator algebra and mode decomposition needed later for the
construction of the graviton two-point function.

We thus promote the classical fields and conjugate momenta to Hermitian operators,

h_{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow\hat{h}_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\quad\pi_{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow\hat{\pi}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(3.1)

and replace their Poisson brackets by equal-time canonical commutation relations,

\displaystyle\bigl[\hat{h}_{00}(\eta,\vec{x}),\hat{\pi}_{00}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr]={}

\displaystyle i\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,,

(3.2a)

\displaystyle\bigl[\hat{h}_{0i}(\eta,\vec{x}),\hat{\pi}_{0j}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr]={}

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}\,i\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,,

(3.2b)

\displaystyle\bigl[\hat{h}_{ij}(\eta,\vec{x}),\hat{\pi}_{kl}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr]={}

\displaystyle\delta_{i(k}\delta_{l)j}\,i\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,.

(3.2c)

These field operators satisfy the same equations of motion (
2.40
)–(
2.45
)
as their classical counterparts. First-class constraints (
2.46
)
are promoted to Hermitian constraint operators by substitutions in (
3.1
).

The constraints (
2.46
) of the theory are implemented as a
requirement on the space of state vectors. It would be inconsistent with
the canonical commutation relations to demand that Hermitian constraint operators
annihilate the state. That is why constraints are implemented at the level of matrix
elements, by demanding that matrix elements
of polynomials of the Hermitian constraint operators vanish between physical states.
In particular, for Gaussian states that we consider, it is sufficient here to require that
all two-point functions of Hermitian constraint operators vanish,

\bigl\langle\Omega\bigr|\hat{\Phi}_{\mu}(x)\hat{\Phi}_{\nu}(x^{\prime})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\bigl\langle\Omega\bigr|\hat{\Phi}_{\mu}(x)\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}(x^{\prime})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\bigl\langle\Omega\bigr|\hat{\Psi}_{\mu}(x)\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}(x^{\prime})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,

(3.3)

for all physical states
\lvert\Omega\rangle
. This requirement is implemented
as a condition on the state vectors by demanding that four particular
non-Hermitian

linear combinations of the constraint operators annihilate the physical state
(see
[
63
]
for a detailed discussion in the vector field case).
The structure of the state space satisfying these conditions becomes most
transparent after decomposing the fields into scalar, vector, and tensor components
and transforming to momentum space. We now turn to these decompositions before
completing the description of the quantized theory.

3.1
Scalar-vector-tensor decomposition

To separate physical and gauge degrees of freedom, it is convenient to
decompose spatial tensors into their scalar, vector, and tensor parts with respect to
spatial rotations. This is achieved by introducing transverse and longitudinal projectors,

\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}=\delta_{ij}\!-\!\frac{\partial_{i}\partial_{j}}{\nabla^{2}}\,,\qquad\qquad\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}=\frac{\partial_{i}\partial_{j}}{\nabla^{2}}\,,

(3.4)

which are idempotent and mutually orthogonal,

\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\mathbb{P}_{jk}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}=\mathbb{P}_{ik}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\,,\qquad\quad\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\mathbb{P}_{jk}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}=\mathbb{P}_{ik}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\,,\qquad\quad\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\mathbb{P}_{jk}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\mathbb{P}_{jk}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}=0\,,

(3.5)

so that any spatial tensor can be decomposed uniquely into scalar, vector, and tensor pieces.

With these projectors, we decompose the canonical variables into their scalar, vector,
and tensor parts. The decomposition is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{00}={}

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{1}\,,\quad

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{0i}=\frac{\partial_{i}}{\nabla^{2}}\hat{S}_{2}+\hat{V}^{1}_{i}\,,\quad

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{ij}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\hat{S}_{3}+\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\hat{S}_{4}+\frac{2}{\nabla^{2}}\partial_{(i}\hat{V}^{2}_{j)}+\hat{T}_{ij}\,.

(3.6a)

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{00}={}

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{1}\,,\quad

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{0i}=-\partial_{i}\hat{\Pi}_{2}+\hat{\Pi}^{1}_{i}\,,\quad

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{ij}=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\hat{\Pi}_{3}}{D\!-\!2}+\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\hat{\Pi}_{4}-\partial_{(i}\hat{\Pi}^{2}_{j)}+\hat{\Pi}_{ij}\,.

(3.6b)

Here the vectors are transverse,

\partial_{i}\hat{V}_{i}^{I}=0\,,\qquad\quad\partial_{i}\hat{\Pi}_{i}^{I}=0\ ,

(3.7)

and the tensors are transverse and traceless,

\partial_{i}\hat{T}_{ij}=0\,,\qquad\quad\partial_{i}\hat{\Pi}_{ij}=0\ ,\qquad\quad\hat{T}_{ii}=0\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\Pi}_{ii}=0\,.

(3.8)

As we show below, this decomposition renders both the canonical structure and the
dynamics block-diagonal. The transverse-traceless tensor modes already carry the physical
graviton degrees of freedom, while the scalar and vector modes are further constrained by
the quantum conditions (
3.3
).

Scalar sector.

From the definitions above, we can express the four scalar variables and their canonical
momenta in terms of the original canonical variables,

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{1}=\hat{h}_{00}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{2}=\partial_{i}\hat{h}_{0i}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{3}=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\hat{h}_{ij}}{D\!-\!2}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{4}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\hat{h}_{ij}\,,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{1}=\hat{\pi}_{00}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{2}=-\frac{\partial_{i}}{\nabla^{2}}\hat{\pi}_{0i}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{3}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\hat{\pi}_{ij}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{4}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle L}\hat{\pi}_{ij}\,.

(3.10)

The nonvanishing canonical commutators in this basis are

\bigl[\hat{S}_{I}(\eta,\vec{x}),\hat{\Pi}_{J}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\delta_{IJ}\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,,\qquad\quad I,J=1,2,3,4\,.

(3.11)

This sector contains eight first-order equations of motion, one for each scalar variable
and its conjugate momentum:

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{S}_{1}={}

\displaystyle\frac{2a^{2-D}}{D\!-\!2}\Bigl[-\bigl(D\!-\!1\!-\!2(D\!-\!2)\alpha\bigr)\hat{\Pi}_{1}+\hat{\Pi}_{3}+\hat{\Pi}_{4}\Bigr]-(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}\hat{S}_{1}\,,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\Pi}_{1}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}\hat{\Pi}_{2}+\mathcal{H}\Bigl[(D\!-\!3)\hat{\Pi}_{1}+\hat{\Pi}_{3}+\hat{\Pi}_{4}\Bigr]+\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}a^{D-2}\nabla^{2}\hat{S}_{3}\,,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{S}_{2}={}

\displaystyle\alpha a^{2-D}\nabla^{2}\hat{\Pi}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}\Bigl[\hat{S}_{1}-(D\!-\!2)\hat{S}_{3}+\hat{S}_{4}\Bigr]-(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{S}_{2}\,,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\Pi}_{2}={}

\displaystyle(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\Pi}_{2}-2\hat{\Pi}_{4}\,,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{S}_{3}={}

\displaystyle\frac{2a^{2-D}}{D\!-\!2}\bigl(\hat{\Pi}_{1}-\hat{\Pi}_{4}\bigr)-\mathcal{H}\hat{S}_{1}\,,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\Pi}_{3}={}

\displaystyle\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}\nabla^{2}\Bigl[\hat{\Pi}_{2}+a^{D-2}\bigl(\hat{S}_{1}-(D\!-\!3)\hat{S}_{3}\bigr)\Bigr]\,,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{S}_{4}={}

\displaystyle\frac{2a^{2-D}}{D\!-\!2}\Bigl[\hat{\Pi}_{1}-\hat{\Pi}_{3}+(D\!-\!3)\hat{\Pi}_{4}\Bigr]+2\hat{S}_{2}-\mathcal{H}\hat{S}_{1}\,,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\Pi}_{4}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}\hat{\Pi}_{2}\,.

(3.19)

Vector sector.

The two transverse vectors and their conjugate momenta, expressed in terms of the
original canonical variables, are

\hat{V}_{i}^{1}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\hat{h}_{0j}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\Pi}_{i}^{1}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\hat{\pi}_{0j}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{V}_{i}^{2}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\partial_{k}\hat{h}_{jk}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\Pi}_{i}^{2}=-2\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\frac{\partial_{k}}{\nabla^{2}}\hat{\pi}_{jk}\,,

(3.20)

so that the nonvanishing commutators in this sector read

\bigl[\hat{V}_{i}^{I}(\eta,\vec{x}),\hat{\Pi}_{j}^{J}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\delta_{IJ}\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,,\qquad\quad I,J=1,2\,.

(3.21)

The four first-order equations of motion in this sector are:

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{V}^{1}_{i}={}

\displaystyle-\alpha a^{2-D}\hat{\Pi}^{1}_{i}+\hat{V}^{2}_{i}-(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{V}^{1}_{i}\,,

(3.22)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\Pi}^{1}_{i}={}

\displaystyle-\nabla^{2}\hat{\Pi}^{2}_{i}+(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\Pi}^{1}_{i}\,,

(3.23)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{V}^{2}_{i}={}

\displaystyle-a^{2-D}\nabla^{2}\hat{\Pi}^{2}_{i}+\nabla^{2}\hat{V}_{i}^{1}\,,

(3.24)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\Pi}^{2}_{i}={}

\displaystyle-\hat{\Pi}^{1}_{i}\,.

(3.25)

Tensor sector.

Finally, the transverse-traceless tensor modes are expressed as

\hat{T}_{ij}=\biggl[\mathbb{P}_{k(i}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\mathbb{P}_{j)l}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}-\frac{\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\mathbb{P}_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}}{D\!-\!2}\biggr]\hat{h}_{kl}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\Pi}_{ij}=\biggl[\mathbb{P}_{k(i}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\mathbb{P}_{j)l}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}-\frac{\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\mathbb{P}_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}}{D\!-\!2}\biggr]\hat{\pi}_{kl}\,.

(3.26)

and they satisfy the following non-vanishing commutation relation

\bigl[\hat{T}_{ij}(\eta,\vec{x}),\hat{\Pi}_{kl}(\eta,\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\biggl[\mathbb{P}_{i(k}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\mathbb{P}_{l)j}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}-\frac{\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}\mathbb{P}_{kl}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}}{D\!-\!2}\biggr]\delta^{D-1}(\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime})\,.

(3.27)

In this sector the equations of motion take the form

\partial_{0}\hat{T}_{ij}=2a^{2-D}\hat{\Pi}_{ij}\,,\qquad\quad\partial_{0}\hat{\Pi}_{ij}=\frac{a^{D-2}}{2}\nabla^{2}\hat{T}_{ij}\,.

(3.28)

Constraints.

The scalar–vector–tensor decomposition in (
3.6
) induces an analogous
decomposition of the Hermitian constraint operators,

\hat{\Phi}_{0}=\hat{K}_{1}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\Phi}_{i}=-\partial_{i}\hat{K}_{2}+\hat{K}_{i}^{1}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\Psi}_{0}=\hat{K}_{3}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\Psi}_{i}=2\partial_{i}\hat{K}_{4}-\nabla^{2}\hat{K}_{i}^{2}\,,

(3.29)

so that only scalar and vector components appear. The scalar components are

\hat{K}_{1}=\hat{\Pi}_{1}\,,\qquad\hat{K}_{2}=\hat{\Pi}_{2}\,,\qquad\hat{K}_{3}=\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}a^{D-2}\nabla^{2}\hat{S}_{3}+\mathcal{H}\bigl(\hat{\Pi}_{3}+\hat{\Pi}_{4}\bigr)\,,\qquad\hat{K}_{4}=\hat{\Pi}_{4}\,,

(3.30)

and the vector components are

\hat{K}_{i}^{1}=\hat{\Pi}_{i}^{1}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{K}_{i}^{2}=\hat{\Pi}_{i}^{2}\,.

(3.31)

The equations of motion for these combinations split into the scalar sector,

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{K}_{1}={}

\displaystyle(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}\hat{K}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}\hat{K}_{2}+\hat{K}_{3}\,,

(3.32)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{K}_{2}={}

\displaystyle(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{K}_{2}-2\hat{K}_{4}\,,

(3.33)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{K}_{3}={}

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\hat{K}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}\nabla^{2}\hat{K}_{2}+\mathcal{H}\hat{K}_{3}-\nabla^{2}\hat{K}_{4}\,,

(3.34)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{K}_{4}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}\hat{K}_{2}\,,

(3.35)

and the vector sector,

\partial_{0}\hat{K}_{i}^{1}=(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{K}_{i}^{1}-\nabla^{2}\hat{K}_{i}^{2}\,,\qquad\quad\partial_{0}\hat{K}_{i}^{2}=-\hat{K}_{i}^{1}\,.

(3.36)

These relations will be used to track the time evolution of the constraint operators
once we move to Fourier space and impose the quantum conditions (
3.3
)
on a mode-by-mode basis.

3.2
Fourier space

Because the background is spatially homogeneous and isotropic, it is natural to work in
Fourier space. Each comoving wavevector
\vec{k}
then evolves independently, and both the
canonical structure and the constraint conditions can be implemented mode by mode in the
tensor, vector, and scalar sectors.

Tensor sector.

In the tensor sector we expand the transverse–traceless operators in a basis of
polarization tensors for each comoving wavevector
\vec{k}
. The two field operators
are written as

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{ij}(\eta,\vec{x})={}

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{a^{\frac{D-2}{2}}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\!\sum_{\sigma=1}^{\frac{D(D-3)}{2}}\!\!\varepsilon_{ij}(\sigma,\vec{k})\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(3.37)

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{ij}(\eta,\vec{x})={}

\displaystyle\frac{a^{\frac{D-2}{2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\!\sum_{\sigma=1}^{\frac{D(D-3)}{2}}\!\!\varepsilon_{ij}(\sigma,\vec{k})\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(3.38)

where the symmetric transverse–traceless polarization tensors satisfy

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{ii}(\sigma,\vec{k})=0\,,\qquad\quad k_{i}\,\varepsilon_{ij}(\sigma,\vec{k})=0\,,\qquad\quad\bigl[\varepsilon_{ij}(\sigma,\vec{k})\bigr]^{*}=\varepsilon_{ij}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,,

(3.39a)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{ij}^{*}(\sigma,\vec{k})\varepsilon_{ij}(\sigma^{\prime},\vec{k})=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\,,\qquad\sum_{\sigma=1}^{\frac{D(D-3)}{2}}\varepsilon^{*}_{ij}(\sigma,\vec{k})\varepsilon_{kl}(\sigma,\vec{k})={\rm P}_{i(k}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\vec{k}){\rm P}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}_{l)j}(\vec{k})-\frac{{\rm P}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}_{ij}(\vec{k}){\rm P}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}_{kl}(\vec{k})}{D\!-\!2}\,,

(3.39b)

where
{\rm P}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}_{ij}(\vec{k})\!=\!\delta_{ij}\!-\!k_{i}k_{j}/k^{2}
is the momentum-space
transverse projector. The momentum-space operators are Hermitian in the sense that

\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(\eta,\vec{k})\!=\!\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}(\eta,-\vec{k})

and
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(\eta,\vec{k})\!=\!\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\eta,-\vec{k})
,
and satisfy the following non-vanishing commutation relation

\bigl[\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k}),\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\eta,\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\,i\delta^{D-1}(\vec{k}\!+\!\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\,.

(3.40)

The momentum–space equations of motion are

\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}=\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}\,,\qquad\quad\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}=-k^{2}\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}\,.

(3.41)

Thus each tensor polarization behaves as an oscillator with a time-dependent mass;
this sector encodes the physical graviton degrees of freedom.

Vector sector.

In the vector sector, the two transverse modes for each comoving wavevector
\vec{k}
are expanded in a basis of polarization vectors, yielding two canonical pairs per polarization. The corresponding operator expansions are

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{I}_{i}(\eta,\vec{x})={}

\displaystyle a^{-\frac{D-2}{2}}\!\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\,g_{I}\!\times\!\sum_{\sigma=1}^{D-2}\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma,\vec{k})\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{I,\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(3.42)

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}^{I}_{i}(\eta,\vec{x})={}

\displaystyle a^{\frac{D-2}{2}}\!\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\,g_{I}^{-1}\!\times\!\sum_{\sigma=1}^{D-2}\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma,\vec{k})\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{I,\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(3.43)

where
I\!=\!1,2
, with coefficients
g_{1}\!=\!1
and
g_{2}\!=\!k
chosen so that all Fourier amplitudes carry the same dimension. The transverse polarization vectors satisfy

\displaystyle k_{i}\,\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma,\vec{k})=0\,,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}^{*}(\sigma,\vec{k})=\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,,

(3.44a)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}^{*}(\sigma,\vec{k})\,\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma^{\prime},\vec{k})=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma=1}^{D-2}\varepsilon_{i}^{*}(\sigma,\vec{k})\,\varepsilon_{j}(\sigma,\vec{k})={\rm P}^{\scriptscriptstyle T}_{ij}(\vec{k})\,.

(3.44b)

Hermiticity implies

\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{I,\sigma}^{\dagger}(\eta,\vec{k})\!=\!\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{I,\sigma}(\eta,-\vec{k})

and

\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{I,\sigma}^{\dagger}(\eta,\vec{k})\!=\!\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{I,\sigma}(\eta,-\vec{k})
.
The momentum-space commutation relations are

\bigl[\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{I,\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k}),\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{J,\sigma^{\prime}}(\eta,\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\delta_{IJ}\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\,i\delta^{D-1}(\vec{k}\!+\!\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\,.

(3.45)

The equations of motion in Fourier space take the form

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{1,\sigma}={}

\displaystyle-\alpha\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1,\sigma}+k\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{2,\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{1,\sigma}\,,

(3.46)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1,\sigma}={}

\displaystyle k\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2,\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1,\sigma}\,,

(3.47)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{2,\sigma}={}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2,\sigma}-k\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{1,\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{2,\sigma}\,,

(3.48)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2,\sigma}={}

\displaystyle-k\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1,\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2,\sigma}\,.

(3.49)

These relations describe the coupled evolution of the two transverse vector modes for each
\vec{k}
, whose contributions to physical correlators are further restricted by the vector constraints.

For the constraints in the vector sector we expand them in Fourier space as

\hat{K}^{I}_{i}(\eta,\vec{x})=a^{\frac{D-2}{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\,g_{I}^{-1}\!\times\!\sum_{\sigma=1}^{D-2}\varepsilon_{i}(\sigma,\vec{k})\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{I,\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(3.50)

such that the equations of motion read

\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1,\sigma}=k\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2,\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1,\sigma}\,,\qquad\quad\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2,\sigma}=-k\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1,\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2,\sigma}\,.

(3.51)

These match Eqs. (
3.47
) and (
3.49
), which is
expected given that by definitions we
have
\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{I,\sigma}\!=\!\mathcal{\hat{P}}_{I,\sigma}
.

Scalar sector.

In the scalar sector, the four canonical scalar pairs are expanded in Fourier modes as

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{I}(\eta,\vec{x})={}

\displaystyle a^{-\frac{D-2}{2}}\!\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\,f_{I}\!\times\!\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{I}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(3.52)

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{I}(\eta,\vec{x})={}

\displaystyle a^{\frac{D-2}{2}}\!\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\,f_{I}^{-1}\!\times\!\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{I}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,

(3.53)

where
I\!=\!1,2,3,4
, and where
f_{1}\!=\!f_{3}\!=\!f_{4}\!=\!1
and
f_{2}\!=\!k
, chosen so that momentum space operators would have the same dimension. Hermiticity of the
momentum-space operators implies

\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{I}^{\dagger}(\eta,\vec{k})\!=\!\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{I}(\eta,-\vec{k})
and

\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{I}^{\dagger}(\eta,\vec{k})\!=\!\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{I}(\eta,-\vec{k})
.
The non-vanishing commutators in the momentum space are

\bigl[\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{I}(\eta,\vec{k}),\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{J}(\eta,\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\delta_{IJ}\,i\delta^{D-1}(\vec{k}\!+\!\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\,,

(3.54)

while the equations of motion are:

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}={}

\displaystyle\frac{2}{D\!-\!2}\Bigl[-\bigl(D\!-\!1\!-\!2(D\!-\!2)\alpha\bigr)\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1}+\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{3}+\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{4}\Bigr]-\frac{D\!-\!4}{2}\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}\,,

(3.55)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1}={}

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2}+\mathcal{H}\Bigl[\frac{D\!-\!4}{2}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1}+\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{3}+\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{4}\Bigr]-\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}k^{2}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{3}\,,

(3.56)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{2}={}

\displaystyle-\alpha\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2}-\frac{k}{2}\Bigl[\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}-(D\!-\!2)\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{3}+\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{4}\Bigr]-\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{2}\,,

(3.57)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2}={}

\displaystyle-2k\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{4}+\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2}\,,

(3.58)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{3}={}

\displaystyle\frac{2}{D\!-\!2}\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1}-\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{4}\bigr)-\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}+\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{3}\,,

(3.59)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{3}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}\Bigl[k\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2}+\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{3}+k^{2}\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}-(D\!-\!3)\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{3}\bigr)\Bigr]\,,

(3.60)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{4}={}

\displaystyle\frac{2}{D\!-\!2}\Bigl[\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1}-\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{3}+(D\!-\!3)\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{4}\Bigr]+2k\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{2}-\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}+\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{4}\,,

(3.61)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{4}={}

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2}-\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{4}\,.

(3.62)

For each
\vec{k}
, the scalar sector forms a closed system of coupled first-order equations whose physical content will be determined by the scalar constraints.

We next introduce the Fourier transforms of the scalar constraint operators,

\hat{K}_{I}(\eta,\vec{x})=a^{\frac{D-2}{2}}\!\!\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\,\ell_{I}\!\times\!\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{I}(\eta,\vec{k})\,,\qquad\quad I=1,2,3,4\,,

(3.63)

where
\ell_{1}\!=\!\ell_{4}\!=\!1
, and
\ell_{3}\!=\!1/\ell_{2}\!=\!k
.
In terms of the canonical momenta and scalar fields the constraint modes are

\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}=\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}=\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{3}=-\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}k\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{3}+\frac{\mathcal{H}}{k}\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{3}\!+\!\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{4}\bigr)\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}=\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{4}\,,

(3.64)

and the corresponding equations of motion follow directly from the scalar dynamics,

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}={}

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}+2\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{3}\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!4)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\,,

(3.65)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}={}

\displaystyle-2k\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}+\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\,,

(3.66)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{3}={}

\displaystyle-k\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}-\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}\bigr)-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!3)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!4)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{3}\,,

(3.67)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}={}

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}\,.

(3.68)

We have thus expressed all canonical variables and scalar constraint operators in terms of tensor, vector, and scalar mode operators in Fourier space, together with their linear evolution equations. In the subsequent sections we solve the mode equations for the physical tensor sector and the gauge-dependent scalar and vector sectors, impose the constraint conditions (
3.3
) in momentum space, and construct the corresponding graviton two-point function. This is facilitated by the results for the
scalar fields in de Sitter, that we recall next.

4
Scalar mode functions and propagators

Before turning to the dynamics of the tensor, vector, and scalar mode operators
introduced in the previous section, and before constructing the graviton two-point
function, it is useful to recall several standard results for scalar fields in de Sitter
space. In this section we summarize the mode functions and two-point functions of
free scalars with various effective masses. These results will serve as building
blocks for the corresponding graviton expressions that follow.

4.1
Scalar mode functions

Scalar mode functions appear throughout de Sitter–space calculations, and it is useful to recall their basic properties. They satisfy the differential equation

\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\lambda^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\mathscr{U}_{\lambda}=0\,,

(4.1)

where
\lambda
is a parameter associated to the effective scalar field mass as

M_{\lambda}^{2}=\biggl[\Bigl(\frac{D\!-\!1}{2}\Bigr)^{\!2}-\lambda^{2}\biggr]H^{2}\,.

(4.2)

The general solution takes the form of a linear combination

\mathscr{U}_{\lambda}(\eta,\vec{k})=A(\vec{k})U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)+B(\vec{k})U_{\lambda}^{*}(\eta,k)\,,

(4.3)

where the positive-frequency Chernikov–Tagirov–Bunch–Davies (CTBD) mode function
[
64
,
65
]
is

U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)=e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}(2\lambda+1)}e^{\frac{-ik}{2H}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{4\mathcal{H}}}\,H_{\lambda}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}\Bigl(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}}\Bigr)\,,

(4.4)

with
H_{\lambda}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}(z)
the Hankel function of the first kind.
In the limit of vanishing Hubble parameter the CTBD mode reduces to the flat-space positive-frequency mode function according to

U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)\ \overset{H\to 0}{\scalebox{1.8}[1]{$\sim$}}\ u(\eta,k)\biggl[1-\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\lambda^{2}\Bigr)\frac{iH}{2k}+\mathcal{O}(H^{2})\biggr]\,,

(4.5)

where
u(\eta,k)=e^{-ik\eta}/\sqrt{2k}
is the standard flat-space positive-frequency mode.
These scalar mode functions will play an important role in expressing the corresponding graviton solutions and two-point functions in subsequent sections.

Recurrence relations for Hankel functions
[
68
,
69
]
imply
corresponding relations among CTBD mode functions, namely

\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\lambda\Bigr)\mathcal{H}\biggr]U_{\lambda}=-ikU_{\lambda+1}\,,\qquad\quad\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\lambda\Bigr)\mathcal{H}\biggr]U_{\lambda}=-ikU_{\lambda-1}\,,

(4.6)

which provide a convenient way of shifting mode indices up or down.
An immediate consequence is a compact expression for the Wronskian normalization,

{\rm Re}\Bigl[U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)U_{\lambda+1}^{*}(\eta,k)\Bigr]=\frac{1}{2k}\,.

(4.7)

The relations (
4.6
), together with the homogeneous mode
equation (
4.1
), also yield the useful identity for a sourced mode equation,

\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\lambda^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\Bigl(\frac{U_{\lambda-1}}{\mathcal{H}}\Bigr)=2ikU_{\lambda}\,.

(4.8)

Another useful identity relates bilinears of CTBD modes
evaluated at different times,

\frac{2\lambda}{k^{2}}\Bigl[\mathcal{H}\partial_{0}^{\prime}+\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\partial_{0}+\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\Bigr]\Bigl[U_{\lambda}(\eta,k)U_{\lambda}^{*}(\eta^{\prime},k)\Bigr]=U_{\lambda+1}(\eta,k)U_{\lambda+1}^{*}(\eta^{\prime},k)-U_{\lambda-1}(\eta,k)U_{\lambda-1}^{*}(\eta^{\prime},k)\,.

(4.9)

Taken together, these relations streamline later computations by reducing derivative
structures and allowing systematic index shifts in products of mode functions.

With this prescription, the scalar two-point functions in de Sitter space take the following form:

i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-6.18059pt\kern 6.56947pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-6.18059pt\kern 6.56947pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-5.44861pt{\tt a\!}\kern 4.08334pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-3.69446pt\kern 4.08334pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.28194pt{\tt a\!}\kern 2.91667pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-2.52779pt\kern 2.91667pt}}}\bigr]_{\lambda}(x;x^{\prime})=\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\bigl(y_{\tt ab}\bigr)+\Delta\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\bigl(y_{\tt ab},u,v\bigr)\,,

(4.13)

The de Sitter invariance of the first contribution is manifested through its dependence on the
de Sitter-invariant length function

y_{\scriptscriptstyle-+}=H^{2}aa^{\prime}\Bigl[\|\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime}\|^{2}-\bigl(\eta\!-\!\eta^{\prime}\!-\!i\varepsilon\bigr)^{\!2}\Bigr]\,,\qquad y_{\scriptscriptstyle++}=H^{2}aa^{\prime}\Bigl[\|\vec{x}\!-\!\vec{x}^{\,\prime}\|^{2}-\bigl(|\eta\!-\!\eta^{\prime}|\!-\!i\varepsilon\bigr)^{\!2}\Bigr]\,,

(4.14)

with
y_{\scriptscriptstyle+-}\!=\!y_{\scriptscriptstyle-+}^{*}
and
y_{\scriptscriptstyle--}\!=\!y_{\scriptscriptstyle++}^{*}
.
In contrast, the de Sitter–breaking part is characterized by its dependence on

u=\ln(aa^{\prime})\,,\qquad\quad v=\ln(a/a^{\prime})\,.

(4.15)

Henceforth, we omit explicitly denoting the Schwinger–Keldysh indices on
y

that determine its
i\varepsilon

The de Sitter invariant part of (

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y)=\frac{H^{D-2}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D-1}{2}\!+\!\lambda\bigr)\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D-1}{2}\!-\!\lambda\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D}{2}\bigr)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(\!\Bigl\{\frac{D\!-\!1}{2}\!+\!\lambda,\frac{D\!-\!1}{2}\!-\!\lambda\Bigr\},\Bigl\{\frac{D}{2}\Bigr\},1\!-\!\frac{y}{4}\biggr)\,,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y)=\frac{H^{D-2}\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D-2}{2}\bigr)}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}\biggl\{\Bigl(\frac{y}{4}\Bigr)^{\!-\frac{D-2}{2}}+\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{4-D}{2}\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\lambda\bigr)\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\lambda\bigr)}\times

\displaystyle\times\!\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\biggl[\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{3}{2}\!+\!\lambda\!+\!n\bigr)\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{3}{2}\!-\!\lambda\!+\!n\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{6-D}{2}\!+\!n\bigr)\,(n\!+\!1)!}\Bigl(\frac{y}{4}\Bigr)^{\!n-\frac{D-4}{2}}-\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D-1}{2}\!+\!\lambda\!+\!n\bigr)\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D-1}{2}\!-\!\lambda\!+\!n\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D}{2}\!+\!n\bigr)\,n!}\Bigl(\frac{y}{4}\Bigr)^{\!n}\biggr]\biggr\}\,.

(4.17)

The de Sitter–breaking contribution, which arises for tachyonic effective
masses
[

and also in the massless case
[
66

,
is given by the multiple series

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y,u,v)=\frac{H^{D-2}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}\frac{\Gamma(\lambda)\,\Gamma(2\lambda)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D-1}{2}\bigr)\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!+\!\lambda\bigr)}\!\!\sum_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor\lambda-\frac{D-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k}c_{nk\ell}

\displaystyle\times\frac{e^{(\lambda-\frac{D-1}{2}-n)u}}{\bigl(\lambda\!-\!\frac{D-1}{2}\!-\!n\bigr)}\Bigl[y\!+\!4\,{\rm sh}^{2}\Bigl(\frac{v}{2}\Bigr)\Bigr]^{k}\,{\rm ch}\bigl[(n\!-\!k\!-\!2\ell)v\bigr]\,,

(4.18)

with coefficients

\displaystyle c_{nk\ell}=\frac{\bigl(-\frac{1}{4}\bigr)^{\!n}}{k!\,\ell!\,(n\!-\!k\!-\!\ell)!}\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D-1}{2}\bigr)\,\Gamma^{2}(1\!-\!\lambda)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{D-1}{2}\!+\!k\bigr)\,\Gamma(\ell\!+\!1\!-\!\lambda)\,\Gamma(n\!-\!k\!-\!\ell\!+\!1\!-\!\lambda)}\,.

(4.19)

\frac{2\lambda}{\nabla^{2}}\Bigl[\mathcal{H}\partial_{0}^{\prime}+\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\partial_{0}+(D\!-\!1)\mathcal{H}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\Bigr]i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-6.18059pt\kern 6.56947pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-6.18059pt\kern 6.56947pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-5.44861pt{\tt a\!}\kern 4.08334pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-3.69446pt\kern 4.08334pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.28194pt{\tt a\!}\kern 2.91667pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-2.52779pt\kern 2.91667pt}}}\bigr]_{\lambda}(x;x^{\prime})=i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-6.18059pt\kern 6.56947pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-6.18059pt\kern 6.56947pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-5.44861pt{\tt a\!}\kern 4.08334pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-3.69446pt\kern 4.08334pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.28194pt{\tt a\!}\kern 2.91667pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-2.52779pt\kern 2.91667pt}}}\bigr]_{\lambda-1}(x;x^{\prime})-i\bigl[\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-6.18059pt\kern 6.56947pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-8.43642pt{\tt a\!}\kern 6.56947pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-6.18059pt\kern 6.56947pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-5.44861pt{\tt a\!}\kern 4.08334pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-3.69446pt\kern 4.08334pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\tt a\!}}}}\Delta^{{\kern-4.28194pt{\tt a\!}\kern 2.91667pt{\tt\!b}}}_{{\kern-2.52779pt\kern 2.91667pt}}}\bigr]_{\lambda+1}(x;x^{\prime})\,,

5
Graviton field dynamics and state

In this section we solve the momentum–space equations of motion:
(
3.41
) for the tensor sector,
(
3.46
)–(
3.49
) for the vector sector,
and (
3.55
)–(
3.62
) for the scalar sector, together with the equations of motion for the constraint operators in the latter two sectors.
The solutions are expressed in terms of the scalar mode functions presented in the previous section, which provide the time–dependent structure common to all three sectors.
We also specify the quantum state for each sector, thereby completing the definition of the framework necessary for computing the graviton two-point function in the subsequent section.

5.1
Tensor sector

The two tensor equations of motion combine into a single homogeneous second–order
equation that determines the field,

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}={}

\displaystyle 0\,.

(5.1)

with

\nu=\frac{D\!-\!1}{2}\,.

(5.2)

The corresponding momentum field is then obtained from

\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}=\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu\Bigr)\mathcal{H}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}\,.

(5.3)

We can recognize the second–order equation (
5.1
) as the scalar
mode equation (
4.1
), while the associated first–order equation is
essentially a recurrence relation (
4.6
). Therefore, the solutions for the
tensor sector field operators are

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})={}

\displaystyle U_{\nu}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\sigma,\vec{k})+U_{\nu}^{*}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{\dagger}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})={}

\displaystyle-ikU_{\nu-1}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\sigma,\vec{k})+ikU_{\nu-1}^{*}(\eta,k)\,\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{\dagger}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,.

(5.5)

The time-independent operators in the solutions above are constants of integration.
Their commutation relations follow from (
3.40
), and
they obey the standard commutation relations for creation and annihilation operators,

\bigl[\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\sigma,\vec{k}),\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{\dagger}(\sigma^{\prime},\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\,\delta^{D-1}(\vec{k}\!-\!\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\,.

(5.6)

They therefore provide the natural operators with which to define the Fock space
of tensor-sector states, whose vacuum is the CTBD analogue specified by

\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(\sigma,\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\forall\vec{k},\sigma\,.

(5.7)

This is the tensor-sector state assumed in the computation of two-point functions.

5.2
Vector sector

In the vector sector the two constraint equations (
3.51
) decouple
from the dynamical ones, so it is convenient to solve them first. They combine into
a single homogeneous second–order equation for the first constraint operator,

\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1,\sigma}=0\,,

(5.8)

and its solution determines the second constraint through

\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2,\sigma}=\frac{1}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu\Bigr)\mathcal{H}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1,\sigma}\,.

(5.9)

Using the results collected in Sec.
4.1
, we can
immediately write down the corresponding mode expansions,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1,\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})={}

\displaystyle kU_{\nu}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\sigma,\vec{k})+kU_{\nu}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}^{\dagger}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,,

(5.10)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2,\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})={}

\displaystyle-ikU_{\nu-1}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\sigma,\vec{k})+ikU_{\nu-1}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}^{\dagger}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,.

(5.11)

These solutions for constraints then immediately determine the two canonical momenta
of the
vector sector,
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1,\sigma}\!=\!\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1,\sigma}
and

\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2,\sigma}\!=\!\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2,\sigma}
.

The remaining two equations (
3.46
) and (
3.48
)
of the vector sector combine into a single second–order equation sourced by solutions
for the constraints,

\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{2,\sigma}=-(1\!-\!\alpha)k\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1,\sigma}\,,

(5.12)

while the remaining field is determined from

\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{1,\sigma}=-\frac{1}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu\Bigr)\mathcal{H}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{2,\sigma}+\frac{\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2,\sigma}}{k}\,.

(5.13)

The corresponding solutions take the form

\displaystyle\!\!\!\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{2,\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})=U_{\nu}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(\sigma,\vec{k})+U_{\nu}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}^{\dagger}(\sigma,-\vec{k})+v_{2}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\sigma,\vec{k})+v_{2}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}^{\dagger}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,,

(5.14)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{1,\sigma}(\eta,\vec{k})=iU_{\nu-1}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(\sigma,\vec{k})-iU_{\nu-1}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}^{\dagger}(\sigma,-\vec{k})+iv_{1}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\sigma,\vec{k})-iv_{1}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}^{\dagger}(\sigma,-\vec{k})\,,

(5.15)

where the particular mode functions satisfy

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]v_{2}={}

\displaystyle-(1\!-\!\alpha)k^{2}U_{\nu}\,,

(5.16)

\displaystyle v_{1}={}

\displaystyle\frac{i}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu\Bigr)\mathcal{H}\biggr]v_{2}-U_{\nu-1}\,.

(5.17)

The particular mode functions are obtained by using the identity in (
4.8
)
together with the recurrence relations (
4.6
),

\displaystyle v_{2}={}

\displaystyle\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)ik}{2\mathcal{H}}\biggl[U_{\nu-1}-\frac{\mathcal{H}}{H}U_{\nu}\biggr]+\frac{1\!-\!(1\!-\!\alpha)\nu}{2}U_{\nu}\,,

(5.18)

\displaystyle v_{1}={}

\displaystyle\frac{(1\!-\!\alpha)ik}{2\mathcal{H}}\biggl[U_{\nu}-\frac{\mathcal{H}}{H}U_{\nu-1}\biggr]-\frac{1\!-\!(1\!-\!\alpha)\nu}{2}U_{\nu-1}\,.

(5.19)

The homogeneous components of these solutions are fixed by imposing the
Wronskian-like normalization condition

{\rm Re}\Bigl(U_{\nu}^{*}v_{1}+U_{\nu-1}^{*}v_{2}\Bigr)=0\,,

(5.20)

and by requiring a regular flat-space limit inferred from (
4.5
),

\displaystyle v_{2}\xrightarrow{H_{0}\to 0}\frac{1}{4}\Bigl[(1\!+\!\alpha)-2(1\!-\!\alpha)ik(\eta\!-\!\eta_{0})\Bigr]u(\eta,k)\,,

(5.21)

\displaystyle v_{1}\xrightarrow{H_{0}\to 0}\frac{1}{4}\Bigl[-(1\!+\!\alpha)-2(1\!-\!\alpha)ik(\eta\!-\!\eta_{0})\Bigr]u(\eta,k)\,.

(5.22)

Having computed the field operators of the vector sector, we can now infer the
commutation relations for the time-independent operators. The only nonvanishing
ones are

\bigl[\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(\sigma,\vec{k}),\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\sigma^{\prime},\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\bigl[\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\sigma,\vec{k}),\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(\sigma^{\prime},\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\bigr]=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\,\delta^{D-1}(\vec{k}\!-\!\vec{k}^{\,\prime})\,,

(5.23)

which shows that these operators are not of canonical creation/annihilation type.
This does not pose a problem; on the contrary, their form is convenient for constructing
the representation of the state space
[
63
]
.

Any physical state must be annihilated by a time-independent, non-Hermitian linear
combination of the vector-sector constraints. The choice of this linear combinations is
not unique, and is rather dictated by convenience. A convenient choice is obtained by
appealing to the flat-space limit in which manifest Lorentz invariance requires the
state to be annihilated by the positive-frequency part of
\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1,\sigma}
.
Using the flat-space limit of the scalar mode functions (
4.5
), this condition
is readily seen to be

\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle P}(\sigma,\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\forall\vec{k},\sigma\,.

(5.24)

This is precisely the choice made in Gupta’s quantization of the graviton
field
[
72
]
, based on the method originally developed for the
electromagnetic field
[
73
,
74
]
, and it generalizes
naturally to de Sitter space.

The construction of the vector sector space of states is completed by defining the
vacuum-like state
\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle
to satisfy

\hat{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle H}(\sigma,\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\forall\vec{k},\sigma\,.

(5.25)

The remainder of the state space is then generated by acting with the two remaining
time-independent operators. This construction yields an indefinite inner-product
space; however, this is not a physical feature, since the physical states are
required to satisfy the subsidiary condition (
5.24
), which
selects a subspace with positive-definite inner product. In computing two-point
functions we will assume a state obeying both (
5.24
) and
(
5.25
).

5.3
Scalar sector

The four constraint equations (
3.65
)–(
3.68
)
decouple from the remaining four equations of motion in the scalar sector. They combine
into two homogeneous second–order equations,

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!(\nu\!-\!1)^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}={}

\displaystyle 0\,,

(5.26a)

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!\nu^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}={}

\displaystyle 0\,,

(5.26b)

which determine two of the fields, while the remaining two are obtained from

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{3}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!(\nu\!-\!1)\Bigr)\mathcal{H}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}-\frac{\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}}{2}\,,

(5.27a)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!\nu\Bigr)\mathcal{H}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\,.

(5.27b)

The two second–order equations (
5.26
)
are in the form of the scalar mode
equation (
4.1
), while Eqs. (
5.27
)
are in the form of recurrence relations (
4.6
),
so that we can immediately write the solutions:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}(\eta,\vec{k})={}

\displaystyle ikU_{\nu-1}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{1}(\vec{k})-ikU_{\nu-1}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{1}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})\,,

(5.28)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}(\eta,\vec{k})={}

\displaystyle kU_{\nu}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{2}(\vec{k})+kU_{\nu}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{2}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})\,,

(5.29)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(\eta,\vec{k})={}

\displaystyle kU_{\nu-2}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{1}(\vec{k})+kU_{\nu-2}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{1}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})-\frac{k}{2}U_{\nu}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{2}(\vec{k})-\frac{k}{2}U_{\nu}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{2}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})\,,

(5.30)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}(\eta,\vec{k})={}

\displaystyle\frac{ik}{2}U_{\nu-1}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{2}(\vec{k})-\frac{ik}{2}U_{\nu-1}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{2}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})\,.

(5.31)

According to their definition (
3.64
), these expressions automatically
determine three of the three canonical momenta,

\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{1}=\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2}=\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\,,\qquad\quad\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{4}=\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}\,,

(5.32)

while the remaining equation we can consider to determine
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{3}

in terms of
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{3}
,

\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{3}=\frac{-2}{(D\!-\!2)k}\biggl[\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{3}-\frac{\mathcal{H}}{k}\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{3}+\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}\bigr)\biggr]\,.

(5.33)

From the remaining four equations we can form two second–order equations, sourced by
the previously obtained constraint solutions,

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!(\nu\!-\!2)^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}={}

\displaystyle-2(1\!-\!\alpha)k\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}+2\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{3}\bigr)\,,

(5.34)

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{0}^{2}+k^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\!-\!(\nu\!-\!1)^{2}\Bigr)\mathcal{H}^{2}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{2}={}

\displaystyle-2(1\!-\!\alpha)k\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}-\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\bigr)\,.

(5.35)

Once these are solved the third canonical momentum can be written as

\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{3}=\frac{D\!-\!2}{2}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!+\!(\nu\!-\!2)\Bigr)\mathcal{H}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}+\bigl[D\!-\!1\!-\!2(D\!-\!2)\alpha\bigr]\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}-\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{4}\,,

(5.36)

which simultaneously fixes
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{3}
through (
5.33
).
This in turn fixes the last scalar,

\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{4}=-\frac{2}{k}\biggl[\partial_{0}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\!+\!(\nu\!-\!1)\Bigr)\mathcal{H}\biggr]\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{2}-\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}+(D\!-\!2)\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{3}-\frac{2\alpha}{k}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{2}\,.

(5.37)

The solutions for the five remaining fields are parametrized by the initial conditions of the
two second–order equations (
5.34
) and (
5.35
),

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}(\eta,\vec{k})=U_{\nu-2}(\eta,k)\hat{d}_{1}(\vec{k})+U_{\nu-2}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{d}_{1}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})+w_{1}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{1}(\vec{k})+w_{1}^{*}(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{1}^{\dagger}(-\vec{k})\,,

(5.38)

four Hermitian constraints (

)–(
5.31

state. As in the vector sector, the choice of these combinations is not unique.
A convenient choice is obtained by examining the flat-space

maintained provided the positive-frequency parts of the first two scalar constraints,

\displaystyle{\tt Minkowski:}\quad\hat{K}_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle(+)}=\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,u(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{1}(\vec{k})\,,\qquad\hat{K}_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(+)}=\int\!\frac{d^{D-1}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\,k^{-1}u(\eta,k)\hat{c}_{2}(\vec{k})\,,

{\tt Minkowski:}\quad\qquad\hat{K}_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle(+)}(\vec{x})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\hat{K}_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(+)}(\vec{x})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\forall\vec{x}\,,

(5.58)

in accordance with the Gupta–Bleuler construction
[
72
]
.

(5.59)

We adopt this same condition in de Sitter space.

To complete the construction of the indefinite-metric state space, one must also
specify the vacuum-like state.
Following the vector-sector analysis, we define it by

\hat{e}_{1}(\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\hat{e}_{2}(\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\forall\vec{k}\,,

(5.60)

so that all other states are generated by acting with the conjugate operators.
Using the transformation (
5.55

\hat{d}_{1}(\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,\qquad\quad\hat{d}_{2}(\vec{k})\bigl|\Omega\bigr\rangle=0\,,

(5.61)

and we assume that the state satisfies both (
5.59
) and
(
5.61
) in the computation of the two-point function in the next section.

7
Discussion

In this work we have employed canonical methods to construct a graviton propagator in
de Sitter space for a one-parameter family of simple non-covariant gauges defined in
(
1.1
). A key advantage of the resulting expression, given at the end of
Sec.
6
, is its tractable and compact structure: the full
propagator is expressed in terms of a small number of scalar propagators with at most
two derivatives acting on them. Moreover, all scalar propagators that appear admit
power-series representations that terminate in
D\!=\!4
, as follows from
(
4.17
). This makes the expression particularly convenient for practical loop
computations and for transparent analyses of gauge dependence in proposed observables,
such as in
[
56
]
.

It is instructive to compare our result with the two-parameter family of gauges
introduced in
[
57
]
, defined by the gauge-fixing action

S_{\rm gf}[h_{\mu\nu}]\!=\!\!\int\!d^{D}x\,a^{D-2}\,\biggl[-\frac{\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathscr{F}_{\mu}\mathscr{F}_{n}u}{2(1\!+\!\delta\alpha)}\biggr]\,,\qquad\mathscr{F}_{\mu}=\eta^{\rho\sigma}\Bigl[\partial_{\rho}h_{\sigma\mu}+(D\!-\!2)\mathcal{H}\delta_{(\rho}^{0}h_{\sigma)\mu}-\frac{1}{2}(1\!+\!\delta\beta)\partial_{\mu}h_{\rho\sigma}\Bigr]\,.

(7.1)

The propagator in that work was obtained perturbatively, to linear order in the
infinitesimal variations
\delta\alpha
and
\delta\beta
.
Our closed-form result exactly reproduces the
\delta\beta\!=\!0
limit of

[
57
]
, demonstrating that the dependence of the propagator on

\alpha
in this two-parameter family is in fact
exactly linear
.
This extends and sharpens the understanding of the structure of this gauge family well
beyond the infinitesimal regime.

We have also verified explicitly that the propagator constructed here satisfies the
correct equation of motion and obeys the full Ward–Takahashi identity, as shown in the
Appendix. In addition, the propagator reduces to the standard Lorentz-invariant form
in the flat-space limit. These checks confirm the internal consistency of the
construction and show that the one-parameter generalization preserves the expected
gauge structure.

The simplicity of the resulting propagator and the presence of a free gauge-fixing parameter
makes it a promising tool for a variety of
applications, including loop computations, analyses of infrared behavior, and systematic
studies of gauge dependence in cosmological graviton observables. Indeed, one such
application utilizing this propagator—demonstrating the gauge independence of the observable introduced in

[
56
]
—is already in progress
[
77
]
.

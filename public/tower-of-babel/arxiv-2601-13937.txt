Abstract
. In 2021, Henk, Schymura and Xue introduced packing minima, associated with a convex body and a lattice, as packing counterparts to the covering minima of Kannan and Lovász. Motivated by conjectures on the volume inequalities for the successive minima, we generalized the definition of the packing minima to the class of all convex bodies that contain the origin in their interior. For these packing minima, we presented several novel volume inequalities and calculated the specific values of the packing minima for several special convex bodies.

primary 52C07; secondary 11H06, 52C05.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{K}^{n}
be the set of all convex bodies, i.e., compact convex sets with non-empty interior in the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{(o)}
be the subset of all convex bodies containing the origin in their interior, i.e.,
{\bf 0}\in\mathrm{int}(K)
. The family of all
o
-symmetric convex bodies, i.e.,
K=-K
, is denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{n}
. If the centroid of a convex body
K
,

\mathrm{cen}(K)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K)}\int_{K}{\bf x}~\mathrm{d}^{n}{\bf x}

is the origin, the
K
is called centered and all centered convex bodies are denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{c}^{n}
; here
\mathrm{vol}(K)
denotes the volume, i.e., the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
K
.

A lattice
\Lambda
is a discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

and by
\det(\Lambda)
we denote its determinant, i.e., if the dimension of
\Lambda
is
k
, then
\det(\Lambda)
is the (
k
-dimensional) volume of a fundamental cell of
\Lambda
.
Let
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the integer lattice, and let
\mathcal{L}^{n}
be the set of all
n
-dimensional lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For
K\in{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{(o)}
and
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
, the
i
-th successive minimum

\lambda_{i}(K,\Lambda)
is the smallest dilation factor
\lambda>0
such that
\lambda K
contains at least
i
linearly independent lattice points of
\Lambda
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(K,\Lambda):=\min\{\lambda>0:\dim(\lambda K\cap\Lambda)=i\}.

Apparently,
\lambda_{1}(K,\Lambda)\leq\lambda_{2}(K,\Lambda)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}(K,\Lambda)
. The concept of successive minima was firstly introduced for
o
-symmetric convex bodies by Minkowski in his fundamental and guiding book "Geometrie der Zahlen" in 1896
[
9
]
.
In order to make this concept of successive minima available for arbitrary convex bodies we also need the difference body
K_{s}
of a convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
defined by
K_{s}:=K-K
. By definition,
K_{s}
is an
o
-symmetric convex body.

Minkowski’s so called
second theorem on successive minima
establishes a beautiful
upper and lower bound on the volume of an
o
-symmetric convex body in terms of its successive minima (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
) which can easily be stated in a more general form for arbitrary convex bodies (see).

Theorem I

(Minkowski’s second theorem on successive minima)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
and
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(K_{s},\Lambda)}\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda)}\leq\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(K_{s},\Lambda)}.

Boths bounds are best possible. The lower bound follows by an easy inclusion argument, the upper bound, however, is considered as a deep and important result in Geometry of Numbers.

Hence, possible generalizations and extensions have been studied over the last hundered years. One very prominent one is due to Davenport from 1946. In order to present it we need

\displaystyle\delta^{l}(K):=\sup\left\{\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\lambda_{1}(K_{s},\Lambda)K)}{\det(\Lambda)}:\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{n}\right\},

the so-called
the lattice packing density
of
K
, i.e., the density of a densest lattice packing of
K
. Note that
\delta^{l}(K)\leq 1
. The first successive minimum
\lambda_{1}(K_{s},\Lambda)
is also called
the packing radius
for
K
and
\Lambda
, as it is the largest dilation factor
\rho>0
such that

\mathrm{int}(\rho K+{\bf x})\cap\mathrm{int}(\rho K+{\bf y})=\emptyset

for any two different lattice points
x
and
y
in
\Lambda
. Here,
\mathrm{int}(\cdot)
denotes the interior of a set. By definition of the packing density it is

(1.1)

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda)}\leq\delta^{l}(K)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(K_{s},\Lambda)}\right)^{n},

and Davenport asked for the following strengthening:

Problem II

(Davenport
[
2
]
)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
. Is it true that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda)}\leq\delta^{l}(K)\cdot\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(K_{s},\Lambda)}~?

Obviously, this would be a significant sharpening of Minkowski’s upper bound in Theorem
I
.
So far this question has only been affirmatively answered for
n=2
and for ellipsoids by Minkowski (see
[
9
]
and
[
3
]
), the case
n=3
has been settled by Woods
[
10
]
. For more information, we refer to
[
3
]
.

Another direction of extensions is to evaluate the successive minima directly with respect to the convex body, instead with respect to the difference body which a priori only yields optimal results for
o
-symmetric convex bodies. In
[
5
]
, Henk et al. studied centered convex bodies in this respect and proved among others an optimal lower bound.

Theorem III

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{c}^{n}
and
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{n+1}{n!}\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(K,\Lambda)}\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda)}

with equality if and only if there exits a basis
{\bf b}_{1},...,{\bf b}_{n}
of
\Lambda
and positive numbers
\nu_{1},...,\nu_{n}>0
such that

\displaystyle K=\mathrm{conv}\{\nu_{1}{\bf b}_{1},...,\nu_{n}{\bf b}_{n},-(\nu_{1}{\bf b}_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{n}{\bf b}_{n})\}.

Here,
\mathrm{conv}(\cdot)
denotes the convex hull of a set. A corresponding upper bound is an open problem and it is related to the so-called Ehrhart conjecture; see
[
4
]
for information on it.

In 2021, Henk et al.
[
6
]
introduced packing minima as a counterpart to the covering minima of Kannan and Lovász
[
7
]
and in order to interpolate between the successive minima of
K
and the ones of the polar body. We slightly generalize their definition to the class of all convex bodies containing the origin in their interior.
For
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{(o)}
,
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
and for
1\leq i\leq n
, we define the
i
-th packing minimum

\rho_{i}(K,\Lambda)
as

\rho_{i}(K,\Lambda)=\inf\{\rho>0:(\rho K+L)\cap\Lambda\neq L\cap\Lambda,\text{ for all }L\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{R}^{n},n-i)\}.

Here
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{R}^{n},k)
denotes the Grassmannian of all
k
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Our first result is an inequality in the spirit of Theorem
III
for these packing minima.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{c}^{n}
and
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
. Then

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{n+1}{n!}\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}(K,\Lambda)}\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda)},

and the inequality is best possible.

As
\rho_{i}(K,\Lambda)\leq\lambda_{n-i+1}(K,\Lambda)
(see Proposition
2.2
) this inequality improves on Theorem
III
. Hence, if we have equality in Theorem
III
then also in the Theorem
1.1
. We remark that for arbitrary convex bodies and with respect to the packing minima
\rho_{i}(K_{s},\Lambda)
of the difference body it was shown in
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}(K_{s},\Lambda)}\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda)}.

Here we complete this lower bound with a "Davenport type" upper bound.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
and
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
. Then,

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda)}\leq\delta^{l}(K)\cdot\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}(K_{s},\Lambda)}.

Apparently, this inequality is weaker than the conjectured one in Problem
II
(see Proposition
2.2
) but it taks into account the shape of the body via its lattice density and, in particular, it improves on the upper bound
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
.

In general, packing minima are hard to compute. For a convex body
K
(up to an invertible diagonal transformation) between the unit cross-polytope and the unit cube, we always have
\rho_{i}(K_{s},\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\lambda_{n-i+1}(K_{s},\mathbb{Z}^{n})
(see Proposition
4.1
), which implies that this kind of convex body and the integer lattice align with Problem
II
, i.e., Davenport’s Problem. However for the centered simplex

\displaystyle T_{n}:=-\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}{\bf e}_{i}+(n+1)\mathrm{conv}\{{\bf 0},{\bf e}_{1},...,{\bf e}_{n}\},

where
{\bf e}_{i}
denotes the
i
-th unit vector, it is a challenging and interesting problem to determine its packing minima.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
i=1,\dots,n
it holds

{\rho}_{i}(T_{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\frac{1}{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{i+1}\right\rfloor+1}.

Observe, that apparently we have
\lambda_{i}(T_{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n})=1
,
i=1,\dots,n
.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we will collect some basic properties of the packing minima, in particular, pointing out their interpolating property between the successive minima and those of the polar body. The proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are given in Section
3
, and the somehow technical proof of Theorem
1.3
is presented in Section
4
.

2.
Basic properties of packing minima

The packing minima of a convex body which were introduced and studied in
[
6
]

are in our notation the functionals
\rho_{i}(K_{s},\Lambda)
. As the symmetry of the body
K_{s}
is not essential
for many of their basic properties we will point out in the proofs only the necessary changes to the proofs presented in
[
6
]
.

But first we introduce some more notation. For
L\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{R}^{n},k)
we denote by
L^{\perp}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{R}^{n},n-k)
its orthogonal complement, and for
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the orthogonal projection of
S
onto
L
is denoted by
S|L
. For a lattice
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
let

\mathcal{G}(\Lambda,k):=\{L\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{R}^{n},k):\dim(L\cap\Lambda)=k\}

the set of all
k
-dimensional linear
lattice planes
of
\Lambda
.
These are exactly these planes such
\Lambda|L^{\perp}
is again a lattice,
i.e., a discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

With these notation we get as in
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]

the following more convenient description of the packing minima.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{(o)}
and
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
it holds

\rho_{i}(K,\Lambda)=\max\{\lambda_{1}(K|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp}):L\in\mathcal{G}(\Lambda,n-i)\}.

Proof.

As in the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
it follows by the characterization of the closed additive subgroups of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that it suffices to consider lattice planes and that

\rho_{i}(K,\Lambda)=\sup\{\lambda_{1}(K|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp}):L\in\mathcal{G}(\Lambda,n-i)\}.

Let
B^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional unit ball, and let
r\,B^{n}\subseteq K
for some
r>0
. Then for any
L\in\mathcal{G}(\Lambda,n-i)
we have along with Minkowski’s upper bound in Theorem
I

\lambda_{1}(K|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp})\leq\lambda_{1}(r\,B^{n}|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp})\leq 2\cdot\left(\frac{\det(\Lambda|L^{\perp})}{\mathrm{vol}_{i}(r\,B^{n}|L^{\perp})}\right)^{\frac{1}{i}},

where
\mathrm{vol}_{i}(\cdot)
denotes the
i
-dimensional volume, i.e., the Lebesgue measure with respect to
L^{\perp}
. As in the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
it follows from this inequality that only finitely many lattice planes
have to be considered in order to find
\sup\{\lambda_{1}(K|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp}):L\in\mathcal{G}(\Lambda,n-i)\}
.
∎

Next for a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
let
S^{\star}:=\{{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\leq 1\text{ for all }{\bf x}\in S\}
be its
polar set
, and for
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
let
\Lambda^{\star}
be its
polar lattice
, i.e.,

\Lambda^{\star}:=\{{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }{\bf x}\in\Lambda\}.

We remark that (see)

(2.1)

\mathcal{G}(\Lambda^{\star},k)=\{L^{\perp}:L\in\mathcal{G}(\Lambda,n-k)\}.

As mentioned in the introduction, the packing minima were introduced in order to interpolate between the successive minima and the ones of the polar body (with respect to the polar lattice). In our setting it reads as follows.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{(o)}
,
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
and let
\gamma\in(0,1]
such that
-\gamma K\subseteq K
. For
1\leq i\leq n
it holds

\frac{\gamma}{\lambda_{i}(K^{\star},\Lambda^{\star})}\leq\rho_{i}(K,\Lambda)\leq\lambda_{n-i+1}(K,\Lambda).

Moreover, we have
\rho_{n}(K,\Lambda)=\lambda_{1}(K,\Lambda)
and
\rho_{1}(K,\Lambda)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(K^{\star},\Lambda^{\star})}
.

Before giving the proof we remark that for an
o
-symmetric convex body
K
we may choose
\gamma=1

and hence, replacing
K
by
K-K
in the above statement gives
[
6
, Proposition 1.1]
.

For the lower bound, assume that
n\geq 2
, and we would say that
\gamma=\frac{1}{n}
is a "nearly best" parameter. Note the centered simplex
T_{n}=-\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}{\bf e}_{i}+(n+1)\mathrm{conv}\{{\bf 0},{\bf e}_{1},...,{\bf e}_{n}\}
, mentioned in Section
1
, and apparently,
-\frac{1}{n}T_{n}\subseteq T_{n}
. Theorem
1.3
yields that

\rho_{i}(T_{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n})\geq\frac{2}{n+2}\geq\frac{1}{n}

fro
i=1,2,...,n
.

Turn to the simplex

T_{n}^{\star}=\mathrm{conv}\left\{-{\bf e}_{1},-{\bf e}_{2},...,-{\bf e}_{n},\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf e}_{i}\right\}.

By the remark after Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
III
and Proposition
2.2
we have for
1\leq i\leq n

\rho_{i}(T_{n}^{\star},\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\lambda_{n-i+1}(T_{n}^{\star},\mathbb{Z}^{n})=1,

which also implies a similar lower bound for
\rho_{i}(T_{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n})
by Proposition
2.2
.
Then we can see that
\gamma=\frac{1}{n}
is definitely a "nearly best" parameter and
T_{n}
is a "nearly best" candidate associated with this parameter.

Proof.

The upper bound can be proven in the same way as in
[
6
, Proposition 1.1]
, and by definition of
\rho_{n}(K,\Lambda)
it equals to
\lambda_{1}(K,\Lambda)
.

For the lower bound, we fix an
L\in\mathcal{G}(\Lambda^{\star},i)
such that

\lambda_{i}(K^{\star}\cap L,\Lambda^{\star}\cap L)=\lambda_{i}(K^{\star},\Lambda^{\star})
. By Proposition
2.1
and (
2.1
) it suffices to show

\gamma\leq\lambda_{i}(K^{\star}\cap L,\Lambda^{\star}\cap L)\lambda_{1}(K|L,\Lambda|L).

As
K^{\star}\cap L^{\perp}=(K|L)^{\star}
and
\Lambda^{\star}\cap L^{\perp}=(\Lambda|L)^{\star}
this can be rewritten as

\gamma\leq\lambda_{i}((K|L)^{\star},(\Lambda|L)^{\star})\lambda_{1}(K|L,\Lambda|L).

In other words, given an
i
-dimensional convex body
M=K|L
with
-\gamma M\subseteq M
and an
i
-dimensional lattice
\Gamma=\Lambda|L
we want to show

(2.2)

\gamma\leq\lambda_{i}(M^{\star},\Gamma^{\star})\lambda_{1}(M,\Gamma).

This follows, however, as in the symmetric setting (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 23.2]
): Let
{\bf v}_{1}\in\Gamma\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}
, and
{\bf w}_{1},\dots,{\bf w}_{i}\in\Gamma^{\star}
be linearly independent such that
(1/\lambda_{1}(M,\Gamma))\,{\bf v}_{1}\in M
and
(1/\lambda_{i}(M^{\star},\Gamma^{\star}))\,{\bf w}_{i}\in M^{\star}
. Then we also have
(1/\lambda_{1}(M,\Gamma))\,\pm\gamma{\bf v}_{1}\in M
and by the definition of the polar body we conclude for
1\leq i\leq n

\gamma\langle\pm{\bf v}_{1},{\bf w}_{i}\rangle\leq\lambda_{i}(M^{\star},\Gamma^{\star})\lambda_{1}(M,\Gamma).

As
{\bf w}_{1},\dots,{\bf w}_{i}\in L
are linearly independent and
{\bf v}_{1}\neq 0
one of the inner products must be non-zero. By adjusting the sign of
{\bf v}_{1}
we may assume that it is positive, and by definition of the polar lattice we get (
2.2
).

Finally we consider the case
i=1
. In view of (
2.1
) we have

\rho_{1}(K,\Lambda)=\max\{\lambda_{1}(K|{\bf u}^{\star},\Lambda|{\bf u}^{\star}):{\bf u}^{\star}\in\Lambda^{\star}\setminus\{\bf 0\}\},

where we write
\cdot|{\bf u}^{\star}
in order to denote the orthogonal projection onto the linear hull of the vector
{\bf u}^{\star}
. If
{\bf u}^{\star}\in\Lambda^{\star}\setminus\{\bf 0\}
is a primitive vector, i.e.,
{\bf u}^{\star}
is the only non-zero lattice vector in
\Lambda^{\star}\cap{\rm conv}\{{\bf 0},{\bf u}^{\star}\}
then
\det(\Lambda|{\bf u}^{*})=1/\|{\bf u}^{\star}\|
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm. Hence,

\lambda_{1}(K|{\bf u}^{\star})=\min\left\{\frac{1}{\mathrm{h}_{K}({\bf u}^{\star})},\frac{1}{\mathrm{h}_{K}(-{\bf u}^{\star})}\right\},

where
\mathrm{h}_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\mathrm{h}_{K}({\bf u})=\max\{\langle{\bf x},{\bf u}\rangle:{\bf x}\in K\}
is the support function of
K
. Denoting by
\mathrm{r}_{K^{\star}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
{\bf u}\mapsto\max\{\mu>0:\mu\,{\bf u}\in K^{\star}\}
the radial function of
K^{\star}
we have by polarity that
\mathrm{h}_{K}({\bf u})=1/\mathrm{r}_{K^{\star}}({\bf u})
. As
\pm{\bf u}^{\star}\in(1/\mathrm{r}_{K^{\star}}(\pm{\bf u}^{\star}))K^{\star}
we get

\lambda_{1}(K|{\bf u}^{\star})=\min\left\{\mathrm{r}_{K^{\star}}({\bf u}^{\star}),\mathrm{r}_{K^{\star}}(-{\bf u}^{\star})\right\}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(K^{\star},\Lambda^{\star})}.

Hence,
\rho_{1}(K,\Lambda)\leq 1/\lambda_{1}(K^{\star},\Lambda^{\star})
.
∎

3.
Volume inequalities for packing minima

The main purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
. To this end we will need the following result from
[
5
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{c}^{n}
and let
{\bf u}_{1}
,
\cdots
,
{\bf u}_{n}\in K
be linearly independent. Then

\mathrm{vol}(K)\geq\frac{n+1}{n!}|\det({\bf u}_{1},\cdots,{\bf u}_{n})|.

Equality is attained if and only if
K=\mathrm{conv}\{{\bf u}_{1},\cdots,{\bf u}_{n},-({\bf u}_{1}+\cdots+{\bf u}_{n})\}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
essentially follows from the following statement.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K\in{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{(o)}
and
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
. There exist linearly independent vectors
{\bf v}_{1},\dots,{\bf v}_{n}\in K
and
{\bf w}_{1},\dots,{\bf w}_{n}\in\Lambda
such that for
i=1,\dots,n

\rho_{n-i+1}(K,\Lambda)\left({\bf v}_{i}|\{{\bf w}_{1},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}\}^{\perp}\right)={\bf w}_{i}|\{{\bf w}_{1},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}\}^{\perp}.

Proof.

We use induction on
n
, and for abbreviation we write
\rho_{i}=\rho_{i}(K,\Lambda)
. For
n=1
we only have
\rho_{1}=\lambda_{1}(K,\Lambda)
and so there exists a
{\bf v}_{1}\in K
such that

\rho_{1}{\bf v}_{1}={\bf w}_{1}\in\Lambda
. Next we assume
n>1
. In view of Proposition
2.2
we have
\rho_{n}=\lambda_{1}(K,\Lambda)
and so let
{\bf v}_{1}\in K
such that

{\bf w}_{1}=\rho_{n}{\bf v}_{1}\in\Lambda
. Next consider the
(n-1)
-dimensional convex body
K^{\prime}=K|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp}
, the
(n-1)
-dimensional lattice
\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp}
and for
1\leq i\leq n-1
let
\rho_{i}^{\prime}=\rho_{i}(K^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
. According to our inductive process we can find linearly independent vectors
{\bf v}_{2}^{\prime},\dots,{\bf v}_{n}^{\prime}\in K
and
{\bf w}^{\prime}_{2},\dots,{\bf w}^{\prime}_{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
such that for
i=2,\dots,n

\rho_{n-i+1}^{\prime}\left({\bf v}_{i}^{\prime}|\{{\bf w}_{2},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}^{\prime}\}^{\perp}\right)={\bf w}_{i}^{\prime}|\{{\bf w}_{2}^{\prime},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}^{\prime}\}^{\perp}.

Observe that for
1\leq i\leq n-1
we have

\rho_{i}^{\prime}\leq\rho_{i},

and hence with
\overline{\bf v}_{i}=(\rho_{i}^{\prime}/\rho_{i}){\bf v}_{i}^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}
,
2\leq i\leq n
, we get

\rho_{n-i+1}\left(\overline{\bf v}_{i}|\{{\bf w}_{2}^{\prime},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}^{\prime}\}^{\perp}\right)={\bf w}_{i}^{\prime}|\{{\bf w}_{2}^{\prime},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}^{\prime}\}^{\perp}.

Finally, let
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
{\bf v}_{i}=\overline{\bf v}_{i}+\alpha_{i}{\bf w}_{1}\in K
and
{\bf w}_{i}={\bf w}_{i}^{\prime}+\beta_{i}{\bf w}_{1}\in\Lambda
for
2\leq i\leq n
. Then for
2\leq i\leq n

\begin{split}\rho_{n-i+1}\left({\bf v}_{i}|\{{\bf w}_{1},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}\}^{\perp}\right)&=\rho_{n-i+1}\left({\bf v}_{i}|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp}|\{{\bf w}_{2}|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}\}^{\perp}\right)\\
&=\rho_{n-i+1}^{\prime}\left(\overline{\bf v}_{i}|\{{\bf w}^{\prime}_{2},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}^{\prime}\}^{\perp}\right)\\
&={\bf w}_{i}^{\prime}|\{{\bf w}_{2}^{\prime},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}^{\prime}\}^{\perp}\\
&={\bf w}_{i}|\{{\bf w}_{1},\dots,{\bf w}_{i-1}\}^{\perp},\end{split}

and so we have found vectors with the desired property.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is now an immediate consequence of the last two lemmas.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let the vectors
{\bf v}_{1},\dots,{\bf v}_{n}\in K
and
{\bf w}_{1},\dots,{\bf w}_{n}\in\Lambda
as in Lemma
3.2
, and let
\rho_{i}=\rho_{i}(K,\Lambda)
,
1\leq i\leq n
. Then Lemma
3.1
gives

\begin{split}\mathrm{vol}(K)&\geq\frac{n+1}{n!}|\det({\bf v}_{1},\cdots,{\bf v}_{n})|\\
&=\frac{n+1}{n!}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}|\det({\bf w}_{1},{\bf v}_{2}|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp},\dots,{\bf v}_{n}|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp})\\
&=\frac{n+1}{n!}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\frac{1}{\rho_{n-1}}|\det({\bf w}_{1},{\bf w}_{2}|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp},{\bf v}_{3}|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp}\dots,{\bf v}_{n}|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp})\\
&=\frac{n+1}{n!}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\frac{1}{\rho_{n-1}}|\det({\bf w}_{1},{\bf w}_{2},{\bf v}_{3}|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp},\dots,{\bf v}_{n}|\{{\bf w}_{1}\}^{\perp})\\
&=\frac{n+1}{n!}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\frac{1}{\rho_{n-1}}|\det({\bf w}_{1},{\bf w}_{2},{\bf v}_{3}|\{{\bf w}_{1},{\bf w}_{2}\}^{\perp}\dots,{\bf v}_{n}|\{{\bf w}_{1},{\bf w}_{2}\}^{\perp})\\
&=\cdots\\
&=\frac{n+1}{n!}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}}\,|\det({\bf w}_{1},\cdots,{\bf w}_{n})|\\
&\geq\frac{n+1}{n!}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}}\,\det(\Lambda).\end{split}

∎

Now we turn to the Davenport type upper bound, for which we need the following result from
[
6
]
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
6
, Lemma 4.4]
)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
, and let
S=\{j\in\{1,...,n-1\}:\rho_{j}(K_{s},\Lambda)>\rho_{n}(K_{s},\Lambda)\}\not=\emptyset
. Let
k^{*}=\max\{i:i\in S\}
be the maximal element in
S
. Then there exists an
L\in\mathcal{G}(\Lambda,n-k^{*})
such that for all
j\in S
,

\rho_{j}(K_{s},\Lambda)=\rho_{j}(K_{s}|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp}).

Theorem
1.2
is a particular case of the next theorem which is a strengthening of
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
and
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
. Further, let
0=j_{0}<j_{1}<\cdots<j_{m}<j_{m+1}=n
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be such that

\begin{split}\mathrm{(a)}&\quad\rho_{j_{1}}(K_{s},\Lambda)>\rho_{j_{2}}(K_{s},\Lambda)>\cdots>\rho_{j_{m}}(K_{s},\Lambda)>\rho_{j_{m+1}}(K_{s},\Lambda)=\rho_{n}(K_{s},\Lambda),\\
\mathrm{(b)}&\quad\rho_{j}(K_{s},\Lambda)\leq\rho_{j_{i}}(K_{s},\Lambda),\quad j\in\{j_{i-1}+1,...,j_{i}\},i\in\{1,...,m+1\}.\end{split}

Then,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda)}\leq\delta^{l}(K)\cdot\overset{m+1}{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}\frac{1}{\rho_{j_{i}}(K_{s},\Lambda)^{j_{i}-j_{i-1}}}.

Proof.

We will use induction on
m
. If
m=0
then
j_{1}=n
and with
\rho_{n}(K_{s},\Lambda)=\lambda_{1}(K_{s},\Lambda)
the inequality becomes (
1.1
). So let
m\geq 1
, and let

S=\{j_{1},j_{2},...,j_{m-1},j_{m}\}.

By Lemma
3.3
, there exists an
L\in\mathcal{G}(\Lambda,n-j_{m})
such that for
l\in S
,

\rho_{l}(K_{s},\Lambda)=\rho_{l}(K_{s}|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp}).

Then, by assumption we have

\mathrm{(a_{1})}\quad\rho_{j_{1}}(K_{s}|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp})>\rho_{j_{2}}(K_{s}|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp})>\cdots>\rho_{j_{m}}(K_{s}|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp}).

Moreover, in view of Proposition
2.1
and assumption (b) from the theorem, we also get for
j\in\{j_{i-1}+1,...,j_{i}\}
and
i\in\{1,...,m\}

\mathrm{(b_{1})}\quad\rho_{j}(K_{s}|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp})\leq\rho_{j}(K_{s},\Lambda)\leq\rho_{j_{i}}(K_{s},\Lambda)=\rho_{j_{i}}(K_{s}|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp}).

Hence, by our induction approach we conclude

(3.2)

\begin{split}\delta^{l}(K|L^{\perp})&\geq\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{j_{m}}(K|L^{\perp})}{\det(\Lambda|L^{\perp})}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{j_{i}}(K_{s}|L^{\perp},\Lambda|L^{\perp})^{j_{i}-j_{i-1}}\\
&=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{j_{m}}(K|L^{\perp})}{\det(\Lambda|L^{\perp})}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{j_{i}}(K_{s},\Lambda)^{j_{i}-j_{i-1}}\end{split}

Let
\widetilde{\Lambda}\subset L^{\perp}
be a densest packing lattice of
K|L^{\perp}
, i.e.,

\delta(K|L^{\perp})=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{j_{m}}(K|L^{\perp})}{\det(\widetilde{\Lambda})},

and let
\overline{\Lambda}\subset L
be
(n-j_{m})
-dimensional lattice

\overline{\Lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(K_{s},\Lambda)}\left(\Lambda\cap L\right).

Then
\widetilde{\Lambda}\oplus\overline{\Lambda}
is a packing lattice of
K
, or equivalently of
\frac{1}{2}K_{s}
, and with (
3.2
) we get

\begin{split}\delta^{l}(K)&\geq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\widetilde{\Lambda}\oplus\overline{\Lambda})}\\
&=\delta(K|L^{\perp})\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\mathrm{vol}_{j_{m}}(K|L^{\perp})}\frac{\lambda_{1}(K_{s},\Lambda)^{n-j_{m}}}{\det(\Lambda\cap L)}\\
&\geq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda|L^{\perp})\det(\Lambda\cap L)}\lambda_{1}(K_{s},\Lambda)^{n-j_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{j_{i}}(K_{s},\Lambda)^{j_{i}-j_{i-1}}\\
&=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda)}\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\rho_{j_{i}}(K_{s},\Lambda)^{j_{i}-j_{i-1}}.\end{split}

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

With the notation of Theorem
3.4
we immediately get by (
3.1
) and condition (b)

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(K)}{\det(\Lambda)}\leq\delta^{l}(K)\cdot\overset{m+1}{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}\frac{1}{\rho_{j_{i}}(K_{s},\Lambda)^{j_{i}-j_{i-1}}}\leq\delta^{l}(K)\cdot\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}\frac{1}{\rho_{{i}}(K_{s},\Lambda)}.

∎

4.
Packing minima of some convex bodies

In this section, we will study the packing minima of some particular convex bodies with respect to the integer lattice.

Firstly, let
C_{n}=\{{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x_{i}|\leq 1\}
be the unit cube and
C_{n}^{\star}=\rm{conv}\{\pm{\bf e}_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
be the unit cross-polytope, the polar body of
C_{n}
. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{A}_{n}=\{\rm{diag}({\it a}_{1},...,{\it a}_{n}):{\it a}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
be the group of all diagonal matrics with positive entries on the diagonal, and let

\mathcal{K}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\{K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}:\text{There exists an }A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\text{ with }C_{n}^{\star}\subseteq AK\subseteq C_{n}\}.

For instance, unconditional bodies or more general,
o
-symmteric locally anti-blocking bodies (see
[
1
,
8
]
) belong to this class.

Let now
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}
with
A=\rm{diag}({\it a}_{1},...,{\it a}_{n})\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
. By the definition above we have

\displaystyle\rm{conv}\left\{\pm\frac{1}{{\it a}_{i}}{\bf e}_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\right\}\subseteq K\subseteq\left\{{\bf x}:|x_{i}|\leq\frac{1}{{\it a}_{i}},1\leq i\leq n\right\}

and assuming
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}
we get for
1\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(K,\mathbb{Z}^{n})=a_{i}.

Apparently,
K^{\star}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}
as well, with associated diagonal matrix
A^{-1}
, and also
K_{s}=K-K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}
with matrix
2A
. Hence we get the following result by Proposition
2.2
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then for
1\leq i\leq n

\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(K_{s}^{\star},\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=\rho_{i}(K_{s},\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\lambda_{n-i+1}(K_{s},\mathbb{Z}^{n}).

Next we turn to the simplex

\displaystyle T_{n}=-\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}{\bf e}_{i}+(n+1)\mathrm{conv}\{{\bf 0},{\bf e}_{1},...,{\bf e}_{n}\}.

As Section
2
shows,
\rho_{i}(T_{n}^{\star},\mathbb{Z}^{n})=1
holds for all
i
, which can be deduced directly by the konwn results. For the completeness, we restate it here as a proposition, which means
T_{n}^{\star}
and the integer lattice also algin with Problem
II
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
i=1,\dots,n
it holds

{\rho}_{i}(T_{n}^{\star},\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\lambda_{n-i+1}(T_{n}^{\star},\mathbb{Z}^{n})=1.

Unfortunately, the packing minima of
T_{n}
itself are much more involved.

The proof of Theorem
1.3

is based on an interesting and elegant observation: along certain special directions, the projection of the entire simplex
T_{n}
coincides with one of its
i
-dimensional subfacets. Moreover, the distribution of the projected integer points happens to satisfy the extremal condition, which allows us to verify the equality by establishing the two opposing inequalities.

For convenience, let
{\bf v}_{0}=(-1,-1,...,-1)
,
{\bf v}_{1}=(n,-1,...,-1)
,
\cdots
,
{\bf v}_{n}=(-1,...,-1,n)
be the vertices of
T_{n}
, i.e.,
T_{n}=\mathrm{conv}\{{\bf v}_{0},{\bf v}_{1},\cdots,{\bf v}_{n}\}
, and let
V(T_{n})=\{{\bf v}_{0},{\bf v}_{1},\cdots,{\bf v}_{n}\}
. Then, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
i=1,2,...,n
, let
L\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},n-i)
and let
F
be an
i
-dimensional face of
T_{n}
such that
\dim(F|L^{\perp})=i
and
{\bf 0}\in F|L^{\perp}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(F|L^{\perp},\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\perp})\leq\frac{1}{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{i+1}\right\rfloor+1}.

Proof.

As introduced above,
V(T_{n})=\{{\bf v}_{0},{\bf v}_{1},\cdots,{\bf v}_{n}\}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
F=\mathrm{conv}\{{\bf v}_{0},{\bf v}_{1},\cdots,{\bf v}_{i}\}
. As
{\bf 0}\in F|L^{\perp}
there exists a

{\bf p}=\tau_{0}{\bf v}_{0}+\tau_{1}{\bf v}_{1}+\cdots+\tau_{i}{\bf v}_{i}\in F\cap L

such that

{\bf p}|L^{\perp}={\bf 0},

where
0\leq\tau_{j}\leq 1
for
j=0,1,...,i
and
\tau_{0}+\tau_{1}+\ldots+\tau_{i}=1
. Note that
(F\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})|L^{\perp}\subseteq(F|L^{\perp})\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\perp})
.

Let
n=(i+1)\cdot m+k
for
m=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{i+1}\right\rfloor
and
k\in\{0,1,...,i\}
, then

n+1=(m+1)+i\cdot m+k.

Now we consider the following cases for different values of
k
.

Case 1.

For
k=i
,
n+1=(i+1)\cdot(m+1)
, then there is a positive integer
i_{0}\in\{0,1,...,i\}
such that
\tau_{i_{0}}\geq\frac{m+1}{n+1}
, otherwise
\tau_{0}+\tau_{1}+\ldots+\tau_{i}<1
and
{\bf p}\notin F
, contradictorily. Then

{\bf p}+\frac{m+1}{n+1}({\bf v}_{j}-{\bf v}_{i_{0}})\in F

holds for any
j\in\{0,1,...,i\}\setminus\{i_{0}\}
. Since
\frac{1}{n+1}({\bf v}_{j}-{\bf v}_{i_{0}})|L^{\perp}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\perp}
and
{\bf p}|L^{\perp}={\bf 0}
, we have

\lambda_{1}(F|L^{\perp},\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\perp})\leq\frac{1}{m+1}.

Case 2.

For
0\leq k\leq i-1
,
n+1=(k+1)\cdot(m+1)+(i-k)\cdot m
.

If there exists some
i_{0}
such that
\frac{m+1}{n+1}\leq\tau_{i_{0}}\leq 1
, then for any
j\in\{0,1,...,i\}\setminus\{i_{0}\}
,

{\bf p}+\frac{m+1}{n+1}({\bf v}_{j}-{\bf v}_{i_{0}})\in F.

If for any
j\in\{0,1,...,i\}
,
0\leq\tau_{j}<\frac{m+1}{n+1}
, we take

{\bf c}=\frac{n_{0}{\bf v}_{0}+n_{1}{\bf v}_{1}+\ldots+n_{i}{\bf v}_{i}}{n+1}\in F\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}

such that
n_{j}=m
or
m+1
for
j=0,1,...,i
, then

\displaystyle{\bf p}+(m+1)({\bf c}-{\bf p})=\underset{j=0}{\overset{i}{\sum}}\left[\frac{n_{j}}{n+1}\cdot(m+1)-m\cdot\tau_{j}\right]\cdot{\bf v}_{j}.

By simple calculation, we have

\frac{n_{j}}{n+1}\cdot(m+1)-m\cdot\tau_{j}\geq 0

and

\underset{j=0}{\overset{i}{\sum}}\left[\frac{n_{j}}{n+1}\cdot(m+1)-m\cdot\tau_{j}\right]=1,

then

{\bf p}+(m+1)({\bf c}-{\bf p})\in F.

By
({\bf c}-{\bf p})|L^{\perp}={\bf c}|L^{\perp}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\perp}
, we have

\lambda_{1}(F|L^{\perp},\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\perp})\leq\frac{1}{m+1}.

Thus, this proof is finished.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
F
be an
i
-dimensional face of
T_{n}
, then there exists an
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},n-i)
such that

\displaystyle T_{n}|L^{\prime\perp}=F|L^{\prime\perp}

and

\displaystyle(T_{n}|L^{\prime\perp})\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\prime\perp})=(F\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})|L^{\prime\perp}.

Proof.

For convenience, we still take the
i
-dimensional face
F
of
T_{n}
with
V(F)=\{{\bf v}_{0},{\bf v}_{1},\cdots,{\bf v}_{i}\}
as an example.

Firstly, we divide
V(T_{n})
into
i+1
pairwise disjoint subsets
V_{0}=\{{\bf v}_{01},\cdots,{\bf v}_{0,n_{0}}\}
,
V_{1}=\{{\bf v}_{11},\cdots,{\bf v}_{1,n_{1}}\}
,
\cdots
,
V_{i}=\{{\bf v}_{i,1},\cdots,{\bf v}_{i,n_{i}}\}
, where
{\bf v}_{k1}={\bf v}_{k}
for
k=0,1,...,i
and
n_{0}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i}=n+1
.

Next, we assume that
L_{j}
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
parallel to conv
V_{j}
and conv
\underset{k=0,k\not=j}{\overset{i}{\cup}}V_{k}
. Let
{\bf b}_{kl}=\frac{{\bf v}_{k,l+1}-{\bf v}_{k1}}{n+1}
and we have
{\bf b}_{kl}\in L_{j}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, where
l=1,2,...,n_{k}-1
for
k=0,1,...,i
. For convenience, let

\Phi=\{{\bf b}_{01},\cdots,{\bf b}_{0n_{0}-1},{\bf b}_{11},\cdots,{\bf b}_{1n_{1}-1},\cdots,{\bf b}_{i,1},\cdots,{\bf b}_{i,n_{i}-1}\}.

Moreover, let
{\bf b}_{k}=\frac{{\bf v}_{k}-{\bf v}_{k+1}}{n+1}
for
k=0,1,...,i
and
{\bf v_{i+1}}={\bf v}_{0}
, then

\widetilde{\Phi}_{j}=\Phi\cup\{{\bf b}_{0},{\bf b}_{1},\cdots,{\bf b}_{j-2},{\bf b}_{j+1},\cdots,{\bf b}_{i}\}\subset L_{j}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

It is easy to see that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{j}\cup\{{\bf b}_{j}\}
is a group of linearly independent primary vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, implying that they are also a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, i.e.,

\mathbb{Z}^{n}=L_{j}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}+\{z_{j}\cdot{\bf b}_{j}:z_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}.

Thus,
L_{j}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},n-1)
.

Assume that
L^{\prime}=\underset{j=0}{\overset{i}{\cap}}L_{j}
, then
L^{\prime}
is parallel to each
\mathrm{conv}V_{j}
and
\Phi
is a basis of
L^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, which yields
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},n-i)
.

For
j_{0}=0,1,...,i
, we have known that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{j_{0}}\cup\{{\bf b}_{j_{0}}\}
is a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then it can be deduced that

\{{\bf b}_{k}|L^{\prime\perp}:k=0,1,...,j_{0}-2,j_{0},j_{0}+1,...,i\}

is a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\prime\perp}
. Since
L^{\prime}
is parallel to each
\mathrm{conv}V_{j}
and for each
k=0,1,...,i
,
V_{k}=\{{\bf v}_{k1},\cdots,{\bf v}_{k,n_{k}}\}
, we have
V_{k}|L^{\prime\perp}={\bf v}_{k}|L^{\prime\perp}
, which implies that

T_{n}|L^{\prime\perp}=F|L^{\prime\perp}=\mathrm{conv}\{{\bf v}_{0}|L^{\prime\perp},{\bf v}_{1}|L^{\prime\perp},...,{\bf v}_{i}|L^{\prime\perp}\}

and

(T_{n}|L^{\prime\perp})\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\prime\perp})=(F|L^{\prime\perp})\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\prime\perp}).

Note that
{\bf v}_{k}={\bf v}_{k1}
. Further, since
{\bf b}_{k}=\frac{{\bf v}_{k}-{\bf v}_{k+1}}{n+1}
, we can also obtain

(\overline{{\bf v}_{k}{\bf v}_{l}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})|L^{\prime\perp}=(\overline{{\bf v}_{k}{\bf v}_{l}}|L^{\prime\perp})\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\prime\perp}),

for
k,l=0,1,...,i
and
k\not=l
,where
\overline{{\bf v}_{k}{\bf v}_{l}}=\text{conv}\{{\bf v}_{k},{\bf v}_{l}\}
, then

(F\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})|L^{\prime\perp}=(F|L^{\prime\perp})\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\prime\perp}).

Therefore,
(T_{n}|L^{\prime\perp})\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\prime\perp})=(F\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})|L^{\prime\perp}
.

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
F
be an
i
-dimensional face of
T_{n}
, then there exists an
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},n-i)
such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(F|L^{\prime\perp},\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\perp})=\frac{1}{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{i+1}\right\rfloor+1}.

Proof.

Take the
(n-i)
-dimensional lattice plane
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},n-i)
constructed in Lemma
4.4
, and we show that it indeed satisfies the equivalent above.

Let
{\bf c}=\frac{n_{0}{\bf v}_{0}+n_{1}{\bf v}_{1}+\ldots+n_{i}{\bf v}_{i}}{n+1}\in F
, where
n_{j}
is the number of vertices in
V_{j}
for

, as set in the proof of Lemma
4.4
. Since
{\bf b}_{jl}=\frac{{\bf v}_{j,l+1}-{\bf v}_{j1}}{n+1}\in L^{\prime}
, where

, we have

{\bf v}_{j1}={\bf v}_{j,l+1}-(n+1){\bf b}_{jl},

{\bf v}_{j1}={\bf v}_{j}
for

. It follows by

\displaystyle\underset{j=0}{\overset{i}{\sum}}\underset{l=1}{\overset{n_{j}}{\sum}}{\bf v}_{j,l}={\bf 0}

and

\displaystyle{\bf c}

\displaystyle=\frac{\underset{j=0}{\overset{i}{\sum}}\underset{l=1}{\overset{n_{j}}{\sum}}{\bf v}_{j,l}-\underset{j=0}{\overset{i}{\sum}}\underset{l=1}{\overset{n_{j}-1}{\sum}}(n+1){\bf b}_{jl}}{n+1}

then

Similarly, assume
n+1=(k+1)\cdot(m+1)+(i-k)\cdot m
for
m=\lfloor\frac{n}{i+1}\rfloor
and
k\in\{0,1,...,i\}
. In particular, let
n_{0}=n_{1}=...=n_{k}=m+1
and
n_{k+1}=n_{k+2}=...=n_{i}=m
, then

{\bf c}=\frac{\overset{k}{\underset{l=0}{\sum}}(m+1){\bf v}_{l}+\overset{i}{\underset{l=k+1}{\sum}}m{\bf v}_{l}}{n+1}\in F\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Take an arbitrary pairwise
i_{0},i_{1}\in\{0,1,...,i\}
. If
\{i_{0},i_{1}\}\subset\{0,1,...,k\}
,

{\bf c}\pm\frac{m+1}{n+1}({\bf v}_{i_{0}}-{\bf v}_{i_{1}})\in\partial(F);

if

,

if

and

,

and

{\bf c}-\frac{m+1}{n+1}({\bf v}_{i_{0}}-{\bf v}_{i_{1}})\in\partial(F).

In addition, let
Q=\{{\bf c}^{\prime}:{\bf c}^{\prime}={\bf c}\pm\frac{1}{n+1}({\bf v}_{i_{0}}-{\bf v}_{i_{1}})\text{ for }i_{0},i_{1}\in\{0,1,...,i\},\text{and }i_{0}\not=i_{1}\}
. Apparently,

Thus, one can get

\lambda_{1}(F_{i}|L^{\prime\perp},\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\prime\perp})=\frac{1}{m+1}=\frac{1}{\lfloor\frac{n}{i+1}\rfloor+1}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{F^{\prime}:F^{\prime}\text{ is an }i\mathrm{-}\text{dimensional face of }T_{n}\}
. Given an arbitrary

, then

is an

then there always exists an
i

such that

and

.

Firstly, by

and Lemma

\lambda_{1}(T_{n}|L^{\perp},\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\perp})\leq\lambda_{1}(F|L^{\perp},\mathbb{Z}^{n}|L^{\perp})\leq\frac{1}{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{i+1}\right\rfloor+1}

holds for any

, where

.
Then, it follows by Lemma

\rho_{i}(T_{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{i+1}\right\rfloor+1}.

For a lower bound of

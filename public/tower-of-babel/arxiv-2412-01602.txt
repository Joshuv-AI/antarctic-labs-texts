Ehrhart Theory of Cosmological Polytopes
Justus Bruckamp, Lina Goltermann, Martina Juhnke, Erik Landin and Liam Solus

Abstract
. The cosmological polytope of a graph G G was recently introduced to give a geometric approach to the computation of wavefunctions for cosmological models with associated Feynman diagram G G . Basic results in the theory of positive geometries dictate that this wavefunction may be computed as a sum of rational functions associated to the facets in a triangulation of the cosmological polytope. The normalized volume of the polytope then provides a complexity estimate for these computations. In this paper, we examine the (Ehrhart) h ∗ h^{\ast} -polynomial of cosmological polytopes. We derive recursive formulas for computing the h ∗ h^{\ast} -polynomial of disjoint unions and 1 1 -sums of graphs. The degree of the h ∗ h^{\ast} -polynomial for any G G is computed and a characterization of palindromicity is given. Using these observations, a tight lower bound on the h ∗ h^{\ast} -polynomial for any G G is identified and explicit formulas for the h ∗ h^{\ast} -polynomials of multitrees and multicycles are derived. The results generalize the existing results on normalized volumes of cosmological polytopes. A tight upper bound and a combinatorial formula for the h ∗ h^{\ast} -polynomial of any cosmological polytope are conjectured.

1.
Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph with set of nodes
V
and multiset of edges
E
.
In particular,
G
is allowed to have multiple edges as well as loops.
Let
\mathbb{R}^{V\cup E}
denote the real-Euclidean space with standard unit vectors
\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in V\cup E
.
The
cosmological polytope
of
G
is

\mathcal{C}_{G}=\conv(\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{f},\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{f},-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{f}~:~f=ij\in E)\subset\mathbb{R}^{V\cup E}.

As the cosmological polytope does not depend on isolated vertices of
G
, we only consider graphs
G
without isolated vertices throughout the whole article. Then, the cosmological polytope
\mathcal{C}_{G}
is a
(|V|+|E|-1)
-dimensional lattice polytope
introduced by
[
1
]
in the study of wavefunctions of scalar models of the universe.
When
G
is interpreted as a Feynman diagram representing a cosmological model, the associated wavefunction may be computed in terms of the so-called
canonical form
associated to the polytope
\mathcal{C}_{G}

[
1
]
.
The canonical form
\Omega_{X}
of a positive geometry
X
is a (unique) top-dimensional differential form with logarithmic singularities only along the boundary components of
X

[
9
]
.
When the positive geometry is a
d
-dimensional convex polytope
P
, such as for
\mathcal{C}_{G}
, its canonical form may be expressed as

\Omega_{P}=\omega\frac{g}{f_{1}\cdots f_{r}},

where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
are the facet-defining linear forms of
P
,
\omega
is a regular differential
d
-form on
P
and
g
is a polynomial, vanishing on the intersections of facets, that do not lie in
P
. The polynomial
g
is also called the
adjoint
polynomial of the dual polytope of
P
(see e.g.,
[
15
]
).

A standard way to compute the canonical form
\Omega_{X}
of a positive geometry is to find a subdivision of
X
into simpler positive geometries
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{k}
, and apply the observation that

(1)

\Omega_{X}=\Omega_{Y_{1}}+\cdots+\Omega_{Y_{k}}.

In physics, these subdivisions often admit physical interpretations as (possibly new) physical theories for the computation of wavefunctions.

In the case of lattice polytopes, such as
\mathcal{C}_{G}
, the simplest subdivisions correspond to (unimodular) triangulations, for which the facet-defining linear forms may be recovered easily from the vertex representation of each full-dimensional simplex in the triangulation.
Recently,
[
7
]
found regular, unimodular triangulations of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
for any graph
G
in terms of square-free Gröbner bases of the toric ideal associated to
\mathcal{C}_{G}
.
They gave explicit combinatorial descriptions of the vertex representations of the full-dimensional simplices in such triangulations when
G
is a tree or cycle.
Such triangulations thereby yield new physical theories for the computation of the wavefunction associated to
G
via (
1
).

The number of full-dimensional simplices in such a triangulation correspond to the number of terms to be computed in the sum (
1
).
Since the triangulations found by
[
7
]
are unimodular, the normalized volume of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
provides complexity estimates on the computation of the desired wavefunction via the triangulation.
This motivates the study of the normalized volume of the cosmological polytope and its refinements via combinatorial generating polynomials, such as the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial.

In this paper, we study the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of the cosmological polytope
\mathcal{C}_{G}
.
We prove that the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of a cosmological polytope is multiplicative with respect to disconnected components (
Proposition

3.1
) as well as the
1
-sum graph operation (
Theorem

3.3
).
We further provide tight lower bounds on the coefficients of the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
for any
G
(
Theorem

3.7
), which naturally yield lower bounds on the complexity of the computation of the canonical form via any unimodular triangulation.
We also derive properties of the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
that are of general combinatorial interest, including its degree (
Theorem

3.16
) and a characterization of palindromicity (
Theorem

3.18
).
Finally, in
Section

4
, we explicitly compute the
h^{\ast}
-polynomials of cosmological polytopes of multitrees (
Theorem

4.14
) and multicycles (
Theorem

4.10
) using the triangulations of
[
7
]
.
By evaluating the resulting polynomials at
1
, we obtain normalized volume formulas generalizing the results of
[
8
]
for simple trees and
[
7
]
for simple cycles.
Some resulting conjectures are posed, including a proposed tight upper bound on the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of a cosmological polytope (
Conjecture

3.12
) and a general combinatorial formula for the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of the cosmological polytope for any graph
G
(
Conjecture

4.20
).

2.
The toric ideal of a cosmological polytope and triangulations

In this section, we describe the Gröbner basis for the toric ideal of a cosmological polytope from
[
7
]
and the corresponding triangulation.
In
Theorem

2.5
, we prove a result to be used later in the paper; namely, that a strict subset of this Gröbner basis is also a generating set for the ideal.

2.1.
Ehrhart theory and half-open decompositions

We start by briefly reviewing the necessary Ehrhart theory (see
[
2
]
and the original paper by
[
6
]
for more details).

Given a
d
-dimensional convex lattice polytope
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a positive integer
t
,
we let
tP=\{t\textbf{p}~:~\textbf{p}\in P\}
denote the
t
-th
dilate
of
P
.
The
Ehrhart series
of
P
is

\Ehr(P;z)=1+\sum_{t>0}|tP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|z^{t}.

The Ehrhart series of
P
may be expressed as a rational function

\Ehr(P;z)=\frac{h^{\ast}(P;z)}{(1-z)^{d+1}}=\frac{h^{\ast}_{d}(P)z^{d}+\ldots+h_{1}^{\ast}(P)z+h_{0}^{\ast}(P)}{(1-z)^{d+1}},

in which the numerator
h^{\ast}(P;z)
is a polynomial of degree at most
d
with only nonnegative integer coefficients
[
12
]
and constant term equal to
1
. The volume of
P
, denoted by
\vol(P)
, is equal to
h^{\ast}(P;1)/d!
and the value
\Vol(P)=d!\vol(P)
is called the
normalized volume
of
P
.
If
P
admits a
unimodular
triangulation, i.e., a triangulation whose maximal simplices have normalized volume equal to
1
, then its normalized volume equals the number of maximal simplices in the triangulation.
Moreover, such triangulations can be used to compute the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial via so-called
half-open decompositions
, which we will now briefly describe.

We say that a point
q\in\mathrm{aff}(P)
is in
general position relative to
P
, if
q
is not contained in the affine hull
\mathrm{aff}(F)
of any facet
F\subseteq P
.
Assuming
q
is in general position relative to
P
, a facet
F\subseteq P
is called
visible
from
q
, if
[p,q]\cap P=\{p\}
for all
p\in F
, where
[p,q]
denotes the line segment from
p
to
q
.
The facet
F\subseteq P
gives rise to the hyperplane
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{aff}(F)
in
\mathrm{aff}(P)
that defines two closed halfspaces, and
F
is visible from
q
if and only if
q
and
P
do not lie in the same halfspace.
By
\mathrm{vis}_{q}(P)
we denote the set of facets of
P
that are visible from
q
, i.e.,

\mathrm{vis}_{q}(P)=\{F\subseteq P\mid F\text{ is a facet of }P\text{ and visible from }q\}.

Given a polytope
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of
P
with maximal simplices
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
, and a point
q
in general position relative to
P
and to
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
, let
\mathbb{H}_{q}\Delta_{i}
be all points of
\Delta_{i}
that are not contained in a from
q
visible facet of
\Delta_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
. Such a set
\mathbb{H}_{q}\Delta_{i}
is also called a
half-open simplex
, and
\mathbb{H}_{q}\Delta_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{H}_{q}\Delta_{m}
yield a so-called
half-open decomposition
of
P
, since
P=\mathbb{H}_{q}\Delta_{1}\dot{\cup}\ldots\dot{\cup}\mathbb{H}_{q}\Delta_{m}
is the disjoint union of
\mathbb{H}_{q}\Delta_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{H}_{q}\Delta_{m}

[
3
, Lemma 5.3.4]
. This implies

\displaystyle|tP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|=|t\mathbb{H}_{q}\Delta_{1}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|+\ldots+|t\mathbb{H}_{q}\Delta_{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}|,

and with that one can show the following statement, which is Theorem 5.5.3 in
[
3
]
and relates half-open decompositions with
h^{\ast}
-polynomials:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a lattice polytope that admits a unimodular triangulation
\mathcal{T}
. Let
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
denote the maximal simplices of
\mathcal{T}
, and let
q\in P
be in general position relative to
P
, and to all
\Delta_{i}
,
i\in[m]
. Then

\displaystyle h^{\ast}_{i}(P)=|\{j\in[m]\colon|\mathrm{vis}_{q}(\Delta_{j})|=i\}|.

In other words,
h^{\ast}_{i}(P)
counts the number of maximal simplices in
\mathcal{T}
, that have exactly
i
visible facets from
q
.

2.2.
Toric ideals and Gröbner bases

In the following, we provide the necessary background concerning toric ideals, restricted to the case that the underlying polytope is a cosmological polytope.
We refer the reader to
[
14
]
for a general discussion of Gröbner bases and toric ideals.
For our purposes it is sufficient to understand how one extracts a triangulation from a Gröbner basis, which is described in the following.

We start with the definition of the toric ideal of a cosmological polytope.
Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, we let
R_{G}
be a polynomial ring over a field
K
whose variables correspond to the lattice points of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
. More precisely, we have

•

a variable
z_{k}
for every
k\in V\cup E
,

•

variables
y_{ijf}
and
y_{jif}
for every
f=ij\in E
, and

•

a variable
t_{f}
for every
f\in E
.

These three types of variables are collectively referred to as
z
-variables,
y
-variables
and
t
-variables
, respectively.
The
z
-variables correspond to the unit vectors
\mathbf{e}_{k}
which are contained in
\mathcal{C}_{G}
for every
k\in(V\cup E)
, whereas the variables
y_{ijf}
,
y_{jif}
and
t_{f}
, respectively, correspond to the vertices
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{f}
,
-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{f}
and
\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{f}
, respectively.
Note that there are no other lattice points in
\mathcal{C}_{G}
.
Similarly, let
K[\mathcal{C}_{G}]=K[\textbf{w}^{\textbf{p}}~:~\textbf{p}\in(\mathcal{C}_{G}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{V\cup E})]
be the
toric ring
of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
; i.e., the ring generated by all (Laurent) monomials
\textbf{w}^{\textbf{p}}=\prod_{v\in V}w_{v}^{p_{v}}\prod_{f\in E}w_{f}^{p_{f}}
, where
p
is a lattice point in
\mathcal{C}_{G}
.
The
toric ideal
of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
, denoted
I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
, is defined as the kernel of the
K
-algebra homomorphism

\begin{split}\varphi_{G}:\,R_{G}&\longrightarrow K[\mathcal{C}_{G}];\\
z_{k}&\longmapsto w_{k},\\
y_{ijf}&\longmapsto w_{i}w_{j}^{-1}w_{f},\\
y_{jif}&\longmapsto w_{i}^{-1}w_{j}w_{f},\\
t_{f}&\longmapsto w_{i}w_{j}w_{f}^{-1}.\end{split}

Since this homomorphism is surjective, we obtain a presentation of
K[\mathcal{C}_{G}]
, and we have
K[\mathcal{C}_{G}]\cong R_{G}/I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
.
The
Hilbert series
of
K[\mathcal{C}_{G}]
is defined as

\mathrm{Hilb}(K[\mathcal{C}_{G}];z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\dim_{K}(R_{G}/I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}})_{n}z^{n},

where the grading on
R_{G}/I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
is defined via

\deg(z_{k})=\deg(y_{ijf})=\deg(y_{jif})=\deg(t_{f})=1

and
(R_{G}/I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}})_{n}
denotes the homogeneous component of
R_{G}/I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
of degree
n
.
Since
\mathcal{C}_{G}
admits a unimodular triangulation due to
[
7
]
,
\mathcal{C}_{G}
has the integer decomposition property (see, e.g.
[
5
, Proposition 2.60]
) meaning that every lattice point
\textbf{p}\in t\mathcal{C}_{G}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{V\cup E}
can be written as

\textbf{p}=\textbf{p}_{1}+\cdots+\textbf{p}_{t}
, with
\textbf{p}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{G}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{V\cup E}
for
i\in[t]
.
With this, it follows from well-known results for general lattice polytopes that

(2)

\mathrm{Hilb}(K[\mathcal{C}_{G}];z)=\Ehr(\mathcal{C}_{G};z)=\frac{h^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{G};z)}{(1-z)^{\dim\mathcal{C}_{G}+1}}.

Hence, the Hilbert series of
K[\mathcal{C}_{G}]
can be used to compute the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
.

To access the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
from this algebraic perspective, it will be useful to understand generating sets for the toric ideal
I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
.
Such a set is provided by a Gröbner basis of
I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
, and we now describe the Gröbner bases found in
[
7
]
for any cosmological polytope
\mathcal{C}_{G}
.

Given
u,v\in V
and a path
P
in
G
between
u
and
v
of length at least
2
, a partition
P_{1}\cup P_{2}
of the edge set
E(P)
of

such that
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
contains the unique edge of
P
incident to
u
and
v
, respectively, gives rise to a so-called
zig-zag pair

(E_{1},E_{2})
of
G
, where
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
is the set of edges from
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
directed towards
v
and
u
, respectively.
Similarly, given a cycle
C
of
G
and a partition
C_{1}\cup C_{2}
of
E(C)
into two blocks (with one possibly empty), we let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be the set of edges in
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
oriented clockwise and counter-clockwise, respectively (where we have fixed an orientation beforehand).
The pair
(E_{1},E_{2})
is then called a
cyclic pair
. In the following, given an edge
ij\in E
, we write
i\to j
and
j\to i
for the corresponding directed edges, directed from
i
to
j
and
j
to
i
, respectively.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a zig-zag pair
(E_{1},E_{2})
its
zig-zag binomial
is

b_{E_{1},E_{2}}=\displaystyle z_{v}\prod_{f=i\to j\in E_{1}}y_{ijf}\prod_{f=i\to j\in E_{2}}z_{f}-z_{u}\prod_{f=i\to j\in E_{2}}y_{ijf}\prod_{f=i\to j\in E_{1}}z_{f}.

Given a cyclic pair
(E_{1},E_{2})
its
cyclic binomial
is

b_{E_{1},E_{2}}=\displaystyle\prod_{f=i\to j\in E_{1}}y_{ijf}\prod_{f=i\to j\in E_{2}}z_{f}-\prod_{f=i\to j\in E_{2}}y_{ijf}\prod_{f=i\to j\in E_{1}}z_{f}.

When either
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
is empty, the cyclic binomial
b_{E_{1},E_{2}}
is referred to as a
cycle binomial
.

It is not hard to see that all binomials from the previous definition belong to the toric ideal
I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
. Moreover, the same can be easily verified for the following set of binomials, referred to as
fundamental binomials
:

(3)

\begin{split}F_{G}=\{&\underline{y_{ijf}y_{jif}}-z_{f}^{2},~\underline{y_{ijf}t_{f}}-z_{i}^{2},~\underline{y_{jif}t_{f}}-z_{j}^{2},\\
&\underline{y_{ijf}z_{j}}-z_{i}z_{f},~\underline{y_{jif}z_{i}}-z_{j}z_{f},~\underline{t_{f}z_{f}}-z_{i}z_{j}~:~f=ij\in E\}.\end{split}

We note that cycle binomials have the property that one term consists only of
y
-variables and the other consists only of
z
-variables. With this in mind,
[
7
]
defined a special family of term orders on the ring
R_{G}
.
A
term order

\preceq
is a total order on the monomials of
R_{G}
that is compatible with multiplication and with the additional property that
1
is the smallest monomial. Given a polynomial
p
in
R_{G}
, its
leading term

\mathrm{lt}_{\preceq}(p)
with respect to
\preceq
is the monomial (appearing with non-zero coefficient) that is maximal with respect to
\preceq
.

Definition 2.3
.

A term order on
R_{G}
is

if the leading terms of the binomials in
F_{G}
are the underlined terms in (
3
), and the leading terms of all cycle binomials are the terms containing only

-variables.

The following theorem, derived in
[
7
, Theorem 2.9]
, gives a Gröbner basis for
I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
7
, Theorem 2.9]

The set

(4)

B_{G}=F_{G}\cup\{b_{E_{1},E_{2}}~:~(E_{1},E_{2})\text{ is a zig-zag pair or a cyclic pair of $G$}\}

is a Gröbner basis for the toric ideal
I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
of the cosmological polytope of
G
with respect to any good term order.

We remark that in
[
7
]
the authors assumed that
G
is a connected graph, but the statement is also true for disconnected graphs since the proof also works in that case.

Being a Gröbner basis, in particular implies that
B_{G}
is a generating set for
I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
.
The next result shows, that
I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
has a significantly smaller generating set which will be crucial for the proof of
Theorem

3.3
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
J_{G}
be the ideal generated by the fundamental binomials of
G
. Then,
b_{E_{1},E_{2}}\in J_{G}
for any zig-zag pair
(E_{1},E_{2})
of
G
. In particular,
I_{\mathcal{C}_{G}}
is generated by the following set

\widetilde{B}_{G}=F_{G}\cup\{b_{E_{1},E_{2}}~:~(E_{1},E_{2})\text{ is a cyclic pair of $G$}\}.

Proof 2.6
.

To show the claim, we prove a stronger statement: Given a path
P
of
G
from
u
to
v
of length at least
2
, a partition
(P_{1},P_{2})
of the edges of
P
, such that
P_{1}
contains the unique edge of
P
incident to
u
, we let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be the edges of
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
directed towards
v
and
u
, respectively. The pair
(E_{1},E_{2})
is called an

. Note that
(E_{1},E_{2})
is a zig-zag pair if and only if the edge incident to

. In particular, we allow that
P_{2}=E_{2}=\emptyset
. An almost zig-zag pair gives rise to a zig-zag binomial in exactly the same way as a zig-zag pair does. Moreover, we will say that a zig-zag pair has

. We show by induction on the length that

for every

(E_{1},E_{2})
.

For the base of induction, assume that
(E_{1},E_{2})
is an almost zig-zag pair of length
2
. Let
P=uvw
be a path of
G
of length
2
, such that
e=uv\in E
and
f=vw\in E
and let

be an almost zig-zag pair for
P
. In the following, we write
y_{uv}
and
y_{vu}
for the variables

and
y_{vue}
, respectively, and similar for other edges, since no confusion can arise due to possible multiple edges.

If

is not just an almost zig-zag pair, but a zig-zag pair in the original sense, then we have

and

. The corresponding zig-zag binomial is then

We can rewrite this binomial as follows

Since

are fundamental binomials of the edge

and

.

If

is an almost zig-zag pair but not a zig-zag pair, then we have

and

. The corresponding zig-zag binomial can then be written as

\displaystyle b_{E_{1},E_{2}}=

\displaystyle z_{w}y_{uv}y_{vw}-z_{u}z_{e}z_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y_{uv}(z_{w}y_{vw}-z_{v}z_{f})+z_{f}(z_{v}y_{uv}-z_{u}z_{e}).

Since

and

are again fundamental binomials for the edge

and

, respectively, if follows that

.

For the induction step, let

and consider a path

k
in
G

General results on

-polynomials of cosmological polytopes

In this section, we provide general results for

-polynomials of cosmological polytopes. In particular, we study the behavior of the

3.1.
Graph operations for computing

-polynomials

Let
G=(V,E)
and

be graphs on disjoint vertex sets.
Given vertices

and

-sum

of

and

with respect to

and

is the graph obtained by taking the union of

and

and identifying the vertices

and

is the graph on vertex set

and edge set

(E\cup F)\setminus(\{uv~:~uv\in E\}\cup\{uw~:~uw\in F\})\cup\{ux_{vw}~:~uv\in E\mbox{ or }uw\in F\}.

Note that if

and

have multiple edges containing

and

has multiple edges containing

.
In particular, the edge set of a graph may be a multiset.

When the identified vertices

Using the Hilbert series expression for

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

be a graph with connected components

Proof 3.2
.

It suffices to show the statement for

. Let
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
and
G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
be the connected components of

.
Since the only lattice points contained in

are its vertices and the unit vectors corresponding to the vertices and edges of

\mathcal{C}_{G}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{V\cup E}=(\mathcal{C}_{G_{1}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{V_{1}\cup E_{1}})\times\{0\}^{V_{2}\cup E_{2}}\cup\{0\}^{V_{1}\cup E_{1}}\times(\mathcal{C}_{G_{2}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{V_{2}\cup E_{2}}),

where we order the coordinates in such a way that the coordinates corresponding to

come first. Note that the above union is disjoint. It follows that

R_{G}\cong R_{{G_{1}}}\otimes_{K}R_{{G_{2}}}

, where we abuse notation and consider

\displaystyle K[\mathcal{C}_{G}]\cong R_{G}/I_{G}

\displaystyle\cong(R_{{G_{1}}}\otimes_{K}R_{{G_{2}}})/(I_{{G_{1}}}+I_{{G_{2}}})

\displaystyle\cong R_{{G_{1}}}/I_{{G_{1}}}\otimes_{K}R_{{G_{2}}}/I_{{G_{2}}}

\displaystyle\cong K[\mathcal{C}_{G_{1}}]\otimes_{K}K[\mathcal{C}_{G_{2}}]

\mathrm{Hilb}(K[\mathcal{C}_{G}];z)=\mathrm{Hilb}(K[\mathcal{C}_{G_{1}}];z)\cdot\mathrm{Hilb}(K[\mathcal{C}_{G_{2}}];z).

As

2

With the help of

Theorem 3.3

Let

.

Let

and

be the vertices of

and

In this section, we show that the

\displaystyle(1+3z)^{|V|-k}(1+z)^{|E|-|V|+k}\preccurlyeq h^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{G};z).

Now assume that

Proof 4.11

On the one hand, by

Instead of counting these facets directly, we first count a slightly larger set. Namely, all sets

consisting of exactly

\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{a}}

First note that variables in such a set

corresponding to different multi-edges of

can be chosen independently. For

denote the number of possibilities to choose

\displaystyle v_{i}(z)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{a_{i}}v_{i,k}z^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{a_{i}}\left(\binom{a_{i}}{k}+2a_{i}\binom{a_{i}-1}{k-1}\right)z^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{a_{i}}\binom{a_{i}}{k}z^{k}+2a_{i}\sum_{k=0}^{a_{i}-1}\binom{a_{i}-1}{k}z^{k+1}

4.3.
Other graphs

The results in
Section

4.1
and
Section

4.2
raise a natural question: Can the same approach be used to compute the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of cosmological polytopes of other classes of graphs.
A reasonable starting point is to extend the method to families of
2
-connected graphs beyond the multicycle.
Based on computational evidence, we propose the following conjecture for one class of
2
-connected graphs.

Conjecture 4.19
.

Let
P_{k}
,
P_{\ell}
, and
P_{m}
be paths of length
k,\ell,m
, respectively, and let
G
be the graph obtained by identifying the three starting nodes and the three ending nodes, then we have

\displaystyle h^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{G};z)=(1+3z)^{k+\ell+m}

\displaystyle-(2z)^{k+\ell}(1+3z)^{m}-(2z)^{k+m}(1+3z)^{\ell}-(2z)^{\ell+m}(1+3z)^{k}

\displaystyle-(2z)^{k+\ell+m}+3\cdot(2z)^{k+\ell+m}.

Note that the family of graphs considered in
Conjecture

4.19
contains the complete bipartite graph
K_{2,3}
.
The normalized volume of
\mathcal{C}_{K_{2,m}}
for
m\geq 1
was computed by Landin in
[
10
]
.
Indeed, evaluating the above polynomial at
1
for
G=K_{2,3}
recovers the normalized volume formula in
[
10
]
.

Thinking more generally,
Corollary

4.7
and
Corollary

4.18
support the following general formula for the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of a cosmological polytope.

Conjecture 4.20
.

Let
G
be a graph and
T
be a triangulation of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
given by a good term order on
R_{G}
. Then

h^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{G};z)=\sum_{S\in T}z^{\textrm{sq}(G_{S})+\textrm{db}(G_{S})},

where
\textrm{sq}(G_{S})
and
\textrm{db}(G_{S})
respectively denote the number of squiggly and double edges in
G_{S}
.

If
Conjecture

4.20
fails to hold for an arbitrary good term order, it may be the case that, for any graph
G
, there exists (at least) one good term order yielding the combinatorial formula for
h^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{G};z)
specified in
Conjecture

4.20
.

Existence of balanced dualizing dg-modules Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 14F08
Michael K. Brown, Andrew J. Soto Levins, Prashanth Sridhar

Abstract
. We describe cohomological conditions that are necessary and sufficient for the existence of balanced dualizing dg-modules, generalizing a theorem of Van den Bergh for balanced dualizing complexes over graded algebras. As a consequence, we show that a dg-algebra satisfying certain finiteness conditions admits a balanced dualizing dg-module if and only if its zeroth cohomology algebra admits a balanced dualizing complex. Additionally, we obtain a host of new examples of dg-algebras whose associated noncommutative spaces satisfy Serre duality.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbf{k}
be a field and
S=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}S_{i}
a Noetherian graded
\mathbf{k}
-algebra such that
S_{0}=\mathbf{k}
. When
S
is commutative, it determines a projective variety
X=\operatorname{Proj}(S)
. In general, no such projective variety exists, but the fundamental insight of noncommutative projective geometry, originating in work of Artin-Zhang
[
AZ94
]
, is that one may associate to
S
a triangulated category
\operatorname{D}_{\operatorname{qgr}}(S)
with many of the same homological features as the bounded derived category
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(X)
. More precisely, the category
\operatorname{D}_{\operatorname{qgr}}(S)
is defined to be the quotient of
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(S)
by complexes
C
such that
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}H(C)<\infty
; when
S
is commutative and generated in degree one,
\operatorname{D}_{\operatorname{qgr}}(S)
is equivalent to
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(X)
.

For example, Yekutieli-Zhang
[
YZ97
]
have proven that a generalization of Serre duality holds for
\operatorname{D}_{\operatorname{qgr}}(S)
when
S
admits a balanced dualizing complex, a notion due to Yekutieli
[
Yek92
]
. We recall that a dualizing complex for
S
is, roughly speaking, a complex
R
such that the functor
\text{{\bf R}\lx@text@underline{$\operatorname{Hom}$}}_{S}(-,R)
determines an equivalence
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(S)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(S)^{\operatorname{op}}
, and a
balanced
dualizing complex has an additional rigidifying property that forces it to be unique up to isomorphism. Van den Bergh subsequently characterized the algebras admitting balanced dualizing complexes
[
VdB97
, Theorem 6.3]
, thus determining exactly when
\operatorname{D}_{\operatorname{qgr}}(S)
enjoys Serre duality. We refer the reader to Yekutieli’s textbook
[
Yek20
]
for a comprehensive discussion of balanced dualizing complexes and the important role they play in noncommutative algebraic geometry.

Suppose now that
A
is a differential bigraded
\mathbf{k}
-algebra, i.e. a dg-algebra with both a cohomological and internal grading (see Section
2
for the precise definition, and see Notation
1.4
for our indexing conventions). Assume
A
satisfies the conditions in Setup
2.1
. One may define the category
\operatorname{D}_{\operatorname{qgr}}(A)
just as in the case of algebras above (see Section
3
for details), and the first- and third-named authors have recently begun a study of the geometric properties of the noncommutative space
\operatorname{D}_{\operatorname{qgr}}(A)

[
BS24
,
BS25
]
, i.e. a study of
derived
noncommutative geometry. In particular, they introduce in
[
BS24
]
the notion of a balanced dualizing dg-module for
A
, which is the natural differential graded generalization of a balanced dualizing complex. Moreover, they prove that
\operatorname{D}_{\operatorname{qgr}}(A)
satisfies Serre duality when

admits a balanced dualizing dg-module
[

, generalizing Yekutieli-Zhang’s noncommutative Serre duality theorem. This motivates the question: under what conditions does

Theorem 1.1

A differential bigraded
\mathbf{k}
-algebra

\mathbf{k}
-algebra

and its opposite algebra

satisfy Artin-Zhang’s condition

[

]
and have finite local cohomological dimension (Definition

).

See Theorem
2.23

when the equivalent conditions in Theorem

Corollary 1.2
.

A differential bigraded
\mathbf{k}
-algebra

as in Setup

admits a balanced dualizing dg-module if and only if
H^{0}(A)
admits a balanced dualizing complex.

Corollary

provides an ample source of examples of dg-algebras with balanced dualizing dg-modules: see Example

underscore how cleanly the theory of balanced dualizing complexes extends to the differential graded setting, building on the evidence already provided by

that the theory of noncommutative projective geometry generalizes quite robustly to dg-algebras.

As an application of Theorem

of perfect complexes in

(which we define in Section

Suppose

-module, in the sense of

Proposition 2.2

The category

2.2.
Local cohomology of differential bigraded modules

Let
\mathfrak{m}
denote the ideal

in

-module

, its

\mathfrak{m}
-torsion

\mathbf{R}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\coloneqq\underset{d\to\infty}{\operatorname{colim}}\text{ }\text{{\bf R}\lx@text@underline{$\operatorname{Hom}$}}_{A}(A/A_{\geq d},M),

[
BS24
, Section 3.1]
. In this subsection, we establish several technical results about derived

-torsion of dg-
A
-modules. The first is an extension of a result of Van den Bergh
[

Definition 2.6
.

Let
S
be a connected

-algebra. The

\operatorname{lcd}(S)\coloneqq\inf\{d\text{ : }H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0\text{ for all $i>d$ and all graded $S$-modules $M$}\}.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\operatorname{lcd}(H^{0}(A))<\infty
, then
\mathbf{R}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\colon\operatorname{D}(A)\to\operatorname{D}(A)
commutes with filtered colimits.

Proof.

Let
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a filtered system of objects in
\operatorname{D}(A)
. Our use of subscripts here collides with our notation for internally graded components, but since we do not refer to the internal grading on the
M_{i}
’s in this proof, this should not cause confusion. Applying appropriate cohomological shifts, it suffices to show that the canonical map

(2.8)

\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{colim}}\text{ }H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M_{i})\to H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{colim}}\text{ }M_{i})

is an isomorphism. Let us first assume that there exists
t\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(M_{i})^{j}=0
for all
j<t
and all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Fix
n\geq 0
, and let
F
be a minimal semifree resolution of
A/A_{\geq n}
as constructed in
[
BS25
, Proposition 2.16]
. It follows from this construction that
F^{j}=0
for
j>0
, and
F^{\geq j}
is a perfect dg-
A
-module for all
j\leq 0
. Fix

. For any dg-
A
-module

satisfying

for

, the natural map

H^{0}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(F,N)\to H^{0}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(F^{\geq s},N)
is an isomorphism. It follows that there is a commutative square

\textstyle{\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{colim}}\text{ }H^{0}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(F,M_{i})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{H^{0}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(F,\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{colim}}\text{ }M_{i})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{colim}}\text{ }H^{0}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(F^{\geq s},M_{i})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Since

is a compact object in

Given

, let
\sigma^{\geq t}
denote the smart truncation functor described in
[
BS25

. We have
\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{colim}}\text{ }(\sigma^{\geq t}M_{i})=\sigma^{\geq t}(\underset{i\in I}{\operatorname{colim}}\text{ }M_{i})

, and let

By

. Let

3.
Serre duality

Theorem
2.23
implies that Serre duality holds for the noncommutative spaces associated to a host of dg-algebras. Before we state the result (Corollary

[
BS24
, Section 4.1]
). Let
\operatorname{D}_{\operatorname{Qgr}}(A)

\operatorname{D}(A)/\operatorname{D}_{\operatorname{Tors}(A)}

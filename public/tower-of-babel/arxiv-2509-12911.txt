Uniqueness of purifications is equivalent to Haag duality
Lauritz van Luijk, Alexander Stottmeister, Henrik Wilming

Abstract
The uniqueness of purifications of quantum states on a system A A up to local unitary transformations on a purifying system B B is central to quantum information theory. We show that, if the two systems are modelled by commuting von Neumann algebras M A M_{A} and M B M_{B} on a Hilbert space ℋ {\mathcal{H}} , then uniqueness of purifications is equivalent to Haag duality M A = M B ′ M_{A}=M_{B}^{\prime} . In particular, the uniqueness of purifications can fail in systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom—even when M A M_{A} and M B M_{B} are commuting factors that jointly generate B ⁡ ( ℋ ) B({\mathcal{H}}) and hence allow for local tomography of all density matrices on ℋ {\mathcal{H}} .

When describing entanglement in such systems, an operator algebraic approach offers a natural, model-free perspective.
In this setting, there is, in general, no tensor product decomposition of the Hilbert space describing subsystems.
Instead, subsystems
A,B,\ldots
correspond to von Neumann algebras
M_{A},M_{B},\ldots
on the full system’s Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Importantly, the von Neumann algebras
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
commute if the corresponding subsystems are distinct.

If both subsystems are purely quantum, i.e., do not contain degrees of freedom that can be measured without being perturbed, then
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
are
factors
, see
[
13
]
or
[
14
, Sec. 4.4]
.
A factor is a von Neumann algebra
M\subset B({\mathcal{H}})
such that
M\cap M^{\prime}={\mathbb{C}}
, where
M^{\prime}
denotes the commutant:
M^{\prime}=\{x\in B({\mathcal{H}}):[x,y]=0\ \forall y\in M\}
.

A typical situation that one considers is that of a bipartition: The full system is divided into the two parts
A
and
B
so that no degrees of freedom are left out.
This immediately leads to our central question:

How does one model the fact that no degree of freedom is left out?

Below we will discuss three different ways to model this requirement: 1) Local tomography, 2) Haag duality, and 3) the
Uhlmann property
, encoding the uniqueness of purifications. In finite dimensions, more generally in a tensor product framework, all of these are equivalent.
In systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom, this equivalence generally fails, motivating to clarify the relation between different models.
For purely quantum systems, Haag duality implies local tomography, but the converse is false.
Our main result is that Haag duality and the Uhlmann property are equivalent.

We discuss below how these different ways of modeling bipartite systems play out in spatial bipartitions of quantum many-body systems
described by pure states
.
The upshot of this discussion is that local tomography is automatic, whereas Haag duality is not.
Instead, the validity of Haag duality depends on the physics of the systems and on the geometry of the bipartition.
Verifying it in concrete models is a notoriously difficult task

1.1
Local Tomography

A (seemingly) minimal requirement that
A
and
B
together constitute the full system is that every density operator
\rho
on
{\mathcal{H}}
should allow for
local tomography
by Alice and Bob: It can be uniquely identified by all correlation functions of the form

\displaystyle(a,b)\mapsto\tr(\rho ab),\qquad a\in M_{A},\ b\in M_{B}.

(1)

The local tomography condition is equivalent to
M_{A}\vee M_{B}=B({\mathcal{H}})
, where
M_{A}\vee M_{B}=(M_{A}\cup M_{B})^{\prime\prime}
is the von Neumann algebra generated by
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
. Since
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
commute, local tomography requires that
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
are factors:

\displaystyle M_{A}\cap M_{A}^{\prime}\subseteq M_{B}^{\prime}\cap M_{A}^{\prime}=(M_{B}\vee M_{A})^{\prime}=B({\mathcal{H}})^{\prime}={\mathbb{C}},

(2)

and similarly
M_{B}\cap M_{B}^{\prime}={\mathbb{C}}
.
Indeed, if
M_{A}
is not a factor, there is a non-trivial unitary operator
u
that commutes with both
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
. Hence, there are vectors
\Psi\neq u\Psi
which cannot be distinguished by local tomography.
To summarize:
Local tomography holds if and only if
M_{A}
and
M_{B}
are factors such that
M_{A}\vee M_{B}=B({\mathcal{H}})
(or, equivalently,
M_{A}^{\prime}\cap M_{B}^{\prime}={\mathbb{C}}
).

1.2
Haag duality

Instead of the local tomography condition, one could also consider a different way to model that no degrees of freedom are left out:
Demanding that Bob has access to all the operators that commute with all of Alice’s operators. Mathematically, this means that

\displaystyle M_{B}=M_{A}^{\prime}.

(3)

This condition is known as
Haag duality
, after a related property in algebraic quantum field theory
[
17
,
2

.

Haag duality (or approximate versions of it) is of vital importance to derive rigorous results on entanglement in systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom

Often considered a technical condition

, it has lacked a clear operational interpretation in terms of quantum information theory so far.

If
M_{A}
is a factor and Haag duality holds, then
M_{B}
is a factor, too.
In this case, Haag duality implies local tomography (and only in this case, since local tomography requires factors).
But the converse is false in general: There exist pairs of factors
M_{A},M_{B}
that allow for local tomography, but the inclusion
M_{A}\subset M_{B}^{\prime}
is strict. Notably, there is no non-trivial operator in
M_{B}^{\prime}
that commutes with
M_{A}
:
Since
M_{A}\vee M_{B}=B({\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle M_{A}^{\prime}\cap M_{B}^{\prime}={\mathbb{C}}.

(4)

We conclude that Haag duality cannot be given an operational interpretation in terms of local tomography. Of course, Haag duality is trivially true in the tensor product framework, where
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
,
M_{A}=B({\mathcal{H}}_{A})\otimes 1
and
M_{B}=1\otimes B({\mathcal{H}}_{B})
.

1.3
Uhlmann property

It is of fundamental importance to quantum information theory that every density matrix has a purification on a larger Hilbert space and that all such purifications are related by a (partial) isometry between the purifying Hilbert spaces.
Purifications are unique up to (partial) isometries.
Operationally, this means that if
\rho
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}
describes Alice’s subsystem and
\Psi_{\rho}
is a purification on
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
, then Bob, acting on
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
by local operations, can prepare any other purification of Alice’s state.

An important consequence of the uniqueness of purifications is
Uhlmann’s theorem

[
40
,
41
]
, which characterizes the Fidelity
F(\rho,\sigma)=\lVert\rho^{1/2}\sigma^{1/2}\rVert_{1}
of a pair of density operators
\rho,\sigma
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
as the maximal overlap of their purifications

F(\rho,\sigma)=\sup_{U}\ \lvert\langle\Psi_{\sigma},(1\otimes U)\Psi_{\rho}\rangle\rvert,

(5)

where
\Psi_{\rho}
and
\Psi_{\sigma}
are two arbitrary purifications in a common extension
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
and where the optimization is over all unitaries
U
on
{\mathcal{K}}
.
Uhlmann’s theorem is central to quantum information theory.
Recently, it has received renewed interest from the point of view of the quantum computational complexity
[
42
,
43
,
44
]
as well as from the point of view of its rigidity
[
45
]
.

As stated above, Uhlmann’s theorem only applies to systems with finitely many degrees of freedom.
The last condition we consider to formalize that no degree of freedom is left out is whether uniqueness of purifications (hence Uhlmann’s theorem) is fulfilled: Starting from any pure state
\Psi
on
{\mathcal{H}}
, is it true that any other
\Phi\in{\mathcal{H}}
with the same reduced state on
M_{A}
as
\Psi
may be reached by acting with local operations by Bob? We formalize this property as follows:

Definition 1

(Uhlmann property)
.

A pair of of commuting von Neumann algebras
M_{A},M_{B}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
has the
Uhlmann property
if for any two pure state vectors
\Phi,\Psi\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\langle\Psi,a\Psi\rangle=\langle\Phi,a\Phi\rangle
for all
a\in M_{A}
implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a unitary
u\in M_{B}
such that
|\langle\Psi,u\Phi\rangle|\geq 1-\varepsilon
.

We emphasize that the Uhlmann property only makes a statement about those states on
M_{A}
that admit a purification on the joint system, but does not require that all reduced states of one of the subsystems have purifications.

In contrast to local tomography and Haag duality, the Uhlmann property is
a priori
not symmetric relative to the subsystems
A
and
B
. It will be a consequence of our main result that it is, in fact, symmetric.

In the definition of the Uhlmann property, we already anticipate that in systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom, one can generally only approximate a given purification
\Phi
up to arbitrary accuracy using unitary operators from
M_{B}
acting on
\Psi
.
However, we will see below that if this is possible, then one can in fact find a local partial isometry, and hence a local quantum channel, that deterministically maps
\Psi
to
\Phi
.

The approximate nature of
Definition

1
has the important consequence that we can freely restrict the unitaries to any weakly-dense subgroup
G_{B}\subset{\mathcal{U}}(M_{B})
of the unitary group
{\mathcal{U}}(M_{B})
, without changing the validity of the Uhlmann property.
This is, for instance, useful when discussing quantum many-body systems, where we can take
G_{B}
to the group of finitely supported unitaries in the region
B
(see
Section

1.4
below).

1.4
Bipartitions of quantum many-body systems in the thermodynamic limit

An important motivation for studying entanglement in operator algebraic settings is given by many-body physics at zero temperature and in the thermodynamic limit. We briefly review their description, see, for example,
[
29
]
for details.
Consider an infinite lattice
\Gamma
of spins with local Hilbert space dimension
d
.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}
be the *-algebra of operators that have finite support on
\Gamma
.
For any subset
A\subset\Gamma
, let
{\mathcal{A}}_{A}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}
be the *-subalgebra of operators with finite support in
A
.
If
A_{1}\subset A_{2}
, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{A_{1}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{A_{2}}
and if
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\emptyset
, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{A_{1}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{A_{2}}
commute.
If
\Gamma=A\cup B
is a bipartition of the lattice, we have
{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}\cong{\mathcal{A}}_{A}\odot{\mathcal{A}}_{B}
, where
“
\odot
”
denotes the algebraic tensor product.
Thus, the analog of Haag duality always holds on the level of operators with finite support: If
x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}
commutes with
{\mathcal{A}}_{A}
, then
x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{B}
.

A ground state of a Hamiltonian is an algebraic state
\omega
on
{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}
.
The ground state sector
{\mathcal{H}}
arises from its GNS representation
\pi
, and for any bipartition
A\cup B=\Gamma
we obtain von Neumann algebras
M_{A}=\pi({\mathcal{A}}_{A})^{\prime\prime}
and
M_{B}=\pi({\mathcal{A}}_{B})^{\prime\prime}
.
If
\omega
is a pure state,
\pi
is irreducible and we automatically have that
M_{A}\vee M_{B}=B({\mathcal{H}})
.
Thus, for any bipartition
\Gamma=A\cup B
, the factors
(M_{A},M_{B})
satisfy the local tomography criterion.
However, in general, Haag duality
M_{A}=M_{B}^{\prime}
fails—although the relative analogue of it,
{\mathcal{A}}_{A}^{\prime}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}={\mathcal{A}}_{B}
, holds on the level of finitely supported operators, as discussed above.

We will see an explicit example of such a failure in
Section

2
. As mentioned in the introduction, proving Haag duality in specific situations is generally difficult and no general method is available; see
[
1
,
3
,
15
,
5
,
16
,
12
]
for examples.

The unitary group of
{\mathcal{A}}_{B}
is weakly dense in
{\mathcal{U}}(M_{B})
.
1
1

1

Consider an increasing sequence of finite subsystems
B_{k}\subset B_{k+1}
such that
B=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}B_{k}
. Since each
{\mathcal{A}}_{B_{k}}
is a finite-dimensional matrix algebra, it is a von Neumann algebra. We have
{\mathcal{A}}_{B}=\cup_{k}{\mathcal{A}}_{B_{k}}
, hence
M_{B}=(\cup_{k}{\mathcal{A}}_{B_{k}})^{\prime\prime}
and it follows from Kaplansky’s theorem
[
46
, Thm. II.4.8]
that
{\mathcal{U}}(M_{B})
is the strong*-closure of
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{A}}_{B})=\cup_{k}{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{A}}_{B_{k}}
, see for example
[
12
, Supplemental Material, Lemma 38]
. In particular, the group
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{A}}_{B})
of finitely localized unitaries are weakly dense in
{\mathcal{U}}(M_{B})
.

Hence, by the comments at the end of the previous section, Uhlmann’s property can be completely formulated in terms of unitary operators with finite support. Thus, if Haag duality holds,
B
can approximately map any purification to any other purification with unitary operators from
{\mathcal{A}}_{B}
.

2
Main result

We are now in the position to state our main result.

Theorem 2
.

A pair of commuting von Neumann algebras
M_{A},M_{B}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
has the Uhlmann property if and only if Haag duality holds.
In this case, if state vectors
\Psi,\Phi\in{\mathcal{H}}
have the same
A
-marginal, there exists a partial isometry
v\in M_{B}
with
v\Psi=\Phi
.

Before we come to the proof of the theorem, which can also be found in the PhD thesis of the first-named author
[
14
]
, let us briefly discuss its consequences.
Recall that every irreducible subfactor
M_{A}\subset M_{B}^{\prime}
gives rise to a bipartite system where local tomography holds, but Haag duality does not.
Since irreducible subfactors exist, see, e.g.,
[
32
]
, we conclude:
If
M_{A},M_{B}
are commuting
factors
on
{\mathcal{H}}
then

\displaystyle\text{local tomography}\quad\hbox{\hskip 10.13885pt\hskip-5.0pt\hbox{$\hbox{\hskip 5.0pt\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{$$}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-5.0pt\raisebox{1.5pt}{\hbox{$\Leftarrow$}}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip 5.0pt}$}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip-10.13885pt\raisebox{-8.5pt}{\hbox{$\not\Rightarrow$}}\hskip-10.13885pt\hskip 10.13885pt}\quad\text{Haag duality}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\text{Uhlmann property}.

(6)

Thus, in general, local tomography does not imply the uniqueness of purifications.

Let us illustrate how severe the failure of the Uhlmann property can be:
There exist irreducible subfactor inclusions
M_{A}\subset M_{B}^{\prime}
together with a state on
M_{B}
and two purifications
\Psi,\Phi
such that

\displaystyle\langle\Psi,u\Phi\rangle=0\qquad\text{for all}\ u\in M_{A}.

(7)

We discuss a physical example below.
Note that, as a corollary, we also see that Uhlmann’s theorem fails in the strongest possible form:
The fidelity between any state on
M_{B}
and itself is
1
(one can define fidelity for general von Neumann algebras
[
47
]
), but the maximum overlap one can achieve with local unitaries of Alice between the two purifications
\Psi
and
\Phi
is zero.

We now illustrate the points that we made above with a physical example observed by P. Naaijkens

Consider the (unique) translation-invariant ground state of Kitaev’s surface code

on an infinite square lattice

Consider the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
arising from the GNS representation of the local algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}
relative to the translation invariant ground state of the surface code.
To every region
\Lambda

M_{\Lambda}

generated by operators with finite support in

(cf.

First, consider the bipartition
A_{1}

Hence, by

Now consider instead the bipartition
A:=A_{1}\cup A_{2}

The state

Proof of
Theorem

2

Haag duality
\Rightarrow
Uhlmann property

Consider two states
\Psi
and
\Phi
that purify the same state
\omega
on
M_{A}
. The subspaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Psi}=\overline{M_{A}\Psi}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Phi}=\overline{M_{A}\Phi}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
both provide GNS representations of
\omega
.
By the uniqueness of the GNS representation up to unitary equivalence
[
46
, Thm. I.9.14]
, there exists a unitary
u:{\mathcal{H}}_{\Psi}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{\Phi}
that intertwines the two representations, i.e., fulfills

\displaystyle ua\Psi=au\Psi=a\Phi,\quad a\in M_{A}.

(10)

Extending
u
to a partial isometry
v:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
, we find that
v
must commute with all elements of
M_{A}
. Hence
v\in M_{A}^{\prime}=M_{B}
by Haag duality.
This shows the second claim.
Since the weak closure of the unitary group
{\mathcal{U}}(M_{B})
is the set of contractions in
M_{B}

[
46
, Ex. II.1]
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a unitary
u\in M_{B}
with
\lvert\langle\Psi,u\Phi\rangle\rvert\geq 1-\varepsilon
.
It follows that the Uhlmann property is fulfilled.

Uhlmann property
\Rightarrow
Haag duality

Let
{\mathcal{U}}(M)
denote the unitary group of a von Neumann algebra
M
.
By the argument in the previous section, the Uhlmann property implies
\overline{{\mathcal{U}}(M_{A})\Psi}=\overline{{\mathcal{U}}(M_{B}^{\prime})\Psi}
for all
\Psi\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
Since a von Neumann algebra is spanned by its unitary group, we thus have
[M_{A}\Psi]=[M_{B}^{\prime}\Psi]
for all
\Psi\in{\mathcal{H}}
, where
[V]
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the closed subspace generated by a subset
V\subset{\mathcal{H}}
.
Since a projection
p\in B({\mathcal{H}})
is in a von Neumann algebra
M\subset B({\mathcal{H}})
if and only if its range
p{\mathcal{H}}
is
M^{\prime}
-invariant, we have
[M_{A}\Psi]\in M_{B}
for all
\Psi\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
Now consider some projection
p\in M_{A}^{\prime}
and let
\{\Omega_{n}\}
be a basis of
p{\mathcal{H}}
.
Then
p=[\cup_{n}M_{A}\Omega_{n}]=\vee_{n}[M_{A}\Omega_{n}]
, where
\vee_{n}p_{n}
denotes the smallest projection
q\in B({\mathcal{H}})
such that
q\geq p_{n}
for all
n
. Since
[M_{A}\Omega_{n}]\in M_{B}
by our previous observation it follows that
p\in M_{B}

[
49
, Cor. 3.7]
.
Hence, every projection
p\in M_{A}^{\prime}
is also an element of
M_{B}
and therefore
M_{B}\subset M_{A}^{\prime}\subset M_{B}
.

4
Discussion and outlook

We have shown that the uniqueness of purifications in a bipartite system modeled by commuting von Neumann algebras
M_{A},M_{B}
is equivalent to Haag duality
M_{A}=M_{B}^{\prime}
. In the case of factors, the failure of both conditions is characterized by an irreducible subfactor inclusion
M_{A}\subset M_{B}^{\prime}
.
It is well known that there exists at most one normal conditional expectation
E:M_{B}^{\prime}\to M_{A}
in such a case. Associated with
E
is an
index
, which can be seen to characterize how much bigger
M_{B}^{\prime}
is compared to
M_{A}

[
31
,
38
,
33
,
34
]
.
2
2

2

If no normal conditional expectation exists, the index is defined to be
\infty
.

The index can also be understood as measuring how much more classical information can be encoded into
E
-invariant states when having access to
M_{B}^{\prime}
instead of
M_{A}

[
26
,
23
]
.
Thus, if
E
has operational meaning, then the index carries operational meaning. In upcoming work, we show that the existence of a conditional expectation that preserves a given marginal state can be given an operational meaning itself
[
50
]
.

An interesting open question is whether the uniqueness of purifications and Haag duality can also be related in a
quantitative
way instead of a qualitative way.
It is not difficult to see that situations as discussed in the example above, where purifications remain orthogonal under arbitrary operations by Alice, exist for arbitrarily large index.
3
3

3

All that is needed is a unitary orthonormal Pimsner-Popa basis
[
51
]
. Examples can, for example, be constructed from outer actions of finite groups on type
{\mathrm{II}}_{1}
factors, in which case the index measures the number of orthogonal purifications (as in the example of the surface code).

It is therefore not clear whether there is a general and meaningful quantitative connection that is measured by the index.

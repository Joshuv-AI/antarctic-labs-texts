1.
Introduction

Elliptic curves are a central research topic in number theory and arithmetic geometry, playing a vital role in various fields, including algebraic geometry. Many Diophantine equations can be connected to elliptic curves, such as those arising in Fermat’s Last Theorem and the congruence number problem. Moreover, when the Mordell-Weil theorem (
[
6
, Ch.VIII, Theorem 6.7]
) asserts that the group of rational points of an elliptic curve is a finitely generated abelian group. Thus, finding only a finite number of generators yields a complete characterization of all rational points on the elliptic curve.

There are various approaches to compute the rank and the torsion subgroup of an elliptic curve and one of them is to apply Galois representation. For an elliptic curve
\mathscr{E}
over a field
F
and a positive integer
N
which is not divisible by the characteristic of
F
, it is known that
\mathscr{E}\left[N\right]\cong\left({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{2}
and the field of definition of
N
-torsion points
F\left(\mathscr{E}\left[N\right]\right)
over
F
is Galois. Given a basis of
\mathcal{B}_{N}=\left\{P_{N},Q_{N}\right\}
of
\mathscr{E}\left[N\right]
, we have an injective group homomorphism
r_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}:\operatorname{Gal}\left(F\left(\mathscr{E}\left[N\right]\right)/F\right)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{N}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
defined by

r_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}\left(\sigma\right)\begin{pmatrix}P_{N}\\
Q_{N}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}P_{N}^{\sigma}\\
Q_{N}^{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}.

For positive divisors
m
and
n
of
N
such that
m\mid n
, we have that
\mathscr{E}\left(F\right)\left[N\right]\supseteq{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if

\operatorname{Im}\left(r_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}\right)\subseteq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{N}{\mathbb{Z}}\right):\begin{aligned} \left(a,b\right)&\equiv\left(1,0\right)\pmod{m}\\
\left(c,d\right)&\equiv\left(0,1\right)\pmod{n}\\
\end{aligned}\right\}.

(See
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
).

The mod-
N
representation
r_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}
is widely studied for the cases when
F={\mathbb{Q}}
. For example, Serre’s open image theorem (
[
5
]
) states that, for each non-CM elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, the mod-
\ell
Galois representations are surjective for almost all primes
\ell
. Moreover, it is conjectured that the upper bound of
\ell
such that mod-
\ell
representation is reducible is uniform, i.e., such an upper bound does not depend on non-CM elliptic curves.

Now, we are ready to state our main result which proves that

G_{e,\mathcal{B}_{N}}:=\rho_{e,\mathcal{B}_{N}}\left(\mathfrak{A}\left({\mathbb{K}}\left(s,t\right)\left(E_{s,t}\left[p^{e}N\right]\right)/{\mathbb{K}}\left(s,t\right)\right)\right).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field of characteristic

Remark 1.4
.

To emphasize the subtlety, consider the subgroup
G\subseteq\left({\mathbb{Z}}/5{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{2}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
generated by
\left(\overline{2},\begin{pmatrix}\overline{0}&\overline{1}\\
\overline{1}&\overline{0}\end{pmatrix}\right)
and
\left(\overline{1},\begin{pmatrix}\overline{1}&\overline{1}\\
\overline{0}&\overline{1}\end{pmatrix}\right)
. Both projections of

\left({\mathbb{Z}}/5{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}
and to
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{2}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
are surjective. However,

\left({\mathbb{Z}}/5{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{\times}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{2}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)

\left(\overline{1},\begin{pmatrix}\overline{0}&\overline{1}\\

and Theorem
1.2
does not guarantee that the combined map
\rho_{e,\mathcal{B}_{N}}
is surjective.

Theorem

enables the following computation of the automorphism group of the

Lemma 3.1
.

For a prime
\ell
and a positive integer

,

(1)

if
\ell\nmid N
, then
\mathfrak{S}_{{N\ell}}\left({N}\right)\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right),
and

(2)

if
\ell\mid N
, then
\mathfrak{S}_{{N\ell}}\left({N}\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{3}
.

Proof.

1
Suppose
\ell\nmid N
. We show that the mod-
\ell
reduction
\pi:\mathfrak{S}_{{N\ell}}\left({N}\right)\to\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)
is an isomorphism using the Chinese remainder theorem. By this theorem, the only
A\in\ker\pi
satisfying
A\equiv I_{2}\pmod{N}
is
A=I_{2}
, where
I_{2}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{N\ell}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
denotes the identity matrix. Hence,
\pi
is injective. Furthermore, for any
B\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\right)
, the Chinese remainder theorem shows the existence of
A\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{N\ell}{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
such that
A\equiv B\pmod{\ell}
and
A\equiv I_{2}\pmod{N}
. Hence,
\pi\left(A\right)=B
and
\pi
is surjective.

2
Suppose
\ell\mid N
. Consider the group isomorphism
\nu:\operatorname{M}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}\right)\to\operatorname{M}_{2}\left(N{\mathbb{Z}}/N\ell{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
of additive groups of
2\times 2
matrices, induced by the multiplication homomorphism
{\mathbb{Z}}\to N{\mathbb{Z}}
given by
x\mapsto Nx
. Since
\ell\mid N
, the relation
\left(I_{2}+\nu\left(A\right)\right)\left(I_{2}+\nu\left(B\right)\right)=I_{2}+\nu\left(A+B\right)
holds for all matrices
A,B\in\operatorname{M}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
, and we have
I_{2}+\nu\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
z&-x\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{S}_{{N\ell}}\left({N}\right)
for all
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}
. In other words, we have a group homomorphism
\gamma:\left({\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\mathbb{Z}}\right)^{3}\to\mathfrak{S}_{{N\ell}}\left({N}\right)
defined by

(4)

\gamma\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{pmatrix}=I_{2}+\nu\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
z&-x\end{pmatrix}.

It is clear that
\gamma
is injective. To show that
\gamma
is surjective, consider any
X\in\mathfrak{S}_{{N\ell}}\left({N}\right)
. Then,
X
is expressed as
I_{2}+\nu\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
for some

. Since
\ell\mid N
, the condition that
\det\left(X\right)=1
implies
d=-a
. Therefore,
\gamma
is surjective, so it is an isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

(1)

If
\ell\in\left\{2,3\right\}
, then there exists a unique proper normal subgroup

of

∎

Lemma 3.5

Lemma 3.9

Lemma 3.10

Lemma 3.11
.

Let

\displaystyle\operatorname{Disc}\left({g}\right)=\sum\limits_{I\in T}r_{I}a^{I},\text{ for some }r_{I}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\text{ where }a^{I}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i}^{d_{i}}.

\displaystyle r_{\left(0,\cdots,0,n-1\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left(\zeta_{2n}^{2i-1}-\zeta_{2n}^{2j-1}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{\frac{n\left(n-1\right)}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{j\neq i}\left(\zeta_{2n}^{2i-1}-\zeta_{2n}^{2j-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{\frac{n\left(n-1\right)}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\left.\frac{d}{dx}\left(x^{n}+1\right)\right|_{x=\zeta_{2n}^{2i-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{\frac{n\left(n-1\right)}{2}}n^{n},

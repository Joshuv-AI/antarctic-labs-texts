On the thermodynamics of the black holes of the
Cano-Ruipérez 4-dimensional string effective action

Abstract




IFT-UAM/CSIC-24-160

MPP-2024-221

November 15
th
, 2024

The Cano–Ruipérez 4-dimensional string effective action is the
simplest consistent truncation of the first-order in
\alpha^{\prime}
heterotic
string effective action compactified on T
6
. This theory contains, on
top of the metric, a dilaton and an axion that couple to the Gauss-Bonnet
term and to the Pontrjagin density which suggests a very strong relation
between the physical and geometrical properties of its
solutions. In this paper we study the thermodynamics of the string
black-hole solutions of this theory using Wald’s formalism. We construct
the on-shell closed generalized Komar, dilaton and axion 2-form charges,
and we used them to find the Smarr formula (that we test on the
Schwarzschild and Kerr solutions) and to prove no-(primary)-hair theorems
for the scalar. We find that
\alpha^{\prime}
plays the role of a thermodynamical
variable with an associated chemical potential. We also notice the
occurrence of non-standard gravitational charges in the Wald entropy and
scalar charges.

In Ref.

All this leads us to consider the on-shell-equivalent theory in which

\alpha^{\prime}

This on-shell-equivalent theory that we will simply call CR2 is, however, more

\displaystyle\left.+\mathbf{E}_{\rm\ell_{s}}\delta\ell_{s}+\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^{(1)}{}_{\rm C}\delta dC\right\}\,.

3
Global symmetries

The original CR action Eq. (
1.1
) is not invariant under constant
shifts of the dilaton, but the modified action CR2 Eq. (
2.1
) is
invariant under

\delta_{m}\phi=m\,,\hskip 14.22636pt\delta_{m}\chi=-m\chi\,,\hskip 14.22636pt\delta_{m}\ell_{s}=\frac{m}{2}\ell_{s}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt\delta_{m}C=-\frac{m}{2}C\,,

(3.1)

where
m
is an infinitesimal, real parameter.

Observe that the rescaling of
\ell_{s}
(hence, of
\alpha^{\prime}
) is a legitimate
transformation of this action because in it
\ell_{s}
is a field.

The Noether current associated to this global symmetry of the CR2 action is

J_{m}\equiv\star d\phi-e^{2\phi}\star d\chi-\tfrac{1}{2}\ell_{s}C\,,\hskip 28.45274ptdJ_{m}\doteq 0\,.

(3.2)

At first sight, when
\ell_{s}
is promoted to a field, the invariance up to
total derivatives of the original CR action under constant shifts of the axion
gets broken. However, it is not difficult to see that the CR2 action is
invariant under the infinitesimal transformations

\delta_{n}\chi=n\ell_{s}^{-2}\,,\hskip 28.45274pt\delta_{n}C=-2n\ell_{s}^{-3}e^{2\phi}\star d\chi\,,

(3.3)

up to a total derivative given by

\delta_{n}S_{\rm CR2}=n\int d\left[\tfrac{1}{4}\omega_{(3)}^{L}-2\ell_{s}^{-2}e^{2\phi}\star d\chi\right]\,.

(3.4)

The associated Noether current is given by

J_{n}=\ell_{s}^{-2}e^{2\phi}\star d\chi-\tfrac{1}{4}\omega_{(3)}^{L}\,,\hskip 28.45274ptdJ_{n}\doteq 0\,.

(3.5)

As shown in Ref.
[
25
]
, in static black-hole spacetimes,
the charges obtained by integrating these 3-forms over spacelike hypersurfaces
vanish identically. In order to characterize the scalar fields one can define,
instead, 2-form charges which are derived from the above 3-form currents by
exploiting the assumption of invariance under the diffeomorphisms generated by
a Killing vector
k
. We will do this in Section
5
, but
we have to discuss how the different fields transform under local Lorentz
transformations and diffeomorphisms first.

4
Local symmetries

4.1
Local Lorentz transformations

We start considering the local Lorentz transformations. Only the Vierbein and
the fields derived from it (spin connection and curvature) have non-trivial
local Lorentz transformations. They take the form

\delta_{\sigma}e^{a}=\sigma^{a}{}_{b}e^{b}\,,\qquad\delta_{\sigma}\omega_{ab}=\mathcal{D}\sigma_{ab}\,,\qquad\delta_{\sigma}R^{ab}=2\sigma^{[a|}{}_{c}R^{c|b]}\,.

(4.1)

The CR2 action is exactly invariant under this transformation. We obtain

0=\delta_{\sigma}S_{\rm CR2}=\int\left\{\mathbf{E}^{a}\wedge e^{b}\sigma_{ab}+d\mathbf{\Theta}(\varphi,\delta_{\sigma}\varphi)\right\}\,,

(4.2)

which implies the following (off-shell) Noether identity

\mathbf{E}^{[a}\wedge e^{b]}=0\,,

(4.3)

and the off-shell-closed Noether current
\mathbf{J}[\sigma]

\mathbf{J}[\sigma]\equiv\mathbf{\Theta}(\varphi,\delta_{\sigma}\varphi)=d{\mathbf{Q}}[\sigma]\,,

(4.4)

where

\mathbf{Q}[\sigma]\equiv-\star(e^{a}\wedge e^{b})\sigma_{ab}-\tfrac{1}{2}\ell_{s}^{2}\left(e^{-\phi}\tilde{R}^{ab}\sigma_{ab}+\chi R^{ab}\sigma_{ab}\right)\,,

(4.5)

is the Noether 2-form charge associated to local Lorentz invariance. In order
to perform this computation it is convenient to use the identity
Eq. (
2.10
).

By construction, this charge is closed on-shell for local Lorentz parameters
that leave the connection invariant,
i.e.
which are covariantly
constant. As we have stressed elsewhere, the invariance of the Vierbein is not
required. Furthermore, as we will see, Wald’s black-hole entropy is actually
given by this charge, for a particular Lorentz parameter.

4.2
Gauge transformations of the auxiliary 3-form field
C

The CR2 action is exactly invariant under the transformations

\delta_{\Lambda}C=d\Lambda\,,

(4.6)

where
\Lambda
is any 2-form. Then, from the general variation of the CR2
action Eq. (
2.8
), we get

0=\delta_{\Lambda}S_{\rm CR2}=\int\left\{\mathbf{E}_{C}\wedge d\Lambda+d\mathbf{\Theta}(\varphi,\delta_{\Lambda}\varphi)\right\}\,,

(4.7)

which leads to the very trivial Noether identity
d\mathbf{E}_{C}=0
and to the
off-shell closed current

\mathbf{J}[\Lambda]=\mathbf{\Theta}(\varphi,\delta_{\Lambda}\varphi)-\mathbf{E}_{C}\wedge\Lambda=d\mathbf{Q}[\Lambda]\,,

(4.8)

with

\mathbf{Q}[\Lambda]\equiv\ell_{s}\Lambda\,.

(4.9)

On-shell and for Killing (or
reducibility
) gauge parameters

invariant (

closed 2-form and the charges obtained by integrating it over closed
2-surfaces satisfy a Gauss law.

Conclusions

In this paper we have studied the thermodynamics of the black-hole solutions of
the Cano–Ruipérez version of the heterotic string theory effective action,
extended as in Ref.
[
16
]
so as to have a spacetime-variable

\alpha^{\prime}
. This extension is necessary to correctly account for all the terms
occurring in the Smarr formula and first law, in which
\alpha^{\prime}
(or

\ell_{s}
) plays the role of a thermodynamical variable as predicted in
Ref.
[
15
,
13
]
and as proven in

[
21
]
. The Smarr formula we have derived also contains a very
peculiar term proportional to the asymptotic value of the axion and which is
associated to the dependence of the Wald entropy on that parameter.

We have also shown how the definitions of scalar charges proposed in
Ref.
[
25
]
generalizing the proposal in
Ref.
[
29
]
also allow us to prove no-(primary)-hair theorems
in this theory. Since in this theory the scalars are sourced by the
Gauss–Bonnet and Pontrjagin 4-forms it is not too surprising that the values
of the scalar charges allowed by these theorems in regular black holes with
bifurcate horizons have a geometrical meaning and are, actually related to the
gravitational charges defined in
Ref.
[
4
]
. Unfortunately, in the examples considered,
the axion charge vanishes. However, it is not difficult to see
9
9

Work in
progress.
that in the Taub–NUT solution it does not and it is (obviously)
related to the NUT charge. Since the NUT charge can be seen as the magnetic
mass (a charge to which the dual graviton would couple) the existence of
relation between these results and the results of
Refs.
[
31
,
32
]
is evident. However, as in other cases
we have studied, although the structure of the charge is the same, the
parameters that actually occur in the expressions we find do not satisfy the
same properties as those that enter in the definitions of the charges and
ensure their closure. In particular, the Lorentz parameter that appears in our
expressions (the binormal to the horizon
n^{ab}
) is only covariantly
constant over the bifurcation sphere and the closure of the 2-form

R_{ab}n^{ab}
requires
n^{ab}
to be covariantly constant outside the
bifurcation sphere. Nevertheless, we think that, since these expressions occur
naturally in the Wald entropy (multiplied by the constant values of the
scalars over the horizon), they contain relevant physical and geometrical
information and should be investigated further. In this sense, we notice that
in higher-dimensional theories containing terms of higher order in curvatures,
further generalizations of the charges constructed in
Ref.
[
4
]
such as, for instance,

R^{a}{}_{c}\wedge R^{cb}n_{ab}\,,\hskip 28.45274ptR^{ab}\wedge R^{cd}\varepsilon_{abcdef}n^{ef}\,,

(7.1)

which are covariantly constant when
n^{ab}
is, will appear as natural
building blocks of the Wald entropy (in 6 dimensions, for the above examples).

Finally, the extension of the Cano–Ruipérez action that we have constructed
allows for a piecewise-constant
\alpha^{\prime}
, regions with different values of

\alpha^{\prime}
being separated by domain walls. Customarily,
\alpha^{\prime}
is always
taken as a constant in string theory and this possibility has never been
considered before, but the existence of domain walls associated to this
parameter and which do not fit in the standard spectrum of extended
perturbative and non-perturbative extended objects is a tantalizing
possibility worth exploring.

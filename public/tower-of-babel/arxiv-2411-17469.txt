The Derived Unipotent Block of p p -Adic GL 2 \GL_{2} as Perfect Complexes over a dg Schur Algebra
Rose Berry

Abstract
For a p p -adic field F F of residual cardinality q q , we provide a triangulated equivalence between the bounded derived category D b ​ ( ℬ 1 ​ ( G ) fg ) {D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)_{\fg}) of finitely generated unipotent representations of G = GL 2 ⁡ ( F ) G=\GL_{2}(F) and perfect complexes over a dg enriched Schur algebra, in the non-banal case of odd characteristic l l dividing q + 1 q+1 . The dg Schur algebra is the dg endomorphism algebra of a projective resolution of a direct sum V V of the parahoric inductions of the trivial representations of the reductive quotients of G G , and V V is shown to be a classical generator of D b ​ ( ℬ 1 ​ ( G ) fg ) {D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)_{\fg}) .

Introduction

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field with residue field
k
, of characteristic
p
and cardinality
q
, and let
G
be the
F
-points of a reductive algebraic group over
F
.

By
[
Ber84
]
, the category of smooth complex representations of
G
is known to decompose into blocks, which are parameterised by inertial supercuspidal support. Furthermore, in almost all cases the structure of these blocks is known via the theory of types(
[
Adl+24a
,
Adl+24
]
). Write
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)
for the unipotent block, that is, the block containing the trivial representation. For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, it was shown, first in
[
BK99
]
and expanded on in
[
Dat17
]
, that each block of
G
is equivalent to some
\mathcal{B}_{1}(H)
, where
H
is a finite product of general linear groups over finite extensions of
F
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)
is equivalent to the category of modules over the Iwahori-Hecke algebra
\mathsf{H}_{R}(n)
, which has an explicit description in terms of generators and relations, and whose modules are well-understood.

A natural question to ask is how much of this structure passes to the category of smooth
R
-representations for
R
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
l
, which we take to be different from
p
. In the case of
l
banal, that is, not dividing the order of the reductive quotient of any parahoric subgroup, all the same results hold. However, in non-banal characteristic much less is known.

It is shown in
[
Vig98
]
that, for
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, there is the same decomposition into blocks (this is however known to fail for other choices of
G
- see
[
Dat18a
]
). Furthermore, there is the same reduction of blocks to the case of
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)
for some product of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
(see
[
Dat18
]
,
[
Chi18
]
). However, the equivalence of
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)
with the category of modules over
\mathsf{H}_{R}(n)
fails. Despite this, some partial results are still known:

Vignéras defines in
[
Vig03
]
a subcategory
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\prime}(G)
of
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)
given by the representations annihilated by a certain ideal
\mathcal{I}
of the global Hecke algebra
\mathsf{H}(G)
of
G
. She shows that
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\prime}(G)
is equivalent to the category of modules over an algebra closely related to
\mathsf{H}_{R}(n)
, the Schur algebra
\mathsf{S}_{R}(n)
. Furthermore, some power of
\mathcal{I}
annihilates
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)
. Vignéras achieves this by giving an explicit progenerator
Q
of the subcategory whose endomorphisms give
\mathsf{S}_{R}(n)
, but also observes that
\mathsf{S}_{R}(n)
is the endomorphisms of a much simpler representation
V
, whose annihilator is
\mathcal{I}
.

We seek to improve this state of affairs at the derived level. Write
{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)_{\fg})
for the bounded derived category of finitely generated unipotent representations, and for a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
with object
G
write
\langle G\rangle_{\mathcal{T}}
for the subcategory classically generated by
G
. We obtain the following main theorem:

Theorem

(Theorems 6.8 and 6.10)
.

Suppose that
n=2
and
l
odd dividing
q+1
. Then
{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)_{\fg})=\langle Q\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)_{\fg})}=\langle V\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)_{\fg})}
.

We may immediately improve this via the theory of dg algebras. Let
V^{\bullet}
be a projective resolution of
V
in
\Mod(G)
, and write
\dgend
for the dg endomorphism algebra of a complex and
\per
for the perfect complexes over a dg algebra.

Corollary

(Corollary 6.11)
.

Under the same hypotheses as the theorem, there is a triangulated equivalence
{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)_{\fg})\simeq\per(\dgend(V^{\bullet}))
.

Due to the relative simplicity of
V
, its projective resolution
V^{\bullet}
can be described explicitly, and future work of the author seeks to give an explicit description of its dg endomorphisms.

To establish that
Q
classically generates
{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)_{\fg})
, we use the results of
[
Dat09
]
to show that the categories under consideration are Noetherian, and extend a result of
[
DY16
]
to positive characteristic to show the following:

Lemma

(Theorem 3.15)
.

For any
n
and any
l\neq p
, the Schur algebra
\mathsf{S}_{R}(n)
has finite global dimension.

To show an equivalence between the categories generated by
Q
and
V
in the case
n=2
and
l
odd dividing
q+1
, we use that the unipotent block
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G_{f})
of the finite reductive quotient
G_{f}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
has cyclic defect group in this characteristic, and that it contains certain finite analogues
Q_{f}
and
V_{f}
of
Q
and
V
. This allows us to use results of
[
Ack06
]
to describe explicitly the full structure of simple and projective modules of
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G_{f})
, and to describe
Q_{f}
and
V_{f}
in terms of these. Thus we may show the following:

Lemma

(Lemma 4.13)
.

\langle Q_{f}\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G_{f})_{\fg})}=\langle V_{f}\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G_{f})_{\fg})}
.

We then attempt to lift this equivalence into the
p
-adic setting. However, a barrier occurs, as
Q
is not parahorically induced from
Q_{f}
. Resolving this amounts to showing the following property of the annihilator
\mathcal{I}_{f}
of
V_{f}
, which is a 2-sided ideal of the finite group algebra and hence may be viewed as a representation of
G_{f}
:

Lemma

(Lemma 5.4)
.

\mathrm{I}_{G_{f},K}^{G}\mathcal{I}_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
.

We show this by giving a full and explicit description of
\mathcal{I}_{f}
and showing it annihilates
V
. To get this explicit description, we again use the structure of
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G_{f})
in terms of projectives, together with results from
[
Pai14
]
giving explicit formulae for their endomorphisms. Thus the final proof rests on a long explicit calculation in the global Hecke algebra of the
p
-adic group.

The main theorem and its corollary allow us to describe
{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)_{\fg})
in terms of a dg algebra whose zeroth cohomology is
\mathsf{S}_{R}(n)
. The current work of the author is in describing this dg algebra, up to quasi-isomorphism, explicitly, to enable an explicit description of its perfect complexes and thus of
{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B}_{1}(G)_{\fg})
.

The methods of this paper, specifically the second and third lemmas, rely on explicit knowledge of the structure of
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G_{f})
. This structure is known in more generality than just
n=2
and
l
odd dividing
q+1
: it is known whenever
\mathcal{B}_{1}(G_{f})
has cyclic defect group. We thus expect this method to generalise to this broader case.

Furthermore, the Schur algebra can be defined for any
G
, and it seems likely that the proof of the first lemma could extend to this case. The generators
V
,
Q
,
V_{f}
, and
Q_{f}
can all be defined in this generality, and one might hope that the other two lemmas hold in this case too, though the method in this paper is unlikely to extend. Should a more general method be found, the main theorem could then be extended to other cases where the block decomposition is known to hold.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let

6
dg Algebras and their Perfect Complexes

We first recall the definitions of dg algebras and their derived categories. For now, let
A
denote an arbitrary
R
-algebra.

Let
(M^{\bullet},d^{\prime})
and
(N^{\bullet},d^{\prime\prime})
be
A
-complexes. The
R
-complex
\dghom_{A}(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})
of dg morphisms has in degree
n
the homogeneous
A
-module homomorphisms
f:M^{\bullet}\rightarrow N^{\bullet}
of degree
n
, with differential

df:=d^{\prime\prime}f-(-1)^{n}fd^{\prime}

for all
f
of degree
n
.

A morphism of complexes is a dg morphism
f
of degree
0
that lies in
\ker(d)
, that is, such that
d^{\prime\prime}f=fd^{\prime}
.

A homotopy of morphisms of complexes
f,g:M^{\bullet}\rightarrow N^{\bullet}
is a dg morphism
h
of degree
-1
such that
dh=f-g
, that is, such that
f-g=d^{\prime\prime}h-hd^{\prime}
. If such an
h
exists we say
f
and
g
are homotopic. This gives an equivalence relation.

Write
K(A)(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})
for the homotopy equivalence classes of morphisms of complexes
f,g:M\rightarrow N
. Then we have that
H^{n}(\dghom_{A}(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet}))=K(A)(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet}[n])
.

We can extend these notions to a richer class of structures than ordinary algebras:

Definition 6.1
.

A dg algebra (short for ’differential graded algebra’) over
R
is an
R
-complex
(B,d)
that is also a graded algebra over
R
, satisfying the graded Leibniz rule

d(fg)=d(f)g+(-1)^{n}fd(g)

for all
f
of degree
n
.

We think of an ordinary (ie non-dg) algebra as a dg algebra with all elements having degree
0
.

The way we shall obtain dg algebras is by taking dg endomorphisms of complexes over ordinary algebras: the dg endomorphism algebra
\dgend_{A}(M^{\bullet})
is the dg algebra whose complex is
\dghom_{A}(M^{\bullet},M^{\bullet})
, with multiplication given by componentwise composition.

A (left) dg module over
B
is an
R
-complex
(M^{\bullet},d^{\prime})
with a graded (left) module action of
B
of degree
0
, compatible with the
R
-action, such that

d^{\prime}(fv)=(df)v+(-1)^{n}f(d^{\prime}v)

for all
f\in B
of degree n.

Let
(M^{\bullet},d^{\prime})
and
(N^{\bullet},d^{\prime\prime})
be dg modules. The complex of dg morphisms of dg modules
\dghom_{B}(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})
is the subcomplex of
\dghom_{R}(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})
consisting of those dg morphisms that are also
B
-module homomorphisms.

Morphisms and homotopies of morphisms of dg modules are the dg morphisms of dg modules that are also morphisms and homotopies of morphisms of complexes respectively.

When
B=A
is an ordinary algebra, the two notions of
\dghom_{A}(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})
agree.

Write
K(B)
for the triangulated category whose objects are dg modules over
B
and whose morphisms are homotopy equivalence classes of morphisms of dg modules. The distinguished triangles in
K(B)
are the exact sequences that are split as graded modules.

A morphism of dg modules
M^{\bullet}\rightarrow N^{\bullet}
is a quasi-isomorphism if the induced morphism of graded modules
H^{\bullet}(M^{\bullet})\rightarrow H^{\bullet}(N^{\bullet})
is an isomorphism.

The derived category of dg modules over
B
, written
D(B)
, is the localisation of
K(B)
with respect to the quasi-isomorphisms, with the inherited triangulated structure. Then any short exact sequence in the category of dg modules is part of a unique distinguished triangle in
D(B)
. In particular, if two dg modules in an exact sequence in the category of dg modules both live in a full triangulated subcategory of
D(B)
, then so does the third, and hence such categories are closed under kernels, cokernels, and extensions in the category of dg modules. Furthermore, if
M^{\bullet}
is a projective resolution of
M
in
A{\operatorname{-Mod}}
, then

H^{n}(\dgend(M^{\bullet}))\cong\Hom_{D(A)}(M,M[n]).

Lemma 6.9

\langle\mathrm{I}_{G_{f},K}^{G}(Q_{f})\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})}=\langle V\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})}
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

5.3
, both
V
and
\mathrm{I}_{G_{f},K}^{G}(Q_{f})
are objects of
{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})
.

By
Corollary

4.12
, there is a short exact sequence, two entries of which are the direct summands of
V_{f}
, and two of which are the direct summands of
Q_{f}
. But as parahoric induction is exact, we thus get an analogous statement for
\mathrm{I}_{G_{f},K}^{G}(Q_{f})
and
V=\mathrm{I}_{G_{f},K}^{G}(V_{f})
. Thus they each classically generate the other.
∎

Theorem 6.10
.

{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})=\langle V\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})}

Proof.

We already know
V\in{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})
from
Proposition

5.3
, and from
Theorem

6.8
that
{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})=\langle Q\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})}
, so it suffices to prove that
Q\in\langle V\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})}
. But we also know from
Lemma

6.9
that
\langle\mathrm{I}_{G_{f},K}^{G}(Q_{f})\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})}=\langle V\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})}
. Hence we only need to show
Q\in\langle\mathrm{I}_{G_{f},K}^{G}(Q_{f})\rangle_{{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})}
. But by
Corollary

5.6
we have that
Q\cong\mathrm{I}_{G_{f},K}^{G}(Q_{f})
.
∎

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
V^{\bullet}
be a projective resolution of
V
in
\Mod(G)
. There is a triangulated equivalence
{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})\simeq\per(\dgend(V^{\bullet}))
.

Proof.

This follows applying
Theorem

6.4
to the category
{D}^{b}(\mathsf{H}_{1}(G)_{\fg})
and its classical generator
V
.
∎

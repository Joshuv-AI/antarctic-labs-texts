Development and evaluation of three multienzyme isothermal rapid amplification assays for fowl adenovirus serotype 4
Authors: Yin Dai, Yueyi Zhong, Fazhi Xu, Siqin Gu, Huiqin Zhou, Jieru Wang, Dongdong Yin, Lei Yin, Xuehuai Shen, Xiaocheng Pan, Xuelan Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Fowl adenovirus serotype 4 (FAdV-4) is the main causative agent of hydropericardium hepatitis syndrome (HHS), which has resulted in huge economic losses to the poultry industry in recent years. Hence, a rapid and simple visual detection method is needed for identification of FAdV-4. In this study, three multienzyme isothermal rapid amplification (MIRA) assays, basic MIRA, MIRA-qPCR and MIRA-LFD were developed for detection of FAdV-4. The amplification primers and reaction conditions were optimized, and the specificity and sensitivity of the assays were evaluated. The MIRA assays were specific for FAdV-4 with no cross-reaction with novel goose astrovirus, H9 subtype avian influenza virus, duck enteritis virus, Muscovy duck reovirus, or duck circovirus. The basic MIRA assay required only one primer pair and the reaction can be completed within 30 min at 36 °C. The MIRA-qPCR and MIRA-LFD assays were completed in 20 min with a minimum detection limit of 1 × 101 copies/μL and 1 × 102 copies/μL, respectively. The results of the MIRA-LFD assay can be observed directly with the naked eye, omitting the need for specialized instruments. The positive rate of three proposed MIRA assays were consistent with that of the conventional PCR assay. The MIRA assays are simple, rapid, and effective diagnostic tools for field detection of FAdV-4.

Introduction

As the main causative pathogen of hydropericardium hepatitis syndrome (HHS), fowl adenovirus serotype 4 (FAdV-4) is a type I avian adenovirus (subgroup C) and the prevalent serotype in many countries (Niu et al., 2018; Ishag et al., 2022; El-Shall et al., 2022; Toroghi et al., 2022). HHS, also known as "Angara disease", was first reported in Ahgara, Pakistan in 1987 (Afzal et al., 1991; Schonewille et al., 2008). Before 2015, the main clinical manifestation of FAdV infection was inclusion body hepatitis. Although the mortality rate is relatively low in China, highly pathogenic FAdV-4 has been linked to epidemics of severe HHS (Liu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022). The clinical characteristics of HHS are mainly swelling and hemorrhage of the kidneys and liver, in addition to hydropericardium syndrome (Wang et al., 2023; Wibowo et al., 2019). HHS can decrease egg production and is associated with a relatively high mortality rate of 20 %–100 %, which has resulted in huge economic losses to the chicken industry (Yin et al., 2020; Bayraktar et al., 2024).

The structural proteins of FAdV-4 include hexon, pentonbase, and fiber (Wang et al., 2018). As the main component of the viral nucleocapsid, the hexon protein has good immunogenicity and can stimulate production of specific antibodies by the host (Rajasekhar and Roy, 2014). The hexon gene, which codes for the type-, group-, and subgroup-specific epitopes, is highly conserved among avian adenoviruses. Thus, many molecular detection methods are based on the hexon gene, including various enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays, polymerase chain reaction (PCR), loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP), and quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) (Liu et al., 2021; Sultan et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024).

Multienzyme isothermal rapid amplification (MIRA) is a novel method for isothermal amplification of nucleic acids, similar to recombinant polymerase amplification. Four core proteins (recombinase, DNA helicase, single-chain antigen-binding protein, and DNA polymerase) were used to amplify a target gene under isothermal conditions (Park and Zhang, 2024). The MIRA method can be completed within 5–30 min at 25–42 °C using only a water bath to maintain a constant temperature. In addition, the MIRA method combined with fluorescence quantitative PCR (MIRA-qPCR) can improve sensitivity (Li et al., 2022). In combination with colloidal gold lateral flow chromatography (MIRA-LFD), the results can be visualized by observation of a colloidal gold test strip (Sun et al., 2021). However, the MIRA method has not yet been applied for detection of FAdV-4. In this study, three rapid MIRA methods (basic, fluorescent, and colloidal gold) were established for detection of FAdV-4 based on the hexon gene.

Materials and methods

Viruses and clinical samples

FAdV-4, novel goose astrovirus (NGAstV), avian influenza virus subtype H9 (AIV-H9), duck enteritis virus (DEV), Muscovy duck reovirus (MDRV), and duck circovirus (DuCV) were stored in our laboratory. From January 2023 to December 2023, clinical samples were collected from diseased and dead poultry from farms in Anhui province and submitted to our laboratory.

Design and synthesis of primers and probes

The recommended length of primers for the MIRA method is 25–35 bp to limit the amplicon length to ≤ 500 bp. To obtain the best diagnostic target for FAdv-4, full-length sequences of the hexon gene were retrieved from the GenBank database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank/). Primer pairs and probes for the MIRA method were designed based on the hexon gene sequence of FAdV-4 strain LC1611 (accession no. MN316650). The sequences of the primers and probes for the MIRA, MIRA-qPCR, and MIRA-LFD assays are listed in Table 1. Extraction of RNA and DNA

Total viral DNA and RNA were extracted using commercial kits (Mei5 Biotechnology Co.,Ltd, Beijing, China) in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. The RNA samples were reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using the M5 First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Mei5 Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). All nucleic acids were stored at -80 °C until assayed.

Preparation standard

The gene sequence containing the target fragment was synthesized and inserted into the recombinant PUC57-FADV-4 plasmid by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd.

Establishment of MIRA assays

The basic MIRA assay was performed using a DNA constant temperature rapid amplification kit (Weifang Amp-Future Biotech, Shandong, China; Catalog number WLB8201KIT). Each 25-μL reaction volume consisted of 14.7 μL of buffer A, 1.0 μL of each primer (10 μM), 2.5 μL of the DNA template, 4.55 μL of double-distilled water (ddH2O), and 1.25 μL of buffer B. After mixing, the reaction solution was heated to a constant temperature of 39 °C for 30 min. The resulting product was mixed with 4 μL of 6× loading buffer and then heated in a water bath at 56 °C for 5 min. Then, 5-μL aliquots were separated by electrophoresis using 2.0 % agarose gels. The best primers were selected based on the test results.

The MIRA-qPCR assay was performed using a DNA constant temperature rapid amplification kit (fluorescent type; Weifang Amp-Future Biotech; Catalog number WLE8202KIT). Each 25-μL reaction volume included 14.7 μL of buffer A, 1.0 μL of each primer (10 μM), 0.3 μL of the probe (10 μM), 1.0 μL of the DNA template, 5.75 μL of ddH2O, and 1.25 μL of buffer B. The mixture was transferred to a reaction tube and immediately heated to 39 °C for 20 min. Fluorescence was measured every 30 s. The best primers were selected based on the test results.

The MIRA-LFD assay was performed using a DNA constant temperature rapid amplification kit (colloidal gold strip type; Weifang Amp-Future Biotech; Catalog number WLN8203KIT). Each 25-μL reaction volume included 14.7 μL of buffer A, 1.0 μL of each primer (10 μM), 0.3 μL of the probe (10 μM), 1.0 μL of the DNA template, 5.75 μL of ddH2O, and 1.25 μL of buffer B. After mixing, the reaction solution was heated to a constant temperature of 39 °C for 12 min. After the reaction, the amplified product was diluted 10 times with ddH2O. Then, the end of the colloidal gold test strip was inserted into the mixture and the results were interpreted within 5 min. The best primers were selected based on the test results.

Condition optimization of the basic MIRA method

The effects of different temperatures (20, 25, 28, 30, 33, 34, 35, 36, 37, 38, 39, and 40 °C) and times (3, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, and 40 min) for the basic MIRA assay were tested to determine the optimal conditions.

Specificity of the three MIRA assays

Nucleic acids of FAdV-4, DEV, AIV-H9, NGoAstV, DuCV, and MDRV were used as templates to determine the specificity of the basic MIRA, MIRA-QPCR, and MIRA-LFD assays. For each assay, ddH2O was used as a negative control.

Sensitivity analysis of three MIRA

The recombinant plasmid pUC57-FAdV-4 was used to determine the sensitivity of the basic MIRA, MIRA-QPCR, and MIRA-LFD assays. The plasmid was serially diluted by 10-fold with ddH2O to concentrations of 1 × 109 to 1 × 101 copies/μL. The sensitivity of three assays was determined based on the minimum detectable concentration.

Evaluation with clinical samples

In total, 25 clinical samples were collected from diseased birds from different farms in Anhui province, China. The animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Institute of Animal Husbandry and Veterinary Science, Anhui Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Permit No: AAAS-IAHVS-Po-2023-0121), in accordance with the requirements of laboratory animal—Guideline for ethical review of animal welfare(National Standards of the People's Republic of China,GB/T35892-2018). The basic MIRA, MIRA-QPCR, MIRA-LFD, and conventional PCR assays were used for detection of FAdV-4. The coincidence rates among the four methods were compared. The conventional PCR assay was performed as described in a previous report (Ren et al., 2019). All PCR products of positive samples were sequenced by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd.

Discussion

HHS is a major infectious disease in chickens that results serious economic losses to the poultry industry. Hence, rapid, simple, and reliable methods are needed to detect suspected FAdV-4 infection. At present, the PCR assay is commonly used for detection of various pathogens (Li et al., 2017). The TaqMan-based real-time PCR and droplet digital PCR assays were developed for specific detection of FAdV-4, as more sensitive alternatives to the conventional PCR assay (Wang et al., 2017; Dong et al., 2019). However, these assays are time-consuming and require expensive instruments. LAMP is a commonly used method for detection of animal pathogens with advantages of a short reaction time, high sensitivity, and ease of operation (Yu et al., 2020; Fan et al., 2023). Other methods for detection of FAdV-4 include the LAMP real-time turbidity method, the LAMP combined Pyrococcus furiosus Argonaute assay, and the LAMP-LFD assay (Yuan et al., 2019; Yu et al., 2024; Zhai et al., 2019). The results of these assays can be visualized with the naked eye or under ultraviolet light, while the LAMP assays requires at least 50 min with high temperatures and four or six primers.

As compared to PCR- and LAMP-based assays, the proposed MIRA assays can be completed within 30 min at 25–42 °C and do not require the use of a thermal cycler (Shang et al., 2021; Sun et al., 2023). A MIRA assay for detection of chicken chaphamaparvovirus was established with a reaction time of only 15 min at 38 °C, and when combined with LFD can be completed in 20 min (Cui et al., 2023). The MIRA-LFD assay for detection of bovine coronavirus is highly sensitive and specific, comparable to RT-qPCR, with a minimum detection limit of 1 × 102 copies/μL (Ji et al., 2023). The MIRA assay can detect duck hepatitis B virus in 10 min at 38 °C, and only 15 min when combined with the LFD method (Xu et al., 2024). The proposed MIRA assays are highly sensitive and specific diagnostic tools for rapid field detection, comparable to the PCR and qPCR assays.

Currently, there is no MIRA assay for detection of FAdV-4. Hence, three MIRA assays were established. The basic MIRA assay only requires a single primer pair and can be completed within 30 min at 36 °C. The MIRA-qPCR and MIRA-LFD assays can be completed in 20 min, about a quarter of the time of the conventional PCR assay. The minimum detection limit of MIRA-qPCR method is 1 × 101 copies/μL, which is 100-fold greater than the basic MIRA assay. The results of the MIRA-LFD assay can be directly observed with the naked eye, omitting the need for specialized instruments. The detection rates of the three MIRA assays were consistent with that of the conventional PCR assay, but provided faster and more convenient detection of FAdV-4.

In summary, the basic MIRA, MIRA-qPCR and MIRA-LFD assays were developed for detection of FAdV-4 in this study. The MIRA assays are simple, rapid, and effective diagnostic tools for field detection, the advantages contribute to the prevention and control of FAdV-4 infection.

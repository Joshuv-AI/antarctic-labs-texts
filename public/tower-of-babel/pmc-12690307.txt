Ectopic hepatocellular carcinoma of the abdominal wall
Authors: Binglin Lai
Published: 2025

A 52-year-old male was admitted to the hospital as elevated alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels were detected during a routine physical examination, with the condition lasting for 2 months and no accompanying symptoms. The patient had no history of hepatitis. Laboratory tests upon admission revealed an AFP level of 1,660.80 ng/mL, while beta-human chorionic gonadotropin (β-hCG) levels were found to be less than 0.6 mIU/mL. An abdominal computed tomography (CT) scan (Figure 1A-1C) revealed a soft tissue mass in the subumbilical extraperitoneal space, supplied by abdominal wall vessels. Under general anesthesia, an abdominal tumor resection was performed. Immunohistochemically, the tumor cells were positive for Hep Par-1, Glypican-3, glutamine synthetase (GS), arginase-1 (Arg-1), and AFP. Negative staining was observed for CK19 and SALL4. Based on the morphological features (Figure 1D) and immunoprofile, the lesion was diagnosed as ectopic hepatocellular carcinoma (EHCC). On postoperative day 40, the AFP level was measured at 59.36 ng/mL. By the 6-month follow-up, the serum AFP level had returned to within normal limits (<10 ng/mL), and no evidence of local recurrence or distant metastasis was identified on imaging studies. EHCC refers to hepatocellular carcinoma originating from ectopic liver tissue located outside the liver, which forms through abnormal migration of liver tissue during embryonic development. Its incidence rate ranges from 0.24% to 0.47%, and it is often incidentally discovered during laparoscopic examination or autopsy (1,2). EHCC can occur in multiple sites, including the gallbladder, peritoneum, retroperitoneum, thoracic cavity, pancreas, adrenal gland, and diaphragm (3,4).

Images in a 52-year-old male with elevated alpha-fetoprotein. Axial (A), sagittal (B), and coronal (C) contrast-enhanced abdominal CT demonstrated a lobulated soft tissue mass in the abdominal wall with moderate enhancement and visible feeding arteries. The arrows indicate ectopic hepatocellular carcinoma. (D) Pathological examination of the specimen revealed tumor cells with large, irregular nuclei arranged in nests or sheets, abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, visible nucleoli, and rare mitotic figures (HE, ×400).

Scattering for time periodic Hamiltonians on graphs
Hiroshi Isozaki, Evgeny, L. Korotyaev

Abstract
. We develop a scattering theory for time-periodic Hamiltonians on discrete graphs, including long-range potentials with zero average for the period, and show the existence and completeness of wave operators.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
34A55, (34B24, 47E05)

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Space-time periodic systems

We consider a scattering problem for a magnetic Schrödinger equation on a
periodic graph
{\mathcal{G}}=({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}})
:

\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}}u(t)=-ih(t)u(t),\quad h(t)=\Delta_{\beta(t)}+\mathfrak{p}+V(t),

(1.1)

where the Hamiltonian
h(t)
is
\tau
-periodic in continuous time

t
in the following sense:
\Delta_{\beta(t)}
is a discrete magnetic
Laplacian with magnetic potential
\beta
on
{\mathcal{V}}
which is

\tau
-periodic in time (see (
1.3
)). The electric potential
V(t)
on

{\mathcal{V}}
is also assumed to be
\tau
-periodic in time, and
\mathfrak{p}:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a time-independent bounded electric potential.

The above operator has a lot of applications to various periodic
media, e.g. nanomedia, in physics, chemistry and engineering, see,
e.g.,
[
33
]
. Approximation by periodic graphs are often used
to study properties of such media, and the problem is reduced to
Schrödinger operators on graphs. It is known that the spectrum of
Schrödinger operators with periodic potentials on periodic
discrete graphs consists of a finite number of flat bands, i.e.
eigenvalues of infinite multiplicities, and an absolutely
continuous part (a union of a finite number of non-degenerate
bands), the latter of which gives rise to scattering. We are
interested in the scattering by periodic Hamiltonians on graphs,
which are also periodic in time.

1.2.
Magnetic Schrödinger operators on graphs

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}})
be a connected infinite graph embedded in

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Here
{\mathcal{V}}
is the set of vertices and
{\mathcal{E}}
is the set of
non-oriented edges. For
x,y\in{\mathcal{V}}
, if there is an edge in
{\mathcal{E}}

starting from
x
and ending at
y
, we say that
x
and
y
are
adjacent and denote by
x\sim y
. Each edge is not oriented at
first, however, we give two orientations for the edges in
{\mathcal{E}}
, and
denote the set of all oriented edges by
{\mathcal{A}}
. The edge starting
from
x
and ending at
y
is denoted by the ordered pair

(x,y)\in{\mathcal{A}}
, and is said to be
incident
to the vertices. We
define the degree
{\varkappa}_{x}\geqslant 1
of a vertex
x\in{\mathcal{V}}
as the number
of all edges in
{\mathcal{A}}
starting from
x
. We assume that

\varkappa_{+}:=\sup_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}}\varkappa_{x}<\infty.

(1.2)

A sequence of directed edges
({\bf e}_{1},{\bf e}_{2},\ldots,{\bf e}_{n})
is called
a
path
if the terminus of the edge
{\bf e}_{s}
coincides with the
origin of the edge
{\bf e}_{s+1}
for all
s=1,\ldots,n-1
. If the
terminus of
{\bf e}_{n}
coincides with the origin of
{\bf e}_{1}
, then the
path is called a
cycle
. The inverse edge of
{\bf e}=(x,y)\in{\mathcal{A}}

is denoted by
\underline{{\bf e}}=(y,x)
. A
magnetic vector potential on

{\mathcal{G}}
is a function
\alpha:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

\alpha(\underline{{\bf e}}\,)=-\alpha({\bf e})
for all
{\bf e}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
\ell^{p}({\mathcal{V}}),p\geqslant 1
be the space of all functions
f:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
equipped with the
norm
\|f\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}({\mathcal{V}})}=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}}|f_{x}|^{p}<\infty.
For a
magnetic vector potential
\alpha:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the

combinatorial magnetic Laplacian

\Delta_{\alpha}
on
f=(f_{x})_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}}\in\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}})
by

\big(\Delta_{\alpha}f\big)(x)={1\over 2}\sum_{{\bf e}=(x,y)\in{\mathcal{A}}}\big(f_{x}-e^{i\alpha({\bf e})}f_{y}\big),\qquad x\in{\mathcal{V}},

(1.3)

where the sum is taken over all oriented edges starting from
the vertex
x
. It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
)
that
\Delta_{\alpha}
is self-adjoint and its spectrum
\sigma(\Delta_{\alpha})
satisfies

\displaystyle\sigma(\Delta_{\alpha})\subset[0,\varkappa_{+}].

(1.4)

If
\alpha=0
, then
\Delta=\Delta_{0}
is the usual Laplacian
\Delta
without
magnetic potential:

\big(\Delta f\big)(x)={1\over 2}\sum_{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{A}}}\big(f_{x}-f_{y}\big),\qquad x\in{\mathcal{V}}.

(1.5)

Remark that there are other definitions of discrete magnetic
Laplacians on graphs: weighted, normalized, standard Laplacians,
see e.g.
[
29
]
,
[
24
]
and references therein. For a
magnetic Laplacian
\Delta_{\alpha}
and a real potential
\mathfrak{p}\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathcal{V}})
, that is
\big(\mathfrak{p}f\big)(x)=\mathfrak{p}_{x}f_{x}
for all

x\in{\mathcal{V}}
, we define a magnetic Schrödinger operator
h_{\alpha}
with
an electric potential
\mathfrak{p}
on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}})
by

h_{\alpha}=\Delta_{\alpha}+\mathfrak{p}.

(1.6)

1.3.
Periodic graphs.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice of rank
d
in

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with a basis
\{{\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{d}\}
, i.e.,
\Gamma=\Big\{{\bf a}:{\bf a}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}{\bf a}_{j},\;n_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\Big\}
.
We define an equivalence relation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
:

x=y\;(\hskip-11.38109pt\mod\Gamma)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x-y\in\Gamma\qquad\forall\,x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

We consider
a locally finite
\Gamma
-periodic graph

{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e. a
graph satisfying the following condition:

\bullet

{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{G}}+{\bf a}
for any
{\bf a}\in\Gamma
and the quotient graph

{\mathcal{G}}_{*}={\mathcal{G}}/\Gamma
is finite.

The basis
{\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{d}
of the lattice
\Gamma
is
called the
periods
of
{\mathcal{G}}
. The
fundamental graph

{\mathcal{G}}_{*}={\mathcal{G}}/\Gamma
is the quotient graph of
{\mathcal{G}}
by
\Gamma
, which is
a graph on the
d
-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}/\Gamma
. It has the vertex
set
{\mathcal{V}}_{*}={\mathcal{V}}/\Gamma
, the set
{\mathcal{E}}_{*}={\mathcal{E}}/\Gamma
of unoriented edges and
the set
{\mathcal{A}}_{*}={\mathcal{A}}/\Gamma
of oriented edges, all of which are finite.

A magnetic vector potential
\alpha:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and an electric potential

\mathfrak{p}:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are called
\Gamma
-periodic on
{\mathcal{G}}
, if they satisfy

\alpha({\bf e}+{\bf a})=\alpha({\bf e}),\quad\mathfrak{p}_{x+{\bf a}}=\mathfrak{p}_{x}\quad\forall\ ({\bf e},{\bf a},x)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times\Gamma\times{\mathcal{V}}.

(1.7)

Let us recall the spectral properties of the Schrödinger operator

h_{\alpha}
studied in
[
24
]
,
[
26
]
. We introduce the Hilbert space

{\mathscr{H}}=L^{2}\Big({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{dk\over(2\pi)^{d}}\,,\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}}_{*})\Big)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}^{\oplus}\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}}_{*})\,{dk\over(2\pi)^{d}}\,,\qquad{\mathbb{T}}^{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/(2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})^{d},

(1.8)

i.e., a constant fiber direct integral, equipped with the norm

\|g\|^{2}_{{\mathscr{H}}}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\|g(k)\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}}_{*})}^{2}\frac{dk}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,,

where the function
g(k)\in\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}}_{*})
for almost all
k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.
The parameter
k
is called the
quasimomentum
. We identify
the vertices of the fundamental graph
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}=({\mathcal{V}}_{*},{\mathcal{E}}_{*})
with the
vertices of the
\Gamma
-periodic graph
{\mathcal{G}}=({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}})
in the
fundamental cell
\Omega
.
In
[
24
]
, we have shown that there
exists a unitary operator
U:\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}})\to{\mathscr{H}}
such that

\displaystyle Uh_{\alpha}U^{-1}=\int^{\oplus}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}h_{\alpha}(k){dk\over(2\pi)^{d}},\quad h_{\alpha}(k)=\Delta_{\alpha}(k)+\mathfrak{p}

(1.9)

and the fiber Schrödinger operator
h_{\alpha}(k)
and the fiber
magnetic Laplacian
\Delta_{\alpha}(k)
act on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}}_{*})
. Here

\Delta_{\alpha}(k)
is some
\nu\times\nu
matrix with entries analytic in

k\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
, where
\nu:=\#{\mathcal{V}}_{*}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\#M
being the number
of elements in a set
M
. Each fiber operator
h_{\alpha}(k)
has
\nu

real eigenvalues labeled in non-decreasing order (counting
multiplicities) by

\lambda_{1}(k)\leqslant\lambda_{2}(k)\leqslant\ldots\leqslant\lambda_{\nu}(k),\qquad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}.

(1.10)

Each
\lambda_{j}(\cdot)

is a real and piecewise analytic function on the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
and
gives rise to the
spectral band

\sigma_{j}(h_{\alpha})
given by

\sigma_{j}(h_{\alpha})=[\lambda_{j}^{-},\lambda_{j}^{+}]=\lambda_{j}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}),\qquad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\nu},\qquad{\mathbb{N}}_{\nu}=\{1,\ldots,\nu\}.

(1.11)

Then the spectrum of the operator
h_{\alpha}
has a band structure and
is a union of a finite number of bands given by

\sigma(h_{\alpha})=\cup_{j=1}^{\nu}\sigma_{j}(h_{\alpha}),\qquad\sigma_{j}(h_{\alpha})=[\lambda_{j}^{-},\lambda_{j}^{+}].

(1.12)

Some of
\lambda_{j}(\cdot)
may be constant, i.e.,

\lambda_{j}(\cdot)=\Lambda_{j}=\mathop{\mathrm{const}}\nolimits
, on some subset
{\mathcal{O}}
of
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
of
positive Lebesgue measure. In this case the operator
h_{\alpha}
on
{\mathcal{G}}

has the eigenvalue
\Lambda_{j}
of infinite multiplicity. We call

\{\Lambda_{j}\}
a
flat band
. Thus, the spectrum of the magnetic
Schrödinger operator
h_{\alpha}
on the periodic graph
{\mathcal{G}}
has the
form

\sigma(h_{\alpha})=\sigma_{ac}(h_{\alpha})\cup\sigma_{fb}(h_{\alpha}),

(1.13)

where
\sigma_{ac}(h_{\alpha})
is the absolutely continuous spectrum, which
is a union of non-degenerate bands, and
\sigma_{fb}(h_{\alpha})
is the set
of all flat bands
\{\Lambda_{j}\}
. An open interval between two
neighboring non-degenerate bands is called a
spectral gap
. It
is possible that
\sigma(h_{\alpha})=\sigma_{fb}(h_{\alpha})
for specific
magnetic fields
\alpha
and
\mathfrak{p}
, see recent results
[
26
]
.
Moreover, it was shown that for any periodic graph and any periodic
vector magnetic potential
\sigma_{ac}(\Delta_{g\alpha}+\mathfrak{p})=\emptyset
only for a
finite number of coupling constants
g
running through any finite
interval. Thus below we assume that
\sigma_{ac}(h_{\alpha})\neq\emptyset
.

We discuss the existence and completeness of wave
operators for
h(t)=\Delta_{\beta(t)}+V(t)
in two situations. In the
first case, in Subsection
1.4
we consider
Hamiltonians on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with time-periodic electro-magnetic
potential, decaying at infinity of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, under the conditions

{\rm MZ_{s}}
and
{\rm VZ_{s}}
with
s=p,a
. In the second case, in
Subsection
1.5
we consider Hamiltonians on more general
graphs with time-periodic magnetic potential and time-independent
bounded electric potential, and in addition, time-periodic electric
potential decaying at space infinity, under the conditions M and V. Our main results are Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.5
and Corollary
1.6
.

1.4.
Scattering in time-periodic systems

We consider two classes of
perturbations for magnetic and electric fields. Let
\mathfrak{B}_{p}
and

\mathfrak{B}_{a}
be the sect of functions
b(x)

:{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\to{\bf R}
such that

\displaystyle b\in\mathfrak{B}_{p}\Longleftrightarrow b^{2}(x)\in\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),\quad\ d\geqslant 3,\quad\ \begin{cases}1\leqslant p<{6\over 5}&{\it if}\ \ d=3,\\
1\leqslant p<{4\over 3}&{\it if}\ \ d\geqslant 4,\end{cases}

(1.14)

\displaystyle b\in\mathfrak{B}_{a}\Longleftrightarrow(1+|x|)^{a}\,b^{2}(x)\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),\quad a>1,\quad d\geqslant 1.

Throughout the paper, we denote a function
q
on
{{\mathbb{R}}}\times{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{d}
as
q=q(t,x)=q_{x}(t)
. Our first class of magnetic,
electric potentials are assumed to satisfy the following conditions
for either
s=p
or
s=a
.

Condition
{\bf MZ_{s}}
:
The magnetic potential

\beta({\bf e},t)
on
{\mathcal{E}}
is
\tau
-periodic in time, i.e.,

\beta({\bf e},t)=\beta({\bf e},t+\tau)
for all
({\bf e},t)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}
. Moreover
there exists
b\in\mathfrak{B}_{s}
such that

\displaystyle|\beta({\bf e},t)|\leqslant 2b_{x}b_{y}\qquad\forall\ {\bf e}=(x,y).

(1.15)

Condition
{\bf VZ_{s}}
:
The electric potential

V
is written as
V=v+q
, where
v,q\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
and
there exists
b\in\mathfrak{B}_{s}
such that letting
Q_{x}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}q_{x}(s)ds
, we have for all
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
{\bf e}=(x,y)

\displaystyle|v_{x}(t)|\leqslant b_{x}^{2},

(1.16)

\displaystyle Q_{x}(\tau)=0,\qquad\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}}|Q_{x}(t)|=o(1)\quad\mathop{\mathrm{as}}\nolimits\quad|x|\to\infty,

(1.17)

\displaystyle|Q_{y}(t)-Q_{x}(t)|\leqslant b_{y}b_{x}.\quad

Our first main theorem is as follows. Let
U(t,s)
be the evolution
operator for
h(t)=\Delta_{\beta(t)}+V(t)
, i.e.
U(t,s)f
is
the unique solution to the initial value problem

i\frac{d}{dt}u(t)=h(t)u,\quad t\in{{\mathbb{R}}},\quad u(s)=f.

(1.18)

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that the magnetic
potential
\beta
and the electric potential
V=q+v
of

h(t)=\Delta_{\beta(t)}+V(t)
on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})

are
\tau
-periodic and satisfy

(1) either ”Conditions
{\rm MZ_{p}}
and Conditions
{\rm VZ_{p}}
”,

(2) or ”Conditions
{\rm MZ_{a}}
and Conditions
{\rm VZ_{a}}
”.

Then the wave operator

\displaystyle W_{\pm}=s-\lim U(0,t)e^{-it\Delta}\qquad as\qquad t\to\pm\infty,

(1.19)

exists and is complete, i.e.
\mathop{\mathrm{Ran}}\nolimits W_{\pm}={\mathcal{H}}_{ac}(U(\tau,0))
.

Remark 1.2
.

1) Theorem
1.1
describes the continuous spectrum of
the monodromy operators
U(\tau,0)
. The discrete spectrum of
U(\tau,0)

in possible gaps in the continuous spectrum, embedded eigenvalues
in the continuous spectrum and their numbers are discussed in
[
19
]
.

2) Eigenvalues of
U(\tau,0)
for complex potentials are studied in
[
21
]
.

As an example, let us consider a potential
V=v
decaying sufficiently rapidly in
x
.

Example 1.3
.

Consider the Hamiltonian
h(t)=\Delta+v(t)
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},d\geqslant 1
, where

v(t)={A\over 1+|x+\sin t|^{a}},a>1
and
A=\mathop{\mathrm{const}}\nolimits
,
|x|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+...+x_{d}^{2}
.
As the potetial
v
satisfies Condition
{\rm VZ_{a}}
, by Theorem
1.1
,
the wave operator
W_{\pm}
in (
1.19
) exists and is complete.

We can also deal with the potential
V=q
, where
q
is not decaying but oscillates sufficiently fast.

Example 1.4
.

Consider the Hamiltonian
h(t)=\Delta+q_{x}(t)
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},d\geqslant 1
,
where the potential
q_{x}(t)=A\cos(|x|^{2d}t)
and
A=\mathop{\mathrm{const}}\nolimits
,

|x|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+...+x_{d}^{2}
. Note that
q
is only oscillating in
t
and

x
, but its integral

Q_{x}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}q_{x}(s)ds={A\sin(|x|^{2d}t)\over|x|^{2d}}

decays in
x
, since
Q_{x}(t)={O(1)\over|x|^{2d}}
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Thus
q
satisfies Condition
{\rm VZ_{a}}
, and by Theorem
1.1
,
the wave operator
W_{\pm}
in (
1.19
) exists and is complete.

1.5.
Scattering in space-time periodic systems

We consider in a more general situation, and study a magnetic Schrödinger equation on a graph

{\mathcal{G}}
:

\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}}u(t)=-ih(t)u(t),\quad h(t)=\Delta_{\beta(t)}+\mathfrak{p}+V(t),

(1.20)

where
h(t)
is a discrete magnetic Laplacian given by (
1.3
) and

\mathfrak{p}:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a bounded electric potential. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},{\mathcal{H}})
,
{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}={\mathbb{R}}/(\tau{\mathbb{Z}})
. Also let
h_{0}=\Delta
be
the free Laplacian on
{\mathcal{G}}
, and
R_{0}(\lambda)=(h_{0}-i\partial_{t}-\lambda)^{-1}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
be the
set of compact operators on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
. We assume that the magnetic
potential
\beta
is
\tau
-periodic in time and satisfies:

Condition M.

The electric potential
\mathfrak{p}
is
time-independent and in
\ell^{\infty}({\mathcal{V}})
. The magnetic potential

\beta(\cdot,t)
on
\ell^{\infty}({\mathcal{E}})
has a form

\beta({\bf e},t)=\alpha({\bf e})+\delta({\bf e},t)
, where
\alpha
and
\delta
are magnetic
potentials and
\delta
is
\tau
-periodic in time (i.e.,

\delta({\bf e},t)=\delta({\bf e},t+\tau)
for all
({\bf e},t)\in{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}
) and
satisfies:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\sum_{{\bf e}\in{\mathcal{E}}}|\sin\delta({\bf e},t)|dt<\infty.

(1.21)

We impose the following condition on the electric potential

V(t)=V(t,x)
in (
1.20
).

Condition V.

The function
V=v+q
, where
v,q
are

\tau
-periodic and satisfy:

\displaystyle v\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},\ell^{1}({\mathcal{V}})),\qquad q\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},\ell^{\infty}({\mathcal{V}})),

(1.22)

and the potential
Q_{x}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}q_{x}(s)ds
for
(x,t)\in{\mathcal{V}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies

\displaystyle Q(\tau)=0,\quad Q\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}})),

(1.23)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf e}=(x,y)\in{\mathcal{A}}}|Q_{x+{\bf e}}(t)-Q_{x}(t)|\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},\ell^{1}({\mathcal{V}})).

(1.24)

The second main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let Schrödinger operators
h(t)=\Delta_{\beta(t)}+\mathfrak{p}+V(t)
and

h_{\alpha}=\Delta_{\alpha}+\mathfrak{p}
act on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}=\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}})
,
where the magnetic potential
\beta=\alpha+\delta
satisfies Condition M, the
electric potential
\mathfrak{p}\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathcal{V}})
is real and the potential

V
satisfies Condition V. We assume that
\sigma_{ac}(h_{\alpha})\neq\emptyset
.
Then the wave operator

W_{\pm}=s-\lim U(0,t)e^{-ith_{\alpha}}P_{ac}(h_{\alpha})\qquad\mathop{\mathrm{as}}\nolimits\ t\to\pm\infty

(1.25)

exists and is complete, i.e.
\mathop{\mathrm{Ran}}\nolimits W_{\pm}={\mathcal{H}}_{ac}(U(\tau,0))
.

We apply Theorem
1.5
to periodic graphs with periodic magnetic
vector potentials
\alpha:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and periodic electric potentials

\mathfrak{p}:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Recall the spectral structure (
1.13
).

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}})
be a periodic graph.
Let
h(t)=\Delta_{\beta(t)}+\mathfrak{p}+V(t)
act on the Hilbert space

{\mathcal{H}}=\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}})
, where the magnetic potential
\beta=\alpha+\delta

satisfies Condition M, the potential
\mathfrak{p}\in\ell^{\infty}({\mathcal{V}})
and the
potential
V
satisfies Condition V. We assume that the Schrödinger
operator
h_{\alpha}=\Delta_{\alpha}+\mathfrak{p}
, where the magnetic potential
\alpha
and
the electric potential
\mathfrak{p}
are real
\Gamma
-periodic, satisfies

\sigma_{ac}(h_{\alpha})\neq\emptyset
. Then the wave operator

W_{\pm}=s-\lim U(0,t)e^{-ith_{\alpha}}P_{ac}(h_{\alpha})\qquad\mathop{\mathrm{as}}\nolimits\ t\to\pm\infty

(1.26)

exists and is complete, i.e.
\mathop{\mathrm{Ran}}\nolimits W_{\pm}={\mathcal{H}}_{ac}(U(\tau,0))
.

As is well-known, for time-independent potentials
V(x)
, there is a
sharp border line of the decay rate for the existence and
completeness of wave operators. The situation is more complicated
for time-dependent perturbations. For the sake of comparison, in
Theorem 6.1, we discuss the case of electric and magnetic potentials
decaying
t
and
x
. It is also well-known that in some cases
strongly oscillating perturbations (in
t
or
x
) form an
exceptional class. We shall also discuss them.

Although the above theorems are apparently similar, the method of
proof is different. Theorem
1.1
is proven by Kato-Kuroda’s
stationary scattering theory. For the proof of Theorems
1.5
, we
use time-dependent scattering theory, especially Howland’s theory of
time-dependent Hamiltonian and the gauge transform.

We can also apply Theorem
1.5
to different graphs, for example,
to trees in which the corresponding Laplacian has an absolutely continuous spectrum. Such
Laplacians on trees are studied by Aptekarev,
Denisov, Yattselev in their paper
[
6
]
.

Different properties of
Schrödinger operators with periodic potentials on periodic graphs
were considered in
[
27
,
29
]
, see also
references therein. Schrödinger operators with periodic magnetic
and electrostatic potentials are discussed in
[
24
,
26
]
.

2.1.
Quasienergy operators

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space.
Throughout the paper
by a

we mean a two-parameter family of unitary
operators

,
t,s\in{{\mathbb{R}}}
, acting on
\mathcal{H}
and
satisfying the following conditions:

\bullet

U(t,s)U(s,r)=U(t,r)
for all
t,s,r\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\bullet

U(t,t)=I
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\bullet

U(t,s)
is strongly continuous in
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Now we discuss the scattering for time periodic Hamiltonian

h(t),t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We assume that

is
\tau
-periodic in time, i.e.,
h(t+\tau)=h(t)
for any

.
Letting
{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}={\mathbb{R}}/(\tau{\mathbb{Z}})
, we introduce the
space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},{\mathcal{H}})
of
{\mathcal{H}}
-valued functions
{\mathbb{R}}\ni t\to f(t)
that are

\tau
–periodic in
t
equipped with norm

\|f\|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}}={1\over\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\|f(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}dt.

For the propagator

U(t,s)
on
{\mathcal{H}}

\widetilde{U}(s):\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\ni f\to(\widetilde{U}(s)f)(t):=\,U(t,t+s)f(t+s),\quad(t,s)\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}\times{\mathbb{R}}

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
(with respect to the parameter
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
). By
Stone’s theorem, this group defines a self-adjoint operator
\widetilde{h}
acting on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
as

\displaystyle(e^{is\widetilde{h}}f)(t)=U(t,t+s)f(t+s),\quad f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}.

We call such an operator

a

and the

the

. We consider the case
when

is the propagator for a family of
\tau
-periodic
self-adjoint operators

. In the case when

is constructed by the standard method of iteration for
the integral equation (see (
4.27
) and
[
11
]
). We introduce a
notation. For a
t

\tau
-periodic bounded operator

on
\mathcal{H}

on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
by

\langle A(t)\rangle:\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\ni f(t)\to A(t)f(t)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.2)

For the propagator
U(t,s)
, the monodromy operator is defined by

M(t)=U(t+\tau,t),\qquad t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.3)

The above conditions for the propagator yield

(2.4)

Evaluating at
s=-\tau

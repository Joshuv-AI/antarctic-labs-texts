Estimating location parameters of several exponential distributions with ordered restriction under Linex loss function
Shrajal Bajpai, Lakshmi Kanta Patra, Suchandan Kayal

Abstract
Some improved estimators of the location parameters of several exponential distributions with ordered restriction are derived and compared numerically using Monte Carlo simulations. Note that the two-parameter exponential distribution is very useful in different areas like survival analysis, reliability engineering and biomedical research, where products have a guaranteed failure-free operating time before failures begin to occur. In the present manuscript, we address the component-wise estimation of location parameters of k ( ≥ 2 ) k~(\geq 2) exponential distributions under an asymmetric Linex loss function. The location parameter represents a minimum guaranteed period before failure. At first, we consider the estimation of the location parameters with ordered scale parameters. Next, we address the estimation of ordered location parameters. For this, we take three different cases into account as follows: ( i ) (i) scale parameters are known, ( i ​ i ) (ii) scale parameters are unknown but equal, ( i ​ i ​ i ) (iii) scale parameters are unknown and unequal. In these cases, we establish general inadmissibility results. Further, using the general result, the inadmissibility of the best affine equivariant estimator is proved. The improved estimators are written in explicit forms. Additionally, we show that the results for several important life-testing schemes namely ( i ) (i) Type-II censoring, ( i ​ i ) (ii) progressive type-II censoring and ( i ​ i ​ i ) (iii) record value data can be obtained using i.i.d sample.Finally, for each case, the Monte Carlo simulation technique is used to compare the performance of the proposed estimators based on their risk values. The numerical results reveal a significant improvement of the proposed estimators. Keywords : Decision theory; best affine equivariant estimator; Linex loss function; censored sample; Monte Carlo simulation; percentage risk improvement. Introduction

In the reliability theory and survival analysis, the two-parameter exponential distribution plays a vital role and is widely used as a lifetime distribution. The location parameter in two-parameter exponential distribution represents the guarantee time, and the reciprocal of scale parameter represents the hazard rate. So, estimating parameters of the two-parameter exponential distribution is an important problem. In reliability engineering and various life testing experiments, we encounter situations where prior ordering of parameters is known. Some examples are as follows.

(i)

Usually, electronic products have lower failure rates in their early age compared to their late age.

(ii)

The amount of time spent on an online shopping platform by a frequent user is less than that of an user who shops online occasionally.

(iii)

The lifetime of local computer hardware will be shorter than that of a branded computer hardware.

(iv)

Waiting time for an express train will be longer than that for a city metro.

(v)

Servicing time for passengers at an airport taken by a newly hired staff is higher than that
of a professional and well-experienced staff.

In addition to these examples, for more details on the estimation problems under order-restricted parameters for various probability distributions, one may refer to
Silvapulle and Sen 2005
and
Van Eeden 2006
. An application of the order-restricted estimation problem in meta-analysis can be found in
Taketomi et al. 2021
. In this paper, we will study the estimation of ordered location parameters for the several exponential distributions. Numerous authors have studied the estimation of ordered parameters of exponential distributions in the literature.
Pal and Kushary 1992
have considered estimating ordered location parameters of two exponential populations with an unknown scale parameter with respect to the squared error loss function. They proved that the unrestricted standard estimators are inadmissible by proposing improved estimators.
Vijayasree and Singh 1993
investigated the mixed estimators of ordered means of two exponential distributions. They have established that a subclass of the mixed estimators beats the usual estimator, the sample mean.
Misra and Singh 1994
have studied the component-wise estimation of ordered restricted location parameter with scale parameters known and unequal under the squared error loss function. They established that the unrestricted minimum risk estimators are inadmissible under ordered restrictions. The authors also studied the mixed estimators. Component-wise estimation of ordered parameters of the
k~(\geq 2)
exponential populations has been studied by
Vijayasree et al. 1995
. They have proved the inadmissibility of the usual estimators of ordered parameters with respect to the squared error loss function.
Misra et al. 2002
have derived the smooth estimator of ordered scale parameters of two gamma distributions using
Brewster and Zidek 1974
methodology. They have established that this smooth estimator dominates the best affine equivariant estimator (BAEE).
Chang and Shinozaki 2002
have considered the problem of estimating linear functions of ordered scale parameters of two gamma distributions.
Nematollahi and Meghnatisi 2011
considered the class of mixed estimators to estimate the ordered scale parameter of gamma distributions. They have obtained a subclass of the mixed estimators that improves upon the usual estimators.

Jana and Kumar 2015
have studied estimating the ordered scale parameter of two exponential distributions with a common guarantee time. They have obtained a uniformly minimum variance unbiased estimator (UMVUE). The authors proved that the restricted MLE (RMLE) is better than the MLE. Also, the authors derived estimators that dominate the UMVUE, MLE, and RMLE under a quadratic loss function.
Petropoulos 2017
dealt with the improved estimation of the ordered scale parameter of two multivariate Lomax distributions with unknown location parameters under a quadratic loss function. He proposed various estimators that improve upon the BAEE.
Patra and Kumar 2018
considered estimating the common hazard rate of two exponential distributions with ordered location parameters under a general scale invariant loss function. They have obtained estimators that dominate the BAEE. For some recent works on estimation of ordered parameters of exponential distributions, we refer to
Bobotas 2019
,

Patra et al. 2021
,
Kayal and Patra 2024
,
Jena and Tripathy 2024
, and
Bajpai and Patra 2025
.

In the literature, very little attention has been given in estimating the ordered location parameters of exponential distributions under the asymmetric Linex loss function. In this work, we study the problem of estimating ordered location parameters of
k~(\geq 2)
exponential distributions. Let
(X_{i1},\ldots,X_{in_{i}})
be a random sample drawn from the
i
-th population
\mathcal{P}_{i},

i=1,\ldots,k~(k\geq 2)
. The probability density function of the
i
-th population
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{i}(x;\mu_{i},\sigma_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}~\exp{\left(-\displaystyle\frac{x-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)},&\textrm{if $x>\mu_{i}$}\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

where
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma_{i}>0
. This paper focuses on the problem of estimating the location parameter
\mu_{i}
under the loss function, given by

L(t)=q\Big[e^{\displaystyle pt}-pt-1\Big],~p\neq 0,~q>0,

(1.4)

where
t=\frac{\delta_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}
. We assume
q=1.

The loss function in (
1.4
) was first proposed by
Varian 1975
. The constant
p
governs the shape of the loss function. From
Zellner 1986
, for
p=1
, this loss function becomes significantly asymmetric, and in this case, the
overestimation is more serious than the underestimation. When
a<0
and
t<0
, the Linex loss function increases exponentially, while for
t>0
it grows almost linearly and vice versa. It can be observed that if
|p|
is close to zero, the Linex loss function becomes almost symmetric and can be approximated by a squared error loss function. For small values of
|p|
, by a Taylor series expansion, we have
e^{pt}-pt-1\approx\frac{p^{2}t^{2}}{2}
, a squared error loss function. Further, the Linex loss function behaves asymmetric when
|p|
is large.
The main contributions of this article are as follows:

(i)

We consider the component-wise estimation of the location parameter
\mu_{i}
when scale parameters satisfy the order restriction
\sigma_{1}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{k}
. We prove a general inadmissibility result for finding improved estimators. Further, it is shown that the BAEE of
\mu_{i}
is inadmissible.

(ii)

Next, we discuss component-wise estimation of
\mu_{i}
under the ordered restriction
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{k}
. The cases when
(i)
all
\sigma_{i}
’s are known,
(ii)

\sigma_{i}
’s are unknown and equal to
\sigma
,
(iii)
all
\sigma_{i}
’s are unequal and unknown are considered. For each case, we have proved general inadmissibility results. Using this result, the inadmissibility of the BAEE is established.

(iii)

We conduct a simulation study to asses the performance of the proposed estimators. In this purpose, we generate
50,000
random samples using MATLAB. The PRIs of the BAEE are tabulated to compare risk performance of the proposed estimators.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
derives estimators of
\mu_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,k
which improve upon the BAEE and MLE under ordered scale parameters followed by a simulation study. In Section
3
we have proposed estimators under ordered location parameters considering various scenarios such as when scale parameters are known and when it is unknown with both equal and unequal cases. In Subsection
3.1
we have considered the problem when scale parameter is known followed by a simulation study, and in Subsections
3.3
and
3.5
estimators are derived when scale parameters are unknown with equal and unequal cases, respectively. Each subsection contains simulation study corresponding to the derived results. Section
4
discusses several life-testing schemes shows that the corresponding result can be obtained using methodologies based on i.i.d sample. Finally, in Section
5
we conclude the work with a summary of the findings.

2
Estimation of location parameters when scale parameters are ordered

In this section, we consider the estimation of
\mu_{i}
when the scale parameters are ordered that is
\sigma_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\sigma_{k}.
For estimating
\mu_{i}
, we consider the loss function as

\displaystyle L(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i},\delta_{i})=\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{\delta_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right\}-p\left(\frac{\delta_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)-1,~p\neq 0.

(2.1)

Define, for
i=1,\dots,k

X_{i(i)}=\min\{X_{i1},\ldots,X_{in_{i}}\}~~\mbox{ and }~~T_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}(X_{ij}-X_{i(1)}).

Denote
\underline{X}=(X_{1(1)},\dots,X_{k(1)})
and
\underline{T}=(T_{1},\dots,T_{k})
. It can be easily seen that, for this case,
(\underline{X},\underline{T})
is a joint complete and sufficient statistic. Further
X_{1(1)},\dots,X_{k(1)},T_{1},\dots,T_{k}
are independently distributed with

X_{i(1)}\sim Exp(\mu_{i},,\sigma_{i}/n_{i})~~\mbox{ and }~~T_{i}\sim Gamma(n_{i}-1,\sigma_{i}).

(2.2)

First, we derive
the unrestricted BAEE of
\mu_{i}
using principal of invariance. For this purpose, we consider the affine group of transformations as

\mathcal{G}_{a_{i},b_{i}}=\{g_{a_{i},b_{i}}(x_{ij})=a_{i}x_{ij}+b_{i},~a_{i}>0,~b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Under this group of transformations, we have the following

(X_{i(1)},T_{i})\rightarrow(a_{i}X_{i(1)}+b_{i},a_{i}T_{i})~~\mbox{ and }~~(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i})\rightarrow(a_{i}\mu_{i}+b_{i},a_{i}\sigma_{i}).

Using standard arguments, the form of the affine equivariant estimator of
\mu_{i}
is obtained as

\displaystyle\delta_{{c_{i}}}(\underline{X},\underline{T})=X_{i(1)}+c_{i}T_{i},

(2.3)

where
c_{i}
is an arbitrary constant. The risk of the estimators of
the form (
2.3
) under the loss function (
2.1
) is given by

\displaystyle R(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i},\delta_{{c_{i}}})=E\left[\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+c_{i}T_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right\}-p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+c_{i}T_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)-1\right].

(2.4)

Differentiating (
2.4
) with respect to
c_{i}
, and then equating to zero we
get the minimizing choice as

\displaystyle c_{0i}=\frac{1}{p}\Big[1-\left(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n_{i}}}\Big],

(2.5)

provided
n_{i}>p.
Thus, we have the following theorem, which provides the BAEE of
\mu_{i}
.

Theorem 2.1

Under the Linex loss function (
2.1
), the best affine equivariant
estimator of
\mu_{i}
is

{\delta_{c_{0i}}}(\underline{X},\underline{T})
, where

c_{0i}=\frac{1}{p}\Big[1-\left(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n_{i}}}\Big],~i=1,\dots,k
.

2.1
Improving upon the best affine equivariant estimator

In this section, we prove a general inadmissibility result for finding improved estimator of
\mu_{i}
. For this purpose, we consider a sub group of the affine group as
\mathcal{G}_{1}=\{g_{a,b_{i}}:g_{a,b_{i}}(x_{ij})=ax_{ij}+b_{i},a>0,b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Under this transformation, the form of the
\mathcal{G}_{1}
-equivariant estimators is obtained as

\displaystyle\delta_{\phi_{i}}(\underline{X},\underline{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{i(1)}+T_{i}\phi_{i}\left(\frac{T_{1}}{T_{i}},\ldots,\frac{T_{i-1}}{T_{i}},\frac{T_{i+1}}{T_{i}},\ldots,\frac{T_{k}}{T_{i}}\right)

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{i(1)}+W_{i}\phi_{i}(\underline{W}),

where
\underline{W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{i-1},W_{i+1},\ldots,W_{k}),

W_{j}=T_{j}/T_{i},~j\neq i,~j=1,\ldots,k
and
W_{i}=T_{i}.

Further, the risk function of the estimator (
2.6
) is

\displaystyle R(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i},{\delta_{\phi}}_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[\left(\exp\left\{{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+W_{i}\phi_{i}(\underline{W})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)}\right\}-{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+W_{i}\phi_{i}(\underline{W})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)}-1\right)\right]

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E^{\underline{W}}R_{1}(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i},{\delta}_{\phi_{i}}),

where
R_{1}(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i},{\delta_{\phi}}_{i})

represents the conditional risk of
{\delta_{\phi}}_{i}
, given

\underline{W}=\underline{w}
. Now, we have

\displaystyle R_{1}(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i},{\delta_{\phi}}_{i})=E\left[\left(\exp\left\{{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+W_{i}\phi_{i}(\underline{W})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)}\right\}-{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+W_{i}\phi_{i}(\underline{W})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)}-1\right)|\underline{W}=\underline{w}\right].

(2.8)

Further,
R_{1}
is a convex function in
\phi_{i}
. Thus, the choice
of
\phi_{i}
which minimizes
R_{1}
can be obtained from

\displaystyle E\left[W_{i}\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+W_{i}\phi_{i}(\underline{W})}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right\}|\underline{W}=\underline{w}\right]=\exp\left\{\displaystyle\frac{p\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right\}E\left(W_{i}|\underline{W}=\underline{w}\right).

(2.9)

From
Vijayasree et al. 1995
,
W_{i}|\underline{W}=\underline{w}
follows
Gamma\left(n,\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}+\sum\limits_{j(\neq i)=1}^{k}\frac{w_{j}}{\sigma_{j}}\right)^{-1}\right)
, where
n=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}
. After some simplification, from Equation (
2.9
), we get

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(\underline{w},\underline{\sigma})=\frac{1}{p}\left(1+\sum_{j(\neq i)=1}^{k}w_{j}\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{j}}\right)\times\left(1-\left(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\right).

(2.10)

Now we apply
Brewster and Zidek 1974
technique to find an improved estimator. For this we need to derive the infimum
and supremum of
\phi_{i}(\underline{w},\underline{\sigma})
with respect to unknown parameters. Now for
p\neq 0,
we define

\displaystyle\phi_{{i}_{*}}(\underline{w})=\inf_{\sigma_{1}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{k}}\phi_{i}(\underline{w},\underline{\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\left(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\right)\left(1+\sum\limits_{j=2}^{k}w_{j}\right)},&\textrm{if $i=1$}\\
{-\infty},&\textrm{if $i=2,\dots,k$}\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle{\phi_{i}}^{*}(\underline{w})=\sup_{\sigma_{1}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{k}}\phi_{i}(\underline{w},\underline{\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\left(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\right)},&\textrm{if $i=1$}\\
{{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\left(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\right)}\left(1+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}w_{j}\right)},&\textrm{if $i=2,\dots,k.$}\end{array}\right.

We further define the following function

\displaystyle\phi_{0i}(\underline{w})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\phi_{i}}_{*}(\underline{w}),&\textrm{if $\phi_{i}(\underline{w})\leq{\phi_{i}}_{*}(\underline{w})$}\\
{\phi_{i}}(\underline{w}),&\textrm{if
${\phi_{i}}_{*}(\underline{w})<\phi_{i}(\underline{w})<{\phi_{i}}^{*}(\underline{w})$}\\
{\phi_{i}}^{*}(\underline{w}),&\textrm{if
$\phi_{i}(\underline{w})\geq{\phi_{i}}^{*}(\underline{w})$}.\end{array}\right.

In the following theorem, we give sufficient condition to find an estimator that dominates the estimator
\delta_{\phi_{i}}(\underline{X},\underline{T})
given in (
2.6
).

Theorem 2.2

The estimator
\delta_{\phi_{0i}(\underline{W})}
has uniformly smaller risk than that of
\delta_{\phi_{i}}
under the Linex loss function in (
2.1
) provided the condition
P_{\underline{\theta}}(\phi_{i0}(\underline{W})\neq\phi_{i}(\underline{W}))>0
holds.

The following corollary provides an estimator which improves upon the BAEE as an immediate application of Theorem
2.2
.

Corollary 2.3

Under the order restriction
\sigma_{1}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{k},

(i)

for estimating
\mu_{1}
, the estimator

\displaystyle\delta_{\phi_{01}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}X_{1(1)}+l_{1}T_{1,}&\textrm{if $c_{01}\leq l_{1}$}\\
X_{1(1)}+c_{01}T_{1},&\textrm{if
$l_{1}<c_{01}<u_{1}$}\\
X_{1(1)}+u_{1}T_{1},&\textrm{if
$c_{01}\geq u_{1}$}\end{array}\right.

has nowhere larger risk than the BAEE under the Linex loss function (
2.1
), where

u_{1}={\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\left(\frac{n_{1}}{n_{1}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\right)}~\mbox{ and }~l_{1}=\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\left(\frac{n_{1}}{n_{1}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\right)\left(1+\sum_{j=2}^{k}W_{j}\right).

(ii)

for estimating
\mu_{i},i=2,\dots,k
the estimator

\displaystyle\delta_{\phi_{0i}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{X_{i(1)}+u_{i}T_{i}},&\textrm{if $c_{0i}\geq{u_{i}}$}\\
X_{i(1)}+c_{0i}T_{i},&\textrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

has nowhere larger risk than the BAEE under the Linex loss function in (
2.1
), where

u_{i}={{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\left(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\right)}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}W_{j}\right)}.

A simulation study

n_{2}

\delta

3
Estimation of ordered location parameters

In this section, we study the estimation of
\mu_{i},~i=1,\dots,k
with the
order restriction
\mu_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{k}
. We consider three different cases, such as

(i)
all
\sigma_{i}
’s are known,
(ii)
all
\sigma_{i}
’s are unknown but equal,
and
(iii)
all
\sigma_{i}
’s are unknown and not necessarily equal.

3.1
Improved estimation of
\mu_{i}
when
\sigma_{i}
’s are known

Here, we consider the problem of finding improved estimators of
\mu_{i},~i=1,\dots,k
with respect to the loss function

\displaystyle L(\mu_{i},\delta_{i})=\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{\delta_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right\}-p\left(\frac{\delta_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)-1,~p\neq 0,

(3.1)

when
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k}
are known. In this case,
\underline{X}
is a complete and sufficient statistic, and
X_{1(1)},\dots,X_{k(1)}
are independently distributed. Consider the location group of transformations as
\mathcal{H}_{a_{i}}=\{g_{a_{i}}:g_{a_{i}}(x_{ij})=x_{ij}+a_{i},~j=1,\ldots,n_{i},i=1,\ldots,k\}
. Under this location group of transformations, the form of a location equivariant estimator is obtained as

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha_{i}}(\underline{X})=X_{i(1)}+\alpha_{i},

(3.2)

where
\alpha_{i}
is a real constant. The following theorem gives the unrestricted
best location equivariant estimator of
\mu_{i}.

Theorem 3.1

Under the Linex loss function in
(\ref{linloos2}),
the unrestricted best location equivariant estimator of
\mu_{i}
is
\delta_{\alpha_{0i}}(\underline{X})
, where
\alpha_{0i}=\frac{1}{p}\ln\left(\frac{n_{i}-p\sigma_{i}}{n_{i}}\right)
, with
p<n_{i}/\sigma_{i}.

Proof:
The proof is straightforward, and hence it is omitted.

Next, we aim to find an estimator that dominates
\delta_{\alpha_{0i}}(\underline{X})
for estimating
\mu_{i}
. For this purpose, consider the estimators of the form

\displaystyle\delta_{\psi_{i}}(\underline{X})=X_{i(1)}+\psi_{i}(\underline{Y}),

(3.3)

where

\underline{Y}=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{i-1},Y_{i+1},\ldots,Y_{k}),~Y_{i}=X_{i(1)}

and
Y_{j}=X_{j(1)}-X_{i(1)},~j\neq i,~j=1,\ldots,k
(see
Vijayasree et al. 1995
). The risk function of
the estimators
\delta_{\psi_{i}}(\underline{X})
is

\displaystyle R(\mu_{i},\delta_{\psi_{i}}(\underline{X}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left\{\displaystyle p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\psi_{i}(\underline{Y})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right\}-p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\psi_{i}(\underline{Y})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E^{\underline{Y}}R_{1}(\mu_{i},\delta_{\psi_{i}}(\underline{X})),

where

R_{1}(\mu_{i},\delta_{\psi_{i}}(\underline{X}))=E\Big[\left(\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\psi_{i}(\underline{Y})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right\}-p\left(\displaystyle\frac{X_{i(1)}+\psi_{i}(\underline{Y})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)-1\right)|\underline{Y}=\underline{y}\Big].

After some calculations, the conditional risk
R_{1}
is minimized at

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(\underline{y},\underline{\mu})=\frac{\sigma_{i}}{p}\ln\left[\frac{\exp\{\frac{p}{\sigma_{i}}\mu_{i}\}}{E\left(\exp\left\{\frac{pX_{i(1)}}{\sigma_{i}}\right\}|\underline{Y}=\underline{y}\right)}\right].

(3.4)

The conditional distribution of
X_{i(1)}
given

\underline{Y}=\underline{y}
is exponential with
location parameter
\mu_{0}
and scale
parameter
q^{-1},
where

\mu_{0}=\max\left\{\mu_{i},\max_{1\leq j\leq k,j\neq i}(\mu_{j}-y_{j})\right\}~~\mbox{and }~~q=\sum_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}\sigma_{j}^{-1}.

Simplifying
(\ref{eq3.4}),
we get

.

the

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{k}}{p}\ln\left(\frac{q\sigma_{k}-p}{q\sigma_{k}}\right)+\min(0,y_{1},\ldots,y_{k-1}),&\textrm{if $i=k$}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle{\psi}_{i*}(\underline{y}),~\mbox{(say)}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{i}}{p}\ln\left(\frac{q\sigma_{i}-p}{q\sigma_{i}}\right)+\min(0,y_{i+1},\ldots,y_{k}),&\textrm{if $i=1,\ldots,k-1$}\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\psi}_{i}^{*}(\underline{y}),~\mbox{(say).}

Deefine

\displaystyle\psi_{0i}(\underline{y})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\psi_{i}}_{*}(\underline{y}),&\textrm{if $\psi_{i}(\underline{y})\leq{\psi_{i}}_{*}(\underline{y})$}\\

3.3
Improved estimation of
\mu_{i}
when
\sigma_{i}
’s are equal but unknown

In this section, we consider the improved estimation of
\mu_{i}
when
\sigma_{1}=\dots=\sigma_{k}=\sigma
and
\sigma
is unknown. The estimation problem will be studied with respect to the loss function

\displaystyle L(\mu_{i},\sigma,\delta_{i})=\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{\delta_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma}\right)\right\}-p\left(\frac{\delta_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma}\right)-1,~p\neq 0.

(3.19)

The joint complete and sufficient statistic for this estimation problem is
(X_{i(1)},\dots,X_{k(1)},T)
, where
T=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}
. Also
X_{i(1)},\dots,X_{k(1)},T
are independently distributed. Further,
T\sim Gamma(n-k,\sigma)
.
Consider a group of transformations as
G_{a,b_{i}}=\{g_{a,b_{i}}(x)=ax_{ij}+b_{i},j=1,\dots,n_{i},i=1,\dots,k\}.
After some simplification, we get the form of the
G_{a,b_{i}}
-equivariant estimators of
\mu_{i}
as

\delta_{\beta_{i}}(X_{i(1)},T)=X_{i(1)}+\beta_{i}T,

where
\beta_{i}
is a real constant. The risk of the estimator
\delta_{\beta_{i}}(X_{i(1)},T)
under the loss function (
3.19
) is

R(\mu_{i},\sigma,\delta_{\beta_{i}})=E\left[\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\beta_{i}T-\mu_{i}}{\sigma}\right)\right\}-p\left({\frac{X_{i(1)}+\beta_{i}T-\mu_{i}}{\sigma}}\right)-1\right].

(3.20)

To minimize the risk, differentiating
R(\mu_{i},\sigma,\delta_{\beta_{i}})
with respect to
\beta_{i},
and then setting it to zero, we obtain the optimal choice of
\beta_{i}
as

\beta_{0i}=\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\left(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k+1}}\right),

provided
n_{i}>p.
Hence, we have following theorem.

Theorem 3.5

Under the Linex loss function in (
3.19
), the best affine equivariant estimator of
\mu_{i}
is
\delta_{\beta_{0i}}(X_{i(1)},T)
, where
\beta_{0i}=\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\left(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k+1}}\right).

Now, our aim is to find an estimator that dominates
\delta_{\beta_{0i}}
. For this purpose, consider a class of estimators as

\delta_{\varphi_{i}}(\underline{X},T)=X_{i(1)}+\varphi_{i}(\underline{Y},T).

The risk of this estimator can be written as

\displaystyle R(\delta_{\varphi_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\varphi_{i}(\underline{Y},T)-\mu_{i}}{\sigma}\right)\right\}-p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\varphi_{i}(\underline{Y},T)-\mu_{i}}{\sigma}\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E^{\underline{Y},T}R^{*}(\delta_{\varphi_{i}}),

where

R^{*}(\delta_{\varphi_{i}})=E\left[\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\varphi_{i}(\underline{Y},T)-\mu_{i}}{\sigma}\right)\right\}-p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\varphi_{i}(\underline{Y},T)-\mu_{i}}{\sigma}\right)-1\big|T=t,\underline{Y}=\underline{y}\right].

After differentiating with respect to
\varphi_{i}(\underline{y},t)
and equating it to zero we obtain

\varphi_{i}(\underline{y},t)=\mu_{i}-\mu_{0}-\frac{\sigma}{p}\ln\left(\frac{n}{n-p}\right),

(3.21)

provided
n>p
and
\mu_{0}=\max\left\{\mu_{i},\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq k,i\neq j}(\mu_{j}-y_{j})\right\}
. The supremum and infimum of (
3.21
) under the restriction
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{k}
are obtained as

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}^{*}(\underline{y})=\sup\limits_{\mu_{\leq}\dots\leq\mu_{k}}\ {\varphi_{i}(\underline{y},t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\min(0,y_{i+1},\ldots,y_{k}),&\textrm{if $i=1,\ldots,k-1$}\\
\displaystyle 0&\textrm{if $i=k$}\end{array}\right.

and

\inf\limits_{\mu_{\leq}\dots\leq\mu_{k}}\ {\varphi_{i}(\underline{y},t)}=-\infty.

Now, define

\displaystyle{\varphi_{0i}(\underline{y},t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(\underline{y},t),&\textrm{if $\varphi_{i}(\underline{y},t)\geq\varphi^{*}(\underline{y},t)$ }\\
\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(\underline{y},t)&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Theorem 3.6

Suppose
\sigma_{1}=\dots=\sigma_{k}=\sigma
and
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k}
satisfy the order restriction
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{k}
. Then, for estimating
\mu_{i}
, the risk of the estimator
X_{i(1)}+\min\left\{\varphi_{i}^{*}(\underline{Y},T),\varphi_{i}(\underline{Y},T)\right\}
is nowhere larger than
X_{i(1)}+\varphi_{i}(\underline{Y},T)
under the Linex loss function provided
P\left(\varphi_{i}^{*}(\underline{Y},T)<\varphi_{i}(\underline{Y},T)\right)\neq 0
.

As an application to the above theorem, we derive the following corollary that provides an estimator which dominates the BAEE.

Corollary 3.7

Let
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{k}
and
\sigma_{i}=\sigma
for
i=1,\dots,k
. For estimating
\mu_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,k
the estimator

\displaystyle{\delta_{\varphi_{0i}}(\underline{Y},T)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle X_{i(1)}+\min\left\{\beta_{0i}T,\min(0,y_{i+1},\ldots,y_{k}\right\},&\textrm{for $i=1\dots k-1$ }\\
\displaystyle X_{k(1)}+\min\{\beta_{0k}T,0\},&\textrm{for $i=k$}\end{array}\right.

dominates the best location equivariant estimator
\delta_{\beta_{0i}}
under the Linex loss function.

Remark 3.1

For estimating
\mu_{2}
, it is observed that
\delta_{\varphi_{0i}}
improves upon the estimator
\delta_{2RML}
.

3.5
Improved estimation
\mu_{i}
when all
\sigma_{i}
’s are unknown and unequal

In this section, we study the estimation of
\mu_{i}
under the order restriction
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{k}
when
\sigma_{i}
’s are unknown and unequal with respect to the loss function (
2.1
). The joint complete and sufficient statistic for this estimation problem is
(X_{1(1)},\dots,X_{k(1)},T_{1},T_{2}\dots,T_{k})
.
Consider an affine group of transformations as

G_{a_{i},b_{i}}=\{g_{a_{i},b_{i}}(x)=ax_{ij}+b_{i},j=1,\dots,n_{i}\},

where

i=1,\dots,k
. The form of affine equivariant estimators is obtained as

\delta_{\kappa_{i}}(X_{i(1)},T_{i})=X_{i(1)}+\kappa_{i}T_{i}.

The risk of estimator under Linex loss function given in (
2.1
) is

R(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i},\delta_{\kappa_{i}})=E\left[\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\kappa_{i}T_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right\}-p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\kappa_{i}T_{i}-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)-1\right].

(3.31)

After differentiating (
3.31
) with respect to
\kappa_{i}
, and then equating to zero, the value of
\kappa_{i}
is obtained as

\kappa_{i}=\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\left({\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n_{i}}}\right),
provided
n_{i}>p
.
Thus, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.8

Under the Linex loss function (
2.1
) the best affine equivariant estimator of
\mu_{i}
is
\delta_{\kappa_{0i}}(X_{i(1)},T_{i})
, where
\kappa_{0i}
is

\kappa_{0i}=\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\left({\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}-p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n_{i}}}\right)
,
n_{i}>p.

Now our goal is to derive an estimator which improves upon
\delta_{\kappa_{0i}}
with respect to the Linex loss function. For this purpose, we consider a class of estimators as

\delta_{\zeta_{i}}=X_{i(1)}+\zeta_{i}(\underline{Y},W_{i}).

The risk function of
\delta_{\zeta_{i}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle R(\underline{\mu},\sigma_{i},\delta_{\zeta_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\zeta_{i}(\underline{Y},W_{i})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right\}-p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\zeta_{i}(\underline{Y},W_{i})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ER^{*}(\underline{\mu},\delta_{\zeta_{i}}),

where
\underline{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k})
and

R^{*}(\underline{\mu},\sigma_{i},\delta_{\zeta_{i}})=E\left[\exp\left\{p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\zeta_{i}(\underline{Y},W_{i})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\right\}-p\left(\frac{X_{i(1)}+\zeta_{i}(\underline{Y},W_{i})-\mu_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)-1\big|\underline{Y},W_{i}=w_{i}\right].

It can be easily noticed that the conditional risk
R^{*}(\underline{\mu},\sigma_{i},\delta_{\zeta_{i}})
is minimized at

\zeta_{i}(\underline{y},w_{i})=\mu_{i}-\mu_{0}-\frac{\sigma_{i}}{p}\ln\left(\frac{n}{n-p}\right),

(3.32)

where
\mu_{0}=\max\left\{\mu_{i},{\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq k,i\neq j}(\mu_{j}-y_{j})}\right\}
. Under the order restriction
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{k}
, the supremum and infimum for
\zeta_{i}(\underline{y},w_{i})
can be obtained as

\displaystyle{\zeta_{i}}^{*}(\underline{y})=\sup\ {\zeta_{i}(\underline{y},w_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\min(0,y_{i+1},\ldots,y_{k}),&\textrm{if $i=1,\ldots,k-1$}\\
\displaystyle 0&\textrm{if $i=k$}\end{array}\right.

and

\inf\ {\zeta_{i}(\underline{Y},W_{i})}=-\infty.

Define

\displaystyle{\zeta_{i}}^{*}(\underline{y}),=\ {\zeta_{0i}(\underline{y},w_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\zeta_{i}^{*}(\underline{y},w_{i}),&\textrm{if $\zeta_{i}(\underline{y},w_{i})\geq\zeta_{i}^{*}(\underline{y},w_{i})$}\\
\displaystyle\zeta_{i}(\underline{y},w_{i})&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Theorem 3.9

Suppose
\sigma_{i},i,=1,\dots,k
are unknown and
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k}
are associated to he order restriction
\mu_{1}\leq\dots<\mu_{k}.
Then the risk of the estimator
X_{i(1)}+\min\left\{{{\zeta}^{*}(\underline{Y},W_{i}),{\zeta}(\underline{Y},W_{i})}\right\}
is nowhere larger than
X_{i(1)}+{{\zeta}(\underline{Y},W_{i})}
under the Linex loss (
2.1
), provided

P_{\underline{\mu}}(\zeta_{i0}(\underline{y},w_{i})\neq\zeta_{i}(\underline{Y},W_{i}))>0
.

An application of the preceding theorem leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 3.10

Consider the order restriction
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{k}
and all
\sigma_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,k
are unknown. To estimate
\mu_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,k
, the estimator

\displaystyle{\delta_{\zeta_{0i}}(\underline{Y},W_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle X_{i(1)}+\min\{\kappa_{0i}T_{i},\min(0,Y_{i+1},\dots,Y_{k})\},&\textrm{for $i=1,\dots,k-1$ }\\
\displaystyle X_{k(1)}+\min\{\kappa_{0k}T_{k},0\}&\textrm{for $i=k$}\end{array}\right.

dominates the estimator
\delta_{\kappa_{0i}}
under the Linex loss function (
2.1
).

4
Application to life testing sampling schemes

In the previous sections, we have derived the results of
\mu_{i},i=1,\dots k
by considering various scenarios under i.i.d. samples. In this section, we address the same estimation problem for three important life-testing schemes, such as
(i)
Type-II censoring
(ii)
progressive Type-II censoring, and (iii) record values.

4.1
Type-II censoring

In the survival analysis and reliability studies, it is not always possible to observe all test units until failure. Various situations may occur, such as units being withdrawn, lost, or deliberately removed from the study before the experiment concludes. Such incomplete observations lead to censored data. One of the most common censoring schemes is Type-II censoring. It occurs when the experimenter decides to terminate the experiment after a specific number of failures. For more details, one can refer to
Balakrishnan and Aggarwala 2000
. Suppose a random sample of size
n_{i}
is drawn from
Exp(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i})
distribution and observations are in order
X_{i(1)}\leq\dots\leq X_{i(n_{i})}
, for
i=1,\dots,k
. Under the Type-II censoring we consider only first
m_{i}
ordered observations such as
X_{i(1)},\dots,X_{i(m_{i})}
, where
m_{i}<n_{i}
. From
Epstein 1956
for
i=1,\dots,k
, we have
X_{i(1)}\sim E(\mu_{i},\frac{\sigma_{i}}{n_{i}})
and
T_{i}\sim\Gamma(m_{i}-1,\sigma_{i})
with
T_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}(X_{i(j)}-X_{i(1)})+(n_{i}-m_{i})(X_{i(n_{i})}-X_{i(1)})
.

(i)

For estimating
\mu_{i}
with
\sigma_{1}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{k}
we have
(X_{1(1)},\dots,X_{k(1)},T_{1},\dots T_{k})
is a joint complete sufficient statistic and it can be easily seen that this setup is similar to (
2.2
). As a consequence, improved estimators for
\mu_{i}
can be derived similar to Section
2
.

(ii)

Now for estimating
\mu_{i}
with the ordered restriction
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{k}
we have the following cases.

(a)
When
\sigma_{i}
are known
(X_{1(1)},\dots,X_{k(1)})
is complete and sufficient statistics, and this setup also aligns to Section
3.1
. Therefore, improved estimators can be derived similar to Section
3.1
.

(b)
When
\sigma_{i}
’s are equal but unknown we have joint sufficient statistic as
(X_{i(1)},\dots X_{k(1)},T)
with
T=\sum_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}
. This case is similar to Section to
3.3
. Thus, the results for
\mu_{i}
under Type-II censoring sample can be obtained using the theorems proved in Subsection
3.3
.
(c)
When
\sigma_{i}
’s are unequal and unknown the joint complete and sufficient statistic is
(X_{1(1)},\dots X_{k(1)},T_{1},\dots T_{k})
. Hence, the improved estimators for
\mu_{i}
will be obtained similar to Section
3.5
.

4.2
Progressive Type-II censoring

Suppose in life-testing studies,
n_{i}
independents units with lifetimes
X_{i1},\dots,X_{in_{i}}
are drawn from exponential
Exp(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i})
distribution. But only
m_{i}
,
(m_{i}\leq n_{i})
are observed continuously until they fail. Here, censoring takes place progressively in
m_{i}
stages. These stages provide failure times of
m_{i}
independent units. When the first failure occurs (at the first stage),
S_{i1}
out of the
n_{i}-1
remaining units are randomly removed from the experiment. Thereafter, at the second failure (at the second stage),
S_{i2}
out of
n_{i}-2-S_{i1}
remaining units are removed randomly. Eventually when the
m_{i}
failure occurs (at
m_{i}
stage) all remaining units
S_{im_{i}}=n_{i}-m_{i}-S_{i1}-S_{i2}-\cdots-S_{im_{i}-1}
are removed. In this procedure, we have observed the lifetimes of
m_{i}
experimental units and the lifetimes are denoted as
X_{i,1:n_{i}},\dots X_{i,m_{i}:n_{i}}
. These ordered failure times denote the progressive Type-II censored sample. For more information on this censoring scheme, see
Balakrishnan and Aggarwala 2000
and
Balakrishnan 2007
. Here, our aim is to estimate
\mu_{i}
based on the progressive Type-II censored samples. From
Patra et al. 2025
, it can be seen that
X_{i,1:n_{i}}\sim E(\mu_{i},\frac{\sigma_{i}}{n_{i}})
and
T_{i}\sim\Gamma(m_{i}-1,\sigma_{i})
, where
T_{i}=\sum_{j=i}^{m_{i}}(S_{ij}+1)(X_{i,j:n_{i}}-X_{i,1:n_{i}})
.

(i)

We consider the problem of estimating
\mu_{i}
under ordered scale parameter that is
\sigma_{1}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{k}
. Here,
(X_{1,1:n_{1}},\dots X_{k,1:n_{k}},T_{1},\dots,T_{k})
is a joint complete sufficient statistic. Therefore, the present setup reduces to model (
2.2
). Hence, improved estimators for
\mu_{i}
can be derived similar to that in Section
2
.

(ii)

Now, we consider estimation of
\mu_{i}
under order restriction
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{k}
.
(a)
When
\sigma_{i}
’s are known,
(X_{1,1:n_{1}},\dots,X_{k,1:n_{k}})
is a joint complete and sufficient statistic. This setup aligns with Section
3.1
, and consequently improvement results can be obtained similarly.
(b)
For the case when
\sigma_{i}
’s are equal but unknown, we have joint sufficient statistic as
(X_{1,:n_{1}},\dots,X_{k,:n_{k}},T)
, which is analogous to Section
3.3
. Therefore, improvement results for
\mu_{i}
under progressive Type-II censoring sample can be proved using the theory derived in Section
3.3
.
(c)
When
\sigma_{i}
’s are unequal and unknown, the joint complete and sufficient statistic is
(X_{1,1:n_{1}},\dots X_{k,1:n_{k}},T_{1},\dots,T_{k})
. Thus, the methodologies to obtain improved estimators for
\mu_{i}
when
\sigma_{i}
’s are unknown under progressive Type-II scheme are similar to Section
3.5
.

4.3
Record values

Record values have wide-range of applications in meteorology, hydrology, and financial market analysis. For generating record values, consider a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
. We define a new sequence of random variables recursively as

Z(1)=1,\quad\text{and for }k\geq 2,\quad Z(k)=\min\{j\mid j>Z(k-1),\,Y_{j}>Y_{Z(k-1)}\}.

The resulting sequence
\{U_{k}=Y_{Z(k)},\,k\geq 1\}
is known as the sequence of maximal record statistics. For further discussion, please refer to
Madi 2008
and
Bobotas and Kourouklis 2011
.
Consider the record sample
X_{i1}^{r},\dots,X_{in_{i}}^{r}
from exponential
Exp(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i})
distribution,
i=1,\dots,k
. From
Bajpai and Patra 2025
it has been noted that
T_{i}=\left(X_{i,n_{i}}^{r}-X_{i,(1)}^{r}\right)\sim\Gamma(n_{i}-1,\sigma_{i}),
and
X_{i(1)}^{r}\sim Exp(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i})
. Here, we are interested in estimating
\mu_{i}
under record values for various sceneries as follows.

(i)

We consider the estimation of
\mu_{i}
where
\sigma_{1}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{k}
. In this case,
(X_{i1}^{r},\cdots,X_{in_{i}}^{r},T_{1},\dots T_{k})
is a joint complete sufficient statistic. Thus, clearly, this model is similar to (
2.2
). So improvement results can be obtained similar to Section
2
.

(ii)

Now we address the estimation of
\mu_{i}
based on the record values when
\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{k}
. Here, we have the following cases.
(a)
When
\sigma_{i}
’s are known, we have
(X_{i1}^{r},\cdots,X_{in_{i}}^{r})
is a complete and sufficient statistic. This model is similar to the model discussed in Section
3.1
, and consequently we can derive the results for finding the improved estimators for record values using the methodology presented in Section
3.1
.
(b)
For the case
\sigma_{i}
’s are equal but unknown, we have sufficient statistic as
(X_{i1}^{r},\cdots,X_{in_{i}}^{r},T)
with
T=\sum_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}
. This setup is similar to the setup given in Section
3.3
. So, the results for finding improved estimators of
\mu_{i}
can be obtained accordingly as derived in Section
3.3
.

(c)
When
\sigma_{i}
’s are unequal and unknown, the complete and sufficient statistics for this case are
(X_{i1}^{r},\cdots,X_{in_{i}}^{r},T_{1},\dots T_{k})
. This setup is similar to that studied in Subsection
3.5
. Therefore, the improved estimators fo
\mu_{i}
will be derived using the theories given in Section
3.5
.

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have considered component-wise estimation of the location parameter of
k(\geq 2)
exponential distributions under the Linex loss function. We have considered the following scenarios: (i) Estimation of location parameters when scale parameters are ordered, (ii) Estimation of ordered location parameters with (a) known scale parameter, (b) equal and unknown scale parameter, and (c) unknown and unequal scale parameters. For each case, general inadmissibility results have been proved. Using the inadmissibility result for each case, we have obtained estimator that dominates the usual estimator. Additionally, we have obtained the estimators that dominate MLE and restricted MLE from these results. A detailed numerical comparison of the risk performances of the proposed estimators has been carried out for each case. We have observed that the proposed estimators perform better than the usual estimators. Insights gained from the simulation study have been discussed in detail. Furthermore, it has been noticed that as the value of
p
increases, the proposed estimators show significant improvement. We have also discussed that using the obtained results one can derive the results for Type-II censoring scheme, progressive Type-II censoring scheme and record values. So, This work provides a unified treatment for different important sampling schemes.

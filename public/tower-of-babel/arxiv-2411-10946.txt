Fully nonlinear parabolic equations of real forms on Hermitian manifolds
Mathew George, Bo Guan

Abstract
. Over many decades fully nonlinear PDEs, and the complex Monge-Ampère equation in particular played a central role in the study of complex manifolds. Most previous works focused on problems that can be expressed through equations involving real ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) forms. As many important questions, especially those linked to higher cohomology classes in complex geometry involve real ( p , p ) (p,p) forms for p > 1 p>1 , there is a strong need to develop PDE techniques to study them. In this paper we consider a fully nonlinear equation for ( p , p ) (p,p) forms on compact Hermitian manifolds. We establish the existence of classical solutions for a large class of these equations by a parabolic approach, proving the long-time existence and convergence of solutions to the elliptic case. Mathematical Subject Classification (MSC2020): 35K10, 35K55, 53C55, 58J35.



1.
Introduction

In the landmark work of Chern
[
5
]
certain differential forms constructed from Hermitian metrics seemed
to effectively describe geometric and topological information associated with complex manifolds, which are now called Chern classes. Studies of these characteristic classes have been undertaken from different points of view in geometry and topology. It would be desirable to also have strong analytical tools to study them. Motivated by this, we study a class of nonlinear PDEs involving real
(p,p)
forms on Hermitian manifolds.

The first Chern class for a compact Kähler manifold consists of closed real
(1,1)
forms and is
represented by the Ricci form of the metric. The Calabi-Yau theorem

[
42
]
asserts that any
(1,1)
form in the first Chern class of a compact Kähler manifold
(M^{n},\omega)
corresponds to the Ricci form of some Kähler metric on
M

in the cohomology class of
\omega
. Initially conjectured by Calabi, this was proved by Yau
[
42
]
by solving the complex Monge-Ampère equation on compact Kähler manifolds.
Since the works of Yau
[
42
]
and Aubin
[
1
]
,
the complex Monge-Ampère equation has been a powerful tool in the study of complex geometry, and
more recently, there have been growing interests in fully nonlinear PDEs on complex manifolds, such as the J-equation and deformed Hermitian Yang-Mills (dHYM) equation.
However, most investigations have been focused on equations or systems involving real

(1,1)
forms.
Because of their important role,
especially in the study of higher cohomology classes, it is natural to extend the nonlinear PDE theory to equations of higher order forms, more precisely real
(p,p)
forms for
p>1
. This is our primary goal in the current paper,
and we shall take a parabolic approach.

Nonlinear parabolic methods have become increasingly prevalent in complex geometry.
In 1985, Cao
[
4
]
first studied Kähler-Ricci flow and using which gave an alternative proof of Calabi-Yau theorem. Donaldson
[
7
]
used nonlinear parabolic equations to establish the Kobayashi-Hitchin correspondence between stable vector bundles on projective algebraic surfaces and Hermitian-Einstein connections.
Later on Uhlenbeck and Yau
[
39
]
extended the results to compact Kähler manifolds.
In 1999, Donaldson
[
8
]
proposed the study of J-flow on Kähler manifolds, which seemed the first
fully nonlinear PDE other than the complex Monge-Ampère equation that appeared in complex geometry. Song and Weinkove
[
30
]
solved J-flow by finding a key necessary and sufficient cone condition for the solvability
(see also
[
40
,
41
]
); their results were generalized by
Fang-Lai-Ma
[
9
]

to Hessian quotient equations.
On Hermitian manifolds, Gill
[
17
]
first considered the parallel of Kähler-Ricci flow, followed by
Sun
[
34
]
who treated complex Hessian quotient parabolic equations,
extending results in
[
9
]
and
[
17
]
,
while Streets and Tian
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
studied curvature flows determined by the second Chern-Ricci form. More recently, Phong-Picard-Zhang
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
introduced new geometric flows,
while Phong and Tô
[
29
]
treated fully nonlinear parabolic equations of the form

(1.1)

\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}=f(\lambda(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi))-\psi

on compact Hermitian manifolds
in a general framework established by Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
3
]
for elliptic equations.

In this paper we consider a new class of fully nonlinear parabolic equations which have significant differences from equation (
1.1
).
The geometric considerations while setting up these equations are twofold. Firstly, many natural invariants of a complex manifold are expressed in terms of real
(p,p)
forms for
p>1
, and secondly in most cases these forms are defined by certain symmetric relations. This leads us to consider the following class of equations

Let
(M^{n},\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
,
and
\Omega
a real
(p,p)
form on
M
where
p
is an integer,
2\leq p\leq n-1
.
As we shall see in Section
2
, there is a natural way to define the eigenvalues of

(p,p)
forms with respect to the metric
\omega

For a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)

let

\Lambda(\Omega_{\phi})\equiv\Lambda(\Omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\wedge\omega^{p-1})=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{N}),

where

N=\frac{n!}{p!(n-p)!},

denote the eigenvalues of
\Omega_{\phi}=\Omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\wedge\omega^{p-1}
with respect to
\omega

be a symmetric function of
N

We shall consider the equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}=f(\Lambda(\Omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\wedge\omega^{p-1}))-\psi

where, in geometric applications,
\Omega
often depends on
\phi
and its gradient, with the initial condition

(1.3)

\displaystyle\phi(z,0)=\phi_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M).

Note that if
\omega

\Omega

is also closed and belongs to the Bott-Chern cohomology class of

So equation (

When

1.2

1.1

In spite of the common background they share in terms of the structure conditions described below,
for general

much more difficult to study, and seems to have rarely been treated before.
To the best of our knowledge, the first fully nonlinear equation involving real
(p,p)

introduced by Fu-Wang-Wu
[

]

[

The approach in these papers was based on a natural duality between
(n-1,n-1)

(1,1)
forms,
and the fact that a positive

(n-1,n-1)
form
\Omega

(n-1)

We shall follow
[
3

to assume that

(of higher dimension

(1.4)

\Gamma_{N}=\{\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\Lambda_{i}>0\}\subset\Gamma,

and satisfies the

(1.5)

f_{i}\equiv\frac{\partial f}{\partial\Lambda_{i}}\geq 0\text{ in $\Gamma$, $1\leq i\leq N$},

(1.6)

(1.7)

\sup\limits_{\partial\Gamma}f<\inf\limits_{M}\psi.

We call a function

admissible
if

\Lambda(\Omega_{\phi})\in\Gamma
in

so that equation (
1.2
) is degenerate parabolic for admissible solutions.

Proposition 1.1
.

Under condition (
1.5
), equation (
1.2

For general

Our main focus in this paper will be on equation (
1.2
) with

\Omega=X[\phi]\equiv X(z,\phi,\partial\phi,\bar{\partial}\phi)

being allowed to depend on
\phi
and its gradient.
To give some geometric motivations which lead to this more general equation,
we call a
(p,p)
form

\partial\bar{\partial}\Omega=0
, and a
(1,1)

is pluriclosed.
For

, pluriclosed metrics are also called

and were extensively studied; see e.g.
[
2

10
,
31
,

,
43
]
and references therein,
while
p
-pluriclosed
(1,1)
forms are known as Gauduchon
[
13
]
and
Astheno-Kähler
[
23
]
for

Recall that the Aeppli cohomology group is defined as

H^{p,p}_{A}(M,\hbox{\bbbld R})=\frac{\{\mbox{pluriclosed real $(p,p)$ forms}\}}{\{\partial\alpha+\bar{\partial}\bar{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Lambda^{p-1,p}(M,\hbox{\bbbld R})\}}.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth real
(p,p)
form and
\gamma\in\hbox{\bbbld R}
. Adopting an idea in
[
35
]
we define for
\phi\in C^{2}(M)

(1.9)

X[\phi]=\Omega+\frac{\gamma\sqrt{-1}}{2}\partial\phi\wedge\bar{\partial}\omega^{p-1}-\frac{\gamma\sqrt{-1}}{2}\bar{\partial}\phi\wedge\partial\omega^{p-1}

and

\Omega_{\phi}=X[\phi]+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\wedge\omega^{p-1}.

Then

(1.10)

\partial\bar{\partial}\Omega_{\phi}=\partial\bar{\partial}\Omega+(1-\gamma)\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\wedge\partial\bar{\partial}\omega^{p-1}.

When

is pluriclosed and

[\Omega_{\phi}-\Omega]=0

as

\Omega_{\phi}-\Omega=\partial\alpha+\bar{\partial}\bar{\alpha},\;\;\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\phi\wedge\omega^{p-1}.

In particular, if
\Omega

and

When

In equation (

.

.

Let

2.
The equation in local coordinates and ellipticity

For
1\leq p\leq n
, denote

\mathfrak{I}=\mathfrak{I}_{p}=\{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{p})\in\hbox{\bbbld N}^{p}:1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq n\}

and fix the order in
\mathfrak{I}
:

I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{p})<J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{p})

provided that
i_{l}<j_{l}
for the first non-equal pair. So

\mathfrak{I}=\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{N}:I_{i}<I_{j}\;\mbox{if $i<j$}\}

where

N=\frac{n!}{p!(n-p)!}.

In local coordinates
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
we write

\omega=\sqrt{-1}g_{i\bar{j}}dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j}

and, for
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{p})\in\mathfrak{I}
,

dz_{I}=dz_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{i_{p}},\;\;d\bar{z}_{I}=d\bar{z}_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\bar{z}_{i_{p}}.

It follows that

\omega^{p}=p!(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}\sum\omega_{I\bar{J}}dz_{I}\wedge d\bar{z}_{J}

where

\omega_{I\bar{J}}=\det(g_{i_{k}\bar{j}_{l}}|_{i_{k}\in I,j_{l}\in J}).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a
(p,p)
form given in local coordinates

\Omega=p!(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}\Omega_{I\bar{J}}dz_{I}\wedge d\bar{z}_{J}.

The
eigenvalues
of
\Omega
with respect to
\omega
are defined to be the
eigenvalues of the matrix
\{\Omega_{I\bar{J}}\}
with respect to
\{\omega_{I\bar{J}}\}
,
i.e. the roots of

\det(\Omega_{I\bar{J}}-\lambda\omega_{I\bar{J}})=0.

It is straightforward to verify that this definition is independent of local coordinates.
Moreover,
\Omega
is a real form if and only if
\{\Omega_{I\bar{J}}\}
is an
N\times N
Hermitian matrix (see e.g. Demailly
[
6
]
).
Consequently, the eigenvalues of a real
(p,p)
form are real numbers.

We now consider the equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}=f(\Lambda(X[\phi]+(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi+\chi)\wedge\omega^{p-1}))-\psi[\phi],

which is clearly an equivalent form of equation (
1.2
),
where
\chi
is a smooth real
(1,1)
form,

X[\phi]=X(z,\phi,\partial\phi,\bar{\partial}\phi)
which is a real

(p,p)
form for given
\phi
, while similarly

\psi[\phi]=\psi(z,\phi,\partial\phi,\bar{\partial}\phi)
;
both
X
and
\psi
are assumed to be smooth in their variables.

For convenience we shall write

\mathfrak{g}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi+\chi
and

Z[\phi]:=X[\phi]+\mathfrak{g}\wedge\omega^{p-1}.

Define the functions
F
,
G
by

F(Z)=G(\mathfrak{g},X)=f(\Lambda(Z)).

In local coordinates we write

\mathfrak{g}=\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{g}_{i\bar{j}}dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j},

X[\phi]=p!(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}X_{I\bar{J}}dz_{I}\wedge d\bar{z}_{J},

Z[\phi]=p!(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}Z_{I\bar{J}}dz_{I}\wedge d\bar{z}_{J},

and

F^{I\bar{J}}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z_{I\bar{J}}}(Z),\;\;G^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{\partial G}{\partial\mathfrak{g}_{i\bar{j}}}(\mathfrak{g}).

It is well known from
[
3
]
that the matrix
\{F^{I\bar{J}}\}
is positive semi-definite if

\Lambda(Z)\in\Gamma
under assumption (
1.5
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Under condition (
1.5
),
\{G^{i\bar{j}}\}
is positive semi-definite for
\Lambda(Z)\in\Gamma
. Consequently, equation (
2.1
) is degenerate parabolic for admissible solutions.

Proof.

At a point on
M
where we assume
g_{i\bar{j}}=\delta_{ij}
, by straightforward calculations,

(2.2)

Z_{I\bar{J}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{I\bar{I}}+\sum_{i\in I}\mathfrak{g}_{i\bar{i}},\;\;I=J,\\
&X_{I\bar{J}}+(-1)^{\alpha^{ij}_{IJ}}{\mathfrak{g}_{i\bar{j}}},\;\;|I\cap J|=p-1,\;i\in I\setminus J,\;j\in J\setminus I,\\
&X_{I\bar{J}},\;\;|I\cap J|\leq p-2\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha^{ij}_{IJ}=(i|I)+(j|J)
and
(i|I)
denotes the position of
i
in
I
so

(i_{k}|I)=k
for
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{p})
.
It follows that

(2.3)

G^{i\bar{j}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{I:i\in I}F^{I\bar{I}},\;\;i=j,\\
&\sum_{|I\cap J|=p-1;i\in I\setminus J,j\in J\setminus I}(-1)^{\alpha^{ij}_{IJ}}F^{I\bar{J}},\;i\neq j.\end{aligned}\right.

We need to rewrite it in a different expression.

,
write

A key observation is the following identity,

(2.4)

G^{i\bar{j}}=\sum_{I^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}:i,j\notin I^{\prime}}(-1)^{\alpha^{ij}_{I^{\prime}_{i}I^{\prime}_{j}}}F^{I^{\prime}_{i}\bar{I^{\prime}}_{j}}.

For each
I^{\prime}
the principal submatrix
\{F^{I^{\prime}_{i}\bar{I^{\prime}}_{j}}\}
is positive semidefinite as so is

\{F^{I\bar{J}}\}
. It follows that
\{G^{i\bar{j}}\}
is a Hermitian matrix, and for any
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\hbox{\bbbld C}^{n}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum G^{i\bar{j}}\xi_{i}\bar{\xi}_{j}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\sum_{I^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}:i,j\notin I^{\prime}}(-1)^{\alpha^{ij}_{I^{\prime}_{i}I^{\prime}_{j}}}F^{I^{\prime}_{i}\bar{I^{\prime}}_{j}}\xi_{i}\bar{\xi}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{I^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}}\sum_{i,j\notin I^{\prime}}F^{I^{\prime}_{i}\bar{I^{\prime}}_{j}}[(-1)^{(i|I^{\prime}_{i})}\xi_{i}][(-1)^{(j|I^{\prime}_{j})}\bar{\xi}_{j}]\geq 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

It should be noted that the submatrices of form

do not include all principal

Lemma 2.3
.

In local coordinates, at a point

Clearly we may assume

Lemma 2.4

By the maximum principle we immediately obtain the following estimates.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume

where
K_{0}\geq 0
is a constant. Then

(4.4)

H_{0}e^{K_{0}t}\leq\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\leq H_{1}e^{K_{0}t}\;\;\mbox{in $M\times[0,T)$}

(4.5)

\min_{M}\phi_{0}+\frac{H_{0}}{K_{0}}e^{K_{0}t}\leq\phi\leq\max_{M}\phi_{0}+\frac{H_{1}}{K_{0}}e^{K_{0}t}\;\;\mbox{in $M\times[0,T)$}

Proof.

By (

It follows from the maximum principle that

e^{-K_{0}t}\partial_{t}\phi\leq\max_{M\times\{t=0\}}e^{-K_{0}t}\partial_{t}\phi=H_{1}.

Therefore,

\partial_{t}\phi\leq H_{1}e^{K_{0}t}.

Similarly,

\mathcal{L}(e^{-K_{0}t}\partial_{t}\phi)=e^{-K_{0}t}(\mathcal{L}\partial_{t}\phi-K_{0}\partial_{t}\phi)\geq 0\;\;\mbox{on $M\cap\{\partial_{t}\phi\leq 0\}$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}(Q+(\alpha-1)h_{t})^{2}-C_{1}Q-C_{2}\alpha h_{t}-C

We derive from (
5.13
) that at
(x_{0},t_{0})
,

(5.15)

\displaystyle(\tilde{Q}+(\alpha-1)h_{t}t_{0})^{2}+C\alpha h_{t}t_{0}^{2}\leq C(1+t_{0}^{2}).

This immediately gives
\tilde{Q}\leq C(1+t_{0})
provided that
h_{t}\geq 0
.

Assume now
h_{t}<0
so
-h_{t}\leq Q+(\alpha-1)h_{t}
. From (
5.15
) we obtain

h_{t}^{2}+C\alpha h_{t}\leq C(1+t_{0}^{-2}).

Therefore,
-\alpha h_{t}\leq C+Ct_{0}^{-1}
. We derive

\tilde{Q}\leq C(1+t_{0})
from (
5.15
) again.

Finally, for any
x\in M
and
0<t<T
, taking
T^{\prime}=t
we obtain

(5.16)

\displaystyle T^{\prime}Q(x,T^{\prime})\leq\tilde{Q}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq C(1+t_{0})\leq C(1+T^{\prime}).

This proves (
5.3
).
∎

It is often convenient to use the following equivalent form of Lemma
5.1
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
u\in C^{3,2}(M\times[0,T))
be a positive solution of (
5.1
),
\alpha>1
.
There are constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

(5.17)

\displaystyle|\partial\log u|^{2}-\alpha\partial_{t}\log u\leq C_{1}+\frac{C_{2}}{t},\;\;0<t<T.

With the aid of Lemma
5.2
we may derive the following Harnack inequality
as in Li-Yau
[
24
]
. For completeness we include an outline of the proof.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
u\in C^{3,2}(M\times[0,T))
be a positive solution of (
5.1
),
\alpha>1
.
Then

(5.18)

\sup_{z\in M}u(z,t)\leq C(t,\tau;\alpha)\inf_{z\in M}u(z,\tau)

for
0<t<\tau<T
, where

(5.19)

C(t,\tau,\alpha)=\Big(\frac{\tau}{t}\Big)^{\beta}\exp\Big(\frac{C_{1}}{\tau-t}+C_{2}(\tau-t)\Big)

and
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
\beta
are positive constants depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
, and

|G^{i\bar{j}}|_{C^{3,1}(M\times[0,T))},\;|\chi^{i}|_{C^{1,1}(M\times[0,T))},\;|\chi^{0}|_{C^{1,1}(M\times[0,T))}

as well as the geometric quantities of
M
.

Proof.

For any two points
z,w\in M
, let
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
be a geodesic with

\gamma(0)=z
and
\gamma(1)=w
.
Define
\eta:[0,1]\to M\times[t,\tau]
by

\eta(s)=(\gamma(s),(1-s)\tau+st),\;0\leq s\leq L.

For
h=\log u
as before, by Schwarz inequality and Lemma
5.2
we have

(5.20)

\displaystyle\log\frac{u(w,t)}{u(z,\tau)}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}h(\eta(s))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\langle\dot{\gamma},\partial h\rangle_{g}-(\tau-t)h_{t})ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{\alpha|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}}{\tau-t}+\frac{\tau-t}{\alpha}(|\partial h|^{2}-\alpha h_{t})\Big)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}}{\tau-t}+\frac{\tau-t}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(C+\frac{C}{(1-s)\tau+st}\Big)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}}{\tau-t}+\frac{\tau-t}{C^{-1}\alpha}+\frac{C}{\alpha}\log\frac{\tau}{t}.

Note that
|\dot{\gamma}|
is dominated by the diameter of
M
. The proof is complete.
∎

We can now use a standard iteration argument to prove the convergence of the
normalized solution in Theorem
1.3
. Assume that
X
,
\chi
and
\psi
are independent of
\phi
(but may still depend on
\partial\phi,\bar{\partial}\phi
).
Let
\omega(t)
be the oscillation of
\phi_{t}

\omega(t):=\sup\limits_{z\in M}\phi_{t}(z,t)-\inf\limits_{z\in M}\phi_{t}(z,t)

and for
k=0,1,2,\ldots
,

\displaystyle v_{k}(z,t)=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{y\in M}\phi_{t}(y,k)-\phi_{t}(z,k+t),

\displaystyle w_{k}(z,t)=

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(z,k+t)-\inf\limits_{y\in M}\phi_{t}(y,k).

It is clear that both
v_{k}
and
w_{k}
satisfy the following equation

(5.21)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}(z,t)=G^{i\bar{j}}(x,k+t)\partial_{i}\partial_{\bar{j}}\varphi+\chi^{i}(x,k+t)\partial_{i}\varphi+\bar{\chi}^{i}(x,k+t)\partial_{\bar{i}}\varphi

Suppose
\phi_{t}(z,k)
is not constant so that
v_{k}
and
w_{k}
are positive somewhere in
M
at time
t=0
.
Note that

\chi^{0}=F^{I\bar{J}}X_{I\bar{J},\phi}+G^{i\bar{j}}\chi_{i\bar{j},\phi}-\psi_{\phi}\equiv 0.

By the maximum principle
v_{k}
and
w_{k}
are both positive on
M
for all
t>0
.
We may apply Theorem
5.3
to
v_{k}
and
w_{k}

with
t=\frac{1}{2}
and
\tau=1
to derive

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{z\in M}\phi_{t}(z,k)-\inf\limits_{z\in M}\phi_{t}\Big(z,k+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\sup\limits_{z\in M}\phi_{t}(z,k)-\sup\limits_{z\in M}\phi_{t}(z,k+1)\Big),

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{z\in M}\phi_{t}\Big(z,k+\frac{1}{2}\Big)-\inf\limits_{z\in M}\phi_{t}(z,k)\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\inf\limits_{z\in M}\phi_{t}(z,k+1)-\inf\limits_{z\in M}\phi_{t}(z,k)\Big)

where
C:=C(\frac{1}{2},1)
.
Adding the two inequalities yields

\omega(k)+\omega\Big(k+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\leq C(\omega(k)-\omega(k+1)).

It follows that

(5.22)

\omega(k+1)\leq\delta\omega(k),\;\;\delta<1.

This still holds by the by maximum principle if
\phi_{t}(z,k)
is constant.
Using the standard iteration argument we derive

\omega(t)\leq Ce^{-\beta t}
for
\beta=-\log{\delta}
.

Note that
\tilde{\phi}_{t}(y,t)=0
for some
y\in M

since
\int_{M}\tilde{\phi}_{t}\omega^{n}=0
. Therefore,

(5.23)

\displaystyle|\tilde{\phi}_{t}(z,t)|=|\tilde{\phi}_{t}(z,t)-\tilde{\phi}_{t}(y,t)|\leq\omega(t)\leq Ce^{-\beta t}

which implies

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\tilde{\phi}(z,t)-C\beta^{-1}e^{-\beta t})\leq 0.

Consequently, the limit

\varphi(z):=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\phi}(z,t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}(\tilde{\phi}(z,t)-C\beta^{-1}e^{-\beta t})

exists for all
z\in M
as
\tilde{\phi}(z,t)-C\beta^{-1}e^{-\beta t}
is uniformly bounded.
Finally, from the
a priori
estimates we see that
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
and is an admissible solution of the elliptic equation (
1.20
).
The proof of Theorem
1.3
is complete.

A Historic Giant Mass: Forequarter Amputation for Advanced Humeral Osteosarcoma in an Adult
Authors: Mouncef Amahtil, Hammouti Mohamed, Mohamed Sadougui, Walid Bouziane, Abdelkrim Daoudi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTOsteosarcoma in adults over 40 is uncommon, and outcomes are less favorable than in younger patients. Nevertheless, older patients should be offered treatment strategies comparable to those used in younger individuals. Aggressive multimodal treatment combining surgical excision and chemotherapy may achieve long‐term remission, even in advanced or infected presentations.

We present the case of a 41‐year‐old woman with a large, infected mass involving the proximal left upper limb. Six months prior to the current admission, she had been evaluated at another hospital, where a biopsy confirmed high‐grade osteoblastic osteosarcoma of the humerus. At that time, a forequarter amputation was proposed as a therapeutic option; however, the patient refused the procedure. She subsequently received nine cycles of chemotherapy with cisplatin and doxorubicin every 21 days, combined with radiotherapy with 66 Gy delivered. This regimen was administered at the referring center as an attempt at local disease control after the patient declined primary surgical management. During this period, the humeral mass progressed rapidly, increasing nearly threefold in size, with the development of skin necrosis, secondary infection, purulent discharge, and a foul odor (Figure 1).

Clinical photograph at presentation showing a giant tumoral mass of the left shoulder with extensive skin involvement, including areas of necrosis and purulent discharge, 6 months after initial diagnosis of high‐grade humeral osteosarcoma.

Upon admission to our institution, ALP and CRP were elevated. Plain radiography and CT scan demonstrated a large destructive osteolytic lesion involving the proximal humerus, with aggressive periosteal reaction and infiltration of the adjacent soft tissues (Figure 2). Staging investigations including chest computed tomography showed no evidence of pulmonary metastases or other distant disease. Because of the progressive local disease, active infection, disfiguring appearance, and failure of prior treatment, limb‐salvage surgery was not considered feasible, as complete oncologic resection with safe margins could not be achieved. After multidisciplinary tumor board discussion, forequarter amputation was indicated as the only curative option, and the patient consented to the procedure.

Plain radiograph of the left humerus demonstrating a large destructive osteolytic lesion extending from the mid‐diaphysis to the humeral head and glenoid, associated with an aggressive periosteal reaction, consistent with advanced osteosarcoma.

A circumferential surgical approach was performed at the root of the limb through macroscopically healthy tissue, ligation of the axillary vessels, resection of the distal two‐thirds of the clavicle, and complete excision of the scapula, resulting in an extended en bloc amputation of the left upper limb. Histopathology confirmed high‐grade osteoblastic osteosarcoma, with negative surgical margins (R0). Four weeks later, wound healing was satisfactory after skin grafting (Figure 3). Adjuvant chemotherapy was subsequently administered with six cycles of chemotherapy with cisplatin and doxorubicin. At 18 months, there was no evidence of local recurrence, disease relapse, or metastatic spread. Psychological support and rehabilitation were offered postoperatively, and the patient reported satisfactory adaptation at follow‐up.

Postoperative clinical view showing satisfactory wound healing after forequarter amputation of the left upper limb, following split‐thickness skin grafting.

Osteosarcoma is the most frequent malignant bone tumor in the pediatric population, with a peak incidence during adolescence. It remains relatively uncommon in older individuals [1]. Several studies have reported that age ≥ 40 years is associated with an increased risk of death compared with younger patients, which may reflect reduced tolerance to aggressive chemotherapy and a higher frequency of non‐extremity tumor sites [2]. In the present case, due to the persistent fetid odor and the disfiguring appearance of the tumor, the patient agreed to undergo forequarter amputation. Although the multidisciplinary tumor board initially expressed concerns that radical surgery might hasten clinical deterioration, the patient recovered well and achieved a favorable outcome. In conclusion, and in accordance with the findings of Naka et al., age alone should not be considered an adverse prognostic factor, including in cases of secondary osteosarcoma, and older patients should be offered treatment strategies comparable to those used in younger individuals, combining intensive chemotherapy with complete surgical excision whenever feasible [3].

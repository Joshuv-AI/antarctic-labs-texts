Quantum Homogenization as a Quantum Steady State Protocol on NISQ Hardware
Alexander Yosifov, Aditya Iyer, Daniel Ebler, Vlatko Vedral

Abstract
Quantum homogenization is a reservoir-based quantum state approximation protocol, which has been successfully implemented in state transformation on quantum hardware. In this work we move beyond that and propose the homogenization as a novel platform for quantum state stabilization and information protection. Using the Heisenberg exchange interactions formalism, we extend the standard quantum homogenization protocol to the dynamically-equivalent ( 𝚂𝚆𝙰𝙿 \mathtt{SWAP} ) α formulation. We then demonstrate its applicability on available noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) processors by presenting a shallow quantum circuit implementation consisting of a sequence of CNOT and single-qubit gates. In light of this, we employ the Beny-Oreshkov generalization of the Knill-Laflamme (KL) conditions for near-optimal recovery channels to show that our proposed ( 𝚂𝚆𝙰𝙿 \mathtt{SWAP} ) α quantum homogenization protocol yields a completely positive, trace preserving (CPTP) map under which the code subspace is correctable. Therefore, the protocol protects quantum information contained in a subsystem of the reservoir Hilbert space under CPTP dynamics.

IV
Robustness of quantum homogenization

In the context of approximate quantum error correction
[
53
]
and following the prescription of
[
32

Namely, assuming there are some noise-induced non-unitary effects (as discussed below), we show that the associated information “leakage“ is minimal and does not disturb the steady state of the quantum homogenizer.

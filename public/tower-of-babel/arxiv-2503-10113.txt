On 2-color partitions where one of the color is multiples of 5 k 5^{k}
Shivashankar C. 1, HemanthKumar B. 2, D. S. Gireesh 3

Abstract
. In this work, we investigate the arithmetic properties of p 1 , 5 k ​ ( n ) p_{1,5^{k}}(n) , which counts 2-color partitions of n n where one of the colors appears only in parts that are multiples of 5 k 5^{k} . By constructing generating functions for p 1 , 5 k ​ ( n ) p_{1,5^{k}}(n) across specific arithmetic progressions, we establish a set of Ramanujan-type infinite family of congruences modulo powers of 5 5 .

1.
Introduction

A partition of a positive integer
n
is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers that sums is
n
. Let
p(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
, with the generating function given by

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{f_{1}}.

Here and throughout the paper, we set

f_{r}:=(q^{r};q^{r})_{\infty}=\prod\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{rm}).

Ramanujan
[
5
]
conjectured, and Watson
[
8
]
proved that

(1.1)

p(5^{k}n+\delta_{k})\equiv 0\pmod{5^{k}},

where
k\geq 1
and
\delta_{k}
is the reciprocal modulo
5^{k}
of
24
.

Hirschhorn and Hunt
[
3
]
proved (
1.1
) by establishing generating functions

(1.2)

\sum_{n\geq 0}p\left(5^{2k-1}n+\delta_{2k-1}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{2k-1,j}\,q^{j-1}\frac{f_{5}^{6j-1}}{f_{1}^{6j}}

and

(1.3)

\sum_{n\geq 0}p\left(5^{2k}n+\delta_{2k}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{2k,j}\,q^{j-1}\frac{f_{5}^{6j}}{f_{1}^{6j+1}},

where the coefficient vectors
\mathbf{x}_{k}=(x_{k,1},x_{k,2},\dots)
are given by

\mathbf{x}_{1}=\left(x_{1,1},x_{1,2},\dots\right)=\left(5,0,0,\dots\right),

and for
k\geq 1
,

x_{k+1,i}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{k,j}\,m_{6j,j+i},\,\,\text{if}\,\,k\,\,\text{is odd},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{k,j}\,m_{6j+1,j+i},\,\,\text{if}\,\,k\,\,\text{is even},\end{cases}

where the first five rows of
M=\left(m_{i,j}\right)_{i,j\geq 1}
are

\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0&0&0&0&\dots\\
2\times 5&5^{3}&0&0&0&0&\dots\\
9&3\times 5^{3}&5^{5}&0&0&0&\dots\\
4&22\times 5^{2}&4\times 5^{5}&5^{7}&0&0&\dots\\
1&4\times 5^{3}&8\times 5^{5}&5^{8}&5^{9}&0&\dots\end{bmatrix}

and for
i\geq 6
,
m_{i,1}=0
, and for
j\geq 2
,

m_{i,j}=25m_{i-1,j-1}+25m_{i-2,j-1}+15m_{i-3,j-1}+5m_{i-4,j-1}+m_{i-5,j-1}.

Let
p_{1,\ell}(n)
be the number of 2-color partitions of
n
where one of the colors appears only in parts that are multiples of
\ell
; its generating function is given by

(1.4)

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,\ell}(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{f_{1}f_{\ell}}.

Ahmed, Baruah, and Dastidar
[
1
]
found several new congruences modulo 5:

(1.5)

p_{1,\ell}\left(25n+t\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5},

where
\ell\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5,10,15,20\}
and
\ell+t=24
. They also conjectured that the congruence also holds for
\ell=7,8,

and

17
. This conjecture was confirmed by Chern
[
2
]
, moreover he proved that

p_{1,4}\left(49n+t\right)\equiv 0\pmod{7},

where
t\in\{11,25,32,39\}
.

In
[
7
]
, Wang derived congruences for
p_{1,5}(n)
analogous to (
1.1
). He proved that

(1.6)

\displaystyle p_{1,5}\left(5^{\beta+1}n+\frac{3\times 5^{\beta+1}+1}{4}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\beta+1}},

(1.7)

\displaystyle p_{1,5}\left(5^{\beta+2}n+\frac{11\times 5^{\beta+1}+1}{4}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\beta+2}},

(1.8)

\displaystyle p_{1,5}\left(5^{\beta+2}n+\frac{19\times 5^{\beta+1}+1}{4}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\beta+2}},

for each
n,\beta\geq 0
. Note that, (
1.6
) is stronger result than (
1.5
) for
\ell=5
.

Ranganath
[
6
]
extended these results to
p_{1,25}(n)
. For each
n,\beta\geq 0
, he proved that

(1.9)

p_{1,25}\left(5^{2\beta+1}n+\frac{7\times 5^{2\beta+1}+13}{12}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\beta+1}}

and

(1.10)

p_{1,25}\left(5^{2\beta+2}n+\frac{11\times 5^{2\beta+2}+13}{12}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\beta+2}}.

We establish congruences for
p_{1,5^{k}}
for all positive integers
k
. The congruences previously derived by Wang and Ranganath can be seen as specific instances of the results presented in this work.

The main results are as follow:

Theorem 1.1
.

For each
n,\beta\geq 0
, and
k\geq 1
, we have

(1.11)

p_{1,5^{2k-1}}\left(5^{2k+\beta-1}n+\frac{18\cdot 5^{2k+\beta-1}+5^{2k-1}+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{2k+\beta-1}},

(1.12)

p_{1,5^{2k}}\left(5^{2k+2\beta-1}n+\frac{14\cdot 5^{2k+2\beta-1}+5^{2k}+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{2k+\beta-1}},

(1.13)

p_{1,5^{2k}}\left(5^{2k+2\beta}n+\frac{22\cdot 5^{2k+2\beta}+5^{2k}+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{2k+\beta}},

(1.14)

p_{1,5^{2k-1}}\left(5^{2k+\beta}n+\frac{(24r+18)\cdot 5^{2k+\beta-1}+5^{2k-1}+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{2k+\beta}},

and

(1.15)

p_{1,5^{2k}}\left(5^{2k+2\beta+2}n+\frac{22\cdot 5^{2k+2\beta+2}+5^{2k}+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{2k+\beta+1}},

where
r\in\{2,3,4\}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we state some lemmas which play a vital role in proving our results.
Let
H
be the “huffing” operator modulo
5
, that is,

H\left(\sum{p_{n}q^{n}}\right)=\sum{p_{1,5n}q^{5n}}.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

If
G=\frac{f_{5}^{6}}{q^{4}f_{1}f_{25}^{5}}
and
u=\frac{f_{5}^{6}}{q^{5}f_{25}^{6}}
, then

(2.1)

H\left(G^{i}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{i}m_{i,j}u^{i-j}.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
i\geq 1
, we have

(2.2)

H\left(q^{i+1}\frac{f_{5}^{6i}}{f_{1}^{6i+1}}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{5i+1}m_{6i+1,i+j}q^{5j}\frac{f_{25}^{6j-1}}{f_{5}^{6j}},

(2.3)

H\left(q^{i+2}\frac{f_{5}^{6i}}{f_{1}^{6i+2}}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{5i+2}m_{6i+2,i+j}\,q^{5j}\frac{f_{25}^{6j-2}}{f_{5}^{6j}},

and

(2.4)

H\left(q^{i}\frac{f_{5}^{6i-1}}{f_{1}^{6i}}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{5i}m_{6i,i+j}\,q^{5j}\frac{f_{25}^{6j}}{f_{5}^{6j+1}}.

Proof.

We can rewrite (
2.1
) as

(2.5)

H\left(q^{i}\frac{f_{25}^{i}}{f_{1}^{i}}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{i}m_{i,j}\,q^{5j}\frac{f_{25}^{6j}}{f_{5}^{6j}}.

From (
2.5
) and the fact that
m_{6i+1,j}=0
for
1\leq j<i,
we have

\displaystyle H\left(\left(q\frac{f_{25}}{f_{1}}\right)^{6i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=i+1}^{6i+1}m_{6i+1,j}\,q^{5j}\frac{f_{25}^{6j}}{f_{5}^{6j}}.

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{5i+1}m_{6i+1,i+j}\,q^{5i+5j}\frac{f_{25}^{6i+6j}}{f_{5}^{6i+6j}},

which yields (
2.2
). Similarly, we can prove (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).
∎

3.
Generating functions

In this section, we establish generating functions for
p_{1,5^{\ell}}(n)
within specific arithmetic progressions.

Theorem 3.1
.

For each
\beta\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
, we have

(3.1)

\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,5^{2k-1}}\left(5^{2k+\beta-1}n+\frac{18\cdot 5^{2k+\beta-1}+5^{2k-1}+1}{24}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,i}\,q^{i-1}\frac{f_{5}^{6i-1}}{f_{1}^{6i+1}}

where the coefficient vectors are defined as follows:

y_{1,j}^{(2k-1)}=x_{2k-1,j}

and

y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,j}=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta,i}m_{6i+1,j+i}

for all
\beta,j\geq 1
.

Proof.

From (
1.4
), we have

(3.2)

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,5^{2k-1}}(n)q^{n}=\dfrac{1}{f_{5^{2k-1}}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(n)q^{n}.

Extracting the terms involving
q^{5^{2k-1}n+\delta_{2k-1}}
on both sides of (
3.2
) and dividing throughout by
q^{\delta_{2k-1}}
, we obtain

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,5^{2k-1}}(5^{2k-1}n+\delta_{2k-1})q^{5^{2k-1}n}=\dfrac{1}{f_{5^{2k-1}}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(5^{2k-1}n+\delta_{2k-1})q^{5^{2k-1}n}.

If we replace
q^{5^{2k-1}}
by
q
and use (
1.2
), we get

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,5^{2k-1}}(5^{2k-1}n+\delta_{2k-1})q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{2k-1,j}\,q^{j-1}\frac{f_{5}^{6j-1}}{f_{1}^{6j+1}},

which is the case
\beta=0
of (
3.1
).

We now assume that (
3.1
) is true for some integer
\beta\geq 0
. Applying the operator
H
to both sides, by (
2.2
), we have

\begin{split}\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,5^{2k-1}}\left(5^{2k+\beta}n+\frac{18\cdot 5^{2k+\beta}+5^{2k-1}+1}{24}\right)q^{5n+5}&=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,i}\,H\left(q^{i+1}\frac{f_{5}^{6i}}{f_{1}^{6i+1}}\right)\times\frac{1}{f_{5}}\\
&=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,i}\sum_{j=1}^{5i+1}m_{6i+1,i+j}\,q^{5j}\frac{f_{25}^{6j-1}}{f_{5}^{6j+1}}\\
&=\sum_{j\geq 1}\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,i}\,m_{6i+1,i+j}\right)q^{5j}\frac{f_{25}^{6j-1}}{f_{5}^{6j+1}}\\
&=\sum_{j\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+2,j}\,q^{5j}\frac{f_{25}^{6j-1}}{f_{5}^{6j+1}}.\end{split}

That is,

\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,5^{2k-1}}\left(5^{2k+\beta}n+\frac{18\cdot 5^{2k+\beta}+5^{2k-1}+1}{24}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+2,i}\,q^{i-1}\frac{f_{5}^{6i-1}}{f_{1}^{6i+1}}.

So we obtain (
3.1
) with
\beta
replaced by
\beta+1
.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For each
\beta\geq 0
, and

on both sides of the above equation and dividing throughout by

(3.5)

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,5^{2k}}(5^{2k-1}n+\delta_{2k-1})q^{5^{2k-1}n}=\dfrac{1}{f_{5^{2k}}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(5^{2k-1}n+\delta_{2k-1})q^{5^{2k-1}n}.

If we replace
q^{5^{2k-1}}
by
q
and use (
1.3
), we get

(3.6)

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,5^{2k}}(5^{2k-1}n+\delta_{2k-1})q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{2k-1,j}\,q^{j-1}\frac{f_{5}^{6j-2}}{f_{1}^{6j}},

which is the case
\beta=0
of (
3.3
).

We now assume that (
3.3
) is true for some integer
\beta\geq 0
. Applying the operator
H
to both sides, by (
2.3
), we have

\begin{split}\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,5^{2k}}\left(5^{2k+2\beta}n+\frac{22\cdot 5^{2k+2\beta}+5^{2k}+1}{24}\right)q^{5n+5}&=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+1,i}\,H\left(q^{i}\frac{f_{5}^{6i-1}}{f_{1}^{6i}}\right)\times\frac{1}{f_{5}}\\
&=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+1,i}\sum_{j=1}^{5i}m_{6i,i+j}\,q^{5j}\frac{f_{25}^{6j}}{f_{5}^{6j+2}}\\

Hence, if (

) is true for some integer
\beta\geq 0
, then (
3.4
) is true for
\beta
.

Suppose that (
3.4
) is true for some integer
\beta\geq 0
. Applying the operating
H
to both sides, by (
2.4
), we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,5^{2k}}\left(5^{2k+2\beta+1}n+\frac{14\cdot 5^{2k+2\beta+1}+5^{2k}+1}{24}\right)q^{5n+5}&=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+2,i}\sum_{j=1}^{5i}m_{6i+2,i+j}q^{5j}\,\frac{f_{25}^{6j-2}}{f_{5}^{6j}}\\

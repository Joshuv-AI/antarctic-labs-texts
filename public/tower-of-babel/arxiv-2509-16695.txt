Picard groups of quotient ring spectra
Ishan Levy, Guchuan Li, Ningchuan Zhang

Abstract
. We develop tools to study Picard groups of quotients of ring spectra by a finitely generated ideal, which we use to show that Pic ⁡ ( E n / I ) = ℤ / 2 \mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{E}_{n}/I)=\mathbb{Z}/2 , where E n \mathrm{E}_{n} is a Lubin–Tate theory and I I is an ideal generated by suitable powers of a regular sequence. We apply this to obtain spectral sequences computing Picard groups of K ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{K}(n) -local generalized Moore algebras, and make some preliminary computations including the height 1 1 case.

2.
Tools for studying Picard groups

In this section, we develop tools to study Picard groups. In doing so, it will be useful to work with categories that are not monoidal, such as perfect module categories of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-rings. Although
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-rings do not have a reasonable notion of a Picard group, there is a relative version of the notion that is often well behaved, namely
2.1
below. It will be convenient to formulate some of our definitions even more generally, namely in terms of pointed small idempotent complete stable
\infty
-categories. We use
\mathsf{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}
to denote the category of small idempotent complete stable
\infty
-categories, and let
\mathsf{Cat}_{*}^{\mathrm{perf}}
denote the category of
\mathbb{E}_{0}
-algebras in
\mathsf{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}
, that is, the category of
\mathcal{C}\in\mathsf{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}
equipped with a specified object
c\in C
. As an example, for an
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-ring
R
the category
\mathsf{Mod}(R)^{\omega}
of perfect left
R
-modules naturally lives in
\mathsf{Cat}_{*}^{\mathrm{perf}}
, as it is equipped with the unit object
R
.

2.2.
The descent tower

Our next goal is to apply the above to study quotients of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-rings by powers of ideals. Ultimately, we want to apply this to study Picard groups of towers of the form
R/v^{n}
for
v\in\pi_{*}R
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
f\colon R\to S
be a map of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-rings. We define
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-rings
D_{i}(f)
that fit into a tower we call the
descent tower of
f

R\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow D_{i}(f)\longrightarrow D_{i-1}(f)\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow D_{0}(f)=S,

where
D_{i+1}(f)
for
i\geq 0
is defined as
D_{i}(f)\times_{D_{i}(f)\otimes_{R}S}S
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
f\colon R\to S
be a map of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-rings.
The underlying tower of unital
R
-bimodules of
D_{i}(f)
is the cofiber of the map
I^{\otimes_{R}(i+1)}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{fib}(f)^{\otimes_{R}i+1}}R
, where
I
is the fiber of the map
R\to S
.

Proof.

This statement can be proven by induction, and is clear in the case
i=0
. For the inductive step, the fiber of the map
R\to D_{i+1}(f)
is the total fiber of the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{i}(f)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{i}(f)\otimes_{R}S.}

Taking horizontal fibers and using the inductive hypothesis, we find it is the fiber of the map
I^{\otimes_{R}i}\to I^{\otimes_{R}i}\otimes_{R}S
, which is
I^{\otimes_{R}(i+1)}
as claimed.
∎

Example 2.12
.

Let
R
be an
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-ring, suppose
v\colon\Sigma^{i}R\to R
is an
R
-bimodule map such that the map of
R
-bimodules
R\to R/v
refines to an
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebra map. Then by
2.11
,
D_{i}(f)
is an
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-ring whose underlying
R
-bimodule is
R/v^{i+1}
.

The following lemma compares our construction to the quotient by powers construction of
[
Bur22
]
:

Lemma 2.13
.

If
R
is an
\mathbb{E}_{3}
-ring and
S
is an
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-
R
-algebra, then
D_{i}(f)
is an
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-
R
-algebra, and is equivalent to the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-
R
-algebra constructed in
[
Bur22
, Theorem 1.5]
on
R/I^{\otimes_{R}i+1}
where
I
is the fiber of
R\to S
.

Proof.

By the uniqueness statement of
[
Bur22
, Theorem 1.5]
, it suffices to prove that
D_{i}(f)
is
I
-compatible in the sense of
[
Bur22
, Definition 5.1]
. This can be proven by first observing that the definition of
D_{i}(f)

2.10
works for any map
f\colon R\to S
of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebras in a presentably monoidal stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, and is natural in monoidal functors
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
. Letting
Q
be as in
[
Bur22
, Definition 4.4]
for
\mathcal{C}=\mathsf{Mod}(R)
and
\mathds{1}/v
being
S
, we can now consider the monoidal functor
(-)^{\tau=1}\colon\mathrm{Def}(\mathsf{Mod}(R);Q)\to\mathsf{Mod}(R)
as in
[
Bur22
, Construction 4.6]
.

Using the monoidal functor
\nu\colon\mathsf{Mod}(R)\to\mathrm{Def}(\mathsf{Mod}(R);Q)
(see loc. cit.),
D_{i}(\nu(f))
is a witness of the fact that
D_{i}(f)
is
I
-compatible, so we are done.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

In fact, a version of
2.13
holds when

is just an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-algebra or when

is just an
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebra map. However, to state it, one needs to extend the result of

to the setting of monoidal categories. Such an extension will appear in a revision of the above.

Finally, we note that the maps in the descent tower are conservative on module categories.

4

In fact, they are descendable in a suitable sense, but we do not pursue this here.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
f\colon R\to S
. Then the maps
f_{i,j}\colon D_{i}(f)\to D_{j}(f)
for
i>j
, induce conservative functors on module categories.

Proof.

Because conservative functors are closed under composition, it suffices to show the lemma for the map
f_{i+1,i}
. If
M
is a
D_{i+1}(f)
-module such that
D_{i}(f)\otimes_{D_{i+1}(f)}M
vanishes, then both
S\otimes_{D_{i+1}(f)}M
and
D_{i}(f)\otimes_{R}S\otimes_{D_{i+1}(f)}M
vanish, so by the pullback square defining
D_{i+1}(f)
, we see that
M
vanishes.
∎

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
f\colon R\to S
be a map of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-rings. Then
\mathsf{Mod}(D_{i+1}(f)\mid D_{i}(f))
is a union of components inside
\Omega^{\infty}(\Sigma I^{\otimes_{R}i+1}\otimes_{R}S)
. In particular, if
\pi_{-1}I^{\otimes_{R}i+1}\otimes_{R}S=0
, then
\mathsf{Mod}(D_{i+1}(f)\mid D_{i}(f))
is connected.

Proof.

Applying
2.9
in the case of the map of Milnor contexts
(D_{i}(f),S,D_{i}(f)\otimes_{R}S)\to(D_{i}(f),S,S)
, we find that it is enough to show that the fiber of the map

\Omega^{\infty}(D_{i}(f)\otimes_{R}S)\longrightarrow\Omega^{\infty}S

over the unit is connected.

This map is a map of spectra with a section coming from the unit map, so its fiber over all points are equivalent and equivalent to
\mathrm{fib}(D_{i}(f)\otimes_{R}S\to S)
. But this fiber is
\Sigma\mathrm{fib}(S\to D_{i}(f)\otimes_{R}S)
, which is equivalent to
\Sigma I^{\otimes_{R}i+1}\otimes_{R}S
by
2.11
.
∎

To demonstrate the purpose of
2.16
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
R
be an
\mathbb{E}_{3}
-ring. Suppose
v\in\pi_{*}(R)
is an element such that
R/v
admits a left unital multiplication in
R
-modules. If
n\geq 3
and
\pi_{-1-n|v|}(R/v)=0
, then the base change map
\mathrm{Pic}(R/v^{n+1})\to\mathrm{Pic}(R/v^{n})
is injective where
R/v^{n+1}\to R/v^{n}
is made into an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-
R
-algebra map using
[
Bur22
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Proof.

By
2.2
, it is enough to show that the relative module space
\mathsf{Mod}(R/v^{n+1}\mid R/v^{n})
is connected.
By
2.16
, the kernel of the base change map on Picard groups
\mathrm{Pic}(R/v^{n+1})\to\mathrm{Pic}(R/v^{n})
is contained in
\pi_{0}\Sigma\Sigma^{n|v|}R/v=\pi_{-1-n|v|}R/v
as claimed.
∎

Example 2.18
.

The assumption
\pi_{-1-n|v|}(R/v)=0
in
2.17
cannot be removed, which we demonstrate with an example. Consider
R=\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{K}(1)}
for an odd prime
p
. Then the maps
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{K}(1)}/p^{n+1})\to\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{K}(1)}/p^{n})
are not injective, because the periodicity of the rings
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{K}(1)}/p^{k+1}
increases as
k
increases, so there are suspensions of the unit in the kernel. However,
\pi_{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{K}(1)}/p)=\mathbb{Z}/p\neq 0
, so indeed, the hypotheses of
2.17
are not satisfied. In fact, the kernel of these maps on Picard groups is
\mathbb{Z}/p
, which maps isomorphically onto
\pi_{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{K}(1)}/p)
, the upper bound on the relative Picard group coming from
2.16
.

2.3.
Picard groups of regular quotients of complete local rings

The goal of this section is to use the tools above to compute Picard groups of certain ring spectra.

Definition 2.19
.

A regular local graded ring is a local Noetherian
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring
R
that contains a regular sequence of homogeneous elements
m_{i}\in\pi_{*}R
such that
\pi_{*}R/(m_{i})
is a graded field.

We now give a particular construction of quotients of
\pi_{*}
-regular local
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-rings by powers of elements in a regular sequence.

Construction 2.20
.

Suppose
R
be an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-ring such that
\pi_{*}R
is concentrated in even degrees. Choose a sequence
m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}
of homogeneous elements of
\pi_{*}R
. In
[
HW18
, Section 2]
, it is shown that there is an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-algebra map
\mathbb{S}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}]\to R
taking
x_{i}
to
m_{i}
, where
\mathbb{S}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}]
is an augmented
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-algebra and each
x_{i}
has multi-grading so that they form a basis. Fixing such a map, given a sequence of positive integers
a_{1},\cdots,a_{k}
, we may form the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-
R
-algebra
R/(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{k}^{a_{k}})
as a relative tensor product
R\otimes_{\mathbb{S}[x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{k}^{a_{k}}]}\mathbb{S}
, where
\mathbb{S}[x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{k}^{a_{k}}]
is the algebra obtained by restricting
\mathbb{S}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}]
to the multi-gradings indicated.

Lemma 2.21
.

Suppose that
R
is as in
2.20
. The tower of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-
R
-algebras

R/x_{1}^{a_{1}}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}R/x_{i}^{\bullet}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}R/x_{k}^{a_{k}}

is isomorphic to the descent tower
D_{\bullet-1}(f)
, where
f
is the map

R/x_{1}^{a_{1}}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}\widehat{R/x_{i}}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}R/x_{k}^{a_{k}}\longrightarrow R/x_{1}^{a_{1}}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}R/x_{i}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}R/x_{k}^{a_{k}}.

Here
\widehat{R/x_{i}}
indicates that the tensor factor involving
R/x_{i}
is missing in the source.

Proof.

We first prove that the descent tower of the map of graded
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-
\mathbb{S}[x_{i}]
-algebras
g\colon\mathbb{S}[x_{i}]\to\mathbb{S}
sending
x_{i}
to zero is the tower
\mathbb{S}[x_{i}]/x_{i}^{\bullet+1}
. There is a comparison map by observing that the descent tower
D_{i}(g)
is concentrated in gradings
\leq i+1
, so it receives a map from
\mathbb{S}[x_{i}]/x^{i+1}
, since it is obtained via the monoidal localization in graded
\mathbb{S}[x_{i}]
-modules away from objects in degrees at least
i+1
. That the map is an equivalence follows from the description of the descent tower from
2.11
.

Note that the descent tower of a map is preserved along monoidal exact functors. Since
f
is the base change of the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-
\mathbb{S}[x_{i}]
-algebra map

R/x_{1}^{a_{1}}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}R[x_{i}]\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}R/x_{k}^{a_{k}}\longrightarrow R/x_{1}^{a_{1}}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}\widehat{R/x_{i}}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}R/x_{k}^{a_{k}}

along the
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-algebra map
\mathbb{S}[x_{i}]\to\mathbb{S}
sending
x_{i}
to
0
, the result follows.
∎

Definition 2.22
.

Let
R
be an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-ring and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathsf{Mod}(R)^{\omega}
be a thick subcategory generated by a single object. Then the category
\mathsf{Mod}(R)^{\wedge}_{\mathcal{C}}
is defined to be the Bousfield localization of
\mathsf{Mod}(R)
with respect to tensoring with some generator
M\in\mathcal{C}
. We note that this is independent of the choice of generator. If
I\trianglelefteq\pi_{*}R
is a finitely generated ideal, we use
\mathsf{Mod}(R)^{\wedge}_{I}
to denote
\mathsf{Mod}(R)^{\wedge}_{\mathcal{C}_{I}}
where
\mathcal{C}_{I}
is the thick subcategory generated by
(R/x_{1})\otimes_{R}(R/x_{2})\otimes\cdots\otimes_{R}(R/x_{n})
where
I=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
. We note this is independent of the choice of generators.

Proposition 2.23
.

Let
R
be an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-ring such that
\pi_{*}R
is even and a complete regular local graded ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Then the map
\mathbb{Z}=\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{S})\to\mathrm{Pic}(\mathsf{Mod}(R))^{\wedge}_{\mathfrak{m}})
is surjective.

In particular, in the above situation,
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathsf{Mod}(R)_{\mathcal{m}})
is cyclic of order depending only on the periodicity of
\pi_{*}R
. Note that this proposition generalizes
[
BR05
, Theorem 37]
and
[
MS16
, Theorem 2.4.6]
.

Proof.

Choose a regular sequence
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
generating
\pi_{*}R
, and consider the ring map
R\to R/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
coming from
2.20
. If
M\in\mathrm{Pic}(R)
, then
-\otimes_{R}M
gives an automorphism of
\mathsf{Mod}(R/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}))
. Since
\pi_{*}(R/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}))
is a field, every module is free, so this automorphism must send the unit to
\Sigma^{n}R/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})
for some
n
. It now follows from running the Bockstein spectral sequences for each
x_{i}
that
M\cong\Sigma^{n}R
.
∎

We next observe that the relative module space for the map
R/(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{m}^{a_{k}})\to R/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})
is connected.

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
R
be an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-ring such that
\pi_{*}R
is even and let
x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}
be a sequence of elements in
\pi_{*}R
such that
\pi_{-1}R/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})=0
. Then
\mathsf{Mod}(R/(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{m}^{a_{m}})\mid R/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}))
is connected.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}
, with the base case being
a_{i}=1
for all
i
, where the result is trivial. So if we fix an
i
, because an extension of connected spaces is connected, in the inductive step, it is enough to show

\mathsf{Mod}(R/(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{i}^{a_{i}+1},\cdots,x_{m}^{a_{m}})\mid(R/(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{m}^{a_{m}}))

is connected. But by
2.12
and
2.21
, this is the relative module space of the map
D_{i}(f)\to D_{i-1}(f)
where
f
is the map
A=R/(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}^{a_{i}}},\cdots,x_{m}^{a_{m}})\to B=R/(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{m}^{a_{m}})
. Thus by
2.16
, it is enough to check that
\pi_{-1}I^{\otimes_{A}i+1}\otimes_{A}B=0
, where
I\to A\to B
is a fiber sequence. But
I\cong A
as an
A
-bimodule, so it is enough to see that
\pi_{-1}B=0
. But this follows because
B
is built under extensions by
R/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})
, which by assumption has
\pi_{-1}
vanishing.
∎

In the regular local case, we can bound the essential image of
\mathrm{Pic}(R/(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{m}^{a_{m}}))
in
\mathrm{Pic}(R/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}))
:

Theorem 2.25
.

Let
R
be an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-ring such that
\pi_{*}R
is even and a complete regular graded ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
generated by the regular sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
. Then for any
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-refinement of the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-

-algebra structure on

from

\mathrm{Pic}(R/(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{m}^{a_{m}}))
is generated by
\Sigma R
.

Proof.

Let
A=R/(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{m}^{a_{m}})
and
B=R/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})
, which has homotopy ring a field.
By
2.24
, it is enough to show that if
M\in\mathrm{Pic}(A)
, then

for some

\mathrm{End}_{B}(B\otimes_{A}M)=\mathrm{map}_{A}(M,B\otimes_{A}M)=\mathrm{map}_{A}(A,(B\otimes_{A}M)\otimes_{A}M^{\vee})=B

\Sigma^{n}B

.

.

Let

(1)

3.2

{}^{\mathrm{Pic}}E_{2}^{s,s}=\mathrm{H}_{c}^{s}(\mathbb{G}_{n};\pi_{t}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{ic}(\mathrm{E}_{n}\otimes M))=\begin{cases}\mathrm{H}_{c}^{0}(\mathbb{G}_{n};\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{E}_{n}\otimes M)),&s=0;\\

•

For

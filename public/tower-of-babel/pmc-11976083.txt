Patterns of mandibular third molar impaction and its relationship with distal caries in the adjacent mandibular second molars: A retrospective study
Authors: Abrar K Demyati
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImpacted third molars can cause significant challenges for dental health. While many of these impacted teeth remain asymptomatic, they can lead to dental caries.ObjectiveTo investigate the patterns of mandibular third molar impaction and its relationship with the occurrence of caries in the adjacent mandibular second molars.Materials and MethodsThis study assessed the dental panoramic radiographs of 1591 participants who visited the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery at the Dental Teaching Hospital at Umm Al-Qura University between 2022 and 2024. The presence of distal caries in the adjacent mandibular second molars was assessed and compared across the different impaction patterns, considering both age and gender.ResultsThe results showed that 29.22 % of the patients had impacted mandibular third molars. The most common patterns of impacted mandibular third molar angulation were mesioangular in the females and horizontal in the males. The majority of these impacted third molars were level A and class I (as per the Pell and Gregory classification).One hundred and eighty-three (39.35 %) of the impacted mandibular third molars had associated carious lesions, with 89 showing distal caries in the adjacent mandibular second molar and 18 showing caries in the third molar itself. The carious lesions were most commonly found in cases of mesioangular, level A, and class I impaction patterns (p < 0.05).ConclusionThe findings of this study shed light to the high prevalence of carious lesions associated with impacted mandibular third molar, especially in cases of mesioangular, level A, and class I impaction patterns. These findings highlight the importance of careful monitoring of impacted third molars to prevent the development of caries in the adjacent teeth.Keywords: Distal caries, Mandibular, Third molar, Second molar, Impaction, Classification

Introduction

An impacted tooth is any tooth that is incapable of erupting into position and reaching the occlusal plane even after two thirds of the tooth's root development is complete (Ozbey et al., 2024). Third molar impaction is a common finding in routine dental examinations, and third molars exhibit a higher impaction rate when compared to other teeth (Ozbey et al., 2024). The typical eruption timeframe for third molars is between 18 and 25 years of age (Khawaja et al., 2015). The literature suggests different causes of third molar impaction, and approximately 40 % of third molar teeth become impacted due to a lack of space (Yildirim et al., 2024, Khawaja et al., 2015).

While many impacted teeth can be asymptomatic, complications can still occur (Altan and Akbulut, 2019). One common complication is the development of dental caries, which may affect the impacted mandibular third molar (IM3M) itself or the adjacent distal surface of the mandibular second molar (M2M) (Altan and Akbulut, 2019). This is considered to be a frequent reason for extracting the lower third or second molars (Jin et al., 2021). In the literature, Nordenram et al. since 1987 found that 15 % of third molar extractions had been due to caries (Nordenram et al., 1987).

On a global level, a meta-analysis was conducted by Glória et al., in 2018 to identify the characteristics of IM3M that associated with an increase in the incidence of caries on the distal surface of M2M and found that IM3M leads to a higher rate of caries on the distal surface of M2M and is often linked to a higher level of occlusal impaction to the bone, as well as more horizontal and mesioangular angulation (Glória et al., 2018).

The risk of developing caries in the M2M is associated with the condition and the angulation pattern of the impacted third molars (Pentapati et al., 2019). This risk is frequently observed and ranges from 7 % to 32 % in cases of partially erupted third molars or with a mesioangular or horizontal IM3M (Syed et al., 2017, Shams et al., 2017). The presence of IM3M makes it challenging to visualize the distal surface of the M2M, which in turn makes it challenging to detect caries in the second molar adjacent to the impacted third molar (AlHobail et al., 2019). Furthermore, the increased risk of caries is due to the location of the IM3M, which is positioned far back in the lower jaw, making this area less accessible to oral hygiene practices (Syed et al., 2017). Additionally, the pressure exerted by the IM3M upon the M2M can lead to bone resorption at the distal cemento-enamel junction, promoting plaque and bacterial accumulation (Pentapati et al., 2019).

The identification of impactions can be clinically determined and confirmed using standard radiographs such as orthopantomograph (OPG), which evaluates the hard tissues of the oral cavity and the surrounding facial structures. In dental practice, OPGs are the most widely used method for examining impacted teeth because they are easier to perform and provide a more comprehensive view of the entire mouth than do other radiographs (Jin et al., 2021).

A retrospective radiographic interpretation study from Saudi Arabia examined OPGs from Riyadh Elm University dental clinics on 313 patients who had 371 (29.6 %) IM3Ms. Among these IM3Ms, vertical impaction was the most common type followed by horizontal impaction. The study found that 39 % of the adjacent M2M had developed caries on the distal surface in cases in which the third molar was impacted (AlHobail et al., 2019).

Currently, a limited number of studies in Saudi Arabia have investigated the relationship between IM3M and distal caries in the adjacent M2M. Due to insufficient evidence, there is not yet strong evidence of a prophylactic preventative measure against distal caries in the second molars. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the patterns of IM3M and its relationship with the development of distal caries in the adjacent M2M, considering the age range and the gender distribution associated with distal caries affecting the M2M.

Methods

A retrospective analysis of 1591 OPGs was carried out between 2022 and 2024 in the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery at the Dental Teaching Hospital at Umm Al-Qura University, Makkah, KSA. The study included high-quality OPGs of both genders, aged between 18 and 60 years, with distal caries present in lower M2M and complete root formation, presence of IM3M on at least one side of the mandible.

Participants were excluded if they had unclear OPGs, were below 18 years of age, had unerupted, missing or incomplete root formation of the IM3M, were missing or had filled adjacent M2M, or had any conditions that could affect tooth alignment, including previous orthodontic treatment, jaw pathology, facial syndromes or hereditary diseases.

Winter’s classification was used to evaluate the angle by measuring the angle between the second and third molar, intersecting longitudinal axes (Winter, 1926). Impacted mandibular third molar angulation was measured by a protractor (180°) with vertical impaction (10°–10°), mesioangular impaction (11°–79°), horizontal impaction (80°–100°) and distoangular impaction (11°–79°).

The Pell and Gregory classification system was used to evaluate the depth of impacted third molars, depending on the occlusal surface and the cementoenamel junction of the M2M, as level A, B, or C. It is also used to classify the IM3M ramus relationship based on the extent of the tooth enclosed by the anterior border of the ramus as class I, II, or III (Pell, 1933).

All radiographs conducted by a qualified technician (GXDP-700TM, Hatfield, USA) and analyzed by a single researcher with a high level of experience in the field who was a consultant in oral and maxillofacial surgery to assess the presence of caries and the patterns of impacted teeth through the OPGs.

Age, gender, impaction angulation, depth, and ramus relationship were variables used to assess carious lesions in lower second molar teeth in relation to the impaction pattern of the third molar.

The Raosoft sample size calculator estimated that a sample of 310 participants was needed to reach a margin of error of 5 % with a 95 % confidence level (Raosoft, 2004). This calculation was based on the typical annual clinic visit of 900 patients. The sample size was increased to 465 participants to increase the sample power.

Statistical analysis

SPSS® software, version 20.0, was used for statistical analysis, and P ≤ 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant with a 95 % confidence interval. The chi-square test was utilized to find the association between caries and third molar impaction.

Ethical approval and consent

This study received approval from the Umm Al-Qura University institutional review board. Patients gave written consent at the initial visit and it was indicated that data and radiographs would be used for research and education without identification.

Results

A total of 1591 patient OPGs were analyzed to detect IM3M. Then, each OPG was studied for the presence of carious lesions in IM3M and M2M. Additionally, the depth, angulation, and relationship with the ramus of IM3M were evaluated. Of the 1591 OPGs, 465 (29.22 %) patients had at least one IM3M. The number of patients with bilateral IM3M impactions was 31 (6.66 %). Females accounted for 243 cases (52.25 %), while males accounted for 222 cases (47.74 %). The study population had a mean age of 25 years (18–60 years), and 367 (79.35 %) were below than 40 of age. Discussion

The development of distal caries in M2M adjacent to IM3M is a continuing process that represents a significant complication associated with IM3M (Jin et al., 2021).

This retrospective study investigated the patterns of IM3M and its relationship with distal caries in the adjacent M2M development in the Makkah region of Saudi Arabia. The current study found that the overall prevalence of IM3M was 29.22 %. This prevalence rate aligned with the ranges reported in the existing research literature in Saudi Arabia, which indicate that the incidence of impacted teeth can vary considerably, ranging from 16.3 % to 58.3 % (Braimah et al., 2021, Alfadil and Almajed, 2020, Alhobail et al., 2019).

Our study also reported a slightly higher female prevalence of IM3M (52.25 %) which coincides with a study conducted by Shaari et al. which evaluated the pattern of impaction among the Iraqi population and reported a higher incidence of IM3M in females than in males (Shaari et al., 2023). In contrast to the gender findings in the current study, some previous studies have reported opposite findings (Adeola et al., 2023).

This gender difference between different studies may be attributed to factors such as earlier tooth eruption in females as well as differences in jaw morphology. In addition, it can be related to differences in the population studied and the methodologies used in this study in comparison to other studies.

The study sample was predominantly in the 18–30 age range, comprising 52.90 % of participants with IM3M. This is consistent with findings from other studies on IM3M, which have found similar age ranges to be the most prevalent (Al-Madani et al., 2024, Braimah et al., 2021, Prajapati et al., 2017). Most third molars would have fully erupted and completed root formation in this age range. As such, this age range is typically the focus of research on impacted third molars.

Regarding the depth, angulation, and relationship with the ramus of IM3M, the most common pattern was level A impaction, mesioangular angulation, and class I ramus relationship (61.34 % in females, 58.21 % in males). These outcomes are consistent with those of previous reports, which have consistently identified mesioangular impaction as the most prevalent type (Toedtling et al., 2023, Alfadil and Almajed, 2020). In contrast, a study conducted by Mohammed Alsaegh et al. in the United Arab Emirates which evaluated the caries status of M2M in relation to the pattern of IM3M found that the most prevalent characteristics of the depth and ramus relationship of IM3M were level B, and class II impactions (Alsaegh et al., 2022).

A significant finding of the current study showed carious lesions in 39.35 % of the cases, distributed with more lesions on the distal surface of the adjacent M2M and a lower number in the IM3M itself. This is consistent with the existing literature, which has reported caries rates up to 39 % (Mumtaz et al., 2022).

Further analysis revealed that the prevalence of carious lesions was highest in specific IM3M impaction patterns. The mesioangular (35.26 %), level A (25.92 %), and class I (34.70 %) impactions exhibited the greatest frequency of associated carious lesions in the adjacent M2M.

The findings of the current study aligned with those reported by Ramat Braimah et al. in their study conducted in the southern region of Saudi Arabia. Furthermore, they found that the prevalence of carious lesions was highest among mesioangular impaction (31.5 %), level A impaction depth (46.6 %), and class I ramus relationship (54.8 %) (Braimah et al., 2021). In addition, Kamran Syed et al. conducted a study in the Abha region of Saudi Arabia to evaluate the prevalence of distal caries in M2M caused by IM3M, and found similar results (Syed et al., 2017). Moreover, they also found that the second most prevalent impaction angulation after mesioangular in the development of distal caries in the M2M was the horizontal angulation pattern (Syed et al., 2017). This is also consistent with our findings, as horizontal angulation was the second most prevalent impaction angulation after mesioangular, which was observed in 9.09 % of cases.

Regarding the depth of impaction of the IM3M, the findings of the current study differed from those reported in a 2017 study by Prajapati et al. that evaluated distal caries in association with IM3M in an Indian population (Prajapati et al., 2017). While the current study found that level A impactions were more prevalent and associated with distal caries in M2M, the Prajapati et al. study reported that level B impactions were more prevalent than level A impactions associated with distal caries in M2M in their study population (Prajapati et al., 2017).

This direct comparison emphasizes the consistency in the patterns of carious lesion distribution associated with specific types of IM3M across different studies and it recommends that future research focus on investigating methods to prevent M2M caries development related to IM3M before it occurs taking into account the time that has elapsed since the IM3M began its eruption.

A limitation of this study is that the use of OPGs may not provide as high-resolution imaging as that provided by periapical radiographs or cone-beam computed tomography. Additionally, the retrospective, single-institution design of the study may be susceptible to selection bias and the findings may not be fully representative of the broader population.

Conclusion

The findings of this study shed light on the high prevalence of carious lesions associated with IM3M, especially in cases of mesioangular, level A, and class I impaction patterns. These results highlight the critical need for close monitoring and prompt intervention to prevent the development of caries in the adjacent M2M. Clinicians should carefully evaluate the position and angulation of IM3M during routine examinations and tailor their management strategies to optimize long-term oral health outcomes for their patients.

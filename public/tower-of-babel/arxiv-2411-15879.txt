1.1.
Automorphism groups

Generators for
GA_{2}(k):={\rm Aut}_{k}(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{2})
were first given by Jung (1942) in
[
21
]
in case the field
k
is algebraically closed of characteristic zero.
Jung showed that
GA_{2}(k)
is generated by its affine linear and triangular subgroups,
{\rm Aff}_{2}(k)
and
BA_{2}(k)
.
A structural description of
GA_{2}(k)
was subsequently given by
Van der Kulk (1953) in
[
36
]
for any characteristic zero field. In particular:

GA_{2}(k)={\rm Aff}_{2}(k)*_{B}BA_{2}(k)\quad{\rm where}\quad B={\rm Aff}_{2}(k)\cap BA_{2}(k)

This was generalized to any field by Nagata (1972) in
[
29
]
.

The exponential automorphisms of
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{2}
were determined by Ebey (1962) in
[
11
]
and Rentschler (1968) in
[
34
]
.
Without using the results of Jung or Van der Kulk, they show that the tame subgroup of
GA_{2}(k)
(generated by its linear and triangular elements) acts transitively
on
{\rm KLND}(B)
.
From this, Rentschler recovers Jung’s theorem. Transitivity also follows from the amalgamated free product structure of
GA_{2}(k)
, since reductive subgroups must conjugate to
GL_{2}(k)
and unipotent subgroups must conjugate to
BA_{2}(k)
; see
[
35
]
, Theorem 8.

For the affine surfaces
xy+az^{2}=b
over a field
K
(
a,b\in K^{*}
), Danilov and Gizatullin (1977) computed the automorphism groups as amalgamated free products in
[
9
]
.
Lamy (2005) derived the same structure for these groups by different methods
[
23
]
.
In the more general case, generators for the automorphism group of a Danielewski surface
S
defined by
xy=P(z)
were given by Makar-Limanov (1990) in
[
25
]
.
Makar-Limanov showed that this group is generated by the linear and triangular automorphisms of
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{3}
which restrict to
S
.
Let
d=P^{\prime}(z)\partial_{y}-x\partial_{z}
and, for
f(x)\in k[x]
, define
\Delta_{f}=\exp(fd)
.
Without using Makar-Limanov’s result, Daigle (2003) showed in
[
6
]
that the subgroup generated by
\{\Delta_{f}\,|\,f\in k[x]\}
and the involution
\tau(x,y,z)=(y,x,z)
acts transitively
on
{\rm KLND}(k[S])
.
Makar-Limanov gives another proof of Daigle’s result in
[
27
]
.
In
[
5
]
, Blanc, Bot and Poloni (2023) used Makar-Limanov’s generators of

to show that
{\rm Aut}_{k}(S)
is the amalgamated free product of its affine and triangular subgroups,
i.e., elements that descend from an affine or triangular automorphism of
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{3}
, respectively.

In
[
24
]
, Liendo, Regeta and Urech (2023) prove that any normal affine
SL_{2}
-surface is uniquely determined by its automorphism group (Proposition 6.8).

1.2.
The Surface
X
.

The papers
[
19
,
15
]
describe normal affine surfaces over
\mathbb{C}
which admit an action of an algebraic group with a big open orbit, i.e., the complement of the orbit is finite.
In the first paper, Gizatullin (1971) deals with the smooth case, and shows that the only such surfaces are
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
Y
and
X
.
In the second paper, Flenner and Zaidenberg (2005) generalize these results to the normal case.

Working over
k=\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
Y
and
X
were studied by Fauntleroy and Magid (1978) in
[
12
]
.
Each surface has a fixed-point free
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action which is the restriction of the
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-action to its upper-triangular unipotent subgroup.
The authors show that each action has a geometric quotient, where
Y/\mathbb{G}_{a}\cong L
is not affine, and
X/\mathbb{G}_{a}\cong\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}
is affine.
In particular,
L
denotes a line with the origin doubled. The
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action on
Y
is locally trivial but the quotient is not separated, so the action is not proper.
On the other hand, the action on
X
is not locally trivial – otherwise it would be globally trivial since
X
would be an
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}
-bundle over
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}
, and this is clearly not the case.
Since a proper action is locally trivial, this implies that the
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action on
X
is not proper.
The authors also calculate the divisor class group (which equals the Picard group) for each surface:
{\rm Cl}(Y)\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
{\rm Cl}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

These surfaces also appear in Huckleberry’s study (1986) of more general complex homogeneous surfaces
[
20
]
.
In
[
28
]
, Masuda and Miyanishi (2003) characterize
X
as the unique
\mathbb{Q}
-homology plane
S
with
ML(S)=\mathbb{C}
and
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})=\pi_{1}(S)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Recall that
a
\mathbb{Q}
-homology plane
S
has
H_{i}(S,\mathbb{Q})=0
for
i\geq 1
.

Y
and
X
also feature in
[
14
]
, in which Flenner and Zaidenberg (2005) study surfaces that admit an effective action of a maximal torus
\mathbb{T}
such that any
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action is conjugate to a subtorus of
\mathbb{T}
.

Bandman and Makar-Limanov (2005) consider the surface

over
k=\mathbb{C}
in
[
4
]
, Example 1. Of particular note, they give a simple set of defining relations for

as a subvariety of

, and they give two independent locally nilpotent derivations on
\mathfrak{R}
. We give these relations in

Since the relations are polynomials over

In
[
17
]
, the author (2024) generalizes Popov’s classification of normal affine

-surfaces with trivial units to any field

Proposition 5.3 shows that

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Gamma\subset{\rm Aut}_{k}(\mathfrak{R})
be generated by

\Gamma

Proof.

Let
\theta\in{\rm LND}(\mathfrak{R})
be given. Since
\mathfrak{R}\in\mathcal{C}(k)
,
{\rm ker}\,\theta=k[h]=k^{[1]}
for some

.
By

5.2

\Theta\in{\rm LND}(\mathfrak{D})

Consequently, there exists

Recall that

\mathfrak{R}

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
T\cong k^{*}
be the torus in
PSL_{2}(k)
and define:

E=\{\exp(f\delta)\,|\,f\in k[x_{0}^{2}]\}\cong(k^{[1]},+)

(a)

\mathcal{T}=E\rtimes T

(b)

{\rm Aut}_{k}(\mathfrak{R})
is generated by
\mathcal{T}
and
\tau|_{\mathfrak{R}}

Proof.

Part (a). Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}
be given. Since
\alpha
is triangular, we have

\alpha(x_{0}^{2})=a_{0}x_{0}^{2}+b\,\,,\,\,\alpha(x_{0}x_{1})=a_{1}x_{0}x_{1}+b_{0}\,\,,\,\,\alpha(x_{1}^{2})=a_{2}x_{1}^{2}+b_{1}

where
a_{i}\in k^{*}
,
b\in k
,
b_{0}\in k[x_{0}^{2}]
and
b_{1}\in k[x_{0}^{2}]+k[x_{0}^{2}]x_{0}x_{1}

The relation

(x_{0}^{2}+b)(a_{2}x_{1}^{2}+b_{1})-(a_{1}x_{0}x_{1}+b_{0})^{2}=0

The left side of this equation is in
k[x_{0}^{2},x_{1}]\cong k^{[2]}
, so the coefficient of
x_{1}^{2}
must be 0. We thus obtain:

(a_{2}-a_{1}^{2})x_{0}^{2}+ba_{2}=0\,\,\text{in}\,\,k[x_{0}^{2}]\implies a_{2}-a_{1}^{2}=ba_{2}=0\implies b=0\implies\alpha(x_{0}^{2})=x_{0}^{2}

This leaves the equation
b_{1}x_{0}^{2}=(2a_{1}+b_{0})b_{0}
, which shows that
\deg_{\delta}(b_{1})=0
and
b_{1}\in k[x_{0}^{2}]
.
Therefore,
b_{1}x_{0}^{2}=(2a_{1}+b_{0})b_{0}

Write

\tilde{b}_{0}\in k[x_{0}^{2}]

\alpha(2x_{0}x_{2}-x_{1}^{2})=1
gives:

\displaystyle x_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{0}^{2}\,\alpha(2x_{0}x_{2}-x_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2x_{0}^{2}\alpha(x_{0}x_{2})-\alpha(x_{0}x_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2x_{0}^{2}\alpha(x_{0}x_{2})-(a_{1}x_{0}x_{1}+b_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2x_{0}^{2}\alpha(x_{0}x_{2})-a_{1}^{2}x_{0}^{2}x_{1}^{2}-2a_{1}b_{0}x_{0}x_{1}-b_{0}^{2}

Dividing by

gives

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\alpha(x_{0}x_{2})-a_{1}^{2}x_{1}^{2}-2a_{1}\tilde{b}_{0}x_{0}x_{1}-x_{0}^{2}\tilde{b}_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\alpha(x_{0}x_{2})-a_{1}^{2}(2x_{0}x_{2}-1)-2a_{1}\tilde{b}_{0}x_{0}x_{1}-x_{0}^{2}\tilde{b}_{0}^{2}

.
Let
\rho\in T

\alpha(x_{0}^{2})=x_{0}^{2}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\alpha(x_{0}x_{1})=x_{0}x_{1}+b_{0}\quad\text{or}\quad\rho\alpha(x_{0}^{2})=x_{0}^{2}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\rho\alpha(x_{0}x_{1})=x_{0}x_{1}+\rho(b_{0})

We can thus assume, with no loss of generality, that

Let

Since

Part (b). Let

9.
Three lemmas for the class
\mathcal{C}(k)

Recall the conditions for membership in
\mathcal{C}(k)
.

(a)

B
is a normal affine
k
-domain,
\dim B=2
,
B^{*}=k^{*}
and
ML(B)\neq B
.

(b)

{\rm ker}\,D\cong_{k}k^{[1]}
for every nonzero

.

Lemma 9.1
.

Suppose that the ring
B
satisfies condition
(a)
. If one of the conditions

(i)

k=\bar{k}
and
B
is rational.

(ii)

k=\bar{k}
and
B
is a UFD.

(iii)

ML(B)=k

holds, then
B\in\mathcal{C}(k)
.

Proof.

Let
D\in{\rm LND}(B)
,
D\neq 0
, and set
A={\rm ker}\,D
.

Assume that (i) holds. The existence of a local slice for
D
implies
{\rm frac}(B)\cong{\rm frac}(A)^{(1)}
.
By hypothesis,
{\rm frac}(B)\cong k^{(2)}
, so by Lüroth’s Theorem we see that
{\rm frac}(A)\cong k^{(1)}
. In addition,
A
is normal (since
B
is normal) and
A^{*}=k^{*}
.
Therefore,
{\rm Spec}(A)
is a smooth rational affine curve with trivial units, i.e.,
{\rm Spec}(A)\cong\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}
.

Assume that condition (ii) holds. Then
A
is a UFD; see
[
16
]
, Lemma 2.8. Since
\dim A=1
and
A^{*}=k^{*}
, it follows that
A\cong k^{[1]}
; see

[
16
]
, Lemma 2.9.

Assume that condition (iii) holds. Then
A\cong k^{[1]}
; see Lemma 2.14 of
[
22
]
.
∎

Lemma 9.2
.

Assume that
k=\bar{k}
and let
B_{1}\in\mathcal{C}(k)
. If
B_{2}
is a
k
-domain such that
B_{1}^{[1]}\cong_{k}B_{2}^{[1]}
then
B_{2}\in\mathcal{C}(k)
.

Proof.

The hypotheses imply that
B_{2}
is a normal affine
k
-domain,
\dim B_{2}=2
, and
B_{2}^{*}=k^{*}
. If
ML(B_{2})=B_{2}
then
ML(B_{2}^{[1]})=B_{2}
(see
[
16
]
, Theorem 2.24).
We thus have

B_{2}=ML(B_{2}^{[1]})=ML(B_{1}^{[1]})\subseteq ML(B_{1})\implies 2=\dim B_{2}\leq\dim ML(B_{1})\leq 1

which is a contradiction. Therefore,
ML(B_{2})\neq B_{2}
and
B_{2}
satisfies condition (a).

For condition (b), let
D\in{\rm LND}(B_{2})
be given,
D\neq 0
.
Since
B_{1}
is rational,
B_{1}^{[1]}=B_{2}^{[1]}
is rational. By Lüroth’s Theorem we see that
{\rm ker}\,D
is rational.
So
{\rm Spec}({\rm ker}\,D)
is a normal (smooth) rational affine curve with trivial units, which means
{\rm Spec}({\rm ker}\,D)\cong\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}
and
{\rm ker}\,D\cong_{k}k^{[1]}
.
∎

Lemma 9.3
.

Suppose that

(a)

Given

.

Proof.

Part (a). Set

. By hypothesis, there exists

such that

.
We may thus assume, with no loss of generality, that

. By
[

]
, Principle 12, there exist

such that
aD=bE
. We may assume that
\gcd_{A}(a,b)=1
.
Let
f,g\in A
be such that
af+bg=1
. We have:

gaD=gbE=(1-af)E\implies E=a(gD-fE)

Since
E
is irreducible as a derivation it follows that
a\in k^{*}
. Irreducibility of
D
now implies
b\in k^{*}
. So
E=cD
for some
c\in k^{*}
.

Part (b). Let
B=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{d}
be a nontrivial
\mathbb{Z}
-grading and let
\mathbb{G}_{m}\to{\rm Aut}_{k}(B)
,
t\to\lambda_{t}
, be the induced

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action. There exists homogeneous
D\in{\rm ILND}(B)
; see
[
16
]
, Principle 15 and Theorem 2.15.
If
d=\deg D
then
\lambda_{t}D\lambda_{t}^{-1}=t^{d}D
for all
t\in k^{*}
. Since
k=\bar{k}
we see that
D
and
cD
are conjugate for all
c\in k^{*}
.
Combined with part (a), this proves part (b).
∎

Assume that

Following is an example for the torus

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle U^{\prime}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle x^{2}z=(1+x)y^{2}-1

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle x^{n}z=p(y)

\displaystyle V_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle x^{n}z=r(x)p(y)

\displaystyle W_{m}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle xz=y^{d_{m}}-1

[
18

and

10.2.
The exponential closure of a group of automorphisms

The automorphism groups for
k^{[2]}
,
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{R}
are structurally quite similar. A closer look at these similarities motivates the following definition.
Assume that
B
is an affine
k
-domain.

Definition 10.4
.

Let
H\subset{\rm Aut}_{k}(B)
be a subgroup.
The
exponential closure
of
H
is the subgroup generated by
H
and the set of exponential automorphisms:

\{\exp(fD)\in{\rm Aut}_{k}(B)\,|\,D\in{\rm LND}(B)\,,\,\exp(D)\in H\,,\,f\in{\rm frac}({\rm ker}\,D)\}

For each of the the rings
k^{[2]}
,
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{R}
, its automorphism group is the exponential closure of its linear subgroup:

{\rm Aut}_{k}(k^{[2]})
is the exponential closure of
GL_{2}(k)
,

{\rm Aut}_{k}(\mathfrak{D})
is the exponential closure of
O_{3}(k)
, and

{\rm Aut}_{k}(\mathfrak{R})
is the exponential closure of
PSL_{2}(k)
.

Linear
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on the polynomial ring
k^{[n]}
are in bijective correspondence with
SL_{2}
-modules of dimension
n
; see
[
16
]
, Proposition 6.3.
For
k^{[2]}
, there is only one nontrivial
SL_{2}
-module, namely,
\mathbb{V}_{1}(k)
, so all linear
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
k^{[2]}
are conjugate by a linear automorphism.
For
k[x,y,z]=k^{[3]}
there are two distinct possibilities,
\mathbb{V}_{2}(k)
and
\mathbb{V}_{0}(k)\oplus\mathbb{V}_{1}(k)
.
The associated locally nilpotent derivations are
D_{2}=x\partial_{y}+y\partial_{z}
and
D_{0}+D_{1}=x\partial_{y}
.
The exponential closure of
GL_{3}(k)
in
{\rm Aut}_{k}(k^{[3]})
thus contains every triangular automorphism, and therefore contains the tame subgroup, and also contains
non-tame automorphisms such as the Nagata automorphism
\exp(fD_{2})
,
f=2xz-y^{2}
. The homogeneous
(2,5)
derivation
D
of
k^{[3]}
is irreducible, and it seems unlikely that
\exp(D)
belongs
to the exponential closure of
GL_{3}(k)
; see
[
16
]
, Section 5.4.

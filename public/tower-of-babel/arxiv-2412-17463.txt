Solar system tests in covariant f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity
Wenyi Wang, Kun Hu, Taishi Katsuragawa

Abstract
We study the Solar System constraints on covariant f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity. The covariant f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) theory is described by the metric and affine connection, where both the torsion and curvature vanish. Considering a model including a higher nonmetricity-scalar correction, f ⁡ ( Q ) = Q + α ​ Q n − 2 ​ Λ f(Q)=Q+\alpha Q^{n}-2\Lambda , we derive static and spherically symmetric solutions, which represent the Schwarzschild-de Sitter solution with higher-order corrections, for two different ansatz of the affine connection. On the obtained spacetime solutions, we investigate the perihelion precession, light deflection, Shapiro delay, Cassini constraint, and gravitational redshift in the f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity. We place bounds on the parameter α \alpha with n = 2 , 3 n=2,3 in our model of f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity, using various observational data in the Solar System.

II
The Static and spherically symmetric Solutions

This section briefly reviews the basics of the
f(Q)
gravity, and we derive the two static and spherically symmetric solutions for two different affine connections.

f(Q)
gravity is characterized by the following action
[
40
]

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int~d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}f(Q)+S_{matter}\,.

(1)

g
is the determinant of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
,
and
f(Q)
is an arbitrary function of the nonmetricity scalar
Q
.
Nonmetricity tensor
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}
is defined by the metric and affine connection
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}&\equiv\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}\\
&=\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\ \alpha\mu}g_{\lambda\nu}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\ \alpha\nu}g_{\mu\lambda}\,.\end{split}

(2)

Nonmetricity scalar
Q
is defined as

\displaystyle Q=-Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}P^{\alpha\mu\nu}\,,

(3)

where
P^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}
is called the nonmetricity conjugate,

\displaystyle P^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}Q^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{(\mu\ \nu)}^{\ \ \alpha}+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q^{\alpha}-\tilde{Q}^{\alpha}\right)g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\delta^{\alpha}_{\ (\mu}Q_{\nu)}\,.

(4)

Q_{\alpha}\equiv Q_{\alpha\ \mu}^{\ \mu}
and
\tilde{Q}_{\alpha}\equiv Q^{\mu}_{\ \alpha\mu}
are traces of the nonmetricity tensor.

Varying Eq. (
1
) with respect to the metric and connection, respectively, we obtain the field equations of
f(Q)
gravity:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}\right)+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}f+f_{Q}\left(P_{\mu\alpha\beta}Q_{\nu}^{\ \alpha\beta}-2Q_{\alpha\beta\mu}P^{\alpha\beta}_{\ \ \ \nu}\right)+8\pi T_{\mu\nu}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\mu\nu}_{\ \ \alpha}\right)\,,

(6)

where we denote the derivative of
f(Q)
with respect to
Q
by
f_{Q}\equiv\partial_{Q}f(Q)
.
Ref.
[
28
]
has shown that using the symmetry reduction of metric and connection imposed by the torsionless and stationary condition, we obtain the following expressions in the spherical coordinate system
(t,r,\theta,\phi)
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{r}g_{tt}&=-\frac{g_{tt}\left[2f_{Q}+g_{rr}\left(Qr^{2}f_{Q}-2f_{Q}-fr^{2}\right)\right]}{2rf_{Q}}+\frac{g_{tt}\left[r^{2}-g_{rr}(\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta})^{2}\right]}{\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}rf_{Q}}f_{QQ}\partial_{r}Q\,,\end{split}

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{r}g_{rr}&=\frac{g_{rr}\left[2f_{Q}+g_{rr}\left(Qr^{2}f_{Q}-2f_{Q}-fr^{2}\right)\right]}{2rf_{Q}}+\frac{g_{rr}\left[r^{2}+g_{rr}(\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta})^{2}+2r\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}\right]}{\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}rf_{Q}}f_{QQ}\partial_{r}Q\,,\end{split}

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q&=-\frac{1}{r^{2}(\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta})^{2}g_{tt}g_{rr}^{2}}\\
&\qquad\times\Big\{\partial_{r}g_{tt}\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}g_{rr}\left[g_{rr}(\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta})^{2}+r(r+2\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta})\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad+g_{tt}\left[-2g_{rr}^{2}(\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta})^{3}\Gamma^{r}_{\ rr}-r^{2}\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}\partial_{r}g_{rr}\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad+g_{tt}g_{rr}\left[2r^{2}+4r\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}+2(\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta})^{2}+2r^{2}\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}\Gamma^{r}_{\ rr}+(\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta})^{3}\partial_{r}g_{rr}\right]\Big\}\,,\end{split}

(9)

where
f_{QQ}\equiv\partial_{QQ}f(Q)
.

The only nonvanishing component of affine connection in Eqs. (
7
)-(
9
) is
\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}
, which satisfies the following differential relation,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}=-1-\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}\Gamma^{r}_{\ rr}\,.

(10)

Regarding the metric components, the line element for a generic static and spherically symmetric spacetime can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-A(r)dt^{2}+B(r)dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,,

(11)

where the
A(r)=-g_{tt}
and
B(r)=g_{rr}
and
d\Omega^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}
.
The nonvanishing component of affine connection the
\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}
can be choose as (more details see Ref.
[
28
]
)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}=\pm\frac{r}{\sqrt{B(r)}}\,.

(12)

In this work, we consider a Lagrangian in the form of
f(Q)=Q+\alpha Q^{n}-2\Lambda
for
n=2,3
.
The
\Lambda
represents the cosmological constant, and
\alpha
is a small constant in our model.
Using the perturbative treatment of the high-order correction of the nonmetricity, we can suppose that the correction to GR or STGR will lead to deviations characterized by
\alpha
from the ordinary Schwarzschild-de Sitter solutions:

\displaystyle A(r)

\displaystyle=g_{tt}^{(0)}+\alpha g_{tt}^{(1)}\,,

(13)

\displaystyle B(r)

\displaystyle=g_{rr}^{(0)}+\alpha g_{rr}^{(1)}\,.

(14)

g_{tt}^{0}
and
g_{rr}^{0}
are ‌corresponding to components of the metric of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter solution in weak-gravity limit

\displaystyle g_{tt}^{(0)}=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{1}{3}\Lambda r^{2}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle g_{rr}^{(0)}=1+\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{1}{3}\Lambda r^{2}\,.

(16)

Employing Eq. (
12
), we solve Eqs. (
7
)-(
10
) and derive the metric components
A(r)
and
B(r)
. Hereafter, the case
\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}=-r/\sqrt{B(r)}
will be referred to as Case I, and another
\Gamma^{r}_{\ \theta\theta}=+r/\sqrt{B(r)}
as Case II.

In Case I, we obtain the following solutions:

\displaystyle A(r)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{1}{3}\Lambda r^{2}-\alpha\cdot\frac{(-1)^{n}2^{n}nM^{2n-1}}{(4n-3)r^{4n-3}}\,,

(17)

\displaystyle B(r)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{1}{3}\Lambda r^{2}+\alpha\cdot\frac{(-1)^{n}2^{n}nM^{2n-1}}{r^{4n-3}}\,,

(18)

and the nonmetricity scalar is given by

\displaystyle Q=\frac{2M^{2}}{r^{4}}+\alpha\cdot\frac{(-1)^{n}2^{n+1}nM^{2}}{r^{4n}}\,.

(19)

In Case II,

\displaystyle A(r)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{1}{3}\Lambda r^{2}-\alpha\cdot\frac{2^{3n-1}}{(2n-3)r^{2n-2}}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle B(r)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{1}{3}\Lambda r^{2}+\alpha\cdot\frac{2^{3n-1}\left(2n^{2}-3n+1\right)}{(2n-3)r^{2n-2}}\,.

(21)

and

\displaystyle Q=\frac{8}{r^{2}}-\alpha\cdot\frac{2^{3n+1}(n-1)}{r^{2n}}\,.

(22)

III.1
Perihelion Precession

We investigate the perihelion precession.
We begin with the general form of the static and spherically symmetric solution in
f(Q)
gravity written as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}A(r)&=1-a_{1}(r)\frac{M}{r}+a_{2}(r)\frac{M^{2}}{r^{2}}\,,\\
B(r)&=1+b_{1}(r)\frac{M}{r}+b_{2}(r)\frac{M^{2}}{r^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(23)

For the metric given by Eq. (
11
),
the normalization condition for four-velocity
u^{\mu}
,
g_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=-\eta
, can be written as

\displaystyle\eta=A(r)\>\left(\frac{dt}{d\lambda}\right)^{2}-B(r)\>\left(\frac{dr}{d\lambda}\right)^{2}-r^{2}\>\left(\frac{d\phi}{d\lambda}\right)^{2}\,.

(24)

where
\lambda
is the affine parameter.
For a timeline (
\eta=1
) test particle, Eq. (
24
) gives

\displaystyle 1=A(r)\>\left(\frac{dt}{d\tau}\right)^{2}-B(r)\>\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^{2}-r^{2}\>\left(\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}\right)^{2}\,.

(25)

For the static and spherically symmetric spacetime (
11
),
the metric is invariant under time translation and
SO(3)
spatial rotation,
which gives two kinds of Killing vectors.
One is the timelike Killing vector
\mathring{\xi}^{\mu}=\left(1,0,0,0\right)
, and another is the space rotational Killing vector
\bar{\xi}_{\mu}=\left(0,0,0,r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right)
.
Considering the equatorial movement of the test particle, i.e.
\theta=\pi/2
,
we can define two kinds of conserved quantities
by using the fact that
\xi_{\mu}u^{\mu}=\text{const.}
along the geodesics:

\displaystyle E=A(r)\frac{dt}{d\tau}\,,\quad L=r^{2}\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}\,.

(26)

E
and
L
are interpreted as the total energy and angular momentum per unit mass of the particle measured by the observer from infinity, respectively.
Upon the use of the above formula, we can rewrite Eq. (
25
) in terms of the conserved quantities as follows:

\displaystyle 1=\frac{E^{2}}{A(r)}-\frac{B(r)L^{2}}{r^{4}}\left(\frac{dr}{d\phi}\right)^{2}-\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\,.

(27)

Using Eq. (
27
), we find the angular distance between the perihelion
r_{-}
and aphelion
r_{+}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi(r_{+})-\phi(r_{-})&=\int_{\phi(r_{-})}^{\phi(r_{+})}d\phi\\
&=\int_{r_{-}}^{r_{+}}\frac{B(r)^{1/2}}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{E^{2}}{L^{2}A(r)}-\frac{1}{L^{2}}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}\>dr\,.\end{split}

(28)

This is followed by the total perihelion precession for per orbit

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=2|\phi(r_{+})-\phi(r_{-})|-2\pi\,.

(29)

Using the fact that
dr/d\phi
vanishes at
r_{-}
and
r_{+}
, which puts the Eq. (
27
) into the following form

\displaystyle 1=\frac{E^{2}}{A(r_{\pm})}-\frac{L^{2}}{r_{\pm}^{2}}\,,

(30)

we can derive the following values for the
E
and
L
:

\displaystyle E^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{A(r_{-})A(r_{+})\left(r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}\right)}{A(r_{-})r_{+}^{2}-A(r_{+})r_{-}^{2}}\,,

(31)

\displaystyle L^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{+}^{2}r_{-}^{2}\left(A(r_{-})-A(r_{+})\right)}{A(r_{+}){r_{-}}^{2}-A(r_{-}){r_{+}}^{2}}\,,

(32)

Plugging the Eqs. (
31
) (
32
) into Eq. (
28
), we obtain the following expression:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phi(r_{+})-\phi(r_{-})\\
&=\int_{r_{-}}^{r_{+}}dr\;\frac{B(r)^{1/2}}{r^{2}}\left\{\frac{A(r_{-})A(r_{+})\left(r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}\right)+A(r)\left[A(r_{+})r_{-}^{2}-A(r_{-})r_{+}^{2}\right]}{r_{+}^{2}r_{-}^{2}\left[A(r_{+})-A(r_{-})\right]A(r)}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right\}^{-1/2}\,.\end{split}

(33)

Note that the bracketed part in Eq. (
33
) vanishes at
r=r_{\pm}
.
Thus, we can rewrite the second square root in Eq. (
28
) as a quadratic function of
1/r
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{A(r_{-})A(r_{+})\left(r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}\right)+A(r)\left(A(r_{+})r_{-}^{2}-A(r_{-})r_{+}^{2}\right)}{r_{+}^{2}r_{-}^{2}\left(A(r_{+})-A(r_{-})\right)A(r)}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\\
&=C(r)\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{r_{+}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r_{-}}-\frac{1}{r}\right)\,.\end{split}

(34)

For the case of slightly elliptic orbits, we have
C(r)\simeq C=const.
.
To determine the constant
C
, it is convenient to introduce the new variable
u=1/r
, which also can be written as:

\displaystyle u\simeq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r_{+}}+\frac{1}{r_{-}}\right)=\frac{1}{a(1-e^{2})}\,,

(35)

where
a
is the semimajor axis and
e
is the orbital eccentricity.
Differentiating both sides of the Eq. (
34
) yields:

\displaystyle C=1-\frac{(u_{+}-u_{-})(u_{+}+u_{-})}{2\left[X(u_{+})-X(u_{-})\right]}X^{\prime\prime}(u)\,,

(36)

where
X(u)
is defined by
X(u)=A(u)^{-1}
, we also have

\displaystyle X(u_{+})-X(u_{-})\approx(u_{+}-u_{-})X^{\prime}(u)\,,

(37)

which simplifies Eq. (
36
) to

\displaystyle C=1-\frac{uX^{\prime\prime}(u)}{X^{\prime}(u)}\,.

(38)

With the help of Eq. (
34
), the expression of angular distance (
33
) can be simplified as

\displaystyle\phi(r_{+})-\phi(r_{-})\simeq\int_{r_{-}}^{r_{+}}\frac{B(r)^{1/2}}{r^{2}}\left[C\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{r_{+}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r_{-}}-\frac{1}{r}\right)\right]^{-1/2}\>dr\,.

(39)

Next, we perform the following change of the variable,

\displaystyle u=\frac{1}{2}(u_{+}+u_{-})+\frac{1}{2}(u_{+}-u_{-})\sin\psi\,,

(40)

while
\psi
is a new integral variable.
At the point of perihelion and aphelion, the corresponding values of
\psi
are taken to be
\psi=-\pi/2
and
\psi=\pi/2
.
Therefore, Eq. (
39
) can be written in terms of the new angular variable
\psi
:

\displaystyle\phi(r_{+})-\phi(r_{-})=C^{-1/2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}B(\psi)^{1/2}\>d\psi\,.

(41)

Utilizing the Eqs. (
23
) and (
38
),
we expand the constant
C
up to the first order in
a_{1}(u)
and
a_{2}(u)

, which is found to be:

\displaystyle\begin{split}C&\simeq 1-2Mua_{1}(u)-2Mu^{2}a_{1}^{\prime}(u)+2M^{2}u^{3}a_{2}^{\prime}(u)+4M^{2}u^{2}a_{2}(u)\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{a_{1}(u)}\left[2Mua_{2}(u)-2ua_{1}^{\prime}(u)+4Mu^{2}a_{2}^{\prime}(u)-u^{2}a_{1}^{\prime\prime}(u)+Mu^{3}a_{2}^{\prime\prime}(u)\right]\,.\end{split}

(42)

Similarly, we carry out the integral in Eq. (
41
) up to first order in
b_{1}(u)
and
b_{2}(u)
, and neglecting their products

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}B(\psi)^{1/2}\>d\psi\simeq\pi+\frac{1}{2}Mu\pi b_{1}(u)+\frac{1}{2}M^{2}u^{2}\pi b_{2}(u)\,.

(43)

Putting Eqs. (
43
) and (
42
) together and inserting them into Eqs. (
29
) and (

), we obtain the total perihelion precession for the case of slightly elliptic orbits:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta\phi&\approx\frac{1}{2}\pi u\Big\{4Ma_{1}(u)+2Mb_{1}(u)-8M^{2}ua_{2}(u)\\
&\qquad\qquad+2Mu\left[Mb_{2}(u)+2a_{1}^{\prime}(u)-2Mua_{2}^{\prime}(u)\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{a_{1}(u)}\left[2+Mub_{1}(u)+M^{2}u^{2}b_{2}(u)\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad\times\left[2Ma_{2}(u)-2a_{1}^{\prime}(u)+4Mua_{2}^{\prime}(u)-ua_{1}^{\prime\prime}(u)+Mu^{2}a_{2}^{\prime\prime}\right]\Big\}\,.\end{split}

(44)

To illustrate our results, we now apply the general expression Eq. (
44
) to the specific solutions we demonstrated in Sec II.
Reading

,
a_{2}(u)
,

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{1}(u)&=2\,,\quad a_{2}(u)=-\frac{\Lambda}{3M^{2}}u^{-4}-\alpha\cdot\frac{(-1)^{n}2^{n}n}{4n-3}\cdot M^{2n-3}u^{4n-5}\,,\\
b_{1}(u)&=2\,,\quad b_{2}(u)=\frac{\Lambda}{3M^{2}}u^{-4}+\alpha\cdot(-1)^{n}2^{n}n\cdot M^{2n-3}u^{4n-5}\,.\end{split}

Substituting

into Eq. (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta\phi&\approx\frac{6\pi M}{a(1-e^{2})}+\frac{\pi\Lambda a^{3}(1-e^{2})^{3}}{M}\\
&\qquad+\alpha\cdot\pi\left[(-2)^{n+1}+n(-1)^{n}2^{n+1}\right]n\cdot\frac{M^{2n-2}}{a^{4n-4}(1-e^{2})^{4n-4}}\\
&\qquad+\alpha\cdot\frac{\pi[3(-2)^{n}+(-2)^{n+1}n+(-1)^{n}n^{2}2^{n+3}]n}{4n-3}\cdot\frac{M^{2n-1}}{a^{4n-3}(1-e^{2})^{4n-3}}\,,\end{split}

When we choose

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta\phi^{\text{I}}_{\text{n=2}}&\approx\frac{6\pi M}{a(1-e^{2})}+\frac{\pi\Lambda a^{3}(1-e^{2})^{3}}{M}\\
&\qquad+\alpha\left[\frac{16\pi M^{2}}{a^{4}(1-e^{2})^{4}}+\frac{40\pi M^{3}}{a^{5}(1-e^{2})^{5}}\right]\,,\end{split}

(47)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta\phi^{\text{I}}_{\text{n=3}}&\approx\frac{6\pi M}{a(1-e^{2})}+\frac{\pi\Lambda a^{3}(1-e^{2})^{3}}{M}\\
&\qquad-\alpha\left[\frac{96\pi M^{4}}{a^{8}(1-e^{2})^{8}}+\frac{168\pi M^{5}}{a^{9}(1-e^{2})^{9}}\right]\,.\end{split}

V
Conclusions

f(Q)
gravity brings new life to GR by providing a novel interpretation of gravity from a different geometrical perspective.
By considering the framework of different affine connections in
f(Q)
gravity, interesting physical effects beyond GR may arise, which would provide new avenues of search for model proposals in
f(Q)
gravity.
In this work, we investigate several effects of
f(Q)
gravity on the astronomical observation and experiments conducted in the Solar System.
We focus on the model in the form
f(Q)=Q+\alpha Q^{n}-2\Lambda
, where the parameter
\alpha
embodies the deviation from GR, and the cosmological constant
\Lambda
is included for completeness.

First, applying the perturbative and weak-gravity approximation, we have derived the static and spherically symmetric solutions (
17
)-(
21
) in our model for two different cases of affine connections.
The outcome of spherical solutions shows that the deviation of the metric solution from the Schwarzschild-de Sitter turned out to be in the power of radial coordinate:
r^{3-4n}
in Case I and
r^{2-2n}
in Case II.
We have confirmed that for
n=2,3
, these deviations decrease as the radial coordinate
r
increases.

Next, using the obtained solutions, we have formulated paths for massive and massless particles to calculate the perihelion precession and light deflection in the Subs.
III.1
and
III.2
.
We have found that for Case II with
n=2
, the deviation from GR with respect to the light deflection is given at the order of
(M/b)^{3}
in Eq. (
76
).
The deflection angle in Case I with
n=2
deviates from GR prediction at the order of
(M/b)^{5}
, which is consistent results in Ref.
[
44
]
,
suggests that Case I deviates less from GR compared to those in Case II.

Finally, to obtain further constraints on
\alpha
,
we have developed our calculations in perihelion precession and light deflection and applied them to other experiments:
The calculation of Shapiro delay is presented in Subs.
III.3
; Cassini constraint in Subs.
III.4
; and the gravitational redshift in Subs.
III.5
.
We have explored the deviations from GR predictions for each type of experiment.
Our results in Eqs. (
87
)-(
89
),
Eq. (
92
)-(
95
),
Eq. (
98
)-(
99
)
show that the different contributions from
\alpha Q^{n}
emerge depending on the choice of the affine connection; for instance, different dependence on the impact parameter
b
and source mass
M
.
Nonetheless, concerning the magnitude of
\Lambda
is very tiny, it is possible to constrain the
\alpha
through experimental data.

In Sec.
IV
, we used the latest observational data to constrain the
\alpha
and obtained a range of values that satisfies each observation data set.
As we can see, for case II,
n=2,3
, the Gravitational redshift (
120
)-(
121
) give the best restrictions on
\alpha
, which the accuracy takes the order of magnitude
10^{-5}-10^{-4}\text{~km}^{2}
for
n=2
and
10^{2}\text{~km}^{4}
for
n=3
.
This is partially because the other tests are performed on a relatively large scale in the Solar System, yielding a negligible higher order term
{\cal O}(1/r^{n})
.
However, the gravitational redshift test is conducted in the vicinity of the Earth, thus avoiding this issue.

It is worth mentioning that for Case I with
n=3
, since the correction term is in power form
\sim Q^{3}
, we have found loose constraints on
\alpha
; for all experiments we considered, the upper bound of
\alpha
turns out to be enormous.
As we mentioned before, the higher order of
Q
implies that the contribution from this factor is extremely small.
Although there is a significant difference in magnitude between the values of
\alpha
for the two choices of
n
, it is mandatory to check the linear term of the nonmetricity scalar
Q
still dominant in the Lagrangian of our model
Q\gg\alpha Q^{n}
.
As an example, for case I, we consider the case of the perihelion precession of Mercury, utilize the obtained constraint
(

For case II, we use the obtained constraint (

When we take a relatively larger value for

Macrophage-MSCs Interplay in Orthodontic Bone Remodelling: Mechanics-Immunity-Stem Cell Axis
Authors: Mingzhu Chen, Yanjie Li, Jiangtian Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDuring orthodontic treatment, the balance of bone remodelling is dynamically regulated by macrophages and site-specific mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) through the mechanics-immunity-stem cell axis. Polarization of macrophages from the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype to the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype is driven by mechanical forces via mechanosensors such as the Piezo1 channel. Macrophage polarization is induced by MSCs through exosomes and cytokine feedback, establishing a positive feedback loop of 'inflammation resolution-bone regeneration'. Moreover, other immune cells such as neutrophils and T lymphocytes participate in modulating the microenvironment. The dynamic balance of bone remodelling is collectively maintained by crosstalk among key signalling pathways: Wnt/β-catenin for osteogenesis, RANKL/RANK for bone resorption, and PI3K-Akt for macrophage polarization. These insights offer actionable targets for developing adjunctive therapies to accelerate orthodontic bone remodelling and improve treatment efficiency. Therefore, understanding the crosstalk mechanism between macrophages and MSCs under mechanical force is of significant clinical importance for targeting this interaction axis to optimize orthodontic treatment and accelerate bone remodelling.Key words: Orthodontic bone remodelling, Macrophages, Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), Crosstalk

Introduction

Orthodontic treatment, a critical modality for correcting dentofacial deformities and restoring oral function and aesthetics, achieves physiological tooth movement by inducing alveolar bone remodelling through sustained mechanical force application.1, 2, 3, 4 With the growing societal demand for oral health and aesthetics, orthodontic therapy has gained widespread clinical adoption.5, 6, 7, 8, 9 However, challenges such as prolonged treatment duration, inefficient bone remodelling, and potential complications persist, making it imperative to investigate the molecular mechanisms of orthodontic bone remodelling to enhance therapeutic outcomes.10, 11, 12 Understanding these mechanisms not only benefits orthodontics but also provides insights into bone regeneration in broader contexts, such as orthopaedic surgery and craniofacial reconstruction.

Macrophages and MSCs are recognized as indispensable regulators of bone remodelling.13, 14, 15 As core immune cells, macrophages can be polarized into the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype (secreting TNF-α to inhibit osteogenesis) or the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype (secreting BMP-2 to promote regeneration)1,16 via mechanosensitive Piezo1 channels17 and microenvironmental signals such as TLR/interferon pathways.18,19 It is important to note that other immune cells, including neutrophils and T lymphocytes, also contribute to the immune microenvironment during bone remodelling. This temporal phenotypic transition (M0→M1→M2) is essential for coordinating local immune responses and intercellular communication.5 The primary MSCs involved in orthodontic tooth movement (OTM) are periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs) and site-specific alveolar bone MSCs, which derived from cranial neural crest cells/dental follicle cells during tooth development. They are induced to differentiate into osteoblasts under mechanical stimulation and are known to regulate macrophage polarization through paracrine networks.1,20 While findings from bone marrow-derived MSCs (BMSCs) are informative, they may not fully represent the context of periodontal tissues due to source heterogeneity. This bidirectional crosstalk plays a crucial role in bone tissue repair and regeneration during orthodontic bone remodelling.5,21

The sequential progression of the “mechanics-immunity-stem cell axis” can be described as follows: (1) Mechanical force application induces tissue strain; (2) Immune cells (e.g., macrophages) sense mechanical cues and polarize accordingly; (3) Polarized immune cells secrete cytokines that regulate MSC behaviour; (4) MSCs differentiate into osteoblasts or modulate immune responses via feedback loops5; Bone resorption and formation reach a new equilibrium, completing orthodontic bone remoulding.

The concept of the "mechanics-immunity-stem cell axis" provides a novel theoretical framework for elucidating the regulatory mechanisms of orthodontic bone remodelling. This theory proposes mechanical force as the initiating signal, which triggers macrophage polarization to modulate the biological behaviours of MSCs, establishing a dynamically balanced regulatory network among these 3 components.22,23 Elucidating the crosstalk mechanism between macrophages and MSCs in orthodontic bone remodelling and deciphering the regulatory patterns of the mechanics-immunity-stem cell axis not only enhances our understanding of the biological processes underlying orthodontic bone remodelling but also provides a critical theoretical basis for optimizing orthodontic treatment protocols and developing innovative therapeutic strategies. This review will summarize the roles of macrophages and MSCs in orthodontic bone remodelling, their mutual crosstalk mechanisms, and the regulatory network of the mechanics-immunity-stem cell axis, aiming to offer insights for both basic research and clinical practice in orthodontics.

Literature search strategy

A systematic search was conducted in PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus (2017-2025) using keywords related to orthodontic bone remodelling, macrophage polarization, mesenchymal stem cells, and relevant signalling pathways. Inclusion criteria encompassed English-language original studies and reviews investigating immune-stem cell crosstalk under mechanical loading. After duplicate removal and screening, 193 articles were selected for qualitative synthesis in this narrative review.

Biological basis of orthodontic bone remodelling

Orthodontic tooth movement is fundamentally mediated by a dynamic equilibrium between alveolar bone resorption and formation, processes that are precisely coordinated through the macrophage-osteoclast axis and MSC-osteoblast axis.24, 25, 26 The application of orthodontic forces induces distinct biological responses at pressure and tension sites: osteoclast-mediated bone resorption predominates on the pressure side, while osteoblast-driven bone formation is characteristic of the tension side.27,28 This spatial regulation of bone remodelling is achieved through synergistic crosstalk between osteoblasts and osteoclasts, which serves as the central regulatory mechanism for orthodontic tooth movement.

Mechanical loading at the pressure side establishes a pro-inflammatory microenvironment predominantly mediated by M1 macrophage activation. These polarized M1 macrophages release pro-osteoclastic cytokines, including tumour necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), and receptor activator of nuclear factor κB ligand (RANKL),29,30 which stimulate osteoclast precursor differentiation and activation. The RANKL/RANK/osteoprotegerin (OPG) signalling axis serves as the principal molecular mechanism governing osteoclastogenesis, coordinating the differentiation, maturation, and functional activity of osteoclasts to mediate bone resorption. Furthermore, mechanical stimulation through the Piezo1 mechanosensitive ion channel induces NLRP3 inflammasome assembly,31,32 thereby amplifying M1 macrophage polarization through a self-reinforcing feedback mechanism that potentiates bone resorptive activity.

In contrast to the pressure side, the tension side microenvironment is characterized by M2 macrophage accumulation, which facilitates mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) recruitment through paracrine secretion of bone morphogenetic protein-2 (BMP-2) and transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β).29,33 Mechanotransduction occurs via integrin-mediated extracellular matrix interactions, leading to activation of the focal adhesion kinase (FAK)-Yes-associated protein (YAP) signalling cascade. This mechanosensitive pathway subsequently initiates Wnt/β-catenin signalling,34,35 which promotes osteogenic differentiation of MSCs and enhances bone matrix deposition.

Notably, the temporal dynamics of macrophage polarization during orthodontic tooth movement reveal distinct phase-dependent characteristics. In the initial inflammatory phase (0-3 days post-force application), a rapid surge in M1 macrophage prevalence is observed, which orchestrates osteoclast activation primarily through upregulated expression of TNF-α and RANKL.36 During the subsequent repair phase (4-14 days), CCL2-mediated monocyte recruitment coincides with a cytokine profile shift characterized by increasing IL-4 and decreasing IL-1β concentrations, driving macrophage polarization toward the M2 phenotype. This transition is accompanied by a significant elevation in BMP-2 secretion.37,38 The final remodelling phase (initiating at >14 days) demonstrates TGF-β/SMAD signalling pathway activation,39,40 which markedly enhances MSC osteogenic differentiation. This phase is characterized by a 50% increase in collagen deposition relative to baseline levels and peak osteocalcin expression,37 indicating successful completion of the bone formation process.

Among mesenchymal stem cells involved in orthodontic bone remodelling, periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs) exhibit distinct mechanosensitive characteristics as tissue-specific progenitors. These cells reside within the periodontal ligament, a specialized connective tissue interposed between the tooth root cementum and the alveolar bone. These cells detect mechanical stimuli through ERK1/2 signalling pathway activation, subsequently undergoing directional differentiation into osteoblasts at tension sites.5 The osteogenic differentiation of PDLSCs has been shown to be specifically regulated by the sLZIP gene through targeted molecular mechanisms.41,42 In contrast, bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) predominantly utilize the TGF-β/BMP signalling cascade to facilitate bone mineralization while concurrently suppressing M1 macrophage-mediated inflammation via prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) secretion.29,33 A sophisticated bidirectional regulatory network exists between these MSC populations and macrophages: osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs is activated by BMP-2 secreted from M2 macrophages,29,43 while specialized CD301b⁺ macrophage subsets enhance bone remodelling through dynamic modulation of the local immune microenvironment.33,44

In summary, orthodontic bone remodelling represents a sophisticated mechanobiological process governed by the intricate interplay between mechanical stimuli and biological signalling networks. This process encompasses mechanosensitive responses of mesenchymal stem cells and macrophages, complex intercellular communication, tightly coupled osteoblast-osteoclast interactions, macrophage-mediated inflammatory modulation, and MSC-driven tissue regeneration.24,29,45 Future investigations should prioritize precise modulation of MSC behaviour,41,42 targeted manipulation of immune cell functions, and strategic regulation of critical signalling pathways.33,40,44 These approaches may yield novel therapeutic paradigms for optimizing orthodontic treatment efficacy through enhanced control of bone remodelling dynamics.

Crosstalk between macrophages and MSCs in orthodontic bone remodelling

The "Mechanics-Immunity-Regeneration" axis coordinates orthodontic bone remodelling through sophisticated interactions between macrophages and MSCs. As key immunomodulators in bone metabolism, macrophages shape the bone microenvironment by switching between M1 (secreting TNF-α/RANKL to promote resorption) and M2 (producing BMP-2/TGF-β to enhance formation) phenotypes.29,33,43 MSCs demonstrate dual functions, differentiating into osteoblasts to drive regeneration while reciprocally regulating macrophage polarization through paracrine signalling.20,46,47

Orthodontic forces initiate this cellular crosstalk via distinct mechanosensing pathways: macrophages detect mechanical signals through Piezo1 channels,31 while MSCs (especially PDLSCs) respond to tension via ERK1/2 signalling.5 Their coordination occurs through cytokine gradients, exosome transfer, and direct contact. This interaction critically balances bone remodelling - M2-derived BMP-2 activates MSC osteogenesis while M1-secreted RANKL promotes osteoclastogenesis.27,43

Important unresolved questions include: the real-time dynamics of pathway crosstalk under loading, the delivery efficiency of exosomal miRNAs,48 and inflammatory microenvironment interference.44,49 Addressing these may enable novel strategies like timed macrophage polarization or engineered MSC exosomes to optimize bone remodelling for precision orthodontics.

Mechanotransduction and regulation in macrophage-MSCs crosstalk

During orthodontic bone remodelling, mechanical stress drives dynamic bone tissue remodelling by regulating interactions between M1/M2 macrophages and mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs). As mechanosensitive immune cells, orthodontic forces directly activate and polarize macrophages in periodontal tissues, promoting their proliferation and participation in inflammatory microenvironment regulation.31 In the early stages of orthodontics, pressure may induce M1 polarization to initiate inflammation,50,51 while prolonged pressure promotes M1-to-M2 transition to accelerate bone remodelling.44 Pressure can upregulate pathways such as NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain-containing protein (NLRP) to promote M2 polarization of macrophages, thereby inhibiting pro-inflammatory factors and enhancing anti-inflammatory responses.32,52 Additionally, studies show that pressure significantly induces high expression of pro-inflammatory factors such as IL-1β, iNOS, and TNF-α in macrophages and upregulates pyroptosis-related markers like NLRP3, cleaved Caspase1, and N-GSDMD/GSDMD to regulate orthodontic bone remodelling.53 Tension influences macrophage adhesion and migration by modulating focal adhesion force nodes.54 Tension releases TGF-β1 through the Piezo1 channel to regulate macrophage polarization to M2, promoting MSCs osteogenic differentiation.17

As the most critical effector cells in bone remodelling, MSCs exhibit significant mechanosensitive characteristics under mechanical force stimulation.55 They sense mechanical stimuli through mechanosensors such as integrins and focal adhesions, then regulate the secretion of circRNA, miRNA, and exosomes to control MSCs' osteogenic differentiation.56, 57, 58 Mechanical stimulation shows a graded effect: low-intensity mechanotransduction promotes osteoprogenitor cell differentiation and activates signalling pathways like MAPK and β-catenin to regulate osteogenesis,55,59 while high-intensity stimulation may cause cell detachment or inhibit osteogenesis.60,61 The effects of mechanical stimulation may also be influenced by cell types62 and the inflammatory microenvironment.63

Cyclic tensile stress (CTS) is the most common form of mechanical stimulation. Studies show that specific parameters of tensile force in 2500 microstrain and 0.5 Hz can significantly promote MSCs osteogenic differentiation, with mechanisms involving YAP nuclear translocation mediated by the integrin αVβ3-actin filament axis and activation of downstream osteogenesis-related genes.34,35 Cytoskeletal reorganization plays a key role in tension-induced osteogenic differentiation.64,65 CTS promotes BMSCs osteogenic differentiation through Wnt/β-catenin pathways,66,67 while PDLSCs enhance osteogenic capacity via ERK/JNK signalling under CTS.68 CTS increases lactic acid production, thereby promoting proliferation, migration, and osteogenic differentiation of alveolar bone marrow MSCs (ABMMCs).69 Besides tensile stress, compressive stress, and fluid shear stress (FSS) also promote BMSCs osteogenesis, where physiological cyclic hydrostatic pressure activates osteogenesis-related pathways via P2 × 7/P2Y purinergic receptors, and the synergistic effect of fluid shear stress and hydrostatic pressure further optimizes osteogenic outcomes.70, 71, 72 Pressure activates the MEK/ERK pathway through mechanosensitive ion channels Piezo1 and focal adhesion kinase (FAK), increasing RANKL expression in osteoblasts to induce osteoclast precursor differentiation.73,74

Mechanical force coordinately drives bone remodelling by regulating macrophage polarization and MSCs osteogenic differentiation. In immunomodulation, compressive force promotes macrophage polarization from pro-inflammatory M1 to anti-inflammatory M2 via activating the Piezo1 channel, TRIM25-mediated TREM1 degradation, and ATF3/WNT4 signalling pathway, thereby secreting osteogenic factors like BMP2 and TGF-β1 while inhibiting inflammatory mediators like IL-1β to form a pro-regenerative microenvironment.31,52,75, 76, 77, 78 In MSCs regulation, mechanical tension force directly enhances osteogenic differentiation via pathways like Wnt3a/GSK-3β/β-catenin,66,79 while M2 macrophages indirectly activate MSCs osteogenic potential through exosomes or cytokines.29,80,81 Their synergistic effect in bone repair is significant: compressive force accelerates M2 polarization to alleviate macrophage inhibition of osteogenesis and promotes MC3T3 cell mineralization.52 The dual mechanisms of immune microenvironment remodelling and stem cell differentiation regulation provide key theoretical bases for bone remodelling.66,75,82

Paracrine dialogue between macrophages and MSCs: mutual regulation by cytokines

During orthodontic bone remodelling, the interaction between M1/M2 macrophages and MSCs mediated by paracrine factors plays a core regulatory role, involving mechanisms such as inflammatory microenvironment regulation, osteoblast-osteoclast differentiation control, and tissue repair and remodelling (Figure 1).

Paracrine dialogue between macrophages and MSCs under orthodontic force: mutual regulation of cytokines.

The crosstalk between M1 macrophages and MSCs via paracrine cytokines and signalling molecules forms a multi-layered regulatory network. MSCs can induce M1 macrophages to switch to an anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype through paracrine effects, while M1 macrophages can feedback-enhance the expression of immunomodulatory factors in hAMSCs.83 Periodic photothermal therapy (PTT) accelerates M1-to-M2 transformation by activating the PI3K-Akt1 signalling pathway, with paracrine factors secreted by M2 macrophages further acting on MSCs.84 MSCs shift the M1/M2 balance toward the M2 phenotype via paracrine effects, thereby promoting the resolution of inflammatory diseases.46 Human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells (HUCMSCs) promote wound healing by reducing inflammatory factors IL-6 and TNF-α secretion in macrophages,85 while BMMSCs-derived exosomes accelerate the synergy between macrophages and stem cells in skin wound repair.86,87 Additionally, CD54 (Intercellular Adhesion Molecule-1, ICAM-1) plays a functional role in non-classical interactions between M1 macrophages and MSCs, suggesting that adhesion molecules participate in immune microenvironment regulation.88

In the early stage of bone repair, the specific roles of coexisting M1 macrophages and MSCs are gradually being elucidated. M1 may activate the CCR5 signalling pathway in MSCs by secreting CCL5, influencing their differentiation or repair functions.39,43 MSCs-derived exosomes inhibit pro-inflammatory activity of IL-10, TGF-β of M1 and promote tissue repair by carrying growth factors and nucleic acids.48,89 Cytokines secreted by MSCs, such as IL-6, Hedgehog, and Wnt, directly interact with macrophages to positively regulate tumour microenvironments or injury repair processes.90 In the spatiotemporal dynamic regulation of bone regeneration, osteogenic factors like BMP2 secreted by M2 macrophages stimulate MSCs differentiation into osteoblasts, while M1 macrophages transiently inhibit this process by releasing TNF-α and other signalling molecules during the inflammatory phase.43,91,92 The interaction between M1 macrophages and MSCs relies on cytokines like IL-10 and CCL5, through PI3K-Akt1/STAT3 pathways, and exosome-mediated paracrine networks to jointly regulate the balance between inflammatory responses and tissue repair,93, 94, 95 providing potential targets for orthodontic bone remodelling.90,96

M2 macrophages activate the SMAD signalling pathway in MSCs by secreting anti-inflammatory factor IL-10 and pro-regenerative factors TGF-β and bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs), enhancing MSCs osteogenic potential,43,91 while reducing the release of inflammatory factors around MSCs through paracrine effects to improve the injury repair microenvironment.85 MSCs polarize pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages into anti-inflammatory M2 phenotypes by secreting cytokines (IL-6, PGE2) and extracellular vesicles (EVs).20,46,47 Among these, exosome-carried miR-27a-3p significantly promotes the transformation of bone marrow-derived macrophages to the M2 phenotype.47 The 2 cell types form a bidirectional feedback regulatory network via cytokines like CCL5 and IL-10: M1 macrophages upregulate IL-10 secretion under the action of MSCs, thereby activating the STAT3 pathway in MSCs to enhance immunomodulatory functions.46,83 The PI3K-Akt pathway is involved in MSCs-induced M1-to-M2 transition,84 while the Wnt/β-catenin pathway may mediate M2 macrophages’ promotion of MSCs osteogenic differentiation.43,97

During trauma repair and bone regeneration, MSCs regulate macrophage polarization via paracrine effects to promote M2-dominated regenerative healing.47 M2 macrophages and MSCs synergistically enhance osteogenesis through signal axes such as BMP2/SMAD and CCL5/CCR5.39,43,91 Additionally, MSCs-derived extracellular matrix (ECM) stabilizes the M2 phenotype and inhibits pyroptosis.36 Although the mechanisms of specific paracrine molecules like exosomal miRNAs require further study,20,48 and the in vivo microenvironment may influence interaction outcomes,90,98 their core role in tissue repair has been widely confirmed.

Under orthodontic loading, macrophage polarization demonstrates distinct temporal dynamics (Figure 2). Initially, M1 macrophages secrete pro-inflammatory factors TNF-α and IL-1β, activating local immune responses and establishing a microenvironment conducive to the initiation of bone remodelling and resorption.31,50,78 Simultaneously, M1-derived oncostatin M (OSM) promotes osteogenic differentiation of MSC.99 Local hypoxia and mechanical strain induced by orthodontic forces directly enhance macrophage mechanosensitivity, driving a shift toward the M2 phenotype.50,100 Over time, compressive force elevates local IL-4 while reducing IL-1β levels, further accelerating M2 polarization, a process also modulated by histone H3 acetylation.50,52 Polarized M2 macrophages secrete anti-inflammatory mediators such as IL-10 and VEGF, which suppress excessive inflammation and promote tissue repair, angiogenesis, and MSC osteogenesis via factors including SDF-1 and TGF-β.66,101, 102, 103 Additionally, M2-secreted BMPs directly activate osteogenic pathways in bone marrow-derived MSCs,104 and the CD301b+ M2 subset enhances bone remodelling by improving the periodontal microenvironment.44

MSCs promote macrophage polarization to the M2 phenotype via exosomes or secreted cytokines like IL-4, IL-10, IL-13, TGF-β, forming a positive feedback loop to alleviate inflammation105, 106, 107 (Figure 3). For example, MSCs conditioned medium treated with berberine (BBR-HB-CM) significantly inhibits M1 polarization, enhances M2 polarization, and effectively relieves orthodontic-related pulp inflammation.108, 109, 110, 111 MSCs secrete Galectin-1 and Oncostatin M (OSM) to inhibit M1 polarization and promote M2 polarization.99,112 MSCs also accelerate M1-to-M2 transition by activating the PI3K-Akt signalling pathway in macrophages, optimizing the bone remodelling microenvironment.84,113 Animal studies in palatal expansion models show a significant positive correlation between M2 macrophage numbers and bone formation, with macrophage depletion impairing osteogenic function.114

Positive feedback loop: the interactions between MSCs and macrophages in inflammation regulation.

The paracrine crosstalk between M1/M2 macrophages and MSCs, through dynamically balancing inflammation and repair, precisely regulating osteogenic differentiation, and integrating mechanical force responses, represents the core mechanism of orthodontic bone remodelling. Regulatory strategies based on this mechanism hold broad clinical promise: exogenous MSCs or their exosomes promote M2 polarization to effectively reduce inflammatory injury.115, 116, 117, 118, 119, 120, 121 Macrophage-derived exosomes enhance MSCs-driven bone regeneration.29,80,92,122, 123, 124 Fusing exosomes from M2 macrophages and bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells can target and promote osteogenesis, significantly improving orthodontic bone remodelling efficiency.125 These findings further confirm that macrophage-MSCs paracrine crosstalk is a potential therapeutic target for accelerating orthodontic bone remodelling (Figure 4).

Crosstalk of signalling pathways: molecular mechanisms of macrophage-MSCs interaction

During orthodontic bone remodelling, the crosstalk between M1/M2 macrophages and MSCs plays a critical regulatory role via key signalling pathways. Among these, PI3K-Akt, Wnt/β-catenin, and TGF-β pathways are central to immune modulation and osteogenesis. M2 polarization, in particular, promotes bone formation and inflammation resolution, providing a basis for developing immunomodulatory adjuncts in orthodontics.126, 127, 128 For example, periodic photothermal therapy (PTT) accelerates the shift from M1 to M2 by activating PI3K-Akt1, which enhances MSC osteogenesis via M2-derived paracrine signals.84 Moreover, MSCs can modulate macrophage polarization by influencing TGF-β and STAT3/BCL-2 pathways, thus improving the inflammatory microenvironment and supporting bone regeneration during orthodontic remodelling.113,129

Wnt/β-catenin Pathway: M2 macrophages secrete Wnt3a to activate the canonical Wnt pathway in MSCs, promoting the phosphorylation of GSK-3β and stabilizing β-catenin, which in turn upregulates RUNX2 and Osterix (Osx) expression, driving the osteogenic differentiation of MSCs.43,143, 131,132 This pathway is closely related to mechanical force responses; the orthodontic tension side PDLSCs promote bone formation through the Wnt5a/β-catenin signalling pathway.133, 134, 135 Furthermore, Wnt signalling contributes to the integration of mechanical stimuli. For example, under orthodontic force, nitric oxide secreted by PDLSCs stabilizes β-catenin by suppressing GSK-3β activity, thereby amplifying osteogenesis on the tension side.133,136 Wnt signalling also exhibits crosstalk with other pathways to cooperatively regulate cellular functions.137

PI3K-Akt Pathway: The PI3K-Akt pathway serves as a central hub integrating immune modulation and osteogenesis during orthodontic bone remodelling. MSCs activate this pathway in macrophages through CD74/MIF receptor signalling via secreted extracellular matrix, inducing M2 polarization and creating a pro-regenerative microenvironment.85 Polarized M2 macrophages subsequently secrete IL-10 and TGF-β, which suppress NF-κB-mediated inflammatory responses through Akt phosphorylation, thereby sustaining an anti-inflammatory milieu favorable for bone formation.138,139 Concurrently, orthodontic mechanical forces activate NF-κB via p38/MAPK signalling, inducing local CCL2 release that recruits CCR2+ macrophages to periodontal tissues. These recruited macrophages secrete PDGF-BB and TGF-β1 to promote MSC osteogenic differentiation, while pressure-side macrophages secrete RANKL to activate osteoclastogenesis via NF-κB and MAPK pathways, balancing resorption and formation.140 Additionally, CCL2 facilitates osteoclast precursor fusion through the ERK1/2-MMP9 pathway and enables direct macrophage-MSC contact, while mitochondrial transfer between these cells regulates MSC metabolism and osteogenic potential.141, 142, 143, 144 Thus, the PI3K-Akt pathway orchestrates a coordinated network of immune-stem cell interactions that dynamically balance bone resorption and formation under mechanical force.

STAT Signalling Network: PDLSCs produce hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) under mechanical stimulation, which directly modifies STAT1 through sulfation, enhancing its phosphorylation. p-STAT1 then enters the nucleus to upregulate IRF1 and NF-κB, driving macrophages to polarize towards M1 and secrete TNF-α and RANKL, activating osteoclast differentiation and promoting early inflammatory bone resorption. In the repair phase, PDGF-BB derived from M1 indirectly stimulates PDLSCs' osteogenic differentiation, collectively regulating orthodontic bone remodelling.145 The MIF pathway inhibits the NF-κB inflammatory response while upregulating STAT3 phosphorylation, promoting M2 polarization.85,130 Polarized M2 macrophages enhance the osteogenic differentiation of MSCs through the YAP/BMP2 pathway, forming a cascade response of "mechanical force-macrophage polarization-osteogenesis."130 STAT3 also regulates MSC survival via BCL-2.138

TGF-β/Smad Pathway: MSCs secrete TGF-β1 to induce macrophages to polarize towards the M2 phenotype, and the polarized M2 macrophages promote BMP2 secretion through Smad2/3 phosphorylation.138,139 BMP2 binds to the BMPR of MSCs, activating Smad1/5/8, which associates with the Runx2 promoter to enhance MSC osteogenic differentiation.130

YAP/BMP2 Mechanical Sensing Axis: Tensile force inhibits the Hippo pathway in MSCs and reduces phosphorylation at the S127/S397 sites of YAP, promoting YAP nuclear translocation.146,147 YAP translocation to the nucleus directly upregulates BMP2 expression, which enhances MSC osteogenesis through both Smad-Runx2 and Wnt/β-catenin pathways.130,131 Concurrently, YAP co-localizes with β-catenin in the nucleus to regulate the target gene XBP1,148 and the absence of β-catenin diminishes YAP-mediated osteogenesis.149 YAP maintains Wnt signal homeostasis by inhibiting the nuclear translocation of DVL3.150 Tensile force also enhances macrophage mechanosensing via the Piezo1-YAP axis.82 Periodic tensile force activates the macrophage-YAP/BMP2 axis to jointly stimulate the Smad-Runx2 and Wnt/β-catenin pathways, regulating MSC osteogenic differentiation involved in orthodontic bone remodelling.

PERK-eIF2α-ATF4 Pathway: Cyclic tensile force significantly triggers the expression of endoplasmic reticulum stress markers (GRP78, CHOP) in hPDLCs. Endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) promotes osteogenic differentiation through the PERK-eIF2α-ATF4 cascade, potentially activated by macrophage-derived inflammatory factor TNF-α.151

MHC-II signalling pathway: M1 and M2 macrophages interact significantly in the MHC-II signalling pathway, and M1 macrophages can polarize to M2 through the MHC-II pathway, accompanied by down-regulation of CXCL9 expression.152 This pathway may influence the inflammatory response during orthodontic tooth movement by modulating the immune microenvironment (Table 1).

Impact of macrophage-MSCs crosstalk on orthodontic bone remodelling outcomes

The dynamic crosstalk between macrophages and MSCs serves as a core regulatory network in orthodontic tooth movement, coordinating bone resorption and formation to achieve physiological bone remodelling. However, dysregulation of this interactive network can lead to imbalanced bone remodelling, resulting in adverse clinical outcomes such as inefficient tooth movement, root resorption, or alveolar bone loss. A deeper understanding of the differences in mechanisms governing this crosstalk under normal force response versus pathological conditions is critical for optimizing orthodontic force systems, preventing complications, and enhancing treatment stability.

Role of crosstalk in normal bone remodelling

During normal orthodontic bone remodelling, macrophages and mesenchymal stem cells coordinately regulate bone resorption on the pressure side and bone formation on the tension side through multi-dimensional, precise crosstalk, ensuring orderly tooth movement and physiological remodelling of the alveolar bone. Upon application of orthodontic force, microenvironmental changes on the pressure side of the periodontal ligament induce macrophage polarization to the M1 phenotype, secreting pro-inflammatory factors such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6. These factors not only directly activate osteoclast precursor cells, inducing their differentiation into mature osteoclasts and enhancing bone resorption activity, but also act on mesenchymal stem cells to promote chemokine secretion, recruiting more osteoclast precursors to the pressure side.29,50,154,155 Meanwhile, mechanical stretching on the tension side gradually polarizes macrophages to the M2 phenotype, releasing anti-inflammatory factors such as interleukin-10 (IL-10) and transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), creating a favourable osteogenic microenvironment for MSCs.156, 157, 158, 159, 160 Under the action of growth factors like TGF-β, mesenchymal stem cells activate key signalling pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin and BMP, initiating osteogenic differentiation, expressing osteogenic transcription factors like Runx2 and Osterix, and synthesizing and secreting bone matrix components to complete new bone formation.161, 162, 163 Additionally, exosome-mediated communication between macrophages and mesenchymal stem cells further reinforces this synergistic effect. Macrophage-derived exosomes carrying regulatory molecules such as miRNAs promote osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells,155,164 while MSCs-secreted exosomes regulate macrophage polarization states to maintain immune microenvironment homeostasis, thereby achieving a dynamic balance between pressure-side bone resorption and tension-side bone formation and ensuring smooth orthodontic treatment.113,164

Crosstalk imbalance and clinical implications abnormal bone remodelling

Under inflammatory or abnormal metabolic conditions, macrophage-MSCs crosstalk may be disrupted, leading to adverse clinical outcomes. M1 macrophages inhibit osteogenic differentiation of MSCs by abnormal metabolism through reactive oxygen species bursts.143,165 Systemic disease can interfere with crosstalk through multiple mechanisms. The hyperglycaemic microenvironment in diabetic patients can inhibit the TGF-β signalling pathway in MSCs and reduce the secretion of anti-inflammatory factors such as PGE2, thereby weakening the inhibitory effect on M1 macrophages.166 Telomere shortening of senescence-related MSCs decreases the content of miR-21 in exosomes, leading to a shift in macrophage polarization towards M1 type.167 In a model of periodontitis complicated with orthodontics, M1 macrophages promote matrix degradation by high expression of enzymes such as MMP-9,78 and persistent M1 polarization inhibits the osteogenic differentiation of MSCs.168 A decrease in M2 weakens anti-inflammatory and pro-repair effects.114 In the absence of M2 support, the osteogenic differentiation efficiency of MSCs is reduced.63 In addition, activation of the ATF6 pathway may exacerbate abnormal bone remodelling by promoting TNF-α transcription in macrophages.169

Although crosstalk between macrophages and MSCs is crucial for normal orthodontic bone remodelling, this precise regulatory network may be disrupted by various factors, leading to abnormal bone remodelling. Excessive orthodontic force exceeding the normal threshold of periodontal ligament mechanical stress causes excessive M1 polarization of macrophages, leading to sustained secretion of pro-inflammatory factors and excessive inflammatory responses.50,51 Excessive TNF-α and IL-1β not only inhibit osteogenic differentiation of PDLSCs but also accelerate osteoblast apoptosis and over-activate osteoclasts, causing excessive alveolar bone resorption on the pressure side, significant reduction in alveolar bone mass, destruction of tooth-supporting tissues, and increased risk of tooth mobility or even loss.66,170 Moreover, abnormal immune status in patients, such as autoimmune diseases (rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus) or long-term use of immunosuppressants, can disrupt normal communication between macrophages and mesenchymal stem cells.90,171 Immunological disorders may lead to abnormal macrophage polarization, failure to timely switch from M1 to M2 phenotype,172,173 persistent inflammation, obstruction of mesenchymal stem cell osteogenesis, insufficient bone formation on the tension side, prolonged orthodontic treatment, and poor post-treatment tooth stability with risk of recurrence and displacement.

Therapeutic strategies to correct such imbalances include: (1) Pharmacological modulation of macrophage polarization (using curcumin or rapamycin to promote M2 phenotype). (2) Exosome-based therapies delivering osteogenic miRNAs to enhance MSC differentiation. (3) Local delivery of pathway-specific agents (Wnt agonists or RANKL inhibitors) to fine-tune bone remodelling. These approaches are supported by ongoing preclinical studies and hold promise for future clinical translation, potentially reducing treatment time and complications in high-risk patients.

Discussion

With advances in biomechanics and immunology, the dynamic crosstalk between macrophages and MSCs in bone remodelling has become a central research focus. This review, based on current evidence, provides the first systematic elucidation of the macrophage-MSC interaction network under orthodontic force, establishing a novel theoretical framework for understanding bone remodelling in orthodontic therapy. The findings not only address key gaps in the interplay between mechanical stimulation and the immune microenvironment, but also inform the development of new bone-regenerative strategies.

At the mechanistic level, this review moves beyond static microenvironment models to examine how distinct mechanical stimulation patterns specifically regulate cellular behaviour. Mechanical forces activate Piezo1 ion channels on macrophage membranes, promoting macrophage proliferation and CD206+ M2 polarization via the Piezo1–AKT/GSK3β axis, while enhancing secretion of osteogenic factors including TGF-β1 and BMP2.17,31,82,174,175 Activated Piezo1 also upregulates Wnt3a and β-catenin expression, boosting osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells—a process further amplified by M2-derived exosomes through the PER2/Wnt/β-catenin axis.66,77,91,176, 177, 178, 179, 180, 181 Notably, the Wnt/β-catenin pathway exhibits dual regulatory roles by suppressing M1 polarization and promoting M2 polarization and forms a crosstalk network with JAK/STAT signalling.182,183 In addition, mechanical stimulation synergistically regulates osteogenesis via the Piezo1-YAP axis and the NFATc1 pathway, establishing a multi-pathway cooperative mechanism.184,185 These results systematically delineate the cascade from mechanical sensing to bone remodelling: Piezo1 activation → macrophage M2 polarization → Wnt/β-catenin pathway activation → osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells, providing a new strategic direction for bone regeneration therapies targeting the mechano-immune-stem cell axis.

At the molecular level, this study elucidates a cascade network of mechano-immune-stem cell interactions. Mechanical stimulation not only directly activates mechanosensors (Piezo1, TRPV4) in macrophages,17,177,180,184 but also modulates cytoskeletal reorganization to influence key signalling pathways, including NF-κB and YAP/TAZ. Notably, anti-inflammatory factors IL-10 and TGF-β secreted by M2 macrophages synergize with mechanical signals to form a positive feedback loop that facilitates bone regeneratio.17,186 This finding provides a new conceptual framework for understanding the coordinated action of mechanical stimulation and the immune microenvironment.

From a translational perspective, this review's elucidation of the macrophage-MSC crosstalk mechanism identifies multiple potential therapeutic targets and provides novel strategies to rebalance dysregulated signalling and optimize orthodontic bone remodelling. Modulating macrophage polarization constitutes a primary approach: specific bioactive molecules such as rapamycin can promote M2 polarization, suppress pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, and enhance anti-inflammatory responses, thereby creating a favourable environment for MSC-mediated osteogenesis.187, 188, 189 Natural compounds including curcumin have also been shown to regulate macrophage polarization and improve the bone-remodelling microenvironment.190, 191, 192 Exosomes, as key intercellular messengers, represent another promising avenue. Targeted modulation of cell-cell communication can be achieved through regulating exosomal secretion or engineering their bioactive cargo. For instance, gene-editing techniques may be utilized to modify exosomal miRNA profiles, enabling delivery of pro-osteogenic miRNAs or silencing of pro-inflammatory miRNAs to correct crosstalk imbalances. Moreover, targeting core signalling pathways in macrophage-MSC interactions has attracted considerable attention. This includes using NF-κB inhibitors to attenuate excessive inflammatory activation in macrophages, or activating the BMP pathway in MSCs to boost their osteogenic potential. These targets lay the groundwork for developing innovative adjunctive orthodontic therapies—such as localized drug-delivery systems and functionalized biomaterials—which may enhance treatment efficiency, shorten duration, and mitigate complications.

From a translational clinical perspective, this research demonstrates significant application potential. Based on the principles of mechano-immune regulation, we propose a “precision biomechanical-immunomodulatory” therapeutic framework, which involves individualized adjustment of orthodontic force parameters according to patient-specific factors such as age, bone density, inflammatory status (e.g., in diabetes or periodontitis), and immune-microenvironment features. In patients with immune-metabolic dysregulation, inappropriate mechanical stimulation may exacerbate macrophage-MSC crosstalk imbalance, leading to prolonged treatment duration, increased risks of root resorption and alveolar bone loss, and even complications such as bone fenestration and dehiscence. To correct such imbalances, the following strategies can be adopted: local delivery of anti-inflammatory cytokines or small-molecule modulators via hydrogels or scaffolds to promote M2 polarization; use of engineered exosomes to deliver reparative miRNAs or proteins that restore intercellular communication; and dynamic force modulation guided by real-time monitoring of inflammatory biomarkers in gingival crevicular fluid. These mechanism-based interventions are expected to improve treatment predictability, shorten treatment duration, and significantly reduce the incidence of complications such as root resorption, bone loss, and fenestration/dehiscence, highlighting the translational prospects of “mechano-immuno-engineering” in orthodontic practice.

In summary, this study integrates knowledge from mechanobiology, immunology, and stem cell biology to systematically elucidate the critical role and molecular mechanisms of macrophage-MSC interactions in mechanically regulated bone remodelling. These findings advance our understanding of bone remodelling and establish a theoretical foundation for developing novel bone-regenerative strategies. Moving forward, precision treatment approaches grounded in mechano-immune principles are poised to transform clinical practice in orthodontics and regenerative medicine.

Conclusion

This review systematically elucidates the molecular mechanisms underlying the interaction between macrophages and MSCs during orthodontic bone remodelling and their pivotal role in the “mechanical-immunity-stem cell” regulatory axis. Research indicates that the essence of orthodontic tooth movement lies in the dynamic process by which mechanical stimulation, mediated by mechanoreceptors such as Piezo1, triggers the reprogramming of the immune-bone metabolic network. On the compression side, M1 macrophages dominate bone resorption through the RANKL/RANK/NFATc1 cascade, while on the tension side, M2 macrophages activate the Wnt/β-catenin and Smad pathways in MSCs by secreting cytokines such as BMP-2 and TGF-β, thereby driving new bone formation. This spatiotemporally specific bone remodelling balance relies on a precisely regulated paracrine network between these 2 cell types, including cytokine-mediated bidirectional communication, miRNA regulation via exosomes, and the integration of mechanosensitive pathways into the immune-osteogenic network.

It is noteworthy that when mechanical forces are excessive or systemic, diseases are present; this delicate crosstalk may become imbalanced, leading to pathological bone remodelling. Based on these findings, we propose 3 translational medical strategies:

Future research should focus on developing clinically applicable mechanical-immunity regulatory devices, establishing personalized treatment prediction models, and exploring the role of epigenetic regulation in bone remodelling, thereby advancing the innovation of translational medicine in dentistry.

A strengthening of the dimensional Brunn–Minkowski conjecture implies the (B)-Conjecture
Sotiris Armeniakos, Jacopo Ulivelli

Abstract
We prove that if a sufficiently regular even log-concave measure satisfies a certain stronger form of the dimensional Brunn-Minkowski conjecture, then it also satisfies the (B)-conjecture. Furthermore, we show that hereditarily convex measures satisfy the aforementioned strengthened form, therefore providing an alternative proof of a recent result by Cordero-Erausquin and Eskenazis stating that a hereditarily convex measure satisfies both conjectures. † † MSC 2020 Classification: 52A40, 52A20, 28C20, 46N20, 47F10. Keywords: convex bodies, Brunn–Minkowski inequality, B-inequality, Gardner–Zvavitch problem, log-concave measures, elliptic PDEs.



1
Introduction

A measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be log-concave if it satisfies the inequality

\mu((1-t)K+tL)\geq\mu(K)^{1-t}\mu(L)^{t}

(1)

for every convex and compact sets
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
t\in[0,1]
, where
K+L
is the Minkowski sum of
K
and
L
. Among the central open problems in the study of log-concave measures are the dimensional Brunn–Minkowski conjecture and the (B)-conjecture.

The dimensional Brunn–Minkowski conjecture asks whether the dimensional concavity of the Lebesgue measure extends to all log-concave measures under symmetry assumptions. More precisely, if a log-concave measure
\mu
is even, that is,
\mu(A)=\mu(-A)
for every
\mu
-measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and if
K,L
are origin-symmetric convex sets, then the conjecture asserts that

\mu((1-t)K+tL)^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq(1-t)\mu(K)^{\frac{1}{n}}+t\mu(L)^{\frac{1}{n}}

(dim-BM)

holds for every
t\in[0,1]
. Notice that (
dim-BM
) in the case of the Lebesgue measure reduces to the celebrated Brunn–Minkowski inequality (see, e.g.,
[
13
, Section 7]
). The dimensional Brunn–Minkowski conjecture was first stated by Gardner and Zvavitch
[
8
]
for the Gaussian measure, a case settled by Eskenazis and Moschidis
[
6
]
, while Cordero-Erausquin and Rotem
[
5
]
proved (
dim-BM
) for a broader class of measures, including sufficiently regular rotationally invariant log-concave measures. Nevertheless, the conjecture remains open for general log-concave measures. See
[
1
,
4
]
for some recent developments and further references.

The (B)-conjecture, seemingly independent of
dim-BM
, concerns the behaviour of suitable dilates of convex bodies with respect to a log-concave measure. It asks whether, for an even log-concave measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the function

t\mapsto\mu(e^{t}K)\,,t\in\mathbb{R},

(B)

is log-concave for every origin-symmetric convex
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For the case of the Gaussian measure, this question was affirmatively answered by Cordero-Erausquin, Fradelizi, and Maurey
[
3
]
. Cordero-Erausquin and Rotem
[
5
]
later proved that the conjecture holds for the same general class of measures as (
dim-BM
) above. More recently, Cordero-Erausquin and Eskenazis
[
4
]
established the conjecture for so-called hereditarily convex measures (see later (
4
)), of which the measures treated in
[
5
]
are a subset.

In
[
4
]
it is shown that hereditarily convex measures satisfy both (
dim-BM
) and (
B
), extending earlier work of Kolesnikov and Livshyts
[
9
]
, who proved that, for sufficiently regular log-concave measures, hereditary convexity implies (
dim-BM
). Although no direct implication between (
dim-BM
) and (
B
) is currently known, we show in this note that a suitable strengthening of (
dim-BM
) yields (
B
). Furthermore, we prove that this strengthening is itself implied by hereditary convexity, thereby providing an alternative route to some of the results in
[
4
]
. Our approach relies on a new method introduced in
[
1
]
, combined with an application of the

L_{2}
method developed by Kolesnikov and Milman
[
10
,
11
]
, which was in turn inspired by
[
3
]
.

Before presenting our results, we introduce some necessary notation. By a classical characterization due to Borell
[
2
]
, a measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is log-concave if and only if there exists a convex function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
such that
\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=e^{-u(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x
. Throughout this paper, we will always consider
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that the Hessian matrix of
u
, denoted by
\nabla^{2}u
, is positive definite.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact convex set with non-empty interior, such that its boundary
\partial K
is a manifold of class
C^{2}
with strictly positive principal curvatures. We shall say that such a set
K
is of class
C^{2}_{+}
. Adopting the notation introduced by Livshyts
[
12
]
, the concavity power of
K
with respect to
\mu
is the maximal exponent
p(\mu,K)
such that

\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\mu\bigl(K(\rho,t)\bigr)^{p(\mu,K)}\right|_{t=0}\leq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every }\rho\in\mathcal{A},

(2)

where
K(\rho,t)
denotes a perturbation of
K
generated by a suitable function
\rho:\partial K\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathcal{A}
is a class of admissible perturbations. We will elaborate more on this point in the next section. By a standard density argument, it can be shown that (
dim-BM
) holds for
\mu
if and only if
p(\mu,K)\geq 1/n
for every origin-symmetric
K
of class
C^{2}_{+}
, see
[
12
, Section 3.2]
for a detailed exposition. Similarly, when studying the validity of (
B
), we may, by a standard density argument, restrict our attention to compact sets with sufficiently regular boundaries.

Given a non-empty compact convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we recall that its support function is defined as
h_{K}(x)=\sup_{y\in K}\langle x,y\rangle
. Moreover, if
K
is of class
C^{2}_{+}
, we denote by
\nu_{K}
its Gauss map. Throughout this note, given a compact convex set
K
with non-empty interior and measure
\mu
with continuous density with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
K
(as will always be the case here), we ubiquitously use the symbol
\,\mathrm{d}\mu
both for integration on
K
and on
\partial K
. In the latter case,
\mu
is understood as its restriction to the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\partial K
. We are now ready to state our first main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be an even convex function such that its Hessian matrix
\nabla^{2}u
is positive definite, and let
\mu
be the associated log-concave measure with density
\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=e^{-u(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x
. Given a convex and compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of class
C^{2}_{+}
, if

\frac{\mu(K)}{\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\leq p(\mu,K),

(3)

then

t\mapsto\mu(e^{t}K)\text{ is log-concave for }t\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular, if
\mu
satisfies (
3
) for every
K
of class
C^{2}_{+}
,
\mu
satisfies the (
B
)-conjecture as well.

We say that the even log-concave measure
\mu
satisfies the strong dimensional Brunn–Minkowski condition if (
3
) holds for every origin-symmetric
K
of class
C^{2}_{+}
. The fact that this condition implies the dimensional Brunn–Minkowski conjecture (
dim-BM
) follows immediately from an integration by parts, together with the symmetry of the problem. See Corollary
2.2
later.

To state our second result, we introduce some additional notation. Given
u
and
\mu
as in Theorem
1.1
, for every
\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we define the
\mu
-weighted Laplacian as
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\psi)=\Delta\psi-\langle\nabla u,\nabla\psi\rangle
. Moreover, we say that a measure
\eta
is log-concave with respect to
\mu
if
\eta
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
and its corresponding density
\,\mathrm{d}\eta/\,\mathrm{d}\mu
is a log-concave function. The notion of hereditary convexity put forth in
[
4
]
, inspired by
[
9
]
in the particular case of log-concave measures, is defined as follows. A log-concave measure
\mu
is hereditarily convex if it is even, and for every even measure
\eta
which is log-concave with respect to
\mu
and every even
\psi
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\psi)\in L_{2}(\eta)
, one has

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}||\nabla^{2}\psi||^{2}_{\rm HS}+\langle\nabla^{2}u\nabla\psi,\nabla\psi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta\geq\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\psi)\,\mathrm{d}\eta\right)^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\left(\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\eta}.

(4)

Here,
||\cdot||_{\rm HS}
denotes the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of matrices, and
|x|^{2}=\langle x,x\rangle
. Our second result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider
\mu
as in Theorem
1.1
. If
\mu
is hereditarily convex, then
\mu
satisfies the strong dimensional Brunn–Minkowski condition.

We shall prove, in fact, that the strong dimensional Brunn–Minkowski condition follows when
\mu
is hereditarily convex only with respect to uniform measures on non-empty compact and convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

To summarize this note, the results presented above show that if
\mu
is an even-log concave measure as in Theorem
1.1
, the implications in
[
4
, (62)]
can be refined as follows:

\mu
verifies (
dim-BM
)

\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\textrm{$\mu$ is hereditarily convex}\Rightarrow\textrm{$\mu$ satisfies the strong dim-BM condition}

\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\textrm{$\mu$ verifies \eqref{eq:B_conj}}.

2
Preliminaries and proofs of the results

Before starting with the proofs, we introduce and recall some auxiliary notions and results. Consider a compact and convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of class
C^{2}_{+}
. First, we elaborate on the concavity power defined in (
2
). There, the perturbations
K(\rho,t)
are defined as follows: As
K
is of class
C^{2}_{+}
, it is well-known that for every
\rho\in C^{2}(\partial K)
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
h_{t}=h_{K}+t\rho(\nu^{-1}_{K})
are the support functions of compact and convex sets of class
C^{2}_{+}
for every
|t|<\varepsilon
. We consider the sets
K(\rho,t)
corresponding to the support functions
h_{t}
. Therefore, if
K
is of class
C^{2}_{+}
, the family of admissible perturbations is, in fact,
C^{2}(\partial K)
. For more details on the theory of convex sets, the reader can consult the monograph by Schneider
[
13
]
.

Given a compact and convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of class
C^{2}_{+}
, we denote its second fundamental form by
{\rm II}
and the tangential gradient on
\partial K
by
\nabla_{\partial K}
. Consider, moreover, a convex function
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with strictly positive Hessian matrix and the measure
\mu
with density
\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=e^{-u(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x
. We write the weighted mean curvature of
\partial K
as
{\rm H}_{\mu}=\mathrm{tr}({\rm II})-\langle\nabla u,\nu_{K}\rangle
. With a slight abuse of notation, we shall use
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote also the restriction of the scalar product of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the tangent spaces of
\partial K
. We write
H^{1}(\partial K,\mu)
for the Sobolev space of functions on
\partial K
with respect to the measure
\mu
. The following statement, proved in
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
, provides an analytic representation for the concavity power
p(\mu,K)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Consider
\mu
such that it satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, and a compact and convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of class
C^{2}_{+}
. Then there exists a unique weak solution
\overline{\rho}\in H^{1}(\partial K,\mu)
to the equation

-\nabla_{\partial K}\cdot({\rm II}^{-1}\nabla_{\partial K}\rho)+\langle\nabla_{\partial K}u,{\rm II}^{-1}\nabla_{\partial K}{\rho}\rangle-{\rm H}_{\mu}\,\rho+\frac{1}{\mu(K)}\int_{\partial K}\rho\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu=1\hskip 20.00003pt\text{on }\partial K,

(5)

and it satisfies

p(\mu,K)=\frac{\mu(K)}{\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu}.

(6)

Moreover,
\overline{\rho}
is even.

For later use, notice that (
6
) allows one to rewrite (
3
) as

\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})\,\mathrm{d}\mu\geq\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(7)

Going back to condition (
3
), observe that integration by parts yields the identity

\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})\,\mathrm{d}\mu=n\mu(K)-\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

If we plug this expression in (
3
), we infer

p(\mu,K)\geq\frac{1}{n-\frac{1}{\mu(K)}\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu}\geq\frac{1}{n},

(8)

where, for the last inequality, we used that whenever
u
is even and convex, then
\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu\geq 0
. As anticipated in the introduction, one has the following immediate consequence.

Corollary 2.2
.

Consider a log-concave measure
\mu
as in Theorem
1.1
. If
\mu
satisfies (
3
) for every compact and convex set of class
C^{2}_{+}
, then (
dim-BM
) holds true.

As a further corollary of (
8
), considering
tK
for
t\to 0^{+}
shows that the infimum of
p(\mu,K)
on all the possible choices of
K
cannot give, for
p(\mu,K)
, a lower bound larger than
1/n
. In fact, as mentioned, for example, in
[
9
]
, an analogous limit argument shows that
1/n\geq\inf_{K}p(\mu,K)
.

We now turn our attention to the PDE (
5
). For
\rho\in C^{2}(\partial K)
let us define the operator

\mathcal{E}(\rho)=-\nabla_{\partial K}\cdot({\rm II}^{-1}\nabla_{\partial K}\rho)+\langle\nabla_{\partial K}u,{\rm II}^{-1}\nabla_{\partial K}{\rho}\rangle-{\rm H}_{\mu}\,\rho+\frac{1}{\mu(K)}\int_{\partial K}\rho\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(9)

Then,
\mathcal{E}
is a uniformly elliptic operator on
\partial K
and
\overline{\rho}
is a weak solution of
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=1
. We remark that in the case that the support function of
K
is assumed to be of class
C^{3}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, all the coefficients in (
9
) are of class at least
C^{1}
. Under this assumption, the function
\overline{\rho}
is, in fact, a classical solution for (
5
), as can be seen through classical elliptic regularity results. See, e.g.,
[
7
]
. Now, observe that integration by parts yields the identity

\int_{\partial K}\mathcal{E}(\rho)\rho\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{\partial K}\langle{\rm II}^{-1}\nabla_{\partial K}\rho,\nabla_{\partial K}\rho\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{\partial K}{\rm H}_{\mu}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\frac{1}{\mu(K)}\left(\int_{\partial K}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{2}.

The right-hand side is positive thanks to the weighted Poincaré inequality (compare
[
11
]
), which can be obtained by direct differentiation of (
1
). Therefore,

\int_{\partial K}\mathcal{E}(\rho)\rho\,\mathrm{d}\mu\geq 0

(10)

for every
\rho\in C^{2}(\partial K)
. We remark that this inequality holds for less regular functions. In fact, it remains true for functions in
H^{1}(\partial K,\mu)
, as proved in
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
. However, in this note, we will always assume that the functions involved are at least
C^{2}
. Additionally, we remark that the action of
\mathcal{E}
on the support function, whenever
h_{K}
is of class
C^{3}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, is

\mathcal{E}(h_{K}(\nu_{K}))=1+\langle\nabla u,x\rangle-\frac{1}{\mu(K)}\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(11)

This equality can be found in
[
1
, (66)]
. It follows from direct computations, which we omit for the sake of brevity.

Next, we recall a local formulation of the (B)-conjecture. Differentiating twice
\mu(e^{t}K)
for
\mu
and
K
as above (see, for example,
[
5
, (10)]
, which in turn generalizes
[
3
, (4)]
), it follows that (
B
) is equivalent to

\frac{\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mu(K)}-\left(\frac{\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mu(K)}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{\int_{K}\langle(\nabla^{2}u)x,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mu(K)}+\frac{\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mu(K)}

(12)

for every compact and origin-symmetric convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with non-empty interior.
For the
\mu
-weighted Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
, we recall the following useful integration by parts identity, which holds for any two functions
\varphi,\psi\in C^{2}(K)
,

\int_{K}\varphi\,\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\psi)\,\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int_{K}\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\partial K}\varphi\langle\nabla\psi,\nu_{K}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(13)

Finally, we will make use of the following Reilly formula, proved in
[
10
, (2.3)]
in the more general context of weighted Riemannian manifolds (compare also
[
4
, (21)]
). Consider a function
\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and its normal derivative
\rho=\langle\nabla\psi,\nu_{K}\rangle
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{K}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\psi)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{{\rm HS}}^{2}+\langle(\nabla^{2}u)\nabla\psi,\nabla\psi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial K}{\rm H}_{\mu}\rho^{2}+\langle{\rm II}\nabla_{\partial K}\psi,\nabla_{\partial K}\psi\rangle-2\langle\nabla_{\partial K}\psi,\nabla_{\partial K}\rho\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(14)

We are now ready to prove our results.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We begin by following the argument in
[
1
, Proposition 5.3]
. Let
\overline{\rho}
be given by (
5
). We may assume by approximation that, in addition, the support function of
K
is of class
C^{3}
and thus
\overline{\rho}\in C^{2}(\partial K)
. Moreover, denote by
\eta
the measure with density
\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\frac{1}{\mu(K)}\mathbbm{1}_{K}\,\mathrm{d}\mu
, where
\mathbbm{1}_{K}
is the characteristic function of
K
such that
\mathbbm{1}_{K}(x)=1
if
x\in K
and
0
otherwise. From (
11
) and since
\mathcal{E}(\overline{\rho})=1
we infer

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})-\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial K}(h_{K}-\overline{\rho})\mathcal{E}(\overline{\rho})\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\int_{\partial K}\mathcal{E}(h_{K}-\overline{\rho})\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta-\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

Then, writing
f(x)=\overline{\rho}(x)-h_{K}(\nu_{K}(x))
,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})-\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{\partial K}f\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta-\int_{\partial K}f\,\mathrm{d}\eta\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta}_{({\rm A})}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta-\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})\,\mathrm{d}\eta\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta}_{({\rm B})}

(15)

We begin by computing the term
({\rm A})
. Notice that by (
11
) and (
5
) we have

\mathcal{E}(f)=\mathcal{E}(\overline{\rho}-h_{K}(\nu_{K}))=-\langle\nabla u,x\rangle+\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

Thus,

({\rm A})=-\int_{\partial K}f\mathcal{E}(f)\,\mathrm{d}\eta\leq 0\,,

where the last inequality is (
10
). We proceed with the term
({\rm B})
. Notice that since for every
x\in\partial K
we have that
h_{K}(\nu_{K}(x))=\langle x,\nu_{K}(x)\rangle=\langle\nabla(|x|^{2}/2),\nu_{K}(x)\rangle
, an application of (
13
) implies

\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\int_{K}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\left(\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\eta=n-\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

(16)

Moreover, integrating by parts again shows

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle h_{K}(\nu_{K})\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\left(\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}\right)\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\int_{K}\langle\nabla\langle\nabla u,x\rangle,x\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=n\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta-\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\int_{K}\langle(\nabla^{2}u)x,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

Therefore, we infer

({\rm B})=\int_{K}\langle(\nabla^{2}u)x,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta-\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\left(\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta\right)^{2}.

(17)

If the strong dimensional Brunn–Minkowski holds, by (
7
) we have that
({\rm A})+({\rm B})\geq 0
and, since
({\rm A})\leq 0
, we conclude that
({\rm B})\geq 0
. That is,

\int_{K}\langle(\nabla^{2}u)x,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta\geq\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta-\left(\int_{K}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta\right)^{2},

which is precisely (
12
). Thus, (
B
) holds, concluding the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For this proof, we apply Hörmander’s
L_{2}
method, following the approach introduced in
[
10
,
11
]
, which can be consulted for further details. See also the discussion in
[
4
, Section 2]
. Consider the function
\overline{\rho}
given by (
5
). Let
\psi
be the unique solution to the Neumann problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\psi=\frac{1}{\mu(K)}\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\mu&\text{in }K,\\[2.0pt]
\displaystyle\langle\nabla\psi,\nu_{K}\rangle=\overline{\rho}&\text{on }\partial K.\end{cases}

(18)

Such solution exists provided that the integral (with respect to
\mu
) of the interior data on
K
is the same as the integral of the boundary data on
\partial K
, which is indeed the case here. By approximation, we can assume
h_{K}
to be of class
C^{3}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, so that
\overline{\rho}\in C^{2}(\partial K)
. Compare the discussion after (
9
). Thus, standard results in elliptic regularity theory (see, for example,
[
7
, Section 6]
) yield that
\psi\in C^{2}(K)
. Consider the normalized measure
\eta
as in the previous proof. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for positive quadratic forms implies that

0\leq\langle{\rm II}\nabla_{\partial K}\psi,\nabla_{\partial K}\psi\rangle-2\langle\nabla_{\partial_{K}}\psi,\nabla_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\rangle+\langle{\rm II}^{-1}\nabla_{\partial K}\overline{\rho},\nabla_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\rangle.

Applying this to the Reilly formula (
2
), for
\mu
,
\psi
and dividing by
\mu(K)
implies

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}\langle{\rm II}^{-1}\nabla_{\partial K}\overline{\rho},\nabla_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta-\int_{\partial K}{\rm H}_{\mu}\overline{\rho}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\left(\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\right)^{2}\geq\int_{K}\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}_{{\rm HS}}+\langle(\nabla u)^{2}\nabla\psi,\nabla\psi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta,

(19)

Furthermore, since by (
5
)
\overline{\rho}
satisfies
\mathcal{E}(\overline{\rho})=1
, multiplying with
\overline{\rho}
and integrating yields

\int_{\partial K}\langle{\rm II}^{-1}\nabla_{\partial K}\overline{\rho},\nabla_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta-\int_{\partial K}{\rm H}_{\mu}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta+\left(\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\right)^{2}=\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

(20)

Notice that this implies that
\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\geq 0
. Furthermore, by
[
1
, Corollary 3.4]
equality in the corresponding Poincaré inequality holds if and only if the function is identically zero. Consequently, we infer that
\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta>0
, as the alternative
\overline{\rho}\equiv 0
would contradict (
5
).
Now, notice that

\int_{K}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\psi\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\int_{K}\frac{1}{\mu(K)}\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta,

by definition of the measure
\eta
. Also, by Theorem
2.1
,
\overline{\rho}
is even and thus so is
\psi
. Furthermore, since
\int_{K}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\frac{x^{2}}{2})\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})\,\mathrm{d}\eta
by (
16
), we conclude by the hereditary convexity assumption (
4
) that

\int_{K}\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}_{{\rm HS}}+\langle(\nabla u)^{2}\nabla\psi,\nabla\psi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\nu\geq\frac{\left(\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\right)^{2}}{\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})\,\mathrm{d}\eta},

which, in combination with (
19
),(
20
) and the fact that
\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta>0
implies

\int_{\partial K}\overline{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\leq\int_{\partial K}h_{K}(\nu_{K})\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

Thus, the strong dimensional Brunn–Minkowski conjecture holds for the measure
\mu
, concluding the proof.

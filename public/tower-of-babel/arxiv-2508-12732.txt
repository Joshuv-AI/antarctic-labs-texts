The new observations about the parameter-dependent Schrödinger-Poisson system
Chen Huang, Sihua Liang, Lei Ma

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the existence results of solutions for the following Schrödinger-Poisson system involving different potentials: { − Δ ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u − λ ​ ϕ ​ u = f ⁡ ( u ) in ​ ℝ 3 , − Δ ​ ϕ = u 2 in ​ ℝ 3 . \begin{cases}-\Delta u+V(x)u-\lambda\phi u=f(u)&\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{3},\\ -\Delta\phi=u^{2}&\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases} We first consider the case that the potential V \displaystyle V is positive and radial so that the mountain pass theorem could be implied. The other case is that the potential V \displaystyle V is coercive and sign-changing, which means that the Schrödinger operator − Δ + V \displaystyle-\Delta+V is allowed to be indefinite. To deal with this more difficult case, by a local linking argument and Morse theory, the system has a nontrivial solution. Furthermore, we also show the asymptotical behavior result of this solution. Additionally, the proofs rely on new observations regarding the solutions of the Poisson equation. As a main novelty with respect to corresponding results in [ 21 , 12 , 20 ] , we only assume that f \displaystyle f satisfies the super-linear growth condition at the origin. We believe that the methodology developed here can be adapted to study related problems concerning the existence of solutions for Schrödinger-Poisson system. Keywords: Schrödinger-Poisson system; Perturbation problems; Mountain pass theorem; Local linking; Non-trivial solutions. Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 35A15, 35B38, 35J50

The modified problem

Since the condition
\displaystyle{\bf(f)}

\displaystyle F

Write

\in(0,1),&\quad\text{if }1\leq|t|\leq 2,\\

Then the directly calculation gives that

\begin{split}|\tilde{F}^{\prime}(t)|&\leq\left|d^{\prime}(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\epsilon}})F(t)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\right|+\left|d(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\epsilon}})f(t)\right|+\left|d^{\prime}(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\epsilon}})\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}|t|^{3}\right|+\left|3(1-d(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}))t^{2}\right|\\

&\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V(x)u_{n}^{2}\right)\text{d}x+\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}\text{d}x\\
&\quad\quad-\left(1+\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\delta|u_{n}|^{2}+\epsilon C_{\delta}|u_{n}|^{3}\right)\text{d}x.\end{split}

Multiplying
\displaystyle|u_{n}|
on both side of
\displaystyle\eqref{eq1}_{2}
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{3}\text{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\nabla\phi_{u_{n}}}\nabla(|u_{n}|)\text{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\phi_{u_{n}}|^{2}\text{d}x+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\text{d}x.

Combining (
1.4
) and (
3.3
) yields

It follows from (
3.2
) and (

) that

\begin{split}&\quad I_{\epsilon}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{\mu}\langle I_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle\\
&\geq\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-\frac{1}{4}\right)-\delta\left(1+\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V(x)u_{n}^{2}\right)\text{d}x\\
&\quad\quad+\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-\frac{1}{4}-C_{\delta}\epsilon\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{3}\text{d}x.\end{split}

Let
\displaystyle\mu=3
and
\displaystyle\delta=\frac{1}{64}
. Then, there exists a constant
\displaystyle\epsilon_{*}>0
small enough such that when
\displaystyle 0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{*}

Consequently,

On the other hand, since

Combining the above two estimates yields

Then there is a subsequence of

(still labeled by
\displaystyle u_{n}

The straightforward computations give

&=\|u_{n}-u\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}-\phi_{u}u)(u_{n}-u)\text{d}x\\
&\quad\quad-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\tilde{F}^{\prime}(\epsilon u_{n})-\tilde{F}^{\prime}(\epsilon u))(u_{n}-u)\text{d}x.\end{split}

In terms of (

Note that
\displaystyle\phi_{u_{n}}\rightarrow\phi_{u}

\displaystyle L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
\displaystyle L^{12/5}{(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

(3.9)

Hence, substituting (

Since

This completes the lemma.

Lemma 3

For any

\displaystyle M_{0}>0

For any
\displaystyle\alpha>1

Since

\displaystyle u

4.
Schrödinger-Poisson system with the sign-changing potential

The purpose of this section is to obtain a nontrivial solutions of system (

\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}

Define the working space as follows:

H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)u^{2}\text{d}x<\infty\},

where the inner product

\displaystyle\tilde{V}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\langle u,u\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}

If
\displaystyle(V_{1})

\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is compact for any
\displaystyle s\in[2,6)
(see Bartsch and Wang
[
5

\displaystyle-\infty<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq\cdots

Each eigenvalue is repeated according to its multiplicity, and let
\displaystyle e_{1},e_{2},\cdots
be the corresponding orthonormal eigenfunctions in
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

4.1.
Cerami condition

In order to prove Theorem
2
, we firstly need to show that the functional
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}
satisfies the Cerami conditions. To this end, we rewrite
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}
in the following form

(4.1)

I_{\epsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+\tilde{V}(x)u^{2}\right)\text{d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}\text{d}x-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon u)\text{d}x-\frac{m}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u^{2}\text{d}x.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be a Cerami sequence for the functional
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}
. There exists a constant
\displaystyle\epsilon_{**}>0
such that if
\displaystyle\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{**})
, then
\displaystyle u_{n}\rightarrow u\text{ in }H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\displaystyle u\in H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}
be a Cerami sequence of
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}
, that is, for some
\displaystyle c\in\mathbb{R}
,

I_{\epsilon}(u_{n})\to c~\text{and}~(1+\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})I_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0\ ~\text{as}~n\to+\infty.

Firstly, we claim that

\rho_{n}:=\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<+\infty.

If it is not true, we may assume
\displaystyle\rho_{n}\to+\infty
. Let
\displaystyle v_{n}=\frac{u_{n}}{\rho_{n}}
. Consequently,
\displaystyle\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Up to a subsequence,
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is compactly embedded in
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we may assume that

\begin{split}v_{n}&\to v~\text{weakly in}~H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3});\\
v_{n}&\to v~\text{strongly in}~L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3});\\
v_{n}&\to v~\text{a.e. in}~\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{split}

Consider

\begin{split}c+o_{n}(1)&=I_{\epsilon}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{3}\langle I_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+\tilde{V}(x)u_{n}^{2}\right)\text{d}x+\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}\text{d}x-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon u_{n})\text{d}x\\
&\quad\quad-\frac{1}{3\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}^{\prime}(\epsilon u_{n})\epsilon u_{n}\text{d}x-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mu^{2}_{n}\text{d}x\\
&\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V(x)u_{n}^{2}\right)\text{d}x+\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}\text{d}x\\
&\quad\quad-\left(1+\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\delta|u_{n}|^{2}+\epsilon C_{\delta}|u_{n}|^{3}\right)\text{d}x-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mu^{2}_{n}\text{d}x\\
&\geq\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{4}\right)-\delta\left(1+\frac{1}{3}\right)\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V(x)u_{n}^{2}\right)\text{d}x\\
&\quad\quad+\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{4}-C_{\delta}\epsilon\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{3}\text{d}x-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mu^{2}_{n}\text{d}x.\end{split}

Take
\displaystyle\delta=\frac{1}{64}
. Then, there exists a constant
\displaystyle\epsilon_{*}>0
small enough such that when
\displaystyle 0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{*}
, one has

\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{4}-C_{\frac{1}{64}}\epsilon\right)\geq 0

and then

c+o_{n}(1)\geq\frac{1}{8}\|u_{n}\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mu^{2}_{n}\text{d}x.

Multiplying both sides by
\displaystyle\rho_{n}^{-2}
, for large
\displaystyle n
, we have

\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)m\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{16}.

Note that
\displaystyle v_{n}\to v
strongly in
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then
\displaystyle v\neq 0
. Thus the Lebesgue measure of the set

\mathcal{M}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:v(x)\neq 0\}

is positive. For
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{M}
, as
\displaystyle n\to+\infty
, we deduce

v_{n}(x)\to v(x)\neq 0~\text{and}~u_{n}(x)=v_{n}(x)\rho_{n}\to+\infty.

On the other hand, it follows from assumptions
\displaystyle({\bf f})
and
\displaystyle({\bf f^{\prime}})
that

(4.2)

\tilde{F}(t)\geq 0\ ~\text{for any}~\epsilon\in(0,\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{4}).

From (
4.2
) and the Fatou lemma, we have

\begin{split}&\indent\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{\phi_{u_{n}}u^{2}_{n}}{4}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon u_{n})+\frac{1}{2}mu_{n}^{2}\right)\rho^{-2}_{n}\text{d}x\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{\phi_{u_{n}}u^{2}_{n}}{4u_{n}^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{F}(\epsilon u_{n})}{\epsilon^{2}u_{n}^{2}}+\frac{mu_{n}^{2}}{2u_{n}^{2}}\right)v^{2}_{n}\text{d}x\\
&\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\phi_{u_{n}}v^{2}_{n}\text{d}x\to+\infty\end{split}

as
\displaystyle n\to\infty
. Thus

\begin{split}c-1&\leq I_{\epsilon}(u_{n})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+\tilde{V}(x)u_{n}^{2}\right)\text{d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}\text{d}x\\
&\indent-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon u_{n})\text{d}x-\frac{m}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u_{n}^{2}\text{d}x\\
&\leq\rho_{n}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{\phi_{u_{n}}u^{2}_{n}}{4}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon u_{n})+\frac{1}{2}mu_{n}^{2}\right)\rho^{-2}_{n}(x)\text{d}x\right)\to-\infty\end{split}

as
\displaystyle n\to\infty
. This is impossible. Therefore, our claim is true.

From the boundedness of
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}
and
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is compactly embedded in
\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})(p\in[2,6))
, up to a sub-sequence we may assume

\begin{split}u_{n}&\to u~\text{weakly in}~H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3});\\
u_{n}&\to u~\text{strongly in}~L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})(p\in[2,6));\\
u_{n}&\to u~\text{a.e. in}~\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{split}

Next let
\displaystyle\epsilon_{**}=\min\{\epsilon_{*},\frac{\delta^{2}_{0}}{4}\}
, we can follow the same argument as Lemma
2
to show that
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}
has a convergent subsequence. ∎

By Morse theory, we know that if
\displaystyle I
satisfies the Cerami conditions,
\displaystyle u={\bf 0}
is an isolated critical point of
\displaystyle I
and the critical points set
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}:=\{u\in H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):I^{\prime}(u)=0\}=\{\bf 0\}
, then
\displaystyle C_{q}(I,\infty)\cong C_{q}(I,0)
for all
\displaystyle q\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\displaystyle C_{q}(I,\infty)\ncong C_{q}(I,0)
for some
\displaystyle q\in\mathbb{N}
then
\displaystyle I
must have a nontrivial critical point. Therefore, one has to compute these groups to get the nontrivial critical point.

Proposition 1
.

(see Bartsch and Li
[
4
]
)
Suppose that
\displaystyle I\in C^{1}(H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\mathbb{R})
satisfies the Cerami condition and
\displaystyle C_{k}(I,0)\neq C_{k}(I,\infty)
for some
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\displaystyle I
has a nontrivial critical point.

To compute the critical groups, we need to decompose the workspace
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Since
\displaystyle 0
is not an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle-\Delta+V(x)
, we can assume that there exists an integer
\displaystyle d\geq 0
such that
\displaystyle 0\in(\lambda_{d},\lambda_{d+1})
, where we set
\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=-\infty
. For
\displaystyle d\geq 1
, let
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{-}=\mbox{span}\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{d}\}\ \mbox{and}\ H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{+}=(H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{-})^{\perp}.
Then
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{-}
and
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{+}
are the negative space and positive space of the quadratic form
\displaystyle B(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}\right)\text{d}x,
respectively. Thus, there exits a constant
\displaystyle\eta>0
, such that

(4.3)

\pm B(u)\geq\eta\|u\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},\ u\in H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\pm}.

4.2.
Critical groups at zero

In this section, we will use the following proposition to compute the critical groups of
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}
at zero.

Proposition 2
.

(see Liu
[
14
]
)
Suppose
\displaystyle I\in C^{1}(H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\mathbb{R})
has a local linking at zero with respect to the decomposition
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{-}\oplus H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{+}
, i.e. for some
\displaystyle r>0
.

(4.4)

\displaystyle I(u)\leq 0\ \mbox{for}\ u\in H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{-}\cap B_{r},\ \mbox{and}\ I(u)>0\ \mbox{for}\ u\in(H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{+}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\})\cap B_{r},

where
\displaystyle B_{r}=\{u\in H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\|u\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<r\}
. If
\displaystyle d=\dim H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{-}<\infty
, then
\displaystyle C_{d}(I,0)\neq 0
.

Lemma 5
.

Under assumptions
\displaystyle{\bf(V_{1})}
,
\displaystyle{\bf(V_{2})}
,
\displaystyle{\bf(f)}
and
\displaystyle{\bf(f^{\prime})}
. Then the functional
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}
has a local linking at zero with respect to decomposition
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{-}\oplus H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{+}
.

Proof.

Recall (
2.2
), for any
\displaystyle\delta>0
and
\displaystyle\epsilon\in(0,\frac{\delta^{2}_{0}}{4})
, there is a constant
\displaystyle C_{\delta}
such that

0\leq\tilde{F}(t)\leq\delta t^{2}+C_{\delta}|t|^{3},

where
\displaystyle C_{\delta}
is a constant not depending on
\displaystyle\epsilon
. Hence, as
\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\to 0
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}(u)\text{d}x\leq o(\|u\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}).

Thus for
\displaystyle u\in H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{+}\setminus\{\bf 0\}
and
\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
small enough, by (
4.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}\right)\text{d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}\text{d}x-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon u)\text{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta}{4}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}-\frac{C}{4}\|u\|^{4}_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+o(\|u\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta}{8}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Then for
\displaystyle r>0
small enough, we obtain

(4.5)

I_{\epsilon}(u)>0\ \mbox{for}\ u\in(H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{+}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\})\cap B_{r}.

On the other hand, for
\displaystyle u\in H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{-}\cap B_{r}
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}\right)\text{d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}\text{d}x-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon u)\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}\text{d}x-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon u)\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

From this and (
4.5
), it implies
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}
has a local linking property at zero.
∎

4.3.
Critical groups at infinity

Lemma 6
.

If assumptions
\displaystyle{\bf(V_{1})}
,
\displaystyle{\bf(V_{2})}
,
\displaystyle{\bf(f)}
and
\displaystyle{\bf(f^{\prime})}
hold. Then
\displaystyle C_{q}(I_{\epsilon},\infty)\cong 0
with
\displaystyle q\in\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}
.

Proof.

Define
\displaystyle S^{\infty}
as the unit sphere in
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Firstly, we can show that

(4.6)

I_{\epsilon}(sh)\to-\infty\ \mbox{as}\ s\to+\infty\ \mbox{for any}\ h\in S^{\infty}.

Similar with Lemma
4
, from
\displaystyle\tilde{F}(t)\geq 0
we deduce

\begin{split}I_{\epsilon}(sh)&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla(sh)|^{2}+\tilde{V}(x)(sh)^{2}\right)\text{d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{(sh)}(sh)^{2}\text{d}x\\
&\indent-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon sh)\text{d}x-\frac{m}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(sh)^{2}\text{d}x\\
&\leq s^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{h}h^{2}\text{d}x\right)\to-\infty.\end{split}

We next shall show the following claim is right.

Claim:
There is a constant
\displaystyle A>0
such that if
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}(u)\leq-A
, then

\frac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{d}t}|_{t=1}I_{\epsilon}(tu)<0.

If this claim is false, there exists a sequence
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

(4.7)

I_{\epsilon}(u_{n})\leq-n\ \mbox{and}\ \langle I_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle=\frac{\mbox{d}}{\mbox{d}t}|_{t=1}I_{\epsilon}(tu_{n})\geq 0.

From (
4.7
), similar with (
3.2
), we have

(4.8)

\begin{split}0&\geq I_{\epsilon}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{3}\langle I_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+\tilde{V}(x)u_{n}^{2}\right)\text{d}x+\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}\text{d}x-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon u_{n})\text{d}x\\
&\quad\quad-\frac{1}{3\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}^{\prime}(\epsilon u_{n})\epsilon u_{n}\text{d}x-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mu^{2}_{n}\text{d}x\\
&\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+V(x)u_{n}^{2}\right)\text{d}x+\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}\text{d}x\\
&\quad\quad-\left(1+\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\delta|u_{n}|^{2}+\epsilon C_{\delta}|u_{n}|^{3}\right)\text{d}x-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mu^{2}_{n}\text{d}x\\
&\geq\frac{1}{8}\|u_{n}\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mu^{2}_{n}\text{d}x,\end{split}

where
\displaystyle\delta
denoted by
\displaystyle\frac{1}{64}
and
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
small.

Notice that if
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then
\displaystyle\{I_{\epsilon}(u_{n})\}
is also bounded. Then by
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}(u_{n})\leq-n
, it implies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+\widetilde{V}(x)|u_{n}|^{2}\right)\text{d}x\to+\infty\ \mbox{as}\ n\to+\infty.

Setting
\displaystyle v_{n}=\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}}
,
\displaystyle\{v_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Up to subsequence, one has

v_{n}\to v\ \mbox{ weakly in}\ H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\ v_{n}\to v\ \mbox{ strongly in}\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\ v_{n}\to v\ \mbox{a.e. in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.

Multiplying both side of (
4.8
) by
\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
, we obtain

\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mv^{2}_{n}\text{d}x\geq\frac{1}{8}.

From
\displaystyle v_{n}\to v
strongly in
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it follows that
\displaystyle v\neq 0
. Thus the Lebesgue measure of the set

\mathcal{M}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:v(x)\neq 0\}

is positive.

By the definition of
\displaystyle\tilde{F}(t)
, conditions
\displaystyle({\bf f})
and
\displaystyle({\bf f^{\prime}})
, for any
\displaystyle\epsilon\in(0,\frac{\delta_{0}}{4})
, it follows that

(4.9)

\tilde{F}^{\prime}(t)t\geq 0.

For
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{M}
, as
\displaystyle n\to+\infty
, we deduce

v_{n}(x)\to v(x)\neq 0~\text{and}~u_{n}(x)=v_{n}(x)\rho_{n}\to+\infty.

It follows from (
4.9
) and the Fatou lemma that

\begin{split}&\indent\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\phi_{u_{n}}u^{2}_{n}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\tilde{F}^{\prime}(\epsilon u_{n})u_{n}+mu_{n}^{2}\right)\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{-2}\text{d}x\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{\phi_{u_{n}}u^{2}_{n}}{u_{n}^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{F}^{\prime}(\epsilon u_{n})\epsilon u_{n}}{\epsilon^{2}u_{n}^{2}}+\frac{mu_{n}^{2}}{u_{n}^{2}}\right)v^{2}_{n}\text{d}x\\
&\geq\int_{\mathcal{M}}\phi_{u_{n}}v^{2}_{n}\text{d}x\to+\infty\end{split}

as
\displaystyle n\to\infty
. Thus

\begin{split}0&\leq\langle I_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+\tilde{V}(x)u_{n}^{2}\right)\text{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u_{n}}u_{n}^{2}\text{d}x\\
&\indent-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}^{\prime}(\epsilon u_{n})\epsilon u_{n}\text{d}x-m\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u_{n}^{2}\text{d}x\\
&\leq\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\phi_{u_{n}}v^{2}_{n}\text{d}x\right)\to-\infty\end{split}

as
\displaystyle n\to\infty
. This is impossible. Therefore, our claim is true.

Due to this claim and (
4.6
), for
\displaystyle B>A
large enough, there exists an unique
\displaystyle T:=T(u)>0
such that

I_{\epsilon}(T(u)u)=-B\ \ \text{for every }u\in S^{\infty}.

It follows from the implicit function theorem that

\displaystyle T
is a continuous function from
\displaystyle S^{\infty}
to
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
.

Thus the deformation retract
\displaystyle\eta:[0,1]\times(H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus B_{1})\to H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
denoted as

\eta(s,u)=(1-s)u+sT(u)u

with
\displaystyle\eta(0,u)=u
,
\displaystyle\eta(1,u)\in I_{\epsilon}^{-B}
. Thus

C_{q}(I_{\epsilon},\infty)=H^{q}(H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),I_{\epsilon}^{-B})\cong H^{q}(H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus B_{1})\cong 0,\ \mbox{for all}\ q\in\mathbb{N}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
6
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
It follows from Lemma
4
that
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}
satisfies the Cerami conditions. By Lemma
5
,
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}
has a local linking at zero with respect to the decomposition
\displaystyle H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{-}\oplus H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{+}
, thus from Proposition
2
, for
\displaystyle d=\dim H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{-1}
, it implies
\displaystyle C_{d}(I_{\epsilon},0)\neq 0.
On the other hand, due to Lemma
6
, we have for all
\displaystyle q\in\mathbb{N}
,
\displaystyle C_{q}(I_{\epsilon},\infty)=0
. Thus, by Proposition
1
,
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}
has a nontrivial critical point
\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}
. Then
\displaystyle(u_{\epsilon},\phi_{u_{\epsilon}})
is a nontrivial solution of system (
2.3
). Similar with Lemma
3
, we can obtain the
\displaystyle L^{\infty}
norm of
\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}
is uniformly bounded about
\displaystyle\epsilon
. Hence, there exists a positive constant
\displaystyle\epsilon^{**}
, such that for any
\displaystyle\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon^{**})
,
\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}
is also the solution of (
1.2
). Consequently,
\displaystyle\epsilon u_{\epsilon}
is the solution of
(
1.1
)
and
\displaystyle\lambda^{**}=\frac{1}{(\epsilon^{**})^{2}}
. Thus Theorem
2
has been proved.

5.
The proof of Theorem
3

Now we study the asymptotical behavior of non-trivial solutions in Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
3
In order to highlight the parameter dependence with respect to
\displaystyle\epsilon
, we rewrite
\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}
denoting the
solution of (
2.3
) and
\displaystyle c_{\epsilon}
denotes the critical value of
\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})
. By Theorem
1
, for any
\displaystyle 0<\epsilon<\epsilon^{*}
,
\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}
is also the solution of system (
1.2
). Consequently,
\displaystyle\epsilon u_{\epsilon}
is the solution of system
(
1.1
)
.
Clearly,
\displaystyle\{u_{\epsilon}\}
is a bounded sequence in
\displaystyle H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Let

I_{0}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}\right)\text{d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}\text{d}x

be the energy functional of the following Choquard equation

(5.1)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}\text{d}y\ \ ~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Then it is easy to say
\displaystyle I_{0}\in C^{1}
in
\displaystyle H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Since
\displaystyle H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is compactly embedded in
\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})(2<p<6)
,
\displaystyle I_{0}
satisfies the (PS) condition.

By (
2.2
), one has

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\left(I_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})-I_{0}(u_{\epsilon})\right)=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{F}(\epsilon u_{\epsilon})\text{d}x=0.\end{split}

Similarly, for any
\displaystyle v\in H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it implies

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\langle I_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{\epsilon})-I_{0}^{\prime}(u_{\epsilon}),v\rangle=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{f}(\epsilon u_{\epsilon})v\text{d}x=0.\end{split}

Thus
\displaystyle\{u_{\epsilon}\}
is a (PS) sequence of
\displaystyle I_{0}
. Then by the (PS) condition of
\displaystyle I_{0}
, we have

u_{\epsilon}\to u_{0}~\text{strongly~in}~H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\ ~\text{as}~\epsilon\to 0,

where
\displaystyle u_{0}
is a critical point of
\displaystyle I_{0}
and it is also a weak solution of equation (
5.1
). Recall that
\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}
and
\displaystyle u^{\lambda}=\epsilon u_{\epsilon}
, hence Theorem
3
has been proved.

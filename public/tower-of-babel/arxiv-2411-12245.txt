Optimal embedding results for fractional Sobolev spaces
Serena Dipierro, Edoardo Proietti Lippi, Caterina Sportelli and Enrico Valdinoci

Abstract
. This paper deals with the fractional Sobolev spaces W s , p ​ ( Ω ) W^{s,p}(\Omega) , with s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] s\in(0,1] and p ∈ [ 1 , + ∞ ] p\in[1,+\infty] . Here, we use the interpolation results in [ 3 ] to provide suitable conditions on the exponents s s and p p so that the spaces W s , p ​ ( Ω ) W^{s,p}(\Omega) realize a continuous embedding when either Ω = ℝ N \Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N} or Ω \Omega is any open and bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary. Our results enhance the classical continuous embedding and, when Ω \Omega is any open bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary, we also improve the classical compact embeddings. All the results stated here are proved to be optimal. Also, our strategy does not require the use of Besov or other interpolation spaces.

To establish our main results, we recall (
1.1
) and we introduce the following notation. For
s\in[0,1]
and
p\in[1,+\infty]
, we define

[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&\mbox{ if }s=0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\left(c_{N,s,p}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/p}&\mbox{ if }s\in(0,1),\\
\\
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&\mbox{ if }s=1.\end{cases}

Here,
c_{N,s,p}
denotes a normalizing constant, given explicitly by

c_{N,s,p}:=\frac{s\,2^{2s-1}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{ps+p+N-2}{2}\right)}{\pi^{N/2}\,\Gamma(1-s)},

see e.g.
[
14
, page 130]
and the references therein.

The normalizing constant is determined in such a way that

\lim_{s\searrow 0}[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=[u]_{W^{0,p}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\lim_{s\nearrow 1}[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=[u]_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

see
[
2
]
.

As customary, we define the fractional Sobolev space

W^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\mbox{ such that }[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}<+\infty\right\}

endowed with the norm

(1.2)

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&\mbox{ if }s=0,\\
\left([u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}+\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\mbox{ if }s\in(0,1),\\
\left(\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\mbox{ if }s=1.\end{cases}

2.1.
Some scaling properties

To start with, we provide some useful scaling properties:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be any measurable function and
\gamma
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, let
v_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\beta}(x):=\varepsilon^{\gamma}u(\varepsilon^{\beta}x)
.

Then, for any
s\in(0,1]
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
,

(2.1)

\|v_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\beta}\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\varepsilon^{\gamma-\frac{\beta N}{p}+\beta s}\left(\varepsilon^{-\beta sp}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Furthermore, if
p=+\infty
, for any
s\in(0,1)
,

(2.2)

\|v_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\beta}\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|v_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\beta}\|_{C^{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\varepsilon^{\gamma}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\varepsilon^{\gamma+\beta s}\sup_{{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\atop{x\neq y}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}.

Proof.

We first prove (
2.1
). A suitable change of variables leads to

\|v_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\beta}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\varepsilon^{\gamma p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(\varepsilon^{\beta}x)|^{p}\,dx=\varepsilon^{\gamma p-\beta N}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

and

(2.3)

[v_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\beta}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\varepsilon^{\gamma p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(\varepsilon^{\beta}x)-u(\varepsilon^{\beta}y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy=\varepsilon^{\gamma p-\beta N+\beta sp}[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Recalling the definition in (
1.2
), we get (
2.1
), as desired.

In order to prove (
2.2
), we observe that

(2.4)

\|v_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\varepsilon^{\gamma}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

and

(2.5)

\begin{split}\sup_{{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\atop{x\neq y}}\frac{|v_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\beta}(x)-v_{\varepsilon,\gamma,\beta}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}=\;&\varepsilon^{\gamma+\beta s}\sup_{{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\atop{x\neq y}}\frac{|u(\varepsilon^{\beta}x)-u(\varepsilon^{\beta}y)|}{|\varepsilon^{\beta}x-\varepsilon^{\beta}y|^{s}}\\
=\;&\varepsilon^{\gamma+\beta s}\sup_{{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\atop{x\neq y}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}},\end{split}

as desired.

),

3.
Auxiliary results

In this section we provide some preliminary results that will be used to prove our main theorems.

3.1.
An interpolation result

We start by recalling the following interpolation result due to Brezis and Mironescu (see
[
3
, Theorem 1]
). We state it in a way which is suitable for our analysis:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0\leqslant s_{1}\leqslant s_{2}\leqslant 1
and
p_{1}
,
p_{2}\in[1,+\infty]

with
s_{2}\neq p_{2}
.
Let
\Omega
be either
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or a bounded Lipschitz domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

For every
\theta\in[0,1]
, let

s_{\theta}:=\theta s_{1}+(1-\theta)s_{2}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad p_{\theta}:=\frac{p_{1}\,p_{2}}{(1-\theta)p_{1}+\theta p_{2}}.

Then, there exists a positive constant
C=C(s_{1},s_{2},p_{1},p_{2},\theta,\Omega)
such that, for any
u\in W^{s_{1},p_{1}}(\Omega)\cap W^{s_{2},p_{2}}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|_{W^{s_{\theta},p_{\theta}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|u\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|u\|_{W^{s_{2},p_{2}}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}.

3.2.
A suitable curve of points

Now, we show that any points
\widetilde{s}
and
\widetilde{p}
satisfying (
1.3
) belong to a suitable curve.
For this, if
sp<N
, we define the fractional Sobolev exponent

p^{*}_{s}:=\frac{Np}{N-sp}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
be such that
sp<N
. Assume that
\widetilde{s}
and
\widetilde{p}
satisfy (
1.3
).

Then, there exists
q\in[p,p^{*}_{s}]
such that the curve
\gamma_{q}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined as

(3.1)

\gamma_{q}(\theta):=\left((1-\theta)s,\frac{pq}{q-\theta(q-p)}\right)

contains the point
(\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p})
, namely there exists
\widetilde{\theta}\in[0,1]
such that
\gamma_{q}({\widetilde{\theta}})=(\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p})
.

Proof.

Notice that if
\widetilde{s}=s
, from (
1.3
)
we see that
\widetilde{p}=p
. In this case we then have
that
\gamma_{q}(0)=(\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p})

for every
q\in[p,p^{*}_{s}]
. Thus, the couple
(\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p})

belongs to the curve
\gamma_{q}
.

Suppose instead that
\widetilde{s}\in[0,s)
and
\widetilde{p}=p
. In this case, taking
q:=p
, we see that
\gamma_{q}(\theta)=((1-\theta)s,p)
.
Therefore, there exists
\widetilde{\theta}\in(0,1]
such that
\gamma_{q}(\widetilde{\theta})=(\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p})
, as desired.

Given these preliminary observations, from now on we suppose that
\widetilde{s}\neq s
and
\widetilde{p}\neq p
.

Moreover, we remark that the condition
\widetilde{s}\widetilde{p}=sp
is incompatible with (
1.3
),
since

\widetilde{s}\widetilde{p}\leqslant\frac{Np\widetilde{s}}{N-(s-\widetilde{s})p}=\frac{\widetilde{s}spp^{*}_{s}}{sp+(p^{*}_{s}-p)\widetilde{s}}=\left(\frac{\widetilde{s}p^{*}_{s}}{\widetilde{s}p^{*}_{s}+p(s-\widetilde{s})}\right)sp<sp.

Therefore, from now on,
we also assume that
\widetilde{s}\widetilde{p}\neq sp
.

Now, we observe that the couple
(\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p})
belongs to the curve
\gamma_{q}
if and only if it is contained in the graph of the function
f_{q}:[0,s]\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

f_{q}(t):=\dfrac{spq}{sp+(q-p)t}.

We set

q:=\frac{p\widetilde{p}(s-\widetilde{s})}{sp-\widetilde{s}\widetilde{p}}

and we have that

\widetilde{p}=\dfrac{spq}{sp+(q-p)\widetilde{s}}=f_{q}(\widetilde{s}).

Hence, it remains to check that

(3.2)

q\in[p,p^{*}_{s}].

For this, we notice that, in light of (
1.3
),

q>\frac{p^{2}(s-\widetilde{s})}{p(s-\widetilde{s})}=p

and

q\leqslant\dfrac{\dfrac{sp^{2}p^{*}_{s}\,(s-\widetilde{s})}{sp+(p^{*}_{s}-p)\widetilde{s}}}{sp-\dfrac{\widetilde{s}spp^{*}_{s}}{sp+(p^{*}_{s}-p)\widetilde{s}}}=\frac{p^{*}_{s}(s-\widetilde{s})}{1-\widetilde{s}}\leqslant p^{*}_{s}.

These considerations proves (
3.2
), as desired.
∎

We now check that if
\widetilde{s}
and
\widetilde{p}
satisfy (
1.8
), they belong to the curve
\gamma_{q}
, as stated in this result:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
be such that
sp<N
. Let
\widetilde{s}
and
\widetilde{p}
satisfy (
1.8

Then, there exists
q\in[1,p^{*}_{s})
such that the curve
\gamma_{q}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined in (
3.1
) contains the point
(\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p})
, namely there exists
\widetilde{\theta}\in(0,1]
such that
\gamma_{q}({\widetilde{\theta}})=(\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p})
.

Proof.

The proof of this result is similar to the proof of Lemma
3.2

If
\widetilde{s}\in[0,s)
and
\widetilde{p}=p
,
we take
q:=p
and we see that
\gamma_{q}(\theta)=((1-\theta)s,p)
.
Therefore, there exists
\widetilde{\theta}\in[0,1]

\gamma_{q}(\widetilde{\theta})=(\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p})

Hence, we will now suppose that
\widetilde{p}\neq p

From (

\widetilde{s}\widetilde{p}\leqslant\frac{Np\widetilde{s}}{N-(s-\widetilde{s})p}=\frac{\widetilde{s}spp^{*}_{s}}{sp+(p^{*}_{s}-p)\widetilde{s}}=\left(\frac{\widetilde{s}p^{*}_{s}}{\widetilde{s}p^{*}_{s}+p(s-\widetilde{s})}\right)sp<sp.

We now show that, under that assumption that the points

3.3.
An embedding result

We now state a preliminary embedding result which will be useful to prove Theorem
1.2
. We point out that our result is similar to
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
, but we provide here a self-contained proof that does not involve the use of Besov or other interpolation spaces.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,+\infty]
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Let
\widetilde{s}\in(0,s)
and
\widetilde{p}\in[1,p]
.

Then, there exists a positive constant
C=C(N,s,p,\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p},\Omega)
such that, for any
u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|_{W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)},

namely, the space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We first suppose that
p\in[1,+\infty)
and we claim that

(3.3)

\|u\|_{L^{\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\,\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Indeed, if
\widetilde{p}=p
, then the claim is obvious. If instead
\widetilde{p}\in[1,p)
, by the Hölder inequality with exponents
p/\widetilde{p}
and
p/(p-\widetilde{p})
,

\|u\|_{L^{\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)}\leqslant|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}-\frac{1}{p}}\,\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

which gives (
3.3
).

We also show that

(3.4)

[u]_{W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\,[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}.

To this end, it is convenient to write

\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\widetilde{p}}}{|x-y|^{N+\widetilde{s}\widetilde{p}}}=\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\widetilde{p}}}{|x-y|^{\frac{N\widetilde{p}}{p}+s\widetilde{p}}}\ \frac{1}{|x-y|^{\frac{N(p-\widetilde{p})}{p}-\widetilde{p}(s-\widetilde{s})}}.

If
\widetilde{p}=p
, then the claim in (
3.4
)
follows from this and the fact that
\Omega
is bounded.

If instead
\widetilde{p}\in[1,p)
,
we use the Hölder inequality with exponents
p/\widetilde{p}
and
p/(p-\widetilde{p})
to find that

\begin{split}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\widetilde{p}}}{|x-y|^{N+\widetilde{s}\widetilde{p}}}\,dx\,dy\leqslant\;&\left(\;\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{\widetilde{p}}{p}}\left(\;\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{N-\frac{p\widetilde{p}(s-\widetilde{s})}{p-\widetilde{p}}}}\right)^{\frac{p-\widetilde{p}}{p}}\\
\leqslant\;&C\left(\;\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{\widetilde{p}}{p}},\end{split}

from which we obtain (
3.4
).

The claim of Lemma
3.5
in this case now follows from (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).

We now focus on the case
p=+\infty
.
If in addition
\widetilde{p}=+\infty
, our claim becomes

\|u\|_{C^{0,\widetilde{s}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|u\|_{C^{0,s}(\Omega)},

which in turn boils down to

(3.5)

[u]_{C^{0,\widetilde{s}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C[u]_{C^{0,s}(\Omega)}.

For this, we observe that, for any
\widetilde{s}\in(0,s)
,

\displaystyle\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\widetilde{s}}}=\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}|x-y|^{s-\widetilde{s}}\leqslant[u]_{C^{0,s}(\Omega)}|x-y|^{s-\widetilde{s}}.

Since
\Omega
is bounded, this implies (
3.5
),
as desired.

If instead
\widetilde{p}\in[1,p)=[1,+\infty)
, our claim becomes

(3.6)

\|u\|_{W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|u\|_{C^{0,s}(\Omega)}.

In this situation, we point out that

\|u\|_{L^{\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{\widetilde{p}}\,dx\leqslant\|u\|^{\widetilde{p}}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|.

Also,

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\widetilde{p}}}{|x-y|^{N+\widetilde{s}\widetilde{p}}}\,dx\,dy\leqslant[u]^{\widetilde{p}}_{C^{0,s}(\Omega)}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|x-y|^{s\widetilde{p}}}{|x-y|^{N+\widetilde{s}\widetilde{p}}}\,dx\,dy\leqslant C[u]^{\widetilde{p}}_{C^{0,s}(\Omega)}.

From the last two displays, we obtain (
3.6
).
∎

4.
Proof of the embedding results

In this section we prove our main embedding results.

4.1.
The case
sp<N

We first provide the following useful observation:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
s\in[0,1]
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
be such that
sp<N
. Let

(4.1)

\widetilde{s}\in[0,s]\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\widetilde{p}\in\left[1,\frac{Np}{N-(s-\widetilde{s})p}\right].

Let
\gamma
be the curve defined in (
1.5
).
Let
0\leqslant\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}\leqslant 1
and let
\gamma(\theta_{1})=(s_{\theta_{1}},p_{\theta_{1}})

and
\gamma(\theta_{2})=(s_{\theta_{2}},p_{\theta_{2}})
.

Then,

(4.2)

0\leqslant s_{\theta_{2}}\leqslant s_{\theta_{1}}\leqslant 1,\qquad\min\{p,\widetilde{p}\}\leqslant p_{\theta_{1}},p_{\theta_{2}}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad p_{\theta_{2}}\leqslant\frac{Np_{\theta_{1}}}{N-(s_{\theta_{1}}-s_{\theta_{2}})p_{\theta_{1}}}.

If
p\leqslant\widetilde{p}
, then
p_{\theta_{1}}\leqslant p_{\theta_{2}}
.

Furthermore, if in addition

(4.3)

\widetilde{p}\geqslant\frac{sp}{sp-(p-1)\widetilde{s}},

then

(4.4)

p_{\theta_{2}}\geqslant\frac{s_{\theta_{1}}p_{\theta_{1}}}{s_{\theta_{1}}p_{\theta_{1}}-(p_{\theta_{1}}-1)s_{\theta_{2}}}.

Moreover,
s_{\theta_{1}}=s_{\theta_{2}}
if and only if
s=\widetilde{s}
,
and
p_{\theta_{1}}=p_{\theta_{2}}
if and only if
p=\widetilde{p}
.

Also,

p_{\theta_{2}}=\frac{Np_{\theta_{1}}}{N-(s_{\theta_{1}}-s_{\theta_{2}})p_{\theta_{1}}}

if and only if

\widetilde{p}=\frac{Np}{N-(s-\widetilde{s})p}.

Proof.

By inspection, one sees that
0\leqslant s_{\theta_{2}}\leqslant s_{\theta_{1}}\leqslant 1
.
Also, for all
\theta\in[0,1]
,

(4.5)

\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{\widetilde{p}+\theta(p-\widetilde{p})}{p\widetilde{p}}=\frac{1}{p}+\theta\left(\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\leqslant\max\left\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\right\}=\frac{1}{\min\{p,\widetilde{p}\}}.

Hence, to complete the proof of (
4.2
),
it remains to check that

(4.6)

p_{\theta_{2}}\leqslant\frac{Np_{\theta_{1}}}{N-(s_{\theta_{1}}-s_{\theta_{2}})p_{\theta_{1}}}.

To this end, we first observe that, thanks to (
4.1
),

\frac{N}{p}-\frac{N}{\widetilde{p}}\leqslant s-\widetilde{s}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{\theta_{1}}}-\frac{1}{p_{\theta_{2}}}=\frac{\widetilde{p}-\theta_{1}(\widetilde{p}-p)}{p\widetilde{p}}-\frac{\widetilde{p}-\theta_{2}(\widetilde{p}-p)}{p\widetilde{p}}=(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\frac{(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})(s-\widetilde{s})}{N}=\frac{s_{\theta_{1}}-s_{\theta_{2}}}{N}.

From this, the desired result in (
4.6
)
plainly follows.

If in addition
p\leqslant\widetilde{p}
,
by the definition of
p_{\theta}
we have that
p_{\theta_{1}}\leqslant p_{\theta_{2}}
.

We now also assume (
4.3
)
and we prove (
4.4
).
For this, we introduce the notation

\alpha:=1-\frac{1}{p},\qquad\widetilde{\alpha}:=1-\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\beta:=s-\widetilde{s}

and we observe that

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{\theta_{1}}}\right)s_{\theta_{2}}-\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{\theta_{2}}}\right)s_{\theta_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\alpha-\theta_{1}(\alpha-\widetilde{\alpha})\big)(s-\theta_{2}\beta)-\big(\alpha-\theta_{2}(\alpha-\widetilde{\alpha})\big)(s-\theta_{1}\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\alpha(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+(\alpha-\widetilde{\alpha})\big(\theta_{2}(s-\theta_{1}\beta)-\theta_{1}(s-\theta_{2}\beta)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\alpha(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+(\alpha-\widetilde{\alpha})(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})\big(\alpha(s-\beta)-\widetilde{\alpha}s\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})\big(\alpha\widetilde{s}-\widetilde{\alpha}s\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\widetilde{s}-\left(1-\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\right)s\right).

In light of this, we have that (
4.3
)
implies (
4.4
).

Finally, the last statements follow from the definition in (
1.5
).

, which concludes the case
\widetilde{p}<p
.

The first statement of Theorem
1.8
is thereby
proved, and we now focus on the second one.

Let
0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}\leqslant 1
and consider
\gamma(\theta_{1})=(s_{\theta_{1}},p_{\theta_{1}})

and
\gamma(\theta_{2})=(s_{\theta_{2}},p_{\theta_{2}})
,
where the curve
\gamma
has been defined in (
1.5
).
Then, Lemma
4.2

gives that we can use the first statement of Theorem
1.8

with
s:=s_{\theta_{1}}
,
p:=p_{\theta_{1}}
,
\widetilde{s}:=s_{\theta_{2}}

and
\widetilde{p}:=p_{\theta_{2}}
and obtain that
W^{s_{\theta_{1}},p_{\theta_{1}}}(\Omega)

is compactly embedded in
W^{s_{\theta_{2}},p_{\theta_{2}}}(\Omega)
,
as desired.
∎

4.3.
The case
sp>N

We now consider the case
sp>N
. We provide some auxiliary statements.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
and
p\in[1,+\infty]
be such that
sp>N
.
Let

(4.14)

\overline{s}\in\left(\frac{sp-N}{p},s\right]\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\overline{p}:=\frac{Np}{N-(s-\overline{s})p}.

Let
\Omega
be either
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or a bounded Lipschitz domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Then,
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
W^{\overline{s},\overline{p}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

To this end, notice that if
\overline{s}=s
then
\overline{p}=p
,
and therefore the claim is trivial.

Otherwise, we observe that,
if
\overline{s}
and
\overline{p}
satisfy (
4.14
),
then the point
(\overline{s},\overline{p})
belongs to the curve

\left(s-\frac{\theta N}{p},\frac{p}{1-\theta}\right)\quad{\mbox{with }}\theta\in[0,1].

This says that we are in the setting of
Theorem
3.1
with
s_{1}:=\frac{sp-N}{p}
,

p_{1}:=+\infty
,
s_{2}:=s
and
p_{2}:=p
. Therefore,
we can exploit the claim of Theorem
3.1

with
s_{\theta}:=\overline{s}
and
p_{\theta}:=\overline{p}
.
In this way, we obtain that
there exists a constant
C=C(N,s,p,\theta)>0
such that

\|u\|_{W^{\overline{s},\overline{p}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|u\|_{C^{0,\frac{sp-N}{p}}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}.

From this and the continuous embedding of
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
in
C^{0,\frac{sp-N}{p}}(\Omega)
(see
[
6
, Theorem 8.2]
),
we thus conclude that

\|u\|_{W^{\overline{s},\overline{p}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)},

which gives the desired result.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
and
p\in[1,+\infty]
.
Let
\widetilde{s}\in[0,s)
.
Let
\Omega
be either
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or a bounded Lipschitz domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Then,
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
W^{\widetilde{s},p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We notice that the point
(\widetilde{s},p)
belongs to the curve
\big((1-\theta)s,p\big)
, with
\theta\in(0,1]
.
As a consequence,
we can use Theorem
3.1
with
s_{1}:=0
,

p_{1}:=p
,
s_{2}:=s
,
p_{2}:=p
,
s_{\theta}:=\widetilde{s}
and
p_{\theta}:=p
, obtaining that there exists a constant
C=C(N,s,p,\theta)>0
such that

\|u\|_{W^{\widetilde{s},p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\theta}\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}\leqslant C\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)},

which implies the desired claim.
∎

We will also need the following observation:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
and
p\in[1,+\infty]
be such that
sp>N
. Let
\widetilde{s}\in[0,s]
and
\widetilde{p}\in[1,+\infty]
.

Let
\gamma
be the curve defined in (
1.5
).
Let
0\leqslant\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}\leqslant 1
and let
\gamma(\theta_{1})=(s_{\theta_{1}},p_{\theta_{1}})

and
\gamma(\theta_{2})=(s_{\theta_{2}},p_{\theta_{2}})
.

Then, we have that
0\leqslant s_{\theta_{2}}\leqslant s_{\theta_{1}}\leqslant 1
and that
\min\{p,\widetilde{p}\}\leqslant p_{\theta_{1}},p_{\theta_{2}}\leqslant+\infty
.

If
p\leqslant\widetilde{p}
, then
p_{\theta_{1}}\leqslant p_{\theta_{2}}
.

Furthermore, if

(4.15)

\dfrac{sp-N}{p}<\widetilde{s}\leqslant s\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad 1\leqslant\widetilde{p}\leqslant\dfrac{Np}{N-(s-\widetilde{s})p},

then

(4.16)

\dfrac{s_{\theta_{1}}p_{\theta_{1}}-N}{p_{\theta_{1}}}<s_{\theta_{2}}\leqslant s_{\theta_{1}}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad 1\leqslant p_{\theta_{2}}\leqslant\dfrac{Np_{\theta_{1}}}{N-(s_{\theta_{1}}-s_{\theta_{2}})p_{\theta_{1}}}.

Moreover,
s_{\theta_{1}}=s_{\theta_{2}}
if and only if
s=\widetilde{s}
,
and
p_{\theta_{1}}=p_{\theta_{2}}
if and only if
p=\widetilde{p}
.

Also,

p_{\theta_{2}}=\frac{Np_{\theta_{1}}}{N-(s_{\theta_{1}}-s_{\theta_{2}})p_{\theta_{1}}}

if and only if

\widetilde{p}=\frac{Np}{N-(s-\widetilde{s})p}.

Proof.

By inspection, one sees that
0\leqslant s_{\theta_{2}}\leqslant s_{\theta_{1}}\leqslant 1

and
p_{\theta_{1}}
,
p_{\theta_{2}}\leqslant+\infty
. Also, the fact that
p_{\theta}\geqslant\min\{p,\widetilde{p}\}
for all
\theta\in[0,1]
follows from the
computations in (
4.5
).

If in addition
p\leqslant\widetilde{p}
, by the definition of
p_{\theta}

it follows that
p_{\theta_{1}}\leqslant p_{\theta_{2}}
.

We now check that (
4.15
)
implies (
4.16
).
For this, we point out that

\displaystyle s_{\theta_{2}}-\dfrac{s_{\theta_{1}}p_{\theta_{1}}-N}{p_{\theta_{1}}}=s_{\theta_{2}}-s_{\theta_{1}}+\frac{N}{p_{\theta_{1}}}=(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})(\widetilde{s}-s)+\frac{N}{p}+N\theta_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}-\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geqslant(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})(\widetilde{s}-s)+\frac{N}{p}-N\theta_{1}\left(\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{N}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\theta_{2}(\widetilde{s}-s)+\frac{N}{p}>-\frac{N\theta_{2}}{p}+\frac{N}{p}\geqslant 0,

which gives the first claim in (
4.16
).

Also,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{\theta_{2}}}-\dfrac{N-(s_{\theta_{1}}-s_{\theta_{2}})p_{\theta_{1}}}{Np_{\theta_{1}}}=\frac{1}{p_{\theta_{2}}}-\frac{1}{p_{\theta_{1}}}+\dfrac{s_{\theta_{1}}-s_{\theta_{2}}}{N}

\displaystyle\quad=(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})\left(\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}-\frac{1}{p}\right)+\frac{(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})(s-\widetilde{s})}{N}\geqslant(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})\left(\frac{\widetilde{s}-s}{N}+\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{N}\right)=0

which gives the second claim in (
4.16
).

Finally, the last statement follows from the definition in (
1.5
).
∎

We now establish Theorems
1.9
and
1.10
.

Proof of Theorems
1.9

and
1.10
.

In what follows,
\Omega
is either
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or an open and bounded
domain with Lipschitz boundary. In the first case, we suppose that (
1.16
) is in force, while in the latter case
we assume (
1.18
).

We provide the proof of the first statement of Theorems
1.9

and
1.10
.

We first consider the case
\widetilde{p}=+\infty
. Then, we are in the case
\widetilde{s}\in[0,(sp-N)/{p}]
.
From
[
6
, Theorem 8.2]
we know that
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
C^{0,\frac{sp-N}{p}}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, we have that
C^{0,\frac{sp-N}{p}}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
C^{0,\widetilde{s}}(\Omega)
. These considerations prove
that
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
C^{0,\widetilde{s}}(\Omega)
, which is the desired statement when
\widetilde{p}=+\infty
.

Now, we deal with the case
\widetilde{p}\in[p,+\infty)
.
We set

(4.17)

\overline{s}:=s-N\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\right)

and we point out that

\overline{s}\in\left(\frac{sp-N}{p},s\right]\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\widetilde{p}=\frac{Np}{N-(s-\overline{s})p}.

Thus, we are in the position of using Lemma
4.3

with
\overline{p}
replaced by
\widetilde{p}
and we deduce that
W^{s,p}(\Omega)

is continuously embedded in
W^{s-N\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\right),\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
.

Also, we claim that

(4.18)

\overline{s}\in[\widetilde{s},s].

Indeed, since
p\leqslant\widetilde{p}
, we have that
\overline{s}\leqslant s
.

Moreover, if
\widetilde{s}\in[0,(sp-N)/p]
, we see that

(4.19)

\widetilde{s}\leqslant s-\frac{N}{p}<s-N\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\right)=\overline{s}.

If instead
\widetilde{s}\in((sp-N)/p,s]
, we use the condition on
\widetilde{p}
in (
1.16
) to see that

\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\geqslant\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{p}

and therefore

(4.20)

\widetilde{s}\leqslant s=s-\frac{N}{p}+\frac{N}{p}\leqslant s-\frac{N}{p}+\frac{N}{\widetilde{p}}+\frac{(s-\widetilde{s})N}{p}=\overline{s}+\frac{(s-\widetilde{s})N}{p}\leqslant\overline{s}.

Gathering these observations, we obtain (
4.18
).

Thanks to (
4.18
), we can exploit Lemma
4.4
with
p

replaced by
\widetilde{p}
and
s
replaced by
\overline{s}
.
In this way, we obtain that
W^{s-N\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\right),\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
.
As a result, we conclude in this case that
W^{s,p}(\Omega)

is continuously embedded in
W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
,
as desired.

If
\Omega
is an open and bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary,
we also consider the case
\widetilde{p}\in[1,p)
. In this situation,
we employ Lemma
3.5
and we deduce that
W^{s,p}(\Omega)

is continuously embedded in
W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
.

This concludes the proof of the first statement of Theorems
1.9

and
1.10
.

We now prove the second claim of Theorems
1.9

and
1.10
.
For this, take
0\leqslant\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}\leqslant 1

and consider
\gamma(\theta_{1})=(s_{\theta_{1}},p_{\theta_{1}})

and
\gamma(\theta_{2})=(s_{\theta_{2}},p_{\theta_{2}})
,
where the curve
\gamma
has been defined in (
1.5
).
Lemma
4.5
allows us
to use the first statement of either Theorem
1.9

or Theorem
1.10

with
s:=s_{\theta_{1}}
,
p:=p_{\theta_{1}}
,
\widetilde{s}:=s_{\theta_{2}}

and
\widetilde{p}:=p_{\theta_{2}}
and obtain the desired embedding.
∎

Now, we prove the desired compact embedding in the case
sp>N
.

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

Let
\widetilde{s}
and
\widetilde{p}
satisfy (
1.20
).

If
\widetilde{p}=p
, the desired compact embedding is a direct
consequence of Lemma
2.7
. Thus, we now distinguish
the cases
\widetilde{p}>p
and
\widetilde{p}<p
.

If
\widetilde{p}\in(p,+\infty)
, we define
\overline{s}
as in (
4.17
)
and we recall that
\overline{s}\in[\widetilde{s},s)
(thanks to (
4.18
) and the fact that
\widetilde{p}\neq p
).

Also, if in particular
\overline{s}\in((sp-N)/p,s)
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{Np}{N-(s-\overline{s})p}=\frac{Np}{N-N\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\right)p}=\frac{p}{1-\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}\right)p}=\widetilde{p}.

As a result, we find that
\overline{s}
and
\widetilde{p}
satisfy
the assumptions in (
1.18
). Therefore,
we can apply Theorem
1.10
and conclude that
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
W^{\overline{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
.

Notice also that
\overline{s}>\widetilde{s}

(recall the computations in (
4.19
) and (
4.20
) and use the fact that
\widetilde{s}<s
).
Hence, we deduce from Lemma
2.7
that
W^{\overline{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)

is compactly embedded in
W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
.
Combining these pieces of information,
we infer that the space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
, as desired.

Now, we deal with the case
\widetilde{p}<p
.
We observe that
\frac{s+\widetilde{s}}{2}\in(0,s)
.
Moreover, if
\frac{s+\widetilde{s}}{2}\in((sp-N)/p,s)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}>\frac{1}{p}>\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{2N}.

Hence,
\frac{s+\widetilde{s}}{2}
and
\widetilde{p}
satisfy
the assumptions in (
1.18
). Therefore,
we can apply Theorem
1.10

to see that
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
W^{\frac{s+\widetilde{s}}{2},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
.

Moreover, since
\frac{s+\widetilde{s}}{2}>\widetilde{s}
, from Lemma
2.7
we infer that
W^{\frac{s+\widetilde{s}}{2},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)

is compactly embedded in
W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
.
Gathering these considerations,
we conclude that the space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
W^{\widetilde{s},\widetilde{p}}(\Omega)
, which concludes the case
\widetilde{p}<p
.

Now, let
0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}\leqslant 1
and consider
\gamma(\theta_{1})=(s_{\theta_{1}},p_{\theta_{1}})

and
\gamma(\theta_{2})=(s_{\theta_{2}},p_{\theta_{2}})
,
where the curve
\gamma
has been defined in (
1.5
).
Then, Lemma
4.5

gives that we can use the first statement of Theorem
1.11

with
s:=s_{\theta_{1}}
,
p:=p_{\theta_{1}}
,
\widetilde{s}:=s_{\theta_{2}}

and
\widetilde{p}:=p_{\theta_{2}}
and obtain that
W^{s_{\theta_{1}},p_{\theta_{1}}}(\Omega)

is compactly embedded in
W^{s_{\theta_{2}},p_{\theta_{2}}}(\Omega)
,
as desired.
∎

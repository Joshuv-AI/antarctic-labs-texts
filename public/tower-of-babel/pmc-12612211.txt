Simultaneous photovoltaic distributed generation and capacitor optimization for enhancing performance indices of radial power distribution system
Authors: P. Rajakumar, P. M. Balasubramaniam, E. Parimalasundar, K. Suresh, P. Aravind
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper presents an effective metaheuristic framework using the Osprey Optimization Algorithm (OOA) for the simultaneous allocation of distributed generation (DG) units and capacitor banks (CB) in radial distribution systems (RDS). The method optimizes the locations and sizing for DG units and CB to minimize active power losses (APL), to reduce voltage deviation (VD), and to enhance voltage stability. The performance of the proposed approach is tested on IEEE 69-bus and 118-bus benchmark RDSs and the real-time Tala Egyptian RDS. The OOA achieved superior results compared to popular heuristic algorithms such as antlion optimizer (ALO), hunter-prey optimizer (HPO), and whale optimizer algorithm (WOA). Specifically, for three units of DG and single capacitor integration in the 69-bus system, OOA reduced the total APL by 75.1%, lowered the total voltage deviation (TVD) by 1.4835 p.u., and improved the total voltage stability index (TVSI) by 3.0229. With optimal assimilation of three units of DG and capacitors each, APL reduction, TVD minimization, and TVSI improvement further extended to 79.9%, 1.5013 p.u., and 2.2787, respectively. Furthermore, OOA validation on a variable-load 69-bus RDS and the real 37-bus Tala Egyptian RDS demonstrated consistent and superior performance, showcasing its robustness. Statistical analyses also confirm OOA’s efficiency and ability to solve DG planning in the distribution networks.

Introduction

Background of study

Distributed generation (DG) integration has become a prominent research area in modern power systems. DG employs small-scale electric power generators ranging from a few kW to several MW in capacity. In contrast to large centralized power generation plants, DG units connect directly to the distribution power networks (DPN). Integrating distributed generation (DG) into distribution power networks (DPN) provides several benefits, such as improving the voltage profile, reducing both active and reactive power losses, enhancing system stability, and improving overall power quality1. The power system networks have encountered severe power losses (PL), voltage deviations (VD), and instability issues in recent times due to an increase in the number of consumers. The erection and upgrading of transmission and distribution power networks to balance this electricity escalation involves high capital investments2. However, integrating low-cost DG units into existing power networks can significantly reduce investment costs, minimize power losses (PL), reduce voltage deviation (VD), and minimize the risk of instability.

DG units leverage minimum impact over voltage profile improvement, since it only supplies active power (AP) into the radial distribution system (RDS). Therefore, capacitors are simultaneously placed with DG units to provide adequate reactive power (RP) support for better voltage regulation. However, the locations and sizes of DG and capacitor units need to be optimized appropriately in the RDS for achieving the desired technical and economic benefits. Conversely, non-optimized inclusion of DG and capacitor units severely increases the VD and PL. Hence, an efficient optimization framework is essential for solving the multi-objective problem of simultaneous DG and capacitor placement to achieve optimal outcomes.

Literature study

Numerous research works were presented in the literature to address the independent and simultaneous DG and capacitor unit placement and sizing problem. A shark optimization algorithm (SOA) was capitalized to size the DG units for reducing APL, improving VP, and enhancing stability of RDS3. Loss sensitivity factor driven simulated annealing algorithm (SSA) was proposed to optimize DG units for minimizing APL and VD of IEEE benchmark RDSs4. An improved symbiotic organisms search (SOS) algorithm was utilized for optimizing several types of DG units to minimize total APL, to enhance VP, and to curtail the risk of instability in various benchmark RDSs5. Multiple units of DGs were optimally assimilated using the chaotic sine cosine algorithm (CSCA) for solving single- and multi-objective framework problems6. The researchers have applied the harmony search algorithm (HSA) to optimize capacitor placement and rating in balanced and unbalanced RDSs7. An integrated technique capitalizing on the benefits of fuzzy and genetic algorithms (GA) was introduced to enhance the performance of variable load RDS via optimal capacitor allocation8. GA-integrated particle swarm optimization (PSO)9, water cycle algorithm10, and backtracking search algorithm11 were introduced to optimally assimilate DG units for enhancing the technical performance of RDS. An improved HSA technique was executed to optimally deploy DG units for minimizing AP losses and bus voltage deviation of distribution power networks (DPN)12. The elephant herd optimization (EHO) approach has been adopted for optimizing DG location and size to enhance technical indices of RDS13. A quasi-oppositional swine influenza model (QOSIM) optimization technique was proposed to optimize DG units into the RDS for achieving various technological benefits14. The whale optimizer algorithm (WOA)-inspired method was presented for discovering a feasible solution to a multi-objective DG allocation problem15. The weighted sum approach was utilized in the proposed optimization study to solve the multi-objective problem. The researchers proposed improved versions of cat swarm optimizer (CSO), PSO, and GA for optimizing the DG units to reduce APL and to minimize VD16. The improved CSO algorithm produced the better solution compared to the other two algorithms. An integrated analytical and metaheuristic algorithm approach has been proposed to optimize capacitor and DG placement and rating in RDS17. The multi-objective simultaneous DG and capacitor placement and sizing problem has been solved using the PSO method18. The DG and capacitor (DG-C) location and size were optimized into the RDS to minimize AP losses, to enhance the voltage profile, and to reduce operational cost. The optimal locations and sizes for DG and capacitor units were determined by implementing a multi-objective non-dominated sorting GA-II to minimize AP losses and to improve voltage stability of the network19. The simulation study was realized on the 33-bus benchmark IEEE RDS. A novel method was introduced using the puma optimization algorithm (POA) to locate the best position and size for three distinct types of DG units in 33-bus and 69-bus RDSs20. DGs were optimized for minimizing AP losses and enhancing VSI. The effectiveness of the novel quasi-oppositional artificial rabbits optimization algorithm (QOAROA) was explored for the multi-objective DG inclusion problem in 33-bus, 69-bus, and 118-bus benchmark power networks21. The effect of optimal DG integration has been investigated on the PL, VD, and operating cost of RDS. Authors employed a novel electric eel foraging optimization (EEFO) algorithm to address the impact of optimal DG placement and sizing in 33-bus and 84-bus RDSs for different loads22. A multi-objective DG framework including AP and RP losses minimization and VD reduction was solved using the proposed EEFO algorithm. Optimal size and location for DG units were identified, proposing a hybrid method that combines levy flight (LF) and the aquila optimization algorithm (AOA)23. The efficacy of the hybrid method was investigated on IEEE benchmarking RDSs for APL minimization and VP improvement. An efficient technique was introduced using the memory-based gorilla troops optimizer algorithm to optimize biomass DG units in the unbalanced 37-bus RDS24. The proposed technique reported better results by addressing the shortcomings of several established algorithms. The secretary bird optimization algorithm (SBOA) was utilized to frame a new methodology for optimizing the biomass DG unit’s placement and size in the RDS25. The Competitive Swarm Optimizer (CSO) algorithm was applied to find the best location and capacity of DG and capacitor units for minimizing the multi-objective fitness function, including APL, operational cost, and total VD26. The CSO-applied methodology was realized on the 34-bus IEEE standard RDS. The distributed energy resources (DERs) placement and sizing problem was solved for enhancing the reliability and voltage stability27. The suggested work was implemented on the two practical RDSs of Ethiopia. Researchers analyzed the impact of hybrid DER power generation units, focusing on objectives related to energy sustainability, technological feasibility, reliability, and environmental issues28. An oppositional-based WOA-tuned fractional-order fuzzy PID controller was proposed to regulate frequency deviation and power deviation in between the generation systems29. The simulation study was executed for hybrid renewable energy power sources. An efficient metaheuristic bit torrent (EM-BT) algorithm was suggested to optimize PV DER and capacitor banks for minimizing APL and improving voltage stability of RDS30. The simulation study was conducted considering 24 h of variable loads, irradiance variation, and temperature effects. A novel hybrid power system combining the SMES and PHES was optimally allocated into the Ethiopian power distribution system to increase the reliability and cost efficiency31. For enhancing financial stability, improving reliability, and minimizing GHG emissions, the suitable size of a hybrid DER was found using metaheuristic optimization algorithms such as non-dominated WOA, multi-objective GWO, and multi-objective PSO32. A metaheuristic approach based on enhanced WOA was proposed to spot the optimal site and to determine the optimal size of hybrid PV–biogas with SMES and PHES33. The HSA-driven technique was applied to integrate 12.25 MW BESS into the RDS for minimizing the cost of energy generation34. The practicality of the work was examined by calculating numerous dependability indices. DG and capacitor unit positions and ratings have been discovered using the thief and police algorithm (TPA) to minimize AP losses, to reduce operating costs (OC), and to curtail carbon emissions35. Reference36 adopted a hybrid method to optimize DG and capacitor units in RDS. The optimal locations were found using voltage stability index (VSI) computation, and the ratings were optimized using GA. The grasshopper optimization (GHO) algorithm was used to solve the multi-objective simultaneous DG and capacitor unit optimization problem with the presence of electric vehicle (EV) charging stations37. Likewise, bald eagle search (BES)38 and moth swarm algorithm (MSA)39-based methods were recommended to optimize DG and capacitor allocations in RDS for PL minimization. A hybrid ACO-ABC method was suggested for computing the best solution to a DG placement problem. The DG unit sites and sizes were identified to enhance voltage stability and to minimize APL of RDS40. The hybrid TS-GA method has been presented for locating the appropriate buses and determining the ratings of DG units. The proposed hybrid approach optimized the DG units to minimize PL and to enhance the voltage profile along the RDS41. Also, the renewable energy DG units were optimized using a novel hybrid technique combining PSO and crow search (CS) algorithms42.

Novelty of the proposed work

Most of the metaheuristic algorithms cited in Table 1 often report premature convergence due to poor exploitation or exploration. Unlike the above algorithms, OOA dynamically regulates the osprey’s movement according to the prey’s location, resulting in a better diversity of solutions for a complex optimization problem. OOA incorporates memory structure to preserve the best solution across the various generations, which directs the new solutions intelligently near to the global optimum. This evades retracing weak areas of the search space, unlike the random exploration of ALO and GWO. Adaptive hunting behavior, a solid exploration–exploitation trade-off, and memory-supervised convergence of OOA make it a highly appropriate choice for solving a complex and mixed-integer problem like ODGCA. Compared to the several traditional algorithms, OOA possesses an adaptive hunting behavior and balanced exploration–exploitation49. Additionally, OOA has proven its superiority over other algorithms by delivering the best optimal solution for several CEC-2017 benchmark functions.

Performance indexes

The proposed OOA approach is framed to optimally integrate both DG and capacitor units simultaneously for minimizing TAPL, reducing voltage deviation (VD), and minimizing the voltage instability risk. The mathematical definition for the multiple objectives is given below.

Total active power losses (TAPL)

The final objective is to minimize the voltage instability risk. VSI measurement provides information about the stability of a bus in scalar form. A VSI value closer to zero indicates a state of voltage instability, whereas a value closer to 1.0 denotes a stable condition. Therefore, DG and capacitor units should be optimally allocated to increase the TVSI and reduce the risk of voltage instability.

Osprey optimizer algorithm: a solution technique

The osprey is a bird famous for its agility, smartness, and hunting accuracy. The osprey flies high above the water surface to locate the prey (fish). Once it locates the prey, it plunges into the water to capture the fish with incredible precision. The Osprey optimizer algorithm (OOA) is a metaheuristic algorithm developed inspired by the hunting behavior of the osprey49. Osprey’s searching process can be mathematically modelled and applied to find optimal solutions for a wide range of problems.

Mathematical modelling

The exploration and exploitation process of OOA is deliberated to simulate the osprey’s character. Every search agent (osprey) in the population gives a solution to the problem variables. However, the solution may differ with respect to their position. Therefore, the candidate solution for the problem variables is represented in a vector. The population is expressed as a matrix in Eq. (5). At the beginning of OOA, the osprey position is initialized randomly by Eq. (6).5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Y = \left[ {\begin{array}{*{20}c} {Y_{1} } \\ \vdots \\ {_{{}} } \\ \vdots \\ {Y_{Z} } \\ \end{array} } \right]_{N \times m} = \left[ {\begin{array}{*{20}c} {y_{1,1} } & \ldots & {y_{1,j} } & \cdots & {y_{1,m} } \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & {\mathinner{\mkern2mu\raise1pt\hbox{.}\mkern2mu \raise4pt\hbox{.}\mkern2mu\raise7pt\hbox{.}\mkern1mu}} & \vdots \\ {} & {} & {} & {} & {} \\ \vdots & {\mathinner{\mkern2mu\raise1pt\hbox{.}\mkern2mu \raise4pt\hbox{.}\mkern2mu\raise7pt\hbox{.}\mkern1mu}} & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ {y_{Z,1} } & \cdots & {y_{Z,j} } & \cdots & {y_{Z,m} } \\ \end{array} } \right]_{Z \times m}$$\end{document}6\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y_{i,j} = Lb_{j} + R_{i,j} \cdot \left( {Hb_{j} - Lb_{j} } \right)$$\end{document}

The fitness value (F) for the objective function is also expressed as a matrix in Eq. (7). The size of the matrix is relying on the size of the populations.7\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F = \left[ {\begin{array}{*{20}c} {F_{1} } \\ \vdots \\ \vdots \\ \vdots \\ {F_{N} } \\ \end{array} } \right]_{N \times 1} = \left[ {\begin{array}{*{20}c} {F(Y_{1} )} \\ \vdots \\ \vdots \\ \vdots \\ {F(Y_{M} )} \\ \end{array} } \right]_{M \times 1}$$\end{document}

The fitness value for the objective function is determined in each iteration of the optimization study. The osprey that is responsible for the least fitness value after an iteration process is considered the best candidate solution, called “elite”. Since the osprey relocates during every iteration, the elite solution should also be updated accordingly after the completion of every iteration.

Exploration

In this phase of the algorithm, the population size gets updated. The location of the hunter (osprey) changes after it catches the prey (fish). This process of the algorithm enhances the exploration feature to escape from local optima entrapment. Moreover, the fittest ospreys in the search space, referring to every osprey, are characterized as fishes (prey), as expressed in Eq. (8).8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$FP_{i} = \left\{ {\left. {Y_{k} } \right|k \in \left\{ {1,2, \ldots ,N} \right\} \wedge F_{k} < F_{i} } \right\} \cup \left\{ {Y_{best} } \right\}$$\end{document}

where, Ybest is the elite solution.

Now the osprey tries to hook the fish located in the search space randomly. In pursuit of catching a fish, the osprey relocates its position in the search space. where, ‘SF’ is a random fish and. ‘I’ is a random number between {1 and 2}.

The movement of osprey depends on both a random number ‘I’ and scaling factor ‘R’. These values are typically reduced during the iteration process. During the early part of the execution of the algorithm, the ‘R’ variation is kept large, causing big movement in the search space, allowing a wider search. But, in the later part, the scaling factor variation is limited to small, causing small adjustments in the osprey movement. Exploitation

Once the osprey hunts the prey, it carries it to a suitable location and consumes it to satiate its appetite. OOA models this behavior as an exploitation phase using Eq. (11). The location of the osprey in the search space changes following the hunting scene. OOA intelligently models this behavior of ospreys as an exploitation process. It is a local search, which exploits the search space near the best-known solution.11a\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y_{i,j}^{P2} = y_{i,j} + \frac{{Lb_{j} + R \cdot \left( {Hb_{j} - Lb_{j} } \right)}}{t}$$\end{document}11b\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y_{i,j}^{P2} = \left\{ {\begin{array}{*{20}c} {y_{i,j}^{P2} } \\ {Lb_{j} } \\ {\begin{array}{*{20}c} {Hb_{j} } & {\begin{array}{*{20}c} , & {if} \\ \end{array} } \\ \end{array} } \\ \end{array} ,} \right.\begin{array}{*{20}c} {\begin{array}{*{20}c} , & {if} & {Lb_{j} \le y_{i,j}^{P2} \le Ub_{j} } \\ \end{array} } \\ {\begin{array}{*{20}c} {if} & {y_{i,j}^{P2} < Lb_{j} } \\ \end{array} } \\ {y_{i,j}^{P2} > Hb_{j} } \\ \end{array}$$\end{document}

Step 4 Increase the iteration count by 1. For iteration = 2 to Iter,max, Apply the exploitation process using Eq. (9) and the exploration principle using Eq. (11) to optimize the site and size of DG and capacitor units, respectively.

Step 5 Run the power flow and find the fitness value for the updated population. Compare the fitness values. If the fitness value is less than the previous best value, replace the elite solution.

Step 6 Is the maximum iteration reached? If yes, move to the next step. Otherwise, move to Step 4.

Step 7 Print the global optima results.

Authors recommendation

The simulation discoveries of the present study outline the significance of simultaneous allocation of DG and capacitor units to achieve stable power loss reduction, voltage enhancement, and stability improvement. Referring to the efficient and robust performance of OOA on both IEEE standard RDSs and a practical RDS, distribution utilities must come forward to adopt the proposed OOA optimization framework as a practical planning tool. However, utilities should begin realizing the proposed method with small- and medium-scale RDSs before deploying it to larger and complex RDSs.

Conclusion

The suggested OOA optimization technique was effectively executed to optimally allocate multiple DG and capacitor units in RDS. The OOA metaheuristic technique investigated the impact of ODGCA on APL, voltage deviation, and voltage stability of IEEE 69-bus and 118-bus RDSs and the real-world Tala RDS. The proposed framework has shown its superiority over established heuristic search algorithms such as HPO, ALO, and WOA by yielding improved technological performance and better convergence behavior. Compared to other algorithms, OOA has given a maximum APL reduction of 79.9% for optimized inclusion of three DG units and three capacitors in 69-bus RDS. Likewise, 81.97% of APL reduction was obtained for optimized five DG units and three capacitor placements in the 118-bus RDS. Additionally, OOA produced more consistent voltage profile enhancement and voltage stability improvement than HPO, ALO, and WOA. OOA also supremely performed to reach an optimal solution with a lesser number of iterations and minimum computation time per run than the rest of the algorithms. The statistical evaluation proved consistency of OOA, producing minimum standard deviation across multiple simulation runs. The simulation outcome of IEEE benchmark RDSs and real-world Tala RDSs establishes the proposed OOA optimization framework as the appropriate heuristic approach for DG planning in the practical power system networks. Unbalanced distribution network study, optimal integration of EV charging stations, and validation of results with hardware-in-the-loop are the potential future directions for the present work.

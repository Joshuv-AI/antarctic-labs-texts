Description of Albimonas marina sp. nov., isolated from sea sand on the Yellow Sea Coast
Authors: Hyunwoo Jung, Young Rok Kim, Eunseo Cho, Jung-Sook Lee, Jung-Hoon Yoon, Ampaitip Sukhoom, Wonyong Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA Gram-stain-negative, anaerobic, short rod-shaped bacterium, designated as CAU 1670T, was isolated from a sea sand sample collected from Masiran Beach, Republic of Korea. Optimal growth conditions for the bacterium were identified as growth media containing 0–3% (w/v) NaCl at 30 °C and pH 5.5. Strain CAU 1670T exhibited high 16S rRNA gene sequence similarity with Albimonas pacifica CGMCC 1.11030T (97.8% similarity) and Albimonas donghaensis DSM 17,890T (94.8% similarity). The phylogeny tree based on the 16 S rRNA gene and core housekeeping gene sequences showed that strain CAU 1670T belonged to the genus Albimonas. The major fatty acids included summed feature 8 (C18:1 ω7c and/or C18:1 ω6c), and the predominant respiratory quinone was Q-10. The G + C content of the type strain genome was 72.6%. The average nucleotide identity and digital DNA–DNA hybridization values between strain CAU 1670T and reference strains were 78.5–86.4% and 22.1–31.0%, respectively. Pan-genome analysis demonstrated that strain CAU 1670T and reference strains shared 2,010 core genes. Based on phenotypic, phylogenetic, and chemotaxonomic characteristics, strain CAU 1670T represents a novel species within the genus Albimonas, for which the name Albimonas marina sp. nov. is proposed. The type strain is CAU 1670T (= KCTC 92734T; = MCCC 1K08481T).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00284-025-04587-2.

Introduction

The genus Albimonas, classified to the family Rhodobacteraceae in the class Alphaproteobacteria, was first proposed by Lim et al. [1]. To date, the genus Albimonas comprises two species, Albimonas donghaensis and Albimonas pacifica [2], validly published under the International Code of Nomenclature of Prokaryotes (ICNP), and listed in the List of Prokaryotic names with Standing in Nomenclature (LPSN; https://lpsn.dsmz.de/, accessed on 11 August 2025). The species of the genus Albimonas are Gram-stain-negative, aerobic, and rod-shaped, with Q-10 as the major respiratory quinone and C18:1 ω7c as the major fatty acid. Members of the genus Albimonas have been isolated from marine environments, especially seawater. This study was driven by the ecological importance of coastal sediments, which serve as reservoirs for phylogenetically diverse and metabolically versatile bacterial communities. Despite their potential, marine sandy environments—such as those found at Masiran Beach—remain largely underexplored. These habitats may harbor previously uncharacterized microbial taxa with promising biotechnological applications, particularly in the areas of nutrient cycling and the production of bioactive secondary metabolites. During the screening of novel bacteria with biotechnology potential, strain CAU 1670T was isolated from a sea sand sample. In this study, a novel Albimonas strain, designated as CAU 1670T, was characterized using a polyphasic approach including genomic, chemotaxonomic, and physiological analyses to determine its taxonomic properties.

Materials and Methods

Isolation and Culture Conditions

For bacterial isolation from a sea sand sample collected from Masiran Beach in Incheon, Republic of Korea (37°26’00.7"N 126°24’53.3"E), 1 g of the sea sand sample was placed in autoclaved 0.85% (w/v) NaCl solution and serially diluted. Each of the diluted samples was spread on Marine Agar 2216 (MA). After the MA plates were placed in an incubator at 30 °C for 5 days, colonies with a cream color were separated and purified by re-streaking on fresh MA plates. Among the colonies exhibiting different morphologies, strain CAU 1670T was selected for further characterization. It represented a distinct colony phenotype and showed relatively low 16 S rRNA gene sequence similarity to recognized species of Albimonas. Although the relative abundance of the novel taxon in the sand sample could not be quantified, CAU 1670T was recovered as a minority colony type, whereas most isolates were affiliated with previously described members of the family Rhodobacteraceae. The purified strain CAU 1670T, which was dissolved in 25% (v/v) glycerol with Marine Broth 2216 (MB; Difco, Detroit, MI, USA), was stored at −80 °C. Strain CAU 1670T was deposited at the Korean Collection for Type Cultures (KCTC; Jeongeup, Korea) and the Marine Culture Collection of China (MCCC; Fujian, China) under the accession numbers KCTC 92,734T and MCCC 1K08481T, respectively. Albimonas pacifica KACC 16,527T and Albimonas donghaensis KCTC 12,586T were selected as reference strains based on 16 S rRNA sequence similarity and acquired from the Korean Agricultural Culture Collection (KACC; Wanju, Korea) and the KCTC, respectively.

16 S rRNA Gene Sequence and Phylogenetic Tree

The full-length 16 S rRNA gene sequence of strain CAU 1670T was extracted from the whole-genome sequence and compared with sequences available in the EzBioCloud database (http://www.ezbiocloud.net/identify) using Basic Local Alignment Search Tool (BLAST; https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi). Multiple sequence alignment was performed using ClustalW [3] with MEGA 7 software [4]. Phylogenetic analysis based on the 16 S rRNA sequence of strain CAU 1670T was also conducted using MEGA 7 software. The neighbor-joining (NJ) [5], maximum-likelihood (ML) [6], and maximum parsimony (MP) [7] methods were applied to generate phylogenetic trees. Genetic distances were assessed using the Kimura two-parameter model [8], and the tree topology was evaluated by bootstrap analysis with 1,000 random replications for each method [9]. To ensure the authenticity of the genome, the 16 S rRNA gene sequence obtained from PCR was compared with the 16 S rRNA gene sequence extracted from the whole genome (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/ NZ_JARHUC000000000.1) by aligning sequences with BLAST.

Whole-genome Sequencing and Genome Analysis

Whole-genome sequencing of strain CAU 1670T was performed by Macrogen (Seoul, Korea) using the Illumina Hiseq Sequencer, and de novo assembly was conducted with SPAdes version 3.15.0. The G + C content of the genomic DNA was measured. For genome analysis, the genomic data were obtained from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database. Rapid Annotation using Subsystem Technology (RAST) [10] and PATRIC (ver 3.35.5) [11] were utilized to annotate the whole-genome sequence. In addition, eggNOG-mapper (ver 5.0) was used to obtain and compare information on the protein function of strain CAU 1670T and reference strains according to the Clusters of Orthologous Groups (COG) [12]. The contamination and completeness of the genome were predicted with CheckM (ver 1.0.18) from the KBase online platform [13] and BUSCO (ver 3.0.2). A total of 15 reference strains exhibiting high similarity to the 16 S rRNA sequence from strain CAU 1670T and available genomic data were obtained from EzBioCloud. The phylogenetic tree was reconstructed using an up-to-date bacterial core gene (UBCG) program [14], which relies on 92 core housekeeping genes from whole bacterial genomes. Analysis of secondary metabolite biosynthesis gene clusters was carried out by antiSMASH (ver 7.1.0) [15]. The dbCAN3 annotation tool comprising HMMER, DIAMOND, Hotpep, and CGC-Finder [16] was used to predict the carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes) of strain CAU 1670T.

Comparative Genome Analysis

For pan-genome analysis, the genome sequence data of strain CAU 1670T and two reference strains acquired from the NCBI database were annotated by Prokka (ver 1.14.6) [17]. Pan-genome calculation was performed using Roary (ver 3.13.0) with a minimum sequence identity of 70% [18]. The results were visualized with the R program and Phandango server (https://jameshadfield.github.io/phandango) [19]. Between strain CAU 1670T and reference strains affiliated to the genus Albimonas, the values of average nucleotide identity (ANI) and digital DNA–DNA hybridization (dDDH) were calculated using the OrthoANI algorithm [20] and Genome-to-Genome Distance Calculator (GGDC 3.0; http://ggdc.dsmz.de/ggdc.php) [21], respectively. Average Amino Acid Identity (AAI) values were obtained with the EzAAI pipeline (v1.2.4) implemented in a Conda environment. Gene prediction was performed using Prodigal, and pairwise protein similarity was assessed with MMseqs2, following the approach outlined by Kim et al. [22]. Furthermore, comparative gene cluster analysis between strains in a Venn diagram was performed by the OrthoVenn3 online server (https://orthovenn3.bioinfotoolkits.net/home) [23].

Morphological, physiological, and Biochemical Analysis

The growth of strain CAU 1670T was tested on several media including MA, tryptic soy agar (TSA), brain heart infusion (BHI) agar, nutrient agar (NA), Luria-Bertani (LB) agar, and glucose yeast extract (GYE) agar under identical conditions (30 °C, aerobic, and 7 days). Growth was examined at 4 °C, 10 °C, 20 °C, 30 °C, 37 °C, and 45 °C, and the pH optimal for growth was evaluated from pH 4.5 to 12.0 (intervals of 0.5 pH units) separately. The pH level was adjusted with sterile biological buffers (1 N H2SO4; pH 4.5–6.5, 2 N NaOH; pH 7.0–12.0). Anaerobic growth was investigated using a Bactron anaerobic chamber (Sheldon Manufacturing Inc., Cornelius, OR, USA). The effect of NaCl on growth was measured by culturing the strains in LB with various NaCl concentrations (0–15%; intervals of 1%, w/v) at 30 °C [24]. After strain CAU 1670T was cultured on MA for 3 days at 30℃, cellular morphology was observed by light microscopy (Model DM1000; Leica Microsystems, Danaher Corp., Wetzlar, Germany). In addition, the presence of flagella was determined by transmission electron microscopy (TEM; Model JEM 1010; JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). Motility was assessed by the hanging drop method [25], and Gram staining was performed with a Gram staining kit (bioMérieux, Marcy-l’Étoile, France) according to the manufacturer’s manual. The activities of catalase and oxidase were determined by adding H2O2 solution (3%, v/v) and tetramethyl-p-phenylenediamine (1% w/v) [26]. The method of Smibert and Krieg [27] was applied to investigate the hydrolysis of starch and casein. Following the manufacturer’s instructions, enzyme activities and other biochemical features were characterized using the API 20E, API 20NE, API 50CH, and API ZYM kits (bioMérieux, Marcy-l’Étoile, France).

Chemotaxonomic Analysis

Strain CAU 1670T and reference strains were lyophilized with methanol/isopropyl ether (3:1, v/v) [28] to extract respiratory quinones, which were identified by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). For fatty acid analysis, CAU 1670T and reference strains were grown on MB at 30 °C for 4 days. After the harvesting of strains in the late-exponential phase, cellular fatty acids were saponified, extracted, and methylated [29]. Profiles were analyzed using the Sherlock™ Microbial Identification System (Version 6.2; MIDI Inc., Newark, DE, USA; http://midi-inc.com/pages/microbial_id.html) with gas chromatography (Agilent Model 6890 N; Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA) [30].

Results and Discussion

Phylogenetic Analysis Based on the 16 S rRNA Gene Sequence

In sequence comparison, the nearly complete 16 S rRNA gene sequence of strain CAU 1670T (1,432 bp; GenBank accession number: OP388933) and the 16 S rRNA sequence extracted from the whole genome shared 99.7% (1432/1466) similarity. The 16 S rRNA gene sequence of strain CAU 1670T was compared with that of closely related strains based on the EzBioCloud database. Strain CAU 1670 was the most closely related to Albimonas pacifica CGMCC 1.11030T (97.8%), followed by Albimonas donghaensis DSM 17,890T (94.8%). A value below 98.7% similarity was used as the threshold to identify different bacterial species [31]. All phylogenetic trees based on the 16 S rRNA gene sequence were constructed using 25 type strains, and Escherichia coli ATCC 11,775T (AB681728) was selected as an outgroup. The phylogenetic tree constructed with the NJ algorithm showed that strain CAU 1670T was part of the genus Albimonas and clustered tightly with Albimonas pacifica CGMCC 1.11030T; this was supported by a bootstrap value of 99% (Fig. 1). Similarly, the trees generated with the ML and MP methods demonstrated a robust phylogenetic relationship (Fig. S1, S2).

Genomic Features

The de novo assembled sequence of strain CAU 1670T was 5.79 Mbp in genome size, containing 22 contigs, with 732,160 bp of N50 values and 371.2× k-mer coverage. A total of 5,268 protein-coding sequences (CDSs) were identified, including 49 tRNAs and 2 rRNAs. The genomic DNA G + C content was 72.6%, which was close to the values of Albimonas pacifica CGMCC 1.11030T (69.0%) and Albimonas donghaensis DSM 17,890T (72.0%) [1, 2]. The genome’s completeness was 100% according to the results of BUSCO, and the contamination level was 0.13% according to the results of CheckM. Differences in the genomic features between strain CAU 1670T and two reference strains are summarized in Table S1. Moreover, the circular genome map of strain CAU 1670T was generated, as shown in Fig. 2. In the phylogenetic tree based on 92 core housekeeping genes, strain CAU 1670T formed a phylogenetic lineage within the genus Albimonas (Fig. 3).

The genes of strain CAU 1670T were annotated by the RAST program and classified into 308 subsystems and 27 categories; the predominant subsystems (> 100 genes) of CAU 1670T were carbohydrates (317 genes), amino acids and derivatives (309 genes), protein metabolism (191 genes), nucleosides and nucleotides (140 genes), cofactors, vitamins, prosthetic groups, pigments (123 genes), and respiration (112 genes). The distribution of genes in subsystem categories among CAU 1670T and phylogenetically related members of the genus Albimonas based on the results of RAST is presented in Table S2. The results of genome analyses between CAU 1670T and reference strains are presented in Table S3. Members of the genus Albimonas harbored genes encoding for 5-FCL-like protein (FTHFL, MTHFR). In addition, genes related to oxidative stress (NSTR, SoxR, NnrS, AhpC, Irr) were found in the genome of strain CAU 1670T. In comparison with the reference strains, formate-tetrahydrofolate ligase, redox-sensitive transcriptional activator, and NnrS (protein involved in the response to NO) were exclusively present in CAU 1670T (Table S2). Strain CAU 1670T had secondary metabolite clusters encoding for terpene, homoserine lactone (hserlactone), and other unspecified ribosomally synthesized and post-translationally modified peptide (RiPP)-like products (Fig. S4, Table S4). A comparison of CAU 1670T and phylogenetically related species using antiSMASH is presented in Table S5. In addition, the distribution of COG categories for strain CAU 1670T is presented in Fig. S4. Based on the COG database, 4,237 CDS genes were divided into functional categories, the three most abundant COG categories, excluding [S] function unknown (782 CDS; 18.5%), were [E] amino acid transport and metabolism (560 CDSs; 13.2%), [C] energy production and conversion (343 CDSs; 8.1%), and [P] inorganic ion transport and metabolism (334 CDSs; 7.9%), which are associated with cellular metabolism. In this analysis, no genes were found in four categories: [A] RNA processing and modification, [R] general function prediction only, [W] extracellular structures, and [Y] nuclear structure. A comparison of COG categories with those of reference strains is shown in Table S6. The results of CAZyme analysis using all three annotation programs (high accuracy) revealed that the genome of CAU 1670T included 66 genes encoding CAZymes. The most abundant CAZymes were glycosyltransferases (GTs, 27), followed by glycoside hydrolases (GHs, 16), auxiliary activities (AAs, 15), carbohydrate esterases (CEs, 5), and carbohydrate-binding modules (CBMs, 3). Notably, a high number of the predicted CAZymes were correlated with the relevant COG functional category ([G] carbohydrate transport and metabolism, 260 CDSs). The predicted CAZymes of phylogenetically closely related strains are listed in Table S7.

Comparative Genome Analysis

The ANI values for the most closely related strains, Albimonas donghaensis and Albimonas pacifica, were 78.5% and 86.4%, respectively, whereas dDDH values were 22.1% and 31.0%, respectively. In addition, the average amino acid identity (AAI) values between strain CAU 1670T and the 2 reference strains were 86.8% with Albimonas pacifica CGMCC 1.11030T and 72.9% with Albimonas donghaensis DSM 17,890T (Table S8). These values were markedly lower than the cut-off thresholds for prokaryotic species (ANI, 95%; dDDH, 70%, AAI, 95–96%) [32–34]. further supporting the delineation of CAU 1670T as a distinct species. The genome size of strain CAU 1670T (5.79 Mbp) and its G + C content (72.6%) are consistent with the genus Albimonas, in which reported members show genome sizes of 5.0–6.0 Mbp and G + C contents ranging from 69.0 to 72.0%. Pan-genome analysis was performed to determine the total number of different genes present in strain CAU 1670T compared with 2 representative strains of Albimonas with whole-genome sequence data in the EzBioCloud database. The program generated a pan-genome of 8,514 genes, of which 2,010 genes were shared by all strains (core genes; 99% ≤ strains), and 6,504 genes were classified as accessory and unique genes. With the addition of genome sequences, the number of pan-genome genes was steadily increased (Fig. 4A); however, the number of core genes was sharply decreased initially (Fig. 4B). Significant genomic differences between the genomes of strain CAU 1670T and two reference strains were identified using a presence/absence gene matrix (Fig. 4C). Morphological, physiological, and Biochemical Features

Strain CAU 1670T was aerobic, Gram-stain-negative, non-spore-forming, short rod-shaped, and non-motile, with a cell size of 1.3–1.8 μm in width and 3.5–4.0 μm in length (Fig. S6). After incubation at 30 °C for 3 days under aerobic conditions, colonies on MA agar plates had a cream color and were punctiform with an entire margin, convex, smooth, and translucent. CAU 1670T grew well on MA agar, showed reduced growth on LB agar, and did not grow on GYE, NA, BHI, and TSA agar. The temperature and pH ranges in which the isolated strain was grown were 20–37 °C (optimum 30 °C) and pH 5.0–10.5 (optimum pH 5.5), respectively. The NaCl concentration range for growth was observed to be 0–9% (w/v), with optimal growth occurring at 0–3% (w/v). CAU 1670T was found to be catalase-positive and oxidase-positive. However, the hydrolysis of casein and starch was not observed. Chemotaxonomic Characteristics

The major respiratory quinone of CAU 1670T was Q-10, which is the predominant respiratory quinone found in Albimonas species. The predominant fatty acids (> 10%) of strain CAU 1670T were summed feature 8 (C18:1 ω7c and/or C18:1 ω6c; 25.5%), C19:0 cyclo ω8c (25.3%), C18:1 ω7c 11-methyl (13.5%), and C16:0, which are similar to those of phylogenetically closely related strains. Taxonomic Conclusion

According to phylogenetic research using the 16 S rRNA gene and genome, CAU 1670T was identified as belonging to the genus Albimonas. Phenotypic and chemotaxonomic features indicated that CAU 1670T represents a novel species of the genus Albimonas; thus, the name Albimonas marina sp. nov. is proposed.

Description of Albimonas marina sp. nov

Albimonas marina (ma.ri’na. L. fem. adj. marina, of the sea, marine).

Albimonas marina is Gram-stain-negative, aerobic, non-spore-forming, non-motile, and short rod-shaped, measuring approximately 3.5–4.0 μm in length and 1.3–1.8 μm in width. Colonies are punctiform, 0.5–1.0 mm in diameter, convex, smooth, translucent, and cream-colored after 3 days of incubation at 30 °C under aerobic conditions on marine agar. Optimal growth occurs at 30 °C (range 20–37 °C), pH 5.5 (range pH 5.0–10.5), and 1–3% NaCl (range 1–9%). Albimonas marina produces catalase and oxidase but does not hydrolyze casein or starch. Carbohydrate utilization tests indicate no acid production from tested carbohydrates. The species produces alkaline phosphatase, esterase (C4), esterase lipase (C8), leucine arylamidase, valine arylamidase, acid phosphatase, and naphthol-AS-BI-phosphohydrolase but not lipase (C14), trypsin, α-chymotrypsin, α-galactosidase, β-galactosidase, α-glucosidase, β-glucosidase, N-acetyl-β-glucosaminidase, α-mannosidase, or α-fucosidase. In biochemical assays, it produces l-tryptophan deaminase but does not produce β-galactosidase, hydrogen sulfide, urease, l-ornithine decarboxylase, indole, gelatinase, acetoin (Voges–Proskauer), or l-arginine dihydrolase, and does not ferment citrate, d-glucose, d-mannitol, inositol, d-sorbitol, l-rhamnose, d-sucrose, d-melibiose, amygdalin, or l-arabinose. Further biochemical characterization shows that it produces β-glucosidase but does not reduce nitrate, produce indole, ferment glucose, produce l-arginine dihydrolase, urease, gelatinase, or β-galactosidase, and does not assimilate d-glucose, d-arabinose, d-mannose, d-mannitol, maltose, adipic acid, malate, potassium gluconate, trisodium citrate, N-acetyl-glucosamine, capric acid, or phenylacetic acid. The major respiratory quinone is Q-10, and the predominant fatty acids are Summed feature 8, C19:0 cyclo ω8c, and C18:1 ω7c 11-methyl.

The type strain CAU 1670T (= KCTC 92734T; = MCCC 1K08481T) was isolated from a sea sand sample collected from Masiran beach in Incheon, Republic of Korea. The G + C content of the genomic DNA is 72.6%. The 16 S rRNA gene sequence and the genomic sequence of strain CAU 1670T have been deposited under the GenBank/EMBL/DDBJ accession numbers OP388933 and JARHUC000000000, respectively.

The Basic Reproduction Number for Bounded Linear Operators on Ordered Banach Spaces
Zachary Gregg, Patrick De Leenheer

Abstract
A basic reproduction number, R 0 R_{0} , is a concept encountered frequently in the study of ecological and epidemiological models. It is routinely used to determine the stability of an extinction or a disease-free fixed point or steady state. It is well-known that for linear models described by non-negative matrices, the spectral radius of the matrix is always contained in an interval with endpoints 1 1 and R 0 R_{0} . Here we extend these results to more general cone-preserving bounded linear operators acting on Banach spaces.

Introduction

A basic reproduction number, denoted
R_{0}
, is a tool used in ecological and epidemiological modeling as a marker for the stability of an extinction or disease-free fixed point or steady state; see
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
8

[

]
and Vandergraft
[
23
]
. In essence, for a linear operator whose spectral radius is difficult or even impossible to compute, a basic reproduction number serves as a proxy for that spectral radius, and is itself defined as the spectral radius of an associated linear operator. To make this statement precise, we review
a result in
[
17
]
by Li and Schneider who define basic reproduction numbers for discrete-time dynamical systems of the form
x_{n+1}=Ax_{n}
, where
x_{n}
is a non-negative vector (entry-wise) representing the state of the system at time step
n
, and
A
is a non-negative matrix (entry-wise). The spectral radius of the matrix
A
is denoted by
r(A)
, and defined as

r(A):=\max\{|\lambda|\text{ : }\lambda\text{ is an eigenvalue of }A\}.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem 3.3 in
[
17
]
)

Let
A
be a nonnegative matrix (entry-wise). Suppose
A
splits as
A=T+F
, where
T
and
F
are nonnegative matrices, and
r(T)<1
. Define the basic reproduction number
R_{0}:=r(F(I-T)^{-1})
. If
R_{0}>0
, then

r(T+\frac{1}{R_{0}}F)=1
, and exactly one of the following cases holds:

1.
R_{0}=r(A)=1,

2.
R_{0}\leq r(A)<1,

3.
R_{0}\geq r(A)>1.

Since
r(A)
being less than or greater than 1 determines the stability of the zero fixed point under the matrix
A
, this shows that
R_{0}
can also be used to determine the stability of the zero fixed point. This theorem gives a general version of the basic reproduction number that can be applied to the linearization of the zero steady state for any nonnegative discrete time dynamical system in
R^{n}
. Note that the basic reproduction number depends on the specific splitting,
A=T+F
, used. Some precautions about and examples using
R_{0}
can be found in
[
6
]
.

In 2009, Thieme gave an extension of this theorem that combines the theory of resolvent positive operators
[
2
]

[
25
]
, completely montone functions
[
14
]
[
20
]
, and superconvex (logconvex) operator families
[
13
]
[
12
]
. The definitions used in the statement of the theorem will be introduced in the following sections, but we state this now to give a comparison with the previous theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem 3.10 in
[
21
]
)
Let
X
be a Banach space with a generating and normal cone

. Suppose
A
is a bounded linear operator that preserves

, and

splits as

, where

and

are both

. Define

1.
R_{0}=1\iff r(A)=1

2.
R_{0}<1\iff r(A)<1

3.

Note that, unlike in the theorem by Li and Schneider, the previous theorem does not guarantee that

is a bound for the spectral radius of the operator

In order to prove this extension, we will need to make use of another theorem by Thieme

2
Background

2.1
Cones

The following generalizes the notion of nonnegativity to the Banach space setting. Throughout let
X\neq\{0\}
be a real Banach space,
X^{*}
the dual space of bounded (continuous) linear functionals on
X
, and
\mathcal{B}(X)
the Banach space of continuous linear operators from
X
to
X
.

Define a
cone

K\subseteq X
to be a closed convex set that is
positively homogeneous
(
x\in K
and
\alpha\geq 0
implies that
\alpha x\in K
), and
pointed
(if
x\in K
and
-x\in K
, then
x=0
). We say
A\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is a cone-preserving operator with respect to
K
, or
K
-preserving, if
A(K)\subseteq K
, i.e.
Ax\in K
for all
x\in K
. When the cone
K
is clear from context, we simply say
A
is cone-preserving. Note that a cone imposes a partial ordering,
``\geq,"
on the Banach space
X
defined by
z\geq y
for elements
y,z\in X
means
z-y\in K
. In addition to assuming that
X
is a Banach space, throughout we will assume that we have fixed a cone
K
that orders our Banach space in this way.

A cone is said to be
generating
if
X=K-K
, i.e. for any
x\in X
there exists
y,z\in K
such that
x=y-z
. It is easy to check that the set of cone-preserving linear operators forms a cone in
\mathcal{B}(X)
when
K
is a generating cone on
X
. Similarly, if
A
and
B
are cone-preserving operators on
X
with respect to
K
, we write
A\geq B
(using a slight abuse of notation since it should clear whether our objects are vectors in
X
vs
\mathcal{B}(X)
) to mean that
A-B
is cone-preserving with respect to
K
.

For a given cone
K
, define the
dual cone
to be the set

K^{*}=\{f\in X^{*}\text{ : }f(x)\geq 0\text{ }\forall x\in K\}.

Despite terminology,
K^{*}
may not satisfy the definitions to be a cone. For example, in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the usual inner product, and the cone given as the nonnegative
x-
axis,
K=\{(a,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\text{ : }a\geq 0\}
, the dual cone is

K^{*}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\text{ : }\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0\text{ }\forall x\in K\}=\{(a,b)\text{ : }a\geq 0\},

which is not pointed. However, whenever
K
is generating,
K^{*}
is a cone. (Actually it is simple consequence of a Hahn-Banach separation theorem that
K^{*}
is a cone if and only if
X=\overline{K-K}
, the topological closure of
K-K
. See chapter six in
[
7
]
for related discussion).

We say a cone is
normal
if there is a constant
\gamma>0
such that whenever
0\leq x\leq y
,
||x||\leq\gamma||y||
. It is well known that a cone is normal if and only if its dual cone is generating, and similarly a cone is generating if and only if its dual cone is normal (see proposition 19.4 in
[
7
]
).

Remark:
In Theorem
1.1
, Li and Schneider consider the Banach space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
ordered by the nonnegative orthant cone
K=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})\text{ : }x_{i}\geq 0\text{ }\forall i=1,2,...,n\}
. This cone is clearly generating: any vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is equal to the difference of two vectors with only nonnegative entries. This cone is also normal; in fact, any cone in a finite dimensional vectorspace is normal by the following lemma:

Lemma 2.1
.

(Lemma 1.2.5 in
[
16
]
) Any cone defined on a finite dimensional, real normed vector space is normal.

Thus, while we will later impose that the Banach space
X
is ordered by a generating and normal cone, this is not a restriction compared to Theorem
1.1
because the nonnegative orthant cone meets both requirements.

2.2
Spectral Theory

Here we review some spectral theory. While our main results are stated for bounded operators, we do need some generalized notions for linear operators which are not necessarily bounded. We refer to chapter IV in
[
10
]
for a more thorough discussion of this theory. For the following, let
A
be a linear operator with dense domain
D(A)\subseteq X
.
A
is assumed to be a closed operator, meaning that the graph of
A
is closed in
X\times X
. Note that any bounded operator on
X
is necessarily a closed operator by the closed graph theorem.

Though we are considering operators on a real Banach space, for various applications of spectral theory we consider the complexification of
(X,||\cdot||)
,
X_{\mathbb{C}}:=X+iX
, with norm given by

||x+iy||_{X_{\mathbb{C}}}=\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}||\cos(\theta)x+\sin(\theta)y||,

for
x,y\in X
. Given a closed operator
A
with domain
D(A)\subseteq X
, we can then extend
A
to its complexification
\tilde{A}
. The operator
\tilde{A}
is a linear operator with domain
D(\tilde{A})=\{x+iy\text{ : }x,y\in D(A)\}
dense in
X_{\mathbb{C}}
. The operator
\tilde{A}
is defined to act on
X_{\mathbb{C}}
by
\tilde{A}(x+iy)=Ax+iAy
, and is a closed operator when
A
is. If
A:X\to X
is bounded, then the norm on
X_{\mathbb{C}}
is such that
||A||=||\tilde{A}||_{X_{\mathbb{C}}}
. For the remainder of the section, we let
A
implicitly refer to the complexification
\tilde{A}
. Define the
resolvent set
of an operator
A
to be

\rho(A):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\text{ : }\lambda I-A\text{ is a bijection from $D(A)$ onto $X.$}\}.

For
\lambda\in\rho(A)
,
(\lambda I-A)^{-1}
is a well defined linear operator on
X
, and is called the
resolvent of
A
with respect to
\lambda
. Moreover, since
A
is a closed operator,
(\lambda I-A)^{-1}
is in fact a bounded linear operator. We will frequently let
I
be understood and instead write
(\lambda-A)^{-1}
. Define the
spectrum
of an operator
A
,
\sigma(A):=\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho(A)
.

For any closed operator, define the
spectral abscissa

s(A):=\sup\{Re(\lambda)\text{ : }\lambda\in\sigma(A)\}
, where
Re(\lambda)
is the real part of
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Note that we allow
s(A)=\infty
if this supremum is not finite, and
s(A)=-\infty
if
\sigma(A)=\emptyset
.

If
A
is a bounded linear operator, it is well known that
\sigma(A)
is a nonempty compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
(see for example Corollary 1.4 in chapter IV of
[
10
]
). For any bounded linear operator
A
, we define the
spectral radius
of
A
to be

r(A):=\sup\{|\lambda|\text{ : }\lambda\in\sigma(A)\}.

Note that
r(A)
is well defined whenever
A
is bounded.

Theorem 2.2
.

(Spectral Mapping Theorem for Polynomials; see Theorem 7.4-2 in
[
15
]
) Let
A
be a bounded linear operator on a complex Banach space
X
, and

p(\lambda)=\alpha_{n}\lambda^{n}+\alpha_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}+\cdots+\alpha_{0}

be a polynomial with
\alpha_{n}\neq 0
. Then

\sigma(p(A))=p(\sigma(A)),

i.e. the spectrum of
p(A)=\alpha_{n}A^{n}+\alpha_{n-1}A^{n}+\cdots+\alpha_{0}I
is
\{\alpha_{n}\mu^{n}+\alpha_{n-1}\mu^{n-1}+\cdots+\alpha_{0}\text{ : }\mu\in\sigma(A)\}
.

The following useful corolary follows from the above spectral mapping theorem:

Corollary 2.3
.

If
A
is a bounded linear operator and
\alpha\geq 0
, then
\alpha r(A)=r(\alpha A)
.

In the case where
X
is a finite dimensional Banach space ordered by a generating and normal cone, the Perron-Frobenius theorem guarantees that the spectral radius of
A
is an eigenvalue with corresponding eigenvector in
K
. However, when
X
is infinite dimensional,
r(A)
is not guaranteed to be an eigenvalue, but is guaranteed to be a point of the spectrum.

Theorem 2.4
.

(Exercise 11 in Chapter 6, Section 19, of
[
7
]
) Let
A
be a bounded linear operator that preserves a generating and normal cone. Then
r(A)\in\sigma(A)
.

We will also need the following comparison theorem:

Theorem 2.5
.

(Ordering of Spectral Radii
[
18
]
) Let
X
be a Banach space with a generating and normal cone
K
. Let
A
and
B
be cone-preserving operators such that
B\leq A
. Then

r(B)\leq r(A).

2.3
Resolvent Positive Operators

Let
X
be a Banach space ordered by a cone
K
, and let
A
be closed linear operator with dense domain in
X
.
A
is said to be
resolvent positive
if there is a ray
(\omega,\infty)\subset\rho(A)
so that
(\lambda-A)^{-1}
is a cone-preserving operator for all
\lambda\in(\omega,\infty)
.

If
A
is the generator of a strongly continuous (
C_{0}
) semigroup, then the semigroup preserves the cone if
A
is resolvent positive. This can be proved by invoking the following representation result for strongly continuous semigroups:

Theorem 2.6
.

(Theorem 8.3 in chapter 1 of
[
19
]
) If
A
is the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
semigroup
T(t)
acting on a Banach space
X
, then for all
t>0
and all
x\in X

T(t)x=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{n}{t}\left(\frac{n}{t}-A\right)^{-1}\right]^{n}x.

This limit is uniform with respect to
t
on any bounded interval in
(0,\infty)
.

Note for any
t>0
,
\left(\frac{n}{t}-A\right)^{-1}
is cone-preserving for all sufficiently large
n
when
A
is resolvent positive. Consequently,
\left[\frac{n}{t}\left(\frac{n}{t}-A\right)^{-1}\right]^{n}
is cone-preserving for all sufficiently large
n
. Since
K
is closed, it follows that
T(t)x
belongs to
K
when
x\in K
.

We will next show that the converse holds as well; that is, cone-preserving
C_{0}
semigroups have resolvent positive infinitesimal generators. To see why, assume
A
is the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
semigroup
T(t)
. Then there exists
M\geq 1
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that

||T(t)||\leq Me^{\omega t}

for all
t\geq 0
(by Theorem 2.2 in
[
19
]
).
Furthermore if
\lambda>\omega
, then
\lambda\in\rho(A)
, and we have the following representation formula for the resolvent of
A
with respect to
\lambda
:

(\lambda-A)^{-1}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}T(t)xdt

for all
x\in X
(see Theorem 5.3 in chapter 1 of
[
19
]
for a more details). It follows that whenever
T(t)
is cone-preserving for all
t\geq 0
, then
(\lambda-A)^{-1}x\in K
for all
x\in K
and all
\lambda>\omega
, using the fact that
K
is positively homogeneous and closed. Recall that the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
semigroup is a closed, densely defined operator. Therefore
A
is resolvent positive when
T(t)
is a cone-preserving semigroup.

Cone-preserving bounded linear operators are particularly simple examples of resolvent-positive operators. To see this, consider the representation of the resolvent of
A
as a Neumann series:

(\lambda-A)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-n-1}A^{n},

which is valid for
\lambda>r(A)
.

2.4
Convexity and Monotonicity of
r(F(\lambda-T^{-1})

Next we state a special case of Theorem 1.1 in
[
20
]
, regarding the behavior of the spectral radius map
\lambda\to r(F(\lambda-T)^{-1})
, which is tailored to our needs.

Theorem 2.7
.

(Theorem 1.1 in
[
20
]
) Let
X
be an ordered Banach space with a generating and normal cone
K
. Suppose
A
is a resolvent positive operator with domain
D(A)
, and
A
splits as
A=B+C
, where
B
is a resolvent positive operator with domain
D(B)=D(A)
,
s(B)<0
, and
C
maps

into

. Then
r(C(\lambda-B)^{-1})
is a non-increasing, convex function of
\lambda\in(s(B),\infty)
, and exactly one of the following cases holds:

1.
r(C(\lambda-B)^{-1})<1
for all
\lambda>s(B)
. In this case,
s(A)=s(B)
.

2. There exist
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
in
(s(B),\infty)
such that
r(C(\lambda_{1}-B)^{-1})\geq 1
and
r(C(\lambda_{2}-B)^{-1})<1
. In this case,

, and furthermore
r(C(s(A)I-B)^{-1})=1
.

Next we translate this result into a version which applies to bounded, cone-preserving linear operators.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
X
be an ordered Banach space with a generating and normal cone
K
. Suppose
\hat{A}
is a bounded, cone-preserving linear operator on
X
. Furthermore suppose that
\hat{A}
splits as
\hat{A}=T+F
where
T
and
F
are bounded, cone-preserving operators with

. Then
r(F(\lambda-T)^{-1})
is a non-increasing, convex function of
\lambda\in(r(T),\infty)
, and exactly one of the following cases holds:

1.
r(F(\lambda-T)^{-1})<1
for all
\lambda>r(T)
. In this case,

.

2. There exists
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
in
(r(T),\infty)
such that
r(F(\lambda_{1}-T)^{-1})\geq 1
and
r(F(\lambda_{2}-T)^{-1})<1
. In this case,

, and furthermore
r(F(r(\hat{A})I-T)^{-1})=1

Proof.
We show that one may take
A=\hat{A}-I
and

with
D(A)=D(B)=X
, and

in the statement of Theorem

. Note that

, as

. Consequently,

shows that

is also resolvent positive. Note also that

. Since

.

Thus we may apply Theorem
2.7
. Throughout we will further use the fact that
s(T-I)=r(T)-1
and
s(\hat{A}-I)=r(\hat{A})-1
, and make the substitution
\mu=\lambda+1
. First, we conclude
r(F(\lambda-(T-I))^{-1})
for
\lambda\in(s(T-I),\infty)

\lambda
, so equivalently
r(F(\mu-T)^{-1})

.

In case 1,

In case 2, the existence of

Lemma 2.9
.

(Proposition 4.3 in
[

) Let

Results

3.1

1\in\sigma\left(\frac{1}{R_{0}}F(\lambda_{1}-T)^{-1}\right)
by (

). It follows that
\tilde{r}(\lambda_{1})\geq 1
. However, by Corollary
2.8
, since
\tilde{r}(\lambda)

3.2
The Nonstrict Trichtomy

We begin with the most general version of the trichotomy, with nonstrict inequalities.

Theorem 3.2
.

The Nonstrict Trichotomy for Bounded Operators

Let
X
be a Banach space ordered by a generating and normal cone
K
. Let
A
be a bounded linear operator that preserves
K
, and splits as
A=T+F
, where
T
and
F
are also
K
-preserving bounded linear operators with
r(T)<1
. Define
R_{0}:=r(F(I-T)^{-1})
. Then exactly one of the following holds:

\displaystyle(a)\text{ }R_{0}\geq

\displaystyle\text{ }r(A)>1,

\displaystyle(b)\text{ }R_{0}=

\displaystyle\text{ }r(A)=1,

\displaystyle(c)\text{ }R_{0}\leq

\displaystyle\text{ }r(A)<1.

Proof.

Let us start by assuming
R_{0}=0
. Then
r(A)<1
by Theorem
1.2
, and we clearly have
R_{0}=0\leq r(A)
as desired since the spectral radius is always nonnegative.

Now assume
R_{0}>0
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have that
r(T+\frac{1}{R_{0}}F)=1
. Consider 3 cases,
R_{0}=1
,
R_{0}>1
, and
0<R_{0}<1
. From Theorem
1.2
, we already have that when
R_{0}=1
,
r(A)=1
. All that is left is to show that
R_{0}\geq r(A)
when
R_{0}>1
and
R_{0}\leq r(A)
when
R_{0}<1
.

Suppose
R_{0}>1
. Then

R_{0}(T+\frac{1}{R_{0}}F)\geq A

since
(R_{0}-1)T
is cone preserving. By Theorem
2.5
, it follows that

r(R_{0}(T+\frac{1}{R_{0}}F))\geq r(A).

From Corollary
2.3
, we have

R_{0}r(T+\frac{1}{R_{0}}F)\geq r(A).

Finally by Lemma
3.1
we have that

R_{0}\geq r(A).

Conversely, if
0<R_{0}<1
, the same argument shows that

R_{0}\leq r(A).\square

3.3
The Strict Trichotomy

A version of Theorem
3.2
with strict inequalities can be obtained by imposing further geometric and spectral assumptions on the operators
A
,
T
, and
F
. First we introduce a number of concepts and results from the literature which will allow us to state these assumptions.

A point
x\in K
is said to be an
almost interior point
if
f(x)>0
for all
f\in K^{*}\setminus\{0\}
; see
[
11
]
. Simiarly, a
strictly positive functional

f\in K^{*}
is a functional that satisfies
f(x)>0
whenever
x\in K\setminus\{0\}
. An bounded, cone-preserving operator
A
is called
semi-nonsupporting
if for any pair
(x,f)\in(K\setminus\{0\})\times(K^{*}\setminus\{0\})
, there is an
n=n(x,f)
(which may depend on
x
and
f
) such that
f(A^{n}x)>0
. Semi-nonsupporting operators generalize the notion of irreducible matrices. For these operators, we have the following:

Theorem 3.3
.

(Theorem 2.2 in
[
18
]
) Let
X
be a Banach space ordered by a generating and normal cone
K
. Let
A
be a bounded cone-preserving, semi-nonsupporting operator on a generating and normal cone, and suppose
r(A)
is a pole of the resolvent map of
A
(the map from
\rho(A)
to
\mathcal{B}(X)
given by
\lambda\to(\lambda-A)^{-1}
). Then

1.
r(A)
is a simple pole of the resolvent map.

2. There is a unique eigenvector
x
in
K
(up to positive scaling), which is an almost interior point, corresponding to
r(A)
. Furthermore, if
v\in K
is an eigenvector of
A
,
v=cx
for some positive constant
c
.

3. There is a strictly positive eigenfunctional
f
of
A
corresponding to
r(A)
.

The condition that
r(A)
is a pole of the resolvent map guarantees that
r(A)
will be an eigenvalue of
A
(see for example Corollary 6.12 in chapter VII of
[
3
]
). A sufficient condition for
r(A)
to be a pole is to assume that
A
is a cone-preserving compact operator on
X
with
r(A)>0
(Theorem VII.4.5 in
[
9
]
guarantees that nonzero spectral values of a compact operator are poles, and Theorem
2.4
guarantees
r(A)
is one of those values).

The following is a version of Theorem
2.5
which gives the strict ordering of spectral radii under additional assumptions.

Theorem 3.4
.

(Theorem 4.3 in
[
18
]
) Let
X
be a Banach space ordered by a generating and normal cone
K
. Let
A
and
B
be bounded cone-preserving operators such that
B\leq A
and
B\neq A
. Furthermore assume that
A
is a semi-nonsupporting operator with eigenvector
x\in K
and eigenfunctional
f\in K^{*}
both corresponding to
r(A)
, and assume that
g\in K^{*}
is an eigenfunctional of
B
corresponding to
r(B)
. Then

r(B)<r(A).

The following lemma allows us to show that if
R_{0}>0
, then
T+\frac{1}{R_{0}}F
is semi-nonsupporting whenever
A
is.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space ordered by a cone
K
. Suppose
B
and
C
are bounded cone-preserving linear operators with
B\leq C
. If
B
is semi-nonsupporting, then so is
C
.

Proof.
We begin by showing that
B^{n}\leq C^{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
whenever
0\leq B\leq C
. Suppose integers
i,j
are such that
i\geq 1
,
j\geq 0
, and
i+j=n
. Then
B^{i}C^{j}\leq B^{i-1}C^{j+1}
follows from

B^{i-1}C^{j+1}-B^{i}C^{j}=(B^{i-1}C-B^{i})C^{j}=B^{i-1}(C-B)C^{j},

because this expression is a composition of cone-preserving operators. Then

B^{n}\leq B^{n-1}C\leq B^{n-2}C^{2}\leq\cdots\leq BC^{n-1}\leq C^{n}.

Now suppose
B
is semi-nonsupporting. Then for any any pair
(x,f)\in(K\setminus\{0\})\times(K^{*}\setminus\{0\})
, there is an
n=n(x,f)
such that
f(B^{n}x)>0
. Clearly for this
x,f
, and the same value of
n
, we have

f(C^{n}x)\geq f(B^{n}x)>0,

showing that
C
is semi-nonsupporting as well.
\square

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
X
be a Banach space ordered by a cone
K
. Suppose
A
is a bounded, cone-preserving, semi-nonsupporting operator that splits as
A=T+F
, with
T
and
F
bounded, cone-preserving operators. Then
aT+bF
is semi-nonsupporting for any
a,b>0
.

Proof.
Let
\alpha=\min\{a,b\}
. Then
aT+bF\geq\alpha(T+F)=\alpha A
. Clearly
\alpha A
is semi-nonsupporting as
f((\alpha A)^{n}x)>0
whenever
f(A^{n}x)>0
,
(x,f)\in(K\setminus\{0\})\times(K^{*}\setminus\{0\})
. Thus by Lemma

3.5
,
aT+bF
is semi-nonsupporting.
\square

Theorem 3.7
.

The Strict Trichotomy for Bounded Operators

Let
X
be a Banach space ordered by a generating and normal cone
K
. Let
A
be a bounded, cone-preserving, semi-nonsupporting, linear operator whose spectral radius is a pole of its resolvent map. Assume that
A
splits as
A=T+F
, for bounded cone-preserving linear operators
T
and
F
with
r(T)<1
. Furthermore, suppose
T
is not the zero operator. Define
R_{0}:=r(F(I-T)^{-1})
and assume
R_{0}>0
. Suppose that
F(I-T)^{-1}
has
R_{0}
as a pole of its resolvent. Then exactly one of the following holds:

\displaystyle(a)\text{ }R_{0}>

\displaystyle\text{ }r(A)>1,

\displaystyle(b)\text{ }R_{0}=

\displaystyle\text{ }r(A)=1,

\displaystyle(c)\text{ }R_{0}<

\displaystyle\text{ }r(A)<1.

Proof.

After applying Theorem
3.2
, it remains only to be shown that
R_{0}>r(A)
when

and
R_{0}<r(A)
when
R_{0}\leq r(A)
.

By Theorem
3.3
,
R_{0}
is an eigenvalue of

in the cone and dual cone, repsectively. Next it is shown that

3.1

r(T+\frac{1}{R_{0}}F)=1

\displaystyle[F(I-T)^{-1}]^{*}f

\displaystyle=R_{0}f

\displaystyle\implies\hskip 28.0pt\frac{1}{R_{0}}F^{*}f

Consider the case where

Example: Leslie Models of Population Growth on
\ell_{p}

Consider an age-structured population model with a countably infinite number of discrete age classes. The state of the population at time step
t
is represented by
x(t)=(x_{i}(t))\in\ell_{p}
for
p\in[1,\infty]
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}\bigcup\{0\}
, where each component
x_{i}(t)
is the population size of age class
i
at time
t
. To construct a Leslie model in this setting, we consider a dynamical system of the form
x(t+1)=Ax(t)
, where
A=T+F
is the combination of a a survival operator
T
and a fertility operator
F
, defined in the following.

Let
f=(f_{i})\in\ell_{q}
(
q
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
) with
f_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
. Each
f_{i}
represents the expected number of offspring in a single time step per individual in age class
i
. For
x=(x_{i})
, define
Fx:=(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}x_{i},0,0,0,....)
. Define a sequence
(t_{i})
such that each
t_{i}
represents the expected fraction of individuals from age class
i
to survive to age class
i+1
in a single time step. It is assumed
0\leq t_{i}\leq 1
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
\limsup_{i\to\infty}t_{i}<1
. The latter condition will be used to show that
r(T)<1
. For
x=(x_{i})
, define
Tx=(0,t_{1}x_{1},t_{2}x_{2},t_{3}x_{3},...)
.

The action of
A=T+F
on an element
x=(x_{i})\in\ell_{p}
can be represented by an infinite dimensional matrix:

\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}&f_{2}&f_{3}&f_{4}&...\\
t_{1}&0&0&0&....\\
0&t_{2}&0&0&....\\
0&0&t_{3}&0&....\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&....\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
x_{3}\\
x_{4}\\
\vdots\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}x_{i}\\
t_{1}x_{1}\\
t_{2}x_{2}\\
t_{3}x_{3}\\
\vdots\\
\end{bmatrix}.

T
and
F
(and therefore
A
) preserve the standard cone in
\ell_{p}
, which is

K=\ell_{p}^{+}:=\{x\in\ell_{p}\text{ : }x_{i}\geq 0\text{ }\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\}.

This cone is clearly generating and normal with normality constant
\gamma=1
.

Claim 1:

T:\ell_{p}\to\ell_{p}
is a
K
-preserving, bounded linear operator, and
r(T)<1
.

Proof.
The fact that
T
is cone-preserving is obvious since
0\leq t_{i}\leq 1
. Also since
0\leq t_{i}\leq 1
,
|t_{i}x_{i}|\leq|x_{i}|
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore
||Tx||_{p}\leq||x||_{p}
for all
x\in\ell_{p}
, and so
T
is bounded.

Since
\limsup_{i\to\infty}t_{i}<1
, there is some
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
t_{i}<1-\varepsilon
for all
i>m
. The sequence
(t_{i})
is component-wise bounded above by a sequence
(s_{i})
defined by

s_{i}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }i\leq m\\
1-\varepsilon&\text{ otherwise}\\
\end{cases}.

Define an operator
S
by
Sx=(0,s_{1}x_{1},s_{2}x_{2},s_{3}x_{3},....)
. It is clear that
S\geq T\geq 0
, so by Theorem
2.5
if we show that
r(S)<1
, then
r(T)<1
. Considering Gelfand’s formula for the spectral radius,

r(S)=\lim_{n\to\infty}||S^{n}||^{\frac{1}{n}},

we fix
x\in\ell_{p}
with
||x||_{p}=1
. Then for
n>m
,

\displaystyle||S^{n}x||_{p}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\varepsilon)^{n-m}||x||_{p}=(1-\varepsilon)^{n-m}.

Then

\displaystyle||S^{n}||=\sup_{||x||_{p}=1}||S^{n}x||_{p}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\varepsilon)^{n-m},

\displaystyle||S^{n}||^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\varepsilon)^{\frac{n-m}{n}},

\displaystyle r(S)=\lim_{n\to\infty}||S^{n}||^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}(1-\varepsilon)^{\frac{n-m}{n}}=(1-\varepsilon)<1.\square

Claim 2:

F
is a
K
-preserving bounded linear operator.

Proof.
It is obvious that
F
is
K
-preserving since
f_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. To see that
F

x=(x_{i})\in\ell_{p}

||Fx||_{p}=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}x_{i}\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|f_{i}x_{i}|\leq||f||_{q}||x||_{p}

by Hölder’s inequality.
\square

Since all of the conditions of Theorem
3.2

R_{0}:=r(F(I-T)^{-1})

(I-T)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0&0&...\\

Since

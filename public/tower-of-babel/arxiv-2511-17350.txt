Abstract
We report results from searches for new physics models via electron recoils using data collected by the LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ) experiment during its first two science runs, with a total exposure of 4.2 tonne-years. The observed data are consistent with a background-only hypothesis. Constraints are derived for several new physics models that predict electronic recoil signals, including electromagnetic interactions of solar neutrinos, solar axion-like particles (ALPs), mirror dark matter, and the absorption of bosonic dark matter candidates. These results represent the most stringent constraints to date for solar ALPs with keV-scale masses and mirror dark matter, and they are competitive with existing limits for other investigated models.

In this Letter, we report on searches for new physics scenarios that produce ER signals, using data collected by the LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ) experiment.
The models investigated include solar axion-like particles (ALPs), bosonic dark matter, exotic electromagnetic interactions of solar neutrinos, and mirror dark matter.
We will first outline the new physics model being tested and theoretical inputs used, then describe the LZ detector and its backgrounds, discuss the data analysis for different signal models, and conclude with the final results from statistical inference.

The last search in this analysis is the electromagnetic interaction of solar neutrinos.

The data used in this search was collected during the first two science runs of the LZ experiment: WIMP search 2022 (WS2022)
3
and WIMP search 2024 (WS2024)
10

The LZ experiment is located 4850 feet underground (4300 mwe) in the Davis Cavern at the Sanford Underground Research Facility (SURF) in Lead, South Dakota, USA.
At the center of the experiment is a dual-phase LXe TPC containing 7 tonnes of active xenon
21
, which also serves as the target medium.
To suppress backgrounds from radioactivity of detector components, the TPC is enclosed within a dual-detector veto system comprised of a two-tonne LXe "skin" detector for tagging gamma rays, and an outer detector (OD) containing 17.3 tonnes of gadolinium-loaded liquid scintillator (GdLS) optimized for neutron detection
21
.
These active components are further surrounded by 238 tonnes of ultra-pure water, providing an additional passive shield and ensuring a low-background environment critical for rare event searches.
More details of the LZ detector can be found in Ref.
21
.

Particle interactions in the TPC produce vacuum ultraviolet scintillation (S1) and ionization electrons.
The electrons that do not recombine with ions drift upward under an electric field to the liquid–gas interface, where they are extracted into the gas phase and yield secondary electroluminescence (S2)
3
.
Both signals are recorded by top and bottom photomultiplier tube (PMT) arrays: the S1–S2 time difference gives the interaction depth, the S2 light pattern on the top array reconstructs the transverse position, and the S2/S1 ratio enables discrimination between ER and NR events
47
.

S1 and S2 signals vary with detector conditions, light collection, and electron extraction efficiencies..
To correct these effects, mono-energetic ER sources including
{}^{131\text{m}}
Xe and
{}^{83\text{m}}
Kr are periodically injected into the LXe bulk via the circulation system
9
to map spatial and temporal variations.
The S1 signal is normalized to the detector center (henceforth dubbed S1c), while the S2 signal is corrected to the radial center at the top of LXe (S2c).
Both signals are expressed in detected photons (phd), with calibration accounting for the double photoelectron emission effect
55
;
70

To reflect the characteristics of different signal models, two ROI definitions are considered in this work.
For all signal models, events are required to satisfy S1c > 3 phd in both science runs.

The introduction of radon tagging has improved LZ’s sensitivity to ER signals.
Fits to toy datasets indicate that incorporating radon tagging reduces the median upper limits on signal events by approximately 10–25
\%
.
The improvement is smaller than the tagging efficiency, since Rn tagging comes with a systematic uncertainty on the background event rates that partly offsets its benefit.
As a result, the upper limits derived from observed data typically improve by about 5–10
\%
, with the exact gain depending on the signal’s spectral shape and underlying physics, particularly the power of the parameter of interest in the event rate.
The only exception is the case of
57
Fe solar ALPs, where the observed upper limit with radon tagging is slightly weaker.
This behavior can be attributed to a local upward fluctuation near 14.4 keV in the data.

In conclusion, we have searched for ER signals indicative of new physics using the combined dataset from the first two science runs of the LZ experiment.
Finding no significant excess above our background model, we set stringent limits on various signal hypotheses, thanks to a combination of low background rate, large exposure, and the introduction of the radon tagging technique.
This analysis yields updated constraints on solar ALPs via ABC processes, Primakoff conversion, and
57
Fe de-excitation, neutrino electromagnetic interactions, mirror dark matter, and bosonic dark matter absorption.
Notably, the limits presented here are the most stringent to date for
57
Fe and Primakoff conversion solar ALPs, mirror dark matter, and the solar neutrino magnetic moment, while our constraints on other tested models remain highly competitive.

Facet numbers of non-centrally symmetric reflexive polytopes arising from posets
AKI MORI, KENTA MORI and HIDEFUMI OHSUGI

Abstract
. Twinned chain polytopes form a broad class of non-centrally symmetric reflexive polytopes and exhibit intriguing structures. In the present paper, we show that the number of facets of d d -dimensional twinned chain polytopes is at most 6 d / 2 6^{d/2} . In case d d is even, the equality holds if and only if the polytope is isomorphic to a free sum of d / 2 d/2 copies of del Pezzo polygons. This result contributes a partial answer to Nill’s conjecture: the number of facets of a d d -dimensional reflexive polytope is at most 6 d / 2 6^{d/2} .

1.
Introduction

A convex polytope
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called a
lattice polytope
if any vertex of
{\mathcal{P}}
belongs to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.
A
reflexive polytope
is a lattice polytope that contains the origin in its interior and has the property that its dual polytope is also a lattice polytope.
The concept of reflexive polytopes was introduced by Batyrev
[
1
]
in the study of Gorenstein toric Fano varieties.
Since then, reflexive polytopes have been extensively studied from various perspectives, including combinatorics, commutative algebra and algebraic geometry.
It is known that there are only finitely many reflexive polytopes in each dimension, up to unimodular equivalence
[
17
]
.
Furthermore, if the dimension
d
satisfies
d\leq 4
, reflexive polytopes can be enumerated
[
14
,
15
]
.

Let
N({\mathcal{P}})
be the number of facets of a lattice polytope
{\mathcal{P}}
.
Nill proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
21
, Conjecture 5.2]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a
d
-dimensional reflexive polytope.
Then
N({\mathcal{P}})\leq 6^{d/2}
.

In Nill’s original paper, this conjecture was stated as an upper bound on the number of vertices.
For dimensions
d\leq 4
, this conjecture is supported by the database of reflexive polytopes:
N({\mathcal{P}})\leq 6
if
d=2
,
N({\mathcal{P}})\leq 14=\lfloor 6^{3/2}\rfloor
if
d=3
, and
N({\mathcal{P}})\leq 36
if
d=4

[
16
]
.
A rigorous proof is known only for
d=2

[
21
, Corollary 3.3]
.
If
{\mathcal{P}}
is a pseudo-symmetric reflexive simplicial
d
-dimensional polytope, then Conjecture
1.1
holds, and the maximum
6^{d/2}
is attained if and only if
{\mathcal{P}}
is a free sum of
d/2
copies of del Pezzo polygons
[
22
]
.
Additionally, Conjecture
1.1
has been proven for symmetric edge polytopes arising from join graphs and connected bipartite graphs
[
20
]
.

Let
(P,\leq_{P})
be a finite partially ordered set (“poset” for short) on
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{d}\}
.
An
antichain
of
P
is a subset
W\subset P
such that

p_{i}
and
p_{j}
are incomparable for any
p_{i},p_{j}\in W
with
i\neq j
.
In particular, the empty set
\emptyset
as well as each singleton
\{p_{i}\}
is an antichain of
P
.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}(P)
denote the set of antichains of
P
.
For each

, we set
\rho(A)=\sum_{p_{i}\in A}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
where
{\mathbf{e}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{e}}_{d}
are the canonical unit coordinate vectors of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
For example,
\rho(\emptyset)
is the origin of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Stanley
[
27
]
defined a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope called the
chain polytope
of a poset
P
as

{\mathcal{C}}(P)=\textrm{conv}(\{\rho(A):A\in{\mathcal{A}}(P)\})\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

For two lattice polytopes
{\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{Q}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we define

\Gamma({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{Q}})=\textrm{conv}({\mathcal{P}}\cup(-{\mathcal{Q}}))\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Let
(Q,\leq_{Q})
be a poset on
Q=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{d}\}

with
P\cap Q=\emptyset
.
Then the polytope
\Gamma({\mathcal{C}}(P),{\mathcal{C}}(Q))
is called the
twinned chain polytope

[
28
]
.
It is known
[
25
]
that
\Gamma({\mathcal{C}}(P),{\mathcal{C}}(Q))
is a reflexive polytope.

The study of
\Gamma({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{Q}})
was inspired by research on centrally symmetric configurations of integer matrices
[
24
]
.
As in the case of symmetric edge polytopes
[
18
]
, many reflexive polytopes exhibit the structure of
\Gamma({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{Q}})
.
In particular, when
{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}
are lattice polytopes arising from posets, important properties such as normality, reflexivity, unimodular equivalence and the Ehrhart polynomials of these polytopes have been studied in
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
28
]
.
In
[

are locally anti-blocking, and hence the

-polynomials of
\Gamma({\mathcal{C}}(P),{\mathcal{C}}(Q))
are
\gamma
-positive.
The
\gamma
-positivity of
h^{*}
-polynomials of locally anti-blocking reflexive polytopes is studied in
[
26
]
.
On the other hand, we define

\Omega({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{Q}})=\textrm{conv}({\mathcal{P}}\times\{1\}\cup(-{\mathcal{Q}})\times\{-1\})\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}.

The polytope

can be regarded as a variation of the Cayley sum of
{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}
.
In particular,

is known as the
Hansen polytope

[
5
]

when

is the
stable set polytope
of a graph.
In
[
4
]
, it is shown that Kalai’s

conjecture
[
13
]
holds for Hansen polytopes of split graphs.
Combinatorial properties of

and

are studied in
[
11
]
and

]
, respectively.
It is known
[

]
that

Moreover,

arises as a projection of

In this sense, twinned chain polytopes form a natural class of reflexive polytopes reflecting certain structural features of
\Omega(2{\mathcal{C}}(P),2{\mathcal{C}}(Q))
while being connected to the combinatorics of posets.

In the present paper, we investigate the number of facets of twinned chain polytopes and confirm Conjecture
1.1

2.
The number of facets of twinned chain polytopes

In the present section, we review the equation of the number of facets of twinned chain polytopes and examine the number of facets based on their geometric structure when these polytopes are associated with elementary posets.

Let

Proposition 2.1

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(P_{1},\leq_{P_{1}})
,
(P_{2},\leq_{P_{2}})
,
(Q_{1},\leq_{Q_{1}})
and
(Q_{2},\leq_{Q_{2}})
be posets with

P_{1}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{s}\}
,
P_{2}=\{p_{s+1},\dots,p_{d}\}
,
Q_{1}=\{q_{1},\dots,q_{s}\}
, and
Q_{2}=\{q_{s+1},\dots,q_{d}\}
.
Then

N(\Gamma(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2}))=N(\Gamma(P_{1},Q_{1}))\cdot N(\Gamma(P_{2},Q_{2})).

In particular, if
N(\Gamma(P_{1},Q_{1}))\leq 6^{\frac{s}{2}}
and
N(\Gamma(P_{2},Q_{2}))\leq 6^{\frac{d-s}{2}}
, then

N(\Gamma(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2}))\leq 6^{\frac{d}{2}}.

Proof.

From
[
7
, Lemma 7.2]
, we have

(2.1)

{\mathcal{C}}(P_{1}\oplus P_{2})\;\cup\;(-{\mathcal{C}}(Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2}))={\mathcal{C}}(P_{1})\oplus{\mathcal{C}}(P_{2})\;\cup\;(-{\mathcal{C}}(Q_{1}))\oplus(-{\mathcal{C}}(Q_{2})).

Taking the convex hull of both sides of (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \;\Gamma(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2})

\displaystyle=\textrm{conv}\Bigl(\bigl({\mathcal{C}}(P_{1})\times\{0\}^{d-s}\;\cup\;\{0\}^{s}\times{\mathcal{C}}(P_{2})\bigr)\;\cup\;\bigl((-{\mathcal{C}}(Q_{1}))\times\{0\}^{d-s}\;\cup\;\{0\}^{s}\times(-{\mathcal{C}}(Q_{2}))\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\textrm{conv}\Bigl(\bigl({\mathcal{C}}(P_{1})\;\cup\;(-{\mathcal{C}}(Q_{1}))\bigr)\times\{0\}^{d-s}\;\cup\;\{0\}^{s}\times\bigl({\mathcal{C}}(P_{2})\;\cup\;(-{\mathcal{C}}(Q_{2}))\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\textrm{conv}\Bigl(\Gamma(P_{1},Q_{1})\times\{0\}^{d-s}\;\cup\;\{0\}^{s}\times\Gamma(P_{2},Q_{2})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\Gamma(P_{1},Q_{1})\oplus\Gamma(P_{2},Q_{2}).

Thus we have

N(\Gamma(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2}))=N(\Gamma(P_{1},Q_{1}))\cdot N(\Gamma(P_{2},Q_{2})).

If
N(\Gamma(P_{1},Q_{1}))\leq 6^{\frac{s}{2}}
and
N(\Gamma(P_{2},Q_{2}))\leq 6^{\frac{d-s}{2}}
, then

N(\Gamma(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2}))\leq 6^{\frac{s}{2}}\cdot 6^{\frac{d-s}{2}}=6^{\frac{d}{2}}.

∎

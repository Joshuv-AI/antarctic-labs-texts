Non-equilibrium state during proton-deuteron exchange at a liquid-liquid interface
Tillmann Buttersack, Niclas Sven Mueller, Giulia Carini, Henrik Haak, Hanna Bordyuh, Dipali Singh, Sandy Gewinner, Marco De Pas, Wieland Schöllkopf, Martin Wolf, Hendrik Bluhm, Nils Huse, Bernd Winter, Gerard Meijer, Alexander Paarmann

Abstract
The exchange of protons and deuterons is a very fast process, even on macroscopic length scales. Here we directly measure quantitatively the formation of HDO within the first 100 microseconds of the reaction at the liquid-liquid interface between D 2 O and H 2 O using a fast-flowing liquid flat jet and infrared spectroscopic imaging. During early stages of the reaction, the rate of HDO formation is limited by the availability of hydroxide and hydronium ions mediating the reaction, rather than simply by diffusion. This reveals a non-equilibrium state of the interface, being fully mixed diffusely yet with HDO concentrations well below equilibrium. We extract the reaction rate constant for the HDO formation in good agreement with picosecond timescales of the elementary proton-deuteron exchange reaction. Quantitative analysis of educt and product concentrations by infrared imaging at well-defined liquid-liquid interfaces as introduced here will enable studying fast kinetics for a wide range of chemical reactions.

Our data suggest that HDO production across the liquid-liquid interface is not limited simply by diffusion, but instead is significantly influenced by the availability of hydroxide and hydronium ions acting as reaction intermediates for the proton-deuteron exchange, see SI for details. To confirm this hypothesis, we acquired data for a series of acidic and basic ion concentrations of heavy and light water, see Fig.
3
A. We show the average HDO concentration in the second half of the LS which clearly increases with increasing ion concentration. A combination of acid and base solutions (purple triangles) leads to neutralization at the liquid-liquid interface, which likely explains the slightly smaller HDO production rate in those cases compared to purely acidic or basic systems (blue and red triangles). Overall, however, our data clearly show that the rate of HDO production across the interface is determined by the ion concentration, but is largely independent of which ions are available.

\approx 10^{6}
) between the equilibrium concentration of HDO in the 1:1 mixture, and the very low concentration of hydroxide and hydronium ions in pure water.

In our experiment, we directly measure the absolute amount of product, rather than monitoring the process on a molecular level. In other words, the kinetics of a very fast reaction can be determined as fast-flowing LSs benefit from the scaling of the reaction rate with the educt concentrations.
To measure kinetics at liquid-liquid interfaces in general, the reaction rate constant
k
needs to match to the spatial resolution of the imaging system and the flow velocity of the LS (
k\approx 10^{3}-10^{4}\,\textrm{s}^{-1}
). As we show here, fast reactions like proton exchanges can be studied by considering scaled kinetics using a pre-equilibrium (e.g., by autoionization). Therefore, we foresee a large potential for our experimental approach to investigate kinetics at liquid-liquid interfaces, such as for instance the proton exchange between fatty acids and aqueous systems at liquid-liquid interfaces, as prototype systems for mimicking cell membranes.

Conclusion

We used mid-infrared spectroscopic imaging to investigate one of the simplest chemical reactions in nature: the formation of semiheavy water from H
2
O and D
2
O. We employed a fast flowing liquid sheet to form the well-defined liquid-liquid interface. The experiment reports on the formation of HDO within the first 100
\mu
s at the interface between D
2
O and H
2
O.
Even though the proton-deuteron exchange is usually considered a fast, diffusion-limited reaction, we demonstrate that the exchange reaction at the interface between H
2
O and D
2
O is instead limited by the scarcity of hydroxide and hydronium ions in pure water, providing a spatially accessible non-equilibrium state of the H
2

Due to the interplay of ion and product concentration, we connected the finite rate of HDO formation to elementary proton-deuteron exchange processes that happen on the picosecond timescale, despite the time resolution of the experimental setup being on the microsecond scale. The combination of fast-flowing jets with a well-defined liquid-liquid interface, and infrared spectro-microscopy is an excellent tool to monitor fast reactions at liquid-liquid interfaces. Moreover, the reaction kinetics can be matched to the experimentally resolvable time window by controlling the educt availability, enabling broad applicability of the approach.

Abstract
. Let ℋ \mathcal{H} be a separable Hilbert space and ℒ 0 ⊂ ℬ ⁡ ( ℋ ) \mathcal{L}_{0}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}) a complete reflexive lattice. Let 𝒦 \mathscr{K} be the direct sum of n 0 n_{0} copies of ℋ \mathcal{H} ( n 0 ∈ ℕ n_{0}\in\mathbb{N} and n 0 ≥ 2 n_{0}\geq 2 ) or the direct sum of countably infinite many copies of ℋ \mathcal{H} respectively. We construct two class of subspace lattices ℒ \mathcal{L} on 𝒦 \mathscr{K} . Let Alg ⁡ ℒ \Alg\mathcal{L} be the corresponding subspace lattice algebra. We show that every local derivation from Alg ⁡ ℒ \Alg\mathcal{L} into ℬ ⁡ ( 𝒦 ) \mathcal{B}(\mathscr{K}) is a derivation.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an algebra. A linear map
D:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
is said to be a derivation if

D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b)

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
. We call a map

\delta:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
a local derivation (see
[
7
]
) if
for any
a
in
\mathcal{A}
there is a derivation
D_{a}
of
\mathcal{A}
, depending on
a
, such that

\delta(a)=D_{a}(a).

Every linear local derivation of a
C^{\ast}
-algebra is continuous (
[
5
]
), and
indeed a derivation (
[
9
]
; see also
[
1
]
).
In particular, every linear local derivation
\delta
on a von Neumann algebra is an inner derivation (
[
6
]
). They are also many results on derivations and local derivations on
non-self-adjoint operator algebras which is parallel to
self-adjoint algebras. We refer to
[
4
,
10
,
11
,
14
]

for more results on local derivations on non-self-adjoint operator algebras.

Now we recall some backgrounds of non-self-adjoint operator algebras. A subset
\mathcal{L}
of subspaces of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called a subspace lattice if it contains
\{0\}
and
\mathcal{H}
and is complete in the sense that it is closed under the formation of arbitrary closed linear spans(denoted by
\vee
) and intersections(denoted by
\wedge
). Let
\mathcal{L}
be a subspace lattice. The subspace lattice algebra
\Alg\mathcal{L}
corresponds to
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\Alg\mathcal{L}=\{A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):Ax\in E\ {\rm{for\ all\ }}x\in E\ {\rm{and\ }}E\in\mathcal{L}\}.

For any
P\in\mathcal{L}
, the immediate predecessor and the immediate successor of
P
are defined by

P_{-}=\vee\{Q\in\mathcal{L}:Q\ngeqslant P\}\ {\rm{for}}\ P\neq 0,\ {\rm{and}}\ 0_{-}=0,

and

P_{+}=\wedge\{Q\in\mathcal{L}:Q\nleqslant P\}\ {\rm{for}}\ P\neq I,\ {\rm{and}}\ I_{+}=I

respectively. If
P
is also in other lattice
\mathcal{L}_{1}
, we will denote by
P_{-}^{\mathcal{L}_{1}}
and
P_{+}^{\mathcal{L}_{1}}
the "predecessor" and "successor" of
P
in
\mathcal{L}_{1}
, respectively.

For a subalgebra
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
{\rm{Lat}}\mathcal{A}
is the lattice of subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
that are left invariant by each operator in
\mathcal{A}
. An algebra
\mathcal{A}
is reflexive if
\mathcal{A}=\Alg{\rm{Lat}}\mathcal{A}
, and a lattice
\mathcal{L}
is reflexive if
\mathcal{L}={\rm{Lat}}\Alg\mathcal{L}
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a subspace lattice. For convenience, we put

{\rm{I_{d}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L})={\rm{span}}\{T\in\Alg\mathcal{L}:T\ {\rm{is\ a\ idempotent\ operator}}\},

and

{\rm{R_{1}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L})={\rm{span}}\{T\in\Alg\mathcal{L}:T\ {\rm{is\ of\ rank}}\ 1\}.

Now we introduce some class of the reflexive subspace lattices that we will consider in this paper (see
[
12
]
). The following notations will be fixed throughout this paper.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{0}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a complete reflexive lattice. Let
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
n_{0}\geq 2
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{n_{0}}=\mathcal{H}^{(n_{0})}
be the direct sum of
n_{0}
copies of
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose

\zeta=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n_{0}})\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{0}}

is a unit vector with
a_{i}\neq 0
for any
1\leq i\leq n_{0}
. Let
Q_{\zeta}
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{K}_{n_{0}}
onto the closed subspace spanned by
\{(a_{1}x,\cdots,a_{n}x):x\in\mathcal{H}\}
. Let
\{E_{ij}\}
be the standard matrix units in the matrix algebra
M_{n_{0}}(\mathbb{C})
and
F_{k}=\sum^{k}_{j=1}E_{jj}\otimes I
for all
1\leq k\leq n_{0}
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}
be the lattice generated by the projections
\{E_{11}\otimes P,Q_{\zeta},F_{k}:P\in\mathcal{L}_{0},2\leq k\leq n_{0}\}
. It is not difficult to see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}=\{E_{11}\otimes P,Q_{\zeta},(E_{11}\otimes P)\vee Q_{\zeta},F_{k},F_{k}\vee Q_{\zeta}:P\in\mathcal{L}_{0},2\leq k\leq n_{0}\}

and

\displaystyle\Alg\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}

\displaystyle=\{\sum_{i=1,j\geq i}^{n_{0}}E_{ij}\otimes T_{ij}:T_{ij}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),T_{11}\in\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{1}}T_{1j}=\sum_{j=2}^{n_{0}}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{2}}T_{2j}=\cdots=T_{n_{0},n_{0}}\}.

Now let

\mathcal{K}_{\infty}=\oplus^{\infty}_{n=1}\mathcal{H}_{n}(=l^{2}(\mathbb{N})\otimes\mathcal{H})

with
\mathcal{H}_{n}=\mathcal{H}
for any
n
. Suppose

\eta=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n},\cdots)\in l^{2}(\mathbb{N})

is a unit vector with
b_{n}\neq 0
for any
n\geq 1
. Let
Q_{\eta}
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
onto the closed subspace
\{(b_{1}x,\cdots,b_{n}x,\cdots):x\in\mathcal{H}\}
. Let
\{E_{ij}\}
be the standard matrix units of
\mathcal{B}(l^{2}(\mathbb{N}))
and
E_{k}=\sum^{k}_{j=1}E_{jj}\otimes I
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
be the lattice generated by the projections
\{E_{11}\otimes P,Q_{\eta},F_{k}:P\in\mathcal{L}_{0},k\geq 2\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\infty}=\{I,E_{11}\otimes P,Q_{\eta},(E_{11}\otimes P)\vee Q_{\eta},E_{k},E_{k}\vee Q_{\eta}:P\in\mathcal{L}_{0},k\geq 2\}

and

\displaystyle\Alg\mathcal{L}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\{\sum_{i=1,j\geq i}^{\infty}E_{ij}\otimes T_{ij}:T_{ij}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),T_{11}\in\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{1}}T_{1j}=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{2}}T_{2j}=\cdots=\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{n}}T_{nj}=\cdots\}.

It follows from
[
12
]
that the lattices
\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
are reflexive subspace lattices when the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is a reflexive subspace lattice.

For simplicity, we will not distinguish an operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and its counterpart
E_{11}\otimes T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}_{n_{0}})
(respective,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})
) in the rest of this paper. We also identify an orthogonal projection
P\in B(\mathcal{K}_{n_{0}})
(respective,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})
) with its range
P(\mathcal{K})
. We use
x\in P
to indicate that a vector
x
is in the range of a orthogonal projection
P
.

Our aim in this note is to show that local derivations on
\Alg\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}
and
\Alg\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
are derivations.

2.
Main results

We first describe the structure of the lattices given in section 1.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}
be the subspace lattice given in section 1. Then

(1)

(E_{11}\otimes P)_{-}=(E_{11}\otimes P_{-}^{\mathcal{L}_{0}})\vee Q_{\zeta}
,
\forall P\in\mathcal{L}_{0}
;

(2)

(F_{k})_{-}=F_{k-1}\vee Q_{\zeta}
,
1<k<n_{0}
;

(3)

(Q_{\zeta})_{-}=F_{n_{0}-1}
;

(4)

I_{-}=I
,
0_{+}=0
.

Proof.

The conclusion follows easily from the definitions and construction of the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}
. We omit the details.
∎

Similar results also hold for
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
and we omit its proof.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
be the subspace lattice given in section 1. Then

(1)

(E_{11}\otimes P)_{-}=(E_{11}\otimes P_{-}^{\mathcal{L}_{0}})\vee Q_{\eta}
,
\forall P\in\mathcal{L}_{0}
;

(2)

(F_{k})_{-}=F_{k-1}\vee Q_{\eta}
,
k\geq 2
;

(3)

(Q_{\eta})_{-}=I
,
I_{-}=I
,
0_{+}=0
.

In the following we always assume that
\mathcal{L}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}},\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\}
. We also denote
\mathscr{K}=\mathcal{K}_{n_{0}}
when
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}
and
\mathscr{K}=\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
when
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

{\rm{R_{1}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L})\subset{\rm{I_{d}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L})
if and only if
{\rm{R_{1}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0})\subset{\rm{I_{d}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0})
.

Proof.

We only prove the case when
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}
since the case when
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
is similar.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
x\otimes f\in\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0}
be a rank 1 operator in
\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0}
. Then we have

T=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}x\otimes f&-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}x\otimes f&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right)\in\Alg\mathcal{L}.

By the hypothesis, there is some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
l_{1},\cdots,l_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
and
T_{1},\cdots,T_{k}\in{\rm{I_{d}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L})
such that
T=l_{1}T_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}T_{k}
. It follows that
T_{i}F_{1}\in\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0}
is idempotent operator for any
i=1,\cdots,k
and
x\otimes f=l_{1}T_{1}F_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}T_{k}F_{k}
. Therefore,
x\otimes f\in{\rm{I_{d}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0})
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Let
x\otimes f\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
be a rank 1 operator. We have

(x\otimes f)(x\otimes f)=\langle x,f\rangle x\otimes f.

If
\langle x,f\rangle\neq 0
, then
x\otimes f
is a nonzero multiple of an idempotent operator.

Suppose that
\langle x,f\rangle=0
. Since
x\otimes f\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
, there is some
2\leq k\leq n_{0}-1
or
P\in\mathcal{L}_{0}
such that
x\in F_{k}
and
f\in(F_{k})_{-}^{\perp}
, or alternatively,
x\in E_{11}\otimes P
and
f\in(E_{11}\otimes P)_{-}^{\perp}
, or else
x\in Q_{\zeta}
and
f\in(Q_{\zeta})_{-}^{\perp}
.

Case 1: If
x\in E_{11}\otimes P
and
f\in(E_{11}\otimes P)_{-}^{\perp}
. Let

x\otimes f=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}T_{11}&T_{12}&\cdots&T_{1,n_{0}}\\
0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right)

and

H=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}T_{11}&-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}T_{11}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right).

Then
T_{11}\in\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0}
is a rank 1 operator and
H\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
. Since
{\rm{R_{1}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0})\subset{\rm{I_{d}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0})
, then there is some
s\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k_{1},\cdots,k_{s}\in\mathbb{C}
and idempotents
S_{1},\cdots,S_{s}\in\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0}
such that
T_{11}=k_{1}S_{1}+\cdots+k_{s}S_{s}
. Let

H_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}S_{i}&-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}S_{i}&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right),\ \forall i=1,\cdots,s.

Then
H_{i}\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
is idempotent operator for all
i=1,\cdots,s
and

H=k_{1}H_{1}+\cdots+k_{s}H_{s}\in{\rm{I_{d}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L}).

On the other hand, we have

x\otimes f-H=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}T_{11}+T_{12}&T_{13}&\cdots&T_{1,n_{0}}\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right)\in\Alg\mathcal{L}.

Hence
E_{11}\otimes I-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}E_{12}\otimes I+(x\otimes f-H),\ E_{11}\otimes I-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}E_{12}\otimes I-(x\otimes f-H)\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
are idempotent operators, and
x\otimes f-H\in{\rm{I_{d}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L})
. Therefore,
x\otimes f
is contained in the linear span of the idempotent operators of
\Alg\mathcal{L}
.

Case 2: If
x\in F_{k}
and
f\in(F_{k})_{-}^{\perp}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
E_{kk}x\neq 0
, otherwise, we may scrutinize the scenario of
k-1
, decrementing
k
until it attains the value
k=1
, at which juncture it falls under Case 1.

Since
F_{k}\nleqslant(F_{k})_{-}=F_{k-1}\vee Q_{\zeta}
, there is some
g\in(F_{k})_{-}^{\perp}
such that
\langle x,g\rangle=1
. Otherwise,
x\in(F_{k})_{-}\wedge F_{k}=(F_{k-1}\vee Q_{\zeta})\wedge F_{k}=F_{k-1}
, which is a contradiction. It is clearly that
x\otimes(f-g),x\otimes(f+g)\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
are two nonzero multiple of idempotent operators and
x\otimes f=\frac{1}{2}x\otimes(f-g)+\frac{1}{2}x\otimes(f+g)
is contained in the linear span of the idempotent operators of
\Alg\mathcal{L}
.

Case 3: If
x\in Q_{\zeta}
and
f\in(Q_{\zeta})_{-}^{\perp}=E_{n_{0},n_{0}}
, then there is some
g\in E_{n_{0},n_{0}}
such that
\langle x,g\rangle=1
. Thus
x\otimes(f-g),x\otimes(f+g)\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
are two nonzero idempotent operators and
x\otimes f
is contained in the linear span of the idempotent operators of
\Alg\mathcal{L}
.

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
\mathscr{H}
is a complex Hilbert space.
Let
\delta
be a local derivation from
\Alg\mathcal{L}
into

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0}
be a decomposable subspace lattice algebra and
{\rm{R_{1}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0})\subset{\rm{I_{d}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0})
. Let
\delta
be a local derivation from
\Alg\mathcal{L}
into
\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{K})
. Then

\delta(MAN)=M\delta(AN)+\delta(MA)N-M\delta(A)N

for any
A,M,N\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
and
M,N
are finite rank operators.

Proof.

We only prove the case when
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}
.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
T\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
be a rank
n
operator. Since
\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0}
is decomposable, it follows from Lemma
2.4
that
\Alg\mathcal{L}
is decomposable. Then there are some rank 1 operators

such that
T=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes f_{i}
. Therefore for any finite rank operators
M,N\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
, it follows from Lemma
2.5
, Lemma
2.3
and the linear of local derivations that

\delta(MAN)=M\delta(AN)+\delta(MA)N-M\delta(A)N

for any
A,M,N\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
and
M,N
are finite rank operators.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

.

Since
I=F_{n_{0}-1}\vee Q_{\zeta}
, then we have
TF_{n_{0}-1}\neq 0
or
TQ_{\zeta}\neq 0

If

T(z\otimes f)=(Tz)\otimes f\neq 0
. If
TQ_{\zeta}\neq 0

\xi\in Q_{\zeta}
and

such that
T\xi\neq 0
and
x\otimes g\in\Alg\mathcal{L}

T(\xi\otimes g)=(T\xi)\otimes g\neq 0
. This proved the Lemma.
∎

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\Alg\mathcal{L}{{}_{0}}
be a decomposable algebra and
{\rm{R_{1}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0})\subset{\rm{I_{d}}}(\Alg\mathcal{L}_{0})
. If
P\nless P_{-}^{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
for any nonzero projection
P\in\mathcal{L}_{0}
, then every local derivation from
\Alg\mathcal{L}
into
\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{K})
is a derivation.

Proof.

We only prove the case when
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{n_{0}}
.
It follows from Proposition
2.7
that

(1)

\delta(MAN)=M\delta(AN)+\delta(MA)N-M\delta(A)N

for any
A,M,N\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
and
M,N
are finite rank operators. Let
A=I
in Equation (
1
). Then we have

(2)

\delta(MN)=M\delta(N)+\delta(M)N

for any finite rank operators
M,N\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
. Replaced
M
by
MA
in Equation (
2
), we obtain

(3)

\delta(MAN)=MA\delta(N)+\delta(MA)N

for any finite rank operators
M,N\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
. Combining with Equation (
1
) and (
3
), we have

M\delta(AN)=M\delta(A)N+MA\delta(N).

By Lemma
2.8
, we have

\delta(AN)=\delta(A)N+A\delta(N)

for any
A,N\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
and
N
is finite rank operator. It follows that

\displaystyle\delta(AB)N

\displaystyle=\delta(ABN)-AB\delta(N)

\displaystyle=\delta(A)BN+A\delta(BN)-AB\delta(N)

\displaystyle=\delta(A)BN+A\delta(B)N

for any
A,B,N\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
and
N
is finite rank operator. It follows from Lemma
2.9
that

\delta(AB)=\delta(A)B+A\delta(B)

for any
A,B\in\Alg\mathcal{L}
. Hence
\delta
is a derivation.
∎

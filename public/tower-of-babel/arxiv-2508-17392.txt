On L 1 L^{1} -approximation of groups
Benjamin Bachner, Alon Dogon and Alexander Lubotzky

Abstract
. A longstanding open problem in the intersection of group theory and operator algebras is whether all groups are MF, that is, approximated by representations with respect to the operator norm. More generally, for 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty , it has been asked by Thom [ 19 ] whether there exist groups which are not approximated with respect to the Schatten p p -norm. The cases of 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty were addressed in previous works [ 9 , 17 ] . We settle the case p = 1 p=1 , solving a case left out by Lubotzky and Oppenheim [ 17 ] .



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathscr{G}=\{(G_{n},d_{n})\}
be a family of groups
G_{n}
equipped with bi-invariant metrics
d_{n}
.
A group
\Gamma
is called
\mathscr{G}
-approximated
if there exist maps (not necessarily homomorphisms)
\varphi_{n}:\Gamma\to G_{n}
, such that:

(1)

The sequence
\varphi_{n}
is an
asymptotic homomrphism
, namely
\forall g,h\in\Gamma,\;\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{n}(\varphi_{n}(gh),\varphi_{n}(g)\varphi_{n}(h))=0
.

(2)

\varphi_{n}

asymptotically separates

\Gamma
, that is,

\forall g\in\Gamma\setminus\{1\},\;\liminf_{n}\;d_{n}(\varphi_{n}(g),1_{G_{n}})>0.

The group
\Gamma
is said to be
\mathscr{G}
-stable
if for every asymptotic homomorphism
\varphi_{n}:\Gamma\to G_{n}
(as in
(1)
above), there exists
true
homomorphisms
\psi_{n}:\Gamma\to G_{n}
such that

\forall g\in\Gamma,\;\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{n}(\varphi_{n}(g),\psi_{n}(g))=0.

The notions of approximation and stability have been studied quite intensively in recent years (cf.
[
19
,
9
,
17
,
7
,
8
,
12
]
and the references therein).

The main example of families of metric groups
\mathscr{G}
studied are:

(1)

\mathscr{G}_{\Hamm}=\{(\Sym(n),d^{\text{Hamm}}_{n})\}_{n}
, where for
\sigma,\tau\in\Sym(n)
,

d^{\Hamm}_{n}(\sigma,\tau)=\frac{1}{n}|\{i\in[n]|\;\sigma(i)\neq\tau(i)\}|.

In all the instances considered in this paper, we have
G_{n}=U(n)
, and the metric
d_{n}
will be defined by a unitarily invariant norm
\|\cdot\|_{n}
on the matrices
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
via
d_{n}(u,v)=\|u-v\|_{n}
for
u,v\in U(n)
. For
A\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, denote
|A|=\sqrt{A^{*}A}
, and now:

(2)

\mathscr{G}_{\Frob}
: The
Frobenius norm

\|A\|_{\Frob}=\Tr{(|A|^{2})}^{1/2}
.

(3)

\mathscr{G}_{\HS}
: The (normalized)
Hilbert–Schmidt norm
, or simply
HS-norm
,
\|A\|_{\HS}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\|A\|_{\Frob}
.

(4)

\mathscr{G}_{p}
: For
1\leq p<\infty
, The
Schatten
p
-norm
defined by
\|A\|_{p}=\Tr(|A|^{p})^{1/p}
(For
p=2
, this is the Frobenius norm).

(5)

\mathscr{G}_{\op}
: The
operator norm
norm
\|A\|_{\op}=\sup_{v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n},\|v\|=1}\|Av\|
. This is considered as the case
p=\infty
and we write
\|A\|_{\infty}=\|A\|_{\op}
.

Finding groups which are not
\mathscr{G}
-approximated is notoriously hard.
One of the motivations to study stability comes from the following observation (see
[
14
,
2
]
for
\mathscr{G}_{\Hamm}
, but valid in all the cases listed above).

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a class as above, where
G_{n}
are linear groups, and
\Gamma
a finitely generated group.
If
\Gamma
is
\mathscr{G}
-approximated and
\mathscr{G}
-stable, then it is residually finite.

This implies that if one found a finitely generated group
\Gamma
which is
\mathscr{G}
-stable and not residually finite, then it is
not

\mathscr{G}
-approximated.
This line of proof was implemented first in
[
9
]
to give examples of non-Frobenius approximated groups, i.e. groups which are not
\mathscr{G}_{2}
-approximated.
Following this breakthrough, Thom asked in his ICM address
[
19
, Question 3.11]
about all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
In response, Lubotzky and Oppenheim
[
17
]
solved almost all cases and gave non-
\mathscr{G}_{p}
-approximated groups for
1<p<\infty
.
These examples are the so called "
\ell
-adic Deligne central extensions", see
[
9
, Section 5.2]
for their construction.
There are thus four cases left to handle:
p=1
, the Hamming distance on permutation groups, the HS-norm and the operator norm.

We prove that
\ell
-adic Deligne central extensions are not
\mathscr{G}_{1}
-approximated,
solving the case
p=1
in Thom’s question.
Interestingly, our method is different from the previous works – we manage to establish that these groups are not
\mathscr{G}_{1}
-approximated, without the need to show that they are
\mathscr{G}_{1}
-stable.

With that said, this is only modest progress as the last three cases are the holy-grail of the subject: Groups which are
\mathscr{G}_{\Hamm}
-approximated are called
sofic
, the ones which are
\mathscr{G}_{\HS}
-approximated are called
hyperlinear
or
Connes embeddable
, and the ones which are
\mathscr{G}_{\op}
-approximated are called
MF
1

1

There are multiple, possibly inequivalent definitions for MF groups in the literature, this is the one introduced in
[
5
]
, see
[
18
]
for the more C
∗
-algebraic one.
.
Whether all groups are sofic, hyperlinear or MF are three major open problems in group theory.
Given the previous works, it is natural to study whether the
\ell
-adic Deligne central extensions are the much desired
concrete
counter examples to these three problems.
The reader is referred to
[
7
,
8
,
15
,
6
]
for efforts to prove they are not sofic, and to
[
12
]
for efforts to prove that
real
Deligne central extensions are not hyperlinear.

The novelty in our approach is the use of at least two
different
norms on
\U(n)
to deduce inapproximability.
Usually, when two different bi-invariant metrics
d_{n}\leq d_{n}^{\prime}
are given on
G_{n}
, it is not clear that
\{G_{n},d_{n}^{\prime}\}
-approximation (respectively,
\{G_{n},d_{n}^{\prime}\}
-stability) implies
\{G_{n},d_{n}\}
-approximation (respectively,
\{G_{n},d_{n}\}
-stability), nor vise versa.

Denote by
\ker_{\mathrm{RF}}(\Gamma)
the kernel of the map from
\Gamma
to its profinite completion
\widehat{\Gamma}
.
In other words,
\ker_{\mathrm{RF}}(\Gamma)
consists of elements in
\Gamma
that are trivial in all finite quotients.

Definition 1.2
.

A finitely generated group
\Gamma
is said to be
of Deligne-type
if there is a central element
J\in\Gamma
of order
2
such that
J\in\ker_{RF}(\Gamma)
.

See
[
11
]
, and many more in
[
16
]
.
Our main theorem is a version of Proposition
1.1
which deals with two different norms, that is applicable to groups of Deligne-type. This refines the previously taken route of proving stability in order to deduce inapproximability.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group of Deligne-type, and let
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.
If
\Gamma
is
\mathscr{G}_{q}
-stable, then it is not
\mathscr{G}_{p}
-approximated.
In particular, if
\Gamma
is Frobenius stable, then it is not
\mathscr{G}_{p}
-approximated for any
1\leq p\leq 2
.

Consequently, the
\ell
-adic Deligne central extensions appearing in
[
9
]
yield groups which are not
\mathscr{G}_{1}
-approximated.
In fact, by
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
and the original work of Deligne
[
11
]
, it follows that the degree
4
-cover of
\Sp_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})
, for
g\geq 3
, is not
\mathscr{G}_{1}
-approximated.

Note that the method here really is different from the one in
[
9
]
and
[
17
]
. There, it was shown that for every
1<p<\infty
there exists a non-residually finite group which is
p
-stable and
hence
not
p
-approximated. Here we only prove that the groups are not
1
-approximated without proving stability; The question of
1
-stability for these groups remains open.

Since the normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm is dominated by the operator norm, we also obtain the following in a similar manner:

Proposition 1.4
.

If a group of Deligne-type is not hyperlinear, then it is not MF.

We also present a suitable refinement to Proposition
1.1
, which serves as a potential approach to tackle the MF question.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group of Deligne type. Assume that the following holds:

(*)

For every
d_{\op}
-asymptotic homomorphism
\varphi_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(k_{n})
, there exists a sequence of homomorphisms
\psi_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(k_{n})
such that for all
x\in\Gamma
,
\lim_{n}\|\varphi_{n}(x)-\psi_{n}(x)\|_{\HS}=0
.

Then
\Gamma
is not MF.

We will refer to condition
(*)
above as is
operator-HS-stablility
(as in
[
13
]
).
Let us suggest the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group such that
H^{2}(\Gamma,V)=0
for every unitary representation of
\Gamma
on a Hilbert space
V
. Then
\Gamma
is operator-HS-stable.

Recall that the
\ell
-adic Deligne central extensions satisfy the above cohomological vanishing condition (in fact, they have property
[T_{2}]
of Bader and Sauer
[
4
]
), hence if Conjecture
1.6

is true, these groups are not MF.

2.
Proofs of the results

2.1.
Unitarily invariant matrix norms

Throughout this subsection, for every
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we fix
unitarily invariant norm

\|\cdot\|
on
\M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
, meaning
\|uxv\|=\|x\|
for all
u,v\in\U(d),x\in\M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Note that all the matrix norms mentioned in the introduction are instances of unitarily invariant norms.
let us recall some basic properties at this level of generality.

Lemma 2.1

(Proposition 1.3 in
[
9
]
)
.

Let
A,B,C\in{\rm M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, it holds that:

(1)

\|ABC\|\leq\|A\|_{\op}\|B\|\|C\|_{\op},

(2)

\lVert A\rVert=\lVert A^{*}\rVert=\lVert|A|\rVert
,

(3)

If
A
and
B
are positive semi-definite matrices and
A\leq B
, then
\lVert A\rVert\leq\lVert B\rVert.

We will need the following, which asserts that any unitary which is close to being an involution is close to a unitary which is also an involution.

Lemma 2.2

(Proposition 1.4 in
[
9
]
)
.

Let
A\in{\rm U}(d)
. Then there is a unitary
B\in{\U}(d)
such that
B^{2}=1
and

\lVert B-A\lVert\leq\lVert 1-A^{2}\rVert,

for all unitarily invariant norms.

Recall the following folklore lemma.
Since we need it at the generality of arbitrary unitarily invariant norms, we provide a proof adapted from
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
M\in\M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, there exists
R\in\U(n)
such that
\|M-R\|\leq\|M^{*}M-1\|
for all unitarily invariant norms.

Proof.

For
M\in\M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, write the Singular Value Decomposition

M=S\Sigma V^{*}
where
V,S\in U(n)
and
\Sigma=\diag(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})

is a diagonal matrix with the singular values
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
.
We have:

\|M^{*}M-1\|=\|V\Sigma S^{*}S\Sigma V^{*}-1\|=\|V\Sigma^{2}V^{*}-1\|=\|\Sigma^{2}-1\|

where we used unitary invariance in the last equality.
Now, since we have for
\lambda\geq 0
,
|\lambda^{2}-1|=|(\lambda-1)||(\lambda+1)|\geq|(\lambda-1)|
, we have that
|\Sigma^{2}-1|\geq|\Sigma-1|
.
Consequently, by
(2),(3)
in Lemma
2.1
, we have:

\|\Sigma-1\|=\||\Sigma-1|\|\leq\||\Sigma^{2}-1|\|=\|=\|\Sigma^{2}-1\|.

Now, define the unitary
R=SV^{*}\in\U(n)
. Observe that again by unitary invariance:

\|M-R\|=\|S\Sigma V^{*}-SV^{*}\|=\|S(\Sigma-1)V^{*}\|=\|\Sigma-1\|\leq\|M^{*}M-1\|.

∎

Using this, we can prove the following regarding restriction of
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-approximate homomorphisms to almost invariant subspaces.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and let
\varphi_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(d_{n})
be a
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-asymptotic homomorphism. Assume that there exist nonzero projections
P_{n}\in\M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
such that
\|\varphi_{n}(g)P_{n}-P_{n}\varphi_{n}(g)\|_{p}\to 0
for all
g\in\Gamma
.
Then there exists a
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-asymptotic homomorphism
\psi_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(\Ima(P_{n}))
such that:

\lim_{n}\|\psi_{n}(g)-P_{n}\varphi_{n}(g)P_{n}\|_{p}=0\text{ for all $g\in\Gamma$.}

Proof.

Define
\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(g)=P_{n}\varphi_{n}(g)P_{n}\in\M_{\rank(P_{n})}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Observe that we have for all
g,h\in\Gamma
:

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(g)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(h)-\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(gh)\|_{p}=\|P_{n}\varphi_{n}(g)P_{n}\varphi_{n}(h)P_{n}-P_{n}\varphi_{n}(gh)P_{n}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{n}\varphi_{n}(g)\varphi_{n}(h)P_{n}-P_{n}\varphi_{n}(gh)P_{n}\|_{p}+\|P_{n}[\varphi_{n}(g),P_{n}]\varphi_{n}(h)P_{n}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{n}(g)\varphi_{n}(h)-\varphi_{n}(gh)\|_{p}+\|[\varphi_{n}(g),P_{n}]\|_{p}.

Note that we used Lemma
2.1
and that
\|P_{n}\|_{\op}=1
.
Consequently, we have
\lim_{n}\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(g)\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(h)-\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(gh)\|_{p}=0
for all
g,h\in\Gamma
.
Since
\varphi_{n}(g)
is unitary, this also shows
\lim_{n}\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(g)^{*}\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(g)-P_{n}\|_{p}=0
.
By Lemma
2.3
, we can replace
\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(g)
by unitaries
\psi_{n}(g)\in\U(\Ima(P_{n}))
which are asymptotically close in
\|\cdot\|_{p}
to
\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(g)
, and
\psi_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(\Ima(P_{n}))
will be the desired asymptotic homomorphism.
∎

2.2.
Approximation of Deligne type groups

We can now prove the main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group of Deligne type, and
J\in\ker_{RF}(\Gamma)
a central element of order
2
.
Let
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, and assume that
\Gamma
is
\mathscr{G}_{q}
-stable.
Assume by contradiction that
\Gamma
is
\mathscr{G}_{p}
-approximated.
Let
\varphi_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(d_{n})
be a
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-asymptotic homomorphism which is
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-separating.
By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|\varphi_{n}(J)-1\|_{p}\geq\delta
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
J^{2}=1
, we have
\lim_{n}\|\varphi_{n}(J)^{2}-1\|_{p}=0
.
Consequently, by Lemma
2.2
there exist
U_{n}\in\U(d_{n})
with
U_{n}^{2}=1
such that
\lim_{n}\|U_{n}-\varphi_{n}(J)\|_{p}=0
.
As such, For all
n
large enough we have
\|U_{n}-1\|_{p}\geq\delta/2
, in particular,
U_{n}\neq 1
.
Let
P_{n}=(U_{n}-1)/2
be the projection onto the
-1
-eigenspace of
U_{n}
.
Since
U_{n}\neq 1
, we have
P_{n}
is a nonzero projection.

Since
J
is central and
\varphi_{n}
is an
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-asymptotic homomorphism, we have
\lim_{n}\|[\varphi_{n}(g),\varphi_{n}(J)]\|_{p}=0
for all
g\in\Gamma
.
Consequently,
\lim_{n}\|[\varphi_{n}(g),U_{n}]|_{p}=\lim_{n}\|[\varphi_{n}(g),P_{n}]\|_{p}=0
, so that
\varphi_{n}(g)
all asymptotically preserve the subspace
\Ima(P_{n})
.
As such, by Proposition
2.4
there exists a
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-asymptotic homomorphism
\psi_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(\Ima(P_{n}))
such that
\lim_{n}\|\psi_{n}(g)-P_{n}\varphi_{n}(g)P_{n}\|_{p}=0
for all
g\in\Gamma
.
In particular:

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}(J)-(-1)\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\|\psi_{n}(J)-P_{n}U_{n}P_{n}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{n}(J)-P_{n}\varphi_{n}(J)P_{n}\|_{p}+\|P_{n}(U_{n}-\varphi_{n}(J))P_{n}\|_{p}

As the rightmost term asymptomatically vanishes, we have
\lim_{n}\|\psi_{n}(J)+1\|_{p}=0
.
Now, since
\|\cdot\|_{p}\geq\|\cdot\|_{q}
, we conclude that
\psi_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(\Ima(P_{n}))
is in particular a
\|\cdot\|_{q}
-asymptotic homomorphism, and that
\lim_{n}\|\psi_{n}(J)+1\|_{q}=0
.
Since
\Gamma
is
\mathscr{G}_{q}
-stable, there exists a sequence of genuine representations
\rho_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(\Ima(P_{n}))
such that
\lim_{n}\|\rho_{n}(g)-\psi_{n}(g)\|_{q}=0
for all
g\in\Gamma
, in particular for
g=J
.
This implies that
\rho_{n}(J)\neq 1
for
n
large enough.
Thus, by Malcev’s theorem, we can find a finite quotient of
\Gamma
in which
J
is not trivial, contradicting
J\in\ker_{RF}(\Gamma)
.
∎

The proof of Proposition
1.4
is exactly as the proof of Theorem
1.3
, replacing
\|\cdot\|_{p}
with the operator norm, and
\|\cdot\|_{q}
with the normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm.
It is not hard to see that the same proof also yields Proposition
1.5

, as the stability was only applied to operator norm asymptotic homomorphisms.
It is worthwhile to note that the argument above, which allows to construct an asymptotic homomorphism
\varphi_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(d_{n})
with the additional property
\varphi_{n}(J)=-1
, is analogous to
[
6
, Corollary 2.5]
in the setting of sofic approximations.

3.
Concluding remarks

We end with a more general version of Theorem
1.3
, which might be of independent interest.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.
Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group with a finite normal subgroup
N
such that
\Gamma/N
is
\mathscr{G}_{q}
-approximated.
If
\Gamma
is
\mathscr{G}_{p}
-approximated, then it is
\mathscr{G}_{q}
-approximated.

In particular, if the quotient
\Gamma/N
is residually finite (this is true for real and
\ell
-adic Deligne extensions), then there is a monotonicity phenomenon for
\mathscr{G}_{p}
-approximability for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, with no reference to stability.

We will only sketch the proof:
We first prove the proposition under the assumption that
N
is central in
\Gamma
.
Assume
\Gamma
is
\mathscr{G}_{p}
-approximated and let
\varphi_{n}:\Gamma\to\U(d_{n})
be a
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-asymptotic homomorphism which is
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-separating.
It is enough to show that any
1\neq g_{0}\in N
can be separated by
\|\cdot\|_{q}
-asymptotic homomorphisms, since all elements in
\Gamma\setminus N
can be separated by
\|\cdot\|_{q}
-asymptotic homomorphisms of
\Gamma/N
by assumption. One can then take the direct sum of finitely many asymptotic homomorphisms separating each nontrivial element of
N
, as well as an almost homomorphism separating elements in
\Gamma\setminus N
, which will be a separating asymptotic homomorphism of
\Gamma
.

Since the finite group
N
is
\mathscr{G}_{p}
-stable (see for example
[
10
]
), there exists a sequence of homomorphisms
\alpha_{n}:N\to U(d_{n})
which are asymptotically close to the restriction of
\varphi_{n}
to
N
in the
p
-norm.
We may replace the restriction of
\varphi_{n}
to
N
by
\alpha_{n}
, and
\varphi_{n}
will remain an asymptotically separating asymptotic homomorphism.
Thus, we can find characters
\chi_{n}\in\widehat{N}
such that
\chi_{n}(g_{0})\neq 1
and
P_{\chi_{n}}
, the projection onto the
\chi_{n}
-isotypic component of
\alpha_{n}
, is nontrivial.
Since
N
is central, it can be shown that
\lim_{n}\|[P_{\chi_{n}},\varphi_{n}(g)]\|_{p}=0
for every
g\in\Gamma
, and as a result we can use Proposition
2.4
to find a
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-asymptotic homomorphism
\psi_{n}:\Gamma\to U(\Ima P_{\chi_{n}})
such that
\psi_{n}(g_{0})
is the scalar matrix with entries
\chi_{n}(g_{0})
.
As
\|\cdot\|_{q}\leq\|\cdot\|_{p}
, we see that
\psi_{n}
is a
\|\cdot\|_{q}
-asymptotic homomorphism which separates
g_{0}
and we are done.

In case
N\triangleleft\Gamma
is not central, we can consider its centralizer
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
.
As
N
is finite and normal,
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
has finite index.
If
\Gamma
is
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-approximated, so is
\Lambda
.
Note that
N\cap\Lambda\leq\Lambda
is central and
\Lambda/(N\cap\Lambda)\simeq\Lambda N/N\leq\Gamma/N
is
\mathscr{G}_{q}
-approximated as well.
It follows from the previous proof that
\Lambda
is
\mathscr{G}_{q}
-approximated.
As
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
has finite index, it is not hard to show using an induction construction that
\Gamma
is
\mathscr{G}_{q}
-approximated as well.

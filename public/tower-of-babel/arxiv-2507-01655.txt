Almost Calabi-Yau with torsion 6-manifolds and the instanton condition


Abstract
It is observed that on a compact almost complex Calabi-Yau with torsion (ACYT) 6-manifold with co-closed Lee form the curvature of the torsion connection is an S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) -instanton if and only if the torsion is parallel with respect to the torsion connection. The same conclusion holds for any (non necessarily compact) balanced ACYT 6-manifold. In particular, on a CYT 6-manifold the Strominger-Bismut connection is an S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) -instanton if and only if the torsion is parallel with respect to the Strominger-Bismut connection provided either the CYT 6-manifold is compact with co-closed Lee form or it is a balanced CYT 6-manifold. Keywords: torsion connection, S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) holonomy, almost Calabi-Yau with torsion S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) -instanton AMS MSC2020: 53C55, 53C21, 53C29, 53Z05

Introduction

Riemannian manifolds with metric connections having totally skew-symmetric torsion and special holonomy received a lot of interest in mathematics and theoretical physics mainly from supersymmetric string theories and supergravity. The main reason comes from the Hull-Strominger system which describes the supersymmetric background in heterotic string theories
[
59
,
34
]
.

The number of preserved supersymmetries depends on the number of parallel spinors with respect to a metric connection
{\nabla}
with totally skew-symmetric torsion
T
. The torsion 3-form
T
is identified with the 3-form field strength in these theories.
The presence of a
\nabla
-parallel spinor leads to restriction of the
holonomy group
Hol(\nabla)
of the torsion connection
\nabla
.
Namely,
Hol(\nabla)
has to be contained in
SU(n)
,
dim=2n

[
59
,
30
,
38
,
39
,
11
,
6
,
7
]
.

Complex non-Kähler geometries appear
in string compactifications and are studied intensively for a long time

[
59
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
25
,
6
,
7
]
.
Hermitian manifolds have widespread applications in both physics and differential geometry in connection with solutions to the Hull-Strominger system see
[
48
,
19
,
20
,
17
,
51
,
55
,
16
,
15

22
,

,

]
and references therein.
On a Hermitian manifold, there exists a unique
connection which preserves the Hermitian structure and has
totally skew-symmetric torsion tensor. Its existence and explicit expression first appeared in Strominger’s seminal paper
[
59
]
in 1986 in connection with the heterotic supersymmetric string background, where he called it the H-connection. Three years later, Bismut formally discussed
and used this connection in his local index theorem paper
[
10
]
, which leads to the name Bismut
connection in literature.
We call this connection the Strominger-Bismut connection. Note that the connection also appeared implicitly earlier (
[
60
]
) and in some literature it was also called the KT connection (Kähler with torsion) or characteristic connection. When the holonomy of the Strominger-Bismut connection is contained in
SU(n)
one has the notion of Calabi-Yau manifold with torsion (CYT) spaces and these manifolds are of great interest in string theories and in mathematics.

Some types of non-complex 6-manifolds have also been invented in the
string theory due to the mirror symmetry and T-duality

[
28
,
29
,
11
,
12
,
45
,
44
,
33
]
.
Almost Hermitian manifolds with totally skew-symmetric Nijenhuis
tensor arise as target spaces of a class of (2,0)-supersymmetric
two-dimensional sigma models
[
54
]
. For the consistency of
the theory, the Nijenhuis tensor has to be parallel with respect
to the torsion connection with holonomy contained in
SU(n)
. The known models are group manifolds as well as the nearly Kähler spaces.
In general, an almost Hermitian manifold does not admit a metric connection preserving the almost Hermitian structure and having totally skew-symmetric torsion. It is shown in
[
18
, Theorem 10.1]
that this is equivalent to the condition that the Nijenhuis tensor of type (0,3)
is totally skew-symmetric, i.e. this is the class
G_{1}
in Gray-Hervella classification
[
27
]
. In this case, the connection is unique. This class contains the Hermitian manifolds with Strominger-Bismut connection as well as the nearly Kähler spaces. In the nearly Kähler case the torsion connection
{\nabla}
is the characteristic connection considered by Gray
[
26
]
, and the torsion
T
and the Nijenhuis tensor are
{\nabla}
-parallel (see e.g.
[
43
,
8
]
). If the holonomy of the torsion connection is contained in
SU(n)
, that is, the Ricci 2-form
\rho
of
{\nabla}
representing the first Chern class is identically zero, then we have the notion of
Almost Calabi-Yau manifold with torsion (briefly ACYT)
.

As it was pointed out in
[
59
]
, the existence of a parallel spinor with respect to a metric connection
{\nabla}
with torsion 3-form, in dimension six, leads to the restriction that its holonomy group has to be contained in
SU(3)
. This means one has to consider an
SU(3)
-structure, i.e. an almost Hermitian manifold
(M^{6},g,J)
with topologically trivial canonical bundle trivialized by a (3,0) with respect to
J
form
\Psi=\Psi^{+}+\sqrt{-1}\,\Psi^{-}
endowed with a metric connection preserving the
SU(3)
structure with totally skew-symmetric torsion.

From the point of view of physics, the Hull-Strominger system is consisted of the Killing spinor equations and the anomaly cancellation condition. The Killing spinor equations lead to consider compact CYT space with an exact Lee form, i.e. the trace of the torsion should be an exact 1-form
[
59
]
. The anomaly cancellation condition expresses the exterior derivative of the torsion 3-form in terms of the difference of the first Pontrjagin 4-form of an instanton connection on a vector bundle and a connection on the tangent bundle. It is shown in
[
37
]
that solutions to the Hull-Strominger system solve the heterotic equations of motion in dimensions 5,6,7 and 8 if and only if the connection on the tangent bundle in the anomally cancellation condition is an instanton (see
[
50
]
and
[
51
]
for extension of this result to all dimensions). The instanton condition (Yang-Mills connection) is highly non-linear PDE and the physically relevant connection is the Hull connection which is defined as the metric connection with torsion
-T
.
It is known that if there exists an SU(3)-instanton on a holomorphic vector bundle on a complex 6-manifold then it is unique because of the non-Kähler version of the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem established by Li-Yau in
[
47
]
.

The main purpose of the paper is to investigate the
SU(3)
–instanton condition of the Strominger-Bismut and
SU(3)
-Hull connections on an ACYT 6-manifold.

Let

{\nabla}
-parallel,

It is known from

Corollary 1.2

Let
(M,g,J,\Psi)

Then the torsion connection

{\nabla}
-parallel,
{\nabla}T=0

In the compact case we show

Let
(M,g,J,\Psi)
be a compact ACYT 6-manifold with co-closed Lee form.

Then the torsion connection
{\nabla}
is an
SU(3)
–instanton if and only if the torsion is
{\nabla}
-parallel,
{\nabla}T=0
.

For a complex space, the next corollary is straightforward consequence of Theorem

{\nabla}

{\nabla}

{\nabla}

Note that Hermitian and almost Hermitian manifolds of type

.

2.1
Metric connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion and its curvature

Let
(M,g,{\nabla})
be a Riemannian manifold with a metric connection
{\nabla}
and
T(X,Y)={\nabla}_{X}Y-{\nabla}_{Y}X-[X,Y]
be its torsion. The torsion of type (0,3) is denoted by the same letter and it is defined by
T(X,Y,Z)=g(T(X,Y),Z)
. The torsion is totally skew-symmetric if
T(X,Y,Z)=-T(X,Z,Y)
.

On a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
n
any metric connection
{\nabla}
with totally skew-symmetric torsion
T
is connected with the Levi-Civita connection
{\nabla}^{g}
of the metric
g
by

{\nabla}^{g}={\nabla}-\frac{1}{2}T\quad leading\quad to\quad{\nabla^{g}}T={\nabla}T+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{T},

(2.1)

where the 4-form
\sigma^{T}
, introduced in
[
18
]
, is defined by

\sigma^{T}(X,Y,Z,V)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{j}\lrcorner T)\wedge(e_{j}\lrcorner T)(X,Y,Z,V),

(2.2)

(e_{a}\lrcorner T)(X,Y)=T(e_{a},X,Y)
is the interior multiplication and
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis.

The properties of the 4-form
\sigma^{T}
are studied in detail in
[
2
]
where it is shown that
\sigma^{T}
measures the ‘degeneracy’ of the 3-form
T
.

The exterior derivative
dT
has the following expression (see e.g.
[
36
,
39
,
18
]
)

\begin{split}dT(X,Y,Z,V)=d^{{\nabla}}T(X,Y,Z,V)+2\sigma^{T}(X,Y,Z,V),\quad where\\
d^{{\nabla}}T(X,Y,Z,V)=(\nabla_{X}T)(Y,Z,V)+(\nabla_{Y}T)(Z,X,V)+(\nabla_{Z}T)(X,Y,V)-(\nabla_{V}T)(X,Y,Z).\end{split}

(2.3)

For the curvature of
{\nabla}
we use the convention
R(X,Y)Z=[\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}]Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z
and
R(X,Y,Z,V)=g(R(X,Y)Z,V)
. It has the well-known properties

R(X,Y,Z,V)=-R(Y,X,Z,V)=-R(X,Y,V,Z)
.

The first Bianchi identity for
\nabla
can be written in the form (see e.g.
[
36
,
39
,
18
]
)

\begin{split}R(X,Y,Z,V)+R(Y,Z,X,V)+R(Z,X,Y,V)\\
=dT(X,Y,Z,V)-\sigma^{T}(X,Y,Z,V)+(\nabla_{V}T)(X,Y,Z).\end{split}

(2.4)

It is proved in
[
18
, p.307]
that the curvature of a metric connection
{\nabla}
with totally skew-symmetric torsion
T
satisfies also the identity

\begin{split}R(X,Y,Z,V)+R(Y,Z,X,V)+R(Z,X,Y,V)-R(V,X,Y,Z)-R(V,Y,Z,X)-R(V,Z,X,Y)\\
=\frac{3}{2}dT(X,Y,Z,V)-\sigma^{T}(X,Y,Z,V),\end{split}

(2.5)

which combined with (
2.4
) yields
[
40
,
41
]

\begin{split}R(V,X,Y,Z)+R(V,Y,Z,X)+R(V,Z,X,Y)=-\frac{1}{2}dT(X,Y,Z,V)+(\nabla_{V}T)(X,Y,Z)\end{split}

(2.6)

It is known that a metric connection
{\nabla}
with torsion 3-form
T
has curvature
R\in S^{2}\Lambda^{2}
, i.e. it satisfies

R(X,Y,Z,V)=R(Z,V,X,Y)

(2.7)

if and only if the covariant derivative of the torsion with respect to the torsion connection is a 4-form.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
36
, Lemma 3.4]
The next equivalences hold for a metric connection with torsion 3-form

({\nabla}_{X}T)(Y,Z,V)=-({\nabla}_{Y}T)(X,Z,V)\Longleftrightarrow R(X,Y,Z,V)=R(Z,V,X,Y))\Longleftrightarrow dT=4{\nabla^{g}}T.

(2.8)

The Ricci tensors and scalar curvatures of the connections
{\nabla^{g}}
and
{\nabla}
are related by
[
18
, Section 2]
, (see also
[
23
, Prop. 3.18]
)

\begin{split}Ric^{g}(X,Y)=Ric(X,Y)+\frac{1}{2}(\delta T)(X,Y)+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(T(X,e_{i}),T(Y,e_{i}));\\
Scal^{g}=Scal+\frac{1}{4}||T||^{2},\qquad Ric(X,Y)-Ric(Y,X)=-(\delta T)(X,Y),\end{split}

(2.9)

where
\delta=(-1)^{np+n+1}*d*
is the co-differential acting on
p
-forms and
*
is the Hodge star operator satisfying
*^{2}=(-1)^{p(n-p)}
.

2.2
Torsion connection preserving an almost Hermitian structure

In this section we recall the notions and existence of a metric linear connection preserving a given almost Hermitian structure and having totally skew-symmetric torsion from
[
18
]
.

Let
(M,g,J)
be an almost Hermitian 2n-dimensional manifold with Riemannian
metric
g
and an almost complex structure
J
satisfying

g(JX,JY)=g(X,Y).

The Nijenhuis tensor
N
, the Kähler
form
F
and the Lee form
\theta
are defined by

N=[J.,J.]-[.,.]-J[J.,.]-J[.,J.],\quad F=g(.,J.),\quad\theta(.)=\delta F(J.),

(2.10)

respectively, where
\delta=-*d*
is the co-differential on an even dimensional manifold.

Consequently, the 1-form
J\theta
defined by
J\theta(X)=-\theta(JX)
is co-closed due to
\delta(J\theta)=-\delta^{2}F=0
.

The Nijenhuis tensor of type (0,3), denoted by the same letter, is defined by
N(X,Y,Z)=g(N(X,Y),Z)
.

In general, on an almost Hermitian manifold, there not always exists a metric connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion preserving the almost Hermitian structure.
It is shown in
[
18
, Theorem 10.1]
that there
exists a unique linear connection
{\nabla}
preserving an almost hermitian
structure and having totally skew-symmetric torsion
T
if and only if
the Nijenhuis tensor
N
is a 3-form. In this case the 3-form
N
is of type (3,0)+(0,3) with respect to the almost complex structure
J
. This is precisely the class
G_{1}
in the Gray-Hervella classification of almost Hermitian manifolds
[
27
]
and includes Hermitian manifolds,
N=0
as well as nearly Kähler spaces,
({\nabla^{g}}_{X}J)X=0
. We call this connection
the torsion connection
.

The torsion
T
of the torsion connection is
determined by
[
18
, Theorem 10.1]

T=JdF+N=-dF(J.,J.,J.)+N=-dF^{+}(J.,J.,J.)+\frac{1}{4}N,

(2.11)

where
dF^{+}
denotes the (1,2)+(2,1)-part of
dF
with respect to
J
and satisfies the equality

\begin{split}dF^{+}(X,Y,Z)=dF^{+}(JXJ,Y,Z)+dF^{+}(JX,Y,JZ)+dF^{+}(X,JY,JZ).\end{split}

(2.12)

The (3,0)+(0,3)-part
dF^{-}
obeys the identity

dF^{-}(JX,Y,Z)=dF^{-}(X,JY,Z)=dF^{-}(X,Y,JZ)

and is determined
completely by the Nijenhuis tensor
[
24
]
. If
N
is a three form then (see e.g.
[
24
,
18
]
)

dF^{-}(X,Y,Z)=\frac{3}{4}N(JX,Y,Z).

The Lee form
\theta:=\delta F\circ J
of the
G_{1}
- manifold is given in terms of the torsion
T
as

\theta(X)=-\frac{1}{2}T(JX,e_{i},Je_{i})=\frac{1}{2}g(T(JX),F)=\frac{1}{2}(F\lrcorner T(JX))~,~~~\theta_{i}={1\over 2}J^{k}{}_{i}T_{kj\ell}F_{j\ell}~,

(2.13)

where
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n},e_{n+1}=Je_{1},\dots,e_{2n}=Je_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis.

When the almost complex structure is integrable,
N=0
, the torsion connection was defined and studied by Strominger
[
59
]
in connection with the heterotic string background and was used by Bismut to prove a local index theorem for
the Dolbeault operator on Hermitian non-Kähler manifold
[
10
]
. This formula was applied also in string theory (see e.g.
[
7
]
). In the Hermitian case, the torsion connection is also
known as the Strominger-Bismut connection and has attained a lot of consideration both in mathematics and physics (see the Introduction).

In the nearly Kähler case, the torsion connection
{\nabla}
is the characteristic connection
considered by Gray,
[
26
]
and the torsion
T
and the Nijenhuis tensor are
{\nabla}-
parallel,
{\nabla}T={\nabla}N=0
(see e.g.
[
43
,
8
]
).

In general
G_{1}
manifold, the holonomy group of
{\nabla}
is contained in
U(n)
. The reduction of the holonomy group of the torsion connection to a subgroup of
SU(n)
can be expressed in terms of its Ricci 2-form which represents the first Chern class, namely

\rho(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}R(X,Y,e_{i},Je_{i})=0.

We remark that the formula
[
39
, (3.16)]
holds in the general case of a
G_{1}
-manifold (see also
[
18
]
), namely, the next formula holds true

\rho(X,Y)=Ric(X,JY)+({\nabla}_{X}\theta)(JY)+\frac{1}{4}dT(X,Y,e_{i},Je_{i}).

Thus, on a
G_{1}
manifold the restricted holonomy group of the torsion connection is contained in
SU(n)
if and only if the next condition holds
[
18
, Theorem 10.5]

Ric(X,Y)+({\nabla}_{X}\theta)(Y)-\frac{1}{4}dT(X,JY,e_{i},Je_{i})=0.

The scalar curvature of the torsion connection and the Riemannian scalar curvature on an ACYT space are given by
[
41
]

\begin{split}Scal=3\delta\theta+2||\theta||^{2}-\frac{1}{3}||dF^{+}||^{2}+\frac{1}{16}||N||^{2};\qquad Scal^{g}=3\delta\theta+2||\theta||^{2}-\frac{1}{3}||dF^{+}||^{2}+\frac{5}{64}||N||^{2}.\end{split}

It is shown in
[
41
]
that if the structure is balanced,
\theta=0
, and the torsion is closed,
dT=0
then
16|dF|^{2}=3|N|^{2}
. In the complex case,
N=0
, this confirms the old result first established in
[
4
]
that a balanced Hermitian manifold with closed torsion must be Kähler.

3
SU(3)-structures

Let
(M,g,J)
be an almost Hermitian 6-manifold with a Riemannian
metric
g
and an almost complex structure
J
, i.e.
(g,J)

define an
U(3)
-structure.

An
SU(3)
-structure is determined by an additional non-degenerate
(3,0)-form
\Psi=\Psi^{+}+\sqrt{-1}\Psi^{-}
of constant norm, or equivalently by a
non-trivial spinor. To be more explicit, we may choose a local
orthonormal frame
e_{1},\dots,e_{6}
, identifying it with the dual
basis via the metric. Write
e_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{p}}
for the
monomial
e_{i_{1}}\wedge e_{i_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{p}}
. An

SU(3)
-structure is described locally by

\begin{split}\Psi=-(e_{1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2})\wedge(e_{3}+\sqrt{-1}e_{4})\wedge(e_{5}+\sqrt{-1}e_{6}),\quad F=-e_{12}-e_{34}-e_{56},\\
\Psi^{+}=-e_{135}+e_{236}+e_{146}+e_{245},\quad\Psi^{-}=-e_{136}-e_{145}-e_{235}+e_{246}.\end{split}

(3.14)

These forms are subject to the compatibility relations

F\wedge\Psi^{\pm}=0,\qquad\Psi^{+}\wedge\Psi^{-}=-\frac{2}{3}F^{3}.

The subgroup of
SO(6)
fixing the forms
F
and
\Psi
simultaneously is
SU(3)
.
The two forms
F
and
\Psi
determine the metric completely. The Lie algebra of
SU(3)

is denoted
\mathfrak{su}(3)
.

The failure of the holonomy group of the Levi-Civita connection
{\nabla}^{g}
of the metric
g
to be reduced to
SU(3)

can be measured by the intrinsic torsion
\tau
, which is identified with
dF,d{\Psi^{+}}
and
d{\Psi^{-}}

and can be decomposed into five classes
[
13
]
,
\tau\in W_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus W_{5}
. The
intrinsic torsion of an
U(n)
- structure belongs to the first four components described
by Gray-Hervella
[
27
]
. The five components of a
SU(3)
-structure are first described
by Chiossi-Salamon
[
13

[

Let

be a 6-dimensional smooth manifold with an

-structure
(g,J,\Psi)

\begin{split}T=-*dF+*(\theta\wedge F)+\frac{1}{4}(N,\Psi^{+})\Psi^{+}+\frac{1}{4}(N,\Psi^{-})\Psi^{-}.\end{split}

The Lee form
\theta
and the functions

can be expressed in terms of the torsion

(4.20)

We also need the next observations from

]

[

\begin{split}C_{\bar{\alpha};\bar{\beta}\gamma}=C_{\bar{\alpha};\bar{\beta}\bar{\gamma}}=C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=C_{\alpha;\beta\bar{\gamma}}=0\,,\quad T^{+}_{\bar{\alpha}\beta\gamma}=-T^{+}_{\beta\bar{\alpha}\gamma}=-C_{\bar{\alpha};\beta\gamma}=C_{\bar{\alpha};\gamma\beta}\,,\\

Lemma 5.7
.

If

–instanton connection with parallel Lee form, then it holds

\begin{split}{\nabla}_{\bar{\alpha}}{\nabla}_{\bar{\beta}}C_{\bar{\gamma};\mu\nu}-{\nabla}_{\bar{\beta}}{\nabla}_{\bar{\alpha}}C_{\bar{\gamma};\mu\nu}=-T^{+}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}\sigma}{\nabla}_{\bar{\sigma}}C_{\bar{\gamma};\mu\nu};\\

{\nabla}_{\bar{\alpha}}{\nabla}_{\bar{\beta}}C_{\bar{\gamma};\alpha\beta}=0.\end{split}

In particular, the norm of the torsion is a constant.

Proof.

The instanton condition yields the curvature 2-form

. Therefore,

0=R_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\times C_{\bar{\gamma};\mu\nu}=R_{\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta},\bar{\gamma},\sigma}C_{\bar{\sigma};\mu\nu}+R_{\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta},\mu,\sigma}C_{\bar{\gamma};\sigma\nu}+R_{\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta},\nu,\sigma}C_{\bar{\gamma};\mu\sigma}.

The Ricci identity for
{\nabla}
reads with the help of (

since

because of (

). This proves the first line of the lemma.

Set
\mu=\alpha,\nu=\beta
into the already proved first equality we obtain the second line using (
5.50
).

To show the last statement, we apply again (
5.49
) to get

{\nabla}_{\bar{\beta}}{\nabla}_{\bar{\alpha}}C_{\bar{\gamma};\beta\alpha}={\nabla}_{\bar{\beta}}{\nabla}_{\bar{\gamma}}C_{\bar{\alpha};\beta\alpha}={\nabla}_{\bar{\beta}}{\nabla}_{\bar{\gamma}}\theta_{\beta}=0

which combined with the already proved second identity of the lemma yields

and therefore

since

.

We start with the next identity

{\nabla}_{i}\delta T_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\delta T_{ia}T_{iaj}\,,

We calculate the left-hand side of (

\begin{split}{\nabla}_{i}\delta T_{ij}={\nabla}_{i}d^{{\nabla}}\theta_{ij}={\nabla}_{i}{\nabla}_{i}\theta_{j}-{\nabla}_{i}{\nabla}_{j}\theta_{i}.\end{split}

(5.55)

Substitute (

{\nabla}_{i}{\nabla}_{i}\theta_{j}-{\nabla}_{i}{\nabla}_{j}\theta_{i}=\frac{1}{2}d^{{\nabla}}\theta_{ab}T_{abj}.

The Ricci identity

{\nabla}_{i}{\nabla}_{j}\theta_{i}={\nabla}_{j}{\nabla}_{i}\theta_{i}+Ric_{js}\theta_{s}-\frac{1}{2}d^{{\nabla}}\theta_{ai}T_{aij}

{\nabla}_{i}{\nabla}_{i}\theta_{j}+{\nabla}_{j}\delta\theta-Ric_{js}\theta_{s}=0.

(5.57)

Multiply the both sides of (

\frac{1}{2}\Delta||\theta|^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}{\nabla^{g}}_{i}{\nabla^{g}}_{i}||\theta||^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}{\nabla}_{i}{\nabla}_{i}||\theta||^{2}=-\theta_{j}{\nabla}_{i}{\nabla}_{i}\theta_{j}-||{\nabla}\theta||^{2}

to get

-\frac{1}{2}\Delta||\theta||^{2}-Ric(\theta,\theta)-||{\nabla}\theta||^{2}=0.

(5.58)

We obtain from (

{\nabla}_{a}T_{bci}{\Phi}_{abcj}=-2{\nabla}_{i}\theta_{j}-4{\nabla}_{j}\theta_{i}.

We get from (

\begin{split}Ric_{ij}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}=\frac{1}{12}dT_{abci}{\Phi}_{abcj}\theta_{i}\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}\theta_{j}{\nabla}_{i}\theta_{j}\\

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{3}=-\frac{2}{t}\,e_{5},\qquad\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{4}=-\frac{2}{t}\,e_{6},\qquad\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{5}=\frac{2}{t}\,e_{3},\qquad\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{6}=\frac{2}{t}\,e_{4},

7.1
Chern instanton

It is known that on a compact non-Kähler CYT manifold with an exact Lee form
\theta=df
for a smooth function
f
, if the curvature of the Chern connection is an
SU(n)
-instanton then
f
must be a constant.

We include here a proof of this fact for completeness based on
[
39
]
. We have

Proposition 7.1
.

On a 2n-dimensional compact CYT manifold if the curvature of the Chern connection is an
SU(n)
-instanton then it is balanced.

Proof.

Let on a compact CYT space
K
be the curvature of the Chern connection and
C=\frac{1}{2}(T(JX,JY,Z)-T(X,Y,Z))
be its torsion. Then
[
39
, Proposition 3.3]
gives the formula

\frac{1}{2}K(e_{i},Je_{i},JX,Y)=\rho^{1,1}(JX,Y)+C(X,e_{i},e_{j})C(Y,e_{i},e_{j})-\frac{1}{4}dT(JX,Y,e_{i},Je_{i}),

where
\rho^{1,1}
is the (1,1) part of
\rho
. Note that
\rho=0
on a CYT manifold.

If
K
is an
SU(n)
-instanton then we get taking the trace in the above formula

0=||C||^{2}-\frac{1}{4}dT(Je_{j},e_{j},e_{i},Je_{i})=\frac{1}{3}||T||^{2}-\frac{1}{3}||T||^{2}+2||\theta||^{2}+2\delta\theta=2||\theta||^{2}+2\delta\theta,

where we used the identities
||C||^{2}=\frac{1}{3}||T||^{2}
and

dT(e_{j},Je_{j},e_{i},Je_{i})=8||\theta||^{2}+8\delta\theta-\frac{4}{3}||T||^{2}

(see
[
39
, (3.24)]
. An integration over the compact space yields
\theta=0
.
∎

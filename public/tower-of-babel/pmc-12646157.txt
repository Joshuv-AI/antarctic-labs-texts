Characteristics, trends, and outcomes of ablation for persistent atrial fibrillation: a Danish cohort study
Authors: Lise Da Riis-Vestergaard, Jacob Tønnesen, Martin H Ruwald, Regitze Hein, Christopher Ryan Zörner, Charlotte Middelfart, Arne Johannessen, Jim Hansen, Rene Husted Worck, Gunnar Gislason, Morten Lock Hansen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsCatheter ablation (CA) is widely used for atrial fibrillation (AF), but pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) alone is less effective in persistent AF (peAF) than paroxysmal AF (PAF). Real-world data on patient selection, outcomes, and complications with PVI or PVI plus (≥1 additional line or CFAE/ganglionated plexi ablation) are limited. We examined characteristics, trends, recurrence, and complications in peAF ablations.Methods and resultsPatients with peAF undergoing first-time catheter ablation between 2010 and 2020 were identified from the Danish National Ablation Database. They were categorized by ablation strategy (PVI or PVI plus), and baseline characteristics and trends were assessed. AF recurrence was analysed using cumulative incidence at one and five years, and across procedural years. Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) and procedure-related complications were also examined. Among 4144 peAF patients, 3.417 received PVI and 727 PVI plus. Baseline characteristics were similar, except the PVI plus group had longer diagnosis-to-ablation-time (mean 5.67 years vs. 3.9 years, P < 0.001) and more severe atrial enlargement (14.8% vs. 9.8%, P < 0.001). AF recurrence was higher with PVI plus at one- and five-years (54% vs. 46%, 77% vs. 68%). Recurrence rates declined over time (2010–2013 vs. 2018–2020). PVI plus use decreased from 58% to 15%. MACE and complication rates were low and similar.ConclusionPVI plus was used less frequently over time and was associated with longer diagnosis-to-ablation times and larger left atria. We observed a proportional decline for both groups in AF recurrence across procedural years, and complication rates were low and similar.

Introduction

Catheter ablation (CA) has shown a clear benefit in terms of freedom from recurrent atrial fibrillation (AF) in a general persistent atrial fibrillation (peAF) population compared to antiarrhythmic medication.1–3 However, recurrence rates after first-time CA for peAF patients are considerably higher than for patients with paroxysmal AF (PAF).4,5

Pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) has long been considered as the cornerstone of AF ablation,6 though this mainly applies for PAF. Whether PVI alone is the optimal lesion set in peAF ablation remains questioned due to the higher recurrence rates and more advanced atrial disease progression. So far inconsistent and conflicting evidence has emerged regarding the optimal approach and ablation strategy for peAF. Due to more extensive atrial structural and electrical remodelling and poorer outcomes in these patients, it has been advocated that a more comprehensive ablation strategy for these patients should be applied.7

Different energy sources, methods, and catheters have been tested and compared with somewhat conflicting results,8–14 and the area is still of great uncertainty.15 Stand-alone PVI though is in most centres the primary choice for first-time CA. In this nationwide cohort study, we examined first-time CA strategies for peAF patients in Denmark, focusing on patient characteristics, trends in ablation strategy use, recurrence rates of AF, and associated complication risks.

Methods

Study design

A nationwide cohort study conducted using real-world data from the Danish healthcare registries assessing patient characteristics, trends, AF recurrence rates, and complications in peAF patients undergoing first-time ablation with PVI or PVI plus.

Data sources

In Denmark all citizens are assigned a unique civil registration number (CPR) at birth or immigration. This can be used to crosslink nationwide administrative registries. In this study we used the Civil Registration System16 containing data on age, sex, migration, and vital status (this register is mandatory and complete); The Danish Register of Causes of Death17 including date of death; The Danish National Patient registry18,19 containing information on hospital admissions, visits to outpatient clinics, and performed procedures, encoded according to the International Classification of Disease, 10th revision (ICD-10) since 1995; The National Prescription registry20 containing information on proclaimed prescriptions, encoded according to the Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical Classification System (ATC-codes) and The Danish National Ablation registry21 containing information on height, weight, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), size of left atrium (LA), and whether AF was paroxysmal or persistent since 2010 according to the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines22 current at the date of ablation. Furthermore, it contains information on procedural characteristics such as ablation strategy (PVI or PVI plus), energy source (radio frequency (RFA) or cryo-balloon (Cryo)), total procedure time, ablation time, X-ray exposure, and duration (see Supplementary material online, Table S1).

Study population

From the Danish National Ablation registry, we identified and included all patients over the age of 18, that underwent first-time ablation for peAF from 1 January 2010 to 31 December 2020. Patients were included at the procedure date and followed until event, death, emigration, or end-of-study 31 December 2022. The definition of peAF followed the ESC guideline applicable at the time of each procedure [2010, 2016, and 2020 updates.23–25] An experienced electrophysiologist assessed the AF subtype on the day of ablation.

Exposure and outcomes

Exposure of interest was ablation strategy (PVI or PVI plus). PVI plus was defined as addition to PVI with one or more of the following variables: Roofline, mitral linear ablation, posterior wall isolation (PWI), complex fractionated atrial electrograms (CFAE), or ganglionated plexi ablation (GP).

This study assessed trends, AF recurrence, major adverse cardiac events, and procedure-related complications in peAF patients undergoing first-time ablation with PVI or PVI plus. AF recurrence was defined as a composite endpoint of hospital admission for AF or atrial flutter (AFL) (ICD-10 I48), redeemed prescriptions for antiarrhythmic drugs (amiodarone C01BD01, dronedarone C01BD07, class 1C anti-arrhythmic medication C01BC), electrical cardioversion (NCSP: BFFA00, BFFA01, BFFA04), or AF re-ablation (NCSP: BFFB04) after a 90-day blanking period.22,23 Major adverse cardiac events within 30 days post-ablation consisted of death, myocardial infarction, stroke, hospitalization for heart failure or bleeding. Procedure-related complications included haematoma, tamponade, and pacemaker implantation within 30 days post-ablation. Codes provided in the Supplementary material online, Table S1.

Procedure characteristics of interest

Ablation procedure characteristics included ablation strategy (PVI or PVI plus), energy source (RFA or Cryo), cases of cavotricuspid isthmus (CTI) block, procedure duration, ablation duration, X-ray dose and duration, and anaesthesia type (general anaesthesia (GA) or conscious sedation (CS)). The five variables used to define PVI plus were one or more of the following registered in addition to PVI: roofline, mitral linear ablation, posterior wall isolation (PWI), complex fractionated atrial electrograms (CFAE), or ganglionated plexi ablation (GP). If the patient received a combination of two or more of the variables, we categorized them as ‘combined’. Procedure and medication codes were used to define exposure, outcomes, procedure characteristics, and comorbidities, as detailed in Supplementary material online, Table S1.

Patient characteristics, comorbidities, and pharmacotherapy variables of interest

Patient characteristics including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), LVEF, and LA size were identified and presented as means with standard deviations or medians with IQR. LA size was assessed using the most recent echocardiography (within 3 months before ablation) and measured as left atrial volume index (mL/m²) and categorized as normal (≤34 mL/m²), moderately enlarged (35–48 mL/m²), or severely enlarged (>48 mL/m²).

Comorbidities were identified based on hospitalization diagnosis until five years before the date of inclusion, and included heart failure (HF), ischaemic heart disease (IHD), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), ischaemic stroke, diabetes mellitus (DM), chronic kidney disease (CKD), and hypertension. Diabetes was defined from use of any diabetes drug, and hypertension was defined from use of two or more antihypertensive medications.26 The diagnoses and ATC codes for comorbidities and medication use are listed in Supplementary material online, Table S1.

CHA2DS2-VASc score was calculated from diagnosis of HF, hypertension, age ≥75, DM, prior stroke or transient ischaemic attack, vascular disease, age 65 to 74, female, presented as medians with IQR.

Medicine use at baseline was identified until 180 days before date of inclusion and included class IC, and class III antiarrhythmic medicine, digoxin, oral anticoagulant, non-loop diuretics, loop diuretics, beta-blockers, calcium channel inhibitors, and renin-angiotensin system inhibitors27 (see Supplementary material online, Table S1).

Statistical analyses

Baseline characteristics were presented as medians with interquartile range [IQR] or means with standard deviations (SD).

Differences between the two groups in procedure data including procedure duration, ablation duration, x-ray dose and duration, cases of CTI block, and anaesthesia type (GA or CS) were descriptively examined and presented by medians with IQR or means with SD. The temporal trend in the use of PVI and PVI plus for CA in Denmark from 2010–2020 was analysed, along with the distribution of the defined PVI plus-variables were examined.

One-year and five-year AF recurrence risks were estimated using Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence curves, accounting for competing death risk and censoring for emigration. Events within the 90 days post-ablation ‘blanking-period’ were excluded in the analysis. The distribution of first reached events in the composite AF recurrence endpoint was analysed at both time points. Crude rates of re-ablations in both groups were calculated.

Multivariable Cox proportional analysis for the primary endpoint was performed and presented as hazard ratios (HR) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals [95% CI]. The model was adjusted for clinically important characteristics including age, sex, BMI, LA size, LVEF, and co-morbidities such as HF, IHD, COPD, DM, CKD, and hypertension. The analysis was also adjusted for procedural year, diagnosis-to-ablation-time (DAT), anaesthesia type (GA or CS), and energy source (RFA or Cryo).

Subgroup analyses were performed for patients with severely enlarged atria, prior AAD use, and a known heart failure diagnosis prior to the ablation. Interaction analyses between ablation strategy, age, sex, energy source and LA size were applied. The impact of missing data was assessed through a sensitivity analysis of the exposure of interest.

Major adverse cardiac events and procedure-related complications within 30 days post-ablation were observed and presented as means with SD.

All data management and statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software [R version 4.2.1.28]

Ethics

In Denmark register-based studies that are conducted for the sole purpose of statistics and scientific research do not require ethical approval or informed consent by law.29 However, the study was approved by the data responsible institute (Central Region of Denmark) in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Additionally, the study was approved by The Danish Clinical Quality Program—National Clinical Registries (RKKP) that administers The Danish National Ablation registry (Approval number: Ablation-2022-12-12).

Results

Study cohort

From 13 412 patients undergoing first-time AF ablation between 2010 and 2020 in the Danish National Ablation Database, 4916 met inclusion criteria of persistent AF and age ≥18 years. After excluding 772 patients with missing data on ablation strategy, vital status, or emigration, the final study population included 4144 patients, of whom 3417 (83%) received PVI and 727 (17%) received PVI plus. The study selection process is illustrated in Figure 1. The groups were comparable regarding; sex [predominantly male (77%)], age (median [IQR] 63.8 [56.8, 69.5] years and 64.2 [58.2, 70] years, P-value 0.098), CHA2DS2-VASc scores (median 2), BMI (mean 28), mean LVEF (>50%), comorbidities, and antiarrhythmic drug use. However, amiodarone use was slightly higher in the PVI group (32% vs. 28%, P = 0.019) and dronedarone use in the PVI plus group (6% vs. 2%, P < 0.001). The PVI plus group had a longer diagnosis-to-ablation time (median 3.86 vs. 1.90 years) and higher prevalence of severely enlarged left atria (14.8% vs. 9.8%, P < 0.001) (Table 1). During the study period of 11 years a total of 450 (11%) patients died, and 21 emigrated and were lost to follow-up.

Temporal trends for the utilization of PVI and PVI plus

Figure 2 illustrates the temporal trends in the use of PVI and PVI plus for peAF patients in Denmark between 2010 and 2020. In 2010, over half of peAF patients underwent PVI plus. However, absolute number were small (PVI: 11, PVI plus: 15). We observed steadily decline of the use of PVI plus in both absolute numbers and proportion of the procedures until 2015, after which it stabilized at 10–15% for the remainder of the study period. Moreover, we observed a decline in total number of procedures performed from 2019 to 2020.

AF recurrence

At one-year follow-up the cumulative incidence of recurrent AF was lower in the PVI group compared to the PVI plus group (46% vs. 54%), with a similar trend observed at five years (68% vs. 77%) (Figure 3). Figures 4 illustrate the distribution of first reached events in the combined endpoint at one- and five-year follow-up. For both groups the most prevalent first reached event was hospital admissions related to AF or AFL (39% and 43% for the PVI group, 43% and 48% for the PVI plus group). The incidence of electric cardioversion and re-ablation remained stable from one to five years and was similar across groups. The use of AAD at one year was around 27% in both groups, decreasing slightly to approximately 22% at five years.

Crude rates of first-time re-ablation during the study period were 40.8% (1395 cases) for the PVI group and 41.9% (305 cases) for the PVI plus group.

The relative hazard ratios (HRs) for recurrent AF were analysed using multivariable Cox proportional hazards analysis. The PVI plus group showed similar outcomes to the PVI group, with an HR of 1.05 (95% CI: 0.94–1.18) (Figure 5). Factors associated with a significantly higher risk of AF recurrence included moderately and severely enlarged left atrium (HR: 1.35 and 1.18), obesity (HR: 1.16), a diagnosis-to-ablation time of more than 2 years (HR: 1.39) and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (HR: 1.45).

Temporal trends in AF recurrence

Analysis across procedural years revealed a proportional decline in recurrence rates for both strategies: 78% vs. 84% (PVI vs. PVI plus) in 2010–2013, 68% vs. 75% in 2014–2017, and 54% vs. 59% in 2018–2020, with no significant differences between the groups (Figure 6).

Subgroup and interaction analysis

Among patients with severely enlarged left atria (PVI: 322 (10%); PVI plus: 95 (15%)), the HR for PVI plus compared to PVI was 1.41 (95% CI: 1.05–1.89). Subgroup analyses of patients with prior AAD use (HR 1.12, 95% CI: 0.95–1.32) and those with a prior HF diagnosis (HR 1.03, 95% CI: 0.83–1.29) revealed no significant differences compared to the main analysis. Furthermore, patients with prior AAD (45% vs. 41%) and known HF (26% vs. 25%) were equally distributed between ablation strategy groups (see Supplementary material online, Table S2).

Interaction analyses showed no significant interactions between ablation strategy and age, sex, or energy source (RFA vs. Cryo). Missing data were low, and sensitivity analyses confirmed that missing data, including LA size, did not affect recurrence rates (see Supplementary material online, Table S3).

Discussion

In this nationwide study from Denmark, covering the period from 2010 to 2020 and including more than 4000 patients with persistent AF, we observed notable differences in patients characteristics and trends between the two ablation strategies, PVI and PVI plus. First, PVI plus was linked to signs of more advanced disease, including longer diagnosis-to-ablation times and larger left atria. Second, the use of PVI plus declined over time. Third, both groups experienced a proportional decline in recurrence rates over the study period, and PVI plus patients had comparable AF recurrence outcomes to PVI patients. Fourth, crude rates of re-ablations were similar for PVI and PVI plus strategy. Finally, complication rates were low and comparable across both strategies.

PVI plus strategy was in our study more often chosen for patients with longer DAT and enlarged arias. Patient subgroups with potentially higher risk of recurrence including those with prior AAD use and known heart failure,30–33 where equally distribution between the groups, suggesting that these factors were not necessarily selection criteria for PVI plus. Results from the EHRA survey 2015 regarding practice for ablation of peAF,34 showed that PVI alone was the primary choice in most centres for first-time ablation, which aligns with the findings of our study. The use of additional techniques showed a wide variation across centres, and success rates were modest and long-term outcome were unknown.

Recent findings from the EU-PORIA registry, which evaluated pulsed-field ablation in persistent AF, also showed no clear advantage of PVI-plus strategies compared with PVI alone, whereas atrial tachycardia recurrences were more common with extended ablation and complication rates remained low.35 Although based on a different energy modality, these observations are broadly comparable to our results and suggest that more extensive ablation does not consistently yield better long-term outcomes in persistent AF.

The REAL-AF registry study36 examined trends in persistent AF ablation from 2021 to 2022 across 25 U.S. centres with 51 high-case volume operators. The study included 1036 patients. Compared to our cohort, their population included more women—particularly in the PVI-plus group (40.7% vs. 22.3%)—and patients in the PVI plus group were older with higher CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores, whereas our groups were more balanced on these patient characteristics. Contrary to our findings, PVI plus was the most common strategy (61.5%), yet outcomes regarding AF recurrence, AAD use at 3 months, and procedural complications were comparable between groups, in line with our results.

The definition of ‘PVI-plus’ encompasses various additional strategies (roof or mitral isthmus lines, posterior wall isolation, CFAE, ganglionated plexi ablation), and conduction block may not always have been achieved or verified. This limitation should be acknowledged, as incomplete lines could theoretically be proarrhythmic. For transparency, we added Supplementary material online, Figure S2, illustrating the combinations of ‘plus’ components in our cohort. In adjusted analyses, however, we found no significant difference in AF recurrence between PVI and PVI-plus (HR 1.05, 95% CI 0.94–1.18), suggesting such effects were not evident in our data. Likewise, the PARTY-PVI trial,37 although in a selected redo population with a mix of AF types, reported no meaningful differences between ‘plus’ strategies, supporting that the specific combination of additional ablation lines may have limited impact.

Complications and MACE within 30 days post-ablation were generally low in our cohort supporting that both PVI and PVI plus are safe approaches. Incidence of pacemaker implantations was twice as high in the PVI plus group; however, this finding is limited by small absolute numbers, nonetheless highlighting a key consideration when selecting a more extensive ablation strategy.

DAT has been shown to have an impact on success rate post-ablation.38–41 Median DAT in our study was above 2 years (2.16 [IQR: 0.65, 6.04]. Although we did not find a difference in the relative risk of AF recurrence between PVI and PVI plus, the absolute risk was 10% higher at both one- and five-year follow-up for the PVI plus group. We observed both a longer DAT (median 3.85 years vs. 1.90 years), and higher proportion of severely enlarged LA (14.8% vs. 9.8%) in the PVI plus group, suggesting more advanced disease and potentially contributing to this observed difference in absolute risk. An earlier intervention, even in patients with peAF, may help prevent such progression and warrants further investigation.

Evidence regarding ablation strategies for peAF remains conflicting with several studies showing both positive outcomes when adding PVI plus including VENUS(2020), ERASE-AF(2022) and EARNEST-PVI(2023),11,11,42) as well as a neutral meta-analysis published in 202243 and negative trials (STAR AF II (2015), DECAAF-II (2022) and CAPLA(2023).9,10,12 Lacking consistent findings across multiple studies, could suggest the need to explore alternative strategies to optimize patient care. As stated in the EHRA Consensus paper 202415 focusing on comprehensively modifying known risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes, obesity, and sleep apnoea might be other viable ways to go.44,45

Whether newer ablation modalities such as pulsed field ablation, improved durability of lesions, and other strategies (i.e. imaging) to improve the outcome of PVI plus vs. PVI alone in peAF patients remains to be further explored in randomized controlled trials.

Strengths and limitations

Data was collected nationwide from all seven ablation centres, potentially minimizing selection bias. The study design and the mandatory nationwide registries minimize the loss to follow-up. Only 21 patients emigrated during the study period and were lost to follow-up. They were censored for in the Aalen-Johansen estimator. In addition the Danish National Ablation Registry21 has been validated with a positive predictive value above 90%. However, the registry does not provide information on procedural decision-making, such as whether PVI plus was chosen based on substrate analysis, induced AT, operator preference, or specific patient characteristics. Therefore, the reasons for performing PVI plus in individual cases remain unknown, which limits the ability to assess its potential benefit in higher-risk patients.

We acknowledge that our composite definition of arrhythmia recurrence does not provide definitive proof of AF, as no ECG documentation was available. Nevertheless, the included events represent clinically relevant manifestations that required professional medical attention, and therefore we believe that it remains relevant for assessing patient outcomes. This approach may underestimation the true recurrence rate, as asymptomatic episodes would not be captured in this study. However, events were evenly distributed between the two groups at both one- and five-year follow-up, suggesting that any potential underestimation would likely be comparable across groups.

There is a potential for residual confounding, as we did not have information on known risk factors associated with AF recurrence post ablation such as patients’ status of tobacco46 and alcohol consumption,47,48 as well as diagnosis of obstructive sleep apnoea.49–51

Clinical perspectives

Our study offers insight into the evolving practices and approaches to ablation for peAF in Denmark over time by employing a large, unselected, real-world population. During the study period technology has emerged with more advanced techniques, catheters, energy sources, as well as operator expertise and knowledge have grown, potentially accounting for the overall relative decline in AF recurrences regardless of ablation strategy applied.

Comparing PVI and PVI plus strategy in an observational study is challenging, as differences in patient selection can lead to imbalances between groups. Operator-dependent factors may influence the choice of ablation strategy, with sicker patients or those with more advanced electrical remodelling more likely to undergo PVI plus. Consequently, outcomes may reflect underlying patient selection rather than procedural efficacy, explaining the higher absolute risk despite the absence of significant differences in the adjusted hazard ratios. Enhancing our understanding of the selection process could pave the way for more personalized treatment strategies, allowing for a tailored approach that optimizes outcomes for each patient.

Conclusion

Strategies to reduce AF recurrences after first-time ablation for persistent AF remain challenging. In this nationwide cohort study, PVI plus was linked to signs of more advanced disease and declined in use over time. Both PVI and PVI plus strategies showed a proportional decline in recurrence rates over time, with comparable AF recurrence outcomes and low complication rates. In this analysis, PVI plus did not provide any added benefit compared to the PVI-only strategy. Patient and operator factors may influence the choice of ablation strategy and outcome.

Dr. Riis-Vestergaard is dedicated to translating population-level data into meaningful improvements in clinical electrophysiology and patient care.

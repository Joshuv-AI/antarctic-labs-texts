Well-posedness of rough 2D Euler equation with bounded vorticity
L. Roveri 1, F. Triggiano 2

Abstract
. We consider the 2D Euler equation with bounded initial vorticity and perturbed by rough transport noise. We show that there exists a unique solution, which coincides with the starting condition advected by the Lagrangian flow. Moreover, the stability of the solution map with respect to the initial vorticity and the rough perturbation yields a Wong-Zakai result for fractional Brownian driving paths.



1.
Introduction

In the last two decades, there has been a significant development in the analysis of fluid dynamics equations perturbed by a transport type noise due to the emergence of the regularization by noise phenomena, namely the ability to restore well-posedness
[
FGP10
,
CM23
,
GL23
,
FL21
]
or the appearance of a spare dissipative effect under a suitable scaling limit
[
FGL21
,
BL24
]
.

While all the above mentioned works are restricted to the case of Brownian motion, similar results have been identified even with less regular noise such as fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H<\frac{1}{2}

[
CG16
,
Cat16
,
GHM23
]
. Since the latter is not a semimartingale, classical stochastic calculus results are unavailable, and the time integral must be built in the rough integral sense. This suggests that rough path theory
[
Lyo98
,
FH20
,
FV10
]
may offer the right tools to treat fluid dynamics equations perturbed by transport type noise with driving signals very irregular in time
[
Hai11
,
GT10
]
.

Lately, the introduction of the "unbounded rough drivers" formalism (
[
BG17
,
Dey+19
]
) has allowed the recovery of classical tools, such as the Grönwall lemma, in the rough path setting. This approach has been successfully applied to obtain well-posedness results and energy estimates for Navier-Stokes and Euler equations perturbed by more general noises
[
HLN19
,
HLN21
,
Cri+22
]
.

In this work, we exploit the unbounded rough drivers’ machinery to study the 2D Euler equation in vorticity form, perturbed by rough transport noise, in the Yudovich setting
[
Yud63
]
, namely with bounded starting condition:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\pt w(t,x)+u(t,x)\sg w(t,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sigma_{j}(x)\sg w(t,x)\dot{Z}^{j}_{t},\\
w(0,x)=w_{0}(x),\quad u(t,\cdot)=K_{BS}*w(t,\cdot),\end{cases}

where
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
,
w_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
\sigma_{j}:\mathbb{T}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
are sufficiently smooth and divergence-free vector fields,
Z^{j}
are finite
p
-variation paths with
p\in[2,3)
, and
K_{BS}
denotes the Biot-Savart kernel.
In particular, we show that there exists a unique weak solution to
Eq.

1.1
and for the corresponding Lagrangian equation. Moreover, we also obtain that the solution map is continuous with respect to the initial condition, the
\ sigma_{j}
and the driver.

From a technical point of view, the main novelties regard the uniqueness proof for both the Lagrangian RDE with log-Lipschitz drift and the 2D Euler equation.
For the former, we prove the solution (in the sense of controlled rough paths, see
[
Gub04
]
) is unique by applying a localization procedure together with a rough Grönwall Lemma. For the latter, we obtain uniqueness by showing that any solution is a Lagrangian solution, namely it is advected by the associated flow. This claim is proved by combining Dobrushin’s strategy from
[
Dob79
]
, i.e. identifying the rough equation satisfied by any observable of the inverse flow map, with a doubling variable scheme.

We conclude the introduction with the notion of solution and the main result. The rest of the paper is organized as follows:
Section

2
contains the basics of the unbounded rough drivers and some results on
p
-variation spaces; in
Section

3
we prove that there exists a solution to 2D Euler equation;
Section

4
is about the well-posedness results for the Lagrangian RDE and, at last, in
Section

5
we conclude the uniqueness for the 2D Euler equation and report a Wong-Zakai result.

Main results

Since the driving signal in

This way, we obtain

\displaystyle w_{s,t}={}

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{t}u_{r}\sg w_{r}dr+\int_{s}^{t}\sigma_{j}\sg w_{r}dZ^{j}_{r}

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{t}u_{r}\sg w_{r}dr+\sigma_{j}\sg w_{s}Z^{j}_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}(\sigma_{j}\sg)\int_{s}^{r}u_{r_{1}}\sg w_{r_{1}}dr_{1}dZ^{j}_{r}

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Localized variation spaces

Since it emerges naturally in rough path theory, let us recall what we mean by the term “control”.
For a deeper description of its properties, we refer e.g. to
[
FV10
]
.

From now on, we are going to denote

\Delta_{T}\coloneqq\{(s,t)|0\leq s\leq t\leq T\},\qquad\Delta_{T}^{2}\coloneqq\{(s,r,t)|0\leq s\leq r\leq t\leq T\},

and, for two-index functions
g:\Delta_{T}\to V
,
(s,r,t)\in\Delta_{T}^{2}
,

\delta g_{s,r,t}\coloneqq g(s,t)-g(s,r)-g(r,t).

In addition, throughout our discussion, we are going to use the notation
a\lesssim b
whenever there is an inequality up to a (positive) constant, i.e. there exists some
C>0
such that
a\leq Cb
. When the constant depends on a parameter,
C=C(par)
, we highlight that as follows:
a\lesssim_{par}b
.

Definition 2.1
.

A continuous function
\omega:\Delta_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a control if

\omega(s,s)=0
, for all
s\in[0,T]
;

\omega
is super-additive, i.e.
\omega(s,u)+\omega(u,t)\leq\omega(s,t)
for all
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{T}
.

Moreover, a control
\omega
is said to be strictly positive if, for all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
,

\omega(s,t)=0\implies s=t.

Another key concept is the
p
-variation of a path, which can be seen as a generalization of Hölder continuity.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a Banach space
V
,
p>0
and a continuous function
g:[0,T]\to V
, we say that
g
has finite
p
-variation,
g\in C^{p\textup{-var}}([0,T],V)
, if

\|g\|_{p}^{p}\coloneqq\sup_{\Pi\in\boldsymbol{\Pi}([0,T])}\sum_{[s,t]\in\Pi}|g_{t}-g_{s}|^{p}<\infty,

where
\boldsymbol{\Pi}([0,T])
is the set of partitions of
[0,T]
,
g_{t}\coloneqq g(t)
and
|\cdot|=\|\cdot\|_{V}
(we are going to adopt this notation for a norm whenever the space
V
is clear from the context).

In the case of a two-index function
g:\Delta_{T}\to V
, the above notation can be adapted by changing
|g_{t}-g_{s}|
into
|g_{s,t}|
, where
g_{s,t}\coloneqq g(s,t)
.

In particular, we are going to extensively use partitions whose mesh is bounded from above by some quantity.
To this aim, we introduce the concept of
localized
p
-variation
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a Banach space
V
,
p>0
, a control
\overline{\omega}
and
L>0
, we denote by
C^{p\textup{-var},2}_{\overline{\omega},L}(V)
the space of
2
-index continuous functions
g:\Delta_{T}\to V

such that

(2.1)

\|g\|_{p,(\overline{\omega},L),[0,T]}^{p}\coloneqq\sup_{\Pi\in\boldsymbol{\Pi}(\overline{\omega},L)}\sum_{[s,t]\in\Pi}|g_{s,t}|^{p}<\infty,

where
\boldsymbol{\Pi}(\overline{\omega},L)
denotes the space of partitions
\Pi
of
[0,T]
such that
\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq L
for all
[s,t]\in\Pi
.

Analogously,
C^{p\textup{-var}}_{\overline{\omega},L}(V)
denotes the space of
1
-index continuous functions
f:[0,T]\to V
such that
Eq.

2.1
holds, with
g_{s,t}\coloneqq|g_{t}-g_{s}|
.

By slightly modifying some results in
[
FV10
]
(see, e.g., Proposition 5.8 and Proposition 5.10), we obtain the following Proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
g\in C^{p\textup{-var},2}_{\overline{\omega},L}(V)
or
g\in C^{p\textup{-var}}_{\overline{\omega},L}(V)
. Then, the function

\omega_{g}:\Delta_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
defined by

\omega^{loc}_{g}(s,t)=\|g\|_{p,(\overline{\omega},L),[s,t]}^{p}

is a control. In addition,
\omega^{loc}_{g}
is the smallest control such that

(2.2)

|g_{s,t}|\leq\omega^{loc}_{g}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{p}}\text{ for all }(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}\text{ such that }\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq L.

Namely, if
\omega
is a control which satisfies
Eq.

2.2
, then

\omega^{loc}_{g}(u,v)\leq\omega(u,v),

for all
(u,v)\in\Delta_{T}
.

We conclude this introduction to localized
p
-variation spaces by showing a result that holds only for
1
-index functions. Let us recall that
p\geq 1
is the only interesting case for
1
-index functions (see
[
FV10
, Proposition 5.2]
).

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
g\in C^{p\textup{-var}}_{\overline{\omega},L}(V)
. Then, the following hold:

1.

C^{p\textup{-var}}_{\overline{\omega},L}=C^{p\textup{-var}}
, i.e. if
g
has finite localized
p
-variation with respect to
(\overline{\omega},L)
, then it has finite
p
-variation. Moreover, the following inequalities hold

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{p,(\overline{\omega},L),[s,t]}^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\|g\|_{p,(\overline{\omega},\frac{L}{2}),[s,t]}^{p}\quad\forall(s,t)\in\Delta_{T};

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{p,[0,T]}^{p}\lesssim\Big(\frac{L}{\overline{\omega}(0,T)}\Big)^{p-1}\|g\|_{p,[0,T],(\overline{\omega},L)}^{p}.

2.

If
\overline{\omega}
is a strictly positive control, then

\lim_{L\to 0}\|g\|_{q,(\overline{\omega},L),[s,t]}=0,

for all
q>p\geq 1
,
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
.

Proof.

1.

Let us prove
Eq.

2.3
. The second inequality,
Eq.

2.4
, can be easily obtained by properly iterating the same strategy.

Given
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
, consider a partition coherent with the localization, namely
P=\{s\eqqcolon t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{N}\coloneqq t\}\in\boldsymbol{\Pi}(\overline{\omega},L)
.

Then, the continuity of
\overline{\omega}
implies that we can find
s_{i}\in(t_{i},t_{i+1})
such that
\overline{\omega}(t_{i},s_{i})\leq\frac{L}{2}
and
\overline{\omega}(s_{i},t_{i+1})\leq\frac{L}{2}
, for all
i=0,\dots,N-1
. Therefore,
Q=P\cup\{s_{i}\}_{i}
is a partition of
[s,t]
that satisfies the localization
(\overline{\omega},\frac{L}{2})
.
At last, the additivity of
1
-index functions’ variation, i.e.
g_{s,t}=g_{s,u}+g_{u,t}
, implies that

\sum_{t_{i}\in P}|g_{t_{i},t_{i+1}}|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\sum_{r_{i}\in Q}|g_{r_{i},r_{i+1}}|^{p}.

The obtained inequality proves the statement.

2.

Notice that

\sum_{[s,t]\in P}|g_{s,t}|^{q}\leq\|g\|_{p}^{p}\sup_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{T},\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq L}|g_{s,t}|^{q-p},

for any partition
\Pi\in\boldsymbol{\Pi}(\overline{\omega},L)
.

Now, let us prove by contradiction that
\lim_{L\to 0}h(L)=0
, where

h(L)=\sup_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{T},\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq L}|g_{s,t}|.

Suppose that
\lim_{L\to 0}h(L)=\delta>0
. Then for all
n\in N
, there exists
(s_{n},t_{n})\in\Delta_{T}
such that
\overline{\omega}(s_{n},t_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}
and
|g_{s_{n},t_{n}}|\geq\frac{\delta}{2}
. Therefore, up to taking a subsequence, there exists
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
such that

(s_{n},t_{n})\to(s,t),\quad\overline{\omega}(s_{n},t_{n})\to\overline{\omega}(s,t),\quad|g_{s,t}|\geq\frac{\delta}{3.12}.

So, we find a contradiction because
\overline{\omega}(s,t)=0
and
\overline{\omega}
is strictly positive.

∎

Remark 2.6
.

The assumptions
q>p
and
\overline{\omega}
being strictly positive cannot be weakened. Indeed,
g(t)=\sqrt{t}
, for
t\in[0,1]
, shows that the third claim is not true for
q=p
. Instead,
\overline{\omega}\equiv 0
indicates that the same claim does not hold without requiring strict positiveness.

2.2.
Localized controlled rough paths

In this section, we recall the definition of rough paths.
We also introduce a generalized version of controlled rough paths as defined in
[
Gub04
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in[2,3)
. A
p
-rough path is a pair

\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})\in C^{p\textup{-var}}(\mathbb{R}^{M})\times C^{\frac{p}{2}\textup{-var},2}(\mathbb{R}^{M\times M})

that satisfies the Chen’s relation, namely

\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}-\mathbb{Z}_{s,u}-\mathbb{Z}_{u,t}=Z_{s,u}\otimes Z_{u,t},\quad\forall(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{T}.

Moreover, a
p
-rough path
\mathbf{Z}
is said geometric if there exists a sequence
\{Z^{n}\}_{n}\subset C^{\infty}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{M})
such that

\mathbf{Z}^{n}_{s,t}\coloneqq(Z^{n}_{s,t},\int_{s}^{t}Z^{n}_{s,r}\otimes dZ^{n}_{r})
converges to
\mathbf{Z}
with respect to the product topology.

Remark 2.8
.

For any
p
-rough path there exists a control
\omega_{Z}
such that

|Z_{s,t}|^{p}\leq\omega_{Z}(s,t),\quad|\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\omega_{Z}(s,t)
.

2.3.
Unbounded rough drivers and
smoothing operators

In order to rewrite our equation (
1.1
) in a more treatable form, we rewrite the integral against
Z
as a Taylor expansion up to order two. In particular, this decomposition produces some sort of “operator-valued” rough path, i.e. linear unbounded operators enjoying rough path properties, and an additional remainder.

Let
\{E^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of Banach spaces such that there is a continuous embedding
E^{m}\subseteq E^{n}
for all
m\geq n
.
From now on, we denote their dual spaces by
E^{-n}
.

The “operator-valued” rough path can be formally defined as follows.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
p\in[2,3)
. A continuous unbounded
p
-rough driver on the scales
\{E^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a pair
(A^{1},A^{2})
of
2
-index maps such that, for all
s,t\in\Delta_{T}
,

\displaystyle A^{1}_{s,t}\in L(E^{-n},E^{-n-1})\text{ for }n\in\{0,1,2\},

\displaystyle A^{2}_{s,t}\in L(E^{-n},E^{-n-2})\text{ for }n\in\{0,1\}.

Moreover, Chen’s relation holds

(2.7)

A^{1}_{s,t}=A^{1}_{s,r}+A^{1}_{r,t}\ \quad A^{2}_{s,t}=A^{2}_{s,r}+A^{2}_{r,t}+A^{1}_{r,t}A^{1}_{s,r},\qquad\forall(s,r,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{T}

and there exists a control
\omega_{A}
such that

(2.8)

\|A^{1}_{s,t}\|_{L(E^{-n},E^{-n-1})}\leq\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad\|A^{2}_{s,t}\|_{L(E^{-n},E^{-n-2})}\leq\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{p}}.

We also need to introduce the concept of smoothing operators. They are crucial for estimating the time regularity of the remainder.

Definition 2.12
.

A family of operators
(J^{\eta})_{\eta\in[0,1]}
is said to be a family of smoothing operators on the scale
\{E^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
if the following inequalities hold

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|J^{\eta}\psi\|_{E^{n+m}}\lesssim\eta^{-m}\|\psi\|_{E^{n}},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|(I-J^{\eta})\psi\|_{E^{n}}\lesssim\eta^{m}\|\psi\|_{E^{n+m}},

for all
\psi\in E^{n+m}
and

.

3.
Existence of solutions

In this section, we aim to show the existence of solutions in the sense of
Definition

1.2
.

3.1.
A priori estimates

We start with a priori estimates on solutions and their remainders. To prove them, we use the following smoothing operators.

Lemma 3.1
.

Consider a (symmetric) mollifier
\rho
with support on the ball of radius equal to 1. Then,
(J^{\eta})_{\eta\in[0,1]}
, defined by
J^{\eta}\psi=\rho_{\eta}\ast\psi
, is a family of smoothing operators on the scale
W^{n,1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
in the sense of
Definition

2.12
.

The first estimate is on the remainder
w^{\natural}
, defined in
Definition

1.2
. We denote by
\omega_{\natural}\coloneqq\omega_{w^{\natural}}^{\text{loc}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
w
be a solution of the problem, in the sense of
Definition

1.2
. Then there exists
\overline{L}>0
such that

(3.1)

\omega_{\natural}(s,t)\lesssim_{p}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{\frac{p}{3}}\omega_{A}(s,t)+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{\frac{2p}{3}}|t-s|^{\frac{p}{3}}\left(\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{3}}+\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{3}}\right),

for all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
s.t.
\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq L
and
\omega_{A}(s,t)\leq\overline{L}
.

Proof.

Let
(s,r,t)\in\Delta_{T}^{2}
such that
\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq L
. It follows from
Eq.

1.2
that, for all
\psi\in W^{3,1}
,

\displaystyle\delta w^{\natural}_{s,r,t}(\psi)={}

\displaystyle-w_{s}\left(\left[A^{1,*}_{s,t}+A^{2,*}_{s,t}\right]\psi\right)+w_{s}\left(\left[A^{1,*}_{s,r}+A^{2,*}_{s,r}\right]\psi\right)

\displaystyle+w_{r}\left(\left[A^{1,*}_{r,t}+A^{2,*}_{r,t}\right]\psi\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle w_{s,r}\left(A^{2,*}_{r,t}\psi\right)+\left(w_{s,r}-w_{s}A^{1,*}_{s,r}\right)\left(A^{1,*}_{r,t}\psi\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle w_{s,r}\left(A^{2,*}_{r,t}\psi\right)+w^{\dagger}_{s,r}\left(A^{1,*}_{r,t}\psi\right),

where in particular we have exploited
Eq.

2.7
and defined

(3.2)

w^{\dagger}_{s,t}\coloneqq w_{s,t}-A^{1}_{s,t}w_{s}=\mu_{s,t}+A^{2}_{s,t}w_{s}+w^{\natural}_{s,t}.

Let now
J^{\eta}
be a smoothing operator, as defined in
Lemma

3.1
: we are going to apply it to
\psi
in order to separately analyze a rougher and a smoother part of its duality product against
\delta w^{\natural}
. Starting with the former,

\displaystyle|\delta w^{\natural}_{s,r,t}\left((I-J^{\eta})\psi\right)|\leq{}

\displaystyle\left|w_{s,r}\left(A^{2,*}_{r,t}(I-J^{\eta})\psi\right)\right|+\left|w^{\dagger}_{s,r}\left(A^{1,*}_{r,t}(I-J^{\eta})\psi\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\Big[\|A^{2,*}_{r,t}(I-J^{\eta})\psi\|_{L^{1}}+\|A^{1,*}_{r,t}(I-J^{\eta})\psi\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle+\|A^{1,*}_{s,r}A^{1,*}_{r,t}(I-J^{\eta})\psi\|_{L^{1}}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\Big[\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{p}}\|(I-J^{\eta})\psi\|_{W^{2,1}}

\displaystyle+\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|(I-J^{\eta})\psi\|_{W^{1,1}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\Big[\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{p}}\eta+\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{p}}\eta^{2}\Big]\|\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}.

As for the smoothed part,

\displaystyle|\delta w^{\natural}_{s,r,t}\left(J^{\eta}\psi\right)|\leq{}

\displaystyle\left|w_{s,r}\left(A^{2,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\right)\right|+\left|w^{\dagger}_{s,r}\left(A^{1,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{s,r}\left(A^{2,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\right)\right|+\left|w_{s}\left(A^{1,*}_{s,r}A^{2,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\right)\right|+\left|w_{s}\left(A^{2,*}_{s,r}A^{2,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|w^{\natural}_{s,r}\left(A^{2,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\right)\right|+\left|\mu_{s,r}\left(A^{1,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\right)\right|+\left|w_{s}\left(A^{2,*}_{s,r}A^{1,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|w^{\natural}_{s,r}\left(A^{1,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{2}\|A^{2,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{1,1}}|t-s|+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|A^{1,*}_{s,r}A^{2,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|A^{2,*}_{s,r}A^{2,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\|_{L^{1}}+\|w^{\natural}_{s,r}\|_{W^{-3,1}}\|A^{2,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}

\displaystyle+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{2}\|A^{1,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{1,1}}|t-s|+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|A^{2,*}_{s,r}A^{1,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle+\|w^{\natural}_{s,r}\|_{W^{-3,1}}\|A^{1,*}_{r,t}J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}.

Due to the regularity properties of both the unbounded rough drivers and the smoothing operators, we can rewrite the estimate as

\displaystyle|\delta w^{\natural}_{s,r,t}(J^{\eta}\psi)|\leq{}

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{2}\|J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{p}}|t-s|+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{3}{p}}

\displaystyle+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{4,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{4}{p}}+\omega_{\natural}(s,t)^{\frac{3}{p}}\|J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{5,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{2}\|J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{2,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{p}}|t-s|+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{3}{p}}

\displaystyle+\omega_{\natural}(s,t)^{\frac{3}{p}}\|J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{4,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{2}\|\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{p}}|t-s|+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{3}{p}}

\displaystyle+\eta^{-1}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{4}{p}}+\eta^{-2}\omega_{\natural}(s,t)^{\frac{3}{p}}\|\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{2}\|\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{p}}|t-s|+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{3}{p}}

\displaystyle+\eta^{-1}\omega_{\natural}(s,t)^{\frac{3}{p}}\|\psi\|_{W^{3,1}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Putting back together
(I-J^{\eta})\psi
and
J^{\eta}\psi
, we can set
\eta=\omega_{A}(s,t)^{1/p}\lambda
for some
\lambda>0
constant. This yields

\|\delta w^{\natural}_{s,r,t}\|_{W^{-3,1}}\lesssim{}(\lambda^{-1}+1+\lambda+\lambda^{2})\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{3}{p}}\\
+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{2}\left(\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{p}}\right)|t-s|+(\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-1})\omega_{\natural}(s,t)^{\frac{3}{p}}.

Now,
Corollary

B.2
implies that

\omega_{\natural}(s,t)\leq C_{p}\Big[(\lambda^{-1}+1+\lambda+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{p}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{\frac{p}{3}}\omega_{A}(s,t)\\
+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{\frac{2p}{3}}\left(\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{3}}+\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{3}}\right)|t-s|^{\frac{p}{3}}+(\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-1})^{\frac{p}{3}}\omega_{\natural}(s,t)\Big].

It is now sufficient to choose
\lambda
in a way such that
C_{p}(\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-1})^{p/3}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\bar{L}>0
such that
\eta\leq 1
to obtain the thesis.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
w
be a solution of the problem, in the sense of

. Then
w\in C^{p\textup{-var}}_{T}W^{-1,1}

\omega_{w}(s,t)\lesssim_{p}\left(1+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\right)^{2p}\left(|t-s|^{p}+\omega_{A}(s,t)+\omega_{\natural}(s,t)\right),

Consider again a family of smoothing operators as in

. Given any

analyzing separately

\displaystyle|w_{s,t}(J^{\eta}\psi)|\leq{}

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{2}\|J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{1,1}}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|J^{\eta}\psi\|_{W^{1,1}}

3.2.
Existence

We discuss here how to prove our existence result.
The strategy outline is classic: first, we prove the existence of solutions with an additional viscous dissipative term.
Then, we prove the convergence of such solutions while letting the viscosity vanish, recovering a solution of the original problem, in the sense of Definition
1.2
.

The proof of the following lemma can be easily obtained by adapting the results in
[
Ben94
,
Yud63
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
w_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\subset C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a family of divergence-free vector fields and
Z\in C^{\infty}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{M})
. Then, there exists a unique solution
w\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
to the following equation

(3.4)

\begin{cases}\pt w+u\sg w-\nu\Delta w=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sigma_{j}\sg w\dot{Z}^{j},\\
w(0)=w_{0},\quad u=K_{BS}*w.\par\end{cases}

In particular, we have the estimate

(3.5)

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq C\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}},

where
C
is a positive constant that does not depend on the viscosity
\nu
.

Thanks to the above, we can start with a smooth approximation of both solution and noise and let them converge.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
w_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\subset C^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a family of divergence-free vector fields and
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathscr{C}^{p\textup{-var}}_{g}
. Then, there exists a solution to
Eq.

1.1
in the sense of
Definition

1.2
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{Z}
is a geometric rough path, there exists a sequence of smooth paths,
\{Z^{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
, whose canonical lifts converge to
\mathbf{Z}
in
\mathscr{C}^{p\textup{-var}}_{g}
.

Let
w^{N}
be the solution to
Eq.

3.4
with initial datum
w_{0}^{N}=\rho_{1/N}\ast w_{0}
, viscosity
\nu=N^{-1}
,
\sigma_{j}^{N}=\sigma_{j}\ast\rho_{1/N}
and (smooth) driving signal
Z^{N}
.
By iterating the equation, following the procedure already used in
Section

1.1
, we can see that
w^{N}
solves an equation of the form

(3.6)

w^{N}_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}\left[\frac{1}{N}\Delta w^{N}_{r}-u^{N}_{r}\sg w_{r}^{N}\right]dr+A^{N,1}_{s,t}w^{N}_{s}+A^{N,2}_{s,t}w^{N}_{s}+w^{N,\natural}_{s,t},

where

A^{N,1}_{s,t}w_{s}=\sigma^{N}_{j}\sg w_{s}Z^{N,j}_{s,t},\qquad A^{N,1}_{s,t}w_{s}=(\sigma^{N}_{j}\sg)(\sigma^{N}_{i}\sg)w_{s}\mathbb{Z}^{N,i,j}_{s,t}

and the remainder
w^{N,\natural}_{s,t}
is properly defined via a suitable adaptation of
Eq.

1.4
.

Adapting the proofs of
Lemma

3.2
and
Lemma

3.3
, one can easily obtain that, uniformly in
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.7)

\omega_{N,\natural}(s,t)\lesssim_{p,w_{0}}\omega_{A}(s,t)+|t-s|^{\frac{p}{3}}\big(\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{3}}+\omega_{A}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{3}}\big)

and

|w^{N}_{s,t}|_{W^{-1,1}}^{p}\lesssim_{p,w_{0}}|t-s|^{p}+\omega_{A}(s,t)+\omega_{\natural}(s,t)

for all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
s.t.
\omega_{A}(s,t)\leq L
.

At this point,
Lemma

A.1
allows to select a subsequence (still denoted by
w^{N}
for the sake of notation) converging to some
w\in C([0,T],W^{-1,1})
.
Testing
Eq.

3.6
against any
\phi\in W^{3,1}
, and passing to the limit
w^{N}\to w
and
\mathbf{Z}^{N}\to\mathbf{Z}
, we can see that such
w
is in fact a solution of
Eq.

1.1
.
In particular, the convergence of the nonlinear transport term is ensured by the fact that
w^{N}\to w
in
C([0,T],W^{-1,2})
implies
u^{N}\to u
in
C([0,T],L^{2})
.

The proof is then complete once we notice that the remainder, defined by
w^{\natural}_{s,t}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}w^{N,\natural}_{s,t}
, has finite
\frac{p}{3}
-variation with respect to a suitable localization thanks to the uniform bound
Eq.

3.7
.
∎

4.
Euler Lagrangian flows

This section is focused on the study of two Lagrangian equations associated with the flow of solutions to
Eq.

1.1
.
We are first going to prove that

\phi_{t}(x)=x+\int_{0}^{t}u(s,\phi_{s}(x))dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{j}(\phi_{r}(x))dZ^{j}_{r},

admits a solution, whenever
u
is a spatial log-Lipschitz drift like the one obtained by applying the Biot-Savart kernel to a bounded function. Afterward, we are going to show a uniqueness result for the non-local RDE

\phi_{t}(x)=x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}K_{BS}(\phi_{r}(x)-\phi_{r}(y))w_{0}(y)dydr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{j}(\phi_{r}(x))dZ^{j}_{r},

where
K_{BS}
is the Biot-Savart kernel and
w_{0}
is a function bounded in space and constant in time.

4.1.
Log-Lipschitz RDE

Consider a bounded, measurable function
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
that is log-Lipschitz in the spatial component, namely

|u(t,x)-u(t,y)|\leq L_{u}\gamma(|x-y|)\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{2},

where we set
L_{u}>0
and
\gamma(r)=r(1-\log r)\mathbb{1}_{(0,1/\operatorname{e})}(r)+(r+1/e)\mathbb{1}_{[1/e,\infty)}(r)
for all
r\geq 0
.
We prove that the RDE

(4.1)

\phi_{t}(x)=x+\int_{0}^{t}u(r,\phi_{r}(x))dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{j}(\phi_{r}(x))dZ^{j}_{r},

is well-posed by a limiting procedure based on the following a priori estimates.
Notice also that, in this section, we denote by
\overline{\sigma}
the functional
\overline{\sigma}:C(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L(\mathbb{R}^{M},C(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2}))
defined by
\overline{\sigma}(f)(z)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sigma_{j}(f(x))z_{j}
, up to extending the
\sigma_{j}
by periodicity. We will denote its Fréchet derivative by
D\overline{\sigma}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be bounded measurable,
\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
be
C^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
-vector fields,
\mathbf{Z}
be a
p
-rough path and
y\in C(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Given a solution
(Y,\overline{\sigma}(Y))\in\mathscr{D}^{p\textup{-var}}_{Z}(C(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2}))
to

Y_{t}=y+\int_{0}^{t}u(r,Y_{r})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\sigma}(Y_{r})d\mathbf{Z}_{r},

it holds that for
q\in(p,3)
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|Y\|_{q\textup{-var}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|R^{Y}\|_{q\textup{-var},(\overline{\omega},\mu_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2},

where
\overline{\omega}(s,t)=\omega_{Z}(s,t)+|t-s|^{p}
and
C_{1},C_{2},\mu_{0}
are positive constants depending only on
\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{3}}
,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}
,
q
and
\omega_{Z}(0,T)
.

Proof.

Let us stress that in this proof we consider controls associated with the
q
- and
q/2
-variations with respect to the localization
(\overline{\omega},\mu)
of several
1
- and
2
-index functions.

If
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
are such that
\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq\mu
, then

\displaystyle|R^{Y}_{s,t}|\leq{}

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{s}^{t}u(r,Y_{r})dr\Big|+\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\overline{\sigma}(Y_{r})d\mathbf{Z}_{r}-\overline{\sigma}(Y_{s})Z_{s,t}-D\overline{\sigma}(Y_{s})\overline{\sigma}(Y_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}\Big|

\displaystyle+|D\overline{\sigma}(Y_{s})\overline{\sigma}(Y_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}|

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}|t-s|+\omega^{loc}_{R^{\overline{\sigma}(Y)}}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{q}}\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\omega^{loc}_{\overline{\sigma}(Y)^{\prime}}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{q}}\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle+\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{1}}^{2}\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}|t-s|+\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{2}}\big(\omega^{loc}_{Y}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{q}}+\omega^{loc}_{R^{Y}}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{q}}\big)\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{2}}^{2}\omega^{loc}_{Y}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{q}}\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{q}}+\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{1}}^{2}\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{q}},

where the second inequality is due to the
Proposition

2.10
, while the third one follows from standard facts (see
[
FH20

) and the localization
(\overline{\omega},\mu)
.

At this point we can apply
Proposition

2.4
and, fixed
\mu
small enough to have
\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{2}}^{q/2}\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{1/2}<1/2
, absorb the control
\omega^{loc}_{R^{Y}}
on the left-hand side:

\displaystyle\omega^{loc}_{R^{Y}}(s,t)\lesssim_{q}{}

\displaystyle 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{\frac{q}{2}}|t-s|^{\frac{q}{2}}+\omega^{loc}_{Y}(s,t)+\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{2}}^{\frac{q}{2}}\omega^{loc}_{Y}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+2\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{2}}^{q}\omega_{Z}(s,t)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q}{}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{\frac{q}{2}}|t-s|^{\frac{q}{2}}+\omega^{loc}_{Y}(s,t)+\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{2}}^{q}\omega_{Z}(s,t).

Also, by definition of
(Y,\overline{\sigma}(Y))\in\mathscr{D}^{p\textup{-var}}_{Z}
,

\displaystyle|Y_{s,t}|\leq{}

\displaystyle|\overline{\sigma}(Y_{s})Z_{s,t}+R^{Y}_{s,t}|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{0}}\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\omega^{loc}_{R^{Y}}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q}

\displaystyle\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{0}}\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}|t-s|+\omega^{loc}_{Y}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{q}}+\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{2}}^{2}\omega_{Z}(s,t)^{\frac{2}{q}},

for all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
such that
\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq\mu
.
Therefore,
Proposition

2.4
implies that there exists a positive constant
C
, depending on
q
,
\|\overline{\sigma}\|_{C^{2}}
and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}
, such that for all

\omega^{loc}_{Y}(s,t)\leq C\mu(1+\mu)+C\omega^{loc}_{Y}(s,t)^{2}.

Therefore, choosing

x_{1,2}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-4C^{2}\mu(1+\mu)}\right),\qquad\mu\leq\mu_{0},

we find that

\omega^{loc}_{Y}(s,t)\leq x_{2}^{\mu}\quad\text{or}\quad\omega^{loc}_{Y}(s,t)\geq x_{1}^{\mu}.

We know by

At the cost of possibly re-choosing it, for a small enough

and

for more details).

2.

Similarly to the previous theorem, consider two sequences
\{Z^{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{M})

in

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\big(\phi_{t}^{N,-1}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=-\big[(\grad^{T}\phi^{N}_{t}\big)^{-1}(\phi_{t}^{N,-1}(x))\big]\big[(\partial_{t}\phi^{N}_{t})(\phi^{N,-1}_{t}(x))\big]

\displaystyle=-\big[(\grad^{T}\phi^{N}_{t}\big)^{-1}(\phi_{t}^{N,-1}(x))\big]\big[u_{t}^{N}(x)-\overline{\sigma}(x)\dot{Z}_{t}^{N}\big].

4.2.
RDEs with non-local drift

Let us consider the second Lagrangian RDE, which plays a crucial role in the proof of uniqueness for solutions of the Euler equation (
1.2
).
In particular, we prove a uniqueness result for the following RDE

(4.15)

\phi_{t}(x)=x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}K_{BS}(\phi_{r}(x)-\phi_{r}(y))w_{0}(y)dydr+\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\sigma}(\phi_{r}(x))d\mathbf{Z}_{r},

where

is a bounded function.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}
be a
p
-geometric rough path,
p\in[2,3)
,
w_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
be
C^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
-vector fields and
\phi_{0}\in C(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

If there exist two controls,
\overline{\omega}_{1}
and
\overline{\omega}_{2}
, and two positive constants,
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, such that
(X,\overline{\sigma}(X))\in\mathscr{D}_{Z,(\overline{\omega}_{1},L_{1})}^{p\textup{-var}}(C(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2}))
and
(Y,\overline{\sigma}(Y))\in\mathscr{D}_{Z,(\overline{\omega}_{2},L_{2})}^{p\textup{-var}}(C(\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2}))
are measure-preserving solutions to
Eq.

4.15
, then

X_{t}=Y_{t}\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

Remark 4.6
.

An existence result for
Eq.

4.15
can be easily produced by combining
Proposition

4.2
,
Theorem

4.3
and the iterative procedure proposed in
[
BFM16
]
. We avoid replicating such method, as it plays no role in the uniqueness of solutions to
Eq.

1.2
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Then,

X_{t}(x)-Y_{t}(x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Big[\big(K_{BS}(X_{r}(x)-X_{r}(y)\big)-\big(K_{BS}(Y_{r}(x)-Y_{r}(y)\big)\Big]w_{0}(y)dydr\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\sigma}(X_{r}(x))-\overline{\sigma}(Y_{r}(x))d\mathbf{Z}_{r}.

By following the proof of
Proposition

4.2
, we end up with

|X_{t}(x)-Y_{t}(x)|\lesssim\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Big|\big(K_{BS}(X_{r}(x)-X_{r}(y)\big)-\big(K_{BS}(Y_{r}(x)-Y_{r}(y)\big)\Big|dydr.

Now, recalling that the Biot-Savart kernel enjoys the property

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|K_{BS}(x-y)-K_{BS}(x^{\prime}-y)|dy\lesssim\gamma(|x-x^{\prime}|),\quad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{2},

we can integrate in space and exploit

At this point, as in

4.3

∎

5.

In this section we exploit the uniqueness results obtained for the Lagrangian flows to prove that there exists a unique solution for the two-dimensional Euler equation (

To this aim, we show that

5.2.
Uniqueness result

We are finally ready to prove the remaining part of

5.3.
Continuity of the solution map

We conclude our work by proving that solutions to
Eq.

1.2
are continuous with respect to initial parameters
w_{0}
,
\sigma
and
\mathbf{Z}
. This is not only important per se, but also immediately yields a Wong-Zakai type result for our problem.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(L^{\infty}_{t,x})^{\text{weak},*}
be
L^{\infty}_{t,x}
equipped with the weak-
*
topology. Then,
the solution map

\displaystyle L^{\infty}\times(C^{3})^{M}\times\mathscr{C}^{p\textup{-var}}_{g}

\displaystyle\to(L^{\infty}_{t,x})^{\text{weak},*}\cap C_{t}W^{-1,1}

\displaystyle(w_{0},\sigma,\mathbf{Z})

\displaystyle\mapsto w

is continuous.

Moreover, if
\{B^{H,N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is a piecewise linear approximation of a fractional Brownian motion
B^{H}
, with Hurst parameter
H\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
, then the solutions
\{w^{N}\}
of
Eq.

1.1
with
\dot{Z}=\dot{B}^{H,N}
converge almost surely to the solution
w
of

(5.9)

\begin{cases}dw+u\sg wdt=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sigma_{j}\sg w\circ\!dB^{H,j},\\
w(0)=w_{0},\quad u=K_{BS}*w.\end{cases}

Proof.

We start by showing that, given a converging sequence of parameters, the solutions depending on it converge themselves.
To this aim, consider
\{w_{0}^{N},\sigma^{N},\mathbf{Z}^{N}\}\subseteq L^{\infty}\times(C^{3})^{M}\times\mathscr{C}^{p\textup{-var}}_{g}
and assume it converges to some
\{w_{0},\sigma,\mathbf{Z}\}
in the same space.
By well-posedness of
Eq.

1.2
, we know that for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a solution
w^{N}
associated to the
N
-th element of the sequence.
In particular, since the bounds we found only depend on initial parameters, the sequence of solutions
\{w^{N}\}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}_{t,x}\cap C^{p\textup{-var}}_{T}W^{-1,1}
: this ensures us that there exists a subsequence
\{w^{N_{k}}\}
converging strongly to
w\in C_{t}W^{-1,1}
(cf.
Lemma

A.1
).

We conclude by noting that the unbounded rough drivers
(A^{N,1},A^{N,2})
, associated to
(\sigma^{N},\mathbf{Z^{N}})
, converge, making us able to prove that
w
solves
Eq.

1.2
like we did for the existence proof, and that, by uniqueness of solutions, it is the whole sequence
\{w^{N}\}
that converges.

At this point, the second statement if trivial: we know (see e.g.
[
FH20
]
) that piecewise linear are one of those approximations of a fractional Brownian motion such that their canonical lift
(B^{H,N},\int_{0}^{\cdot}B^{H,N}\otimes dB^{H,N})
converges to
(B^{H},\mathbb{B}^{H})
in the rough path topology almost surely.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

In the case
H=\frac{1}{2}
,
B^{H}
is a standard Brownian motion.
Then it is well known that piecewise linear approximations and their canonical lifts converge to Stratonovich enhanced Brownian motion,
(B,\mathbb{B}^{\textup{Strat}})
.
Since (Stratonovich) integration against an enhanced Brownian motion coincides almost surely with standard Stratonovich integration, this yields that
w^{N}
associated to
\mathbf{B}^{H,N}
converge to a probabilistically strong solution of
Eq.

5.9
.
In this case, we end up recovering the result obtained in
[
BFM16
]
.

Remark 5.9
.

The reasoning behind the proof of the Wong-Zakai statement in
Theorem

5.7
actually holds in more general cases. For example, it holds for Gaussian rough paths satisfying the hypothesis of
[
FH20
, Theorem 10.4]
, for which it can be proven that piecewise linear approximations converge in rough path topology.

Appendix A
A compactness argument

We prove here a compactness result, which plays a crucial role in
Theorem

3.5
.

Lemma A.1
.

Consider a sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})\cap C^{p\textup{-var}}(W^{-1,1})
such that
\|g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}
is uniformly bounded and there exist
L>0
, two controls
\omega
and
\overline{\omega}
satisfying

\|(g_{n})_{s,t}\|_{W^{-1,1}}\leq\omega(s,t)^{1/p}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}\text{ s.t. }\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq L.

Then,
\{g_{n}\}_{n}
is compact in
C([0,T],W^{-1,1})
.

Proof.

For all
\alpha\in(0,L]
, define the operator
J_{\alpha}f(s)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{s}^{s+\alpha}f(r)dr
, where we impose
f(r)=f(T)
for all
r>T
. The following hold true:

1.

For any fixed
\alpha
,
J_{\alpha}g_{n}:[0,T]\to W^{-1,1}
are equicontinuous functions;

2.

As
\alpha
vanishes,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|J_{\alpha}g_{n}-g_{n}\|_{C_{t}W^{-1,1}_{x}}\to 0
;

3.

For all
s\in[0,T]
and
\alpha\in(0,L]
,
\left\{J_{\alpha}g_{n}(s)\right\}_{n}
is relatively compact in
W^{-1,1}
;

4.

There exists a subsequence
g_{n_{k}}
that converges weakly-* to some
g\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

The first and second properties follow from the hypothesis, the third one from
W^{1,1}
being compactly embedded into
L^{1}
, and the last one from standard results in functional analysis.

Let now
\alpha
be fixed: Ascoli-Arzelà theorem yields a subsequence, denoted again by
\{J_{\alpha}g_{n_{k}}\}
, such that
J_{\alpha}g_{n_{k}}
converges to some
h
in
C_{t}W^{-1,1}_{x}
. Then, point
4
above and the continuity of
J_{\alpha}
imply that
h=J_{\alpha}g
. In particular, any subsequence of
J_{\alpha}g_{n_{k}}
has a sub-subsequence that converges to
h
; it follows that the full sequence
J_{\alpha}g_{n_{k}}
must converge to
h
.

Eventually, the result follows from the inequality

\|g_{n_{k}}-g_{n_{l}}\|_{C_{t}W^{-1,1}}\leq\|g_{n_{k}}-J_{\alpha}g_{n_{k}}\|_{C_{t}W^{-1,1}}\\
+\|J_{\alpha}g_{n_{k}}-J_{\alpha}g_{n_{l}}\|_{C_{t}W^{-1,1}_{x}}+\|J_{\alpha}g_{n_{l}}-g_{n_{l}}\|_{C_{t}W^{-1,1}_{x}}.

∎

Appendix B
Sewing Lemma and Rough Gronwall Lemma

Sewing Lemma is crucial in rough paths theory since it allows for the building of rough integrals. We report a proper version of the Sewing Lemma, which can be found in
[
Dey+19
]
. Its proof is a straightforward modification of
[
FH20
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\xi\in(0,1)
,
L>0
and
V
be a Banach space. Consider two controls
\omega,\overline{\omega}
and a
2
-index function
h:\Delta_{T}\to V
such that

|\delta h_{s,u,t}|\coloneqq|h_{s,t}-h_{s,u}-h_{u,t}|\leq\omega(s,t)^{\frac{1}{\xi}},

for all
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{T}
with
\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq L
.

Then, there exists a unique path
I(h):[0,T]\to V
with
I(h)_{0}=0
such that the 2-index function

\Lambda_{s,t}\coloneqq I(h)_{s,t}-h_{s,t}\in C^{\xi\textup{-var},2}_{\overline{\omega},L}
.
Moreover, there exists
C_{\xi}>0
such that

|\Lambda_{s,t}|\leq C_{\xi}\omega(s,t)^{\frac{1}{\xi}},

for all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
with
\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq L
.

In addition if
h\in C^{p\textup{-var},2}_{\overline{\omega},L}(V)
for some
p\geq\xi
, then
I\in C^{p\textup{-var}}(V)
.

Corollary B.2
.

Consider
h:\Delta_{T}\to V
such that
h\in C^{\xi\textup{-var},2}_{\overline{\omega},L}(V)
and it satisfies the assumptions of
Lemma

B.1
. Then,

|h_{s,t}|\leq C_{\xi}\omega(s,t)^{\frac{1}{\xi}},

for all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
s.t.
\overline{\omega}(s,t)\leq L
.

The second reported result is a generalization of the Rough Gronwall Lemma firstly proved in
[
Dey+19
]
.

Lemma B.3
.

Consider a function
G:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, three controls
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
and some constants
L,C^{\prime}>0
,
C\geq 1
,
k>k^{\prime}\geq 1
.

Suppose that the following conditions hold:

1.

\omega_{2}(s,t)\leq\omega_{1}(s,t)
for all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
;

2.

For all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}
with
\omega_{1}(s,t)\leq L
,

G_{s,t}\leq C\big(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}G_{r}+C^{\prime}\big)\omega_{1}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{k}}+\omega_{2}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{k^{\prime}}}+\omega_{3}(s,t).

Then, it holds

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}G_{t}\leq 2\exp\Big(\frac{\omega_{1}(0,T)}{\alpha L}\Big)\Big\{G_{0}

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\omega_{3}(0,t)\exp\Big(\frac{-\omega_{1}(0,t)}{\alpha L}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\omega_{2}(0,t)^{\frac{1-\theta}{k^{\prime}}}\exp\Big(\frac{-\omega_{1}(0,t)}{\alpha L}\Big)\Big)+C^{\prime}\Big\},

where
\theta=\frac{k^{\prime}}{k}
and
\alpha=min\Big(1,\frac{1}{L(2Ce^{2})^{k}}\Big)
.

Proof.

For any
t\in[0,T]
, consider the following functions:

\displaystyle G_{\leq t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{s\in[0,t]}G_{s},

\displaystyle H_{t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\exp\Big(-\frac{\omega_{1}(0,t)}{\alpha L}\Big)\Big(G_{\leq t}+\omega_{2}(0,t)^{\frac{1-\theta}{k^{\prime}}}+C^{\prime}\Big),

\displaystyle H_{\leq t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{s\in[0,t]}H_{s}.

Since
\omega_{1}
is a continuous function, then there exists a partition
P=\{t_{0}\coloneqq 0,\dots,t_{N}\coloneqq T\}
such that
\omega_{1}(t_{j-1},t_{j})=\alpha L
for all
j\in\{1,\cdots,N-1\}
.

Consider
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
, then

\displaystyle G_{0,t}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i-2}G_{t_{j},t_{j+1}}+G_{t_{i-1},t}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i-2}\Big[C(G_{\leq t_{j+1}}+C^{\prime})\omega_{1}(t_{j},t_{j+1})^{\frac{1}{k}}+\omega_{2}(t_{j},t_{j+1})^{\frac{1}{k^{\prime}}}+\omega_{3}(t_{j},t_{j+1})\Big]

\displaystyle+C(G_{\leq t}+C^{\prime})\omega_{1}(t_{i-1},t)^{\frac{1}{k}}+\omega_{2}(t_{i-1},t)^{\frac{1}{k^{\prime}}}+\omega_{3}(t_{i-1},t)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i-2}\Big[C(\alpha L)^{\frac{1}{k}}(G_{\leq t_{j+1}}+C^{\prime})+\omega_{2}(0,t_{j+1})^{\frac{1-\theta}{k^{\prime}}}\omega_{1}(t_{j},t_{j+1})^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big]

\displaystyle+C(G_{\leq t_{i}}+C^{\prime})(\alpha L)^{\frac{1}{k}}+\omega_{2}(0,t_{i})^{\frac{1-\theta}{k^{\prime}}}\omega_{1}(t_{i-1},t)^{\frac{1}{k}}+\omega_{3}(0,t)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C(\alpha L)^{\frac{1}{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\Big[G_{\leq t_{j+1}}+\omega_{2}(0,t_{j+1})^{\frac{1-\theta}{k^{\prime}}}+C^{\prime}\Big]+\omega_{3}(0,t),

where the first and second inequalities are due to the hypothesis.

Now,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\Big[G_{\leq t_{j+1}}+\omega_{2}(0,t_{j+1})^{\frac{1-\theta}{k^{\prime}}}+C^{\prime}\Big]={}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}H_{t_{j+1}}\exp\Big(\frac{\omega_{1}(0,t_{j+1})}{\alpha L}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle H_{\leq T}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\exp(j+1)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle H_{\leq T}\exp(i+1).

Therefore,

(B.1)

G_{t}\leq G_{0}+\omega_{3}(0,t)+C(\alpha L)^{\frac{1}{k}}H_{\leq T}\exp(i+1).

We exploit
Eq.

B.1
to estimate
H_{\leq T}
. Indeed, given
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
, then

\displaystyle H_{t}={}

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\frac{\omega_{1}(0,t)}{\alpha L}\Big)\Big(G_{\leq t}+\omega_{2}(0,t)^{\frac{1-\theta}{k^{\prime}}}+C^{\prime}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\frac{\omega_{1}(0,t)}{\alpha L}\Big)

\displaystyle\Big[G_{0}+\omega_{3}(0,t)+C(\alpha L)^{1/k}H_{\leq T}\exp(i+1)+\omega_{2}(0,t)^{\frac{1-\theta}{k^{\prime}}}+C^{\prime}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle G_{0}+\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\Big(\omega_{3}(0,r)\exp\Big(-\frac{\omega_{1}(0,r)}{\alpha L}\Big)\Big)+

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\Big(\omega_{2}(0,r)^{\frac{1-\theta}{k^{\prime}}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\omega_{1}(0,r)}{\alpha L}\Big)\Big)+C(\alpha L)^{\frac{1}{k}}\operatorname{e}^{2}H_{\leq T}+C^{\prime}.

Therefore, taking the supremum in the time variable
t
implies that

(B.2)

H_{\leq T}\leq 2G_{0}+2\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\Big(\omega_{3}(0,r)\exp\Big(-\frac{\omega_{1}(0,r)}{\alpha L}\Big)\Big)\\
+2\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\Big(\omega_{2}(0,r)^{\frac{1-\theta}{k^{\prime}}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\omega_{1}(0,r)}{\alpha L}\Big)\Big)+2C^{\prime}.

At last, notice that

\displaystyle G_{\leq T}\leq H_{T}\exp\Big(\frac{\omega_{1}(0,T)}{\alpha L}\Big)\leq H_{\leq T}\exp\Big(\frac{\omega_{1}(0,T)}{\alpha L}\Big),

which proves the result once it is combined with
Eq.

B.2
.
∎

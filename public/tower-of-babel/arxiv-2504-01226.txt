Irreducibility of the parabolic induction of essentially Speh representations and a representation of Arthur type over a p p -adic field
Barbara Bošnjak, Alexander Stadler

Abstract
Let F F be a p p -adic field. In this article, we consider representations of split special orthogonal groups SO 2 ​ n + 1 ​ ( F ) \mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}(F) and symplectic groups Sp 2 ​ n ​ ( F ) \mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(F) of rank n n . We denote by π 1 × … × π r ⋊ π \pi_{1}\times\ldots\times\pi_{r}\rtimes\pi the normalized parabolically induced representation of either. Now let u i u_{i} be essentially Speh representations and π \pi a representation of Arthur type. We prove that the parabolic induction u 1 × … × u r ⋊ π u_{1}\times\ldots\times u_{r}\rtimes\pi is irreducible if and only if u i × u j u_{i}\times u_{j} , u i × u j ∨ u_{i}\times u_{j}^{\vee} and u i ⋊ π u_{i}\rtimes\pi are irreducible for all choices of i ≠ j i\neq j . If u i u_{i} are Speh representations, we determine the composition series of the above parabolically induced representation. Through this, we are able to produce a new collection of unitary representations.



1
Introduction

A central problem of representation theory is the classification of the unitary dual (i.e. the class of irreducible unitary representations) of classical
p
-adic groups. It is still an open question and the main motivation for the problems solved in this article.
The groups of our interest are split special odd orthogonal groups
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}(F)
or symplectic groups
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(F)
of rank
n
, which we both denote by
G_{n}
.
Here
F
stands for a
p
-adic field.
The unitary dual of these groups
G_{n}
is explored in various directions by representation theorists. To mention some of them, we note that, for Levi subgroups of corank at most 3, Tadić described the irreducible unitary subquotients of parabolically induced representations of
G_{n}
in
[
26
]
.
The
generic dual
of
G_{n}
has been determined by Lapid, Muić and Tadić in
[
17
]
.
The unramified unitary dual of
G_{n}
was determined by Muić and Tadić in
[
29
]
.
In
[
18
]
, Matić constructed a class of unitarizabile non-tempered representations using the Aubert involution.

The relation between the question of unitarizability of representations of classical
p
-adic groups
G_{n}
and Arthur packets is studied by Tadić in
[
27
]
, and further explored in
[
10
]
by Hazeltine, Jiang, Liu, Lo and Zhang.
Considering a natural topology on unitary duals, the aforementioned authors state conjectures linking Arthur type representations with isolated representations and the representations appearing at the critical points, in this way indicating that the representations of Arthur type should play an important role in the classification of the unitary dual of the group
G_{n}
.
The notion of a representation
\pi
of
Arthur type
was introduced by Arthur in
[
1
, Chapter 8]
and says that
\pi
is the local factor of a square-integrable automorphic representation and in particular, unitary.
Closely related to Arthur type representations are the
essentially Speh representations
.
They are the main building blocks in the classification of unitary irreducible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
.
This classification was a major milestone, achieved by Tadić in 1986
[
25
]
.

Parabolic induction is an important tool in the smooth complex representation theory of the classical groups
G_{n}
, which is a method to construct representations of a group from representations of its Levi subgroups.
A natural step towards understanding the unitary dual of the groups
G_{n}
is to consider the
parabolically induced
representations of the tensor product of essentially Speh and Arthur type representations.
For representations
\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{r}
and
\pi_{0}
of the groups
\mathrm{GL}_{d_{1}}(F),\ldots,\mathrm{GL}_{d_{r}}(F)
and
G_{n_{0}}
respectively, we denote by
\pi_{1}\times\cdots\times\pi_{r}\rtimes\pi_{0}
the normalized parabolically induced representation.
In this article, we are interested in an irreducibility criterion for this representation. The natural conjecture, which is well-known by the experts (however, we haven’t seen it written down before), is the following:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{r}
be irreducible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{d_{1}}(F),...,\mathrm{GL}_{d_{r}}(F)
and let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G_{n_{0}}
. Then the parabolically induced representation
\pi_{1}\times\ldots\times\pi_{r}\rtimes\pi
is irreducible if and only if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\pi_{i}\times\pi_{j}\\
\pi_{i}\times\pi_{j}^{\vee}\\
\pi_{i}\rtimes\pi\end{cases}\textit{are irreducible for}\ 1\leq i,j\leq r,\ i\neq j.

Here
\pi_{j}^{\vee}
denotes the
contragredient representation
of
\pi_{j}
. A similar conjectural criterion exists for the parabolically induced representation
\pi_{1}\times\ldots\times\pi_{r}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
and it is known for the case where all but at most two of the representations
\pi_{i}
are square irreducible (
[
11
, Corollary 4.3]
). We will prove Conjecture
1.1
when
\pi_{i}=u_{i}
are
essentially Speh
and when
\pi
is of
Arthur type
(defined in Section
3.2
).

Indeed, the next theorem is our main result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}
be essentially Speh representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{d_{1}}(F),...,\mathrm{GL}_{d_{r}}(F)
and let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G_{n_{0}}
of Arthur type. Then the parabolically induced representation
u_{1}\times\ldots\times u_{r}\rtimes\pi
is irreducible if and only if

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{i}\times u_{j}\\
u_{i}\times u_{j}^{\vee}\\
u_{i}\rtimes\pi\end{cases}\text{are irreducible for}\ 1\leq i,j\leq r,\ i\neq j.

(Irr)

In case that the
u_{i}
’s are actually Speh representations, one can say even more. Besides the irreducibility result in this special case (which is also the first step in the proof of the Theorem
1.2
and carried out in Chapter
3
), we can also determine the composition series of the representation
u_{1}\times\ldots\times u_{r}\rtimes\pi
(see Corollary
3.32
). The key to understanding representations of Arthur type and Arthur packets is the combinatorial construction by Mœglin
[
19
]
, which has been refined by Xu
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
and Atobe
[
2
]
. We rely heavily on Atobe’s work and simplify some notation, to obtain the desired decomposition.

In the general case, where
u_{i}
are essentially Speh representations, we extensively use two articles, which are due to Tadić
[
24
]
and Atobe
[
3
]
.
Namely, there are explicit criteria to check the left-hand side of (
Irr
).
Tadić proved in
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
a criterion for the reducibility of the induced representation from two essentially Speh representations.
In
[
3
]
, Atobe described the socle, i.e. the maximal semisimple subrepresentation, of a representation of
G_{n}
of the form
u|\cdot|^{s}\rtimes\pi
, where
u
is a Speh representation. We generalize these methods to several essentially Speh representations. Our main tool are the Jacquet modules, mostly in the form of derivatives (see Subsection
2.4
).

Let us state a corollary of Theorem
1.2
in a form that emphasizes its role in the classification of the unitary dual of the groups
G_{n}
.
We recall that Tadić’s classification states that every irreducible unitary representation of a general linear group is of the form
u_{1}\times\cdots\times u_{r}
, for some essentially Speh representations
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}
. For classical groups
G_{n}
, one gets the following unitarity result due to Deformation (
[
17
, Chapter 2]
):

Corollary 1.3
.

For
1\leq i\leq r
let
\rho_{i}
be self-dual supercuspidal representations. Let
u_{i}\coloneqq u_{\rho_{i}}(a_{i},b_{i})
be Speh representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}}(F),\ldots,\mathrm{GL}_{n_{r}}(F)
respectively and let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G_{n_{0}}
of Arthur type. Let
s_{i}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
s_{i}(0)=0
be continuous for
1\leq i\leq r
. We define

\displaystyle\pi_{t}\coloneqq u_{1}|\cdot|^{s_{1}(t)}\times\ldots\times u_{r}|\cdot|^{s_{r}(t)}\rtimes\pi

as a representation of
G_{n_{0}+n_{1}+\ldots+n_{r}}
. If for
0\leq t<1
it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{i}|\cdot|^{s_{i}(t)}\times u_{j}|\cdot|^{s_{j}(t)}\\
u_{i}|\cdot|^{s_{i}(t)}\times u_{j}^{\vee}|\cdot|^{-s_{j}(t)}\\
u_{i}|\cdot|^{s_{i}(t)}\rtimes\pi\end{cases}\text{are irreducible for}\ 1\leq i,j\leq r,\ i\neq j,

(1.1)

then
\pi_{t}
is unitarizable for all
t\in[0,1)
and so is every subquotient of
\pi_{1}
.

Note that in
[
17
, Chapter 2]
the representation
\pi_{t}
is also required to be hermitian, but this follows from irreducibility in this case, because

\displaystyle\pi_{t}^{+}\cong u_{1}^{\vee}|\cdot|^{-s_{1}(t)}\times\ldots\times u_{r}^{\vee}|\cdot|^{-s_{r}(t)}\rtimes\pi\cong\pi_{t}.

For a long time now, the endoscopic classification of Arthur
[
1
]
relied on results that were expected to hold, but not yet proven. Recently, this gap has been closed in
[
7
]
and the results presented here are only dependent on the validity of the twisted weighted fundamental Lemma (
[
23
, Section II.4.4]
).
One would expect similar results to hold for other classical groups than the ones treated here (
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}(F)
and
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(F)
). However, the necessary methods are so far only available in these cases, due to Atobe’s work.

Let us describe the organization of this article into chapters:
In Chapter
2
we define notations used throughout the article and state the main theorems in the surrounding theory of the problem.
In Chapter
3
, we introduce the notion of extended multi-segments and explain how parabolic induction of Speh representations and Arthur type representations behaves in terms of extended multi-segments.
The results behind the proof of Theorem
1.2
are developed in Chapter
4
.
It is organized by different types of essentially Speh representations appearing in the induction.
Finally in the Appendix, we provide some proofs omitted in Chapter
3
and give a formula for the Aubert dual of representations of Arthur type.

2.1
Notations

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field and let
G
be the
F
-points of a reductive group. Let
P
denote a parabolic subgroup of
G
. We denote by
\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}
the normalized parabolic induction along
P
and by
\mathrm{Jac}_{P}
its left adjoint functor, the Jacquet functor. A representation of
G
is called

, if it is not a composition factor of any representation of the form
\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\tau)
, where
P
is a proper parabolic subgroup of

and
\tau
is a representation of the corresponding Levi subgroup
M
. We let
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
,
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
,
\mathrm{Irr}_{unit}(G)
,
\mathrm{Cusp}^{\perp}(G)
and
\mathrm{Cusp}_{unit}(G)
denote the set of equivalence classes of smooth
\mathbb{C}
-representations of
G
, and its subsets of irreducible, unitary (irreducible), self-dual supercuspidal and unitary supercuspidal representations respectively. Furthermore, let
\mathscr{R}(G)
denote the Grothendieck group of smooth representations of finite length of
G
.
Let
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
denote the general linear group of rank
n
and let
G_{n}
either denote the split special orthogonal group
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}(F)
or the symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(F)
of rank
n
over
F
. We define
\mathscr{R}(\mathrm{GL})=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{R}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
and
\mathscr{R}(G)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{R}(G_{n})
. We let
\text{Irr}(GL)\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\text{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
and similarly
\text{Irr}(G)\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\text{Irr}(G_{n})
.
For
\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}(GL_{n}(F))
or
\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G_{n})
, we denote by
[\pi]
its semisimplification, i.e. the direct sum of its composition factors.
If
\pi_{1}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
is a subquotient of
\pi
, we write
\pi_{1}\leq\pi
.
By
\mathrm{soc}(\pi)
we denote the socle of
\pi
, which is the maximal semisimple subrepresentation of
\pi
and we denote by
\pi^{\vee}
the contragradient (or dual) representation of
\pi
. By
\overline{\pi}
, we denote the complex conjugate representation of
\pi
, which acts on the vector space
(\overline{V_{\pi}},\overline{\cdot})
, where
\overline{V_{\pi}}=V_{\pi}
,
\alpha\overline{\cdot}v=\overline{\alpha}v
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
v\in V_{\pi}
and
\overline{\pi}(g)(v)=\pi(g)(v)
. Furthermore, we denote by
\pi^{+}=\overline{\pi^{\vee}}\cong\overline{\pi}^{\vee}
the hermitian contragredient of
\pi
. The irreducible representation
\pi
is
hermitian
(i.e. there exists a non-degenerate
G
-invariant hermitian form on
\pi
) if and only if
\pi^{+}\cong\pi
. The representation
\pi
is
unitary
, if it is hermitian and the hermitian form is definite.

The Geometric Lemma (see

9

is multiplicative, i.e. for

m^{*}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})=m^{*}(\pi_{1})\times m^{*}(\pi_{2})

The multiplication on the right hand side is determined by
(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})\times(\tau_{1}^{\prime}\otimes\tau_{2}^{\prime})=(\tau_{1}\times\tau_{1}^{\prime})\otimes(\tau_{2}\times\tau_{2}^{\prime})
.

A

[x,y]_{\rho}
(we sometimes drop the index

if it is clear from the context) is a set of supercuspidal representations of the form

[x,y]_{\rho}\coloneqq\{\rho|\cdot|^{x},\rho|\cdot|^{x-1},\ldots,\rho|\cdot|^{y}\},

where
\rho\in\mathrm{Cusp}_{\mathrm{unit}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
,
\rho|\cdot|^{z}
denotes the twist of the representation
\rho
by the character
|\cdot|^{z}
and

such that
x-y\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\geq y
. Note that for every supercuspidal representation
\rho
, there exists a

such that
\chi_{\rho}|\cdot|^{z}
is unitary, where
\chi_{\rho}
is the central character of
\rho
. But this implies that
\rho|\cdot|^{z}
itself is unitary. Hence we can restrict the definition of segments to unitary
\rho
. We call the set

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{\rho}\coloneqq\{\rho|\cdot|^{z}\ |z\in\mathbb{Z}\}

the
line
of
\rho
. For the segment
\Delta\coloneqq[x,y]_{\rho}
, we denote

\displaystyle\overset{\leftarrow}{\Delta}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[x-1,y-1]_{\rho}

\displaystyle\min(\Delta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq y

\displaystyle\max(\Delta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq x.

We define the
Steinberg representation

\Delta_{\rho}[x,y]
as the unique irreducible subrepresentation of

\rho|\cdot|^{x}\times\cdots\times\rho|\cdot|^{y}.

This is an essentially discrete series representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{d(x-y+1)}(F)

\Delta_{\rho}[x,x+1]

The Langlands classification (

\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))

\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{Cusp}_{unit}(\mathrm{GL}_{d_{i}}(F))

We call the segments
[x_{1},y_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\ldots,[x_{r},y_{r}]_{\rho_{r}}

of

.

\mathfrak{m}=\Delta_{1}+\ldots+\Delta_{r}

We define

\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\hskip 8.53581pt&v_{1}\hskip 8.53581pt\\
\hskip 8.53581ptx_{1}&\hskip 8.53581pty_{1}\end{pmatrix}<_{\text{strong}}\begin{pmatrix}u_{2}\hskip 8.53581pt&v_{2}\hskip 8.53581pt\\
\hskip 8.53581ptx_{2}&\hskip 8.53581pty_{2}\end{pmatrix}\Longleftrightarrow u_{1}<u_{2},\penalty\ v_{1}<v_{2},\penalty\ x_{1}<x_{2},\penalty\ y_{1}<y_{2}.

We will vastly use the following theorem on the reducibility of a representation induced from two essentially Speh representations.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
)

Let
n,n_{1}
and
n_{2}
be positive integers such that
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
.
Let
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
be essentially Speh representations of
GL_{n_{1}}(F)
and
GL_{n_{2}}(F)
. Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathrm{Cusp}_{unit}(GL_{n}(F))
and
u_{i},v_{i},x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
, such that

\pi_{i}=u_{ess}\begin{pmatrix}u_{i}\hskip 8.53581pt&v_{i}\hskip 8.53581pt\\
\hskip 8.53581ptx_{i}&\hskip 8.53581pty_{i}\end{pmatrix}^{(\rho_{i})}

for
i=1,2
. Then the parabolically induced representation
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
reduces if and only if

(1)

[u_{1},y_{1}]_{\rho_{1}}\cup[u_{2},y_{2}]_{\rho_{2}}
is a segment,

(2)

\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\hskip 8.53581pt&v_{1}\hskip 8.53581pt\\
\hskip 8.53581ptx_{1}&\hskip 8.53581pty_{1}\end{pmatrix}<_{\text{strong}}\begin{pmatrix}u_{2}\hskip 8.53581pt&v_{2}\hskip 8.53581pt\\
\hskip 8.53581ptx_{2}&\hskip 8.53581pty_{2}\end{pmatrix}

or

\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\hskip 8.53581pt&v_{1}\hskip 8.53581pt\\
\hskip 8.53581ptx_{1}&\hskip 8.53581pty_{1}\end{pmatrix}>_{\text{strong}}\begin{pmatrix}u_{2}\hskip 8.53581pt&v_{2}\hskip 8.53581pt\\
\hskip 8.53581ptx_{2}&\hskip 8.53581pty_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, if
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
is irreducible, then
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\cong\pi_{2}\times\pi_{1}
.

Note that, in particular, this implies that the parabolic induction of two Speh representations is always irreducible. Also, it implies that
u_{\rho_{1}}(a_{1},b_{1})|\cdot|^{s_{1}}\times u_{\rho_{2}}(a_{2},b_{2})|\cdot|^{s_{2}}
is irreducible if
\rho_{1}\not\cong\rho_{2}
.

2.3
Classical Groups

Next, we recall some notations for representations of the classical groups
G_{n}
. Fix a
F
-rational Borel subgroup consisting of upper-triangular matrices of
G_{n}
.
Then the Levi subgroup
M
of a standard parabolic subgroup
P
of
G_{n}
has the form
M\cong\mathrm{GL}_{d_{1}}(F)\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}_{d_{r}}(F)\times G_{n_{0}}
such that
n=n_{0}+(d_{1}+\ldots+d_{r})
. For representations
\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{r}
and
\pi_{0}
of the groups
\mathrm{GL}_{d_{1}}(F),\ldots,\mathrm{GL}_{d_{r}}(F)
and
G_{n_{0}}
respectively, we denote by

\pi_{1}\times\cdots\times\pi_{r}\rtimes\pi_{0}\coloneqq\operatorname{Ind}_{P}^{G_{n}}(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\pi_{r}\boxtimes\pi_{0})

the normalized parabolically induced representation.
The Langlands classification (
[
3
, Section 2.2]
) for
G_{n}
says that every
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{n})
is the unique irreducible subrepresentation of some parabolically induced representation
\Delta_{\rho_{1}}[x_{1},y_{1}]\times\cdots\times\Delta_{\rho_{r}}[x_{r},y_{r}]\rtimes\pi_{0}
, where

•

\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{Cusp}_{unit}(\mathrm{GL}_{d_{i}}(F))
for
i=1,\ldots,r
;

•

x_{1}+y_{1}\leq\cdots\leq x_{r}+y_{r}<0
;

•

\pi_{0}
is an irreducible tempered representation of
G_{n_{0}}
;

•

n=n_{0}+(d_{1}+\ldots+d_{r})
.

In this case, we write

\pi=L(\Delta_{\rho_{1}}[x_{1},y_{1}],\ldots,\Delta_{\rho_{r}}[x_{r},y_{r}];\pi_{0}),

and call
(\Delta_{\rho_{1}}[x_{1},y_{1}],\ldots,\Delta_{\rho_{r}}[x_{r},y_{r}];\pi_{0})
the Langlands data for
\pi
.

The Jacquet functor with respect to a parabolic subgroup
P
is denoted
\mathrm{Jac}_{P}
.
Let
P_{d}
denote the maximal parabolic subgroup of
G_{n}(F)
with Levi subgroup isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F)\times G_{n-d}(F)
. We consider a map
\mu^{*}:\mathscr{R}(G)\to\mathscr{R}(\mathrm{GL})\otimes\mathscr{R}(G)
defined by

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(\pi)=\sum_{d=0}^{n}[\text{Jac}_{P_{d}}(\pi)],

(2.3)

for
\pi\in\mathscr{R}(G_{n})
.
In
[
28
]
, M. Tadić defined a map
M^{*}:\mathscr{R}(\mathrm{GL})\to\mathscr{R}(\mathrm{GL})\otimes\mathscr{R}(\mathrm{GL})
by

\displaystyle M^{*}=(m\otimes 1)\circ(\cdot^{\vee}\otimes m^{*})\circ s\circ m^{*}

(2.4)

where
s:\sum x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\mapsto\sum y_{i}\otimes x_{i}
.
Note that
M^{*}
is a multiplicative map.
It is the map used to obtain a description of
\mu^{*}
applied to a parabolically induced representation (this is known as the
Geometric Lemma
or more specifically
Tadić’s Formula
):

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
28
, Theorem 5.4]
)

For
\pi\in\mathscr{R}(\mathrm{GL})
and
\sigma\in\mathscr{R}(G)

\mu^{*}(\pi\rtimes\sigma)=M^{*}(\pi)\rtimes\mu^{*}(\sigma),

where the product on the right hand side is determined by
(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})\rtimes(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\sigma^{\prime})=(\pi_{1}\times\pi^{\prime})\otimes(\pi_{2}\rtimes\sigma^{\prime})
.

For an induced representation
\pi\rtimes\sigma
, where
\pi
is a ladder representation with Langlands data
(\Delta_{\rho}[x_{1},y_{1}],\ldots,\Delta_{\rho}[x_{k},y_{k}])
, we combine the result of Theorem
2.4
with the description of the image of the map
m^{*}
applied to a ladder representation in (
2.2
).
We define
\mathop{\text{Lad}}(\pi)^{\prime}
as the set of all pairs
\big((c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}),(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})\big)
in
\mathop{\text{Lad}}(\pi)\times\mathop{\text{Lad}}(\pi)
such that
c_{i}\leq d_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(\pi\rtimes\sigma)=\sum\limits_{\mathop{\text{Lad}}(\pi)^{\prime}}L(\Delta_{\rho^{\vee}}[-y_{k},-c_{k}],\ldots,\Delta_{\rho^{\vee}}[-y_{1},-c_{1}])\times

(2.5)

\displaystyle L(\mathop{\Delta_{\rho}}[x_{1},d_{1}+1],\ldots,\mathop{\Delta_{\rho}}[x_{k},d_{k}+1])\otimes L(\mathop{\Delta_{\rho}}[d_{1},c_{1}+1],\ldots,\mathop{\Delta_{\rho}}[d_{k},c_{k}+1])\rtimes\mu^{*}(\sigma)

up to semisimplification.

In
[
22
]
, the covariant functor
\mathrm{MVW}:\mathrm{Rep}(G_{n})\rightarrow\mathrm{Rep}(G_{n})
is defined. It has the following properties:

•

If
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{n})
, then
\pi^{\mathrm{MVW}}=\pi^{\vee}
,

•

If
\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G_{n_{0}})
and
\tau\in\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F))
, then
(\tau\rtimes\pi)^{\mathrm{MVW}}\cong\tau\rtimes\pi^{\mathrm{MVW}}
.

We use it to show for example the following: If
\tau\rtimes\pi
is irreducible for
\tau\in\mathrm{Irr}(GL_{d}(F))
and
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{n_{0}})
, then

\displaystyle\tau\rtimes\pi

\displaystyle\cong(\tau^{\vee}\rtimes\pi^{\vee})^{\vee}\cong(\tau^{\vee}\rtimes\pi^{\vee})^{\mathrm{MVW}}

\displaystyle\cong\tau^{\vee}\rtimes\pi.

2.4
Derivatives

In this section, we recall the theory of derivatives, established in
[
6
]
and in
[
3
, Section 3.1]
. It is a computational tool, that allows one to reduce questions about representations of
G
to representations of a certain subgroup, by utilizing the Geometric Lemma (Theorem
2.4
). First, we recall the notion of derivatives for representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
(
[
5
, Definition 2.3]
and
[
3
, Section A.1]
):

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\pi\in\mathscr{R}(\mathrm{GL})
and let
\sigma
be either supercuspidal or equal to
Z_{\rho}[0,1]=L(\{\rho\},\{\rho|\cdot|^{1}\})
for some supercuspidal
\rho
. For a non-negative integer
k
, we define the left and right derivatives
L^{(k)}_{\sigma}
and
R^{(k)}_{\sigma}
of
\pi
to be the semisimple representations satisfying

\displaystyle m^{*}(\pi)=\sigma^{k}\otimes L^{(k)}_{\sigma}(\pi)+\displaystyle\sum_{i}\pi_{i}\otimes\tau_{i},

(2.6)

where
\pi_{i}\otimes\tau_{i}
are irreducible representations such that
\pi_{i}\not\cong\sigma^{k}
and

\displaystyle m^{*}(\pi)=R^{(k)}_{\sigma}(\pi)\otimes\sigma^{k}+\displaystyle\sum_{i}\pi_{i}\otimes\tau_{i},

(2.7)

where
\pi_{i}\otimes\tau_{i}
are irreducible representations such that
\tau_{i}\not\cong\sigma^{k}

. For
k=1
, we define
L_{\sigma}=L_{\sigma}^{(1)}
and
R_{\sigma}=R_{\sigma}^{(1)}
. The representation
\pi
is called left (resp. right)
\sigma
-
reduced
if
L_{\sigma}(\pi)=0
(resp.
R_{\sigma}(\pi)=0
). When
L_{\sigma}^{(k+1)}(\pi)=0
but
L_{\sigma}^{(k)}(\pi)\neq 0
, we call
L_{\sigma}^{(k)}(\pi)\neq 0
the highest derivative and denote it by
L_{\sigma}^{max}(\pi)
(analogously for the right derivative).

Remark 2.6
.

If
\sigma
is supercuspidal, the highest (left or right) derivative

of an irreducible representation

is irreducible (
[
13
, Lemma 2.1.2]
). In this case,

is the unique irreducible subrepresentation of
\sigma^{k}\rtimes L_{\sigma}^{(k)}(\pi)
. If
\pi
is irreducible and left

-reduced, then

is irreducible and again

is the unique irreducible subrepresentation of
Z_{\rho}[0,1]^{k}\rtimes L_{Z_{\rho}[0,1]}^{(k)}(\pi)

[
5
, Theorem 2.4]
).

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\pi\in\mathscr{R}(G)
and let

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(\pi)=\sigma^{k}\otimes D^{(k)}_{\sigma}(\pi)+\displaystyle\sum_{i}\pi_{i}\otimes\pi_{i}^{\prime},

where
\mu^{*}
is the map defined in (
2.3
) and
\pi_{i}\otimes\pi_{i}^{\prime}
are irreducible representations such that
\pi_{i}\not\cong\sigma^{k}
. For

D_{\sigma}=D_{\sigma}^{(1)}
.
The representation
\pi
is called
\sigma
-

if

.

As we see in Theorem
2.4
, the map

is closely related to the map

.
This motivates the following definition, analogous to Definition

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\pi\in\mathscr{R}(GL)
and let
\sigma\in\mathrm{Irr}(GL)

\sigma=Z_{\rho}[0,1]
for a supercuspidal
\rho

of

\displaystyle M^{*}(\pi)=\sigma^{k}\otimes M^{(k)}_{\sigma}(\pi)+\displaystyle\sum_{i}\pi_{i}\otimes\pi_{i}^{\prime},

Note that we do

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\pi\in\mathscr{R}(GL_{n})
and let
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(GL)
be supercuspidal. If
M_{\rho}(\pi)=0
, then for every constituent
\tau\otimes\sigma\leq M^{*}(\pi)
, the representation
\tau
is left
\rho
-reduced.

Proof.

Every such constituent
\tau
is a subquotient of
\tau_{3}^{\vee}\times\tau_{1}
, where
\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}\otimes\tau_{3}\leq[\mathrm{Jac}_{(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})}(\pi)]
with

. If
\tau
were not
\rho
-reduced, we would have
\rho\otimes\delta\leq[\mathrm{Jac}_{(d,d_{1}+d_{3}-d)}(\tau_{3}^{\vee}\times\tau_{1})]

\rho\otimes\delta^{\prime}\leq[\mathrm{Jac}_{(d,d_{3}-d)}(\tau_{3}^{\vee})]

\rho\otimes\delta^{\prime}\leq[\mathrm{Jac}_{(d,d_{1}-d)}(\tau_{1})]

appears in
[\mathrm{Jac}_{(0,n-d,d)}(\pi)]
, in the latter case a constituent

in
[\mathrm{Jac}_{(d,n-d,0)}(\pi)]

.
∎

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\pi_{1}\in\mathscr{R}(G)
and let

be either a supercuspidal representation or

Analogously, for
\pi_{2}\in\mathscr{R}(GL)

M^{(k)}_{\sigma}(\pi_{2})\neq 0

M^{(k)}_{\sigma}(\pi_{2})
the maximal

\pi_{2}
and set

Let us recall two important facts about the maximal derivatives from Section 3 in
[

For a representation

, a supercuspidal representation

Assume for the rest of this section that
\rho

\pi
is
\rho|\cdot|^{1}
-reduced, i.e.
D_{\rho|\cdot|^{1}}(\pi)=0

Definition 2.11
.

For a segment

\begin{cases}D^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{x}}\circ\ldots\circ D^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{y}}\text{, if }\rho\not\in[x,y]_{\rho}\\

(

Definition 2.13
.

Let
\pi\in\mathscr{R}(GL)
. Let
\sigma
be a supercuspidal representation or

\displaystyle\mathrm{mult}(D_{\sigma},\pi)\coloneqq k.

\displaystyle\mathrm{mult}(M_{\sigma},\pi)

\displaystyle\coloneqq k,

\displaystyle\mathrm{mult}(L_{\sigma},\pi)

\displaystyle\coloneqq k.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
\pi\in\mathscr{R}(GL)
and
\sigma\in\mathscr{R}(G)
. Let
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(GL)
be a supercuspidal representation. Then

\displaystyle D^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}}(\pi\rtimes\sigma)

\displaystyle=M^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}}(\pi)\rtimes D^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}}(\sigma),

\displaystyle\mathrm{mult}(D_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}},\pi\rtimes\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{mult}(M_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}},\pi)+\mathrm{mult}(D_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}},\sigma).

Assume now that
M_{\rho|\cdot|^{1}}(\pi)=0
and
D_{\rho|\cdot|^{1}}(\sigma)=0
(
\sigma
is
\rho|\cdot|^{1}
-reduced). Then

\displaystyle D^{\max}_{Z_{\rho}[0,1]}(\pi\rtimes\sigma)

\displaystyle=M^{\max}_{Z_{\rho}[0,1]}(\pi)\rtimes D^{\max}_{Z_{\rho}[0,1]}(\sigma),

\displaystyle\mathrm{mult}(D_{Z_{\rho}[0,1]},\pi\rtimes\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{mult}(M_{Z_{\rho}[0,1]},\pi)+\mathrm{mult}(D_{Z_{\rho}[0,1]},\sigma).

Proof.

Write

\displaystyle M^{*}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\pi_{1}^{(i)}\otimes\pi_{2}^{(i)},

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\tau_{1}^{(j)}\otimes\sigma_{2}^{(j)}.

By Theorem
2.4
we have

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(\pi\rtimes\sigma)

\displaystyle=M^{*}(\pi)\rtimes\mu^{*}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\left[(\pi_{1}^{(i)}\times\tau_{1}^{(j)})\otimes(\pi_{2}^{(i)}\rtimes\sigma_{2}^{(j)})\right].

Let us denote
k_{1}=\text{mult}(M_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}},\pi)
and
k_{2}=\text{mult}(D_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}},\sigma)
.
Now clearly

\displaystyle D^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}}(\pi\rtimes\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\\
\pi_{1}^{(i)}\cong(\rho|\cdot|^{z})^{k_{1}}\\
\tau_{1}^{(j)}\cong(\rho|\cdot|^{z})^{k_{2}}\end{subarray}}\left[\pi_{2}^{(i)}\rtimes\sigma_{2}^{(j)}\right]=M^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}}(\pi)\rtimes D^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{z}}(\sigma),

which also implies the equality for the multiplicities. For the second assertion, we denote

. Hence, we get

\displaystyle(Z_{\rho}[0,1])^{k}\leq(\pi_{1}^{(i)}\times\tau_{1}^{(j)}).

Now this shows that the supercuspidal support of both
\pi_{1}^{(i)}
and
\tau_{1}^{(j)}
consists only of
\rho
and
\rho|\cdot|^{1}
(with multiplicities). Therefore
\pi_{1}^{(i)}
is of the form

\displaystyle L(i_{1}\cdot[0,0]_{\rho}+j_{1}\cdot[1,1]_{\rho}+(k_{1}-i_{1}-j_{1})\cdot[1,0]_{\rho})

and
\tau_{1}^{(j)}
of the form

\displaystyle L(i_{2}\cdot[0,0]_{\rho}+j_{2}\cdot[1,1]_{\rho}+(k_{2}-i_{2}-j_{2})\cdot[1,0]_{\rho}).

Since
\sigma
is
\rho|\cdot|^{1}
-reduced, so is
\tau_{1}^{(j)}
. By Lemma
2.9
, we know that
\pi_{1}^{(i)}
is
\rho|\cdot|^{1}
-reduced as well. Now this implies that

and

as well as

and
i_{2}\geq j_{2}
. But since

, we have

and

. As in the proof of

\pi_{1}^{(i)}\cong(Z_{\rho}[0,1])^{i_{1}}
and
\tau_{1}^{(j)}\cong(Z_{\rho}[0,1])^{i_{2}}

∎

Remark 2.15
.

Most importantly, this implies

\displaystyle\left(D^{\max}_{Z_{\rho}[0,1]}\circ D^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{1}}\right)(\pi\rtimes\sigma)=(M^{\max}_{Z_{\rho}[0,1]}\circ M^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{1}})(\pi)\rtimes(D^{\max}_{Z_{\rho}[0,1]}\circ D^{\max}_{\rho|\cdot|^{1}})(\sigma).

(2.9)

Remark 2.18
.

Since we extensively use the operator
mult
, in this remark we will describe how it is applied in the most common setting in the paper. Namely, we often show that
\text{mult}(D^{\max}_{\sigma})
is the same for two irreducible representations which are related in some way.

Let
\sigma,\pi_{1},\pi_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Irr}(GL)
,
\sigma
supercuspidal and
\pi,\pi_{2}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
. Assume that
\pi_{1}\rtimes\pi_{2}
is an irreducible representation such that

\pi_{1}\rtimes\pi_{2}\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\prime}\rtimes\pi.

Moreover, assume that

\text{mult}(M_{\sigma},\pi_{1})=\text{mult}(M_{\sigma},\pi_{1}^{\prime})=:k_{1}
and
\pi_{1}^{\prime}\times\sigma
is an irreducible representation.
If we denote
k=\text{mult}(D_{\sigma},\pi)
and
k_{2}=\text{mult}(D_{\sigma},\pi_{2})
, using the assumptions we get:

\pi_{1}\rtimes\pi_{2}\hookrightarrow\sigma^{k_{1}+k}\times M^{\max}_{\sigma}(\pi_{1}^{\prime})\rtimes D^{\max}_{\sigma}(\pi).

From the Frobenius reciprocity, it follows
\text{mult}(D_{\sigma},\pi_{1}\rtimes\pi_{2})\geq k_{1}+k
.
On the other hand, the exactness of the Jacquet functor implies
\text{mult}(D^{\max}_{\sigma}(\pi_{1}\rtimes\pi_{2}))\leq k_{1}+k
.
Hence, we have the equalities

k_{1}+k_{2}=\text{mult}(D_{\sigma},\pi_{1}\rtimes\pi_{2})=k_{1}+k.

This implies
\text{mult}(D_{\sigma},\pi_{2})=\text{mult}(D_{\sigma},\pi).

Representations of Arthur type

3.1
Arthur parameters

Denote by
\hat{G}_{n}
the complex dual group of
G_{n}
. Namely,
\hat{G}_{n}=\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
if
G_{n}=\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}(F)
, and
\hat{G}_{n}=\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}(\mathbb{C})
if
G_{n}=\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(F)
. An Arthur parameter for
G_{n}
is the
\hat{G}_{n}
-conjugacy class of an admissible homomorphism

\psi:W_{F}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\hat{G}_{n},

such that the image of the Weil group
W_{F}
is bounded. By composing with the standard representation of
\hat{G}_{n}
, we can regard
\psi
as a representation of
W_{F}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. It decomposes as

\psi=\bigoplus_{\rho}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I_{\rho}}\rho\boxtimes S_{a_{i}}\boxtimes S_{b_{i}}\right),

where

•

\rho
runs over the equivalence classes of unitary irreducible supercuspidal representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F)
, which is identified with an irreducible bounded representation of
W_{F}
by the local Langlands correspondence for the general linear groups;

•

S_{a}
is the unique irreducible algebraic representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of dimension
a
.

For
\rho\in\mathrm{Cusp}^{\perp}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F))
, there exists a non-degenerate
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F)
-invariant bilinear form on
\rho
. We call
\rho

orthogonal
if this form is symmetric and
symplectic
if it is skew-symmetric.
Let
\psi
be as above. We say that
\psi
is of
good parity
if
\rho\boxtimes S_{a_{i}}\boxtimes S_{b_{i}}
is self-dual of the same type as
\psi
for any
\rho
and
i\in I_{\rho}
, i.e.,

•

\rho\in\mathrm{Cusp}^{\perp}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F))
is orthogonal and
a_{i}+b_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
if
G_{n}=\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(F)
(resp.
a_{i}+b_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
if
G_{n}=\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}(F)
); or

•

\rho\in\mathrm{Cusp}^{\perp}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F))
is symplectic and
a_{i}+b_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
if
G_{n}=\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(F)
(resp.
a_{i}+b_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
if
G_{n}=\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}(F)
).

Let
\Psi(G_{n})\supset\Psi^{\mathrm{gp}}(G_{n})
be the sets of equivalence classes of Arthur parameters and Arthur parameters of good parity, respectively. Also, we set
\Phi^{\mathrm{temp}}(G_{n})
to be the subset of
\Psi(G_{n})
consisting of tempered Arthur parameters, i.e., Arthur parameters
\psi
which are trivial on the second
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Finally, we set
\Phi^{\mathrm{gp}}(G_{n})=\Psi^{\mathrm{gp}}(G_{n})\cap\Phi^{\mathrm{temp}}(G_{n})
.

For
\psi=\bigoplus_{\rho}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I_{\rho}}\rho\boxtimes S_{a_{i}}\boxtimes S_{b_{i}}\right)\in\Psi^{\mathrm{gp}}(G_{n})
, define the enhanced component group by

\mathcal{A}_{\psi}=\bigoplus_{\rho}\bigoplus_{i\in I_{\rho}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})_{\alpha_{\rho,i}},

i.e.,
\mathcal{A}_{\psi}
is a
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
-vector space with a canonical basis
\alpha_{\rho,i}
corresponding to
\rho\boxtimes S_{a_{i}}\boxtimes S_{b_{i}}
. The component group
\mathcal{S}_{\psi}
is the quotient of
\mathcal{A}_{\psi}
by the subgroup generated by

•

\alpha_{\rho,i}+\alpha_{\rho,j}
such that
\rho\boxtimes S_{a_{i}}\boxtimes S_{b_{i}}=\rho\boxtimes S_{a_{j}}\boxtimes S_{b_{j}}
; and

•

z_{\psi}=\sum_{\rho}\sum_{i\in I_{\rho}}\alpha_{\rho,i}
, which is called the central element of
\mathcal{A}_{\psi}
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{S}_{\psi}}\subset\hat{\mathcal{A}_{\psi}}
be the Pontryagin duals of
\mathcal{S}_{\psi}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\psi}
, respectively. When
\varepsilon\in\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}
, we write
\varepsilon(\rho\boxtimes S_{a_{i}}\boxtimes S_{b_{i}})\coloneqq\varepsilon(\alpha_{\rho,i})\in\{\pm 1\}
.

3.2
Arthur packets

Let
\mathrm{Irr}^{\mathrm{unit}}(G_{n})
(resp.
\mathrm{Irr}^{\mathrm{temp}}(G_{n}))
be the set of equivalence classes of irreducible unitary (resp. tempered) representations of

. To an Arthur parameter

[
1

associated an Arthur packet

\mathrm{Irr}^{\mathrm{unit}}(G_{n})

\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{n})
is of Arthur type if
\pi\in\Pi_{\psi}
for some

is unitary (
[
1

). If
\psi
is of good parity, we say that
\pi
is of good parity as well.

Mœglin
[
20
]
showed that
\Pi_{\psi}
is multiplicity-free, i.e., a subset of
\mathrm{Irr}^{\mathrm{unit}}(G_{n})
. By
[
1
, Theorem 1.5.1(b)]

is a tempered Arthur parameter, then
\Pi_{\phi}
is a subset of
\mathrm{Irr}^{\mathrm{temp}}(G_{n})
and

\mathrm{Irr}^{\mathrm{temp}}(G_{n})=\bigsqcup_{\phi\in\Phi^{\mathrm{temp}}(G_{n})}\Pi_{\phi}\quad\text{(disjoint union)}.

However,
\Pi_{\psi_{1}}\cap\Pi_{\psi_{2}}\neq\emptyset
even if
\psi_{1}\not\cong\psi_{2}
in general.
If
\psi=\bigoplus_{\rho}\bigoplus_{i\in I_{\rho}}\rho\boxtimes S_{a_{i}}\boxtimes S_{b_{i}}
, set

\tau_{\psi}=\bigtimes_{\rho}\bigtimes_{i\in I_{\rho}}u_{\rho}(a_{i},b_{i})

to be a product of (unitary) Speh representations, which is an irreducible unitary representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)
with

.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
19
, Theorem 6]
,
[
31
, Proposition 8.11]
)
.

Any

can be decomposed as

\psi=\psi_{1}\oplus\psi_{0}\oplus\psi_{1}^{\vee},

where

•

\psi_{0}\in\Psi^{\mathrm{gp}}(G_{n_{0}})
;

•

\psi_{1}
is a direct sum of irreducible representations of

which are not self-dual of the same type as
\psi
.

For
\pi_{0}\in\Pi_{\psi_{0}}
, the parabolically induced representation
\tau_{\psi_{1}}\rtimes\pi_{0}
is irreducible and independent of the choice of
\psi_{1}
. Moreover,

\Pi_{\psi}=\{\tau_{\psi_{1}}\rtimes\pi_{0}\mid\pi_{0}\in\Pi_{\psi_{0}}\}.

Corollary 3.2
.

If
\psi
is an Arthur parameter of good parity,
\pi\in\Pi_{\psi}
and

a Speh representation such that

is not of good parity, then

Proof.

Since
\psi
is of good parity, define

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\rho\boxtimes S_{a}\boxtimes S_{b}

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\psi_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\psi\oplus(\psi_{1}^{\prime})^{\vee}.

Then Proposition
3.1
implies that

is irreducible.
∎

We also say that a Speh representation
u_{\rho}(a,b)
is of
good parity with respect to
\pi
, with the Arthur parameter
\psi
corresponding to
\pi
, if

\displaystyle\psi\oplus(\rho\boxtimes S_{a}\boxtimes S_{b})^{\oplus 2}

Throughout this paper, we implicitly fix a Whittaker datum for

. Let
\psi\in\Psi^{\mathrm{gp}}(G_{n})
so that we have defined the component group

. In
[
1

If

is tempered, this map is bijective. When
\pi\in\Pi_{\phi}
corresponds to
\epsilon\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\phi}

.

Extended multi-segments are an explicit way to parameterize the representations in a given Arthur packet. We define extended multi-segments in a slightly modified version of that given by Atobe in

(c)

\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\mu\equiv b\mod{2}
,

(d)

|\mu|\leq b
,

where
b\coloneqq A-B+1

We call the tuple

a

\displaystyle S_{i}^{\rho}=([A_{i}^{\rho},B_{i}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\mu_{i}^{\rho})

(b)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1})\left(\overline{\bigcup_{\rho\in C_{\mathcal{S}}}\left\{\left(\left[A_{i}^{\rho},B_{i}^{\rho}\right]_{\rho},l_{i}^{\rho},\eta_{i}^{\rho}\right)\right\}_{i\in I_{\rho}}}\right)

3.5
Parabolic induction of Speh and Arthur type representations

As we are working towards a proof of Theorem
1.2
, it is important to understand how representations of Arthur type behave with parabolic induction. If we are inducing with Speh representations, there is a description in terms of extended multi-segments, which we will give now.

Definition 3.24
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\in\mathrm{SRep}
. For an extended segment
([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)
, denote by
\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}
the extended multi-segment that is obtained by inserting
([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)
twice into
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}
at consecutive indices
i^{\prime}
and
i^{\prime}+1
, such that the resulting extended multi-segments is standard. That is, the sequence
(\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)})_{\rho}
is of the form

\displaystyle(([A_{1}^{\rho},B_{1}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\mu_{1}^{\rho}),\ldots,([C,D]_{\rho},\nu),([C,D]_{\rho},\nu),\ldots,([A_{n_{\rho}}^{\rho},B_{n_{\rho}}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\mu_{n_{\rho}}^{\rho}))

with
([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)
as the
i^{\prime}
-th and
(i^{\prime}+1)
-th element of the sequence. More generally, we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})}\coloneqq(\ldots(\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1})})_{([C_{2},D_{2}]_{\rho_{2}},\nu_{2})}\ldots)_{([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})}.

the extended multi-segment that is obtained by successively inserting each of the extended segments
([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})
twice into
\mathcal{S}_{\rho_{i}}
respectively such that the resulting extended multi-segment is standard. We call two identical consecutive extended segments a block.

Remark 3.25
.

Note that the position of the index in our definition differs from the one given in
[
3
, Theorem 4.4]
for
\mathcal{E}_{(l,\eta)}
, but the corresponding extended multi-segments are equivalent and the
\mu
-parameters coincide, except at the inserted extended segments, but that will be of no concern for us, because the decomposition in Proposition
3.27
sums over all allowed parameters (see the proof).

Lemma 3.26
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\in\mathrm{SRep}
. Let

([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho},\nu_{1}),\ldots,([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho},\nu_{k})

be extended segments (with the same supercuspidal representation
\rho
).

1.

For every permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,...,k\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{([C_{\sigma(1)},D_{\sigma(1)}]_{\rho},\nu_{\sigma(1)}),...,([C_{\sigma(k)},D_{\sigma(k)}]_{\rho},\nu_{\sigma(k)})}=\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho},\nu_{k})}.

2.

\mathcal{S}_{([C_{i},D_{i}]_{\rho},\nu_{i})}
is indeed an extended multi-segment, i.e. it satisfies the sign condition.

3.

Condition (
3.5
) is satisfied for
\mathcal{S}_{([C_{i},D_{i}]_{\rho},\nu_{i})}
if and only if it is also satisfied for
\mathcal{S}
and for the indices
i^{\prime}
and
i^{\prime}+1
of
([C_{i},D_{i}]_{\rho},\nu_{i})
in
\mathcal{S}_{([C_{i},D_{i}]_{\rho},\nu_{i})}
.

Proof.

1.

We consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho},\nu_{1}),([C_{2},D_{2}]_{\rho},\nu_{2})}=(\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho},\nu_{1})})_{([C_{2},D_{2}]_{\rho},\nu_{2})}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{([C_{2},D_{2}]_{\rho},\nu_{2}),([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho},\nu_{1})}=(\mathcal{S}_{([C_{2},D_{2}]_{\rho},\nu_{2})})_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho},\nu_{1})}.

These are the same, except when
D_{1}=D_{2}
and
C_{1}=C_{2}
, in which case it is easy to see that reordering the blocks does not change the parameters
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
. Now any permutation can be obtained through several such transpositions.

2.

The left hand side of the sign condition reads the same but with the addition of

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{\nu_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor+\nu_{i}\sum_{j<i^{\prime}}(b_{j}-1)+\left\lfloor\frac{\nu_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor+\nu_{i}\sum_{j<i^{\prime}+1}(b_{j}-1)\equiv\nu_{i}(b_{i}-1)\equiv 0\mod{2}.

This implies that the sign condition is the same for
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}_{([C_{i},D_{i}]_{\rho},\nu_{i})}
.

3.

This is clear.

∎

Proposition 3.27
.

(
[
3
, Theorem 4.4]
)

For
\mathcal{S}\in\mathrm{SRep}
,
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
\psi_{\mathcal{S}}\oplus(\rho\boxtimes S_{a}\boxtimes S_{b})^{\oplus 2}
is of good parity and
A=\frac{a+b}{2}-1
,
B=\frac{a-b}{2}
. We have that

\displaystyle u_{\rho}(a,b)\rtimes\pi(\mathcal{S})\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\mu|\leq b\\
\mu\equiv b\mod{2}\end{subarray}}\pi(\mathcal{S}_{([A,B]_{\rho},\mu)}).

Proof.

This is just a rewriting of Atobe’s theorem into our notation. Set
\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S})
. Then by
[
3
, Theorem 4.4]
, we have

\displaystyle u_{\rho}(a,b)\rtimes\pi(\mathcal{E})\cong\bigoplus_{(l,\eta)}\pi(\mathcal{E}_{(l,\eta)}),

where
(l,\eta)
sums over all pairs of parameters that make
([A,B]_{\rho},l,\eta)
an extended segment in the sense of
[
3
]
. Now
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{(l,\eta)}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{(l,\eta)})
is the extended multi-segment
\mathcal{S}
with the extended segment
([A,B]_{\rho},\delta_{i^{\prime\prime}}^{\rho}\eta(b-2l))
(we set
\mu(l,\eta)\coloneqq\delta_{i^{\prime\prime}}^{\rho}\eta(b-2l))
) added twice at the positions
i^{\prime\prime}
and
i^{\prime\prime}+1
of the sequence
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}
, with the property that
B_{j}^{\rho}>B
for
j\geq i^{\prime\prime}
and
B_{j}^{\rho}\leq B
for
j<i^{\prime\prime}
in
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}
(recall that
\mathcal{S}
is standard). Now to get the standard form of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{(l,\eta)}
, we have to reorder both extended segments
([A,B]_{\rho},\delta_{i^{\prime\prime}}^{\rho}\eta(b-2l))
with the extended segments
S_{i^{\prime\prime}-1},\ldots,S_{i^{\prime}}
in this order, where
i^{\prime}
the largest index such that
B_{i^{\prime}-1}^{\rho}<B
or
B_{i^{\prime}-1}^{\rho}=B
and
A_{i^{\prime}-1}^{\rho}\geq A
. Hence the standard form of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{(l,\eta)}
is given by
\mathcal{S}_{([A,B]_{\rho},\mu(l,\eta))}
, where we define

\displaystyle\mu(l,\eta)\coloneqq 2\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{i^{\prime\prime}-i^{\prime}-1}(-1)^{j}\mu_{i^{\prime}+j}^{\rho}+(-1)^{i^{\prime\prime}-i^{\prime}}(\delta_{i^{\prime\prime}}^{\rho}\eta(b-2l))\equiv b\mod{2}.

If
\pi(\mathcal{E}_{(l,\eta)})=\pi(\mathcal{S}_{(l,\eta)}^{\prime})\neq 0
, then
\pi(\mathcal{S}_{([A,B]_{\rho},\mu(l,\eta))})\cong\pi(\mathcal{S}_{(l,\eta)}^{\prime})
and
|\mu(l,\eta)|\leq b
(Proposition
3.14
). The mapping of parameters
\mu(l,\eta)
is injective except for
2l=b
and
\eta=\pm 1
, but this corresponds to the two equivalent extended multi-segments
\mathcal{E}_{(l,\eta)}
. On the other hand, if
\pi(\mathcal{S}_{([A,B]_{\rho},\mu)})\neq 0
, we have that
\mathcal{S}_{([A,B]_{\rho},\mu)}\sim\mathcal{S}_{(l,\eta)}^{\prime}
where
\mu(l,\eta)=\mu
and hence
\mathcal{E}_{(l,\eta)}
must be a valid extended multi-segment.
∎

Lemma 3.28
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{\rho\in C_{\mathcal{S}}}\{(([A_{i}^{\rho},B_{i}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\mu_{i}^{\rho}))_{i=1}^{n_{\rho}}\}\in\mathrm{AdExMult}
be standard. Let
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\rho\in\mathrm{Cusp}^{\perp}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F))
such that
\psi_{\mathcal{S}}\oplus(\rho\boxtimes S_{c}\boxtimes S_{d})^{\oplus 2}
is of good parity and
C=\frac{c+d}{2}-1
,
D=\frac{c-d}{2}
. Let
([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)
be an extended segment and let
1\leq i<j\leq n_{\rho}
such that for the index
i^{\prime}
of the first
([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)
in
(\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)})_{\rho}
, we have
i<i^{\prime}\leq j
. Assume that
S_{i}^{\rho}
and
S_{j}^{\rho}
are connected in
\mathcal{S}
.

1.

If
S_{i}^{\rho}
and
S_{j}^{\rho}
are connected in
\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}
, then

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho})=\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho}).

2.

If
S_{i}^{\rho}
and
S_{j}^{\rho}
are not connected in
\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}
, then
S_{i}^{\rho}
and
([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)
as well as
([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)
and
S_{j}^{\rho}
are connected and

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho})|

\displaystyle\leq|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\nu)|+|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\nu,\mu_{j}^{\rho})|.

Proof.

1.

We have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho})=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=i}^{i^{\prime}-2}(-1)^{|\{m<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{m+1}^{\rho}-\mu_{m}^{\rho})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\{i^{\prime}-1<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\nu-\mu_{i^{\prime}-1}^{\rho})+(-1)^{|\{i^{\prime}-1<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{i^{\prime}}^{\rho}-\nu)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{m=i^{\prime}}^{j-1}(-1)^{|\{m<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{m+1}^{\rho}-\mu_{m}^{\rho})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=i}^{j-1}(-1)^{|\{m<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{m+1}^{\rho}-\mu_{m}^{\rho})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho}).

2.

There are two cases: If
A_{i}^{\rho}<A_{j}^{\rho}
, we must have
A_{i}^{\rho}<C<A_{j}^{\rho}
. Note that for
i^{\prime}\leq k<j
, we have

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\Leftrightarrow C\geq A_{k}^{\rho},

since otherwise
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
would not be connected. Similarly, if
A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{j}^{\rho}
, we must have
A_{i}^{\rho}\geq C\geq A_{j}^{\rho}
and for
i^{\prime}\leq k<j
, we have

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\Leftrightarrow C\geq A_{k}^{\rho},

since otherwise
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
would not be connected. In either case, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=i}^{j-1}(-1)^{|\{m<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{m+1}^{\rho}-\mu_{m}^{\rho})

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=i}^{i^{\prime}-2}(-1)^{|\{m<k<i^{\prime}|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{m+1}^{\rho}-\mu_{m}^{\rho})+(\nu-\mu_{i^{\prime}-1}^{\rho})

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\nu,\mu_{j}^{\rho})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\{i^{\prime}-1<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{i^{\prime}}^{\rho}-\nu)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=i^{\prime}}^{j-1}(-1)^{|\{m<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{m+1}^{\rho}-\mu_{m}^{\rho}).

Now we note

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho})|=\left|\sum_{m=i}^{i^{\prime}-2}(-1)^{|\{m<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{m+1}^{\rho}-\mu_{m}^{\rho})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(-1)^{|\{i^{\prime}-1<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{i^{\prime}}^{\rho}-\mu_{i^{\prime}-1}^{\rho})+\sum_{m=i^{\prime}}^{j-1}(-1)^{|\{m<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{m+1}^{\rho}-\mu_{m}^{\rho})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|(-1)^{|\{i^{\prime}-1<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}\left(\sum_{m=i}^{i^{\prime}-2}(-1)^{|\{m<k<i^{\prime}|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{m+1}^{\rho}-\mu_{m}^{\rho})+(\nu-\mu_{i^{\prime}-1}^{\rho})\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(-1)^{|\{i^{\prime}-1<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{i^{\prime}}^{\rho}-\nu)+\sum_{m=i^{\prime}}^{j-1}(-1)^{|\{m<k<j|\ A_{i}^{\rho}\geq A_{k}^{\rho}\}|}(\mu_{m+1}^{\rho}-\mu_{m}^{\rho})\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\nu)|+|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\nu,\mu_{j}^{\rho})|.

∎

Lemma 3.29
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{\rho\in C_{\mathcal{S}}}\{(([A_{i}^{\rho},B_{i}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\mu_{i}^{\rho}))_{i=1}^{n_{\rho}}\}\in\mathrm{AdExMult}
be standard. Let
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\rho\in\mathrm{Cusp}^{\perp}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F))
such that
\psi_{\mathcal{S}}\oplus(\rho\boxtimes S_{c}\boxtimes S_{d})^{\oplus 2}
is of good parity and
C=\frac{c+d}{2}-1
,
D=\frac{c-d}{2}
. Let
([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)
be an extended segment.
We write the sequence
(\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)})_{\rho}
as

\displaystyle(([A_{1}^{\rho},B_{1}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\mu_{1}^{\rho}),\ldots,([C,D]_{\rho},\nu),([C,D]_{\rho},\nu),\ldots,([A_{n_{\rho}}^{\rho},B_{n_{\rho}}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\mu_{n_{\rho}}^{\rho}))

with
([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)
as the
i^{\prime}
-th and
(i^{\prime}+1)
-th element of the sequence. Then
\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}\in\mathrm{SRep}
if and only

1.

\mathcal{S}\in\mathrm{SRep}
.

2.

Condition (
3.5
) is satisfied for
(\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)})_{\rho}
at the index
i^{\prime}
.

3.

For all extended segments
S_{i}^{\rho}=([A_{i}^{\rho},B_{i}^{\rho}],\mu_{i}^{\rho})
appearing in
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}
such that
S_{i}^{\rho}\sim([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)
in
\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}
, it holds that

\displaystyle|C-A_{i}^{\rho}|+|D-B_{i}^{\rho}|\geq|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\nu)|.

Proof.

For the first direction, assume
\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}\in\mathrm{SRep}
. Items 2. and 3. follow directly from Theorem
3.20
. To prove 1., we use Theorem again
3.20
: Assume
1\leq i<j\leq n_{\rho}
such that
S_{i}^{\rho}
and
S_{j}^{\rho}
are connected in
\mathcal{S}
. If it is not the case that
i<i^{\prime}\leq j
, then
\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho})=\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho})
and the desired inequality (
3.8
) holds. If
i<i^{\prime}\leq j
, there are two cases: If
S_{i}^{\rho}
and
S_{j}^{\rho}
are connected in
\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}
, we have
\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho})=\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho})
by 1. in Lemma
3.28
. If
S_{i}^{\rho}
and
S_{j}^{\rho}
are not connected in
\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}
, by 2. in Lemma
3.28

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\mu_{j}^{\rho})|

\displaystyle\leq|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\nu)|+|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\nu,\mu_{j}^{\rho})|

\displaystyle\leq|C-A_{i}^{\rho}|+|D-B_{i}^{\rho}|+|A_{j}^{\rho}-C|+|B_{j}^{\rho}-D|

\displaystyle=|A_{j}^{\rho}-A_{i}^{\rho}|+|B_{j}^{\rho}-B_{i}^{\rho}|.

For the other direction, the only case of Theorem
3.20
we have to consider is if

and

are connected in

Corollary 3.30
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{\rho\in C_{\mathcal{S}}}\{(([A_{i}^{\rho},B_{i}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\mu_{i}^{\rho}))_{i=1}^{n_{\rho}}\}\in\mathrm{SRep}
and let
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\rho\in\mathrm{Cusp}^{\perp}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F))
such that
\psi_{\mathcal{S}}\oplus(\rho\boxtimes S_{c}\boxtimes S_{d})^{\oplus 2}
is of good parity and
C=\frac{c+d}{2}-1
,
D=\frac{c-d}{2}
. Let
I\subset\{1,\ldots,n_{\rho}\}
be the indices such that

S_{i}^{\rho}\sim([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)

in
\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}
(for any
\nu
) and denote for
i\in I
by
N_{i}
the set

\{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}|\ |C-A_{i}^{\rho}|+|D-B_{i}^{\rho}|\geq|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}}(\mu_{i}^{\rho},\nu)|\}.

If we denote

\displaystyle N\coloneqq\{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}|\ |\nu|\leq d,\ |\hat{\nu}|\leq c,\ \nu\equiv d\mod{2}\}\cap\bigcap_{i\in I}N_{i},

then

\displaystyle u_{\rho}(c,d)\rtimes\pi(\mathcal{S})\cong\bigoplus_{\nu\in N}\pi(\mathcal{S}_{([C,D]_{\rho},\nu)}).

Moreover
u_{\rho}(c,d)\rtimes\pi(\mathcal{S})
is irreducible if and only if
N
is a singleton. The set
N
has the form
\{\nu,\nu+2,\ldots,\ \nu+2n\}
for some integers
\nu
and
n
.

Proof.

The first part is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.27
and Lemma
3.29
. Since all inequalities that elements
\nu
of
N
must satisfy are of the form
|\nu-x|\leq y
, their intersection is an interval of
\mathbb{R}
. Intersecting with
2\mathbb{Z}+d
shows that
N
has the stated form.
∎

For the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we also need to understand how parabolic induction with multiple Speh representations behaves with representations of Arthur type. We have the following:

Theorem 3.31
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{\rho\in C_{\mathcal{S}}}\{(([A_{i}^{\rho},B_{i}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\mu_{i}^{\rho}))_{i=1}^{n_{\rho}}\}\in\mathrm{SRep}

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathcal{S}}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\rho_{i}\boxtimes S_{c_{i}}\boxtimes S_{d_{i}})^{\oplus 2}

\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})}\in\mathrm{SRep}

if and only if

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{([C_{i},D_{i}]_{\rho_{i}},\nu_{i})}\in\mathrm{SRep}

for

and

\displaystyle|C_{l}-C_{m}|+|D_{l}-D_{m}|\geq|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})}}(\nu_{m},\nu_{l})|

and
([C_{m},D_{m}]_{\rho_{m}},\nu_{m})
of extended segments with

\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})}\in\mathrm{SRep}

Proof.

First, we prove the “
\Leftarrow
” -direction:

We prove the statement by induction over
k
. If
k=1
, this is trivial. Assume now we have

\displaystyle|C_{l}-C_{m}|+|D_{l}-D_{m}|\geq|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})}}(\nu_{m},\nu_{l})|

for all pairs
([C_{l},D_{l}]_{\rho_{l}},\nu_{l})
and
([C_{m},D_{m}]_{\rho_{m}},\nu_{m})
of extended segments with
\rho_{l}\cong\rho_{m}
that are connected in
\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})}
. By Lemma
3.28
, this implies that

\displaystyle|C_{l}-C_{m}|+|D_{l}-D_{m}|\geq|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k-1},D_{k-1}]_{\rho_{k-1}},\nu_{k-1})}}(\nu_{m},\nu_{l})|

for all pairs
([C_{l},D_{l}]_{\rho_{l}},\nu_{l})
and
([C_{m},D_{m}]_{\rho_{m}},\nu_{m})
of extended segments with
\rho_{l}\cong\rho_{m}
that are connected in
\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{k-1},D_{k-1}]_{\rho_{k-1}},\nu_{k-1})}
.
By induction hypothesis we have

\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{i},D_{i}]_{\rho_{i}},\nu_{i}),...,([C_{k-1},D_{k-1}]_{\rho_{k-1}},\nu_{k-1})}\in\mathrm{SRep}.

Now we want to apply Lemma
3.29
to

\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{i},D_{i}]_{\rho_{i}},\nu_{i}),...,([C_{k-1},D_{k-1}]_{\rho_{k-1}},\nu_{k-1})}

and the extended segment
([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})
.
Condition 1. and 3. are satisfied. For condition 2., we have to prove that condition (
3.5
) is satisfied at the extended segments
([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})
. But this follows from

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{([C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}},\nu_{k})}\in\mathrm{SRep},

Corollary 3.33
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\in\mathrm{SRep}
and let
k\geq 2
be an integer. Let
c_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{Cusp}^{\perp}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(F))
such that

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathcal{S}}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\rho_{i}\boxtimes S_{c_{i}}\boxtimes S_{d_{i}})^{\oplus 2}

is of good parity and such that
(c_{i},d_{i})\neq(c_{j},d_{j})
if
i\neq j
. Assume moreover that
u_{\rho_{i}}(c_{i},d_{i})\rtimes\pi(\mathcal{S})
is reducible for each
i=1,\ldots,k
. Then there is one of the Speh representations
u_{\rho_{i}}(c_{i},d_{i})
and a constituent
\pi^{\prime}
of

\displaystyle\bigtimes_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{k}u_{\rho_{j}}(c_{j},d_{j})\rtimes\pi(\mathcal{S}),

such that

\displaystyle u_{\rho_{i}}(c_{i},d_{i})\rtimes\pi^{\prime}

is reducible.

Proof.

Let us denote

\displaystyle N_{i}\coloneqq\{\nu_{i}\in 2\mathbb{Z}+d_{i}|\ \mathcal{S}_{([C_{i},D_{i}],\nu_{i})}\in\mathrm{SRep}\},

which consist by Corollary
3.30
of consecutive integers of same parity. By assumption
|N_{i}|\geq 2
for all
i
. The statement we want to prove is equivalent to the fact that
N(\mathcal{S},[C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},...,[C_{k},D_{k}]_{\rho_{k}})
contains two elements
(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{i-1},\nu_{i},\nu_{i+1},\ldots,\nu_{k})
and
(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{i-1},\nu_{i}+2,\nu_{i+1},\ldots,\nu_{k})
for some
i
. We call these
adjacent lattice points
. We prove it by induction over
k
.

If
k=2
, we set

. If the two extended segments are not connected, we are done. Else, according to Theorem
3.31
,

is given by

\displaystyle N=\{(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\in N_{1}\times N_{2}|\ |\Delta_{\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),([C_{2},D_{2}]_{\rho_{2}},\nu_{2})}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})|\leq|C_{2}-C_{1}|+|D_{2}-D_{1}|\}.

This inequality is of the shape

\displaystyle|\nu_{1}+\varepsilon\nu_{2}+x|\leq y,

where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
and
x,y
are integers with
y\geq 1
. Moreover, it holds that
\nu_{1}+\varepsilon\nu_{2}+x\equiv y\mod{2}
.
Let
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\in N
. Define

\displaystyle\delta\coloneqq-\mathrm{sgn}(\nu_{1}+\varepsilon\nu_{2}+x)

with the convention
\mathrm{sgn}(0)=1
. If
\nu_{1}+2\delta\in N_{1}
, we are done, because
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
and
(\nu_{1}+2\delta,\nu_{2})
are then adjacent lattice points in
N
. Similarly if
\nu_{2}+2\delta\varepsilon\in N_{2}
. So let us assume that neither is true. Since
|N_{1}|\geq 2
, we have
\nu_{1}-2\delta\in N_{1}
. There must be some
\nu_{2}^{\prime}\in N_{2}
such that
(\nu_{1}-2\delta,\nu_{2}^{\prime})\in N
. This means that the inequality

\displaystyle|\nu_{1}-2\delta+\varepsilon\nu_{2}^{\prime}+x|\leq y

holds. Now

\displaystyle\nu_{1}-2\delta+\varepsilon\nu_{2}^{\prime}+x

\displaystyle=(\nu_{1}-2\delta+\varepsilon\nu_{2}+x)+\varepsilon(\nu_{2}^{\prime}-\nu_{2})

\displaystyle=(\nu_{1}-2\delta+\varepsilon\nu_{2}+x)+(-2\delta n)

for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and both bracketed expressions have the same sign. This implies

\displaystyle|\nu_{1}-2\delta+\varepsilon\nu_{2}+x|\leq y

and hence
(\nu_{1}-2\delta,\nu_{2})\in N
, which is an adjacent lattice point to
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
.

Assume now that it holds for
k-1
. This means that there must be some
i
such that

(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{i-1},\nu_{i},\nu_{i+1},\ldots,\nu_{k-1})

and

(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{i-1},\nu_{i}+2,\nu_{i+1},\ldots,\nu_{k-1})

are in
N(\mathcal{S},[C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},...,[C_{k-1},D_{k-1}]_{\rho_{k-1}})
. We set

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{S}_{([C_{1},D_{1}]_{\rho_{1}},\nu_{1}),...,([C_{i-1},D_{i-1}]_{\rho_{i-1}},\nu_{i-1}),([C_{i+1},D_{i+1}]_{\rho_{i+1}},\nu_{i+1}),...,([C_{k-1},D_{k-1}]_{\rho_{k-1}},\nu_{k-1})},

which must be in
\mathrm{SRep}
. Moreover, we denote

\displaystyle N_{j}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{\nu_{j}^{\prime}\in 2\mathbb{Z}+d_{j}|\ \mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{([C_{j},D_{j}],\nu_{j}^{\prime})}\in\mathrm{SRep}\}

for
j=i,k
. By induction hypothesis, we have
\nu_{i},\nu_{i}+2\in N_{i}^{\prime}
. If
|N_{k}^{\prime}|=1
(it must be non-empty), the lattice points

(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{i-1},\nu_{i},\nu_{i+1},\ldots,\nu_{k-1},\nu_{k})

and

(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{i-1},\nu_{i}+2,\nu_{i+1},\ldots,\nu_{k-1},\nu_{k})

are adjacent (where

) and must be in

. Else, if

we see the existence of two adjacent lattice points in the same set by application of the basis of induction on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
∎

4.1
Outline of the proof

One direction of this theorem is very easy to see. Namely, assume

is an irreducible representation. Assume (
Irr
) we false. If they exist, let
i<j
be minimal indices such that

were reducible because of the exactness of parabolic induction, a contradiction. If no such indices exist, we have

\displaystyle u_{1}\times\ldots\times u_{i}\times\ldots\times u_{r}\rtimes\pi

which implies that

4.2
General statements

Let us prove a slightly altered irreducibility criterion of Atobe in
[
3

, which allows us to reduce the proof of irreducibility to socle irreducibility in many of the following cases (this is a standard argument in the theory):

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\tau\in\mathrm{Irr}(GL)
and
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
.
Then
\tau\rtimes\pi
is irreducible if and only if all of the following conditions hold:

(i)

\mathrm{soc}(\tau\rtimes\pi)
is irreducible,

(ii)

\mathrm{soc}(\tau^{\vee}\rtimes\pi)
is irreducible,

(iii)

\mathrm{soc}(\tau\rtimes\pi)\cong\mathrm{soc}(\tau^{\vee}\rtimes\pi)
,

(iv)

\mathrm{soc}(\tau\rtimes\pi)
appears in the composition series of the representation
\tau\rtimes\pi
with multiplicity one.

Proof.

Note that if we assume that the representation
\tau\rtimes\pi
is irreducible, then all four conditions follow directly.
Namely, an irreducible representation is isomorphic to its socle and, in the Grothendieck group
\mathscr{R}(G)
, we have
\tau\rtimes\pi=\tau^{\vee}\rtimes\pi.

On the other hand, let us assume the four conditions from the statement of the proposition hold.
We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{soc}\left(\tau\rtimes\pi\right)\cong

\displaystyle\mathrm{soc}\left(\tau^{\vee}\rtimes\pi\right)\cong\mathrm{cosoc}\left(\tau\rtimes\pi^{\vee}\right)^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\mathrm{cosoc}\left(\tau\rtimes\pi^{\vee}\right)^{\mathrm{MVW}}\cong\mathrm{cosoc}\left(\tau\rtimes\pi\right).

Now the socle and cosocle of
\tau\rtimes\pi
are isomorphic, irreducible, and appear in its semisimplification with multiplicity one, which implies that the induced is irreducible.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer, let

Next, we will show how we can reduce the proof irreducibility of

to the case where there are no unitary Spehs

4.11

\pi_{\mu^{\prime}}
be an irreducible summand of

\displaystyle u_{\rho_{i}}(a_{i},b_{i})\rtimes\pi_{\mu^{\prime}}

, we have the following characterization:

\mathrm{soc}(u_{\rho}(2s,b)|\cdot|^{a/2}\rtimes\left(\bigtimes_{i=1}^{n}u_{\rho_{i}}(a_{i},b_{i})\rtimes\pi_{\mu^{\prime}}\right))

if and only if for the non-negative integers

We will now proceed to prove Theorem

Lemma 4.17

i,j

Assume that
\sigma
is an irreducible subquotient of
u_{\rho_{k}}(2s_{k},b_{k})|\cdot|^{a_{k}/2}\rtimes\delta

By definition of the derivatives, we have that the representation

\displaystyle\tau_{\sigma}\coloneqq(\rho_{k}|\cdot|^{B_{k}+s_{k}})^{(k_{1,1}+1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes(\rho_{k}|\cdot|^{A_{k}-s_{k}+1})^{(k_{2s_{k},b_{k}}+1)}\otimes D^{\max}_{(a_{k},b_{k},s_{k})}(\sigma)

\sigma

To see this, note that for

\displaystyle\tau_{\delta}\coloneqq(\rho_{k}|\cdot|^{B_{k}+s_{k}})^{(k_{1,1}+1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes(\rho_{k}|\cdot|^{A_{k}-s_{k}+1})^{(k_{2s_{k},b_{k}}+1)}\otimes D^{\max}_{(a_{k},b_{k},s_{k})}(\delta)

A.2
The Aubert dual and deformation

Atobe describes further constructions in terms of extended multi-segments. We translate them into our slightly altered definition. In
[
8
]
, Aubert defines an involution on
\mathrm{Irr}(G_{n})
, known as the
Aubert dual
. As outlined in
[
2
, Section 6]
the Aubert dual can be defined as follows:

Definition A.3
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G_{n}
. There exists a sign
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
such that the Aubert dual

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}=\varepsilon\sum_{P}(-1)^{\mathrm{dim}A_{M}}[\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G_{n}}(\mathrm{Jac}_{P}(\pi))]

is again an irreducible representation of
G_{n}
, where
P=MN
runs over all standard parabolic subgroups of
G_{n}
and
A_{M}
is the maximal split torus of the center of
M
.

Given a representation
\pi(\mathcal{E})
of Arthur type, Atobe gives a formula for an extended multi-segment
\hat{\mathcal{E}}
such that
\pi(\hat{\mathcal{E}})
is the Aubert dual of
\pi(\mathcal{E})
. We will recall this formula in our notation (i.e. we describe
\hat{\mathcal{S}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\hat{\mathcal{E}})
):

Definition A.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{\rho\in C_{\mathcal{S}}}\{(([A_{i}^{\rho},B_{i}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\mu_{i}^{\rho}))_{i=1}^{n_{\rho}}\}\in\mathrm{AdExMult}
. Define

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{S}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\rho\in C_{\mathcal{S}}}\{(([A_{n_{\rho}-i+1}^{\rho},-B_{n_{\rho}-i+1}^{\rho}]_{\rho},\hat{\mu}_{n_{\rho}-i+1}^{\rho}))_{i=1}^{n_{\rho}}\},

where

\displaystyle\hat{\mu_{i}}^{\rho}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\mu_{i}\qquad&if\ B_{i}^{\rho}\in\mathbb{Z},\\
\mu_{i}-1\qquad&if\ B_{i}^{\rho}\not\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{matrix}\right.

Theorem A.5
.

(
[
2
, Theorem 6.2]
)

If
\mathcal{S}\in\mathrm{Rep}
, its Aubert dual is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\pi(\mathcal{S})}\cong\pi(\hat{\mathcal{S}}).

Atobe gives another equivalence relation of extended multisegments, that leaves the emerging representation invariant. It is called
deformation
and described in
[
2
, Section 5.2]
. We translate it into our definition:

Let
\mathcal{S}\in\mathrm{Rep}
be an extended multi-segment. Let
S_{k-1}=([A_{k-1},B_{k-1}]_{\rho},\mu_{k-1})
and
S_{k}=([A_{k},B_{k}]_{\rho},\mu_{k})
be consecutive extended segments of
\mathcal{S}
where
A_{k}\geq A_{k-1}
and
B_{k}\geq B_{k-1}
such that

\displaystyle|A_{k}-A_{k-1}|+|B_{k}-B_{k-1}|=|\mu_{k}-\mu_{k-1}|.

Replace them by

\displaystyle S_{k-1}^{\prime}\coloneqq([A_{k},B_{k-1}]_{\rho},\mu_{k-1}-\mathrm{sgn}(\mu_{k}-\mu_{k-1})(A_{k}-A_{k-1}))

and

\displaystyle S_{k}^{\prime}\coloneqq([A_{k-1},B_{k}]_{\rho},\mu_{k}-\mathrm{sgn}(\mu_{k}-\mu_{k-1})(A_{k}-A_{k-1}))

to define
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
(with the convention
\mathrm{sgn}(0)=1
, although it is irrelevant). By
[
4
, Theorem 3.5]
it follows that for
\mathcal{S}\in\mathrm{Rep}
, we have
\pi(\mathcal{S})\cong\pi(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.

New C 0 C^{0} interior penalty method for Monge-Ampère equations
Tianyang Chu, Hailong Guo, Zhimin Zhang

Abstract
Monge-Ampère equation is a prototype second-order fully nonlinear partial differential equation. In this paper, we propose a new idea to design and analyze the C 0 C^{0} interior penalty method to approximation the viscosity solution of the Monge-Ampère equation. The new methods is inspired from the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate. Based on the vanishing moment representation, we approximate the Monge-Ampère equation by the fourth order semi-linear equation with some additional boundary conditions. We will use the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimates to ensure the well-posedness of the numerical scheme and derive the error estimates.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the numerical approximation of the following fully nonlinear Monge-Ampère equation with Dirichlet boundary condition

\displaystyle\det(D^{2}u)

\displaystyle=f\quad\text{in $\varOmega$},

(1a)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=g\quad\text{on $\partial\varOmega$},

(1b)

where
\varOmega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
is a bounded polygonal convex domain with boundary
\varGamma=\partial\varOmega
, and
\det(D^{2}u)
denotes the determinant of the Hessian matrix
D^{2}u
.

The Monge-Ampère equation serves as a prototypical example of fully nonlinear partial differential equations
[
10
]
. It arises in many important applications such as differential geometry
[
29
,
24
]
, the reflector design problem
[
43
]
, and optimal transports
[
42
,
3
]
. For the Monge-Ampère equation, the classical solution may not exist on a convex domain even though functions
f
and
g
are smooth
[
27
]
. Given the fully nonlinear nature of (
1
), traditional weak solution theories based on variational calculus are not directly applicable. Consequently, alternative solution concepts such as Aleksandrov solutions and viscosity solutions have emerged. The viscosity solution theory of Monge-Ampère equation have been well developed in the last half century. For comprehensive insights into these theories and related developments, readers are referred to the monographs
[
10
,
24
,
29
,
27
]
and the references therein.

The development of numerical methods for the Monge-Ampère equation falls behind its PDE theory. In 1988, Oliker and Prussner
[
39
]
constructed the first finite difference methods to compute the Aleksandrov solutions. The first practical numerical method for the Monge-Ampère equation came 20 years later when Oberman proposed the wide stencil finite difference method in 2008
[
38
]
based on the framework of Barles and Souganidis
[
2
]
. Since then, there have been extensive advances in the numerical methods for the Monge-Ampère equation. Famous examples include filter schemes
[
25
]
,
L^{2}
-projection methods
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
32
,
1
]
, least squares methods
[
9
,
14
]
, vanishing moment methods
[
19
,
20
,
31
,
12
,
21
,
22
]
, two-scale finite element methods
[
37
,
36
]
, and et al. For comprehensive lists of numerical methods for the the Monge-Ampère equation, interested readers are referred to the recent review papers
[
17
,
33
,
34
]
.

The main purpose of our paper is to design a new
C^{0}
interior penalty (C0IP) method using the vanishing moment approximation of (
1
)
[
19
,
20
,
31
]
. For this end, we approximate equation (
1
) by the following fourth-order semi-linear partial differential equation (PDE) with an additional boundary condition:

\displaystyle-\epsilon\varDelta^{2}u^{\epsilon}+\det(D^{2}u^{\epsilon})=f

\displaystyle\quad\text{in $\varOmega$},

(2a)

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}=g

\displaystyle\quad\text{on $\partial\varOmega$},

(2b)

\displaystyle\varDelta u^{\epsilon}=\epsilon

\displaystyle\quad\text{on $\partial\varOmega$}.

(2c)

For the PDE theory of (
2
), it has been proven in the two-dimensional case
[
21
]
and in the
d

d\geq 2)
radially symmetric case
[
23
]
that for any
\epsilon>0
and
f>0
, (
2
) has a unique convex solution
u^{\epsilon}
. Moreover,
u^{\epsilon}
converges uniformly as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, and we have the following
a priori
bounds:

\displaystyle\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{j}(\varOmega)}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{\frac{1-j}{2}})\ (j=2,\;3),

\displaystyle\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{W^{j,\infty}(\varOmega)}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{1-j})\ (j=1,\;2),

(3)

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}),

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1}),

where
\Phi^{\epsilon}
denotes the cofactor matrix of
D^{2}u^{\epsilon}
.
Based on these findings, we make the following assumption on the solution
u^{\epsilon}
:

Assumption 1
.

For problem (
2
), we assume that there exists a unique convex solution
u^{\epsilon}
such that
u^{\epsilon}\in H^{3}(\varOmega)\cap W^{2,\infty}(\varOmega)
, and satisfies (
3
).

The classical finite element methods for fourth-order elliptic PDEs, such as the
C^{1}
conforming finite element method
[
22
]
, the Morley nonconforming finite element method
[
31
]
, mixed finite element method
[
19
]
, and the recovery-based linear finite element method
[
12
]
, have been utilized to solve the semi-linear problem (
2
). Compared to the aforementioned finite element methods, the C0IP method using standard Lagrange elements stands out for its flexibility and ease of implementation, making it a preferred choice for high-order PDE solvers
[
8
,
15
]
. However, extending C0IP methods to address the model problem (
2
) is not straightforward. Although some numerical results are presented in
[
34
]
, the convergence analysis of the C0IP method remains an
open problem
, as stated in the review paper
[
34
]
.

In this paper, we try to answer this open question by proposing a new C0IP method with erorr analysis.
Our methodology for designing the new C0IP methods is to use the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate
[
35
]
as the main analytic tool. The discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate was originally used to develop numerical methods for the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation with Cordes coefficients
[
35
,
40
,
41
]
. Although the Monge-Ampére equation (
1
) can be reformulated as an HJB equation
[
18
,
26
]
, the numerical methods for the HJB equation
[
40
,
41
,
35
]
including the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate can be used.
To the best of our knowledge, it is the first time the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate has been used to design a C0IP method to compute the viscosity solution of (
1
). The discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate not only inspires us to design a new C0IP method for the nonlinear equation with linear jumps only on interior edges and averaging terms but also allows us to establish a discrete Sobolev inequality, which is a key ingredient in the fixed-point argument.

Compared to the existing C0IP method for the Monge-Ampere equation in
[
5
,
6
,
32
]
, the new proposed method has several advantages. First, our penalty only involves linear terms, contrasting with the fact that nonlinear penalties are needed to ensure stability for the methods in
[
5
,
6
,
32
]
. Second, our method is designed to compute the viscosity solution, which requires lower regularity for convergence compared to the existing methods that compute classical solutions and may fail for singular solutions. Third, the weak formulation is much simpler, especially in 3D cases.

The remaining sections are organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notations for finite element spaces and present the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate. Section
3
is dedicated to designing and analyzing a C0IP formulation for the linearized Monge-Ampère equation, leveraging the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate. In Section
4
, we first formulate the new C0IP method; then, we prove well-posedness and establish error estimates for the discrete formulation using the Bownder fixed-point technique. Section
5
demonstrates the performance of the proposed method through a series of numerical examples. Finally, some conclusions are drawn in Section
6
.

2.1
Notations

Let
|D|
represent the measure of a measurable set
D

W^{s,p}(D)

[
16
]
on the domain

s=0
or
p=2
, we have
W^{0,p}(D):=L^{p}(D)
and

. The norm and semi-norm on
W^{s,p}(D)
are denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s,p}(D)}
and

H_{0}^{1}(D)
be the subspace of
H^{1}(D)
that comprises functions vanishing on
\partial D
. The inner product on
L^{2}(D)
is denoted as
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(D)}
. We introduce the following notation:

V:=H^{2}(\varOmega),\quad V^{0}:=H^{2}(\varOmega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\varOmega),\quad V^{g}:=\{v\in V;\,v|_{\partial\varOmega}=g\}.

Let

be a quasi-uniform, shape-regular, and conforming simplicial triangulation of
\varOmega

[
7
,
13
]
. For each

, we define
h_{K}:=\text{diam}(K)
and

. The set of interior edges/faces is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{b}
denotes the set of boundary edges/faces. Similarly, we define
h_{F}:=\text{diam}(F)
for any
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}
.
The jump of a vector-valued function
{\bm{w}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
on an interior edge/face

is defined as:

\llbracket{\bm{w}}\rrbracket|_{F}={\bm{w}}^{+}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{+}|_{F}+{\bm{w}}^{-}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{-}|_{F},

where
{\bm{n}}_{+}
and
{\bm{n}}_{-}
denote the unit outward normals of
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
, respectively.
Additionally, the average of a piecewise smooth function

\{\hskip-3.99994pt\{w\}\hskip-3.99994pt\}|_{F}=\frac{1}{2}\left(w^{+}|_{F}+w^{-}|_{F}\right).

Let

denote the space of polynomials of degree less than or equal to

\mathbb{P}_{k}(\varOmega;\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\varOmega):v|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)\text{ for all }K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

The norm

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}^{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}.

Similar notation is used for

. Furthermore, we define the inner product

for the piecewise smooth function space as

(u,v)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(u,v)_{L^{2}(K)}.

The standard Lagrange finite element space on

is defined as

V_{h}=\mathbb{P}_{k}(\varOmega;\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap H^{1}(\varOmega),\quad V_{h}^{0}=V_{h}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\varOmega).

To simplify notation, the elementwise Hessian matrix (Laplacian) for

is still denoted by

(
\varDelta v_{h}
).
For any function

For the interpolation

\|v-v_{I}\|_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}+h\|v-v_{I}\|_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}+h^{2}\|v-v_{I}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\leq Ch^{\min\{k+1,s\}}\|v\|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}.

(4)

Throughout the paper, the letter

2.2
Discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate

In this subsection, we introduce the discrete Mirand-Talenti estimate, which serves as the main fundamental tool in designing and analyzing the new
C^{0}
interior penalty finite element method. Before that, we recall the following Miranda-Talenti estimate

Theorem 2
(Miranda-Talenti estimate
[
28
,
30
]
).

Suppose
\varOmega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded convex domain. Then, for all
v\in H^{2}(\varOmega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\varOmega)
, the following inequality holds:

\|D^{2}v\|_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}\leq\|\varDelta v\|_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}.

One crucial ingredient for establishing the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate is the introduction of an enrichment operator
E_{h}
. Let
E_{h}
denote the enrichment operator defined in
[
35
]
, which maps
v_{h}\in V^{0}_{h}
to a subspace of
H^{2}(\varOmega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\varOmega)
. With the aid of the enrich operator
E_{h}
,
[
35
]
proved the following discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate:

Theorem 3
(Discrete Miranda-Talenti estimates).

Let
\varOmega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d=2,3)
be a convex polytope. Then, for any
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
, we have

\|D^{2}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\leq\|\varDelta v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}+C_{{\dagger}}\Bigg(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\big\|\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\big\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(5)

for some constant
C_{{\dagger}}
independent of
h
and
v_{h}
.

Noticing that
|E_{h}v_{h}|_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}\leq C|E_{h}v_{h}|_{H^{2}(\varOmega)}
, following the proof of
[
35
, Lemma 3 and Theorem 1]
, we can show that

\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\leq C\left(\|\varDelta v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}+\Bigg(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\big\|\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\big\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

(6)

for some constant
C
. It triggers us to define a new mesh-dependent norm on
V_{h}^{0}
as

\|v_{h}\|_{h}^{2}:=\|D^{2}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\big\|\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\big\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}.

(7)

It is not hard to see that

\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{h}.

(8)

Armed with the new norm, we can establish the following discrete Sobolev inequality:

Theorem 4
(Discrete Sobolev inequality).

For any
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
, we have

\|v_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{h}.

(9)

Proof.

First, notice that
H^{2}(\varOmega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\varOmega)
and
E_{h}v_{h}\in H^{2}(\varOmega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\varOmega)
. Then, by the triangle inequality and the inverse inequality
[
7
,
13
]
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|E_{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}+\|v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}

\displaystyle\leq C|E_{h}v_{h}|_{H^{2}(\varOmega)}+Ch^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}

\displaystyle\leq C|v_{h}|_{H^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}+C|v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h}|_{H^{2}(\varOmega)}+Ch^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|v_{h}-E_{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{h},

where we have used
[
35
, Lemma 3]
in the last inequality.

3
Linearization and finite element approximation

In this section, we consider the linearization of the model equation (
2
) near
u^{\epsilon}
and investigate its finite element approximation. It serves the building block to prove the existence, uniqueness, and error analysis of the numerical methods for the fourth order semi-linear PDE (
2
).

To linearize the model equation (
2
), we use the following identity from
[
11
]
:

\det(D^{2}(u^{\epsilon}+tv))=\det(D^{2}u^{\epsilon})+t\,\mbox{tr}(\Phi^{\epsilon}D^{2}v)+\cdots+t^{n}\det(D^{2}v),

(10)

where

\Phi^{\epsilon}:=\mbox{cof}(D^{2}u^{\epsilon}).

(11)

By differentiating (
10
) at
t=0
, the linearized form of the operator
M^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}):=\epsilon\varDelta^{2}u^{\epsilon}-\det(D^{2}u^{\epsilon})
reads as

L_{u^{\epsilon}}(v):=\epsilon\varDelta^{2}v-\Phi^{\epsilon}:D^{2}v=\epsilon\varDelta^{2}v-\text{div}(\Phi^{\epsilon}\nabla v),

(12)

where we have used the divergence-free property of the cofactor matrix
\Phi^{\epsilon}

[
31
, Lemma 7]
. In the above equation,
A:B
denotes the Frobenius inner product of two
d\times d
matrices
A
and
B
.

We focus on the approximation of the following linearized equation

\displaystyle L_{u^{\epsilon}}(v)=\varphi

\displaystyle\quad\text{in $\varOmega$},

(13a)

\displaystyle v=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{on $\partial\varOmega$},

(13b)

\displaystyle\varDelta v=\psi

\displaystyle\quad\text{on $\partial\varOmega$},

(13c)

where
\varphi\in V^{0}_{*}
,
\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\varOmega)
, and
V^{0}_{*}
is the dual space of
V^{0}
.

To define the finite element method for (
13
), we begin by converting (
13
) into its weak form. Multiplying both sides of the equations by a test function
w\in V^{0}
and applying the divergence theorem, we obtain the variational formulation for (
13
) as follows: Find
v\in V_{0}
such that

a(v,w)=\langle\varphi,w\rangle+\epsilon(\psi,\nabla w\cdot{\bm{n}})_{L^{2}(\partial\varOmega)}\quad\forall w\in V_{0},

(14)

where

a(v,w)=\epsilon(\varDelta v,\varDelta w)_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}+(\Phi^{\epsilon}\nabla v,\nabla w)_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}.

(15)

Under Assumption
1
, where
u^{\epsilon}
is strictly convex, it follows that the matrix
\Phi^{\epsilon}
is positive definite. Consequently, we can demonstrate that

a(v,v)\geq C\epsilon\|v\|_{H^{2}(\varOmega)}^{2}.

According to the Lax-Milgram lemma
[
16
]
, the variational problem (
14
) is well-posed.

Define the discrete bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
as

\displaystyle a_{h}(v_{h},w_{h})

\displaystyle=\epsilon\left(\varDelta v_{h},\varDelta w_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}-\epsilon\sum_{F\in F_{h}^{i}}\left(\{\hskip-3.99994pt\{\varDelta v_{h}\}\hskip-3.99994pt\},\llbracket\nabla w_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}

(16)

\displaystyle-\epsilon\sum_{F\in F_{h}^{i}}\left(\{\hskip-3.99994pt\{\varDelta w_{h}\}\hskip-3.99994pt\},\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}-\left(\Phi^{\epsilon}:D^{2}v_{h},w_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}.

In light of the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate, we stabilize the discrete bilinear form by the jump of the flux. We define the stablized bilinear form
A_{h}^{\sigma}(\cdot,\cdot)
as

A_{h}^{\sigma}(v_{h},w_{h})=a_{h}(v_{h},w_{h})+\sigma(\epsilon+\epsilon^{-3})\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\left(\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket,\llbracket\nabla w_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{F})},

(17)

where
\sigma=\mathcal{O}(1)
is a penalty parameter independent of
h
and
\epsilon
.

The C0IP method of (
13
) is to find
v_{h}\in V^{0}_{h}
such that

A_{h}^{\sigma}(v_{h},w_{h})=\langle\varphi,w_{h}\rangle+\epsilon(\psi,\nabla w_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})_{L^{2}(\partial\varOmega)},

(18)

for all
w_{h}\in V^{0}_{h}
.

For the C0IP method in (
18
), we can establish the well-posedness.

Theorem 5
.

There exists
\sigma_{0}>0
such that for any
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
, there exists a unique
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
such that (
18
) holds.

Proof.

Boundedness of
A_{h}^{\sigma}
can be easily proven using the trace inequality and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality. We only need to establish the existence of
\sigma_{0}>0
such that

a_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})+\sigma(\epsilon+\epsilon^{-3})\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\|\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{F})}^{2}\geq C\epsilon\|v_{h}\|_{h}^{2},

(19)

for any
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
.
Using integration by parts, we can deduce that

\displaystyle a_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})=

\displaystyle\epsilon\left(\varDelta v_{h},\varDelta v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}-2\epsilon\sum_{F\in F_{h}^{i}}\left(\{\hskip-3.99994pt\{\varDelta v_{h}\}\hskip-3.99994pt\},\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle+(\Phi^{\epsilon}\nabla v_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}-\sum_{F\in F_{h}^{i}}\left(\llbracket\Phi^{\epsilon}\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket,v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(F)},

Applying Holder’s inequality and the trace inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\left(\{\hskip-3.99994pt\{\varDelta v_{h}\}\hskip-3.99994pt\},\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}\|\{\hskip-3.99994pt\{\varDelta v_{h}\}\hskip-3.99994pt\}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\big\|\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\big\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\varDelta v_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\big\|\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\big\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\left\|\varDelta v_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}^{2}+C\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\big\|\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\big\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2},

where we have used the Young’s inequality in the last inequality.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{F\in F_{h}^{i}}\left(\llbracket\Phi^{\epsilon}\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket,v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(F)}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\big\|\llbracket\Phi^{\epsilon}\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\big\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon^{-1}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\big\|\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\big\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{4}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}^{2}+C\epsilon^{-3}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\big\|\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\big\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}.

Then, (
19
) follows by combining the above two estimates with the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate (
5
).

Remark 6
.

If we have a uniform lower bound on
\Phi^{\epsilon}
such that

{\bm{x}}^{T}\Phi^{\epsilon}{\bm{x}}\geq C\|{\bm{x}}\|^{2}\quad\forall\epsilon>0,

then
\epsilon^{-3}
in (

) can be replaced by

.

Furthermore, we can get the following error estimate in discrete
H^{2}
norm.

Theorem 7
.

Let
v
be the solution of (
14
) and
v_{I}
be its interpolation in
V_{h}
. Suppose
v\in V^{0}\cap H^{s}(\varOmega)
, then there holds

\|v_{h}-v_{I}\|_{h}\leq C\left(1+\epsilon^{-2}+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)h^{l}|v|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)},

(20)

and

\|v-v_{h}\|_{h}\leq C\left(1+\epsilon^{-2}+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)h^{l}|v|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)},

(21)

where
l=\min(k-1,s-2)
.

Proof.

Let
w_{h}=v_{h}-v_{I}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\sigma}(w_{h},w_{h})

\displaystyle=A_{h}^{\sigma}(v_{h},w_{h})-A_{h}^{\sigma}(v_{I},w_{h})

(22)

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi,w_{h}\rangle+\epsilon(\psi,\nabla w_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})_{L^{2}(\partial\varOmega)}-A_{h}^{\sigma}(v_{I},w_{h}).

Using the equation (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\varphi,w_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=(\epsilon\varDelta^{2}v-\Phi^{\epsilon}:D^{2}v,w_{h})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\epsilon(\nabla\varDelta v,\nabla w_{h})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}-(\Phi^{\epsilon}:D^{2}v,w_{h})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(\varDelta v,\varDelta w_{h})_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}-\epsilon\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\left(\varDelta v,\llbracket\nabla w_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle-\epsilon(\psi,\nabla w_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})_{L^{2}(\partial\varOmega)}-(\Phi^{\epsilon}:D^{2}v,w_{h})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}.

Substituting (
23
) into (
22
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\sigma}(w_{h},w_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(\varDelta v,\varDelta w_{h})_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}-\epsilon\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\left(\varDelta v,\llbracket\nabla w_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}-(\Phi^{\epsilon}:D^{2}v,w_{h})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}-A_{h}^{\sigma}(v_{I},w_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\epsilon(\varDelta v-\varDelta v_{I},\varDelta w_{h})_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}

\displaystyle-\epsilon\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\left(\varDelta v-\{\hskip-3.99994pt\{\varDelta v_{I}\}\hskip-3.99994pt\},\llbracket\nabla w_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}-\epsilon\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\left(\{\hskip-3.99994pt\{\varDelta w_{h}\}\hskip-3.99994pt\},\llbracket\nabla v-\nabla v_{I}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle-(\Phi^{\epsilon}:D^{2}(v-v_{I}),w_{h})_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}-\sigma(\epsilon+\epsilon^{-3})\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\left(\llbracket\nabla v_{I}-\nabla v\rrbracket,\llbracket\nabla w_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}.

Using the trace inequality and (
4
), we obtain:

\displaystyle|A_{h}^{\sigma}(w_{h},w_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon h^{l}|v|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}\|w_{h}\|_{h}+C\epsilon^{-1}h^{l}|v|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}\|w_{h}\|_{h}+C\sigma(\epsilon+\epsilon^{-3})h^{l}|v|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}\|w_{h}\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\left(1+\epsilon^{-2}+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)h^{l}|v|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}\|w_{h}\|_{h}.

Combining the previous estimate with the coercivity (
19
), we have established (
20
). Moreover, (
21
) directly follows from (
20
) and (
4
).

4
C^{0}
IP methods for the model equation

In this section, we formulate the C0IP equation for the nonlinear model equation (
2
) and perform the error analysis.

4.1
Numerical scheme

The weak form of the model equation (
2
) reads as: Seeking
u^{\epsilon}\in V^{g}
such that

-\epsilon(\varDelta u^{\epsilon},\varDelta v)_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}+\Big(\det(D^{2}u^{\epsilon}),v\Big)_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}=(f,v)_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}-\epsilon\langle\epsilon,\nabla v\cdot{\bm{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\varOmega)},\quad\forall v\in V^{0}.

(24)

To facilicate the definition of numerical scheme, we introduce the new bilinear form
b_{h}^{\sigma}(\cdot,\cdot)
as

\displaystyle b_{h}^{\sigma}(v_{h},w_{h})=

\displaystyle\sigma(\epsilon+\epsilon^{-3})\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}^{-1}\left(\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket,\llbracket\nabla w_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}

(25)

\displaystyle-\epsilon\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\left(\{\hskip-3.99994pt\{\varDelta v_{h}\}\hskip-3.99994pt\},\llbracket\nabla w_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}-\epsilon\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\left(\{\hskip-3.99994pt\{\varDelta w_{h}\}\hskip-3.99994pt\},\llbracket\nabla v_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{L^{2}(F)}.

The new C0IP method for the model problem is defined to finding

u_{h}^{\epsilon}\in V_{h}^{g}
such that

\displaystyle-\epsilon\Big(\varDelta u_{h}^{\epsilon},\varDelta v_{h}\Big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}+\big(\det(D^{2}u_{h}^{\epsilon}),v_{h}\big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}-b_{h}^{\sigma}(u^{\epsilon}_{h},v_{h})

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f,v_{h})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}-\epsilon\langle\epsilon,\nabla v_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\varOmega)},

for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
.

It’s worth noting that the term
\big(\det(D^{2}u_{h}^{\epsilon}),v_{h}\big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}
is nonlinear. In the next subsection, we will establish the existence and uniqueness of the solution for (
26
).

4.2
Well-posedness and
H^{2}
error estimate for the numerical scheme

In this section, we establish the well-posedness of (
26
) and simultaneously prove the convergence rate using the combined fixed point and linearization techniques as in
[
19
,
31
]
.

We first define a linear operator
T:V_{h}^{g}\to V_{h}^{g}
. For any given
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{g}
, let
T(v_{h})\in V_{h}^{g}
denote the solution of the following problem:

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\sigma}(v_{h}-T(v_{h}),w_{h})=\epsilon\Big(\varDelta v_{h},\varDelta w_{h}\Big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}-\big(\det(D^{2}v_{h}),w_{h}\big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}

(27)

\displaystyle+b_{h}^{\sigma}(v_{h},w_{h})+(f,w_{h})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}-(\epsilon^{2},\nabla w_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}})_{L^{2}(\partial\varOmega)},\quad\forall w_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}.

Theorem
5
tells that the map
T
is well-defined. It is not hard to see that the fixed point of
T
is the solution of (
26
) and vice versa. We now proceed to prove the existence of such a fixed point in the vicinity of the interpolation of
u^{\epsilon}
. For this purpose, we define a neighborhood near
u^{\epsilon}_{I}
as

\mathbb{B}_{h}(\rho):=\{v_{h}\in V_{h}^{g}:\ \|v_{h}-u_{I}^{\epsilon}\|_{h}\leq\rho\}.

(28)

We commence our proof by establishing the following lemma

Lemma 8
.

Suppose
u^{\epsilon}\in H^{s}(\varOmega)
. Then, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\|u_{I}^{\epsilon}-T(u_{I}^{\epsilon})\|_{h}\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{-1}\left(1+\epsilon^{-d}+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}

(29)

for some constant
C_{1}>0
.

Proof.

Let
w_{h}^{\epsilon}=u_{I}^{\epsilon}-T(u_{I}^{\epsilon})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\sigma}(w_{h}^{\epsilon},w_{h}^{\epsilon})=

\displaystyle\epsilon\Big(\varDelta u_{I}^{\epsilon},\varDelta w_{h}^{\epsilon}\Big)_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}-\big(\det(D^{2}u_{I}^{\epsilon}),w_{h}^{\epsilon}\big)_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}

(30)

\displaystyle+b_{h}^{\sigma}(u_{I}^{\epsilon},w_{h}^{\epsilon})+(f,w_{h}^{\epsilon})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}-(\epsilon^{2},\nabla w_{h}^{\epsilon}\cdot{\bm{n}})_{L^{2}(\partial\varOmega)}.

Using the definition of the model equation (
2
), we can derive that:

\displaystyle(f,w_{h}^{\epsilon})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon(\varDelta^{2}u^{\epsilon},w_{h}^{\epsilon})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}+(\det(D^{2}u^{\epsilon}),w_{h}^{\epsilon})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}

(31)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(\nabla\varDelta u^{\epsilon},\nabla w_{h}^{\epsilon})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}+(\det(D^{2}u^{\epsilon}),w_{h}^{\epsilon})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon(\varDelta u^{\epsilon},\varDelta w_{h}^{\epsilon})_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}+\epsilon\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}(\varDelta u^{\epsilon},\llbracket\nabla w_{h}^{\epsilon}\rrbracket)_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle+(\epsilon^{2},\nabla w_{h}^{\epsilon}\cdot{\bm{n}})_{L^{2}(\partial\varOmega)}+(\det(D^{2}u^{\epsilon}),w_{h}^{\epsilon})_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}.

Combining the above two equalities, utilizing the trace inequality, and using the same estimation techniques in Theorem
7
, along with the interpolation result (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\sigma}(w_{h}^{\epsilon},w_{h}^{\epsilon})=

\displaystyle\epsilon\Big(\varDelta u_{I}^{\epsilon}-\varDelta u^{\epsilon},\varDelta w_{h}^{\epsilon}\Big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}+\epsilon\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}(\varDelta u^{\epsilon},\llbracket\nabla w_{h}^{\epsilon}\rrbracket)_{L^{2}(F)}

(32)

\displaystyle+\big(\det(D^{2}u^{\epsilon})-\det(D^{2}u_{I}^{\epsilon}),w_{h}^{\epsilon}\big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}+b_{h}^{\sigma}(u_{I}^{\epsilon},w_{h}^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\left(1+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}|u^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}\|w_{h}^{\epsilon}\|_{h}

\displaystyle+\big(\det(D^{2}u^{\epsilon})-\det(D^{2}u_{I}^{\epsilon}),w_{h}^{\epsilon}\big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}.

To bound the last term in (
32
), we apply the Mean Value Theorem and deduce that

\big(\det(D^{2}u^{\epsilon})-\det(D^{2}u_{I}^{\epsilon}),w_{h}^{\epsilon}\big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}=\Big(\Psi^{\epsilon}:(D^{2}u^{\epsilon}-D^{2}u_{I}^{\epsilon}),w_{h}^{\epsilon}\Big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})},

where
\Psi^{\epsilon}=\text{cof}(D^{2}u^{\epsilon}-\theta(D^{2}u^{\epsilon}-D^{2}u_{I}^{\epsilon}))
for some
\theta\in[0,1]
.
Using
[
22
, Lemma 4.1]
and the priori estimate (
3
), we have

\|\Psi^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}\leq C\|D^{2}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}^{d-1}\leq C\epsilon^{1-d}.

Combining the above two estimates with (
32
), we obtain:

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\sigma}(w_{h}^{\epsilon},w_{h}^{\epsilon})\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\left(1+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}|u^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}\|w_{h}^{\epsilon}\|_{h}

\displaystyle+C\epsilon^{1-d}h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}|u^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}\|w_{h}^{\epsilon}\|_{h}.

The desired result is derived from the coercivity (
19
).

Next, we introduce a lemma to demonstrate the contraction property of the operator
T

Lemma 9
.

For any
v_{h},w_{h}\in\mathbb{B}_{h}(\rho)
there holds

\|T(v_{h})-T(w_{h})\|_{h}\leq C(h,\rho,\epsilon)\|v_{h}-w_{h}\|_{h},

(33)

where
C(h,\rho,\epsilon)=C_{2}\epsilon^{1-d}\Big(\epsilon^{-1}h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}+\rho\Big)
, for some constant
C_{2}>0
.

Proof.

Let
z_{h}=v_{h}-w_{h}
. Using the definition of
T
and (
17
), we have, for any
\eta_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
:

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\sigma}(T(v_{h})-T(w_{h}),\eta_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\det(D^{2}v_{h})-\det(D^{2}w_{h}),\eta_{h}\Big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}-\Big(\Phi^{\epsilon}:D^{2}z_{h},\eta_{h}\Big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Psi_{h}:D^{2}z_{h},\eta_{h})_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}-\Big(\Phi^{\epsilon}:D^{2}z_{h},\eta_{h}\Big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})},

where we have utilized the Mean Value Theorem in the last equality, and
\Psi_{h}=\text{cof}(D^{2}w_{h}+\theta(D^{2}v_{h}-D^{2}w_{h}))
for some
\theta\in[0,1]
.
Next, we rewrite the above expression as

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\sigma}(T(v_{h})-T(w_{h}),\eta_{h})=\Big((\Psi_{h}-\Phi^{\epsilon}):D^{2}z_{h},\eta_{h}\Big)_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}.

To estimate
A_{h}^{\sigma}(T(v_{h})-T(w_{h}),\eta_{h})
, we first quote the following estimate from
[
22
, Lemma 4.3]

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{h}-\Phi^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\leq C\epsilon^{2-d}(h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{2}(\varOmega)}+\rho).

Using the above estimate, the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, and the discrete Sobolev inequality (
9
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\sigma}(T(v_{h})-T(w_{h}),\eta_{h})

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{2-d}(h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{3}(\varOmega)}+\rho)\|z_{h}\|_{h}\|\eta_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{2-d}(h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{3}(\varOmega)}+\rho)\|z_{h}\|_{h}\|\eta_{h}\|_{h}.

The desired result follows by setting
\eta_{h}=T(v_{h})-T(w_{h})
and applying the
coercivity (
19
).

Now, we are well-prepared to establish the well-posedness of our numerical scheme and derive the
H^{2}
error estimate.

Theorem 10
.

Suppose
u^{\epsilon}\in H^{s}(\varOmega)
. Then, there exists a unique
u_{h}^{\epsilon}\in V_{h}^{g}
satisfying (
26
), with the error estimates given by

\|u^{\epsilon}-u_{h}^{\epsilon}\|_{h}\leq C_{4}\epsilon^{-1}\left(1+\epsilon^{-d}+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}.

(34)

Proof.

Let
\rho_{0}=2C_{1}\epsilon^{-1}\left(1+\epsilon^{-d}+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}
. Then, the constant
C(h,\rho_{0},\epsilon)
becomes

\displaystyle C(h,\rho_{0},\epsilon)=C_{2}\epsilon^{-d}\Big(1+2C_{1}\left(1+\epsilon^{-d}+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)\|u\|_{H^{2}(\varOmega)}\Big)h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}.

(35)

We choose
h_{0}
such that for
h<h_{0}
, we have

C(h,\rho_{0},\epsilon)<\frac{1}{2}

(36)

According to Lemma
9
, for
v_{h},w_{h}\in\mathbb{B}_{h}(\rho_{0})
and
h<h_{0}
, we then have

\displaystyle\|T(v_{h})-T(w_{h})\|_{h}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|v_{h}-w_{h}\|_{h}.

(37)

Furthermore, Lemma
8
and (
37
) imply that for any
z_{h}\in\mathbb{B}_{h}(\rho_{0})

\displaystyle\|T(z_{h})-u_{I}^{\epsilon}\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T(z_{h})-T(u_{I}^{\epsilon})\|_{h}+\|T(u_{I}^{\epsilon})-u_{I}^{\epsilon}\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|z_{h}-u_{I}^{\epsilon}\|_{h}+C_{1}\epsilon^{-1}\left(1+\epsilon^{-d}+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}.

It means that
T(z_{h})\in\mathbb{B}_{h}(\rho_{0})
.
By the Brouwer’s Fixed Point Theorem
[
16
]
, there exists a unique
u_{h}^{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{B}_{h}(\rho_{0})
such that
T(u_{h}^{\epsilon})=u_{h}^{\epsilon}
, which means
u_{h}^{\epsilon}
is a unique solution of the numerical scheme. To obtain the
H^{2}
error estimate, we apply the triangle inequality and deduce that

\displaystyle\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{h}\|_{h}\leq

\displaystyle\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{I}\|_{h}+\|u^{\epsilon}_{I}-u^{\epsilon}_{h}\|_{h}

(38)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{\min(k-1,s-2)}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}+\rho_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\epsilon^{-1}\left(1+\epsilon^{-d}+\sigma(1+\epsilon^{-4})\right)h^{\min(k-1,s-2)}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\varOmega)}.

This concludes the proof.

What’s interesting about the data in this table is that we only observe a suboptimal order of
\mathcal{O}(h^{k-1})
for the
L^{2}
error and
H^{1}
error for cubic elements, contrasting with the optimal convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(h^{k})
for quadratic elements. This is despite the error for cubic elements being several digits in magnitude less than the corresponding error for quadratic elements.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose and analyze a new C0IP method for computing the viscosity solution of the fully nonlinear Monge-Ampère equations. The key idea is to apply the discrete Miranda-Talenti estimate. We prove optimal error estimates in
H^{2}
-norm. A series of benchmark examples are provided to demonstrate the theoretical results.

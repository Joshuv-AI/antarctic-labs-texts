On Explicit Solutions to Fixed-Point Equations in Propositional Dynamic Logic
Tim S. Lyon

Abstract
Propositional dynamic logic ( 𝖯𝖣𝖫 \mathsf{PDL} ) is an important modal logic used to specify and reason about the behavior of software. A challenging problem in the context of 𝖯𝖣𝖫 \mathsf{PDL} is solving fixed-point equations, i.e., formulae of the form x ≡ φ ⁡ ( x ) x\equiv\varphi(x) such that x x is a propositional variable and φ ⁡ ( x ) \varphi(x) is a formula containing x x . A solution to such an equation is a formula ψ \psi that omits x x and satisfies ψ ≡ φ ⁡ ( ψ ) \psi\equiv\varphi(\psi) , where φ ⁡ ( ψ ) \varphi(\psi) is obtained by replacing all occurrences of x x with ψ \psi in φ ⁡ ( x ) \varphi(x) . In this paper, we identify a novel class of 𝖯𝖣𝖫 \mathsf{PDL} formulae arranged in two dual hierarchies for which every fixed-point equation x ≡ φ ⁡ ( x ) x\equiv\varphi(x) has a solution. Moreover, we not only prove the existence of solutions for all such equations, but also provide an explicit solution ψ \psi for each fixed-point equation.

Introduction

Propositional dynamic logic (
\mathsf{PDL}
) was introduced by Fischer and Ladner
[
4
]
as a modal logic for modeling program execution. A characteristic feature of the logic is the inclusion of modalities of the form
[\alpha]
and
\langle\alpha\rangle
such that
\alpha
is taken to be a computer program in a programming language. A formula of the form
[\alpha]\varphi
is read as ‘the proposition
\varphi
holds in all states reachable by executing the program
\alpha
’ and a formula of the form
\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi
is read as ‘the proposition
\varphi
holds in some state reachable by executing the program
\alpha
.’

\mathsf{PDL}
is widely regarded for its expressivity, decidability, and capacity for modeling dynamic systems. The logic’s high degree of expressive power is due to the inclusion of fixed-point constructs, viz., the modalities
\langle\alpha^{*}\rangle
and
[\alpha^{*}]
, which permit the specification of (co)inductive definitions. Such operators are essential in program specification and verification for describing behaviors over potentially infinite structures such as safety properties (e.g., ‘no bad state will be reached’), liveness properties (e.g., ‘a good state will eventually be reached’), and termination (e.g., ‘every execution eventually ends’); cf.
[
3
]
.

In the context of
\mathsf{PDL}
, finding explicit solutions for
fixed-point equations
has proven to be a challenging problem. A fixed-point equation is a formula of the form
x\equiv\varphi(x)
such that
x
is a propositional variable and
\varphi(x)
is a formula containing
x
. A
solution
to such an equation is a formula
\psi
not containing
x
and satisfying
\psi\equiv\varphi(\psi)
, where
\varphi(\psi)
is obtained by replacing every occurrence of
x
in
\varphi
with
\psi
. Aside from being a problem of intrinsic theoretical interest, solving fixed-point equations has relevance to the longstanding open problem of if
\mathsf{PDL}
enjoys Craig interpolation
[
2
]
.

In fact, the observations made in this paper grew out of the author’s investigation of the Craig interpolation property for
\mathsf{PDL}
.
As a case in point, a proof-theoretic methodology relying on cyclic sequent systems has been used in recent years to establish interpolation for certain modal fixed-point logics
[
1
,
8
]
. A critical aspect of this methodology is the solution of fixed-point equations in the construction of interpolants. Naturally, this demonstrates the value of solving such equations in the context of computing interpolants and establishing interpolation properties. Moreover, this also has practical relevance as Craig interpolation can be used to speed-up model-checking algorithms
[
6
]
.

To best of the author’s knowledge, there appears to be little to no work on solving fixed-point equations for
\mathsf{PDL}
. Therefore, the aim of the current article is to take a first step toward rectifying this gap in the literature. This paper makes two contributions: first, we identify a non-trivial class of
\mathsf{PDL}
formulae (arranged in two dual hierarchies of increasing formulaic complexity) such that for every formula
\varphi(x)
in the class,
x\equiv\varphi(x)
has a solution. Second, we not only prove the existence of solutions for all such equations, but provide an explicit witness
\psi
as a solution for each equation. We note that although it is straightforward to
verify
the solutions to such equations (see Theorem
3.1
), the novelty of the present work consists of having identified the class of solvable equations in the first place.

Solving PDL Fixed-Point Equations

A
fixed-point equation
is defined to be a formula of the form
x\equiv\varphi(x)
such that
x\in\mathsf{Var}
and
\varphi(x)\in\mathscr{L}
. Given formulae
\psi,\varphi(x)\in\mathscr{L}

\varphi(\psi)
denote the formula obtained by replacing
\psi
for each occurrence of
x
in
\varphi(x)
. We define a
solution
to a fixed-point equation
x\equiv\varphi(x)
to be a formula
\psi\in\mathscr{L}_{x}
such that
\psi\equiv\varphi(\psi)
is valid. In this section, we show that if
\varphi(x)
is in one of two forms, dubbed
\Uppi^{x}
-form
and
\Upsigma^{x}
-form
, then
x\equiv\varphi(x)
has a solution.

Let us fix a variable

\mathscr{L}
of increasing complexity relative to
x
. The base level of the hierarchy is the set
\Uppi_{0}^{x}

\Uppi_{2n+1}^{x}
and even levels

are defined as shown below with
n\in\mathbb{N}

\Uppi^{x}

\Upsigma^{x}:=\{\overline{\varphi}\ |\ \varphi\in\Uppi^{x}\}

4
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we identified a non-trivial class of solvable
\mathsf{PDL}
fixed-point equations obtained by taking the union
\Uppi^{x}\cup\Upsigma^{x}
of two dual hierarchies of increasing formulaic complexity. We provided explicit solutions to each fixed-point equation dependent upon which level of which hierarchy the equation was associated with. Although it is straightforward to verify that the formulae
\lambda_{1}
–
\lambda_{4}
are solutions to their respective fixed-point equations, the novelty of the present work consisted in having identified the class
\Uppi^{x}\cup\Upsigma^{x}
and these solutions in the first place.

In future work, we aim to investigate larger classes of solvable
\mathsf{PDL}
fixed-point equations. For example, observe that for any formula
\varphi(x)\in\Uppi^{x}\cup\Upsigma^{x}
of form (
1
) or (
3
), the variable
x
does not occur anywhere in its context, i.e., in any
\varphi_{i}
,
\psi_{i}
, or
\alpha_{i}
. Therefore, it would be interesting to look into solutions to equations where this restriction is lifted. As discussed in the introduction, we also aim to use solutions to fixed-point equations, accompanied by proof-theoretic techniques
[
1
,
8
]
, to investigate Craig interpolation for
\mathsf{PDL}
and to extrapolate these techniques to other modal fixed-point logics.

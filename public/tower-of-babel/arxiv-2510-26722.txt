Non-Convex Over-the-Air Heterogeneous Federated Learning: A Bias–Variance Trade-off
Muhammad Faraz Ul Abrar, Nicolò Michelusi

Abstract
Over-the-air (OTA) federated learning (FL) has been well recognized as a scalable paradigm that exploits the waveform superposition of the wireless multiple-access channel to aggregate model updates in a single use. Existing OTA-FL designs largely enforce zero-bias model updates by either assuming homogeneous wireless conditions (equal path loss across devices) or forcing zero bias updates to guarantee convergence. Under heterogeneous wireless scenarios, however, such designs are constrained by the weakest device and inflate the update variance. Moreover, prior analyses of biased OTA-FL largely address convex objectives, while most modern AI models are highly non-convex. Motivated by these gaps, we study OTA-FL with stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for general smooth non-convex objectives under wireless heterogeneity. We develop novel OTA-FL SGD updates that allow a structured, time-invariant model bias while facilitating reduced variance updates. We derive a finite-time stationarity bound (expected time average squared gradient norm) that explicitly reveals a bias–variance trade-off. To optimize this trade-off, we pose a non-convex joint OTA power-control design and develop an efficient successive convex approximation (SCA) algorithm that requires only statistical CSI at the base station. Experiments on a non-convex image classification task validate the approach: the SCA-based design accelerates convergence via an optimized bias and improves generalization over prior OTA-FL baselines.

I
Introduction

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a key paradigm for privacy-preserving distributed learning, allowing a massive number of devices to collaboratively train a model without sharing raw data. In a standard FL setup,
N
clients with private datasets coordinate with a central parameter server (PS), e.g., an edge or cloud node, by transmitting only model parameters or gradients
[
2
,
3
]
. The objective is to solve

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}^{*}\;=\;\arg\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(\mathbf{w})\;\triangleq\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m\in[N]}f_{m}(\mathbf{w}),

(P)

where
f_{m}(\mathbf{w})
represents the local objective function of device
m
, and
F(\mathbf{w})
is the global objective (loss) function.
Problem (
P
) is typically solved by first-order methods (e.g., mini-batch SGD): in each round, the PS broadcasts the current model
\mathbf{w}_{t}
, devices compute local gradients on their data and transmit them wirelessly to the PS. The PS then aggregates the received updates to obtain
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}
and re-broadcasts it to begin the next round. This process repeats for multiple rounds until the global objective converges
[
4
]
.

Nevertheless, realizing FL over wireless networks requires iterative exchange of high-dimensional gradients over noisy, bandwidth-constrained wireless channels, and hence causes a major performance bottleneck. A growing body of work, therefore, studies FL over wireless with robustness to fading and noise
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
, and proposes device-scheduling strategies that account for channel gain fluctuations
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. Among these, over-the-air (OTA) computation has emerged as a particularly scalable approach for wireless FL. OTA-FL works on the principle of waveform superposition on the multiple-access channel (MAC), whereby simultaneous transmissions yield “single-shot” model or gradient aggregation at the PS, enabling fast FL updates
[
5
,
6
,
11
]
. To ensure unbiased estimation via OTA aggregation, the typical requirement is to align phases and equalize scales via device
pre-scalers
and a PS
post-scaler
. Over fading MACs, this coherent aggregation effectively requires channel inversion at the devices, and hence the feasible pre-scaling is dictated by the “weakest” device.

Thresholding and related heuristics have been proposed to mitigate this issue in OTA-FL literature
[
6
,
11
]
. However, most prior OTA-FL analyses either assume
wireless homogeneity
(equal average path loss across devices) to ensure zero average bias or explicitly enforce zero bias to facilitate convergence guarantees
[
6
,
5
,
7
,
12
]
. In realistic heterogeneous deployments, where devices experience varying large-scale channel conditions, imposing zero bias can severely increase model update variance (due to weak devices), whereas relaxing the zero bias design constraint, as done in
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
, can introduce unpredictable bias. In addition, much of the existing convergence theory for OTA-FL is developed under (strongly) convex objectives, see e.g.,
[
12
,
14
,
8
]
, which mismatches modern non-convex ML models deployed in practice.

To fill these gaps, we study OTA-FL under wireless heterogeneity with SGD updates for general smooth non-convex objectives. Building on and extending our prior work on biased OTA-FL in the convex setting
[
8
]
, we design OTA-FL updates that admit a structured, time-invariant model bias to reduce update variance, and we generalize the analysis to non-convex objectives. We establish a finite-time stationarity convergence bound (expected average squared gradient norm) that reveals a bias-variance trade-off. Building on this bound, we pose a joint OTA power-control problem and, unlike the heuristic designs proposed in
[
8
]
, develop an efficient successive convex approximation (SCA) algorithm that operates with statistical channel state information (CSI) at the PS, avoiding the heavy overhead of global instantaneous CSI. Finally, experiments on a non-convex learning task corroborate the theory, showing that the SCA-based design accelerates convergence by optimizing the allowed bias and achieves a target generalization performance faster than prior OTA-FL baselines.

II
System Model and over-the-air FL

We consider a wireless network of
N
distributed devices and a base station (also acting as the PS) aiming to collaboratively learn an FL model, as shown in Fig.
1
. Each device
m
owns a private local dataset
\mathcal{D}_{m}
containing
D
data points, with associated local objective function
f_{m}(\mathbf{w})=\frac{1}{D}\,\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathcal{D}_{m}}\phi(\mathbf{w},\boldsymbol{\xi})
, only computable at device
m
, where
\phi(\mathbf{w},\cdot)
is the sample-wise loss function,
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is a data point and
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the
d
-dimensional learning parameter. The goal is to solve (
P
) via distributed SGD over multiple FL rounds. Specifically, at the start of round
t
, the PS broadcasts the latest FL model
\mathbf{w}_{t}
to each device in the network. Each device
m
then uses a randomly drawn mini-batch
\mathcal{B}_{m,t}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{m}
of fixed size
|\mathcal{B}_{m,t}|=B
to estimate its local gradient
\boldsymbol{g}_{m,t}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathcal{B}_{m,t}}\phi(\mathbf{w}_{t},\boldsymbol{\xi})
. Subsequently, each device uploads its estimated local gradient to the PS. Ideally, the PS aims to aggregate these gradients without error to compute the true global stochastic gradient:

\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m\in[N]}\boldsymbol{g}_{m,t},

(1)

and subsequently updates the FL model with a fixed learning step size
\eta>0
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{t+1}=\mathbf{w}_{t}-{\eta}\overline{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t}.

(2)

This procedure is repeated until a target accuracy is reached or a fixed number of FL rounds is completed. Nonetheless, computing the global gradient in (
1
) requires noiseless aggregation of all the local gradients, i.e., they need to be perfectly received at the PS, and aggregated with uniform weights
\frac{1}{N}
. However, in practice,
local gradients are obtained through a noisy wireless channel, introducing errors in the estimation of the global gradient
\overline{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t}
, which is discussed next.

II-A

Over-the-air transmission over a fading MAC

We model the channel from device
m
to the PS in round
t
as flat Rayleigh fading,
h_{m,t}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\Lambda_{m})
, independent across devices and i.i.d. over
t
. Here, the parameter
\Lambda_{m}
represents the average
channel gain, which is dependent on large-scale propagation conditions and is assumed to be constant throughout the FL runtime. We assume the PS has knowledge of this statistical CSI, i.e., the set
\{\Lambda_{m}\}
, but not the instantaneous CSI
\{h_{m,t}\}
. Critically, unlike existing works

[
6
,
5
,
7
,
12
]
assuming identical average path loss across devices (
\Lambda_{m}{=}\Lambda_{n},\forall m,n\in[N]
), we consider a heterogeneous wireless environment where devices experience distinct
path losses.

In this work, we consider local gradient uplink transmission using the OTA aggregation scheme. OTA-FL exploits the superposition property of the wireless multiple-access channel to perform joint computation-and-communication, enabling single-shot aggregation at the PS. Following
[
5
,
6
,
11
,
7
,
16
,
12
]
, we assume devices are perfectly synchronized during gradient upload.
Denoting by
\mathbf{x}_{m,t}
the signal transmitted by device
m
in round
t
, the PS receives the signal

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{t}=\sum_{m\in[N]}h_{m,t}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{m,t}\;+\;\mathbf{z}_{t},

(3)

where

\mathbf{z}_{t}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},N_{0}\mathbf{I})

is the additive white Gaussian noise, i.i.d. over
t
. To approximate the ideal gradient aggregation in (
1
) using the signal model in (
3
), each device pre-scales its local gradient using an OTA pre-scaler
\gamma_{m}
and adopts a truncated channel inversion power control strategy, namely,

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{m,t}=\frac{1}{{h}_{m,t}}\chi_{m,t}\gamma_{m}\boldsymbol{g}_{m,t},

(4)

where
\mathbf{\chi}_{m,t}
is the OTA transmission indicator, defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{\chi}_{m,t}=\begin{cases}1,\,\quad\text{ if }|h_{m,t}|\geq\frac{G_{\text{max}}\gamma_{m}}{\sqrt{dE_{s}}},\\
0,\quad\,\,\,\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(5)

Here,
E_{s}
is the maximum per-sample energy budget, and
G_{\text{max}}
is an upper bound on
\|\boldsymbol{g}_{m,t}\|
(see Assumption
2
). By construction, a device remains silent when
|h_{m,t}|<\frac{G_{\text{max}}\gamma_{m}}{\sqrt{dE_{s}}}
to ensure the energy constraint
\|\mathbf{x}_{m,t}\|^{2}/d\leq E_{s}\,,\forall m,t
. The decision in (
5
) is decentralized using the instantaneous uplink CSI
h_{m,t}
, which can be
acquired with negligible overhead via a downlink pilot, by leveraging channel reciprocity
[
17
]
. With this design, the PS estimates the global gradient (
1
) as

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t}=\frac{\mathbf{y}_{t}}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{m\in[N]}\chi_{m,t}\gamma_{m}\boldsymbol{g}_{m,t}\;+\;\frac{\mathbf{z}_{t}}{\alpha},

(6)

where
\alpha>0
is a post-scaler, designed in the next section. Owing to concurrent uplink transmissions, the gradient upload time per round is independent of the number of active devices and equals
d/B
, where
B
is the system bandwidth.

II-B

Biased Over-the-Air-FL

With the estimated gradient
\hat{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t}
in (
6
), the PS updates the global model as

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{t+1}=\mathbf{w}_{t}-\eta\hat{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t},

(7)

in place of the ideal SGD update in (
2
).
Conditioned on
\mathbf{w}_{t}
and the mini-batch selections, the received signal satisfies

\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{y}_{t}]=\sum_{m\in[N]}\alpha_{m}\,\boldsymbol{g}_{m,t}
with

\alpha_{m}\triangleq\gamma_{m}\,\mathbb{E}[\chi_{m,t}]={\gamma_{m}\exp\{-\tfrac{\gamma_{m}^{2}G_{\text{max}}^{2}}{d\,\Lambda_{m}\,E_{s}}\}}
, where the expectation is over channel fading and receiver noise. Choosing the post-scaler

\alpha\triangleq\sum_{m\in[N]}\alpha_{m}
yields a global gradient estimate such that the
expected
gradient estimate is a convex combination of local gradients,

\tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t}\;\triangleq\;\mathbb{E}[\hat{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t}\mid\mathbf{w}_{t}]\;=\;\sum_{m\in[N]}p_{m}\,\boldsymbol{g}_{m,t},

(8)

with weights
p_{m}\triangleq\alpha_{m}/\alpha
,
0\leq p_{m}\leq 1
,
\sum_{m}p_{m}=1
. We interpret
p_{m}
as the

average participation level
of device
m
induced by the OTA-FL design. Consequently, the update rule in (
7
) acts as an SGD method that, on average, descends along
\tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t}
in place of the ideal aggregate
\overline{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t}
from (
1
). Therefore, these updates minimize a different objective function than (
P
), on average, given by

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\mathbf{w})=\sum_{m\in[N]}p_{m}f_{m}(\mathbf{w}).

(
\tilde{\text{P}}
)

This can be readily verified by observing that
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\boldsymbol{g}}_{t}]=\nabla\tilde{F}(\mathbf{w}_{t})
with expectation taken over the mini-batch data selection.

It is to be highlighted that prior OTA-FL works

[
18
,
10
,
6
,
5
,
7
,
12
]

either assume homogeneous large-scale wireless conditions or enforce zero-bias aggregation, thereby ensuring uniform participation
p_{m}=\frac{1}{N}
for all
m\in[N]
, so that minimizing (

~
P

) becomes equivalent to (
P
). While effective under homogeneous conditions, these designs become constrained by the weakest channel device in heterogeneous wireless networks, leading to high-variance model updates (see, e.g.,
[
11
,
8
,
14
]
). Moreover, under wireless heterogeneity, such schemes
effectively minimize a
different
objective, leading to objective inconsistency

[
19
]
and a non-negligible model bias. As a result, their convergence guarantees are not applicable in the considered scenario.

Our proposed OTA-FL aggregation design generalizes existing schemes by introducing a controllable, non-zero average bias, thereby subsuming zero-bias designs (
p_{m}=\tfrac{1}{N}
) as a special case. This formulation exposes an explicit
bias–variance trade-off that can be optimized to improve convergence. While some recent works
[
14
,
15
]
also consider biased OTA-FL, they typically allow a generic, unstructured bias that is difficult to analyze and control. In contrast, building on our prior work
[
8
]
, we employ a structured, time-invariant bias that admits tractable finite-time guarantees. Note that, unlike the prior study, which treated (strongly) convex objectives and offered heuristic designs, here we derive a convergence bound for non-convex objectives and optimize the bias-variance trade-off via an efficient SCA method, which is done in the next section.

III
Convergence Analysis and pre-scaler design

In this section, we theoretically characterize the learning performance of the proposed OTA-FL scheme as a function of the device pre-scalers

\{\gamma_{m}\}
. Since the global objective
F(\mathbf{w})
is typically
non-convex
, we study convergence in terms of stationarity. To this end, our metric of interest is the expected (finite-time) average squared gradient norm of the global objective, given by
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|^{2}]
.
To study the convergence, we make the following standard assumptions:

Assumption 1
.

For each device
m\in[N]
, the local objective
f_{m}(\cdot)
is
L
-smooth, i.e.,

\|\nabla f_{m}(\mathbf{x})-\nabla f_{m}(\mathbf{y})\|\leq L\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|,\quad\forall\,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and is lower bounded, that is, there exists
f_{m}^{\inf}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f_{m}(\mathbf{w})\geq f_{m}^{\inf}\,,\forall\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Consequently, any convex combination
\sum_{m\in[N]}p_{m}f_{m}(\cdot)
(including
F(\cdot)
with
p_{m}=1/N
and
\tilde{F}(\cdot)
) is
L
-smooth and lower bounded by
\sum_{m\in[N]}p_{m}f_{m}^{\inf}
.

Assumption 2
.

The sample-wise loss gradient for any given individual data sample
\xi
is uniformly bounded, i.e.,
\|\nabla\phi(\mathbf{w},\xi)\|\leq G_{\text{max}},\forall\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It follows from the triangle inequality that
\|\mathbf{g}_{m,t}\|\leq G_{\text{max}},\forall m,t
.

Assumption 3
.

The mini-batch local gradient
\boldsymbol{g}_{m,t}
is an unbiased estimate of the full-batch local gradient with bounded variance, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{g}_{m,t}|\mathbf{w}_{t}]=\nabla f_{m}(\mathbf{w}_{t})
and
\mathrm{var}(\boldsymbol{g}_{m,t}|\mathbf{w}_{t})\leq\sigma_{m}^{2},\,\forall m\in[N],\,\mathbf{w}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t\geq 0
.

Assumption 4
.

The variance of local gradients to global gradient is bounded, that is, there exists
\kappa>0
such that for all
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m\in[N]}\|\nabla f_{m}(\mathbf{w})-\nabla F(\mathbf{w})\|^{2}\leq\kappa^{2}
. Under Assumption
2
, it further follows that
\kappa\leq 2G_{\text{max}}
.

Assumptions
1
(smoothness) and
3
are standard in FL analyses (e.g.,
[
20
,
12
,
15
]
), while Assumption
2
is also commonly adopted by many wireless FL works, see e.g.,
[
21
,
12
,
15
]
. The lower-boundedness in Assumption
1
is mild and typical for non-convex objectives
[
7
,
15
]
. Finally, Assumption
4
, often called bounded gradient dissimilarity or data divergence, captures data heterogeneity and is widely used in the literature (e.g.,
[
22
]
).

III-A

Main Convergence Results

Now, we are ready to present our main finite-time stationarity convergence result. Since the proposed OTA-FL updates in (
7
) on average track the biased objective
\tilde{F}(\mathbf{w)}
in (

~
P

), we approach the analysis by splitting
\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|^{2}
into:
\|\nabla\tilde{F}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\|^{2}
and
\|\nabla F(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\nabla\tilde{F}(\mathbf{w}_{t})\|^{2}
.
With this, the convergence bound is then given as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumptions 1-4 and a fixed learning step size
0<\eta\leq 1/L
, after
T
FL rounds it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla F(\mathbf{w}_{t})\|^{2}\big]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4\,\max_{m\in[N]}(f_{m}(\mathbf{w}_{0})-f_{m}^{\inf})}{\eta\,T}

\displaystyle+2\eta L\zeta+

\displaystyle 2N\kappa^{2}\sum_{m\in[N]}\Big(p_{m}-\tfrac{1}{N}\Big)^{2},

(9)

where
\zeta
is the gradient estimation variance, bounded as:

\displaystyle\zeta\!\triangleq

\displaystyle G_{\max}^{2}\underbrace{\sum_{m\in[N]}\left(\frac{p_{m}\gamma_{m}}{\alpha}-p_{m}^{2}\right)}_{\text{transmission variance}}+\underbrace{\sum_{m\in[N]}p_{m}^{2}\sigma_{m}^{2}}_{\text{mini-batch variance}}

\displaystyle\!+\!\underbrace{\frac{dN_{0}}{\alpha^{2}}}_{\text{receiver noise}}.

(10)

The proof sketch of Theorem
1
is provided in the Appendix.

Theorem
1
via (
9
) and (
10
) characterizes the convergence behavior of the proposed OTA-FL scheme through three key components: (1) an optimization term which depends on the initial FL model
\mathbf{w}_{0}
that decays as
O(1/(\eta T))
; (2) a
noisy global gradient estimation variance term; and (3) a model bias term induced by non-uniform average participation. Moreover, the variance term
\zeta

splits further into: (a) transmission variance arising from truncated channel inversion, and intermittent devices transmission of the local gradients as a result of channel gain fluctuations, which randomizes device participation at each round; (b)
mini-batch variance from stochastic mini-batch gradients; and (c) additive receiver noise. We highlight that the choice of pre-scalers
\{\gamma_{m}\}
affects all three key components in the variance term as well as governs the resultant average model bias, critically revealing the fundamental bias-variance trade-off at the heart of this work. In particular, choosing smaller

\gamma_{m}
lowers transmission variance and the bias term but also reduces
\alpha
, amplifying the receiver-noise term. Conversely,
increasing
\gamma_{m}
can enlarge
\alpha
and suppress receiver noise, but shifts
\{p_{m}\}
away from uniform, introducing a non-zero model bias. This trade-off calls for a careful optimization of the design parameters, which we develop in the next section.

III-B

OTA pre-scalers design

Here, we study the OTA-FL power control strategy to accelerate convergence. To this end, we consider the following pre-scaler design problem:

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\{\gamma_{m}\},\gamma_{m}>0\,,m\in[N]}2\eta L\zeta+2N\kappa^{2}\sum_{m\in[N]}\Big(p_{m}-\tfrac{1}{N}\Big)^{2},

(P1)

corresponding to the convergence bound in Theorem
1
excluding the initialization term, which is independent of the device pre-scalers
\{\gamma_{m}\}
. One can verify that due to the complicated dependence of bias and variance terms on the choice of pre-scalers
\{\gamma_{m}\}
, (
P1
) is a non-convex optimization. Leveraging variable dependencies, the optimization problem can also be expressed as a minimization over the (coupled) variables
\mathcal{X}\triangleq\{\{\gamma_{m}\},\{p_{m}\},\alpha\}
, subject to the following additional coupling constraints: (i)
\gamma_{m}\exp\!\{-\tfrac{\gamma_{m}^{2}G_{\max}^{2}}{d\Lambda_{m}E_{s}}\}=\alpha p_{m}
(i.e.,
\alpha_{m}=\alpha p_{m}
);
(ii)
0<\gamma_{m}\leq\gamma_{m,\max}
, with
\gamma_{m,\max}=\sqrt{\tfrac{d\Lambda_{m}E_{s}}{2G_{\max}^{2}}}
;
(iii)
0\leq\alpha\leq\min_{n}\frac{\alpha_{n,\max}}{p_{n}}
, with
\alpha_{m,\max}=\sqrt{\tfrac{d\Lambda_{m}E_{s}}{2e\,G_{\max}^{2}}}
;
(iv)
p_{m}\in[0,1]
and
\sum_{m}p_{m}=1
(probability simplex).
Note that
\alpha_{m}(\gamma_{m})
is quasi-concave and peaks at
\gamma_{m,\max}
with value
\alpha_{m,\max}
, thus (iii) enforces feasibility of (i) by ensuring
\alpha p_{m}=\alpha_{m}\leq\alpha_{m,\max}
for every
m
. Moreover, for fixed
(\alpha,p_{m})
the transmission-variance term increases with
\gamma_{m}
, and (i) admits two roots
\gamma_{m,1}\leq\gamma_{m,\max}\leq\gamma_{m,2}
, therefore the constraint (ii) causes no loss of optimality. Note that this optimization problem remains non-convex with non-convex terms present in both the objective and constraints. To address this, we adopt a successive convex approximation (SCA) approach, which iteratively solves convex surrogates of the original problem by linearizing the non-convex components around the current iterate. This process is guaranteed to converge to a stationary point of the original non-convex problem
[
23
]
.

To employ the SCA procedure, at iteration
k
, given anchors
\{\bar{\gamma}_{m}\}
,
\{\bar{p}_{m}\}
,
\bar{\alpha}
, we:
(i) introduce epigraph variables
z_{m}
and convexify
the first component in the transmission variance (
10
) as

\frac{p_{m}\gamma_{m}}{\alpha}\leq z_{m}
via a log-linear surrogate (see (
11b
));
(ii) linearize the second component in the transmission variance

-p_{m}^{2}
around
\bar{p}_{m}
in the objective (see (
11a
));
(iii) relax and convexify
\gamma_{m}e^{-\frac{\gamma_{m}^{2}G_{\max}^{2}}{d\Lambda_{m}E_{s}}}=\alpha p_{m}
by taking logs and first-order expansions at
(\bar{\alpha},\bar{p}_{m})
(see (
11c
));
(iv) convexify the bound on
\alpha
using a first-order approximation of
1/\alpha
at
\bar{\alpha}
(see (
11d
)).
This yields the convex subproblem at iteration
k
:

\displaystyle\min_{\{\gamma_{m}\},\{p_{m}\},\{z_{m}\},\alpha}\ \eta L\Big(G_{\max}^{2}\sum_{m}z_{m}+\tfrac{dN_{0}}{\alpha^{2}}+\sum_{m}p_{m}^{2}\sigma_{m}^{2}

\displaystyle{-}G_{\max}^{2}\sum_{m}\bar{p}_{m}(2p_{m}-\bar{p}_{m})\Big)+N\kappa^{2}\sum_{m}\Big(p_{m}-\tfrac{1}{N}\Big)^{2},

(11a)

\displaystyle\text{s.t. }\forall m\in[N]:

\displaystyle\quad\ln(\bar{\gamma}_{m}\bar{p}_{m})+\tfrac{\gamma_{m}}{\bar{\gamma}_{m}}+\tfrac{p_{m}}{\bar{p}_{m}}-2\;\leq\;\ln z_{m}+\ln\alpha,

(11b)

\displaystyle\quad\ln(\bar{\alpha}\,\bar{p}_{m})+\tfrac{\alpha}{\bar{\alpha}}+\tfrac{p_{m}}{\bar{p}_{m}}-2\;\leq\;\ln\gamma_{m}-\tfrac{\gamma_{m}^{2}G_{\max}^{2}}{d\Lambda_{m}E_{s}},

(11c)

\displaystyle\quad 0\leq\gamma_{m}\leq\gamma_{m,\max},\quad\frac{p_{m}}{\alpha_{m,\max}}\leq\frac{2\bar{\alpha}-\alpha}{\bar{\alpha}^{2}},\ \alpha\geq 0,

(11d)

\displaystyle\quad 0\leq p_{m}\leq 1,\ \sum_{m}p_{m}=1.

(11e)

Each subproblem in (
11
) is convex and can be solved efficiently (e.g., via CVX). Updating the anchors with the optimizer and iterating yields a high-quality solution to the original non-convex problem.

IV
Numerical Results

In this section, we evaluate the proposed SCA-optimized OTA-FL design. To this end, we study the popular handwritten digit classification problem in an FL setting on the widely used MNIST dataset
[
24
]
, which consists of C = 10 classes from “0” to “9” with each image of size 28 x 28 pixels. We performed experiments on a three-layer neural network architecture with ReLU non-linear activations and a hidden layer dimension of 1024, yielding a
d=814{,}090
-dimensional parameter vector
\mathbf{w}
. The FL training uses an
\ell_{2}
-regularized cross-entropy loss at each device, with a regularization coefficient 0.01. We highlight that this setup results in a non-convex OTA-FL problem due to the choice of our model architecture. We consider the FL problem with
N=10
devices uniformly deployed within a radius of
r_{\text{max}}=1750
m from the PS situated at the center. The communication bandwidth is
B

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied non-convex OTA-FL under heterogeneous wireless conditions. Departing from conventional zero-bias model updates, we proposed a novel OTA aggregation scheme that introduces a structured, time-invariant model bias to mitigate the high update variance caused by channel disparities. We established convergence to a stationary point and derived a bound, which explicitly exposes a bias-variance trade-off driven by device power control design. Guided by this theoretical insight, we formulated a non-convex joint power-control design and developed an efficient SCA algorithm that requires only statistical CSI at the base station. Numerical results on a non-convex image classification task validated our theory, demonstrating that under wireless heterogeneity, allowing a controlled bias can noticeably accelerate model convergence and improve model generalization relative to existing state-of-the-art OTA-FL baselines.

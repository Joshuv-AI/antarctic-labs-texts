A bang-bang optimal control for a nonlinear system modeling the Gate Control Theory of Pain
G. Díaz, J.I. Díaz

Abstract
We consider a nonlinear system of coupled ordinary differential equations (representing the excitatory, inhibitory, and T-cell potentials) based on the Gate Control Theory of Pain, initially proposed by R. Melzack and P.D. Wall in 1965, and later mathematically modeled by N.F. Britton and S.M. Skevington in 1988. Our main results focus on an optimal control problem associated with this model, where the short frequency, understood as a bounded time-dependent function, is treated as the control variable. The cost function accounts for a person’s pain at a given final time and incorporates additional criteria. We demonstrate the uniqueness of the optimal control and establish the bang-bang nature of the control. In a previous section, we extend the mathematical analysis of the model developed by Britton and Skevington by presenting a series of mathematical inequalities. These inequalities strengthen the model’s alignment with the principal requirements for reproducing the core structure of Pain Theory.



Keywords
: Gate Control Theory of Pain, nonlinear system of ordinary
differential equations, bang-bang optimal control, monotone dependence
of solutions on the data.

AMS Subject Classifications: 34A34, 34C60, 34H05, 49J30, 49K15.

Introduction and some preliminaries

The purpose of this paper is to analyze an optimal control problem associated with the so-called ”Pain Gate Theory” proposed in 1965 by R. Melzack and P. Wall (
[
MW1
]
) and later confirmed and extended in their 1982 revision (see
[
MW2
]
). A mathematical model related to this theory was proposed by N.F. Britton and S.M. Skevington
[
BS
]
(see also the review
[
Na
]
). This theory forms the basis of the so-called ”neurostimulators,” devices implanted in patients with chronic pain to alleviate their discomfort. Our mathematical study consists of two parts. In the first part, we demonstrate that, under appropriate structural assumptions, the corresponding coupled system has a unique solution. Additionally, we analyze various qualitative properties of the solutions, focusing particularly on their continuous and monotonic dependence on the data. In the second part, we examine the optimal control problem associated with the equation below for the potentials
{\bf V}(t)=\big({\rm V}_{{\rm E}}(t),{\rm V}_{{\rm I}}(t),{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}(t)\big)^{\tt t}
, considering the short frequency
x_{s}
as the control variable. The system under consideration can be expressed as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\overset{\bullet}{{\bf V}}}(t)={\bf f}\big({\bf V}(t),x_{s}(t)\big),\quad 0\leq t,\\[4.97931pt]
{\bf V}(0)={\bf V}_{0},\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
{\bf V}_{0}=({\rm V}_{{\rm E}0},{\rm V}_{{\rm I}0},{\rm V}_{{\rm T}0})^{\tt t}
and

{\bf f}\big({\bf V},x_{s}\big)=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\big(-{\rm V}_{\rm E}+{\rm V}_{{\rm E}0}+g_{s{\rm E}}(x_{s})\big)\tau_{\rm E}^{-1}\\[2.84544pt]
\big(-{\rm V}_{\rm I}+{\rm V}_{{\rm I}0}+{\rm G}_{{\rm T}}({\rm V}_{{\rm T}}+{\rm C}_{{\rm I}}\big)\tau_{\rm I}^{-1}\\[2.84544pt]
\big(-{\rm V}_{\rm T}+{\rm V}_{{\rm T}0}-{\rm G}_{{\rm I}}({\rm V}_{{\rm I}})+{\rm G}_{{\rm E}}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm E}}\big)+g_{s{\rm T}}(x_{s})+g_{l{\rm T}}(x_{l})\tau_{\rm T}^{-1}\end{array}\right)

(2)

(see more details in (
18
) and (
24
) below).
The formulation as an optimal control problem arises from minimizing a functional in which the pain at the final time is assumed to be an increasing function of the
{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}(t_{f})
potential (for a given final time
t_{f}>0)
)

{\rm W}({\bf V}_{0})\doteq\min_{x_{s}\in\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{J}_{{\bf V}_{0}}(x_{s}),\quad\mathbb{J}_{{\bf V}_{0}}(x_{s})\doteq\int^{t_{f}}_{0}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}|{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}(\sigma)|^{2}-{\rm Q}g_{s{\rm T}}\big(x_{s}(\sigma)\right)d\sigma+{\rm S}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm T}}(t_{f})\big).

(3)

Here, the set of controls is given by
\mathcal{U}={\rm L}^{\infty}\big({\mathbb{R}}_{+}:[\underline{x_{s}},\overline{x_{s}}]\big)
,
{\rm Q}
is a given positive constant, and
{\rm S}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
function satisfying
0<{\rm S}_{-}^{\prime}\leq{\rm S}^{\prime}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm T}}\big)\leq{\rm S}^{\prime}_{+}
. From a practical point of view, and as is common in Control Theory, the first term of the functional
\mathbb{J}_{{\rm V}}
represents the ”cost of the state” (which, indirectly, implies that we aim to minimize the electrical energy). The second term represents a quantitative measure of pain at a prescribed (but arbitrary) fixed final time.

This functional is not convex with respect to the control due to the nonlinearity of the state equations; thus, the uniqueness of the minimum is not guaranteed by the abstract theory. Nevertheless, we will prove that the Pontryagin Principle applies. Consequently, we will demonstrate the existence of a ”bang-bang” type control and explicitly characterize the unique control that has a single point of discontinuity.

The organization of this paper is as follows: Section 2 is devoted to a description of the modeling, leading to the coupled system under consideration. The well-posedness of such a system and, especially, the monotonicity properties of the components of the solution with respect to the data are presented in Section 3, where we provide several quantitative estimates that give a sharper formulation of some of the results in
[
BS
]
. We also present a series of figures showing the simulation of some special cases using
MATLAB
. Finally, Section 4, containing the main results of this paper, is devoted to the study of the optimal control problem.

We mention that our mathematical approach to this model contains several improvements over the important paper by
[
BS
]
, and, for instance, includes a series of figures resulting from some
MATLAB
approximations. Moreover, the consideration of the associated control problem is entirely new compared to the previous literature on the model. Clearly, the consideration of the control study of the model leads to a very useful methodology in this process since it can be adapted to each patient in a more singularized way (physical characteristics, pain sensitivity, etc.).

2
On the mathematical model

The key ideas for the construction of a mathematical model related to pain were presented in the seminal paper by N.F. Britton and S.M. Skevington in 1988
[
BS
]
. As stated in that work (where some earlier references are cited), any theory of pain must be able to account for the following observations:
(i) Increased stimulation of the small nerve fibers in the skin usually increases pain.
(ii) Increased stimulation of the large nerve fibers may increase pain temporarily, but in the long term, it may relieve it.
(iii) Pain relief can be achieved through electrical stimulation of the grey matter in the midbrain.
(iv) Injuries that would normally cause severe pain sometimes cause little or no pain, or the onset of pain is delayed.
(v) In some cases, the anticipation of pain is enough to raise anxiety levels, thereby intensifying the perception of pain.

in the large fibers of the considered area of skin. We will assume that the frequency of the outputs
from the cognitive control and the descending inhibitory control are strictly increasing functions of the inputs, i.e.,

\overbrace{x_{d}=\varphi(x_{\rm T}),}^{\hbox{\tiny{descending inhibitory control}}}\qquad\overbrace{x_{c}=\psi(x_{l})}^{\hbox{\tiny cognitive control}},

(4)

Theorem 2

assumed

\quad{}_{\Box}

4
Optimal Control Problem

In this section, we consider the optimal control problem associated with the equation (
16
) for the potentials
{\bf V}(t)=\big({\rm V}_{{\rm E}}(t),{\rm V}_{{\rm I}}(t),{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}(t)\big)^{\tt t}
when they are controlled by the short frequency
x_{s}
. Let us emphasize the system in the following way:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\overset{\bullet}{{\bf V}}}(t)={\bf f}\big({\bf V}(t),x_{s}(t)\big),\quad 0\leq t,\\[4.97931pt]
{\bf V}(0)={\bf V}_{0},\end{array}\right.

(36)

where
{\bf V}_{0}=({\rm V}_{{\rm E}0},{\rm V}_{{\rm I}0},{\rm V}_{{\rm T}0})^{\tt t}

\big(-{\rm V}_{\rm I}+{\rm G}_{{\rm T}}({\rm V}_{{\rm T}})+{\rm C}_{{\rm I}}\big)\tau_{\rm I}^{-1}\\[2.84544pt]
\big(-{\rm V}_{\rm T}-{\rm G}_{{\rm I}}({\rm V}_{{\rm I}})+{\rm G}_{{\rm E}}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm E}}\big)+\underbrace{{\rm V}_{{\rm T}0}+g_{s{\rm T}}(x_{s})+g_{l{\rm T}}(x_{l})}_{{\rm C}_{{\rm T}}}\big)\tau_{\rm T}^{-1}\end{array}\right)

(37)

(see (

(38)

\mathcal{U}={\rm L}^{\infty}\big({\mathbb{R}}_{+}:[\underline{x_{s}},\overline{x_{s}}]\big)
,
{\rm Q}
is a given positive constant and
{\rm S}
a
\mathcal{C}^{1}

0<{\rm S}_{-}^{\prime}\leq{\rm S}^{\prime}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm T}}\big)\leq{\rm S}^{\prime}_{+}

[

]
we consider the states,
{\bf V}

{\bf P}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\overset{\bullet}{{\bf V}^{*}}}(t)={\rm D}_{{\bf P}}{\rm H}\big({\bf V}^{*}(t),{\bf P}^{*}(t),x_{s}^{*}(t)\big),&\quad{\bf V}^{*}(0)={\bf V}_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\\[3.55658pt]

\displaystyle{\overset{\bullet}{{\bf P}^{*}}}(t)=\mathcal{A}(t){\bf P}^{*}(t)+{\bf F}(t),

0\\
{\rm V}^{*}_{{\rm T}}(t)\end{array}\right)

(see (

\Phi(t-t_{f})\doteq\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\rm P}_{{\rm E}1}^{*}(t)&{\rm P}_{{\rm E}2}^{*}(t)&{\rm P}_{{\rm E}3}^{*}(t)\\[4.26773pt]

Theorem 4
(Existence of an Optimal Control)

Let us assume that

the functions
{\rm G}_{{\rm T}}({\rm V})
and
{\rm G}_{{\rm E}}({\rm V})
are convex and the function
{\rm G}_{{\rm I}}({\rm V})
is concave

(45)

(

18

)

x_{s}^{*}\in{\rm L}^{\infty}(\underline{x},\overline{x})

Proof
. The result follows from the proof of Theorem 5.2.2 of
[
BP

\mathcal{F}({\bf V})=\left\{({\bf y},y_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}:~y_{0}\geq\dfrac{1}{2}|{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}|^{2}+{\rm Q}g_{s{\rm T}}(x_{s}),~{\bf y}(t)={\bf f}\big({\bf V}(t),x_{s}\big),~x_{s}\in[\underline{x},\overline{x}]\right\},\quad{\bf V}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

\quad{}_{\Box}

Remark 15

We emphasize that the above proof does not require the convexity of the functional
\mathbb{J}_{{\bf V}_{0}}(x_{s})
with respect to the control
x_{s}(\cdot)
. This remark was already pointed out in
[
Tr
]

\quad{}_{\Box}

The property
{\rm P}_{{\rm E}}^{*}(t)\equiv 0

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm H}\big({\bf V}^{*},{\bf P}^{*},a\big)&\hskip-5.69046pt=\left(-{\rm V}_{\rm I}^{*}+{\rm G}_{{\rm T}}({\rm V}_{{\rm T}}^{*})+{\rm C}_{{\rm I}}\right)\tau_{\rm I}^{-1}{\rm P}^{*}_{{\rm I}}\\
&+\left(-({\rm V}_{\rm T}^{*}-{\rm V}_{{\rm T}0})+g_{s{\rm T}}(a)+g_{l{\rm T}}(x_{l})+{\rm G}_{{\rm E}}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm E}}^{*}\big)-{\rm G}_{\rm I}\big({\rm V}_{\rm I}^{*}\big)\right)\tau_{\rm T}^{-1}{\rm P}^{*}_{{\rm T}}\\
&-\dfrac{1}{2}({\rm V}_{\rm T}^{*}(\sigma))^{2}+{\rm Q}g_{s{\rm T}}(a).\end{array}

Therefore the Pontryagin Maximum Principle (see

A reduced version of it leads to

\big(\tau_{\rm T}^{-1}{\rm P}^{*}_{{\rm T}}(t)-{\rm Q}\big)g_{s{\rm T}}(x_{s}^{*}(t))=\max_{a\in[\underline{x_{s}},\overline{x_{s}}]}\left[\big(\tau_{\rm T}^{-1}{\rm P}^{*}_{{\rm T}}(t)+{\rm Q}\big)g_{s{\rm T}}(a)\right].

This shows that the optimal control
x_{s}^{*}(t)
is determined by the maximum values of the bounded real function.

[\underline{x_{s}},\overline{x_{s}}]\ni a\mapsto\big(\tau_{\rm T}^{-1}{\rm P}^{*}_{{\rm T}}(t)+{\rm Q}\big)g_{s{\rm T}}(a),

(46)

for almost any
t
.

Theorem 5
(Uniqueness of the optimal control)

Assume

{\rm S}^{\prime}_{+}<\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q}

(49)

and the compatibility condition

0<\ln\left(\dfrac{{\rm K}-{\rm S}^{\prime}_{-}}{{\rm K}-\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q}}\right)^{\tau_{{\rm T}}}<t_{f},

(50)

where
{\rm K}
is a constant large enough such that

{\rm K}>\max\left\{-\min_{t\in[0,\tau_{f}]}{\rm G}_{{\rm E}}^{\prime}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm E}}^{*}(t)\big){\rm P}_{{\rm I}}^{*}(t)+{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t),{\rm S}_{-}^{\prime},\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q},\dfrac{\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q}e^{\frac{t_{f}}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}-{\rm S}^{\prime}_{-}}{e^{\frac{t_{f}}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}-1}\right\}.

(51)

Then the optimal control is the bang-bang type control

x_{s}^{*}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\underline{x_{s}},&0<t<t_{*},\\[2.84544pt]
\overline{x_{s}},&t_{*}<t<t_{f}.\end{array}\right.

(52)

Moreover, the switching time
t^{*}
verifies the lower estimate

0<t_{f}-\ln\left(\dfrac{{\rm K}-{\rm S}^{\prime}_{-}}{{\rm K}-\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q}}\right)^{\tau_{{\rm T}}}<t_{*}.

(53)

Proof
. The final problem on
{\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}
is

\tau_{{\rm T}}\displaystyle{\overset{\bullet}{{\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}}}(t)={\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t)-{\rm G}_{{\rm E}}^{\prime}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm E}}^{*}(t)\big){\rm P}_{{\rm I}}^{*}(t)-{\rm V}^{*}_{{\rm E}}(t),\quad{\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t_{f})=-{\rm S}^{\prime}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t_{f})\big).

Then we get the inequality

\tau_{{\rm T}}\displaystyle{\overset{\bullet}{{\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}}}(t)>{\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t)+{\rm K},\quad\hbox{ with }{\rm K}>-\max_{t\in[0,t_{f}]}\left({\rm G}_{{\rm E}}^{\prime}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm E}}^{*}(t)\big){\rm P}_{{\rm I}}^{*}(t)+{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t)\right).

Therefore

\left(\displaystyle{\overset{\bullet}{{\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}}}(t)-\dfrac{{\rm P}^{*}_{{\rm T}}(t)}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}\right)e^{-\frac{t}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}>\dfrac{{\rm K}}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}e^{-\frac{t}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\big({\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t)+{\rm K}\big)e^{-\frac{t}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}\right)>0,

implies

\big({\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t_{2})+{\rm K}\big)e^{-\frac{t_{2}}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}>\big({\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t_{1})+{\rm K}\big)e^{-\frac{t_{1}}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}},

(54)

whenever
t_{2}>t_{1}
.
Next, by means of (
51
), we prove that assumption (
49
) implies the existence of a unique switching time
t_{*}
. Indeed, otherwise

{\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t)>-\tau_{{\rm E}}{\rm Q}\quad\hbox{for all $t$ in the interval $[0,t_{f}]$}

(see (
49
)). Then, from (
54

\big({\rm K}-{\rm S}_{-}^{\prime}\big)e^{-\frac{t_{f}}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}>\big({\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t_{f})+{\rm K}\big)e^{-\frac{t_{f}}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}>\big({\rm P}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t)+{\rm K}\big)e^{-\frac{t}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}>\big({\rm K}-\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q}\big)e^{-\frac{t}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}},

whence by choosing
{\rm K}>\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q}
we deduce
{\rm K}>{\rm S}_{-}^{\prime}
and

\big({\rm K}-{\rm S}_{-}^{\prime}\big)e^{-\frac{t_{f}-\sigma}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}>{\rm K}-\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q}\quad\hbox{for all $t$ in the interval $[0,t_{f}]$}.

Then, since
{\rm S}_{-}^{\prime}\leq{\rm S}_{+}^{\prime}<\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q}

49

0<\ln\left(\dfrac{{\rm K}-{\rm S}^{\prime}_{-}}{{\rm K}-\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q}}\right)^{\tau_{{\rm T}}}<t_{f}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\rm K}>\dfrac{\tau_{{\rm T}}{\rm Q}e^{\frac{t_{f}}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}-{\rm S}^{\prime}_{-}}{e^{\frac{t_{f}}{\tau_{{\rm T}}}}-1},

where we are assuming (
51

Consequently, there exists a time

Theorem 6
(Characterization of the optimal switching time)

Under the assumptions of Theorem
53
, the optimal time of change
t_{*}
of the optimal control
(

)

\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t_{*}}\int^{t_{f}}_{0}|{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}^{*}\big(\sigma;t_{*}\big)|^{2}d\sigma-{\rm Q}\big(g_{s{\rm T}}\big(\overline{x_{s}}\big)-g_{s{\rm T}}\big(\underline{x_{s}}\big)\big)+{\rm S}^{\prime}\big({\rm V}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t_{f};t_{*})\big)\dfrac{\partial{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t_{f};t_{*})\big)}{\partial t_{*}}=0,

(57)

where we have denoting by
{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t;t_{*})
to the optimal state .

Proof
For every time
\widehat{t}_{*}\in[0,t_{f}]
we consider a general bang-bang control

x_{s}^{*}(t;\widehat{t}_{*})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{x_{s}},&0\leq t<\widehat{t}_{*},\\[2.84544pt]
\underline{x_{s}},&\widehat{t}_{*}<t\leq t_{f}.\end{array}\right.

(58)

Then, by the above Theorem we have that
\widehat{t}_{*}=t_{*}\in\big(0,t_{f}\big)
for the
optimal control
(

Let us denote by
\widehat{\rm V}_{{\rm T}}^{*}(t;\widehat{t}_{*})
the associate state corresponding to the control
x_{s}^{*}(t;\widehat{t}_{*})

\Phi(\widehat{t}_{*})=\mathbb{J}(x_{s}^{*}(\cdot;\widehat{t}_{*})).

Therefore, one has

\min_{x_{s}\in\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{J}(x_{s})=\mathbb{J}(x_{s}^{*})=\Phi(t_{*})=\min_{\widehat{t}_{*}\in[0,t_{f}]}\Phi(\widehat{t}_{*}).

Thus, the optimal switching time
t_{*}
must satisfies

\left.\dfrac{d\Phi(\widehat{t}_{*})}{d\widehat{t}_{*}}\right|_{\widehat{t}_{*}=t_{*}}=0.

\quad{}_{\Box}

Coming back to the equation of the

Then

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm V}_{{\rm E}}^{*}(t)&\hskip-5.69046pt=g_{s{\rm E}}\big(\underline{x_{s}}\big)\left(1-e^{-\frac{t}{\tau_{{\rm E}}}}\right)+{\rm V}_{{\rm E}0},\quad 0\leq t\leq t_{*},\\[5.69046pt]
&\quad\left({\rm V}_{{\rm E}0}+g\big(\overline{x_{s}}\big)\right)\left(1-e^{-\frac{t-t_{*}}{\tau_{{\rm E}}}}\right)+\left(g\big(\underline{x_{s}}\big)\left(1-e^{-\frac{t_{*}}{\tau_{{\rm E}}}}\right)+{\rm V}_{{\rm E}0}\right)e^{-\frac{t-t_{*}}{\tau_{{\rm E}}}},\quad t_{*}<t\leq t_{f}.\end{array}

(59)

\quad{}_{\Box}

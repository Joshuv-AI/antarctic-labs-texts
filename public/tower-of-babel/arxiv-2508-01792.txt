Strong relations between discrete surfaces, poset-based connected manifolds, and normal pseudomanifolds
Nicolas Boutry

Abstract
In this paper, we study some relationships existing between some particular mathematical structures: discrete surfaces coming from discrete topology and mathematical morphology, poset-based connected manifolds coming from discrete topology, and normal pseudomanifolds which are much used in discrete geometry and topological data analysis. We will also show that, even when poset-based connected manifolds are assumed to be simplicial complexes, and then supplied with many additional topological properties, they are not necessarily smooth. A set of sufficient conditions to ensure that poset-based connected manifolds are smooth will be provided.

As recalled in

On an Alexandrov spaces

Definition 2

•

The only poset being at the same time a PCM and a discrete surface is the empty order. In other words, this set is isomorphic to a

•

Let

•

Let

•

Let

Let

Proposition 5
.

When
X
is a simplicial complex of rank
n\geq 0
, then for any
x\in X
and for any

, then
\left|\beta^{\square}_{X}(y)\cap\alpha^{\square}_{X}(x)\right|
is a
(\mathrm{dim}(x)-\mathrm{dim}(y)-2)
-surface.

Proof:
We recall that when
X
is a simplicial complex of rank
n\geq 0
, then
|\alpha^{\square}_{X}(x)|
is a discrete
(\mathrm{rk}(x,X)-1)
-surface by Proposition
4
. Now, let

be an element of
\alpha^{\square}_{X}(x)
, then
\left|\theta^{\square}_{\alpha^{\square}_{X}(x)}(y)\right|
is a discrete
(\mathrm{rk}(x,X)-2)
-surface. However,

\theta^{\square}_{\alpha^{\square}_{X}(x)}(y)=\beta^{\square}_{\alpha^{\square}_{X}(x)}(y)*\alpha^{\square}_{\alpha^{\square}_{X}(x)}(y)=(\beta^{\square}_{X}(y)\cap\alpha^{\square}_{X}(x))*\alpha^{\square}_{X}(y),

so

is a
(\mathrm{rk}(x,X)-\mathrm{rk}(y,X)-2)
-surface (since
|\alpha^{\square}_{X}(y)|
is a discrete
(\mathrm{rk}(y,X)-1)
-surface). Since in simplicial complexes, the rank is equal to the dimension, we obtain that

is a
(\mathrm{dim}(x)-\mathrm{dim}(y)-2)
-surface.

The proof is done. ∎

3.2
Properties of the border (Part 1)

Lemma 1
.

Let
X
be a simplicial complex of rank
n\geq 0
, and
h
be an element of
\Delta X
. Then we have the following remarkable property:

\theta^{\square}_{\Delta X}(h)\subseteq\Delta\theta^{\square}_{X}(h).

Proof:
Let
X
be some simplicial complex of rank
n\geq 0
. Let
h
be some element of
X
, and let
h^{\prime}
be some element of
\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)
.

We obtain naturally that
h^{\prime}\in\Delta X
and that
h^{\prime}\in\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)
.

We want to show that
h^{\prime}\in\Delta\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)
, that is, that:

\left|\theta^{\square}_{\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)}(h^{\prime})\right|

is
not
a discrete
(n-2)
-surface.

First case :
when
h^{\prime}\in\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)
, we can decompose this term in this way:

\left|\theta^{\square}_{\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)}(h^{\prime})\right|=\left|\beta^{\square}_{\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)}(h^{\prime})\right|*\left|\alpha^{\square}_{\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)}(h^{\prime})\right|=\left|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})\right|*\left|\alpha^{\square}_{\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)}(h^{\prime})\right|,

where we can decompose the last term:

\left|\alpha^{\square}_{\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)}(h^{\prime})\right|=|\alpha^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})\cap\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)|=\left|\theta^{\square}_{\alpha^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})}(h)\right|=\left|\beta^{\square}_{\alpha^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})}(h)\right|*\left|\alpha^{\square}_{\alpha^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})}(h)\right|,

equal to
\left|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)\cap\alpha^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})\right|*\left|\alpha^{\square}_{X}(h)\right|

which is the join of the
(\mathrm{rk}(h^{\prime},X)-\mathrm{rk}(h,X)-2)
by Proposition
5
and the
(\mathrm{rk}(h,X)-1)
-surface, that is, a
(\mathrm{rk}(h^{\prime},X)-2)
-surface. Now, let us assume that
\left|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})\right|
(H) is a
(n-\mathrm{rk}(h^{\prime},X)-1)
-surface, it would imply that

|\theta^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})|=|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})|*|\alpha^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})|

is a discrete
(n-\mathrm{rk}(h^{\prime},X)-1)+(\mathrm{rk}(h^{\prime},X)-1)+1=(n-1)
-surface, which is impossible since
h^{\prime}\in\Delta X
. So (H) is false. This implies that
\left|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})\right|
is not a discrete
(n-\mathrm{rk}(h^{\prime},X)-1)
-surface, so
h^{\prime}\in\Delta\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)
.

Secund case :
when
h^{\prime}\in\alpha^{\square}_{X}(h)
, we obtain a similar reasoning.

Thus, we have in any case that
\theta^{\square}_{\Delta X}(h)\subseteq\Delta\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)
and the proof is done. ∎

This leads to the strong following proposition.

Proposition 13
.

Let
X
be some simplicial complex. When

is an
n

Let

. When
n=0
, the pseudomanifold

is a degenerated set made of one vertex, then it is a
0
-PCM. When
n\geq 1
and

, then
|X|
is connected (since it is a pseudomanifold) and additionally, for any

,

is an
(n-1)
-surface, so

is a
1
-surface.

The case that we have to treat now is then when

admits a (non-empty) border (
\triangle X\neq\emptyset
) with
n\geq 1
. Let
|X|
satisfy these properties and let us prove it is an
n
-PCM.
|X|
is a pseudomanifold, then it is connected. Also, for any
h\in X
, when
h\not\in\triangle X
,
|\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)|
is an
(n-1)
-surface. We want then to show that, when

,
|\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)|
is an
(n-1)
-PCM.

Let us prove that by induction with as induction hypothesis for
k\geq 1
:

H_{k}:=\left\{\forall\ h\in\triangle X,\ |\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)|\text{ is an $(n-\mathrm{dim}(h)-1)$-PCM with $\mathrm{dim}(h)=n-k$}\right\}.

Initialization
(k=0)
:
When
\mathrm{dim}(h)=n
, we obtain naturally that for any face
h
of dimension
n
,
|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)|
is the empty order, that is, a
(-1)
-PCM and
H_{0}
is true.

Initialization
(k=1)
:
When
\mathrm{dim}(h)=n-1
, then
\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)
is made of one or two
n
-faces since
X
is a pseudomanifold. However, the case with two elements, that is, two
n
-faces of
X
, is impossible since in this case we obtain that
|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)|
is a
0
-surface, and then by the joining operation,
|\theta^{\square}_{X}(h)|
is an
(n-1)
-surface, thus
h\not\in\triangle X
, which contradicts the hypotheses. Thus,
|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)|
is a
0
-PCM (made of only one
n
-face) and
H_{1}
is true.

Heredity (
k\geq 2
)
: we assume that
H_{k^{\prime}}
is true for
k^{\prime}\in[0,k-1]
, and we want to prove that this implies
H_{k}
. Now, let us treat the case
\mathrm{dim}(h)=n-k\geq 2
. Since
|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)|
is isomorphic to
|\mathrm{lk}(h,X)|
and

is normal by hypothesis, then
|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)|
is connected. Now, we want to prove that for any
h^{\prime}\in\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)
, the term
\left|\theta^{\square}_{\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)}(h^{\prime})\right|
is either an
(n-\mathrm{dim}(h)-2)
-surface or an
(n-\mathrm{dim}(h)-2)
-PCM.

•

When
h^{\prime}\not\in\triangle X
, then
|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})|
is an
(n-\mathrm{dim}(h^{\prime})-1)
-surface, so we obtain:

|\theta^{\square}_{\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)}(h^{\prime})|=|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})|*|(\alpha^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})\cap\beta^{\square}_{X}(h))|

which is the join of a
(n-\mathrm{dim}(h^{\prime})-1)
-surface by Proposition
4
and a
(\mathrm{dim}(h^{\prime})-\mathrm{dim}(h)-2)
-surface by Proposition
5
, it is then a
(n-\mathrm{dim}(h)-2)
-surface by Proposition
2
.

•

When

: since

, we have
\mathrm{dim}(h^{\prime})\geq\mathrm{dim}(h)+1
, that is,

\mathrm{dim}(h^{\prime})\in\llbracket n-k+1,n\rrbracket=\llbracket n-(k-1),n-0\rrbracket,

which corresponds to the hypotheses

, …,
H_{0}
, assumed to be true (they are induction hypotheses). Thus, we know that
|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})|
is an
(n-\mathrm{dim}(h^{\prime})-1)
-PCM, so we obtain:

\left|\theta^{\square}_{\beta^{\square}_{X}(h)}(h^{\prime})\right|=|\beta^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})|*|(\alpha^{\square}_{X}(h^{\prime})\cap\beta^{\square}_{X}(h))|

which is the join of a
(n-\mathrm{dim}(h^{\prime})-1)
-PCM by Proposition
4
and a
(\mathrm{dim}(h^{\prime})-\mathrm{dim}(h)-2)
-surface by Proposition
5
, it is then a
(n-\mathrm{dim}(h)-2)
-PCM.

The proof is done. ∎

4.3
Generalization of our main results

Let us conclude this section with an important theorem.

Theorem 3
.

When
X
is a simplicial complex of rank
n\geq 0
,

•

either
\triangle X=\emptyset
, and in this case
X
is a discrete surface iff it is a normal pseudomanifold,

•

or
\triangle X\neq\emptyset
, and
X
is a
n
-PCM iff it is a normal pseudomanifold.

In other words,
n
-PCM’s and discrete surfaces are a
generalization
of normal pseudomanifolds to posets.

When

6
Conclusion

We will have seen that in the specific context of simplicial complexes, discrete surfaces are equivalent to normal pseudomanifolds without border and that
n
-PCM’s are equivalent to normal pseudomanifold with border. We will have also seen that it means a very strong property of these two combinatorial structures: PCM’s and discrete surfaces are together the generalization of normal pseudomanifolds from simplicial complexes to posets.

Furthermore, we will have seen a counter-example showing that there exist
n
-PCM’s which are not smooth
n
-PCM’s, even if they are simplicial complexes (and then supplied with many additional properties). This clearly shows how much it is important for normal pseudomanifolds to be smooth
n
-PCM’s and not only
n
-PCM’s (assuming that when their border is not empty).

7
Future works

The theoretical results we obtain here have a direct consequence from a
computational
point of view: starting from a simplicial complex
X
, and assuming that the border
\triangle X
(empty or not) is already computed or given, checking with some algorithm whether
X
is a normal
n
-pseudomanifold will indicate if
X
is an
n
-PCM (when
\Delta X\neq\emptyset
) and if
X
is a discrete
n
-surface (when
\Delta X=\emptyset
). In other words, we will be able to determine the nature of
X
without the computationally expensive
recursive
procedures induced by the definitions of PCM’s or discrete surfaces. We plan then in the future to implement algorithms able to compute a border, to check whether a simplicial complex is a normal pseudomanifold, a PCM or a discrete surface, and to calculate their respective algorithmic complexities.

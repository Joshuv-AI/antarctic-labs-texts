Eccentric millisecond pulsar + subdwarf B star from rotationally delayed accretion-induced-collapse scenario Eccentric millisecond pulsar + subdwarf B star from rotationally delayed accretion-induced-collapse scenario – References 2018
Xiang-Cun Meng

Abstract
Neutron star + helium star systems are attracting more and more attentions. Following the rotationally delayed accretion-induced-collapse (RD-AIC) scenario, I predict that there could be eccentric millisecond pulsar + subdwarf B (MSP + sdB), at least eccentric neutron star + subdwarf B (NS + sdB) systems in the Galaxy. I show the predictions on their orbital parameters, including MSP mass, secondary mass, eccentricity and orbital period. Based on two detailed binary population synthesis calculations, we find that their Galactic birth rate is ( 0.67 − 1.5 ) × 10 − 4 ​ yr − 1 (0.67-1.5)\times 10^{\rm-4}~{\rm yr^{\rm-1}} . Then, a very conservative upper limit of their number in the Galaxy is 6700-15000. They have an age of hundreds of Myr and then should be discovered in relatively young environments. In addition, most of the MSPs in the eccentric MSP + sdB systems have a mass less massive than 1.5 M ⊙ M_{\odot} . I simply discuss their future potential applications in astrophysical fields.



1
INTRODUCTION

Millisecond pulsars (MSPs) are characterized by a weak surface
magnetic fields of
B\sim 10^{\rm 8}
G, a spin-period of shorter
than 30 ms, a period derivative of less than
10^{\rm-19}{\rm s\,s^{\rm-1}}
and a nearly circular orbit. They are very important
to constrain the equation of state (EoS) of neutron stars (NSs,

Özel & Freire 2016
). It is widely accepted that the MSPs are
produced from low-mass X-ray binaries (LMXBs) via a recycling
process, in which a pulsar that passed through the death line is
spun up to millisecond period via accretion from its Roche
lobe-filling companion (
Alpar et al. 1982
;

Radhakrishnan & Srinivasan 1982
;
Bhattacharyya & van den Heuvel 1991
). The tidal
torques during accretion cause the binary systems to be highly
circular (
Phinney & Kulkarni 1994
). This scenario has been proved by
the discoveries of the bridges between LMXBs and MSPs
(
Wijnands & van der Klis 1998
;
Archibald et al. 2009
) and the facts that
almost all of the fully recycled MSPs with helium white dwarf (WD)
companions in the Galaxy have very small eccentricities
(
Manchester et al. 2005
).

However, the discoveries of several eccentric MSPs indicate that
the nature behind recycling scenario could be more complicated
than imaginary before. The companions of the discovered eccentric
MSPs may be main sequence (MS) stars or low-mass helium WDs
(
Champion 2008
;
Barr et al. 2017
;
Octau et al. 2018
;

Stovall et al. 2019
;
Zhu et al. 2019
;
Serylak et al. 2022
). The
origin of the eccentric MSPs is still unclear, although several
possible scenarios are proposed. Generally, the proposed models
refer to asymmetrical mass loss from the system or interaction
between the binary MSPs and their environments. I) The dynamical
perturbation in a hierarchical triple system ejects one member and
then leaves an eccentric binary (
Freire et al. 2011
). II) Due to
mass loss from the progenitor system of an eccentric MSP, a
circumbinary (CB) disk may form around the binary system. The CB
disk could interact with the inner binary system, which leads to
the growth of the eccentricity of the binary system
(
Antoniadis 2014
). III) A kick from a series of
asymmetrical mass ejections from proto-WD may also results in the
formation of the eccentric MSPs (
Han & Li 2021
;
Tang et al. 2023
). IV) The
accretion-induced collapse (AIC) of a rapidly rotating
super-chandrasekhar mass WD could directly form a MSP, during
which the mass loss may lead to an eccentric binary MSP
(
Freire & Tauris 2014
;
Wang & Gong 2023
). V) Similarly, the phase transition could
occur from a rapidly rotating massive NS to a strange star, and a
possible sudden mass loss during the phase transition could also
produce an eccentric MSP binary (
Jiang et al. 2015
). VI) The
large eccentricity of an eccentric MSP is resonantly driven by
convective flows when a red giant convective eddy turnover time
matches the tidally locked red giant’s spin period
(
Ginzburg & Chiang 2022
).

NS + subdwarf B (sdB) systems are getting more and more attentions on both theoretical and observational sides, and some projects have been proposed to search for NS + sdB systems for more than 10 years (e.g.
Geier et al. 2011
;
Wu et al. 2020
). Recently,
a compact binary system consisting of a MSP + a stripped helium star
is reported (
Yang et al. 2025
). This MSP system is in a circular orbit,
as predicted by standard recycling scenario (
Guo et al. 2025
). This discovery are arousing some questions in the field now, e.g.,
considering that the discovery of some eccentric MSP + He WD systems challenges the standard recycling scenario, are there eccentric MSP + sdB systems? If yes, how many do the eccentric MSP + sdB systems exist
in our Galaxy? What properties do they have? In this paper, we want to address these problems following the model
proposed by
Freire & Tauris 2014
, which is referred as rotationally
delayed AIC (RD-AIC) scenario.

Recently, to study the properties of the surviving companions of
Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) in supernova remnants (SNRs) and based
on the so-called spin-up/spin-down model (
Justham 2011
;

Di Stefano & Kilic 2012
),
Meng & Li 2019
found that before
supernova explosion, the companion of a rapidly rotating
carbon-oxygen (CO) WD may be a sdB star (see also

Justham 2011
). If the rapidly rotating WD is an
oxygen-neon-magnesium (ONeMg) WD, an AIC could occur, rather than
a SN Ia. Following the RD-AIC scenario, the newborn system could
consist of a MSP + a sdB star with an eccentric orbit, at least an
eccentric NS + sdB system. In this paper, I will study the
properties of the potential undiscovered eccentric MSP + sdB
systems (
Oostrum et al. 2020
).

In section
2
, I simply describe my methods, and I
present the results in section
3
. In section

4
, I show discussions and my main conclusions.

2
METHODS

As far as the potential eccentric MSP + sdB systems from the
RD-AIC scenario, their lifetimes may be much shorter than normal
MSPs from recycled scenario because massive WDs tend to have
higher magnetic fields (
10^{\rm 6}\sim 10^{\rm 9}
G,

Wickramasinghe & Ferrario 2005
), which leads to the post-AIC MSPs with a strong
magnetic field of
10^{\rm 11}\sim 10^{\rm 14}
G, as high as
magnetar. Only those from the WDs with very weak magnetic fields
may show the properties of normal MSPs (see also

Ivanova et al. 2008
). Here, I simply assume that a post-AIC NS
behaves as a MSP no matter how strong its magnetic field is, and
then how long its lifetime. I will discuss this further in
Sec.
4
.

Following
Meng & Li 2019
, the initial binary systems are ONeMg WD +
MS systems, and mass transfer begins when the companions are on
the MS or in the Hertzsprung gap (HG) (see also details in

Meng & Podsiadlowski 2017
). The basic methods and assumptions for the
binary evolution are the same to
Meng & Li 2019
before AIC, and
then we do not give the repetitious details here. As far as the maximum WD is concerned, a rigid rotation is assumed for the pre-AIC WD and then the maximum WD mass is 1.48

M_{\odot}
in
Freire & Tauris 2014
(see
Meng & Li 2019
and

Tauris et al. 2013
). Actually, if a differential rotation is considered, the maximum mass may be up to 4.0
M_{\odot}
(
Yoon & Langer 2004
;
Yoon & Langer 2005
). In this paper, I assume that the pre-AIC WD may be differentially rotating if its mass exceeds 1.48

M_{\odot}
. The WD mass in this paper is then as massive as 2.6
M_{\odot}
. The rapidly rotating super-Chandrasekhar WDs will experience a spin-down phase before an AIC process. However, at present, many uncertainties are still not resolved in the
spin-up/spin-down model, e.g. the exact spin-down timescale and the exact time of the onset of the spin-down phase (
Meng & Podsiadlowski 2013
). Following
Meng & Li 2019
and
Meng & Luo 2021
, I define a pseudo-spin-down timescale,
\tau_{\rm sp}=10^{\rm 7}
yr, in which
\tau_{\rm sp}
represents a time interval from the moment of
M_{\rm WD}=1.378

M_{\odot}
to the time of AIC (see also
Meng & Podsiadlowski 2013
)

. For the AIC process, I take similar

). This means that the

a

\frac{a}{a_{\rm 0}}=\frac{M_{\rm 0}-\Delta M}{M_{\rm 0}-2\Delta M},

Freire & Tauris 2014

Based on the calculations in
Meng & Li 2019
, I may get the
parameter space in the initial orbital period - secondary mass
plane in which an ONeMg WD + MS system may become an eccentric MSP
+ sdB system after AIC. To obtain the birth rate of the eccentric
MSP + sdB systems, I made two binary population synthesis (BPS)
calculations using the rapid binary evolution code developed by

Hurley et al. 2000
;
Hurley et al. 2002
. I assume that if the initial orbital period,

P_{\rm orb}^{\rm i}
, and the initial secondary mass,
M_{\rm 2}^{\rm i}
, of an ONeMg WD + MS system are located in the
appropriate region in the (
\log P^{\rm i}-M_{\rm 2}^{\rm i}
)
plane for the eccentric MSP + sdB systems at the onset of Roche
lobe overflow (RLOF), an eccentric MSP + sdB system can be
produced. I followed the evolution of
10^{\rm 8}
sample
binaries, where the primordial binary samples are generated in a
Monte Carlo way. The assumptions and the input parameters for the
Monte Carlo simulations are the same to that in
Meng & Podsiadlowski 2017
,
and I do not show the repetitious details here. As I will show
in the next section, the common-envelope (CE) ejection efficiency,

\alpha_{\rm CE}
, is a very important parameter to affect the
birth rate of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems. Following

Meng & Podsiadlowski 2017
, I take
\alpha_{\rm CE}=1.0
or
\alpha_{\rm CE}=3.0
(see
Meng & Podsiadlowski 2017
for details)

3
RESULTS

3.1
Properties of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems

Measuring the mass of a massive NS is a very important way to
constrain the EoS of NSs, and a significant progress has been made
in this field (
Demorest et al. 2010
;
Cromartie et al. 2020
). In
Fig.
1
, I show the masses of MSPs and sdB stars in eccentric
binaries from the RD-AIC scenario for different initial WD masses.
As shown in the figure, the MSPs have a mass between 1.25

M_{\odot}
and 2.2
M_{\odot}
. The maximum MSP mass here is much
larger than that in
Freire & Tauris 2014
just because I assume that
the pre-AIC WD may be differentially rotating. The sdB stars have
a mass from 0.38
M_{\odot}
to 0.65
M_{\odot}
as standard
binary model predicted (
Han et al. 2003
). In the (
M_{\rm NS}
-
M_{\rm 2}
) plane, the systems are clearly divided into two branches, which are derived from the different evolutionary stages of the companions and differences in mass-transfer rates at the onset of RLOF for initial WD + MS systems (see detailed explanation in
Meng & Podsiadlowski 2017
)

In Fig.
2
, I show the distribution of the
eccentricities and the orbital periods of the post-AIC systems.
For a symmetric-collapse assumption (panel a), the RD-AIC scenario
leads to a very narrow range of post-AIC eccentricities:
0.09-0.17, which is slightly larger than that of the eccentric MSP
+ He WD systems from the RD-AIC scenario because of a more massive
pre-AIC WD (
Freire & Tauris 2014
). However, the post-AIC orbital
periods here range from
\sim 1.6
days to
\sim 150
days which is
much larger than that for eccentric MSP + He WD systems. The
orbital period of a post-AIC system mainly depends on its pre-AIC
binary evolution and then on the initial binary parameters of a WD
+ MS system (see also
Meng & Luo 2021
).

The eccentricity and orbital period of a post-AIC system is quite
sensitive to a kick to the MSP during the AIC. I use the formulae
in
Hills 1983
to check the potential dynamical effect to four different systems by
assuming different small kick velocities, where the direction of
the kick velocity is generated by a Monte-Carlo way. The results
are shown in the panel (b) of Fig.
2
. Similar to that
in
Freire & Tauris 2014
, the eccentricity and the orbital period of a
given system distribute in a
\surd
-shape region in the
(
e-\log P_{\rm orb}
) plane for a relatively larger kick
velocity, while in a narrow trip for a relatively smaller kick
velocity. The panel (b) shows that for a system with an
asymmetrical AIC, the post-AIC eccentricity may be as large as
0.3, even though the kick velocity is as small as 5
{\rm km\,{\rm s^{\rm-1}}}
. In addition, as expected in the formulae of

Hills 1983
, for a given value of the kick velocity, the
longer the pre-AIC orbital period, the larger the maximum of and
then the range of the post-AIC eccentricity.

3.2
Birth rate of eccentric MSP + sdB systems

Based on the parameter spaces, we carried out two BPS calculation
to obtain the birth rate of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems. In
the Fig.
4
, I show the evolution of the birth rates,

\nu
, of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems from the RD-AIC scenario
for a constant Galactic star formation rate of SFR =
5\,M_{\odot}\,{\rm yr}^{\rm-1}
(left panel) and a single starburst of
10^{\rm 11}

M_{\odot}
(right panel). The Galactic birth rate of these
systems is then
(0.67-1.5)\times 10^{\rm-4}~{\rm yr^{\rm-1}}
, which
is significantly smaller than the total birth rate of AIC events
(
Wang 2018
). Then, the number of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems may be calculated by
\nu\times\tau_{\rm MSP}
, where
\tau_{\rm MSP}
is the lifetime of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems. Assuming an optimistic lifetime of
10^{\rm 8}
yr for the MSPs with strong magnetic fields and a typical life
of
10^{\rm 8}
yr for a sdB star (
Ivanova et al. 2008
), the
number of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems in the Galaxy is then
6700 - 15000, which is similar to that of NS + sdB systems from
core collapse supernovae (
Wu et al. 2020
). The delay time for the
eccentric MSP + sdB systems is between 160 Myr and 630 Myr and the
peak of the birth rate for a single starburst locates at
\sim 300

Myr (right panel). So, these systems belong to relatively young
population as their progenitor systems indicate. Assuming the lifetime of
10^{\rm 8}
yr for sdB stars
and MSPs with strong magnetic fields, there are (6-15) such
systems in a cluster of
10^{\rm 6}

M_{\odot}
with an age of

\sim 300
Myr. This number is much smaller than that of total AIC
events in a cluster (
Ivanova et al. 2008
). Considering that the
dynamical interaction is very efficient in a cluster and then the
eccentric MSP + sdB systems with a long orbital period are likely
to be disrupted, such a number estimation could even be too
optimistic. I address this issue in the next section again.

3.3
Distribution of WDs

The main different assumptions between this paper and
Freire & Tauris 2014
is that the WDs before AIC may be differential rotating, rathe than just rigid rotating, and then the WD before AIC may be more massive than 1.48
M_{\odot}
. However, there might be various efficient angular momentum transfer mechanisms, which means that different rotation patterns would be possible. Generally speaking, the WDs are rigid rotating before AIC if their masses are smaller than 1.48
M_{\odot}
. So, a mass distribution of the WDs before AIC may provide informations on how important of the rigid rotating WDs to form the eccentric MSP + sdB systems. In Fig.
5
, I show the distribution of initial WD mass and the WD mass before AIC for different
\alpha_{\rm CE}
. It is clearly shown in the left panel of Fig.
5
that a smaller
\alpha_{\rm CE}
leads to the peak of the initial WD mass distribution to move to a higher value. This is derived from a fact that all the systems experience a CE phase before they become the ONeMg WD + MS systems. A system with a less massive WD is more likely to merge for a smaller
\alpha_{\rm CE}
value after a common envelope phase, i.e. as a result, a system with a more massive WD is more likely to survive from the common envelope evolution and then becomes a potential progenitor system of an eccentric MSP + sdB system.

For the distribution of the WD mass before AIC, three properties are worthy to be noticed in the right panel of Fig.
5
. Firstly, only about 5% to 14% WDs have a mass less than 1.48
M_{\odot}
, which indicates that the rigid rotation is not a dominant case for the AIC channel. This is mainly from the fact that an ONeMg WD generally has a large initial mass and then is easy to exceed the limit of the rigid rotation after a mass transfer occurs between the WD and its massive companion (more massive than 2.5
M_{\odot}
and see also Fig.
3
). Secondly, the peak of the distribution moves to a lower value for a higher
\alpha_{\rm CE}
, which is directly correlated with the distribution of the initial WD mass (see the left panel of Fig.
5
). Thirdly, about 50% to 70% WD have a mass less massive than about 1.68
M_{\odot}
and only about 2% to 7% WDs have a mass more massive than 2
M_{\odot}
, although in principle, a 2.6
M_{\odot}
WD is possible before AIC. This means that most of the MSPs in the eccentric MSP + sdB systems have a mass less massive than
\sim 1.5

M_{\odot}
, which provides another information to be verified by a large sample of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems in the future.

4
DISCUSSIONS AND CONCLUSIONS

In this paper, following the suggestion of
Freire & Tauris 2014
, i.e,
the RD-AIC scenario could produce the eccentric MSP + He WD
systems, I predict that there could be 6700-15000 eccentric MSP +
sdB systems in the Galaxy, whose orbital parameters are also given, e.g. their eccentricities are between 0.09 and 0.17 for a symmetric-collapse assumption (see Fig.
1
and Fig.
2
), and most of them have a MSP less massive than 1.5
M_{\odot}
. To produce such a system, the mass transfer must begin in HG for an initial ONeMg WD + MS system. Their delay time is hundreds of Myr and peaks at
\sim 300
Myr. So, such systems would be discovered in relatively young environments, e.g. in the
Galactic thin disc. In particular, considering a low kick velocity for an AIC process, such a system may have a low peculiar velocity and a disk orbit in the Galaxy. A potential discovery of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems would provide another indirect evidence for the AIC process.

To explain the observed properties of the eccentric MSP + He WD systems,
Freire & Tauris 2014
assume that the delay time of the WDs may be longer than
10^{\rm 9}
yrs, while a pseudo-spin-down timescale of
10^{\rm 7}
yrs is assumed here. There are two reasons for such long delay-time assumption in
Freire & Tauris 2014
. One is that before AIC occurs, the RG companions evolve to WD branch and mass transfer stops. Then after AIC, the eccentric MSP + He WD systems may maintain their eccentric orbits. The other is that the super-Chandrasekhar WD would have enough time to
cool down and undergo RD-AIC. In addition, in
Freire & Tauris 2014
, it is assumed that the super-Chandrasekhar ONeMg WDs are rigid rotating, which means that the WD masses are less than 1.48
M_{\odot}
(
Yoon & Langer 2004
), while we assume differential rotating WDs and then the WDs may be more massive than 2
M_{\odot}
, as shown in Figs.
5
and
6
. If our assumptions work for the eccentric MSP + sdB systems, one may expect that in principle, our assumptions have a potential to explain the properties of the observed eccentric MSP + He WD systems.

The assumption of a spin-down timescale of
10^{\rm 7}
yrs may fulfill above two constraints, at least does not conflict with them.
Firstly, a delay time of
\sim 10^{\rm 7}
yrs is definitely long enough to permit a WD of
\sim 0.3

M_{\odot}
to enter the cooling branch of WD before AIC (
Chen et al. 2017
;
Chen et al. 2017
). Secondly, although the timescale to undergo RD-AIC is quite uncertain, i.e in a range of
10^{\rm 5}
yrs to
10^{\rm 9}
yrs, depending on the redistribution of angular momentum in a super-Chandrasekhar ONeMg WD (
Yoon & Langer 2004
;
Yoon & Langer 2005
), it is still possible to occur an AIC in a super-Chandrasekhar ONeMg WD in
\sim 10^{\rm 7}
yrs, especially for those as massive as 2.4
M_{\odot}
(
Hachisu et al. 2012
; also
Meng & Podsiadlowski 2013
).

The measurement of the relativistic Shapiro delay or the rate of
advance of periastron to an eccentric system can obtain the NS
mass (
Demorest et al. 2010
;
Özel & Freire 2016
;

Cromartie et al. 2020
). Some properties of the eccentric MSP systems may be well explained by the RD-AIC scenario (
Freire & Tauris 2014
;
Wang & Gong 2023
),
while some could not, e.g. some pulsar masses are significantly more massive than the prediction from the rigid rotation RD-AIC hypothesis of
Freire & Tauris 2014
(
Barr et al. 2017
;
Serylak et al. 2022
). If the eccentric MSP systems are from the differential rotating RD-AIC scenario, the measurement of the eccentricity may deduce the pre-AIC WD mass of the NS by

4.2
Uncertainties and potential applications for eccentric MSP + sdB systems

The predicted number of eccentric MSP + sdB systems in the Galaxy
heavily depends on the lifetime of the MSPs with strong magnetic
and
10^{\rm 8}
yr may be a conservative upper limit for the
lifetime. For example, the characteristic age of a magnetar may be
as short as
10^{\rm 3}
yr (
Igoshev et al. 2021
), which means that it may be less than 1 for the number of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems from the RD-AIC scenario. In addition, the spin-down timescale before AIC also significantly affects the number of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems. In this paper, we set the pseudo-spin-down timescale
\tau_{\rm sp}=10^{\rm 7}
yr, which is estimated according to some observational constraints of type Ia supernovae. However, in principle,
\tau_{\rm sp}
could be much longer than
10^{\rm 7}
yr before an AIC process since the AIC process is completely different from type Ia supernova explosion. If
\tau_{\rm sp}
were significantly longer than
10^{\rm 8}
so that the sdB companions become CO WDs before AIC, there would not be such eccentric MSP + sdB systems predicted from the RD-AIC scenario, and then the systems would become eccentric MSP + CO WD ones. So, the predicted number of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems in this paper
should be taken as a very conservative upper limit. Even if all
the post-AIC MSPs from the RD-AIC scenario had a typical lifetime
of normal MSPs, the number of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems is
still smaller than that of normal MSPs by about one order of
magnitude (
Bhattacharyya & Roy 2021
). Moreover, the discovery
of the eccentric MSP + sdB systems in the future may be helpful to
resolve whether or not the observational fact that the majority of
the MSPs have very low magnetic fields is a selection effect
because of their longer lifetimes (
Freire 2013
).

The sdB stars in such systems have a mass ranging from 0.38

M_{\odot}
to 0.65
M_{\odot}
and then they generally do not
experience a red-giant (RG) phase (
Justham et al. 2011
).
Considering that the orbital period is relatively long, the
eccentric MSP + sdB systems will not experience a mass-transfer
phase and then the eccentricity could not change until the systems
become eccentric NS + CO WD systems. The population of the
eccentric NS + CO WD systems from the eccentric MSP + sdB ones is thus relatively older than the eccentric MSP + sdB systems. So, a statistical study on the populations of the eccentric MSP + sdB and eccentric NS + CO WD
systems from future surveys might be helpful to judge whether the
RD-AIC scenario works or not. Unfortunately, until now, no system
fulfilling our prediction is discovered in the Galactic field.

The MSPs in global clusters (GCs) are thought to be from the AIC
channel (
van den Heuvel 2010
). However, due to the old ages
of GCs, no eccentric MSP + sdB system is discovered in the GCs.
Such systems could evolve to normal pulsar + CO WD stage with an
eccentric orbits. J1750-3703A and J1824-2452D might belong to this
population since these eccentric systems have a slowly rotating
pulsar and their companion masses and orbital periods are
consistent with our predictions

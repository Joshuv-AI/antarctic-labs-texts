Abstract
. We present combinatorial/geometric obstructions induced by the factorization over the integers of the Chern polynomial of the bundle of logarithmic vector fields associated to a complex projective plane curve. Our results generalize at the same time similar results on projective lines arrangements whose characteristic polynomial factors over the integers and results on free curves. We give a splitting criterion for a rank 2 vector bundle, in terms of restrictions to smooth conics.

Introduction

Our objects of study in this note will be projective plane curves, in particular arrangements of
lines in the complex projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}=\mathbb{P}^{2}\mathbb{C}
. All curves will be assumed to be reduced.
We will give an overview of the general context and the main results, while precise definitions of the notions involved are found in section §
2
.

Abe shows in
[
1
]
that the characteristic polynomial of an arrangement of projective lines
induces restrictions on the number of intersection points of the lines in the arrangement, and on the number of intersection points of the arrangement to an arbitrary line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. In
[
5

, Faenzi and the second author give results of the same flavour, but in terms of the Chern polynomial
c_{\mathcal{T}_{{\pazocal{A}}}}(t)
of the bundle of logarithmic vector fields
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\pazocal{A}}}
associated to the arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}
(see section
2
for a definition).
It is shown in
[
9

that, for locally free arrangements, in particular for projective lines arrangements, the characteristic polynomial of the arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}
and the Chern polynomial
c_{\mathcal{T}_{{\pazocal{A}}}}(t)
determine one another (the relation is to be understood via the Poincaré polynomial of the complement of the arrangement, see
[
11
]
).
For this reason, we will recall here condensed versions of the results from
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
phrased in terms of the Chern polynomial, in Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be an arrangement of projective lines such that, for some
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},a\leq b
, we have

c_{\mathcal{T}_{{\pazocal{A}}}}(t)=(at-1)(bt-1).

(i) For
L\in{\pazocal{A}}
, denote
{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}:={\pazocal{A}}\setminus\{L\}
. Then:

(1)

For any
L\in{\pazocal{A}}
,
|{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}\cap L|\leq a+1
or
|{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}\cap L|\geq b+1
.

(2)

If there is a line
L\in{\pazocal{A}}
such that
|{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}\cap L|\in\{a+1,b+1\}
, then
{\pazocal{A}}
is free.

(3)

If there is a line
L\in{\pazocal{A}}
such that
|{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}\cap L|>b+1
, then
{\pazocal{A}}
is not free.

(ii) For any line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2},\;L\notin{\pazocal{A}}
, either
|{\pazocal{A}}\cap L|\leq a+1
or
|{\pazocal{A}}\cap L|\geq b+1
.

When the arrangement
{\pazocal{A}}
is free with exponents
(a,b)
we have a factorization of the Chern polynomial
c_{\mathcal{T}_{{\pazocal{A}}}}(t)=(at-1)(bt-1)
,
hence the above result applies in particular to free arrangements. Moreover, for free arrangements, a stronger statement holds:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be a free projective lines arrangement with exponents
(a,b),\;a\leq b
.
(i) Let
L\in{\pazocal{A}}
and
{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}:={\pazocal{A}}\setminus\{L\}
. Then
|{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}\cap L|\leq a+1
or
|{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}\cap L|=b+1
.

(ii) For any
L\notin{\pazocal{A}}
, either
|{\pazocal{A}}\cap L|=a+1
or
|{\pazocal{A}}\cap L|\geq b+1
.

The above result is generalized to arbitrary reduced curves in the complex projective plane in
[
7
, Theorem 2.6]
. This generalization, recalled in Theorem
1.3
below, takes into account the complexity of the singularities of a curve, via the invariant
\epsilon(\cdot,\cdot)
introduced in
[
3

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a curve, we will denote by
\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
the set of irreducible components of
{\mathcal{C}}
. For the definition of the invariant

see section §

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a reduced free curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with exponents
(a,b),a\leq b
.

(i) If
L\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
is a line and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigcup_{C\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})\setminus\{L\}}C
, then either

|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|\leq a+1-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
or
|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|=b+1-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
.

(ii) If
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a line such that
L\notin\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
, then either
|{\mathcal{C}}\cap L|=a+1-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}\cup L,{\mathcal{C}})
or
|{\mathcal{C}}\cap L|\geq b+1-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}\cup L,{\mathcal{C}})
.

1.3
under a weaker hypothesis than freeness, a hypothesis on the factorization of the Chern polynomial.This result generalizes to curves Theorem

There is a growing interest in the study of a natural generalization of projective lines arrangements inside the class of projective plane curves, the so called arrangements of conics and lines (or CL arrangements), see for instance
[
14
,
2

12

. They are defined as curves whose irreducible components are smooth irreducible curves of genus
0

holds when we consider a smooth conic
C_{0}
instead of a line
L
. More precisely, if one can give obstructions on the number of singular points of a curve
{\mathcal{C}}
, situated on a smooth conic
C_{0}\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
and on the number of intersection points of
{\mathcal{C}}
to an arbitrary smooth conic
C_{0}
, under certain conditions on the curve
{\mathcal{C}}
.
In
[
7
]
one gives a positive answer to this question, when
{\mathcal{C}}
is a free curve:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a reduced free curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with exponents
(a,b),\;a\leq b
.

(i) If
C_{0}\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
is an arbitrary smooth conic, let

{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigcup_{C\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})\setminus\{C_{0}\}}C

and
k=|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap C_{0}|+\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
.

(1)

If
k=2m
, then the only possible values for
m
are
m=b
or
m\leq a
.

(2)

k=2m+1
, then
m=a-1=b-1
or
m\leq a-1
.

(ii) If
C_{0}\notin\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
is an arbitrary smooth conic, let
k=|{\mathcal{C}}\cap C_{0}|+\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0},{\mathcal{C}})
.

(1)

If
k=2m
, then either
m=a
or
m\geq b
.

(2)

If
k=2m+1
, then either
m=a=b
or
m\geq b
.

The proofs of Theorems
A
,
B1
and
B2
rely on the existence of short exact sequences of vector bundles arising from addition-deletion operations of smooth rational curves (i.e. lines and smooth conics), applied to an arbitrary curve.

We propose a new approach to the addition-deletion of a smooth conic to a curve, in subsection §
4.5
. The advantage of this approach is that the exact sequence we use has a parameter that depends solely on Tjurina numbers, as opposed to
[
7
]
, where the parameter involved in the conditions that describe the result of addition-deletion is defined in terms of both Tjurina and Milnor numbers, see
[
7
, Theorems 3.14 and 3.16]
.
Moreover, this yields a formula that involves the Milnor numbers of the singularities of a curve
{\mathcal{C}}
and the Milnor numbers of the singularities of its addition
{\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0}
that are situated on the smooth conic
C_{0}\notin\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
. This formula is stated in Proposition
4.4
. To the best of our knowledge, this formula was not previously known.

Our last main results, proved in section §
4
, are two general results on rank
2
vector bundles that are key in the proofs of Theorems
B1
and
B2
. The first one describes the splitting type of the restriction to a smooth conic of an arbitrary vector bundle, under some hypothesis on its Chern classes.

Theorem C
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a rank
2
vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
c_{1}(\mathcal{E})=c\leq 0
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{E})=0
. Then, for any smooth conic
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
, there exists a positive integer
r
, more precisely
r=0
and in this case
\mathcal{E}={\pazocal O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\oplus{\pazocal O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(c)
or

, depending on

, such that the restriction of
\mathcal{E}
to
C
splits as

\mathcal{E}|_{C}=\mathcal{O}_{C}(\frac{r}{2})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}(c-\frac{r}{2}).

Finally we give a freeness criterion for rank
2
vector bundles, in terms of restrictions of the vector bundle to smooth conics, in line with Yoshinaga’s freeness criterion relative to the restriction of a rank
2
vector bundle to a line (recalled in Theorem

).

Theorem D
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a rank
2
vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
c_{\mathcal{E}}(t)=(at-1)(bt-1)
.
Then there exists a smooth conic
C_{0}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
{\mathcal{E}}|_{C_{0}}={\pazocal O}_{C_{0}}(-a)\oplus{\pazocal O}_{C_{0}}(-b)
if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}
splits as
{\mathcal{E}}={\pazocal O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-a)\oplus{\pazocal O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-b).

2.
Preliminaries

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{2}\mathbb{C}
defined as the zero set of the homogeneous polynomial
f_{{\mathcal{C}}}\in S:=\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
. Denote by
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
the logarithmic derivations module defined by

D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})\coloneqq\{\theta\in Der(S)\;|\;\theta(f_{{\mathcal{C}}})=0\}.

and by
\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
the sheafification of the derivations module
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
.

The curve
{\mathcal{C}}
is called free if
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
is free as an
S
-module. This is equivalent to saying that the associated vector bundle,
\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
, is free, i.e., it splits as a direct sum of line bundles.

Let us recall a well known freeness criterion for rank
2
vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
17
, Theorem 1.45]
)

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a rank
2
vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
a line. Let
{\mathcal{E}}|_{L}={\pazocal O}_{L}(-e_{1})\oplus{\pazocal O}_{L}(-e_{2})
. Then
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})\geq e_{1}e_{2}
, furthermore

c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})-e_{1}e_{2}=\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathrm{Coker}(\Gamma_{*}({\mathcal{E}})\overset{\pi_{L}}{\longrightarrow}(\Gamma_{*}({\mathcal{E}}|_{L}))

where
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})
is the second Chern number of
{\mathcal{E}}
and
\pi_{L}
is the morphism of graded modules induced by the restriction to
L
of the vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{E}}
is splitting if and only if
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=e_{1}e_{2}
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is an arbitrary reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
p\in\mathrm{Sing}({\mathcal{C}})
, denote by
\mu_{p}({\mathcal{C}}),\;\tau_{p}({\mathcal{C}})
, the Milnor number, respectively the Tjurina number, of the singularity
p
. Let

\epsilon_{p}({\mathcal{C}})\coloneqq\mu_{p}({\mathcal{C}})-\tau_{p}({\mathcal{C}})

One can see
\epsilon_{p}({\mathcal{C}})
as a measure of the defect from quasihomogeneity of
p\in\mathrm{Sing}({\mathcal{C}})
, since
p
is quasihomogeneous if and only if the Milnor and Tjurina numbers are equal.

If
{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\;{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
are two reduced curves with no common irreducible component and
p\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
, denote

\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{2},{\mathcal{C}}_{1})_{p}\coloneqq\epsilon_{p}({\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{2})-\epsilon_{p}({\mathcal{C}}_{1})

and

\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{2},{\mathcal{C}}_{1})\coloneqq\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{2},{\mathcal{C}}_{1})_{p}.

Please notice that our notation for the invariant
\epsilon(\cdot,\cdot)
, which was introduced in
[
3
]
, is slightly different from the one in
[
3
]
and
[
7
]
:
\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{2},{\mathcal{C}}_{1})
was denoted as
\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{C}}_{2})
in the previously mentioned articles.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}:f_{2}=0
. In what follows, assume moreover that
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
is smooth of genus

. Then, by

(see also

(1)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}}(1-\deg(f_{2}))\overset{f_{2}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}(1)\rightarrow i_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}(D))\rightarrow 0.

3.
On the number of singularities of
{\mathcal{C}}
situated on a line

In this section, we assume
{\mathcal{C}}
is a reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that there exists a line
L\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
, and we denote
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\coloneqq\cup_{C\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})\setminus\{L\}}C
.

This next exact sequence is just a particular case of the exact sequence (
1
), where we take
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}={\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},\;{\mathcal{C}}_{2}=L
a line, and we use the fact that a divisor on a line is determined by its degree.

(2)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}(-1)\overset{\alpha_{L}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}))\rightarrow 0.

By dualization of the exact sequence (

(3)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{\vee}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{\vee}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}(1)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|+\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}))\rightarrow 0.

Since

(4)

Let

be the first, respectively the second Chern number of

and let

c_{\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}}(t)=1+c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}})t+c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}})t^{2}

Lemma 3.1

(

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a rank

c_{1}(\mathcal{E}(k))=2k+c_{1}(\mathcal{E})\;\;\mathrm{and}\;\;c_{2}(\mathcal{E}(k))=k^{2}+kc_{1}(\mathcal{E})+c_{2}(\mathcal{E}).

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
A

From the hypothesis we have that
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}})=-a-b
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}})=ab
.
By Remark
3.2
,
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(a))=a-b
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(a))=0
.
So we can apply Lemma
3.1
for the vector bundle

\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(a)
. It follows that there exists an integer
s=s(L)\geq 0
such that

\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}|_{L}=\mathcal{O}_{L}(-b-s)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{L}(-a+s).

From (
2
), in particular, we have a surjective map

\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})).

Tensor this map by
\mathcal{O}_{L}
to obtain again a surjection with domain
\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}=\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}|_{L}:

\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}|_{L}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})),

i.e. a surjection

(5)

\mathcal{O}_{L}(-b-s)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{L}(-a+s)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})).

The fact that the map from (
5
) is a surjection already implies that
1-|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})\notin]-b,-a[
, i.e. claim (1)(a) holds.

If either
|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|+\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=a+1
or
|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|+\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=b+1
, then this implies

, so
\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}|_{L}=\mathcal{O}_{L}(-b)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{L}(-a)
, i.e. the splitting type of
\mathcal{T}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
onto

is

0\rightarrow{\pazocal O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-2)\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}(-2)\rightarrow 0

where

is the same than before.
This gives without any difficulty

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\pazocal O}_{C_{1}}(-2)\rightarrow 0.

With the notations of Theorem
B1
we have
k=4
, so

, and

; this gives an example where
m<a
and

4.5.
The addition-deletion of a smooth conic to a curve, revisited

In
[
7
]
the result of addition-deletion of a smooth conic to a free curve is described using the exact sequence (
8
). We suggest here an alternative approach, via the exact sequence (
15
) below.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
C_{0}
a smooth conic which is not a component of
{\mathcal{C}}
.
We get the exact sequence:

(15)

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\rightarrow{\pazocal O}_{C_{0}}(-\frac{k_{0}}{2})\rightarrow 0.

From this exact sequence we can deduce a relation between invariants of the curves
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0}
and the integer
k_{0}
. Indeed, the Chern polynomial
c_{{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}}(t)
being the product (modulo
t^{3}
) of the Chern polynomials
c_{{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0}}}(t)
and
c_{{\pazocal O}_{C_{0}}(-\frac{k_{0}}{2})}(t)

we obtain the following relation:

Lemma 4.3
.

k_{0}=-2+\tau({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0},{\mathcal{C}})-2\times\mathrm{deg}({\mathcal{C}}),

where
\tau({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0},{\mathcal{C}}):=\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{C}}\cap C_{0}}[\tau_{p}({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0})-\tau_{p}({\mathcal{C}})]

Proof.

It is a direct computation. Indeed, the Chern polynomial
of
{\pazocal O}_{C_{0}}(-\frac{k_{0}}{2})
is

1+2t+(k_{0}+4)t^{2}

and if
D
is a reduced divisor the Chern polynomial of
{\mathcal{T}}_{D}
is

1+(1-\deg(D))\,t+((\mathrm{deg}(D)-1)^{2}-\tau(D))\,t^{2}

where
\tau(D)
is the total Tjurina number of
D
.
∎

Dualizing the exact sequence (
15
) we find again (after a shift)

(16)

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(-2)\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0}}\rightarrow{\pazocal O}_{C_{0}}(\frac{-2\deg({\mathcal{C}})+2+k_{0}}{2})\rightarrow 0.

We had seen before, see (
9
), that

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(-2)\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0}}\rightarrow{\pazocal O}_{C_{0}}(-\frac{k}{2})\rightarrow 0

where
k:=|{\mathcal{C}}\cap C_{0}|+\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0},{\mathcal{C}})
.
This gives the identity

(17)

k+k_{0}=2(\deg({\mathcal{C}})-1),

i.e.

k_{0}+2=2\deg({\mathcal{C}})-|{\mathcal{C}}\cap C_{0}|-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0},{\mathcal{C}}).

According to the previous lemma this gives also

\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0},{\mathcal{C}})=-\tau({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0},{\mathcal{C}})+4\deg({\mathcal{C}})-|{\mathcal{C}}\cap C_{0}|.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a curve and
C_{0}
a smooth conic such that
C_{0}\notin\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
. Then

(18)

\sum_{P\in{\mathcal{C}}\cap C_{0}}[\mu_{P}({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0})-\mu_{P}({\mathcal{C}})+1]=4\deg({\mathcal{C}}).

Proof.

Just replace in (
17
) the parameters
k,k_{0}
by their formulas recalled above.
Indeed

k+k_{0}=|\mathcal{C}\cap C_{0}|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C}\cup C_{0},{\mathcal{C}})-2+\tau(\mathcal{C}\cup C_{0},\mathcal{C})-2\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{C})

=|\mathcal{C}\cap C_{0}|+\sum_{P\in\mathcal{C}\cap C_{0}}[\epsilon_{p}(\mathcal{C}\cup C_{0})-\epsilon_{p}(\mathcal{C})]-2+\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{C}}\cap C_{0}}[\tau_{p}({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0})-\tau_{p}({\mathcal{C}})]-2\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{C})

=|\mathcal{C}\cap C_{0}|+\sum_{P\in\mathcal{C}\cap C_{0}}[\mu_{P}({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0})-\mu_{P}({\mathcal{C}})]-2-2\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{C}).

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a curve,
C_{0}
a smooth conic such that
C_{0}\notin\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
and let
\delta
be the delta-invariant. Then

\sum_{P\in{\mathcal{C}}\cap C_{0}}[\delta_{P}({\mathcal{C}}\cup C_{0})-\delta_{P}({\mathcal{C}})]=2\deg({\mathcal{C}}).

Proof.

Recall the Milnor formula
\mu=2\delta-r+1
, where
r
is the number of branches at a plane curve singularity
P
and
\delta,\mu
are the delta invariant, respectively the Milnor number, at
P
. Then the conclusion follows from (
18
) by a direct computation.
∎

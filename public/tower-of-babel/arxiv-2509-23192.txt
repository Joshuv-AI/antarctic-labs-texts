A Besov-based integration-by-parts method for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations
Xinyu Cheng, Zhaonan Luo, Sheng Wang

Abstract
. This note introduces a novel numerical analysis framework for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations based on Besov spaces. The key contribution of this note is to establish the stability and convergence of a semi-implicit time-stepping scheme by deriving precise error estimates in the B ∞ , 1 0 B^{0}_{\infty,1} and B ∞ , 2 0 B^{0}_{\infty,2} spaces. Another contribution of our analysis is the detailed treatment of the B ∞ , 2 0 B^{0}_{\infty,2} case, where a crucial integration-by-parts technique is employed to adeptly handle the nonlinear advection term. This technique allows for a refined estimate that effectively transfers derivatives onto the test functions, mitigating the inherent analytical challenges posed by the low regularity of these spaces. Our results provide sharper, more localized error bounds than in classical Sobolev spaces, directly linking the scheme’s convergence to the critical regularity of the continuous solution. This work underscores the advantage of Besov spaces for the numerical analysis of nonlinear fluid PDEs.



1.
Introduction

The numerical analysis of the incompressible fluid equations remains a topic of profound and enduring interest, standing at the crossroads of theoretical mathematics (partial differential equations and analysis) and computational fluid dynamics (engineering). While the (global) well-posedness theory for these equations is famously challenging, the development of robust and efficient numerical schemes is equally critical for predictive simulations across science and engineering. A vast body of literature is devoted to constructing discretization methods, with a predominant focus on error estimates formulated within the classical Sobolev spaces, such as
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
H^{s}(\Omega)
. These frameworks, though powerful, can sometimes obscure finer-grained information about the solution, particularly concerning its pointwise behavior or spaces closely related to the well-posedness theory of the equations themselves.

In this paper, we introduce a novel numerical analysis framework grounded in the theory of Besov spaces. Specifically speaking, we will perform a rigorous error analysis for a semi-implicit time-stepping scheme applied to the Navier-Stokes equations, deriving error bounds in the
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
and
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
norms. Here the incompressible Navier-Stokes system (NS) we consider are described as below:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,\qquad\nabla\cdot u=0;\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0}.\end{cases}

The solution
u:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the velocity field and
p:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is the pressure. Moreover, the divergence-free condition
\nabla\cdot u=0
is to guarantee the incompressiblity. Since we are interested in the analysis framework, for the sake of implicit we shall only consider the periodic boundary condition:
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{2}
. And the semi-implicit scheme we consider is the following:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}&\frac{u^{n+1}-u^{n}}{\tau}+(u^{n}\cdot\nabla)u^{n+1}+\nabla p^{n}=\nu\Delta u^{n+1},\\
&\mathop{\rm div}u^{n+1}=0,\end{cases}

where
\tau
is the time step. Moreover in (
1.2
) the viscosity term
\nu\Delta u^{n+1}
is unknown and the nonlinear term
u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}
is indeed linear with respect to
u^{n+1}
at
n+1
-th time step. In addition we can further derive that
\mathop{\rm div}u^{n}=0
for all
n
by induction.

This choice of functional setting is motivated by several compelling factors. Firstly, Besov spaces like
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
are known to be critical for the existence and uniqueness theory of Navier-Stokes and Euler equations; indeed the global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem of the Euler equations in the borderline Besov space
B^{\frac{3}{p}+1}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
p\in[1,\infty]
is known to be open, cf. Bourgain-Li
[
6
,
7
]
. These spaces provide a natural and less restrictive environment for analyzing solutions compared to Sobolev spaces, particularly for problems involving rough initial data or intricate vortex structures. Secondly, an error estimate in
B^{0}_{\infty,\cdot}
offers a stronger, more localized description over the pointwise error of the numerical solution than an
L^{2}
-estimate. It is worth mentioning here in a recent work
[
12
]
, a numerical method of low regularity assumption was developed to study the surface quasi-geostrophic equations in a
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
set-up, which motivates us to develop a more general
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
framework for impressible fluid equations such as Navier-Stokes and Euler equations in this presenting note. Moreover, in another recent work
[
11
]
we observe that a simple integration-by-parts can help to lower the regularity assumption, which motivates us to study the
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
numerical space.

Our work demonstrates that the proposed semi-implicit scheme is stable and convergent within this refined functional framework. The analysis reveals how the regularity of the exact solution, measured in these Besov norms, directly dictates the convergence rates of the numerical method. By bridging the gap between the analytical functional setting of the continuous equations and the discrete numerical analysis, this approach not only provides stronger theoretical guarantees but also offers deeper insight into the structure of the error. We anticipate that this Besov-based framework will open new avenues for the analysis and development of numerical methods for a broader class of nonlinear fluid PDEs where such spaces are intrinsic to the well-posedness theory.

2.
Littlewood-Paley theory and Besov spaces

In this section we give a biref introduction to the Littlewood-Paley theory and Besov spaces that will be often times used. We first define the Littlewood-Paley dyadic decomposition on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Assume
\chi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,1]
is smooth, monotone, and satisfying

\chi(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad 0\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2};\\
\frac{1}{2},\quad x=\frac{3}{4};\\
0,\quad x\geq 1.\end{cases}

We then define
\varphi(x)=\chi(x)-\chi(2x)
, then

\chi(|x|)+\sum_{j\geq 1}\varphi\left(\frac{|x|}{2^{j}}\right)\equiv 1,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Definition 2.1
.

Assume
f
is a distribution on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with

f(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{f}(k)e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}.

We define the Littlewood-Paley operators as follows:

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{0}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{f}(k)\chi\left(|k|\right)e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}=\hat{f}(0);\\
\Delta_{j}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{f}(k)\varphi\left(\frac{|k|}{2^{j}}\right)e^{2\pi ik\cdot x},\ j\geq 1;\\
S_{j}f=\Delta_{0}f+\sum_{1\leq l\leq j}\Delta_{l}f,\ j\geq 1.\end{cases}

Hence the dyadic decomposition of
f
is given by

f=\sum_{j\geq 0}\Delta_{j}f.

In particular, throughout this paper if we further assume
f
is of zero mean, then the Littlewood-Paley decomposition of
f
can be decomposed as below:

(2.2)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\geq 1}\Delta_{j}f.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R},\ 1\leq p,r\leq\infty.
The inhomogeneous Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}
is defined by

(2.3)

B^{s}_{p,r}=\{u\in S^{\prime}:\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}=\Big\|(2^{js}\|\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{p}})_{j}\Big\|_{l^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty\}.

Moreover, the homogeneous Besov norm and inhomogeneous Besov norm coincide when the function has zero mean.

The Bernstein’s inequality is given below.

Lemma 2.1

(Bernstein’s inequality)
.

(i):

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

C\|\Lambda^{s}\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq 2^{js}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C^{\prime}\|\Lambda^{s}\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}

with some constants
C
and
C^{\prime}
depending only on
p
and
s
.

(ii):

Moreover, for
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, we have

\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim 2^{jd(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}},

where the constant depends only on
p
and
q
.

Let
u
and
v
be tempered distributions in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, then the non-homogeneous
\rm paraproduct
of
v
by
u
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle T_{u}v=\sum_{j}S_{j-1}u\Delta_{j}v,

and the non-homogeneous
\rm remainder
of
u
and
v
is defined by

\displaystyle R(u,v)=\sum_{|k-j|\leq 1}\Delta_{k}u\Delta_{j}v\triangleq\sum_{k\geq 0}\Delta_{k}u\tilde{\Delta}_{k}v.

At least formally, we obtain the so-called Bony’s decomposition:

\displaystyle uv=T_{u}v+T_{v}u+R(u,v).

We have the following product rules.

Lemma 2.5

(Product rules)
.

For nonlinear terms that satisfy the div-curl structure, i.e.
\mathop{\rm div}u=0
, the following
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
estimate holds

\|u\cdot\nabla v\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\|u\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|v\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}},

for some absolute constant
C>0
.

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

Using Bony’s decomposition, there holds

u\cdot\nabla v=T_{u}\nabla v+T_{\nabla v}u+R(u,\nabla v).

By Lemma
2.2
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|T_{u}\nabla v+T_{\nabla v}u\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|\nabla v\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}.

By virtue of Lemma
2.3
and the fact that
u\cdot\nabla v=\mathrm{div\,}(v\otimes u)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|R(u,\nabla v)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|R(u,v)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|v\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

We thus finish the proof of Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

We would like to highlight here that in general the
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
space is not a multiplicative algebra, namely,
\|f\cdot g\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}
cannot be bounded by

\|f\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\cdot\|g\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}
. However if they satisfy the div-curl structure as in Lemma
2.5
above, then a similar version holds:

\|f\cdot\nabla g\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|g\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

Furthermore, if we in addition assume both
f
and
g
are of zero mean in
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, then such subspace is an algebra:

\|f\cdot\nabla g\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|\nabla g\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}.

One should also note that
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
is not a multiplicative algebra either.

, then

\displaystyle\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\rm div(u\cdot\nabla v)\|_{B^{\sigma}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\min(\|u\|_{B^{\sigma}_{\infty,1}}\|v\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}},\|u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|v\|_{B^{\sigma}_{\infty,1}}).

Proof of Lemma
2.9
.

We refer the readers to
[

]
and
[
2
]
for the details.

3.
Unconditional stability of the scheme in Besov spaces

In this section we first show the stability of the semi-implicit scheme as in Theorem
1.1
. Recall that the semi-implicit scheme

(3.1)

\frac{u^{n+1}-u^{n}}{\tau}+\left(u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}\right)+\nabla p^{n}=\nu\Delta u^{n+1},\quad\nabla\cdot u^{n+1}=0

where
\tau
is the size of the time step and we define
M=\frac{T}{\tau}
, the total number of steps. To solve
u^{n+1}
, we apply the Leray projection
\mathbb{P}
to the equation (
3.1
) to obtain that

(3.2)

\frac{u^{n+1}-u^{n}}{\tau}+\mathbb{P}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1})=\nu\Delta u^{n+1}.

Then we apply the Littlewood-Paley projection
\Delta_{j}
to (
3.2
) (note that
\Delta_{j}
commutes with both
\mathbb{P}
and
\Delta
) and thus obtain that

(3.3)

\frac{\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta_{j}u^{n}}{\tau}+\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1})=\nu\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1},

or equivalently

(3.4)

\frac{\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta_{j}u^{n}}{\tau}+(u^{n}\cdot\nabla\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}u^{n+1})-\left([u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}]u^{n+1}\right)=\nu\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}.

Then we multiply (
3.4
) by
|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}
and integrate. Note that by the divergence free condition
\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}=\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}
, we thus have

\displaystyle\langle u^{n}\cdot\nabla\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}u^{n+1},|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\rangle=0.

Using Plancherel formula and noting that the frequency of
\Delta_{j}
is supported in a circular annulus with radius approximately
2^{j}
, we infer that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}e^{n+1},|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\langle\mathcal{F}\left(\Delta\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right),\mathcal{F}\left(|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\langle-|\xi|^{2}\mathcal{F}\left(\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right),\mathcal{F}\left(|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2^{2j}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\langle\mathcal{F}\left(\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right),\mathcal{F}\left(|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=-2^{2j}\langle\Delta_{j}e^{n+1},|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=-2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p}_{L^{p}}.

Then it follows by Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+2^{2j}\nu\tau\|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq\|\Delta_{j}u^{n}\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}+\widetilde{C}\tau\|[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}]u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1},

which implies

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}+2^{2j}\nu\tau\|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\Delta_{j}u^{n}\|_{L^{p}}+\widetilde{C}\tau\|[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}]u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}.

Part (i).
We now show the
B^{s}_{\infty,1}
stability for
s\geq 1
. Inductively, we assume
\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}\leq 8\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}+C_{s}2^{(2+s)j}\nu\tau\|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}\leq 2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}u^{n}\|_{L^{p}}+\widetilde{C_{s}}2^{sj}\tau\|[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}]u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}.

Then by letting
p\to\infty
and summing in
j
we obtain that

(3.6)

\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}\leq\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}+\left\|2^{sj}\left(\|[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}]u^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)_{j}\right\|_{l^{1}}.

By Lemma
2.4
with
s\geq 1
, we know that

(3.7)

\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}\leq\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}+C_{s}\tau\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}.

Here
C_{s}>0
only depends on
s\geq 1
.

Remark 3.1
.

The local existence theory of nonlinear PDEs usually assumes sufficiently small time interval
T
. Similar to the discussion in
[
11
]
, without loss of generality we can take the time interval
T=\frac{1}{8C_{s}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}}
. By copying the time interval repeatedly, we can extend the time interval to the whole life-span
T_{\text{max}}
. Whether
T_{\text{max}}=\infty
is open for the three dimensional incompressible NS equations.

As discussed above in Remark
3.1
, there is no loss to assume
T=\frac{1}{8C_{s}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}}
and
\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}\leq 8\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}
. We thus get that

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}+C_{s}\tau\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}+8C_{s}\frac{T}{M}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}+\frac{1}{M}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}},

which implies that

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}\leq(1+h)\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}},

where
h:=\frac{1}{M-1}\leq 1
. Iterating the inequality above we can complete the proof:

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+h)^{n+1}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+h)^{M+1}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+h)^{\frac{1}{h}+2}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq 8\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}.

Here the last inequality follows from the fact that
(1+h)^{2+\frac{1}{h}}\leq 8
for
h<1
. We then conclude (
1.4
).

Part (ii).
We now show thte
B^{s}_{\infty,2}
stability
(s>1)
.

Inductively, we assume
\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}\leq 8\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}
. We have

\displaystyle 2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}+C_{s}2^{(2+s)j}\nu\tau\|\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}\leq 2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}u^{n}\|_{L^{p}}+\widetilde{C_{s}}2^{sj}\tau\|[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}]u^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}.

Then by letting
p\to\infty
and taking
l^{2}
-norm for
j
we obtain that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}\leq\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}+C_{s}\tau\left\|2^{sj}\left(\|[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}]u^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)_{j}\right\|_{l^{2}}.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}\leq

\displaystyle\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}+C_{s}\tau(\|\nabla u^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}+\|\nabla u^{n}\|_{B^{s-1}_{\infty,2}}\|\nabla u^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}+C_{s}\tau(\|u^{n}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}+\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}+C_{s}\tau\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,2}},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
2.7
noting
s>1
. Here
C_{s}>0
only depends on
s>1
. By Remark
3.1
, we can assume
T=\frac{1}{8C_{s}\|u_{0}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}}
from the local theory and
\|u^{n}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}\leq 8\|u_{0}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}
. Similarly we get that

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{n}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}+C_{s}\tau\|u^{n}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{n}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}+8C_{s}\frac{T}{M}\|u_{0}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{n}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}+\frac{1}{M}\|u^{n+1}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}},

which implies that

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}\leq(1+h)\|u^{n}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}},

where
h:=\frac{1}{M-1}
. This leads to

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}\leq 8\|u_{0}\|_{B_{\infty,2}^{s}}.

This then proves (
1.5
). Note that here we only requires
h=\frac{1}{M-1}\leq 1
, or equivalently
\tau\leq T
, a very mild restriction.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that following embeddings hold:
B^{0}_{\infty,1}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
and
B^{0}_{\infty,1}\hookrightarrow B^{0}_{\infty,2}
; however no inclusion relation holds between
L^{\infty}
and
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
.

4.
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
error estimates

In this section we shall prove the
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
error estimates by proving Theorem
1.2
. Assume
u(t)
is the exact solution to the NS equations (
1.1
). Then by the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

\displaystyle u\left(t_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=u\left(t_{n}\right)+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\partial_{t‘}udt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=u\left(t_{n}\right)+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}-\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)+\nu\Delta u\,dt^{\prime}.

This together with (
3.4
) imply that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u^{n}-\tau\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1})+\tau\nu\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n}\right)+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u)-\nu\Delta\Delta_{j}u\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u^{n}-\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n}\right)+\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta\Delta_{j}u\,dt^{\prime}+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u-u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1})\,dt^{\prime}.

Then, we consider the
L^{p}
-estimates for
\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}:=\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right)
. We multiply (
4.1
) by
|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}
and integrate:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{j}e^{n+1},|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta_{j}e^{n},|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle+\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta\Delta_{j}u,|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u-u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

It follows by Hölder’s inequality that

(4.3)

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n}\|_{L^{p}}.

For
I_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-\Delta\Delta_{j}u,|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneqq I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}.

Note that

\displaystyle I_{2,1}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\tau\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}e^{n+1},|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle

.

It follows by (
3.5
) that

(4.4)

I_{2,1}\leq-\nu\tau 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\leq 0.

Similarly we can estimate
I_{2,2}
.

\displaystyle I_{2,2}

\displaystyle=-\nu 2^{2j}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-\Delta_{j}u,|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\nu 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\|\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{p}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\nu 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\|\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t^{\prime}}\partial_{t}\Delta_{j}u\,ds\|_{L^{p}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\nu 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t^{\prime}}\|\partial_{t}\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{p}}\,dsdt^{\prime}.

Noting that by the Leray projected PDE (
1.1
) we have

\partial_{t}\Delta_{j}u=-\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u)+\nu\Delta\Delta_{j}u.

Therefore we can derive that

I_{2,2}\leq\nu\tau^{2}2^{2j}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}.

It then follows that

(4.5)

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\nu\tau^{2}2^{2j}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}.

Notice that we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle u\cdot\nabla u-u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}

\displaystyle=u\cdot\nabla u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle+u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle+u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-u^{n}\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle+u^{n}\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}.

Then we can rewrite
I_{3}
as follows

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla u\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(\left(u\left(t_{n}\right)-u^{n}\right)\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(u^{n}\cdot\nabla\left(u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-u^{n+1}\right)\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=I_{3,1}+I_{3,2}+I_{3,3}+I_{3,4}

Using (
1.1
) and the uniform estimates (
1.4
), we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle I_{3,1}=

\displaystyle\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\left(\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}u\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\left[\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]u,|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau\left(\|u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\|\left[\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\tau^{2}2^{j}\left(\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}},

and

(4.8)

\displaystyle I_{3,2}=

\displaystyle\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\left(u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\left[u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau\left(\|u\left(t_{n}\right)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\Delta_{j}\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\|\left[u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\tau^{2}2^{j}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}.

Similarly, we infer that

(4.9)

\displaystyle I_{3,3}

\displaystyle\leq\tau\|\Delta_{j}\left(e^{n}\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}.

Lastly, by the divergence free condition it follows that

(4.10)

\displaystyle I_{3,4}=

\displaystyle\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\left(u^{n}\cdot\nabla\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-u^{n+1}\right)\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\left[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-u^{n+1}\right)\right],|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\left[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-u^{n+1}\right)\right],|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau\|\left[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right]\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}.

Therefore collecting all the estimates (
4.7
)-(
4.10
) we can conclude that

(4.11)

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq

\displaystyle C\tau^{2}2^{j}\left(\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}2^{j}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+\tau\|\Delta_{j}\left(e^{n}\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right]\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}.

Based on the estimates (
4.2
), (
4.3
), (
4.5
) and(
4.11
) above we finally conclude (by dividing both sides by
\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}
) that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}\leq

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}e^{n}\|_{L^{p}}+\nu\tau^{2}2^{2j}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}2^{j}\left(\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}2^{j}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}

\displaystyle+\tau\|\Delta_{j}\left(e^{n}\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right]\|_{L^{p}}.

Using Lemma
2.5
and the uniform estimate (
1.4
), we have

(4.12)

\|e^{n}\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

On the other hand, by the commutator estimate Lemma
2.4
and the stability estimate (
1.4
) we have

(4.13)

\sum_{j}\|\left[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right]\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u^{n}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

As a result, by (
4.12
)-(
4.13
), letting
p\to\infty
and summing
j
, we know

\displaystyle\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq

\displaystyle\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}+\nu\tau^{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\right)+\nu^{2}\tau^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{4}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\left(\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle+C\tau^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,1}}\right)

\displaystyle+C\tau\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

By introducing the time integral of
u-u(t_{n})
and
u-u(t_{n+1})
we get

\displaystyle\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq

\displaystyle\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}+\nu\tau^{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\right)+\nu^{2}\tau^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{4}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\left(\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle+C\tau^{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,1}}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle+C\tau^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,1}}\right)

\displaystyle+C\tau\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

Therefore if
u_{0}\in B^{4}_{\infty,1}
, this implies that

(4.14)

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq\frac{1+C^{*}\tau}{1-C^{*}\tau}\|u^{n}-u\left(t_{n}\right)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}+\frac{C^{*}(1+\nu^{2})\tau^{2}}{1-C^{*}\tau},

for some
C^{*}
depending on
\|u_{0}\|_{B^{4}_{\infty,1}}
. Applying the discrete Grönwall’s inequality by iterating the inequality (
4.14
) we get the desired error estimate:

(4.15)

\sup_{n}\|u^{n}-u\left(t_{n}\right)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\tau

for some
C>0
, which then concludes (
1.6
). The higher order error estimate (
1.7
) is a direct corollary and we then omit the details.

5.
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
error estimates

Similar to the
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
error estimates, we again perform the
L^{p}
-estimates for Littlewood-Paley projected
\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}:=\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right)
. We multiply (
4.1
) by
|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}
and integrate to obtain that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{j}e^{n+1},|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta_{j}e^{n},|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle+\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta\Delta_{j}u,|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u-u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=I^{\prime}_{1}+I^{\prime}_{2}+I^{\prime}_{3}.

Here, the estimates of
I^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}
is same as the
I_{1}
and therefore the following holds:

(5.2)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{1}\leq\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n}\|_{L^{p}}.

For
I^{\prime}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-\Delta\Delta_{j}u,|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneqq I^{\prime}_{2,1}+I^{\prime}_{2,2}.

The estimates of
I^{\prime}_{2,1}
is similar to
I_{2,1}
. Indeed we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{2,1}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\tau\langle\Delta\Delta_{j}e^{n+1},|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\nu\tau 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p}_{L^{p}}.

Unlike the previous
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
error case, the
-\nu\tau 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p}_{L^{p}}
term will play an important role in reducing the regularity. To continue, for
I^{{}^{\prime}}_{2,2}
we get

(5.4)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{2,2}\coloneqq

\displaystyle-\nu 2^{2j}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-\Delta_{j}u,|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\nu 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\|\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{p}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\nu 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\|\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t^{\prime}}\partial_{t}\Delta_{j}u\,ds\|_{L^{p}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\nu 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t^{\prime}}\|\partial_{t}\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{p}}\,ds\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nu\tau 2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\nu\tau^{3}2^{2j}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu^{2}\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-2}_{L^{p}}.

It then follows by (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) that

(5.5)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\nu\tau^{3}2^{2j}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu^{2}\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-2}_{L^{p}}.

For
I^{\prime}_{3}
, we will estimate similarly as before:

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla u\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(\left(u\left(t_{n}\right)-u^{n}\right)\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(u^{n}\cdot\nabla\left(u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-u^{n+1}\right)\right),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=I^{\prime}_{3,1}+I^{\prime}_{3,2}+I^{\prime}_{3,3}+I^{\prime}_{3,4}

We first consider the term
I^{\prime}_{3,1}
. Unlike the
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
case, we shall only estimate by Hölder’s inequality:

(5.6)

I^{\prime}_{3,1}\leq\tau\|\Delta_{j}\left(\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}.

Similarly we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{3,2}\leq

\displaystyle\tau^{2}2^{j}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

and

(5.8)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{3,3}

\displaystyle\leq\tau\|\Delta_{j}\left(e^{n}\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}.

Lastly, again by the divergence free condition it follows that

(5.9)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{3,4}=

\displaystyle-\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\left[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\left(u\left(t_{n+1}\right)-u^{n+1}\right)\right],|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau\|\left[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right]\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}.

Therefore by collecting the estimates (
5.2
), (
5.5
) and (
5.6
)-(
5.9
) we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{p}}\leq

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}e^{n}\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}+\frac{1}{2}\nu\tau^{3}2^{2j}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu^{2}\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\Delta_{j}\left(\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}2^{j}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\left(\|\Delta_{j}\left(u\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}+\nu\|\Delta_{j}\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\right)\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+\tau\|\Delta_{j}\left(e^{n}\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|\left[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right]\|_{L^{p}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}

It is worth pointing our there that the inequality above was derived by dividing both sides by
\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-2}
. This is the main difference in the
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
setting as in the
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
case. Note that by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Lemma
2.8
, we have the following estimates hold for any
t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1})
:

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\|\Delta_{j}\left(\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|(\|\Delta_{j}\left(\left(u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla u\right)\|_{L^{\infty}})_{j}\right\|_{l^{2}}\left\|(\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}})_{j}\right\|_{l^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(u-u(t_{n}))\cdot\nabla u\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u-u(t_{n})\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|u\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}},

and

(5.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\|\Delta_{j}\left(e^{n}\cdot\nabla u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|e^{n}\cdot\nabla u(t_{n+1})\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|u(t_{n+1})\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}.

By Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.7
we have

(5.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\|\left[u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\|\left[u\left(t_{n}\right)\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}\right]\left(u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)_{j}\right\|_{l^{2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(t_{n})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(t_{n})\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}.

Moreover, we have

(5.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\|\left[u^{n}\cdot\nabla,\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\right]\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u^{n}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}.

Now by letting
p\to\infty
, summing
j
, applying the uniform estimates(
1.5
) and stability estimates Theorem
1.1
and collecting all of the estimates (
5.10
)-(
5.13
) we have

\displaystyle\|e^{n+1}\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\leq

\displaystyle\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}+C\nu\tau^{3}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{4}_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}+\nu^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{3}_{\infty,2}}\right)\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|u-u\left(t_{n}\right)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}+\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,2}}\right)\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|u-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}.

Again by introducing the time integral of
u-u(t_{n})
and
u-u(t_{n+1})
we get

\displaystyle\|e^{n+1}\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\leq

\displaystyle\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}+C\nu\tau^{3}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{4}_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}+\nu^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{3}_{\infty,2}}\right)\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle+C\tau^{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{1}_{\infty,2}}+\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}+\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,2}}\right)\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle+C\tau^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{1}_{\infty,2}}+\nu\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\right)\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle+C\tau\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,2}}\|e^{n+1}\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality one can get

\displaystyle\|e^{n+1}\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\leq\|e^{n}\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}+C\tau(\|e^{n+1}\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}+\|e^{n}\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}})+C\tau^{3}(1+\nu+\nu\tau^{2}+\nu^{3}\tau^{2}),

which implies that

(5.15)

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\leq\frac{1+C^{*}\tau}{1-C^{*}\tau}\|u^{n}-u\left(t_{n}\right)\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}+\frac{C^{*}(1+\nu+\nu\tau^{2}+\nu^{3}\tau^{2})\tau^{3}}{1-C^{*}\tau},

where
C^{*}
depends on
\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,2}}
. Iterating the inequality (
5.15
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\|u^{n}-u\left(t_{n}\right)\|^{2}_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\leq C\tau^{2},

for some
C>0
. This then concludes (
1.8
). The higher order error estimate (
1.9
) is a direct corollary and we also leave the details to the readers.

6.
Extension to the full discretization

In this section we briefly discuss how to extend the proposed semi-implicit scheme to full discretization. More precisely speaking we give a brief instruction on how to apply finite element methods or Fourier spectral methods.

6.1.
Finite element method

Our semi-implicit method can be extended with standard finite element methods. For example, a pair of finite element subspace of mesh size
h
,
V_{h}\times Q_{h}\subset H_{0}^{1}\times L_{0}^{2}
with the following properties are able to fulfill the task. Here
H_{0}^{1}
and
L^{2}_{0}
contain
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
functions of zero mean respectively. Indeed we refer the readers to
[
38
]
and
[
36
]
for more a detailed set-up.

(P1)

There exists a linear projection operator
\Pi_{h}:H^{1}_{0}\rightarrow V_{h}
such that

(i)

\nabla\cdot\Pi_{h}v=P_{Q_{h}}\nabla\cdot v
for
v\in H^{1}_{0}
, where
P_{Q_{h}}:L^{2}_{0}\rightarrow Q_{h}
denotes the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection.

(ii)

The following approximation property holds for
v\in H^{1}_{0}\cap H^{m}
:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|_{H^{s}}\leq Ch^{m-s}\|v\|_{H^{m}},\quad 0\leq s\leq 1,\quad 1\leq m\leq 2.

(P2)

\nabla\cdot v_{h}\in Q_{h}
for
v_{h}\in V_{h}
.

The two properties above guarantee the inf-sup condition for the pair
V_{h}\times Q_{h}
, i.e.,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{C(\nabla\cdot v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}}}\quad\forall\,q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

It is known that the discrete divergence-free subspace of
V_{h}
coincides with its pointwise divergence-free subspace, i.e.,

(6.3)

\displaystyle X_{h}:=\{v_{h}\in V_{h}:(\nabla\cdot v_{h},q_{h})=0\,\,\,\forall\,q_{h}\in Q_{h}\}=\{v_{h}\in V_{h}:\nabla\cdot v_{h}=0\}.

Therefore,

V_{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}\quad\mbox{and}\quad X_{h}\subset\dot{H}^{1}_{0}\subset\dot{L}^{2}=X,

where
\dot{H}^{1}_{0}
and
\dot{L}^{2}
are divergence-free subspaces of
H^{1}_{0}
and
L^{2}
. Then it suffices to find
u_{h}^{n}\in X_{h}
,
n=1,\dots,N
, such that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \bigg(\frac{u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n}}{\tau},v_{h}\bigg)+\nu(\nabla u_{h}^{n+1},\nabla v_{h})+(u_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla u_{h}^{n+1},v_{h})&=0\quad\forall\,v_{h}\in X_{h},\,\,\,\\
u_{h}^{0}&=P_{X_{h}}u^{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
P_{X_{h}}:\dot{L}^{2}\rightarrow X_{h}
denotes the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
X_{h}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
u^{0}\in\dot{L}^{2}
and assume that the finite element space
X_{h}\times Q_{h}
has properties (P1)–(P2). Assume that the solution of the NS problem (
1.1
) has the following regularity:

(6.5)

\displaystyle u\in C([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{3}),

then the fully discrete solution given by (
6.4
) has the following error bound:

(6.6)

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{n}-u(n\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\tau+h),

where the constant
C
depends only on
u^{0}
and
T
.

The proof can be found in
[
38
]
. It is worth pointing out that the assumption
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{3})
can be seen through an integration-by-parts technique as in
[
11
]
without introducing the smoothing effect of NS equations as in
[
38
]
. Moreover, due to the embeddings
B^{4}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
B^{3}_{\infty,2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
one can still obtain the
L^{2}
-error bound (
6.6
) under the regularity assumptions

(6.7)

\displaystyle u\in C([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;B^{4}_{\infty,1})\quad\mbox{or}\quad u\in C([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;B^{3}_{\infty,2})

as in Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
.

On the other hand, it seems very challenging to obtain a Besov error bounds such as
\|u_{h}^{n}-u(n\tau)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}
or
\|u_{h}^{n}-u(n\tau)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}
under the same assumptions in (
6.7
) above. The main difficulty is lack of approximation property (P1) of the finite element projection in Besov spaces. Indeed there are many works in such area and we refer the readers to
[
1
,
5
,
19
,
20
]
for more discussion. More specifically speaking,
L^{p}
approximation of the finite element space can be obtained as long as the function is in
B^{\alpha}_{p,p}
from the physical point of view instead of frequency. However, it seems that one requires extra regularity to get
\|u_{h}^{n}-u(n\tau)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}
error due to lack of the tools. Since we focus on a general Besov framework, we will not dig further in this direction in this paper.

6.2.
Fourier spectral Picard iteration method

In this subsection we show that the semi-implicit scheme can be extended to the full discretization using a Fourier spectral Picard iteration method:

(6.8)

\begin{cases}&\frac{u^{(m+1)}-u^{n}}{\tau}+\mathbb{P}\Pi_{N}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{(m)})=\nu\Delta u^{(m+1)},\\
&\mathop{\rm div}u^{(m+1)}=0,\\
&u^{(0)}=u^{n},\quad u^{0}=\Pi_{N}u_{0},\end{cases}

where
\tau
is the time step. For
N\geq 2
, we introduce the space

X_{N}=\text{span}\left\{\cos(k\cdot x)\ ,\ \sin(k\cdot x):\ \ k=(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\ ,\ |k|_{\infty}=\max\{|k_{1}|,|k_{2}|\}\in[1,N]\right\}.

We define
\Pi_{N}
to be the Fourier truncation operator
1\leq|k|_{\infty}\leq N
. Here at each time step
n
we implement an iteration of
u^{(m)}
and eventually
u^{(m)}
converges. We therefore define
\lim_{m}u^{(m)}=u^{n+1}
up to a local tolerance. Such iteration (
6.8
) converges and we refer the readers to
[
11
]
for the details. The reason that we introduce such Picard iteration is to simplify the computation; indeed one can still compute (
1.3
) via Fourier spectral method with a convolution solver. The semi-implicit Fourier spectral (Picard iteration) scheme we consider is the following:

(6.9)

\begin{cases}&\frac{u^{n+1}-u^{n}}{\tau}+\mathbb{P}\Pi_{N}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1})=\nu\Delta u^{n+1},\\
&\mathop{\rm div}u^{n+1}=0,\\
&u^{0}=\Pi_{N}u_{0}.\end{cases}

We have the following Besov error estimate for the scheme (
6.9
).

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume that
u(t,x)
is the exact solution to (
1.1
).

(i)
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
-error:

Assume
u\in C([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;B^{4}_{\infty,1})
, then the following
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
-error estimate holds:

(6.10)

\sup_{n}\|u^{n}-u\left(n\tau\right)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq C_{1}(\tau+N^{-3}).

(ii)
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
-error:

Assume
u\in C([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;B^{3}_{\infty,2})
, then the following
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
-error estimate holds for any
\delta>0
:

(6.11)

\sup_{n}\|u^{n}-u\left(n\tau\right)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,2}}\leq C_{2}(\tau+N^{-2+\delta}).

Here the constant
C_{1}>0
above only depends on the initial condition
u_{0}
while
C_{2}>0
depends on
u_{0}
and
\delta>0
. Both constants are independent of the viscosity
\nu
.

The proof of Theorem
6.2
is very similar to
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
and therefore we only sketch the proof here. First we note that
\Pi_{N}u_{0}\in X_{N}
and therefore by induction we have
u^{n}\in X_{N}\ ,\forall n\geq 0
. In addition we can further derive that
\mathop{\rm div}u^{n}=0
for all
n
. We then discretize the continuous NS equation as follows:

\displaystyle u^{n+1}-u\left(t_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=u^{n}-\tau\Pi_{N}\mathbb{P}(u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1})+\tau\nu\Delta u^{n+1}-u\left(t_{n}\right)+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)-\nu\Delta u\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=u^{n}-u\left(t_{n}\right)+\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\Delta u^{n+1}-\Delta u\,dt^{\prime}+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\Pi_{N}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u-u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1})\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\left(\mathcal{I}-\Pi_{N}\right)\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)\,dt^{\prime}.

It is clear that applying the Little-wood Paley projection
\Delta_{j}
we can derive that

(6.12)

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u^{n}-\Delta_{j}u\left(t_{n}\right)+\nu\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\Delta\Delta_{j}u^{n+1}-\Delta\Delta_{j}u\,dt^{\prime}+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\Pi_{N}\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u-u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1})\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\left(\mathcal{I}-\Pi_{N}\right)\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u)\,dt^{\prime}.

Note that
\Pi_{N}\Delta_{j}=\Delta_{j}
or
0
, therefore

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{3}\coloneqq\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\langle\mathbb{P}\Pi_{N}\Delta_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u-u^{n}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}),|\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}|^{p-2}\Delta_{j}e^{n+1}\rangle\,dt^{\prime}

can be estimated exactly as
I_{3}
or
I_{3}^{\prime}
. Now the only extra term (compared to (
4.1
)) is
\sum_{j}\|B_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}
or
\sum_{j}\|B_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}
, where

\displaystyle B_{n}=\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\left(\mathcal{I}-\Pi_{N}\right)\mathbb{P}\Delta_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u)\,dt^{\prime}.

Recall the definition of the frequency cut-off operator
\Delta_{j}
back in (
2.1
). In short words,
\sum_{j}(\mathcal{I}-\Pi_{N})\Delta_{j}\approx\sum_{2^{j}\geq N}\Delta_{j}
in our setting. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\|B_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sum_{2^{j}\geq N}N^{-3}\|\nabla^{3}B_{n}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\sum_{2^{j}\geq N}\tau N^{-3}\|\nabla^{3}\Delta_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\infty}}.

Therefore we get by Lemma
2.6

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\|B_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\tau N^{-3}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}B^{4}_{\infty,1}}.

The following error estimate then holds:

\displaystyle\|e^{n+1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq\frac{1+C^{*}\tau}{1-C^{*}\tau}\|e^{n}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}+\frac{C^{*}(1+\nu)\tau^{2}}{1-C^{*}\tau}+\frac{C^{*}\tau N^{-3}}{1-C^{*}\tau},

which proves (
6.10
). On the other hand, we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\|B_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{2^{j}\geq N}N^{-4+2\delta}\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{2-\delta}B_{n}\|^{2}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\tau^{2}N^{-4+2\delta}\|u\cdot\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}B^{2-\delta}_{\infty,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\tau^{2}N^{-4+2\delta}\|u\|^{4}_{L^{\infty}_{t}B^{3}_{\infty,2}}.

The error estimate (
6.11
) then follows. We see that the non-sharpness of
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
-error
N^{-2+\delta}
results from the fact that
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
is not a multiplicative algebra, see Lemma
2.8
or Lemma
2.7
and the discussion therein.

8.
Concluding remark

In this paper, we have established a new Besov numerical framework for the Navier-Stokes and Euler equations, proving rigorous error estimates for a semi-implicit scheme in the
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
and
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
spaces. Our analysis demonstrates that this approach yields sharper, more localized convergence results than those attainable in classical Sobolev spaces, effectively bridging a gap between the analytical functional setting of the continuous equations and their discrete counterparts. A pivotal element of this work is the refined treatment of the
B^{0}_{\infty,2}
case, where a targeted integration-by-parts technique was essential for managing the nonlinear advection term. This technique allowed for a precise transfer of derivatives, enabling a stable error estimate that directly reflects the critical regularity of the true solution. This methodological innovation not only resolves a key technical hurdle in low-regularity spaces but also provides a blueprint for analyzing other nonlinear fluid PDEs where similar terms pose analytical challenges.

This Besov-based framework opens several promising directions for future research. Immediate extensions include adapting the analysis to more complex models, such as the Navier-Stokes-Q-tensor systems, where interfacial dynamics and coupling phenomena could benefit from this finer-grained error control. Ultimately, this work underscores the significant potential of leveraging analytical function spaces in numerical analysis to develop more robust and theoretically sound computational methods.

Complete Skin Clearance is Associated with HADS-Defined Absence of Anxiety and Depressive Symptoms After IL-17A Inhibitor Treatment in Moderate-to-Severe Psoriasis: Evidence from the SPEECH Cohort
Authors: Zhenquan Xuan, Yingbao Zhen, Didaer Muheyati, Dawei Huang, Lian Cui, Zeyu Chen, Min Dai, Yuxiong Jiang, Yangfeng Ding, Qian Yu, Yuling Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPsoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated disease associated with anxiety, depressive symptoms, and impaired quality of life, potentially involving interacting psychosocial and inflammatory pathways. During biologic treatment, psychological symptoms may improve alongside skin disease, but whether complete skin clearance (CSC) is associated with Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS)-defined absence of anxiety and depressive symptoms remains unclear.ObjectiveTo examine whether CSC, defined as PASI100, was associated with HADS-defined absence of anxiety and depressive symptoms after 12-week IL-17A inhibitor treatment in moderate-to-severe psoriasis.MethodsPatients receiving IL-17A inhibitors were identified from the Shanghai Psoriasis Effectiveness Evaluation CoHort (SPEECH). Eligible patients had complete baseline data, week-12 Psoriasis area and severity index (PASI) and HADS assessments. PASI responses represented percentage improvement from baseline and were categorized as PASI<75, PASI75–<100, or PASI100. The primary outcomes were week-12 HADS-A = 0 and HADS-D = 0, and the secondary outcomes were week-12 HADS-A and HADS-D as continuous outcomes. Logistic and linear regression, subgroup, and sensitivity analyses were performed.ResultsAmong 233 patients, 32 (13.7%) achieved PASI<75, 99 (42.5%) PASI75–<100, and 102 (43.8%) PASI100. Baseline HADS-A and HADS-D were comparable across groups. Compared with PASI100, PASI75–<100 was associated with lower odds of HADS-A = 0 (adjusted OR [aOR], 0.40; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.20–0.78) and HADS-D = 0 (aOR, 0.48; 95% CI, 0.25–0.95), and higher week-12 HADS-A (adjusted β, 1.10; 95% CI, 0.34–1.86) and HADS-D (adjusted β, 0.95; 95% CI, 0.18–1.72). For PASI<75 versus PASI100, the corresponding aORs for HADS-A = 0 and HADS-D = 0 were 0.22 (95% CI, 0.08–0.62) and 0.27 (95% CI, 0.09–0.81), respectively. Subgroup and sensitivity analyses yielded consistent findings.ConclusionCSC was associated with a higher likelihood of HADS-defined absence of anxiety and depressive symptoms in the treatment of moderate-to-severe psoriasis.

Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated systemic inflammatory disease associated with substantial physical, psychological, and quality-of-life burden.1,2 The association between psoriasis and anxiety and depressive symptoms is likely multifactorial. Visible lesions, symptom-related distress, treatment burden, and social stigma may interact with shared systemic inflammatory and neuroimmune pathways.3,4 Systematic reviews and meta-analyses have documented increased prevalence and odds of anxiety and depressive symptoms among patients with psoriasis.5,6 Population-based data have also shown increased risks of anxiety and depression,7 while real-world analyses indicate substantial economic burden when anxiety or depression coexists with moderate-to-severe psoriasis.8 Anxiety and depressive symptoms have also been associated with more severe quality-of-life impairment, even after adjustments for psoriasis severity.9 They are also associated with shame, worry, and difficulties in daily activities and social life.10 Together, these findings highlight anxiety and depressive symptoms as clinically relevant dimensions of psoriasis burden that merit attention alongside skin disease severity and quality of life.

Biologic therapies have enabled high levels of skin clearance in moderate-to-severe psoriasis. IL-17A inhibitors, including secukinumab and ixekizumab, provide a suitable treatment setting for this analysis because they can achieve complete skin clearance (CSC) in a substantial proportion of patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis.11,12 Accordingly, treatment goals in moderate-to-severe psoriasis have moved beyond the traditional Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI)75 benchmark toward more ambitious and clinically meaningful targets, including CSC.13,14

CSC, commonly expressed as PASI100, has been associated with greater Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) improvement and more favorable patient-reported outcomes than lower levels of skin clearance.15,16 Studies of biologic treatment, including IL-17A inhibitors, have also reported reductions in anxiety and depressive symptoms alongside improvements in psoriasis severity.17–20 However, HADS outcomes across PASI<75, PASI75–<100, and PASI100 remain insufficiently characterized, particularly whether PASI100 is associated with more favorable psychological outcomes than PASI75–<100.

Shanghai Psoriasis Effectiveness Evaluation CoHort (SPEECH) is a prospective, multicentre, real-world cohort established to evaluate treatment outcomes and disease burden among Chinese patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis.21,22 In this post hoc analysis, we primarily examined whether PASI100, representing CSC at week 12, was associated with HADS-defined absence of anxiety and depressive symptoms compared with PASI75–<100 among patients treated with IL-17A inhibitors. PASI<75 was included as an additional comparator, and week-12 HADS-A and HADS-D were also evaluated as continuous outcomes.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This study was a post hoc analysis of the prospective, multicentre, real-world observational SPEECH cohort.21 Patients were recruited from November 2022 to May 2025. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. This prospective cohort study has been registered at www.chictr.org.cn (registration number ChiCTR2000036186).

Participants

Eligible patients were adults with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis who were enrolled in SPEECH and received an IL-17A inhibitor. The IL-17A inhibitors represented in the analytic cohort were secukinumab and ixekizumab. The dosing and regimen patterns of secukinumab and ixekizumab are summarized in Figure S1. Complete-case analysis was used for the corresponding analysis. Patients with missing baseline data, unavailable follow-up information, or medication noncompliance were not included in the main analysis. The main analysis included 233 patients. Patients were categorized according to the percentage improvement in PASI from baseline to week 12: PASI<75, defined as <75% improvement from baseline PASI; PASI75–<100, defined as ≥75% but <100% improvement from baseline PASI; and PASI100, defined as 100% improvement from baseline PASI, corresponding to CSC.

Data Collection and Assessments

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking status, alcohol use, early-onset psoriasis, comorbidities, previous psoriasis treatments, baseline PASI, baseline DLQI, and baseline HADS values. The HADS anxiety and depression subscales were recorded as HADS-A and HADS-D, respectively. HADS-A and HADS-D each range from 0 to 21, with higher values indicating greater symptom burden.23 HADS-A and HADS-D were assessed at baseline and at the week-12 assessment.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes were week-12 HADS-defined absence of anxiety and depressive symptoms, defined separately as HADS-A = 0 and HADS-D = 0. Conventional HADS thresholds are used to screen for clinically relevant symptom burden, whereas the present analysis examined the minimum possible symptom burden recorded by each HADS subscale. Accordingly, HADS-A = 0 and HADS-D = 0 were used as stringent, study-defined descriptive endpoints, consistent with the study’s focus on CSC. Similar zero-value outcomes have previously been reported in psoriasis cohort research.22 These endpoints were not intended to indicate complete resolution of anxiety or depression or to provide evidence of psychiatric remission. The secondary outcomes were week-12 HADS-A and HADS-D analyzed as continuous outcomes, thereby avoiding reliance solely on this dichotomization. The primary comparison was PASI75–<100 versus PASI100, with PASI100 used as the reference group. PASI<75 was included as an additional comparator to contextualize associations across PASI response levels.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs), and categorical variables as numbers and percentages. Baseline characteristics were compared across the three week-12 PASI response groups using the Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous variables and Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate. Group comparisons of HADS outcomes were performed across week-12 PASI response groups. The proportions of patients with HADS-A = 0 or HADS-D = 0 were compared using Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, followed by pairwise comparisons when appropriate, whereas HADS-A and HADS-D values were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis test followed by Dunn’s post hoc test.

Associations between week-12 PASI response group and psychological outcomes were examined using logistic and linear regression models. Univariable models included PASI response group only, whereas multivariable models adjusted for age, sex, BMI, baseline PASI, baseline DLQI, outcome-specific baseline HADS value, psoriatic arthritis, IL-17A inhibitor type, and previous acitretin treatment. Logistic regression was used for the primary binary outcomes, HADS-A = 0 and HADS-D = 0 at week 12, and linear regression was used for the secondary continuous outcomes, week-12 HADS-A and HADS-D. PASI100 was used as the reference group in all models. Results from logistic regression models are reported as odds ratios (ORs) or adjusted odds ratios (aORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Results from linear regression models are reported as β coefficients with 95% CIs. Subgroup analyses were performed for the primary outcomes across sex, BMI, baseline PASI, baseline DLQI, smoking status, alcohol use, and IL-17A inhibitor type. All models included the three PASI response categories, with PASI100 as the reference group. Interaction P values for the PASI75–<100 versus PASI100 contrast were calculated using likelihood ratio tests comparing models with and without the corresponding interaction term. Sensitivity analyses were performed after excluding patients with baseline HADS-A = 0 or HADS-D = 0 from the corresponding outcome analyses. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. R version 4.5.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) was used for all statistical analyses.

Results

Baseline

A total of 2553 patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis were enrolled in the SPEECH cohort. After applying the treatment eligibility criteria, 1917 patients were excluded because the treatment group was not recorded or because they received non-IL-17A inhibitors, leaving 636 patients receiving IL-17A inhibitors. After excluding patients with missing baseline data, 322 patients had complete baseline data. During the 12-week follow-up, 89 patients were further excluded because follow-up data were unavailable or medication was not used as scheduled. Finally, 233 patients were included in the main analysis (Figure 1). The analytic cohort was predominantly male (74.2%), with a median age of 44.00 years and median baseline PASI of 14.40; detailed baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. As shown in Table S1, baseline characteristics differed across PASI response groups for BMI, psoriatic arthritis, previous acitretin treatment, PASI, and DLQI (P < 0.05). Baseline HADS-A and HADS-D did not differ statistically across response groups (P > 0.05). To further assess HADS-defined absence of anxiety and depressive symptoms, we compared the proportions of patients achieving HADS-A = 0 or HADS-D = 0. At week 12, there were a greater proportion of patients achieving HADS-A = 0 observed in PASI100 group (60.8%) compared with PASI75–<100 (41.4%) and PASI<75 (28.1%) groups (all pairwise P < 0.05) (Figure 2C). Subgroup and Sensitivity Analyses

Subgroup analyses found no statistically significant interactions across sex, BMI, baseline PASI, baseline DLQI, smoking status, alcohol use, or IL-17A inhibitor type (all P for interaction > 0.05), providing no evidence that the associations of PASI75–<100 versus PASI100 with HADS-A = 0 or HADS-D = 0 differed across the prespecified subgroups (Figure 3). Sensitivity analyses excluding patients with baseline HADS-A = 0 or HADS-D = 0 from the corresponding outcome analyses yielded directionally consistent results. Compared with PASI100, PASI75–<100 remained associated with higher week-12 HADS-A and HADS-D and lower odds of HADS-A = 0 and HADS-D = 0 (Table S2). Discussion

In this study, we found that CSC at week 12 was associated with the most favorable psychological outcomes among patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis treated with IL-17A inhibitors. Compared with PASI100, PASI75–<100 was associated with lower odds of HADS-defined absence of anxiety and depressive symptoms and with higher week-12 HADS-A and HADS-D. The findings remained directionally consistent in sensitivity analyses excluding patients with HADS-A = 0 or HADS-D = 0 at baseline. These findings suggest that the distinction between PASI100 and PASI75–<100 may be relevant not only for skin clearance, but also for short-term psychological status.

An earlier SPEECH analysis compared HADS outcomes across different biologic treatments,22 whereas the present study evaluated HADS-defined absence of anxiety and depressive symptoms in relation to week-12 PASI response level among patients receiving IL-17A inhibitors. Previous studies have associated CSC with greater DLQI improvement and more favorable patient-reported outcomes than lower levels of skin clearance.15,16,24–26 In line with treat-to-target recommendations emphasizing higher skin-clearance targets,27–29 the present study extends this evidence by evaluating HADS-defined absence of anxiety and depressive symptoms across PASI<75, PASI75–<100, and PASI100. The direct comparison between PASI100 and PASI75–<100 further examines whether CSC is associated with more favorable psychological status than substantial but incomplete skin clearance.

Among patients with psoriasis, anxiety and depressive symptoms are associated with poorer quality of life and greater difficulties in daily activities, work, and social life.9,10 These psychological symptoms may therefore contribute to overall disease burden beyond that captured by objective skin severity alone. Potential psychosocial pathways linking improvement in psoriasis severity with better psychological outcomes include reduced visibility of lesions, symptom-related distress, perceived stigmatization, and social avoidance, together with improved daily and interpersonal functioning.10,30 From a biological perspective, psoriasis and psychological symptoms may share inflammatory and neuroimmune mechanisms involving TNF-α signaling and the IL-23/IL-17 axis.3,4,31 Attenuation of inflammatory activity during psoriasis treatment has therefore been proposed as one potential explanation for concurrent psychological improvement. These findings underscore the value of evaluating psychological status alongside PASI response and dermatology-specific quality of life in patients receiving biologic treatment.

Previous studies of secukinumab and ixekizumab reported improvements in anxiety or depressive symptom burden during treatment.17,18 A previous prospective study of patients receiving NB-UVB similarly found that reductions in PASI, BSA, and PGA were associated with reductions in HADS-A and HADS-D.32 The present study extends this evidence by focusing on HADS-defined absence of anxiety and depressive symptoms across achieved PASI response levels, particularly the distinction between PASI100 and PASI75–<100.

This study has limitations. First, as a post hoc analysis of a prospective observational cohort, the study is susceptible to residual confounding, reverse causation, and selection bias.33,34 Week-12 PASI response and HADS outcomes were assessed contemporaneously, precluding determination of temporal ordering or causality. Second, HADS is a self-report screening instrument and does not replace a structured psychiatric assessment; HADS-A = 0 and HADS-D = 0 should be interpreted as stringent, HADS-defined absence of reported symptoms rather than complete resolution of anxiety or depression or evidence of psychiatric remission.23,35–37 Third, blood-based inflammatory markers were not included in the assessment framework of the present analysis; therefore, the potential contribution of systemic inflammation to the observed associations could not be evaluated. Finally, the 12-week follow-up and restriction to patients receiving IL-17A inhibitors limit the assessment of longer-term outcomes and generalizability to other treatments.

Conclusion

Overall, our findings suggest that CSC may be a clinically meaningful treatment state when psychological outcomes are considered alongside skin clearance. Future studies should evaluate whether these associations persist over longer follow-up, whether they generalize across biologic classes, and whether formal mental-health assessment can clarify the relationship between CSC and psychological symptom-free status.

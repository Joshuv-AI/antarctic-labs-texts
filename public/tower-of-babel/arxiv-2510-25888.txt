The Cauchy problem for gradient generalized Ricci solitons on a bundle gerbe
Severin Bunk, Miguel Pino, C. S. Shahbazi

Abstract
. We prove well-posedness of the analytic Cauchy problem for gradient generalized Ricci solitons on an abelian bundle gerbe and solve the initial data equations on every compact Riemann surface. Along the way, we provide a novel characterization of the self-similar solutions of the generalized Ricci flow by means of families of automorphisms of the underlying abelian bundle gerbe covering families of diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity.



1.
Introduction

The generalized Ricci flow
[
16
,
28
]
is a natural generalization of the celebrated Ricci flow
[
17
]
. This evolution flow, which originates in the string theory literature as the renormalization group flow of the bosonic string
[
31
]
, describes the evolution of a Riemannian metric
g
coupled to a
b-field
. Its self-similar solutions, known as generalized Ricci solitons, are expected to foster remarkable applications in the classification and uniformization of compact complex surfaces
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
. The main goal of this article is to study the Riemannian Cauchy problem for gradient generalized Ricci solitons, proving its well-posedness for analytic initial data.

The generalized Ricci flow is typically studied either on the bare underlying manifold, as a flow for metrics and closed three-forms, or on an exact Courant algebroid, as a flow of generalized metrics and divergence operators
[
14
,
16
]
. Here, motivated by supergravity, we adopt a different perspective: by
Dirac quantization
, the three-form flux occurring in the generalized Ricci flow must be integral, and hence can be interpreted as the curvature of a curving on a bundle gerbe equipped with a connective structure
[
25
,
4
]
. Consequently, the theory of
\mathrm{U}(1)
bundle gerbes can be used to give a gauge-theoretic global geometric interpretation of the generalized Ricci flow and, in particular, of the notion of
b-field
as a curving on a bundle gerbe. This brings the generalized Ricci flow into the realm of
higher geometry
and in line with the ongoing modern development of supergravity and string theory as a higher gauge theory
[
2
,
5
,
37
]
.

Considering the generalized Ricci flow as an evolution flow on a bundle gerbe with a fixed connective structure, we define its self-similar flows as those evolving by
families
of automorphisms, namely stable isomorphisms
[
38
,
39
]
covering possibly non-trivial diffeomorphisms of the underlying manifold. This is delicate, since it requires a sensible notion of
smooth
family of automorphisms at a level of generality that we have not found in the literature. Recently, an exponential map for a large class of 2-groups, including the 2-group of stable isomorphisms of a bundle gerbe, was obtained in
[
37
, Theorem 1.4]
. However, we cannot directly apply this result to our purposes, as we need to consider automorphisms that cover diffeomorphisms of the underlying manifold. Instead, we propose an explicit natural definition of a smooth family of automorphisms under a technical condition on the submersion underlying the given bundle gerbe, which builds on the general action that we obtain in Theorem
2.12
. This results in a novel computation of the generalized Ricci soliton system via the action of a smooth family of higher isomorphisms. This should be compared with the computation of the generalized Ricci soliton system carried out in
[
16
]
via families of automorphisms of an exact Courant algebroid. Focusing on
gradient
generalized Ricci solitons, a streamlined version of our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

The Cauchy problem for the gradient generalized Ricci soliton system on a
\mathrm{U}(1)
bundle gerbe is well-posed for analytic initial data.

The reader is referred to Theorem
3.15
for more details. To prove this result, we first transform the gradient generalized Ricci soliton from the
string frame
to the
Einstein frame
, and then reduce the metric, curving, and dilaton to families of objects that satisfy a system of second-order evolution equations and
time-dependent
constraints on a given hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
. This requires reducing the abelian bundle gerbe to the Cauchy surface, which results in an abelian bundle gerbe equipped with certain time-dependent extra data. We then apply the Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem after an adequate change of variables to a set of local variables that is gauge-invariant with respect to stable isomorphisms. Finally, the result follows from a series of long computations, deferred to the appendix, which guarantee that the local flow preserves the constraint equations. This theorem can be understood as a natural generalization of the classical results of Koiso
[
20
]
on the Riemannian Cauchy problem for Einstein metrics to the framework of higher geometry. We then consider the constraint equations of the Cauchy problem of the gradient generalized Ricci soliton on a two-dimensional oriented manifold, proving the following result, see Theorem
3.19
for more details.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a compact and oriented two-dimensional manifold. For every conformal class
[h]
of Riemannian metrics on
\Sigma
, there exists a solution to the constraint equations of the NS gradient generalized Ricci soliton system whose Riemannian metric belongs to
[h]
.

As an immediate consequence of this theorem, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(\Sigma,h)
be a punctured Riemann surface. Then, there exists an NS gradient generalized Ricci soliton
(g,b,\phi)
on a bundle gerbe over a three-manifold
M
, and an embedding
\iota\colon\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
such that
\iota^{\ast}g\in[h]
. In particular, there exist infinitely many different topological types of three-dimensional NS gradient generalized Ricci solitons.

Here, by an
NS gradient generalized Ricci soliton
we refer to a gradient generalized Ricci soliton that also satisfies the
dilaton equation
of NS supergravity and is therefore an honest solution of the bosonic equations of the latter. We hope that these results can be exploited in the future to construct new complete gradient generalized Ricci solitons in the spirit of
[
30
]
. In a different direction, it would be interesting to generalize these results to the case of
Heterotic solitons

[
23
]
, which arise as self-similar solutions to the Heterotic renormalization group flow at second order
[
24
,
29
]
. This flow yields a natural generalization of the notion of generalized Ricci flow that involves a Riemann curvature term
squared
, which makes the analysis involved notably different from the generalized Ricci flow case.

2.
The generalized Ricci flow on a bundle gerbe

This section introduces the generalized Ricci flow
[
28
,
31
]
on a fixed bundle gerbe
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
over an oriented manifold
M
. Our primary objective is to characterize its self-similar flows, which lead to the notion of
generalized Ricci-soliton

[
16
]
.
Let us briefly recall the definition of a bundle gerbe—for full details, we refer the reader to
[
38
,
39
,
25
,
4
,
7
]
.
Given a surjective submersion
\pi\colon Y\to M
, we denote its
n
-fold fiber product with itself over
M
by
Y^{[n]}=Y\times_{M}\cdots\times_{M}Y
(it is the
(n{-}1)
-st level in the Čech nerve of
\pi
).

Definition 2.1
.

A
bundle gerbe
on
M
consists of a surjective submersion
\pi\colon Y\to M
, a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle
\mathcal{P}\to Y^{[2]}
, and a bundle isomorphism:

(1)

\mu\colon\pi^{\ast}_{12}\mathcal{P}\otimes\pi^{\ast}_{23}\mathcal{P}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\pi^{\ast}_{13}\mathcal{P}

over
Y^{[3]}
, where:

\pi_{ij}\colon Y\times_{M}Y\times_{M}Y\to Y\times_{M}Y\,,\qquad i,j=1,2,3

denotes the projection onto the
i
-th and
j
-th factors. This bundle isomorphism (sometimes called gerbe multiplication) has to satisfy an associativity condition over
Y^{[4]}
. A
connective structure
on a bundle gerbe as above consists of a connection on the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle
\mathcal{P}
such that the bundle morphism
\mu
is connection-preserving. A
curving
on a bundle gerbe with connective structure is a
2
-form
b\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
such that
\pi_{2}^{*}b-\pi_{1}^{*}b=F_{A}
in
\Omega^{2}(Y^{[2]})
.

We denote a bundle gerbe with connective structure as a quadruple
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
.
From now on, all bundle gerbes appearing in this article will be endowed with a connective structure unless stated otherwise.
Thus, to save notation, the term bundle gerbe shall always include the choice of a connective structure.
A compatible pair
(A,b)
of a connective structure and a curving on a bundle gerbe is called a
connection
on the bundle gerbe.

Remark 2.2
.

A more compact way of defining a bundle gerbe is as a central extension of Lie groupoids:

Y\times\mathrm{U}(1)\to(\mathcal{P},A)\to Y\times_{M}Y\rightrightarrows Y\xrightarrow{\pi}M

where the
Y\times\mathrm{U}(1)
is understood as the Lie groupoid whose source and target maps are both equal and given by the canonical projection
Y\times\mathrm{U}(1)\to Y
.

Although the generalized Ricci flow is usually considered as an evolution flow for generalized metrics on an exact Courant algebroid
[
16
]
, from a supergravity point of view, and thanks to the celebrated Dirac quantization condition for the supergravity
H-flux
, it is also natural to consider it as a geometric flow on a geometric model for the singular cohomology group
H^{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})
rather than
H^{3}(M,\mathbb{R})
. In this note, we choose this geometric model to be a bundle gerbe
[
25
,
4
]
. In this context, it is noteworthy that generalized metrics on exact Courant algebroids with integral Ševera class are closely related to pairs of Riemannian metrics and curvings on a bundle gerbe with connective structure. Indeed, Hitchin’s generalized tangent bundle construction associates to each gerbe with connective structure an exact Courant algebroid, and this construction exhausts all integral exact Courant algebroids up to isomorphism
[
18
]
. Then, generalized metrics on the associated Courant algebroid are in bijection with pairs of a Riemannian metric on the base manifold and a curving on the gerbe with connective structure. However, gerbes have a richer structure of morphisms between them, as well as a natural notion of tensor product that categorifies the abelian group structure on
H^{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. From a gauge theory perspective, gerbes (with connective structure) are to principal bundles as Courant algebroids are to the Atiyah algebroids of those principal bundles (for more details on this relationship, we refer the reader to
[
11
,
10
]
). Thus, through this lens, it seems very natural to consider the
b
-field, a 2-form gauge field, as a curving on a bundle gerbe with connective structure. However, to our knowledge, geometric flows have not been systematically studied in combination with higher geometric structures beyond the algebroid point of view
[
14
,
15
]
, and so we will need to first set up the appropriate stage.

2.1.
The generalized Ricci flow

Given a curving
b\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
we denote its curvature by
H_{b}\in\Omega^{3}(M)
. This is a closed three-form defined on
M
with periods in
2\pi\mathbb{Z}
; it is defined as the unique
3
-form on
M
such that
\pi^{*}H_{b}=\mathrm{d}b
on
Y
. The exact simplicial differential of the simplicial manifold generated as the Čech nerve of
Y\to M
will be denoted by
\delta\colon\Omega^{k}(Y^{[j]})\to\Omega^{k}(Y^{[j+1]})
for any

, where

is the
k
-fold fibered product of

with itself.

Definition 2.3
.

The
generalized Ricci flow
on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
is the following curvature evolution flow:

(2)

\partial_{t}g_{t}=-2\mathrm{Ric}^{g_{t}}+H_{b_{t}}\circ_{g_{t}}H_{b_{t}}\,,\qquad\partial_{t}b_{t}=-\nabla^{g_{t}\ast}H_{b_{t}}

for pairs
\left\{g_{t},b_{t}\right\}
consisting of a smooth family
g_{t}
of Riemannian metrics on
M
and a smooth family
b_{t}\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
of curvings. Here we have set:

(H_{b_{t}}\circ_{g_{t}}H_{b_{t}})(v_{1},v_{2})=\langle H_{b_{t}}(v_{1}),H_{b_{t}}(v_{2})\rangle_{g_{t}}\,,\qquad\forall\,\,v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

in terms of the determinant metric
\langle-,-\rangle_{g_{t}}
induced by
g_{t}
on the exterior forms on
M
.

Hence, initial data for the generalized Ricci flow consist of pairs of the form
(g,b)
, where
g
is a Riemannian metric
M
and
b
is a curving on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
.

Remark 2.4
.

The set of curvings on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
is an affine space modelled on the two-forms
\Omega^{2}(M)
on
M
, that is, if
b_{1},b_{2}\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
are curvings on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
, then there exists a unique two-form
b_{0}\in\Omega(M)
such that:

b_{1}-b_{2}=\pi^{\ast}b_{0}

Therefore, the
time
derivative
\partial_{t}b_{t}
of a smooth family
b_{t}
of curvings, which is a priori a family of two-forms on
Y
satisfying
F_{A}=\delta b_{t}\in\Omega^{2}(Y\times_{M}Y)
, is in fact canonically defined as a family of two-forms on
M
, and is understood as such in the second equation of (
2
).

2.2.

Remark 2.14
.

We conclude this section by remarking that a diffeomorphism

isotopic to the identity on

admits a lift
\bar{f}
along the map

in many interesting cases, including when

is any principal bundle on
M
(or, for instance, the diffeological based path fibration of

). This is the case, for example, whenever

is the universal cover of
M
. If
\pi\colon Y\to M
is a surjective submersion and

, then any bundle gerbe on

can be defined, up to 1-isomorphism, using the surjective submersion
\pi
: indeed, since bundle gerbes satisfy descent
[

]
, any gerbe on

can be written as bundle gerbe descent data for

are the same as the constituents of a bundle gerbe on

n

\mathbb{S}^{n}

2.3.
Self-similar flows

Let
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
denote the set of initial data on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
, that is, the set of pairs
(g,b)
consisting of a Riemannian metric on
M
and a curving on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
. From Definition
2.3
we readily see that such a pair
(g,b)\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
is a generalized Ricci flow if and only if:

\mathrm{Ric}^{g}=\frac{1}{2}H_{b}\circ_{g}H_{b}\,,\qquad\nabla^{g\ast}H_{b}=0

Solutions to these equations are
fixed points
of the generalized Ricci flow and can be considered as having
trivial
evolution. There is a more general class of generalized Ricci flows that can be considered as evolving trivially, namely those that evolve via symmetries of the system, which in our framework correspond to families of automorphisms in
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
. We define a
self-similar
generalized Ricci flow as a generalized Ricci flow of the form:

(g_{t},b_{t})=(g,b)\cdot(\mathcal{Q}_{t},\Theta_{t},\xi_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})\,,\qquad(g,b)\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)

for a
smooth
family of automorphisms
(\mathcal{Q}_{t},\Theta_{t},\xi_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})
parametrized by an open interval with Cartesian coordinate
t
. Here the
dot
denotes the natural right action of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
, namely:

(25)

(g_{t},b_{t})=(g,b)\cdot(\mathcal{Q}_{t},\Theta_{t},\xi_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})=(f_{t}^{\ast}g,\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}b-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\Theta_{t}})

Remark 2.15
.

That is, a self-similar flow may be seen as a ‘homotopy stationary point’ of the flow:
the set of pairs
(g,b)
of curvings and metrics carries an action of the automorphism 2-group
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
(or rather its group of isomorphism classes).
This induces a notion of morphisms between curvings.
That is, the action induces the so-called action groupoid.
A self-similar flow now consists in specifying, for each time
t
, a value
(g_{t},b_{t})
of the flow, which may be different from
(g_{0},b_{0})
, but at the same time also specifying a morphism
(\mathcal{Q}_{t},\Theta_{t},\xi_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})
in the action groupoid which witnesses that the point
(g_{t},b_{t})
is still isomorphic—though, in general, no longer equal—to the initial point
(g_{0},b_{0})
.

There is a smooth theory for the automorphism 2-groups
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
in
[
10
]
, developed in a smooth higher-geometric setting (using the language of simplicial and
\infty
-presehaves on cartesian spaces), but here we employ a restricted notion of a smooth family of automorphisms
(\mathcal{Q}_{t},\Theta_{t},\xi_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})
, which will be sufficient for our purposes. First, we consider
f_{t}
to be a smooth isotopy of diffeomorphisms such that
f_{0}=\mathrm{Id}
is the identity on
M
. Secondly, we assume that this isotopy
f_{t}
admits a smooth lift
\bar{f}_{t}\colon Y\to Y
to an isotopy of
Y
with
\bar{f}_{t}=\mathrm{Id}_{Y}
, which we consider as given. Existence of such a smooth lift is, in general, obstructed, but it always exists in important cases, for instance when
\pi\colon Y\to M
is the universal cover of
M
. The lift
\bar{f}_{t}\colon Y\to Y
fixes, in turn, the smooth map
\xi_{t}
as stated in the previous diagram:

\displaystyle\xi_{t}=\mathrm{Id}\times\bar{f}_{t}\colon Y\times_{\pi}Y\to Y\times^{f_{t}}_{\pi}Y

which can hence be omitted for ease of notation. Since
f_{t}
is an isotopy to the identity at
t=0
, it follows that we can choose
\bar{f}_{t}
to be an isotopy to the identity at
t=0
, and therefore we may take
Q
to be a
fixed
trivial
\mathrm{U}(1)
bundle on
Y\times_{\pi}Y
. Finally, we choose a family of connections
\Theta_{t}
and a family of automorphisms:

(26)

\Psi_{t}\colon(\varrho^{\ast}_{1}\mathcal{P},\varrho^{\ast}_{1}A)\otimes(\mathfrak{z}^{\ast}_{(2)}\mathcal{Q},\mathfrak{z}^{\ast}_{(2)}\Theta_{t})\to(\mathfrak{z}^{\ast}_{(1)}Q,\mathfrak{z}^{\ast}_{(1)}\Theta_{t})\otimes(\varrho^{\ast}_{2}\mathcal{P},\varrho^{\ast}_{2}A)

such that
(\mathcal{Q},\Theta_{t},\xi_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})
fits into the master diagram for every
t
. This yields a well-defined notion of family of smooth automorphisms
(\mathcal{Q},\Theta_{t},\xi_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})
of
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
, which we require to be the
identity
at
t=0
, under the assumption that isotopies of
M
can be lifted to isotopies of
Y
along
\pi\colon Y\to M
. By this discussion, we also obtain the following result.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
f_{t}\colon M\to M
be an isotopy to the identity at
t=0
and assume there exists a smooth lift
\bar{f}_{t}\colon Y\to Y
of
f_{t}
that is an isotopy to the identity. Then, there exists family
\Theta_{t}
of connections on
Q
and a family of automophisms
\Psi_{t}
as in (
26
), such that
(\mathcal{Q},\Theta_{t},\xi_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})
is a family of automorphisms of
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
.

We characterize now the self-similar solutions of the generalized Ricci flow on an abelian bundle gerbe
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
, similar to the characterization obtained in
[
16
]
within the framework of generalized metrics on exact Courant algebroids.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
(g,b)\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
. There exists a family
(\mathcal{Q},\Theta_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
such that the pair:

(27)

(g_{t},b_{t})=(\mathcal{Q},\Theta_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})\cdot(g,b)=(f_{t}^{\ast}g,\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}b-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\Theta_{t}})

is a generalized Ricci flow only if:

(28)

\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{v}g-\frac{1}{2}H_{b}\circ_{g}H_{b}=0\,,\qquad\nabla^{g\ast}H_{b}+H_{b}(v)+\alpha=0

for a vector field
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and a closed two-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{2}(M)
on
M
. Conversely, if
(g,b)\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
satisfies (
28
) for a vector field
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and a closed two-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{2}(M)
such that
\pi^{\ast}\alpha\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
is exact, then there exists a family of automorphisms
(\mathcal{Q},\Theta_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
for which (
27
) is a generalized Ricci flow on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
.

Proof.

We proceed by plugging
(g_{t},b_{t})=(f_{t}^{\ast}g,\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}b-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\Theta_{t}})
into the equations of the generalized Ricci flow, given in (
2
). We first compute:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{t}=\partial_{t}f^{\ast}_{t}g=f^{\ast}_{t}\mathcal{L}_{v_{t}}g

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b_{t}=\partial_{t}(\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}b-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\Theta_{t}})=\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}\mathcal{L}_{\bar{v}_{t}}b-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\partial_{t}\Theta_{t}}=\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}((\pi^{\ast}H_{b})(\bar{v}_{t})+\mathrm{d}_{Y}(b(\bar{v}_{t})))-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\partial_{t}\Theta_{t}}

where
v_{t}
is the family of vector fields on
M
generated by
f_{t}
via the relation:

v_{t}\circ f_{t}=\partial_{t}f_{t}

and the symbol
\mathcal{L}
denotes the Lie derivative. Similarly,
\bar{v}_{t}
is the family of vector fields on
Y
generated by
\bar{f}_{t}
:

\bar{v}_{t}\circ f_{t}=\partial_{t}\bar{f}_{t}

Note that
\mathrm{d}\pi(\bar{v}_{t})=v_{t}
. This immediately implies that
(g_{t},b_{t})
satisfies the Einstein evolution equation in (
2
) if and only if:

f^{\ast}_{t}\mathcal{L}_{v_{t}}g=-2f^{\ast}_{t}\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+f^{\ast}_{t}(H_{b}\circ_{g}H_{b})\,\,\Leftrightarrow\,\,\mathcal{L}_{v_{t}}g=-2\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+H_{b}\circ_{g}H_{b}

Hence, evaluating this equation at
t=0
, we obtain the first equation in the statement. Regarding the Maxwell evolution equation in (
2
), that is, the evolution equation for
b_{t}
, we observe that:

\delta(\mathrm{d}_{Y}(\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}b(\bar{v}_{t}))-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\partial_{t}\Theta_{t}})=0

where
\delta
is the simplicial differential of the Čech nerve of
\pi\colon Y\to M
. Therefore, there exists a smooth family
\alpha_{t}
of closed one-forms such that:

\pi^{\ast}\alpha_{t}=\mathrm{d}_{Y}(\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}b(\bar{v}_{t}))-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\partial_{t}\Theta_{t}}

and consequently:

\nabla^{g\ast}H_{b}+H_{b}(v_{t})+\alpha_{t}=0

Evaluating this equation at
t=0
we obtain the second equation in the statement. For the converse, assume
(g,b)\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
satisfies equations (
28
) for a certain
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and a closed two-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{2}(M)
such that
\pi^{\ast}\alpha=\mathrm{d}\theta\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
is exact. Consider the family
f_{t}
of diffeomorphisms connected to the identity generated by
v
and its lift
\bar{f}_{t}\colon Y\to Y
, with corresponding vector field
\bar{v}\in\mathfrak{X}(Y)
. By Lemma
2.16
, there exists a family of connections
\Theta_{t}
and a family of isomorphisms
\Psi_{t}
as in (
26
) such that
(\mathcal{Q},\Theta_{t},\xi_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})
is a family of automorphisms of
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
. Then, we define:

g_{t}=f^{\ast}_{t}g\,,\qquad b_{t}=b\cdot(\mathcal{Q},\Theta_{t},\xi_{t},\Psi_{t},f_{t})=\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}b-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\Theta_{t}}

where
f_{t}
is the family of automorphisms generated by
v
and
\bar{f}_{t}\colon Y\to Y
is a lift of
f_{t}
, with corresponding vector field
\bar{v}\in\mathfrak{X}(Y)
. We compute:

\partial_{t}g_{t}=\partial_{t}f^{\ast}_{t}g=f^{\ast}_{t}\mathcal{L}_{v}g=f^{\ast}_{t}(-2\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+H_{b}\circ_{g}H_{b})=-2\mathrm{Ric}^{g_{t}}+(f^{\ast}_{t}H_{b})\circ_{g_{t}}(f^{\ast}_{t}H_{b})=-2\mathrm{Ric}^{g_{t}}+H_{b_{t}}\circ_{g_{t}}H_{b_{t}}

and hence the evolution equation for
g_{t}
in Equation (
2
) is satisfied. Regarding
b_{t}
, we have:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b_{t}=\partial_{t}(\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}b-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\Theta_{t}})=\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}\mathcal{L}_{\bar{v}}b-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\partial_{t}\Theta_{t}}=\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}(\pi^{\ast}H_{b}(v)+\mathrm{d}_{Y}(b(\bar{v})))-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\partial_{t}\Theta_{t}}

\displaystyle=\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}(-\pi^{\ast}(\alpha+\nabla^{g\ast}H_{b})+\mathrm{d}_{Y}(b(\bar{v})))-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\partial_{t}\Theta_{t}}=-\nabla^{g_{t}\ast}H_{b_{t}}-\mathrm{d}_{Y}(\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}\theta)+\mathrm{d}_{Y}(\bar{f}^{\ast}_{t}b(\bar{v}))-\Delta_{\pi}^{\ast}F_{\partial_{t}\Theta_{t}}

Therefore, choosing
\Theta_{t}
so that:

\Theta_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\bar{f}^{\ast}_{s}(b(\bar{v})+\theta)\,\mathrm{d}s\in\Omega^{1}(Y)

modulo a closed one-form on
Y
, we conclude that
(g_{t},b_{t})
is a generalized Ricci flow.

This result motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.18
.

A
steady generalized Ricci soliton
on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
is a triple
(g,b,v)
consisting of a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
, a curving
b
on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
and a vector field
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
satisfying the following differential system:

(29)

\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{v}g-\frac{1}{2}H_{b}\circ_{g}H_{b}=0\,,\qquad\nabla^{g\ast}H_{b}+H_{b}(v)=0

A steady generalized Ricci soliton
(g,b,v)
is
gradient
if
v^{\flat_{g}}=\nabla^{g}\phi
for a smooth function
C^{\infty}(M)
.

Remark 2.19
.

It is also possible to define
shrinking
and
expanding
generalized Ricci solitons, see for example
[
16
,
30
]
. Since these do not seem to have a direct supergravity interpretation, we restrict in this note to
steady
generalized Ricci solitons.

We will omit the term
steady
in the following for simplicity in the exposition. Equations (
29
) define the
generalized Ricci soliton system
. Since we will exclusively consider steady generalized Ricci solitons, in the following we will omit the term
steady
for simplicity in the exposition. We will denote gradient Ricci solitons as triples of the form
(g,b,\phi)
, and we will refer to the function
\phi
as the
dilaton
of the generalized Ricci soliton. We will refer to the first equation in (
29
) as the Einstein equation, whereas we will refer to the second equation in (
29
) as the
Maxwell equation
. This terminology is rooted in the theoretical physics interpretation of gradient generalized Ricci solitons as solutions to a certain supergravity theory. For gradient generalized Ricci solitons, the Einstein equation simplifies to:

\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\phi-\frac{1}{2}H_{b}\circ_{g}H_{b}=0

The following result is well-known; an explicit proof can be found, for instance, in
[
16
, Proposition 4.33]
or
[
24
, Proposition 3.7]
.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
(g,b,\phi)
be a gradient Ricci soliton. The following formula holds:

(30)

\nabla^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}\phi+|\mathrm{d}\phi|_{g}^{2}-|H_{b}|^{2}_{g}=\lambda(g,b,\phi)

for a real constant
\lambda(g,b,\phi)\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.21
.

The factor 6 discrepancy with respect to
[
16
, Proposition 4.33]
is due to the fact that we are using the determinant norm rather than the tensor norm on forms.

A gradient generalized Ricci soliton
(g,b,\phi)
satisfies the equations of motion of bosonic NS-NS supergravity if and only if
\lambda(g,b,\phi)=0
. When this is the case, Equation (
30
) corresponds to the
dilaton equation
of bosonic NS-NS supergravity. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.22
.

A gradient generalized Ricci soliton
(g,b,\phi)
is
NS
if
\lambda(g,b,\phi)=0
.

We will denote by
\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
the configuration space of triples of the form
(g,b,\phi)
, and by
\mathrm{Sol}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)\subset\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
, the set of gradient Ricci solitons on
(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
.

Remark 2.23
.

The
infinitesimal
results we have obtained above by differentiating families of automorphisms can be understood within the general and comprehensive framework for infinitesimal symmetries of bundle gerbes developed in
[
12
,
21
]
in terms of multiplicative vector fields on the Lie groupoid underlying a bundle gerbe. This is however beyond the scope of this note.

2.4.
The generalized Ricci soliton system in the Einstein Frame

The gradient generalized Ricci soliton system admits a variational interpretation through the following action functional, which is inherited from the NS-NS supergravity bosonic action:

(31)

\displaystyle S[g,b]=\int_{M}\nu_{g}e^{-\phi}(s^{g}+|\mathrm{d}\phi|^{2}_{g}-\frac{1}{2}|H_{b}|_{g}^{2})

where
s^{g}\in C^{\infty}(M)
denotes the scalar curvature of
g
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
is considered as given. The fact that in this functional the scalar curvature occurs with a multiplicative factor of the dilaton is related to the fact that the Einstein equation of the generalized Ricci soliton system includes a term of the form
\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\phi\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\odot T^{\ast}M)
, which turns out to be problematic regarding the analytic properties of the system. This can be remedied by means of a conformal transformation of the metric that eliminates such a factor and brings the Einstein equation to its
standard form
, usually referred to as the
Einstein frame
in the theoretical physics literature. To do this, we consider the bijection:

\displaystyle(-)_{E}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)\to\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)\,,\qquad(g,b,\phi)\mapsto(g_{E}=e^{-\frac{2\phi}{n-1}}\,g,b,\phi)

where
\dim(M)=n+1
.

Lemma 2.24
.

A triple
(g,b,\phi)\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
is a gradient generalized Ricci soliton if and only if
(g_{E},b,\phi)
satisfies the
following differential system:

(32)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{g_{E}}-\frac{1}{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}\phi\otimes\mathrm{d}\phi+\frac{1}{\,n-1\,}(e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}|H_{b}|_{g_{E}}^{2}+\lambda\,e^{\frac{2\phi}{n-1}})\,g_{E}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}\,H_{b}\circ_{g_{E}}H_{b}=0

(33)

\displaystyle\delta^{g_{E}}H_{b}+\frac{4}{n-1}H_{b}(\mathrm{d}\phi^{\sharp_{g_{E}}})=0\,,\qquad\delta^{g_{E}}\mathrm{d}\phi=e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}|H_{b}|_{g_{E}}^{2}+\lambda\,e^{\frac{2\phi}{n-1}}

for a real constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.25
.

By Lemma
2.20
, the second equation in (
33
), to which we still refer as the
dilaton equation
of the system, is redundant. However, it is convenient to consider it explicitly as part of the gradient generalized Ricci soliton system to study its Cauchy problem.

Proof.

Setting
g=e^{\frac{2\phi}{n-1}}g_{E}
, we begin with the classical identity:

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}_{v_{1}}v_{2}=\nabla^{g_{E}}_{v_{1}}v_{2}+\frac{1}{n-1}v_{1}(\phi)v_{2}+\frac{1}{n-1}v_{2}(\phi)v_{1}-\frac{1}{n-1}g_{E}(v_{1},v_{2})(\mathrm{d}\phi)^{\sharp_{g_{E}}}

From this, we obtain the standard identities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{g}=\mathrm{Ric}^{g_{E}}-(\nabla^{g_{E}}\mathrm{d}\phi-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathrm{d}\phi\otimes\mathrm{d}\phi)+\frac{1}{n-1}(\delta^{g_{E}}\mathrm{d}\phi-\lvert\mathrm{d}\phi\rvert^{2}_{g_{E}})\,g_{E}

\displaystyle\delta^{g}\alpha=e^{-\frac{2\phi}{n-1}}(\delta^{g_{E}}\alpha-\frac{n+1-2k}{n-1}\alpha(\mathrm{d}\phi)^{\sharp_{g_{E}}})

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\phi=\nabla^{g_{E}}\mathrm{d}\phi-\frac{2}{n-1}\mathrm{d}\phi\otimes\mathrm{d}\phi+\frac{1}{n-1}|\mathrm{d}\phi|_{g_{E}}^{2}g_{E}

Using the previous relations, we compute:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{g}+\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\phi-\frac{1}{2}H_{b}\circ_{g}H_{b}=\mathrm{Ric}^{g_{E}}+\frac{1}{\,n-1\,}\delta^{g_{E}}\mathrm{d}\phi\,g_{E}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}\,H_{b}\circ_{g_{E}}H_{b}-\frac{1}{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}\phi\otimes\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle\delta^{g}H_{b}+H_{b}(\mathrm{d}\phi)^{\sharp_{g}}=e^{-\frac{2\phi}{n-1}}(\delta^{g_{E}}H_{b}+\frac{4}{n-1}H_{b}(\mathrm{d}\phi)^{\sharp_{g_{E}}})

\displaystyle\nabla^{g\ast}\mathrm{d}\phi+|\mathrm{d}\phi|_{g}^{2}-|H_{b}|^{2}_{g}=e^{-\frac{2\phi}{n-1}}\delta^{g_{E}}\mathrm{d}\phi-e^{-\frac{6\phi}{n-1}}\ |H_{b}|_{g_{E}}^{2}

Combining these equations, we conclude.
∎

Remark 2.26
.

Recall the identity
e^{-\phi}\nu_{g}s^{g}=\nu_{g_{E}}s^{g_{E}}
, which shows explicitly that the scalar curvature term in (
31
) becomes the Einstein-Hilbert term in the Einstein frame.

In the following, we will exclusively consider the gradient generalized Ricci soliton system in the Einstein frame, that is, we will be working with equations (
32
) and (
33
), omitting the subscript
E
in
g_{E}
for ease of notation. There is no loss of generality in this choice since
(-)_{E}\colon\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)\to\mathrm{Conf}(\mathcal{P},A,\pi,\mu)
is, by construction, a bijection both at the level of configuration and solution spaces.

3.
The Cauchy problem

In this section, we study the Cauchy problem for gradient generalized Ricci solitons on a bundle gerbe. In order to set up the Riemannian Cauchy problem for the gradient generalized Ricci soliton system, we fix a smooth hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
and consider the generalized Ricci soliton system on a tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma
in
M
, which we identify with
\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
for an interval
\mathcal{I}
with Cartesian coordinate
\tau
. Hence, we consider the gradient generalized Ricci soliton system on the product manifold
\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
and we proceed by reinterpreting the configuration space of the system in terms of families of objects defined over
\Sigma
satisfying the appropriate reinterpretation of the gradient generalized Ricci soliton system as a system of evolution equations on
\Sigma
. We begin with the globally hyperbolic reduction of a curving on a bundle gerbe on
M=\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
, which is one of the main ingredients of the generalized Ricci soliton system. We proceed along the lines of
[
33
, Chapter 6]
.

3.1.
Time-dependent reduction of bundle gerbes with connection

Let
\bar{\mathcal{C}}:=(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{A},\bar{Y})
be a bundle gerbe on
M=\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
. Since the goal is to reduce the generalized Ricci soliton system on
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
, we will assume that the
topological data
contained in
\mathcal{C}
, namely
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{Y})
, is given by the pull-back of a bundle gerbe
(\mathcal{P},Y)
defined over
\Sigma
, while we will allow the connective structure
\bar{A}
to be genuinely
time-dependent
, that is, not necessarily given by the pull-back of a fixed connective structure on
(\mathcal{P},Y)
. We will refer to such bundle gerbes as
reducible
bundle gerbes on
\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
. This setup captures the dynamics of the Cauchy problem for gradient generalized Ricci solitons on a bundle gerbe in its full generality. Since the submersion
\bar{Y}\to M
of a reducible bundle gerbe on
\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
is, by assumption, a pull-back submersion, it follows that it is isomorphic to:

\bar{Y}=\mathcal{I}\times Y\to\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma

where the projection on the first factor is the identity map and
Y\to\Sigma
is the submersion underlying the bundle gerbe
(\mathcal{P},Y)
. Similarly:

\bar{Y}\times_{M}\bar{Y}=\mathcal{I}\times Y\times_{\Sigma}Y\to\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma

with arrow restricting to the identity map
\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{I}
on the first factor. Since
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
is the pull-back of
\mathcal{P}\to Y\times_{\Sigma}Y
to
\mathcal{I}\times Y\times_{\Sigma}Y
, we have:

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{I}\times Y\times_{\Sigma}Y

again with the arrow restricting to the identity map
\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{I}
on the first factor. The principal action of the group
\mathrm{U}(1)
on
\bar{\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{P}
is the one induced by the principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
action carried by
\mathcal{P}
and the trivial action on the
\mathcal{I}
factor. Let
\bar{A}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{P},\mathbb{R})
be a connective structure on
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
. Then we can write:

\bar{A}=\Psi_{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+A_{\tau}

where
\Psi_{\tau}
is a family of functions on
\mathcal{P}
and
A_{\tau}
is a family of connections on
\mathcal{P}
. Recall that
\tau
denotes the Cartesian coordinate on
\mathcal{I}
. Since
\bar{A}
is a connection on
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
, it is in particular invariant under the
\mathrm{U}(1)
action of the principal bundle
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
, and since this action is ineffective on
\mathcal{I}
we conclude that
\Psi_{\tau}
defines in fact a family of invariant functions on
\mathcal{P}
whence it descends to a family of invariant functions on
Y\times_{\Sigma}Y
that we denote by the same symbol for ease of notation. Since
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{A},\bar{Y})
is a bundle gerbe, it comes equipped with an isomorphism:

(\bar{\pi}^{\ast}_{12}\bar{\mathcal{P}}\otimes\bar{\pi}^{\ast}_{23}\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{\pi}^{\ast}_{12}\bar{A}\otimes\bar{\pi}^{\ast}_{23}\bar{A})\xrightarrow{\simeq}(\bar{\pi}^{\ast}_{13}\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{\pi}^{\ast}_{13}\bar{A})

where
\bar{\pi}\colon\bar{Y}\to M
and the notation
\pi_{ij}\colon\bar{Y}\times_{M}\bar{Y}\times_{M}\bar{Y}\to\bar{Y}\times_{M}\bar{Y}
forgets the entry not labelled neither by
i
nor
j
. This implies
\delta\Psi_{\tau}=0
, where
\delta
is the simplicial differential of the Čech nerve of
\pi\colon Y\to M
. Since this differential is exact, it follows that
\Psi_{\tau}
descends through
\delta
to a family of functions on
Y
that we denote again by
\Psi_{\tau}
. The preceding discussion thus leads to the following correspondence.

Proposition 3.1
.

There is a natural one-to-one correspondence between reducible bundle gerbes
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{Y},\bar{A})
on
M=\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
and tuples
(\mathcal{P},Y,A_{\tau},\Psi_{\tau})
, consisting of a bundle gerbe
(\mathcal{P},Y)
on
\Sigma
, a family of functions
\Psi_{\tau}
on
Y
, and a family of connective structures
A_{\tau}
on
(\mathcal{P},Y)
.

We will refer to
(\mathcal{P},Y,A_{\tau},\Psi_{\tau})
as the
reduction
of
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{Y},\bar{A})
. A direct computation gives the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

The curvature
\mathcal{F}_{\bar{A}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{I}\times Y\times_{\Sigma}Y)
of
\bar{A}=\Psi_{\tau}\mathrm{d}\tau+A_{\tau}
satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\bar{A}}=\mathrm{d}_{Y^{[2]}}\Psi_{\tau}\wedge\mathrm{d}\tau+\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\partial_{\tau}A_{\tau}+\mathcal{F}_{A_{\tau}}

where
\mathrm{d}_{Y^{[2]}}\colon\Omega^{\bullet}(Y\times_{\Sigma}Y)\to\Omega^{\bullet}(Y\times_{\Sigma}Y)
is the exterior derivative on
\Omega^{\bullet}(Y\times_{\Sigma}Y)
and
\mathcal{F}_{A_{\tau}}
is the family of curvatures of
A_{\tau}
.

A curving on
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{Y},\bar{A})
is by definition a two-form
\bar{b}\in\Omega^{2}(\bar{Y}\times_{M}\bar{Y})
satisfying:

\bar{\delta}\bar{b}=F_{\bar{A}}

Since
\bar{Y}\times_{M}\bar{Y}=\mathcal{I}\times Y\times_{\Sigma}Y
, we can canonically write every curving
\bar{b}
on
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{Y},\bar{A})
as follows:

(34)

\bar{b}=\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge a_{\tau}+b_{\tau}

for uniquely determined families of one forms
a_{\tau}
and two-forms
b_{\tau}
on
Y
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{Y},\bar{A})
be a reducible bundle gerbe with connective structure on
M=\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
and let
(\mathcal{P},Y,A_{\tau},\Psi_{\tau})
be its reduction on
\Sigma
. A two-form
\bar{b}\in\Omega^{2}(\bar{Y}\times_{M}\bar{Y})
is a curving on
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{Y},\bar{A})
if and only if, in the decomposition given in (
34
),
b_{\tau}
is a family of curvings on
(\mathcal{P},A,Y)
and
a_{\tau}
is a family of one-forms on
Y
satisfying:

(35)

\delta a_{\tau}=\partial_{\tau}A_{\tau}-\mathrm{d}_{Y^{[2]}}\Psi_{\tau}

for every
\tau\in\mathcal{I}
, where
\delta
is the simplicial differential of the Čech nerve of
\pi\colon Y\to M
.

Proof.

Plugging equation (
34
) in
\bar{\delta}\bar{b}=F_{\bar{A}}
we have:

\bar{\delta}\bar{b}=\bar{\delta}(\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge a_{\tau}+b_{\tau})=\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\delta a_{\tau}+\delta b_{\tau}=\mathcal{F}_{\bar{A}}=\mathrm{d}_{Y^{[2]}}\Psi_{\tau}\wedge\mathrm{d}\tau+\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\partial_{\tau}A_{\tau}+\mathcal{F}_{A_{\tau}}

where we have used Lemma
3.2
. Isolating by tensor type, this gives:

\delta b_{\tau}=F_{A_{\tau}}\,,\qquad\delta a_{\tau}=\partial_{\tau}A_{\tau}-\mathrm{d}_{Y^{[2]}}\Psi_{\tau}

and thus we conclude.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

There is a natural one-to-one correspondence between curvings on a reducible bundle gerbe
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{Y},\bar{A})
with reduction
(\mathcal{P},Y,A_{\tau},\Psi_{\tau})
and pairs
(a_{\tau},b_{\tau})
consisting of a family
b_{\tau}
of curvings on
(\mathcal{P},Y,A_{\tau})
and a family of one-forms
a_{\tau}
on
Y
satisfying Equation (
35
).

Let
\bar{b}=\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge a_{\tau}+b_{\tau}
be a curving on a reducible bundle gerbe over
\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
. Then:

\mathrm{d}_{\bar{Y}}\bar{b}=\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge(\partial_{\tau}b_{\tau}-\mathrm{d}_{Y}a_{\tau})+\mathrm{d}_{Y}b_{\tau}

where
\mathrm{d}_{\bar{Y}}
is the exterior derivative on
\bar{Y}
and
\mathrm{d}_{Y}
is the exterior derivative on
Y
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The family of two-forms
(\partial_{\tau}b_{\tau}-\mathrm{d}_{Y}a_{\tau})\in\Omega^{2}(Y)
satisfies
\delta(\partial_{\tau}b_{\tau}-\mathrm{d}_{Y}a_{\tau})=0
for every
\tau\in\mathcal{I}
, where
\delta
denotes the simplicial differential of the Čech nerve of
\pi\colon Y\to M
.

Proof.

We compute:

\delta(\partial_{\tau}b_{\tau}-\mathrm{d}_{Y}a_{\tau})=\partial_{\tau}\delta b_{\tau}-\mathrm{d}_{Y^{[2]}}\delta a_{\tau}=\partial_{\tau}\mathcal{F}_{A_{\tau}}-\mathrm{d}_{Y^{[2]}}(\partial_{\tau}A_{\tau}-\mathrm{d}_{Y^{[2]}}\Psi_{\tau})=0

where we have used Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Since
\delta
is exact, by the previous lemma, it follows that there exists a unique family of two-forms
\psi_{\tau}\in\Omega^{2}(\Sigma)
on
\Sigma
such that:

\pi^{\ast}\psi_{\tau}=\partial_{\tau}b_{\tau}-\mathrm{d}_{Y}a_{\tau}

Since
\mathrm{d}_{\bar{Y}}\bar{b}=\bar{\pi}^{\ast}H_{\bar{b}}
for a uniquely determined three-form
\bar{H}_{\bar{b}}\in\Omega^{3}(M)
, it follows that:

(36)

H_{\bar{b}}=\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\psi_{\tau}+H_{b_{\tau}}

where
H_{b_{\tau}}
is the curvature of the family of curvings
b_{\tau}
. In particular:

\displaystyle\langle H_{\bar{b}},H_{\bar{b}}\rangle_{\bar{g}}=|\psi_{\tau}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}}+|H_{b_{\tau}}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}}

Therefore, every curving
\bar{b}
on a reducible bundle gerbe is equivalent to a pair
(a_{\tau},b_{\tau})
as introduced in Lemma
3.3
, and this pair defines in turn a family of two-forms
\psi_{\tau}
on
\Sigma
as described above.

Definition 3.6
.

The family of two-forms
\psi_{\tau}
is the
derived
family of two-forms of the curving
\bar{b}
on the reducible bundle gerbe
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{A},\bar{Y})
.

The fact that
H_{\bar{b}}\in\Omega^{3}(\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma)
is closed translates into:

\partial_{\tau}H_{b_{\tau}}=\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\psi_{\tau}\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}H_{b_{\tau}}=0

Since
b_{\tau}
is a family of curvings on
(\mathcal{P},A,Y)
, the second condition holds automatically. In particular, we have:

H_{b_{\tau}}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}c_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\omega

for a time-independent integral closed three-form
\omega/2\pi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
.

3.2.
The Cauchy evolution flow

In this subsection, we characterize the gradient generalized Ricci solitons on a reducible bundle gerbe
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{A},\bar{Y})
on
\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
in terms of solutions to a prescribed system of flow equations on the reduction
(\mathcal{P},Y,A_{\tau},\Psi_{\tau})
of
(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{A},\bar{Y})
to
\Sigma
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Elements
(g,\bar{b},\bar{\phi})\in\mathrm{Conf}(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{A},\bar{Y})
are in one-to-one correspondence with tuples
(h_{\tau},a_{\tau},b_{\tau},\phi_{\tau})
as introduced above.

We will refer
(h_{\tau},a_{\tau},b_{\tau},\phi_{\tau})
as the
reduction
of
(g,\bar{b},\bar{\phi})
. We set:

\Sigma_{\tau}:=\left\{\tau\right\}\times\Sigma\hookrightarrow M=\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma\,,\qquad\Sigma:=\left\{0\right\}\times\Sigma\hookrightarrow M

Tuples of the form
(h_{\tau},a_{\tau},b_{\tau},\phi_{\tau})
constitute the variables of the system of flow equations defined by gradient generalized Ricci solitons on
(\mathcal{P},Y,A_{\tau},\Psi_{\tau})
, that we present in Proposition
3.13
. We denote by
\nabla^{h_{\tau}}
the Levi-Civita connection on
(\Sigma_{\tau},h_{\tau})
. A direct computation gives the following formulae.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
g=\mathrm{d}\tau\otimes\mathrm{d}\tau+h_{\tau}
be a Riemannian metric on
M=\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
. Then, for every family of one-forms
\beta_{\tau}\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)
we have:

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}\Sigma_{t}\odot T^{\ast}\Sigma_{t})\,,\quad\nabla^{g}\beta_{\tau}=\mathrm{d}\tau\otimes\partial_{\tau}\beta_{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\odot(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau})(\beta^{\sharp_{h_{\tau}}}_{\tau})+\nabla^{h_{\tau}}\beta_{\tau}

where the superscript
\sharp_{h_{\tau}}
denotes the musical isomorphism with respect to
h_{\tau}
.

Remark 3.9
.

Note that
-2^{-1}\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}
is the shape operator or scalar second fundamental form of the embedded manifold
\Sigma_{\tau}\hookrightarrow M=\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
.

Lemma 3.10
.

A tuple
(g,\bar{b},\bar{\phi})\in\mathrm{Conf}(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{A},\bar{Y})
satisfies the Einstein equation (
32
) if and only if its reduction
(h_{\tau},a_{\tau},b_{\tau},\phi_{\tau})
satisfies the following differential system:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\tau}=2\mathrm{Ric}^{h_{\tau}}+\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}\circ_{h_{\tau}}\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau})\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}-\frac{2}{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi_{\tau}\otimes\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi_{\tau}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\,n-1\,}(e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}(|\psi_{\tau}|_{h_{\tau}}^{2}+|H_{b_{\tau}}|_{g}^{2})+\lambda\,e^{\frac{2\phi}{n-1}})\,h_{\tau}-e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}\,(\psi_{\tau}\circ_{h_{\tau}}\psi_{\tau}+H_{b_{\tau}}\circ_{h_{\tau}}H_{b_{\tau}})

\displaystyle\delta^{h_{\tau}}\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}+\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau})+\frac{2}{n-1}\,\partial_{\tau}\phi_{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi_{\tau}+e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}\,\psi_{\tau}\lrcorner_{h_{\tau}}H_{b_{\tau}}=0

\displaystyle s^{h_{\tau}}+\frac{1}{4}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}|_{h_{\tau}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau})^{2}+\lambda\,e^{\frac{2\phi}{n-1}}+\frac{1}{n-1}((\partial_{\tau}\phi_{\tau})^{2}-|\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi_{\tau}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}})+\frac{1}{2}(|\psi_{\tau}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}}-|H_{b_{\tau}}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}})e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}=0

where
\psi_{\tau}
is the derived family of two-forms associated to
\bar{b}\in\Omega^{2}(\bar{Y})
.

Proof.

Let
(g,\bar{b},\bar{\phi})
be a reducible gradient generalized Ricci soliton with reduction
(h_{\tau},a_{\tau},b_{\tau},\phi_{\tau})
. A standard computation shows that the Ricci curvature tensor of
g=\mathrm{d}\tau\otimes\mathrm{d}\tau+h_{\tau}
on
\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
decomposes as follows:

(37)

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{Ric}^{g}=(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}\circ_{h_{\tau}}\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau})-\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\tau}))\,\mathrm{d}\tau\otimes\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle-\mathrm{d}\tau\odot(\delta^{h_{\tau}}\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}+\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}))+2\mathrm{Ric}^{h_{\tau}}+\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}\circ_{h_{\tau}}\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau})\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}-\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\tau}

(38)

s^{g}=s^{h_{\tau}}+\frac{3}{4}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}|_{h_{\tau}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau})^{2}-\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\tau})

Furthermore:

\displaystyle s^{g}-2\mathrm{Ric}^{g}(\partial_{\tau},\partial_{\tau})=s^{h_{\tau}}+\frac{1}{4}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}|_{h_{\tau}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\text{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}{h}_{\tau})^{2}

We decompose the Einstein equation (

) on
\mathcal{I}\times\Sigma
accordingly, that is:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{g}(\partial_{\tau},\partial_{\tau})+\frac{1}{\,n-1\,}(e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}|H_{\bar{b}}|_{g_{E}}^{2}+\lambda\,e^{\frac{2\phi}{n-1}}-(\partial_{\tau}\phi)^{2})-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}\langle H_{\bar{b}}(\partial_{\tau}),H_{\bar{b}}(\partial_{\tau})\rangle_{g}=

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{h_{\tau}}\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}+\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}))-\frac{1}{n-1}\,\partial_{\tau}\phi_{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi_{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\tfrac{4\phi_{\tau}}{n-1}}\,\psi_{\tau}\lrcorner_{h_{\tau}}H_{b_{\tau}}=0

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{g}|_{T\Sigma_{\tau}\times T\Sigma_{\tau}}-\frac{1}{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi\otimes\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi+\frac{1}{\,n-1\,}(e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}|H_{\bar{b}}|_{g}^{2}+\lambda\,e^{\frac{2\phi}{n-1}})\,h_{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\tfrac{4\phi}{n-1}}\,(H_{\bar{b}}\circ_{g}H_{\bar{b}})|_{T\Sigma_{\tau}\times T\Sigma_{\tau}}=

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ric}^{h_{\tau}}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}\circ_{h_{\tau}}\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau})\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\tau}-\frac{1}{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi_{\tau}\otimes\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi_{\tau}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\,n-1\,}(e^{-\tfrac{4\phi_{\tau}}{n-1}}(|\psi_{\tau}|_{h_{\tau}}^{2}+|H_{b_{\tau}}|_{g}^{2})+\lambda\,e^{\frac{2\phi_{\tau}}{n-1}})\,h_{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\tfrac{4\phi_{\tau}}{n-1}}\,(\psi_{\tau}\circ_{h_{\tau}}\psi_{\tau}+H_{b_{\tau}}\circ_{h_{\tau}}H_{b_{\tau}})=0

where we have repeatedly used Equation (
36
). Taking the trace of the
T\Sigma_{\tau}\times T\Sigma_{\tau}
component of the Einstein equation (

) we obtain:

\displaystyle s^{h_{\tau}}+\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}|_{h_{\tau}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\tau})-\frac{1}{\,n-1\,}|\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi_{\tau}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\,n-1\,}(|\psi_{\tau}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}}e^{-\tfrac{4\phi_{\tau}}{n-1}}+\frac{3-n}{2}|H_{b_{\tau}}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}}e^{-\tfrac{4\phi_{\tau}}{n-1}}+n\lambda\,e^{\frac{2\phi_{\tau}}{n-1}})=0

Combining this equation with the

component of the Einstein equation (

) we obtain:

s^{h_{\tau}}+\frac{1}{4}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}|_{h_{\tau}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau})^{2}+\lambda\,e^{\frac{2\phi_{\tau}}{n-1}}+\frac{(\partial_{\tau}\phi_{\tau})^{2}-|\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi_{\tau}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}}}{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}(|\psi_{\tau}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}}-|H_{b_{\tau}}|^{2}_{h_{\tau}})e^{-\tfrac{4\phi_{\tau}}{n-1}}=0

and thus we conclude.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

A tuple
(g,\bar{b},\bar{\phi})\in\mathrm{Conf}(\bar{\mathcal{P}},\bar{A},\bar{Y})
satisfies the Maxwell equation in (
33
) if and only if its reduction
(h_{\tau},a_{\tau},b_{\tau},\phi_{\tau})
satisfies the following differential system:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\tau}=\delta^{h_{\tau}}H_{b_{\tau}}+\frac{4}{n-1}H_{b_{\tau}}(\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi^{\sharp_{h_{\tau}}})+\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}\Delta_{1}\psi_{\tau}+(\frac{4}{n-1}\partial_{\tau}\phi_{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{h_{\tau}}(\partial_{\tau}h_{\tau}))\,\psi_{\tau}

\displaystyle\delta^{h_{\tau}}\psi_{\tau}+\frac{4}{n-1}\psi_{\tau}(\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi^{\sharp_{h_{\tau}}})=0

where
\psi_{\tau}
is the derived family of two-forms associated to
\bar{b}\in\Omega^{2}(\bar{Y})
.

Proof.

Let
(g,\bar{b},\bar{\phi})
be a reducible gradient generalized Ricci soliton with reduction
(h_{\tau},a_{\tau},b_{\tau},\phi_{\tau})
satisfying the Maxwell equation in (
33
). Using Equation (
36
) together with Lemma
3.8
, we compute:

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{\tau}}H_{\bar{b}}=\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{\tau}}(\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\psi_{\tau}+H_{b_{\tau}})=\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{\tau}}\psi_{\tau}+\nabla^{g}_{\partial_{\tau}}H_{b_{\tau}}

\displaystyle-\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge(\delta^{h_{\tau}}\psi_{\tau}+\frac{4}{n-1}\psi_{\tau}(\mathrm{d}_{\Sigma}\phi^{\sharp_{h_{\tau}}}))

and we conclude.

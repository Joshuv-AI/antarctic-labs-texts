Spectrum of invariant measures via generic points
Sejal Babel, Melih Emin Can, Dominik Kwietniak, Piotr Oprocha

Abstract
. We describe the spectrum of an ergodic invariant measure by examining the behaviour of its generic points. We define regular Wiener–Wintner generic points for a measure to generalise the characterisation of generic points for discrete spectrum measure from Lenz et al. [Ergodic Theory and Dynamical Systems vol. 44 (2024), no. 2, 524–568]. We also study limits of sequences of generic points with respect to the Besicovitch pseudometric. This translates to results about limits of measures with respect to the metric rho-bar ρ ¯ \bar{\rho} generalising Ornstein’s d-bar metric. We study how the spectrum behaves when passing to the limit and we prove that points generic for discrete spectrum, totally ergodic, or (weakly) mixing measures, property K, zero entropy measures form a closed set with respect to the Besicovitch pseudometric. Hence, the same holds for corresponding measures with respect to the rho-bar metric. Our methods have already been used to prove existence of ergodic measures with desired properties, in particular with discrete spectrum. They also lead to a new proof of rational discrete spectrum of the Mirsky measure associated with a given set of ℬ \mathscr{B} -free numbers.

1.
Introduction

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous map on a compact metric space. The pair
(X,T)
is referred to as a
topological dynamical system
. We call
x\in X
a
generic point
for
\mu
if for every
f\in C(X)
we have

(1.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^{j}(x))=\int_{X}f\text{ d}\mu.

We consider the following problem: how to determine the properties of
\mu
given a
\mu
-generic point
x
? We would like to deduce properties of
\mu
by looking only at the statistical behaviour of the orbit
x
. Note that every ergodic
T
-invariant measure always has a generic point, but non-ergodic measures need not to have them. Nevertheless, in some topological dynamical systems every invariant measure has generic points, see
[
1
,
14
,
16
,
24
,
33
,
49
]
. In
[
42
]
, Oxtoby gave a characterisation of generic points of ergodic measures. Many authors explored similar criteria for ergodicity, see
[
25
,
9
,
7
,
54
]
. In particular, the wonderful book by Weiss
[
54
]
devoted a lot of space to problems of this type.

Here, we address two variants of our motivating question.
First, we would like to check if the spectrum of a measure could be recovered from behaviour of a generic point. We are inspired by results obtained by Lenz and his coauthors
[
37
,
38
]
. We note that
[
37
,
38
]
use a more general setting of ergodic topological dynamical systems given by actions of locally
compact,
\sigma
-compact abelian groups on metrizable compact spaces. On the other hand,
[
37
,
38
]
concentrate on systems with discrete (pure-point) spectrum. In particular,
[
38
]
contains a characterisation of ergodic systems with discrete spectrum via the existence of Besicovitch almost periodic points. Inspired by these results, and using the Wiener–Wintner ergodic theorem
[
3
]
, we define regular Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic points. Roughly speaking, a point
x
is Wiener–Wintner generic for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
if (
1.1
) holds and the Wiener–Wintner theorem is satisfied along the orbit of
x
for every continuous
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
. The latter means that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
the following limit exists

\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\xi^{-j}f(T^{j}(x)).

Regularity of a Wiener–Wintner generic point
x
means that

\norm{P_{\Kron}(f)}^{2}_{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\lvert\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)\rvert^{2},

where
\norm{P_{\Kron}(f)}^{2}_{2}
is the length of the projection of
f
onto the Kronecker factor of
\mu
.
We prove that a regular Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic point captures the spectral properties of the measure it generates. We note that regular Wiener–Wintner generic points for ergodic
\mu
with discrete spectrum are Besicovitch almost periodic points studied in
[
38
]
.

Second, we would like to determine which properties of generic points are closed with respect to
Besicovitch pseudometric

D_{B}
on
(X,T)
which is given for
x,x^{\prime}\in X
by

(1.2)

D_{B}(x,x^{\prime})=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\rho(T^{n}(x),T^{n}(x^{\prime})),

where
\rho
is a metric on
X
.
The idea of measuring the average distance along two orbits using
D_{B}
is so natural that it is hard to point who was the first to use it. The pseudometric
D_{B}
bears the name of Besicovitch, because he utilised a similar pseudo-distance studying almost periodic functions, though the terminology is not universally adopted.
Besicovitch and related pseudometrics proved to be useful in dynamics multiple times, see
[
4
,
8
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
28
,
29
,
11
,
33
,
40
]
.

Here, we connect
D_{B}
with a metric
\bar{\rho}
on the space of all
T
-invariant measures. The metric
\bar{\rho}
generalises Ornstein’s d-bar metric for finite-valued ergodic discrete-time stochastic processes. The aforementioned processes appear naturally in ergodic theory from
studying measure-preserving transformations through their action on finite measurable partitions of the underlying measure space. In that context, finiteness of the partition becomes discreteness of the state space in which the variables of the process take their values. In topological dynamics it seems desirable to have tools applicable directly to homeomorphisms on general state spaces, rather than the pair consisting of the transformation and a fixed partition.

We study how the spectrum behaves when we take
\bar{\rho}
-limit of a sequence of measures. We show that the spectrum of the
\bar{\rho}
-limit measure is contained in the set of
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
that belong to the spectra of all except possibly finitely many measures in the
\bar{\rho}
-convergent sequence. The result combines application of the theory introduced earlier with the results we provide for
\bar{\rho}
and its connection with the Besicovitch pseudo-distance.
In addition, we prove that points generic for ergodic measures, discrete spectrum measures, (weakly) mixing measures, zero entropy measures, measures with property K, form a
D_{B}
-closed sets and translate these results to limits of measures with respect to
\bar{\rho}
. For some of these properties, namely discrete spectrum and zero-entropy systems, our results are a special case of the
D_{B}
-closedness of generic points for measures belonging to one of characteristic classes (see below and
[
27
]
) that was recently established by the authors of
[
6
]
. While some of these aforementioned results were known in one form or another, they were primarily studied in the context of shift-invariant measures on symbolic spaces and Ornstein’s d-bar metric. Other results seem to be new even for shift-invariant measures. Furthermore, we offer a uniform approach and extend these results beyond the setting of shift spaces. Our proofs for
\bar{\rho}
convergence do not depend on their shift-invariant d-bar predecessors (in cases, when such results are available). Our methods also lead to a new proof of rational discrete spectrum of the Mirsky measure associated with a given set of
\mathscr{B}
-free numbers, which highlight utility of this new approach. We discuss some other applications in the last section.

2.
Preliminaries

For the reminder of this text we assume that
(X,T)
is an
invertible
topological dynamical system, that is,
X
is a compact metric space and
T\colon X\to X
is a homeomorphism. All results presented here remain true if
T
is just a continuous map from
X
to itself. For a proof one needs to pass to the natural extension and use the results presented here in conjunction with the well-known properties of the natural extension. The details are left to an interested reader.

Let
\rho
be a compatible metric on
X
. Denote by
C(X)
the separable Banach space of continuous maps
f\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
endowed with the norm
\norm{f}_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(X)
be the space of all Borel probability measures on
X
endowed with the weak
∗
topology. We say that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
is
T
-invariant
, if
\mu(T^{-1}(B))=\mu(B)
for every Borel set
B\subseteq X
. We write
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
for the family of all
T
-invariant measures. A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
is
ergodic
if for every Borel set
B\subseteq X
with
T^{-1}(B)=B
either
\mu(B)=0
or
\mu(B)=1
. The set of all ergodic measures is denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)
. The set
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
is always nonempty, compact, and convex subset of
\mathcal{M}(X)
and
\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)
is the set of extreme points of
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
.

For
x\in X
we write
\delta(x)\in\mathcal{M}(X)
for the Dirac measure supported on
\{x\}
.
Let
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive integers with
n_{k}\nearrow\infty
.
We say that
x\in X
is
quasi-generic for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
along
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
if for every
f\in C(X)
we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}f(T^{j}(x))=\int f\text{ d}\mu.

We say that
x\in X
is a
generic point
for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
if
x
is quasi-generic for
\mu
along
every strictly increasing sequence
(n_{k})
. We write
\Gen(\mu)
for the set of
\mu
generic points.
A measure
\mu
is ergodic if and only if
\mu(\Gen(\mu))=1
(see
[
42
]
).

Fixing a
T
-invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
we obtain an invertible
measure-preserving system denoted by
(X,T,\mu)
. We omit the
\sigma
-algebra from our notation, as in our considerations the
\sigma
-algebra is always the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
X
or its completion. We write
L^{1}(X,\mu)
(respectively,
L^{2}(X,\mu)
) for the corresponding Banach (respectively, Hilbert) space of equivalence classes of integrable (respectively, square-integrable) complex-valued functions on
X
. Let
U_{T}
be the associated
Koopman operator
, that is
U_{T}\colon L^{2}(X,\mu)\to L^{2}(X,\mu)
is the unitary operator given by
U_{T}(f)=f\circ T
.

The
eigenvalues
and
eigenfunctions
of
(X,T,\mu)
are eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the associated Koopman operator. For the basic properties of eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
U_{T}
we refer the reader to
[
53
, pp. 68–69]
.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we slightly abuse the terminology and call
\ker(U_{T}-\xi\text{Id})
the eigenspace of
\xi
. We denote by
P_{\xi}
the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(X,\mu)
onto the eigenspace of
\xi
. Clearly,
\ker(U_{T}-\xi\text{Id})\neq\{0\}
and
P_{\xi}\neq 0
if and only if
\xi
is an eigenvalue of
(X,T,\mu)
.
We denote by
\mathcal{K}
the closed linear subspace of
L^{2}(X,\mu)
spanned by the eigenfunctions of
(X,T,\mu)
and by

P_{\mathcal{K}}\colon L^{2}(X,\mu)\to\mathcal{K}
the associated orthogonal projection. When we refer to
\mathcal{K}
, the context always makes it clear which
L^{2}
space is being considered.

We say that
(X,T,\mu)
has
discrete
(or
pure-point
)
spectrum
, if the linear span of eigenfunctions of
U_{T}
is dense in
L^{2}(X,\mu)
.

3.
Wiener–Wintner generic points

Let
f\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and
n\geq 1
. Given
x\in X
we define

(3.1)

\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\xi^{-j}f(T^{j}(x)).

Whenever we write
\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
we mean

(3.2)

\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x),

in particular, writing
\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
we always assume that the sequence
(\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x))_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges.
The celebrated Wiener–Wintner ergodic theorem
[
3
]
states that given a measure-preserving system
(X,T,\mu)
and
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
then for
\mu
-a.e.
x
the limit
\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)

exists for all
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, that is, there exists a Borel set
X_{f}
with
\mu(X_{f})=1
such that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
x\in X_{f}

the sequence
\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
converges as
n\to\infty
.

Inspired by the concept of generic points, we define Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic points.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
x\in X
is a
Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic point
if

x
is a generic point for
\mu
and for every
f\in C(X)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
the sequence
(\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x))_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges as
n\to\infty
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that the existence of the limit in (
3.2
) is not enough to associate a unique measure to a point
x
.
Every point such that the sequence
(\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x))_{n=1}^{\infty}
defined by (
3.1
)
converges for every
f\in C(X)
and
\xi=1
is a generic point for some
T
-invariant measure. Indeed, let
x\in X
be such that
\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
exists for every
f\in C(X)
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Consider the map
C(X)\ni f\mapsto\Av[f,\bar{1}](x)\in\mathbb{C}
. It is a continuous positive linear functional on
C(X)
, so by the Riesz representation theorem there exists a Borel probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
such that for every
f\in C(X)
we have

(3.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^{j}(x))=\int f\text{ d}\mu.

Observe that (
3.3
) implies
\int f\text{ d}\mu=\int f\circ T\text{ d}\mu
for every
f\in C(X)
, which means that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
.

The lemmas below help us to decide whether a point is Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic. We omit their straightforward proofs. Recall that
\clspn(\mathcal{D})
stands for the closure of the linear span of
\mathcal{D}\subseteq C(X)
in the
\sup
-norm
\norm{\cdot}_{\infty}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x\in X
and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. If
(n(k))_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is strictly increasing and
\mathcal{D}\subseteq C(X)
are such that for every
f\in\mathcal{D}
the sequence
(\Av_{n(k)}[f,\bar{\xi}](x))_{k=1}^{\infty}
has a limit
\Phi_{\xi}(f)
, then the map
\clspn(\mathcal{D})\ni f\mapsto\Phi_{\xi}(f)\in\mathbb{C}
is well-defined, continuous, and linear.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a countable set with
\clspn(\mathcal{D})=C(X)
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
. A point
x\in X
is a Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic if and only if for every
f\in\mathcal{D}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
the sequence (
3.1
) converges as
n\to\infty
and (
3.3
) holds.

Using Lemma
3.4
and the Wiener–Wintner theorem we will easily see that for every ergodic
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
we have that
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
is a Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic point.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
(X,T)
is a topological dynamical system and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)
, then there exists a Borel set
X_{\mu}
with
\mu(X_{\mu})=1
such that every

x\in X_{\mu}
is a Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic point.

Proof.

By ergodicity, there exists a Borel set
X_{1}
consisting of
\mu
generic points with
\mu(X_{1})=1
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be a countable dense subset of
C(X)
. For every
f\in\mathcal{D}
we use Wiener–Wintner theorem to find a Borel set
X_{f}
with
\mu(X_{f})=1
such that for every

x\in X_{f}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
the limit (
3.1
) exists. Therefore, for every

x\in X_{\mu}=X_{1}\cap\bigcap_{f\in D}X_{f}

we have that (
3.3
) holds for every
f\in\mathcal{D}
and the limit (
3.1
) exists for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
f\in\mathcal{D}
. By Lemma
3.4
the same holds for every
f\in C(X)
. ∎

Remark 3.6
.

It is easy to see that for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
the set of accumulation points of sequences

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^{j}(x))\xi^{-j}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n+k-1}f(T^{j}(x))\xi^{-j}

coincide. It follows that one of these sequences converges if and only if the other one converges. In that case, the limits coincide.

The following lemma demonstrates that generic points can detect eigenvalues of the Koopman operator.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
,
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
x\in X
be a
\mu
-generic point. Then either
\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
exists and equals
0
for every
f\in C(X)
or
\xi
is an eigenvalue of the Koopman operator
U_{T}
on
L^{2}(X,\mu)
and there exists
e_{\xi}\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
that is a corresponding eigenfunction satisfying
\norm{e_\xi}_{2}\leq 1
and

(3.4)

\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)=\langle f,e_{\xi}\rangle=\int f\bar{e}_{\xi}\text{ d}\mu.

provided
f\in C(X)
is such that
\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
exists.

Proof.

Fix
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Let
x\in X
be a
\mu
-generic point such that for some
f_{0}\in C(X)
we have that either the limit
\Av[f_{0},\bar{\xi}](x)
exists but it is not equal to
0
or the limit
\Av[f_{0},\bar{\xi}](x)
does not exists. In any case, the sequence
(\Av_{n}[f_{0},\bar{\xi}](x))^{\infty}_{n=1}
has a subsequence converging to some
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Take
\mathcal{D}
to be a countable dense subset of
C(X)
containing
f_{0}
. Let
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be the sequence of indices of that subsequence. Using a standard diagonal argument and passing to further subsequences of
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
if necessary, we may assume that for every
f\in\mathcal{D}
the sequence

\left(\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}f(T^{j}(x))\xi^{-j}\right)_{k=1}^{\infty}

converges as
k\to\infty
. For every
f\in\mathcal{D}
we define

\Phi_{\xi}(f):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}f(T^{j}(x))\xi^{-j}\in\mathbb{C}.

We use Lemma
3.3

to extend it to
\Phi_{\xi}\colon C(X)\to\mathbb{C}
.
In this way, we get a continuous linear transformation defined on
C(X)\subseteq L^{2}(X,\mu)
.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, for every
n\geq 1
and
f\in C(X)
we have

\left\lvert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^{j}(x))\xi^{-j}\right\rvert^{2}\leq\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\lvert f(T^{j}(x))\rvert^{2}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\lvert\xi^{-j}\rvert^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\lvert f(T^{j}(x))\rvert^{2}.

Passing with
n\to\infty
along
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
and using that
x
is
\mu
-generic, we obtain

\left|\Phi_{\xi}(f)\right|^{2}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}f(T^{j}(x))\xi^{-j}\right|^{2}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}|f(T^{j}(x))|^{2}=\int|f|^{2}\text{ d}\mu.

This means that for every
f\in C(X)
the linear map
\Phi_{\xi}
satisfies

(3.5)

|\Phi_{\xi}(f)|^{2}\leq||f||^{2}_{2},

so it is a continuous linear functional on
C(X)
endowed with the
L^{2}(X,\mu)
norm. Now, by the Hahn-Banach Theorem (e.g. see
[
39
, Thm. 7.4.1.]
)
we see that
\Phi_{\xi}
extends to a continuous linear functional on
L^{2}(X,\mu)
with the same norm, in particular
\norm{\Phi_\xi}\leq 1
. The density of
C(X)
in
L^{2}(X,\mu)
implies that the extension is unique and we keep denoting it by
\Phi_{\xi}
. By the Riesz representation theorem, there exists a unique
e_{\xi}\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
such that
\norm{e_\xi}_{2}=\norm{\Phi_\xi}\leq 1
and for every
f\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
we have

(3.6)

\Phi_{\xi}(f)=\langle f,e_{\xi}\rangle=\int f\bar{e}_{\xi}\text{ d}\mu.

Clearly,
e_{\xi}\neq 0
, because
\Phi_{\xi}(f_{0})=\langle f_{0},e_{\xi}\rangle=\gamma\neq 0
.
In order to show that
e_{\xi}
is an eigenfunction of
U_{T}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\xi
we need to show
e_{\xi}\circ T=\xi e_{\xi}
. Using continuity of the inner product and density of
C(X)
in
L^{2}(X,\mu)
it is enough to show that for every
f\in C(X)
we have

(3.7)

\langle f,U_{T}(e_{\xi})\rangle=\langle f,\xi e_{\xi}\rangle.

To this end, we fix
f\in C(X)
. Since
U_{T}
is unitary we have

(3.8)

\langle f,U_{T}(e_{\xi})\rangle=\langle U^{-1}_{T}(f),e_{\xi}\rangle=\int(f\circ T^{-1})\bar{e}_{\xi}\text{ d}\mu=\Phi_{\xi}(f\circ T^{-1}).

By the definition of
\Phi_{\xi}
we have

(3.9)

\Phi_{\xi}(f\circ T^{-1})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}f\circ T^{-1}(T^{j}(x))\xi^{-j}.

Clearly, for every
n\geq 1
it holds

(3.10)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(f\circ T^{-1}(T^{j}(x))\right)\xi^{-j}=\xi^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=-1}^{n-2}\left(f\circ T^{j}(x)\right)\xi^{-j}.

Using Remark
3.6
and noting that
\xi^{-1}=\bar{\xi}
we obtain

(3.11)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\xi^{-1}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=-1}^{n_{k}-2}\left(f\circ T^{j}(x)\right)\xi^{-j}=\bar{\xi}\Phi_{\xi}(f).

Combining
(
3.8
), (
3.9
), (
3.10
), and (
3.11
) we get

\langle f,U_{T}(e_{\xi})\rangle=\Phi_{\xi}(f\circ T^{-1})=\bar{\xi}\Phi_{\xi}(f)=\bar{\xi}\langle f,e_{\xi}\rangle=\langle f,\xi e_{\xi}\rangle.

Hence, (
3.7
) is true, so
\xi\in\Spec(X,T,\mu)
. If
A[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
exists, then we have
\Phi_{\xi}(f)=A[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
, so (
3.4
) holds. ∎

Remark 3.8
.

An alternative approach to the proof of Lemma
3.7
was suggested to us by Mariusz Lemańczyk. It is based on
intertwining Markov operators
associated with joinings of measure-preserving systems. Recall that a
joining
of
(X,T,\mu)
and
(Y,S,\nu)
is a
T\times S
-invariant measure on
X\times Y
whose projections, to the first, respectively, second coordinate is
\mu
, respectively,
\nu
. These operators were introduced by Vershik and since then had been used extensively, see, e.g.
[
36
,
35
,
44
]
. For more details, we refer to
[
44
]
or
[
36
, Section 1.3]
.

Fix
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and define a bounded arithmetic function
\mathbf{u}\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
by
\mathbf{u}(n)=\xi^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Consider
\mathbf{u}
as an element of the product space
(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\mathbb{Z}}
. The
Furstenberg system

X_{\xi}
associated with
\mathbf{u}
is the closure of the orbit of
\mathbf{u}
under the shift action on
(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\mathbb{Z}}
. In other words,
X_{\xi}:=\overline{\{S^{k}(\mathbf{u}):k\in\mathbb{Z}\}}
, where
S\colon(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\mathbb{Z}}\to(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the left-shift operator acting on
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
by
S((x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}})=(x_{k+1})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
. The topological dynamical system
(X_{\xi},S)
is topologically conjugated to the closure of the orbit of the point
1\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
under the action of the rotation of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
by
\xi
. In particular,
(X_{\xi},S)
admits an unique ergodic
S
-invariant measure denoted by
\lambda_{\xi}
.

Let
x\in X
be a generic point for
\mu
. Consider the point
(x,\mathbf{u})
in the product topological dynamical system
(X\times(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\mathbb{Z}},T\times S)
. There is a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that there is a measure
\rho_{\xi}
defined by

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}\delta_{(S^{j}(\mathbf{u}),T^{j}(x))}=\rho_{\xi}.

It is easy to see that
\rho_{\xi}
is a joining of
\lambda_{\xi}
and
\mu
. Consequently, there exists an associated Markov operator
\varphi_{\rho}\colon L^{2}(X_{\xi},\lambda_{\xi})\to L^{2}(X,\mu)

defined for
g\in L^{2}(X_{\xi},\lambda_{\xi})
and
f\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
by

\langle\varphi_{\rho}(g),f\rangle=\int_{X_{\xi}\times X}g(v)\overline{f(y)}\text{ d}\rho_{\xi}(v,y).

The Markov operator is intertwining, that is

(3.12)

\varphi_{\rho}\circ U_{S}=U_{T}\circ\varphi_{\rho}.

The projection onto the zeroth coordinate,
\pi_{0}\colon(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
, is an eigenfunction of
(X_{\xi},S,\lambda_{\xi})
(corresponding to the eigenvalue
\xi
). It follows immediately from the intertwining relationship (
3.12
) that
\varphi_{\rho}(\pi_{0})
, if non-zero, is an eigenfunction of
(X,T,\mu)
also corresponding to the eigenvalue
\xi
.
Hence for every
f\in C(X)
and
x
as above we have

\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}f(T^{j}(x))\xi^{-j}=\\
=\int_{X_{\xi}\times X}f(y)\overline{\pi_{0}(v)}\text{ d}\rho_{\xi}(v,y)=\int_{X}f\overline{\varphi_{\rho}(\pi_{0})}d\mu.

Therefore, the function
e_{\xi}
in Lemma
3.7
is precisely the image of the eigenfunction of
(X_{\xi},S,\lambda_{\xi})
through the Markov operator associated with the joining
\rho_{\xi}
.

Assumptions of
Lemma
3.7
suggests the definition of frequency presented below. We are inspired by
D. Lenz, T. Spindeler, and N. Strungaru
[
38
]
, who considered similarly defined notion of frequency. For
G=\mathbb{Z}
every frequency in the sense of
[

, Definition 3.4]
is also a frequency in the sense of Definition
3.9
.

Definition 3.9
.

We say that
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}

is a

of
x\in X
if there is
f\in C(X)
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x)\right|=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^{j}(x))\xi^{-j}\right|>0.

We denote the set of all frequencies of
x
by
\Freq(x)
.

Corollary 3.10
.

If
x\in X
is a
\mu
-generic point, then

\Freq(x)\subseteq\Spec(X,T,\mu)
. Furthermore, if
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus\Spec(X,T,\mu)
and
f\in C(X)
, then
\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
exists and
\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)=0
.

Remark 3.11
.

Note that the topological Wiener–Wintner theorem due to Walters
[
52
, Corollary 2]
(see also
[
54
, Theorem 3.5]
) implies that if
\mu
is ergodic, then for
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

for every
\mu
-generic point
x\in X
and every
f\in C(X)
we have that
\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
exists and
\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)=0
;

(2)

\xi\notin\Spec(X,T,\mu)
.

Therefore, Corollary
3.10
strengthens the implication (
2
)
\implies
(
1
) by removing the assumption that
\mu
is ergodic. It follows that if
\mu
is weakly mixing then every
\mu
-generic point is Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic.

The next result is an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.7
and Remark
3.11
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
and
x\in X
be a
\mu
-generic point. Then

is a Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic point if and only if for every
f\in C(X)
and
\xi\in\Spec(X,T,\mu)
the sequence
(\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x))_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to

we have

(3.13)

\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\lvert\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)\rvert^{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\Spec(X,T,\mu)}\lvert\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)\rvert^{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\Freq(x)}\lvert\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)\rvert^{2}.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
, and
x\in X
be generic for
\mu
. For every
\xi\in\Freq(x)
let us assume that
A[f,\bar{\xi}](x)
exists for every
f\in C(X)
and take

to be the function provided by Lemma
3.7
so that (
3.4
) holds. If

, then

(3.14)

\lVert P_{\mathcal{K}}(f)\rVert^{2}_{2}\geq\sum_{\xi\in\Freq(x)}|\langle f,e_{\xi}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\Freq(x)}\lvert\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)\rvert^{2}.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma

that the vectors

are orthonormal and can be extended to an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{K}
. Furthermore, for every
\xi\in\Freq(x)
it holds

(3.15)

|\langle f,e_{\xi}\rangle|^{2}=\norm{e_\xi}^{2}\left\lvert\langle f,\frac{e_{\xi}}{\norm{e_\xi}}\rangle\right\rvert^{2}\leq|\langle f,\frac{e_{\xi}}{\norm{e_\xi}}\rangle|^{2}.

Therefore

\sum_{\xi\in\Freq(x)}\lvert\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)\rvert^{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\Freq(x)}|\langle f,e_{\xi}\rangle|^{2}\leq\sum_{\xi\in\Freq(x)}|\langle f,\frac{e_{\xi}}{\norm{e_\xi}}\rangle|^{2}\leq\lVert P_{\mathcal{K}}(f)\rVert^{2}_{2}.\qed

4.
Frequencies of Wiener–Wintner Generic Points and Spectra of Measures

In this section we will introduce and characterise regular Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic points. Then we will connect regularity of Wiener–Wintner generic points with ergodicity and spectrum of the associated measure. Corollary
3.13
suggests that we distinguish those Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic points for which (

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
.
A Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic point
x\in X
is

if for every
f\in C(X)
it holds

(4.1)

\norm{P_{\Kron}(f)}^{2}_{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\Freq(x)}\lvert\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)\rvert^{2}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
and
x\in X
be a
\mu
-generic point. If the set

E=\{e_{\xi}:\xi\in\Freq(x)\},

where
e_{\xi}\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
is the eigenfunction of
U_{T}
provided by Lemma
3.7
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{K}
, then
\mu
is ergodic and
\Freq(x)=\Spec(X,T,\mu)
.

Proof.

By Remark
3.11
, we have
\Freq(x)\subseteq\Spec(X,T,\mu)
. Now, if there were
\hat{\xi}\in\Spec(X,T,\mu)\setminus\Freq(x)
, then the corresponding eigenfunction
\hat{e}
would be orthogonal to the space spanned by
\{e_{\xi}\colon\xi\in\Freq(x)\}=\mathcal{K}
, which is impossible since
\hat{e}\in\mathcal{K}
. Furthermore, for each
\xi\in\Spec(X,T,\mu)=\Freq(x)
the eigenspace
\ker(U_{T}-\xi\text{Id})
is one-dimensional. In particular, the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
1
is one-dimensional, which means that all
T
-invariant functions are
\mu
-a.e. constant. This implies ergodicity.
∎

Lemma

provides us some useful characterisations of regularity of a Wiener–Wintner

(

(

-generic.

For uniquely ergodic topological dynamical systems with continuous eigenfunctions all points turn out to be regular Wiener–Wintner generic for the unique invariant measure of the system.

Theorem 4.7
.

If
(X,T)
is a uniquely ergodic topological dynamical system with continuous eigenfunctions, then every
x\in X
is a regular Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic point, where
\mu
stands for the unique
T
-invariant measure.

Proof.

Fix
f\in C(X)
. Recall that
P_{\xi}(f)
denotes the projection on the eigenspace of
\xi
when
\xi\in\Spec(X,T,\mu)
and
P_{\xi}(f)=0
otherwise.

By the uniform version of Wiener–Wintner theorem
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
,
we obtain that for every
x\in X
, for every

, and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
we have

\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x)=P_{\xi}(f)(x).

Furthermore,
P_{\xi}(f)
is a continuous function. Hence, every
x\in X
is Wiener–Wintner
\mu
-generic, so
\Freq(x)=\Spec(X,T,\mu)
by Lemma

.
For every

and

we have

\lvert P_{\xi}(f)(T^{k}(x))\rvert^{2}=\lvert\xi^{-k}P_{\xi}(f)(x)\rvert^{2}=\lvert P_{\xi}(f)(x)\rvert^{2},

so

is a continuous

-invariant function. By unique ergodicity, we see that for every

\lvert P_{\xi}(f)(x)\rvert^{2}=\int\lvert P_{\xi}(f)(y)\rvert^{2}\text{ d}\mu(y)=\norm{P_\xi(f)}_{2}^{2}.

Therefore

\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x)|^{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\norm{P_\xi(f)}^{2}_{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\Spec(X,T,\mu)}\norm{P_\xi(f)}_{2}^{2}=\norm{P_\Kron(f)}_{2}^{2}.\qed

in (

6.
Besicovitch pseudometric and its relatives

The aim of this section is to state a few technical results about Besicovitch pseudometric and related notions that we need later. Although several of these findings have been previously published, their original form does not meet our requirements. Hence, we reformulate them here.

Let
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers.
The
upper/lower asymptotic density

d^{*}_{(n_{k})}(Q)
/
d^{(n_{k})}_{*}(Q)
of
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
along
(n_{k})
is given by

(6.1)

d^{*}_{(n_{k})}(Q)=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\left|Q\cap[0,n_{k})\right|}{n_{k}}\text{ and }d^{(n_{k})}_{*}(Q)=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\left|Q\cap[0,n_{k})\right|}{n_{k}}.

By
d^{*}(S)/d_{*}(S)
we denote the upper/lower asymptotic density of
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
along the sequence
n_{k}=k
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The
Besicovitch pseudometric along
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
on
(X,T)
is given for
x,x^{\prime}\in X
by

(6.2)

D^{(n_{k})}_{B}(x,x^{\prime})=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}\rho(T^{j}(x),T^{j}(x^{\prime})).

Furthermore, we introduce a variant of the pseudometric used in
[
34
, Lemma 2]
, however here it is based on the upper density along
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
instead of
d^{*}
. Given a topological dynamical system
(X,T)
, a sequence
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
, and
x,y\in X
we set

(6.3)

\tilde{D}_{B}^{(n_{k})}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\delta>0\mid d^{*}_{(n_{k})}\left(\{j\in\mathbb{N}\mid\rho(T^{j}(x),T^{j}(y))\geq\delta\}\right)<\delta\right\}.

An obvious modification of the proof of
[
34
, Lemma 2]
yields the following useful result, which we state for the sake of completness.

Lemma 6.1
.

If
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is a strictly increasing sequence, then pseudometrics
D_{B}^{(n_{k})}
and
\tilde{D}_{B}^{(n_{k})}
are uniformly equivalent on
(X,T)
.

The proof of our next result follows the same lines as the proofs of
[
34
, Lemma 6]
and
[
34
, Theorem 15]
. The obvious changes must take into account that all the measures in Theorem
6.2
are generated along the subsequence
(n_{k})
, while the statements in
[
34
]
use
n_{k}=k
for every
k\geq 1
. More precisely, one needs to use Lemma
6.1
in place of
[
34
, Lemma 2]
and an appropriate modification of

[
34
, Theorem 14]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system and
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be strictly increasing. If for every
m\geq 1
the point
x_{m}\in X
is quasi-generic for
\mu_{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
along
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}

and
x\in X
is such that

\lim_{m\to\infty}D^{(n_{k})}_{B}(x,x_{m})=0,

then
x
is quasi-generic along
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
for some
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
, where
\mu_{m}
converges to
\mu
in the weak
∗
topology
as
m\to\infty
.
Furthermore, if
\mu_{m}
is ergodic for every
m\geq 1
, then so is
\mu
.

Consider a finite discrete space
A
(an alphabet). Let
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the topological space being a product of a bi-infinite sequence of copies of
A
. Let
\sigma\colon A^{\mathbb{Z}}\to A^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the shift transformation, where
\sigma(x)_{i}=x_{i+1}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
\bar{d}_{(n_{k})}
-pseudometric along a strictly increasing sequence
(n_{k})
on
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
measures the upper asymptotic density along
(n_{k})
of the set of nonnegative coordinates at which two
A
-valued biinfinite sequences differ, that is, for
x=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
y=(y_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
we set

\bar{d}_{(n_{k})}(x,y)=d^{*}_{(n_{k})}\{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:x_{j}\neq y_{j}\}=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}|\{0\leq j<n_{k}:x_{j}\neq y_{j}\}|.

Let
\rho
be any metric on
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
compatible with the product topology. It turns out that the Besicovitch pseudometric along
(n_{k})
generated by
\rho
on
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
is uniformly equivalent with the
\bar{d}_{(n_{k})}
-pseudometric. A proof follows the same lines as the proof of
[
34
, Theorem 4]
, which yields the same conclusion in case that
n_{k}=k
.

Lemma 6.3
.

If
A
is a finite discrete space,
\rho
is a metric on
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
compatible with the product topology, and
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is a strictly increasing sequence, then pseudometrics:
D_{B}^{(n_{k})}
given by (
6.2
) and
\bar{d}_{(n_{k})}
are uniformly equivalent on
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The next Lemma has not appeared in
[
34
]
in any form, hence we will present its proof in detail.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
f\in C(X)
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that if
x,x^{\prime}\in X
satisfy

D_{B}(x,x^{\prime})<\delta
, then there is
N\geq 1
such that for every
n\geq N
and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
we have

\left|\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x)-\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x^{\prime})\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in C(X)
. We pick
\alpha>0
such that if
z,z^{\prime}\in X
and
\rho(z,z^{\prime})<\alpha
, then
\lvert f(z)-f(z^{\prime})\rvert<{\varepsilon}/{3}
. Set
\beta=\min\{\alpha,\varepsilon/(4\max\{1,\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\})\}
. Next, we use Lemma
6.1
for
n_{k}=k
to choose
\delta>0
so small that
D_{B}(x,x^{\prime})<\delta
implies

d_{*}\left(\left\{j\geq 0:\rho\left(T^{j}(x),T^{j}(x^{\prime})\right)<\beta\right\}\right)\geq 1-\beta.

It follows that there is
N\geq 1
such that for every
n\geq N
we have

\frac{1}{n}\left\lvert\left\{0\leq j<n:\rho\left(T^{j}(x),T^{j}(x^{\prime})\right)<\alpha\right\}\right\rvert\geq 1-\dfrac{\varepsilon}{3\max\{1,\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\}}.

Take
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Fix
n\geq N
and define

I(n)=\left\{0\leq j<n:\rho\left(T^{j}(x),T^{j}(x^{\prime})\right)<\alpha\right\}.

Note that
j\in I(n)
implies
|f(T^{j}(x))-f(T^{j}(x^{\prime}))|<\varepsilon/3
.
Let
J(n)
be the complement of
I(n)
in
\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Hence,

(6.4)

\dfrac{1}{n}\lvert J(n)\rvert\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}<\dfrac{\varepsilon}{3}\frac{\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}}{\max\{1,\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\}}\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Furthermore, for
j\in J(n)
we have the trivial bound

\left|f\left(T^{j}(x)\right)-f\left(T^{j}(x^{\prime})\right)\right|\leq 2\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\lvert\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x)

\displaystyle-\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x^{\prime})\rvert=\left|\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f\left(T^{j}(x)\right)\xi^{-j}-\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f\left(T^{j}(x^{\prime})\right)\xi^{-j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left|f\left(T^{j}(x)\right)-f\left(T^{j}(x^{\prime})\right)\right|\left|\xi^{-j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{j\in I(n)}|f(T^{j}(x))-f(T^{j}(x^{\prime}))|+\sum_{j\in J(n)}|f(T^{j}(x))-f(T^{j}(x^{\prime}))|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{n}\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon}{3}\lvert I(n)\rvert+2\lvert J(n)\rvert\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\right)\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon}{3}+2\dfrac{\varepsilon}{3}=\varepsilon.

This completes the proof.
∎

7.
A new metric on the space of invariant measures

We study the metric
\bar{\rho}
on the space
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
. We show that
\bar{\rho}
is a metric inducing a topology stronger than the weak
∗
topology. The metric
\bar{\rho}
generalises Ornstein’s
\bar{d}_{\mathcal{M}}
-metric on the space of shift-invariant measures (see
[
41
,
47
]
). We denote Ornstein’s
\bar{d}
-metric by
\bar{d}_{\mathcal{M}}
to avoid confusion with the pseudometric
\bar{d}
on
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
as discussed in §
6
. We recall its definition below. To our best knowledge, the metric
\bar{\rho}
was not studied before. Nevertheless, some generalisations of Ornstein’s
\bar{d}_{\mathcal{M}}
-distance similar to
\bar{\rho}
have been considered before. R. M. Gray, D. L. Neuhoff and P. C. Shields in
[
26
]
used one of these generalisations in applications to coding theory. The results of
[
26
]
are phrased in the language of stochastic processes, rather than in that of homeomorphisms and their invariant measures. In particular,
\bar{d}_{\mathcal{M}}
is viewed there as a distance between finite valued discrete-time stochastic processes, and its generalisation in
[
26
]
is a metric for discrete-time stochastic processes having values in separable metric spaces. Another generalisation was provided by Schwarz
[
46
]
, who also used the framework of discrete time processes to prove some results of D. S. Ornstein without invoking an approximation by discrete partitions.

Assume that
(X,T)
and
(Y,S)
are topological dynamical systems. By
\pi^{X}
(respectively,
\pi^{Y}
) we denote the projection of
X\times Y
onto
X
(respectively,
Y
). If
\lambda
is a measure on
X\times Y
, then we define the push-forward measure
\pi^{Z}_{*}(\lambda)
on
Z=X
(respectively,
Y
) by
\pi^{Z}_{*}(\lambda)(B)=\lambda((\pi^{Z})^{-1}(B))
. A
joining
of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)
is a
T\times S
-invariant measure
\lambda
on
X\times Y
such that
\pi^{X}_{*}(\lambda)=\mu
and
\pi^{Y}_{*}(\lambda)=\nu
. Let
J(\mu,\nu)
stand for the set of all joinings of
\mu
and
\nu
. This is a nonempty, closed, and convex subset of
\mathcal{M}_{T\times S}(X\times Y)
. Let
F\colon X\times Y\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous function. We define

(7.1)

\bar{F}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\lambda\in J(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times Y}F(x,y)\text{ d}\lambda(x,y).

Note that the map
J(\mu,\nu)\ni\lambda\mapsto\int_{X\times Y}F(x,y)\text{ d}\lambda(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}
is convex and continuous. Hence, the infimum in (
7.1
) is attained at some extreme point of
J(\mu,\nu)
. Furthermore, if
\mu
and
\nu
are both ergodic, then the set
J^{e}(\mu,\nu)
of extreme points of
J(\mu,\nu)
consists of ergodic
T\times S
-invariant measures.

Plugging
(Y,S)=(X,T)
and
F=\rho
(where
\rho
is, as usual, a compatible metric on
X
) into (
7.1
) we obtain a function
\bar{\rho}
given for
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
by

(7.2)

\bar{\rho}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\lambda\in J(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times X}\rho(x,y)\text{ d}\lambda(x,y).

It is easy to see (cf. the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 21.1.2]
) that
\bar{\rho}
is a metric on
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
(the proof of the triangle inequality hinges on the gluing lemma
[
23
, Theorem 16.11.1]
).
The topology induced by
\bar{\rho}
on
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
is stronger than the weak
∗
topology on
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
, because for every
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
we have

W_{1}(\mu,\nu)\leq\bar{\rho}(\mu,\nu)
, where
W_{1}
stands for the Wasserstein metric inducing the weak
∗
topology (see
[
23
]
). We record this fact as a remark for further reference.

Remark 7.1
.

If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
and
(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
is a sequence such that
\bar{\rho}(\mu_{n},\mu)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
,
then
\mu_{n}
converges to
\mu
in the weak
∗
topology.

We recall the definition of Ornstein’s metric
\bar{d}_{\mathcal{M}}
. Note that Ornstein’s d-bar distance
\bar{d}_{\mathcal{M}}
is usually denoted by
\bar{d}
in the literature, but we frequently use the latter symbol for other purposes, so we need to distinguish between
\bar{d}
and
\bar{d}_{\mathcal{M}}
.
Fix an alphabet
A
and define
d_{0}\colon A^{\mathbb{Z}}\times A^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\{0,1\}
by

d_{0}((x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},(y_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }x_{0}\neq y_{0},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
d_{0}
is a continuous function, so we take
(Y,S)=(X,T)=(A^{\mathbb{Z}},\sigma)
and
F=d_{0}
in (
7.1
) to get a function given for

\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(A^{\mathbb{Z}})
by

(7.3)

\bar{d}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mu,\nu)=\bar{d}_{0}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\lambda\in J(\mu,\nu)}\int_{A^{\mathbb{Z}}\times A^{\mathbb{Z}}}d_{0}(x,y)\text{ d}\lambda(x,y).

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
(X,T)
and
(Y,S)
be topological dynamical systems and
F\colon X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous.
Assume that
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
n_{k}\nearrow\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Suppose
x\in X
(respectively,
y\in Y
) is quasi-generic along
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
(respectively,
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)
). Then

(7.4)

\bar{F}(\mu,\nu)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}F(T^{j}(x),S^{j}(y)).

If, in addition,
\mu
and
\nu
are ergodic and if
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
and
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
are Borel sets such that
\mu(X^{\prime})=\nu(Y^{\prime})=1
, then
there exist Borel sets
X^{\prime\prime}\subseteq X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime\prime}\subseteq Y^{\prime}
such that
\mu(X^{\prime\prime})=\nu(Y^{\prime\prime})=1
and for every
x^{\prime\prime}\in X^{\prime\prime}
there exists
y^{\prime\prime}\in Y^{\prime\prime}
such that

(7.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}F(T^{j}(x^{\prime\prime}),S^{j}(y^{\prime\prime}))=\bar{F}(\mu,\nu).

Proof.

Suppose
x\in X
(respectively,
y\in Y
) is quasi-generic along
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
(respectively,
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)
). Without loss of generality,
we assume

(7.6)

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}F(T^{j}(x),S^{j}(y))=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{{n}_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{{n}_{k}-1}F(T^{j}(x),S^{j}(y)).

For
k\geq 1
consider the probability measure

\delta_{k}
on
X\times Y
given by

(7.7)

\delta_{k}=\dfrac{1}{{n}_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{{n}_{k}-1}\delta_{(T\times S)^{j}(x,y)}.

Since
\mathcal{M}(X\times Y)
is compact, without loss of generality, we assume that
(\delta_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges to
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{T\times S}\left(X\times Y\right)
(otherwise, we pass to a converging subsequence and rename it
(\delta_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
). Combining (
7.6
) with (
7.7
) we obtain

(7.8)

\int_{X\times Y}F\text{ d}\lambda=\int_{X\times Y}F\text{ d}(\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\delta_{k})=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}F(T^{j}(x),S^{j}(y)).

Since
\lambda
is a joining of
\mu
and
\nu
, (
7.1
) together with (
7.8
) yield (
7.4
).

Now, assume that
\mu
and
\nu
are ergodic. Consider Borel subsets
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
and
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
such that
\mu(X^{\prime})=\nu(Y^{\prime})=1
. Let
\lambda\in J(\mu,\nu)
be such that
\bar{F}(\mu,\nu)=\int F\text{ d}\lambda
. Since
J(\mu,\nu)
is nonempty, convex and compact, the infimum in (
7.1
) must be attained at an extreme point. As
\mu
and
\nu
are ergodic, the set of extreme points
J^{e}(\mu,\nu)
of
J(\mu,\nu)
consists of ergodic joinings, so
\lambda
must be ergodic.

We denote the set of
\lambda
-generic points by
\Gen(\lambda)
.
Let

(7.9)

L^{\prime}=\left((\pi^{X})^{-1}(X^{\prime})\cap(\pi^{Y})^{-1}(Y^{\prime})\right)\cap\Gen(\lambda)\subseteq X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}.

Note that
\lambda\big((\pi^{X})^{-1}(X^{\prime})\big)=\lambda\big((\pi^{Y})^{-1}(Y^{\prime})\big)=1
.
It follows that
\lambda\left(L^{\prime}\right)=1
. If
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in L^{\prime}
, then by the ergodic theorem we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}F\left(T^{j}(x^{\prime}),S^{j}(y^{\prime})\right)=\int_{X\times Y}F\text{ d}\lambda.

Since
\lambda
is a regular measure, for every
n\geq 1
there is a compact set
K_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq L^{\prime}
with
\lambda(K_{n}^{\prime})>1-1/n
. Let

X_{n}^{\prime\prime}=(\pi^{X})(K_{n}^{\prime})
and
Y_{n}^{\prime\prime}=(\pi^{Y})(K_{n}^{\prime})
. Clearly,
X_{n}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq X^{\prime}
and
Y_{n}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq Y^{\prime}
are compact sets satisfying

\mu(X_{n}^{\prime\prime})=\lambda\big((\pi^{X})^{-1}(X^{\prime\prime}_{n})\big)\geq\lambda(K_{n}^{\prime})>1-1/n
and

\nu(Y_{n}^{\prime\prime})=\lambda\big((\pi^{Y})^{-1}(Y^{\prime\prime}_{n})\big)\geq\lambda(K_{n}^{\prime})>1-1/n
. Denote

X^{\prime\prime}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}^{\prime\prime}\text{ and }Y^{\prime\prime}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}Y_{n}^{\prime\prime}.

Now,
X^{\prime\prime}\subseteq X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime\prime}\subseteq Y^{\prime}
are
\sigma
-compact, hence Borel measurable sets with full measure. Furthermore, for every
x^{\prime\prime}\in X^{\prime\prime}
there exists
y^{\prime\prime}\in Y^{\prime\prime}
such that (
7.5
) holds.
∎

Taking
(Y,S)=(X,T)
and
F=\rho
(the metric on

), we obtain the following as a consequence of Theorem
7.2
.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
.

(1)

If
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is a strictly increasing sequence,
x\in X
(respectively,
y\in Y
) is quasi-generic along
(n_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
for
\mu
(respectively,
\nu
), and

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}\rho(T^{j}(x),T^{j}(y))\leq\varepsilon,

.

(2)

Let
X_{\mu}\subseteq\Gen(\mu)
and
X_{\nu}\subseteq\Gen(\nu)
be Borel sets such that
\mu(X_{\mu})=\nu(X_{\nu})=1
.
If
\bar{\rho}(\mu,\nu)<\varepsilon
for some

, then there exist Borel sets
X^{\prime}_{\mu}\subseteq X_{\mu}
, and
X^{\prime}_{\nu}\subseteq X_{\nu}
such that
\mu(X^{\prime}_{\mu})=\nu(X^{\prime}_{\nu})=1
and for every

there exists

with

.

Lemma 7.4
.

The metric
\bar{\rho}
is complete.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
be a Cauchy sequence with respect to
\bar{\rho}
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
with the weak
∗
topology is compact, passing to a subsequence if necessary, we assume that
(\mu_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}
is weak
∗
convergent and we denote its limit by
\mu
. It remains to show that
\bar{\rho}(\mu_{m},\mu)\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
be such that for every
i,j\geq M
we have
\bar{\rho}(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})<\varepsilon
. We claim that for every
m\geq M
we have
\bar{\rho}(\mu,\mu_{m})\leq\varepsilon
. To this end we fix
m\geq M
and for every
\ell>m
use the definition of
\bar{\rho}
to find
\lambda_{\ell}\in J(\mu_{m},\mu_{\ell})
such that

\bar{\rho}(\mu_{m},\mu_{\ell})\leq\int_{X\times X}\rho(x,y)\text{ d}\lambda_{\ell}(x,y)<\varepsilon.

Since the space
\mathcal{M}_{T\times T}(X\times X)
is compact, the sequence

(\lambda_{\ell})_{\ell=m+1}^{\infty}
has a convergent subsequence
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{T\times T}(X\times X)
, which is easily seen to be a joining of

Lemma 7.5
.

If

and

are such that

, then

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)
and

be such that

\bar{\rho}(\mu_{m},\mu)\to 0\text{ as }m\to\infty.

Fix

and take

. Let

Let

Markov’s inequality and (

\hat{\Lambda}
-a.e.

is an ergodic
T\times T
-invariant measure having

as the first marginal.
Let

be the projection on the second coordinate. Write

\int_{X}f\text{ d}\mu=\int_{X^{2}}f\circ\pi\text{ d}\Lambda=\int_{\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T\times T}(X^{2})}\int_{X^{2}}f\circ\pi\text{ d}\lambda\text{ d}\hat{\Lambda}(\lambda)=\\

In other words,
\hat{\zeta}=\pi_{**}\hat{\Lambda}
is the ergodic decomposition of

\hat{\zeta}\left(\{\zeta\in\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X):\bar{\rho}(\mu_{m},\zeta)\geq\varepsilon\}\right)<\varepsilon.

imply that

Lemma 8.1
.

(\mu_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
.
Assume that for each
m\geq 1

\Freq(x)\subseteq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\Freq(x_{m}):=\bigcup_{M=1}^{\infty}\bigcap_{m=M}^{\infty}\Freq(x_{m}).

Let
f\in C(X)
. Fix

. Our assumption means that

(8.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x_{m})

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x)

\displaystyle=\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x).

(8.4)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x_{m})=\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x).

\varepsilon>0
. Using Lemma
6.4
, choose
\delta>0
for
\varepsilon/3
and
f\in C(X)
.
Take

sufficiently large to guarantee

. Now, Lemma
6.4
implies that
for all but finitely many

’s we have

(8.5)

\left|\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x)-\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x_{m})\right|<\varepsilon/3.

On the other hand, (
8.2
) and (
8.3
) imply that for all

’s large enough it also holds

(8.6)

\displaystyle\left|\Av_{n}[f,\bar{\xi}](x_{m})-\Av[f,\bar{\xi}](x_{m})\right|<\varepsilon/3,

(8.7)

Lemma 8.2
.

Suppose that
(x_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}\subseteq X

the point

is

-generic
for a mixing

We need to consider various

for

and write

for the scalar product on

. Since continuous functions are dense in

[

that it is enough to check that for every

we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle U_{T}^{n}(f),f\rangle_{m}=\langle f,1\rangle_{m}\cdot\langle 1,f\rangle_{m}.

Fix

and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\Psi_{m}(f)=\langle f,1\rangle_{m}\cdot\langle 1,f\rangle_{m}
and
\Psi(f)=\langle f,1\rangle\cdot\langle 1,f\rangle
. Given

and

we clearly have

\left|\langle U_{T}^{n}(f),f\rangle-\Psi(f)\right|\\
\leq\left|\langle U_{T}^{n}(f),f\rangle-\langle U_{T}^{n}(f),f\rangle_{m}\right|+\left|\langle U_{T}^{n}(f),f\rangle_{m}-\Psi_{m}(f)\right|+\left|\Psi_{m}(f)-\Psi(f)\right|.

Our assumption reads that for every

and
\varepsilon>0
there is

such that for every

. Also, there is

we have

.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\geq 1
we write
y_{n}=T^{n}(x)
and

. With this notation, using that

and

are generic points, we have

\displaystyle\langle U_{T}^{n}(f),f\rangle=\int f\circ T^{n}\cdot\bar{f}\text{ d}\mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}f(T^{j}(y_{n}))\overline{f(T^{j}(x))},

\displaystyle\langle U_{T}^{n}(f),f\rangle_{m}=\int f\circ T^{n}\cdot\bar{f}\text{ d}\mu_{m}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}f(T^{j}(y^{(m)}_{n}))\overline{f(T^{j}(x_{m}))}.

and for every sufficiently large

Characteristic classes are families of measure-preserving systems introduced in
[
7
]
and are related to an extension of Sarnak’s conjecture beyond zero-entropy systems studied in
[
7
]
. A
characteristic class
is the collection
\mathcal{C}
of all measure-preserving systems that is closed under taking factors and (countable) joinings. Recently, the authors of
[
6
]
proved that for every characteristic class
\mathcal{C}
the set
\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}
is closed with respect to
\bar{\rho}
. In particular, they established in
[
6
]
that if
(x_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence such that for each
m\geq 1
the point
x_{m}
is generic for some
\mu_{m}\in\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}
and
x_{m}
converges to
x
in the Besicovitch pseudometric, then
x
is generic for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}
. Since the family of all measure-preserving systems with discrete spectrum forms a characteristic class, the following result is an immediate consequence of
[
6
]
, where
\mathcal{C}
is the collection of measures with discrete spectrum. It can be also proved directly using techniques similar to those used in the proof of Lemma
8.2
. We present that proof for completeness.

\left\lvert\norm{f\circ T^p-f\circ T^q}^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu_{m})}-\norm{f\circ T^p-f\circ T^q}^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}\right\rvert\\
\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left\lvert\lvert f(x_{p+j}^{(m)})-f(x_{q+j}^{(m)})\rvert^{2}-\lvert f(\bar{x}_{p+j})-f(\bar{x}_{q+j})\rvert^{2}\right\rvert.

For every

into three pairwise disjoint subsets, in a similar way as in the proof of Lemma

Theorem 8.4
.

8.1.
Disjointness of measure preserving systems

By a standard measure preserving system we understand here a system of the form
(X,T,\mu)
, where
(X,T)
is a topological dynamical system and
\mu
is a completion of
T
-invariant Borel probability measure. We refer to such as system as to
\mu
when
(X,T)
is irrelevant for our considerations. Usually, measure preserving systems are understood to be given by automorphisms of a standard Lebesgue space, but every measure preserving system given by such an automorphism is isomorphic to the one given by a completion of an invariant measure for a topological dynamical system. We say that measure preserving system
(X,T,\mu)
and
(Y,S,\nu)
are
disjoint
(for short, we say that
\mu
and
\nu
are disjoint) if
\mu\times\nu
is the only joining of
\mu
and
\nu
. We write
\mu\perp\nu
to denote that
\mu
and
\nu
are disjoint measures.
Given a class of measure preserving systems
\mathscr{C}
we write

\mathscr{C}_{\perp}=\{\mu:\forall\nu\in\mathscr{C}\ \mu\perp\nu\}\}

for the class of all measure preserving system disjoint with every member of
\mathscr{C}
. The class of all measure preserving system disjoint with every ergodic member of
\mathscr{C}

is denoted by
\mathscr{C}_{\perp}^{e}
. Our next theorem says that
for every topological dynamical system
(X,T)
the set
\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)\cap\mathscr{C}_{\perp}^{e}
is
\bar{\rho}
closed.

Theorem 8.5
.

Let
(\mu_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)
be such that
\mu_{m}\in\mathscr{C}_{\perp}^{e}
for every
m\geq 1
for some class
\mathscr{C}
of measure preserving systems.
If
\bar{\rho}(\mu_{m},\mu)\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, then

\mu\in\mathscr{C}_{\perp}^{e}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
be such that
\mu_{m}\in\mathscr{C}_{\perp}^{e}
for every
m\geq 1
and
\bar{\rho}(\mu_{m},\mu)\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. By Lemma
7.5
we obtain that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}_{T}(X)
.
Take any ergodic
\nu^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}
. Let
(Y,S)
be a strictly ergodic topological model for
\nu^{\prime}
, that is
(Y,S)
is a topological dynamical systems such that
\mathcal{M}^{e}_{S}(Y)=\{\nu\}
, where
\nu
is isomorphic with
\nu^{\prime}
. Let
\lambda\in J^{e}(\mu,\nu)
. We want to show
\lambda=\mu\times\nu
and it is enough to prove that for
f\in C(X)
and
g\in C(Y)
we have

(8.15)

\int f(x)g(y)\text{ d}\lambda(x,y)=\left(\int f\text{ d}\mu\right)\cdot\left(\int g\text{ d}\nu\right).

To this end, we note that since
\lambda
is ergodic and
\mu
is its marginal, there is a Borel set
X_{\lambda}\subseteq\Gen(\mu)
such that
\mu(X_{\lambda})=1
and for every
x\in X_{\lambda}
there is
y\in Y
such that
(x,y)\in\Gen(\lambda)
. Hence, by Theorem
7.2
(cf. Corollary
7.3
(
2
)) there is a Borel set
X^{*}\subseteq\Gen(\mu)
such that if
x\in X^{*}
, then there is
y\in Y
such that
(x,y)\in\Gen(\lambda)
and for every
m\geq 1
there is
x_{m}\in\Gen(\mu_{m})
with
D_{B}(x,x_{m})=\bar{\rho}(\mu,\mu_{m})
.
For
x\in X
and
y\in Y
set
h(x,y)=f(x)g(y)
and

\Av_{n}[h](x,y)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}h(T^{j}(x),S^{j}(y))=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^{j}(x))\cdot g(S^{j}(y)).

Given a continuous function
\varphi
on a compact metric space and a Borel probability measure
\eta
on the same space we define

\tilde{\varphi}(\eta)=\int\varphi\text{ d}\eta.

Fix
x\in X^{*}
and
y\in Y
such that
(x,y)\in\Gen(\lambda)
. Note that since
(Y,S)
is strictly ergodic we have
y\in\Gen(\nu)
and so by
\mu_{m}\perp\nu
we have that
(x_{m},y)\in\Gen(\mu_{m}\times\nu)
for
m\geq 1
.
Let
\beta=\varepsilon/(6\max\{1,\norm{g}_{\infty}\})
. Using continuity of
f
we find
\alpha>0
such that if
x,x^{\prime}\in X
satisfy

\rho(x,x^{\prime})\leq\alpha
, then
\left\lvert f(x)-f(x^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\beta
.
For every
n,m\geq 1
we have

(8.16)

\left|\Av_{n}[h](x,y)-\tilde{f}(\mu)\tilde{g}(\nu)\right|\leq\left|\Av_{n}[h](x,y)-\Av_{n}[h](x_{m},y)\right|+\\
+\left|\Av_{n}[h](x_{m},y)-\tilde{f}(\mu_{m})\tilde{g}(\nu)\right|+\left|\tilde{f}(\mu_{m})\tilde{g}(\nu)-\tilde{f}(\mu)\tilde{g}(\nu)\right|.

Since
h=f\cdot g
is continuous and
(x_{m},y)\in\Gen(\mu_{m}\times\nu)
, we see that for every
m\geq 1
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Av_{n}[h](x_{m},y)=\int h\text{ d}(\mu_{m}\times\nu)=\tilde{f}(\mu_{m})\tilde{g}(\nu).

By Remark
7.1
,
\bar{\rho}(\mu_{m},\mu)\to 0
as
m\to\infty
implies weak
∗
convergence, thus
\tilde{f}(\mu_{m})\to\tilde{f}(\mu)
as
m\to\infty
. It remains to show that
\left|\Av_{n}[h](x,y)-\Av_{n}[h](x_{m},y)\right|
can be arbitrarily small for all sufficiently large
m
and
n
. Note that

(8.17)

\left|\Av_{n}[h](x,y)-\Av_{n}[h](x_{m},y)\right|\leq\norm{g}_{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left|f(T^{j}(x))-f(T^{j}(x_{m}))\right|.

Now, for every
n\geq 1
we divide the set
\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
into two disjoint subsets:

(1)

Those indices
j
in
[0,n)
such that

\rho(T^{j}(x),T^{j}(x_{m}))\leq\alpha.

For these
j
’s we have

(8.18)

\norm{g}_{\infty}\left|f(T^{j}(x))-f(T^{j}(x_{m}))\right|\leq\beta\norm{f}_{\infty}\norm{g}_{\infty}<\varepsilon/6.

Denote the set of these indices by
G^{(m)}(n)
.

(2)

The indices
j
in
B^{(m)}(n)=[0,n)\setminus G^{(m)}(n)
. For these
j
’s we have

(8.19)

\norm{g}_{\infty}\left|f(T^{j}(x))-f(T^{j}(x_{m}))\right|\leq 2\norm{f}_{\infty}\norm{g}_{\infty}.

Combining (
8.18
) and (
8.19
) we have the following bound for the right hand side of (
8.17
):

\frac{1}{n}\bigg(|G^{(m)}(n)|\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{6}+|B^{(m)}(n)|\cdot 2\norm{f}^{2}_{\infty}\norm{g}_{\infty}\bigg).

Using Lemma
6.1
and
D_{B}(x_{m},x)\to 0
as
m\to\infty
it can be easily observed that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left|G^{(m)}(n)\right|=1\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left|B^{(m)}(n)\right|=0.

Therefore for all sufficiently large
m
and
n
we can bound each term of the right hand side of (
8.16
) by
\varepsilon/3
.
Summing up, for every
\varepsilon>0
and all sufficiently large
n
’s we have
\left|\Av_{n}[h](x,y)-\tilde{f}(\mu)\tilde{g}(\nu)\right|<\varepsilon
, and consequently

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Av_{n}[h](x,y)=\left(\int f\text{ d}\mu\right)\cdot\left(\int g\text{ d}\nu\right).

On the other hand, we picked
(x,y)
, so that it is a generic point for
\lambda
. Since
h=f\cdot g
is continuous, we see that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Av_{n}[h](x,y)=\int f(x)g(y)\text{ d}\lambda(x,y).

We obtain that (
8.15
) holds for every
f\in C(X)
and
g\in C(Y)
, as needed. The proof is completed.
∎

9.1.
Application for
\mathscr{B}

Our first example of a family of symbolic dynamical systems to which we apply Corollary

\mathscr{B}

11

We have chosen
\mathscr{B}
-free shifts, as they form ’natural’ (rather than ’constructed merely for illustration’) set of examples. We do not prove anything new about

\bar{d}_{\mathcal{M}}

\bar{d}

[
7

\mathscr{B}

Sets of

[

\left(\eta_{\mathscr{B}}\right)_{j}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }j\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{B}},\\

Definition 9.1

The

9.2.
Density of ergodic discrete rational spectrum measures for topological dynamical systems with the specification property

Alexandre Trilles in his PhD thesis
[
51
]
proves the following result:
If
(X,T)
is a topological dynamical system with the specification property, then the set of ergodic measures with full support and discrete rational spectrum is dense in
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
.

This strengthens a result of Sigmund
[
48
]
, who proved density of zero entropy fully supported ergodic measures.

The specification property enables the construction of periodic points whose orbits can approximately trace given segments of orbits, we skip the definition here. The proof uses the specification property to construct a sequence of periodic points which converges in the Besicovitch pseudometric to generic point of ergodic measure being close to a fixed in advance invariant measure in the weak
∗
topology. Then the toolbox developed here yields that this ergodic invariant measure close to the fixed one has rational discrete spectrum.

Using the same idea, one can prove that
for every
\beta>1
, the set of ergodic measures with full support and discrete rational spectrum is dense in
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X_{\beta})
, where
X_{\beta}
is the
\beta
-shift.

9.3.
Density of ergodic measures among all invariant measures for topological dynamical systems with the vague specification property

Recently,
[
10
]
proves that any topological dynamical system with the vague specification property has ergodic measures that are dense in the space of its all invariant measures. The paper extends methods from
[
29
]
and
[
11
]
from symbolic to general topological dynamical systems relying on the
\bar{\rho}
metric and its properties presented here.

First, the authors of
[
10
]
construct a sequence of approximating systems for the natural extension
(X_{T},S)
of a topological dynamical system
(X,T)
. Each approximating topological dynamical system consists of
\delta
-chains for some
\delta>0
. It is shown that the periodic specification property holds for these approximations, provided the initial system is chain-mixing. The specification property ensures the density of ergodic measures in the simplex of invariant measures of each approximation.
The crucial technical step is to demonstrate that the simplices of invariant measures of these approximating systems converge to the simplex of invariant measures of the original system in the Hausdorff metric induced by
\bar{\rho}
. It remains to note that this strong form of convergence (in the Hausdorff metric induced by the
\bar{\rho}
-metric rather than just weak

We believe that our result will prove to be useful and there are more applications to come. For example, Lorenzo J. Díaz, Katrin Gelfert, and Michał Rams in a series of papers studied ergodic measures of some skew-products aiming at understanding invariant measures of partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms. In that setting, some measures are naturally approximated by measures whose support is contained in some horseshoe. With some additional assumptions one can prove that the approximation means here “up to an arbitrarily small
\bar{\rho}

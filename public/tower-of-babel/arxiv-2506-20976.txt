Eigenvalue bounds for distance-edge colorings
Aida Abiad, Luuk Reijnders

Abstract
For a fixed positive integer t t , we consider the graph colouring problem in which edges at distance at most t t are given distinct colours. We obtain sharp lower bounds for the distance- t t chromatic index, the least number of colours necessary for such a colouring. Our bounds are of algebraic nature; they depend on the eigenvalues of the line graph and on a polynomial which can be found using integer linear programming methods. We show several graph classes that attain equality for our bounds, and also present some computational results which illustrate the bound’s performance. Lastly, we investigate the implications the spectral approach has to the Erdős-Nešetřil conjecture, and derive some conditions which a graph must satisfy if we could use it to obtain a counter example through the proposed spectral methods. Keywords: Graph coloring; Strong chromatic index; Distance edge-colouring, Eigenvalues

Introduction

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and a positive integer
t
, a
distance-
t
edge-colouring
of
G
is a colouring of the edges such that no two edges within distance
t
are given the same colour. Here, the
distance between two edges
is defined as the number of vertices in a shortest path between them. Incident edges have distance
1
and thus a distance-
1
edge-colouring is just a proper edge-colouring. The
distance-
t
chromatic index
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\chi_{t}^{\prime}(G)
, is the smallest number of colours required to colour all the edges of
G
, such that no two edges within distance
t
share a colour. The
distance-
t
chromatic number
of graph, denoted by
\chi_{t}(G)
, is the smallest number of colours required to colour all the vertices of
G
, such that no two vertices within distance
t
share a colour. The
t
-th power of a graph

G
, denoted by
G^{t}
, is a graph which has the same vertex set as
G
, and two vertices are adjacent if and only if they are within distance
t
in
G
. The
line graph
of a graph
G=(V,E)
, denoted by
L(G)
, has as vertex set
E
, with two vertices adjacent if and only if their corresponding edges in
V
are adjacent. The distance-
t
edge-colouring problem is related to colouring powers of graphs as follows

(1)

\chi_{t}^{\prime}(G)=\chi_{t}(L(G))=\chi_{1}(L(G)^{t}).

Research into the classical
t=1
case goes quite a way back. In particular, in 1964 Vizing, in the form of his eponymous theorem, proved that
\chi^{\prime}(G)\in\{\Delta,\Delta+1\}
, where
\Delta
is the maximum degree of
G
. However, determining whether
\chi^{\prime}(G)
is
\Delta
or
\Delta+1
remains an NP-complete problem
[
18
]
, even for regular graphs
[
26
]
.

Distance-
2
edge-colourings (also known as
strong edge-colourings
) have a rich history which goes back to 1985 (cf.
[
13
]
), and its research has been largely focused on the following conjecture by Erdős and Nešetřil:

Conjecture 1
.

[
13
, Section 1]

\chi_{2}^{\prime}(G)\leq\begin{cases}\frac{5\Delta^{2}}{4}&\text{ if }\Delta\text{ even,}\\
\frac{5\Delta^{2}-2\Delta+1}{4}&\text{ if }\Delta\text{ odd.}\end{cases}

While many efforts have been devoted to this conjecture (see e.g.
[
28
,
8
,
19
]
), it still remains wide open. Additionally, quite some work has been done on determining
\chi_{2}^{\prime}(G)
for specific classes of graphs (see
[
33
]
for an overview) and on bounding
\chi_{2}^{\prime}(G)
for random graphs (see e.g.
[
29
,
32
,
10
,
15
]
).

The general distance-
t
chromatic index was first introduced by Skupień in the early 1990’s
[
30
]
, and since then it has been studied from numerous angles. Skupień himself looked into of the distance-
t
chromatic index of paths, cycles and trees
[
30
]
, as well as of hypercube graphs, using a connection to coding theory
[
31
]
. The distance-
t
chromatic index of hypercube graphs, as well as meshes, was later determined exactly by Drira et al.
[
12
]
, who were motivated by a connection to collision-free communication in wireless sensor networks. Ito et al.
[
20
]
developed two polynomial-time algorithms for finding distance-
t
edge-colourings of partial
k
-trees and planar graphs. Recently, this parameter has garnered quite some attention in the form of a distance-
t
Erdős-Nešetřil problem, the distance-
t
analog of Conjecture
1
. This entails obtaining upper bounds of the form
\chi^{\prime}_{t}(G)\leq b\Delta^{t}
. A result by Kang and Manggala
[
23
]
provided a construction which shows
\chi^{\prime}_{t}(G)=\Omega(\Delta^{t})
, motivating research into bounds of the form
b\Delta^{t}
. Bounds of this form have since then been obtained using the probabilistic method
[
21
,
9
]
. Additionally, the asymptotic behaviour of
\chi^{\prime}_{t}(G)
in terms of
\Delta
has been investigated for graphs of given girth
[
27
,
21
]
and for graphs without a specific cycle length
[
24
]
, using (probabilistic) constructions.

As seen above, until now most research has been devoted to derive upper bounds for the distance-
t
chromatic number. This has largely been done using the probabilistic method. In this paper, we investigate the distance-
t
chromatic index of graphs using the spectral graph theory machinery. This allows us to derive two sharp lower bounds on the distance chromatic index that depend only on the eigenvalues of the line graph (and not the power of the line graph, the spectrum of which is in general unrelated to the spectrum of the line graph itself), and on the choice of a real polynomial of degree
t
. Regarding the latter, we show that finding the best possible lower bound for a given graph reduces to solve a linear integer optimization problem. For small
t
, we prove that our bonds are sharp for some graph classes. For general
t
, we illustrate the bounds tightness by means of computational results. Lastly, we investigate what the spectral approach says about Conjecture
1
and its distance-
t
analog.

The remaining of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we fix the used notation and recall some preliminary results on line graphs. In Section
3
we present
two new lower bounds on
\chi_{t}^{\prime}(G)
, which depend on some polynomial optimization, and we derive the best polynomial for one of the bounds for
t=2,3
. In Section
4
we look at the performance of the new eigenvalue bounds, both theoretically and computationally. In particular we prove that the second lower bound is tight for several graph classes. Finally, in Section
5
we investigate what this spectral approach says to the distance-
t
Erdős-Nešetřil problem, and derive strict conditions on the spectrum that any counterexample found through this spectral method would need to satisfy.

Preliminaries

Throughout, let
G=(V,E)
be a simple, undirected graph on
\absolutevalue{V}=n
with
\absolutevalue{E}=m
edges. We denote by
A
the adjacency matrix of
G
, and we call the eigenvalues of

the

of

\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
, and when we only want to consider distinct eigenvalues, we instead use the notation
\theta_{0}>\theta_{1}>\cdots>\theta_{d}
. When talking about adjacency eigenvalues of the line graph

, we will instead use
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{\prime}_{m}
and
\theta^{\prime}_{0}>\cdots>\theta^{\prime}_{d^{\prime}}
.

The

of a vertex

, denoted

, is the total amount of edges that

is incident to. The
maximum degree
of a graph, denoted by
\Delta
, is the maximum of

over all vertices
v\in V
. If
d(v)=k
for all
v\in V
, then we call the graph
k
-regular
.

Line graphs are a frequently used tool which allow us to connect edge and vertex versions of the same type of graph parameter, see e.g.
[
30
,
31
,
23
]
. There are some preliminary results that we need in order to exploit this link.

Lemma 2
.

[
4
, Chapter 3]

Let
G
be a
k
-regular graph, then
L(G)
is
(2k-2)
-regular.

Since the new bounds will use the spectrum of the line graph, it is useful to characterize it in terms of the spectrum of the original graph. While in general no such connection is known, for regular graphs there is the following characterization.

Corollary 3
.

[
4
, Theorem 3.8]

Let
G
be a
k
-regular graph with
n
vertices and
m=\frac{nk}{2}
edges and adjacency eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots>\lambda_{n}
. Then the line graph
L(G)
has adjacency eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}+k-2\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{n}+k-2
and the eigenvalues
-2
an additional
m-n
times.

In a similar vein, there is in general no connection between the spectrum of a (line) graph and the power graph of said (line) graph. See e.g.
[
11
]
and
[
2
, Section 2]
. This gives a strong argument for our bounds in Section
3
, as they depend only on the spectrum of the original (line) graph and do not require the power graph.

We finally note that the spectrum of the line graph is equivalent to the spectrum of the signless Laplacian
[
7
, Proposition 1.4.1]
, but we do not use this connection in this paper.

\chi^{\prime}_{t}(G)\geq\frac{\absolutevalue{E}}{\min\{\absolutevalue{\{i : p(\lambda'_i) \geq w'(p) \}},\absolutevalue{\{i : p(\lambda'_i) \leq W'(p) \}}\}}.

Proof.

We apply
[

to

4
Tightness and performance of the lower bounds

In this section we study how the bounds from Section

For general

Proof of Proposition

.

In order to obtain a bound from Corollary

, we first need to calculate

Next, by combining Theorem

\theta^{\prime}_{s}\leq-\frac{\theta^{\prime 2}_{0}+\theta^{\prime}_{0}\theta^{\prime}_{d^{\prime}}-\Delta^{\prime}_{3}}{\theta^{\prime}_{0}(\theta^{\prime}_{d^{\prime}}+1)}=-\frac{16-12-4}{-4}=1.

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}_{3}(GM(2k))

5
On the distance-
t
Erdős-Nešetřil problem

In 1985 Erdős and Nešetřil asked what the best upper bound on
\chi_{2}^{\prime}(G)
is in terms of
\Delta
. This spawned the earlier seen Conjecture
1
.

Much work has been done on finding upper bounds of the form
\chi^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq b_{2}\Delta^{2}
(or equivalently
\chi^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq(2-\varepsilon_{2})\Delta^{2}
), for large enough
\Delta
. We already saw Erdős and Nešetřil conjectured
b_{2}
could be as low as
\frac{5}{4}
, but even whether or not
b_{2}<2
was unknown. The only thing that was known was that
b_{2}
could not be less than
\frac{5}{4}
. In 1997 Molley and Reed
[
28
]
managed to prove
b_{2}<2
by using the probabilistic method to show
\chi^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq 1.998\Delta^{2}
. This bound was further tightened to
\chi^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq 1.93\Delta^{2}
by Bruhn and Joos
[
8
]
, and to
\chi^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq 1.772\Delta^{2}
by Hurley et al.
[
19
]
. Most recently, Kang
[
22
]
announced a bound of
\chi^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq 1.730\Delta^{2}
was obtained, though this is not yet published. Additionally, much research has been done into
\chi_{2}^{\prime}(G)
for specific classes of graphs, see the introduction of
[
33
]
where some of this progress is laid out.

For the general distance-
t
chromatic index, a trivial upper bound is
\chi_{t}^{\prime}(G)\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{t}(\Delta-1)^{j}+1
. Just like for the
t=2
case, bounds of the form
\chi^{\prime}_{t}(G)\leq b\Delta^{t}
(or equivalently
\chi^{\prime}_{t}(G)\leq(1-\varepsilon)\Delta^{t})
have received much attention, spurred on by the fact that
\chi_{t}^{\prime}(G)=\Omega(\Delta^{t})

[
23
]
. In 2014 Kaiser and Kang
[
21
]
provided the first such bound by using the probabilistic method, which showed that for large enough
\Delta
we have
\chi_{t}^{\prime}(G)\leq 1.99992\Delta^{t}
. In addition to this bound, Kaiser and Kang also derived some results on the behaviour of
\chi^{\prime}_{t}(G)
given
G
has a certain girth. An improvement to the bound was recently made by Cambie et al.
[
9
]
, where they showed that for large enough
\Delta
we have
\chi_{t}^{\prime}(G)\leq 1.941\Delta^{t}
.

In this section, we apply our spectral approach to the Erdős-Nešetřil conjecture. First, we show why Wilf’s bound (Theorem
20
) cannot be used to strengthen the known results. Then, we look at the Hoffman-type bound (Corollary
8
), and derive some conditions on the spectrum that a potential counterexample from our approach would have to satisfy.

Consider an arbitrary edge
(u,v)\in E(G)
. We want to know how many vertices are within distance
2
of
(u,v)
in
L(G)
. We already know by Lemma
2
that there are
2(k-1)
vertices adjacent to
(u,v)
. Now let us count the vertices at exactly distance
2
.

•

First, we count the vertices of the form
(w,x)
where
(u,w)\in E
. There are
k-1
choices for
w
, and exactly
\lambda
of these will also be adjacent to
u
. For these
\lambda
choices of
w
, we have
k-2
choices of
x
. The remaining
k-1-\lambda
choices of
w
have
k-1
choices of
x
. Hence in total we have

(k-1-\lambda)(k-1)+\lambda(k-2),

vertices at distance
2
of this type.

•

Next, we want to count the vertices of the form
(w,x)
where
(v,w)\in E
. There are again
k-1
choices for
w
, but
\lambda
of these were already considered in the previous case. For the remaining
(k-1-\lambda)
choices of
w
, we have
k-1
choices of
x
. However, exactly
\mu-1
of these choices are also adjacent to
v
(i.e.
(x,v)\in E(G)
), so they were already counted in the previous case. Thus we only have an additional

(k-1-\lambda)(k-1-(\mu-1))=(k-1-\lambda)(k-\mu)

vertices at distance
2
.

In total we find

\displaystyle 2(k-1)+(k-1-\lambda)(k-1)+\lambda(k-2)+(k-1-\lambda)(k-\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2k^{2}+(1+\lambda)(\mu-1)-k(1+\lambda+\mu).

vertices within distance
2
. Since this holds for arbitrary
(u,v)
, we find
L(G)^{2}
is regular and thus its largest eigenvalue is equal to the maximum degree. Hence, Wilf’s bound is

\displaystyle\chi_{2}^{\prime}(G)\leq 2k^{2}+(1+\lambda)(\mu-1)-k(1+\lambda+\mu)+1.\qed

As there are a large amount of strongly regular graphs known and documented (see
[
6
]
and
[
5
]
), Lemma
23
allows us to investigate the behaviour of Wilf’s bound on a large class of graphs. In particular, we are able to find an infinite family of strongly regular graphs of Lie type, for which Wilf’s bound gets arbitrarily close to
\chi^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq 2\Delta^{2}
.

Corollary 24
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a graph
G
of maximum degree
\Delta
such that Proposition
21
gives a bound worse than

\chi^{\prime}_{2}(G)\leq(2-\varepsilon)\Delta^{2}.

Proof.

We use the Lie type SRG construction
E_{6}(q)
(see
[
6
, Table 10.8]
), which has parameters

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{12}-1)(q^{9}-1)}{(q^{4}-1)(q-1)},

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=\frac{q(q^{3}-1)(q^{8}-1)}{q-1},

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=k-1-\frac{q^{7}(q^{5}-1)}{q-1},

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{3}+1)(q^{4}-1)}{q-1}.

Plugging these values into Lemma
23
gives us the bound

\displaystyle B(q)=2k^{2}+(1+\lambda)(\mu-1)-k(1+\lambda+\mu).

It is not hard to see that
k=O(q^{11}),\lambda=O(q^{8})
and
\mu=O(q^{6})
, hence

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{B(q)}{k^{2}}=2.

Thus, as we take
q
larger, the Wilf bound approaches
2\Delta^{2}
for
E_{6}(q)
.
∎

Thus, we conclude that Wilf’s bound will not help in making progress towards Conjecture
1
. However, we cannot currently rule out that Wilf’s bound could be applied to the
t\geq 3
Erdős-Nešetřil problem.

5.2
Hoffman-type bound and the Erdős-Nešetřil conjecture

In Section
3
we derived a spectral Hoffman-type lower bound on
\chi_{2}^{\prime}(G)
(Corollary
8
). In theory, if there is a graph
G
for which the bound from Corollary
8
gives a lower bound larger than
\frac{5\Delta^{2}}{4}
, this would disprove Conjecture
1
. In this section, we show that, if such a graph were to exist, then it (or its line graph) must have a large subinterval
I\subset[\theta_{d},\theta_{0}]
such that none of the eigenvalues are contained in
I
. In particular, the length of
I
(i.e. the distance between
I
’s suprememum and infimum) must be of order
O(\sqrt{\Delta})
or in some cases even
O(\Delta)
.

Proposition 25
.

Let
G
be a
k
-regular graph,
k\geq 2
, for which the Hoffman-type bound (Corollary
9
) gives a lower bound larger than
ck^{2}
for some
c>1
. Then
G
has no eigenvalues in the interval

\displaystyle\left(-1,-2+\frac{4\sqrt{8c^{2}-6c}}{4c-1}\right)

\displaystyle\text{ if }k=2,

\displaystyle\left(-k+1,\frac{ck-k}{ck-1}\right)

\displaystyle\text{ if }k\geq 3.

In particular, for
k\geq 3
, there is an interval
I\subset[-k,k]
of size
\absolutevalue{I}\geq k
which contains no eigenvalues of
G
.

Proof.

For a
k
-regular graph
G
the bound from Corollary
9
has the form

B(\theta_{i-1}^{\prime})=\frac{(2k-2-\theta^{\prime}_{i})(2k-2-\theta^{\prime}_{i-1})}{2k-2+\theta^{\prime}_{i}\theta^{\prime}_{i-1}}.

By solving
B(\theta_{i-1}^{\prime})\geq ck^{2}
we obtain the inequality

\theta^{\prime}_{i-1}\geq\frac{(2k-2)(ck^{2}+\theta^{\prime}_{i}-2k+2)}{-\theta^{\prime}_{i}(ck^{2}-1)-2k+2},

and hence

\theta^{\prime}_{i-1}-\theta^{\prime}_{i}\geq\frac{(2k-2)(ck^{2}+\theta^{\prime}_{i}-2k+2)}{-\theta^{\prime}_{i}(ck^{2}-1)-2k+2}-\theta^{\prime}_{i}=:d(\theta^{\prime}_{i}).

By finding the solutions
d^{\prime}(\theta_{i}^{\prime})=0
and using that since
L(G)
is a line graph, all its eigenvalues are larger than
-2
(by e.g. the equivalence with the PSD signless Laplacian as seen in
[
7
, Proposition 1.4.1]
), we can find that
d(\theta^{\prime}_{i})
has its minimum at

\mu:=\max\left\{-2,\frac{-(2k-2)-\sqrt{2ck^{2}(k-1)(1+k(ck-2))}}{ck^{2}-1}\right\}.

For
k=2
, it becomes

\mu=\frac{-(2k-2)-\sqrt{2ck^{2}(k-1)(1+k(ck-2))}}{ck^{2}-1}.

Hence, we obtain the bound

\theta^{\prime}_{i-1}-\theta^{\prime}_{i}\geq\frac{2\sqrt{2ck^{2}(k-1)(1+k(ck-2))}}{ck^{2}-1}=\frac{4\sqrt{8c^{2}-6c}}{4c-1}.

Once again using that
\theta_{i}\geq-2
, we find that
L(G)
must have no eigenvalues in the interval

\left(-1,\frac{4\sqrt{8c^{2}-6c}}{4c-1}-2\right).

For
k\geq 3
,
\mu=-2
, and we obtain the bound

\theta^{\prime}_{i-1}-\theta^{\prime}_{i}\geq\frac{k(ck+c-2)}{ck-1}.

In this case,
L(G)
must have no eigenvalues in the interval

\left(-1,\frac{k(ck+c-2)}{ck-1}-2\right).

Finally, using Corollary
3
we can translate these conditions on the spectrum of
L(G)
into conditions on the spectrum of
G
. In particular,
G
must have no eigenvalues in the range

\displaystyle\left(-1,-2+\frac{4\sqrt{8c^{2}-6c}}{4c-1}\right)

\displaystyle\text{ if }k=2,

\displaystyle\left(-k+1,\frac{ck-k}{ck-1}\right)

\displaystyle\text{ if }k\geq 3.

We can also derive a version of Proposition
25
which holds for general graphs, where now the line graph must have a large gap in its spectrum.

Proposition 26
.

Let
G
be a graph with maximum degree
\Delta\geq 2
for which the Hoffman-type bound (Corollary
8
) gives a lower bound larger than
c\Delta^{2}
for some
c>1
. Then
L(G)
has no eigenvalues in the interval

\displaystyle\left(-1,\frac{2\sqrt{c\Delta^{3}(c\Delta^{2}-(c+1)\Delta+1}}{c\Delta^{2}-1}-2\right)

\displaystyle\text{ if }\Delta<5,

\displaystyle\left(-1,\frac{c\Delta^{3}-(c+1)\Delta^{2}+1}{2c\Delta^{2}-\Delta-1}\right)

\displaystyle\text{ if }\Delta\geq 5.

We omit the proof of Proposition
26
, as it is similar to that of Proposition
25
, only much more cumbersome.

A similar analysis can likely be performed for the Hoffman-type bound on
\chi_{3}^{\prime}(G)
(Corollary
10
), albeit more technical.

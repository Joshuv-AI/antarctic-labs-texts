Experimental and Computational Studies on Domain-Swapped Structure Stabilization of an Antibody Light Chain by Disulfide Bond Introduction
Authors: Wahyu Fitriana, Takahiro Sakai, Lian Duan, Kowit Hengphasatporn, Yasuteru Shigeta, Tsuyoshi Mashima, Taizo Uda, Emi Hifumi, Shun Hirota
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractDevelopment of different platforms would be useful for designing functional antibodies to improve the efficiency of antibody-based drugs. Three-dimensional domain swapping (3D-DS) may occur in the variable region of antibody light chain #4C214A, and a pair of domain-swapped dimers may interact with each other to form a tetramer. In this study, to stabilize the 3D-DS dimer structure in #4C214A, Val2 in strand A (swapping region) and Thr97 in strand G were replaced with Cys residues, generating #4 V2C/T97C/C214A with a Cys2–Cys97 disulfide bond that cross-links strands A and G of different protomers. The #4 V2C/T97C/C214A tetramer did not dissociate into monomers at low protein concentration (6 μM); however, some of the tetramers were converted to monomers by disulfide bond reduction. Two-dimensional free energy profile analysis for the tetramerization of two 3D-DS dimers was performed by molecular dynamics simulation. These results show that disulfide bond introduction is useful for controlling the dimerization/dissociation of the variable region through 3D-DS.

Introduction

Antibodies (immunoglobulins) are Y-shaped proteins comprising two heavy and two light chains. The fragment of the variable region (Fv) contains complementarity-determining regions that are flexible and important for antigen recognition.1−3 Owing to their specific antigen binding properties, monoclonal antibodies have been successfully used in pharmaceutical and medical applications.4−7 To improve the efficiency of antibody drugs, various artificial antibodies have been engineered. For example, bispecific antibodies that exhibit binding activity to two different antigens have been developed.8−12 Two different single-chain Fv have been linked in tandem with an artificial peptide linker to prevent dissociation between the heavy chain variable region (VH) and light chain variable region (VL).13 Although various antibody drugs have been developed, structural deviations in antibodies are often difficult to control. Thus, it would be useful to develop different methods for engineering and controlling functional antibodies.

The light chain of an antibody generally consists of a constant region (CL) and a VL. Light chains are prone to aggregation, leading to systemic immunoglobulin light chain amyloidosis.14,15 We have previously shown that light chain #4C214A, an alanine variant of light chain #4 at Cys214 (according to Kabat numbering)16 in CL that forms a disulfide bond with the Cys of the heavy chain, is in equilibrium between monomeric and tetrameric states in the solution.17 Light chain #4 is an antibody light chain that may exhibit catalytic activity.18,19 This light chain forms various conformations and aggregates.20 In contrast, light chain #4C214A shows less conformations, and the tetrameric state of #4C214A possesses more secondary structures than the monomeric state.17 The VL region of #4 also exhibits a monomer/tetramer equilibrium, which is responsible for the tetramerization of #4C214A.17 In the crystal structure of the #4 VL tetramer (PDB ID: 8KAD), the C-terminal strand G was exchanged between protomers exhibiting a three-dimensional (3D) domain-swapped dimer structure. Additionally, a pair of domain-swapped dimers interacted with each other through hydrophobic interactions. 3D domain swapping (3D-DS) is a process where the same structural region is exchanged three-dimensionally between different molecules of the same protein.21,22 The three-dimensional structure of the 3D-DS oligomer is similar to the corresponding monomer structure, except for the hinge region that connects the exchanged regions. 3D-DS is commonly found in proteins,21−27 including amyloidogenic proteins.21 Similar 3D-DS dimer structures to that of #4C214A VL have been reported for immunoglobulin superfamily (IgSF) proteins, such as the light-chain-like domain CTLA-4,28 IgSF domain of CD47,29 and llama VHH domain.30 Although monomers and tetramers of #4C214A were observed in the solution, no dimer was observed,17 due to the attack of water molecules on the hinge loop region of the 3D-DS dimer, which was elucidated by molecular dynamics (MD) simulation.31

3D-DS may be useful for constructing bispecific antibodies. However, proteins that undergo 3D-DS may exist in equilibrium, making the development of methods for controlling 3D-DS in antibodies important. On the other hand, disulfide bonds have been introduced into proteins to control their structural changes and functions.32−37 The stability of heavy chain antibodies (nanobodies) was gained by introducing a disulfide bond between two opposite β-strands in the hydrophobic core of the heavy chain variable region.200 Additionally, the 3D-DS dimer of an Osp A variant has been stabilized with disulfide bonds between the introduced Cys residues of different protomers.38 A disulfide bond at the C-terminus has been shown to inhibit formation of C-swapped dimers in amphibian onconase.39,40 The 3D-DS structures of cystatin C and its scaffold SQT-1 have been stabilized by introducing a disulfide bond into the protein.41,42 In this study, two Cys residues were introduced into #4C214A to lock the monomer and 3D-DS dimer conformations with intramolecular and interprotomer disulfide bonds, respectively, inhibiting interconversion between the monomeric and dimeric states (Figures 1 and S1).

Results and Discussion

Selection of Residues for Disulfide Bond Introduction to #4C214A

The positions at which the Cys residues were introduced in #4C214A to form a disulfide bond in VL were evaluated based on the 3D-DS dimer crystal structure of #4 VL (PDB ID: 8KAD(17)). The amino acid residues were selected based on the following criteria: (1) distance between Cα of Cys residues between 3.0 and 7.5 Å and (2) distance between Cβ of Cys residues ≤5.5 Å.43,44 For the #4 V2C/T97C VL monomer not including the constant region, a disulfide bond was observed with Cα and Cβ distances of 6.3 and 4.2 Å, respectively, in the structure predicted by ColabFold (Figure 2). In the predicted #4 V2C/T97C VL 3D-DS dimer structure, the Cα and Cβ distances between the introduced Cys residues were 5.9 and 3.5 Å, respectively. Thus, Val2 in strand A and Thr97 in strand G were replaced with Cys.

Analysis of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A Monomer and Tetramer

#4 V2C/T97C/C214A obtained from Escherichia (E.) coli was purified by performing ion-exchange chromatography twice (Hi-Trap SP HP and MonoS columns) and subsequently size exclusion chromatography (SEC; HiLoad 26/600 Superdex 200 pg column). In the SEC chromatogram, peaks corresponding to the high- and low-molecular-weight species were detected at approximately 180 and 220 mL, respectively (Figure 3A). The molecular weights were 97 and 23 kDa for high- and low-molecular-weight species, respectively, according to the calibration curve (Figure 3B). These values were in good agreement with the calculated molecular masses of the tetramer (100.4 kDa) and monomer (25.1 kDa). However, only monomers and tetramers were detected in the SEC chromatogram of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A, which is similar to that of #4C214A.17

Formation of the #4 V2C/T97C/C214A tetramer was investigated at different protein concentrations by SEC (Superdex 200 Increase 10/300 GL column) using the high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) system (Figure 3C). Tetramers were observed at 12.7 mL in the SEC chromatogram at 100 μM protein concentration (Figure 3C, red trace a). When the #4 V2C/T97C/C214A solution was diluted to 6 μM and incubated at 25 °C for 20 h, an extra peak was observed at approximately 13.4 mL with no monomer peak at approximately 16.2 mL (Figure 3C, pink trace b). The mass of the species detected at 13.4 mL was estimated to be approximately 50 kDa by SEC with multiangle light scattering (SEC-MALS) analysis, indicating dimer formation (Figure 3D). These results indicate that the dissociation of the 3D-DS dimer into monomers was inhibited by the introduction of a disulfide bond. In contrast, no tetramer was produced from the #4 V2C/T97C/C214A monomer fraction at 100 μM (Figure 3C, green trace c), in which an intramolecular disulfide bond was presumably introduced between Cys2 and Cys97, thereby stabilizing the monomer.

An extra disulfide bond was previously used to inhibit 3D-DS.41,42 Strand G at the C-terminus is exchanged between protomers in the 3D-DS structure of #4 VL.17 Val2 and Thr97 of #4 VL are located at the terminal strands, A and G, and these strands may exhibit a high degree of mobility. Thus, the intramolecular Cys2–Cys97 disulfide bond in the monomer and the interprotomer Cys2–Cys97 disulfide bond between the protomers in the 3D-DS dimer may have effectively stabilized the protein structures.

The calculated total solvation-free energies were −4710.89 kcal mol–1 for the #4 VL tetramer and −4441.16 kcal mol–1 for two 3D-DS dimers in explicit water molecules by 3D-reference interaction site model calculations.17 These values indicate that the #4 VL tetramer is more stable than two 3D-DS dimers by −269.73 kcal mol–1, which corresponds to the free energy of dimer-to-tetramer formation. The dimer of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A was observed as a minor peak in the SEC chromatogram at 6 μM protein concentration (Figure 3C), demonstrating that dimers can exist without interacting with each other at low protein concentrations when the interprotomer Cys2–Cys97 disulfide bond is introduced by Cys mutation. The introduced disulfide bond would inhibit the dissociation of the 3D-DS dimer into monomers, allowing the detection of dimers at low protein concentrations in the solution.

In the sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) analysis, the SEC monomer fraction of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A exhibited a band at a slightly lower position than that of #4C214A under nonreduced conditions (Figure 3E). In contrast, a band was detected at a similar position for the #4 V2C/T97C/C214A monomer to that of #4C214A when treated with the reducing agent 2-mercaptoethanol (Figure 3E). These results support the hypothesis that the #4 V2C/T97C/C214A monomer forms an intramolecular Cys2–Cys97 disulfide bond. For the SEC tetramer fraction of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A, a band corresponding to the dimer was detected by SDS-PAGE under nonreduced conditions (Figure 3F), which result was consistent with the formation of tetramers through hydrophobic interactions between pairs of 3D-DS dimers.17 Under reduced conditions, a band for the SEC tetramer fraction was detected at a similar monomer position to that of the SEC monomer fraction in SDS-PAGE analysis (Figure 3F). These results show that the tetramer species detected by SEC (Figure 3A) were composed of a pair of disulfide-linked dimers.

Effect of Reduction and Reoxidation of the Disulfide Bond on #4 V2C/T97C/C214A Tetramerization

To investigate the conversion between the tetrameric and monomeric states of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A via disulfide bond reduction, the tetramer was incubated with 500 mM dithioerythritol (DTT) at 15 °C for 3 h. After removing DTT, the sample was analyzed by SEC (Superdex 200 Increase 10/300 GL column) and SDS-PAGE under nonreduced conditions (Figure 4). Monomer, dimer, and tetramer were detected in the SEC chromatogram of the reduced sample at elution volumes of 16.2, 13.4, and 12.7 mL, respectively (Figure 4A). These results indicate that the tetramer can be converted to monomers when the interprotomer Cys2–Cys97 disulfide bond is reduced. The tetramer was primarily observed after reduction, showing that it was also stable when the disulfide bond was reduced and the noncovalent interactions at the protomer interface were responsible for the association between dimers (Figure 4A, trace b).

DTT reduction and reoxidation of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A tetramer. (A) SEC chromatograms of (a) purified tetramer, (b) after reduction, and after subsequent reoxidation for (c) 22 and (d) 40 h. Monitoring wavelength was 280 nm. (B) SDS-PAGE analysis of the monomer and tetramer SEC fractions without the 2-mercaptoethanol treatment: after purification, after subsequent reduction, and after reoxidation for 22, 30, and 40 h.

After the reoxidation of the reduced #4 V2C/T97C/C214A, the intensity of the tetramer peak decreased in the SEC chromatogram, whereas that of the monomer peak increased (Figure 4A, traces c and d). Although the intensity of the tetramer peak did not change significantly in the SEC chromatogram as the period of air exposure was increased (Figure 4A, traces c and d), the intensity of the dimer band in the SDS-PAGE analysis increased (Figure 4B). These results indicate that the interprotomer Cys2–Cys97disulfide bond was formed within the 3D-DS dimer upon air exposure to the reduced #4 V2C/T97C/C214A in the tetrameric state.

Although the amount of dimers increased and that of the monomers decreased with longer reoxidation times after reduction in the SDS-PAGE analysis (Figure 4B), the intensity of the tetramer peak slightly decreased in the SEC chromatogram for longer oxidation times without significant change in the dimer peak intensity. These results indicated that the dimer may be slightly perturbed by formation of a disulfide bond, decreasing the formation of tetramers. Three monomer bands were observed in the SDS-PAGE analysis of the tetramer SEC fraction without 2-mercaptoethanol treatment after reduction and subsequent reoxidation, although the proteins showed bands at similar positions in the SDS-PAGE analysis under reduced conditions (Figure 4B). Because antibodies are flexible, non-native (scrambled) disulfide bonds between the original (Cys23 and Cys95) and introduced (Cys2 and Cys97) Cys residues may have been produced, inhibiting the formation of dimers due to the perturbation of the protein structure.

Structural Dynamics of 3D-DS Dimer

The MD simulation of #4 V2C/T97C VL confirmed that the introduction of a disulfide bond between Cys2 and Cys97 plays an important role in stabilizing the 3D-DS dimer up to 1 μs. The hydrogen bond (HB) interactions between chains A and B over the simulation time showed a similar pattern to those observed in the crystal tetrameric17 and simulated dimeric31 structures of #4 VL (Figure S2A,B). Interestingly, the disulfide bonds significantly enhanced the stability of HBs in the hinge loop region (HB1 and HB1’, Table S1) superior to those in #4 VL. The Cα- root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of the entire dimeric structure was 12 Å, which was relatively high (Figure S2C). On the other hand, the Cα-RMSD values of unit X (residues 1A–93A and 100B–107B) and unit Y (residues 1B–93B and 100A–107A) were smaller at around 3.5 and 2.5 Å, respectively. These results indicate that the 3D-DS structure was maintained throughout the simulation. Based on the density-based spatial clustering of applications with noise (DBSCAN) applied to the principal component analysis (PCA) data, all replicates revealed a single cluster (Figure S2D,E), which referred to the constant fluctuation between units X and Y at the soffit of the 3D-DS dimer owing to the hydrophobic interactions between the aromatic residues residing at the soffit side, as previously observed in #4 VL.17,31

The #4 V2C/T97C VL 3D-DS dimer maintained its original conformation during the MD simulation due to the interprotomer Cys2–Cys97 disulfide bond. The amino acid network interaction around the Cys2–Cys97 region exhibited similar interaction patterns in chains A and B (Figure 5), in which the van der Waals force was the major interaction. Notably, Cys97 showed a highly stable HB interaction with Met89 (approximately 100%) and Gln90 (approximately 88–99%), which reside near the hinge loop of the other chain in the dimer. The residues surrounding the introduced disulfide bond formed an amino acid interaction network, preventing the collapse of the hinge loop region. A previous study revealed weak interactions between residues in the hinge loop, which collapsed due to the attack of water molecules at Gln90A and Thr97B in #4 VL, expanding of the gap between the hinge loops.31 These networks, on the other side, keep the hydrophobic cores apart, thus stabilizing the overall conformation of the 3D-DS dimer.

The blue line represents the hydrogen bonding interaction. (B) Amino acid interaction network around Cys2–Cys97 is elucidated in terms of van der Waals and hydrogen bonding interactions with the percentage of HBoccupancy throughout the 1 μs simulation.

MD Simulation of the 3D-DS Dimer Association

To explore the association process of #4 V2C/T97C VL 3D-DS dimers, a 1 μs unbiased MD simulation was conducted. Two 3D-DS dimers (3D-DSA/B and 3D-DSC/D) were placed 75 Å apart from each other within the same periodic boundary conditions (PBC) box to ensure no initial interaction. Classical MD simulation was performed using the same procedure as described in the experimental section. Based on dCOMA/B···C/D, the dimers rapidly moved toward each other and associated during the initial phase of simulation (approximately 0.1 μs, dCOMA/B···C/D = 22.98 Å), as shown in Figure 6A. Following this initial association, these dimers engaged in a dynamic search for an optimal tetrameric configuration, which continued until the simulation reached 0.6 μs with the minimum dCOMA/B···C/D (7.55 Å). Subsequently, the Cys2–Cys97 disulfide bonds in both chains were reduced, and the MD simulation was sequentially prolonged until 1.0 μs. The complex remained stable in the tetramer comprising two 3D-DS dimers with a similar dCOMA/B···C/D to that before reduction (8.35 Å). These results supported the hypothesis that 3D-DS dimers rapidly associated and were dynamically converted into a stable tetrameric configuration for #4 V2C/T97C VL.

(B) 2D-FEL profiles of the 3D-DS tetramerization process over 1 μs unbiased MD simulation calculated from the plot of dCOMA/B···C/D versus #Atom contact, where dCOMA/B···C/D ranges from 0 to 30 Å. The free energy ranged from 0 to 5 kBT. The GMS is noted in the 2D-FEL. The two-dimensional free energy landscape (2D-FEL) profile of the #4 V2C/T97C VL tetramer system with two 3D-DS dimers provided further insights into the tetramerization process based on the Markov state model (MSM), showing the relative free energy (kBT) of two the 3D-DS dimers across its conformational space. According to the 2D-FEL for dCOMA/B···C/D versus the number of atom contacts (#Atom contact) (Figure 6B), the #4 V2C/T97C tetramer formed after two 3D-DS dimers moved closer to each other with the optimal range of dCOMA/B···C/D of approximatly 7 to 12 Å (highlighted by the red bar in Figure 6B). Unbiased trajectory analysis revealed that the 3D-DS dimers reached a global minimum state (GMS), indicating their progression toward an optimal conformation for successful tetramerization (Figure 6B). In particular, the GMS for #Atom contact showed an optimal number (approximately 350 atoms) as the dimers approached each other to approximately 7 Å. This trend was consistent with the patterns observed for the 2D-FEL of RMSD (Figure S3). In the unbound state, the dimers exhibited a higher free energy. However, as they moved closer to approximately 12 Å, they began to stabilize through hydrophobic interactions, similar to that observed for #4 VL.31 These results highlight that the increase in atomic contacts related to hydrophobic interactions was a key factor in driving the dimers toward a GMS, lowering the free energy and making the tetramer structure thermodynamically favorable, which was consistent with the experimental results (Figure 4A).

Apart from the energetic terms, the two 3D-DS dimers exhibited higher fluctuations in (i) the initial phase of the simulation (0–0.1 μs) as compared to (ii) after tetramerization and (iii) after reduction based on the standard deviation (SD) (Figure 6C), consistent to that observed in previous MD simulations.17,31 The stability of each 3D-DS dimer increased remarkably by the transition of dimers to the tetramer. SD of the average RMSD of 3D-DSA/B decreased from 1.84 Å in the initial phase to 0.34 and 0.22 Å after tetramerization and reduction, respectively, and that of 3D-DSC/D decreased from 1.06 Å to 0.22 and 0.20 Å, respectively. In (i) the initial phase of the simulation, the stabilization of the 3D-DS dimer structure was attributed to the maintenance of the arch-shaped structure. This is especially reinforced by the disulfide bonds between Cys2 and Cys97, as they stabilize the arch structure but allow for fluctuations that may promote the necessary breathing motions for tetramer formation. (iii) After the reduction of the Cys2–Cys97 disulfide bond, the 3D-DS dimers maintained the tetramer complex until the end of 1 μs MD simulation. In this MD simulation, a 3D-DS dimer association process was observed owing to the disulfide bond. However, after formation of the tetramer, this complex was stabilized by hydrophobic interactions similar to #4 VL.17 The hydrophobic interaction analysis of the #4 V2C/T97C VL tetramer in the MD simulation showed that 6 out of 8 hydrophobic interactions (numbered 1–8) could exist in the #4 V2C/T97C VL tetramer (Figure S4A), similar to those observed in the #4 VL tetramer.17 Especially, hydrophobic interaction 3 in #4 V2C/T97C VL was absent due to Pro44D forming hydrogen bonds with the surrounding Gln45B, Leu46B, and Ser56B (interactions 9–11). Only hydrophobic interaction 7 completely disappeared in the MD simulation of #4 V2C/T97C VL (Figure S4B). Additionally, Cα-RMSD of the #4 V2C/T97C VL tetramer structure relative to the #4 VL structure was 7.57 ± 0.16 Å (Figure S4C). These results support the hypothesis that #4 V2C/T97C VL and #4 VL exhibit similar patterns in tetramerization.

Conclusions

Dimers were detected for the #4 V2C/T97C/C214A light chain at low protein concentration (6 μM), and dissociation of 3D-DS dimers to monomers was inhibited by the introduction of a disulfide bond between Cys2, the Cys residue of the swapped region of one protomer, and Cys97, the Cys residue in the rest region of the protein of the other protomer. The tetramers were converted to monomers only when the disulfide bond was reduced. The tetramerization of the #4 V2C/T97C/C214A light chain was elucidated by MD simulations. Association of 3D-DS dimers was elucidated using unbiased MD simulations, providing insights at the atomic level. These results demonstrate stabilization of the 3D-DS dimer structure of an antibody light chain and control of the monomer-tetramer transition by introducing a disulfide bond between the protomers.

Antibodies usually have two antigen recognition sites that are formed by the heavy and light chain variable regions. 3D-DS may occur at one antigen recognition site by controlling 3D-DS, whereas the antigen recognition ability can be maintained at the other site without undergoing 3D-DS. In fact, stabilization of 3D-DS structures has been reported by designing the hinge loop.38,45,46 Although there are several methods to link antibodies to construct bispecific antibodies, such as chemical modification and protein fusion, 3D-DS would be an alternative method to link antibodies without using other molecules or peptide linkers. 3D-DS combined with disulfide bond introduction may be useful for the construction of future bispecific antibodies.

Materials and Methods

Construction of the Plasmid Encoding #4 V2C/T97C/C214A

The pET-20b(+) vector plasmid encoding the V2C/T97C/C214A (Kabat numbering) variant (#4 V2C/T97C/C214A) of human antibody light chain #4, which belongs to subgroup II of the human kappa light chain, was constructed through two consecutive polymerase chain reaction (PCR) using the PrimeSTAR Max DNA Polymerase Mutagenesis Kit (Takara Bio) with forward and reverse primers (Eurofins Genomics, Japan; Table S2). The plasmid that encoded the C214A variant of #4 (#4C214A) between the NcoI and XhoI sites of the pET-20b(+) vector plasmid was used as a template.47 In the first PCR, The T97C point mutation was performed, which produced the plasmid encoding the T97C/C214A variant. Subsequently, the constructed plasmid was used as a template for the V2C point mutation in the second PCR, producing a plasmid encoding #4 V2C/T97C/C214A. DNA target genes were labeled using the BigDye Terminator v3.1 Cycle Sequencing Kit (Applied Biosystems, Inc., Foster City, CA, USA) and verified using an ABI PRISM 3100 Genetic Analyzer system (Applied Biosystems, Inc.). The amino acid and DNA sequences of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A are summarized in Table S3.

Expression and Purification of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A

The plasmid encoding #4 V2C/T97C/C214A was transformed into E. coli BL21(DE3) pLysS cells. The E. coli cells were precultured in 100 mL Luria–Bertani (LB) media containing 100 μg/mL ampicillin in a 500 mL flask at 37 °C for 21–22 h until the OD at 600 nm reached approximately 3.5–4.0. The precultured solution (50 mL) was transferred into 2 L LB media containing 100 μg/mL ampicillin in a 5 L flask, and the cells were cultured at 18 °C. When the OD at 600 nm reached approximately 0.6–0.8 after 6–7 h of culture, isopropyl β-d-1-thiogalactopyranoside (Wako; final concentration 800 μM) was added to the culture to induce overexpression of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A. The culture was further incubated until the OD at 600 nm reached 2.5–3.5.

#4 V2C/T97C/C214A was purified according to a previously reported method with modifications.47 The cells were harvested by centrifugation (9000 rpm, 4 °C, 12 min). After the cell pellet was frozen at −30 °C and thawed slowly under running water, the cells were resuspended in 25 mM Tris–HCl buffer, pH 8.0, containing 20 mM imidazole and 250 mM NaCl, where 3 mL of the buffer was used for 1 g of the cell pellet. The cells were disrupted on an ice bath by sonication for 2 min (1 s on and 1 s off cycle for 4 min) three times at 10 min intervals. The lysed cells were centrifuged (18,000 rpm, 4 °C, 20 min), and the supernatant was filtrated using a membrane filter (pore size 0.22 μm). The obtained solution was purified by nickel-chelating chromatography (HisTrap HP column, 5 mL, Cytiva) using a fast protein liquid chromatography (FPLC) system (BioLogic DuoFlow 10, Bio-Rad) (flow rate, 5.0 mL/min; monitoring wavelength, 280 nm; temperature, 4 °C). The buffer gradient consisted of 25 mM Tris–HCl buffer, pH 8.0, containing 250 mM NaCl and the same buffer, containing 250 mM NaCl and 300 mM imidazole. After dialysis of the solution containing #4 V2C/T97C/C214A against 50 mM sodium acetate buffer, pH 5.5, it was subjected to cation exchange chromatography (HiTrap SP HP column, 5 mL, Cytiva) using the same FPLC system (flow rate, 1.0 mL/min; monitoring wavelength, 280 nm; gradient, 50 mM sodium acetate buffer, pH 5.5, and the same buffer containing 1 M NaCl; temperature, 4 °C), where the monomer eluted earlier than the tetramer with lower ionic strengths. The tetramer was further purified by cation exchange chromatography (Mono S 5/50 column, Cytiva) using the FPLC system (BioLogic DuoFlow 10) (flow rate, 0.6 mL/min; monitoring wavelength, 280 nm; gradient, 50 mM sodium acetate buffer, pH 5.5, and the same buffer containing 300 mM Na2SO4; temperature, 4 °C). The buffer was replaced with 10 mM phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), using an Amicon Ultra-15 (MWCO 10,000 NMWL, Merck, Millipore). The monomer and tetramer were purified individually by SEC (HiLoad 26/60 Superdex 200 pg, Cytiva) using the FPLC system (BioLogic DuoFlow 10) (flow rate, 2.5 mL/min; monitoring wavelength, 280 nm; buffer, PBS; temperature, 4 °C). Protein purity was checked using SDS-PAGE on a 14% acrylamide gel. The concentration of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A was determined using a BioSpec-nano Spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, Japan) with the molar extinction coefficient of 23.3 mM–1 cm–1 at 280 nm calculated from the contents of Trp, Tyr, and disulfide bond (produced by Cys).48,49

Structure Prediction of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A

The predicted 3D structures of the #4 V2C/T97C/C214A monomer and the 3D-DS dimer were obtained using the ColabFold ver. 1.5.5 software (https://colab.research.google.com). The crystal structure of the #4C214A VL tetramer (PDB ID: 8KAD) was used as a template.49 The amino acid sequences (Table S3) were used as the input for the prediction.

SEC Analysis

A 100 μM (monomer unit) #4 V2C/T97C/C214A tetramer solution was incubated at 25 °C for 20 h and subsequently analyzed by SEC with a Superdex 200 Increase 10/300 GL column (Cytiva) using an HPLC system. The HPLC system was equipped with a diode array detector (SPDM20A, Shimadzu), a liquid chromatograph pump (LC-20AB, Shimadzu), degasser (DGU-20A3, Shimadzu), and an autosampler (SIL-20AC HT, Shimadzu). The size-exclusion HPLC maintained a flow rate of 0.6 mL/min, a monitoring UV–vis detector settled at 280 nm, and PBS, at a temperature of 4 °C. The #4 V2C/T97C/C214A tetramer solution was further diluted to a concentration of 6 μM (monomer unit), incubated at 25 °C for 20 h, and reanalyzed by SEC. The #4 V2C/T97C/C214A monomer fraction at 100 μM was incubated at 25 °C for 20 h and analyzed under same SEC conditions.

SEC-MALS Analysis of #4 V2C/T97C/C214A

#4 V2C/T97C/C214A was analyzed by SEC-MALS using a Superdex 200 Increase 10/300 column equipped with a miniDAWN detector (Wyatt Technology Corporation) and an RID-20A refractive index detector (Shimadzu). The column was equilibrated with PBS and operated at a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min at 25 °C. A total volume of 100 μL of the #4 V2C/T97C/C214A solution at 10 μM (monomer unit) was employed for each sample. Data processing and analysis were performed using the Astra X software (Wyatt Technologies), for which a typical dn/dc value for proteins (0.185 mL/g) was assumed.

Reduction and Reoxidation of Disulfide Bonds in #4 V2C/T97C/C214A

The #4 V2C/T97C/C214A tetramer (60 μM, monomer unit) in 100 μL PBS was reduced using 500 mM DTT at 15 °C for 3 h. The #4 V2C/T97C/C214A tetramer solution was applied to a PD-10 column to remove the DTT. The protein solution was concentrated to 20 μM, followed by incubation at 4 °C to reoxidize the Cys residues to reconstruct the disulfide bond. The protein solution after reduction and those after reoxidation for 22 and 40 h were analyzed by SEC using the HPLC system under the same conditions as above and SDS-PAGE.

Molecular Dynamics Simulation

The 3D model structures of the #4 V2C/T97C VL were obtained in 3D-DS dimeric and tetrameric states using ColabFold.50 MD simulations were performed with the model structures for 1 μs with three independent replicates using the AMBER 2251 software under PBC. The protonation states of the amino acids were assigned using Propka via the PDB 2PQR Web server.52 The dimer and tetramer structures were solvated in a TIP3P water cubic box,53 Na+ ions were added to neutralize the system, and disulfide bonds were created using tLEaP.54 The MD conditions for these systems were similar to those in our previous study.55 Briefly, the MD simulation was initiated with the 3D-DS dimer structure. The system was first minimized using 1,500 steps of steepest descent followed by 1500 steps of conjugate gradient. After initial energy minimization, the system was heated to 300 K over 200 ps under NVT (constant volume and temperature) conditions. Subsequently, the system entered the 1 μs production phase, performed under NPT conditions. The MD trajectory of the #4 V2C/T97C VL tetramer was examined to elucidate intermolecular hydrophobic interactions of the tetramer during MD simulations, utilizing PyContact.56 In PyContact, the distance criterion for hydrophobic interaction analysis was set at 4.5 Å, capturing both strong interactions with closer distances (approximately 4.0 Å) and weaker interactions up to 5.0 Å, which reflected less direct hydrophobic contacts.56 The trajectory analysis was conducted, including assessments of RMSD, #Atom contact, and HB interactions using the CPPTRAJ module.57 Furthermore, conformational changes within the dimer system were investigated using PCA.58 DBSCAN clustering was employed to identify clusters of arbitrary shapes and effectively handle noise data implemented in the Scikit-learn package59 for data clustering analysis.60 The residue network interactions were generated using the Residue Interaction Network Generator v3.061 and visualized by Cytoscape 3.10.1.62

To explore the tetramerization of 3D-DS dimers, an unbiased MD simulation lasting for 1 μs was conducted. In this simulation, two 3D-DS dimers (3D-DSA/B and 3D-DSC/D) were positioned 75 Å apart within the same PBC box to ensure that the initial structure of each dimer did not interaction. Subsequently, a classical MD simulation of this system was conducted for 0.6 μs using the same procedure as described above. Subsequently, the Cys2–Cys97 disulfide bonds in both chains were reduced and prolonged for the simulation up to 1 μs. The 2D-FEL profile was conducted based on the MSM using PyEMMA.63 CPPTRAJ was used to calculate collective variables, including the distance between the center of mass of 3D-DSA/B and 3D-DSC/D (dCOMA/B···C/D) plotted versus (i) #Atom contact between 3D-DS dimers, (ii) Cα-RMSD of chain A/B (), and (iii) Cα-RMSD of chain C/D (). The 3D structures were visualized using VMD 1.9.6.64

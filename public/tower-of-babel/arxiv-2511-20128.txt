Cauchy problem for a Schrödinger-type equation related to the Riemann zeta function
Mohamed Bensaid

Abstract
We study the Cauchy problem in the space H 1 ​ ( Σ ) H^{1}(\Sigma) for a nonlinear damped Schrödinger equation of the form i ​ u t + Δ ​ u + i ​ λ ​ u ​ ζ ​ ( | u | + 1 ) = 0 , u ⁡ ( 0 , x ) = u 0 , iu_{t}+\Delta u+i\lambda u\,\zeta(|u|+1)=0,\quad u(0,x)=u_{0}, where ζ \zeta denotes the Riemann zeta function. This equation involves a damping term inspired by a fundamental object from number theory. We first establish the uniqueness of solutions in the sense of distributions. Then, by considering a regularized problem, we prove the existence of a global solution in H 1 ​ ( Σ ) H^{1}(\Sigma) , using uniform energy estimates and compactness arguments. Finally, we show that the limiting solution indeed satisfies the original equation in the weak sense,In the last part of this work, we provide an explicit estimate for the mass associated with the equation (E0) i ​ v t + Δ ​ v + i ​ λ ​ v | v | = 0 , v ⁡ ( 0 , x ) = v 0 . iv_{t}+\Delta v+i\lambda\frac{v}{|v|}=0,\qquad v(0,x)=v_{0}.



Introduction

The Schrödinger equation plays a central role in mathematical physics, particularly in the modeling of quantum phenomena. When nonlinear damping terms are added, it also allows the description of dissipative effects, leading to rich and complex dynamical behaviors. In this work, we study a Cauchy problem associated with a damped version of the Schrödinger equation, where the damping depends on a function related to the famous Riemann zeta function.

The Riemann zeta function, initially defined for
\real(s)>1
by

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}},

plays a fundamental role in number theory, notably through its deep connection with the distribution of prime numbers. Its analytic continuation, as well as the famous Riemann hypothesis concerning the location of its nontrivial zeros, constitute one of the most important problems in modern mathematics.

In our study, we consider a damping term defined in terms of the function
\zeta(x)
. We first establish the uniqueness of solutions to the problem in the sense of distributions, before employing a regularization approach to prove the existence of weak solutions in
H^{1}
.

Furthermore, we consider a Riemannian manifold
\Sigma
, which we assume to be compact and of finite dimension
d
. This is necessary because, if we work in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the function
x\mapsto x\,\zeta(x+1)
no longer belongs to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, making the control of the nonlinear term more delicate.

As
x\to 0
, we have the asymptotic behaviour

\zeta(x+1)\sim\frac{1}{x}.

In this context, the Cauchy theory in
H^{1}(\Sigma)
for the equation

(E0)

iv_{t}+\Delta v+i\lambda\frac{v}{|v|}=0,\qquad v(0,x)=v_{0},

was established in
[
3
]
by Rémi Carles and Clément Gallo.
In their paper, Carles and Gallo prove only the inequality for the mass,

\|v(t)\|_{2}\leq\|v_{0}\|_{2},

since one computes

\partial_{t}\|v(t)\|_{2}^{2}=-2\lambda\int|v(t)|\,dx.

In our work, we provide an explicit estimate for this mass quantity.
To obtain it, we make use of the behaviour of the Riemann zeta function.
We first discuss the Cauchy problem for the zeta–NLS equation, and then we derive the mass estimate directly.

In the final section, we also study the nonlinearity combined with a logarithmic term.

Construction of the Solution in
H^{1}(\Sigma)

Let
(\Sigma,g)
be a compact Riemannian surface. We consider the Cauchy problem

(
E
)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+i\lambda\,u\,\zeta(|u|+1)=0,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Sigma,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\Sigma),\end{cases}

where
\lambda>0
and
\zeta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the Riemann zeta function.

We construct solutions via a regularization by adding a parameter
\varepsilon>0
:

(
E_{\varepsilon}
)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}+\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+i\lambda\,u_{\varepsilon}\,\zeta(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon)=0,\\
u_{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

Proposition 1

(Uniqueness)
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{1}(\Sigma))
be two (distributional) solutions of (

E

) with the same initial data. If
\lambda>0
, then
u_{1}\equiv u_{2}
.

Proof.

Set
w=u_{1}-u_{2}
. Since
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are solutions, we have

i\partial_{t}w+\Delta w+i\lambda\big(u_{1}\zeta(|u_{1}|+1)-u_{2}\zeta(|u_{2}|+1)\big)=0.

We take the
L^{2}
inner product with
w
and consider the real part:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}=\real\langle\partial_{t}w,w\rangle=\real\langle-i\Delta w-\lambda(u_{1}\zeta(|u_{1}|+1)-u_{2}\zeta(|u_{2}|+1)),w\big\rangle.

The term containing
\Delta
is purely imaginary, so its real part vanishes. Hence,

\frac{d}{dt}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}=-2\lambda\,\real\langle u_{1}\zeta(|u_{1}|+1)-u_{2}\zeta(|u_{2}|+1),w\rangle.

To conclude, we use the following monotonicity lemma (with
f(x)=x\mapsto x\zeta(x+1)
):

\real((uf(|u|)-vf(|v|))(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\big)\geq 0\qquad\forall u,v\in\mathbb{C}.

This lemma (proved below) implies that the right-hand side is
\leq 0
, hence

\frac{d}{dt}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}\leq 0.

Since
w(0)=0
(same initial data), it follows that
\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}\equiv 0
, and therefore
u_{1}\equiv u_{2}
.

\Box

Lemma 2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a function such that
xf(x)
is non-decreasing. Then, for all
u,v\in\mathbb{C}
,

\real((uf(|u|)-vf(|v|))(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\big)\geq 0.

Proof.

Expanding the real part gives

\displaystyle\real((uf(|u|)-vf(|v|))(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\big)

\displaystyle=|u|^{2}f(|u|)+|v|^{2}f(|v|)-\real(u\overline{v})(f(|u|)+f(|v|))

\displaystyle\geq|u|^{2}f(|u|)+|v|^{2}f(|v|)-|u||v|(f(|u|)+f(|v|))

\displaystyle=(|u|-|v|)\big(|u|f(|u|)-|v|f(|v|)\big).

The product above is non-negative since
x\mapsto xf(x)
is non-decreasing, which proves the inequality.

\Box

H^{1}(\Sigma)
. Consider the following regularized problem:

(E_{\epsilon}):\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon}+\Delta u_{\epsilon}+i\lambda u_{\epsilon}\zeta(|u_{\epsilon}|+1+\epsilon)=0,\\
u_{\epsilon}(0,x)=u_{0}.\end{array}\right.

For a fixed
\varepsilon>0
, equation (

E
ε

. Therefore, by a fixed-point argument (see Chapter 3 of
[
2
]
), there exists a maximal solution

u_{\varepsilon}\in C([0,T_{\varepsilon});H^{1}(\Sigma))\cap C^{1}([0,T_{\varepsilon});H^{-1}(\Sigma)).

The estimates below show that
T_{\varepsilon}=+\infty
and provide uniform bounds with respect to
\varepsilon
.

Multiplying (

E
ε

) by
\overline{u_{\varepsilon}}
, integrating over
\Sigma
, and taking the real part yields

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}=-\lambda\int_{\Sigma}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\zeta(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon)\,dx.

Under the assumption
\lambda>0
and if
\zeta(\cdot)\geq 0
(or if the product is non-negative), we obtain in particular

(1)

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)},\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Thus, the mass is uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon
.

Lemma 3
.

(2)

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)},\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Proof.

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}=\real\langle\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon},-\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\rangle.

Replacing
\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=-i\Delta u_{\varepsilon}-\lambda u_{\varepsilon}\zeta(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon)
, the term involving
\Delta
is purely imaginary and vanishes, giving

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}=-\lambda\,\real\langle u_{\varepsilon}\zeta(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon),\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\rangle.

By integration by parts,

\real\langle u_{\varepsilon}\zeta(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon),\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\rangle=-\int_{\Sigma}\nabla\big(u_{\varepsilon}\zeta(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon)\big)\cdot\overline{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}\,dx.

Expanding the gradient and taking the real part, we obtain

(3)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}=-\lambda\int_{\Sigma}\zeta(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\lambda\int_{\Sigma}|u_{\varepsilon}|\zeta^{\prime}(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon)\real\frac{u_{\varepsilon}\nabla|u_{\varepsilon}|\cdot\overline{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}}{|u_{\varepsilon}|}\,dx.

By Lemma
19
, the function
f:x\mapsto x\zeta(x+1+\epsilon)
is non-decreasing, so
-f^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
. In particular,

-\zeta(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\epsilon)\leq|u_{\varepsilon}|\zeta^{\prime}(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\epsilon)\leq 0.

Thus,

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}\leq\lambda\int_{\Sigma}|u_{\varepsilon}|\zeta^{\prime}(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon)\left(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\real\frac{u_{\varepsilon}\nabla|u_{\varepsilon}|\cdot\overline{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}}{|u_{\varepsilon}|}\right)dx\leq 0.

Remark 1
.

Here we used that

\left|\real\frac{u_{\varepsilon}\nabla|u_{\varepsilon}|\cdot\overline{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}}{|u_{\varepsilon}|}\right|\leq|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2},\quad\zeta^{\prime}(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon)\leq 0.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus, the lemma follows:

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)},\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

\Box

The inequalities (

Lemma 4
.

Up to extraction of a subsequence, the family
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
converges in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];L^{2}(\Sigma))
, and the limit is denoted by
u
.

Proof.

From the previous estimates, the family
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{0<\varepsilon<1}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{1}(\Sigma))
. Moreover, from equation (

E
ε

),

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=-i\Delta u_{\varepsilon}-\lambda u_{\varepsilon}\zeta(|u_{\varepsilon}|+1+\varepsilon).

The first term belongs to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{-1}(\Sigma))

\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{-1}

\sup_{\varepsilon}\|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Sigma))}<\infty.

We then apply the Aubin–Lions lemma (compactness in time): since

H^{1}(\Sigma)\overset{\text{compact}}{\hookrightarrow}L^{2}(\Sigma)\overset{\text{continuous}}{\hookrightarrow}H^{-1}(\Sigma),

and
(u_{\varepsilon})
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Sigma))

(\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon})
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Sigma))
, there exists a subsequence (still denoted
u_{\varepsilon}
) and a function

u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Sigma))

u_{\varepsilon}\to u\quad\text{in }C([0,T];L^{2}(\Sigma)).

Lemma 5
.

The function
u
, defined in Lemma
4
, satisfies:

1.

u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{1}(\Sigma))
.

2.

u_{\epsilon}(t)\rightharpoonup u(t)
weakly in
H^{1}(\Sigma)
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Proof.

1.

For any
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, by integration by parts and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:

\left|\int_{\Sigma}u_{\epsilon}\nabla\phi\,dx\right|\leq M\|\phi\|_{2}.

Passing to the limit, we deduce:

\left|\int_{\Sigma}u\nabla\phi\,dx\right|\leq M\|\phi\|_{2},

which shows that
u(t)\in H^{1}(\Sigma)
and
\|\nabla u(t)\|_{2}\leq M
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

2.

To show weak convergence in
H^{1}(\Sigma)
: for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma)
,

\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u_{\epsilon}(t)\,\phi\,dx\to_{\epsilon}\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u(t)\,\phi\,dx.

Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma)
is dense in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
, for any
\phi\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
and
\delta>0
, choose
\phi_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that

\|\phi_{n}-\phi\|_{2}<\delta.

Then,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\,\phi-\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u\,\phi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u_{\epsilon}(\phi-\phi_{n})\right|+\left|\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\phi_{n}-\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u\phi_{n}\right|+\left|\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u(\phi_{n}-\phi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq M\delta+\left|\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\phi_{n}-\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u\phi_{n}\right|+\|\nabla u\|_{2}\delta.

Taking the limit superior as
\epsilon\to 0
gives

\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\left|\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\,\phi-\int_{\Sigma}\nabla u\,\phi\right|\leq(M+\|\nabla u\|_{2})\delta.

Since
\delta>0
is arbitrary, this proves weak convergence in
H^{1}(\Sigma)
.

Lemma 6
.

For all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

u_{\epsilon}(t)\,\zeta(|u_{\epsilon}(t)|+1+\epsilon)\to u(t)\,\zeta(|u(t)|+1)\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(\Sigma).

Proof.

For all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Sigma
, we have

|u_{\epsilon}(t,x)\zeta(|u_{\epsilon}|+1+\epsilon)|^{2}\leq 2\big(1+|u_{\epsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\big).

is bounded and

, the sequence
u_{\epsilon}\zeta(|u_{\epsilon}|+1+\epsilon)

such that

u_{\epsilon}\zeta(|u_{\epsilon}|+1+\epsilon)\;\;\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\;\;V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}(\Sigma)).

Moreover, up to a subsequence,

u_{\epsilon}\to u\quad\text{in}\quad C([0,T],L^{2}(\Sigma)).

Hence, for

\Box

Let
u

iu_{t}+\Delta u+i\lambda u\zeta(|u|+1)=0\quad\text{in }H^{-1}(\Sigma),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Let

and

Lemma 8
.

Let
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},H^{1}(\Sigma))
be the solution obtained in Theorem
7
. Then, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq e^{-\lambda t}\,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}.

In particular,

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }t\to+\infty.

Proof.

We have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}=-\lambda\int_{\Sigma}|u(t)|^{2}\,\zeta(|u(t)|+1)\,dx\leq-\lambda\int_{\Sigma}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx,

where the last inequality follows from
\zeta(|u|+1)\geq 1
.
By Grönwall’s lemma, it follows that

\Box

Lemma 9
.

There exists
c>0
such that, for all
z,s\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,

\real((f(z)-f(s))(\overline{z}-\overline{s})\big)\geq c|z-s|^{2}.

where
f(z):=z\zeta(\absolutevalue{z}+1)

Proof.

We write

f(z)-f(s)=\int_{0}^{1}Df(s+t(z-s))\cdot(z-s)\,dt,

where

Df(w)\cdot h=\zeta(|w|+1)h+w\,\zeta^{\prime}(|w|+1)\frac{\real(\overline{w}h)}{|w|}.

Set
\delta_{t}=s+t(z-s)
and
h=z-s
. Then

\real((f(z)-f(s))(\overline{z}-\overline{s})\big)=\real\int_{0}^{1}\left(\zeta(|\delta_{t}|+1)|h|^{2}+\zeta^{\prime}(|\delta_{t}|+1)\frac{(\real(\overline{\delta_{t}}h))^{2}}{|\delta_{t}|}\right)dt.

Since
\zeta^{\prime}(\cdot)<0
, using
(\real(\overline{\delta_{t}}h))^{2}\leq|\delta_{t}|^{2}|h|^{2}
, we get

\real((f(z)-f(s))(\overline{z}-\overline{s})\big)\geq\int_{0}^{1}(\zeta(|\delta_{t}|+1)+|\delta_{t}|\,\zeta^{\prime}(|\delta_{t}|+1))|h|^{2}\,dt.

On the other hand, for
r>0
,

\zeta(r+1)+r\zeta^{\prime}(r+1)=1+\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{1-r\ln n}{n^{r+1}}\geq 1+\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1-r\ln t}{t^{r+1}}dt=1-\frac{\ln 2}{2^{r}}\geq 1-\ln 2.

Hence,

\real((f(z)-f(s))(\overline{z}-\overline{s})\big)\geq c|z-s|^{2},

with
c:=1-\ln 2>0
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\,\real\int(f(u)-\frac{v}{|v|})\,(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\,dx

\displaystyle=-\lambda\,\real\int(f(u)-f(v))\,(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\,dx-\lambda\,\real\int(f(v)-\frac{v}{|v|})\,(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\,dx

.

\left|\imaginary\int_{\Sigma}\nabla(u_{\varepsilon}\log(|u_\varepsilon|+\varepsilon))\cdot\overline{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}\right|\leq\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx.

Applying Grönwall’s inequality, we obtain

(6)

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq e^{|\mu|t}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}.

The estimates (
5
)–(
6
) give

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma)}\leq C_{T},

so that the family
(u_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon<1}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Sigma))
.
Repeating the compactness argument as in the NLS-Zeta case allows us to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 14
.

Given
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Sigma)
, there exists a unique solution
u
satisfying

u\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{1}(\Sigma))\cap W^{1,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{-1}(\Sigma)).

Moreover,

(7)

iu_{t}+\Delta u+i\lambda\,u\,\zeta(|u|+1)+\mu\,u\,\log(|u|)=0\quad\text{in }H^{-1}(\Sigma),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Estimates for the Riemann Zeta Function

Lemma 15
.

For all
x>1
, we have

\frac{1}{x-1}\leq\zeta(x)\leq\frac{1}{x-1}+1.

Proof.

Recall that for any continuous decreasing function
f
, we have

f(n)\leq\int_{n-1}^{n}f(x)\,dx\leq f(n-1).

Applying this to
f(x)=1/x^{s}
for
s>1
, which is decreasing, gives the desired bounds.

\Box

Lemma 16
.

For all
x>1
,

|\zeta^{\prime}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\ln(z)}{z^{x}}\,dz.

In particular,

|\zeta^{\prime}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{\ln(2)\,(x-1)^{2}}.

Proof.

The same technique as in the previous lemma applies, except here
f(z)=\ln(z)/z^{x}
is not decreasing on
(1,2]
.

We write

|\zeta^{\prime}(x)|\leq\frac{\ln 2}{2^{x}}+\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\ln(z)}{z^{x}}\,dz.

Moreover, for
z\in(1,2)
,
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{2^{x}}{z^{x}}=\infty
, so there exists
B>0
such that for all
x>B
,

\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\ln(z)}{z^{x}}\,dz\geq\frac{\ln 2}{2^{x}}.

For
x\in(1,B]
, we have

\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{\ln(z)}{z^{x}}\,dz\geq\frac{\ln 2}{2^{x}}.

\Box

Lemma 17
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
x,y>0
,

|\zeta(x+1)-\zeta(y+1)|\leq C\frac{|x-y|}{xy}.

Proof.

We have

Lemma 18
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
x,y>0
and all
\epsilon,\delta>0
, we have

\big|x\zeta(x+1+\epsilon)-y\zeta(y+1+\delta)\big|\leq C\frac{|x-y|+|\epsilon-\delta|}{\min(x,y)}.

Proof.

Using the fundamental theorem of calculus along a linear path:

\displaystyle|x\zeta(x+1+\epsilon)-y\zeta(y+1+\delta)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}\Big((tx+(1-t)y)\zeta(t(x+\epsilon)+(1-t)(y+\delta)+1)\Big)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}|x-y|\,\zeta(\dots)\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}(tx+(1-t)y)|(x-y)+(\epsilon-\delta)|\,|\zeta^{\prime}(\dots)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|x-y|}{\min(x,y)}+C\frac{|x-y|+|\epsilon-\delta|}{\min(x,y)}.

\Box

Lemma 19
.

For all
\epsilon\geq 0
, the function

x\mapsto x\zeta(x+1+\epsilon)

is increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Proof.

Set
f(x)=x\zeta(x+1+\epsilon)
, then

f^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1-x\ln(n)}{n^{1+x+\epsilon}}.

For

,

\frac{1-x\ln(n)}{n^{1+x+\epsilon}}\geq\frac{1-x\ln(t)}{t^{1+x+\epsilon}}.

Hence

f^{\prime}(x)\geq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1-x\ln(t)}{t^{1+x+\epsilon}}dt1+\frac{\epsilon}{(x+\epsilon)^{2}}-\frac{x}{x+\epsilon}\ln(2)\geq 1-\ln(2)>0.

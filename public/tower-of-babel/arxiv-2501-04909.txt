On the Heat equation involving a Grushin operator in Marcinkiewicz spaces Thanks: *
Alessia E. Kogoj, Maria E. Lima, Arlúcio Viana

Abstract
. In this work, we give sufficient conditions for the existence and uniqueness of the heat equation involving the operator Δ 𝒢 = 1 2 ​ ( Δ x + | x | 2 ​ Δ y ) \Delta_{\mathcal{G}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2}\Delta_{y}\right) in Marcinkiewicz spaces. Furthermore, we provide sufficient conditions for the existence of positive, symmetric, and self-similar solutions.

1.
Introduction and main results

Since Fujita’s paper
[
13
]
, the semilinear heat equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}(x,t)=\Delta u(x,t)+|u(x,t)|^{\rho-1}u(x,t),\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},

\rho>1
, is of great mathematical interest.

It is well-known that if
u_{0}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be nonnegative and nonzero, then

(1)

If
1<\rho<1+\frac{2}{N}
, there exists no positive global solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).

(2)

If
\rho>1+\frac{2}{N}
, there exists
u_{0}\in L^{\frac{N}{2}(\rho-1)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that there exists a global positive solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).

The critical case
\rho=1+\frac{2}{N}
was resolved by Weissler
[
31
]
. Initial conditions in Lebesgue spaces were considered in
[
5
,
31
]
.

In this work, we will replace the classical Laplacian with the operator

(1.3)

\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2}\Delta_{y}\right),

where
\Delta_{x},\Delta_{y}
denote the classical Laplacian in the variables
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, respectively.

This operator, nowadays called Grushin-type (see
[
16
,
17
]
), actually belongs to the general class of operators studied by Hörmander in
[
18
]
and is hypoelliptic. Moreover, it is also a particular case of the degenerate elliptic operators studied in
[
19
]
by Kogoj and Lanconelli. The first to introduce and study a metric and a consequent underlying sub-Riemannian structure for such operators were Franchi and Lanconelli in their seminal papers in the early 1980s

[
11
,
10
,
12
]
.
In recent years, many works have appeared in the literature dealing with second-order linear and semilinear degenerate elliptic PDOs, which fall into this class. We refer to
[
1
,
3
,
8
,
20
,
23
]
and the references therein for recent results involving the Grushin-type operators.

We are dealing with the following problem

(1.4)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}u+|u|^{\rho-1}u,\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N+k},

\displaystyle u(0)=u_{0},\ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N+k}.

In bounded domains, we can find well-posedness, long-time dynamics, and the existence of attractors for semilinear equations involving degenerate elliptic operators containing the Grushin ones in
[
21
,
22
,
25
]
. In particular, the authors of
[
25
]
applied the Galerkin method to prove the existence of solutions of parabolic and pseudo-parabolic equations associated with Hörmander-type operators, where they used Sobolev-type estimates, which are available for bounded domains. They also proved the existence and upper continuity of attractors.

In
[
27
]
, Oliveira and Viana prove existence, uniqueness, continuous dependence and blowup alternative of local mild solutions for (
1.4
)–(
1.4
) with initial conditions in Lebesgue spaces. Also, they obtain the existence of global solutions in the special case of
u_{0}\in L^{\frac{N}{2}(\rho-1)}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
with a sufficiently small norm.

In this manuscript, by working in Marcinkiewicz spaces, we allow larger (in Lebesgue norm) initial data to be taken into account to obtain global solutions. More precisely, we give sufficient conditions for the existence and uniqueness of mild solutions for (
1.4
)–(
1.4
), with initial conditions in the critical Marcinkiewicz space
L^{(p,\infty)}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, with

(1.5)

p=\frac{N+2k}{2}(\rho-1).

Then, we prove the existence of positive, symmetric and self-similar solutions. Indeed, for example, if
u_{0}(x,y)=\varepsilon|x|^{-\frac{2}{\rho-1}}|y|^{-\frac{1}{\rho-1}}
and
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, Theorems
1.1
(a) and
1.2
gives the existence of a positive, self-similar and symmetric solution of (
1.4
)–(
1.4
) in
L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{(p,\infty)})
. Notice that such an initial condition has an infinity
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
-norm, so we cannot apply those results in
[
27
]
. In other words, Marcinkiewicz spaces allow singular homogeneous initial conditions that generate positive, self-similar solutions. Moreover, Theorem
1.1
also gives sufficient conditions for uniqueness, regularity and time-decay of the mild solutions.

The number
N+2k
is the so-called homogeneous dimension attached to the operator in (
1.3
) (see
[
20
]
).

Here, we will rely on the

expression of the heat kernel of the Grushin operator (see e.g.

for

To prove this, the authors in
[
27
]
followed the Geometric Method in
[
6

to find the heat kernel of the heat equation with quadratic potential and then applied the partial Fourier transform on the variable

[
29

. More recently, Stempak
[
30

obtained that closed form for the kernel by applying the functional calculus of the Grushin operator and using the Hankel transform.

and the heat kernel (

2.
Key results

Most of the notations we use in this paper are standard. Lorentz and Marcinkiewicz spaces
L^{(p,q)}
and
L^{(p,\infty)}
are as defined in
[
4
,
9
]
. The norm in these spaces will be denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{(p,q)}
. Next, we collect the main properties of these spaces.

Consider a measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The
f^{*}
rearrangement function is defined by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\left\{s>0:m\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>s\right\}\right)\leq t\right\},\ t>0,

where
m
is the measure
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-Lebesgue. The averaging function
f^{**}
is defined by

f^{**}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)ds,\ t>0.

Let
0<p\leqslant\infty,0<q\leqslant\infty
. The Lorentz space,
L^{(p,q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, is the set of all measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that
\|f\|_{(p,q)}^{*}<\infty
, where

(2.1)

\|f\|^{*}_{(p,q)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\left[\frac{q}{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(t)\right)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}},\ t>0}&\text{ if }0<p<\infty,0<q<\infty,\\
\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(t),&\text{ if }0<p\leqslant\infty,q=\infty.\\
\end{array}\right.

Since
\|f\|^{*}_{(p,q)}
does not satisfy the triangular inequality,
L^{(p,q)}
is metrizable with the norm
\|f\|_{(p,q)}
given by

(2.2)

\|f\|_{(p,q)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{q}{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{**}(t)\right]^{q}dt/t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},&\text{ if }\ 1<p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty;\\
\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{**}(t),&\text{ if }\ 1<p\leq\infty,\ q=\infty.\end{array}\right.

When
q=\infty
, the space
L^{(p,\infty)}
is called the Marcinkiewics space or weak-
L^{p}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The spaces
L^{(p,q)}
with the norms
\|f\|_{(p,q)}
are Banach spaces and

(2.3)

\|f\|^{*}_{(p,q)}\leq\|f\|_{(p,q)}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|f\|^{*}_{(p,q)},

where
1<p\leq\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
. If, in addition,

be a

\displaystyle(L^{(p,q)})^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{(p,\infty)},\qquad 1<p<\infty,\ 0<q\leq 1,

\displaystyle(L^{(p,q)})^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})},\qquad 1<p<\infty,\ 0<q<\infty.

Moreover, given

, with
1<p<\infty
and

, exists

a measurable function such that

Let

(e)

The last comment concerns the nonexistence of global solutions for (
1.4
)–(
1.4
). For this, define the following energy functional associated with it:

(5.4)

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}u|^{2}\ dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}|u|^{p+1}dx.

Now, define the Sobolev space

H^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}):\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\},

endowed with the norm

\|u\|=\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}u\|_{L^{2}}.

Here
\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}=(\nabla_{x},|x|\nabla_{y})
denotes the intrinsic gradient of
\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}
. Let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap H_{\mathcal{G}}^{1}
be such that
E(u_{0})<0
. If the solution
u
belongs to
C((0,T);H_{\mathcal{G}}^{1})
, then we can apply the following Green identity. Let
B_{1}(R)
and
B_{2}(R)
be balls of radius
R>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
R^{k}
, respectively. For
B(R)=B_{1}(R)\times B_{2}(R)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+k}
, we have

\int_{B(R)}u\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}v=\int_{B_{2}(R)}\int_{S_{1}(R)}u\frac{\partial v}{\nu_{x}}+\int_{B_{1}(R)}\int_{S_{2}(R)}v\frac{\partial v}{\nu_{x}}-\int_{B(R)}\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}u\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}v.

In particular, for
v=u\in C_{0}^{\infty}
, for
R>0
, we get

\int_{B(R)}u\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}v=-\int_{B(R)}|\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}u|^{2}.

By density, those identities should work with
v,u\in H_{\mathcal{G}}^{1}
. Hence, if the solution
u\in C((0,T);H_{\mathcal{G}}^{1})\cap L^{\rho+1}
, then we can prove that
E(u(\cdot))\in C([0,T])\cap C^{1}((0,T))
and

\frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}u_{t}^{2}.

This can be proved by following
[
28
, Lemma 17.5]
with the Green identities above. From this on, the Levine concavity method
[
24
]
can be applied to show that
T:=T_{\max}\left(u_{0}\right)<\infty
.

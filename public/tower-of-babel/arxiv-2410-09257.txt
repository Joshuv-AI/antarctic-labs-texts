Two-person Positive Shortest Path Games Have Nash Equilibria in Pure Stationary Strategies
Endre Boros, Khaled Elbassioni, Vladimir Gurvich, Mikhail Vyalyi

Abstract
We prove that every finite two-person shortest path game, where the local cost of every move is positive for each player, has a Nash equilibrium (NE) in pure stationary strategies, which can be computed in polynomial time. We also extend the existence result to infinite graphs with finite out-degrees. Moreover, our proof gives that a terminal NE (in which the play is a path from the initial position to a terminal) exists provided at least one of the two players can guarantee reaching a terminal. If none of the players can do it, in other words, if each of the two players has a strategy that separates all terminals from the initial position s s , then, obviously, a cyclic NE exists, although its cost is infinite for both players, since we restrict ourselves to positive games. We conjecture that a terminal NE exists too, provided there exists a directed path from s s to a terminal. However, this is open. We extend our result to short paths interdiction games, where at each vertex, we allow one player to block some of the arcs and the other player to choose one of the non-blocked arcs. Assuming that blocking sets are chosen from an independence system given by an oracle, we give an algorithm for computing a NE in time O ​ ( | E | ​ ( log ⁡ | V | + τ ) ) O(|E|(\log|V|+\tau)) , where V V is the set of vertices, E E is the set of arcs, and τ \tau is the maximum time taken by the oracle on any input. Keywords: two-person shortest path games, Nash equilibrium in pure stationary strategies, short paths interdiction, independence system.

u\in V
. In this case, given a pair of strategies
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
, the play is defined by an
(s,t)
-path (or an
s
-lasso) in the subgraph defined by the arcs
\bigcup_{u\in V\setminus\{t\}}\sigma_{1}(u)\cap\sigma_{2}(u)
. Equivalently, we may also define the game by the family of dependent sets
\mathcal{I}^{*}
of player 1 and the family of independent sets
\mathcal{D}^{*}
of player 2.

In terms of algorithms, since the number of independent sets at each vertex can be exponential, we will need to specify how the independence systems can be succinctly described. Assume that, for each vertex
u\in V\setminus\{t\}
, the family of independent sets
\mathcal{I}(u)
is given by an

, that is, an algorithm that decides, for any given

, whether
X
is independent (equivalently,
X\in\mathcal{I}(u)
). For instance, we one can imagine a situation where the blocking of each arc
e\in E
incurs some cost
c(e)>0
, and there is a budget of
\delta(u)
at each vertex
u\in V\setminus\{t\}
such that the blocking cost at
u
cannot exceed
\delta(u).
In such a case, the independence system at
u
can be described by a linear inequality:
\mathcal{I}(u)=\big\{I\subseteq E(u)~|~\sum_{e\in I}c(e)\leq\delta(u)\big\}
.

Theorem
1
admits the following generalization.

Theorem 8

Every finite positive two-person SP interdiction game has a NE (in pure stationary strategies). Assuming that the independence system at each vertex is given by a polynomial time oracle, such a NE can be computed in polynomial time.

Potential Transformations

Out proofs rely heavily on the use of potential transformations defined as follows.
Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, a length function
r:E\mapsto\mathbb{R}
and a
potential
function

\phi:V\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, the potential transformation of

is defined by

\bar{r}(u,v)=r(u,v)+\phi(v)-\phi(u)~\text{ for all }~(u,v)\in E.

It is important to note that such potential transformation does not change the

(s,t)
-paths with respect to the total
r
-cost and fixed vertices

and

. In particular, applying such transformations to the costs of the two players in an SP game does not affect the existence of a NE in the game.

, and for vertices

, denote by

the subpath of

starting at
u\in V(p)
and ending at

.

4.2
A Polynomial-time Algorithm

Consider an SP interdiction game
\Gamma=(G=(V,E),s,t,\mathcal{I},r_{1},r_{2})
. Assume that, for each vertex
u\in V\setminus\{t\}
, the family of independent sets
\mathcal{I}(u)
is given by an independence oracle and denote by
\tau
the maximum time taken by the oracle on any set
X\subseteq E(u)
, over all
u\in V
. Any subgraph of
G
obtained by removing a subset of arcs
I(u)\in\mathcal{I}(u)
, at every
u\in V
, will be called
admissible
. We recall the following result, implicitly proved in
[
17
,
16
]
.

Lemma 14

Given a directed graph
G=(V,E)
with associated non-negative arc lengths
r:E\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and a target vertex
t
, there is an
O(|E|(\log|V|+\tau))
algorithm (Modified Dijkstra’s) that computes a potential function
\phi:V\to\mathbb{R}
, and independent sets
\widehat{I}(u)\in\mathcal{I}(u)
, for
u\in V\setminus\{t\}
, such that

\displaystyle\phi(u)

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle r(u,v)+\phi(v)\text{ for all $(u,v)\in\widehat{I}(u)$,}

(7a)

\displaystyle\phi(u)

\displaystyle\leq r(u,v)+\phi(v),\text{ for all $(u,v)\in E(u)\setminus\widehat{I}(u)$,}

(7b)

\displaystyle\phi(u)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle r(u,v_{u})+\phi(v_{u}),\text{ for some $(u,v_{u})\in E(u)\setminus\widehat{I}(u)$,}

(7c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(u)

\displaystyle\not\ni\{(u,v)\in E(u)~|~\phi(u)\geq r(u,v)+\phi(v)\}.

(7d)

Note that conditions (
7b
)-(
7d
) imply that

\phi(u)
is the maximum shortest
r
-distance from
u
to
t
in any admissible subgraph of
G
. Note also that there is
one
admissible graph which maximizes all such distances from all vertices to
t
.
For completeness,
we include a proof of Lemma
14
in the appendix.

We say that player

can force
+\infty
in an SP interdiction game, if it has a blocking strategy that prevents player
2
from reaching the terminal starting from

. A terminal NE is a NE in which the costs of both players are finite.

Lemma 15

Consider an SP interdiction game
\Gamma=(G=(V,E),s,t,\mathcal{I},r_{1},r_{2})
, in which player
1
cannot force
+\infty
. Then
\Gamma
has a terminal NE that can be computed in
O(|E|\log|V|+\tau|E|)

Proof
: Let
\phi_{2}
be the potential function computed in Lemma
14
with respect to the cost function
r=r_{2}
, and let
\widehat{I}(u)
, for
U\in V\setminus\{t\}
, be the obtained blocking sets. Let
B=\{u\in V~|~\phi_{2}(u)=+\infty\}
,
U=V\setminus B
, and denote by
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
the arc set in the subgraph induced by
U
.

Note that by our assumption, we have
s\in U
. Furthermore, player
1
has a strategy that prevents player
2
from leaving the set
B
, but none that forces entering it (from

\begin{array}[]{l}\forall u\in B,~\{(u,v)\in E(u)~|~v\in U\}\in\mathcal{I}(u),\\
\forall u\in U,~\{(u,v)\in E(u)~|~v\in U\}\not\in\mathcal{I}(u).\end{array}

(8)

We then use
\phi_{2}
and apply a potential transformation to the

r_{2}
-length
function in the subgraph induced by
U
.
Then for the transformed lengths
\bar{r}_{2}

that, for all

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq 0\displaystyle\text{ for all $(u,v)\in\widehat{I}(u)$,}

(9a)

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle\geq 0,\text{ for all $(u,v)\in E(u)\cap E^{\prime}\setminus\widehat{I}(u)$,}

(9b)

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{2}(u,v_{u})

\displaystyle=0\displaystyle,\text{ for some $(u,v_{u})\in E(u)\cap E^{\prime}\setminus\widehat{I}(u)$,}

(9c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(u)

\displaystyle\not\ni\{(u,v)\in E(u)\cap E^{\prime}~|~\bar{r}_{2}(u,v)\leq 0\}.

(9d)

Let
E^{\prime\prime}=\{(u,v)\in E^{\prime}~|~\bar{r}_{2}(u,v)\leq 0\}
and note that (

) (and the absence of

) implies that there is an

(s,t)
-path
in

.
With every

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\min_{(u,v^{\prime})\in E(u)\cap E^{\prime}\setminus I(u,v)}\bar{r}_{2}(u,v^{\prime}).

(10)

Next, let

-shortest

, and for

\sigma_{1}(u)=\begin{cases}I(u,v),&\text{ for }u\in V(p)\text{ such that }(u,v)\text{ is an arc of }p,\\
\widehat{I}(u),&\text{ for }u\in U\setminus V(p),\\
\{(u,v)\in E(u)~|~v\in U\},&\text{ for }u\in B.\end{cases}

Note that (
8
) implies that
\sigma_{1}
is well-defined. For
u\in V\setminus\{t\}

\sigma_{2}(u)=\begin{cases}E(u)\cap E^{\prime\prime},&\text{ for }u\in U,\\
D\in\mathcal{D}(u),&\text{ for }u\in B.\end{cases}

Note by (
9d

\sigma_{2}(u)\in\mathcal{D}(u)
.
We claim that the situation
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
is a terminal NE. Note that
p

(s,t)
-path in the graph

The definition of

Proof of Theorem
8
.

Consider an SP interdiction game
\Gamma=(G=(V,E),s,t,\mathcal{I},r_{1},r_{2})
. If both players can force
+\infty
, then the corresponding strategies define a (cyclic) NE. Such strategies can be computed in
O(|E|\log|V|+\tau|E|)
by applying Modified Dijkstra’s algorithm as in Lemma
14
. If player 1 cannot force
+\infty
, then a terminal NE exists by Lemma
15
. If player 2 cannot force
+\infty
, then in view of Remark
7
, we can consider the SP interdiction game defined by the dual independence system and conclude again by Lemma
15
that it has a terminal NE. The theorem follows.

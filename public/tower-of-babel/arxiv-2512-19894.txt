Equivariant Koszul Cohomology of Canonical Curves
Kostas Karagiannis, Aristides Kontogeorgis, Konstantia Manousou Sotiropoulou

Abstract
. This paper investigates the representation-theoretic structure of the Koszul cohomology of a smooth projective variety X X over an algebraically closed field k k , admitting an action of a finite group G G of order coprime to char ⁡ ( k ) {\rm char}(k) . Properties of G G -equivariant functors are employed to show that the associated Koszul complex is a complex of k ​ G kG -modules, and to generalize known dimension formulas to identities between virtual representations. In the case of canonical curves, explicit formulas are obtained by combining the theory of equivariant Euler characteristics and equivariant Riemann-Roch theorems with that of generating functions for Schur functors.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and historical background

The genealogy of the Koszul complex begins with Hilbert’s foundational 1890 paper
[
13
]
, where it appears implicitly in his proof of the famous syzygy theorem that bears his name. However, its formal algebraic structure was not codified until Jean-Louis Koszul’s 1950 paper
[
17
]
. Though his primary motivation was topological, Koszul’s treatment provided the abstract definitions and results that characterize the modern understanding of the complex today.

The modern era of the theory began with Mark Green, who introduced the geometric counterpart of the Koszul complex
[
10
]
and demonstrated that geometric properties of projective varieties could be reinterpreted through homological vanishing theorems. Building on this foundation, Green and Lazarsfeld extended several classical results concerning syzygies of projective varieties into the language of Koszul cohomology, see the surveys
[
11
]
and
[
18
]
. Subsequent breakthroughs eventually resolved influential conjectures on the subject, leading to a period of significant growth for the field; see the preface of
[
1
]
for a detailed account of this expansion and its various applications. Despite the depth of these developments, a direction that remains largely unexplored is the extent to which these results extend to the generalized setting where a finite group is permitted to act on the variety.

The paradigm of studying varieties through associated vector spaces naturally evolves into the study of group representations when a finite group acts on the variety via automorphisms. In this setting, the
G
-module structure of these spaces provides the key to understanding the underlying action. This is most classically illustrated by smooth projective curves, where the dictionary between geometry and function fields allows one to apply the machinery of Galois theory. By utilizing global-to-local methods, such as ramification theory and the study of decomposition groups, one can recover global geometric properties from the behavior of these field extensions.

Building on this perspective, the main aim of this paper is to investigate the behavior of the Koszul complex and its cohomology in the equivariant setting, where a finite group
G
acts on a variety
X
. Our focus lies primarily on the case of smooth projective curves, with particular emphasis on canonical curves. We seek to explore the interplay between the geometry of the group action and the representation-theoretic structure of the Koszul cohomology groups, with the goal of obtaining results in both directions.

1.2.
Outline of the paper

In Section 2, we consider a smooth irreducible scheme
X
over an algebraically closed field
k
acted upon by a finite group
G
whose order is coprime to the characteristic of
k
. After reviewing the definitions of
G
-equivariant quasicoherent sheaves and
G
-equivariant functors, we establish in Proposition
2.2.1
a general criterion for a functor to be
G
-equivariant. This allows us to verify in Proposition
2.3.1
and Proposition
2.4.2
that the constituent operations in Green and Lazarsfeld’s functorial construction of the Koszul complex are
G
-equivariant, while endowing them with canonical
G
-structures derived solely from the action on
X
. This framework culminates in Theorem
2.6.2
, in which we not only show that the Koszul complex is a complex of
kG
-modules, but also express the equivariant structure of its cohomology in terms of Zariski cohomology, lifting the classical construction of Green and Lazarsfeld into the equivariant setting.

In Section 3, we focus on canonical curves and provide a formula for differences of Koszul representations in Corollary
3.2.1
, motivated by the non-equivariant setting where such differences are explicitly computable via Riemann–Roch. Within the equivariant framework, this formula naturally splits into two distinct components. The first directly relates to the theory of equivariant Euler characteristics; to evaluate it, we use the equivariant Riemann–Roch theorem of Ellingsrud and Lønsted (Theorem
3.3.3
) and provide in Theorem
3.4.5
an explicit decomposition into a direct sum of irreducible representations. The second component remarkably turns out to be related to the theory of Schur functors, which are central to the representation theory of
\text{GL}_{n}
and are intimately connected to the combinatorics of Young tableaux. Since closed-form expressions for irreducible decompositions of such modules are unattainable due to the notorious difficulty of the plethysm problem, we provide explicit generating functions both for the character values of this module in Theorem
3.5.1
and for its class in the representation ring of
G
in Theorem
3.5.2
.

Lemma 2.1.2
.

{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
is an abelian category.

Proof.

This is straightforward and well known, see for example
[
22
, 1.4]
; we indicatively verify that

of a

\displaystyle\rho_{g}^{*}(\ker f)=\ker\rho_{g}^{*}(f)=\ker(\psi_{g}^{-1}\circ f\circ\phi_{g})=\ker(f\circ\phi_{g})\subseteq\phi_{g}^{-1}(\ker f).

\displaystyle\rho_{g}^{*}({\rm Im}f)={\rm Im}\rho_{g}^{*}(f)={\rm Im}(\psi_{g}^{-1}\circ f\circ\phi_{g})={\rm Im}(f\circ\phi_{g})\subseteq{\rm Im}f.

Hence, the restriction

of

\psi_{g}
induces a well-defined linearization

∎

G
-action on
H^{0}(V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X})
induced by that
G
-linearization is given by
\zeta_{g}(s)=(\theta\otimes c_{g})\circ\rho_{g}^{*}(s)\circ c_{g}^{-1}
.

If
f:V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}
is a
G
-morphism, then
\phi_{g}\circ\rho_{g}^{*}(f)=f\circ\kappa_{g}=f\circ(\theta_{g}\otimes c_{g})
. For
s\in H^{0}(V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X})
,

(\eta_{g}\circ H^{0}(f))(s)=\eta_{g}(f\circ s)=\phi_{g}\circ\rho_{g}^{*}(f\circ s)\circ c_{g}^{-1}=f\circ(\theta_{g}\otimes c_{g})\circ\rho_{g}^{*}(s)\circ c_{g}^{-1}=f\circ\zeta_{g}(s)=(H^{0}(f)\circ\zeta_{g})(s)

and thus
H^{0}(f)
is a
kG
-module homomorphism.

Conversely, the non-equivariant adjunction bijection
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm QC}(X)}(V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X},\mathscr{F})\cong{\rm Hom}_{k}(V,H^{0}(\mathscr{F}))
implies that any
k
-linear map
V\cong H^{0}(V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X})\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathscr{F})
must be of the form
H^{0}(f)
for some
f:V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}
. The computations above demonstrate that if
H^{0}(f)
is a
kG
-module homomorphism then

\eta_{g}(f\circ s)=f\circ\zeta_{g}(s)\Leftrightarrow\phi_{g}\circ\rho_{g}^{*}(f)\circ\rho_{g}^{*}(s)\circ c_{g}^{-1}=f\circ(\theta_{g}\otimes c_{g})\circ\rho_{g}^{*}(s)\circ c_{g^{-1}}.

Right-composition with
c_{g}\circ\rho^{*}_{g^{-1}}(s)
gives
\phi_{g}\circ\rho_{g}^{*}(f)=f\circ\kappa_{g}=f\circ(\theta_{g}\otimes c_{g})
, and so
f
is a
G
-morphism.
∎

2.4.
Contraction maps

Let
\mathscr{F}\in{\rm QCoh}(X)
and let
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Any permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
in the symmetric group defines a unique natural isomorphism
\tau_{\sigma}:\mathscr{F}^{\otimes p}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}^{\otimes p}
, often referred to as the
braiding
morphism, which acts by permuting the tensor factors. For
1\leq i\leq p
let
\sigma_{i}\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
be the cycle
(i,i+1,\ldots,p)
and define

{\rm Alt}_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{p-i}\tau_{\sigma_{i}}:\mathscr{F}^{\otimes p}\longrightarrow\mathscr{F}^{\otimes p}.

By construction,
{\rm Alt}_{p}
is alternating and
G
-equivariant. Thus, if
\pi_{p}:\mathscr{F}^{\otimes p}\twoheadrightarrow\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{F}
denotes the natural quotient map, the universal property of exterior powers implies that there exists a unique
G
-morphism
\Delta_{p}
that makes the following diagram commute.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{F}^{\otimes p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{F}^{\otimes p-1}\otimes\mathscr{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{F}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Alt}_{p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{p-1}\otimes\text{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{p}}

It should be noted that locally on the level of sections
\Delta_{p}
is given by

s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{p}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{p-i}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{i-1}\wedge s_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{p}\otimes s_{i}.

Definition 2.4.1
.

The
contraction map
associated to a morphism
f:\mathscr{F}\rightarrow\mathscr{G}
in
{\rm QCoh}(X)
is

\delta_{f,p}:\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{F}\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{F}\otimes\mathscr{G},\;\delta_{f,p}=({\rm id}_{\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{F}}\otimes f)\circ\Delta_{p}.

By construction, contraction maps associated to
G
-morphisms are
G
-morphisms themselves. This allows us to extend a fundamental ingredient of the construction of Koszul complexes to the
G
-equivariant setting.

Proposition 2.4.2
.

A short exact sequence of vector bundles in
{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)

0\rightarrow\mathscr{H}\overset{g}{\rightarrow}\mathscr{F}\overset{f}{\rightarrow}\mathscr{G}\rightarrow 0

with
{\rm rank}(\mathscr{G})=1
, gives rise to a short exact sequence in
{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)

0\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{H}\overset{\wedge^{p}(g)}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{F}\overset{\delta_{f,p}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{H}\otimes\mathscr{G}\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

The contraction map
\delta_{f,p}
is a
G
-map, and by Lemma
2.1.2
its kernel, image and cokernel are in
{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
. It thus suffices to prove the statement in the non-equivariant case; this is well known and can be found in
[
8
, 3.2]
or
[
14
, 4.13]
. Indicatively, its proof goes as follows: if
\mathscr{U}=\{U_{a}\}_{a\in A}
is an affine open cover of
X
, then one has, for all
a\in A
, split short exact sequences of
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{a})
-modules

0\rightarrow\mathscr{H}\mid_{U_{a}}\overset{g\mid_{U_{a}}}{\rightarrow}\mathscr{F}\mid_{U_{a}}\overset{f\mid_{U_{a}}}{\rightarrow}\mathscr{G}\mid_{U_{a}}\rightarrow 0
. Then

\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{F}\mid_{U_{a}}\cong\bigoplus_{m+n=p}\bigwedge^{m}\mathscr{H}\mid_{U_{a}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{a})}\bigwedge^{n}\mathscr{G}\mid_{U_{a}}\cong\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{H}\mid_{U_{a}}\oplus\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{H}\mid_{U_{a}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{a})}\mathscr{G}\mid_{U_{a}},

since
\bigwedge^{n}\mathscr{G}\mid_{U_{a}}=0
for all
n>{\rm rank}(\mathscr{G})=1
, with
\wedge^{p}(g)\mid_{U_{a}}
the inclusion of the first factor and
\delta_{f,p}\mid_{U_{a}}
the projection onto the second. Hence
\ker\delta_{f,p}\cong\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{H}\text{ and }{\rm Im}{\delta_{f,p}}=\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{H}\otimes\mathscr{G}.

∎

2.5.
The Koszul complex

Let
L
be a very ample line bundle on
X
, and let

V=H^{0}(L)=H^{0}(X,L)\text{ and }S={\rm Sym}(V)=\bigoplus_{q\geq 0}{\rm Sym}^{q}(V).

Definition 2.5.1
.

The Koszul complex associated to the pair
(X,L)
is the complex of graded
S
-modules

\cdots\overset{d_{p+2,q-2}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p+1}V\otimes H^{0}(L^{\otimes(q-1)})\overset{d_{p+1,q-1}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p}V\otimes H^{0}(L^{\otimes q})\overset{d_{p,q}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p-1}V\otimes H^{0}(L^{\otimes(q+1)})\overset{d_{p-1,q+1}}{\longrightarrow}\cdots

with differentials given by

d_{p,q}(s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{p}\otimes f)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{i}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{i-1}\wedge s_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{p}\otimes(fs_{i}).

Its cohomology
\ker d_{p,q}/{\rm Im}\;d_{p+1,q-1}
will be denoted by
K_{p,q}(X,L)
and referred to as the
(p,q)
-th Koszul cohomology of the pair
(X,L)
.

The Koszul complex can be built functorially from the evaluation map
{\rm ev}_{L}:V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow L
which on the level of sections is given by
s\otimes f\mapsto fs
, see
[
1
, §2.1]
. One then obtains formulas for the dimensions of
K_{p,q}(X,L)
over
k
in terms of that of the Zariski cohomology of
L
, its tensor powers and the kernel of the evaluation map, see
[
1
, Proposition 2.5]
or
[
6
, Proposition 3.2]
. This section revisits the construction to verify that if
L\in{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
then every step is
G
-equivariant; our aim is to generalize the stated dimension formulas into relationships between the induced
G
-representations.

2.6.
Equivariant Koszul cohomology

Let
L\in{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
be a very ample line bundle on
X
, so that by Proposition
2.3.1
,
V=H^{0}(L)
is a
kG
-module and
V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\in{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
. Let

\Phi:{\rm End}_{kG}(V)\rightarrow{\rm Hom}_{{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)}(V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X},L)

be the bijection associated to the adjunction
-\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\dashv H^{0}(-)
of Proposition
2.3.2
.

Definition 2.6.1
.

The map
\Phi({\rm id}_{V}):V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow L
will be denoted by
{\rm ev}_{L}
or simply
{\rm ev}
and referred to as the evaluation map associated to
L
. Its kernel will be denoted by
\mathscr{M}_{L}
and referred to as the kernel bundle or Lazarsfeld bundle.

In what follows, we write
[W]
for the virtual representation corresponding to a
kG
-module
W
.

Theorem 2.6.2
.

The Koszul complex associated to a pair
(X,L)
with
L
very ample and
L\in{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
is a complex of
kG
-modules; the virtual representation of its cohomology satisfies the identity

[K_{p,q}(X,L)]=[H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes q}\big)]-[H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p+1}V\otimes L^{\otimes(q-1)}\big)]+[H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p+1}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes(q-1)}\big)].

Proof.

By construction,
{\rm ev}_{L}
is a
G
-morphism, and by Lemma
2.1.2
,
\mathscr{M}_{L}\in{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
. Thus

0\rightarrow\mathscr{M}_{L}\overset{i}{\rightarrow}V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\overset{{\rm ev}}{\rightarrow}L\rightarrow 0

is a short exact sequence in
{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
and by Proposition
2.4.2
, the same is true for

0\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{L}\overset{\wedge^{p}(i)}{\rightarrow}\bigwedge^{p}(V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X})\overset{\delta_{p,{\rm ev}}}{\rightarrow}\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L\rightarrow 0,

where
\delta_{{\rm ev},p}
is the contraction map of Definition
2.4.1
associated to the evaluation map. Applying the exact
G
-functor
-\otimes L^{\otimes q}
, see Proposition
2.3.1
, and setting
f_{p,q}=\wedge^{p}(i)\otimes{\rm id}_{L^{\otimes q}},\;g_{p,q}=\delta_{{\rm ev},p}\otimes{\rm id}_{L^{\otimes q}}
gives that

0\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes q}\overset{f_{p,q}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p}V\otimes L^{\otimes q}\overset{g_{p,q}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes(q+1)}\rightarrow 0

is also a short exact sequence in
{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
. The compositions

\delta_{p,q}:\bigwedge^{p}V\otimes L^{\otimes q}\overset{g_{p,q}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes(q+1)}\overset{f_{p-1,q+1}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p-1}V\otimes L^{\otimes q+1};

are
G
-morphisms, and thus Proposition
2.3.1
implies that
d_{p,q}=H^{0}(\delta_{p,q})
are maps of
kG
-modules. They agree with the Koszul differentials of Definition
2.5.1
and thus the complex of
kG
-modules

\cdots\overset{d_{p+2,q-2}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p+1}V\otimes H^{0}(L^{\otimes(q-1)})\overset{d_{p+1,q-1}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p}V\otimes H^{0}(L^{\otimes q})\overset{d_{p,q}}{\longrightarrow}\bigwedge^{p-1}V\otimes H^{0}(L^{\otimes(q+1)})\overset{d_{p-1,q+1}}{\longrightarrow}\cdots

is exactly the Koszul complex as defined above.

One then applies the left exact
G
-functor
H^{0}(-)
to (
2.6
) to obtain the exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes q}\big)\overset{H^{0}(f_{p,q})}{\longrightarrow}H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p}V\otimes L^{\otimes q}\big)\overset{H^{0}(g_{p,q})}{\longrightarrow}H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes(q+1)}\big)

and computes

\displaystyle\ker d_{p,q}

\displaystyle=\ker H^{0}(f_{p-1,q+1}\circ g_{p,q})=\ker H^{0}(g_{p,q})={\rm Im}H^{0}(f_{p,q})\cong H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes q}\big)

\displaystyle{\rm Im}d_{p+1,q-1}

\displaystyle={\rm Im}H^{0}(f_{p,q}\circ g_{p+1,q-1})\cong{\rm Im}H^{0}(g_{p+1,q-1})\cong\bigwedge^{p+1}V\otimes H^{0}(L^{\otimes(q-1)})/H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p+1}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes(q-1)}\big),

to conclude that

[K_{p,q}(X,L)]=[H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes q}\big)]-[H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p+1}V\otimes L^{\otimes(q-1)}\big)]+[H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p+1}\mathscr{M}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes(q-1)}\big)].

∎

3.
Explicit formulas for canonical curves

Let
(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
be a smooth, projective, irreducible curve of genus
g\geq 4
over an algebraically closed field
k
, and let
G
be a finite group of order coprime to the characteristic of
k
acting faithfully on the right on
X
.

3.1.
Equivariant structure of the canonical bundle

Let
L=\Omega_{X}
be the canonical bundle on
X
and write
\{c_{g}\}_{g\in G}
for the
G
-linearization of
\Omega_{X}
. If
d:\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow\Omega_{X}
denotes the universal derivation, then for all
g\in G
, the maps

D_{g}:\mathscr{O}_{X}\overset{c_{g}^{-1}}{\longrightarrow}\rho_{g}^{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}\overset{\rho^{*}_{g}(d)}{\longrightarrow}\rho_{g}^{*}\Omega_{X}

are
k
-derivations. The universal property of the pair
(\Omega_{X},d)
then gives the existence of unique isomorphisms
a_{g}:\rho_{g}^{*}\Omega_{X}\rightarrow\Omega_{X}
, satisfying the cocycle condition of Definition
2.1.1
. Thus
\Omega_{X}\in{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
.

3.2.
Virtual Differences of Koszul Representations

. For
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let
K_{p,q}(X,\Omega_{X})
be the Koszul cohomology groups of Definition
2.5.1
. Then by
[
6
, 3.6]

K_{0,0}(X,\Omega_{X})=k=K_{g-2,3}(X,\Omega_{X})
and
K_{p,q}(X,\Omega_{X})=0
for all other values of
(p,q)
except when
q=1
and
1\leq p\leq g-3
, or
q=2
and
1\leq p\leq g-3
. This leads us to consider the following differences of virtual representations, mimicking the approach of calculating differences of
k
-vector space dimensions.

Corollary 3.2.1
.

Let
V=H^{0}(\Omega_{X})
and let
\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}=\ker{\rm ev}_{\Omega_{X}}
. For
1\leq p\leq g-3
,

[K_{p,1}(X,\Omega_{X})]-[K_{p-1,2}(X,\Omega_{X})]=[\bigwedge^{p}V\otimes V]-[\bigwedge^{p+1}V]-[H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)].

Proof.

Noting that
H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p+1}\mathscr{M}_{L}\big)
vanishes, see
[
1
, Remark 2.6]
, the result follows by Theorem
2.6.2
.
∎

To calculate the right hand side of the above equation one employs the theory of
G
-equivariant Euler characteristics. To this end, we first review the results of Ellingsrud and Lønsted from
[
5
]
which provide explicit decompositions of the virtual representations
[H^{0}(\mathscr{E})]-[H^{1}(\mathscr{E})]
for
G
-equivariant vector bundles
\mathscr{E}
on
X
. For a more recent treatment of the subject and generalizations, see
[
16
]
.

3.3.
Equivariant Euler characteristics.

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow Y=X/G
be the quotient map of the action of
G
on
X
and let
G_{P}={\rm Stab}_{G}(P)
be the decomposition group of
P\in X
. It is a cyclic group of order equal to the ramification index of
P
; the latter is an invariant of the orbit of
P
and thus may be denoted by
e_{Q}
, with
Q=\pi(P)\in Y
. Write
\{\chi_{P}^{d}:\;0\leq d\leq e_{Q}-1\}
for the irreducible characters of
G_{P}
, where
\chi_{P}
is the fundamental character of
G_{P}
obtained by its action on the local uniformizer
u_{P}
at
P
considered modulo
\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{2}=\langle u_{P}^{2}\rangle
. Note that for any
P\in\pi^{-1}(Q)
, one has that
e_{Q}>1
, i.e.
G_{P}
is non-trivial, if and only if
P\in\mathfrak{r}\subset X
, the ramification locus of
\pi
; equivalently
Q\in\mathfrak{b}\subset Y
, the branch locus of
\pi
.

Definition 3.3.1
.

Let
Q\in\mathfrak{b}
, let
P\in\pi^{-1}(Q)\subseteq\mathfrak{r}
and let
\mathscr{E}\in{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
be a vector bundle.

(1)

The completed fiber of
\mathscr{E}
over
P
is the
kG_{P}
-module

V_{P}(\mathscr{E})=(\mathscr{E}\otimes_{C_{Q}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,P})\otimes_{\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,P}}k,\text{ where }C_{Q}=\mathscr{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{Y}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{Y,Q}.

(2)

The local multiplicities of an irreducible
kG
-module
I
with respect to
\mathscr{E}
are the integers

n_{d,Q,I}(\mathscr{E})=\langle\chi_{P}^{d},V_{P}(\mathscr{E})\otimes{\rm Res}^{G}_{G_{P}}(I)\rangle,\text{ where }0\leq d\leq e_{Q}-1.

In the special case that
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{O}_{X}
, we write
n_{d,Q,I}
for
n_{d,Q,I}(\mathscr{O}_{X})=\langle\chi_{P}^{d},{\rm Res}^{G}_{G_{P}}(I)\rangle
.

Remark.
The terminology
completed fiber
is justified as follows. By the discussion following
[
5
, Lemma 3.4]
,

C_{Q}
is isomorphic to the product of the complete local rings
\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,P^{\prime}}
with
P^{\prime}
running over all points in
\pi^{-1}(Q)
. Thus
\mathscr{E}\otimes_{C_{Q}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,P}\cong\mathscr{E}\otimes_{\prod\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,P^{\prime}}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,P}\cong\widehat{\mathscr{E}_{P}}
is the completion of the stalk of
\mathscr{E}
at the point
P
and
V_{P}(\mathscr{E})\cong\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{P}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,P}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,P}/\mathfrak{m}_{P}
is its fiber over
P
.

Let
{\rm Rep}_{k}(G)
be the representation ring of
G
over
k
and let
[W]\in{\rm Rep_{k}}(G)
denote the virtual representation corresponding to a
kG
-module
W
.

Definition 3.3.2
.

The equivariant Euler characteristic of a
G
-equivariant vector bundle
\mathscr{E}\in{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
is the virtual representation

\chi_{G}(\mathscr{E})=[H^{0}(\mathscr{E})]-[H^{1}(\mathscr{E})]\in{\rm Rep}_{k}(G).

The reader is referred to
[
5
, (3.7)]
for the proof of the following.

Theorem 3.3.3
.

If
\mathscr{E}\in{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
is a vector bundle on
X
, then

\chi_{G}(\mathscr{E})=\left(\frac{1}{|G|}\deg(\mathscr{E})+(1-g_{Y})\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{E})\right)[kG]-\sum_{I\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\sum_{Q\in Y}\sum_{d=1}^{e_{Q}}\left(1-\frac{d}{e_{Q}}\right)n_{d,Q,I}(\mathscr{E})[I],

where
Y=X/G
,
g_{Y}
is the genus of
Y
,
{\rm Irr}(G)
is the set of irreducible representations of
G
,
e_{Q}
is the ramification index of
P\in\pi^{-1}(Q)
and the
n_{d,Q,I}(\mathscr{E})
are as in Definition
3.3.1
.

Setting
G
to be the trivial group, the ramification terms vanish and the quotient map becomes the identity, and so the result above specializes to the classical Riemann–Roch theorem for vector bundles on curves. Among its many applications, the latter may be used to calculate Euler characteristics of direct sums, tensor products and exterior powers. The goal being to explore similar techniques in the equivariant case, we first examine the behaviour of
V_{P}(-)
with respect to these operations.

Lemma 3.3.4
.

Let
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}^{\prime},\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}
be
G
-equivariant vector bundles.

(1)

If
0\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0
is a short exact sequence in
{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
, then

V(\mathscr{E})\cong V(\mathscr{E}^{\prime})\oplus V(\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime})
.

(2)

V_{P}(\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{\prime})\cong V_{P}(\mathscr{E})\otimes V_{P}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime})
and
V_{P}\big(\bigwedge^{r}\mathscr{E}\big)\cong\bigwedge^{r}V_{P}(\mathscr{E})
.

(3)

If
W\in kG_{P}
-mod, then
V_{P}(W\otimes\mathscr{E})\cong W\otimes V_{P}(\mathscr{E})
.

Proof.

Using the isomorphism
V_{P}(\mathscr{E})\cong\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{P}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,P}}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{X,P}/\mathfrak{m}_{P}
discussed in the Remark following Definition
3.3.1
, we regard
V_{P}(\mathscr{E})
as the fiber of a completed stalk. Formation of stalks commutes with direct sums, tensor products and exterior powers
[
9
, 7.4.1, 7.4.7 and 7.20.5]
. Completion commutes with direct sums
[
4
, 7.15]
; interpreting it as flat base change,
[
4
, 7.2]
, it also does so with tensor products,
[
19
, Appendix A, Formula 11]
, and exterior powers,
[
19
, Appendix C, (4)]
. The exact same arguments apply to taking fibers, as it amounts to tensoring free modules with residue fields.
∎

It follows directly from part (1) of Lemma
3.3.4
that equivariant Euler characteristics are additive with respect to short exact sequences. While their behavior under general tensor products and exterior powers is governed by more complex combinatorial rules, the specific case of tensoring with trivial bundles is tractable and particularly relevant to the present analysis.

Corollary 3.3.5
.

Let
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}^{\prime},\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}
be
G
-equivariant vector bundles.

(1)

If
0\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0
is a short exact sequence in
{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
, then

\chi_{G}(\mathscr{E})=\chi_{G}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime})+\chi_{G}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime})
.

(2)

If
W\in kG
-mod, then

\chi_{G}(W\otimes\mathscr{E})=\dim_{k}(W)\left(\frac{1}{|G|}\deg(\mathscr{E})+(1-g_{Y})\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{E})\right)[kG]\\
-\sum_{I\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\sum_{Q\in Y}\sum_{d=1}^{e_{Q}}\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\left(1-\frac{d}{e_{Q}}\right)\langle\chi_{P}^{d-k},W\rangle\;n_{k,Q,I}(\mathscr{E})[I].

Proof.

(1) follows immediately by part(1) of Lemma
3.3.4
. For (2), since
\deg(W\otimes\mathscr{E})=\dim_{k}(W)\deg(\mathscr{E})
and
{\rm rk}(W\otimes\mathscr{E})=\dim_{k}(W){\rm rk}(\mathscr{E})
, the coefficient of
k[G]
in
\chi_{G}(W\otimes\mathscr{E})
is
\dim_{k}(W)
times the coefficient of
k[G]
in
\chi_{G}(\mathscr{E})
. Regarding the triple sum, parts (2) and (3) of Lemma
3.3.4
imply that

n_{d,Q,I}(W\otimes\mathscr{E})=\langle\chi_{P}^{d},V_{P}(W\otimes\mathscr{E})\otimes I\rangle=\langle\chi_{P}^{d},W\otimes V_{P}(\mathscr{E})\otimes I\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\langle\chi_{P}^{d-k},W\rangle\;n_{k,Q,I}(\mathscr{E}).

∎

Ranks, degrees and genera are, more often than not, straightforward to compute; thus, the main challenge to obtaining explicit results from Theorem
3.3.3
is to determine its local ingredients. The best that one can hope for is to express each
n_{d,Q,I}(\mathscr{E})
in terms of
n_{d,Q,I}=n_{d,Q,I}(\mathscr{O}_{X})
.
For
\mathscr{E}=\Omega_{X}^{\otimes m},\;m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, one has the following formula, which is
implicit in
[
5
, 3.9]
; for clarity of the exposition, we include a short proof.

Lemma 3.3.6
.

For all
Q\in Y,\;1\leq d\leq e_{Q},\;I\in{\rm Irr}(G)
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

n_{d,Q,I}\big(\Omega_{X}^{\otimes m}\big)=n_{e_{Q}\left\{\frac{m-d}{e_{Q}}\right\},Q,I^{\vee}},

where
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the fractional part of
x\in\mathbb{Z}
and
I^{\vee}
is the dual of
I
.

Proof.

If
P\in\pi^{-1}(Q)
, then
V_{P}(\Omega_{X})\cong\mathfrak{m}_{P}/\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{2}
as
kG_{P}
-modules, and so the character of
V_{P}(\Omega_{X})
is the fundamental character
\chi_{P}
. Observing that
n_{d,Q,I}=n_{e_{Q}-d,Q,I^{\vee}}
, one writes

V_{P}\big(\Omega_{X}^{\otimes m}\big)\otimes{\rm Res}^{G}_{G_{P}}(I)=\sum_{d=1}^{e_{Q}}n_{d,Q,I}\chi_{P}^{d+m}=\sum_{d=1}^{e_{Q}}n_{e_{Q}-d,Q,I^{\vee}}\chi_{P}^{d+m}=\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}n_{d,Q,I^{\vee}}\chi_{P}^{e_{Q}-d+m}=\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}n_{d,Q,I^{\vee}}\chi_{P}^{m-d},

and the result follows as the remainder of the division of
m-d
by
e_{Q}
is exactly
e_{Q}\left\{\frac{m-d}{e_{Q}}\right\}
. It should be noted that the latter takes values between
0
and
e_{Q}-1
, even though
1\leq d\leq e_{Q}
.
∎

3.4.
Decomposition of the kernel bundle term

We proceed with applying the results of the previous section to determine the
kG
-structure of
[H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)]
. First we show that this can be obtained directly from Theorem (
3.3.3
).

Proposition 3.4.1
.

H^{1}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)=0
if
p\neq g-1
, and
H^{1}\big(\bigwedge^{g-1}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)=k
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
2.6.2
, the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\rightarrow H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow\Omega_{X}\rightarrow 0
gives rise, for any
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, to a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes{\Omega_{X}}^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}V\otimes{\Omega_{X}}^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes{\Omega_{X}}^{\otimes 3}\rightarrow 0.

Noting that
H^{1}(\Omega^{\otimes 2})=0
, the induced long exact sequence in cohomology in
{\rm Vect}_{k}
takes the form

0\rightarrow H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}^{\otimes 2}\otimes{\Omega_{X}}\big)\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}V\otimes H^{0}\big({\Omega_{X}}^{\otimes 2}\big)\rightarrow H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes{\Omega_{X}}^{\otimes 3}\big)\rightarrow H^{1}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes{\Omega_{X}}^{\otimes 2}\big)\rightarrow 0.

Theorem
2.6.2
implies that
\dim_{k}H^{1}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes{\Omega_{X}}^{\otimes 2}\big)=\dim_{k}K_{p-1,3}(X,\Omega_{X})
and the result follows, see the discussion preceding Corollary
3.2.1
.
∎

Next, we compute the rank and the degree of
\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes{\Omega_{X}}^{\otimes 2}
.

Proposition 3.4.2
.

{\rm rk}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)=\binom{g-1}{p}\text{ and }\deg(\bigwedge^{p}M_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{q})=2(2g-2-p)\binom{g-1}{p}.

Proof.

Both
{\rm rk}
and
{\rm deg}
are additive in short exact sequences. As
{\rm rk}\big(\Omega_{X}\big)=1
and
{\rm deg}\big(\Omega_{X}\big)=2g-2
,
0\rightarrow\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\rightarrow H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow{\Omega}_{X}\rightarrow 0
gives
{\rm rk}\big(\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\big)=g-1\text{ and }{\rm deg}\big(\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\big)=-2g+2
. Thus

{\rm rk}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\big)=\binom{{\rm rk}(\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}})}{p}=\binom{g-1}{p},\;{\rm deg}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\big)=\binom{{\rm rk}(\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}})-1}{p-1}{\rm deg}\big(\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\big)=\binom{g-2}{p-1}(2-2g).

Finally,

\displaystyle{\rm rk}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)

\displaystyle={\rm rk}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\big)\cdot{\rm rk}\big(\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)=\binom{g-1}{p}\text{ and }

\displaystyle{\rm deg}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)

\displaystyle={\rm rk}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\big)\cdot{\rm deg}\big(\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)+{\rm deg}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\big)\cdot{\rm rk}\big(\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)

\displaystyle=(4g-4)\binom{g-1}{p}-(2g-2)\binom{g-2}{p-1}=4(g-1)\binom{g-1}{p}-2p\binom{g-1}{p}.

∎

It remains to compute the
n_{d,Q,I}(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2})
; by Lemma
3.3.6
, it suffices to express them in terms of the
n_{d,Q,I}(\Omega_{X}^{\otimes m})
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. With this objective in mind, recall from
[
7
, 23.39]
that the representation ring of
G_{P}
over
k
is endowed with a
\lambda
-ring structure, with
\lambda^{n}(W)=\bigwedge^{n}W
. If
\lambda_{t}(V)=\sum_{F}n\bigwedge^{n}Vt^{n}
denotes the respective generating function and
0\rightarrow W_{1}\rightarrow W_{2}\rightarrow W_{3}\rightarrow 0
is a short exact sequence of
kG_{P}
-modules then by
[
3
, 12.5]
one has that

\lambda_{t}(W_{2})=\lambda_{t}({W_{1}})\lambda_{t}(W_{3})\text{ and }\left(\lambda_{t}(W_{3})\right)^{-1}=\sigma_{-t}(W_{3}):=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}{\rm Sym}^{n}(W_{3})t^{n}.

This has two implications that are of interest to this paper. First, that expanding
\lambda_{t}(W_{1})=\lambda_{t}(W_{2})\sigma_{-t}(W_{3})
and equating coefficients yields the following identity of virtual representations

(1)

\bigwedge^{p}[W_{1}]=\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\bigwedge^{p-i}[W_{2}]\cdot[{\rm Sym}^{i}(W_{3})].

Second, that for non-negative integers
a_{0},\ldots,a_{e_{Q}-1}
,

\lambda_{t}\left(\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}a_{d}\chi_{P}^{d}\right)=\prod_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}(1+\chi_{P}^{d}t)^{a_{d}}=\sum_{0\leq k_{0},\ldots k_{e_{Q}-1}\leq a_{d}}\left(\prod_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\binom{a_{d}}{k_{d}}\right)\chi^{\sum d\cdot k_{d}}t^{\sum k_{d}},

and thus, extracting the coefficient of
t^{r}
,

(2)

\bigwedge^{r}\left(\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}a_{d}\chi_{P}^{d}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{0}+\cdots+k_{e_{Q}-1}=r\\
0\leq k_{d}\leq a_{d}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\binom{a_{d}}{k_{d}}\right)\chi^{\sum d\cdot k_{d}}.

Proposition 3.4.3
.

If
Q\in Y,\;0\leq d\leq e_{Q}-1,\;I\in{\rm Irr}(G)
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, then

n_{d,Q,I}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}(-1)^{i}\langle\chi_{P}^{d-k},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle\;n_{k,Q,I}(\Omega_{X}^{\otimes(i+2)}).

Proof.

Part (1) of Lemma
3.3.4
applied to the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\rightarrow H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\rightarrow{\Omega}_{X}\rightarrow 0
in
{\rm QCoh}_{G}(X)
gives rise to the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow V_{P}(\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}})\rightarrow H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rightarrow V_{P}(\Omega_{X})\rightarrow 0
of
kG_{P}
-modules. As
V_{P}(\Omega_{X})
is 1-dimensional,
{\rm Sym}^{i}(V_{P}(\Omega_{X}))\cong V_{P}(\Omega_{X})^{\otimes i}
and so eq. (
1
) gives

\bigwedge^{p}[V_{P}(\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}})]=\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\bigwedge^{p-i}[H^{0}(\Omega_{X})]\cdot[V_{P}(\Omega_{X})]^{i}.

Combining this with part (2) of Lemma
3.3.4
yields

\big[V_{P}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)\big]=\bigwedge^{p}[V_{P}(\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}})]\cdot[V_{P}(\Omega_{X})]^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\bigwedge^{p-i}[H^{0}(\Omega_{X})]\cdot[V_{P}(\Omega_{X})]^{i+2},

and thus, as in the discussion preceding Lemma
3.3.6
,

\displaystyle n_{d,Q,I}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\langle\chi_{P}^{d},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\otimes V_{P}(\Omega_{X}^{\otimes(i+2)})\otimes I\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}(-1)^{i}\langle\chi_{P}^{d-k},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle\;\langle\chi_{P}^{k},V_{P}(\Omega_{X}^{\otimes(i+2)})\otimes I\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}(-1)^{i}\langle\chi_{P}^{d-k},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle\;n_{k,Q,I}(\Omega_{X}^{\otimes(i+2)}).

∎

To give an explicit formula for the local multiplicities of
\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})
, we recall that by
[
5
, 3.9]

(3)

[H^{0}\big(\Omega_{X}\big)]=\sum_{I\in{\rm Irr}(G)}m_{I}[I],\text{ where }m_{I}=(g_{Y}-1)\dim I+\sum_{Q\in Y}\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\left\langle\frac{-d}{e_{Q}}\right\rangle n_{d,Q,I}+\delta_{I,I_{\rm triv}}

where
\delta_{I,I_{\rm triv}}=1
if
I=I_{\rm triv}
and
0
otherwise.

Lemma 3.4.4
.

For
Q\in Y,\;0\leq d\leq e_{Q}-1,\;r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\{m_{I}:\;I\in{\rm Irr}(G)\}
as in eq. (
3
),

\langle\chi_{P}^{d},\bigwedge^{r}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{j}\leq\sum_{I}m_{I}n_{j,Q,I}\\
\sum k_{j}=r\\
\sum jk_{j}\equiv d\mod e_{Q}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\left(\!\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\sum_{I\in{\rm Irr}(G)}m_{I}\cdot n_{j,Q,I}\\
k_{j}\end{array}\!\right).

Proof.

The restriction functor commutes with direct sums and exterior powers, and so

\displaystyle[{\rm Res}^{G}_{G_{P}}\big(\bigwedge^{r}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\big)]

\displaystyle=\bigwedge^{r}\sum_{I\in{\rm Irr}(G)}m_{I}{\rm Res}^{G}_{G_{P}}\big([I]\big)=\bigwedge^{r}\sum_{I\in{\rm Irr}(G)}m_{I}\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}n_{d,Q,I}\chi_{P}^{d}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge^{r}\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\left(\sum_{I\in{\rm Irr}(G)}m_{I}\cdot n_{d,Q,I}\right)\chi_{P}^{d}.

The result follows by imposing the relation
\sum_{j}jk_{j}\equiv d\;{\rm mod}\;e_{Q}
to eq. (
2
) to extract the coefficient of
\chi_{P}^{d}
.
∎

For the reader’s convenience, and since the formula below is the main result of the section, we recall within the statement the notation for all of its constituent components.

Theorem 3.4.5
.

Let
g_{X}
be the genus of
X
and
g_{Y}
the genus of
Y=X/G
. For each
Q\in Y
, let
e_{Q}
be the ramification index of
P\in\pi^{-1}(Q)
, let
G_{P}
be its decomposition group,
\chi_{P}
be the fundamental character of
\mathfrak{m}_{P}/\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{2}
and let
n_{k,Q,I}=\langle\chi_{P}^{k},I\rangle
, for
I\in{\rm Irr}(G),\;0\leq k\leq e_{Q}-1
. Then

[H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)]=\binom{g_{X}-1}{p}\left(\frac{2(g_{X}-1-p)}{|G|}+g_{Y}-1\right)[kG]\\
+\sum_{I\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}\sum_{Q\in Y}\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\langle\chi_{P}^{d},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\left\{\frac{d+i+1-k}{e_{Q}}\right\}n_{k,Q,I}[I].

Proof.

Combining Theorem (
3.3.3
) and Proposition
3.4.1
, gives

\displaystyle[H^{0}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)]=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|G|}\deg\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)+(1-g_{Y})\mathrm{rk}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)\right)[kG]

\displaystyle-\sum_{I\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\sum_{Q\in Y}\sum_{d=1}^{e_{Q}}\left(1-\frac{d}{e_{Q}}\right)n_{d,Q,I}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)\cdot[I].

Substituting the degree and the rank from Proposition
3.4.2
into the coefficient of
[kG]
gives

(4)

\binom{g_{X}-1}{p}\left(\frac{2(2g_{X}-2-p)}{|G|}+(1-g_{Y})\right)[kG].

Regarding the triple sum, Proposition
3.4.3
and Lemma
3.3.6
imply that

\left(1-\frac{d}{e_{Q}}\right)n_{d,Q,I}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}(-1)^{i}\left(1-\frac{d}{e_{Q}}\right)\;\langle\chi_{P}^{d-k},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle\;n_{e_{Q}\left\{\frac{i+2-k}{e_{Q}}\right\},Q,I^{\vee}},

and observing that
k^{\prime}=e_{Q}\left\{\frac{i+2-k}{e_{Q}}\right\}
if and only if
k=e_{Q}\left\{\frac{i+2-k^{\prime}}{e_{Q}}\right\}
allows us to rewrite the right hand side

\sum_{i=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}(-1)^{i}\left(1-\frac{d}{e_{Q}}\right)\;\langle\chi_{P}^{d-e_{Q}\left\{\frac{i+2-k}{e_{Q}}\right\}},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle\;n_{k,Q,I^{\vee}}.

Finally,
d-e_{Q}\left\{\frac{i+2-k}{e_{Q}}\right\}\equiv e_{Q}\left\{\frac{d-i-2+k}{e_{Q}}\right\}{\rm mod}\;e_{Q}
. Setting
d^{\prime}=e_{Q}\left\{\frac{d-i-2+k}{e_{Q}}\right\}
forces

d=1+e_{Q}\left\{\frac{d^{\prime}+i+1-k}{e_{Q}}\right\}
,
to ensure that
1\leq d\leq e_{Q}
with
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq e_{Q}-1
.
Thus, we arrive at

\sum_{d=1}^{e_{Q}}\left(1-\frac{d}{e_{Q}}\right)n_{d,Q,I}\big(\bigwedge^{p}\mathscr{M}_{\Omega_{X}}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\big)=\\
\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}(-1)^{i}\left(1-\frac{1}{e_{Q}}-\left\{\frac{d+i+1-k}{e_{Q}}\right\}\right)\;\langle\chi_{P}^{d},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle\;n_{k,Q,I^{\vee}}.

The term
(1-\frac{1}{e_{Q}})
is independent of the summation indices, and so we write the RHS as

(5)

\left(1-\frac{1}{e_{Q}}\right)\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\langle\chi_{P}^{d},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}n_{k,Q,I^{\vee}}\\
-\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}(-1)^{i}\left\{\frac{d+i+1-k}{e_{Q}}\right\}\;\langle\chi_{P}^{d},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle\;n_{k,Q,I^{\vee}}.

Next, we observe that
\sum_{k}n_{k,Q,I^{\vee}}=\dim I^{\vee}
and

\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\langle\chi_{P}^{d},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\binom{g_{X}}{p-i}=\binom{g_{X}-1}{p}

and so

\sum_{Q\in Y}\sum_{I\in{\rm Irr}(G)}\left(1-\frac{1}{e_{Q}}\right)\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\langle\chi_{P}^{d},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle\sum_{k=0}^{e_{Q}-1}n_{k,Q,I^{\vee}}[I]=\sum_{Q\in Y}\left(1-\frac{1}{e_{Q}}\right)\binom{g_{X}-1}{p}[kG].

Using the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, the RHS becomes

\binom{g_{X}-1}{p}\left(\frac{2(g_{X}-1)}{|G|}-2(g_{Y}-1)\right)[kG]

and combining this with eq. (
4
) gives the final coefficient of
[kG]
. The remaining term is obtained from the second line of eq. (
5
). The reader should keep in mind that an explicit formula for the integers
\langle\chi_{P}^{d},\bigwedge^{p-i}H^{0}(\Omega_{X})\rangle
has been given in Lemma
3.4.4
.
∎

3.5.
Schur functors and generating functions

Let
V=H^{0}(\Omega_{X})
. In this section we study the virtual representation
[\bigwedge^{p}V\otimes V]-[\bigwedge^{p+1}V]
that appears in Corollary
3.2.1
. This can be realized as the kernel of the canonical epimorphism

\bigwedge^{p}V\otimes V\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p+1}V,\;v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{p}\otimes v\mapsto;v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{p}\wedge v,

which, see for example
[
7
, p.79]
, is isomorphic to

\mathbb{S}_{p}(V):=\mathbb{S}_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(V)=\mathbb{S}_{(2,1^{p-2})}(V),

the
Schur functor
or
Weyl module
associated to the hook partition
(2,1^{p-2})=(2,1,\ldots,1)
.

Deriving explicit closed formulas for the multiplicities in the irreducible decomposition of such modules is practically unattainable. In what follows, we utilize the theory of generating functions to present the two classical equivalent techniques to circumvent this issue.

For each
g\in G
, the generating polynomials

{\rm Sch}_{V}(t,g)=\sum_{p=0}^{\dim V}\chi_{\mathbb{S}_{p}(V)}(g)t^{p}\in k[t]

are given by the result below.

Theorem 3.5.1
.

For
g\in G
and
I\in{\rm Irr}(G)
, let
\xi_{I,1},\ldots,\xi_{I,\dim I}
be the eigenvalues of the matrix of the action of
g
on
I
and let

m_{I}=(g_{Y}-1)\dim I+\sum_{Q\in Y}\sum_{d=0}^{e_{Q}-1}\left\langle\frac{-d}{e_{Q}}\right\rangle n_{d,Q,I}+\delta_{I,I_{\rm triv}}

be the multiplicity of
I
in the decomposition of
V
as in eq. (
3
). Then the character value
\chi_{\mathbb{S}_{p}(V)}(g)
is the coefficient of
t^{p}
in the expression below.

\frac{1}{t}\left[\left(\sum_{I}m_{I}\cdot\chi_{I}(g)t-1\right)\prod_{I\in{\rm Irr}(G)}\prod_{j=1}^{\dim I}(1+t\cdot\xi_{I,j}(g))^{m_{I}}+1\right]\in k[t].

Proof.

For each
g\in G
, let

{\rm Wd}_{V}(t,g)=\sum_{p=0}^{\dim V}\chi_{\bigwedge^{p}V}(g)t^{p}\in k[t]

and observe that

\sum_{p=0}^{\dim V}\chi_{\bigwedge^{p+1}V}(g)t^{p}=\frac{1}{t}\left({\rm Wd}_{V}(t,g)-1\right).

By definition of
\mathbb{S}_{p}(V)
, one has that
\chi_{\mathbb{S}_{p}(V)}(g)=\chi_{\bigwedge^{p}V}(g)\chi_{V}(g)-\chi_{\bigwedge^{p+1}V}(g)
, and so

(6)

{\rm Sch}_{V}(t,g)=\chi_{V}(g){\rm Wd}_{V}(t,g)-\frac{1}{t}\left({\rm Wd}_{V}(t,g)-1\right)=\frac{1}{t}\left[(\chi_{V}(g)t-1){\rm Wd}_{V}(t,g)+1\right].

If
m_{I}
is the multiplicity of
I\in{\rm Irr}(G)
in the decomposition of
V
, see eq. (
3
), then

\chi_{V}(g)=\sum_{I}m_{I}\chi_{I}(g),

so

{\rm Wd}_{V}(t,g)=\prod_{I\in{\rm Irr}(G)}{\rm Wd}_{I}(t,g)^{m_{I}}.

Let
\rho_{I}(g)
be the matrix of the action of
g
on
I
, let
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\dim I}\}
be a basis of eigenvectors and let
\xi_{I,1},\ldots,\xi_{I,\dim I}
be the corresponding eigenvalues. Then
g\in G
acts on a basis vector of
\bigwedge^{p}I
by

g(v_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{j_{p}})=\xi_{I,j_{1}}\cdots\xi_{I,j_{p}}(v_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{j_{p}}),

and so

\chi_{\bigwedge^{p}I}(g)=\sum_{j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{p}}\xi_{I,j_{1}}\cdots\xi_{I,j_{p}}=e_{p}(\xi_{I,1},\ldots,\xi_{I,\dim I}),

where
e_{p}
denotes the
p
-th elementary symmetric polynomial. Thus

{\rm Wd}_{I}(t,g)=\det({\rm Id}+t\rho_{I}(g))=\prod_{j=1}^{\dim I}(1+t\cdot\xi_{I,j}(g)),

and substituting

{\rm Wd}_{V}(t,g)=\prod_{I\in{\rm Irr}(G)}\prod_{j=1}^{\dim I}(1+t\cdot\xi_{I,j}(g))^{m_{I}}

into eq. (
6
) gives the final result.
∎

One may use the above formula to obtain the character table of
\mathbb{S}_{p}(V)
by extracting the coefficient of
t^{p}
from each
{\rm Sch}_{V}(t,g),\;g\in G
. An alternative, albeit equivalent viewpoint, is to aim at the multiplicity of each irreducible
I
in the decomposition of
\mathbb{S}_{p}(V)
as follows: instead of considering generating functions in
k[t]
for each
g\in G
, we switch to generating functions in
{\rm Rep}_{k}(G)[t]

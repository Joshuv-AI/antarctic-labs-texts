The real analytic structure of the Teichmüller space of circle diffeomorphisms with Zygmund continuous derivatives Thanks: Research supported by Japan Society for the Promotion of Science (KAKENHI 23K25775 and 23K17656)
Katsuhiko Matsuzaki

Abstract
. We apply the methods of simultaneous uniformization and composition operators on Besov spaces to the Teichmüller space T Z T^{Z} of circle diffeomorphisms with Zygmund continuous derivatives. As consequences, we obtain the following: (1) a new proof of the correspondence between quasiconformal self-homeomorphisms of the unit disk with complex dilatations of linear decay order and their quasisymmetric extensions to the unit circle with regularity in the Zygmund continuously differentiable class; (2) a real-analytic equivalence of T Z T^{Z} with the real Banach space of Zygmund continuous functions on the unit circle.

1.
Introduction

Let
{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}(\mathbb{S})
be the group of all orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms

h:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{S}
of the unit circle whose non-degenerate derivatives
h^{\prime}
are continuous and satisfy the
Zygmund condition

|h^{\prime}(e^{i(\theta+t)})-2h^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})+h^{\prime}(e^{i(\theta-t)})|\leq Ct

(1.1)

for all
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{S}
and
t>0
. Here,
C>0
is a constant independent of
e^{i\theta}
and
t
.
A continuous function
h^{\prime}
satisfying this condition has the modulus of continuity

|h^{\prime}(e^{i\theta+t})-h^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})|=O(t\log(1/t))\quad(t\to 0).

(1.2)

It follows that
h^{\prime}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
; namely,

|h^{\prime}(e^{i(\theta+t)})-h^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})|\leq C^{\prime}t^{\alpha}
holds.
The group of all orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms

h
of
\mathbb{S}
such that
h^{\prime}
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous is denoted by
{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
.
For these elements, the normalization is imposed by fixing three points
1
,
i
, and
-i
,
and the subgroups consisting of all normalized elements are denoted by

{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
and
{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
, respectively.

As we will see below,
{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
can be regarded as
the Teichmüller space
T^{Z}
, and
{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
as
T^{\alpha}
.
In this paper, however, we focus on
T^{Z}
, which is defined as a subspace of
the universal Teichmüller space
T
in the following way.

The space of Beltrami coefficients on the exterior unit disk

\mathbb{D}^{*}=\{z\mid|z|>1\}\cup\{\infty\}
is defined by

M(\mathbb{D}^{*})=\{\mu\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{*})\mid\|\mu\|_{\infty}<1\}.

Let
H(\mu):\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
be the normalized quasiconformal self-homeomorphism
whose complex dilatation
\bar{\partial}H/\partial H
is
\mu\in M(\mathbb{D}^{*})
.
Such an
H(\mu)
extends uniquely to the unit circle
\mathbb{S}

as a quasisymmetric self-homeomorphism
h_{\mu}
.
The normalization of
H(\mu)
is determined by that of
h_{\mu}
, and hence
H(\mu)
is uniquely determined by
\mu\in M(\mathbb{D}^{*})
.
Denote by
{\rm QS}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
the group of all normalized quasisymmetric self-homeomorphisms of
\mathbb{S}
.
The inclusion relation

{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}_{*}(\mathbb{S})\subset{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}_{*}(\mathbb{S})\subset{\rm QS}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
holds.

The boundary extension
H(\mu)|_{\mathbb{S}}=h_{\mu}

defines a surjection

\pi:M(\mathbb{D}^{*})\to{\rm QS}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
.
The Teichmüller equivalenc
\mu\sim\nu
in
M(\mathbb{D}^{*})
is defined by the coincidence

h_{\mu}=h_{\nu}
in
{\rm QS}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
, and
the universal Teichmüller space
T
is defined to be
the quotient space
M(\mathbb{D}^{*})/\sim
. Hence,
T
can be identified with
{\rm QS}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
.
We call this quotient map and also
\pi
the
Teichmüller projection
.

To introduce the Teichmüller space
T^{Z}
,
we impose the linear degeneration condition at the boundary on Beltrami coefficients:

M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})=\{\mu\in M(\mathbb{D}^{*})\mid\|\mu\|_{Z}=\|\mu\|_{1}=\underset{|z|>1}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\,((|z|-1)^{-1}\lor 1)|\mu(z)|<\infty\}.

Then Tang and Wu
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
proved
the precise correspondence of
M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})
to
{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}_{*}(\mathbb{S})

under the Teichmüller projection
\pi:M(\mathbb{D}^{*})\to{\rm QS}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

\pi(M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*}))={\rm Diff}^{1+Z}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
.

Thus, the Teichmüller space
T^{Z}
, defined as
M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})/\sim
, is
identified with
{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
.
In the first part of this paper, we reconstruct the proof of this theorem.
We note that the Teichmüller space
T^{\alpha}

(0<\alpha<1)
, defined as
M^{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{*})/\sim
, is
identified with
{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
in
[
13
]
, where

M^{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}^{*})
consists of all Beltrami coefficients
\mu\in M(\mathbb{D}^{*})

satisfying
\|\mu\|_{\alpha}<\infty
. See Theorem
7.1
for the definition of this norm.

To clarify the arguments, we divide the statement of Theorem
1.1
into two parts:

Claim 1
.

\pi(M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*}))
is contained in
{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
.

Claim 2
.

\pi:M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})\to{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
is surjective; that is, for any
h\in{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}_{*}(\mathbb{S})
, there exists a quasiconformal extension
H:\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
whose
complex dilatation
\mu_{H}=\bar{\partial}H/\partial H
belongs to
M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})
.

The theory of the Teichmüller space
T^{Z}=M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})/\sim
has been developed
from this definition involving quasiconformal mappings, similarly to the universal Teichmüller space (see
[
11
, Chapter 3]
).
For
\mu\in M(\mathbb{D}^{*})
, let
F_{\mu}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
be the conformal map of the unit disk

\mathbb{D}
that
admits a quasiconformal extension to the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with complex dilatation on

\mathbb{D}^{*}
,
satisfying the normalization
F_{\mu}(0)=0
,
F_{\mu}^{\prime}(0)=1
, and
F_{\mu}(\infty)=\infty
.
To consider its pre-Schwarzian derivative
N_{F_{\mu}}=(\log(F_{\mu})^{\prime})^{\prime}
and
the Schwarzian derivative
S_{F_{\mu}}=(N_{F_{\mu}})^{\prime}-(N_{F_{\mu}})^{2}/2
,
we prepare the corresponding Banach spaces in
{\rm Hol}(\mathbb{D})
, the space of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
:

\displaystyle B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle=\{\Phi\in{\rm Hol}(\mathbb{D})\mid\|\Phi\|_{B^{Z}}<\infty\},\quad\|\Phi\|_{B^{Z}}=|\Phi^{\prime}(0)|+\sup_{|z|<1}(1-|z|^{2})|\Phi^{\prime\prime}(z)|;

\displaystyle A^{Z}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle=\{\Psi\in{\rm Hol}(\mathbb{D})\mid\|\Psi\|_{A^{Z}}<\infty\},\quad\|\Psi\|_{A^{Z}}=\sup_{|z|<1}(1-|z|^{2})|\Psi(z)|.

Here, we regard
B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
as a Banach space modulo constant functions.

The theory of
T^{Z}
is based on the following characterization of
\mu\in M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})
,
which is summarized in
[
21
]
. After this work, a complex Banach manifold structure was
provided for
T^{Z}
via the Schwarzian derivative map
S:M(\mathbb{D}^{*})\to A^{Z}(\mathbb{D})

defined by
\mu\mapsto S_{F_{\mu}}
, which factors through
the Teichmüller projection into
the Bers embedding
\alpha:T^{Z}\to A^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
15
, Theorem 3]
). Moreover,
the fiber bundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{Z}
over
T_{Z}
in
B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
is induced by
the
pre-Schwarzian derivative map

L:M(\mathbb{D}^{*})\to A^{Z}(\mathbb{D})

defined by
\mu\mapsto\log(F_{\mu})^{\prime}
(see
[
16
, Theorem 2]
).
Both
S
and
L
are holomorphic split submersions onto their images.

Theorem 1.2
.

For a conformal homeomorphism
F_{\mu}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
given by
\mu\in M(\mathbb{D}^{*})
,
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mu\in M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})
;

(2)

\log(F_{\mu})^{\prime}\in B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
;

(3)

S_{F_{\mu}}\in A^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
;

(4)

(F_{\mu})^{\prime\prime\prime}\in A^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
;

(5)

(F_{\mu})^{\prime}
extends continuously to
\mathbb{S}
and
(F_{\mu})^{\prime}|_{\mathbb{S}}

satisfies the Zygmund condition.

The inclusion
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
follows from Dyn
′
kin
[
7
, Theorem 1]
,
whereas
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
was proved by Becker and Pommerenke
[
2
, Satz 4]
.
Moreover,
(2)\ {\rm or}\ (3)\Rightarrow(1)
is shown by Becker
[
1
, Theorem 3]
.
In Theorem
7.1
, we give a different proof of
(1)\Rightarrow(3)
.
The equivalence
(4)\Leftrightarrow(5)
is due to Zygmund
[
23
, Theorem 13]
; see also Theorem
4.1
.
Tang and Wu
[
21
, Theorem 2.6]
proved the chain of implications
(3)\Rightarrow(4)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
in this order.
The equivalence
(2)\Leftrightarrow(5)
also follows from Zygmund’s theorem,
under the circumstance mentioned in the following remark.

Remark 1
.

In our situation, where
F_{\mu}
maps
\mathbb{D}
conformally onto a domain in
\mathbb{C}

bounded by a non-degenerate

curve,

(F_{\mu})^{\prime}
always extends continuously to
\mathbb{S}

and
(F_{\mu})^{\prime}|_{\mathbb{S}}
does not vanish (see
[

condition (5) in Theorem

can equivalently be stated as

satisfying the Zygmund condition. See the second paragraph of Section

For later use, we define the
following Banach spaces of Zygmund continuous and differentiable functions:

\displaystyle C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})=\{\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{S})\mid\|\phi\|_{C^{Z}}<\infty\},\quad\|\phi\|_{C^{Z}}=\sup_{e^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{S}},\,t>0}\frac{|\phi(e^{i(\theta+t)})-2\phi(e^{i\theta})+\phi(e^{i(\theta-t)})|}{t};

\displaystyle C^{1+Z}(\mathbb{S})=\{\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{S})\mid\|\psi\|_{C^{1+Z}}<\infty\},\quad\|\psi\|_{C^{1+Z}}=\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{C^{Z}}+\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}.

We can alternatively define them as the Besov spaces

and

2.
Besov spaces and interpolation

We introduce function spaces that appear in this paper and
then show certain properties of these spaces from a functional-analytic perspective.

For
0<\alpha<1
, let
C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
be the set of
\alpha
-Hölder
continuous functions
\phi
on
\mathbb{S}
. By lifting
\phi
to the universal cover
\mathbb{R}

and denoting it by the same symbol, we may regard
\phi
as a periodic function on
\mathbb{R}
,
that is, on
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. See diagram (
1.3
). Hereafter, we represent
the functions on
\mathbb{S}
in this way not using
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{S}
.
The seminorm of
C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
is defined by

\|\phi\|_{C^{\alpha}}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{S}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

Let
C^{L}(\mathbb{S})
be the set of Lipschitz continuous functions

\phi
on
\mathbb{S}
.
The seminorm of
C^{L}(\mathbb{S})
is defined by

\|\phi\|_{C^{L}}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{S}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|}{|x-y|}.

Moreover, let
C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
be the set of Zygmund
continuous functions
\phi
on
\mathbb{S}
.
The seminorm of
C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
is defined by

\|\phi\|_{C^{Z}}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{S}}\frac{|\phi(x)+\phi(y)-2\phi((x+y)/2)|}{|x-y|}.

Modulo constant functions, the above seminorms become norms, and
the function spaces defined above become Banach spaces.
There are inclusion relations

C^{L}(\mathbb{S})\hookrightarrow C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})\hookrightarrow C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
,
and the inclusion maps are continuous. In particular,

\|\phi\|_{C^{Z}}\leq\|\phi\|_{C^{L}}
holds.

These function spaces can be generalized by introducing Besov spaces.
For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
the
m
-th order difference of a function
\phi
on
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
is defined as

\Delta_{t}^{1}\phi(x)=\phi(x+t)-\phi(x);\quad\Delta_{t}^{m+1}\phi(x)=\Delta_{t}^{m}\phi(x+t)-\Delta_{t}^{m}\phi(x).

Definition 1
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<p,\,q\leq\infty
, a seminorm of an integrable
function
\phi
on
\mathbb{S}
is given by

\|\phi\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}}=\left(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|t|^{-sq}\|\Delta_{t}^{\lfloor s\rfloor+1}\phi\|_{L_{p}}^{q}\frac{dt}{|t|}\right)^{1/q}.

When
q=\infty
, the
q
-th root of the integral above is understood as the supremum by the usual convention.
The set of those
\phi
with
\|\phi\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}}<\infty
is defined as the homogeneous
Besov space

\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{S})
.

In this paper, we only deal with the case
p=q=\infty
. For
0<\alpha<1
,
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})
is
identified with
C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
, and
\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})
is
identified with
C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
(see
[
12
, p.541]
).
By contrast, the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
,
with seminorm
\|\phi\|_{\dot{W}^{1}_{\infty}}=\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}
, is identified with
C^{L}(\mathbb{S})
.

We use interpolation for Besov spaces.
In general, real interpolation of Banach spaces is defined as follows.

Definition 2
.

Let
(X_{0},\|\cdot\|_{X_{0}})
and
(X_{1},\|\cdot\|_{X_{1}})

be Banach spaces that are continuously embedded into a common topological vector space,
which we call an admissible pair of Banach spaces. For
t>0
,

K(x,t)=\inf\,\{\|x_{0}\|_{X_{0}}+t\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}}\mid x=x_{0}+x_{1},\ x_{0}\in X_{0},\ x_{1}\in X_{1}\}

is defined for
x\in X_{0}+X_{1}
. Then, for
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
q\geq 1
, the set

(X_{0},X_{1})_{\sigma,q}=\{x\in X_{0}+X_{1}\mid\|x\|_{\sigma,q}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,t)^{q}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty\}

is defined to be the
real interpolation space
of
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
.

The real interpolation space
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\sigma,q}
is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\sigma,q}

(see
[
12
, Theorem 16.5]
).
For interpolation of Besov spaces, the following result is known (see
[
12
, Corollary 17.42]
).
The above definition of real interpolation can also be applied to homogeneous Besov spaces with seminorms
(see
[
12
, Remark 17.31]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
0<\sigma<1
, and
0<s_{0}<s_{1}
. Then

(\dot{B}^{s_{0}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{S}),\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{S}))_{\sigma,q}=\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{S}),

where
s=(1-\sigma)s_{0}+\sigma s_{1}
.

The boundedness of associated operators under real interpolation holds in the following form (see
[
12
, Theorem 16.12]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(X_{0},\|\cdot\|_{X_{0}})
and
(X_{1},\|\cdot\|_{X_{1}})

be an admissible pair of Banach spaces. Let
P:X_{0}+X_{1}\to X_{0}+X_{1}
be a linear transformation
such that its restrictions
P|_{X_{0}}
and
P|_{X_{1}}
are bounded linear transformations
on
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
, respectively. Then, for any
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
q\geq 1
,

P|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\sigma,q}}
is a bounded linear transformation on
the real interpolation space
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\sigma,q}
, and its operator norm satisfies

\|P|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\sigma,q}}\|\leq\|P|_{X_{0}}\|^{1-\sigma}\|P|_{X_{1}}\|^{\sigma}.

3.
Solution to Claim
1

Let
{\rm QS}(\mathbb{S})
denote the group of quasisymmetric
self-homeomorphisms of
\mathbb{S}
.
For
0<\alpha<1
, let
{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
be the subgroup of
{\rm QS}(\mathbb{S})

consisting of orientation-preserving self-diffeomorphisms
h:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{S}
(with non-vanishing derivatives)
such that the derivative
h^{\prime}
belongs to
C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
.
This condition is equivalent to requiring
\log h^{\prime}\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
.
The fact that
{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
is a group under composition can be verified easily.
In fact, this is a topological group in an appropriate topology (see
[
14
, Proposition 5.2]
).

Analogously, we define
{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}(\mathbb{S})
to be the subset of
{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
consisting of all such
h
that
h^{\prime}
belongs to
C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
.
In this case, this is equivalent to the condition that
\log h^{\prime}\in C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
since
h^{\prime}\neq 0
.
Indeed, a straightforward computation of the second order difference of
\log h^{\prime}

using (
1.2
) yields the desired estimate.
We show later in Proposition
3.2
that
{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}(\mathbb{S})

is a subgroup of
{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{S})\subset{\rm QS}(\mathbb{S})
.

We utilize
conformal welding
to prove Claim
1

h_{\mu}:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{S}
for

\mu\in M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})
into
the boundary extensions of conformal homeomorphisms
F_{\mu}:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
and
G^{\mu^{-1}}:\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\Omega^{*}

such that
h_{\mu}=(G^{\mu^{-1}}|_{\mathbb{S}})^{-1}\circ F_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{S}}
. From this, we obtain

\log h_{\mu}^{\prime}=-\log g^{\prime}\circ h_{\mu}+\log f^{\prime}

(3.1)

for
f=F_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{S}}
and
g=G^{\mu^{-1}}|_{\mathbb{S}}
.
Note that
h_{\mu}
belongs to
{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)

For

Lemma 3.1
.

For any

with

the composition operator
P_{h}
on

defined by

is a Banach automorphism of
C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
. Moreover, the operator norm of
P_{h}
is bounded by

\|h\|_{C^{1+\alpha}}
.

Proof.

Recall that
C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
coincides with
\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})
.
Then, we represent
\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})
by the real interpolation

\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})=(\dot{B}^{1-\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S}),\dot{B}^{1+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S}))_{1/2,\infty}

as a special case of Proposition
2.1
.
Here,
\dot{B}^{1-\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})=C^{1-\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
, and we set

\ddot{B}^{1+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})=\dot{B}^{1+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})\cap\dot{W}^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{S})

with seminorm
\|\phi\|_{\dot{B}^{1+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}+\|\phi\|_{\dot{W}^{1}_{\infty}}

C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{S})=\{\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{S})\mid\|\psi\|_{C^{1+\alpha}}:=\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{C^{\alpha}}+\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}<\infty\}.

Since
(\dot{B}^{1-\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S}),\dot{W}^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{S}))_{1/2,\infty}=\dot{B}^{1-\alpha/2}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})
by
[

,
which contains

, we eventually have

C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})=\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})=(\dot{B}^{1-\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S}),\ddot{B}^{1+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S}))_{1/2,\infty}.

(3.2)

For
\phi_{1}\in\dot{B}^{1-\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})=C^{1-\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
,
we have

\displaystyle|\phi_{1}\circ h(x)-\phi_{1}\circ h(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{1}\|_{C^{1-\alpha}}|h(x)-h(y)|^{1-\alpha}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{1}\|_{C^{1-\alpha}}\|h^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}^{1-\alpha}|x-y|^{1-\alpha}.

(3.4)

This implies that the composition operator
P_{h}
is bounded on

with operator norm at most
\|h^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}^{1-\alpha}\leq\|h\|_{C^{1+\alpha}}^{1-\alpha}
.

For
\phi_{2}\in\ddot{B}^{1+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})=C^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
,
we have

\displaystyle\ \quad|(\phi_{2}\circ h)^{\prime}(x)-(\phi_{2}\circ h)^{\prime}(y)|=|(\phi_{2})^{\prime}(h(x))h^{\prime}(x)-(\phi_{2})^{\prime}(h(y))h^{\prime}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\phi_{2})^{\prime}\|_{C^{\alpha}}\|h^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}^{1+\alpha}|x-y|^{\alpha}+\|(\phi_{2})^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|h^{\prime}\|_{C^{\alpha}}|x-y|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{2}\|_{C^{1+\alpha}}(\|h^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}+\|h^{\prime}\|_{C^{\alpha}})^{1+\alpha}|x-y|^{\alpha}.

As

is also bounded by

this implies that the composition operator
P_{h}
is bounded on
\ddot{B}^{1+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})
with operator norm at most
\|h\|_{C^{1+\alpha}}^{1+\alpha}
.

By these bounds for

on

and

2.2
implies that
P_{h}
is a bounded operator acting on the real interpolation of

in (

Since

and

is bounded on

Hence,

.
∎

Remark 2

We equip

In this setting, a standard estimate of a Beltrami coefficient under composition might be useful.

4.
The correspondence of
C^{Z}
and
B^{Z}

The following theorem is due to Zygmund. The boundedness of the operator can be
seen from the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 5.3]
. A proof in a more general setting can be found in
[
20
, V. Proposition 8]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

A holomorphic function
\Phi
in
B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
extends continuously to
\mathbb{S}
, thereby defining a function

\phi=\Phi|_{\mathbb{S}}
in
C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
.
The boundary extension operator
E:B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})\to C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
given in this way is
a Banach isomorphism onto its image.

The inverse
E^{-1}:E(B^{Z}(\mathbb{D}))\to B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
is given by the Poisson integral of
\phi
.
Moreover, this operator extends to all of
C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
as the
Szegö projection
defined by the Cauchy integral

{\mathcal{S}}(\phi)(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{\phi(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\zeta\quad(z\in\mathbb{D})

for
\phi\in C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
.
Then
{\mathcal{S}}(\phi)
belongs to
B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
.

On the other hand,
the
Hilbert transform
is defined by the singular integral

{\mathcal{H}}(\phi)(x)=\frac{1}{\pi i}\,{\rm p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{\phi(\zeta)}{\zeta-x}\,d\zeta\quad(x\in\mathbb{S}).

It is known that
{\mathcal{H}}
maps
C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
to itself.
This is a bounded linear operator on the Besov space
C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})=\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{S})

(see
[
9
, Proposition 4.7]
). Combined with Theorem
4.1
, this yields:

Proposition 4.2
.

The following hold:

(1)

{\mathcal{H}}:C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})\to C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
is a Banach automorphism with
{\mathcal{H}}\circ{\mathcal{H}}=I
;

(2)

{\mathcal{S}}:C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})\to B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
is a bounded linear operator such that

E\circ{\mathcal{S}}:C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})\to C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
is a bounded projection onto

E(B^{Z}(\mathbb{D}))
satisfying

E\circ{\mathcal{S}}=\frac{1}{2}(I+\mathcal{H})
.

We also consider the boundary extension operator
E:B^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})\to C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})

on
\mathbb{D}^{*}
. Here, the corresponding space of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{*}
can be given simply by reflection as

B^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})=\{\Psi\in{\rm Hol}(\mathbb{D}^{*})\mid\Psi(z)=\Phi(z^{*})^{*},\ \Phi\in B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})\}

with norm
\|\Psi\|_{B^{Z}}=\|\Phi\|_{B^{Z}}
, where
z^{*}=1/\bar{z}
is the reflection point of
z

with respect to
\mathbb{S}
.
By defining the Szegö projection

{\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\phi)(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{\phi(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\zeta\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}^{*}),

where the orientation of the line integral on
\mathbb{S}
is taken counterclockwise, we see that

E\circ{\mathcal{S}}^{*}
coincides with the bounded projection
\frac{1}{2}(I-\mathcal{H})

onto
E(B^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*}))
.
Then the identifications
E(B^{Z}(\mathbb{D}))\cong B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
and
E(B^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*}))\cong B^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})

under both boundary extension operators
E
yield
the topological direct sum decomposition

C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})\cong B^{Z}(\mathbb{D})\oplus B^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*}).

(4.1)

For
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\in M^{Z}(\mathbb{D})\times M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})
,
let
G(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be the normalized quasiconformal self-homeomorphism with

\mu_{G}|_{\mathbb{D}}=\mu_{1}
,
\mu_{G}|_{\mathbb{D}^{*}}=\mu_{2}
.
The normalization is imposed by fixing
0
,
1
, and
\infty
; this ensures that the image of
\mathbb{S}

under
G(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is bounded.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
\mu_{1}\in M^{Z}(\mathbb{D})
and
\mu_{2}\in M^{Z}(\mathbb{D}^{*})
, then

\log\gamma^{\prime}\in C^{Z}(\mathbb{S})
for

Proof.

Let

, where
\mu^{*}
denotes the reflection of a Beltrami coefficient

\mu
with respect to
\mathbb{S}
.
Then

by
[

, and we have

belongs to

and

\Lambda([\mu_{1}],[\nu_{1}])=\Lambda([\mu_{2}],[\nu_{2}])
. Then,

G(\mu_{1},\nu_{1})|_{\mathbb{R}}=G(\mu_{2},\nu_{2})|_{\mathbb{R}}
by the normalization fixing the three points on
\mathbb{R}
.
This implies that
[\mu_{1}]=[\mu_{2}]
and
[\nu_{1}]=[\nu_{2}]
, which can be verified
by the same proof as
[
22
, Proposition 4.1]
.
Hence,
\Lambda
is injective.
∎

Now, Claim
2
is deduced from the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

The image of
\Lambda
contains
the real subspace
{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof of Claim
2
.

We lift any quasisymmetric homeomorphism in
{\rm Diff}^{1+Z}(\mathbb{S})
to
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

such that
\log h^{\prime}\in{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
. By Theorem
5.2
, this lies in the image of
\Lambda
,
which implies that there exists
\mu\in M^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})
such that
\Lambda([\mu],[\bar{\mu}])=\log h^{\prime}
.
Hence,
h=G(\mu,\bar{\mu})|_{\mathbb{R}}=H(\mu)|_{\mathbb{R}}
, and thus the claim follows.
∎

Theorem
5.2
is proved by applying the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.3
.

\Lambda
is locally biholomorphic at the origin
([0],[0])\in T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the derivative of
\Lambda
at
([0],[0])
is surjective.
Then the inverse mapping theorem yields the statement.
If we fix the first coordinate as
[0]
, then taking the Teichmüller projection for (
4.5
), we see that

\Lambda([0],\cdot)
is nothing but
the pre-Bers embedding
\beta:T^{Z}_{*}\to B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})
, where
B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})
is identified
with its image in
C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
under the boundary extension isomorphism
E
.
We note that in the case of the half-plane, the pre-Schwarzian derivative map

L:M^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H}^{*})\to B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})
is factored through the Teichmüller projection to
\beta
,
as with the Bers embedding.

It follows that
the derivative
d_{([0],[0])}\Lambda
maps the tangent subspace along the second coordinate onto
B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})
.
In the same way,
d_{([0],[0])}\Lambda
maps the tangent subspace along the first coordinate
onto
B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H}^{*})
. Since
C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})=B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})\oplus B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H}^{*})

by (
5.1
), we have that
d_{([0],[0])}\Lambda
is surjective.
∎

Let
M^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})
be the set of all Beltrami coefficients on

\mathbb{H}
that are
0
in some neighborhood of
\mathbb{R}
,
and define
T^{\omega}=M^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})/\sim
. Then

T^{\omega}
is a dense subset of
T^{Z}
. This is because
for every
\mu\in M^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})

the cut-off sequence
\mu\cdot 1_{\{{\rm Im}z>1/n\}}\in M^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})

converges to
\mu
in
M^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})
as
n\to\infty
in the norm

\|\cdot\|_{Z}
.
Let
{\rm Re}\,C^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
be the linear subspace of
{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})

consisting of real-analytic periodic functions.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
h=H(\mu)|_{\mathbb{R}}
for
\mu\in M_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})
. Then
[\mu]\in T^{\omega}

if and only if
\log h^{\prime}\in{\rm Re}\,C^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

If
\mu\in M^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})
, then
H(\mu)
is conformal in some neighborhood of
\mathbb{R}
.
In particular,
h
is real-analytic on
\mathbb{R}
, and so is
\log h^{\prime}
.
Conversely, if
\log h^{\prime}\in{\rm Re}\,C^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
, then

h(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\exp(\log h^{\prime}(t))\,dt
is a real-analytic self-diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
equivariant under the action of

x\mapsto x+2\pi
.
This extends to an equivariant conformal homeomorphism on some neighborhood of
\mathbb{R}
, and further extends
quasiconformally to
\mathbb{H}
. The complex dilatation
\mu^{\prime}
of this quasiconformal self-homeomorphism
H

is Teichmüller equivalent to
\mu
and belongs to
M_{\rm per}^{\omega}(\mathbb{H})
. This shows that

[\mu]\in T^{\omega}
.
∎

Remark 3
.

We have transferred our arguments from
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{S}
to
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{R}
with periodicity.
The reason for doing so is the following advantage:
(1) The pre-Schwarzian derivative map

, or more generally,
\widetilde{\Lambda}
given by (

),
is well defined as a map from the Teichmüller space;
(2) For a suitably given function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}
,
a homeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
can be constructed simply by
\int_{0}^{x}\exp(\phi(t))\,dt
, as in the proof of Proposition
5.4
.

Let
\Delta(T^{Z})=\{([\mu],[\bar{\mu}])\in T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}\mid[\mu]\in T^{Z}\}
be the anti-diagonal axis of
T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}
,
which is a real-analytic submanifold real-analytically equivalent to
T^{Z}
. For
T^{\omega}
, we also define

\Delta(T^{\omega})=\{([\mu],[\bar{\mu}])\mid[\mu]\in T^{\omega}\}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

{\rm Re}\,C^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
is dense in
{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
and
is contained in the image of
\Lambda
.

Proof.

Since
{\rm Re}\,C^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
is a linear subspace of
{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
,
it suffices to show denseness near the origin. By Lemma
5.3
,
\Lambda
is a local homeomorphism on
some neighborhood of the origin, and by Proposition
5.4
,

\Delta(T^{\omega})\subset T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}
and

{\rm Re}\,C^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})\subset C^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})

correspond under
\Lambda
. Since
T^{\omega}
is dense in

, we have that

{\rm Re}\,C^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
is dense in
{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
.
The fact that
{\rm Re}\,C^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
is contained in the image of
\Lambda

also follows from Proposition
5.4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

By Lemma
5.3
, there is a neighborhood
U
of
([0],[0])
in
T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}

such that
\Lambda
maps
U
biholomorphically onto a neighborhood
\Lambda(U)
of the origin in
C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
.
By Lemma
5.5
, any
\phi\in{\rm Re}\,C^{\omega}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
is represented as
\phi=\Lambda([\mu],[\bar{\mu}])

for some

, that is,
\phi=\log h^{\prime}
for

.
Applying (

), we have

\Lambda(R_{[\mu]}(U))=Q_{h}(\Lambda(U))=P_{h}(\Lambda(U))+\phi.

Since this is a neighborhood of

.
∎

6.
The real-analytic structure of
T^{Z}

We have shown in Lemma
4.4
that
\Lambda
is a holomorphic injection.
In this section, we assert more:

Theorem 6.1
.

The map
\Lambda:T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}\to C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})

is a biholomorphism onto its image.

By Theorem
5.2
,
C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
lies in the image of
\Lambda
, and in fact,

\Lambda(\Delta(T^{Z}))={\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
.
Moreover,
\Lambda
is locally biholomorphic on a neighborhood
U
of
([0],[0])\in T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}

by Lemma
5.3
, and, by right translations as in the proof of Theorem
5.2
, this extends to a neighborhood of
\Delta(T^{Z})
.
Thus,
\Lambda
is a biholomorphism from a neighborhood of
\Delta(T^{Z})
onto its image, which is a neighborhood of

{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
in
C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
.
This already yields the following corollary to Theorem
6.1

without establishing the biholomorphy of
\Lambda
on the entire
T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}
.

Corollary 6.2
.

The Teichmüller space
T^{Z}
is real-analytically equivalent to
the real Banach space
{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
.

Corollary 6.3
.

T^{Z}
is real-analytically contractible; that is,

T^{Z}
contracts to a point via a real-analytic homotopy.

Remark 4
.

Our arguments also apply to the Teichmüller space
T^{\alpha}\cong{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}_{*}(\mathbb{S})

for
0<\alpha<1
investigated in
[
14
]
.
Hence,
T^{\alpha}
is real-analytically equivalent to
the real Banach space
{\rm Re}\,C^{\alpha}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
, and

T^{\alpha}
is real-analytically contractible. Topological contractibility of
T^{\alpha}
was
proved by using a global continuous section to the Teichmüller projection constructed by
the barycentric extension of the elements of
{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{S})
(see
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

We outline the proof, since the full arguments appear in
[
18
, Theorem 9.1]
for a different
Teichmüller space, and essentially the same method applies here.

Since
\Lambda
is a holomorphic injection by Lemma
4.4
,
it suffices to prove that the inverse
\Lambda^{-1}
is holomorphic.
By the inverse mapping theorem (see
[
4
]
), it is enough to show that the derivative
d\Lambda
is surjective.
The proof of the surjectivity of

d_{([\mu],[\nu])}\Lambda:{\mathscr{T}}_{([\mu],[\nu])}(T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*})\to C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})

on the tangent space
{\mathscr{T}}_{([\mu],[\nu])}(T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*})
at
([\mu],[\nu])\in T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}

proceeds in the following steps.

(i)
The image of the tangent space

{\mathscr{T}}_{([\mu],[\nu])}(T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*})\cong B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H}^{*})\oplus B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})

under
d_{([\mu],[\nu])}\Lambda
is the algebraic sum

P_{h_{\mu}}B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H})+P_{h_{\nu}}B^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{H}^{*}),

where
h_{\mu}=H(\mu)|_{\mathbb{R}}
and
h_{\nu}=H(\nu)|_{\mathbb{R}}
.

(ii)
If
\Lambda([\mu_{0}],[\nu_{0}])
lies in the real subspace
i\,{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
, then

d_{([\mu_{0}],[\nu_{0}])}\Lambda
is surjective onto
i\,{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
.
It follows from this and (i) that
d_{([\mu_{0}],[\nu_{0}])}\Lambda
is surjective for such a pair
([\mu_{0}],[\nu_{0}])
.

(iii)
We establish (ii)
by relying on the result in
[
22
]
concerning the biholomorphy of
\Lambda
on a certain domain
in the product of the BMO Teichmüller spaces. This domain contains
T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}
, and
the surjectivity of
d\Lambda
for this larger space can be invoked.

(iv)
If
\log\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\in i\,{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
, then
\gamma_{0}
is parametrized by arclength.
Any curve
\gamma
with
\log\gamma^{\prime}=\Lambda([\mu],[\nu])
can be reparametrized by arclength, and
this is achieved by an affine translation
Q_{h}
of
C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
.

(v)
For
\Lambda([\mu_{0}],[\nu_{0}])=\log\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\in i\,{\rm Re}\,C^{Z}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R})
,
move
\log\gamma_{0}^{\prime}
by the affine translation
Q_{h}
, and
translate
([\mu_{0}],[\nu_{0}])
in parallel by the right translation
R_{[\lambda]}

with
H(\lambda)|_{\mathbb{R}}=h
.
Then, by (ii) and Proposition
4.5
,
the surjectivity of
d_{([\mu],[\nu])}\Lambda
at any point
([\mu],[\nu])\in T^{Z}\times T^{Z}_{*}
follows.
∎

7.
Another proof of
M^{Z}\Rightarrow A^{Z}

In this section, as an appendix, we give a technical but constructive proof of the implication
(1)\Rightarrow(3)
in
Theorem
1.2
. It also make the estimate of the norm of a Schwarizan derivative
in terms of the norm of a Beltrami coefficient clear.
This method is used in
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
to prove
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
in the case of
T^{\alpha}
,
but it can also be applied to the present case.
We state the results more generally for
0<\alpha<2
, though our interest is in
\alpha=1
.
For ease of presentation, we replace the hyperbolic density
1-|\zeta|^{2}
in
\mathbb{D}
by
1-|\zeta|
; since

1-|\zeta|\leq 1-|\zeta|^{2}\leq 2(1-|\zeta|)
, this causes no difficulty.

Theorem 7.1
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,2)
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\alpha

such that if a Beltrami coefficient
\mu\in M(\mathbb{D}^{*})
satisfies

\|\mu\|_{\alpha}:=\underset{|z|>1}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\,((|z|-1)^{-\alpha}\lor 1)|\mu(z)|<\infty,

then for the quasiconformal self-homeomorphism
F_{\mu}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with complex dilatation
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}^{*}

and
0
on
\mathbb{D}
, the Schwarzian derivative of
F_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{D}}
satisfies

(1-|\zeta|)^{2-\alpha}|S_{F_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{D}}}(\zeta)|\leq C\|\mu\|_{\alpha}

for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
.

We prepare the following two lemmas for the proof of this theorem.

Lemma 7.2
.

For any
\alpha
with
0<\alpha<2
,
there exists a constant
\lambda

(0<\lambda<1)
depending only on
\alpha
such that
if a sequence
\{s_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
of positive real numbers satisfies the recurrence relation

\left(\frac{1}{1+s_{n-1}}\right)^{2}{s_{n}}^{\alpha}=\lambda^{n}

for every
n\geq 1
and
s_{0}=1
,
then
\{s_{n}\}
is increasing and diverges to
+\infty
.

Proof.

The recurrence relation is equivalent to

s_{n}=\lambda^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}(1+s_{n-1})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}

for every
n\geq 1
with
s_{0}=1
. Modifying this formula, consider

s^{\prime}_{n}=\lambda^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}{s^{\prime}_{n-1}}^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}

(7.1)

for every
n\geq 2
, with the initial value
s^{\prime}_{1}=s_{1}=(4\lambda)^{1/\alpha}
.
It is easy to see that
s_{n}\geq s^{\prime}_{n}
for every
n\geq 1
. Hence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}s^{\prime}_{n}=+\infty
implies
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}=+\infty
.
Moreover, if
\{s^{\prime}_{n}\}
is increasing, then so is
\{s_{n}\}
.

Set
b_{n}=s^{\prime}_{n+1}/s^{\prime}_{n}
. Then (
7.1
) becomes

b_{n}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(b_{n-1})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}

for every
n\geq 2
, and

b_{1}=\frac{s^{\prime}_{2}}{s^{\prime}_{1}}=\frac{\lambda^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}(4\lambda)^{\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}}}}{(4\lambda)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}.

Taking logarithms yields

\log b_{n}=\frac{2}{\alpha}\log b_{n-1}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\lambda,\qquad\log b_{1}=\left(\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\log\lambda+\left(\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\log 4.

From these equations, if

\log b_{1}>\frac{-\log\lambda}{2-\alpha},

then
\log b_{n}
is positive and uniformly bounded away from
0
for all
n\geq 1
.
Choosing
\lambda<1
sufficiently close to
1
achieves this.
For instance, one may take
\lambda>\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{(2-\alpha)^{2}/(2+\alpha)}
.
Thus
\{s^{\prime}_{n}\}
is increasing and diverges to
+\infty
.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

For a finite sequence of real numbers

1=R_{-1}<R_{0}<R_{1}<\cdots<R_{N}<R_{N+1}=+\infty,

let
A_{i}=\{R_{i}<|z|<R_{i+1}\}
be an annulus in
\mathbb{D}^{*}
for each
i=-1,0,\ldots,N
.
Define

\mu_{i}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mu(z)\,\quad(z\in A_{i}),\\[2.0pt]
\ 0\qquad\,(z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus A_{i}),\end{array}\right.

for
\mu\in M(\mathbb{D}^{*})
, and set
k_{i}=\|\mu_{i}\|_{L_{\infty}}
. Then

|S_{F_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{D}}}(\zeta)|\leq 12\sum_{i=-1}^{N}\frac{k_{i}}{(R_{i}-|\zeta|)^{2}}\qquad(\zeta\in\mathbb{D}).

Proof.

First take a quasiconformal self-homeomorphism
F_{N}=F_{\mu_{N}}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with complex dilatation

\mu_{N}
and consider the push-forward
\tilde{\mu}_{N-1}=(F_{N})_{*}\mu_{N-1}
.
Then take

F_{N-1}=F_{\tilde{\mu}_{N-1}}
and the push-forward

\tilde{\mu}_{N-2}=(F_{N-1}\circ F_{N})_{*}\mu_{N-2}
,
and continue inductively. For each
i\geq 0
,
let
F_{i}=F_{\tilde{\mu}_{i}}
be a quasiconformal self-homeomorphism with complex dilatation

\tilde{\mu}_{i}
, and let

\tilde{\mu}_{i-1}=(F_{i}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N})_{*}\mu_{i-1}
be the push-forward
of
\mu_{i-1}
by
F_{i}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N}
.
Finally, choose
F_{-1}=F_{\tilde{\mu}_{-1}}
so that

F_{-1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N}=F_{\mu}
.

The chain rule for Schwarzians shows that

\displaystyle S_{F_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{D}}}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=S_{F_{N}}(\zeta)+\cdots+S_{F_{-1}}(F_{0}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N}(\zeta))(F_{0}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N})^{\prime}(\zeta)^{2}

(7.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=-1}^{N}S_{F_{i}}(F_{i+1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N}(\zeta))(F_{i+1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N})^{\prime}(\zeta)^{2}.

(7.3)

By the standard estimate for the Schwarzian derivative,
the conformal homeomorphism
F_{N}
of the disk

\Omega_{N}=\{|\zeta|<R_{N}\}
into
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies

|S_{F_{N}}(\zeta)|\leq\frac{6k_{N}R_{N}^{2}}{(R_{N}^{2}-|\zeta|^{2})^{2}}\leq\frac{6k_{N}}{(R_{N}-|\zeta|)^{2}}.

(7.4)

On the other hand, the conformal homeomorphism
F_{i}
of the
quasidisk
\Omega_{i}
with hyperbolic density
\rho_{\Omega_{i}}
into
\mathbb{C}
for
-1\leq i\leq N-1
,
where
\Omega_{i}
is the image of the disk
\{|\zeta|<R_{i}\}
under

F_{i+1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N}
, satisfies

|S_{F_{i}}(\omega)|\leq 2\cdot 6k_{i}\,\rho_{\Omega_{i}}(\omega)^{2}

for every
\omega\in\Omega_{i}
.
Setting
\omega=F_{i+1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N}(\zeta)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\ |S_{F_{i}}(F_{i+1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N}(\zeta))(F_{i+1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N})^{\prime}(\zeta)^{2}|

(7.5)

\displaystyle\leq 12k_{i}\,\rho_{\Omega_{i}}(F_{i+1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N}(\zeta))^{2}|(F_{i+1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{N})^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}=\frac{12k_{i}R_{i}^{2}}{(R_{i}^{2}-|\zeta|^{2})^{2}}\leq\frac{12k_{i}}{(R_{i}-|\zeta|)^{2}}

(7.6)

for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
.
Plugging (
7.4
) and (
7.5
) into (
7.2
) gives

|S_{F_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{D}}}(\zeta)|\leq 12\sum_{i=-1}^{N}\frac{k_{i}}{(R_{i}-|\zeta|)^{2}}

for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

Fix
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
and estimate
(1-|\zeta|)^{2}|S_{F_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{D}}}(\zeta)|
.
Let
\tau=1-|\zeta|
and
\ell=\|\mu\|_{\alpha}
.
With the initial condition
t_{0}=\tau
, define
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
inductively by

\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau+t_{n-1}}\right)^{2}\cdot\ell{t_{n}}^{\alpha}=\lambda^{n}\cdot\ell\tau^{\alpha}

(7.7)

for some constant
\lambda
with
0<\lambda<1
.
This is equivalent to

\left(\frac{1}{1+s_{n-1}}\right)^{2}{s_{n}}^{\alpha}=\lambda^{n}

upon setting
s_{n}=t_{n}/\tau
with
s_{0}=1
.
By Lemma
7.1
,
we can choose
\lambda=\lambda(\alpha)\in(0,1)
such that the sequence
\{s_{n}\}
, and hence
\{t_{n}\}
,
is increasing and diverges to
+\infty
.
In particular, there is a smallest positive integer
N
such that
\ell{t_{N+1}}^{\alpha}\geq 1
.

As in Lemma
7.3
, define

A_{n}=\{R_{n}<|z|<R_{n+1}\}

(n=-1,0,\ldots,N)
with

R_{n}=1+t_{n}
,
R_{-1}=1
(
t_{-1}=0
), and
R_{N+1}=+\infty
.
Set
\mu_{n}=\mu\cdot 1_{A_{n}}
and
k_{n}=\|\mu_{n}\|_{L_{\infty}}
. Then

k_{n}\leq\ell{t_{n+1}}^{\alpha}
.
Applying Lemma
7.3
yields

(1-|\zeta|)^{2}|S_{F_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{D}}}(\zeta)|\leq 12(1-|\zeta|)^{2}\sum_{n=-1}^{N}\frac{k_{n}}{(R_{n}-|\zeta|)^{2}}\leq 12\sum_{n=-1}^{N}\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau+t_{n}}\right)^{2}\cdot\ell{t_{n+1}}^{\alpha}.

(7.8)

By the recurrence relation (
7.7
),
the last sum in (
7.8
) equals
\sum_{n=-1}^{N}\lambda^{n+1}\cdot\ell\tau^{\alpha}
, and hence

12\sum_{n=-1}^{N}\lambda^{n+1}\cdot\ell\tau^{\alpha}<\frac{12\ell}{1-\lambda}\tau^{\alpha}=\frac{12}{1-\lambda}\|\mu\|_{\alpha}(1-|\zeta|)^{\alpha}.

Therefore,

(1-|\zeta|)^{2-\alpha}|S_{F_{\mu}|_{\mathbb{D}}}(\zeta)|\leq C\|\mu\|_{\alpha}

with
C=12/(1-\lambda)
.
∎

Nonequilibrium Gaussian Distribution for Coupled Linear Langevin Equations
Hidetsugu Sakaguchi

Abstract
Explicit forms of nonequilibrium Gaussian distributions and heat flows are obtained for the Fokker-Planck equation corresponding to the coupled linear Langevin equations of two and three variables.



The statistical mechanics can be expressed with the Langevin equation and Fokker-Planck equation
[
1
]
.
Coupled linear Langevin equations have been studied in equilibrium and nonequilibrium statistical mechanics for interacting particles
[
2
]
. Non-reciprocity is a recent topic in several nonequilibrium systems
[
4
]
.
General theory of the Langevin equations and Fokker-Planck equations and the relation to the Gaussian distributions are discussed in the book by Gardiner
[
3
]
. In this short note, we show the explicit form of the Gaussian distribution and heat flows for coupled linear Langevin equations under the two or three heat reservoirs with different temperatures as the simplest example of linear nonequilibrium problems. The model equations are

\displaystyle\frac{dx}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-ax+by+\xi_{x}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{dy}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle cx-dy+\xi_{y}(t),

(1)

where
\xi_{x}(t)
and
\xi_{y}
are the Gaussian white noises satisfying
\langle\xi_{x}(t)\xi_{x}(t^{\prime})\rangle=2T_{x}\delta(t-t^{\prime})
and
\langle\xi_{y}(t)\xi_{y}(t^{\prime})=2T_{y}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\rangle
. The thermal equilibrium is not assumed. The corresponding Fokker Planck equation is

\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\{(ax-by)P\}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\{(-cx+dy)P\}+T_{x}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial x^{2}}+T_{y}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial y^{2}}.

(2)

Since the two variables
x
and
y
are generated from the linear combination of Gaussian noises, the stationary probability distribution is expected to obey the Gaussian distribution:

P=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\alpha\gamma-\beta^{2}/4}}e^{-\alpha x^{2}-\beta xy-\gamma y^{2}}.

(3)

Substitution of Eq. (
3
) into Eq. (
2
) yields

\displaystyle a+d-2\alpha T_{x}-2\gamma T_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle-2a\alpha+4T_{x}\alpha^{2}+c\beta+T_{y}\beta^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle-a\beta+2b\alpha+4T_{x}\alpha\beta-d\beta+2c\gamma+4T_{y}\gamma\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle b\beta+T_{x}\beta^{2}-2d\gamma+4T_{y}\gamma^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

The three variables
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
are explicitly expressed as

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(a+d)(T_{y}a^{2}+T_{y}ad+T_{x}c^{2}-T_{y}bc)}{2T_{x}T_{y}(a+d)^{2}+2(T_{x}c-T_{y}b)^{2}},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(a+d)(T_{y}b^{2}-T_{x}bc+T_{x}ad+T_{x}d^{2})}{2T_{x}T_{y}(a+d)^{2}+2(T_{x}c-T_{y}b)^{2}},

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2b\alpha+2c\gamma}{a+d}.

(4)

For the Guassian distribution (
3
),

\langle x^{2}\rangle=\frac{2\gamma}{4\alpha\gamma-\beta^{2}},\;\;\langle y^{2}\rangle=\frac{2\alpha}{4\alpha\gamma-\beta^{2}},\;\;\langle xy\rangle=-\frac{\beta}{4\alpha\gamma-\beta^{2}}.

(5)

The probability flows
J_{x}
and
J_{y}
are expressed as

\displaystyle J_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(ax-by)P+T_{x}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x},

\displaystyle J_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-cx+dy)P+T_{y}\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}

(6)

If
J_{x}=J_{y}=0
, the detailed balance condition is satisfied, and the thermal equilibrium state is realized. The equilibrium distribution is expressed as

P\propto\exp\{(-ax^{2}+2bxy-(dc/b)y^{2})/(2T_{x})\}

(7)

where
T_{y}
needs to satisfy
T_{y}=(c/b)T_{x}
.
In a system of two variables, there is a temperature
T_{y}
where the detailed balance condition is satisfied even if the coupling coefficients are not reciprocal
c\neq b
.
If
bT_{y}\neq cT_{x}
, the nonequilibrium stationary state is realized, and the probability flows exist.

If
a=0
,
b=1
,
c=-k
,
d=\eta
, and
T_{x}=0
, Eq. (1) is

\frac{dx}{dt}=v,\;\frac{dv}{dt}=-kx-\eta y+\xi_{y}(t),

The stationary distribution is
P\propto e^{-(kx^{2}+y^{2})/(2T)}
where
T=T_{y}/\eta
.
If
y
is interpreted as the velocity, this is the Boltzmann distribution for the total energy,
E=(1/2)kx^{2}+(1/2)v^{2}
for the temperature
T
, mass
m=1
, and the Boltzmann constant
k_{\rm B}=1
. Since
J_{x}
and
J_{y}
are not zero, the detailed balance condition is not satisfied for the Fokker-Planck equation of
x
and
y
.

In the reciprocal system
b=c
, the heat flows into the two heat reservoirs are calculated as
[
5
]

\displaystyle Q_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(ax-by)J_{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2a^{2}\gamma+2ab\beta+2b^{2}\alpha-4T_{x}a\alpha\gamma+T_{x}a\beta^{2}}{4\alpha\gamma-\beta^{2}},

\displaystyle Q_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(-cx+dy)J_{y}\rangle

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2c^{2}\gamma+2cd\beta+2d^{2}\alpha+T_{y}d\beta^{2}-4T_{y}d\alpha\gamma}{4\alpha\gamma-\beta^{2}}.

For
a=d=k+l
and
b=c=l
, Eq. (1) is
dx/dt=-kx+l(y-x)+\xi_{x}(t)
and
dy/dt=-ky+l((x-y)+\xi_{y}(t)
, and the heat flow
Q=Q_{x}
is explicitly expressed as

Q=\frac{Q_{x}-Q_{y}}{2}=\frac{l^{2}}{2(k+l)}(T_{y}-T_{x}).

(9)

Next, we consider the coupled linear Langevin equations with three variables. The Langevin equations are expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{dx}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{xx}x+a_{xy}y+a_{az}z+\xi_{x}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{dy}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{yx}x+a_{yy}y+a_{yz}z+\xi_{y}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{dz}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{zx}x+a_{zy}y+a_{zz}z+\xi_{z}(t),

(10)

where
\xi_{x}(t)
,
\xi_{y}
, and
\xi_{z}(y)
are the Gaussian white noises satisfying
\langle\xi_{x}(t)\xi_{x}(t^{\prime})\rangle=2T_{x}\delta(t-t^{\prime})
,
\langle\xi_{y}(t)\xi_{y}(t^{\prime})=2T_{y}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\rangle
, and
\langle\xi_{z}(t)\xi_{z}(t^{\prime})=2T_{z}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\rangle

The stationary solution of the corresponding Fokker-Planck equation is expressed as

P\propto\exp(-b_{xx}x^{2}-b_{yy}y^{2}-b_{zz}z^{2}-b_{xy}xy^{b}_{yz}yz-b_{zx}zx).

(11)

the coefficients
b_{xx},\cdots,b_{zz}
satisfy

\displaystyle-a_{xx}-a_{yy}-a_{zz}-2(T_{x}b_{xx}+T_{y}b_{yy}+T_{z}b_{zz})=0,

\displaystyle 2a_{xx}b_{xx}+a_{yx}b_{xy}+a_{zx}b_{zx}+4T_{x}b_{xx}^{2}+T_{y}b_{xy}^{2}+T_{z}b_{zx}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle a_{xy}b_{xy}+2a_{yy}b_{yy}+a_{zy}b_{yz}+T_{x}b_{xy}^{2}+4T_{y}b_{yy}^{2}+T_{z}b_{yz}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle a_{xz}b_{zx}+a_{yz}b_{yz}+2a_{zz}b_{zz}+T_{x}b_{zx}^{2}+T_{y}b_{yz}^{2}+4T_{z}b_{zz}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle 2a_{xy}b_{xx}+a_{xx}b_{xy}+2a_{yx}b_{yy}+a_{yy}b_{xy}+a_{zx}b_{yz}+a_{zy}b_{zx}+4T_{x}b_{xx}b_{xy}+4T_{y}b_{xy}b_{yy}+2T_{z}b_{zx}b_{yz}=0,

\displaystyle a_{xy}b_{zx}+a_{xz}b_{xy}+a_{yy}b_{yz}+2a_{yz}b_{yy}+2a_{zy}b_{zz}+a_{zz}b_{yz}+2T_{x}b_{xy}b_{zx}+4T_{y}b_{yy}b_{yz}+4T_{z}b_{yz}b_{zz}=0,

\displaystyle a_{xx}b_{zx}+2a_{xz}b_{xx}+a_{yx}b_{yz}+a_{yz}b_{xy}+2a_{zx}b_{zz}+a_{zz}b_{zx}+4T_{x}b_{xx}b_{zx}+2T_{y}b_{xy}b_{yz}+4T_{z}b_{zx}b_{zz}=0.

(12)

If the coupling constants are reciprocal, i.e.,
a_{xy}=a_{yx}
,
a_{yz}=a_{zy}
, and
a_{zx}=a_{xz}
, there is a potential energy,
U=(1/2)a_{xx}x^{2}+(1/2)a_{yy}y^{2}+(1/2)a_{zz}z^{2}+a_{xy}xy+a_{yz}yz+a_{zx}zx
, and the thermal equilibrium distribution is given by
P\propto\exp(-U/T)
at
T_{x}=T_{y}=T_{z}=T
. If the coupling constants are not reciprocal, the conservativity is not generally satisfied in three-variable systems
[
6
]
. Even in the non-reciprocal system, the stationary Gaussian distribution is obtained.
For
a_{xx}=a_{yy}=a_{zz}=-3
,
a_{xy}=a_{xz}=a_{yz}=a_{yz}=a_{zx}=1
,
a_{zy}=0.2
,
T_{x}=1
,
T_{y}=2
, and
T_{z}=4
,
b_{xx}=1.2780
,
b_{yy}=0.75509
,
b_{zz}=0.4280
,
b_{xy}=-0.8690
,
b_{yz}=-0.1713
, and
b_{zx}=-0.4979
were numerically obtained for the coupled nonlinear equations (12).
Figure 1(a) compares the stationary solution of the Fokker Planck equation corresponding to the Langevin equation Eq. (
10
) obtained by direct numerical simulation and the Gaussian distribution Eq. (
11
) along the diagonal
x=y=z
. The Gaussian distribution is obtained even in the nonequilibrium stationary state.

Q_{x}
,

as a function of

.

In the reciprocal system, the heat flows into the three reservoirs can be calculated from

\displaystyle Q_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(-a_{xx}x-a_{xy}y-a_{xz}z)\frac{\partial J_{x}}{\partial x}\rangle,

\displaystyle Q_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(-a_{yx}x-a_{yy}y-a_{yz}z)\frac{\partial J_{y}}{\partial y}\rangle,

\displaystyle Q_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(-a_{zx}x-a_{zy}y-a_{zz}z)\frac{\partial J_{z}}{\partial z}\rangle.

(13)

Here, the averages with respect to the Gaussian distribution can be given by

\displaystyle\langle x^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4b_{yy}b_{zz}-b_{yz}^{2}}{8S},\langle y^{2}\rangle=\frac{4b_{xx}b_{zz}-b_{zx}^{2}}{8S},

\displaystyle\langle z^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4b_{xx}b_{yy}-b_{xy}^{2}}{8S},\langle xy\rangle=\frac{b_{yz}b_{zx}-2b_{zz}b_{xy}}{8S},

\displaystyle\langle yz\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b_{xy}b_{zx}-2b_{xx}b_{yz}}{8S},\langle zx\rangle=\frac{b_{xy}b_{yz}-2b_{yy}b_{zx}}{8S},

where

S=b_{xx}b_{yy}b_{zz}+(b_{xy}b_{yz}b_{zx}-b_{xx}b_{yz}^{2}-b_{yy}b_{zx}^{2}-b_{zz}b_{xy}^{2})/4.

For example, the heat flows
Q_{x},Q_{y}
and
Q_{z}
are evaluated as
Q_{x}=0.8
,
Q_{y}=0.2
, and
Q_{z}=-1.0
for
a_{xx}=a_{yy}=a_{zz}=-3
,
a_{xy}=a_{xz}=a_{yz}=a_{yz}=a_{zx}=a_{zy}=1
,
T_{x}=1
,
T_{y}=2
, and
T_{z}=4
.
Q_{x}+Q_{y}+Q_{z}=0
is always satisfied. Figure 1(b) shows
Q_{x}
,
Q_{y}
, and
Q_{z}
as a function of
T_{y}
for
T_{x}=1
,
T_{z}=4
,
a_{xx}=a_{yy}=a_{zz}=-3
, and
a_{xy}=a_{xz}=a_{yz}=a_{yz}=a_{zx}=a_{zy}=1
.
Q_{x}
,
Q_{y}
, and
Q_{z}
satisfy
Q_{x}=0.2(T_{z}-T_{x})+0.2(T_{y}-T_{x})
,
Q_{y}=0.2(T_{z}-T_{y})+0.2(T_{x}-T_{y})
, and
Q_{z}=0.2(T_{x}-T_{z})+0.2(T_{y}-T_{z})
. The heat flows are proportional to the temperature differences in the coupled linear systems.

We have shown the Gaussian distributions and heat flows for the coupled linear Langevin equations under the two or three heat reservoirs with different temperatures as one of the simplest linear nonequilibrium problems.

TFF1-mediated suppression of the TCA cycle promotes bone metastasis in triple-negative breast cancer
Authors: Hanshen Chen, Yiping Xu, Yixuan Liu, Yijie Tang, Jiapeng Qiu, Luyao Lei, Peicheng Lin, Yiran Chen, Xiaodan Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•TFF1 overexpression promotes bone metastasis in TNBC.•TFF1-mediated metabolic reprogramming drives metastatic phenotype in TNBC.•Targeting the TFF is a potential strategy for treating TNBC metastasis.Keywords: Triple-negative breast cancer, Bone metastasis, TCA cycle, TFF1, CRISPR/Cas9 screening

Introduction

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) lacks the expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and HER2, accounts for 10–20% of all breast cancer cases and has the worst prognosis because of its aggressive nature and few treatment options [[1], [2], [3]]. The bone metastases cause severe skeletal-related events (SREs) like pathologic fractures, spinal cord compression, and hypercalcemia, which significantly diminish the mobility, quality of life, and survival of the individuals affected [4]. Among new metastatic breast cancer diagnoses, 62.5% show bone metastasis, with Luminal breast cancer making up 70.5% of these instances [5,6]. TNBC shows a higher tendency for metastasis in visceral organs such as the lung and brain, with less expression of bone-specific adhesion molecules and chemokines [7,8]. Bone metastasis is not commonly seen in TNBC, but its presence is linked to a poor prognosis, making the investigation of its mechanisms and treatment strategies clinically significant [9].

Bone metastasis is driven by a complex interplay between tumor cells and the bone microenvironment, involving multiple signaling pathways and cellular interactions [10,11]. Critical regulatory processes involve the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), tumor-produced factors such as parathyroid hormone-related protein (PTHrP), and the activation of the RANK/RANKL/OPG pathway [[12], [13], [14]]. Luminal subtypes have a significantly higher incidence of bone metastases than other subtypes [15]. This difference can be partly explained by the expression of chemokine receptors, such as chemokines, that are more prevalent in Luminal tumors and are directed towards bone [4]. In addition, ER signaling in Luminal breast cancer may boost interactions with bone stromal cells and change the bone microenvironment to facilitate metastatic colonization [16]. In luminal breast cancer, the ER-regulated secretory protein SCUBE2 facilitates bone-specific metastasis by triggering Hedgehog-mediated osteoblastic niches [17]. Researcher found that LTβ from tumors activates NF-κB2 in osteoblasts, leading to the secretion of CCL2/5, which enhances tumor-osteoblast adhesion and osteoclast formation [18]. The process of bone metastasis in TNBC differs significantly from that in the Luminal subtypes. In triple-negative breast cancer, silencing CTNND1 leads to bone metastasis by activating the CXCR4/CXCL12 pathway, which encourages neutrophil infiltration and inhibits cytotoxic T-cell activity [19]. Bone metastasis in TNBC is characterized by osteolytic destruction caused by factors secreted by the tumor and activation of the Akt/mTOR pathway, consistent with the known role of S6K1 [[20], [21], [22]].

To precisely identify the central regulators responsible for the osseous colonization of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), we employed a convergent functional genomics strategy: combining CRISPR/Cas9 library screening with transcriptome sequencing (RNA-seq). Initially, we developed the MDA-MB-231/MB cells by performing five repeated cycles of intracardiac injection, harvesting bone metastases, and ex vivo culture, thereby enriching a population with acquired bone-metastatic ability. By comparing the differences in the transcriptome levels of the two types of cells through RNA-seq, we obtained the acquired bone metastasis-related genes of TNBC cells. To determine the primary genes involved in bone metastasis of TNBC, we introduced a CRISPR/Cas9 knockout library in MDA-MB-231 cells and subjected them to in vivo selection via intracardiac injection, allowing metastatic outgrowth in bone. Deep sequencing of sgRNA distributions in bone metastases allowed for the quantification of individual guide enrichment or depletion relative to the baseline. The integration of transcriptomic profiling and functional CRISPR/Cas9 library screening was used to identify significant drivers of bone metastasis in TNBC. This approach enabled us to emphasize genes that are both crucial for bone colonization and differentially expressed.

Methods

Cell lines and cell culture

The human TNBC cell line MDA-MB-231 was obtained from Procell. Cells were authenticated using STR and cultured in DMEM medium with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibico) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. They were kept at 37°C in a humidified environment with 5% CO₂.

Establishment of bone-metastatic TNBC cells

The MDA-MB-231/BM subline, which targets bones, was developed from the original MDA-MB-231 triple-negative breast cancer cell line by undergoing five consecutive in vivo selection rounds (P1–P5) to increase bone affinity. Intracardiac injection of parental cells was performed into the left ventricle of female NOD-SCID mice (5×105 cells per mouse). Upon development of metastasis-related morbidity, tumor cells specifically metastasized to bone were aseptically excised, dissociated into single-cell suspensions, and expanded in vitro under standard culture conditions. Cells derived from these bone metastases were then reinjected intracardially into a new cohort of NOD-SCID mice to initiate the subsequent round of selection. This cycle of in vivo passage was repeated consecutively for five generations (P1 to P5). Following the fifth round (P5), cells from bone metastases were expanded in vitro to develop the stable bone-metastatic subline known as MDA-MB-231/BM.

RNA sequencing

For RNA sequencing, RNA integrity was assessed using the RNA Nano 6000 Assay Kit of the Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). Differential expression analysis of two groups (MDA-MB-231/MB vs. MDA-MB-231, three biological replicates) was performed using the DESeq2 R package (1.20.0). Genes with an absolute |log2FC| ≥ 0.585 and an adjusted P-value (Padj) < 0.05 found by DESeq2 were assigned as differentially expressed. We used clusterProfiler R package to test the statistical enrichment of differential expression genes in KEGG pathways.

In vivo CRISPR/Cas9 library screening to identify genes linked to bone metastasis in TNBC cells

The Toronto human knockout pooled library (TKOv3) was a gift from Jason Moffat (Addgene #125517). This library contains 70948 sgRNAs targeting 18053 protein-coding genes. Library amplification and virus preparation were conducted in accordance with the Moffat Lab protocols. The TKOv3 lentiviral library was employed to transduce MDA-MB-231 cells at an MOI below 0.3, guaranteeing that each cell acquired a single sgRNA. Forty-eight hours post-infection, cells underwent selection with 1 ng/μL puromycin for 7 days to remove non-transduced cells, establishing the baseline cell pool (Day 0) with over 1000 coverage, and cells with a coverage of 500 were preserved by freezing for future preparation. A cohort of 40 NOD-SCID mice (6 weeks old, female) received intracardiac injections of the T0 cell pool (5×105 cells per mouse). Six weeks following implantation, bone metastases were dissected aseptically, and genomic DNA was extracted from the pooled metastatic tissues. The abundance of sgRNAs was amplified from genomic DNA through PCR with barcoded primers and analyzed using next generation sequencing (NGS). The relative abundance of each sgRNA in bone metastases and compared to the T0 baseline was quantified using MAGeCK-VISPR algorithm.

Wound Healing Assay

A sterile 200-μL pipette tip was used to scratch confluent monolayers of adherent cells, forming uniform linear wounds, and PBS washes were applied to gently remove dislodged cells. To reduce interference from proliferation, cells were incubated in a medium without serum. After 24 h, wound closure was observed using a microscope.

Transwell migration and invasion assays

For the invasion assays, polycarbonate membranes were coated with Matrigel and polymerized at 37°C for an hour, while the migration assays utilized membranes that were not coated. Cells were serum-starved for 12 h, harvested with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA, and resuspended in serum-free medium at 5×10⁵ cells/mL. Subsequently, 200 μL of cell suspension was added to the upper chamber, with the lower chamber containing 600 μL of complete medium with 20% FBS as a chemoattractant. After incubating for 24 hours, non-migratory cells were eliminated with a cotton swab, and the cells that migrated or invaded the underside of the membrane were fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with crystal violet.

Establishment of PDHA1/TFF1 knockout cells

Cas9-expressing MDA-MB-231 cells were transduced with lentiviral particles encoding the PDHA1/TFF1 sgRNAs construct for 48 h, then cultured in media with puromycin (1 ng/μL) to select successfully transduced cells. Western blotting was performed to assess knockout efficiency. The sgRNA sequences were listed as follows: sgPDHA1: 5’- GAAATGTGACCTTCACC GGC-3’, sgTFF1: 5’- GAACAAGGTGATCTGCGCCC-3’.

Western blotting

Antibodies used were anti-human PDHA1 Antibody (#66119-1-Ig), anti-human TFF1 Antibody (#13734-1-AP), anti-β-Actin antibody (#20536-1-AP) from Proteintech. Cells were isolated using RIPA buffer (#PC101, Epizyme) and Protease Inhibitor Cocktail (#GRF101, Epizyme) as per the instructions provided by the manufacturer. The protein was subjected to SDS-PAGE electrophoresis, transferred onto a PVDF membrane, and detected using chemiluminescence (Omni-ECL, Epizyme), with β-Actin serving as a loading control.

Oxygen Consumption rates (OCRs) measurement

MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-231/MB cells were seeded in XFp cell culture microplates (Agilent Technologies) at a density of 15000 cells per well and incubated overnight. The cells were rinsed with assay medium at pH 7.0 and placed in a non-CO2 incubator for one hour prior to analysis. The cells were rinsed with assay medium at a pH of 7.0 and placed in a non-CO2 incubator for 1 h prior to analysis. oligomycin (1.5 μM), FCCP (1 μM), and rotenone/antimycin A (Rot/AA; 0.5 μM) mixture were sequentially injected into the wells at indicated time points prior to OCR measurements by the Seahorse XFp Analyzer (Agilent). All experiments were conducted six times with different biological samples.

Animal experiments

Animal experiments were carried out in accordance with Institute of Biophysics, Chinese Academy of Science’s Policy on Care and Use of Laboratory Animal. Male 6-week-old Nod-SCID mice were obtained from the Shanghai Wushi Experimental Animal Center (Shanghai, China). To investigate the influence of TFF1 knockout on TNBC metastasis, luciferase-labeled MDA-MB-231/MB transduced with either sgTFF1 or control sgRNA were injected intracardially into Nod-SCID mice (6 weeks old, female, n=10 per group). Six weeks following implantation, bioluminescence imaging was conducted utilizing the IVIS Spectrum system (PerkinElmer). Mice harboring luciferase-expressing metastatic tumors received an intraperitoneal injection of D-luciferin at a dosage of 150 mg/kg, administered 10 minutes prior to imaging.

Statistical analysis

Data were shown as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Non-paired Student’s t test was used to compare the means of two groups using GraphPad Prism 10. The correlation analysis was performed using Spearman correlation method. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was used to compare Nod-SCID mice survival using a log-rank test (GraphPad Prism 10).

Results

Establishment and functional evaluation of bone-metastatic TNBC cells

We established the MDA-MB-231/BM subline, a bone-metastatic variant of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), by conducting five rounds of in vivo selection (P1–P5) (Fig. 1A). The procedure included injecting parental MDA-MB-231 cells into the hearts of NOD-SCID mice, isolating bone metastases, growing them in vitro, and reinjecting them over five generations. RNA-seq analysis was performed to identify genes with differential expression between the final round of bone-metastatic TNBC cell line and the parental cell line. Parental MDA-MB-231 cells were transfected with the TKOv3 library [23] to establish a baseline cell pool (T0) for parallel CRISPR screening, which was then injected into Nod-SCID mice (n=40) via the intracardial injection. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) facilitated the identification of sgRNAs that were either increased or decreased in bone metastases, uncovering genes connected to bone metastasis (BMRG) (Fig. 1A). Functional tests showed that MDA-MB-231/BM cells had a significantly enhanced capacity for metastasis (Fig. 1B-D). The MDA-MB-231/BM cells demonstrate a more rapid wound healing process than the parental cells (Fig. 1B). Transwell migration and invasion assays indicated a notable increase in cells crossing the basement membrane (Fig. 1C). In vivo imaging of mice using bioluminescence indicated that P2 round bone-metastatic MDA-MB-231 cells had a greater ability for systemic metastasis, while the final MDA-MB-231/MB cell line was specifically prone to bone metastasis (Fig. 1D).

Establishment and functional evaluation of bone-metastatic TNBC cells. (A) Experimental workflow for generating the bone-metastatic subline and CRISPR screening. (B) Wound healing assays reveal that MDA-MB-231/BM cells close wounds significantly more rapidly than parental MDA-MB-231 cells at both 0 h and 24 h. (C) Transwell assays show that MDA-MB-231/BM cells have significantly higher migration and Matrigel invasion abilities compared to the parental cells. (D) Bioluminescence imaging uncovers that the final MDA-MB-231/BM subline (P5) possesses an increased bone-specific metastatic potential compared to the original parental cells (P0) and P2 round bone-metastatic cell line. Error bars indicate mean ± SD; *p < 0.05, ***p < 0.001 (Student’s t-test).

Dual-omics research uncovers TCA cycle and adaptation to hypoxia in TNBC bone metastasis

The dual-omics study highlighted a disruption in crucial mitochondrial metabolic regulators, with a significant depletion of essential genes in the TCA cycle, including SDHB, IDH3A, PDHA1, MDH1 and OGDHL. Along with the simultaneous enrichment of glycolytic enzymes (G6PD, PGK1, and PKM) and focal adhesion modulators (MAPK1, COL4A5) (Fig. 3A). The transcriptomic heatmap analysis indicated a decrease in the expression of essential TCA cycle genes in MDA-MB-231/BM (Fig. 3B). The analysis of intersections uncovered shared pathway involvements: TCA cycle (SDHB, MDH1, IDH3A, OGDH, OGDHL, SUCLA2, PDHA1 and IDH1), directly connected to enrichment of HIF-1 signaling (PDHA1, MAPK1, PGK1), indicating that PDHA1 plays a crucial role in metabolic reprogramming under hypoxic conditions (Fig. 3C). KEGG enrichment of differentially expressed genes in MDA-MB-231/MB cells enriched in Carbon metabolism, Autophagy, TCA cycle, HIF-1 signaling pathway and VEGF signaling pathway (Fig. 3D).

TCA cycle dysfunction contributes to the metastasis of TNBC

CRISPR screening showed that the cells with sgRNAs of core TCA cycle enzymes are enriched in metastatic tumors, yet they are significantly depleted in MDA-MB-231/MB (Fig. 4A). Protein-protein interaction analysis indicated a strong functional association among the TCA cycle enzymes (Fig. 4B). The sgRNA abundances of core TCA cycle genes (SDHB, IDH3A, OGDHL and IDH1) were significantly higher in metastatic tumors (Fig. 4C). We next analyzed the proteomes (CPTAC) and transcriptomes (TCGA) of biopsies obtained from breast cancer tissues and adjacent non-tumor tissues. As observed in Fig. 4D, core enzymes of TCA cycle are down-regulated in both in protein and transcriptional levels. Although SDHB scored higher in the standalone CRISPR screen, PDHA1 was selected for further investigation as it uniquely intersected both the TCA cycle and the hypoxia-adaptive HIF-1 signaling pathways in our dual-omics analysis.

Integrated proteomic (CPTAC) and transcriptomic (TCGA) analysis of paired tumor and adjacent tissues indicates a consistent reduction in TCA cycle enzymes at both mRNA and protein levels.

Suppression of the TCA cycle promotes TNBC bone metastasis

To study the impact of the TCA cycle on TNBC bone metastasis, we knocked out PDHA1 expression in MDA-MB-231. Western blot analysis verified the successful knockout of PDHA1 in MDA-MB-231 cells when compared to non-targeting controls, using β-Actin as a loading control (Fig. 5A). Wound healing tests showed significantly enhanced migration ability in PDHA1 KO cells compared to NC cells (Fig. 5B). PDHA1 KO cells demonstrated significantly enhanced migration and invasion abilities in Transwell assays when compared with NC controls. (Fig. 5C). Mitochondrial stress tests using the Seahorse platform exposed severe respiration impairment in MDA-MB-231/BM cell line, which revealed diminished basal/maximal oxygen consumption rates and lower ATP generation (Fig. 5D). Consistently, the JC-1 staining indicates mitochondrial membrane potential was decreased in MDA-MB-231/BM. (Fig. 5E). To further provide direct evidence that PDHA1 and TCA cycle activity contribute to bone metastasis, we performed a direct rescue experiment by overexpressing PDHA1 in MDA-MB-231/BM cells. Consistently, restoration of PDHA1 significantly impaired the migration and invasion capacities of these cells (Fig. S1).

TFF1 suppresses the TCA cycle and enhances TNBC metastasis

We performed an in-depth analysis of the dual-omics results to investigate how TNBC influences the TCA cycle to enhance metastasis. We found that Trefoil Factor 1 (TFF1) [24], belonging to the trefoil family, was the gene with the highest level of overexpression in MDA-MB-231/BM cells compared to the parental cells (Fig. 3B). CRISPR screening concurrently indicates a decrease in the abundance of MDA-MB-231 cells with TFF1 knockout (Fig. 3C). TCGA data analysis showed that TFF1 expression had a negative correlation with the expression of important TCA cycle enzymes, especially PDHA1 (Pearson correlation: -0.46, p < 0.001) (Fig. 6A). Through the analysis of breast cancer molecular subtypes, the expression of TFF1 is particularly elevated in luminal breast cancer, surpassing its levels in normal tissues and other molecular subtypes (TCGA cohort) (Fig. 6B). To detect the expression levels of TFF1 and PDHA1 in various TNBC cell lines at different round (WT, P1-P5) of bone metastasis selection, we performed Western blot and observed that as bone metastasis tendency grew stronger, TFF1 was markedly upregulated, while PDHA1 experienced a downregulation (Fig. 6C). The rescue experiment shows that knocking out TFF1 in MDA-MB-231/BM cells can restore the dysfunction of PDHA1 (Fig. 6D). Would healing assay shows that TFF1 knockout downregulate the migration ability of MDA-MB-231/BM cells (Fig. 6E). Transwell migration and invasion assays shows that TFF1 knockout downregulate the migration and invasion abilities of MDA-MB-231/BM cells (Fig. 6F). Mitochondrial stress tests showed TFF1 knockout in MDA-MB-231/BM cell line could recovery basal/maximal oxygen consumption rates and higher ATP generation (Fig. 6G).

TFF1 knockout suppresses bone metastasis and prolongs survival in vivo

Bioluminescence imaging (BLI) revealed attenuated bone metastatic signals in Nod-SCID mice (n=10 per group) injected with MDA-MB-231/BM TFF1 knockout cells versus parental MDA-MB-231/BM 6 weeks after injection (Fig. 7A). Quantification confirmed significant reduction in BLI intensity for TFF1 knockout MDA-MB-231/BM cells (Fig. 7B). The Kaplan-Meier curves showed a notably extended survival in the TFF1-KO group (n=10, p = 0.0019) (Fig. 7C). Histopathology (H&E staining) validated reduced osseous lesion burden in TFF1-KO group (Fig. 7D).

Discussion

Bone metastasis remains the most severe complications of breast cancer, greatly affecting patient morbidity and survival [25]. Although, the overall occurrence of bone metastasis in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is less than in luminal subtypes, once bone metastasis occurs in TNBC patients, the prognosis is extremely poor [24]. Our research uncovers a crucial metabolic reprogramming process that allows TNBC to acquire the ability to metastasize to bones. Through dual-omics approaches involving transcriptomic profiling and CRISPR/Cas9 functional screening, Trefoil Factor 1 (TFF1) was identified as a significant contributor to bone metastasis in TNBC by suppressing the TCA cycle. Earlier research has consistently shown that luminal breast cancers are more likely to metastasize to the bones, partially because they express chemokine receptors that home to bone and estrogen signaling pathways that alter the bone microenvironment [26]. Importantly, TFF1 is a well-known gene responsive to estrogen, showing high expression levels in luminal breast cancers, and its increased expression has been closely linked to a higher risk of bone colonization [27]. Our analysis of TCGA data confirmed that TFF1 expression is markedly higher in luminal subtypes as opposed to TNBC and normal breast tissue, hinting at a subtype-specific regulatory influence on metastasis.

We extend this paradigm by proving that TNBC, even in the absence of estrogen receptor signaling, can anomalously upregulate TFF1 expression as it gains bone-metastatic properties. Using a bone metastasis selection model in vivo, we found that the bone-metastasis TNBC subline MDA-MB-231/BM has markedly elevated TFF1 levels compared to its parental cells. The upregulation was accompanied by the inhibition of core TCA cycle genes, including PDHA1, IDH3A, and SDHB, essential for mitochondrial respiration and energy metabolism. Seahorse-based functional assays confirmed that TNBC cell lines with a tendency for bone metastasis showed impaired mitochondrial respiration, reduced ATP production, and decreased mitochondrial membrane potential. Our dual-omics studies and follow-up tests confirmed that TFF1 functions as an upstream inhibitor of the TCA cycle and contributes to the bone metastasis ability of TNBC. Interestingly, the deletion of TFF1 in bone-metastatic TNBC cells resulted in the recovery of PDHA1 expression, the restoration of mitochondrial oxidative function, and a notable decrease in migration and invasion in vitro. Moreover, in vivo metastasis evaluations showed that eliminating TFF1 not only lessened skeletal tumor burden but also considerably lengthened the survival time of mice. These results demonstrate that TFF1-driven metabolic reprogramming is key for TNBC cells to adapt to and settle in the bone microenvironment, which is known for being hypoxic and requiring significant energy.

In our perspective, we propose that TFF1-induced suppression of the TCA cycle represents a metabolic adaptation of TNBC cells to the bone marrow microenvironment. Although the bone microenvironment is energetically demanding, it is also characterized by hypoxia and immunosuppressive factors; under such stress, proliferating cells heavily reliant on canonical oxidative phosphorylation are susceptible to ROS accumulation. By downregulating the TCA cycle, TFF1 may drive a metabolic shift toward alternative pathways, such as enhanced glycolysis or altered amino acid utilization, thereby mitigating oxidative damage and conferring a survival advantage during the initial seeding and colonization phases. Intriguingly, the anomalous upregulation of this classical estrogen-responsive gene in ER-negative TNBC further implies that localized hypoxic cues or alternative transcription factors within the bone niche might act as powerful non-canonical triggers to drive TFF1 expression. This intriguing phenotypic mimicry highlights the extreme metabolic plasticity of aggressive TNBC seeds, which can pragmatically usurp Luminal-like metabolic traits to overcome their lineage constraints and adapt to the specific evolutionary pressures of the bone microenvironment.

Overall, our results indicate that while TFF1 is typically considered a marker for luminal breast cancer, its abnormal expression in TNBC acts as a metabolic switch that facilitates bone metastasis by inhibiting the TCA cycle. This surprising convergence of luminal and triple-negative breast cancers, both relying on TFF1, highlights the critical role of metabolic adaptability in the progression of metastasis. Our research suggests that TFF1 could serve not only as a biomarker but also as a potential therapeutic target for the prevention or treatment of bone metastasis in breast cancer. Targeting the TFF1–TCA axis might present a unique, subtype-specific method to disrupt the metabolic adaptability necessary for bone colonization in TNBC..

Conclusion

Our study identifies TFF1 as a key suppressor of the TCA cycle that promotes bone metastasis in TNBC. Loss of TFF1 results in improved mitochondrial function and a marked reduction in TNBC bone metastasis in both in vitro and in vivo studies. The results indicate a vital and targetable metabolic vulnerability in metastatic TNBC.

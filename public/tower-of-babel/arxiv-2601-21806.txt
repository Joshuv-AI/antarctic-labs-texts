Schrödinger’s principle eliminates the EPR-locality paradox
Walter F. Wreszinski

Abstract
We introduce a principle, implicitly contained in Schrödinger’s paper [ Schr35 ] , which allows a proof of the non-existence of the EPR-locality paradox in the Copenhagen interpretation of quantum mechanics. The paradox is shown to be well-posed already in the case of the simplest example of an entangled state of two spins one-half, independently of the (well-taken) objections by Araki and Yanase that the measurement of spin is not a local measurement. We assume that any measurement results in the collapse of the wave-packet.

The arguments of Araki and Yanase are certainly correct and well-taken, but, nevertheless, we argue that the paradox is well posed, due to

Notice that in (

We formulate Schrödinger’s description of this “trait” as an independent principle:

Schrödinger’s principle
: The characteristic “trait” of quantum mechanics is that two physical systems that are brought into
temporary interaction and then completely separated, can no longer be described,
even after the interaction has utterly ceased
, by
individual wave-functions for each of the two systems.

The word “interaction” used by Schrödinger in Schrödinger’s principle refers to the formation of an entangled state such as
(
1

]
)

Proof.

Under assumption 1), if the initial state is subject to a dynamics satisfying the assumptions of the Lieb-Robinson bound (
3

there is a finite group velocity FGV (
4

We now come to part 2.). Schrödinger’s principle implies that any measurement on Bob’s spin or Alice’s spin is to be regarded as

4
Schrödinger’s basic contribution to elucidate the essential features of quantum mechanics

We have seen that the proof of well-posedness and non-existence of the EPR-locality paradox depends on what we called Schrödiger’s
principle. It should be considered as part of what we believe are the
two
essential features of quantum mechanics, both
related to entanglement: a.) that
``1+1\neq 2^{\prime\prime}
, i.e., in the superposition of two product vectors in (
1
), it is wrong to say
that the system is described
either
by the first vector
or
by the second vector; b.) that
``2X1\neq 2^{\prime\prime}
, that is,

each
of the product two-spin states cannot be described by
individual
wave-vectors associated to one-spin systems.
In each case the false description in words corresponds to a “classical line of thought”, as denounced by Schrödinger. Case a.)
has been nicely explained by Feynman in sections 1-1 to 1-6 of Feynman’s lectures vol.3: ignoring this point amounts to implicit ignorance
of a phase, and, therefore, of the interference phenomenon. Case b.) is, however, more subtle, and, ignorance of this aspect is responsible
for the existence of the EPR-locality paradox, which, as we have shown in
3.1
, is well-posed, and depends on Schrödinger’s
principle in order to be declared non-existent.

On the Sumset of Sets of Size k k
Vincent Schinina

Abstract
The set ℛ G ​ ( h , k ) \mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k) consists of all possible sizes for the h h -fold sumset of sets containing k k elements from an additive abelian group G G . The exact makeup of this set is still unknown, but there has been progress towards determining which integers are present. We know that ℛ G ​ ( h , k ) ⊆ [ h ​ k − h + 1 , ( h + k − 1 h ) ] \mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)\subseteq\left[hk-h+1,\binom{h+k-1}{h}\right] , where the right side is an interval of integers that includes the endpoints. These endpoints are known to be attained. We will prove that the integers in [ h ​ k − h + 2 , h ​ k − 1 ] \left[hk-h+2,hk-1\right] are not possible sizes for the h h -fold sumset of a set containing k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 elements of a torsion-free additive abelian group G G . Furthermore, we will confirm that this interval can’t be made larger by exhibiting a subset of G G whose h h -fold sumset has size h ​ k hk .

Theorems about Ordered Additive Abelian Groups

In the previous section, we defined an ordered additive abelian group and some notation. We will now prove some known facts about them. Note that the additive abelian group properties will be used without referencing them.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be an ordered additive abelian group with respect to
\preceq
and let
a,b,c,d\in G
. If
a\preceq b
and
c\prec d
, then
a+c\prec b+d
.

Proof.

We begin by assuming that
a\preceq b
and
c\prec d
. Using the fact that
G
is ordered, we add
c
to both sides of
a\preceq b
(doesn’t require the ordered assumption if equality occurs) and
b
to both sides of
c\prec d
. This gives us

a+c\preceq b+c\quad\text{and}\quad b+c\prec b+d,

respectively. By the transitive property (or substitution if equality occurs), we see that
a+c\prec b+d
, as wanted.
∎

Lemma 2.2

Let
G

\preceq

Proof.

We start by assuming that

. For existence, we use the fact that
G
is ordered and add

to both sides. With simplifications, we obtain
0\prec-a+b

. So, let

For uniqueness, we assume that, there also exists

. By adding
-a
to both sides and simplifying, we see that

Conversely, suppose there exists a unique

A Missing Interval from
\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)

Before we can prove that the integers in
\left[hk-h+2,hk-1\right]
are missing from
\mathcal{R}_{G}(h,k)
, we need to prove a known fact about
h
-fold sumsets in an additive abelian group and then we will need to confirm some other useful preliminary results:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A=\left\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}\right\}
and
B=\left\{a_{1}+b,a_{2}+b,\dots,a_{k}+b\right\}
be subsets of an additive abelian group
G
. Note that
B
is a translation of
A
by
b
. Then,
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert hA\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert hB\right\rvert}
. In other words, the size of a sumset is translation invariant.

Proof.

To show that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert hA\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert hB\right\rvert}
, we will prove that
hB=\left\{a+hb\mid a\in hA\right\}
. First, we will confirm that
hB\subseteq\left\{a+hb\mid a\in hA\right\}
. Let
x\in hB
. By ?THM?
,

x=c_{1}\left(a_{1}+b\right)+\cdots+c_{k}\left(a_{k}+b\right),

for some
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}=h
. Using our notation and the abelian group properties, we can rewrite this as follows:

x=\left(c_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}a_{k}\right)+\left(c_{1}b+\cdots+c_{k}b\right).

Since
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}=h
and
A=\left\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}\right\}
, then the first summand is
h
-fold sum of elements of
A
and the second summand is just
b
added to itself
h
times. So,
a=c_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}a_{k}\in hA
, by ?THM?
. Hence, we have that

x=a+hb,

which means that
x\in\left\{a+hb\mid a\in hA\right\}
. Therefore, we get that
hB\subseteq\left\{a+hb\mid a\in hA\right\}
.

Next, we will show that
\left\{a+hb\mid a\in hA\right\}\subseteq hB
. Let
x\in\left\{a+hb\mid a\in hA\right\}
. By definition, this means that
x=a+hb
, for some
a\in hA
. Since
a\in hA
, then, by ?THM?
,

a=c_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}a_{k},

for some
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}=h
. By substitution, this leaves us with

x=\left(c_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}a_{k}\right)+\left(c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}\right)b.

Using our notation and the abelian group properties, we can rewrite this as follows:

x=c_{1}\left(a_{1}+b\right)+\cdots+c_{k}\left(a_{k}+b\right).

Since
B=\left\{a_{1}+b,a_{2}+b,\dots,a_{k}+b\right\}
, then
x
is an
h
-fold sum of elements in
B
, which means
x\in hB
, by ?THM?
. Hence,
\left\{a+hb\mid a\in hA\right\}\subseteq hB
.

With this, we can now conclude that
hB=\left\{a+hb\mid a\in hA\right\}
. Observe that the set on the right side is the same size as
hA
. Thus, it follows that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert hA\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert hB\right\rvert}
.
∎

.

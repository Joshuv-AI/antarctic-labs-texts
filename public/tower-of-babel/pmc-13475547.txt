Early Changes in the Neutrophil-to-Lymphocyte Ratio are Associated with Conversion Outcomes in Advanced Hepatocellular Carcinoma Treated with HAIC-Based Triple Therapy
Authors: Yang Wang, Xiaoyi Duan, Huanzhang Niu, Chaoyang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundReliable early biomarkers for identifying conversion potential during HAIC-based triple therapy in advanced unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC) remain lacking. This study evaluated the predictive value of dynamic systemic inflammatory remodeling during treatment.MethodsIn this single-center retrospective cohort study, we included 296 patients with advanced uHCC treated with hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy combined with tyrosine kinase inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitors. Patients were stratified according to conversion status. ΔNLR was calculated as the NLR measured after the first treatment cycle minus the baseline NLR. Predictors for conversion were identified using logistic regression and evaluated via receiver operating characteristic analysis. A predictive nomogram was subsequently constructed and validated using calibration and decision curve analyses.ResultsAmong 296 patients, 125 (42.2%) achieved successful conversion resection. Baseline NLR showed minimal predictive value for conversion (AUC = 0.501), whereas ΔNLR demonstrated significantly improved predictive performance (AUC = 0.819). The optimal cohort-derived cutoff was ΔNLR ≤ 0.20, with a sensitivity of 72.0% and a specificity of 91.8%. Multivariate analysis showed that higher ΔNLR was independently associated with lower odds of successful conversion (OR = 0.415, 95% CI: 0.328–0.524, P < 0.001). Patients in the conversion group achieved significantly improved tumor responses, overall survival, and progression-free survival. The nomogram incorporating ΔNLR demonstrated good calibration and clinical utility.ConclusionLower ΔNLR values after one treatment cycle were associated with successful conversion resection during HAIC-based triple therapy for advanced uHCC. ΔNLR may serve as a candidate early marker of conversion potential, pending prospective external validation.Keywords: hepatocellular carcinoma, hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy, conversion therapy, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the leading drivers of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Most patients present with unresectable disease (uHCC) at the time of diagnosis, often accompanied by a high tumor burden or macrovascular invasion, where the survival benefits of traditional systemic monotherapy remain extremely limited.1,2 In recent years, triple combination therapy consisting of hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy (HAIC), tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has emerged as a promising treatment strategy for advanced HCC by leveraging the potent local cytotoxicity of HAIC.3,4 This regimen has demonstrated profound synergistic antitumor activity and encouraging objective response rates, making conversion therapy a highly promising clinical strategy. Previous studies of HAIC-based combination therapy have reported objective response rates of approximately 67.6–80.7% and conversion-resection rates of 38.9–77.2%; however, most were small, single-arm, or retrospective studies with heterogeneous regimens and conversion criteria, and evidence regarding early predictors of successful conversion remains limited.3,5 Consequently, it provides a subset of patients who initially had no opportunity for surgery with the possibility of achieving secondary radical resection through deep biological downstaging.6 Solid clinical evidence indicates that successfully crossing the threshold of conversion and achieving an R0 resection is the core pillar for improving long-term survival outcomes of patients with advanced HCC, enabling long-term survival and even potential cure. However, while triple therapy drives efficient tumor shrinkage, it also reveals substantial individual heterogeneity in treatment response.7 The time window for conversion therapy is transient; therefore, accurately identifying patients with true conversion potential early in the course of intervention to secure the optimal surgical timing while avoiding overtreatment, reducing ineffective drug exposure, and minimizing cumulative organ toxicity remains a critical decision-making bottleneck in the field of HCC conversion therapy.8

HAIC-based triple therapy may be accompanied by changes in systemic inflammatory status, which can be longitudinally assessed using routinely available peripheral blood indices. Unfortunately, current evaluation systems used in clinical practice to predict HCC treatment response and prognostic stratification still rely heavily on baseline static parameters, including maximum initial tumor diameter, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels, and pre-treatment peripheral blood systemic inflammatory indices (such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio [NLR]).9–12 Although these baseline characteristics offer certain prognostic stratification value in traditional palliative interventions, they essentially represent only a static snapshot of tumor aggressiveness and host baseline immunity prior to intervention. Under the pressure of intensive local and systemic combined therapies, static indices completely lack the sensitivity required to track the evolutionary trajectory of the tumor microenvironment and dynamic host immune responses following drug exposure.13 It is frequently observed in clinical practice that patients who ultimately achieve successful conversion resection and those who experience early disease progression exhibit high overlap in their initial anatomical stage and baseline inflammatory burden. This strongly indicates that the realization of conversion potential is by no means solely determined by initial static anatomical boundaries, but depends more heavily on the dynamic trajectory of host systemic inflammatory remodeling during the early phase of combined intervention.14 Although previous studies have linked dynamic NLR changes to treatment response and survival in HCC, their association with successful conversion resection after HAIC-based triple therapy remains unclear.15,16 ΔNLR was selected a priori because its assessment after one treatment cycle provides an early within-patient measure of changes in the balance between neutrophil-mediated inflammation and lymphocyte-mediated antitumor immunity using routinely available blood counts, although its superiority over other dynamic inflammatory indices remains to be established.17,18

We hypothesized that early changes in NLR after one treatment cycle would be associated with successful conversion and provide additional information beyond baseline NLR. The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the association and discriminative performance of ΔNLR for successful conversion resection in patients receiving HAIC-based triple therapy. By providing an early indication of conversion potential, ΔNLR may complement radiological evaluation and help inform the timing of MDT reassessment, treatment continuation or modification, and surgical planning.

Methods

Study Design and Patient Selection

This study is a single-center, retrospective cohort study. We consecutively enrolled patients with advanced unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC) who received triple therapy consisting of hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy, tyrosine kinase inhibitors, and immune checkpoint inhibitors at our center between May 2020 and December 2025. Retrospective data extraction and analysis were performed after completion of the study period. This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the principles of Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in 2013).19 The study was formally approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Henan University of Science and Technology (Approval No. 2287), and the requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. All patient data were de-identified before analysis and handled confidentially throughout data extraction, storage, and analysis.

The inclusion criteria were: (1) age > 18 years; (2) HCC diagnosis confirmed by typical dynamic contrast-enhanced imaging (CT/MRI) or pathology, according to the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) or Chinese National Liver Cancer (CNLC) guidelines; (3) baseline assessment by our multidisciplinary team (MDT) confirming unresectability (including technical unresectability or oncological features such as macrovascular invasion); (4) completion of at least two cycles of HAIC plus TKIs and ICIs; and (5) availability of comprehensive baseline and follow-up clinical, laboratory, and imaging data. Exclusion criteria included: (1) prior history of surgical hepatectomy or liver transplantation; (2) presence of other primary malignancies; and (3) severe baseline cardiac, hepatic, or renal dysfunction, or missing critical clinical data. Based on whether they successfully achieved surgical conversion and underwent radical resection, the cohort was stratified into the Conversion group and the Non-conversion group. After applying rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria, 296 patients were enrolled and stratified into the conversion group (n = 125) and the non-conversion group (n = 171) based on their ultimate surgical outcomes.

Treatment Regimen

All patients received HAIC combined with a TKI and a PD-1 inhibitor. Treatment regimens were selected according to institutional practice and patient accessibility. HAIC was performed using the classic modified FOLFOX regimen (oxaliplatin, leucovorin, and 5-fluorouracil).20 Under DSA guidance, super-selective catheterization was performed to reach the tumor-feeding arteries. HAIC was administered using a modified FOLFOX regimen consisting of oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2 infused over 2 h, leucovorin 400 mg/m2 infused over 2 h, followed by fluorouracil 400 mg/m2 as a bolus and 2400 mg/m2 as a continuous infusion over 46 h. HAIC was generally repeated every 3 weeks until conversion surgery, disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or treatment withdrawal. Treatment was delayed or the chemotherapy dose was reduced according to the severity of hematological and non-hematological toxicities.

Systemic therapy was initiated concurrently with HAIC. The standard starting doses were lenvatinib 8 mg once daily for patients weighing <60 kg and 12 mg once daily for those weighing ≥60 kg, sorafenib 400 mg twice daily, or apatinib 250 mg once daily. Sintilimab, tislelizumab, or camrelizumab was administered intravenously at 200 mg every 3 weeks. TKIs were interrupted or dose-reduced according to the severity of treatment-related toxicity, whereas PD-1 inhibitors were withheld or permanently discontinued rather than dose-reduced in accordance with their prescribing information.

Assessment of Conversion Resection

During systemic intervention, patients underwent high-resolution triple-phase contrast-enhanced CT or MRI every 2 to 3 treatment cycles (approximately every 6 to 9 weeks). These results were strictly reviewed by an MDT comprising experienced hepatobiliary surgeons, interventional radiologists, oncologists, and radiologists. Indications for successful conversion resection were strictly defined as: (1) significant tumor regression or downstaging, making R0 resection anatomically feasible; (2) sufficient future remnant liver (FLR) volume as assessed by standard 3D imaging reconstruction; (3) significant regression or stabilization of tumor thrombi in major vascular trunks, with no uncontrollable distant extrahepatic metastases; and (4) good performance status and Child-Pugh class indicating hepatic reserve sufficient to tolerate radical surgery. MDT assessment was used to determine surgical eligibility rather than to define the final conversion outcome. Successful conversion was defined as completion of curative-intent hepatectomy with histologically confirmed negative microscopic margins (R0 resection). Radiological response was evaluated according to mRECIST, and no fixed percentage of tumor shrinkage or complete vascular recanalization was required. In patients with vascular invasion, resection was considered feasible only when the involved vascular segment could be completely removed or reconstructed while achieving R0 resection.

Clinical Data and Inflammatory Index Collection

Baseline demographic variables, clinico-pathological parameters, and routine laboratory data were systematically collected. Baseline laboratory values were defined as results from peripheral blood samples collected within one week prior to the first HAIC session. Absolute neutrophil and lymphocyte counts were extracted from the complete blood count to calculate the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR).

To quantify the early systemic immune-inflammatory remodeling effect, this study introduced a dynamic parameter, ΔNLR, defined as the difference between the NLR measured after one cycle of combination therapy and the baseline NLR (ΔNLR=NLRpost−treatment−NLRbaseline). Post-treatment blood samples were obtained during routine assessment before the second treatment cycle; however, the exact sampling day was not protocol-standardized and varied according to the clinical schedule.

Efficacy and Follow-Up Assessment

Local tumor response was assessed independently by two senior radiologists using the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) in a double-blind manner, with discrepancies resolved by a third senior physician. Best overall response was categorized as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD). The objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the percentage of patients achieving CR or PR; the disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the sum of CR, PR, and SD cases.

The primary survival endpoint was overall survival (OS), defined as the time from the initiation of the first combination treatment to death from any cause or the last follow-up. The secondary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the time from treatment initiation to the first documentation of tumor progression, death, or end of follow-up. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were collected throughout the follow-up period and graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE v5.0).21

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), while categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages. Intergroup comparisons were performed using independent-samples t-tests or Mann–Whitney U-tests, as appropriate for the data distribution. Survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the Log rank test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to evaluate the predictive performance of inflammatory indices for conversion, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. The optimal cutoff for ΔNLR was determined by maximizing the Youden index, and its sensitivity and specificity were internally assessed using 1000 stratified bootstrap resamples. Factors associated with conversion were analyzed using Logistic regression models. Univariate analysis was performed first, and variables with P < 0.10 were included in the multivariate Logistic regression model to identify independent predictors. Results are expressed as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The study included all consecutively eligible patients during the predefined study period. With 125 conversion events and two predictors in the final model, the events-per-variable ratio was 62.5, indicating an adequate sample size for exploratory model development. Sensitivity analyses were performed using proportional NLR change, calculated as (post-treatment NLR − baseline NLR)/baseline NLR, and the log-transformed NLR ratio, calculated as log(post-treatment NLR/baseline NLR). TKI and ICI categories were additionally incorporated as categorical covariates in a sensitivity multivariable model. Drug-stratified analyses and interaction tests were also performed.

A nomogram was constructed based on the independent predictors to quantify the probability of successful conversion. The agreement between predicted and observed conversion rates was assessed using calibration curves, and the clinical net benefit was evaluated using Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) across different threshold probabilities. Analyses were performed using available cases, with the number of observations reported for variables containing missing data; no imputation was performed. All statistical analyses were performed using RStudio, and a two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient Selection Flow and Baseline Characteristics

A total of 394 patients with advanced unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma who received HAIC plus TKIs and ICIs were retrospectively screened for this study. Based on the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, 98 patients were excluded, including 42 with a prior history of hepatectomy, 37 with other primary malignancies, and 19 due to missing critical clinical or imaging data. Ultimately, 296 patients were included in the final analysis. Among them, 125 patients successfully achieved conversion resection following systemic treatment and were assigned to the Conversion group, while 171 patients failed to meet the conversion criteria and were assigned to the Non-conversion group (Figure 1).

The baseline clinico-pathological characteristics of the two groups are summarized in Table 1. There were no statistically significant differences between the conversion and non-conversion groups regarding age, sex, Child-Pugh class, BCLC stage, etiological distribution, or tumor distribution (all P > 0.05).

Analysis of laboratory parameters demonstrated a high degree of baseline consistency between the two groups in terms of routine blood counts, liver and renal function, and coagulation profiles. Regarding baseline inflammatory markers, no significant differences were observed between the two groups in static inflammatory parameters, including absolute neutrophil counts, absolute lymphocyte counts, and the NLR (all P > 0.05). Association of Conversion Status with Tumor Response and Survival Outcomes

Tumor response evaluations based on the mRECIST criteria (Table 2) revealed that patients in the conversion group exhibited significantly improved tumor responses. The proportions of complete response and partial response in the conversion group were 24.8% and 28.8%, respectively, which were significantly higher than the 7.0% and 15.2% observed in the non-conversion group. Correspondingly, the objective response rate (ORR) in the conversion group reached 53.6%, significantly higher than the 22.2% in the non-conversion group; the disease control rate (DCR) was also significantly improved (88.0% vs 48.5%). Meanwhile, the incidence of progressive disease was as high as 51.5% in the non-conversion group compared to only 12.0% in the conversion group, suggesting that successful conversion is typically accompanied by more substantial tumor shrinkage and superior disease control.

Patients in the conversion group achieved significantly prolonged PFS (log-rank P = 0.003), with a median PFS of 15.0 months (95% CI: 12.0–17.8), whereas the median PFS in the non-conversion group was 10.6 months (95% CI: 9.0–12.1).

Predictive Performance of Dynamic Inflammatory Indices for Conversion

To quantify the early prognostic value of systemic inflammatory trajectories on conversion outcomes, we compared the predictive performance of a single static baseline index (Figure 3A) versus the early dynamic remodeling parameter (ΔNLR) (Figure 3B). ROC curve analysis confirmed that baseline static NLR had extremely poor discriminative ability for conversion potential. It had an AUC of only 0.501 (Figure 3C), which was close to random performance. In contrast, ΔNLR captures early systemic inflammatory remodeling. It showed good predictive ability, with the ROC curve shifting clearly toward the upper left and the AUC rising significantly to 0.819 (Figure 3D). The maximum Youden index identified ΔNLR ≤ 0.20 as the optimal cohort-derived cutoff, yielding a sensitivity of 72.0% and a specificity of 91.8%. In addition, sensitivity analyses using proportional NLR change and the log-transformed NLR ratio yielded similar AUCs of 0.809 and 0.801, respectively, supporting the robustness of the primary analysis based on absolute ΔNLR.

Factors Associated with Conversion and Prediction Model Construction

Univariate and multivariate Logistic regression models were utilized to identify independent clinical determinants of successful conversion (Table 3). Univariate exploration indicated that tumor size, red blood cell counts, and early ΔNLR were potentially associated with conversion opportunities (P < 0.10). Detailed results for all screened laboratory parameters are presented in Supplementary Table S1. Multivariable analysis showed that higher ΔNLR, representing a greater increase or smaller decrease in NLR after one treatment cycle, was associated with lower odds of successful conversion (OR = 0.415, 95% CI: 0.328–0.524; P < 0.001), whereas baseline tumor size <10 cm was associated with higher odds of successful conversion (OR = 2.401, 95% CI: 1.109–5.198; P = 0.026).

Subsequent subgroup forest plot analysis (Figure 4) revealed that across various clinical strata subdivided by ECOG PS, BCLC stage, etiology, and Child-Pugh class, the effect sizes (OR values) of ΔNLR for predicting successful conversion consistently remained to the left of the reference line (indicating a consistent inverse association between ΔNLR and successful conversion). Furthermore, interaction tests showed no significant heterogeneous interference (all P for interaction > 0.05). These findings suggest that early inflammatory remodeling may provide predictive value beyond baseline anatomical staging, serving as a highly sensitive, universal marker across the broader patient population. After adjustment for TKI and ICI categories, higher ΔNLR remained independently associated with lower odds of successful conversion (adjusted OR = 0.410, 95% CI: 0.324–0.519; P < 0.001). Drug-stratified analyses showed consistent inverse associations within each TKI and ICI category, with no significant interaction according to TKI or ICI type (P for interaction = 0.161 and 0.791, respectively; Supplementary Tables S2–S4).

Relying on the identified independent driving parameters and critical a priori clinical variables, we constructed a visual nomogram model incorporating baseline tumor size and ΔNLR scores to assist in early quantitative clinical decision-making (Figure 5A). To assess whether a simpler presentation could retain comparable performance, we additionally constructed a 0–2-point score by assigning one point each for ΔNLR ≤ 0.20 and tumor size <10 cm. The simplified score yielded an AUC of 0.803, compared with 0.826 for the original continuous model. The nomogram was therefore retained because it preserves the continuous information of ΔNLR and provides individualized probabilities of successful conversion. The calibration curve generated via internal resampling validation (Figure 5B) demonstrated good agreement along the diagonal between the nomogram-predicted conversion probabilities and the actual observed frequencies, confirming high predictive accuracy. Further Decision Curve Analysis (DCA, Figure 5C) indicated that utilizing this quantitative nomogram to guide early clinical triage and intervention adjustments yields substantial clinical net benefit across a wide range of predefined threshold probabilities, supporting its potential clinical utility.

The overall incidence of severe Grade 3–4 adverse events remained low, mainly manifesting as isolated cases of severe diarrhea, elevated transaminases, and secondary myelosuppressive thrombocytopenia. Notably, potentially fatal immune-related adverse events (irAEs) such as pneumonitis occurred at an extremely low rate (Grade 3–4 incidence < 1.0%). Specific toxicities such as reactive cutaneous capillary endothelial proliferation (RCCEP) were similarly rare, and all were effectively reversed following the prompt implementation of standard clinical management protocols. No unexpected or fatal toxicity signals were captured throughout the study period, confirming that high-intensity triple conversion therapy with HAIC plus TKIs and ICIs possesses a good safety profile for clinical application.

Discussion

With triple therapy (HAIC combined with TKIs and ICIs) demonstrating unprecedented tumor shrinkage in advanced unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC), the clinical management of advanced HCC is undergoing a paradigm shift from palliative maintenance toward conversion resection. Based on a large-scale, real-world cohort, this study not only confirms that successful conversion surgery can significantly reverse long-term survival outcomes but, more importantly, reveals the potential clinical value (ΔNLR) in predicting conversion potential. This finding suggests limitations of relying solely on baseline static indices for prognostic stratification and emphasizes the decisive role of dynamic biological trajectories in capturing the real-time interplay between the tumor and the host.

The most significant finding of this study is the dramatic contrast in predictive performance: while the baseline NLR at a single pre-treatment time point was nearly equivalent to random chance in predicting successful conversion (AUC = 0.501), the early dynamic change (ΔNLR) exhibited excellent discriminative power (AUC = 0.819). This disparity profoundly exposes the inherent limitations of baseline static indicators: they merely represent a static snapshot of tumor aggressiveness and host immune status prior to intervention, failing to capture the complex microenvironmental evolution following the initiation of combination therapy. Under the intense pressure of triple therapy, the tumor microenvironment undergoes substantial changes. The achievement of successful conversion is not solely anchored by initial anatomical boundaries or baseline inflammatory burden, but rather may be associated with dynamic changes in host inflammatory and immune status during treatment under pharmacological stress. This shift from focusing on initial state to evolutionary trajectory provides a more biologically profound basis for decision-making in precision conversion therapy.

A lower ΔNLR value was associated with a higher likelihood of successful conversion and may reflect a reduction in peripheral systemic inflammatory burden; however, peripheral NLR cannot directly demonstrate local tumor immune activation or intratumoral immune remodeling. It is worth noting that successful conversion signifies more than just anatomical shrinkage on imaging; it may also reflect favorable biological responses beyond anatomical tumor shrinkage.22 Patients who achieved successful conversion tended to have lower ΔNLR values, indicating a greater reduction in peripheral systemic inflammatory burden during treatment. However, peripheral NLR cannot directly characterize intratumoral immune dynamics or immune checkpoint activity, and its biological significance requires validation using tumor-tissue immune profiling.

In clinical practice, the time window for conversion therapy is extremely precious and fleeting.23 Prolonged ineffective intervention not only leads to the enrichment of resistant clones but also impairs the patient’s organ reserve due to cumulative drug toxicity, potentially causing them to lose the opportunity for surgery.24 The nomogram provides a convenient means of estimating individualized conversion probabilities and may support future risk stratification; however, its clinical implementation requires external validation and prospective evaluation. Compared with other dynamic biomarkers and radiomics-based models, ΔNLR is inexpensive and routinely available; however, its incremental value remains to be established through direct comparative and multimodal validation.25,26 For patients whose inflammatory burden is not effectively downregulated in the early stage of treatment, clinicians should be alert to the high risk of conversion failure and consider timely regimen adjustments or intensified multidisciplinary interventions to avoid the closure of the valuable surgical window.

This study has several limitations. First, it was a single-center retrospective study with potential selection bias, requiring multicenter external validation. Second, although treatment-adjusted and drug-stratified analyses showed a consistent inverse association between ΔNLR and successful conversion, the relatively small sample sizes of individual drug subgroups and the retrospective design limit definitive conclusions regarding regimen-specific generalizability. In addition, the exact timing of post-treatment blood sampling was not protocol-standardized, which may have introduced measurement variability in ΔNLR. Finally, ΔNLR was not directly compared with other dynamic inflammatory markers. Therefore, these findings should be considered hypothesis-generating, and the predictive performance of ΔNLR and the proposed model requires prospective multicenter external validation before clinical implementation.

In conclusion, in this single-center retrospective cohort, early changes in NLR were associated with successful conversion resection following HAIC-based triple therapy for advanced HCC. ΔNLR may serve as a candidate marker for conversion assessment; however, its biological basis, proposed cutoff, clinical utility, and generalizability across heterogeneous treatment regimens require prospective multicenter validation.

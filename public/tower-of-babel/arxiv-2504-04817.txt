Robustness of topological phases on aperiodic lattices
Yuezhao Li

Abstract
. We study the robustness of topological phases on aperiodic lattices by constructing *-homomorphisms from the groupoid model to the coarse-geometric model of observable C*-algebras. These *-homomorphisms induce maps in K-theory and Kasparov theory. We show that the strong topological phases in the groupoid model are detected by position spectral triples. We show that topological phases coming from stacking along another Delone set are always weak in the coarse-geometric sense.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we aim at a new look at the following question:

How to understand the robustness of topological phases of a discrete
physical system, described by a generic aperiodic point pattern?

We begin with some background around it. Topological insulators are
materials that are insulating in the bulk but nevertheless possess metallic
edges. The current flowing on the edge is usually quite robust under
disorder coming from impurity of the crystal. Starting from Bellissard,
van Elst and Schulz-Baldes

[
4
,
5
]
,
the non-commutative and C*-algebraic tools have been intensively applied
to the study of such materials and in particular, leading to an
interpretation of the Kubo formula in the integer quantum Hall effect
(IQHE) as a quantised index pairing.

In physics literature, the model Hamiltonian of a topological insulator is
usually given by a tight-binding, short-range operator supported on a
periodic, square
lattice, invariant under the translation by its unit cell vector. Such a
Hamiltonian
H
(or its spectral projection) belong to a (noncommutative)
torus
A_{\vartheta}=\mathbb{C}\rtimes_{\vartheta}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
the noncommutativity coming from a 2-cocycle twist
\vartheta

given by the external magnetic
field. If the Hamiltonian is not translation invariant, then Bellissard
suggested to replace
A_{\vartheta}
by a twisted crossed product
\mathrm{C}(\Omega)\rtimes_{\vartheta}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where
\Omega
is a compact
space called the hull of
H
.
This description is applicable even if the underlying space is no longer
a square lattice, but comes from a tiling subject to some properties, cf.

[
1
,
26
]
.

There are, however, amorphous materials, like liquid crystals and glass,
that cannot be modelled using such methods. Even if for quasi-crystals such
that the method of

[
1
,
26
]

applies, then one has to essentially provide a (non-canonical)
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-labelling of the underlying
lattice, under which the Hamiltonian remains short-range
(cf.
[
11
]
).

These suggest to use more general point sets in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
, modelling the atomic sites of this material, as their
underlying geometric spaces. The dynamics thereon
are studied by more general actions other than a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-translation.
It was explained in

[
3
]
that
such point sets should be
Delone sets
(
Definition

3.1
).
Let
\Lambda
be a Delone set, whose points are considered as the sites
in a physical system. How should one model the observable C*-algebra
A
from it?

The choice of an observable C*-algebra should be aligned with the
following principle: it should be
large
enough to contain all
possible Hamiltonians, and
small
enough to supply useful homotopy
theory (K-theory). In recent years, there have been two main approaches to
this modelling problem, which provide toolkits to compute invariants of
topological phases:

•

“dynamical” approach

describes a crossed product C*-algebra,
covariant for the
groupoid
actions on the aperiodic point pattern,
then restricts to the dynamical hull of the point pattern.
This gives a groupoid C*-algebra.

•

“coarse-geometric” approach

describes a C*-algebra which is stable
under all
short-range
,
locally–finite-rank
perturbations
that do not close the gap. This leads to a Roe C*-algebra.

The groupoid approach gives an étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}\rightrightarrows\Omega_{0}
from a Delone set
\Lambda
, and yields a “tight-binding” groupoid C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda})
,
cf.
[
3
,
11
,
10
,
22
]
.
This is a generalisation of the well-studied periodic model: if
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, then
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is
the group C*-algebra of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Here tight-binding means the
following: the regular representation of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda})
is
given by a Hilbert
\mathrm{C}(\Omega_{0})
-module, or equivalently, a
continuous field of Hilbert spaces over
\Omega_{0}
, where
\Omega_{0}
is unit space of the groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}
. The fibres of
this continuous field are canonically identified with
\ell^{2}(\omega)
,
where
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
is a Delone set, either as a translated copy
of
\Lambda
, or as a weak*-limit of such sets. Thus
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda})
consists of families of model Hamiltonians
(H_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega_{0}}
, where
H_{\omega}
acts on
\ell^{2}(\omega)
, in a covariant way with respect to the groupoid action.

The coarse-geometric approach describes a C*-algebra which is stable
under all possible short-range perturbations. This gives rise to a (uniform
or non-uniform) Roe C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
from
\Lambda
,
cf.
[
19
,
12
]
.
We choose to work with the non-uniform Roe C*-algebras, whose advantages
over the uniform ones were explained in
[
12
]
.

In this setup, a Delone set
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is considered as a
discrete metric space. Then
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
carries an ample representation
of
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Lambda)
, which generates a Roe C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
. Physically, a short-range Hamiltonian
H
has
matrix coeffcients
H_{x,y}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\left\langle x\,\middle|H\middle|\,\mathopen{}y\right\rangle
of fast enough
decay, thus can be approximated by controlled operators. Every unit cell
should have finite degrees of freedom, coming from the number of electron
orbits and their spins. Thus the restriction of
H
to any finite
region should have finite-rank. Therefore, the Roe C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
can be viewed as the universal C*-algebra that
contains all such Hamiltonians.

If we model a topological insulator on a Delone set
\Lambda
by the
Roe C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
, then its topological phase is
robust in a very strong sense: such topological phases (and their
associated topological invariants) are robust under any
short-range, locally–finite-rank perturbation that preserves the
spectral gap and the symmetry of the system.
It follows from the K-theory of
(real) Roe C*-algebra (cf. (
3.24
))
that such topological phase are completely classified by their topological
invariants, which are
\mathbb{Z}
- or
\mathbb{Z}/2
-valued indiced that belong to
the real K-theory groups of
\mathbb{R}
.
The groupoid model
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda})
, on the other hand, has
more involved K-theory and numerical invariants. One natural question is
in which sense those topological phases described by the groupoid model
are still robust. Moreover, do they
lead to interesting invariants that do not occur in the coarse-geometric
model?

Questions related to this type of robustness are usually answered by
showing that the numerical index is still continuous, even for some more
general perturbations of the Hamiltonian (which does not have to belong to
the observable C*-algebra), together with a “quantisation” statement
that the range of the numerical index is
\mathbb{Z}
. This implies that such
perturbations leave the topological invariants unchanged.

We describe a different approach to this robustness question by
comparing the groupoid model with the coarse-geometric model on
\Lambda
using the regular representation. The topological invariants defined in

[
10
]
factor through this
representation. Therefore, since
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
is “robust”, one
only needs to understand which topological phases in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda})
may still survive there. To this end, we introduce
a handy definition of position spectral triples
(
Definition

2.11
). Both the groupoid model and the
coarse-geometric model possess such type of spectral triples. In
particular, the position spectral triple
\xi^{\mathrm{Roe}}_{\omega}
over the Roe
C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\omega)
induces an isomorphism
\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{d,0},\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\omega))\to\mathbb{Z}

(
Theorem

3.33
). Then we show that
those topological invariants of the groupoid model, given by localising the
“bulk cycle”
\prescript{}{d}{\lambda}_{\Omega_{0}}
at
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
, are pullbacks
of the KK-class of
\xi^{\mathrm{Roe}}_{\omega}
under the comparison
*-homomorphism
\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}))\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\omega)

(
Theorem

4.1
). Such invariants are therefore
strong in the sense of
[
12
]
.

As another application, we show in
Theorem

4.10
that
certain numerical invariants on Delone sets, coming from “stacking”
lower-dimensional topological phases, must be weak, i.e. unstable under
perturbation. This generalises a result in

[
12
]
, which explains why certain topological
phases of the periodic model ought to be weak. More precisely, the authors
of
[
12
]
compare the
periodic model and the coarse-geometric model using an injective
*-homomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. It vanishes
on all but one
\mathbb{Z}
-component of K-theory. In particular, topological
phases coming from “stacking” lower-dimensional topological phases will
all vanish in
\operatorname{K}_{*}(\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
. This allows us to conclude that the
induced maps
\operatorname{K}_{*}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))\to\operatorname{K}_{*}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}))
also vanishes
there. We show that one can replace
\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
by a product Delone set
of the form
\Lambda\times L
, where
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{q}
are both Delone sets. Using the same strategy in

[
12
]
, we show that stacked topological phases
factor through the Roe C*-algebra of a flasque space, which has vanishing
K-theory. This allows us to conclude that such topological phases and their
numerical invariants have to vanish in the target Roe C*-algebra.

2.
Topological phases and K-theory

In the simplest form, a (non-interacting) quantum system is
described by an unbounded, self-adjoint operator
H
(the Hamiltonian)
acting on a complex, separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
A
topological insulator
is a quantum system
which carries certain symmetries, giving rise to
topological
invariants
that distinguish different
topological phases
of this
system.

The links between topological insulators and (real, bivariant)
K-theory has been investigated throughly in the recent decade,
cf.
[
28
,
17
,
8
]
.
In our case of interest, the links are made as follows:

(1)

For aperiodic materials, the physically
relevant symmetries are time-reversal symmetry, particle-hole symmetry and
chiral symmetry. These symmetries may be combined and give rise to several
symmetry types of the system. The
topological phase of such a material is represented by the class
[H]\in\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0},A)
of the Hamiltonian
in a certain bivariant K-theory (in fact,
\operatorname{KO}
-theory) group of a real C*-algebra
A
(the observable
real C*-algebra),
cf.
[
28
]
*Table 1

[
8
]
*Table 1

[
17
]
*Section 6.

(2)

The topological invariant of a topological insulator can be computed
as an
index pairing
. That is, a
Kasparov product
of the form

\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0},A)\times\operatorname{KK}(A\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d},\mathbb{R})\to\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,d},\mathbb{R})\simeq\operatorname{KO}_{n-d}(\mathbb{R}),

where

\operatorname{KO}_{n-d}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if }n-d\equiv 0,4{\hphantom{,6,7}}\!\mod 8;\\
\mathbb{Z}/2&\text{if }n-d\equiv 1,2{\hphantom{,6,7}}\!\mod 8;\\
0&\text{if }n-d\equiv 3,5,6,7\!\mod 8,\end{cases}

is the receptacle of
\mathbb{Z}
- or
\mathbb{Z}/2
-valued indices that can be
measured in physical experiments.

2.1.
“Real” and real C*-algebras

When we consider physical systems with anti-unitary symmetries, like
time-reversal or particle-hole symmetries, then we must represent them as
anti-unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}
which commute or anti-commute
with the Hamiltonian
H
.
This turns the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
into a “real” Hilbert
space
(\mathcal{H},\Theta)
, and the observable
C*-algebra into a “real” C*-algebra
(A,\mathfrak{r})
. The
latter was introduced by Kasparov
[
14
]
*Section 1,
Definition 3.

We recall the definition of “real” and real C*-algebras and Hilbert
spaces. A “real” C*-algebra is sometimes referred to as
a Real C*-algebra (cf.
[
9
]
,
note the upper case R) or a
\mathrm{C}^{*,\mathrm{r}}
-algebra
(cf.
[
17
]
) in the literature.

Definition 2.1

(
[
17
]
*Definition
3.7)
.

A “real” structure on a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded C*-algebra
A
is
a conjugate-linear,
grading-preserving *-automorphism
\mathfrak{r}\colon A\to A

of order 2. A “real”
C*-algebra is a C*-algebra together with a “real” structure on it.

A “real” involution on a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a conjugate-linear, grading-preserving automorphism
\Theta\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}

of order two. A “real” Hilbert space is a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

together with a “real” involution
\Theta
on it.

A representation of a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded “real” C*-algebra
A
is a
*-homomorphism
\pi\colon A\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
for a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded “real” Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, which intertwines both
the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-gradings and the “real” structures.

Let
\Theta
be a “real” involution on a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded
Hilbert space. Then
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
becomes a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded
“real” C*-algebra with “real” structure

T\mapsto\Theta T\Theta^{*}
. A (possibly unbounded) operator on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be “real” if it commutes with
\Theta
.

Now we recall real C*-algebras and real Hilbert spaces.

Definition 2.2
.

A real Hilbert space is a Hilbert space over
\mathbb{R}
. A real C*-algebra
is a norm-closed subalgebra of
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H_{\mathbb{R}}})
, where
\mathcal{H_{\mathbb{R}}}
is a Hilbert space over
\mathbb{R}
.

If
(A,\mathfrak{r})
is a “real” C*-algebra, then the fixed points
of its real structure

A^{\mathfrak{r}}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\left\{a\in A\;\middle|\;\mathfrak{r}(a)=a\right\}

is a real C*-algebra. Conversely, if
A_{\mathbb{R}}
is a real C*-algebra,
then
A_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
is a “real” C*-algebra with “real”
structure

\mathfrak{r}(a\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}z)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}a\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{z}.

Let
(X,\tau)
be an locally compact, Hausdorff involutive space,
that is, a locally compact Hausdorff space
X

together with a homeomorphism
\tau\colon X\to X
satisfying
\tau^{2}=\operatorname{id}_{X}
. If
X
is a manifold, then we also say
(X,\tau)

is a “real” manifold. The C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}_{0}(X)
carries a “real”
structure

\mathfrak{r}(f)(x)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\overline{f(\tau(x))},

yielding a “real” C*-algebra
(\mathrm{C}_{0}(X),\mathfrak{r})
as well as
its corresponding real C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}_{0}(X)^{\mathfrak{r}}
.
The “real” Gelfand–Naimark theorem due to Arens and Kaplansky

[
2
]
*Theorem 9.1 asserts
that every commutative “real” C*-algebra is isomorphic to
(\mathrm{C}_{0}(X),\mathfrak{r})
for some involutive space
(X,\tau)
; and
every commutative real C*-algebra is isomorphic to

\mathrm{C}_{0}(X)^{\mathfrak{r}}
.

2.1.1.
Clifford algebras

Let
\mathbb{C}\ell_{p,q}
be the finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded “real”
C*-algebra
generated by
\gamma^{1},\dots,\gamma^{p},\rho^{1},\dots,\rho^{q}
, satisfying:

•

\gamma^{1},\dots,\gamma^{p}
are odd, self-adjoint, involutive and
real;

•

\rho^{1},\dots,\rho^{q}
are odd,
anti-self-adjoint, anti-involutive and real;

•

\gamma^{1},\dots,\gamma^{p},\dots,\rho^{1},\dots,\rho^{q}
mutually
anti-commute.

That is, we require that for all
i,j
:

\displaystyle(\gamma^{j})^{2}=1,\quad

\displaystyle(\gamma^{j})^{*}=\gamma^{j},\quad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(\gamma^{j})=\gamma^{j};

\displaystyle(\rho^{j})^{2}=-1,\quad

\displaystyle(\rho^{j})^{*}=-\rho^{j},\quad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(\rho^{j})=\rho^{j}.

The real subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}\ell_{p,q}
is the
\mathbb{R}
-algebra generated by the
same generators and relations. We write
\mathrm{C}\ell_{p,q}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}(\mathbb{C}\ell_{p,q})^{\mathfrak{r}}
for this
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded, real C*-algebra.
Up to Morita equivalence of graded real
C*-algebras, there are only eight possible
\mathrm{C}\ell_{p,q}

satisfying

\mathrm{C}\ell_{p,q}\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\simeq\mathrm{C}\ell_{p+p^{\prime},q+q^{\prime}}.

Moreover,
\mathrm{C}\ell_{1,1}
is isomorphic to the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded
real C*-algebra
\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, whose grading is given by
diagonal–off-diagonal elements. Thus

\mathrm{C}\ell_{d,d}\simeq\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{M}_{2^{d}}(\mathbb{R}).

Kasparov has constructed a canonical representation of
\mathbb{C}\ell_{p,q}

in
[
15
]
. Let
\mathbb{C}^{d}
be the
“real” Hilbert space with basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{d}
and “real”
involution

\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}e_{i}\;\longmapsto\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}\overline{c_{i}}e_{i}.

Let
\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}
be the exterior algebra of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. It is graded by
the subspace of odd or even differential forms

\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}=\bigwedge\nolimits^{\text{odd}}\mathbb{C}^{d}\oplus\bigwedge\nolimits^{\text{even}}\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
The “real” structure on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
extends to

\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}
, that is,

\sum_{i_{i}i_{2}\dots i_{k}}a_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{k}}e_{i_{1}}\wedge e_{i_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{k}}\longmapsto\sum_{i_{i}i_{2}\dots i_{k}}\overline{a_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{k}}}e_{i_{1}}\wedge e_{i_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{k}},

turning
\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}
into a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded “real” Hilbert space.

Definition and Lemma 2.3
.

Let
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
and
\lambda_{j}\colon\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}\to\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}

be the exterior product with
e_{j}
, that is,
\lambda_{j}(\omega)=e_{j}\wedge\omega
. Then its adjoint
\lambda_{j}^{*}\colon\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}\to\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}

is contraction with
e_{j}
, that is,
\lambda_{j}^{*}(\omega)=e_{j}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\omega
.

As a consequence, there is a representation of the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded
“real”
C*
-algebra
\mathbb{C}\ell_{p,q}
on
\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{p+q}
, sending
\gamma^{j}
to
\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{j}^{*}
and
\rho^{j}

to
\lambda_{p+j}-\lambda_{p+j}^{*}
. We call it the standard
representation of
\mathbb{C}\ell_{p,q}
.

Proof.

The operators
\lambda_{j}
and their adjoints satisfy

\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{j}\lambda_{i}=0,\quad\lambda_{i}^{*}\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{j}\lambda_{i}^{*}=\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle.

Moreover,
\lambda_{j}
and
\lambda_{j}^{*}
are odd and real for all
j
. So we have

(\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{j}^{*})^{2}=1,\quad(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j}^{*})^{2}=-1,\quad(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{i}^{*})(\lambda_{p+j}-\lambda_{p+j}^{*})=0.

Thus the operators
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{1}^{*},\dots\lambda_{r}+\lambda_{r}^{*},\dots,\lambda_{p+1}-\lambda_{p+1}^{*},\dots\lambda_{p+q}-\lambda_{p+q}^{*}
generate a copy of
\mathbb{C}\ell_{p+q}
in
\mathbb{B}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{p+q})
.
∎

2.1.2.
The
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded “real” C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\mathbb{C}\ell_{0,d}

Next, we describe a representation of the graded “real”
C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\mathbb{C}\ell_{0,d}
following

[
12
]
*Section 4.
The grading on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\mathbb{C}\ell_{0,d}
is the tensor
product of the trivial grading on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
with the
standard grading on
\mathbb{C}\ell_{0,d}
.
The “real” structure on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is given by the pointwise complex
conjugation, that is,

\mathfrak{r}(f)(n)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\overline{f(n)},\quad f\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),\;n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Then the regular representation of the complex C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
extends to a *-representation on the “real” Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, whose “real” structure is given by pointwise conjugation.
The “real” structure on
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\mathbb{C}\ell_{0,d}
is the tensor product
of this “real” structure with the standard one on
\mathbb{C}\ell_{0,d}
.

The Fourier transform maps the “real” C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
to
the “real” C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Here
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is a “real”

d
-torus, with involution
z\mapsto\overline{z}
.

Lemma 2.4

(cf.
[
12
]
*Section
4)
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded, “real” Hilbert spaces

(2.5)

U\colon\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}\xrightarrow{\sim}L^{2}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathrm{T}^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)

given by

\left\lvert k\right\rangle\otimes e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{l}}\mapsto\frac{z^{k}}{z_{i_{1}}\dots z_{i_{l}}}\mathrm{d}z_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{i_{l}},

where

z^{k}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}z_{1}^{k_{1}}z_{2}^{k_{2}}\dots z_{d}^{k_{d}},\quad k=(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

This induces an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded “real”
C*
-algebras

\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\mathbb{C}\ell_{0,d}\simeq\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\otimes\mathbb{C}\ell_{0,d}\simeq\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{C}\ell(\mathbb{T}^{d}))

where
\mathbb{C}\ell(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is the “real” Clifford bundle of the “real”
manifold
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. In particular,
under this isomorphism, the canonical representation of
\mathbb{C}\ell_{0,d}
as
in
Definition and Lemma

2.3
is
mapped to the Clifford multiplication of
\mathbb{C}\ell(\mathbb{T}^{d})
on

L^{2}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathrm{T}^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)
.

We note that the “real” structure
\mathfrak{r}
on the “real” C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
induced by the involution
z\mapsto\overline{z}

is given by

(\mathfrak{r}f)(x)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\overline{f(-x)},

thus the fixed point algebra is

\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{d})^{\mathfrak{r}}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\left\{f\in\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\;\middle|\;\overline{f(z)}=f(\overline{z})\right\}.

Under the Fourier transform,
\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{d})^{\mathfrak{r}}
is identified
with the real group C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}
,
cf.
Section

2.3.1
.

Let
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}
be the
j
-th position operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})

defined by

{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\phi(x)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}x_{j}\phi(x),\quad\phi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),\;x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Thus the operator
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\gamma^{j}
is
an odd, essentially
self-adjoint operator on the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded “real” Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

The Fourier transform (
2.5
) induces a unitary
equivalence between the unbounded operator
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}={\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\gamma^{j}

on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}
and the Hodge–de Rham operator

\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{*}
on
L^{2}(\bigwedge^{*}\mathrm{T}^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d})
.
Both operators preserve the real subspace
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}\simeq L^{2}(\bigwedge^{*}\mathrm{T}^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}
, and hence are “real”.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\lambda_{j}\left(\left\lvert k\right\rangle\otimes e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{l}}\right)

\displaystyle=k_{j}\left\lvert k\right\rangle\otimes e_{j}\wedge e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{l}}

\displaystyle=U^{*}\left(k_{j}z^{k}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{j}}{z_{j}}\wedge\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{i_{1}}}{z_{i_{1}}}\wedge\dots\wedge\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{i_{l}}}{z_{i_{l}}}\right).

The de Rham operator
\mathrm{d}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{z^{k}}{z_{i_{1}}\dots z_{i_{l}}}\mathrm{d}z_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{i_{l}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots(k_{j}z_{j}^{k_{j}-1}\mathrm{d}z_{j})\cdots z_{d}^{k_{d}}\wedge\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{i_{1}}}{z_{i_{1}}}\wedge\dots\wedge\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{i_{l}}}{z_{i_{l}}}.

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}k_{j}z^{k}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{j}}{z_{j}}\wedge\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{i_{1}}}{z_{i_{1}}}\wedge\dots\wedge\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{i_{l}}}{z_{i_{l}}}.

So
U^{*}\mathrm{d}U
acts by
\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\lambda_{j}
on

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Therefore,
\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{*}
is unitarily equivalent to the operator

\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes(\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{j}^{*})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\gamma^{j}

by the standard representation of
\mathbb{C}\ell_{d,0}
in

Definition and Lemma

2.3
. The generators
\gamma^{j}

are “real” in
\mathbb{C}\ell_{d,0}
, hence maps
\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The
j
-th position operator
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}
preserves
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}
because
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\left\lvert k\right\rangle=k_{j}\left\lvert k\right\rangle
and

is real for any

.
Therefore the operator
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\gamma^{j}

preserves
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

2.2.
Kasparov theory for real C*-algebras

In his seminal work
[
15
]
, Kasparov
introduced a bivariant K-theory for
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded C*-algebras.
Elements in his bivariant K-theory group
\operatorname{KK}(A,B)
may be viewed as
“generalised homomorphisms” from
A
to
B
. Kasparov’s
constructions are general enough to be transferred to the setting of real
C*-algebras
(cf.
[
8
]
*Appendix
A).

For every pair of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded real C*-algebras
A
and
B
,
there is an abelian group (the so-called Kasparov group)
\operatorname{KK}(A,B)
. It
generalises the real K-theory of ungraded real
C*-algebras (that is,
\operatorname{KO}
-theory) in the following way:
if
A=\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0}
and
B
is an ungraded real C*-algebra, then
there is an isomorphism
(cf.
[
15
]
*Section 5, Theorem 7

[
7
]
*Lemma 9.1):

(2.7)

\operatorname{KO}_{n}(B)\simeq\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0},B).

Given
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded real C*-algebras
A_{1},A_{2},B_{1},B_{2},D
, there is a
natural group homomorphism

\times_{D}\colon\operatorname{KK}(A_{1},B_{1}\otimes D)\times\operatorname{KK}(A_{2}\otimes D,B_{2})\to\operatorname{KK}(A_{1}\otimes A_{2},B_{1}\otimes B_{2})

called the
Kasparov product
,
cf.
[
15
]
*Section 4, Theorem 4.
We shall need the following formal constructions with the
Kasparov product, and refer to

[
15
,
8
]

for details.

Functoriality

Let
f\colon A\to B
and
g\colon B\to C
be *-homomorphisms between real C*-algebras. Then
f
gives an
element
[f]\in\operatorname{KK}(A,B)
and
[g]\in\operatorname{KK}(B,C)
. Moreover, we have

[f]\times_{B}[g]=[g\circ f]\in\operatorname{KK}(A,C).

Graded
\operatorname{KO}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
-module structure of
\operatorname{KO}_{*}(B)

Let
A_{1}=\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0}
,
A_{2}=\mathrm{C}\ell_{m,0}
,
B_{1}=B_{2}=D=\mathbb{R}
.
Then the Kasparov product gives a group homomorphism

\times_{\mathbb{R}}\colon\underbrace{\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0},\mathbb{R})}_{\simeq\operatorname{KO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}\times\underbrace{\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{m,0},\mathbb{R})}_{\simeq\operatorname{KO}_{m}(\mathbb{R})}\to\underbrace{\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{m+n,0},\mathbb{R})}_{\simeq\operatorname{KO}_{m+n}(\mathbb{R})},

where the isomorphisms are given as in (
2.7
). This
turns
\operatorname{KO}_{*}(\mathbb{R})\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\bigoplus_{n}\operatorname{KO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
into a graded
commutative ring.

Now let

A_{1}=\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0}
,
A_{2}=\mathrm{C}\ell_{m,0}
,
B_{1}=D=\mathbb{R}
and
B_{2}=B
be
an ungraded real C*-algebra.
Then the Kasparov product gives a group homomorphism

\times_{\mathbb{R}}\colon\underbrace{\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0},\mathbb{R})}_{\simeq\operatorname{KO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}\times\underbrace{\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{m,0},B)}_{\simeq\operatorname{KO}_{m}(B)}\to\underbrace{\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{m+n,0},B)}_{\simeq\operatorname{KO}_{m+n}(B)},

which turns
\operatorname{KO}_{*}(B)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\bigoplus_{n}\operatorname{KO}_{n}(B)
into a graded
commutative module over
\operatorname{KO}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
.

Index pairing

Let
A_{1}=\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0}
,
A_{2}=\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d}
,
B_{1}=B_{2}=\mathbb{R}
, then
the Kasparov product gives a group homomorphism

\times_{D}\colon\underbrace{\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0},D)}_{\simeq\operatorname{KO}_{n}(D)}\times\operatorname{KK}(D\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d},\mathbb{R})\to\underbrace{\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,d},\mathbb{R})}_{\simeq\operatorname{KO}_{n-d}(\mathbb{R})}.

Thus every element
\alpha\in\operatorname{KK}(D\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d},\mathbb{R})
induces a group
homomorphism

(2.8)

\times_{D}\alpha\colon\operatorname{KO}_{n}(D)\to\operatorname{KO}_{n-d}(\mathbb{R}).

Moreover, since the Kasparov product is functorial,
\alpha
actually
induces an

\operatorname{KO}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
-module homomorphism

\operatorname{KO}_{*}(D)\to\operatorname{KO}_{*-d}(\mathbb{R})
.

The group homomorphism (
2.8
)
induced by an element
\alpha\in\operatorname{KK}(D\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d},\mathbb{R})

is called an
index pairing
. Such an element
\alpha

can be represented by a real spectral triple.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
A
be a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded real C*-algebra. A real spectral
triple
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)
over
A
consists of:

•

A
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, together
with a grading-preserving *-representation

\varphi\colon A\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
;

•

A dense *-subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\subseteq A
;

•

An unbounded, self-adjoint odd operator

D\colon\operatorname{dom}D\subseteq\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
;

such that:

•

\varphi(a)(1+D^{2})^{-1}
is compact for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
;

•

For every
a\in\mathcal{A}
,
\varphi(a)

maps
\operatorname{dom}D
into
\operatorname{dom}D
; and the graded commutator

[D,\varphi(a)]
extends to a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
.

Let
\xi\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{H},D)
be a
real spectral triple over a real
C*-algebra
B
, where
B
is represented on
\mathcal{H}
via

\varphi\colon B\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
Then
\xi
represents a class
[\xi]\in\operatorname{KK}(B,\mathbb{R})
.

Let
f\colon A\to B
be a
*-homomorphism. Then the *-algebra
\mathcal{A}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}f^{-1}(\mathcal{B})
is dense in
A
, and

f^{*}\xi\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)
is a real spectral triple
over
A
, where
A
is
represented on
\mathcal{H}
via
\varphi\circ f\colon A\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

We call
f^{*}\xi\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)

in the above example, the

pullback
spectral triple of
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{H},D)
along

f
. Functoriality of Kasparov product states the following:

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
f\colon A\to B
be a
*
-homomorphism between real

C*
-algebras. Let
\xi
be a real spectral

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0},A)}

.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

is a real C*-algebra,
which carries a *-representation
\varphi\colon A\to\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K})
for some separable real
Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
;
\mathcal{A}\subseteq A
is a dense
*-subalgebra;

•

A\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d}
is represented on
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}

through the tensor product of
\varphi
and the standard representation
of
\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d}
;

•

{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}
is the
j

\ell^{2}(\Lambda)

({\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\phi)(x)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}x_{j}\phi(x),\qquad\phi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Lambda),\;x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d};

\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d}
are the generators of
\mathrm{C}\ell_{d,0}
,
represented on
\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
via the standard

The condition
\xi_{\Lambda}
being a real spectral triple says that:

•

\varphi(a)(1+{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}^{2})^{-1}\in\mathbb{K}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
;

•

For every

,
\varphi(a)
maps
\operatorname{dom}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}

into
\operatorname{dom}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}
, and
[{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}},\varphi(a)]
extends to a bounded
operator on
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}

We note the occurence of an
auxiliary separable Hilbert space

.

is given in

Remark

3.2
for

a Delone set (cf.

Lemma 2.13
.

Every position spectral triple is
locally compact
. That is, the
matrix elements

\varphi(a)_{x,y}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\langle x\,|\varphi(a)|\,\mathopen{}y\rangle

are compact for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
x,y\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

By assumption of a real spectral triple, we have that

\varphi(a)\left(1+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}\otimes\gamma^{j}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}\in\mathbb{K}\left(\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

for all

. Therefore,

\left\langle x\,\middle|\varphi(a)\left(1+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}\otimes\gamma^{j}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}\middle|\,\mathopen{}y\right\rangle=\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{j}^{2}\right)^{-1}\varphi(a)_{x,y}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\varphi(a)_{x,y}\in\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{K})

2.3.1.
The fundamental class of
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d}

The last goal of this section is to construct a position spectral triple
\xi_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},N}
over
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d}
,
where
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}
is the
real group C*-algebra of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Moreover, we shall show that taking
the Kasparov product with the
class
[\xi_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},N}]\in\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d},\mathbb{R})

gives a surjective homomorphism

\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{d,0},\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}})\to\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{d,d},\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}
be the real convolution
algebra of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, whose elements are
real-valued, finitely supported functions
f\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, and carries the following convolution product and involution:

(f*g)(x)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\sum_{x_{1}+x_{2}=x}f(x_{1})g(x_{2}),\quad f^{*}(x)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}f(-x).

The left multiplication of
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}
on itself
extends to a injective *-representation

\lambda\colon\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})).

The closure of
\lambda(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}})
in the real
C*-algebra
\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R}))
is the real group C*-algebra

\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Fix a natural number
N
and extend
\lambda
entrywise to

\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
. This gives a representation

\lambda\otimes\operatorname{id}_{N}\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{N})\simeq\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{N})).

To get a position spectral triple over
\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}})
, we need
another separable real Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
.
Let
\mathbb{R}^{N}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}
be any isometric
embedding. Then it induces a corner embedding

e_{N}\colon\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{K})
and gives an invertible element in
\operatorname{KK}(\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R})
. We represent
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\mathcal{K}
via

\lambda\otimes e_{N}
. This gives a position spectral triple

(2.14)

\xi_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},N}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\left(\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}})\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d},\quad\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}\otimes\gamma^{j}\right).

which represents a class
[\xi_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},N}]\in\operatorname{KK}(\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}})\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d},\mathbb{R})
.
This is the same class for each
N
(hence also for
N=1
) because

[e_{N}]\in\operatorname{KK}(\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R}))
is invertible.

Theorem 2.15
.

Taking the Kasparov product with
[\xi_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},N}]
,

\times_{\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}})}[\xi_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},N}]\colon\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{d,0},\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\mathbb{R}}))\to\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{d,d},\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{Z}

is a surjective group homomorphism.

Proof.

The result is well-known, cf.
[
15
]
*Section
5, Theorem 7, whilst we need to appeal to a variant of KK-theory for
“real” C*-algebras called KKR-theory (also defined by Kasparov in

[
15
]
).

Let
(\mathbb{T}^{d},\tau\colon z\mapsto\overline{z})
be the
“real”
d
-torus. Then Kasparov showed that the “real” spectral
triple

(2.16)

\left(\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{C}\ell(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\;L^{2}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathrm{T}^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}),\;\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}^{*}\right)

represents the KKR-thereotic
fundamental class in
\operatorname{KKR}(\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{C}\ell(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\mathbb{C})
. That is, there
exists a class
\beta\in\operatorname{KKR}(\mathbb{C},\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{C}\ell(\mathbb{T}^{d})))
(“the Bott

A *-homomorphism between

Definition 3.19

(
[

]
*Definition

Let
(X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
and

A map

is called

, if the following holds:

(1)

The
expansion function

\omega_{f}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, given by the formula

\omega_{f}(r)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\sup\left\{\mathrm{d}_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\;\middle|\;\mathrm{d}_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq r\right\}

satisfies
\omega_{f}(r)<+\infty
for all

(2)

The map
f

f^{-1}(K)\subseteq X
is pre-compact for
any compact set
K\subseteq Y
.

Two coarse maps
f,g\colon X\rightrightarrows Y
are
close
, if
there exists
c\geq 0
such that for all
x\in X
,
d_{Y}(f(x),g(x))\leq c
.

Definition and Lemma 3.20

(
[
29
]
*Definition

Let
(X,\mathcal{H}_{X})
and
(Y,\mathcal{H}_{Y})
be ample modules
and let

be a coarse map. A covering isometry for

is an isometry
V\colon\mathcal{H}_{X}\to\mathcal{H}_{Y}
such that

\mathrm{d}(y,f(x))<t

(y,x)\in\operatorname{supp}(V)

The
*
-homomorphism

restricts to a
*
-homomorphism
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(X,\mathcal{H}_{X})\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(Y,\mathcal{H}_{Y})
. Its induced maps

f_{*}\colon\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0},\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(X,\mathcal{H}_{X}))\to\operatorname{KK}(\mathrm{C}\ell_{n,0},\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(Y,\mathcal{H}_{Y}))

depends only on

and not on the choice of

The
coarse category
consists of proper metric spaces as objects, and
equivalence classes of coarse maps as arrows, where two coarse maps are
equivalent if and only if they are close.
A

is an isomorphism in the
coarse category. If
X\subseteq Y
is a metric subspace that is
coarsely equivalent to

, then we also say that

is

coarsely dense
in
Y
.
In particular, any Delone set
\Lambda
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

is coarsely dense in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proposition 3.22
.

Let

3.2.1.
Position spectral triple over the Roe C*-algebra

Fix a closed subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the subspace metric,
e.g. a Delone set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A relation
between the Roe C*-algebra on
X
and the “position” operators has
been considered in
[
12
]
*Section 2.2 as
follows. For
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
let
\mathrm{e}_{t}
be the bounded continuous function on
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

\mathrm{e}_{t}(x)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\cdot x}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}t_{j}x_{j}},\qquad x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then the map
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{b}}(X)
given by
t\mapsto\mathrm{e}_{t}
is
continuous in the strict topology of
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{b}}(X)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{X}
be an ample
X
-module.
The representation
\varrho\colon\mathrm{C}_{0}(X)\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{X})

extends to a strictly continuous, *-representation

\overline{\varrho}\colon\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{b}}(X)\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{X})
. Thus
the map

\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{b}}(X)\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{X}),\qquad t\mapsto\mathrm{e}_{t}\mapsto\varrho(\mathrm{e}_{t})

is continuous for the norm topology on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{X})
. If
X
does not contain discrete components,
and
\mathcal{H}_{X}
is the standard
X
-module
as in
Example

3.15
, then the restriction of
\sigma
to
the
j
-th coordinate component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
gives the flow

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}}
generated by
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}
.

By conjugation,
\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{X})
induces a
group homomorphism
\operatorname{Ad}\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{X}))
.
We say that an operator
T\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{X})
is continuous with
respect to
\operatorname{Ad}\sigma
if and only if the map
t\mapsto\operatorname{Ad}\sigma_{t}(T)
is
continuous for the norm topology on
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
. The property
of being a norm limit of controlled operators can be described as a
continuity property for the action
\operatorname{Ad}\sigma
:

Lemma 3.25

(
[
12
]
*Theorem
4)
.

An operator
T\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}_{X})
is a norm limit of controlled
operators if and only if it is continuous with respect to
\operatorname{Ad}\sigma
. Thus
T\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(X,\mathcal{H}_{X})
if and only if it is locally compact and continuous
with respect to
\operatorname{Ad}\sigma
.

We take a slight different viewpoint, interpreting the previous lemma as
follows: the Roe C*-algebra is the largest C*-algebra, over which one
can have a position spectral triple:

Theorem 3.26
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a discrete subset.
If

\xi\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\left(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d},\quad\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}\otimes\gamma^{j}\right)

is a position spectral triple

(
cf.
Definition

2.11
)
, where
\mathcal{A}
is a dense
*
-subalgebra of a
C*
-algebra
A

represented on
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K}
by

\varphi\colon A\to\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K})
.
Then
\varphi(\mathcal{A})
is contained in the
Roe
C*
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda,\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})

defined by the standard
\Lambda
-module

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K}

as in
Example

3.15
.

Proof.

Identifying
A
and its dense subalgebra

\mathcal{A}
with their images under

\varphi
, we may assume that
\varphi\colon A\to\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K})

is injective. Then we must show that every

a\in\mathcal{A}
is locally compact and
can be approximated by controlled operators.
Being locally compact amounts to saying that
\langle x\,|\varphi(a)|\,\mathopen{}y\rangle\in\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{K})
for all
x,y\in\Lambda
, which always holds by

Lemma

2.13
(providing that
\xi

is

a position spectral triple.)

Now we show that every
a\in\mathcal{A}
is a norm limit of controlled
operators. For convenience, below we write
\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}
for the
operator
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}
on
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K}
.

Since
[a\otimes\rho^{i},\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}\otimes\gamma^{j}]
is bounded
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
and

i,j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, we have
[a,\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}]

is a bounded operator
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, i.e. there exists
C>0
such that
\left\lVert[a,\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}]\right\rVert\leq C
.

By
Lemma

3.25
, it suffices to show that
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
j=1,\dots,d
, the map

\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda,\mathcal{K})),\qquad t\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}a\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}

is continuous in the norm topology on
\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda,\mathcal{K}))
. It follows from

[\mathrm{e}^{it\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}},a]=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}st\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}a\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(1-s)t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}\right)\mathrm{d}s=\mathrm{i}t\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}st\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}[\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j},a]\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(1-s)t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}\mathrm{d}s

that

\left\lVert[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}},a]\right\rVert\leq t\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert[\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j},a]\right\rVert\mathrm{d}s\leq Ct.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}a\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}s\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}a\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(s-t)\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}a\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(s-t)\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}-a\right)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(s-t)\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}},a]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(s-t)\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(s-t)\hat{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}}_{j}},a]\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C(s-t).

So the map
t\mapsto\operatorname{Ad}{\sigma_{t}}(a)
is continuous for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
, and thus
a\in\mathcal{A}
is a norm limit of controlled operators.
∎

Thus if the *-algebra
\mathcal{A}
in the position spectral triple
is dense in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
, then it defines a spectral triple over

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
. This amounts to choosing a suitable dense
*-subalgebra inside
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
. The spectral triple below
has been implicitly used by Ewert and Meyer in the proof of

[
12
]
*Theorem 7.

Definition and Lemma 3.27
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a discrete countable set.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
be the collection of operators

T\in\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda,\mathcal{K}))
satisfying:

•

T
is controlled and locally compact
;

•

\sup_{y\in\Lambda}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\left\lVert T_{x,y}\right\rVert<+\infty
and

\sup_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\Lambda}\left\lVert T_{x,y}\right\rVert<+\infty
.

Then

(3.28)

\xi^{\mathrm{Roe}}_{\Lambda}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\left(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d},\quad\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{K}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}\otimes\gamma^{j}\right)

is a position spectral triple over
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Roe}}(\Lambda)
,
x,y\in\Lambda
. Then

\langle x\,|[T,{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}]|\,\mathopen{}y\rangle=\langle x\,|T({\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}})|\,\mathopen{}y\rangle-\langle x\,|({\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}})T|\,\mathopen{}y\rangle=(y_{j}-x_{j})T_{x,y}.

Since
T
is controlled, there exists
R>0
such that
\langle x\,|T|\,\mathopen{}y\rangle=0
whenever
\mathrm{d}(x,y)>R
. Thus

\left\lVert[T,{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}]\right\rVert\leq\sup_{y\in\Lambda}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}R\cdot\left\lVert T_{x,y}\right\rVert<+\infty.

So
T
preserves the
domain of
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}

and boundedly commutes with it for
every
j=1,\dots,d
.

Therefore, for any
S\in\mathrm{C}\ell_{0,d}
,
T\otimes S
preserves the domain of

{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}\otimes\gamma^{j}
,
and boundedly commutes with
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}
.
The local compactness of
T
implies that the operator

T\left(1+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}\otimes\gamma^{j}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}=T\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{X}}_{j}^{2}\right)^{-1}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}}

is compact. Therefore,
\xi^{\mathrm{Roe}}_{\Lambda}
is a position spectral triple.

Proposition 4.4

A convenient way to define the “stacking” map is by describing the
C*-algebra of the product Delone set as a spatial tensor product. We
note the following well-known lemma:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows\Omega_{1}

and
\mathcal{G}_{2}\rightrightarrows\Omega_{2}

be étale groupoids. Let
\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\mathcal{G}_{2}

\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}

\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{1})\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{2})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\mathcal{G}_{2}),\quad\Phi(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}f_{1}(\gamma_{1})f_{2}(\gamma_{2}),

where
\otimes
refers to the spatial tensor product.

Proof.

This lemma is well-known, and we sketch a proof here.

Write
L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{1})
,
L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{2})
and
L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\mathcal{G}_{2})
for the Hilbert C*-modules over
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{1})
,
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{2})
and
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2})
as in

. We claim that

canonically isomorphic to the external tensor product Hilbert C*-module

under the identification

\Psi\colon\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{1})\otimes\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{2})\simeq\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}),\quad\Psi(f\otimes g)(x,y)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}f(x)\cdot g(y).

To see this, let
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{G}_{1})
and

g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{G}_{2})
,
Then their tensor products

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\mathcal{G}_{2})
.
Given

and

\displaystyle\left\langle f_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in r^{-1}(x)}f_{1}(\gamma)f_{2}(\gamma),\quad\left\langle g_{1},g_{2}\right\rangle(y)=\sum_{\eta\in r^{-1}(y)}g_{1}(\eta)g_{2}(\eta),

\displaystyle\left\langle f_{1}\otimes g_{1},f_{2}\otimes g_{2}\right\rangle(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\gamma,\eta)\in r^{-1}(x,y)}(f_{1}\otimes g_{1})(\gamma,\eta)(f_{2}\otimes g_{2})(\gamma,\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in r^{-1}(x)}\sum_{\eta\in r^{-1}(y)}f_{1}(\gamma)g_{2}(\gamma)g_{1}(\eta)g_{2}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle(x)\cdot\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle(y).

Thus
L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\mathcal{G}_{2})\simeq L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{1})\otimes L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{2})
as Hilbert C*-modules
over
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2})\simeq\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{1})\otimes\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{2})
. This induces an injective
*-homomorphism (cf.
[

]

[

\Phi\colon\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{1})}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{1}))\otimes\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{2})}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{2}))\to\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2})}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\mathcal{G}_{2})).

In particular, for

f_{1}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{G}_{1})
and
f_{2}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{G}_{2})
,
viewed as convolution

Corollary 4.6
.

There is a canonical isomorphism

\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda\times L})\simeq\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda})\otimes\mathrm{C}^{*}(\mathcal{G}_{L}).

Proof.

By

and

and

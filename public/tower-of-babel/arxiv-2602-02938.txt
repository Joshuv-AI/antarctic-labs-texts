A Correspondence between Billiards and Geodesics
Daniele Giannetto

Abstract
From a geometric viewpoint, billiard trajectories and geodesics are related by mutual approximation results. In one direction, it is known that every geodesic curve in the boundary of a smooth convex body can be approximated by a sequence of billiard trajectories inside of it. We establish the other direction by proving that, for Riemannian billiard tables (under mild assumptions), there exists a family of fold-type surfaces such that every sequence of geodesic segments on these surfaces has a subsequence that converges to a billiard trajectory in the table. In particular, this is true for convex Euclidean tables. We also describe a more general class of tables to which this result applies and present explicit non-Euclidean examples.

Introduction

A billiard trajectory in a Euclidean domain
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a smooth boundary
\partial K
is defined as a piecewise straight line in the interior of
K
: upon reaching the boundary, it undergoes a reflection satisfying the rule that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
It is well known that billiard dynamics in
K
highly depends on the shape of
\partial K
; it can vary from being extremely rich to being not well-defined everywhere (see
[
8
]
). Definitions of generalized billiards can be given in ambient non-Euclidean or pseudo-Riemannian geometries (see for instance
[
3
]
) and for non-smooth tables (see
[
4
]
).

From a geometrical perspective, billiard maps are closely related to geodesic flows. On the one hand, if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a closed strictly convex Euclidean domain with sufficiently smooth boundary, it is always possible to approximate geodesics in the boundary
\partial K
by means of billiard trajectories that live inside
K
.
Namely, one can consider billiard trajectories with incidence angle at a given point of the boundary tending to zero, i.e. with the incoming velocity vector of the billiard trajectory approximating a given tangent vector of a geodesic on the boundary. More formally, the following result was proved in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem B of
[
4
]
)
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose boundary is of class
C^{2,1}
and has a positive definite second fundamental form at every point. Let
p_{0}\in\partial K
and
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of billiard trajectories of
K
starting from
p_{0}
. Then, if the initial direction of
\gamma_{k}
converges to a tangent vector
v\in T_{p_{0}}(\partial K)
, we have that the sequence
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally uniformly to the corresponding geodesic of the boundary.

For the sake of completeness of this paper, a proof sketch of this theorem is presented in Appendix A.

On the other hand, billiard trajectories can also be regarded as limits of sequences of geodesic segments. For instance, a billiard trajectory in the closed unit disk
\overline{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be regarded as the limit of a sequence of geodesic segments, each lying on an ellipsoid of revolution whose one semiaxis tends to 0. More formally, let

be the closed unit disk in the

be the ellipsoid whose

The purpose of this paper is to prove that a similar construction and convergence result can be carried out for a general Riemannian billiard table under mild curvature assumptions. In what follows, we briefly describe the structure of the paper.

Section 2 establishes notations and collects well-known results from the theory of metric geometry that will be important in Section 3. In particular, we will focus on the notion of quasigeodesic curves in Riemannian manifolds with boundary satisfying a lower curvature bound. Those curves are strongly related to both geodesics and billiard trajectories. Details of proofs for propositions from Section 2 (and further discussion on these topics) can be found in

[

Section 3 is the core of the present work. Theorem
3.6
shows that, for every billiard table satisfying a lower curvature bound, every quasigeodesic curve is a billiard trajectory.
Next, if
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a hyperplane and
K\subset H
is a billiard table, we show that, for every
p_{0}\in K
and for every open bounded neighborhood
U\subset H
of
p_{0}
, it is possible to construct a family of hypersurfaces

that “flattens” onto

as

in a fashion similar to the family of ellipsoids presented above. Theorem

makes rigorous this flattening behaviour in terms of Hausdorff convergence of bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Then, the following theorem summarizes one of the main results of the paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K\subset H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a convex Euclidean billiard table whose boundary is of class
C^{2}
. Then, for every
p_{0}\in\partial K
and every decreasing sequence
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+}
in
(0,1)
, there is
T>0
such that every sequence of arclength-parameterized geodesic segments

\left\{\gamma_{k}:[-T,T]\rightarrow M_{\lambda_{k}}^{p_{0}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

that satisfies
\gamma_{k}(0)=p_{0}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
admits a subsequence
\{\gamma_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges to a continuous curve
\gamma:[-T,T]\rightarrow K
uniformly in
[-T,T]
as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover
\gamma
is a billiard trajectory of
K
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is postponed to Section 4. Notice that the above theorem generalizes the convergence result presented before for the family of ellipsoids with a vanishing semiaxis to every convex Euclidean billiard table with
C^{2}
boundary.

Moreover, we prove that the same result holds for a wider class of Riemannian billiard tables. Indeed, let
\overline{g}
be a complete smooth Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, let
K\subset H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a billiard table with
C^{2}
boundary and let
p_{0}\in\partial K
be a boundary point that satisfies the following assumption.

Assumption (H).

There exists an open bounded neighborhood
U\subset H
of
p_{0}
such that the family of folds
\left\{M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}\right\}_{\lambda\in(0,1)}
over

is made of spaces of curvature bounded below by some
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
uniformly in
\lambda\in(0,1)
.

Then, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every decreasing sequence
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+}
in
(0,1)
, there is
T>0
such that every sequence of arclength-parameterized geodesic segments

\left\{\gamma_{k}:[-T,T]\rightarrow M_{\lambda_{k}}^{p_{0}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

that satisfies
\gamma_{k}(0)=p_{0}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
admits a subsequence
\{\gamma_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges to a continuous curve
\gamma:[-T,T]\rightarrow K
uniformly in
[-T,T]
as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover
\gamma
is a billiard trajectory of
K
.

Section 4 presents examples of Riemannian metrics
\overline{g}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and billiard tables to which Theorem
1.3
is applicable or nonapplicable. In particular, Proposition
4.2
together with Theorem
1.3
proves Theorem
1.2
. Also, examples in negative and positive constant curvature are provided explicitly.

Strategy of proof of Theorem
1.3

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is presented in Section 3.3 and it is divided into two steps. The first one is to prove that there exists
T>0
such that every sequence of geodesic segments

\left\{\gamma_{k}:[-T,T]\rightarrow M_{\lambda_{k}}^{p_{0}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

such that
\gamma_{k}(0)=p_{0}
admits a subsequence that converges to some
\gamma:[-T,T]\rightarrow K
uniformly in
[-T,T]
.

The second step is to prove that every limit curve
\gamma:[-T,T]\rightarrow K
produced by the first step is a billiard trajectory of
K
. To conclude, Theorem
3.6
of Section 3 implies that it suffices to prove that every
\gamma
is a so-called
\kappa
-quasigeodesic of
K\cap U
. This is where assumption
(H)
becomes crucial as it ensures that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the manifolds
M_{\lambda_{k}}^{p_{0}}
and
K\cap U
share the same lower bound in curvature.
Then, the conclusion follows from the fact that under a uniform lower bound of curvatures the uniform limit of a sequence of
\kappa
-quasigeodesics is again a
\kappa
-quasigeodesic.

Notation and conventions

(M,g)

n

Definition 2.1
.

2.2
Comparison theory in complete Riemannian manifolds and quasigeodesic curves

Let
(M,g)
, with
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, be a complete orientable smooth
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold (with or without boundary) and let
d_{g}:M\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the corresponding Riemannian distance function. In what follows, we will assume that
(M,g)
is a space of curvature bounded below by some
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. Also, we will let

\alpha_{\kappa}:=\begin{cases}\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\kappa}}&\text{if}\ \kappa>0,\\
+\infty&\text{if}\ \kappa\leq 0\end{cases}

denote the diameter of the unique complete simply connected 2-dimensional surface of constant curvature
\kappa
.

Let
p\in M
and
c:[a,b]\rightarrow M
be an arclength-parameterized geodesic segment such that
0<d_{g}(p,c(t))<\alpha_{\kappa}
for all

[
5
]

t\mapsto d_{g}(p,c(t))

satisfies the following concavity condition in the form of a differential inequality; see

7
]

f_{p}^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq 1-\kappa f_{p}(t),\quad\text{for all}\ t\in(a,b),

(1)

where
f_{p}(t)=\rho_{\kappa}(d_{g}(p,c(t)))

\rho_{\kappa}(r)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(1-\cos\left(r\sqrt{\kappa}\right)\right),&\mbox{if }\kappa>0,\\
\frac{r^{2}}{2},&\mbox{if }\kappa=0,\\
\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(1-\cosh\left(r\sqrt{-\kappa}\right)\right),&\mbox{if }\kappa<0.\end{cases}

Remark 2.9
.

Since the continuous function

might not be twice differentiable everywhere in
(a,b)

Remark 2.10
.

The function

2.3
Hausdorff convergence and quasigeodesic curves

Let
\overline{g}
be a complete smooth Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
d_{\overline{g}}
denote the corresponding Riemannian distance function. We recall that, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the distance between
x
and
M
is defined as

\mbox{dist}_{\overline{g}}(x,M):=\inf\left\{d_{\overline{g}}(x,y):\ y\in M\right\}.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
\{M_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
and
M
be connected bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that
M_{m}
converges to
M
in
the Hausdorff sense with respect to
\overline{g}
as
m\rightarrow\infty
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
m\geq N
we have

\begin{split}\mbox{dist}_{\overline{g}}(x,M)\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for each}\ x\in M_{m},\\
\mbox{dist}_{\overline{g}}(y,M_{m})\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for each}\ y\in M.\end{split}

Assume now that
\{M_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of compact orientable Riemannian submanifolds (with or without boundary) and there is
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
such that each
M_{m}
is a space of curvature bounded below by
\kappa
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. It is known (see Proposition 10.7.1 of
[
1
]
) that if there exists a compact orientable Riemannian submanifold
M
that is the Hausdorff limit of the sequence
\{M_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
, then
M
is a space of curvature bounded below by
\kappa
as well. The same result generalizes to a sequence of bounded orientable submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is converging to a bounded orientable submanifold in the Hausdorff sense with respect to
\overline{g}
.

Under the same assumptions, the following proposition describes uniform limits of
\kappa
-quasigeodesics.

Proposition 2.18

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\{c_{m}:[a,b]\rightarrow M_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
\kappa
-quasigeodesic curves. If
c_{m}:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
converges to a continuous curve
c:[a,b]\rightarrow M
uniformly in
[a,b]
as
m\rightarrow\infty
, then
c
is a
\kappa
-quasigeodesic of
M
. In particular, uniform limits of
\kappa
-quasigeodesics in
M
are
\kappa
-quasigeodesics.

3
Geodesics approximating Billiards

3.1
Billiard tables, billiard trajectories and quasigeodesics

The goal of this section is to prove that billiard trajectories are related to quasigeodesic curves introduced in Section 2.2. In what follows, we assume that
\overline{g}
is a complete smooth Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We start by recalling several basic definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open connected subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that
\partial\Omega
is
of class
C^{2}
if for every
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
there is an open bounded neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a function
f\in C^{2}(\overline{U})
with a regular value at
0
such that

\overline{\Omega}\cap U=\{x\in U:f(x)\geq 0\},\quad\partial\Omega\cap U=f^{-1}(0).

Definition 3.2
.

The Riemannian manifold with boundary
(K,g)
is called a
billiard table
if
K
is the closure of an open orientable connected subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\partial K
is of class
C^{2}
and
g
is the metric on
K
induced by
\overline{g}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(K,g)
be a billiard table and
x_{0}\in\partial K
. Two unit vectors
u,v
that belong to the tangent cone
C_{x_{0}}(K)\subset T_{x_{0}}K
satisfy the
billiard reflection law
if
g_{x_{0}}(u+v,w)=0
for all
w\in T_{x_{0}}(\partial K)
.

Remark 3.4
.

This definition of the billiard reflection law coincides with the classical one, in which the Riemannian acute angles formed by the unit vectors
u,v\in C_{x_{0}}(K)
with
T_{x_{0}}(\partial K)
are equal. Also, if
\nu(x_{0})
denotes the unique unit normal vector at
x_{0}\in\partial K
pointing into
K
, then two unit vectors
u,v\in C_{x_{0}}(K)
satisfy the billiard reflection law if and only if there exists
s\geq 0
such that

u+v=s\ \nu(x_{0}).

Definition 3.5
.

Let
K
be a billiard table and
c:[a,b]\rightarrow K
be a Lipschitz regular curve. Then
c:[a,b]\rightarrow K
is called a
billiard trajectory
if it is parameterized by arclength and it is locally length-minimizing at all but a set of
bounce times

\mathcal{T}:=\{t_{0}\in[a,b]:\ c(t_{0})\in\partial K\}\subseteq[a,b]

such that for every
t_{0}\in\mathcal{T}
, the right and left tangent vectors
c^{+}(t_{0})
,
c^{-}(t_{0})
satisfy the billiard reflection law.

It is worth noticing that we do not exclude the possibility that the bounces of a billiard trajectory accumulate in finite time (see
[
2
]
for an example) or that parts of a billiard trajectory are contained in
\partial K
. Typically, additional regularity and convexity assumptions are imposed to rule out such trajectories, as their presence makes the billiard evolution ill-defined. However, their existence does not affect the validity of the main theorem of the paper.

The second part of the section is devoted to proving the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(K,g)
be a billiard table and assume that it is a space of curvature bounded below by some
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. Then every
\kappa
-quasigeodesic of
K
is a billiard trajectory.

We start the proof with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(K,g)
be a billiard table and
x_{0}\in\partial K
. Then two unit vectors
u,v\in C_{x_{0}}(K)
satisfy the billiard reflection law if and only if they are polar.

Proof.

Let
\nu(x_{0})\in T_{x_{0}}K
be the unique unit normal vector to
\partial K
at
x_{0}
pointing into
K
. Then the tangent space
T_{x_{0}}K
splits into the orthogonal direct sum

T_{x_{0}}K=T_{x_{0}}(\partial K)\oplus\mbox{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\nu(x_{0})\}.

Also, we recall that every vector
w\in C_{x_{0}}(K)
has the form
w=\tilde{w}+r\ \nu(x_{0})
with
\tilde{w}\in T_{x_{0}}(\partial K)
and
r\geq 0
.

(\Rightarrow)

Assume that two unit vectors
u,v\in C_{x_{0}}(K)
satisfy the billiard reflection law, i.e.
u+v=s\ \nu(x_{0})
for some
s\geq 0
(see Remark
3.4
). Then, for every
w=\tilde{w}+r\ \nu(x_{0})
, we compute

g_{x_{0}}(u+v,w)=g_{x_{0}}(s\ \nu(x_{0}),\tilde{w}+r\ \nu(x_{0}))=sr\ g_{x_{0}}(\nu(x_{0}),\nu(x_{0}))=sr\geq 0.

Hence
u
and
v
are polar.

(\Leftarrow)

Assume that two unit vectors
u,v\in C_{x_{0}}(K)
are polar. Let
w\in T_{x_{0}}(\partial K)
and recall that
-w\in T_{x_{0}}(\partial K)
. Then, by definition of polar vectors, we have

g_{x_{0}}(u+v,w)\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad g_{x_{0}}(u+v,-w)\geq 0,

which imply
g_{x_{0}}(u+v,w)=0
. Therefore
u
and
v
satisfy the billiard reflection law.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Let
c:[a,b]\rightarrow K
be a
\kappa
-quasigeodesic of
K
. Let
\mathcal{T}\subset[a,b]
be the set of times
t_{0}\in[a,b]
such that
c(t_{0})\in\partial K
. Proposition
2.16
implies that for each
t_{0}\in\mathcal{T}
, the vectors
c^{+}(t_{0})
and
c^{-}(t_{0})
are polar (or, by Lemma
3.7
, they satisfy the billiard reflection law). To conclude, it remains to show that
c
is locally length-minimizing for every
t_{*}\in(a,b)\setminus\mathcal{T}
.

We first claim that if
t_{*}\in[a,b]\setminus\mathcal{T}
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
(t_{*}-\varepsilon,t_{*}+\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{T}=\emptyset
. Indeed, if it were not the case, there would be a sequence of times
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{T}
such that
t_{i}\rightarrow t_{*}
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. But, since
c
is a continuous function and
\partial K
is a closed set, we would have
c(t_{*})=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}c(t_{i})\in\partial K
, contradicting the assumption
t_{*}\notin\mathcal{T}
.

Therefore, we fix
t_{*}\in(a,b)\setminus\mathcal{T}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
(t_{*}-\varepsilon,t_{*}+\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{T}=\emptyset
and we observe that the curve

\tilde{c}:=c\big|_{(t_{*}-\varepsilon,t_{*}+\varepsilon)}:(t_{*}-\varepsilon,t_{*}+\varepsilon)\rightarrow K

is a
\kappa
-quasigeodesic that is entirely contained in
\mbox{Int}(K)
. Now, since
\mbox{Int}(K)
is a Riemannian manifold without boundary, Proposition
2.15
implies that
\tilde{c}
is a geodesic of
\mbox{Int}(K)
. Hence the curve
c
is locally length-minimizing for
t_{*}
. Thus,
c
is a billiard trajectory by definition.

∎

Let

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
through the embedding

\overline{g}
be a smooth complete Riemannian metric on

Example 3.10

Let

be the closed unit disk in

p_{0}\in\partial K
and
U
is an open bounded subset of
H

M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}=\Big\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ x_{3}^{2}=\lambda^{2}(1-x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})\Big\}=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\frac{x_{3}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}=1\right\}.

Looking at the above examples, it is intuitively clear that as
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
, the manifolds
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
flatten onto
K\cap U
. The following theorem formalizes this flattening behaviour, which occurs for every billiard table
K
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
K\subset H
be a billiard table, let
p_{0}\in\partial K
, let
U\subset H
be an open bounded neighborhood of
p_{0}
and let
\left\{M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}\right\}_{\lambda\in(0,1)}
be the family of folds over
K\cap U
. Then
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
converges to
K\cap U
in the Hausdorff sense with respect to
\overline{g}
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

Proof.

First observe that every
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
is bounded. Indeed, we have
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}=M^{+}\cup M^{-}
where
M^{\pm}
are contained in the images of the smooth embeddings

\phi^{\pm}:K\cap\overline{U}\rightarrow M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}},\quad(x,0)\mapsto\left(x,\pm\lambda\sqrt{f(x)}\right),

which are compact since so is
K\cap\overline{U}
.

Next we prove the convergence result for the Euclidean metric
\overline{g}_{Euc}=dx^{2}+dx_{n+1}^{2}
. We consider the maximum

D:=\max\left\{\sqrt{f(x)}:\ (x,0)\in\overline{K\cap U}\right\}\geq 0

of the function
f
in
\overline{K\cap U}
, which is well defined since
\overline{K\cap U}
is compact.
Let
q=(x,x_{n+1})\in M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
and recall that we have
(x,0)\in K\cap U
and
|x_{n+1}|=\lambda\sqrt{f(x)}
. Then, we have

\mbox{dist}_{Euc}(q,K\cap U)\leq|q-(x,0)|=|(x,x_{n+1})-(x,0)|=|x_{n+1}|\leq D\lambda,

by definition of distance between a point and a set. Similarly, if
p=(x,0)\in K\cap U
, we have

\mbox{dist}_{Euc}\left(p,M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}\right)\leq\left|(x,0)-\left(x,\lambda\sqrt{f(x)}\right)\right|=\lambda\sqrt{f(x)}\leq D\lambda.

Therefore,
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
converges to
K\cap U
in the Hausdorff sense with respect to
\overline{g}_{Euc}
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

Now, let
\overline{g}
be a smooth complete Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then the topology induced by the Riemannian distance
d_{\overline{g}}
coincides with the Euclidean topology of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and, as a consequence, the Euclidean distance and
d_{\overline{g}}
are uniformly equivalent on every compact set of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Take an open bounded subset
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that
V\cap H=U
and
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}\subset V
for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
. The equivalence of metrics in
\overline{V}
implies that there exists a constant
C=C(\overline{V})>0
such that
d_{\overline{g}}(x,y)\leq C|x-y|
for every
x,y\in\overline{V}
. In particular, for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have

\mbox{dist}_{\overline{g}}(q,K\cap U)\leq C\ \mbox{dist}_{Euc}(q,K\cap U),\ \forall q\in M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}},

\mbox{dist}_{\overline{g}}(p,M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}})\leq C\ \mbox{dist}_{Euc}(p,M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}),\ \forall p\in K\cap U.

Thus,
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
converges to
K\cap U
in the Hausdorff sense with respect to
\overline{g}
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

∎

3.3
Proof of the main theorem

This subsection is devoted to proving Theorem
1.3
. Let
K\subset H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a billiard table and
p_{0}\in\partial K
such that the following assumption holds.

Assumption (H).

There exists an open, bounded neighborhood
U\subset H
of
p_{0}
and a
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
, the fold
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
over
K\cap U
is a space of curvature bounded below by
\kappa
.

Theorem 3.12

(= Theorem
1.3
)
.

There exists
T>0
such that, for every decreasing sequence
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+}
in
(0,1)
, every sequence of arclength-parameterized geodesic segments

\left\{\gamma_{k}:[-T,T]\rightarrow M_{\lambda_{k}}^{p_{0}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

that satisfies
\gamma_{k}(0)=p_{0}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
admits a subsequence
\{\gamma_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges to a continuous curve
\gamma:[-T,T]\rightarrow K
uniformly in
[-T,T]
as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover,
\gamma
is a billiard trajectory of
K
.

Proof.

We take
T>0
such that every continuous rectifiable curve
c:[-T,T]\rightarrow H
, with
c(0)=p_{0}
and whose length is at most
2T
, lies within
U
.
Then, let
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+}
be a decreasing sequence in
(0,1)
and let
g_{k}
denote the Riemannian metric on
M_{k}:=M_{\lambda_{k}}^{p_{0}}
induced by
\overline{g}
.
Also, let
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of arclength-parameterized geodesic segments
\gamma_{k}:[-T,T]\rightarrow M_{k}
such that
\gamma_{k}(0)=p_{0}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

We claim that
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the assumptions of the Ascoli-Arzelà theorem. Indeed:

•

since each
\gamma_{k}
is parameterized by arclength, we have

d_{\overline{g}}(\gamma_{k}(t),p_{0})=|t|\leq T,

for every
t\in[-T,T]
. This implies that the sequence is uniformly bounded.

•

Similarly, take
t,s\in[-T,T]
and observe that

d_{\overline{g}}(\gamma_{k}(t),\gamma_{k}(s))=|t-s|.

Then the sequence
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant 1.

As a consequence, the Ascoli-Arzelà theorem implies that there exists a subsequence
\{\gamma_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges to a certain continuous curve
\gamma:[-T,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
uniformly in
[-T,T]
as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover,
\gamma
is Lipschitz and parameterized by arclength since, for every
t,s\in[-T,T]
, we have

d_{\overline{g}}(\gamma(t),\gamma(s))=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\ d_{\overline{g}}(\gamma_{m}(t),\gamma_{m}(s))=|t-s|.

Now, let
t\in[-T,T]
. We claim that
\gamma(t)\in K\cap U
. Since
\gamma_{k}(t)\in M_{k}
, we have

\mbox{dist}_{\overline{g}}(\gamma(t),K\cap U)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mbox{dist}_{\overline{g}}(\gamma_{m}(t),K\cap U)=0,

as in the proof of Theorem
3.11
, which implies that
\gamma(t)\in K\cap\overline{U}
. Moreover, since the length of
\gamma:[-T,T]\rightarrow H
is
2T
, we conclude that
\gamma(t)\in K\cap U
by our choice of
T>0
.

To conclude the proof, it is enough to show that
K\cap U
is a space with curvature bounded below by some
\kappa
and that
\gamma
is a
\kappa
-quasigeodesic of
K\cap U
. Indeed, then
\gamma
would be a billiard trajectory of
K\cap U
by Theorem
3.6
and hence a billiard trajectory of
K
.

Take
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
as in assumption
(H)
and we notice that
K\cap U
is a space of curvature bounded below by
\kappa
(see discussion in Section 2.3) since
M_{k}
converges to
K\cap U
in the Hausdorff sense with respect to
\overline{g}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, Proposition
2.18
implies that
\gamma
must be a
\kappa
-quasigeodesic of
K\cap U
since
\gamma
is the uniform limit of a sequence of geodesic segments (that are
\kappa
-quasigeodesics according to Proposition
2.15
).

∎

Examples in constant curvature geometries

In this section we present types of billiard tables
K
to which Theorem
1.3
is (not) applicable, i.e. assumption
(H)
is (not) satisfied. In what follows, we set

H=\{(x,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:\ x_{n+1}=0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

and let
K\subset H
be a billiard table. We recall that for
p_{0}\in\partial K
, there is an open bounded neighborhood
U\subset H
of
p_{0}
and a function
f\in C^{2}(\overline{U})
with a regular value at
0
such that

K\cap U=\{(x,0)\in H:\ f(x)\geq 0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\partial K\cap U=f^{-1}(0).

(2)

Also, we will denote by
Df(x)
and
D^{2}f(x)
the Euclidean gradient and Hessian matrix of
f\in C^{2}(\overline{U})
at
(x,0)\in U
respectively.

4.1
Flat geometry

Let
\overline{g}=dx^{2}+dx_{n+1}^{2}
be the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. It turns out that not all billiard tables in the hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfy assumption
(H)
at every boundary point, such as in the following example.

Example 4.1
.

Let
n=2
and let

K=\{(x_{1},x_{2},0)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}\geq 0\}

be the concave portion of the
x_{1}x_{2}
-plane whose boundary is the parabola
\{x_{2}=x_{1}^{2}\}
. Let
p_{0}=(0,0,0)\in\partial K
and let
U\subset H
be an open, bounded neighborhood of
p_{0}
. Then, a direct computation shows that the Gaussian curvature of the surface
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
over
K\cap U
at
p_{0}
is

\tilde{K}(p_{0})=-\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}},

which is not bounded below uniformly in
\lambda
.

However, Theorem
1.3
is applicable to every convex billiard table in the hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
K\subset H
be a convex billiard table. Then, for every
p_{0}\in\partial K
, there exists an open bounded neighborhood
U\subset H
of
p_{0}
and
f\in C^{2}(\overline{U})
satisfying (
2
) such that the sectional curvatures of the fold
(M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}},g_{\lambda})
over
K\cap U
are nonnegative at every point for each
\lambda\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
p_{0}\in\partial K
be a boundary point. Since
K
is a convex billiard table, there exists an open bounded neighborhood
U\subset H
of
p_{0}
and
f\in C^{2}(\overline{U})
satisfying (
2
) such that
D^{2}f(x)
is negative semi-definite for every
(x,0)\in K\cap U
.

Here, we let
\{M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}\}_{\lambda\in(0,1)}
denote the family of folds over
K\cap U
. Since
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
is the zero-level set of the function

F_{\lambda}(x,x_{n+1})=x_{n+1}^{2}-\lambda^{2}f(x),

we know that, for every
q=(x,x_{n+1})\in M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
, the scalar second fundamental form of
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
at
q
(associated to the unit normal field given by the gradient of
F_{\lambda}
) reads

h_{q}(v,w)=\frac{v^{T}D^{2}F_{\lambda}(q)\ w}{|DF_{\lambda}(q)|},\quad\text{for}\ v,w\in T_{q}M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}},

where
DF_{\lambda}(q)
and
D^{2}F_{\lambda}(q)
denote the Euclidean gradient and Hessian matrix of
F_{\lambda}
at
q
respectively.
We further notice that

D^{2}F_{\lambda}(x,x_{n+1})=\begin{pmatrix}-\lambda^{2}\ D^{2}f(x)&0\\
0&2\end{pmatrix},

which is positive semi-definite since
D^{2}f(x)
is negative semi-definite. Thus, the bilinear form
h_{q}
is positive semi-definite.

Now, we recall that for every pair of linearly independent vectors
v,w\in T_{q}M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
, the Gauss equation for a Riemannian hypersurface yields that the sectional curvature of
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
at
q
associated to the plane spanned by
v,w
is

\mbox{sec}^{q}_{\lambda}(v,w)=\frac{h_{q}(v,v)h_{q}(w,w)-h_{q}(v,w)^{2}}{\overline{g}_{q}(v,v)\overline{g}_{q}(w,w)-\overline{g}_{q}(v,w)^{2}}

(see Theorem 8.13 of
[
5
]
). Since
h_{q}
is positive semi-definite and
\overline{g}_{q}
is positive definite, it is well known that for all
v,w\in T_{q}M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
it holds

h_{q}(v,v)h_{q}(w,w)-h_{q}(v,w)^{2}\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{g}_{q}(v,v)\overline{g}_{q}(w,w)-\overline{g}_{q}(v,w)^{2}>0.

This implies that
\mbox{sec}_{\lambda}^{q}(v,w)\geq 0
for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
.

∎

4.2
Negatively curved geometry

Let us consider the Riemannian metric
\overline{g}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n+1}\ \overline{g}_{ij}\ dx_{i}dx_{j}
, with

\overline{g}_{ij}(x,x_{n+1})=\delta_{ij}-\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{1+r^{2}},\quad r^{2}:=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n+1}^{2},

which turns
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
into a space of constant curvature
-1
.

Remark 4.3
.

One can check that the metric
\overline{g}
coincides with the pullback
\psi^{*}\eta
, where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\rightarrow S^{+}
is a smooth parametrization of the upper sheet
S^{+}\subset\{x_{n+2}>0\}
of the two-sheeted hyperboloid

S=\{(x,x_{n+1},x_{n+2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+2}:\ x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n+1}^{2}-x_{n+2}^{2}=-1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+2}

and
\eta
is the Riemannian metric on
S^{+}
induced by the Minkowski pseudo-metric in
\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
.

Similarly to the Euclidean case, even if there are billiard tables in the hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for which assumption
(H)
is not satisfied near every boundary point, Theorem
4.4
gives sufficient conditions on the geometry of the table for which
(H)
holds.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
K\subset H
be a billiard table and
p_{0}\in\partial K
. Assume that there is an open, bounded neighborhood
U\subset H
of
p_{0}
and a function
f\in C^{2}(\overline{U})
satisfying (
2
), such that
2f(x)-x\cdot Df(x)\geq 0
and
D^{2}f(x)
is negative semi-definite for every
(x,0)\in K\cap U
. Then, for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
, the sectional curvatures of the fold
(M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}},g_{\lambda})
over
K\cap U
are bounded below by
-1
at every point.

Proof.

We notice that for each
\lambda\in(0,1)
the fold
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
over
K\cap U
is the zero-level set of the function

F_{\lambda}(x,x_{n+1}):=x_{n+1}^{2}-\lambda^{2}f(x).

Then, for every
q\in M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
, the second fundamental form of
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
at
q
(associated to the unit normal field given by the gradient of
F_{\lambda}
) is

h_{q}(v,w)=\frac{v^{T}\Hess F_{\lambda}(q)\ w}{\|\grad F_{\lambda}(q)\|},\quad v,w\in T_{q}M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}},

where
\Hess F_{\lambda}(q)
and
\grad F_{\lambda}(q)
denote the Riemannian Hessian and gradient of
F_{\lambda}
at
q
, respectively.
A well-known formula of Riemannian geometry says that

(\Hess F_{\lambda}(q))_{ij}=(D^{2}F_{\lambda}(q))_{ij}+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{\partial F_{\lambda}}{\partial x_{k}}(q)\ \Gamma_{ij}^{k}(q),

where
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}(q)
are the Christoffel symbols of
\overline{g}
at
q=(x,x_{n+1})
. Then, a direct computation shows that

\Hess F_{\lambda}(q)=D^{2}F_{\lambda}(q)+\lambda^{2}\big(2f(x)-x\cdot Df(x)\big)\ G(q),

where the matrix
G(q)=(\overline{g}_{ij}(q))_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is positive definite and

D^{2}F_{\lambda}(q)=\begin{pmatrix}-\lambda^{2}\ D^{2}f(x)&0\\
0&2\end{pmatrix}

is positive semi-definite since
D^{2}f(x)
is negative semi-definite. Moreover, since
2f(x)-x\cdot Df(x)\geq 0
for every
x\in K\cap U
, we have that
\Hess F_{\lambda}(q)
is positive semi-definite as it is the sum of two positive semi-definite matrices. This implies that the bilinear form
h_{q}
is positive semi-definite as well.

Now, let
q\in M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
and let
v,w\in T_{q}M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
be linearly independent. Then, mimicking the proof of Theorem
4.2
, the Gauss equation for a Riemannian hypersurface yields that

\mbox{sec}_{\lambda}^{q}(v,w)=-1+\frac{h_{q}(v,v)h_{q}(w,w)-h_{q}(v,w)^{2}}{\overline{g}_{q}(v,v)\overline{g}_{q}(w,w)-\overline{g}_{q}(v,w)^{2}}\geq-1

since
h_{q}
is positive semi-definite and
\overline{g}_{q}
is positive definite. Hence all sectional curvatures of each fold are bounded below by
-1
.

∎

4.3
Positively curved geometry

Let us consider the Riemannian metric

\overline{g}=\frac{4}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}(dx_{1}^{2}+\dots+dx_{n+1}^{2}),\quad r^{2}:=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n+1}^{2},

which turns
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
into a Riemannian manifold of constant curvature
+1
.

Remark 4.5
.

One can check that the metric
\overline{g}
coincides with the pullback
(\varphi_{N}^{-1})^{*}g_{S}
where

\varphi_{N}:\ S^{n+1}\setminus\{N\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

is the stereographic projection from the north pole of the
(n+1)
-dimensional sphere
S^{n+1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
g_{S}
is the metric on
S^{n+1}
induced by the Euclidean metric of
\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
.

Let
H=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:\ x_{n+1}=0\}
be the hyperplane, let
K\subset H
be a billiard table and
p_{0}\in\partial K
. Apparently there are no simple conditions on the geometry of
K
near
p_{0}
that are equivalent to
(H)
. In general, one has to construct a suitable neighborhood
U\subset H
of
p_{0}
such that estimates on the sectional curvatures of each fold
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
over
K\cap U
can be carried out. In what follows, we present an example of such a procedure.

Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n+1}\}
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and we assume that the billiard table is the half-space

K=\{x\in H:\ x_{1}\geq 0\}.

Let
p_{0}=(0,\dots,0)\in\partial K
and we define

U:=\left\{(x,0)\in H:\ -\frac{3}{2}<3x_{1}^{2}-1-x_{2}^{2}-\dots-x_{n}^{2}<0\right\}\cap B(p_{0},1)\subset H,

where
B(p_{0},1)
is the Euclidean open ball centered at
p_{0}
with radius
1
. Since the function

x\mapsto 3x_{1}^{2}-1-x_{2}^{2}-\dots-x_{n}^{2}

is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and sends
p_{0}
to
-1
, we notice that
U\subset H
is an open, bounded neighborhood of
p_{0}
. Now, let
\left\{M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}\right\}_{\lambda\in(0,1)}
be the family of folds over
K\cap U
and recall that each
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
is the zero-level set of the function

F_{\lambda}(x,x_{n+1}):=x_{n+1}^{2}-\lambda^{2}x_{1}.

Then, the following proposition collects the result of some tedious but rather straightforward computations that we omit.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
q=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1})\in M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
. Then we have that

•

the tangent space
T_{q}M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
to
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
at
q
is spanned by the vectors

v_{1}=2x_{n+1}e_{1}+\lambda^{2}e_{n+1},\qquad v_{i}=e_{i},\quad i\in\{2,\dots,n\};

(3)

•

the Riemannian gradient of
F_{\lambda}
at
q
is given by

\grad F_{\lambda}(q)=\frac{(1+r^{2})^{2}}{4}(-\lambda^{2}\ e_{1}+2x_{n+1}e_{n+1}),\quad r^{2}:=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n+1}^{2};

•

the Riemannian Hessian of
F_{\lambda}
at
q\in M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
is given by

v_{i}^{T}\Hess F_{\lambda}(q)\ v_{j}=\begin{cases}2\lambda^{4}-\frac{8\lambda^{4}x_{1}^{2}}{1+r^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda^{6}x_{1}}{1+r^{2}},&\mbox{if }i=j=1,\\
-\frac{2\lambda^{2}x_{1}}{1+r^{2}},&\mbox{if }i=j\neq 1,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,n}
are as in (
3
).

Similarly to the previous subsections, we consider the scalar second fundamental form (associated to the unit normal field given by
\grad F_{\lambda}
) of
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
at
q
, which is given by

h_{q}(v_{i},v_{j})=\frac{v_{i}^{T}\Hess F_{\lambda}(q)\ v_{j}}{\|\grad F_{\lambda}(q)\|},

for every
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then, the Gauss equation for a Riemannian hypersurface yields that the sectional curvature of
M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
at
q
associated to the plane spanned by
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
, with
i\neq j
, is

\mbox{sec}_{\lambda}^{q}(v_{i},v_{j})=1+\frac{h_{q}(v_{i},v_{i})h_{q}(v_{j},v_{j})-h_{q}(v_{i},v_{j})^{2}}{\overline{g}_{q}(v_{i},v_{i})\overline{g}_{q}(v_{j},v_{j})-\overline{g}_{q}(v_{i},v_{j})^{2}}.

A direct computation gives us the following formulas for
\mbox{sec}_{\lambda}^{q}(v_{i},v_{j})
.

•

If
i,j\in\{2,\dots,n\}
,
i\neq j
, we have

\mbox{sec}_{\lambda}^{q}(v_{i},v_{j})=1+\frac{4\lambda^{4}x_{1}^{2}}{\|\mbox{grad}\ F_{\lambda}(q)\|^{2}\ \overline{g}_{q}(v_{i},v_{i})\ \overline{g}_{q}(v_{j},v_{j})}\geq 1.

•

If
j\in\{2,\dots,n\}
, we have

\mbox{sec}_{\lambda}^{q}(v_{1},v_{j})=1+\xi_{\lambda}(x),\quad\xi_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\lambda^{2}x_{1}(3x_{1}^{2}-1-x_{2}^{2}-\dots-x_{n}^{2})}{(4x_{1}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}.

We claim that the function
\xi_{\lambda}
is bounded below in
K\cap U
uniformly in
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Indeed, one can check that, for
(x_{1},\lambda)\in[0,\infty)\times(0,1)
, it holds

0\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}x_{1}}{(4x_{1}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{16}.

Therefore, for
(x,0)\in K\cap U
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have

\xi_{\lambda}(x)\geq-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\lambda^{2}x_{1}}{(4x_{1}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}\geq-\frac{3}{32}.

Hence, since for every
q\in M_{\lambda}^{p_{0}}
and
i,j,k\in\{2,\dots,n\}
we know
(x,0)\in K\cap U
, we have

\mbox{sec}_{\lambda}^{q}(v_{i},v_{j})\geq 1\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\mbox{sec}_{\lambda}^{q}(v_{1},v_{k})\geq 0.

This implies that assumption
(H)
is satisfied for
p_{0}=(0,\dots,0)
and Theorem
1.3
is applicable.

Appendix A
Billiards approximating Geodesics

The goal of this appendix is to make this paper self-contained and sketch the proof of Theorem
1.1
which states that geodesics on the boundary of a convex billiard table are approximated by internal billiard trajectories. The details of the proof are contained in Sections 4 and 5 of
[
4
]
. Here, we will consider only billiard tables
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which every billiard trajectory
c:[a,b]\rightarrow K
that is not entirely contained in
\partial K
can be extended uniquely for all times without experiencing infinitely many bounces in finite time.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the closure of an open orientable connected subset of the Euclidean space. We say that
K
is a
convex billiard table in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if its boundary
\partial K
is of class
C^{2,1}
and for every
p_{0}\in\partial K
the scalar second fundamental form at
p_{0}
(associated to the inward pointing unit normal field to
\partial K
) is positive definite.

Remark A.1
.

Notice that every convex billiard table
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a space of non-negative curvature according to Definition
2.1
.

According to
[
2
]
, if
K
is a convex billiard table in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and if
c:[a,b]\rightarrow K
is a billiard trajectory in
K
, the set of bounce times

\mathcal{T}=\{t_{0}\in[a,b]:\ c(t_{0})\in\partial K\}

of the trajectory
c
either is finite or coincides with the entire interval
[a,b]
. In particular, all billiard trajectories that are not entirely contained in
\partial K
can be extended to a billiard trajectory
\tilde{c}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow K
such that the set
\mathcal{T}=\{t_{0}:\ \tilde{c}(t_{0})\in\partial K\}
is discrete.

Here, Lemma 5.5 of
[
4
]
complements Theorem
3.6
of Section 3.

Lemma A.2

(= Lemma 5.5 of
[
4
]
)
.

Let
K
be a convex billiard table in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then every 0-quasigeodesic of
K
which is contained in
\partial K
is also a 0-quasigeodesic of
\partial K
(with respect to its intrinsic metric).

Corollary A.3
.

Let
K
be a convex billiard table in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then a Lipschitz curve
c:[a,b]\rightarrow K
which is not entirely contained in
\partial K
is a billiard trajectory if and only if it is a
0
-quasigeodesic.

Proof.

In one direction, Theorem
3.6
of Section 3 proves that every
0
-quasigeodesic of
K
is a billiard trajectory.

In the other direction, let
c:[a,b]\rightarrow K
be a billiard trajectory that is not entirely contained in
\partial K
. Then, since
K
is a convex billiard table, the set of bounce times of
c
is finite. If it is empty, then
c
is a geodesic segment of
K
and in particular a
0
-quasigeodesic. If
\mathcal{T}=\{t_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
is such that
t_{i}\leq t_{i+1}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
, we notice that the billiard trajectory
c
is the concatenation of the geodesic segments

\left\{c_{i}:[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\rightarrow K\right\}_{i=1,\dots,N-1}.

Moreover, the tangent vectors
c_{i}^{-}(t_{i+1})
and
c_{i+1}^{+}(t_{i+1})
are polar for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
according to Lemma
3.7
.

But then, Proposition
2.16
implies that the whole concatenation is a
0
-quasigeodesic of
K
.

∎

Now we are ready to derive Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem A.4

(= Theorem
1.1
)
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose boundary is of class
C^{2,1}
and has a positive definite second fundamental form at every point. Let
p_{0}\in\partial K
and
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of billiard trajectories of
K
starting from
p_{0}
. Then, if the initial direction of
\gamma_{k}
converges to a tangent vector
v\in T_{p_{0}}(\partial K)
, we have that the sequence
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally uniformly to the corresponding geodesic of the boundary.

We start the proof by recalling Lemma 5.3 of
[
4
]
.

Lemma A.5

(= Lemma 5.3 of
[
4
]
)
.

Let
K
be a convex billiard table in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If the initial directions of a sequence of billiard trajectories converge to a tangent vector in the boundary, then the sequence of billiard trajectories converges to a continuous curve of the boundary uniformly on every compact set of times.

Proof of Theorem
A.4
.

Let
p_{0}\in\partial K
and let us consider a sequence of billiard trajectories

\left\{c_{k}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow K\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

with
c_{k}(0)=p_{0}\in\partial K
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and such that there is
v\in T_{p_{0}}(\partial K)
for which
c_{k}^{+}(0)\rightarrow v
in
T_{p_{0}}K
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
Here, Lemma
A.5
implies that the sequence
\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to some continuous curve
c:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\partial K
uniformly on every compact set of times
[a,b]\subset[0,\infty)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Now, let us fix
[a,b]\subset[0,\infty)
and denote

\tilde{c}_{k}:=c_{k}\big|_{[a,b]},\quad\tilde{c}:=c\big|_{[a,b]}.

We notice that Corollary
A.3
implies that
\tilde{c}_{k}
is a
0
-quasigeodesic of
K
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, since
\tilde{c}
is the uniform limit of
\tilde{c}_{k}
, Proposition
2.18
implies that
\tilde{c}
is
0
-quasigeodesic of
K
as well.
Moreover, Lemma
A.2
implies that
\tilde{c}
is a
0
-quasigeodesic of
\partial K
with respect to its intrinsic metric.

In conclusion, since
\partial K
is a Riemannian manifold without boundary and a space of non-negative curvature, Proposition
2.15
yields that
\tilde{c}
is a geodesic segment of
\partial K
. Hence
c:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\partial K
is the unique geodesic of
\partial K
starting from
p_{0}
and with initial direction

c^{+}(0)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}c^{+}_{k}(0)=v.

∎

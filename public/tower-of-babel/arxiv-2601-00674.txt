1.2.
Main results

The main goal of this article is to obtain some properties of the minimal sequence, which are useful in
[

]
. We also provide some (partly conjectural) representation theoretic interpretations in Part

. The main results are the following subsequence and commutativity phenomenons:

Theorem 1.3
.

(=Theorem
8.6
)
Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. If
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
is minimal to
\pi
, then any submultisegment
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
of

is also minimal to
\pi

.

Theorem 1.4
.

(=Theorem

)
Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. If
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
is minimal to
\pi
, then for any submultisegment
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{n}
, we have:

(1)

\mathfrak{n}-\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is minimal to

; and

(2)

D_{\mathfrak{n}-\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}(\pi)\cong D_{\mathfrak{n}}(\pi)

By using Theorem

multiple times, we have:

.

Let

. Let

be minimal to

. Write the segments in

as

One important ingredient in studying the commutativity is a notion of

In order to demonstrate the above non-straightforward results, it is unavoidable to work on some details. The main idea of the proofs for Theorems

We remark that it has been known that the representation theory of reductive

2.
Highest derivative multisegments

The highest derivative multisegment is introduced in
[
Ch25
, Section 8.1]
as a main tool for the entire study. In this section, we first recall the definition and then prove a new realization theorem, which is of independent interests.

2.1.
Highest derivative multisegments

For
c\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
, define
\mathfrak{mxpt}^{a}(\pi,c)
to be the maximal multisegment such that

(1)

for any
\Delta\in\mathfrak{mxpt}^{a}(\pi,c)
,
a(\Delta)=c
; and

(2)

D_{\mathfrak{mxpt}^{a}(\pi,c)}(\pi)\neq 0
.

Here the maximality is to take the lexicographical ordering on the
b(\Delta)
values for all segments in
\mathfrak{mxpt}^{a}(\pi,c)
. See
[
Ch25
]
for details and examples. Define the
highest derivative multisegment
of
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
to be

\mathfrak{hd}(\pi):=\sum_{c\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{mxpt}^{a}(\pi,c).

It is shown in
[
Ch25
, Theorem 7.3]
that
D_{\mathfrak{hd}(\pi)}(\pi)
is the highest derivative of
\pi
in the sense of
[
Ze80
]
. An effecient algorithm in computing the highest derivative multisegment is given in
[
CP25+
]
. However, a key point is that effects of derivatives can be reflected from the removal process on the highest derivative multisegment, and so we can directly study removal processes.

2.2.
Realization Theorem

For
d,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and a cuspidal representation
\rho
, define
u_{\rho}(d,m)
to be the unique simple quotient of

\mathrm{St}\left(\left[-\frac{d-m}{2},\frac{d+m-2}{2}\right]_{\rho}\right)\times\mathrm{St}\left(\left[-\frac{d-m}{2}-1,\frac{d+m-2}{2}-1\right]_{\rho}\right)\times\ldots\times\mathrm{St}\left(\left[-\frac{d+m-2}{2},\frac{d-m}{2}\right]_{\rho}\right).

In other words,
u_{\rho}(d,m)
is the Langlands quotient of the above parabolically induced module, see
[
Ch25
, Section 2.6]

An irreducible representation
\pi

]

[

]

]

]

, called the infinity multisegment. We also set
\mathfrak{r}(\Delta,\infty)=\infty
.

3.3.
Properties of removal process

For a segment
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}\neq\emptyset
, let
{}^{-}\Delta=[a+1,b]_{\rho}
. Two non-empty segments
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}
and
\Delta^{\prime}=[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{\rho}
in
\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
are said to be
linked
if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

a<a^{\prime}\leq b+1\leq b^{\prime}
; or

(2)

a^{\prime}<a\leq b^{\prime}+1\leq b
.

Otherwise,
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
are called to be not linked or unlinked. For two linked segments
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
, we write
\Delta<\Delta^{\prime}
if it is in the first condition above. Otherwise, we write
\Delta^{\prime}<\Delta
. For example,
[2,3]_{\rho}<[4,5]_{\rho}
.

We recall the following properties for computations:

Lemma 3.2
.

[

Let

is
admissible
to

4.
Non-overlapping property for a sequence

In
[
Ch25b
]
, we have shown some characterizations for the minimality of two segment case. The goal of this section is to generalize a so-called non-overlapping property to a multisegment case. For this, we first recall some ingredients in
[
Ch25b
]
: fine chains and local minimizability. Those ingredients are combinatorial in nature and so most statements will be formulated for
\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
rather than
\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
.

4.1.
Zelevinsky ordering

For two linked segments
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}
and
\Delta^{\prime}=[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{\rho}
with
\Delta<\Delta^{\prime}
, we define:

\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime}=[a,b^{\prime}]_{\rho},\quad\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}=[a^{\prime},b]_{\rho}.

A multisegment
\mathfrak{n}
is said to be obtained from another multisegment
\mathfrak{m}
by an
elementary intersection-union process
if there exists a pair of linked segments
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{m}
such that

\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{m}-\Delta-\Delta^{\prime}+\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime}+\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}.

Here the subtractions mean the (multi-)set theoretic subtractions and additions mean the (multi-)set theoretic unions. We shall also use such notions for subtractions and additions later. Note that
\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}
is possibly the empty set and in such case, we simply drop the term. For example,

\left\{[1,3]_{\rho},[2]_{\rho},[4,5]_{\rho}\right\}
and
\left\{[1,2]_{\rho},[2,5]_{\rho}\right\}
are obtained from
\left\{[1,2]_{\rho},[2,3]_{\rho},[4,5]_{\rho}\right\}
by elementary intersection-union processes.

For two multisegments
\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
, we wrtie
\mathfrak{n}\leq_{Z}\mathfrak{m}
if
\mathfrak{n}
is obtained from
\mathfrak{m}
by a sequence of intersection-union processes, or
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}
. It is well-known from
[
Ze80
]
that
\leq_{Z}
defines a partial ordering on
\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
.

4.2.
Non-overlapping property and intermediate segment property

We first define the
\eta
-invariant, which is crucial in studying the minimality of sequences of derivatives:

Definition 4.1
.

(1)

Let
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Let
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
be admissible to
\mathfrak{h}
. Define

\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h}):=(\varepsilon_{[a,b]_{\rho}}(\mathfrak{h}),\varepsilon_{[a+1,b]_{\rho}}(\mathfrak{h}),\ldots,\varepsilon_{[b,b]_{\rho}}(\mathfrak{h})).

(2)

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\Delta\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
be admissible to
\pi
. Define
\eta_{\Delta}(\pi)=\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{hd}(\pi))
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
with
\Delta<\Delta^{\prime}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Suppose
\Delta
is admissible to
\mathfrak{h}
.

(1)

We say that
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{h})
satisfies the
non-overlapping property
if

\eta_{\Delta^{\prime}}(D_{\Delta}(\pi))=\eta_{\Delta^{\prime}}(\pi).

(2)

We say that
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{h})
satisfies the
intermediate segment property
if there exists a segment
\widetilde{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{h}
such that

a(\Delta)\leq a(\widetilde{\Delta})<a(\Delta^{\prime}),\mbox{ and }a(\widetilde{\Delta})\leq b(\Delta)\leq b(\widetilde{\Delta})<b(\Delta^{\prime}).

We remark that the original formulation of the non-overlapping property in
[
Ch25b
]
is phrased in terms of some properties among segments related to intersections between segments. We shall use the above equivalent combinatorial formulation (see
[
Ch25b
, Proposition 9.5]
), which will be more useful for our study later.

Proposition 4.3
.

[
Ch25b
, Proposition 9.5]

Let
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{h}
be as in Definition
4.2
. We further assume that
\Delta^{\prime}
is admissible to
\mathfrak{h}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{h})
satisfies the non-overlapping property.

(2)

(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{h})
satisfies the intermediate segment property.

(3)

\left\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\right\}
is the
\leq_{Z}
-minimal element in
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\right\},\mathfrak{h}))
, where
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\right\},\mathfrak{h})
is defined as:

\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\right\},\mathfrak{h}):=\left\{\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}:\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\right\},\mathfrak{h})\right\}.

4.3.
Fine chains

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
and let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Let
a
be the smallest integer such that
\mathfrak{n}[a]\neq\emptyset
. Write
\mathfrak{n}[a]=\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{k}\right\}
.

—

Define
\mathfrak{r}_{1}=\mathfrak{h}
. For
i\geq 2
, define

\mathfrak{r}_{i}:=\mathfrak{r}(\Delta_{i-1},\ldots,\mathfrak{r}(\Delta_{1},\mathfrak{h})\ldots).

Define

\mathfrak{fs}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h}):=\left\{\Upsilon(\Delta_{1},\mathfrak{r}_{1}),\ldots,\Upsilon(\Delta_{k},\mathfrak{r}_{k})\right\}.

—

Define

\mathfrak{trr}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h}):=\mathfrak{h}-\mathfrak{fs}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h})+{}^{-}(\mathfrak{fs}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h}))

and

\mathfrak{trd}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h}):=\mathfrak{n}-\mathfrak{n}[a]+{}^{-}(\mathfrak{n}[a]).

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
be admissible to
\mathfrak{h}
. Set
\mathfrak{h}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{0}=\mathfrak{n}
. Define recursively

\mathfrak{h}_{i}=\mathfrak{trr}(\mathfrak{n}_{i-1},\mathfrak{h}_{i-1}),\quad\mathfrak{n}_{i}=\mathfrak{trd}(\mathfrak{n}_{i-1},\mathfrak{h}_{i-1}).

The
fine chain for the removal process
for
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h})
(or simply fine chain for
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h})
) is the sequence

\mathfrak{fs}(\mathfrak{n}_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{0}),\mathfrak{fs}(\mathfrak{n}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{1}),\ldots

The sequences
\mathfrak{h}_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{1},\ldots
and
\mathfrak{n}_{0},\mathfrak{n}_{1},\ldots
will also be useful later.

Lemma 4.6
.

We use the notations in Definition
4.5
. Then, for all
i
,

\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n}_{i},\mathfrak{h}_{i}).

Proof.

This follows from repeated uses of Lemma
3.2
(1), or a slightly stronger statement of
[
Ch25b
, Lemma 3.4]
.
∎

4.4.
Local minimizability

Definition 4.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
and let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Let
a
be the smallest integer such that
\mathfrak{n}[a]\neq\emptyset
. We say that
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h})
is
locally minimizable
if there exists a segment
\Delta
in
\mathfrak{n}[a+1]
such that

|\left\{\widetilde{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{n}[a]:\Delta\subset\widetilde{\Delta}\right\}|<|\left\{\widetilde{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{fs}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h}):\Delta\subset\widetilde{\Delta}\right\}|.

Note that each
\widetilde{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{n}[a]
satisfying
\Delta\subset\widetilde{\Delta}
guarantees the corresponding first segment
\overline{\Delta}
in the removal sequence satisfying
\Delta\subset\overline{\Delta}
. Roughly speaking, when the difference of two cardinalities in Definition
4.7
is non-zero, one can find a "short" segment in
\mathfrak{n}[a]
to do the intersection-union process which still does not change the choices of first segments in the removal processes.

Theorem 4.8
.

[
Ch25b
, Lemma 6.1 and Lemma 6.2]

Let
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
be admissible to
\mathfrak{h}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{1},\ldots
and
\mathfrak{n}_{0},\mathfrak{n}_{1},\ldots
be as constructed in Definition
4.5
. Then
\mathfrak{n}
is minimal to
\mathfrak{h}
if and only if
(\mathfrak{n}_{i},\mathfrak{h}_{i})
is not locally minimizable for all
i
.

4.5.
Non-overlapping property for a sequence

We now generalize Proposition
4.3
to a multisegment situation, which will be used in Section
7.3
. We first prove a lemma:

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}
and
\Delta^{\prime}=[c,d]_{\rho}
be two segments. Suppose
\Delta^{\prime}<\Delta
. Let
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
such that
\Delta^{\prime}
is admissible to
\mathfrak{h}
. If
\Delta\subset\Upsilon(\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{h})
, then
\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{r}(\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{h}))\neq\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h})
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}
be the removal sequence for
(\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{h})
. Let
i^{*}
, which exists from our assumption, be the largest integer such that
b(\Delta)\leq b(\Delta_{i^{*}})
. Write
\Delta^{tr}_{i^{*}}=[a_{i^{*}},b_{i^{*}}]_{\rho}
. Then
\Delta_{i^{*}}^{tr}\neq\emptyset
contributes extra one to
\varepsilon_{[a_{i^{*}},b]_{\rho}}
for
\mathfrak{r}(\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{h})
. However, the segments
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}
as well as
\Delta^{tr}_{j}

(j\neq i^{*})
do not contribute to
\varepsilon_{[a_{i^{*}},b]_{\rho}}
. This implies the desired inequality.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
be minimal to
\mathfrak{h}
. Let
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}
be a segment such that
a(\Delta^{\prime})<a
and
b(\Delta^{\prime})<b
for any
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{n}
. Then
\mathfrak{n}+\Delta
is still minimal to
\mathfrak{h}
if and only if

\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h}))=\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h}).

Proof.

We construct a sequence of multisegments
\mathfrak{n}_{0},\mathfrak{n}_{1},\mathfrak{n}_{2},\ldots
and
\mathfrak{h}_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{2},\ldots
as in Definition
4.5
. By Lemma
4.6
, we have:

(*)\quad\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n}_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{0})=\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{1})=\ldots

Let
a_{i}
be the smallest integer such that
\mathfrak{n}_{i}[a_{i}]\neq\emptyset
.

Let
i^{*}
be the index such that
a_{i^{*}}=c-1
. If such index does not exist, it implies that
b(\widetilde{\Delta})<c-1
for all
\widetilde{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{n}
. In such case, by a direct computation on removal process using Definition
3.1
(3) and (4), one has
\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h})[x]=\mathfrak{h}[x]
for
x\geq c
. In particular,
\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h}))=\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h})
; and
\mathfrak{n}+\Delta
is still minimal since
\Delta
is unlinked to any segment in
\mathfrak{n}
. In other words, both conditions are automatically satisfied if such
i^{*}
does not exist.

We now assume such
i^{*}
exists. We first prove the only if direction. By the minimality condition and Theorem
4.8
,
(\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}}+\Delta,\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}})
is not locally minimizable. On the other hand, the hypothesis in this proposition guarantees that any segment
\widetilde{\Delta}
in
\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}}
satisfies
\Delta\not\in\widetilde{\Delta}
and so

|\left\{\widetilde{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}}[a]:\Delta\subset\widetilde{\Delta}\right\}|=0.

The local non-minimizability on
(\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}}+\Delta,\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}})
implies that

|\left\{\widetilde{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{fs}(\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}}+\Delta,\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}}):\Delta\subset\widetilde{\Delta}\right\}|=0.

In other words, for any segment
[x,y]_{\rho}
in
\mathfrak{fs}(\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}}+\Delta,\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}})=\mathfrak{fs}(\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}},\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}})
(the equaltiy follows from Definition
4.5
), it must take the form
[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}]_{\rho}
for
y^{\prime}\leq b-1
. Thus the segments involved or produced in the removal process (by the nesting property) cannot contribute to
\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}},\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}}))
and so

(**)\quad\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}},\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}}))=\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}}).

Moreover,
\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}}
is obtained by truncating left points for
\nu^{x}\rho
for some
x\leq c-2
. Thus,
\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h}_{0})=\ldots=\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}})
by Definitions
4.4
and
4.5
. Combining with (*) and (**), we have the desired equation, proving the only if direction.

We now prove the if direction. By the construction of
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
(Definition
4.5
), we have:

\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h})=\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}})

Now with (*) and the hypothesis
\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h})=\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h}))
, we again have that

\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}},\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}}))=\eta_{\Delta}(\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}}).

This condition says that
\mathfrak{fs}(\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}},\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}})
cannot take the form
[c-1,y]_{\rho}
for some
y\geq b
, by Lemma
4.9
and the assumption that any segment in
\mathfrak{n}
(and so
\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}}
) satisfies
b(\Delta)<b
. In other words,
(\mathfrak{n}_{i^{*}},\mathfrak{h}_{i^{*}})
is not locally minimizable. The local non-minimizability for other pairs
(\mathfrak{n}_{j},\mathfrak{h}_{j})
for
j<i^{*}
follows from the minimality of
\mathfrak{n}
. Then the minimality of
\mathfrak{n}+\Delta
to
\mathfrak{h}
follows from Theorem
4.8
.
∎

5.
Two segment basic case (commutativity)

5.1.
Lemma for unlinked segments

We recall the following first commutativity (see e.g.
[
Ch25
, Lemma 4.4]
), which will be used later:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
be unlinked. For any
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
,

D_{\Delta^{\prime}}\circ D_{\Delta}(\pi)\cong D_{\Delta}\circ D_{\Delta^{\prime}}(\pi).

5.2.
Intermediate segment property under a derivative

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
be admissible to
\pi
. Let
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
. Suppose
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{hd}(\pi))
satisfies the non-overlapping property or intermediate segment property. If
\Delta^{\prime\prime}\subset\Delta^{\prime}
, then
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{hd}(D_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}(\pi)))
also satisfies the non-overlapping property and the intermediate segment property.

Proof.

Since the non-overlapping property and the intermediate segment property are equivalent by Proposition
4.3
, it suffices to see that
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{hd}(D_{\Delta^{\prime}}(\pi)))
also satisfies the intermediate segment property.

Write
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}
,
\Delta^{\prime}=[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{\rho}
and
\Delta^{\prime\prime}=[a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime}]_{\rho}
. By Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
, we have that for any segment
\widetilde{\Delta}=[\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b}]_{\rho}
,

(*)\quad\varepsilon_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\mathfrak{r}(\Delta^{\prime\prime},\pi))\leq\varepsilon_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(D_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}(\pi)).

From this, one can recover
\mathfrak{hd}(\pi)[c]
and
\mathfrak{r}(\Delta,\pi)[c]
for each integer
c
. The missing part of (*) between
\mathfrak{r}(\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{hd}(\pi))
and
\mathfrak{hd}(D_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}(\pi))
is on some values
a^{\prime\prime}-1,\ldots,b^{\prime\prime}-1<b^{\prime}
. Thus, one obtains
\mathfrak{hd}(D_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}(\pi))
by prolonging some segments in
\mathfrak{r}(\Delta^{\prime\prime},\mathfrak{hd}(\pi))
using (possibly some of)
a^{\prime\prime}-1,\ldots,b^{\prime\prime}-1
.

Now, by the intermediate segment property for
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{hd}(\pi))
, there exists a segment of the form
[c,d]_{\rho}
satisfying:

a\leq c<a^{\prime},\quad b\leq d<b^{\prime}.

Now, by the above process of obtaining
\mathfrak{hd}(D_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}(\pi))
, the segment
[c,d]_{\rho}
can be prolonged to the form
[c,e]_{\rho}
in
\mathfrak{r}(\Delta^{\prime\prime},\pi)
for some
e\leq b^{\prime}-1
. Then the segment
[c,e]_{\rho}
gives the desired requirement for the intermediate segment property for
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},D_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}(\pi))
. Thus, by Proposition
4.3
, the triple satisfies the two properties.
∎

For a segment
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
and
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
, define:

|\eta|_{\Delta}(\pi)=\varepsilon_{[a,b]_{\rho}}(\pi)+\varepsilon_{[a+1,b]_{\rho}}(\pi)+\ldots+\varepsilon_{[b,b]_{\rho}}(\pi).

In view of (
3.2
),
|\eta|_{\Delta}(\pi)
measures the number of segments
\overline{\Delta}
in
\mathfrak{hd}(\pi)
satisfying that
\nu^{a}\rho\leq a(\overline{\Delta})\leq\nu^{b}\rho
and
\nu^{b}\rho\leq b(\overline{\Delta})
. For example, suppose we have
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
such that

\mathfrak{hd}(\pi)=\left\{[1,4]_{\rho},[1,3]_{\rho},[1,2]_{\rho},[2,5]_{\rho},[2,4]_{\rho}\right\}.

Then
|\eta|_{[1,3]_{\rho}}(\pi)=4
, which is contributed from
[1,4]_{\rho},[1,3]_{\rho}
for
\varepsilon_{[1,3]_{\rho}}(\pi)
and from
[2,5]_{\rho},[2,4]_{\rho}
for
\varepsilon_{[2,3]_{\rho}}(\pi)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
. Suppose
\Delta
is admissible to
\pi
. Suppose
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\mathfrak{hd}(\pi))
satisfies the non-overlapping property or the intermediate segment property. Let
\widetilde{\Delta}=\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime}
.

|\eta|_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\pi)-|\eta|_{\Delta^{\prime}}(\pi)=|\eta|_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(D_{\Delta}(\pi))-|\eta|_{\Delta^{\prime}}(D_{\Delta}(\pi)).

Proof.

Write the removal sequence for
(\Delta,\pi)
to be:

\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}.

By the intermediate segment property, there exists a segment
\Delta_{k}
such that
a(\Delta_{k})<a(\Delta^{\prime})
and
b(\Delta_{k})<b(\Delta^{\prime})
. Let
k^{*}
be the smallest such integer. Then, with the nesting property, only
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{k^{*}-1}
(among
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}
) contribute to
|\eta|_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\pi)-|\eta|_{\Delta^{\prime}}(\pi)
.

Write
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}
and
\Delta^{\prime}=[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{\rho}
. By Theorem
3.4
, for
a\leq c\leq a^{\prime}-1

\varepsilon_{[c,b^{\prime}]_{\rho}}(D_{\Delta}(\pi))=\varepsilon_{[c,b^{\prime}]_{\rho}}(\mathfrak{r}(\Delta,\pi)).

Then, only
\Delta_{1}^{tr},\ldots,\Delta_{k^{*}-1}^{tr}
(among
\Delta_{1}^{tr},\ldots,\Delta_{r}^{tr}
) can contribute to
|\eta|_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\pi)-|\eta|_{\Delta^{\prime}}(\pi)
and so this implies the equality.
∎

5.3.
Commutativity and minimality for two segment case

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
be admissible to
\pi
. Suppose
\Delta_{1}<\Delta_{2}
and
(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\mathfrak{hd}(\pi))
satisfies the non-overlapping property, or equivalently

D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}}(\pi).

Then

D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\cong D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi).

Proof.

The equivalence of the two conditions follows from Proposition
4.3
.

We shall use the notations in the proof. Indeed, in view of a criteria of commutativity in
[
Ch25
, Proposition 6.2]
, it suffices to prove

D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}}(\pi).

Let
\widetilde{\Delta}=\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}
. To this end, it suffices to show that

(5.3)

\displaystyle(|\eta|_{\widetilde{\Delta}}-|\eta|_{\Delta_{2}})(D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi))=(|\eta|_{\widetilde{\Delta}}-|\eta|_{\Delta_{2}})(\pi).

Note that, by the unlinked part of Theorem
3.4
,

(|\eta|_{\widetilde{\Delta}}-|\eta|_{\Delta_{2}})(D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi))=(|\eta|_{\widetilde{\Delta}}-|\eta|_{\Delta_{2}})(\pi)

On the other hand, by Proposition
4.3
(2),
(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\pi)
satisfies the intermediate segment property and so
(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi))
also satisfies the intermediate segment property by Lemma
5.2
. Now, by Lemma
5.3
,

(|\eta|_{\widetilde{\Delta}}-|\eta|_{\Delta_{2}})(D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi))=(|\eta|_{\widetilde{\Delta}}-|\eta|_{\Delta_{2}})(D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi)).

Combining above two equations, we have (
5.3
) as desired.
∎

We shall give a proof of Proposition

from a representation-theoretic perspective (see Lemma
10.2

6.
Some preliminary results for subsequent and commutativity properties

6.1.
Cancellative property

Proposition 6.1
.

(Cancellative property)
Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
be multisegments with respective segments in the following respective ascending sequences:

\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{p}^{\prime},\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}

and

\Delta_{1}^{\prime\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{q}^{\prime\prime},\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}.

Then
\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime},\pi)=\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime\prime},\pi)
if and only if

\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{p}^{\prime}\right\},\pi)=\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta_{1}^{\prime\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{q}^{\prime\prime}\right\},\pi).

Proof.

The if direction is straightforward. We now consider the only if direction. By Theorem
2.3
, let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
such that
\mathfrak{hd}(\pi)=\mathfrak{h}
. By Theorem
3.3
, we have that

D_{\Delta_{r}}\circ\ldots\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta_{p}^{\prime}}\circ\ldots\circ D_{\Delta_{1}^{\prime}}(\pi)\cong D_{\Delta_{r}}\circ\ldots\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta_{q}^{\prime}}\circ\ldots\circ D_{\Delta_{1}^{\prime}}(\pi).

For any irreducible
\tau\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
and any segment
\Delta\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
, denote by
I_{\Delta}(\tau)
the unique irreducible submodule of
\pi\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta)
. Now, by uniqueness,
I_{\Delta_{i}}\circ D_{\Delta_{i}}(\tau)\cong\tau
if
D_{\Delta_{i}}(\tau)\neq 0
for any
i
and irreducible
\tau
. Hence, we cancel the derivatives
D_{\Delta_{r}},\ldots,D_{\Delta_{1}}
to obtain:

D_{\Delta_{p}^{\prime}}\circ\ldots\circ D_{\Delta_{1}^{\prime}}(\pi)\cong D_{\Delta_{q}^{\prime}}\circ\ldots\circ D_{\Delta_{1}^{\prime}}(\pi).

∎

6.2.
First subsequent property

We shall frequently use the following simple fact:

Lemma 6.2
.

[
Ze80
, Section 6.7]

Let
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}
be a sequence of segments in an ascending order. Suppose
\Delta_{k}
and
\Delta_{k+1}
are linked for some
k
. Then

\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{k-1},\Delta_{k}\cup\Delta_{k+1},\Delta_{k}\cap\Delta_{k+1},\Delta_{k+2},\ldots,\Delta_{r}

is also in an ascending order.

A particular case of the lemma is that if
\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime\prime}
are in an ascending order, then one has:

(1)

\Delta^{\prime}\cap\Delta^{\prime\prime},\Delta^{\prime}\cup\Delta^{\prime\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime\prime}
is also in an ascending order;

(2)

\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}\cap\Delta^{\prime\prime\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}\cup\Delta^{\prime\prime\prime}
is also in an ascending order.

From these two simple cases, one can deduce some other variations needed in the following Proposition
6.3
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{\pi}\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}
be minimal to
\pi
. We write the segments in
\mathfrak{n}
in an ascending order

\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}.

Then, for any
s\leq r
,

(1)

\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{s}\right\}
is still minimal to
\pi
; and

(2)

\left\{\Delta_{s+1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}\right\}
is minimal to
\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{s}\right\},\pi)
.

Proof.

We only prove (1), and (2) can be proved similarly.

The admissibility follows from definitions (and Lemma
3.2
(5)). Let

\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}=\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{s}\right\}.

We pick two linked segments
\Delta_{i}
and
\Delta_{j}
in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
and we set

\mathfrak{n}^{\prime\prime}=\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}-\left\{\Delta_{i},\Delta_{j}\right\}+\Delta_{i}\cup\Delta_{j}+\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta_{j}.

It suffices to show that
\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime\prime},\mathfrak{h})\neq\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h})
. To this end, we first write the segments in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime\prime}
in an ascending order:

\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{s}^{\prime}.

(There are
s-1
segments if
\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta_{j}=\emptyset
, but the below arguments could be still applied.) It follows from Lemma
6.2
that
\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{s}^{\prime},\Delta_{s+1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}
are still in an ascending order.

Now we return to the proof. The minimality of
\mathfrak{n}
implies that

\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{s}^{\prime},\Delta_{s+1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}\right\},\pi)\neq\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{s},\Delta_{s+1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}\right\},\pi).

By Proposition
6.1
,

\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{s}^{\prime}\right\},\pi)\neq\mathfrak{r}(\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{s}\right\},\pi),

as desired.
∎

Lemma 7.1

For (1), we consider two cases.

For (2), it is similar, but

in above notation becomes

. The term
\Delta_{1}
explains

∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}=\left\{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}\right\}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
in an ascending order. Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}

\mathfrak{m}

. Then
\left\{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{3}\right\}
is also minimal to
D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.3

\left\{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\right\}
is minimal to
\pi
. By Proposition
5.4
(for the linked case between
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}

(for the unlinked case between
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}

D_{\Delta_{3}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\cong D_{\Delta_{3}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi).

The minimality is automatic if
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{3}
are unlinked. So we shall assume that
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{3}
are linked. There are three possibilities:

—

\Delta_{2}
is unlinked to
\Delta_{1}
(and so

\mathfrak{m}
and Proposition
6.3
imply this case.

—

\Delta_{2}
is unlinked to
\Delta_{3}

(i)

When

(resp.

(ii)

to

. Then, by Lemma

to show that

(iii)

and

(iv)

also give that
\Delta_{1}

7.4.

We now need some inputs from representation theory to prove a combinatorics result. Let

(depending on

) be the unipotent radical containing matrices of the form
\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-i}&*\\
&I_{i}\end{pmatrix}

\pi

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho},\Delta^{\prime}=[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{\rho}
be two segments such that
\Delta<\Delta^{\prime}
. Let
\omega=\mathrm{St}(\left\{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\right\})
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a multisegment whose segments
\Delta^{\prime\prime}=[a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime}]_{\rho}
satisfy that

and

\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})\times\omega
is irreducible and

\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})\times\omega\cong\omega\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m}).

Proof.

It is well-known that the second assertion implies the first one. We only have to prove the first one. Since
\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})
can be written as
\times_{\Delta\in\mathfrak{m}}\mathrm{St}(\Delta)
, it reduces to the case that
\mathfrak{m}
contains only one segment and so now we consider
\mathfrak{m}=\left\{\widetilde{\Delta}\right\}
.

We analyse possible composition factors of

\mathrm{St}(\widetilde{\Delta})\times\omega.

Since we know that a composition factor of
\mathrm{St}(\widetilde{\Delta})\times\omega
is also a composition factor of
\mathrm{St}(\widetilde{\Delta})\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta)\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta^{\prime})
, the possible composition factors are

\mathrm{St}(\left\{\widetilde{\Delta},\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\right\}),\mathrm{St}(\widetilde{\Delta}\cup\Delta^{\prime}+\widetilde{\Delta}\cap\Delta^{\prime}+\Delta),\mathrm{St}(\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime}+\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}+\widetilde{\Delta}).

We denote the three representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
respectively.

Thus it suffices to show that the last two composition factors cannot appear in
\mathrm{St}(\widetilde{\Delta})\times\omega
. We first consider
\pi_{2}
. Note that
\pi_{2}
is generic. However,
\omega
is not generic and so
\mathrm{St}(\widetilde{\Delta})\times\omega
cannot contains a generic composition factor and so
\pi_{2}
cannot appear in
\mathrm{St}(\widetilde{\Delta})\times\omega
.

We now consider
\pi_{3}
. Let

. Then
(\pi_{3})_{N_{l}}
has the composition factor
\mathrm{St}(\Delta)\boxtimes\mathrm{St}(\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime})
. Now we consider composition factors in
(\mathrm{St}(\widetilde{\Delta})\times\omega)_{N_{l}}
. If
(\mathrm{St}(\widetilde{\Delta})\times\omega)_{N_{l}}
contains the factor
\mathrm{St}(\Delta)\boxtimes\mathrm{St}(\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime})
, a simple composition factor is a simple composition factor in

\mathrm{St}(\Delta)\times\omega_{1}\boxtimes\omega_{2},

where
\omega_{1}\boxtimes\omega_{2}
is a simple composition factor in
\omega_{N_{l}}
. However, the possibilities of those composition factors are well-known and it is impossible for
\omega_{2}
to be the factor
\mathrm{St}(\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime})
.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let

(2)

We have shown in Lemma

\pi
. Thus, we have

D_{\Delta_{3}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\Delta_{2}\cup\Delta_{3}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}\cap\Delta_{3}}(\pi).

By a standard argument (see e.g. proof of
[
Ch25
, Proposition 6.2]
), we have that
\pi
is the unique simple submodule of

D_{\Delta_{3}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\left\{\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}\right\}).

Write
\Delta_{2}=[a_{2},b_{2}]_{\rho}
. Let

\mathfrak{m}=\sum_{c<a_{2}}\varepsilon_{[c,b_{2}]_{\rho}}(D_{\Delta_{3}}(\pi))\cdot[c,b_{2}]_{\rho},

and

\mathfrak{p}=\sum_{c<a_{2}}\varepsilon_{[c,b_{2}]_{\rho}}(\pi)\cdot[c,b_{2}]_{\rho}.

Thus, we have:

D_{\Delta_{3}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi)\hookrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{m}}\circ D_{\Delta_{3}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})

and so

\pi\hookrightarrow D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\left\{\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}\right\})\hookrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{m}}\circ D_{\Delta_{3}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})\times\mathrm{St}(\left\{\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}\right\}).

By Lemma
7.5
,

\pi\hookrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{m}}\circ D_{\Delta_{3}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}}(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\left\{\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}\right\})\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m}).

This implies that
D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\neq 0
and so
\mathfrak{m}
is a submultisegment of
\mathfrak{p}
. On the other hand, using Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
3.5
, we also have that
\mathfrak{p}
is a submultisegment of
\mathfrak{m}
. Hence,
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{p}
. Translating to
\eta
-invariants, we obtain (1).

We now prove (2). Write
\Delta_{1}=[a_{1},b_{1}]_{\rho}
. Suppose
(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\pi)
satisfies the intermediate segment property. Then there exists a segment
[a,b]_{\rho}
in
\mathfrak{hd}(\pi)
satisfying that
a_{1}\leq a<a_{2}
and
b_{1}\leq b<b_{2}
. Then,

\varepsilon_{[a,b]_{\rho}}(\pi)>\varepsilon_{[a,b_{2}]_{\rho}}(\pi)

By (1), we have that

\varepsilon_{[a,b_{2}]_{\rho}}(\pi)=\varepsilon_{[a,b_{2}]_{\rho}}(D_{\Delta_{3}}(\pi)).

By Theorem
3.5
,

\varepsilon_{[a,b]_{\rho}}(D_{\Delta_{3}}(\pi))\geq\varepsilon_{[a,b]_{\rho}}(\pi).

Combining the above equalities and inequalities, we have:

\varepsilon_{[a,b]_{\rho}}(D_{\Delta_{3}}(\pi))>\varepsilon_{[a,b_{2}]_{\rho}}(D_{\Delta}(\pi)).

This implies that there exists a segment
[a,b^{\prime}]_{\rho}
in
\mathfrak{hd}(D_{\Delta_{3}}(\pi))
with
b^{\prime}<b_{2}
. Thus
(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},D_{\Delta_{3}}(\pi))
satisfies the intermediate segment property.
∎

8.
Subsequent property of minimal sequences

8.1.
Consecutive pairs

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Two segments
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
in
\mathfrak{m}
are said to be
consecutive
in
\mathfrak{m}
if

—

\Delta_{1}<\Delta_{2}
i.e.
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
are linked with
a(\Delta_{1})<a(\Delta_{2})

—

there is no other segment
\Delta^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{m}
such that

a(\Delta_{1})\leq a(\Delta^{\prime})\leq a(\Delta_{2}),\quad b(\Delta_{1})\leq b(\Delta^{\prime})\leq b(\Delta_{2})

and
\Delta^{\prime}
is linked to either
\Delta_{1}
or
\Delta_{2}
.

(The last linkedness condition guarantees that
\Delta^{\prime}\neq\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}
and
\Delta^{\prime}\neq\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}
.)

Example 8.2
.

—

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\left\{[0,3],[1,4],[2,5]\right\}
. Then
[0,3],[1,4]
form a pair of consecutive segments. Similarly,
[1,4],[2,5]
also form a pair of consecutive segments, but
[0,3],[2,5]
do not form a pair of consecutive segments.

—

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\left\{[0,4],[1,2],[2,5]\right\}
. Then
[0,4],[2,5]
form a pair of consecutive segments; and
[1,2],[2,5]
also form a pair of consecutive segments.

—

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\left\{[0,3],[1,3],[2,4],[2,5]\right\}
. Then
[1,3],[2,4]
form a pair of consecutive segments, while
[0,3],[2,4]
do not form a pair of consecutive segments.

The terminology of consecutive segments is suggested by its property in the intersection-union process.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
be linked segments with
\Delta_{1}<\Delta_{2}
. Suppose there exists a segment
\Delta^{\prime}
satisfying the conditions in the second bullet of Definition
8.1
. Then, if
\Delta^{\prime}
is linked to
\Delta_{i}
(
i=1,2
), then

\left\{\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2},\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2},\Delta^{\prime}\right\}\leq_{Z}\left\{\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta^{\prime},\Delta_{i}\cup\Delta^{\prime},\Delta_{j}\right\}\leq_{Z}\left\{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta^{\prime}\right\}.

Here
j
is the index other than
i
i.e.
j\in\left\{1,2\right\}-\left\{i\right\}
.

The above lemma follows from a direct checking and we omit the details. A simple combinatorics give the following:

Lemma 8.4
.

(1)

Let
\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\leq_{Z}\mathfrak{m}
with
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\neq\mathfrak{m}
. Then there exists a pair of consecutive segments
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{m}
such that for the multisegment
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}
obtained from
\mathfrak{m}
by the elementary intersection-union process involving
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
,
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\leq_{Z}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}\leq_{Z}\mathfrak{m}
.

(2)

Let
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Let
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
be a pair of consecutive segments in
\mathfrak{m}
with
\Delta<\Delta^{\prime}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
be the submultisegment of
\mathfrak{m}-\Delta-\Delta^{\prime}
that contains all the segments
\widetilde{\Delta}
with
a(\Delta^{\prime})\leq a(\widetilde{\Delta})
or
b(\Delta^{\prime})\leq b(\widetilde{\Delta})
. Write the segments in
\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}-\Delta-\Delta^{\prime}
in an ascending order:
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}
and write the segments in
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
in an ascending order:
\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{s}^{\prime}
. Then the sequence:

\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r},\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{s}^{\prime}

is ascending.

Proof.

For (1), it suffices to show for
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
obtained by a pair of elementary intersection-union operation involving
\overline{\Delta}
and
\overline{\Delta}^{\prime}
. If the segments involved in the operation are consecutive, then the statement is immediate. Otherwise, there exists a segment
\widetilde{\Delta}
such that
\widetilde{\Delta}
is linked to either
\overline{\Delta}
or
\overline{\Delta}^{\prime}
, and produce a multisegment
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\leq_{Z}\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}\leq_{Z}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}
. We repeat the process if such linked pair is still not consecutive. Note that if
\widetilde{\Delta}
is linked to
\overline{\Delta}
(resp.
\overline{\Delta}^{\prime}
), we must have
\widetilde{\Delta}\cap\overline{\Delta}
(resp.
\widetilde{\Delta}\cap\overline{\Delta}^{\prime}
) strictly longer than
\overline{\Delta}\cap\overline{\Delta}^{\prime}
, and hence after repeating the process several times, we obtain desired consecutive segments.

For (2), it is a direct check from the definition of an ascending order.
∎

8.2.
Minimality under commutativity (second basic case)

We first prove a commutativity result of minimal sequences, which is useful in proving Theorem
8.6
.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}
. Let
\Delta,\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\ldots,\Delta_{r}
be in an ascending order and minimal to
\pi
. Then
\left\{\Delta,\Delta_{k+1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}\right\}
is also minimal to
D_{\Delta_{k}}\circ\ldots\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\neq 0
, and

D_{\Delta_{r}}\circ\ldots\circ D_{\Delta_{k+1}}\circ D_{\Delta}\circ D_{\Delta_{k}}\circ\ldots D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\cong D_{\Delta_{r}}\circ\ldots\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta}(\pi).

Proof.

By induction, it suffices to show when
k=1
. The case that
\Delta
and
\Delta_{1}
are unlinked is easy by Lemma
5.1
and Proposition
6.3
(1). Suppose
\left\{\Delta,\Delta_{2},\ldots,\Delta_{r}\right\}
is not minimal to
D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)
to arrive a contradiction. By Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
8.4
(1), there exists a pair of consecutive segments
\Delta^{\prime}<\Delta^{\prime\prime}
such that the multisegment obtained from the intersection-union operation of those two segments
\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}
gives the same derivative on
\pi
.

Note that
\left\{\Delta_{2},\ldots,\Delta_{r}\right\}
is minimal to
D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta}(\pi)\cong D_{\Delta}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)
(the last isomorphism by Proposition
5.4
). Thus the remaining possible cases to be considered could be that one of
\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}
is
\Delta
and so
\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta
. Let

\mathfrak{n}=\left\{\Delta_{i}:a(\Delta_{i})\leq a(\Delta)\quad\mbox{or}\quad b(\Delta_{i})\leq b(\Delta)\right\}

Then any
\Delta_{i}
is unlinked to
\Delta
and
\Delta_{1}
by using the ascending property for the sequence
\Delta,\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}
(and
\Delta<\Delta_{1}
). (In particular,
\Delta_{1}
is not in
\mathfrak{n}
.)

Let
\mathfrak{m}=\left\{\Delta_{2},\ldots,\Delta_{r}\right\}
. Now, since we chose
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime\prime}
to be consecutive, we can arrange and relabel the segments in an ascending order:

\widetilde{\Delta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Delta}_{l},\Delta,\Delta^{\prime\prime},\widetilde{\Delta}_{k+1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Delta}_{r-1},

where all
\widetilde{\Delta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Delta}_{l}
are all elements in
\mathfrak{n}
and
\widetilde{\Delta}_{k+1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Delta}_{r-1}
are all elements in
\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{n}
.

Case 1:

\mathfrak{n}=\emptyset
. We still have that
\Delta,\Delta_{1},\Delta^{\prime\prime},\widetilde{\Delta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Delta}_{r-1}
form an ascending order and is minimal to
\pi
. In particular, we have
\Delta,\Delta_{1},\Delta^{\prime\prime}
is minimal to
\pi
by the cancellative property. Thus

D_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\circ D_{\Delta}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}\cup\Delta}\circ D_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}\cap\Delta}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)

by Lemma
7.2
and Proposition
5.4
. Since

\Delta^{\prime\prime}\cap\Delta,\Delta^{\prime\prime}\cup\Delta,\widetilde{\Delta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Delta}_{r-1}

still form an ascending order (Lemma
6.2
), applying
D_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}},\ldots,D_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{r-1}}
gives different derivatives on
\pi
(Proposition
6.1
) and so this gives a contradiction.

Case 2:

\mathfrak{n}\neq\emptyset
.
This implies that
\Delta+\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{n}
is not minimal to
D_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)
. However, we have that, by using unlinkedness discussed in the second paragraph,

D_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\cong D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{n}}(\pi).

and
\Delta+\Delta_{1}+\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{n}
is minimal to
D_{\mathfrak{n}}(\pi)
by Proposition
6.3
. However, from Case 1, we have that
\Delta+\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{n}
is minimal to

D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{n}}(\pi)(\cong D_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)).

This gives a contradiction.
∎

8.3.
Minimality of a subsequent sequence

Theorem 8.6
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
be minimal to
\pi
. Then any submultisegment of
\mathfrak{n}
is also minimal to
\pi
.

Proof.

By an induction, it suffices to show the minimality for
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{n}-\Delta
for any segment
\Delta
in
\mathfrak{n}
. By Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
8.4
, it reduces to check that for any multisegment
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
obtained from
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
by an elementary intersection-union operations involving two consecutive segments,

D_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}(\pi).

Denote such two consecutive segments by
\widetilde{\Delta}<\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be obtained by the intersection-union process from
\mathfrak{n}
involving
\widetilde{\Delta}
and
\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}
. Then
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}-\Delta
.

We consider following possibilities:

(1)

Case 1: Suppose
\widetilde{\Delta}
and
\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}
still form a pair of consecutive segments in
\mathfrak{n}
. Then we write the segments in
\mathfrak{n}
as in Lemma
8.4
(with obvious notation replacement):

\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r},\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime},\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{s}^{\prime}.

(a)

Case 1(a):
\Delta
appears in one of
\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{s}^{\prime}
. If
D_{\mathfrak{n}-\Delta}(\pi)=D_{\mathfrak{m}-\Delta}(\pi)
, then the cancellative property (Proposition
6.1
) and Lemma
8.4
(also see Proposition
6.3
) imply that

D_{\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r},\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\right\}}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r},\widetilde{\Delta}\cup\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime},\widetilde{\Delta}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\right\}}(\pi).

However, this implies
D_{\mathfrak{n}}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)
by applying
D_{\Delta_{1}^{\prime}},\ldots,D_{\Delta_{s}^{\prime}}
with
D_{\Delta}
omitted.

(b)

Case 1(b):
\Delta
appears in one of
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}
. Let

\mathfrak{p}=\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r},\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\right\}-\Delta

and let
\mathfrak{q}
be obtained from
\mathfrak{p}
by an elementary intersection-union process on
\widetilde{\Delta}
and
\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}
. By the cancellative property (Proposition
6.1
), it suffices to show that
D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\mathfrak{q}}(\pi)
.

Let
\tau=D_{\mathfrak{p}-\widetilde{\Delta}-\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}-\Delta}(\pi)
. By repeatedly using Lemma
8.5
, we have:

D_{\mathfrak{p}+\Delta}(\pi)\cong D_{\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}}\circ D_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\circ D_{\Delta}(\tau)

and
\left\{\Delta,\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\right\}
is minimal to
\tau
. Now by Lemma
7.3
, we have that

D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)=D_{\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}}\circ D_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\tau)\not\cong D_{\widetilde{\Delta}\cup\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}}\circ D_{\widetilde{\Delta}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}}(\tau)=D_{\mathfrak{q}}(\pi)

as desired.

(2)

Case 2:
\widetilde{\Delta}
and
\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}
do not form a consecutive pair. We then first write the segments in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
as in Lemma
8.4
:

\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r},\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime},\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{s}^{\prime}.

Since the pair is not consecutive, the segment
\Delta
must take the form as in the second bullet of Definition
8.1
. Then one can still check that

\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r},\widetilde{\Delta},\Delta,\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime},\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{s}^{\prime}

is an ascending sequence. Now let
\tau=D_{\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}\right\}}(\pi)
. By the cancellative property (Proposition
6.1
), it suffices to show that

\quad D_{\left\{\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\right\}}(\tau)\not\cong D_{\left\{\widetilde{\Delta}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime},\widetilde{\Delta}\cup\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\right\}}(\tau).

This follows from the basic case of Lemma
7.4
.

∎

9.
Commutativity and minimality

In this section, we study the commutativity for a minimal sequence.

9.1.
Commutativity and minimality (anther basic case)

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
be minimal to
\pi
. Let
\Delta\in\mathfrak{m}
. Then
D_{\mathfrak{m}-\Delta}\circ D_{\Delta}(\pi)\cong D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)
.

Proof.

We write the segments in
\mathfrak{m}-\Delta
in an ascending order:
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}
. By Proposition
6.3
(1), we can reduce to the case that
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{r},\Delta
still form an ascending order. By Proposition
6.3
(2) and the basic case (Proposition
5.4
),

D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\cong D_{\Delta}\circ D_{\mathfrak{m}-\Delta}(\pi)\cong D_{\Delta_{r}}\circ D_{\Delta}\circ D_{\mathfrak{m}-\Delta-\Delta_{r}}(\pi).

By Theorem
8.6
,
\mathfrak{m}-\Delta_{r}
is still minimal to
\pi
. We now inductively obtain the statement.
∎

We first study a special case of commutativity and minimality, and we shall prove a full version in Theorem
9.3
.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
be minimal to
\pi
. Let
c
(resp.
d
) be the largest integer such that
\mathfrak{m}[c]\neq 0
(resp.
\mathfrak{m}\langle d\rangle\neq 0
). Let
\Delta\in\mathfrak{m}[c]
or
\in\mathfrak{m}\langle d\rangle
. Then
\mathfrak{m}-\Delta
is minimal to
D_{\Delta}(\pi)
.

Proof.

The condition in the lemma guarantees that
\Delta
can be arranged in the last one for an ascending order for
\mathfrak{m}
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{m}-\Delta
is not minimal to
D_{\Delta}(\pi)
. Let
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}-\Delta
. By Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
8.4
, it suffices to show that for any pair
\widetilde{\Delta}<\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}
of consecutive segments,

\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}:=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}-\left\{\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\right\}+\widetilde{\Delta}\cup\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}+\widetilde{\Delta}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}

does not give the same derivative on
D_{\Delta}(\pi)
i.e.

(*)\quad D_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}\circ D_{\Delta}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}\circ D_{\Delta}(\pi).

Now we arrange and relabel the segments in
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
as in Lemma
8.4
:

\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{k},\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime},\Delta_{k+3},\ldots,\Delta_{r},

which is in an ascending order. By Proposition
6.3
, in order to show (*), it suffices to show that
\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{k},\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\right\}
is still minimal to
D_{\Delta}(\pi)
. Let

\tau=D_{\Delta_{k}}\circ\ldots\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi),\mbox{ and }\tau^{\prime}=D_{\Delta_{k}}\circ\ldots D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta}(\pi).

By Lemma
6.2
and Proposition
6.1
, it suffices to prove

(9.4)

\displaystyle D_{\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}}\circ D_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\tau^{\prime})\not\cong D_{\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\cup\widetilde{\Delta}}\circ D_{\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}}(\tau^{\prime}).

Let
\mathfrak{p}=\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{k}\right\}
. To this end, by the subsequent property (Theorem
8.6
), we have that
\mathfrak{p}+\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}+\widetilde{\Delta}+\Delta
is minimal to
\pi
. Thus, we also have that
\left\{\Delta,\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime},\widetilde{\Delta}\right\}
is minimal to
\tau=D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)
by Proposition
6.3
. Now, Lemma
7.7
implies:

D_{\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}}\circ D_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\circ D_{\Delta}(\tau)\not\cong D_{\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\cup\widetilde{\Delta}}\circ D_{\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}}\circ D_{\Delta}(\tau).

On the other hand, by the subsequent property (Theorem
8.6
), it gives that
\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{k},\Delta\right\}
is minimal to
\pi
. Then combining with Lemma
9.1
, we have
D_{\Delta}(\tau)\cong\tau^{\prime}
. Combining, we have the desired non-isomorphism (
9.4
).
∎

9.2.
Commutativity and minimality (one segment case)

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
be minimal to
\pi
. Let
\Delta\in\mathfrak{n}
. Then
\mathfrak{n}-\Delta
is minimal to
D_{\Delta}(\pi)
and

D_{\mathfrak{n}-\Delta}\circ D_{\Delta}(\pi)\cong D_{\mathfrak{n}}(\pi).

Proof.

We first prove the second assertion. We write the segments in
\mathfrak{n}
in an ascending order:

\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{k},\Delta,\Delta_{k+1},\ldots,\Delta_{r}

with
b(\Delta_{1})\leq\ldots\leq b(\Delta)\leq\ldots\leq b(\Delta_{r})
. Then, by Proposition
6.3
,
\left\{\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{k},\Delta\right\}
is still minimal to
\mathfrak{n}
. Then, the second assertion follows from Lemma
9.2
.

We now prove the first assertion. By Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
8.4
, it suffices to consider for a consecutive pair of segments. Let
\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}
be a pair of consecutive segments in
\mathfrak{n}-\Delta
. Let

\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{n}-\Delta-\left\{\widetilde{\Delta},\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}\right\}+\widetilde{\Delta}\cup\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}+\widetilde{\Delta}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}.

We are going to show

D_{\mathfrak{n}-\Delta}\circ D_{\Delta}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}\circ D_{\Delta}(\pi).

We consider the following two cases:

(1)

\widetilde{\Delta}
and
\widetilde{\Delta}^{\prime}
still form a pair of consecutive segments in
\mathfrak{n}

11.1.
Minimality for two segments

Proposition 11.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
be a pair of linked segments with
\Delta_{1}<\Delta_{2}
. Suppose

D_{\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}}(\pi)\not\cong D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi).

Then the unique non-zero map

D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\boxtimes(\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1})\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{2}))\rightarrow\pi_{N},

where
N=N_{l_{abs}(\Delta_{1})+l_{abs}(\Delta_{2})}
, is injective.

Proof.

Suppose the map is not injective. Since
D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\boxtimes(\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1})\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{2}))
is indecomposable and has length
2
, the image of the map can only be isomorphic to
D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\boxtimes(\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2})\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}))
. This implies that

D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\boxtimes(\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2})\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}))

is a submodule of
\pi_{N}
. Then, applying Frobenius reciprocity, we have that
\pi
is the unique submodule of
D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2})
(see
[
LM16
,
Ch24
]
).

Recall that
\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2})\cong\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2})\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2})
. Let
\tau
be the unique submodule of
D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2})
so that

(*)\quad D_{\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}}(\tau)\cong D_{\Delta_{2}}\circ D_{\Delta_{1}}(\pi).

Then the uniqueness of submodule above also forces that

\pi\hookrightarrow\tau\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2})

and so

. Combinbing with (*), we have:

giving a contradiction.

\sigma
of

, let
I_{\sigma}(\pi)
be the unique submodule of

(see

, also see
[

,

14.1.
Non-isomorphic integrals

For two segments
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
in
\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
,

(1)

Suppose
\Delta_{1}<\Delta_{2}
. Define
\mathrm{St}(\left\{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\right\})
to be the unique irreducible quotient of
\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{2})\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1})
.

(2)

Suppose
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
are not linked. Define
\mathrm{St}(\left\{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\right\})
to be the irreducible module
\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1})\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{2})
.

Proposition 14.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
be two linked segments. Let
\Delta_{1}^{\prime}=\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}
and let
\Delta_{2}^{\prime}=\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}
(possibly the empty set). Let
\sigma=\mathrm{St}(\left\{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\right\})
and let
\sigma^{\prime}=\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1}^{\prime}+\Delta_{2}^{\prime})
. Then
I_{\sigma}(\pi)\not\cong I_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\pi)
.

Proof.

We shall use the invariant
\mathfrak{mx}(.,\Delta_{1})
to distinguish the two representations. Write
\Delta_{1}=[a_{1},b_{1}]_{\rho}
and
\Delta_{2}=[a_{2},b_{2}]_{\rho}
. Switching labels if necessary, we may and shall assume
a_{1}<a_{2}
. We shall prove by an induction on the sum
l_{abs}(\Delta_{1})+l_{abs}(\Delta_{2})
. When the sum is
2
, the argument is similar to the cases below and we omit the details.

Case 1:

a_{1}\neq a_{2}-1
. Let
k=\varepsilon_{[a_{1}]_{\rho}}(\sigma)
. We have that
\varepsilon_{[a_{1}]_{\rho}}(I_{\sigma}(\pi)),\varepsilon_{[a_{2}]_{\rho}}(I_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\pi))=\varepsilon_{[a_{1}]_{\rho}}(\pi)+1
. Let
\kappa=\mathrm{St}(\left\{{}^{-}\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\right\})
and let
\kappa^{\prime}=\mathrm{St}(\left\{{}^{-}\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\Delta_{2}^{\prime}\right\}
. Furthermore,

D_{[a_{1}]_{\rho}}^{k+1}(I_{\sigma}(\pi))=I_{\kappa}(D_{[a_{1}]_{\rho}}^{k}(\pi)),\quad D_{[a_{1}]_{\rho}}^{k+1}(I_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\pi))=I_{\kappa^{\prime}}(D_{[a_{1}]_{\rho}}^{k}(\pi)).

Then, by induction, we have that
I_{\kappa}(D_{[a_{1}]_{\rho}}^{k}(\pi))\not\cong I_{\kappa^{\prime}}(D_{[a_{1}]_{\rho}}^{k}(\pi))
as desired.

Case 2:

a_{1}=a_{2}-1
. In this case, let
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{mx}(\pi,\Delta_{1})
. Then

\pi\hookrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})

Now let
\widetilde{\sigma}=D_{\Delta_{2}^{\prime}}(\sigma)
. We have that

\displaystyle I_{\sigma}(\pi)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\pi\times\sigma

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})\times\sigma

\displaystyle\cong D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\times\sigma\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\times\widetilde{\sigma}\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{2}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{m}),

where the isomorphism in the third line follows from
\mathrm{St}(\Delta^{\prime})\times\sigma\cong\sigma\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta^{\prime})
for any
\Delta_{1}
-saturated segment
\Delta^{\prime}
(see e.g.
[
MW86
, Lemme II 10.1]
).

Since
\mathfrak{mx}(D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi),\Delta_{1})=\emptyset
and
\mathfrak{mx}(\widetilde{\sigma},\Delta_{1})=\emptyset
, we have that

\mathfrak{mx}(I_{\sigma}(\pi),\Delta_{1})=\mathfrak{m}+\Delta_{2}^{\prime}

The last equality follows from an application on the geometric lemma (see details from the proof of
[
Ch24
, Proposition 11.1]
).

Similarly, let
\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}=D_{\Delta_{1}}\circ D_{\Delta_{2}^{\prime}}(\sigma^{\prime})
. We also have that:

\displaystyle I_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\pi)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\pi\times\sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})\times\sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle\cong D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\times\sigma^{\prime}\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\times\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{1})\times\mathrm{St}(\Delta_{2}^{\prime})\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\times\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{m}+\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}^{\prime})

Again,
\mathfrak{mx}(D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi),\Delta_{1})=\emptyset
and
\mathfrak{mx}(\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime},\Delta_{1})=\emptyset
. Thus,

\mathfrak{mx}(I_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\pi),\Delta_{1})=\mathfrak{m}+\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}^{\prime}.

Thus, comparing the invariant
\mathfrak{mx}(.,\Delta_{1})
, we have
I_{\sigma}(\pi)\not\cong I_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\pi)
.
∎

\Delta^{\prime}
in

\eta_{\Delta}(D_{\mathfrak{n}-\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}}(\pi))=0

\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}

\Delta

\mathfrak{n}
as follows:

\pi\hookrightarrow(D_{\mathfrak{q}_{r}}\circ\dots\circ D_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}})(\pi)\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\times\ldots\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{q}_{1}).

Let
l=l_{abs}(\mathfrak{n})
. One may expect there is a natural map:

D_{\mathfrak{n}}(\pi)\boxtimes\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{n})\rightarrow(D_{\mathfrak{q}_{r}}\circ\dots\circ D_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}})(\pi)\dot{\times}^{1}(\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\times\ldots\times\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{q}_{1}))_{N_{l}},

where
\dot{\times}^{1}
is a parabolic induction from a
G_{n_{1}}\times G_{n_{2}}\times G_{l}
-representation to a
G_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\times G_{l}
representation. Here
n_{1}=n-l_{abs}(\mathfrak{q}_{1})-\ldots-l_{abs}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})
and
n_{2}=l_{abs}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})+\ldots+l_{abs}(\mathfrak{p}_{r})
.

15.3.
An inductive construction of simple quotients of Bernstein-Zelevinsky derivatives

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\Delta\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
and let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{mx}(\pi,\Delta)
. Note that
D_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)
is a simple quotient of the
l_{abs}(\mathfrak{n}+\mathfrak{p})
-th Bernstein-Zelevinsky derivative of
\mathrm{St}(\mathfrak{p})\times D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)
if
\eta_{\Delta}(D_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi))=0
. Then one may ask if
D_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)
is also a simple quotient of the
l_{abs}(\mathfrak{n}+\mathfrak{p})
-th Bernstein-Zelevinsky derivative of
\pi
. In a special case, we have the following criteria using commutativity:

Proposition 15.2
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\rho}
. Let
\Delta\in\mathrm{Seg}_{\rho}
and let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{mx}(\pi,\Delta)
.

(1)

Suppose
\mathfrak{n}
is minimal to
D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)
and
\eta_{\Delta}(D_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi))=0
. Suppose further that for any segment
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{n}
,
b(\Delta^{\prime})<b(\Delta)
. If
D_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)\cong D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)
for some
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
, then
\mathfrak{n}+\mathfrak{p}
is minimal to
\pi
.

(2)

Suppose
\mathfrak{m}
is minimal to
\pi
and
\eta_{\Delta}(D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi))=0
. Suppose further that for any segment
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{m}
,
b(\Delta^{\prime})\leq b(\Delta)
. Then
D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)\cong D_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)
and
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}+\mathfrak{p}
for some multisegment
\mathfrak{n}
minimal to
D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)
.

Proof.

We first consider (1). Suppose
D_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)\cong D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)
for some
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Mult}_{\rho}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathfrak{m}
is minimal to
\pi
. Then, by Corollary
15.1
,
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
. By the subsequent property and commutativty property (Theorem
9.4
),
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is also minimal to
D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)
and
D_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}\circ D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)\cong D_{\mathfrak{m}}(\pi)
. By Theorem
1.2
, we then have that
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
and this implies (1).

We now consider (2). By Corollary
15.1
,
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{m}
and so
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{n}
for some multisegment
\mathfrak{n}
. By the subsequent property and commutativity property (Theorem
9.4
), we also have that
\mathfrak{n}
is minimal to
D_{\mathfrak{p}}(\pi)
.
∎

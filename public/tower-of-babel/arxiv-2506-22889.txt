Separating polynomial invariants over non-closed fields of finite abelian groups
Mátyás Domokos

Abstract
. It is proved that for any finite dimensional representation of a prime order group over the field of rational numbers, polynomial invariants of degree at most 3 3 separate the orbits. A result providing an upper degree bound for separating invariants for representations of finite abelian groups over algebraically closed base fields of non-modular characteristic is generalized for the case of base fields that are not algebraically closed (like the fields of real or rational numbers).

1.
Introduction

This work is largely motivated by
[
1
]
,
where the authors develop an approach to multireference alignment using invariant theory.
Multireference alignment is a basic problem in applied mathematics, which arose in connection with structural biology, image recognition, and signal processing
(see e.g. the introduction of
[
2
]
).
It was shown in
[
23
]
that the sample complexity of multireference alignment is related to the orbit recovery problem for the cyclic group
\mathrm{C}_{n}

acting by cyclic shifts on the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In particular, in
[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
a Galois theoretic proof is given for the fact that for the regular representation
over
\mathbb{R}
of a finite abelian group,
the field of rational invariants is generated by polynomial invariants of degree at most
3

(and hence generic orbits can be separated by polynomial invariants of degree at most
3
). This is in fact a special case of a theorem on the regular representation of an arbitrary
finite group (see
[
1
, Remark 4.3]
,

[
16
]
; a generalization is given in
[
3
]
),
but here first we shall focus on the special case of cyclic groups of prime order
p
.
It is proved in
[
4
, Proposition 3.13]

that for a faithful finite dimensional real representation of
\mathrm{C}_{p}
,
polynomial invariants of degree smaller than
p
are not sufficient to separate the orbits.
In sharp contrast with that result, our Theorem
4.1
says that different orbits in
\mathbb{Q}^{p}
under the regular representation of
\mathrm{C}_{p}
can always be separated by invariants of degree at most
3
. As a consequence, the same conclusion holds for any finite dimensional representation of
\mathrm{C}_{p}
over the field
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers. As far as we know, this is the first example in the literature of a finite group whose separating Noether number over an infinite field
\mathbb{F}

is not equal to its separating Noether number over the algebraic closure
of
\mathbb{F}
(see Section
2
for the definition of the separating Noether number).
Also our Theorem
4.1
gives the first example of a field
\mathbb{F}
and an infinite set of pairwise non-isomorphic groups
such that the separating Noether numbers
over
\mathbb{F}
of the groups in the given set stay below a uniform upper bound.
Note that a similar scenario does not occur for the ordinary Noether number (defined in terms of generating invariants, as opposed to separating invariants),
because
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
(and
[
24
]
in the modular case) imply that for any field
\mathbb{F}
, any infinite set of pairwise non-isomorphic finite groups contains an element
with arbitrarily large Noether number.
The study of separating invariants over the field of rational numbers may have
interest from the point of view of applications, because the numerical data in applications are rational (have finite decimal representation), so the orbit recovery problem over
\mathbb{Q}
has relevance.

We provide a theoretical explanation for the phenomenon revealed by Theorem
4.1
by placing it into a more general context.
In fact a second motivation for this paper is that while degree bounds for separating invariants of finite abelian groups over the
complex field are relatively well understood (see
[
12
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
),
only sporadic information is available about the exact degree bounds for real representations of these groups. It is desirable to fill in this gap in the
literature, because several applications require to work over the real field (see for example the problems mentioned in the
introduction of
[
6
]
, or see
[
7
]
).
Recall that every representation of a finite abelian group over an algebraically closed base field of non-modular characteristic
decomposes as a direct sum of
1
-dimensional representations, and therefore the corresponding algebra of polynomial invariants
is generated by monomials. Separating sets of monomials were characterized in
[
12
]
(see
[
13
]
and

[
15
]
for similar results on not necessarily finite diagonalizable group actions).
Here in Theorem
5.4
we work out the generalization of this result appropriate for representations of finite abelian groups over non-algebraically closed base fields, where we can not diagonalize a representation. Theorem
5.4
clarifies the reason why the degree bound for separating invariants
is smaller in general, than the corresponding degree bound when we replace the base field by its algebraic closure.

We begin in Section
2
by recalling some basic concepts and notation of invariant theory.
In Section
3
we deduce from an observation on the block monoid Proposition
3.2
,
which is a variant of

[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
about generically
separating invariants on the complex regular representation of a finite abelian group.
This is then used to prove Theorem
4.1
on the regular representation of
\mathrm{C}_{p}
over the field
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers.
We conclude Section
4
by Proposition
4.4
and Proposition
4.5
, showing that
Theorem
4.1
does not generalize for cyclic groups of composite order, or for non-abelian groups.
In Section
5
we first recall in Proposition
5.1
the classification of irreducible representations of a finite abelian group
G

over a not necessarily algebraically closed base field of non-modular characteristic. We state and prove Theorem
5.4
giving a sufficient
condition for a set of monomials (defined over the algebraic closure of the base field
\mathbb{F}
) to separate the orbits in the regular representation of
G
over
\mathbb{F}
,
and deduce in Corollary
5.6
an improved bound on the separating Noether number of
G
over
\mathbb{F}
.
This improvement is illustrated by a concrete example in Proposition
5.8
and Section
6
.

2.
Preliminaries in invariant theory

Let us recall some standard notation of invariant theory.
Throughout this paper
G
will be a finite group, and
\mathbb{F}
a field whose characteristic does not divide the order of
G
.
Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}
, endowed with a representation of
G
on
V
; that is, we are given a
homomorphism
\rho
from
G
into the group
\mathrm{GL}(V)
of invertible linear transformations of
V
.
Sometimes we shall refer to a representation as the pair
(V,\rho)
, or we say that
V
is an
\mathbb{F}G
-module.
Write
\mathbb{F}[V]
for the coordinate ring of
V
. This is a polynomial algebra in
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
commuting variables
endowed with the
standard grading (where the variables have degree
1
).
The variables are thought of as the coordinate functions on
V
with respect to a chosen basis,
and the elements of
\mathbb{F}[V]
represent
\mathbb{F}
-valued polynomial functions on
V
(when
\mathbb{F}
is infinite,

\mathbb{F}[V]
can be identified with the
algebra of
\mathbb{F}
-valued polynomial functions on
V
).
We write
\deg(f)
for the degree
of a non-zero element
f
of
\mathbb{F}[V]
.
The action of
G
on
V
induces an action of
G
on
\mathbb{F}[V]

via graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebra automorphisms
(for
f\in\mathbb{F}]V]
,
g\in G
,
v\in V
we have
(g\cdot f)(v)=f(g^{-1}v)
),
and we denote by
\mathbb{F}[V]^{G}
the subalgebra of polynomial

G
-invariants on
V
(the polynomials fixed by each group element).
This is a graded subalgebra of
\mathbb{F}[V]
.
For
v\in V
we write
G\cdot v\subset V
for the
G
-orbit of
v
.
It is immediate from the definition that
G\cdot v=G\cdot w
implies
f(v)=f(w)
for all

f\in\mathbb{F}[V]^{G}
. The converse also holds (see
[
10
, Theorem 3.12.1]
,

[
19
, Theorem 16]
or
[
14
, Lemma 3.1]
):

(1)

G\cdot v\neq G\cdot w\iff\exists f\in\mathbb{F}[V]^{G}\colon f(v)\neq f(w).

The algebra
\mathbb{F}[V]^{G}
is generated by invariants of degree at most
|G|

(see
[
22
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
).
We shall denote by
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)
the minimal positive integer
d
such that
for any
v,w\in V
with
G\cdot v\neq G\cdot w
, there exists an
f\in\mathbb{F}[V]^{G}
of degree
at most
d
with
f(v)\neq f(w)
.
Furthermore, we write
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{F}}(G)
for the maximum of
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,V)
, where

V
ranges over the finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}G
-modules. We call
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{F}}(G)
the

separating Noether number of
G
over
\mathbb{F}
;
its study was initiated in
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
.
By the facts mentioned above, we have

(2)

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{F}}(G)\leq\beta^{\mathbb{F}}(G)\leq|G|,

where
\beta^{\mathbb{F}}(G)
is the maximum of
\beta(G,V)
, as

V
ranges over the finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}G
-modules, and

\beta(G,V)
is the maximal degree of an element in a minimal homogeneous generating system
of
\mathbb{F}[V]^{G}
. The study of the
Noether number

\beta^{\mathbb{F}}(G)
began in
[
29
]
, and much of the presently known results on its exact value
are summarized in
[
9
]
.

3.
An observation on the block monoid of a finite abelian group

Let
G
be a finite abelian group, written multiplicatively.
Denote by
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{N}_{0})
the set of non-negative integer valued functions on
G
. This is a monoid, with pointwise addition of functions.
It is a submonoid of the abelian group
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})
of integer valued functions on
G
, with pointwise addition of functions.
Clearly
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is a free abelian group of rank
|G|
.
For an element
s\in\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})
we call

|s|:=\sum_{g\in G}|s(g)|

the
length of
s
.
The element
s
of
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{N}_{0})
can be thought of as a sequence over
G
of length
|s|
,
where the order of the elements in the sequence is disregarded, and
s(g)
is
the number of occurrences of
g
in the sequence
s
.
The submonoid

\mathcal{B}(G):=\{s\in\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{N}_{0})\mid\prod_{g\in G}g^{s(g)}=1_{G}\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{N}_{0})

is called the
monoid of product-one sequences over
G
(it is also called
the
block monoid of
G
, influenced by the terminology of
[
21
]
).
The abelian group
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is freely generated by
\delta_{g}
,
g\in G
, where

(3)

\delta_{g}(x)=\begin{cases}1\text{ if }x=g;\\
0\text{ if }x\neq g.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.1
.

The block monoid
\mathcal{B}(G)
is contained in the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})

generated by

(4)

S:=\{\delta_{1_{G}},\quad\delta_{g}+\delta_{g^{-1}},\quad\delta_{g}+\delta_{h}+\delta_{(gh)^{-1}}\mid g,h\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}\}.

Proof.

Denote by
A
the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule in
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})
given as

A:=\{s\in\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})\mid\prod_{g\in G}g^{s(g)}=1_{G}\}
.
Clearly
A
contains
\mathcal{B}(G)
, therefore it is sufficient to show that

A
is contained in
\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
, the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})
generated by
S
. Take any
s\in A
. We prove by induction on
|s|
that
s
belongs to

\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
|s|=0
, then
s
is the zero element of
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})
, so it belongs to all
\mathbb{Z}
-submodules of
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})
. Assume next that
|s|>0
, and all
s^{\prime}\in A

with
|s^{\prime}|<|s|
belong to
\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
If
|s|=1
then necessarily
s=\delta_{1_{G}}
or

-\delta_{1_{G}}
, so
s
belongs to
\langle\delta_{1_{G}}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Moreover, if
s(1_{G})\neq 0
, then replacing
s
by
-s
if necessary,
we may assume that
s(1_{G})>0
, hence for
s^{\prime}:=s-\delta_{1_{G}}\in A
we have
|s^{\prime}|=|s|-1
. By the induction
hypothesis
s^{\prime}\in\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
, implying in turn that

s=s^{\prime}+\delta_{1_{G}}\in\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
From now on we assume
that
s(1_{G})=0
. If
|s|=2
, then
s\in A
implies
s=\pm(\delta_{g}+\delta_{g^{-1}})
, hence

s\in\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
by definition of
S
.
Assume finally that
|s|\geq 3
. Then
\sum_{s(g)>0}s(g)\geq 2
or

\sum_{s(g)<0}s(g)\leq-2
. Replacing
s
by
-s
if necessary we may assume
that the first option holds, so there exist elements
g,h\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
such that
for
s^{\prime}:=s-(\delta_{g}+\delta_{h})
we have
|s^{\prime}|=|s|-2
.
Set
s^{\prime\prime}:=s-(\delta_{g}+\delta_{h}+\delta_{(gh)^{-1}})
. Then
s^{\prime\prime}\in A
and
|s^{\prime\prime}|\leq|s^{\prime}|+1=|s|-1
,
hence
s^{\prime\prime}\in\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
by the induction hypothesis, implying in turn that

s=s^{\prime\prime}+(\delta_{g}+\delta_{h}+\delta_{(gh)^{-1}})\in\langle S\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
∎

Let
K
be an extension of
\mathbb{F}
containing an element of multiplicative order
\mathrm{exp}(G)
,
where
\mathrm{exp}(G)
is the exponent of
G
(the least common multiple of the orders of the elements of
G
).
For example, we may take for
K
the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}
.
Denote by
\widehat{G}
the group of characters of
G
. That is,

\widehat{G}
is the set of group homomorphisms from
G
into the multiplicative
group of
K
, endowed with pointwise multiplication.
Then
\widehat{G}
is isomorphic to
G
.

Take a polynomial algebra
K[x_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}]

whose variables are labeled by the elements of
\widehat{G}
.
The group
G
acts on
K[x_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}]
via
K
-algebra automorphisms, such that for each
\chi\in\widehat{G}
and
g\in G
we have

g\cdot x_{\chi}=\chi(g)^{-1}x.

It follows that each monomial spans a
G
-invariant subspace in the polynomial ring,
and for
b\in\mathcal{F}(\widehat{G},\mathbb{N}_{0})
, the monomial

x^{b}:=\prod_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}x_{\chi}^{b(\chi)}
is
G
-invariant if and only if
b\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
.
Consequently, we have the well known equality

(5)

K[x_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}]^{G}=\mathrm{Span}_{K}\{x^{b}\mid b\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})\}.

Consider the monomials associated to
S
(defined in (
4
)):

(6)

T:=\{x^{s}\mid s\in S\}=\{x_{1_{\widehat{G}}},\quad x_{\chi}x_{\chi^{-1}},\quad x_{\chi}x_{\rho}x_{(\chi\rho)^{-1}}\mid\chi,\rho\in\widehat{G}\setminus\{1_{\widehat{G}}\}\}.

Obviously, the elements of
T
are
G
-invariant.

The elements of
K[x_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}]
represent
K
-valued polynomial functions on the
|\widehat{G}|
-dimensional
K
-vector space

V

with basis
\{e_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}\}
, namely for

v=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}v_{\chi}e_{\chi}
and
x_{\rho}
we have

x_{\rho}(v)=v_{\rho}
.
The action of
G
on
K[x_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}]
is in fact induced by the following
representation of
G
on
V
:

\text{for }g\in G,\quad v=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}v_{\chi}e_{\chi}\in V\quad\text{ we have }g\cdot v=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}\chi(g)v_{\chi}e_{\chi}.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
v,w
be vectors in
V
such that

v_{\chi}\neq 0
and
w_{\chi}\neq 0
for all
\chi\in\widehat{G}\setminus\{1_{\widehat{G}}\}
.
Then the
G
-orbit of
v
coincides with the
G
-orbit of
w
if and only if

f(v)=f(w)
for all
f\in T
(defined in (
6
)).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, for any
b\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
,
there exist
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\in T
,
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\in T\setminus\{x_{1_{\widehat{G}}}\}
such that

x^{b}=t_{1}\cdots t_{k}u_{1}^{-1}\cdots u_{m}^{-1}
(this equality takes place in the
Laurent polynomial ring
K[x_{\chi}^{\pm 1}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}]
).
The variable
x_{1_{\widehat{G}}}
does not appear in the elements of
T\setminus\{x_{1_{\widehat{G}}}\}
,
therefore none of
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
involves the variable
x_{1_{\widehat{G}}}
,
so
u_{j}(z)\neq 0
for
z\in\{v,w\}
and
j=1,\dots,m
.
Consequently, for
z\in\{v,w\}
we have

x^{b}(z)=t_{1}(z)\cdots t_{k}(z)u_{1}(z)^{-1}\cdots u_{m}(z)^{-1}
.
It follows that
f(v)=f(w)
for all
f\in T
if and only if
x^{b}(v)=x^{b}(w)
for all

b\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
.
Thus by (
5
),
f(v)=f(w)
for all
f\in T
if and only if
h(v)=h(w)
for all

h\in K[x_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}]^{G}
.
Since different orbits of a finite group can be separated by polynomial invariants
(see (
1
)),
we conclude that
f(v)=f(w)
for all
f\in T
if and only

G\cdot v=G\cdot w
.
∎

4.
Prime order cyclic groups over the rationals

Any finite group
G
acts on itself via left multiplication. This induces a linear action on the

\mathbb{F}
-vector space
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
of
\mathbb{F}
-valued functions on
G
:
for
f\in\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
,
g,h\in G
we have
(g\cdot f)(h)=f(g^{-1}h)
.
This representation
is called the
regular representation of
G
over
\mathbb{F}
.
More concretely, the elements
\delta_{g}
(
g\in G
) defined in (
3
)
form a
\mathbb{F}
-vector space basis in
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
, and for
h\in G
we have

h\cdot\delta_{g}=\delta_{hg}
.

When the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
does not divide
|G|
, all irreducible representations
of
G
over
\mathbb{F}
occur as a direct summand in
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
. Moreover, when

\mathbb{F}
is also algebraically closed, the multiplicity
of each irreducible representation of
G
over

\mathbb{F}
equals the dimension of the representation.

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider the regular representation on
V:=\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Q})
of the cyclic group
G:=\mathrm{C}_{p}
of prime order
p

over the field
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers.
Then for any
v,w\in V
with
G\cdot v\neq G\cdot w
there exists an

f\in\mathbb{Q}[V]^{G}
with
\deg(f)\leq 3
and
f(v)\neq f(w)
.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a primitive
p
th root of
1
, and
K:=\mathbb{Q}(\omega)
the
p
th cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
For a character
\chi\in\widehat{G}
set

e_{\chi}:=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)^{-1}\delta_{g},

where
\{\delta_{g}\mid g\in G\}
is the basis of
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Q})
defined in (
3
).
Then
\{e_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}\}
is a
K
-vector space basis of
V_{K}:=K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}V
,
satisfying

g\cdot e_{\chi}=\chi(g)e_{\chi}\quad\text{ for all }g\in G,\quad\chi\in\widehat{G}.

Note that
\chi(g)^{-1}
is the complex conjugate
of
\chi(g)
, and therefore the second orthogonality relation of characters
(cf.
[
30
, Chapter 2, Proposition 7]
) implies that

(7)

\delta_{g}=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}\chi(g)e_{\chi}\quad\text{ for }g\in G.

Now assume that for
v,w\in V
,
f(v)=f(w)
for all
f\in\mathbb{Q}[V]^{G}
with

\deg(f)\leq 3
. We claim that
G\cdot v=G\cdot w
.
Since the
G
-action on
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}V
was obtained
from the representation of
G
on
V
by extending scalars, each homogeneous component
of
K[K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}V]^{G}
is spanned as a
K
-vector space
by the corresponding homogeneous component of
\mathbb{Q}[V]^{G}
.
Therefore we have that
h(v)=h(w)
for all
h\in K[K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}V]^{G}
with

\deg(h)\leq 3
. In particular,

(8)

h(v)=h(w)\quad\text{ for all }\quad h\in T,

where
T
is defined in
(
6
), and
K[K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}V]=K[x_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}]
,
and
\{x_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}\}
is the basis dual to the basis

\{e_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}\}
in
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}V
.

By (
7
), the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector subspace
V=\{\sum_{g\in G}v_{g}\delta_{g}\mid v_{g}\in\mathbb{Q}\}
of

K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}V
is

V=\{\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}(\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)v_{g})e_{\chi}\mid v_{g}\in\mathbb{Q}\}.

Note that for
\chi\in\widehat{G}\setminus\{1_{\widehat{G}}\}
we have

\{\chi(g)\mid g\in G\}=\{1,\omega,\omega^{2},\dots,\omega^{p-1}\}.

The minimal polynomial of
\omega
over
\mathbb{Q}
is
1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{p-1}
.
Therefore
\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)z_{g}=0
for some

z=\sum_{g\in G}z_{g}\delta_{g}\in V
and
\chi\in\widehat{G}\setminus\{1_{\widehat{G}}\}
if and only if
z=c\sum_{g\in G}\delta_{g}
for some
c\in\mathbb{Q}
,
or in other words,
z_{g}=c\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
g\in G
. Moreover, in this case
z=cpe_{1_{\widehat{G}}}
.
So

V\subset\mathbb{Q}e_{1_{\widehat{G}}}\bigcup\{\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}c_{\chi}e_{\chi}\mid c_{\chi}\in K,\ c_{\chi}\neq 0\text{ for }\chi\neq 1_{\widehat{G}}\}.

If both
v
and
w
belong to
\mathbb{Q}\sum_{g\in G}\delta_{g}
, then

v=c_{1}\sum_{g\in G}\delta_{g}=c_{1}pe_{1_{\widehat{G}}}
,
w=c_{2}\sum_{g\in G}\delta_{g}=c_{2}pe_{1_{\widehat{G}}}
,
hence by (
8
) we have
c_{1}p=x_{1_{\widehat{G}}}(v)=x_{1_{\widehat{G}}}(w)=c_{2}p
,
implying that
c_{1}=c_{2}
, and therefore
v=w
.

If at least one of
v,w
, say
v
does not belong to
\mathbb{Q}\sum_{g\in G}\delta_{g}
, then take any
\chi\notin\widehat{G}\setminus\{1_{\widehat{G}}\}
.
We have that
(x_{\chi}x_{\chi^{-1}})(v)\neq 0
, whence by (
8
) we get

(x_{\chi}x_{\chi^{-1}})(w)\neq 0
, implying in turn that

w
does not belong to
\mathbb{Q}\sum_{g\in G}\delta_{g}
.

So it remains to deal with the case when
none of
v
and
w
belong to
\mathbb{Q}\sum_{g\in G}\delta_{g}
. Then their coordinates
with respect to the basis
\{e_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}\}
are all non-zero, except possibly the coordinate
corresponding to
e_{1_{\widehat{G}}}
. By (
8
) and
Proposition
3.2
we conclude that
G\cdot v=G\cdot w
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

For the cyclic group
\mathrm{C}_{p}
of prime order
p
we have the equality

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{C}_{p})=\begin{cases}3&\quad\text{ for }p\geq 3;\\
2&\quad\text{ for }p=2.\end{cases}

Proof.

For
p=2
we have
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{C}_{2})\leq 2
by (
2
), and
the regular representation of
\mathrm{C}_{2}
shows that we have equality here.
For
p>2
we know that
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathbb{Q}))\leq 3

by Theorem
4.1
. Moreover, any finite dimensional representation
of
\mathrm{C}_{p}
over
\mathbb{Q}
is a direct summand of

\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathbb{Q})^{\oplus m}
, the direct sum of
m
copies of the
regular representation for some
m
, hence

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{C}_{p})\leq\max_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\{\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathbb{Q})^{\oplus m})\}
.
The latter number equals
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathbb{Q})^{\oplus 2})

by
[
11
, Proposition 4.3 and 4.4]
. Moreover,

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathbb{Q})^{\oplus 2})=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathbb{Q}))
by
[
14
, Theorem 3.4]
.
So we proved the inequality
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{C}_{p})\leq 3
.

To prove the reverse inequality for
p\geq 3
, consider the regular representation

\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathbb{Q})
. Denote by
g
a generator of
\mathrm{C_{p}}
.
Consider the element
v:=\delta_{1_{G}}-\delta_{g}
and its negative
-v
.
Denote by
\{x_{h}\mid h\in G\}
the basis dual to the basis

\{\delta_{h}\mid h\in G\}
in
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{C}_{p},\mathbb{Q})
.
Then
\mathbb{Q}[V]
is the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{Q}[x_{h}\mid h\in G]
, and

g\cdot x_{h}=x_{gh}
.
Since
G
acts on
\mathbb{Q}[V]
by permuting the variables,
the homogeneous components of

\mathbb{Q}[V]^{\mathrm{C}_{p}}
are spanned by orbit sums of monomials.
The only degree
1
orbit sum
\sum_{h\in\mathrm{C}_{p}}x_{h}

vanishes on both
v
and
w
.
The orbit sum of
x_{1_{G}}^{2}

takes value 2 on both
v
and
w
, whereas the orbit sum of

x_{1_{G}}x_{g}
takes value
-1
on both
v
and
w
. All the other quadratic orbit sums vanish on both
v
and
-v
. Thus
v
and
-v
can not be separated by invariants of degree at most
2
. However, they have different
\mathrm{C}_{p}
-orbits,
because the orbit sum of
x_{1_{G}}^{2}x_{g}
takes value
-1
on
v
and value
1
on
-v
,
when
p>2
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

For comparison, we mention the well known equality
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{C}_{p})=p
.
Moreover, it is proved in
[
4
, Proposition 3.13]
that
even over the field
\mathbb{R}
of real numbers we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathrm{C}_{p})=p
.

Theorem
4.1
does not extend to all cyclic groups whose order is not prime,
as Proposition

below shows:

Proposition 4.4
.

The points
v:=[3,4,-3.-4]^{T}\in\mathbb{Q}^{4}
and

w:=[5,0,-5,0]^{T}\in\mathbb{Q}^{4}
have different
\mathrm{C_{4}}
-orbit,
where the cyclic group
\mathrm{C}_{4}
acts on
\mathbb{Q}^{4}
by cyclically permuting the coordinates of vectors, and all polynomial
\mathrm{C}_{4}

\mathbb{Q}^{4}
of degree at most

take the same value on

and

.
In particular, we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{C}_{4})=4.

Denote by

the coordinate functions on
\mathbb{Q}^{4}
, so for a generator

of
\mathrm{C}_{4}
we have
g\cdot x_{1}=x_{2}
,
g\cdot x_{2}=x_{3}
,

g\cdot x_{3}=x_{4}
,
g\cdot x_{4}=x_{1}
. The algebra
\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]^{\mathrm{C}_{4}}
of polynomial

\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]^{\mathrm{C}_{4}}

f_{2}:=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}

f_{5}=x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}x_{3}^{2}+x_{3}x_{4}^{2}+x_{4}x_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle[f_{1}(v),f_{2}(v),f_{3}(v),f_{4}(v),f_{5}(v),f_{6}(v),f_{7}(v)]

\displaystyle=[0,50,0,0,0,674,168]

\displaystyle[f_{1}(w),f_{2}(w),f_{3}(w),f_{4}(w),f_{5}(w),f_{6}(w),f_{7}(w)]

\displaystyle=[0,50,0,0,0,1250,0],

.

).
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Invariants of degree at most
3
are not sufficient to separate all the orbits in
the regular representation of
\mathrm{S}_{3}
over the field of rational numbers.
Moreover, we have

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{S}_{3})=4.

The regular representation of

over
\mathbb{Q}
contains as a direct summand

\mathrm{S}_{3}

and the sign representation of
\mathrm{S}_{3}
.

do not separate all the orbits in this

, a permutation

maps

to
x_{g(i)}
, and

,

.

.
The reverse inequality holds by (

(this was shown first in

]
; see

]
for more references).
∎

5.

Throughout this section
G

Let

\mathbb{F}
by an element

whose multiplicative order is

\mathrm{exp}(G)

.

.

of

\mathbb{F}
.

and

for

and

\gamma\cdot\chi
is the character of

mapping

to
\gamma(\chi(g))
, and for

\gamma(f)
is the function on
G
mapping
g\in G
to
\gamma(f(g))
.
The
\mathbb{F}
-subspace
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
of
\mathcal{F}(G,K)
is the fixed point set of
\Gamma
in

\mathcal{F}(G,K)
.
Denote by
\widehat{G}/\Gamma
a chosen set of representatives of the
\Gamma
-orbits in
\widehat{G}
.
The irreducible representations of
G
over
K
are all
1
-dimensional. For

\chi\in\widehat{G}
denote by
(K,\chi)
the
1
-dimensional
K
-vector space
K
on which

G
acts via the character
\chi
: for
g\in G
and
\lambda\in K
we have

g\cdot\lambda=\chi(g)\lambda
. The irreducible representations of
G
over
\mathbb{F}
are in a natural bijection with

\widehat{G}/\Gamma
, as follows.

For each
\chi\in\widehat{G}/\Gamma
we construct a representation of
G
over
\mathbb{F}
.
The image
\chi(G)
of
G
under
\chi
is a subgroup of the multiplicative group

K^{\times}
of
K
, hence is cyclic. Its order
d_{\chi}
is a divisor of
\mathrm{exp}(G)
.
We
choose an element
g_{\chi}\in G
such that
\omega_{\chi}:=\chi(g_{\chi})
generates the subgroup
\chi(G)

of

(i.e. the multiplicative order of
\omega_{\chi}
is
d_{\chi}

The set of roots of
the minimal polynomial

of
\omega_{\chi}
over
\mathbb{F}
is the

\Gamma
-orbit of
\omega_{\chi}
, whence
\ell_{\chi}:=\deg(m^{\omega_{\chi}}_{\mathbb{F}})=|\Gamma\cdot\omega_{\chi}|

Let
A_{\chi}\in\mathbb{F}^{\ell_{\chi}\times\ell_{\chi}}
be the companion matrix of
m^{\omega_{\chi}}_{\mathbb{F}}
,
so the characteristic polynomial (and the minimal polynomial) of
A_{\chi}
is
m^{\omega_{\chi}}_{\mathbb{F}}
.
Then
A_{\chi}^{d_{\chi}}
is the
\ell_{\chi}\times\ell_{\chi}
identity matrix, and the set of eigenvalues of

A_{\chi}
is the
\Gamma
-orbit of
\omega_{\chi}
.
Lift to
G
the representation of
G/\ker(\chi)\cong\chi(G)
given by

g_{\chi}^{j}\ker(\chi)\mapsto A_{\chi}^{j}
for
j=0,1,\dots,d-1
.
Denote by
\rho_{\chi}:G\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell_{\chi}})
the corresponding group homomorphism,
so
(\mathbb{F}^{\ell_{\chi}},\rho_{\chi})
is a representation of

over
\mathbb{F}
.
Note that
\ker(\rho_{\chi})=\ker(\chi)
(because
\rho_{\chi}(g_{\chi})=A_{\chi}
has eigenvalues with multiplicative order
d_{\chi}=|G/\ker(\chi)|
).
Moreover, we denote by
(K^{\ell_{\chi}},\rho_{\chi})
the representation
obtained from
(\mathbb{F}^{\ell_{\chi}},\rho_{\chi})
by extending the scalars from
\mathbb{F}
to
K
.
The following statement must be well known, but we do not know a reference for it.

Proposition 5.1
.

(i)

The representation
(K^{\ell_{\chi}},\rho_{\chi})
is isomorphic to

\bigoplus_{\psi\in\Gamma\cdot\chi}(K,\psi)
, where
\Gamma\cdot\chi
is the

\Gamma
-orbit of
\chi
.

(ii)

The representation
(\mathbb{F}^{\ell_{\chi}},\rho_{\chi})
is irreducible for all
\chi\in\widehat{G}/\Gamma
.

(iii)

The regular representation
(\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F}),\rho_{\mathrm{reg}})
of

over
\mathbb{F}

is isomorphic to the direct sum

\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat{G}/\Gamma}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell_{\chi}},\rho_{\chi})

where
\widehat{G}/\Gamma

\Gamma
-orbits in

Proof.

(i): Since

\ell_{\chi}
distinct eigenvalues in

is the
\Gamma
-orbit of

.
Moreover,
\ker(\chi)=\ker(\rho_{\chi})

g_{\chi}
generates
G/\ker(\rho_{\chi})

(Kv_{\omega},\rho_{\chi}|_{Kv_{\omega}})\cong(K,\psi)

\gamma\cdot\chi

.

\Gamma\cdot\omega_{\chi}

.

We have

\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})=\{\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}c_{\psi}f_{\psi}\in\mathcal{F}(G,K)\mid c_{\gamma\psi}=\gamma(c_{\psi})\in K\text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma,\ \psi\in\widehat{G}\}.

Proof.

Recall that
\Gamma
acts on
\mathcal{F}(G,K)
as
(\gamma(f))(g)=\gamma(f(g))
for

\gamma\in\Gamma
,
f\in\mathcal{F}(G,K)
and
g\in G
. This is an action on the
K
-vector space
\mathcal{F}(G,K)

via semilinear transformations. That is,
for
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{F}(G,K)
,
c_{1},c_{2}\in K
, and
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have

\gamma(c_{1}f_{1}+c_{2}f_{2})=\gamma(c_{1})\gamma(f_{1})+\gamma(c_{2})\gamma(f_{2})
.
Therefore we have

\gamma(\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}c_{\psi}f_{\psi})=\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}\gamma(c_{\psi})\gamma(f_{\psi})=\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}\gamma(c_{\psi})f_{\gamma\cdot\psi}

(the second equality above holds by (
12
) in Lemma
5.2
),
implying that

(13)

\gamma(\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}c_{\psi}f_{\psi})=\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}c_{\psi}f_{\psi}\iff\forall\psi\in\widehat{G}:\gamma(c_{\psi})=c_{\gamma\cdot\psi}.

By definition of the
\Gamma
-action, and since
\mathbb{F}
is the fixed subfield of
\Gamma
in
K
,
we have

\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})=\{f\in\mathcal{F}(G,K)\mid\forall\gamma\in\Gamma:\gamma(f)=f\}
.
So our statement follows by (
13
).
∎

For a subset
M
of
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})\subset\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})
and a subset

I\subseteq\widehat{G}
, set

M_{I}:=\{m\in M\mid m(\psi)=0\text{ for all }\psi\in\widehat{G}\setminus I\},

and write
\langle M_{I}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
for the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})

generated by
M_{I}
.
Note that when
M
is a submonoid of
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
, then

M_{I}
is also a submonoid of
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
.
The coordinate ring of
\mathcal{F}(G,K)
is a polynomial algebra

K[x_{\psi}\mid\psi\in\widehat{G}]
, where
\{x_{\psi}\mid\psi\in\widehat{G}\}
is a basis
dual to the basis
\{f_{\psi}\mid\psi\in\widehat{G}\}
in
\mathcal{F}(G,K)
.
In particular, we have that
g\cdot x_{\psi}=\psi(g)^{-1}x_{\psi}
for
g\in G
.
For
m\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
we set

x^{m}:=\prod_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}x_{\psi}^{m(\psi)}
.
As we pointed out in (
5
),
\{x^{m}\mid m\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})\}
is a
K
-vector space basis
of
K[x_{\psi}\mid\psi\in\widehat{G}]^{G}
.
If
m\in M_{I}
, then
x^{m}
belongs to the subalgebra
K[x_{\psi}\mid\psi\in I]
of

K[x_{\psi}\mid\psi\in\widehat{G}]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
M
be a subset of
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
. The polynomial

G
-invariants
\{x^{m}\mid m\in M\}
separate the
G
-orbits in the subspace

\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
of
\mathcal{F}(G,K)
if condition (*) below holds for
M
:

(*)\text{ for all }\Gamma\text{-stable subsets}\ I\text{ of }\widehat{G},\text{ we have }\langle M_{I}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}\supseteq\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}.

To prove Theorem
5.4
we need Lemma
5.5
below.
Recall the direct sum decomposition

\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat{G}/\Gamma}V_{\chi}
from (
10
),
so
v\in\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
can be written as

v=(v_{\chi}\mid\chi\in\widehat{G}/\Gamma)
, where
v_{\chi}\in V_{\chi}
.
Set
\mathrm{supp}(v):=\{\chi\in\widehat{G}/\Gamma\mid v_{\chi}\neq 0\}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

For
v\in\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
,
\psi\in\widehat{G}
, and
m\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
we have

(i)

x_{\psi}(v)\neq 0
if and only if
\psi\in\bigcup_{\chi\in\mathrm{supp}(v)}\Gamma\cdot\chi
.

(ii)

x^{m}(v)\neq 0
if and only if
m\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}
where
I=\bigcup_{\chi\in\mathrm{supp}(v)}\Gamma\cdot\chi
.

Proof.

(i) Recall that
v_{\chi}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma\cdot\chi}x_{\lambda}(v)f_{\lambda}
.
If
x_{\psi}(v)
is non-zero, then
v_{\mu}\neq 0
for the unique
\mu\in\widehat{G}/\Gamma
for which

\psi\in\Gamma\cdot\mu
. Then
\mu\in\mathrm{supp}(v)
, and so
\psi\in\bigcup_{\chi\in\mathrm{supp}(v)}\Gamma\cdot\chi
.
For the reverse implication assume that
\psi\in\Gamma\cdot\chi
for some
\chi\in\mathrm{supp}(v)
. So
v_{\chi}\neq 0

and thus
x_{\lambda}(v)\neq 0
for some
\lambda
from
\Gamma\cdot\chi=\Gamma\cdot\psi
.
Here
\psi,\lambda\in\widehat{G}
belong to the same
\Gamma
-orbit in
\widehat{G}
, hence by Lemma
5.3
,

x_{\lambda}(v)\neq 0
implies
x_{\psi}(v)\neq 0
.

(ii) follows from (i).
∎

Proof of
Theorem
5.4
.

Suppose that
M\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
satisfies condition (*), and

v,w\in\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
with
G\cdot v\neq G\cdot w
.
We have to show that there exists an
m\in M
with
x^{m}(v)\neq x^{m}(w)
.

First we deal with the case when
\mathrm{supp}(v)\neq\mathrm{supp}(w)
.
Then by symmetry we may assume that there exists a
\chi\in\mathrm{supp}(v)\setminus\mathrm{supp}(w)
.
Let
I
be the
\Gamma
-orbit of
\chi
. The element
\mathrm{ord}(\chi)\delta_{\chi}
belongs to

\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}
, therefore by assumption (*) for
M
, there exist
m,m^{\prime}\in M_{I}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}
with

\mathrm{ord}(\chi)\delta_{\chi}=m-m^{\prime}
. It follows that
m(\chi)>0
.
By Lemma
5.5
we have
x^{m}(v)\neq 0
, whereas
x^{m}(w)=0
.
In particular, we found an
m\in M
with
x^{m}(v)\neq x^{m}(w)
.

From now on we assume that
\mathrm{supp}(v)=\mathrm{supp}(w)
.
Since
G\cdot v\neq G\cdot w
, by (
1
) and (
5
) we know that
there exists a
b\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
with
x^{b}(v)\neq x^{b}(w)
.
Set
I:=\bigcup_{\chi\in\mathrm{supp}(v)}\Gamma\cdot\chi=\bigcup_{\chi\in\mathrm{supp}(w)}\Gamma\cdot\chi
.
Then
b\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}

(otherwise by Lemma
5.5
(ii)
we would have
x^{b}(v)=0=x^{b}(w)
, contrary to the choice of
b
).
We can write
b
as
b=m-m^{\prime}
for some
m,m^{\prime}\in M_{I}
by assumption (*) on
M
.
By Lemma
5.5
(ii) we know that none of
x^{m}(v)
,
x^{m^{\prime}}(v)
,
x^{m}(w)
,
x^{m^{\prime}}(w)
is zero.
Consequently,
x^{b}(v)=\frac{x^{m}(v)}{x^{m^{\prime}}(v)}
and
x^{b}(w)=\frac{x^{m}(w)}{x^{m^{\prime}}(w)}
.
Therefore
x^{b}(v)\neq x^{b}(w)
implies that
x^{m}(v)\neq x^{m}(w)
or
x^{m^{\prime}}(v)\neq x^{m^{\prime}}(w)
, and we are done.

\square

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
d
be a positive integer satisfying that
for all
\Gamma
-stable subsets
I
of
\widehat{G}
,
the monoid
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}
is contained in the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of

\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{Z})
generated by

\{m\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}\mid\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}m(\psi)\leq d\}
.
Then we have

(i)

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F}))\leq d
.

(ii)

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{F}}(G)\leq d
.

Proof.

(i) Assume that the positive integer
d
satisfies that for all
\Gamma
-stable subsets
I
of
\widehat{G}
,
the monoid
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}
is contained in the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of

\mathcal{F}(\widehat{G},\mathbb{Z})
generated by

\{m\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}\mid\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}m(\psi)\leq d\}
.
Setting
M:=\{m\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})\mid\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}m(\psi)\leq d\}
we have
that
M
satisfies condition (*) from Theorem
5.4
,
hence the
G
-invariants

\{x^{m}\mid m\in M\}
in
K[\mathcal{F}(G,K)]

separate the
G
-orbits in the
\mathbb{F}
-vector subspace

\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
of
\mathcal{F}(G,K)
.
Note that
\mathrm{Span}_{K}\{x^{m}\mid m\in M\}
is
the union of the homogeneous components of
K[\mathcal{F}(G,K)]^{G}
of degree at most
d
by
(
5
). Recall that for all
j
, the degree
j
homogeneous component of
K[\mathcal{F}(G,K)]^{G}

is spanned as a
K
-vector space by the degree
j
homogeneous component of
\mathbb{F}[\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})]^{G}
.
Since the homogeneous components of
K[\mathcal{F}(G,K)]^{G}
of degree at most
d
separate the
G
-orbits in

\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})
, the same holds for its spanning subset, consisting of the elements of degree at most
d
in
\mathbb{F}[\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})]^{G}
.

(ii) Every finite dimensional representation of
G
is a direct summand in a direct sum of several copies of the
regular representation. Therefore it is sufficient to show that

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{F}}(G,V)\leq d
where

V=\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})=\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})^{\oplus n}
.
Then
K\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}V\cong\bigoplus_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}(K,\psi)^{\oplus n}
,
and the coordinate ring
K[K\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}V]
has variables

\{x_{\psi}^{(1)},\dots,x_{\psi}^{(n)}\mid\psi\in\widehat{G}\}
with

g\cdot x_{\psi}^{(j)}=\psi^{-1}(g)x_{\psi}^{(j)}
.
A variant of Lemma
5.3
, Lemma
5.5
,
and Theorem
5.4
for this more general setup
can be proved in the same way as before, the only difference is that
the notation becomes considerably more complicated.
Alternatively, if
\mathbb{F}
is large enough (say
\mathbb{F}
is infinite),
then one may refer to
[
14
, Theorem 3.4 (ii)]
implying that

\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F})^{\oplus n})=\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,\mathcal{F}(G,\mathbb{F}))
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

A characterization of the subsets
M\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
such that

\{x^{m}\mid m\in M\}
separates the
G
-orbits in
\mathcal{F}(G,K)
was given in

[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
(see
[
5
, Proposition 3.2]
for some additional information on
separating monomials), yielding in
[
12
, Corollary 2.6]
a combinatorial characterization of
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{K}(G)
in terms of the block monoid of

\widehat{G}
. Theorem
5.4
and Corollary
5.6
generalize
[
12
, Proposition 2.3]

(one direction of
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
) and one inequality of
[
12
, Corollary 2.6]

for the case of base fields that are not necessarily algebraically closed.

As an application (and illustration) of Corollary
5.6
we compute the
separating Noether number over the field
\mathbb{R}
of real numbers of the
direct product
\mathrm{C}_{3}\times\mathrm{C}_{3}
of two copies of the cyclic group
\mathrm{C}_{3}

of order
3
:

Proposition 5.8
.

We have the equality
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathrm{C}_{3}\times\mathrm{C}_{3})=3
.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{C}_{3}
is a homomorphic image of
\mathrm{C}_{3}\times\mathrm{C}_{3}
and
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathrm{C}_{3})=3

by
[
4
, Proposition 3.13]
, we have
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathrm{C}_{3}\times\mathrm{C}_{3})\geq 3
.
The reverse inequality
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathrm{C}_{3}\times\mathrm{C}_{3})\leq 3
can be obtained applying
Corollary
5.6
for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
,
K=\mathbb{C}
, and
G=\mathrm{C}_{3}\times\mathrm{C}_{3}
.
Then
\Gamma
is the automorphism group of the field extension
\mathbb{C}
of
\mathbb{R}
,
so
\Gamma
is the two-element group generated by complex conjugation.
In particular, the
\Gamma
-orbit of a character
\chi
of
G
consists of the character
\chi
and
its inverse
\chi^{-1}\in\widehat{G}
. The character group
\widehat{G}
is also isomorphic to

\mathrm{C}_{3}\times\mathrm{C}_{3}
, so it is generated by two order
3
characters
\psi_{1}

and
\psi_{2}
. Writing
\chi_{(i,j)}:=\psi_{1}^{i}\psi_{2}^{j}
, we have

\widehat{G}=\{\chi_{(i,j)}\mid(i,j)\in\{0,1,2\}\times\{0,1,2\}
.
The partition of
\widehat{G}
into the disjoint union of
\Gamma
-orbits is the following:

\widehat{G}=\{\chi_{(0,0)}\}\sqcup\{\chi_{(1,0)},\chi_{(2,0)}\}\sqcup\{\chi_{(0,1)},\chi_{(0,2)}\}\sqcup\{\chi_{(1,1)},\chi_{(2,2)}\}\sqcup\{\chi_{(1,2)},\chi_{(2,1)}\}

In order to have a simpler notation for the elements of
\mathcal{F}(\widehat{G},\mathbb{Z})
, write

\delta_{(i,j)}:=\delta_{\chi_{(i,j)}}
for the basis elements.
The additive monoid
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
is generated by its irreducible elements,
where an element of
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
is said to be
irreducible

if it is not the sum of two non-zero elements of
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
.
Recall that the length of
b\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
is
|b|=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}b(\chi)
.
Clearly
\delta_{(0,0)}
is the only length
1
element of
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
,
and
\delta_{\chi}+\delta_{\chi^{-1}}
for
\chi\neq\chi_{(0,0)}
are the only length
2
irreducible elements
in
\mathcal{B}_{G}
. The automorphism group of the group
\widehat{G}
acts naturally by

\mathbb{Z}
-module automorphisms on
\mathcal{F}(\widehat{G},\mathbb{Z})
and by monoid automorphisms of the
submonoid
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
. We claim that up to such automorphisms, the only irreducible elements
in
\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
with length greater than
2
are

\displaystyle 3\delta_{(1,0)},\qquad\delta_{(1,0)}+\delta_{(0,1)}+\delta_{(2,2)},

\displaystyle b_{1}:=\delta_{(1,0)}+\delta_{(0,1)}+2\delta_{(1,1)},\qquad b_{2}:=\delta_{(1,0)}+2\delta_{(0,1)}+\delta_{(2,1)},

\displaystyle b_{3}:=2\delta_{(1,0)}+2\delta_{(0,1)}+\delta_{(1,1)}.

Indeed, if for an irreducible
b\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
with
|b|>2

there are two different non-trivial characters
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
with
b(\sigma_{1})\neq 0
and

b(\sigma_{2})\neq 0
, then applying an automorphism of
\widehat{G}
we may reduce to the case

\sigma_{1}=\chi_{(1,0)}
and
\sigma_{2}=\chi_{(0,1)}
.
Moreover,
b(\chi_{(1,0)})\in\{1,2\}
and
b(\chi_{(1,0)})\in\{1,2\}
.
After these observations it is easy to sort out the few possibilities, and
conclude the claim. In particular,
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
represent the orbits of all irreducible elements in

\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
with length strictly greater than
3
.

Set
M:=\{b\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})\mid|b|\leq 3\}

If
b_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}
for some
\Gamma
-stable subset
I

of
\widehat{G}
, then

\chi_{(1,0)},\chi_{(0,1)},\chi_{(1,1)},\chi_{(2,2)}\in I
, and
the equality

\delta_{(1,0)}+\delta_{(0,1)}+2\delta_{(1,1)}=(\delta_{(1,0)}+\delta_{(0,1)}+\delta_{(2,2)})+2(\delta_{(1,1)}+\delta_{(2,2)})-3\delta_{(2,2)}

shows that
b_{1}\in\langle M_{I}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

If
b_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}
for some
\Gamma
-stable subset
I

of
\widehat{G}
, then

\chi_{(1,0)},\chi_{(0,1)},\chi_{(2,1)},\chi_{(0,2)}\in I
, and
the equality

\delta_{(1,0)}+2\delta_{(0,1)}+\delta_{(2,1)}=(\delta_{(1,0)}+\delta_{(0,2)}+\delta_{(2,1)})+2(\delta_{(0,1)}+\delta_{(0,2)})-3\delta_{(0,2)}

shows that
b_{2}\in\langle M_{I}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

If
b_{3}\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})_{I}
for some
\Gamma
-stable subset
I

of
\widehat{G}
, then

\chi_{(1,0)},\chi_{(0,1)},\chi_{(1,1)},\chi_{(2,2)}\in I
, and
the equality

2\delta_{(1,0)}+2\delta_{(0,1)}+\delta_{(1,1)}=2(\delta_{(1,0)}+\delta_{(0,1)}+\delta_{(2,2)})+(\delta_{(1,1)}+\delta_{(2,2)})-3\delta_{(2,2)}

shows that
b_{3}\in\langle M_{I}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Thus we verified that the condition of Corollary
5.6
is satisfied by
d=3
,
hence the desired inequality
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathrm{C}_{3}\times\mathrm{C}_{3})\leq 3

holds.
∎

A federated deep learning framework with distributed hybrid character-level and attention mechanisms for scalable and cost-efficient fake news detection
Authors: K Nithya, C R Dhivyaa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFake news detection is an essential task for media and news organizations to maintain the trust and reliability of the published content. Due to the rapid growth of online users and the spread of misinformation through malicious sources, the fake news circulates quickly across digital platforms. Hence, developing an accurate and efficient fake news detection model is crucial for social welfare. Although many studies have been conducted, achieving high accuracy remains challenging task, especially when dealing with rare and infrequent words. Traditional embedding models such as Word2Vec and FastText perform only word-level vectorization, while transformer-based models like BERT handle subword token more effectively. However, selecting the most suitable subword pieces for vector representation raises challenges. The research introduces a character-based embedding approach to generate fine-grained vector representations by analysing each character. A hybrid CharBERT-Optimized CNN and attention-based stacked Bi-LSTM with cost-sensitive learning (COASBC) model is proposed to classify fake news. In addition, a federated learning (FL) framework is integrated to enable distributed training across multiple news sources for improving generalization of model without sharing raw data. It reduces centralized computational cost, enhances data security and scalability of the model. Furthermore, statistical analysis and complexity analysis are conducted to evaluate the efficiency and computational feasibility of the proposed model. The generalization ability of the model is also analysed using Truth Seeker dataset 2023 that contains short-text tweets. Experimental evaluation using the LIAR, Fake and Real News datasets, FakeNewsNet, and WELFake Dataset demonstrates that the proposed Federated COASBC model achieves superior accuracy, precision, and reliability compared to existing state-of-the-art fake news detection methods and it maintains high computational efficiency.Keywords: Rumor detection, CharBERT, Stacked Bi-LSTM, Character embedding, Attention mechanism, Federated learningSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Social media platforms such as Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, and YouTube have been developed into vital communication mediums that allow instant sharing of messages among users at any time. The big challenge for online social network1 users is to find reliable information due to the existence of incorrect information. Because of the instance access of social media, false/rumor messages2,3 that spread among a huge online crowd also causes severe harm to society and mislead the social network users. Unfortunately, social networking sites in recent years have transformed this issue into a significant one. Therefore, there is a great need for automated approaches for detecting rumors in a timely manner. The identification and detection of rumor information is one of the most crucial steps in preventing the spread of rumor. There are a wide variety of existing rumor detection techniques that deal with machine4 and deep learning methods5.

To represent the natural language text in to vector, various supervised and unsupervised word embedding techniques known as Word2Vec, Glove, FastText and BERT are used. While representing the same word in different context, few embedding techniques provides same vector. It will not justify the word semantic properly. To solve this issue, some of the context based embedding techniques are employed which produces different word vector for same word present in different context6. Moreover, most of the word embedding techniques identifies only the word vector for already learned words. The word vector for new word that is not present in the dictionary is provided by introducing out of vocabulary handling word embedding techniques. Character based embedding technique is introduced in this work which reads the sequence of input characters as tokens for predicting the output. This sequence of input characters is converted into vector and a new unlearned words vector is produced with this character vector. Convolutional neural network helps to produce word vector from the character vectors. Multiple CNN’s are involved in this word vector formation process.

A Onan7 proposed embedding scheme to obtain the vectors of the input texts and significant topics are extracted from the input text collections. The ensemble based clustering analysis is also incorporated to improve the performance of the prediction. Onan8 examined sarcasm in the form of nonliteral language, wherein words conveyed opposite meanings to express sentiments. The proposed a deep learning-based approach and compared topic-enriched word embeddings to traditional ones used for effective sarcasm identification. The traditional detection model uses, Machine Learning methods known as Naive Bayes, logistic regression, support vector machine, etc., for rumor detection which required perfect training data while performing classification. Training the fake contents is a big challenge in this research. Onan9 used text classification for finding the characteristics of data, primarily focusing on expressive, appellative, and informative categories. Supervised machine learning algorithms were commonly employed, necessitating the detection of a rich set of features for robust classification. The output is combined to obtain robust classification results.

Nowadays, Deep Learning (DL) models are replaced instead of ML and it have been widely used in the field of text feature analysis where their enormous potential has been shown. In existing, few deep learning-based feature selection methods are used to extract efficient features with single feature selection technique. Also, it is possible to extract most important and accurate features with the utilization of hybrid or sequential feature selection approaches to attain improved performance. Onan10 aimed to optimize the analysing sentiment of instructor evaluation reviews using a recurrent neural network (RNN) model, demonstrating superior performance over traditional machine learning classifiers through comprehensive empirical analysis. Onan11 adopted a powerful model for prediction with strong classification performance in MOOC reviews was showcased by integrating ensemble and deep learning paradigms. The contribution encompassed addressing diverse research questions related to sentiment analysis in educational data. The DL algorithms such as CNN, LSTM12, GRU13, Bi-LSTM14, Bi-GRU etc., are selectively used for effective rumor text classification task.

In recent years, the increasing volume of decentralized data across multiple news platforms have motivated the adoption of Federated Learning (FL)15,16 in fake news detection17. Federated Learning18,19 enables collaborative model training across multiple devices or data sources without transferring raw data to a central server. This approach not only protects sensitive information but also enhances model generalization by learning from diverse and distributed datasets. Integrating federated learning20 into fake news detection21 frameworks allows multiple media organizations to contribute to a shared model securely, thereby improving detection accuracy while significantly reducing centralized computational costs.

In recent years, transformer-based word embedding models have been developed for vector generation, but it will also not handle out-of-vocabulary words and misspelled words properly. Character based embedding models can mitigate this problem by embedding each character to produce the word vector. Onan28 analysed reviews of products from twitter by utilizing the integration model of TF-IDF and CNN-LSTM for classification. Feature extraction is an essential step of rumor detection. Federated CharBERT-optimized CNN and attention based SBi-LSTM with cost sensitive (COASBC)

The proposed Federated COASBC framework is a scalable solution for fake news detection. The integration of federated learning68 with a hybrid deep learning architecture69 is shown in Fig. 1. Unlike conventional centralized models, the federated framework enables multiple clients to collaboratively train a shared model without exchanging raw data and protect the sensitive information across media platforms.

Federated COASBC architecture.

Non-IID (non-independent and identically distributed) datasets such as LIAR, Fake and Real News (ISOT), FakeNewsNet, and WELFake Dataset are used for model evaluation. The ISOT dataset consists of real news articles collected from Reuters and fake news articles gathered from multiple unreliable sources identified by Politifact and Wikipedia. LIAR dataset contains short statements collected over a period of approximately ten years from Politifact, covering a wide range of speakers, topics, and contextual settings. FakeNewsNet is a comprehensive dataset that integrates news content with social context from platforms like Twitter, combining information from reliable fact-checking sources. The WELFake dataset is constructed by combining multiple existing datasets from Kaggle, the McIntire fake news dataset, Reuters, and the BuzzFeed Political dataset to create a larger and more diverse corpus. In the federated set up, Client 1 is assigned the Fake and Real News dataset, Client 2 uses the LIAR dataset, Client 3 uses the FakeNewsNet dataset, and Client 4 uses the WELFake dataset. Each client independently trains the local CharBert-Optimized CNN and Attention-based70 stacked Bi-LSTM with Cost-Sensitive Learning (COASBC) model on its dataset. The model integrates character-level embeddings, convolutional neural networks, attention mechanisms, and stacked Bi-LSTM layers to find both fine-grained and contextual textual patterns. Cost-sensitive learning is incorporated to address class imbalance between fake and real news instances to improve performance of the model. After local training, clients transmit only the updated model parameters to a central server for aggregation. The server employs the FedProx algorithm to handle node heterogeneity and non-IID datasets. FedProx introduces a proximal term in each client’s local loss to restrict large deviations from the current global model for stabilizing convergence across clients with diverse data distributions and computational capabilities. Each client updates its local model while remaining close to the global model, improving stability and convergence of the federated training process. The global model is iteratively updated and redistributed to clients for subsequent training rounds, refining the shared model through direct data and gradient sharing. This federated approach allows the system to leverage diverse datasets distributed across multiple clients, improving model generalization to heterogeneous news sources. By integrating FedProx with the COASBC model, the framework achieves a balance between accuracy, scalability, and computational efficiency. The federated setup eliminates the need for centralized data storage, ensuring that raw news content never leaves local clients, while still enabling the global model to learn from multiple sources. Experimental evaluation on the Fake and Real News, LIAR datasets FakeNewsNet, and WELFake Dataset demonstrates that the proposed framework maintains high accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and robustness, even in a distributed, heterogeneous environment.

Design of the COASBC model for fake news classification

The proposed work explains detection of fake news using character embedding with optimized CNN model along with attention based stacked Bi-LSTM model. This proposed work has significant stages namely character embedding with Optimized CNN, attention based efficient feature selection with stacked Bi-LSTM, and cost sensitivity-based rumour classification model. Figure 2 states the general overview of proposed work. The proposed COASBC architecture includes preprocessing stage, character embedding, an optimized convolutional neural network, an attention-based stacked Bi-LSTM and softmax-based classification layer. The text is preprocessed by applying stop-word removal, special character elimination, tokenization, and stemming to reduce noise and standardize the input data before feature extraction. After preprocessing, the text is transformed into character-level representations using a CharBERT embedding module which captures precise character information from each word. It effectively handles misspelling, and rare words in social media news content. The character-based embeddings represent text using individual characters rather than treating each word as a single unit. It allows the model to capture detailed patterns such as spelling variations, prefixes, suffixes, and morphological structures. It generates richer and more informative representations for effective feature extraction.

Proposed CharBERT-optimized CNN and attention based SBi-LSTM with cost sensitive (COASBC).

The generated embeddings are passed through an optimized convolutional neural network (OPCNN) which is designed to extract local and discriminative semantic features from the news text. The extracted feature maps are processed by an attention-based stacked Bi-LSTM that learns long-range contextual dependencies and sequential relationships in the text. The attention mechanism dynamically highlights important words and phrases that are most relevant for accurate classification. The final feature representations are passed to a dense layer. The SoftMax activation function is used to normalize the output vector and convert it into probability values for each class. To address class imbalance problem, cost-sensitive learning is applied by computing class weights for each class based on the distribution of training samples. These class weights assign different levels of importance to each class label during the classification process. It improves prediction reliability and reduces biased learning toward majority classes.

CharBERT model with multiple CNN

Character embedding is an effective word selection method. Several language representation models have adopted the transformers architecture for efficient feature embedding. In the famous transformer BERT model, if any of the word which does not occur in its vocabulary then it splits the unknown word in to word pieces. Each word piece is embedded using embedding matrix while using this model it only focuses on subword instead of the entire word. These word pieces not always carry the prefix of the word, sometimes the preceding portion of the word may not be available. It is difficult to identify the exact word embedding of the given word token. CharBERT is a modified form of BERT that attempts to produce single embeddings for single word instead of word piece. It does not have any word piece vocabulary. It consists of character encoder and character decoder. Through character encoder, it can read the sequence of input characters as tokens, and it produces character vectors. The “base-uncased” type of BERT is used for CharBERT and it is a bidirectional transformer. This sequence of input characters is converted into vector. The overview of BERT and CharBERT is given in Fig. 3.

Overview of BERT and character BERT model.

The sequence of character vectors is fed to multiple character CNN’s. It scans n character sequences at a time and it embeds the characters in the 16 dimensions by using small 1D-Multiple CNNs. All the character CNN outputs are aggregated into single vector. If the dataset contains misspelled words, then the traditional models ignore the incorrect words. The proposed COASBC model adopts CharBERT for identifying the word vector for the incorrect words and its sample representation is depicted in Fig. 4.

Optimized convolutional neural network (OPCNN)

In the concept of neural network, tuning of hyper parameters plays a major role which improves the quality of the model and also the ability of generalizing new data. In the proposed Optimized Convolutional Neural Network (OPCNN), Bayesian Optimization based hyperopt with Tree of Parzen Estimators (TPE) is employed for choosing the optimal hyper parameters of neural network. It uses sequential model-based optimization for minimizing the validation loss by selecting the best hyper parameters. It provides an interface for configuring the search space of the variables and customizing an evaluation function with the loss function to assess the parameters in search space. Hyperopt utilizes TPE, that search through an hyperparameter space of values for minimizing the loss function to yield better performance on both training and testing datasets.

Tuning the hyperparameters of CNN

In proposed Optimized Convolutional Neural Network (OPCNN), hyper-parameters of hidden layers are tuned and further fed as input to the stacked Bi-LSTM attention layer. OPCNN possess different layers for handling the word embedding formed by CharBERT. The layers such as, Dropout, convolution, pooling and flatten. The OPCNN model takes word embedding as input and produces one dimensional feature vector as output from the flatten layer as shown in Fig. 5.

Optimized convolutional neural network (OPCNN)71.

Dropout Layer

During training process certain neurons are chosen and ignored randomly to prevent from complex computation.

Convolutional layer

The convolutional layer contains set of filters and kernels which is used for the representation of words with various length. The significant operation is done are non-linear operation which is pass through activation function to generate the final output.

Max-pooling layer

The output of convolutional layer and to reduce the number of computational operation Max-pooling layer is effectively applied in the down-samples of the input. The main function of Max-pooling layer is reducing the size of spatial representation and number of parametric values and computations in the model.

Flatten layer

Flatten layer is available in the middle of the convolutional and the fully connected layer. It transforms features in 2-D matrix format into featured vector that can be given to fully connected layers.

The hyperparameters such as the number of neurons, dropout rate, filter size, kernel size, pooling, batch size, and activation function directly influence the neural network’s structure and functionality. It has a set of values for various parameters. The number of neurons in every hidden layer is usually between the size of the input and output layers. The neurons are adjusted between 10 and 200. To prevent overfitting, the dropout regularization technique is implemented, which ignores the contribution of some neurons towards the next layer and it is set to the range of 0.1 to 0.6. By using filters, features are extracted parallelly by determining the size of the receptive field that has a varying number of filters from 32 to 512 for the given input. In addition, each filter hires the Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation function. Another hyperparameter is kernel size, it will be varied from 2 to 4. Usually, a kernel is a small vector that contains the weights. To reduce the number of features from the feature vector, the pooling layer performs the max operation and has a pool size of 3,6. Batch size decides the number of samples that will be passed through the network at one time. Hence, it has a range of values such as 32, 64, 73, 128, and 146. All these tuning is performed for a various number of epochs varying from 1 to 200.

The Bayesian optimization process using Hyperopt with the Tree of Parzen Estimators (TPE) algorithm is carried out for a few numbers of iterations. In each iteration, a single hyperparameter configuration is sampled from the defined search space and OPCNN model is trained using the configuration. The validation loss is computed and used as the objective function to guide the optimization. The TPE algorithm updates its probabilistic model after each iteration to focus on promising hyperparameter regions. The optimization process terminates after the maximum number of iterations is reached or if no further improvement in validation loss is observed. The hyperparameter set that achieves the minimum validation loss is selected as optimal configuration. Stacked Bi-LSTM (SBi-LSTM) with attention layer

The features attained from the optimized CNN are fed to a SBi-LSTM model that produces a sequence representation. This section presents the Stacked Bidirectional-LSTM (SBi-LSTM) based fake news analysis model and it comprises stacking of two Bi-directional LSTM layers in a sequential manner. Traditional LSTM RNN have been extended as Bi-LSTM and the multiple Bi-LSTM layers are stacked which process the input multiple times and retain sequential information in a much efficient manner. Stacked Bi-LSTM can get rich contextual information from both past and future time sequences. During research, the stacked network achieved much better results than single layer of LSTM or Bi-LSTM.

Using stacked Bi-LSTM as in Fig. 6, sequence prediction on the text data has been performed. The stacked layer architecture improves the performance of fake news detection system, and it extracts the deep contextual features. This final feature vector is fed to an attention layer that selects the highly correlated features for final classification. The output of the last hidden layer is forwarded to the Softmax classifier to identify, whether the given data is real or fake. While, investigating the result of the increasing number of stacked Bi-LSTM Layers it is observed that the prediction loss reduced when the number of Bi-LSTM layers was increased. Also, the model learns more features from the dataset with decreased loss and increased training time.

Attention mechanism

Generally, all words in a sentence are not contribute equally to the representation of sentence. So, the attention mechanism is implemented to capture the significant influence of the words in a sentence. It reduces the number of learnable parameters and improves the accuracy of the model. Evaluation metrics

The performance of the proposed character embedding with attention mechanism is measured based on precision, recall, accuracy and F1-score.

Accuracy is calculated by finding the ratio between the correct classification and total number of classifications.

Precision is computed by taking the ratio between number of correct positive prediction and total number of positive predictions.

Recall is measured by taking ratio between number of correct positive prediction and total number of correct predictions.

F1-Score is mean of precision and recall.

Empirical analysis of CharBERT for robust text classification

To evaluate the robustness of the proposed model, 20% of the true statements (11,138 samples) and 20% of the false statements (10,490 samples) from the Truth Seeker Dataset (2023) were reserved as an unseen test set which consists of user-generated tweets. In particular, the dataset exhibits substantial linguistic noise, including misspellings, informal abbreviations, elongated words (e.g., “soooo”, “goooood”), and irregular token formations. For example, tokens such as “ANDDDDD”, “ALRIGHTTT”, “Aaaaannnnd”, “AMEEEEN”, and “ALLLLL” are frequently observed, reflecting common patterns of character repetition and non-standard spelling in social media text. These characteristics make the dataset particularly suitable for assessing the robustness of models designed to handle out-of-vocabulary (OOV) and morphologically complex inputs. To further validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach, we evaluated its performance in comparison with baseline transformer models such as BERT and RoBERTa under identical experimental settings. BERT and RoBERTa achieve strong baseline performance, with RoBERTa consistently outperforming BERT due to its improved pretraining strategy and better contextual representation. However, both models rely on subword tokenization, making them sensitive to misspellings words, which slightly limits their performance. CharBERT addresses this limitation by incorporating character-level representations, enabling it to better capture subword patterns and handle noisy text, resulting in improved precision, recall, and F1-score. Conclusion

Fake news detection demands advanced and intelligent models capable of addressing the complexity and diversity of online information. The proposed Federated COASBC framework introduces a hybrid architecture that integrates CharBERT embeddings for contextual character-level representation, an optimized CNN (OPCNN) for automated hyperparameter tuning through Hyperopt and an Attention-based Stacked Bi-LSTM for capturing long-range dependencies with focused attention on significant textual features. To overcome the issue of class imbalance, cost-sensitive learning is incorporated to provide balanced and reliable classification performance. In this approach, federated learning enables decentralized model training that enhances scalability and improves computational efficiency by distributing the training process across multiple clients. The model was evaluated on four benchmark datasets such as LIAR, Fake & Real News (ISOT), FakeNewsNet and WELFake datasets, which achieved superior results compared to traditional deep neural networks and other state-of-the-art methods. The proposed model achieved 79.18% accuracy, 77.12% precision, 78.34% recall, and 77.62% F1-score on the LIAR dataset. It attained 99.72% accuracy, 99.35% precision, 99.02% recall, and 99.18% F1-score on the Fake and Real News (ISOT) dataset. On the FakeNewsNet dataset, the model achieved 99.28% accuracy, 99.15% precision, 98.96% recall, and 99.05% F1-score. For the WELFake dataset, the model obtained 98.61% accuracy, 98.42% precision, 98.18% recall, and 98.30% F1-score. In addition, statistical analysis and model complexity analysis are conducted to assess the robustness of the model. The generalization of model is evaluated on Truth Seeker Dataset and achieved 98.3% accuracy. The results proved that the Federated-COASBC model effectively enhances fake news detection by improving accuracy, strong generalization, cost efficiency and computational optimization. The proposed framework was evaluated in a simulated federated learning setup with four clients, and the results confirm its effectiveness within this controlled experimental environment. Large-scale deployment, communication overhead per federated round, latency under realistic network conditions, and edge-device feasibility will be analyzed in future work.

On absolutely exotic diffeomorphisms of 4-manifolds
Hokuto Konno, Abhishek Mallick, Masaki Taniguchi

Abstract
. We prove that there exist infinitely many contractible compact smooth 4 4 -manifolds C C that admit absolutely exotic diffeomorphisms of infinite order in π 0 ​ ( Diff ​ ( C ) ) \pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(C)) . By “absolutely”, we mean that isotopies are not required to be relative to the boundary. This follows from a theorem that produces absolutely exotic diffeomorphisms from relatively exotic diffeomorphisms, analogous to a theorem of Akbulut and Ruberman that produces absolutely exotic 4-manifolds from relatively exotic 4-manifolds.



1.
Main results

Given a smooth manifold
W
with boundary, we denote by
\mathrm{Diff}(W)
the group of diffeomorphisms of
W
.
It has a natural subgroup
\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W)
, consisting of diffeomorphisms that are equal to the identity in a neighborhood of
\partial W
.
Similarly, we define the absolute and relative homeomorphism groups, denoted by
\mathrm{Homeo}(W)
and
\mathrm{Homeo}_{\partial}(W)
.
We say that
f\in\mathrm{Diff}(W)
is an
absolutely exotic diffeomorphism
if
f
represents a non-trivial element of

\operatorname{Ker}\bigl(\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(W))\to\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Homeo}(W))\bigr),

and that
f\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W)
is a
relatively exotic diffeomorphism
if
f
represents a non-trivial element of

\operatorname{Ker}\bigl(\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))\to\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Homeo}_{\partial}(W))\bigr).

Recently, relatively exotic diffeomorphisms of contractible compact
4
-manifolds have been actively studied using families gauge theory
[
10
,
12
,
11
,
9
,
8
,
15
]
, most of which can be easily seen to be not absolutely exotic.
In addition, employing a different technique, Gabai–Gay–Hartman
[
4
]
announced that
D^{4}
admits a relatively exotic diffeomorphism of order
2
in the mapping class group, which would provide the first confirmed example of an absolutely exotic diffeomorphism of a contractible
4
-manifold (see Remark
3.1
).

In this short note, we prove that there exist many contractible compact
4
-manifolds admitting absolutely exotic diffeomorphisms of infinite order:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist infinitely many contractible compact smooth
4
-manifolds
C
that admit absolutely exotic diffeomorphisms of infinite order in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(C))
.

More precisely, we construct infinitely many such mutually non-diffeomorphic manifolds
C
, distinguished by the Heegaard Floer homologies of their boundaries.
The boundaries of those
C
we shall give are Haken, hence irreducible.

1.1
follows from the following general result, which produces absolutely exotic diffeomorphisms from relatively exotic diffeomorphisms, analogous to a theorem of Akbulut–Ruberman
[
2
]
that constructs absolutely exotic
4
-manifolds from relatively exotic
4
-manifolds:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
W
be a compact smooth
4
-manifold with boundary.
Then
W
admits a smooth codimension-0 embedding into a compact smooth
4
-manifold
V
with boundary, homotopy equivalent to
W
, with the following property:
if
f\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W)
is a relatively exotic diffeomorphism of
W
, then its extension to
V
by the identity is an absolutely exotic diffeomorphism of
V
.

In
[
12
,
9
,
8
]
, it was proven that certain contractible compact
4
-manifolds
W
admit a relatively exotic diffeomorphism
f
of infinite order in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
.
Applying
1.2
to powers of
f
then shows that there exists a contractible
4
-manifold
C
admitting an absolutely exotic diffeomorphism of infinite order in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(C))
.
We shall see how to obtain infinitely many such
C
.

Remark 1.3
.

Absolutely exotic diffeomorphisms of other
4
-manifolds with boundary have already been studied in
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
using the families Kronheimer–Mrowka invariant, which differs from the technique employed in this paper. The smallest example that can be obtained from
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
has
b_{2}=4
.

Using (the proof of)
1.2
, we can easily upgrade results on relatively exotic diffeomorphisms to results on absolutely exotic diffeomorphisms.
For example, from a theorem of Lin
[
13
]
, we have:

Corollary 1.4
.

There exists a simply-connected, compact, smooth
4
-manifold with boundary that admits an absolutely exotic diffeomorphism which survives after taking the connected sum with a single copy of
S^{2}\times S^{2}
.

We shall also discuss higher homotopy groups of absolute diffeomorphism groups in
section

4
.

2.
Akbulut–Ruberman cobordisms

1.2
follows from a key construction of homology cobordisms due to Akbulut and Ruberman
[
2
]
, which yields absolutely exotic 4-manifolds from relatively exotic 4-manifolds, such as corks.
What we need is a slight analysis of their construction, together with an input from
3
-dimensional topology, which can be summarized as follows; it provides a comparison result between the relative and absolute diffeomorphism groups.
To state this, let

i:\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Diff}(W)

denote the natural inclusion map.
We say that a smooth oriented cobordism
X
from
M
to
N
is
invertible
if there exists a smooth oriented cobordism
X^{\prime}
from
N
to
M
such that
X\cup_{N}X^{\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
M\times[0,1]
.
We observe that the invertible homology cobordism constructed by Akbulut and Ruberman Akbulut–Ruberman
[
2
, Section 2]
possesses the following remarkable property:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
W
be a compact smooth
4
-manifold with boundary.
Then there exists an invertible homology cobordism
X
from
\partial W
to a
3
-manifold
N
such that the
4
-manifold

V=W\cup_{\partial W}X

satisfies the following property:

•

V
is homotopy equivalent to
W
.

•

i_{\ast}:\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(V))\to\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(V))
is injective.

•

i_{\ast}:\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(V))\to\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}(V))
is isomorphic for
n>0
.

Proof.

Given
W
, Akbulut–Ruberman
[
2
, Proof of Theorem A]
constructed a certain invertible homology cobordism
X
from
M=\partial W
to a 3-manifold
N
for which
V=W\cup_{M}X
is homotopy equivalent to
W
.
(See
[
17
, Lemma 3.8]
for a detailed proof of the homotopy equivalence.)
As mentioned in
[
2
, Proof of Theorem 6.1]
, the
3
-manifold
N
is obtained by gluing hyperbolic
3
-manifolds along incompressible tori, and hence is a Haken manifold.
Recall that a graph manifold is characterized as an irreducible
3
-manifold whose JSJ decomposition has no hyperbolic pieces.
Thus,
N
is not a graph manifold, and in particular it is not a Seifert fibered
3
-manifold.
However, in general, a Haken manifold
Y
that is not Seifert fibered satisfies that all higher homotopy groups of
\mathrm{Diff}(Y)
vanish
(see
[
6
, Theorem 2]
). Hence,

(1)

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}(N))=0\quad\text{for all }n>0.

On the other hand, the inclusion map
i:\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(V)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Diff}(V)
fits into the exact sequence

(2)

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(V)\xrightarrow{i}\mathrm{Diff}(V)\xrightarrow{r}\mathrm{Diff}(N),

where
r:\mathrm{Diff}(V)\to\mathrm{Diff}(N)
is the restriction map to the boundary, which is surjective onto a union of connected components.
This exact seqence gives rise to long exact sequences of homotopy groups, whereas the very final map on
\pi_{0}
need not be surjective.
The remaining assertions of
2.1
follow from these long exact sequences together with (
1
).
∎

3.
From relative to absolute

We now prove the results stated in
section

1
.

Proof of
1.2
.

From
2.1
, we obtain an invertible homology cobordism
X
from
M=\partial W
to some 3-manifold
N
such that

i_{\ast}:\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(V))\to\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(V))

is injective for

V=W\cup_{\partial W}X
.
By the invertibility of
X
, there exists a cobordism
X^{\prime}
from
N
to
M
such that
X\cup_{N}X^{\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
M\times[0,1]
.
Fix a diffeomorphism

X\cup_{N}X^{\prime}\cong M\times[0,1],

which induces a diffeomorphism

(3)

\displaystyle V\cup_{N}X^{\prime}=W\cup_{M}X\cup_{N}X^{\prime}\cong W

Via the diffeomorphism (
3
), the diffeomorphism

f\cup\operatorname{id}_{X}\cup\operatorname{id}_{X^{\prime}}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W\cup_{M}X\cup_{N}X^{\prime})

corresponds to
f\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W)
.
Hence, the nontriviality of the relative mapping class

[f]\in\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
implies that

[f\cup\operatorname{id}_{X}]
is nontrivial in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(V))
.
Since

i_{\ast}:\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(V))\to\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(V))

is injective by
2.1
,
it follows that
[f\cup\operatorname{id}_{X}]
is nontrivial in

\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(V))
.

On the other hand, since the topological mapping class
[f]_{\mathrm{top}}
is trivial in

\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Homeo}_{\partial}(W))
,

[f\cup\operatorname{id}_{X}]_{\mathrm{top}}
is trivial in

\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Homeo}(V))
.
Therefore,
f\cup\operatorname{id}_{X}
is an absolutely exotic
diffeomorphism of
V
.
∎

Proof of
1.1
.

The first ingredient is the theorem of Kang, Park, and the third author
[
8
]
, which states that for any contractible compact smooth
4
-manifold
W
whose boundary is a Seifert-fibered
3
-manifold, the boundary Dehn twist
\tau\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W)
is a relatively exotic diffeomorphism and has infinite order in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
.
Let
V
be the
4
-manifold given by
1.2
, which is homotopy equivalent to
W
and contains
W
as an embedded submanifold.
Then
V
is contractible, and we see that the extension of
\tau
to
V
is an absolutely exotic diffeomorphism of
V
of infinite order in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(V))
by applying
1.2
to powers of
\tau
.

Infinitely many examples of such
V
, distinguished by the Floer homologies of
\partial V
, can be obtained as follows.
Let
W_{n}
be a sequence of contractible, compact smooth
4
-manifolds whose boundaries are Seifert-fibered
3
-manifolds, and suppose that the ranks of their reduced Heegaard Floer homologies satisfy

(4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}H\!F^{\mathrm{red}}(\partial W_{n})\to+\infty

as
n\to+\infty
, where we consider Floer homology with
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
-coefficients.
To be specific, consider the Casson–Harer family, a well-known sequence of Brieskorn homology
3
-spheres that bound contractible smooth
4
-manifolds.
If we choose a certain subfamily
\{Y_{n}\}_{n}
of the Casson–Harer family and take contractible smooth
4
-manifolds
W_{n}
bounded by
Y_{n}
, the family
\{W_{n}\}_{n}
satisfies the assumption (
4
).
For instance,

Y_{n}=\Sigma(2n,2n-1,2n+1)

is such an example (see
[
1
, Table 2]
) for
n\geq 2
.

Let
V_{n}
be the
4
-manifold, homotopy equivalent to
W_{n}
, given by
1.2
.
After passing to a subsequence if necessary, we see that the
V_{n}
are pairwise distinct, and, as noted above, each
V_{n}
admits an absolutely exotic diffeomorphism of infinite order.

To show that
\{V_{n}\}_{n}
contains a pairwise distinct subfamily, it suffices to prove that

(5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}H\!F^{\mathrm{red}}(\partial V_{n})\to+\infty

as
n\to+\infty
.
This follows immediately from the inequality

(6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}H\!F^{\mathrm{red}}(\partial W_{n})\leq\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}H\!F^{\mathrm{red}}(\partial V_{n})

together with (
4
). The inequality (
6
) is established as follows.

Recall from the proof of
1.2
that each
V_{n}
is obtained as

V_{n}=W_{n}\cup_{\partial W_{n}}X_{n},

where
X_{n}
is an invertible homology cobordism for each
n
.
Let
X_{n}^{\prime}
be a smooth cobordism from
\partial V_{n}
to
\partial W_{n}
such that
X_{n}\cup_{\partial V_{n}}X_{n}^{\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
\partial W_{n}\times[0,1]
.
Then the cobordism map

H\!F^{+}(X_{n}\cup_{\partial V_{n}}X_{n}^{\prime}):H\!F^{+}(\partial W_{n})\to H\!F^{+}(\partial W_{n})

in the plus version of Heegaard Floer homology is the identity map.
Hence,

H\!F^{+}(X_{n}):H\!F^{+}(\partial W_{n})\to H\!F^{+}(\partial V_{n})

is injective.
Since the reduced Floer homology is characterized as the
U
-torsion part of the plus Floer homology, and the cobordism map
H\!F^{+}(X_{n})
is
U
-equivariant, it follows that the restriction of
H\!F^{+}(X_{n})
defines a well-defined, injective homomorphism

H\!F^{\mathrm{red}}(X_{n}):H\!F^{\mathrm{red}}(\partial W_{n})\to H\!F^{\mathrm{red}}(\partial V_{n}),

which verifies (
6
).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Gabai–Gay–Hartman
[
4
]
announced that some diffeomorphism
f\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(D^{4})
(of order at most
2
in the mapping class group) is non-trivial in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(D^{4}))
, and hence relatively exotic by topological triviality via the Alexander trick.
We remark that the relative exotica of
f
immediately implies that
f
is an absolutely exotic diffeomorphism of
D^{4}
.
Indeed, an exact sequence analogous to (
2
) for
V=D^{4}
induces an exact sequence

\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3}))\xrightarrow{\partial_{\ast}}\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(D^{4}))\xrightarrow{i_{\ast}}\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(D^{4})).

The image of the generator of
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Diff}(S^{3}))\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
under
\partial_{\ast}
is the boundary Dehn twist of
D^{4}
, which can be seen to be trivial in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(D^{4}))
by using the standard circle action on
D^{4}
.
Hence
i_{\ast}
is injective, so the diffeomorphism
f
is non-trivial in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(D^{4}))
.

In contrast, most other previously known relatively exotic diffeomorphisms of compact contractible
4
-manifolds
C

[
10
,
9
,
8
]
are boundary Dehn twists of
4
-manifolds with Seifert boundaries, and are therefore obviously trivial in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(C))
.

It is worth further examining the Gabai-Gay-Hartman example
[
4
]
.
If we apply the proof of
1.2
to their example
f\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(D^{4})
discussed in Remark
3.1
, we obtain:

Remark 3.2
.

Let
W
be a compact, oriented, smooth
4
-manifold such that there exists a compact, oriented, smooth
4
-manifold
W^{\prime}
with
\partial W\cong-\partial W^{\prime}
and
W\cup_{\partial W}W^{\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
S^{4}
.
Then
W
admits a relatively exotic diffeomorphism of order 2 in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
.
Moreover, if
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Diff}(\partial W))=0
, then
W
admits an absolutely exotic diffeomorphism of order 2 in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
.

To see this, fix a smooth embedding
D^{4}\hookrightarrow W
away from
\partial W
.
Via this embedding, consider the extension to
W
of the diffeomorphism
f\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(D^{4})
that is announced to be non-trivial in
[
4
]
.
We can further extend
f
to
W\cup_{\partial W}W^{\prime}
. Since
W\cup_{\partial W}W^{\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
S^{4}
, the non-triviality of
f
in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(D^{4}))
(and hence in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}(S^{4}))
) implies that
f
is non-trivial in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
, yielding an order-2 relatively exotic diffeomorphism.
Moreover, if
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Diff}(\partial W))=0
, then, as in the proof of
1.2
, the map
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))\to\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Diff}(W))
is injective, so that

is an order-2 absolutely exotic diffeomorphism of
W
.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Let
W
be the punctured
K3
surface
K3\setminus\operatorname{int}D^{4}
.
Lin
[
13
]
proved that the boundary Dehn twist of
W\#S^{2}\times S^{2}
is a relatively exotic diffeomorphism.
The proof of
1.2
provides an invertible homology cobordism
X
from
S^{3}
to a homology 3-sphere
N
with
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Diff}(N))=0
.
As in the proof of
1.2
, the invertibility of
X
and that
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Diff}(N))=0
imply that the extension of the Dehn twist to
V=W\cup_{\partial W}X
is an absolutely exotic diffeomorphism of

, following from the fact that the boundary Dehn twist on

The same argument with the relative exotica of the boundary Dehn twist on

4.
Higher homotopy groups

So far, we have focused on
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff})
, but this can be easily generalized to
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff})
for any
n\geq 0
.
Given a smooth manifold
W
with boundary, we say that a homotopy class
\gamma\in\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}(W))
is
absolutely exotic
if
\gamma
is a non-trivial element of

\operatorname{Ker}\bigl(\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}(W))\to\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Homeo}(W))\bigr),

and that
\gamma\in\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
is
relatively exotic
if
\gamma
is a non-trivial element of

\operatorname{Ker}\bigl(\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))\to\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Homeo}_{\partial}(W))\bigr).

If
W
is embedded into a smooth manifold
V
as a codimension-
0
submanifold, then a family of diffeomorphisms
\{f_{b}\}_{b\in S^{n}}\subset\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W)
representing
\gamma
can be extended to
V
by the identity outside
W
, yielding an element of
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}(V))
.
With this preparation,
1.2
can be generalized as follows:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
W
be a compact smooth
4
-manifold with boundary and let
n\geq 0
.
Then
W
admits a smooth codimension-0 embedding into a compact smooth
4
-manifold
V
with boundary, homotopy equivalent to
W
, with the following property:
if a homotopy class
\gamma\in\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
is relatively exotic, then its extension to
V
by the identity lying in
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}(V))
is absolutely exotic.

Proof.

This follows from the proof of
1.2
, with
f
,
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff})
, and
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Homeo})
replaced by
\{f_{b}\}_{b\in S^{n}}
,
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff})
, and
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Homeo})
, respectively, where
\{f_{b}\}_{b\in S^{n}}
is a family of diffeomorphisms representing
\gamma
.
∎

Using
4.1
, we can produce many examples of
4
-manifolds that support absolutely exotic homotopy classes in
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff})
while remaining rather small compared with
n
.

Example 4.2
.

Given
n\geq 0
, Auckly–Ruberman
[
3
]
constructed an exotic homotopy class
\gamma\in\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}(Z))
for some closed
4
-manifold
Z
.
For example, one may take

Z=K3\#(n+1)(S^{2}\times S^{2}),

and their construction arises from the fact that the
K3
surface and its logarithmic transforms are exotic.
By their construction of
\gamma
, it is straightforward to see that
\gamma
can be represented by a family of diffeomorphisms
\{f_{b}\}_{b\in S^{n}}\subset\mathrm{Diff}(Z)
whose supports are contained in

W=N(2)\#(n+1)(S^{2}\times S^{2}),

where
N(2)
denotes the Gompf nucleus.
Regard each
f_{b}
as a relative diffeomorphism of
W
, so that
\gamma\in\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
.
Then the non-triviality of
\gamma
in
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}(Z))
immediately implies that
\gamma
is non-trivial in
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
.
On the other hand, since the triviality of
\gamma
in
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Homeo}(Z))
follows from the fact that
N(2)
and its logarithmic transforms are homeomorphic, it follows that
\gamma
is trivial in
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Homeo}_{\partial}(W))
.
Thus
\gamma\in\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}_{\partial}(W))
is relatively exotic.

Let
V
be the
4
-manifold given by
4.1
, which is homotopy equivalent to
W
and contains
W
as an embedded submanifold.
Then
\gamma
extends to an element of
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{Diff}(V))
that is absolutely exotic.

Remark 4.3
.

Enterprising readers can further reduce the support of the aforementioned diffeomorphisms from
W
to
C\#(n+1)S^{2}\times S^{2}
, where
C
is an embedded contractible manifold in

, following the work in
[

]
by the first and the second authors.

Remark 4.4
.

For higher homotopy groups, another example can be obtained from Watanabe’s result
[
16
]
on the rational higher homotopy group of the diffeomorphism group of
S^{4}
, without using our technique.

In
[
16
]
, he provided infinite order elements in
\pi_{i}(\operatorname{Diff}(D^{4},\partial))
for certain positive
i
’s. By using the fibration (
2
) applied for

and the affirmative answer to the Smale conjecture

\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S^{3})\cong SO(4)

by Hatcher
[
5
]
,
since the higher homotopy groups of

are finite, sufficiently high powers of Watanabe’s elements in
\pi_{i}(\operatorname{Diff}_{\partial}(D^{4}))
survive in
\pi_{i}(\operatorname{Diff}(D^{4}))
, which are trivial in
\pi_{i}(\operatorname{Homeo}_{\partial}(D^{4}))

\pi_{i}(\operatorname{Diff}(D^{4}))\to\pi_{i}(\operatorname{Homeo}(D^{4}))

is not finite for certain positive

’s.

Threshold dynamics in time-delay systems: polynomial β \beta -control in a pressing process and connections to blow-up
Masato Kimura, Hirotaka Kuma, Yikan Liu, Kazunori Matsui, Masahiro Yamamoto, Zhenxing Yang

Abstract
This paper addresses a press control problem in straightening machines with small time delays due to system communication. To handle this, we propose a generalized β \beta -control method, which replaces conventional linear velocity control with a polynomial of degree β ≥ 1 \beta\geq 1 . The resulting model is a delay differential equation (DDE), for which we derive basic properties through nondimensionalization and analysis. Numerical experiments suggest the existence of a threshold initial velocity separating overshoot and non-overshoot dynamics, which we formulate as a conjecture. Based on this, we design a control algorithm under velocity constraints and confirm its effectiveness. We also highlight a connection between threshold behavior and finite-time blow-up in DDEs. This study provides a practical control strategy and contributes new insights into threshold dynamics and blow-up phenomena in delay systems.



Keywords:
Press control, Delay differential equation, Blow-up

Introduction

Optimal control is essential for modern automated manufacturing, where increasingly sophisticated technologies demand maximum efficiency. Although control theory has a long history, it continues to evolve in response to technological advancements. The mathematical modeling and analysis of the target system play a critical role in developing effective control algorithms. When the process at the core of the system is described by a well-structured mathematical model, it becomes possible to propose robust strategies for optimal control.

One of the most widely used methods is PID control
[
1
,
3
]
, which remains prevalent in industrial applications. However, time delays—arising from communication and computation in feedback loops—can destabilize such systems, leading to overshoot, instability, or even chaotic behavior.

Delay differential equations (DDEs)
[
2
,
11
]
provide a mathematical framework for modeling systems with time delays and have been extensively studied, especially in engineering contexts
[
6
,
10
]
. Nonetheless, the dynamics of DDEs are often difficult to analyze, even for simple cases. Challenges such as instability, chaos
[
7
,
13
,
14
]
, and finite-time blow-up
[
5
,
4
,
8
]
remain active areas of research.

In this study, we address a press control problem in a straightening machine, modeled by a specific class of DDEs, where the initial velocity serves as the control variable. We first derive a nondimensionalized DDE model and clarify the basic mathematical properties of its solutions. Numerical experiments suggest the existence of a critical threshold for the initial value that separates two types of behavior: overshooting and asymptotic convergence. We formulate this observation as a conjecture and, under its validity, propose an effective control algorithm. This method also accommodates a constraint on maximum velocity.

Moreover, the conjectured threshold phenomenon has another implication. It enables an interpretation of the system’s behavior in terms of finite-time blow-up. We demonstrate a connection between the press control problem and blow-up dynamics, offering new insight into blow-up phenomena in DDEs.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section
2
introduces the industrial context and formulates the control problem based on empirical data, presenting a generalized
\beta
-control model. Section
3
derives the nondimensionalized DDE, analyzes its mathematical properties, and proposes the central conjecture based on numerical evidence. Section
4
applies the conjecture to construct a practical control algorithm under a maximum velocity constraint, validated through further simulations. Section
5
reinterprets these results as finite-time blow-up phenomena for a related DDE, allowing us to analyze blow-up rates and threshold behavior. Finally, Section
6
summarizes our results and reflects on the broader significance of this work, illustrating how a practical industrial problem can lead to new mathematical developments in control and delay systems.

2
Industrial Background and Problem Formulation

2.1
Straightening press and process description

We focus on a straightening press machine used to correct bends in heat-treated automotive shafts. These shafts often exhibit residual deformation after heat treatment, which can lead to undesirable vibration and noise during operation. Thus, precise straightening is necessary. The straightening process typically involves the following steps:

1.

Measure the shaft strain using several sensors placed along its length.

2.

Determine the pressing position, direction, and stroke length required to straighten the shaft.

3.

Control the press machine to perform pressing according to the parameters decided in step 2.

4.

Repeat steps 1–3 until the strain falls below the specified tolerance.

For more details on shaft strain measurement, see
[
9
,
12
,
15
]
.
In this study, we focus on step 3: determining how to accurately press the shaft by a prescribed stroke. In particular, we consider the case where the press velocity

V
is determined by the following generalized control rule:

\displaystyle V(X)=v_{0}\left|\frac{\ell-X}{\ell}\right|^{\beta},

(2.1)

where
\beta\geq 1
,

is the initial velocity,

\ell
is the target pressing stroke, and

is the position measured by the sensor
at time
t\geq 0
with the initial position
X(0)=0
.

We refer to this control law as the

In conventional control systems, a simpler rule with exponent

Our proposed method generalizes this by introducing the exponent

3
Nondimensionalized DDE Model

3.1
Scaling and basic properties

The system (
2.3
) together with the initial condition (
2.4
) is equivalent to the following problem.
For
v_{0},\ell,\tau>0
with
v_{0}\tau<\ell
, and
\beta\geq 1
, find
x:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{dx}{dt}(t)&=v_{0}\left|\frac{\ell-x(t-\tau)}{\ell}\right|^{\beta}&&(t>\tau),\\
x(t)&=v_{0}t&&(0\leq t\leq\tau).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

To nondimensionalize system (
3.1
), we introduce the following dimensionless quantities:

\displaystyle s\coloneqq\frac{t}{\tau},\quad u(s)\coloneqq\frac{x(\tau s)}{\ell},\quad w_{0}\coloneqq\frac{\tau v_{0}}{\ell}\in(0,1).

(3.2)

Then, for all
s>1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{du}{ds}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau}{\ell}\frac{dx}{dt}(\tau s)=\frac{\tau v_{0}}{\ell}\left|\frac{\ell-x(\tau s-\tau)}{\ell}\right|^{\beta}

\displaystyle=w_{0}\left|1-u(s-1)\right|^{\beta},

while for
0\leq s\leq 1
, it follows that

u(s)=\frac{x(\tau s)}{\ell}=\frac{v_{0}\tau s}{\ell}=w_{0}s.

Hence, substituting (
3.2
) into (
3.1
) gives the following nondimensionalized system:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{du}{ds}(s)&=w_{0}\left|1-u(s-1)\right|^{\beta}&&(s>1),\\
u(s)&=w_{0}s&&(0\leq s\leq 1),\end{aligned}\right.

with
0<w_{0}<1
.

For convenience in the discussion that follows, we rename the independent variable
s
back to
t
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{du}{dt}(t)&=w_{0}\left|1-u(t-1)\right|^{\beta}&&(t>1),\\
u(t)&=w_{0}t&&(0\leq t\leq 1).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
\beta>0
and
w_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a unique function
u\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
that satisfies (
3.3
).

Proof.

Define
f(u)\coloneqq w_{0}|1-u|^{\beta}
. Clearly,
f\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
.
We construct
u(t)
inductively. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define

u(t)=u(k)+\int_{k}^{t}f(u(s-1))\,ds\quad\text{for }t\in[k,k+1],

where
u(t)=w_{0}t
for
t\in[0,1]
.
This defines a continuous function
u\in C^{0}([0,\infty))
satisfying
\frac{du}{dt}(t)=f(u(t-1))
for
t\in(1,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
, and hence
u\in C^{1}([1,\infty))
.

To show differentiability at
t=1
, note that

\frac{d^{\pm}u}{dt}(1)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to+0}\frac{u(1\pm\varepsilon)-u(1)}{\pm\varepsilon}=w_{0},

so
u\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
, as desired.
∎

In the remainder of this section, we restrict attention to the case
\beta\geq 1
,
w_{0}\in(0,1)
. The solution of (
3.3
) will henceforth be denoted by
u(t;\beta,w_{0})
.

From the first equation in (
3.3
), it is evident that
u
is non-decreasing. The next theorem shows that the derivative
\frac{du}{dt}(t)
never vanishes on an interval, implying that
u(t)
cannot remain constant at 1 over any nontrivial interval. It may approach 1 asymptotically but never reach a plateau.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
w_{0}>0
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a+1<b
.
Suppose
u\in C^{0}([a,b])\cap C^{1}([a+1,b])
, and assume

u(t)<1\quad\text{for all }t\in[a,a+1],

and

\displaystyle\frac{du}{dt}(t)=w_{0}\left|1-u(t-1)\right|^{\beta}\quad(a+1\leq t\leq b).

(3.4)

If there exists
t_{*}\in(a+1,b)
such that
u(t_{*})=1
, then the following hold:

\displaystyle\frac{du}{dt}(t_{*})>0,\quad u(t)\begin{cases}<1&(a\leq t<t_{*}),\\
=1&(t=t_{*}),\\
>1&(t_{*}<t\leq b).\end{cases}

(3.5)

Proof.

We first show
u(t)<1
for all
t\in[a,t_{*})
.
Since
u(t)<1
for
t\in[a,a+1]
, it suffices to consider
t\in(a+1,t_{*})
.
Suppose for contradiction that there exists
c\in(a+1,t_{*})
such that
u(c)=1
.
Since (
3.4
) implies
\frac{du}{dt}(t)\geq 0
on
[a+1,b]
,
u
is non-decreasing there, so
u(t)=1
for all
t\in[c,t_{*}]
.

Then
\frac{du}{dt}(t)=0
on
[c,t_{*}]
, and (
3.4
) yields
u(t-1)=1
for
t\in[c,t_{*}]
, i.e.,
u(s)=1
for
s\in[c-1,t_{*}-1]
.

If
c-1>a+1
, we can repeat this argument with the interval
[c-1,t_{*}-1]
in place of
[c,t_{*}]
.
Continuing inductively, we eventually reach
u(s)=1
for some
s\in[a,a+1]
, contradicting the assumption.
Hence
u(t)<1
for all
t\in[a,t_{*})
.

Now for
t\in[a+1,t_{*}+1)\cap[a+1,b]
, it follows that

\frac{du}{dt}(t)=w_{0}\left|1-u(t-1)\right|^{\beta}>0,

in particular
\frac{du}{dt}(t_{*})>0
.
Integrating over any small interval after
t_{*}
, we obtain
u(t)>1
for all
t\in(t_{*},b]
, completing the proof.
∎

\displaystyle 0<w_{0}<\tilde{w}_{0}<1

In other words, if
w_{0}>g(\beta)

overshoots the value 1. If
w_{0}\leq g(\beta)
,

remains strictly less than 1 and approaches 1 asymptotically as

3.3
Conjecture

Based on the numerical experiments presented in Subsection
3.2
, we propose the following conjecture regarding the dynamics of the DDE (
3.3
).

Conjecture 3.3
.

For
\beta\geq 1
, there exists a unique value
g(\beta)\in(0,1)
such that the solution
u(t;\beta,w_{0})
of (
3.3
) exhibits the following behavior:

(i)

If
w_{0}\in(g(\beta),1)
, there exists a constant
T_{*}=T_{*}(\beta,w_{0})>0
such that

\displaystyle u(t;\beta,w_{0})\begin{cases}<1&\text{for }0\leq t<T_{*},\\
=1&\text{at }t=T_{*},\\
>1&\text{for }t>T_{*}.\end{cases}

(ii)

If
w_{0}\in(0,g(\beta)]
, then

\displaystyle u(t;\beta,w_{0})\begin{cases}<1&\text{for }t\geq 0,\\
\to 1&\text{as }t\to\infty.\end{cases}

(iii)

The comparison properties (
3.2
) and (
3.9
) hold.

Cases (i) and (ii) describe the two qualitatively distinct behaviors observed in our numerical simulations: overshooting and non-overshooting, respectively. The critical value
g(\beta)
serves as a threshold separating these behaviors.

The monotonicity property (
3.2
) with respect to the initial value in (iii) enables the use of the bisection method to numerically identify
g(\beta)
.
It is worth noting that this monotonicity has been proven analytically for the interval
0<t\leq 2
:

Proposition 3.4
.

Fix
\beta\geq 1
. If

\displaystyle 0<w_{0}<\tilde{w}_{0}<1,

then

\displaystyle u(t;\beta,w_{0})<u(t;\beta,\tilde{w}_{0})\quad\text{for }0<t\leq 2.

Proof.

Since the case
0<t\leq 1
follows directly from the initial condition, we focus on the interval
1<t\leq 2
.

Let
u(t)\coloneqq u(t;\beta,w_{0})
. For
t\in[1,2]
, we compute

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=u(1)+\int_{1}^{t}w_{0}\left(1-w_{0}(s-1)\right)^{\beta}\,ds

\displaystyle=w_{0}+\frac{1}{\beta+1}\left(1-\left(1-w_{0}(t-1)\right)^{\beta+1}\right),

which is monotonically increasing with respect to
w_{0}
for fixed
t\in[1,2]
, proving the result.
∎

This conjecture is also related to the mathematical analysis of finite-time blow-up phenomena in delay differential equations, which will be further discussed in Section
5
.

Applications

We consider the press control problem under a given maximum velocity constraint. In practical settings, press machines have a maximum velocity
v_{\rm max}
due to safety and mechanical limitations. If
\beta
is fixed, selecting the initial velocity as
\ell G/\tau~(G\coloneqq g(\beta))
is optimal according to (
3.10
). Therefore, if
\ell G/\tau\leq v_{\rm max}
, i.e.,
\ell\leq\ell_{1}\coloneqq\tau v_{\rm max}/G

\displaystyle V(X)=\frac{\ell G}{\tau}\left|\frac{\ell-X}{\ell}\right|^{\beta}.

(4.1)

Here,
X

2.2
) and is set to
0
for

V(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{\rm max}&\text{if }\ell_{1}<\ell-X,\\
{\displaystyle v_{\rm max}\left|\frac{\ell-X}{\ell}\right|^{\beta}}&\text{if }\ell_{1}\geq\ell-X.\end{array}\right.

This can be summarized more compactly as:

\displaystyle V(X)=v_{0}\left|\frac{\min\{\ell_{1},\ell-X\}}{\min\{\ell_{1},\ell\}}\right|^{\beta},

5
Blow-up Phenomenon of DDEs

As mentioned in the introduction, the behavior of DDE solutions is complex, and many aspects remain unclear. For example, regarding the finite-time blow-up phenomenon of solutions,
[
5
,
8
]
analyze DDEs of the type

\frac{dz}{dt}(t)=f(z(t))h(z(t-1)).

In this section, we explain how the mathematical analysis and overshoot conjecture for DDEs in Section
3
can be applied to the blow-up problem for solutions of DDEs of the above type.

For the nondimensionalized system (
3.3
), applying the variable transformation
z=\frac{1}{1-u}
and noting that
\frac{du}{dt}=z^{-2}\frac{dz}{dt}
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{dz}{dt}(t)&=w_{0}\frac{(z(t))^{2}}{(z(t-1))^{\beta}}&&\text{if }t>1,\\
z(t)&=\frac{1}{1-w_{0}t}&&\text{if }0\leq t\leq 1,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

In this formulation, whether or not
u
overshoots (i.e., exceeds 1) corresponds to whether or not
z
blows up in finite time.

From Theorem
3.2
, we can deduce that if blow-up occurs, the blow-up rate satisfies
z(t)\sim\frac{c}{t_{*}-t}
as
t\to t_{*}^{-}
, which is of the same order as the blow-up rate of the ordinary differential equation
z^{\prime}(t)=\alpha z(t)^{2}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
h\in C^{0}((0,\infty))
with
h(s)>0
for
s>0
.
Let
a,t_{*}\in\mathbb{R}
with
a+1<t_{*}
.
Assume that

z\in C^{0}([a,t_{*}))\cap C^{1}([a+1,t_{*}))
,

z(t)>0
, and

\displaystyle\frac{dz}{dt}(t)=(z(t))^{2}h(z(t-1))\quad(a+1\leq t<t_{*}).

If
z(t)\to\infty
as
t\to t_{*}^{-}
, then

\displaystyle z(t)=\frac{c}{t_{*}-t}+o\left(\frac{1}{t_{*}-t}\right)\quad\text{as }t\to t_{*}^{-},

(5.2)

where
c\coloneqq h(z(t_{*}-1))^{-1}>0
, and
o
is Landau’s symbol.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle y(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}{\displaystyle-\frac{1}{z(t)}}&(a\leq t<t_{*})\\
0&(t=t_{*}).\end{cases}

Then
y\in C^{0}([a,t_{*}])
and
y(t_{*})=0
.

For
a+1\leq t<t_{*}
, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{dy}{dt}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z(t)^{2}}\cdot\frac{dz}{dt}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z(t)^{2}}\cdot(z(t))^{2}h(z(t-1))

\displaystyle=h(z(t-1)).

In particular, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to t_{*}^{-}}\frac{dy}{dt}(t)=h(z(t_{*}-1))=c^{-1}>0.

Thus,
y\in C^{1}([a+1,t^{*}])
and
\frac{dy}{dt}(t_{*})=c^{-1}
.
Hence, we compute

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to t_{*}^{-}}(t_{*}-t)z(t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to t_{*}^{-}}\frac{t_{*}-t}{-y(t)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to t_{*}^{-}}\frac{t_{*}-t}{y(t_{*})-y(t)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dy}{dt}(t_{*})\right)^{-1}=c,

which implies the asymptotic behavior (
5.2
).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

In Theorem
5.1
, if
h(s)=w_{0}s^{-\beta}
, then for
t\in(t_{*}-\varepsilon,t_{*})
(with
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small), the function
u(t)=1+y(t)
satisfies
u(t)\in(0,1)
and

\displaystyle\frac{du}{dt}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{dy}{dt}(t)=h(z(t-1))

\displaystyle=h\left(\frac{1}{1-u(t-1)}\right)=w_{0}(1-u(t-1))^{\beta},

which is exactly the same equation as (
3.4
).
This shows that Theorem
5.1
is simply an alternative formulation of Theorem
3.2
.

Furthermore, Conjecture
3.3
suggests a complete characterization of blow-up behavior in terms of the initial parameter
w_{0}
. Specifically, it predicts the existence of a critical threshold
g(\beta)
that determines whether finite-time blow-up occurs. Using the transformation
z=\frac{1}{1-u}
, the behavior described in Conjecture
3.3
can be directly translated into statements about the blow-up properties and comparison principles for solutions of (
5.1
).

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that Conjecture
3.3
holds.
Let
g(\beta)
and
T_{*}=T_{*}(\beta,w_{0})>0
be as described in Conjecture
3.3
.
For
\beta\geq 1
, the solution
z(t;\beta,w_{0})
of (
5.1
) satisfies the following properties.

(i)

For
w_{0}\in(g(\beta),1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle z(t;\beta,w_{0})\begin{cases}<\infty&(0\leq t<T_{*})\\
\to\infty&(t\to T_{*}^{-}).\end{cases}

(ii)

For
w_{0}\in(0,g(\beta)]
, it holds that

\displaystyle z(t;\beta,w_{0})\begin{cases}<\infty&(t\geq 0)\\
\to\infty&(\text{as }t\to\infty).\end{cases}

(iii)

The comparison properties

\displaystyle w_{0}<\tilde{w}_{0}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\,

\displaystyle z(t;\beta,w_{0})<z(t;\beta,\tilde{w}_{0})\quad(t>0)

and

\displaystyle\beta<\tilde{\beta}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\,

\displaystyle z(t;\beta,g(\beta))<z(t;\tilde{\beta},g(\tilde{\beta}))\quad(t>0)

hold.

6
From Industrial Challenge to Mathematical Development

In this study, we investigated the press control problem in straightening machines—used to reduce post-heat-treatment warping and improve the linear accuracy of automotive shafts—from a mathematical viewpoint. Among various factors affecting the control performance, we focused on the small but non-negligible time delay caused by communication and information processing between the machine and the control system. This analysis was grounded in empirical data from actual industrial equipment. To address this, we proposed a novel control strategy that generalizes conventional linear velocity control to a polynomial
\beta
-control of arbitrary degree
\beta\geq 1
.

The proposed control strategy led to a mathematical formulation as a delay differential equation (DDE) problem, with the initial velocity serving as a control parameter. In Subsection
3.1
, we introduced a nondimensionalized version of the model and rigorously established several fundamental mathematical properties: the global existence of a unique solution, and the characterization of overshoot behavior. These results provide a solid theoretical foundation for analyzing the control problem.

In Subsection
3.2
, numerical simulations revealed the existence of a threshold initial velocity
w_{0}=g(\beta)
that determines whether overshoot occurs, depending on the value of
\beta
. These observations were formulated as a mathematical conjecture in Subsection
3.3
, which also included partial evidence supporting its validity.

In Section
4
, we proposed a new algorithm for the press control problem under a maximum velocity constraint, utilizing this conjecture, and demonstrated its effectiveness through numerical examples. Furthermore, in Section
5
, we showed that our analysis and the conjecture in Section
3
are closely related to the finite-time blow-up phenomenon in the solutions of DDEs, derived an estimate of the blow-up rate, and showed the existence of a threshold value that would hold if the conjecture is true.

This study not only addressed a real-world industrial challenge through mathematical modeling and control design, but also contributed new theoretical insights into the dynamics of delay differential equations.
It is widely recognized that the collaboration between industry and mathematics will become increasingly important in the future. Though modest, we believe this study represents a meaningful first step in contributing to such interdisciplinary efforts.

As for future mathematical directions, a rigorous proof of Conjecture
3.3
remains an important task. Additionally, the relationship between control and blow-up behavior in DDEs (Section
5
) is still an underexplored area, and further research is highly anticipated.

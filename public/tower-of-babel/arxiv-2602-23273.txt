Extrinsic Spin Splitter Currents in Altermagnets
Sanjay Sarkar, Sayan Sarkar, Amit agarwal

Abstract
Altermagnets exhibit momentum-dependent spin splitting despite having zero net magnetization. This enables a spin-splitter effect in which an external electric field generates transverse spin currents by separating oppositely polarized carriers. Here, we develop a unified semiclassical theory of linear extrinsic spin-splitter currents, incorporating impurity-induced side-jump and skew-scattering contributions, and apply it to the d d -wave altermagnet FeSb 2 \text{FeSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} . We demonstrate that asymmetric impurity scattering provides a dominant channel for spin-splitter currents. Remarkably, the resulting extrinsic spin conductivity is time-reversal even, in contrast to previously studied spin-splitter responses arising from symmetric scattering.



I
Introduction

Altermagnetism has recently emerged as a distinct class of magnetic order that combines key characteristics of both ferromagnets and antiferromagnets
Hayami et al. 2019
;
Šmejkal et al. 2022a
;
Šmejkal et al. 2022b
;
Roig et al. 2024
;
Song et al. 2025
;
Mostovoy 2025
. Altermagnets are collinear antiferromagnets with zero net magnetization, yet they exhibit pronounced momentum-dependent spin splitting of electronic bands, reminiscent of ferromagnets
Yuan et al. 2024
;
Zeng et al. 2025
;
Yang et al. 2025
;
Ma et al. 2025
;
Lee et al. 2025
. In contrast to conventional antiferromagnets, where opposite spin sublattices are related by translation or inversion, the magnetic sublattices in altermagnets are connected by crystal rotation symmetries combined with spin operations
Šmejkal et al. 2022a
;
Turek 2022
;
Jungwirth et al. 2024
;
Bhowal and Spaldin 2024
. This unconventional symmetry structure leads to strongly spin-polarized itinerant electrons even in the absence of significant spin–orbit coupling (SOC) and gives rise to unconventional spin transport phenomena
Karube et al. 2022
;
Das et al. 2023
;
Lyu and Li 2024
;
Kapri 2025
;
Zarzuela et al. 2025
;
Shi et al. 2025
;
Fu et al. 2025
;
Sarkar et al. 2025a
.

A direct consequence of momentum-dependent spin splitting in altermagnets is the spin splitter effect, in which the spin-dependent anisotropy of the electronic band structure allows an external electric field to drive carriers with opposite spin polarization in different directions, generating transverse spin currents without net charge flow
Zhang et al. 2018
;
González-Hernández et al. 2021
. Owing to its nonrelativistic origin and symmetry protection, the spin splitter effect is fundamentally distinct from conventional spin Hall responses driven by relativistic SOC
Hirsch 1999
;
Sinova et al. 2015
;
Zheng et al. 2024
;
Roy et al. 2022
;
Sarkar et al. 2025b
. Recent theoretical studies have explored spin splitter currents arising from intrinsic band-structure effects as well as from symmetric impurity scattering
González-Hernández et al. 2021
;
Yi et al. 2025
;
Yang et al. 2026
. However, a systematic understanding of disorder-induced extrinsic contributions remains incomplete. In realistic materials, impurity scattering is unavoidable and can introduce additional transport channels that qualitatively modify spin responses. In particular, extrinsic mechanisms such as side-jump and skew scattering
Du et al. 2019
;
Datta et al. 2024
;
Ahmed et al. 2025
, arising from coordinate shifts during scattering events and asymmetric scattering probabilities, have not yet been systematically investigated for spin-splitter currents in alternagnets. Understanding these contributions is crucial for establishing a complete description of spin transport in altermagnets.

Here, we address this gap by developing a semiclassical theory of the linear extrinsic spin splitter current and applying it to the
d
-wave altermagnet FeSb
2
. We demonstrate that asymmetric impurity scattering can generate sizable spin splitter currents and, in experimentally relevant regimes, even dominate over intrinsic contributions. Remarkably, we find that these scattering induced responses are governed by band-geometric quantities, including the Berry curvature (BC) and spin Berry curvature (SBC), and are
\mathcal{T}
-even in nature, in contrast to earlier studies
González-Hernández et al. 2021
. Furthermore, we analyze the role of SOC and show that these extrinsic contributions are finite only in its presence.

II
Semiclassical description of spin current

In this section, we first define the spin current operator, then derive the Boltzmann equation with symmetric and asymmetric scattering contributions and use it to obtain explicit expressions for intrinsic and extrinsic spin conductivities.

The spin current in a weakly disordered crystal subject to a weak electric field
{\bm{E}}
, arises from both the field-induced and disorder-induced modifications of the carrier dynamics. Within the Boltzmann transport framework, the expectation value of the spin current is expressed as,

J_{a}^{\nu}=\sum_{l}j_{a,l}^{\nu}f_{l}~,

(1)

where
a
denotes the direction of flow and
\nu
the direction of spin polarization. The summation is over the Bloch carrier states
\ket{l}=\ket{n {\bm k}}
, with
n
and
{\bm{k}}
being the band index and crystal momentum, respectively. In Eq. (
1
),
f_{l}
is the Fermi–Dirac distribution, and
j_{a,l}^{\nu}
is the quantum-mechanical expectation value of the spin-current operator in state
l
.

The total spin-current operator, including corrections to the Bloch state induced by the external electric field
Zhang et al. 2024
and impurity scattering
Xiao and Niu 2017
, can be naturally decomposed as,

j_{a,l}^{\nu}=j_{a,l}^{\nu,0}+j_{a,l}^{\nu,{\rm anm}}+j_{a,l}^{\nu,{\rm sj}}~.

(2)

Here,
j_{a,l}^{\nu,0}=\langle l|\hat{j}_{a}^{\nu}|l\rangle
is the expectation value of the spin-current operator in the unperturbed Bloch state. The spin current operator,
\hat{j}_{a}^{\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}\{\hat{s}^{\nu},\hat{v}^{a}\}
, is defined as the anticommutator of the spin (
\hat{s}^{\nu}
) and velocity operators (
\hat{v}^{a}
).

The field-induced correction
j_{a,l}^{\nu,{\rm anm}}
captures the anomalous contribution arising from the interband coherence in the presence of an external electric field. It is given by

j_{a,l}^{\nu,{\rm anm}}=-\frac{e}{\hbar}E_{b}\Omega_{l}^{\nu,ab},

(3)

where

\Omega^{\nu,ab}_{l}=-2\hbar^{2}\,\mathrm{Im}\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n}\frac{(j_{a}^{\nu})_{nn^{\prime}}({\bm{k}})\,v^{b}_{n^{\prime}n}({\bm{k}})}{(\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}{\bm{k}}})^{2}}~,

(4)

is the
spin Berry curvature
(SBC). It captures the band geometric origin of the intrinsic spin Hall effect
Sinova et al. 2004
;
Guo et al. 2005
;
Guo et al. 2008
;
Sun et al. 2016
. In Eq. (
4
), for a given operator
\hat{A}
, we define

A_{n^{\prime}n}(\mathbf{k})\equiv\langle u_{n^{\prime}\mathbf{k}}|\hat{A}|u_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
,
where
|u_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
denotes the cell periodic part of the unperturbed Bloch state
\ket{n\bm k}
.

Additionally, impurity scattering modifies the spin current through a side-jump mechanism associated with coordinate shifts of itinerant spins during scattering. The extrinsic side-jump contribution
j_{a,l}^{\nu,{\rm sj}}
represents the spin-current analogue of the side-jump velocity. It is defined as
Xiao and Niu 2017
;
Xiao et al. 2019a

\displaystyle j^{\nu,{\rm sj}}_{a,l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\pi\sum_{n^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}W_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\delta(\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})

(5)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\Bigg[\sum_{n^{\prime\prime}\neq n^{\prime}}\frac{\langle u_{n{\bm{k}}}|u_{n^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}|u_{n{\bm{k}}}\rangle(j_{a}^{\nu})_{n^{\prime}n^{\prime\prime}}({\bm{k}^{\prime}})}{\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{n^{\prime\prime}\neq n}\frac{\langle u_{n^{\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}}|u_{n^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}|u_{n{\bm{k}}}\rangle(j_{a}^{\nu})_{nn^{\prime\prime}}({\bm{k}})}{\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}}}\Bigg].

Here,
W_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}=W_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime},{\bm{k}}}

is the Born amplitude describing impurity scattering.
For static impurities,
W_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}=\langle|V^{0}_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}|^{2}\rangle_{\rm dis}
,
with
\langle\cdots\rangle_{\rm dis}
denoting configurational averaging over random impurity distributions. The detailed derivation of Eq. (
5
) is provided in Appendix
D
.

Semiclassical Boltzmann equation

To describe disorder-induced spin transport, we employ the semiclassical Boltzmann transport formalism. In the presence of elastic impurity scattering and homogeneous perturbations, the non-equilibrium occupation function
f_{l}
satisfies
Ashcroft and Mermin 1976

\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial t}+\dot{\bm{k}}\!\cdot\!\partial_{\bm{k}}f_{l}=I_{\text{el}}\{f_{l}\}~.

(6)

Here,
I_{\text{el}}\{f_{l}\}
is the elastic collision integral accounting for disorder induced scattering between Bloch states. Using the Born approximation, it is given by

I_{\text{el}}\{f_{l}\}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}(w_{l^{\prime}l}f_{l}-w_{ll^{\prime}}f_{l^{\prime}})~.

(7)

The scattering rate
w_{ll^{\prime}}
between states
l
and
l^{\prime}
is governed by Fermi’s Golden rule
Ashcroft and Mermin 1976
,

w_{ll^{\prime}}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\Big\langle\big|\langle l|V_{\text{imp}}|{l^{\prime}}^{\rm dis}\rangle\big|^{2}\Big\rangle_{\text{dis}}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})~.

(8)

Here,
V_{\mathrm{imp}}
represents the impurity potential, and
|{l^{\prime}}^{\rm dis}\rangle
is the eigenstate of the full Hamiltonian
H(\bm{k})=H_{0}(\bm{k})+V_{\mathrm{imp}}
. It satisfies the Lippmann-Schwinger equation
Sakurai and Napolitano 2017
,

|l^{\rm dis}\rangle=|l\rangle+\frac{V_{\text{imp}}}{\varepsilon_{l}-H_{0}+i\eta}~|l^{\rm dis}\rangle~,

(9)

where the infinitesimal
i\eta\to 0^{+}
ensures the outgoing boundary condition for the scattering states.

Although microscopic time-reversal symmetry enforces constraints on scattering amplitudes, disorder averaging and higher-order processes can generate antisymmetric components in the effective scattering rate. Thus, in general
w_{ll^{\prime}}\neq w_{l^{\prime}l}
.
It is therefore convenient to decompose the scattering rate into its symmetric and antisymmetric parts as

w_{ll^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{S}}=w_{l^{\prime}l}^{\mathrm{S}}=\frac{w_{ll^{\prime}}+w_{l^{\prime}l}}{2}~,~~w_{ll^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{A}}=-w_{l^{\prime}l}^{\mathrm{A}}=\frac{w_{ll^{\prime}}-w_{l^{\prime}l}}{2}~.

(10)

The symmetric part
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{S}}
describes conventional relaxation processes,
typically treated within the relaxation-time approximation.
In contrast, the antisymmetric component
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{A}}
gives rise to skew-scattering contributions.

For weak disorder, the scattering rate can be systematically expanded in powers of the impurity potential. Retaining terms up to fourth order in the impurity potential
Xiao et al. 2019b
, we have

w_{l^{\prime}l}\approx w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(2)}+w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(3),A}+w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(4),A}~.

(11)

Here,
w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(3),A}
and
w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(4),A}
denote the antisymmetric contributions arising at third and fourth order, respectively. These terms give rise to skew-scattering effects. The symmetric parts of these higher-order terms merely renormalize the leading second-order contribution and are therefore neglected.

The second-order term can be further decomposed as

w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(2)}=w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(\rm 2S)}+w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(2),\mathrm{cs}}~,

(12)

with the symmetric field-independent part

w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(\rm 2S)}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\langle|V_{l^{\prime}l}|^{2}\rangle_{\rm dis}\,\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})~,

(13)

and

w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(2),\mathrm{cs}}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\langle|V_{l^{\prime}l}|^{2}\rangle_{\rm dis}\,\frac{\partial\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})}{\partial\varepsilon_{l}}\,e{\bm{E}}\cdot\delta{\bm{r}}_{l^{\prime}l}~,

(14)

captures the coordinate-shift correction induced by the external electric field
\bm{E}
. This term originates from the real-space displacement of the electron wave packet during an impurity scattering event. The coordinate shift
\delta\bm{r}_{l^{\prime}l}
of the wave packet is given by
Sinitsyn et al. 2006

\delta{\bm{r}}_{l^{\prime}l}=\langle u_{l^{\prime}}|i\partial_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{l^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle u_{l}|i\partial_{\bm{k}}|u_{l}\rangle-(\partial_{\bm{k}}+\partial_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\arg(V_{l^{\prime}l})~.

(15)

Here, the operator “
\arg
” denotes the phase (argument) of a complex number. This coordinate shift plays a crucial role in the side-jump contribution to spin transport. Having categorized the scattering rates by symmetry and physical origin, we next decompose the collision integral and the nonequilibrium distribution function into intrinsic, side-jump, and skew-scattering parts.

Building on the different physical origins of the impurity scattering rates discussed above, the associated elastic collision integral can be naturally decomposed into distinct contributions. In particular, we separate the collision term into intrinsic, side-jump, and skew-scattering components,
I_{\mathrm{el}}(f_{l})=I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{in}}(f_{l})+I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sj}}(f_{l})+I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk}}(f_{l}),

and these distinct components have the form
Du et al. 2019

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{in}}(f_{l})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{ll^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{2S}}(f_{l}-f_{l^{\prime}})~,

(16a)

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sj}}(f_{l})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2),\mathrm{cs}}(f_{l}-f_{l^{\prime}})~,

(16b)

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk}}(f_{l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{ll^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{A}}(f_{l}+f_{l^{\prime}})~.

(16c)

Using the antisymmetry property

w_{ll^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{A}}=-w_{l^{\prime}l}^{\mathrm{A}}
,
the skew-scattering collision term reduces to a symmetric combination of distribution functions,

(f_{l}+f_{l^{\prime}})
. The intrinsic term

I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{in}}

originates from symmetric second-order scattering and governs conventional momentum relaxation. The side-jump contribution

I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sj}}

arises from the coordinate-shift correction to scattering in the presence of an external electric field, while the skew-scattering term

I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk}}

reflects the antisymmetric part of the scattering rate. As discussed in Section
II.1
, the antisymmetric scattering rate

w_{ll^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{A}}
originates from higher-order impurity scattering and can be further decomposed into third- and fourth-order contributions [see Eq. (
11
)].

Accordingly, the skew-scattering collision integral naturally splits into two physically distinct channels
Guo et al. 2024
,

I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk}}(f_{l})=I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk3}}(f_{l})+I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk4}}(f_{l})~,

(17)

with

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk3}}(f_{l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3),A}(f_{l}+f_{l^{\prime}})~,

(18a)

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk4}}(f_{l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(4),A}(f_{l}+f_{l^{\prime}})~.

(18b)

Here, the term

I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk3}}
describes the non-Gaussian skew-scattering contribution arising at third order in the impurity potential. In contrast,

I_{\mathrm{el}}^{\mathrm{sk4}}
represents the Gaussian skew scattering contribution originating from fourth-order scattering processes.

Combining all these terms, the nonequilibrium distribution function can be expanded as,

f_{l}=f_{l}^{\mathrm{in}}+f_{l}^{\mathrm{sj}}+f_{l}^{\mathrm{sk3}}+f_{l}^{\mathrm{sk4}}~.

(19)

Here,
f_{l}^{\mathrm{in}}
represents the intrinsic part determined by symmetric scattering, while the remaining terms encode disorder-induced corrections linear in
{\bm{E}}
. Considering the steady state distribution function with
\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial t}=0
and substituting Eq. (
19
) into the Boltzmann equation yields the coupled relations

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{k}}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}f^{\mathrm{in}}_{l}

\displaystyle=I^{\mathrm{in}}_{\mathrm{el}}(f^{\mathrm{in}}_{l})~,

(20a)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{k}}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}f^{\mathrm{sj}}_{l}

\displaystyle=I^{\mathrm{in}}_{\mathrm{el}}(f^{\mathrm{sj}}_{l})+I^{\mathrm{sj}}_{\mathrm{el}}(f^{\mathrm{in}}_{l})~,

(20b)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{k}}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}f^{\mathrm{sk3}}_{l}

\displaystyle=I^{\mathrm{in}}_{\mathrm{el}}(f^{\mathrm{sk3}}_{l})+I^{\mathrm{sk3}}_{\mathrm{el}}(f^{\mathrm{in}}_{l})~,

(20c)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{k}}\cdot\partial_{\bm{k}}f^{\mathrm{sk4}}_{l}

\displaystyle=I^{\mathrm{in}}_{\mathrm{el}}(f^{\mathrm{sk4}}_{l})+I^{\mathrm{sk4}}_{\mathrm{el}}(f^{\mathrm{in}}_{l})~.

(20d)

Here, we have neglected the mixed terms between side-jump and skew-scattering channels as their effects are subleading in the weak-disorder limit. We solve these coupled equations perturbatively in Appendix
A
to obtain linear-order corrections to
f_{l}
, and use them to determine the intrinsic and extrinsic contributions to the spin splitter current. With the nonequilibrium distribution function determined for each scattering channel, we are now in a position to evaluate the corresponding contributions to the linear spin current and spin conductivity.

II.3
Linear spin current and spin conductivity

We now evaluate each linear spin conductivity contribution explicitly and identify its scaling with the relaxation time and its symmetry character. Expanding the general expression for the spin current [Eq. (
1
)] to first order in
{\bm{E}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle j_{a}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\Big[j_{a,l}^{\nu,0}\big(f_{l}^{\mathrm{in},(1)}+f_{l}^{\mathrm{sj},(1)}+f_{l}^{\mathrm{sk3},(1)}+f_{l}^{\mathrm{sk4},(1)}\big)

(21)

\displaystyle+~j_{a,l}^{\nu,{\rm anm}}f_{l}^{0}+j_{a,l}^{\nu,{\rm sj}}f_{l}^{{\rm in},(1)}\Big]~.

Thus, the total spin current can be decomposed into additive contributions associated with distinct microscopic mechanisms,

j_{a}^{\nu}=j_{a}^{\nu,{\rm in}}+j_{a}^{\nu,{\rm sj}}+j_{a}^{\nu,{\rm sk3}}+j_{a}^{\nu,{\rm sk4}}~.

(22)

The individual components are given by,

\displaystyle j_{a}^{\nu,{\rm in}}

(23c)

\displaystyle j_{a}^{\nu,{\rm sk4}}

(23d)

\displaystyle\sigma_{ab}^{\nu,{\rm in}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{\hbar}\sum_{l}\left(j_{a,l}^{\nu,0}\tau\partial_{k_{b}}f_{l}^{(0)}-\Omega_{l}^{\nu,ab}f_{l}^{(0)}\right)~,

\delta
-function impurities. We have,

V(\bm{r})=\sum_{i}V_{i}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{i})~,

(26)

where
V_{i}
is the impurity strength at position
{\bm{R}}_{i}
, and the summation runs over all impurity sites. Following the systematic simplifications developed in Refs.
Varshney and Agarwal 2026
and detailed in Appendix
B
,
C
, and
D
, the side-jump velocity and the antisymmetric scattering rates can be expressed directly in terms of the Berry curvature and SBC. We obtain,

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}^{\rm sj}_{n,{\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}n_{i}V_{0}^{2}\mathcal{D}_{n}(\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}})[{\bm{k}}\times{\bm{\Omega}}_{n}(\bm{k})]~,

(27a)

\displaystyle w^{(3),A}_{n,{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}

In deriving these simplified expressions, we assume that either inversion symmetry (

These results highlight that all extrinsic spin-transport contributions are governed by band-geometric quantities in nonmagnetic or centrosymmetric materials.

The symmetry properties summarized in Table

allow us to determine the time-reversal character of the individual spin-conductivity contributions in Eq. (

III.1
SOC and Néel vector dependence

Beyond doping, spin–orbit coupling (SOC) plays a crucial role in determining the magnitude of the extrinsic spin-splitter current. Figure
5
(a) shows the dependence of various spin-conductivity components on the SOC strength. We find that both the side-jump and skew-scattering contributions vanish in the absence of SOC, whereas the symmetric-scattering (Drude-type) contribution remains finite even when SOC is zero. This behavior follows from the explicit dependence of the skew-scattering and side-jump terms on band-geometric quantities such as the Berry curvature and spin Berry curvature. For the altermagnetic model (Eq.
28
) considered here, the Berry curvature components vary linearly with the SOC strength (
\lambda
), depending on the spin component of SOC parallel to the Néel order. Consequently, in the absence of SOC, the Berry curvature vanishes identically. Moreover, a finite spin Berry curvature requires interband spin mixing, which is also absent when SOC is zero. Therefore, SOC is essential for enabling the asymmetric impurity-scattering processes that generate the extrinsic spin-splitter current.

The
\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{N}^{2}

denotes the Néel order. Such spin currents generate damping-like spin–orbit torques

Chęciński et al. 2017

IV
Conclusion

In summary, we developed a unified semiclassical theory of the linear extrinsic spin-splitter current in altermagnets, systematically incorporating disorder-induced side-jump and skew-scattering mechanisms. Going beyond previous studies focused on intrinsic and symmetric-scattering contributions
González-Hernández et al. 2021
;
Yi et al. 2025
, our results demonstrate that contributions arising from asymmetric scattering can dominate the spin-splitter current. Applying the theory to the
d
-wave altermagnet
\text{FeSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, we showed that asymmetric impurity scattering generates sizable transverse spin currents that can exceed intrinsic contributions, yielding a large spin Hall angle (
\sim 0.8
) and highly efficient spin–charge conversion.

Our symmetry analysis reveals that while the conventional Drude contribution is
\cal{T}
-odd, the extrinsic spin-splitter conductivities are intrinsically
\cal{T}
-even. Our numerical results for
\text{FeSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
also reflect this and the spin conductivity does not simply reverse sign under Néel-vector reversal. We further showed that these contributions require finite relativistic spin–orbit coupling. Taken together, our findings establish disorder induced extrinsic scattering as a constructive mechanism for generating and controlling spin currents in altermagnets, providing new directions for altermagnetic spintronic functionalities.

Appendix B
Simplification of scattering rates

In this section, we derive simplified expressions for the impurity scattering rates entering the semiclassical Boltzmann equation. Our goal is to reduce the general scattering amplitudes to forms suitable for numerical evaluation of the spin conductivity.
In the presence of elastic impurity scattering, the nonequilibrium distribution function
f_{l}
satisfies the semiclassical Boltzmann equation

\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial t}+\dot{\bm{k}}\!\cdot\!\partial_{\bm{k}}f_{l}=I_{\text{el}}\{f_{l}\}~,

(38)

where
I_{\text{el}}\{f_{l}\}
is the elastic collision integral. Within the Born approximation, it can be written as

I_{\text{el}}\{f_{l}\}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}(w_{l^{\prime}l}f_{l}-w_{ll^{\prime}}f_{l^{\prime}})~,

(39)

where the scattering rate
w_{l^{\prime}l}
between states
l
and
l^{\prime}
is governed by Fermi’s Golden rule,

w_{ll^{\prime}}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\left\langle|\bra{l}V\ket{l^{\rm dis}}|^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{dis}}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}).

(40)

Expanding the Lippmann–Schwinger equation up to second order in the impurity potential yields

\displaystyle\ket{l^{\rm dis}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ket{l}+(\varepsilon_{l}-H_{0}+i\eta)^{-1}(\hat{V}+\hat{V}(\varepsilon_{l}-H_{0}+i\eta)\hat{V})\ket{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ket{l}+\sum_{l^{\prime}}\frac{V_{l^{\prime}l}}{(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}+i\eta)}\ket{l'}+\sum_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}\frac{V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}}{(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}+i\eta)(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta)}\ket{l'}~.

Substituting this expansion into Eq. (
40
), we get

w_{ll^{\prime}}=w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2)}+w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3)}+w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(4)}+\cdots,

(41)

where
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(\nu)}\propto V^{\nu}
represent the
\nu
-th order scattering, for
\nu=2,3,4\cdots
. The explicit expressions of these scattering rates are given by

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\left\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\right\rangle_{\text{dis}}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})~,

(42)

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}\bigg[\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta}+\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}-i\eta}\bigg]\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})~,

(43)

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}l^{\prime\prime\prime}}\bigg[\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime\prime}l^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{(\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}-i\eta)(\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime\prime}}+i\eta)}+\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l^{\prime\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime\prime}l^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{(\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta)(\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime\prime}}+i\eta)}

(44)

\displaystyle+\frac{\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}\rangle_{\text{dis}}}{(\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}-i\eta)(\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime\prime}}-i\eta)}\bigg]\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})~.

The second-order scattering rate
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2)}
is strictly symmetric under exchange of initial and final states,
l\leftrightarrow l^{\prime}
. On the other hand
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(3)}
and
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(4)}
don’t follow such relations. So one can actually separate the symmetric and anti-symmetric parts of these scattering rates as follows,

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(\nu),S}=\frac{w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(\nu)}+w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(\nu)}}{2}~,~~~w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(\nu),A}=\frac{w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(\nu)}-w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(\nu)}}{2}~.

(45)

The symmetric parts of these higher-order terms merely renormalize the leading second-order contribution and do not generate qualitatively new transport phenomena. Consequently, for the third- and fourth-order scattering processes, only their antisymmetric components are relevant for transport. With this separation, the elastic collision integral naturally decomposes into symmetric and antisymmetric parts as,
I^{s}_{\text{el}}\{f_{l}\}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(2)}(f_{l}-f_{l^{\prime}})
and
I^{a}_{\text{el}}\{f_{l}\}=-\sum_{l^{\prime}}(w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(3),A}+w_{l^{\prime}l}^{(4),A})(f_{l}+f_{l^{\prime}})
, respectively.

If we take into account the work done by the electric field as an electron gets displaced within the unit cell during
the collision, the second-order in the impurity part, is modified as

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2)}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\left\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\right\rangle_{\text{dis}}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}+e{\bm{E}}\cdot\delta{\bm{r}}_{ll^{\prime}})~,

(46)

where
\delta{\bm{r}}_{ll^{\prime}}
is the coordinate shift defined in Eq. (

Here, we only consider the modification to the second-order part and neglect mixed contributions from skew scattering and coordinate shifts, as they are subleading in the weak-disorder limit. Hence, the second-order part is decomposed as a symmetric field-independent part and a coordinate shift correction term induced by the external electric field [see Eq. (
12
)].

The symmetric field-independent part governs conventional momentum relaxation, the coordinate shift correction term gives the side jump contribution, and the antisymmetric part encodes asymmetric scattering processes responsible for skew-scattering contributions to the spin response. Our next step is to simplify these scattering rates and express them in forms suitable for numerical evaluation of the spin conductivity. We begin with the symmetric second-order scattering rate
w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2S)}

\displaystyle w_{ll^{\prime}}^{(2S)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\left\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime}l}\right\rangle_{\text{dis}}\delta(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}})=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\left\langle V_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{k}}}\right\rangle_{\text{dis}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})~.

\displaystyle\bra{ n \bm k}V\ket{ n'\bm k'}

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\int d{\bm{r}}V_{i}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{i})e^{i(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\cdot\bm{r}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle=\sum_{i}V_{i}e^{i(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\cdot\bm{R}_{i}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle=V^{0}_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle~.

and the disorder average can be further simplified to

We now turn to the antisymmetric third-order scattering rate,

Substituting Eq. (

\displaystyle(4\pi^{2}/\hbar){\rm Im}\sum_{n^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\langle V_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}^{0}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{0}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{k}}}^{0}\rangle_{\rm dis}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}|u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}})~.

\bm{k}

Guo et al. 2024

is the impurity concentration and

\displaystyle w_{n,{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{(3),A}=\frac{4\pi^{2}n_{i}V_{1}^{3}}{\hbar}{\rm Im}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}})~.

Here we have applied Wick’s theorem to factorize the disorder average as

\langle V_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime\prime}}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}}\rangle_{\rm dis}=\langle V_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle_{\rm dis}\langle V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime\prime}}V_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime\prime}{\bm{k}}}\rangle_{\rm dis}.

To proceed further, we need to explicitly evaluate the Bloch-state overlaps entering the disorder matrix elements.

Due to the short-range scattering potential, the electron experiences a small momentum transfer during scattering events. This leads to
q=({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\to 0
. Within this limit, we expand the Bloch state
\ket{u_{ n \kb'}}

(53)

Here we have taken Einstein’s summation condition for coordinate indices

(54)

Using this into Eq. (

(55)

Now let’s take the case of

\displaystyle\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle 1+i(q_{b}^{\prime}-q_{b})\mathcal{R}_{nn}^{b}+q_{b}q_{c}^{\prime}\langle\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{b}u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}q_{b}q_{c}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{b}\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}q_{b}^{\prime}q_{c}^{\prime}\langle\partial_{b}\partial_{c}u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}}\rangle~,

Using the above in Eq. (

\displaystyle w^{(4),A}_{n,{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}

(57)

\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime\prime}}1/(\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime\prime}})\approx 1/(\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}})

{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime\prime}\to{\bm{k}}

-Brillouin zone . So the final expression for the scattering rate comes out as,

w^{(4),A}_{n,{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}=-\dfrac{2\pi^{2}n_{i}^{2}V_{0}^{4}}{\hbar}\sum_{n^{\prime}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}\bigg[({\bm{k}}^{\prime}\times\bm{k})+({\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}\times{\bm{k}}^{\prime})+(\bm{k}\times{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime})\bigg]\cdot\bm{\Omega}_{nn^{\prime}}\dfrac{\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k^{\prime}}})\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}})}{(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}})}~.

(58)

We now specialize to systems with an even energy dispersion under wave-vector inversion,

\varepsilon_{n}({\bm{k}})=\varepsilon_{n}(-{\bm{k}})~,

(59)

a condition naturally satisfied in the presence of either inversion (
\mathcal{P}

) symmetry.
Under this symmetry, Brillouin-zone summations over odd functions of momentum vanish. In particular,

\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})=0~,\qquad\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}})=0~.

(60)

Furthermore, using the definition of the band-resolved density of states (DOS),

\mathcal{D}_{n}(\varepsilon)=\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\delta(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})~,

(61)

the remaining momentum summations reduce to

\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\delta(\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}}-\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})=\mathcal{D}_{n}(\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}})~.

(62)

Substituting these symmetry constraints into Eq. (
56
) and Eq. (
58
), we get the simplified form of Eq. (
27c
) and Eq. (
27d
), respectively.

Appendix C
Simplification of the side-jump velocity in terms of Berry curvature

Starting from Eqs. (
35
) and (
46
), the side-jump velocity can be written as

{\bm{v}}^{\rm sj}_{n}({\bm{k}})=-\frac{2\pi n_{i}V_{0}^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{n^{\prime}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}|\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle|^{2}\delta(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}})\delta{\bm{r}}_{nn}({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime})~.

(63)

Here,
\delta r^{a}_{nn}({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime})
denotes the coordinate shift during an elastic scattering event and is given by

\delta r^{a}_{nn}({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime})=R^{a}_{nn}({\bm{k}})-R^{a}_{nn}({\bm{k}}^{\prime})-(\partial_{a}+\partial^{\prime}_{a}){\rm arg}(\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle)~,

(64)

where the intraband Berry connection is

R^{a}_{nn}({\bm{k}})=\langle u_{n{\bm{k}}}|i\partial_{a}u_{n{\bm{k}}}\rangle~.

(65)

Now using the identity of Eq. (
53
), we can show that
\partial^{\prime}_{a}|u_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle=\ket{\partial_a u_{n \kb}}+q_{b}\ket{ \partial_a \partial_b u_{n \kb}}
, which allows us to express Berry connection at
{\bm{k}}^{\prime}
as

R^{a}_{nn}({\bm{k}}^{\prime})=R^{a}_{nn}({\bm{k}})+iq_{b}\bigg[\bra{u_{n\kb}}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}u_{n{\bm{k}}}\rangle+\bra{\partial_b u_{n\kb}}\partial_{a}u_{n{\bm{k}}}\rangle\bigg]~.

Employing the identity
\bra{u_{n\kb}}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}u_{n{\bm{k}}}\rangle=-\bra{\partial_a u_{n\kb}}\partial_{b}u_{n{\bm{k}}}\rangle-i\partial_{a}R^{b}_{nn}
, we obtain

R^{a}_{nn}({\bm{k}}^{\prime})=R^{a}_{nn}({\bm{k}})-q_{b}\Omega^{ab}_{n}({\bm{k}})+q_{b}\partial_{a}R^{b}_{nn}({\bm{k}})~,

where the Berry curvature is

\Omega^{ab}_{n}({\bm{k}})=i\left[\langle\partial_{a}u_{n{\bm{k}}}|\partial_{b}u_{n{\bm{k}}}\rangle-\langle\partial_{b}u_{n{\bm{k}}}|\partial_{a}u_{n{\bm{k}}}\rangle\right]~.

(66)

Now using Eq. (
54
), the overlap yields

\mathrm{arg}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle=-q_{b}R^{b}_{nn}({\bm{k}})~,

(67)

and

(\partial_{a}+\partial^{\prime}_{a})\mathrm{arg}\langle u_{n\bm{k}}|u_{n\bm{k}^{\prime}}\rangle=-q_{b}\partial_{a}R^{b}_{nn}({\bm{k}})~.

(68)

Substituting these expressions into Eq. (
64
), the coordinate shift simplifies to

\delta r^{a}_{nn}({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime})=q_{b}\Omega^{ab}_{n}({\bm{k}})~.

(69)

Using
\Omega^{ab}_{n}=\epsilon_{abc}\Omega^{c}_{n}
, we obtain the compact vector form

\delta{\bm{r}}_{nn}({\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime})=({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{k}})\times{\bm{\Omega}}_{n}({\bm{k}}).

(70)

In the long-wavelength limit,
|\bra{u_{n \kb}}u_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle|^{2}\approx 1
and the side-jump velocity reduces to

{\bm{v}}^{\rm sj}_{n}({\bm{k}})=\frac{2\pi n_{i}V_{0}^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}[({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime})\times{\bm{\Omega}}_{n}({\bm{k}})]\delta(\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}}-\varepsilon_{n{\bm{k}}^{\prime}})~.

(71)

Under the symmetry condition of Eq. (
59
), i.e.,
\varepsilon_{n}({\bm{k}})=\varepsilon_{n}(-{\bm{k}})
, the side-jump velocity reduce to the compact form given in Eq. (
27a
).

Appendix D
Side-jump correction to Spin Current operator

In this appendix, we explicitly calculate the spin side jump velocity
J_{a,l}^{\nu,{\rm sj}}
defined in Eq. (
2
).
The disorder-induced correction to the Bloch state
\ket{\delta^{\rm dis} l}
gives rise to side-jump contribution. To obtain this correction, we employ the Lippmann–Schwinger equation in the presence of an impurity potential
\hat{V}
,
\ket{l^{\rm dis}}=\ket{l}+(\varepsilon_{l}-H_{0}+i\eta)^{-1}\hat{T}\ket{l}
where
\hat{T}=\hat{V}+\hat{V}(\varepsilon_{l}-H_{0}+i\eta)\hat{T}
. The disorder-induced correction to the wave function is then
\ket{\delta^{\rm dis}l}=\ket{l^{\rm dis}}-\ket{l}
. Using this correction, the side-jump contribution to the spin current is given by

(72)

where
\langle...\rangle_{\rm dis}
denotes disorder averaging. The lowest-order nonvanishing contribution arises at second order in the impurity potential. Explicitly, these terms read

\displaystyle\langle 2{\rm Re}\bra{l}\hat{j}^{\nu}_{a}\ket{\delta^{\rm dis} l}\rangle_{\rm dis}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\rm Re}\sum_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}\dfrac{\bra{l}\hat{j}^{\nu}_{a}\ket{l'}\langle V_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}\rangle_{\rm dis}}{(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}+i\eta)(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta)}

\displaystyle 2{\rm Re}\sum_{n^{\prime}n^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}\dfrac{\bra{ n \bm k}\hat{j}^{\nu}_{a}\ket{ n'\bm k'}\langle\bra{ n' \bm k'}V\ket{ n''\bm k''}\bra{ n''\bm k''}V\ket{ n\bm k}\rangle_{\rm dis}}{(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}+i\eta)(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta)}~,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}}\dfrac{\bra{l'}\hat{j}^{\nu}_{a}\ket{l''}\langle V_{ll^{\prime}}V_{l^{\prime\prime}l}\rangle_{\rm dis}}{(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime}}-i\eta)(\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta)}=\sum_{n^{\prime}n^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}\dfrac{\bra{ n' \bm k'}\hat{j}^{\nu}_{a}\ket{ n''\bm k''}\langle\bra{ n \bm k}V\ket{ n'\bm k'}\bra{ n''\bm k''}V\ket{ n\bm k}\rangle_{\rm dis}}{(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}-i\eta)(\varepsilon_{n\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{n^{\prime\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}+i\eta)}~.

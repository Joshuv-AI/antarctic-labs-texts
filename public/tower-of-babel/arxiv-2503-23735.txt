Altermagnetism and Weak Ferromagnetism
I. V. Solovyev, S. A. Nikolaev, A. Tanaka

Abstract
Using a realistic model relevant to La 2 CuO 4 and other altermagnetic perovskite oxides, we study interrelations between weak ferromagnetism (WF), anomalous Hall effect (AHE), and net orbital magnetization (OM). All of them can be linked to the form of Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM) interactions. Nevertheless, while spin WF is induced by the DM vector components having the same sign in all equivalent bonds, AHE and OM are related to alternating-sign components, which do not contribute to any canting of spins. The microscopic model remains invariant under the symmetry operation { 𝒮 | 𝐭 } \{\mathcal{S}|{\bf t}\} , combining the shift 𝐭 {\bf t} of antiferromagnetically coupled sublattices to each other with the spin flip 𝒮 \mathcal{S} . Thus, the band structure remains Kramers-degenerate, but the time-reversal symmetry is broken, providing a possibility to realize AHE in antiferromagnetic substances. The altermagnetic splitting of bands, breaking the { 𝒮 | 𝐭 } \{\mathcal{S}|{\bf t}\} symmetry, does not play a major role in the problem. More important is the orthorhombic strain, responsible for finite values of AHE and OM.



Introduction
. Altermagnetism is regarded as a new phase of matter, where a nearly antiferromagnetic (AFM) alignment of spins coexists with the spin splitting of bands and robust time-reversal symmetry (
\mathcal{T}
) breaking, which are typical for ferromagnetic (FM) systems
SmejkalPRX1
;
SmejkalPRX2
;
LingBai
. Nevertheless, this new classification raises new questions, especially on how it fits into our previous knowledge on AFM materials. It is certainly true that lifting of Kramers’ degeneracy of AFM bands, which was predicted largely due to development of first-principles calculations based on density-functional theory (DFT), is a new aspect of the problem
Noda
;
Okugawa
. On the other hand, the possibility of breaking
\mathcal{T}
in certain classes of AFM systems has been known for decades. As was pointed out by Dzyaloshinskii
Dzyaloshinskii1991
, using phenomenological symmetry arguments, the materials whose magnetic unit cell coincide with the crystallographic one present a special type of antiferromagnetism, giving rise to such phenomena as weak ferromagnetism (WF)
Dzyaloshinskii_weakF
, piezomagnetism
DzyaloshinskiiPM
, and magnetoelectricity
DzyaloshinskiiME
. A very detailed classification was given by Turov
TurovBook
;
TurovUFN
, who argued that there are two major classes of unconventional antiferromagnets, depending on whether the spatial inversion
\mathcal{I}
enters the magnetic group in combination with
\mathcal{T}
or alone. The first scenario corresponds to magnetoelectricity, while the second one, which encompasses WF and piezomagnetism, has clear similarity to what is now called “altermagnetism”. Canonically, WF refers to net spin magnetic moments in otherwise AFM substances, while altermagnetism emerged from the analysis of the anomalous Hall effect (AHE)
SmejkalSA
. However, from the phenomenological point of view, these two effects are identical to each other: both manifest that
\mathcal{T}
is macroscopically broken and the existence of AHE automatically implies the existence of WF, no matter how “weak” it is. Actually, in his monograph
TurovBook
, Turov considered not only WF, but also AHE and many other phenomena expected in weak ferromagnets. Particularly, he explicitly stated that AHE can be induced by AFM order. Apparently, this is the most one can say within phenomenological theories, which do not provide any information about the magnitude of the effect or its microscopic origin.

Therefore, it is legitimate to ask: Is altermagnetism a weak ferromagnetism? In this work, we will provide a microscopic analysis of this problem by considering a realistic toy model of altermagnetism relevant to La
2
CuO
4
– the prominent superconductor parent material – and other perovskites. The microscopic theory of WF is basically the theory of Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM) interactions, proposed by Moriya
Moriya_weakF
for Mott insulators and extended to treat other types of magnetic systems
review2024
. Depending on the symmetry, DM interactions can have several components. Some of them, which have the same sign in all equivalent bonds, support WF. However, there are many examples when the signs of DM interactions alternate so that their contribution to the FM moment (or any other canting of spins) cancels out. A notable example is the DM interactions between two magnetic sublattices in CrO
2
, having the same structure as altermagnetic RuO
2
: these interactions are relatively strong, but alternate among eight neighboring bonds
review2024
. Then, what is the role of these DM interactions? In this respect, we will argue that these alternating components of DM interactions bear direct implications to such phenomena as AHE and orbital magnetization
{\cal M}
. Moreover, while the spin splitting and breaking of
\mathcal{T}
are regarded as two hallmarks of altermagnetism, these are rather different effects, which do not necessarily need to coexist. Particularly, we will show that the bands can remain Kramers-degenerate even when

is broken, yelding AHE and
{\cal M}
. On the other hand, the band splitting, which generates the spin current, can occur without spin-orbit coupling (SOC), when AHE vanishes

;
Naka_Spintronics
. Finally, in 1997, long before the modern era of altermagnetism, one of us proposed that weak ferromagnets La
M

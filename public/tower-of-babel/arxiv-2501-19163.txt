Exact Floquet solutions in a Parity-Time-Symmetric Rabi Model
M. Baradaran, D. Braak, L.M. Nieto, S. Zarrinkamar, [1ex] Department of Physics, Faculty of Science, University of Hradec Králové Rokitanského 62, 500 03 Hradec Králové, Czechia, [1ex] Institute of Physics, University of Augsburg, 86135 Augsburg, Germany, [1ex] Departamento de Física Teórica, Atómica y Óptica, Laboratory for Disruptive Interdisciplinary Science (LaDIS), Universidad de Valladolid, 47011 Valladolid, Spain, [1ex] Departament of Basic Sciences, Garmsar Branch, Islamic Azad University, Garmsar, Iran

Abstract
It is shown that a semiclassical Rabi model with parity-time (PT) symmetry has a hidden s ​ l ​ ( 2 ) sl(2) symmetry and hence possesses quasi-exact solutions. These are located precisely at the exceptional points of the spectrum, the boundaries of the PT-symmetric phase. The corresponding constraints on the model parameters can be interpreted as a resonance relationship between the constant and periodic driving terms.



Keywords:
Parity-time symmetric Rabi model, quasi-exact solutions, multi-photon resonance, Floquet theory

2
Formulation and General Solution via Lie-algebraic Approach

We consider the following non-Hermitian time-dependent Hamiltonian
[
26
]

H=i\frac{\gamma}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{\nu(t)}{2}\sigma_{x}\,,

(1)

where
\sigma_{x,z}
are Pauli matrices acting on a pseudo-spinor
(a_{1}(t),a_{2}(t))^{T}
and

\nu(t)=\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}\cos(\omega t),

being
\nu_{1}
and
\omega
the modulation amplitude and frequency, respectively, while
\nu_{0}
represents the qubit splitting energy. In addition,
\gamma
denotes the strength of the loss/gain term, characteristic of PT-symmetric dissipative systems. Hence, the two-level probability amplitudes
a_{1}(t)
and
a_{2}(t)
satisfy the coupled first-order differential equations

\begin{gathered}i\frac{da_{1}(\tau)}{d\tau}=\frac{\nu(\tau)}{2\omega}a_{2}(\tau)+i\frac{\gamma}{2\omega}a_{1}(\tau),\\
i\frac{da_{2}(\tau)}{d\tau}=\frac{\nu(\tau)}{2\omega}a_{1}(\tau)-i\frac{\gamma}{2\omega}a_{2}(\tau),\end{gathered}

(2)

where we have set
\tau=\omega t
and
\hbar=1
.
Following
[
26
]
, we introduce the combinations

c_{1}(t)=(a_{1}(t)+a_{2}(t))/2,\qquad c_{2}(t)=(a_{1}(t)-a_{2}(t))/2,

(3)

and define

\displaystyle z=e^{i\tau},

(4)

\displaystyle c_{j}(z)=e^{\frac{\nu_{1}}{2\omega}\cos(\tau)}z^{-\frac{\nu_{0}}{2\omega}}\phi_{j}(z),\qquad j=1,2.

After eliminating
\phi_{2}(z)
, we obtain a second-order differential equation for
\phi_{1}(z)\equiv\phi(z)

[
26
]
,

z^{2}\frac{d^{2}\phi(z)}{dz^{2}}+\left(\frac{\nu_{1}}{2\omega}z^{2}+(1-\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega})z-\frac{\nu_{1}}{2\omega}\right)\frac{d\phi(z)}{dz}+\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}}{4\omega^{2}}+\frac{\nu_{1}}{2\omega}(1-\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega})z\right)\phi(z)=0.

(5)

This equation has the form of an eigenvalue equation
\tilde{H}\phi(z)=\lambda\phi(z)
with fixed eigenvalue
\lambda=0
.

We will now show that the differential operator
\tilde{H}
in (
5
) has a “hidden” algebraic structure if certain conditions are met, which will be specified below.
This means that
\tilde{H}
can be expressed as an element of the universal enveloping algebra of
sl(2)
, acting on a
finite-dimensional
representation. The general form of such a quasi-exactly solvable operator
H_{qes}
reads
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
,

H_{qes}=C_{++}{J}_{n}^{+}{J}_{n}^{+}+C_{+0}{J}_{n}^{+}{J}_{n}^{0}+C_{+-}{J}_{n}^{+}{J}_{n}^{-}+C_{0-}{J}_{n}^{0}{J}_{n}^{-}+C_{--}{J}_{n}^{-}{J}_{n}^{-}+C_{+}{J}_{n}^{+}+C_{0}{J}_{n}^{0}+C_{-}{J}_{n}^{-}+C,

(6)

in which the generators of
sl(2)
are represented as first-order differential operators,

J_{n}^{+}=z^{2}\,\frac{d}{dz}-n\,z,\qquad J_{n}^{0}=z\,\frac{d}{dz}-\frac{n}{2},\qquad J_{n}^{-}=\frac{d}{dz}.

(7)

They satisfy the commutation relations

[J^{+}_{n},J^{-}_{n}]=-2J^{0}_{n},\qquad[J^{\pm}_{n},J^{0}_{n}]=\mp J^{\pm}_{n}.

(8)

For a nonnegative integer
n
, any product of operators (
7
) leaves invariant the
(n+1)
-dimensional space of polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{n}[z]=\langle 1,z,z^{2},\dots,z^{n}\rangle
with maximum order
n
because the ascending ladder operator
J_{n}^{+}
annihilates
z^{n}
, which is therefore the highest weight vector of the
(2j+1)
-dimensional representation of
sl(2)
with
j=(n-1)/2
.
The constant function is annihilated by
J_{n}^{-}
, which is the lowest weight vector (
n=0
corresponds to the trivial representation with
J_{0}^{\pm,0}\langle 1\rangle=0
). In other words,
H_{qes}
preserves the finite-dimensional space of polynomials of the form

\phi_{n}(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}{c_{m}z^{m}}.

(9)

Substituting (
7
) into (
6
), the operator
H_{qes}
is given by a second-order differential operator with polynomial coefficients,

H_{qes}=P_{4}(z)\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}+P_{3}(z)\frac{d}{dz}+P_{2}(z),

(10)

in which

\displaystyle P_{4}(z)=C_{++}z^{4}+C_{+0}z^{3}+C_{+-}z^{2}+C_{0-}z+C_{--},

(11)

\displaystyle P_{3}(z)=C_{++}(2-2n)z^{3}+\left(C_{+}+C_{+0}\left(1-\frac{3n}{2}\right)\right)z^{2}+\left(C_{0}-nC_{+-}\right)z+\left(C_{-}-\frac{n}{2}C_{0-}\right),

\displaystyle P_{2}(z)=C_{++}n(n-1)z^{2}+\left(\frac{n^{2}}{2}C_{+0}-nC_{+}\right)z+\left(C-\frac{n}{2}C_{0}\right).

Now, comparing (
5
) with (
11
), we find that if the constraint

\nu_{0}=(n+1)\omega

(12)

holds, the equation (
5
) can be written as
\tilde{H}\phi_{n}(z)=0
and
\tilde{H}
given in terms of
J_{n}^{\pm}
:

\tilde{H}={J}_{n}^{+}{J}_{n}^{-}+\frac{\nu_{1}}{2\omega}\left({J}_{n}^{+}-{J}_{n}^{-}\right)+\frac{\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}}{4\omega^{2}}.

(13)

The condition (
12
) fixing
\nu_{0}
in terms of
\omega
and
n
is necessary to cancel terms proportional to
z
in (
5
) that cannot be absorbed in an expression containing
J_{n}^{+}
.
Since
\tilde{H}
leaves the space
\mathcal{P}_{n}[z]
invariant, it can be expressed as a matrix
\tilde{H}_{n}
of dimension
(n+1)\times(n+1)
. Using as basis
v_{0}=1,v_{1}=z,\ldots v_{n}=z^{n}
, we obtain the matrix elements of
J_{n}^{\pm}
and
J_{n}^{+}J_{n}^{-}
from (
7
) as

J^{+}_{n}(i,j)=-(n-j)\delta_{i,j+1},\quad J^{-}_{n}(i,j)=j\delta_{i,j-1},\quad J^{+}_{n}J^{-}_{n}(i,j)=(j-n-1)j\delta_{i,j},

(14)

for
i,j=0\ldots n
.
Therefore,
\tilde{H}_{n}
is a tridiagonal matrix with elements

\tilde{H}_{n}(i,j)=\left((j-n-1)j+\frac{\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}}{4\omega^{2}}\right)\delta_{i,j}-\frac{\nu_{1}}{2\omega}\big((n-j)\delta_{i,j+1}+j\delta_{i,j-1}\big).

(15)

The condition for the existence of a non-trivial solution of
\tilde{H}_{n}\phi_{n}=0
is
\det(\tilde{H}_{n})=0
, which gives a second condition that the parameters
\nu_{1},\gamma
and
\omega
must satisfy.
We then report explicit solutions for
n=0,1,2
and give the constraints on the determinants for
n
up to 5.

•

For
n=0
, that is
\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega}=1
, we have
\phi_{0}(z)=c_{0}
for which the equation
\tilde{H}_{0}\,\phi_{0}(z)=0
becomes
(\nu_{1}^{2}-\gamma^{2})c_{0}=0
.
Thus, a non-trivial solution requires

\nu_{1}^{2}=\gamma^{2},

meaning that the driving amplitude
\nu_{1}
equals the gain/loss strength
\gamma
(both quantities can be chosen positive without loss of generality). The condition does not depend on the driving frequency
\omega
, so this special solution does not involve a resonance phenomenon in the proper sense.

•

For
n=1
(
\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega}=2
), we have for
\phi_{1}(z)
in the basis given above
\phi_{1}=(c_{0},c_{1})^{T}
for which the equation
\tilde{H}_{1}\phi_{1}(z)=0
reads

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}}{4\omega^{2}}&-\frac{\nu_{1}}{2\omega}\\
-\frac{\nu_{1}}{2\omega}&\frac{\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}}{4\omega^{2}}-1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{0}\\
c_{1}\end{array}\right)=0,

The condition
\det(\tilde{H}_{1})=0
yields,

(\nu_{1}^{2}-\gamma^{2})^{2}=4\omega^{2}\gamma^{2},

(16)

and the coefficients
c_{0},c_{1}
satisfy

\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}=\frac{\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}}{2\omega\nu_{1}}.

We observe that in this case, the condition on
\nu_{1}
and
\gamma
involves the driving frequency
\omega
, so it corresponds to a resonance between the gain/loss process and the driving. From (
16
), explicit solutions of
\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}
are obtained as

\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\pm\frac{2\gamma}{\omega}.

•

For
n=2
, meaning
\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega}=3
, the matrix equation
\tilde{H}_{2}\,\phi_{2}(z)=0
reads,

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}}{4\omega^{2}}&-\frac{\nu_{1}}{2\omega}&0\\
-\frac{\nu_{1}}{\omega}&\frac{\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}}{4\omega^{2}}-2&-\frac{\nu_{1}}{\omega}\\
0&-\frac{\nu_{1}}{2\omega}&\frac{\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}}{4\omega^{2}}-2\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{0}\\
c_{1}\\
c_{2}\end{array}\right)=0.

The determinant condition reads now

\left(\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{3}-16\left(\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-64\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}=0,

(17)

and, consequently, a nontrivial solution for the matrix equation is obtained as

c_{1}=\frac{4\omega\nu_{1}\left(\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}-8\omega^{2}\right)}{8\omega^{2}\left(\nu_{1}^{2}-2\gamma^{2}\right)+\left(\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}+64\omega^{4}}\,c_{0},\quad c_{2}=\frac{8\omega^{2}\nu_{1}^{2}}{8\omega^{2}\left(\nu_{1}^{2}-2\gamma^{2}\right)+\left(\gamma^{2}-\nu_{1}^{2}\right)^{2}+64\omega^{4}}\,c_{0}.

(18)

From (
17
), we obtain three solutions for
\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}
in the form of Cardano’s formula, which are of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{4}{\xi}\sqrt[3]{\frac{2}{3}}\right)\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+2\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2/3}\xi,

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{4}{\xi}\sqrt[3]{-\frac{2}{3}}\right)\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+2\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2/3}\xi,

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{4}{\xi}(-1)^{2/3}\sqrt[3]{\frac{2}{3}}\right)\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-2\sqrt[3]{-1}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2/3}\xi,

in which
\xi=\sqrt[3]{\frac{9\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{81\gamma^{4}\omega^{2}-12\gamma^{6}}}{\omega^{3}}}
.

In a similar way, one can compute the determinant conditions for larger values of
n
. Let us summarize our results determined explicitly for
n=0,1,...,5
, or equivalently, for
\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega}=1,2,...,6
:

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega}=1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega}=2\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{2}-4\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega}=3\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{3}-16\left(\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-64\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega}=4\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{4}-40\left(\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+48\left(11\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-8\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-2304\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{0}}{\omega}=5\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{5}-80\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{3}\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+64\left(\frac{37\gamma^{4}}{\omega^{4}}-\frac{58\gamma^{2}\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}\omega^{2}}+\frac{21\nu_{1}^{4}}{\omega^{4}}\right)\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}

(19)

\displaystyle\qquad-6144\left(\frac{5\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{3\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+147456\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}=0,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{6}-140\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{4}\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+112\left(\frac{69\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{32\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\omega^{2}}

3
Conclusions

In this work we considered the algebraic structure of a special PT-symmetric semi-classical Rabi model. It turns out that the condition for the existence of a finite-dimensional invariant subspace has the form of a “resonance condition” between the driving frequency and its constant term:
\nu_{0}/\omega=n+1
.
The time-dependent Schrödinger equation then has a quasi-exact solution corresponding to a so-called “exceptional point” at the edge of the PT-symmetric region in parameter space
[
26
]
.
The invariant subspace belongs to the spin-
(n/2)
representation of
sl(2)

[
27
]
, since the time-dependent Hamiltonian can be written in terms of
sl(2)
-generators acting in this representation.
This allows to give determinantal expressions for the additional relation between the driving strength
\nu_{1}
and the dissipative coupling
\gamma
in closed form.
The trivial representation
n=0
is a special case, in which the gain/loss term exactly balances the driving and leads to the simplest type of exceptional point. The quasi-exact solution is usually unique for a given parameter set
\{\nu_{0}/\omega,\nu_{1}/\omega,\gamma/\omega\}
, with the exception of the case with time-independent driving,
\nu_{1}=0
. Interestingly, some of the two possible Floquet solutions for fixed gain/loss
\gamma
continue to exist (at least approximately) for
\nu_{1}\neq 0
if the quotient
\nu_{0}/\omega
is a large integer. Having two initial states with exact time development at resonance in the driven system may be of some value for applications of our semi-classical system in quantum information technology.

We observe that (
5
) falls into the class of double confluent Heun equations
[
30
]
. It has two irregular singular points of
s
-rank two at
z=0
and
z=\infty
, which are created by a confluence of two pairs of regular singular points (the same class is sometimes called “biconfluent”
[
31
,
32
]
).
If the exponents of second kind vanish at both
z=0
and
z=\infty
, and thus allow a Frobenius expansion of the solution around
z=0
, it can be expressed as a polynomial in
z
if certain constraints on the parameters are satisfied
[
31
,
32
]
.
These restrictions are reduced in our case to the condition (
12
) and the vanishing of the determinant of the matrix (
15
).

It seems that other quasi-exactly solvable models could also be a class of PT-symmetric models and provide a better and deeper understanding of the structure of the solutions. We are working to carry out a fairly complete study of this interesting exploration.

Highly promoted CO2 separation of poly(ether-block-amide) based mixed matrix membranes using MOFs@aminoclay architectures as fillers
Authors: Zohreh Tajdini, Elahe Ahmadi Feijani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this research, a collection of new composites constructed from aminoclays (MgAC and ZrAC) and MOFs are inserted into the PEBAX matrix for gas separation inspections. In fabricated UiO-66-(COOH)2@CuBDC@MgAC and MIL-121@CuBDC@MgAC composites series, amine groups of MgAC, unoccupied Cu ions of CuBDC and free carboxylic acid in the pores of UiO-66-(COOH)2 and MIL-121 are intensely involved in superior CO2 permeability of prepared MMMs from these composites. Besides, the nanosheet formation of CuBDC MOF induced by MgAC sheets develops the interphase regions (MOF-MOF and PEBAX-composites) which are also responsible for such obtained CO2 permeability against N2. Additionally, lower pore diameter of CuBDC in comparison with UiO-66-(COOH)2 and MIL-121 can exert sieving effect for crowded gas molecules which pass through the UiO-66-(COOH)2 and MIL-121 layers. In these series, PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-4 demonstrates CO2 permeability of 320.15 barrer and CO2/N2 selectivity of 147.27. A new aminoclay based on zirconium is synthesized and added to the constructed composites as the outer layer. The provided ZrAC@UiO-66-(COOH)2@CuBDC@MgAC and ZrAC@MIL-121@CuBDC@MgAC composites are also examined as fillers in PEBAX matrix. Apparently, the interphases of MMMs are gotten close to their ideal states by ZrAC sheets existence in composites and gas behavior of all MMMs are ameliorated compared to the last MMMs series. In this regard, PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-4 exhibits CO2 permeability of 407.77 barrer and CO2/N2 selectivity of 87.46. The fabricated composites and membranes are identified by FT-IR, XRD, SEM and HRTEM. The membranes are also characterized by TGA, surface contact angle and Stress-Strain analysis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-80813-4.Keywords: Gas separation, MOF, Aminoclay, PEBAX, Mixed matrix membraneSubject terms: Process chemistry, Environmental chemistry

Introduction

The separations are the substantial steps in industrial activities which a lot of costs and energy consumption are allocated to them. The energy consumption is more remarkable in common traditional separation process (distillation, adsorption, absorption, crystallization, extraction, stripping) compared to the relatively newer ones (membrane based microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration)1. For example, CO2 can be effectively separated by absorption in amines liquids but recovery of result mixture is heat consuming and make this process economically unsatisfied. The expenditure of applying this CO2separation system in whole power industry of United States can be equal of the 30% of annual income related to the gross domestic product of this country2. Therefore, emersion of low consuming energy technology for substituting or promoting the existence ones has been completely predictable. This may be the main cause that membrane separations have been in the center of attention especially in recent decade. In this regard, extensive membranes with a wide range of materials and modules have been engineered to be effective for diminishing global warming trend by separating CO2as a greenhouse gas3. Among membrane materials, the polymeric ones possess significant superiority although they have gas permeability/selectivity tradeoff weakness. A promising approach to mitigate this limitation is the development of mixed matrix membranes (MMMs). These membranes incorporate inorganic or organic fillers within a polymeric matrix, offering a synergistic combination of properties. The fillers in MMMs alter the main gas transport mechanism of polymer membranes called solution-diffusion into a mixture of mechanisms including solution-diffusion, viscous flow, Knudsen, surface and molecular diffusion depending on fillers structure and the interfacial region status4. Among all used fillers in MMMs, a large number of studies have been devoted to metal organic frameworks (MOFs) with superior features such as high degree of surface area and aligned porosity, high affinity toward gas adsorption, well specified pore size and structure, tunable pore chemistry, rational resistance against mechanical and thermal stress, etc. In spite of organic moiety existence in MOFs structures which make them fillers with high compatibility with polymers, their pore sizes are not appropriate for some separation such as CO2/N2due to the nearly similar kinetic diameter of these gases. This matter has been addressed by revealing attempts for constructing dual channel porous fillers. By synthesizing various metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) alongside each other, it is possible to leverage the advantages of their diverse pore sizes and functional groups to achieve desired separations. In some cases, a framework with larger pore apertures can facilitate higher gas permeability, while another framework with smaller pore openings or additional functional groups may enhance selectivity towards a specific species. Furthermore, dual-pore fillers can improve interactions with the polymer matrix, effectively mitigating interfacial defects in mixed matrix membranes. These interfacial flaws are the major challenges ahead of scaling up the MMMs in industrial gas separation processes. So dual channel porous fillers are appeared as interesting policy to dominate the interfacial defects and also boosting gas separation properties of MMMs5.

Recently Liu et al. synthesizes a heterogeneous filler based on core-shell zeolitic imidazolate frameworks in which ZIF-L(Co) ZIF-8 are as core and shell, respectively. Due to the higher effective charge of Zn relative to Co, ZIF-8 shows more capability of CO2 adsorption. On the contrary, ZIF-L(Co) screens CO2 over CH4 due to its pore size (0.34 nm) which is between CO2 (0.33 nm) and CH4(0.38 nm) kinetic diameter sizes. In addition of promoted gas separation, the ZIF-8 shell connects to the PEBAX-1657 chains by hydrogen bonds and creates a defect free interfacial. The ideal interfacial region makes the MMMs mechanically high stable6. In another study by Song et al., a core-shell MOF/ZIF structure containing UiO-66-NH2 as core and ZIF-8 as shell is incorporated into polysulfone matrix. The large pores (0.6 nm) of UiO-66-NH2 create paths for faster transporting of gas molecules while the smaller pores (0.4 nm) of ZIF-8 exert molecular sieving mechanism for higher CO2/N2 gas selectivity. By this kind of pore engineering, remarkable CO2 permeability of 45.2 barrer and CO2/N2gas selectivity increase of 710% compared to the pare polysulfone membrane is registered. No interfacial defects is reported in this research indicating this strategy is a promising method to manufacture ideal MMMs7. In another MOFs engineering, Wu et al. construct a shell of MOF-74 on MOF-801 and its result MMM (26 wt%) with 6FDA-durene ((6FDA = 4,4′-(hexafluoroisopropylidene) diphthalic anhydride; durene = 2,3,5,6-tetramethyl-1,4-phenylenediamine) provides ethylene/ethane selectivity of 5.91 which in comparison with pure 6FDA-durene, 76% enhancement is recorded. MOF-74 conducts gas molecules through their 1-D channels toward the MOF-801 with small pore size. Conducted gas transport through the MMM and high interaction level of rough MOF-74 shell which contains abundant open metal sites with polymer leads to such obtained gas separation improvement8. To the best of our knowledge, the only aminoclay application in MMMs for gas separation has been reported by Lv et al. In this report, ZIF-8@aminoclay composites are built and doped into PEBAX matrix. ZIF@aminoclay provides two paths for transporting gas molecules, the internal path which is ZIF bulk and the external path which is created by aminoclay layers. The result MMM demonstrates CO2 permeability of 620 barrer and CO2/CH4selectivity of 40. The MMM surpasses the 2008 upper bound which shows multi-channel composites can be promising material for prospering MMM in gas separations9.

Amino organophyllosilicates khown as aminoclays (AC) are constructed from organotrialkoxylane and metal salts. The first synthesis of AC was presented by Mann et al. in 1997 through the sol-gel procedure10. ACs have a high density of amine functionalities which seems to be proper choices for separation of acidic gases like CO211,12. In addition, their high dispersity in water, easy synthesis and less environmental harmfulness makes them a special category among the widespread clays materials. Magnesium aminoclay (MgAC) is the most popular aminoclay has been used in various applications from catalysis of chemical reaction to biological carriers13. Amine functional groups abundance in AC can induce MOFs synthesis in nanoscale form11,12.

Copper terephthalic or copper 1,4-benzenedicarboxylate (CuBDC) is a two dimensional MOF with one dimensional channels (5.2 Å) consisting of BDC ligands which connect copper dimers to form paddlewheel structure. This MOF can be synthesized in nanosheet form to be more effective in CO2adsorption14. This MOF was synthesized by Mori et al. for the first time and then was structurally identified by Carson et al. in 2014 15,16. The layers of CuBDC are stacked together by van der waals interactions. The pore size of CuBDC can be appropriate for exerting the screening effect for CO2 gas with lower kinetic diameter of 3.3 Å against N2 with larger kinetic diameter of 3.6 Å. In addition of screening effect, the CuBDC has been recognized as a material with high tendency for CO2adsorption. The nanosheet structure of CuBDC can provide higher surface to interact with the polymer matrix and reduces the flaws in interfacial area. The thickness of nanosheets can be varied from 10 to 400 nm. The nanosheets with lower number of monolayers can be in favor of both gas diffusion and interfacial interaction with polymer matrix in MMMs17. Using aminoclay as platform can assist to form CuBDC nanosheets in lower populations. The nanosheets with low thickness can shorten the penetration path for the gas molecules especially for the preferential one, means CO217.

MOFs with extra free functional groups in their structures can also been perfect choices for CO2 adsorption or separation. UiO-66-(COOH)2 was first prepared by yang et al. in 2013 by a facile method using water as solvent. In three dimensional structure of UiO-66-(COOH)2, octahedral units of zirconium oxide are connected through the pyromellitic acid as a ligand with four carboxylic acid functionality. The MOF structure contains cages in octahedral and tetrahedral form. Due to the extra acid functional groups, pore size and volume of UiO-66-(COOH)2 are reported in lower values relative to the UiO-66. The pore size of 6.8 Å is expressed for UiO-66-(COOH)2 which is larger than the kinetic diameters of both CO2 and N2 molecules. However, this MOF has been revealed as a promising candidate for CO2 adsorption due to interaction of the free carboxylic acid groups in its pores with CO2molecules18,19.

The second selected MOF with additional free functional groups in structure is MIL-121 which was introduced by Loiseau et al. in 2010. Three dimensional structure of this MOF is manufactured by linking the AlO4(OH)2 units with octahedral structure by two carboxylate groups of pyromellitic and the other two carboxylate groups of the ligand preserved their free protonated form. The pores with size of 8.7 × 5.7 Å are in open form due to the existence of free carboxylate groups in the pores. Large pores of MIL-121 can participate in gas permeability with no screening effect. Meanwhile due to the CO2 interaction capability of free carboxylate groups in the pores, This MOF not only provides the broad ways to permeate gas molecules but the abundant interacting sites in its pores can impress gas selectivity toward CO220,21.

In this research, based on designing of multi channels fillers strategy, a collection of mixed matrix membranes (MMMs) with poly(ether-block-amide) matrix is constructed and used for separation of CO2 and N2 gases. The added composites to the poly(ether-block-amide) matrix have been made of aminoclays (MgAC, ZrAC) and metal-organic frameworks. Two composite categories of UiO-66-(COOH)2@CuBDC@MgAC and MIL-121@CuBDC@MgAC are utilized as fillers for polymer matrix. Each composite category by various constituents contains four composites and therefore eight MMMs with 10 wt% are fabricated from eight composites. Then, a layer of ZrAC is constructed over the last eight composites (ZrAC@UiO-66-(COOH)2@CuBDC@MgAC and ZrAC@MIL-121@CuBDC@MgAC) and eight MMMs with 10 wt% are prepared from these new composites. The permeability of CO2 and N2 gases and CO2/N2 selectivity are measured through constant pressure/variable volume procedure.

Experimental

Materials

The materials in this paper provided from Sigma-Aldrich are as follows: MgCl2.6H2O, Cu(NO3)2.3H2O, ZrCl4, Al(NO3)3.9H2O, (3-aminopropyl) triethoxysilane (APTES), benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid (BDC), 1.2.4.5-benzenetetracarboxylic acid (BTEC) and CH3COOH, N, N-dimethylformamide. The named materials (> 99% of purity) are used as received with no purification process implementation. The CO2 and N2 gases are provided from Roham gas company with purity of 99.9%. PEBAX-1657 is purchased from Arkema, France.

Synthesis of pure MgAC, CuBDC, UiO-66-(COOH)2, MIL-121

MgAC is synthesized by dissolving 0.84 g of MgCl2.6H2O in 20 ml absolute ethanol and dropwise adding of APTES (1.3 ml) into it. After 24 h stirring the emulsion, the white solid product is centrifuged and washing with ethanol is done several times. MgAC is dried at 60 C for 6 h22. In synthesis of CuBDC, 0.4 g Cu(NO3)2.3H2O and 0.275 g BDC are fully dissolved in a mixture of DMF (20 ml) solvents and the result blue solution is heated in an autoclave at 120 °C for 24 h. Then the cooled product is centrifuged and rinsed with DMF/H2O for three or four times. The achieved CuBDC is dried at 80 °C for 12 h 23. In synthesis of UiO-66-(COOH)2, 100 ml of deionized H2O/CH3COOH (60/40) is poured into a reflux equipped flask and BTEC (2.45 g) and ZrCl4(2.33 g) are added to the solvents mixture. The flask is kept under reflux at 100 °C for 24 h with stirring. Then, the white product is centrifuged and immersed in anhydrous methanol for three days in which methanol is exchanged every day. The final precipitate should not smell acetic acid and is dried at 80 °C for 12 h18. Hydrothermal procedure is employed to synthesize MIL-121. In this regard, 0.8 gr of BTEC and 2.33 gr of Al(NO3)3.9H2O are dissolved in 60 ml of deionized H2O at 60 °C. The solution is delivered in an autoclave. Closed autoclave is maintained in oven at 80 °C for 24 h. The result white material is centrifuged and rinsed with deionized water for several times. The product is dried at 70 °C for 12 h 20.

Synthesis of three components composites (Zr@Cu@Mg and Al@Cu@Mg)

For synthesis of composites, MgAC is dispersed in CuBDC precursor i.e. Cu(NO3)2.3H2O and BDC in DMF/H2O by stirring at room temperature. The synthesis procedure is followed according to the CuBDC synthesis mentioned above. The product of this synthesis is used for next step which is UiO-66-(COOH)2 (Fig. 1) or MIL-121 synthesis. The product of MgAC and CuBDC from last step is added to the UiO-66-(COOH)2 or MIL-121 precursor and procedure is continued according to the pure UiO-66-(COOH)2 or MIL-121 synthesis. For each combination, MgAC-CuBDC-UiO-66-(COOH)2 and MgAC-CuBDC-MIL-121, four composites are constructed which the precursor amounts of outer layers, UiO-66-(COOH)2 (2.45 gr BTEC and 2.33 gr ZrCl4) or MIL-121 (0.8 gr BTEC and 2.33 gr Al(NO3)3.9H2O) are constant in all four. The composites are UiO-66-(COOH)2@CuBDC@MgAC (1–4) and MIL-121@CuBDC@MgAC (1–4) which are abbreviated as Zr@Cu@Mg (1–4) and Al@Cu@Mg (1–4), respectively. The MgAC amount in composites numbers 1 and 3 is 29.1 mg (5 wt% relative to the used for CuBDC synthesis) and in numbers 2 and 4 is 116.5 mg (5 wt% relative to the used for CuBDC synthesis). The amount of CuBDC precursor (1.165 gr Cu(NO3)2.3H2O and 0.801 gr BDC) in composites 1 and 2 is equal and different from that (2.330 gr Cu(NO3)2.3H2O and 1.602 gr BDC) of composites numbers 3 and 4 (Table 1).

Synthesis of four components composites (Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg and Zr@Al@Cu@Mg)

For covering the last constructed composites with Zr-aminoclay, a suspension of each composite (1 gr) in 20 ml ethanol is used and like MgAC synthesis, 0.84 gr ZrCl4 is poured to the suspension. Then 1.3 ml APTES is gradually mixed with the solution and the solution is left to be stirred for 24 h at room temperature. The Continuation is according to the MgAC synthesis procedure. The composites are ZrAC@UiO-66-(COOH)2@CuBDC@MgAC (1–4) and ZrAC@MIL-121@CuBDC@MgAC (1–4) which are abbreviated as Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg (1–4) and Zr@Al@Cu@Mg (1–4), respectively.

Membrane fabrication

Proper amount of PEBAX is dissolved in mixture of 7 ml ethanol and 3 ml H2O by stirring at 80 °C under reflux for 5 h. The prepared PEBAX solution is decanted into a petri dish with diameter of 8 cm. the result film is achieved by placing petri dish in oven at 80 °C for 3 h. For MMMs preparation, the same plan is carried out except there is addition of filler stage. In this regard, the weighed filler is suspended in ethanol/H2O by stirring rigorously overnight. Thereafter, before polymer addition, the mixture is bath sonicated for 20 min to eliminate the filler agglomeration. Then, PEBAX is added in two steps in which during the first one, 10% of PEBAX is added and the rest of it is added after 3 h. This method has been known as priming plan for polymer-based composite fabricate. The thickness of the fabricated membranes is determined by averaging the thickness of 5 different points of each membrane. The average 60–80 μm is recorded by using a micrometer for such measurement.

Characterization

The FT-IR to characterize functional groups of samples through wavenumbers 400–4000 cm−1. The powder samples are prepared in tablet form by using KBr. The copper target (Kα1 λ = 1.54, 40 mA, 40 kV) equipped diffractometer apparatus (PHILIPS, PW1730) is employed to record X-ray diffraction patterns. The patterns are earned at 2θ of 5 to 40̊ with step size of 0.05 using a sample holder made of single crystal of silicon with no diffraction pattern. Perkin-Elmer thermal analyzer provides thermogravimetric analysis of samples under air environment from room temperature to 600 °C with heating rate of 5 °C/min utilizing 5 mg sample for each experiment. Scanning electron microscopy (TESCAN, MIRA II) is used to obtain pictures from powder samples and membranes. The imaging of membranes is recorded from cross-section areas. In sample preparation process, the membranes are placed in N2 liquid for few seconds then are tapped to be broken. The samples are fixed from broken area in holders. Before imaging, the gold covering of samples are accomplished by sputtering process due to the non-conductivity of samples. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and high resolution Transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) are achieved by TEM Philips EM 208 S and HR-TEM FEI TECNAI F20. Water contact angle is accomplished to show the level of hydrophilicity of constructed membranes. For accomplishment, a droplet of deionized water is launched onto the dried membrane surface by a micro-syringe. Then, the image of water droplet status on the membrane surface is recorded by e high-resolution camera.

Results and discussions

Characterization of pure MgAC, CuBDC, UiO-66-(COOH)2 and MIL-121

The FT-IR spectra, XRD patterns and N2 adsorption-desorption of MgAC, CuBDC, UiO-66-(COOH)2 and MIL-121 are provided in Fig. 3. MgAC (Si8R8Mg6O16(OH)4) (R = CH2CH2NH2) is composed of two tetrahedral aminopropyl functionalized silicate sheets surrounding a sheet of octahedral MgO/OH in the middle. The mentioned structure is a 2:1 smectite format such as talc. In FT-IR spectrum of MgAC (Fig. 3a), the appeared peaks can be assigned as follows: OH/3478 cm−1, CH2/2954, 3010 cm−1, NH2/1617 cm−1, CH2/1452 cm−1, Si-O-Si/ 1137 cm−1, Si-OH/1075 cm−1, Si-O-C/818 cm−1 and Mg-O/591 cm−1. The XRD patterns (Fig. 3b) of MgAC shows peaks at 2θ = 6.4, 23.1, and 35 to d(001) = 1.41 nm, d(110),(020) = 0.38 nm and d(130),(200)= 0.27 nm13. As shown in Fig. 3a, the characterizing functional groups of CuBDC peaks are placed corresponding to the following wavenumbers: aromatic C-H/2926, 3070 cm−1, C = O/1658 cm−1, aromatic C = C/1570, 1501, 1388, cm−1, C-O/923 cm−1, Cu-O/738, 468 cm−1. The drying CuBDC in 180 ˚C leads to removing coordinated DMF molecules from CuBDC structure. Therefore, the recorded XRD peaks (Fig. 3b) are according with the pattern of desolvated CuBDC structure reported in literature. The peaks at 2θ = 8.2, 15.3 and 26.5˚ are indicative of (110), (201) and (131) crystalline planes23.

FT-IR spectra (a) and XRD patterns (b) of synthesized parent materials (the inset in (b) is MgAC XRD with higher intensity).

In FT-IR of UiO-66-(COOH)2 (Fig. 3a), the peaks at 1727 and 3404 cm−1 indicate the C = O and OH groups of the ligand which are in high population due to the extra carboxylic acid functionality. Two main peaks relative to the crystallographic planes of (111) and (200) at 2θ = 7.4 and 8.4˚ are revealed in XRD patterns of UiO-66-(COOH)2 (Fig. 3b)18,19. The advent peaks in FT-IR spectra of MIL-121 (Fig. 3a) are assigned as follows: Non-coordinated -COOH/3019, 2684, 2545 cm−1, -COOH with hydrogen bond/3478 cm−1, C = O of -COO− connected to Al+3/1617 cm−1, C = O of free -COOH/1666, 1759 cm−1. The XRD pattern of MIL-121 (Fig. 3b) demonstrates some characteristic peaks at 2θ = 8.5, 12.9, 14.5, 17, 19.4, 21.9, 25.8, 29.6˚ depicting crystalline planes of (110), (020), (111), (220), (400), (330) and (222) which are in agreement with the pattern reported in literature20,21.

Zr@Cu@Mg and Al@Cu@Mg composites and corresponding MMMs

Characterization of Zr@Cu@Mg and Al@Cu@Mg composites

The prepared three components composites made from MgAC and CuBDC with outer layer of UiO-66-(COOH)2 or MIL-121 are investigated by FT-IR and XRD analysis (Fig. 4). The FT-IR of all composites shows characteristic peak of unoccupied C = O in COOH groups at wave number of 1737–1746 cm−1 for Zr@Cu@Mg composites and at 1703–1713 cm−1for Al@Cu@Mg composites. The shift in peaks is attributed to the unsimilar chemical environment of each composite24. The two strong peaks at 1626 and 1450 cm−1 for Zr@Cu@Mg composites and at 1585 and 1436 cm−1 for are indicative of asymmetric and symmetric stretch of COO of involved carboxylate groups in matching with metals in composites. The peak at 1506 cm−1 can be assigned to the C = C of benzene ring for Zr@Cu@Mg composites. The equivalent peak for Al@Cu@Mg composites is placed at 1514 cm−1.

FT-IR spectra and XRD patterns of synthesized Zr@Cu@Mg and Al@Cu@Mg composites.

The XRD patterns of the four Zr@Cu@Mg composites are nearly similar to each other and also resembling to the XRD pattern of pure UiO-66-(COOH)2. Observation of CuBDC peaks is disabled probably due to the nanosheets formation of this material instead of bundles of layers. MgAC layers can act as platforms for growing CuBDC nanosheets. Abundant of anchors provided by MgAC platforms leads to prevent of staking up of CuBDC sheets. The main crystallographic plane of CuBDC nanosheets is (201) which seems to be covered with the peaks belong to UiO-66-(COOH)2otherwise, the diffraction peaks related to the bulk CuBDC are not observed14. In the case of Al@Cu@Mg composites, the scenario is different. The composite numbers of 1, 2 and 4 of Al@Cu@Mg show the same pattern with different intensities. These peaks are revealed at 2θ = 8.5, 10.3, 17.3, 25.2, 27.9 and 29.7˚. Since CuBDC is formed in nanosheets by induction of MgAC active sites, the observed peaks can be due to the preferred orientation of MIL-121 in the present of CuBDC nanosheets. Meanwhile, the Al@Cu@Mg-3 composite demonstrates the same XRD pattern of pure MIL-121 which might be due to the high concentration of free MIL-121 formation.

The SEM, TEM, HRTEM and selected area electron diffraction (SAED) images of Zr@Cu@Mg-4 composite are provided in Fig. 5 (a-e). The TEM of Zr@Cu@Mg-4 composite shows separated round shapes with size of 50–200 nm while the particle aggregation is evident in its SEM. The HRTEM and fast Fourier transform (FFT) patterns (the insets of HRTEM images) show the existence of various crystalline planes in composite.

Characterization of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs

The characteristic properties of pure PEBAX and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs, FT-IR and TGA are shown in Fig. 6. The achieved spectrum for pure PEBAX membrane indicates peak at 1113 cm−1 which belongs to the C-O-C stretching of flexible polyethyleneoxide. The rigid polyamide functional groups of -N-H-, O-C = O and H-N-C = O are characterized by peaks at 3330, 1745 and 1664 cm−125. In the spectra of MMMs compared to the pure PEBAX some shifts and new peaks are detected. For instance, the peak at 1745 cm−1 is shifted to the 1750–1765 cm−1 for MMMs. Existence of polar functional groups in PEBAX backbone and constructed composites can enable the interactions which impress the frequency of bonds vibrations in IR consequently provide shifts in observed peaks in contrast with pure PEBAX membrane. In four PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs a new peak is observed at 772 cm−1which might be related to the Cu-O26. This peak is shifted to the higher wavenumber (807 cm−1) in the case of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs. The other new peak in four PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs is located at 645 cm−1which can be due to the Al-O connection27. In PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs, Zr-(OC) asymmetric stretch creates a new peak at 560 cm−1 26. The extra peak at 530 cm−1for all MMMs can be assigned to the Mg-O bond22.

FT-IR (a), TGA (b) of prepared membranes.

The TGA and DTG curves of pure membrane and MMMs demonstrate equivalent pattern (Fig. 6 (b) and Fig. S1(Supporting Information)). They all show a step before 100 ˚C belongs to the solvent elimination from membranes. This step contains almost 4% of weight reduction. Then there is a plateau up to the second step of weight reduction which commences about 300 ˚C and reaches to about 420 ˚C. This step is the major degradation process which is related to the polymer deterioration28. The commencement temperature of this step is different for pure PEBAX membrane and MMMs depending on their thermal stability. For pure PEBAX, this temperature is 340 ˚C and for Al@Cu@Mg based MMMs, this temperature is increased to 347, 343, 356 and 349 ˚C in order of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 to PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-4. Meanwhile, the Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs start degradation process in lower temperature compared to that of pure PEBAX membrane, in which the temperatures of 297, 331, 326 and 298 ˚C are recorded for PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-1 to PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-4, respectively. Since PEBAX is formed from two different flexible and rigid parts, its the thermal degradation is happened in two steps apart from the solvent elimination step. In TGA curves in Fig. 6(b), the third step is obviously present in temperature range of 440 to 525 ˚C. This step can be attributed to the oxidation degradation of polymer residue29.

The effect of constructed composites on Young’s modulus, elongation and tensile strength of membranes are explored by providing stress-strain curves and the results are gathered in Table 2and depicted in Fig. S3 (Supporting Information). In an overall view, the PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs render more desirable mechanical properties compared to PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs. In the case of Young’s modulus which is a criterion for withstanding of membranes against deforming, all MMMs are in promoted states compared to the pure PEBAX membrane. Among PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs, PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-4 show higher Young’s modulus compared to those of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-2. In PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-4, the CuBDC content is higher and it seems that in addition of better filler-polymer interaction, the MOF-MOF interaction is also in good situation. On the contrary, for PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs, the PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-2 MMMs demonstrate higher Young’s modulus relative to the PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-3 and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-4 MMMs. This means in higher CuBDC content composites, the polymer-filler or MOF-MOF interactions gets in undesirable situation. In PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-2, the polymer chains might penetrate into the pores of composites in higher level or chain rigidification around composites is stronger, that can be the reason of lower elongation at break of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 (3.93%) and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-2 (5.18%) relative to the PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-3 (18.10%) and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-4 (11.16%). However, the minimum tensile strength of MMMs belongs to PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-2. Since tensile strength depends on bearing the load, this bearing should happen in whole membrane including the polymer-filler and MOF-MOF boundaries. Lack of integration in MMM originated from the polymer-filler and MOF-MOF interaction or filler agglomeration even in imperceptible way can disrupt the load bearing and decreases tensile strength30. The highest elongation at fracture and Young’s modulus are reported for PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-3 MMM with desirable level of tensile strength (6.11 MPa) compared to the other MMMs. Apparently, this composition behaves good in both elasticity and plastic deformation areas. Since PEBAX contains both flexible and stiff segments and the interaction of both segments with composites are not the same, so various mechanical behavior can be observed. Ductility and brittleness are dominant features in PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs, respectively while Young’s moduli of both categories are higher than that of pure PEBAX membrane. The higher tensile strength of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs collection in comparison with PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg collection shows existence of some incompatibility and weaknesses points in the latter ones.

Gas permeability behavior of fabricated MMMs

Each constituents of the composites (fillers), MgAC, CuBDC, UiO-66-(COOH)2 and MIL-121 have intrinsic features which make them appropriate choices for CO2separation. According to the XRD results of synthesized composites, absence of CuBDC diffraction peaks can be originated from the lack of CuBDC layers stacking in high thicknesses31. Indeed MgAC can provide surfaces for anchoring the first Cu ions through the pendant amines. Then the 2D CuBDC sheets are fabricated around the MgACs. High numbers of MgAC platforms for CuBDC sheets growth prevent piling up the CuBDC sheets and lead to decrease sheets population in each platforms site. In addition of this role for MgAC, the amines groups of aminoclay can be involved in forming carbamate by reversible reaction with CO232. This event can help to facilitate CO2 transport through the MMMs.

CuBDC is a microporous MOF which contains holes with pore window of 5.2 Å. This size can differentiate the transport of CO2 with lower kinetic diameter and higher diffusion rate against N2. In addition of molecular sieving, CuBDC embraces unsaturated Cu spots which have high tendency for CO2adsorption17. Therefore, CuBDC can participate in gas permeability of MMMs by both screening and surface diffusion mechanisms. In composites structure, the CuBDC layers are flourished with UiO-66-(COOH)2 or MIL-121. Thus, the interphase posture between CuBDC and UiO-66-(COOH)2 or MIL-121 can be as important as the MOFs structures.

UiO-66-(COOH)2 has been recognized as a high chemical stable and CO2 friendly MOF which can be synthesized through a simple protocol in water environment. Distinguished characteristic of UiO-66-(COOH)2 is the decorated pores with excess carboxylic acid functional groups which are strong interacting sites for CO2 permeability. On the other hand, large pores of this MOF (6.8 Å) can intensify speedy gas molecules diffusion with superiority diffusion relative to CO2. MIL-121 is another MOF synthesized with pyromellitic acid with four carboxylic acid functionality which two acid groups are taken part in MOF building and the other two are decorating the MOF pores. Although large pore size of this MOF can let gas molecules transport with no size based screening, CO2 diffusion is in more preference compared with N2 due to the interaction of pendant carboxyl groups in MOF pores with CO2molecules19.

Gas permeability of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs

The pure gas permeability of constructed membranes are measured using pure flow of each gas. Measurements are accomplished at feed pressure of 5 bar and constant temperature of 323 K. The gathered numbers are drawn as chart in Fig. 9. In elucidation of gas separation mechanism of MMMs, it can be noted that besides of solution-diffusion mechanism of PEBAX matrix, all constituents of composites and the interfacial region of composites-PEBAX are involved for gas transport through the MMMs. For an ideal status, the following hypothesis can be explained for the composites participation in gas transport in MMMs: In PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg or PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs series, large pores of UiO-66-(COOH)2 or MIL-121 provide paths for moving gas molecules especially for CO2 molecules due to the interaction with the free –COOH groups in MOF’s structure. Then transferred gas molecules from UiO-66-(COOH)2 pores are exposed to the with lower pore diameter. In addition of higher screening power of CuBDC pores, its CO2-philic nature stimulates more CO2 molecules for transferring. After CuBDC nanosheets, the gas molecules are faced with aminoclay layars. Both layers tortuosity and amine functional groups of aminoclay exerts another screening effect in favor of CO2 gas with lower kinetic diameter and higher potency for interactions. Depending on composition of various constructed composites, each parts (UiO-66-(COOH)2 or MIL-121, CuBDC, MgAC) might have higher or lower effect in gas permeability of MMMs. Furthermore, the MOF-MOF and composites-PEBAX interfacial areas should also be considered for obtaining correct justification of gas permeability and selectivity of MMMs. In following paragraphs, the MMMs are explored individually and together for their gas behaviors.

Gas permeabilities and CO2/N2 selectivity of constructed membranes (Black and green columns are referred to CO2 and N2 permeabilities, respectively).

CO2 permeability and CO2/N2 selectivity of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs are higher than those of pure PEBAX membrane. PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-2 show higher CO2 permeability and lower CO2/N2 selectivity in comparison with PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-1. These two MMMs consist of equal CuBDC and UiO-66-(COOH)2 but different MgAC. There might an increase in free volume of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-2 compared to the PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-1 33. This free volume can originated from free MgAC sheets in these MMMs which is explained as follows.

In the case of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-4, both CO2 permeability and CO2/N2 selectivity are enhanced from number 3 to number 4. In these two MMMs, the contents of CuBDC and UiO-66-(COOH)2 are identical but value of MgAC is increased from 3 to 4. It seems enhancing in CuBDC value in composites (3 and 4) renders affirmative effect on gas separation behavior of MMMs. When CuBDC value is increased in composites (3 and 4), the stock of MgAC is playing the role of platforms for growth of CuBDC planes while in Zr@Cu@Mg-1 and Zr@Cu@Mg-2 with half CuBDC content, all of the MgAC stock is not participated in interested composites formation. Thus, the formed free MgAC sheets can cover the surface of the composites or can be separated as filler in MMMs. To have a criterion for performance of MgAC in PEBAX, the MMMs contains 10 wt% of MgAC is also built and shows CO2 permeability of 235 barrer and CO2/N2 selectivity of 12.69. Although 10 wt% of MgAC in PEBAX provides 30% CO2 permeability increment but CO2/N2 selectivity decreases 55% relative to the pure PEBAX membrane. Indeed, it is expected that with the intimate interacting of the existing amine groups in MgAC structure with PEBAX backbone, this MMM demonstrates the opposite observed gas behavior. One explanation for this observation is the increasing in free volume of PEBAX with the presence of MgAC sheets. Since PEBAX is formed from both flexible and rigid blocks, this idea is not far-fetched. The MgAC sheets can interfere to decrease PEBAX crystallinity by communication with functional groups of the chains. Decrease in crystallinity leads to increase in free volume of the PEBAX.

Now in PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-2 which there is possibility of free MgAC sheets formation especially in PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-2, in spite of desire CO2 permeability, CO2/N2 selectivity is too low compared with PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-4. On the other hand, a MMM with 10 wt% of pure CuBDC is constructed and display CO2 permeability of 254.85 barrer and CO2/N2 selectivity of 94.15. These results can tempt the researcher in gas separation MMMs to be big fans of CuBDC and PEBAX combination. Therefore, not only perfect organized composites in PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-4 are effective in rendering such high CO2 permeability and CO2/N2 selectivity but high content of CuBDC with CO2-friend nature is also evidently impressive as well23. In the meantime, UiO-66-(COOH)2 is the outer layer of Zr@Cu@Mg composites plays crucial role in gas permeability of MMMs. In addition of selective tendency of UiO-66-(COOH)2 toward CO2, the most interphase interaction of PEBAX with composites is up to it. According to the SEM study and mechanical properties of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs, no empty holes at the interphase are deduced. Large pores of UiO-66-(COOH)2 can be host for penetration of PEBAX chains especially the flexible PEO blocks. This penetration can guarantee the matching of composites to the PEBAX and lowers the weaknesses at the interphases. On the other hand, diminishing pore size of UiO-66-(COOH)2 by polymer chains can induce molecular sieving effect in UiO-66-(COOH)2. So, UiO-66-(COOH)2 discriminates gas molecules according their size and interaction power which obviously CO2 is the winner of competition against N234. Even in the meantime, chain tightening may also occur around the composites, but due to the good plasticity of these membranes in the mechanical test, this morphology is much less likely to occur. If this morphology would occur in high extent, it is expected that the MMMs will become fragile while the opposite trend is recorded.

In addition of composite constituents properties, the interphase of the MOF-MOF is a serious section of the composite which can be as effective as the MOFs themselves in gas separation of MMMs8. Intense difference between CO2/N2 selectivity of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-2 with PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-4 can be also relevant to the interphase of CuBDC and UiO-66-(COOH)2 in which smaller pores with higher sized sieving effect toward gases are formed. Hence, intrinsic MgAC and MOFs features, preventation of MgAC free sheets creating and complete composites formation, MOF-MOF interphase and PEBAX/composites interaction are all the hypothesized issues for justifying such privileged gas permeability and selectivity especially in the cases of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-4 MMMs. Apparently, in these MMMs collections, both polymer-composites and MOF-MOF interphases have shown synergistic effect in gas separation of MMMs from number 1 to 4.

Gas permeability of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs

In the gas performance consideration of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs (Fig. 9), the opposite trend is noticeable. The opposite trend means that PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-2 MMMs better gas separation acting compared to the PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-3 and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-4. The only component that has been changed from Al@Cu@Mg-1 and Al@Cu@Mg-2 to Al@Cu@Mg-3 and Al@Cu@Mg-4 is CuBDC value. By increasing this value, the gas separation of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs is droped while CuBDC is a proper candidate for CO2 separation. Since thermal, mechanical (Young’s modoli) and morphological analysis of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs are not failed, so deficiency in interphase of polymer matrix and composites cannot be responsible for such gas separation depletion. One conceivable hypothesis can be concerned to the MOF-MOF interphase in Al@Cu@Mg composites. It seems some large pores might be created at the interphase of MOF-MOF which transport gases with lower selectivity. In Al@Cu@Mg-4 relative to Al@Cu@Mg-3, MgAC is increased which leads to formation of CuBDC in high stack numbers with lower layers. Therefore, the interphase of CuBDC-MIL-121 is enhanced in Al@Cu@Mg-4 compare to Al@Cu@Mg-3. As mentioned, this interphase is likely not favored for gas separation of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs, so PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-4 MMM renders the worst gas separation behavior. It should be noted that filler agglomeration happens in some level in PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-4 which cannot be ignored in the gas behavior of this MMM. Although, the CO2 permeability of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-4 demonstrates 147% increment relative to the pure PEBAX membrane but in the similar comparison, its CO2/N2 selectivity is decreased 83%.

Exceptional CO2/N2 selectivity of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 MMM can be related to what happens at the PEBAX and composite outer layer which is MIL-121. High penetration of polymer chains into the large pores of MIL-121 can diminish transport paths of gases especially the bigger ones. The MMM constructed from pure MIL-121 and PEBAX show CO2 permeability of 218.44 barrer and CO2/N2 selectivity of 53.57. Among the MMMs constructed from the pure form of three selected MOFs (CuBDC, UiO-66-(COOH)2 and MIL-121), the lowest values of CO2 permeability and CO2/N2 selectivity belong to one which is prepared with MIL-121. In describing the matter that why PEBAX chains penetration effect is not sensible in PEBAX/MIL-121 MMM, it can be mentioned that other existing interaction sites in composites such as amine groups of MgAC or unoccupied Cu ions in CuBDC can be significantly effective in pulling the polymer chains into the pores. Even the created large pores at the MOF-MOF cannot be neglected for such polymer chains penetration. So partly pore blockage of filler and rigidified polymer chains at the interphase are the most potent reasons for such low CO2 permeability and high CO2/N2 selectivity of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 MMM. By moving from PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 to PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-2, 3 and 4, the polymer/composite interphase effect on gas separation behavior of MMM is replaced by MOF-MOF interphase effect. Thus the gas exhibition of the PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 and 2 MMMs are mostly influenced by polymer-composites interphase while for PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-3 and 4, the prominent effect is originated from MOF-MOF interphase. It should be noticed that both partly pore blockage of filler and rigidified polymer chains are occurred at the polymer-Al@Cu@Mg composites interphacial regions but their effects are neutralized by virtues of CuBDC-MIL-121 interphases for PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-3 and 4. The high brittleness (lower elongation at break %) of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs compared to PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs can be an evidence of occurring the mentioned interphacial morphologies for PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs in much higher extent. Penetration of PEBAX chains into pores of MIL-121 can limit the chains movements and rigidified segments are the fragile parts of the PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs which both lead to flexibility diminishing. On the other hand, in addition of elongation at break, tensile strengths of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg MMMs are in more unfavorable conditions compared to those of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs. The reason can be attributed to the CuBDC/MIL-121 interphase area which can acted as infirmity sites which let MMMs fail in lower tensile strength.

After addition of APTES to the Zr4+ added emulsion of Zr@Cu@Mg or Al@Cu@Mg composites, the APTES is connected to any unoccupied ion sites. So, there is possibility of formation of lower amount of Zr-aminoclay in composites since both Zr4+ and APTES are being busy with other free coordination sites in composites.

Gas separation behavior of PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg and PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg MMMs

After impact exploration of Zr@Cu@Mg and Al@Cu@Mg composites on PEBAX gas separation, the Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg and Zr@Al@Cu@Mg composites are also prepared and inserted to PEBAX matrix and interesting results are recorded (Fig. 12). In addition of mentioned explanation in Sect. 3.2.3.1 for gas transport through the MMMs by composites, in PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs series, an additional separation effect is employed by ZrAC layers and higher CO2 permeability is achieved with high CO2/N2 selectivity.

Gas permeabilities and CO2/N2 selectivity of constructed membranes (Black and green columns are referred to CO2 and N2 permeabilities, respectively).

The MMM containing ZrAC as the only filler is also fabricated and CO2 permeability and CO2/N2 selectivity of 235.25 and 12.69 are recorded, respectively. It is clear that ZrAC is not a proper filler for insertion into PEBAX in gas separation application. The same thing is also reported for MgAC. But cooporation of aminoclays with MOFs apparently provides synergistic effect in gas separation.

In Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg and Zr@Al@Cu@Mg composites synthesis, by addition of ZrCl4 to the Zr@Cu@Mg and Al@Cu@Mg, the long distance disciplines of MOFs are exposed to destroy. ZrCl4 is counted as a Lewis acid and can coordinate to free functional groups of composites or even grab coordinating sites from other metallic cations. Therefore, composites become amorphised and interacting sites increase. By following the ZrAC synthesis protocol and APTES addition, a large number of amine groups are appeared in Composites. APTES molecules might participate in ZrAC formation or connect to any free coordinating site in composites. Thus, it seems that ZrAC can balance both interphase interactions in polymer-composites and MOF-MOF. In PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs, the small pores of CuBDC-UiO-66-(COOH)2 interphase are probably influenced by ZrCl4 interference and lose their size exclusion in some level. On the other hand, the PEBAX chains are more involved with interaction of abundant amine groups of APTES and their penetrations into composites structure are declined. So the chain rigidification and partly pore blockage phenomena are greatly decreased.

The CO2 permeability and CO2/N2 selectivity of PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-2 MMMs are considerably increased relative to the PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-2 MMMs. The reason of this trend can be high population of interacting spots such as amine groups of ZrAC, unoccupied metal cations and unbound carboxylates with CO2 as a quadrupole molecule against N238. Additionally, chaos is not occurred only in MOFs structures but the MOF-MOF interphase is also impressed and the small pores turn into the bigger one which can transport gas molecules with higher rate but with preserving selectivity toward CO2 due to the mentioned interactions. On the other hand, ZrAC can remove polymer-composites interphase morphologies (chain rigidification and partly pore blockage) which leads to gas permeability increase. Meanwhile since the polymer-composites interphase does not tolerate undesired morphologies, CO2/N2 selectivity is also enhanced. Since in addition of all interactions, a chemical interaction can also form between amine groups and PEBAX chains according to the Fig. 13, therefore, the polymer-composites interphase can almost be supposed to be defect free.

Possible chemical structure between PEBAX and APTES.

In the case of PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-4, their CO2 permeability and CO2/N2 selectivity experienced increment and decrement, respectively, relative to their analogue membranes with Zr@Cu@Mg composites. The same justification mentioned above is governed here, too but there is a difference. The area of MOF-MOF and polymer-composites interphases in PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and 4 MMMs are higher than those of PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-1 and 2. Therefore, the decrease in CO2/N2 selectivity can be logical. It is important to notice that although the CO2/N2 selectivity by adding ZrAC is decreased for PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and 4 MMMs but the selectivity is still high and magnificent.

By addition ZrAC to Al@Cu@Mg and constructing PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg collections, a different gas separation behavior for each MMM is observed compared to its PEBAX/ Al@Cu@Mg analogues. In PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg MMMs collection by increasing CuBDC and MgAC of the composites, gas separation improves. It seems the nonselective CuBDC-MIL-121 interphase with large pores convert to a layer with irregular small pores with high selectivity talent. Therefore, with increasing MOF-MOF interphase from Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-1 to 4, the gas separation is also getting better. That is why the incredible CO2/N2 selectivity of 225 and 155.31 is recorded for PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-2 and 4 MMMs against 74.71 and 5.28 for PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-2 and 4 MMMs. Meanwhile, the decrease in CO2 permeability of PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-2 and 4 MMMs (251.96 and 294.81 barrer) relative to PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-2 and 4 MMMs (320.91 and 436.89 barrer) should be pointed. On the other hand, the PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-1 MMM exhibits too much lower CO2/N2 selectivity (57.41) and higher CO2 permeability (359.17 barrer) in comparison with PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 MMM (352.94, 152.64 barrer). As expressed before, the gas performance of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 is impressed by partly pore blockage and chain rigidification interphacial morphologies. By ZrAC, these morphologies disappear and higher gas molecules with low preference pass through the PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-1 MMM. Both CO2 permeability and CO2/N2 selectivity of PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-3 MMM are ameliorated compared to those of PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-3 MMM. With lower MgAC content of Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-3, the MOF-MOF interphase is less, so, its CO2/N2 selectivity is increased in lower level compared to PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-2 and 4 MMMs. On the other hand, by removing polymer-composites interphase with gas barrier feature, the CO2 permeability of PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-3 MMM enhances to 382.28 barrer from 320.91 barrer for PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-3 MMM.

Water surface contact angle and gas separation behavior of MMMs

The water contact angles of MMMs and pure PEBAX are illustrated in Fig. 14. The contact angle of pure PEBAX membrane is 70.5° which confirms the hydrophilic nature of this copolymer. By adding the composites to the PEBAX matrix, different higher and lower contact angles of MMMs relative to the pure membrane are recorded. The contact angle can be concerned to both morphological and chemical aspects of membranes. When the population of hydrophilic functional groups are enhanced, the membrane surface tendency to the water is increased and contact angle is decreased. From morphological point of view, the membrane crystallinity should be considered. When the crystallinity of the membrane is increased and the chains are packed tighter, the contact angle can be enhanced39,40. In addition of mentioned effects, the contact angle is also impressed by surface roughness described by Wenzel41. Therefore, based on the dominant phenomenon in each membrane, the contact angles are reported in various values. The crystallinity and surface roughness are not discussed in this paper, so the observed gas behavior is explained with the recorded contact angles. In PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg MMMs, the PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and 4 with high CO2/N2 selectivities, shows surface angles lower than those of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-1 and 2. In PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg collection, the PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 and 2 which demonstrate higher CO2/N2 selectivities compared to PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-3 and 4, possess lower contact angle especially PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1. With addition of ZrAC to the composites, the contact angles of PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-2 are decreased compared to the PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-1 and PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-2 while the contact angles of PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and 4 are increased relative to PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-3 and 4. These trends are corresponded to the CO2/N2 selectivities of the MMMs in which lower contact angle leads to higher CO2/N2 selectivity. However, there is some irregularity in PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg collection. For instance, the contact angles of PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-2 and 3 are increased relative to PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-2 and 3 while their CO2/N2 selectivities are increased, too. These can be probably related to other effects mentioned above.

Conclusion

In this paper, MMMs are prepared by inserting the fabricated composites from Mg-aminoclay, CuBDC, UiO-66-(COOH)2 and MIL-121 MOFs into PEBAX background. Various factors are effective in marvelous CO2 separation relative to N2. The engineered Zr@Cu@Mg and Al@Cu@Mg composites are strongly in favor of CO2 passage. The amine groups of MgAC can facilitated the CO2 transport by provisional formation of carbamate. Furthermore, the sheets of MgAC induce formation of CuBDC in nanosheets instead of bulk form. In addition of molecular sieving of CO2 against N2 by CuBDC nanosheets, the unoccupied Cu cations can also adsorb CO2 in high population. The large pores of UiO-66-(COOH)2 and MIL-121 MOFs can provide highways for gas transporting but due to the interaction between the free carboxylic acid groups in their pores with CO2 as a gas with quadrupole moment, gas transporting of these MOFs favors CO2 in competition with N2. Alongside with composites virtues, the interphases are effective in gas separation behavior of MMMs as well. Two MOF-MOF and PEBAX-composites interphases are determining the gas separations of MMMs. In PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg series, both interphases are synergized from PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-1 to 4 while in PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg series, the interphases show opposite trends from PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-1 to 4. The MMMs of PEBAX/Zr@Cu@Mg-4 and PEBAX/Al@Cu@Mg-2 provide desired CO2 permeability of 320.15 and 320.91 barrer with CO2/N2 selectivity of 147.27 and 74.71, respectively. In the next step, Zr-aminoclay is added to the Zr@Cu@Mg and Al@Cu@Mg composites and MMMs are also prepared. The Zr-AC synthesis over the last composites impresses both MOF-MOF and PEBAX-composites interphases positively and improved gas separation is rendered by new fabricated MMMs. Among them, PEBAX/Zr@Zr@Cu@Mg-4 and PEBAX/Zr@Al@Cu@Mg-3 demonstrate improved CO2 permeability of 407.77 and 382.28 barrer with CO2/N2 selectivity of 87.46 and 72.5, respectively.

Integrated assessment and spatial coordination analysis of water resources carrying capacity in the Changbai mountain headwater region using cloud model and geodetector approach
Authors: Yuqian Wang, Siyu Nie, Weimeng Han, BingWen Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe evaluation of Water Resources Carrying Capacity (WRCC) plays a critical role in understanding regional resource dynamics and guiding the sustainable allocation of water resources. Addressing the inherent uncertainties in hydrological systems and the subjectivity associated with indicator weighting, this study focuses on the headwater region of the Changbai Mountain area. A total of 28 indicators were selected from the “production–living–ecological” framework. Cloud model coupled with entropy weight method and entropy value method were applied to comprehensively assess WRCC, while a geodetector was employed to identify key driving factors. Furthermore, the coordination of WRCC among four regions was quantitatively evaluated by using the Coupling Coordination Degree Model (CCDM). Key findings include: (1) The WRCC of Fusong County was at Level II (good carrying capacity) from 2013 to 2016 and in 2019, and at Level III (carrying capacity) in all other years; Jingyu County was at Level II in 2021–2022, and at Level III in all other years; Changbai Korean Autonomous County was at Level III throughout; Linjiang City was at Level II in 2019–2020, and at Level III in all other years. (2) WRCC indices generally declined from northeast to southwest. (3) Urbanization rate (0.712), water consumption rate for forestry (0.584), animal husbandry and fishery (0.539) were the most significant drivers. (4) Coupling degrees among regions ranged from 0.973 to 0.996, with most areas transitioning from imbalance to coordination, except Jingyu County. The study provides scientific insights for optimizing water resource allocation and balancing economic development, human livelihoods, and ecological preservation in the Changbai Mountain area and the middle and lower reaches of the cities in Jilin Province.Keywords: Water resources carrying capacity, Cloud model, Entropy method, Coupled coordination degree, GeodetectorSubject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Environmental sciences, Environmental social sciences, Hydrology

Introduction

Water resources are a fundamental natural resource that sustains ecological balance and supports socio-economic development. As urbanization accelerates, populations grow, and industrial structures adjust, conflicts frequently arise between the diverse demands for water resources across economic, social, and ecological domains, and the limitations of water resources in their development and utilization. Water resource management measures adopted by authorities in response to these contradictions are typically reactive, incremental, and delayed. Therefore, conducting research on water resources carrying capacity (WRCC) has both significant theoretical and practical value for establishing a scientific water resource regulation mechanism and promoting high-quality development. WRCC refers to the maximum scale of ecological environment, industrial and agricultural development, urbanization level, and population size that a region can support under current technological capabilities and sustainable development principles, based on the available water resources. In overloaded areas, WRCC research helps identify bottlenecks and optimize the coordinated allocation of water resources and other factors such as population, industry, and the ecological environment, thereby achieving scientific and efficient water resource utilization. In areas with sufficient carrying capacity, it can assess resource development potential, formulate spatially balanced development strategies, and improve the overall coordination and sustainable development capabilities of the region.

The concept of carrying capacity originated in mechanics in the 1970s1, reflecting the maximum load-bearing capacity of an object before destruction. It has since become a crucial foundation for measuring and managing human sustainable development, as well as for studying and assessing the relationships between the economy, society, and the natural environment. However, there is a lack of unified understanding and in-depth analysis of the essence of WRCC. Research on WRCC in China began in the 1980s2–4. Currently, most scholars, including Zhu Y5, Xu Z6, Feng Z7, Zhang Z8, and others, have drawn upon earlier definitions of land resource carrying capacity to define WRCC, using the population capacity that water resources can sustain as the evaluation indicator. Currently, no unified view exists on the concept of WRCC, which can be broadly categorized into two types. Some scholars argue that WRCC refers to the maximum scale of agriculture, industry, urban development, and population that a region’s water resources can sustain at a given stage of social and technological development, without damaging the social and ecological systems. It is a comprehensive objective that evolves with changes in socio-economic and technological development9. Other scholars define WRCC as the maximum support capacity of water resources for socio-economic development at a specific historical stage, based on foreseeable technological, economic, and social development levels, adhering to the principle of sustainable development, and maintaining the benign development of the ecological environment, after reasonable optimization and allocation10.

In terms of evaluation frameworks, previous scholars have typically constructed evaluation systems based on frameworks such as water-society-economy-ecological environment11, input-consumption-vitality-regulation-output12 and driving forces-pressures-state-impacts-responses-management (DPSIRM)13. While these frameworks are theoretically sound, their hierarchical structure and complex indicator systems make them difficult to fully support model construction and indicator quantification in small-scale or data-scarce regions, potentially leading to practical challenges in implementation. This paper adopts a “production-living-ecological” ternary system for indicator classification, based on concepts widely advocated by the state in recent years in national land-use planning and ecological civilization construction, and thus has strong practical significance and policy adaptability. This framework identifies elements directly related to WRCC from a practical perspective, avoiding issues such as conceptual overlap and redundant indicators. This helps to reflect the spatial functional differences of water resource service recipients, enhancing the targeting, scientific rigor, and operational feasibility of indicator selection, and better aligning with the analytical needs of sustainable water resource utilization at the regional scale. It also exhibits a certain degree of adaptability and transferability.

At present, there were many research methods used to study the WRCC, mainly hierarchical analysis (AHP)14, principal component analysis (PCA)15、fuzzy comprehensive evaluation method16, weighted TOPSIS modeling17, entropy value method18, and the integrated application of a variety of methods. Cui Y et al.19, Song Q et al.20, Liu Q et al.21 evaluated and analyzed the WRCC of different regions by using a combination of different methods and entropy value method to mitigate subjective bias.

However, due to the uncertainty and openness of water resources, accurately delineating the boundary values of indicators remains a challenge during the establishment of the evaluation indicator system. Studies indicates that cloud modeling22,23 can integrate qualitative concepts with quantitative values, effectively addressing the uncertainties of critical evaluation indicators and ensuring that evaluation outcomes more accurately reflect the actual conditions of complex water resource systems. For example, Cheng K et al.24 used a cloud model to generate the WRCC evaluation level in Heilongjiang Province, while Liu P et al.25 introduced the EFAST-Cloud model, grounded in cloud modeling theory, for a thorough assessment of WRCC in Henan Province.

Additionally, the drivers of WRCC are further analyzed using geodetector due to the complexity of the processes influencing water resources. The geodetector26 method is highly effective in examining the many connections within the water resources system, it facilitates the evaluation of the impact of individual factors on the WRCC. This research provides a comprehensive and targeted scientific foundation for the effective management and planning of water resources. Notably, He G27 and He W et al.28 used geodetector to explore the spatial differentiation of the coupled coordination degree of WRCC’s spatio-temporal heterogeneity in Anhui Province, as well as the influence of the driving factors on the carrying capacity of the water environment in the Yangtze River Economic Zone.

The Coupling Coordination Degree Model (CCDM) has been extensively utilised in the analysis of WRCC. The notion of coupling, derived from the physical sciences, pertains to the dynamic interactions between distinct systems29, while the degree of coupling denotes the intensity of interactions and the level of correlation between the pertinent systems. Current studies, mostly focus on the analysis of the coupling relationship between WRCC and other factors. Among them, Xie Q et al.30, Zhang Z et al.31, and Li Y32, used CCDM to explore the coupling relationship between WRCC and high-quality development, food production resilience index, and new urbanization in different regions. Regions are not isolated from each other, and there is a strong connection between water resource utilization and economic development. Therefore, exploring the coupling relationship between WRCC in different regions is an important step in WRCC research.

As the understanding of the holistic nature of the water resources system deepens, the research focus has gradually expanded to the source area33,34. The source zone, as the origin of water resources, significantly influences the WRCC of the entire basin, with its water resources’ amount and distribution affecting the overall water usage patterns of the basin. Wu L et al.35 evaluated and predicted the WRCC in the upper part of the Heihe River Basin; Chen Q et al.36 estimated the WRCC of the Yangtze River Delta. Changbai Mountain, as an important ecological barrier and source of recharge for the Songhua River Basin, not only supports local ecosystem maintenance and socio-economic activities but also ensures the supply of water resources to downstream and neighboring regions.

In summarize, cloud model coupled with entropy weighting method and entropy value method were adopted to evaluate the WRCC of the Changbai Mountain Headwaters Area qualitatively and quantitatively from 2013 to 2022, and to clarify the characteristics of the spatial and temporal evolution. Secondly, geodetector was combined with the analysis to elucidate the driving role of factors on WRCC. Lastly, the coupled and coordinated relationship of the WRCC across the various regions in the study area was analyzed using the CCDM. This study aims to enhance the precision and efficacy of decision-making support for the scientific management of water resources, ecological environmental protection, and regional sustainable development in the Changbai Mountain source area. It also seeks to mitigate the conflict between water resources and economic and social development in the Changbai Mountain region and the middle and lower reaches of the Songhua River in Jilin Province, while providing scientific references for the WRCC assessment in other analogous water source areas. The main contributions of this study are outlined below. First, the Changbai Mountain source was selected as the research object, contributing to the improvement of the overall coordination level and sustainable development capacity of the region. Second, indicators were selected, and an evaluation system was constructed from the perspective of “production-living-ecological”. Third, cloud model coupled with entropy weighting method and entropy value method were used to conduct qualitative and quantitative evaluations of WRCC. Fourth, by integrating geodetector, the study explores the driving factors that influence WRCC. Fifth, by employing the CCDM, the study analyzes the coupling relationships between the four regions in terms of WRCC and provides recommendations for strengthening inter-regional water resource development.

Methodology and data source

Overview of the study area

Baishan City, Jilin Province, is located in the hinterland of the Changbai Mountains, with the Yalu River and Songhua River flowing through it. The city has a total of 432 large and small rivers within its territory, abundant water resources, and a well-developed water system. Baishan City is located in the southeast of Jilin Province, at coordinates 126°7′E–128°18′E and 41°21′N–42°48′N. It is situated in a humid and semi-humid region, with over 80% forest coverage and 70% water functional area standard, contributing to a favorable ecological environment and significant development potential. According to statistics, the city’s multi-year average total water resources amount to 8.025 billion cubic meters, with per capita water availability 2.7 times the national average.

Fusong County, Jingyu County, Changbai Korean Autonomous County, and Linjiang City, all situated within Baishan City (Fig. 1), maintain a significant connection to the source area of the Changbai Mountain range. This source area is a crucial water resource and serves as the origin of numerous rivers. Specifically, Fusong County is positioned at the northern foothills of the Changbai Mountains, with portions of its territory classified as part of the headwaters region. This area is characterized by a multitude of rivers that originate from the Changbai Mountain system, providing substantial water resources that are vital for water systems such as the Songhua River. Changbai Korean Autonomous County is located at the southern base of Changbai Mountain, adjacent to the right bank of the upper Yalu River, which is recognized as one of the principal sources of the Yalu River. Although Linjiang City is not situated directly within the core headwaters area, it is connected to the upper reaches of several rivers that derive from the Changbai Mountain headwaters, with the ecological conditions of these headwaters influencing the recharge of these rivers. The water resources of these four counties and cities are intricately linked to the Changbai Mountain source area. The status of these water resources dictates not only the quantity and quality of water available to the four municipalities but also impacts the industries that rely on these resources. Such industries include the ginseng industry in Fusong County, the mineral water industry in Jingyu County, cold-water fish farming in Changbai Korean Autonomous County, and specialty agriculture in Linjiang City.

Research framework

Figure 2 illustrates the research framework for this study, addressing four primary issues. First, the research aimed to ascertain the weights of the comprehensive evaluation indicators utilizing entropy weighting method. Second, the study employed cloud model coupled with entropy weighting method and entropy value method to assess the WRCC in the study area from 2013 to 2022, while analyzing the developmental trends and spatial distribution characteristics of this capacity. Third, the geodetector was utilized to conduct factor detection, thereby elucidating the impact of each indicator on the WRCC. Finally, the CCDM was applied to investigate the interrelationships among the four regions within the study area, with the objective of providing a scientific foundation and practical guidance for water resources management and regional sustainability.

Construction of the indicator system

Evaluation system construction

The selection of evaluation indicators and the establishment of an evaluation indicator system were fundamental for effectively assessing the WRCC of the study area. According to the principles of WRCC evaluation and the specific characteristics of the study area, and drawing on relevant research37–39, the production subsystem primarily selected indicators such as the water consumption of industry and agriculture, forestry, animal husbandry and fishery, arable land area, and gross domestic product per capita. The living subsystem primarily selected indicators such as the permissible extraction amount of mineral water resources, domestic water consumption, and the water resources development and utilization rate. The ecological subsystem primarily included indicators such as ecological water consumption, rainfall, and the forest coverage rate. A sustainable evaluation system embodying the “production-living-ecological” framework was developed, and 28 indicators were selected as evaluation criteria to establish the evaluation index system for the WRCC of the study area, as illustrated in Table 1.

Geodetector

Geodetector comprises a set of statistical techniques designed to identify spatial disparities and examine the fundamental variables contributing to these differences. The core principle of geodetector is based on the idea that a substantial effect of an independent variable on a dependent variable will be reflected in the spatial distribution of both variables. This study aims to employ geodetector to assess the influence of various driving factors on WRCC in the designated research area.

Step 1: Since all quantities in this study are numerical, they must be discretized by applying K-means cluster analysis using IBM SPSS Statistics (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences, Version 26, URL link: https://www.ibm.com/products/spssstatistics) software before employing geodetector.

Step 2: Utilise Excel to import the sample data (Y, X) into the geodetector software created by Wang Jinfeng’s research team, then run the software to assess the explanatory power of the independent variable X on the dependent variable Y. This analysis will evaluate significant differences in the impacts of several independent factors X on the dependent variable Y, along with the interactions among these independent variables X concerning the dependent variable.

Factor detection entails assessing the degree to which factor X explains the spatial variation of attribute Y, measured by the q-value. The q-value varies from 0 to 1, with higher values signifying greater regional differentiation of Y. A higher q-value implies that the independent variable X possesses greater explanatory power concerning attribute Y, whilst a lower q-value denotes a weaker association52. In an extreme scenario, a q-value of 1 indicates that factor X completely dictates the spatial distribution of Y, whereas a q-value of 0 suggests no correlation between factor X and Y53. By assessing the degree of coupling and coordination regarding the WRCC across these regions, we can effectively analyze the variations in the relationship between WRCC and coupling across different time periods. This analysis offered theoretical insights to facilitate the synergistic development of the region.

Over the past decade, the coupling degree of WRCC among Fusong County, Jingyu County, Changbai Korean Autonomous County, and Linjiang City had ranged from 0.973 to 0.996 with a general annual increase observed in the development trend (Fig. 11). This aligns with the developmental status of WRCC in each aforementioned location. Furthermore, the coupling coordination level among regions from 2013 to 2022 was predominantly characterised by a condition of good coordination, with the exception of 2014, when it approached dislocation. The degree of coupling coordination exhibited a general upward tendency.

The other three regions exhibit a pairwise adjacency pattern, with the coupling coordination degree calculation results (see Fig. 12) as follows: the degree between Fusong County and Linjiang City fluctuates between 0.698 and 0.744; the degree between Fusong County and Changbai Korean Autonomous County increases from 0.616 to 0.720; and the degree between Changbai Korean Autonomous County and Linjiang City rises from 0.626 to 0.718.

The coupling coordination degree between Fusong County, Changbai Korean Autonomous County and Linjiang City.

The four counties and cities in the Changbai Mountain headwater region share close geographical proximity and hydrological interdependence, all receiving recharge from the Changbai water system and exhibiting similar runoff and precipitation patterns. This natural connectivity fosters coordinated demands for water allocation and quality protection. Especially between Linjiang City and Fusong County, there is a good coupling coordination degree. However, with the advancement of urbanization, the demand for water resources continues to increase. Greater attention needs to be given to the rational distribution and management of water resources between upstream and downstream regions, ensuring the protection of upstream water sources and meeting downstream water demands. Efforts should be made to increase investment in water resource protection in upstream areas, while optimizing water resource allocation downstream to prevent over-extraction and pollution.

From the perspective of economic development, the industrial structures of the four regions exhibit functional complementarity. Fusong County prioritizes mineral water and eco-tourism industries; Linjiang City emphasizes hydropower generation and pharmaceuticals; Jingyu County focuses on mining and specialty agriculture; while Changbai Korean Autonomous County develops ecological industries within its borderland setting. These differentiated development paths support collaborative water use strategies. For instance, Fusong’s high-quality water demand for the mineral water sector is indirectly sustained through ecological preservation in upstream areas such as Jingyu County and Changbai Korean Autonomous County. Meanwhile, Linjiang’s hydropower expertise offers technical spillovers for regional water infrastructure management. This industrial synergy underpins cooperative enhancement of regional WRCC.

Recent policies, such as the “river chief system” and water conservation action, have strengthened institutional collaboration, contributing to improved coupling coordination degrees since the near-dysfunctional levels observed in 2014. These dynamics highlight the critical role of geographic linkage, industrial synergy, and governance integration in promoting sustainable water resource management.

Discussion

In terms of evaluation frameworks, previous scholars have typically constructed evaluation systems based on frameworks such as water-society-economy-ecological environment, input-consumption-vitality-regulation-output, and drivers-pressures-state-impact-responses-management (DPSIRM). While these frameworks are theoretically sound, their hierarchical structure and complex indicator systems make them difficult to fully support model construction and indicator quantification in small-scale or data-scarce regions, potentially leading to practical implementation challenges. This study adopts a “production-living-ecological” ternary system for indicator classification, which is based on the concepts widely advocated by the state in recent years in terms of national land-use planning and ecological civilization construction. This approach has strong practical guidance significance and policy adaptability. Under similar regional characteristics, data conditions, and research objectives, this evaluation system possesses a certain degree of universality, particularly suitable for WRCC analysis in other regions with strong ecological sensitivity, significant spatial differences in water resources, or development constraints. By appropriately adjusting and optimizing the framework of this study, it can be expanded for application to representative regions such as the karst mountain areas of Southwest China, the source regions of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, and the southern hilly ecological barriers, thereby providing theoretical support and practical pathways for the sustainable management of water resources in different types of ecological zones.

Future studies should prioritize the inclusion of indicators that explicitly capture upstream–downstream interactions, such as transboundary water flux, ecological service value transfers, and watershed-scale connectivity indices. The application of the geodetector in this study has proven effective in revealing the relative importance of driving factors and provides a theoretical foundation for indicator refinement. However, the current indicator system remains limited in granularity. Future research should incorporate field-based investigations and multidisciplinary integration, including hydrology, socioeconomics, and ecology, to identify micro-level variables that better reflect the complex characteristics of WRCC in the Changbai Mountain source region.

In this study, cloud model coupled with the entropy weighting method and entropy value method alone were used to conduct a dynamic evaluation of the WRCC of four regions in the Changbai Mountain source area from 2013 to 2022. The two methods were basically consistent in their assessment of the trend of carrying capacity over time, both showing a “fluctuating upward” characteristic, indicating that they have strong consistency and robustness in trend identification, thereby enhancing the credibility of the research conclusions.

From the specific results, the cloud model coupled with the entropy weighting method demonstrated greater precision in classification, capable of identifying critical states such as “sustainable” and “good sustainable,” highlighting its advantages in handling system ambiguity, uncertainty, and nonlinearity, thereby providing a basis for phased management and policy intervention. In contrast, the entropy value method relies on indicator variability for objective weighting, emphasizing the quantitative ranking of comprehensive carrying capacity across regions. The results show that Linjiang City has the strongest overall carrying capacity, while Jingyu County is relatively weaker. Different regions exhibit varying degrees of growth trends, enhancing the operability of horizontal comparisons and vertical evolutionary analysis between regions. Overall, the cloud model’s flexible expression in state judgment and classification compensates for the entropy value method’s shortcomings in handling fuzzy boundaries; meanwhile, the entropy value method is more intuitive in revealing trend changes and regional rankings. The combination of the two methods not only enhances the adaptability and accuracy of the evaluation system but also builds an effective bridge between qualitative identification and quantitative ranking. The mutual validation of the two methods further strengthens the scientific rigor and robustness of the research results.

Additionally, the cloud model’s ability to express uncertainty and ambiguity endows this evaluation framework with strong regional adaptability and transferability, particularly suitable for ecologically sensitive, water resource-constrained regions with high water resource variability. This provides a reference method for evaluating WRCC in similar regions.

Based on a dynamic analysis of the evolution of WRCC in the Changbai Mountain source region from 2013 to 2022 and the identification of its driving mechanisms, this paper proposes the following targeted and actionable policy recommendations to enhance the sustainable carrying capacity of the regional water resource system and provide guidance for future integrated basin management:

Given the spatiotemporal heterogeneity and uncertainty of WRCC within the region, a dynamic monitoring system for WRCC should be established. Leveraging remote sensing, big data, and geospatial detection technologies, long-term, integrated water-quantity-quality intelligent monitoring and early warning systems should be implemented to enhance the scientific rigor and foresight of management responses. In areas such as Fusong County and Jingyu County, WRCC is closely related to water use efficiency. It is recommended to promote efficient water-saving irrigation technologies, upgrade clean production processes for mineral water production, and implement industrial water-saving renovations based on local conditions. Additionally, by strengthening management of agricultural and domestic water use, guiding agricultural structural optimization, encouraging the development of water-saving industries and high-value-added economies, and reducing rigid pressure on water resources.

Differences in the carrying capacity of the domestic subsystem reflect uneven urban-rural water development. It is recommended to strengthen urban water supply network renovations, rural drinking water project maintenance, and emergency water supply capacity construction in areas with significant potential for improving domestic water use carrying capacity (such as Fusong County). The ecological subsystem carrying capacity in Jingyu County and Changbai Korean Autonomous County has improved significantly, indicating the effectiveness of ecological restoration and water source conservation policies. It is recommended to continue promoting afforestation, reforestation, wetland protection, and the construction of ecological buffer zones, while increasing efforts to restore small watershed ecosystems and enhance regional water conservation capacity.

Comprehensive analysis shows that carrying capacity exhibits a distinct northeast-southwest axial distribution in space, necessitating strengthened coordinated allocation of water resources within river basins. It is recommended to advance cross-county water storage and regulation infrastructure construction based on carrying capacity levels and water resource abundance-deficiency patterns, strengthen upstream-downstream coordination and collaboration, establish inter-regional water resource allocation and compensation mechanisms, and achieve optimized resource allocation at the spatial level. According to driver factor analysis, the proportion of the tertiary industry, water consumption per unit of GDP, and urbanization rate are key factors in enhancing WRCC. It is recommended that localities leverage their resource endowments to promote economic structural transformation from high-water-consuming, low-value-added industries toward water-saving, green, and service-oriented sectors, fostering a new development pattern characterized by high coupling between resources, industries, and ecology.

Conclusion

This study conducted a comprehensive qualitative and quantitative evaluation of the WRCC in the study area by cloud model coupled with entropy weighting method and entropy value method. Based on these evaluations, the CCDM was employed to investigate the interrelationships among WRCC subsystems across counties and cities, revealing the evolutionary patterns of the internal water resources system. Furthermore, the geodetector was applied to explore the dominant driving forces affecting WRCC.

The WRCC of Fusong County was at Level II (good carrying capacity) from 2013 to 2016 and in 2019, and at Level III (carrying capacity) in all other years. The WRCC of Jingyu County was at Level II in 2021–2022, and at Level III in all other years. The WRCC of Changbai Korean Autonomous County remained at Level III throughout the study period. The WRCC of Linjiang City was at Level II in 2019–2020, and at Level III in all other years.

The quantitative evaluation results derived from the entropy weight method indicated a generally increasing trend in the WRCC composite index across the four regions from 2013 to 2022. The order of WRCC levels from highest to lowest was: Linjiang City > Fusong County > Changbai Korean Autonomous County > Jingyu County. Spatially, the WRCC index displayed a gradient pattern along the northeast-southwest axis, generally decreasing toward both ends. However, inter-annual variations were observed: in 2013, the highest WRCC was in the southwest and the lowest in the southeast; in 2016 and 2022, the maximum values shifted to the northeast and the minima to the northwest.

The impact intensity of each driving factor on WRCC exhibited significant temporal variation and regional heterogeneity. Over the past decade, the primary influencing factors on WRCC in the source region of the Changbaishan Mountains, ranked in descending order, included: urbanization rate, forestry water consumption rate, output from animal husbandry and fisheries, per capita ammonia nitrogen emissions, per capita GDP, forest coverage rate, the proportion of the tertiary industry, degree of groundwater development and utilization, natural population growth rate, water supply penetration rate, and arable land ratio.

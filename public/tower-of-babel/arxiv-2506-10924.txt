A space-time interface-fitted method for moving-subdomain distributed control problems with energy regularization Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Quang Huy Nguyen, Phuong Cuc Hoang, Van Chien Le, Thi Thanh Mai Ta

Abstract
This paper investigates a space-time interface-fitted approximation of a moving-interface optimal control problem with energy regularization. We reformulate the optimality conditions into a variational problem involving both the state and adjoint. This problem is shown to be equivalent to our optimal control problem. Based on fully unstructured, space-time interface-fitted meshes, we propose and analyze a Petrov-Galerkin approximation of the problem. An optimal error estimate with respect to a discrete norm is established under a specific regularity assumption on the state and adjoint. Several numerical results are presented to corroborate our theoretical results.



keywords

distributed control problems, moving-subdomain problems, space-time interface-fitted method, error estimates.

1.1
Relevant literature

Within the scope of finite element methods (FEMs), numerous techniques for discretizing space and time in time-dependent optimal control problems have been thoroughly investigated. These techniques are typically classified into two principal categories: separate discretization and ”all-at-once” discretization. The former involves the combination of a temporal discretization scheme, such as the Euler method
[
27
,
6
]
, the Crank-Nicolson method
[
22
,
32
,
35
]
, or the discontinuous Galerkin method
[
2
,
21
,
24
]
, with a particular FEM for spatial discretization. Notably, combining adaptive techniques or parallel solvers with these methods can be challenging
[
12
,
13
]
. In contrast, the second approach, known as the space-time method, treats the time variable as an additional spatial variable, with the temporal derivative of the solution considered as a convection term along the time direction
[
16
,
14
]
. This method has been applied in
[
15
,
17
]
to discretize parabolic optimal control problems with energy regularization. In
[
15
]
, the authors established optimal error estimates for the state and adjoint in a discrete trial space norm. Using the perturbed Schur complement equation, they subsequently derived a higher-order estimate in the
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
-norm for the state in
[
17
]
. However, in all the aforementioned papers, the spatial domain does not contain an interface.

When the spatial domain is split by an interface, the solutions to PDE problems often exhibit low global spatial regularity, which prevents classical FEMs from converging at optimal rates
[
1
]
. Over the past few decades, numerous approaches have been developed to overcome this limitation. Prominent examples include the extended FEM
[
37
]
, the immersed FEM
[
9
,
8
]
, and the interface-fitted FEM
[
3
]
. Despite these advances, time-dependent optimal control problems involving interfaces have received relatively little attention. To the best of our knowledge, only the papers
[
36
,
30
]
have addressed such problems, in which they employed the classical
\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
-regularization. In
[
36
]
, Zhang et al. employed the immersed FEM to discretize an optimal control problem for a parabolic transmission equation. They derived optimal error estimates for the control, state, and adjoint in various cases. In contrast, using the interface-fitted FEM, Ta et al.
[
30
]
discretized an inverse problem for the advection-diffusion equation. Under suitable assumptions, the authors obtained different error estimates for three source discretizations.

1.2
Contributions and outline

This paper proposes a numerical method for the moving-subdomain distributed control problem (
2
). Our approach is to eliminate the control, thereby reformulating the optimality conditions into a variational problem involving both the state and adjoint. The problem is then discretized in a space-time setting, where finite element spaces are constructed on fully unstructured, interface-fitted meshes
[
25
]
. In the context of moving-interface problems, this method eliminates the need for re-meshing at each time step while still providing a reasonable interface resolution. Moreover, it is flexible with respect to adaptability and parallelization. Unlike
[
23
,
7
,
11
,
15
,
5
]
, where the authors discretized the optimality conditions via equivalent variational problems and established error estimates under global regularity assumptions of the state and adjoint, our work focuses on addressing the non-smoothness across the interface of these variables. Specifically, to interpolate functions with locally high but globally low regularity, we employ Stein extension operators
[
29
,
4
]
. We derive a nearly optimal estimate for the one-dimensional spatial domain and a sub-optimal estimate for the two-dimensional spatial domain. Under stronger assumptions, the convergence rate is optimal for both one-dimensional and two-dimensional spatial domains.

The manuscript is structured as follows. In
Section

2
, we first present the essential functional settings, followed by a study of the existence and uniqueness of solutions to Problem (
2
), along with the corresponding optimality conditions. These optimality conditions are then reformulated into Problem (
15
), which is a variational problem involving both the state and adjoint. We discuss the unique solvability of this problem in
Section

3
. Next,
Section

4
presents the finite element discretization of Problem (
15
), and different error estimates for the state and adjoint are established under specific regularity assumptions. Finally, we illustrate our theoretical results with numerical examples in
Section

5
.

Preliminaries

For
w\in\Ws
, its distributional time derivative is defined as

\partial_{t}w\left(\xi\right):=-\int\limits_{Q_{T}}w\partial_{t}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\xi\in\Cs_{0}^{1}\left(Q_{T}\right).

Then, we introduce the space
\Us:=\left\{w\in\Ws\mid\partial_{t}w\in\Ws^{\prime}\right\}
and its two subspaces

\Us_{0}:=\left\{w\in\Us\mid w\left(\cdot,0\right)=0\right\}\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad\Us_{T}:=\left\{w\in\Us\mid w\left(\cdot,T\right)=0\right\},

furnished with the graph norm

\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Us}:=\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ws}+\left\lVert{\partial_{t}w}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ws^{\prime}}\qquad\qquad\forall w\in\Us.

It is worth mentioning that the space
\Us
is identical to the Bochner space

\left\{{w\in\Ls^{2}\left(\left(0,T\right),\Hs^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)\right)\mid\partial_{t}w\in\Ls^{2}\left(\left(0,T\right),\Hs^{-1}\left(\Omega\right)\right)}\right\},

\Us_{0}
and

The variational formulation of Problem (

such that

a\left(u,\varphi\right)=\left\langle{f,\varphi}\right\rangle\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ws,

(3)

where the bilinear form

is given by

a\left(u,\varphi\right):=\left\langle{\partial_{t}u,\varphi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u\right)\varphi+\kappa\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t,

and
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
\Ws^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle\min_{f\in\Ws^{\prime}}J\left(f\right),

(4)

\displaystyle\text{ subject to \eqref{eq: weak state equation}}.

Since

is a continuous and strictly convex functional on the Hilbert space

, this problem admits a unique solution

, see the proof of

Next, we derive the optimality conditions. To do so, we introduce the following adjoint problem: Find

such that

a^{\prime}\left(p\left(f\right),\phi\right)=\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(u\left(f\right)-u_{d}\right)\phi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\phi\in\Ws,

(5)

where the bilinear form

a^{\prime}\left(p,\phi\right):=-\left\langle{\partial_{t}p,\phi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{Q_{T}}-\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla p\right)\phi+\kappa\nabla p\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t.

We also need the Riesz representation: For any

\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\kappa\nabla z\left(\widetilde{w}\right)\cdot\nabla\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\left\langle{\widetilde{w},\zeta}\right\rangle\qquad\qquad\forall\zeta\in\Ws.

(6)

Moreover, we have

\left\lVert{\widetilde{w}}\right\rVert_{\Ws^{\prime}}^{2}=\left\langle{\widetilde{w},z\left(\widetilde{w}\right)}\right\rangle=\left\lVert{z\left(\widetilde{w}\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ws}^{2}.

(7)

Theorem 1
.

An element

On the one hand, we integrate by parts with

Reduced optimality system

Following the approach of Langer et al. in
[
16
, Section 3]
, we now reduce the system (
8
)–(
10
) into a variational problem where the control
f^{\ast}
is absent. We then prove that solving that problem is equivalent to solving Problem (
4
), and hence implicitly obtain the unique solvability of the problem.

To eliminate
f^{\ast}
from the system (
8
)–(
10
), we use (
8
), (
6
), and (
10
). We arrive at

a\left(u^{\ast},\varphi\right)=\left\langle{f^{\ast},\varphi}\right\rangle=\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\kappa\nabla z\left(f^{\ast}\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=-\dfrac{1}{\eta}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\kappa\nabla p^{\ast}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ws,

a\left(u^{\ast},\varphi\right)+\dfrac{1}{\eta}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\kappa\nabla p^{\ast}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=0\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ws.

(13)

Notably, this equation expresses a relation between

and

without the explicit presence of

-\left\langle{\partial_{t}p^{\ast},\phi}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\partial_{t}\phi,p^{\ast}}\right\rangle\qquad\qquad\forall\phi\in\Us_{0}.

In addition, the arguments of (

Motivated by (

-\partial_{t}\overline{p}\left(\phi\right)=-\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\phi\right)\overline{p}+\kappa\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\overline{p}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\overline{u}-u_{d}\right)\phi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\forall\phi\in\Cs^{1}_{0}\left(Q_{T}\right).

Since
\Cs^{1}_{0}\left(Q_{T}\right)
is dense in
\Ws
, the right-hand side of this equality defines a bounded linear functional of
\phi
on
\Ws
. Therefore, we imply that
\partial_{t}\overline{p}\in\Ws^{\prime}
, and hence
\overline{p}\in\Us
. Moreover, the following equality holds

-\left\langle{\partial_{t}\overline{p},\phi}\right\rangle=-\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\phi\right)\overline{p}+\kappa\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\overline{p}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\overline{u}-u_{d}\right)\phi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\forall\phi\in\Ws.

We then take
\phi\in\Us_{0}\subset\Ws
and subtract this from (
16
) to obtain
\left\langle{\partial_{t}\phi,\overline{p}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{\partial_{t}\overline{p},\phi}\right\rangle=0
, which leads to

\int\limits_{\Omega}\phi\left(\boldsymbol{x},T\right)\overline{p}\left(\boldsymbol{x},T\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=0\qquad\qquad\forall\phi\in\Us_{0},

using the integration by parts. Therefore, we conclude that
\overline{p}\left(\cdot,T\right)=0
, and thus
\overline{p}\in\Us_{T}
.

We now prove the second statement. Thanks to the arguments for (
14
), we can rewrite (
16
) as

a^{\prime}\left(\overline{p},\phi\right)=\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\overline{u}-u_{d}\right)\phi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\phi\in\Us_{0}.

By invoking the density of
\Us_{0}
in
\Ws
, we hence imply

a^{\prime}\left(\overline{p},\phi\right)=\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\overline{u}-u_{d}\right)\phi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\phi\in\Ws,

(17)

which recovers Problem (
9
). Subsequently, we choose
\phi=0_{\Us_{0}}
in (
15
) to obtain

a\left(\overline{u},\varphi\right)=-\dfrac{1}{\eta}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\kappa\nabla\overline{p}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ws.

By further defining
\overline{z}:=-\frac{1}{\eta}\overline{p}
in
\Ws
and using (
6
), we conclude the existence of an element
\overline{f}\in\Ws^{\prime}
such that

a\left(\overline{u},\varphi\right)=\left\langle{\overline{f},\varphi}\right\rangle\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Ws,

(18)

which corresponds precisely to Problem (
8
). The conclusion follows by combining
Theorem

1
with (
17
) and (
18
).

From this theorem, we see that the system (
8
)–(
10
) is equivalent to Problem (
15
). Notably, the latter offers the advantage of enabling an all-at-once space-time treatment of the state and adjoint, as opposed to classical gradient-based sequential methods. As a result, we consider discretizing Problem (
15
) as an efficient way to discretize our optimal control problem.

Moreover,
Theorem

2
guarantees the uniqueness of the solution to Problem (
15
). This follows from the unique determination of the pair
\left(u^{\ast},p^{\ast}\right)\in\Us_{0}\times\Us_{T}
and the second part of the theorem. We thus obtain the following result:

Corollary 3
.

Given
u_{d}\in\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
. Then, Problem (
15
) admits a unique solution
\left(\overline{u},\overline{p}\right)\in\Us_{0}\times\Us_{T}
.

Finally, it is important to note that once Problem (
15
) is solved, the optimal control
f^{\ast}
can be obtained directly via Problem (
8
).

4
Finite element discretization

Assume that
\Omega
is a polyhedron in
\mathbb{R}^{m}

\left(m=1\text{ or }2\right)
. Let
\left\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}_{h\in\left(0,h^{\dagger}\right)}
be a family of quasi-uniform, interface-fitted meshes of the cylinder
Q_{T}=\Omega\times\left(0,T\right)
, with mesh size
h\in\left(0,h^{\dagger}\right)
, where
h^{\dagger}>0
is given
[
25
, Section 3.1]
. Notably, this triangulation does not allow
\Gamma^{\ast}
to cut the triangles (or tetrahedra) arbitrarily. In particular,
\Gamma^{\ast}
goes through exactly two (or three) vertices of all interface triangles (or tetrahedra), and forms with each corresponding edge (or face) a small discrepancy region. We denote by
\Gamma_{h}^{\ast}
the linear approximation of
\Gamma^{\ast}
, consisting of all edges (or faces) with vertices lying on
\Gamma^{\ast}
. This discrete interface partitions
Q_{T}
into two subdomains
Q_{1,h}
and
Q_{2,h}
. Finally, the mismatch between space-time subdomains and their discrete counterparts is defined as

S_{h}:=\left(Q_{1}\setminus\overline{Q_{1,h}}\right)\cup\left(Q_{2}\setminus\overline{Q_{2,h}}\right).

Throughout this section, the notation
C
denotes a generic positive constant that
does not depend on the control
f^{\ast}
or the mesh size
h
, but may depend on the cylinder
Q_{T}
, the position of
\Gamma^{\ast}
, the norm
\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{\infty}\left(Q_{T}\right)}
, the coefficient
\kappa
, and the parameter
\eta
. Its value may vary depending on the context.

4.1
Space-time interface-fitted method

On the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the finite element spaces as

\Ws_{h}:=\left\{w_{h}\in\Cs\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)\mid w_{h\,\mid\,K}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\left(K\right)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}\cap\Ws,

and

\Us_{h}:=\Ws_{h}\cap\Us_{0}.

The Petrov-Galerkin approximation of Problem (
15
) is then given by: Determine
\left(\overline{u}_{h},\overline{p}_{h}\right)\in\Us_{h}\times\Ws_{h}
such that

\mathcal{A}_{h}\left(\left(\overline{u}_{h},\overline{p}_{h}\right),\left(\varphi_{h},\phi_{h}\right)\right)=\int\limits_{Q_{T}}u_{d}\phi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\left(\varphi_{h},\phi_{h}\right)\in\Ws_{h}\times\Us_{h},

(19)

where the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}:\left(\Us_{0}\times\Ws\right)\times\left(\Ws\times\Us_{0}\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

\mathcal{A}_{h}\left(\left(u,p\right),\left(\varphi,\phi\right)\right):=a_{h}\left(u,\varphi\right)+\dfrac{1}{\eta}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\kappa_{h}\nabla p\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int\limits_{Q_{T}}u\phi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t-a_{h}\left(\phi,p\right).

The bilinear form
a_{h}:\Us_{0}\times\Ws\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

a_{h}\left(u,\varphi\right):=\left\langle{\partial_{t}u,\varphi}\right\rangle+\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u\right)\varphi+\kappa_{h}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t,

and
\kappa_{h}
is a mesh-dependent positive constant

\kappa_{h}:=\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}>0&\text{in}\quad Q_{1,h},\\
\kappa_{2}>0&\text{in}\quad Q_{2,h}.\end{cases}

Regarding numerical analysis, let us introduce the following seminorms

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\kappa_{i}\left|{\nabla w}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall w\in\Hs^{1,0}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right),

and

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}^{2}:={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}w\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\qquad\qquad\forall w\in\Hs^{1}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right),

where
z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}w\right)\in\Ws_{h}
denotes the unique solution to the problem

\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\kappa_{h}\nabla z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}w\right)\cdot\nabla\zeta_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int\limits_{Q_{i,h}}\left(\partial_{t}w\right)\zeta_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\zeta_{h}\in\Ws_{h}.

(20)

Since these seminorms are defined for functions whose gradient may be discontinuous across
\Gamma_{h}^{\ast}
, they are well-suited for the analysis of discrete problems. In what follows, we equip the spaces
\Ws_{h}
and
\Us_{h}
with the seminorms
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}
, respectively.

Lemma 4
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
\left(u_{h},p_{h}\right)\in\Us_{h}\times\Ws_{h}
, the following stability condition holds

\sup_{\left(\varphi_{h},\phi_{h}\right)\in\Ws_{h}\times\Us_{h}\setminus\left\{\left(0_{\Ws_{h}},0_{\Us_{h}}\right)\right\}}\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{h}\left(\left(u_{h},p_{h}\right),\left(\varphi_{h},\phi_{h}\right)\right)}{\sqrt{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}}}\geq C\sqrt{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}}.

Proof.

We first fix
\left(u_{h},p_{h}\right)\in\left(\Us_{h},\Ws_{h}\right)
and choose
\varphi_{h}=\mu u_{h}+z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)+\lambda p_{h}\in\Ws_{h}
and
\phi_{h}=\lambda u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
, where
\mu>0,\lambda>0
are sufficiently large numbers that will be specified later. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}\left(\left(u_{h},p_{h}\right),\left(\varphi_{h},\phi_{h}\right)\right)=

(21)

\displaystyle=\mu a_{h}\left(u_{h},u_{h}\right)+a_{h}\left(u_{h},z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right)+\dfrac{1}{\eta}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\kappa_{h}\nabla p_{h}\cdot\nabla\left(\mu u_{h}+z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\lambda}{\eta}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\lambda\left\lVert{u_{h}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}.

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
21
), we integrate by parts with
u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
and apply the technique in (
12
). The following estimate holds

a_{h}\left(u_{h},u_{h}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u_{h}\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{u_{h}\left(\cdot,0\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\geq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}.

(22)

By using (
20
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we bound the second term on the right-hand side of (
21
) as follows

\displaystyle a_{h}\left(u_{h},z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right)=

\displaystyle\quad={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u_{h}\right)z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)+\kappa_{h}\nabla u_{h}\cdot\nabla z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad\geq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}-\left(\dfrac{C}{4\epsilon}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\epsilon\left\lVert{z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\dfrac{1}{4\epsilon}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\epsilon{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\right),

for any
\epsilon>0
. Moreover, we recall from
[
4
, Lemma 3.27]
the Poincaré–Steklov inequality

\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\qquad\qquad\forall w\in\Ws.

(23)

After applying this inequality and rearranging the terms, we end up with

a_{h}\left(u_{h},z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right)\geq\left(1-C\epsilon\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}-\dfrac{C}{\epsilon}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}.

(24)

We now estimate the third term on the right-hand side of (
21
). By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\kappa_{h}\nabla p_{h}\cdot\nabla\left(\mu u_{h}+z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq

\displaystyle\quad\geq-\mu\left(\dfrac{1}{2\eta}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\dfrac{\eta}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\right)-\left(\dfrac{1}{4\epsilon}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\epsilon{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=-\dfrac{\mu\eta}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}-\epsilon{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}-\left(\dfrac{\mu}{2\eta}+\dfrac{1}{4\epsilon}\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}.

By inserting (
22
), (
24
), and this into (
21
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}\left(\left(u_{h},p_{h}\right),\left(\varphi_{h},\phi_{h}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\dfrac{\mu}{2}-\dfrac{C}{\epsilon}\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\left(1-C\epsilon-\dfrac{\epsilon}{\eta}\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{1}{\eta}\left(\lambda-\dfrac{\mu}{2\eta}-\dfrac{1}{4\epsilon}\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}.

Here, we choose sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
such that
1-C\epsilon-\frac{\epsilon}{\eta}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, then choose sufficiently large
\mu>0
and
\lambda>0
such that
\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{C}{\epsilon}\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda-\frac{\mu}{2\eta}-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\geq\frac{\eta}{2}
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}\left(\left(u_{h},p_{h}\right),\left(\varphi_{h},\phi_{h}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\right).

Moreover, it follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mu u_{h}+z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}u_{h}\right)+\lambda p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\lambda u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}

\displaystyle\leq C\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\right).

Therefore, we conclude that

\mathcal{A}_{h}\left(\left(u_{h},p_{h}\right),\left(\varphi_{h},\phi_{h}\right)\right)\geq C\sqrt{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|p_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}}\sqrt{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\phi_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}}.

By letting
\left(u_{h},p_{h}\right)
vary over
\Us_{h}\times\Ws_{h}
, we obtain the desired result.

This lemma and the fact that the discrete trial and test spaces are of the same dimension ensure the unique solvability of Problem (
19
).

Theorem 5
.

For a given
u_{d}\in\Ls^{2}\left(Q_{T}\right)
, there exists a unique solution
\left(\overline{u}_{h},\overline{p}_{h}\right)\in\Us_{h}\times\Ws_{h}
to Problem (
19
).

4.2
Error estimates

This section estimates the error of discretizing Problem (
15
) by the space-time interface-fitted method. To derive the desired estimates, we need several auxiliary results.

First, consider a local high regularity function
w\in\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
and denote by
w_{i}\in\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{i}\right)
its restriction to
Q_{i}\ \left(i=1,2\right)
, where
s>0
is given. Recall from
[
4
, Theorem 2.30]
that if
\Gamma^{\ast}
is Lipschitz continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}

\left(m=1\text{ or }2\right)
, then there exist smooth extensions
\Es_{i}:\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{i}\right)\rightarrow\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{T}\right)
such that

\Es_{i}w=w_{i}\qquad\text{in }Q_{i},\qquad\qquad\left\lVert{\Es_{i}w}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{w_{i}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{i}\right)}\qquad(i=1,2).

(25)

On the other hand, while the gradient of the solution to Problem (
15
) is discontinuous across
\Gamma^{\ast}
, that of Problem (
19
) can only pose discontinuity across
\Gamma^{\ast}_{h}
. As a result, the space
\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right)
is more favorable than
\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
when dealing with Problem (
19
). To bridge these two spaces, let us introduce the following operator

\pi_{s}:\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)\rightarrow\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1,h}\cup Q_{2,h}\right),\qquad w\mapsto\pi_{s}w:=\begin{cases}\Es_{1}w&\text{in}\quad Q_{1,h},\\
\Es_{2}w&\text{in}\quad Q_{2,h}.\end{cases}

Clearly,
\pi_{s}w
and
w
coincide everywhere in
Q_{T}
except on the mismatch region
S_{h}
. Regarding their difference within this region, we combine
[
25
, Theorems 3.3 and 3.2]
and
[
25
, Lemma 3.3]
to get the following lemma:

Lemma 6
.

If
w\in\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)
with
s\in\left[2,\frac{m+3}{2}\right]
, then we can find
h^{\dagger}>0
such that for all

Next, we study the interpolation approximability. Consider

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|e_{\pi}\left(w\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ast}\leq Ch\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\right)}.

Now, we prove the last one. By using (

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|e_{\pi}\left(w\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\ast}

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|e_{\pi}\left(w\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|z_{h}\left(\partial_{t}\left(e_{\pi}\left(w\right)\right)\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

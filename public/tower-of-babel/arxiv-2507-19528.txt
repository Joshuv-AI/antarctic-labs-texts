The Eighth Power Moments of Δ ⁡ ( x ) \Delta(x)

Abstract




Abstract
. Using Voronoi’s truncated formula for
\Delta(x)
involving Bessel functions, the first author derives an asymptotic formula for the eighth-power moments with an error term of order

Introduction

Dirichlet’s divisor problem concerns calculating the sum of the number of divisors of all positive integers
n
less than a real number
x
(
x\geqslant 1
). When studying the divisor problem, Dirichlet
[
1
]
first used a partitioning method and obtained:

\displaystyle D(x)=x\log x+(2\gamma-1)x+O(\sqrt{x}),

(1)

thereby finding an upper bound for
\Delta(x)
, where

\displaystyle\Delta(x)=D(x)-x\log x-(2\gamma-1)x,

(2)

and
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Many subsequent studies attempted to improve the upper bound of
\Delta(x)
using new methods, but there is still a gap from the conjecture
\Delta(x)\ll x^{1/4+\varepsilon}
. If we let
\Delta(x)\ll x^{\alpha}(\log x)^{\beta}
, the existing results are:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha=\frac{1}{3}\sim 0.33333\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \ \beta=1\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{ (Voronoi \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{27}{}{}]}, 1904) }\\
\alpha=\frac{27}{82}\sim 0.32927\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \ \ \ \beta=\frac{11}{41}\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{ (Van der Corput \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{30}{}{}]}, 1928) }\end{array}

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{35}{108}+\varepsilon\sim 0.32407\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \ \ \beta=0\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \ \text{ (Kolesnik \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{31}{}{}]}, 1982) }

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{131}{416}\sim 0.31490\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \beta=\frac{26947}{8320}\quad\quad\ \ \text{ (Huxley \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{2}{}{}]}, 2003) }

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{517}{1648}+\varepsilon\sim 0.31371\quad\quad\quad\ \ \ \ \beta=0\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{(Bourgain and Watt \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{28}{}{}]}, 2017) }

To further understand the properties of
\Delta(x)
, researchers have also studied the
k
-th power integral mean of
\Delta(x)
, i.e., the asymptotic formula for
\int_{1}^{X}\Delta^{k}(x)dx
where
k=1,2,3,\cdots
.

When calculating the
k
-th power integral mean of
\Delta(x)
, the following conclusions can be obtained:

First, in the early 20th century, Voronoi
[
14
]
first proved:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{X}\Delta(x)dx=\frac{X}{4}+O(X^{3/4}),

(3)

Subsequently, Cramér
[
17
]
obtained the asymptotic formula for the second power mean:

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{X}\Delta^{2}(x)dx=\frac{(\zeta(3/2))^{4}}{6\pi^{2}\zeta(3)}X^{3/2}+O\left(X^{5/4+\varepsilon}\right).

(4)

In 1956, Dong Guangchang
[
3
]
improved the asymptotic formula for the second power mean and obtained:

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x}\Delta^{2}(X)dx=\frac{\zeta(3/2)^{4}}{6\pi^{2}\zeta(3)}X^{3/2}+O\left(X\log^{5}X\right),

(5)

In 1990, Tsang
[
5
]
began studying the cases
k=3
and
k=4
, obtaining the following asymptotic formulas:

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{T}\Delta^{3}(x)dx=\frac{3C_{1}}{28\pi^{3}}T^{7/4}+O\left(T^{7/4-\delta_{3}+\varepsilon}\right),

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{T}\Delta^{4}(x)dx=\frac{3C_{2}}{64\pi^{4}}T^{2}+O\left(T^{2-\delta_{4}+\varepsilon}\right),

(7)

where
\delta_{3}=1/14
,
\delta_{4}=1/23
,

\displaystyle C_{1}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,h\in\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle(\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta))^{-3/2}h^{-9/4}|\mu(h)|d\left(\alpha^{2}h\right)

(8)

\displaystyle d\left(\beta^{2}h\right)d((\alpha+\beta)^{2}h),

\displaystyle C_{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,l,k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{m}=\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{l}\end{subarray}}(nmkl)^{-3/4}d(n)d(m)d(k)d(l).

(9)

Thereafter, Zhai
[
6
]
used Tsang’s method to prove that
\delta_{3}=1/4
and
\delta_{4}=2/41
hold. In 2004, Ivić and Sargos
[
7
]
also derived the asymptotic formulas (
6
) and (
7
), with their
\delta_{3}
and
\delta_{4}
being 7/20 and 1/12, respectively, improving Tsang’s results
[
5
]
. Similarly, for the generalized expression of the mean integral of
\Delta(x)
, the following conclusion holds:

In 1985, Ivić
[
4
]
proved the estimate:

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{X}|\Delta(x)|^{A}dx\ll T^{1+\frac{A}{4}+\varepsilon}\ (0\leq A\leq 35/4).

(10)

In 2003, Zhai
[
8
]
also obtained the generalized expression for the mean integral of
\Delta(x)
, stating that for any fixed real number
\frac{267}{27}>A>9
and
3\leqslant k\leqslant A
,

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{T}\Delta^{k}(x)dx=C_{k}T^{1+k/4}+O\left(T^{1+k/4-\delta_{k}+\varepsilon}\right)

(11)

holds, where
C_{k}
and
0<\delta_{k}<1+k/4
are constants expressible explicitly. When seeking the
k
-th power mean estimation of
\Delta(x)
, i.e., the asymptotic formula for
\int_{1}^{X}\Delta^{k}(x)dx
(
k=1,2,3,\cdots
), the main tool used is the truncation formula of
\Delta(x)
proved by Voronoi
[

in the last century:

\displaystyle\Delta(x)=-\frac{2\sqrt{x}}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d(n)}{\sqrt{n}}\left(K_{1}(4\pi\sqrt{nx})+\frac{\pi}{2}Y_{1}(4\pi\sqrt{nx})\right),

(12)

where
K_{1}
and
Y_{1}
are Bessel functions, and the commonly used truncation formula is:

\displaystyle\Delta(x)=\frac{x^{1/4}}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\leqslant N}\frac{d(n)}{n^{3/4}}\cos(4\pi\sqrt{nx}-\pi/4)+O\left(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}N^{-1/2}\right).

(13)

The above methods and conclusions have greatly assisted this paper in deriving the asymptotic formula for
\int_{2}^{X}\Delta^{8}(x)dx

\int_{2}^{X}\Delta^{8}(x)dx
and the following conclusions.

.

For any fixed integer

\int_{2}^{X}\Delta^{8}(x)dx=\frac{35C_{7}-28C_{4}}{2048\pi^{8}}\int_{2}^{X}x^{2}dx+O\left(X^{3-1/254+\varepsilon}\right),

where

\displaystyle C_{4}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k,l,r,s,t,j\in\mathbb{N}\\
\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{m}+\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{l}+\sqrt{r}+\sqrt{s}=\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{j}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle(nmklrstj)^{-3/4}d(n)d(m)

(14)

\displaystyle d(k)d(l)d(r)d(s)d(t)d(j)

\displaystyle C_{7}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,l,k,r,s,t,j\in\mathbb{N}\\
\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{m}+\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{l}=\sqrt{r}+\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{j}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle(nmklrstj)^{-3/4}d(n)d(m)

(15)

\displaystyle d(k)d(l)d(r)d(s)d(t)d(j).

Theorem 2
.

For any fixed

\displaystyle\int_{X}^{X+H}\Delta^{8}(x)dx=C\left((X+H)^{3}-X^{3}\right)\left(1+O\left(X^{-k}\right)\right),

Lemma 8
.

Let
1\leqslant N\leqslant M\leqslant K\leqslant L
,
1\leqslant R\leqslant S\leqslant T\leqslant J
,

, and suppose
\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{m}+\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{l}\pm\sqrt{r}\neq\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{j}

,

,

,

,

In particular, when

Proof.

Let

\Delta_{\pm}^{2}+2\sqrt{j}\Delta_{\pm}=l-j+2\sqrt{l}u_{\pm}+\left(u_{\pm}\right)^{2}+O(\delta L).

From

\displaystyle\left|l-j+2\sqrt{l}u_{\pm}+u_{\pm}\right|\leqslant\left(\delta^{2}+2\delta\right)L\ll\delta L

(23)

Thus

does not exceed the number of solutions to inequality (

), which can be found by case analysis.

If
\delta\gg\frac{1}{L}
, then

A_{\pm}(N,M,K,L,R,S,T,J)\ll\delta NMKLRSTJ.

If
\delta\leqslant\frac{1}{2L}
, we have

\displaystyle 0<\left\|j-l-2u_{\pm}\sqrt{l}-u_{\pm}^{2}\right\|=\left\|u_{\pm}^{2}+2u_{\pm}\sqrt{l}\right\|<\delta L\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

(24)

Thus, for a fixed array
(n,m,k,l,r,s,t,j)
, there is at most one

satisfying (

). If such an
l
exists, let
\alpha=2u_{\pm}
,

, and the number of solutions to (

.

When

, by Lemma
3
,

B_{\pm}(N,M,K,R,S,T)\ll\left(\delta L^{2}+L^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)NMKRST,

When

, since
J\asymp L
, if
u_{\pm}=0
, then
j=l
and
\Delta_{\pm}=0
, contradicting
\left|\Delta_{\pm}\right|>0
. Similarly, using the above method, we get:

\displaystyle\left|2\sqrt{k}(\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{m}\pm\sqrt{r}-\sqrt{s})+(\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{m}\pm\sqrt{r}-\sqrt{s})^{2}+k-t\right|<\delta_{1}\sqrt{\frac{K}{L}},

where

is a constant, so

We then classify
\delta_{1}
: When
\delta_{1}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{L}{K}}

\displaystyle 0<\left\|2\sqrt{k}(\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{m}\pm\sqrt{r}-\sqrt{s})+(\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{m}\pm\sqrt{r}-\sqrt{s})^{2}\right\|<\delta_{1}\sqrt{\frac{K}{L}}.

(25)

Thus, except for fixed

, there is at most one
k
satisfying (

exists, we conclude:

When
|\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{m}\pm\sqrt{r}-\sqrt{s}|\gg 1
, by Lemma 1.5,

\displaystyle B_{\pm}(N,M,K,R,S,T)

\displaystyle\ll\log_{2}T\left(\delta_{1}\sqrt{\frac{K}{L}}K+K^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)NMRS

\displaystyle\ll NMRS\left(K+K^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right),

\delta_{2}

\displaystyle B_{\pm}(N,M,K,R,S,T)

\displaystyle\ll\log_{2}T\log_{2}R\sum_{\delta_{2}\leqslant|\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{m}-\sqrt{r}-\sqrt{s}|\leqslant 2\delta_{2}}1

and particularly when

Lemma 11
.

Let
N>2
, and

, then we can show that

\displaystyle\int_{U}^{2U}|S(x,N,k)|^{8}dx\ll\left(UN^{4}+N^{8-1/k}\right)N^{\varepsilon}.

\displaystyle I(\mathbf{h})=(N,2N-h_{1}-h_{2}-h_{3}],

\displaystyle f_{3}(n;\mathbf{h})=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{3}f(n+\mathbf{h}\cdot\mathbf{t})}{\partial t_{1}\partial t_{2}\partial t_{2}}dt_{1}dt_{2}dt_{3}.

\displaystyle|S(x,N,k)|^{8}\leqslant

\displaystyle 8^{7}\left\{N^{4}+\sum_{1\leqslant h_{1}\leqslant H}\sum_{1\leqslant h_{2}\leqslant H^{1/2}}\sum_{1\leqslant h_{3}\leqslant H^{1/4}}\left|S_{3}(x,N,k,\mathbf{h})\right|\right\}

\displaystyle N^{4}+\sum_{1\leqslant h_{1}\leqslant N}\sum_{1\leqslant h_{2}\leqslant N^{2}}\sum_{1\leqslant h_{3}\leqslant N^{4}}\left|S_{3}(x,N,k,\mathbf{h})\right|.

\displaystyle-\left(n+h_{1}\right)^{k}-\left(n+h_{2}\right)^{1/k}-\left(n+h_{3}\right)^{1/k}-\left(n+h_{1}+h_{2}+h_{3}\right)^{1/k},

\int_{H}^{2H}S_{6}(x)dx\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{\frac{71}{24}+\varepsilon}+H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}L^{-\frac{3}{5}}|\Delta_{-}|^{-1}.

By Lemma
4
,
|\Delta_{-}|\gg L^{-\frac{127}{2}}
and
|\Delta_{-}|\gg H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, so

\displaystyle\int_{H}^{2H}S_{6}(x)dx

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{\frac{71}{24}+\varepsilon}+\min\left(H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}L^{\frac{389}{20}},H^{3+\varepsilon}L^{-\frac{3}{5}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{\frac{71}{24}+\varepsilon}+\left(H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}L^{\frac{389}{20}}\right)^{\frac{6}{395}}\left(H^{3+\varepsilon}L^{-\frac{2}{5}}\right)^{\frac{389}{395}}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{3-\frac{1}{254}+\varepsilon}.

Thus
\int_{H}^{2H}S_{6}(x)dx\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{3-\frac{1}{254}+\varepsilon}
, and similarly
\int_{H}^{2H}S_{5}(x)dx\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{3-\frac{1}{254}+\varepsilon}
.

Following the same method for
\int_{H}^{2H}S_{5}(x)dx
and
\int_{H}^{2H}S_{6}(x)dx
, we can find the integral means of
\int_{H}^{2H}S_{2}(x)dx
and
\int_{H}^{2H}S_{3}(x)dx
. By Lemma
9
, Lemma
11
, Lemma
14
, and the above methods, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{H}^{2H}S_{2}(x)dx\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}Y^{\frac{3}{2}}+H^{3-\frac{1}{254}+\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\int_{H}^{2H}S_{3}(x)dx\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}Y^{\frac{3}{2}}+H^{3-\frac{1}{254}+\varepsilon}.

Finally, by first derivative estimation,

\displaystyle\int_{H}^{2H}S_{1}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{128}\int_{H}^{2H}\sum qx^{2}\cos(4\pi U\sqrt{x})

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{3+\varepsilon}\sum_{0<n,m,k,l,r,s,t,j\leqslant Y}q\min\left(1,U^{-1}(\sqrt{H})^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}Y^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Therefore,

\int_{H}^{2H}\left|{\sum}_{Y}(x)\right|^{8}dx=\left(\frac{35C_{7}}{128}-\frac{7C_{4}}{32}\right)\int_{H}^{2H}x^{2}dx+O\left(H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}Y^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)+O\left(H^{3-\frac{1}{254}+\varepsilon}\right),

and since
Y\ll H^{11/36}
, the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem 1.1

To calculate the error term of
\int_{H}^{2H}\Delta^{8}(x)dx
, we use Voronoi’s truncation formula. Taking
N=H
in (
13
):

\displaystyle\Delta(x)=\frac{x^{1/4}}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\leqslant Y}d(n)n^{-3/4}\cos\left(4\pi\sqrt{nx}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+R_{Y,H}(x)+O_{\varepsilon}\left(x^{\varepsilon}\right).

where
H\leqslant X
. The eighth integral mean of

transforms to:

\displaystyle\int_{H}^{2H}\Delta^{8}(x)dx=

\displaystyle(\pi\sqrt{2})^{-8}\int_{H}^{2H}\left|{\sum}_{Y}(x)\right|^{8}dx+O\left(\int_{H}^{2H}R_{Y,H}(x)\left|{\sum}_{Y}(x)\right|^{7}dx\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\int_{H}^{2H}|R_{Y,H}(x)|^{8}dx\right).

We derive

\displaystyle\int_{H}^{2H}R_{Y,H}^{2}(x)dx

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{1+\epsilon}+\int_{H}^{2H}\left|x^{1/4}\sum_{Y<n\leq H}d(n)n^{-3/4}e(2\sqrt{nx})\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{1+\epsilon}+H^{3/2}\sum_{Y<n\leq H}d^{2}(n)n^{-3/2}+H\sum_{Y<m<n\leq H}\frac{d(m)d(n)}{(mn)^{3/4}(\sqrt{n}-\sqrt{m})}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}\frac{H^{3/2}\log^{3}H}{Y^{1/2}},

using the estimates

\sum_{n\leq v}d^{2}(n)\ll v\log^{3}v

and

\sum_{Y<m<n\leq H}\frac{d(n)d(m)}{(nm)^{3/4}(\sqrt{n}-\sqrt{m})}\ll H^{\varepsilon}.

Take
A_{0}=267/27
and
H^{\varepsilon}\leq Y\leq H^{11/36}
. For any
2<A<A_{0}
, by Lemma
15
and Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle\int_{H}^{2H}|R_{Y,H}(x)|^{A}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{H}^{2H}|R_{Y,H}(x)|^{\frac{2(A_{0}-A)}{A_{0}-2}+\frac{A_{0}(A-2)}{A_{0}-2}}dx

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{H}^{2H}|R_{Y,H}(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{A_{0}-A}{A_{0}-2}}\left(\int_{H}^{2H}|R_{Y,H}(x)|^{A_{0}}dx\right)^{\frac{A-2}{A_{0}-2}}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{1+A/4+\varepsilon}Y^{-\frac{A_{0}-A}{2(A_{0}-2)}}.

In particular, when

and

\displaystyle\int_{H}^{2H}|R_{Y,H}(x)|^{8}dx

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{3+\varepsilon}Y^{-\frac{267/27-8}{2(267/27-2)}}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{3-187/5112+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{3-1/254+\varepsilon}.

\displaystyle\int_{H}^{2H}R_{Y,H}(x)\left|{\sum}_{Y}(x)\right|^{7}dx

\displaystyle\ll\left(\int_{H}^{2H}|{\sum}_{Y}(x)|^{8}dx\right)^{\frac{7}{8}}\left(\int_{H}^{2H}|R_{Y,H}(x)|^{8}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{8}}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}(H^{3+\varepsilon})^{\frac{7}{8}}(H^{3-187/5112+\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{8}}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{3+\varepsilon-187/40896}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{3-1/254+\varepsilon}.

Combining with the estimate of
\int_{H}^{2H}\left|{\sum}_{Y}(x)\right|^{8}dx

where

1

.

Identification of a novel plant polerovirus in the whitefly Aleuroclava gordoniae
Authors: Hai-Yan Zhu, Jun-Min Li, Hai-Jian Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTWe report the genome sequence of Aleuroclava gordoniae-associated polerovirus (AgAP), identified from the whitefly Aleuroclava gordoniae. The 5,650-nt AgAP genome contains 6 open reading frames. Phylogenetic analysis places AgAP within the genus Polerovirus, which comprises plant-infecting viruses. This study provides a genomic resource for further investigation of virus–insect associations.

ANNOUNCEMENT

The genus Polerovirus comprises plant viruses with monopartite, positive-sense RNA genomes of approximately 5–6 kb in length, typically containing six open reading frames (ORFs), designated ORF0 to ORF5 (1). Members of this genus are primarily transmitted by aphids in a circulative, persistent manner (2). An exception is pepper whitefly-borne vein yellows virus, which is transmitted by the whitefly Bemisia tabaci (3).

In this study, we identified a novel polerovirus from the gut of whitefly Aleuroclava gordoniae using transcriptomic sequencing of dissected tissues (this virus was not isolated or cultured). Specimens of A. gordoniae were collected from Cinnamomum burmanni trees in Guilin (25.238° N, 110.175° E), Guangxi, China, in July 2023 (Fig. 1A). The guts and the remaining-body tissues (excluding the gut) were dissected separately in phosphate-buffered saline solution using forceps. No surface sterilization procedure was performed. A total of 10 insects were pooled into 2 samples (gut and remaining-body tissues; no biological replicates). The two samples were homogenized in TRIzol reagent (Takara, Dalian, China) for total RNA extraction. Complementary DNA library construction was performed by Novogene (Tianjin, China) using their standard RNA-seq pipeline (4). Paired-end sequencing (150 bp) was conducted on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Raw reads (27,160,830 read pairs for gut sample and 25,133,340 read pairs for remaining-body tissues) were processed using Cutadapt (v1.9) to remove adaptor contamination, low-quality bases, and undetermined bases. Clean read quality was verified using FastQC (v0.10.1) (Q30 >95% for both samples). The filtered reads were not normalized prior to assembly and were subjected to de novo assembly using Trinity (v2.6.6) (5), yielding a total of 182,824 contigs (N50 = 1,552; total assembled bases = 152.4 Mb). Default parameters were used except where otherwise noted. All contigs were then searched against the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) viral RefSeq database (accessed in May 2024) using DIAMOND BLASTx (v2.0.11.149) with an E-value threshold of 1 × 10−20. A near-complete viral genome of 5,650 nt (GC% = 43.3%; coverage = 655×) was recovered and, based on subsequent phylogenetic analysis, was tentatively named Aleuroclava gordoniae-associated polerovirus (AgAP).

Raw reads from both samples were mapped to the AgAP-associated contig using Bowtie2 (v2.5.3) (9) with default parameters. For the gut sample, 24,680 reads (single-end equivalent) mapped to this contig, accounting for approximately 0.09% of the total filtered read pairs, whereas no reads mapped in the remaining-body tissues. The absence of AgAP-derived reads in remaining-body tissues warrants further investigation. Six ORFs were predicted using ORFfinder (www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/orffinder) with the standard genetic code and default parameters (Table 1, Fig. 1B). For phylogenetic analysis, representative members of the family Solemoviridae were selected from the NCBI RefSeq database based on taxonomic assignment and availability of full-length RdRP sequences. Phylogenetic analysis of members of the family Solemoviridae based on RdRP sequences indicated that AgAP was most closely related to persimmon polerovirus and clusters within the genus Polerovirus (Fig. 1C). The near-complete genome of AgAP presented here provides a foundation for future investigations into its biology and ecology.

Abstract
. We provide quantitative weighted weak type estimates for non-integral square functions in the critical case p = 2 p=2 in terms of the A p A_{p} and reverse Hölder constants associated to the weight. The method of proof uses a decoupling of the role of the weights via a quantitative version of Gehring’s lemma. The results can be extended to other p p in the range of boundedness of the square function at hand.

A weight

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{Q\,\,\text{cube }}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w\,\mathrm{d}{\mu}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w^{1-p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}{\mu}\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{p}}:=\sup_{Q\,\,\text{cube }}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{\mu}\right)^{1/p}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w\,\mathrm{d}{\mu}\right)^{-1}<\infty.

Our main result reads as follows:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p_{0}<2<q_{0}
. For any function
f\in L^{2}(w)
, and weight
w\in A_{\frac{2}{p_{0}}}\cap\operatorname{RH}_{(\frac{q_{0}}{2})^{\prime}}
we have

\|S(f)\|_{L^{2,\infty}(w)}\leq C[w]_{A_{2/p_{0}}}^{1/2}[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}^{1/2}\eta_{\varepsilon}([w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}},[w]_{A_{\infty}})^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)},

where

(1.5)

\eta_{\varepsilon}([w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}},[w]_{A_{\infty}}):=[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{q_{0}^{*}(q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon-1)}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\log[w]_{A_{\infty}}\Big)

and
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{q_{0}^{*}}{2^{d+1}[w^{q_{0}^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}-1}
.
The estimate is uniform in the weight.

As an immediate corollary by taking
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2^{d+1}[w^{q_{0}^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}}
, we have the following.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
p_{0}<2<q_{0}
. For any function
f\in L^{2}(w)
, and weight
w\in A_{\frac{2}{p_{0}}}\cap\operatorname{RH}_{(\frac{q_{0}}{2})^{\prime}}
we have

\|S(f)\|_{L^{2,\infty}(w)}\leq C[w]_{A_{2/p_{0}}}^{1/2}[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}^{1/2}\eta([w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}},[w^{q_{0}^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}},[w]_{A_{\infty}})^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(w)},

where

(1.7)

\eta([w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}},[w^{q_{0}^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}},[w]_{A_{\infty}}):=[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}^{1-\gamma}[w^{q_{0}^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}\big([w^{q_{0}^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}+\log[w]_{A_{\infty}}\big),

for some small
\gamma<\frac{1}{4}
. The estimate is uniform in the weight.

Our result is the first quantitative weighted weak
L^{p}
estimate for non-integral square functions. Even for non-integral Riesz transforms, the quantitative weighted weak
L^{p}
estimates are unknown, see
[
5
]
for the quantitative strong type estimates. We do not know how to tackle the latter with our current techniques. There is a different type of result for non-integral square functions at the endpoint
p_{0}
due to Nieraeth and Stockdale
[
17
]
, where weighted weak
L^{p_{0}}
estimates are replaced by mixed type estimates.

In order to understand the estimate obtained in Theorem
1.4
, where the contribution of
[w]_{A_{2/p_{0}}}
seems to be smaller than the contribution from
[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}
, we need to say a few words about the proof. We first reduce the problem by use of a sparse domination form for the operator developed by one of the authors in
[
4
]
. Then, the proof strategy contemplates a decoupling between the
A_{2/p_{0}}
and the
\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}
roles of the weight at hand. The need for quantitative estimates of Gehring’s lemma for weights in the reverse Hölder class (see
[
8
]
) appears very naturally. Gehring’s celebrated work asserts that a weight in a reverse Hölder class
\operatorname{RH}_{q}
, for some
1<q<\infty
, must also be in the class
\operatorname{RH}_{q+\varepsilon}
for some small
\varepsilon
.

We can also provide weighted weak
L^{p}
estimates for the non-integral square functions when
p_{0}<p<q_{0}
,
p\neq 2
, using a quantitative extrapolation result in this setting. It would be of interest to know if there is an extrapolation argument that also decouples the Muckenhoupt and the reverse Hölder roles of the weight. It would most likely lead to a refinement of our estimates.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

2.1.
Sharp reverse Hölder inequality

We start by establishing some reverse Hölder estimates that are crucial to the problem at hand. They can be seen as a quantitative version of Gehring’s lemma and they are deduced from sharp reverse Hölder estimates considered for
w\in A_{\infty}
.

Below and in the following, we use the notation
w(G):=\int_{G}w(x)\,dx
. Moreover, throughout this section,
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes a fixed cube with sides parallel to the coordinate axes, and
M
will denote the maximal function restricted to the dyadic subcubes of
Q
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
w=w1_{Q}
. If
w\in\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}
, for
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{q_{0}^{*}}{\tau_{d}[w^{q_{0}^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}-1}
we have

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon}\,dx\leq 2[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}^{q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w\,dx\Big)^{q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

We use the well-known fact that
w\in\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}
if and only if
w^{q_{0}^{*}}\in A_{\infty}
(see
[
20
]
) and the sharp reverse Hölder inequality from
[
12
]
*Theorem 2.3 for the weight
w^{q_{0}^{*}}
to obtain

(2.2)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon}\,dx\leq 2\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{q_{0}^{*}}\,dx\Big)^{1+\varepsilon/q_{0}^{*}}.

for
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{q_{0}^{*}}{2^{d+1}[w^{q_{0}^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}-1}
. On the right hand side of (
2.2
) we use the definition of
w\in\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}
, which gives us the desired estimate.
∎

We now apply Proposition
2.1
to get the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
w\in\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}
.
For
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{q_{0}^{*}}{2^{d+1}[w^{q_{0}^{*}}]_{A_{\infty}}-1}
we define

(2.4)

\theta:=\frac{q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon-1}{q_{0}^{*}-1}.

Then for
E\subset Q
we have

(2.5)

\frac{w^{q_{0}^{*}}(E)}{w^{q_{0}^{*}}(Q)}\lesssim[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}^{(q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon)/\theta}\Big(\frac{w(E)}{w(Q)}\Big)^{1/\theta^{\prime}}.

Proof.

First we observe that, by using Proposition
2.1
,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}w^{(q_{0}^{*}-1)\theta}\,dw\Big)^{1/\theta}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{|Q|}{w(Q)}\Big)^{1/\theta}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon}\,dx\Big)^{1/\theta}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1/\theta}[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}^{(q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon)/\theta}\Big(\frac{|Q|}{w(Q)}\Big)^{1/\theta}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w\,dx\Big)^{\frac{q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon}{\theta}}

(2.6)

\displaystyle=2^{1/\theta}[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}^{(q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon)/\theta}\langle w\rangle_{Q}^{q_{0}^{*}-1}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1/\theta}[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}^{(q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon)/\theta}\frac{w^{q_{0}^{*}}(Q)}{w(Q)}.

Using this and Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\frac{w^{q_{0}^{*}}(E)}{w(Q)}=\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{E}w^{q_{0}^{*}-1}\,dw

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{E}w^{(q_{0}^{*}-1)\theta}\,dw\Big)^{1/\theta}\Big(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}1_{E}\,dw\Big)^{1/\theta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1/\theta}[w]_{\operatorname{RH}_{q_{0}^{*}}}^{(q_{0}^{*}+\varepsilon)/\theta}\frac{w^{q_{0}^{*}}(Q)}{w(Q)}\Big(\frac{w(E)}{w(Q)}\Big)^{1/\theta^{\prime}}.

Hence
(
2.5
)
holds.

∎

Remark 2.8
.

We conjecture the following

.

If

and

Let us recall the relation in

which is a better bound than the one in Corollary

2.2.
Some known facts and a sparse domination

We start by writing some elementary facts as lemmas; their proofs are left to the reader. Let
M_{p_{0}}
be the dyadic maximal function defined using
p_{0}

Lemma 2.12
.

Let

. Then

(2.13)

\lVert M_{p_{0}}f\rVert_{L^{2,\infty}(w)}\lesssim[w]_{A_{2/p_{0}}}^{1/2}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(w)},

where

and

is its associated constant.

The equivalence in items (1)–(3) below follows, for instance, from

Lemma 2.14
.

The following are equivalent: for

(1)

S(\cdot)\,:\,L^{p}(w)\mapsto L^{p,\infty}(w),

(2)

S(\sigma\cdot)\,:\,L^{p}(\sigma)\mapsto L^{p,\infty}(w),
where
\sigma=w^{1-p^{\prime}}
.

(3)

For every
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists

\langle S(f\sigma)^{2},1_{G^{\prime}}w\rangle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\sigma)}^{2}w(G)^{1-2/p}.

Definition 2.15
.

Consider a system of dyadic cubes

. A collection of dyadic cubes

.

]
*Theorem 1.7

Let

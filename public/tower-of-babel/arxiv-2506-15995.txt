Pion Valence Structure at Intermediate x in the Residual Field Model
Joseph Maerovitz, Alan Sosa, Misak Sargsian, Christopher Leon

Abstract
We calculate the valence parton distribution function of the pion, for the range of 0.1 ≤ x ≤ 0.6 0.1\leq x\leq 0.6 , based on the soft dynamics of the partons within the valence-residual field model. Within this model we assume that the soft partonic structure of the pion is defined by the valence current q ​ q ¯ q\bar{q} cluster embedded in the residual field of the pion. The parton distribution function is calculated using phenomenological wave functions for the q ​ q ¯ q\bar{q} cluster and residual field. Our result indicate that the peak position of x weighted valence parton distribution depends on the mass of the residual system and the parameters of the system are obtained by fitting the height and the peak position of this distribution to extracted PDFs. This comparison reveals that unlike for the case of nucleon PDF, very little residual mass is needed to describe existing pion PDFs at intermediate x.



I
Introduction

As the lightest hadron and a pseudo-Goldstone boson of chiral symmetry breaking, the pion holds a unique position in hadronic and nuclear physics. Understanding its structure is essential for understanding Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) nature of
strong interaction of nucleons which in part is mediated through the pion exchange. Exploring pion structure at different light-front momentum domain
of quarks and gluons will help also to trace the the transition from the partonic to the hadronic degrees of freedom in nuclear interactions at extreme conditions.

One quantity that allows to explore the dynamical structure of pion is its
parton distribution function (PDF). PDFs measured over a wide range of
x,
0<x<1
can allow to address such questions as the dynamics of the
valence distribution as well the role of the sea-quarks and gluons in the pion. It is interesting to explore the role of the valence, sea-quarks and gluons
in pion especially from the point of view of their role in mediating interactions
between nucleons in nuclei.

Much progress has been made in recent years in the study of pion parton distribution functions (PDFs)
Barry et al. 2018a
;
Barry et al. 2021a
.
Recent analysis resulted in a high quality valence quark distribution in the pion as well as produced an unexpected result for PDFs at
x\to 1
.
At
x\to 1
if hard gluon exchange plays dominating role
in generating PDFs, then within perturbative QCD one predicts
an analytic form of pion PDFs to be
(1-x)^{2}
Farrar and Jackson 1975
;
Berger and Brodsky 1979
;
Gunion et al. 1984
. However the mentioned analysis resulted in a behavior of

(1-x)^{\beta}
, with
\beta\approx 1.01
.

Another aspect of the mentioned analysis is obtaining high quality PDF’s at
intermediate
x
.
For intermediate
x
(0.1-0.6) the nonperturbative QCD dynamics define
the structure of parton PDFs. One prominent feature of valence parton
PDFs in this region is that the x weighted partonic distribution (
xf_{i}(x)
)
shows prominent peak associated with the average momentum fraction
carried by valence quarks. As it was shown in our recent work
Leon and Sargsian 2020
;
Leon and Sargsian 2022

modeling such distribution allows to ascertain the role of the
quark-gluon system residual to the valence quarks in the hadron.
In Ref.
Leon and Sargsian 2020
We developed a model within residual mean field approximation which introduces Light-Front wave functions of
valence quark cluster embedded in the residual field of quark-gluon system
described by residual LF wave function.
Within this model for the case of nucleon it was found that at the
starting
Q^{2}_{0}
when one expects partonic picture to be relevant,
the
xf_{i}(x)
for valence quark PDFs of nucleon peaks at

x_{p}\leq{1\over 4}
not at
{1\over 3}
as naively expected.
By describing these peaking properties of valence PDFs of nucleons, we found that it requires the residual system to have a mass comparable to pion mass.
As such the model indirectly favored the existence of the structure in
the nucleon resembling a pion cloud.

In the current work, we develop a residual field model for the description of
pion focusing on the domain of
0.1\leq x\leq 0.6
and investigating the possible
properties of the residual system in the pion.

The model divides the hadron into a valence core and a residual structure while utilizing flavor number conservation to treat the valence structure as effectively a system with a fixed number of fermions. The outline for this work is as follows: Sec.
II
provides a description of the theoretical framework being used and a review of the residual field model. In Sec.
III
we derive the PDFs based on the model and
in Sec.
V
we compare the numerical estimates of our results with recently obtained phenomenological parameterization of pion PDFs in
the range of
0.1\leq x\leq 0.6
. Our focus in
this comparison is to evaluate the mass and the distribution of
possible residual system in the pion.
Sec.
VI
contains conclusions and outlook. Technical details for certain calculations and specific mathematical definitions are presented in the Appendix.

II
Theoretical Framework

In light front (LF) quantization one probes quantum fields at fixed light-cone time,
x^{+}=t+z
, which provides several simplifications in describing high energy processes
Brodsky et al. 1998
. The LF approach provides a framework to study hadrons in terms of their light front wave functions (LFWFs). In principle, knowing the LFWFs would allow us to specify completely a hadronic state. However, finding the LFWFs would involve solving an infinite set of coupled integral equations, and thus often in practice they are modeled. The modeling of the valence LFWFs for the pion has been done to study the distribution amplitude of the pion
Huang et al. 1994
. There has also been modeling on the pion based on AdS/CFT correspondence and light front holography
Trawiński 2016a
;
Trawiński 2016b
.

A hadron in a ket state represented by
|P\rangle
can be described through an expansion in terms of the LFWFs (
\psi_{i/N}\left(\{x_{i},\mathbf{k}_{i}\}_{i}\right)
)
Brodsky et al. 1998
;
Kovchegov and Levin 2012
:

|P\rangle=\sum_{i}\int[d\mu_{i}]\psi_{i/N}\left(\{x_{j},\mathbf{k}_{j}\}_{j}\right)|i\rangle

(1)

where the sum is over all Fock states
|i\rangle
and
[d\mu_{i}]
is the relevant integration measure. Color, spin, etc. degrees of freedom have been suppressed.

One can relate the experimentally measurable PDFs to the LFWFs to constrain them.
In the
\overline{MS}
factorization scheme, the PDF for a quark of flavor
i
is defined as
Sterman et al. 1995
;
Belitsky and Radyushkin 2005
:

\displaystyle f_{i}(x)=\int\frac{dz^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ixP^{+}z^{-}}\langle P|\bar{\psi}_{i}(z)\gamma^{+}\psi_{i}(0)|P\rangle|_{z^{+},z^{j}_{\perp}=0}

(2)

Where we have employed the use of the light cone gauge
A^{+}=0
.

Our approach in this work is phenomenological, instead of taking an infinite Fock state expansion as in Eq.
1
, we approximate the
|P\rangle
state through the transition of
N\rightarrow VR
, where
V
is a valence core and
R
is a residual state and define phenomenological wave functions
in such way that they represent the solution of Weinberg type equation on light-front.
Such wave functions are inherently non-perturbative and need to be modeled based on
the prominent features of the structure of the hadrons.

While quark number is not conserved, flavor is. The residual field model utilizes this fact by separating the hadron into a core of valence fermions whose number is conserved and a residual structure which contains everything besides the valence quarks, such as sea quarks and gluons and possibly a meson cloud in the case of the nucleon. This picture is expected to hold for moderate to high x,
x\gtrsim 0.1
. At lower
x
, Regge dynamics dominate and the model likely ceases to be valid.
According to residual field model the deep inelastic scattering from a hadron (baryons or mesons) in leading approximation can be described by the Light-Front diagram of
Fig.(
1(a)
) in which scattering takes place with the valence quark from
the cluster. The consideration of the residual system in the scattering is equivalent
to the spectral function representation of the partonic structure of hadron in which
case valence quark distribution indirectly related to the dynamical characteristics of
the residual system.

V.2
Interpretation of the Results

The parameters of the model that provide the best fit to the empirical PDF allows to
accertain the mechanism dominating parton dynamics at the range where the peak of
x-weighted PDF distribution is observed. For this we first analyze the response function
of the residual system presented in Eq.(
44
). As Fig.
3
shows
the response function has a wide distribution at
x_{R}
slightly peaking at
x_{R}\approx 0.5
.

Figure 3
:
The
x_{R}
dependence of response function
R(x_{R})
at different
Q^{2}
.

This indicates that the valence
q\bar{q}
system carries a total momentum fraction
1-x_{R}\approx 0.5
. Since
x
-Bjorken corresponds to the Light-Front momentum fraction of
interacting quark, then one concludes that the observed distribution indicates the dominance of the
Feynman mechanism
Feynman 1972
according to which the interacting quark carries the most of the Light-front momentum fraction with remaining momenta distributed among wee partons.

Another observation, as it was noted above, the result of
m_{R}\approx 0
, this indicates that
the residual system does not exhibit any structure (as it was case of nucleon PDFs).
On the other hand the large
m_{V}\approx 0.95
GeV indicate the large virtuality of
interacting quark comparable with the
Q_{0}
of the probe. This together with the
relatively small
B_{R}
indicates that while interacting valence quark is highly virtual, the
residual system is spread over the bulk size of the pion.

This picture is consistent with the phenomenology that nucleon interactions in a nucleus proceed through
the pion exchange in which no quark-interchanges are involved.

Finally, the surprising result of the fit is that the considered model leaves little room for
hard mechanism of high x PDF generated though the hard gluon exchange between valence
q\bar{q}

system.

VI
Conclusion and Outlook

We have calculated pion valence parton distribution within residual field model, which was
previously applied to the calculation of Nucleon PDFs. The models assume no hard
interaction between valence quarks and it separates
q\bar{q}
valence cluster from
the residual system. Both,
q\bar{q}
and
V-R
systems are described by nonperturbative
Light-Front wave functions that satisfies relativistic normalization. These wavefunctions allow to introduce the residual response function as well as the response function of the
q\bar{q}
valence cluster. The pion PDF represents the convolution of these response functions.
We analyzed the parameters of the model by fitting the position and the height of the
x-weighted PDF using empirical JAM18 distribution.

The fitting indicates that one can describe the above attributes of pion PDF only if
the mass of the residual system is close to zero. However we obtain large virtual mass for
the
q\bar{q}
cluster comparable to the
Q_{0}
of the probe. Analysis of the response function
of the residual system indicates that the dominating mechanism is similar to the Feynman mechanism in which interacting parton carries almost all the momentum of the valence quark system, with the remaining light-front momentum distributed to the non-interacting wee partons.

The fit allows to describe the empirical PDF reasonably well in the range of
0.1\leq x\leq 0.85

leaving little room for the hard mechanism of generating PDFs at large x.
The model practically resolves the observed
(1-x)^{\beta}
,
\beta\approx 1
scaling relation of
the pion PDFs at large x.

With the parameters of
q\bar{q}
and residual system Light-Front wave functions fixed
one will be able to make numerical predictions for different observables such as generalized parton distribution functions (GPDs) or transverse momentum dependent parton distribution functions (TMDs) in pion that will allow for additional
verification of the model.

Appendix A
Mandelstam s with many particles

For
n
particles each with 4-momentum

k^{\mu}_{i}=\left(k^{+}_{i},\frac{k_{\perp,i}^{2}+m^{2}_{i}}{k_{i}^{+}},\mathbf{k}^{\perp}_{i}\right)

(46)

k^{\mu}_{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k^{\mu}_{i}=(x_{T}P^{+},k^{-}_{T},\mathbf{k}^{\perp}_{T})

s_{n}
is:

s_{n}=\Bigg(\sum_{i}^{n}k^{\mu}_{i}\Bigg)^{2}=\sum_{i}^{n}\frac{\tilde{k}_{i,\perp}^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}{\beta_{i}}

(47)

where:

\beta_{i}=\frac{x_{i}}{x_{T}}

(48)

\mathbf{\tilde{k}}_{i,\perp}=\mathbf{k}_{i,\perp}-\beta_{i}\mathbf{k}_{T,\perp}

(49)

The is seen by:

\displaystyle s_{n}

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\sum_{i}^{n}k^{\mu}_{i}\Bigg)^{2}=\Bigg(\sum_{i}^{n}k^{+}_{i}\Bigg)\Bigg(\sum_{i}^{n}k^{-}_{i}\Bigg)-\Bigg(\sum_{i}^{n}\mathbf{k}_{i,\perp}\Bigg)^{2}

(50)

\displaystyle=(x_{T}P^{+})\Bigg(\sum_{i}^{n}\frac{{k}_{i,\perp}^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}{x_{i}P^{+}}\Bigg)-k^{2}_{T,\perp}

(51)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{k^{2}_{i,\perp}+m^{2}_{i}}{\frac{x_{i}}{x_{T}}}-k^{2}_{T,\perp}

(52)

Using the definition of
\beta_{i}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_{i,\perp}
:

\displaystyle s_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{\tilde{k}^{2}_{i,\perp}+2\beta_{i}\mathbf{k}_{T,\perp}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_{i,\perp}+\beta_{i}^{2}k_{T,\perp}+m^{2}_{i}}{\beta_{i}}-k^{2}_{T,\perp}

(53)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{\tilde{k}^{2}_{i,\perp}+2\beta_{i}\mathbf{k}_{T,\perp}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_{i,\perp}+\beta_{i}^{2}k_{T,\perp}+m^{2}_{i}}{\beta_{i}}-k^{2}_{T,\perp}

(54)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\Bigg(\frac{\tilde{k}^{2}_{i,\perp}+m^{2}_{i}}{\beta_{i}}+2k_{T,\perp}\cdot\tilde{k}_{i,\perp}+\beta_{i}k^{2}_{T,\perp}\Bigg)-k^{2}_{T,\perp}

(55)

Using the fact that
\sum_{i}\beta_{i}=1
and
\sum_{i}\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_{i,\perp}=\sum_{i}({\mathbf{k}}_{i,\perp}-\beta_{i}\mathbf{k}_{T})=\mathbf{0}
,

s_{n}=\sum_{i}\Bigg(\frac{\tilde{k}^{2}_{i,\perp}+m^{2}_{i}}{\beta_{i}}+k^{2}_{T,\perp}-k^{2}_{T,\perp}\Bigg)

(56)

Then,

s_{n}=\sum_{i}\frac{\tilde{k}^{2}_{i,\perp}+m^{2}_{i}}{\beta_{i}}

(57)

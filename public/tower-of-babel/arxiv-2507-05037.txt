Generalizing blocking semiovals in finite projective planes
Marilena Crupi, Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
. Blocking semiovals and the determination of their (minimum) sizes constitute one of the central research topics in finite projective geometry. In this article we introduce the concept of blocking set with the r ∞ r_{\infty} -property in a finite projective plane PG ​ ( 2 , q ) \textup{PG}(2,q) , with r ∞ r_{\infty} a line of PG ​ ( 2 , q ) \textup{PG}(2,q) and q q a prime power. This notion greatly generalizes that of blocking semioval. We address the question of determining those integers k k for which there exists a blocking set of size k k with the r ∞ r_{\infty} -property. To solve this problem, we build new theory which deeply analyzes the interplay between blocking sets in finite projective and affine planes.

1.
Introduction

Blocking sets are a fundamental concept in combinatorial geometry and finite geometry, and they play a critical role in various areas of mathematics, including design theory, coding theory, and the study of finite projective and affine planes. Their intriguing properties and wide range of applications make blocking sets a rich subject of study in both theoretical and applied mathematics.

Indeed, blocking sets can be seen as a bridge between theoretical combinatorial concepts and practical applications in various fields of mathematics and engineering. Their study not only enhances our understanding of finite geometries but also contributes to advancements in technology and science through their applications in coding theory, design theory, and beyond.

Roughly speaking a
blocking set
in a finite projective plane is defined as a set of points such that every line in the plane intersects the set in at least one point. More formally, consider a projective plane
\textup{PG}(2,q)
of order
q
, where
q
is a prime power. A subset
{\mathcal{B}}
of the point set of
\textup{PG}(2,q)
is called a blocking set if every line in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
contains at least one point from
{\mathcal{B}}
and does not contain any line.

Such a definition can also be introduced in the context of the finite affine plane
\textup{AG}(2,q)
where
q
is a prime power.

To visualize this, imagine a finite projective plane where lines and points follow specific incidence properties. A blocking set ensures that no matter which line one chooses, there will always be at least one point from the blocking set on that line. This simple yet powerful property underpins the utility of blocking sets in various applications.

A crucial question in the study of blocking sets concerns determining their minimum size. For a projective plane of order
q
, it is known that any blocking set contains at least
q+\sqrt{q}+1
points
[
8
]
. This bound is significant because it represents the smallest number of points needed to ensure the blocking property. Understanding these minimal blocking sets is essential because they provide insight into the most efficient ways to achieve the blocking condition. A minimal blocking set has the property that it does not contain any proper subset which is also a blocking set.

In a projective plane, a
semioval
is a set of points
{\mathcal{B}}
such that there is a unique tangent
line, that is, a line with one point of contact, at each point. A
blocking semioval
is a set of points in a projective plane that is both a blocking set and a semioval. Hubaut
[
16
]
has shown that a semioval contains at least
q+1
points. For a blocking semioval
{\mathcal{B}}
we have
q+\sqrt{q}+1\leq|{\mathcal{B}}|\leq q\sqrt{q}+1

[
8
,
16
]
(see also
[
9
]
).

The notion of semiovals has been around since the 1970’s (
[
5
,
26
,
18
]
), but the study of semiovals has been motivated by the pioneering paper of Batten
[
2

and initiated by Dover in
[

. While the study of blocking semiovals was originally motivated by Batten
[
2

in connection with cryptography, other authors studied these objects, because they are interesting in their own right.

A classical example of blocking semioval is given by the vertexless triangle in a finite projective plane of order
q>2
. If
a,b,c
are any three non concurrent lines in the plane, the vertexless triangle

is defined as the set of all points which lie on exactly one of these lines, that is, the set of points on the sides of this triangle without the vertices.

is a blocking semioval of size

The problem of determining blocking semiovals of a given size is open. Moreover, the question of what the true lower bound on the size of a blocking semioval remains to be answered, since the upper bound

is reached by any vertexless triangle. Furthermore, given

Let
\textup{PG}(2,q)
be a projective plane. In the present paper, inspired by the notion of blocking semioval, we introduce the concept of

-property
. A blocking set

2.
Generalities on blocking sets

In this section for the reader’s convenience we collect some notions and results we need for the development of the article.

Let
\textup{PG}(2,q)
be the classical projective plane of order

for

has

We fix some notation. In

Given a projective plane

Let

We quote the next definitions from
[
15
, Chapter 13]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A blocking set of
\textup{PG}(2,q)
is a set of points
{\mathcal{B}}
which meets every line but does not contain any line. A blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}
in
\textup{AG}(2,q)
is a set of points which meets every line of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
.

A blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}
of
\textup{PG}(2,q)

(\textup{AG}(2,q)
, respectively
)
is called minimal if no proper subset of
{\mathcal{B}}
is a blocking set of
\textup{PG}(2,q)

(\textup{AG}(2,q)
, respectively
)
.

Note that in the affine case
{\mathcal{B}}
may contain some line of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
.

Geometrically, by
[
15
, Lemma 13.1]
a blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
is minimal if and only if, for every point

of
{\mathcal{B}}
, there exists at least a tangent
\ell
to
{\mathcal{B}}
passing through
P
, that is some line
\ell
such that

.

Definition 2.2
.

A semioval is a set
{\mathcal{K}}
of points of
\textup{PG}(2,q)
such that for every
P\in{\mathcal{K}}
there exists a unique line
\ell
of
\textup{PG}(2,q)
such that
\ell\cap{\mathcal{K}}=P
.

Combining the Definitions

and

, we obtain the concept of

, that is, a set of points in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
which is both a semioval and blocking set.

One can observe that blocking semiovals are necessarily minimal blocking sets, as deleting a point of a blocking semioval

will cause that the tangent to
{\mathcal{K}}
at that point is unblocked. On the other hand, a blocking semioval is also a maximal semioval. Indeed, adding any point to a blocking semioval
{\mathcal{K}}
will cause the added point to have no tangent, as every line through that point must already meet
{\mathcal{K}}
.

Remark 2.3
.

By
[
15
, Corollary 13.3]
, a blocking set exists in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
if and only if

. Hence, hereafter we always tacitly assume that

In any finite projective plane of order
q>2
, let
a,b,c
be any three non concurrent lines. Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be the set of all points which lie on exactly one of these lines, that is, the set of points on the sides of this triangle without the vertices. Then
{\mathcal{T}}
establishes the existence of blocking semiovals of size
3(q-1)
in all finite projective planes (see, for instance
[
9
]
) except for the

, which does not contain blocking sets.

We close the section with some results from

,

,

]
.

T=\ell\setminus\{D_{1},\dots,D_{q-2},A,A^{\prime}\}
.

Observe that
BT,AC
are two tangents to
{\mathcal{B}}
through
X
, and
CT,AB
are two tangents to
{\mathcal{B}}
through
Y
. Moreover, for every
P\in{\mathcal{B}}\cap\{a\setminus A^{\prime}\}
,
PT,PA
are tangents and for every
P\in{\mathcal{B}}\cap\{\ell\setminus A^{\prime}\}
,
PB,PC
are tangents. Finally, since every line has at least
q+1\geq 5
points, there exist at least two tangents through
A^{\prime}
.

∎

From Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
, the next statement follows.

Theorem 3.10
.

In
\textup{PG}(2,q)
,
q\geq 5
, there exists a minimal blocking set of size
k
, for every
2q\leq k\leq 3q-5
, with the
r_{\infty}
-property which is not a blocking semioval.

Theorem 3.11
.

In
\textup{PG}(2,q)
,
q\geq 5
, every minimal blocking set built with a
k
-construction, with
2q\leq k\leq 3q-5
, has the
r_{\infty}
-property with respect to every point
P\in{\mathcal{B}}\cap(\{a\setminus\bigcup_{i,j=1}^{n}\{B_{i}C_{j}\cap a\}\}\cup b\cup c)
.

Proof.

For every point
P\in{\mathcal{B}}\cap c
the only tangent is
PC
, for every point
P\in{\mathcal{B}}\cap b
the only tangent is
PB
. For every point
D_{i}
there are two tangents, then we must exclude these points and also points
P
of
{\mathcal{B}}\cap\{a\setminus A^{\prime}\}
, which are intersection of the lines
B_{i}C_{j}
with
a
, because for these points we have the tangents
B_{i}C_{j}
and
PA
. Similarly, for
A^{\prime}
there are at least two tangents
B_{1}C_{2}
and
B_{2}C_{1}
.

∎

Combining Theorem
3.10
with Theorem
2.4
, we obtain the next result.

Theorem 3.12
.

If
q\geq 5
, then there exists a minimal blocking set with the
r_{\infty}
-property of size
k
for every
k
such that
2q\leq k\leq 3(q-1)
.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.10
the desired blocking sets exist for any
2q\leq k\leq 3q-5
. For
k=3q-4
the assertion follows from Theorem
2.4
(b) together with Remark
3.2
. Finally, for
k=3q-3
we can apply Remark
3.3
.

∎

4.
Blocking sets with the
\Pi
-property in the Affine Planes

We have seen in Theorem
3.10
that for
q\geq 4
, and any
2q\leq k\leq 3q-5
, there exists a blocking set of size
k
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
having the
r_{\infty}
-property. The natural question arises whether there exists any blocking set in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
,
q\geq 4
, with the
r_{\infty}
-property, having size
k\leq 2q-1
. Surprisingly, we have

Theorem 4.1
.

Every blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
with size
k\leq 2q-1
does not verify the
r_{\infty}
-property with respect to any point
P\in{\mathcal{B}}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that Lemma
3.9
is a particular case of Theorem
4.1
.

In order to prove the theorem, firstly we investigate classes of minimal blocking (semiovals) sets in the affine plane
\textup{AG}(2,q)
. The next general lemma will be fundamental. It can be considered as a kind of permanence property.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a minimal blocking set in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
, which has the
r_{\infty}
-property with respect to
P\in{\mathcal{B}}
, then
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{B}}\setminus P
is a minimal blocking set of
\textup{AG}(2,q)=\textup{PG}(2,q)\setminus r_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Every line of
\textup{PG}(2,q)
through
P
, which is different from
r_{\infty}
, intersects
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
, and every line not through
P
intersects
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
. Thus
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
is a blocking set of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
. Let
P^{\prime}
be a point of
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
, then there is a line
r
of
\textup{PG}(2,q)
that intersects
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
exactly in
P^{\prime}
and consequently a line of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
that intersects
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
exactly in
P^{\prime}
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
is minimal.

∎

.

In
\textup{PG}(2,3)
with the set of points
\{ABCDEFGHIJKLM\}
, we consider the minimal blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}=\{ABCDFI\}
. Next picture describes
\textup{PG}(2,3)
.

, since

a=(BKIM),\,\,\,b=(ADGK),\,\,\,c=(CFGM).

We consider the line

Example
4.4
highlights that in the affine plane
\textup{AG}(2,3)
we have minimal blocking sets of size
5
, since we have only the vertexless triangles whose size is
3(q-1)=6
as minimal blocking sets in the projective plane
\textup{PG}(2,3)
.

Now we examine the existence of minimal blocking sets in
\textup{AG}(2,q)
,
q\geq 5
. Combining Theorems
3.10
and
3.12
with Lemma
4.3
we obtain the following two results.

Theorem 4.5
.

In
\textup{AG}(2,q)
,
q\geq 5
, there exists a minimal blocking set of size
k
for every
k
such that
2q-1\leq k\leq 3q-6
.

Theorem 4.6
.

In
\textup{AG}(2,q)
, if
q\geq 5
is odd, there exists a minimal blocking set of size
k
for every
k
such that
2q-1\leq k\leq 3q-4
.

In what follows, sometimes to avoid any ambiguity we will speak about a
projective blocking set
when we refer to a blocking set of the projective plane
\textup{PG}(2,q)
, and of an
affine blocking set
when we refer to a blocking set of the affine plane
\textup{AG}(2,q)
.

In order to introduce some special affine blocking sets and to highlight their connection with some projective blocking sets, we give the next definition.

Definition 4.7
.

A parallel class in an affine plane
\textup{AG}(2,q)
is the set of all lines parallel to a line
\ell
. We denote it by
\Pi_{\ell}
and call it the direction
\Pi_{\ell}
.

For instance, in Example
4.4
, the lines
(ALM)
,
(BCD)
,
(EFK)
of
\textup{AG}(2,3)
are parallel.

In what follows we will regard any parallel class, generated by a line
\ell
of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
, as a new point.

Furthermore, we collect all new points into a new line
r_{\infty}
. Then we can define the projective plane
\textup{PG}(2,q)
as follows:
\textup{PG}(2,q)=\textup{AG}(2,q)\cup r_{\infty}
. See for instance
[
21
]
and the reference therein.

We will indicate a parallel class also by
\Pi_{P}
if it is the parallel class of all lines passing through
P\in r_{\infty}
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
.

Definition 4.8
.

A blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}
in
\textup{AG}(2,q)
has the
\Pi
-property with respect to a direction
\Pi_{\ell}
if there exists a parallel class
\Pi_{\ell}
verifying the following conditions:

(j)

through every point
Q\in{\mathcal{B}}
there exists a line
m\notin\Pi_{\ell}
tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}
,

(jj)

not one line of
\Pi_{\ell}
is contained in
{\mathcal{B}}
.

Now with (j’) we indicate the following condition that implies the condition (j):

(j’)

through every point
Q\in{\mathcal{B}}
there exists a unique tangent
m\notin\Pi_{\ell}
to
{\mathcal{B}}
.

Blocking sets that verify the conditions (j) and (jj) in Definition
4.8
are necessarily minimal and blocking set that verify the conditions (j’) and (jj) are also maximal.

Example 4.9
.

Let
a,b
be two parallel lines of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
and let
c
be another line not parallel to
a,b
. Set
A=b\cap c
and
B=a\cap c
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}=(a\cup b\cup c)\setminus\{A,B\}
is a minimal blocking set of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
of size
3q-4
that has the
\Pi
-property with respect to the direction
\Pi_{c}
. Note that in this example every line in
\Pi_{c}
is a secant to
{\mathcal{B}}
, and the conditions (j’) and (jj) are verified.

Example 4.10
.

Let
a,b
be two parallel lines of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
,
q\geq 5
, and let
c
be another line not parallel to
a,b
. Set
A=b\cap c
and
B=a\cap c
. Choose a line
\ell
through
A
not the line
c
or
b
and a point
D_{1}
on
\ell
but not on
b
or
a
. Let

A^{\prime}=\ell\cap a,\,C_{1}=r\cap c,\,B_{1}=BD_{1}\cap b,\,C_{2}=A^{\prime}B_{1}\cap c,\,D_{2}=s\cap\ell,\,B_{2}=BD_{2}\cap b,

with
r
the parallel line through
D_{1}
to
a
and
s
the parallel line through
C_{2}
to
a
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}=((a\cup b\cup c)\cup\{D_{1},D_{2}\})\setminus\{A,B,B_{1},B_{2},C_{1},C_{2}\}
is a minimal blocking set of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
of size
3q-6
, that has the
\Pi
-property with respect to the direction
\Pi_{c}
. The condition (j’) is not verified, because for
D_{1},D_{2}
there are two tangents.

Now we fix a line
\ell=r_{\infty}
in the projective plane
\textup{PG}(2,q)
and a point
P\in\ell
. We indicate with
{\mathcal{X}}_{P,\ell}
the set of all minimal blocking sets in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
with the
r_{\infty}
-property with respect to
P
, such that the unique tangent to any of these blocking sets is the line
\ell
, and with
{\mathcal{Y}}_{\Pi_{P}}
the set of all minimal blocking sets in
\textup{AG}(2,q)=\textup{PG}(2,q)\setminus\ell
with the
\Pi
-property with respect to a direction
\Pi_{P}
.

Next, we show that every blocking set in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
with the
r_{\infty}
-property with respect to
P\in r_{\infty}
determines a minimal blocking set in
\textup{AG}(2,q)=\textup{PG}(2,q)\setminus r_{\infty}
with the
\Pi
-property with respect to a direction
\Pi_{P}
and viceversa.

Proposition 4.11
.

The map
\alpha:{\mathcal{X}}_{P,\ell}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}_{\Pi_{P}}
defined as follows

{\mathcal{B}}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{P,\ell}\longmapsto{\mathcal{B}}\setminus P\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{\Pi_{P}}

is a bijection.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.3
, it follows that
{\mathcal{B}}\setminus P
is a minimal blocking sets of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
, and from the definition of
\alpha
, it follows that the mapping is injective.

Now we prove that
{\mathcal{B}}\setminus P
has the
\Pi
-property with respect to a direction
\Pi_{P}
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{B}}\setminus P
contains a line in
\Pi_{P}
, then
{\mathcal{B}}
contains a line in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
and
{\mathcal{B}}
is not a blocking set. This verifies the condition (jj) of Definition
4.8
. Let
\ell^{\prime}\in\Pi_{P}
be a line tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}\setminus P
in
\textup{AG}(2,q)
, then
\ell^{\prime}
is not tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
. Consequently there is a tangent
m\notin\Pi_{P}
to
{\mathcal{B}}
passing through
Q\in\ell^{\prime}\cap({\mathcal{B}}\setminus P)
. This line
m
is also a tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}\setminus P
, since it does not contain the point
P
, and this implies the condition (j) of Definition
4.8
. Finally, the claim is proved.

Now we start from a minimal blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{\Pi_{P}}
of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
. It is trivial that
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cup P
is a minimal blocking set in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
. It contains a line, this line must pass through
P
, and consequently there is a line in
\Pi_{P}
which is contained in
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
, that contradicts (jj) of Definition
4.8
. This implies that
{\mathcal{B}}
is a blocking set of
\textup{PG}(2,q)
. For every point of
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
, there exists a tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
in the affine plane that is not in
\Pi_{P}
and thus the same tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}
in the projective plane. Through
P
there exists the unique tangent
\ell
to
{\mathcal{B}}
and this means that
{\mathcal{B}}
has the

r_{\infty}
-property with respect to
P
, and the unique tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}
is the line
\ell
. To finish the proof
\alpha({\mathcal{B}})={\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
.

∎

According to the definition given in the projective case (Setup
3.7
), we say that a minimal blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
of
\textup{AG}(2,q)
is built with a
k
-construction,
2q\leq k\leq 3q-5
, if considered a minimal blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}
of
\textup{PG}(2,q)
built with a
k
-construction, chosen a point
P
for which
{\mathcal{B}}
has the
r_{\infty}
-property and such that the unique tangent through
P
to
{\mathcal{B}}
is
\ell
, we have
\alpha({\mathcal{B}})={\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
.

Observe that Example
4.9
and Example
4.10
are built starting from a vertexless triangle in the projective plane of the order
q
, and from a
(3q-5)
-construction, respectively.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a minimal blocking set in
\textup{AG}(2,q)
,
q\geq 5
, built with a
k
-construction with
2q\leq k\leq 3q-5
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}
has the
\Pi
-property with respect to a direction
\Pi_{\ell}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Lemma
3.8
and Proposition
4.11
.

∎

The following key result was shown by Jamison
[
17
]
and, with a simpler proof, by Brouwer and Schrijver
[
7
]
. See also
[
15
, Corollary 13.46]
.

Lemma 4.13
.

Every blocking set of the lines in
\textup{AG}(2,q)
has a size greater than or equal to
2q-1
.

We are now in the position to prove our main result in the section.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a blocking set with the
r_{\infty}
-property with respect to a point
P
of size
k\leq 2q-1
. Since there exists a tangent through
P
to
{\mathcal{B}}
, any blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
with
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{B}}
must contain
P
. Therefore there exists a minimal blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{B}}
with the
r_{\infty}
-property with respect to the same point
P
. From Proposition
4.11
, there exists a blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\setminus P
in
\textup{AG}(2,q)
with size
k^{\prime}<2q-1
, which contradicts Lemma
4.13
.

∎

Definition 4.14
.

A blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}
in
\textup{AG}(2,q)
has the
\Pi
-strong property with respect to a direction
\Pi_{\ell}
if the conditions
(j’)
and
(jj)
are verified.

Let us denote with
{\mathcal{Z}}_{P,\ell}
the set of all blocking semiovals in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
which contain the point
P
and such that
\ell
is the unique tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}
through
P
, and with
{\mathcal{W}}_{P,\ell}
the set of all blocking semiovals in
\textup{AG}(2,q)=\textup{PG}(2,q)\setminus\ell
with the
\Pi
-strong property with respect to a direction
\Pi_{P}
.

The next result shows that every
{\mathcal{B}}\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{P,\ell}
determines a blocking semioval in
\textup{AG}(2,q)=\textup{PG}(2,q)\setminus\ell
with the
\Pi
-property with respect to a direction
\Pi_{P}
.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
\alpha:{\mathcal{X}}_{P,\ell}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}_{\Pi_{P}}
be the bijection defined as follows:

{\mathcal{B}}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{P,\ell}\longmapsto{\mathcal{B}}\setminus P\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{\Pi_{P}}.

Then
\alpha({\mathcal{Z}}_{P,\ell})={\mathcal{W}}_{P,\ell}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a blocking semioval. From Proposition
4.11
, it is sufficient to prove condition (j’). For every
Q\in{\mathcal{B}}
(
Q\neq P
) there exists a unique tangent
m
to
{\mathcal{B}}
. This tangent does not contain
P
, then
m\notin\Pi_{\ell}
in the affine plane. Starting from a minimal blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
in
\textup{AG}(2,q)
that verifies the conditions (j’) and (jj), then for every point
Q\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
the unique tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
is also the unique tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cup P
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
. At the end for
P
the unique tangent is
\ell
. This proves that
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cup P
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
is a blocking semioval.

∎

5.
Conclusions and Open questions

In the previous sections we have discussed the existence of certain blocking sets with the
r_{\infty}
-property in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
having a given size.

Let us consider the case
q=4
.

In
\textup{PG}(2,4)
we have only minimal blocking sets of size
k\in\{7,8,9\}
. For
k=9
, there are the vertexless triangles which have the
r_{\infty}
-property. If
k=7
, there are no blocking sets with the
r_{\infty}
-property (Theorem
4.1
).

The next example, provided by one of the referees, shows that there exists a blocking set with the
r_{\infty}
-property of size
3q-4
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
for
q=4
.

Recall that a proper subplane
{\mathcal{B}}
of a projective plane
\textup{PG}(2,q)
is called a
Baer subplane
if each line of
\textup{PG}(2,q)
contains a point in
{\mathcal{B}}
and, dually, each point of
\textup{PG}(2,q)
is incident with a line in
{\mathcal{B}}
.

Example 5.1
.

Let
R
and
T
be two points of a Baer subplane
{\mathcal{B}}
in
\textup{PG}(2,4)
. Let
t
be one of the two tangent lines to
{\mathcal{B}}
at
T
and
r_{1},r_{2}
be the two tangent lines to
{\mathcal{B}}
at
R
. Finally, for
i=1,2
, denote the point
t\cap r_{i}
by
T_{i}
. Then it is easy to check that
({\mathcal{B}}\setminus\{R\})\cup\{T_{1},T_{2}\}
is a minimal blocking set of cardinality
8=3q-4
and it has the
r_{\infty}
-property at
T
. Indeed, the unique tangent is the other tangent to
{\mathcal{B}}
at
T
.

Let
q\geq 5
. By Theorem
4.1
, for any
1\leq k\leq 2q-1
, no blocking set with the
r_{\infty}
-property of size
k
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
exists. Whereas, by Theorem
3.10
, there exists a blocking set
{\mathcal{B}}
with the
r_{\infty}
-property of size
k
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
, for all
2q\leq k\leq 3q-5
and the size
k=3(q-1)
is achieved if
{\mathcal{B}}
is a vertexless triangle. Furthermore, by Theorem
2.4
, if
q\geq 5
, there exists such a set
{\mathcal{B}}
, in fact a blocking semioval, of size
k=3q-4
.

These considerations lead to the following open question.

Open question 5.2
.

Are there blocking sets with the
r_{\infty}
-property of size
k>3(q-1)
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
with
q\geq 5
?

By Theorem
2.4
(a), we know that
|{\mathcal{B}}|\leq 3(q-1)
for
q>5
and any blocking semioval
{\mathcal{B}}
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
with
x_{q-1}\neq 0
. For instance any vertexless triangle which is a blocking semioval of size
3(q-1)
satisfy the property
x_{q-1}\neq 0
. For
2q+2\leq k\leq 3q-5
we do not know whether there exist blocking semiovals of size
k
. By Lemma
3.8
, for
2q\leq k\leq 3q-5
, we only know that any blocking set built with a
k
-construction verifies the
r_{\infty}
-property but is not a semioval.

Open question 5.3
.

Determine those integers
2q+2\leq k\leq 3q-5
,
q>5
, for which there exists a blocking semioval of size
k
in
\textup{PG}(2,q)
.

The case
k=3q-4
, with
q\geq 5
, was addressed by Dover and Suetake
[
9
,
25
]
.

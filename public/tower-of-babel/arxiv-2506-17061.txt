Non-uniform bounds for non-normal approximation via Stein’s method with applications to the Curie–Weiss model and the imitative monomer-dimer model
Lê Vǎn Thành ∗, Nguyen Ngoc Tu

Abstract
This paper establishes a non-uniform Berry–Esseen bound for non-normal approximation using Stein’s method. The main theorem generalizes the result of the authors in [Comptes Rendus Mathématique, 2024] to the context of non-normal approximation. As an application of the main result, we derive non-uniform Berry–Esseen bounds in non-central limit theorems for the magnetization in the Curie–Weiss model and the imitative monomer-dimer model. These extend some existing results in the literature, including Theorem 2.1 of Chatterjee and Shao [Ann. Appl. Probab., 2011] and Theorem 1 of Chen [J. Math. Physics, 2016].

Introduction

Let
(W,W^{\prime})
be an exchangeable pair, that is,
(W,W^{\prime})
and
(W^{\prime},W)
have the same distribution.
Stein
stein1986approximate
developed a new method and proved a normal approximation for
W
under the linear regression condition

\mathbb{E}(W^{\prime}|W)=(1-\lambda)W,

(1.1)

for some
0<\lambda<1
.
Chatterjee and Shao
chatterjee2011nonnormal
, and Eichelsbacher and Löwe
eichelsbacher2010stein
expanded Stein’s method to accommodate a broader class of distributional approximations beyond the normal case. They generalized the linear regression condition (
1.1
) to a nonlinear form

\mathbb{E}\left(W^{\prime}\mid W\right)=W-\lambda\psi(W)+R,

(1.2)

where
0<\lambda<1
, function
\psi(x)
depends on a continuous distribution under consideration and
R
is a random variable.

While much of the work on Stein’s method has concentrated on uniform error bounds,
several achievements have demonstrated its effectiveness in proving non-uniform Berry–Esseen bounds (Chen et al.
chen2021error
, Chen and Shao
chen2001non
;
chen2004normal
)
and the Cramér-type moderate deviation (Chen et al.
chen2013stein
, Mukherjee et al.
mukherjee2024moderate
, Shao et al.
shao2021cramer
, Zhang
zhang2023cramer
).
Very recently, Butzek and Eichelsbacher
butzek2024non
, Dung et al.
dung2025non
, Eichelsbacher
eichelsbacher2024stein
, Liu et al.
liu2021non
, and Thành and Tu
thanh2025non
extended Stein’s method to obtain non-uniform bounds in
normal and non-normal approximations.
Compared to the Cramér-type moderate deviation,
non-uniform Berry–Esseen results typically require weaker conditions and provide a bound with a more explicit constant.

The present paper is a follow-up to
thanh2025non
,
where the authors used a non-uniform concentration inequality approach in Stein’s method for normal approximation
for exchangeable pairs. Here, we generalize the main theorem of
thanh2025non
to cover both normal and non-normal approximations.
The main result is applied to derive non-uniform Berry–Esseen bounds in non-central limit theorems for the magnetization in the
critical Curie–Weiss model
and the critical imitative monomer-dimer model. These results extend Theorem 2.1 of Chatterjee and Shao
chatterjee2011nonnormal
,
Theorem 3.8 of Eichelsbacher and Löwe
eichelsbacher2010stein
and Theorem 1 of Chen
chen2016limit
to the non-uniform Berry–Esseen bounds.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
presents a general theorem on non-uniform Berry–Esseen bound for non-normal approximation of exchangeable pairs.
In Section
3
, we present five preliminary lemmas and the proof of Theorem
2.3
.
Non-uniform Berry–Esseen bounds
in the non-central limit theorems for the magnetization in the critical Curie–Weiss model and the
critical imitative monomer-dimer model
are presented in Section
4
.

2
A non-uniform Berry–Esseen bound for non-normal approximation of exchangeable pairs

Let
k
be a positive integer,
a_{k}
a positive constant and

Y_{k}
a random variable with probability density function

p_{k}(x)=b_{k}\exp\left\{-a_{k}x^{2k}\right\},\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R},

(2.1)

where
b_{k}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left\{-a_{k}x^{2k}\right\}\operatorname{d}x\right)^{-1}
is the normalizing constant.
Note that when
k=1
and
a_{k}=1/2
,
Y_{k}
is a standard normal distribution random variable.
Let
(W,W^{\prime})
be an exchangeable pair satisfying

\mathbb{E}\left(W^{\prime}\mid W\right)=W-\lambda\psi(W)+R,

(2.2)

where
0<\lambda<1
and
\psi(x)=2ka_{k}x^{2k-1}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Eichelsbacher and Löwe
eichelsbacher2010stein

established uniform Berry–Esseen bounds for the random variable
W
. From Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 2.4 of Eichelsbacher and Löwe
eichelsbacher2010stein
,
we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(Eichelsbacher and Löwe
eichelsbacher2010stein
)

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
Y_{k}
be a random variable distributed according to
p_{k}
as given in (
2.1
).
Let
(W,W^{\prime})
be an exchangeable pair of random variables satisfying (
2.2
), and let
\Delta:=W-W^{\prime}
.
Then

\begin{split}&\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}(W\leq z)-\mathbb{P}(Y_{k}\leq z)\right|\\
&\leq C(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2k-1})\left(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{E}({\Delta}^{2}|W)\right)^{2}}+\dfrac{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}R^{2}}}{\lambda}+a+\frac{a^{3}}{\lambda}+\dfrac{\mathbb{E}\Delta^{2}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|>a)}{\lambda}\right),\end{split}

where
C
is a constant depending only on the density
p_{k}
.

Recently, Eichelsbacher
eichelsbacher2024stein
used results from Liu et al.
liu2021non
and Shao and Zhang
shao2019berry

to obtain a non-uniform Berry–Esseen bound for bounded exchangeable pairs.
The following result is a consequence of Theorem 2.5 of Eichelsbacher
eichelsbacher2024stein
.

Theorem 2.2

(Eichelsbacher
eichelsbacher2024stein
)

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
Y_{k}
be a random variable distributed according to
p_{k}
as given in (
2.1
).
Let
(W,W^{\prime})
be an exchangeable pair of random variables satisfying (
2.2
), and let
\Delta:=W-W^{\prime}
.
Assume that
\mathbb{E}|\psi(W)|^{2}<\infty
and
|\Delta|\leq a
for some
a>0
. Then for any
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\left|\mathbb{P}(W\leq z)-\mathbb{P}(Y_{k}\leq z)\right|\leq\dfrac{C}{\left(1+|z|^{2k-1}\right)}\left(\left|\mathbb{E}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{2}|W)\right)\right|+\dfrac{\mathbb{E}|R|}{\lambda}+3a\right),

(2.3)

where
C
is a constant depending only on
p_{k}
and
\mathbb{E}|\psi(W)|^{2}
.

In Remark 3 of Eichelsbacher
eichelsbacher2024stein
, the author noted that if the random variable
R
in (
2.2
) is absent,
then the term
1/(1+|z|^{2k-1})
in (
2.3
) can be improved to
1/(1+|z|^{p})
for
p\geq 2k-1

provided that
\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}<\infty
.
However, in many applications, the exchangeable pair
(W,W^{\prime})

only satisfies (
2.2
), making it essential to develop new techniques to address this limitation.

The main objective of the present paper is to generalize the main result in
thanh2025non

to cover both normal and non-normal approximations. Our proof is based on a non-uniform concentration inequality, which differs from those used by
Eichelsbacher
eichelsbacher2024stein
and Liu et al.
liu2021non
.
This approach allows for strengthening the term

1/(1+|z|^{2k-1})
in (
2.3
) to
1/(1+|z|)^{p}
for any
p\geq 2k-1
, even in the presence of the remainder

R
. This advancement broadens the applicability of non-uniform Berry–Esseen bounds and provides sharper error estimates in various models.
The main result, Theorem
2.3
, also extends Theorem
2.2
to the case where
\Delta
may not be bounded.
For the normal case (
k=1
and
a_{k}=1/2
), Theorem
2.3
reduces to the case of non-uniform bound for normal approximation, which is Theorem 1.2 in
thanh2025non
. Moreover, the constant
C(p)
in Theorem 1.2 in
thanh2025non
is not as explicit as in Theorem
2.3
.

Theorem 2.3

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
Y_{k}
be a random variable distributed according to
p_{k}
as given in (
2.1
).
Let
(W,W^{\prime})
be an exchangeable pair of random variables satisfying (
2.2
) and let
\Delta=W-W^{\prime}
.
Then for any
a>0
,
p\geq 2k-1
and
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\begin{split}&|\mathbb{P}(W\leq z)-\mathbb{P}(Y_{k}\leq z)|\\
&\leq\frac{B^{p}(p^{p/k}+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})}{(1+|z|)^{p}}\left(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{E}({\Delta}^{2}|W)\right)^{2}}+\dfrac{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}R^{2}}}{\lambda}+a+\frac{a^{3}}{\lambda}+\dfrac{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Delta^{4}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|>a)}}{\lambda}\right),\end{split}

where
B
is a constant which depends only on
k
and
a_{k}
.

3
Proof of Theorem
2.3

In this section, we provide a proof of Theorem
2.3
. Throughout this section, we use the notations defined in Section
2
.
The symbol
B
denotes a constant which depends only on
k
and
a_{k}
and can be different for each appearance.
If
\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}=\infty
, then the result is trivial. Therefore, it suffices to consider the case
\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}<\infty
.

To establish Theorem
2.3
, we first introduce some preliminary lemmas.
The first lemma is a simple consequence of Lyapunov’s inequality. See
Thành and Tu
thanh2025non
for a proof.

Lemma 1

Let
p\geq 1
,
0<s\leq 2p
and let
|c|\leq 100
. Then

\mathbb{E}|W+c|^{s}\leq B^{p}(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{p}).

(3.1)

The next lemma generalizes the non-uniform concentration inequality in Lemma 2.2 of
Thành and Tu
thanh2025non
, and its proof follows a similar approach to that of
Lemma 2.2 of Thành and Tu
thanh2025non
.

Lemma 2

Let
p\geq 2k-1
and
z\geq 5
. Then for
0<a<1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta^{2}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|\leq a)\mathbf{1}(z-a\leq W\leq z+a)\right)\leq\dfrac{B^{p}(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})a(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}R^{2}}+\lambda)}{(1+z)^{p}}.

(3.2)

Proof.

As in Lemma 2.2 of Thành and Tu
thanh2025non
, we set

f(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ for }x<z-2a,\\
(1+x+2a)^{p}(x-z+2a)&\text{ for }z-2a\leq x\leq z+2a,\\
4a(1+x+2a)^{p}&\text{ for }x>z+2a.\\
\end{cases}

Applying the exchangeability and (
2.2
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left((W-W^{\prime})(f(W)-f(W^{\prime}))\right)&=2\mathbb{E}Wf(W)-2\mathbb{E}W^{\prime}f(W)\\
&=2\mathbb{E}Wf(W)-2\mathbb{E}(f(W)\mathbb{E}(W^{\prime}|W))\\
&=2\mathbb{E}Wf(W)-2\mathbb{E}f(W)(W-\lambda\psi(W)+R)\\
&=2\lambda\mathbb{E}\psi(W)f(W)-2\mathbb{E}f(W)R.\end{split}

(3.3)

We note that
f
is continuous, increasing,
f^{\prime}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}
, and

\begin{split}f^{\prime}(x)\geq(1+x+2a)^{p}\geq(1+z)^{p}\text{ for }z-2a\leq x\leq z+2a.\end{split}

(3.4)

Therefore

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left((W-W^{\prime})(f(W)-f(W^{\prime}))\right)\\
&=\mathbb{E}\Delta\int_{-\Delta}^{0}f^{\prime}(W+t)\operatorname{d}t\\
&\geq\mathbb{E}\Delta\int_{-\Delta}^{0}\mathbf{1}(|t|\leq a)\mathbf{1}(z-a\leq W\leq z+a)f^{\prime}(W+t)\operatorname{d}t\\
&\geq\mathbb{E}\Delta\int_{-\Delta}^{0}\mathbf{1}(|t|\leq a)\mathbf{1}(z-a\leq W\leq z+a)(1+z)^{p}\operatorname{d}t\\
&\geq(1+z)^{p}\mathbb{E}\Delta\int_{-\Delta}^{0}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|\leq a)\mathbf{1}(z-a\leq W\leq z+a)\operatorname{d}t\\
&=(1+z)^{p}\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta^{2}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|\leq a)\mathbf{1}(z-a\leq W\leq z+a)\right),\end{split}

(3.5)

where we have applied (
3.4
) in the second inequality.
By using
definition of
f
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality
and Lemma
1
, we have

\begin{split}&2\lambda\mathbb{E}\psi(W)f(W)-2\mathbb{E}f(W)R\\
&\leq 2\lambda\mathbb{E}(4a|1+W+2a|^{p}2ka_{k}|W|^{2k-1})+2\mathbb{E}(4a|1+W+2a|^{p}|R|)\\
&\leq 8a\left(2ka_{k}\lambda\sqrt{\mathbb{E}|W+1+2a|^{2p}\mathbb{E}|W|^{4k-2}}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}|W+1+2a|^{2p}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}R^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq B^{p}(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})a(\lambda+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}R^{2}}).\end{split}

(3.6)

Combining (
3.3
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) yields (
3.2
).

The proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

Let
P_{k}(z)=\mathbb{P}(Y_{k}\leq z)=\int_{-\infty}^{z}p_{k}(x)\operatorname{d}x
. For
z\in\mathbb{R}
, let
f_{z}
be the solution to the Stein equation

f^{\prime}(x)-\psi(x)f(x)=\mathbf{1}(x\leq z)-P_{k}(z),

(3.7)

and let
g_{z}(x)=(\psi(x)f_{z}(x))^{\prime}

and

Lemma 3

(Eichelsbacher and Löwe

)

Let
z\in\mathbb{R}
and let
P_{k}
,
f_{z}
and
g_{z}
be defined as above. The following statements hold.

(i)

For

1-P_{k}(x)\leq\min\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{b_{k}}{2ka_{k}x^{2k-1}}\right)\exp\left(-a_{k}x^{2k}\right),

(3.8)

and therefore, for

P_{k}(x)=1-P_{k}(-x)\leq\min\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{b_{k}}{2ka_{k}|x|^{2k-1}}\right)\exp\left(-a_{k}x^{2k}\right).

(3.9)

(ii)

The explicit formular for
f_{z}
is

f_{z}(x)=\begin{cases}(1-P_{k}(z))P_{k}(x)\exp({a_{k}x^{2k}})b_{k}^{-1}&\text{if }\ x<z,\\
P_{k}(z)(1-P_{k}(x))\exp({a_{k}x^{2k}})b_{k}^{-1}&\text{if }\ x\geq z.\end{cases}

(3.10)

(iii)

The explicit formular for

is

f_{z}^{\prime}(x)=\begin{cases}(1-P_{k}(z))\left[1+2ka_{k}x^{2k-1}P_{k}(x)\exp({a_{k}x^{2k}})b_{k}^{-1}\right]&\text{if }\ x<z,\\

(3.11)

(iv)

For all

, we have

0<f_{z}(x)\leq\dfrac{1}{2b_{k}},\text{ and }|f_{z}^{\prime}(x)|\leq 1.

(3.12)

(v)

For
x<z
, we have

g_{z}(x)=(1-P_{k}(z))\left[b_{k}^{-1}P_{k}(x)\exp(a_{k}x^{2k})\left((4k^{2}-2k)a_{k}x^{2k-2}+4k^{2}a_{k}^{2}x^{4k-2}\right)+2ka_{k}x^{2k-1}\right].

For any fixed
a>0
, elementary calculations show

\exp(-az^{2k})\leq\frac{B^{p}p^{p/k}}{(1+z)^{2p}}\text{ for all }z>0.

(3.13)

Hereafter, we will use (
3.13
) without explicitly mentioning it.
The following two lemmas are generalizations of Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4 in Thành and Tu
thanh2025non
, respectively.

Lemma 4

For
z\geq 5
and
\xi
is a random variable satisfying
0\leq|\xi|\leq|\Delta|
. Then

\mathbb{E}(f_{z}(W))^{2}\leq\dfrac{B^{p}(p^{p/k}+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})}{(1+z)^{2p}},

(3.14)

and

\mathbb{E}(f_{z}^{\prime}(W+\xi))^{2}\leq\dfrac{B^{p}(p^{p/k}+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})}{(1+z)^{2p}}.

(3.15)

Proof.

Firstly, we prove (
3.14
).
To bound

, we write

\mathbb{E}(f_{z}(W))^{2}=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

(3.16)

where

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\mathbb{E}(f_{z}(W))^{2}\mathbf{1}(W<0),\ I_{2}=\mathbb{E}(f_{z}(W))^{2}\mathbf{1}(W>z/2),\ I_{3}=\mathbb{E}(f_{z}(W))^{2}\mathbf{1}(0\leq W\leq z/2).\end{split}

From (

) and (

0<f_{z}(x)\leq B(1-P_{k}(z)),\ x<0.

(3.17)

By noting that

) and (

\begin{split}I_{1}\leq B(1-P_{k}(z))^{2}\leq B\exp\left(-2a_{k}z^{2k}\right)\leq\dfrac{B^{p}p^{p/k}}{(1+z)^{2p}}.\end{split}

(3.18)

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq\left(\frac{1}{2b_{k}}\right)^{2}\mathbb{P}(1+W>1+z/2)\\

By noting that

Lemma 5

Let
z\geq 5
and
|u|\leq 100
. Then

\mathbb{E}g_{z}(W+u)\leq\dfrac{B^{p}(p^{p/k}+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})}{(1+z)^{p}}.

(3.27)

Proof.

Since
\psi(x)=2ka_{k}x^{2k-1}
,
|u|\leq 100
and

, we have from (

) and Lemma

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}g_{z}^{2}(W+u)&\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left((\psi^{\prime}(W+u)f_{z}(W+u))^{2}+(\psi(W+u)f_{z}^{\prime}(W+u))^{2}\right)\\

&\leq B^{p}(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}).\end{split}

(3.28)

Applying (

again, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}g_{z}(W+u)\mathbf{1}(W+u>z/2)&\leq(\mathbb{E}g_{z}^{2}(W+u))^{1/2}(\mathbb{E}\mathbf{1}(W+u>z/2))^{1/2}\\
&\leq\frac{(\mathbb{E}g_{z}^{2}(W+u))^{1/2}(\mathbb{E}|W+u|^{2p})^{1/2}}{(z/2)^{p}}\\
&\leq\dfrac{B^{p}(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})}{(1+z)^{p}}.\end{split}

(3.29)

From Lemma

0<g(x)\leq B(1-P_{k}(z))\leq\frac{B^{p}p^{p/k}}{(1+z)^{p}},\ x<0,

It thus follows from (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}g_{z}(W+u)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}g_{z}(W+u)\left(\mathbf{1}(W+u<0)+\mathbf{1}(0\leq W+u\leq z/2)+\mathbf{1}(W+u>z/2)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{B^{p}p^{p/k}}{(1+z)^{p}}+g_{z}(z/2)+\mathbb{E}(g_{z}(W+u)\mathbf{1}(W+u>z/2))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{B^{p}(p^{p/k}+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})}{(1+z)^{p}},

thereby establishing (
3.27
).
∎

With Lemmas
2
,
4
, and
5
established, the proof of Theorem
2.3

proceeds along the same lines as the proof of Theorem 1.2 in Thành and Tu
thanh2025non
. For completeness and to keep the paper self-contained, we provide the details below.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

To bound
\mathbb{P}(W\leq z)-\mathbb{P}(Y_{k}\leq z)
, it suffices to consider
z\geq 0
since we can simply apply the result to
-W
when
z<0
. In view of Theorem
2.1

and the fact that

\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{2}I(|\Delta|>a))\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{4}I(|\Delta|>a))},

we may assume that
z\geq 5
.
Since

\begin{split}|\mathbb{P}(W\leq z)-P_{k}(z)|&=|\mathbb{P}(W>z)-(1-P_{k}(z))|\\
&\leq\dfrac{\mathbb{E}|1+W|^{2p}}{(1+z)^{2p}}+(1-P_{k}(z))\\
&\leq\dfrac{C(p)\left(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\right)}{(1+|z|)^{p}},\\
\end{split}

the conclusion of the theorem holds if
a\geq 1
. It remains consider the case
0<a<1
.

Let
f_{z}
be the solution to Stein’s equation (
3.7
). Then applying (
3.3
) again, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}(W\leq z)-P_{k}(z)\\
&=\mathbb{E}(f_{z}^{\prime}(W)-\psi(W)f_{z}(W))\\
&=\mathbb{E}f_{z}^{\prime}(W)-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{E}(W-W^{\prime})(f_{z}(W)-f_{z}(W^{\prime}))-\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{E}(f_{z}(W)R)\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[f^{\prime}_{z}(W)\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}(W-W^{\prime})^{2}\right)\right]-\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{E}(f_{z}(W)R)\\
&\quad-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{E}[(W-W^{\prime})(f_{z}(W)-f_{z}(W^{\prime})-(W-W^{\prime})f^{\prime}_{z}(W))]\\
&:=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}.\end{split}

(3.32)

Using Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Lemma
4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |T_{1}|&\leq\dfrac{C(p)(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})}{(1+z)^{p}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{E}((W-W^{\prime})^{2}|W)\right)^{2}},\\
|T_{2}|&\leq\dfrac{C(p)(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})}{(1+z)^{p}}\dfrac{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}(R^{2})}}{\lambda}.\end{aligned}

(3.33)

It remains to bound
T_{3}
. By recalling
\Delta=W-W^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (-2\lambda)T_{3}&=\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta(f_{z}(W)-f_{z}(W-\Delta)-\Delta f_{z}^{\prime}(W))\right)\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|>a)(f_{z}(W)-f_{z}(W-\Delta)-\Delta f_{z}^{\prime}(W))\right)\\
&\quad\quad+\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|\leq a)(f_{z}(W)-f_{z}(W-\Delta)-\Delta f_{z}^{\prime}(W))\right)\\
&:=T_{3,1}+T_{3,2}.\end{aligned}

(3.34)

By applying Taylor’s expansion, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
3.15
), we obtain,
for a random variable
\xi
satisfying
0\leq|\xi|\leq|\Delta|
, that

\displaystyle|T_{3,1}|

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta^{2}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|>a)(f_{z}^{\prime}(W+\xi)-f_{z}^{\prime}(W))\right)|

(3.35)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta^{2}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|>a)(f_{z}^{\prime}(W+\xi)\right)|+|\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta^{2}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|>a)f_{z}^{\prime}(W))\right)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\Delta^{4}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|>a)\mathbb{E}(f_{z}^{\prime}(W+\xi))^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\mathbb{E}\Delta^{4}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|>a)\mathbb{E}(f_{z}^{\prime}(W))^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C(p)(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Delta^{4}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|>a)}}{(1+z)^{p}}.

From the Stein equation (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle T_{3,2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|\leq a)\int_{-\Delta}^{0}(f_{z}^{\prime}(W+t)-f_{z}^{\prime}(W))\operatorname{d}t\right)

(3.36)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|\leq a)\int_{-\Delta}^{0}(W+t)f_{z}(W+t)-Wf_{z}(W)\operatorname{d}t\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|\leq a)\int_{-\Delta}^{0}(\mathbf{1}(W+t\leq z)-\mathbf{1}(W\leq z))\operatorname{d}t\right)

\displaystyle:=T_{3,2,1}+T_{3,2,2}.

By using Lemma
5
and noting that
g_{z}(w)\geq 0
for all
w
, we have

\displaystyle|T_{3,2,1}|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|\leq a)\int_{-\Delta}^{0}\int_{0}^{t}g_{z}(W+u)\operatorname{d}u\operatorname{d}t\right)\right|

(3.37)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(0\leq\Delta\leq a)\int_{-\Delta}^{0}\int_{0}^{t}g_{z}(W+u)\operatorname{d}u\operatorname{d}t\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(-a\leq\Delta\leq 0)\int_{-\Delta}^{0}\int_{0}^{t}g_{z}(W+u)\operatorname{d}u\operatorname{d}t\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq a\int_{0}^{a}\int_{-t}^{0}\mathbb{E}g_{z}(W+u)\operatorname{d}u\operatorname{d}t+a\int_{0}^{a}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}g_{z}(W+u)\operatorname{d}u\operatorname{d}t

\displaystyle=a\int_{0}^{a}\int_{-t}^{t}\mathbb{E}g_{z}(W+u)\operatorname{d}u\operatorname{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C(p)(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})a^{3}}{(1+z)^{p}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle|T_{3,2,2}|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(0\leq\Delta\leq a)\int_{-\Delta}^{0}\mathbf{1}(z<W\leq z-t)\operatorname{d}t\right)\right|

(3.38)

\displaystyle+\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(-a\leq\Delta<0)\int_{-\Delta}^{0}\left(\mathbf{1}(W\leq z-t)-\mathbf{1}(W\leq z)\right)\operatorname{d}t\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta\mathbf{1}(0\leq\Delta\leq a)\int_{-\Delta}^{0}\mathbf{1}(z-a\leq W\leq z+a)\operatorname{d}t\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left(-\Delta\mathbf{1}(-a\leq\Delta<0)\int_{0}^{-\Delta}\mathbf{1}(z-a\leq W\leq z+a)\operatorname{d}t\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta^{2}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|\leq a)\mathbf{1}(z-a\leq W\leq z+a)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(p)(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})a(\lambda+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}R^{2}})}{(1+z)^{p}},

where we have applied Lemma
2
in the last inequality.
Combining (
3.36
), (
3.37
) and (
3.38
) yields

\displaystyle|T_{3,2}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(p)(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})(a^{3}+a(\lambda+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}R^{2}}))}{(1+z)^{p}}.

(3.39)

Combining (
3.34
), (
3.35
) and (
3.39
) yields

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |T_{3}|&\leq\dfrac{C(p)(1+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p})}{(1+z)^{p}}\left(\dfrac{a\sqrt{\mathbb{E}R^{2}}}{\lambda}+a+\dfrac{a^{3}}{\lambda}+\dfrac{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Delta^{4}\mathbf{1}(|\Delta|>a)}}{\lambda}\right).\end{aligned}

(3.40)

The conclusion of the theorem follows from (

), (

) and (

4
Applications

In this section, we apply Theorem
2.3
to establish non-uniform
Berry–Esseen bounds for the magnetization in the critical Curie–Weiss model and
the critical imitative monomer-dimer model.
Throughout this section, the symbol
B
denotes a universal constant
which can be different for each appearance.

4.1
The critical Curie–Weiss model

Consider the classical Curie–Weiss model
for
n
spins
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
. The joint distribution of
\sigma
is

\mathbb{P}(\sigma)=\dfrac{1}{Z_{\beta}}\exp\left(\dfrac{\beta}{n}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\right),

where
Z_{\beta}
is the normalizing constant and
\beta>0
is the inverse temperature.
We are interested in distribution approximations
of the total spin (or magnetization)
S_{n}=\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{n}
.
It has been shown that the asymptotic behavior of
S_{n}
changes when
\beta

crosses the critical value
1
.
If
0<\beta<1
or
\beta>1
, then
S_{n}
obeys a central limit theorem. However, if
\beta=1
, then
S_{n}

has a non-normal limit. Precisely,
Ellis and Newman
ellis1978limit
proved that if
\beta=1
, then
S_{n}/n^{3/4}
converges to
Y

in distribution, where
Y
is a random variable with probability density function

p(x)=K\exp(-x^{4}/12),

(4.1)

and
K
is the normalizing constant.

Chatterjee and Shao
chatterjee2011nonnormal
, and Eichelsbacher and Löwe
eichelsbacher2010stein
established
the uniform Berry–Esseen bound for the Ellis–Newman non-central limit theorem
with rate
n^{-1/2}
by using Stein’s method for exchangeable pairs.
The Cramér-type moderate deviation for
S_{n}
was established by
Can and Pham
can2017cramer
, and Shao et al.
shao2021cramer

with the same rate.

In this subsection, we apply Theorem
2.3
to establish a non-uniform
Berry–Esseen bound for the total spin
S_{n}
. Theorem
4.1
extends Theorem 2.1
of Chatterjee and Shao
chatterjee2011nonnormal
and Theorem 3.8 of Eichelsbacher and Löwe
eichelsbacher2010stein

to the non-uniform Berry–Esseen bound.

Theorem 4.1

Let
p\geq 3
. Consider the critical Curie–Weiss model and let
W:=W_{n}=S_{n}/n^{3/4}
, where

S_{n}=\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{n}
denotes the total spin.
Then for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\left|\mathbb{P}\left(W\leq z\right)-\mathbb{P}(Y\leq z)\right|\leq\dfrac{B^{p}p^{p/2}}{(1+|z|)^{p}n^{1/2}},

(4.2)

where
Y
is a random variable with probability density function as given in (
4.1
).

Proof.

Let
\sigma^{\prime}=\{\sigma_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\sigma_{n}^{\prime}\}
,
where for each
i
fixed,
\sigma_{i}^{\prime}
is an independent copy of
\sigma_{i}
given
\{\sigma_{j},j\neq i\}
,
that is, given
\{\sigma_{j},j\neq i\}
,

\sigma_{i}^{\prime}
and
\sigma_{i}
have the same distribution and
\sigma_{i}^{\prime}
is conditionally independent of
\sigma_{i}
.
Let
I
be a random index independent of all others and uniformly distributed over
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and let

W^{\prime}=\frac{S_{n}-\sigma_{I}+\sigma_{I}^{\prime}}{n^{3/4}}.

Let
\psi(x)=(x^{4}/12)^{\prime}=x^{3}/3
and
\lambda=1/n^{3/2}
.
Chatterjee and Shao
chatterjee2011nonnormal
proved that
(W,W^{\prime})
is an exchangeable pair satisfying

\mathbb{E}(W^{\prime}|W)=W-\lambda\psi(W)+R,

(4.3)

where
R
is a random variable.
We will prove that

\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\leq 20^{p/2}p^{p/2},

(4.4)

\frac{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}|R|^{2}}}{\lambda}\leq\frac{B}{n^{1/2}},

(4.5)

and

\mathbb{E}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{E}({\Delta}^{2}|W)\right)^{2}\leq\frac{B}{n},

(4.6)

where
\Delta=W-W^{\prime}
. By (
4.3
) and the fact that

|\Delta|=\left|\frac{\sigma_{I}-\sigma_{I}^{\prime}}{n^{3/4}}\right|\leq\frac{2}{n^{3/4}},

(4.7)

we are now able to apply Theorem
2.3
with
k=2
,
p_{k}(x)=K\exp(-x^{4}/12)
and
a=2/n^{3/4}
.
By collecting the bounds in (
4.4
)–(
4.6
), we obtain (
4.2
).

It remains to prove (
4.4
)–(
4.6
).
From inequality (5.28) in Chatterjee and Shao
chatterjee2011nonnormal
, we have

|R|=\left|\mathbb{E}(W-W^{\prime}|\sigma)-\frac{W^{3}}{3n^{3/2}}\right|\leq\frac{2|W|^{5}}{15n^{2}}+\frac{|W|}{n^{2}}+\frac{1}{n^{11/4}}.

(4.8)

First, assume that
p\geq 3
is an integer.
Since
W
is measurable with respect to
\sigma
, multiplying
|W^{2p-3}|
on both sides
of the inequality in (
4.8
) and then taking the expectation yields

\left|\mathbb{E}(W-W^{\prime})W^{2p-3}-\frac{\mathbb{E}W^{2p}}{3n^{3/2}}\right|\leq\frac{2\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p+2}}{15n^{2}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-2}}{n^{2}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-3}}{n^{11/4}},

(4.9)

and therefore

\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\leq\left|3n^{3/2}\mathbb{E}(W-W^{\prime})W^{2p-3}\right|+\frac{2\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p+2}}{5n^{1/2}}+\frac{3\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-2}}{n^{1/2}}+\frac{3\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-3}}{n^{5/4}}.

(4.10)

By using the exchangeability of
(W,W^{\prime})
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}(W^{\prime}-W)W^{2p-3}&=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(W^{\prime}-W)(W^{2p-3}-(W^{\prime})^{2p-3})\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(W^{\prime}-W)^{2}(W^{2p-4}+W^{2p-5}W^{\prime}+\cdots+(W^{\prime})^{2p-4}).\end{split}

(4.11)

For any
0\leq k\leq 2p-4
, we have from Hölder’s inequality that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}|W^{2p-4-k}(W^{\prime})^{k}|\leq\left(\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}\right)^{(2p-4-k)/(2p-4)}\left(\mathbb{E}|W^{\prime}|^{2p-4}\right)^{k/(2p-4)}=\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}.\end{split}

(4.12)

Combining (
4.7
), (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) yields

\begin{split}\left|\mathbb{E}(W^{\prime}-W)W^{2p-3}\right|\leq 2(2p-3)n^{-3/2}\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}.\end{split}

(4.13)

Since
|W|\leq n^{1/4}
,

\frac{2\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p+2}}{5n^{1/2}}+\frac{3\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-2}}{n^{1/2}}+\frac{3\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-3}}{n^{5/4}}\leq\frac{2\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}}{5}+3\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}+\frac{3\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}}{n}.

(4.14)

Combining (
4.10
), (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) yields

\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\leq 6(2p-3)\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}+\frac{2\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}}{5}+6\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4},

which is equivalent to

\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\leq 20(p-1)\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}.

(4.15)

Since

\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}\leq\left(\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\right)^{(p-2)/p},

we obtain from (
4.15
) that

\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\leq 20^{p/2}(p-1)^{p/2}

(4.16)

thereby establishing (
4.4
). Now, consider the case where
p\geq 3
is a real number.
Let
\lceil p\rceil
denote the smallest integer that is greater or equal to
p
.
Applying Jensen’s inequality and (
4.16
) with
p
replaced by
\lceil p\rceil
, we have

\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\leq\left(\mathbb{E}|W|^{2\lceil p\rceil}\right)^{p/\lceil p\rceil}\leq\left(20^{\lceil p\rceil/2}(\lceil p\rceil-1)^{\lceil p\rceil/2}\right)^{p/\lceil p\rceil}\leq 20^{p/2}p^{p/2}

again establishing (
4.4
).

By applying (
4.4
) with
p=5
, (
4.8
) and Jensen’s inequality, we obtain (
4.5
).
Chatterjee and Shao
chatterjee2011nonnormal
(see the proof of Lemma 5.1 in
chatterjee2011nonnormal
) also proved that

\left|2n^{-3/2}-\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{2}|\sigma)\right|\leq 2n^{-5/2}+2n^{-2}W^{2}.

(4.17)

Since
\lambda=n^{-3/2}
and
W
is measurable with respect to
\sigma
, (
4.17
) implies

\mathbb{E}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{E}(\Delta^{2}|W)\right)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left(n^{-1}+n^{-1/2}W^{2}\right)^{2}\leq 2(n^{-2}+n^{-1}\mathbb{E}W^{4}).

(4.18)

By applying (
4.4
) with
p=3
, (
4.18
) and Jensen’s inequality, we obtain (
4.6
).

The proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

(4.19)

using Theorem
2.3
.

Compared to (

), Theorem
4.1
improves the term
(1+|z|^{3})
to

(1+|z|^{p})
for any

. In
can2017cramer

.

4.2
The imitative monomer-dimer mean-field model

A mean-field version of the monomer-dimer model with imitative interaction, called the imitative monomer-dimer model, is introduced as follows.
For
n\geq 1
, let
G=(V,E)
be a complete graph with vertex set
V=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and edge set
E=\{\{u,v\}:u,v\in V,u<v\}
.
A dimer configuration on the graph
G
is a set
D
of pairwise nonincident
edges satisfying the condition that if
\{u,v\}\in D
, then for all
w\neq v
,
(u,w)\notin D
.
Let
\mathcal{D}
denote the set of all dimer configurations.
Given a dimer configuration
D
, the set of monomers
\mathcal{M}(D)
is the collection of dimer-free vertices.
The Hamiltonian of the model with an imitation coefficient
J\geq 0
and an external field
h\in\mathbb{R}
is given by

-H(D)=n\left(Jm(D)^{2}+\left(\frac{\log n}{2}+h-J\right)m(D)\right)

for all
D\in\mathcal{D}
, where

m(D)=|\mathcal{M}(D)|/n.

The associated Gibbs measure is defined as

\mathbb{P}(D)=\frac{e^{-H(D)}}{\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}}e^{-H(D)}}.

Let

\displaystyle\tilde{p}(x)=-Jx^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(1-g(\tau(x))+\log(1-g(\tau(x)))),

(4.20)

where

g(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{e^{4x}+4e^{2x}}-e^{2x}\right),\quad\tau(x)=(2x-1)J+h.

Alberici et al.
alberici2014mean
showed that there exists a function

\gamma:\left(J_{c},\infty\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\gamma\left(J_{c}\right)=h_{c}
,
where
J_{c}=1/(4(3-2\sqrt{2}))
and
h_{c}=(\log(12-8\sqrt{2})-1)/4
, such that if
(J,h)\notin\Gamma
, where
\Gamma=\left\{(J,\gamma(J)):J>J_{c}\right\}
, then the function

\tilde{p}(x)
has a unique maximizer
m_{0}
that satisfies
m_{0}=g\left(\tau\left(m_{0}\right)\right)
. Moreover, if
(J,h)\notin\Gamma\cup\left\{\left(J_{c},h_{c}\right)\right\}
, then
{\tilde{p}}^{\prime\prime}\left(m_{0}\right)<0
, and if
(J,h)=\left(J_{c},h_{c}\right)
, then
m_{0}=m_{c}=2-\sqrt{2}
,
{\tilde{p}}^{\prime}\left(m_{c}\right)={\tilde{p}}^{\prime\prime}\left(m_{c}\right)={\tilde{p}}^{(3)}\left(m_{c}\right)=0,{\tilde{p}}^{(4)}\left(m_{c}\right)<0
.
Alberici et al.
alberici2014mean
proved that if
(J,h)\notin\Gamma\cup\left\{\left(J_{c},h_{c}\right)\right\}
,
then
m(D)
concentrates at
m_{0}
and has a normal limit.
However, if
(J,h)=\left(J_{c},h_{c}\right)
, then

m(D)
still concentrates at
m_{0}
and but has a non-normal limit. Specifically, they proved that in this latter case,

n^{1/4}\left(m(D)-m_{0}\right)
converges to
Y
in distribution, where
Y
is a random variable with the density function proportional to

\exp({\tilde{p}}^{(4)}\left(m_{c}\right)x^{4}/24)
.

Chen
chen2016limit
used Stein’s method to
obtain the rate of convergence for the above limit theorems of
Alberici et al.
alberici2014mean
. To prove his result, Chen
chen2016limit

reformulated the monomer-dimer model as a Curie–Weiss model with additional weights.
Let
n\geq 1
and
\Sigma=\{0,1\}^{n}
. For each
\sigma=\left(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\right)\in\Sigma
, define a Hamiltonian

-H(\sigma)=n\left(Jm(\sigma)^{2}+\left(\frac{\log n}{2}+h-J\right)m(\sigma)\right),

where

m(\sigma)=\frac{\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{n}}{n}

is the magnetization of the configuration
\sigma
. Let
\mathcal{A}(\sigma)
denote the set of all sites
i\in V
such that
\sigma_{i}=1
, and let
D(\sigma)
denote the total number of dimer configurations
D\in\mathcal{D}
with
\mathcal{M}(D)=\mathcal{A}(\sigma)
. By introducing the Gibbs measure

\mathbb{P}(\sigma)=\frac{D(\sigma)\exp(-H(\sigma))}{\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma}D(\tau)\exp(-H(\tau))},

Chen
chen2016limit
showed that

\mathbb{P}\left(m(\sigma)=t/n\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(m(D)=t/n\right)\ \text{ for all }\ t=0,1,\ldots,n.

Therefore, proving the limit theorems for the monomer density in the monomer-dimer model reduces to analyzing the magnetization in the weighted Curie–Weiss model.

Let
\lambda_{c}=-{\tilde{p}}^{(4)}\left(m_{c}\right)>0
and let
Y
be a random variable with probability density function

\displaystyle p(x)=Ke^{-\lambda_{c}x^{4}/24},

(4.21)

where
K
is the normalizing constant. Chen
chen2016limit
proved that if

(J,h)=\left(J_{c},h_{c}\right)
, then one has the following uniform Berry–Esseen bound:

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(n^{1/4}(m(\sigma)-m_{c})\leq z\right)-\mathbb{P}(Y\leq z)\right|\leq\frac{B}{n^{1/4}}.

(4.22)

More recently, Shao et al.
shao2021cramer
established a Cramér type moderate deviation for
m(\sigma)
.
In this subsection, we will apply Theorem
2.3
to extend (
4.22
) to a non-uniform Berry–Esseen bound.

Theorem 4.2

Let
p\geq 3
. Under the above setting, if
(J,h)=\left(J_{c},h_{c}\right)
, then for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\left|\mathbb{P}\left(n^{1/4}(m(\sigma)-m_{c})\leq z\right)-\mathbb{P}(Y\leq z)\right|\leq\dfrac{B^{p}p^{p/2}}{(1+|z|)^{p}n^{1/4}},

(4.23)

where
Y
is a random variable with probability density function as given in (
4.21
).

Proof.

For any
\sigma\in\Sigma,\{u,v\}\in D
and
s,t\in\{0,1\}
, let
\sigma_{u,v}^{s,t}
denote the configuration
\tau\in\Sigma
, such that
\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}
for
i\neq u,v
and
\tau_{u}=s,\tau_{v}=t
. Let (
\sigma_{u}^{\prime},\sigma_{v}^{\prime}
) be independent of (
\sigma_{u},\sigma_{v}
) and follow the conditional distribution

\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma_{u}^{\prime}=s,\sigma_{v}^{\prime}=t\mid\sigma\right)=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma_{uv}^{st}\right)}{\sum_{s,t\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma_{uv}^{st}\right)}.

Let

W=n^{1/4}(m(\sigma)-m_{c})\ \text{ and }\ W^{\prime}=n^{1/4}\left(m(\sigma)-\frac{\sigma_{u}+\sigma_{v}-\sigma_{u}^{\prime}-\sigma_{v}^{\prime}}{n}-m_{c}\right).

From Lemma 1 of Chen
chen2016limit
(see also the proof of Theorem 3.2 of Shao et al.
shao2021cramer
), we have
that
(W,W^{\prime})
is an exchangeable pair satisfying

\mathbb{E}(W^{\prime}|W)=W-\lambda\psi(W)+R,

(4.24)

where

\lambda=\dfrac{2(1-m_{c})\left(m_{c}^{2}+(1-m_{c})e^{2\tau(m_{c})}\right)}{\left((1-m_{c})+e^{2\tau(m_{c})}\right)n^{3/2}},\ \psi(x)=\left(\frac{\lambda_{c}x^{4}}{24}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{\lambda_{c}x^{3}}{6},

and
R
is a random variable with

|R|\leq\frac{B\left(|W|^{4}+1\right)}{n^{7/4}}.

(4.25)

We will prove that

\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\leq B^{p}p^{p/2},

(4.26)

\frac{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}|R|^{2}}}{\lambda}\leq\frac{B}{n^{1/4}},

(4.27)

and

\mathbb{E}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{E}({\Delta}^{2}|W)\right)^{2}\leq\frac{B}{n^{1/2}},

(4.28)

where
\Delta=W-W^{\prime}
. By (
4.24
) and the fact that
|\Delta|\leq 2/n^{3/4}
,
we are now able to apply Theorem
2.3
with
k=2
,
p_{k}(x)=K\exp(-\lambda_{c}x^{4}/24)
and
a=2/n^{3/4}
.
By collecting the bounds in (
4.26
)–(
4.28
), we obtain (
4.23
).

It remains to prove (
4.26
)–(
4.28
).
From (
4.24
) and (
4.25
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{\lambda\lambda_{c}|W|^{3}}{6}&=|\mathbb{E}(W-W^{\prime}|W)+R|\\
&\leq|\mathbb{E}(W-W^{\prime}|W)|+\frac{B\left(|W|^{4}+1\right)}{n^{7/4}}.\end{split}

(4.29)

First, assume that
p\geq 3
is an integer.
Since
0\leq m(\sigma)\leq 1
and
m_{c}=2-\sqrt{2}
,
|W|\leq n^{1/4}
. By using this bound and (
4.29
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}&\leq\frac{6}{\lambda\lambda_{c}}\left(\left|\mathbb{E}(W-W^{\prime})W^{2p-3}\right|+\frac{B\left(\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p+1}+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-3}\right)}{n^{7/4}}\right)\\
&\leq B\left(n^{3/2}\left|\mathbb{E}(W-W^{\prime})W^{2p-3}\right|+n^{-1/4}\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p+1}+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}\right).\end{split}

(4.30)

As in the proof of (
4.13
), we also have

\begin{split}\left|\mathbb{E}(W^{\prime}-W)W^{2p-3}\right|\leq 2(2p-3)n^{-3/2}\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}.\end{split}

(4.31)

For any
\delta>0
, Chen
chen2016limit
showed that there exist some
\eta>0
and a constant
B
such that

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(|W|>\delta n^{1/4})\leq Be^{-n\eta}.\end{split}

(4.32)

Since
|W|\leq n^{1/4}
, it follows from (
4.32
) that

\begin{split}n^{-1/4}\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p+1}&=n^{-1/4}\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p+1}\left(\mathbf{1}(|W|\leq\delta n^{1/4})+\mathbf{1}(|W|>\delta n^{1/4})\right)\\
&\leq\delta\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}+Bn^{p/2}e^{-n\eta}.\end{split}

(4.33)

Combining (
4.30
), (
4.31
) and (
4.33
) yields

(1-\delta B)\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\leq B\left((2p-3)\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}+n^{p/2}e^{-n\eta}+\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}\right).

(4.34)

By choosing
\delta
sufficiently small and noting that
n^{p/2}e^{-n\eta}\leq B^{p}p^{p/2}
,
we obtain from (
4.34
) that

\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\leq B\left(p\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}+B^{p}p^{p/2}\right).

(4.35)

Set
u=\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}
. Then by Jensen’s inequality, we have
\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p-4}\leq u^{(p-2)/p}
.
Therefore, (
4.35
) yields

u\leq B\left(pu^{(p-2)/p}+B^{p}p^{p/2}\right).

(4.36)

If
u^{(p-2)/p}\leq B^{p}p^{p/2}
, then

u\leq B^{p^{2}/(p-2)}p^{p^{2}/(2(p-2))}\leq B^{p+6}p^{(p+6)/2}

thereby implying

\mathbb{E}|W|^{2p}\leq B^{p}p^{p/2}.

(4.37)

If
u^{(p-2)/p}>B^{p}p^{p/2}
, then it follows from (
4.36
) that

u\leq B(p+1)u^{(p-2)/p}
, which establishes (
4.37
) again. Thus, (
4.26
) holds when
p\geq 3
is an integer.
Now, if
p\geq 3
is a real number, then (
4.26
) also follows by applying (
4.37
) with
p
replaced by
\lceil p\rceil
and using Jensen’s inequality.

By applying (
4.26
) with
p=4
, (
4.25
) and Jensen’s inequality, we obtain (
4.27
).
Chen
chen2016limit
(see the proof of Lemma 1 in
chen2016limit
) also proved that

\left|\frac{1}{2\lambda}\mathbb{E}\left(\Delta^{2}\mid W\right)-1\right|\leq\frac{B\left(|W|+1\right)}{n^{1/4}}.

(4.38)

By applying (
4.26
) with
p=3
, (
4.38
) and Jensen’s inequality, we obtain (
4.28
).

The proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

Remark 2
.

In
thanh2025non
, the normal approximation result (Theorem
2.3
for the case
k=1
and
a_{k}=1/2
) was applied to derive non-uniform Berry–Esseen bounds for
N
-vector models (see, e.g.,
kirkpatrick2016asymptotics
;
thanh2019error
) and Jack measures (see, e.g.,
chen2021error
;
fulman2004stein
;
fulman2011zero
).
It can also be used to obtain non-uniform Berry–Esseen bounds for the Curie–Weiss model and the imitative monomer-dimer model away from the critical point. We leave it to the interested reader.

Inflation with Gauss-Bonnet Correction and Higgs Potential
Zahra Ahghari, Mehrdad Farhoudi

Abstract
We investigate the cosmological inflation for the Einstein-Hilbert action plus the Higgs potential function and the Gauss-Bonnet term coupled with the Higgs scalar field through a dilaton-like coupling. Then, using the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric and considering the appropriate slow-roll parameters, we derive the necessary equations of motion. In the proposed model, since the e e -folding integral cannot be easily solved analytically, we first utilize a well-known Taylor expansion. Then, with a certain range of values derived for the model parameters, utilizing a mixture of several diagrams and numerical analysis methods, we obtain results for the tensor-to-scalar ratio and the scalar spectral index that are in good agreement with the latest observational data, particularly from ACT DR6, within the acceptable range of the e e -folding values. Also, in the absence of the Gauss-Bonnet term, we find that the inflationary observables are roughly the same as the predictions of the chaotic inflation model.

Introduction

The cosmic inflation theory has been agreed upon by many
cosmologists as a suitable way to explain fundamental problems of
the standard model of cosmology, such as the flatness problem and
the horizon problem in the early universe. For this purpose, the
inflation generated based on a canonical scalar field with minimal
coupling is one of the simplest methods available, wherein the
scalar field slowly rolls during the inflation. In this type of

measurement of temperature anisotropy in the cosmic microwave
background (CMB) can be used. The latest CMB data obtained from
the Planck satellite and the recent sixth data release (DR6) of

4
Conclusions

We have considered the Gauss-Bonnet term coupled with the Higgs
scalar field added to the Einstein-Hilbert action. Then, we have
derived the equations of motion and, while employing the FLRW
metric, the modified Friedmann equations. Thereafter, we have
approximated these equations during inflation by considering the
appropriate slow-roll parameters. Moreover, the proposed model
includes the Higgs potential and the coupling coefficient as a
dilaton-like coupling.

Under these assumptions, we encountered calculations to obtain the
tensor-to-scalar ratio and the scalar spectral index that are
rather complicated and cannot be easily solved analytically.
Hence, further approximations have been needed in order to derive
the inflationary phenomenology. In this regard, in the first
stage, we did not proceed directly with numerical analysis
methods, and instead we have found that it is productive by first
employing a well-known Taylor expansion, we can then solve the

e
-folding integral analytically. Meanwhile, this procedure has
caused a constraint on the model parameters. However, we still
need to determine the function of
e
-folding through numerical
analysis methods for a certain range of derived values for the
model parameters in order to have acceptable values for it.

In the process of this work, several plots have been drawn and at
the same time numerical analysis methods have been used.
Thereupon, in the specified range of the model parameters, we have
drawn the inverse function of the
e
-folding function numerically
and scrutinized to find the best fitted curve for the inflaton
scalar field in an acceptable range of the
e
-folding number. At
last, while utilizing the best fitted function for the inflaton
scalar field, in order to attain acceptable values for the
tensor-to-scalar ratio and the scalar spectral index, we have
plotted them against each other with the appropriate values
obtained for the model parameters such that the
e
-folding values
fall within the acceptable range of observational data.

Finally, for the proposed model, with a certain range of values
derived for the model parameters, we have obtained results for the
tensor-to-scalar ratio and the scalar spectral index that are in
good agreement with the latest observational data, particularly
from ACT DR6, within the acceptable range of the
e
-folding
values. However, there is not much choice for the values of the
model parameters. Of course, one of the key points of this work is
to achieve results through analytical analysis methods. Also, in
the absence of the Gauss-Bonnet term, we find that the
inflationary observables are roughly the same as the predictions
of the chaotic inflation model. However, in this case, the value
obtained for the tensor-to-scalar ratio is not consistent with the
Planck observable data, while the very acceptable values obtained
for it in the presence of the Gauss-Bonnet term indicate the
significant effect of including the Gauss-Bonnet correction. In
addition, the results of the model show that the speed of
gravitational waves during inflation is greater than one (i.e.,
the speed of light) but very close to it, and its very slight
difference from one is due to the presence of the Gauss-Bonnet
correction.

On the vanishing of coefficients of η 26 \eta^{26}
S. Krishnamoorthy and T. Dalal

Abstract
. J.-P. Serre, in his paper [ 7 ] , established a sufficient condition on n n for the n n -th coefficient of the series η 26 \eta^{26} to vanish. However, the question that whether this is a necessary condition remained unanswered. In this paper, using the theory of Hecke eigenforms explored by Serre, we prove some partial cases for the converse part.

11F11

1.
Introduction

The Dedekind’s eta-function is defined by

\displaystyle\eta(z):=q^{1/24}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{m}),

where
q=e^{2\pi iz}
,
{\mathrm{Im}}(z)>0
. For an integer
r
, the
r
-th power of
\eta
can be expressed as the infinite sum

\displaystyle\eta^{r}(z)=q^{r/24}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{r}(n)q^{n},

where the coefficients
p_{r}(n)
are defined by the identity

\displaystyle\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{m})^{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{r}(n)q^{n}.

There has been much interest in
p_{r}(n)
, in particular in its vanishing. It turns out that, for certain values of the exponent
r
, the set of
n
such that
p_{r}(n)\neq 0
has density zero. In that case, the series
\eta^{r}
is said to be lacunary. This is the case for
r=1,3
as shown by the identities of Euler and Jacobi, respectively (
[
2
]
, Ch: XIX):

\displaystyle\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{(3n^{2}+n)/2}=1-q-q^{2}+q^{5}+q^{7}-q^{12}-q^{15}+q^{22}+q^{26}-\cdots,

\displaystyle\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{m})^{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(2n+1)q^{(n^{2}+n)/2}=1-3q+5q^{3}-7q^{6}+9q^{10}-11q^{15}+13q^{21}-\cdots.

Computer calculations make it probable that there is no other odd values of
r
for which the series
\eta^{r}
is lacunary. On the other hand, for even
r
, Ramanujan
[
5
]
proved that
\eta^{r}
is lacunary for
r=2,4,6,8
. Later, it was proved (
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
) that this is also the case for
r=10,14\text{ and }26
. Finally, Serre
[
7
]
proved that these are the only cases for even
r
such that
\eta^{r}
is lacunary.

For
r=2,4,6,8,10\text{ and }14
, Serre
[
7
]
also established necessary and sufficient conditions on
n
so that
p_{r}(n)=0
. Furthermore, for
r=26
, he proved the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
7
]

If
n
satisfies one of the following conditions, then
p_{26}(n)=0
.

•

There exists a prime number
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
whose highest power dividing
12n+13
is odd; and there exists a prime number
p^{\prime}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
whose highest power dividing
12n+13
is odd.

•

12n+13
is a square; and all of its prime factors are congruent to
11
modulo
12
.

This theorem is also proved by Chan et. al.
[
1
]
by establishing an explicit formula for
p_{26}(n)
. However, whether the converse is true or not remained unanswered. Let us denote the highest power of a prime number
p
dividing an integer
n
as
ord_{p}(n)
.
Note that the following statement is the converse of Theorem
1.1
.

Statement 1.2
.

If
p_{26}(n)=0
, then
n
must satisfy one of the following conditions:

I.

There exists a prime number
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
such that
ord_{p}(12n+13)
is odd; and there exists a prime number
p^{\prime}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
such that
ord_{p^{\prime}}(12n+13)
is odd.

II.

12n+13
is a square; and all of its prime factors are congruent to
11
modulo
12
.

Observe that Statement
1.2
is equivalent to check the following statement:

Statement 1.3
.

If
n
satisfies one of the following conditions, then
p_{26}(n)\neq 0
:

N1.

For every prime
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
,
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(12n+13)
is even and there exists a prime
p^{\prime}\not\equiv 11\pmod{12}
such that
\mathrm{ord}_{p^{\prime}}(12n+13)>0.

N2.

For every prime
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
,
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(12n+13)
is even and there exists a prime
p^{\prime}\not\equiv 11\pmod{12}
such that
\mathrm{ord}_{p^{\prime}}(12n+13)>0.

In this article, we first verify some partial cases for N1 and N2. Finally as applications of those results, we prove the following necessary and sufficient conditions.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{p}(12n+1)\not\equiv 4\pmod{5}
for every prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
. Then

p_{26}(25n+1)=0
if and only if there exists a prime number
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{p}(12n+1)
is odd; and there exists a prime number
p^{\prime}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{p^{\prime}}(12n+1)
is odd.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{p}(12n+1)\not\equiv 6\pmod{7}
for every prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
. Then

p_{26}(49n+3)=0
if and only if there exists a prime number
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{p}(12n+1)
is odd; and there exists a prime number
p^{\prime}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{p^{\prime}}(12n+1)
is odd.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us consider an imaginary quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
with discriminant
d
. We denote the ring of integers of
K
by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Suppose,
c
is a Hecke character on
K
of exponent
k-1
and conductor
\mathfrak{f}_{c}
, where
\mathfrak{f}_{c}
is a nonzero ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Now we define the series

\displaystyle\phi_{K,c}(z):=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}c(\mathfrak{a})q^{N(\mathfrak{a})}\quad(q:=e^{2\pi iz},{\mathrm{Im}}(z)>0),

where the sum is taken over all ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
which are coprime to
\mathfrak{f}_{c}
. We have that
\phi_{K,c}(z)
is a normalized Hecke eigenform of weight
k
, level
|d|\cdot N(\mathfrak{f}_{c})
and character
\epsilon_{K}\omega_{c}
, where
\epsilon_{K}
and
\omega_{c}
are Dirichlet characters defined as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{K}(p)=\left(\frac{d}{p}\right)\text{ for primes }p\text{ such that }p\nmid 2d

and

\displaystyle\omega_{c}(n)=\frac{c(n\mathcal{O}_{K})}{n^{k-1}}\text{ for integers }n\text{ such that the ideal }n\mathcal{O}_{K}\text{ is coprime to }\mathfrak{f}_{c}.

For more details please refer to
[
7
]
.

Firstly, consider the number field
K_{1}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
of discriminant
-3
and the ideal
\mathfrak{f}_{1}=4\sqrt{-3}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{K_{1}}
of norm

. We now define Hecke characters

\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K_{1}}

\mathfrak{f}_{1}
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{K_{1}}
is a principal ideal domain, we may choose a generator
a=x+y\sqrt{-3}
of
\mathfrak{a}
with
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x+y\equiv 1\pmod{2}
and
x\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. We define

\displaystyle c_{1\pm}(\mathfrak{a})=(-1)^{(x\mp y-1)/2}a^{12}.

It can be easily verified that
c_{1\pm}
thus defined are Hecke characters on
K_{1}
of exponent
12
and conductor
\mathfrak{f}_{1}
, and that
\epsilon_{K_{1}}\omega_{c_{1+}}=\left(\frac{-1}{\bullet}\right)=\epsilon_{K_{1}}\omega_{c_{1-}}
.

Next, consider the number field
K_{2}=\mathbb{Q}(i)
of discriminant
-4
and the ideal
\mathfrak{f}_{2}=6\cdot\mathcal{O}_{K_{2}}
of norm
36
. We now define Hecke characters
c_{2\pm}
on
K_{2}
. Let
\mathfrak{b}
be an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K_{2}}
coprime to
\mathfrak{f}_{2}
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{K_{2}}
is a principal ideal domain, we may choose a generator
b
of
\mathfrak{b}
and determine
m\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle b\equiv i^{m}\pmod{2\cdot\mathcal{O}_{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad b\equiv(1-i)^{n}\pmod{3\cdot\mathcal{O}_{2}}.

We define

\displaystyle c_{2\pm}(\mathfrak{b})=(-1)^{3m}(\pm i)^{3n}b^{12}.

It can be easily verified that
c_{2\pm}
thus defined are Hecke characters on
K_{2}
of exponent
12
and conductor
\mathfrak{f}_{2}
, and that
\epsilon_{K_{2}}\omega_{c_{2+}}=\left(\frac{-1}{\bullet}\right)=\epsilon_{K_{2}}\omega_{c_{2-}}
.

By
[
7
]
, we have

\displaystyle\eta^{26}(12z)=\frac{1}{32617728}\left(\phi_{K_{1},c_{1+}}(z)+\phi_{K_{1},c_{1-}}(z)-\phi_{K_{2},c_{2+}}(z)-\phi_{K_{2},c_{2-}}(z)\right),

and so
\eta^{26}(12z)
is a linear combination of forms of
CM
-type of weight
13
, level
144
and character
\left(\frac{-1}{\bullet}\right)
. We write

\displaystyle\phi_{K_{1},c_{1\pm}}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t_{1\pm}(n)q^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{K_{2},c_{2\pm}}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t_{2\pm}(n)q^{n}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{26}(n)q^{12n+13}=\frac{1}{32617728}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(t_{1+}(n)+t_{1-}(n)-t_{2+}(n)-t_{2-}(n)\big)q^{n}\right).

Comparing the coefficients of the terms involving
q^{12n+13}
, we get

(1)

\displaystyle p_{26}(n)=\frac{1}{32617728}\big(t_{1+}(12n+13)+t_{1-}(12n+13)-t_{2+}(12n+13)-t_{2-}(12n+13)\big)

for every nonnegative integer
n
.

Let the prime decomposition of
12n+13
be given by

12n+13=\prod_{prime\ p}p^{\alpha_{p}},\ \mathrm{where}\ \alpha_{p}:=ord_{p}(12n+13).

In view of the properties of Hecke eigenforms, we have

(2)

\displaystyle t_{1\pm}(12n+13)=\prod_{p}t_{1\pm}(p^{\alpha_{p}})\quad\text{and}\quad t_{2\pm}(12n+13)=\prod_{p}t_{2\pm}(p^{\alpha_{p}}),

Let us have a look at
t_{1+}(p^{\alpha})
,
t_{1-}(p^{\alpha})
,
t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})
,
t_{2-}(p^{\alpha})
for a prime
p\geq 5
. Let
t
denote any one among
t_{1\pm}
and
t_{2\pm}
.
By the properties of Hecke eigenforms,
if
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then

(3)

t(p^{r})=t(p)t(p^{r-1})-p^{12}t(p^{r-2})

for any integer
r\geq 2
. Equivalently
for any
\beta\geq 0
,

(4)

\displaystyle t(p^{2\beta})

\displaystyle=t(p)^{2\beta}-\binom{2\beta-1}{1}p^{12}t(p)^{2\beta-2}+\dots+(-1)^{\beta-1}\binom{\beta+1}{2}p^{12\beta-12}t(p)^{2}+(-1)^{\beta}p^{12\beta},

(5)

\displaystyle t(p^{2\beta+1})

\displaystyle=t(p)^{2\beta+1}-\binom{2\beta}{1}p^{12}t(p)^{2\beta-1}+\dots+(-1)^{\beta-1}\binom{\beta+2}{3}p^{12\beta-12}t(p)^{3}+(-1)^{\beta}\binom{\beta+1}{1}p^{12\beta}t(p).

On the other hand if
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
then

(6)

t(p^{r})=t(p)t(p^{r-1})+p^{12}t(p^{r-2})

for any integer
r\geq 2
. Equivalently
for any
\beta\geq 0
,

(7)

\displaystyle t(p^{2\beta})

\displaystyle=t(p)^{2\beta}+\binom{2\beta-1}{1}p^{12}t(p)^{2\beta-2}+\dots+\binom{\beta+1}{2}p^{12\beta-12}t(p)^{2}+p^{12\beta},

(8)

\displaystyle t(p^{2\beta+1})

\displaystyle=t(p)^{2\beta+1}+\binom{2\beta}{1}p^{12}t(p)^{2\beta-1}+\dots+\binom{\beta+2}{3}p^{12\beta-12}t(p)^{3}+\binom{\beta+1}{1}p^{12\beta}t(p).

Firstly, let
p\equiv 11\pmod{12}
. Since

\displaystyle t_{1\pm}(p^{\alpha})=t_{2\pm}(p^{\alpha})=\begin{cases}p^{6\alpha}&\text{ if $\alpha$ is even,}\\

Secondly, let
p\equiv 7\pmod{12}
. Since
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, therefore
p
is inert in

. Consequently,
t_{2\pm}(p)=0
. By (

) and (
8
), we have

(10)

\displaystyle t_{2\pm}(p^{\alpha})=\begin{cases}p^{6\alpha}&\text{ if $\alpha$ is even,}\\
0&\text{ if $\alpha$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Since
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, therefore we can write
p=z^{2}+3w^{2}=(z+w\sqrt{-3})(z-w\sqrt{-3})
for some

(11)

\displaystyle t_{1+}(p)=-(z+w\sqrt{-3})^{12}+(z-w\sqrt{-3})^{12}=-t_{1-}(p).

By (

.

Thirdly, let
p\equiv 5\pmod{12}
. Since
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, therefore
p
is inert in

. Consequently,
t_{1\pm}(p)=0
. By (

) and (

(12)

\displaystyle t_{1\pm}(p^{\alpha})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\alpha/2}p^{6\alpha}&\text{ if $\alpha$ is even,}\\
0&\text{ if $\alpha$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Since
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, therefore we can write
p=x^{2}+y^{2}=(x+iy)(x-iy)
for some
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
with
x
odd and
y

and

(13)

\displaystyle t_{2+}(p)=-i(x+iy)^{12}+i(x-iy)^{12}=-t_{2-}(p).

By (

\alpha

t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})=-t_{2-}(p^{\alpha})
. By (

), for any even
\alpha

t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})=t_{2-}(p^{\alpha})
.

Finally, let
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
. Since
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}

p=z^{2}+3w^{2}=(z+w\sqrt{-3})(z-w\sqrt{-3})

z
odd and

(14)

\displaystyle t_{1\pm}(p)=\begin{cases}(z+w\sqrt{-3})^{12}+(z-w\sqrt{-3})^{12}&\text{if $z+w\equiv 1\equiv z-w\pmod{4}$,}\\

Since
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}

\displaystyle t_{2\pm}(p)=\begin{cases}(x+iy)^{12}+(x-iy)^{12}&\text{if }x\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}\text{ , }y\equiv 0\pmod{3},\\

3.
Some Important Lemmas

In this section, we study some properties of
t_{1\pm}
and
t_{2\pm}
.

3.1.
Divisibility properties of
t_{1\pm}
and
t_{2\pm}

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
be a prime. If
t_{2+}(p)=2\pmod{5}
, then

t_{2+}(p^{5l+n})\equiv t_{2+}(p^{5(l-1)+n})\pmod{5}\ \mathrm{for}\ l\in\mathbb{N}\ \mathrm{and}\ n\geq 0.

Proof.

Recall that we have the Hecke relation (cf. (
3
))

(16)

t_{2+}(p^{r})\equiv 2t_{2+}(p^{r-1})-t_{2+}(p^{r-2})\pmod{5}\ \mathrm{for}\ r\geq 2.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq 0
.
By repeated application of (
16
), we get

\displaystyle t_{2+}(p^{5l+n})

\displaystyle\equiv 2t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-1})-t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-2})\pmod{5}

\displaystyle\equiv 2(2t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-2})-t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-3}))-t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-2})\pmod{5}

\displaystyle\equiv 3t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-2})-2t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-3})\pmod{5}

\displaystyle\equiv 3(2t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-3})-t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-4}))-2t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-3})\pmod{5}

\displaystyle\equiv 4t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-3})-3t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-4})\pmod{5}

\displaystyle\equiv 4(2t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-4})-t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-5}))-3t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-4})\pmod{5}

\displaystyle\equiv 5t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-4})-4t_{2+}(p^{5l+n-5})\pmod{5}

\displaystyle\equiv t_{2+}(p^{5(l-1)+n})\pmod{5}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Observe that a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
shows that for any prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
, if
t_{2+}(p)=3\pmod{5}
, then

t_{2+}(p^{5l+n})\equiv-t_{2+}(p^{5(l-1)+n})\pmod{5}\ \mathrm{for}\ l\in\mathbb{N}\ \mathrm{and}\ n\geq 0.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
be a prime. If
t_{1+}(p)=2\pmod{7}
, then

t_{1+}(p^{7l+n})\equiv t_{1+}(p^{7(l-1)+n})\pmod{7}\ \mathrm{for}\ l\in\mathbb{N}\ \mathrm{and}\ n\geq 0.

Proof.

Recall that we have the Hecke relation (cf. (
3
))

(17)

t_{1+}(p^{r})\equiv 2t_{1+}(p^{r-1})-t_{1+}(p^{r-2})\pmod{7}\ \mathrm{for}\ r\geq 2.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq 0
.
By repeated application of (
17
), we get

\displaystyle t_{1+}(p^{7l+n})

\displaystyle\equiv 2t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-1})-t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-2})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv 2(2t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-2})-t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-3}))-t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-2})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv 3t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-2})-2t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-3})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv 3(2t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-3})-t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-4}))-2t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-3})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv 4t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-3})-3t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-4})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv 4(2t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-4})-t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-5}))-3t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-4})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv 5t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-4})-4t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-5})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv 5(2t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-5})-t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-6}))-4t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-5})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv 6t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-5})-5t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-6})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv 6(2t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-6})-t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-7}))-5t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-6})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv 7t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-6})-6t_{1+}(p^{7l+n-7})\pmod{7}

\displaystyle\equiv t_{1+}(p^{7(l-1)+n})\pmod{7}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

For any prime
p\equiv 5\pmod{12}
we have

(i)

5\nmid t_{2+}(5)
,
5|t_{2+}(p)
for
p\neq 5
. Hence
5\nmid t_{2+}(p^{2\alpha})
for any prime
p\equiv 5\pmod{12}
.

(ii)

3.2.
Non-vanishing of
t_{1+}(p^{\alpha})-t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})
, when
\alpha>1
is even

Lemma 3.8
.

For a prime number
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
, we have
ord_{2}(t_{1+}(p)-t_{2+}(p))<\infty
and so
t_{1+}(p)-t_{2+}(p)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Since
p\equiv 1\pmod{12}
, therefore we can write

\displaystyle p=x^{2}+y^{2},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{Z},\quad x\text{ is odd,}

\displaystyle p=z^{2}+3w^{2},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{Z},\quad z\text{ is odd.}

Thus,

\displaystyle t_{1+}(p)=(+/-)\left((x+iy)^{12}+(x-iy)^{12}\right),\quad t_{2+}(p)=(+/-)\left((z+w\sqrt{-3})^{12}+(z-w\sqrt{-3})^{12}\right).

We have already observed that the sign (
+
or
-
) can be safely ignored as the same sign appears on both
t_{1+}(p)
and
t_{2+}(p)
.

Now,

(41)

\displaystyle t_{1+}(p)

\displaystyle=2p^{6}-144p^{5}x^{2}+1680p^{4}x^{4}-7168p^{3}x^{6}+13824p^{2}x^{8}-12288px^{10}+4096x^{12},

(42)

\displaystyle t_{2+}(p)

\displaystyle=2p^{6}-144p^{5}z^{2}+1680p^{4}z^{4}-7168p^{3}z^{6}+13824p^{2}z^{8}-12288pz^{10}+4096z^{12}.

So,

\displaystyle t_{1+}(p)-t_{2+}(p)=

\displaystyle-144p^{5}(x^{2}-z^{2})+1680p^{4}(x^{4}-z^{4})-7168p^{3}(x^{6}-z^{6})+13824p^{2}(x^{8}-z^{8})

\displaystyle-12288p(x^{10}-z^{10})+4096(x^{12}-z^{12})

Clearly, on the RHS, the highest power of
2
dividing the first term is strictly less than the highest power of
2
dividing each of the other terms. Hence,
ord_{2}\left(t_{1+}(p)-t_{2+}(p)\right)<\infty
and so
t_{1+}(p)-t_{2+}(p)\neq 0
.

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

For a prime

and
\alpha>1

ord_{2}\left(t_{1+}(p^{\alpha})-t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})\right)<\infty
and so
t_{1+}(p^{\alpha})-t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})\neq 0

Proof.

We write

, where
a,b
are odd integers and

. First we assume,

for some
\beta\geq 1
. By (
5
), we have

(43)

\displaystyle t_{1+}(p^{2\beta+1})-t_{2+}(p^{2\beta+1})

\displaystyle=\left(t_{1+}(p)^{2\beta+1}-t_{2+}(p)^{2\beta+1}\right)-\binom{2\beta}{1}p^{12}\left(t_{1+}(p)^{2\beta-1}-t_{2+}(p)^{2\beta-1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\dots+(-1)^{\beta}\binom{\beta+1}{1}p^{12\beta}\left(t_{1+}(p)-t_{2+}(p)\right)

\displaystyle=\binom{\beta+(\beta+1)}{2(\beta+1)-1}2^{2(\beta+1)-1}(a^{2\beta+1}-b^{2\beta+1})-\binom{\beta+\beta}{2\beta-1}2^{2\beta-1}p^{12}(a^{2\beta-1}-b^{2\beta-1})

\displaystyle\quad+\dots+(-1)^{\beta}(\beta+1)2p^{12\beta}(a-b).

Let
ord_{2}(2(\beta+1))=\gamma
. For any
\kappa=2,3,\dots,\beta+1
, we have

\displaystyle\binom{\beta+\kappa}{2\kappa-1}2^{2\kappa-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\beta+\kappa)\cdots(\beta+1)\beta(\beta-1)\cdots(\beta-(\kappa-2))}{(2\kappa-1)!}\cdot 2^{2\kappa-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\beta+\kappa)\cdots(\beta+1)\beta(\beta-1)\cdots(\beta-\kappa+2)}{u}\cdot 2^{2\kappa-1-s},

where
u
is odd and
s=(\kappa-1)+\left\lfloor\frac{\kappa-1}{2}\right\rfloor+\dots+\left\lfloor\frac{\kappa-1}{2^{n}}\right\rfloor
,
n
being the largest non-negative integer such that
2^{n}\leq\kappa-1
. Now,

\displaystyle 2\kappa-1-s

\displaystyle=2\kappa-1-\left((\kappa-1)+\left\lfloor\frac{\kappa-1}{2}\right\rfloor+\dots+\left\lfloor\frac{\kappa-1}{2^{n}}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\kappa-1-\left((\kappa-1)+{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}+\dots+{\frac{\kappa-1}{2^{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\kappa-1}{2^{n}}

\displaystyle\geq 2.

So, we have

\displaystyle\binom{\beta+\kappa}{2\kappa-1}2^{2\kappa-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\beta+\kappa)\cdots(2(\beta+1))\beta(\beta-1)\cdots(\beta-\kappa+2)}{u}\cdot 2^{2\kappa-2-s}

\displaystyle=2^{\gamma+1}M.

Now from (
43
),
ord_{2}\left(t_{1+}(p^{\alpha})-t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})\right)<\infty
and so
t_{1+}(p^{\alpha})-t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})\neq 0
.

Next, we assume,
\alpha=2\beta
for some
\beta\geq 1
. By (
4
), we have

(44)

\displaystyle t_{1+}(p^{2\beta})-t_{2+}(p^{2\beta})

\displaystyle=\left(t_{1+}(p)^{2\beta}-t_{2+}(p)^{2\beta}\right)-\binom{2\beta-1}{1}p^{12}\left(t_{1+}(p)^{2\beta-2}-t_{2+}(p)^{2\beta-2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\dots+(-1)^{\beta-1}\binom{\beta+1}{2}p^{12\beta-12}\left(t_{1+}(p)^{2}-t_{2+}(p)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\binom{\beta+\beta}{2\beta}2^{2\beta}(a^{2\beta}-b^{2\beta})-\binom{\beta+(\beta-1)}{2(\beta-1)}2^{2\beta-2}(a^{2\beta-2}-b^{2\beta-2})

\displaystyle\quad+\dots+(-1)^{\beta-1}\binom{\beta+1}{2}2^{2}p^{12\beta-12}(a^{2}-b^{2}).

If
\beta=1
, then

\displaystyle t_{1+}(p^{2})-t_{2+}(p^{2})=4(a^{2}-b^{2})\neq 0.

Moreover,
ord_{2}\left(t_{1+}(p^{2})-t_{2+}(p^{2})\right)<\infty
. We assume,
\beta\geq 2
. Note that
\binom{\beta+1}{2}2^{2}=2\beta(\beta+1)
. Let
ord_{2}(2\beta(\beta+1))=\delta
. For any
\kappa=2,3,\dots,\beta
, we have

\displaystyle\binom{\beta+\kappa}{2\kappa}2^{2\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\beta+\kappa)\cdots(\beta+1)\beta(\beta-1)\cdots(\beta-\kappa+1)}{(2\kappa)!}\cdot 2^{2\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\beta+\kappa)\cdots(\beta+1)\beta(\beta-1)\cdots(\beta-\kappa+1)}{v}\cdot 2^{2\kappa-s^{\prime}},

where
v
is odd and
s^{\prime}=\kappa+\left\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\right\rfloor+\dots+\left\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2^{n}}\right\rfloor
,
n
being the largest non-negative integer such that
2^{n}\leq\kappa
. Now,

\displaystyle 2\kappa-s^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\kappa-\left(\kappa+\left\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2}\right\rfloor+\dots+\left\lfloor\frac{\kappa}{2^{n}}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\kappa-\left(\kappa+\frac{\kappa}{2}+\dots+\frac{\kappa}{2^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{2^{n}}

\displaystyle\geq 1.

So, we have

\displaystyle\binom{\beta+\kappa}{2\kappa}2^{2\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\beta+\kappa)\cdots(2(\beta+1)\beta)(\beta-1)\cdots(\beta-\kappa+1)}{v}\cdot 2^{2\kappa-s^{\prime}-1}

\displaystyle=2^{\delta+(\kappa-1)}N.

But
\kappa-1\geq 1
. Now from (
44
),
ord_{2}\left(t_{1+}(p^{\alpha})-t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})\right)<\infty
and so
t_{1+}(p^{\alpha})-t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})\neq 0
.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

For a prime
p\equiv 5\pmod{12}
and
\alpha>1
even, we have
ord_{2}\left(t_{1+}(p^{\alpha})-t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})\right)<\infty
and so
t_{1+}(p^{\alpha})-t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=2\beta
. In view of (
13
), we can write
t_{2+}(p)^{2}=2c
for some integer

4

12
), we have

\displaystyle t_{1+}(p^{2\beta})-

\displaystyle t_{2+}(p^{2\beta})

\displaystyle=-\left(t_{2+}(p)^{2\beta}-\binom{2\beta-1}{1}p^{12}t_{2+}(p)^{2\beta-2}+\dots+(-1)^{\beta-1}\binom{\beta+1}{2}p^{12\beta-12}t_{2+}(p)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\binom{\beta+\beta}{2\beta}2^{2\beta}c^{2\beta}-\binom{\beta+(\beta-1)}{2(\beta-1)}2^{2\beta-2}c^{2\beta-2}+\dots+(-1)^{\beta-1}\binom{\beta+1}{2}2^{2}p^{12\beta-12}c^{2}\right).

Now we can finish the proof by proceeding with the same argument as in the case of
\alpha
being even in the proof of Lemma
3.9
.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

For a prime
p\equiv 7\pmod{12}

∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
12n+13=p^{\alpha}
, where

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let for every prime number
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
,
{\mathrm{ord}}_{p}(12n+13)
is even; and there exists a prime number
p^{\prime}\equiv 5\pmod{12}
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{p^{\prime}}(12n+13)
is odd. Then
p_{26}(n)\neq 0
.

Proof.

By (
2
) and (
12
), it is clear that
t_{1\pm}(12n+13)=0
. Now, by (
2
), (
9
), (
10
) and (
15
), we have

\displaystyle t_{2+}(12n+13)+t_{2-}(12n+13)=\prod_{p\not\equiv 5(12)}t_{2+}(p^{\alpha_{p}})\left(\prod_{p\equiv 5(12)}t_{2+}(p^{\alpha_{p}})+\prod_{p\equiv 5(12)}t_{2-}(p^{\alpha_{p}})\right).

Also note that if
p\equiv 5\pmod{12}
then
t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})=t_{2-}(p^{\alpha})
for any even
\alpha
and
t_{2+}(p^{\alpha})=-t_{2-}(p^{\alpha})
for any odd
\alpha

\displaystyle\prod_{p\equiv 5(12)}t_{2+}(p^{\alpha_{p}})+\prod_{p\equiv 5(12)}t_{2-}(p^{\alpha_{p}})

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 5(12)\\
\alpha_{p}\text{ even}\end{subarray}}t_{2+}(p^{\alpha_{p}})\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 5(12)\\
\alpha_{p}\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}t_{2+}(p^{\alpha_{p}})+\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 5(12)\\
\alpha_{p}\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}t_{2-}(p^{\alpha_{p}})\right)

\displaystyle=(1+(-1)^{\mu})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 5(12)\\
\alpha_{p}\text{ even}\end{subarray}}t_{2+}(p^{\alpha_{p}})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 5(12)\\
\alpha_{p}\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}t_{2+}(p^{\alpha_{p}}),

\alpha_{p}
for

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\displaystyle 12n+13=\prod_{p\equiv 1\pmod{12}}p^{\alpha_{p}}\prod_{p\equiv 5\pmod{12}}p^{2\beta_{p}}\prod_{p\equiv 7\pmod{12}}p^{2\gamma_{p}}\prod_{p\equiv 11\pmod{12}}p^{2\delta_{p}}

.

By the assumption on

t_{1+}(12n+13)=t_{1-}(12n+13)

t_{2+}(12n+13)=t_{2-}(12n+13)
.

3.5
, (

. Since

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\displaystyle 12n+13=\prod_{p\equiv 1\pmod{12}}p^{\alpha_{p}}\prod_{p\equiv 5\pmod{12}}p^{2\beta_{p}}\prod_{p\equiv 7\pmod{12}}p^{2\gamma_{p}}\prod_{p\equiv 11\pmod{12}}p^{2\delta_{p}}

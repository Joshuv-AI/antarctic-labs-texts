Reply to the Letter Regarding Atrial Tachycardia Utilizing Bachmann's Bundle as an Epicardial Bypass: A Case Suggestive of a Dual‐Loop Mechanism
Authors: Ryutaro Sakurai, Yosuke Nakatani, Kensuke Fujiwara, Takashi Miyamoto
Published: 2026

We thank the authors for their thoughtful comments. As they point out, direct epicardial mapping would provide the most definitive means of identifying a specific epicardial structure, and the lack of direct mapping of Bachmann's bundle (BB) is an important limitation of our case. However, our interpretation that BB served as an epicardial bypass was based not solely on the presence of breakthrough activation on the anterior wall, but on the combination of multiple electrophysiological findings and their anatomical distribution.

During the initial roof‐dependent AT, endocardial activation was interrupted by an extensive low‐voltage area (LVA) on the anterior wall, followed by widespread breakthrough activation anterior to the LVA, where fragmented potentials were recorded. Previous high‐resolution mapping studies have demonstrated that multiple breakthrough activation is characteristic of a pseudo‐focal activation pattern rather than a true focal origin (Takigawa et al. 2021; Nakatani et al. 2021). We agree that neither widespread breakthrough activation nor fragmented potentials alone can identify a specific epicardial structure. However, BB courses broadly along the anterosuperior left atrium and has extensive myocardial connections to the anterior left atrial wall, whereas the septopulmonary bundle is predominantly distributed over the posterior left atrium (Nakatani et al. 2021; Nayak et al. 2020). Therefore, the anatomical distribution of the widespread anterior breakthrough across the anterior LVA observed in this case was considered more compatible with BB involvement than with the septopulmonary bundle.

Further mechanistic evidence was obtained during the localized‐reentrant AT, which demonstrated counterclockwise reentrant activation on the anterior wall. The first linear ablation connecting the lateral portion of the LVA to the left pulmonary vein isolation line did not affect the AT. Subsequently, fragmented potentials were identified in the lateral portion of the LVA, and ablation targeting these potentials terminated the AT within 6 s. Thus, in addition to the circuit geometry inferred from the endocardial activation map, the functional response to ablation supported the presence of a reentrant circuit involving an epicardial bypass.

The proposed dual‐loop mechanism was also not based solely on the geometry of the activation map. During the initial roof‐dependent AT, a counterclockwise localized‐reentry‐like activation pattern, similar to that observed during the subsequent localized‐reentrant AT, was already present on the anterior wall. Furthermore, during roof ablation, the initial AT transitioned to the localized‐reentrant AT without prolongation of the tachycardia cycle length. These observations raised the possibility that a roof‐dependent loop and an anterior localized‐reentrant loop coexisted during the initial AT, with the anterior loop persisting after interruption of the roof‐dependent loop. Nevertheless, because entrainment pacing was not performed at multiple sites on the anterior wall during the initial AT, sequential transformation between two distinct ATs cannot be completely excluded. Accordingly, as stated in our original report, the dual‐loop mechanism remains hypothetical.

We agree that direct epicardial mapping and additional entrainment findings, when available, would strengthen the identification of both the epicardial structure and the reentrant circuit. However, given the invasiveness of epicardial access, performing such an approach solely to establish the anatomical identity of an epicardial bypass may not always be clinically justified. In the present case, we believe that the combination of the activation pattern, its anatomical distribution, and the response to ablation collectively supported BB‐mediated epicardial bypass as the most plausible mechanism.

Computational insights into transition metal-based BaCoX3 (X = Cl, Br, I) halide perovskites for spintronics, photovoltaics, and renewable energy devices
Authors: Arafat Rahman, Alamgir Kabir, Tareq Mahmud
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAb-initio simulations using density functional theory (DFT) were employed to investigate the structural, mechanical, electronic, magnetic, optical, and thermoelectric properties of halide perovskites (X = Cl, Br, I). Structural optimization and mechanical stability assessments confirm the reliability of these perovskites in a hexagonal Pmc symmetry. The stability of the ferromagnetic phase was validated through total crystal energy minimization via Murnaghan’s equation of state. Electronic band structures and density of states, derived from the generalized gradient approximation (GGA), reveal a semiconducting ferromagnetic nature in the spin up channel, spotlighting their potential in semiconductor spintronic applications. Phonon dispersion analysis of and revealed positive phonon modes throughout the entire Brillouin zone, confirming their dynamical stability. In contrast, demonstrated dynamical instability. The elastic constants confirm the mechanical stability and ductile nature of the perovskites. Optical and dielectric properties of these perovskites show significant UV absorption and photoconductivity, making them highly suitable for optoelectronic and solar cell applications. Finally, transport properties, such as the Seebeck coefficient, electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, power factor, and figure of merit (ZT) unveil their exceptional thermoelectric performance. Combining half-metallic ferromagnetic traits with superior thermoelectric and optoelectronic performance positions compounds as exceptional candidates for applications in spintronics, optoelectronics, and thermoelectrics. The concept of half-metallic ferromagnets was first introduced in 1983 by R.A De Groot et al.1. Following this significant discovery, half-metallic ferromagnets rapidly became a focal point of theoretical and experimental research due to their potential applications in spintronics, magnetic sensors, magnetic random access memory (MRAM) and quantum computing.2–6 A half-metallic material displays metallic behavior in one spin channel and exhibits insulating or semiconducting characteristics in the other. This unique characteristic arises due to the presence of a band gap in proximity to the Fermi level, resulting in a complete spin polarization at Fermi level.7,8 The magnitude of spin polarization (SP) evaluated at the Fermi level is defined by, SP 9. Unlike conventional currents, which lose energy as heat, spin-polarized currents do not dissipate energy as heat, making them highly suitable for electronic devices that offer reduced power consumption, faster data processing speeds and cost-effectiveness10.

Diamagnetic and paramagnetic materials possess symmetric densities of states (DOS) for spin-up and spin-down channels, hence they do not show spin polarization. In contrast, ferromagnetic materials exhibit asymmetric DOS; therefore, their spin polarization (SP) is always greater than zero but less than or equal to one. This property enables ferromagnetic materials to produce highly spin-polarized currents. As a result, the antiparallel magnetization alignment in half-metallic ferromagnetic layers results in extremely high resistance, while the parallel configuration yields zero resistance11. Therefore, the introduction of these materials into GMR (Giant magnetoresistance) devices would also lead to a significant increase in the efficiency of spintronic devices.

Perovskite materials, primarily existing as oxides with a general formula , have been extensively studied due to their diverse chemical and physical properties. Here, A and B represent cations, while X denotes an anion, typically comprising oxides or halogens. The element A is commonly an alkali or alkaline metal, whereas B encompasses transition or inner transition elements. These materials have shown significant promise in various applications, including spintronics, optoelectronics, and thermoelectrics. In recent years, numerous half-metallic ferromagnets have been identified, including Heusler alloys (half, full, and quaternary12,13), 14, spinel 15,16, transition metal oxides such as 17, double perovskites like 18 and 19, and halide double perovskites (; M = Mn, Co, and Ni)20. Additionally, many metal halide perovskites have demonstrated half-metallic properties, with high spin-polarization at the Fermi level.21–24 Among these materials, those with transition metals at the B-site stand out for their remarkable half-metallic ferromagnetic behavior. For instance, compounds such as (M = Ir, Rh), (X = Cl, Br, I), (X = F , Cl, Br, I), and exhibit half-metallicity, with transition metals like Cr, Rh, and Ir playing a crucial role in their conducting properties25–28. Remarkably, the A-site atom does not affect the electronic structure of these materials, as the half-metallic character remains unchanged when varying the A-site from K to Rb to Cs.

Furthermore, the discovery of ultralow thermal conductivity in 29 marked a significant breakthrough in the study of halide perovskites, driving extensive research into their transport properties. These materials are well-regarded for their exceptional optoelectronic and thermoelectric attributes, positioning them as highly promising candidates for next-generation energy applications30–34. Their room temperature Seebeck coefficient has been found to surpass that of other solution-processed materials, such as organics, making them even more attractive for thermoelectric devices35. Singh et al. recently demonstrated the high thermoelectric performance of (X = F and Cl) halide perovskites, reporting figure of merit (ZT) values of 2.4 for p-type and 2.1 for p-type at room temperature36. Further studies on other halide perovskites, such as (X = Cl, Br, I)37, (X = Cl, Br)38, and (X = Cl, Br, I)30, have highlighted their potential for thermoelectric efficiency. For instance, and were found to exhibit ZT values of 0.69 and 0.75 at room temperature, with improved performance at higher temperatures up to 1200 K. Investigations into the transport properties of and has demonstrated substantial thermoelectric potential, with these materials achieving a ZT value close to 0.9939. Moreover, halide perovskites featuring Ba at the A-site, such as (X = K, Rb), (X = Cl, Br, I), and (M = Ag, Cu), shows excellent optical properties40–42, while is characterized by its superior optical and thermoelectric properties43. These observations suggest that the inclusion of Ba significantly enhances the multifunctional performance of these perovskites.

Despite the numerous applications of halide perovskites, there is a noticeable gap in the literature on perovskites featuring Ba at the A-site, combined with Co at the B-site, and a halogen anion (X = Cl, Br, I) at the X-site. This gap presents a significant opportunity to explore these materials, as the unique compositional structure of these (X = Cl, Br, I) perovskites could lead to exceptional electronic properties and diverse potential applications. Therefore, this study aims to explore the versatility of in advanced technologies such as spintronics, optoelectronics, and thermoelectrics using Density Functional Theory (DFT).

Computational method

The structural parameters, alongside the electronic, magnetic, elastic, and optical properties of (X= Br, Cl,I) perovskites, are determined by using spin-polarized density functional theory calculations with the Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP)44–47. Projector Augmented Wave (PAW) potentials are utilized to model interactions between electrons and atomic cores which enhances the accuracy of DFT calculations by effectively representing the electron density near the atomic cores through augmented wavefunctions48. PAW potentials enable a more accurate description of electronic properties while also accounting for core electron interactions. The geometrical optimizations are carried out using the Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA) for exchange-correlation interactions into the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional49,50. The rationale for choosing the GGA-PBE functional is its computational efficiency and its much better accuracy than LDA functional over a wide range of systems. Conjugate-gradient algorithm is employed for the relaxation of ions into their lowest energy state configurations. The energies and forces on each ion are converged to within eV/atom and 0.02 eV/Å. The cutoff energy for the plane-wave basis set is extended up to 350 eV which is 1.3 times the maximum energy cutoff specified in the pseudopotentials. VASPKIT’s Monkhorst-Pack51 scheme is used to generate a 9 9 6 grid, considering the symmetry of the hexagonal structure.The lattice parameters were obtained by geometrical optimization using VASP, with the ISIF tag set to 3. This specific setting permits the relaxation of atomic positions, cell shape, and cell volume, thereby allowing all these parameters to vary during the optimization process. These criteria are standard in similar DFT studies and are sufficient to achieve accurate results52–55. Our calculations treat the following electrons as valence electrons: Ba-5 5 6, Co-3 4, Br-4 4, Cl-3 3 and I-5 5.

An important parameter in the context of spintronics is the half-metallic (HM) gap, which is defined as the energy difference between the bottom of the conduction band (since it is closer to the Fermi level as compared to the valence band for the semi-conducting state) and the Fermi level61. It represents the minimum energy required to induce spin excitation and generate holes in the minority spin channel. Usually, a small HM gap implies that half-metallicity is unstable and may be weakened under slight lattice strain. Hence, half-metallic materials with large HM gaps are preffered for practical spintronic applications62. According to the data presented in Table 2, exhibits the largest half-metallic (HM) gap of 0.630 eV, followed by with an HM gap of 0.418 eV, and with the smallest HM gap of 0.404 eV. also demonstrates a significantly larger HM gap compared to other half-metals, including half-Heusler compounds63, (M = Mg and Ca)10, and CoCrP and CoCrAs64. This enhanced HM gap suggests its superior suitability for spintronic applications, where a larger HM gap contributes to increased stability and efficiency in spin-dependent electronic processes62. Moreover, the large HM gap in indicates that this compound could outperform other materials in practical spintronic applications, such as magnetoresistive-based biomedical devices, nanomagnetic logic, spin-torque oscillators, quantum computing, spin field-effect transistors (spin FETs), and racetrack memory10,65–67. Therefore, the substantial HM gap observed in makes it the most suitable candidate for spintronic applications.

Magnetic susceptibility is a measure of how much a material will become magnetized when exposed to an external magnetic field. As shown in Fig. 6(d), decreases with increasing temperature, which is a characteristic of ferromagnetic materials. This phenomenon can be explained by the thermal agitation of magnetic moments within the material. At lower temperatures, the magnetic domains are more aligned, resulting in higher magnetization and thus a higher magnetic susceptibility. However, as temperature increases, thermal energy disrupts this alignment by increasing the random motion of magnetic moments. This leads to a reduction in the net magnetization of the material and, consequently, a decrease in magnetic susceptibility. This behavior aligns with the Curie-Weiss law, which describes the temperature dependence of magnetic susceptibility in ferromagnetic materials. Conclusion

In this study, we conducted a thorough investigation of the electronic, magnetic, elastic, optical, and thermoelectric properties of halide perovskites (X = Cl, Br, I), employing density functional theory (DFT). The analysis of spin-polarized band structures and density of states (DOS) reveal that these compounds exhibit half-metallicity, characterized by a significant band gap in the spin-up channels and 100% spin polarization at the Fermi level. The spin magnetic moment value for (X = Cl, Br, I) was found to be 2 B for all compounds, primarily due to the hybridization of Co 3d orbitals and p orbitals of the Cl, Br, and I halogens.

Among all the perovskites, , in particular, demonstrates outstanding performance across several properties, making it the prime candidate for practical applications. Its large half-metallic gap is essential for the effective performance of spintronic devices, as it facilitates efficient spin polarization and transport. The material also exhibits excellent elastic and mechanical properties, showing ductility, anisotropy, and compliance with Born stability criteria, which ensures its mechanical integrity in various applications. Moreover, the high optical conductivity of , particularly in the UV region, positions it as a strong candidate for optoelectronic devices. Additionally, its notable thermoelectric parameters, including a high Seebeck coefficient, electrical and thermal conductivity, and power factor, highlight its potential in energy conversion and thermoelectric applications.

Multi-cluster distributed optimization in open multi-agent systems over directed graphs with acknowledgement messages
Evagoras Makridis, Gabriele Oliva, Themistoklis Charalambous

Abstract
In this paper, we tackle the problem of distributed optimization over directed networks in open multi-agent systems (OMAS), where agents may dynamically join or leave, causing persistent changes in network topology and problem dimension. These disruptions not only pose significant challenges to maintaining convergence and stability in distributed optimization algorithms, but could also break the network topology into multiple clusters, each one associated with its own set of objective functions. To address this, we propose a novel Open Distributed Optimization Algorithm with Gradient Tracking ( Open-GT ), which employs: (a) a dynamic mechanism for detecting active out-neighbors through acknowledgement messages, and (b) a fully distributed max-consensus procedure to spread information regarding agent departures, in possibly unbalanced directed networks. We show that when all active agents execute Open-GT , the optimization process in each formed cluster remains consistent, while the agents converge to their cluster-wide optimal solution if there exists a time after which the network remains unchanged. Finally, we validate our approach in a simulated environment with dynamically changing agent populations, demonstrating its resilience to network variations and its ability to support distributed optimization under OMAS dynamics.



I
Introduction

Distributed optimization in multi-agent systems (MAS) has attracted significant interest due to applications in sensor networks, robotic coordination, and large-scale machine learning. Agents collaborate to solve a global optimization problem using only local information exchanged with neighbors. Traditional methods typically assume a fixed number of agents and model communication via static undirected
[
1
]
or directed graphs
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. However, in realistic scenarios, the agent population changes over time, motivating the shift towards open MAS (OMAS), where agents may join or leave dynamically at runtime.

This dynamic setting introduces several challenges. In particular, while existing works often require persistent network connectivity
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
, in practice, global connectivity may be unnecessary. Instead, agent arrivals and departures naturally induce multiple clusters, each forming a connected sub-network with its own active agents and local minimizer. Thus, a practical focus shifts to ensuring connectivity within each cluster, allowing for flexible and scalable distributed optimization in dynamic OMAS.

In realistic OMAS settings, agents often experience asymmetric communication due to network constraints or variations in transmission power. While prior works have addressed OMAS under undirected communication
[
6
,
7
,
11
,
10
]
, such assumptions limit their applicability in dynamic environments where agents may join or leave during runtime.
In contrast, directed communication offers improved scalability and adaptability, but introduces new challenges for algorithm design. Existing results for directed networks largely focus on the distributed average consensus problem; see,
e.g.,
[
12
,
13
]
.
More recently,
[
14
]
considered distributed optimization in OMAS with directed communication. Although this approach deals with distributed optimization in directed networks of OMAS, it assumes that the active OMAS remains strongly connected throughout the optimization process which might be restrictive in open networks where agents may depart. Additionally,
[
14
]
presumes agents have prior knowledge of their active out-neighbors, which can be a strong requirement in networks with directional information flow.

In dynamically evolving, asymmetric networks, ensuring consistent information flow is particularly challenging, as agents may become disconnected, limiting their ability to collaboratively optimize towards the true global objective of the active OMAS. Furthermore, when agents are unaware of changes in their out-neighborhood,
e.g.,
agent departures, they cannot assign correct communication weights on their outgoing links, leading to incorrect (biased) convergence. Moreover, disconnections may split the network into multiple isolated clusters, within which agents are both unable and no longer obligated to coordinate globally. Consequently, optimization efforts become localized, with each cluster of agents independently optimizing its own objective function rather than collectively optimizing toward the global objective of the overall OMAS.

Towards this direction, we introduce the following contributions. We propose a novel framework for distributed optimization in OMAS that supports dynamic cluster formation, allowing agent departures and arrivals to split or merge the network under directed and time-varying communication links, as illustrated in Fig.
1
. More specifically, we propose a distributed optimization algorithm for clustered OMAS, hereinafter referred to as
Open-GT
, that optimizes cluster-wide objective functions, while incorporating the exchange of acknowledgment messages to detect local topology changes. These messages enable agents to identify topology changes via max-consensus iterations performed between optimization rounds. Upon detecting such changes, agents can reassign the weights on their outgoing links, ensuring correctness on their optimization variables which are driven towards the new minimizer, despite changes in the network’s composition. We support these contributions with theoretical analysis and simulation results, demonstrating the correctness and effectiveness of
Open-GT
in OMAS settings.

II-C

Problem Formulation

We consider an OMAS whose network composition at time
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is represented by a time-varying directed graph
\mathcal{G}_{k}=(\mathcal{V}_{k},\mathcal{E}_{k})
. Due to the system’s openness, disjoint clusters
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{q}\in\mathcal{Q}
(with
\mathcal{Q}
a finite set) may form due to agent departures and arrivals. Each cluster
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{q}=(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{q},\mathcal{E}_{k}^{q})
corresponds to a strongly connected component of the maximal digraph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{E}:=\cup_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{k}
is the set of all potential interactions. The set of active times of the cluster
q\in\mathcal{Q}
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}^{q}=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\mathcal{V}_{k}^{q}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{k}\}
. Each cluster has
|\mathcal{V}_{k}^{q}|
agents, with all clusters being disjoint at any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
i.e.,
,
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{q}\cap\mathcal{V}_{k}^{q^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for
q\neq q^{\prime}
and
\cup_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{q}=\mathcal{V}_{k}
. Here it is important to note that agents interact only with their immediate neighbors without being aware of the cluster they belong to.

Within this setting, we study a distributed optimization problem in which active agents
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
aim to compute an optimal solution
x_{k}^{q\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
that minimizes the sum of local cost functions in their current cluster. Each agent
v_{j}
holds a possibly time-varying convex local cost function
f_{j,k}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, allowing agents to rejoin the system with updated objectives and new initial conditions. Agents maintain local estimates
x_{j,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and update them iteratively to converge to
x_{k}^{q\ast}
. That is, agents in cluster
q
aim to reach consensus on a common minimizer of the sum of their active local costs,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\operatorname*{minimize}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\ f_{k}^{q}(x)=\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}^{q}}f_{j,k}(x),

(6)

where
f_{k}^{q}
denotes the sum of local cost functions that belong to cluster
q
at time
k
.

Remark 1.

Clearly, when the topology of the OMAS can only form one cluster
|\mathcal{Q}|=1
, the problem in (
6
) reduces to the classical distributed optimization problem in OMAS. If a
trivial cluster
exists, i.e.,
|\mathcal{V}_{k}^{q}|=1
, then the cluster (the agent) solves a local optimization problem based on its local variables and without the need of communication.

II-D

Assumptions

In order to present the proposed distributed algorithm and its analysis, let us now state some assumptions.

Assumption 1.

(Lipschitz gradient)

The local function
f_{j,k}
of agent
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}
is continuously differentiable and has
L_{j}
-
Lipschitz continuous gradients for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, that is, there exists a constant
L_{j}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla f_{j,k}(x)-\nabla f_{j,k}(y)\|\leq L_{j}\|x-y\|,

(7)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Assumption 2.

(Strong convexity)

The global objective function
f_{k}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{V}}f_{j,k}
is
\mu
-strongly convex for some
\mu>0
, and all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle f_{k}(y)\geq f_{k}(x)+\nabla f_{k}(x)^{\top}(y-x)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-x\|_{2}^{2},

(8)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Assumption 3.

(
\beta
-strongly connected cluster sequence).

The sequence of time-varying sub-digraphs which model the agents’ interactions within cluster
q
, i.e.,
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{q}=(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{q},\mathcal{E}_{k}^{q})
is
\beta
-strongly connected
, i.e., there exists an integer
\beta>0
such that for all
k\in\mathcal{K}^{q}
, the union of sub-digraphs

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k:\beta}^{q}:=\Bigl\{\mathcal{V}_{k}^{q},\cup_{t=k\beta}^{(k+1)\beta-1}\mathcal{E}^{q}_{t}\Bigr\},

(9)

is strongly connected.

Assumption
3
relaxes the strong connectivity condition used in
[
14
]
, allowing agents to form disconnected clusters and minimize their cluster-wide objective. For fixed-agent sets with time-varying links,
i.e.,
\mathcal{G}_{k}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}_{k})
, such assumption is standard in distributed optimization
[
2
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.

Assumption 4.

(Acknowledgement messages)

At the beginning of each time step
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, each active agent
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
can send an
1
-bit message
\alpha_{j,k}=1
to its in-neighbors
v_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}^{\texttt{in}}
, which is only received by the currently active ones
v_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{j,k}^{\texttt{in}}
.

Assumption
4
, inspired by protocols such as TCP, ARQ/HARQ, and ALOHA, models
1
-bit acknowledgment messages exchanged over dedicated, narrowband control channels that are distinct from the directed data channels. Specifically, for each directed data channel, a corresponding reverse control channel is used to carry these acknowledgments. Over such channels, agents
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
exchange
1
-bit acknowledgments

18
,
19
]
to detect their active out-neighbors

Dynamic weight assignment:

In directed networks, remaining agents
v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}_{k+1}
may continue sending information to departed out-neighbors

c_{lj,k}

\mathcal{N}_{j,k}^{\texttt{out}}

v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}_{k}

\displaystyle c_{lj,k}=\begin{cases}\hfil\frac{1}{1+d_{j,k}^{\texttt{out}}},\!\!\!&\text{ if }\alpha_{l,k}=1\lor l=j,\\
\hfil 0,&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

Although this mechanism would allow all the agents to properly assign their weights, this would not be enough for the algorithm in (
11

Coordinating departure information:
A missing acknowledgment message signals the departure of an out-neighbor of agent

\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}:=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{x}_{1,k},~\widehat{x}_{2,k},~\ldots,~\widehat{x}_{n,k}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
. Further defining the arrival trigger for agent
v_{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{k}
as
\psi_{j,k}:=\alpha_{j,k}(1-\alpha_{j,k-1})
, and stacking for each agent we have
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{k}:=\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{1,k},~\psi_{2,k},~\ldots,~\psi_{n,k}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
. Similarly, we can stack the coordinated departure information of all agents into
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}:=\begin{bmatrix}h_{1,k},~h_{2,k},~\ldots,~h_{n,k}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
. Now we can define the matrices
H_{k}:=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k})
and
\Psi_{k}:=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{k})
. Moreover, for analysis, one can collect all the push-weights to form the nonnegative matrix
C_{k}:=\{c_{lj,k}\}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n\times n}
, whose columns that correspond to active agents sum up to
1
, while all the entries that correspond to inactive agents are
0
. Now, the equivalent vector-matrix representation of the proposed algorithm for all potentially active agents is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-\Psi_{k+1})C_{k}(\mathbf{x}_{k}-\gamma\mathbf{w}_{k})+\Psi_{k+1}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1},

(16a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-\Psi_{k+1})C_{k}\mathbf{y}_{k}+\Psi_{k+1}\mathbf{1},

(16b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{y_{k+1}))^{-1}}\mathbf{x}_{k+1},

(16c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I-H_{k})(C_{k}\mathbf{w}_{k}-\mathbf{g}_{k})+\mathbf{g}_{k+1}.

(16d)

IV
Analysis

In the OMAS setting, explicit convergence cannot be guaranteed due to the perturbations introduced by agent arrivals and departures, which continually shift cluster minimizers. Instead, we analyze the
eventual correctness
of the iterations in (
16
), by examining whether agents’ estimates track the evolving minimizer of their current cluster. Assuming that the OMAS becomes stable after some finite time
k^{\prime}
, we study the convergence behavior during this stable period.

The following lemma shows that the sum of the local
w
-iterates in (
11d
) is equal to the sum of the local gradients
g_{j,k}=\nabla f_{j,k}(z_{j,k})
at any time
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, using the column stochasticity property of the weights
c_{ij,k}
that correspond to active agents in any given cluster
q\in\mathcal{Q}
.

Lemma 1.

Let Assumptions
3
and
4
hold. Then the following holds for each cluster
q\in\mathcal{Q}
at any
k\in\mathcal{K}^{q}

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}^{q}_{k}}w_{j,k}=\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}^{q}_{k}}g_{j,k}.

(17)

Proof.

We prove the lemma by induction. First observe that,
\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{J}^{q}_{k}}w_{j,k}=\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{J}^{q}_{k}}g_{j,k}
by the (re)-initialization of
v_{j}\in\mathcal{J}^{q}_{k}
. Now, assuming that (
17
) holds true for a given
k
, we have to show that it holds true at
k+1
.

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}^{q}_{k+1}}w_{j,k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}^{q}_{k+1}}\left(\sum_{v_{i}\in\mathcal{V}^{q}_{k}}c_{ji,k}v_{i,k}+g_{j,k+1}-g_{j,k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}^{q}_{k}}w_{j,k}+\!\!\!\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}^{q}_{k+1}}g_{j,k+1}-\!\!\!\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}^{q}_{k+1}}g_{j,k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}^{q}_{k}}g_{j,k}+\!\!\!\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}^{q}_{k+1}}g_{j,k+1}-\!\!\!\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}^{q}_{k+1}}g_{j,k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}^{q}_{k+1}}g_{j,k+1}.

Thus, since

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}^{q}_{k+1}}w_{j,k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}^{q}_{k+1}}w_{j,k+1}+\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{J}^{q}_{k+1}}w_{j,k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}^{q}_{k+1}}g_{j,k+1},

the proof is complete.
∎

Now suppose that at some
k^{\prime}
, the arrivals and departures of agents are stopped. The formed clusters are now fixed in terms of agents, and they include only the remaining agents
v_{j}\in\mathcal{R}_{k^{\prime}}
. Hence, for
k\geq k^{\prime}
we have
\mathcal{V}_{k}=\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{R}_{k^{\prime}}
. In what follows we analyze the convergence of the remaining agents for each cluster independently. Let us further define the variables of the remaining agents in each cluster
q\in\mathcal{Q}
. Let
\mathbf{x}_{k+1}^{q}
,
\mathbf{y}_{k+1}^{q}
,
\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{q}
, and
\mathbf{w}_{k+1}^{q}
the stack of variables maintained by the agents in cluster
q\in\mathcal{Q}
during
k\in\mathcal{K}^{q}
.

The vector-matrix form of the iterations of the agents that remain in cluster
q
for
k\geq k^{\prime}
is now reduced to the one of the Push-DIGing algorithm in
[
3
]
for closed networks with time-varying links. Hence the iterations in (
16
) for the cluster
q
and for
k\geq k^{\prime}
, gives
\Psi_{k}^{q}=I
and
H_{k}^{q}=I
, and the iterations of the agents in the cluster become:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{k+1}^{q}

\displaystyle=C_{k}^{q}(\mathbf{x}_{k}^{q}-\gamma\mathbf{w}_{k}^{q}),

(18a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{k+1}^{q}

\displaystyle=C_{k}^{q}\mathbf{y}_{k}^{q},

(18b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{q}

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{y_{k+1}^{q}))^{-1}}\mathbf{x}_{k+1}^{q},

(18c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{k+1}^{q}

\displaystyle=C_{k}^{q}\mathbf{w}_{k}^{q}+\mathbf{g}_{k+1}^{q}-\mathbf{g}_{k}^{q},

(18d)

where
C_{k}^{q}

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we addressed the distributed optimization in open multi-agent systems with directed, time-varying communication. We proposed
Open-GT
, a novel algorithm that enables agents to adapt to dynamic topology changes using acknowledgment messages to detect active out-neighbors and a max-consensus mechanism to propagate departure information. Our preliminary analysis shows that
Open-GT
maintains consistency within dynamically formed clusters and converges to the cluster-wide minimizer once cluster membership stabilizes. Simulations further demonstrate the algorithm’s robustness to network variations, highlighting its practical effectiveness in dynamic OMAS environments.

Well-posedness of Lur’e systems with feedthrough
Chris Guiver, Hartmut Logemann

Abstract




Abstract.
For a large class of Lur’e systems with time-varying nonlinearities and feedthrough we consider several well-posedness issues, namely:
existence, continuation, blow-up in finite-time, forward completeness and uniqueness of solutions. Lur’e systems with feedthrough are systems of forced, nonlinear ordinary differential equations coupled with a nonlinear algebraic equation determining the output
of the system. The presence of feedthrough means that the algebraic equation is implicit in the output, and, in general, the output may not be expressible by an analytic formula in terms of the state and the input. Simple examples illustrate that the well-posedness properties of such systems are not necessarily guaranteed by assumptions sufficient for the corresponding well-posedness properties of Lur’e systems without feedthrough. We provide
sufficient conditions for the well-posedness properties mentioned above, using global inversion
theorems from real analysis and tools from non-smooth analysis and differential inclusions. The theory is illustrated with examples.

Keywords.
Differential inclusions,
existence of solutions,
feedthrough, global inversion theorems,
Lur’e systems,
nonlinear differential equations

Lemma 2.2

z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the function
t\mapsto g(t,z)
is measurable. It is well known that, if
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
Caratheodory function
and
w:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is measurable, then the function
t\mapsto g(t,w(t))
is measurable,
see, for example,
[
5
, Lemma 8.2.3]
.

Next, we review a number of concepts related to set-valued functions. Details can be found, for example in
[
5
,
18
]
and
[
50
, Chapter 2]
. For a set-valued map
G
from
{\rm dom}(G)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
to
2^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, the power set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we shall use the notation
G:{\rm dom}(G)\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Delta(G):=\{\xi\in{\rm dom}(G):G(\xi)\not=\emptyset\}
. We say that
G
is
upper semicontinuous at

\xi\in\Delta(G)
if, for every open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
G(\xi)\subset U
, there exists an open neighbourhood
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
of
\xi
such that
G(\zeta)\subset U
for all
\zeta\in V
. The map
G
is called
upper semicontinuous on
S\subset\Delta(G)
if it is upper semicontinuous at every point in
S
. We simply say that
G
is
upper semicontinuous
if
G
is upper semicontinuous on
\Delta(G)
. It is well known
that
G
is upper semicontinuous if, and only if, for every closed
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists
a closed
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
G^{-1}(Y)=X\cap\Delta(G)
, where

G^{-1}(Y):=\{\xi\in{\rm dom}(G):G(\xi)\cap Y\not=\emptyset\}=\{\xi\in\Delta(G):G(\xi)\cap Y\not=\emptyset\},

the preimage of
Y
under
G
. A set-valued map
G:{\rm dom}(G)\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
measurable
if, for every open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the set
G^{-1}(U)
is Lebesgue measurable. A
measurable selection
of a set-valued map
G:{\rm dom}(G)\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a measurable function
g:{\rm dom}(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
g(\xi)\in G(\xi)
for all
\xi\in{\rm dom}(G)
. Under the assumption that
\Delta(G)={\rm dom}(G)
, if a measurable function
\tilde{g}:{\rm dom}(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies
\tilde{g}(\xi)\in G(\xi)
for almost every
\xi\in{\rm dom}(G)
, then there exists a measurable selection
g
of
G
such that
g(\xi)=\tilde{g}(\xi)
for almost every
\xi\in{\rm dom}\,G
. Therefore, the function
\tilde{g}
will also be referred to as a measurable selection of
G
.

Finally, the following convention applies to mixed “almost everywhere” and “all” quantifications: for
g:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
T\subset\mathbb{R}
and
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the statement

\mbox{$g(t,z)$ has property $P$}\quad\mbox{for a.e. $t\in T$ and all $z\in S$},

should be understood as follows: there exists a set
E\subset T
of measure zero such that

\mbox{$g(t,z)$ has property $P$}\quad\forall\,(t,z)\in(T\backslash E)\times S,

that is the “a.e.” part of the statement holds uniformly with respect to
z\in S
.

3
Lur’e systems with feedthrough: definitions and concepts

For fixed
m,m_{\rm e},n,p\in\mathbb{N}
, let

(A,B,B_{\rm e},C,D,D_{\rm e})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times m_{\rm e}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}\times\mathbb{R}^{p\times m_{\rm e}}.

With the sextuple
(A,B,B_{\rm e},C,D,D_{\rm e})
, we associate the
following controlled and observed linear state-space system

\dot{x}=Ax+Bu+B_{\rm e}v,\quad y=Cx+Du+D_{\rm e}v.

(3.1)

Frequently, we will refer to (
3.1
) as the linear system
S:=(A,B,B_{\rm e},C,D,D_{\rm e})
.

Application of nonlinear output feedback of the form
u(t)=f(t,y(t))
yields the closed-loop system (
1.1
), which will be denoted by
S^{f}:=(A,B,B_{\rm e},C,D,D_{\rm e},f)
.
For the following, it will be convenient to define
f\circ y
by

(f\circ y)(t):=f(t,y(t)).

If
f
does not depend on
t
, then
f\circ y
is simply the usual composition of the functions
f
and
y
. The function
v
is an external input to the nonlinear feedback system (
1.1
). It will always be assumed that
f
is a Carathordory function.

Lur’e systems of the form (
1.1
) capture a large number of scenarios of interest, four of which are considered below.

Scenario 1.
The system

\dot{x}=Ax+B(f\circ y)+v_{1},\quad y=Cx+D(f\circ y)+v_{2}

is of the form (
1.1
) with
m_{\rm e}=n+p
,
B_{\rm e}=(I,0)
,
D_{\rm e}=(0,I)

and
v=(v_{1}^{\top},v_{2}^{\top})^{\top}
.

Scenario 2.
Consider the following feedback scheme subject to output disturbances

\dot{x}=Ax+Bu,\quad z=Cx+Du,\quad u=f\circ(z+d),

where
z
is the undisturbed output and
d
is the output disturbance signal. Setting

y:=z+d
, we have that

\dot{x}=Ax+B(f\circ y),\quad y=Cx+D(f\circ y)+d,

which is of the form (
1.1
) with
m_{\rm e}=p
,
B_{\rm e}=0
,
D_{\rm e}=I

and
v=d
. Note that any boundedness properties of
y
can be used to infer boundedness properties of
z
, provided suitable bounds on
d
are known.

Scenario 3.
Here we consider the case of different nonlinearities in the state and output equations, namely

\dot{x}=Ax+\tilde{B}(f_{1}\circ y)+B_{\rm e}v,\quad y=Cx+\tilde{D}(f_{2}\circ y)+D_{\rm e}v,

where
\tilde{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m_{1}}
,
\tilde{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m_{2}}
,
f_{1}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}

and
f_{2}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}}
. The above system can be expressed in the form (
1.1
)
with

m:=m_{1}+m_{2},\quad B:=(\tilde{B},0),\quad D:=(0,\tilde{D})\quad\mbox{and}\quad f:=\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}\\
f_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Scenario 4.
In this scenario, we consider a so-called
4
-block feedback scheme.
The underlying linear system is given by

\dot{x}=Ax+Bu,\quad y=Cx+Du,

with
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,

B=(B_{1},B_{2}),\quad C=\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}\\
C_{2}\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad D=\begin{pmatrix}D_{11}&D_{12}\\
D_{21}&D_{22}\end{pmatrix},

where
B_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m_{i}}
,
C_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{i}\times n}
and
D_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{i}\times m_{j}}
,

i,j=1,2
. Partitioning
u
and
y
accordingly,

u=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,u_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}},\quad y=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,y_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{i}},\quad i=1,2,

consider the feedback system given by

\dot{x}=Ax+Bu,\quad y=Cx+Du,\quad u_{1}=f\circ y_{2},\quad u_{2}=v

where
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
and
v
is an external input. The system can be written as

\dot{x}=Ax+B_{1}(f\circ y_{2})+B_{2}v,\quad y_{2}=C_{2}x+D_{21}(f\circ y_{2})+D_{22}v,\quad y_{1}=C_{1}x+D_{11}u_{1}+D_{12}v.

Note that the first two equations constitute a system of the form (
1.1
) with
m=m_{1}
,
m_{\rm e}=m_{2}
,
p=p_{2}
,
B_{\rm e}=B_{2}
,
C=C_{2}
,
D=D_{21}
and
D_{\rm e}=D_{22}
. Furthermore, we note that
y_{1}
is completely determined by
v
,
u_{1}=f\circ y_{1}
and
x
, and hence by the first two equations (into which
y_{1}
does not enter).

The
behaviour

{\mathcal{B}}(S)
of the linear system
S
(or of (
3.1
)) is the linear subspace of all quadruples

(u,v,x,y)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{m})\times L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{m_{\rm e}})\times W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{p})\,,

which satisfy (
3.1
) for almost every
t\geq 0
. The elements of
{\mathcal{B}}(S)
are
called
trajectories
of
S
.

Let
t_{0}\geq 0
. The
behaviour

{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})
of
S^{f}
(or of (
1.1
)) on

[t_{0},\infty)
is the set of all triples

(v,x,y)\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{m_{\rm e}})\times W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}([t_{0},\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{1}_{\rm loc}([t_{0},\infty),\mathbb{R}^{p})\,,

such that
f\circ y\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}([t_{0},\infty),\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
(v,x,y)
satisfies
(
1.1
) for almost every
t\geq t_{0}
. Elements in
{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})
will also be referred to as
trajectories
of
S^{f}
on
[t_{0},\infty)
or trajectories of
S^{f}
with initial time
t_{0}
. It is convenient to set
{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f}):={\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},0)
, the elements of which will simply be referred to as
trajectories
of
S^{f}
.

In the following, if
t_{0}\geq 0
and
z
is a function defined on
[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau)
, where
0<\tau\leq\infty
, we define
z^{t_{0}}
on
[0,\tau)
by
z^{t_{0}}(t)=z(t+t_{0})

for all
t\in[0,\tau)
. Similarly, we set
f^{t_{0}}(t,\xi):=f^{t_{0}}(t+t_{0},\xi)
for all
t\geq 0

and all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. As
f
is a Caratheodory function, so is
f^{t_{0}}
. We note that

(v,x,y)\in{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})\Longleftrightarrow(v^{t_{0}},x^{t_{0}},y^{t_{0}})\in{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f^{t_{0}}})

and

(v,x,y)\in{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})\Longleftrightarrow(f^{t_{0}}\circ y^{t_{0}},v^{t_{0}},x^{t_{0}},y^{t_{0}})\in{\mathcal{B}}(S).

If the nonlinearity
f
does not depend on
t
, then the above equivalences remain valid when

f^{t_{0}}
is replaced by
f
.

For
t_{0}\geq 0
and
t_{0}<\tau\leq\infty
, a triple
(v,x,y)
is said to be a
pre-trajectory
of
S^{f}
(or of (
1.1
)) on
[t_{0},\tau)
, if

(v,x,y)\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{m_{\rm e}})\times W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}([t_{0},\tau),\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{1}_{\rm loc}([t_{0},\tau),\mathbb{R}^{p}),

f\circ y\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}([t_{0},\tau),\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
(v,x,y)
satisfies (
1.1
) for almost every
t\in[t_{0},\tau)
. If
(v,x,y)
is a pre-trajectory of
S^{f}
on
[t_{0},\tau)
, then we say that
(v,x,y)
is
maximally defined
if there does not exist a pre-trajectory

(v,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
of
S^{f}
on
[t_{0},\tilde{\tau})
such that
\tilde{\tau}>\tau
and

(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})|_{[t_{0},\tau)}=(x,y)
. The following result shows that every
pre-trajectory can be extended to a maximally defined pre-trajectory.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(v,x,y)
be a pre-trajectory of (
1.1
) on
[t_{0},\tau)
, where

0\leq t_{0}<\tau<\infty
. There exists a maximally defined pre-trajectory
(v,x_{\rm m},y_{\rm m})
of (
1.1
) on
[t_{0},\tau_{\rm m})
such that
\tau\leq\tau_{\rm m}\leq\infty
and
(x_{\rm m},y_{\rm m})|_{[t_{0},\tau)}=(x,y)
.

The proof is based on an application of Zorn’s lemma and is a generalization of a similar argument familiar from the theory of ordinary differential equations. For the convenience of the reader, we have included the proof in the Appendix.

A maximally defined pre-trajectory on
[t_{0},\tau)
will also be referred as a
maximally defined pre-trajectory with initial time
t_{0}
.
Maximally defined pre-trajectories will play an important role in the the following and
therefore we define:

\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0}):=\{\mbox{maximally defined pre-trajectories of $S^{f}$ with initial time $t_{0}$}\}.

It is convenient to set
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f}):=\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},0)
. Obviously,
{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})\subset\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})
, and furthermore,

(v,x,y)\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})\Longleftrightarrow(v^{t_{0}},x^{t_{0}},y^{t_{0}})\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f^{t_{0}}}).

If, for every maximally defined pre-trajectory
(v,x,y)\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})
with bounded interval
[t_{0},\tau)
of definition, where
t_{0}<\tau<\infty
, it holds that

\limsup_{t\uparrow\tau}\|x(t)\|+\int_{t_{0}}^{\tau}\big(\|y(s)\|+\|f(s,y(s))\|\big)\,{\rm d}s=\infty,

then (
1.1
) is said to have the
blow-up property
.

The following result is not surprising: it shows that the blow-up property holds, provided
that a suitable local existence assumption is satisfied.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that, for all
t_{0}\geq 0
, all
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
v\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{m_{\rm e}})
, there exists
(v,x,y)\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})
such that
x(t_{0})=x^{0}
.
Then, for a maximally defined pre-trajectory
(v,x,y)\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})
with bounded maximal interval of definition
[t_{0},\tau)

(
where
t_{0}<\tau<\infty
)
, it holds that
\int_{t_{0}}^{\tau}\big(\|y(t)\|+\|Bf(t,y(t))\|\big){\rm d}t=\infty
. In
particular, (
1.1
) has the blow-up property.

Proof.

Let
(v,x,y)
be a pre-trajectory of (
1.1
) defined on the finite interval
[t_{0},\tau)
and assume that
\int_{t_{0}}^{\tau}\big(\|y(t)\|+\|Bf(t,y(t)\|\big){\rm d}t<\infty
. It is sufficient to show that
(v,x,y)
is not maximally defined.
As
\int_{t_{0}}^{\tau}\|Bf(t,y(t)\|{\rm d}t<\infty
, an application of the variation-of-parameters
formula to (
1.1a
) shows that the limit
\lim_{t\uparrow\tau}x(t)=:x(\tau)
exists. It follows from the hypothesis that there exists a pre-trajectory
(v,\hat{x},\hat{y})\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},\tau)
defined on
[\tau,\theta)
with
\tau<\theta\leq\infty
and such that
\hat{x}(\tau)=x(\tau)
. Setting

\tilde{x}(t):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &x(t),\quad t_{0}\leq t<\tau\\
&\hat{x}(t),\quad\tau\leq t<\theta\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tilde{y}(t):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &y(t),\quad t_{0}\leq t<\tau\\
&\hat{y}(t),\quad\tau\leq t<\theta,\end{aligned}\right.

it is clear that
\tilde{x}\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}([t_{0},\theta),\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\tilde{y}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}([t_{0},\theta),\mathbb{R}^{p})
,
f\circ\tilde{y}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}([t_{0},\theta),\mathbb{R}^{m})
and

\dot{\tilde{x}}(t)=A\tilde{x}(t)+Bf(t,\tilde{y}(t))+B_{\rm e}v(t),\quad\tilde{y}(t)=C\tilde{x}(t)+Df(t,\tilde{y}(t))+D_{\rm e}v(t),\quad\mbox{for a.e.
$t\in[t_{0},\theta)$.}

Consequently,
(v,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
is a pre-trajectory of (
1.1
) on
[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+\theta)
which extends
(v,x,y)
, showing that
(v,x,y)
is not maximally defined.
∎

Note that the hypothesis in Proposition
3.2
holds if, and only if, for all
t_{0}\geq 0
, all
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
v\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{m_{\rm e}})
, there exists
(v,x,y)\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f^{t_{0}}})
such that
x(0)=x^{0}
. Furthermore, in the case wherein

f(t,\xi)=f(\xi)
(that is,
f
does not depend on
t
), the hypothesis in Proposition
3.2
is satisfied if, and only if, for all
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
v\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{m_{\rm e}})
, there exists
(v,x,y)\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f})
such that
x(0)=x^{0}
.

For later purposes, we define the forward completeness and uniqueness properties.
System (
1.1
) is said to be
forward complete
if
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})={\mathcal{B}}(S^{f},t_{0})
for every
t_{0}\geq 0
, that is, every maximally defined
pre-trajectory is a trajectory. Finally, we say that (
1.1
)
has the
uniqueness property
if, for every
v\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{m_{\rm e}})
and every
t_{0}\geq 0
, any two pre-trajectories

(v,x,y)
and
(v,\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
on
[t_{0},\tau)
and
[t_{0},\tilde{\tau})
,
respectively, where
t_{0}<\tau\leq\tilde{\tau}\leq\infty
, coincide on
[t_{0},\tau)
if
x(t_{0})=\tilde{x}(t_{0})
.

The following simple examples with time-independent globally Lipschitz
f
show that if
D\not=0
, then

•

for given
v\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{m_{\rm e}})
and
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the set of pre-trajectories
(v,x,y)
such that
x(t_{0})=x^{0}
may be empty;

•

the blow-up property may fail to hold;

•

for given
v\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{m_{\rm e}})
and
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the set of pre-trajectories
(v,x,y)
such that
x(t_{0})=x^{0}
may contain several elements despite
f
being (globally) Lipschitz;

•

pre-trajectories may blow up in finite-time (implying that the system is not forward complete) despite
f
being linearly bounded.

The first three of the four examples below are from
[
27
]
, but as they are important in the context of the present paper, we repeat them here in a somewhat abbreviated form for the benefit of the reader. Unsurprisingly, the examples will show that lack of surjectivity or injectivity of the map
I-Df
can cause problems regarding existence, uniqueness and blow-up.

Example 3.3
.

(a)

Consider (
1.1
) with

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,B=B_{\rm e}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,C=(1,0),\,\,\,D=D_{\rm e}=1,

and nonlinearity
f
given by

f(\xi)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\xi+1,&&\xi<-2,\\
&\xi/2,&&-2\leq\xi\leq 2,\\
&\xi-1,&&\xi>2.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
x^{0}=(a,0)^{\top}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and assume that
(0,x,y)
is a pre-trajectory defined on some interval
[0,\tau)
and such that
x(0)=x^{0}
. Noting that
Ce^{At}B\equiv 0
and using the variation-of-parameters formula, it follows from (
1.1
) that

y(t)-f(y(t))=e^{t}a,\quad\forall\>t\in[0,\tau).

(3.2)

As

\xi-Df(\xi)=\xi-f(\xi)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &-1,&&\xi<-2,\\
&\xi/2,&&-2\leq\xi\leq 2,\\
&1,&&\xi>2,\end{aligned}\right.

we see that (
3.2
) does not have a solution
for any
t\geq 0
if
|a|>1
. Hence, there does not exist any pre-trajectory
(0,x,y)

such that
x(0)=(a,0)^{\top}
if
|a|>1
. We note that
I-Df
is not surjective.

(b)

We consider the example introduced in part
(a)
, now with
a=1/2
. In this case, we see that (
3.2
), has the unique solution
y(t)=e^{t}
for every
t\in[0,\ln 2)
and does not have a solution whenever
t>\ln 2
.
Setting
y(t):=e^{t}
for all
t\in[0,\ln 2)
and

x(t):=e^{At}x^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}Bf(y(s)){\rm d}s=\begin{pmatrix}e^{t}/2\\
(e^{t}-1)/2\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall\>t\in[0,\ln 2),

we conclude that
(0,x,y)
is a maximally defined pre-trajectory satisfying

x(0)=(1/2,0)^{\top}
. It is clear that

\limsup_{t\to\ln 2}\|x(t)\|+\int_{0}^{\ln 2}(|y(s)|+|f(y(s))|)\,{\rm d}s<\infty,

showing that the system does not have the blow-up property.

(c)

Consider the scalar system

\dot{x}=-x+f\circ y+v,\quad y=x+f\circ y+v,

which is (
1.1
) with
A=-1
and
B=B_{\rm e}=C=D=D_{\rm e}=1
. Consider the nonlinearity
f
given by

f(\xi)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &-3/4,&&\xi<-1/2,\\
&\xi(1-\xi),&&-1/2\leq\xi\leq 1/2,\\
&1/4,&&\xi>1/2.\end{aligned}\right.

It is clear that
I-Df
is not injective. Let
x_{1}(t)\equiv 1/4
,
y_{1}(t)\equiv 1/2
and

x_{2}(t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(e^{-t}-1/2)^{2},&&0\leq t\leq\ln 2,\\
&0,&&t>\ln 2,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad y_{2}(t)=-\sqrt{x_{2}(t)}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1/2-e^{-t},&&0\leq t\leq\ln 2,\\
&0,&&t>\ln 2\,.\end{aligned}\right.

It is easy to check that
(0,x_{1},y_{1}),(0,x_{2},y_{2})\in{\mathcal{B}}(S^{f})
. Since
x_{1}(0)=1/4=x_{2}(0)
, we see that the system does not have the uniqueness property. Obviously,
f

(5.2)

or

\langle D({\rm d}f_{t})(\xi)\zeta,\zeta\rangle\geq b_{2}\|\zeta\|^{2}\quad\forall\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\,\,\forall\,t\in T,\,\,\forall\,\xi\in\big(\mathbb{R}^{p}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,\rho)\big)\backslash N_{f_{t}},

(5.3)

then
F_{t}
is radially unbounded, locally uniformly in
t
.

Proof.

Let
T\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
be compact and let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,\rho)
. Define
\xi_{\rho}:=(\rho/\|\xi\|)\xi\in\partial\mathbb{B}(0,\rho)
and note that
[\xi_{\rho},\xi]\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,\rho)
.
By statement
(2)
of
Lemma

2.4
,

\langle Df_{t}(\xi)-Df_{t}(\xi_{\rho}),\xi-\xi_{\rho}\rangle\leq b_{1}\|\xi-\xi_{\rho}\|^{2}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,\rho),\,\,\forall\,t\in T.

or

\langle Df_{t}(\xi_{\rho})-Df_{t}(\xi),\xi-\xi_{\rho}\rangle\leq-b_{2}\|\xi-\xi_{\rho}\|^{2}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,\rho),\,\,\forall\,t\in T,

depending on whether (
5.2
) or (
5.3
) is satisfied. Setting
\varepsilon:=1-b_{1}
if (
5.2
) holds and
\varepsilon:=b_{2}-1
if (
5.3
) holds, we have that
\varepsilon>0
and it follows that

\langle F_{t}(\xi)-F_{t}(\xi_{\rho}),\xi-\xi_{\rho}\rangle\geq\varepsilon\|\xi-\xi_{\rho}\|^{2}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,\rho),\,\,\forall\,t\in T.

By hypothesis, there exists
b>0
such that
\|Df(t,\zeta)\|\leq b
for all
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}(0,\rho)
and
t\in T
, whence
\|F_{t}(\zeta)\|\leq b+\rho
for all
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}(0,\rho)
and
t\in T
. Therefore, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\backslash\mathbb{B}(0,\rho)
and all
t\in T
,

\|F_{t}(\xi)\|\geq\varepsilon\|\xi-\xi_{\rho}\|-\|F_{t}(\xi_{\rho})\|\geq\varepsilon\|\xi-\xi_{\rho}\|-(b+\rho)\geq\varepsilon\|\xi\|-\big(b+(\varepsilon+1)\rho\big),

proving the claim.
∎

A.2
Set-valued maps

Recall that a map
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is said to be closed if the image of every closed set is closed.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a function. The following statements hold.

(1)

If
g
is closed, then the set-valued map
g^{-1}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n},\,z\mapsto g^{-1}(z)

(
that is, each
z
is mapped to its fibre under
g
)
is upper semicontinuous.

(2)

If
g
is continuous and radially unbounded, then
g
is closed.

Proof.

(1)

Set
G:=g^{-1}
and let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be closed. As
g
is closed, it is a routine exercise to show
that
\Delta(G)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:g^{-1}(\xi)\not=\emptyset\}
is closed. Consequently, to establish upper
semicontinuity of
G
, it is sufficient to show that

G^{-1}(Y):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:G(\xi)\cap Y\not=\emptyset\}=\{\xi\in\Delta(G):G(\xi)\cap Y\not=\emptyset\}

is closed. Let
(\xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a convergent sequence in
G^{-1}(Y)
with limit
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We have to show that
\xi\in G^{-1}(Y)
. To this end, let
z_{k}\in G(\xi_{k})\cap Y\not=\emptyset
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. As
g(z_{k})=\xi_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that
\xi_{k}\in g(Y)
. By hypothesis,
g(Y)
is closed, and thus,
\xi\in g(Y)
. Consequently,
\xi=g(y)
for some
y\in Y
, implying that
y\in g^{-1}(\xi)=G(\xi)
and hence,
y\in G(\xi)\cap Y
. We conclude that
G(\xi)\cap Y\not=\emptyset
, showing that
\xi\in G^{-1}(Y)
.

(2)
Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be closed and let
(\xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a convergent sequence
in
g(X)
with limit
\xi
. Let
x_{k}\in X
be such that
g(x_{k})=\xi_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. As

(\xi_{k})
is bounded, so is
(x_{k})
, by the radial unboundedness of
g
. Therefore there exists
a convergent subsequence
(x_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(x_{k})
with limit
x
, where
x\in X
, by the
closedness of
X
. The function
g
is continuous, and thus,

\xi=\lim_{j\to\infty}\xi_{k_{j}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}g(x_{k_{j}})=g(x).

Hence,
\xi\in g(X)
, showing that
g(X)
is closed.
∎

A.3
General version of Gronwall’s lemma

As the version of Gronwall’s lemma which has been used in Section
4
is somewhat more general than what were able to find in the literature, we include the proof.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
0\leq t_{0}<\tau\leq\infty
,
c\geq 0
,
h\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
be non-negative and

y\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
, where
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
are such that
1/p+1/q=1
. If

y(t)\leq c+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}h(s)y(s){\rm d}s,\quad\mbox{for a.e. $t\in[t_{0},\tau)$},

then
y(t)\leq ce^{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}h(s){\rm d}s}
for almost every
t\in[t_{0},\tau)
.

Proof.

Setting
Y(t):=c+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}h(s)y(s){\rm d}s
for all
t\in[t_{0},\tau)
,
we have that
\dot{Y}(t)=h(t)y(t)\leq h(t)Y(t)
for almost every
t\in[t_{0},\tau)
, and so,

\dot{Y}(t)-h(t)Y(t)\leq 0,\quad\mbox{for a.e. $t\in[t_{0},\tau)$}.

The function
w:[t_{0},\tau)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

w(t)=Y(t)e^{-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}h(s){\rm d}s}\quad\forall\>t\in[t_{0},\tau)

is absolutely continuous and

\dot{w}(t)=\big(\dot{Y}(t)-h(t)Y(t)\big)e^{-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}h(s){\rm d}s}\leq 0,\quad\mbox{for a.e. $t\in[t_{0},\tau)$},

showing that
w
is non-increasing. Consequently,

Y(t)e^{-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}h(s){\rm d}s}=w(t)\leq w(t_{0})=Y(t_{0})=c,\quad\forall\>t\in[t_{0},\tau),

and thus,

y(t)\leq Y(t)\leq ce^{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}h(s){\rm d}s},\quad\mbox{for a.e. $t\in[t_{0},\tau)$},

completing the proof.
∎

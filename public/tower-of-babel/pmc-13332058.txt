Fifteen-year trends in cancer incidence and mortality among older adults aged 65 years and older in Martinique, a Caribbean population-based registry study, 2008–2022
Authors: Adrian Puello, Murielle Beaubrun-Renard, Jonathan Macni, Stephen Ulric-Gervaise, Manon Boullard, Mylène Vestris, Aimée Pierre-Louis, Sophia George, Moustapha Dramé, Clarisse Joachim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractProstate, breast, stomach, colon-rectum, lung, and hematologic cancers represent a regional public health burden among older adults in the Caribbean. We described 15-year trends in cancer incidence and mortality among older adults in Martinique. We conducted a population-based cohort study (2008–2022) from the population-based cancer registry of Martinique. Age-Standardized Incidence and Mortality Rates (ASIR and ASMR) were estimated using the Segi/Doll world standard population and Annual Average Percent Change (AAPC) with 95% confidence interval in patients ≥ 65 years, by tumor site and sex. We recorded 15,400 cancer cases over the entire study period (2008–2022), with 64.4% diagnosed in males. Overall ASIR remained broadly stable: in men, 2,257.8 per 100,000 (95% CI: 2,172.6-2,342.9) in 2008–2012 and 2,053.1 (95% CI: 1,982.8-2,230.4) in 2018–2022; in women, 847.8 (95% CI: 801.8-893.8) in 2008–2012 and 862.1 (95% CI: 821.2-902.9) in 2018–2022. Prostate cancer ASIR decreased from 1,355.5 per 100,000 (95% CI: 1,288.6-1,422.4) in 2008–2012 to 1,303.7 (95% CI: 1,242.0–1,365.5) in 2013–2017, and then significantly to 1,102.2 (95% CI: 1,049.6-1,154.9) in 2018–2022.Prostate cancer ASMR also declined from 293.50 per 100,000 (95% CI: 263.30-323.70) in 2008–2012 to 225.80 (95% CI: 205.00-246.50) in 2018–2022. Women breast cancer ASIR increased significantly from 177.3 per 100,000 (95% CI: 155.4-199.2) in 2008–2012 to 198.0 (95% CI: 176.5-219.6) in 2013–2017 and to 230.7 (95% CI: 208.9-252.5) in 2018–2022. Male lung and bronchial cancer incidence showed a significant AAPC decrease of -12.5% (95% CI: -23.3 to -0.13) in 2018–2022. Colorectal cancer ASMR in women remained stable: 68.2 per 100,000 (95% CI: 55.1–81.3) in 2008–2012, 63.8 (95% CI: 53.0-74.6) in 2013–2017, and 71.4 (95% CI: 60.3–82.5) in 2018–2022, and male stomach cancer AAPC decreased significantly during 2018–2022: -11.5% (95% CI: -20.1 to -1.94). Between 2008 and 2022, cancer incidence among older adults in Martinique showed decreasing prostate and lung cancer but increasing breast cancer, consistent with Caribbean and global trends. These patterns reflect screening practices, treatment advances, and COVID-19 disruptions. Future research should prioritize age-tailored cancer management and uninterrupted treatment access.Subject terms: Cancer, Diseases, Medical research, Oncology

Introduction

In 2022, an estimated 9.6 million new cases (51% of all cancer cases diagnosed, all ages combined) were diagnosed in adults aged 65 or older worldwide. In the Caribbean, an estimated 76,229 new cancer cases and 50,017 deaths occurred in adults aged 65 years or older1,2. The cancer burden among older adults in the Caribbean is driven by population aging, the shift to non-communicable diseases, and healthcare inequalities3. This is further influenced by the region’s diverse genetic backgrounds, lifestyles, environmental exposures, and varying healthcare infrastructures4. Martinique, a French Caribbean territory where older adults account for 33% of the population5,6, faces significant challenges in addressing the cancer burden among its aging population. Despite being part of France, Martinique has the second-lowest physician-to-population ratio within the country, which poses substantial barriers to accessing timely and adequate healthcare services7. In this context, a French national cancer strategic plan emphasizes addressing disparities in care for older adults, influenced by socioeconomic status, healthcare access, environmental factors, and genetics8,9.

Common cancers affecting older adults in the Caribbean region include prostate, breast, colorectal, lung, and stomach cancers10,11. Despite declines in prostate cancer incidence in high-income countries, mortality remains high in the Caribbean6. Breast cancer is the leading cause of cancer death among women, largely due to late-stage diagnoses, while colorectal cancer ranks second in mortality, particularly among those aged 65–74 8,9. Although the overall incidence of digestive cancers is lower compared with mainland France, stomach cancer shows significantly higher rates of incidence and mortality, especially in older adult men11,12. The standardized incidence ratios in men and women for stomach cancer are notably higher than in other regions of mainland France11. The reported increase in Multiple Myeloma cases in the French Caribbean territories by the French Cancer Registry Network highlights the need to analyze temporal and regional trends to uncover potential risk factors, assess healthcare access, and evaluate the impact of public health interventions13,14.

Previous studies have predominantly focused on all ages, aggregating cancer data without examining age-specific trends, overlooking the unique cancer profiles of the older adult population in the Caribbean10,15. The incidence and mortality among older adults with cancer remain disproportionately high, typically associated with disparities such as late-stage diagnosis, limited access to advanced treatment, and comorbidities16. The lack of robust and updated data on cancer incidence and mortality among older adults in the Caribbean hinders efforts to identify trends, assess risk factors, and develop tailored interventions10,16,17. Our study analyses the incidence and mortality trends among older adults in Martinique from 2008 to 2022 as the first comprehensive report for this period in the aging population.

Methods

Data sources

The Martinique population-based cancer registry, established in 1981 as one of the oldest in the French Caribbean territories, actively collaborates with a wide range of local health organizations to ensure comprehensive data collection11. This process integrates the single public hospital (Centre Hospitalier Universitaire de la Martinique) discharge reports, laboratory results, pathology findings, social security classifications of chronic diseases, and private clinical files. The Martinique cancer registry employs a combined active and passive case ascertainment process. Data extracted from these sources are systematically cross-matched and harmonized to ensure completeness, accuracy, and reliability. The registry’s 27-year operational experience prior to our study period (2008–2022) ensures data collection maturity, standardized procedures, and robust trend analysis capabilities. Mortality data were obtained from the French Epidemiological Centre on Medical Causes of Death (CepiDc), a unit of the French National Institute of Health and Medical Research (INSERM). Mortality data were coded according to the International Classification of Diseases, 10th revision (ICD-10). However, mortality data from 2012 were excluded due to officially reported inconsistencies and quality issues in the CepiDc database, rendering them unreliable for analysis.

Study design and population

This retrospective population-based study included all patients with a new invasive cancer diagnosed between January 1, 2008, and December 31, 2022, in Martinique. We analyzed 15,400 new cancer cases among patients aged ≥ 65 years. Data was provided by the registry in anonymized form, with all personal identifiers removed prior to extraction, using cancer diagnoses coded according to the World Health Organization International Classification of Diseases for Oncology (ICD-O). To provide a comprehensive overview of the cancer burden in Martinique, all registered cancer sites were included in the analysis during the study period, encompassing both the six most frequent cancer types and other recorded malignancies (e.g., bladder, kidney/renal cell, pancreatic, and uterine cancers) with the exception of non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC, C44), as presented in Fig. 1. For more specific analyses, Figs. 2, 3 and 4, and 5; Tables 1 and 2 focus on the six most frequent cancer types in Martinique: prostate (C61), Women breast (C50), colorectal (C18-C20), lung and bronchial (C33-C34), stomach (C16), and hematological malignancies, including Multiple Myeloma (C90 (9732/3)) and Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL) (C82-C85). These sites were selected for detailed analysis based on their high incidence and mortality rates during the study period.

Statistical analysis

Age-Standardized Incidence and Mortality Rates (ASIR and ASMR) were estimated using Segi/Doll the world standard population with 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) in patients ≥ 65 years, by tumor site and sex for each 5-year period (2008–2012, 2013–2017, 2018–2022) to enable comparisons of cancer rates over time, independently of the effects of age (18,19). Population denominators stratified by age and sex were obtained from the French National Institute of Statistics and Economic Studies (INSEE), available in: https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/8331297. We used quasi-Poisson regression models to address overdispersion in the data and to calculate the Annual Average Percent Change (AAPC) in incidence rates with 95% CI for the entire study period (2008–2022), as well as for 5-year intervals. The calculation was based on the formula: AAPC = (exp(β) − 1) × 100, where the 95% CI was derived from the quasi-Poisson model, incorporating an offset of log (population) to account for population size. Trends were considered statistically significant when the 95% CI for AAPC did not overlap zero. Additionally, ASIR and ASMR (per 100,000) were plotted for individual years, and trend lines were smoothed using 3-year centered moving averages to reduce year-to-year fluctuations due to small case counts20. For each year, the smoothed rate represents the average of that year and the immediately preceding and following years. Moving averages could not be calculated for the first and last years of the observation period. For annual time-series analyses, mortality rates, and period-specific calculations covering 2008–2012, the year 2012 was excluded due to data quality issues in the CepiDc database to ensure accuracy and consistency. Statistically significant AAPC values and their 95% CI are reported in the Results section and Figures. All statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software with RStudio (version 2026.01.0 + 392) and (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Results

Patient characteristics and overall trends

Between 2008 and 2022, a total of 15,400 new invasive cancer cases were diagnosed in patients aged ≥ 65 years in Martinique, involving all cancer sites excluding non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC). Of these, 9,913 cases were in men (64.4%) and 5,487 in women (35.6%), with a median of 1,027 cases reported per year. The six most frequent cancer types (prostate, Women breast, colorectal, lung and bronchial, stomach, and hematological malignancies (Multiple Myeloma + NHL) selected for detailed trend analysis accounted for 11,045 cases (71.7%) of this total burden. ASIR and ASMR trends for men and women are presented in Tables 1 and 2, and Figs. 1 and 2 and Three-year moving averages of Age Standardized rate per 100,000 for Incidence and Mortality by Year of diagnosis or death in Figs. 3, 4 and 5.

Prostate

ASIR for prostate cancer varied from a peak of approximately 1,478 per 100,000 in 2008 to approximately 1,006 per 100,000 in 2022 (Fig. 2A). The AAPC was − 2.26% (95% CI: -2.87 to -1.65) for the overall period 2008–2022 and − 3.32% (95% CI: -6.43 to -0.11) for 2018–2022 (Fig. 2A). A total of 1,416 males died from prostate cancer over the period, representing 26.3% of all diagnosed prostate cancer cases (Table 1).

Female breast cancer

Female breast cancer ASIR rose from 177.30 (95% CI: 155.37-199.22) per 100,000 person-years in 2008–2012, to 198.00 (95% CI: 176.48-219.62) in 2013–2017, and further to 230.70 (95% CI: 208.86-252.54) in 2018–2022. In contrast, female breast cancer ASMR fluctuated across periods: 60.10 (95% CI: 47.00-73.20) in 2008–2012, rising to 72.70 (95% CI: 60.10–85.30) in 2013–2017, then declining to 63.40 (95% CI: 52.70–74.20) per 100,000 in 2018–2022 (Table 2; Fig. 2B). The overall AAPC for female breast cancer incidence was 2.37% (95% CI: 1.00 to 3.76) for 2008–2022, reaching statistical significance, while the 2018–2022 AAPC of 2.65% (95% CI: -3.57 to 9.28) did not reach significance.

Colorectal cancer

Colorectal cancer ASIR remained broadly stable across the three study periods. The overlapping confidence intervals across all three periods indicate no statistically significant change in colorectal cancer incidence over the 15-year study period. Among men ASIR was 205.30 (95% CI: 180.06-230.56) per 100,000 person-years in 2008–2012, decreased slightly to 199.30 (95% CI: 176.71-221.84) in 2013–2017, and returned to 206.20 (95% CI: 184.69-227.76) in 2018–2022. Among women, colorectal cancer ASIR varied from 139.10 (95% CI: 120.76–157.50) in 2008–2012, to 125.70 (95% CI: 109.33-142.16) in 2013–2017, and 145.10 (95% CI: 128.46-161.68) in 2018–2022. Women colorectal cancer ASMR fluctuated from 68.20 (95% CI: 55.10–81.30) in 2008–2012, to 63.80 (95% CI: 53.00-74.60) in 2013–2017, and 71.40 (95% CI: 60.30–82.50) in 2018–2022, with no statistically significant trend (Tables 1 and 2; Fig. 3).

Stomach cancer

Among men, stomach cancer ASIR declined from 106.80 (95% CI: 88.86–124.71) per 100,000 person-years in 2008–2012, to 116.40 (95% CI: 99.11–133.76) in 2013–2017, and to 92.67 (95% CI: 78.65–106.70) in 2018–2022, with a significant AAPC of -11.5% (95% CI: -20.1 to -1.94) in the most recent period. Male stomach cancer ASMR also declined from 95.70 (95% CI: 76.80–114.50) in 2008–2012, to 87.30 (95% CI: 72.90–101.70) in 2013–2017, and 61.90 (95% CI: 50.60–73.20) in 2018–2022 (Table 1; Fig. 4B).

Among women, stomach cancer ASIR declined from 62.90 (95% CI: 50.85–74.94) in 2008–2012, to 48.97 (95% CI: 39.60-58.33) in 2013–2017, and 38.77 (95% CI: 30.76–46.78) in 2018–2022, with a significant AAPC of -4.38% (95% CI: -6.62 to -2.08) for the overall period 2008–2022. Women’s stomach cancer ASMR declined progressively from 36.80 (95% CI: 27.10–46.50) in 2008–2012, to 32.50 (95% CI: 25.40–39.50) in 2013–2017, and 20.80 (95% CI: 15.60–26.00) in 2018–2022 (Tables 1 and 2 and Fig. 4B).

Lung and bronchial cancer

In men, lung cancer ASIR was 65.26 (95% CI: 50.82–79.70) in 2008–2012, rose to 77.08 (95% CI: 62.94–91.22) in 2013–2017, then declined to 63.91 (95% CI: 51.51–76.31) in 2018–2022. Male ASMR declined from 92.10 (95% CI: 73.10–111.00) in 2008–2012, to 78.50 (95% CI: 64.30–92.80) in 2013–2017, and 69.40 (95% CI: 56.90–81.90) in 2018–2022.

In women, lung cancer ASIR declined from 45.54 (95% CI: 34.75–56.32) in 2008–2012, to 44.94 (95% CI: 34.91–54.97) in 2013–2017, and 38.83 (95% CI: 30.20-47.47) in 2018–2022. Women lung cancer ASMR was 35.90 (95% CI: 25.70–46.00) in 2008–2012, 35.80 (95% CI: 27.20–44.40) in 2013–2017 and increased to 43.20 (95% CI: 34.40–51.90) in 2018–2022. The male AAPC of incidence decreased significantly only in 2018–2022: -12.5% (95% CI: -23.3 to -0.13) (Tables 1 and 2; Fig. 4A).

Hematologic malignancies

Among men, Multiple Myeloma ASIR rose from 55.99 (95% CI: 42.97–69.01) in 2008–2012 to 69.50 (95% CI: 56.81–82.19) in 2018–2022, while NHL ASIR declined from 58.12 (95% CI: 44.71–71.54) to 47.76 (95% CI: 37.43–58.08). Among women, Multiple Myeloma ASIR declined from 46.52 (95% CI: 35.95–57.08) in 2008–2012 to 39.84 (95% CI: 31.21–48.48) in 2018–2022, and NHL ASIR from 42.77 (95% CI: 32.47–53.06) to 25.93 (95% CI: 18.73–33.13). Multiple Myeloma ASMR in women was 27.30 (95% CI: 18.90–35.80) in 2008–2012, 22.70 (95% CI: 16.20–29.20) in 2013–2017, and 23.50 (95% CI: 17.30–29.60) in 2018–2022. Women’s NHL ASMR was 6.70 (95% CI: 2.90–10.60) in 2008–2012, rose to 15.90 (95% CI: 10.20–21.70) in 2013–2017, then declined to 9.90 (95% CI: 5.70–14.10) in 2018–2022 (Tables 1 and 2; Fig. 5A-B).

Discussion

This population-based study examined 15-year trends in cancer incidence and mortality among adults aged 65 years and older in Martinique (2008–2022). Overall cancer incidence across all sites (excluding NMSC) remained stable in both sexes, while mortality declined significantly in men. Site-specific analyses revealed declining incidence of stomach cancer in women and prostate cancer in men, with accelerated mortality reductions in lung, bronchial, and stomach cancers in men during 2018–2022. Conversely, female breast cancer incidence rose consistently in women, and colorectal cancer showed no improvement despite established screening programs. Understanding why these patterns diverge requires examining the unique Martinique landscape in which cancer unfolds in this aging Caribbean population.

These divergent patterns cannot be understood in isolation from the healthcare landscape of Martinique, a small island where geography, infrastructure, and cultural identity converge to shape cancer outcomes. The healthcare system in Martinique shows challenges for cancer control in older adults (65 + years). As a small island of approximately 350,000 inhabitants, all specialized oncology services are centralized at the single public university hospital (Centre Hospitalier Universitaire de la Martinique), with very limited private preventive and oncology practice17. This centralization creates specific access barriers for the 65 + population and also, the island geography results in travel times exceeding 60–90 min from remote northern and southern communities. Unlike younger patients, older adults have higher rates of mobility limitations, lack of driving ability, and dependence on family caregivers for transportation, potentially leading to treatment delays, missed appointments, or suboptimal treatment completion15. Additionally, Martinique is ethnically a diverse population with individuals of African diaspora, European, and mixed ancestry, presents distinct genetic susceptibility profiles, cultural practices, and healthcare-seeking behaviors compared to mainland France21. Combined with infrastructural constraints and screening age exclusions, these factors shape the cancer patterns observed in the 65 + population22.

Against this backdrop of structural constraints, a demographic transformation has been quietly unfolding. Adults aged 65 and older in Martinique grew by 29% (from 19,618 to 25,268) between 2008 and 2022, rising from 5.0% to 7.2% of the total population and driving the growing cancer burden5. This demographic shift intersects critically with the design of organized screening programs. In mainland France, organized cancer screening programs follow European Code Against Cancer recommendations: biennial mammography for female breast cancer and biennial faecal immunochemical test for colorectal cancer, both targeting adults aged 50–74 years. Critically, these programs exclude individuals aged 75+, forcing this substantial segment of our 65 + study population to depend entirely on symptomatic presentation rather than early detection through screening21.

Our study period encompasses the COVID-19 pandemic (2020–2022), which compounded existing vulnerabilities among adults aged 65 + with cancer. In Martinique, cancer incidence declined briefly in 2020 and rebounded partially in 2021–2022, while mortality rates remained stable. This pattern mirrors pandemic-related disruptions documented worldwide, where screening rates dropped across all cancer types1,16. Beyond screening interruptions, adults aged 65 + faced compounding barriers: heightened COVID-19 mortality risk drove stricter shielding behaviours and healthcare avoidance, delaying symptomatic cancer presentations; limited digital health literacy restricted telemedicine access; and dependence on family members subject to movement restrictions further reduced transportation access2,23.

Martinique centralized CHU de la Martinique structure produced dual effects: it enabled coordinated delivery of essential therapeutic services (chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgery) for diagnosed patients, while simultaneously amplifying the impact of program interruptions, as no alternative screening sites existed when organized programs suspended operations25,26. Mortality stability despite these interruptions suggests treatment prioritization for diagnosed cases; however, diagnostic delays during 2020–2021 risk manifesting as increased late-stage presentations in subsequent years, disproportionately affecting older adults who depend on symptomatic rather than screen-based cancer detection.

When individual tumor sites are examined, our study confirms that, among older men in Martinique, prostate cancer showed favorable trends, with steadily declining incidence and progressively declining mortality across all three periods. These patterns likely reflect a shift toward more Prostate-Specific Antigen (PSA)-based detection, reducing overdiagnosis of indolent disease, alongside cumulative advances in localized and metastatic disease management, including modern radiotherapy and androgen receptor-targeted agents. Caribbean and African diaspora populations carry a higher prostate cancer burden, partly attributable to genetic susceptibility and differential access to timely care26,27; yet in Martinique, declining mortality suggests improved care pathways, including earlier stage-at-diagnosis documentation and broader treatment access.

In contrast to the promising Prostate trends, female breast cancer and Colorectal cancer tells a strikingly different story. As the leading cancer among older women in Martinique, female breast cancer incidence in Martinique among ages 65 + rose consistently throughout 2008–2022, despite the implementation of organized screening in 2004 (targeting women aged 50–74 years)22. As our study period begins four years post-implementation, beyond the typical 3–5 year window of screening-driven detection increases, this rising trend likely reflects genuine increases in underlying risk factors (obesity, physical inactivity, and hormonal factors), advances in diagnostic technology, and a shifting age distribution within the 65 + population, with growing proportions of women aged 65–74 remaining within screening eligibility while those aged 75 + fall outside program coverage21. Additionally, Martinique is the second French region with the lowest physician-to-population ratio7 with a lower screening participation (42.8% in 2021–2022) compared with mainland France (> 50%), indicating substantial unmet need, particularly among older women in remote areas facing transportation and mobility barriers. Compared with other Caribbean islands, Martinique has a higher incidence than that observed in the rest of the Caribbean but remains lower than that observed in mainland France28. These patterns are particularly concerning given Martinique has higher prevalence of triple-negative female breast cancer and lower survival rates in metastatic disease characteristics associated with African diaspora ancestry, which amplify the impact of delayed detection29,30. Ensuring comprehensive and accessible female breast cancer services for older women, including during public health crises and periods of logistical disruption, is therefore a critical priority.

Colorectal cancer demonstrated a heterogeneous pattern in the 65 + population, with rising or persistently elevated mortality contrasting with stable or moderately varying incidence. Incidence remained largely stable in men, while in women it initially declined before rising in later years. Mortality fluctuated without a consistent trend overall, with a notable late-period increase observed in women. These patterns suggest suboptimal screening coverage, uneven program participation, and inequitable treatment access in Martinique. Globally, mortality reductions in colorectal cancer are attributable to organized screening (faecal immunochemical test /colonoscopy), polypectomy, and advances in multimodal therapy31,32. Caribbean older adults remain insufficiently characterized across cancer types33. In contrast, global evidence demonstrates declining colorectal cancer incidence in older populations where organized screening is established, alongside rising incidence in younger cohorts across several regions34,35. The suboptimal outcomes observed in Martinique’s older population likely reflect multiple converging factors: limited access to early diagnosis, treatment delays, and elevated clinical frailty associated with multimorbidity, complex care needs, and social vulnerability.

Stomach cancer showed a consistent downward trend among older adults, with declining incidence in both sexes, more pronounced in women and a sharper decline in mortality than in incidence. These findings align with global trends driven by reduced Helicobacter pylori prevalence, improved food preservation, and declining smoking rates36,37, and may additionally reflect improvements in clinical protocols and access to medical and surgical treatments over the study period.

Lung and bronchial cancer is the fourth most frequent cancer in both sexes. Men experienced a mid-period (2013–2017) incidence rise, followed by a sustained mortality decline; women showed a modest incidence decline but a rise in mortality in the most recent period (2018–2022). We observed that epidemiological patterns differed markedly between men and women. In men, ASIR rose during 2013–2017 before declining in 2018–2022, while mortality declined consistently across all periods. However, in women, the incidence is flat or slightly decreasing, but mortality has risen in the most recent period (2018–2022), suggesting an increasing fatal burden.

Globally, many factors are associated with these sex-divergent trends in tobacco epidemic stages in many settings, where men’s smoking declines and mortality changes. In Martinique, smoking prevalence declined across age groups under 60 years (e.g., from 22% to 17% in 18–30 year-olds) but increased slightly among those aged 61–75 years (5.5% to 6.5%) 38. Declining male mortality further reflects therapeutic advances such as earlier stage III detection, refined surgical care, and EGFR/ALK and PD-1/PD-L1 targeted agents, that have collectively improved lung cancer survival across multiple countries14. The pandemic adds interpretive complexity here: misclassification between COVID-19 and lung cancer deaths, combined with diagnostic delays and barriers to care access, likely contributed to both the incidence dip in 2020 and the subsequent mortality patterns observed in both sexes39.

We observed a rise in Multiple Myeloma and a decline in NHL in men. Among women, Multiple Myeloma and NHL incidence both declined. Multiple Myeloma mortality remained broadly stable, while NHL mortality showed a transient rise in 2013–2017 before declining in 2018–2022. Over the past 10 years, treatments, access, and innovation in the Caribbean have advanced substantially10. Notably, Multiple Myeloma incidence has risen modestly in some regions, partly due to population aging and improved detection, while survival gains stem from innovative treatment. Factors such as age of onset and mortality differences exist among racial and ethnic minority patients diagnosed with Multiple Myeloma40. In terms of continuity of health services (diagnosis and follow-up), it is also noteworthy that although certain subgroups show incidence spikes around specific years followed by partial normalization, mortality trajectories remain flat or declining. This suggests that brief diagnostic increases (medical operational factors, short-term non-programmatic interventions) do not necessarily translate into sustained increases in death per standardized population.

Several limitations of this study merit acknowledgement. First, we did not estimate the AAPC for mortality due to missing data from 2012, which could introduce uncertainty in time-series analyses. Second, the choice of a standard population for ASIR/ASMR may obscure cohort effects; we used the world standard population recognized by institutions like the WHO and International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC). Third, registry data may be influenced by variations in diagnostic practices, turn-over of physicians and coding. During the study, organized screening programs were in place, with utilization dropping in 2020–2021 due to COVID-19 but rebounding partially in 2021–2022. These shifts likely contributed to changes in cancer incidence and stage. Short-term mortality was less impacted due to ongoing oncologic treatments. Our findings are specific to Martinique and cannot be automatically extrapolated to other Caribbean territories; nevertheless, the observed patterns provide a relevant reference framework for similarly structured small island health systems with aging populations and constrained screening resources. We recommend similar registry-linked studies across the Caribbean that consider socioeconomic factors and health metrics to confirm trends and support tailored cancer control strategies, as outlined by the IARC Caribbean Cancer Registry Hub15.

In conclusion, cancer trends among older adults in Martinique (2008–2022) broadly align with Caribbean and global observations, with differences in magnitude and timing likely reflecting screening intensity (Women breast), therapeutic advances (prostate and lung cancers), and COVID-19 related disruptions. Strengthening sex- and site-specific surveillance integrating screening programs data, and care continuity during crises may improve equitable, targeted cancer control for older adults in Martinique.

The Best Two-Term Underapproximation Using Numbers From Fibonacci-Type Sequences Thanks: The author would like to thank Dr. H. V. Chu, Department of Mathematics, Texas A&M University, for introducing the author to this project and for his feedback on the research progress.
Shiliaev Mark

Abstract
. This paper studies the greedy two-term underapproximation of θ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \theta\in(0,1] using reciprocals of numbers from a Fibonacci-type sequence ( c n ) n = 1 ∞ (c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty} . We find the set of θ \theta whose greedy two-term underapproximation is the best among all two-term underapproximations using 1 / c n 1/c_{n} ’s. We then derive a neat description of the set when ( c n ) n = 1 ∞ (c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty} is the Fibonacci sequence or the Lucas sequence.

11A67, 11B99

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\theta
be a number in
(0,1]
. An
underapproximation
of
\theta
is a series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1/a_{n}
, where
a_{n}
’s are positive integers and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1/a_{n}\leq\theta
. In this paper, we shall restrict
{a_{n}}
’s to a subset
A
of the natural numbers. In particular, a
greedy
underaproximation of
\theta
over a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is formed stepwise, where at each step, we pick the smallest number
n
in
{A}
such that

1/n
is smaller than the remainder after the previous step.
Formally, we define a function
G:(0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\mbox{ as }G(\theta)=\lfloor 1/\theta\rfloor.
We then obtain a sequence
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
recursively as follows:

c_{1}=\min\{a\in A:a>G(\theta)\}\mbox{ and }c_{n}=\min\left\{a\in A:a>G\left(\theta-\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{c_{m}}\right)\right\}.

Consequently, the greedy underapproximation of
\theta
over
A
is
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1/c_{n}

If
A=\mathbb{N}
, we get the classical greedy underapproximations, which have been studied in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
9
]
. A natural question is whether the greedy algorithm gives the best finite underapproximation
\sum_{n=1}^{m}1/c_{n},m\in\mathbb{N}
, at each step. One may focus on two-term underapproximations as in
[
5
,
7
,
9
]
, since they are the first non-trivial approximations resulting from the greedy process. Surprisingly, two-term underapproximations can be used to prove results about the long-term behavior of greedy underapproximations. For example, Kovač
[
7
]
proved that the Lebesgue measure of all real numbers whose finite greedy underapproximations, are eventually the best is 0.

If
A
consists of powers of 2, then the greedy process would give the best finite underapproximation out of all finite underapproximations using integers from
A
at every step, due to how sparse the set
A
is. We would like to know what happens if
A
is less dense than
\mathbb{N}
but denser than the powers of 2. A good candidate for an investigation is the Fibonacci sequence
(F_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
defined as:
F_{0}=0
,
F_{1}=1
,
F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}
for
n\geq 2
. We shall consider more general integer sequences
(a_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfying:

a)

a_{0}>0
,
a_{1}\geq 1
,
a_{0}\leq a_{1}
,

b)

a_{n}=a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}
for
n\geq 2
,

c)

\chi:=a_{0}^{2}+a_{1}a_{0}-a_{1}^{2}>0
.

From Condition a) and b), we obtain

1\leq a_{1}\ \ <\ a_{2}\ <\ a_{3}\ <\ a_{4}\ <\ \cdots.

For
\theta\in(0,1]
, let
g_{1}(\theta)
be the smallest index
n\geq 1
such that

\frac{1}{a_{g_{1}(\theta)}}\ <\ \theta.

Let
g_{2}(\theta)
be the smallest index
n\geq g_{1}(\theta)
such that

\frac{1}{a_{g_{2}(\theta)}}\ <\ \theta-\frac{1}{a_{g_{1}(\theta)}}.

Set

\mathcal{G}(\theta)\ :=\ \frac{1}{a_{g_{1}(\theta)}}+\frac{1}{a_{g_{2}(\theta)}},

called the greedy two-term underapproximation of
\theta
using terms from the sequence
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\ldots
.
Our goal is to determine when
\mathcal{G}(\theta)
is the best two-term underapproximation of
\theta
out of all possible underapproximations from the collection

\mathcal{A}(\theta,2)\ =\ \left\{\frac{1}{a_{m}}+\frac{1}{a_{n}}\,:\,1\leq m\leq n\mbox{ and }\frac{1}{a_{m}}+\frac{1}{a_{n}}<\theta\right\}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
.
The greedy two-term underapproximation
\mathcal{G}(\theta)
is the best underapproximation out of
\mathcal{A}(\theta,2)
if and only if

\theta\ \notin\ \bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2n+4}},\frac{1}{a_{2n+2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2n+3+\xi(n)}}\right],

where
\xi(n)
is the largest nonnegative integer such that

a_{2n+2}F_{\xi(n)}+a_{2n+3}F_{\xi(n)+1}\ \leq\ \frac{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}a_{2n+4}}{a_{0}^{2}+a_{1}a_{0}-a_{1}^{2}}.

(1.1)

Remark 1.2
.

The function
\xi:\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
is well-defined because for
n\geq 0

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}a_{2n+4}}{a_{0}^{2}+a_{1}a_{0}-a_{1}^{2}}\ \ >\ \frac{a_{1}a_{2n+3}a_{3}}{a_{0}^{2}+a_{1}a_{0}-a_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{a_{1}(2a_{1}+a_{0})a_{2n+3}}{a_{0}^{2}+a_{1}a_{0}-a_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{(2a^{2}_{1}+a_{1}a_{0})a_{2n+3}}{a_{0}^{2}+a_{1}a_{0}-a_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\ >\ a_{2n+3}\ =\ a_{2n+2}F_{0}+a_{2n+3}F_{1}.

This paper is structured as follows: Section 2 outlines all preliminary results, Section 3 proves Theorem 1.1, while Section 4 applies it to some sequences to find a simpler way of defining
\theta(n)
for them.

2.
Preliminary results

Lemma 2.1
.

For
m,n\geq 0
, we have

a_{n+m}\ =\ F_{n-1}a_{m}+F_{n}a_{m+1}.

Proof.

Recall the well-known identity, whose proof is in the Appendix:

F_{n+m}\ =\ F_{n-1}F_{m}+F_{n}F_{m+1},\mbox{ for all }m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.1)

Due to the recurrence relation of
(a_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
, we have

a_{n}\ =\ a_{0}F_{n-1}+a_{1}F_{n},\mbox{ for all }n\geq 0,

(2.2)

which can be easily proved by induction. Choose
m,n\geq 0
. By (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),

\displaystyle a_{n+m}

\displaystyle\ =\ a_{0}F_{n+m-1}+a_{1}F_{n+m}

\displaystyle\ =\ a_{0}(F_{n-1}F_{m-1}+F_{n}F_{m})+a_{1}(F_{n-1}F_{m}+F_{n}F_{m+1})

\displaystyle\ =\ F_{n-1}(a_{0}F_{m-1}+a_{1}F_{m})+F_{n}(a_{0}F_{m}+a_{1}F_{m+1})

\displaystyle\ =\ a_{m}F_{n-1}+a_{m+1}F_{n}.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

a_{n}a_{n+3}-a_{n+1}a_{n+2}\ =\ (-1)^{n}\chi.

(2.3)

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. Base case: for
n=0
,

a_{0}a_{3}-a_{1}a_{2}\ =\ a_{0}(2a_{1}+a_{0})-a_{1}(a_{0}+a_{1})\ =\ a^{2}_{0}+a_{0}a_{1}-a_{1}^{2}\ =\ \chi.

Inductive hypothesis: suppose that (
2.3
) holds for
n=k
for some
k\geq 0
. We have

\displaystyle a_{k+1}a_{k+4}-a_{k+2}a_{k+3}

\displaystyle\ =\ a_{k+1}(a_{k+3}+a_{k+2})-(a_{k+1}+a_{k})a_{k+3}

\displaystyle\ =\ -(a_{k}a_{k+3}-a_{k+1}a_{k+2})

\displaystyle\ =\ -(-1)^{k}\chi\ =\ (-1)^{k+1}\chi.

This completes our proof.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

For
n\geq 0
,
\xi(n)
is the largest nonnegative integer such that

\frac{1}{a_{2n+3+\xi(n)}}\ \geq\ \frac{\chi}{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}a_{2n+4}}.

(2.4)

Equivalently,

\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2n+4}}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{a_{2n+2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2n+3+\xi(n)}}.

(2.5)

Proof.

To prove (
2.4
), we use (
1.1
) and Lemma
2.1
. To prove (
2.5
), use (
2.3
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2n+4}}\ =\ \frac{1}{a_{2n+4}}-\frac{a_{2n+1}}{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}-a_{2n+1}a_{2n+4}}{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}a_{2n+4}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{\chi}{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}a_{2n+4}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \frac{1}{a_{2n+3+\xi(n)}}.

∎

Corollary 2.4
.

For
n\geq 0
,

\frac{1}{a_{2n+1}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+2}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}\ >\ 0.

Proof.

By (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{2n+1}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+2}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{a_{2n+2}-a_{2n+1}}{a_{2n+1}a_{2n+2}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}-a_{2n+1}a_{2n+3}-a_{2n+1}a_{2n+2}}{a_{2n+1}a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{a_{2n}a_{2n+3}-a_{2n+1}a_{2n+2}}{a_{2n+1}a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{\chi}{a_{2n+1}a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}}\ >\ 0.

∎

Corollary 2.5
.

For
n\geq 0
,

\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2n+4}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+2}}\ >\ 0.

Proof.

From the proof of Corollary
2.3
,

\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2n+4}}\ =\ \frac{\chi}{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}a_{2n+4}}\ >\ 0.

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For
n\geq 0
,

\frac{1}{a_{2n+2}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+5}}\ >\ 0.

Proof.

Write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{2n+2}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+5}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{a_{2n+1}}{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+5}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{a_{2n+1}a_{2n+5}-a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}}{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}a_{2n+5}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{a_{2n+1}(a_{2n+3}+a_{2n+4})-(a_{2n+1}+a_{2n})a_{2n+3}}{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}a_{2n+5}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{a_{2n+1}a_{2n+4}-a_{2n}a_{2n+3}}{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}a_{2n+5}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{a_{2n+1}a_{2n+2}+a_{2n+3}(a_{2n+1}-a_{2n})}{a_{2n+2}a_{2n+3}a_{2n+5}}>0.

\frac{1}{a_{2n+2}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+3}}-\frac{1}{a_{2n+5}}\ >\ 0,

as desired.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose that for some
m\geq 0
, we have

\frac{1}{a_{2m+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+4}}\ <\ \theta\ \leq\ \frac{1}{a_{2m+2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+3+\xi(m)}}.

(3.1)

We shall show that
\mathcal{G}(\theta)
is not the best two-term underapproximation out of
A(\theta,2)
.

By Corollaries
2.4
and
2.5
,

\frac{1}{a_{2m+2}}\ <\ \frac{1}{a_{2m+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+4}}\ <\ \theta\ \leq\ \frac{1}{a_{2m+2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+3+\xi(m)}}\ <\ \frac{1}{a_{2m+1}}.

Hence,
g_{1}(\theta)=2m+2
. It then follows from (
3.1
) and Corollary
2.3
that

\mathcal{G}(\theta)\ \leq\ \frac{1}{a_{2m+2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+4+\xi(m)}}\ <\ \frac{1}{a_{2m+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+4}}\ <\ \theta,

so
\mathcal{G}(\theta)
is not the best two-term underapproximation.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathcal{G}(\theta)
is not the best two-term underapproximation of
\theta
. Let
g_{1}(\theta)=k
. If
k
is odd, then Corollary
2.4
gives

\frac{1}{a_{k}}\ >\ \frac{1}{a_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{a_{k+2}},

making
\mathcal{G}(\theta)
the best. Hence,
k
must be even, i.e.,
k=2m+2
for some
m\geq 0
. Since
\mathcal{G}(\theta)
is not the best, there are
\ell_{2}>\ell_{1}>2m+2
such that

\frac{1}{a_{2m+2}}\ <\ \mathcal{G}(\theta)\ <\ \frac{1}{a_{\ell_{1}}}+\frac{1}{a_{\ell_{2}}}\ <\ \theta.

(3.2)

By Lemma
2.6
,

\frac{1}{a_{2m+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+5}}\ <\ \frac{1}{a_{2m+2}}\ <\ \frac{1}{a_{\ell_{1}}}+\frac{1}{a_{\ell_{2}}}\ <\ \theta.

It follows that
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=(2m+3,2m+4)
, and

\frac{1}{a_{2m+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+4}}\ <\ \theta.

By Corollary
2.3
,

\frac{1}{a_{2m+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+4}}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{a_{2m+2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+3+\xi(m)}}.

(3.3)

If

\theta>a^{-1}_{2m+2}+a^{-1}_{2m+3+\xi(m)}
, then

\frac{1}{a_{\ell_{1}}}+\frac{1}{a_{\ell_{2}}}\ =\ \frac{1}{a_{2m+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+4}}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{a_{2m+2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+3+\xi(m)}}\ \leq\ \mathcal{G}(\theta).

This contradicts (
3.2
). Therefore,

\theta\in\left(\frac{1}{a_{2m+3}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+4}},\frac{1}{a_{2m+2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2m+3+\xi(m)}}\right].

∎

4.
Application to some sequences

The goal of this section is to apply (
1.1
) to specific sequences. The first one is going to the Fibonacci sequence
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Let

a_{n}=F_{n+1}
then it is easy to see that
(a_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfies all conditions.

Proposition 4.1
.

The greedy two-term underapproximation
\mathcal{G}(\theta)
is the best underapproximation out of
\mathcal{A}(\theta,2)
if and only if

\theta\ \notin\ \bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{F_{2n+4}}+\frac{1}{F_{2n+5}},\frac{1}{F_{2n+3}}+\frac{1}{F_{6n+8}}\right],

Proof.

We want to show that if
\xi(n)
is the largest nonnegative integer such that

F_{2n+3}F_{\xi(n)}+F_{2n+4}F_{\xi(n)+1}\ \leq\ {F_{2n+3}F_{2n+4}F_{2n+5}}.

then
\xi(n)=4n+4

Using the Binet’s formula, we have

\displaystyle F_{2n+3}F_{2n+4}F_{2n+5}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{(\varphi^{2n+3}-\varphi^{-2n-3})(\varphi^{2n+4}-\varphi^{-2n-4})(\varphi^{2n+5}-\varphi^{-2n-5})}{5\sqrt{5}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{\varphi^{6n+12}-\varphi^{-6n-12}-(\varphi^{2n+6}-\varphi^{-2n-6}+\varphi^{2n+4}-\varphi^{-2n-4}+\varphi^{2n+2}-\varphi^{-2n-2})}{5\sqrt{5}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{F_{6n+12}-F_{2n+6}-F_{2n+4}-F_{2n+2}}{5}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{F_{6n+12}-F_{2n+6}-F_{2n+5}+F_{2n+1}}{5}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{3F_{6n+9}+2F_{6n+8}-F_{2n+7}+F_{2n+1}}{5}

By (
2.1
)

F_{2n+3}F_{\xi(n)}+F_{2n+4}F_{\xi(n)+1}=F_{2n+4+\xi(n)}.

So now it’s enough to prove that

F_{6n+8}\leq\frac{3F_{6n+9}+2F_{6n+8}-F_{2n+7}+F_{2n+1}}{5}<F_{6n+9}.

Which is true because

\displaystyle F_{6n+8}<\frac{2F_{6n+9}+3F_{6n+8}}{5}

\displaystyle\ <\ \frac{3F_{6n+9}+2F_{6n+8}-F_{2n+7}+F_{2n+1}}{5}

\displaystyle\ <\ \frac{4F_{6n+9}+F_{6n+8}}{5}

\displaystyle\ <\ F_{6n+9}

∎

Next, consider the Lucas sequence
(L_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
is defined as
L_{0}=2,L_{1}=1,L_{n}=L_{n-1}+L_{n-2}
where
n\geq 2
. Set
a_{n}=L_{n+2}
. Then it is easy to see that
(a_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfies all conditions. So we will be working with
(L_{n})_{n=2}^{\infty}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The greedy two-term underapproximation
\mathcal{G}(\theta)
is the best underapproximation out of
\mathcal{A}(\theta,2)
if and only if

\theta\ \notin\ \bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{L_{2n+5}}+\frac{1}{L_{2n+6}},\frac{1}{L_{2n+4}}+\frac{1}{L_{6n+11}}\right].

Proof.

We want to show that if
\xi(n)
is the largest nonnegative integer such that

L_{2n+4}F_{\xi(n)}+L_{2n+5}F_{\xi(n)+1}\ \leq\ \frac{L_{2n+4}L_{2n+5}L_{2n+6}}{5},

then

\xi(n)=4n+6.

The proof is similar to the proof of Proposition 4.1:

\displaystyle\frac{L_{2n+4}L_{2n+5}L_{2n+6}}{5}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{(\varphi^{2n+4}+\varphi^{-2n-4})(\varphi^{2n+5}+\varphi^{-2n-5})(\varphi^{2n+6}+\varphi^{-2n-6})}{5}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{L_{6n+15}+L_{2n+7}+L_{2n+5}+L_{2n+3}}{5}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{L_{6n+15}+L_{2n+8}-L_{2n+2}}{5}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{3L_{6n+12}+2L_{6n+11}+L_{2n+8}-L_{2n+2}}{5}.

By (
2.1
),

L_{2n+4}F_{\xi(n)}+L_{2n+5}F_{\xi(n)+1}\ =\ L_{2n+4+\xi(n)}.

So we need to check that:

L_{6n+11}\leq\frac{3L_{6n+12}+2L_{6n+11}+L_{2n+8}-L_{2n+2}}{5}<L_{6n+12},

which is true because

\displaystyle L_{6n+11}<\frac{2L_{6n+12}+3L_{6n+11}}{5}

\displaystyle\ <\ \frac{3L_{6n+12}+2L_{6n+11}+L_{2n+8}-L_{2n+2}}{5}

\displaystyle\ <\ \frac{4L_{6n+12}+L_{6n+11}}{5}

\displaystyle\ <\ L_{6n+12}.

∎

5.
Further investigation

Inspired by
[
7
]
, one possible direction would be to find the Lebesgue measure of all real numbers whose finite greedy underapproximations
\sum_{i=1}^{m}1/F_{n_{i}},m\in\mathbb{N},
are eventually the best among all using reciprocals of Fibonacci numbers.

We would like to investigate the greedy two-term underapproximations using terms from a sequence
(c_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
, where
(c_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfies
c_{n}=ac_{n-1}+bc_{n-2},\mbox{ for }a,b\in\mathbb{N}
or a recurrence of higher order. One of the difficulties in solving this problem is how involved the algebra becomes without nice equations as in Lemma 2.2.

6.
Appendix

Proof of (
2.1
).

Fix
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. We induct on
n
. Base cases: for
n=0
,

F_{-1}F_{m}+F_{0}F_{m+1}\ =\ F_{m}\ =\ F_{0+m};

for
n=1
,

F_{0}F_{m}+F_{1}F_{m+1}\ =\ F_{1+m}.

Inductive hypothesis: suppose that (
2.1
) holds for
n=k\geq 1
. We have

\displaystyle F_{k+1+m}\ =\ F_{k+m}+F_{(k-1)+m}

\displaystyle\ =\ (F_{k-1}F_{m}+F_{k}F_{m+1})+(F_{k-2}F_{m}+F_{k-1}F_{m+1})

\displaystyle\ =\ (F_{k-1}+F_{k-2})F_{m}+(F_{k}+F_{k-1})F_{m+1}

\displaystyle\ =\ F_{k}F_{m}+F_{k+1}F_{m+1}.

Therefore, (
2.1
) holds for all
n\geq 0
.

We prove (
2.1
) for
n<0
.
Inductive hypothesis: suppose that (
2.1
) holds for all
n\geq k
for some
k\leq 0
. We have

\displaystyle F_{k-1+m}\ =\ F_{k+1+m}-F_{k+m}

\displaystyle\ =\ (F_{k}F_{m}+F_{k+1}F_{m+1})-(F_{k-1}F_{m}+F_{k}F_{m+1})

\displaystyle\ =\ (F_{k}-F_{k-1})F_{m}+(F_{k+1}-F_{k})F_{m+1}

\displaystyle\ =\ F_{k-2}F_{m}+F_{k-1}F_{m+1}.

This completes our proof.
∎

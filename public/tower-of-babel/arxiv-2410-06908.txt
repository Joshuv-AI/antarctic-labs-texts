Ivan Gadjev 1, Parvan Parvanov 2, Rumen Uluchev 3

Abstract
. Here we study the approximation properties of a modified Goodman-Sharma operator recently considered by Acu and Agrawal in [ 1 ] . This operator is linear but not positive. It has the advantage of a higher order of approximation of functions compared with the Goodman-Sharma operator. We prove direct and strong converse theorems in terms of a related K-functional.

Introduction

In 1987, W. Chen and idenpendently T. N. T. Goodman and A. Sharma presented at conferences
in China and Bulgaria, respectively a new modification of the classical Bernstein operators.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and functions
f(x)\in C[0,1]
they introduce the linear operator
(see
[
5
]
and
[
9
,
10
]
):

(1.1)

\ U_{n}(f,x)=f(0)P_{n,0}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\!\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\!(n-1)P_{n-2,k-1}(t)f(t)\,dt\Big)P_{n,k}(x)+f(1)P_{n,n}(x),

where

(1.2)

P_{n,k}(x)=\binom{n}{k}x^{k}(1-x)^{n-k},\qquad k=0,\ldots,n.

Operators of this kind were investigated by many authors (see
[
14
]
,
[
4
]
,

[
13
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
7
,
8
]
,
[
2
]
, etc.) and are
generally known as genuine Bernstein-Durrmeyer operators. Note that the operators in (
1.1
) are
actually a limit case of Bernstein type operators with Jacobi weights studied by Berens and
Xu
[
3
]
.

If we set

u_{n,k}(f)=\begin{cases}f(0),&k=0,\\
(n-1)\int_{0}^{1}P_{n-2,k-1}(t)f(t)\,dt,&k=1,\ldots,n-1,\\
f(1),&k=n,\end{cases}

the operators defined in (
1.1
) take the form

U_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}u_{n,k}(f)P_{n,k}(x)\qquad\text{or}\qquad U_{n}f=\sum_{k=0}^{n}u_{n,k}(f)P_{n,k}.

Let us denote, as usual, by

\varphi(x)=x(1-x)

the weight function which is naturally connected to the second derivative of the Bernstein operator.
Also, we set

(1.3)

\widetilde{D}f(x):=\varphi(x)f^{\prime\prime}(x)

and

\widetilde{D}^{2}f:=\widetilde{D}\widetilde{D}f,\qquad\widetilde{D}^{\ell+1}f:=\widetilde{D}\widetilde{D}^{\ell}f,\qquad\ell=2,3\ldots\,.

Recently, Acu and Agrawal
[
1
]
studied a family of Bernstein-Durrmeyer operators, as they modify
U_{n}f
by replacing the Bernstein basis polynomials
P_{n,k}
with linear combinations
of Bernstein basis polynomials of lower degree with coefficients which are polynomials of
appropriate degree. For special choice of the parameters, these operators lack the positivity but
have a higher than
O(n^{-1})
order of approximation. For example, Acu and Agrawal considered
operators with
O(n^{-2})
and
O(n^{-3})
rate of approximation, see
[
1
, Section 3]
.

The results presented in
[
1
]
inspired the authors of the current paper to explore in
more depth the operators explicitly defined by

(1.4)

\widetilde{U}_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}u_{n,k}(f)\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x),\qquad x\in[0,1],

where

(1.5)

\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)=P_{n,k}(x)-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}P_{n,k}(x).

By defining an appropriate K-functional, we prove direct and strong converse inequality of type B in terminology of
[
6
]
. In order to state our main result, we need some definitions.

Let
L_{\infty}[0,1]
be the space of all Lebesgue measurable and essentially bounded functions in

[0,1]
and
AC_{loc}(0,1)
consists of the functions absolutely continuous in any subinterval

[a,b]\subset(0,1)
. Let us set

W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]:=\big\{g\,:\,g,g^{\prime}\in AC_{loc}(0,1),\ \widetilde{D}g\in L_{\infty}[0,1]\big\}.

By
W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
we denote the subspace of
W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]
of functions
g
satisfying the additional boundary conditions

\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\widetilde{D}g=0,\qquad\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}\widetilde{D}g=0.

Henceforth, by
\|\cdot\|
we mean the uniform norm on the interval
[0,1]
. For functions

f\in C[0,1]
and
t>0
we define the K-functional

(1.6)

K(f,t):=\inf\big\{\|f-g\|+t\|\widetilde{D}^{2}g\|\,:\,g\,\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1],\,\widetilde{D}g\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]\big\}.

Here we investigate the error of approximation of functions
f\in C[0,1]
by the modified
Goodman-Sharma operator (
1.4
). Our main results read as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 2
, and
f\in C[0,1]
, then

\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\big\|\leq(1+\sqrt{3})\,K\Big(f,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big).

Theorem 1.2
.

For every function
f\in C[0,1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 2
, there exist constants
C,L>0

such that

K\Big(f,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\leq C\,\frac{\ell^{2}}{n^{2}}\big(\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\big\|+\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}f-f)\big\|\big).

for all
\ell\geq Ln
.

Remark
. Another way to state Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
is: there exists a natural number
k
such that

K\Big(f,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\sim\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\big\|+\big\|\widetilde{U}_{kn}f-f\big\|.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 1 state of the art is described. Preliminary and
auxiliary results are presented in Section 2. Section 3 includes an estimation of the norm of
the operator
\widetilde{U}_{n}
, a Jackson type inequality and a proof of the direct inequality in
Theorem
1.1
. The last Section 4 is devoted to a converse result for the modified
Goodman-Sharma operator (
1.4
). Inequalities of the Voronovskaya type and Bernstein type
for
\widetilde{U}_{n}
are proved using the differential operator
\widetilde{D}
, defined in (
1.3
).
Theorem
1.2
represents a strong converse inequality of Type B, according to Ditzian-Ivanov
classification in
[
6
]
. Complete proof of the converse theorem is given.

2.
Preliminaries and Auxiliary Results

By
B_{n}f
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote the Bernstein operators determined for functions
f
,

B_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}f\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)P_{n,k}(x),\qquad x\in[0,1],

where
P_{n,k}
are the Bernstein basis polynomials (
1.2
). The Bernstein operators central
moments play important role in many applications and they are defined by

\mu_{n,i}(x)=B_{n}\big((t-x)^{i},x\big)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big)^{i}P_{n,k}(x),\qquad i=0,1,\ldots\,.

We summarize some well known useful properties of the Bernstein polynomials. Further on we assume

P_{n,k}:=0
if
k<0
or
k>n
.

Proposition 2.1

(see, e.g.
[
12
]
)
.

(a) The following identities are valid:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}kP_{n,k}(x)=nx,\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{n}(n-k)P_{n,k}(x)=n(1-x),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}k(k-1)P_{n,k}(x)=n(n-1)x^{2},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(n-k)(n-k-1)P_{n,k}(x)=n(n-1)(1-x)^{2},

(2.4)

\displaystyle P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)=n\big[P_{n-1,k-1}(x)-P_{n-1,k}(x)\big],

(2.5)

\displaystyle P_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)=n(n-1)\big[P_{n-2,k-2}(x)-2P_{n-2,k-1}(x)+P_{n-2,k}(x)\big].

(b) For the low-order moments
\mu_{n,i}(x)
we have:

\displaystyle\mu_{n,0}(x)

\displaystyle=B_{n}\big((t-x)^{0},x\big)=1,

\displaystyle\mu_{n,1}(x)

\displaystyle=B_{n}\big((t-x),x\big)=0,

\displaystyle\mu_{n,2}(x)

\displaystyle=B_{n}\big((t-x)^{2},x\big)=\frac{\varphi(x)}{n},

\displaystyle\mu_{n,3}(x)

\displaystyle=B_{n}\big((t-x)^{3},x\big)=\frac{(1-2x)\varphi(x)}{n^{2}},

\displaystyle\mu_{n,4}(x)

\displaystyle=B_{n}\big((t-x)^{4},x\big)=\frac{3(n-2)\varphi^{2}(x)}{n^{3}}+\frac{\varphi(x)}{n^{3}}.

The operators
U_{n}
,
\widetilde{U}_{n}
and the differential operator
\widetilde{D}
satisfy interesting properties.

Proposition 2.2
.

If the operators
U_{n}
,
\widetilde{U}_{n}
and the differential operator
\widetilde{D}
are defined as in
(
1.1
), (
1.4
) and (
1.3
), respectively, then

(a)
\widetilde{D}U_{n}f=U_{n}\widetilde{D}f
for
f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
;

(b)
\widetilde{U}_{n}f=U_{n}\big(f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}f\big)
for
f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
;

(c)
\widetilde{D}\widetilde{U}_{n}f=\widetilde{U}_{n}\widetilde{D}f
for
f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
;

(d)
U_{n}\widetilde{U}_{n}f=\widetilde{U}_{n}U_{n}f
for
f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
;

(e)
\widetilde{U}_{m}\widetilde{U}_{n}f=\widetilde{U}_{n}\widetilde{U}_{m}f
for
f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
;

(f)
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{U}_{n}f=f
for
f\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]
;

(g)
\displaystyle\big\|\widetilde{D}U_{n}f\big\|\leq\big\|\widetilde{D}f\big\|
for
f\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]
.

Proof.

For the proof of (a), see
[
14
, Lemma 4.2]
.

We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{n}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}u_{n,k}(f)\widetilde{P}_{n,k}

\displaystyle=u_{n,0}(f)\Big(P_{n,0}-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}P_{n,0}\Big)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}u_{n,k}(f)\Big(P_{n,k}-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}P_{n,k}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+u_{n,n}(f)\Big(P_{n,n}-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}P_{n,n}\Big)

\displaystyle=u_{n,0}(f)P_{n,0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}u_{n,k}(f)P_{n,k}+u_{n,n}(f)P_{n,n}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\varphi}{n}\bigg(u_{n,0}(f)P_{n,0}^{\prime\prime}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}u_{n,k}(f)P_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}+u_{n,n}(f)P_{n,n}^{\prime\prime}\bigg)

\displaystyle=U_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\varphi U_{n}^{\prime\prime}f.

Then from (a) we obtain

\widetilde{U}_{n}f=U_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}U_{n}f=U_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,U_{n}\widetilde{D}f=U_{n}\Big(f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}f\Big)

which proves (b).

Now, commutative properties (c) and (d) follow from (b) and (a):

\widetilde{D}\widetilde{U}_{n}f=\widetilde{D}U_{n}\Big(f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}f\Big)=U_{n}\Big(\widetilde{D}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}\widetilde{D}f\Big)=\widetilde{U}_{n}(\widetilde{D}f),

and

\displaystyle U_{n}\widetilde{U}_{n}f

\displaystyle=U_{n}U_{n}\Big(f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}f\Big)=U_{n}U_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,U_{n}U_{n}\widetilde{D}f

\displaystyle=U_{n}U_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,U_{n}\widetilde{D}U_{n}f=\widetilde{U}_{n}U_{n}f.

The operators
\widetilde{U}_{n}
commute in the sense of (e), since

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{m}\widetilde{U}_{n}f

\displaystyle=\widetilde{U}_{m}U_{n}\big(f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}f\big)

\displaystyle=U_{m}U_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,U_{m}U_{n}\widetilde{D}f-\frac{1}{m}\,\widetilde{D}U_{m}U_{n}f+\frac{1}{mn}\,U_{m}\widetilde{D}^{2}U_{n}f

\displaystyle=U_{m}U_{n}\Big(f-\frac{m+n}{mn}\,\widetilde{D}f+\frac{1}{mn}\,\widetilde{D}^{2}f\Big).

The same expression on the right-hand side we obtain for
\widetilde{U}_{n}\widetilde{U}_{m}f
because of properties
(a), (b) and
U_{m}U_{n}f=U_{n}U_{m}f
.

We recall two more properties of the operator
U_{n}
and function
f\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]
(see
[
14
, eqs. (4.8), (2.4)]
):

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|U_{n}f-f\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\,\big\|\widetilde{D}f\big\|,

\displaystyle\big\|U_{n}\widetilde{D}f\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\widetilde{D}f\big\|.

Therefore

\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\|=\Big\|U_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,U_{n}\widetilde{D}f-f\Big\|\leq\|U_{n}f-f\|+\frac{1}{n}\,\big\|U_{n}\widetilde{D}f\big\|\leq\frac{2}{n}\,\|\widetilde{D}f\|,

hence
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\|=0
, i.e. the limit (f) holds true.

From the proof of Lemma 4.2 in
[
14
]
for every
g\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]
we have

\widetilde{D}U_{n}g(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}P_{n,k}(x)\int_{0}^{1}(n-1)P_{n-2,k-1}(t)\widetilde{D}g(t)\,dt,

From the above representation we obtain

|\widetilde{D}U_{n}g(x)|\leq\big\|\widetilde{D}g\big\|\,\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}P_{n,k}(x)\int_{0}^{1}(n-1)P_{n-2,k-1}(t)\,dt\leq\big\|\widetilde{D}g\big\|,

which proves (g).
∎

We now introduce a function that will prove useful in our investigations:

(2.7)

\displaystyle T_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle:=k(k-1)\frac{1-x}{x}-2k(n-k)+(n-k)(n-k-1)\frac{x}{1-x}

\displaystyle=n\bigg[-1-\frac{1-2x}{\varphi(x)}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big)+\frac{n}{\varphi(x)}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big)^{2}\bigg].

Observe that

(2.8)

\displaystyle T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{k(k-1)}{x^{2}}+\frac{(n-k)(n-k-1)}{(1-x)^{2}},

(2.9)

\displaystyle T_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2k(k-1)}{x^{3}}+\frac{2(n-k)(n-k-1)}{(1-x)^{3}}>0,\qquad x\in(0,1).

Proposition 2.3
.

(a) The following relation concerning
P_{n,k}
,
T_{n,k}
and differential operator
\widetilde{D}
holds:

(2.10)

\widetilde{D}P_{n,k}(x)=T_{n,k}(x)P_{n,k}(x).

(b) If
\,\alpha
is an arbitrary real number, then

\Phi(\alpha):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\alpha-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\Big)^{2}P_{n,k}(x)=\alpha^{2}+2-\frac{2}{n}.

Proof.

(a) From (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and

\varphi(x)P_{n,k}(x)=\frac{(k+1)(n-k+1)}{(n+1)(n+2)}\,P_{n+2,k+1}(x),

it follows that

\displaystyle\varphi(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=n(n-1)\big[\varphi(x)P_{n-2,k-2}(x)-2\varphi(x)P_{n-2,k-1}(x)+\varphi(x)P_{n-2,k}(x)\big]

\displaystyle=n(n-1)\Big[\frac{(k-1)(n-k+1)}{n(n-1)}\,P_{n,k-1}(x)-2\,\frac{k(n-k)}{n(n-1)}\,P_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle\hskip 159.33542pt+\frac{(k+1)(n-k-1)}{n(n-1)}\,P_{n,k+1}(x)\Big]

\displaystyle=(k-1)(n-k+1)\,P_{n,k-1}(x)-2k(n-k)\,P_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+(k+1)(n-k-1)\,P_{n,k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\Big[k(k-1)\frac{1-x}{x}-2k(n-k)+(n-k)(n-k-1)\frac{x}{1-x}\Big]P_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle=T_{n,k}(x)P_{n,k}(x),

i.e. the identity (
2.10
).

(b) We apply the formulae for the Bernstein operator moments in Proposition
2.1
(b):

\displaystyle\Phi(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big[\alpha+1+\frac{1-2x}{\varphi(x)}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big)-\frac{n}{\varphi(x)}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big)^{\!2}\Big]^{2}P_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big[(\alpha+1)^{2}+\frac{(1-2x)^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(x)}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big)^{2}+\frac{n^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(x)}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big)^{4}+\frac{2(\alpha+1)(1-2x)}{\varphi(x)}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 45.5244pt-\frac{2(\alpha+1)n}{\varphi(x)}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big)^{2}-\frac{2n(1-2x)}{\varphi^{2}(x)}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big)^{3}\Big]P_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle=(\alpha+1)^{2}\mu_{n,0}(x)+\frac{(1-2x)^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(x)}\,\mu_{n,2}(x)+\frac{n^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(x)}\,\mu_{n,4}(x)+\frac{2(\alpha+1)(1-2x)}{\varphi(x)}\,\mu_{n,1}(x)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\frac{2(\alpha+1)n}{\varphi(x)}\,\mu_{n,2}(x)-\frac{2n(1-2x)}{\varphi^{2}(x)}\,\mu_{n,3}(x)

\displaystyle=(\alpha+1)^{2}\cdot 1+\frac{(1-2x)^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(x)}\,\frac{\varphi(x)}{n}+\frac{n^{2}}{\varphi^{2}(x)}\,\frac{(3n-6)\varphi^{2}(x)+\varphi(x)}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{2(\alpha+1)(1-2x)}{\varphi(x)}\cdot 0-\frac{2(\alpha+1)n}{\varphi(x)}\,\frac{\varphi(x)}{n}-\frac{2n(1-2x)}{\varphi^{2}(x)}\,\frac{(1-2x)\varphi(x)}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle=(\alpha+1)^{2}+\frac{1-4\varphi(x)}{n\varphi(x)}+\frac{(3n-6)\varphi(x)+1}{n\varphi(x)}-2(\alpha+1)-\frac{2(1-4\varphi(x))}{n\varphi(x)}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}+2\alpha+1+\frac{1}{n\varphi(x)}-\frac{4}{n}+3-\frac{6}{n}+\frac{1}{n\varphi(x)}-2\alpha-2-\frac{2}{n\varphi(x)}+\frac{8}{n}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}+2-\frac{2}{n}.

∎

Auxiliary technical results will be useful for further estimations.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 2
, and

\lambda(n):=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)},\qquad\theta(n):=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)^{2}},

then

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\leq\lambda(n)\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}},

(2.12)

\displaystyle\theta(n)\leq\frac{4}{9n^{3}}.

Proof.

Since
\frac{k}{k-1}\frac{n-1}{n}\leq 1
for
k\geq n
, we have for
the lower estimate of
\lambda(n)

\lambda(n)\geq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}.\frac{k}{k-1}.\frac{n-1}{n}=\frac{n-1}{n}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k-1)k(k+1)}=\frac{n-1}{n}.\frac{1}{2(n-1)n}=\frac{1}{2n^{2}}.

For the upper estimates of
\lambda(n)
and
\theta(n)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda(n)<\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k-1)k(k+1)}=\frac{1}{2n(n-1)}<\frac{1}{n^{2}},

\displaystyle\theta(n)<\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k-1)k(k+1)(k+2)}=\frac{1}{3n(n^{2}-1)}<\frac{4}{9n^{3}}.

∎

3.
A Direct Theorem

We will first prove the next upper estimate for the norm of the operator
\widetilde{U}_{n}
defined in (
1.4
).

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in C[0,1]
, then

(3.1)

\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f\big\|\leq\sqrt{3}\,\|f\|,\quad\mbox{i.e.}\quad\|\widetilde{U}_{n}\|\leq\sqrt{3}.

Proof.

We have

\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)=P_{n,k}(x)-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}P_{n,k}(x)=\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\Big)P_{n,k}(x).

Then for
x\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{U}_{n}(f,x)\big|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}u_{n,k}(f)\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)\right|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}|u_{n,k}(f)|\,\big|\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)\big|=\|f\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big|1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\Big|P_{n,k}(x).

Applying Cauchy inequality we obtain

\big|\widetilde{U}_{n}(f,x)\big|\leq\|f\|\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\Big)^{2}P_{n,k}(x)}\,\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{n}P_{n,k}(x)}.

Since
\sum_{k=0}^{n}P_{n,k}(x)=1
identically, by Proposition
2.3
(b) with
\alpha=1
we find

\big|\widetilde{U}_{n}(f,x)\big|\leq\sqrt{3-\frac{2}{n}}\,\|f\|<\sqrt{3}\,\|f\|,\qquad x\in[0,1].

Hence, inequality (
3.1
) follows.
∎

In order to prove a direct theorem for the approximation rate for functions
f
by the operator

\widetilde{U}_{n}f
we need a Jackson type inequality.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
and
\widetilde{D}f\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]
, then

(3.2)

\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\big\|\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\|\widetilde{D}^{2}f\|.

Proof.

Having in mind the relation

U_{k}f-U_{k+1}f=\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}f,

(see
[
14
, Lemma 4.1]
) and Proposition
2.1
(a) for
f\,\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{k}f-\widetilde{U}_{k+1}f

\displaystyle=U_{k}f-\frac{1}{k}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k}f-U_{k+1}f+\frac{1}{k+1}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}f

\displaystyle=U_{k}f-U_{k+1}f+\frac{1}{k+1}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}f-\frac{1}{k}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k}f

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}\Big)\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}f+\frac{1}{k+1}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}f-\frac{1}{k}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k}f

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{k}\big(\widetilde{D}U_{k}f-\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}f\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{k}\big(U_{k}\widetilde{D}f-U_{k+1}\widetilde{D}f\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}\widetilde{D}f,

i.e.

(3.3)

\widetilde{U}_{k}f-\widetilde{U}_{k+1}f=-\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}\widetilde{D}f.

Therefore for every
s>n
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{n}f-\widetilde{U}_{s}f=\sum_{k=n}^{s-1}\big(\widetilde{U}_{k}f-\widetilde{U}_{k+1}f\big)=-\sum_{k=n}^{s-1}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}\widetilde{D}f.

Letting
s\rightarrow\infty
and by Proposition
2.2
(a) and (f) we obtain

(3.4)

\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f=-\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}\,\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}\widetilde{D}f

Then from Proposition
2.1
(g) for
\widetilde{D}f\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]

\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\|\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}\,\big\|\widetilde{D}U_{k+1}\widetilde{D}f\big\|\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}\,\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}f\big\|.

Proposition
2.4
, (
2.11
), yields

\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

Therefore

\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\big\|\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\,\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}f\big\|.

∎

A direct result on the approximation rate of functions
f\in C[0,1]
by the operators
(
1.4
) in means of the K-functional (
1.6
) follows immediately from both
lemmas above.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
g
be arbitrary function, such that
g\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
and
\widetilde{D}g\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]
. Then by Lemma
3.1

and Lemma
3.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-\widetilde{U}_{n}g\big\|+\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}g-g\big\|+\|g-f\|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{3})\|f-g\|+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\,\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}g\big\|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{3})\Big(\|f-g\|+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\,\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}g\big\|\Big).

Taking infimum over all functions
g
with
g\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
and
\widetilde{D}g\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]
we obtain

\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\big\|\leq(1+\sqrt{3})\,K\Big(f,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big).

∎

4.
A Strong Converse Result

First, we will prove a Voronovskaya type result for the operator
\widetilde{U}_{n}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\,\lambda(n)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}
,

\,\theta(n)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)^{2}}
and
f\in C[0,1]
is such that

f,\,\widetilde{D}f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
and
\widetilde{D}^{3}f\in L_{\infty}[0,1]
, then

(4.1)

\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f+\lambda(n)\widetilde{D}^{2}f\big\|\leq\theta(n)\,\big\|\widetilde{D}^{3}f\big\|.

Proof.

We have

\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f+\lambda(n)\widetilde{D}^{2}f=-\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{U_{k+1}\widetilde{D}^{2}f}{k^{2}(k+1)}+\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{D}^{2}f}{k^{2}(k+1)}=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{D}^{2}f-U_{k+1}\widetilde{D}^{2}f}{k^{2}(k+1)},

see the proof of Lemma
3.2
, eq. (
3.3
). Then

\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f+\lambda(n)\widetilde{D}^{2}f\big\|\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}\,\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}f-U_{k+1}\widetilde{D}^{2}f\big\|.

Using (
2.6
) with
\widetilde{D}^{2}f
instead of
f
we obtain

\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f+\lambda(n)\widetilde{D}^{2}f\big\|\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(k+1)}\cdot\frac{1}{(k+1)}\,\big\|\widetilde{D}\widetilde{D}^{2}f\big\|=\theta(n)\,\big\|\widetilde{D}^{3}f\big\|.

∎

We need an inequality of Bernstein type.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 2
and
f\in C[0,1]
. Then the following inequality holds true

(4.2)

\|\widetilde{D}\widetilde{U}_{n}f\|\leq\widetilde{C}\,n\|f\|,

where
\widetilde{C}=6.5+\sqrt{6}
.

Proof.

Since

\big|\widetilde{D}\widetilde{U}_{n}(f,x)\big|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}|u_{n,k}(f)|\,\big|\widetilde{D}\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)\big|\leq\|f\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|\widetilde{D}\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)\big|,

it is sufficient to find an upper estimate for the quantity

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|\widetilde{D}\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)\big|=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|\varphi(x)\widetilde{P}_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)\big|.

Remind that, according to (
2.10
), we have the relation

\widetilde{D}P_{n,k}(x)=\varphi(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)=T_{n,k}(x)P_{n,k}(x).

Hence

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)=P_{n,k}(x)-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}P_{n,k}(x)=\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\!\Big)P_{n,k}(x),

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)=\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\!\Big)^{\prime\prime}P_{n,k}(x)+2\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\!\Big)^{\prime}P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)+\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\!\Big)P_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x),

Then,

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)\widetilde{P}_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varphi(x)}{n}\,T_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)P_{n,k}(x)-\frac{2\varphi(x)}{n}\,T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)+\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\!\Big)\varphi(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varphi(x)}{n}\,T_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)P_{n,k}(x)-\frac{2\varphi(x)}{n}\,T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)+\!\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\!\Big)T_{n,k}(x)P_{n,k}(x).

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|\widetilde{D}\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)\big|

\displaystyle\leq a_{n}(x)+b_{n}(x)+c_{n}(x),

where

\displaystyle a_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi(x)}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|T_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)\big|P_{n,k}(x),

\displaystyle b_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\varphi(x)}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\big|,

\displaystyle c_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big|\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\Big)T_{n,k}(x)\Big|P_{n,k}(x).

1. Estimate for
a_{n}(x)
.
From (
2.9
) and (
2.2
)–(
2.3
),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)P_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\frac{2k(k-1)}{x^{3}}+\frac{2(n-k)(n-k-1)}{(1-x)^{3}}\Big)P_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{x^{3}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}k(k-1)P_{n,k}(x)+\frac{2}{(1-x)^{3}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(n-k)(n-k-1)P_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{x^{3}}\,n(n-1)x^{2}+\frac{2}{(1-x)^{3}}\,n(n-1)(1-x)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n(n-1)}{\varphi(x)}.

Having in mind
T_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
in (
2.9
), we obtain

(4.3)

a_{n}(x)=\frac{\varphi(x)}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)P_{n,k}(x)=2(n-1).

2. Estimate for
b_{n}(x)
.
Observe that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\,P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\big|=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|T_{n,k}^{\prime}(1-x)\,P_{n,k}^{\prime}(1-x)\big|,

hence, there is a symmetry of the function
b_{n}(x)
in
x=\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore, it is sufficient to
estimate
b_{n}(x)
for
x\in\big[0,\frac{1}{2}\big]
.

We will show that in
\big[0,\frac{1}{2}\big]
the function
b_{n}(x)
has exactly

\big\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\big\rfloor
local extrema
h_{k}
attained at points in intervals

\big(\frac{k-1}{n},\frac{k}{n}\big]
,
k=1,\ldots,\big\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\big\rfloor
,
respectively. We will estimate all the local maxima
h_{k}
and then an estimate for
b_{n}(x)

will follow immediately.

(i)

First, we prove that

S(x):=\frac{-2\varphi(x)}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)=4(n-1).

From (
2.4
) and (
2.8
),

\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)=n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\big(T_{n,k+1}^{\prime}(x)-T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\big)P_{n-1,k}(x).

Since

\displaystyle T_{n,k+1}^{\prime}(x)-T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle\!=\!\frac{(n-k-1)(n-k-2)}{(1-x)^{2}}-\frac{(k+1)k}{x^{2}}+\frac{k(k-1)}{x^{2}}-\frac{(n-k)(n-k-1)}{(1-x)^{2}}

\displaystyle\!=\!-\frac{2k}{x^{2}}-\frac{2(n-k-1)}{(1-x)^{2}},

using (
2.1
) we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2n}{x^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}kP_{n-1,k}(x)-\frac{2n}{(1-x)^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(n-k-1)P_{n-1,k}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2n}{x^{2}}\,(n-1)x-\frac{2n}{(1-x)^{2}}\,(n-1)(1-x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2n(n-1)}{\varphi(x)}.

Therefore,

(4.4)

S(x)=\frac{-2\varphi(x)}{n}\cdot\frac{-2n(n-1)}{\varphi(x)}=4(n-1).

(ii)

By (
2.9
),
T_{n,k}^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
, hence
-T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)
strictly decreases for

x\in(0,1)
.

For
k=0,1
we have
-T_{n,k}^{\prime}(0^{+})<0
, then
-T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)<0
,
x\in(0,1)
, and

\varphi(x)T_{n,1}^{\prime}(x)
has its only zero in
[0,1)
at
\xi_{1}=0
.

For
k=2,\ldots,n-2
, we have
-T_{n,k}^{\prime}(0^{+})>0
, and
T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)
has a unique
simple zero at

\xi_{k}=\tfrac{\sqrt{\binom{k}{2}}}{\sqrt{\binom{k}{2}}+\sqrt{\binom{n-k}{2}}}\in\big(\tfrac{k-1}{n},\tfrac{k}{n}\big).

For
k=n-1,n
, we have
-T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)>0
for
x\in(0,1)
, and

-\varphi(x)T_{n,n}^{\prime}(x)=0
only for
\xi_{n}=1
in
(0,1]
.

(iii)

For the Bernstein basis polynomials in
(0,1)
we have

P_{n,0}^{\prime}(x)=-n(1-x)^{n-1}<0
,

P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)=n\binom{n}{k}x^{k-1}(1-x)^{n-k-1}\big(\frac{k}{n}-x\big)
, and

P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)=0
only if
x=\frac{k}{n}
,

P_{n,n}^{\prime}(x)=nx^{n-1}>0
.

(iv)

Now, from (ii) and (iii) for
x\in(0,1)
,

-\varphi(x)T_{n,0}^{\prime}(x)\,P_{n,0}^{\prime}(x)>0
,

-\varphi(x)T_{n,1}^{\prime}(x)\,P_{n,1}^{\prime}(x)>0
for
x\in\big(\xi_{1},\frac{1}{n}\big)=\big(0,\frac{1}{n}\big)
,

-\varphi(x)T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\,P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)>0
for
x\in\big(\frac{k-1}{n},\xi_{k}\big)
,
k=2,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
,

-\varphi(x)T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\,P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)<0
for
x\in\big(\xi_{k},\frac{k}{n}\big)
,
k=2,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
,

-\varphi(x)T_{n,n}^{\prime}(x)\,P_{n,n}^{\prime}(x)>0
,

(v)

From the observations in (ii)–(iv) it follows that

-\varphi(x)T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\,P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)>0,\qquad k=0,\ldots,n

except

\displaystyle-\varphi(x)T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\,P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)<0,\quad

\displaystyle x\in\big(\xi_{k},\tfrac{k}{n}\big),

\displaystyle\quad k=1,\ldots,\big\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\big\rfloor,

\displaystyle-\varphi(x)T_{n,n-k}^{\prime}(x)\,P_{n,n-k}^{\prime}(x)<0,\quad

\displaystyle x\in\big(\tfrac{n-k}{n},\xi_{n-k}\big),

\displaystyle\quad k=1,\ldots,\big\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\big\rfloor.

Hence,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big|\frac{-2\varphi(x)T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)}{n}\,P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\Big|=S(x)=4(n-1),\quad x\in\big[0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big]\setminus\textstyle{\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}}\,(\xi_{k},\tfrac{k}{n}).

Therefore, for
k=1,\ldots,\big\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\big\rfloor
,

(4.5)

b_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}4(n-1),&\ x\in\big[\frac{k-1}{n},\xi_{k}\big],\\
4(n-1)+\dfrac{2\varphi(x)}{n}\big|T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\big|,&\ x\in\big[\xi_{k},\frac{k}{n}].\end{cases}

Moreover,

b_{n}(x)=4(n-1),\qquad x\in\big[\tfrac{n-2}{2n},\tfrac{n+2}{2n}\big],\ n\ \text{even},\ \text{and}\ x\in\big[\tfrac{n-1}{2n},\tfrac{n+1}{2n}\big],\ n\ \text{odd}.

(vi)

This means that we have to estimate the maxima of the functions

s_{k}(x):=\bigg|\frac{-2\varphi(x)T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)}{n}\,P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\bigg|,\qquad x\in\big[\xi_{k},\tfrac{k}{n}\big],\quad k=1,\ldots,\big\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\big\rfloor.

By (iv) for
k=1
we have:

s_{1}(x)=\frac{-2\varphi(x)T_{n,1}^{\prime}(x)}{n}\,P_{n,1}^{\prime}(x)=2n(n-1)(n-2)x\Big(\frac{1}{n}-x\Big)(1-x)^{n-3}.

Since

\max_{x\in[0,1/n]}x\Big(\frac{1}{n}-x\Big)=\frac{1}{4n^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad(1-x)^{n-3}\leq 1,

we obtain

(4.6)

h_{1}:=\max_{x\in[0,1/n]}s_{1}(x)\leq\frac{2n(n-1)(n-2)}{4n^{2}}\leq\frac{n}{2}.

Let us fix
k\in\big\{2,\ldots,\big\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\big\rfloor\big\}
. We
estimate the local extremum

h_{k}:=\max_{x\in[\xi_{k},k/n]}s_{k}(x).

According to (iv) we have

s_{k}(x)=\frac{2\varphi(x)}{n}\,T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)P_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{2\varphi(x)}{n}\,T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\binom{n}{k}x^{k-1}(1-x)^{n-k-1}\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big),

i.e.

(4.7)

s_{k}(x)=\frac{2}{n}\,T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)P_{n,k}(x)\Big(\frac{k}{n}-x\Big).

Function
T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)
is strictly increasing in
\big[\frac{k-1}{n},\frac{k}{n}\big]
and
change sign only at point
\xi_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{\binom{k}{2}}}{\sqrt{\binom{k}{2}}+\sqrt{\binom{n-k}{2}}}
.
Then, for
x\in\big[\xi_{k},\frac{k}{n}\big]
,

\max_{x\in[\xi_{k},k/n]}\,T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)=T_{n,k}^{\prime}\big(\tfrac{k}{n}\big)=-\frac{k(k-1)n^{2}}{k^{2}}+\frac{(n-k)(n-k-1)n^{2}}{(n-k)^{2}}=n^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{n-k}\Big).

The function
h(x)=\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{n-x}
is decreasing in
\big(0,\frac{n}{2}\big)

since
h^{\prime}(x)=\big(\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{n-x})^{\prime}<0
, hence for

k\in\big\{2,\ldots,\big\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\big\rfloor\big\}

(4.8)

T_{n,k}^{\prime}(x)\leq n^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{n-k}\Big)\leq n^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n-2}\Big)\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}.

Also,
\frac{k-1}{n}\leq\xi_{k}\leq\frac{k}{n}
and for
x\in\big[\xi_{k},\frac{k}{n}\big]

we have
\frac{k}{n}-x\leq\frac{1}{n}
. Since
0\leq P_{n,k}(x)\leq 1
in
[0,1]
,
it follows from (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) that

h_{k}\leq\frac{2}{n}\cdot\frac{n^{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\leq 1.

Taking into account (
4.6
), for
n\geq 2
we have

(4.9)

h_{k}\leq h_{1}\leq\frac{n}{2},\qquad k=1,\ldots,\big\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\big\rfloor.

Finally, for
b_{n}(x)
, using (
4.5
) and (
4.9
), we obtain the estimate

b_{n}(x)\leq 4(n-1)+\max_{1\leq k\leq\big\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\big\rfloor}h_{k}\leq 4(n-1)+\frac{n}{2},

or

(4.10)

b_{n}(x)\leq 4.5\,n,\qquad x\in[0,1].

3. Estimation of
c_{n}(x)
.
We apply Cauchy inequality and
Proposition
2.3
(b) with
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
:

\displaystyle c_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big|T_{n,k}(x)\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\Big)\Big|P_{n,k}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{n,k}^{2}(x)P_{n,k}(x)}\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\,T_{n,k}(x)\Big)^{2}P_{n,k}(x)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Phi(0)n^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{\Phi(1)}=n\sqrt{2-\frac{2}{n}}\cdot\sqrt{3-\frac{2}{n}}.

Then,

(4.11)

c_{n}(x)\leq\sqrt{6}\,n,\qquad x\in[0,1].

From (
4.3
), (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) we obtain

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|\widetilde{D}\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)\big|\leq a_{n}(x)+b_{n}(x)+c_{n}(x)\leq 2(n-1)+4.5\,n+\sqrt{6}\,n\leq\big(6.5+\sqrt{6}\big)n.

Therefore

\big\|\widetilde{D}\widetilde{U}_{n}f\big\|\leq\widetilde{C}n\|f\|,\qquad\widetilde{C}:=6.5+\sqrt{6}.

∎

Now we are ready to prove a strong converse inequality of Type B accordind to Ditzian-Ivanov
classification.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We follow the approach of Ditzian and Ivanov
[
6
]
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 2
,
f\in C[0,1]
and
\lambda(n)
,
\theta(n)
be defined as in
Proposition
2.4
. From the Voronovskaya type inequality in Lemma
4.1
for the
operator
\widetilde{U}_{\ell}
and function
\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{2}f
instead of
f
we have

\displaystyle\lambda(\ell)\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|

\displaystyle=\big\|\lambda(\ell)\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|

\displaystyle=\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f-\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f+\lambda(\ell)\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f-\widetilde{U}_{\ell}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f+\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f-\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f+\lambda(\ell)\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|+\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f-\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\theta(\ell)\big\|\widetilde{D}^{3}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|+\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}\big(\widetilde{U}_{\ell}f-f\big)\big\|.

Now, using Lemma
4.2
for the function
\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{2}f
and in addition Lemma
3.1

repeatedly three times we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda(\ell)\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\,n\,\theta(\ell)\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{2}f\big\|+3\sqrt{3}\,\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}f-f\big\|

\displaystyle=\widetilde{C}\,n\,\theta(\ell)\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{2}(f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f)+\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|+3\sqrt{3}\,\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}f-f\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\,n\,\theta(\ell)\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{2}(f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f)\big\|+\widetilde{C}\,n\,\theta(\ell)\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|+3\sqrt{3}\,\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}f-f\big\|.

Applying the Bernstein type inequality Lemma
4.2
twice for
f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f
yields

\lambda(\ell)\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|\leq\widetilde{C}^{3}n^{3}\theta(\ell)\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f\big\|+3\sqrt{3}\,\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}-f\big\|+\widetilde{C}\,n\,\theta(\ell)\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|.

From inequalities (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) of Proposition
2.4
we get

\frac{1}{2\ell^{2}}\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|\leq\frac{4\widetilde{C}^{3}n^{3}}{9\ell^{3}}\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f\big\|+3\sqrt{3}\,\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}-f\big\|+\frac{4\widetilde{C}n}{9\ell^{3}}\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|.

Let us choose
\ell
sufficiently large such that

\frac{4\widetilde{C}n}{9\ell^{3}}\leq\frac{1}{4\ell^{2}},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\ell\geq\frac{16\widetilde{C}}{9}\,n.

If we set
L=\frac{16\widetilde{C}}{9}
, for all integers
\ell\geq Ln
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\ell^{2}}\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|\leq\frac{4\widetilde{C}^{3}n^{3}}{9\ell^{3}}\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f\big\|+3\sqrt{3}\,\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}-f\big\|+\frac{1}{4\ell^{2}}\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\ell^{2}}\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|\leq\frac{4\widetilde{C}^{3}n^{3}}{9\ell^{3}}\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f\big\|+3\sqrt{3}\,\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}-f\big\|,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|\leq\widetilde{C}^{2}\,\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f\big\|+12\sqrt{3}\,\frac{\ell^{2}}{n^{2}}\,\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}-f\big\|.

By using Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f\big\|+\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-\widetilde{U}_{n}^{2}f\big\|+\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}^{2}f-\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{3}+(\sqrt{3})^{2})\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f\big\|,

and we obtain the inequality

(4.13)

\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|\leq(4+\sqrt{3})\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}f\big\|.

It remains to complete the estimation of the K-functional. Since

\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1]
, from (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) it
follows

\displaystyle K\Big(f,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\inf\Big\{\|f-g\|+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\,\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}g\big\|:\ g\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1],\,\widetilde{D}g\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1]\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|f-\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\|+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\,\big\|\widetilde{D}^{2}\widetilde{U}_{n}^{3}f\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(4+\sqrt{3}+\widetilde{C}^{2}\Big)\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\big\|+12\sqrt{3}\,\frac{\ell^{2}}{n^{2}}\,\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}f-f\big\|.

Therefore,

K\Big(f,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\leq C\,\frac{\ell^{2}}{n^{2}}\big(\big\|\widetilde{U}_{n}f-f\big\|+\big\|\widetilde{U}_{\ell}f-f)\big\|\big)

for all
\ell\geq Ln
, where
C=4+\sqrt{3}+\widetilde{C}^{2}
and
L=\frac{16\widetilde{C}}{9}
,
\widetilde{C}=6.5+\sqrt{6}
.
∎

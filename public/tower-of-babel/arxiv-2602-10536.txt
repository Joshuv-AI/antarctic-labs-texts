Inequalities involving polynomials and quasimodular forms
Seewoo Lee

Abstract
. In this paper, we study inequalities involving polynomials and quasimodular forms. More precisely, we focus on the monotonicity of the functions of the form t ↦ t m ​ F ​ ( i ​ t ) t\mapsto t^{m}F(it) where F F is a quasimodular form and m > 0 m>0 . As an application, we construct infinitely many positive quasimodular forms of level > 1 >1 . We also give alternative proofs of modular form inequalities used in the proof of optimality of Leech lattice packing and universal optimality of the lattice by Cohn, Kumar, Miller, Radchenko, and Viazovska.



1   Introduction

In 2016, Viazovska gave a surprisingly short and elegant proof of the optimality of
\mathrm{E}_{8}
-sphere packing in dimension
8

[
17
]
, based on the Cohn–Elkies’ linear programming bound
[
3
]
.
Due to its simplicity, it took only one extra week to solve the problem for dimension 24, with Cohn, Kumar, Miller, and Radchenko
[
4
]
.
The main idea is to construct
magic functions
that satisfy the desired properties for the Cohn–Elkies bound, and they could achieve this using
quasimodular forms
.
In particular, the required positivity of the magic functions reduces to certain quasimodular form inequalities.
Later, the same group of people proved the stronger claim; the
\mathrm{E}_{8}
and Leech lattice are
universally optimal
, i.e. they are minimizers of a large class of potential functions among all possible configurations in 8 and 24 dimensions
[
5
]
.
The proof is based on the Fourier interpolation formula, where the authors need to prove inequalities involving quasimodular forms and polynomials that are considerably harder than the inequalities in the sphere packing paper.

The original proofs of the inequalities by Viazovska and Cohn et al. are based on rigorous numerical analysis, and it is natural to ask if there are more conceptual proofs of them.
Romik
[
15
]
gave such a proof for the inequalities for the 8-dimensional sphere packing, and the author gave alternative proofs for both 8 and 24-dimensional packings
[
12
]
which are algebraic in nature.
We may also ask if the inequalities for the universal optimality can be proved in a similar way, which is one of the main motivations of the paper.
The simplest but nontrivial inequality in
[
5
, p. 1067]
is (3b): if

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1,0}

\displaystyle=12\pi i(-E_{2}E_{4}E_{6}+E_{6}^{2}+720\Delta)

(1)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1,1}

\displaystyle=2\pi^{2}(E_{2}^{2}E_{4}E_{6}-2E_{2}E_{6}^{2}-1728E_{2}\Delta+E_{4}^{2}E_{6})

(2)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\tau\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1,1}(\tau)+\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1,0}(\tau),

(3)

then the inequality reads as

i\widehat{\mathcal{E}_{1}}(it)>0

(4)

for all
t>0
.
Here
E_{2},E_{4},E_{6}
are Eisenstein series and
\Delta
is the discriminant form (see
2.1
).

In this paper, instead of only focusing on giving a new proof of (
4
), we develop a more general framework to prove similar inequalities.
More precisely, we study the monotonicity of the functions of the form

t\mapsto t^{m}F(it)

(5)

for a quasimodular form
F
and
m>0
, or equivalently, the positivity of the function

t\mapsto-F(it)+\frac{2\pi t}{m}F^{\prime}(it).

(6)

We give several applications of the monotonicity of such functions.
First of all, it can be used to construct positive quasimodular forms of level
>1
(which are not completely positive in general, see Corollary
4.1
), and we use them to give another proof of one of the quasimodular form inequalities for the optimality of Leech lattice packing
[
4
]
(see Section
4.2
).
Also, we show that the inequality (3b) used in the proof of the universal optimality of the Leech lattice
[
5
]
is equivalent to the monotonicity of (
5
) for
m=11
and
F=X_{12,1}
, the extremal quasimodular form of weight
12
and depth
1
(see Section
2.3
for the definition), where the monotonicity of the corresponding function (
5
) can be proven by a general theorem (Proposition
3.7
).
Inspired from the above examples, we also propose several conjectures on the monotonicity when
F
is another extremal quasimodular form (Conjecture
3.18
) and the density of the positive coefficients of positive quasimodular forms (Conjecture
5.2
).
Accompanying Sage and Lean codes that checks certain identites and inequalities are available at the GitHub repository
https://github.com/seewoo5/posqmf
.

2   Preliminaries

2.1   Modular forms and quasimodular forms

Modular form
of weight
w
and level
\Gamma\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is a holomorphic function
f:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}
on the upper half-plane
\mathcal{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Im(z)>0\}
satisfying the transformation property

f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)=(cz+d)^{w}f(z)\quad\text{for all }\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma

and analytic conditions at cusps of
\Gamma
(see e.g.
[
6
]
).
When
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma
,
f(z+1)=f(z)
and
f
admits a Fourier expansion
f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{n}
for
q=e^{2\pi iz}
.
The standard examples include the Eisenstein series and the discriminant form

\displaystyle E_{4}(z)

\displaystyle=1+240\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{3}(n)q^{n}

(7)

\displaystyle E_{6}(z)

\displaystyle=1-504\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{5}(n)q^{n}

(8)

\displaystyle\Delta(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{4}(z)^{3}-E_{6}(z)^{2}}{1728}=q\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})^{24}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau(n)q^{n}

(9)

where
\tau(n)
is the Ramanujan tau function.
These modular forms have level
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and weight
4,6,12
, respectively, and the ring of modular forms of level
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is generated by
E_{4}
and
E_{6}
.

Quasimodular forms
are holomorphic functions that satisfy “almost-modular” properties, i.e. not exactly invariant under the slash action, but the error term can be expressed in terms of a combination of rational terms and modular forms.
More precisely, a holomorphic function
F:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a
quasimodular form
of weight
w
, depth
s
, and level
\Gamma
if there exists holomorphic functions
F_{0},F_{1},\dots,F_{s}
such that

(F|_{w}\gamma)(z):=(cz+d)^{-w}F\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)=\sum_{r=0}^{s}F_{r}(z)\left(\frac{c}{cz+d}\right)^{r},\quad\text{for all }\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma.

(See Section 5.3 of
[
1
]
for more details and applications of quasimodular forms.)
The most important example is the weight 2 Eisenstein series, which can be defined in terms of Fourier expansion:

E_{2}(z)=1-24\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma_{1}(n)q^{n}

(10)

which satisfies the following equation

E_{2}\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)=(cz+d)^{2}E_{2}(z)+\frac{6c}{\pi i}(cz+d)\quad\text{for all }\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

(11)

for any
z\in\mathcal{H}
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
In particular, taking
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
z=it
with
t>0
, we have

E_{2}\left(\frac{i}{t}\right)=-t^{2}E_{2}(it)+\frac{6t}{\pi}.

(12)

It is known that the ring of quasimodular forms of level
1
is generated by the Eisenstein series
E_{2},E_{4}
and
E_{6}

[
11
]
, and the depth of
F
is equal to the degree of
E_{2}
in the polynomial expression of
F
in terms of
E_{2},E_{4},E_{6}
.

Every quasimodular form arises as a linear combination of derivatives of modular forms.
In particular, if
F
is a quasimodular form of weight
w
and depth
s
, its derivative
F^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\mathrm{d}F}{\mathrm{d}z}
is a quasimodular form of weight
w+2
and depth
\leq s+1
.
The derivatives can be computed using the product formula and Ramanujan’s formulas:

E_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{E_{2}^{2}-E_{4}}{12},\quad E_{4}^{\prime}=\frac{E_{2}E_{4}-E_{6}}{3},\quad E_{6}^{\prime}=\frac{E_{2}E_{6}-E_{4}^{2}}{2}.

(13)

The following identity on
E_{2}
will be used later in one of Example
4.2
.

Lemma 2.1
.

6E_{2}(2z)=4E_{2}(2z)+E_{2}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)+E_{2}\left(\frac{z+1}{2}\right).

(14)

Proof.

This is an Exercise 5.19 of
[
16
]
.
For completeness, we provide a proof here. The main idea is to compare the Fourier coefficients of both sides. Comparing coefficients of
q^{n/2}
, (
14
) is equivalent to

6\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)=4\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{n}{4}\right)+\sigma_{1}(n)(1+(-1)^{n})

where
\sigma_{1}(a)=0
if
a\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
When
n
is odd,
\sigma_{1}(n/2)=\sigma_{1}(n/4)=0
and
1+(-1)^{n}=0
, so the equality holds.
When
n
is even, let
n=2^{k}m
where
m
is odd and
k\geq 1
.
If
k=1
, then
\sigma_{1}(n/2)=\sigma_{1}(m)
and
\sigma_{1}(n/4)=0
, and the equation reduces to
6\sigma_{1}(m)=2\sigma_{1}(2m)
, which follows from the multiplicativity of
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{1}(2)=3
.
For
k\geq 2
, again using multiplicativity, we have

\sigma_{1}(n)=\sigma_{1}(2^{k})\sigma_{1}(m)=(1+2+2^{2}+\cdots+2^{k})\sigma_{1}(m)=(2^{k+1}-1)\sigma_{1}(m)

and similar holds for
\sigma_{1}(n/2)
and
\sigma_{1}(n/4)
, and the equation reduces to
6(2^{k}-1)=4(2^{k-1}-1)+2(2^{k+1}-1)
which is true.
∎

2.2   Positive quasimodular forms

One of the core step of Viazovska and Cohn et al.’s proof of the optimality of
E_{8}
and Leech lattice packing
[
17
,
4
]
is to show that the
magic functions
satisfy the desired linear constraints, which reduce to inequalities on quasimodular forms.
The original proofs in loc. cit. are based on the interval arithmetic and Sturm’s bound, while the subsequent papers by Romik
[
15
]
and the author
[
12
]
provide more conceptual proof of the inequalities.
In particular, the author develops a general theory of
positive
and
completely positive
quasimodular forms in
[
12
]
, where the derivative and Serre derivative play key roles and gives simple and algebraic proofs of the quasimodular form inequalities that arise in the sphere packing papers.
The results in loc. cit., on positivity and (Serre) derivative, are summarized in Proposition
2.3
.

Definition 2.2

(L.
[
12
]
)
.

A quasimodular form
F
is
positive
if it takes nonnegative real values on the positive imaginary axis, i.e.
F(it)\geq 0
for
t>0
.
A quasimodular form
F
is
completely positive
if all the Fourier coefficients of
F
are real and nonnegative.
We denote the set of positive (resp. completely positive) quasimodular forms of weight
w
, depth
s
, and level
\Gamma
by
\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,+}(\Gamma)
(resp.
\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,++}(\Gamma)
).

Proposition 2.3

(L.
[
12
]
)
.

Let
F=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}a_{n}q^{n}\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s}
and
F^{\prime}=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}na_{n}q^{n}\in\mathcal{QM}_{w+2}^{s+1}
.
For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\partial_{k}F:=F^{\prime}-\frac{k}{12}E_{2}F
be the Serre derivative of
F
.

(1)

If
F
is a cusp form,
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,++}
if and only if
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{QM}_{w+2}^{s+1,++}
.

(2)

If
F
is a cusp form, then
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{QM}_{w+2}^{s+1,+}
implies
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,+}
.

(3)

F
is completely positive if and only if its derivatives are all positive.

(4)

If
\partial_{k}F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w+2}^{s+1,+}
for some
k
and all the Fourier coefficients of
F
are real and the first nonzero Fourier coefficient of
F
is positive, then
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,+}
.

(5)

If
F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w}^{s,++}
and
n_{0}\geq k/12\geq 0
, then
\partial_{k}F\in\mathcal{QM}_{w+2}^{s+1,++}
.

3   Monotonicity of
t^{m}F(it)

In this section, we provide several methods to prove the monotonicity of the function (
5
), via Lambert series expansion (Section
3.1
) or reducing it to a quasimodular form inequality (Section
3.2
).

3.1   Lambert series approach

For certain low-weight quasimodular forms
F(z)
, it is possible to prove monotonicity of
t^{m}F(it)
via
Lambert series
, i.e. expansions of the form

F(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}\frac{q^{n}}{(1-q^{n})^{k}}.

Once we know that
b_{n}\geq 0
for all
n
, monotonicity of
t^{m}F(it)
reduces to that of

Lemma 3.1

3.2   Two sufficient conditions and more examples

We introduce two simple sufficient conditions (Proposition
3.7
and
3.16
) which are useful to prove monotonicity of
t^{m}F(it)
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
F
be a quasimodular form and let
m\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Assume that

(1)

F
and
F^{\prime}
are positive quasimodular forms,

(2)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{F(it)}{tF^{\prime}(it)}=\frac{2\pi}{m},

(3)

(m+1)(F^{\prime})^{2}-mF^{\prime\prime}F
is positive.

Then the function
t\mapsto t^{m}F(it)
is monotone decreasing on
(0,\infty)
.
Also,
m
is the largest possible constant such that
t^{m}F(it)
is monotone decreasing.

Proof.

Consider the function

h(t):=\frac{F(it)}{F^{\prime}(it)}.

The limit condition implies that
h(0):=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}h(t)=0
and
h^{\prime}(0)=\frac{2\pi}{m}
.
In other words, the tangent line of
h(t)
at
t=0
is
l(t)=\frac{2\pi}{m}t
.
Direct computation shows that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t^{m}F(it))=mt^{m-1}F(it)+t^{m}(-2\pi)F^{\prime}(it)=mt^{m-1}F^{\prime}(it)\left(h(t)-l(t)\right),

so it is enough to show that
h(t)<l(t)
for all
t>0
.
Consider their difference
g(t)=l(t)-h(t)
.
The limit condition implies that
g(0)=0
and it is enough to show that
g(t)
is monotone increasing, and this is equivalent to

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}g(t)=\frac{2\pi}{m}+2\pi\cdot\frac{(F^{\prime}(it))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(it)F(it)}{(F^{\prime}(it))^{2}}>0\Leftrightarrow(m+1)(F^{\prime})^{2}-mF^{\prime\prime}F>0.

The last claim follows from considering the behavior of
h(t)
near
t=0
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

The modular form
(m+1)(F^{\prime})^{2}-mF^{\prime\prime}F
is a constant multiple of the Rankin–Cohen bracket
[
14
,
2
]
of
F
with itself.
More precisely, Martin and Royer
[
13
]
generalized the notion of Rankin–Cohen brackets for quasimodular forms as

\Phi_{n;k,s;\ell,t}(F,G)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-1)^{r}\binom{k-s+n-1}{n-r}\binom{\ell-t+n-1}{r}(D^{r}F)(D^{n-r}G)

for
0\leq s\leq k/2
and
0\leq t\leq\ell/2
.
This matches with the usual Rankin–Cohen bracket
[F,G]_{n}^{(k,\ell)}
when
s=t=0
.
One can directly check that

\Phi_{2;m,0;m,0}(F,F)=[F,F]_{2}^{(m,m)}=\sum_{r=0}^{2}(-1)^{r}\binom{m+1}{2-r}\binom{m+1}{r}(D^{r}F)(D^{2-r}F)=(m+1)mF^{\prime\prime}F-(m+1)^{2}(F^{\prime})^{2}

and the 3rd condition of Proposition
3.7
is equivalent to the negativity of
\Phi_{2;m,0;m,0}(F,F)
.
In particular, when
F
has weight
w
and depth
s
, then for
m=w-s

(m+1)(F^{\prime})^{2}-mF^{\prime\prime}F=-\frac{1}{m+1}\Phi_{2;w,s;w,s}(F,F).

is a quasimodular form of weight
2w+4
and depth at most
2s
.

Remark 3.9
.

For any completely positive quasimodular form
F
, we have
F^{\prime\prime}F-(F^{\prime})^{2}>0
.
In fact, this holds for any completely monotone functions (e.g. see
[
7
]
).

4.1   Positive quasimodular forms of higher levels

Let

Then for

If we further assume that

The following proposition shows that one can obtain a stronger result if the monotonicity of

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
F
be a quasimodular form and let
m\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Assume that
t\mapsto t^{m}F(it)
is monotone decreasing for
t>0
.

is a positive quasimodular form of level
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.

N^{m}
is optimal, i.e. for any

,
F(z)-cF(Nz)
is not positive.

Proof.

Since
t^{m}F(it)
is monotone decreasing,

t^{m}F(it)-(Nt)^{m}F(iNt)=t^{m}(F(it)-N^{m}F(iNt))

is positive for
t>0
.

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{m}F(it)=L>0
, then

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{F(it)}{F(iNt)}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{N^{m}\cdot t^{m}F(it)}{(Nt)^{m}F(iNt)}=\frac{N^{m}\cdot L}{L}=N^{m}

which shows the optimality of the constant.
∎

5   Conclusion

In this paper, we studied monotonicity of the functions of the form
t\mapsto t^{m}F(it)
where
F
is a quasimodular form and
m>0
, and presented several applications of it.
In particular, we show that one can construct positive quasimodular forms of level
>1
using the monotonicity, where most of the examples we found in Section
4.1
are not completely positive.
We computed the natural density of the set
\{n:a_{n}>0\}
for the Fourier coefficients of these examples, and it is natural to ask if the density is bounded from below by a positive constant for every positive quasimodular form.

Question 5.1
.

Is there a positive constant
c\in(0,1)
such that for every positive quasimodular form
F=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}a_{n}q^{n}
, the natural density of the set
\{n:a_{n}>0\}
is at least
c
?

In Example
4.2
, the natural density of the set
\{n:a_{n}>0\}
for each
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4}
is
\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}
respectively, which shows
c\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
We conjecture that
c=\frac{1}{2}
is indeed the answer to Question
5.1
.

Conjecture 5.2
.

For every positive quasimodular form
F=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}a_{n}q^{n}
, the natural density of the set
\{n:a_{n}>0\}
exists and is at least
\frac{1}{2}
.

Kane, Krishnamoorthy, and Lau
[
9
]
showed that, when
F
is a linear combination of cusp forms or their derivatives, prime-indexed coefficients have infinitely many sign changes.
In particular, there is no completely positive quasimodular form of the form
\sum_{j=0}^{s}D^{j}F_{j}
with all
F_{j}
cusp forms.
However, it is still possible that such quasimodular form is positive.
For example, consider the weight
16
and depth
2
quasimodular form

F=X_{4,2}\Delta=\frac{1}{312}(E_{4}\Delta-\Delta^{\prime\prime})=q^{2}-18q^{3}+1240q^{4}-220q^{5}-1620q^{6}+11676q^{7}+\cdots.

This is a linear combination of (derivatives of) cusp forms which is also positive.
It seems that the natural density of the set
\{n:a_{n}>0\}
for
F
is
\frac{1}{2}
.

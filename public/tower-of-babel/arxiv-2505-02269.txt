Information geometry and entanglement under phase-space deformation through nonsymplectic congruence transformation
Shilpa Nandi, Pinaki Patra

Abstract
The Fisher-Rao (FR) information matrix is a central object in multiparameter quantum estimation theory. The geometry of a quantum state can be envisaged through the Riemannian manifold generated by the FR-metric corresponding to the quantum state. Interestingly, any congruence transformation G ​ L ​ ( 2 ​ n , ℝ ) GL(2n,\mathbb{R}) in phase space leaves the FR-distance for Gaussian states invariant. In the present paper, we investigate whether this isometry affects the entanglement in the bipartite system. It turns out that the entanglement-generating congruent transformation depends upon the system and the symplectic structure of phase-space. To make our study relevant to physical systems, we choose Bopp’s shift in phase space as an example of G ​ L ​ ( 2 ​ n , ℝ ) GL(2n,\mathbb{R}) , so that the results can be interpreted in terms of noncommutative (NC) phase-space deformation. We provide a quantitative estimation for the dependence of symplectic eigenvalues on the deformation parameters and explain the induced entanglement through phase-space deformation. The coexistence of FR-isometry with deformation-dependent entanglement demonstrates that statistical distinguishability and quantum correlations constitute complementary aspects of the geometry of Gaussian quantum systems. With the help of toy models of oscillators in NC-space, we illustrate our results quantitatively.

where

(5)

(7)

FTeigen
]

(43)

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{c,i}=a_{i}b_{i}-c_{i}^{2},\;\Delta_{d,i}=a_{i}b_{i}-d_{i}^{2},\;i=1,2.

(44)

\displaystyle K_{c}=a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1}-2c_{1}c_{2},\;K_{d}=a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1}-2d_{1}d_{2}.

(45)

Consequently, the distance function reads

d(\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2})=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(\ln^{2}\lambda_{c,+}+\ln^{2}\lambda_{c,-}+\ln^{2}\lambda_{d,+}+\ln^{2}\lambda_{d,-})}.

(46)

One can note that, if we change index
1\leftrightarrow 2
, the eigenvalues changes as
\lambda_{j}\to\lambda_{j}^{-1}
. Moreover, if
\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma_{2}
, then
K_{c}=2\Delta_{c},\;K_{d}=2\Delta_{d}
, which gives
\lambda_{c,\pm}=\lambda_{d\pm}=1
. In other words, the consistency conditions for a metric holds, as

d_{FR}(\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2})=d_{FR}(\Sigma_{2},\Sigma_{1}),\;d_{FR}(\Sigma,\Sigma)=0.

(47)

An important property of this distance is its invariance under a simultaneous invertible congruence transformation. Let us consider that the congruence transformation
S\in GL(2n,\mathbb{R})
transforms two covariance matrix as

\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}=S\Sigma_{1}S^{T},\;\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}=S\Sigma_{2}S^{T};\;\;\mbox{Det}(S)\neq 0.

(48)

The generalized eigenvalues for the transformed pair are obtained from

\mbox{Det}(\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}-\lambda\Sigma_{1}^{\prime})=\mbox{Det}[S(\Sigma_{2}-\lambda\Sigma_{1})S^{T}]=\mbox{Det}(\Sigma_{2}-\lambda\Sigma_{1})=0

(49)

That means the set of eigenvalues remain unchanged after any invertible congruence
GL(2n,\mathbb{R})
. In other words, every invertible congruence transformation acts as a isometry of the Fisher-Rao geometry of the manifold of positive-definite covariance matrices.

It is important to distinguish the statistical geometrical statement of invariance of FR-metric, from the general separability criteria of Gaussian states. In particular, FR-metric solely depends on covariance matrix, whereas the separability criteria depends additionally on the symplectic structure. Thus the relevant structure for separability condition is

(\Sigma,\Omega)\to(S\Sigma S^{T},S\Omega S^{T}).

(50)

Consequently, invariance of FR-distance under congruence does not, by itself imply invariance of Gaussian PPT/separability classification when underlying canonical structure is deformed.

In the subsequent section, we shall demostrate this this for generic bipartite symmetric Gaussian states under noncommutative deformation of phase-space.

V.1
Noise matrix and separability criterion

Wigner distribution (
W
) and the density operator
(\hat{\rho})
are related
through the definition

W(X_{c})=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}\int d^{2}x^{\prime}\langle x-x^{\prime}|\hat{\rho}|x+x^{\prime}\rangle e^{2ix^{\prime}.p/\hbar},

(175)

where
X_{c}=(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})=(x_{1},p_{1},x_{2},p_{2})^{T}
is the classical co-ordinate vector. We define

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{X}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{X}_{\alpha}-\langle\hat{X}_{\alpha}\rangle_{\rho},\mbox{with}\;\langle\hat{X}_{\alpha}\rangle_{\rho}=\mbox{Tr}(\hat{X}_{\alpha}\hat{\rho});\;\alpha=1,2,3,4.

(176)

\displaystyle\Delta X_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{\alpha}-\langle X_{\alpha}\rangle_{W},\;\mbox{with}\;\langle X_{\alpha}\rangle_{W}=\int W(X_{c})X_{\alpha}d^{4}X_{c}.

(177)

The four components
\Delta\hat{X}
satisfy the same commutation relations as
\hat{X}
. Moreover, the phase-space average
\langle X_{\alpha}\rangle_{W}
with respect to the Wigner distribution
W
is equal to the average
\langle\hat{X}_{\alpha}\rangle_{\rho}
with respect to the density operator
\hat{\rho}
. We define the covariance matrix
\mathcal{V}_{c}
through the matrix elements

\mathcal{V}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\langle\{\Delta\hat{X}_{\alpha},\Delta\hat{X}_{\beta}\}\rangle=\mbox{Tr}(\frac{1}{2}\{\Delta\hat{X}_{\alpha},\Delta\hat{X}_{\beta}\}\hat{\rho})=\int\Delta X_{\alpha}\Delta X_{\beta}W(X_{c})d^{4}X_{c}.

(178)

Since
\mathcal{V}_{c}
is symmetric, we can write it in the block form

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{c}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}V_{11}&V_{12}\\
V_{12}^{T}&V_{22}\end{array}\right).

Using the connection (
V
) between the commutative space and NC-space co-ordinates, one can see that the NC-space covariance matrix
\mathcal{V}_{nc}
is given by

\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{nc}=\Upsilon_{D}\mathcal{V}_{c}\Upsilon_{D}^{T}.

(182)

The fundamental commutation relations (
123
) of commutative space implies that a bonafide covariance matrix must satisfy the Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty principle (RSUP)

\mathcal{V}_{c}+\frac{i}{2}\hbar J\geq 0.

(183)

In NC-space, the equivalent statement for the RSUP reads

\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{nc}+\frac{i}{2}\hbar_{e}\tilde{J}\geq 0.

(184)

A generic local transformation
S_{1}\bigoplus S_{2}
, acts on
\mathcal{V}_{c}
as

V_{jj}\to S_{j}V_{jj}S_{j}^{T},\;V_{12}\to S_{1}V_{12}S_{2}^{T};\;\mbox{with}\;S_{j}\in Sp(2,\mathbb{R}),\;j=1,2.

(185)

One can identify that following four quantities are local invariant with respect to transformation belonging to the
Sp(2,\mathbb{R})\bigotimes Sp(2,\mathbb{R})\subset Sp(4,\mathbb{R})
.

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}=Det(V_{jj}),\Delta_{12}=Det(V_{12}),\Delta_{\mathcal{V}_{c}}=Det(\mathcal{V}_{c}),\tau_{v}=\mbox{Tr}(V_{11}J_{2}V_{12}J_{2}V_{22}J_{2}V_{12}^{T}J_{2}).

(186)

Using Williamson’s theorem
[
45
,
46
]
, one can show that the RSUP (
183
) can be rewritten as
Sp(2,\mathbb{R})\bigotimes Sp(2,\mathbb{R})
invariant statement

\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}+(\hbar^{2}/4-\Delta_{12})^{2}-\tau_{v}\geq\hbar^{2}(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2})/4.

(187)

Under mirror reflection (Peres-Horodecki partial transpose)

,
\Delta_{2}
and
\tau_{v}
remain invariant; whereas

flips sign. Therefore, the requirement that the covariance matrix of a separable state has to obey the following necessary condition.

Ps=\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}+(\hbar^{2}/4-|\Delta_{12}|)^{2}-\tau_{v}-\hbar^{2}(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2})/4\geq 0,

which turns out to be sufficient for all bipartite-Gaussian state.

Now, for a generic ground state (

\psi(x_{1},x_{2})=\mathcal{N}_{0}e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{T}\Lambda x}
, corresponds to

(189)

Here the following notation for the matrix
\Lambda
and its elements

have been used.

\displaystyle\Lambda=[\Lambda_{jk}]_{j,k=1}^{2}=\Lambda_{r}+i\Lambda_{c}=[\Lambda_{jkr}]_{j,k=1}^{2}+i[\Lambda_{jkc}]_{j,k=1}^{2},

(190)

where

, and
x=(x_{1},x_{2})^{T},\;p=(p_{1},p_{2})^{T}

The expectation values through the Wigner distribution

\displaystyle(4\hbar^{2}/m_{12}+\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}\theta^{2})(\eta/m_{12}+\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}\theta)^{2}(\eta^{2}/m_{12}+4\hbar^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2})=(4\hbar^{2}/m_{12}+\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}\theta^{2})

\displaystyle(\eta/m_{12}+\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}\theta)^{2}(\eta^{2}/m_{12}+4\hbar^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}),\;\mbox{with}\;m_{12}=m_{1}.m_{2}.

(204)

\theta,\eta

\tilde{\omega}_{1}\neq\tilde{\omega}_{2}

If we recall that the off-diagonal term for the isotropic oscillator in NC-deformed space under consideration is merely an angular momentum operator, which commutes with the rest part of Hamiltonian in the equivalent commutative space. Thus, the ground state (Gaussian) is just product of two ordinary oscillators, results into the separable states. However, for anisotropic oscillator, the deformed angular momentum operator does not commute with the rest portion of the Hamiltonian, which results into the entangled state. Thus, we can conclude that the entanglement is created via the NC-deformation of space for anisotropic oscillator. Moreover, even in NC-space, an anisotropic oscillator also supports separable states. For instance, let us consider without loss of generality

All the other frequency admits the entanglement between coordinate degrees of freedom. Since the separability of the bipartite Gaussian state for an anisotropic oscillator in NCS is satisfied for only a very special choice of anisotropic parameters, it is worth mentioning that the bipartite Gaussian state is almost always entangled in NC-space.

The covariance matrix (

If we parametrize

\displaystyle g_{00}=g_{11}=\frac{2(a^{2}+c^{2})}{(a^{2}-c^{2})^{2}},\;g_{01}=g_{10}=-\frac{4ac}{(a^{2}-c^{2})^{2}}.

(206)

The Fisher information matrix

has the eigenvalues
\lambda_{g\pm}=\frac{2}{(a\mp c)^{2}}
, and

can be diagonalized (locally) with the similarity transformation

\displaystyle g_{d}=\mbox{diag}(\lambda_{g+},\lambda_{g-})=Q_{g}^{\dagger}gQ_{g},\;\mbox{with}\;Q_{g}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
-1&1\end{array}\right).

Accordingly, in this diagonal representation of the FR-matrix the co-ordinate vector
(a,c)^{T}
is transformed into (locally)

(a,c)\to(u_{0},v_{0}):=(u_{0},v_{0})^{T}=Q_{g}(a,c)^{T},

(210)

which reduces the metric to

ds_{FR}^{2}=\frac{du_{0}^{2}}{u_{0}^{2}}+\frac{dv_{0}^{2}}{v_{0}^{2}}.

(211)

Introducing

and
v_{0}=e^{v}

ds_{FR}^{2}=du^{2}+dv^{2}.

(212)

Therefore the two-parameter Fisher-Rao manifold induced by the pure states is actually flat (Ricci scalar
R=0
). Thus one can conclude that while the phase-space deformation through congruence transformation may generate entanglement, it leaves the curvature of the state manifold flat.

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we have investigated the information geometry and quantum-correlation properties of Gaussian states under phase-space deformation induced by nonsymplectic congruence transformations. Our analysis highlights an important distinction between the statistical geometry encoded by covariance matrices and the quantum-mechanical structure associated with the symplectic form and partial transposition. A central result is that the Fisher-Rao (FR) distance between Gaussian states remains invariant under an invertible congruence transformation. Consequently, the nonsymplectic transformations considered here act as isometries of the Gaussian statistical manifold. This invariance does not require preservation of the original symplectic form and therefore extends beyond ordinary canonical transformations. From an information-geometric perspective, statistical distinguishability between Gaussian states is preserved even when the underlying phase-space structure is deformed.

The situation is different for the PPT analysis. In comparing the undeformed and deformed descriptions, we adopt a fixed operational convention in which the same bipartition and the same partial-transpose matrix are used in both cases. Under this fixed partial-transpose convention, invariance of the FR distance does not imply invariance of the PPT spectrum. In particular, the FR geometry and the PPT criterion probe different aspects of a Gaussian quantum system: the former is determined by its statistical covariance geometry, whereas the latter additionally depends on its symplectic structure.
The toy model provides an explicit illustration of this distinction. Within the fixed partial-transpose prescription, the minimum eigenvalue becomes sensitive to the noncommutative (NC) phase-space parameters and may cross the PPT threshold in appropriate parameter regimes. Such a crossing establishes the signature of entanglement.

In summary, the present framework provides a consistent information-geometric description of Gaussian states in deformed phase spaces while clarifying the additional role played by symplectic and bipartite structures in quantum-correlation criteria. The coexistence of FR- isometry with deformation-dependent PPT behavior demonstrates that statistical distinguishability and quantum correlations constitute complementary aspects of the geometry of Gaussian quantum systems. Thus, the results of the present paper may provide a useful starting point for further investigations of information geometry, multimode quantum correlations, and modified canonical structures in deformed phase-space theories.

Abstract 1 - ORAL PRESENTATION Real-World Experience with Outpatient Administration of Cycle 1 Bispecific Antibody Therapy in Hematologic Malignancies

Elrazi Ali, Mohamed Hegazi, Robert Emmons, Hassaan Yasin, Ju-Hsien Chao, Megan Burd, Lindsay Figg

James Graham Brown Cancer Center, University of Louisville, Louisville, KY, USA

Introduction: Bispecific antibodies are increasingly used in hematologic malignancies but are commonly administered inpatient during ramp-up dosing of cycle 1 due to concerns for cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS). Inpatient administration increases healthcare utilization and may impact patient experience negatively. Real-world data supporting outpatient administration during cycle 1 are limited. Methods: At the Brown Cancer Center, we conducted a retrospective study of adult patients who initiated cycle 1 of bispecific antibody therapy as outpatient in the time period between January 2023 and December 2025. Agents included glofitamab, blinatumomab, teclistamab, and talquetamab. Outcomes included CRS, ICANS, hospitalization, treatment completion, mortality, and length of stay (LOS). Results: Of 70 patients screened, 18 received outpatient cycle 1 bispecific antibody therapy. Cytokine release syndrome occurred in 4/18 patients, all requiring hospitalization with a length of stay of 2 to 3 days. One patient developed ICANS, and one patient was hospitalized for a non- treatment-related reason. Based on manufacturer recommendations, the expected inpatient monitoring would be as follows for each drug: (teclistamab: 6 days, talquetamab: 8 days, blinatumomab: 3 days, glofitamab: 1 day). For our 18 patients, it will translate to 97 hospital days. The actual number of hospitalization days was 14, resulting in net savings of 83 hospital admission days. All patients completed cycle 1 therapy. No grade ≥ 3 toxicities or treatment-related deaths were observed. Conclusions: Our study showed that giving bispecific antibodies to patients in an outpatient setting during the first treatment cycle was safe and practical, with structured monitoring. Overall, the side effects from treatment were manageable. This approach can lead to cost savings, decreased hospitalization (85% reduction in LOS), and possibly higher patient satisfaction. Still, larger studies are needed to better select patients, improve predictions for side effects, and further clarify the safety of administering bispecific antibodies on an outpatient basis.

Abstract 2 - ORAL PRESENTATION Utilization of CSF Liquid Biopsy to Successfully Diagnose and Treat EGFR Mutation in New Diagnosis of Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer with Leptomeningeal Spread

Monica Gobrial, Goetz Kloecker

Division of Hematology and Medical Oncology, Brown Cancer Center, Department of Medicine, University of Louisville, Louisville, KY, USA

Introduction: Leptomeningeal carcinomatosis is a rare condition occurring in 5% to 15% of cancer patients. Historically, it has been difficult to diagnose and treat resulting in a poor overall prognosis of only a few months. Emerging technology, namely the use of CSF liquid biopsy, has led to easier diagnosis and screening for targetable mutations which improves disease control and overall survival. We present a case report demonstrating how this utility led to successful treatment of a patient. Case: A 72-year-old African American woman presented with a 2-week history of altered mental status and progressive weakness. MRI showed a ring-enhancing lesion in the right basal ganglia along with multiple meningeal lesions. Subsequent CT chest/abdomen/pelvis returned with multiple pulmonary nodules. Biopsy was obtained and revealed a new diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma. CSF cytology was also positive for atypical cells. Decision was then made to request ctDNA and CSF liquid biopsy, which ultimately resulted with EGFR mutation, prompting initiation of a third-generation EGFR TKI, osimertinib. Upon discharge, patient was minimally conversational and immobile, but she returned for 2-week follow-up with improved mentation and ambulating with a walker. Over the next 2 years, she continued to have significant clinical improvement, with MRIs showing complete resolution of meningeal changes and CTs without disease progression. Unfortunately, 2 years after initial presentation, she had leptomeningeal recurrence and elected not to pursue aggressive systemic options. Discussion: CSF cytology, the current gold standard for diagnosis of leptomeningeal disease, is only positive in 50-60% of cases necessitating additional repeat lumbar punctures with better sampling sizes, or missed/delayed diagnosis altogether. Emerging technology such as utilization of liquid biopsy, on the other hand, has shown ability to detect tumors in 82% of cases. Additionally, there is an added benefit of assessing for targetable mutations, which can be used to guide treatment in a meaningful way such as in our patient's case. The early detection of EGFR mutation and the subsequent prompt initiation of osimertinib undoubtedly led to improved quality of life and increased survival for our patient. Current studies similarly show improved median survival of at least 5 months for those treated with osimertinib as well as progression-free survival of 9.5 months compared to those treated with other TKIs. Our patient twice exceeded this median, ultimately showing the significance of how early detection with the utilization of liquid biopsy and treatment with targeted therapy is crucial to altering a patient's disease course and prolonging life.

Abstract 3 - ORAL PRESENTATION Prognostic Impact of Stromal Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocytes (sTILs) and CD8+ T-Cell Density in Early-Stage Triple-Negative Breast Cancer: A Prospective Cohort Study

Yana Hasan, Zuheir Al-Shehabi, Nader Abedallaa

Latakia University Hospital, Latakia, Syria

Introduction: Stromal tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (sTILs) are an established prognostic biomarker in triple- negative breast cancer (TNBC), but the differential roles of CD8+ and CD4+ T-cell subsets remain unclear, particularly in early-stage disease. We evaluated the prognostic value of sTILs and T-cell subsets in patients with TNBC treated with adjuvant chemotherapy. Methods: This prospective cohort study analyzed 109 stage I-III patients with TNBC treated with surgery and adjuvant anthracycline-taxane chemotherapy at Latakia University Hospital, Syria. The study was approved by the institutional review board of Latakia University Hospital, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants. Stromal TILs were quantified on hematoxylin-eosin slides using international guidelines. CD8+ and CD4+ T-cells were assessed via immunohistochemistry (anti-CD8/SP57, anti-CD4/SP35). Associations with 3-year recurrence-free survival (RFS) were analyzed using Kaplan-Meier estimates and Cox regression. Results: High sTILs (≥30%) were observed in 59.6% of patients and correlated with younger age (P = 0.009), smaller tumor size (P = 0.026), node-negative disease (P = 0.017), and higher histologic grade (P = 0.037). Patients with high sTILs had significantly better 3- year RFS (72.3% vs. 40.9%, P = 0.002). Elevated CD8+ T- cell density was independently associated with reduced recurrence (adjusted HR: 3.12, 95% CI: 1.8-5.4; P < 0.001), while CD4+ T-cells showed no prognostic significance (P = 0.41). Conclusion: Beyond sTILs, CD8+ T-cell infiltration enhances prognostic stratification in early-stage TNBC. Integrating CD8+ density into clinical assessments may improve risk prediction and guide therapeutic decisions.

Abstract 4 - POSTER PRESENTATION Late Progression After Immunotherapy in Lynch Syndrome-Associated MSI-High Pancreatic Cancer

Elrazi Ali, Vivek Sharma

James Graham Brown Cancer Center, University of Louisville, Louisville, KY, USA

Introduction: Patients with Lynch syndrome are at increased risk for multiple malignancies and commonly have microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) tumors, making them suitable candidates for immunotherapy. In metastatic pancreatic cancer, immunotherapy in MSI-H disease can result in durable responses. However, after prolonged disease control, distinguishing true tumor progression from the emergence of a new malignant clone can be challenging and complicates subsequent management. We report a patient with Lynch syndrome who developed disease progression after 3 years of stable disease on immunotherapy. Case: This case is a 64-year- old man with PMH of type 2 diabetes and Lynch syndrome (MSH2 pathogenic mutation). He has a history of colorectal cancer treated in 2004. In 2021, an MRI abdomen showed a 4.8-cm pancreatic body mass abutting the celiac artery and a 6.8-cm pancreatic tail mass. Staging scans showed lung nodules consistent with metastasis. He was diagnosed with metastatic pancreatic cancer through liver biopsy, and NGS showed MSI high status, tumor burden mutation 30. He was started on pembrolizumab and had excellent control for 3 years until August 2025, when the patient was admitted with obstructive jaundice and fever, with acute cholangitis and a bilirubin of 17.2. MRI abdomen showed a new 3.2 × 2.8-cm mass at the pancreatic head/duodenal bulb. He had ERCP and Endoscopic US with stent placement and FNA. PET scan showed controlled disease at the previous metastatic sites and a new pancreatic head lesion. Given concern for either immunotherapy resistance or the emergence of a new primary malignancy, as the previous metastatic sites were stable, treatment was continued with Pembrolizumab, and the patient started on FLOFOX due to elevated bilirubin. After that, the pancreatic head mass biopsy came back showing intact nuclear expression of MMR proteins MLH1 and PMS2, with loss of MSH2 and MSH6 on immunostains. As a result, the patient was placed on a clinical trial with a novel immunotherapy agent. Conclusion: Patients with Lynch syndrome and MSI-H pancreatic cancer may experience durable responses to immunotherapy; however, new lesions can arise despite ongoing disease control. Careful radiographic assessment and individualized treatment approaches, including continuation of immunotherapy and enrollment in clinical trials, are critical in managing these complex scenarios.

Abstract 5 Efficacy of Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy Across Breast Cancer Subtypes: A Retrospective Analysis of Triple Negative,HER-2 Positive and Hormonal Positive Disease

Yara Al-Kanash, Emad Dawoud, Jawaher Ansari

Seha Tewam Hospital, Abu Dhabi, UAE

Introduction: Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) has become an integral part of breast cancer management, particularly in patients with locally advanced disease. Its main role is in tumor down staging, increment in breast- conserving surgery rates and tailoring adjuvant treatment based on response, it is also a reflection of chemosensitivity difference across different subtypes. However, response to NAC varies, it has also distinct outcomes observed in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), hormone receptor-positive/HER2-negative disease, and HER2-positive disease. Methods: A retrospective analysis was conducted on 238 patients with breast cancer who received NAC at Tawam Hospital between 2022 and 2025. Patients were categorized into TNBC, hormone receptor-positive, and HER2-positive subgroups. Demographics, tumor characteristics, clinical and pathological staging and surgical outcomes were collected. The primary endpoint was pathologic complete response (pCR), with secondary endpoints including tumor down staging rates, surgical approach achieved, rate of recurrence or metastasis, and chemotherapy-related side effects, as well as event-free survival outcomes. Descriptive statistics, Chi-square testing, and marginal homogeneity testing were used. Results: The median age was 46 years. The majority of patients exhibited an ECOG PS of 0-1, IDC was the most predominant histology with 93%. Patients were categorized into 68 patients with TNBC, 51 patients with hormone receptor- positive/HER2 negative, and 119 patients with HER2- positive disease. Most of the patients were in stage IIA/IIB for HER2-positive breast cancer and TNBC (73% and 69% respectively); however, in hormonal positive mostly were stage IIB/IIIA of 84%. The primary end point was rate of pCR achieved with neoadjuvant chemotherapy in different molecular subtypes of breast cancer. PCR rates were 64% in TNBC, 49% in HER-2 positive patients, and the least was seen in hormonal-positive breast cancer with a rate of 6%. In regards to type of surgery, breast conservative surgery was achieved in 62% of patients with HER2-positive disease, 59% in hormonal receptor-positive, and 81% in TNBC. Disease recurrence rate was around 2% in hormonal positive breast cancer, 7% in HER2-positive breast cancer, and 6% in TNBC. Conclusion: NAC demonstrates variable efficacy across breast cancer subtypes, with TNBC and HER2-positive disease showing higher chemosensitivity compared to hormone receptor-positive tumors manifested in better pCR rates. These findings reinforce the importance of tailoring NAC to tumor biology, stage, and molecular subtype in order to optimize patient outcomes.

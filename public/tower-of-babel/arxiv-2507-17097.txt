On local rings of finite syzygy representation type
Souvik Dey, Kaito Kimura, Jian Liu, Yuya Otake

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative Noetherian local ring. We give a characterization of when the completion of R R has an isolated singularity. This result simultaneously improves a theorem of Dao and Takahashi and a theorem of Bahlekeh, Hakimian, Salarian, and Takahashi. As an application, we strengthen the Auslander–Huneke–Leuschke–Wiegand theorem in the form refined by Dao and Takahashi. We further investigate the ascent and descent of finite and countable syzygy representation type along the canonical map from R R to its completion. As a consequence, we obtain a complete affirmative answer to Schreyer’s conjecture. We also explore analogues of Chen’s questions in the context of finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type over Cohen–Macaulay rings. The main result in this direction shows that if R R is Cohen–Macaulay and there are only finitely many non-isomorphic indecomposable maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules that are locally free on the punctured spectrum, then either R R is a hypersurface or every Gorenstein projective module is projective, and every Gorenstein projective module over the completion of R R is a direct sum of finitely generated ones. Finally, we study dominant local rings, introduced by Takahashi, under certain finite representation type conditions, and identify a new class of virtually Gorenstein rings.

1.
Introduction

Cohen–Macaulay representation theory, which studies the category of maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over Cohen–Macaulay rings, began in the 1970s. A fundamental problem is to determine when a ring has
finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type
, that is, when there exist only finitely many non-isomorphic indecomposable maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules.

For a Cohen–Macaulay local ring
R
,
Auslander
[
3
]
established that if
R
is complete and of finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type, then it has an isolated singularity. Leuschke and Wiegand
[
43
]
extended this result to excellent local rings. Finally, Huneke and Leuschke
[
32
]
removed the assumption of excellence, showing that the conclusion holds for all Cohen–Macaulay local rings. Herzog
[
30
]
observed that a complete Gorenstein local ring with finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type must be a hypersurface.
In the 1980s, under mild assumptions, complete Gorenstein local rings having finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type were classified as simple hypersurface singularities by Buchweitz, Greuel, and Schreyer
[
14
]
, and Kn̈orrer
[
40
]
.

Recently, Dao and Takahashi
[
22
]
introduced the notion of the dimension of a subcategory of an abelian category with enough projective objects.
In the setting where
R
is Cohen–Macaulay, they proved that
R
has an isolated singularity if the category of maximal Cohen–Macaulay
R
-modules which are locally free on the punctured spectrum of
R
, denoted by
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
, has finite dimension, and the converse also holds when
R
is, in addition, equicharacteristic complete. As an application, they improved the Auslander–Huneke–Leuschke–Wiegand theorem by showing that if there are only finitely many non-isomorphic maximal Cohen–Macaulay
R
-modules that are locally free on the punctured spectrum, then
R
has an isolated singularity

Theorem
1.1
characterizes when the completion
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity. When
R
is a complete Cohen–Macaulay local ring and
\mathcal{X}=\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
, it strengthens the result of Dao and Takahashi mentioned in the last paragraph; see Corollary
3.14
. When
\mathcal{X}=\Omega^{n}\operatorname{mod}(R)
, it specializes to a result of Bahlekeh, Hakimian, Salarian, and Takahashi
[
6
]
; see Corollary
3.9
. It also generalizes a recent result of the first author, Lank, and Takahashi
[
23
]
; see Remark
3.15
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(See
3.7
) Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a commutative Noetherian local ring with depth
t
and Krull dimension
d
. For a subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
such that
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)\subseteq\mathcal{X}\subseteq\Omega^{m}\operatorname{mod}(R)
for some
n\geq 0
and
m\geq d
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{X}\subseteq|\bigoplus_{i=t}^{d}\Omega^{i}k|_{r}
for some
r\geq 1
.

(2)

There exists
G\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
such that
\mathcal{X}\subseteq[G]_{r}
for some
r\geq 1
.

(3)

The ideal
\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\Omega\mathcal{X})
contains some power of the maximal ideal.

(4)

The ideal
\mathsf{ca}(R)
contains some power of the maximal ideal.

(5)

\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity.

In the above result,
\operatorname{mod}(R)
(resp.
\fl(R)
) is the category of finitely generated (resp. finite length)
R
-modules. The category
\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
is the full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
consisting of modules that are locally free on the punctured spectrum. The notation
|G|_{r}
refers to the category generated from
G
by taking
r
-extensions and
1
direct summands, and
[G]_{r}
denotes the ball of radius
r
centered at
G
; cf.
3.3
. The ideal
\mathsf{ca}(R)
is the cohomological annihilator introduced by Iyengar and Takahashi
[
35
]
; cf.
3.5
.

A subcategory
\mathcal{X}
is said to have
finite (resp. countable) representation type
if there are only finitely (resp. countably) many non-isomorphic indecomposable modules belonging to
\mathcal{X}
. The condition that the category of maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over a ring
R
, denoted by
\mathsf{CM}(R)
, has finite representation type, is by definition, the condition that
R
has finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type.

In general, for
n>\dim R
,
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)
is a much smaller subcategory than
\Omega\mathsf{CM}(R)
, however, we surprisingly note that for local CM rings of dimension
2
, the finite representation type of these two subcategories are equivalent.

Theorem 1.2
.

(see
4.10
) Let
R
be a local Cohen–Macaulay ring of dimension
2
and with algebraically closed residue field. Then, the following are equivalent.

(1)

\Omega\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type.

(2)

\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\fl(R)
has finite representation type for some
n\geq 0
.

The following, which strengthens the improved version of the Auslander–Huneke–Leuschke–Wiegand theorem due to Dao and Takahashi
[
22
]
, is a consequence of Theorem
1.1
. In the case
n=1
, it asserts that if
\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity, and hence so does
R
.

Corollary 1.3
.

(See
3.18
) Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring.
If
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type for some
n\geq 0
, then
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. Schreyer
[
51
]
conjectured that
R
has finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type if and only if its completion
\widehat{R}
does. This conjecture was confirmed by Leuschke and Wiegand
[
43
]
in the case where
R
is excellent.
Theorem
1.4
, the main result of Section
4
, provides a complete affirmative answer to Schreyer’s conjecture via its case
n=0
in part (2). The case
n=0
of
Theorem 1.3 (2), i.e., the original Schreyer’s conjecture, was also independently
resolved by Toshinori Kobayashi, although he did not make it public; we thank Takahashi for bringing this to our attention.

In the broader context of modern Cohen–Macaulay representation theory, interest has expanded from maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules to include their higher syzygies and higher torsionfree modules. Theorem
1.4
extends Schreyer’s conjecture to these more general classes and answers the corresponding Schreyer-type questions affirmatively. Building on the work of Kobayashi, Lyle, and Takahashi
[
41
]
, we investigate a syzygy-type problem related to a result of theirs and obtain a generalization; see Proposition
4.13
.
In the result below,
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(R)
is the category of
n
-torsionfree
R
-modules; c.f.
2.8
.

Theorem 1.4
.

(see
4.8
)
Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with Krull dimension
d
. For each
n\geq 0
, we have:

(1)

\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite (resp. countable) representation type if and only if
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(\widehat{R})
has finite (resp. countable) representation type.

(2)

\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type if and only if
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type.

(3)

Suppose that
n\in\{d,d+1\}
.
Then
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(R)
has finite representation type if and only if
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type.

For an Artin algebra
A
, Chen
[
15
, Main Theorem]
, proved that if
A
is Gorenstein, then the category of finitely generated left Gorenstein projective
A
-modules has finite representation type if and only if every left Gorenstein projective
A
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated ones. This result serves as a Gorenstein analogue of Auslander’s celebrated theorem,
which asserts that an Artin algebra
A
is of finite representation type (i.e., there are only finitely many non-isomorphic indecomposable finitely generated left
A
-modules) if and
only if every left
A
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated modules; see
[
1
,
2
]
. In addition, Chen
[
16
, Appendix C]
raised the following questions for an Artin algebra
A
:

•

Is it true that
\Gproj(A)
has finite representation type if and only if every left Gorenstein projective
A
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated ones?

•

If
\Gproj(A)
has finite representation type, is
A
necessarily virtually Gorenstein (c.f.
2.11
)?

•

If
\Gproj(A)=\proj(A)
, does it follow that
\GProj(A)=\Proj(A)
?

In the above,
\Proj(A)
(resp.
\proj(A))
is the category of left projective (resp. finitely generated projective)
A
-modules,
\GProj(A)
(resp.
\Gproj(A))
is the category of left Gorenstein projective (resp. finitely generated Gorenstein projective)
A
-modules.

In
[
9
, Theorem 4.10]
, Beligiannis observed that an Artin algebra
A
satisfies the property that every left Gorenstein projective
A
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated ones if and only if
A
is virtually Gorenstein and
\Gproj(A)
has finite representation type.
In Section
5
, we investigate the following analogues of Chen’s questions
[
16
]
in the context of finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type over Cohen–Macaulay rings:

Question 1.5
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type.

(a)

Is every left Gorenstein projective
A
-module necessarily a direct sum of finitely generated ones?

(b)

Is
R
necessarily virtually Gorenstein?

(c)

If, in addition,
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
, does it follow that
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)?

The main result of Section
5
is Theorem
1.6
, which provides affirmative answers to Questions (a), (b), and (c) above when
R
is assumed to be complete.

Theorem 1.6
.

(See
5.3
)
Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. Assume
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type. Then:

(1)

Either
R
is a hypersurface or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

(2)

Every Gorenstein projective
\widehat{R}
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated ones.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring. If
\Gproj(R)
has finite representation type, Christensen, Piepmeyer, Striuli, and Takahashi
[
19
, Theorem B]
showed that either
R
is Gorenstein or
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
. In particular, when
R
is Cohen–Macaulay and of finite Cohen–Macaulay representation type, it follows that either
R
is Gorenstein or
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
; this application of loc. cit. can also be deduced from Theorem
1.6
(1).

More recently, Takahashi
[
55
]
introduced the notion of
dominant local rings
, and discovered several remarkable properties of such rings—including a classification of thick subcategories in the bounded derived category of a local ring whose certain localizations are dominant. In Section
6
, we establish the following results concerning dominant local rings, providing a class of virtually Gorenstein rings that are not Gorenstein. Another consequence of Theorem
1.7
is the following: Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring of Krull dimension
2
with an algebraically closed residue field. If
\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then either
R
is a hypersurface or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
; see Proposition
6.13
.

Theorem 1.7
.

(See
6.12
)
Let
R
be a dominant local ring. Assume
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(R)\cap\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type for some
n\leq\operatorname{depth}(R)+1
. Then:

(1)

Either
R
is a hypersurface or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

(2)

\widehat{R}
is virtually Grorenstein.

2.
Notation and terminology

Throughout this article, let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Denote by
\operatorname{Mod}(R)
the category of
R
-modules, and by
\operatorname{mod}(R)
the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(R)
consisting of finitely generated
R
-modules. Let
\fl(R)
denote the full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
consisting of modules of finite length. We write
\dim(R)
for the Krull dimension of
R
, and
\operatorname{depth}(M)
for the depth of an
R
-module
M
for each
M\in\operatorname{mod}(R)
. For a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
, the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion of an
R
-module
M
is denoted by
\widehat{M}
. For a full subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
, the
additive closure

\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
is defined as the subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
consisting of direct summands of finite direct sum of modules in
\mathcal{X}
.

2.1
.

Spectrum, singular locus, and isolated singularity.

Let
\Spec(R)
denote the set of all prime ideals of
R
, equipped with the Zariski topology. The closed subsets in this topology are of the form

\operatorname{V}(I)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}

for some ideal
I
of
R
.

The
singular locus

\Sing(R)
of
R
is the set of prime ideals of
R
such that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is not regular; recall that a commutative Noetherian ring
R
is regular if and only if each finitely generated
R
-module has finite projective dimension.
We say that
R
has an
isolated singularity
if any non-maximal prime ideal of
R
does not belong to
\Sing(R)
.

2.2
.

Annihilator and support of modules.

Let
M
be an
R
-module. We write
\operatorname{ann}_{R}M
to be the
annihialtor
of
M
over
R
. That is,
\operatorname{ann}_{R}M=\{a\in R\mid a\cdot M=0\}
. For simplicity, we will use
\operatorname{ann}M
to represent
\operatorname{ann}_{R}M
if there is no confusion.
The
support
of
M
over
R
is

\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid M_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\}.

If
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module, then
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}M=\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}M)
.

2.3
.

An
R
-module
M
is said to be
locally free on the punctured spectrum
if
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a free
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module for any non-maximal prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.
Let
\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
denote the full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
consisting of modules that are locally free on the punctured spectrum.

2.4
.

Maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules.
A finitely generated
R
-module
M
is called
maximal Cohen–Macaulay
if
\operatorname{depth}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
for each prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
; see
[
12
,
44
,
58
]
for more details.
The full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
consisting of maximal Cohen–Macaulay
R
-modules is denoted by
\mathsf{CM}(R)
. A commutative Noetherian ring
R
is said to be
Cohen–Macaulay
if
R\in\mathsf{CM}(R)
.

Let
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
denote the full subcategory of
\mathsf{CM}(R)
consisting of modules that are locally free on the punctured spectrum. That is,
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathsf{CM}(R)\cap\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
.

2.5
.

Resolving subcategories.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
. The subcategory
\mathcal{X}
is said to be
resolving
if
\mathcal{X}
contains
\proj(R)
and it satisfies the following conditions: for any short exact sequence
0\rightarrow M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}\rightarrow M_{3}\rightarrow 0
in
\operatorname{mod}(R)
,

(1)

If
M_{1},M_{3}\in\mathcal{X}
, then
M_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
;

(2)

If
M_{2},M_{3}\in\mathcal{X}
, then
M_{1}\in\mathcal{X}
.

For example, if
R
is Cohen–Macaulay, then
\mathsf{CM}(R)
is a resolving subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
.

2.6
.

Finite (resp. countable) representation type
.
Let
\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
.
We say that
\mathcal{X}
has
finite (resp. countable) representation type
if there are only finitely (resp. countably) many isomorphism classes of indecomposable modules belonging to
\mathcal{X}
.

It is also common to say that
R
has finite Cohen–Macaulay type when
\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type.

2.7
.

Syzygy modules.

For a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, let
\Omega_{R}(M)
denote the
first syzygy
of
M
. That is, there is a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Omega_{R}M\rightarrow P\rightarrow M\to 0,

where
P
is a finitely generated projective
R
-module.
By Schanuel’s Lemma,
\Omega_{R}M

is independent of the choice of the projective resolution of M up to projective summands.

We put
\Omega^{0}_{R}M=M
, and the
n
-th syzygy

\Omega^{n}_{R}M
is defined inductively by
\Omega^{n}_{R}M=\Omega_{R}(\Omega^{n-1}_{R}M)
for each
n>0
. If there is no confusion, we will use
\Omega^{n}M
to represent
\Omega^{n}_{R}M
.
For a subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
, we denote

\Omega^{n}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{M\in\operatorname{mod}(R)\mid M\cong\Omega^{n}X\text{ for some }X\in\mathcal{X}\}.

For a local ring
R
and a full subcategory
\mathcal{Y}
of
\operatorname{mod}(\widehat{R})
, we will use
\Omega^{n}\mathcal{Y}
to represent
\Omega^{n}_{\widehat{R}}\mathcal{Y}
in this article.

2.8
.

Auslander transpose and
n
-torsionfree modules.
Assume
R
is a local ring and
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module. Let

F=\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}F_{0}\rightarrow M\to 0

be a minimal free resolution of
M
. Denote by
(-)^{\ast}
the
R
-dual
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,R)
. The
(Auslander) transpose
of
M
, denoted by
\operatorname{Tr}_{R}M
, is defined to be the cokernal of
(d_{1})^{\ast}
; see
[
4
]
for more details. Note that
\Omega^{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{R}M\cong M^{\ast}
and there exists an exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\operatorname{Tr}_{R}M,R)\to M\xrightarrow{\varphi_{M}}M^{\ast\ast}\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{R}(\operatorname{Tr}_{R}M,R)\to 0,

where
\varphi_{M}:M\to M^{\ast\ast}
is the canonical homomorphism given by
\varphi_{M}(x)(f)=f(x)
for
x\in M
and
f\in M^{\ast}
.
Moreover, the image of
\varphi_{M}
is isomorphic to
\Omega_{R}\operatorname{Tr}_{R}\Omega_{R}\operatorname{Tr}_{R}M
up to projective summands; see
[
4
, Appendix]
.

For each
n\geq 0
,
M
is called
n
-torsionfree
if
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\operatorname{Tr}_{R}M,R)=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. For each
M\in\operatorname{mod}(R)
, if
M
is
n
-torsionfree, then
M
is a
n
-th syzygy module; see
[
4
, Theorem 2.17]
.
The full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
consisting of
n
-torsionfree
R
-modules is denoted by
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(R)
. We denote
\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathsf{TF}_{n}(R)\cap\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
.

2.9
.

Gorenstein rings and hypersurfaces.
For a commutative Noetherian ring
R
, it is said to be
Gorenstein
provided that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
has finite injective dimension as an
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.
For example, every regular ring is Gorenstein.

For a commutative Noetherian local ring
R
, it is said to be a
hypersurface
if its completion
\widehat{R}
is isomorphic to a quotient of a regular local ring by a principal ideal. Every hypersurface is Gorenstein.

2.10
.

Gorenstein projective (resp. injective) modules.

The notions of Gorenstein projective and Gorenstein injective modules will be used in Section
5
and Section
6
. For details, see
[
29
, Chapter 10]
.

Let
\Proj(R)
(resp.
\Inj(R)
) denote the category of projective (resp. injective)
R
-modules.
An
R
-module
M
is called
Gorenstein projective
(resp.
Gorenstein injective
) if there exists an acyclic complex of projective (resp. injective)
R
-modules

\mathbf{P}:\cdots\longrightarrow P_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}P_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}P_{-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\quad\text{(resp. }\mathbf{I}:\cdots\longrightarrow I_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}I_{0}\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}}I_{-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\text{)}

such that
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathbf{P},Q)
(resp.
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(E,\mathbf{I})
) remains acyclic for every
Q\in\Proj(R)
(resp.
E\in\Inj(R)
), and
M
is isomorphic to the image of
d_{0}
(resp.
\partial_{0}
). For example, any projective (resp. injective)
R
-module is Gorenstein projective (resp. Gorenstein injective).
We write
\GProj(R)
(resp.
\GInj(R)
) as the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(R)
consisting of Gorenstein projective modules.

Let
\Gproj(R)
denote the full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
consisting of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
R
-modules. If
R
is Gorenstein, then
\Gproj(R)=\mathsf{CM}(R)
; see
[
4
, Proposition 3.8]
and

2.11
.

Virtually Goresntein rings.

In
[
11
]
, Beligiannis and Reiten introduced the notion of virtually Gorenstein Artin algebras. This concept was extended to commutative Noetherian rings of finite Krull dimension by Zareh-Khoshchehreh, Asgharzadeh, and Divaani-Aazar
[
59
]
. Recently, Di, Liang, and Wang
[
27
]
further generalized the notion of virtually Gorenstein rings to the setting of arbitrary rings.

Precisely, a commutative Noetherian ring
R
is said to be
virtually Gorenstein
if
\GProj(R)^{\perp}=^{\perp}\GInj(R)
, where
\GProj(R)^{\perp}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{Y\in\operatorname{Mod}(R)\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(X,Y)=0\text{ for all }i>0\text{ and }X\in\GProj(R)\}
and
{}^{\perp}\GInj(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{X\in\operatorname{Mod}(R)\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(X,Y)=0\text{ for all }i>0\text{ and }Y\in\GInj(R)\}
. It is known that any Gorenstein ring is virtually Gorenstein; see
[
59
, Example 3.13 (i)]
for finite Krull dimension case and
[
34
, Theorem A.1]
for the general case.

Thick subcategories.
Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category. A full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{T}
is called
thick
provided that it is closed under suspensions, cones, and direct summands.
For a subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{T}
. Let
\thick_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{C})
denote the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
\mathcal{C}
.

2.13
.

Derived categories.

Let
\mathsf{D}(R)
denote the
derived category
of
R
-modules. It is a triangulated category equipped with a suspension functor
[1]
, where for each complex
X
, the shifted complex
X[1]
is given by
(X[1])_{i}=X_{i-1}
and
\partial_{X[1]}=-\partial_{X}
. The
bounded derived category
, denoted by
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
, is the full subcategory of
\mathsf{D}(R)
consisting of complexes
X
whose total homology
\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{H}_{i}(X)
is finitely generated over
R
. Note that
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
inherits the triangulated structure from
\mathsf{D}(R)
and forms a thick subcategory of
\mathsf{D}(R)
.

3.
Finite syzygy representation type

In this section, we investigate finite syzygy representation type. The main results are Theorems
3.7
and
3.23
. In the following, the notation
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)
(resp.
\Omega^{n}\fl(\widehat{R})
will represent
\Omega^{n}_{R}\fl(R)
(resp.
\Omega^{n}_{\widehat{R}}\fl(\widehat{R}))
for each
n\geq 0
; see
2.7
.
For subcategories
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
, set
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigoplus_{i>0,X\in\mathcal{X},Y\in\mathcal{Y}}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(X,Y).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
. If
\mathcal{X}
contains
\Omega^{n}\fl({R})
for some
n\geq 0
, then, for each
m\geq 0
,

\Sing(R)\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\Omega^{m}\fl(R)))\text{ and }\Sing(\widehat{R})\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\Omega^{m}\fl(R)))

Proof.

Set
I=\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}(\Omega^{n}\fl(R),\Omega^{m}\fl(R))
.
By
[
48
, Proposition 3.6]
,
\Sing(R)\subseteq\operatorname{V}(I)
. Since
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, we have

\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\Omega^{m}\fl(R))\subseteq I
. Hence,

\Sing(R)\subseteq\operatorname{V}(I)\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\Omega^{m}\fl(R))).

Note that there is an equivalence

\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}-\colon\fl(R)\xrightarrow{\cong}\fl(\widehat{R}).

This implies that
I\subseteq\operatorname{ann}_{\widehat{R}}\operatorname{Ext}_{\widehat{R}}(\Omega^{n}\fl(\widehat{R}),\Omega^{m}\fl(\widehat{R})
. In particular,
\widehat{I}\subseteq\operatorname{ann}_{\widehat{R}}\operatorname{Ext}_{\widehat{R}}(\Omega^{n}\fl(\widehat{R}),\Omega^{m}\fl(\widehat{R}))
. It follows that

\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\Omega^{m}\fl(R))\subseteq\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}I=\widehat{I}\subseteq\operatorname{ann}_{\widehat{R}}\operatorname{Ext}_{\widehat{R}}(\Omega^{n}\fl(\widehat{R}),\Omega^{m}_{\widehat{R}}\fl(\widehat{R})).

This yields the second inclusion below:

\Sing(\widehat{R})\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}_{\widehat{R}}\operatorname{Ext}_{\widehat{R}}(\Omega^{n}\fl(\widehat{R}),\Omega^{m}\fl(\widehat{R})))\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\Omega^{n}\fl({R}),\Omega^{m}\fl({R}))),

where the first inclusion is by
[
48
, Proposition 3.6]
.
∎

3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
. Set

\operatorname{NF}(\mathcal{X})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid X_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is not free for some }X\in\mathcal{X}\}

to be the
nonfree locus
of
\mathcal{X}
. If
\mathcal{X}=\{X\}
consists of a single module
X\in\operatorname{mod}(R)
, we write
\operatorname{NF}(X)
in place of
\operatorname{NF}(\{X\})
. In this case,
\operatorname{NF}(X)=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(X,\Omega X)
.

3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be subcategories of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
. Denote by
\mathcal{X}\circ\mathcal{Y}
the subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
consisting of modules
Z
which fit into a short exact sequence
0\to X\to Z\to Y\to 0
with
X\in\mathcal{X}
and
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
. In
[
21
, Definitions 2.1 and 5.1]
,
Dao and Takahashi introduced two constructions based on this operation:

(1) An
ascending chain of full subcategories built out of
\mathcal{X}
as follows:
For
r=1
,
|\mathcal{X}|_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}|\mathcal{X}|
, where
|\mathcal{X}|\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
; For
r>1
,
|\mathcal{X}|_{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}||\mathcal{X}|_{r-1}\circ\mathcal{X}|
. For each
i,j\geq 1
,
||\mathcal{X}|_{i}\circ|\mathcal{X}|_{j}|=|\mathcal{X}|_{i+j}
, and hence
||\mathcal{X}|_{i}|_{j}=|\mathcal{X}|_{ij}
.

(2) The
ball of radius
r
centered at
\mathcal{X}
as follows:
For
r=1
,
[\mathcal{X}]_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[\mathcal{X}]
, where
[\mathcal{X}]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{add}\{R\cup\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\Omega^{i}\mathcal{X}\}
;
For
r>1
,
[\mathcal{X}]_{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[[\mathcal{X}]_{r-1}\circ[\mathcal{X}]]
.
Similarly, this satisfies
[[\mathcal{X}]_{i}\circ[\mathcal{X}]_{j}]=[\mathcal{X}]_{i+j}
for each
i,j\geq 1
, and hence
[[\mathcal{X}]_{i}]_{j}=[\mathcal{X}]_{ij}
.

In
[
22
, Definition 3.3]
, Dao and Takahashi introduced the
dimension
of
\mathcal{X}
, denoted by

\dim\mathcal{X}
, to be the infimum of the integers
n\geq 0
such that
\mathcal{X}=[G]_{n+1}
for some

G\in\mathcal{X}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
.
Then:

(1)

\operatorname{NF}(\mathcal{X})\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{mod}(R)))

(2)

If there exists
G\in\operatorname{mod}(R)
and
i\geq 0
such that
\mathcal{X}\subseteq[G]_{i}
, then

\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{mod}(R)))\subseteq\operatorname{NF}(G).

Proof.

(1) The statement follows from the equality
\operatorname{NF}(\mathcal{X})=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{mod}(R))
; see
[
22
, Remark 5.2]
.
Next, we prove (2). Since
\mathcal{X}\subseteq[G]_{i}
, we have

\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{mod}(R)))\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}([G]_{i},\operatorname{mod}(R))).

It follows from
[
22
, Lemma 5.3]
that
\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}([G]_{i},\operatorname{mod}(R)))=\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(G,\operatorname{mod}(R)))
. Note that
\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(G,\operatorname{mod}(R))=\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(G,\Omega^{1}(G))
; see
[
35
, Lemma 2.14]
. Thus, we conclude that

\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{mod}(R)))\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(G,\Omega G)=\operatorname{NF}(G).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.5
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. For each integer
n\geq 0
, denote by
\mathsf{ca}^{n}(R)
the ideal consisting of elements
a\in R
such that
a\cdot\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{n}(M,N)=0
for all finitely generated
R
-modules
M
and
N
.
The
cohomology annihilator
, introduced by Iyengar and Takahashi
[
35
, Definition 2.1]
, is defined as the union

\mathsf{ca}(R):=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{ca}^{n}(R).

Since
R
is Noetherian, the ascending chain of ideals

\mathsf{ca}^{0}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{ca}^{1}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{ca}^{2}(R)\subseteq\cdots

stabilizes, and hence
\mathsf{ca}(R)=\mathsf{ca}^{n}(R)
for some
n\geq 0
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
R
be a quasi-excellent local ring. Then

\Sing(R)=\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))

for each
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)\subseteq\mathcal{X}\subseteq\Omega^{m}\operatorname{mod}(R)
and each
\Omega^{l}\fl(R)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\operatorname{mod}(R)
with
n,m\geq 0
and
m\geq\dim(R)
.

Proof.

Set
d=\dim(R)
. By Lemma
3.1
, one has

\Sing(R)\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\Omega^{n}\fl(R),\Omega^{l}\fl(R))).
Combining with
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\Omega^{l}\fl(R)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
, we get that
\Sing(R)\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))
.

It remains to prove
\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))\subseteq\Sing(R)
.
Note that
\mathsf{ca}^{d+1}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
as
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\Omega^{m}\operatorname{mod}(R)
with
m\geq d
. Hence,

\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\mathsf{ca}^{d+1}(R)).

Since
R
is quasi-excellent,
[
39
, Theorem 1.1]
implies that
\mathsf{ca}^{d+1}(R)
defines the singular locus of
R
. That is,

\Sing(R)=\operatorname{V}(\mathsf{ca}^{d+1}(R)).

This completes the proof.
∎

In
[
22
, Theorem 1.1 (1)]
, Dao and Takahashi observed that, for an equicharacteristic complete Cohen–Macaulay local ring
R
with a perfect residue field, the ring
R
has an isolated singularity if and only if the dimension of
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
is finite, that is, there exists an object
G\in\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
and an integer
r\geq 0
such that
\mathsf{CM}_{0}R=[G]_{r}
.

When
R
is not necessarily Cohen–Macaulay, we may not know whether
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
contains any nontrivial objects, so we need to consider a different subcategory, as we do below. It is worth mentioning that our study applies to arbitrary local rings, without imposing any additional assumptions.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a commutative Noetherian local ring with depth
t
and Krull dimension
d
. For a subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
, consider the following conditions:

(1)

\mathcal{X}\subseteq|\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}\Omega^{i}k|_{r}
for some
r\geq 1
.

(2)

There exists
G\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
such that
\mathcal{X}\subseteq|G|_{r}
for some
r\geq 1
.

(3)

There exists
G\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
such that
\mathcal{X}\subseteq[G]_{r}
for some
r\geq 1
.

(4)

For each subcategory
\mathcal{Y}
of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
, the ideal
\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
contains some power of the maximal ideal.

(5)

There exists a subcategory
\mathcal{Y}
of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
containing
\Omega\mathcal{X}
such that the ideal
\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
contains some power of the maximal ideal.

(6)

\mathsf{ca}(R)
contains some power of the maximal ideal.

(7)

\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity.

Then
(1)\iff(2)\iff(3)\iff(4)\iff(5)
and
(6)\iff(7)
. The implication
(5)\Longrightarrow(6)
holds if, in addition,
\mathcal{X}
contains
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)
for some
n\geq 0
.

All the above conditions are equivalent if, in addition,
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)\subseteq\mathcal{X}\subseteq\Omega^{m}\operatorname{mod}(R)
for some
n\geq 0
and
m\geq d
. In this case, they are also equivalent to the following condition:

(8)
\mathcal{X}\subseteq|\bigoplus_{i=t}^{d}\Omega^{i}k|_{r}
for some
r\geq 1
.

Proof.

The implications
(1)\Longrightarrow(2)\Longrightarrow(3)
and
(4)\Longrightarrow(5)
are trivial. The equivalence
(6)\iff(7)
follows from
[
38
, Proposition 2.4 (1)]
.

(3)\Longrightarrow(4)
. By Lemma
3.4
(2),
\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{mod}(R)))\subseteq\operatorname{NF}(G).
Note that
\operatorname{NF}(G)\subseteq\{\mathfrak{m}\}
as
G\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
. Thus,
\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{mod}(R)))\subseteq\{\mathfrak{m}\}
. This yields that
\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{mod}(R))
contains some power of the maximal ideal. The desired result follows as
\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{mod}(R))\subseteq\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
.

(5)\Longrightarrow(1)
. This can be proved by the same argument as in the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 3.1 (1)]
. Assume
\mathfrak{m}^{s}\subseteq\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
for some
s>0
. Choose a system of parameter
{\bm{x}}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{s}
of
R
.
For each
M\in\mathcal{X}
, let
H_{i}
be the
i
-th Koszul homology of
M
with respect to
{\bm{x}}
for each integer
i
. Note that
\Omega M\in\mathcal{Y}
as
\Omega\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
by the assumption. Hence,
({\bm{x}})\cdot\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,\Omega M)=0
.

It follows from
[
54
, Corollary 3.2(2)]
that for each
1\leq i\leq d
, there is an short exact sequence

0\rightarrow H_{i}\rightarrow E_{i}\rightarrow\Omega E_{i-1}\rightarrow 0

with
E_{0}=H_{0}
such that
M
is a dirct summand of
E_{d}
.
Thus,
M
belongs to
|\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}\Omega^{i}H_{d-i}|_{d+1}
.
On the other hand, note that
R/({\bm{x}})
is Artinian. Let
l
denote the Loewy length of
R/({\bm{x}})
. That is,
l=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}\mid(\mathfrak{m}/({\bm{x}}))^{i}=0\}
. For each
0\leq i\leq d
, since
{\bm{x}}
annihilates
H_{i}
, we have
\mathfrak{m}^{l}\cdot\operatorname{H}_{i}=0
.
This implies that, for each
0\leq i\leq d
,
H_{d-i}
is in
|k|_{l}
, and hence
\Omega^{i}H_{d-i}
is in
|\Omega^{i}k|_{l}
.
Therefore, we obtain

M\in|\bigoplus_{i=t}^{d}\Omega^{i}H_{d-i}|_{d+1}\subseteq|\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}\Omega^{i}k|_{l(d+1)}.

This yields
(1)
.

Assume, in addition,
\mathcal{X}
contains
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)
for some
n\geq 0
. Choose
\mathcal{Y}=\fl(R)
. Assume
(4)
holds. This yields that
\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
contains some powers of
\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}
. By Lemma
3.1
,
(7)
holds. This yields that
(4)\Longrightarrow(7)
, and hence
(5)\Longrightarrow(6)
as
(4)\iff(5)
and
(6)\iff(7)
.

Next, assume, in addition,
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)\subseteq\mathcal{X}\subseteq\Omega^{m}\operatorname{mod}(R)
for some
n\geq 0
and
m\geq d
.

(6)\Longrightarrow(5)
. Since
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\Omega^{m}\operatorname{mod}(R)
, we have
\mathsf{ca}^{m+1}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\Omega\mathcal{X})
. As

\mathsf{ca}^{d+1}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{ca}^{m+1}(R)
, and hence
\mathsf{ca}^{d+1}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\Omega\mathcal{X})

\operatorname{V}(\mathsf{ca}(R))=\operatorname{V}(\mathsf{ca}^{d+1}(R)).

Hence, the assumption in
(6)
implies that
\mathsf{ca}^{d+1}(R)
contains some power of the maximal ideal. It follows that
\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}(\mathcal{X},\Omega\mathcal{X})
also contains some power of the maximal ideal. Setting

completes the proof.

It remains to show that all the conditions are equivalent to
(8)
. The implication
(8)\Longrightarrow(1)
holds trivially. To prove
(1)\Longrightarrow(8)
, it suffices to prove
(5)\Longrightarrow(8)
as
(1)\iff(5)
. This follows from a similar argument as in the proof of
(5)\Longrightarrow(1)
. Assume that condition

, let

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
that is closed under finite direct sums and direct summands. If
\mathcal{X}
has finite representation type, then
\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{add}M
for some

. The converse holds if, in addition,

is semilocal.

Proof.

Assume
\mathcal{X}
has finitely many indecomposable modules. Let

be all representatives of isomorphism classes of indecomposable modules belonging to

. Let

and

. Let

, and let

be an indecomposable decomposition of

. Since
\mathcal{X}
is closed under direct summands, for each

, there exists

such that

, and hence

. This implies that
\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{add}M
.

Assume
\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{add}M
for some
M\in\mathcal{X}
and
R
is semilocal. By
[
57
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\mathcal{X}
has finitely many indecomposable modules.
∎

and

Next, we present several examples of local rings with finite syzygy representation type.

Example 3.17
.

Let
k
be a field.

(1) Let
R=k\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(x^{m},xy,y^{n})
, where
m,n\geq 2
.
Then
R
is an Artinian local ring.
In particular,
\fl(R)=\operatorname{mod}(R)
and
R
has an isolated singularity.
Since
R
is not a principal ideal ring,
\operatorname{mod}(R)
does not have finite representation type; see
[
44
, Theorem 3.3]
for instance.
On the other hand,
(x,y)=(x)\oplus(y)
in
R
.
By
[
56
, Proposition 2.5]
, for any
M\in\operatorname{mod}(R)
, there is an isomorphism

\Omega_{R}^{2}M\cong\Omega_{A}^{2}(M/yM)\oplus\Omega_{B}^{2}(M/xM)\oplus(y)^{\oplus a}\oplus(x)^{\oplus b}

for some integers
a,b\geq 0
, where
A=R/(y)
and
B=R/(x)
.
Hence,
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{2}\operatorname{mod}(R)
has finite representation type as
A
and
B
are Artinian principal ideal rings; see
[
44
, Theorem 3.3]
again.
When
m=n=2
,
\Omega\operatorname{mod}(R)
has finite representation type because
\Omega\operatorname{mod}(R)
belongs to
\operatorname{mod}(k)
.

(2) Consider the local ring
R=k\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(x^{2},xy)
.
This is a typical non-Cohen–Macaulay local ring of depth 0 and dimension 1.
Take an indecomposable torsionless
R
-module
M
which is not free.
Then, since
M
is a submodule of a module of the form
\mathfrak{m}R^{a}
for
a\geq 1
, it is killed by the element
x\in R
.
The quotient ring
R/(x)\cong k\llbracket y\rrbracket
is a principal ideal domain. Hence, by the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain,
M
is isomorphic to either
R/(x)
or
R/(x,y^{n})
for some
n\geq 1
.
But the
R
-module
R/(x,y^{n})
is not torsionless when
n\geq 2
.
Indeed, there is an isomorphism
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/(x,y^{n}),R)\cong\operatorname{ann}(x,y^{n});~f\mapsto f(\overline{1})
. This implies that

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/(x,y^{n}),R)\cong(x);~f\mapsto f(\overline{1})

for
n\geq 2
. It follows that there is no injective map from
R/(x,y^{n})
to a free
R
-module if
n\geq 2
. Thus,
M
is isomorphic to either
R/(x)
or
R/(x,y)
. Consequently,
\Omega\operatorname{mod}(R)
has finite representation type.

It is known that a Cohen–Macaulay local ring
R
with finite Cohen–Macaulay type has an isolated singularity; this is due to Auslander when
R
is complete, due to Leuschke and Wiegand when
R
is excellent, and due to Huneke and Leuschke for the general case. Recently, Dao and Takahashi
[
22
, Corollary 1.2]
improved this famous result to the following: for a Cohen–Macaulay local ring
R
, if
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then
R
has an isolated singularity.

The following result improves the above result of Dao and Takahashi.

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. Then:

(1)

If
\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity, and hence
\Omega\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type.

(2)

If
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type for some
n\geq 0
, then
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity, and hence
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type.

Proof.

Let
d
denote the Krull dimension of
R
. By
3.13
,
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)=\operatorname{add}\Omega^{d}\fl(R)
. This yields that
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)=\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n+d}\fl(R)
. If
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, it follows from that Corollary
3.12
that
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity. Consequently,
\mathsf{CM}(R)=\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
. This proves (2).

For (1), by
3.16
,

\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)=\operatorname{add}\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R).

The desired result now follows from (2).
∎

4.
Schreyer-type questions

The main result in this section is Theorem
1.4
from the introduction. We also obtain a syzygy version of a result of Kobayashi, Lyle, and Takahashi
[
41
, Theorem 4.4]
; see Proposition
4.13
. In the following, we will study the relationships between certain subcategories of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
and subcategories of
\operatorname{mod}(\widehat{R})
by comparing
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\fl(R)
and
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\fl(\widehat{R})
.
We prepare a basic lemma for this purpose.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
\mathcal{X}
be a subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
.
The following are equivalent.

(1)

\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
has countable representation type.

(2)

There are only countably many isomorphism classes of modules belonging to
\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
.

(3)

There are only countably many isomorphism classes of modules belonging to
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

The implication
(2)\Longrightarrow(3)
is straightforward.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
. Suppose that
R
has countable
\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
-representation type.
Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
be all representatives of isomorphism classes of indecomposable modules belonging to
\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
.
We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of non-negative integers.
For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a surjection
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}^{m};~n\mapsto(a_{1}^{(m,n)},\ldots,a_{m}^{(m,n)})
.
Then the map

\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X};~(m,n)\mapsto X_{1}^{a_{1}^{(m,n)}}\oplus\cdots\oplus X_{m}^{a_{m}^{(m,n)}}

is surjective up to isomorphism.

(3)\Longrightarrow(1)
. Assume that there are only countably many isomorphism classes of modules belonging to
\mathcal{X}
.
Put
\mathcal{Y}=\{X_{1}^{a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus X_{m}^{a_{m}}\mid m>0,X_{i}\in\mathcal{X},a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
for all
1\leq i\leq m\}
.
Using the same argument as in the previous paragraph, it follows that there are only countably many isomorphism classes of modules belonging to
\mathcal{Y}
.
For each
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
, there are, up to isomorphism, only finitely many indecomposable modules which are direct summands of
Y
by
[
57
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Any indecomposable module belonging to
\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
is isomorphic to a direct summand of some module belonging to
\mathcal{Y}
, which means that
R
has countable
\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
-representation type.
∎

The following result concerns the ascent and descent of finite and countable representation types for the categories of syzygies of finite length modules.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
n\geq 0
be an integer.
Then
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\fl(R)
has finite (resp. countable) representation type if and only if
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\fl(\widehat{R})
has finite (resp. countable) representation type.

Proof.

First, we deal with the case of finite representation types.
The “if” part is an immediate consequence of
[
57
, Theorem 1.4]
.
Any
\widehat{R}
-module belonging to
\fl(\widehat{R})
is in
\fl(R)
as an
R
-module.
For all
M\in\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\fl(\widehat{R})
, there exists
N\in\Omega^{n}\fl(R)
such that
M
is a direct summand of
N\otimes_{R}\widehat{R}
.
The “only if” part now follows from
[
57
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Next, we handle the case of countable representation types.
Since the objects of
\fl(R)
and
\fl(\widehat{R})
correspond one-to-one, there is a natural one-to-one correspondence between the sets of isomorphism classes of modules in
\Omega^{n}\fl(R)
and
\Omega^{n}\fl(\widehat{R})
by
[
44
, Corollary 1.15]
.
The assertion now follows from Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a commutative Noetherian local ring with Krull dimension
d
. Then:

(1)

\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)=\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}^{0}_{n}(R)=\left\{M\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)\mid\operatorname{depth}(M)\geq n\right\}
for each
0\leq n\leq\operatorname{depth}(R)
.

(2)

\mathsf{TF}_{n+1}^{0}(R)=\Omega\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)=\Omega\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}^{0}_{n}(R)
for each
0\leq n\leq\operatorname{depth}(R)
.

(3)

If, in addition,
R
is Cohen–Macaulay, then

\mathsf{TF}_{d}^{0}(R)=\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}_{d}^{0}(R)=\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)\text{ and }\mathsf{TF}_{d+1}^{0}(R)=\Omega\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}_{d}^{0}(R)=\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R).

Proof.

The statement (3) follows immediately from (1) and (2). Next, assume
0\leq n\leq\operatorname{depth}(R)
.

(1) The equality
\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}^{0}_{n}(R)=\left\{M\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}R\mid\operatorname{depth}(M)\geq n\right\}
is by the definition of
\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{0}(R)
. Since
n\leq\operatorname{depth}(R)
,
\mathfrak{m}\notin\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\operatorname{depth}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq n-1\}
. In particular, for each
M\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
,
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is free for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\operatorname{depth}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq n-1\}
. By
[
28
, Proposition 2.4 (b)]
,
\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)=\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}^{0}_{n}(R)
.

(2) The equality
\Omega\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)=\Omega\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}^{0}_{n}(R)
follows from (1). It remains to prove
\mathsf{TF}_{n+1}^{0}(R)=\Omega\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)
.

For each
M\in\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)
, take a minimal free resolution of
M^{\ast}
as follows:

F_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{n}}F_{n-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}F_{0}\rightarrow M^{\ast}\rightarrow 0.

By the hypothesis,
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\operatorname{Tr}_{R}M,R)=0
for all
1\leq i<n+1
. This implies that
M\cong M^{\ast\ast}
and
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M^{\ast},R)=0
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
(see
2.8
), and hence the above free resolution of
M^{\ast}
induces an exact sequence:

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow F_{0}^{\ast}\xrightarrow{d_{1}^{\ast}}F_{1}^{\ast}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{n-1}^{\ast}\xrightarrow{d_{n}^{\ast}}F_{n}^{\ast}\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0

where
C=\operatorname{Coker}(d_{n}^{\ast})
. It follows that
M=\Omega^{n+1}C
. We claim that
C\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
. Indeed, since
M\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
, one has
M^{\ast}\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
as well. It follows that the free resolution of
M^{\ast}
above splits after localization at any non-maximal prime ideal. In particular, for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
,
\Ker(d_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}
is projective and
C_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\Ker(d_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ast}
. It follows that
C\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
, and hence
\Ima(d_{1}^{\ast})=\Omega^{n}C
is in
\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
. Moreover,
\operatorname{depth}(\Omega^{n}N)\geq n
by the depth formula; see
[
12
, 1.3.7]
. We conclude that
\Ima(d_{1}^{\ast})=\Omega^{n}(C)\in\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)
by (1). This implies that
M=\Omega\Ima(d_{1}^{\ast})\in\Omega\mathsf{TF}_{t}^{0}(R)
.

Conversely, let
M\in\Omega\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)
. By
[
4
, Theorem 2.17]
, any
n
-torsionfree module is a
n
-th syzygy module. It follows that

M
is an
(n+1)
-th syzygy. Combining with the assumption that
M
is locally free on the punctured spectrum, we deduce from
[
45
, Theorem 43]
that
M
is
(n+1)
-torsionfree. Thus,
M\in\mathsf{TF}_{n+1}^{0}(R)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Proposition
4.3
(1) strengthens the first equality in
[
26
, Corollary 4.3]
, which was proved by the first author and Takahashi.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring. If
\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)
has finite representation type for some
n\leq\operatorname{depth}(R)+1
, then
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity.

Proof.

If
n\leq\operatorname{depth}(R)
and
\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then Proposition
4.3
implies that
\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{0}(R)
also has finite representation type. Since
\Omega\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{0}(R)\subseteq\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{0}(R)
, it follows that
\Omega\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{0}(R)
has finite representation type as well. By Theorem
3.23
, we get that
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity.

If
n=\operatorname{depth}(R)+1
and
\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)
has finite representation type, the conclusion follows directly from Proposition
4.3
and Theorem
3.23
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

(1) Let
t
denote the depth of a local ring
R
. For each
n\geq t
,

\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}_{n}^{0}(R)=\{M\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)\mid\operatorname{depth}(M)\geq t\}=\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}_{t}^{0}(R).

Combining with this, Proposition
4.3
shows that Theorem
3.23
and Corollary
4.5
are equivalent statements.

(2) If
0\leq m\leq n
, then
\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{TF}_{m}^{0}(R)
. It follows that Corollary
4.5
is also equivalent to the following: Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring with depth

. If
\mathsf{TF}_{t+1}^{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity.

4.7
.

For a Cohen–Macaulay local ring
R
, Schreyer conjectured in
[
51
, Conjecture 7.3]
that

R
has finite CM type if and only if the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion
\widehat{R}
has finite CM type.

Leuschke and Wiegand
[
43
, Main Theorem]
established that the conjecture holds if, in addition,
R
is excellent. They also proved that the conjecture fails without the Cohen–Macaulay assumption; see
[
43
, Examples 2.1 and 2.2]
.

The case
n=0
in statement (2) of the following theorem provides an affirmative answer to Schreyer’s conjecture.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with Krull dimension
d
. For each
n\geq 0
, we have:

(1)

\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite (resp. countable) representation type if and only if
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(\widehat{R})
has finite (resp. countable) representation type.

(2)

\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type if and only if
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type.

(3)

Suppose that
n\in\{d,d+1\}
.
Then
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(R)
has finite representation type if and only if
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type.

Proof.

(1) For any Cohen–Macaulay local ring
A
of dimension
d
,
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(A)=\operatorname{add}\Omega^{d}\fl(A)
by
3.13
, and hence we have
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(A)=\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n+d}\fl(A)
.
Therefore, it follows from Corollary
3.12
and Proposition
4.2
that the statement (1) holds.

(2) Let
A\in\{R,\widehat{R}\}
.
If
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(A)
has finite representation type, then Corollary
3.18
implies that
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity. Consequently,
\mathsf{CM}(R)=\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
and
\mathsf{CM}(\widehat{R})=\mathsf{CM}_{0}(\widehat{R})
. Thus, the equivalence in (2) follows from (1).

(3) Let
A\in\{R,\widehat{R}\}
. By Proposition
4.3
,
\mathsf{TF}_{d}^{0}(A)=\mathsf{CM}_{0}(A)
and
\mathsf{TF}_{d+1}^{0}(A)=\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(A)
. Moreover,
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(A)
and
\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(A)
are closed under direct summands; see
3.16
for the second one. Thus, (1) implies that
\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)
has finite representation type if and only if
\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type. Assume
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(A)
has finite representation type, then Corollary
4.5
implies that
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity, and hence
\mathsf{CM}(A)=\mathsf{CM}_{0}(A)
. Combining with
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(A)\subseteq\mathsf{CM}(A)
, we conclude that
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(R)=\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)
and
\mathsf{TF}_{n}(\widehat{R})=\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(\widehat{R})
. Thus, the equivalence in (3) holds.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

(1) Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. Combining with Corollary
3.18
, parts (1) and (2) of Theorem
4.8
imply that, for each
n\in\{0,1\}
, the following are equivalent.

•

\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type.

•

\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type.

•

\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type.

•

\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type.

(2) For a Cohen–Macaulay local ring
R
, the fact that finite Cohen–Macaulay type descends from
\widehat{R}
to
R
is due to Wiegand
[
57
]
.
Conversely, along the same line as the proof of Theorem
4.8
(2), one can show that finite Cohen–Macaulay type ascends from
R
to
\widehat{R}
by combining Takahashi’s result
[
52
, Corollary 3.6]
with a recent observation of the second author
[
38
, Remark 2.10]
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. Consider the following conditions:

(1)

\Omega\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type.

(2)

\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\fl(R)
has finite representation type for some
n\geq 0
.

(3)

\widehat{R}
has isolated singularity and the Grothendieck group of
\operatorname{mod}(\widehat{R})
is a finitely generated abelian group.

(4)

\Spec(\widehat{R})
has isolated rational singularities (cf.
[
44
, Definition 6.32]
).

Then
(1)\Longrightarrow(2)\Longrightarrow(3)
. If, in addition,
\dim(R)=2
and the residue field of
R
is algebraically closed, then all the above conditions are equivalent, and they imply that
R
has minimal multiplicity.

Proof.

Let
d
denote the Krull dimension of
R
.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
. By
[
25
, Lemma 3.1(2)]
, we have
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{d+1}\fl(R)\subseteq\Omega\mathsf{CM}(R)
, hence the claim.

(2)\Longrightarrow(3)
. By
4.2
,
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\fl(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type. Hence, we may assume
R
is complete. By
3.12
,
R
has an isolated singularity. By
[
24
, 4.6]
,
n\geq d
. It follows from
3.13
that
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type. Let
H_{1},...,H_{l}
be these finitely many indecomposable objects. Then, for each
M\in\operatorname{mod}(R)
, there exists non-negative integers
a_{1},...,a_{l}
such that
\Omega^{n}M\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}H_{i}^{\oplus a_{i}}
. Since
[M]\in\mathbb{Z}[R]+\mathbb{Z}[\Omega^{n}M]
in the Grothendieck group, we conclude that the group is finitely generated as an abelian group by
[R],[H_{1}],...,[H_{l}]
.

Next, assume, in addition,
\dim(R)=2
and the residue field of
R
is algebraically closed.

(3)\Longrightarrow(4)
. Note that
\widehat{R}
is excellent, Henselian, and has the same residue field as
R
. Since
\dim\widehat{R}=2
,
\widehat{R}
satisfies
(R_{1})
, i.e.,
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\widehat{R}
with height at most
1
. Since
\widehat{R}
is also Cohen–Macaulay, by Serre’s criteria,
\widehat{R}
is normal. Combining with the fact that
\widehat{R}
is local, we conclude that
\widehat{R}
is an integral domain. So,
\Spec(\widehat{R})
has rational singularities by
[
20
, Corollary 3.3]
.

(4)\Longrightarrow(1)
. Note that
\widehat{R}
is excellent, Henselian, and has the same residue field as
R
. Since
\dim\widehat{R}=2
,
\widehat{R}
satisfies
(R_{1})
, i.e.,
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\widehat{R}
with height at most
1
. Since
\widehat{R}
is also Cohen–Macaulay, by Serre’s criteria,
\widehat{R}
is normal. Combining with the fact that
\widehat{R}
is local, we conclude that
\widehat{R}
is an integral domain. By
[
20
, Corollary 3.3]
,
\Omega\mathsf{CM}(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type. The desired result of (1) now follows from Theorem
4.8
.

Finally, by
[
44
, Corollary 6.36]
, if one of the above equivalent conditions holds, then
\widehat{R}
has minimal multiplicity, and hence so does
R
.
∎

At the end of this section, we consider a syzygy version of
\mathsf{CM}_{+}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathsf{CM}(R)\setminus\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
studied by Kobayashi, Lyle, and Takahashi
[
41
]
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
, set
\mathcal{X}_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{X}\cap\operatorname{mod}_{0}(R)
and
\mathcal{X}_{+}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{0}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
\mathcal{X}
a full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}(R)
.
Suppose that for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sing(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
, there exists
n\geq 0
such that
\Omega^{n}(R/\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{X}
.
If
(\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X})_{+}
has finite (resp. countable) representation type, then
\Sing(R)
is a finite (resp. countable) set.

Proof.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sing(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
.
Since
\Omega_{R}^{n}(R/\mathfrak{p})
is in
\mathcal{X}
for some
n\geq 0
, there are indecomposable modules
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
belonging to
(\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X})_{+}
, positive integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
, and
Y\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}R
such that

\Omega^{n}(R/\mathfrak{p})\cong X_{1}^{\oplus a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus X_{m}^{\oplus a_{m}}\oplus Y.

Noting that
\mathfrak{p}
belongs to
\Sing(R)
, we get the equality below:

\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(\Omega^{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}),\Omega^{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}))=\bigcap_{1\leq i,j\leq m}\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(X_{i},X_{j})\cap\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(Y,M)

for some
R
-module
M
, where the second equality is by the decomposition of
\Omega^{n}(R/\mathfrak{p})
.
Since
\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(Y,M)
contains some power of the maximal ideal of
R
, we conclude that
\mathfrak{p}
is equal to
\operatorname{ann}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(X_{i},X_{j})
for some
1\leq i,j\leq m
.
It follows that only finite (resp. countable) ideals are represented in this way.
∎

The following result is a direct consequence of Lemma
4.11
. It is a syzygy version of
[
41
, Theorem 4.2]
and its proof is similar as well.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
n\geq 0
. Then:

(1)

If
(\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\operatorname{mod}(R))_{+}
has finite (resp. countable) representation type, then
\Sing(R)
is a finite (resp. countable) set.

(2)

If
R
is Cohen–Macaulay and
(\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R))_{+}
has finite (resp. countable) representation type, then
\Sing(R)
is a finite (resp. countable) set.

The following result is a syzygy version of
[
41
, Theorem 4.4]
.
However, since
\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R)
is not necessarily closed under direct summands for
n\geq 2
, the same proof does not work.
We were able to address that issue by utilizing
[
57
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
.
Suppose that
(\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R))_{+}
has finite (resp. countable) representation type.
Then
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
has finite (resp. countable) representation type for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a non-maximal prime ideal of
R
and let
M\in\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
There exists an
R
-module
L
such that
L_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathsf{CM}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
M
is a direct summand of
\Omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{n}L_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since
R
admits a canonical module, we can take a maximal Cohen–Macaulay approximation
0\to Y\to X\to L\to 0
of
L
, where
X\in\mathsf{CM}(R)
and
Y\in\operatorname{mod}(R)
has finite injective dimension.
The exact sequence
0\to Y_{\mathfrak{p}}\to X_{\mathfrak{p}}\to L_{\mathfrak{p}}\to 0
splits as
L_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathsf{CM}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
Y_{\mathfrak{p}}
has finite injective dimension over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
; see
[
12
, 3.1.24]
.
This implies that
M
is a direct summand of
\Omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{n}X_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
The
R
-module
\Omega_{R}^{n}X
is in
\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R)
.
There are indecomposable modules
K_{1},\ldots,K_{m}
belonging to
(\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R))_{+}
, positive integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
, and
N\in\operatorname{mod}_{0}R
such that

\Omega_{R}^{n}X\cong K_{1}^{\oplus a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus K_{m}^{\oplus a_{m}}\oplus N.

We obtain
M\in\operatorname{add}\{(K_{1})_{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus\cdots\oplus(K_{m})_{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus R_{\mathfrak{p}}\}
.
It follows from
[
57
, Theorem 1.1]
that there are, up to isomorphism, only finitely many indecomposable
R
-modules in the subcategory
\operatorname{add}\{(K_{1})_{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus\cdots\oplus(K_{m})_{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus R_{\mathfrak{p}}\}
of
\operatorname{mod}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
; see also Lemma
3.11
.
On the other hand, there are only finitely (resp. countably) many subcategories of
\operatorname{mod}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
defined in such a way since
(\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}(R))_{+}
has finite (resp. countable) representation type.
∎

5.
On local rings of finite Cohen–Macaulay type

In this section, we study the questions of Chen
[
16
]
in the context of Cohen–Macaulay type; see
5.2
. The main result in this section is Theorem
5.3
.

5.1
.

Let
A
be an Artin algebra over a commutative Artinian ring. In
[
16
, Appendix C]
, Chen raised the following questions:

(1)

Is it true that
\Gproj(A)
has finite representation type if and only if every left Gorenstein projective
A
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated ones?

(2)

If
\Gproj(A)
has finite representation type, is
A
necessarily virtually Gorenstein (c.f.
2.11
)?

(3)

If
\Gproj(A)=\proj(A)
, does it follow that
\GProj(A)=\Proj(A)
?

Here,
\Proj(A)
(resp.
\proj(A))
is the category of left (resp. finitely generated left) projective
A
-modules, and
\GProj(A)
(resp.
\Gproj(A))
is the category of left (resp. finitely generated left) Gorenstein projective
A
-modules.

As shown by Chen
[
15
, Main Theorem]
, Question (1) has an affirmative answer when
A
is a Gorenstein Artin algebra. This result can be viewed as a Gorenstein analogue of Auslander’s celebrated theorem,
which establishes that an Artin algebra
A
is of finite representation type (i.e., there are only finitely many indecomposable finitely generated left
A
-modules up to isomorphisms) if and
only if every left
A
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated modules; see
[
1
,
2
]
. In
[
9
, Theorem 4.10]
, Beligiannis observed that an Artin algebra
A
satisfies the property that every left Gorenstein projective
A
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated ones if and only if
A
is virtually Gorenstein and
\Gproj(A)
has finite representation type.

It is worth noting that a positive answer to Question (2) was proposed in Beligiannis’s article
[
8
, Example 8.4(2)]
. However, as Beligiannis later pointed out to Chen, the argument is incorrect; see
[
16
, Appendix C]
. Therefore, both Question (1) and Question (2) remain open.
Also, note that if Question (1) is true, then so is Question (3).

5.2
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. Motivated by the questions of Chen in
5.1
,
we raise the following questions:

(a)

If
R
has finite Cohen–Macaulay type, is every left Gorenstein projective
A
-module necessarily a direct sum of finitely generated ones?

(b)

If
R
has finite Cohen–Macaulay type, is
R
necessarily virtually Gorenstein?

(c)

If
R
has finite Cohen–Macaulay type and
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
, does it follow that

\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)?

The following theorem is the main result of this section, and its proof will be presented at the end. As applications, Questions (a), (b), and (c) in
5.2
have affirmative answers when
R
is additionally assumed to be complete; for (b), see Corollary
5.8
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. Then:

(1)

If
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then either
R
is a hypersurface or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

(2)

If
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then every Gorenstein projective
\widehat{R}
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated ones.

(3)

If
\GProj(R)\neq\Proj(R)
, then the following are equivalent.

(a)

\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type.

(b)

\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type.

(c)

R
is a hypersurface and every Gorenstein projective
\widehat{R}
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated ones.

Remark 5.4
.

(1) For a commutative Noetherian local ring
R
, if
\Gproj(R)
has finite representation type, Christensen, Piepmeyer, Striuli, and Takahashi
[
19
, Theorem B]
established that either
R
is Gorenstein or
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
. In particular, when
R
is Cohen–Macaulay and has finite Cohen–Macaulay type, it follows that either
R
is Gorenstein or
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
. It is known that a Gorenstein local ring with finite Cohen–Macaulay type is a hypersurface; see
[
44
, Theorem 9.15]
or
5.14
. If
\GProj(R)=\GProj(R)
, then in particular
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
. Whether the converse holds remains unknown. Therefore, the significance of Theorem
5.3
(1) lies in its conclusion about big modules.

(2) Assume, in addition,
R
is a complete Gorenstein local ring. In this case, the equivalence
(a)\iff(d)
in Theorem
5.3
(3) is due to Beligiannis
[
9
, Theorem 4.20]
, and this result is used in the proof of Theorem
5.3
. More recently, Beligiannis’s result just mentioned was recovered by Bahlekeh, Fotouhi, and Salarian
[
5
, Theorem 6.8]
using a different method.

5.5
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. If
R
is Gorenstein and every Gorenstein projective
\widehat{R}
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated ones, then

(1) Let
\underline{\GProj}(R)
denote the stable category of
\GProj(R)
modulo projective modules. The objects of
\underline{\GProj}(R)
are the same as those of
\GProj(R)
. For any
M,N\in\underline{\GProj}(R)
, the morphism space

\operatorname{Hom}_{\underline{\GProj}(R)}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)/{\mathcal{P}}(M,N),

where
\mathcal{P}(M,N)
consists of morphisms in
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)
which factor through a projective module. Similarly, let
\overline{\GInj}(R)
denote the stable category of
\GInj(R)
modulo injective modules; see
[
42
, Section 7]
.

(2) Assume, in addition,
R
has a dualizing complex
D
. In
[
33
, Theorems 5.3 and 5.4]
, Iyengar and Krause observed that
\underline{\GProj}(R)
and
\overline{\GInj}(R)
are compactly generated triangulated categories and there are triangle equivalences

\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)/\thick_{\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)}(R,D)\xrightarrow{\simeq}(\underline{\GProj}(R)^{c})^{\rm op}\text{ and }\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)/\thick_{\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)}(R,D)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\overline{\GInj}(R)^{c},

where
\mathcal{T}^{c}
is the full subcategory of compact objects of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
.
The statements in
[
33
]
are formulated in terms of the homotopy category of totally acyclic complexes of projective (resp. injective) pmodules. These can be reformulated as stated here, using the classical triangle equivalences between the stable category of Gorenstein projective (resp. Gorenstein injective)
R
-modules and the homotopy category of totally acyclic complexes of projective (resp. injective) modules; see
[
42
, Proposition 7.2]
.
As a consequence of the above equivalences, there is an equivalence

\overline{\GInj}(R)^{c}\xrightarrow{\simeq}(\underline{\GProj}(R)^{c})^{\rm op}.

Combining the following result with Theorem
5.3
(1), we obtain Corollary
5.8
. It gives an affirmative answer to Question (b) in
5.2
, provided that
R
is complete.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring with a dualizing complex. If
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
, then
R
is virtually Gorenstein.

Proof.

By assumption,
\GProj(R)^{\perp}=\operatorname{Mod}(R)
. By
5.5
, there is an equivalence

\overline{\GInj}(R)^{c}\xrightarrow{\simeq}(\underline{\GProj}(R)^{c})^{\rm op}.

Combining with the assumption
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
, we have
\overline{\GInj}(R)^{c}=0
. This implies that
\overline{\GInj}(R)=0
as
\overline{\GInj}(R)
is compactly generated; see
5.5
. That is,
\GInj(R)=\Inj(R)
, and hence
{}^{\perp}\GInj(R)=\operatorname{Mod}(R)
. Thus,
\GProj(R)^{\perp}=\operatorname{Mod}(R)=^{\perp}\GInj(R)
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

If
R
is Artinian, Lemma
5.6
was proved by Beligiannis
[
8
, Section 8]
, who also showed that it holds for any Artinian algebra.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
R
be a complete Cohen–Macaulay ring. If
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then
R
is virtually Gorenstein.

The rest of the section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
5.3
, which will be presented at the end.

5.9
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. The
finitistic projective dimension
of
R
, denoted by
\FPD(R)
, is the number
{\rm sup}\{\pd_{R}(M)\mid M\in\operatorname{Mod}(R)\text{ with }\pd_{R}(M)<\infty\}
. By
[
7
, Corollary 5.5]
and
[
50
, Theorem 3.2.6]
,

\FPD(R)=\dim(R).

It is known that
R
has finite Krull dimension if, in addition,
R
is local or
R
has a dualizing complex.

Assume that
\FPD(R)
is finite, Jensen
[
36
, Proposition 6]
observed that any flat
R
-module has finite projective dimension, and hence a module with finite flat dimension has finite projective dimension.

The following result characterizes the property
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring with finite Krull dimension. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

(2)

\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
and every Gorenstein projective
R
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
R
-modules.

(3)

\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
and every Gorenstein projective
R
-module is a filtered colimit of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
R
-modules.

Proof.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
. The assumption of (1) yields that
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
.
Let
M
be a Gorenstein projective
R
-module. Since
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
,
M
is projective. Since
R
is commutative Noetherian, the projective
R
-module
M
is a direct sum of finitely generated projective
R
-modules; see
[
47
, Fact 3.1]
for example.

(2)\Longrightarrow(3)
is trivial.

(3)\Longrightarrow(1)
. Let
M
be a Gorenstein projective
R
-module. The assumption yields that
M
is a filtered colimit of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
R
-modules. Since
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
, each finitely generated projective is projective, and hence flat. We conclude that
M
is a filtered colimit of finitely generated flat
R
-modules. It follows that
M
is a flat
R
-module. By
\FPD(R)<\infty
,
M
has finite projective dimension; see
5.9
. Combining with that
M
is Gorenstein projective, we get that
M
is projective. It follows that
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

Let
R
be a commutative Artinian ring.

(1) By
[
9
, Corollary 4.11]
,
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
is equivalent that
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
and
R
is virtually Gorenstein. This can be also proved by combining Lemma
5.10
with
[
10
, Theorem 5]

(2) Assume, in addition,
R
is virtually Gorenstein, it follows from
[
10
, Theorem 5]
that
\Gproj(R)
is contravariantly finite in
\operatorname{mod}(R)
. By
[
19
, Theorem C]
,
R
is either Gorenstein or
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
.

By the above remark, we get:

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
R
be a commutative Artinian ring which is not self-injective. Then
R
is virtually Gorenstein if and only if
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring. If
\GProj(\widehat{R})=\Proj(\widehat{R})
, then
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a Gorenstein projective
R
-module. By
5.9
and
[
31
, Proposition 3.4]
,
M
is Gorenstein flat over
R
. Hence,
\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}M
is Gorenstein flat over
\widehat{R}
; see
[
31
, Proposition 3.10]
. It follows from
[
18
, Theorem I]
that
\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}M
has finite Gorenstein projective dimension over
\widehat{R}
. By assumption, each Gorenstein projective
\widehat{R}
-module is projective over
\widehat{R}
, and hence
\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}M
has finite projective dimension over
\widehat{R}
. In particular,
\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}M
has finite flat dimension over
\widehat{R}
. This yields that
M
has finite flat dimension over
R
because
\widehat{R}
is faithfully flat as an
R
-module. By
5.9
,
M
has finite projective dimension over
R
. Combining with that
M
is Gorenstein projective over
R
, we get that
M
is projective over
R
. This finishes the proof.
∎

The following result is a syzygy version of a result of Herzog
[
30
]
, and the proof is adapted from
[
44
, Theorem 9.15]
.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein local ring. If
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type for some
n\geq 0
, then
R
is a hypersurface.

Proof.

This follows from the argument used in the proof of
[
44
, Theorem 9.15]
. Let
M=\Omega^{n+d}(k)
, where
d=\dim(R)
and
k
is the residue field of
R
. Decompose
M
as

M=M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{t},

where each
M_{i}
is indecomposable and
M_{i}\cong R
if and only if
i>s
. Note that
M_{i}\in\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
. Since
\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
, we conclude that
\Omega^{j}(\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R))\subseteq\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
for each
j\geq 0
, and hence
\Omega^{j}(M)
are in
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
for each
j\geq 0
. Combining with that
\operatorname{add}\Omega^{n}\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, the same argument in the proof of
[
44
, Theorem 9.15]
implies that
R
is a hypersurface.
∎

For each
M\in\operatorname{mod}(R)
, let
\operatorname{res}(M)
denote the smallest resolving subcategory containing
M
.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. If
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then either
R
is a hypersurface or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type. Theorem
4.8
implies that
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type. On the other hand, if
\widehat{R}
is a hypersurface, then
R
is a hypersurface. Moreover, by Lemma
5.13
, if
\GProj(\widehat{R})=\Proj(\widehat{R})
, then
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
. Thus, we may assume
R
is complete, namely
R=\widehat{R}
. Under the complete assumption,
R
has a canonical module, denoted by
\omega
; see
[
12
, Corollary 3.3.8]
.

Assume
R
is not a hypersurface. Next, we prove
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
. By Corollary
3.18
,
R
has at isolated singularity, and hence
\mathsf{CM}(R)=\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
. Combining with the assumption that
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, we get that
R
is not a Gorenstein ring as
R
is not a hypersurface; see Proposition
5.14
. It follows that
\operatorname{res}_{\operatorname{mod}(R)}(\omega)\neq\operatorname{add}R
. Hence,
[
53
, Corollary 6.9]
implies that
\mathsf{CM}(R)=\operatorname{res}(\omega)
. Combining this with the fact that sufficiently high syzygies of any module belong to
\mathsf{CM}(R)
,
we conclude that
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)=\thick_{\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)}(R,\omega)
. Combining with
5.5
,
we get that
\underline{\GProj}(R)^{c}=0
. This implies that
\underline{\GProj}(R)=0
as
\underline{\GProj}(R)
is compactly generated; see
5.5
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
R
be a complete Cohen–Macaulay local ring. If
\GProj(R)\neq\Proj(R)
, then the following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type.

(2)

\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type.

(3)

R
is a hypersurface and every Gorenstein projective
R
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
R
-modules.

(4)

R
is Gorenstein and every Gorenstein projective
R
-module is a filtered colimit of finitely generated projective
R
-modules.

Proof.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
and
(3)\Longrightarrow(4)
are trivial.

(2)\Longrightarrow(3)
. Assume
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type. Proposition
5.15
yields that
R
is either a hypersurface or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
. By the assumption that
\GProj(R)\neq\Proj(R)
, we conclude that
R
is a hypersurface, and hence
R
is Gorenstein. We claim that
\Gproj(R)\neq\proj(R)
. If not, assume that
\Gproj(R)=\proj(R)
. Since
R
is Gorenstein, for each
M\in\operatorname{mod}(R)
, every high enough syzygy of
M
is Gorenstein projective, and hence every high enough syzygy of
M
is projective. Thus,
R
is regular. This yields that
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
. This contradicts with the assumption that
\GProj(R)\neq\Proj(R)
. Thus, the claim
\Gproj(R)\neq\proj(R)
follows. Combining with that
R
is Gorenstein,
\mathsf{CM}(R)=\Gproj(R)
. By
[
9
, Theorem 4.20]
, we get that every Gorenstein projective
R
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
R
-modules.

(4)\Longrightarrow(1)
. Since
R
is Gorenstein, we conlcude as
(2)\Longrightarrow(3)
that
\Gproj(R)\neq\proj(R)
. By
[
9
, Theorem 4.20]
,
\Gproj(R)
has finite representation type. Note that
\Gproj(R)=\mathsf{CM}(R)
. We conclude that
\mathsf{CM}(R)
has finite representation type.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

(1) This follows from Proposition
5.15
.

(2) Assume
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type. If
\GProj(\widehat{R})=\Proj(\widehat{R})
, then it follows from Lemma
5.10
that every Gorenstein projective
\widehat{R}
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated Gorenstein projective

-modules. Assume now
\GProj(\widehat{R})\neq\Proj(\widehat{R})
. Since
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, it follows from Theorem
4.8
that
\mathsf{CM}_{0}(\widehat{R})
has finite representation type.
Hence,
Proposition
5.16
yields that every Gorenstein projective
\widehat{R}
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
\widehat{R}
-modules.

(3) Assume

. Combining this with Lemma

, we have
\GProj(\widehat{R})\neq\Proj(\widehat{R})

6.
Applications on dominant local rings

In this section, we investigate dominant local rings introduced by Takahashi
[
55
]
. The main result in this section is Theorem
1.7
from the introduction.

6.1
.

For a commutative Noetherian ring
R
, the
singularity category
of
R
is defined as the Verdier quotient

\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)/\thick_{\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)}(R).

This category was introduced by Buchweitz
[
13
, 5, Def. 1.2.2]
under the name “stable derived category”; see also
[
49
]
. The name “singularity category” is justified by the fact that
R
is regular if and only if
\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)=0
.

6.2
.

A commutative Noetherian local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is said to be
dominant
, as introduced by Takahashi
[
55
, Corollary 10.8]
, if for each nonzero complex
M\in\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)
,
k\in\thick_{\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)}(M)
. This definition is equivalent to that, for each
M\in\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
with
\pd_{R}(M)=\infty
,
k\in\thick_{\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)}(R,M)
.

Example 6.3
.

(1) Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a commutative Artinian local ring with
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=0
. Then
R
is dominant.

(2) By
[
55
, Proposition 5.10]
, a hypersurface is a dominant local ring. This can be proved by the classification of thick subcategories over hypersurfaces given in
[
52
, Theorem 6.6]
.

(3) Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with minimal multiplicity and an infinite residue field. It follows from
[
55
, Proposition 5.10]
that
R
is dominant.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
R
be a dominant local ring with an isolated singularity. For each nonzero complex
M
in
\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)
, then

\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)=\thick_{\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)}(R,M)\text{ and }~\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)=\thick_{\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)}(M).

Proof.

Two results in the statement are equivalent. We prove the latter one. Let
k
denote the residue field of
R
. Since
R
has an isolated singularity,
\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)=\thick_{\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)}(k)
; see
[
37
, Proposition A.2]
. By definition of the dominant local ring,
k\in\thick_{\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)}(M)
.
Thus,
\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)=\thick_{\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)}(M)
.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring with a dualizing complex. If
R
is dominant with an isolated singularity, then either
R
is Gorenstein or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

Proof.

Let
D
be a dualizing complex of
R
. Assume
R
is not Gorenstein. Then
D
is not zero in
\mathsf{D}_{\sg}(R)
; see
[
17
, Theorem 3.3.4]
. It follows from
Lemma
6.4
that

\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)=\thick_{\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)}(R,D).

Combining with
5.5
,
we conclude that
\underline{\GProj}(R)^{c}=0
. This implies that
\underline{\GProj}(R)=0
as
\underline{\GProj}(R)
is compactly generated; see
5.5
. Hence,
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega
. The conclusion of Lemma
6.5
remains valid if the assumption that
R
is dominant is weakened to the condition that
R
is quasi-dominant in the sense of

. Note that if, in addition,

is almost Gorenstein but not Gorenstein and the residue field of

is quasi-dominant; see
[

, Proposition 10.15]
.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring with a dualizing complex. If

is dominant with an isolated singularity, then

is virtually Gorenstein.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.5
, either
R
is Gorenstein or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
. If

is Gorenstein, then

yields that

is virtually Gorenstein. Assume

, then it follows immediately from Lemma

∎

Remark 6.8
.

(1) The dominance assumption on

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring. If

\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)

Since

Remark 6.11
.

For a local ring

Theorem 6.12
.

Let
R
be a dominant local ring. Assume
\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R)
has finite representation type for some
n\leq\operatorname{depth}(R)+1
. Then:

(1)

Either
R
is a hypersurface or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

(2)

\widehat{R}
is virtually Grorenstein.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.23
,
\widehat{R}
has an isolated singularity. In view of Remark
6.11
,
\widehat{R}
is dominant. It follows from Proposition
6.10
that either
\widehat{R}
is Gorenstein or
\GProj(\widehat{R})=\Proj(\widehat{R})
. Combining this with Lemma
5.6
, we conclude that
\widehat{R}
is virtually Gorenstein. Hence, (2) holds.

As shown above, either
\widehat{R}
is Gorenstein or
\GProj(\widehat{R})=\Proj(\widehat{R})
, this yields that either
R
is Gorenstein or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
; see
5.13
. It remains to show that
R
is a hypersurface when it is Gorenstein.

Assume
R
is Gorenstein. By
Proposition
4.3
, we have
\mathsf{TF}_{d}^{0}(R)=\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
, where
d=\dim(R)
. This yields the first equality below:

\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)=\Omega\mathsf{TF}_{d}^{0}(R)=\mathsf{TF}_{d+1}^{0}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{TF}_{n}^{0}(R),

where the second equality is also by Proposition
4.3
, and the inclusion is because
n\leq\operatorname{depth}(R)+1=d+1
.
Since
\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)=\operatorname{add}\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
(see
3.16
), Proposition
5.14
implies that
R
is a hypersurface. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 6.13
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring. Assume
R
satisfies one of the following three conditions:

(1)

R
is dominant.

(2)

R
has minimal multiplicity, and
k
is an infinite field.

(3)

\dim(R)=2
, and

is an algebraically closed field.

If, in addition,
\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, then

R
is either a hypersurface or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

Proof.

Assume (3) holds. By Proposition
4.10
,
R
has minimal mulitiplicity. Thus, the condition (3) implies the condition (2). Assume (2) holds. By Example
6.3
,
R
is dominant, and hence the condition (1) holds. It remains to show that the desired result holds under the assumption of (1).

Assume (1) holds. By Proposition
4.3
,
\mathsf{TF}_{d+1}^{0}(R)=\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
, where
d=\dim(R)
. Since
\Omega\mathsf{CM}_{0}(R)
has finite representation type, it follows from Theorem
6.12
that either
R
is a hypersurface or
\GProj(R)=\Proj(R)
.

In general, it is not easy to determine whether a non-Gorenstein ring is virtually Gorenstein. The following result identifies several classes of rings that are virtually Gorenstein. It builds on earlier results as well as the work of Takahashi
[

]
.

Corollary 6.14
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a commutative Noetherian local ring. Then
R
is virtually Gorenstein if
R
satisfies one of the following conditions:

(1)
R
is Artinian and is dominant.

(2)

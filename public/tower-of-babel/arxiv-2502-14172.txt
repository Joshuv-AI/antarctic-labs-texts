A Finite Sample Analysis of Distributional TD Learning with Linear Function Approximation
Yang Peng, Kaicheng Jin, Liangyu Zhang, Zhihua Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we study the finite-sample statistical rates of distributional temporal difference (TD) learning with linear function approximation. The aim of distributional TD learning is to estimate the return distribution of a discounted Markov decision process for a given policy π \pi . Previous works on statistical analysis of distributional TD learning mainly focus on the tabular case. In contrast, we first consider the linear function approximation setting and derive sharp finite-sample rates. Our theoretical results demonstrate that the sample complexity of linear distributional TD learning matches that of classic linear TD learning. This implies that, with linear function approximation, learning the full distribution of the return from streaming data is no more difficult than learning its expectation (value function). To derive tight sample complexity bounds, we conduct a fine-grained analysis of the linear-categorical Bellman equation and employ the exponential stability arguments for products of random matrices. Our results provide new insights into the statistical efficiency of distributional reinforcement learning algorithms.

In this paper, we consider the Polyak-Ruppert tail averaging

Remark 1

(Comparison with Existing Linear Distributional TD Learning Algorithms)
.

Our
Linear-CTD
can be regarded as a preconditioned version of vanilla stochastic semi-gradient descent (SSGD) with the probability mass function (PMF) representation
[
4
, Section 9.6]
.
See Appendix
D.6
for the PMF representation, and Appendix
D.7
for a self-contained derivation of SSGD with PMF representations.
The preconditioning technique is a commonly used methodology to accelerate solving optimization problems by reducing the condition number.
We precondition the vanilla algorithm by removing the matrix
{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}
(see Eqn. (
26
)), whose condition number scales with
K^{2}
(Lemma
G.2
).
By introducing the preconditioner
({\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}})^{-1}
, our
Linear-CTD
(Eqn. (
13
)) can achieve a convergence rate independent of
K
, which the vanilla form cannot achieve.
See Remark
5
and Appendix
F
for theoretical and experimental evidence respectively.

We comment that
Linear-CTD
(Eqn. (
13
)) is equivalent to SSGD with CDF representation, which was also considered in
Lyle et al. [32]
.
The difference is that our
Linear-CTD
normalizes the distribution so that the total mass of return distributions always be
1
, while the algorithm in
Lyle et al. [32]
does not.
See Appendix
D.7
for a self-contained derivation of SSGD with CDF representations.

Bellemare et al. [4, Section 9.5]
also proposed a softmax-based linear-categorical algorithm, which is closer to the practical C51 algorithm
[
2
]
.
However, the nonlinearity due to softmax makes it difficult for analysis.
We will leave the analysis for it as future work.

Remark 2

(
Linear-CTD
is mean-preserving)
.

A key property of
Linear-CTD
is mean preservation.
That is, if we use identical initializations (
{\mathbb{E}}_{G\sim\bm{\eta}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}}}[G]={\bm{V}}_{{\bm{\psi}}_{0}}
) and an identical data stream to update in both
Linear-CTD
and
Linear-TD
, it follows that
{\mathbb{E}}_{G\sim\bm{\eta}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{t}}}[G]={\bm{V}}_{{\bm{\psi}}_{t}}
for all
t
.
However, the mean-preserving property does not hold for the SSGD with the PMF representation.
In this sense, our
Linear-CTD
is indeed the generalization of
Linear-TD
.
See Appendix
D.8
for details.

4
Non-Asymptotic Statistical Analysis

In our task,
the quality of estimator
\bm{\eta}_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}}
is measured by
\mu_{\pi}
-weighted
1
-Wasserstein error
W_{1,\mu_{\pi}}(\bm{\eta}_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}},\bm{\eta}^{\pi})
.
By triangle inequality, the error can be decomposed into the approximation error and the estimation error:
W_{1,\mu_{\pi}}(\bm{\eta}^{\pi},\bm{\eta}_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}}){\leq}W_{1,\mu_{\pi}}(\bm{\eta}^{\pi},\bm{\eta}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}){+}W_{1,\mu_{\pi}}(\bm{\eta}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}},\bm{\eta}_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}})
.
Proposition
3.2
already provided an upper bound for the approximation error
W_{1,\mu_{\pi}}(\bm{\eta}^{\pi},\bm{\eta}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}})
, so it suffices to control the estimation error
W_{1,\mu_{\pi}}(\bm{\eta}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}},\bm{\eta}_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}})
, denoted
{\mathcal{L}}(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T})
.

In the following theorem, we give non-asymptotic convergence rates of
{\mathcal{L}}(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T})
.
We start from the generative model setting,
i.e.
, in the
t
-th iteration, we collect samples
s_{t}\sim\mu_{\pi}(\cdot),a_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|s_{t}),(r_{t},s_{t}^{\prime})\sim{\mathcal{P}}(\cdot,\cdot|s_{t},a_{t})
from the generative model, and we replace
s_{t+1}
with
s_{t}^{\prime}
in Eqn. (
13
).
We give
L^{p}
and high-probability convergence results in this setting.
These results can be extended to the Markovian setting,
i.e.
, using the streaming data
\left\{{\left({s_{t},a_{t},r_{t}}\right)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}

.

4.1
L^{2}
Convergence

We first provide non-asymptotic convergence rates of
{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}[({\mathcal{L}}(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}))^{2}]
, which do not grow with the number of supports
K
.
The
L^{p}
(
p>2
) convergence results can be found in Theorem
E.1
.

Theorem 4.1

(
L^{2}
Convergence)
.

For any
K\geq(1-\gamma)^{-1}
and
\alpha\in(0,(1-\sqrt{\gamma})/76)
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}[({\mathcal{L}}(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}))^{2}]\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{1}}+1}{\sqrt{T}(1-\gamma)\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}}}\left({1+\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}}}}\right)+\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{1}}+1}{T\sqrt{\alpha}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}\lambda_{\min}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-\frac{1}{2}\alpha(1-\sqrt{\gamma})\lambda_{\min})^{T/2}}{T\sqrt{\alpha}(1-\gamma)\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}}}\left({\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}{+}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1{-}\gamma)\lambda_{\min}}}}\right)\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{2}},

where
\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{1}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}(1-\gamma)}\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{{\bm{I}}_{K}\otimes{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\phi}}}}
and
\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{2}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}(1-\gamma)}\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|
.

In the upper bound, the first term of order
T^{-1{/}2}
is dominant.
The second term of order
T^{-1}
has a worse dependence on
\alpha^{-1}
, leading to a sample size barrier (Eqn. (
15
)).
The third term, corresponding to the initialization error, decays at a geometric rate and can be thus ignored.
To prove Theorem
4.1
, we conduct a fine-grained analysis of the linear-categorical Bellman equation and apply the exponential stability argument

[
54
]
.
We outline the proof in Section
5
.
In Remark
3
, we compare our Theorem
4.1
with the
L^{2}
convergence rate of classic
Linear-TD
and conclude that learning the distribution of the return is as easy as learning its expectation (value function) with linear function approximation.

Rowland et al. [51]
,
Peng et al. [43]
discovered this phenomenon in the tabular setting, and we extended it to the function approximation setting.

Remark 3

(Comparison with Convergence Rate of
Linear-TD
)
.

The only difference between our Theorem
4.1
and the tight
L^{2}
convergence rate of classic
Linear-TD
(see Appendix
C.2
) lies in replacing
\|\bm{\psi}^{\star}\|_{{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\phi}}}}
(resp.
\|\bm{\psi}_{0}{-}\bm{\psi}^{\star}\|
) in
Linear-TD
with

\|{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}\|_{V_{1}}
(resp.
\|{\bm{\theta}}_{0}{-}{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}\|_{V_{2}}
).
We claim that the two pairs should be of the same order, respectively.
Note that
\|{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}\|_{V_{1}}
and
\|{\bm{\theta}}_{0}{-}{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}\|_{V_{2}}
are of order
{\mathcal{O}}((1{-}\gamma)^{-1})

if
{\eta}_{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}(s)
and
{\eta}_{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}}(s)
are valid probability distributions for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
.
This is because in this case,
F_{k}(s;{\bm{\theta}})={\bm{\phi}}(s)^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}(k){+}({k{+}1})/({K{+}1})\in[0,1]
for
{\bm{\theta}}\in\left\{{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star},{\bm{\theta}}_{0}}\right\}
.
While in
Linear-TD
,
\|{\bm{\psi}}^{\star}\|_{{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{\bm{\phi}}}}
and
\|{\bm{\psi}}_{0}{-}{\bm{\psi}}^{\star}\|
are also of order
{\mathcal{O}}((1{-}\gamma)^{-1})
if
V_{{\bm{\psi}}}(s)={\bm{\phi}}(s)^{\top}{\bm{\psi}}\in[0,(1{-}\gamma)^{-1}]
for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\bm{\psi}}\in\{{\bm{\psi}}^{\star},{\bm{\psi}}_{0}\}
.
It is thus reasonable to consider the two pairs with the same order, respectively.
Similar arguments also hold in other convergence results presented in this paper.
Therefore, in this sense, our results match those of
Linear-TD
.

One can translate Theorem
4.1
into a sample complexity bound.

Corollary 4.1
.

Under the same conditions as in Theorem
4.1
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, suppose

\displaystyle T\gtrsim

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|^{2}_{V_{1}}+1}{\varepsilon^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2}\lambda_{\min}}\left({1+\frac{\alpha}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}}}\right)+\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{1}}+1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\alpha}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}\lambda_{\min}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}}\left({\log\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}{-}{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{2}}}{\varepsilon}+\log\left({\frac{1}{T\sqrt{\alpha}(1{-}\gamma)\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}}}\left({\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}{+}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1{-}\gamma)\lambda_{\min}}}}\right)}\right)}\right).

Then it holds that
{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}[({\mathcal{L}}(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}))^{2}]\leq\varepsilon
.

Instance-Independent Step Size.

If we take the largest possible instance-independent step size,
i.e.
,
\alpha\simeq(1-\gamma)
, and consider
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we obtain the sample complexity bound

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:largest_{s}tep_{s}ize_{l}2_{s}ample_{c}omplexity}T=\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}\left({\varepsilon^{-2}(1-\gamma)^{-2}\lambda_{\min}^{-2}\left({\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|^{2}_{V_{1}}+1}\right)}\right).

(14)

Optimal Instance-Dependent Step Size.

If we take the optimal instance-dependent step size
\alpha\simeq(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}
which involves the unknown
\lambda_{\min}
, we obtain a better sample complexity bound

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:instance_{d}ependent_{s}tep_{s}ize_{l}2_{s}ample_{c}omplexity}T=\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}\left({\left({\varepsilon^{-2}+{\lambda_{\min}^{-1}}}\right)(1-\gamma)^{-2}\lambda_{\min}^{-1}\left({\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|^{2}_{V_{1}}+1}\right)}\right).

(15)

There is a sample size barrier in the bound, that is, the dependence on
\lambda_{\min}
is the optimal
\lambda_{\min}^{-1}
only when
\varepsilon{=}\tilde{{\mathcal{O}}}(\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}})
, or equivalently, we require a large enough (independent of
\varepsilon
) update steps

.

These results match the recent results for classic
Linear-TD
with a constant step size
[
26
,
54
]
.
It is possible to break the sample size barrier in Eqn. (
15
) as in
Linear-TD
by applying variance-reduction techniques
[
27
]
.
We leave it for future work.

4.2
Convergence with High Probability and Markovian Samples

Applying the
L^{p}
convergence result (Theorem
E.1
) with
p=2\log(1/\delta)

and Markov’s inequality, we immediately obtain the high-probability convergence result.

Theorem 4.2

(High-Probability Convergence)
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, suppose
K\geq(1-\gamma)^{-1}
,
\alpha\in(0,(1-\sqrt{\gamma}){/}[38\log(T/\delta^{2})])
, and

\displaystyle T=\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|^{2}_{V_{1}}{+}1}{\varepsilon^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2}\lambda_{\min}}\left({1{+}\frac{\alpha\log\frac{1}{\delta}}{(1{-}\gamma)\lambda_{\min}}}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta}{+}\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{1}}{+}1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\alpha}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}\lambda_{\min}}\log\frac{1}{\delta}{+}\frac{\log\frac{\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}_{0}{-}{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{2}}}{\varepsilon}}{\alpha(1{-}\gamma)\lambda_{\min}}\Bigg).

Then with probability at least
1-\delta
, it holds that
{\mathcal{L}}\left({\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}}\right)\leq\varepsilon
.
Here, the
\widetilde{O}\left({\cdot}\right)
does not hide polynomials of
\log(1/\delta)
(but hides logarithm terms of
\log(1/\delta)
).

Again, we will obtain concrete sample complexity bounds as in Eqn. (
14
) or Eqn. (
15
) if we use different step sizes.
Compared with the theoretical results for classic
Linear-TD
, our results match
Samsonov et al. [54, Theorem 4]
.

[
54
]
also considered the constant step size, but obtained a worse dependence on
\log\left({1/\delta}\right)
than
Wu et al. [69, Theorem 4]
which uses the polynomial-decaying step size
\alpha_{t}=\alpha_{0}t^{-\beta}
with
\beta\in(1/2,1)
instead.

Remark 4

(Markovian Setting)
.

Using the same argument as in the proof of
Samsonov et al. [54, Theorem 6]

Compared with the bound in the generative model setting (Theorem

), the bound in the Markovian setting will have an additional dependency on

, where

is the mixing time of the Markov chain

in

.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have bridged a critical theoretical gap in distributional reinforcement learning by establishing the non-asymptotic sample complexity of distributional TD learning with linear function approximation.
Specifically, we have proposed
Linear-CTD
, which is derived by solving the linear-categorical projected Bellman equation.
By carefully analyzing the Bellman equation and using the exponential stability arguments, we have shown tight sample complexity bounds for the proposed algorithm.
Our finite-sample rates match the state-of-the-art sample complexity bounds for conventional TD learning.
These theoretical findings demonstrate that learning the full return distribution under linear function approximation can be statistically as easy as conventional TD learning for value function estimation.
Our numerical experiments have provided empirical validation of our theoretical results.
Finally, we have noted that it would be possible to improve the convergence rates by applying variance-reduction techniques or using polynomial-decaying step sizes, which we leave for future work.

E.5
Convergence Results for SSGD with the PMF Representation

In this section, we present the counterparts of Lemma
5.1
, Lemma
5.2
, Lemma
5.3
and Theorem
4.1
for stochastic semi-gradient descent (SSGD) with the probability mass function (PMF) representation.
These results will additionally depend on
K
.
The additional
K
-dependent terms arise because the condition number of
{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}
scales with
K^{2}
(Lemma
G.2
).
These terms are inevitable within our theoretical framework.
The proofs of these results require only minor modifications to the original proofs, and we omit them for brevity.

In fact, in Appendix
F
, we validate some theoretical results through numerical experiments.
To be concrete, we find that empirically, as

increases, to ensure convergence, the step size of the vanilla algorithm in
[

, Section 9.6]
indeed needs to decay at a rate of
K^{-2}
.
In contrast, the step size of our
Linear-CTD
does not need to be adjusted when

increases.
Moreover, we find that

empirically consistently outperforms the vanilla algorithm under different

Recall Eqn. (

), the updating scheme of the algorithm is

\displaystyle{\bm{W}}_{t}\leftarrow

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{W}}_{t-1}-\alpha{\bm{\phi}}(s_{t})\left({{\bm{p}}_{{\bm{w}}_{t-1}}(s_{t})-{\bm{p}}_{\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{t}^{\pi}{\bm{\eta}_{{\bm{w}}_{t-1}}}}(s_{t})}\right)^{\top}{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}

\displaystyle{\bm{W}}_{t}{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\leftarrow}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{W}}_{t{-}1}{\bm{C}}^{\top}{-}\alpha{\bm{\phi}}(s_{t})\left[{{\bm{\phi}}(s_{t})^{\top}{\bm{W}}_{t{-}1}{\bm{C}}^{\top}{-}{\bm{\phi}}(s_{t{+}1})^{\top}{\bm{W}}_{t{-}1}{\bm{C}}^{\top}({\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r_{t}){\bm{C}}^{-1})^{\top}-\frac{1}{K{+}1}\left({\sum_{j=0}^{K}{\bm{g}}_{j}(r_{t}){-}\bm{1}_{K}}\right)^{\top}{\bm{C}}^{\top}}\right]{\bm{C}}{\bm{C}}^{\top},

here we drop the additional

in the step size for brevity.
Letting

\displaystyle{\bm{\Theta}}_{\operatorname{PMF},t}{\leftarrow}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}}_{\operatorname{PMF},t-1}{-}\alpha{\bm{\phi}}(s_{t})\left[{{\bm{\phi}}(s_{t})^{\top}{\bm{\Theta}}_{\operatorname{PMF},t{-}1}-{\bm{\phi}}(s_{t{+}1})^{\top}{\bm{\Theta}}_{\operatorname{PMF},t{-}1}({\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r_{t}){\bm{C}}^{-1})^{\top}{-}\frac{1}{K{+}1}\left({\sum_{j=0}^{K}{\bm{g}}_{j}(r_{t}){-}\bm{1}_{K}}\right)^{\top}{\bm{C}}^{\top}}\right]{\bm{C}}{\bm{C}}^{\top}.

Lemma E.1
.

For any

, it holds that

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:eq_{i}n_{l}em_{l}oss_{b}ound_{P}MF}{\mathcal{L}}({\bm{\theta}})\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}(1-\gamma)^{2}\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}}}\left\|{\bar{{\bm{A}}}_{\operatorname{PMF}}\left({{{\bm{\theta}}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right)}\right\|.

(40)

achieves the same order of bound as prior results for

(Lemma

.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}[({\mathcal{L}}(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{\operatorname{PMF},T}))^{2}]\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{K^{2}\left({\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{1}}+1}\right)}{\sqrt{T}(1-\gamma)\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}}}\left({1+K\sqrt{\frac{\alpha K^{2}}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}}}}\right)+\frac{K^{3}\left({\left\|{{\bm{\theta}}^{\star}}\right\|_{V_{1}}+1}\right)}{T\sqrt{\alpha K^{2}}(1-\gamma)^{\frac{3}{2}}\lambda_{\min}}

Lemma G.1
.

For any
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in{\mathscr{P}}^{\mathsf{sign}}
, we have
W_{1}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\gamma}}\ell_{2}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
.

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle W_{1}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}|F_{\nu_{1}}(x)-F_{\nu_{2}}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}1^{2}dx}\sqrt{\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}|F_{\nu_{1}}(x)-F_{\nu_{2}}(x)|^{2}dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\gamma}}\ell_{2}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}).

∎

Lemma G.2
.

Let
{\bm{C}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{K\times K}
be the matrix defined in Eqn. (
11
), it holds that the eigenvalues of
{\bm{C}}^{T}{\bm{C}}
are
1/(4\cos^{2}(k\pi/(2K+1))
for
k\in[K]
, and thus

\left\|{{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}}\right\|=\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{4K+2}}\leq K^{2},\quad\left\|{\left({{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}}\right)^{-1}}\right\|=4\cos^{2}\frac{\pi}{2K+1}\leq 4.

Proof.

One can check that

{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}=\begin{bmatrix}K&K-1&\cdots&2&1\\
K-1&K-1&\cdots&2&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
2&2&\cdots&2&1\\
1&1&\cdots&1&1\end{bmatrix},

\left({{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}}\right)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
-1&2&-1&\cdots&0&0\\
0&-1&2&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&2&-1\\
0&0&0&\cdots&-1&1\end{bmatrix}.

Then, one can work with the the inverse of
{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}
and calculate its singular values by induction, which has similar forms to the analysis of Toeplitz’s matrix.
See
Godsil [18]
for more details.
∎

Lemma G.3
.

For any
r\in[0,1]
, it holds that
\left\|{{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\gamma}
and
\left\|{\left({{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}}\right)^{1/2}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r)\left({{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}}\right)^{-1/2}}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\gamma}
.

Proof.

One can check that

{\bm{C}}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
-1&1&\cdots&0&0\\
0&-1&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&-1&1\end{bmatrix}.

It is clear that

\displaystyle\left\|{\left({{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}}\right)^{1/2}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r)\left({{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}}\right)^{-1/2}}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\gamma}

\displaystyle\iff\left({{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}}\right)^{1/2}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r)({\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}})^{-1}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}^{\top}(r)\left({{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}}\right)^{1/2}\preccurlyeq\gamma{\bm{I}}_{K}

\displaystyle\iff\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r)({\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}})^{-1}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}^{\top}(r)\preccurlyeq\gamma({\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}})^{-1}

\displaystyle\iff{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r)({\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}})^{-1}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}^{\top}(r){\bm{C}}^{\top}\preccurlyeq\gamma{\bm{I}}_{K}

\displaystyle\iff\left\|{{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\gamma}.

By Lemma
H.2
and an upper bound on the spectral norm (Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem)
[
55
, Theorem 7.3]
, we obtain that

\left\|{{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\left\|{{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}}\right\|_{1}\left\|{{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}}\right\|_{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{1\cdot\gamma}=\sqrt{\gamma}.

∎

Lemma G.4
.

Suppose
K\geq(1-\gamma)^{-1}
,
\nu=(K+1)^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\delta_{x_{k}}
is the discrete uniform distribution, then for any
r\in[0,1]
, it holds that

\ell_{2}\left({(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\nu),\nu}\right)\leq 3\sqrt{1-\gamma}.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\nu}
be the continuous uniform distribution on
\left[{0,(1-\gamma)^{-1}+\iota_{K}}\right]
, we consider the following decomposition

\displaystyle\ell_{2}\left({\nu,(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\nu)}\right)\leq\ell_{2}\left({\nu,\tilde{\nu}}\right)+\ell_{2}\left({\tilde{\nu},(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\tilde{\nu})}\right)+\ell_{2}\left({(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\tilde{\nu}),(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\nu)}\right).

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\ell_{2}\left({\nu,\tilde{\nu}}\right)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(K+1)\int_{0}^{\iota_{K}}\left({(1-\gamma)\frac{K}{K+1}x}\right)^{2}dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{3K(K+1)(1-\gamma)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K\sqrt{1-\gamma}}.

By the contraction property, we have

\displaystyle\ell_{2}\left({(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\nu),(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\tilde{\nu})}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\gamma}\ell_{2}\left({\nu,\tilde{\nu}}\right)\leq\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{K\sqrt{1-\gamma}}.

We only need to bound
\ell_{2}\left({\tilde{\nu},(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\tilde{\nu})}\right)
.
We can find that
(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\tilde{\nu})
is the continuous uniform distribution on
\left[{r,r+\gamma\iota_{K}+\gamma(1-\gamma)^{-1}}\right]
, and the upper bound is less than the upper bound of
\nu
, namely,
r+\gamma\iota_{K}+\gamma(1-\gamma)^{-1}\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1}+\gamma\iota_{K}<(1-\gamma)^{-1}+\iota_{K}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\ell_{2}^{2}\left({\tilde{\nu},(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\tilde{\nu})}\right)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\left({(1-\gamma)\frac{K}{K+1}x}\right)^{2}dx+\int_{r}^{r+\gamma\iota_{K}+\gamma(1-\gamma)^{-1}}\left[{(1-\gamma)\frac{K}{K+1}\left({x-\frac{x-r}{\gamma}}\right)}\right]^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{r+\gamma\iota_{K}+\gamma(1-\gamma)^{-1}}^{(1-\gamma)^{-1}+\iota_{K}}\left({1-(1-\gamma)\frac{K}{K+1}x}\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\gamma)^{2}K^{2}r^{3}}{3(K+1)^{2}}+\left({\frac{(1-\gamma)\gamma K^{2}r^{3}}{3(K+1)^{2}}+\frac{(1-\gamma)\gamma K^{2}\left({\frac{K+1}{K}-r}\right)^{3}}{3(K+1)^{2}}}\right)+\frac{(1-\gamma)^{2}K^{2}\left({\frac{K+1}{K}-r}\right)^{3}}{3(K+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\gamma)^{2}+(1-\gamma)\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\gamma.

To summarize, we have

\displaystyle\ell_{2}\left({\nu,(b_{r,\gamma})_{\#}(\nu)}\right)\leq\frac{1}{K\sqrt{1-\gamma}}+\sqrt{1-\gamma}+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{K\sqrt{1-\gamma}}\leq 3\sqrt{1-\gamma},

where we used the assumption
K\geq(1-\gamma)^{-1}
.
∎

Appendix H
Analysis of the Categorical Projected Bellman Matrix

Recall that
\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r)={\bm{G}}(r)-\bm{1}_{K}^{\top}\otimes{\bm{g}}_{K}(r)
. We extend the definition in Theorem
3.1
and let
g_{j,k}(r)=h\left({(r+\gamma x_{j}-x_{k})/\iota_{K}}\right)_{+}=h(r/\iota_{K}+\gamma j-k)
for
j,k\in\{0,1,\cdots,K\}
where
h(x)=\left({1-\left|{x}\right|}\right)_{+}
.

Lemma H.1
.

For any
r\in[0,1]
and any
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,K\}
, in
{\bm{g}}_{k}(r)
there is either only one nonzero entry or two adjacent nonzero entries.

Proof.

It is clear that
h(x)>0\iff-1<x<1
.
Let
k_{j}(r)=\min\ \{k:g_{j,k}(r)>0\}
, then
k_{j}(r)=\min\{k:r/\iota_{K}+\gamma j-k<1\}=\min\{k:0\leq r/\iota_{K}+\gamma j-k<1\}
.
The existence of
k_{j}(r)
is due to

r/\iota_{K}+\gamma j-K\leq 1/\iota_{K}+\gamma j-K\leq(1-\gamma)K+\gamma K-K=0<1.

Let
a_{j}(r):=r/\iota_{K}+\gamma j-k_{j}(r)\in[0,1)
. Then
g_{j,k_{j}(r)}(r)=h(a_{j}(r))=1-a_{j}(r)
and
g_{j,k_{j}(r)+1}(r)=h(a_{j}(r)-1)=a_{j}(r)
are the only entries that can be nonzeros.
∎

The following results are immediate corollaries.

Corollary H.1
.

\sum_{k=0}^{i}g_{j,k}(r)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ for}\ 0\leq i<k_{j}(r),\\
1-a_{j}(r),&\text{ for}\ i=k_{j}(r),\\
1,&\text{ for}\ k_{j}(r)<i\leq K.\\
\end{cases}

Corollary H.2
.

k_{j+1}(r)=\begin{cases}k_{j}(r),&\text{ if}\ a_{j}(r)\leq 1-\gamma,\\
k_{j}(r)+1,&\text{ if}\ a_{j}(r)>1-\gamma.\\
\end{cases}

As a result,

a_{j+1}(r)=\begin{cases}a_{j}(r)+\gamma,&\text{ if}\ a_{j}(r)\leq 1-\gamma,\\
a_{j}(r)+\gamma-1,&\text{ if}\ a_{j}(r)>1-\gamma.\\
\end{cases}

Lemma H.2
.

All entries in
{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}
are non-negative.
\left\|{{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}}\right\|_{\infty}=\gamma
and
\left\|{{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}}\right\|_{1}\leq 1
.

Proof.

By definition the entries of
\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r)
are

(\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r))_{j,i}=g_{j,i}(r)-g_{K,i}(r)\quad\text{for }j,i\in\{0,1,\cdots,K-1\}.

Using the previous corollaries, through direct calculation we have that if
k_{j+1}(r)=k_{j}(r)
,

({\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1})_{j,i}=\sum_{k=0}^{i}g_{j,k}(r)-\sum_{k=0}^{i}g_{j+1,k}(r)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ for}\ 0\leq i<k_{j}(r),\\
a_{j+1}(r)-a_{j}(r),&\text{ for}\ i=k_{j}(r),\\
0,&\text{ for}\ k_{j}(r)<i<K.\\
\end{cases}

And if
k_{j+1}(r)=k_{j}(r)+1
,

({\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1})_{j,i}=\sum_{k=0}^{i}g_{j,k}(r)-\sum_{k=0}^{i}g_{j+1,k}(r)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ for}\ 0<i<k_{j}(r),\\
1-a_{j}(r),&\text{ for}\ i=k_{j}(r),\\
a_{j+1}(r),&\text{ for}\ i=k_{j+1}(r),\\
0,&\text{ for}\ k_{j}(r)<i<K.\\
\end{cases}

As a result, all entries in
{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}
is non-negative. Moreover, the sum of each column and
\left\|{{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}}\right\|_{\infty}
is
\gamma
since

\sum_{i=0}^{K-1}({\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1})_{j,i}=\begin{cases}a_{j+1}(r)-a_{j}(r)=\gamma,&\text{ if}\ k_{j+1}(r)=k_{j}(r),\\
1-a_{j}(r)+a_{j+1}(r)=\gamma,&\text{ if}\ k_{j+1}(r)=k_{j}(r)+1.\\
\end{cases}

Moreover, the row sum of
{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}
is

\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}({\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1})_{j,i}=\sum_{m=0}^{i}g_{0,m}(r)-\sum_{m=0}^{i}g_{K,m}(r)\leq 1-0=1.

Thus, it holds that
\left\|{{\bm{C}}\tilde{{\bm{G}}}(r){\bm{C}}^{-1}}\right\|_{1}\leq 1
.
∎

Abstract
. Given two ideals I I and J J of the ring 𝒪 n \mathcal{O}_{n} of analytic function germs f : ( ℂ n , 0 ) ⟶ ℂ f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C} , we show a sharp lower bound for the log canonical threshold of I ​ J IJ in terms of the sequences of mixed Łojasiewicz exponents of them. In particular, in the case where J J is the maximal ideal, the corresponding equality holds if and only if the integral closure of I I equals some power of the maximal ideal.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{O}_{n}
denote the ring of holomorphic function germs
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and let us denote by
\mathbf{m}_{n}
the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
. Given a germ
f\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
with isolated singularity at the origin, let
\mu^{*}(f)
be the vector of mixed Milnor numbers

(\mu^{(n)}(f),\dots,\mu^{(1)}(f),\mu^{(0)}(f))
, introduced by Teissier in
[
54
]
. We recall that
\mu^{(i)}(f)
denotes the Milnor number of the restriction
f|_{H}
of
f
to a generic linear subspace
H
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of dimension
i
, for all
i=0,1,\dots,n
. Therefore
\mu^{(n)}(f)
is the Milnor number of
f
, which is commonly denoted by
\mu(f)
. Moreover
\mu^{(1)}(f)=\operatorname{ord}(f)-1
and
\mu^{(0)}(f)=1
, where
\operatorname{ord}(f)
denotes the order of
f
, that is, the maximum of those
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
such that
f\in\mathbf{m}_{n}^{r}
.
The study of mixed Milnor numbers of function germs of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
, including the relations between them, motivated the introduction in
[
54
]
of the sequence of mixed multiplicities attached to any pair of ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
of finite colength. This idea was further explored by Rees
[
46
]
, thus leading to an essential research line in commutative algebra. In particular, Rees introduced the notion of mixed multiplicity
e(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})

of any given collection
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
of
\mathbf{m}
-primary ideals in a Noetherian local ring of dimension
n
and
gave results about the computation of this number via the notion of joint reduction of ideals. Formulas relating the multiplicity of a product of ideals with the family of mixed multiplicities attached to them are also given in
[
46
,
54
]
and have been of fundamental interest
in the study of the constancy of several invariants attached to a given analytic deformation
f_{t}:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
of fuction germs (we refer to

[
26
,
25
]
for subsequent generalizations in the context of isolated complete intersection singularities).

In addition, if we fix an analytic function germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
and any integer
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, Teissier studied in
[
55
, §5]
and
[
57
]
the Łojasiewicz exponent of the Jacobian ideal of the restriction
f|_{H}
of
f
to a generic linear subspace
H
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of dimension
i
. Similarly to
[
57
]
, let us denote by
\theta^{(i)}(f)
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the
family of numbers thus obtained. Teissier conjectured in
[
57
, p. 7]
that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\theta^{(i)}(f)+1}\leqslant\sigma_{0}(f)

where
\sigma_{0}(f)
denotes the Arnold exponent of
f
(this number is also called in
[
33
, p. 273]
the
complex singular index
of
f
). This conjecture has been proved by Dirks and Mustaţă in
[
21
]
in a more general context. We also refer to
[
22
,
37
]

for previous results in this direction. Let us recall that, if
\operatorname{lct}(f)
denotes the log canonical threshold of
f
, then

\operatorname{lct}(f)=\min\{1,\sigma_{0}(f)\}
, as can be see in
[
33
, Theorem 9.5]
. Kim proved in
[
34
]
the inequality

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\theta^{(i)}(f)+1}\leqslant\operatorname{lct}(\mathbf{m}_{n}J(f)),

where we denote by
\operatorname{lct}(I)
the log canonical threshold of a given ideal
I
of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
(see
[
41
,
33
]
).
Inequality (
1
) has been our main motivation in this paper.

If
I
denotes an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
of finite colength, Hickel introduced in
[
29
]
the sequence of
Łojasiewicz exponents of
I
that appear by restricting a given generating system of
I
to generic linear subspaces of different dimensions.
Additionally, we introduced in
[
1
]
the notion of mixed Łojasiewicz exponent
\mathcal{L}_{0}(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})

of a collection of
d
ideals
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
in a given Noetherian local ring of dimension
n
under the condition that
\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})<\infty
, where
\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})
denotes what we call the
Rees’ mixed multiplicity of
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}

(see (
5
)). Hence, if
I
is an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
of finite
colength and
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we can set
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)=\mathcal{L}_{0}(I,\dots,I,\mathbf{m}_{n},\dots,\mathbf{m}_{n})
, where
I
is repeated
i
times and
\mathbf{m}_{n}

is repeated
n-i
times. As can be seen in
[
9
, Lemma 3.9]
, the sequence of numbers
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(n)}(I)\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(1)}(I)
thus obtained coincides with the above-mentioned sequence of numbers defined by Hickel in
[
29
]
.
In this article we extend (
1
) by replacing the Jacobian ideal by an arbitrary ideal
I
of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
of finite colength and characterize the corresponding equality. That is, in Theorem
3.8
we prove that

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)+1}\leqslant\operatorname{lct}\left(\mathbf{m}_{n}I\right).

and equality holds if and only if there exists some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
such that
\overline{I}=\mathbf{m}_{n}^{r}
.
This result will be supported by Theorem
3.6
, which is the other main result of the article, where we give a chain of inequalities relating
\operatorname{lct}(IJ)
, for any pair of ideals
I
and
J
of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
of finite colength, with the sequences of mixed Łojasiewicz exponents
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(J)
, for
i=1,\dots,n
. At the end of the article we analyze how inequality (
1
) behaves when applied to the
Briançon-Speder example
f_{t}:(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
(see
[
13
]
).

Let us remark that the kind of results that we have obtained takes part of the more general project of determining
the connections between Łojasiewicz exponents and log canonical thresholds of ideals.
Let us fix a coordinate system
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
I
be an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
. Let
\Gamma_{+}(I)
denote the Newton polyhedron of
I

with respect to the coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
(see
[
11
]
for instance). We say that
I
is
monomial
when
I

admits a generating system formed by monomials in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.
Let us recall that if
I
is monomial, then the log canonical threshold of
I
is expressed by means of the Newton polyhedron of
I
(see (
22
)), by a result of Howald
[
30
]
. As can be seen in
[
10
, Proposition 5.3]
, if
\overline{I}
is monomial and
I
has finite colength, then
\Gamma_{+}(I)
also determines all rational powers of
I
and therefore it gives any Łojasiewicz exponent
\mathcal{L}_{J}(I)
, where
J
denotes
any other proper ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
(see Section
2
for the definition of
\mathcal{L}_{J}(I)
and other preliminary concepts and results). Both results allowed us to find that
\operatorname{lct}(I)=\mathcal{L}_{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}(I)
when
\overline{I}
is a
monomial ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
(see
[
10
, Theorem 5.4]
). In view of these facts and the existence of a deformation of
I
into
its initial ideal (after fixing a multiplicative order of the coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
) by a flat family of ideals (see
[
28
, Corollary 7.5.2]
), one could expect that, in general log canonical thresholds and Łojasiewicz exponents
have the same nature, in other words, have very similar properties. However, semicontinuity in deformations is an essential feature that differentiates them.

Log canonical thresholds of ideals are lower semicontinuous in deformations of ideals, as can be seen in
[
19
]
or
[
41
, p. 418]
. Moreover, if
f_{t}:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
is a
\mu
-constant deformation of analytic function germs, the numbers
\operatorname{lct}(f_{t})
remain constant. As a counterpart, the problem of deciding the constancy of
\mathcal{L}_{0}(J(f_{t}))
in Milnor constant deformations is still an open problem. By the results of Teissier
[
55
]
(see also
[
57
]
) it follows that the Łojasiewicz exponent of the ideal
J(f_{t})
is lower semicontinuous if the deformation
f_{t}
is
\mu
-constant. This result was generalized by A. Płoski in
[
45
]
to deformations of map germs
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
with constant multiplicity. We also refer to
[
39
,
50
,
51
]
for further works related with the
semicontinuity of Łojasiewicz exponents in deformations of ideals.

2.
Mixed Łojasiewicz exponents

Given two proper ideals
I
and
J
of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
with
V(I)\subseteq V(J)
, if
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
and
g:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{q},0)
are
analytic maps whose component functions generate
I
and
J
, respectively, then the
Łojasiewicz exponent of
I
with respect to
J
, denoted by

\mathcal{L}_{J}(I)
, is defined as as the infimum of those
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
for which there exists a constant
C>0
and an open
neighbourhood
U
of
0
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\|g(x)\|^{\alpha}\leqslant C\|f(x)\|

for all
x\in U
. We will denote
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{m}_{n}}(I)
simply by
\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)
and we call this number the
Łojasiewicz exponent of
I
.
The numbers
\mathcal{L}_{J}(I)
have several characterizations, as can be seen in
[
36
, Théorème 7.2]
.
We refer to
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
14
,
23
,
27
,
35
,
42
,
43
,
44
,
52
,
53
,
56
]
for additional information regarding geometrical, topological and algebraic aspects of the numbers thus defined.

Let us denote by
\overline{I}
the integral closure of
I
. By
[
36
]
, we also have that

(3)

\mathcal{L}_{J}(I)=\min\left\{\frac{r}{s}:J^{r}\subseteq\overline{I^{s}},\,r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\right\}.

Moreover
\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)
is equal to the number
\tau^{*}(I)
defined by
D’Angelo in
[
16
, p. 621]
. That is

(4)

\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)=\sup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}}\bigg(\inf_{h\in I}\frac{\operatorname{ord}(h\circ\gamma)}{\operatorname{ord}(\gamma)}\bigg),

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of analytic maps
(\mathbb{C},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
. The number on the right of (
4
) is called in
[
16
,
17
]

the
order of contact of
I
.

Given a Noetherian local ring
(R,\mathbf{m})
, if
I
and
J
is a pair of ideals of
R
such that
\sqrt{J}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
,
the expression of
\mathcal{L}_{J}(I)
given in (
3
) in terms of the integral closures leads to the definition of

Łojasiewicz exponent of
I
with respect to
J
also for pairs of proper ideals
I
and
J
in arbitrary Noetherian local rings such that
\sqrt{J}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
.
If additionally, we assume that
I
is
\mathbf{m}
-primary and
R
is quasi-unmixed (see
[
31
, p. 401]
or

[
38
, p. 251]
), then the Rees’ multiplicity theorem (see
[
31
, p. 222]
) allows to write

\mathcal{L}_{J}(I)=\min\left\{\frac{r}{s}:e(I^{s})=e(J^{r}+I^{s}),\,r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\right\}.

where
e(I)
denotes the Samuel multiplicity of
I
(see
[
31
,
38
,
58
]
).

Let
(R,\mathbf{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
n
. If
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
is a collection of
n

\mathbf{m}
-primary ideals of
R
, then
we denote by
e(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})
the
mixed multiplicity of
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
(see
[
31
,
46
]
). Let us recall that when
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
coincide with a given ideal
I
of
R
, then
e(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})=e(I)
. Given an integer

i\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
, we define
e_{i}(I)
as the mixed multiplicity
e(I,\dots,I,\mathbf{m},\dots,\mathbf{m})
, where
I
is repeated
i
times
and
\mathbf{m}
is repeated
n-i
times. Hence we have
e_{n}(I)=e(I)
,
e_{1}(I)=\operatorname{ord}(I)
and
e_{0}(I)=e(\mathbf{m})
, where
\operatorname{ord}(I)
denotes the order of the ideal, that is, the maximum of those
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
such that
I\subseteq\mathbf{m}^{r}
.

More generally, in
[
5
]
we introduced the number
\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})
, where now all the ideals are not assumed to
be
\mathbf{m}
-primary:

(5)

\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})=\max_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}}\,e\big(I_{1}+\mathbf{m}^{r},\dots,I_{n}+\mathbf{m}^{r}\big).

As a consequence of
[
5
, Proposition 2.9]
, if the residual field
R/\mathbf{m}
is infinite,
the condition
\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})<\infty
is equivalent to the existence of elements
g_{i}\in I_{i}
, for all
i=1,\dots,n
, such that
the ideal
\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\rangle
is
\mathbf{m}
-primary. So, if
\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n}<\infty
, then
\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})
is the lowest possible value of the multiplicity
e(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
, where
(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
ranges in the family of
d
tuples of elements of
I_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{n}
generating an ideal of finite colength. We observed that the integer
\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})
, when finite, is equal to the multiplicity defined by Rees in
[
47
, p. 181]
, via the notion of general extension of a local ring (see
[
47
, p. 145]
and
[
48
]
), for certain sets of ideals not necessarily of finite colength. This motivates us to refer
to
\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})
as the
Rees’ mixed multiplicity of
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
.

Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
be ideals of
R
for which
\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})<\infty
. We define the following number (see
[
1
, p. 392]
):

(6)

r(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})=\min\big\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}:\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})=e\left(I_{1}+\mathbf{m}^{r},\dots,I_{n}+\mathbf{m}^{r}\right)\big\}.

Therefore, motivated by
[
1
, Corollary 3.4]
, we defined the
Łojasiewicz exponent of
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
, denoted by

\mathcal{L}_{0}(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})
, as

\mathcal{L}_{0}(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})=\min_{s\geqslant 1}\frac{r(I_{1}^{s},\dots,I_{n}^{s})}{s}.

If
I
denotes an
\mathbf{m}
-primary ideal of
R
, then we set
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)=\mathcal{L}_{0}(I,\dots,I,\mathbf{m},\dots,\mathbf{m})
, where
I
is repeated
i
times and
\mathbf{m}
is repeated
n-i
times. As shown in
[
9
, Proposition 3.9]
, if
R
is a regular with infinite residue field
\mathbf{k}
and we fix an integer
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the number
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)
is equal to the Łojasiewicz exponent of the image of
I
in the quotient ring
R/\langle h_{1},\dots,h_{n-i}\rangle
, where
h_{1},\dots,h_{n-i}
are linear forms chosen generically in
\mathbf{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
So the numbers
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)
coincide with the numbers
\nu^{(i)}_{I}
defined by Hickel in
[
29
, p. 635]
.
As a consequence of
[
4
, Proposition 3.5]
, we have that

\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)=\inf\left\{\frac{r}{s}:r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1},r\geqslant s,\,e_{i}(I^{s}+\mathbf{m}^{r})=e_{i}(I^{s})\right\}.

for all
i=1,\dots,n
.

Given a subset
\textnormal{{L}}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
,
\textnormal{{L}}\neq\emptyset
, we define

\mathbb{C}^{n}_{\textnormal{{L}}}=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:x_{i}=0,\,\textnormal{for all $i\notin\textnormal{{L}}$}\}
. The natural inclusion
\varphi:\mathbb{C}^{n}_{\textnormal{{L}}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}

induces a morphism
\varphi^{*}:\mathcal{O}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{n,\textnormal{{L}}}
by composition with
\varphi
, where
\mathcal{O}_{n,\texttt{L}}
denote the subring of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
formed by all function germs of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
that depend only on the variables
x_{i}
such that
i\in\texttt{L}
. Hence, given any ideal
I
of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
,
we denote by
I^{\textnormal{{L}}}
the image
\varphi^{*}(I)
. Let us recall the following result about the computation of the sequence
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{0}(I)
when
I
is a monomial ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
9
]

Let us fix a coordinate system
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}

with respect to this coordinate system and let us suppose that
I
has finite colength. Then, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have

(7)

\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)=\max\big\{\operatorname{ord}(I^{\textnormal{{L}}}):\textnormal{{L}}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\},\,|\textnormal{{L}}|=n-i+1\big\}.

Here we recall a result proven in
[
9
]
that we will apply later.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
9
]

Let
(R,\mathbf{m})
be a quasi-unmixed Noetherian local ring of dimension
n
.
Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{n},J
be ideals of
R
such that

\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})<\infty
,
\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n-1},J)<\infty
and
I_{n}
has finite colength.
Then

(8)

\frac{\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n})}{\sigma(I_{1},\dots,I_{n-1},J)}\leqslant\mathcal{L}_{J}(I_{n}).

In particular, if
I
is an ideal of
R
of finite colength, we have

(9)

\frac{e(I)}{e_{n-1}(I)}\leqslant\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)

and equality holds if and only if

(10)

e_{n-1}(I)^{n}e(I)=e(I^{e_{n-1}(I)}+\mathbf{m}^{e(I)}).

Let
I
be an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
of finite colength. By
[
29
, p. 635]
(see also
[
9
, Corollary 3.8]
) we know that

(11)

e(I)\leqslant\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(1)}(I)\cdots\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(n)}(I).

Because of this result, we say that
I
is a
Hickel ideal
or that
I
has
maximal multiplicity

when equality holds in (
11
). We refer to
[
2
]
for a characterization of
the equality
e(I)=\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(1)}(I)\cdots\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(n)}(I)
when
\overline{I}
is a monomial ideal.

3.
Log canonical threshold and Łojasiewicz exponents

Let
I
be an arbitrary
\mathbf{m}
-primary ideal of a given Noetherian local ring
(R,\mathbf{m})
of dimension
n
. We define the

Demailly-Pham number of
I
, denoted by
\operatorname{DP}(I)
, as

(12)

\operatorname{DP}(I)=\frac{1}{e_{1}(I)}+\frac{e_{1}(I)}{e_{2}(I)}+\cdots+\frac{e_{n-1}(I)}{e_{n}(I)}

where we recall that
e_{i}(I)
denotes the mixed multiplicity
e(I,\dots,I,\mathbf{m},\dots,\mathbf{m})
, with
I
repeated
i
times and
\mathbf{m}

repeated
n-i
times, for all
i=1,\dots,n
.

Let
I
be an ideal of finite colength of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
. By a result of Demailly and Pham in
[
20
]
, it is known that

(13)

\operatorname{DP}(I)\leqslant\operatorname{lct}(I).

where
\operatorname{lct}(I)
denotes the log canonical threshold of
I
. If
I
is an arbitrary ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}
is a generating system
of
I
, we recall that

(14)

\operatorname{lct}(I)=\sup\big\{s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}:\bigl(|g_{1}(x)|^{2}+\cdots+|g_{r}(x)|^{2}\bigr)^{-s}\ \textrm{ is locally
integrable at $0$}\big\}.

It is straightforward to see that the member on the right of (
14
) does not depend on the
choice of a generating system of
I
. We refer to
[
33
,
41
]
for several equivalent
formulations of the log canonical threshold of an ideal and known properties of this concept.

We will refer to (
13
)
as the Demailly-Pham inequality. The number
\operatorname{DP}(I)
verifies the following fundamental result (which is equivalent to the Rees’ multiplicity theorem).

Theorem 3.1
.

[
3
]

Let
R
be a Noetherian quasi-unmixed local ring.
Let
I_{1},I_{2}
be two proper ideals of finite colength of
R
such that
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
. Then

(15)

\operatorname{DP}(I_{1})\leqslant\operatorname{DP}(I_{2})

and equality holds if and only if
\overline{I_{1}}=\overline{I_{2}}
.

We say that a given property
(P_{t})
depending on a parameter
t\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
holds for all
|t|\ll 1
if there exists
an open ball
U
centered at
0
in
\mathbb{C}^{r}
such that the said property
(P_{t})
holds provided that
t\in U
.

Let
f_{t}:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
be an analytic deformation, where
t
belongs to a parameter space
\mathbb{C}^{r}
. Let us suppose that
f_{t}

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
I
and
J
be proper ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
of finite colength. Then

(17)

\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(IJ)\leqslant\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)+\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(J)

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Equality holds provided that either
I
and
J
are projectively equivalent or
J=\mathbf{m}_{n}
.

Proof.

Let us prove first that

(18)

\mathcal{L}_{0}(IJ)\leqslant\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)+\mathcal{L}_{0}(J).

Let
a,b,r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)\leqslant\frac{a}{b}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}(J)\leqslant\frac{r}{s}
. Therefore,

\mathbf{m}^{a}\subseteq\overline{I^{b}}
and
\mathbf{m}^{r}\subseteq\overline{J^{s}}
.
Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}^{sa}

\displaystyle\subseteq{\overline{I^{b}}}^{\,s}\subseteq\overline{{\overline{I^{b}}}^{\,s}}=\overline{I^{bs}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}^{rb}

\displaystyle\subseteq\overline{J^{s}}\subseteq\overline{{\overline{J^{s}}}^{\,b}}=\overline{J^{bs}}.

Hence

\mathbf{m}^{sa+rb}\subseteq\overline{I^{bs}}\,\overline{J^{bs}}\subseteq\overline{{\overline{I^{bs}}\,\overline{J^{bs}}}}=\overline{(IJ)^{bs}}.

The above relation shows that
\mathcal{L}_{0}(IJ)\leqslant\frac{sa+rb}{bs}=\frac{a}{b}+\frac{r}{s}
. Therefore, relation (
18
) follows.
Let us remark that the same argument shown above also proves relation (
18
) for any pair of proper ideals
I
and
J
of
any given Noetherian local ring.

Let us fix an integer
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. By
[
9
, Lemma 3.9]
,
there exist a family of
n-i
linear forms
h_{1},\dots,h_{n-i}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that, if
H
denotes the ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
generated by these forms and
R_{H}
denotes
the quotient ring
\mathcal{O}_{n}/H
, then we have

(19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{0}(IR_{H})

(20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(J)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{0}(JR_{H})

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(IJ)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{0}(IJR_{H}).

Therefore, by applying relation (
18
) in the context of ideals of
R_{H}
, we obtain (
17
).

Let us suppose that
I
and
J
are projectively equivalent. So let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
such that

\overline{I^{a}}=\overline{J^{b}}
. In particular we obtain that
a\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)=b\mathcal{L}_{0}(J)
and

\overline{I^{a}J^{a}}=\overline{J^{b}J^{a}}=\overline{J^{a+b}}.

Therefore

a\mathcal{L}_{0}(IJ)=\mathcal{L}_{0}(I^{a}J^{a})=\mathcal{L}_{0}(J^{a+b})=(a+b)\mathcal{L}_{0}(J).

That is

\mathcal{L}_{0}(IJ)=\frac{a+b}{b}\mathcal{L}_{0}(J)=\frac{a}{b}\mathcal{L}_{0}(J)+\mathcal{L}_{0}(J)=\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)+\mathcal{L}_{0}(J).

The same argument shown above serves to show the equality
\mathcal{L}_{0}(IJ)=\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)+\mathcal{L}_{0}(J)
for any pair
of projectively equivalent ideals
I
and
J
of
any given Noetherian local ring. Let us fix an index
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and let us consider
relations (
19
)-(
21
), for a given ideal
H
generated by
n-i
linear forms of
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
The projective equivalence
I
and
J
implies that
IR_{H}
and
JR_{H}
are projectively equivalent,
since
\overline{I^{a}R_{H}}=\overline{\overline{J^{b}}R_{H}}
.
So, by relations (
19
)-(
21
) we conclude that

\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(IJ)=\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{0}(I)+\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{0}(J)

for all
i=1,\dots,n
.

Let us suppose now that
J=\mathbf{m}_{n}
. Let
\frac{p}{q}\geqslant\mathcal{L}_{0}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)
, where
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
. Since
\mathbf{m}_{n}I
is contained in
\mathbf{m}_{n}
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{0}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)\geqslant\mathcal{L}_{0}(\mathbf{m}_{n})=1
, so
p\geqslant q
. Therefore we have

\mathbf{m}_{n}^{q}\mathbf{m}_{n}^{p-q}\subseteq\overline{\mathbf{m}_{n}^{q}I^{q}}.

Thus the cancellation theorem for integral closures (see
[
31
, p. 20]
) implies that

\mathbf{m}_{n}^{p-q}\subseteq\overline{I^{q}}.

So
\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)\leqslant\frac{p-q}{q}=\frac{p}{q}-1
. In particular, we obtain that
\mathcal{L}_{0}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)\geqslant\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)+1
.
The reverse inequality holds by (
18
), thus
\mathcal{L}_{0}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)=\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)+1
.
Let
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. As before, by considering the images of
I
and
\mathbf{m}_{n}I
in
R_{H}
, being

an ideal
generated by

linear forms of
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{0}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)=\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{0}(I)+1
.

Remark 3.5
.

Obviously, the inequality
\mathcal{L}_{0}(IJ)\leqslant\mathcal{L}_{0}(I)+\mathcal{L}_{0}(J)
can be strict. This happens, for instance,
for the ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{2}
given by
I=\langle x^{2},y^{3}\rangle
and
J=\langle x^{3},y^{2}\rangle
.

In the next result we generalize the inequality of
[
34
, Theorem 1.1]
to any pair of ideals
I
and
J
.
We provide a chain of inequalities relating mixed Łojasiewicz exponents, mixed multiplicities and log canonical thresholds; we also give conditions implying that the corresponding equalities hold.

Let us first recall the main result of Howald in
[
30
]
stating that if
I
is a monomial ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{n}

(with respect to a given system of coordinates in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
), then
\operatorname{lct}(I)
can be obtained through the Newton polyhedron of
I

by the formula

(22)

\operatorname{lct}(I)=\frac{1}{\min\{\mu>0:\mu\cdot(1,\dots,1)\in\Gamma_{+}(I)\}}.

As shown in
[
10
, Theorem 5.4]
, if
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
denote the coordinates in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with respect to which

I
is monomial, the right part of (

) can be written as
\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}(I)}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
I
and
J
be ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
of finite colength. Then

(23)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)+\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(J)}\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(IJ)}\leqslant\operatorname{DP}(IJ)\leqslant\operatorname{lct}(IJ).

Moreover the following holds:

(a)

If
I
and
J
are projectively equivalent and
I
, or
J
, is diagonal, then all inequalities of
(
23
)
become equalities.

(b)

If
I
and
J
are projectively equivalent or
J=\mathbf{m}_{n}
, then equality holds in the first inequality of
(
23
)
.

(c)

Equality holds in the second inequality of
(
23
)
if and only if
IJ
is a Hickel ideal.

Proof.

The first inequality of (
23
) is a direct consequence of (
17
). The second inequality comes from
Proposition
3.2
.
The third inequality is the Demailly-Pham inequality (
13
).

Let us prove item
(a)
. Let us suppose that
I
and
J
are projectively equivalent. Let us fix coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with respect to which
I
is diagonal.
So there exist
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
such that

\overline{I}=\overline{\langle x_{1}^{r_{1}},\dots,x_{n}^{r_{n}}\rangle}.

By permuting the order of the variables, if necessary, we can assume that
r_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant r_{n}
. Let us see that
J
is also diagonal with respect to
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
such that
\overline{I^{a}}=\overline{J^{b}}
. Therefore

(24)

\overline{J^{b}}=\overline{\langle x_{1}^{ar_{1}},\dots,x_{n}^{ar_{n}}\rangle}.

The ideal on the right of (
24
) is a monomial ideal. So, by the theorem of characterization of ideals with monomial integral closure
(see
[
11
, Theorem 2.11]
) we conclude that
J^{b}
is Newton non-degenerate.
In particular,
J
is Newton non-degenerate. This implies that
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{0}(J)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geqslant 1}
, by (
7
).
Also by (
7
), we have that

\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(J^{b})=b\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(J)=ar_{i}

for all
i=1,\dots,n
. That is
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(J)=\frac{ar_{i}}{b}
and
b
divides
ar_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
.
Let us denote
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(J)
by
s_{i}
, for all
i=1,\dots,n
. Therefore

\overline{J^{b}}=\overline{\langle x_{1}^{ar_{1}},\dots,x_{n}^{ar_{n}}\rangle}=\overline{\langle x_{1}^{bs_{1}},\dots,x_{n}^{bs_{n}}\rangle}=\overline{\langle x_{1}^{s_{1}},\dots,x_{n}^{s_{n}}\rangle^{b}}.

Hence we have

\overline{J}=\overline{\langle x_{1}^{s_{1}},\dots,x_{n}^{s_{n}}\rangle}.

That is,
J
is diagonal. Since
\frac{r_{i}}{s_{i}}=\frac{a}{b}
, for all
i=1,\dots,n
, we also conclude that

\overline{IJ}=\overline{\overline{I}\,\overline{J}}=\overline{\langle x_{1}^{r_{1}+s_{1}},\dots,x_{n}^{r_{n}+s_{n}}\rangle}.

So
IJ
is also diagonal, which implies that
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(IJ)=\frac{1}{r_{i}+s_{i}}
, for all
i=1,\dots,n
. By Howald’s identity (
22

\operatorname{lct}(IJ)=\frac{1}{r_{1}+s_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{n}+s_{n}}=B(IJ).

Hence item
(a)
follows.

Items
(b)
and
(c)
are direct applications of Proposition
3.2
and Theorem
3.4
,
respectively.

Remark 3.7
.

(a)

\mathbb{C}^{n}

I

The characterization of the equality
\operatorname{DP}(I)=\operatorname{lct}(I)
is still an open problem.
We conjecture that, given an ideal
I
of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
, the equality
\operatorname{DP}(I)=\operatorname{lct}(I)
holds if and only if

becomes diagonal with respect to a suitable coordinate system

, that is, there

\varphi:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)

.

(b)

Let us suppose that
I
and
J
are projectively equivalent ideals, so let

such that

. Then it follows that
\overline{I^{b}J^{b}}=\overline{I^{a+b}}
and therefore

\displaystyle B(IJ)

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{a+b}B(I)

\displaystyle\operatorname{DP}(IJ)

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{a+b}\operatorname{DP}(I)

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{a+b}\operatorname{lct}(I).

Consequently the condition

projectively equivalent to

.

(c)

The inequalities of (

are ideals of

of Theorem

.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
I
be an ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
of finite colength. Then

(25)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)+1}=B(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)\leqslant\operatorname{DP}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)\leqslant\operatorname{lct}\left(\mathbf{m}_{n}I\right).

(

there exists some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
such that
\overline{I}=\mathbf{m}_{n}^{r}
.

Proof.

Relation (
25

J=\mathbf{m}_{n}
of (
23
), joined with the fact that

\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{0}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)=\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{0}(I)+1
, for all
i=1,\dots,n
(Theorem
3.4
). Let us suppose that
\overline{I}=\mathbf{m}_{n}^{r}
, for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
. In particular,
r=\operatorname{ord}(I)
. Hence
\overline{\mathbf{m}_{n}I}=\mathbf{m}_{n}^{r+1}
and this implies that
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)=\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(\mathbf{m}_{n}^{r+1})=r+1

\operatorname{lct}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)=\operatorname{lct}(\mathbf{m}_{n}^{r+1})=\frac{1}{r+1}

Conversely, let us suppose that the inequalities of (
25

we have
B(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)=\operatorname{DP}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)
. This implies that
\mathbf{m}_{n}I
is a Hickel ideal, by Proposition
3.2
.

Let
a_{i}=\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(I)

a_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant a_{n}
and we have
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(i)}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)=a_{i}+1

Let us first observe that

(26)

\displaystyle e(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}e_{i}(I)\leqslant 1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}a_{1}\cdots a_{i}

(27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(1)}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)\cdots\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(n)}(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)

\displaystyle=(a_{1}+1)\cdots(a_{n}+1)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{1\leqslant k_{1}<\cdots<k_{i}\leqslant n}a_{k_{1}}\cdots a_{k_{i}}\right).

[

implies that, given any index

for all set of indices

for which

. This fact implies that

(29)

e(\mathbf{m}_{n}I)\leqslant 1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}a_{1}\cdots a_{i}\leqslant 1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{1\leqslant k_{1}<\cdots<k_{i}\leqslant n}a_{k_{1}}\cdots a_{k_{i}}\right).

and therefore

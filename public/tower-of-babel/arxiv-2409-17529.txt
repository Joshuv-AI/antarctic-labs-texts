Let

If in some event

Definition 2

Let
X,Y\in\cal L

Y=(y_{1},S_{1};

\ldots;y_{n},S_{n})

\Psi(X,Y)=(\psi(x_{1},y_{1}),p_{1};\ldots;\psi(x_{n},y_{n}),p_{n})

Definition 3

The preference relation
\succeq

\psi
and a continuous functional

which is defined over regret lotteries such that for any

X\succeq Y\quad\mbox{if and only if }\quad V(\Psi(X,Y))\geqslant 0

Let
X=(x_{1},S_{1};\ldots;x_{n},S_{n})\in\cal L

So it is implicit that the assumptions of Theorem 1 are invoked in proving Proposition 1, but we did not define the notion of continuity and monotonicity that the preference relation satisfies. Implicitly we assumed continuity w.r.t. convergence in distribution, which makes the proposition trivial. Here we show that it holds even if continuity wrt convergence in probability is assumed.

Proposition 1

(Probabilistic equivalence)
. Let
\succeq
be a complete, transitive, continuous w.r.t. convergence in probability, and state-wise monotonic, regret-based preference relation over
\cal L
. For any two random variables
X,Y\in{\cal L}
, if
F_{X}=F_{Y}
, then
X\sim Y
.

Proof:
Let
X=(x_{1},S_{1};\ldots;x_{n},S_{n})
and
Y=(y_{1},S_{1}^{\prime};\ldots;

y_{n},S_{n}^{\prime})
be such that
F_{X}=F_{Y}
.

Case 1:

S_{i}=S_{i}^{\prime}
and
{\rm P}(S_{i})=\frac{1}{n}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then there is a permutation
\hat{\pi}
such that
Y=\hat{\pi}(X)
. Obviously,
\Psi(X,\hat{\pi}(X))=\Psi(\hat{\pi}^{i}(X),

\hat{\pi}^{i+1}(X))
.

3

This is the only place where the assumption of regret-based
\succeq
is used in the proof. Thus, the proposition can be proved under a weaker assumption that
X\succ\pi(X)
implies
\pi(X)\succ\pi^{2}(X)
.
Hence, as there exists
m\leqslant n!
such that
\hat{\pi}^{m}(X)=X
, it follows by transitivity that for all
i
,
X\sim\hat{\pi}^{i}(X)
. In particular,
X\sim Y
.

Case 2:
For all
i,j
,
{\rm P}(S_{i}\cap S^{\prime}_{j})
is a rational number. Let
N
be a common denominator of all these fractions.
X
and
Y
can now be written as in case 1 with equiprobable events
T_{1},\ldots,T_{N}
.

Case 3:
There exist
i
and
j
, such that
P(S_{i}\cap S^{\prime}_{j})
is irrational. For
x_{1}<\ldots<x_{n}
and
y_{1}<\ldots<y_{n}
, let
X=(x_{1},S_{1};\ldots;x_{n},S_{n})
and
Y=(y_{1},S^{\prime}_{1};

\ldots;y_{n},S^{\prime}_{n})
be such that
F_{X}=F_{Y}
. Then
x_{i}=y_{i}
and
p_{i}:=P(S_{i})=P(S^{\prime}_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}
be the set of intersections
\{S_{i}\cap S^{\prime}_{j}:P(S_{i}\cap S^{\prime}_{j})>0\}
. Clearly,
\sum_{j}\{P(T_{j}):X(T_{j})=x_{i}\}=\sum_{j}\{P(T_{j}):Y(T_{j})=x_{i}\}=p_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.

For
k=1,2,\ldots
, define
\nu(T_{j},k)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\nu(T_{i},k)}{2^{k}}<P(T_{j})\leqslant\frac{\nu(T_{j},k)+1}{2^{k}}

For
k
such that
\frac{1}{2^{k}}<\min_{j}\{P(T_{j})\}
, define a partition
T^{k}=\{T^{k}_{jh}:j=1,\ldots,m,\,h=1,\ldots,\nu(T_{j},k)\}
of
[0,1]
satisfying

•

\sum_{h=1}^{\nu(T_{j},k)}P(T^{k}_{jh})=P(T_{j})
,
j=1,\ldots,m
.

•

For
j=1,\ldots,m
and
h=1,\ldots,\nu(T_{j},k)-1
,
P(T^{k}_{jh})=\frac{1}{2^{k}}
.

That is,
T^{k}
partitions each
T_{j}
into
\nu(T_{j},k)-1
events with probability
\frac{1}{2^{k}}
each, and one event with probablity not greater than
\frac{1}{2^{k}}
. Define
X^{k},Y^{k}
such that

•

For
j=1,\ldots,m
and
h=1,\ldots,\nu(T_{j},k)-1

X^{k}=X
and
Y^{k}=Y
.

•

For
j=1,\ldots,m
and
h=\nu(T_{j},k)
,
X^{k}=Y^{k}=c
, where
c\not\in\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.

Observe that
X^{k}
disagrees with
X
and
Y^{k}
disagrees with
Y
on at most
m

T^{k}
. Hence, for every
i

P(X^{k}=x_{i})\geqslant P(X=x_{i})-\frac{m}{2^{k}}
and
P(Y^{k}=x_{i})\geqslant P(Y=x_{i})-\frac{m}{2^{k}}
. Note that by definition,
P(X^{k}=x_{i})\leqslant P(X=x_{i})
and
P(Y^{k}=x_{i})\leqslant P(Y=x_{i})
. It thus follows that

\displaystyle|P(X^{k}=x_{i})-P(Y^{k}=x_{i})|\leqslant\frac{m}{2^{k}},\qquad\forall i

(Recall that
P(X=x_{i})=P(Y=x_{i})
.) Modify
X^{k}
and
Y^{k}
as follows. If

d2^{k}

yields

to yield

\bar{Y}^{k}

(a)
F_{\bar{X}^{k}}=F_{\bar{Y}^{k}}

(b)

,

, hence
\bar{X}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}X
and
\bar{Y}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y

(c) For every

P(\bar{X}^{k}=x_{i})=P(\bar{Y}^{k}=x_{i})=\frac{\ell_{i}}{2^{k}}
for some integer

By (a), (c), and case 2,
\bar{X}^{k}\sim\bar{Y}^{k}
, and by (b) and continuity,
X\sim Y
.

Proposition 1 implies that
\succeq
satisfies a stronger form of continuity and monotonicity, as shown next.

Let
F_{X}
be the cdf of
X\in\cal L
and
F_{X^{k}}
be the cdf of
X^{k}\in\cal L
.
A sequence of random variables
X^{k}

converges in distribution
to
X

X^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}X
, if

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}F_{X^{k}}(x)=F_{X}(x)

at every

is continuous (see Billingsley
[
2
, p. 338]
).

A preference relation
\succeq
is
continuous w.r.t. convergence in distribution
if

for all

X\succeq Y
and
Y\succeq X^{k}
for all
k
and
X^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}X
implies
Y\succeq X
.

Corollary 1

Let
\succeq
be a regret-based preference relation over
\cal L
. Assume that
\succeq
satisfies the assumptions of Proposition 1. Then
\succeq
is (i) monotonic w.r.t. first-order stochastic dominance (FOSD) and (ii) continuous w.r.t. convergence in distribution.

Proof:
(i) Let
X=(x_{1},S_{1};\ldots;x_{n},S_{n})
and
Y=(y_{1},S_{1}^{\prime};\ldots;

y_{n},S_{n}^{\prime})
be such that
F_{X}
strictly dominates
F_{Y}
by FOSD. One can construct two random variables
X^{\prime},\,Y^{\prime}
with cdfs
F_{X}
and
F_{Y}
respectively such that
X^{\prime}=(x_{1}^{\prime},T_{1};\ldots;x_{m}^{\prime},T_{m})
,
Y^{\prime}=(y_{1}^{\prime},T_{1};…;y_{m}^{\prime},T_{m})
,
x_{i}^{\prime}\geqslant y_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i
. Observe that each
x_{i}^{\prime}\in\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and each
y_{i}^{\prime}\in\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
. As
F_{X}
strictly dominates
F_{Y}
, for at least one
i
, we have
x_{i}^{\prime}>y_{i}^{\prime}
. Therefore, state-wise monotonicity implies that
X^{\prime}\succ Y^{\prime}
. By Proposition 1,
X^{\prime}\sim X
,
Y^{\prime}\sim Y
and by transitivity,
X\succ Y
. Thus
\succeq
satisfies monotonicity w.r.t. FOSD.

(ii) Suppose that
X^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}X
and that
X^{k}\succeq Y
for all
k
. We show that
X\succeq Y
. By Skohorod’s Theorem (see Billingsley
[
2
, p. 343]
), there exists a sequence of random variables
\bar{X}^{k}
such that
\bar{X}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}X
and
F_{\bar{X}^{k}}=F_{X^{k}}
. By Proposition 1,
\bar{X}^{k}\sim X^{k}
. Therefore,
X^{k}\succeq Y
for all
k
and transitivity imply that
\bar{X}^{k}\succeq Y
for all
k
. Continuity w.r.t. convergence in probability implies that
X\succeq Y
.

\blacksquare

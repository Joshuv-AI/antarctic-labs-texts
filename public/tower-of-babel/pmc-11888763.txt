Digit Ratio as Predictors of Dental Caries: A Study of the 2D:4D Relationship
Authors: M Ishwarya, Vasanthakumari Ananthan, Selvabalaji Arumugam, Ekambareswaran Krishnan, S Umamaheswari, Madhumathy Rajendran
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:This cross-sectional study aimed to investigate the association between caries risk and the 2D:4D ratio, a potential marker of prenatal hormone exposure, among children aged 5–12 years.Methods:A total of 250 children were examined. Caries indices decayed, missing and filled teeth/decayed, extracted and filled teeth (DMFT/deft) were recorded, and 2D:4D ratios were measured using a vernier caliper. Chi-square statistical analysis was conducted to explore the relationship between 2D:4D ratios and caries risk.Results:The study revealed a higher prevalence of caries among children with a low 2D:4D ratio (<1), suggesting elevated prenatal androgen levels. Specifically, 123 out of 147 individuals (83.67%) with a 2D:4D ratio less than 1 presented a high caries index, compared to 32 out of 103 individuals (31.07%) with a ratio equal to or greater than 1. Gender-specific differences were observed, with males exhibiting lower mean 2D:4D ratios than females.Conclusion:These findings suggest a potential role for the 2D:4D ratio as a predictive marker for dental caries risk in children.

INTRODUCTION

Dental caries is a common infectious disease in children, which is more prevalent than asthma and hay fever and caused by bacteria like Streptococcus mutans and Lactobacillus species.[1] It is irreversible, leading to pain, reduced quality of life, and affecting a child’s nutrition and growth. Hereditary factors contribute to high caries rates, with recent research focusing on genetic taste sensitivity for early risk identification.[2] Early detection utilizing various diagnostic methods is crucial for effective management. The 2D:4D ratio, reflecting prenatal hormone exposure, has emerged as a potential predictor of caries susceptibility.[34] Males typically have lower 2D:4D ratios than females due to genes involved in digit and gonad development. This ratio may provide insights into dietary preferences and taste perception, aiding in early caries risk identification.[5]

This study aims to explore the role of the hormonal fingerprint (2D:4D ratio) in early caries detection in children, emphasizing the need for further research on its link to dental caries in the Melmaruvathur region.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This cross-sectional study involved 250 children aged 5–12 years who visited the Department of Pedodontics and Preventive Dentistry at Adhiparasakthi Dental College and Hospital. This study was conducted over 6 months (from August 2023 to February 2024) after obtaining approval from the institution’s review board, with informed consent obtained from parents. Children without systemic illnesses and healthy individuals without physical or mental disabilities were included, while those whose parents declined consent were excluded.

Clinical examination

Children were seated in the dental chair under the illumination of the chair light for a thorough clinical examination. Two examiners, both trained and calibrated, conducted the examination. They documented caries indices (deft and DMFT), identifying participants with a complete DMFT/deft score of >5 as having a high caries rate. Caries were observed if lesions were detected on occlusal or smooth surfaces, irrespective of their activity status.

Calculation of 2D:4D ratio

The precise measurement of the index (2D) and ring (4D) finger lengths was taken from the ventral proximal crease of each digit to its distal end, using a vernier caliper. In instances of multiple creases, measurements were taken from the proximal crease to the greatest extent. The digit ratio was computed by dividing these measurements, The average of multiple measurements for both hands was calculated independently. Participants were classified according to their 2D:4D ratio, as those with ratios less than 1 and ≥1. All collected data underwent thorough tabulation and analysis utilizing statistical software, Statistical Package for the Social Sciences.

RESULTS

The survey included a total of 250 children, with 127 (50.8%) identified as males and 123 (49.2%) as females. Among the male participants, 98 (77.17%) exhibited a 2D:4D ratio less than 1, while 29 (22.83%) had a ratio equal to or greater than 1. Conversely, in the female group, 49 (39.84%) demonstrated a 2D:4D ratio less than 1, and 74 (60.16%) exhibited a ratio equal to or greater than 1 [Table 1]. Further analysis revealed that among the 147 individuals with a 2D:4D ratio less than 1, 123 (83.67%) presented a high caries index, contrasting with the 24 (16.33%) individuals who had a low caries index. Among the 103 participants with a 2D:4D ratio equal to or greater than 1, 32 (31.07%) [Table 2] displayed a high caries index, while 71 (68.93%) exhibited a low caries index. These findings provide valuable insights into the distribution of 2D:4D ratios among males and females, as well as their association with caries index levels.

DISCUSSION

Many recent studies have linked the 2D:4D ratio to various human traits and behaviors, but there is limited dental research on its impact on oral health. The 2D:4D ratio is a potential marker for prenatal hormone exposure and gene expression; it is established during the second trimester and remains consistent postnatally. It may indicate the effects of prenatal sex hormones on physiology, brain development, behavior, and taste perception. In our study of 250 children, we explored the association between caries risk and the 2D:4D ratio. Our results showed that males had a lower average 2D:4D ratio compared to females, consistent with previous research by Manning et al.[6] and Beegum et al.[7] We observed a statistically significant correlation between 2D:4D ratios and sex, with males having lower 2D:4D ratios than females. This suggests higher prenatal testosterone and lower prenatal estrogen levels in males. The 2D:4D ratio, indicating a longer fourth digit relative to the second digit, is a reliable marker of prenatal testosterone exposure. Our findings align with those of Dey and Kapoor,[8] who reported similar results.

Lutchmaya et al.[9] examined the relationship between 2D:4D ratios and fetal hormone levels in 33 children and found sexual dimorphism in the 2D:4D ratio. Children with a low 2D:4D ratio exhibited a high caries index, consistent with the findings of Garg et al.,[10] who linked a low 2D:4D ratio (indicative of high prenatal androgen levels) with increased caries. Hormones may influence taste and dietary preferences, affecting the caries index. The 2D:4D ratio provides insights into prenatal development, behavior, disease susceptibility, and dental caries risk. However, further research with larger, diverse samples is needed to validate its use in pediatric dentistry.[10] The present study, with 250 participants, explores the 2D:4D ratio as a predictor of dental caries in children, highlighting potential prenatal hormonal links to caries risk. Limitations include its cross-sectional design, geographic focus, measurement variability, and lack of consideration for factors like diet and socioeconomic status. Future research should address these limitations and use advanced diagnostic tools. Future studies can also focus on early loss of primary teeth and its association with 2d: 4d ratio.[11]

CONCLUSION

This study found that a lower 2D:4D ratio in children aged 5–12 is associated with higher caries risk, suggesting it could be a predictive marker for dental caries. Despite limitations like its cross-sectional design and not accounting for diet and socioeconomic status, the 2D:4D ratio shows promise as a noninvasive screening tool. Future research should validate these findings with longitudinal studies and diverse populations to enhance its application in predicting caries risk and improving oral health outcomes.

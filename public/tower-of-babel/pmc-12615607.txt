Intelligent identification method for rice seedling growth stages and its application in laser supplementary lighting control research
Authors: Xiaoyan Liu, Leijinyu Zhou, Bei Jiang, Helong Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurately identifying the growth stages of rice seedlings is crucial for managing factory nurseries and ensuring consistent seedling quality. This study introduces PGL-ShuffleNetV2, a lightweight and advanced model designed for efficient and accurate recognition of rice seedling growth stages. The proposed model achieves a streamlined architecture by: 1). removing the second 1 × 1 convolution in the downsampling block’s right branch. 2) Reducing the repetition of basic units for improved efficiency. Additionally, the GELU activation function replaces ReLU to enhance nonlinear representation capabilities, and a parallel weighted hybrid attention module (PWMAM) is incorporated to improve feature extraction. Experimental results demonstrate that PGL-ShuffleNetV2 achieves a remarkable 98.80% recognition accuracy and a 98.82% F1 score, with a compact model size of just 0.84 MB. Its optimal balance between accuracy and parameter efficiency makes it highly suitable for deployment on resource-constrained devices, enabling effective monitoring and management of rice seedlings in factory nursery environments. Based on the advantages of this model, this study further applied it to rice seedlings under laser supplementary lighting conditions to investigate the impact of laser on the growth stages of seedlings, providing technical support for the application of laser technology in intelligent seedling cultivation.Keywords: Rice seedling growth stage, Lightweight model, Attention mechanisms, Factory nurseries, Laser fill lightingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Plant sciences

Introduction

Rice is one of the most important staple crops worldwide, providing a primary food source for nearly half of the global population1. Economically, the global rice market is valued at several hundred billion US dollars, and worldwide rice production is projected to increase by 5.8% to 567 million tons (Mt) by 20302. The seedling stage plays a critical role in rice cultivation, as seedling quality directly influences subsequent yield. During this stage, temperature and humidity are regulated according to the specific environmental requirements of seedlings at each growth phase. Therefore, accurate identification of rice seedling growth stages is essential for implementing efficient and optimized seedling management practices.

The growth stages of rice seedlings are typically classified according to the BBCH (Biologische Bundesanstalt, Bundessortenamt und Chemische Industrie) scale3, an internationally recognized decimal system for crop development. During the seedling period, particular attention is given to key phases within growth stage 1 (leaf development), including the full expansion of the first leaf (BBCH11), second leaf (BBCH12), and third leaf (BBCH13). Rice transplanting is typically performed no later than BBCH134. Currently, the identification of seedling growth stages primarily relies on manual field observations, which are both time-consuming and labor-intensive. Therefore, there is an urgent need for low-cost, efficient, and accurate methods for seedling growth stage recognition.

Optimal light conditions can significantly improve rice seedling quality. Laser technology has attracted considerable attention in agriculture owing to its superior photoelectric conversion efficiency. Studies have shown that laser stimulation of seeds or seedlings can affect crop yield5. Laser irradiation can accelerate starch degradation, thereby promoting plant growth and development6. However, research on the effects of laser treatment on vigorous rice seedlings remains limited. Therefore, investigating the effects of laser treatment on rice seedling growth is imperative for developing novel approaches to cultivate robust seedlings.

The application of computer vision in agriculture has become increasingly important7. Previous studies have applied Otsu’s thresholding to segment rapeseed seedlings8 and combined morphological processing with dual-thresholding to monitor early-stage corn growth9. In multispectral remote sensing, features such as the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) and the Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI) are commonly used for weed identification10. However, most of these approaches rely on thresholding and require manual parameter tuning, thereby limiting their automation and practical applicability.

Quan et al.11 proposed an enhanced Faster R-CNN model for monitoring corn seedlings at various growth stages. Wan et al.12 optimized the U-Net architecture to segment and identify corn growth stages from field images. Hussain et al.13 and Teimouri et al.14 utilized deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) to identify weed characteristics across growth stages. The models, including Faster R-CNN, belong to the two-stage object detection family. Although these models achieve high accuracy, they impose substantial computational demands and exhibit slow inference speeds, rendering them unsuitable for real-time applications.

In contrast, single-stage detection models such as YOLO have been widely applied in agricultural scenarios, integrating object localization and classification into a single regression task. These models have been applied to fruit detection in crops such as apples15, tomatoes16, corn17, and peppers18, as well as to pest and disease identification19. Paul et al. employed YOLOv9 for nighttime chili pepper detection in greenhouse environments20 and YOLOv8 for bell pepper detection and growth stage classification21. Moreover, Vision Transformers (ViTs) have demonstrated accuracy comparable to, or even surpassing, that of CNNs in tasks such as plant disease identification22 and corn seed classification23.

However, agricultural applications are often deployed on resource-constrained edge devices, such as drones and field robots, which impose strict constraints on power consumption, computational capacity, and storage. Consequently, lightweight architectures, such as MobileNet24 and ShuffleNet25, are favored over large-scale ViT or standard YOLO models. For example, ShuffleNetV2 achieved over 96% accuracy in corn seed sorting26 and tomato disease detection27, while maintaining extremely low FLOPs and model size.

Therefore, this study focuses on rice seedlings as the primary research subject and employs laser illumination as the control method. By integrating image recognition technology, it achieves synergistic optimization across three key components: lightweight network architecture, activation functions, and attention mechanisms. On this basis, an improved model, termed PGL-ShuffleNetV2, (where PGL represents the Parallel Weight Mixing Attention mechanism, Gaussian Error Linear Unit, and L emphasizes the Lightweight structure). Materials and methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

Data acquisition

Data collection for rice seedling growth stages in this study was divided into two components: model training data and laser effect analysis data. Model training data were collected in the greenhouse facilities at Jilin Agricultural University’s research Base (Fig. 2).

Data acquisition.

Rice seedling imaging was performed between April and May 2023, encompassing the critical growth stages from BBCH11 to BBCH13. The Honor X10 smartphone, featuring a 40 MP main camera, an 8 MP ultra-wide camera, and a 2 MP macro lens, was used for imaging (Table 1). For each sampling session, 100 seedlings were randomly selected from each plot. When more than 80 seedlings exhibited consistent growth stages, the corresponding stage was designated as the representative growth stage for that plot. Through meticulous annotation of these seedling growth stages, a dataset comprising three distinct growth stages, totaling 458 images, was constructed. The dataset included 120, 185, and 153 images for each respective growth stage.

Model construction

To achieve fast and accurate recognition of rice seedling growth stages, this study adopts the lightweight ShuffleNetV2 network and optimizes it by eliminating redundant layers, replacing activation functions, and integrating an attention mechanism, resulting in the proposed PGL-ShuffleNetV2 model, as shown in Fig. 6.

PGL-ShuffleNetV2 structure. PWMAM: Parallel Weighted Mixed Attention Module. The The number of basic units in ShuffleNetV2’s stage 1, stage 2, and stage 3 is set to 2, 4, and 2.

The model leverages ShuffleNetV2 as a foundation for constructing a backbone feature extraction network. It reduces the number of parameters by eliminating 1 × 1 convolutional layers and decreasing network depth. Additionally, it incorporates the GELU activation function to address the gradient vanishing issue and enhances feature extraction performance. By introducing a parallel weight fusion attention mechanism, the model improves the representation of relevant features while mitigating the impact of extraneous ones.

ShuffleNetV2

In 2018, Ma et al. proposed ShuffleNetV2. The fundamental structure of ShuffleNetV2 consists of two main types of units. The basic unit divides the input channels into two parts: the left branch remains unchanged, without any operation; the right branch performs a 1 × 1 convolution, a 3 × 3 depthwise convolution, and another 1 × 1 convolution sequentially, and finally concatenates the two parts at the bottom of the module. Subsequently, a random mixing operation is applied to the output feature map channels, as shown in Fig. 7a. The downsampling unit assigns all feature maps to the left and right branches, concatenates the output feature maps from both branches at the bottom of the module, and then performs a channel shuffle operation, as shown in Fig. 7b.

Evaluation metrics

In this study, Accuracy (Acc), Precision (P), Recall (R), and F1-score (F1) were adopted as the performance evaluation metrics for the model, as defined in Eqs. (8)–(11).

TP (True Positive): The number of seedling images correctly classified by the model as belonging to a specific growth stage.

FP (False Positive): The number of seedling images that belong to other growth stages but are incorrectly classified by the model as belonging to the current target stage.

TN (True Negative): The number of images correctly classified by the model as not belonging to the current target stage.

FN (False Negative): The number of seedling images that belong to the current target stage but are incorrectly classified by the model as belonging to other stages.

Additionally, FLOPs serve as a key metric for assessing a model’s computational efficiency. Lower FLOPs indicate fewer required floating-point operations, which directly enhance the model’s capability to perform rapid, real-time identification of seedling growth stages on mobile field devices. Simultaneously, reducing the number of model parameters decreases hardware deployment costs and improves the model’s practical applicability.

Discussion

Discussion on model performance improvement

Lightweight modelling

Lightweight models are essential for deploying deep learning algorithms directly on edge computing devices. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) typically extract object features by increasing network depth. However, merely increasing network depth can result in overfitting to irrelevant features, while simultaneously increasing the number of parameters and computational complexity. As a lightweight architecture, ShuffleNetV2 integrates an adequate number of convolutional operations within its network design. Accordingly, we optimized the number of units across stages, achieving high accuracy while reducing model parameters. These results are consistent with the findings reported by Xu et al.36 and Jin et al.37. This parameter-reduction strategy provides a more efficient approach for deploying models on edge devices.

Improved attention mechanisms

Attention mechanisms are extensively employed to enhance a model’s ability to focus on salient features within regions of interest, thereby improving overall performance38. The Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) attention mechanism captures the relative importance of each channel, thereby endowing the network with enhanced discriminative capability39. The Efficient Channel Attention (ECA) mechanism achieves substantial performance gains while introducing minimal additional parameters by facilitating appropriate cross-channel information exchange40. The Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) integrates attention across both channel and spatial dimensions, thereby enhancing the model’s contextual representation capability.

This study proposes the PWMAM, which adaptively integrates features by connecting the CAM and SAM in parallel, incorporating a weighted fusion strategy. Compared with existing approaches41, PWMAM autonomously adjusts the contribution weights of channel and spatial attention, thereby more effectively mitigating feature dimensionality imbalance. This significantly enhances the feature representation of rice seedlings, constituting a critical factor underlying the model’s performance improvement.

Although PGL-ShuffleNetV2 has been optimized for lightweight deployment, its real-time performance on resource-constrained embedded devices remains limited. Future work could investigate advanced techniques, including neural architecture search (NAS) and knowledge distillation, to identify more computationally efficient architectures.

Effects of laser on the growth stages of rice seedlings

Light significantly influences the growth of rice seedlings. Li et al.42 reported that exposure to combined red and blue light significantly increased total leaf number in Brasenia schreberi seedlings. Ren et al.43 demonstrated that both leaf length and leaf number under red light exceeded those under blue light. These observations are consistent with the results of the present study, in which red light and its combinations with blue light (R1B1, R3B1, and R3B2) significantly promoted vegetative growth. The present study investigated the effects of laser supplemental lighting on rice seedlings from a phenotypic perspective. Nonetheless, the underlying mechanisms by which laser treatment influences photosynthesis, hormone regulation, and gene expression in rice seedlings remain to be elucidated.

Conclusions

This study introduces the PGL-ShuffleNetV2 model, specifically designed to accurately identify the growth stages of rice seedlings. With an impressive accuracy rate of 98.8%, this model represents a substantial improvement of 3.6% over its predecessor, all while reducing the number of parameters by 33.3%. When compared to notable models such as ResNet18, GhostNet, MobileNetV2, and EfficientNet, the enhanced PGL-ShuffleNetV2 stands out for its compact size and superior accuracy, making it an ideal candidate for deployment on small mobile devices. This remarkable performance underscores its significant potential for monitoring rice seedling growth in agricultural production. In this study, the PGL-ShuffleNetV2 model was integrated with laser treatment experiments to investigate the effects of laser exposure on rice seedling growth stages. The results indicate that rice seedlings subjected to laser treatments exhibit distinct developmental changes across growth stages. Red light and red-blue combination laser treatments effectively enhance leaf development, resulting in accelerated leaf growth. These findings provide important insights for the optimization of rice seedling cultivation techniques.

Despite the aforementioned achievements, this study presents several limitations. First, the experimental data utilized in this study are relatively idealized. Additional data accounting for factors such as uneven lighting, weather variations, and shadow occlusion is required to further enhance the model’s generalization ability and robustness under complex scenarios. Second, future research will focus on integrating this model into lightweight IoT devices and embedded development platforms. This integration will facilitate the development of a real-time monitoring system that combines recognition, analysis, and decision-making functionalities, thereby serving as a decision-support tool for agricultural production processes.

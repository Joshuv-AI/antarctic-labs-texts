The function

In

3.
The Case
a=1

From the table given in the introduction we have that

\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}(1)q^{k}=\frac{1}{f_{3}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}p(k)q^{3k}

where
p(k)
is the number of integer partitions of
k
.
Therefore

\displaystyle MO(1,t;N)=\sum_{3k+\binom{n+1}{2}=N}p(k)\cdot c_{n}(1,t)

(6)

where

\displaystyle c_{n}(1,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n+k}(2n+1)\binom{n+k+1}{2k+1}}{n+k+1}\binom{k}{t}3^{k-t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n+k}\left(\binom{n+k+1}{2k+1}+\binom{n+k}{2k+1}\right)\binom{k}{t}3^{k-t}.

We already noted that
MO(1,t;3N+2)=0
identically for all
t
. Next, we significantly extend the congruence in (
5
).

Theorem 3.1
.

For all
J\geq 0
and
N\geq 0
,

MO(1,3J+3;3N+1)\equiv MO(1,3J+2;3N+1)\equiv 0\pmod{3}.

Proof.

Since by (
6
),

MO(1,t;3N+1)=\sum_{3k+\binom{n+1}{2}=3N+1}p(k)\cdot c_{n}(1,t),

it suffices to check the case when

\binom{n+1}{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, that is
n=3m+1
for some integer
m
, and show that

c_{3m+1}(1,t)=\sum_{k=t}^{3m+1}(-1)^{3m+k+1}\left(\binom{3m+k+2}{2k+1}+\binom{3m+k+1}{2k+1}\right)\binom{k}{t}3^{k-t}

is divisible by
3
.
Actually, each summand is immediately divisible by
3
due to the term
3^{k-t}
when
k>t
. For
k=t
, replacing this value to compute the corresponding term, we obtain

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+t}

\displaystyle\left(\binom{3m+t+2}{2t+1}+\binom{3m+t+1}{2t+1}\right)\binom{t}{t}3^{t-t}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+t}\frac{3(2m+1)}{2t+1}\binom{3m+t+1}{2t}

which is divisible by
3
as long as
2t+1\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, that is
t\not\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. Our result follows.
∎

The next result provides a natural strengthening of Theorem
3.1
by increasing the modulus from 3 to 9.

Theorem 3.2
.

For all
J\geq 0
and
N\geq 0
,

MO(1,3J+2;3N+1)\equiv 0\pmod{9}.

Proof.

As in the previous proof,
by (
6
), it suffices to show that

c_{3m+1}(1,t)=\sum_{k=t}^{3m+1}(-1)^{n+k}\left(\binom{3m+k+2}{2k+1}+\binom{3m+k+1}{2k+1}\right)\binom{k}{t}3^{k-t}

is divisible by
9
when
t=3J+2
.

Each summand

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+k}

\displaystyle\left(\binom{3m+k+2}{2k+1}+\binom{3m+k+1}{2k+1}\right)\binom{k}{t}3^{k-t}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+k}\frac{3(2m+1)}{2k+1}\binom{3m+k+1}{2k}\binom{k}{t}3^{k-t}

is immediately divisible by
9
due to the term
3^{k-t}
for
k>t+1
. Let us consider the cases
k=t
and
k=t+1
.

If
k=t=3J+2
then the term is

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+k}

\displaystyle\frac{3(2m+1)}{6J+5}\binom{3(m+J+1)}{6J+4}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+k}\frac{9(2m+1)(m+J+1)}{(6J+5)(6J+4)}\binom{3(m+J+1)-1}{6J+3}\equiv 0\pmod{9}.

If
k=t+1=3J+3
then the term is

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+k}

\displaystyle\frac{3(2m+1)}{6J+7}\binom{3m+3J+4}{6J+6}(3J+3)\cdot 3\equiv 0\pmod{9}.

Combining all of the above facts yields our result.
∎

The next result is related to the well-known congruences of Ramanujan
[
18
]
: For all
N\geq 0
,

\displaystyle p(5N+4)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{5},

\displaystyle p(7N+5)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{7},

\displaystyle p(11N+6)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{11}.

Theorem 3.3
.

For all
J\geq 0
and all
N\geq 0
,

\displaystyle MO(1,5J+4;5N+2)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{5},

\displaystyle MO(1,7J+6;7N+1)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{7},

\displaystyle MO(1,11J+10;11N+7)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{11}.

Proof.

We prove the first congruence. The other two follow in a similar way.
If
t=5J+4
then, by Lucas’s theorem (see for instance
[
9
]
),
\binom{k}{t}\equiv 0\pmod{5}
when
k\not\equiv 4\pmod{5}
. Similarly, if
k\equiv 4\pmod{5}
then

\binom{n+k+1}{2k+1}+\binom{n+k}{2k+1}\equiv 0\pmod{5}

when
n\not\equiv 0,4\pmod{5}
. Hence, if
c_{n}(1,5J+4)
is not divisible by
5
then
n\equiv 0
or
n\equiv 4\pmod{5}
which implies that
\binom{n+1}{2}
is divisible by
5
.
Thus by (
6
),

MO(1,5J+4;5N+2)=\sum_{3k+\binom{n+1}{2}=5N+2}p(k)\cdot c_{n}(1,5J+4)\equiv 0\pmod{5}

because when
\binom{n+1}{2}
is divisible by
5
then
3k\equiv 2\pmod{5}
, that is
k\equiv 4\pmod{5}
. In that case,
p(k)\equiv 0\pmod{5}
by the first of Ramanujan’s congruences recalled above.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

For all
J\geq 2
and all
N\geq 0
,

MO(-1,2^{J}-2;3N+2)\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

Proof.

By (
7

MO(-1,2^{J}-2;3N+2)=\sum_{k+\binom{n+1}{2}=3N+2}\overline{B}_{3}(k)\cdot c_{n}(-1,2^{J}-2)\equiv 0\pmod{4}

because

1) if
n\equiv 0,2\pmod{3}
then
\binom{n+1}{2}\equiv 0\pmod{3}
which implies that
k\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
\overline{B}_{3}(k)\equiv 0\pmod{4}

2) if
n\equiv 1\pmod{3}
then
\binom{n+1}{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
which implies that
k\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
\overline{B}_{3}(k)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
and
c_{n}(-1,2^{J}-2)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.

Indeed, the last congruence holds due to the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{z^{2^{J}-2}(1+z)}{(1-z+z^{2})^{2^{J}-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{2^{J}-2}(1+z)^{2^{J}}(1+z^{3})}{(1+z^{3})^{2^{J}}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{z^{2^{J}-2}(1+z^{2^{J}})(1+z^{3})}{1+z^{3\cdot 2^{J}}}\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{z^{2^{J}-2}+z^{2^{j}+1}+z^{2^{J+1}-2}+z^{2^{J+1}+1}}{1+z^{3\cdot 2^{J}}}\pmod{2}.

Thus, since in the numerator each exponent is not congruent to
1\pmod{3}
, we may conclude that if
n\equiv 1\pmod{3}
then
c_{n}(-1,2^{J}-2)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.
∎

We next prove an isolated congruence modulo 5 which was rather unexpected.

Theorem 4.3
.

For all
N\geq 0
,

MO(-1,3;15N+13)\equiv 0\pmod{5}.

Proof.

We have that

c_{n}(-1,3)=(-1)^{n-1}[z^{n}]\frac{z^{3}(1+z)}{(1-z+z^{2})^{4}}.

Moreover

\displaystyle\frac{z^{3}(1+z)}{(1-z+z^{2})^{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{3}(1+z)(1-z+z^{2})}{(1-z+z^{2})^{5}}\equiv\frac{z^{3}(1+z^{3})}{1-z^{5}+z^{10}}

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{3}(1+z^{3})(1+z^{5})}{1-z^{15}}\equiv\frac{z^{3}+z^{8}+z^{6}+z^{11}}{1-z^{15}}\pmod{5}.

Thus, it follows that if
c_{n}(-1,3)\not\equiv 0\pmod{5}
then
n\equiv 3,6,8,11\pmod{15}
and therefore
\binom{n+1}{2}\equiv 6\pmod{15}
.
Hence, by (
7
),

MO(-1,3;15N+13)=\sum_{k+\binom{n+1}{2}=15N+13}\overline{B}_{3}(k)\cdot c_{n}(-1,3)\equiv 0\pmod{5}

because
\overline{B}_{3}(k)\equiv 0\pmod{5}
when
k\equiv 13-6=7\pmod{15}
.
∎

8.
Appendix:
\overline{B}_{3}(15n+7)\equiv 0\pmod{5}

We close this paper by providing an elementary proof that, for all
n\geq 0
,

\overline{B}_{3}(15n+7)\equiv 0\pmod{5}.

(This fact was used in the proof of Theorem
4.3
above.)

From Ballantine and Merca
[
6
]
, we know that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{B}_{3}(n)q^{n}=\frac{f_{2}f_{3}}{f_{1}^{2}f_{6}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{p}(n)q^{n}\cdot\frac{f_{3}}{f_{6}}

where
\overline{p}(n)
is the number of overpartitions of
n

[
7
,
12
]
.
Recalling that

(-q;-q)_{\infty}=\frac{f_{2}^{3}}{f_{1}f_{4}},

it follows that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{B}_{3}(n)(-q)^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{p}(n)(-q)^{n}\cdot\frac{\frac{f_{6}^{3}}{f_{3}f_{12}}}{f_{6}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{p}(n)(-q)^{n}\cdot\frac{f_{6}^{2}}{f_{3}f_{12}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{B}_{3}(3n+1)(-q)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{p}(3n+1)(-q)^{n}\cdot\frac{f_{2}^{2}}{f_{1}f_{4}}

\displaystyle=2\frac{f_{1}^{6}f_{4}^{6}f_{6}^{9}}{f_{2}^{16}f_{3}^{3}f_{12}^{3}}

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{f_{1}f_{4}f_{6}^{2}}{f_{2}^{3}f_{3}f_{12}}\right)^{5}\cdot\frac{f_{1}f_{4}}{f_{2}}\cdot\frac{f_{3}^{2}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{6}}

where we applied
[
12
, see the formula at the end of page 4]

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{p}(3n+1)(-q)^{n}=2\frac{\varphi(q^{3})^{2}}{\varphi(q)^{4}}\cdot X(q)=2\frac{f_{1}^{7}f_{4}^{7}f_{6}^{9}}{f_{2}^{18}f_{3}^{3}f_{12}^{3}},

with

\varphi(q)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}=\frac{f_{2}^{5}}{f_{1}^{2}f_{4}^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad X(q)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{3n^{2}+2n}=\frac{f_{2}^{2}f_{3}f_{12}}{f_{1}f_{4}f_{6}}.

Hence

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{B}_{3}(3n+1)(-q)^{n}\equiv F(q^{5})\cdot\frac{f_{1}f_{4}}{f_{2}}\cdot\frac{f_{3}^{2}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{6}}\pmod{5}

where

F(q^{5})=2\frac{f_{5}f_{20}f_{30}^{2}}{f_{10}^{3}f_{15}f_{60}}\equiv 2\left(\frac{f_{1}f_{4}f_{6}^{2}}{f_{2}^{3}f_{3}f_{12}}\right)^{5}\pmod{5}.

Since
15n+7=3(5n+2)+1
, it suffices to show that the coefficient of
q^{5n+2}
in

\frac{f_{1}f_{4}}{f_{2}}\cdot\frac{f_{3}^{2}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{6}}
is divisible by
5
.
Indeed, from
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
, we see that

\frac{f_{1}f_{4}}{f_{2}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-q)^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}}

and

\frac{f_{3}^{2}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{6}}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{j}{3}jq^{j^{2}-1}.

Thus

[q^{5n+2}]\frac{f_{1}f_{4}}{f_{2}}\cdot\frac{f_{3}^{2}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{6}}=\sum_{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}+j^{2}-1=5n+2}\left((-1)^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}}\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{j}{3}j\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5}

because
\frac{k(k+1)}{2}+j^{2}-1=5n+2
implies
k(k+1)+2j^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{5}
.
Notice that, modulo 5,
k(k+1)\in\{0,1,2\}
and
2j^{2}\in\{0,2,3\}
. Thus the previous congruence is satisfied only when
k(k+1)\equiv 1
and
2j^{2}\equiv 0
. Hence we may conclude that
j
is divisible by
5
, and this yields our result.

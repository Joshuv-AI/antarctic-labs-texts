Spin dynamics of an easy-plane Dirac spin liquid in a frustrated XY model: Application to honeycomb cobaltates
Anjishnu Bose, Arun Paramekanti

Abstract
Recent work has shown that the honeycomb lattice spin- 1 / 2 1/2 J 1 J_{1} - J 3 J_{3} XY model, with nearest-neighbor ferromagnetic exchange J 1 J_{1} and frustration induced by third-neighbor antiferromagnetic exchange J 3 J_{3} , may be relevant to a wide range of cobaltate materials. We explore a variational Monte Carlo study of Gutzwiller projected wavefunctions for this model and show that an easy-plane Dirac spin liquid (DSL) is a viable ‘parent’ state for the competing magnetic orders observed in these materials, including ferromagnetic, zig-zag, spiral, and double zig-zag orders at intermediate frustration, and show that such broken symmetry states can be easily polarized by a weak in-plane magnetic field consistent with experiments. We formulate a modified parton theory for such frustrated spin models, and explore the potential instabilities of the DSL due to residual parton interactions within a random phase approximation (RPA), both at zero magnetic field and in a nonzero in-plane field. The broken symmetry states which emerge in the vicinity of this Dirac spin liquid include ferromagnetic, zig-zag, and incommensurate spiral orders, with a phase diagram which is consistent with VMC and density matrix renormalization group studies. We calculate the dynamical spin response of the easy-plane DSL, including RPA corrections, near the boundary of the ordered states, and present results for THz spectroscopy and inelastic neutron scattering, at zero field as well as in an in-plane magnetic field, and discuss experimental implications.

We explore an approach where we take the spin Hamiltonian

Going beyond mean field theory for the first term amounts to incorporating order parameter fluctuations, e.g. within a random phase approximation (RPA), while going beyond mean field theory in the second term leads to a

\alpha

\alpha=0

\alpha=1

\alpha^{*}
incorporating both aspects. Here, we will simply fix an optimal

to recover salient features of the ground state phase diagram of the frustrated spin model. We then use this to compute the dynamical spin response of the system,
showing that it captures many of the qualitative features such as sharp peaks coexisting with
broad continuum scattering observed in Terahertz (THz) spectroscopy and inelastic neutron scattering.

II
Methodology

Consider a general lattice model of spin-
1/2

{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{{{\bm{\delta}}}}J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})\sum_{\mathbf{r}}S_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r})S_{j}^{b}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}})\,,

(1)

where
i,j
represent sublattices,
\mathbf{r}
marks the unit cell
s.t.

(\mathbf{r},i)
and
(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}},j)
are neighboring sites, while
a,b
indicate spin-directions. Now, the spin-operator can be decomposed in terms of spin-
1/2
fermionic spinons
S_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r},\tau)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)\cdot\sigma^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\cdot f_{i,\beta}(\mathbf{r},\tau)
, under the constraint that
\sum_{\alpha}f_{i,\alpha}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r},\tau)f_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)=1
which can be imposed using a Lagrange multiplier
a_{0}(\mathbf{r},\tau)
. Hence, The total parton action looks like (replacing
{\mathcal{O}}^{\dagger}
with
\bar{{\mathcal{O}}}
in the path-integral)

{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,a_{0}]\!=\!\int\!\!d\tau\left\{\sum_{\mathbf{r}}{{\bar{f}}}_{i,\alpha}\left[\partial_{\tau}-a_{0}\right]f_{i,\alpha}+{\mathcal{H}}\right\}\,,

(2)

and the partition function being
{\mathcal{Z}}=\int{\mathcal{D}}[{{\bar{f}}},f\,,a_{0}]\>e^{-{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,a_{0}]}
. Such a parton action involves a Hamiltonian with a four-fermion interaction, which can be decoupled using Hubbard-Stratonovich (HS) fields.
However, this decoupling can be done in multiple channels : on-site magnetic Weiss fields
h_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r})
, or parton hopping fields living on bonds
w_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}}/2)
. The magnetic channel Hubbard Stratonovich transformation on a single bond
(i,j)
looks like

\exp\left(-{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}\right)=\int dh\exp\left(h_{i}^{a}(J^{-1})_{ij}^{ab}h_{j}^{b}-2S_{i}^{a}h_{i}^{a}\right)\,,

(3)

while in the hopping channel it looks like

\begin{split}\exp\left(-{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}\right)&=\int d\bar{w}dw\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{4} J_{ij}^{ab} \s^a_{\a\b}\s^{b}_{\g\d}\bigg[\\
&\brf_{i, \a}f_{j, \d}\bar{w}_{ij}^{\b\g} + \brf_{j, \g}f_{i, \b}w_{ij}^{\a\d} - w_{ij}^{\a\d}\bar{w}_{ij}^{\b\g} \bigg]\bigg)\,.\end{split}

(4)

When
h_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r},\tau)
condenses breaking spin-rotation symmetry, the spins gain an expectation value in the ground state with the same pattern as the Weiss fields
\sum_{{{\bm{\delta}}}}J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})\expectationvalue{S_j^b(\br)}=\expectationvalue{h_i^a(\br)}
. Meanwhile, if
w_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{r},\tau)
condense, we get the fermions hopping on the lattice in some frozen background gauge flux (the same gauge redundancy which arises when writing the spins using fermions) determined by the phases of
\expectationvalue{w_{ij}^{\a\b}}

[
30
,
4
]
. These ground state expectation values of the condensates can equivalently be calculated in self-consistent mean-field theory as well.

Now, in general, one can perform this decomposition on each interacting bond with some relative ratios as
{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}\xrightarrow{MFT}(1-\alpha_{ij}){\mathcal{H}}^{w}_{ij}+\alpha_{ij}{\mathcal{H}}^{h}_{ij}
where
\alpha_{ij}\in[0,1]
captures increasing quantum corrections (
\alpha_{ij}>0
) starting from purely classical physics (
\alpha_{ij}=0
). The full path integral is over the fermionic spinons
f_{i,\alpha}
, the Lagrange multiplier
a_{0}
(forming the temporal part of the gauge field), the hopping fields
w_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}
(which can be split into an absolute value and a phase, with the phase providing the spatial component of the gauge field), and the Weiss fields
h_{i}^{a}
. As noted earlier, if calculated
exactly
, the final partition function should be independent of the set of all
\alpha_{ij}
parameters denoted by
\{\alpha\}
. However, if we simplify by assuming that the
w_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}
fields are condensed around their mean-field values (thereby suppressing gauge fluctuations altogether), then the resulting effective action in general will depend on
\{\alpha\}
,
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\{\alpha\}]=e^{-{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\{\alpha\}]}
such that

\begin{split}e^{-{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\{\alpha\}]}&=e^{-{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[{{\bar{w}}},w;\{\alpha\}]}\!\int\!{\mathcal{D}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,h]e^{-{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,h;\{\alpha\}]},\end{split}

(5)

where
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[{{\bar{w}}},w;\{\alpha\}]
is just the mean-field free energy of the spin liquid. The action being integrated over
{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,h;\{\alpha\}]
, consists of three parts : (i) free fermions hopping at half-filling on a lattice with the hoppings determined by the mean-field values of
\expectationvalue{w_{ij}^{\a\b}}
for a given spin-interaction
J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})
, and chosen value of
\alpha_{ij}({{\bm{\delta}}})
, denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[{{\bar{f}}},f;\{\alpha\}]
, (ii) quadratic action of the Weiss fields
h_{i}^{a}
denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[h;\{\alpha\}]
, and (iii) the spin-spin interaction between fermions and the Weiss field denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{int}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,h;\{\alpha\}]
(details given in Appendix.
A
). One can now integrate out the fermions as they only appear to quadratic order in all the terms, and get an effective action of the Weiss fields,
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[h;\{\alpha\}]
. To quadratic order (equivalent to RPA), this looks like (switching to momentum and imaginary frequency space)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[h;\{\alpha\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\Omega}\sum_{{{\bf{Q}}}}h_{i}^{a}(-{{\bf{Q}}},-i\Omega)h_{j}^{b}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega)

(6)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\big[{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})-\chi_{0}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})\big]_{ij}^{ab},

where
{\mathcal{J}}_{ij}^{ab}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})\equiv(1-\alpha_{ij}({{\bm{\delta}}}))J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})
is the re-scaled spin-interaction
written in momentum space, and
\chi_{0}
is the bare magnetic susceptibility of the fermions (which depends on
\{\alpha\}
through the hoppings as well). One can then integrate out the Weiss fields and get the free energy

\begin{split}{\cal F}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\{\alpha\}]&={\mathcal{S}}_{0}[{{\bar{w}}},w;\{\alpha\}]+\\
&\sum_{i\Omega,{{\bf{Q}}}}\mathrm{Tr}\ln\left({\mathcal{J}}^{-1}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})-\chi_{0}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})\right)\end{split}

(7)

The total free energy can now in principle be minimized with respect to
\{\alpha\}
to provide optimal values
\{\alpha^{*}\}
. However, one can also treat them as tuning parameters which may be constrained such that the RPA phase diagram reproduces a true many-body numerical calculation of the ground state using any other method (like DMRG or VMC). As we will see in the later sections, the dynamic response functions also obtained in this RPA process exhibits continuum features expected from
f
-spinon excitation of the spin-liquid. Moreover, it also demonstrates well defined features expected from magnon-like excitations
of the classical order, which emerge as two-particle bound states of the spinons under RPA, tracked by the
h
Weiss fields.

VII
Discussion

In this paper, we have shown that a modified phenomenological parton approach including RPA corrections is a useful route to understanding the phase diagram and spin dynamics in magnetic systems with weak ordering and a proximate spin-liquid state. In this approach, we split the spin Hamiltonian (written in terms of fermionic spinons) into two parts and decompose each using different HS fields : classical dynamic Weiss fields, as well as spinon hopping fields assumed to be condensed around their mean-field value. Subsequently we integrate out the spinons and are left with an effective action of the Weiss fields to leading order (equivalent to RPA). We then focus on a class of
J_{1}
-
J_{3}
XY spin Hamiltonian on the honeycomb lattice relevant to the honeycomb cobaltates which showcase a plethora of magnetic orderings in a relatively narrow parameter regime. We show using VMC that there exists a regime where a pure DSL, and DSL+weak orderings like FM, ZZ, dZZ, and spiral order are all within
1
-
2\%
of each other in their optimized energies. Focusing on this regime, we employ our modified RPA method and reproduce the full many-body phase diagram. Furthermore, we illustrate how our RPA corrected magnetic susceptibility reproduces key features in THz spectroscopy
[
41
]
and inelastic neutron scattering experiments on BaCo
2
(AsO
4
)
2
[
14
,
59
]
. Following that, we study the system under an external in-plane Zeeman field and look at the phase diagram, magnetization curve, as well as physical response functions. We again find very good agreement with experimental results, especially for the polarizing field strength.

For future directions, it would be interesting to develop a controlled diagrammatic route to our methodology which can be formally justified for spin-
1/2
systems. Such a controlled setup can in principle be extended to include diagrams beyond RPA, and might perhaps lead to quantitative agreements with experimental results. Additionally, it would useful to explore the calculation of optimal parameters
\{\alpha^{*}\}
from the full free energy given in Eq.(
7
). It would also be interesting to study and connect our current results to the low energy theory which would consist of
N_{f}=4
flavour fermions coupled to a
SU(2)
gauge theory (not a
U(1)
)
[
28
,
29
,
27
,
26
]
. Specifically, one needs to understand what role do monopoles play in these possibly weakly ordered Dirac spin-liquids.

On the experimental side, there is a huge playground of materials which exhibit weak ordering but which may not be completely characterized by simple LSWT. There have been a series of work on triangular lattice anti-ferromagnet YbMgGaO
4
where a similar RPA enhanced mean-field spinon fermi surface state has been successful in describing inelastic neutron scattering experiments
[
67
,
68
,
69
]
. Cobalt based materials on the triangular lattice are also described by some easy-plane spin-
1/2
Hamiltonian
e.g.
Na
2
BaCo(PO
4
)
2
[
70
]
, and K
2
Co(SeO
3
)
2
[
71
]
. Experiments on these materials show weak ordering at low temperatures, but exhibit spin-liquid like continuum in their INS spectrum, which makes them appropriate candidates for applying our methodology. In contrast, there is another triangular lattice material YbZn
2
GaO
5
which has recently garnered interest
[
72
,
73
,
74
]
. It is a candidate material for hosting a Dirac spin liquid itself, described by a
J_{1}
-
J_{2}
easy-axis model, and hence would be interesting to study using our approach as well. Investigating these extensions and candidate materials would improve our understanding of the interplay between spinons and magnons in the dynamics of frustrated magnets.

Appendix A
Path Integral Formulation

Suppose we have a general spin Hamiltonian on a lattice

{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)=\sum_{{{\bm{\delta}}}}J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})\sum_{\mathbf{r}}S_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r},\tau)S_{j}^{b}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}},\tau)\,,

(12)

where
\mathbf{r}
marks the unit cell,
i\,,j
represent sublattice degree of freedom, and
a\,,b
represent spin directions. Now we decompose the spin operator using fermionic partons as

S_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r},\tau)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)\cdot\sigma^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\cdot f_{i,\beta}(\mathbf{r},\tau)\,,

(13)

under the constraint that

\sum_{\alpha}f^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)f_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)=1\>\>\>\forall\>\>\>\mathbf{r}\,,i\,,\tau\,.

(14)

This constraint can be imposed using a Lagrange multiplier
a_{0}(\mathbf{r},\tau)
. The total parton action hence looks like (at zero temperature)

{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,a_{0}]=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\left\{\sum_{\mathbf{r}}{{\bar{f}}}_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)\left[\partial_{\tau}-a_{0}(\mathbf{r},\tau)\right]f_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)+{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)\right\}\,,

(15)

and the path integral looks like

{\mathcal{Z}}=\int{\mathcal{D}}[{{\bar{f}}},f\,,a_{0}]\>e^{-{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,a_{0}]}\,.

(16)

A.1
Hubbard Stratonovich Decoupling

The action consists of a four-fermion interaction term. This can be decoupled using Hubbard Stratonovich (HS) fields. However, this can be done in two channels : (a) when the fermions are hopping, (b) when there is magnetic ordering. Furthermore, both these channels can exist simultaneously with some relative ratio which can depend on the bond being decoupled as well.

In the ordering channel, we have that

\exp\left(-S_{i}^{a}J_{ij}^{ab}S_{j}^{b}\right)=\int\prod_{a}dm_{i}^{a}\exp\left(m_{i}^{a}J_{ij}^{ab}m_{j}^{b}-2S_{i}^{a}J_{ij}^{ab}m_{j}^{b}\right)\,,

(17)

where
m_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r},\tau)
can be interpreted as the dynamic magnetization on the site labeled by
(\mathbf{r},i)
(we will be using the magnetization instead of the usual Weiss fields to simplify some steps, but at the end will switch back to Weiss fields). For the full Hamiltonian, we get

\begin{split}\exp\left(-\int d\tau{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)\right)=\int{\mathcal{D}}[m]\exp\left(\sum_{{{\bm{\delta}}}}\sum_{\mathbf{r}}J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})\left[m_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r},\tau)m_{j}^{b}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}},\tau)-2S_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r},\tau)m_{j}^{b}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}},\tau)\right]\right)\,.\end{split}

(18)

If this HS field condenses
i.e.

\expectationvalue{m_i^a(\br, \t)}=\expectationvalue{S_i^a(\br)}=m_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r})
and gets some finite expectaion value, the system will undergo symmetry breaking and exhibit magnetic ordering. The lowest energy condensate can be found through self-consistent mean-field theory. However, one can also consider excitations around such a condensate
m_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r},\tau)=m_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r})+\delta m(\mathbf{r},\tau)
. If we consider excitations around the mean-field condensate to leading order which will be quadratic, it is equivalent to performing random phase approximation (RPA). Meanwhile, in the hopping channel, we have that

\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}S_{i}^{a}J_{ij}^{ab}S_{j}^{b}\right)=\int\prod_{\alpha,\beta}d(w_{ij}^{ab})^{*}dw_{ij}^{ab}\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}J_{ij}^{ab}\sigma^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\sigma^{b}_{\gamma\delta}\left[{{\bar{f}}}_{i,\alpha}f_{j,\delta}(w_{ij}^{\beta\gamma})^{*}+{{\bar{f}}}_{j,\gamma}f_{i,\beta}w_{ij}^{\alpha\delta}-w_{ij}^{\alpha\delta}(w_{ij}^{\beta\gamma})^{*}\right]\right)\,,

(19)

where the hopping HS field lives on the bonds, instead of on-site. For the full Hamiltonian, we get

\begin{split}\exp\left(-\int d\tau{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)\right)&=\int{\mathcal{D}}[{{\bar{w}}},w]\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{4} \sum_{\bd}\sum_{\br}J_{ij}^{ab}(\bd) \s^a_{\a\b}\s^{b}_{\g\d} \bigg[- w_{ij}^{\a\d}(\br+\bd/2, \t)\brw_{ij}^{\b\g}(\br+\bd/2, \t) \\
& +\brf_{i, \a}(\br, \t)f_{j, \d}(\br+\bd, \t)\brw_{ij}^{\b\g}(\br+\bd/2, \t) + \brf_{j, \g}(\br+\bd, \t)f_{i, \b}(\br, \t)w_{ij}^{\a\d}(\br+\bd/2, \t) \bigg]\bigg)\,,\end{split}

(20)

When
w_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}
gains an expectation value
\expectationvalue{w_{ij}^{\a\b}(\br+\bd/2, \t)}=\expectationvalue{f^{\dagger}_{i,\a}(\br)f_{j, \b}(\br+\bd)}=w_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}}/2)
, we get the fermions hopping on the lattice in some frozen background gauge flux determined by the phases of
w_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}
. This gauge is the same gauge redundancy which arises when writing Eq.(
13
), which for simplicity’s sake we are going to assume is just
U(1)
(can be
SU(2)
also) .
This separation can be made explicit by writing
w_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}=|w_{ij}^{ab}|e^{-ia_{ij}}
, such that phase modulations are captured by gauge excitations around the background gauge flux which can be determined self-consistently.

Now, in general, we can imagine splitting the Hamiltonian on each bond as
{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}=\alpha_{ij}{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}^{w}+(1-\alpha_{ij}){\mathcal{H}}_{{ij}^{m}}
, where
{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}^{w}
is decomposed using Eq.(
20
), while
{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}^{m}
is decomposed using Eq.(
18
). In total we can write down the full path integral as

{\mathcal{Z}}=\int{\mathcal{D}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,{{\bar{w}}},w,m,a_{0}]e^{-{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[{{\bar{f}}},f,a_{0}]}e^{-{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,{{\bar{w}}},w;\{\alpha\}]}e^{-{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,m;\{1-\alpha\}]}\,.

(21)

If we calculate the path integral
exactly
, then the partition function
should not
depend on
\{\alpha\}
. However, we are going to assume that the
w_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}
fields are condensed around their uniform mean-field values
i.e.
all the gauge fluctuations in the system are suppressed. Then, the resulting partition function can in general depend on
\{\alpha\}
as

\begin{split}{\mathcal{Z}}&=\int{\mathcal{D}}[a_{0},{{\bar{w}}},w]e^{-{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[a_{0}\,,{{\bar{w}}},w;\{\alpha\}]}\,,\\
e^{-{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[a_{0}\,,{{\bar{w}}},w;\{\alpha\}]}&=\exp\left(-\sum_{\mathbf{r},{{\bm{\delta}}}}\frac{\alpha_{ij}({{\bm{\delta}}})J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})}{4}\sigma^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\sigma^{b}_{\gamma\delta}w_{ij}^{\alpha\delta}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}}/2,\tau){{\bar{w}}}_{ij}^{\beta\gamma}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}}/2,\tau)\right)\int{\mathcal{D}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,m]e^{-{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,m;\{\alpha\}]}\,,\end{split}

(22)

where

\begin{split}{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,m;\{\alpha\}]&=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\sum_{\mathbf{r}}\bigg\{{{\bar{f}}}_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)\left[\partial_{\tau}-a_{0}\right]f_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)\\
&-\sum_{\mathbf{r}}\sum_{{{\bm{\delta}}}}\left[{{\bar{f}}}_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)t_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}({{\bm{\delta}}};\{\alpha\})f_{j,\beta}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}},\tau)+\mathrm{h.c.}\right]\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{r}}\sum_{{{\bm{\delta}}}}(1-\alpha_{ij}({{\bm{\delta}}}))J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})\left[{{\bar{f}}}_{i,\alpha}(\mathbf{r},\tau)\sigma_{\alpha\beta}^{a}m_{j}^{b}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}},\tau)f_{i,\beta}(\mathbf{r},\tau)+(\mathrm{i\leftrightarrow j})\right]\\
&-\sum_{\mathbf{r}}\sum_{{{\bm{\delta}}}}(1-\alpha_{ij}({{\bm{\delta}}}))J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})m_{i}^{a}(\mathbf{r},\tau)m_{j}^{b}(\mathbf{r}+{{\bm{\delta}}},\tau)\,,\\
&\equiv{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[{{\bar{f}}},f;\{\alpha\}]+{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{int}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,m;\{\alpha\}]+{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[m;\{\alpha\}]\,,\end{split}

(23)

where
t_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}({{\bm{\delta}}};\{\alpha\})=(1/4)\alpha_{ij}({{\bm{\delta}}})J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})\sigma^{a}_{\alpha\delta}\sigma^{b}_{\gamma\beta}{{\bar{w}}}_{ij}^{\delta\gamma}
.

A.2
Integrating out spinons

We switch to momentum space and write down each piece of the action as

\begin{split}{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[{{\bar{f}}},f;\{\alpha\}]&=\sum_{i\omega}\sum_{{{\bf{k}}}}{{\bar{f}}}_{i,\alpha}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega)\left[(i\omega-a_{0})\delta_{ij}\delta^{\alpha\beta}+{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}({{\bf{k}}};\{\alpha\})\right]f_{j,\beta}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega)\,,\\
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[m;\{\alpha\}]&=-\sum_{i\omega}\sum_{{{\bf{k}}}}m_{i}^{a}(-{{\bf{k}}},-i\omega){\mathcal{J}}_{ij}^{ab}({{\bf{k}}};\{\alpha\})m_{b}^{j}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega)\,,\\
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{int}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,m;\{\alpha\}]&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\Omega,i\omega}\sum_{{{\bf{Q}}},{{\bf{k}}}}m_{i}^{a}(-{{\bf{Q}}},-i\Omega){\mathcal{J}}_{ij}^{ab}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\}){{\bar{f}}}_{j,\alpha}({{\bf{k}}}+{{\bf{Q}}},i\omega+i\Omega)\sigma^{b}_{\alpha\beta}f_{j,\beta}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega)\,,\end{split}

(24)

where
J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bf{k}}})=\sum_{{{\bm{\delta}}}}J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})e^{-i{{\bf{k}}}\cdot{{\bm{\delta}}}}
,
\tilde{J}_{ij}^{ab}({{\bf{k}}};\{\alpha\})=\sum_{{{\bm{\delta}}}}\alpha_{ij}({{\bm{\delta}}})J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bm{\delta}}})e^{-i{{\bf{k}}}\cdot{{\bm{\delta}}}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}_{ij}^{ab}({{\bf{k}}};\{\alpha\})=J_{ij}^{ab}({{\bf{k}}})-\tilde{J}_{ij}^{ab}({{\bf{k}}};\{\alpha\})
. The free hopping Hamiltonian in momentum space looks like

{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}({{\bf{k}}};\{\alpha\})=-\sum_{{{\bm{\delta}}}}t_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}({{\bm{\delta}}};\{\alpha\})e^{-i{{\bf{k}}}\cdot{{\bm{\delta}}}}
. Combining everything we get

\begin{split}{\mathcal{S}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,m;\{\alpha\}]&=\sum_{i\omega}\sum_{{{\bf{k}}}}\left\{{{\bar{f}}}_{i,\alpha}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega)\left[{\mathcal{G}}_{0}^{-1}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega;\{\alpha\})\right]_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}f_{j,\beta}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega)+m_{i}^{a}(-{{\bf{k}}},-i\omega)\left[G_{m}^{-1}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega;\{\alpha\})\right]_{ij}^{ab}m_{j}^{b}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega)\right\}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\Omega,i\omega}\sum_{{{\bf{Q}}},{{\bf{k}}}}{\mathcal{J}}_{ij}^{ab}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})\sigma^{b}_{\alpha\beta}m_{i}^{a}(-{{\bf{Q}}},-i\Omega){{\bar{f}}}_{j,\alpha}({{\bf{k}}}+{{\bf{Q}}},i\omega+i\Omega)f_{j,\beta}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega)\,.\end{split}

(25)

We then want to integrate out the spinons and get an effective action in
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[m;\{\alpha\}]
as

e^{-{\mathcal{S}}[m;\{\alpha\}]}=\int{\mathcal{D}}[{{\bar{f}}},f]e^{-{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[{{\bar{f}}},f;\{\alpha\}]}e^{-{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{int}}[{{\bar{f}}},f,m;\{\alpha\}]}={\mathcal{Z}}_{0}\expectationvalue{e^{-\ms_{\mathrm{int}}[\brf, f, m ; \{\a\}]}}_{0}\,.

(26)

Now, we assume that the magnetization field is fluctuating weakly around its mean-field value
m=\expectationvalue{m}+\delta m
. However, if our mean-field solution is a spin liquid itself,
\expectationvalue{m}=0
. Hence, the expectation value in Eq.(
26
) can be calculated in perturbation theory since the magnetization field
m
itself can be treated as weak
\expectationvalue{e^{-\ms_{\mathrm{int}}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-\frac{1}{n!})\expectationvalue{\ms_{\mathrm{int}}^n}
. We can further drop the first order term which is linear in
m
since that term is just a disconnected diagram and will get canceled in the normalization. At second order, we get

\begin{split}\expectationvalue{\ms_{\mathrm{int}}^2}&=\sum_{i\Omega}\sum_{{{\bf{Q}}}}m_{i}^{a}(-{{\bf{Q}}},-i\Omega)\cdot[{\mathcal{J}}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})\cdot\chi_{0}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})\cdot{\mathcal{J}}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})]_{ij}^{ab}\cdot m_{j}^{b}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega)\,,\end{split}

(27)

where
\chi_{0}
is the bare susceptibility bubble diagram

\chi_{0}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})_{ij}^{ab}=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i\omega}\sum_{{{\bf{k}}}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma^{a}\cdot{\mathcal{G}}_{0,ji}^{T}({{\bf{k}}},i\omega;\{\alpha\})\cdot\sigma^{b}\cdot{\mathcal{G}}_{0,ij}^{T}({{\bf{k}}}+{{\bf{Q}}},i\omega+i\Omega;\{\alpha\})\right]\,.

(28)

Combining with
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}[m]
, we have

{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[m;\{\alpha\}]=\sum_{i\Omega}\sum_{{{\bf{Q}}}}m_{i}^{a}(-{{\bf{Q}}},-i\Omega)\cdot[{\mathcal{J}}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})-{\mathcal{J}}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})\cdot\chi_{0}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})\cdot{\mathcal{J}}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})]_{ij}^{ab}\cdot m_{j}^{b}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega)\,.

(29)

This gives us that

\expectationvalue{m_i^a(-\bQ, -i\W)m_j^b(\bQ, i\W)}=\left(\left[{\mathcal{J}}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})-{\mathcal{J}}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})\cdot\chi_{0}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})\cdot{\mathcal{J}}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})\right]^{-1}\right)_{ij}^{ab}\,.

(30)

We can now switch back to the usual weiss fields
h_{i}^{a}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega)={\mathcal{J}}_{ij}^{ab}({{\bf{Q}}})\cdot m_{j}^{b}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega)
instead of the magnetization, and get

{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[h;\{\alpha\}]=\sum_{i\Omega}\sum_{{{\bf{Q}}}}h_{i}^{a}(-{{\bf{Q}}},-i\Omega)\cdot[{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})-\chi_{0}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})]_{ij}^{ab}\cdot h_{j}^{b}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega)\,.

(31)

\expectationvalue{h_i^a(-\bQ, -i\W)h_j^b(\bQ, i\W)}=[{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})-\chi_{0}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})]_{ij}^{ab}\,.

(32)

In the effective action, if
\det[{\mathcal{J}}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})^{-1}-\chi_{0}({{\bf{Q}}},\Omega+i\eta;\{\alpha\})]<0
for some
{{\bf{Q}}}_{c}
at
\Omega=0
, we have an instability.

A.3
RPA corrections to response functions

To get the physical susceptibility of the system, we have to introduce an external field
h_{\mathrm{ext}}
, and find the effective action in terms of these fields. Doing so will give us an action with the same form as Eq.(
31
), but with the RPA corrected susceptibility which will look like

{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eff}}[h_{\mathrm{ext}};\{\alpha\}]=\sum_{i\Omega}\sum_{{{\bf{Q}}}}h_{\mathrm{ext},i}^{a}(-{{\bf{Q}}},-i\Omega)\cdot[\chi^{-1}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})]_{ij}^{ab}\cdot h_{\mathrm{ext},j}^{b}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega)\,,

(33)

where
\chi^{-1}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})=\chi_{0}^{-1}({{\bf{Q}}},i\Omega;\{\alpha\})-{\mathcal{J}}({{\bf{Q}}};\{\alpha\})
.

Appendix B
VMC

In our VMC implementation, the auxillary parton Hamiltonian we have considered looks like

\begin{split}{\mathcal{H}}^{f}=-t_{1}\sum_{\expectationvalue{i, j}_{1}}\left(f^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}\sigma^{z}_{\alpha\beta}f_{j,\beta}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right)-t_{3}\sum_{\expectationvalue{i, j}_{3}}\left(f^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}\sigma^{z}_{\alpha\beta}f_{j,\beta}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}h_{i}^{a}f^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}\sigma^{a}_{\alpha\beta}f_{i,\beta}\,,\end{split}

(34)

where
t_{1}=1
can be held fixed. The site-dependent Weiss fields
h_{i}^{a}
depend on the specific ordering pattern being considered. In this paper we worked with a
L=3\times 12\times 2
honeycomb lattice with a quadrupled (along one direction) unit cell to accommodate the different ordering patterns as shown in Fig.
13
. All the in-plane collinear orders can be captured by a single weiss field strength
h
whereas the co-planar spiral order requires three independent Weiss fields to keep track of.

The mean-field ground state
\ket{\psi_{\mathrm{MFT}}}
can then be found by diagonalizing this quadratic Hamiltonian and filling states upto half-filling. This mean-field state variational state is then Gutzwiller projected to get the full many-body spin wavefunction
\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi(\{t_3, h_i, g_{ij}\})}=\exp\left(-\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z}\right)\hat{P}\ket{\psi_{\mathrm{MFT}}(t_3, h_i)}
, where
g_{ij}
are Jastrow factors to further enhance spin-correlations. We then measure the expectation value of the full spin Hamiltonian in Eq.(
9
) as
E(\{t_{3},h_{i},g_{ij}\})=\expectationvalue{\psi| \mh^{\ms} |\psi}/\expectationvalue{\psi|\psi}
by sampling the many-body wavefunction through 10,000 (thermalization) + 10,000 (measurement) Monte Carlo sweeps. This energy is then optimized
w.r.t
the variational parameters of the wavefunction using stochastic reconfiguration gradient descent
[
18
]
for 250 steps. The optimal values of the third neighbour hopping is shown in Fig.

t_{3}/t_{1}\approx 0.1
-
0.2
in the intermediate regime of interest. Furthermore, in-plane spin-spin correlation on first and third neighbours are reported in Fig.

Abstract
. We prove the existence of a localization spectral sequence for the hat variant of Guth and Manolescu’s recent construction of real Heegaard Floer homology, and apply it to branched double covers and strongly invertible knots. Our construction applies to real Lagrangian Floer homology in exact symplectic manifolds equipped with anti-symplectic involutions more generally, and may be of independent interest to symplectic geometers.



1.
Introduction

In the past few years, there has been considerable interest in gauge-theoretic invariants of three-manifolds and four-manifolds equipped with an involution, called real manifolds. In the Seiberg-Witten case, this line of study originated with Tian-Wang
[
40
]
and Nakamura
[
27
,
26
]
and has had numerous recent remarkable applications, including for example
[
21
,
24
,
20
,
18
,
1
]
.

Ozsváth and Szabó’s Heegaard Floer homology is a Lagrangian Floer theoretic counterpart of Seiberg-Witten theory
[
30
,
28
]
. Recently, Guth and Manolescu
[
10
]
gave a construction of real Heegaard Floer homology associated to a three-manifold
Y
with an orientation-preserving involution
\tau
whose fixed point set has codimension two; such a pair
(Y,\tau)
will be called a
real three-manifold
. More generally, they give a construction of real Lagrangian Floer theory, as we now explain.

Suppose that
(M,\omega)
is a symplectic manifold with two Lagrangians
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
, satisfying suitable hypotheses for defining Lagrangian Floer homology. (We will detail our hypotheses explicitly in the theorems that follow and in Section
2
.) Suppose further that
M
admits an anti-symplectic involution
R
which exchanges
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
; that is,
L_{1}=R(L_{0})
. In this case, the fixed point set
M^{R}
is also a Lagrangian submanifold of
M
. In its simplest description, the real Lagrangian Floer homology of
(M,L_{0},L_{1})
defined in
[
10
]
is

\mathit{HFR}(M,L_{0},L_{1}):=\mathit{HF}(M,L_{0},M^{R}).

Here the right-hand side is the ordinary Lagrangian Floer homology of the two Lagrangians
L_{0}
and
M^{R}
in
M
. The application to the case of Heegaard Floer homology then proceeds as follows. Recall that the Heegaard Floer homology of a 3-manifold
Y
is computed by choosing a Heegaard diagram
(\Sigma,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},w)
such that
\Sigma
is a surface of genus
g
with two sets of attaching curves
\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{\beta}
specifying handlebodies
U_{\alpha}
and
U_{\beta}
, and
Y=U_{\alpha}\cup_{\Sigma}U_{\beta}
, with
w
denoting a basepoint away from the attaching curves. Heegaard Floer homology has several flavors, which are given by suitable versions of the Lagrangian Floer homology of
(\Sym^{g}(\Sigma),\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}})
, where
\Sym^{g}(\Sigma)
denotes the symmetric product of the surface together with a symplectic form which is a product form away from the diagonal, and
\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}}
and
\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}}
are the tori formed by taking the products of the two sets of attaching curves. In particular,
\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y)
is given by
\mathit{HF}(\Sym^{g}(\Sigma-\{w\}),\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}})
. The construction splits along the set of
\spin^{c}
structures on
Y
, so that
\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y)=\oplus_{\mathfrak{s}\in\Spin^{c}(Y)}\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y,\mathfrak{s})
. Given a real three-manifold
(Y,\tau)
, Guth and Manolescu
[
10
, Proposition 3.2]
use work of Nagase
[
25
, Proposition 2.4]
to show there exists a Heegaard diagram
H=(\Sigma,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},w)
for
Y
which is symmetric with respect to the action in the sense that that
\tau
restricts to an orientation-reversing action
R
exchanging
\alpha
curves with
\beta
curves and fixing the basepoint. This means that
\tau
exchanges the two handlebodies in the decomposition
Y=U_{\alpha}\cup_{\Sigma}U_{\beta}
determined by the Heegaard diagram. They call this data
(H,R)
a real Heegaard diagram for
(Y,\tau)
. Under these circumstances the induced map, also called
R
, on
\mathit{HF}(\Sym^{g}(\Sigma),\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}})
is anti-symplectic on the symmetric product and exchanges the Lagrangians. One may therefore take

\widehat{\mathit{HFR}}(Y,\tau):=HF(\Sym^{g}(\Sigma-\{w\}),\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\Sym^{g}(\Sigma-\{w\})^{R})

and similarly for other variants. The construction splits along
\spin^{c}
structures and further splits over real
\spin^{c}
structures
\mathfrak{s}_{r}
, which we discuss in Section
2
.

As detailed above, the case of real Lagrangian and Heegaard Floer theory deals with an anti-symplectic involution exchanging the Lagrangians. Let us contrast it to the situation of a symplectic involution
S
on
(M,\omega)
which fixes each of a pair of Lagrangians
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
setwise, which has been extensively studied in Lagrangian and Heegaard Floer homology. In this situation the fixed sets
(M^{S},L_{0}^{S},L_{1}^{S})
, if connected, are a new triple consisting of a symplectic manifold with Lagrangians, such that one may consider
\mathit{HF}(M^{S},L_{0}^{S},L_{1}^{S})
. A major technical tool in this situation is the localization theorem of Seidel-Smith
[
38
]
and Large
[
22
]
, which states that under nontrivial K-theoretic hypotheses, detailed in Section
3
, one has a spectral sequence

\mathit{HF}(M,L_{0},L_{1})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]\rightrightarrows\mathit{HF}(M^{S},L_{0}^{S},L_{1}^{S})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}].

Here and throughout,
\mathbb{F}
is the field of two elements and
\theta
has degree one, so that
H^{*}(B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z};\mathbb{F})\simeq\mathbb{F}[\theta]
. It follows that there is a dimension inequality between the Floer homology of
(M,L_{0},L_{1})
and
(M^{S},L_{0}^{S},L_{1}^{S})
; this is the analog of the classical theorem of P. A. Smith for topological actions
[
39
]
, as reformulated by Borel
[
2
]
. This theorem has been applied broadly to Heegaard Floer theory via the following basic strategy: for some pair
(Y,L)
consisting of a 3-manifold
Y
with a possibly empty link
L
and an orientaton-preserving action
\tau
on
(Y,L)
, one constructs a Heegaard diagram for
(Y,L)
such that the restriction of
\tau
to the Heegaard surface
\Sigma
is orientation-preserving and
\tau
exchanges
\alpha
curves with
\alpha
curves and
\beta
curves with
\beta
curves, so that
\tau
preserves each of
U_{\alpha}
and
U_{\beta}
. One obtains the symplectic situation described above; that is, the induced map on
(\Sym^{g}(S),\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}})
is symplectic on
\Sym^{g}(S)
and preserves each of
\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}}
and
\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}}
setwise. The first application of this, originally proved by the author
[
15
]
and refined by the author with Lipshitz and Sarkar
[
12
]
, was a spectral sequence

(1.1)

\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(\Sigma(K),\widetilde{K},\mathfrak{s}_{0})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]\rightrightarrows\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(S^{3},K).

Here
\Sigma(K)
is the branched double cover of a knot
K
in
S^{3}
, the knot
\widetilde{K}
is the lift of
K
in the branched double cover,
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
is the unique spinnable
\spin^{c}
structure on the branched double cover, and
\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(Y,K)
is the knot version of Heegaard Floer homology for a nullhomologous knot in a 3-manifold
Y
, due to Ozsváth-Szabó
[
29
,
32
]
and J. Rasmussen
[
35
]
. Similarly, the author, Lipshitz, and Lidman
[
11
]
showed that if
L\subseteq Y
is an
\ell
-component
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-nullhomologous link in
Y
a three-manifold,
\Sigma(Y,L)\xrightarrow{\pi}Y
is a branched cover of
(Y,L)
, and
\mathfrak{s}
is a
\spin^{c}
structure on
\Sigma(Y,L)
which is the pullback
\pi^{*}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
of some
\spin^{c}
structure
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
on
Y
, there is a similarly-constructed spectral sequence

(1.2)

\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(\Sigma(Y,L),\mathfrak{s})\otimes H^{*}((S^{1})^{\ell-1})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]\rightrightarrows\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}\in\Spin^{c}(Y)\\
\pi^{*}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{s}\end{subarray}}\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y,\mathfrak{s}^{\prime})\otimes H^{*}((S^{1})^{\ell-1})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}].

Here the sum on the right-hand side is taken over all
\spin^{c}
structures on
Y
whose pullback to
\Sigma(Y,L)
is
\mathfrak{s}
. In particular, this implies that if
\Sigma(Y,L)
is a Heegaard Floer
L
-space, which is to say a rational homology sphere with
\dim(\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y,\mathfrak{s}))=1
in each
\spin^{c}
structure
\mathfrak{s}
, then so is
Y

[
11
, Corollary 1.2]
.

In Remark 6.6 of their paper
[
10
]
, Guth and Manolescu conjecture that there is a localization spectral sequence for the hat variant of their real Heegaard Floer homology. To wit, if
\tau
is the covering involution on
\Sigma(K)
, they expect that for each
\spin^{c}
structure
\mathfrak{s}\in\Spin^{c}(\Sigma(K))
there is a spectral sequence

\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(\Sigma(K),\mathfrak{s})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]\rightrightarrows\widehat{HFR}(\Sigma(K),\mathfrak{s},\tau)\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}].

For branched double covers of knots, every
\spin^{c}
structure
\mathfrak{s}
has a unique associated real
\spin^{c}
structure, so the right-hand side could equivalently be phrased with
\mathfrak{s}_{r}
in place of
\mathfrak{s}
. In this note, we supply a proof of their conjecture, via showing the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,L_{0},L_{1})
be a triple such that
M
is an exact symplectic manifold which has the structure of a symplectization near infinity, and
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
are compact exact Lagrangians. Suppose that
R
is an anti-symplectic involution on
M
which interchanges
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
. There is a spectral sequence whose
E_{1}
page is

HF(M,L_{0},L_{1})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]

and whose
E_{\infty}
page is isomorphic to

\mathit{HFR}(M,L_{0},L_{1})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}].

The strategy of the proof is to replace the anti-symplectic involution by a symplectic involution to which the localization theorem of Seidel-Smith and Large may be applied. Specifically, we replace
(M,\omega)
with
(M\times M^{-},\omega\oplus-\omega)
. Allowing
R
to interchange the factors of the product gives a symplectic involution on
M\times M^{-}
. For our two Lagrangians we consider the product
L_{0}\times L_{1}
and the diagonal
\Delta
, each of which is preserved setwise by
S
. Standard Lagrangian Floer theory shows that

\mathit{HF}(M,L_{0},L_{1})\simeq\mathit{HF}(M\times M^{-},L_{0}\times L_{1},\Delta).

The fixed sets
(M^{S},\Delta^{S},(L_{0}\times L_{1})^{S})
are embedded copies of
(M,L_{0},M^{R})
, so that

\mathit{HF}(M^{S},\Delta^{S},(L_{0}\times L_{1})^{S})\simeq\mathit{HFR}(M,L_{0},L_{1}).

After checking the technical hypotheses of Large’s version of the localization theorem the result follows. The details are carried out in Section
4
.

This theorem then has the following special case in Heegaard Floer theory. In the statement that follows, the map
\tau_{*}
is the action on
\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y)
associated to the diffeomorphism
\tau
on Heegaard Floer homology as studied by Juhász-Thurston-Zemke
[
19
]
. Recall that if
Y
is a rational homology sphere, this map is well-defined; if
Y
is not, it is necessary to fix a basepoint
w
, which we always choose to be on the fixed set of
\tau
. We include
w
in the notation where its presence is relevant. The map
\iota
is the conjugation involution on Heegaard Floer homology studied by the author and Manolescu
[
14
]
. The constructions of these maps are reviewed in Section
2.3
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
Y
be a 3-manifold admitting an involution
\tau
whose fixed set has codimension two together with
w
a basepoint on the fixed set, and let
\mathfrak{s}\in\Spin^{c}(Y)
be a
spin^{c}
structure on
Y
. There is a spectral sequence whose
E_{1}
page is isomorphic to

\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y,w,\mathfrak{s})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]

and whose
E_{\infty}
page is isomorphic to

\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{s}_{r}\in\mathfrak{s}}\widehat{\mathit{HFR}}(Y,\mathfrak{s}_{r},\tau)\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}].

Here the sum
\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{s}_{r}\in\mathfrak{s}}
is taken over the set of real
\spin^{c}
structures associated to
\mathfrak{s}
. Furthermore if
(Y,\tau)
admits a real Heegaard diagram
H=(\Sigma,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},w)
with the property that there is an
R
-symmetric family of almost complex structures
J
on
\Sigma
such that
\Sym^{g}(J)
achieves transversality for
(\Sym^{g}(\Sigma-\{w\}),\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}})
, then the
d_{1}
differential of this spectral sequence is

(1+\iota_{*}\tau_{*})\theta.

In particular, if
\Sigma(K)
is the branched double cover of a knot
K
in the three-sphere, let
\mathfrak{s}\in\Spin^{c}(\Sigma(K))
be a
\spin^{c}
structure on
\Sigma(K)
and
\mathfrak{s}_{r}
be its unique associated real
\spin^{c}
structure. There is a spectral sequence whose
E_{1}
page is isomorphic to

\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(\Sigma(K),\mathfrak{s})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]

and whose
E_{\infty}
page is isomorphic to

\widehat{\mathit{HFR}}(\Sigma(K),\mathfrak{s}_{r},\tau)\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}].

Let us remark on the condition associated to the computation of the
d_{1}
differential. Guth and Manolescu show that not every real Heegaard diagram admits an
R
-symmetric family of almost complex structures achieving transversality
[
10
, Remark 2.4, Example 4.7]
. However, this does not rule out the possibility that every three-manifold possesses some real Heegaard diagram satisfying the condition above; for example, in the analogous case of symplectic actions, the arguments of
[
12
, Section 5.2]
show this is satisfied by equivariant diagrams which are nice in the sense of Sarkar and Wang
[
37
]
. We expect that in order to show the computation of the
d_{1}
differential above holds for all three-manifolds, it suffices to first show that the Sarkar-Wang algorithm can be run symmetrically on a real Heegaard diagram, and then to use the resulting real nice diagrams to carry out an index computation of a similar flavor to
[
12
, Section 5.2]
.

We now discuss how the spectral sequence for the branched double cover of Theorem
1.2
differs from (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). If
Y
is a real 3-manifold, the induced isomorphism
\tau_{*}
sends
\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y,w,\mathfrak{s})\rightarrow\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(Y,w,\overline{\mathfrak{s}})
, where
\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
is the conjugate
\spin^{c}
structure to
\mathfrak{s}
. In the spectral sequence (
1.2
), the
d_{1}
differential is
(1+\tau_{*})\theta
, and similarly for (
1.1
) with the action induced by
\tau
on the knot Floer homology. Therefore the Heegaard Floer homology in
\spin^{c}
structures which are not conjugation invariant cannot survive to the
E_{2}
page of either spectral sequence, and we obtain interesting spectral sequences from only the conjugation-invariant
\spin^{c}
structures. For branched double covers of knots, there is only one conjugation-invariant
\spin^{c}
structure, namely
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
, resulting in the form of the spectral sequence (
1.1
) seen.

This cancellation does not occur in the spectral sequence of Theorem
1.2
, where the
d_{1}
differential fixes the
\spin^{c}
structure. Indeed, this spectral sequence splits along
\spin^{c}
structures and is interesting for any
\spin^{c}
structure
\mathfrak{s}
. This tallies with the computation of the
d_{1}
differential: the map
\iota
also conjugates the
\spin^{c}
structure, so that in total
\iota_{*}\tau_{*}
preserves
\spin^{c}
structures.

As Guth and Manolescu point out, Theorem
1.2
has the following corollary for branched double covers which are Heegaard Floer
L
-spaces. Recall that by taking the Euler characteristic of real Heegaard Floer homology in the branched double cover, they define a new “Miyazawa-type” knot invariant
\chi_{\mathfrak{s}}(K)=\chi(\widehat{\mathit{HFR}}(\Sigma(K),\mathfrak{s},\tau)).

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma(K)
be an
L
-space. Then for every
\spin^{c}
structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
\Sigma(K)
,

\dim\left(\widehat{\mathit{HFR}}(\Sigma(K),\mathfrak{s},\tau)\right)=1.

It follows that
\chi_{\mathfrak{s}}(K)=\pm 1
for all
\mathfrak{s}\in\Spin^{c}(\Sigma(K))
.

The initial constructions of
[
10
]
are solely for involutions
\tau
on
3
-manifolds
Y
. However, Theorem
1.1
also has applications to the case of an action
\tau
on
(Y,K)
where
K
is a nullhomologous knot in
Y
. As previously, one requires that the fixed set of
\tau
on
Y
have codimension two. We will consider the case of a knot in the three-sphere which is
strongly invertible
; that is, there is an involution on
(S^{3},K)
which is orientation-preserving on
S^{3}
and orientation-reversing on
K
, so that the fixed set of the involution is an unknotted axis intersecting
K
in two points. The knot therefore divides the axis into two halves. A choice of half-axis and an orientation for this half-axis is a
direction

[
36
, Section 1]
. After making this choice we obtain a directed strongly invertible knot.

Recall that the knot Floer homology of a nullhomologous knot
K
in a three manifold
Y
is constructed from a Heegaard diagram
H=(\Sigma,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},w,z)
such that
(\Sigma,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},w)
is a Heegaard diagram for
Y
and
z
is placed such that connecting
w
to
z
in
U_{\alpha}
and
z
to
w
in
U_{\beta}
recovers the knot
K
. The hat variant of the knot Floer homology is then
\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(Y,K)=HF(\Sym^{g}(\Sigma-\{z,w\}),\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}})
. The result splits along an integral Alexander grading, or equivalently relative
\spin^{c}
structure, so that
\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(S^{3},K)\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(S^{3},K,i)
.

Following the same recipe as above, suppose one has a Heegaard diagram
H=(\Sigma,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},w,z)
for a strongly invertible knot
K
which is symmetric in the sense that
\tau|_{\Sigma}=R
is orientation-reversing, fixes
z
and
w
, and exchanges
\alpha
curves with
\beta
curves. If
K
is directed we additionally insist that
z
be the starting point of the oriented half-axis and
w
be the endpoint. One can then define an associated real knot Floer homology, to wit

\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H):=\mathit{HFR}(\Sym^{g}(\Sigma-\{w,z\}),\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}}).

The result splits as
\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H)\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H,i)
along Alexander gradings; we do not attempt to consider real Alexander gradings in the present work. One expects the arguments of
[
10
, Section 3]
may be adapted to show that this theory is an invariant of the directed strongly invertible knot, although given the limited focus of this note we do not attempt a full argument here. However, in Section
5.2
we construct doubly-pointed real Heegaard diagrams for directed strongly invertible knots
K
, and show the existence of the following spectral sequence.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
K\subseteq S^{3}
be a directed strongly invertible knot. There is a spectral sequence whose
E_{1}
page is

\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(S^{3},K,w,z)\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]

and whose
E_{\infty}
page is isomorphic to

\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H)\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]

where
H
is any real doubly-pointed Heegaard diagram for the directed strongly invertible knot. Moreover if the directed strongly invertible knot admits a real Heegaard diagram
H=(\Sigma,\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}},w,z)
with the property that there is an
R
-symmetric family of almost complex structures
J
on
\Sigma
such that
\Sym^{g}(J)
achieves transversality for
(\Sym^{g}(\Sigma-\{w,z\}),\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}})
, then the
d_{1}
differential of this spectral sequence is

(1+(\iota_{K}\tau_{K})_{*})\theta.

Analogously to the previous statement,
\iota_{K}
denotes the involutive knot Floer conjugation map of the author and Manolescu
[
14
]
, and
\tau_{K}
denotes the action of the knot symmetry as constructed by Mallick
[
23
]
and Dai, Mallick, and Stoffregen
[
7
]
on the knot Floer homology; we review these maps in Section
2.3
. The role of the direction in the statement is as follows. Each of the maps
\iota_{K}
and
\tau_{K}
is well-defined up to chain homotopy equivalence, but they are not simultaneously well-defined up to chain homotopy equivalence without a choice of direction
[
7
, Section 3.5]
. In general, reversing the direction corresponds to postcomposing (“twisting”) one of
\tau_{K}
or
\iota_{K}
by the Sarkar involution; the two possible postcompositions produce chain homotopy equivalent triples
[
7
, Lemma 2.21]
.

We observe some examples of the spectral sequence of Proposition
1.4
for simple cases.

Example 1.5
.

Recall that an
L
-space knot is a knot which admits a positive surgery to a Heegaard Floer
L
-space. If
K
is an
L
-space knot, then
\dim(\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(K,i))\leq 1
for all
i

[
31
]
. Therefore if
K
is a strongly invertible
L
-space knot, such as for example a torus knot with its unique strong inversion, the spectral sequence of Proposition
1.4
collapses on the
E_{1}
page. Indeed, if
H
is a real Heegaard diagram for
K
, we conclude that in each Alexander grading
\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H,i)\simeq\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(S^{3},K,i)
, ignoring the homological grading, for any real Heegaard diagram
H
for
(S^{3},K)
.

Example 1.6
.

Recall that a thin knot
K
is one for which the difference between the Alexander grading and homological grading of
\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(S^{3},K)
is a constant; in particular, such that in each Alexander grading the knot Floer homology lies in a single homological grading. The set of thin knots includes the alternating knots. Since the spectral sequence of Proposition
1.4
splits along Alexander gradings, it follows that for a strongly invertible thin knot, the only potentially nontrivial differential in the spectral sequence of Proposition
1.4
is
d_{1}
. The spectral sequence therefore collapses on the
E_{2}
page.

At the close of Section
5.2
we additionally consider some more speculative computations which assume that it is possible to realize the computation of the
d_{1}
differential of Proposition
1.4
.

Remark 1.7
.

The construction of the spectral sequence of Proposition
1.4
uses Heegaard diagrams adapted from
transvergent
diagrams for strongly invertible knots; in the language introduced by Boyle and Issa
[
4
,
3
]
, a transvergent diagram is one in which the symmetry axis lies in the plane of the page, as in Figure
5.1
. Recently, following the general strategy for Heegaard diagrams with orientation-preserving actions discussed above, Parikh
[
33
]
constructed a spectral sequence for strongly invertible knots using Heegaard diagrams adapted from
intravergent
diagrams, which is to say diagrams in which the symmetry axis is perpendicular to the page, of the form:

\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(S^{3},K)\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]\rightrightarrows\widehat{\mathit{HF}}(S^{3})\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}]\simeq\mathbb{F}[\theta,\theta^{-1}].

Similarly to the case of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), the differential on the
E_{1}
page of Parikh’s spectral sequence is
(1+(\tau_{K})_{*})\theta
; in particular, it reverses the sign of the Alexander grading, hence immediately cancelling
\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(S^{3},K,i)
and
\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(S^{3},K,-i)
for
i\neq 0
. Parikh’s spectral sequence can therefore be taken to start at
\mathit{HFK}(S^{3},K,0)
. This is not true of the spectral sequence of Proposition
1.4
, the differentials of which fix the Alexander grading.

Separately from the constructions of this paper, one may ask whether it is possible to define a localization spectral sequence from Heegaard diagrams adapted from transvergent diagrams which agrees with Parikh’s as far as the
E_{1}
page; that is, whose
d_{1}
differential is
(1+(\tau_{K})_{*})\theta
. The reader should compare this to the analogous situation in the setting of Khovanov homology: it was recently shown by Chen and Yang
[
6
]
that in the Khovanov setting the actions arising from transvergent and intravergent diagrams agree up to chain homotopy, leaving open the question of whether the spectral sequences induced by these actions agree. In the setting of Heegaard Floer homology, Juhász-Thurston-Zemke naturality
[
19
]
as applied by Mallick
[
23
, Section 6]
implies the actions induced on
\widehat{HFK}
by
\tau
are the same for any Heegaard diagram, but this does not itself imply the remaining pages of localization spectral sequences defined from these two constructions of the action must agree.

Remark 1.8
.

We expect that the spectral sequences of Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.4
may be shown to be invariants of the three-manifold and its involution, or of the directed strongly invertible knot, as appropriate, by combining the techniques of
[
10
]
with the arguments used in
[
12
, Section 5]
to show invariance of other localization spectral sequences in Heegaard Floer theory.

Organization

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review Guth and Manolescu’s construction of real Lagrangian Floer homology and real Heegaard Floer homology. We also review the construction of the involutive symmetry and actions induced by diffeomorphism in Heegaard Floer theory. In Section

5
we discuss applications of Theorem
1.1
to Heegaard Floer theory, and in particular prove Theorems

2.
Review of real Lagrangian Floer and Heegaard Floer homology

In this section we briefly review some features of Guth and Manolescu’s real Lagrangian Floer and Heegaard Floer theories, focusing on those necessary to this note. In the final subsection we additionally review the construction of certain maps in ordinary Heegaard Floer theory, which will helpful to computing the

2.1.
Real Lagrangian Floer theory

Here we briefly collect the hypotheses and definitions of the version of real Lagrangian Floer theory required for this note, most of which were already mentioned in the introduction. We assume the reader is familiar with standard Lagrangian Floer homology as in
[
8

(M,\omega)

Guth and Manolescu equivalently construct this theory as a count of disks invariant under the action of

be the space of time-dependent families of almost complex structures which are compatible with

We call such families of almost complex structures

is nonempty and contractible; as Guth and Manolescu point out, this follows from (for example) the proof of
[

Remark 2.2
.

In
[
10
]
the authors also define their theory for monotone symplectic manifolds. As this note is focused on localization results which are only known in the exact case, we do not include this part of their definition here.

2.2.1.
Real Euler and
\spin^{c}
structures

We now give an abbreviated review of real
\spin^{c}
structures, focusing on those aspects necessary to our proofs. A full discussion appears in
[
10
, Section 3.5]
.

Following Turaev’s interpretation of
\spin^{c}
structures on three manifolds
[
41
]
, we recall that an
Euler structure
is a choice of non-vanishing vector field
v
on
Y
, up to the following equivalence relation: two nonvanishing vector fields
v_{0}
and
v_{1}
are homologous if
v_{0}
is homotopic to
v_{1}
outside of some ball

. The set
\mathrm{Vec}(Y)
of Euler structures is an affine copy of

, and in bijection with the set of
\spin^{c}
structures
\Spin^{c}(Y)
. One obtains
\overline{\mathfrak{s}}
the
conjugate
\spin^{c}
structure
to
\mathfrak{s}
by replacing
v
with
-v
.

Given a Heegaard diagram
H
for
Y
, Ozsváth and Szabó
[
30
, Section 2.6]
define a map

\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}}\pitchfork\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}}\rightarrow\Spin^{c}(Y)

partitioning intersection points into
\spin^{c}
structures as follows. One may choose a self-indexing Morse
f
function associated to
H
, with a single index zero and index three critical point, and
g
critical points of index one and two, so the surface
\Sigma
is the level set
f^{-1}(3/2)
, the
\alpha
curves are the intersections of the ascending manifolds of index one critical points with
\Sigma
, and the
\beta
curves are the intersections of the descending manifolds of index two critical points with
\Sigma
. Given this setup, every intersection point
{\bf x}
determines a
g
-tuple of flowlines from index two to index one critical points, which we consider along with a flowline from the index three critical point to the index zero critical point passing through
w
. On the complement of a neighborhood of these trajectories the gradient vector field
df
of
f
is nonvanishing; on a neighborhood of these trajectories it can be homotoped to be nonvanishing. Hence it determines an Euler structure, and therefore a
\spin^{c}
structure
\mathfrak{s}_{w}({\bf x})
. Moreover, Ozsváth and Szabó show
[
30
, Section 2.4]
that
\Spin^{c}(Y)
is in bijection with the path space
P(\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}})
in
\Sym^{g}(\Sigma-\{w\})
, and two intersection points
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are in the same
\spin^{c}
structure if their corresponding constant paths lie in the same path component of the path space
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}},\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}})
.

Let us now review the analog of this story in the real case. Given a real three-manifold
(Y,\tau)
, Guth and Manolescu show that the fixed intersection points in
(\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}}\pitchfork\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}})^{R}
may be further partitioned into
real
\spin^{c}
structures
. For our purposes, it suffices to note that these also have a description in terms of vector fields on
Y
up to an appropriate equivalence relation, as follows. A
real vector field
on
Y
is a nonvanishing vector field
v
such that
\tau_{*}v=-v
. An Euler structure on
Y

admits a real structure
if it can be represented by a real vector field. Two real vector fields
v_{0}
and
v_{1}
are real homologous under the following conditions. Firstly,
v_{0}
and
v_{1}
must be homologous as vector fields. Secondly, if
TY
is given a
\tau
-invariant metric and we consider the complex linear bundles with complex anti-linear involutions
(\langle v_{0}\rangle^{\perp},\tau_{*})
and
(\langle v_{1}\rangle^{\perp},\tau_{*})
, these are equivalent as real complex line bundles, that is, there is an isomorphism between them which commutes with
\tau_{*}
. The set
\mathrm{RVec}(Y,\tau)
of real Euler structures is then the set of real vector fields up to the equivalence relation of being real homologous, and is in bijection with the set of real
\spin^{c}
structures on
\mathrm{R}\Spin^{c}(Y,\tau)
, which we will not need to otherwise define. Notice that this implies that a real
\spin^{c}
structure always has an ordinary underlying
\spin^{c}
structure, and any given ordinary
\spin^{c}
structure has some number of associated real
\spin^{c}
structures, including the possibility of zero.

We now consider how invariant intersection points determine real Euler structures. Given a real Heegaard diagram, the associated self-indexing Morse function
f\colon Y\rightarrow[0,3]
may be chosen such that
f\circ\tau=3-f
, implying that
\tau_{*}(df)=-df
. In this case the index one and index two critical points are interchanged by
\tau
, and given an invariant intersection point
{\bf x}
, the collection of flowlines from index one to index two critical points defined by the elements of
{\bf x}
is also invariant, with a negation. It follows that
{\bf x}
specifies a real Euler structure, and thus a real
\spin^{c}
structure, denoted
\mathfrak{s}^{R}_{w}({\bf x})
.

We make the following observation regarding the action of
R
on the generators associated to a real Heegaard diagram
(H,R)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(H,R)
be a real Heegaard diagram for a real 3-manifold
(Y,\tau)
. Then if
{\bf x}
and
R({\bf x})
are both regarded as generators in
\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(H)
, we have
\mathfrak{s}_{w}(R({\bf x}))=\mathfrak{s}_{w}({\bf x})
.

Proof.

Consider a real Heegaard diagram
(H,R)
for
(Y,\tau)
. If
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\alpha}}\pitchfork\mathbb{T}_{\bm{\beta}}
, so that
{\bf x}
is a generator in
\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(H)
, then the construction of
f
implies that
\tau_{*}(df)=-df
. Therefore if we regard
R({\bf x})
as a generator of
\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(R(H))
, we have that
\mathfrak{s}_{w}((R({\bf x})))=\overline{\mathfrak{s}_{w}(x)}
. However, if instead we regard
R({\bf x})
as a generator of
\widehat{\mathit{CF}}(H)
, then given a set of flowlines for

specified by
{\bf x}
, applying

and then reversing the sign produces a set of flowlines for
f
specified by
R({\bf x})
. We conclude that, if both intersection points
{\bf x}
and

are regarded as lying in

\mathfrak{s}_{w}(R({\bf x}))=\mathfrak{s}_{w}({\bf x})

Finally, we briefly discuss the case of knots. Recall that if
H=(\Sigma,\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},w,z)
is a Heegaard diagram for a knot

in the three-sphere, then there is an assignment of an Alexander grading

to each intersection point

Lemma 2.7

If
(H,R)
is a real Heegaard diagram for a strongly invertible knot

We first note that since

\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(\mathcal{H})\xrightarrow{\eta_{\rho}}\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(\rho\mathcal{H})\xrightarrow{\Phi_{\rho\mathcal{H},\tau\mathcal{H}^{r}}}\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(\tau\mathcal{H}^{r})\xrightarrow{\eta_{\tau}}\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(\mathcal{H}^{r})\xrightarrow{sw}\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(\mathcal{H}).

Here
\eta_{\tau}
is the chain isomorphism coming from applying
\tau
to
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r})
, bearing in mind that
\tau
is topologically an involution. The resulting composition
\tau_{K}
has
\tau_{K}^{2}
chain homotopic to the identity
[
23
, Section 6]
.

We observe that in the special case that there is a family of
R
-symmetric almost complex structures achieving transversality, we have that
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r})
is precisely
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
. Analogously to the 3-manifold case, we note the following property of the composition of these maps
\iota_{K}
and
\tau_{K}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
H
be a real Heegaard diagram for a strongly invertible knot
K
admitting a compatible family of almost complex structures
J
such that
J
is
R
-symmetric and
\Sym^{g}(J)
achieves transversality. Then the composition
\iota_{K}\circ\tau_{K}
is chain homotopic to
\eta_{\tau}\circ\eta_{K}\circ sw
.

Proof.

We may manipulate the composition as follows.

\displaystyle\iota_{K}\circ\tau_{K}

\displaystyle\simeq\iota_{K}\circ\tau_{K}^{-1}

\displaystyle\simeq\eta_{K}\circ\Phi_{\rho(\mathcal{H}),\overline{\mathcal{H}}}\circ\eta_{\rho}\circ\eta_{\rho}^{-1}\circ\Phi_{\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r}),\rho(\mathcal{H})}\circ\eta_{\tau}\circ sw

\displaystyle\simeq\eta_{K}\circ\Phi_{\rho(\mathcal{H}),\overline{\mathcal{H}}}\circ\Phi_{\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r}),\rho(\mathcal{H})}\circ\eta_{\tau}\circ sw

\displaystyle\simeq\eta_{K}\circ\eta_{\tau}\circ sw.

The above rearrangement uses the following: firstly,
\eta_{\tau}
and
sw
are their own inverses. Secondly, up to homotopy the inverse of
\Phi_{\rho(\mathcal{H}),\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r})}
is the naturality map
\Phi_{\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r}),\rho(\mathcal{H})}
. Finally, as noted above,
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r})
is
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
. Note that it is also the case that
sw
commutes with both
\eta_{\tau}
and
\eta_{K}
and in particular can be placed at any location in the composition above.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

The expert reader may be aware that it is more common to define
\tau_{K}
using Heegaard data for which the basepoints are interchanged rather than fixed, as in
[

]
and the initial definitions of
[

]
. We briefly discuss the correspondence between these pictures, which also helps to explain the role of the direction in our discussion. Given a strongly invertible knot

, the fixed axis divides

into two subarcs. Given a decoration on

, Dai-Mallick-Stoffregen place basepoints

and

on

such that they determine an orientation on

in which the subarc containing

has orientation matching that of the oriented half-axis

. With this choice, they show that
(\mathit{CFK}^{-}(S^{3},K),\tau_{K},\iota_{K})
is determined up to chain homotopy equivalence. In this work, we must work with fixed basepoints; we place
z
at the starting point of the oriented axis and
w
at the endpoint, so that pushing

and

by a quarter-circle in the direction of the orientation produces a

and

compatible with the direction in the sense of Dai-Mallick-Stoffregen. Mallick
[

shows that the map associated to this quarter Dehn twist interchanges

defined by the formula above with
\tau_{K}
as defined for the nonfixed basepoints
z^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}
, up to homotopy. The same map also interchanges
\iota_{K}
defined for on
\mathit{CFK}(S^{3},K,z,w)
and
\mathit{CFK}(S^{3},K,z^{\prime},w^{\prime})
up to homotopy

, so our choice of the placement of the

Finally, note that it is previously known that

is an involution up to homotopy, even though

Review of localization in Lagrangian Floer theory

In this section we recall Large’s generalization of Seidel and Smith’s localization theorem for Lagrangian Floer theory. We begin by reviewing the following definitions, adapted from

Definition 3.1

Let

and

. A

•

.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}=(E,F_{0},F_{1})
and
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}=(E^{\prime},F_{0}^{\prime},F_{1}^{\prime})

(M,L_{0},L_{1})

. A

and

.

Let
(M,\omega)
be an exact symplectic manifold which is convex at infinity, and let

and
L_{1}
be two exact Lagrangians such that for each

, either

is compact, or it is the case that

is a symplectization near infinity and
L_{i}
is conical. (A Lagrangian is conical if near infinity it is the cone on a Legendrian in a level set of the exhausting function on

be a symplectic involution on

3
3

3

More typically the involution is denoted

or
\iota

is used for the involutive conjugation involution,

is used for symplectic actions on symplectic manifolds, and

is used for anti-symplectic actions on symplectic manifolds.
Let
(M^{S},L_{0}^{S},L_{1}^{S})
denote the fixed sets with respect to

is connected or that all of the connected components of

have the same dimension, it is straightforward to see that

Definition 3.3
.

A

is a stable isomorphism of polarization data between the tangent polarization

We remind the reader that, since the symplectic group deformation retracts onto the unitary group, the theory of symplectic vector bundles is isomorphic to the theory of complex vector bundles. Therefore, the definitions above may be equivalently rephrased in terms of complex bundles with half-dimensional totally real subbundles.

We are now ready to state Large’s localization theorem, as follows.

.

]
Suppose that

This generalizes Seidel and Smith’s previous work in the case that the normal polarization is stably isomorphic to the trivial polarization

4.
Proof of localization for real Lagrangian Floer homology

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
. In accord with the hypotheses, let
(M,L_{0},L_{1})
be a triple such that
M
is exact and has the structure of a symplectization near infinity, and
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
are compact exact Lagrangians. As previously, suppose that there is an antisymplectic involution

on

and

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

As explained in the Introduction, our strategy is to replace the anti-symplectic involution
R
with a symplectic involution
S
on a product symplectic manifold. In particular, consider the symplectic manifold
M\times M^{-}
with symplectic form
\widetilde{\omega}=\omega\oplus-\omega
. This manifold contains Lagrangian submanifolds
\mathcal{L}=L_{0}\times L_{1}
and
\Delta=\{(x,x):x\in M\}
. We note that

is still exact, since if

then
\widetilde{\omega}=d\left(\lambda\oplus-\lambda\right)
. Similarly, both
\mathcal{L}
and
\Delta
are exact Lagrangians. We see that
\mathcal{L}
is compact; as for
\Delta
, one may check that if

has the structure of a symplectization near infinity then the same is true of

, and the diagonal is a conical Lagrangian; the details are carried out in
[
17

. It is additionally well-known that

\mathit{HF}(M\times M^{-},\mathcal{L},\Delta)\simeq HF(M,L_{0},L_{1})

via folding strips; c.f., e.g.,
[
9

or
[
17
, Lemma 2.9]
for a review.

We now consider an involution on this product, as follows.

\displaystyle S:M\times M^{-}\rightarrow M\times M^{-}

\displaystyle(x,y)\mapsto(R(y),R(x))

We first observe that
S^{*}(\widetilde{\omega})=R^{*}(-\omega)\oplus R^{*}\omega=\omega\oplus-\omega=\widetilde{\omega}
, so

is indeed symplectic; furthermore since

, we see that

is also an involution. Moreover with respect to

\displaystyle(M\times M^{-})^{S}

\displaystyle=\{(x,R(x)):x\in M\}

\displaystyle\Delta^{S}

\displaystyle=\{(x,x):x=R(x)\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{S}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y):x\in L_{0},y\in L_{1},y=R(x)\}.

We see that if we consider the symplectic embedding

\displaystyle\phi\colon(M,\omega)

\displaystyle\rightarrow(M\times M^{-},\widetilde{\omega})

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto(x,R(x))

we find that
\phi(M)=(M\times M^{-})^{S}
up to a factor of
1/2
on the symplectic form, and furthermore

and

. We conclude that

HF((M\times M^{-})^{S},\mathcal{L}^{S},\Delta^{S})\simeq HFR(M,L_{0},L_{1}).

It remains to check that

admits a stable tangent normal isomorphism, after which we may apply Theorem
3.4
to obtain the spectral sequence of Theorem
1.1
. We observe that the tangent bundle to to

inside

is

(4.1)

T_{(x,R(x))}((M\times M^{-})^{S})=\{(v,R_{*}v):v\in T_{x}M\}.

To parametrize the normal bundle, we choose an almost complex structure
J
on

which is compatible with
\omega

\omega(Jv,Jw)=\omega(v,w)
and

for all nonzero

. We further insist that

be

-symmetric in the sense that

, which as discussed in Section

is always possible to arrange. (Note that this almost complex structure need not achieve transversality, since it is being used purely to establish the vector bundle isomorphism of Theorem
3.4
.) We then have that

is an almost complex structure on

compatible with
\widetilde{\omega}
with the property that
JS_{*}=S_{*}J
. We may write the normal bundle to

in

in the form

(4.2)

N_{(x,R(x))}((M\times M^{-})^{S})=\{(Jv,JR_{*}v):v\in T_{x}M\}.

We pause to confirm that this is correct. It suffices to check that every vector in (
4.2
) is orthogonal to every vector in (
4.1
), since they are each clearly half-dimensional vector bundles. Recall that the metric on

is determined by the symplectic form and choice of compatible almost complex structure, and is given by

g_{\widetilde{J}}\langle(v_{1},v_{2}),(w_{1},w_{2})\rangle:=\widetilde{\omega}((v_{1},v_{2}),\widetilde{J}(w_{1},w_{2})).

We therefore see that given
v,w\in T_{x}M
, we have that

\displaystyle g_{\widetilde{J}}\langle(v,R_{*}v),(Jw,JR_{*}w)\rangle

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\omega}((v,R_{*}v),\widetilde{J}(Jw,JR_{*}w))

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\omega}((v,R_{*}v),(J^{2}w,-J^{2}R_{*}w))

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\omega}((v,R_{*}v),(-w,R_{*}w))

\displaystyle=\omega(v,-w)-\omega(R_{*}v,R_{*}w)

\displaystyle=-\omega(v,w)+\omega(v,w)

\displaystyle=0.

We now describe a stable tangent-normal isomorphism between the tangent and normal polarizations of
((M\times M^{-})^{S},\Delta^{S},\mathcal{L}^{S})
. Our starting map is

\displaystyle\psi:T((M\times M^{-})^{S})

\displaystyle\rightarrow N((M\times M^{-})^{S})

\displaystyle(v,R_{*}v)

\displaystyle\mapsto(Jv,JR_{*}v)

We begin by checking that this is indeed an isomorphism of complex vector bundles; in particular, we see that

\psi(\widetilde{J}(v,R_{*}v))=\psi((Jv,-JR_{*}v))=(J^{2}v,-J^{2}R_{*}v)=\widetilde{J}(Jv,JR_{*}v)=\widetilde{J}\psi(v,R_{*}v).

\mathcal{L}^{S}
inside of

\displaystyle T_{(x,x)}\Delta

\displaystyle T_{(x,x)}\Delta^{S}

\displaystyle=\{(v,v):R_{*}v=v\}

\displaystyle N_{(x,x)}\Delta^{S}

\displaystyle=\{(Jw,Jw):R_{*}w=w\}.

Proof of Proposition

Let

the action
S

it splits along Alexander gradings. An argument again nearly identical to the preceding case confirms that in the presence of a family of

-symmetric almost complex structures achieving transversality, the action induced by the map

\eta_{K}\circ\eta_{\tau}\circ sw

Example 5.1
.

We conclude with two speculative computations, both carried out under the conjectural assumption that the
d_{1}
differential of the spectral sequence of Proposition
1.4
is indeed
(1+(\iota_{K}\tau_{K})_{*})\theta
. We begin with the figure-eight knot, which is thin. The knot Floer homology is five dimensional. Using one typical set of names for the generators (see, e.g.
[
7
, Figure 5]
), we have a chain complex with trivial differential

\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(S^{3},4_{1},1)\simeq\mathbb{F}\langle b\rangle,\qquad\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(S^{3},4_{1},0)\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}\langle a,x,e\rangle,\qquad\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(S^{3},4_{1},-1)=\mathbb{F}\langle c\rangle.

The map
\iota_{K}
was computed for thin knots in
[
14
, Section 8]
. On
\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(S^{3},4_{1})
it is

\iota_{K}(b)=c\qquad\iota_{K}(c)=b\qquad\iota_{K}(a)=a+x\qquad\iota_{K}(x)=x+e\qquad\iota_{K}(e)=e.

The figure-eight knot has two inequivalent strong inversions, related by mirroring. The maps associated to them have been computed by Dai, Mallick, and Stoffregen
[
7
, Example 2.26]
and are

\tau_{K}(b)=c\qquad\tau_{K}(c)=b\qquad\tau_{K}(a)=a+x\qquad\tau_{K}(x)=x\qquad\tau_{K}(e)=e

and

\sigma_{K}(b)=c\qquad\sigma_{K}(c)=b\qquad\sigma_{K}(a)=a\qquad\sigma_{K}(x)=x+e\qquad\sigma_{K}(e)=e.

In this case the potential twist from changing the direction does not affect the
(\iota_{K},\tau_{K})
or
(\iota_{K},\sigma_{K})
chain homotopy equivalence class of the resulting complex, and therefore we may ignore it. We see that
\iota_{K}\tau_{K}
fixes
b,c,a+x
and interchanges
x
and
x+e
, whereas
\iota_{K}\sigma_{K}
fixes
b,c,x
and interchanges
a
and
a+x
. In particular we conclude that if
H
is a Heegaard diagram for the figure-eight knot with either strong inversion we have

\dim\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H,1)=\dim\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H,0)=\dim\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H,-1)=1.

Note that the author is not presently aware of an example of a thin knot for which, if one assumes that the
d_{1}
differential is
(1+(\iota_{K}\tau_{K})_{*})\theta
, it does not follow that
\dim\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H,0)=1
, although this may be an artifact of the fact there there exist relatively few computations of
\tau_{K}
in the literature.

We next consider the equivariant connect sum of the torus knot
K=T_{3,4}
with itself with a connected sum of the unique strong inversion on each summand. The equivariant connected sum is an operation on directed strongly invertible knots is due to Sakuma
[
36
]
; a nice picture appears in
[
7
, Definition 2.4]
. In particular, this operation depends on the direction, but our choice of directions does not impact the following algebra. The knot Floer homology of the original knot has a chain complex with trivial differential

\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(S^{3},K,3)\simeq\mathbb{F}\langle x_{31}\rangle,\qquad\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(S^{3},K,-3)\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}\langle x_{32}\rangle

\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(S^{3},K,2)\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}\langle x_{21}\rangle\qquad\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(S^{3},K,-2)\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}\langle x_{22}\rangle

\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(S^{3},4_{1},0)=\mathbb{F}\langle x_{0}\rangle.

The maps
\iota_{K}
and
\tau_{K}
are both the unique Alexander-grading reversing chain isomorphism, fixing
x_{0}
and exchanging
x_{j1}
with
x_{j2}
for
j=2,3
. We now consider the connected sum of the knot with itself. Ozsváth and Szabó show that knot Floer homology has a Künneth formula
[
29
, Section 7]
, and in particular
\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(K_{1}\#K_{1},i)\simeq\bigoplus_{i_{1}+i_{2}=i}\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(K_{1},i_{1})\otimes\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(K_{2},i_{2})
. Focusing on Alexander grading zero, which will be the most interesting, we see that

\widehat{\mathit{CFK}}(K\#K,0)\simeq\mathbb{F}\langle x_{0}x_{0},x_{21}x_{22},x_{22}x_{21},x_{31}x_{32},x_{32}x_{31}\rangle

Here concatenation denotes tensor product. The maps
\iota_{K}
and
\tau_{K}
do not have the same product formulas. Applying the tensor product formula for
\tau_{K}
of
[
7
, Theorem 1.7]
, we see that
\tau_{K}
is the map interchanging the two factors in the tensor product. Applying the product formula for
\iota_{K}
of
[
43
, Theorem 1.1]
, which requires the full knot Floer complex
CFK^{-}(T_{3,4})
to compute, we see that
\iota_{K}
is the map which sends
x_{22}x_{21}\mapsto x_{21}x_{22}+x_{31}x_{32}
and otherwise exchanges the factors in the tensor product. This implies that the composition
\iota_{K}\tau_{K}
is given by

x_{21}x_{22}\leftrightarrow x_{21}x_{22}+x_{31}x_{32}\qquad\qquad x_{0}x_{0},x_{22}x_{21},x_{32}x_{31}\text{ fixed.}

We conclude that
\dim(\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H,0))=3
. A similar computation shows that in all other Alexander gradings
\iota_{K}=\tau_{K}
and therefore
\dim(\widehat{\mathit{HFKR}}(H,i))=\dim(\widehat{\mathit{HFK}}(K\#K,i))
.

Remark 5.2
.

The enthusiastic reader is cautioned that not all knot symmetries should be expected to admit a notion of real knot Floer homology. Guth and Manolescu’s construction assumes an orientation-preserving symmetry on a 3-manifold
Y
whose fixed set is nonempty and has codimension two; this means that one should not expect to be able to deal with (for example) freely two-periodic or strongly amphichiral knots. More subtly, if
H
is a real Heegaard diagram for some symmetric knot
K
with
w
and
z
fixed by
R
, then
R(H)
is necessarily a Heegaard diagram for
K
with its orientation reversed: the orientation of the surface and the order of the alpha and beta curves changes, but the basepoints remain in place. This suggests there is no real doubly-pointed Heegaard diagram for the lift
\widetilde{K}
of a knot
K
in its branched double cover, where the action preserves the orientation of the knot, and in particular no analog of the spectral sequence (
1.1
) in real knot Floer homology. On the other hand, if one were to develop a notion of real Heegaard Floer knot homology for which the
z
and
w
basepoints may be away from the fixed set and interchanged by the action, it seems that it could be possible to study two-periodic knots; we leave this development for the future.

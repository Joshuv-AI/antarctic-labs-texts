A generalized definition of the isothermal compressibility in (2+1)-flavor QCD
D. A. Clarke, J. Goswami, F. Karsch, P. Petreczky

Abstract
We introduce a generalized definition of the isothermal compressibility ( κ T , σ Q 2 \kappa_{T,\sigma_{Q}^{2}} ) calculable by keeping net conserved charge fluctuations rather than total number densities constant. We present lattice QCD results for this isothermal compressibility, expressed in terms of fluctuations of conserved charges that are related to baryon ( B B ), electric charge ( Q Q ) and strangeness ( S S ) quantum numbers. This generalized isothermal compressibility is compared with hadron resonance gas model calculations as well as with heavy-ion collision data obtained at RHIC and the LHC. We find κ T , σ Q 2 = 13.8 ​ ( 1.3 ) \kappa_{T,\sigma_{Q}^{2}}=13.8(1.3) fm 3 /GeV at T pc , 0 = 156.5 ​ ( 1.5 ) T_{\rm pc,0}=156.5(1.5) MeV and μ ^ B = 0 \hat{\mu}_{B}=0 . This finding is consistent with the rescaled result of the ALICE Collaboration, where we replaced the number of charged hadrons ( N ch N_{\rm ch} ) by the total number of hadrons ( N tot N_{\rm tot} ) at freeze-out. Normalizing this result with the QCD pressure ( P P ) we find that the isothermal compressibility on the pseudo-critical line stays close to that of an ideal gas , i.e. P ​ κ T , σ Q 2 ≃ 1 P\kappa_{T,\sigma_{Q}^{2}}\simeq 1 .



Introduction —

A central goal of theoretical and experimental studies of strong-interaction
matter is to understand its different phases and the properties of matter in these
phases. Thermal properties of strong-interaction matter
Rajagopal and Wilczek 2000
;
Busza et al. 2018
, e.g. the equation of state, velocity of sound or its viscosity, are widely studied experimentally in heavy-ion collisions
(HIC) and theoretically in the framework of the theory of strong interactions, Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). The small shear and bulk viscosities, determined
in HIC and calculated in lattice QCD simulations
Meyer 2007
;
Dusling and Schäfer 2012
;
Altenkort et al. 2023
, led to the conclusion
that this matter is an “almost perfect fluid”
Heinz 2005
.
We will show here that in the vicinity of the pseudo-critical temperature, which corresponds
to the freeze-out temperature in HIC, the isothermal compressibility stays close to that of an ideal gas.

The current phenomenological
discussion
Mrowczynski 1998
;
Hartnack et al. 2006
;
Dexheimer et al. 2008

and experimental determination
Adare et al. 2008
;
Mukherjee et al. 2018
;
Acharya et al. 2021

of the isothermal compressibility (
\kappa_{T}
) of strong-interaction matter relies on
a statistical physics definition

Mrowczynski 1998
;
Landau and Lifshitz 1969
;
Huang 1987
, which is valid for systems in which particle
number can be considered as a well defined conserved quantity,

\kappa_{T}\;=\;-\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial P}\right)_{T,N}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial\mu^{2}}=\frac{1}{T^{4}\,\hat{n}^{2}}\,\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{P}}{\partial\hat{\mu}^{2}}\,,

(1)

with pressure
P
, spatial volume
V
, temperature
T
,

n=N/V
denoting the particle number density, and
\mu
being the
chemical potential coupling to the number operator.
Here and in the following we use the notation

\hat{O}\equiv OT^{-k}

with
k\in\mathbb{Z}
chosen so that
\hat{O}
is dimensionless. The application of such an approach to the analysis of properties
of strong-interaction matter,
created in HIC at the time of hadronization (freeze-out),
makes use of the fact that relativistic hadron resonance
gas (HRG) models provide a rather good description of the thermodynamics
of QCD in the vicinity of the freeze-out temperature.

The concept of well defined particle number densities, however, cannot be carried over
straightforwardly to a first-principle, QCD-based calculation of the
isothermal compressibility of matter, where the conserved charges are related to differences of particle and anti-particle numbers rather
than total particle numbers,
i.e.
net-baryon number (
N_{B}
), net-electric charge (
N_{Q}
)
and net-strangeness (
N_{S}
). In fact, defining
\kappa_{T}
by keeping net-number densities rather than total number
densities fixed amounts to replacing in Eq. (
1
) the total number
density
n
by a net-charge number density
n_{Q}
or
n_{B}
.
In the limit of vanishing chemical potential this clearly
leads to a divergent isothermal compressibility which easily is verified in
non-interacting HRG model calculations and explicitly shown in lattice QCD
calculations
Gupta et al. 2014
.

In this letter we will discuss a generalized version of the
isothermal compressibility which does not explicitly rely on the particle
content of a thermal medium but makes use of conserved charges and their
fluctuations that are accessible in QCD calculations as well
as measurements in HIC. We consider the isothermal
compressibility of strong-interaction matter at fixed value of the
ratio of net-electric charge to net-baryon number,
r_{Q}=N_{Q}/N_{B}
,
and fixed net-strangeness to net-baryon number ratio,
r_{S}=N_{S}/N_{B}
.
Furthermore, the partial derivative of volume with respect to pressure
is taken, keeping a function (
f_{Q}
) of the net-electric charge fixed,

\kappa_{{}_{\scriptstyle T,f_{Q},r_{Q},r_{S}}}\;=\;-\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial P}\right)_{T,f_{Q},r_{Q},r_{S}}\,.

(2)

In particular we consider the cases of fixed net-electric charge,

f_{Q}=V\partial P/\partial\mu_{Q}\equiv N_{Q}
, and fixed net-electric charge fluctuations,

f_{Q}=VT\partial^{2}P/\partial\mu_{Q}^{2}\equiv\sigma^{2}_{Q}
. The latter choice is motivated by
the observation that
total particle numbers in a non-interacting HRG are well approximated by net-charge fluctuations
and that HRG models provide a good description of lattice QCD calculations close
to the freeze-out temperature.

However, as pointed out, the former choice leads to a divergent compressibility at vanishing chemical potentials even in HRG model calculations.

Isothermal compressibility in HRG models —

We briefly recapitulate here the calculation of the isothermal compressibility
\kappa_{T,\vec{N}}
in non-interacting HRG
models, which is performed for a fixed number of particles and anti-particles (
\vec{N}
)
to circumvent the problem of divergences at
\mu=0
, and which is used also in experimental determinations
of
\kappa_{T,\vec{N}}
. Calculations of
\kappa_{T,\vec{N}}
in
the framework of HRG models take advantage of the
fact that in a grand canonical prescription of the HRG model the pressure and other thermodynamic observables are explicitly given in terms of sums of contributions from different particle species.
Although the grand canonical ensemble generally is defined as a function of
T
,
V
, and conserved‑charge chemical potentials

\vec{\mu}=(\mu_{B},\mu_{Q},\mu_{S})
,

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{T}{V}\,\ln\mathcal{Z}\!\bigl(T,V,\vec{\mu}\bigr)\;,

(3)

in a non-interacting HRG model, one may project onto different particle and anti-particle sectors by introducing
a chemical potential,
\mu_{i}
, for each particle species
i
, which we set to zero after taking appropriate derivatives,
i.e.

we introduce
\mu_{H,i}=B\mu_{B}+Q\mu_{Q}+S\mu_{S}+\mu_{i}
for each hadron with quantum numbers
(B,Q,S)
.
With this one obtains particle numbers and
fluctuations in each sector

;
Mukherjee et al. 2018

\displaystyle N_{i}

\displaystyle=\left.V\frac{\partial P}{\partial\mu_{i}}\right|_{\mu_{i}=0}=Vn_{i}=VT^{3}\hat{n}_{i}\;,

(4)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left.T\frac{\partial N_{i}}{\partial\mu_{i}}\right|_{\mu_{i}=0}=VT\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial\mu_{i}^{2}}=VT^{3}\hat{\chi}_{2}^{i}\;.

(5)

The inverse of the isothermal compressibility in a non-interacting HRG is then obtained as
Mukherjee et al. 2018

\displaystyle\kappa_{T,\vec{N}}^{-1}=-V\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial V}\right)_{T,\vec{N}}=T^{4}\hskip-8.53581pt\sum_{i\in{\rm hadrons}}\frac{\hat{n}_{i}}{\omega_{i}}\;,

(6)

where
\omega_{i}=\sigma_{i}^{2}/N_{i}
are the scaled variances.

In a non-interacting HRG, Boltzmann-statistics is a good approximation for all baryons. As a consequence,
for all baryons
\omega_{i}=1
for all
T
and
\vec{\mu}
. For mesons appreciable deviations
from unity only arise for pions and kaons. In all other cases
\omega_{i}\simeq 1
to better than 0.2%.

In order to make contact with experimental determinations of
\kappa_{T,\vec{N}}
, which is based on a measurement of charged particle yields and fluctuations, we separate in Eq. (
6
) contributions from charged and neutral
hadrons,

\displaystyle\kappa_{T,\vec{N}}^{-1}=T^{4}\hskip-8.53581pt\sum_{i\in{\rm charged}}\frac{\hat{n}_{i}}{\omega_{i}}+T^{4}\hskip-8.53581pt\sum_{i\in{\rm neutral}}\frac{\hat{n}_{i}}{\omega_{i}}.

(7)

As in HRG model calculations the scaled variances for different particle species vary little, introducing
a common scaled variance for all charged (
\omega_{\rm ch}
), neutral (
\omega_{\rm 0}
), or even all hadrons (
\omega_{\rm tot}
) provides a good approximation,

\displaystyle\kappa_{T,\vec{N}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\simeq T^{4}\frac{\hat{n}_{\rm ch}}{\omega_{\rm ch}}+T^{4}\frac{\hat{n}_{0}}{\omega_{0}}\simeq T\frac{n_{\rm tot}}{\omega_{\rm tot}}\;,

(8)

where we introduced

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{X}=\sum_{i\in X}\hat{n}_{i}\;\;,\;\;\omega_{X}=\frac{\sum\limits_{i\in X}\sigma^{2}_{i}}{\sum\limits_{{i}\in X}N_{i}}\;\;,\;\;X={\rm ch},0,{\rm tot}

(9)

for charged, neutral and total number of hadrons, respectively.

We note that the determination of
\kappa_{T,\vec{N}}
from HIC on particle
yields and fluctuations

Adare et al. 2008
;
Mukherjee et al. 2018
;
Acharya et al. 2021

only on the first term
in the middle expression
of Eq. (

neutral hadrons to the compressibility is neglected.
To the extent that the total number of hadrons in HRG model calculations as well as in heavy-ion collisions
is almost a factor of two larger than the number of charged hadrons

Generalized isothermal compressibility —

The HRG model calculation discussed above of course
has the disadvantage that it explicitly relies on
the introduction of point-like, non-interacting particles as fundamental (conserved charge) degrees of freedom.
That is not possible in QCD, and even in the HRG context, it
ignores that the truly conserved charges are
B,Q,S

and eventually also the quantum numbers of heavier quarks.
Rather than defining the isothermal compressibility
by using
\partial V/\partial P
taken at fixed
(T,\vec{N})

we thus introduce a generalized version of the
isothermal compressibility for strangeness-neutral (
r_{S}=0
) (2+1)-flavor QCD
with fixed
r_{Q}

\displaystyle\kappa_{{}_{\scriptstyle T,f_{Q},r_{Q},0}}T^{4}=-\frac{1}{f_{Q}\hat{n}_{B}}\left(\frac{\partial f_{Q}}{\partial\hat{\mu}_{B}}k_{B}+\frac{\partial f_{Q}}{\partial\hat{\mu}_{Q}}k_{Q}+\frac{\partial f_{Q}}{\partial\hat{\mu}_{S}}k_{S}\right)\;,

(11)

where we keep a function
f_{Q}
of the net-electric charge
Q

The partial derivatives on the right hand side are understood to be taken at fixed
T,V

keeping the remaining two chemical potentials fixed, and

\displaystyle k_{B}

\displaystyle=-(1+(2r_{Q}-1)A)/D\;,

(12)

\displaystyle=(\hat{\chi}_{11}^{BS}/\hat{\chi}_{2}^{S}-(2r_{Q}-1)A)/D\;,

(13)

\displaystyle=2(2r_{Q}-1)A/D\;,

(14)

with

\displaystyle A=\frac{\hat{\chi}_{2}^{S}\hat{\chi}_{11}^{BQ}-\hat{\chi}_{11}^{BS}\hat{\chi}_{11}^{QS}}{\hat{\chi}_{2}^{S}(\hat{\chi}_{11}^{BQ}-2\hat{\chi}_{2}^{Q}+\hat{\chi}_{11}^{QS})}\;.

Here the dimensionless generalized susceptibilities

Bollweg et al. 2021

\displaystyle T_{\rm pc}(\hat{\mu}_{B})=T_{\rm pc,0}\left(1-\kappa_{2}\hat{\mu}_{B}^{2}+{\cal O}(\hat{\mu}_{B}^{4})\right)\;.

from Ref.

As pointed out in Eq. (
10

T

to the inverse of the pressure. It thus is reasonable to express
\kappa_{T,\sigma_{Q}^{2}}

\displaystyle\left.P\kappa_{{}_{\scriptstyle T,\sigma^{2}}}\right|_{T_{pc}(\hat{\mu}_{B})}=0.80(12)+0.06(1)\hat{\mu}_{B}^{2}+{\cal O}(\hat{\mu}_{B}^{4})\;.

We thus find that the isothermal compressibility in QCD at the pseudo-critical temperature is close to

i.e

Summary —

We introduced and examined the properties of a generalized isothermal compressibility which is suitable for the
analysis of compressibility of matter in which the number of particles is not a conserved quantity. We calculated
the compressibility of strong-interaction matter keeping temperature and the fluctuations of net-electric charge
fixed, which in a non-interacting HRG is a good proxy for total charged particle numbers.
We found that this generalized compressibility varies little on the pseudo-critical line and stays close to the
value
\kappa_{T,\sigma_{Q}^{2}}=13.8(1.3)
fm
3
/GeV obtained at
(T_{\rm pc},\mu_{B})=(156.5,0)\ {\rm MeV}
. This finding
is consistent with the rescaled result of the ALICE Collaboration, where we replaced the number of charged hadrons
by the total number of hadrons at freeze-out. It suggests that the isothermal compressibility of strong-interaction matter
along the pseudo-critical transition line,
or at the time of hadronization in HIC, stays close to that of an ideal (Boltzmann) gas.

TOWANDA, a borough and the county-seat of Bradford county, Pennsylvania, U.S.A., on the west bank of the Susquehanna river, about 50 m. N.W. of Wilkes-Barré. Pop. (1890), 4169; (1900), 4663 (322 foreign-born), (1910) 4281. Towanda is served by the Lehigh Valley and the Susquehanna & New York railways. It is situated about 730 ft. above the sea, and
is surrounded by high hills. Towanda contains the museum of the Bradford County Historical Society. The borough is
in a farming, dairying and stock-raising region, and has various
manufactures. The first settlement was made by William Means
in 1786, the village was laid out in 1812, became the county seat
in the same year, was variously known for some years
as Meansville, Overton, Williamson, Monmouth and Towanda,
and in 1828 was incorporated as the borough of Towanda. Its name is an Indian word said to mean “ where we bury the dead.”

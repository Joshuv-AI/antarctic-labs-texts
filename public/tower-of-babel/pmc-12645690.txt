Introduction

Rhabdomyoma is the most prevalent fetal cardiac tumor and can be detected via routine prenatal ultrasound from the 15th gestational week onward [3]. The incidence of this tumor ranges from 0.02 to 0.17% in liveborn infants and 0.12% in prenatal fetal studies [5, 14]. Tuberous Sclerosis Complex (TSC), an autosomal dominant disorder characterized by cellular and tissue dysplasia in multiple organs, which can lead to neurologic and behavioral abnormalities, but the rhabdomyoma are frequently the first and most obvious clue for TSC, and then the other manifestations develop later [1, 6, 11]. The two known genes, TSC1 located at 9q34.3 and TSC2 located at 16p13.3, are crucial disease-causing genes for TSC. Loss of function of TSC1/TSC2 leads to hyperactivity of mTORC1, which drives abnormal cellular growth and tumorigenesis [2, 15].

Next-generation sequencing (NGS) offers deep, high-throughput, and parallel genome sequencing. With its expanding application in prenatal diagnosis, the diagnostic rate for fetuses with CRs has significantly improved, thereby enhancing the capacity for genetic counseling. In the present study, Trio-WES was conducted to identify the genetic causes in 12 fetuses with sonographically detected CRs, and TSC was subsequently diagnosed in all 12 cases. We integrated detailed parental phenotypes, echocardiography, and genetic analysis to conduct a comprehensive assessment of these 12 CR fetuses. The combination of ultrasound and genetic tests enabled us to effectively diagnose prenatal TSC cases. We also identified one novel deletion involving the TSC1 gene and two novel variants in the TSC2 gene, thereby expanding the spectrum of TSC variants.

Materials and methods

Ethical compliance

The research was in compliance with the Helsinki Declaration and approved by the Ethics Committee of Changsha Hospital for Maternal and Child Health Care (EC20230421-02).

Subjects and sample collection

In this study, we included twelve prenatal cases of cardiac rhabdomyoma which were collected in the prenatal Diagnosis Central of Hunan Provincial Maternal and Child Health Care Hospital from 2017 to 2022. The reasons for prenatal diagnosis were cardiac rhabdomyoma presented in all of these fetuses found by ultrasound. The results of prenatal ultrasound examination are shown in Fig. 1 for part of these fetuses. After obtained the informed consents, amniocentesis cells of each fetus were collected via amniocentesis for genetic testing, and peripheral blood were collected from parents to determine inheritance patterns of any variant. Karyotype and SNP-array/CNV seq were performed simultaneously with NGS or before.

Next-generation sequencing

Genomic DNA samples from 12 fetuses and their respective parents were isolated using the Qiagen DNA Blood Midi/Mini kit (Qiagen GmbH, Germany). Extracted DNA underwent enzymatic fragmentation targeting ~ 200-bp segments, followed by end repair and 3'-terminal adenine (A) addition. Indexed adapters were ligated to the resulting fragments, and products of approximately 320 bp were size-selected using XP beads. PCR amplification was subsequently performed. Enrichment of exonic regions was achieved through hybridization capture with the Nano WES system (following manufacturer specifications). Captured libraries were eluted, PCR-amplified, and subjected to quality control: enrichment efficiency was assessed via qPCR, while fragment size distribution and concentration were determined using an Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent Technologies, USA). Final libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq6000 platform (150-bp paired-end reads). Base calling and raw data generation from image files utilized CASAVA (v1.82). Variants identified were validated by Sanger sequencing in trios.

Data analysis

Raw sequencing reads underwent alignment to the GRCh37/hg19 reference genome via the Burrows-Wheeler Aligner (BWA). PCR duplicates were subsequently eliminated using Picard v1.57. Variant pathogenicity classification adhered to American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) standards, categorizing variants as: Pathogenic, Likely Pathogenic, Benign, Likely Benign, or Variants of Uncertain Significance (VUS). For copy number variation (CNV) detection, the Sprinkle pipeline (BerryGenomics) was implemented. This integrated:(i) XHMM-based principal component analysis for noise reduction, (ii) GC-content and bias adjustment through CNV Kit's fix module, (iii) Exon-level and large-segment CNV identification via copy number calculation.

Predicted structure models

To assess the structural and functional implications of the G370S and A563P variants in TSC2, computational modeling was performed using VarSite and PyMOL. The wild-type TSC2 protein complex structure (PDB ID: not specified) was retrieved from the Protein Data Bank Europe (PDBe) as the reference framework. PyMOL facilitated: (i) Structural alignment between wild-type and mutant conformations, (ii) In silico mutagenesis to generate variant models, (iii) Visualization of atomic-level perturbations. This integrated approach enabled systematic evaluation of mutation-induced structural perturbations and their potential functional consequences.

Results

General information and prenatal ultrasound results

A total of 12 fetuses with CR were included in the study. The average age of the 12 pregnant women was 27.83 years (range 22–31 years). The mean GA of CRs detected during routine ultrasound scans was 28.67 ± 2.32 weeks (range 24–32 weeks). All CRs were detected in the ventricle, interventricular septum, and the atrium, especially frequent in the left ventricle. All subjects did not detect arrhythmia during the pregnancy. Beside CRs, irregular low echo in the brain was detected in Case 12 (Fig. 1F). Due to abnormal ultrasound results or positive genetic test results, all the families chose to terminate the pregnancy.

Identification of TSC1 and TSC2 variants

In general, TSC1 and TSC2 variants were detected in all 12 fetuses (100%). Roughly 25% of these variants had not been reported previously (Fig. 2A). The P/LP variant spectrum in TSC1/TSC2 was 4 (33%) nonsense, 4 (33%) missense, 2 (17%) frameshift, 1 (8%) splice, 1 (8%) small deletion (Fig. 2B). Mutants of TSC1 account for 25% (3/12) and TSC2 account for 75% (Fig. 2C). Two-thirds of these variants were de novo (Fig. 2D), and of the familial cases, 25% variants was paternal inherited (Table 1). CNV seq results revealed a 780 Kb-sized deletion on 9q34.13 (arr[hg19] 9q34.13 (132286422–133062068) × 1) in case 1, encompassing the entire of TSC1 gene, which was inherited from mother (Fig. 2E). In case 5, the de novo frameshift mutant of c.1106delT (p.Gln370Serfs*19) in TSC2 (NM_000548.4) is classified to be pathogenic according to the ACMG guideline (PVS1 + PM2_supporting + PS2). In case 7, the de novo missense mutant of c.1687G > C (p.Ala563Pro) in TSC2 (NM_000548.4) is classified to be likely pathogenic (PS2 + PM2_supporting + PP3). To our knowledge, the three variants have not been published in the relevant medical literature or reported in the disease-related variation databases, such as HGMD or ClinVar.

Discussion

Rhabdomyoma is the most prevalent fetal cardiac tumor and can be detected via routine prenatal ultrasound from the 15th gestational week onward [3], which is considered as a major criteria diagnostic for TSC [10]. Rhabdomyoma is frequently the first and most obvious clue for TSC, which could be detected during prenatal period, and then the other manifestations develop later. Tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC, OMIM 191100) is an autosomal dominant disorder having an incidence of 1 in 6,000 to 1 in 10,000 live births [4], 30% are inherited while 70% are de novo variants. The clinical manifestations are extremely variable among patients, characterized by the development of multiple hamartomas in many internal organs including the heart, lungs, kidneys, and skin, as well as a variety of neurological features including intellectual handicap, autistic disorders, and epilepsy [4, 8]. In our study, we found single or multiple CRs in 12 fetuses, most of their parents presented with normal manifestation except case 1, of which the mother mentioned occasionally epilepsy before 6 years old.

The two known genes associated with TSC were TSC1 and TSC2, codified in the loci 9p34 and 16p13, respectively. These two genes encode Hamartin and Tuberin which form a GTPase activating protein (GAP) complex, which inhibits the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway [2, 15]. mTOR serves as a central regulator of a number of important physiological functions, such as cell growth and proliferation, metabolism, and protein synthesis [12]. The mutation of TSC1 or TSC2 leads to dysfunctions of mTOR, while the increased cell growth dysregulation leads to the variety of tumors. The mutation types of TSC1 and TSC2 differ from each other, nonsense or frameshift mutations which lead to protein truncation are seen more in TSC1, while missense mutations and rearrangements are seen more in TSC2 [9]. Of the two genes, mutations of TSC2 account for more than 70 ~ 90% cases [13]. By the end of April 2024, over 6200 different allelic mutations of TSC1/TSC2 from TSC patients have been cataloged in Leiden Open Variation Database (LOVD, http://chromium.lovd.nl/LOVD2/TSC), including 1412mutations in TSC1 and 4793 mutations in TSC2, respectively. In our study, one-thirds of these variants were familial inherited (Fig. 2D), and of which 25% variants was paternal inherited (Table 1). The variants of TSC2 account for 75% of all variants, but no large deletions and rearrangements were detected. Besides the reported mutations, we find three novel variants of TSC1 and TSC2 (Table 1).

There is sufficient evidence for haploinsufficiency of TSC1and TSC2 genes to cause TSC. In our study, the 0.78 Mb deletion at 9q34 region detected in case 1 compassing the whole TSC1 gene, which has never been reported before, but there were several smaller CNVs in the database of ClinVar. Variation ID 534520: arr[GRCh38] 9q34.13 (132896229–132928878) × 1 compassing partial TSC1 deletion, and Variation ID 1696834: arr[GRCh38] 9q34.13(132691768_133013020) × 1, compassing the whole TSC1 gene. A Japanese family with two sibling diagnosis of TSC carried an intragenic deletion (NC_000009.11: g.135777040_135780539del). In Case 1, the fetus presented one echogenicity in LV (7.1 × 4.4 mm) suspicious of rhabdomyomas (Fig. 1A), whose mother and grandmother also carried the 0.78 Mb deletion at 9q34, but both of them presented no obviously phenotypes of TSC except for facial hemangioma.

For the other two novel variants in TSC2 gene, c.1106delT was a frameshift mutant, c.1687G > C was a missense mutant, both were not reported in previous literatures or HGMD database. In case 5, the variant c.1106delT was classified to be pathogenic, several factors were taken into consideration to assess its pathogenicity: (1) this frameshift variant produces a new truncated protein, while the TSC2 is definite gene where LOF is a known mechanism of disease (PVS1); (2) the absence of this variant in various reference population databases, including 1000 Genomes Project, CNGBdb, ExAC, and gnomAD, supports its rarity (PM2_supporting); (3) the variant was considered to be de novo (both maternity and paternity confirmed) in the patient with disease and no family history (PS2). Based on these considerations, this variant was interpreted as a pathogenic variant following the ACMG guidelines (PVS1 + PM2_supporting + PS2). Li et al. mentioned the nearby mutation of c.1143delG (p.Thr382Profs*7) lead to the same truncated protein in 2011 [7]. The silico protein modeling revealed that the c.1106delT variant causes a loss of 1418 amino acids, including the Rap-GAP domain (Fig. 2G). In case 7, the variant c.1687G > C was classified to be likely pathogenic, several factors were taken into consideration to assess its pathogenicity: (1) the variant was considered to be de novo (both maternity and paternity confirmed) in the patient with disease and no family history (PS2), (2) the absence of this variant in various reference population databases, including 1000 Genomes Project, CNGBdb, ExAC, and gnomAD, supports its rarity (PM2_supporting); (3) multiple bioinformatics tools predicted deleterious effects on TSC2 (PP3). Based on these considerations, this variant was interpreted to be likely pathogenic variant following the ACMG guidelines (PS2 + PM2_supporting + PP3). The original alanine has an aliphatic side chain contained only carbon and hydrogen atoms, which is hydrophobic, while the variant residue is a proline has a rigid side chain restricting the conformation of the protein at this point. VarSite diagram illustrated various sequences and structural features of the TSC2 protein, the newly identified A563P has CADD score above 25.60, indicating its likely deleterious nature. In the A563P mutant model, the hydrogen bonds between lysine at position 559 and threonine at position 562 were deleted (Fig. 2F). Taken together, it is highly probable that these two novel variants induce disfunction of TSC2 protein.

In conclusion, our study identified two novel variants (c.1687G > C and c.1106delT) in TSC2 gene and a 780 Kb-sized deletion on 9q34.13 covering the TSC1 gene, which expand the landscape of variant spectrums of TSC1/TSC2 in TSC patients and contribute to the genetic diagnosis and counseling of this disorder. For the rhabdomyoma are frequently the first and most obvious clue for TSC, combination of ultrasound and genetics tests helped us effectively to diagnose the prenatal cases of TSC.

Experimental study on the nonlimit active earth pressure of finite soil in proximity engineering
Authors: Zhenbo Zhang, Baosheng Qie, Fei Xu, Wenzheng Wang, Zhichun Liu, Xuebing Lin, An Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study focuses on the active earth pressure distribution of finite soil masses in urban proximity engineering, with a specific emphasis on nonlimit conditions. Through laboratory model tests, the relationship between the nonlimit earth pressure and displacement of the finite soil mass is investigated. Furthermore, the distribution law of the nonlimit earth pressure is explored, considering the influence of the existing structure, proximity distance, and soil cover thickness of the existing structure on the nonlimit earth pressure distribution within the finite soil mass. The findings indicate the following: (1) the distribution of the nonlimit active earth pressure along the depth of the retaining walls is nonlinear, exhibiting an initial increase, followed by a decrease near the bottom of the existing structure and then another decrease after an initial increase; (2) the nonlimit active earth pressure and ultimate active earth pressure of semi-infinite soil are greater than those of finite soil; (3) as the proximity distance increases, the nonlimit active earth pressure of finite soil gradually increases, and the rate of decrease in earth pressure at the existing structure floor decreases; (4) with increasing soil cover thickness of the existing structure, the nonlimit active earth pressure and ultimate active earth pressure of finite soil gradually increase, and the position of change along the depth of the retaining walls initially increases, then decreases, and finally continues to increase with increasing depth. The aforementioned research offers a foundation for determining the appropriate finite soil pressure in proximity engineering.Keywords: Existing subway station, Nonlimit soil pressure, Adjacent foundation pit, Limited soil, Model testSubject terms: Engineering, Civil engineering

Introduction

With the rapid development of underground space, existing structures (subway stations and tunnel sections) with overlying soil often fall within the influence range of new foundation pits, making rational use of this space essential. Finite soil is formed between the foundation pit and the existing structure, which is different from traditional semi-infinite body foundation pit engineering. Therefore, the classical earth pressure calculation method is no longer applicable, and the distribution of the earth pressure load is different. Existing methods for determining earth pressure cannot accurately predict the earth pressure of limited soil, making it challenging for designers, constructors, and researchers to accurately calculate and analyze earth pressure.

Under pit-in-pit conditions, Pan1 deduced a calculation formula for the passive earth pressure on the basis of limit equilibrium theory and the differential force balance method, considering the soil cohesion and friction between the retaining structure and the sliding soil. It is proposed that the existence of the inner pit reduces the passive earth pressure of the outer pit in the pit-in-pit structure. To control the deformation and stability of deep excavation walls under seismic and static loads, Mahdi2–5 used the finite element method, considered a behavior model of hardened soil, and used quasistatic analysis to analyze the influence of different parameters on the performance of deep foundation pit walls and guard truss structures. In addition, when the failure wedge behind the retaining wall lacks sufficient space for development, changes in the soil stress distribution in the active state, as well as variations in the size and position of the resultant force acting on the wall, are studied. By studying the influence of the mechanical (axial stiffness of the nail and friction coefficient of the soil–nail interface) and geometric (angle, length, and vertical and horizontal spacing) characteristics of the nail on the dynamic response and amplification characteristics of the trench wall under loose and dense soil conditions, the self-stabilizing alternative of the soil nail wall is analyzed. The finite element method is used to study the performance of the constraint wall via pseudostatic seismic analysis, and the optimal performance of the nail is analyzed. Veiskarami6 used the lower bound analysis method, combined with finite element and linear programming optimization techniques, to study the static and seismic active and passive earth pressures of vertical retaining walls in anisotropic sand. Fathipour7 analyzed the effects of flow conditions, the soil‒water characteristic curve (SWRC), and the water table on the response control parameters of unsaturated backfill retaining walls through numerical simulations. Moreover, the effects of variations in wall height, soil type, effective internal friction angle, soil wall roughness, and inherent anisotropy of the soil on active and passive earth pressures are analyzed. Liu8 introduced a novel approach for calculating the finite soil pressure. This method accounts for the stress deflection resulting from the soil arch, intermediate primary stress, and uniform extra force applied to the soil surface. Chen9, on the basis of the soil arching effect and considering the tangential friction between the wall and the soil, obtained the earth pressure formula of finite soil using the horizontal thin layer element method. Rui10 carried out model tests on five modes of movement (translation, rotation around the bottom, rotation around the top, counterclockwise rotation around the midpoint, and clockwise rotation around the midpoint) to study the effects of the displacement mode of the retaining wall on the earth pressure distribution behind the wall and the soil slip deformation and failure mode. Lai11 studied the earth pressure calculation model of finite soil in a nonlimit state by using the thin-layer microelement method for the RB mode (rotation around the bottom of the wall). The problem of finite soil pressure in a new foundation hole near a subway station was investigated by Zhang11,12, who introduced several finite soil fracture surface models and produced a calculation formula for finite earth pressure on the basis of the positional relationship between the subway station and the supporting structure of the foundation pit. In addition, the new foundation pit project in the neighborhood of the existing subway station is relied upon. A variety of failure modes of the finite soil mass are proposed according to the location relationship between the subway station and the foundation pit. A simple method for calculating the resultant active earth pressure of the finite soil mass is proposed, using the thin-layer microelement method and considering the friction between the soil and the structure interface.

Research on the earth pressure of finite soil is becoming increasingly abundant. Considering that the earth pressure cannot reach the limit state in practical engineering, Bang13 argued that the transition of the soil from the static state to the limit state is a gradual process and proposed the concept of a nonlimit state. The nonlimit earth pressure is between the static earth pressure and the limit earth pressure. To adapt to engineering practices, scholars have studied the earth pressure under nonlimit states.

Xu14 considered the soil arching effect in the nonlimit state and deduced an analytical formula for the active earth pressure of finite soil. Rui15 studied the changes and laws of the soil pressure and internal force of a pile‒anchor support system in deep foundation pit engineering. The results showed that the overall soil pressure in the nonlimit state tends to be distributed in a ‘Z’ shape with depth. Considering the shear stress between soil layers, Ma16 established a calculation method for the nonlimit earth pressure of retaining walls under translational mode. Hu17 used model tests and particle image velocimetry to analyze the shape, displacement, and shear strain of a sand slip failure surface in a passive zone and analyzed the relationships between mechanical parameters and horizontal displacement along the retaining wall. Lin18 used the finite element method to study the failure mechanism of narrow fill under different wall motion conditions and established an analytical method for determining the earth pressure while considering the displacement mode and nonlimit state of the retaining wall.

Earth pressure is closely related to engineering design and construction. Accurate analysis, prediction, and calculation of the earth pressure can help designers analyze the stress distribution of soil, select appropriate construction methods and support schemes, and reduce the deformation and failure of soil. This provides the basis for stability evaluation in the construction process and ensures the safety of the project and personnel. To better understand and analyze the characteristics and laws of earth pressure, this paper provides researchers with an analytical method to explore earth pressure, improve the theory of soil mechanics, and classify the earth pressures of different types and modes of action. This paper uses a new foundation pit project in the neighborhood of the Xuanwumen subway station in Beijing as a case study to provide a basis for engineering design and construction, ensuring the safety of the project. However, the literature does not provide the variation law of the nonlimit earth pressure of finite soil. Therefore, this paper studies the nonlimit active earth pressure of finite soil.

Background

A new foundation pit is built on the north side of the Xuanwumen subway station (an existing structure) in Beijing. The size of the foundation pit is 65.1 m × 13.7 m, and the maximum excavation depth is 11.5 m. A Φ600 mm @ 1100 mm bored pile and two steel supports are combined to support the foundation pit. The size of the entrance and exit is 40.8 m × 3.5 m, and the maximum height is 4.5 m. The stratum is sandy silt without groundwater. The engineering location is shown in Fig. 1.

Analysis of the test results

Variation law of the earth pressure on the retaining walls

The calculation results for the experiments conducted under tests 2, 6, and 8 are presented in Fig. 9. Figure 9 shows the relationship between the horizontal coordinate, which represents the ratio of the cumulative displacement value (s) of the retaining walls from its static state to the height of the wall (H), and the vertical coordinate, which represents the ratio of the horizontal earth pressure (σxw) to the product of the unit weight of the soil (γ) and the height of the wall (H).

Variation curve of horizontal earth pressure with wall displacement.

The observed horizontal ground pressures at various depths of the retaining walls exhibit an initial abrupt decrease, followed by progressive deceleration, and ultimately reach a state of stability as the relative displacement of the retaining walls increases, as depicted in Fig. 9.

The earth pressure variation trend can be categorized into three stages as the relative displacement of the retaining walls increases. These stages are characterized by a quick decrease, followed by a slow decline, and finally a stable stage. The limit group of the soil mass can be seen as the point of demarcation between the slow decrease stage and the stable stage10,19. The displacements necessary for tests 2, 6, and 8 to reach the limit state are depicted in Fig. 9 as 0.2%H (2 mm), 0.2%H (2 mm), and 0.25%H (2.5 mm), respectively. The nonlimit active earth pressures of semi-infinite soil and finite soil exhibit nonlinear variations throughout the depth of the retaining walls. The nonlimit active earth pressure of the finite soil mass initially increases with increasing depth of the retaining walls. However, it sharply decreases upon reaching the depth of the floor of the existing structure. An increasing trend is subsequently observed along the depth of the retaining walls until it reaches a peak value. After reaching this peak, the nonlimit active earth pressure gradually decreases. One possible explanation for this phenomenon is that the finite soil mass located between the existing structure and the retaining walls experiences deformation and subsidence at a relatively high rate in response to the movement of the retaining walls. As a result, stress is released within this region, leading to a decrease in the measured earth pressure.

Parameter analysis

The second part of this study examines the correlation between nonlimit earth pressure and displacement under a specific operating environment. It further investigates the impact of the existing structure, proximity distance, and soil cover thickness of the existing structure on the nonlimit earth pressure.

Presence or absence of an existing structure

The analysis focuses on the influence of the existing structure on earth pressure by comparing the outcomes of tests 2 and 8. The displacement of the retaining walls is considered at three different states: 0%H (at rest), 0.15%H (nonlimit state), and 0.25%H (limit state). The test results for tests 2 and 8 are illustrated in Fig. 16.

Earth pressure distributions with or without an existing structure.

On the basis of the findings depicted in Fig. 16, it can be inferred that the presence of the existing structure has a minimal effect on the static earth pressure value for semi-infinite soil retaining walls.

In the context of soil testing, the nonlimit active earth pressure and ultimate active earth pressure tend to be greater in semi-infinite soil tests without an existing structure than in finite soil tests with an existing structure.

The soil pressure adjacent to the lower plate of the existing structure initially increases, subsequently decreases, and then increases again as the depth of the retaining walls increases. Furthermore, the magnitude of the earth pressure with the existing structure is lower than that without the existing structure.

Proximity distance

The analysis of the impact of the proximity distance on the earth pressure involves a comparison of the outcomes obtained via tests 1 to 4. The test findings depicted in Fig. 17 illustrate the displacement of the retaining walls at three different states: 0%H (at rest), 0.15%H (nonlimit state), and 0.25%H (limit state).

Earth pressure distributions with the existing structure at different proximity distances.

On the basis of the findings depicted in Fig. 17, the following conclusions can be drawn:

Changes in the proximity distance affect the magnitudes of the nonlimit active earth pressure and the ultimate active earth pressure. As the proximity distance increases, the nonlimit active earth pressure and ultimate active earth pressure of finite soil gradually increase, whereas the rate of decrease in earth pressure at the existing structure floor becomes progressively slower, assuming that the soil thickness remains constant.

Soil cover thickness of the existing structure

This analysis examines the impact of the soil cover thickness of the existing structure by comparing the findings of tests 2, 5, 6, and 7. The test results depicted in Fig. 18 illustrate the displacement of the retaining walls at three different states: 0%H (at rest), 0.15%H (nonlimit state), and 0.25%H (limit state).

Earth pressure distributions with the existing structure at different cover thicknesses.

On the basis of the findings depicted in Fig. 18, the following conclusions can be drawn:

Changes in the soil cover thickness of the existing structure minimally affect the magnitudes of the nonlimit active earth pressure and the ultimate active earth pressure. At a constant distance, the nonlimit active earth pressure and ultimate active earth pressure of the finite soil gradually increase as the soil cover thickness of the existing structure increases. Additionally, the position at which the nonlimit active earth pressure and ultimate active earth pressure of the finite soil change also varies with the depth of the retaining walls. Initially, it increases, then decreases, and finally increases again.

Conclusions

In this study, an experimental investigation of the distribution of active earth pressure on retaining walls is conducted under eight different translational modes, taking into account the relationship between the position of the foundation pit and the existing structure. The study also examines the displacement of the soil reaching its limit state in the existing structure and analyses the correlation between the nonlimit earth pressure and displacement of the finite soil. Furthermore, the distribution of the nonlimit earth pressure on finite soil is examined, considering the presence or absence of the existing structure, the proximity distance between the soil and the existing structure, and the soil cover thickness of the existing structure. The primary findings can be summarized as follows:

The active earth pressure, which is not limited by any constraints, exhibits nonlinear variation along the depth of the retaining walls. Specifically, the nonlimit active earth pressure of the finite soil initially increases with depth along the retaining walls. However, it subsequently decreases near the floor of the existing structure, followed by another increase and subsequent decrease. The magnitude of soil displacement necessary to reach the limit state varies across different operational circumstances. The displacement of the soil necessary to achieve the limit state is minimal (s = 2 mm) in tests 1–7 and maximal (s = 2.5 mm) in test 8.

The active earth pressure, which is not limited by any constraints, exhibits nonlinear variation along the depth of the retaining walls. However, it subsequently decreases near the floor of the existing structure, followed by another increase and subsequent decrease. The magnitude of soil displacement necessary to reach the limit state varies across different operational circumstances. The displacement of the soil necessary to achieve the limit state is minimal (s = 2 mm) in tests 1–7 and maximal (s = 2.5 mm) in test 8.

The presence of the existing structure has a minimal effect on the static earth pressure exerted on the retaining walls. Additionally, the nonlimit active earth pressure and ultimate active earth pressure of the semi-infinite soil typically exceed those of the finite soil containing the existing structure.

Discussion

This paper examines the new foundation pit in the neighborhood of the Xuanwumen subway station in Beijing and investigates the earth pressure of this specific project. Parameters such as the scope of application of the project, the limitations of the stratum, and the movement mode of the retaining wall influence the earth pressure. However, whether changes in these parameters affect the research conclusions of this paper requires further study.

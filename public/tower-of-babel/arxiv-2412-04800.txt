Equation of state of rhenium under high temperatures and pressures predicted by ensemble theory
Yue-Yue Tian, Hui-fen Zhang, Bo-Yuan Ning, Xi-Jing Ning

Abstract
The high-temperature and high-pressure equations of states (EOSs) of rhenium up to 3000 K and 900 GPa are predicted by a recently developed method in the framework of statistical ensemble theory with ab initio computational precision. The predicted isothermal EOSs are generally consistent with semi-empirical calculations below 150 GPa and 3000 K. Especially, the predicted isobaric EOS at one atmosphere is in good agreement with previous experiments. Moreover, the bulk modulus obtained in this work is closer to the experimental measurements than other theoretical works. Based on our calculations, the disputes between previous experiments are analyzed, and it is expected that the EOSs predicted under extreme conditions might be verified in future experiments.



I
Introduction

The rare metal of rhenium (Re) has unique applications to high-temperature materials due to its high melting point (
\sim
3453 K), ductility and diffusional viscosity, for instance, to mediate the fragility of fusion nuclear materials
[
1
]
, or to increase the high-temperature deformation resistance of turbine blade materials
[
2
,
3
]
. In addition, the large bulk modulus of Re makes it play a vital role in high-pressure researches. For example, Re is commonly selected to be as the gasket material in the diamond-anvil cell (DAC)
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
because of its high compressive strength to retain appreciable thickness up to several hundred gigapascal (GPa)
[
7
]
. As to internally heated DAC, Re is an excellent choice for containing and heating samples, and thus, is always utilized as an internal pressure gauge for determining pressures in high-temperature X-ray diffraction experiments
[
8
]
. Because of its specialties in the realm of extreme-condition physics researches, it is indeed necessary to acquire a reliable equation of state (EOS) which can readily determine the thermodynamic properties of Re.

Although investigations on EOSs of Re started about fifty years ago
[
9
,
10
]
, there still exist controversies over the EOS under high pressures at ambient temperature among recent measurements
[
5
,
11
,
6
,
8
,
4
,
12
]
and the high-temperature EOSs of Re have not been much studied extensively. Dubrovinsky
et al
.
[
4
]
used static double-stage DAC (ds-DAC) to compress Re and achieved a pressure record of 640 GPa at room temperature for the first time. However, the obtained EOS in Ref.
[
4
]
exhibits about 13% difference between the one measured by Anzellini
et al
.
[
6
]
. Such an inconsistence was also noted by another ds-DAC experiment conducted later by Sakai
et al
.
[
12
]
. In a detailed analysis, Saikai
et al
. pointed out that the two EOSs have large deviations and the corresponding pressures are
630
GPa and
430
GPa based on the two pressure calibrations in Refs.
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
respectively when the volume is compressed to
V/V_{0}=0.633
.

Theoretical tools of first-principles quantum mechanics may provide helpful insights to tackle with the problems concerning extreme-condition measurements. For the EOS of Re, predictions based on either molecular dynamics simulations
[
13
]
or static energy calculations
[
14
,
15
,
15
]
coincided with the experimental data
[
6
,
4
,
11
,
9
,
10
,

in the lower pressure zone (
\leq 150

17

[
6
]
and
[
10
]
. However, it should be noted that most of the above computations were implemented at 0 K, which may be insufficient to include finite-temperature thermodynamic effects on the EOS, let alone the high-temperature situations.

The statistical ensemble theory in fact has already formulated a complete theoretical framework to include the thermal effects and paved a strict way to directly derive EOS at any temperature and pressure by calculating the partition function (PF) without any parameters or empirical hypothesis
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
. Unfortunately, the exact solution to the PF of a system consisting of
N
particles involves a 3
N
-fold configurational integral, the computational cost of which makes it difficult to implement the advanced first-principles computations. Multiple methods have been developed to solve the calculation problem of PF, such as the phonon model based on quasi-harmonic approximation (QHA)
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
, particle in a cell model (PIC)
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
, classical mean-field (CMF) approach
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
an so on. However, the accuracy of QHA was questioned for not sufficiently considering the anharmonic vibrations of atoms at high temperature
[
35
]
or with large atomic volumes
[
36
]
. The accuracy of PIC may also be limited due to the neglects of interatomic correlations and diffusions, i.e., the vibrations of atoms are independent with each other in the framework of PIC
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
, and previous works
[
37
,
38
]
already showed less precision of the CMF model, which is further simplification to the configurational integral on the basis of the PIC model.

Different from previous methods, we recently developed a direct integral approach (DIA) to the PF without any empirical or artificial parameters
[
39
]
, and DIA has been successfully applied to calculate the EOSs of solid copper
[
39
]
, argon
[
36
,
40
]
, 2-D materials
[
41
]
, tungsten
[
37
]
and reproduce phase transition of metal vanadium
[
42
]
, zirconium
[
43
]
and aluminum
[
44
]
. Especially, a recent work concerning with the EOS of iridium
[
38
]
showed that the results from DIA are much more precise than CMF and QHA.
In the present work, we applied DIA to solve the PF of crystal hexagonal close-packed (
hcp
) Re and predicted the high-temperature and high-pressure EOSs in the range up to 3000 K and 900 GPa. We firstly compared room-temperature EOS predicted by DIA with existing literatures up to 900 GPa and settled down the disputes over the pressure calibration among experiments. Then the isothermal EOSs and bulk modulus were predicted by DIA in a range of 3000 K and 150 GPa, and compared with limited literatures. The structure of the paper is as following: In Section
II
, details of the implementation of DIA and parameters of DFT are elaborated. In Section
III
, careful comparisons and a discussion of the calculated results with the available experiments are presented, and conclusional remarks are finally made in the Section
IV
.

II
Theoretical Model and Computation Method

II.1
Direct Integral Approach to PF

For a system containing
N
atoms confined within volume
V
at temperature
T
, the atoms are regarded as
N
point particles with cartesian coordinate
\textbf{{q}}^{N}=\{\textbf{{q}}_{1},\textbf{{q}}_{2},\cdots\textbf{{q}}_{N}\}
and the total energy,
U(\textbf{{q}}^{N})
, as the function of
\textbf{{q}}^{N}
can be computed by quantum mechanics. With the knowledge of
U(\textbf{{q}}^{N})
, PF of the system reads

Z=\frac{1}{N!}(\frac{2\pi m}{\beta h^{2}})^{\frac{3}{2}N}Q,

(1)

with configurational integral

Q=\int d{\textbf{{q}}}^{N}e^{-\beta U({\textbf{{q}}}^{N})},

(2)

where
h
is the Planck constant and
\beta=1/k_{B}T
with
k_{B}
the Boltzmann constant. If the configurational integral
Q
is solved, the pressure (
P
),

P=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial lnZ}{\partial V},

(3)

Helmholtz FE (
F
),

F=-\frac{1}{\beta}lnZ,

(4)

and all of the other thermodynamic quantities can be obtained.

For a crystal with
N
atoms at their ideal lattice sites
\textbf{{Q}}^{N}=\{\textbf{{Q}}_{1},\textbf{{Q}}_{2},\cdots\textbf{{Q}}_{N}\}
and the total energy
U_{0}(\textbf{{Q}}^{N})
, we firstly introduce a transformation,

\textbf{{q}}^{\prime N}=\textbf{{q}}^{N}-\textbf{{Q}}^{N},U^{\prime}(\textbf{{q}}^{\prime N})=U(\textbf{{q}}^{N})-U_{0}(\textbf{{Q}}^{N}),

(5)

where
\textbf{{q}}^{\prime N}
and
U^{\prime}(\textbf{{q}}^{\prime N})
represent the displacements of atoms away from their ideal lattice positions and the corresponding total energy differences with respect to
U_{0}(\textbf{{Q}}^{N})
. By inserting Eq. (5) into Eq. (2), configurational integral
Q
is expressed as

Q=e^{-\beta U_{0}}\int d\textbf{{q}}^{\prime N}e^{-\beta U^{\prime}(\textbf{{q}}^{\prime N})}.

(6)

Clearly,
U^{\prime}(\textbf{{q}}^{\prime N})
is positive within all the integral domain and has minimum at the origin
(U^{\prime}(0)=0)
. According to DIA
[
39
]
, the integral in Eq. (6) equals to an effective 3
N
-dimentional volume,

Q=e^{-\beta U_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\mathcal{L}_{i_{x}}\mathcal{L}_{i_{y}}\mathcal{L}_{i_{z}}),

(7)

with the effective length

\mathcal{L}_{ix}=\int e^{-\beta U^{\prime}(0\cdots q^{\prime}_{ix}\cdots 0)}dq^{\prime}_{ix},

(8)

\mathcal{L}_{iy}=\int e^{-\beta U^{\prime}\left(0\cdots q^{\prime}_{iy}\cdots 0\right)}dq^{\prime}_{iy},

(9)

and

\mathcal{L}_{iz}=\int e^{-\beta U^{\prime}(0\cdots q^{\prime}_{iz}\cdots 0)}dq^{\prime}_{iz},

(10)

where
q^{\prime}_{ix}
(
q^{\prime}_{iy}
or
q^{\prime}_{iz}
) is the
x
(
y
or
z
) coordinate of the
i
th atom moving away off its ideal lattice site along with the other two degrees of freedom of the atom and all the other atoms fixed.

For
hcp
Re, all the atoms are equivalent and therefore, the configurational integral is calculated as

Q=e^{-\beta U_{0}}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{x}\mathcal{L}_{y}\mathcal{L}_{z}\Big)^{N}.

(11)

For implementing the calculations, we constructed a 3 × 3 × 2 supercell containing 36 atoms with basis vectors
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{1}}=a\cdot(1,0,0)
,
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{2}}=a\cdot(1/2,\sqrt{3}/2,0)
and
\boldsymbol{\alpha_{3}}=(0,0,\mathrm{\textit{c}})
, where
a
and
c
are the lattice parameters. The
hcp
system is firstly relaxed to find the most stable structural parameters, and then, as shown in Fig. 1(a), an arbitrary atom (red one in Fig.
1
(a)) was chosen to move away along
x
(
y
or
z
) direction step by step with an interval of 0.05 Å to gain the total energy
U^{\prime}(x^{\prime})
(
U^{\prime}(y^{\prime})
or
U^{\prime}(z^{\prime})
), which were calculated by DFT. Afterwards, the cubic spline interpolation algorithm
[
45
]
was used to smoothen the energy curve
U^{\prime}
, and the configurational integral
Q
and the PF of the structures at given volume can be calculated via Eq. (8-11) and (1). Changing the volume of the structure by enlarging or shrinking the lattice constants, a series of
U^{\prime}
as a function of volume were obtained (
U^{\prime}(x^{\prime})
as an example is shown in Fig.
1
(b)). Correspondingly, a set of configurational integral
Q
dependent on volume could be achieved, and further the EOSs were calculated based on Eqs. (1) and (3).

II.2
Computational Parameters of DFT

DFT calculations in the present work were implemented in Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package
[
46
,
47
]
, in which Projector Augmented Wave (PAW) pseudopotential
[
48
,
49
]
and Perdew Burke Ernzerhof (PBE) of general gradient approximation (GGA)
[
50
]
were selected to describe the electron-ion interactions and electron-electron exchange-correlation functional with 7 valence electrons (
6s^{2}5d^{5}
) considered. A
\Gamma
-centered 11 × 11 × 11 uniform k-mesh grid is set to sample the Brillouin zone by the Monkhorst-Pack scheme together with the electron self-consistent tolerance for total energy being
1\times 10^{-6}
eV and the plane-wave energy cutoff being 400 eV. Convergence verifications of all these parameters were conducted for the structure with the smallest volume (
a_{\mathrm{c}}=0.80a
) considered in the present work that the fluctuation of the total energy was much less than
10^{-3}
eV/atom. In order to check the influences of different functionals on the results,
we compared the EOS calculated by the local-density approximation (LDA), PBE and PBEsol at 0 K with the experimental results and found that PBE performs better at low pressure, as shown in Fig. S1 in the supplementary materials.

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we predicted the EOS of
hcp
Re in the temperature range of 300-3000 K up to 900 GPa by solving the PF via DIA without any empirical or artificial parameters.
Since DIA is based on equilibrium statistical physics, the results should be consist with hydrostatic experiments. Unfortunately, no hydrostatic experimental results are available except for the one concerning isobaric EOS at one atmosphere, which is in good agreement with our calculated result.
Compared with the results of quasi-hydrostatic compression experiments at room temperature, our calculated results present relative deviations of less than 10% below 200 GPa.
Furthermore, the semi-empirical high-temperature EOSs up to 150 GPa generally agree well with our results.
These facts indicate that the EOSs of Re calculated by DIA can be a reliable reference, and more future experimental researches on Re at high temperatures and high pressures are needed to verify our predictions.

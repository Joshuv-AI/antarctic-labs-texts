Gauge-invariant charges of the dual graviton
Chris Hull, Ulf Lindström, Maxwell L. Velásquez Cotini Hutt

Abstract
The free graviton theory given by linearising Einstein’s theory has a dual formulation in terms of a dual graviton field. The dual graviton theory has two gauge invariances giving rise to two conserved charges, while the ADM charges of the graviton theory become magnetic charges for the dual graviton theory. These charges can be ill-defined in topologically non-trivial settings and we find improvement terms that can be added to these to give gauge-invariant conserved charges. These gauge-invariant charges, which have local expressions in both the graviton and dual graviton formulation, give topological operators of the theory that should be considered as the generators of the genuine symmetries of the theory.

In terms of

Since the 1-form currents

On-shell, i.e. using (

Hence there are charges

The secondary Noether currents can be written

(see also

In particular,

6

6

In

In the following, we will show that a topological term can be added to

2.4
Four dimensions

In four dimensions, the dual graviton field is a symmetric tensor gauge field
D_{\mu\nu}
and so is of the same type as the original graviton field
h_{\mu\nu}
. In this case, the two types of gauge transformations in (
7
) degenerate and reduce to the form of a linearised diffeomorphism:

\delta D_{\mu\nu}=2\partial_{(\mu}\tilde{\xi}_{\nu)}\,,

(51)

for a 1-form parameter
\tilde{\xi}
.
Therefore, invariances of the four-dimensional dual graviton are given by parameters
\tilde{\xi}_{\mu}=\alpha\tilde{k}_{\mu}
, where
\tilde{k}
is a Killing vector.
7
7

7

While both
k
and
\tilde{k}
are Killing vectors of Minkowski space, they will play a slightly different rôle in the following.
The corresponding global symmetries are then analogous to those of the graviton discussed in section

. There is a 2-form current

\tilde{J}[\tilde{k}]^{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\sigma}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\mu\nu|\rho\sigma}\tilde{k}_{\rho}-\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\mu\sigma|\rho\nu}\partial_{\sigma}\tilde{k}_{\rho}\,,

(52)

where

\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\mu\nu|\rho\sigma}=-3\eta^{\mu\nu\alpha|\rho\sigma\beta}D_{\alpha\beta}\,.

(53)

which is conserved,
\differential^{\dagger}\tilde{J}[\tilde{k}]=0
, in regions where
E_{\mu\nu}=0

where
E_{\mu\nu}
is the Einstein tensor for the dual graviton
D_{\mu\nu}
.
On using the dual graviton field equations,
E_{\mu\nu}=U_{\mu\nu}
, these are regions where the dual energy-momentum tensor vanishes:
U_{\mu\nu}=0
. There is then a charge,

\tilde{Q}[\tilde{k}]=\int_{\Sigma}\star\tilde{J}[\tilde{k}]\,,

(54)

where
\Sigma
is a codimension-2 cycle contained in such a region.

Let us compare the four-dimensional charges
\tilde{Q}[\tilde{k}]
with the charges
Q[\lambda]
and
Q[\kappa]
which are present for the higher-dimensional dual graviton. We note that
the current
J[\lambda]
in (
48
) gives,
for
d=4
, the current
\tilde{J}[\tilde{k}]
. That is, the charges
Q[\lambda]
reduce to the charges
\tilde{Q}[\tilde{k}]
in
d=4
.
This is in accordance with the fact that the defining equation for the KY
(d-3)
-form
\lambda
, (
35
), reduces to the Killing vector equation in
d=4
.
The defining equation of the
\kappa
-tensors, (
37
), implies that the
\kappa
are constant 1-forms in
d=4
dimensions. These are a subset of the Killing vectors
\lambda
, and the
Q[\kappa]
become a subset of the
Q[\lambda]
.

Instead of the minimal currents introduced in

3.2
Improved dual Penrose 2-form

We now discuss the analogous construction in the dual graviton theory. Analogously to the

(d-2)

Demanding that this is conserved on-shell in regions where

3.3
Triviality of
\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{C}

It was shown in

\mathcal{A}

The

A CKY 3-form,

\partial_{\mu}M_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\tilde{M}_{\mu\alpha\beta\gamma}+3\eta_{\mu[\alpha}\hat{M}_{\beta\gamma]}\,,

(80)

where

\tilde{M}_{\mu\alpha\beta\gamma}=\partial_{[\mu}M_{\alpha\beta\gamma]},\quad\hat{M}_{\beta\gamma}=\frac{1}{d-2}\partial^{\alpha}M_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\,.

(81)

The most general solution to this equation is of a similar form to (
59

[
8
]
for the general form of the solution to the CKY equation of any rank on Minkowski space). CKY 3-forms on Minkowski space have the property that their divergence,
\hat{M}

[

]
. It follows that the
\mathcal{A}
- and
\mathcal{C}
-type CKY 2-forms can be written as
K=\hat{M}
for a CKY 3-form

.

Consider now the globally-defined 3-form

X[M]_{\mu\nu\rho}=\tilde{S}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[6.73412pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.81006pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha\beta}}}_{{{[\mu\nu}\mathchoice{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.9061pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.21864pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}M_{\rho]\alpha\beta}\,.

Using (

15
), (
16
), and (

\partial^{\rho}X[M]_{\mu\nu\rho}\doteq(d-4)\tilde{S}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\hat{M}^{\alpha\beta}=(d-4)\tilde{Y}_{+}[\hat{M}]_{\mu\nu}

on-shell in regions where

.
Therefore, when

, the improved dual Penrose current
\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]
is co-exact when

is an
\mathcal{A}
- or
\mathcal{C}
-type CKY 2-form. It follows that the dual Penrose charges associated with these CKY 2-forms vanish on-shell:

\tilde{Q}[K]=\int_{\Sigma}\star\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]\doteq\int_{\Sigma}\differential\star X[M]=0\,,

(84)

as
\partial\Sigma=0
and
X[M]
is a globally defined tensor field. The
\mathcal{B}
- and
\mathcal{D}
-type CKY 2-forms

\mathcal{B}
- and
\mathcal{D}
-type dual Penrose charges can be non-vanishing.

In

\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]

3.5
Dual
(d-3)
-form symmetries

It has been argued in
[
25
]
that higher-form symmetries should come in dual pairs and so, in particular, there should be the same number of 1-form symmetries as
(d-3)
-form symmetries. For example, in Maxwell theory there is one of each. This has been demonstrated in the graviton formulation of linear gravity in
[
10
,
5
]
and we therefore expect the same result to hold in the dual formulation.
Let us briefly review how this result is seen in the graviton formulation before proceeding to the dual description.
The 1-form symmetries of the graviton are generated by the Penrose charges
Q[K]
in (
58
). In four dimensions, there are 20 independent Penrose charges, generating a group
\mathbb{R}^{20}
of 1-form symmetries.
As commented in section
3.3
, in
d>4
dimensions the
\mathcal{A}
- and
\mathcal{C}
-type Penrose charges
Q[K]
vanish in the graviton formulation, leaving only the
\mathcal{B}
- and
\mathcal{D}
-type charges generating a group
\mathbb{R}^{d(d+1)/2}
of 1-form symmetries.
In the graviton formulation, the
(d-3)
-form symmetries are generated by charges

q[K]=\int_{\Sigma}Y_{+}[K]\,,

(96)

where
\Sigma
is a 2-cycle. In four dimensions, this charge is conserved (i.e. does not depend on deformations of
\Sigma
) for all CKY 2-forms
K
. However, they are not independent of the Penrose charges
Q[K]
in
d=4
, and so the total number of 1-form symmetries remains as 20.
In
d>4
dimensions,
q[K]
is only conserved if
K
is a closed CKY 2-form (i.e. an
\mathcal{A}
- or
\mathcal{B}
-type CKY tensor) and generate a group
\mathbb{R}^{d(d+1)/2}
of
(d-3)
-form symmetries.
Indeed, we see the same number of 1-form and
(d-3)
-form symmetries in all dimensions.

Let us now check the same result holds in the dual formulation of the graviton theory.
In section
3.3
we found that in
d=4
all CKY 2-forms
K
give rise to non-vanishing dual Penrose charges
\tilde{Q}[K]
, whereas in
d>4
dimensions only the
\mathcal{B}
- and
\mathcal{D}
-type CKY tensors give non-vanishing charges. Counting the number of independent CKY tensors, this gives a 1-form symmetry group
\mathbb{R}^{20}
in
d=4
and
\mathbb{R}^{d(d+1)/2}
in
d>4
dimensions.
Let us now consider the
(d-3)
-form symmetries. A continuous
(d-3)
-form symmetry is related to a conserved
(d-2)
-form current or, equivalently, a closed 2-form current. Therefore, let us analyse when the improved dual Penrose 2-form
\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]
is closed on-shell. In regions where
U=0
, (
16
) and (
20
) imply that
\partial_{[\mu}\tilde{S}_{\alpha\beta]\gamma\delta}=0
on-shell, so we have

\partial_{[\rho}\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]_{\mu\nu]}=\tilde{S}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[6.73412pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.81006pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha\beta}}}_{{{[\mu\nu}\mathchoice{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.9061pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.21864pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\partial_{\rho]}K_{\alpha\beta}=\tilde{S}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[6.73412pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.81006pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha\beta}}}_{{{[\mu\nu}\mathchoice{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.9061pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.21864pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\tilde{K}_{\rho]\alpha\beta}-2\tilde{S}_{\beta[\rho\mu\nu]}\hat{K}^{\beta}=\tilde{S}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[6.73412pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.81006pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha\beta}}}_{{{[\mu\nu}\mathchoice{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.9061pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.21864pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\tilde{K}_{\rho]\alpha\beta}\,,

(97)

where we have used the CKY equation (
56
) in the second equality and (
20
) in the final equality.

In
d>4
, (
97
) implies that
\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]
is closed when
\tilde{K}=0
, which is the case for the
\mathcal{A}
- and
\mathcal{B}
-type solutions in (
59
).
Therefore, when
K
is an
\mathcal{A}
- or
\mathcal{B}
-type CKY 2-form, we define the conserved charges

\tilde{q}[K]=\int_{\Sigma}\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]\,,

(98)

where
\Sigma
is a 2-cycle contained in a region where
U=0
. These charges generate a group
\mathbb{R}^{d(d+1)/2}
of
(d-3)
-form symmetries of the dual graviton theory, matching the number of 1-form symmetries found above.

When
d=4
, however, (
97
) implies that
\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]
is closed for all CKY 2-forms
K
. This can be seen by dualising,

\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{\rho\mu\nu\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{n}}\partial_{\rho}\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{3}S^{\rho\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{n}|\mu\nu}\tilde{K}_{\rho\mu\nu}\,.

(99)

In
d=4
, we have
n=d-3=1
and so the result vanishes on-shell from the algebraic Bianchi identity (
10
).
This would seem to imply that there are a further 20 one-form symmetries in
d=4
generated by the
\tilde{q}[K]
. However, the
\tilde{q}[K]
are not independent of the
\tilde{Q}[K]
and they generate the same 20 one-form symmetries. This can be seen by dualising
\tilde{S}
and using standard properties of the Levi-Civita symbol:

\begin{split}\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]_{\mu\nu}&=\tilde{S}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}K^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\kappa\lambda}S^{{{\rho\sigma}\mathchoice{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.9061pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.21864pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[8.78293pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[8.78293pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.3333pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.80948pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha\beta}}}(\star K)_{\kappa\lambda}=-6S^{{{\rho\sigma}\mathchoice{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[6.73412pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.81006pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[8.78293pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[8.78293pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.3333pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.80948pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{[\mu\nu}}}(\star K)_{\rho\sigma]}\\
&=-S_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(\star K)^{\rho\sigma}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\tilde{S}^{\alpha\beta\rho\sigma}(\star K)_{\rho\sigma}=-\star\tilde{Y}_{+}[\star K]_{\mu\nu}\,.\end{split}

(100)

This equation relates the dual improved Penrose current associated with a CKY 2-form
K
to the Hodge dual of the dual improved Penrose current associated with
\star K
in four dimensions. However, the since the Hodge dual of a CKY 2-form is itself a CKY 2-form
[
19
,
20
]
,

\tilde{q}[K]=\int_{S}\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]=-\int_{S}\star\tilde{Y}_{+}[\star K]=\tilde{Q}[-\star K]\,.

(101)

and so are equal to the dual Penrose charges
\tilde{Q}[K^{\prime}]
for another CKY tensor
K^{\prime}=-\star K
. Therefore, there are only 20 independent charges in
d=4
dimensions, which can be parametrised by the
\tilde{Q}[K]
.

3.6
Covariant currents for the
\kappa
-charges

Consider the case of
d>4
dimensions. Then there are charges
Q[\lambda]
and
Q[\kappa]
associated with invariances of the dual graviton. We have seen that the dual Penrose charges give a covariant expression for the
Q[\lambda]
in the previous subsections. We now consider the
Q[\kappa]
and a covariant expression for them.

As remarked in section
2.3
, the 2-form currents

, defined in terms of

in (

), can be written locally in terms of

in the duality gauge (
25

In
[
6
]
, assuming this choice of gauge, the covariant 2-form
\Omega[V]
in (
5
) was built from a contraction of the Riemann tensor with a [4,2]-tensor
V
such that

\Omega[V]_{\mu\nu}=J[\kappa]_{\mu\nu}+\partial^{\rho}\Phi_{\mu\nu\rho}

provided that
V
satisfies

\partial^{\mu}V_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\gamma\delta}=\frac{(-1)^{d}}{d-1}\tilde{\kappa}_{\nu\alpha\beta[\gamma|\delta]}\,.

(103)

In (
102
), we have defined the 3-form

\Phi_{\mu\nu\rho}=2\Gamma^{\gamma\delta|\beta}V_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\gamma\delta}\,.

(104)

One simple solution of the condition (
103

V_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\gamma\delta}=\frac{(-1)^{d}}{d-1}\tilde{\kappa}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|[\gamma}x_{\delta]}\,,

(105)

where
x^{\mu}
are the coordinates of the background Minkowski spacetime.
11
11

11

If the background spacetime has non-trivial topology, e.g. toroidal directions, then the components of
V
given in (
105
) are not necessarily well-defined. In some cases, it is still possible to find explicit solutions to (
103
)
[
6
]
.

Integrating (
102
) over a codimension-2 cycle, we have a relation between the charge associated with the covariant current
\Omega[V]
, the
Q[\kappa]
charge, and a topological charge,

\mathbb{Q}[V]\equiv\int_{\Sigma}\star\Omega[V]=Q[\kappa]+\int_{\Sigma}\differential\star\Phi\,,

(106)

such that the
\mathbb{Q}[V]
can be seen as a covariantisation of the
Q[\kappa]
defined only in terms of the Riemann tensor.

We now describe the analogous construction for the dual graviton formulation.
Consider the 2-forms

\tilde{\Omega}[V]_{\mu\nu}=\tilde{S}^{\gamma\delta\alpha\beta}V_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\gamma\delta}\,,

(107)

related to
\Omega[V]
in (
5
) by the duality (
24
).
Then we have

\tilde{\Omega}[V]_{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\delta}(\star\tilde{\Gamma})^{\alpha\beta|\gamma}V_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\gamma\delta}=J[\kappa]_{\mu\nu}+\partial^{\rho}\tilde{\Phi}_{\mu\nu\rho}\,,

(108)

where
\tilde{\Phi}
is a 3-form with components

\tilde{\Phi}_{\mu\nu\rho}=(\star\tilde{\Gamma})^{\gamma\delta|\alpha}V_{\mu\nu\alpha\rho|\gamma\delta}\,,

(109)

which is related to
\Phi
by the gravitational duality provided we choose the gauge (
25
).
In reaching (
108
) we have used (
14
) in the first equality and (
47
) in the second. It then follows from the on-shell conservation of
J[\kappa]
in the dual graviton theory that
\tilde{\Omega}[V]_{\mu\nu}
is conserved on-shell in regions where
U=0
. Integrating over a codimension-2 cycle
\Sigma
contained in such a region, the charge defined by integrating
\star\tilde{\Omega}[V]
is related to
Q[\kappa]
and a topological charge:

\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}[V]\equiv\int_{\Sigma}\star\tilde{\Omega}[V]=Q[\kappa]+\int_{\Sigma}\differential\star\tilde{\Phi}\,.

(110)

From (
109
),
\tilde{\Phi}
is globally defined when

is globally defined. In cases where it is global, the integrand of final term in (
110
) is exact and so its integral vanishes. We then find that the gauge-invariant charge
\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}[V]
gives precisely the charge
Q[\kappa]
which was defined in terms of the non-gauge-invariant current
J[\kappa]
. For non-globally defined field configurations, the final term will contribute in general. We can then see the current
\tilde{\Omega}[V]
as the covariantisation of the
J[\kappa]
current.

As mentioned in the previous subsection, it has been suggested that 1-form and
(d-3)
-form symmetries come in dual pairs. The existence of non-trivial
\mathbb{Q}[V]
and
\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}[V]
charges depends on some non-trivial topology of the background spacetime (e.g. compact dimensions, or excision of some points or regions)
[
6
]
. These charges generate 1-form symmetries, and it would be interesting to understand if there are additional
(d-3)
-form symmetries dual to them on such non-trivial backgrounds.

4.1
Four dimensions

In
d=4
dimensions, there are 20 CKY 2-forms
K
and the Penrose and dual Penrose charges corresponding to all of them are non-vanishing. The results of section
3
give a relation between these charges and the charges
Q[k]
and
\tilde{Q}[\tilde{k}]
associated with invariances of the graviton and dual graviton respectively. Here,
k
and
\tilde{k}
are Killing vectors related to

by (
63
) and (
79
) respectively.

In particular, the
\mathcal{A}
-type CKY 2-forms have
k=0
and
\tilde{k}=0
. Therefore, (
64
) and (
77
) imply that the
\mathcal{A}
-type Penrose charges are ‘magnetic’ in both the graviton and dual graviton formulations; that is, the
\mathcal{A}
-type currents
Y_{+}[\mathcal{A}]
and
\tilde{Y}_{+}[\mathcal{A}]
are both identically conserved, and the corresponding charge is an integral of a total divergence. As such, the charge is non-vanishing only when
h
and
D
are not globally defined.
This behaviour is not seen in standard
p
-form gauge theories, where electric and magnetic charges are interchanged under the duality.
Having charges which are magnetic in both formulations is novel to gauge theories with fields in more general Lorentz representations. Commonly, e.g. in Maxwell theory, the objects which carry magnetic charges in one formulation of the theory are solitonic, but can be described locally in the dual formulation where they carry electric charge. The fact that the
\mathcal{A}
-type Penrose charges are magnetic in both formulations implies that the objects which carry these charges are intrinsically solitonic. In
[
8
]
it was found that one solution carrying the
\mathcal{A}
-type charge in the graviton description is a linearisation of the C-metric.

The
\mathcal{B}
-type CKY 2-forms have non-zero and constant
k=-\mathcal{B}/2
, but
\tilde{k}=0
. That is, they relate to the ADM momenta, but not to any of the
\tilde{Q}[\tilde{k}]
. Therefore, (
64
) implies that the
\mathcal{B}
-type Penrose charges give the sum of the ADM momenta and a magnetic charge
\int\differential\star Z[K]
in the graviton formulation, while (
77
) implies that they are purely magnetic in the dual graviton formulation, i.e. of the form
\int\differential\star\tilde{Z}[K]
. Consider a field configuration where
h
is globally defined but
D
is not, then
\int\differential\star Z[K]=0
and so equating (
64
) and (
77
) gives

Q[k]=\int_{\Sigma}\differential\star\tilde{Z}[K]\,,

(115)

where the left-hand side is the ADM momentum associated with a constant Killing vector
k
, and the right-hand side is a magnetic charge for the dual graviton
D
. This is the expected behaviour of an electro-magnetic duality, where an electric charge in one formulation is manifest as a magnetic charge in the dual formulation. This is juxtaposed with the
\mathcal{A}
-type charges discussed above, where the charge is magnetic in both formulations.

The
\mathcal{C}
-type charges have the opposite behaviour since a
\mathcal{C}
-type CKY tensor corresponds to
k=0
but a constant non-zero
\tilde{k}=-3\star\mathcal{C}
in four dimensions. Therefore (
64
) implies that the
\mathcal{C}
-type Penrose charges are magnetic in the graviton formulation but (
77
) implies that they give the sum of the
\tilde{Q}[\tilde{k}]
and a magnetic charge
\int\differential\star\tilde{Z}[K]
in the dual graviton formulation. Consider a field configuration for which
D
is globally defined but
h
is not, then
\int\differential\star\tilde{Z}[K]=0
, so equating (
64
) and (
77
) gives

\tilde{Q}[\tilde{k}]=\int_{\Sigma}\differential\star Z[K]\,.

(116)

Therefore, the charges
\tilde{Q}[\tilde{k}]
corresponding to constant
\tilde{k}
are electric charges in the dual graviton formulation, but magnetic in the graviton description.

Finally, consider the
\mathcal{D}
-type Penrose charges. The
\mathcal{D}
-type CKY 2-forms have
k_{\mu}=\mathcal{D}_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu}
and
\tilde{k}_{\mu}=-\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\mathcal{D}^{\nu\rho}x^{\sigma}
and so correspond to the Lorentz boost Killing vectors. Then (
64
) implies that the
\mathcal{D}
-type Penrose charges give the sum of the ADM angular momenta and a magnetic charge in the
h
formulation. On the other hand, (
77
) implies that the
\mathcal{D}
-type Penrose charges give the sum of the
\tilde{Q}[\tilde{k}]
and a magnetic charge in the
D
formulation. We remark that the
\mathcal{D}
-type Penrose charges are electric in both formulations of the theory; that is,
Y_{+}[\mathcal{D}]
and
\tilde{Y}_{+}[\mathcal{D}]
are only conserved on-shell in both formulations. Similarly to the
\mathcal{A}
-type charges, this behaviour is not observed in Maxwell theory where electric and magnetic charges are interchanged under duality. This implies that the objects carrying the
\mathcal{D}
-type charges are intrinsically ‘electric’ in both formulations. In
[
8
]
it was shown that a linearisation of the Kerr metric gives a four-dimensional solution carrying the
\mathcal{D}
-type Penrose charge.

4.2
d>4
dimensions

In

dimensions, we found in section

5
Summary of the relationships between charges

In this section we provide a brief summary of the results found here and in
[
5
,
6
]
. This is intended to clarify the relationships between the different charges in different subcases.
For each type of CKY 2-form
K
in (
59
), there are Penrose charges
Q[K]
which, in regions without sources, can be written in terms of either
h
or
D
. Below, we give the relation between the Penrose charges
Q[K]
and the charges
Q[k]
and
Q[\lambda]
(or
\tilde{Q}[\tilde{k}]
in
d=4
) in the graviton formulation and dual graviton formulation respectively. We also review the relation of the
\mathbb{Q}[V]
to the
Q[\kappa]
in the dual formulation.

In each case, the Penrose charge
Q[K]
is gauge-invariant and it is related to a topological charge, or to a Noether charge plus a topological charge. Here, we call a charge topological if it is the integral of a closed form, and will vanish unless the form represents a non-trivial cohomology class. We write the topological charges in the form
\int\differential\star Z
for some
Z
and so these will vanish if
Z
is globally-defined. In the cases in which the Penrose charge is the sum of a Noether charge and a topological charge, the Noether and topological charges are not separately gauge-invariant in general, and so the total derivative term can be thought of as an improvement added to the Noether charge to render it gauge-invariant. If the corresponding gauge field is globally-defined, the topological term is trivial and the Penrose charge is precisely the usual Noether charge.

6
Conclusion and outlook

We have constructed gauge-invariant 2-form conserved currents
\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]

\tilde{\Omega}[V]
(defined in (

For certain tensors

\tilde{Q}[K]
and

\tilde{Y}_{+}[K]
can be integrated over 2-cycles to give charges
\tilde{q}[K]
generating
(d-3)
-form symmetries.
The dual graviton field

h_{\mu\nu}
.
The two formulations are related by gravitational duality
[

\tilde{Q}[K]

and

(defined in (

), (
106
) and (

) respectively) which are written in terms of the graviton field
h
.
That is, the 1-form and
(d-3)
-form global symmetries which these charges generate match exactly between the two formulations of the theory.

and

charges are not gauge-invariant in topologically non-trivial situations, as the integrand can change by a closed form which is not exact
[
5
]
.
The gauge-invariant charges
Q[K]
and
\tilde{Q}[K]
, however, are true conserved quantities of the graviton theory. In certain circumstances (restricting the topology of the field configurations), the ADM charges
Q[k]
and the
Q[\lambda]
charges will also be gauge-invariant.

Given the importance of the charges
Q[K]
and
\tilde{Q}[K]
, another objective of this work has been to investigate their properties under gravitational duality. We find that the map between electric and magnetic charges under duality is considerably more interesting for a spin-2 field than for standard
p
-form gauge fields. In particular, in the graviton description the subset of these charges corresponding to
\mathcal{B}
- and
\mathcal{D}
-type CKY tensors
K
(see (
59
)) are electric charges in the sense that they are conserved on-shell only in the absence of sources. In contrast, the
\mathcal{A}
- and
\mathcal{C}
-type CKY tensors give rise to identically conserved currents and, therefore, to magnetic charges. In the dual formulation of the theory, the
\mathcal{C}
- and
\mathcal{D}
-type charges are electric, while the
\mathcal{A}
- and
\mathcal{B}
-type charges are magnetic. That is, the
\mathcal{A}
-type charges are magnetic in both descriptions, whereas the
\mathcal{D}
-type charges are electric in both. This is in contrast to the behaviour of similar charges in
p
-form gauge theories, where electric and magnetic charges are exchanged under duality.

In this paper we have discussed currents and charges related to second rank CKY tensors.
It is natural to seek generalisations of our discussion to
higher rank covariant currents such as

R^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[6.73412pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.81006pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha\beta}}}_{{{[\mu\nu}\mathchoice{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.9061pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.21864pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}K_{\rho]\alpha\beta},\quad R^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[11.1355pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[6.73412pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.81006pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha\beta}}}_{{{[\mu\nu}\mathchoice{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.9061pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.21864pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}K_{\rho\sigma]\alpha\beta},\quad\dots

(127)

with

a higher rank CKY tensor.
However, the corresponding charges vanish, as may be shown via the following short argument.

Consider the rank-

and
K
is a
p

to be a KY
p

(p+1)
CKY tensor. Inserting this into

then implies that it is also co-exact:

where

While the free graviton theory has a dual formulation in terms of the dual graviton, there is no such duality for the non-linear Einstein theory, and indeed there is not a local covariant interacting theory of the dual graviton

]
.
Nonetheless, the dual graviton theory has proved useful in constructing magnetic charges for the free graviton theory, and one can aim to extend these to magnetic charges for the non-linear theory. We will discuss such extensions elsewhere.

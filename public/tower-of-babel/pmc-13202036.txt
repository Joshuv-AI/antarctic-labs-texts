Phase 1/2 study of pegenzileukin, a pegylated recombinant non-alpha IL-2, with cemiplimab for the treatment of advanced unresectable or metastatic skin cancers
Authors: Carlos Rojas, Laurent Mortier, John J Park, Luis Matamala, Adyb Baakili, Saleem Rao, Jing Xu, Laurent Andrieu, Rui Wang, Hong Wang, Tobias Paehler, Giovanni Abbadessa, Paolo A Ascierto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPegenzileukin (SAR444245), a pegylated non-alpha recombinant human interleukin (IL)-2 variant, avoids engagement of the IL-2 receptor α subunit (IL-2Rα) chain and associated toxicities, while retaining engagement of IL-2Rβγ, for activation of effector T cell and natural killer (NK) cell. The first-in-human study of pegenzileukin with pembrolizumab showed initial efficacy and tolerable safety in advanced and metastatic solid tumors. In this study, we evaluate pegenzileukin with cemiplimab for advanced or metastatic melanoma (MM) and cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (CSCC).MethodsThis phase 1/2, open-label, multicenter study evaluated drug combinations in patients with MM (Cohort A) and CSCC (Cohort B). Pegenzileukin was given at two dose levels (16 µg/kg: Cohort A1 and B1 and 24 µg/kg: Cohort A2 and B2) during dose escalation and recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) during dose expansion, while cemiplimab was given at a dose of 350 mg; both drugs were administered every 3 weeks as a 30-minute intravenous infusion. The primary endpoint was the objective response rate (ORR); secondary endpoints included dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs), progression-free survival (PFS), safety, and exploratory biomarker endpoints.ResultsA total of 46 patients were enrolled. At the RP2D of pegenzileukin (16 µg/kg, n=20 in Cohort A1 and n=16 in Cohort B1), the ORR was 40% in Cohort A1 and 56.3% in Cohort B1. In Cohort A1, one patient achieved a complete response, and seven achieved a partial response (PR). In Cohort B1, nine patients achieved a PR. At the RP2D, 75.0% and 85.7% responders, respectively, in Cohort A1 and Cohort B1 had a duration of response of ≥12 months. The PFS rate at 6 months was 55.0% in Cohort A1 and 70.7% in Cohort B1. Five DLTs occurred in four patients: Cohort A had one case of ≥3 Grade thrombocytopenia and one of Grade 2 cytokine release syndrome, while Cohort B had one case of ≥3 Grade alanine transaminase/aspartate transaminase increases and one infusion-related reaction (IRR). Grade 1 or 2 IRRs were the most common adverse events. Treatment led to robust expansion of NK and CD8 T-cells, without an increase of regulatory T cells.ConclusionPegenzileukin in combination with cemiplimab at the RP2D of 16 µg/kg showed a manageable safety profile and promising translational data supporting its mechanism of action.Trial registration numberNCT04913220.

Introduction

Melanoma and cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (CSCC) are common skin cancers, arising from the malignant proliferation of melanocytes and keratinocytes in the epidermis, respectively.1 2 Melanoma is less common but highly aggressive, with an estimated 100,640 new cases and 8290 deaths in the USA in 2024.24 The 5-year survival rate drops from >99% to 35% once it spreads to distant organs.3 CSCC is the second most common type of non-melanoma skin cancer, affecting 250,000 people annually in the USA.2 Immunosuppression significantly increases the risk of both melanoma and CSCC, making immune regulation crucial for managing these cancers.1 5 6

Immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4 (CTLA4) and programmed cell death-1 (PD-1) have revolutionized melanoma and CSCC treatment by blocking T-cell inactivation and enhancing the antitumor immune response.7 8 PD-1 inhibitors pembrolizumab and nivolumab are the mainstay treatments for locally advanced and metastatic melanoma, while cemiplimab and pembrolizumab are approved for locally advanced or metastatic CSCC, not suitable for curative surgery or radiation.1 8 Despite their effectiveness, about 50%–60% of patients do not respond to PD-1 inhibitors.913 Combining CTLA-4 inhibitor ipilimumab with PD-1 inhibitors in advanced melanoma shows higher and deeper response rates compared with monotherapies, but it also increases immune-related toxicities.7 10 14 Therefore, novel immune strategies are needed to achieve better efficacy and a more favorable safety profile.

Recombinant human interleukin (IL)-2 (aldesleukin) is a pleiotropic cytokine approved in 1992 for metastatic renal cell carcinoma and in 1998 for metastatic melanoma. IL-2 activation of the IL-2 receptor βγ complex causes expansion of effector T cells and natural killer (NK) cells. However, its clinical use is limited by binding to IL-2 receptor α subunit (IL-2Rα or CD25) on regulatory T cells (Tregs), which suppresses immune response and on cells like vascular endothelium, type 2 innate lymphoid cells, and eosinophils, leading to toxicities such as vascular leak syndrome. In addition, the short half-life of aldesleukin requires frequent dosing to maintain therapeutic levels.15

Pegenzileukin (SAR444245) is a recombinant human IL-2 variant that is site-specifically pegylated, with IL-2 conjugated to a 30 kDa polyethylene glycol (PEG) molecule. The site-specific pegylation provides a “non-alpha” pharmacologic profile for pegenzileukin, which is designed to prevent engagement of the IL-2Rα chain while retaining engagement of IL-2Rβγ, thereby maintaining effector T cell and NK cell expansion without stimulating Tregs.16 This key difference sets it apart from NKTR-214 (bempegaldesleukin), which employs six PEG chains, resulting in limited binding to IL-2Rα while preferential binding to IL-2Rβγ, with some expansion of Tregs noted in periphery.17 Pegenzileukin monotherapy was tested at doses as high as 40 µg/kg with only mild cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and three dose limiting toxicities,18 whereas bempegaldesleukin was stopped at around 12 µg/kg due to hypotension, syncope or CRS observed in patients.17 In naïve non-tumor bearing mice, administration of pegenzileukin resulted in an increased half-life and expansion of peripheral CD8+T cells and NK cells, without significantly expanding Tregs relative to aldesleukin. In syngeneic B16-F10 tumor-bearing mice, pegenzileukin resulted in tumor-infiltrating CD8+T cells and NK cells and dose-dependent reduction in tumor growth.15 The interim analysis from the HAMMER trial, which is the first-in-human study, showed initial efficacy and a tolerable safety profile with pegenzileukin monotherapy and in combination with PD-1 inhibitor, pembrolizumab, in advanced and metastatic solid tumors.19

In this study, we report the results from the Pegathor Skin 201 phase 1/2 study to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and pharmacokinetics, of pegenzileukin in combination with cemiplimab (anti-PD-1) for the treatment of advanced and metastatic melanoma and CSCC.

Methods

Study design

This phase 1/2, multicohort, uncontrolled, non-randomized, open-label, multicenter study was conducted at 24 centers across seven countries (USA, Australia, Chile, France, Germany, Italy, and Spain).

The study started with a 28-day screening period, followed by a continuous treatment until disease progression (PD), unacceptable adverse events (AEs), other discontinuation criteria, or completion of 35 cycles, with an end of treatment (EOT) visit occurring 30 days after the last treatment or before starting new therapy. This was followed by an observation period with follow-up assessments every 3 months and follow-up to monitor patient’s survival (online supplemental figure S1).

The drug combination, pegenzileukin with cemiplimab, was tested in patients with melanoma (Cohort A) and CSCC (Cohort B). Both drugs were administered every 3 weeks (Q3W) on day 1 of each 21-day cycle for up to 35 cycles. Pegenzileukin was given as an intravenous infusion over 30 min, at two dose levels (DL) of 16 µg/kg (DL1: Cohort A1 and B1) and 24 µg/kg (DL2: Cohort A2 and B2) during dose escalation and recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) during dose expansion. Cemiplimab was given over a 30-minute intravenous infusion at a fixed dose of 350 mg.

The study began with a dose escalation phase to determine the RP2D of pegenzileukin in combination with cemiplimab. Two dose levels (16 and 24 µg/kg) at Q3W schedule were selected based on the therapeutic window established in the HAMMER trial, where doses up to 40 µg/kg were tolerated and minimal dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) observed.19 20 Unlike historical high-dose IL-2 regimens where cumulative toxicity often led to early treatment discontinuation, the dose range in this study was selected based on prior tolerability data from the HAMMER trial rather than traditional toxicity-driven escalation. Dose escalation followed the modified toxicity probability interval 2 design. The starting dose was 16 µg/kg Q3W (DL1), with potential adjustments to 8 µg/kg (DL1) or 24 µg/kg (DL2) based on DLTs and overall safety assessments. Each dose cohort included at least three patients for confirming DLT and at least six for the RP2D.

This study was terminated on October 21, 2022, for non-safety reasons. The decision was driven by a strategic shift toward evaluating more intensive dose schedules in separate studies, as informed by the evolving landscape. No safety or efficacy concerns prompted this decision. At the time of termination, the expansion phase for Cohort A and Cohort B had been initiated, and all participants enrolled to study at the time of study termination were allowed to continue treatment until protocol-defined treatment discontinuation criteria were met.

The study was approved by all local institutional review boards and was performed in accordance with Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04913220). All patients provided written informed consent.

Patient eligibility

Adult patients with histologically confirmed unresectable locally advanced or metastatic melanoma (Cohort A) without prior systemic therapy, or histologically confirmed metastatic or locally advanced CSCC unsuitable for curative surgery or radiation (Cohort B), who have received no more than two lines of systemic therapy. Patients must have at least one measurable lesion (per Response Evaluation Criteria In Solid Tumors (RECIST) V.1.1 criteria for Cohort A and RECIST V.1.1 criteria or modified WHO criteria for Cohort B). Females of childbearing potential had to use effective contraception and not be pregnant or breastfeeding; males had to use contraception and avoid sperm donation during treatment. Patients are excluded if they had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of ≥2, predicted life expectancy ≤3 months, poor organ function, baseline SpO2 ≤92%, active brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease, history of allogenic transplant, prior antitumor therapy (within 28 days or less than five times the half-life, whichever is shorter), major surgery or local intervention within 28 days, lung disease, comorbidity requiring corticosteroid therapy, recent antibiotic use (within 14 day of start of therapy), severe cardiac condition, active autoimmune disease, or a second malignancy. Additional exclusions include prior immune checkpoint inhibitors except in the context of adjuvant or neoadjuvant, recent adjuvant/neoadjuvant therapy.

Study assessments

The primary objective was to determine the antitumor activity of pegenzileukin in combination with cemiplimab. The secondary objectives included the assessments of DLTs, the safety profile and the RP2D, pharmacokinetics (PK). The exploratory objectives involved monitoring immune cell expansion, cytokine levels and other biomarkers of responses.

The efficacy endpoints, the objective response rate (ORR, defined as the proportion of patients who have a confirmed complete response (CR) or partial response (PR)), time to response (defined as time from the first drug administration to the first tumor assessment with response of PR or CR), duration of response (defined as time from the first confirmed CR or PR until documented PD or death from any cause, whichever comes first), clinical benefit rate (confirmed CR or PR at any time or stable disease (SD) of at least 6 months), progression-free survival (PFS, defined as time from first drug administration to the first documented disease progression or death due to any cause, whichever comes first) were determined by investigator per RECIST V.1.1 criteria in Cohort A patients or per RECIST V.1.1 or modified WHO criteria, or composite criteria in Cohort B patients.

The incidence of DLTs was monitored during the 21-day observation period of the first cycle. Events considered as DLTs, unless due to disease progression or unrelated to pegenzileukin, included hematologic abnormalities such as Grade 4 neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, Grade 3 thrombocytopenia requiring transfusion, and any Grade 4 thrombocytopenia. Non-hematologic abnormalities included Grade 3 or higher increases in alanine transaminase (ALT) and aspartate transaminase (AST), vascular leak syndrome, hypotension, CRS, any AE not resolving to Grade 2 or lower within 7 days of standard medical management, and Grade 4 laboratory abnormalities. Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were collected from informed consent until 30 days following last study treatment, with serious adverse events (SAEs) and adverse events of special interest (AESIs) collected until 90 days post-treatment and reported within 24 hours. SAEs and AESIs were followed until resolution or stabilization (no change for ≥3 months), and AEs of Grade >1 were followed until resolution to Grade 0–1 or initiation of new antineoplastic therapy, whichever occurred first. TEAEs and laboratory abnormalities, SAEs were assessed per National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI CTCAE) V.5.0, with CRS graded per American Society for Transplantation and Cellular Therapy (ASTCT) consensus criteria. Dose modification rules for pegenzileukin and cemiplimab are summarized across common toxicity types (online supplemental table S1). Attribution of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) to individual agents was challenging given the overlapping mechanisms of PD-1 inhibition and IL-2 pathway activation. When an AE was considered immune-related, both agents were held per-protocol. Investigators used clinical judgment and established case definitions for PD-1 inhibitor-associated irAEs (diarrhea/colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, and other organ-specific toxicities). Where toxicity was attributed to the combination or pegenzileukin alone, re-initiation of cemiplimab monotherapy could be considered after sponsor consultation.

Blood samples were taken at selected time points for a sparse PK evaluation of pegenzileukin and cemiplimab (days 1, 2, and 3) over the first cycle and then mainly at end of infusion in cycles 2, 4, 7, and 10. Pegenzileukin (plasma) and cemiplimab (serum) concentrations were determined using validated liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry assay (pegenzileukin) or an immunoassay with lower limits of quantification of 5 ng/mL (pegenzileukin) and 78 ng/mL (cemiplimab). Results were summarized using descriptive statistics.

Peripheral blood samples and tumor biopsy were collected during the study for exploratory biomarker analyses to assess target engagement, as well as immune modulatory PD effects of pegenzileukin.

For whole blood immunophenotyping flow cytometric analysis, peripheral blood samples were collected in Cyto-Chex BCT tubes at specified time points during cycle 1 and cycle 2. T cell, NK cell proliferation and activation, as well as the T cells, B cells, NK cells (TBNK) true count panels were used. These panels included key immune, proliferation-related and activation-related markers, such as CD45, CD3, CD4, CD8, CD25, FoxP3, CD16, CD56, Ki67, that can detect proliferating CD8 T cells (Ki67+CD8+), proliferating NK cells (Ki67+CD56+), and Tregs (CD25+FoxP3+). The TBNK panel can measure absolute cell count of CD3+, CD4+, CD8+T cells, CD19+B cells, and CD16+CD56+ NK cells. After red blood cell lysis, cells were stained using fluorescently labeled antibodies specific for the surface markers listed above. Samples were subsequently fixed, permeabilized, and then stained with intracellular antibodies. Stained samples were analyzed and resulting data were then reported by the testing laboratory (CellCarta, Montreal, Canada).

Cytokines levels, including tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, IL-6, IL-5, IL-8, interferon (IFN)-γ, were measured at specified time points during cycle 1 and cycle 2 in human serum samples using MSD V-PLEX Plus Proinflammatory Panel 1 and Cytokine Panel Human Kits from baseline to post-treatment by testing laboratory (CellCarta, Montreal, Canada).

Tumor tissues were analyzed to assess changes in infiltrating immune cells (eg, CD8+T cells, CD56+NK cells) and immune markers (eg, programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) and PD-1) in tumor formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue (FFPE). Specimens were stained using multiplex Immunohistochemistry (IHC) panels that contained T and NK cell-specific antibodies, such as CD8 (clone C8/14BB), CD56 (clone MRQ-42). Samples were analyzed by Navigate BioPharma (Carlsbad, California, USA). In addition, FFPE tumor samples were analyzed using single-plex IHC to stain PD-L1 (clone 22C3) and PD-1 (rabbit clone EPR4877)2 markers, which was performed by Mosaic Laboratories (Lake Forest, California, USA).

Statistical analysis

The exposed population includes all patients who provided informed consent and received at least one dose (even incomplete) of study drug (pegenzileukin or cemiplimab). The efficacy population includes all patients from the exposed population with at least one evaluable post-baseline tumor assessment or who have permanently discontinued study treatment. The DLT-evaluable population includes all patients in the dose escalation phase who have been treated and observed for at least 21 days, including those who experienced a DLT. The PK and pharmacodynamic (PDy) population include all patients from the exposed population with at least one PK and PDy assessment, respectively, available after the first dose of study drug.

The ORR was summarized for the efficacy population with descriptive statistics. In addition, a two-sided 90% CI was computed using the Clopper-Pearson method. Time to response, duration of response, and clinical benefit rate were assessed in patients who achieved confirmed objective response in the efficacy population. The PFS was summarized using the Kaplan-Meier method. The median PFS times and associated 90% CI were also provided. 90% CI were constructed using a log–log transformation of the survival function and the methods of Brookmeyer and Crowley. Safety and biomarker results were summarized for the exposed population using descriptive statistics.

Boxplots with data points and lines were used to visualize the distribution of immune cells at baseline and peak of post baseline to reveal the change in trend after treatment. Box center and upper/lower lines indicate the median and upper/lower quartile, respectively. Vertical lines above and below the box indicate 1.5 times the IQR. One-sided paired t-test was performed to test the significance of immune cells expansion at peak after treatment.

Results

Patient population and drug exposure

As of the data cut-off date (September 21, 2023), a total of 46 patients were enrolled across all four cohorts. A total of 19 participants, 12 in Cohort A and 7 in Cohort B, were enrolled during the dose escalation phase. At the time of data cut-off, 8 out of 27 patients with melanoma (Cohort A) and 6 out of 19 patients with CSCC (Cohort B) were still on treatment (online supplemental table S2).

Patients in Cohort A and Cohort B were generally older (≥60 years), predominantly male, had lower body mass index, and lower performance status scores (table 1). The median time from diagnosis to first administration of pegenzileukin+cemiplimab was ≤2 years in all groups. The majority of patients (>80%) in all groups were in advanced stages of cancer (Stage III-IV). Tumor was mostly metastatic in Cohort A, while in Cohort B, they were both metastatic and locally advanced. Few patients received prior anticancer treatment in all cohorts (table 1).

Duration of exposure was 4.8 and 10.3 months in Cohort A1 and A2 and 10.9 and 4.8 months in Cohort B1 and B2, respectively, for pegenzileukin 16 and 24 µg/kg doses (online supplemental table S3). More patients in these cohorts completed more cycles—eight patients (40.0%) in Cohort A1 (pegenzileukin 16 µg/kg+cemiplimab), four patients (57.1%) in Cohort A2 (pegenzileukin 24 µg/kg+cemiplimab) completed at least 11 cycles, and eight patients (50.0%) in Cohort B1 (pegenzileukin 16 µg/kg+cemiplimab) completed at least 16 cycles (online supplemental table S3).

Efficacy

At RP2D of pegenzileukin 16 µg/kg, the ORR was 40% (8/20) (90% CI 21.7 to 60.6) in Cohort A1 and 56.3% (9/16) (90% CI 33.3 to 77.3) in Cohort B1. In Cohort A1, out of 20 patients, 1 achieved a best overall response (BOR) of confirmed CR with a time to CR of 4.1 months, and 7 achieved PR. SD was the BOR in 5 of 20 patients (25%). In Cohort B1, out of 16 patients, 9 achieved a BOR of PR, with none achieving CR. SD was the BOR in 2 of 16 patients (12.5%). A shorter time to response was observed with RP2D pegenzileukin in Cohort A1 (2.1 months) compared with Cohort B1 (2.8 months) (table 2).

Safety

A total of five DLT events occurred across the cohorts. In Cohort A1, one DLT of Grade 3 thrombocytopenia was reported in a patient receiving a pegenzileukin dose of 16 µg/kg combined with cemiplimab, and one case of Grade 2 CRS was reported in Cohort A2 at a higher dose of 24 µg/kg. The CRS occurred on cycle 1 day 2, with the patient presenting with hypoxia, hypotension, fever, shiver, and transient loss of consciousness requiring hospitalization. The pegenzileukin dose was reduced at cycle 2; no action was taken with cemiplimab. Symptoms resolved within 2–4 days with supportive care including corticosteroids and oxygen therapy. In Cohort B, DLTs of Grade 3 ALT and AST increases were reported in one patient at a pegenzileukin dose of 16 µg/kg (Cohort B1), and one DLT of Grade 3 infusion-related reaction (IRR) was reported in a patient at a dose of 24 µg/kg (Cohort B2).

Across all the four cohorts, IRRs were the most frequent reported TEAEs. Other common TEAEs included CRS, hypertension, diarrhea, nausea, pruritus, asthenia, fatigue, and weight loss (table 3 and online supplemental table S4). The most frequently reported infusion reaction symptom in each cohort was fever, all of which were Grade 1 or 2. Most TEAEs in the infusion reaction category occurred on the day of infusion and resolved within 3 days. Details of infusion reactions are provided in online supplemental table S5. The most frequent AESIs were IRR (n=12 in Cohort A and n=16 in Cohort B), CRS (n=2, each in Cohort A and Cohort B), and COVID-19 (n=3, each in Cohort A and Cohort B).

The recommended dose of pegenzileukin was selected based on the safety and efficacy data from 19 patients who participated in the dose escalation phase, with 9 and 10 patients receiving pegenzileukin at doses of 16 µg/kg and 24 µg/kg, respectively. Although the number of TEAEs meeting DLT criteria was similar for both doses, the overall safety profile favored the 16 µg/kg dose when combined with cemiplimab, due to slightly increased severity of immune-related TEAEs at the higher dose (CRS, Cohort A2 vs A1: 3/7 (42.9%) vs 4/20 (20.0%) and Cohort B2 vs B1: 1/3 (33.3%) vs 3/16 (18.8%) for higher vs lower doses, respectively). Therefore, pegenzileukin at 16 µg/kg with cemiplimab 350 mg Q3W was selected as the preferred RP2D, and enrollment continued at this dose level.

Four patients in Cohort A1 receiving pegenzileukin at a dose of 16 µg/kg died, with two deaths due to PD and two due to AEs (immune-mediated myocarditis and pulmonary embolism). Additionally, two patients in Cohort A2 at a dose of 24 µg/kg died from progressive disease. In Cohort B1, two patients receiving pegenzileukin at a dose of 16 µg/kg also died due to PD.

In Cohort A1 (pegenzileukin 16 µg/kg+cemiplimab), nine patients (45.0%) experienced at least one Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent SAE, with immune-mediated myocarditis reported in three patients (15.0%), all of which were Grade ≥3 events. Two cases occurred in male patients aged 18–64 years with metastatic melanoma. The first presented on cycle 2 day 21 with asymptomatic myocarditis detected through routine monitoring, showing elevated troponin and reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) (50%). Following corticosteroid treatment, the patient recovered with LVEF normalizing to 60%. The second presented on cycle 1 day 22 with Grade 4 myocarditis and myositis, elevated troponin, transaminases, and ECG abnormalities. Despite intensive treatment including corticosteroids and cardiac support, the patient died due to refractory cardiogenic shock on day 40. Both study drugs were permanently discontinued in both cases, and events were assessed as related to both SAR444245 and cemiplimab. The third case occurred in a female patient aged ≥85 years with metastatic amelanotic melanoma, diabetes and hypertension on cycle 6 day 3, presenting with dyspnea and fatigue, elevated troponin, and pleural effusions. Following prednisone treatment and temporary drug delay, the patient fully recovered by cycle 6 day 33 and successfully resumed therapy through cycle 17. The event was assessed as related to both SAR444245 and cemiplimab. At the higher dose in Cohort A2 (pegenzileukin 24 µg/kg+cemiplimab), two patients (28.6%) had at least one Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent SAE, and CRS was reported in three patients (42.9%), though none were Grade ≥3. In Cohort B1 (pegenzileukin 16 µg/kg+cemiplimab), seven patients (43.8%) experienced at least one Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent SAE, with IRRs reported in two patients (12.5%), neither of which were Grade ≥3. At the higher dose in Cohort B2 (pegenzileukin 24 µg/kg+cemiplimab), one of three patients (33.3%) experienced a Grade 4 IRR.

Pharmacokinetics

No signs for effect of co-administered drugs on pegenzileukin concentrations in plasma were observed. Overall, pegenzileukin concentrations in plasma were in the expected range for the doses administered in study.

Biomarker analysis

Treatment with pegenzileukin (16 and 24 µg/kg dose level) and cemiplimab led to a robust expansion of NK and CD8 T cells, along with significant upregulation of proliferating (Ki67+) NK and CD8 T cells at the peak post-treatment in patients’ blood. For NK cells, the increase was higher with the higher dose group 24 µg/kg compared with the 16 µg/kg group. At the same time, there was no significant modulation of Tregs (figure 3). Cytokine levels (IFN-γ, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-ɑ) were elevated following treatment regardless of CRS occurrence, suggesting cytokine elevation represents an expected pharmacodynamic effect of IL-2 therapy rather than a specific marker of CRS (online supplemental table S6).

IHC analysis did not show any trend of association between PD-1, PD-L1, and CD8 expression with tumor burden reduction. Additionally, a very low number of NK cells were observed in the tumor microenvironment (online supplemental figure S3).

Discussion

This phase 1/2 study demonstrated that the combination of pegenzileukin administered Q3W at the RP2D of 16 µg/kg with cemiplimab (350 mg) has a generally manageable safety profile in patients with advanced and metastatic melanoma or CSCC.

At the RP2D, this combination therapy showed efficacy that was comparable to previous studies on PD-1 inhibitors, with around 40% ORR in melanoma and 50% ORR in CSCC.91113 The efficacy profile was better in the CSCC cohort B1, with an ORR of 56.3% and 85.7% achieving a duration of response ≥12 months, compared with the melanoma Cohort A1, which had an ORR of 40% and 75% achieving a duration of response ≥12 months. Bempegaldesleukin monotherapy showed no ORR17 while in combination with nivolumab it showed an ORR of 59.5% (22/37), with seven CR in patients with advanced solid tumors.21 Similarly, the combination of pegenzileukin and pembrolizumab demonstrated an antitumor response in the interim results of the HAMMER trial for advanced and metastatic solid tumors.19

In this evaluation of pegenzileukin and cemiplimab combination, common AEs associated with cemiplimab such as fatigue and diarrhea were observed.12 Additionally, IRRs were the most common TEAEs, followed by CRS. Similarly, a phase 1 dose-escalation trial on bempegaldesleukin, a CD122-preferential IL-2 agonist, in combination with PD-1 inhibitor nivolumab in 38 patients with advanced solid tumors, reported many of the same TEAEs, along with influenza-like symptoms, rash, fatigue, pruritus, arthralgia, decreased appetite, and headache.21 The most common infusion reaction in this study was fever, all Grade 1 or 2 in intensity, occurring on the day of infusion and resolving within 3 days. Notably, no events of capillary leak syndrome, which is common with aldesleukin therapy, were reported. This was consistent with another study of pegenzileukin in combination with PD-1 inhibitor pembrolizumab for advanced and metastatic solid tumors, where the most common TEAEs were influenza-like symptoms, fever, nausea, vomiting, and chills, all of which resolved with symptomatic treatment.19

Assessment of biomarkers showed a robust expansion of CD8 T cells and NK cells, with upregulation of proliferating Ki67+CD8 T cells and NK cells, without increasing Tregs, and increased serum levels of cytokines. These results were consistent with preclinical evidence15 16 and support the mechanism of action of pegenzileukin, which blocks the engagement of the IL-2Rα chain while retaining engagement of IL-2Rβγ. Similarly, the combination of pegenzileukin and pembrolizumab showed an expansion of CD8 T cells and NK cells, without increasing CD4 Tregs or eosinophil counts16 (associated with vascular leak syndrome), in advanced and metastatic solid tumors.19

This study has limitations inherent to open label, non-randomized, uncontrolled studies, including potential bias and limited generalizability. Lack of comparator arm for cemiplimab monotherapy, the small sample size and lack of statistical power limits the interpretation of results.

In conclusion, results from this phase 1/2 study showed that pegenzileukin in combination with cemiplimab at RP2D of 16 µg/kg has promising biomarker data supporting pegenzileukin’ mechanism of action involving the upregulation of CD8 T cells and NK cells, and a manageable safety profile. This combination showed initial efficacy; however, the study was terminated due to the sponsor’s decision. These findings could potentially help optimize immunotherapy regimens in the future for treatment of melanoma and CSCC.

Enumeration of interval-closed sets via Motzkin paths and quarter-plane walks
Sergi Elizalde, Nadia Lafrenière, Joel Brewster Lewis, Erin McNicholas, Jessica Striker, Amanda Welch

Abstract
We find a generating function for interval-closed sets of the product of two chains poset by constructing a bijection to certain bicolored Motzkin paths. We also find a functional equation for the generating function of interval-closed sets of truncated rectangle posets, including the type A A root poset, by constructing a bijection to certain quarter-plane walks.



1
Introduction

Interval-closed sets of partially ordered sets, or posets, are an interesting generalization of both order ideals (downward-closed subsets) and order filters (upward-closed subsets). Also called convex subsets, the interval-closed sets of a poset
P
are defined to be the subsets
I\subseteq P
such that if
x,y\in I
and there is an element
z
with
x<z<y
, then
z\in I
. In other words,
I
contains all elements of
P
between any two elements of
I
. Interval-closed sets are important in operations research and arise in applications such as project scheduling and assembly line balance

[
8
]
.

Although order ideals of posets have been well studied from enumerative, bijective, and dynamical perspectives, interval-closed sets have not received as much attention.
A recent paper
[
3
]
initiated the study of interval-closed sets of various families of posets from enumerative and dynamical perspectives.
In this paper, we continue to study the enumeration of interval-closed sets of specific families of posets, finding useful bijections along the way, while in the companion paper
[
6
]
, we extend the study of interval-closed set rowmotion dynamics.

The main results of the present paper include a generating function for interval-closed sets of the product of two chains poset
[m]\times[n]
, from which we extract explicit formulas for small values of
m
, and functional equations for the generating functions of interval-closed sets of truncated rectangle posets, a family that includes the type
A
root posets. In both cases, we define bijections from interval-closed sets to various kinds of lattice paths, namely, certain bicolored Motzkin paths and quarter-plane walks.

Our first main result, stated as Theorem
3.3
, is a bijection between the set of interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]
and the set of bicolored Motzkin paths with certain restrictions; specifically, the number of up steps and horizontal steps of the first color is
m
, the number of down steps and horizontal steps of the second color is
n
, and no horizontal step of the second color on the
x
-axis is followed by a horizontal step of the first color.
We use this bijection to find the following generating function.

Theorem
3.5
.

The generating function of interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]
is given by

\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\left|\mathcal{IC}([m]\times[n])\right|\,x^{m}y^{n}=\frac{2}{1-x-y+2xy+\sqrt{(1-x-y)^{2}-4xy}}.

One may use this generating function to extract counting formulas for fixed values of
m
, such as the following result.

Corollary
3.6
.

The cardinality of
\mathcal{IC}([3]\times[n])
is

\frac{n^{6}+9n^{5}+61n^{4}+159n^{3}+370n^{2}+264n+144}{144}.

Let
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
denote the type
B_{n}
minuscule poset (illustrated in Figure
7
), whose interval-closed sets are in bijection with vertically symmetric interval-closed sets of
[n]\times[n]
.

Theorem
3.9
.

The generating function of interval-closed sets of
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
is given by

\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|\mathcal{IC}(\mathfrak{B}_{n})\right|\,x^{n}=\frac{4-10x+8x^{2}}{2-11x+14x^{2}-8x^{3}-(2-3x)\sqrt{1-4x}}.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{n}
denote the type
A_{n}
positive root poset (illustrated in Figure
8
). In Theorem
4.1
, we construct a bijection between the set of interval-closed sets of
\mathbf{A}_{n-1}
and the set of lattice walks in the first quadrant that start and end at the origin and consist of
2n
steps from the set
\{(1,0),(-1,0),(1,-1),(-1,1)\}
, where no
(-1,0)
step on the
x
-axis is immediately followed by a
(1,0)
step.
We use this bijection to derive the following functional equation for the generating function.

Theorem
4.3
.

The generating function of interval-closed sets of
\mathbf{A}_{n-1}
can be expressed as

\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|\mathcal{IC}(\mathbf{A}_{n-1})\right|z^{2n}=F(0,0,z),

where
F(x,y):=F(x,y,z)
satisfies the functional equation

F(x,y)=1+z\left(x+\frac{1}{x}+\frac{x}{y}+\frac{y}{x}\right)F(x,y)-z\left(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{y}{x}\right)F(0,y)-z\,\frac{x}{y}F(x,0)-z^{2}\,\left(F(x,0)-F(0,0)\right).

We derive in Theorems
4.5
and
4.7
generalizations of these theorems to the poset obtained by truncating the bottom
d
ranks from
[m]\times[n]
. (Note that
\mathbf{A}_{n-1}
may be obtained by truncating the bottom
n
ranks from
[n]\times[n]
.)
We also find a similar functional equation in Theorem
4.4
for symmetric ICS of
\mathbf{A}_{n-1}
and use this to extract the enumeration of ICS of the type
B
positive root poset (illustrated in Figure
10

Definitions and background

Let
P
be a partially ordered set (poset). All posets in this paper are finite. Below we introduce the poset-theoretic definitions that are most relevant to this paper, and refer to

Definition 2.1
.

Let
I\subseteq P
. We say that
I
is an
interval-closed set (ICS)
of

x,y\in I
and

such that

z\in I
. Let
\mathcal{IC}(P)
denote the set of all interval-closed sets of
P
.

Definition 2.2
.

A subset

is an
order ideal
if whenever
b\in J
and
a\leq b
, we have
a\in J
. A subset

is an
order filter
if whenever
a\in K
and
a\leq b
, we have
b\in K
.
Given

, and let

denote the smallest order filter containing
S
.

Definition 2.3
.

The

. In this paper, we study the poset constructed as the

of two chains. Its elements are
[m]\times[n]=\{(i,j)\ |\ 1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n\}
, and the partial order is given by
(a,b)\leq(c,d)
if and only if
a\leq c
and
b\leq d
.

Our convention is to draw the Hasse diagram of
[m]\times[n]

(1,1)
at the bottom, incrementing the first coordinate in the northeast direction and the second coordinate in the northwest direction, as in Figure

Definition 2.4
.

An

. The

n

In

The number of interval-closed sets of

3
Interval-closed sets of rectangle posets and bicolored Motzkin paths

In this section, we prove Theorem
3.5
, which gives a generating function enumerating interval-closed sets of the poset
[m]\times[n]
. We begin by giving two bijections from interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]
to pairs of lattice paths. The first pair
(L,U)

consists of the
upper
and
lower
paths that trace out the smallest order ideal and order filter, respectively, containing an interval-closed set. We discuss this bijection and its implications in Subsection
3.1
. In Subsection
3.2
we give a bijection to the pair of paths
(B,T)
(
bottom
and
top
paths) which trace out, respectively, the largest order ideal that does not contain the ICS and the smallest order ideal that does contain the ICS.
We then prove Theorem
3.3
, which uses these paths to give a bijection between
\mathcal{IC}([m]\times[n])
and certain bicolored Motzkin paths. Subsection
3.3
uses this bijection to prove Theorem
3.5
.
Subsection
3.4
extracts the coefficients of this generating function for small parameter values, giving for example a formula for
\left|\mathcal{IC}([3]\times[n])\right|
. Subsection
3.5
translates statistics between interval-closed sets and Motzkin paths via the bijection of Theorem
3.3
. Finally, Subsection
3.6
proves Theorem
3.9
, giving a generating function for interval-closed sets of the type
B_{n}
minuscule poset, or, equivalently, vertically symmetric ICS in
[n]\times[n]

3.1
A bijection to pairs of paths

In this subsection, we associate a pair of paths
(L,U)
to each interval-closed set in
[m]\times[n]
. We then use these paths in Proposition
3.1
to show that certain interval-closed sets, which we call
full
, are enumerated by the Narayana numbers. Finally, we characterize in Lemma
3.2
several subsets of the poset in terms of these paths.

Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{m,n}
the set of lattice paths in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
from
(0,n)
to
(m+n,m)
with steps
\textnormal{{u}}=(1,1)
and
\textnormal{{d}}=(1,-1)
. It is well known that
\left|\mathcal{L}_{m,n}\right|=\binom{m+n}{m}
. There is a standard bijection between order ideals of
[m]\times[n]
and
\mathcal{L}_{m,n}
(see e.g.,
[
13
, Def. 4.14, Fig. 6]
). This bijection proceeds by constructing, on the dual graph of the Hasse diagram, a path that separates the order ideal from the rest of the poset. The path begins to the left of the leftmost poset element (
(1,n)
in poset coordinates), ends to the right of the rightmost poset element (
(m,1)
in poset coordinates), and consists of
m
up-steps
u
and
n
down-steps
d
.
(Note that the Cartesian coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which we use for the paths, are different from the coordinates that we use to refer to elements of the poset.)
A similar path may be constructed to separate an order filter from the rest of the poset.

Given an interval-closed set
I
of
[m]\times[n]
, let us describe how to associate a pair of lattice paths
(L,U)
to
I
. Let
U
be the path separating the order ideal
\Delta(I)
from the rest of the poset, and
L
be the path separating the order filter
\nabla(I)
from the rest of the poset.
Both paths begin at
\left(0,n\right)
, end at
\left(m+n,m\right)
, and consist of steps
\textnormal{{u}}=(1,1)
and
\textnormal{{d}}=(1,-1)
. Among all such paths, the
upper path

U
is the lowest path
that leaves all the elements of
I
below it,
while the
lower path

L
is the highest path
that leaves all the elements of
I
above it.
See Figure

for an example.

Proposition 3.1

The number of full interval-closed subsets of

N(m+n-1,n)=\frac{1}{m+n-1}\binom{m+n-1}{m}\binom{m+n-1}{n}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let the poset
P
be a full interval-closed set of
[m]\times[n]
.
Given
I\in\mathcal{IC}(P)

with lower path
L
and upper path
U
, one has the following characterization of the elements of

according to their position in relation to

and

•

and below

are exactly those in

•

the elements below both

\Delta{(I)}\setminus I

•

the elements above both

and

are exactly those in
\nabla{(I)}\setminus I
, and

•

the elements below
L
and above

are those that are incomparable with

.

Proof.

By definition, the elements of
P
below

are exactly those in the order ideal
\Delta{(I)}
, and the elements of
P

are exactly those in the order filter
\nabla{(I)}
.
An element

. Hence,

For the fourth statement, note that elements below

and above

are those in

that are neither larger nor smaller than any element in

3.2
From pairs of paths to bicolored Motzkin paths

In this subsection, we associate a slightly different pair of paths
(B,T)
to each interval-closed set in
[m]\times[n]

as an intermediate step towards a bijection between
\mathcal{IC}([m]\times[n])
and certain bicolored Motzkin paths.

As described in Section
3.1
, the set of order ideals of
[m]\times[n]
is in natural bijection with the set of lattice paths
\mathcal{L}_{m,n}
from
(0,n)
to
(m+n,m)
with steps
u
and
d
.
Let
J_{1},J_{2}
be order ideals of
[m]\times[n]
, and let
B,T\in\mathcal{L}_{m,n}
be their corresponding lattice paths. Then
J_{1}\subseteq J_{2}
if and only if
B
lies weakly below
T
. We will write this as
B\leq T
.
Let
\mathcal{L}^{2}_{m,n}=\{(B,T):B,T\in\mathcal{L}_{m,n},B\leq T\}
.

Our goal is to enumerate interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]
.
Any interval-closed set can be expressed as
J_{2}\setminus J_{1}
for some pair of order ideals
J_{1},J_{2}
such that
J_{1}\subseteq J_{2}
, and any such pair of order ideals determines an ICS. However,
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
are not unique in general; for example, the empty set can be written as
J\setminus J
for any order ideal
J
.

In general, given
(B,T)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{m,n}
, the steps where
B
and
T
coincide are irrelevant when determining the corresponding interval-closed set. This is because the interval-closed set has elements in the
i
th vertical “file” (i.e., elements
(a,b)\in[m]\times[n]
such that
b-a=i+n-1
) if and only if the
i
th step of
B
is strictly below the
i
th step of
T
.
Thus, interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]
are in bijection with equivalence classes of pairs
(B,T)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{m,n}
, where the equivalence relation allows us to freely change the portions of
B
and
T
where these two paths coincide, as long as we preserve the portions of
B
and
T
that are disjoint. To enumerate these equivalence classes, let us introduce another type of lattice paths.

Denote by
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\ell}
the set of
bicolored Motzkin paths
of length
\ell
. These are lattice paths from
(0,0)
to
(\ell,0)
that never go below the
x
-axis and consist of steps of four types:
\textnormal{{u}}=(1,1)
,
\textnormal{{d}}=(1,-1)
, and two kinds of horizontal steps
(1,0)
, which we will denote by
\textnormal{{h}}_{1}
and
\textnormal{{h}}_{2}
.
Denote by
u(M)
the number of
u
steps in
M
, and define
d(M)
,
h_{1}(M)
and
h_{2}(M)
similarly. Let
\mathcal{M}^{2}=\bigcup_{\ell\geq 0}\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\ell}
.

Consider the following well known bijection (see e.g.,
[
4
]
) between
\bigcup_{m+n=\ell}\mathcal{L}^{2}_{m,n}
and
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\ell}
.
Given
(B,T)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{m,n}
and
\ell=m+n
, let

M\in\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\ell}
be the path whose
i
th step
m_{i}
is determined by the
i
th steps of
B
and
T
, as follows:

m_{i}=\begin{cases}\textnormal{{u}}&\text{if $b_{i}=\textnormal{{d}}$ and $t_{i}=\textnormal{{u}}$},\\
\textnormal{{d}}&\text{if $b_{i}=\textnormal{{u}}$ and $t_{i}=\textnormal{{d}}$},\\
\textnormal{{h}}_{1}&\text{if $b_{i}=\textnormal{{u}}$ and $t_{i}=\textnormal{{u}}$},\\
\textnormal{{h}}_{2}&\text{if $b_{i}=\textnormal{{d}}$ and $t_{i}=\textnormal{{d}}$}.\end{cases}

(1)

Under this bijection, we have
(B,T)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{m,n}
if and only if
u(M)+h_{1}(M)=m
and
d(M)+h_{2}(M)=n
. Let
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{m,n}
denote the set of
M\in\mathcal{M}^{2}_{m+n}
such that
u(M)+h_{1}(M)=m
and
d(M)+h_{2}(M)=n
.

The fact that
B\leq T
guarantees that
M
stays weakly above the
x
-axis, and that steps where
B
and
T
coincide correspond to horizontal steps (
\textnormal{{h}}_{1}
or
\textnormal{{h}}_{2}
) of
M
that lie on the
x
-axis.
In particular, changing steps where
B
and
T
coincide (while preserving the portions where
B
and
T
are disjoint) corresponds to rearranging the horizontal steps of
M
within each maximal block of adjacent horizontal steps on the
x
-axis.
Thus, interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]
are in bijection with equivalence classes of paths in
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{m,n}
, where the equivalence relation is given by the above rearrangements. An easy way to pick one representative from each equivalence class is to consider paths where no
\textnormal{{h}}_{2}
on the
x
-axis is immediately followed by a
\textnormal{{h}}_{1}
, i.e., every block of horizontal steps on the
x
-axis is of the form
\textnormal{{h}}_{1}^{r}\textnormal{{h}}_{2}^{s}
for some
r,s\geq 0
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}_{\ell}
, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}_{m,n}
respectively be the sets of paths in
\mathcal{M}^{2}
,
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\ell}
, and
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{m,n}
with this property. In terms of the paths
(B,T)
, this convention for picking a representative corresponds to requiring the blocks where
B
and
T
coincide to be of the form
\textnormal{{u}}^{r}\textnormal{{d}}^{s}
. In particular, the resulting path
B
coincides with the path
L
of the previous subsection.

The above discussion yields the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

The set
\mathcal{IC}([m]\times[n])
of interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]
is in bijection with the set
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}_{m,n}
of bicolored Motzkin paths where no
\textnormal{{h}}_{2}
on the
x
-axis is immediately followed by a
\textnormal{{h}}_{1}
, and such that
u(M)+h_{1}(M)=m
and
\textnormal{{d}}(M)+h_{2}(M)=n
.

3.3
Deriving the generating function

In this subsection, we obtain an expression for the generating function

A(x,y)=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\left|\mathcal{IC}([m]\times[n])\right|\,x^{m}y^{n}

of interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]

Theorem 3.5
.

The generating function of interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]
is given by

A(x,y)=\frac{2}{1-x-y+2xy+\sqrt{(1-x-y)^{2}-4xy}}.

Proof.

Using the bijection of Theorem
3.3
, we can write

A(x,y)=\sum_{M\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}}x^{u(M)+h_{1}(M)}y^{d(M)+h_{2}(M)}.

We start by recalling the derivation of the generating function for bicolored Motzkin paths,

C(x,y)=\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}^{2}}x^{u(M)+h_{1}(M)}y^{d(M)+h_{2}(M)},

as in
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
. Any non-empty path in
\mathcal{M}^{2}
is either of the form
M=\textnormal{{h}}_{1}M^{\prime}
or
M=\textnormal{{h}}_{2}M^{\prime}
, where
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{2}
, or of the form
M=\textnormal{{u}}M_{1}\textnormal{{d}}M_{2}
, where
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathcal{M}^{2}
. This gives the equation

C(x,y)=1+(x+y)C(x,y)+xyC(x,y)^{2},

from which we conclude

C(x,y)=\frac{1-x-y-\sqrt{(1-x-y)^{2}-4xy}}{2xy}.

(2)

We now give a similar decomposition for non-empty paths in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}
. Paths that start with a horizontal step must be of the form
M=\textnormal{{h}}_{1}M^{\prime}
, where
M^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}
, or
M=\textnormal{{h}}_{2}M^{\prime}
, where
M^{\prime}
is any path in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}
that does not start with
\textnormal{{h}}_{1}
. Paths that start with an up-step are of the form
M=\textnormal{{u}}M_{1}\textnormal{{d}}M_{2}
, where
M_{1}\in\mathcal{M}^{2}
and
M_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{2}
.
This decomposition yields the equation

A(x,y)=1+xA(x,y)+y(A(x,y)-xA(x,y))+xyC(x,y)A(x,y),

from which we conclude

A(x,y)=\frac{1}{1-x-y+xy-xyC(x,y)}=\frac{2}{1-x-y+2xy+\sqrt{(1-x-y)^{2}-4xy}}.\qed

Equation (
2
) gives an alternative proof of Proposition
3.1
: via the bijection in Section
3.2
, full interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]
correspond to pairs
(B,T)
where

and

only touch at their endpoints, which in turn correspond to bicolored Motzkin paths that only touch the
x

\textnormal{{u}}M\textnormal{{d}}
, where

, and so their generating function is

xy\,C(x,y)=\frac{1-x-y-\sqrt{(1-x-y)^{2}-4xy}}{2}.

The coefficient of

in this generating function is

.

3.4
Extracting formulas for small parameter values

From the expression in Theorem
3.5
, one can obtain generating functions counting interval-closed sets of
[m]\times[n]
where one of the parameters is fixed.
For example, differentiating twice with respect to
x
, we have

\frac{\partial^{2}A(x,y)}{\partial x^{2}}=\sum_{m\geq 2,n\geq 0}m(m-1)\left|\mathcal{IC}([m]\times[n])\right|\,x^{m-2}y^{n}.

Setting
x=0
and using Theorem
3.5
, we get

\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|\mathcal{IC}([2]\times[n])\right|\,y^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\partial^{2}A(x,y)}{\partial x^{2}}\right|_{x=0}=\frac{1-y+3y^{2}-2y^{3}+y^{4}}{(1-y)^{5}}.

Extracting the coefficient of
y^{n}
gives

\left|\mathcal{IC}([2]\times[n])\right|=\binom{n+4}{4}-\binom{n+3}{4}+3\binom{n+2}{4}-2\binom{n+1}{4}+\binom{n}{4}=\frac{n^{4}+4n^{3}+17n^{2}+14n+12}{12},

recovering Theorem
2.6
.

Similarly, we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|\mathcal{IC}([3]\times[n])\right|\,y^{n}=\frac{1}{6}\left.\frac{\partial^{3}A(x,y)}{\partial x^{3}}\right|_{x=0}=\frac{1+5y^{2}-5y^{3}+6y^{4}-3y^{5}+y^{6}}{(1-y)^{7}},

from where we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.6
.

The cardinality of
\mathcal{IC}([3]\times[n])
is

\frac{n^{6}+9n^{5}+61n^{4}+159n^{3}+370n^{2}+264n+144}{144}.

In general, for any fixed
m
, we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|\mathcal{IC}([m]\times[n])\right|\,y^{n}=\frac{1}{m!}\left.\frac{\partial^{m}A(x,y)}{\partial x^{m}}\right|_{x=0},

which is a rational generating function, since the square roots in the partial derivatives of
A(x,y)
disappear when setting
x=0
. Extracting the coefficient of
y^{n}
gives an expression for
\mathcal{IC}([m]\times[n])
, which, according to our computations for
m\leq 10
, seems to be a polynomial in
n
of degree
2m
with non-negative coefficients.

4.4
Translating statistics between interval-closed sets and quarter-plane walks

Similarly to Theorem
3.7
, the quarter-plane walks obtained via the bijection from Theorem
4.5
give us information about the associated interval-closed set.
In the next theorem, a return of the walk to the
x
-axis is a
se
step ending on the
x
-axis, and a return to the
y
-axis is a
w
or
nw
step ending on the
y
-axis (we use this term even if the walk did not start on the

-axis).

Theorem 4.8
.

Let

, and let
W\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{n-r,m-r}_{m+n}
be its image under the bijection from Theorem
4.5
. Then,

(a)

the cardinality of
I
is the sum of the heights (
y
-coordinates) after each step of
W
,

(b)

the number of connected components of
I
is the number of returns of
W
to the
x
-axis, and

(c)

the number of minimal elements of
P_{m\times n;r}
that are in
I
is the number of returns of
W
to the
y
-axis, not counting its last step.

Proof.

Let
I\in\mathcal{IC}(P_{m\times n;r})
, and let
B
and
T
be the nested lattice paths from the bijection in Subsection
4.3
.
These are the same paths that we would have associated to
I
in Subsection
3.2
by viewing
I
as an interval-closed set of
[m]\times[n]
.
Therefore, as in the proof of Theorem
3.7
, we have
|I|=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}d_{i}(B,T)
, where
d_{i}(B,T)
is the distance between
B
and
T
after
i
steps.

Let us show that
\frac{1}{2}d_{i}(B,T)
is also the
y
-coordinate at the end of the
i
-th step of the walk
W
.
As in the proof of Theorem
3.7
,
\frac{1}{2}d_{i}(B,T)
is the difference between the number of
u
steps of
B
and
T
within their first
i
steps. Using equation (
4
), this difference is equal to the number of
nw
steps (which occur in positions where
T
has a

step but
B
does not) minus the number of
se
steps (which occur in positions where
B
has a
u
step but
T
does not) within the first
i
steps of
W
, which in turn equals the
y
-coordinate of
W
after
i
steps, noting that
W
starts on the
x
-axis.
Summing over

, we obtain part (a).

The connected components of

correspond to the maximal blocks where

is strictly below

, or equivalently,

is strictly above the

To prove part (c), observe that, after any given number of steps, the height of the path

always equals the

. This is because

starts at

and, by equation (

),
its

, and it is

.
An element of

returns to the

By construction, the path

that are in

correspond exactly to the returns of

to the

.
∎

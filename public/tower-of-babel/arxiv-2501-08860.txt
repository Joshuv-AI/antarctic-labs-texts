Supplemental Material for “Unconventional spin Hall effect in 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} symmetric spin-orbit coupled quantum gases”
Hui Tang, Guan-Hua Huang, Shizhong Zhang, Zhongbo Yan, Zhigang Wu

Abstract




This Supplemental Material includes the following two sections: (I) spin Hall conductivity of the Bose condensate and (II) numerical simulations of the unconventional SHE.

I
Spin Hall conductivity of the Bose condensate

In this section, we provide more details on the derivation of Eq. (7) of the main text for the spin Hall conductivity of the Bose condensate in the in-plane magnetized phase, in the absence of the harmonic trap.
We begin with the many-body Hamiltonian for
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}
symmetric spin-orbit coupled Bose gas with anisotropic interactions

\hat{H}=\int d^{2}\bm{r}\,\left[\sum_{\sigma}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}(\bm{r})[h(\bm{r})-\mu_{B}]\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}(\bm{r})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}g_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\hat{\psi}_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\hat{\psi}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{r})\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}(\bm{r})\right],

(S1)

where
\mu_{B}
is the chemical potential and the interaction strengths satisfy
g_{\uparrow\uparrow}=g_{\downarrow\downarrow}>g_{\uparrow\downarrow}
. The condensate wave function
\langle\hat{\bm{\psi}}\rangle=\sqrt{N}{\bm{\Phi}}
, where
\hat{\bm{\psi}}\equiv(\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow},\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow})^{T}
and
{\bm{\Phi}}
is normalized to unity, is determined by the Gross-Pitaevskii equation (5) of the main text.

Figure S1:
The magnetization density
\bm{m}_{l}(\bm{r})
of each
{\bm{\Phi}}_{l}
, shown in an area containing four lattice sites, where the uniform background color indicates a vanishing
z
-component of the magnetization. Here the parameters are the same as Fig. 2 of the main text, i.e.,
V_{0}=8E_{r}
,
M_{0}=1.5E_{r}
,
\rho g_{\uparrow\uparrow}=0.26E_{r}
and
\rho g_{\uparrow\downarrow}=0.22E_{r}
.

In the in-plane magnetized phase, the condensate wave function is a equal-weight supposition of the degenerate Bloch states at the
\Gamma
point, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\bm{\Phi}}(\bm{r})=\sum_{n}\left[c_{n}{\bm{\phi}}_{n0}(\bm{r})+c_{\bar{n}}{\bm{\phi}}_{\bar{n}0}(\bm{r})\right],

(S2)

where
{\bm{\phi}}_{\bar{n}0}(\bm{r})=\mathcal{PT}{\bm{\phi}}_{n0}(\bm{r})
is the
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric partner of
{\bm{\phi}}_{n0}(\bm{r})
and
|c_{n}|=|c_{\bar{n}}|
. For the system parameters considered by us, we find that
|c_{0}|^{2}+|c_{\bar{0}}|^{2}>0.99
and so the atoms essentially condensate in the lowest two degenerate Bloch states. Furthermore, it turns out that the relative phase between these two states can take four possible values
\theta_{l}=l\pi/2-\pi/4
(
l=1,\cdots,4
), leading to a four-fold degeneracy in the condensate mode. Namely, to a very good approximation, the four degenerate condensate modes are given by

\displaystyle{\bm{\Phi}}_{l}(\bm{r})\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[{\bm{\phi}}_{00}(\bm{r})+e^{i\theta_{l}}{\bm{\phi}}_{\bar{0}0}(\bm{r})\right].

(S3)

Each of these four modes has a distinctive magnetization density defined as

\displaystyle{\bm{m}}_{l}(\bm{r})={\bm{\Phi}}^{\dagger}_{l}(\bm{r}){\bm{s}}{\bm{\Phi}}_{l}(\bm{r}),

(S4)

the integration of which yields the total magnetization

{\bm{M}}_{l}=\int d^{2}\bm{r}\,{\bm{m}}_{l}(\bm{r})
. The magnetization densities for these four modes are shown in Fig.
S1
.

To determine the elementary excitations, we substitute

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}(\bm{r})=\sqrt{N}\Phi_{\sigma}(\bm{r})+\delta\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}(\bm{r})

(S5)

into the Hamiltonian (
S1
) to obtain Bogoliubov de Gennes (BdG) Hamiltonian

\hat{H}_{B}=\int d^{2}\bm{r}\,\bm{\delta\hat{\psi}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\mathcal{H}_{B}(\bm{r})\bm{\delta\hat{\psi}}(\bm{r}),

(S6)

where
\bm{\delta\hat{\psi}}=(\delta\hat{\psi}^{T},\delta\hat{\psi}^{\dagger})^{T}
and

\mathcal{H}_{B}(\bm{r})=\begin{pmatrix}h_{0}-\mu_{B}+\mathcal{M}&\mathcal{N}\\
\mathcal{N}^{*}&h_{0}^{*}-\mu_{B}+\mathcal{M}^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

(S7)

The Bogoliubov Hamiltonian in Eq. (
S6
) can be brought into a diagonalized form by the Bogoliubov transformation

\delta\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}(\bm{r})=\sum_{n\bm{k}}u_{n\bm{k}\sigma}(\bm{r})\hat{\alpha}_{n\bm{k}}-v_{n\bm{k}\sigma}^{*}(\bm{r})\hat{\alpha}_{n\bm{k}}^{\dagger},

(S8)

where
\bm{k}
belongs to the first Brillouin zone and
n=0,1,2,...
is the band index;
\hat{\alpha}_{n\bm{k}}^{\dagger}
is the creation operator for the Bogoliubov quasiparticle, and
u_{n\bm{k}\sigma}
and
v_{n\bm{k}\sigma}
are the Bogoliubov amplitudes determined by Eq. (6) of the main text. The Bogoliubov amplitudes satisfy the normalization condition

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\bm{r}[{\bm{u}}^{\dagger}_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r}){\bm{u}}_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r})-{\bm{v}}^{\dagger}_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r}){\bm{v}}_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r})]=1,

(S9)

where
{\bm{u}}_{n\bm{k}}=(u_{n\bm{k}\uparrow},u_{n\bm{k}\downarrow})^{T}
and
{\bm{v}}_{n\bm{k}}=(v_{n\bm{k}\uparrow},v_{n\bm{k}\downarrow})^{T}
.

To derive Eq. (7) of the main text, we start from the definition

\sigma^{s}_{\mu\nu}=-\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{A\omega}{\rm Im}\chi_{\mu\nu}^{s}(\omega)

(S10)

where
A
is system’s area and
\chi_{\mu\nu}^{s}(\omega)
is the Fourier transform of the retarded correlation function

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu\nu}^{s}(t-t^{\prime})=-i\theta(t-t^{\prime})\langle[\hat{J}_{\mu}^{s}(t),\hat{J}_{\nu}(t^{\prime})]\rangle.

(S11)

The current and spin current operator are respectively

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{\mu}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2mi}\int d^{2}\bm{r}\,\left[\hat{\bm{\psi}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\sigma_{0}\partial_{\mu}\hat{\bm{\psi}}(\bm{r})-h.c.\right];

(S12)

\displaystyle\hat{J}^{s}_{\nu}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2mi}\int d^{2}\bm{r}\,\left[\hat{\bm{\psi}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\frac{\sigma_{z}}{2}\partial_{\nu}\hat{\bm{\psi}}(\bm{r})-h.c.\right].

(S13)

Substituting Eqs. (
S5
) and (
S8
) into the above equations, we find to the linear order in the quasi-particle operators

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{N}\sum_{n\bm{k}}\left[\mathcal{J}_{\mu,0n}(\bm{k})\hat{\alpha}_{n\bm{k}}+h.c.\right];

(S14)

\displaystyle\hat{J}^{s}_{\nu}

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{N}\sum_{n\bm{k}}\left[\mathcal{J}_{\mu,0n}^{s}(\bm{k})\hat{\alpha}_{n\bm{k}}+h.c.\right].

(S15)

Here

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(s)}_{\mu,0n}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\bm{r}\left[{\bm{\Phi}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})j_{\mu}^{(s)}{\bm{u}}_{n{\bm{k}}}(\bm{r})-{\bm{v}}^{T}_{n{\bm{k}}}(\bm{r})j_{\mu}^{(s)}{\bm{\Phi}}(\bm{r})\right]

\displaystyle\equiv\langle\bm{\Phi}|j_{\mu}^{(s)}|\bm{u}_{n\bm{k}}\rangle-\langle\bm{v}^{*}_{n\bm{k}}|j_{\mu}^{(s)}|\bm{\Phi}\rangle,

(S16)

where the single-particle current operators are
{\bm{j}}={\bm{p}}/m
and
{\bm{j}}^{s}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{s_{z},{\bm{j}}\right\}
. Because of quasi-momentum conservation we in fact have

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(s)}_{\mu,0n}(\bm{k})=\delta_{\bm{k},0}\mathcal{J}^{(s)}_{\mu,0n}(0).

(S17)

Using the spectrum representation and solutions of BdG equation, the spin Hall conductivity can then be expressed as

\displaystyle\sigma^{s}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{A\omega}{\rm Im}\sum_{n\neq 0}\left[\frac{\mathcal{J}^{s}_{\mu,0n}(0)\mathcal{J}_{\nu,n0}(0)}{\omega-\mathcal{E}_{n0}+\mathcal{E}_{00}+i0^{+}}-\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\nu,0n}(0)\mathcal{J}_{\mu,n0}^{s}(0)}{\omega+\mathcal{E}_{n0}-\mathcal{E}_{00}+i0^{+}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{A}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{{\rm Im}\left[\mathcal{J}^{s}_{\mu,0n}(0)\mathcal{J}^{*}_{\nu,0n}(0)\right]}{(\mathcal{E}_{n0}-\mathcal{E}_{00})^{2}}.

(S18)

II
Numerical simulations of the unconventional SHE

In this section we provide more details on the numerical simulations of the unconventional SHE in a harmonically trapped Bose condensate. In particular, we describe the preparation of the initial state and the condensate dynamics associated with the SHE. Our numerical simulations are performed for a quasi-2D two-component
87
Rb gas with
N\approx 3.2\times 10^{5}
atoms confined in a harmonic trap with frequency
\omega_{0}=2\pi\times 30
Hz
Sun et al. 2018
. Other relevant system parameters are
k_{L}=7.98\times 10^{6}
m
-1
,
V_{0}=8E_{r}
,
M_{0}=1.5E_{r}
,
g_{\uparrow\uparrow}\approx 1.99\times 10^{-11}
Hz
\cdot
m
2
and
g_{\uparrow\downarrow}=1.70\times 10^{-11}
Hz
\cdot
m
2
. For real space discretization, we use a
3000\times 3000
grid with an area of about
37\mu
m
\times

37\mu
m. For the time evolution of the Gross-Pitaevskii (GP) equation, the four-order splitting operator method is implemented in order to achieve high precision
Sornborger and Stewart 1999
;
Bao and Cai 2013
.

We prepare the system in the ground state
{\bm{\Phi}}_{\rm tr}
, obtained from solving the time-independent GP equation

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\left\{[h_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{r})+V_{\rm tr}(\bm{r})\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}]\Phi_{\sigma^{\prime}}+Ng_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}|\Phi_{\sigma^{\prime}}|^{2}\Phi_{\sigma}\right\}=\mu_{B}\Phi_{\sigma}

(S19)

using the method of imaginary time propagation. Since there is a four-fold degeneracy in the condensate mode, for concreteness, we choose the ground state that is adiabatically connected to
{\bm{\Phi}}_{1}
of the uniform system when we switch off the trap. In fact, we have made sure that system parameters at the center of the trap are identical to those of the uniform system in the main text, i.e., at the center of the trap we have
\rho g_{\uparrow\uparrow}=0.26E_{r}
and
\rho g_{\uparrow\downarrow}=0.22E_{r}
. Thus the condensate wave function of the trapped system at the center can be benchmarked against that of the uniform system. This comparison is shown in Fig.
S2
, where we find excellent agreement between these two. This agreement is one of the criteria we use to check the convergence of the ground state. For a global view of the condensate, we have plotted in Fig.
S3
the cell-averaged density
\rho(r)
corresponding to this initial state of the trapped condensate.

To probe the SHE, we displace the trap by a distance of
d=4.43\mu
m along the
x
-direction and numerically evolve the time-dependent GP equation

\displaystyle i{\partial_{t}\Phi_{\sigma}}(\bm{r},t)=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}[(h_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+{\tilde{V}}_{\rm tr}(\bm{r})\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}})\Phi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{r},t)+Ng_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}|\Phi_{\sigma^{\prime}}|^{2}\Phi_{\sigma}(\bm{r},t)]

(S20)

from the initial state
{\bm{\Phi}}(\bm{r},0)={\bm{\Phi}}_{\rm tr}
determined above. For convenience, we have placed the center of the initial trap
V_{\rm tr}(\bm{r})
at
x=-d
so that the center of the displaced trap
\tilde{V}_{\rm tr}(\bm{r})
is now at the origin, i.e.,
V_{\rm tr}(\bm{r})=\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{0}^{2}(\bm{r}+d\hat{\bm{x}})^{2}
and
\tilde{V}_{\rm tr}(\bm{r})=\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{0}^{2}r^{2}
. Examples of the condensate wave function
{\bm{\Phi}}(\bm{r},t)
at various times are shown in Fig.
S4
. These wave functions are then used to calculate the center of mass dynamics of the spin components as well as the currents shown in Fig. 3a and Fig. 3b of the main text. Finally, the simulation is repeated for trap displacements along all directions to determine the angular dependence of the spin Hall currents shown in Fig. 3d.

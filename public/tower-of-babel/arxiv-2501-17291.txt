Polyanalytic Hermite polynomials associated with the elliptic Ginibre model
Nizar Demni ∗, Zouhaïr Mouayn ∗ Aix-Marseille University CNRS Centrale Marseille I2M-UMR 7373, 39 Rue F. Joliot Curie, 13453 Marseille, France e-mail: nizar.demni@univ-amu.fr ⋄ Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Sciences and Technics (M’Ghila), Sultan Moulay Slimane University, BP. 523, Béni Mellal 23000, Morocco

Abstract
Motivated by the connection between the eigenvalues of the complex Ginibre matrix model and the magnetic Laplacian in the complex plane, we derive analogues of the complex Hermite polynomials for the elliptic Ginibre model. To this end, we appeal to squeezed creation and annihilation operators arising from the Bogoliubov transformation of creation and annihilation operators on the Bargmann-Fock space. The obtained polynomials are then expressed as linear combinations of products of Hermite polynomials and share the same orthogonality relation with holomorphic Hermite polynomials. Moreover, this expression allows to identify them with the 2 ​ D 2D -Hermite polynomials associated to a unimodular complex symmetric 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrix. Afterwards, we derive, for any Landau level, a closed formula for the kernel of the isometry mapping the basis of (rescaled) holomorphic Hermite polynomials to the corresponding complex Hermite polynomials. This kernel is also interpreted in terms of the two-photon coherent states and the metaplectic representation of the S ​ U ​ ( 1 , 1 ) SU(1,1) group.



1
Introduction and main results

1.1
The Ginibre model and complex Hermite polynomials

The complex Hermite polynomials
(H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z}))_{m,n\geq 0}
,
also called Itô Hermite polynomials, arise in various contexts including
probability theory, combinatorics and mathematical physics (see e.g. the
review paper
[
7
]
). They are planar polynomials in
(z,\overline{z})
which are orthogonal with respect to the two-dimensional Gaussian measure

\omega_{0}(z)dz:=\pi^{-1}e^{-|z|^{2}}dz,

(1.1)

dz
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C=R}^{2},
and may be defined
by the Rodrigues formula

H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z})=(-1)^{m+n}e^{|z|^{2}}\partial_{z}^{n}\partial_{\overline{z}}^{m}(e^{-|z|^{2}}).

(1.2)

In random matrix theory, the measure
\omega_{0}(z)dz
is closely related
to the so-called Ginibre model: a complex matrix whose entries are
independent standard Gaussian variables. Indeed, the eigenvalues of such a
matrix form a determinantal point process with respect to this measure (
[
6
]
). Letting the size of the Ginibre matrix tend to infinity, one obtains
the Ginibre determinantal point process whose kernel is
K_{0}(z,w)=e^{z\overline{w}}
. The latter is the reproducing kernel of Bargmann-Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{0}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
consisting of holomorphic
functions in the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{0}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\omega_{0}(z)dz)
. Beside, it turns out that
\mathcal{F}_{0}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
coincides with the null space

\mathcal{A}_{0}\left(\mathbb{C}\right):=\left\{\phi\in\mathfrak{H}_{0},\text{ }\widetilde{\Delta}\phi=0\right\}

(1.3)

of the second order differential operator

\widetilde{\Delta}:=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial\overline{z}}+\overline{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}}

(1.4)

called magnetic Laplacian
[
3
]
and whose spectrum in
\mathfrak{H}_{0}

consists of non negative integers
n=0,1,2,...
. For any
n\geq 0
, an
orthogonal basis of the
n
-th eigenspace

\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right):=\left\{\phi\in\mathfrak{H}_{0},\text{ }\widetilde{\Delta}\phi=n\phi\right\}

(1.5)

is given by the complex Hermite polynomials
(H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z}))_{m\geq 0}
and the corresponding reproducing kernel
[
3
]
is
expressed as

K_{n}(z,w)=K_{0}(z,w)L_{n}^{\left(0\right)}(|z-w|),\text{ }z,w\in\mathbb{C}\text{,}

(1.6)

where
L_{n}^{\left(0\right)}
is the
n
-th Laguerre polynomial. More
informatin on the
L^{2}
spectral theory of
\widetilde{\Delta}
can be
found in
[
13
]
. Since
K_{n}
is the kernel of an orthogonal projection,
then it gives rise to a determinantal point process (
[
11
]
, theorem 3,
p.12) referred to as Ginibre-type and studied in
[
8
]
,
[
14
]
and

[
12
]
.

1.2
Deformation: the elliptic Ginibre model

Let
\tau\in[0,1)
and let
U_{1},U_{2}
be two independent
matrices drawn from the
Gaussian Unitary Ensemble
(GUE). In this
respect, recall that the GUE consists of Hermitian matrices with Gaussian
independent entries. Then

J_{\tau}:=\sqrt{1+\tau}U_{1}+i\sqrt{1-\tau}U_{2}

(1.7)

is known as the elliptic Ginibre model
[
6
]
and interpolates between
the Ginibre model when
\tau=0
and GUE in the degenerate limit as
\tau\rightarrow 1^{-}
. Its eigenvalues process is again a determinantal point
process whose kernel is expressed through the rescaled Hermite polynomials

H_{m}(z,\tau):=\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{m/2}H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right),m\geq 0,

(1.8)

where the real Hermite polynomials

H_{m}\left(x\right)=m!\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}m\right\rfloor}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{\ell}\left(2x\right)^{m-2\ell}}{\ell!\left(m-2\ell\right)!},\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}\text{.}

(1.9)

are chosen to be orthogonal on
\mathbb{R}
with respect to the weight
e^{-x^{2}}
. The polynomials
H_{m}(z,\tau)
are orthogonal with respect to
the elliptic measure

\omega_{\tau}(z)d\nu\left(z\right):=\pi^{-1}e^{-(|z|^{2}-\tau\Re(z^{2}))/(1-\tau^{2})}dz.

(1.10)

In particular,
H_{m}(z,0)=z^{m},

m\geq 0
and
\left(H_{m}(z,\tau)\right)_{m\geq 0}
still belongs to the null (Bargmann-Fock) space of the
magnetic Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}
for any
\tau\in\left[0,1\right)
. It is therefore natural to raise the following question: given a Landau
level
n\geq 1,
what are the ‘natural’ deformed analogues, say
\left(H_{m,n}\left(z,\overline{z},\tau\right)\right)_{m\geq 0}
of the complex
Hermite polynomials
\left(H_{m,n}\left(z,\overline{z}\right)\right)_{m\geq 0}?.
By ‘natural’, we here mean the sought polynomials must ensure
the commutativity of the following diagram:

\begin{array}[]{lcr}H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},\tau)&\overset{\tau=0}{\longrightarrow}&H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z})\\
.&.&.\\
n=0\big\downarrow&.&n=0\big\downarrow\\
.&.&.\\
H_{m}(z,\tau)&\overset{\tau=0}{\longrightarrow}&z^{m}\end{array},

(1.11)

and must be
n
-polyanalytic for any fixed
n\geq 0
as well. In other
words, they should satisfy

\partial_{\overline{z}}^{n+1}H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},\tau)=0,\quad m\geq 0,

(1.12)

since belonging to the
n
-th eigenspace
\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
of the operator
\widetilde{\Delta}.

By the virtue of
\left(1.2\right)
, it is also tempting to define ‘
à la Rodriguez
’ the polynomials

(-1)^{m+n}[\omega_{\tau}(z)]^{-1}\partial_{z}^{n}\partial_{\overline{z}}^{m}(\omega_{\tau}(z)),\quad m,n\geq 0,

(1.13)

which clearly reduce to
H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z})
when
\tau=0
. However, a
quick inspection shows that the polynomials in
\left(1.13\right)
are no
longer holomorphic at the lowest Landau level
n=0
and for any
\tau\in(0,1)
. Another attempt suggested by the expansion of the complex Hermite
polynomials

H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z})=\sum_{k=0}^{m\wedge n}(-1)^{k}k!\binom{m}{k}\binom{n}{k}z^{m-k}\overline{z}^{n-k},

(1.14)

would substitutes holomorphic monomials by
holomorphic Hermite polynomials as follows

\sum_{k=0}^{m\wedge n}(-1)^{k}k!\binom{m}{k}\binom{n}{k}H_{m-k}(z,\tau)\overline{z}^{n-k},\quad m,n,\geq 0,

(1.15)

which are obviously
n
-polyanalytic for any fixed
n\geq 0
. However, one
may prove for instance that the orthogonality with respect to
\omega_{\tau}
fails for
n=1
.

In order to find out the family of polynomials satisfying both the
orthogonality with respect to
\omega_{\tau},

\tau\in\left[0,1\right),
and the poly-analyticity property, one needs to combine:
\left(i\right)
the construction of the complex Hermite polynomials
H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z})
by means of creation and annihilation operators associated to the
magnetic Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}
(see e.g.,
[
8
]
) with
\left(ii\right)
the construction of the holomorphic Hermite polynomials by means
of the deformed (squeezed) operators
[
27
]
.

Actually, the first construction stems from the Heisenberg commutation
relation satisfied by creation and annihilation operators, which ensures
both needed properties (orthogonality and poly-analyticity). As to the
second construction, it exhibits squeezing of these operators while
preserving their commutation relation (this is known as the Bogoliubov
transformation). After careful computations, we obtain the following
representation of the deformed complex Hermite polynomials.

Theorem 1.1.
For any Landau level
n\geq 0,
an orthogonal basis of the eigenspace

\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right):=\left\{\phi\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{C},\omega_{\tau}\left(z\right)dz\right),\widetilde{\Delta}\phi=n\phi\right\}

(1.16)

is given by the following squeezed complex Hermite polynomials:

H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z};\tau)=\frac{\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{\left(m+n\right)/2}}{\sqrt{m!}}\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge m}i^{n-k}k!\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{2^{k}\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)^{k/2}}{\tau^{k}}H_{m-k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)H_{n-k}\left(\frac{i\left(\overline{z}-\tau z\right)}{\sqrt{2\tau\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)}}\right)

(1.17)

The formula
\left(1.17\right)
motivates the fact that
H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z};\tau)
may be related to the so-called
2D
-Hermite
polynomials
H_{m,n}^{\left(R\right)}
defined by their generating
function function
[
17
]
:

\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle R\gamma,\gamma\right\rangle+\left\langle R\xi,\gamma\right\rangle\right)=\sum\limits_{n,m=0}\frac{\gamma_{1}^{n}\gamma_{2}^{m}}{n!m!}H_{n,m}^{\left(R\right)}\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)

(1.18)

where
R
is a symmetric matrix,
\gamma=\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
\xi=\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Indeed, it is the case and the following relation holds.

Corollary 1.1
.
For any
m,n\geq 0,
the
polynomials
H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z};\tau)
may also be expressed
in terms of the
2D
-Hermite polynomial
H_{n,m}^{\left(R_{\tau}\right)}

as

H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z};\tau)=\frac{i^{n}}{\sqrt{m!}}H_{n,m}^{\left(R_{\tau}\right)}\left(\overline{z},\frac{i\left(\tau\overline{z}-z\right)}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}\right)

(1.19)

where
R_{\tau}\in SL\left(2,\mathbb{C}\right)
is the
symmetric matrix given by

R_{\tau}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tau&i\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\\
i\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}&\tau\end{array}\right).

(1.20)

Another result we shall prove in this paper provides an isometric map
T_{\tau,n}
from the subspace
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

of entire functions in the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{\tau}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\omega_{\tau}(z)dz)
into the
n
-th eigenspace
\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
of
\widetilde{\Delta}
in
\mathfrak{H}_{0}.
More precisely, this map is given by the following integral
transform.

Proposition 1.1.
T_{\tau,n}:\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

is defined by

T_{\tau,n}\left[\phi\right]\left(z\right)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)}\phi\left(w\right)\omega_{\tau}(w)dw

(1.21)

where the integral kernel is given by

W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)=\left(-\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\right)^{n}\frac{e^{\overline{z}w-\tau\overline{z}^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{n!}}H_{n}\left(\frac{w-z}{\sqrt{2\tau}}-\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right).

(1.22)

Using the symmetry relation
H_{n}\left(-x\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n}H_{n}\left(x\right),

x\in\mathbb{R},
the above kernel is
equivalently written as

W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\right)^{n}\frac{e^{\overline{z}w-\tau\overline{z}^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{n!}}H_{n}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}+\frac{z-w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right).

(1.23)

In particular,

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)=\frac{e^{\overline{z}w}}{\sqrt{n!}}\left(z-w\right)^{n}

(1.24)

which is the kernel of the transform
T_{0,n}:

\mathcal{A}_{0}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right).
For
\tau\in(0,1),
this kernel
W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)
is also
interpreted in terms of the two-photon coherent states
[
18
]
and the
metaplectic representation
[
19
]
of the
SU(1,1)
group.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we intoduce the squeezed
complex Hermite polynomials which will stand for the
polyanalytic

Hermite polynomials
H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z};\tau)
associated with the
elliptic Ginibre model and we discuss their relation to the
2D
-Hermite
polynomials
H_{n,m}^{\left(R_{\tau}\right)}.
Section 3 is devoted to
construct the integral transform
T_{\tau,n}
whose kernel will be
interpreted in terms of the two-photon coherent states.

2
Squeezed complex Hermite polynomials

2.1
The magnetic picture:
\tau=0

We shall revisit the construction of
H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z})
by means of
the creation and annihilation operators associated with
\widetilde{\Delta}
. For this, we will follow the general scheme of the
Fock
representation
for the Hamiltonian of the harmonic oscillator, see
\left(\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Dirac}{}{}]}, pp.16-18}\right)
for the general theory.

The Hamiltonian operator describing the dynamics of a particle of charge
e

and mass
m_{\ast}
on the Euclidean
xy
-plane, while interacting with a
perpendicular constant homogeneous magnetic field, is given by the operator

H:=\frac{1}{2m_{\ast}}\left(i\hbar\nabla-\frac{e}{c}\mathbb{A}_{\nu}\right)^{2}

(2.1)

where
\hbar
denotes Planck’s constant,
c
is the light speed and
i
the
imaginary unit. Denote by
\nu>0
the strenght of the magnetic field
\mathbb{B}
and select the symmetric gauge

\mathbb{A}_{\nu}\mathbb{=-}\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{r\times}\mathbb{B}\mathbf{=}\left(-\frac{\nu}{2}y,\frac{\nu}{2}x\right)

(2.2)

where
\mathbf{r}=\left(x,y\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For simplicity, we
set
m_{\ast}=e=c=\hbar=1
in
\left(2.1\right),
leading to the Landau
Hamiltonian

H_{L}^{\nu}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(i\partial_{x}-\frac{\nu}{2}y\right)^{2}+\left(i\partial_{y}+\frac{\nu}{2}x\right)^{2}\right)

(2.3)

acting on the Hilbert space
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},dxdy\right)
. The
spectrum of
H_{L}^{\nu}
consists of infinite number of eigenvalues with
infinite multiplicity of the form

\epsilon_{n}^{\nu}:=\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\nu,\text{ }n=0,1,2,...\text{ .}

(2.4)

We may intertwine the operator
H_{L}^{2\nu}
by the unitary
transformation
Q:\phi\mapsto e^{\frac{1}{2}\nu|z|^{2}}\phi
from
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},dxdy\right)
into
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{C},e^{-\nu|z|^{2}}dz\right)
, the so-called ground state transformation as
follows

\Delta_{\nu}:=e^{\frac{1}{2}\nu|z|^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}H_{L}^{2\nu}-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|z|^{2}}=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial\overline{z}}+\nu\overline{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}}

(2.5)

For
\nu=1,\Delta_{1}=\widetilde{\Delta}
in
\left(2.5\right)
and

H_{L}:=\frac{1}{2}H_{L}^{2\nu}-\frac{\nu}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left[-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\partial_{x}+\frac{i}{2}y\right)^{2}+\left(\partial_{y}-\frac{i}{2}x\right)^{2}\right)\right]-1\right)

(2.6)

which decomposes as

H_{L}=:A^{\ast}A:=\left(\partial_{z}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{z}\right)\left(-\partial_{\overline{z}}-\frac{1}{2}z\right).

(2.7)

The annihilation
A
and the creation operators
A^{\star}
satisfy the
Heisenberg commutation relation
[A,A^{\star}]=1
. Yet, the following
operators also do:

B=-\partial_{z}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{z},\quad B^{\star}=\partial_{\overline{z}}-\frac{1}{2}z,

(2.8)

That is
[B,B^{\star}]=1
, and commute with
A
and
A^{\star}
as
well. As explained in
[
1
]
, the essence of the operators
\left(B,B^{\star}\right)
stems from the change of orientation of the magnetic
vector field along the direction orthogonal to the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, or equivalently to the choice of the vector
potential
(y,-x,0)
. At the mathematical level, the (commuting) algebras
\{A,A^{\star}\}
and
\{B,B^{\star}\}
reflect the complex conjugation
z\mapsto\overline{z}
(holomorphic and anti-holomorphic structures) and the
two choices of the vector potentials reflect the complex structures
z\mapsto\pm iz
.

Now, let

\psi_{0}(z,\overline{z}):=\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}},

then
A\psi_{0}=B\psi_{0}=0
and the eigenstates of
H_{L}
corresponding
to the lowest Landau level
n=0,
are given by (
[
27
]
):

\psi_{m}^{(0)}(z,\overline{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}(-B^{\star})^{m}\psi_{0}(z,\overline{z})=\frac{z^{m}}{\sqrt{\pi m!}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}},\quad m\geq 0.

(2.9)

More generally, the eigenstates corresponding to highest Landau levels
n\geq 0
are given by (
[
8
]
):

\psi_{m}^{(n)}(z,\overline{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}(A^{\star})^{n}\psi_{m}^{(0)}(z,\overline{z})=H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z})e^{-\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}},\text{ \ \ }m,n\geq 0.

(2.10)

This formula stems from the commutation relation
[H,A^{\star}]=A^{\star}

which allows to come from the
n
-th Landau level to the
(n+1)
-th one.
Keeping in mind the invertible transformation
Q
above, one obtains the
spectral resolution of
\widetilde{\Delta}
in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{C},\omega_{0}(z)dz\right)\mathbf{.}

2.2
The deformed setting
\tau\in\left[0,1\right)
:
squeezing the lowest Landau level

In this paragraph, we briefly recall the construction of the holomorphic
Hermite polynomials using squeezed creation and annihilation operators. Our
presentation is taken from
[
27
]
. For a fixed radiation mode with
photon annihilation operator
B,
let us consider the Bogoliubov
transformation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}B_{\mu}=(\cosh\mu)B-(\sinh\mu)B^{\star}\\
B_{\mu}^{\star}=-(\sinh\mu)B+(\cosh\mu)B^{\star}\end{array}\right.

(2.11)

From
\cosh^{2}\mu-\sinh^{2}\mu=1,
it follows that

\left[B_{\mu},B_{\mu}^{\ast}\right]=1

(2.12)

which means that the transformation
\left(2.11\right)
leaves the
commuator
\left[B,B^{\ast}\right]
invariant. Thus, the change of
variables from
\left(B,B^{\ast}\right)
to
\left(B_{\mu},B_{\mu}^{\ast}\right)
is a linear cononical transformation
[
10
]
. To find
out the ground state one has to solve the equation

B_{\mu}\psi=0.

(2.13)

A solution to this equation is given by

\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(|z|^{2}-\tau z^{2})},\text{ \ \ \ }\tau=\tanh\mu.

(2.14)

From the latter one, we propose the weight function

\omega_{\tau}(z)dz=\left|\psi_{\mu}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}},\frac{\overline{z}}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}\right)\right|^{2}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}.

(2.15)

Moreover, the deformed analogue of
\left(2.1\right)
is
\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{For-Jan}{}{}]}\right):

\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}(-B_{\mu}^{\star})^{m}\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}\left(\frac{\tanh\mu}{2}\right)^{m/2}H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z}),\quad m\geq 0.

(2.16)

Note in passing that with the substitutions

\tau=\tanh\mu,\text{ \ }z\mapsto z\cosh\mu=\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}},

(2.17)

the right hand side of
\left(2.16\right)
transforms into:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{m/2}H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)\psi_{\mu}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}},\frac{\overline{z}}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}\right)

=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}H_{m}(z,\tau)\psi_{\mu}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}},\frac{\overline{z}}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}\right)

(2.18)

Before proceeding to the definition of the squeezed complex Hermite
polynomials, we would like to stress that though
H_{m}\left(z,\tau\right)
were identified in
[
27
]
through their recurrence relation, one
may rather use the Rodrigues formula for the Hermite polynomials. Indeed,
the squeezed creation operator
-B_{\mu}^{\ast}
may be written as

-B_{\mu}^{\ast}=(\sinh\mu)B-(\cosh\mu)B^{\ast}

(2.19)

=-\left((\sinh\mu)\left(\partial_{z}+\frac{\overline{z}}{2}\right)+\left(\cosh\mu\right)\left(\partial_{\overline{z}}+\frac{z}{2}\right)-z\cosh\mu\right)

(2.20)

=-e^{-z\overline{z}/2}\left(\left(\sinh\mu\right)\partial_{z}+\left(\cosh\mu\right)\partial_{\overline{z}}-z\cosh\mu\right)e^{z\overline{z}/2}.

(2.21)

Whence we deduce that

\left(-B_{\mu}^{\ast}\right)^{m}\left[\psi_{\mu}\right]\left(z,\overline{z}\right)=\left(-1\right)^{m}e^{-z\overline{z}/2}\left((\sinh\mu)\partial_{z}+(\cosh\mu)\partial_{\overline{z}}-z\cosh\mu\right)^{m}\left[e^{\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\tanh\mu}\right]

(2.22)

=\left(-1\right)^{m}e^{-z\overline{z}/2}\left((\sinh\mu)\partial_{z}-z\cosh\mu\right)^{m}\left[e^{\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\tanh\mu}\right]

(2.23)

=\left(-\sinh\mu\right)^{m}e^{-z\overline{z}/2}\left(\partial_{z}-z\cosh\mu\right)^{m}\left[e^{\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\tanh\mu}\right],

(2.24)

where
\left(2.20\right)
being a consequence of the holomorphy of
z\mapsto e^{\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\tanh\mu}.
Noting further that

\partial_{z}-z\cosh\mu=e^{\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\coth\mu}\partial_{z}\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\coth\mu}\right),

(2.25)

and using the Rodrigues formula for the Hermite polynomials, it follows that

\left(-B_{\mu}^{\ast}\right)^{m}\left[\psi_{\mu}\right]\left(z,\overline{z}\right)=\left(-\sinh\mu\right)^{m}e^{-z\overline{z}/2}e^{\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\coth\mu}\partial_{z}\left(e^{-z^{2}/\sinh 2\mu}\right)

(2.26)

=\frac{\left(\sinh\mu\right)^{m}}{\left(\sinh 2\mu\right)^{m/2}}e^{-z\overline{z}/2}e^{\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\coth\mu}e^{-z^{2}/\sinh 2\mu}H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)

(2.27)

=\left(\frac{\tanh\mu}{2}\right)^{m/2}H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\psi_{\mu}\left(z,\overline{z}\right)

(2.28)

as in
\left(2.16\right).

2.3
Defining and computing the squeezed complex Hermite polynomials

By mimiking
\left(2.10\right),
the commutation relation
\left[H_{L},A^{\ast}\right]=A^{\ast}
motivates the following.

Definition 2.1.
For any

n\geq 0,

let

\psi_{m}^{(n)}(z,\overline{z},\tanh\mu):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}(A^{\star})^{n}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}\left(\frac{\tanh\mu}{2}\right)^{m/2}H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})\right].

(2.29)

In this respect, the following proposition is a major step toward the proof
of Theorem 1.1.

Proposition 2.1.
For any

n\geq 0,\left(\psi_{m}^{(n)}(z,\overline{z},\tanh\mu)\right)_{m\geq 0}

are
orthogonal eigenfunctions of
H_{L}
in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{C},dz\right),
corresponding to the
n
th Landau level
.

Proof.
By the virtue of the commutation relation
[H,A^{\star}]=A^{\star}
, the family
(\psi_{m}^{(n)}(z,\overline{z},\tanh\mu))_{m\geq 0}
belongs to the
n
-th eigenspace of
H_{L}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},dz)
. As to the orthogonality property, it follows from that of
(\psi_{m}^{(0)}(z,\overline{z},\tanh\mu))_{m\geq 0}
or equivalently of the
holomorphic Hermite polynomials with respect to
\omega_{\tau}(z)dz
.
Indeed,
A
and
A^{\star}
are adjoint to each other in
L^{2}(\mathbb{C},dz)
and Lemma 2.2 in
[
8
]
shows that

A^{n}(A^{\star})^{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}[H+k].

(2.30)

Consequently, for any
m,m^{\prime}\geq 0
,

\langle\psi_{m}^{(n)},\psi_{m^{\prime}}^{(n)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C},dz)}=\langle(A^{n}(a^{\star})^{n}\psi_{m}^{(0)},\psi_{m^{\prime}}^{(0)}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C},dz)}=n!\delta_{mm^{\prime}},

(2.31)

as claimed.

We now are ready to prove Theorem 1.1. Indeed,
(A^{\star})^{n}
is a
differential operator with polynomial coefficients in
(z,\overline{z})
and
as such,

\psi_{m}^{(n)}(z,\overline{z},\tanh\mu)=G_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},\tanh\mu)\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z}),

(2.32)

for some polynomials
G_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},\tanh\mu)
. Up to a
rescaling, these polynomials are polyanalytic analogues of the holomorphic
Hermite polynomials
H_{m}(z,\tau=\tanh\mu)
for the higher Landau levels

n\geq 1
, or equivalently the squeezed complex Hermite polynomials.

2.4
Polyanalytic Hermite polynomials associated with the elliptic
Ginibre model

In this paragraph, we shall prove the following result:

Proposition 2.2.

For any
m,n\geq 0
,

G_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},\tanh(\mu))=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}\left(\frac{\tanh(\mu)}{2}\right)^{m/2}\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge m}k!\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{2^{k}}{\sinh^{k/2}(2\mu)}\left(i\sqrt{\frac{\tanh(\mu)}{2}}\right)^{n-k}

(2.33)

\times H_{m-k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh(2\mu)}}\right)H_{n-k}\left(-i\sqrt{\frac{\tanh(\mu)}{2}}z+i\frac{\overline{z}}{\sqrt{2\tanh(\mu)}}\right)

Proof.
Recall that
A^{\star}=\partial_{z}-(\overline{z}/2)
.
Since
[\partial_{z},\overline{z}]=0,
then

(A^{\star})^{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left(-\frac{\overline{z}}{2}\right)^{n-j}\partial_{z}^{j}.

(2.34)

Using the differential relation
\partial_{z}H_{m}(z)=2mH_{m-1}(z)

together with Leibniz formula, we get for any
0\leq j\leq n
,

\partial_{z}^{j}\left[H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}\frac{2^{k}m!}{\sinh^{k/2}(2\mu)(m-k)!}H_{m-k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\partial_{z}^{j-k}\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z}),

(2.35)

where
H_{m-k}=0
if
k>m
. Consequently, we, successively, get

(A^{\star})^{n}\left[H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})\right]

(2.36)

=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left(-\frac{\overline{z}}{2}\right)^{n-j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}\frac{2^{k}m!}{(m-k)!\sinh^{k/2}2\mu}H_{m-k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\partial_{z}^{j-k}\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})

(2.37)

=\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{2^{k}}{\sinh^{k/2}2\mu}H_{m-k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\sum_{j=k}^{n}\frac{n!}{(n-j)!(j-k)!}\left(-\frac{\overline{z}}{2}\right)^{n-j}\partial_{z}^{j-k}\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})

(2.38)

=\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge m}k!\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{2^{k}}{\sinh^{k/2}2\mu}H_{m-k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n-k}{j}\left(-\frac{\overline{z}}{2}\right)^{n-k-j}\partial_{z}^{j}\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z}).

(2.39)

Next, we infer from
[
5
]
that

\partial_{z}^{j}\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})=(-1)^{j}I_{j}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\tanh\mu}(z,\overline{z})\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})

(2.40)

where

I_{j}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\tanh\mu}(z,\overline{z})=I_{j}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\tanh\mu}(z,\overline{z}|0)

(2.41)

in the notations of that paper
\left(\text{see eq.}\left(1.3\right)\right)
. Actually, Corollay 2.9 there shows that

(-1)^{j}I_{j}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\tanh\mu}(z,\overline{z})=\left(i\sqrt{\tanh\mu}/\sqrt{2}\right)^{j}H_{j}\left(\frac{z\tanh\mu-\overline{z}/2}{i\sqrt{2\tanh\mu}}\right).

(2.42)

As a result, we obtain

(A^{\star})^{n}\left[H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})\right]=\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge m}k!\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{2^{k}}{\sinh^{k/2}2\mu}H_{m-k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)

\times\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n-k}{j}\left(-\frac{\overline{z}}{2}\right)^{n-k-j}[i\sqrt{\tanh\mu}/\sqrt{2}]^{j}H_{j}\left(\frac{z(\tanh\mu)-\overline{z}/2}{i\sqrt{2\tanh\mu}}\right).

(2.43)

Applying the Taylor expansion, for the Hermite polynomials

H_{n-k}\left(u+t\right)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-k}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n-k\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(2u\right)^{n-k-j}H_{j}\left(t\right),

(2.44)

to the inner sum, we further get

(A^{\star})^{n}\left[H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})\right]=\psi_{\mu}(z,\overline{z})\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge m}k!\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{2^{k}}{\sinh^{k/2}2\mu}

\left(i\sqrt{\frac{\tanh\mu}{2}}\right)^{n-k}H_{m-k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\sinh 2\mu}}\right)H_{n-k}\left(-i\sqrt{\frac{\tanh\mu}{2}}z+i\frac{\overline{z}}{\sqrt{2\tanh\mu}}\right)

(2.45)

Keeping in mind
\left(2.32\right)
, the announced expression of
G_{mn.n}(z,\overline{z},\tanh\mu)
follows.

In this expression, we again substitute

\tanh\mu\rightarrow\tau,\text{ }z\mapsto z/\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}=z\cosh\mu,

(2.46)

and get the expression of
H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},\tau)
displayed in
Theorem 1.1, with the form

H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},\tau):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{(m+n)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge m}i^{n-k}k!\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{2^{k}(1-\tau^{2})^{k/2}}{\tau^{k}}

(2.47)

\times H_{m-k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)H_{n-k}\left(\frac{i(\overline{z}-\tau z)}{\sqrt{2\tau(1-\tau^{2})}}\right).

Remark 2.1
. Letting
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we have

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{m/2}\frac{2^{k/2}(1-\tau^{2})^{k/2}}{\tau^{k/2}}H_{m-k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)=z^{m-k},

(2.48)

and similarly

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{(n-k)/2}H_{n-k}\left(-i\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2(1-\tau^{2})}}z+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2\tau(1-\tau^{2})}}\overline{z}\right)=i^{n-k}\overline{z}^{n-k}.

(2.49)

It follows that

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},\tau)=(-1)^{n}H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z}).

(2.50)

If we discard the factor
1/\sqrt{m!}
and use the generating function for
Hermite polynomials, then we readily derive

\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\sqrt{m!}H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},\tau)\frac{u^{m}}{m!}\frac{v^{n}}{n!}=\exp\left(\tau(v^{2}-u^{2})/2+uz-v(\overline{z}-\tau z)/\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}+uv\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\right).

(2.51)

In particular, we recover the known result (see for instance
[
7
]
,
eq. (3.1)):

\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\sqrt{m!}H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},0)\frac{u^{m}}{m!}\frac{(-v)^{n}}{n!}=e^{uz+v\overline{z}-uv}.

(2.52)

Remark 2.2.
From the

property
H_{n-k}\left(u\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n-k}H_{n-k}\left(-u\right)
,
one readly gets
the relation

\overline{H_{m,n}(z,\overline{z},\tau)}=H_{m,n}(\overline{z},z,\tau).

2.5
Relation to
2D
-Hermite polynomials
:
proof of Corollary 1.2

Following
[
17
]
the two-dimensional Hermite polynomials
H_{n,m}^{\left(R\right)}\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)
associated to a symmetric
matrix
R=\left(r_{kl}\right)_{1\leq l,k\leq 2}
are defined by the
following generating function

\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle R\gamma,\gamma\right\rangle+\left\langle R\xi,\gamma\right\rangle\right)=\sum\limits_{n,m=0}\frac{\gamma_{1}^{n}\gamma_{2}^{m}}{n!m!}H_{n,m}^{\left(R\right)}\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)

(2.53)

where
\xi=\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)\in

\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
\gamma=\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{2},\xi_{1},\xi_{2},

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
are arbitrary complex numbers and
\ \left\langle R\gamma,\gamma\right\rangle=\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{2}\gamma_{j}r_{jk}\gamma_{k}.
These polynomials may be expressed through one
variable Hermite polynomials as
\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{DM}{}{}]}\text{, eq.8}\right):

H_{n,m}^{\left(R\right)}\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)=\left(\frac{r_{11}^{n}r_{22}^{m}}{2^{n+m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n\wedge m}\left(\frac{-2r_{12}}{\sqrt{r_{11}r_{22}}}\right)^{k}\frac{n!m!}{\left(n-k\right)!\left(m-k\right)!k!}H_{n-k}\left(\frac{\zeta_{1}}{\sqrt{2r_{11}}}\right)H_{m-k}\left(\frac{\zeta_{2}}{\sqrt{2r_{22}}}\right)

(2.54)

where
\zeta=\left(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is
connected to
\xi=\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)
by

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}r_{11}&r_{12}\\
r_{12}&r_{22}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\xi_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\zeta_{1}\\
\zeta_{2}\end{array}\right).

(2.55)

By the virtue of Theorem 1.1, we are led to consider the unimodular
symmetric matrix
R=R_{\tau}\in\mathcal{S}L\left(2,\mathbb{C}\right),

whose entries are given by

r_{11}=r_{22}=\tau,\text{ \ \ }r_{12}=r_{21}=i\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}},

(2.56)

in which case the
2D
-Hermite polynomials read

H_{n,m}^{\left(R\right)}\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)=\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(n+m\right)}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n\wedge m}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}{i\tau}\right)^{k}k!\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
k\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
k\end{array}\right)H_{m-k}\left(\frac{\zeta_{1}}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)H_{n-k}\left(\frac{\zeta_{2}}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)

(2.57)

Consequently, we may choose
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
as

\zeta_{1}=z,\text{ }\zeta_{2}=\frac{i\left(\overline{z}-\tau z\right)}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}

(2.58)

or equivalently, by virtue of
\left(2.55\right):

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\xi_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\end{array}\right)=R_{\tau}^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\zeta_{1}\\
\zeta_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tau&-i\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\\
-i\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}&\tau\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}z\\
\frac{i\left(\overline{z}-\tau z\right)}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{z}\\
\frac{i\left(\tau\overline{z}-z\right)}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}\end{array}\right)

(2.59)

Taking into account the factor
i^{n}/\sqrt{m!}
the corollary is proved.

3
An integral transform
T_{\tau,m}:\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

3.1
The kernel
W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)
of
T_{\tau,m}

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
be the space of entire
functions in the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{\tau}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\omega_{\tau}(z)dz).
An orthogonal basis of this space is given by the
normalized holomorphic Hermite polynomials
\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Byu-For}{}{}]},\text{ p.21}\right)
:

h_{\tau,k}\left(w\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k!}}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{k/2}H_{k}\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right),\text{ }k\geq 0.

(3.1)

Let us recall
\left(1.16\right)
where we denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
the
n
-th polyanalytic eigenspace of the
operator
\widetilde{\Delta}
in
\mathfrak{H}_{\tau},
a basis of which
consists of the normalized complex Hermite polynomials

\phi_{m,n}\left(z,\overline{z}\right):=\frac{H_{m,n}\left(z,\overline{z}\right)}{\sqrt{m!n!}}

(3.2)

These polynomials may be expressed as (
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Mou}{}{}]}):

\phi_{k,m}\left(z\right)=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m\wedge k}}{\sqrt{m!k!}}\left(m\wedge k\right)!\left|z\right|^{\left|m-k\right|}e^{-i\left(m-k\right)\theta}L_{m\wedge k}^{\left|m-k\right|}\left(z\overline{z}\right),\ z=re^{i\theta},

(3.3)

where
L_{m}^{\left(\alpha\right)}
is the generalized Laguerre
polynomial defined by

L_{m}^{\left(\alpha\right)}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{m!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}\left(-m\right)_{k}\left(k+\alpha+1\right)_{m-k}x^{k}.

(3.4)

The kernel of the transformation
T_{\tau,m}
is therefore given by

W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}h_{\tau,k}\left(w\right)\overline{\phi_{k,n}\left(z\right)}

(3.5)

=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n\wedge k}}{\sqrt{\pi n!k!}}\left(n\wedge k\right)!\left|z\right|^{\left|n-k\right|}e^{i\left(n-k\right)\theta}L_{n\wedge k}^{\left|n-k\right|}\left(z\overline{z}\right)h_{\tau,k}\left(w\right)

(3.6)

Proof of Proposition 1.3.
We split
\left(3.6\right)
into two
parts as follows

W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)=W^{(<\infty)}\left(z,w\right)+W^{\left(\infty\right)}\left(z,w\right)

(3.7)

where

W^{(<\infty)}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}\sqrt{k!}}{\sqrt{\pi n!}}\left|z\right|^{n-k}e^{i\left(n-k\right)\theta}L_{k}^{n-k}\left(z\overline{z}\right)h_{\tau,k}\left(w\right)

(3.8)

-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}\sqrt{n!}}{\sqrt{\pi k!}}\left|z\right|^{k-n}e^{i\left(n-k\right)\theta}L_{n}^{k-m}\left(z\overline{z}\right)h_{\tau,k}\left(w\right)

By using the identity
\left(\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Sz}{}{}]}},\text{p.98}\right)
:

L_{j}^{\left(-s\right)}\left(t\right)=\frac{\left(j-s\right)!}{j!}\left(-t\right)^{s}L_{j-s}^{\left(s\right)}\left(t\right),\text{ \ }1\leq s\leq j

(3.9)

for
s=k-m
and
t=z\overline{z}
, we obtain that

\left(-1\right)^{k}\frac{\sqrt{k!}}{\sqrt{m!}}\left|z\right|^{m-k}e^{i\left(m-k\right)\theta}\left[L_{k}^{m-k}\left(z\overline{z}\right)\right]=\left(-1\right)^{m}\frac{\sqrt{m!}}{\sqrt{k!}}e^{i\left(m-k\right)\theta}\left|z\right|^{k-m}L_{m}^{\left(k-m\right)}\left(z\overline{z}\right).

(3.10)

Consequently,

W^{(<\infty)}=0,

(3.11)

and we are left with

W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)=W^{\left(\infty\right)}\left(z,w\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n}\sqrt{n!}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}\left|z\right|^{m-n}e^{i\left(n-m\right)\theta}L_{n}^{m-n}\left(z\overline{z}\right)h_{\tau,m}\left(w\right)

(3.12)

=\left(-1\right)^{n}\sqrt{n!}\frac{1}{\overline{z}^{n}}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\overline{z}^{m}L_{n}^{m-n}\left(z\overline{z}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{m/2}H_{m}\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right).

(3.13)

Let

\mathcal{S}^{\left(\infty\right)}\left(z,w\right)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right)^{m}L_{n}^{m-n}\left(z\overline{z}\right)H_{m}\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)

(3.14)

and use the expansion of the generalized Laguerre polynomial
\left(3.4\right)
to rewrite
\mathcal{S}^{\left(\infty\right)}\left(z,w\right)
as

\mathcal{S}^{\left(\infty\right)}\left(z,w\right)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\left(-n\right)_{k}}{k!}\left|z\right|^{2k}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\left(m+k-n+1\right)_{n-k}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right)^{m}H_{m}\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right).

(3.15)

Now, notice that the shifted factorial
\left(m+k-n+1\right)_{n-k}=0
for
any
m<n-k
while

\left(m+k-n+1\right)_{n-k}=\frac{m!}{\left(m+k-n\right)!},\text{ }m\geq n-k,\text{ }0\leq k\leq n.

(3.16)

Consequently,

\mathcal{S}^{\left(\infty\right)}\left(z,w\right)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\left(-n\right)_{k}}{k!}\left|z\right|^{2k}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right)^{n-k}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right)^{m}H_{m+n-k}\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right).

(3.17)

The inner series may be expressed as
\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Sin-Sri}{}{}]}\text{, Eq.20}\right):

\sum\limits_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right)^{m}H_{m+n-k}\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)=e^{\overline{zw}-\tau\overline{z}^{2}/2}H_{n-k}\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}-\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right).

(3.18)

Finally,

\mathcal{S}^{\left(\infty\right)}(z,w)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right)^{n}\frac{e^{\overline{zw}-\tau\overline{z}^{2}/2}}{n!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
k\end{array}\right)\left(-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\tau}}z\right)^{k}H_{n-k}\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}-\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right)

(3.19)

and appealing again to
\left(2.44\right),
we end up with

\mathcal{S}^{\left(\infty\right)}\left(z,w\right)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right)^{n}\frac{e^{\overline{zw}-\tau\overline{z}^{2}/2}}{n!}H_{n}\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}-\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\tau}}z\right)

(3.20)

Equivalently,

W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n}\sqrt{n!}\frac{1}{\overline{z}^{n}}\mathcal{S}^{\left(\infty\right)}\left(z,w\right)

=\left(-\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\right)^{nn}\frac{e^{\overline{zw}-\tau\overline{z}^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{n!}}H_{n}\left(\frac{w-z}{\sqrt{2\tau}}-\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{2}}\overline{z}\right)

(3.21)

and the proposition is proved.

A quantum mechanical interpretation of the kernel

Le us recall the squeeze operator

S\left(\xi\right)=e^{\xi K_{+}-\overline{\xi}K_{-}},\text{ \ \ \ }\xi\in\mathbb{C}

]
, as

K_{+}=\frac{1}{2}z^{2},\text{ \ \ }K_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}^{2},\text{
\ }K_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(z\partial_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\right)

(4.3)

The operator
S\left(\xi\right)
is unitary. That is,

S\left(\xi\right)^{\ast}=S\left(\xi\right)^{-1}=S\left(-\xi\right),\text{ \ }\xi\in\mathbb{C}

(4.4)

For our purpose, we may consider
\xi\geq 0
and set
\tau=\tanh\xi
.
Next, we recall the distanglement formula

e^{\xi K_{+}-\overline{\xi}K_{-}}=e^{\tau K_{+}}e^{\left(Log\left(1-\left|\tau\right|^{2}\right)\right)K_{0}}e^{-\overline{\tau}K_{-}}

(4.5)

used by the authors (
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{AGHS}{}{}]},
Theorem.5.1) who proved that

S\left(\xi\right)\left[w\mapsto\frac{w^{k}}{\sqrt{k!}}\right]=\left(1-\left|\tau\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tau w^{2}}\left(\frac{\overline{\tau}}{2}\right)^{{}^{\frac{1}{2}k}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k!}}H_{k}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1-\left|\tau\right|^{2}}{2\overline{\tau}}}w\right)

(4.6)

By another side, the kernel
W_{\tau,n}
expands as

W_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\phi_{k,n}\left(z\right)}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}k}H_{k}\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)

(4.7)

or, equivalently,

\left(1-\left|\tau\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tau w^{2}}W_{\tau,n}\left(z,\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}w\right)=

(4.8)

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\phi_{k,n}\left(z\right)}\left(1-\left|\tau\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tau w^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{k!}}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}k}H_{k}\left(\frac{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}w}{\sqrt{2\tau}}\right)\right].

Therefore,

\left(1-\left|\tau\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tau w^{2}}W_{\tau,n}\left(z,\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}w\right)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\phi_{k,n}\left(z\right)}\left[S\left(\xi\right)\left[w\mapsto\frac{w^{k}}{\sqrt{k!}}\right]\right]

(4.9)

=S\left(\xi\right)\left[w\mapsto\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\phi_{k,n}\left(z\right)}\frac{w^{k}}{\sqrt{k!}}\right]

(4.10)

where the inner sum, viewed as a function of
w
,

w\mapsto\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\phi_{k,n}\left(z\right)}\frac{w^{k}}{\sqrt{k!}}

(4.11)

belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
and also turns out to
be the expansion (with polyanalytic coefficients
\left\{\phi_{k,n}\left(z\right)\right\}
within the Hilbertian probabilistic scheme
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Gaz}{}{}]})

of a non normalized coherent state. In this respect, one also notices that
the basis vectors
\frac{1}{\sqrt{k!}}w^{k}=B_{0}\left[h_{k}\right]\left(w\right)
are Bargmann transforms
B_{0}:L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{0}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
of Hermite functions
h_{k}\left(x\right)
on
\mathbb{R}
. The latter ones are the
number states
of the harmonic oscilator. Therefore, we may normalize the
wavefunction in
(4.11)
as

\langle w\left|z,n\right\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\phi_{k,n}\left(z\right)}\frac{w^{k}}{\sqrt{k!}},

(4.12)

and rewrite it as

\langle w\left|z,n\right\rangle=B_{0}\left[x\longmapsto e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\phi_{k,n}\left(z\right)}h_{k}\left(x\right)\right]\left(w\right).

(4.13)

Whence, we recognize the normalized wavefunction of the displaced Fock state
with respect to the Schrödinger represention
D\left(z\right)
of the
Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{1}
in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Mou2}{}{}]}\right):

\langle x\left|z,n\right\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\phi_{k,n}\left(z\right)}h_{k}\left(x\right)=D\left(z\right)\left[h_{n}\right]\left(x\right).

(4.14)

Therefore,
\left(4.13\right)
takes the form

\langle w\left|z,n\right\rangle=B_{0}\left[x\longmapsto\langle x\left|z,n\right\rangle\right]\left(w\right)=B_{0}\left[x\mapsto D\left(z\right)\left[h_{n}\right]\left(x\right)\right]\left(w\right).

(4.15)

Combining all these facts together, we arrive at

\widetilde{W}_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right):=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}}\left(1-\left|\tau\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tau w^{2}}W_{\tau,n}\left(z,\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}w\right)=S\left(\xi\right)\left[w\mapsto\langle w\left|z,n\right\rangle\right]

(4.16)

which means that this kernel is the squeezed Bargmann analytic
representation of displaced Fock states
D\left(z\right)\left[h_{n}\right]
or, in other words,

\widetilde{W}_{\tau,n}\left(z,w\right)=S\left(\xi\right)\left[w\mapsto B_{0}\left[x\mapsto D\left(z\right)\left[h_{n}\right]\left(x\right)\right]\left(w\right)\right].

(4.17)

Note that the lowest Landau level
n=0,
the kernel
\left(4.17\right)

reduces further to

\widetilde{W}_{\tau,0}\left(z,w\right)=\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\tau w^{2}+\overline{z}w-\frac{1}{2}\tau\overline{z}^{2}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}}.

(4.18)

But since
w\mapsto

\widetilde{W}_{\tau,0}\left(z,w\right)
belongs to

\mathcal{A}_{0}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\subset

L^{2}\left(\mathbb{C},e^{-\left|w\right|^{2}}dw\right)
, we may transfer it back into
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{C},dw\right)
via the inverse ground state
transformation
f\mapsto e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left|w\right|^{2}}f
as
follows

e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left|w\right|^{2}}\widetilde{W}_{\tau,0}\left(z,w\right)=\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\tau w^{2}+\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\overline{z}w-\frac{1}{2}\tau\overline{z}^{2}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left|w\right|^{2}}.

(4.19)

Next, by introducing the parameters
\left(a,b\right)
such that

\tau=\tanh\xi=\frac{\sinh\xi}{\cosh\xi}=\frac{b}{a},\text{ }\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{a^{2}}

(4.20)

Then
\left(5.35\right)
may be rewritten

e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left|w\right|^{2}}\widetilde{W}_{\tau,0}\left(z,w\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2a}\left(2\overline{z}w-b\overline{z}^{2}+bw^{2}\right)\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left|w\right|^{2}}\equiv\langle z\left|w\right\rangle_{g}.

(4.21)

This last expression was obtained in (
[
18
]
, Eq.3.20) as the
wavefunction of the
two-photon coherent state
(TPCS)

of
the radiation field. There, the concept of TPCS was introduced in order to
generalize the Glauber
[
24
]
coherent state

A\left|\zeta\right\rangle=\zeta\left|\zeta\right\rangle

(4.22)

by replacing the anhilation operator
A
by its Bogoliubov transform
\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{BV}{}{}]}\right):

A_{g}=aA+bA^{\ast},\text{ }a^{2}-b^{2}=1

(4.23)

and the generalized coherent state
\left|w\right\rangle_{g}
was
defined as eigenfunction of the new annihilation operator
A_{g}
as

A\left|w\right\rangle=w\left|w\right\rangle_{g}.

(4.24)

The wavefunction
\langle z\left|w\right\rangle_{g}
in
\left(4.21\right)
has been expressed in the coherence
z
-coordinate. In other
words,
\langle z\left|w\right\rangle_{g}
is the
canonical
coherent state representation
of the TPCS, given by the scalar product
\langle z\left|w\right\rangle_{g}
of the TPCS
\left|w\right\rangle_{g}
and the classical Glauber coherent state
\left|z\right\rangle
of the harmonic oscillator
A^{\ast}A,
which solves the
problem
A\left|z\right\rangle=z\left|z\right\rangle.

Note also that the kernel
\left(4.21\right)
corresponds to the
Metaplectic representation
of the group
SU\left(1,1\right)
in the
Bargmann-Fock space
\mathcal{A}_{0}\left(\mathbb{C}\right),
see (
[
23
]
, p.181) and
[
19
]
.

1.
Introduction

The heat equation is a fundamental partial differential equation in mathematics. Since Joseph Fourier’s renowned work in 1822, it has been extensively studied across various contexts and is recognized as a key element in multiple areas of mathematics (see, for example,
[
Gri09
]
). The following classical result on long-time asymptotic convergence is analogous to the Central Limit Theorem in probability, but within the framework of partial differential equations. For more details on this property, we refer to the expository survey
[
V“’az18
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider the heat equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)\,=\,\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,x),\qquad\,t>0,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\[5.0pt]
u(0,x)\,=\,u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

where the initial data
u_{0}
belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Denote by
M=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,u_{0}(x)
the mass of
u_{0}

and by
G_{t}(x)\,=\,(4\pi{t})^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/4t}
the heat kernel.
Then the solution to (
1.1
) satisfies:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)-MG_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\longrightarrow\,0

and

(1.3)

\displaystyle t^{\frac{n}{2}}\,\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)-MG_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\longrightarrow\,0

as
t\rightarrow\infty
. The
L^{p}
(
1<p<\infty
) norm estimates
follow by interpolation:

(1.4)

\displaystyle t^{\frac{n}{2p^{\prime}}}\,\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)-MG_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\longrightarrow\,0,

where
\|G_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\asymp t^{\frac{n}{2p^{\prime}}}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Here,
p^{\prime}
denotes the dual exponent of
p
, defined by the formula

\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.

On manifolds of non-negative Ricci curvature, the results (
1.4
) were generalized in
[
GPZ23
]
. Analogous properties on negatively curved manifolds
were first investigated by Vázquez in his recent work
[
V“’az22
]
,
which deals with real hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
. More precisely, it was shown in
[
V“’az22
]
that on
\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathbb{R})
, (
1.2
) fails for general absolutely integrable initial
data
u_{0}
but is still true on
\mathbb{H}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
if the function
u_{0}
is spherically symmetric around the origin. A generalization of these results was
obtained in
[
APZ23
]
in the more general setting of symmetric spaces of
non-compact type by using tools of harmonic analysis. Let us elaborate.
Given a symmetric space
\mathbb{X}=G/K
of non compact type,
let us denote by
\Delta
the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{X}

and by
h_{t}
the associated heat kernel, i.e.,
the bi-
K
-invariant convolution kernel of the semi-group
e^{t\Delta}
. The heat equation writes as follows:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)\,=\,\Delta_{x}u(t,x),\qquad u(0,x)\,=\,u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{X},\,t>0.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
APZ23
]
)
Let
u_{0}\in{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}
be a bi-
K
-invariant initial condition
and
M=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,u_{0}(x)
be its mass. Then the solution to
the heat equation (
1.5
) satisfies

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)-Mh_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}\,\longrightarrow\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\,t\rightarrow\infty.

Moreover, this convergence fails in general without the bi-
K
-invariance assumption.
In addition, for
p>1
and
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
, the following hold

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)-Mh_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\,

\displaystyle=\,\mathrm{O}\big(t^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}e^{-|\rho|^{2}t}\big),

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)-Mh_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}\,

\displaystyle=\,\mathrm{o}\big(t^{-\frac{\nu}{2p^{\prime}}}e^{-\tfrac{|\rho|^{2}t}{p^{\prime}}}\big)\qquad\,1<p<\infty,\quad\text{if}\quad u_{0}\quad\text{is bi-$K$-invariant,}

as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Here
\nu
is the so-called dimension at infinity of
\mathbb{X}
and
|\rho|^{2}
coincides with the bottom of the
L^{2}
spectrum of the Laplace-Beltrami operator.

Interestingly, these results for
p>1
are not of Euclidean flavor, in the sense of convergence in (
1.4
); more precisely, given that
\|h_{t}\|_{\infty}\asymp t^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}e^{-|\rho|^{2}t}
for
t>1
, it follows from (
1.7
) that
\|h_{t}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\,\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)-\,M\,h_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\longrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\longrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longrightarrow}}}\,0
(see
[
APZ23
, Remark 3.6]
for details). Even further, the right hand side of (
1.8
) is not comparable to
\|h_{t}\|_{p}
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
. This prompted Naik et al in
[
NRS24
]
to seek, instead of the mass
M
, a complex number
z
(which turns out to be unique, if one restricts to a certain class of bi-
K
-invariant initial data), so that for appropriate bi-
K
-invariant data one has

\|h_{t}\|_{p}^{-1}\,\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)\,-z\,h_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}\,\longrightarrow\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\,t\rightarrow\infty.

Let us state their result. We say that a function
u_{0}:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
belongs to
\mathcal{L}^{p}(G//K)
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, if
u_{0}
is bi-
K
-invariant and

\int_{G}\textrm{d}x\,|u_{0}(x)|\,\varphi_{i(\frac{2}{p}-1)\rho}(x)<+\infty,\qquad\text{if}\quad 1\leq p<2,

and

\int_{G}\textrm{d}x\,|u_{0}(x)|\,\varphi_{0}(x)<+\infty,\qquad\text{if}\quad 2\leq p\leq\infty,

where
\varphi_{\lambda}
denotes the elementary spherical function of index
\lambda
.
Then, define accordingly

M_{p}:=\int_{G}\textrm{d}x\,u_{0}(x)\,\varphi_{i(\frac{2}{p}-1)\rho}(x)=\mathcal{H}u_{0}\left(i(2/p-1)\rho\right)\qquad\text{if}\quad 1\leq p<2,

and

M_{p}:=\int_{G}\textrm{d}x\,u_{0}(x)\,\varphi_{0}(x)=\mathcal{H}u_{0}(0),\qquad\text{if}\quad 2\leq p\leq\infty.

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
NRS24
]
)
Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(G//K)

and
M_{p}
be defined as above. Then the solution to
the heat equation (
1.5
) with initial condition
u_{0}
satisfies

(1.9)

\displaystyle\|h_{t}\|_{p}^{-1}\,\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)\,-\,M_{p}\,h_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}\,\longrightarrow\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\,t\rightarrow\infty.

Observe that when
u_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(G//K)={L}^{1}(G//K)
,
M_{1}=\int_{G}\textrm{d}x\,u_{0}(x)
.
Thus the convergence (
1.9
) agrees with the bi-
K
-invariant
L^{1}
result
previously obtained in
[
V“’az22
]
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
, as well as the bi-
K
-invariant
L^{1}
results of
[
APZ23
]
on arbitrary rank symmetric spaces and gives an analogue of the Euclidean
L^{p}
convergence in the spirit of
[
V“’az18
]
.

However, the result above shows
L^{p}
convergence restricted to
bi-
K
-invariant
initial data. In the non-bi-
K
-invariant case, Vázquez showed in three-dimensional hyperbolic space that (
1.9
) for
p=1
fails for non-radial initial data, and we established in
[
APZ23
]
such a counterexample for
p=1
not only for
\mathbb{H}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
,

n\neq 3
, but also for the symmetric spaces of general rank. Here, we illuminate this phenomenon by pushing the strong
L^{p}
convergence of (
1.9
) to
non-bi-
K
-invariant initial data
. To do this, we replace the constant
M_{p}
by a
function

M_{p}(u_{0})(gK)
,
g\in G
, which, however, boils down to the constants above, if the initial datum is bi-
K
-invariant. In this way, the counterexamples mentioned above, obtained simply by displaced heat kernels
h_{t}(yK,\cdot K)
,
y\notin K
, now obey convergence. Our proof is different and shorter than the one pursued in
[
NRS24
]
, which follows ideas from
[
APZ23
]
where the main work was done on the
“frequency”
side; instead, our approach mainly relies on heat kernels asymptotics, eventually obtaining more information this way.

To introduce our results, let us introduce some notation. Recall the Cartan decomposition
G=K\overline{A^{+}}K
and the Iwasawa decomposition
G=NAK
of the group
G
, according to which every
g\in G
can be written as

g=k(g)(\exp g^{+})k^{\prime}(g)
and
g=n(g)(\exp A(g))\kappa(g)
, respectively.
Consider first the weights
w_{p}
on
\mathbb{X}
:

w_{p}(gK):=e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle\rho,g^{+}\rangle},\quad 1\leq p\leq 2,\quad\text{and}\quad w_{p}(gK):=e^{\langle\rho,g^{+}\rangle},\quad 2\leq p\leq\infty.

Next, consider the following weighted
L^{1}
space defined by

L_{w_{p}}(\mathbb{X}):=\left\{u_{0}:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}:\,\int_{G}\textrm{d}x\,|u_{0}(x)|\,w_{p}(x)<+\infty\right\}.

Define also the mass functions by

M_{p}(u_{0})(g):=\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,u_{0}(yK)\,e^{\frac{2}{p}\,\langle\rho,A(k^{-1}(g)y)\rangle},\qquad 1\leq p\leq 2

and by

M_{p}(u_{0})(g):=\frac{(u_{0}*\varphi_{0})(g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)},\qquad 2\leq p\leq\infty,

(any of the two masses can be used in the critical case
p=2
).
Then our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
, and let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{X})
,
u_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(G//K)
or
u_{0}\in L_{w_{p}}(\mathbb{X})\subseteq L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
.
Consider
M_{p}(u_{0})(\cdot)
as above. Then the solution to
the heat equation (
1.5
) satisfies

(1.10)

\displaystyle\|h_{t}\|_{p}^{-1}\,\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)-M_{p}(u_{0})(\cdot)\,h_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}\,\longrightarrow\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\,t\rightarrow\infty.

In the bi-
K
-invariant case, the mass functions boil down to constants, that is
M_{p}(u_{0})(gK)=\linebreak\mathcal{H}u_{0}\left(i(2/p-1)\rho\right)
if
1\leq p\leq 2
,
M_{p}(u_{0})(gK)=\mathcal{H}u_{0}(0)
if
2\leq p\leq\infty
, and
L^{1}(G//K)\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}(G//K)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

To the best of our knowledge this is the first affirmative result, even for the case of hyperbolic space, dealing with the long-time convergence of the solution to the heat equation beyond the bi-
K
-invariant case. A mass function was considered in
[
APZ23
]
but only for the distinguished Laplacian. Clearly, if
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{X})
then
u_{0}\in L_{w_{p}}(\mathbb{X})
, but we want to emphasize that all previously known non-symmetric solutions which served as counterexamples, namely displaced heat kernels, now obey convergence. It is an interesting non-Euclidean phenomenon that the mass should be a function rather than a constant, and that the
L^{p}
propagation of the heat kernel results in dramatically different behaviors of the mass functions above and below the critical point
p=2
. For the interested reader who is not familiar with higher rank symmetric spaces, we give explicit computations of these mass functions and weights in the case of real hyperbolic space in the Appendix. Our
Theorem

1.4
is a generalization of (
1.9
) and of the main results in
[
V“’az22
,
APZ23
,
NRS24
]
for symmetric initial data, and settles the problem as it clarifies the long time behavior of solutions
which are not necessarily
symmetric.

Unlike the approach (mainly) pursued in
[
APZ23
]
or
[
NRS24
]
, we do not work on the
“frequency side”
, that is, with the use of the spherical transform or the Helgason-Fourier transform (which, in the case that the mass is a function, would be hard to handle). Instead, we work directly on
\mathbb{X}
with a careful analysis of the heat kernel in the so-called
L^{p}
critical region (for all notions and notations, we refer the reader to Section
2
).
On the other hand, whether the result of
Theorem

1.4
holds true for all
L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
initial data is still an open question for further study.

This paper is organized as follows. After the present introduction
in
Sect.

1
and preliminaries in
Sect.

2
,
we discuss
L^{p}
critical regions and mass functions in

Sect.

3
.
In
Sect.

4
, we prove our main results related to the
L^{p}
convergence of solutions to the heat equation for
1\leq p<2
, while we in Section
Sect.

5
we treat the range
2\leq p\leq\infty
. An Appendix is given in the end with explicit computations for the real hyperbolic space case.

Throughout this paper, the notation

A\lesssim{B}
between two positive expressions means that
there is a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq{C}B
.
The notation
A\asymp{B}
means that
A\lesssim{B}
and
B\lesssim{A}
. Also,
A(t)\sim B(t)
means that
A(t)/B(t)\rightarrow 1
as

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first review spherical Fourier analysis
on Riemannian symmetric spaces of non-compact type. The notation is standard
and follows
[
Hel78
,
Hel00
,
GaVa88
]
.
Next we recall the asymptotic concentration of the heat kernel.
We refer to
[
AnJi99
,
AnOs03
]
for more details on the heat kernel
analysis in this setting.

2.1.
Non-compact Riemannian symmetric spaces

Let
G
be a semi-simple Lie group, connected, non-compact, with finite center,
and
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. The homogeneous space

\mathbb{X}=G/K
is a Riemannian symmetric space of non-compact type.
Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
be the Cartan decomposition
of the Lie algebra of
G
. The Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
induces
a
K
-invariant inner product
\langle\,.\,,\,.\,\rangle
on
\mathfrak{p}
,
hence a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric on
G/K
.
We denote by
d(\,.\,,\,.\,)
the Riemannian distance on
\mathbb{X}
.

Fix a maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{p}
.
The rank of
\mathbb{X}
is the dimension
\ell
of
\mathfrak{a}
.
We identify
\mathfrak{a}
with its dual
\mathfrak{a}^{*}

by means of the inner product inherited from
\mathfrak{p}
. Sometimes we shall use coordinates on
\mathfrak{a}
; when we do, we shall always refer to the
coordinates associated to the orthonormal basis
\delta_{1},...,\delta_{\ell-1},\,\rho/|\rho|
, where
\delta_{1},...,\delta_{\ell-1},
is any orthonormal basis of
\rho^{\perp}.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathfrak{a}
be the root system of

(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
and denote by
W
the Weyl group
associated with
\Sigma
.
Once a positive Weyl chamber
\mathfrak{a}^{+}\subset\mathfrak{a}

has been selected,
\Sigma^{+}
(resp.
\Sigma_{r}^{+}

or
\Sigma_{s}^{+}
) denotes the corresponding set of positive roots
(resp. positive reduced, i.e., indivisible roots or simple roots).
Let
n
be the dimension and
\nu
be the pseudo-dimension
(or dimension at infinity) of
\mathbb{X}
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\textstyle n\,=\,\ell+\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,m_{\alpha}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\nu\,=\,\ell+2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|

where
m_{\alpha}
denotes the dimension of the positive root subspace

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\,=\,\{{X\in\mathfrak{g}\,|\,[H,X]=\langle{\alpha,H}\rangle{X},\,\forall\,H\in\mathfrak{a}}\}.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be the nilpotent Lie subalgebra
of
\mathfrak{g}
associated with
\Sigma^{+}

and let
N=\exp\mathfrak{n}
be the corresponding
Lie subgroup of
G
. We have the decompositions

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\,G\,=\,N\,(\exp\mathfrak{a})\,K\qquad&\textnormal{(Iwasawa)},\\[5.0pt]
\,G\,=\,K\,(\exp\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}})\,K\qquad&\textnormal{(Cartan)}.\end{cases}

Denote by
A(x)\in\mathfrak{a}
and
x^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}

the middle components of
x\in{G}
in these two decompositions, and by

|x|=|x^{+}|
the distance to the origin. For all
x,y\in{G}
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle|A(xK)|\,\leq\,|x|\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad|x^{+}-y^{+}|\,\leq\,d(xK,yK),

see for instance
[
AnJi99
, Lemma 2.1.2]
. For an element
x\in G
, another way to compare its middle components
A(x)
in the Iwasawa decomposition and
x^{+}
the Cartan decomposition, is the following consequence of Kostant’s convexity lemma,
[
APZ23
, Lemma 4.8]
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\langle{\rho,A(x)}\rangle\,\leq\,\langle{\rho,x^{+}}\rangle.

In the Cartan decomposition, the Haar measure
on
G
writes

\displaystyle\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,u_{0}(x)\,=\,|K/{\mathbb{M}}|\,\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k_{1}}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{+}}\,\delta(x^{+})\,\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k_{2}}\,u_{0}(k_{1}(\exp x^{+})k_{2})\,,

with density

(2.4)

\displaystyle\delta(x^{+})\,=\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,(\sinh\langle{\alpha,x^{+}}\rangle)^{m_{\alpha}}\,\asymp\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\Big(\frac{\langle\alpha,x^{+}\rangle}{1+\langle\alpha,x^{+}\rangle}\Big)^{m_{\alpha}}\,e^{2\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle}\qquad\forall\,x^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}.

Here
K
is equipped with its normalized Haar measure,

\mathbb{M}
denotes the centralizer of
\exp\mathfrak{a}
in
K
and the volume
of
K/{\mathbb{M}}
can be computed explicitly, see
[
AnJi99
, Eq (2.2.4)]
.
Recall that
\rho\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
denotes the half sum of all positive roots

\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}
counted with their multiplicities
m_{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle\rho\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,m_{\alpha}\,\alpha.

We also define

\rho_{\min}:=\min_{x^{+}\in\mathfrak{a}^{+},\;\|x^{+}\|=1}\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle\in(0,\|\rho\|].

Finally, we say that a vector
H\in\mathfrak{a}
lies on a wall if there exists a
root
\alpha\in\Sigma
such that
\langle{\alpha,H}\rangle=0
.
Otherwise, we say that
H
stays away from the walls.

2.2.
Spherical Fourier analysis

Let
\mathcal{S}(K\backslash{G}/K)
be the Schwartz space of bi-
K
-invariant
functions on
G
. The spherical Fourier transform (Harish-Chandra transform)

\mathcal{H}
is defined by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}f(\lambda)\,=\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,\varphi_{-\lambda}(x)\,u_{0}(x)\qquad\forall\,\lambda\in\mathfrak{a},\ \forall\,f\in\mathcal{S}(K\backslash{G/K}),

where
\varphi_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(K\backslash{G/K})
is the
spherical function of index
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})^{W}
the subspace
of
W
-invariant functions in the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})
.
Then
\mathcal{H}
is an isomorphism between
\mathcal{S}(K\backslash{G/K})

and
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})^{W}
. The inverse spherical Fourier transform
is given by

(2.6)

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)\,=\,C_{0}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}\,|\mathbf{c(\lambda)}|^{-2}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,\mathcal{H}f(\lambda)\qquad\forall\,x\in{G},\ \forall\,f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})^{W},

where the constant
C_{0}=2^{n-\ell}/(2\pi)^{\ell}|K/{\mathbb{M}}||W|
depends only
on the geometry of
\mathbb{X}
, and
|\mathbf{c(\lambda)}|^{-2}
is the
so-called Plancherel density. We next review some elementary facts about
the Plancherel density and the elementary spherical functions.

2.2.1.
Plancherel density

According to the Gindikin-Karpelevič formula for the Harish-Chandra

\mathbf{c}
-function, we can write the Plancherel density as

\displaystyle|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,=\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}\,\Big|\mathbf{c}_{\alpha}\Big(\frac{\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}\Big)\Big|^{-2}

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}_{\alpha}(z)\,=\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle}+\frac{1}{2}m_{\alpha})}{\Gamma(\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle})}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle}+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}m_{2\alpha})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle}+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha})}\,\frac{\Gamma(iz)}{\Gamma(iz+\frac{1}{2}m_{\alpha})}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{i}{2}z+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha})}{\Gamma(\frac{i}{2}z+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}m_{2\alpha})}.

It holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}(\lambda)\,=\,\bm{\pi}(i\lambda)\,\mathbf{c}(\lambda)\,

where
\bm{\pi}(i\lambda)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}\langle{\alpha,\lambda}\rangle
, and

\mathbf{b}(-\lambda)^{\pm 1}
is a holomorphic function for

\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}+i\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
, which has the following
behavior:

(2.7)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{b}(-\lambda)|^{\pm 1}\,\asymp\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}\,(1+|\langle{\alpha,\lambda}\rangle|)^{\mp\frac{m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha}}{2}\pm 1}.

Its derivatives can be estimated by

(2.8)

\displaystyle p(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda})\mathbf{b}(-\lambda)^{\pm 1}\,=\,\mathrm{O}\big(|\mathbf{b}(-\lambda)|^{\pm 1}\big),

where
p(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda})
is any differential
polynomial.

The following result will be needed later.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x,y\in G
such that
|x^{+}|=\textrm{O}(t)
and
y
is bounded. Then, as
t\rightarrow\infty
, it holds

\frac{\textbf{b}\left(-i\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{2t}\right)^{-1}}{\textbf{b}\left(-i\frac{x^{+}}{2t}\right)^{-1}}=1+\textnormal{O}\big(t^{-1}\big).

Proof.

Observe first that owing to (
2.7
), for all
x\in G
such that
|x^{+}|=\textrm{O}(t)
, we have

(2.9)

\textbf{b}\left(-i\frac{x^{+}}{2t}\right)^{-1}\asymp 1.

Since
y
is bounded, on the one hand we have
|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|=d(xK,yK)=\textrm{O}(t)
by the triangle inequality, and on the other hand by the mean value theorem we get

\displaystyle\left|\textbf{b}\left(-i\frac{x^{+}}{2t}\right)^{-1}-\textbf{b}\left(-i\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{2t}\right)^{-1}\right|\lesssim\left|\frac{x^{+}}{2t}-\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{2t}\right|\lesssim\frac{|y|}{t}\lesssim t^{-1}.

Here, we used the derivative bound (
2.8
) combined with (
2.9
), and the fact that

|(y^{-1}x)^{+}-x^{+}|\leq|y|,

by (
2.2
). Dividing with

\textbf{b}\left(-i\frac{x^{+}}{2t}\right)^{-1}
, we get the desired result.
∎

2.2.2.
Spherical functions

For every
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}
, the spherical function

\varphi_{\lambda}
is a smooth bi-
K
-invariant eigenfunction of all

G
-invariant differential operators on
\mathbb{X}
, in particular of the
Laplace-Beltrami operator:

-\Delta\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,=\,(\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle+|\rho|^{2})\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x).

It is symmetric in the sense that

\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{-1})=\varphi_{-\lambda}(x)
,
W
-invariant in
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}
,
and it is given by the integral representation

(2.10)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,=\,\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k}\,e^{\langle{i\lambda+\rho,\,A(kx)}\rangle}.

Furthermore, it holds

(2.11)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(y^{-1}x)\,=\,\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k}\,e^{\langle{i\lambda+\rho,\,A(kx)}\rangle}\,e^{\langle{-i\lambda+\rho,\,A(ky)}\rangle},

see for instance
[
Hel94
, Ch. III,
\lx@sectionsign 1
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Recall that all elementary spherical functions
\varphi_{\lambda}

with parameter
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}
are controlled by the ground spherical
function
\varphi_{0}
, which satisfies the global estimate

(2.12)

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(\exp{H})\,\asymp\,\Big\{\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}1+\langle\alpha,H\rangle\Big\}\,e^{-\langle\rho,H\rangle}\qquad\forall\,H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}.

Moreover, the ground spherical function satisfies

(2.13)

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(\exp{H})\,\sim\,C_{2}\,\bm{\pi}(H)\,e^{-\langle{\rho,H}\rangle}

as
\mu(H)\rightarrow\infty
, where
C_{2}=\bm{\pi}(\rho_{0})^{-1}\mathbf{b}(0)
,
see for instance
[
AnJi99
, Proposition 2.2.12.(ii)]
. Finally, for all
q>0
we have

0<\varphi_{iq\rho}(\exp H)=\varphi_{-iq\rho}(\exp H)\leq e^{q\langle\rho,H\rangle}\varphi_{0}(\exp H),

see
[
GaVa88
, Proposition 4.6.1]
. Then it becomes clear that
\varphi_{\pm i\rho}\equiv 1
and that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
implies
\int_{\mathbb{X}}\textrm{d}x\,|f(x)|\,\varphi_{\pm iq\rho}(x)<+\infty
for all
q\in(0,1)
.
For further properties of spherical functions

\varphi_{\lambda}
we refer to
[
GaVa88
, Chap.4]
and

[
Hel00
, Chap.IV]
.

2.3.
Heat kernel on symmetric spaces

The heat kernel on
\mathbb{X}
is a positive bi-
K
-invariant right
convolution kernel, i.e.,
h_{t}(xK,yK)=h_{t}(y^{-1}x)>0
,
which is thus determined by its restriction
to the positive Weyl chamber.
According to the inversion formula of the spherical Fourier transform,
the heat kernel is given by

(2.14)

\displaystyle h_{t}(x)\,=\,C_{0}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}\,|\mathbf{c(\lambda)}|^{-2}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,e^{-t(|\lambda|^{2}+|\rho|^{2})}

and satisfies the global estimate

(2.15)

\displaystyle h_{t}(x)\,\asymp\,t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\,\Big\{{\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}(1+t+\langle{\alpha,x^{+}}\rangle)^{\frac{m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha}}{2}-1}}\Big\}\,\varphi_{0}(x)e^{-|\rho|^{2}t-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}

for all
t>0
and
x^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
,
see
[
AnJi99
,
AnOs03
]
.

Let
x\in G
. If

\mu(x^{+})=\min_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\langle{\alpha,x^{+}}\rangle\rightarrow\infty
, so in this sense
x^{+}
stays away from walls, or if
|x|=\textrm{O}(t)
, then the large time
behavior of the heat kernel
h_{t}(x)
can be described more accurately by the following
asymptotics
[
AnJi99
, Theorem 5.1.1]
:

(2.16)

\displaystyle h_{t}(\exp x^{+})\,\sim\,C_{1}\,t^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}\,\mathbf{b}\big(-i\tfrac{x^{+}}{2t}\big)^{-1}\,\varphi_{0}(\exp{x^{+}})\,e^{-|\rho|^{2}t-\frac{|x^{+}|^{2}}{4t}}

as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
Here
C_{1}=C_{0}2^{-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}|W|\pi^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\bm{\pi}(\rho_{0})\mathbf{b}(0)^{-1}
is a positive constant, and
\rho_{0}

denotes the half sum of all positive reduced roots.

3.
Critical regions and mass functions

In this section we discuss the notion
L^{p}
concentration of the heat kernel, which will be a crucial concept for our results.

To begin with, it is well-known that the behavior of the
L^{p}
norms
\|h_{t}\|_{p}
of the heat kernel
h_{t}
can be deduced from the pointwise estimates in (
2.15
). More precisely, for
t
large, it was proved in
[
AnJi99
, Proposition 4.1]
that

\left\|h_{t}\right\|_{p}\asymp\begin{cases}t^{-\frac{\ell}{2p^{\prime}}}e^{-\frac{4}{pp^{\prime}}|\rho|^{2}t}&\text{ if }1\leq p<2\\
t^{-\frac{\ell}{4}-\frac{|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}{2}}e^{-|\rho|^{2}t}&\text{ if }p=2,\\
t^{-\frac{\ell}{2}-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}e^{-|\rho|^{2}t}&\text{ if }2<p\leq+\infty.\end{cases}

Davies
[
Dav89
, Corollary 5.7.3]
, observed that heat diffusion on real hyperbolic spaces propagates asymptotically with finite speed. This striking phenomenon was later extended to non-compact symmetric spaces, by
analytic means in
[
AnSe92
]
and probabilistic ones in
[
B94
]
. The general
L^{p}
setting was considered in
[
AnJi99
]
. More precisely, the following lemma shows where the heat kernel

h_{t}
concentrates for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We denote by
(\widehat{H,\rho})
the angle between
H\in\mathfrak{a}
and
\rho
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
AnJi99
, Theorem 4.1.2.]

(i) Case
1\leq p<2
: For
t
large, consider the box

B_{p}(t)=\left\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:\quad 2|\rho_{p}|t-r(t)\leq|H|\leq 2|\rho_{p}|t+r(t),\quad(\widehat{H,\rho})\leq\theta(t)\right\}

centered at the point
2t\rho_{p}:=4\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right)t\rho
in
\mathfrak{a}
, where
r(t)
and
\theta(t)
are positive with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ l }{\frac{r(t)}{t}\rightarrow 0}\\
{\frac{r(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\rightarrow+\infty}\end{array}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\theta(t)\rightarrow 0\\
\sqrt{t}\theta(t)\rightarrow+\infty\end{array}\quad\text{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty.\right.\right.

Then the
L^{p}
norm of
h_{t}
concentrates asymptotically in the bi-K-orbit of
\exp B_{p}(t)
:

\left\|h_{t}\right\|_{p}^{-1}\left\{\int_{G\backslash K\left\{\exp B_{p}(t)\right\}K}dx\left|h_{t}(x)\right|^{p}\right\}^{1/p}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty.

(ii) Case
p=2
: We have a similar concentration

\left\|h_{t}\right\|_{2}^{-1}\left\{\int_{G\backslash K\left\{\exp B_{2}(t)\right\}K}dx\left|h_{t}(x)\right|^{2}\right\}^{1/2}\longrightarrow 0

with respect to the box

B_{2}(t)=\left\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:\quad r_{1}(t)\leq|H|\leq r_{2}(t),\quad\omega(H)\geq r_{3}(t)\right\},

where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ l }{r_{1}(t)\rightarrow+\infty}\\
{\frac{r_{1}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\rightarrow 0}\end{array},\quad\frac{r_{2}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\rightarrow+\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}r_{3}(t)\rightarrow+\infty\\
\frac{r_{3}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\rightarrow 0\end{array}\quad\text{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty.\right.\right.

(iii) Case
2<p<+\infty
:

\left\|h_{t}\right\|_{p}^{-1}\left\{\int_{|x|\geq R(t)}dx\left|h_{t}(x)\right|^{p}\right\}^{1/p}\longrightarrow 0

if
\frac{R(t)}{\log t}\rightarrow+\infty
,
\frac{R(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(iv) Case
p=+\infty
:
h_{t}(x)
reaches its maximum at
x=e
and

\left\|h_{t}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\sup_{|x|\geq R(t)}h_{t}(x)\longrightarrow 0

if
\frac{R(t)}{\log t}\rightarrow+\infty
,
\frac{R(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Notice that
p=2
is the only case which resembles the Euclidean setting.
We will be referring to the above domains of heat concentration as
L^{p}
critical regions
. It is also worth mentioning that rates of convergence can be explicitly computed, and they will depend on the quantities in
t
used to define the regions, by using the heat kernel estimates (
2.15
). For instance, in the
L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
case, rates can be found in
[
AnSe92
]
or
[
APZ23
, Lemma 2.1]
, but for the sake of brevity we will avoid doing so.

We now consider the following mass functions of initial data, whenever the integrals make sense: set

M_{p}(u_{0})(g):=\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,u_{0}(yK)\,e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle\rho,A(k(g)^{-1}y)\rangle},\qquad 1\leq p<2

where
g=k(g)(\exp g^{+})k^{\prime}(g)
in the Cartan decomposition, while we set

M_{p}(u_{0})(g):=\frac{(u_{0}*\varphi_{0})(g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)},\qquad 2\leq p\leq\infty.

Clearly, these functions are right-
K
-invariant, so they descend to functions on
\mathbb{X}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Consider the weights
w_{p}
on
\mathbb{X}
given by

{w}_{p}(gK)=e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle\rho,g^{+}\rangle},\quad k\in K,\quad\text{where}\quad g=k(g)(\exp g^{+})k^{\prime}(g),\quad 1\leq p<2,

and

{w}_{p}(gK)=e^{\langle\rho,g^{+}\rangle},\quad 2\leq p\leq\infty,

and recall that by (
2.3
) that

\langle\rho,A(kg)\rangle\leq\langle\rho,(kg)^{+}\rangle=\langle\rho,g^{+}\rangle\quad\text{for all}\quad k\in K.

Assume first that
1\leq p<2
. Then it becomes clear that for initial data
u_{0}
such that

\int_{G}\textrm{d}g\,|u_{0}(gK)|\,e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle\rho,g^{+}\rangle}<+\infty,

the mass function
M_{p}(u_{0})(\cdot)
for
1\leq p<2
is bounded.

Let us now discuss the case
2\leq p\leq\infty
and the case of initial data
u_{0}
such that

\int_{G}\textrm{d}g\,|u_{0}(gK)|\,e^{\langle\rho,g^{+}\rangle}<+\infty.

Owing to (
2.11
), we have the following local Harnack inequality:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)\,=\,\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k}\,e^{\langle\rho,A(kg)\rangle}\,e^{\langle{\rho,A(ky)}\rangle}\,\leq e^{\langle\rho,y^{+}\rangle}\,\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k}\,e^{\langle{\rho,A(kg)\rangle}}\,\leq\,e^{\langle\rho,y^{+}\rangle}\,\varphi_{0}(g),

which holds for every

y,g\in{G}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(u_{0}*\varphi_{0})(g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\right|\leq\varphi_{0}(g)^{-1}\int_{G}\textrm{d}y\,|u_{0}(y)|\,\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)\leq\int_{G}\textrm{d}y\,|u_{0}(y)|\,e^{\langle\rho,y^{+}\rangle}.

This establishes that the mass function
M_{p}(u_{0})(\cdot)
,
2\leq p\leq\infty
, is bounded.

Clearly, the mass functions
M_{p}(u_{0})(\cdot)
are continuous and bounded for all
p\in[1,\infty]
if
{u}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{X})
, since
\langle\rho,y^{+}\rangle\leq|\rho||y|
.

Remark 3.3
.

It is remarkable the different behavior of mass functions depending on whether
p
is above or below the critical point
p=2
. This contrast is a consequence of the different
L^{p}
heat concentration. However, it is worth mentioning that for
p=2
a mass function can be defined both ways and we can get for both the
L^{2}
result of
Theorem

1.4
. We refer for this to
Proposition

4.6
, which emphasizes that the case
p=2
is indeed critical. Let us elaborate.

Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{X}})
.
Denote by
\mathbb{M}
the the centralizer of
\exp{\mathfrak{a}}
in
K
.
Recall that the Helgason-Fourier transform

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}u_{0}(\lambda,k\mathbb{M})\,=\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,u_{0}(gK)\,e^{\langle{-i\lambda+\rho,\,A(k^{-1}g)}\rangle},

boils down to the transform (
2.5
)
when

is bi-
K
-invariant.
It follows that

M_{p}(u_{0})(g)=\mathcal{H}u_{0}\left(i\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\rho,k(g)\mathbb{M}\right),\quad 1\leq p\leq 2.

Consider now on the one hand the spherical principal series, which are realized on the boundary
K/\mathbb{M}
by

\bigl[\pi_{\lambda}(y)\,\xi\bigr](kM)=\underbrace{e^{-\langle\rho+i\lambda,H(y^{-1}k)\rangle}}_{e^{\langle\rho+i\lambda,A(k^{-1}y)\rangle}}\xi(\kappa(y^{-1}k)\mathbb{M})\,,

where

in the Iwasawa decomposition

.
Then

is equal to

(3.3)

\bigl[\pi_{-i(2/p-1)\rho}(u_{0})\,1\bigr](k(g)\mathbb{M})=\int_{G}dy\,u_{0}(y)\,\bigl[\pi_{-i(2/p-1)\rho}(y)\,1\bigr](k(g)\mathbb{M})

or to the Helgason-Fourier transform, as already mentioned,
evaluated at
\lambda=i(2/p-1)\rho
and
k(g)\mathbb{M}
. On the other hand, notice that

\varphi_{\pm i(2/p-1)\rho}(g)=\langle\pi_{\mp i(2/p-1)\rho}(x)\,1,1\rangle\,,

hence

\tfrac{u_{0}\,*\,\varphi_{\pm i(2/p-1)\rho}(g)}{\varphi_{\pm i(2/p-1)\rho}(g)}=\tfrac{\langle\pi_{\mp i(2/p-1)\rho}(g)\,1,\pi_{\pm i(2/p-1)\rho}(u_{0})1\rangle}{\langle\pi_{\mp i(2/p-1)\rho}(g)\,1,1\rangle}

is quite different from (
3.3
), so the expression of the mass function for

does not resemble its corresponding expression for
p>2
.

Remark 3.4
.

If
u_{0}
is in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(G//K)
, then we notice that for every
p
the mass function boils down to a constant. Indeed, dealing with
p\in[1,2]
first,
observe that for any
k\in K
we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,u_{0}(yK)\,e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle\rho,A(ky)\rangle}=\mathcal{H}{u}_{0}\left(\pm i(\frac{2}{p}-1)\rho\right)=\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,\varphi_{(\frac{2}{p}-1)i\rho}u_{0}(yK).

Notice that when

, we simply obtain
\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,u_{0}(yK)

. Thus we obtain the mass usually considered in the Euclidean case or in
[
V“’az22
,
APZ23
]
. Hence the mass function boils down to a constant, namely to

\displaystyle{M}_{p}\,=\,\mathcal{H}u_{0}(\pm(2/p-1)i\rho)\quad\text{if}\quad f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(G//K),\quad p\in[1,2].

Similarly, when

,

\displaystyle\frac{(u_{0}*\varphi_{0})(g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,

\displaystyle=\,\tfrac{1}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,u_{0}(y)\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)

\displaystyle=\,\tfrac{1}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,u_{0}(y)\,\underbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k}\,\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}kg)}_{\varphi_{0}(g)\,\varphi_{0}(y)}\,=\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,u_{0}(y)\,\varphi_{0}(y).

In this sense, our notion of mass generalizes all constant masses considered before in

4.

Our aim in this section is to prove the

In this section we will assume that with the notation of

\theta(t)
in the description of the

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p<2
and
y\in{G}\smallsetminus{K}
,
|y|<\xi
.
Then, for every

in the critical region
K(\exp B_{p}(t))K

\displaystyle\frac{h_{t}(gK,yK)}{h_{t}(gK,eK)}\,=\,e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle{\rho,\,A(k(g)^{-1}y)}\rangle}+\textnormal{O}\big(\tfrac{r(t)}{t}\big)\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\,t\rightarrow\infty,

where

in the Cartan decomposition.

For the proof of
Proposition

4.1
we need the following two lemmas, which describe the effect of a small translation on the critical region
K\{\exp B_{p}(t)\}K
, for
1\leq p\leq 2
. For our purposes, a modification of certain arguments in
[
APZ23
]
for

(i)). However, we have different propagation behaviors for

which we also want to consider later. Moreover, since the arguments in

]
were directly related to the heat equation, we prefer to present the following two results which are rather general and might be useful to other contexts. Recall that
(\widehat{H,\rho})
denotes the angle between
H\in\mathfrak{a}
and
\rho

Lemma 4.2
.

Define in
\mathfrak{a}
the set

\Theta_{t}=\{\,x^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:\quad|x^{+}|\geq R(t),\quad(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})\leq\theta(t)\,\},

where

and
\theta(t)
are positive and monotonic, such that

R(t)\rightarrow+\infty,\qquad\theta(t)\rightarrow 0,\qquad R(t)^{-1}=\textrm{O}(\theta(t)^{2}).

Let
x\in K(\exp\Theta_{t})K
and let
y
be bounded. Then as
t\rightarrow\infty
,

(i)

\frac{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}{|x^{+}|},\frac{|x^{+}|}{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}

are both equal to
1+\textnormal{O}\big(R(t)^{-1}\big)
.

(ii)

\frac{x^{+}}{|x^{+}|}
and
\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}

are both equal to

\frac{\rho}{|\rho|}+\textnormal{O}\big(\theta(t)\big)
.

(iii)

For every
\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}
,

\frac{\langle{\alpha,(y^{-1}x)^{+}}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,x^{+}}\rangle}=1+\textnormal{O}\big(\theta(t)\big)
.

(iv)

d(xK,eK)-d(xK,yK)=\langle{\frac{\rho}{|\rho|},A(k(x)^{-1}y)}\rangle+\textnormal{O}\big(\theta(t)\big)
,
x=k(x)(\exp x^{+})k^{\prime}(x)
.

Proof.

The proof is an adaptation of
[
P24
, Lemma 3.7]
. Assume that
d(xK,eK)\geq R(t)
and
d(yK,eK)\leq\xi
, which implies by the triangle inequality that
d(xK,yK)\geq\frac{1}{2}\,R(t)
, for

large enough.

We deduce first
(i)
by using

\displaystyle\frac{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}{|x^{+}|}\,=\frac{d(xK,yK)}{d(xK,eK)}=\,1+\textrm{O}\big(R(t)^{-1}\big).

The second assertion follows similarly.

Next, for (ii), since
\cos(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})=1+\textrm{O}(\theta(t)^{2})
, we first have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{x^{+}}{|x^{+}|}-\frac{\rho}{|\rho|}\right|^{2}=2\left(1-\langle\frac{\rho}{|\rho|},\frac{x^{+}}{|x^{+}|}\rangle\right)=\textrm{O}(\theta(t)^{2}),

while for the second asymptotics in (ii), we work similarly, observing that
(y^{-1}x)^{+}=x^{+}+\textrm{O}(1)
implies

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\rho}{|\rho|},\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{|x^{+}|}{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}\langle\frac{\rho}{|\rho|},\frac{x^{+}}{|x^{+}|}\rangle+\textrm{O}(|x|^{-1})

\displaystyle=\langle\frac{\rho}{|\rho|},\frac{x^{+}}{|x^{+}|}\rangle+\textrm{O}(R(t)^{-1})

(4.2)

\displaystyle=1+\textrm{O}(\theta(t)^{2}),

using (i), (
4.1
) and the fact that
R(t)^{-1}=\textrm{O}(\theta(t)^{2})
.

Let us next deduce (iii) from
(i)
and
(ii)
.
For every positive root
\alpha

\displaystyle\frac{\langle{\alpha,(y^{-1}x)^{+}}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,x^{+}}\rangle}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\langle{\alpha,\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\frac{x^{+}}{|x^{+}|}}\rangle}\,\frac{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}{|x^{+}|}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\langle{\alpha,\frac{\rho}{|\rho|}}\rangle+\textrm{O}\big(\theta(t)\big)}{\langle{\alpha,\frac{\rho}{|\rho|}}\rangle+\textrm{O}\big(\theta(t)\big)}\,\Big\{{1+\textrm{O}\big(R(t)^{-1}\big)}\Big\}\,=\,1+\textrm{O}(\theta(t)),

by the assumption on

and the fact that

decreases to

, Lemma 3.8]
. To lighten notation, write

in the Cartan decomposition and consider the Iwasawa decomposition

for some

. Then

\displaystyle d(xK,yK)\,

\displaystyle=\,d\big(k(\exp{x^{+}})K,kn(k^{-1}y)(\exp{A(k^{-1}y)})K\big)

\displaystyle=\,d\big(\exp{(-x^{+})}[n(k^{-1}y)]^{-1}(\exp{x^{+}})K,\exp{(A(k^{-1}y)}-x^{+})K\big).

and we write

\displaystyle d(xK,eK)-d(xK,yK)\,

\displaystyle=\,\overbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}d(xK,eK)-d\big(\exp{(A(k^{-1}y)}-x^{+})K,eK\big)}^{I}

\displaystyle+\,\underbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}d\big(\exp{(A(k^{-1}y)}-x^{+})K,eK\big)-d(xK,yK)}_{II}.

On the one hand,

tends exponentially fast to

\displaystyle I\,=\,|x^{+}|-|A(k^{-1}y)-x^{+}|\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{2\langle{x^{+},A(k^{-1}y)}\rangle-|A(k^{-1}y)|^{2}}{|x^{+}|+|A(k^{-1}y)-x^{+}|}

\displaystyle=\,\big\langle{\tfrac{x^{+}}{|x^{+}|},A(k^{-1}y)}\big\rangle\,+\textrm{O}\big(\tfrac{1}{|x^{+}|}\big)

\displaystyle=\,\big\langle{\tfrac{\rho}{|\rho|},A(k^{-1}y)}\big\rangle\,+\textrm{O}\big(\theta(t)\big)

by using
(ii)
, the fact that

is a compact subset of

and that

. Then,

\zeta_{\ell}\asymp|g^{+}|,\quad\frac{|\zeta|}{\zeta_{\ell}}=\textrm{O}(\varepsilon(t)).

Therefore, for any
\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}
,

\frac{\langle\alpha,g^{+}\rangle}{\zeta_{\ell}}=\langle\beta,\frac{\zeta}{\zeta_{\ell}}\rangle+\beta_{\ell}\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\beta_{\ell}

for
t
large enough, thus the claim follows by
\min_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\langle\alpha,g^{+}\rangle\geq\text{const}(\Sigma)\,\zeta_{\ell}\gtrsim|g^{+}|\gtrsim\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}
.

It remains therefore to prove, as already mentioned, that

\|h_{t}\|_{2}^{-1}\,\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)-\widetilde{M}_{2}(u_{0})(\cdot)\,h_{t}\|_{L^{2}(K\{\exp\widetilde{B}_{2}(t)\}K)}\,\longrightarrow\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\,t\rightarrow\infty.

To this end, since

(4.16)

\displaystyle u(t,g)\,-\,\widetilde{M}_{2}(u_{0})(g)\,h_{t}(g)\,=\,h_{t}(g)\,\int_{K\exp\{B(0,\xi)\}K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,u_{0}(yK)\,\Big\{{\frac{h_{t}(y^{-1}g)}{h_{t}(g)}-e^{\langle\rho,A(k(g)^{-1}y)\rangle}\Big\}},

we see that the crucial ingredient is asymptotics of the heat kernel quotient
h_{t}(y^{-1}g)/h_{t}(g)
, when
g\in K\{\exp\widetilde{B}_{2}(t)\}K
and
|y|<\xi
. On the one hand, it is proven in
[
APZ23
, Lemma 4.7]
that

(4.17)

\frac{h_{t}(y^{-1}g)}{h_{t}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}},\qquad\forall\,g\in{K\{\exp\widetilde{B}_{2}(t)\}K},\,\,\forall\,|y|<\xi,

where
\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}\rightarrow+\infty
,
\varepsilon(t)\rightarrow 0
. On the other hand, one can obtain

\displaystyle g^{-1}y\,\in\,K\{\exp\widetilde{B}_{2}(t)^{\prime}\}K\qquad\forall\,g\in{K\{\exp\widetilde{B}_{2}(t)\}K},\,\,\forall\,|y|<\xi,

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}_{2}(t)^{\prime}\,=\,\big\{{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:\quad\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}(t)\sqrt{t}\leq|H|\leq\tfrac{2\sqrt{t}}{\varepsilon(t)}\,\,\,\textnormal{and}\,\,\,(\widehat{H,\rho})=\textrm{O}(\varepsilon(t))}\big\}.

This follows from the proof of
Lemma

4.3
concerning the effect of a small translation to the angle with the
\rho
axis, while for the radii the claim follows simply by the triangle inequality. Thus the asymptotics (
2.13
) for the ground spherical function hold true (notice that these are not available if
g
lies in any
L^{p}
critical region for
p>2
, since those contain the origin). In fact, according to
[
APZ23
, Proposition 4.2]
the asymptotics for
\varphi_{0}
can be sharpened in the present region:

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(\exp{H})\,=\,\Big\{{C_{3}+\textnormal{O}\big(\tfrac{1}{\mu(H)}}\big)\Big\}\,\bm{\pi}(H)\,e^{-\langle{\rho,H}\rangle},

for some explicit constant
C_{3}>0
. Then

\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,=\underbrace{\tfrac{C_{3}+\mathrm{O}\big(\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}}\big)}{C_{3}+\mathrm{O}\big(\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}}\big)}}_{1+\mathrm{O}\big(\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}}\big)}\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}\frac{\langle\alpha,(y^{-1}g)^{+}\rangle}{\langle\alpha,g^{+}\rangle}\,e^{-\langle{\rho,(y^{-1}g)^{+}}\rangle+\langle{\rho,g^{+}}\rangle}.

Thus, invoking
Lemma

4.2
(iii) and (iv) for
R(t)=\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}
and
\theta(t)=\varepsilon(t)
we get

(4.18)

\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}=e^{\langle\rho,A(k(g)^{-1}y)\rangle}+\textrm{O}(\varepsilon(t)).

From (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) it follows that

\frac{h_{t}(y^{-1}g)}{h_{t}(g)}=e^{\langle\rho,A(k(g)^{-1}y)\rangle}+\textrm{O}(\varepsilon(t)),\quad g\in K\{\exp\widetilde{B}_{2}(t)\}K,\quad|y|<\xi,

which can be in turn used in (
4.16
), so that with standard arguments as in the case
1\leq p<2
one gets the
L^{2}
convergence. The proof is now complete.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

In
[
NRS24
]
it is proved that if
u_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(G//K)
,
1\leq p\leq 2
, then the only
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\|h_{t}\|_{p}^{-1}\,\|u_{0}\ast h_{t}-z\,h_{t}\|_{p}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty,\quad 1\leq p\leq 2.

is
z=M_{p}=\int_{G}\textrm{d}y\,u_{0}(y)\,\varphi_{\pm i(2/p-1)\rho}(y)=\mathcal{H}u_{0}(\pm i(2/p-1)\rho)
. However, this is not the case if one allows for functions, instead of constants. Let us elaborate: let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(G//K)
and
s>0
, and consider the function

\displaystyle M_{p}^{s}(u_{0})(g)

\displaystyle:=\int_{G}\textrm{d}y\,\frac{2+d(gK,yK)^{s}}{1+d(gK,yK)^{s}}\,u_{0}(y)\,\varphi_{\pm i(2/p-1)\rho}(y)

\displaystyle=M_{p}+\int_{G}\textrm{d}y\,\frac{1}{1+d(gK,yK)^{s}}\,u_{0}(y)\,\varphi_{\pm i(2/p-1)\rho}(y).

Clearly, this is well-defined and bounded for
u_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(G//K)
(observe that
|M_{p}^{s}(u_{0})(g)|\leq 2\int_{G}|u_{0}|\,\varphi_{\pm i(2/p-1)\rho}
for all
g\in G
). Thus outside the
L^{p}
critical region of
Theorem

3.1
, a crude triangle inequality yields

\|h_{t}\|_{p}^{-1}\,\|u_{0}\ast h_{t}-M_{p}^{s}(u_{0})(\cdot)\,h_{t}\|_{L^{p}(G\smallsetminus K\{\exp B_{p}(t)\}K)}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty,\quad 1\leq p\leq 2.

Now let us discuss convergence inside the critical region. For
g\in K\{\exp B_{p}(t)\}K
and
y
bounded, we have
d(gK,yK)\gtrsim t^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}
for all
1\leq p\leq 2
if
t
is sufficiently large. In other words,
M_{p}^{s}(u_{0})(g)=M_{p}+\textrm{O}\left(\mathcal{H}|u_{0}|(i(2/p-1)\rho)\,t^{s(-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)}\right),
when
g\in K\{\exp B_{p}(t)\}K
. The desired
L^{p}
convergence result in
K\{\exp B_{p}(t)\}K
now follows from (
1.9
) and the triangle inequality. Altogether, we have shown that

\|h_{t}\|_{p}^{-1}\,\|u_{0}\ast h_{t}-M_{p}^{s}(u_{0})(\cdot)\,h_{t}\|_{p}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty,\quad 1\leq p\leq 2,\quad\forall s>0.

5.
L^{p}
convergence,
2\leq p\leq\infty

Our aim in this section is to prove the
L^{p}
asymptotics (
1.10
) for
p\in[2,\infty]
. To this end, we again turn most of our attention, as in the
1\leq p<2
case, in the
L^{p}
critical region as defined in
Theorem

3.1
. We aim again to use heat kernel asymptotics; these are still available since
\frac{|x|}{t}\rightarrow 0
when
x\in K\{\exp B_{p}(t)\}K
, according to (
2.16
); however,
x^{+}
can be near the walls for
p>2
. For the case
p=2
, we consider throughout this whole section that the
L^{2}
concentration region is

\displaystyle B_{2}(t)\,=\,\big\{{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:\quad\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}\leq|H|\leq\tfrac{\sqrt{t}}{\varepsilon(t)}\,\,\,\textnormal{and}\,\,\,\mu(H)\geq\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}}\big\},

where
\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}\rightarrow+\infty
,
\varepsilon(t)\rightarrow 0
.

The following lemma plays a key role in the proof of
Theorem

1.4
for
p\in[2,\infty]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
2\leq p\leq\infty
and let
\delta_{p}(t)
be the positive function given by

\delta_{2}(t)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}}
if
p=2
, where
\varepsilon(t)\sqrt{t}\rightarrow+\infty
,
\varepsilon(t)\rightarrow 0
, and given by
\delta_{p}(t)=\frac{R(t)}{\sqrt{t}}
if
p\in(2,+\infty]
, where
\frac{R(t)}{\log t}\rightarrow+\infty
,
\frac{R(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\rightarrow 0
.
Then, for bounded
y\in{G}
and for all
g
in the
L^{p}
critical region

K\{\exp B_{p}(t)\}K
, the following asymptotic behavior holds:

\displaystyle\frac{h_{t}(y^{-1}g)}{h_{t}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,=\,\mathrm{O}(\delta_{p}(t))\qquad\textnormal{as}\,\,\,t\longrightarrow\infty.

Proof.

For
p=2
, the claim has been established in
[
APZ23
, Lemma 4.7]
. For
p\in(2,+\infty]
, a careful inspection of
[
Ank91
, p.1060, Step 5]
reveals that one can show that

h_{t}(g)=\left(C_{0}+\textrm{O}\left(\frac{R(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right)\,t^{-\frac{\ell}{2}-|\Sigma^{++}|}\,e^{-|\rho|^{2}t}\,\varphi_{0}(g)\,e^{-\frac{|g^{+}|^{2}}{4t}}

(the constant
C_{0}
can be computed explicitly) when
|g^{+}|\leq R(t)=\textrm{o}(\sqrt{t})
. Thus since
y
is bounded, we also have
|(y^{-1}g)^{+}|=\textrm{O}(R(t))
by the triangle inequality. Therefore we can write

\displaystyle\frac{h_{t}(y^{-1}g)}{h_{t}(g)}\,=\,\underbrace{\tfrac{C_{0}+\textrm{O}\left(\frac{R(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\big)}{C_{0}+\textrm{O}\left(\frac{R(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\right)}}_{1+\mathrm{O}\big(\frac{R(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\big)}\,\,\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,e^{-\tfrac{|(y^{-1}g)^{+}|^{2}}{4t}+\tfrac{|g^{+}|^{2}}{4t}}

and

\displaystyle\exp\{-\tfrac{|(y^{-1}g)^{+}|^{2}}{4t}+\tfrac{|g^{+}|^{2}}{4t}\}=\exp\{\underbrace{\frac{|(y^{-1}g)^{+}|+|g^{+}|}{4t}}_{=\,\textrm{O}\left(\frac{R(t)}{t}\right)}\,(\underbrace{|g^{+}|-|(y^{-1}g)^{+}|}_{=\,\textrm{O}(1)})\}=1+\textrm{O}\left(\frac{R(t)}{t}\right).

Hence, since by (
3.1
) the quotient
\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)/\varphi_{0}(g)
is bounded when
y
is bounded, we conclude

\displaystyle\frac{h_{t}(y^{-1}g)}{h_{t}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,=\,\textrm{O}\left(\frac{R(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

∎

We are now ready to prove the
L^{p}
convergence of
Theorem

1.4
,
2\leq p\leq\infty
, for continuous and compactly supported initial data.

Proof.

Assume that
\supp u_{0}\subseteq K\{\exp B(0,\xi)\}K
, for some
\xi>0
. We aim to study the difference

\displaystyle u(t,g)\,-\,M_{p}(u_{0})(g)\,h_{t}(g)\,

\displaystyle=(u_{0}*h_{t})(g)\,-\,{h}_{t}(g)\,\frac{(u_{0}*\varphi_{0})(g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}

(5.1)

\displaystyle=\,h_{t}(g)\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,u_{0}(yK)\,\Big\{{\frac{h_{t}(y^{-1}g)}{h_{t}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}}\Big\}.

According to the previous lemma, we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{t}(y^{-1}g)}{h_{t}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,=\,\mathrm{O}(\delta_{p}(t))\qquad\forall\,g\in K\{\exp B_{p}(t)\}K,\,\,\forall\,y\in\textrm{supp}u_{0}.

Then, for
2\leq p<\infty
the
L^{p}
integral of

{u}(t,\cdot)-M_{p}(u_{0})(\cdot)\,{h}_{t}
over the critical region satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\int_{K\left\{\exp B_{p}(t)\right\}K}\textrm{d}{g}\,|u(t,g)\,-\,{M}_{p}(u_{0})(g)\,{h}_{t}(g)|^{p}\,\right)^{1/p}\lesssim\,\delta_{p}(t)\,\underbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}\left(\int_{G}\textrm{d}{g}\,{h}_{t}(g)^{p}\right)^{1/p}}_{\|h_{t}\|_{p}}\,\|u_{0}\|_{1},

hence it is
\textrm{o}(\|h_{t}\|_{p})
; meanwhile, for
p=\infty
, we get from (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) that

\|h_{t}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\sup_{|g|\leq R(t)}|u(t,\,g)\,-\,M_{\infty}(u_{0})(g)\,h_{t}(g)|\lesssim\textrm{O}(\delta_{\infty}(t))\,\|u_{0}\|_{1}.

Let us now work outside the critical region. As in the case of
1\leq p<2
, it will become evident that now the expression of the mass function
M_{p}(u_{0})
plays no particular role; instead, its important feature will simply be that it is bounded when
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{X})
.

We deal first with the case
p=\infty
. We have

|u(t,g)\,-\,M_{\infty}(u_{0})(g)\,h_{t}(g)|\leq\sup_{|g|>R(t)}|h_{t}(g)|\|u_{0}\|_{1}+\sup_{|g|>R(t)}|M_{\infty}(u_{0})(g)||h_{t}(g)|.

Using the estimates (
2.12
) for the heat kernel and (
2.12
) for the ground spherical function, we deduce that there are
N_{1},N_{2}>0
such that for
|g|>R(t)
,

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\,e^{|\rho|^{2}t}\,h_{t}(g)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{N_{1}}\,(1+|g|)^{N_{2}}\,e^{-\langle\rho,g^{+}\rangle}\lesssim t^{N_{1}}\,(1+|g|)^{N_{2}}\,e^{-\rho_{\textrm{min}}|g|}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-N}\qquad\forall N>0,

since
\frac{R(t)}{\log t}\rightarrow+\infty
. It follows by the boundedness of
M_{\infty}(u_{0})
that

\|h_{t}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\sup_{|g|>R(t)}|u(t,g)-M_{\infty}(u_{0})(g)\,h_{t}(g)|\lesssim t^{-N}\qquad\forall N>0.

Finally we treat the case
2\leq p<\infty
. Thus we have to check that the
L^{p}
integral,
p\geq 2

\displaystyle\left(\int_{G\smallsetminus K\left\{\exp B_{p}(t)\right\}K}\textrm{d}{g}\,|u(t,g)\,-M_{p}(u_{0})(g)\,{h}_{t}(g)|^{p}\,\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\,\left(\int_{G\smallsetminus K\left\{\exp B_{p}(t)\right\}K}\textrm{d}{g}\,|u(t,g)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\,\left(\int_{G\smallsetminus K\left\{\exp B_{p}(t)\right\}K}\textrm{d}{g}\,|M_{p}(u_{0})(g)|^{p}\,h_{t}(g)^{p}\right)^{1/p}

is also
\textrm{o}(\|h_{t}\|_{p})
. On the one hand, for the second summand above, we know that
{M_{p}(u_{0})}
is bounded
and that the heat kernel
{h}_{t}
asymptotically concentrates in the
L^{p}
sense in the region

K\left\{\exp B_{p}(t)\right\}K
, hence

\displaystyle\|h_{t}\|_{p}^{-1}\,\int_{G\smallsetminus K\left\{\exp B_{p}(t)\right\}K}\textrm{d}{g}\,|{M}_{p}(u_{0})(g)|^{p}\,{h}_{t}(g)^{p}\,\longrightarrow\,0

as
t\rightarrow\infty
. On the other hand, we distinguish cases in
p
for the second summand. If
p=2
, then notice that for all

y\in\supp{u_{0}}
and for all
g\in{G}
such that

g^{+}\notin B_{2}(t)
one has

(5.3)

\displaystyle(y^{-1}g)^{+}\,\notin\,{B_{2}(t)}^{\prime\prime}=\,\big\{{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\,|\,\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(t)\sqrt{t}\leq|H|\leq\tfrac{\sqrt{t}}{\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(t)}\,\,\,\textnormal{and}\,\,\,\mu(H)\geq\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(t)\sqrt{t}}\big\}

where
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(t)=2\varepsilon(t)
(see the proof of
[
APZ23
, Lemma 4.7]
). Similarly, if
p>2
, then observe that for all

y\in\supp{u_{0}}
and for all
g\in B_{p}(t)
then

\displaystyle(y^{-1}g)^{+}\,\notin\,{B_{p}(t)}^{\prime\prime}=\,\left\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:\quad|H|\leq\frac{R(t)}{2}\right\},\qquad p>2.

Hence an application of the Minkowski inequality for integrals, as in the case
1\leq p<2
, yields

\displaystyle\|u(t,\,\cdot\,)\|_{L^{p}(G\smallsetminus K\left\{\exp B_{p}(t)\right\}K)}=\textrm{o}(\|h_{t}\|_{p}).

This concludes the proof of the heat asymptotics in
L^{p}
,
2\leq p\leq\infty
and for initial data
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{X})
.
∎

6.
Appendix

In this section, we focus only on real hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}=\mathbb{H}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
. We write down explicitly the mass functions
M_{p}
, as well as the weighted
L^{1}
spaces, hoping that it will be illuminating to those who are more familiar to hyperbolic space than arbitrary rank symmetric spaces. Let us also point out that in this Appendix we will not always write down the error terms of asymptotics, for reasons of brevity. We first introduce some notation.

We use the model of hyperbolic space given by the upper sheet of hyperboloid

\mathbb{H}^{n}=\{(x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\;x_{0}^{2}-x_{1}^{2}-...-x_{n}^{2}=1,\;x_{0}>0\}.

Using polar coordinates, we write

x_{0}=\cosh r\quad\text{and}\quad(x_{1},...,x_{n})=(\sinh r)\,\omega\quad\text{with}\quad r\geq 0\,\;\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

The distance of a point
\Omega=\Omega(r,\omega)=\bigl(\cosh r,(\sinh r)\,\omega\bigr)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}

to the origin
O=(1,0,...,0)
of the hyperboloid is

d(\Omega,O)=r.

More generally, the distance between two arbitrary points

\Omega=\Omega(r,\omega)
and
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega(s,\omega^{\prime})
satisfies

(6.1)

\cosh d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})=(\cosh r)(\cosh s)-(\sinh r)(\sinh s)\,\omega\cdot\omega^{\prime}.

Denote the geodesic ball around the origin of radius
\xi>0
by
B(O,\xi).
The volume element is given by

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}d\Omega\,f(\Omega)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}d\omega\,u_{0}(r,\omega)\,(\sinh r)^{n-1}.

Recall that for
radial

\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
functions, the spherical Fourier transform is defined by

\mathcal{H}u_{0}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}d\Omega\,u_{0}(\Omega)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(\Omega)=C\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,u_{0}(r)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\,(\sinh r)^{n-1},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

where the elementary spherical function of index
\lambda
is radial in space, even in
\lambda
, and is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(r)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}d\omega\,(\cosh r-\sinh r(\vartheta\cdot\omega))^{-i\lambda-\frac{n-1}{2}}

(6.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,(\cosh r\pm\sinh r\cos\theta)^{\mp i\lambda-\frac{n-1}{2}}(\sin\theta)^{N-2},

(see for instance
[
GaVa88
, Proposition 3.1.4]
,
[
Hel00
, Ch. IV, Theorem 4.3]
, or
[
Koo84
, p.40]
). Notice that it holds
\varphi_{0}(r)\asymp(1+r)\,e^{-\frac{n-1}{2}r}
globally and
\varphi_{0}(r)\sim r\,e^{-\frac{n-1}{2}r}
as
r\rightarrow+\infty
.

The solution to the heat equation with initial data
u_{0}
is given by

u(t,\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}d\Omega^{\prime}\,h_{t}(d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}))\,u_{0}(\Omega^{\prime}),

where
h_{t}
denotes the heat kernel on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
; it is a radial function, given as the inverse spherical transform of
e^{-t(\lambda^{2}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4})}
.
According to
[
AnJi99
, p.1080]
, one has the following complete asymptotics:

(6.3)

h_{t}(r)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}\,\gamma\left(\frac{r}{2t}\right)\,t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,r\,e^{-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}t-\frac{n-1}{2}r-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}},

as
t\rightarrow+\infty
and
r\rightarrow+\infty
, where
\gamma(s)=\frac{\Gamma(s+1/2)\Gamma(s/2+(n-1)/4)}{\Gamma(s+1)\Gamma(s/2+1/4)}
.

The
L^{p}
critical regions now boil down to

\textbf{B}_{p}(t)=\left\{\Omega\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\quad\left|d(\Omega,O)-\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)(n-1)t\right|\leq r(t)\right\},\quad 1\leq p<2,

where
r(t)
is as in
Theorem

3.1
. Likewise,

\textbf{B}_{2}(t)=\left\{\Omega\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\quad r_{1}(t)\leq d(\Omega,O)\leq r_{2}(t)\right\},

and

\textbf{B}_{p}(t)=\left\{\Omega\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\quad d(\Omega,O)\leq R(t)\right\},\quad 2<p\leq\infty,

where again we use the notation of
Theorem

3.1
for the quantities
r_{1}(t),r_{2}(t),R(t)
.

As clear from the proof in the higher rank case, the essential ingredient is the asymptotics of the quotient
h_{t}(d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}))/h_{t}(d(\Omega,O))
, when
\Omega\in\textbf{B}_{p}(t)
and
d(\Omega^{\prime},O)
is bounded. Even further, the exponential terms will be the ones giving the crucial information, since it can be shown that
\frac{\gamma(d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})/2t)}{\gamma(d(\Omega,O)/2t)}\sim 1
when
d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}),d(\Omega,O)=\textrm{O}(t)
(see
Lemma

2.1
).

Let us write

\Omega=\Omega(r,\omega)\in\textbf{B}_{p}(t)
and
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega(s,\omega^{\prime})\in B(O,\xi)
. Then clearly

d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})-d(\Omega,O)=O(1),\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

In the case
1\leq p\leq 2
, we can even be more precise, since when
t\rightarrow+\infty
we also have
r\rightarrow+\infty
. More specifically, using the distance formula (
6.1
) one can compute that

\displaystyle d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})-d(\Omega,O)

\displaystyle=\cosh^{-1}(\cosh r\,\cosh s-\sinh r\,\sinh s\,\omega\cdot\omega^{\prime})-r

(6.4)

\displaystyle\sim\log(\cosh s-\sinh s\,\omega\cdot\omega^{\prime})\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty,\quad 1\leq p\leq 2.

On the other hand, we can write

(6.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})^{2}-d(\Omega,O)^{2}}{4t}

\displaystyle=(d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})-d(\Omega,O))\,\frac{d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})+d(\Omega,O)}{4t},

where the quotient
(d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})+d(\Omega,O))/4t
is equal to
\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)\frac{n-1}{2}+\textrm{O}\left(\frac{r(t)}{t}\right)
when
1\leq p<2
, while it is
\textrm{O}\left(\frac{r_{2}(t)}{t}\right)
when
p=2
, and
\textrm{O}\left(\frac{R(t)}{t}\right)
when
2<p\leq\infty
.

It follows from (
6.5
) and (
6.4
) that

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{n-1}{2}(d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})-d(\Omega,O))}e^{-\frac{d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})^{2}-d(\Omega,O)^{2}}{4t}}\sim(\cosh s-\sinh s\,\omega\cdot\omega^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{p}(n-1)},

when
\Omega\in\textbf{B}_{p}(t)
,
\Omega^{\prime}\in B(O,\xi)
,
1\leq p\leq 2
. This implies by (
6.3
) that

\frac{h_{t}(d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}))}{h_{t}(d(\Omega,O))}\sim(\cosh s-\sinh s\,\omega\cdot\omega^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{p}(n-1)},\quad\Omega\in\textbf{B}_{p}(t),\,\Omega^{\prime}\in B(O,\xi),\quad 1\leq p\leq 2.

On the other hand, it follows from
Lemma

5.1
that

\frac{h_{t}(d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}))}{h_{t}(d(\Omega,O))}\sim\frac{\varphi_{0}(d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}))}{\varphi_{0}(d(\Omega,O))},\quad\Omega\in\textbf{B}_{p}(t),\,\Omega^{\prime}\in B(O,\xi),\quad 2\leq p\leq\infty.

Thus, for
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, let us define the mass function by

M_{p}(u_{0})(\Omega)=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}d\omega^{\prime}\,u_{0}(s,\omega^{\prime})\,(\cosh s-\sinh s\,\omega\cdot\omega^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{p}(n-1)}\quad\text{if}\quad 1\leq p\leq 2,

and by

M_{p}(u_{0})(\Omega)=\frac{1}{\varphi_{0}(d(\Omega,O))}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}d\Omega^{\prime}\,u_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})\,\varphi_{0}(d(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}))\quad\text{if}\quad 2\leq p\leq\infty.

Then, for radial
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, the mass functions boil down to

M_{p}(u_{0})(\Omega)=M_{p}=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,u_{0}(s)\,\varphi_{i(\frac{2}{p}-1)\frac{n-1}{2}}(s)=\mathcal{H}u_{0}\left(\pm\,i(\frac{2}{p}-1)\frac{n-1}{2}\right),\quad 1\leq p<2,

and

M_{p}(u_{0})(\Omega)=M_{p}=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,u_{0}(s)\,\varphi_{0}(s)=\mathcal{H}f\left(0\right),\quad 2\leq p\leq\infty.

Following the arbitrary rank approach, now we have all the ingredients to show that for all initial data
u_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\text{rad}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, or
u_{0}
such that
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}d\Omega\,|u_{0}(\Omega)|\,w_{p}(\Omega)<+\infty
, where

w_{p}(\Omega)=e^{\frac{n-1}{p}d(\Omega,O)}\quad\text{if}\quad 1\leq p\leq 2\quad\text{and}\quad w_{p}(\Omega)=e^{\frac{n-1}{2}d(\Omega,O)}\quad\text{if}\quad 2\leq p\leq\infty,

the solution
u(t,\cdot)
satisfies

\|h_{t}\|_{p}^{-1}\,\|u(t,\cdot)\,-\,M_{p}(u_{0})(\cdot)\,f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty.

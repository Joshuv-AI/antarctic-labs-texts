Sphericity and Analyticity of a strictly pdeusoconvex hypersurface in low regularity I
Ilya Kossovskiy, Dmitri Zaitsev

Abstract
. In our earlier work [ KZ22 ] , we introduced an analytic regularizability theory for smooth strictly pseudoconvex hypersurfaces in complex space. That is, we found a necessary and sufficient condition for a hypersurface to be CR-equivalent to an analytic target. The condition amount to the holomorphic extension property for a smooth function on a totally real submanifold, both the function and the submanifold being uniquely associated with the given hypersurface. In the present paper, we develop our method further. First, we extend the result in [ KZ22 ] to hypersurfaces of finite (possibly low) smoothness. Second, we introduce a new tool for studying CR hypersurfaces in low regularity called regularizing ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) sections . Using the latter key tool, we solve the open problem of checking the sphericity of a strictly pseudoconvex hypersurface in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} in low regularity, precisely in the regularity C k , 2 ≤ k < 7 C^{k},\,2\leq k<7 which is not covered by the classical Cartan-Tanaka-Chern-Moser theory. As an application of our theory, we deduce the sphericity of a strictly pseudoconvex hypersurface in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} of regularity C 6 C^{6} with vanishing Cartan-Chern CR-curvature.



1.
Introduction

Let
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1},\,n\geq 1
,
be a strictly pseudoconvex real hypersurface.
We call
M

spherical
at a point
p\in M

if there exists a
CR-diffeomorphism
between neighborhoods of
p
in
M

and of a point
p^{\prime}
in the standard sphere
S^{2n+1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.
The celebrated Cartan-Tanaka-Chern-Moser theory
[
Ca32
,
Ta62
,
CM74
]
provides a compete answer to the problem of shericity of a

smooth

strictly pseudoconvex hypersurface. This is done by building the so-called
canonical Cartan connection
associated with
M
and its CR structure,
and subsequently computing its curvature.
The sphericity at a point
p
is then
equivalent to the vanishing
in a neighborhood of
p
of certain principal curvature components
of the Cartan connection.
However,
already
the
construction of the Cartan connection on
M
and its curvature
requires the regularity of at least
C^{7}
(as can be verified by the construction in each of the cited paper, and also those relying on the theory of
Parabolic Geometries
, see e.g.
[
CS09
]
).

We shall note here that, even though the computation of the principal curvature components in the Cartan-Tanaka-Chern-Moser theory requires derivatives of the defining function of respectively the order
\leq 6
(
n=1
) or the order
\leq 4
(
n\geq 2
), concluding the sphericity of a hypersurfaces with vanishing principal curvature components requires the
full connection and its curvature
(which relies on derivatives of the defining function of order
\leq 7
, as discussed above).

In this way, the corresponding sphericity problem in low regularity
remained open since the 1970’s:

Open Problem.

Find necessary and sufficient conditions for
a strictly pseudoconvex real hypersurface

M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

of class
C^{k}
, where
k<7
,
to be spherical at a point
p\in M
.

More precisely, we shall adopt the following terminology:

Definition 1.1
.

A real hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

of class
C^{k},\,k\geq 2

is called
spherical of class
C^{s}

at a point
p\in M
,

s\leq k
,
if there exists a
C^{s}
CR-diffeomorphism
between a neighborhood of
p
in
M

and an open subset of the standard sphere

S^{2n+1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.
We call
M

spherical at
p

(without specifying the regularity class
of the CR-diffeomorphism)
if it is spherical at
p
of class
C^{0}
.

As discussed in Section 2, one can assume
s
here to be as large as
s=k-1/2
(unless
k-1/2\in\mathbb{N}
, in which case
s
can be chosen as
s=k-1/2-\epsilon
with any small
\epsilon>0
).

The above Open Problem is closely related to numerous theorems in Complex Analysis asserting that domains with spherical boundaries are biholomorphic to a ball or are universally covered by a ball (and then are certain ball quotients), see e.g. Chern-Ji
[
CJ96
]
, Huang-Ji
[
HJ02
]
, Nemirovski-Shafikov
[
NS05
,
NS06
]
, Li-Luk
[
LL06
]
, Huang-Xiao
[
HX21
]
.
These results often assume
merely
C^{2}
regularity of the boundary,
while
no
criterion for inspecting the sphericity
based on the Cartan connection
is available in such a low regularity!

More broadly, understanding low regularity
in problems of geometry and PDEs
is known to be difficult
and brings new insights.

The main goal of the present paper is to provide
a solution to the above Problem
for

M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
of class
C^{k}
with
k\geq 2
in the dimension
n+1=2
,
see
Theorem 1
below. Higher dimensions
n+1\geq 3
are to be addressed in the upcoming paper
[
KZ25
]
(which is mainly due to the fact that the Cartan-Tanaka-Chern-Moser theory looks quite different respectively in dimension
2
and in higher dimensions).
To obtain sharper regularity results,
we also consider Hölder classes
C^{k}

for arbitrary nonnegative number
k\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Our sphericity criterion amounts to an (explicit) system of
differential equations for the defining function of a hypersurface,
and the requirement of the higher than expected regularity of
several functions uniquely associated with the hypersurface
(the latter can be computed explicitly in terms of the defining function).

Our approach relies on
the recent
analytic regularizability theory

by the authors in
[
KZ22
]
.
That theory provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a

C^{\infty}

smooth

strictly pseudoconvex hypersurface (
Condition E
, where “E” stands for “extension”) to be (locally)
CR-diffeomorphic to a real-analytic hypersurface.
Condition E is formulated in terms of a certain

holomorphic extension property

for a distinguished function invariantly associated with the hypersurface (called here the
\Phi
-function
).
This condition
established in
[
KZ22
]
in the
C^{\infty}
category,
is here extended to the case of low regularity (see Section 2) and subsequently used for addressing the sphericity problem.
An important role is played by the new
tool introduced in this paper that we call

regularizing vector fields
.
The latter are certain
(0,1)
vector fields
whose differentiation
of the
1
-jet section
induced by the complex tangent bundle

T^{\mathbb{C}}M

gives CR functions of higher than expected regularity.

More precisely,
consider the holomorphic fiber bundle
J^{1,n}

of complex hyperplanes over
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

and its canonical section over
M

(1.1)

{\sf(th-section)}~s_{M}^{1}\colon M\to J^{1,n},\quad q\mapsto(q,[T^{\mathbb{C}}_{q}M]).

Fixing a system of local holomorphic coordinates
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}
yields a (local) trivialization of the bundle
J^{1,n}
, and we can write

(1.2)

{\sf(th-def)}~s_{M}^{1}(q)=(q,\theta(q))\in M\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset M\times\mathbb{P}^{n},

where
we use the standard embedding of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
into the projective space and

\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n})
is of class
C^{k-1}
.
It is easy to express
\theta

in terms of a local defining function
\rho
of
M

with
\rho_{w}\neq 0

in local holomorphic coordinates

(1.3)

{\sf(th-coor)}~\theta_{j}=-\frac{\rho_{z_{j}}}{\rho_{w}},\quad j=1,\ldots,n,\quad(z,w)=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},w)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}.

We now introduce our first new tool
for dealing with low regularity.
When writing
C^{k}
,
we shall always consider arbitrary real
k\geq 0
, and all vector fields are assumed to be of class
C^{0}

unless specified otherwise.

Definition 1.2
.

(regu)

Let
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

be a real hypersurface of class
C^{k}
,
k\geq 2
,
with
\theta
as in (
1.2
) or (
1.3
).
A
(0,1)
vector field
L
on
M

is called
regularizing of class
C^{s}

(or
C^{s}
-regularizing),
s\leq k
,
if
L\theta
is a (vector-valued)
CR function on
M
of class
C^{s}
.

Note that there is no assumption
on the regularity of
L
itself,
only on the derivative
L\theta
of
\theta
along
L

(in fact, certain regularity follows automatically,
see
1.3
below).
Also note
that regularizing vector fields
L

are defined for any class
C^{s}
with
s\leq k
,
even if
L
itself could be of lower regularity.

1.1
.

(reg-basis)

It is easy to construct
regularizing
(0,1)
vector fields
on a
Levi-nondegenerate pseudoconvex hypersurface
M
.
Indeed, choose any basis
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}

of
(0,1)
vector fields
of class
C^{k-1}

and normalize
them to satisfy

(1.4)

{\sf(kron)}~L_{j}\theta_{l}=\delta_{jl}

(where we use the Kronecker
\delta
). This is always possible
by multiplying with the inverse of the
matrix
(L_{j}\theta_{k})

(which is of class
C^{k-2}
),
the latter matrix
being invertible
in view of the Levi-nondegeneracy.
Since the constant functions
\delta_{jl}

are CR and of class
C^{s}
on
M
for any
s\leq k
,
all
L_{j}
are
C^{s}
-regularizing.
So constructed
C^{s}
-regularizing
vector fields are themselves of class
C^{k-2}
,
which is allowed to be lower than
s
.

1.2
.

The particular system of regularizing vector fields constructed in
1.1
is called
(the system of) basic regularizing
(0,1)
sections
.

1.3
.

(basis-l)

Given any basis
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}

of regularizing
(0,1)
vector fields
(that always exists in view of Remark
1.1
)
for a Levi-nondegenerate hypersurface
M
,
an arbitrary
(0,1)
vector field

L
is regularizing of class
C^{s}

precisely whenever its expansion

L=c_{1}L_{1}+\ldots+c_{n}L_{n}

has all coefficients
c_{j}

being CR functions of class
C^{s}
.
Indeed, since

\begin{pmatrix}L\theta_{1}\\
\vdots\\
L\theta_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}\theta_{1}&\cdots&L_{n}\theta_{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
L_{1}\theta_{n}&\cdots&L_{n}\theta_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\\
\vdots\\
c_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}

and the matrix

(L_{j}\theta_{l})

is CR of class
C^{s}

and
is invertible
by the Levi-nondegeneracy,

L\theta
is CR of class
C^{s}

if and only if the coefficients

c_{j}
are all CR of class
C^{s}
.
In particular, using the basis
of
C^{s}
-regularizing
(0,1)

vector fields
of class
C^{k-2}

constructed in

1.1
,
it follows that any
C^{s}
-regularizing
(0,1)

vector field
L
is automatically
of class (at least)
C^{\min(s,k-2)}
.

To formulate our sphericity criteria in low regularity,
we also need the
2
-jet version of the above
\theta
-function,
i.e. the function
\Phi
valued in symmetric
n\times n
matrices
such that

(1.5)

{\sf(Phi-def)}~j^{2}_{q}Q_{q}=(q,\theta(q),\Phi(q))\in M\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\subset J^{2,n},

where
J^{2,n}
is the holomorphic fiber bundle
of
2
-jets of complex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

and

j^{2}_{q}Q_{q}
is the
2
-jet of the formal Segre variety
that is well-defined for
k\geq 2
.
Similarly to (
1.3
),
the function
\Phi
can be calculated
in terms of a local defining function
\rho
of
M

with
\rho_{w}\neq 0

by the formula

(1.6)

{\sf(Phi00)}~\Phi=(\Phi_{ij}),\quad\Phi_{ij}=\frac{1}{(\rho_{w})^{3}}\begin{vmatrix}0&\rho_{z_{j}}&\rho_{w}\\
\rho_{z_{i}}&\rho_{z_{i}z_{j}}&\rho_{z_{i}w}\\
\rho_{w}&\rho_{z_{j}w}&\rho_{ww}\end{vmatrix},\quad i,j=1,...,n.

We are now ready to formulate
our first sphericity result in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

1
.

(main-2)

Let

M\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}

be a strictly pseudoconvex real hypersurface
of class
C^{k},\,k\geq 2,

and
L
a (nonzero) regularizing

(0,1)

vector field on
M
of class
C^{s}
.
Then the following holds:

(i) if
2\leq s\leq k
and
M
is spherical of class
C^{s}
, then one has

(1.7)

{\sf(main-cond)}~\Phi,L\Phi,L^{2}\Phi,L^{3}\Phi\in C^{s-2},\quad L^{4}\Phi=0.

(ii) if
1\leq s\leq k
and the conditions

(1.8)

{\sf(main-cond^{\prime})}~\theta,\Phi,L\Phi,L^{2}\Phi,L^{3}\Phi\in C^{s},\quad L^{4}\Phi=0.

are satisfied, then
M
is spherical of class
C^{(s+1)-}
.

Theorem 1
has the following two immediate corollaries.

First, for the regularity
k>7/2
, we are able to formulate a
complete criterion
of sphericity:

2
.

(main-2-iff)

Let

M\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}

be a strictly pseudoconvex real hypersurface
of class
C^{k},\,k>7/2

and
L
a (nonzero) regularizing

(0,1)

vector field on
M
of class
C^{1}
.
Then
M

is spherical
at a point
p

if and only if

(1.9)

{\sf(main-cond1)}~L\Phi,L^{2}\Phi,L^{3}\Phi\in C^{1},\quad L^{4}\Phi=0.

Second, in the ”super low” regularity
2\leq k\leq 7/2
, we can formulate a sufficient condition for sphericity.

1.4
.

(main-2-suff)

Let

M\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}

be a strictly pseudoconvex real hypersurface
of class
C^{k},\,2\leq k\leq 7/2,

and
L
a (nonzero) regularizing

(0,1)

vector field on
M
of class
C^{1}
.
Then
M

is spherical
at a point
p

if

(1.10)

{\sf(main-cond2)}~\Phi,L\Phi,L^{2}\Phi,L^{3}\Phi\in C^{1},\quad L^{4}\Phi=0.

1.5
.

(cond-indep)

Another important feature of
our conditions in
Theorem 1

is their independence of the choice of
L
.
Indeed, since
L
is nonzero,
it forms a basis of
(0,1)
vector fields
(in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
). It can be verified from Remark
1.3
: in the
\mathbb{C}^{2}
case,
Remark
1.3

implies that any other
regularizing vector field
\widetilde{L}

of class
C^{s}

is a multiple of
L

by a CR function of class
C^{s}

and since
(0,1)
vector fields
commute with CR functions,
condition (
1.7
)
remains invariant
when
L
is replaced by
\widetilde{L}
.

Finally, we have an application of
Theorem 1
directly to the Cartan-Tanaka-Chern-Moser theory (in low regularity). Precisely, we prove the following result with optimal regularity:

3
.

(zerocurv)

A strictly pseudoconvex hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
of class
C^{6}
is spherical if and only if its Cartan invariant (the CR curvature) vanishes on
M
.

Note that
6
is precisely the number of derivatives needed to define the CR curvature in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
hence the regularity order
6
is optimal.
On the other hand,
as discussed above, this result can
not
be deduced from Cartan-Tanaka-Chern-Moser theory,
which requires higher order differentiation
to conclude sphericity by standard methods.

2.
Condition E for smoothly embedded real hypersurfaces

2.1.
The method of associated differential equations

The study of the relationship between the geometry of
real hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and 2nd order ODEs

(2.1)

{\sf(wzz)}~w^{\prime}{}^{\prime}{}=\Phi(z,w,w^{\prime}{}).

goes back to Segre
[
Se31
]

and Cartan
[
Ca32
]
,
see also Webster
[
We77
,
We00
]

More generally, the geometry of a real hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1},\,n\geq 1
, is related to
that of a complete second order system of PDEs

(2.2)

{\sf(wzkzl)}~w_{z_{k}z_{l}}=\Phi_{kl}(z_{1},...,z_{n},w,w_{z_{1}},...,w_{z_{n}}),\quad\Phi_{kl}=\Phi_{lk},\quad k,l=1,...,n,

Moreover,
in the real-analytic case
,
this relation becomes more
explicit
by means of the Segre family.
Namely,
to any real-analytic Levi-nondegenerate hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1},\,n\geq 1
, one can uniquely associate a holomorphic ODE (
2.1
) (
n=1
) or a holomorphic PDE system (
2.2
) (
n\geq 2
),
whose solutions are precisely the Segre varieties.
The Segre family of
M
plays a role of a
“mediator” between the hypersurface and the associated differential equations.
For recent work on associated differential equations in the degenerate setting, see e.g. the papers

[
KS16a
,
KS16b
,
KL18
]
of the first author with Lamel and Shafikov.

The associated differential equation procedure is particularly simple in the case of a Levi-nondegenerate hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. In this case the Segre family is an
anti-holomorphic
2-parameter family of complex holomorphic curves.
It then follows from the standard ODE theory that there exists a unique ODE (
2.1
), for which the Segre varieties are precisely the graphs of solutions. This ODE is called
the associated ODE.

In general, both right hand sides in (
2.1
),(
2.2
) appear as functions determining the
2
-jet of a Segre variety
at a given point
as a function of the
1
-jet at the same point.
More explicitly, we use coordinates

(z,w)=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},w)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{C}^{n+1}.

Fix a
real-analytic
hypersurface

M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

passing through the origin,
and choose a
sufficiently small neighborhood
U

of the origin.
When
M
is Levi-nodegenerate, we can associate a

complete second order system of holomorphic PDEs

to
M
,
which is uniquely determined by the condition that the differential equations are satisfied by all the
graphing functions
h(z,\zeta)=w(z)
of the
Segre varities
\{Q_{\zeta}\}_{\zeta\in U}
of
M
in a
neighbourhood of the origin.

To be more precise, we consider a
so-called
complex defining
equation
(see, e.g.,
[
BER99
]
),

w=\rho(z,\bar{z},\bar{w}),

of
M
near the origin, which one
obtains by substituting

u=\frac{1}{2}(w+\bar{w}),\,v=\frac{1}{2i}(w-\bar{w})

into
a real-analytic defining equation and
solving for
w
as function of
(z,\bar{z},\bar{w})

by the implicit function theorem.
The Segre
variety
Q_{x}
of a point

x=(a,b)\in U,\quad a\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\,b\in\mathbb{C},

is now given
as the graph of the function

(2.3)

{\sf(segredf)}~w(z)=\rho(z,\bar{a},\bar{b}),

where we slightly abuse the notation
using the same letter
w
for
both the last coordinate and
the function
w(z)
defining a Segre variety.
Differentiating (
2.3
) we obtain

(2.4)

{\sf(segreder)}~w_{z_{j}}=\rho_{z_{j}}(z,\bar{a},\bar{b}),\quad j=1,\ldots,n.

Considering (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) as a holomorphic
system of equations with the unknowns
\bar{a},\bar{b}
,
in view of the Levi-nondegeneracy of
M
,
an
application of the implicit function theorem yields holomorphic functions

A_{1},...,A_{n},B
such that
(
2.3
) and (
2.4
) are solved by

\bar{a}_{j}=A_{j}(z,w,w^{\prime}),\quad\bar{b}=B(z,w,w^{\prime}),

where we write

w^{\prime}=(w_{z_{1}},\ldots,w_{z_{n}}).

The implicit function theorem applies here because the
Jacobian of the system coincides with the Levi determinant of
M

for
(z,w)\in M
(
[
BER99
]
). Differentiating (
2.3
) twice
and substituting the above solution for
\bar{a},\bar{b}
finally
yields

(2.5)

{\sf(segreder2)}~w_{z_{k}z_{l}}=\rho_{z_{k}z_{l}}(z,A(z,w,w^{\prime}),B(z,w,w^{\prime}))=:\Phi_{kl}(z,w,w^{\prime}),\quad k,l=1,\ldots,n,

or, more invariantly,
i.e. independent of the coordinate choice,

(2.6)

{\sf(segreder2^{\prime})}~j^{2}_{(z,w)}Q_{x}=\Phi(x,j^{1}_{(z,w)}Q_{x}).

Now (
2.5
)
(or (
2.6
))
is the desired complete system of holomorphic second order PDEs
denoted by
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}(M)
.

2.1
.

(PDEdef)

We call
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}(M)

the system of PDEs
associated with
M
.

2.2.
Condition E

Our main results rely on a
new

analytic regularizability
condition
for hypersurfaces of low regularity.
In the
C^{\infty}
case,
such condition was
introduced in
[
KZ22
]
and called

Condition E (“E” for extension)
.
We shall give below both an invariant
and a coordinate-based formulations of it.
For the basic concepts in CR Geometry (such as Segre varieties and formal submanifolds) we refer to
[
BER99
]
, and for

and related concepts to
[
CS09
]
.

Let

\pi\colon J^{1,n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1},

be the bundle
of
1
-jets of complex hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
,
which is a projective holomorphic
fiber bundle over
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with the fiber
isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
and let

M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1},\,n\geq 1

be (for the moment) a (

) smooth

real hypersurface.
Then the complex tangent bundle

induces
the natural smooth (global) embedding

\varphi\colon M\to J^{1,n},\quad x\mapsto\bigl(x,[T^{\mathbb{C}}_{x}M]\bigr).

The image

\varphi(M)=:M_{J}\subset J^{1,n}

is a smooth totally real

(2n+1)
-dimensional real submanifold in the
(2n+1)
-dimensional complex manifold
J^{1,n}

by an observation of
Webster
[
We77
]
.
Next,
associated with
M
is the smooth (weakly) pseudoconvex real hypersurface

\widehat{M}:=\pi^{-1}(M)\subset J^{1,n}.

The manifold
M_{J}
is a smooth real submanifold in
\widehat{M}
.
Note that
\widehat{M}
itself is locally CR-equivalent
to
M\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
(and thus is
holomorphically degenerate
, see
[
BER99
]
). In what follows we denote by

the

,
by which we mean
the subset of an open neighborhood

U

of a point of
M

defined by
\rho<0
, i.e.

where
\rho
is a local defining function of
M

with

and the
complex hessian satisfying

\partial\bar{\partial}\rho(X,\overline{X})\geq 0

for
X\in T^{10}M
.
Given such
U^{+}
,
we write

\widehat{U}^{+}:=\pi^{-1}(U^{+}).

Then
\widehat{M}

is obviously (weakly) pseudoconvex
and
\widehat{U}^{+}
is the pseudoconvex side of
\widehat{M}
.
Since all our considerations are local,
the exact choice of neighborhoods

We next fix a point

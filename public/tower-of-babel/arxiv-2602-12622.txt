Efficient Personalized Federated PCA with Manifold Optimization for IoT Anomaly Detection
Xianchao Xiu, Chenyi Huang, Wei Zhang, Wanquan Liu

Abstract
Internet of things (IoT) networks face increasing security threats due to their distributed nature and resource constraints. Although federated learning (FL) has gained prominence as a privacy-preserving framework for distributed IoT environments, current federated principal component analysis (PCA) methods lack the integration of personalization and robustness, which are critical for effective anomaly detection. To address these limitations, we propose an efficient personalized federated PCA (FedEP) method for anomaly detection in IoT networks. The proposed model achieves personalization through introducing local representations with the ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -norm for element-wise sparsity, while maintaining robustness via enforcing local models with the ℓ 2 , 1 \ell_{2,1} -norm for row-wise sparsity. To solve this non-convex problem, we develop a manifold optimization algorithm based on the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) with rigorous theoretical convergence guarantees. Experimental results confirm that the proposed FedEP outperforms the state-of-the-art FedPG, achieving excellent F1-scores and accuracy in various IoT security scenarios. Our code will be available at https://github.com/xianchaoxiu/FedEP .

II-B

Sparse PCA

PCA is a foundational dimensionality reduction method in unsupervised learning, aiming to find an orthogonal projection matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
(
m\ll n
) that minimizes the reconstruction residual. The mathematical model is

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{W}&\|X-WW^{\top}X\|_{\text{F}}^{2}\\
\text{s.t.}&W^{\top}W=I_{m}.\end{array}

(1)

Although the above PCA is efficient in practice, it generates a dense matrix
W
that can be sensitive to irrelevant features. To overcome this problem, sparse PCA (SPCA)
[
33
]
introduces the
\ell_{1}
-norm regularization term to encourage sparsity in
W
, which is described as

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{W}&\|X-WW^{\top}X\|_{\text{F}}^{2}+\beta\|W\|_{2,1}\\
\text{s.t.}&W^{\top}W=I_{m},\end{array}

(2)

where
\beta>0
is the parameter to tune the sparsity of
W
. Another impressive work is robust sparse PCA (RSPCA)
[
29
]
, which addresses the challenge of gross corruptions by decomposing the data into a low-rank component and a sparse error matrix. The model is given by

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{W,S}&\|(I-WW^{\top})(X-S)\|_{\text{F}}^{2}+\alpha\|S\|_{1}\\
\text{s.t.}&W^{\top}W=I_{m},\end{array}

(3)

where
S
captures the outlier-induced noise, allowing the subspace
W
to accurately represent the underlying data structure, and
\alpha>0
is the parameter to balance the noise level of
S
.

II-C

FedPCA for IoT Anomaly Detection

In the field of IoT, PCA-based anomaly detection identifies malicious traffic by projecting high-dimensional telemetry into a low-rank subspace. A sample
\mathbf{x}^{i}
is marked as an anomaly if its reconstruction error

S(\mathbf{x}^{i})=\|(I-WW^{\top})\mathbf{x}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}

(4)

exceeds a threshold
[
11
]
.
However, traditional PCA struggles to handle malicious outliers and the privacy risks associated with centralized data aggregation.

To this end, FedPCA
[
24
]
was developed, with the FedPCA on Grassmann (FedPG) framework being a representative method that seeks a global consensus among
d
gateways. Suppose that
X_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is the local data in the
i
-th local client, and
W_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
is the corresponding projection matrix, then FedPG is given by

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{\{W_{i}\},V}&\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|X_{i}-W_{i}W_{i}^{\top}X_{i}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\\
\text{s.t.}&W_{i}=V,\quad W_{i}^{\top}W_{i}=I_{m},\quad\forall i\in[d],\end{array}

(5)

where
[d]=\{1,2,\cdots,d\}
, and
V
is the global consensus variable.

1
), FedPG demonstrates outstanding performance in IoT anomaly detection.

III
The Proposed Method

III-A

Problem Formulation

In this paper, we propose an efficient personalized
federated PCA (FedEP) by relaxing the consensus into a personalized objective with bi-sparse regularization, simultaneously enhancing local adaptability and structural robustness. The objective is formulated as

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{\{W_{i}\},\{S_{i}\},V}&\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\|(I-W_{i}W_{i}^{\top})(X_{i}-S_{i})\|_{\textrm{F}}^{2}\\
&+~\alpha\|S_{i}\|_{1}+\beta\|W_{i}\|_{2,1})\\
\textrm{s.t.}&W_{i}=V,\quad W_{i}^{\top}W_{i}=I,\quad\forall i\in[d].\end{array}

(6)

Compared with existing frameworks like FedPG
[
24
]
, our FedEP offers two distinct advantages:

•

(
Adaptive Personalization
) The sparse component
S_{i}
absorbs local-specific noise and outliers, allowing the model to adapt to the non-IID nature of IoT data.

•

(
Structural Robustness
) The
\ell_{2,1}
-norm on
W_{i}
enforces row-wise sparsity, making it resilient to corrupted features while reducing the computation.

III-B

Optimization Algorithm

For the sake of description, denote

p(S_{i},W_{i})=\alpha\|S_{i}\|_{1}+\beta\|W_{i}\|_{2,1}.

(7)

To facilitate optimization, we introduce the auxiliary variable
U_{i}
, which represents the target of projection of the denoised and purified local data.
Then, problem (
6
) is transformed into

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{\{W_{i}\},\{S_{i}\},\{U_{i}\},V}&\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\|(I-W_{i}W_{i}^{\top})U_{i}\|_{\textrm{F}}^{2}+p(S_{i},W_{i})\\
\textrm{s.t.}&X_{i}-S_{i}=U_{i},\quad\forall i\in[d],\\
&W_{i}-V=0,\quad\forall i\in[d],\\
&W_{i}^{\top}W_{i}=I,\quad\forall i\in[d].\end{array}

(8)

According to the ADMM scheme, the global augmented Lagrangian function
\mathcal{L}
can be decomposed into the sum of
d
local functions as

\begin{array}[]{cl}&\mathcal{L}(\{W_{i}\},\{S_{i}\},\{U_{i}\},V;\{\Lambda_{i}\},\{\Pi_{i}\})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal{L}_{i}(W_{i},S_{i},U_{i},V;\Lambda_{i},\Pi_{i}),\end{array}

(9)

where
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{i}(W_{i},S_{i},U_{i},V;\Lambda_{i},\Pi_{i})

(10)

\displaystyle=\|(I-W_{i}W_{i}^{\top})U_{i}\|_{\textrm{F}}^{2}+p(S_{i},W_{i})

\displaystyle+\langle\Lambda_{i},X_{i}-S_{i}-U_{i}\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\|X_{i}-S_{i}-U_{i}\|_{\textrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\langle\Pi_{i},W_{i}-V\rangle+\frac{\nu}{2}\|W_{i}-V\|_{\textrm{F}}^{2}.

Here,
\Lambda_{i}
and
\Pi_{i}
are the Lagrange multipliers, and
\mu,\nu>0
are the penalty parameters. This decentralized structure ensures that each client
i
can update its local variables
W_{i}
independently, while global synchronization is only required for the consensus variable
V
, thereby preserving data privacy and reducing communication overhead.

Algorithm 1

FedEP via ADMM with Manifold Optimization

III-B
1
Update
W_{i}

The subproblem with respect to
W_{i}
can be formulated as

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{W_{i}\in\mathrm{St}(n,m)}&\|(I_{n}-W_{i}W_{i}^{\top})U_{i}^{k}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\beta\|W_{i}\|_{2,1}\\
&+~\frac{\nu}{2}\|W_{i}-Z_{i}^{k}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},\end{array}

(11)

where
Z_{i}^{k}=V^{k}-\Pi_{i}^{k}/\nu
.
The objective function in (
11
) consists of two smooth components and a non-smooth regularization term, and subject to the non-convex Stiefel manifold, which makes it computationally challenging.
For simplicity, we reformulate problem (
11
) as

\min_{W_{i}\in\mathrm{St}(n,m)}\quad F(W_{i})=H(W_{i})+g(W_{i}),

(12)

where

\displaystyle H(W_{i})

\displaystyle=\|U_{i}^{k}-W_{i}W_{i}^{\top}U_{i}^{k}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\|W_{i}-Z_{i}^{k}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

(13)

\displaystyle g(W_{i})

\displaystyle=\beta\|W_{i}\|_{2,1}.

Obviously,
H(W_{i})
is smooth,
g(W_{i})
is non-smooth.
The optimization process begins by deriving the Euclidean gradient of
H(W_{i})
. By expanding the Frobenius norm terms and exploiting the orthogonality property
W_{i}^{\top}W_{i}=I_{m}
, the gradient at the current iterate
W_{i}^{k}
is obtained as

\nabla H(W_{i}^{k})=-2U_{i}^{k}(U_{i}^{k})^{\top}W_{i}^{k}+\nu(W_{i}^{k}-Z_{i}^{k}).

(14)

To deal with
g(W_{i})
while strictly satisfying the geometry of the Stiefel manifold, the alternating manifold proximal gradient method
[
34
]
is considered. The direction
D_{i}
is determined by solving

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{D_{i}}&\langle\nabla H(W_{i}^{k}),D_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2t}\|D_{i}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\beta\|W_{i}^{k}+D_{i}\|_{2,1}\\
\text{s.t.}&D^{\top}W_{i}^{k}+(W_{i}^{k})^{\top}D=0,\end{array}

(15)

where
t>0
is the step size. Once the optimal direction
D_{i}^{*}
is computed, the next iterate is obtained via a retraction operation
W_{i}^{k+1}=\mathrm{Retr}_{W_{i}^{k}}(\alpha D_{i}^{*})
, where
\alpha
is a step size determined by a backtracking line search to ensure sufficient descent.

Let
\Lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
be the symmetric Lagrange multiplier associated with the tangent space constraint. The first-order optimality condition leads to a closed-form expression for
D_{i}
in terms of
\Lambda_{i}
as

D_{i}(\Lambda_{i})=\mathrm{prox}_{2,1}(B(\Lambda_{i}),t\beta)-W_{i}^{k},

(16)

where
B(\Lambda_{i})=W_{i}^{k}-t(\nabla H(W_{i}^{k})-W_{i}^{k}\Lambda_{i})
, and
\mathrm{prox}_{2,1}
is the proximal operator associated with the
\ell_{2,1}
-norm
[
35
]
. By substituting
D_{i}(\Lambda_{i})
back into the tangent space constraint, problem
15
reduces to finding the root of the following equation system

Q(\Lambda_{i})=D_{i}(\Lambda_{i})^{\top}W_{i}^{k}+(W_{i}^{k})^{\top}D_{i}(\Lambda_{i})=0.

(17)

Inspired by
[
36
]
, the regularized semi-smooth Newton (SSN) method is applied to solve
Q(\Lambda_{i})=0
, which guarantees fast convergence for finding the optimal descent direction.

Update
S_{i}

Through simple algebraic operations, the
S_{i}
-subproblem can be easily written as

\min_{S_{i}}\quad\alpha\|S_{i}\|_{1}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|S_{i}-M_{i}^{k}\|_{\textrm{F}}^{2},

(18)

where
M_{i}^{k}=X_{i}-U_{i}^{k}+\Lambda_{i}^{k}/\mu
. According to

, the closed-form solution is characterized by the following soft-thresholding operator

(19)

where

means the element-wise product.

U_{i}

Once
W_{i}^{k+1}
and

have been obtained, the

-subproblem can be solved by

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{U_{i}}&\|(I-W_{i}^{k+1}(W_{i}^{k+1})^{\top})U_{i}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\\
&+~\frac{\mu}{2}\|U_{i}-(X_{i}-S_{i}^{k+1}+\Lambda_{i}^{k}/\mu)\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\end{array}

Taking the derivative with respect to

, it is easy to obtain

\displaystyle 2(I-W_{i}^{k+1}(W_{i}^{k+1})^{\top})U_{i}

(21)

\displaystyle+\mu(U_{i}-(X_{i}-S_{i}^{k+1}+\Lambda_{i}^{k}/\mu))=0.

Let

, to speed up the calculation of matrix inversion, the famous Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula can be used

]
, thus the solution is

Fixing other variables, the global

which has the following closed-form solution

According to

]

III-C

Convergence Analysis

Let
(\{W_{i}^{k}\},\{S_{i}^{k}\},\{U_{i}^{k}\},V^{k};\{\Lambda_{i}^{k}\},\{\Pi_{i}^{k}\})
be the generated sequence by Algorithm
1
. Then we establish the convergence result in the following theorem.

Theorem III.1

The augmented Lagrangian function sequence
\{\mathcal{L}(\{W_{i}^{k}\},\{S_{i}^{k}\},\{U_{i}^{k}\},V^{k};\{\Lambda_{i}^{k}\},\{\Pi_{i}^{k}\})\}
is nonincreasing.

Proof:

Following a similar line as
[
34
]
, the corresponding objectives of (
11
) are nonincreasing. Thus, it is concluded that

\begin{array}[]{cl}&\mathcal{L}_{i}(W_{i}^{k+1},S_{i}^{k},U_{i}^{k},V^{k};\Lambda_{i}^{k},\Pi_{i}^{k})\\
&\leq\mathcal{L}_{i}(W_{i}^{k},S_{i}^{k},U_{i}^{k},V^{k};\Lambda_{i}^{k},\Pi_{i}^{k}),\quad\forall i\in[d].\end{array}

(27)

Since the
S_{i},U_{i},V
-subproblems are all convex, the minimization steps satisfy the following inequalities

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(W_{i}^{k+1},S_{i}^{k+1},U_{i}^{k+1},V^{k+1};\Lambda_{i}^{k},\Pi_{i}^{k})

(28)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}(W_{i}^{k+1},S_{i}^{k+1},U_{i}^{k+1},V^{k};\Lambda_{i}^{k},\Pi_{i}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{i}(W_{i}^{k+1},S_{i}^{k+1},U_{i}^{k},V^{k};\Lambda_{i}^{k},\Pi_{i}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{i}(W_{i}^{k+1},S_{i}^{k},U_{i}^{k},V^{k};\Lambda_{i}^{k},\Pi_{i}^{k}),\quad\forall i\in[d].

Regarding the
\Lambda_{i},\Pi_{i}
-subproblems, the following statements hold

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(W_{i}^{k+1},S_{i}^{k+1},U_{i}^{k+1},V^{k+1};\Lambda_{i}^{k+1},\Pi_{i}^{k+1})

(29)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}(W_{i}^{k+1},S_{i}^{k+1},U_{i}^{k+1},V^{k+1};\Lambda_{i}^{k+1},\Pi_{i}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}(W_{i}^{k+1},S_{i}^{k+1},U_{i}^{k+1},V^{k+1};\Lambda_{i}^{k},\Pi_{i}^{k}),\quad\forall i\in[d].

Combining the above inequalities (
27
), (
28
), and (
29
), it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\{W_{i}^{k+1}\},\{S_{i}^{k+1}\},\{U_{i}^{k+1}\},V^{k+1};\{\Lambda_{i}^{k+1}\},\{\Pi_{i}^{k+1}\})

(30)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}(\{W_{i}^{k}\},\{S_{i}^{k}\},\{U_{i}^{k}\},V^{k};\{\Lambda_{i}^{k}\},\{\Pi_{i}^{k}\}),

which shows that the generated augmented Lagrangian function
\{\mathcal{L}(\{W_{i}^{k}\},\{S_{i}^{k}\},\{U_{i}^{k}\},V^{k};\{\Lambda_{i}^{k}\},\{\Pi_{i}^{k}\})\}
is nonincreasing, and thus the proof is completed.
∎

Evaluation Metrics

To evaluate the performance of compared methods, five standard metrics are utilized. Let
TP
,
TN
,
FP
, and
FN
represent true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives, respectively.

•

Accuracy (
Acc
): The overall proportion of correctly classified records, defined as

\textrm{Acc}=\frac{\textrm{TP}+\textrm{TN}}{\textrm{TP}+\textrm{TN}+\textrm{FP}+\textrm{FN}}.

(31)

•

Precision (
Pre
): The ratio of correctly identified attacks to all predicted attack records, defined as

\textrm{Pre}=\frac{\textrm{TP}}{\textrm{TP}+\textrm{FP}}.

(32)

•

Recall (
Rec
): The ratio of correctly detected attacks to all actual attack records, defined as

\textrm{Rec}=\frac{\textrm{TP}}{\textrm{TP}+\textrm{FN}}.

(33)

•

False Negative Rate (
FNR
): The fraction of actual anomalies that are incorrectly classified as normal, defined as

\textrm{FNR}=\frac{\textrm{FN}}{\textrm{TP}+\textrm{FN}}.

(34)

•

F1-score (
F1-score
): The harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced measure of model performance, defined as

\textrm{F1-score}=\frac{2\textrm{TP}}{2\textrm{TP}+\textrm{FP}+\textrm{FN}}.

(35)

We would like to point out that larger Acc
(\uparrow)
, Pre
(\uparrow)
, Rec
(\uparrow)
, F1-score
(\uparrow)
values, and smaller FNR
(\downarrow)
values, indicate better anomaly detection results.

Unlike FedPG, which necessitates retrieving all records to a centralized server for global variable learning, FedEP preserves data locality through its personalized sparse framework.
As summarized in Table
II
, FedEP obtains comparable performance for all three datasets, particularly in the core metrics of accuracy and F1 score.
Specifically, on TON-IoT, FedEP achieves an accuracy of 90.48% and an F1-score of 94.52%, representing a steady improvement over FedPG.
The advantage of the proposed method becomes more pronounced on the highly challenging UNSW-NB15, where FedEP yields a substantial 2.38% gain in accuracy and a 1.77% boost in F1-score.
Similar results can also be found on NSL-KDD.
The performance improvement in various intrusion detection scenarios highlights the effectiveness of integrating personalized sparse regularization, which can capture heterogeneous local patterns and maintain high detection accuracy.

Furthermore, Fig.
2
shows the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves of compared methods. It is concluded that FedEP achieves higher area under the curve (AUC) values across all datasets, i.e., 0.8305 on TON-IoT, 0.8632 on UNSW-NB15, and 0.8899 on NSL-KDD, compared to 0.8132, 0.8483, and 0.8851 of FedPG, respectively. This indicates that FedEP has a better trade-off between true positive rate and false positive rate under different network environments.

In addition, Fig.
3
illustrates the evolution of model accuracy across global communication rounds.
It is found that on all three datasets, FedEP achieves its peak accuracy within the first 10 communication rounds and maintains a stable performance plateau thereafter.
This confirms the effectiveness of personalized initialization in FedEP, which allows the global model to be better fine-tuned for local distributions, thereby minimizing the number of rounds required to reach optimal performance in federated IoT systems.

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{\{W_{i}\},V}&\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|(I-W_{i}W_{i}^{\top})X_{i}\|_{\textrm{F}}^{2}\\
\textrm{s.t.}&W_{i}=V,\quad W_{i}^{\top}W_{i}=I,\quad\forall i\in[d].\end{array}

Case II:

S_{i}

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{\{W_{i}\},\{S_{i}\},V}&\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\|(I-W_{i}W_{i}^{\top})(X_{i}-S_{i})\|_{\textrm{F}}^{2}+\alpha\|S_{i}\|_{1})\\
\textrm{s.t.}&W_{i}=V,\quad W_{i}^{\top}W_{i}=I,\quad\forall i\in[d].\end{array}

(37)

•

Case III:

\ell_{2,1}

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{\{W_{i}\},V}&\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\|(I-W_{i}W_{i}^{\top})X_{i}\|_{\textrm{F}}^{2}+\beta\|W_{i}\|_{2,1})\\
\textrm{s.t.}&W_{i}=V,\quad W_{i}^{\top}W_{i}=I,\quad\forall i\in[d].\end{array}

(38)

It is the complete objective function in (

).

On TON-IoT, the complete model (Case IV) achieves the highest accuracy, i.e., 90.48%, and F1-score, i.e., 94.52%.
Although the performance gains over Case I - Case III are incremental, the consistent improvement across all metrics indicates that the integration of error decomposition and row-wise sparsity enhances representation stability.
The effectiveness of our proposed FedEP is particularly evident on UNSW-NB15.
While Cases I and III yield a 100% recall, their low accuracy and precision suggest a tendency toward over-prediction.
In contrast, Case IV achieves a more balanced performance with the highest accuracy and F1-score, demonstrating that the
\ell_{2,1}
-norm filters redundant features and the sparse matrix
S_{i}
effectively mitigates outlier interference.
On NSL-KDD, Case IV maintains marginal superiority with 84.24% accuracy and 85.52% F1-score.
The results of all three datasets confirm that the combination of personalization and sparsity enables FedEP to maintain robust detection performance in complex scenarios while processing redundant or corrupted local data.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose an efficient personalized federated PCA (FedEP) framework for anomaly detection in IoT networks. Unlike existing federated PCA methods that enforce strict consensus constraints, FedEP enables local gateways to maintain personalized model parameters while benefiting from global knowledge sharing. The integration of robust sparse regularization with
\ell_{2,1}
-norm and
\ell_{1}
-norm enhances the model’s ability to handle noise and outliers in heterogeneous IoT environments.
Furthermore, we develop an efficient manifold optimization algorithm based on ADMM with theoretical convergence guarantees.
Numerical experiments verify that our proposed FedEP achieves better accuracy and higher computational efficiency compared to the benchmark FedPG.

In the future, we are interested in extending FedEP to nonlinear models using kernel-based techniques or deep learning. Additionally, investigating the compressibility of personalized models to reduce communication overhead is crucial for scalable deployment on resource-constrained IoT devices.

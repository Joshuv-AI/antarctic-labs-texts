A posteriori error estimates for a modified Morley FEM Thanks: AKD acknowledges the financial support of ANRF under Grant No. ANRF/ARG/009285. Thanks: DG was supported by the European Research Council, StG DAFNE , ID 891734.
A.K. Dond, D. Gallistl, S. Nayak, M. Schedensack

Abstract
. Residual-based a posteriori error estimators are derived for the modified Morley FEM, proposed by Wang, Xu, Hu [J. Comput. Math, 24(2), 2006], for the singularly perturbed biharmonic equation and the nonlinear von Kármán equations. The error estimators are proven to be reliable and efficient. Moreover, an adaptive algorithm driven by these error estimators is investigated in numerical experiments.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
65N12, 65N15, 65N30, 74K20

1.
Introduction

Conforming finite element methods (FEM) for fourth-order
problems require globally
\mathcal{C}^{1}
continuity across
element boundaries such that relatively high polynomial
degrees have to be used.
The Morley FEM
[
Mor68
]
overcomes this drawback
by breaking with the conformity of the trial space
and enforcing the continuity in the vertices of the
underlying triangles and the normal derivatives on the
faces only. However, these discrete functions are in
general not only not in the Sobolev space
H^{2}(\Omega)
,
but also not in the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\Omega)
.
While it is well-understood that the Morley FEM
converges for the biharmonic problem
[
Cia78
,
Gud10
]
,
the authors of
[
NTW01
]
show
that it may diverge for second order problems.

This paper proves a posteriori error estimates for two
fourth-order problems with second-order terms.
The first problem is the singularly perturbed
fourth-order problem
in a bounded, open, polygonal Lipschitz-domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
:
Seek
u\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
with

(1.1)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u-\Delta u=f\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

where
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
In
[
WXH06
]
(with a generalisation to 3D in

[
WM07
]
), the authors define
a modified Morley FEM by employing
the nodal interpolation operator of the Morley
finite element function in the second-order term.
As the nodal evaluations are degrees of freedom of
the Morley finite element functions, this interpolation
operator is easily computable.
The authors of
[
WXH06
]

bound the error a priori by
h^{1/2}
,
h
being
the maximal mesh size, where the
reduced convergence rate results from the boundary layer
of the solution. This motivates adaptive mesh refinement.

Adaptive mesh-refinement is usually driven by a reliable
and efficient error estimator
\eta
, which means that
the error estimator is (up to some higher-order terms)
an upper and lower bound for the error.
In
[
ZW08
]
an a posteriori error estimator
is derived for the modified Morley FEM for the singularly
perturbed fourth-order equation, but its efficiency relies
on the smoothness of the exact solution.
For a mixed FEM on convex domains,
[
DLZ22
]
derive a posteriori
error estimates.
In
[
GT24
]
the authors derive a posteriori
error estimates under some abstract assumptions that include
a wide class of nonconforming FEMs, but the assumptions
do not apply to the modified Morley FEM.

In the first part of this paper, we derive an a posteriori error estimator

\eta
and prove that it is reliable and efficient in the sense of

\displaystyle\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}+\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eta

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}+\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\mathrm{osc}_{\varepsilon}(f,\mathcal{T})

for the singular perturbed
fourth-order equation, where
I_{h}
denotes the nodal
interpolation operator. The precise definition of the energy
norm
\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert\bullet\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}
and the oscillations
can be found in Section
3
.
The proof relies on techniques developed in

[
Ver98
]
for the singularly perturbed
reaction-diffusion equation.

The second part of this paper is concerned with the
nonlinear von Kármán equations, which seek in their strong form

\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
with

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}\psi_{1}

\displaystyle=[\psi_{1},\psi_{2}]+f,

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}\psi_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{1},\psi_{1}]

in the bounded polygonal Lipschitz-domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Here, the Monge-Ampère form or von Kármán bracket
[\bullet,\bullet]

is defined as

\displaystyle[\theta,\chi]:=\theta_{xx}\chi_{yy}+\theta_{yy}\chi_{xx}-2\theta_{xy}\chi_{xy}=\operatorname{cof}(D^{2}\theta):D^{2}\chi,

where
\operatorname{cof}(A)=(A_{22},-A_{12};-A_{21},A_{11})
denotes the
cofactor of a two dimensional matrix
A
and
:
is the Frobenius
inner product.
This von Kármán bracket introduces a nonlinearity in the equations,
which in the weak form can be equivalently included by a term like

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{cof}(D^{2}\psi_{1}):D^{2}\psi_{2}\,\theta\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(\operatorname{cof}(D^{2}\psi_{1})\nabla\psi_{2})\cdot\nabla\theta\,dx

for all test functions
\theta\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
However, this equality is no longer true for nonconforming Morley functions
and
[
MN16
]
employs the second version in a
Morley FEM for the von Kármán equations, but without reliable and efficient
a posteriori error estimates.
To overcome the difficulties in the a posteriori error estimates,

[
CMN20
]
uses the first integral in
(
1.2
) for a nonconforming Morley FEM, such that the
nonlinearity can be treated similar to an
L^{2}
term instead
of a Laplace-type term. However, in an optimal control problem
in
[
CDNS22
]
, the missing
symmetry between the second and the third variable in the first
integral in (
1.2
) caused some problems in the
a posteriori error analysis for the adjoint equation.

The second part of this paper uses the second version in (
1.2
)
and adopts the ideas of the first part,
namely to include the nodal interpolation operator in this integral
(the nonlinearity) to derive a reliable and efficient error estimator.

The remaining parts of this article are organised as follows:
Section
2
defines the discrete spaces and
operators. Section
3
defines the singularly perturbed
biharmonic problem, its discretization and the error estimator
for this problem and proves the reliability and efficiency
of this error estimator, while Section
4

is devoted to the von Kármán equations and its reliable
and efficient error estimator.
Section
5
concludes the paper with numerical
experiments.

Standard notation on Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces
applies throughout this paper. The
L^{2}
inner product is denoted
by

(v,w)_{L^{2}(\omega)}
for a domain
\omega\subseteq\Omega

and
\lVert\bullet\rVert_{\omega}:=\lVert\bullet\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega)}
,
while the
H^{k}
norm over
\omega\subseteq\Omega

is denoted by

\lVert\bullet\rVert_{H^{k}(\omega)}
.
The notation
A\lesssim B
abbreviates
A\leq CB
for some
constant
C
that is independent of the mesh size and
the singular perturbation parameter
\varepsilon>0
.
The notation
A\approx B
abbreviates
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a regular triangulation of the open, bounded, and connected
Lipschitz-polygon
\Omega
from a shape-regular
family.
Let
\mathcal{N}
denote the set of vertices in
\mathcal{T}
and

\mathcal{N}(\Omega)
the set of interior vertices.
Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of faces of
\mathcal{T}
and

\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
the set of interior faces.
For any interior face
F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
we fix
the two triangles
T_{+},T_{-}\in\mathcal{T}
with
F=T_{+}\cap T_{-}
,
while for a boundary face, let
T_{+}\in\mathcal{T}
be the unique
triangle with
F\subseteq T_{+}
.
Then, let
\nu_{F}=\nu_{T_{+}}|_{F}
be the unit normal
on
F
and
\tau_{F}=(0,-1;1,0)\nu_{F}
denotes the unit tangent on
F
.
Furthermore, for an interior face
F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
,
let
[v]_{F}:=v|_{T_{+}}-v|_{T_{-}}
denote the jump across
F

and for a boundary face
F\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
,
let
[v]_{F}:=v|_{T_{+}}
.
For an interior face
F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
,
let
\omega_{F}:=T_{+}\cup T_{-}
, while for a boundary face
F
,
define the face patch by
\omega_{F}=T_{+}
.
The set of faces of a triangle
T
reads
\mathcal{F}(T)
.
For
T\in\mathcal{T}
define the element patch

\omega_{T}:=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}\omega_{F}
.
The enlarged patch of an element
T
is given by

\Omega_{T}:=\bigcup\{K\in\mathcal{T}\,|\,K\cap T\neq\emptyset\}
.
The patch of a vertex
z
of
\mathcal{T}
is defined by

\omega_{z}:=\bigcup\{T\in\mathcal{T}\,|\,z\in T\}
.
For a face
F
, the set

\Omega_{F}=\cup_{z\in\mathcal{N}\cap F}\omega_{z}

is the union of the nodal patches related to the vertices
belonging to the face
F
.
The diameter of
T
and
F
is denoted by
h_{T}
and
h_{F}
,
respectively. The piecewise constant mesh-size function

h_{\mathcal{T}}
is defined by
h_{\mathcal{T}}|_{T}=h_{T}
.

The broken Sobolev space
H^{k}(\mathcal{T})
is defined by

\displaystyle H^{k}(\mathcal{T}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,|\,\forall T\in\mathcal{T}:\,v|_{T}\in H^{2}(T)\}.

Let
P_{k}(\mathcal{T})
denote the space
of piecewise polynomials with respect to
\mathcal{T}

of degree not larger than
k

and let
\Pi_{k}
denote the
L^{2}
projection to
P_{k}(\mathcal{T})
.
Further define

\displaystyle S^{k}(\mathcal{T}):=P_{k}(\mathcal{T})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)\qquad\text{and}\qquad S^{k}_{0}(\mathcal{T}):=P_{k}(\mathcal{T})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Define the space of Morley finite element functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T}):=\left\{v_{h}\in P_{2}(\mathcal{T})\;\left|\;\begin{aligned} &v_{h}\text{ is continuous at }\mathcal{N},\\
&\forall F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega):\;[\nabla v_{h}(\operatorname{mid}(F))]_{F}=0\end{aligned}\right\}\right.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T}):=\left\{v_{h}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T})\;\left|\;\begin{aligned} &\forall z\in\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{N}(\Omega):\;v_{h}(z)=0,\\
&\forall F\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}(\Omega):\;\nabla v_{h}(\operatorname{mid}(F))=0\end{aligned}\right\}\right..

Morley interpolation operator.

Let
I_{\mathcal{M}}:H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T})
denote the
Morley interpolation operator with the property that

(I_{\mathcal{M}}v)(z)=v(z)
,
the integral mean property

(2.1)

\displaystyle(D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}I_{\mathcal{M}}v)|_{T}=\fint_{T}D^{2}v\,dx\qquad\text{for all }T\in\mathcal{T}\text{ and all }v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),

and the approximation and stability estimates

[
HSX12
]

(2.2)

\sum_{j=0}^{2}\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{-j}D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2-j}(v-I_{\mathcal{M}}v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert D^{2}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Quasi-interpolation operator.

Furthermore, let
I_{\mathrm{qi}}:H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T})

denote a quasi-interpolation that follows a canonical construction

[
Osw94
]
:
A function is first mapped by the
L^{2}
projection
to the piecewise quadratic polynomials. This is concatenated
with averaging the degrees of freedom, which
assigns as nodal value to each interior vertex
z
the arithmetic
mean of the nodal values at
z
on each triangle containing

z
; and as normal derivative at the midpoint of any interior
the average
of the two normal derivatives at that point from the two
neighbouring elements.
With known interpolation bounds for
averaging operators
[
Osw94
,
DE12
]

it can be proved that it satisfies

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2}\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{-j}D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2-j}(v-I_{\mathrm{qi}}v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert D^{2}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}(v-I_{\mathrm{qi}}v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(v-I_{\mathrm{qi}}v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

See also
[
BS08
, Chapter 10.6]
.

Nodal interpolation operator.

Furthermore, let
I_{h}:H^{2}(\Omega)\cup\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T})\to S^{1}(\mathcal{T})

denote the nodal interpolation
operator, which is
H^{2}
stable and has the
approximation properties
[
BS08
]

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2}\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{-j}D^{2-j}(v-I_{h}v)\rVert_{L^{p}(T)}\leq C(p)\lVert D^{2}v\rVert_{L^{p}(T)}.

Enriching operator.

Let
J:\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T})\to H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
denote an enriching operator
with the properties
[
BGS10
]

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2}\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{-j}D^{2-j}_{\mathrm{pw}}(v_{h}-Jv_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

and

(2.6)

\displaystyle(Jv_{h})(z)=v_{h}(z)

and with the (local) estimates

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(v_{h}-Jv_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F},F\cap T\neq\emptyset}h_{F}^{-1}\lVert[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}},

\displaystyle h_{F}^{-1}\lVert[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(v_{h}-v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})},

cf.
[
BGS10
]
.

3.
Singularly perturbed biharmonic problem

This section considers the singularly perturbed biharmonic problem
(
1.1
).
The bilinear form
a_{\varepsilon}:H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}

is defined by

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon}(u,v):=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}D^{2}u:D^{2}v\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

This bilinear form can be extended to

a_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}:H^{2}(\mathcal{T})\times H^{2}(\mathcal{T})\to\mathbb{R}
,
which is defined by the piecewise
application of the differential operators, i.e.,

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}(u,v):=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}u:D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}v\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}u\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v\,dx\quad\text{for }u,v\in H^{2}(\mathcal{T}).

For the discretization define the discrete
bilinear form

a_{\varepsilon,h}:\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T})\to\mathbb{R}

that includes the nodal interpolation operator
I_{h}
on continuous

P_{1}
elements in the gradient terms

[
WXH06
]

by

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon,h}(u_{h},v_{h}):=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}u_{h}:D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}v_{h}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nabla I_{h}v_{h}\,dx

for all
u_{h},v_{h}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{T})
.
The corresponding norms are defined as

\displaystyle\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon}:=a_{\varepsilon}(v,v)^{1/2},\qquad\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}:=a_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}(v,v)^{1/2}

and the respective local norm on patch-like subdomains

\omega\subseteq\Omega
as

\displaystyle\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw},\omega}^{2}:=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\omega}\lvert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}v\rvert^{2}\,dx+\int_{\omega}|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v\rvert^{2}\,dx\quad\text{for all }v\in H^{2}(\mathcal{T}(\omega)),

where
\mathcal{T}(\omega)\subseteq\mathcal{T}
denotes the set of triangles covering

\overline{\omega}.

The weak formulation of (
1.1
)
seeks
u\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
with

(3.1)

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon}(u,v)=(f,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\text{for all }v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),

while the discretization seeks
u_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T})

with

(3.2)

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon,h}(u_{h},v_{h})=(f,I_{h}v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\text{for all }v_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T}).

A priori error estimates for this modified Morley FEM
can be found in
[
WXH06
]
, while

[
ZW08
]
defines an a posteriori error estimator,
which is reliable, but efficiency does only hold up to
higher-order Sobolev norms of the exact solution.

Let
\kappa_{T}:=\min\{1,h_{T}/\varepsilon\}
and
define the local error estimator contributions

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}(T)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}(\varepsilon/\kappa_{T})\,\lVert[\nabla u_{h}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}},

\displaystyle\mu_{I_{h}}(T)

\displaystyle:=\lVert\nabla(u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)},

\displaystyle\eta_{f}(T)

\displaystyle:=\lVert h_{T}\kappa_{T}f\rVert_{L^{2}(T)},

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(T)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)\cap\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}\varepsilon^{3}\kappa_{T}\lVert[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\,\nu]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}},

\displaystyle\eta_{2}(T)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)\cap\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}h_{T}\kappa_{T}^{2}\lVert[\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nu]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}},

\displaystyle\eta(T)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}^{2}(T)+\mu_{I_{h}}^{2}(T)+\eta_{f}^{2}(T)+\eta_{1}^{2}(T)+\eta_{2}^{2}(T)},

and the global error estimator

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\eta^{2}(T)}.

The following theorem proves the reliability of the error estimator.

Theorem 3.1

(reliability)
.

The exact solution
u\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
to (
3.1
)
and the discrete solution
u_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T})

to (
3.2
) satisfy

\displaystyle\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\eta^{2}.

The proof of this theorem is preceded by the following lemma
that bounds
the nonconformity error by
\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}+\mu_{I_{h}}
similar as in
(
2.7
), but for the
\varepsilon
-dependent norm.
The proof proceeds similar as in the proof of
the reliability estimate of
[
GT24
, Lemma 3.5]
.
However, the assumptions of that Lemma are not
satisfied for the Morley FEM, and, hence, the definition
of the smoothing operator
\hat{\Pi}_{C}
has to be modified
in order to make the estimate independent of the ratio

h/\varepsilon
. This results in the additional
term
\mu_{I_{h}}
in the right-hand side.

Lemma 3.2

(a posteriori estimate for nonconformity error)
.

Any
u_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T})
satisfies

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}-v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}\lesssim\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}+\mu_{I_{h}}.

Proof.

The first step consists in the localisation of

[
GT24
, Lemma 3.4]
that proves

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}-v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{y\in\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{B}(y,u_{h})

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(y,u_{h})=\min_{v_{y}\in V(\omega_{y})}\Bigg(\frac{1}{h_{y}^{2}\kappa_{y}^{2}}\lVert u_{h}-v_{y}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{y})}^{2}+\frac{1}{\kappa_{y}^{2}}\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(u_{h}-v_{y})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{y})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u_{h}-v_{y})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{y})}^{2}\Bigg),

where
\kappa_{y}=\min\{1,h_{y}/\varepsilon\}
with

h_{y}=\max\{h_{T}\,|\,T\in\mathcal{T},\,T\subseteq\omega_{y}\}
and

V(\omega_{y}):=\{v|_{\omega_{y}}\;|\;v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\}
.
We now fix an arbitrary vertex
y
.
Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
be a locally uniformly refined triangulation of
\omega_{y}
with
(maximal) mesh-size
\hat{h}\approx\min\{\varepsilon,h_{y}\}

(with no refinement and thus
\hat{h}=h_{y}
in the case
h_{y}\leq\varepsilon
).
Let
\hat{V}_{C}\subseteq V(\omega_{y})
denote the conforming
Hsieh–Clough–Tocher (HCT)
finite element space
[
Cia78
]
with respect to the triangulation

\hat{\mathcal{T}}
. On
\partial\Omega\cap\partial\omega_{y}
,
the functions of
\hat{V}_{C}
have clamped boundary conditions,
whereas on the remaining part of
\partial\omega_{y}
, no boundary
condition is prescribed.
Let
\mathcal{A}
denote some averaging operator that
averages over all adjacent triangles that are contained
in the patch
\omega_{y}
.
Define a smoothing operator

\hat{\Pi}_{C}:\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T})\to\hat{V}_{C}
by

\displaystyle(\hat{\Pi}_{C}v_{h})(z)=(I_{h}v_{h})(z)\quad\text{and}\quad(\nabla\hat{\Pi}_{C}v_{h})(z)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{h})(z)

for all interior (with respect to
\Omega
)
vertices
z
of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
,
as well as

\displaystyle\fint_{F}\partial_{\nu_{F}}(\hat{\Pi}_{C}v_{h})\,ds=\fint_{F}\mathcal{A}(\partial_{\mathrm{pw},\nu_{F}}v_{h})\,ds

for all interior (with respect to
\Omega
)
faces
F
of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}

with a fixed normal
\nu_{F}
.
Note that the HCT degrees of freedom are defind as averages
because the quantities
\fint_{F}\partial_{\mathrm{pw},\nu_{F}}v_{h}\,ds

are possibly multi-valued on (fine) edges
F
of
\hat{T}
.

Case 1/2.

We begin with the case that
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
coincides with
\mathcal{T}

on
\omega_{y}
, which means that
h_{y}\lesssim\hat{h}
and thus

h_{y}\lesssim\varepsilon
so that
\kappa_{y}\approx h_{y}/\varepsilon
.
The conformity
\hat{V}_{C}\subseteq V(\omega_{y})

of the HCT finite element space
and inverse estimates on the scale
\hat{h}
(depending on
y
) show

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(y,u_{h})\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{h_{y}^{4}}\lVert u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{y})}^{2}.

Further, the nodal values satisfy
\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h}(z)=I_{h}u_{h}(z)=u_{h}(z)
.
In this case, standard estimates

[
Gal15
, Proposition 2.5]

bound
\mathcal{B}(y,u_{h})
by the sum of all
\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}(T)^{2}

for
T
belonging to
\omega_{y}
.

Case 2/2.

In the remaining case that
\hat{h}<h_{y}
and thus

\varepsilon\lesssim h_{y}
and
\kappa_{y}\approx 1
,
we split the difference

u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h}=(u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h})+(I_{h}u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h}).

The function
u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h}
is piecewise polynomial with
respect to
\mathcal{T}
.
Therefore, the triangle inequality,
inverse inequalities, the discrete inequality

\displaystyle\lVert u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim h_{T}\lVert\nabla(u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)},

for the norms inside
\mathcal{B}(y,u_{h})

and
\varepsilon\approx\hat{h}

reveal

\mathcal{B}(y,u_{h})\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lVert I_{h}u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{y})}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{y})}^{2}.

On every triangle
\hat{T}
of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
,
the degrees of freedom of the HCT element are given by

L^{(0)}_{\hat{z}}v=v(\hat{z})

and

L^{(1,j)}_{\hat{z}}v=\partial_{j}v(\hat{z})

for the vertices
\hat{z}
of
\hat{T}
and
j\in\{1,2\}
,
and

L^{(1,\nu)}_{\hat{F}}v=\fint_{\hat{F}}\partial_{\nu_{\hat{F}}}v\,ds

for the faces
\hat{F}
of
\hat{T}
.
We do not explicitly include the dependence on
\hat{T}

in the notation because it will be clear from the context.
The local basis functions dual to these degrees of freedoms
are denoted by
\hat{\varphi}^{(0)}_{\hat{z}}
,

\hat{\varphi}^{(1,j)}_{\hat{z}}
,

\hat{\varphi}^{(1,\nu)}_{\hat{F}}
.

For any vertex
\hat{z}
of
\hat{T}
,
the definition of
\hat{\Pi}_{C}
implies

(I_{h}u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h})(\hat{z})=L^{(0)}_{\hat{z}}(I_{h}u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h})=0
.
Therefore, the difference
(I_{h}u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h})

can be expanded as follows

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(I_{h}u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h})|_{\hat{T}}=\sum_{(d,p)}L^{(1,d)}_{p}(I_{h}u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h})\varphi^{(1,d)}_{p}\\
&=\sum_{(d,p)}L^{(1,d)}_{p}(I_{h}u_{h}-u_{h})\varphi^{(1,d)}_{p}+\sum_{(d,p)}L^{(1,d)}_{p}(u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h})\varphi^{(1,d)}_{p},\end{aligned}

where the sums run over the nine possible pairs
(d,p)

of type
(j,\hat{z})
and
(\nu,\hat{F})
for

j=1,2
, the vertices
\hat{z}
, and the faces
\hat{F}

of
\hat{T}
.

The basis functions
associated to the derivative degrees of freedom scale
in the
L^{2}
norm as follows

\displaystyle\lVert\varphi^{(1,d)}_{p}\rVert_{\hat{T}}\approx\hat{h}^{2}.

A direct scaling argument therefore shows for the squared norm of
the first sum of the right-hand side of
(
3.3
) that

\left\lVert\sum_{(d,p)}L^{(1,d)}_{p}(I_{h}u_{h}-u_{h})\varphi^{(1,d)}_{p}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\hat{T})}^{2}\lesssim\hat{h}^{2}\lVert\nabla(u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\hat{T})}^{2}.

Moreover, for the squared norm of the second term
on the right-hand side of (
3.3
)
we compute with the definition
of
\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h}
and the scaling of the basis functions

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{(d,p)}L^{(1,d)}_{p}(u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h})\varphi_{p}^{(1,d)}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\hat{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{h}^{4}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{\hat{z}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{T})}\lvert(\partial_{j}u_{h}-\mathcal{A}(\partial_{j}u_{h}))(\hat{z})\rvert^{2}+\sum_{\hat{F}\in\mathcal{F}(\hat{T})}\left\lvert\fint_{\hat{F}}(\partial_{\nu_{\hat{F}}}u_{h}-\mathcal{A}(\partial_{\mathrm{pw},\nu_{\hat{F}}}u_{h}))\,ds\right\rvert^{2}\Bigg)

where
\mathcal{N}(\hat{T})
and
\mathcal{F}(\hat{T})
are the sets
of vertices and faces of
\hat{T}
, respectively.
With standard arguments
[
BS08
]
, the differences
to the average can be bounded by the jumps across all faces of

\hat{\mathcal{T}}
having one of their endpoints in
\hat{T}
,
namely

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{(d,p)}L^{(1,d)}_{p}(u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h})\varphi_{p}^{(1,d)}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\hat{T})}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{F}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}},\hat{F}\cap\hat{T}\neq\emptyset,\\
\hat{F}\not\subseteq(\overline{\partial\omega_{y}\setminus\partial\Omega})\end{subarray}}\hat{h}^{3}\lVert[\nabla u_{h}]_{\hat{F}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\hat{F})}^{2}

where
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
denotes the set of faces in
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
.
Note that faces on
\partial\omega_{y}
that are not on
\partial\Omega

are excluded in the sum because the averaging takes only values from
inside of
\omega_{y}
into account.
Combining the foregoing estimates and summing over all elements
of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}
, we obtain
with the bounded overlap of such sets from the shape-regularity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\lVert I_{h}u_{h}-\hat{\Pi}_{C}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{y})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\hat{h}^{2}\lVert\nabla(u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{y})}^{2}+\sum_{\hat{F}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}},\hat{F}\not\subseteq(\overline{\partial\omega_{y}\setminus\partial\Omega})}\hat{h}^{3}\lVert[\nabla u_{h}]_{\hat{F}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\hat{F})}^{2}\right).

Note that
[\nabla u_{h}]_{\hat{F}}=0
, if

\hat{F}\not\subseteq F
for all coarse faces
F\in\mathcal{F}
,
and therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{\hat{F}\in\hat{\mathcal{F}},\hat{F}\not\subseteq(\overline{\partial\omega_{y}\setminus\partial\Omega})}\lVert[\nabla u_{h}]_{\hat{F}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\hat{F})}^{2}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F},y\in F}\lVert[\nabla u_{h}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}.

Since in the present case
\hat{h}\approx\varepsilon
, the result follows.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

In the definition of
\hat{\Pi}_{C}
in the foregoing proof,
the nodal values of
I_{h}u_{h}
are taken instead of
simple averages of
u_{h}
. The latter choice would not lead
to an efficient bound with the proof techique employed
here. This has been commented on in

[
GT24
, Remark 3.8]
, where the averaging process
was analyzed and the authors concluded efficiency for
the case of continuous trial functions.
In the present case of the Morley element with discontinuous
trial functions, the nodal values are taken from the continuous
object
I_{h}u_{h}
, which results in an efficient bound.

Remark 3.4
.

In
[
WM07
]
, the authors introduce a generalisation
of the modified Morley FEM for the singularly perturbed biharmonic
problem to 3d. The nodal interpolation operator in the

L^{2}
term has to be replaced by the interpolation in a space
of functions whose integral mean over edges are continuous, but
that could be discontinuous in general.
The analysis of Lemma
3.2
relies
on the fact that the degrees of freedom associated with

L^{2}
terms vanish in (
3.3
).
This would not longer be the case in 3d, and therefore
the analysis in 3d requires a different approach.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Step 1 (Error split).

The triangle inequality leads to

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}+\mu_{I_{h}}.

The error in the energy norm can be split as

\displaystyle\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\inf_{v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u_{h}-v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}^{2}+\sup_{v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon}=1}\left((f,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-a_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}(u_{h},v)\right)^{2}.

The first term is bounded with the help of
Lemma
3.2
by
\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}^{2}+\mu_{I_{h}}^{2}
.
For the analysis of the second term,
let
v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon}=1
and
set
e_{v}:=v-I_{\mathrm{qi}}v
for the Morley quasi interpolation operator

I_{\mathrm{qi}}:H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T})

from (
2.3
).
The expression inside the squared supremum then reads

\displaystyle(f,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-a_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw}}(u_{h},v)=\sum_{j=1}^{4}T_{j}

with

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle:=(f,e_{v})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(f,I_{\mathrm{qi}}v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle:=-\varepsilon^{2}(D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h},D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle:=-(\nabla I_{h}u_{h},\nabla(v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v))_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle T_{4}

\displaystyle:=(\nabla(I_{h}u_{h}-u_{h}),\nabla v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

It remains to bound these terms by the error estimator.

Step 2 (Bound of
T_{1}
).

The estimates
(
2.3
)
of the nonconforming Morley quasi interpolation operator,
the Cauchy inequality, and
\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon}=1

prove

\displaystyle(f,v-I_{\mathrm{qi}}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\{\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert D^{2}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\min\{h_{\mathcal{T}},h_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}/\varepsilon\}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\eta.

Similarly, the
H^{2}
approximation property of
I_{h}
and
the inverse estimate combined with the
H^{1}
and
H^{2}
stability of

I_{qi}
lead to

(f,I_{\mathrm{qi}}v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eta.

Step 3 (Bound of
T_{2}
).

Since the jump and the average of

D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\,\nu_{F}
over
F
is constant for any
F\in\mathcal{F}

and
[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v}]_{F}
is affine on
F
and vanishes in the midpoint of
F
,
a piecewise integration by parts for
T_{2}
leads to

(3.4)

\displaystyle|T_{2}|=|\varepsilon^{2}(D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h},D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}|=\left|\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}([D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\,\nu]_{F},\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v})_{L^{2}(F)}\right|.

The multiplicative trace inequality proves for some

T_{F}\in\mathcal{T}
with
F\subseteq T_{F}
that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v}\|_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{F}^{-1/2}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v}\|_{L^{2}(T_{F})}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v}\|_{L^{2}(T_{F})}^{1/2}\|D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v}\|_{L^{2}(T_{F})}^{1/2}.

The approximation and stability properties from
(
2.3
) then prove

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim\sqrt{\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T_{F}})}\,\|D^{2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T_{F}})}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1/2}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\Omega_{T_{F}}}.

On the other hand, (
3.5
)
together with the approximation and stability properties
from (
2.3
) and Young’s inequality
leads to

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v}\|_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{F}^{-1/2}\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(T_{F})}+h_{F}^{1/2}\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(T_{F})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{F}^{1/2}\lVert D^{2}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T_{F}})}\lesssim(h_{F}^{1/2}/\varepsilon)\,\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\Omega_{T_{F}}}.

The combination of the foregoing displayed formulae leads to

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}e_{v}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim\min\{\varepsilon^{-1/2},h_{F}^{1/2}/\varepsilon\}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\Omega_{T_{F}}}.

This and (
3.4
) bound
T_{2}
as

\displaystyle|T_{2}|\lesssim\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}\min\{\varepsilon^{3/2},h_{F}^{1/2}\varepsilon\}^{2}\lVert[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\,\nu]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}}.

Step 4 (Bound of
T_{3}
).

Since
\nabla I_{h}u_{h}
is piecewise constant, a piecewise integration
by parts and
v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

imply for
T_{3}
that

\displaystyle-T_{3}=(\nabla I_{h}u_{h},\nabla(v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}([\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nu_{F}]_{F},v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v)_{L^{2}(F)}.

Note that the
H^{2}
stability of the nodal interpolation
operator
I_{h}
from (
2.4
),
an inverse inequality, and the
H^{1}
stability of
I_{\mathrm{qi}}

imply for any
T\in\mathcal{T}
that

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-1}\lVert I_{\mathrm{qi}}v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle+\lVert\nabla(I_{\mathrm{qi}}v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v)\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim h_{T}\lVert D^{2}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim\lVert\nabla I_{\mathrm{qi}}v\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})},

and, therefore, the operator
I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}
enjoys the
same approximation and stability properties
(
2.3
) as
I_{\mathrm{qi}}
.
Hence, the trace inequality and the

H^{1}
approximation and stability properties prove

\displaystyle\lVert v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim h_{T}^{1/2}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{F})}\leq h_{T}^{1/2}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\Omega_{F}},

while the
H^{2}
approximation and stability properties show

\displaystyle\lVert v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim h_{T}^{3/2}\lVert D^{2}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{F})}\leq h_{T}^{3/2}\varepsilon^{-1}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\Omega_{F}}.

The combination of these two estimates leads to

\displaystyle\lVert v-I_{h}I_{\mathrm{qi}}v\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim h_{T}^{1/2}\kappa_{T}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert v\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\Omega_{F}}.

This and the finite overlap of the patches
eventually show

\displaystyle|T_{3}|\lesssim\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}h_{T}\kappa_{T}^{2}\,\lVert[\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nu_{F}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}}.

Step 5 (Bound of
T_{4}
).

Finally, the last term
T_{4}

is estimated with a Cauchy inequality by

\displaystyle T_{4}=(\nabla(I_{h}u_{h}-u_{h}),\nabla v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\nabla(u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Define the
\varepsilon
-dependent oscillations

\displaystyle\mathrm{osc}_{\varepsilon}(f,\mathcal{T}(\omega_{T})):=\lVert\min\{h_{\mathcal{T}},h_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}/\varepsilon\}(f-\Pi_{0}f)\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{T})},

where
\Pi_{0}
denotes the
L^{2}
projection on the piecewise
constants with respect to
\mathcal{T}
.
The following theorem proves the efficiency of the error estimator.

Theorem 3.5

(efficiency)
.

The error estimator satisfies, for every
T\in\mathcal{T}
,

\displaystyle\eta^{2}(T)\lesssim\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw},\omega_{T}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{T})}^{2}+\mathrm{osc}_{\varepsilon}(f,\mathcal{T}(\omega_{T})).

Proof.

Efficiency of
\mu_{I_{h}}
.

A triangle inequality and the definition of the

(\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw},T)
-norm
proves

\displaystyle\mu_{I_{h}}(T)=\lVert\nabla(u_{h}-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}+\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}+\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw},T}.

Efficiency of
\eta_{f}
.

The term
\eta_{f}(T)
is estimated by the bubble function
technique
[
Ver13
]
.
Let
\flat_{T}=\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}\lambda_{3}^{2}\in H^{2}_{0}(T)

be the
H^{2}_{0}
bubble function, where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}

are the barycentric coordinates of
T
.
Define
\varphi_{T}:=\Pi_{0}f\flat_{T}
.
An equivalence of norms argument shows

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\int_{T}f\varphi_{T}\,dx+\lVert f-\Pi_{0}f\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}.

Since
u\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
solves (
3.1
),
it satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{T}f\varphi_{T}\,dx=\varepsilon^{2}(D^{2}u,D^{2}\varphi_{T})_{L^{2}(T)}+(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi_{T})_{L^{2}(T)}.

Since
D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}
and
\nabla I_{h}u_{h}
are constant on
T
,
the vanishing boundary conditions of
\varphi_{T}
and
\nabla\varphi_{T}

imply

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}(D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h},D^{2}\varphi_{T})_{L^{2}(T)}+(\nabla I_{h}u_{h},\nabla\varphi_{T})_{L^{2}(T)}=0.

The combination of the two previously displayed inequalities
leads to

\displaystyle\int_{T}f\varphi_{T}\,dx=\varepsilon^{2}(D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-u_{h}),D^{2}\varphi_{T})_{L^{2}(T)}+(\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h}),\nabla\varphi_{T})_{L^{2}(T)}.

The combination of the previous inequalities with
a Cauchy inequality therefore proves

\displaystyle\int_{T}f\varphi_{T}\,dx\lesssim\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert\varphi_{T}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw},T}\left(\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw},T}+\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}\right).

The definition of
\varphi_{T}
and a scaling argument show that

\displaystyle\min\{h_{T},h_{T}^{2}/\varepsilon\}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert\varphi_{T}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw},T}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}.

The combination of the previous inequalities with
a Cauchy inequality therefore proves

\displaystyle\min\{h_{T},h_{T}^{2}/\varepsilon\}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw},T}+\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}+\mathrm{osc}_{\varepsilon}(f,\{T\}).

Efficiency of
\eta_{1}
.

For the estimation of
\eta_{1}
, fix
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
let
F\in\mathcal{F}(T)
.
We first split the error
in the tangential and the normal component, i.e.,

\displaystyle\lVert[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}\leq\lVert[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}+\lVert[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\tau_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}.

Since
h_{F}^{-1}\min\{\varepsilon^{3/2},h_{F}^{1/2}\varepsilon\}\leq(\varepsilon/\kappa_{T})^{1/2}
, the tangential component
times
\varepsilon^{3/2}\kappa_{T}^{1/2}

is bounded by
\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}(T)
through an inverse inequality.
To bound the normal component, we
follow the idea of

[
GT24
]
, see also
[
Ver13
]
for the
second-order case, and
employ an edge bubble function
\chi_{F,\delta}
that takes
the singular perturbation into account.
To this end, let
0<\delta\leq 1
.
The function
\chi_{F,\delta}
is then constructed as a bubble
function of a triangles with height
\delta h_{F}
over the face
F

and has the properties

\chi_{F,\delta}\in H^{2}_{0}(\omega_{F})
,

\chi_{F,\delta}|_{F}=0
,
and the scaling properties

(3.7)

\displaystyle h_{F}\delta\lVert\partial\chi_{F,\delta}/\partial\nu_{F}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(F)}

\displaystyle\approx h_{F}^{-1}\delta^{-1/2}\lVert\chi_{F,\delta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}\approx\delta^{1/2}\lVert\nabla\chi_{F,\delta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}

\displaystyle\approx h_{F}\delta^{3/2}\lVert D^{2}\chi_{F,\delta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}\approx 1.

Details can be found in
[
Ver98
,
Ver13
,
GT24
]
.

Define
\varphi:=[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F}\nabla\chi_{F,\delta}
.
Since
\nabla\chi_{F,\delta}
is a quadratic bubble
along
F
pointing in normal direction, it follows

\displaystyle(h_{F}\delta)^{-1}\lVert[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{F}([D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F})\nu_{F}\cdot\varphi\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{F}([D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F})\cdot\varphi\,ds=\int_{\omega_{F}}D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}u_{h}:D\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=([D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F})\int_{\omega_{F}}D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}u_{h}:D^{2}\chi_{F,\delta}\,dx.

Since
\chi_{F,\delta}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

is a suitable test function for problem (
3.1
),
we arrive at

\displaystyle h_{F}^{-1/2}\delta^{-1}\,\lVert[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{\omega_{F}}D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u_{h}-u):D^{2}\chi_{F,\delta}\,dx+\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\omega_{F}}f\,\chi_{F,\delta}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\omega_{F}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\chi_{F,\delta}\,dx.

Since
\nabla I_{h}u_{h}
is piecewise constant and

\chi_{F,\delta}|_{\partial T}=0
,
the last term equals

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\omega_{F}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\chi_{F,\delta}\,dx=\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\omega_{F}}\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\cdot\nabla\chi_{F,\delta}\,dx.

The combination of the foregoing displayed formulae
leads to

\displaystyle\lVert[D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{F}^{1/2}\delta\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}\,\lVert D^{2}\chi_{F,\delta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon^{-2}h_{F}^{1/2}\delta\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}\,\lVert\chi_{F,\delta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon^{-2}h_{F}^{1/2}\delta\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}\,\lVert\nabla\chi_{F,\delta}\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}.

Let
\delta:=\min\{1,\varepsilon/h_{F}\}
.
Then
\kappa_{T}/\delta\approx h_{F}/\varepsilon
.
This and
the scaling of the bubble function from (
3.7
)
then prove

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{3/2}\kappa_{T}^{1/2}\lVert[D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\varepsilon\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}+\varepsilon\kappa_{T}^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}+\kappa_{T}\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}.

Since
\varepsilon\kappa_{T}\leq h_{T}
and
\kappa_{T}\leq 1
, the efficiency
of
h_{T}\kappa_{T}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}

therefore proves

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{3/2}\kappa_{T}^{1/2}\lVert[D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw},\omega_{F}}+\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}+\mathrm{osc}_{\varepsilon}(f,\{T_{+},T_{-}\}).

Efficiency of
\eta_{2}
.

For the efficiency of

h_{T}^{1/2}\kappa_{T}\,\lVert[\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nu_{F}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}
,
define the function
\flat_{F}=\lambda_{a}^{2}\lambda_{b}^{2}\in H^{2}_{0}(\omega_{F})

and let
\varphi:=[\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nu_{F}]_{F}\flat_{F}
.
Then

\displaystyle\lVert[\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nu_{F}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{F}[\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nu_{F}]_{F}\,\varphi\,ds=\int_{\omega_{F}}\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega_{F}}\nabla(I_{h}u_{h}-u)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx+\int_{\omega_{F}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx.

Since
\varphi\in H^{2}_{0}(\omega_{F})
is a suitable test function
in (
3.1
), the
last term equals

\displaystyle\int_{\omega_{F}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int_{\omega_{F}}f\,\varphi\,dx-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\omega_{F}}D^{2}u:D^{2}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\omega_{F}}f\,\varphi\,dx-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\omega_{F}}D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-u_{h}):D^{2}\varphi\,dx-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\omega_{F}}D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}:D^{2}\varphi\,dx.

Piecewise integration by parts for the last term leads to

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\omega_{F}}D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}:D^{2}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{F}[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\lVert[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}.

The scaling of
\varphi
reads

\displaystyle h_{T}^{1/2}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}\approx h_{T}^{-1}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}\approx\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}

\displaystyle\approx h_{T}\lVert D^{2}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}

\displaystyle\approx\lvert[\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nu_{F}]_{F}\rvert.

This together with the above displayed formulae leads to

\displaystyle h_{T}^{1/2}\lVert[\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nu_{F}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}+h_{T}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\varepsilon^{2}h_{T}^{-1}\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}+\varepsilon^{2}h_{T}^{-1/2}\lVert[D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}\nu_{F}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}.

This,
\kappa_{T}\leq 1
,

\varepsilon h_{T}^{-1}\kappa_{T}=\min\{1,\varepsilon/h_{T}\}\leq 1
,
and

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{1/2}h_{T}^{-1/2}\kappa_{T}=\min\{\varepsilon^{1/2}h_{T}^{-1/2},h_{T}^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1/2}\}\leq\kappa_{T}^{1/2}

imply

\displaystyle h_{T}^{1/2}\kappa_{T}\lVert[\nabla I_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nu_{F}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim\lVert\nabla(u-I_{h}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}+\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert u-u_{h}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{pw},\omega_{F}}

\displaystyle+\eta_{f}(T_{+})+\eta_{f}(T_{-})+\eta_{1}(T_{+}).

This and the efficiency of
\eta_{f}
and
\eta_{1}
proves the
efficiency of
\eta_{2}
.

Efficiency of
\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}
.

In the case
h_{T}\leq\varepsilon
, the efficiency of
\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}(T)

can be proved with standard arguments, see

[
BNS07
,
HS09
]
.
If
\varepsilon<h_{T}
, the multiplicative trace inequality
shows as in
[
GT24
]
that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lVert[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{h}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{h_{F}}\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}^{2}+\varepsilon\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}\,\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\varepsilon\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}^{2}+\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}.

∎

4.
The von Kármán equations

This section is devoted to the nonlinear von Kármán equations
posed on the polygonal, bounded Lipschitz-domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Section
4.1
introduces the weak formulation,
while Section
4.2
introduces the discrete
problem and states an a priori error estimate.
Section
4.3
contains the main part, namely the
definition of the error estimator and the proof of its
reliability and efficiency.

In this section, let
H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
(resp.
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
)
denote the vector valued space
(H^{k}(\Omega))^{2}

(resp.
(L^{p}(\Omega))^{2}
).

4.1.
The continuous problem

Define the bilinear form
A:H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\times H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}

and the trilinear form

B:H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\times H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\times H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}

for
\Psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
,
\Phi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
, and

\Theta=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
by

\displaystyle A(\Psi,\Phi)

\displaystyle=a(\psi_{1},\varphi_{1})+a(\psi_{2},\varphi_{2}),\qquad\text{for all }\Psi,\Phi\in H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),

and

\displaystyle B(\Theta,\Psi,\Phi)

\displaystyle=b(\theta_{1},\psi_{2},\varphi_{1})+b(\theta_{2},\psi_{1},\varphi_{1})-b(\theta_{1},\psi_{1},\varphi_{2})

for all
\Theta\in H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),\Psi,\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
where the bilinear and trilinear forms

a:H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
and

b:H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
are defined by

\displaystyle a(\psi,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}D^{2}\psi:D^{2}\varphi\,dx,\qquad\text{for all }\psi,\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle b(\theta,\psi,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\operatorname{cof}(D^{2}\theta)\nabla\psi)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\qquad\text{for all }\theta\in H^{2}(\Omega),\psi,\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Furthermore, define the right-hand side
F:L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle F(\Phi):=\int_{\Omega}f\,\varphi_{1}\,dx\qquad\text{for all }\Phi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

The continuous problem seeks
\Psi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
with

(4.1)

\displaystyle A(\Psi,\Phi)+B(\Psi,\Psi,\Phi)=F(\Phi)\qquad\text{for all }\Phi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

This problem is equivalent to
N(\Psi,\bullet)=0
on
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})

with

\displaystyle N(\Psi;\Phi):=A(\Psi,\Phi)+B(\Psi,\Psi,\Phi)-F(\Phi)

with derivatives

(4.2a)

\displaystyle DN(\Psi;\Xi,\Phi)

\displaystyle=\langle A\Xi+B^{\prime}(\Psi)\Xi,\Phi\rangle,

(4.2b)

\displaystyle\langle B^{\prime}(\Psi)\Xi,\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=2B(\Psi,\Xi,\Phi),

(4.2c)

\displaystyle D^{2}N(\Psi;\Xi,\Xi,\Phi)

\displaystyle=\langle B^{\prime\prime}(\Xi,\Xi),\Phi\rangle=2B(\Xi,\Xi,\Phi).

The following theorem states the existence and regularity
of an exact solution.

Theorem 4.1
.

There exists at least one solution
\Psi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})

to (
4.1
).
If
f
is sufficiently small, the solution is unique.
Moreover, there exists
a positive parameter
\alpha

(with
1/2<\alpha\leq 1
in the case of clamped boundary conditions)
such that
\Psi\in H^{2+\alpha}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lVert\Psi\rVert_{H^{2+\alpha}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

The proof of the existence is contained in

[
Cia22
, Theorem 5.8-3, pp. 416]

and
[
Kni67
, Theorem 4, pp. 238]
.
The proof of the uniqueness is contained in

[
Kni67
, Theorem 5]
.
The regularity theory for the biharmonic problem

[
BR80
,
Gri92
]
proves
(
4.3
).
∎

Throughout the remaining parts of the paper, we assume,
that the exact solution
\Psi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
is

nonsingular
, that is
[
Kel75
, Def. 2.4, pp.466]

the Fréchet derivative is nonsingular. This then implies
the continuous inf-sup condition of the linearized form

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lVert D^{2}\Phi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\sup_{\Theta\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{DN(\Psi;\Phi,\Theta)}{\lVert D^{2}\Theta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

The remaining part of this section summarizes some
frequently used inequalities.
For any
1\leq q<\infty
the following embedding holds for

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}

(4.5)

\displaystyle H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega),\qquad\lVert v\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C(q)\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Furthermore, Sobolev’s inequality
[
AF03
]
states that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|D^{2}v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C(\beta,q)\|v\|_{H^{2+\beta}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for }0\leq\beta<1\text{ and }2\leq q\leq\frac{2}{1-\beta}

and

(4.7)

\displaystyle\lVert v\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C(\beta)\lVert v\rVert_{H^{1+\beta}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for }\beta>0.

The regularity index
\alpha\geq 1/2
therefore implies

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|D^{2}v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{2+\alpha}(\Omega)}

on Lipschitz polygons.

The
\mathcal{T}
-piecewise version of
B
where the Hessian and the gradient
are replaced by their piecewise conterparts is denoted by
B_{\mathrm{pw}}

(see also Section
4.2
).

Lemma 4.2

(upper bounds of
B
and its variants)
.

The following inequalities hold

(4.9)

\displaystyle B(\Theta,\Psi,\Phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\Theta\rVert_{H^{2+\alpha}(\Omega)}\,\lVert\nabla\Psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert\nabla\Phi\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

for all
\Theta\in H^{2+\alpha}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),\Psi\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),\Phi\in W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})

and
\alpha/2\leq q<\infty
.
Moreover,

(4.10)

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta,\Psi,\Phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Theta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert\nabla\Psi\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,\lVert D^{2}\Phi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Theta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert D^{2}\Psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert D^{2}\Phi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for all
\Theta\in H^{2}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\Psi,\Phi\in H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

p>2
(where the constant depends on
p
). Furthermore,

(4.12)

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta,\Psi,\Phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Theta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert D^{2}\Psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert\nabla\Phi\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

for all
\Theta\in H^{2}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\Psi,\Phi\in H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

q>2
(where the constant depends on
q
), and

(4.13)

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta,\Psi,\Phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Theta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert\nabla\Psi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert\nabla\Phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

for all
\Theta\in H^{2}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\Psi\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),\Phi\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Hölder inequalities and the
embedding (
4.5
) and the Sobolev inequality (
4.6
).
∎

4.2.
The discretization and a priori error analysis

The discrete problem employs the discrete multilinear forms

A_{\mathrm{pw}}:H^{2}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2})\times H^{2}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}
and

B_{h}:H^{2}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2})\times H^{1}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2})\times H^{1}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}

with

\displaystyle a_{\mathrm{pw}}(\psi,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\psi:D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\varphi\,dx,

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi,\Phi)

\displaystyle=a_{\mathrm{pw}}(\psi_{1},\varphi_{1})+a_{\mathrm{pw}}(\psi_{2},\varphi_{2}),

\displaystyle b_{\mathrm{pw}}(\theta,\psi,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\operatorname{cof}(D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\theta)\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\psi)\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\varphi\,dx,

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta,\Psi,\Phi)

\displaystyle=b_{\mathrm{pw}}(\theta_{1},\psi_{2},\varphi_{1})+b_{\mathrm{pw}}(\theta_{2},\psi_{1},\varphi_{1})-b_{\mathrm{pw}}(\theta_{1},\psi_{1},\varphi_{2}),

\displaystyle B_{h}(\Theta,\Psi,\Phi)

\displaystyle=b_{\mathrm{pw}}(\theta_{1},I_{h}\psi_{2},I_{h}\varphi_{1})+b_{\mathrm{pw}}(\theta_{2},I_{h}\psi_{1},I_{h}\varphi_{1})-b_{\mathrm{pw}}(\theta_{1},I_{h}\psi_{1},I_{h}\varphi_{2}),

where
I_{h}
denotes the nodal interpolation operator, see also
Section
2
.
If we let
I_{h}
act component-wise, we see the relation

B_{h}(\Theta,\Psi,\Phi)=B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta,I_{h}\Psi,I_{h}\Phi)
.
The discrete problem seeks
\Psi_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2})
with

(4.14)

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},\Phi_{h})+B_{h}(\Psi_{h},\Psi_{h},\Phi_{h})=F(\Phi_{h})\qquad\text{for all }\Phi_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Remark 4.3
.

Alternatively, the right-hand side in the discrete system
(
4.14
) can be modified to
F(I_{h}\Phi_{h})

as in the prior section. Due to the approximation properties
of
I_{h}
, the arguments of the subsequent error analysis
apply to the modified formulation, too.

The following theorem proves the existence of a discrete
solution of (
4.14
) and an a priori error estimate.
The error estimate will be employed in the
a posteriori error analysis below.

Theorem 4.4
.

For sufficiently small
h
, there exists a (locally unique) discrete
solution
\Psi_{h}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
(
4.14
) and it satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{\alpha},

with
\alpha
from (
4.3
).

Proof.

The proof follows similar as in
[
MN16
]

and is outlined in the appendix.
∎

The a posteriori error estimates in Theorems
4.7

and
4.8
below will contain the term

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

as part of the total error.
The following theorem shows that the predicted convergence rate
for this term is (at least) as good as that predicted for the
H^{2}
error

\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Remark 4.5
.

The following inverse inequality is used, e.g., in the
proof of Theorem
4.6
below.
Let the function
v_{h}
be piecewise polynomial with respect to the mesh

\mathcal{T}
. A standard scaling argument shows the
discrete inequality

(4.15)

\displaystyle\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{(2-q)/q}v_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for any }2\leq q<\infty,

where the constant hidden in the notation
\lesssim
depends on the
polynomial degree.

Theorem 4.6

(
H^{1}
error estimate for nodal interpolant)
.

For sufficiently small
h
, the discrete solution
\Psi_{h}

satisfies the error estimate

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h.

Proof.

The proof follows similar as in
[
MN16
, Theorem 4.7]

and only the differences are given here.

The triangle inequality
leads for
\rho_{h}=\Psi_{h}-I_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi
to

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla(\Psi-I_{h}I_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla(I_{h}\rho_{h}-\rho_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\lVert\nabla(\rho_{h}-J\rho_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla J\rho_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The approximation and stability properties
of the nodal interpolation operator from (
2.4
),
the Morley interpolation operator from (
2.2
),
and the enriching operator from (
2.5
)
together with the stability of the continuous and discrete
problem bound the first three terms on the right-hand side
by
h
.
The last term can be bounded as in
[
MN16
, Theorem 4.7]

by
h
plus the additional term

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h},I_{h}\zeta)-B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},\Psi_{h},I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta)

where
\zeta\in H^{2+\alpha}(\Omega)
is the solution of a dual problem.
Since
I_{h}\zeta=I_{h}I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta
, this term can be estimated via

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h},I_{h}\zeta)-B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},\Psi_{h},I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta)

\displaystyle\qquad=B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h}-\Psi_{h},I_{h}\zeta-\zeta)+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h}-\Psi_{h},\zeta)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},\Psi_{h}-\Psi,I_{h}I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta-I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta)+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},\Psi,I_{h}I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta-I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\lVert\nabla(\Psi_{h}-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla(\zeta-I_{h}\zeta)\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\lVert\nabla(\Psi_{h}-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla\zeta\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\lVert\nabla(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla(I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta-I_{h}I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta)\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\lVert\nabla\Psi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla(I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta-I_{h}I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where the stability of the continuous and discrete system
was employed in the last step.
The inverse inequality (
4.15
),
the approximation properties of the nodal interpolation
operator, the embeddings (
4.8
)
and (
4.5
), and the a priori error
estimate from Theorem
4.4

yield

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(\Psi_{h}-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla(I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta-I_{h}I_{\mathcal{M}}\zeta)\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{1/2},

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(\zeta-I_{h}\zeta)\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\lVert D^{2}\zeta\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lesssim h\lVert\zeta\rVert_{H^{2+\alpha}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{\alpha}.

Since
\alpha\geq 1/2
,
this together with the approximation properties
(
2.4
)
and (
4.7
)
eventually bounds the additional terms by
h
.
∎

4.3.
Error estimator

Define the edge contributions

(4.16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} A_{1}&:=\operatorname{cof}(D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\psi_{h,1})\nabla I_{h}\psi_{h,2}+\operatorname{cof}(D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\psi_{h,2})\nabla I_{h}\psi_{h,1}\\
\text{and }A_{2}&:=\operatorname{cof}(D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\psi_{h,1})\nabla I_{h}\psi_{h,1}.\end{aligned}

We define the error estimator contributions

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}(T)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}h_{T}^{-1}\lVert[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\Psi_{h}]_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}},

\displaystyle\mu_{I_{h}}(T)

\displaystyle:=\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)},

\displaystyle\eta_{f}(T)

\displaystyle:=\lVert h_{T}^{2}f\rVert_{L^{2}(T)},

\displaystyle\eta_{j}(T)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)\cap\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}h_{T}^{3}\lVert[A_{j}]_{F}\nu_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}}\quad\text{for }j=1,2,

and the global error estimator by

\displaystyle\eta:=\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}^{2}(T)+\mu_{I_{h}}^{2}(T)+\eta_{f}^{2}(T)+\eta_{1}^{2}(T)+\eta_{2}^{2}(T)\right)\right)^{1/2}.

The following theorem proves the reliability of the error
estimator, while the efficiency is proved in
Theorem
4.8
below.

Theorem 4.7

(reliability)
.

If
h_{\mathrm{max}}
is sufficiently small, then the error estimator is
reliable in the sense that

\displaystyle\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla(\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eta.

Proof.

Let
J
denote the enriching operator from Section
2
.
The inf-sup condition (
4.4
) of the linearized
problem guarantees the existence
of
\Theta\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
with

\lVert D^{2}\Theta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
and

\displaystyle\lVert D^{2}(\Psi-J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim DN(\Psi;\Psi-J\Psi_{h},\Theta).

Since
N
is quadratic and
\Psi
is the exact solution
with
N(\Psi,\cdot)=0
,
the following Taylor expansion around
\Psi

is exact,
see also
[
CMN20
]
,

\displaystyle N(J\Psi_{h};\Theta)=DN(\Psi;J\Psi_{h}-\Psi;\Theta)+\frac{1}{2}D^{2}N(\Psi;J\Psi_{h}-\Psi,J\Psi_{h}-\Psi,\Theta).

Combining the two foregoing formulas with the representation
(
4.2c
) results in

\displaystyle\lVert D^{2}(\Psi-J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim-N(J\Psi_{h};\Theta)+B(J\Psi_{h}-\Psi,J\Psi_{h}-\Psi,\Theta).

We apply estimate (
4.11
) to the right-hand side
of this estimate, which leads to

\displaystyle\lVert D^{2}(\Psi-J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim-N(J\Psi_{h};\Theta)+\lVert D^{2}(J\Psi_{h}-\Psi)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

From the a priori error analysis it is known that

J\Psi_{h}-\Psi\to 0
for decreasing mesh size, and since the error
term appears quadratically on the right-hand side, eventually we
deduce under the assumption
h_{\max}\ll 1
that

\displaystyle\lVert D^{2}(\Psi-J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim-N(J\Psi_{h};\Theta).

The definition of
N
and the
discrete equation (
4.14
) imply for the Morley interpolation
operator
I_{\mathcal{M}}
from Section
2
that

\displaystyle-N(J\Psi_{h};\Theta)

\displaystyle=-A(J\Psi_{h},\Theta)-B(J\Psi_{h},J\Psi_{h},\Theta)+F(\Theta)

\displaystyle=A_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{\mathcal{M}}\Theta)-A(J\Psi_{h},\Theta)+F(\Theta-I_{\mathcal{M}}\Theta)

\displaystyle\qquad+B_{h}(\Psi_{h},\Psi_{h},I_{\mathcal{M}}\Theta)-B(J\Psi_{h},J\Psi_{h},\Theta).

The integral mean property of the Morley interpolation
operator (
2.1
), the normalization

\lVert D^{2}\Theta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
, and (
2.7
) imply

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{\mathcal{M}}\Theta)-A(J\Psi_{h},\Theta)

\displaystyle=A_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},\Theta)-A(J\Psi_{h},\Theta)=A_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h},\Theta)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eta.

Furthermore, the approximation properties of

I_{\mathcal{M}}
from (
2.2
)
and
\lVert D^{2}\Theta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
imply

\displaystyle F(\Theta-I_{\mathcal{M}}\Theta)=\int_{\Omega}f(\theta_{1}-I_{\mathcal{M}}\theta_{1})\,dx\lesssim\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eta.

Combining the foregoing estimates results in

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lVert D^{2}(\Psi-J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eta+\left|B_{h}(\Psi_{h},\Psi_{h},I_{\mathcal{M}}\Theta)-B(J\Psi_{h},J\Psi_{h},\Theta)\right|.

It therefore remains to estimate the terms on the right-hand side
of (
4.17
) involving
B
and
B_{h}
.
The definition of
B_{h}
and

I_{h}I_{\mathcal{M}}\Theta=I_{h}\Theta
(see Section
2
) imply

(4.18)

\displaystyle B_{h}(\Psi_{h},\Psi_{h},I_{\mathcal{M}}\Theta)-B(J\Psi_{h},J\Psi_{h},\Theta)

\displaystyle=B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Theta)-B(J\Psi_{h},J\Psi_{h},\Theta)

\displaystyle=B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h},J\Psi_{h},\Theta)+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-\Psi,I_{h}\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h},\Theta)

\displaystyle+B(\Psi,I_{h}\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h},\Theta)-B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h},\Theta-I_{h}\Theta).

Since
\lVert D^{2}\Theta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
,
the inequality (
4.11
) implies for
the first term on the right-hand side
of (
4.18
) that

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h},J\Psi_{h},\Theta)\lesssim\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert D^{2}J\Psi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The stability properties of the enrichment operator and the
stability of the discrete problem lead to

\lVert D^{2}J\Psi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim 1
, and, therefore,
(
2.7
) implies

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h},J\Psi_{h},\Theta)\lesssim\sqrt{\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}(T)^{2}}\leq\eta.

For the second term of the right-hand side of
(
4.18
) we use (
4.10
)
to conclude that

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-\Psi,I_{h}\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h},\Theta)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq C(p)\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla(J\Psi_{h}-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

for some
2<p\leq 4
.
The inverse inequality (
4.15
)
and the approximation properties of
I_{h}
from
(
2.4
) together with
(
2.6
) and the stability of the
enriching operator (
2.5
) imply
for
\gamma:={1+(2-p)/p}\geq 1/2
that

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(J\Psi_{h}-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{(2-p)/p}\nabla(J\Psi_{h}-I_{h}J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{1+(2-p)/p}D^{2}J\Psi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{\gamma}D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Psi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h_{\max}^{\gamma}.

The stability of the continuous problem (
4.3
)
and (
4.9
) together with
the embedding (
4.5
)
lead for the third term on the right-hand side
of (
4.18
) to

\displaystyle B(\Psi,I_{h}\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h},\Theta)\lesssim\lVert\Psi\rVert_{H^{2+\alpha}(\Omega)}\,\rVert\nabla(J\Psi_{h}-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

A triangle and a discrete Poincaré inequality

[
Bre03
]
(note that the integral mean of

[\nabla\Psi_{h}]_{F}
vanishes on each face) then imply

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(J\Psi_{h}-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h}-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\Psi_{h}-J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\eta\lesssim\eta.

Since
\operatorname{cof}(D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\psi_{h,j})\nabla I_{h}\psi_{h,k}
is piecewise
constant for
j,k\in\{1,2\}
,
a piecewise integration by parts leads for the fourth term
in (
4.18
)
to

\displaystyle-B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h},\Theta-I_{h}\Theta)

\displaystyle\;=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}\bigg(\int_{F}(\theta_{2}-I_{h}\theta_{2})\,[\operatorname{cof}(D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\psi_{h,1})\nabla I_{h}\psi_{h,1}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{F}(\theta_{1}-I_{h}\theta_{1})\,[\operatorname{cof}(D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\psi_{h,1})\nabla I_{h}\psi_{h,2}+\operatorname{cof}(D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}\psi_{h,2})\nabla I_{h}\psi_{h,1}]_{F}\cdot\nu_{F}\,ds\bigg).

A Cauchy and a trace inequality and the approximation properties
of the nodal interpolation operator (
2.4
)
eventually bound the right-hand side by
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}
.

The combination of the previous inequalities for the terms on the
right-hand side of (
4.18
) with
(

) leads to

\displaystyle\lVert D^{2}(\Psi-J\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eta+h_{\max}^{\gamma}\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The triangle inequality and (
2.7
)
therefore lead to

\displaystyle\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eta+h_{\max}^{\gamma}\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

For sufficiently small

on the right-hand side can be absorbed.

Define the oscillations of
f
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T}(\omega_{T})):=\lVert h_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}(f-\Pi_{0}f)\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{T})}.

Theorem 4.8

(efficiency)
.

The error estimator is efficient in the sense that

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}(T)

\displaystyle\lesssim\min_{\Phi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\Psi_{h}-\Phi)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})},

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\mu_{I_{h}}^{2}(T)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla(\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\eta_{f}(T)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}+\|\nabla(\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}+\mathrm{osc}(f,\{T\}),

\displaystyle\eta_{j}(T)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert D_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\omega_{T})}+\|\nabla(\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{T})}+\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T}(\omega_{T})),

for
j=1,2
.

Remark 4.9
.

The error estimator
\eta_{I_{h}}
is only globally
efficient due to the global Poincaré inequality.
However, we have the local version

\displaystyle\mu_{I_{h}}^{2}(T)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}+\lVert\nabla(\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}.

Proof.

Efficiency of
\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}
.

The efficiency of
\mu_{\mathrm{NC}}
follows from (
2.7
).

Efficiency of
\mu_{I_{h}}
.

Since
\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\Psi_{h}
is a Crouzeix-Raviart function,
the piecewise Poincaré inequality
[
Bre03
]

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

together with a triangle inequality proves the efficiency
of
\mu_{I_{h}}
.

Efficiency of
\eta_{f}
.

Let
\flat_{T}\in H^{2}_{0}(T)
denote the
H^{2}
volume bubble function
with
\|\varphi_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\approx 1

and set
\varphi:=\Pi_{0}f\,\flat_{T}
.
Then

\displaystyle\lVert\Pi_{0}f\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\int_{T}\Pi_{0}f\varphi\,dx=\int_{T}f\varphi\,dx+\int_{T}(\Pi_{0}f-f)\varphi\,dx.

The scaling
\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert\Pi_{0}f\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}

proves for the last term

\displaystyle\int_{T}(\Pi_{0}f-f)\varphi\,dx\lesssim h_{T}^{-2}\lVert\Pi_{0}f\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}\mathrm{osc}(f,\{T\}).

Since
\varphi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, the continuous problem
(
4.1
) implies for the second term
for
\Phi=(\varphi,0)

\displaystyle\int_{T}f\varphi\,dx=A(\Psi,\Phi)+B(\Psi,\Psi,\Phi).

Since
\varphi\in H^{2}_{0}(T)
, it holds that
A_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},\Phi)=0
.
Moreover, since
D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Psi_{h}
and
\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}I_{h}\Psi_{h}
are
piecewise constant, we also have from integration by parts that

B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h},\Phi)=0
. We thus obtain

\displaystyle\int_{T}f\varphi\,dx=A_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h},\Phi)+B(\Psi,\Psi,\Phi)-B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h},\Phi).

We use the multilinearity of
B_{\mathrm{pw}}
and compute

\displaystyle B(\Psi,\Psi,\Phi)-B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h},\Phi)=B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h},\Psi,\Phi)+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h},\Phi).

We note that
\|D^{2}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h_{T}^{-2}\|\Pi_{0}f\|_{L^{2}(T)}

and estimate the last two terms
with (
4.11
) and (
4.13
)
as follows

B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h},\Psi,\Phi)\lesssim\|D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi-\Psi_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}\,\|D^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{2}(T)}\,h_{T}^{-2}\,\|\Pi_{0}f\|_{L^{2}(T)}

and

B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h},\Phi)\leq\|D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Psi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\,\|\nabla(\Psi-I_{h}\Psi_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}\,\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}.

From scaling we have

\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\lesssim h_{T}^{-1}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim h_{T}^{-2}\|\Pi_{0}f\|_{L^{2}(T)}
.

Efficiency of
\eta_{1}
.

For the efficiency of
\eta_{1}(T)
, recall the abbreviation

from (

)
and

for

The continuous problem (
4.1
) implies

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h},\Phi)=B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi_{h},I_{h}\Psi_{h},\Phi)-B(\Psi,\Psi,\Phi)-A(\Psi,\Phi)+F(\Phi).

The first two terms are estimated via

Furthermore, a piecewise integration by parts
and
\Phi=(\varphi,0)
with

\displaystyle\lVert\Phi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

Appendix A
Outline of the proof of Theorem
4.4

The proof follows as in
[
MN16
]
with the
following small modifications.

The first step consists in the proof of the
discrete inf-sup condition

\displaystyle\sup_{\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Theta_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{NC}}(\Theta_{h},\Phi_{h})\gtrsim\lVert D^{2}_{\mathrm{pw}}\Phi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for the perturbed linearized bilinear form

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{NC}}(\Theta_{h},\Phi_{h})

\displaystyle\qquad:=A_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta_{h},\Phi_{h})+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(I_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi,I_{h}\Theta_{h},I_{h}\Phi_{h})+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta_{h},I_{h}I_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi,I_{h}\Phi_{h}),

compare also with
[
MN16
, Lemma 4.1]
without
the nodal interpolation operator.
This is a consequence of the fact that the perturbation
with respect to the linearized bilinear form

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{NC}}(\Theta_{h},\Phi_{h}):=A_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta_{h},\Phi_{h})+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Psi,\Theta_{h},\Phi_{h})+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta_{h},\Psi,\Phi_{h}).

is only in the lower-order term
B_{\mathrm{pw}}
.

In the second step, it can be shown that the map

\mu:\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathcal{T};\mathbb{R}^{2})

defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{NC}}(\mu(\Theta_{h}),\Phi_{h})

\displaystyle=F(\Phi_{h})+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(I_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi,I_{h}\Theta_{h},I_{h}\Phi_{h})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta_{h},I_{h}I_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi,I_{h}\Phi_{h})-B_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Theta_{h},I_{h}\Theta_{h},I_{h}\Phi_{h})

maps a ball around
I_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi
into itself.
The proof follows the lines of
[
MN16
, Theorem 4.2]

employing additionally the property
(
2.4
).
Moreover,
\mu
is (locally around
I_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi
)
a contraction, which implies the (locally unique)
solvability of the discrete problem.

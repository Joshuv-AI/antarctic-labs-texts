Irreducible cuspidal 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n + 1 \mathfrak{sl}_{n+1} -modules from finite-dimensional modules over the minimal nilpotent finite W W -algebra
Genqiang Liu and Mingjie Li

Abstract
. A weight 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n + 1 \mathfrak{sl}_{n+1} -module with finite-dimensional weight spaces is called cuspidal if every root vector acts injectively on it. We establish a direct connection between such modules and the minimal nilpotent finite W W -algebra W ⁡ ( e ) W(e) of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n + 1 \mathfrak{sl}_{n+1} . In particular, every weight space of an irreducible cuspidal module carries a natural structure of an irreducible finite-dimensional W ⁡ ( e ) W(e) -module. Using an explicit embedding τ : W ⁡ ( e ) ↪ U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ) \tau\colon W(e)\hookrightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}) , we prove that every finite-dimensional irreducible W ⁡ ( e ) W(e) -module is an irreducible quotient of V ​ ( λ ) τ V(\lambda)^{\tau} for some finite-dimensional irreducible 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} -module V ⁡ ( λ ) V(\lambda) . We then determine the composition factors of V ​ ( λ ) τ V(\lambda)^{\tau} , characterize its irreducibility in terms of a dot-action orbit, and obtain explicit realizations of all irreducible cuspidal 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n + 1 \mathfrak{sl}_{n+1} -modules, without using the twisted localization method and the coherent family introduced in [ M ] .

centralizer, irreducible module, finite
W
-algebra, cuspidal module

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and
e
a nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{g}
. The finite
W
-algebra
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
was introduced by Premet
[
Pr1
]
as a quantization of the Slodowy slice through the adjoint orbit of
e
.
In fact, finite
W
-algebras appeared earlier in mathematical physics via BRST cohomology
under a slightly different guise, see for example
[
BT
,
DV
,
Tj
]
.
In the case when
e
is a principal nilpotent element, it was proven by Kostant
[
K
]
that the corresponding finite
W
-algebra is isomorphic to the center
Z(\mathfrak{g})
of
U(\mathfrak{g})
. In
[
Pr2
]
, Premet determined generators and defining relations of
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
when
e
is a minimal nilpotent element. In
[
BK
]
, Brundan and Kleshchev described any finite
W
-algebra associated to nilpotent orbits in general linear Lie algebras as a factor algebra of a shifted Yangian. This Yangian realization by generators and relations makes it possible to study the representation theory of finite
W
-algebras in terms of purely combinatorial data. In
[
BK2
]
, Brundan and Kleshchev used the relationship between shifted Yangians and the finite
W
-algebras to study highest weight representations over the finite
W
-algebras of type
A
. There is a close connection between finite-dimensional irreducible representations of

U(\mathfrak{g},e)
and primitive ideals of
U(\mathfrak{g})
. In
[
L
]
, Losev proved
that there is a bijection between the primitive ideals of
U(\mathfrak{g})
whose associated variety is the
closure of the adjoint orbit of
e
, and the orbits of the component group of the centralizer of
e
on
the isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional irreducible
U(\mathfrak{g},e)
-modules.
This classification result was refined in
[
LO
]
.
The recent works in
[
PT
,
T
]
focused on the classification of one-dimensional
W
-modules. The category of finite-dimensional modules over the minimal
nilpotent finite
W
-algebras was studied in
[
Pe
]
. There are several good surveys of the theory of finite
W
-algebras, see for example
[
L2
,
W
]
.

Fernando and Futorny reduced the classification of irreducible weight modules
with finite-dimensional weight spaces to that of cuspidal modules for Levi
subalgebras
[
F
,
Fu
]
. Among simple complex Lie algebras, cuspidal modules
occur only in types
A
and
C
. Mathieu classified them using coherent
families and twisted localization
[
M
]
. Their categories and block
structures were subsequently investigated in
[
BKLM
,
GS1
,
GS2
,
GS3
,
MS2
]
,
and cuspidal modules also provide relaxed highest weight modules for vertex
algebras
[
ACD
,
FKR
,
KR
,
KR2
]
. These methods give a powerful classification,
but the structure carried by an individual weight space is less explicit.
The purpose of this paper is to make that structure concrete in type
A
by
using a minimal nilpotent finite
W
-algebra. Finite
W
-algebras can be used to study Whittaker modules of corresponding Lie algebras, see the appendix given by Serge Skryabin in
[
Pr1
]
. But they are rarely used to study weight modules, especially cuspidal modules. In this paper, we offer a direct connection between cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-modules and finite-dimensional modules over the minimal nilpotent finite
W
-algebra.

Let
e=e_{10}+\dots+e_{n0}
which is a minimal nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
be the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
. In
[
LL
]
, it was shown that the finite
W
-algebra
W(e)
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
associated with
e
is isomorphic to the centralizer
W
of the subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{n}\oplus(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}e_{0i})
in some localization
U_{S}

of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
, see Theorem
2.3
. Moreover, Theorem 19 in
[
LL
]
established an equivalence between a block with a generalized central character of the cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module category
and a block of the finite-dimensional
W(e)
-module category.
By Theorem
2.2
, every weight space of an irreducible cuspidal module
is an irreducible finite-dimensional
W
-module, see Corollary
2.4
. So irreducible cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-modules were reduced to finite-dimensional irreducible modules over
W(e)
. Our main algebraic tool in this paper is an explicit embedding
\tau\colon W(e)\hookrightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}).
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review in detail several facts about

W(e)
, including the centralizer realization of
W(e)
from
[
LL
]
and the injective homomorphism
\tau
from
W
to
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
.
In Section
3
, we study finite-dimensional irreducible modules over the algebra
\sigma\tau(W)
, where

is the inner automorphism of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
and

X=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{kn}
. Using highest-weight module theory, we show that if
M
is a finite-dimensional irreducible
\sigma\tau(W)
-module, then there is a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda)
such that
M
is isomorphic to an irreducible quotient
of
V(\lambda)
, see Theorem
3.8
. It should be mentioned that
[
BK2
]
provides a sufficient and necessary condition for irreducible highest weight modules over finite
W
-algebras of type
A
to be finite-dimensional, extending the theory of Yangians due to Tarasov
[
Ta
]
and Drinfeld
[
Dr
]
. Our method is more specific and elementary.
We also show that restricted to
\sigma\tau(W)
, the composition length of

is at most
2
, and determine all its irreducible subquotients, see Theorem
3.15
. We also show that these two statements
also hold for
W
, see Theorems
3.17
and
3.18
.
In Section
4
, using the results in Section 3, we give specific realizations of
all irreducible cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-modules, see Theorem
4.2
and Corollary
4.5
.

and

. For

.
For a Lie algebra

We write

. For an

. For an associative algebra

(resp. a Lie algebra

), let

(resp.

) be the center of

(resp.

). An algebra homomorphism

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some necessary preliminaries, including
the algebra
W(e)
, a centralizer realization of
W(e)
,
cuspidal modules over
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
, and a
Miura type map

\tau:W\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
.

2.1.
The definition of
W(e)

For a fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, let
\mathfrak{gl}_{n+1}
be the general linear Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{gl}_{n+1}
consists of
(n+1)\times(n+1)
-complex matrices.
Let
e_{ij}
denote the
(n+1)\times(n+1)
-matrix unit whose
(i,j)
-entry is
1
and
0
elsewhere,
0\leq i,j\leq n
. Then
\{e_{ij}\mid 0\leq i,j\leq n\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n+1}
. Set
I_{n-1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{kk}
,
I_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{kk}
and
I_{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}e_{kk}
.
Let
\mathfrak{h}_{n}=\oplus_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{C}(e_{ii}-e_{i+1,i+1})
which is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
. In
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
, let
h_{i}=e_{ii}-\frac{1}{n+1}I_{n+1}
for any
i\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
.
Define a
\mathbb{Z}
-gradation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
:

\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}=\mathfrak{g}(-2)\oplus\mathfrak{g}(0)\oplus\mathfrak{g}(2),

where
\mathfrak{g}(2)=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}e_{i0}
,
\mathfrak{g}(-2)=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}e_{0i}
, and
\mathfrak{g}(0)\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is the subalgebra spanned by
e_{ij},1\leq i\neq j\leq n
and
h_{k},k=1,\dots,n
.

Let
G=SL_{n+1}
acting on
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
by conjugation.
Denote by
\mathcal{N}
the null cone which consists of all nilpotent elements in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
. Consider the conjugate action of
G
on
\mathcal{N}
. The set of nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
under this action is naturally a poset with partial order
\leq
as follows:
O^{\prime}\leq O
if and only if
O^{\prime}\subset\overline{O}
,
where
\overline{O}
is the closure of
O
.
There exists a unique
minimal orbit
O_{\text{min}}
in
\mathcal{N}
of the smallest positive dimension.
Every nilpotent matrix in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}

is conjugate to a matrix in
Jordan canonical form parameterized
by a partition
p=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots)
of
n+1
. In particular,
the associated Jordan block of
O_{\text{min}}
is
(2,1^{n-1})
.

Throughout this paper, we fix a nilpotent element
e=e_{10}+\dots+e_{n0}
in

\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
. Since
e
is conjugate to
e_{10}
,
e
is a minimal nilpotent element.

Lemma 2.1
.

The decomposition

\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}=\mathfrak{g}(-2)\oplus\mathfrak{g}(0)\oplus\mathfrak{g}(2)

is a good
\mathbb{Z}
-grading
for
e
, see
[
EK
]
. That is
e\in\mathfrak{g}(2)
,

\text{ad}e:\mathfrak{g}(0)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(2)
is surjective, and
\text{ad}e:\mathfrak{g}(-2)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(0)
is injective.

Proof.

For any
1\leq i\neq k\leq n
,
[e_{ik},e]=e_{i0}
. So
\text{ad}e:\mathfrak{g}(0)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(2)
is surjective.
If
[\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}e_{0k},e]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}e_{00}-\sum_{k,i=1}^{n}a_{k}e_{ik}=0
, then
a_{1}=\dots=a_{n}=0
. Hence
\text{ad}e:\mathfrak{g}(-2)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(0)
is injective.
∎

The map
\theta:\mathfrak{g}(-2)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},X\mapsto\text{tr}(Xe)
defines a one dimensional

\mathfrak{g}(-2)
-module
\mathbb{C}_{\mathbf{1}}:=\mathbb{C}v_{\mathbf{1}}
. It is easy to see that
\theta(e_{0i})=1
, for any
1\leq i\leq n
. Let

\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{1}}
be the left ideal of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
generated by

e_{01}-1,\dots,e_{0n}-1
.
Define the induced
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module
(called generalized Gelfand-Graev module)

(2.1)

Q_{\mathbf{1}}:=U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}(-2))}\mathbb{C}_{\mathbf{1}}\cong U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})/\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{1}}.

The finite
W
-algebra
W(e)
is defined to be
the endomorphism algebra

W(e):=\text{End}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}}(Q_{\mathbf{1}})^{\text{op}}.

This definition of finite
W
algebras is due to Premet, see
[
Pr1
]
.
Two different good
\mathbb{Z}
-gradings of
e
are shown to lead to isomorphic finite

W
-algebras, see
[
BG
]
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}_{n}:=\mathfrak{g}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{g}(0)
which is a maximal parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
. The PBW theorem implies that

U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})=U(\mathfrak{p}_{n})\oplus\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{1}}.

So as a vector space,
Q_{\mathbf{1}}\cong U(\mathfrak{p}_{n})
.
Applying Frobenius reciprocity,
W(e)
is isomorphic to the
subalgebra

U(\mathfrak{p}_{n})^{\mathfrak{g}(-2)}=\{y\in U(\mathfrak{p}_{n})\mid[e_{0i},y]\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{1}},\text{for}\ 1\leq i\leq n\},

since
\xi(v_{\mathbf{1}})
corresponds to an element in
U(\mathfrak{p}_{n})^{\mathfrak{g}(-2)}
,
for any
\xi\in\text{End}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}}(Q_{\mathbf{1}})
.

2.2.
Centralizer realization of
W(e)

In this subsection, we recall an explicit realization of the finite
W
-algebra
W(e)
given in
[
LL
]
.

By Lemma 4.2 in
[
M
]
, the set

S:=\{e_{01}^{i_{1}}\dots e_{0n}^{i_{n}}\mid i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}

is an Ore subset of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
.
Let
U_{S}
be the localization
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
with respect to
S
. Let
B
be the subalgebra of
U_{S}
generated by
h_{i},e_{0k},e_{0k}^{-1},1\leq k,i\leq n
.
Let
W
be the centralizer of
B
of
U_{S}
.
Define the following distinguished elements in
U_{S}
:

(2.2)

\displaystyle x_{ij}

\displaystyle=e_{ij}e_{0i}e_{0j}^{-1}-h_{i},

\displaystyle\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=e_{k0}e_{0k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{kj}-\frac{\delta_{jk}}{n+1}I_{n+1})(h_{j}-1)e_{0k}e_{0j}^{-1},

for all
i,j,k=1,\dots,n
with

i\neq j
.

The next theorem tells us that
W
is a tensor product factor of

U_{S}
, see Lemma 7 and Theorem 8 in
[
LL
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

We have the following statements.

(
a
)

U_{S}\cong B\otimes W
,
Z(U_{S})\cong Z(W)
.

(
b
)

All the ordered monomials in
x_{ij},\omega_{k}
,
i,j,k=1,\dots,n
with

i\neq j
form a basis of
W

over
\mathbb{C}
.

It seems that the isomorphism in Theorem
2.2
may
have some potential connection with the conjecture of Gelfand and Kirillov on skew-field of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
, see
[
GK
]
.The following theorem is Theorem 9 in
[
LL
]
. Here we give a new proof.

Theorem 2.3
.

We have the algebra isomorphism
W\cong W(e)
.

Proof.

Let
B_{\mathbf{1}}=B\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}(-2))}\mathbb{C}_{\mathbf{1}}
. As a vector space,
B_{\mathbf{1}}=\mathbb{C}[h_{1},\dots,h_{n}]v_{\mathbf{1}}
. From

(e_{0i}-1)g(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})v_{\mathbf{1}}=(g(h_{1},\dots,h_{i}+1,\dots,h_{n})-g(h_{1},\dots,h_{n}))v_{\mathbf{1}},

we see that
(e_{0i}-1)
decreases the degree of
h_{i}
in
g(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})v_{\mathbf{1}}
. Consequently any nonzero
B
-submodule of
B_{\mathbf{1}}
must contain the generator
v_{\mathbf{1}}
. So
B_{\mathbf{1}}
is an irreducible
B
-module.
Then by Theorem
2.2
, the module
Q_{\mathbf{1}}
defined by (
2.1
) is isomorphic to
B_{\mathbf{1}}\otimes W
.
Since
B
is an associative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra of countable dimension,
by Schur’s Lemma (see Lemma 1 in
[
PS
]
),
\text{End}_{B}(B_{\mathbf{1}})\cong\mathbb{C}
.
Then

W(e)\cong\text{End}_{B\otimes W}(B_{\mathbf{1}}\otimes W)^{\text{op}}\cong\text{End}_{W}(W)^{\text{op}}\cong W
.
∎

2.3.
Weight
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-modules and
finite-dimensional
W
-modules

An
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module
M
is a weight module if

M=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}M_{\alpha},\qquad M_{\alpha}=\{v\in M\mid h_{i}v=\alpha_{i}v\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n\}.

Denote
\mathrm{Supp}(M):=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid M_{\alpha}\neq 0\}
.
A weight

\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module
M
is cuspidal if all weight spaces
M_{\alpha}
are finite-dimensional, and
e_{ij}:M\rightarrow M
is injective for all
i,j:0\leq i\neq j\leq n
. Denote by
\mathcal{C}
the category of all cuspidal

\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-modules.
The classification of all irreducible weight
modules with finite-dimensional weight spaces over reductive Lie algebras can be reduced to the classification of irreducible cuspidal modules, see
[
F
]
. The classification of irreducible cuspidal modules was given in
[
M
]
using the twisted localization method and the coherent family. However, the characterization of weight spaces of any cuspidal module is not clear.
In this paper, we will use
finite-dimensional irreducible
W
-modules to realize all irreducible cuspidal

\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-modules.

Corollary 2.4
.

If
M=\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}M_{\mu+\alpha}
is an irreducible cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module,
then
M_{\mu}
is an irreducible finite-dimensional
W
-module.

Proof.

Since every root vector acts bijectively on

M
,
M
can be extended to be a
U_{S}
-module. From
[W,\mathfrak{h}_{n}]=0
,

WM_{\mu}\subset M_{\mu}
, i.e.,
M_{\mu}
is a
W
-module.
By the isomorphism
U_{S}\cong B\otimes W
, we have that

M=\mathbb{C}[e_{01}^{\pm 1},\dots,e_{0n}^{\pm 1}]\otimes M_{\mu}
.
If
V
is a nonzero
W
-submodule of
M_{\mu}
, then
\mathbb{C}[e_{01}^{\pm 1},\dots,e_{0n}^{\pm 1}]\otimes V
is a nonzero

\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-submodule of
M
.
The irreducibility of

M
implies that
V=M_{\mu}
. Therefore
M_{\mu}
is an irreducible
W
-module.
∎

2.4.
An algebra homomorphism from
W
to
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})

By Theorem
2.3
, we identify
W(e)
with the subalgebra
W
of
U_{S}
. Using the isomorphisms
W\cong W(e)
and
W(e)\cong U(\mathfrak{p}_{n})^{\mathfrak{g}(-2)}
, by identifying
h_{i}
with
e_{ii}
for
1\leq i\leq n
,
we have the algebra homomorphism

\phi:W\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{p}_{n})

such that:

(2.3)

\displaystyle x_{ij}

\displaystyle\mapsto e_{ij}-e_{ii},\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq n,

\displaystyle\omega_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto e_{i0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{ij}e_{jj}-e_{ij}),\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Consider the composition of
\phi
and the canonical projection
from
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
to
\mathfrak{p}_{n}/\mathfrak{g}(2)\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
,
we have the following homomorphism.

Lemma 2.5
.

There is an injective homomorphism

\tau:W\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})

such that:

(2.4)

\displaystyle x_{ij}

\displaystyle\mapsto e_{ij}-e_{ii},\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq n,

\displaystyle\omega_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\gamma_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{ij}e_{jj}-e_{ij}),\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Proof.

We show that
\tau
is injective. Denote
X_{ij}=e_{ij}-e_{ii},i\neq j,X_{i}=e_{ii}
. Then
\{X_{ij},X_{i}\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.
From

\gamma_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=1\atop j\neq i}^{n}((X_{ij}+X_{i})X_{j}-X_{ij}-X_{i})+X_{i}^{2}-X_{i},

it follows that

\tau(\omega_{1}^{r_{1}}\dots\omega_{n}^{r_{n}})=\gamma_{1}^{r_{1}}\dots\gamma_{n}^{r_{n}}=X_{1}^{2r_{1}}\dots X_{n}^{2r_{n}}+g(X_{ij},X_{i}),

where
g(X_{ij},X_{i})\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
such that any PBW monomial in
g(X_{ij},X_{i})

is not equal to
X_{1}^{2r_{1}}\dots X_{n}^{2r_{n}}
.
By (b) in Theorem
2.2
, a general monomial in
W
has the form

\prod_{i\neq j}x_{ij}^{\alpha_{ij}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\omega_{k}^{r_{k}}.

After applying
\tau
, its leading term is

\prod_{i\neq j}X_{ij}^{\alpha_{ij}}X_{1}^{2r_{1}}\dots X_{n}^{2r_{n}},

up to lower-order terms.
Hence

\tau
maps nonzero elements
in
W

to nonzero elements in
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
.
Therefore
\tau
is injective.
∎

By Lemma
2.5
, any
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V

can be defined to be a
W
-module denoted by
V^{\tau}

such that
x\cdot v=\tau(x)v
for all
x\in W,v\in V
.
We will show that any finite-dimensional irreducible
W
-module is
isomorphic to an irreducible
W
-quotient of the
W
-module
V^{\tau}
for some finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V
.

Remark 2.6
.

The homomorphism
\tau
is similar to the Miura map

\mu
introduced by Premet, see Remark 2.2 in
[
Pr2
]
.

3.
Finite-dimensional irreducible

-modules

In this section, we study finite-dimensional irreducible
W
-modules using a subalgebra
\sigma\tau(W)\subset U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
which is isomorphic to
W
. The subalgebra
\sigma\tau(W)
has a triangular decomposition. Thus we can study finite-dimensional irreducible
\sigma\tau(W)
-modules using the theory of highest weight modules.

3.1.
The structure of
\sigma\tau(W)

Let
\sigma:=\textup{exp}(-\textup{ad}X)
which is an automorphism of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, where
X=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{kn}
.
It turns out that the algebra
\sigma\tau(W)
is easier
than
W
.

The following lemma is used to find good generators of
\sigma\tau(W)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
, we have

(
1
)

\sigma(e_{ij}-e_{in})=e_{ij}
.

(
2
)

\sigma(e_{nj}-e_{nn})=e_{nj}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{kj}
.

(
3
)

\sigma(\gamma_{i})=\gamma_{i}+e_{in}(I_{n-1}-n+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{ij}e_{jn}
.

(
4
)

\sigma(\gamma_{n})=-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sigma(\gamma_{k})+\gamma_{n}+e_{nn}(I_{n-1}-n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{nk}e_{kn}
.

Proof.

(1)
It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\sigma(e_{ij}-e_{in})

\displaystyle=e_{ij}-e_{in}+[e_{ij}-e_{in},X]

\displaystyle=e_{ij}-e_{in}+e_{in}=e_{ij}.

(2) From

[e_{nj}-e_{nn},X]=e_{nn}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{kj}+X

and

(\mathrm{ad}X)^{2}(e_{nj}-e_{nn})=-2X,

we have

\displaystyle\sigma(e_{nj}-e_{nn})

\displaystyle=e_{nj}-e_{nn}+e_{nn}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{kj}

\displaystyle=e_{nj}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{kj}.

(3)
Since

\displaystyle\ [\gamma_{i},X]

\displaystyle=[\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{ij}e_{jj}-e_{ij}),\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{kn}]=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(e_{in}e_{jj}+e_{ij}e_{jn})-e_{in}X-(n-1)e_{in}

and

\displaystyle\ (\mathrm{ad}X)^{2}(\gamma_{i})

\displaystyle=[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(e_{in}e_{jj}+e_{ij}e_{jn}),\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{kn}]=2e_{in}X,

we obtain

\displaystyle\ \sigma(\gamma_{i})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{i}+e_{in}(I_{n-1}-n+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{ij}e_{jn}.

(4)
From

\displaystyle\ [\gamma_{n},X]

\displaystyle=[\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{nj}e_{jj}-e_{nj}),\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{kn}]

\displaystyle=e_{nn}I_{n-1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}-(n-1)e_{nn}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{nk}e_{kn}-e_{nn}X,

\displaystyle(\mathrm{ad}X)^{2}(\gamma_{n})

\displaystyle=[e_{nn}I_{n-1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}-(n-1)e_{nn}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{nk}e_{kn}-e_{nn}X,X]

\displaystyle=-2XI_{n-1}+2e_{nn}X-2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{kj}e_{jn}+2(n-1)X+2X^{2},

and

\displaystyle\ -(\mathrm{ad}X)^{3}(\gamma_{n})

\displaystyle=[-2XI_{n-1}+2e_{nn}X-2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{kj}e_{jn},X]=-6X^{2},

it follows that

\displaystyle\sigma(\gamma_{n})=

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}+e_{nn}I_{n-1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{k}-(n-1)e_{nn}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{nk}e_{kn}

\displaystyle-XI_{n-1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{kj}e_{jn}+(n-1)X,

and

\displaystyle\sigma(\gamma_{n})+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sigma(\gamma_{k})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n}+e_{nn}I_{n-1}-(n-1)e_{nn}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{nk}e_{kn}

\displaystyle-XI_{n-1}+(n-1)X+X(I_{n-1}-n+1)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n}+e_{nn}(I_{n-1}-n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{nk}e_{kn}.

Then (4) follows.

∎

The following lemma gives generators
of the algebra
\sigma\tau(W)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The elements

e_{ij},e_{ni},1\leq i,j\leq n-1,

y_{k}:=e_{kn}(I_{n}-n)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{kj}e_{jn},1\leq k\leq n,

belong to
\sigma\tau(W)
. Moreover

\sigma\tau(W)
is generated by these elements.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}
be the Lie subalgebra spanned by
e_{ij},1\leq i,j\leq n-1
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have
\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1},\{e_{ni}|1\leq i\leq n-1\}\subset\sigma\tau(W)
.
Then the first stament follows from

\displaystyle\ \sigma(\gamma_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{kj}e_{jj}-e_{kj})+e_{kn}(I_{n-1}-n+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{kj}e_{jn}

\displaystyle\equiv e_{kn}e_{nn}-e_{kn}+e_{kn}(I_{n-1}-n+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{kj}e_{jn}\ \ (\text{mod}\ U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}))

\displaystyle\equiv y_{k}\ \ (\text{mod}\ U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1})),1\leq k\leq n-1,

and

\displaystyle\sigma(\gamma_{n})+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sigma(\gamma_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{nj}e_{jj}-e_{nj})+e_{nn}(I_{n-1}-n+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_{nk}e_{kn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(e_{nj}e_{jj}-e_{nj})+y_{n}.

The second statement follows from (b) in Theorem
2.2
.
∎

The next lemma shows that
the structure of
\sigma\tau(W)
is very similar to that of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
.
The subspace
\mathrm{Span}\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1},y_{n}\}

is like the
n
-th column of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
1\leq i,j,k\leq n-1
, we have

(1)

[e_{ij},y_{k}]=\delta_{jk}y_{i}
,
[y_{i},y_{k}]=0
,
[e_{ij},y_{n}]=0
.

(2)

[e_{nj},y_{k}]=\delta_{jk}y_{n}-e_{kj}(I_{n-1}-n)-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{kl}e_{lj}
.

(3)

[e_{nj},y_{n}]=-e_{nj}(I_{n-1}-n)-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{nl}e_{lj}
.

Proof.

We check (2) and (3). Explicitly

\displaystyle\ [e_{nj},y_{k}]=

\displaystyle[e_{nj},e_{kn}(I_{n}-n)+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{kl}e_{ln}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}e_{nn}(I_{n}-n)-e_{kj}(I_{n}-n)+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\delta_{jk}e_{nl}e_{ln}+e_{kj}e_{nn}-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{kl}e_{lj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}y_{n}-e_{kj}(I_{n}-n)+e_{kj}e_{nn}-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{kl}e_{lj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}y_{n}-e_{kj}(I_{n-1}-n)-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{kl}e_{lj},

and

\displaystyle\ [y_{n},e_{nk}]=

\displaystyle[e_{nn}(I_{n}-n)+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{nl}e_{ln},e_{nk}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{nk}(I_{n}-n)-e_{nk}e_{nn}+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{nl}e_{lk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{nk}(I_{n-1}-n)+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{nl}e_{lk}.

(1) also follows from direct calculations.
∎

Example 3.4
.

If
n=2
,
\sigma\tau(W)
is generated by

e_{11},y_{1},y_{2},e_{21}
subject to the following relation:

(1)

[e_{11},y_{1}]=y_{1}
,
[y_{1},e_{21}]=2e_{11}(e_{11}-1)-y_{2}
,
[e_{11},e_{21}]=-e_{21}
.

(2)

[e_{11},y_{2}]=0
,
[y_{2},e_{21}]=2e_{21}(e_{11}-1)

and
C=y_{2}+e_{11}^{2}-e_{11}
is a central element.

In this case,
W
is isomorphic to the algebra
W_{3}^{(2)}
introduced in

[
Tj
]
.

By Theorem
2.2
, Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
, the algebra
\sigma\tau(W)
has the following triangular decomposition.

Proposition 3.5
.

\sigma\tau(W)=\sigma\tau(W)^{-}\otimes\sigma\tau(W)^{0}\otimes\sigma\tau(W)^{+},

where
\sigma\tau(W)^{+}
is generated by
y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1},e_{ij},1\leq i<j\leq n-1
,
\sigma\tau(W)^{0}
is generated by
y_{n},e_{ii},1\leq i\leq n-1
,
and
\sigma\tau(W)^{-}
is generated by
e_{n1},\dots,e_{n,n-1},e_{ij},1\leq j<i\leq n-1
.

Similar to
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, for
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
, we define
the Verma module

M_{\sigma\tau(W)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n}):=\sigma\tau(W)/J(\lambda,\eta_{n}),

where
J(\lambda,\eta_{n})
is the left ideal generated by

\sigma\tau(W)^{+}
,
y_{n}-\eta_{n}
, and
e_{ii}-\lambda_{i}

for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. The triangular decomposition implies that this
module has a unique simple quotient, denoted by

L_{\sigma\tau(W)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})
. More generally, a
quotient of
M_{\sigma\tau(W)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})
is called a
highest weight module of highest weight
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})
.

The following lemma will be used in the proof of Proposition
3.7
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
M
be a highest weight module over
\sigma\tau(W)
of the highest weight
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})
and
v^{+}
one of its highest weight vectors. Then for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, and
s\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, we have

\displaystyle\ y_{s}e_{ns}^{k}v^{+}

\displaystyle=ke_{ns}^{k-1}\Big((e_{ss}+1-k)^{2}+(e_{ss}+1-k)(I_{n-1}-n)\Big)v^{+}

\displaystyle+ke_{ns}^{k-1}\Big(\sum_{l=1\atop l\neq s}^{n-1}e_{sl}e_{ls}-y_{n}\Big)v^{+}-k(k-1)\sum_{l=1\atop l\neq s}^{n-1}e_{ns}^{k-2}e_{nl}e_{ls}v^{+}.

Proof.

We can compute that

\displaystyle\ y_{n}e_{ns}^{k-i-1}v^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-i-2}e_{ns}^{j}\Big(e_{ns}(I_{n-1}-n)+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{nl}e_{ls}\Big)e_{ns}^{k-i-2-j}v^{+}+e_{ns}^{k-i-1}y_{n}v^{+}

\displaystyle=e_{ns}^{k-i-1}\Big(-i^{2}-i(I_{n-1}-n+e_{ss}+3-2k)+(k-1)(I_{n-1}-n+e_{ss}+2-k)\Big)v^{+}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l\neq s}^{n-1}(k-i-1)e_{ns}^{k-i-2}e_{nl}e_{ls}v^{+}+e_{ns}^{k-i-1}y_{n}v^{+},

and

\displaystyle\ \sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{sl}e_{ls}e_{ns}^{k-i-1}v^{+}

\displaystyle=e_{ss}e_{ss}e_{ns}^{k-i-1}v^{+}+\sum_{l\neq s}^{n-1}e_{sl}e_{ls}e_{ns}^{k-i-1}v^{+}

\displaystyle=e_{ns}^{k-i-1}(e_{ss}+i+1-k)^{2}v^{+}-\sum_{l\neq s}^{n-1}(k-i-1)e_{ns}^{k-i-2}e_{nl}e_{ls}v^{+}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l\neq s}^{n-1}e_{ns}^{k-i-1}e_{sl}e_{ls}v^{+}

\displaystyle=e_{ns}^{k-i-1}\Big(i^{2}+2i(e_{ss}+1-k)+(e_{ss}+1-k)^{2}\Big)v^{+}

\displaystyle-\sum_{l\neq s}^{n-1}(k-i-1)e_{ns}^{k-i-2}e_{nl}e_{ls}v^{+}+\sum_{l\neq s}^{n-1}e_{ns}^{k-i-1}e_{sl}e_{ls}v^{+}.

Then

\displaystyle\ y_{s}e_{ns}^{k}v^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}e_{ns}^{i}\Big(e_{ss}(I_{n-1}-n)+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}e_{sl}e_{ls}-y_{n}\Big)e_{ns}^{k-i-1}v^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}e_{ns}^{k-1}\Big(i^{2}+i(e_{ss}+2-2k+I_{n-1}-n)+(e_{ss}+1-k)(I_{n-1}-n+1-k)\Big)v^{+}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}e_{ns}^{k-1}\Big(i^{2}+2i(e_{ss}+1-k)+(e_{ss}+1-k)^{2}\Big)v^{+}+\sum_{l\neq s}^{n-1}ke_{ns}^{k-1}e_{sl}e_{ls}v^{+}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}e_{ns}^{k-1}\Big(i^{2}+i(I_{n-1}-n+e_{ss}+3-2k)-(k-1)(I_{n-1}-n+e_{ss}+2-k)\Big)v^{+}

\displaystyle-k(k-1)\sum_{l\neq s}^{n-1}e_{ns}^{k-2}e_{nl}e_{ls}v_{\lambda}-ke_{ns}^{k-1}y_{n}v^{+}

\displaystyle=ke_{ns}^{k-1}\Big((e_{ss}+1-k)^{2}+(e_{ss}+1-k)(I_{n-1}-n)+\sum_{l\neq s}^{n-1}e_{sl}e_{ls}-y_{n}\Big)v^{+}

\displaystyle-k(k-1)\sum_{l\neq s}^{n-1}e_{ns}^{k-2}e_{nl}e_{ls}v^{+}.

∎

3.2.
Finite-dimensional irreducible modules over
\sigma\tau(W)

In this subsection, we will determine when
L_{\sigma\tau(W)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})
is finite-dimensional and the relation
between
L_{\sigma\tau(W)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})
and a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module.

Proposition 3.7
.

For
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, if the module
L_{\sigma\tau(W)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})
is finite-dimensional, then

(3.1)

\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\qquad(1\leq i\leq n-2),

and there is
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}

such that

\eta_{n}=(\lambda_{n-1}+1-k)(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-1}-n+\lambda_{n-1}+1-k).

Proof.

By the theory of finite-dimensional modules over
\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}
,
(
3.1
) holds. Since the subspace
\text{span}\{e_{n,n-1}^{i}v^{+}|i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
of

L_{\sigma\tau(W)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})
is finite-dimensional, we can choose some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that

e_{n,n-1}^{k}v^{+}=0
but
e_{n,n-1}^{k-1}v^{+}\neq 0
. Next we determine the
action of
y_{n-1}
on
e_{n,n-1}^{k}v^{+}
.

By Lemma
3.6
and
e_{l,n-1}v^{+}=0
for
l<n-1
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle y_{n-1}e_{n,n-1}^{k}v^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ke_{n,n-1}^{k-1}\Big((e_{n-1,n-1}+1-k)^{2}+(e_{n-1,n-1}+1-k)(I_{n-1}-n)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{n-2}e_{n-1,l}e_{l,n-1}-y_{n}\Big)v^{+}-k(k-1)\sum_{l=1}^{n-2}e_{n,n-1}^{k-2}e_{n,l}e_{l,n-1}v^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ke_{n,n-1}^{k-1}\Big((e_{n-1,n-1}+1-k)^{2}+(I_{n-1}-n)(e_{n-1,n-1}+1-k)-y_{n}\Big)v^{+}=0.

Therefore
(\lambda_{n-1}+1-k)^{2}+(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-1}-n)(\lambda_{n-1}+1-k)-\eta_{n}=0
.
∎

We view every
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

as a linear map
\lambda
on
(\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}e_{ii})

such that
\lambda(e_{ii})=\lambda_{i}
for any
i
.
For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
V(\lambda)

be the irreducible highest weight module over
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
with the highest weight
\lambda
. Let
\Lambda_{n}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
.
It is well known that
V(\lambda)
is finite-dimensional if and only if
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}
,
see
[
H
, Theorem 21.2]
.

Note that
\sigma\tau(W)\subset U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, see Lemma
3.2
. Restricted to
\sigma\tau(W)
, any finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module has a finite-dimensional irreducible
\sigma\tau(W)
-quotient module.
We will show that the converse statement is still true.

Theorem 3.8
.

If
M
is a finite-dimensional irreducible
\sigma\tau(W)
-module, then there is a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda)
such that
M
is isomorphic to an irreducible
\sigma\tau(W)
-quotient module
of
V(\lambda)
.

Proof.

We first explain that
M
is a highest weight module. The action of

I_{n-1}
on
M
has a finite number of eigenvalues.
Let
K\subset M
be the eigenspace of
I_{n-1}
corresponding to
eigenvalue with the maximal real part.
Since
[I_{n-1},y_{j}]=y_{j}
, the maximality implies that
y_{j}K=0

for all
j<n
.
The relations in (1) of
Lemma
3.3
show that
K
is stable under
\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}
and
y_{n}
. As a
finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}
-module,
K
is completely reducible. Thus
K
must contain
a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}
-highest weight vector. By
fact that
[\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1},y_{n}]=0
, we can choose a common eigenvector
v^{+}\in K

of
e_{11},\dots,e_{n-1,n-1},y_{n}
such that
e_{ij}v^{+}=0

for all
1\leq i<j<n
.
Therefore

M\cong L_{\sigma\tau(W)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})

for suitable
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.

By Proposition
3.7
,

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n-2}-\lambda_{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

and there is
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}

such that

(3.2)

\eta_{n}=(\lambda_{n-1}+1-k)(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-1}-n+\lambda_{n-1}+1-k).

Let
\lambda_{n}:=\lambda_{n-1}+1-k
. Then
\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n}=k-1\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and

\eta_{n}=\lambda_{n}(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-1}+\lambda_{n}-n)
.
This means that the irreducible highest weight
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
is finite-dimensional. Restricted to
\sigma\tau(W)
,
the highest weight vector
v_{\lambda}
of
V(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
is annihilated by

\sigma\tau(W)^{+}
, and

y_{n}v_{\lambda}=(e_{nn}(I_{n}-n)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{nj}e_{jn})v_{\lambda}=\eta_{n}v_{\lambda}.

Therefore
V(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})

has a quotient module isomorphic to
L_{\sigma\tau(W)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})
.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

For
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
L_{\sigma\tau(W)}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\eta_{n})
is finite-dimensional if and only if

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n-2}-\lambda_{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

and there is
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}

such that

\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\quad\eta_{n}=\lambda_{n}(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-1}+\lambda_{n}-n).

For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}
, let

V(\lambda)
be the finite-dimensional irreducible
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module
with highest weight
\lambda
.
Denote

I(\lambda)=\{\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots\mu_{n-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\mid\lambda_{1}\geq\mu_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\lambda_{n-1}\geq\mu_{n-1}\geq\lambda_{n}\},

where
a\geq b
implies that
a-b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
If
\mu\in I(\lambda)
, we say that
\mu

interlaces
\lambda
. Then by the branching rule
[
GW
, Theorem 8.1.2]
, we have the following multiplicity-free decomposition:

(3.3)

V(\lambda)=\oplus_{\mu\in I(\lambda)}V_{n-1}(\mu),

where
V_{n-1}(\mu)
is the finite-dimensional irreducible

\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}
-module of the highest weight
\mu
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let

V(\lambda)
be the finite-dimensional irreducible
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module
with highest weight
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}
. Suppose that there is

s\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
such that

(3.4)

k:=\lambda_{s}-s+|\lambda|\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\qquad s-|\lambda|-\lambda_{s+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Set

\nu=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s-1},s-|\lambda|,\lambda_{s+1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}).

Then
\nu
interlaces
\lambda
. Let
v_{\nu}
be a

\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}
-highest weight vector in the unique summand

V_{n-1}(\nu)
of the branching decomposition
(
3.3
).
Then

\sigma\tau(W)^{+}v_{\nu}=0.

Consequently,
v_{\nu}
generates a proper
\sigma\tau(W)
-submodule of

V(\lambda)
. In particular, the restriction of
V(\lambda)
to

\sigma\tau(W)
is reducible.

Proof.

By (
3.4
), we have

\lambda_{s}-\nu_{s}=k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\qquad\nu_{s}-\lambda_{s+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Thus
\nu
interlaces
\lambda
. Hence the branching rule gives a unique
summand
V_{n-1}(\nu)
.

For
m\geq 1
, put

\Omega_{m}:=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}e_{ij}e_{ji},\qquad c_{m}(\alpha):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}\bigl(\alpha_{j}+m+1-2j\bigr).

Thus
\Omega_{m}
acts on the irreducible highest weight

\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
-module of highest weight
\alpha
by the scalar

c_{m}(\alpha)
. Define

D:=y_{n}+\frac{1}{2}e_{nn}(e_{nn}-1).

Since
y_{n}
and
e_{nn}
commute with
\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}
, so does
D

From

Y_{n}
due to Tarasov
[
Ta
]
and Drinfeld
[
Dr
]
. The advantage of our method is more explicit, avoiding using
representations over shifted Yangians. Moreover, we can determine the irreducibility of any finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda)
when it was restricted to
\sigma\tau(W)
in Theorem
3.15
.

4.
Realizations of irreducible cuspidal modules over
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}

In this section, as an application, we give realizations of
all irreducible cuspidal modules over
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
using

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules and the induced module technique.

4.1.
Explicit realizations of cuspidal modules

For
\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a finite-dimensional
W
-module
N
, let each
h_{i}

act on it as the scalar
\mu_{i}
.
Define

G_{\mu}(N)=\text{Ind}_{U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})W}^{U_{S}}N=U_{S}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})W}N.

It is clear that
G_{\mu}(N)=\mathbb{C}[e_{01}^{\pm 1},\dots,e_{0n}^{\pm 1}]\otimes N
by the isomorphism in Theorem
2.2
.
From (
2.2
),
the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module structure on
G_{\mu}(N)
satisfies:

\displaystyle h_{k}\cdot(e^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(\mu_{k}-r_{k})e^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v,

\displaystyle e_{0k}\cdot(e^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=e^{\mathbf{r}+e_{k}}\otimes v,

\displaystyle e_{ij}\cdot(e^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=e^{\mathbf{r}-e_{i}+e_{j}}\otimes x_{ij}v+(\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)e^{\mathbf{r}-e_{i}+e_{j}}\otimes v,\ i\neq j,

\displaystyle e_{i0}\cdot(e^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=e^{\mathbf{r}-e_{i}}\otimes\Big(\omega_{i}v-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mu_{j}-r_{j})x_{ij}v-(|\mu|-|\mathbf{r}|)(\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)v\Big),

where
v\in N,e^{\mathbf{r}}=e_{01}^{r_{1}}\dots e_{0n}^{r_{n}},\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},1\leq i,j,k\leq n
, and
x_{ii}:=0
.

In particular if
V
is a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module, then

G_{\mu}(V^{\tau})
is an
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module such that:

(4.1)

\displaystyle e_{ij}\cdot(e^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=e^{\mathbf{r}-e_{i}+e_{j}}\otimes(e_{ij}-e_{ii}+\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)v,\ i\neq j,

\displaystyle e_{i0}\cdot(e^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=e^{\mathbf{r}-e_{i}}\otimes\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{ij}e_{jj}-e_{ij})v-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mu_{j}-r_{j})(e_{ij}-e_{ii})v

\displaystyle-(|\mu|-|\mathbf{r}|)(\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)v\Big).

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
V(\lambda)
is a finite-dimensional
irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module.
Then for
\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
is a cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module if and only if

(4.2)

\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z},\ |\mu|+\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z},\ \text{for any}\ i\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
e_{0i}
acts injectively on
G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
. Since
V(\lambda)
is a weight module,
V(\lambda)=\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}V(\lambda)_{\alpha}
, where
V(\lambda)_{\alpha}:=\{w\in V(\lambda)\mid e_{kk}w=\alpha_{k}w,k=1\dots,n\}
.
For any nonzero
v=\sum_{\alpha\in I}v_{\alpha}\in V(\lambda)
, where
I\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a finite subset,
0\neq v_{\alpha}\in V(\lambda)_{\alpha}
, we have that

\displaystyle(e_{ij}-e_{ii}+\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)v

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in I}e_{ij}v_{\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha\in I}(-\alpha_{i}+\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)v_{\alpha}.

Then by the first formula in (
4.1
), for
j\neq i
, we can see that
e_{ij}\cdot(e^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)\neq 0
if
\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
, since
\lambda_{i}-\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Conversely, if
G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
is cuspidal,
choose
\alpha\in\mathrm{supp}V(\lambda)
such that

\alpha_{i}
is maximal, then from
(e_{ij}-e_{ii}+\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)v_{\alpha}=(-\alpha_{i}+\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)v_{\alpha}\neq 0
, we have
\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
.
So
e_{ij}

acts injectively on
G_{\mu}(V(\lambda))
if and only if
\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{ij}e_{jj}-e_{ij})v-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mu_{j}-r_{j})(e_{ij}-e_{ii})v-(|\mu|-|\mathbf{r}|)(\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)v

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{ij}(\alpha_{j}-1)v_{\alpha}-\sum_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mu_{j}-r_{j})(e_{ij}-\alpha_{i})v_{\alpha}-\sum_{\alpha\in I}(|\mu|-|\mathbf{r}|)(\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)v_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}e_{ij}(\alpha_{j}-1-\mu_{j}+r_{j})v_{\alpha}-\sum_{\alpha\in I}(|\mu|-|\mathbf{r}|+\alpha_{i})(-\alpha_{i}+\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)v_{\alpha}.

Then by the second formula in (
4.1
), considering the coefficient
(|\mu|-|\mathbf{r}|+\alpha_{i})(-\alpha_{i}+\mu_{i}-r_{i}+1)\neq 0
, we conclude that
e_{i0}
acts injectively on
G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
if and only if

\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
and
|\mu|+\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for any

i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
∎

By Theorem
3.18
, the composition length of
G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
is at most
2
. Now we are ready to give a new classification of irreducible
cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-modules.

Theorem 4.2
.

[Realization Theorem] If
M
is an irreducible cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module such that
\mathrm{supp}M=\mu+\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for some

\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
then there is a finite-dimensional irreducible

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda)
such that
M
is isomorphic to
an irreducible quotient module of
G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
, where
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}
satisfies the condition (
4.2
) and
\tau:W\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
is defined by (
2.4
).

Proof.

Since every root vector acts bijectively on

M
,
M
can be extended to be a
U_{S}
-module. Let
N

be the weight space
M_{\mu}
. From
[W,\mathfrak{h}_{n}]=0
,

WN\subset N
, i.e.,
N
is a
W
-module.
By the isomorphism
U_{S}\cong B\otimes W
,

M\cong G_{\mu}(N)
. The irreducibility of

M
implies that
N
is an irreducible
W
-module.
By Theorem
3.17
, there is a finite-dimensional
irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda)
such that
N
is isomorphic to an irreducible
W
-quotient module of
V(\lambda)^{\tau}
. Therefore
M
is isomorphic to
an irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-quotient module of
G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
.
By Theorem
3.15
, that
N
is also isomorphic to an irreducible
W
-submodule of
V(\lambda^{\prime})^{\tau}
for another
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}
. By the similar proof for Lemma
4.1
,

\lambda
satisfies the condition (
4.2
).
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Recall that
\mathfrak{g}(0)\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. The
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda)
can be extended to a module over the parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{n}:=\mathfrak{g}(0)\ltimes\mathfrak{g}(-2)
by defining
\mathfrak{g}(-2)V(\lambda)=0
. The parabolic Verma module
M_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{n}}(\lambda)
over
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
is

U(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{n})}V(\lambda)
. Denote the unique irreducible quotient of
M_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{n}}(\lambda)
by
L_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{n}}(\lambda)
. In
[
M
]
,
it was shown that an irreducible cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module is isomorphic to a twisted localization of some
L_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{n}}(\lambda)
, see also Theorem 8.3 in
[
GG
]
. Therefore Theorem
4.2
gives an explicit realization
of some irreducible twisted localization of some
L_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{n}}(\lambda)
.

4.2.
Relation between
G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
and the Shen-Larsson module

Finally we discuss the connection between
G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
and
T(P(\mu),V(\lambda))
defined by Proposition
3.11
.
For
\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
P(\mu)=x^{\mu}\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
, where

x^{\mu}=x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{\mu_{n}}
. Then
P(\mu)
is a
D_{n}
-module under the natural actions of derivations.
By (
3.5
) , one can check that
T(P(\mu-\lambda),V(\lambda))
is a cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module if and only if
\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
and
|\mu|+\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for any

i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
and
|\mu|+\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for any

i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then

T(P(\mu-\lambda),V(\lambda))\cong G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
.

Proof.

For convenience, denote
M=T(P(\mu-\lambda),V(\lambda))
.
Since
M
is a cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module,
we have that
M\cong G_{\mu}({M_{\mu}})
. Next we need to show
the
W
-module
M_{\mu}
is isomorphic to
V(\lambda)^{\tau}
.
Let
V(\lambda)=\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}V(\lambda)_{\alpha}

be the weight space decomposition of
V(\lambda)
.
As a vector space,
M_{\mu}=\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Supp}V(\lambda)}x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes V(\lambda)_{\alpha}
.
Define
an action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
on
M_{\mu}
such that

e_{ij}\circ(x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes v_{\alpha})=\frac{\mu_{i}-\alpha_{i}+\delta_{ij}}{\mu_{j}-\alpha_{j}+1}x^{\mu-\alpha+e_{j}-e_{i}}\otimes e_{ij}v_{\alpha},

where
v_{\alpha}\in V(\lambda)_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathrm{Supp}V(\lambda)
.
Let
c_{\alpha}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma(\mu_{k}-\alpha_{k}+1)
,
where
\Gamma
is the Gamma function satisfying

(4.3)

\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Define
\Phi:M_{\mu}\rightarrow V(\lambda)
such that

\Phi(x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes v_{\alpha})=c_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}
, which is a vector space isomorphism. By (
4.3
),

\Phi(e_{ij}\circ(x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes v_{\alpha}))=e_{ij}\Phi(x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes v_{\alpha})
. Hence
\Phi
is a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module isomorphism. By Proposition
3.11
, the action of
W
on
M_{\mu}

satisfies:

\displaystyle x_{ij}(x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes v_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{i}x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes v_{\alpha}+\frac{\mu_{i}-\alpha_{i}+\delta_{ij}}{\mu_{j}-\alpha_{j}+1}x^{\mu-\alpha+e_{j}-e_{i}}\otimes e_{ij}v_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(e_{ij}-e_{ii})\circ(x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes v_{\alpha})

and

\displaystyle\omega_{k}(x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes v_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\Big(e_{k0}e_{0k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{kj}-\frac{\delta_{jk}}{n+1}I_{n+1})(h_{j}-1)e_{0k}e_{0j}^{-1}\Big)(x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes v_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(\mu_{k}-\alpha_{k}+\delta_{jk})(\alpha_{j}-1)}{\mu_{j}-\alpha_{j}+1}x^{\mu-\alpha+e_{j}-e_{k}}\otimes e_{kj}v_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{kj}e_{jj}-e_{kj})\circ(x^{\mu-\alpha}\otimes v_{\alpha}),

for all
i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
with
i\neq j
.
Hence the
W
-module
M_{\mu}
is isomorphic to
V(\lambda)^{\tau}
.
Therefore
M\cong G_{\mu}(V(\lambda)^{\tau})
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

If
M
is an irreducible cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-module such that
\mathrm{supp}M=\mu+\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for some

\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
then there is a finite-dimensional irreducible

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda)
such that
M
is isomorphic to
an irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-quotient module of
T(P(\mu-\lambda),V(\lambda))
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfies that
\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
and
|\mu|+\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for any

i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

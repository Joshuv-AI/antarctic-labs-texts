Otal-Peigné’s Theorem for Gromov-hyperbolic spaces
Nicola Cavallucci

Abstract
. We extend the classical Otal-Peigné’s Theorem to the class of proper, Gromov-hyperbolic spaces that are line-convex. Namely, we prove that when a group acts discretely and virtually freely by isometries on a metric space in this class then its critical exponent equals the topological entropy of the geodesic flow of the quotient metric space. We also show examples of proper, Gromov-hyperbolic spaces that are not line-convex and for which the statement fails.



1.
Introduction

The relation between the topological entropy of the geodesic flow of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with non-positive sectional curvature and the critical exponent of the discrete group of isometries
\Gamma\cong\pi_{1}(M)
acting freely on the universal covering
\tilde{M}
of
M
has been intensively studied during the years.
A.Manning (
[
Man79
]
) proved that in case of compact manifolds with non-positive sectional curvature the topological entropy of the geodesic flow equals the volume entropy of the universal covering, and so it is the critical exponent of the group
\Gamma
.

We recall that the critical exponent of a group
\Gamma
acting discretely by isometries on a metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is classically defined as

(1)

h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma):=\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\#(\Gamma x\cap B(x,T)),

where
x
is any point of
{\rm X}
.

Manning’s result has been generalized to compact, locally geodesically complete, locally
\textup{CAT}(0)
spaces by R.Ricks in
[
Ric21
, Theorem A]
.

We are interested in the much more complicated case of non-cocompact actions. For groups acting on Riemannian manifolds the following result is due to J.P.Otal and M.Peigné in the torsion-free case (cp.
[
OP04
]
, see also
[
Led13
]
), and to F.Paulin, M.Pollicott and B.Schapira in the case of groups with torsion (cp.
[
PPS15
, Theorem 6.1]
).

Theorem

(Otal-Peigné)
.

Let
\tilde{M}
be a complete, simply connected, Riemannian manifold with pinched, negative sectional curvature, i.e.
-b^{2}\leq\textup{Sec}_{g}\leq-a^{2}<0
. Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary, discrete group of isometries of
\tilde{M}
.
Then the topological entropy of the geodesic flow on
M:=\Gamma\backslash\tilde{M}
equals the critical exponent of
\Gamma
.

The price to pay in order to consider every non-elementary group acting discretely on
\tilde{M}
is the condition on the sectional curvature. While the lower bound is quite natural, since every compact manifold has such a bound, the negative upper bound marks a difference among the cocompact case and the general one.

The purpose of this paper is to extend Otal-Peigné’s Theorem to a wider class of metric spaces by weakening the regularity of the space and the upper curvature bound and by essentially removing the lower bound on the curvature.

The upper curvature bound is replaced by Gromov hyperbolicity. This class is extremely wild and contains many non-manifold examples. Moreover, this condition is different in nature from a negative upper bound on the sectional curvature, or a CAT
(-1)
-condition, since it does not give any information on the local geometry of the metric space.

For Gromov-hyperbolic metric spaces
{\rm X}
and discrete groups of isometries
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
, we need to clarify the definition of the quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
. We denote by
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
the space of all geodesic lines of
{\rm X}
. It supports a natural action
\Phi_{t}
by
\mathbb{R}
via reparametrization. The dynamical system
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
is called the geodesic flow of
{\rm X}
. Every isometry of
{\rm X}
acts on
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
and commutes with the flow
\Phi_{t}
. The quotient geodesic flow appearing in Theorem
A
is the dynamical system
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
, where
\bar{\Phi}_{1}
is the induced action on the quotient space
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
.

This definition, proposed for instance in
[
Rob03
,
DT23
]
, may seem artificial and not related to the classical notion of geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
. However, if
{\rm X}
is
\textup{CAT}(-1)
, or even Busemann convex, and
\Gamma
is torsion-free then the dynamical system
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
is conjugated to
(\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
, where
\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X})
is the space of local geodesics of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
and
\Phi_{1}
is again the standard reparametrization. This result is proven in Corollary
3.8
.
For more details we refer to Section
3
.

The topological entropy of the quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is then defined as the topological entropy of the dynamical system
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
. By the variational principle, it can be expressed in the following form:

(2)

h_{\text{top}}:=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{1}}h_{\mu},

where
\mathcal{E}_{1}
is the set of ergodic invariant measures of the dynamical system and
h_{\mu}
is the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of
\mu
. We refer to Section
4
for the definitions.

The equivalent of Otal-Peigné’s Theorem would then be the following: for every Gromov-hyperbolic metric space
{\rm X}
and every discrete
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
, the critical exponent of
\Gamma
equals the topological entropy of the quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
.

However, Gromov hyperbolicity alone is too weak, namely there are Gromov hyperbolic spaces for which this version of Otal-Peigné’s Theorem does not hold (see Theorem
B
).

We need to introduce an additional weak upper bound on the curvature through the next definition: a metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is said to be
line-convex
if for every two geodesic lines
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}\to{\rm X}
, the function
t\mapsto{\sf d}(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))
is convex. For instance, every Busemann convex space is line-convex, but the vice versa is false, see for instance the space of Theorem
E
, which is line-convex but not simply connected. In general also the line-convexity property does not give control on the local geometry of the metric space, since it is preserved by local perturbations of the metric space that are not seen by any geodesic line.

The main result of this work is the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, line-convex, Gromov hyperbolic metric space. Let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete, virtually torsion-free and non-elementary. Assume that
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<+\infty
. Then the topological entropy of the quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
equals the critical exponent of
\Gamma
.

The quantity
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
is what we call the
ergodic entropy
of the action of
\Gamma
on
{\rm X}
. It is defined as follows:

(3)

h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma):=\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\text{Cov}(\overline{B}(x,T);\Lambda_{\text{erg}};r),

where
x
is a fixed basepoint of
{\rm X}
and
\text{Cov}(\overline{B}(x,T);\Lambda_{\text{erg}},r)
denotes the minimal number of
r
-balls needed to cover the set of points that belong to
\overline{B}(x,T)
and that lie in a geodesic line with endpoints in the ergodic limit set
\Lambda_{\text{erg}}(\Gamma)\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
.

The ergodic limit set of the group
\Gamma
has been introduced in
[
Cav24
]
. A point
z\in\partial{\rm X}
belongs to
\Lambda_{\text{erg}}(\Gamma)
if a geodesic ray
\xi_{x,z}
joining the basepoint
x
to
z
satisfies the following property: for some
\tau>0
and some increasing sequence of real numbers
\vartheta_{i}
such that
\exists\lim_{i\to+\infty}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{i}\in(0,+\infty)
it holds that for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
t_{i}\in[\vartheta_{i},\vartheta_{i+1}]
such that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z}(t_{i}),\Gamma x)\leq\tau
. For instance, if the sequence
\vartheta_{i}
is of the form
i\cdot\sigma
then
z
belongs to the set of uniformly radial limit points and the projection of a geodesic ray
\xi_{x,z}
on
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is entirely contained in a compact subset of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
. The condition defining
\Lambda_{\text{erg}}(\Gamma)
is a generalization of uniformly radial points, and if
z\in\Lambda_{\text{erg}}(\Gamma)
then the projection of a geodesic ray
\xi_{x,z}
on
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
may be not contained in a compact region, but it returns to a fixed compact region infinitely many times and the sequence of returning times is asymptotically linear.

From a dynamical point of view, this condition comes directly from Birkhoff’s ergodic Theorem, and indeed every ergodic measure on the quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is concentrated on classes of geodesics with endpoints in
\Lambda_{\text{erg}}(\Gamma)
, see Proposition
7.1
and
[
Cav24
, Theorem C]
.

The assumption
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<+\infty
appearing in Theorem
A
plays the role of a very weak lower bound on the curvature. For instance it is always satisfied when
\Gamma
is quasiconvex cocompact, see Corollary
2.20
. Also, it is always satisfied under other weak geometric constraints. A metric space
{\rm X}
has
bounded geometry
if

(4)

\sup_{x\in{\rm X}}\textup{Cov}(\overline{B}(x,R),r)<+\infty\quad\text{for every }0<r\leq R,

where
\textup{Cov}(\overline{B}(x,R),r)
denotes the minimal number of balls of radius
r
needed to cover the ball
\overline{B}(x,R)
. This assumption is extremely weak: notice that the supremum in (
4
) is always finite on every compact region of
{\rm X}
, so the definition asks just for a uniform bound in the whole space. If a Gromov-hyperbolic metric space has bounded geometry then the ergodic entropy of
\Gamma
coincides with the Minkowski dimension of the limit set of
\Gamma
and this dimension is finite (cp. Corollary
2.18
). Therefore Theorem
A
applies to line-convex, Gromov-hyperbolic metric spaces with bounded geometry. Conditon (
4
) can be further weakened as we show in Section
2
.

The condition
h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<\infty
is not too far from the optimal
h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<\infty
. Indeed, at least for spaces with bounded geometry,
h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
equals the Minkowski dimension of the limit set of
\Gamma
, while
h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
equals the Hausdorff dimension of the radial limit set by the classical Bishop-Jones’ Theorem (cp.
[
BJ97
]
,
[
Pau97
]
,
[
DSU17
]
).
We refer to Section
2
for more details and examples.

The proof of the original Otal-Peigné’s Theorem, also in
[
PPS15
]
, is based on local arguments where the negative sectional curvature assumption is exploited. The proof of the inequality
h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\leq h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
has been extended, with the same techniques, to geodesically complete, CAT
(-1)
spaces in
[
DT23
]
, assuming the finiteness of the Minkowski dimension of the space
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. This condition, somehow transversal to our
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<\infty
, is more sensible to the local geometry of
{\rm X}
.

This classical approach cannot work in the setup of Theorem
A
due to the lack of control on the local geometry of the spaces under consideration. Therefore we will follow a completely new approach involving asymptotic arguments only.
This new strategy allows to prove the inequality
h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\leq h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
also for groups with arbitrary torsion. On the other hand, it really needs the line-convexity of
{\rm X}
in order to work.

Theorem B
.

There exists a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space
{\rm X}
with bounded geometry, but not line-convex, and a discrete, non-elementary, cocompact, torsion-free group
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
such that

h_{\textup{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})>h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma).

Our asymptotic approach gives the proof of the inequality
h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\geq h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
for every Gromov hyperbolic space, without further assumptions.

Theorem C
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete, virtually torsion-free and non-elementary. Then

h_{\textup{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\geq h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma).

We do not know if Theorem
C
is true for every discrete group with arbitrary torsion, as it is in the manifold case because of Paulin-Pollicott-Shapira’s version of the original Otal-Peigné’s Theorem.

In this regard, one could try, as suggested in Remark 6.1 of
[
DT23
]
, to adapt the proof of Theorem
C
given in
[
PPS15
, Lemma 6.7]
to the
\textup{CAT}(-1)
setting. However, the proof of
[
PPS15
, Lemma 6.7]
is based on
[
PPS15
, Proposition 4.9]
, which uses the lower bound on the sectional curvature. However it could be possible to extend the proof of
[
PPS15
, Proposition 4.9]
to geodesically complete, packed, CAT
(-1)
spaces following the ideas of
[
CS24
]
in order to produce free subgroups of a torsion-free group of isometries.
It is not clear to the author how to get Theorem
C
for every group with arbitrary torsion, even in the simplified setting of geodesically complete, CAT
(-1)
spaces.

Interesting geometric phenomena can occur. Let
{\rm X}
be a
\textup{CAT}(-1)
space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete, non-elementary and virtually torsion-free. Let
\Gamma_{0}
be a torsion-free, finite index, normal subgroup of
\Gamma
. As stated above, the two flows
(\Gamma_{0}\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
and
(\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma_{0}\backslash{\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
are conjugated. Moreover, the finite group
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
acts naturally on both spaces commuting with the flows. On
(\Gamma_{0}\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
it acts via the quotient action of the global action of
\Gamma
on
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
, and the quotient is exactly
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
. On
(\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma_{0}\backslash{\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
it acts via the natural action on local geodesics induced by
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
as group of isometries of
\Gamma_{0}\backslash{\rm X}
. The resulting flow is
((\Gamma/\Gamma_{0})\backslash\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma_{0}\backslash{\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
.

The next result shows that these actions do not commute, and the flows
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
and
((\Gamma/\Gamma_{0})\backslash\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma_{0}\backslash{\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
are in general not conjugated as they can have different topological entropy.

Theorem D
.

Let
T_{4}
be the regular tree of valency
4
and let
\Gamma_{0}
be the free group on two generators with its standard action on
T_{4}
. Then there exists a discrete, cocompact group
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}(T_{4})
containing
\Gamma_{0}
as finite index normal subgroup such that

(i)

h_{\textup{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{4}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=\log 3
;

(ii)

h_{\textup{top}}((\Gamma/\Gamma_{0})\backslash\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma_{0}\backslash T_{4}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=0
.

Another interesting phenomenon is the following.

Theorem E
.

There exists a proper, geodesic, line-convex, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space
{\rm X}
with bounded geometry and a discrete, torsion-free, cocompact group
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
such that:

(i)

\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is a locally
\textup{CAT}(-1)
, locally geodesically complete, compact metric space. However,
{\rm X}
is not simply connected, so it is not the universal cover of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
, and
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
is a proper subset of
\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X})
;

(ii)

it holds that

h_{\textup{top}}(\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}),\Phi_{1})=h_{\textup{crit}}(\tilde{{\rm X}};\pi_{1}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}))>h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)=h_{\textup{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1}),

where
\tilde{{\rm X}}
is the universal cover of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
.

A last comment on the second part of the original version of Otal-Peigné’s Theorem for manifolds, that states that there is at most one measure of maximal entropy, i.e. realizing the supremum in (
2
). This is not true in general for proper, Gromov-hyperbolic spaces that are line-convex.

Example 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
be discrete, cocompact and torsion-free, so
M=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2}
is a compact hyperbolic surface. Let
Q
be any compact geodesic metric space and let
{\rm X}:=\mathbb{H}^{2}\times Q
equipped with the product metric. Then
{\rm X}
is proper and geodesic. It is also Gromov-hyperbolic since it is at bounded distance from
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Since every geodesic on
{\rm X}
is the product of geodesics of the factors (cp.
[
BH13
, Proposition I.5.13]
), we have that every geodesic line of
{\rm X}
is of the form
t\mapsto(\gamma(t),q)
where
\gamma
is a geodesic line in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
q\in Q
, i.e.
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})=\textup{Geod}(\mathbb{H}^{2})\times Q
. This shows that
{\rm X}
is line-convex. Observe that
\Gamma
acts discretely, cocompactly and freely on
{\rm X}
by acting on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
as above and trivially on
Q
. Theorem
A
, that we can apply by Corollary
2.20
, implies that
h_{\textup{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)=h_{\textup{crit}}(\mathbb{H}^{2};\Gamma)=1
. Let
\mu
be the unique measure of maximal entropy of
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
provided by Otal-Peigné’s Theorem for manifolds. Observe that
h_{\mu}=h_{\textup{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}(\mathbb{H}^{2}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=h_{\textup{crit}}(\mathbb{H}^{2};\Gamma)=1
. For every
q\in Q
let
\delta_{q}
be the Dirac measure at
q
and define
\nu_{q}:=\mu\otimes\delta_{q}
on
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})=\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}(\mathbb{H}^{2})\times Q
. A direct computation shows that
\nu_{q}
is
\bar{\Phi}_{1}
-invariant and that
h_{\nu_{q}}\geq 1
, implying that
\nu_{q}
realizes
h_{\textup{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
for every
q\in Q
. Observe that these measures are concentrated on different subsets, that are necessarily disjoint since every such measure is ergodic.

1.1.
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
we will recall the basic definitions and we will discuss and compare in detail several notions of lower bounds on the curvature for arbitrary metric spaces and for Gromov-hyperbolic ones. The goal is to give a better intuition of the ergodic entropy appearing in Theorem
A
.

In Section
3
we will recall basic properties of the space of geodesic lines of a metric space and we will introduce the geodesic flow. In Section
4
we will discuss several notions of entropies of a dynamical system, with applications to the case of the geodesic flow.
Section
5
is the last preparatory part: here we introduce the notion of
f
-entropy of a subset of the boundary at infinity of a Gromov-hyperbolic space and we compare it to other classical quantities. This section completes the study of Section
2.3
.

Finally, in the last three sections we will prove the main theorems.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin by recalling some notations. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space. The
open (resp.closed) ball
of radius
r
and center
x\in X
is denoted by
B(x,r)
(resp.
\overline{B}(x,r)
). If we want to specify the metric we write
B_{\sf d}(x,r)
(resp.
\overline{B}_{\sf d}(x,r))
. A metric space is
proper
if every closed ball is compact.

A
geodesic
is an isometric embedding
\gamma\colon I\to{\rm X}
where
I=[a,b]
is a a bounded interval of
\mathbb{R}
. The points
\gamma(a),\gamma(b)
are called the endpoints of
\gamma
. A metric space
{\rm X}
is called geodesic if for every couple of points
x,y\in{\rm X}
there exists a geodesic whose endpoints are
x
and
y
. Every such geodesic will be denoted, with an abuse of notation, by
[x,y]
. A
geodesic ray
is an isometric embedding
\xi\colon[0,+\infty)\to{\rm X}
while a
geodesic line
is an isometric embedding
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to{\rm X}
.
A map
\gamma\colon I\to{\rm X}
, where
I
is an interval of
\mathbb{R}
, is a
local geodesic
if for every
t\in I
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\gamma|_{[t-\varepsilon,t+\varepsilon]}
is a geodesic. The space of local geodesic lines, namely the set of local geodesics of
{\rm X}
defined over
\mathbb{R}
, is denoted by
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
, while its subspace of geodesic lines is denoted by
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
.

The
evaluation map at time
s\in\mathbb{R}
is the function
e_{s}\colon\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})\to{\rm X}
,
\gamma\mapsto\gamma(s)
.

A metric space is
geodesically complete
if every geodesic can be extended to a geodesic line. It is
locally geodesically complete
if every local geodesic can be extended to a local geodesic line. A metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is
line-convex
if for every two geodesic lines
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}\to{\rm X}
, the function
t\mapsto{\sf d}(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))
is convex.

Let
{\rm X}
be a metric space,
B\subseteq{\rm X}
and
r>0
. A subset
S
of
B
is called
r
-separated
if
{\sf d}(y,y^{\prime})>r
for all
y,y^{\prime}\in S
, while it is called
r
-dense
if for all
y\in B
there exists
z\in S
such that
{\sf d}(y,z)\leq r
.
We define
\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}(B,r)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
as the maximal cardinality of a
r
-separated subset of
B
and
\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(B,r)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
as the minimal cardinality of a
r
-dense subset. When the metric
{\sf d}
is clear from the context, we just omit it. The two quantities above are classically related, more precisely:

(5)

\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}(B,2r)\leq\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(B,2r)\leq\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}(B,r)

for every
B\subseteq{\rm X}
and every
r>0
.

2.1.
Bounded geometry, metric doubling, covering entropy and geodesic entropy

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper metric space. There are several notions that can be used to control the geometry of
{\rm X}
. In (
4
) we introduced the notion of
bounded geometry
, where we ask that

(6)

\sup_{x\in{\rm X}}\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,R),r)=:C_{R,r}<+\infty\quad\text{for every }0<r\leq R.

A related notion is the metric doubling condition. We say that
{\rm X}
is
metrically doubling up to scale
r_{0}
if there exists
C_{D}\geq 1
such that
\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,2r),r)\leq C_{D}
for every
x\in{\rm X}
and every
0<r\leq r_{0}
. Here the choice of the packing function instead of the covering one is not important in view of (
5
): it has been done to precisely cite the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic metric space that is metrically doubling up to scale
r_{0}
with constant
C_{D}
. Then for every
x\in{\rm X}
and every
0<r\leq R
it holds that

\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,R),r)\leq C_{D}^{2}(1+C_{D}^{2})^{\frac{R}{\min\{r,r_{0}\}}-1}.

In particular
{\rm X}
has bounded geometry.

Proof.

A space as in the assumptions satisfies
\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,3r),r)\leq C_{D}^{2}
for every
x\in{\rm X}
and every
r\leq\frac{r_{0}}{2}
. Therefore the thesis follows by
[
CS21
, Lemma 4.7]
.
∎

Another possible bound on the geometric complexity of
{\rm X}
is via its
covering entropy
. It is defined as

h_{\textup{Cov}}({\rm X}):=\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T),r).

This quantity does not depend on the choice of the basepoint
x\in{\rm X}
. Moreover one can replace
\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T),r)
with
\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T),r)
without changing the value of the limit, because of (
5
). Every space which is metrically doubling up to some scale has finite covering entropy.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic metric space with bounded geometry. Then the function

r\mapsto\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T),r)

is constant. Moreover, if
{\rm X}
is metrically doubling up to scale
r_{0}
with constant
C_{D}
then
h_{\textup{Cov}}({\rm X})\leq\frac{\log(1+C_{D}^{2})}{r_{0}}<+\infty
.

Proof.

The function
r\mapsto\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T),r)
is decreasing, se we just need to check one inequality. Let
0<r\leq R
. Let
C_{R,r}<+\infty
be the constant in (
6
). Then

\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T),r)\leq\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T),R)\cdot C_{R,r}

for every
T>0
. This implies the first part of the thesis. As a consequence we have that
h_{\textup{Cov}}({\rm X})\leq\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log C_{T,r}
for every
r>0
. The right hand side may be finite or not, depending on the behaviour of the function
T\mapsto C_{T,r}
. Now, if in addition
{\rm X}
is metrically doubling up to scale
r_{0}
with constant
C_{D}
, then
C_{T,2r_{0}}\leq C_{D}^{2}(1+C_{D}^{2})^{\frac{T}{r_{0}}-1}
because of Lemma
2.1
and (
5
). This gives the second part of the statement.
∎

The covering entropy can be finite also for spaces that have no bounded geometry.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\{d_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of integers. Let
{\rm X}
be the space obtained by taking
\mathbb{R}
and gluing
d_{n}
segments of length
1
at the point
n\in\mathbb{N}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. For every fixed
r>0
we need at most
(n+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}d_{j})\cdot\frac{1}{r}
points to cover the ball
\overline{B}(0,n)
. Choosing for instance
d_{n}=n
we obtain that
h_{\text{Cov}}({\rm X})=0
. On the other hand, the same choice of
d_{n}
implies that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(n,1),\frac{1}{4})=+\infty
.

One can weaken the condition
h_{\text{Cov}}({\rm X})<+\infty
using the
geodesic covering entropy
of
{\rm X}
. Its definition is similar to the one of the covering entropy, but we ask to cover only the points of
\overline{B}(x,T)
that lie in some geodesic line. More precisely,

h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X}):=\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T)\cap e_{0}(\textup{Geod}({\rm X})),r).

By definition,
h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X})\leq h_{\textup{Cov}}({\rm X})
. The inequality may be strict.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\{d_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of integers. Let
{\rm X}
be the space obtained by taking
\mathbb{R}
and gluing
d_{n}
segments of length
1
at the point
n\in\mathbb{N}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.
If
r\leq\frac{1}{2}
then
\textup{Cov}(\overline{B}(0,n),r)\geq n+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}d_{j}
. So
h_{\textup{Cov}}({\rm X})\geq\varlimsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\log(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}d_{j})}{n}
. By choosing the sequence
\{d_{n}\}
appropriately we can get any value in
[0,+\infty]
. On the other hand, every geodesic line of
{\rm X}
must live in the copy of
\mathbb{R}
inside
{\rm X}
. So,
h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X})=0
for every choice of
\{d_{n}\}
. Observe that
{\rm X}
is line-convex.

Other bounds on the geometric complexity will be presented in Section
2.3
in case of Gromov-hyperbolic spaces. They would resemble the bounds we gave in this section.

The condition appearing in Theorem
A
is weaker than all the conditions we discussed in this section.

2.2.
Gromov hyperbolic spaces

References for this subsection are
[
CDP90
,
BH13
]
.
Let
X
be a metric space and let
x,y,z\in{\rm X}
. The
Gromov product
of
y
and
z
with respect to
x
is

(y,z)_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\big(d(x,y)+d(x,z)-d(y,z)\big).

{\rm X}
is called
\delta
-hyperbolic
if for every
x,y,z,w\in{\rm X}
the following
4-points condition
hold:

(7)

(x,z)_{w}\geq\min\{(x,y)_{w},(y,z)_{w}\}-\delta

or, equivalently,

(8)

d(x,y)+d(z,w)\leq\max\{d(x,z)+d(y,w),d(x,w)+d(y,z)\}+2\delta.

{\rm X}
is
Gromov hyperbolic
if it is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta\geq 0
. Gromov-hyperbolicity should be considered as a negative-curvature condition at large scale: for instance every CAT
(\kappa)
metric space, with
\kappa<0
is
\delta
-hyperbolic for a constant
\delta
depending only on
\kappa
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space and let
x
be a point of
{\rm X}
. The
Gromov boundary
of
{\rm X}
is defined as the quotient

\partial{\rm X}:=\left\{(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq{\rm X}\,:\,\lim_{n,m\to+\infty}(z_{n},z_{m})_{x}=+\infty\right\}\hskip 2.84526pt/_{\sim},

where
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of points in
{\rm X}
and
\sim
is the equivalence relation defined by
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sim(z_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
if and only if
\lim_{n,m\to+\infty}(z_{n},z_{m}^{\prime})_{x}=+\infty
.
We will write
z:=[(z_{n})]\in\partial{\rm X}
for short, and we say that
(z_{n})

converges
to
z
. This definition does not depend on the basepoint.

There is a natural topology on
{\rm X}\cup\partial{\rm X}
that extends the metric topology of
{\rm X}
.

Every geodesic ray
\xi
defines a point
\xi^{+}:=[(\xi(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}]
of the Gromov boundary
\partial{\rm X}
: we say that
\xi

joins

\xi(0)

to

\xi^{+}
. Moreover for every
z\in\partial{\rm X}
and every
x\in{\rm X}
it is possible to find a geodesic ray
\xi
such that
\xi(0)=x
and
\xi^{+}=z
. Indeed if
(z_{n})
is a sequence of points converging to
z
then, by properness of
{\rm X}
, a sequence of geodesics
[x,z_{n}]
subconverges to a geodesic ray
\xi
which has the properties above (cp.
[
BH13
, Lemma III.3.13]
). A geodesic ray joining
x
to
z\in\partial{\rm X}
will be denoted by
\xi_{x,z}
.

The relation between Gromov product and geodesic rays is highlighted in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
Cav23
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper,
\delta
-hyperbolic metric space,
z,z^{\prime}\in\partial{\rm X}
,
x\in{\rm X}
and
b>0
. Then for all geodesic rays
\xi_{x,z},\xi_{x,z^{\prime}}
it holds that

(i)

if
(z,z^{\prime})_{x}\geq T
then
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z}(T-\delta),\xi_{x,z^{\prime}}(T-\delta))\leq 4\delta
;

(ii)

if
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z}(T),\xi_{x,z^{\prime}}(T))<2b
then
(z,z^{\prime})_{x}>T-b
.

The following is a standard computation, see for instance
[
BCGS17
, Proposition 8.10]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper,
\delta
-hyperbolic metric space. Let
\xi,\xi^{\prime}
be two geodesic rays with
\xi^{+}=\xi^{\prime+}
. Then there exist
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
such that
t_{1}+t_{2}={\sf d}(\xi(0),\xi^{\prime}(0))
and
{\sf d}(\xi(t+t_{1}),\xi^{\prime}(t+t_{2}))\leq 8\delta
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Given a subset
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
, we define the
quasi-convex hull
of
C
, and we denote it by
\textup{QC-Hull}(C)\subseteq{\rm X}
, as the union of all geodesic lines with endpoints in
C
. We will need the following basic, but important, facts.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
Cav22
, Lemma 5.4]
)
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic,
\delta
-hyperbolic space.
Let
x\in{\rm X}
and
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
be a subset with
\#C\geq 2
. Then for every
z\in C
there exists a geodesic line
\gamma
with endpoints in
C
such that for every geodesic ray
\xi_{x,z}
it holds that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z}(t),\gamma(t))\leq 22\delta+{\sf d}(x,\textup{QC-Hull}(C))
for every
t\geq 0
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic,
\delta
-hyperbolic space and let
x\in{\rm X}
. Let
\gamma
be a geodesic line of
{\rm X}
parametrized in such a way that
\gamma(0)
is a point of
\gamma
which is closest to
x
. Let
\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}
be a geodesic ray. For every
t\geq 0
let
y_{t}
be the unique point on
\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}
such that
{\sf d}(x,y_{t})={\sf d}(x,\gamma(t))
. Then
{\sf d}(\gamma(t),y_{t})\leq 144\delta
.

Proof.

Let
t\geq 0
. By
[
Cav22
, Lemma 5.2]
we can find a point
y^{\prime}
on
\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}
such that
{\sf d}(\gamma(t),y^{\prime})\leq 72\delta
, and so
{\sf d}(x,\gamma(t))-72\delta\leq{\sf d}(x,y^{\prime})\leq{\sf d}(x,\gamma(t))+72\delta
. Then
{\sf d}(y_{t},y^{\prime})\leq 72\delta
, so
{\sf d}(\gamma(t),y_{t})\leq 144\delta
.
∎

Let
x\in{\rm X}
be a basepoint.
As in
[
Pau96
]
and
[
Cav23
]
we define the
generalized visual ball of center
z\in\partial{\rm X}
and radius
\rho\geq 0
as

B_{x}(z,\rho)=\bigg\{z^{\prime}\in\partial{\rm X}\,:\,(z,z^{\prime})_{x}>\log\frac{1}{\rho}\bigg\}.

Generalized visual balls are related to shadows. The
shadow of radius
r>0
casted by a point
y\in X
with center
x
is the set

\text{Shad}_{x}(y,r)=\{z\in\partial{\rm X}\,:\,\xi_{x,z}\cap B(y,r)\neq\emptyset\text{ for every geodesic ray }\xi_{x,z}\}.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
Cav23
, Lemma 4.8]
)
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper,
\delta
-hyperbolic metric space. Let
z\in\partial{\rm X}
,
x\in{\rm X}
and
T\geq 0
. Then for every
r>0
it holds that

\textup{Shad}_{x}\left(\xi_{x,z}\left(T\right),r\right)\subseteq B_{x}(z,e^{-T+r}).

Using generalized visual balls one can define the classical fractal dimensions, like the Hausdorff dimension, etc. of a subset of
\partial{\rm X}
. For instance, the lower and upper Minkowski dimension of a subset
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
are respectively

\underline{\text{MD}}(C):=\varliminf_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\log\text{Cov}_{x}(C,\rho)}{\log\frac{1}{\rho}},\qquad\overline{\text{MD}}(C):=\varlimsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\log\text{Cov}_{x}(C,\rho)}{\log\frac{1}{\rho}},

where
\text{Cov}_{x}(C,\rho)
denotes the minimal number of generalized visual balls of radius
\rho
needed to cover
C
. These quantities do not depend on the choice of the basepoint
x\in{\rm X}
.

2.3.
Geodesic entropy of subsets of the boundary

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space and let
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
. For
B\subseteq{\rm X}
,
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
and
r>0
we define

\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(B;C;r):=\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(B\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C),r).

It represents the minimal number of
r
-balls needed to cover the points of
B
that lie inside some geodesic lines with endpoints in
C
.

We introduce the adaptation of the geodesic covering entropy introduced in Section
2.1
to subsets of the boundary at infinity. The
geodesic covering entropy of
{\rm X}
with respect to
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
is the quantity

(9)

h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X};C):=\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T);C;r).

A similar definition has been considered in
[
Cav22
]
, under the name of covering entropy of
C
. In order to avoid confusion we prefer to call it geodesic covering entropy since it refers to points belonging to geodesic lines.

If
C=\partial{\rm X}
then
h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X};C)=h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X})
as defined in Section
2.1
.

The geodesic covering entropy of
{\rm X}
with respect to
C
bounds from above the Minkowski dimension.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space and let
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
. Then
\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)\leq h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X};C)
.

Proof.

If
\#C<2
then
\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)=0
and there is nothing to prove. So we can assume that
\#C\geq 2
.
Let
\delta
be the hyperbolicity constant of
{\rm X}
.
Fix
x\in{\rm X}
and let
M:={\sf d}(x,\text{QC-Hull}(C))
. We will show a stronger statement that will be useful also later. Namely, we show that

(10)

\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(e_{T}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,M);C)),r),

where
\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,M);C)
denotes the set of geodesic lines
\gamma
with both endpoints in
C
and with
\gamma(0)\in\overline{B}(x,M)
. Since
e_{T}
is the evaluation map at time
T
, every point of
e_{T}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,M);C))
belongs to
\overline{B}(x,M+T)\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C)
, so (
10
) implies the thesis.

We now prove (
10
). For every
T>M
let
\{y_{i}\}
be a
1
-dense subset of
e_{T}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,M);C))
. For every
i
let
\gamma_{i}\in\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,M),C)
such that
\gamma_{i}(T)=y_{i}
. Let
z\in C
. By Lemma
2.7
there exists a geodesic line
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,M);C)
with
\gamma^{+}=z
and
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z}(t),\gamma(t))\leq M
for every
t\geq 0
. Then there exists
i
such that
{\sf d}(\gamma(T),y_{i})\leq 1
. Moreover, since
T>M
we can apply Lemma
2.8
to find that
{\sf d}(y_{i},\xi_{x,\gamma_{i}^{+}}(S))\leq 144\delta
, where
S={\sf d}(x,y_{i})
satisfies
|S-T|\leq M
. The triangular inequality implies that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z}(T),\xi_{x,\gamma_{i}^{+}}(T))\leq 2M+1+144\delta
. Then
(z,\gamma_{i}^{+})_{x}>T-M-\frac{1}{2}-72\delta
by Lemma
2.5
. Defining
\rho:=e^{-T}
we get that
\textup{Cov}_{x}(C,e^{M+\frac{1}{2}+72\delta}\rho)\leq\#\{\gamma_{i}^{+}\}
. Hence,

(11)

\displaystyle\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)=\varlimsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\log\textup{Cov}_{x}(C,\rho)}{\log\frac{1}{\rho}}

\displaystyle=\varlimsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\log\textup{Cov}_{x}(C,e^{M+\frac{1}{2}+72\delta}\rho)}{\log\frac{1}{\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(e_{T}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,M);C)),1)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(e_{T}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,M);C)),r).

∎

We say that
{\rm X}
has
bounded geometry with respect to
C
if

(12)

\sup_{y\in\textup{QC-Hull}(C)}\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(y,R);C;r)=:C_{R,r}<+\infty\quad\text{for all }0<r\leq R.

Even when
C=\partial{\rm X}
, condition (
12
) is weaker than (
6
). Indeed the supremum is only among points that are along geodesic lines, actually along geodesic lines with endpoints in
C
. Moreover, also the set to cover is not the whole ball
\overline{B}(x,R)
, but only its intersection with the set of geodesic lines with endpoints in
C
.
The following result is similar to Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space, let
x\in{\rm X}
and let
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
. Suppose
{\rm X}
has bounded geometry with respect to
C
. Then the function

r\mapsto\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T);C;r)

is constant.

Proof.

The function
r\mapsto\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T);C;r)
is decreasing. For the other direction we fix
0<r\leq R
and we consider the constant
C_{R,r}
given by (
12
). Then

(13)

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T);C;r)

\displaystyle\leq\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\left(\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T);C,R)\cdot C_{R,r}\right)

\displaystyle=\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T);C;R).

∎

As a consequence, if (
12
) holds then we have equality in Lemma
2.10
. This is a result that extends
[
Cav22
, Theorem D]
.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space and let
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
be such that
\#C\geq 2
. Suppose
{\rm X}
has bounded geometry with respect to
C
. Then
h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X};C)=\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)
.

Proof.

Proposition
2.10
gives that
\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)\leq h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X};C)
, so we need to show the other inequality. Let
\delta
be the hyperbolicity constant of
{\rm X}
, let
x\in{\rm X}
and let
M:={\sf d}(x,\text{QC-Hull}(C))+22\delta
. We claim that

(14)

h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X};C)=\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf d}}(\overline{B}(x,T);C;294\delta)=\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf d}}(S(x,T);C;294\delta),

where
S(x,T)
denotes the set of points at distance exactly
T
from
x
.

Let us suppose for the moment that (
14
) holds and we show how to conclude the proof. Let
T>M
be fixed and set
\rho:=e^{-T}
. Let
\{z_{i}\}\subseteq C
be a minimal
\rho
-dense subset of
C
, i.e. for every
z\in C
there exists
i
such that
(z,z_{i})_{x}\geq T
. Lemma
2.7
gives the existence of geodesic lines
\gamma_{i}
with endpoints in
C
,
\gamma_{i}^{+}=z_{i}
and
{\sf d}(x,\gamma_{i}(0))\leq M
. We consider a point
y_{i}
along
\gamma_{i}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,+\infty)}$}
such that
{\sf d}(x,y_{i})=T
, so
y_{i}\in S(x,T)\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C)
. We observe that Lemma
2.8
implies that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z_{i}}(T),y_{i})\leq 144\delta
for every geodesic ray
\xi_{x,z_{i}}
.

We claim that
\{y_{i}\}
is
294\delta
-dense in
S(x,T)\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C)
.
Let
y\in S(x,T)\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C)
, in particular it belongs to a geodesic line
\gamma
with endpoints in
C
. We parametrize
\gamma
in such a way that
\gamma(0)
is a closest point of
\gamma
to
x
and
y\in\gamma\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,+\infty)}$}

{\sf d}(y,\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}(T))\leq 144\delta
. The point
\gamma^{+}
belongs to
C
, then there exists
i
such that
(\gamma^{+},z_{i})_{x}\geq T
. Lemma
2.5
says that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z_{i}}(T),\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}(T))\leq 6\delta
. Therefore
{\sf d}(y,y_{i})\leq 294\delta
and the claim is proved. This implies that

(15)

\displaystyle\textup{Cov}_{{\sf d}}(S(x,T);C;294\delta)\leq\#\{y_{i}\}=\#\{z_{i}\}=\textup{Cov}_{x}(C,\rho),

leading to

(16)

\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf d}}(S(x,T);C;294\delta)\leq\varlimsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\log\textup{Cov}_{x}(C,\rho)}{\log\frac{1}{\rho}}=\overline{\textup{MD}}(C).

. Moreover it is obvious that

\overline{B}(x,T)\subseteq\bigcup_{k=0}^{\lfloor T\rfloor}A(x,k,k+1)
, where

\textup{Cov}_{{\sf d}}(\overline{B}(x,T);C;294\delta)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor T\rfloor}\textup{Cov}_{{\sf d}}(A(x,k,k+1);C;294\delta).

It is therefore enough to prove that
\textup{Cov}_{{\sf d}}(A(x,k,k+1);C;294\delta)\leq\textup{Cov}_{{\sf d}}(S(x,T);C;294\delta)
for every
k=M,\ldots,\lfloor T-291\delta-1\rfloor
.

We fix such a

. Let
\{y_{i}\}
be a
294\delta
-dense subset of

. For every
i
we take a geodesic line
\gamma_{i}
with endpoints in
C
containing
y_{i}
. We parametrize
\gamma_{i}
in such a way that
\gamma_{i}(0)
is a closest point of
\gamma_{i}
to
x
and
y_{i}
belongs to
\gamma_{i}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,+\infty)}$}
. We consider a geodesic ray
\xi_{x,\gamma_{i}^{+}}
and we apply Lemma
2.7
in order to find another geodesic line
\gamma_{i}^{\prime}
with endpoints in
C
,
\gamma_{i}^{\prime+}=\gamma_{i}^{+}
and parametrized in such a way that
\gamma_{i}^{\prime}(0)
is a closest point of
\gamma_{i}^{\prime}
to
x
and
{\sf d}(x,\gamma_{i}^{\prime}(0))\leq M
. We take a point
x_{i}
that belongs to
\gamma_{i}^{\prime}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,+\infty)}$}
and that satisfies
{\sf d}(x,x_{i})\in[k,k+1]
. This is possible since
k\geq M
and
{\sf d}(x,\gamma_{i}^{\prime}(0))\leq M
.

We claim that the set
\{x_{i}\}\in\text{QC-Hull}(C)\cap A(x,k,k+1)
is
294\delta
-dense. Let
y\in\text{QC-Hull}(C)\cap A(x,k,k+1)
, let
\gamma
be a geodesic line with endpoints in
C
containing
y
and parametrized in such a way that
\gamma(0)
is a closest point of
\gamma
to
x
and
x\in\gamma\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,+\infty)}$}
. Let
y_{T}
be a point of
\gamma\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,+\infty)}$}
belonging to
S(x,T)
. Then there exists
i
such that
{\sf d}(y_{T},y_{i})\leq 294\delta
. We consider geodesic rays
\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}
and
\xi_{x,\gamma_{i}^{+}}
. Lemma
2.8
implies that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}(T),\xi_{x,\gamma_{i}^{+}}(T))\leq 582\delta
. This means, by Lemma
2.5
, that
(\gamma_{i}^{+},\gamma^{+})_{x}\geq T-291\delta
. Therefore
(\gamma_{i}^{\prime+},\gamma^{+})_{x}\geq T-291\delta
, since
\gamma_{i}^{\prime+}=\gamma_{i}^{+}
. Again by Lemma
2.5
we get that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}(t),\xi_{x,\gamma_{i}^{\prime+}}(t))\leq 6\delta
for every
t\in[0,T-291\delta]
. Another double application of Lemma
2.8
gives that
{\sf d}(x_{i},y)\leq 294\delta
, where we used the fact that both
{\sf d}(x,x_{i})
and
{\sf d}(x,y)
are at most
k+1\leq T-291\delta
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We end this section with another property implied by the bounded geometry assumption.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space, let
x\in{\rm X}
and let
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
. Suppose
{\rm X}
has bounded geometry with respect to
C
. Then
C
is doubling in the following sense: there exists
C_{D}>0
such that

(17)

\textup{Cov}_{x}\left(B_{x}(z,\rho),\frac{\rho}{2}\right)\leq C_{D}

for every
z\in C
, every
\rho>0
. In particular,
\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)<+\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\delta
be the hyperbolicity constant of
{\rm X}
. Let
z\in C
and
\rho>0
. Set
T:=\log\frac{1}{\rho}
.
Let
S:=72\delta+1+\log 2
and let
R:=S+6\delta
. Let
\{x_{i}\}
be a
1
-dense subset of
\overline{B}(\xi_{x,z}(T),R)\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C)
of cardinality at most
C_{D}:=C_{R,1}
, where the right hand side comes directly from (
12
). For every
i
let
\gamma_{i}
be a geodesic line with endpoints in
C
and parametrized in such a way that
\gamma_{i}(0)
is a closest point of
\gamma_{i}
to
x
and
x_{i}\in\gamma_{i}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,+\infty)}$}
. We claim that the set
\{\gamma_{i}^{+}\}
is
\frac{\rho}{2}
-dense in
B_{x}(z,\rho)
, i.e. for every
w\in B_{x}(z,\rho)
there exists
i
such that
(w,\gamma_{i}^{+})_{x}>\log\frac{2}{\rho}=T+\log 2
. Let
w\in B_{x}(z,\rho)
. By Lemma
2.5
we have that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z}(T),\xi_{x,w}(T))\leq 6\delta
, so the point
\xi_{x,w}(T+S)
belongs to
\overline{B}(\xi_{x,z}(T),R)
. Therefore there exists
i
such that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,w}(T+S),x_{i})\leq 1
. Lemma
2.8
implies that
{\sf d}(x_{i},\xi_{x,\gamma_{i}^{+}}(t))\leq 144\delta
where
t
is such that
{\sf d}(x,\xi_{x,\gamma_{i}^{+}}(t))={\sf d}(x,x_{i})
. Since
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,w}(T+S),x_{i})\leq 1
we obtain that
{\sf d}(x_{i},\xi_{x,\gamma_{i}^{+}}(T+S))\leq 144\delta+1
. This means that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,\gamma_{i}^{+}}(T+S),\xi_{x,w}(T+S))\leq 144\delta+2
. Lemma
2.5
says that
(\gamma_{i}^{+},w)_{x}>T+S-72\delta-1=T+\log 2=\log\frac{2}{\rho}
, i.e.
w\in B_{x}(\gamma_{i}^{+},\frac{\rho}{2})
.

For the second part we argue as follows. The diameter of
\partial{\rm X}
is at most
1
, so it is for
C
. In other words,
C\subseteq B(z,1)
for every
z\in C
. For every
k>0
, by iterating (
17
), we obtain that
\textup{Cov}_{x}(C,\frac{1}{2^{k}})\leq C_{D}^{k}
. Similarly, for every
\rho>0
we have that
\textup{Cov}_{x}(C,\rho)\leq C_{D}^{\log_{2}\frac{1}{\rho}}
.
This implies that

\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)\leq\varlimsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\log_{2}\frac{1}{\rho}\cdot\log C_{D}}{\log\frac{1}{\rho}}=\frac{\log C_{D}}{\log 2}<+\infty.

∎

2.4.
Groups of isometries and Bishop-Jones Theorem

If
{\rm X}
is a proper metric space we denote its group of isometries by
\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
and we endow it with the uniform convergence on compact subsets of
{\rm X}
. A subgroup
\Gamma
of
\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
is called
discrete
if the following equivalent conditions hold:

(a)

\Gamma
is discrete as a subspace of
\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
;

(b)

for all
x\in{\rm X}
and
R\geq 0
the set
\Sigma_{R}(x)=\{g\in\Gamma\,:\,gx\in\overline{B}(x,R)\}
is finite.

The critical exponent of a discrete group of isometries
\Gamma
acting on a proper metric space
{\rm X}
can be defined using the Poincaré series, or alternatively (
[
Cav23
]
,
[
Coo93
]
), as

(18)

h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)=\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\#(\Gamma x\cap B(x,T)),

where
x
is a fixed point of
{\rm X}
. This quantity does not depend on the choice of
x
.

We specialize the situation to the case of a proper, Gromov hyperbolic metric space
{\rm X}
. Every isometry of
{\rm X}
acts naturally on
\partial{\rm X}
and the resulting map on
{\rm X}\cup\partial{\rm X}
is a homeomorphism.
The
limit set

\Lambda(\Gamma)
of a discrete group of isometries
\Gamma
is the set of accumulation points of the orbit
\Gamma x
on
\partial{\rm X}
, where
x
is any point of
{\rm X}
; it is the smallest
\Gamma
-invariant closed set of the Gromov boundary (cp.
[
Coo93
, Theorem 5.1]
) and it does not depend on
x
. The group
\Gamma
is said to be
non-elementary
if
\#\Lambda(\Gamma)>2
. If
{\rm X}
is Gromov-hyperbolic and
\Gamma
is non-elementary then the limit superior in (
1
) is a true limit, see
[
Cav24
, Theorem B]
.

There are several interesting subsets of the limit set like the radial limit set, the uniformly radial limit set and the ergodic limit set. The last one has been defined in
[
Cav24
]
. They are related to important sets of the quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
.

In order to recall their definition, we fix a basepoint
x\in{\rm X}
, a positive real number
\tau>0
and an increasing sequence of real numbers
\Theta=\{\vartheta_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{i\to+\infty}\vartheta_{i}=+\infty
. We define

\Lambda_{\tau,\Theta}(\Gamma)
as the set of points
z\in\partial{\rm X}
for which there exists a geodesic ray
\xi_{x,z}
satisfying the following property: for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
t_{i}\in[\vartheta_{i},\vartheta_{i+1}]
such that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z}(t_{i}),\Gamma x)\leq\tau
.

By choosing different classes of sequences
\Theta
, we define the different sets we recalled above. The
radial limit set
is

\Lambda_{\text{rad}}(\Gamma):=\bigcup_{\tau>0}\bigcup_{\Theta}\Lambda_{\tau,\Theta},

where the second union is taken among all possible sequences
\Theta
as above. If we restrict the class of sequences we consider, we define the other sets. For
\tau>0
we define
\Lambda_{\tau}(\Gamma):=\Lambda_{\tau,\{i\tau\}}(\Gamma)
. The
uniform radial limit set
of
\Gamma
is

(19)

\Lambda_{\text{u-rad}}(\Gamma):=\bigcup_{\tau>0}\Lambda_{\tau}(\Gamma).

We define
\Theta_{\text{erg}}
as the set of increasing sequences of positive real numbers
\Theta=\{\vartheta_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\exists\lim_{i\to+\infty}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{i}\in(0,+\infty)
. The
ergodic limit set
of
\Gamma
is the set

(20)

\Lambda_{\text{erg}}(\Gamma):=\bigcup_{\tau>0}\bigcup_{\Theta\in\Theta_{\text{erg}}}\Lambda_{\tau,\Theta}(\Gamma).

The ergodic limit set plays an important role in relation with the quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
, as
[
Cav24
, Theorem C]
shows. In Section
7
we will expand this relation.

In the sequel we will need other subsets. Let
c\in(0,+\infty)
,
\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We define

\Theta_{c,\varepsilon,n}:=\left\{\{\vartheta_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ increasing sequence of real numbers}\,:\,\left|\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{i}-c\right|<\varepsilon\text{ for all }i\geq n\right\}

and

\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}(\Gamma):=\bigcup_{\Theta\in\Theta_{c,\varepsilon,n}}\Lambda_{\tau,\Theta}(\Gamma).

Notice that a sequence
\{\vartheta_{i}\}\in\Theta_{c,\varepsilon,n}
satisfies
c-\varepsilon\leq\varliminf_{i\to+\infty}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{i}\leq\varlimsup_{i\to+\infty}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{i}\leq c+\varepsilon
.

When
\Gamma
is clear from the context, we simply omit it from the notation and we write
\Lambda,\Lambda_{\text{rad}},\Lambda_{\text{u-rad}},\Lambda_{\text{erg}},\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}
. The sets
\Lambda,\Lambda_{\text{rad}},\Lambda_{\text{u-rad}}
and
\Lambda_{\text{erg}}
are
\Gamma
-invariant (
[
Cav24
, Lemma 4.2]
), while the sets
\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}
are not in general.

In the proof of Theorem
C
we will use following version of Bishop-Jones’ Theorem that has been proved in the remark after the proof of
[
Cav24
, Theorem B]
.

Theorem 2.14

(
[
BJ97
,
Pau97
,
DSU17
,
Cav24
]
)
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete and non-elementary. Then

(21)

\displaystyle h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)=\sup_{\tau\geq 0}\underline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda_{\tau}).

One of the key steps in the proof of Theorem
A
will be to show that every ergodic measure of the quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is concentrated on the set of geodesics with endpoints in
\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}
for some choices of the parameters.
For the proof of one inequality in Theorem
A
we will need
the next estimate, whose proof follows the same lines of
[
Cav24
, Theorem B]
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space, let
x\in{\rm X}
and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete. For every
\tau,c,\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

\overline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n})\leq h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)\cdot\frac{c+\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
\delta
be the hyperbolicity constant of
{\rm X}
.
For simplicity, we set
h:=h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
.
We fix an error term
\eta>0
. Let
T_{\eta}>0
be such that

\frac{1}{T}\log\#(\Gamma x\cap B(x,T))\leq h+\eta

for every
T\geq T_{\eta}
.
Let
\rho\leq e^{-\max\{T_{\eta},n(c-\varepsilon)\}}
. We consider
j_{\rho}\in\mathbb{N}
with the following property:
(j_{\rho}-1)(c-\varepsilon)<\log\frac{1}{\rho}\leq j_{\rho}(c-\varepsilon)
. The condition on
\rho
gives
\log\frac{1}{\rho}\geq n(c-\varepsilon)
, implying
j_{\rho}\geq n
.

We consider the set of elements
g\in\Gamma
such that

(22)

j_{\rho}(c-\varepsilon)-\tau\leq{\sf d}(x,gx)\leq(j_{\rho}+1)(c+\varepsilon)+\tau.

For every such
g
we consider the shadow
\text{Shad}_{x}(gx,2\tau+8\delta)
and we claim that this set of shadows covers
\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}
.

Let
z\in\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}
. By definition,
z\in\Lambda_{\tau,\{\vartheta_{i}\}}
with the property that for every
i\geq n
it holds that
(c-\varepsilon)i\leq\vartheta_{i}\leq(c+\varepsilon)i
. In particular this holds for
j_{\rho}
, i.e.
(c-\varepsilon)j_{\rho}\leq\vartheta_{j_{\rho}}\leq(c+\varepsilon)j_{\rho}
.
Hence there exists a geodesic ray
\xi_{x,z}
and a time
t
satisfying

j_{\rho}(c-\varepsilon)\leq\vartheta_{j_{\rho}}\leq t\leq\vartheta_{j_{\rho}+1}\leq(j_{\rho}+1)(c+\varepsilon),\qquad d(\xi_{x,z}(t),\Gamma x)\leq\tau.

So there exists
g\in\Gamma
satisfying (
22
) such that
z\in\text{Shad}_{x}(gx,2\tau+8\delta)
, by Lemma
2.6
.
Moreover, all these shadows are casted by points at distance at least
j_{\rho}(c-\varepsilon)-\tau
from
x
, so at distance at least
\log\frac{1}{e^{\tau}\rho}
from
x
. We need to estimate the number of such
g
’s.
By the assumption on
\rho
we get that this number is less than or equal to
e^{(h+\eta)[(j_{\rho}+1)(c+\varepsilon)+\tau]}.

Hence, by Lemma
2.9
, we conclude that
\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}
is covered by at most
e^{(h+\eta)[(j_{\rho}+1)(c+\varepsilon)+\tau]}
generalized visual balls of radius
e^{3\tau+8\delta}\rho
. Thus,

\displaystyle\overline{\text{MD}}(\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n})

\displaystyle=\varlimsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\log\text{Cov}_{x}(\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n},e^{3\tau+8\delta}\rho)}{\log\frac{1}{e^{3\tau+8\delta}\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq\varlimsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{(h+\eta)[(j_{\rho}+1)(c+\varepsilon)+\tau]}{-3\tau-8\delta+(j_{\rho}-1)(c-\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\leq(h+\eta)\cdot\frac{c+\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}.

The thesis follows by the arbitrariness of
\eta>0
.
∎

2.5.
Ergodic entropy of a group of isometries

Let
{\rm X}
be a geodesic, proper, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space. Let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete. We define the
ergodic entropy
of
\Gamma
as

h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma):=h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\text{erg}}(\Gamma)).

This is the notion that appears in Theorem
A
. We observe the following property.

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete and non-elementary. Then
h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)\leq h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.14
we have that
h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)=\sup_{\tau\geq 0}\underline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda_{\tau})
. Proposition
2.10
implies that
\underline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda_{\tau})\leq h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\tau})
for every
\tau\geq 0
big enough. The thesis follows since for every such
\tau
we have that
\Lambda_{\tau}\subseteq\Lambda_{\text{erg}}
, so
\underline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda_{\tau})\leq h_{\textup{Geod}}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\text{erg}})
.
∎

In particular, under the hypotheses of Theorem
A
we have that
h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<+\infty
.

Remark 2.17
.

In this remark we explain one difference between the conditions
h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<+\infty
and
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<+\infty
. In the definition of
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
we take into account the covering at every scale
r>0
and then we consider the limit for
r
going to zero, while in the definition of
h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
we just count the spread of the orbit, without looking at a particular scale. When we want to define quantities related to the topological entropy of the geodesic flow we really need to consider every possible scale
r>0
. Indeed in the classical definition of topological entropy that we will see in Section
3
, every scale
r>0
is considered. The problem of the scales will be central in the proof of Theorem
A
, and in Section
7
we will see how the condition
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<+\infty
, together with the line-convexity, gives a solution to this problem. However, the dependence of
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
on every scale
r>0
is quite weak as the next corollary explains.

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space, let
x\in{\rm X}
and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete and non-elementary. Suppose
{\rm X}
has bounded geometry with respect to
\Lambda_{\textup{erg}}
. Then

(i)

the function
r\mapsto\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\sf d}(\overline{B}(x,T);\Lambda_{\textup{erg}};r)
is constant;

(ii)

h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)=\overline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda)<+\infty
.

Proof.

The first statement follows immediately from Lemma
2.11
. Moreover, Proposition
2.12
implies that
h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)=\overline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda_{\text{erg}})=\overline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda)
, where the last equality comes from the fact that
\Lambda_{\text{erg}}
is dense in
\Lambda
, being
\Gamma
-invariant. The finiteness of
h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
is then consequence of Proposition
2.13
.
∎

Remark 2.19
.

We can now see a second difference between
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
and
h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
. If
{\rm X}
is a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space with bounded geometry with respect to
\Lambda_{\textup{erg}}
then
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
coincides with the Minkowski dimension of the limit set
\Lambda
, or equivalently of the uniformly radial limit set
\Lambda_{\textup{u-rad}}
, by density. On the other hand the Bishop-Jones’ Theorem in the version of
[
DSU17
]
says that
h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
coincides with the Hausdorff dimension of
\Lambda_{\textup{u-rad}}
. Therefore the difference between the two quantities, at least under these assumptions, relies on the fractal properties of the set
\Lambda_{\textup{u-rad}}
.

Additionally we remark that there exist manifold examples where the two quantities above differ. For instance in
[
PS19
]
there is an example of a simply connected manifold
\tilde{M}
with pinched negative sectional curvature admitting a finite volume, discrete, torsion-free group of isometries
\Gamma
, but such that
h_{\textup{crit}}(\tilde{M};\Gamma)
is strictly smaller than the volume entropy of
\tilde{M}
. In this case
\Lambda(\Gamma)=\partial\tilde{M}
, so
[
Cav22
, Theorem B]
implies that the volume entropy of
\tilde{M}
coincides with
\overline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda)
.

For quasiconvex cocompact groups, i.e. groups
\Gamma
such that the quotient space
\Gamma\backslash\text{QC-Hull}(\Lambda(\Gamma))
is compact, we have equality between the ergodic entropy and the critical exponent.

Corollary 2.20
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete, non-elementary and quasiconvex cocompact. Then it holds that
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)=h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<+\infty
.

Proof.

By quasiconvex cocompactness we have that
h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<+\infty
by
[
Cav23
, Proposition 5.7]
and that
{\rm X}
has bounded geometry with respect to
\Lambda
by
[
Cav23
, Lemma 5.4]
.
By
[
DSU17
, Theorem 12.2.7]
we have that
\Lambda=\Lambda_{\textup{u-rad}}
.
The conclusion follows by the discussion in Remark
2.19
, since
\Lambda
is Ahlfors regular (cp.
[
Coo93
]
,
[
Cav23
]
) and so its Hausdorff and Minkowski dimensions coincide.
∎

3.
Geodesic flow

In this section we introduce the notion of geodesic flow on the quotient of a metric space by a group action. Before doing that we need to state some basic properties of the quotient metric space.

3.1.
Quotient metric space

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper metric space and
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete. We denote by
\pi\colon{\rm X}\to\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
the standard projection on the quotient space. On
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
it is defined the standard pseudometric
\bar{{\sf d}}([x],[y]):=\inf_{g\in\Gamma}{\sf d}(x,gy)
. Since the action is discrete then this pseudometric is actually a metric.
The map
\pi
is
1
-Lipschitz.

The
systole of
\Gamma
at a point
x\in{\rm X}
is defined by
\text{sys}(\Gamma,x):=\inf_{g\in\Gamma\setminus\{\text{id}\}}{\sf d}(x,gx)
. The map
x\mapsto\text{sys}(\Gamma,x)
is lower semicontinuous and
\Gamma
-invariant, so it defines a lower semicontinuous function on the metric space
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
. Moreover, if
\Gamma
acts freely, i.e. if
gx=x
for some
x\in{\rm X}
implies that
g=\text{id}
, then
\text{sys}(\Gamma,x)>0
for every
x\in{\rm X}
. As a consequence we have the following useful fact.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper metric space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete and free. For every compact set
K\subseteq\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
, there exists a positive constant
\sigma>0
such that the systole of
\Gamma
at every point of
K
is bigger than or equal to
\sigma
.

The following lemma is standard.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper metric space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete and free. Then

(i)

for all
x,y\in{\rm X}
such that
\bar{{\sf d}}([x],[y])<\frac{\textup{sys}(\Gamma,x)}{2}
there exists a unique
g\in\Gamma
such that
{\sf d}(x,gy)<\frac{\textup{sys}(\Gamma,x)}{2}
. In particular
{\sf d}(x,gy)=\bar{{\sf d}}([x],[y])
;

(ii)

if
{\rm X}
is a length space then
\pi
is a locally isometric covering.

3.2.
The space of local geodesics

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic metric space and let
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
be its space of globally defined local geodesics. We equip it with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. This is a metrizable topology. We use metrics that are slightly different to the ones introduced in
[
Cav22
]
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the class of continuous functions
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to(0,1]
satisfying

(a)

f(0)=1
;

(b)

f(s)=f(-s)
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
;

(c)

\varlimsup_{s\to+\infty}2|s|f(s)=0
.

The class
\mathcal{F}
is not empty, as it contains for instance the functions of the form
f_{a}(s):=e^{-a|s|}
, for every
a>0
. For every
f\in\mathcal{F}
, we define the quantity

(23)

{\sf D}_{f}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}):=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}{\sf d}(\gamma(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s))f(s),

where
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper metric space. Then

(i)

for every
\gamma\in\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
and for every
t<s
it holds
{\sf d}(\gamma(t),\gamma(s))\leq\ell(\gamma|_{[t,s]})=|s-t|
, where
\ell(\cdot)
denotes the length of a curve;

(ii)

for every
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
and for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
it holds that

{\sf D}_{f}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\leq{\sf d}(\gamma(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0))+\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}2|s|f(s).

Moreover,
{\sf D}_{f}(\cdot,\cdot)
defines a distance on
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
which induces its topology. Finally,

{\sf d}(\gamma(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0))\leq{\sf D}_{f}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}).

Proof.

The proof of (i) is well known. The first inequality in (ii) follows directly from (i). The fact that the right hand side is finite follows by property (c) above. The last inequality in (ii) follows by property (a), while
{\sf D}_{f}
is a distance since
{\sf d}
is. If
\gamma_{j}
converges uniformly to
\gamma_{\infty}
on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
then
{\sf D}_{f}(\gamma_{j},\gamma_{\infty})\to 0
, again because of property (c) above. Vice versa, if
{\sf D}_{f}(\gamma_{j},\gamma_{\infty})\to 0
then
\gamma_{j}
converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
to
\gamma_{\infty}
because
f
is positive and continuous.
∎

The set of geodesic lines,
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
, is a closed subset of
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
. For Busemann convex metric space, so for instance in the
\textup{CAT}(-1)
case,
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})=\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
.

Example 3.4
.

The space
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
is not necessarily locally compact. Consider for instance the space
{\rm X}
obtained by gluing infinitely many circles
{\rm X}_{i}
of radius
\frac{1}{i}
by one common point, endowed with the length metric.
{\rm X}
is compact, but there are sequences of local geodesics that converge to the map constantly equal to the gluing point.

On the other hand the space
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
is locally compact and even proper, with the metric induced by
f\in\mathcal{F}
. This is because of Lemma
3.3
and Ascoli-Arzelà’s Theorem.

In view of Example
3.4
, we mainly focus on the space
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
instead than on
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
, even if many of the things we are going to say apply also to
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
.

Every isometry
g
of
{\rm X}
acts on
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
by
(g\gamma)(\cdot)=g\gamma(\cdot)
,
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. The action is continuous, so
g
defines a homeomorphism of
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. Thus every group of isometries of
{\rm X}
acts by homeomorphisms on
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. The same action can be naturally defined on
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
.

In the next result we show that also
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
is metrizable and proper, for every discrete group
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper metric space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete. For every
f\in\mathcal{F}
, the action of
\Gamma
on
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),{\sf D}_{f})
is by isometries and
\Gamma
, as a subgroup of
\textup{Isom}(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),{\sf D}_{f})
, is discrete. In particular, the space
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
is metrizable and proper.
If, moreover,
\Gamma
acts freely on
{\rm X}
, then it acts freely also on
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
.

Proof.

The fact that the action of
\Gamma
on
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),{\sf D}_{f})
is by isometries follows directly from (
23
). Lemma
3.3
implies that for every
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
and every
R\geq 0
it holds that

\#\{g\in\Gamma\,:\,{\sf D}_{f}(g\gamma,\gamma)\leq R\}\leq\#\{g\in\Gamma\,:\,{\sf d}(g\gamma(0),\gamma(0))\leq R\}<+\infty.

This shows that
\Gamma
is discrete as subgroup of isometries of
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),{\sf D}_{f})
. The discussion in Section
3.1
implies that the space
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
is metrizable via the quotient metric
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}
induced by
{\sf D}_{f}
. Let
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
and let
[\gamma_{k}]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
be a sequence such that
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}([\gamma_{k}],[\gamma])\leq R
for every
k
. By definition of quotient metric, for every
k
there exists
g_{k}\in\Gamma
such that
{\sf D}_{f}(g_{k}\gamma_{k},\gamma)\leq R
. Lemma
3.3
and Ascoli-Arzelà’s Theorem imply that there exists
\gamma_{\infty}\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
and a subsequence
\{k_{j}\}
such that
g_{k_{j}}\gamma_{k_{j}}
converges to
\gamma_{\infty}
. By definition, this means that
[\gamma_{k_{j}}]
converges to
[\gamma_{\infty}]
, so the
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}
balls in
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
are compact.

Finally, suppose that
\Gamma
acts freely on
{\rm X}
and let
g\in\Gamma
be such that
g\gamma=\gamma
for some
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. By definition,
g\gamma(0)=\gamma(0)
and so
g=\text{id}
.
∎

Under some additional assumptions, the space
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
can be identified with the space of all local geodesics of the quotient space
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
. We first need a result about the space of local geodesics.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic metric space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete and free. Then the map

\Pi\colon\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})\mapsto\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}),\quad\Pi(\gamma)(\cdot)=(\pi\circ\gamma)(\cdot),

where
\pi\colon{\rm X}\to\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is the projection map,
is continuous, surjective and
\Gamma
-invariant. So it induces a map

\bar{\Pi}\colon\Gamma\backslash\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})\mapsto\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X})

which is a homeomorphism.

Proof.

First of all,
\Pi
is well defined. Indeed the projection of a local geodesic of
{\rm X}
on
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is a local geodesic, by Lemma
3.2
.
Moreover,
\Pi
is
\Gamma
-invariant: if
\gamma=g\gamma^{\prime}
for some
g\in\Gamma
and
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
, then
(\pi\circ\gamma)(\cdot)=(\pi\circ\gamma^{\prime})(\cdot)
.
Furthermore, every map
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
can be lifted to a map
\tilde{\gamma}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\ X
since
\pi\colon{\rm X}\to\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is a covering map by Lemma
3.2
. If
\gamma
is a local geodesic of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
then
\tilde{\gamma}
is a local geodesic of
{\rm X}
, again by Lemma
3.2
. This shows that
\Pi
is surjective. Since
\pi
is
1
-Lipschitz when we equip both
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
and
\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X})
with the metric induced by the same
f\in\mathcal{F}
, then
\Pi
is continuous.

It remains to show that the map
\bar{\Pi}
is a homeomorphism. Let us show it is injective: suppose
\Pi(\gamma)=\Pi(\gamma^{\prime})
, i.e.
(\pi\circ\gamma)(t)=(\pi\circ\gamma^{\prime})(t)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
there is a unique
g_{t}\in\Gamma
such that
g_{t}\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\gamma(t)
, by Lemma
3.2
.
We consider the set
I=\{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,g_{t}=g_{0}\}
. It is closed: indeed if
t_{k}\to t_{\infty}
with
t_{k}\in I
then we have

\displaystyle{\sf d}(g_{t_{\infty}}\gamma^{\prime}(t_{\infty}),g_{0}\gamma^{\prime}(t_{\infty}))

\displaystyle\leq{\sf d}(g_{t_{\infty}}\gamma^{\prime}(t_{\infty}),g_{t_{k}}\gamma^{\prime}(t_{k}))+{\sf d}(g_{t_{k}}\gamma^{\prime}(t_{k}),g_{0}\gamma^{\prime}(t_{\infty}))

\displaystyle\leq{\sf d}(\gamma(t_{\infty}),\gamma(t_{k}))+{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(t_{k}),\gamma^{\prime}(t_{\infty}))

and the last quantity is as small as we want. So
g_{t_{\infty}}=g_{0}
, i.e.
t_{\infty}\in I
. Moreover
I
is open: let
t\in I
and suppose there is a sequence
t_{k}\to t
such that
g_{t_{k}}\neq g_{0}
. Then

{\sf d}(\gamma(t),g_{t_{k}}\gamma^{\prime}(t))\leq{\sf d}(\gamma(t),\gamma(t_{k}))+{\sf d}(g_{t_{k}}\gamma^{\prime}(t_{k}),g_{t_{k}}\gamma^{\prime}(t))\leq 2|t-t_{k}|.

When
2|t-t_{k}|<\frac{\text{sys}(\Gamma,\gamma^{\prime}(t))}{2}
we get
{\sf d}(g_{0}^{-1}g_{t_{k}}\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))<\frac{\text{sys}(\Gamma,\gamma^{\prime}(t))}{2}
, so
g_{0}^{-1}g_{t_{k}}=\text{id}
by Lemma
3.2
. This means that
g_{t_{k}}=g_{0}
for every
t_{k}
sufficiently close to
t
. Since
I
is non empty we conclude that
I=\mathbb{R}
, i.e.
\gamma(t)=g_{0}\gamma^{\prime}(t)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. So
\gamma=g_{0}\gamma^{\prime}
, i.e.
\bar{\Pi}
is injective.

The continuity of
\bar{\Pi}^{-1}
can be checked on sequences since both spaces are metrizable by Lemma
3.3
and Proposition
3.5
: observe that the proof of Proposition
3.5
says that also
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
is metrizable.
Let
p\colon\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})\to\Gamma\backslash\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
be the standard projection map induced by the action of
\Gamma
on
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
. Let
\gamma_{k},\gamma_{\infty}\in\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X})
be such that
\gamma_{k}\to\gamma_{\infty}
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. Let
T\geq 0
and let
\varepsilon>0
be a real number smaller than half the systole of the compact set
\gamma_{\infty}([-T,T])
. Let
\tilde{\gamma}_{k},\tilde{\gamma}_{\infty}
be covering local geodesics of
\gamma_{k},\gamma_{\infty}
respectively. We have
{\sf d}(\gamma_{k}(t),\gamma_{\infty}(t))<\varepsilon
for every
t\in[-T,T]
for
k
big enough. So, for every
t\in[-T,T]
there exists a unique
g_{k}(t)\in\Gamma
such that
{\sf d}(g_{k}(t)\tilde{\gamma}_{k}(t),\tilde{\gamma}_{\infty}(t))<\varepsilon
by Lemma
3.2
.
Arguing as before we conclude that for every
k
big enough it holds that
g_{k}(t)=g_{k}(0)=:g_{k}\in\Gamma
for every
t\in[-T,T]
. This implies that
p(\tilde{\gamma}_{k})
converges uniformly on
[-T,T]
to
p(\tilde{\gamma}_{\infty})
. Since this is true for every
T\geq 0
we get the continuity of
\bar{\Pi}^{-1}
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic metric space such that every local geodesic of
{\rm X}
is a global geodesic. Let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete and free. Then
\bar{\Pi}
is a homeomorphism between
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
and
\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X})
.

The assumptions are satisfied for instance by Busemann convex metric spaces and in particular by
\textup{CAT}(-1)
spaces.

3.3.
The geodesic flow

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper metric space and let
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
be its space of local geodesic lines. There is a natural action of
\mathbb{R}
on
\text{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
defined by reparametrization:

(24)

\Phi_{t}\gamma(\cdot)=\gamma(\cdot+t)

for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
It is a continuous action, i.e. the map
\mathbb{R}\to\text{Homeo}(\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X}))
,
t\mapsto\Phi_{t}
, is a continuous homomorphism of groups. In particular
\Phi_{t}
is a homeomorphism of
\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X})
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Phi_{t}\circ\Phi_{s}=\Phi_{t+s}
for every
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
. The dynamical system
(\textup{Loc-Geod}({\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
is called the
local geodesic flow of
{\rm X}
. By definition,
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
is preserved by the action of
\Phi_{t}
. The dynamical system
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
is called the
geodesic flow of
{\rm X}
.

The action of
\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
on
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
commutes with the action
\Phi_{t}
, in the sense that
g(\Phi_{t}\gamma)=\Phi_{t}(g\gamma)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and every
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. Therefore,
\Phi_{t}
induces a continuous action by
\mathbb{R}
on
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
by homeomorphisms, that we denote by
\bar{\Phi}_{t}
.
The
quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is the dynamical system
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
.
This is the flow of which we compute the topological entropy in Theorem
A
.

For
\textup{CAT}(-1)
spaces, the quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is conjugated to the local geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic metric space such that every local geodesic of
{\rm X}
is a global geodesic. Let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete and free. Then the map
\bar{\Pi}
of Proposition
3.6
is a conjugation between the dynamical systems
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
and
(\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
, i.e.
\bar{\Pi}\circ\bar{\Phi}_{1}=\Phi_{1}\circ\bar{\Pi}
.

Proof.

Let
p\colon\textup{Geod}({\rm X})\to\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
be the standard projection map. For every
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
we have that

(\bar{\Pi}\circ\bar{\Phi}_{1})([\gamma(\cdot)])=\bar{\Pi}([\gamma(1+\cdot)])=(\pi\circ\gamma)(1+\cdot)=\Phi_{1}((\pi\circ\gamma)(\cdot))=(\Phi_{1}\circ\bar{\Pi})([\gamma(\cdot)]).

∎

4.
The entropies of a dynamical system

A dynamical system is a couple
({\rm Y},T)
where
{\rm Y}
is a topological space and
T\colon{\rm Y}\to{\rm Y}
is a continuous map. We will always denote by
\mathscr{B}
the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel subsets of
{\rm Y}
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{1}({\rm Y},T)
the set of probability measure on
({\rm Y},\mathscr{B})
that are
T
-invariant, i.e. such that
T_{\#}\mu=\mu
. We recall that a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}({\rm Y},T)
is ergodic if the
\mu
-measure of every subset
A
of
{\rm Y}
such that
T^{-1}A\subseteq A
is either
0
or
1
. We denote the set of ergodic measures by
\mathcal{E}_{1}({\rm Y},T)
. The measure-theoretic entropy of the dynamical system
({\rm Y},T)
is by definition:

(25)

h_{\text{meas}}({\rm Y},T):=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}({\rm Y},T)}h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{1}({\rm Y},T)}h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T),

where
h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T)
denotes the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of the measure
\mu
, whose definition we now recall.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}({\rm Y},T)
.
A
finite Borel partition
of
{\rm Y}
is a set
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\}
, where
Q_{i}\subseteq{\rm Y}
is Borel for every
i=1,\ldots,k
,
Q_{i}\cap Q_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
and
{\rm Y}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i}
. The
\mu
-entropy of
\mathcal{Q}
is the quantity

H_{\mu}(\mathcal{Q}):=-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu(Q_{i})\log\mu(Q_{i}).

Here we use the convention that
0\cdot\log 0=0
.
Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\bigvee_{j=0}^{n}T^{-j}\mathcal{Q}:=\left\{Q_{i_{0}}\cap T^{-1}Q_{i_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap T^{-n}Q_{i_{n}}\,:\,i_{l}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\right\}.

Notice that
\bigvee_{j=0}^{n}T^{-j}\mathcal{Q}
is again a finite Borel partition of
{\rm Y}
. So we set

h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T,\mathcal{Q}):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}H_{\mu}\left(\bigvee_{j=0}^{n}T^{-j}\mathcal{Q}\right).

The limit on the right hand side exists. Finally, the
Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of
\mu
is

h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T):=\sup_{Q}h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T,\mathcal{Q}),

where the supremum is taken among all possible finite Borel partitions of
{\rm Y}
.
A direct consequence of the definition of the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy is the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
({\rm Y},T)
be a dynamical system and let
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{1}({\rm Y},T)
. Suppose that there exists a Borel subset
{\rm Y}^{\prime}\subseteq{\rm Y}
such that
T({\rm Y}^{\prime})={\rm Y}^{\prime}
and
\mu({\rm Y}^{\prime})=1
. Then
h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T)=h_{\mu\llcorner{\rm Y}^{\prime}}({\rm Y}^{\prime},T)
, where
\mu\llcorner{\rm Y}^{\prime}
is the restriction of
\mu
to
{\rm Y}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Given a finite Borel partition
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\}
of
{\rm Y}^{\prime}
we define
\mathcal{Q}^{e}:=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k},{\rm Y}\setminus{\rm Y}^{\prime}\}
, which is a finite Borel partition of
{\rm Y}
. As collection of sets,
\bigvee_{j=0}^{n}T^{-j}\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\bigvee_{j=0}^{n}T^{-j}\mathcal{Q}^{e}
. From the definitions we have that
h_{\mu\llcorner{\rm Y}^{\prime}}({\rm Y}^{\prime},T,\mathcal{Q})\leq h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T,\mathcal{Q}^{e})
. Since this is true for every finite Borel partition of
{\rm Y}^{\prime}
we deduce that
h_{\mu\llcorner{\rm Y}^{\prime}}({\rm Y}^{\prime},T)\leq h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T)
.

On the other hand let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\}
be a finite Borel partition of
{\rm Y}
and define
\mathcal{Q}^{r}:=\{Q_{1}\cap{\rm Y}^{\prime},\ldots,Q_{k}\cap{\rm Y}^{\prime}\}
, a finite Borel partition of
{\rm Y}^{\prime}
.
Since
T({\rm Y}\setminus{\rm Y}^{\prime})={\rm Y}\setminus{\rm Y}^{\prime}
and
\mu({\rm Y}\setminus{\rm Y}^{\prime})=0
, it follows that
h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T,\mathcal{Q})=h_{\mu\llcorner{\rm Y}^{\prime}}({\rm Y}^{\prime},T,\mathcal{Q}^{r})
. By the arbitrariness of
\mathcal{Q}
we conclude that
h_{\mu}({\rm Y},T)\leq h_{\mu\llcorner{\rm Y}^{\prime}}({\rm Y}^{\prime},T)
.
∎

Another notion of entropy, introduced by R.Bowen in
[
Bow73
]
, is defined for dynamical systems
({\rm Y},T)
such that
{\rm Y}
is metrizable. The
topological entropy
of
({\rm Y},T)
is defined by

(26)

h_{\textup{top}}({\rm Y},T)=\inf_{\text{\texthtd}}\sup_{K}\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\textup{Cov}_{\text{\texthtd}^{n}}(K,r),

where the infimum is taken among all metrics
\texthtd
inducing the topology of
{\rm Y}
, the supremum is among all compact subsets of
{\rm Y}
,
\text{\texthtd}^{n}
is the
dynamical metric

(27)

\text{\texthtd}^{n}(y,y^{\prime}):=\max_{i=0,\ldots,n-1}\text{\texthtd}(T^{i}y,T^{i}y^{\prime})

and
\textup{Cov}_{\text{\texthtd}^{n}}(K,r)
denotes the minimal number of balls of radius
r
, with respect to the metric
\text{\texthtd}^{n}
, needed to cover
K
.

The results around the variational principle of
[
HKR95
]
can be summerized as follows.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
({\rm Y},T)
be a metrizable dynamical system.

(i)

It holds that
h_{\textup{meas}}({\rm Y},T)\leq h_{\textup{top}}({\rm Y},T)
.

(ii)

If
{\rm Y}
is locally compact then
h_{\textup{meas}}({\rm Y},T)=h_{\textup{top}}({\rm Y},T)
.

(iii)

If
{\rm Y}
is compact, then the right hand side of (
26
) does not depend on the metric
\texthtd
inducing the topology of
{\rm Y}
. In other words

h_{\textup{top}}({\rm Y},T)=\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\textup{Cov}_{\textup{\texthtd}^{n}}({\rm Y},r),

for every choice of
\texthtd
.

We observe that in the right hand side of (
26
), the compact subsets
K
are not required to be
T
-invariant, where by
T
-invariant we mean
T(K)=K
. For a generic dynamical system, the restriction in (
26
) to
T
-invariant compact subsets gives a strictly smaller quantity, see the remark below
[
HKR95
, Lemma 1.6]
.
We call it the
invariant topological entropy
, which is by definition

(28)

h_{\textup{inv-top}}({\rm Y},T):=\inf_{\text{\texthtd}}\sup_{K\,\,\,T\text{-inv.}}\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\textup{Cov}_{\text{\texthtd}^{n}}(K,r).

When
K
is a compact
T
-invariant subset then the dynamical system
(K,T)
is supported on a compact metrizable space, so the quantity

\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\textup{Cov}_{\text{\texthtd}^{n}}(K,r)

does not depend on the choice of the metric
\texthtd
and it coincides with the topological entropy of the dynamical system
(K,T)
by Proposition
4.2
.(iii). Therefore

(29)

h_{\textup{inv-top}}({\rm Y},T)=\sup_{K\,\,\,T\text{-inv.}}h_{\text{top}}(K,T).

Let
({\rm Y},T)
be a dynamical system and let
\Gamma
be a group of homeomorphisms of
{\rm Y}
commuting with
T
, i.e.
Tg(y)=gT(y)
for every
g\in\Gamma
and every
y\in{\rm Y}
. In this situation, the map
T
induces a continuous function
\bar{T}\colon\Gamma\backslash{\rm Y}\to\Gamma\backslash{\rm Y}
,
\bar{T}([y])=[Ty]
and so we have the dynamical system
(\Gamma\backslash{\rm Y},\bar{T})
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
({\rm Y},T)
be a dynamical system. Suppose
{\rm Y}
is metrizable with a metric
{\sf d}
. Let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm Y},{\sf d})
commuting with
T
. If
\Gamma
is finite then

h_{\textup{inv-top}}({\rm Y},T)=h_{\textup{inv-top}}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm Y},\bar{T}).

Proof.

Let
p\colon{\rm Y}\to\Gamma\backslash{\rm Y}
be the standard projection. If
K
is a compact
T
-invariant subset of
{\rm Y}
, then
p(K)
is a compact
\bar{T}
-invariant subset. Vice versa, if
K
is a compact
\bar{T}
-invariant subset of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm Y}
then
p^{-1}(K)
is a compact,
T
-invariant subset of
{\rm Y}
.
By Proposition
4.2
, we can compute the topological entropy of every compact
T
-invariant subset of
{\rm Y}
using
{\sf d}
and the entropy of every compact
\bar{T}
-invariant subset of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm Y}
using the quotient metric
\bar{{\sf d}}
.
Since

\textup{Cov}_{\bar{{\sf d}}^{n}}(p(K),r)\leq\textup{Cov}_{{\sf d}^{n}}(K,r)\leq\#\Gamma\cdot\textup{Cov}_{\bar{{\sf d}}^{n}}(p(K),r)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every
r>0
and every
K\subseteq{\rm Y}
, we get the thesis.
∎

4.1.
Topological entropy of flows

Let
{\rm Y}
be a metrizable topological space and denote by
\text{Homeo}({\rm Y})
the group of self-homeomorphisms of
{\rm Y}
endowed with the compact-open topology. A flow on
{\rm Y}
is a continuous homomorphism
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\text{Homeo}({\rm Y})
,
t\mapsto\Phi_{t}\colon{\rm Y}\to{\rm Y}
, so
\Phi_{0}=\text{id}
and
\Phi_{t}\circ\Phi_{s}=\Phi_{t+s}
. Classically, the topological entropy of a flow is defined via the following formula

(30)

h_{\text{top}}^{\text{flow}}({\rm Y},\Phi):=\inf_{\textup{\texthtd}}\sup_{K}\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\textup{\texthtd}^{T}}(K,r),

where the infimum is among all metrics inducing the topology of
{\rm Y}
, the supremum is among all compact subsets of
{\rm Y}
and

(31)

\textup{\texthtd}^{T}(y,y^{\prime})=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\textup{\texthtd}(\Phi_{t}(y),\Phi_{t}(y^{\prime})).

The difference between (
30
) and (
26
) relies in the different definition of distance in (
31
) with respect to (
27
). The next lemma focuses on the relation between these two definitions.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
{\rm Y}
be a metrizable topological space and let
\Phi
be a flow on it. Consider the dynamical system
({\rm Y},\Phi_{1})
. Then
h_{\textup{top}}({\rm Y},\Phi_{1})\leq h_{\textup{top}}^{\textup{flow}}({\rm Y},\Phi)
. Moreover, if
{\rm Y}
is compact and the maps
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
are uniformly equicontinuous then
h_{\textup{top}}({\rm Y},\Phi_{1})=h_{\textup{top}}^{\textup{flow}}({\rm Y},\Phi)
.

The uniform equicontinuity means that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\text{\texthtd}(y,y^{\prime})<\delta
then
\text{\texthtd}(\Phi_{t}(y),\Phi_{t}(y^{\prime}))<\varepsilon
for every
y,y^{\prime}\in{\rm Y}
and every
t\in[0,1]
. Here
\texthtd
is any metric inducing the topology of
{\rm Y}
. By compactness of
{\rm Y}
and of
[0,1]
the notion of uniform continuity does not depend on the specific metric
\texthtd
.

Proof.

The inequality follows from (
26
) and (
30
) since
\text{\texthtd}^{T}(y,y^{\prime})\geq\text{\texthtd}^{\lfloor T\rfloor}(y,y^{\prime})
for every
y,y^{\prime}\in{\rm Y}
. On the other hand, if
{\rm Y}
is compact we have that

h_{\text{top}}({\rm Y},\Phi_{1})=\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\text{Cov}_{\text{\texthtd}^{n}}({\rm Y},r)

where
\texthtd
is any metric inducing the topology of
{\rm Y}
, by Proposition
4.2
, while

h_{\text{top}}^{\text{flow}}({\rm Y},\Phi)\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\text{Cov}_{\text{\texthtd}^{T}}({\rm Y},r).

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\delta>0
be as in the definition of uniform equicontinuity of the maps
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
.
Let
\{y_{i}\}
be a subset of
{\rm Y}
realizing
\text{Cov}_{\text{\texthtd}^{\lfloor T\rfloor}}({\rm Y},\delta)
. We claim that this set is
\varepsilon
-dense with respect to
\text{\texthtd}^{T}
. Fix
y\in{\rm Y}
and take
i
such that
\text{\texthtd}^{\lfloor T\rfloor}(y,y_{i})<\delta
. For every
t\in[0,T]
we consider
n_{t}:=\lfloor t\rfloor
and we observe that

\text{\texthtd}(\Phi_{t}(y),\Phi_{t}(y_{i}))=\text{\texthtd}(\Phi_{t-n_{t}}(\Phi_{n_{t}}(y)),\Phi_{t-n_{t}}(\Phi_{n_{t}}(y_{i}))).

Since
\text{\texthtd}(\Phi_{n_{t}}(y),\Phi_{n_{t}}(y_{i}))<\delta
and since
t-n_{t}\in[0,1]
we deduce that
\text{\texthtd}(\Phi_{t}(y),\Phi_{t}(y_{i}))<\varepsilon
. Since this is true for every
t\in[0,T]
we have that
\text{\texthtd}^{T}(y,y_{i})<\varepsilon
. Then
\textup{Cov}_{\text{\texthtd}^{T}}({\rm Y},\varepsilon)\leq\textup{Cov}_{\text{\texthtd}^{\lfloor T\rfloor}}({\rm Y},\delta)
. The thesis follows.
∎

4.2.
Topological entropy of the geodesic flow

We specialize the discussion to the case of the geodesic flow. Let
{\rm X}
be a proper metric space. Remember that the geodesic flow of
{\rm X}
is the dynamical system
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
, where
\Phi_{1}
is defined in (
24
). Let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete. The quotient geodesic flow of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
is the dynamical system
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
. This is the flow whose topological entropy appears in Theorem
A
. By Proposition
3.5
,
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
is a locally compact metrizable space, so
h_{\text{meas}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
, by Proposition
4.2
.

The famous Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, in the case of the quotient geodesic flow, reads as follows.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper metric space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{1}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
. Then, for every
f\in L^{1}(\mu)
it holds that

(32)

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(f\circ\bar{\Phi}_{j})([\gamma])=\int f\,d\mu

for
\mu
-a.e.
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
. In other words, the limit in (
32
) exists for
\mu
-a.e.
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
and equals the right hand side.

Since conjugated dynamical systems have same topological entropy, then

h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=h_{\text{top}}(\textup{Loc-Geod}(\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}),\Phi_{1})

under the assumptions of Corollary
3.8
. In particular this applies to proper
\textup{CAT}(-1)
spaces
{\rm X}
and torsion-free, discrete groups
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
.

5.
f
-entropy on Gromov-hyperbolic metric spaces

In order to prove Theorem
A
we need a way to connect the topological entropy of the dynamical system
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
to the topological entropy of the dynamical system
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
. However, if one uses the definition of topological entropy of
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
given in Section
4
, then
h_{\text{top}}(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\Phi_{1})=0
, see
[
Cav22
, Lemma 4.1]
. In
[
Cav22
]
we proposed a modification of the definition of the topological entropy of
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\Phi_{1})
introducing the Lipschitz-topological entropy. In this paper we will use a slightly different notion that simplifies the exposition.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space and let
x\in{\rm X}
. For every
A\subseteq{\rm X}
and every
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
we set

(33)

\textup{Geod}(A;C):=\left\{\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})\,:\,\gamma(0)\in A,\,\gamma^{\pm}\in C\right\}.

This notation is coherent with the one introduced in (
10
).
Let
f\in\mathcal{F}
as introduced in Section
3.2
. We define the
upper
f
-entropy of
C
as

(34)

\overline{h}_{f}(C):=\sup_{R\geq 0}\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\text{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r)

and the
lower
f
-entropy of
C
as

(35)

\underline{h}_{f}(C):=\sup_{R\geq 0}\lim_{r\to 0}\varliminf_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\text{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r),

where the dynamical distance
{\sf D}_{f}^{T}
is

{\sf D}_{f}^{T}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\sf D}_{f}(\Phi_{t}\gamma,\Phi_{t}\gamma^{\prime}).

This last definition is the same as the one in (
31
). The upper and lower
f
-entropies do not depend on the choice of the basepoint
x\in{\rm X}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space, let
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
and let
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Then

\overline{h}_{f}(C)\geq\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{h}_{f}(C)\geq\underline{\textup{MD}}(C).

Proof.

We give the proof for the upper entropy and the upper Minkowski dimension only, the other case being analogous. Let
\delta
be the hyperbolicity constant of
{\rm X}
.
Let
x\in{\rm X}
and let
M:={\sf d}(x,\text{QC-Hull}(C))+22\delta
. Notice that

{\sf d}(e_{T}(\gamma),e_{T}(\gamma^{\prime}))={\sf d}(\gamma(T),\gamma^{\prime}(T))\leq{\sf D}_{f}(\Phi_{T}\gamma,\Phi_{T}\gamma^{\prime})\leq{\sf D}_{f}^{T}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})

for every
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
by Lemma
3.3
. This implies that

(36)

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r)\geq\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf d}}(e_{T}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,M);C)),r)

for every
r>0
and every
R\geq M
. The thesis follows by (
10
).
∎

The opposite inequalities are false in general. They are true under additional assumptions, as we will see in Section
7
.

6.
The proof of Theorem
C

In this section we prove Theorem
C
. Let us recall the setup. Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space, let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete, non-elementary and virtually torsion-free and let
x\in{\rm X}
be a basepoint.
The goal is to show that
h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\geq h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
. We first reduce the proof to the torsion-free case.

Lemma 6.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
C
, let
\Gamma_{0}\triangleleft\Gamma
be a finite index, torsion-free subgroup. Then
h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma_{0})=h_{\textup{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
and
h_{\textup{inv-top}}(\Gamma_{0}\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=h_{\textup{inv-top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
.

Proof.

The statement about the critical exponent is known. As described in Section
4
, the dynamical system
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
coincides with the quotient of the dynamical system
(\Gamma_{0}\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
modulo the finite group
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
. In particular the action of
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
commutes with
\bar{\Phi}_{1}
. Moreover, this dynamical system is metrizable via the metric
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}
introduced in Section
3.2
, with respect to which
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
acts by isometries, see Proposition
3.5
. The thesis follows by Lemma
4.3
.
∎

The strategy to prove Theorem
C
relies on the following chain of inequalities:

(37)

\displaystyle h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\geq h_{\text{inv-top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\geq\sup_{\tau\geq 0}\underline{h}_{f}(\Lambda_{\tau})\geq\sup_{\tau\geq 0}\underline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda_{\tau})\geq h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma),

where the sets
\Lambda_{\tau}\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
have been defined in Section
2.4
. The first inequality is true by definition, as we recalled in Section
4
.
The third inequality is Proposition
5.1
. The last inequality is Theorem
2.14
. We have reduced the proof of Theorem
C
to the proof of

(38)

h_{\text{inv-top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\geq\sup_{\tau\geq 0}\underline{h}_{f}(\Lambda_{\tau}),

and we can further assume that
\Gamma
is torsion-free by Lemma
6.1
.

We need to identify the compact,
\bar{\Phi}_{1}
-invariant subsets of
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. For every
\tau\geq 0
we define

K_{\tau}=\{[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})\,:\,{\sf d}(\gamma(n),\Gamma x)\leq\tau\text{ for every }n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We observe that the set is well posed, since the condition on the representative of
[\gamma]
is
\Gamma
-invariant.
Under the assumptions of Corollary
3.8
, the sets
K_{\tau}
are comparable via
\bar{\Pi}
to the set of local geodesic lines of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
that are contained in a compact region of
\Gamma\backslash{\rm X}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

K_{\tau}
is a compact,
\bar{\Phi}_{1}
-invariant subset for every
\tau\geq 0
. Moreover every compact,
\bar{\Phi}_{1}
-invariant subset of
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
is contained in
K_{\tau}
for some
\tau\geq 0
.

Proof.

For every
\tau\geq 0
, the set
K_{\tau}
satisfies
\bar{\Phi}_{1}(K_{\tau})\subseteq K_{\tau}
and
\bar{\Phi}_{1}^{-1}(K_{\tau})\subseteq K_{\tau}
, so it is
\bar{\Phi}_{1}
-invariant. Let
\{[\gamma_{k}]\}_{k}\subseteq K_{\tau}
be a sequence. By definition, for every
k
there exists
g_{k}\in\Gamma
such that
{\sf d}(g_{k}\gamma_{k}(0),x)\leq\tau
. By Ascoli-Arzelà’s Theorem, the sequence
g_{k}\gamma_{k}
subconverges to a geodesic
\gamma_{\infty}\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. Therefore
[\gamma_{k}]
subconverges to
[\gamma_{\infty}]
. Moreover, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have that
{\sf d}(g_{k}\gamma_{k}(n),\Gamma x)\leq\tau
, implying that
{\sf d}(\gamma_{\infty}(n),\Gamma x)\leq\tau
, i.e.
[\gamma_{\infty}]\in K_{\tau}
. This completes the proof of the first statement.

Let
K
be a compact,
\bar{\Phi}_{1}
-invariant subset of
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
.
Suppose that
K
is not contained in
K_{\tau}
for every
\tau\geq 0
. Then, for every
k\geq 0
there exists
[\gamma_{k}]\in K
such that
{\sf d}(\gamma_{k}(n_{k}),\Gamma x)>k
for some
n_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
. For every
k
we reparametrize
\gamma_{k}
so that
{\sf d}(\gamma_{k}(0),\Gamma x)>k
. Since
K
is
\bar{\Phi}_{1}
-invariant then the equivalence classes of the reparametrized geodesics belong again to
K
. Since
K
is compact we can find a subsequence, denoted again by
[\gamma_{k}]
, that converges to
[\gamma_{\infty}]\in K
. This means that there exist
g_{k}\in\Gamma
such that
g_{k}\gamma_{k}
converges to
\gamma_{\infty}
, in particular
g_{k}\gamma_{k}(0)
converges to
\gamma_{\infty}(0)
. But
{\sf d}(g_{k}\gamma_{k}(0),\Gamma x)>k
for every
k
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
C
.

As discussed above, the proof is reduced to show (
38
) under the additional assumption that
\Gamma
is torsion-free. By Lemma
6.2
we have that

(39)

h_{\text{inv-top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=\sup_{\tau\geq 0}h_{\text{top}}(K_{\tau},\bar{\Phi}_{1}).

The proof of (
38
) will be a consequence of the following fact. For every
\tau\geq 0
, for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
, and for every
R\geq 0
it holds that

(40)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\varliminf_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);\Lambda_{\tau}),r)

\displaystyle\leq h_{\text{top}}^{\text{flow}}(K_{2\tau+8\delta+R},\bar{\Phi})

\displaystyle=h_{\text{top}}(K_{2\tau+8\delta+R},\bar{\Phi}_{1}),

where the last equality follows by Lemma
4.4
and the fact that the maps
\{\bar{\Phi}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
are uniformly continuous with respect to the metric
\bar{{\sf D}}_{e^{-|s|}}
. Indeed, for
t\in[0,1]
we have that

(41)

\displaystyle{\sf D}_{e^{-|s|}}(\Phi_{t}(\gamma),\Phi_{t}(\gamma^{\prime}))=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}{\sf d}(\gamma(s+t),\gamma^{\prime}(s+t))e^{-|s|}={\sf D}_{e^{-|s|}}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{-|s|}}{e^{-|s+t|}}=e^{2}{\sf D}_{e^{-|s|}}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}).

This means that the maps
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
are uniformly Lipschitz with respect to
{\sf D}_{e^{-|s|}}
, so the quotient maps
\{\bar{\Phi}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
are uniformly continuous with respect to the metric
\bar{{\sf D}}_{e^{-|s|}}
.

We observe that if (
40
) holds, then we can take the supremum over
R\geq 0
to get that

\underline{h}_{f}(\Lambda_{\tau})\leq h_{\text{inv-top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})

for every
\tau\geq 0
. Taking the supremum over
\tau\geq 0
we finally obtain (
38
).

It remains to prove the inequality in (
40
). We fix
\tau,R\geq 0
and
f\in\mathcal{F}
. By Proposition
3.5
the metric
{\sf D}_{f}
defines a quotient metric
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}
on
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
and that can be used for the computation of the topological entropy of the compact dynamical system
(K_{2\tau+8\delta+R},\bar{\Phi}_{1})
, see Proposition
4.2
. We fix
T\geq 0
. Let
\{[\gamma_{i}]\}
be a subset of
K_{2\tau+8\delta+R}
realizing
\textup{Cov}_{\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{T}}(K_{2\tau+8\delta+R},r)
. We define

A_{T}:=\left\{\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})\,:\,[\gamma]=[\gamma_{i}]\text{ for some }i\text{ and }{\sf d}(\gamma(0),x)\leq R+r\right\}.

The group
\Gamma
acts by isometries on
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),{\sf D}_{f})
by Proposition
3.5
. Let
\sigma>0
be the systole of
\Gamma
on the compact set
K_{2\tau+8\delta+R}
: it is positive by Lemma
3.1
. For every two
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
such that
[\gamma]=[\gamma^{\prime}]=[\gamma_{i}]
we have that either
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
or
{\sf D}_{f}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})>\frac{\sigma}{2}
, by Lemma
3.2
. This implies that either
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
or
{\sf d}(\gamma(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0))>\frac{\sigma}{2}
by Lemma
3.3
.
Therefore

\#A_{T}\leq\textup{Cov}_{\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{T}}(K_{2\tau+8\delta+R},r)\cdot\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}\left(\overline{B}(x,R+r),\frac{\sigma}{2}\right).

Observe that the second factor does not depend on
T
. So, inequality (
40
) is true if we show that
A_{T}
is
r
-dense in
\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);\Lambda_{\tau})
with respect to
{\sf D}_{f}^{T}
for every
r<\frac{\sigma}{2}
. Indeed, in that case we have that

\lim_{r\to 0}\varliminf_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);\Lambda_{\tau}),r)\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\varliminf_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{T}}(K_{2\tau+8\delta+R},r).

The right hand side is exactly
h_{\text{top}}^{\text{flow}}(K_{2\tau+8\delta+R},\bar{\Phi})
.

It remains to prove that
A_{T}
is
r
-dense with respect to
{\sf D}_{f}^{T}
in
\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);\Lambda_{\tau})
for
r<\frac{\sigma}{2}
. Let
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);\Lambda_{\tau})
. By definition, there exist geodesic rays
\xi_{x,\gamma^{\pm}}
such that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,\gamma^{\pm}}(n),\Gamma x)\leq 2\tau
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
2.6
we deduce that
{\sf d}(\gamma(n),\Gamma x)\leq 2\tau+8\delta+R
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, because
{\sf d}(\gamma(0),x)\leq R
. By definition,
[\gamma]\in K_{2\tau+8\delta+R}
. Hence we can find
i
such that
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{T}([\gamma],[\gamma_{i}])\leq r
.
By definition of quotient metric, for every
t\in[0,T]
there exists
g_{t}\in\Gamma
such that

{\sf D}_{f}(\Phi_{t}(\gamma),g_{t}\Phi_{t}(\gamma_{i}))={\sf D}_{f}(\Phi_{t}(\gamma),\Phi_{t}(g_{t}\gamma_{i}))\leq r.

The isometry
g_{t}\in\Gamma
is unique by Lemma
3.2
, since
r<\frac{\sigma}{2}
. Notice that
{\sf d}(\gamma(0),g_{0}\gamma_{i}(0))\leq r
because of Lemma
3.3
, so
{\sf d}(g_{0}\gamma_{i}(0),x)\leq R+r
. In other words,
g_{0}\gamma_{i}\in A_{T}
. We consider the set
I=\{t\in[0,T]\,:\,g_{t}=g_{0}\}
and we show that it is open and closed. Suppose
t_{k}\to t_{\infty}
with
t_{k}\in I
. Then
{\sf D}_{f}(\Phi_{t_{k}}(\gamma),\Phi_{t_{k}}(g_{0}\gamma_{i}))\leq r
for every
k
. Therefore,
{\sf D}_{f}(\Phi_{t_{\infty}}(\gamma),\Phi_{t_{\infty}}(g_{0}\gamma_{i}))\leq r
, showing that
I
is closed. On the other hand let
t\in I
and suppose that there exists a sequence
t_{k}\to t
with
g_{t_{k}}\neq g_{0}
. We have that
{\sf d}(g_{t_{k}}\gamma_{i}(0),\gamma_{i}(0))\leq 2t_{k}+2r
, so by discreteness we can find a constant subsequence, that we denote again by
g_{t_{k}}
, such that
g_{t_{k}}=g^{\prime}\neq g_{0}
. But this implies that
{\sf D}_{f}(\Phi_{t}(\gamma),\Phi_{t}(g^{\prime}\gamma_{i}))\leq r
, so
g^{\prime}=g_{0}
by uniqueness. This contradiction shows that
I
is open, thus
I=[0,T]
. Therefore
g_{0}\gamma_{i}\in A_{T}
is such that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\sf D}_{f}(\Phi_{t}(\gamma),\Phi_{t}(g_{0}\gamma_{i}))\leq r
. This concludes the proof.
∎

7.
The proof of Theorem
A

In this section we prove Theorem
A
. Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, line-convex, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete and non-elementary. We suppose that
h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)<+\infty
. Our goal is to show that
h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\leq h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
, which is sufficient in view of Theorem
C
.

Recall that the space
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
is locally compact, by Proposition
3.5
. So, by Proposition
4.2
, the topological entropy of
(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
satisfies

h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{1}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})}h_{\mu}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1}).

The strategy is the following. Recall the subsets
\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}
defined in Section
2.4
. We fix a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{1}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
. Then we find
c>0
with the following property: for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\tau_{\varepsilon}>0
and
n_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}))=1
. Next, we prove following chain of inequalities:

(42)

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})

\displaystyle=h_{\mu}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\overline{h}_{f}(\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}})

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}})+h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)\cdot\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)\cdot\frac{c+\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}+h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)\cdot\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}.

By taking
\varepsilon\to 0
we conclude that
h_{\mu}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\leq h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
for every ergodic measure
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{1}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
, yelding the proof.

Let us start with the first step, which is a more accurate version of
[
Cav24
, Theorem C]
and it is based on Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space, let
x\in{\rm X}
and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete. For every
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{1}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
there exists
c\in[1,+\infty)
satisfying the following. For every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\tau_{\varepsilon}>0
and
n_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\textup{erg}}))=1
.

Proof.

Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq{\rm X}
be a countable set such that
{\rm X}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}B(x_{i},1)
. For every
i
let

V_{i}:=\{\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})\,:\,\gamma(0)\in B(x_{i},1)\}

and

U_{i}:=\Gamma\backslash V_{i}\subseteq\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}).

Since
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a covering of
\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
, also
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a covering of
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. Then, there exists
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu(U_{i_{0}})=\sigma>0
.
To every
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
we associate the set of integers
\Theta([\gamma])=\{\vartheta_{i}([\gamma])\}
defined recursively by

\vartheta_{0}([\gamma])=0,\qquad\vartheta_{i+1}([\gamma])=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N},n>\vartheta_{i}([\gamma])\text{ s.t. }\bar{\Phi}_{n}([\gamma])\in U_{i_{0}}\}.

We apply Proposition
4.5
to the indicator function of the set
U_{i_{0}}
, namely
\chi_{U_{i_{0}}}
, obtaining that for
\mu
-a.e.
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
it holds that

\exists\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(\chi_{U_{i_{0}}}\circ\bar{\Phi}_{j})([\gamma])=\mu(U_{i_{0}})=\sigma\in(0,1].

Observe that
(\chi_{U_{i_{0}}}\circ\bar{\Phi}_{j})([\gamma])=1
if and only if
j\in\Theta([\gamma])
and it is
0
otherwise. So

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(\chi_{U_{i_{0}}}\circ\bar{\Phi}_{j})([\gamma])=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{\#\Theta([\gamma])\cap[0,N-1]}{N},

and the right hand side is by definition the density of the set
\Theta([\gamma])
. Given the standard increasing enumeration
\{\vartheta_{0}([\gamma]),\vartheta_{1}([\gamma]),\ldots\}
of
\Theta([\gamma])
, it holds that

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{\#\Theta([\gamma])\cap[0,N-1]}{N}=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{N}{\vartheta_{N}([\gamma])}.

So, for
\mu
-a.e.
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
we have that

(43)

\exists\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{\vartheta_{N}([\gamma])}{N}=\frac{1}{\sigma}\in[1,+\infty).

In the same way, applying the same argument to the flow at time
-1
, we get that for
\mu
-a.e.
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
we have

(44)

\exists\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{\vartheta_{N}([-\gamma])}{N}=\frac{1}{\sigma}\in[1,+\infty).

Here
-\gamma
denotes the curve
-\gamma(t):=\gamma(-t)
. We deduce that (
43
) and (
44
) hold together for
\mu
-a.e.
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
.

We claim that the proposition holds with
c:=\frac{1}{\sigma}\in[1,+\infty)
. We fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
be such that (
43
) and (
44
) holds.
We notice that an integer
n
satisfies
n\in\Theta([\gamma])
if and only if there exists a representative
g\gamma
of
[\gamma]
, with
g\in\Gamma
, such that
\Phi_{n}(g\gamma)\in V_{i_{0}}
, i.e.
g\gamma(n)\in B(x_{i_{0}},1)
. In other words
n\in\Theta([\gamma])
if and only if

(45)

{\sf d}(\gamma(n),\Gamma x_{i_{0}})<1.

We fix a geodesic ray
\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}
. By Lemma
2.6
we have that
d(\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}(t),\gamma(t))\leq 8\delta+{\sf d}(x,\gamma(0))
for every
t\geq 0
. This, together with (
45
) says that
d(\xi_{x,\gamma^{+}}(\vartheta_{N}([\gamma])),\Gamma x_{i_{0}})<8\delta+{\sf d}(x,\gamma(0))+1
. By definition this means that
\gamma^{+}\in\Lambda_{\tau,\Theta([\gamma])}
, where
\tau=8\delta+{\sf d}(x,\gamma(0))+1
.
By the properties of the sequence
\Theta([\gamma])
proved in (
43
), we have that

\gamma^{+}\in\Lambda_{\text{erg}}\cap\bigcup_{\tau>0}\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}=\Lambda_{\text{erg}}\cap\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda_{k,c,\varepsilon,n}

for
\mu
-a.e.
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. Repeating the argument for
\gamma^{-}
, we get that

\gamma^{\pm}\in\Lambda_{\text{erg}}\cap\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda_{k,c,\varepsilon,n}

for
\mu
-a.e.
[\gamma]\in\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
.

We have just showed that

\mu(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\text{erg}}\cap\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda_{k,c,\varepsilon,n})=1
.
But

\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}\left({\rm X};\Lambda_{\text{erg}}\cap\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda_{k,c,\varepsilon,n}\right)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{k,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}).

Each set
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{k,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}})
is
\bar{\Phi}_{1}
-invariant, since its definition depends only on the values of the geodesics at infinity. Therefore, since
\mu
is ergodic,
\mu(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{k,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}))\in\{0,1\}
for every
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
. We conclude that there exists
k_{\varepsilon},n_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{k_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}))=1
.
∎

We proceed to the second step, which provides an almost opposite inequality to Proposition
5.1
which holds for the sets
\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}
under the additional assumptions of Theorem
A
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, line-convex, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space, let
x\in{\rm X}
and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete. For every
\tau,c,\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

\overline{h}_{f}(\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\textup{erg}})\leq\overline{\textup{MD}}(\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\textup{erg}})+h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)\cdot\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}.

This is the only point where we use the line-convexity. Actually, it will be used in the next lemma which is inspired to
[
Cav22
, Lemma 4.4]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, line-convex, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space, let
x\in{\rm X}
and let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}({\rm X})
be discrete. For every
\tau,c,\varepsilon>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
R\geq 0
and
0<r\leq r^{\prime}
it holds that

\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\sup_{\gamma\in\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\textup{erg}})}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime}),r)\leq h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)\cdot\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}.

Before discussing the proof, we show how to get Proposition
7.2
from Lemma
7.3
.

Proof of Proposition
7.2
.

Let
\delta
be the iperbolicity constant of
{\rm X}
.
In order to simplify the notation we set
C:=\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}
.
By definition (
34
) we have that

(46)

\overline{h}_{f}(C)=\sup_{R\geq 0}\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r),

where
f\in\mathcal{F}
. We claim that the decreasing function

r\mapsto\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r)

is almost constant. For that, we fix
0<r\leq r^{\prime}
. Then
\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r)
is bounded from above by

(47)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\log\left(\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r^{\prime})\cdot\sup_{\gamma\in\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C)}\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime}),r)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r^{\prime})+\frac{1}{T}\log\sup_{\gamma\in\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C)}\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime}),r).

Taking the limit superior for
T
going to
+\infty
we get that
\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r)
is smaller than or equal to

\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r^{\prime})+h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)\cdot\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon},

where we used Lemma
7.3
.

We can apply this property to
r^{\prime}:=R+{\sf d}(x,\text{QC-Hull}(C))+44\delta+\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}2|s|f(s)
and an arbitrary
r<r^{\prime}
. By taking the limit for
r
going to
0
we get that

(48)

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{f}(C)\leq\sup_{R\geq 0}\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r^{\prime})+h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)\cdot\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon},

We now claim that

(49)

\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r^{\prime})\leq\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)

for every
R\geq{\sf d}(x,\text{QC-Hull}(C))+22\delta=:M
.
Let
T\geq 0
and define
\rho:=e^{-T}
. Let
\{z_{i}\}
be a
\rho
-dense subset of
C
, i.e. for every
z\in C
there exists
i
such that
(z,z_{i})_{x}\geq T
. Lemma
2.7
gives geodesic lines
\gamma_{i}\in\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,M);C)
such that
\gamma_{i}^{+}=z_{i}
. We claim that the set
\{\gamma_{i}\}
is
r^{\prime}
-dense in
\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C)
with respect to
{\sf D}_{f}^{T}
. Let
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C)
and let
i
be such that
(\gamma^{+},z_{i})_{x}\geq T
. By construction,
{\sf d}(\gamma(0),\gamma_{i}(0))\leq R+M
. Moreover, using Lemma
2.5
and twice Lemma
2.7
we get that
{\sf d}(\gamma(s),\gamma_{i}(s))\leq R+M+22\delta
for every
s\in[0,T]
. We estimate

{\sf D}_{f}^{T}(\gamma,\gamma_{i})=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}{\sf d}(\gamma(s+t),\gamma_{i}(s+t))f(s)

dividing the supremum in three parts.

If
s+t\leq 0
then

{\sf d}(\gamma(s+t),\gamma_{i}(s+t))\leq 2|s+t|+R+M\leq 2|s|+R+M,

implying
\sup_{s+t\leq 0}{\sf d}(\gamma(s+t),\gamma_{i}(s+t))f(s)\leq\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}2|s|f(s)+R+M
.

If
0\leq s+t\leq T
then

{\sf d}(\gamma(s+t),\gamma_{i}(s+t))\leq R+M+22\delta

implying
\sup_{0\leq s+t\leq T}{\sf d}(\gamma(s+t),\gamma_{i}(s+t))f(s)\leq R+M+22\delta
.

If
s+t\geq T
then

{\sf d}(\gamma(s+t),\gamma_{i}(s+t))\leq 2|s+t-T|+R+M+22\delta\leq 2|s|+R+M+22\delta,

implying
\sup_{s+t\geq T}{\sf d}(\gamma(s+t),\gamma_{i}(s+t))f(s)\leq\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}2|s|f(s)+R+M+22\delta
.

Therefore we conclude that
{\sf D}_{f}^{T}(\gamma,\gamma_{i})\leq r^{\prime}
. Hence,

(50)

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r^{\prime})\leq\varlimsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\log\textup{Cov}_{x}(C,\rho)}{\log\frac{1}{\rho}}=\overline{\textup{MD}}(C).

∎

Remark 7.4
.

The main idea in the proof of Proposition
7.2
is the following. While it is always true that
\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)\leq\overline{h}_{f}(C)
for every
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
by Proposition
5.1
, the opposite inequality can be false in general. However, if the function

(51)

r\mapsto\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C),r)

is constant then one can use a large enough value of
r
to obtain the opposite inequality
\overline{h}_{f}(C)\leq\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)
. This should be compared with what we did in Section
2.3
.

In general there is no reason for which the function (
51
) is constant. However, for the special subset
C=\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\textup{erg}}
we get that the associated function (
51
) is almost constant, with an error which is controlled by
h_{\textup{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
. This is enough to get the estimate of Proposition
7.2
. The almost constancy of the function is provided by Lemma
7.3
, which therefore plays a crucial role.

In order to clarify the idea behind the proof of Lemma
7.3
we first provide an easier version for spaces with bounded geometry.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, line-convex metric space with bounded geometry. Let
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
0<r\leq r^{\prime}
. Then

\lim_{T\to+\infty}\sup_{\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime}),r)=0.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. Let
S>0
such that
2|s|f(s)\leq\frac{r}{4}
for every
s\geq S
. Let
T\geq S
. Our goal is to bound from above the quantity
\textup{Pack}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime}),\frac{r}{2})
.
Let
\gamma^{\prime}\in\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime})
. By Lemma
3.3
we get that
{\sf d}(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))={\sf d}((\Phi_{t}(\gamma))(0),(\Phi_{t}(\gamma^{\prime}))(0))\leq r^{\prime}
for every
t\in[0,T]
. The triangular inequality implies that

{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(T+S),\gamma(T))\leq S+r^{\prime},\qquad{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(-S),\gamma(0))\leq S+r^{\prime}.

In other words, for every
\gamma^{\prime}\in\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime})
we have that
\gamma^{\prime}(T+S)\in\overline{B}(\gamma(T),S+r^{\prime})
and
\gamma^{\prime}(-S)\in\overline{B}(\gamma(0),S+r^{\prime})
. Moreover, suppose that two geodesics
\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime}\in\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime})
satisfy
{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(T+S),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(T+S))\leq\frac{r}{4}
and
{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(-S),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(-S))\leq\frac{r}{4}
. By line-convexity of the space we deduce that
{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s))\leq\frac{r}{4}
for every
s\in[-S,T+S]
. In order to estimate

(52)

\displaystyle{\sf D}_{f}^{T}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(s+t),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s+t))f(s),

we divide the second supremum in three parts.

If
s+t\leq-S
then
{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(s+t),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s+t))\leq\frac{r}{4}+2|s+t+S|\leq\frac{r}{4}+2|s|
, so

\sup_{s+t\leq-S}{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(s+t),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s+t))f(s)\leq\sup_{s+t\leq-S}\left(\frac{r}{4}+2|s|\right)f(s)\leq\frac{r}{2}.

If
-S\leq s+T\leq T+S
then
{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(s+t),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s+t))\leq\frac{r}{4}
and

\sup_{-S\leq s+t\leq T+S}{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(s+t),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s+t))f(s)\leq\frac{r}{4}.

If
s+t\geq T+S
then
{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(s+t),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s+t))\leq\frac{r}{4}+2|s+t-T-S|\leq\frac{r}{4}+2|s|
, because
T+S-t\geq S\geq 0
, so

\sup_{s+t\geq T+S}{\sf d}(\gamma^{\prime}(s+t),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s+t))f(s)\leq\sup_{s+t\geq T+S}\left(\frac{r}{4}+2|s|\right)f(s)\leq\frac{r}{2},

where the last inequality follows since
s\geq S
. Therefore,

{\sf D}_{f}^{T}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime})\leq\frac{r}{2}.

This means that if two elements
\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime}
of
\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime})
are at
{\sf D}_{f}^{T}
-distance bigger than
\frac{r}{2}
, then at least one of the two couple of points
(\gamma^{\prime}(-S),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(-S))
,
(\gamma^{\prime}(T+S),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(T+S))
are
\frac{r}{4}
-separated in
{\rm X}
.
This provides the bound

\textup{Pack}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}\left(\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime}),\frac{r}{2}\right)\leq\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}\left(\overline{B}(\gamma(0),S+r^{\prime}),\frac{r}{4}\right)\cdot\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}\left(\overline{B}(\gamma(T),S+r^{\prime}),\frac{r}{4}\right).

By (
5
) and the fact that
{\rm X}
has bounded geometry we get that the quantities

\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}\left(\overline{B}(\gamma(0),S+r^{\prime}),\frac{r}{4}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\textup{Pack}_{\sf d}\left(\overline{B}(\gamma(T),S+r^{\prime}),\frac{r}{4}\right)

can be bound from above uniformly in
T
and in
\gamma
. Therefore

(53)

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\sup_{\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime}),r)

\displaystyle\leq\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\sup_{\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Pack}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}\left(\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime}),\frac{r}{2}\right)=0.

∎

Remark 7.6
.

The line-convexity assumption in Lemma
7.5
ensures the following property. In order to have that
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
are close on the interval
[0,T]
it is enough to test their closeness at two times: at time
0
and at time
T
. This property fails for non line-convex spaces, like the example of Theorem
B
where its lackness gives origin to additional topological entropy.

Remark 7.7
.

Following the discussion of Remark
7.4
we observe that as a consequence of Lemma
7.5
we have the following fact. Let
{\rm X}
be a proper, geodesic, line-convex, Gromov-hyperbolic metric space with bounded geometry. Then for every
C\subseteq\partial{\rm X}
we have that
\overline{h}_{f}(C)=\overline{\textup{MD}}(C)
. This should be compared with the results of
[
Cav22
, §6]
.

Observe that in the proof of Lemma
7.5
, the fact that the metric space has bounded geometry has been used only once, namely to bound the cardinality of a separated set inside balls of fixed radius but different centers. The idea for Lemma
7.3
is to obtain such a bound using the fact that every geodesic in
\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}
is at controlled distance to a point of
\Gamma x
and to reduce the packing problem to balls with fixed center. The price to pay is that the balls have radii going to
+\infty
, and that is where the condition on the ergodic entropy of the action of
\Gamma
on
{\rm X}
plays a role.

Proof of Lemma
7.3
.

In order to simplify the notations we set
C:=\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}
.
Let
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);C)
. Let
S>0
be such that
2|s|f(s)\leq\frac{r}{4}
for every
s\geq S
. Let
T\geq\max\{(c+\varepsilon)n,S\}
. The same computations of the proof of Lemma
7.5
show that
\textup{Pack}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}\left(\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime}),\frac{r}{2}\right)
is bounded from above by

(54)

\displaystyle\textup{Pack}_{{\sf d}}\left(\overline{B}(\gamma(0),S+r^{\prime})\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C),\frac{r}{4}\right)\cdot\textup{Pack}_{{\sf d}}\left(\overline{B}(\gamma(T),S+r^{\prime})\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C),\frac{r}{4}\right).

The quantity
\textup{Pack}_{{\sf d}}\left(\overline{B}(\gamma(0),S+r^{\prime})\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C),\frac{r}{4}\right)
is bounded uniformly from above with respect to
\gamma
because
{\rm X}
is proper and
\gamma(0)
belongs to the fixed compact set
\overline{B}(x,R)
.

It remains to bound
\textup{Pack}_{{\sf d}}\left(\overline{B}(\gamma(T),S+r^{\prime})\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C),\frac{r}{4}\right)
from above. Let
z=\gamma^{+}\in\partial{\rm X}
and let
\xi_{x,z}
be a geodesic ray appearing in the condition
z\in\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}
. More precisely, the following conditions are true:

(i)

(c-\varepsilon)i\leq\vartheta_{i}\leq(c+\varepsilon)i
for every
i\geq n
.

(ii)

For every
i
there exists
t_{i}\in[\vartheta_{i},\vartheta_{i+1}]
such that
{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z}(t_{i}),\Gamma x)\leq\tau
.

By assumptions, we restricted to the values
T\geq(c+\varepsilon)n\geq\vartheta_{n}
. Then there exists
i_{T}\geq n
such that
T\in[\vartheta_{i_{T}},\vartheta_{i_{T}+1}]
and we have that

{\sf d}(\xi_{x,z}(T),\Gamma x)\leq|T-t_{i_{T}}|+\tau\leq\vartheta_{i_{T}+1}-\vartheta_{i_{T}}+\tau\leq 2\varepsilon i_{T}+c+\varepsilon+\tau\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}T+c+\varepsilon+\tau.

By Lemma
2.6
we know that
{\sf d}(\gamma(T),\xi_{x,z}(T))\leq R+8\delta
, so
{\sf d}(\gamma(T),\Gamma x)\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}T+c+\varepsilon+\tau+R+8\delta
.
We can now estimate

(55)

\displaystyle\textup{Pack}_{{\sf d}}

\displaystyle\left(\overline{B}(\gamma(T),S+r^{\prime})\cap\text{QC-Hull}(C),\frac{r}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\textup{Pack}_{{\sf d}}\left(\overline{B}\left(x,S+r^{\prime}+\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}T+c+\varepsilon+\tau+R+8\delta\right)\cap\text{QC-Hull}(\Lambda_{\text{erg}}),\frac{r}{4}\right).

Observe that in this inequality we replaced the set
C
with the larger set
\Lambda_{\text{erg}}
, which is
\Gamma
-invariant. In particular the set
\text{QC-Hull}(\Lambda_{\text{erg}})
is
\Gamma
-invariant too and we can translate our counting problem to a counting problem on the ball centered at

.

By definition of
h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)
we get that

(56)

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\sup_{\gamma\in\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);\Lambda_{\tau,c,\varepsilon,n}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}})}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Pack}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}\left(\overline{B}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{T}}(\gamma,r^{\prime}),\frac{r}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\textup{Pack}_{{\sf d}}\left(\overline{B}\left(x,S+r^{\prime}+\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}T+c+\varepsilon+\tau+R+8\delta\right)\cap\text{QC-Hull}(\Lambda_{\text{erg}}),\frac{r}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h_{\text{erg}}({\rm X};\Gamma)\cdot\frac{2\varepsilon}{c-\varepsilon}.

∎

We have all the ingredients to give the

Proof of Theorem
A
.

In view of Theorem
C
and the discussion at the beginning of the section, it is enough to show that (

) holds. So, we fix
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{1}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
. Proposition
7.1
gives

such that, for every

and

such that

. The relations

4.1
and Proposition
4.2
. We now prove that

h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\leq\overline{h}_{f}(\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}),

where
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Since
\Gamma
acts discretely and by isometries on
(\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),{\sf D}_{f})
, by Proposition
3.5
, then
{\sf D}_{f}
induces the quotient metric
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}
on
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
that restricts to a metric on
\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}})
. Let
K\subseteq\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}})
be compact. We claim that there exists
R\geq 0
such that
K\subseteq\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}})
. Indeed, if not, for every

converges to
[\gamma_{\infty}]

converges to

from above by

(57)

\displaystyle\sup_{R\geq 0}\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\textup{Cov}_{\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}),r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{R\geq 0}\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\textup{Cov}_{{\sf D}_{f}^{n}}(\textup{Geod}(\overline{B}(x,R);\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}),r),

because the projection map

(58)

\displaystyle\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}([\gamma],[\gamma^{\prime}])=\sup_{i=0,\ldots,n}\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}(\bar{\Phi}_{i}([\gamma]),\bar{\Phi}_{i}([\gamma^{\prime}]))

\displaystyle=\sup_{i=0,\ldots,n}\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}([\Phi_{i}(\gamma)],[\Phi_{i}(\gamma^{\prime})])

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{i=0,\ldots,n}{{\sf D}}_{f}(\Phi_{i}(\gamma),\Phi_{i}(\gamma^{\prime}))={\sf D}_{f}^{n}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})

for every
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
and every

.

By taking the limit superior over all real numbers instead of the integers, we obtain that

h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X};\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\leq\overline{h}_{f}(\Lambda_{\tau_{\varepsilon},c,\varepsilon,n_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Lambda_{\text{erg}}).

Finally, Propositions

and

give that

By taking the limit for

∎

8.
The proofs of Theorems

,
D
,
E

In this section we will present the proofs of the Theorems
B
,
D
and
E
. They will be mainly based on the computation of the topological entropy of the following model example. Let
F_{\ell}=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\rangle
be the free group on
\ell
generators. Let
T_{2\ell}
be the regular tree of valency
2\ell
, which is also the Cayley graph of
F_{\ell}
. The action of
F_{\ell}
on the geodesically complete,
\textup{CAT}(-1)
space
T_{2\ell}
is discrete and cocompact.

Lemma 8.1
.

We have that
h_{\textup{crit}}(T_{2\ell};F_{\ell})=\log(2\ell-1)
.

Proof.

Arguing as in
[
Cav23
, Proposition 5.7]
one gets that
h_{\textup{crit}}(T_{2\ell};F_{\ell})
coincides with the so called covering entropy of
T_{2\ell}
, and so with every notion of entropy of
T_{2\ell}
studied in
[
Cav22
]
. Choosing the volume entropy with respect to the
1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{1}
, we get that

h_{\textup{crit}}(T_{2\ell};F_{\ell})=\varlimsup_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\mathcal{H}^{1}(B(e,T)).

Since
\mathcal{H}^{1}(B(e,T))=2\ell\cdot(2\ell-1)^{\lfloor T-1\rfloor}(1+(2\ell-1)(T-\lfloor T\rfloor))
we get that

h_{\textup{crit}}(T_{2\ell};F_{\ell})=\log(2\ell-1).

∎

We compute explicitily the topological entropy of the dynamical system
(F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
, which equals
\log(2\ell-1)
by Theorem
A
and Lemma
8.1
. Indeed this computation serves as a model for similar ones that will be performed later.

Proposition 8.2
.

We have that
h_{\textup{top}}(F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=\log(2\ell-1)
.

Proof.

We need to parametrize the space
F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell})
. Let
\Sigma
be the set of symbols
\Sigma:=\{a_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,a_{\ell}^{\pm 1}\}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Sigma)
be the set of sequences
w=(w_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, with
w_{i}\in\Sigma
. To every
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Sigma)
we can associate a curve of
T_{2\ell}
in the following way. Remember that
T_{2\ell}
is the Cayley graph of the group
F_{\ell}=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\rangle
. Then each symbol
a_{i}^{\pm 1}\in\Sigma
is associated to the geodesic segment that joins
e
with the vertex
a_{i}^{\pm 1}
. The curve associated to
w=(w_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the curve associated to the infinite word
w
as element of
F_{\ell}
.
We call this curve
\gamma_{w}
. Notice that
\gamma_{w}
is a geodesic if and only if
w
belongs to the set
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma):=\{(w_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Sigma)\,:\,w_{i+1}\neq w_{i}^{-1}\text{ for every }i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. With ’red’ we mean reduced, since these sequences are exactly the reduced words in
F_{\ell}
.

Moreover, every geodesic
\gamma
of
T_{2\ell}
such that
\gamma(0)=e
is of the form
\gamma=\gamma_{w}
for some
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)
. Since
F_{\ell}
acts transitively on the vertices of
T_{2\ell}
we deduce the following. Every
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell})
is of the form
\gamma=g\Phi_{t}(\gamma_{w})
for some
t\in[0,1]
,
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)
and
g\in F_{\ell}
. This implies that every element
[\gamma]\in F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell})
is of the form
\bar{\Phi}_{t}([\gamma_{w}])
for some
t\in[0,1]
and
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)
.

Recall that the dynamical system
(F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
is compact, so we can use the metric
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}
, with
f(s)=e^{-|s|}\in\mathcal{F}
, to compute its topological entropy, by Proposition
4.2
.
For every
k<m\in\mathbb{Z}
we consider the set

\mathcal{A}_{k,m}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)=\{(v_{i})_{i=k,\ldots,m}\,:\,v_{i}\in\Sigma\text{ and }v_{i+1}\neq v_{i}^{-1}\text{ for every }i=k,\ldots,m-1\}
. We fix
0<r<1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
and we choose
k_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sup_{s\geq k_{r}-1}se^{-s}<\frac{r}{8}
. For every element of
v=(v_{i})\in\mathcal{A}_{-k_{r},n+k_{r}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)
we choose one element
w_{v}=(w_{i})\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)
such that
v_{i}=w_{i}
for every
i=-k_{r},\ldots,n+k_{r}
. We claim that the set

\mathcal{B}_{r,n}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\frac{2}{r}}\bar{\Phi}_{\frac{rj}{2}}(\{[\gamma_{w_{v}}]\,:\,v\in\mathcal{A}^{\text{red}}_{-k_{r},n+k_{r}}(\Sigma)\})

is
r
-dense in
F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell})
with respect to the metric
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}
. For that, we first compute

(59)

\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}([\gamma],\bar{\Phi}_{t}([\gamma]))=\max_{i=0,\ldots,n}\inf_{g\in F_{\ell}}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}{\sf d}(g\gamma(i+s),\gamma(i+t+s))e^{-|s|}=\max_{i=0,\ldots,n}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}te^{-|s|}=t

for every
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell})
and every
t\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Here we used that the systole of
F_{\ell}
on
T_{2\ell}
is
1
. Let now
[\gamma]\in F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell})
be arbitrary. We know that there exists
t\in[0,1]
and
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)
such that
[\gamma]=\bar{\Phi}_{t}([\gamma_{w}])
. We find
j\in\{1,\ldots\frac{2}{r}\}
such that
|t-\frac{rj}{2}|<\frac{r}{2}
and we define
[\gamma^{\prime}]:=\bar{\Phi}_{\frac{rj}{2}}([\gamma_{w}])
. By (
59
) we get that
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}([\gamma],[\gamma^{\prime}])<\frac{r}{2}
. Let now
v=(w_{-k_{r}},\ldots,w_{n+k_{r}})\in\mathcal{A}_{-k_{r},n+k_{r}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)
and
w_{v}\in\mathcal{A}^{\text{red}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Sigma)
be its associated element. By definition,
\bar{\Phi}_{\frac{rj}{2}}([\gamma_{w_{v}}])\in\mathcal{B}_{r,n}
. If we prove that
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}([\gamma^{\prime}],\bar{\Phi}_{\frac{rj}{2}}([\gamma_{w_{v}}]))<\frac{r}{2}
we conclude the proof of the claim, since then
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}([\gamma],\bar{\Phi}_{\frac{rj}{2}}([\gamma_{w_{v}}]))<r
. Observe that, by definition,
\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\Phi_{\frac{rj}{2}}(\gamma_{w_{v}})(t)
for every
t\in[-k_{r}+1,n+k_{r}]
. On the other hand for every
t
we have
{\sf d}(F_{\ell}\gamma^{\prime}(t),\Phi_{\frac{rj}{2}}(\gamma_{w_{v}})(t))\leq 1
because the diameter of
F_{\ell}\backslash T_{2\ell}
is
1
.
Therefore a raw estimate gives that

(60)

\displaystyle\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}\left([\gamma^{\prime}],\bar{\Phi}_{\frac{rj}{2}}([\gamma_{w_{v}}])\right)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i=0,\ldots,n}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}{\sf d}\left(\gamma^{\prime}(s+i),\Phi_{\frac{rj}{2}}(\gamma_{w_{v}})(s+i)\right)e^{-|s|}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i=0,\ldots,n}\sup_{s\in(-\infty,-k_{r}+1-i]}2|s|e^{-|s|}+\sup_{s\in[n+k_{r}-i,+\infty)}2|s|e^{-|s|}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{s\in[k_{r}-1,+\infty)}4|s|e^{-s}<\frac{r}{2},

because of the choice of
k_{r}
. Hence we proved that the set
\mathcal{B}_{r,n}
is
r
-dense in
F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell})
. So,

(61)

\displaystyle\textup{Cov}_{\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}}(F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell}),r)\leq\#\mathcal{B}_{r,n}=\frac{2}{r}\#\mathcal{A}_{-k_{r},n+k_{r}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)=\frac{2}{r}\cdot(2\ell)\cdot(2\ell-1)^{n+2k_{r}-1}.

Therefore

h_{\text{top}}(F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{2}{r}\cdot(2\ell)\cdot(2\ell-1)^{n+2k_{r}-1}\right)=\log(2\ell-1).

It remains to bound
h_{\text{top}}(F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
from below. For that, we claim that the set

\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\prime}:=\{[\gamma_{w_{v}}]\,:\,v\in\mathcal{A}_{-n+1,0}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)\}

is
\frac{1}{3}
-separated with respect to the metric
\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}
. Indeed, for every two distinct elements
[\gamma_{w_{v}}],[\gamma_{w_{v^{\prime}}}]\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\prime}
there exists
i\in\{-n+1,\ldots,0\}
such that
v_{i}\neq v_{i}^{\prime}
. Then

\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}([\gamma_{w_{v}}],[\gamma_{w_{v^{\prime}}}])\geq\inf_{g\in F_{\ell}}{{\sf D}}_{f}(g\Phi_{i}(\gamma_{w_{v}}),\Phi_{i}(\gamma_{w_{v^{\prime}}}))\geq\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{1}{4}}>\frac{1}{3}.

Here we used that
\inf_{g\in F_{\ell}}{\sf d}(g\gamma_{w_{v}}(i+\frac{1}{4}),\gamma_{w_{v^{\prime}}}(i+\frac{1}{4}))=\frac{1}{2}
.
Therefore, if
r<\frac{1}{3}
we necessarily have that

(62)

\displaystyle\textup{Cov}_{\bar{{\sf D}}_{f}^{n}}(F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell}),r)\geq\#\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\prime}=\#\mathcal{A}_{-n+1,0}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)=(2\ell)\cdot(2\ell-1)^{n-2},

and so

h_{\text{top}}(F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{2\ell}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})\geq\lim_{r\to 0}\varlimsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left((2\ell)\cdot(2\ell-1)^{n-2}\right)=\log(2\ell-1).

∎

Building on these computations we will provide the proofs of the remaining three theorems.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. We consider the metric space
{\rm X}
built in this way. We start with
T_{2\ell}
and for every couple of adjacent vertices we glue another edge of length
1
. Then
{\rm X}
is proper, geodesic, geodesically complete and metrically doubling up to scale
1
. It is also Gromov-hyperbolic being at bounded Hausdorff distance from
T_{2\ell}
, so quasi-isometric to it. However, it is not line-convex. As group of isometries we take
F_{\ell}
that acts preserving its action on
T_{2\ell}
and sending added edges to the corresponding added edges. This action on
{\rm X}
is by isometries, discrete, cocompact, non-elementary and of course
F_{\ell}
is torsion-free. Arguing as in Lemma
8.1
we get that
h_{\text{crit}}({\rm X};F_{\ell})=\log(2\ell-1)
. But, on the other hand, the topological entropy of
(F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})
equals
\log(4\ell-2)
. This follows by the same computations of Proposition
8.2
, once we parametrize the space
F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
. In this case, for every
a_{i}^{\pm 1}\in F_{\ell}
we denote by
\tilde{a}_{i}^{\pm 1}
the geodesic segment which was added to
T_{2\ell}
in the definition of
{\rm X}
and which joins the same endpoints of the geodesic segment associated to
a_{i}^{\pm 1}
. Then, with the notation of Proposition
8.2
one has to use the set of symbols
\Sigma:=\{a_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,a_{\ell}^{\pm 1},\tilde{a}_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,\tilde{a}_{\ell}^{\pm 1}\}
. Moreover every geodesic
\gamma\in\textup{Geod}({\rm X})
with
\gamma(0)=e
is of the form
\gamma=\gamma_{w}
for some
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma)
, where now

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\text{red}}(\Sigma):=\{(w_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Sigma)\,:\,w_{i+1}\neq w_{i}^{-1},\tilde{w}_{i}^{-1}\}
. The rest of the computations are identical and lead to the equality
h_{\text{top}}(F_{\ell}\backslash\textup{Geod}({\rm X}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=\log(4\ell-2)
.
∎

We continue with the

Proof of Theorem
D
.

We think again to
T_{4}
as the Cayley graph of
F_{2}=\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle
. Let
\tilde{a}_{1},\tilde{a}_{2}
be the axes of the isometries
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
. We consider the standard topological immersion of
T_{4}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with edges labelled by
a_{1}
parallel to the
x
-axis, and edges labelled by
a_{2}
parallel to the
y
-axis.
We denote by
R_{\theta}
the Euclidean rotation of angle
\theta
. Then

defines an isometric automorphism of

. We set

. A direct computation shows that
R_{\frac{\pi}{2}}a_{1}R_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{-1}=a_{2}
and
R_{\frac{\pi}{2}}a_{2}R_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{-1}=a_{1}
, so

and
[\Gamma:F_{2}]\leq\#\langle R_{\frac{\pi}{2}}\rangle=4
. In particular
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}(T_{4})
is discrete, cocompact and virtually torsion-free.
Theorem
A
, Lemma
6.1
and Lemma
8.1
imply that

h_{\text{top}}(\Gamma\backslash\textup{Geod}(T_{4}),\bar{\Phi}_{1})=h_{\text{crit}}(T_{4};\Gamma)=h_{\text{crit}}(T_{4};F_{2})=\log 3.

On the other hand we need to consider the action of

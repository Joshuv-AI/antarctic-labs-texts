Approximation of biholomorphic maps between Runge domains by holomorphic automorphisms
Franc Forstnerič

Abstract
. We show that biholomorphic maps between certain pairs of Runge domains in the complex affine space ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} , n > 1 n>1 , and in Stein manifolds with the density property, are limits of holomorphic automorphisms. This generalizes several results in the literature and provides a considerably simpler proof.

1.
Introduction

A holomorphic vector field
V
on the complex Euclidean space
\mathbb{C}^{n}

is said to be complete if its flow
\phi_{t}(z)
, solving the initial
value problem

\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{t}(z)=V(\phi_{t}(z)),\quad\phi_{0}(z)=z\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

exists for every
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Such a vector field
V
is also
complete in complex time
t\in\mathbb{C}
by
[
8
, Corollary 2.2]
,
and hence
\{\phi_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{C}}
is a complex 1-parameter subgroup
of the holomorphic automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The same holds if we merely assume that
V
is complete in positive time;
see Ahern, Flores, and Rosay
[
1
]
.

Let
\mathbb{B}(0,\epsilon)
denote the ball of radius
\epsilon
around
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
We say that the origin
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a

globally attracting fixed point
of
V
if
V(0)=0
and the following two conditions hold:

(1)

For every
\epsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0
such that

\phi_{t}(z)\in\mathbb{B}(0,\epsilon)
for every
z\in\mathbb{B}(0,\delta)
and
t\geq 0
.

(2)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\phi_{t}(z)=0
holds for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

A domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be invariant
under the positive time flow of
V
if
\phi_{t}(z)\in\Omega
for every

z\in\Omega
and
t\geq 0
.
Following Graham et al.
[
11
]
,
such a domain is called
spirallike
for
V
.
It was shown by Chatterjee and Gorai

[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
that a spirallike domain

\Omega
is Runge in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, that is, the restrictions
of holomorphic polynomials on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
to
\Omega
form
a dense subset of the space
\mathscr{O}(\Omega)
of holomorphic
functions on
\Omega
. In particular, every starshaped domain in

\mathbb{C}^{n}
is Runge (El Kasimi
[
6
]
),
and Hamada
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
proved this
for linear vector fields.

In this note we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
V
is a complete holomorphic vector field
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n>1
, with a globally attracting
fixed point
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the domain
0\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

is invariant under the positive time flow of
V
.
Then, every biholomorphic map from
\Omega

onto a Runge domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
can be approximated uniformly
on compacts in
\Omega
by holomorphic automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

By
[
2
]
, it follows that a biholomorphic map

F:\Omega\to F(\Omega)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
in Theorem
1.1

can be approximated uniformly on compacts by
compositions of shears and generalized shears.
We refer to
[
7
]
for a recent survey
of this theory.

For a starshaped domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, Theorem

1.1
coincides with
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]

due to Andersén and Lempert.
Theorem
1.1
generalizes the results of Hamada
(see
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]
in which the vector field
V
is linear)
and Chatterjee and Gorai
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
.
Their results give the same conclusion under additional conditions
on the flow of
V
, and their proofs (especially the one in

[
4
]
) are fairly involved.
The papers
[
12
,
4
]

include applications to the theory of Loewner
partial differential equation; see Arosio, Bracci and Wold

[
3
]
for the latter.

Here we show that Theorem
1.1
is an
elementary corollary to the main result of

[
10
]
,
and no additional conditions on the vector field are necessary.

We wish to point out that very little seems to be known about
globally attracting complete nonlinear holomorphic vector fields
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n>1
.
It was proved by Rebelo
[
13
]
that a complete holomorphic
vector field on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
has a nonvanishing two-jet at each fixed point.
It seems unknown whether such a vector field
can have more than one fixed point.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
V
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be as in the theorem.
By
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
,

\Omega
is Runge in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We shall prove
that every biholomorphic map
F:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}

onto a Runge domain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

is a limit of holomorphic automorphisms
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, uniformly on compacts in
\Omega
.

We may clearly assume that
F(0)=0
and the derivative

DF(0)
is the identity map.
Thus,
F
is a small perturbation of the identity near the origin.
In particular, choosing
\epsilon>0
small enough,
we have
\mathbb{B}(0,\epsilon)\subset\Omega
and
the image
F(\mathbb{B}(0,\epsilon))
is convex. Hence, the restricted map

F:\mathbb{B}(0,\epsilon)\to F(\mathbb{B}(0,\epsilon))
is a limit of
holomorphic automorphisms by
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]

(which is a special case of
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
).
Fix such an
\epsilon
.

Note that
\Omega_{t}:=\phi_{t}(\Omega)\subset\Omega

is Runge in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for each
t\geq 0
since
\phi_{t}\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
Assume first that the domain
\Omega
is bounded, so

\overline{\Omega}
is compact.
Conditions (1), (2) on
V
imply that there is a
t_{0}>0
such that

\phi_{t}(\overline{\Omega})\subset\mathbb{B}(0,\epsilon)
for all
t\geq t_{0}
.
Let
\psi_{t}:\Omega^{\prime}\to\Omega^{\prime}
for
t\geq 0
be the unique
holomorphic map which is
F
-conjugate to
\phi_{t}:\Omega\to\Omega
:

F\circ\phi_{t}=\psi_{t}\circ F,\quad t\geq 0.

Thus,
\psi_{t}
maps
\Omega^{\prime}
biholomorphically onto

\Omega^{\prime}_{t}:=\psi_{t}(\Omega^{\prime})=F(\Omega_{t})\subset\Omega^{\prime}

for every
t\geq 0
, and
\psi_{0}
is the identity on
\Omega^{\prime}
.
Since
\Omega_{t}
is Runge in
\Omega
for every
t\geq 0

and the map
F:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
is biholomorphic, we infer
that
\Omega^{\prime}_{t}
is Runge in
\Omega^{\prime}
,
and hence also in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Consider the family

(1.1)

F_{t}=F\circ\phi_{t}:\Omega\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{\prime}_{t},\quad t\geq 0.

This is an isotopy of biholomorphic maps from the Runge domain
\Omega

onto the family of Runge domains
\Omega^{\prime}_{t}
, with
F_{t}
depending
smoothly on
t
. Since

\overline{\Omega}_{t_{0}}=\phi_{t_{0}}(\overline{\Omega})\subset\mathbb{B}(0,\epsilon)

and the restricted map
F:\mathbb{B}(0,\epsilon)\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a limit of
automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the map
F_{t_{0}}=F\circ\phi_{t_{0}}
is
also a limit of automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. By

[
10
, Theorem 1.1]

it follows that every map in the isotopy (
1.1
), and in particular
the map
F_{0}=F:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
, is a limit of automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

The general case follows by observing that a domain

\Omega
in the theorem is exhausted by bounded
domains
\Omega_{0}\Subset\Omega
invariant
under the positive time flow of
V
. We leave the elementary proof
to the reader. Let
F:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
be a biholomorphic
map onto a Runge domain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Since
\Omega_{0}
is Runge in
\Omega
,

F(\Omega_{0})=\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
is Runge in
\Omega^{\prime}
, and hence
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The above argument then shows that

F:\Omega_{0}\to\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
is a limit of automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
∎

Remark 1.2
.

Chatterjee and Gorai stated the analogue of

[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
also for symplectic vector fields
on affine spaces
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
with the standard holomorphic
symplectic form; see
[
4
, Theorem 1.10 (ii)]
.
Note however that such a vector field is also volume preserving,
and hence it does not have any attracting fixed points.

A minor variation of the proof of Theorem
1.1

also gives the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a pseudoconvex Runge domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n>1
.
Assume that
V
is an
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-complete holomorphic vector field
on
\Omega
with a globally attracting fixed point
p\in\Omega

such that the flow
\{\phi_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
of
V
satisfies

\phi_{t_{0}}(\Omega)\Subset\Omega
for some
t_{0}>0
.
Then, every biholomorphic map
F:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}

onto a Runge domain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a limit of holomorphic
automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, uniformly on compacts in
\Omega
.

Proof.

The condition implies that the flow
\{\phi_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
of
V

on
\Omega
exists for all
t\geq 0
and satisfies

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\phi_{t}(z)=p
for all
z\in\Omega
.
Note that
\phi_{t}:\Omega\to\Omega_{t}=\phi_{t}(\Omega)\subset\Omega

is a biholomorphic map for every
t\geq 0
,
and
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}

is a continuously decreasing family of pseudoconvex domains
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which are therefore Runge in one another by
Docquier and Grauert
[
5
, Satz 19]
.
Fix
\epsilon>0
such that
\mathbb{B}(p,\epsilon)\subset\Omega
.
Since
\phi_{t_{0}}(\Omega)\Subset\Omega
is relatively compact
in
\Omega
, there is a
t_{1}>t_{0}
such that

\phi_{t_{1}}(\Omega)\Subset\mathbb{B}(p,\epsilon)
. Choosing
\epsilon>0

small enough, the argument in the proof of
Theorem
1.1
carries over.
∎

Replacing
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by a Stein manifold
X
with Varolin’s density property
(see
[
14
,
15
]
and

[
9
, Sect. 4.10]
), Theorem
1.3
still holds,
with the same proof. The relevant version of
the result on approximation of isotopies of biholomorphic maps
between pseudoconvex Runge domains in
X
by holomorphic
automorphisms of
X
is given by

[
9
, Theorem 4.10.5]
.
For a recent survey on Stein manifolds with the density property,
we refer to
[
7
, Sect. 2]
.

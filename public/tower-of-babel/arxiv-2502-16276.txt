On approximate Pareto solutions in nonsmooth interval-valued multiobjective optimization with data uncertainty in constraints Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
VU HONG QUAN 1, DUONG THI VIET AN 2, NGUYEN VAN TUYEN 3,∗

Abstract
. This paper deals with approximate Pareto solutions of a nonsmooth interval-valued multiobjective optimization problem with data uncertainty in constraints. We first introduce some kinds of approximate Pareto solutions for the robust counterpart ( RMP ) of the problem in question by considering the lower-upper interval order relation including: (almost, almost regular) ℰ \mathcal{E} -Pareto solution and (almost, almost regular) ℰ \mathcal{E} -quasi Pareto solution. By using a scalar penalty function, we obtain a result on the existence of an almost regular ℰ \mathcal{E} -Pareto solution of ( RMP ) that satisfies the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker necessary optimality condition up to a given precision. We then establish sufficient conditions and Wolfe-type ℰ \mathcal{E} -duality relations for approximate Pareto solutions of ( RMP ) under the assumption of generalized convexity. In addition, we present a dual multiobjective problem to the primal one via the ℰ \mathcal{E} -interval-valued vector Lagrangian function and examine duality relations.

3.
Approximate optimality conditions

For each
j\in J:=\{1,\ldots,p\}
, let
\mathcal{V}_{j}
be a nonempty compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}
,
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
. In this paper, we are interested in studying approximate solutions of the following multiobjective problem with multiple interval-valued objective functions:

(UMP)

\displaystyle LU-

\displaystyle\mathrm{Min}\;f(x):=\left(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{m}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}\ \ x\in\{x\in S\,:\,g_{j}(x,v_{j})\leq 0,j=1,\ldots p\},

where
f_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathcal{K}_{c}
,
i\in I:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
, are interval-valued functions and defined respectively by
f_{i}(x)=[f_{i}^{L}(x),f_{i}^{U}(x)]
with
f_{i}^{L},

f_{i}^{U}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
are locally Lipschitz functions satisfying
f_{i}^{L}(x)\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ f_{i}^{U}(x)
for all
x\in S
and
i\in I
;
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}
,
j\in J
, are uncertain parameters,

g_{j}(\cdot,\cdot)\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}\to\mathbb{R}
,
j\in J
, are given functions, and
S
is a nonempty and closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In what follows, for each
j\in J
, we assume that the function
g_{j}(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the following conditions:

(
\mathcal{C}_{1}
)

For a given point
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is
\delta_{j}^{\bar{x}}
such that the function
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}\mapsto g_{j}(x,v_{j})
is u.s.c. for each
x\in B(\bar{x},\delta_{j}^{\bar{x}})
, and the function
g_{j}(\cdot,v_{j}),v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}
, are Lipschitz with the same rank
K_{j}
on
B(\bar{x},\delta_{j}^{\bar{x}})
, i.e.,

|g_{j}(x_{1},v_{j})-g_{j}(x_{2},v_{j})|\leq K_{j}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\ \ \forall x_{1},x_{2}\in B(\bar{x},\delta_{j}^{\bar{x}}),\forall v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}.

(
\mathcal{C}_{2}
)

The multifunction
(x,v_{j})\in B(\bar{x},\delta_{j}^{\bar{x}})\times\mathcal{V}_{j}\rightrightarrows\partial_{x}g_{j}(x,v_{j})
is closed at
(\bar{x},\bar{v}_{j})
for each
\bar{v}_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}(\bar{x})
, where the symbol
\partial_{x}
stands for the limiting subdifferential operation with respect to variable
x
.

For studying problem (
UMP
), one usually associates with it the so-called robust counterpart:

(RMP)

\displaystyle LU-

\displaystyle\mathrm{Min}\;f(x):=\left(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{m}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}\ \ x\in\Omega:=\{x\in S\,:\,g_{j}(x,v_{j})\leq 0,\forall v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j},\ \ \forall j\in J\}.

We now introduce approximate solutions of (
RMP
) with respect to
LU
interval order relation. Let
\epsilon_{i}^{L}
,
\epsilon_{i}^{U}
,
i\in I
, be fixed real numbers satisfying

\sum\limits_{i\in I}(\epsilon_{i}^{L}+\epsilon_{i}^{U})>0
. Put
\theta:=\sum\limits_{i\in I}(\epsilon_{i}^{L}+\epsilon_{i}^{U})
and
\mathcal{E}:=(\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{m})
, where
\mathcal{E}_{i}:=[\epsilon_{i}^{L},\epsilon_{i}^{U}]
. For each

, put
g_{j}(x):=\max\limits_{v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}}g_{j}(x,v_{j})
. Then, one has

\Omega=\{x\in S\,:\,g_{j}(x)\leq 0,\ \ j\in J\}.

The
\mathcal{E}
-feasible set

is defined by

\Omega_{\mathcal{E}}:=\{x\in S\,:\,g_{j}(x)\leq\sqrt{\theta},\ \ j\in J\}.

Clearly,

.

For a given point
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the active indices in
\mathcal{V}_{j}
at

is denoted by

and defined as

\mathcal{V}_{j}(\bar{x}):=\{v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}\,:\,g_{j}(\bar{x},v_{j})=g_{j}(\bar{x})\}.

The following concepts of solutions can be found in the literature; see, e.g.,

.

Let

Example 3.1

Let
f:=(f_{1},f_{2}),
where

f_{1}(x)=[x_{1}-1,x_{2}+1],\ f_{2}(x)=[-x_{1},-x_{2}+2],\ \forall x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

and let

Consider the problem (

Then, one has

\Omega:=\{x\in S\,:x_{1}\leq 1,x_{2}\leq 1\}
.
For
\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}_{2}=\left[0;\tfrac{1}{2}\right].
Then,
\theta=1,

\Omega_{\mathcal{E}}:=\{x\in S\,:x_{1}\leq 2,x_{2}\leq 2\}.

Take

-Pareto solution of (

is not an almost
\mathcal{E}
-Pareto solution of (

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f_{i}(x)\leq_{LU}f_{i}(z)-\mathcal{E}_{i},\ \ &\forall i\in I:=\{1,2\},\\
f_{k}(x)<_{LU}f_{k}(z)-\mathcal{E}_{k},\ \ &\text{for at least}\ \ k\in I.\end{cases}

The first inequality of (

\begin{cases}f^{L}_{i}(x)\leq f^{L}_{i}(z)-\mathcal{E}^{U}_{i}\\
f^{U}_{i}(x)\leq f^{U}_{i}(z)-\mathcal{E}^{L}_{i}\end{cases}\Leftrightarrow\begin{cases}x_{1}-1\leq\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\\
x_{2}+1\leq 3-0\\
-x_{1}\leq-1-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\\
-x_{2}+2\leq 0,\end{cases}

x_{1}\geq\dfrac{3}{2}\\

x_{2}\leq 2,\end{cases}

which is impossible. Thus,

\mathcal{E}
-Pareto solution
of (

\mathcal{E}
-Pareto solution of (

), where
\varphi(x)=4x+1
and
\Omega=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x\geq 1\}
. It is clear that
z=0
is an almost
\theta
-quasi solution of (
P
) as

\varphi(z)\leq\varphi(x)+\sqrt{\theta}\|x-z\|\ \forall x\in\Omega.

Therefore, according to Lemma
3.1
we conclude that
z
is an almost
\mathcal{E}
-quasi Pareto solution (
RMP
).

4.
Approximate duality theorems of the Wolfe-type

For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
, put

\displaystyle L(y,\lambda):=(L_{1}(y,\lambda),\ldots,L_{m}(y,\lambda))=\left(\left[L^{L}_{1}(y,\lambda),L^{U}_{1}(y,\lambda)\right],\ldots,\left[L^{L}_{m}(y,\lambda),L^{U}_{m}(y,\lambda)\right]\right),

where

\displaystyle L^{L}_{i}(y,\lambda):=f^{L}_{i}(y)+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(y),\ L^{U}_{i}(y,\lambda):=f^{U}_{i}(y)+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(y),\ \ j\in J.

By Theorem
3.2
, for a given
\mathcal{E}
, there exists a KKT pair up to
\mathcal{E}
, denoted by
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
, of (
RMP
). We now consider the following dual problem of the Wolfe-type for (
RMP
) with respect to
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
:

(RMD)

\displaystyle LU-

\displaystyle\mathrm{Max}\ \ L(y,\lambda)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}\ \ (y,\lambda)\in\Omega_{D},

where the feasible set is defined by

\displaystyle\Omega_{D}:=\Big\{(y,\lambda)\in

\displaystyle S\times\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}\,:\,0\in\sum_{i\in I}[\partial f_{i}^{L}(y)+\partial f_{i}^{U}(y)]+\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}\mathrm{co}\,\{\partial_{x}g_{j}(y,v_{j})\,:\,v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}(y)\}+

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ N(y;S)+\sqrt{\theta}\mathbb{B},\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}},\ \ j\in J(\mathcal{E}):=\big\{j\in J:0<g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})\leq\sqrt{\theta}\big\}\Big\}

with
\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}:=(\lambda_{1\mathcal{E}},\ldots,\lambda_{p\mathcal{E}})
.

It should be noticed that approximate quasi Pareto solutions of the dual problem (
RMD
) are defined similarly as in Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
by replacing the relations
\leq_{LU}
and
<_{LU}
by
\geq_{LU}
and
>_{LU}
, respectively. For example, we say that
(\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})\in\Omega_{D}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-quasi Pareto solution
of (
RMD
) if there is no
(y,\lambda)\in\Omega_{D}
such that

L_{i}(y,\lambda)-\frac{\mathcal{E}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|y-\bar{y}\|\geq_{LU}L_{i}(\bar{y},\bar{\lambda}),\ \ \forall i\in I,

where at least one of the inequalities is strict.

The following theorem describes duality relations for approximate quasi Pareto solutions between the primal problem (
RMP
) and the dual one (
RMD
).

Theorem 4.1

(
\mathcal{E}
-duality)
.

Let
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
be a KKT pair up to
\mathcal{E}
of (
RMP
) and consider the dual problem (
RMD
) with respect to
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
. If
(f,g)
is generalized convex on
S
at every
y\in S
, then
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
is an
\mathcal{E}
-quasi Pareto solution of (
RMD
).

Proof.

On the contrary, suppose that
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
is not an
\mathcal{E}
-quasi Pareto solution of (
RMD
), then there exists
(y,\lambda)\in\Omega_{D}
such that

L_{i}(y,\lambda)-\frac{\mathcal{E}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|y-z_{\mathcal{E}}\|\geq_{LU}L_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}),\ \ \forall i\in I,

where at least one of the inequalities is strict, or, equivalently,

\displaystyle f^{L}_{i}(y)+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(y)-\frac{\mathcal{E}^{U}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|y-z_{\mathcal{E}}\|

\displaystyle\geq f^{L}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}),

\displaystyle f^{U}_{i}(y)+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(y)-\frac{\mathcal{E}^{L}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|y-z_{\mathcal{E}}\|

\displaystyle\geq f^{U}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}),\ \ \forall i\in I,

with at least one strict inequality. Hence

(20)

\sum_{i\in I}[f^{L}_{i}(y)+f^{U}_{i}(y)]+\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(y)-\sqrt{\theta}\|y-z_{\mathcal{E}}\|>\sum_{i\in I}[f^{L}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+f^{U}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]+\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}).

Since
(y,\lambda)\in\Omega_{D}
, there exist
z_{i}^{*L}\in\partial f_{i}^{L}(y)
,
z_{i}^{*U}\in\partial f_{i}^{U}(y)
,
i\in I
,
\lambda_{jk}\geq 0
,
x^{*}_{jk}\in\partial_{x}g_{j}(y,v_{jk})
,
v_{jk}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}(y)
,
k=1,\ldots,k_{j}
,
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{jk}=1
,
b^{*}\in\mathbb{B}
, and
\omega^{*}\in N(y;S)
such that

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}(z_{i}^{*L}+z_{i}^{*U})+\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{jk}x^{*}_{jk}+\sqrt{\theta}b^{*}+\omega^{*}=0,

(22)

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}},\ \ j\in J(\mathcal{E}).

By the generalized convexity of
(f,g)
at
y
, there exists
\omega\in[N(y;S)]^{\circ}
such that

(23)

\begin{split}&f_{i}^{L}(z_{\mathcal{E}})-f_{i}^{L}(y)\geq\langle z_{i}^{*L},\omega\rangle,\ \ \forall i\in I,\\
&f_{i}^{U}(z_{\mathcal{E}})-f_{i}^{U}(y)\geq\langle z_{i}^{*U},\omega\rangle,\ \ \forall i\in I,\\
&g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}},v_{jk})-g_{j}(y,v_{jk})\geq\langle x^{*}_{jk},\omega\rangle,\ \ v_{jk}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}(y),j\in J,\ \ \text{and}\ \ \\
&\langle b^{*},\omega\rangle\leq\|z_{\mathcal{E}}-y\|,\ \ \forall b^{*}\in\mathbb{B}.\end{split}

It follows from (
20
), (
21
), and (
23
) that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\langle-\omega^{*},\omega\rangle=\sum_{i\in I}[\langle z_{i}^{*L},\omega\rangle+\langle z_{i}^{*U},\omega\rangle]+\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{jk}\langle x^{*}_{jk},\omega\rangle+\sqrt{\theta}\langle b^{*},\omega\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I}[f_{i}^{L}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+f_{i}^{U}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]-\sum_{i\in I}[f^{L}_{i}(y)+f^{U}_{i}(y)]+\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{jk}[g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}},v_{jk})-g_{j}(y,v_{jk})]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sqrt{\theta}\|z_{\mathcal{E}}-y\|

\displaystyle<\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(y)-\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{jk}[g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}},v_{jk})-g_{j}(y,v_{jk})]

(24)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}\bigg[g_{j}(y)-\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{jk}g_{j}(y,v_{jk})\bigg]\!+\!\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}\lambda_{jk}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})\!-\!\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}),

where the last inequality comes from (
22
) and the fact that
g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}},v_{j})\leq g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})
for all
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}
and
j\in J
. Since
v_{jk}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}(y)
and
\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{jk}=1
, one has

(25)

g_{j}(y)-\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{jk}g_{j}(y,v_{jk})=g_{j}(y)-\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{jk}g_{j}(y)=0,\ \ \forall j\in J.

Clearly,

(26)

\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}\lambda_{jk}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})-\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})=\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{jk}\bigg)-\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})=0.

Now, combining (
26
), (
25
) and (
24
), we arrive at a contradiction. The proof is complete.
∎

In the following, we establish the converse-like duality relation between approximate quasi Pareto solutions of the primal problem (

) and its corresponding dual problem (

).

Theorem 4.2

(
\mathcal{E}
-converse like duality)
.

Let
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
be a KKT pair up to
\mathcal{E}
of (
RMP
) and consider the dual problem (
RMD
) with respect to
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
. Assume that
(y,\lambda)
is a feasible solution of (
RMD
). If
(f,g)
is generalized convex on
S
at
y
and the following condition holds

(28)

g_{j}(x)\leq g_{j}(y),\ \ \forall x\in\Omega,j\in J,

then there is no
x\in\Omega
such that

(29)

f_{i}(x)\leq_{LU}f_{i}(y)-\frac{\mathcal{E}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|x-y\|,\ \ \forall i\in I,

with at least one strict inequality.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that there exists
x\in\Omega
satisfying (
29
) with at least one strict inequality, i.e.,

f^{L}_{i}(x)\leq f^{L}_{i}(y)-\frac{\mathcal{E}^{U}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|x-y\|\ \ \text{and}\ \ f^{U}_{i}(x)\leq f^{U}_{i}(y)-\frac{\mathcal{E}^{L}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|x-y\|,\ \ \forall i\in I,

where at least one of the inequalities is strict. Hence

(30)

\sum_{i\in I}[f_{i}^{L}(x)-f_{i}^{L}(y)]+\sum_{i\in I}[f_{i}^{U}(x)-f_{i}^{U}(y)]+\sqrt{\theta}\|x-y\|<0.

Since
(y,\lambda)\in\Omega_{D}
, there exist
z_{i}^{*L}\in\partial f_{i}^{L}(y)
,
z_{i}^{*U}\in\partial f_{i}^{U}(y)
,
i\in I
,
\lambda_{jk}\geq 0
,
x^{*}_{jk}\in\partial_{x}g_{j}(y,v_{jk})
,
v_{jk}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}(y)
,
k=1,\ldots,k_{j}
,
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{jk}=1
,
b^{*}\in\mathbb{B}
, and
\omega^{*}\in N(y;S)
such that

(31)

\sum_{i\in I}(z_{i}^{*L}+z_{i}^{*U})+\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{jk}x^{*}_{jk}+\sqrt{\theta}b^{*}+\omega^{*}=0.

From the generalized convexity of
(f,g)
at
y
, one can find
\omega\in[N(y;S)]^{\circ}
such that

(32)

\begin{split}&f_{i}^{L}(x)-f_{i}^{L}(y)\geq\langle z_{i}^{*L},\omega\rangle,\ \ \forall i\in I,\\
&f_{i}^{U}(x)-f_{i}^{U}(y)\geq\langle z_{i}^{*U},\omega\rangle,\ \ \forall i\in I,\\
&g_{j}(x,v_{jk})-g_{j}(y,v_{jk})\geq\langle x^{*}_{jk},\omega\rangle,\ \ v_{jk}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}(y),j\in J,\ \ \text{and}\ \ \\
&\langle b^{*},\omega\rangle\leq\|x-y\|,\ \ \forall b^{*}\in\mathbb{B}.\end{split}

Combining (
32
), (
31
), (
30
), and (
28
), we get a contradiction

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\langle-\omega^{*},\omega\rangle=\sum_{i\in I}[\langle z_{i}^{*L},\omega\rangle+\langle z_{i}^{*U},\omega\rangle]+\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{jk}\langle x^{*}_{jk},\omega\rangle+\sqrt{\theta}\langle b^{*},\omega\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I}[f_{i}^{L}(x)-f_{i}^{L}(y)]+\sum_{i\in I}[f^{U}_{i}(x)-f^{U}_{i}(y)]+\sqrt{\theta}\|z_{\mathcal{E}}-y\|

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{jk}[g_{j}(x,v_{jk})-g_{j}(y,v_{jk})]

\displaystyle<\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{jk}[g_{j}(x,v_{jk})-g_{j}(y,v_{jk})]\leq\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{jk}[g_{j}(x)-g_{j}(y,v_{jk})]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{jk}[g_{j}(y)-g_{j}(y,v_{jk})]=0,

where the last equality comes from the fact that
g_{j}(y)=g_{j}(y,v_{jk})
for all

,
k=1,\ldots,k_{j}
, and
j\in J
. The proof is complete.
∎

5.
Quasi-
\mathcal{E}
Pareto saddle point

Let
\mathcal{A}:=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{m})
and
\mathcal{B}:=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{m})
, where
A_{i}
,
B_{i}
,
i\in I
, are intervals in
\mathcal{K}_{c}
. In what follows, we use the following notations for convenience.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle>_{LU}\mathcal{B}\Leftrightarrow\begin{cases}A_{i}\geq_{LU}B_{i},\ \ \forall i\in I,\\
A_{k}>_{LU}B_{k},\ \ \text{for at least one}\ \ k\in I.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\ngtr_{LU}\mathcal{B}\ \ \text{is the negation of}\ \ \mathcal{A}>_{LU}\mathcal{B}.

We consider an
\mathcal{E}
-interval-valued vector Lagrangian by setting

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lambda,y,\mu):=\left([F_{1}^{L}(x,\lambda,y,\mu),F_{1}^{U}(x,\lambda,y,\mu)],\ldots,[F_{m}^{L}(x,\lambda,y,\mu),F_{m}^{U}(x,\lambda,y,\mu)]\right),

\displaystyle F_{i}^{L}(x,\lambda,y,\mu):=f^{L}_{i}(x)+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(x)+\frac{\mathcal{E}^{L}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|x-y\|-\frac{\mathcal{E}^{L}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|\lambda-\mu\|_{1}

\displaystyle F_{i}^{U}(x,\lambda,y,\mu):=f^{U}_{i}(x)+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(x)+\frac{\mathcal{E}^{U}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|x-y\|-\frac{\mathcal{E}^{U}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|\lambda-\mu\|_{1},\ \ i\in I,

where
(y,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
and

\|\lambda-\mu\|_{1}:=\sum_{j\in J}|\lambda_{j}-\mu_{j}|.

Definition 5.1
.

A point
(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
is called a
quasi-
\mathcal{E}
Pareto saddle point
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(\bar{x},\lambda,\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})\ngtr_{LU}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})\ngtr_{LU}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\bar{\lambda},\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
be a KKT pair up to
\mathcal{E}
of (
RMP
). If
(f,g)
is generalized convex on
S
at
z_{\mathcal{E}}
, then
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
is a quasi-
\mathcal{E}
Pareto saddle point of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
such that

(33)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda,z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})>_{LU}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}},z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}).

This means that

F_{i}^{L}(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda,z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})\geq F_{i}^{L}(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}},z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})\ \ \text{and}\ \ F_{i}^{U}(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda,z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})\geq F_{i}^{U}(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}},z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}),\ \ \forall i\in I,

where at least one of the inequalities is strict. This means that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})-\frac{\mathcal{E}^{L}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|\lambda-\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})-\frac{\mathcal{E}^{U}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|\lambda-\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}),\ \ \forall i\in I,

with at least one strict inequality. Hence

\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})-\sqrt{\theta}\|\lambda-\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{1}>\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}),

or, equivalently,

(34)

\sum_{j\in J}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}})g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})>\sqrt{\theta}\|\lambda-\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{1}.

By definition of
J(\mathcal{E})
, we see that
\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}=0
and
\lambda_{j}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})\leq 0
for all
j\notin J(\mathcal{E})
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}})g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J(\mathcal{E})}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}})g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J(\mathcal{E})}|\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}|\sqrt{\theta}\leq\sqrt{\theta}\|\lambda-\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{1},

contrary to (
34
). This means that there is no
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
satisfying (
33
).

We now assume that there exists
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}},z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})>_{LU}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lambda_{\mathcal{E}},z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}),

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle f^{L}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle\geq f^{L}_{i}(x)+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(x)+\frac{\mathcal{E}^{L}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|z_{\mathcal{E}}-x\|

\displaystyle f^{U}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle\geq f^{U}_{i}(x)+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(x)+\frac{\mathcal{E}^{U}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|z_{\mathcal{E}}-x\|,\ \ \forall i\in I,

where at least one of the inequalities is strict. This implies that

\sum_{i\in I}[f^{L}_{i}(x)-f^{L}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]+\sum_{i\in I}[f^{U}_{i}(x)-f^{U}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]+\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}[g_{j}(x)-g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]+\sqrt{\theta}\|z_{\mathcal{E}}-x\|<0.

Since
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
is a KKT pair up to
\mathcal{E}
of (
RMP
), there exist
z_{i}^{*L}\in\partial f_{i}^{L}(z_{\mathcal{E}})
,
z_{i}^{*U}\in\partial f_{i}^{U}(z_{\mathcal{E}})
,
i\in I
,
\lambda_{jk}\geq 0
,
x^{*}_{jk}\in\partial_{x}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}},v_{jk})
,
v_{jk}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})
,
k=1,\ldots,k_{j}
,
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{jk}=1
,
b^{*}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, and
\omega^{*}\in N(z_{\mathcal{E}};S)
such that

(35)

\sum_{i\in I}(z_{i}^{*L}+z_{i}^{*U})+\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}\lambda_{jk}x^{*}_{jk}+\sqrt{\theta}b^{*}+\omega^{*}=0.

By the generalized convexity of
(f,g)
at
z_{\mathcal{E}}
, there exists
\omega\in[N(z_{\mathcal{E}};S)]^{\circ}
such that

(36)

\begin{split}&f_{i}^{L}(x)-f_{i}^{L}(z_{\mathcal{E}})\geq\langle z_{i}^{*L},\omega\rangle,\ \ \forall i\in I,\\
&f_{i}^{U}(x)-f_{i}^{U}(z_{\mathcal{E}})\geq\langle z_{i}^{*U},\omega\rangle,\ \ \forall i\in I,\\
&g_{j}(x,v_{jk})-g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}},v_{jk})\geq\langle x^{*}_{jk},\omega\rangle,\ \ v_{jk}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}),j\in J,\ \ \text{and}\ \ \\
&\langle b^{*},\omega\rangle\leq\|x-z_{\mathcal{E}}\|,\ \ \forall b^{*}\in\mathbb{B}.\end{split}

Combining (
35
) and (
36
), we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\langle-\omega^{*},\omega\rangle=\sum_{i\in I}[\langle z_{i}^{*L},\omega\rangle+\langle z_{i}^{*U},\omega\rangle]+\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}\lambda_{jk}\langle x^{*}_{jk},\omega\rangle+\sqrt{\theta}\langle b^{*},\omega\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I}[f_{i}^{L}(x)-f_{i}^{L}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]+\sum_{i\in I}[f^{U}_{i}(x)-f^{U}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]+\sqrt{\theta}\|x-z_{\mathcal{E}}\|

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}\lambda_{jk}[g_{j}(x,v_{jk})-g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}},v_{jk})]

\displaystyle<\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}\lambda_{jk}[g_{j}(x,v_{jk})-g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}},v_{jk})]-\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}[g_{j}(x)-g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J(\mathcal{E})}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}\lambda_{jk}[g_{j}(x,v_{jk})-g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}},v_{jk})]-\sum_{j\in J(\mathcal{E})}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}[g_{j}(x)-g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J(\mathcal{E})}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}\lambda_{jk}[g_{j}(x)-g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]-\sum_{j\in J(\mathcal{E})}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}[g_{j}(x)-g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})]=0,

a contradiction. The proof is complete.
∎

The following example shows that the generalized convexity of
(f,g)

on
S
used in Theorem
5.1
cannot be omitted.

Example 5.1
.

Let
f=(f_{1},f_{2})
be defined by

f_{1}(x)=[2x,2x+1]
,

f_{2}(x)=[-x^{2},-x^{2}+1],

S=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x\geq-3\}
, and
g(x,v)=-x-v+1,v\in[0,1]
.
Put
\mathcal{E}_{1}=[0,\frac{1}{2}],\mathcal{E}_{2}=[0,\frac{1}{2}]
.
It is not difficult to show that
(f,g)
is not generalized convex on
S
at
z=0
.
From the data, we get
g(x)=-x+1\leq 0
and
\Omega=\{x\in S:x\geq 1\}.

Consider at the point
\left({x_{\mathcal{E}}},{\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}}\right)=\left(0,4\right)
. By the direct computation, we have:

\displaystyle\partial f_{1}^{L}(x_{\mathcal{E}})=\{2\},\ \partial f_{1}^{U}(x_{\mathcal{E}})=\{2\},

\displaystyle\partial f_{2}^{L}(x_{\mathcal{E}})=\{0\},\ \partial f_{2}^{U}(x_{\mathcal{E}})=\{0\},

\displaystyle\partial g(z)=\{-1\},N(x_{\mathcal{E}};S)=\{0\},

and it is easy to see that
\left({x_{\mathcal{E}}},{\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}}\right)=\left(0,4\right)
is a KKT pair up to
\mathcal{E}
of (
RMP
).

We consider an
\mathcal{E}
-interval-valued vector Lagrangian by setting

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lambda,y,\mu):=\left([F_{1}^{L}(x,\lambda,y,\mu),F_{1}^{U}(x,\lambda,y,\mu)],[F_{2}^{L}(x,\lambda,y,\mu),F_{2}^{U}(x,\lambda,y,\mu)]\right),

\displaystyle F_{1}^{L}(x,\lambda,y,\mu):=2x+\frac{1}{4}\lambda(-x+1)+\frac{0}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|x-y\|-\frac{0}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|\lambda-\mu\|_{1},

\displaystyle F_{2}^{L}(x,\lambda,y,\mu):=-x^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lambda(-x+1)+\frac{0}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|x-y\|-\frac{0}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|\lambda-\mu\|_{1},

\displaystyle F_{1}^{U}(x,\lambda,y,\mu):=2x+1+\frac{1}{4}\lambda(-x+1)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|-\frac{1}{2}\|\lambda-\mu\|_{1},

\displaystyle F_{2}^{U}(x,\lambda,y,\mu):=-x^{2}+1+\frac{1}{4}\lambda(-x+1)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|-\frac{1}{2}\|\lambda-\mu\|_{1},

where
(y,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
and

\|\lambda-\mu\|_{1}:=|\lambda_{j}-\mu_{j}|.
At the point
\left({x_{\mathcal{E}}},{\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}}\right)=\left(0,4\right)
, one has

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(0,4,0,4)=([1,2],[1,2]).

We now consider the point
\left(x,\bar{\lambda}\right)=\left(-2,4\right)
, then

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(-2,4,0,4)=([-1,1],[-1,1]).

So, there exists
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})>_{LU}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\bar{\lambda},\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}).

In the other words,
(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})
is not a quasi-
\mathcal{E}
Pareto saddle point of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

We close this section by presenting converse-like duality relations for the quasi-
\mathcal{E}
Pareto saddle point of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
and almost
\mathcal{E}
-quasi Pareto solution of (
RMP
).

Theorem 5.2
.

If
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
is a quasi-
\mathcal{E}
Pareto saddle point of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
and
g_{j}(x)\leq g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})
for all
x\in S
and
j\in J
, then
z_{\mathcal{E}}
is an almost
\mathcal{E}
-quasi Pareto solution of (
RMP
).

Proof.

Since
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
is a quasi-
\mathcal{E}
Pareto saddle point of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
, there is no
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
such that

(37)

\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})-\sqrt{\theta}\|\lambda-\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{1}>\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}),

or, equivalently,

\sum_{j\in J}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}})g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})>\sqrt{\theta}\|\lambda-\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{1}.

If
z_{\mathcal{E}}\notin\Omega_{\mathcal{E}}
, then
g_{k}(z_{\mathcal{E}})>0
for some
k\in J
. So, we get

\displaystyle g_{k}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+\lambda_{k\mathcal{E}}g_{k}(z_{\mathcal{E}})>\theta+\lambda_{k\mathcal{E}}g_{k}(z_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq j}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+(1+\lambda_{k\mathcal{E}})g_{j}z_{\mathcal{E}}>\theta+\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}).

Choosing
\bar{\lambda_{k}}=1+\lambda_{k\mathcal{E}}
and
\bar{\lambda_{j}}=\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}},

for all
j\not=k
, one obtains

\sum_{j\in J}\bar{\lambda_{j}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})-\theta\|\bar{\lambda}-\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}\|_{1}>\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}}),

which contradicts (
37
). Therefore, we conclude that
z_{\mathcal{E}}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Now, we use the other inequality for a quasi-
\mathcal{E}
-Pareto-saddle point. Since
(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
is a quasi-
\mathcal{E}
Pareto saddle point of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
then there is not
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}},z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})>_{LU}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lambda_{\mathcal{E}},z_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}),

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle f^{L}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle\geq f^{L}_{i}(x)+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(x)+\frac{\mathcal{E}^{L}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|z_{\mathcal{E}}-x\|

\displaystyle f^{U}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle\geq f^{U}_{i}(x)+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j\mathcal{E}}g_{j}(x)+\frac{\mathcal{E}^{U}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|z_{\mathcal{E}}-x\|,\ \ \forall i\in I,

where at least one of the inequalities is strict.
However,
g_{j}(x)\leq g_{j}(z_{\mathcal{E}})
, for
j\in J
and
x\in\Omega
.
Thus, we conclude that there is no
x\in\Omega
such that

\displaystyle f^{L}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle\geq f^{L}_{i}(x)+\frac{\mathcal{E}^{L}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|z_{\mathcal{E}}-x\|

\displaystyle f^{U}_{i}(z_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle\geq f^{U}_{i}(x)+\frac{\mathcal{E}^{U}_{i}}{\sqrt{\theta}}\|z_{\mathcal{E}}-x\|,\ \ \forall i\in I,

with at least one strict inequality. In the other words,

z_{\mathcal{E}}
is an almost
\mathcal{E}
-quasi Pareto solution of (
RMP
). The proof is complete.
∎

6.
Conclusions

In this paper, we focus on studying approximate Pareto solutions of nonsmooth interval-valued multiobjective optimization problems with data uncertainty in constraints. On the one hand, we introduce some types of approximate Pareto solutions for the robust counterpart (
RMP
) of the problem in question. By using a scalar penalty function, we obtain a result on the existence of an almost regular
\mathcal{E}
-Pareto solution of (
RMP
) that satisfies the KKT necessary optimality condition up to a given precision. We then present sufficient optimality conditions under the assumptions of generalized convexity. On the other hand, we suggest establishing Wolfe-type
\mathcal{E}
-duality theorems for approximate solutions of the considered problem. We also consider the
\mathcal{E}
-interval-valued vector Lagrangian function and the quasi-
\mathcal{E}
Pareto saddle point.

354 | Development and Validation of a Clinical Model Predicting Successful Vaginal Delivery in Large‐for‐Gestational‐Age Pregnancies Study

Manal Massalha; Gharid Nourallah Bekdache; Bryon DeFrance; Kinga Malinowski; Jon F. Barrett; Eran M. Ashwal

McMaster University, Hamilton, Canada

Objective: To develop and validate a clinical prediction model estimating the probability of successful vaginal delivery in patients with Large‐for‐gestational‐age (LGA) fetuses who undergo a trial of labor, using routinely available clinical parameters.

Study Design: Retrospective single‐center cohort of singleton LGA pregnancies ≥34 weeks undergoing trial of labor (2011–2024). A multivariable logistic regression model predicting vaginal delivery was developed (2018–2024) using maternal and obstetric predictors with multiple imputation. Model performance was evaluated by bootstrap internal validation, temporal testing, and external validation, assessing discrimination, calibration, and overall accuracy.

Results: Among 876 patients in the development cohort, 668 (76.3%) achieved vaginal delivery. Independent predictors in multivariable analysis included: multiparous with prior vaginal delivery versus nulliparous (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 16.47, 95% CI 10.44–25.97, p < 0.001), pre‐gestational diabetes (aOR 0.42, 95% CI 0.23–0.78, p = 0.006), induced labor (aOR 0.55, 95% CI 0.36–0.84, p = 0.005), maternal age (aOR 0.94 per year, 95% CI 0.91–0.98, p = 0.003), and gestational age (aOR 0.84 per week, 95% CI 0.73–0.98, p = 0.025). Notably, birth weight percentile was not significant (aOR 0.98, p = 0.60). Model discrimination was excellent: apparent C‐statistic 0.847 (95% CI 0.817–0.877), optimism‐corrected 0.837, temporal validation 0.853, external validation 0.737. Calibration was excellent (Hosmer‐Lemeshow p = 0.933, slope 1.004). Decision curve analysis demonstrated net benefit across threshold probabilities 0.30–0.95. Among vaginal deliveries, LGA‐associated adverse outcomes included shoulder dystocia (16.3%), severe perineal trauma (8.8%), brachial plexus injury (0.6%), and neonatal bone fracture (0.3%).

Conclusion: This validated model accurately predicts successful vaginal delivery in LGA pregnancies undergoing labor. Prior vaginal delivery is the strongest predictor, while birth weight percentile adds little. It supports individualized counseling and shared decision‐making.

355 | Middle Cerebral Artery Peak Systolic Velocity and Neonatal Outcomes in Late‐Onset Fetal Growth Restriction

Manal Massalha1; Misgav Rottenstreich2; Bryon DeFrance1; Kinga Malinowski1; Jon F. Barrett1; Eran M. Ashwal1

1McMaster University, Hamilton, Canada; 2Shamir Medical Center, Zerifin, Israel

Objective: To evaluate whether the anemia‐derived middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity (MCA‐PSV) ≥1.5 5 multiples of the median (MoM) threshold predicts adverse neonatal outcomes in non‐anemic late‐onset fetal growth restriction (FGR). While elevated MCA‐PSV occurs in FGR via cerebral vasodilation, its prognostic value in this context remains unclear.

Study Design: Retrospective cohort of singleton late‐onset FGR pregnancies (Delphi criteria, ≥32 weeks) at Canadian tertiary centers (2017–2024), excluding anemia, major anomalies, and chromosomal abnormalities. MCA‐PSV was classified as elevated (≥1.5 MoM) or normal, and evaluated against a composite neonatal outcome (cord pH <7.10, Apgar <7, emergency cesarean, RDS, phototherapy, hypoglycemia, or neonatal death) using test characteristics, AUC, and multivariable analysis.

Results: Among 910 eligible cases (mean gestational age 36.7 ± 1.6 weeks), 63 (6.9%) had MCA‐PSV ≥1.5 MoM. The composite adverse outcome occurred in 50.8% of cases with elevated MCA‐PSV compared to 47.2% with normal MCA‐PSV (unadjusted OR 1.15, 95% CI 0.69–1.92, p = 0.603), representing an absolute difference of only 3.6%. Test characteristics were very poor: sensitivity 6.0%, specificity 93.9%, positive likelihood ratio 0.99, negative likelihood ratio 1.00. Adding MCA‐PSV to models that included gestational age and standard Doppler parameters provided minimal additional discrimination (ΔAUC 0.004, p = 0.024). In multivariable analysis, elevated MCA‐PSV was associated with a marginally significant inverse relationship to adverse outcomes (aOR 0.52, 95% CI 0.28–0.98, p = 0.043). Among individual outcomes, only respiratory distress syndrome was more common with elevated MCA‐PSV (14.3% vs 5.9%, p = 0.016).

Conclusion: MCA‐PSV ≥1.5 MoM does not predict adverse outcomes in late‐onset FGR (without anemia). It showed no diagnostic value (LR + 0.99; LR – 1.00), negligible model improvement (ΔAUC 0.004), and minimal real‐world outcome differences despite a statistical association. The anemia‐derived threshold should not guide surveillance or delivery timing in this population.

357 | Disparities in Maternal Mortality: The Case of Latin America and the Caribbean

Paola M. Calvo1; Arlene Calvo2; Humberto Lopez Castillo3

1Universidad Latina de Panamá, Panama city, Panama, Panama; 2University of South Florida, Panama City, Panama; 3University of Central Florida, Orlando, FL

Objective: To analyze maternal mortality ratios (MMR) across Latin America and the Caribbean (LAC) during a 10‐year pooled period by estimating intraregional heterogeneity.

Study Design: Descriptive analysis of LAC maternal mortality between 2013–2023. Countries with at least five years of reported maternal deaths and live births were included, based on data extracted from the PAHO Core Indicators Portal. Maternal deaths and live births were aggregated across the 10‐year period to calculate pooled MMRs using the standard formula, with 95% confidence intervals. Countries were grouped into tertiles of high‐, medium‐, and low‐MMRs to compare patterns and explore factors contributing to regional disparities.

Results: 30 LAC countries/territories were included. MMRs ranged from 13.83 (Trinidad and Tobago) to 229.1 (Saint Kitts and Nevis) with a 72.6 mean, 65.35 median, and 177.9 SD. Countries with high MMR were mostly smaller Caribbean states. Countries with the lower MMR tertiles were mostly located in South America.

Conclusion: Small countries, mostly from the Caribbean region, present elevated ratios, depicting smaller populations with limited resources. Larger South American countries had lower MMRs and presented more robust health systems. Reducing maternal mortality disparities in LAC countries demands sustained political commitment, strengthened health systems, and equitable resource distribution.

359 | A Moderate Intensity Exercise Program During Pregnancy and Health Related Quality of Life

Maia Brik1; Miguel Sánchez‐Polan2; Cristina Silva3; Paloma Hernando Lopez de la Manzanara4; Maria Angeles Diaz Blanco5; Aranzazu Martin Arias6; Joaquin Temprado7; Carmen Fortuny7; Ruben Barakat3

1Hospital Vall d´Hebron Institute of Research, Barcelona, Spain; 2Universidad de Comillas, Madrid, Spain; 3Universidad Politécnica de Madrid, Madrid, Spain; 4Hospital Puerta de Hierro, Madrid, Spain; 5Hospital Severo Ochoa de Leganés, Leganés, Spain; 6Hospital de Torrejón, Madrid, Spain; 7Hospital Vall d´Hebron, Barcelona, Spain

Objective: Health Related Quality of Life (HRQoL) refers to how a pregnant person perceives their physical, mental, and social well‐being, and it is a predictor of poor maternal and infant outcomes. The aim of the research is to investigate whether a physical activity program during pregnancy influences the HQQoL.

Study Design: Study design: a secondary analysis of a two‐branch multicenter randomized controlled trial was performed in five recruiting hospital centers in Spain. A population of 556 singleton pregnant individuals with no medical contraindications for exercising during pregnancy, was included.

Intervention included a moderate intensity physical activity program from 13–16 weeks to 39 weeks following Barakat´s Model (aerobic, strength, coordination, balance and pelvic floor), three weekly hybrid online and on‐site sessions. A comparison analysis of both groups at 34–36 weeks, and 6–8 weeks postpartum was performed.

The main outcome measures included HRQoL (SF‐12 questionnaire: Physical, PC, and Mental component, MC) and the secondary outcomes were sleep quality (PSQI questionnaire), disability (MODQ questionnaire) and obstetric outcomes (preterm birth, low birth weight and cesarean section).

Results: HRQoL scores were similar among groups. However, there was an improvement in the quality of sleep at the end of the pregnancy in the intervention group compared to the control (6.1 ± 3.1 vs. 7.2 ± 3.5, p = 0.015), and a trend of better perception of disability (12.6 ± 13.6 vs. 16.6 ± 15.2, p = 0.056) during postpartum. Participants with program adherence above 80% showed better PC Health‐Related Quality of Life compared to lower adherences (51.3 ± 7.7 vs. 42.7 ± 14.6, p = 0.029) during postpartum.

Conclusion: A moderate intensity physical activity program improves the quality of sleep and may affect the disability perception. In addition, when increased adherence to the program, it improves the PC Health‐Related Quality of Life during postpartum.

360 | Does Uteroplacental Insufficiency Mediate Adverse Outcomes Attributed to Social Deprivation?

Thomas Hanton1; Basia Chmielewska2; Asma Khalil3

1St George's University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, St George's University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, United Kingdom; 2St George's University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, London, United Kingdom; 3City St George's, University of London, London, United Kingdom

Objective: To determine whether area‐level social deprivation is associated with uteroplacental vascular resistance across pregnancy, assessed by mean uterine artery pulsatility index (UtA‐PI), and to assess the association of gestational age, ethnicity and hypertensive disorder of pregnancy (HDP) status.

Study Design: We performed a retrospective cohort study of 21,581 singleton pregnancies delivering at a large UK tertiary maternity unit between January 2020 and January 2025. Women were routinely offered first‐trimester dating (T1), second‐trimester anomaly (T2) and third‐trimester growth scans (T3). Mean UtA‐PI was analysed at each timepoint and Index of Multiple Deprivation (IMD) deciles were calculated from postcodes collected at T1. Prespecified secondary analyses examined HDP status and ethnicity.

Results: 14,108 women had UtA‐PI recorded at all three scans and a valid postcode. Mean UtA‐PI declined across gestation (1.502 at T1, 0.898 at T2 and 0.664 at T3), consistent with physiological reduction in uteroplacental resistance. There was little evidence that social deprivation materially influenced UtA‐PI at any trimester after accounting for gestational age. At T2, each increase in IMD decile was associated with only a 0.01‐unit difference in UtA‐PI, suggesting a statistically detectable but clinically small effect. In contrast, ethnicity showed a more consistent association: compared with White women, Black women had higher adjusted mean UtA‐PI at T1, T2 and T3, with similar but smaller differences in South Asian women. Women who developed HDP had higher blood pressure throughout pregnancy, but deprivation gradients in UtA‐PI remained modest.

Conclusion: In this large longitudinal cohort, social deprivation was not associated with a clinically meaningful difference in UtA‐PI across pregnancy. This suggests that socioeconomic inequalities in HDP may not primarily operate through altered uteroplacental Doppler resistance, and that other pathways may be more important. The persistent ethnic differences in UtA‐PI warrant further investigation.

Logistic regression adjusted for maternal age and ethnicity.

Conclusion: Greater socioeconomic deprivation was associated with increased odds of placental insufficiency, particularly HDP.

Objective: To evaluate whether vacuum‐assisted delivery (VAD) independently increases the risk of maternal postpartum infection, given conflicting evidence and ongoing debate regarding the need for prophylactic antibiotics.

Study Design: retrospective cohort study was conducted at a tertiary university‐affiliated center (2011–2023). Singleton vaginal deliveries ≥37 weeks were included. VAD cases were compared with spontaneous vaginal deliveries (SVD). To minimize confounding, cases with intrapartum fever, prolonged rupture of membranes (≥24 h), GBS colonization, intrapartum antibiotics, manual placental removal, severe perineal tears, or postpartum prostaglandin use were excluded. Postpartum infection was defined as fever ≥38°C within 2–48 hours requiring antibiotic treatment. Multivariable logistic regression was used to assess independent associations.

Results: Among 78,288 eligible deliveries, 6137 (8.0%) were VAD. Postpartum infection was more frequent in the VAD group compared with SVD (0.7% vs 0.2%, p < 0.001). Women undergoing VAD had higher rates of nulliparity, prior cesarean delivery, IVF, higher BMI, labor induction, epidural use, PROM, and prolonged labor stages. After adjustment for confounders, VAD remained independently associated with postpartum infection (adjusted OR 2.8, 95% CI 1.5–5.3, p = 0.001; Table 1.

Conclusion: VAD is independently associated with an increased risk of postpartum infection, despite overall low absolute rates. These findings support consideration of a risk‐based approach to antibiotic prophylaxis following operative vaginal delivery rather than routine administration.

Objective: The objective of this study was to assess the incidence and the risk factors of severe postpartum hemorrhage in women with posterior low‐lying placenta and prior cesarean without prenatal suspicion of placenta accreta spectrum.

Study Design: The study population is derived from the PACCRETA population‐based study conducted in the 176 maternity units of 12 French regions from November 2013 through October 2015. All women with posterior low‐lying placenta and prior caesarean without prenatal suspicion of PAS were included in this analysis. The main outcome measure was a composite criterion of severe PPH defined by either estimated blood loss of 1500 ml or more, transfusion of 4 or more units of packed red blood cells, embolization, or surgical treatment.

Results: Of the 520,114 women comprising the source population, 75 (0.14 per 1000 women; 95% CI, 0.11–0.18) met the inclusion criteria. Among these women, severe PPH occurred in 9 i.e severe PPH incidence of 9.3% (95% CI, 4.2–18.9). Unsuspected PAS was diagnosed at delivery in 4 women of the study population (5.3%). Conclusion: About 1 in 11 women with posterior low‐lying and prior cesarean without antenatal suspicion of PAS has severe PPH. Factors associated with severe PPH are unsuspected PAS diagnosed at delivery and 1–9 mm versus 10–19 mm placenta‐os distance.

365 | Pregnancy in Women Aged 49 and Above: Comparative Study on Psychosocial Follow‐Up, Maternal and Neonatal Outcomes

Anne Pinton1; Felicia Joinau‐Zoulovits2

1Hôpital Trousseau, Paris, France; 2Inserm (the French National Institute of Health and Medical Research), Paris, France

Objective: To assess the medical and psychosocial follow‐up, as well as obstetrical, neonatal complications and postpartum length of hospital stay, among women aged 49 years and older compared to women aged 25–35.

Study Design: We conducted a retrospective, observational, monocentric, case‐control study between 2016 and 2023 in a level III maternity unit. Women aged 49 years and older who gave birth were compared with a matched control group aged 25–35 years using a 1:2 ratio. Data was collected on demographics, pregnancy monitoring, psychosocial support, obstetric complications, delivery outcomes, neonatal issues and prolonged postpartum hospitalization.

Results: The study included 39 women aged 49 years and older and 78 controls. Advanced maternal age was significantly associated with higher rates of assisted reproductive technology (97% vs. 5%, p < 0.01), single parenthood (33% vs. 3%, p < 0.01), and psychosocial support during pregnancy (33% vs. 8%, p < 0.01). Obstetrical complications were significantly more frequent among women aged 49 years and older, including higher rates of preeclampsia (28% vs. 4%, p < 0.01), caesarean section (71% vs. 14%, p < 0.01) and postpartum hemorrhage (51% vs. 15%, p < 0.01). After adjustment, maternal age above 49 years remained strongly associated with prolonged postpartum hospitalization (aOR 4.24, 95% CI 1.14–17.4, p < 0.01) as emergency caesarean (aOR 6.17, 95% CI 1.39–31.8, p = 0.01) and preterm delivery (aOR 5.88 95% CI 1,41–33,3, p = 0.02).

Conclusion: Pregnancy at advanced maternal age (≥49 years) is associated with greater medical and psychosocial needs and increased obstetrical complications, highlighting the need for tailored prenatal and postnatal care.

366 | Malignancy Risk Among Term‐Born Offspring Following Antenatal Corticosteroid Exposure: A Population‐Based Cohort Study

Sapir Ellouk1; Tamar Wainstock2; Eyal Sheiner3

1Soroka University Medical Center, Beersheba, Israel; 2Department of Epidemiology and Health Services Evaluation, Ben‐Gurion University of the Negev, Beer Sheva, Israel; 3Soroka University Medical Center, Beer Sheva, Israel

Objective: Antenatal corticosteroids (ACS) are widely used to accelerate fetal lung maturity and reduce neonatal morbidity and mortality in the setting of anticipated preterm birth. Emerging evidence has raised concerns about potential risks when exposure occurs in pregnancies that ultimately result in term delivery. Prior studies have reported associations with small for gestational age and neonatal hypoglycemia; however, data on long‐term outcomes in term‐born children are limited. This study aimed to examine the association between ACS exposure and long‐term malignancy risk in term‐born children.

Study Design: We conducted a population‐based retrospective cohort study of singleton term deliveries from 1998 to 2021. Long‐term malignancy outcomes were compared between term‐born offspring exposed to ACS before 34 weeks of gestation and unexposed offspring, using integrated community and hospitalization databases. Cumulative incidence was evaluated using Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, and Cox proportional hazards models were applied to adjust for potential confounders.

Results: Among 182,626 term deliveries, 2093 offspring (1.1%) were exposed to antenatal corticosteroids (ACS) before 34 weeks of gestation. After adjusting for maternal age, gestational age, cesarean delivery, hypertensive disorders, and diabetes mellitus, ACS exposure was not significantly associated with overall malignancy morbidity (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR], 1.36; 95% CI, 0.91–2.04; p = 0.132).

Conclusion: Antenatal corticosteroid exposure before 34 weeks of gestation was not associated with an increased risk of overall malignancy in term offspring after adjustment for confounders.

367 | Gremlin 2 in the Placenta and its Role in Preeclampsia Pathophysiology

Lucy A. Bartho1; Jemima Seager1; Susan P. Walker2; Tuong‐Vi Nguyen1; Stephen Tong2; Tu'uhevaha J. Kaitu'u‐Lino1

1University of Melbourne, Melbourne, Australia; 2University of Melbourne Department of Obstetrics, Gynaecology and Newborn Health, Melbourne, Australia

Objective: Preeclampsia is a pregnancy complication caused by placental dysfunction. Gremlin 2 (GREM2) is a secreted protein essential for organ development. The role of GREM2 in the placenta and preeclampsia remains unknown. This study aimed to assess GREM2 as a potential biomarker for preeclampsia and characterise its function in the placenta.

Study Design: Maternal plasma and placentas were collected from women with early‐onset preeclampsia (< 34 weeks’ gestation), at 36 weeks’ gestation prior to diagnosis of term preeclampsia. GREM2 concentration was measured in plasma by ELISA, and GREM2 mRNA and protein expression were measured in placental tissue by RT‐qPCR and ELISA, respectively. Human trophoblast stem cells (hTSCs) were differentiated into syncytiotrophoblast cells over 96 hours to assess changes in GREM2 expression during differentiation. Functional studies involved overexpression and knockdown of GREM2, and measurement of anti‐angiogenesis, hypoxia and GREM2 associated genes.

Results: Circulating GREM2 was significantly elevated in 42 patients with early‐onset preeclampsia (< 34 weeks’ gestation, p < 0.0001) compared to 22 controls. GREM2 was not altered in plasma collected at 36 weeks’ gestation prior to preeclampsia diagnosis at term (n = 28 vs n = 92). GREM2 mRNA (p < 0.0001, preeclampsia, n = 79 vs control, n = 18) and protein (p = 0.002, preeclampsia n = 43 vs control, n = 20) expression were increased in placentas from patients with early‐onset preeclampsia. In hTSCs, GREM2 mRNA expression was significantly increased during differentiation into syncytiotrophoblasts (p = 0.04). GREM2 overexpression reduced expression of the antioxidant molecule thioredoxin in syncytiotrophoblasts (1200 pg/mL, p = 0.03), while GREM2 knockdown decreased cytotrophoblast marker CDH1 (p = 0.02).

Conclusion: GREM2 is differentially expressed in preeclampsia, with elevated circulating and placental levels in early‐onset disease. GREM2 is expressed in syncytiotrophoblasts and may regulate antioxidant defences in the placenta. Collectively, these findings give insight into GREM2 as a potential biomarker and its function in the placenta.

368 | Cyanoacrylate Glue vs. Subcuticular Suture for Cesarean Section Skin Closure: A Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis

Reid Rebinsky; Christopher M. Nash; Jocelyn Stairs

Dalhousie University, Halifax, Canada

Objective: To compare the risk of postoperative surgical site infection with the use of cyanoacrylate glue vs. subcuticular suture alone during skin closure at the time of cesarean section. Secondary objectives included comparing the risk of wound complications including hematoma, seroma, and skin separation between groups.

Study Design: We performed a prospectively registered systematic review and meta‐analysis of randomized controlled trials and cohort studies published from database inception until October of 2025. Cochrane Library, MEDLINE, EMBASE and Clinicaltrials.gov databases were searched. Two independent reviewers screened articles for eligibility. We included studies comparing surgical site infection in patients undergoing a transverse incision cesarean section who had received cyanoacrylate glue as an adjuvant to subcuticular skin closure vs. those who had received subcuticular suture alone. Random effects meta‐analyses were used to estimate risk ratios (RR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI). Statistical heterogeneity was estimated using the I2 statistic. The Cochrane risk of bias tool (RoB 2.0) and Newcastle‐Ottawa quality assessment scales were used to assess the included studies for bias.

Results: 2143 articles were screened for eligibility and 8 peer‐reviewed studies were included. 2005 participants were included with 1079 receiving cyanoacrylate glue and 926 receiving suture alone. There was a decreased risk of surgical site infection after cesarean section among those who received glue (RR 0.60; 95% CI [0.37, 0.97]) compared to suture alone. There was no difference in risk of hematoma or seroma (RR 2.34; 95% CI [0.67, 8.18]) or in risk of wound separation (RR 0.72; 95% CI [0.51, 1.01]) between groups.

Conclusion: Use of cyanoacrylate glue as an adjuvant to subcuticular skin closure at the time of cesarean section is associated with a decreased risk of surgical site infection compared to suture alone. Further research is required to assess whether using glue is a cost‐effective strategy to reduce surgical site infection.

369 | Early Pregnancy Steroid Hormones are Associated With Uterine Vascular Resistance, Placental Vascularization, and Hemodynamic Profiles

Lucrezia Viscioni1; Agnese Caneschi2; Chiara Lubrano2; Ottavio Cassardo2; Michele Orsi2; Elisa Sabattini1; Federica Locati3; Valentina Romagnoli4; Marina Tartarone4; Martina Zoppetti4; Irene Cetin3; Francesca Parisi3 On behalf of Università degli Studi di Milano, Fondazione IRCCS Ca' Granda, Ospedale Maggiore Policlinico, Milano, Italia

Ostetricia, Fondazione IRCCS Cà Granda Ospedale Maggiore Policlinico, Milano, Italy; 4Università degli Studi di Milano, Milano, Italy

Objective: To determine whether early maternal steroid hormone profiles are associated with uterine vascular resistance, placental vascularization, and maternal cardiovascular adaptation in pregnancy.

Study Design: Prospective cohort study including healthy women with spontaneous pregnancies. Maternal serum steroid hormones were measured at 5–7 weeks’ gestation. Principal component analysis (PCA) identified two endocrine profiles. Uterine artery pulsatility index (UtA‐PI) was assessed at 11–13 weeks. At 26 weeks placental vascularization was evaluated using 3D vascular index (VI), angiogenic balance by sFlt1/PlGF ratio and maternal hemodynamics using Ultrasonic Cardiac Output Monitor (USCOM).

Conclusion: Androgenic/luteal profile is associated with higher early uterine vascular resistance and a mid‐gestation phenotype with increased placental vascularization but reduced cardiac output, suggesting compensatory or inefficient angiogenesis. Estrogenic–glucocorticoid profile is associated with increased placental vascularization and a more favorable angiogenic balance. These findings indicate that early endocrine milieu critically shapes uteroplacental vascular development and maternal cardiovascular adaptations.

370 | Infant Feeding Type During Delivery Hospitalization and Early Postpartum Cardiometabolic Hospital Readmission

Matthew D. Mitts; Lisbet S. Lundsberg; Colleen Sinnott; Caitlin Partridge; Jennifer F. Culhane

Yale University, New Haven, CT

Objective: Breastfeeding (BF) is associated with improved maternal cardiometabolic outcomes, but its relationship with early postpartum (PP) readmission remains poorly understood. We examined whether infant feeding type during the delivery admission is associated with hypertension‐ (HTN) and diabetes‐related (DM) hospital readmission within 6 weeks PP.

Study Design: We performed a retrospective cohort study of all singleton, term deliveries in a hospital system from 2014–2024. Infant feeding recorded in the electronic health record during delivery admission hospitalization was categorized as BF (>90% breastfed) or mixed/formula feeding (MF) (≤90% breastfed). Neonates with feeding data outside minimum bounds or admitted to the NICU were excluded. Outcomes were HTN‐related inpatient readmission (IR) or emergency department readmission (ED) and DM‐related IR or ED within 6 weeks PP. Bivariate analyses identified variables (p < 0.05) for inclusion in multivariable logistic regressions.

Results: Among 97,277 deliveries, 48.6% were BF, and 51.4% MF. Patient attributes differed significantly by feeding type (Table 1). 1Sapienza University of Rome, Rome, Italy; 2Rutgers University, New Brunswick, NJ; 3McGovern Medical School at UTHealth, Houston, TX; 4Medical College of Georgia at Augusta University, Augusta, GA; 5ChristianaCare, Newark, DE

Objective: In low‐risk pregnancies at 37 to 41 wks, neonatal seizure (NS) occurs in 0.2/1,000 live births and is associated with adverse outcomes. There is, however, a paucity of data on neonatal and maternal outcomes in pregnancies complicated by hypertensive disorders (HDP) and subsequent neonatal seizures (NS). We hypothesized that among individuals with HDP at 37 to 41 weeks, NS, compared to no NS, are associated with significantly increased adverse outcomes in the neonatal‐maternal dyad.

Study Design: We conducted a population‐based retrospective cohort study using US vital statistics data (2016–2023). We excluded those without labor, delivery <37.0 or > 41.6 weeks, malpresentation, diabetes, multiples, anomalies, and no seizure data. The co‐primary outcome was composite neonatal adverse outcome (CNAO;5‐min Apgar <5, ventilation >6 hours, or neonatal death) and infant mortality. Secondary outcomes included composite maternal adverse outcome (CMAO; transfusion, ICU admission, uterine rupture, or hysterectomy). Multivariable Poisson regression models were used.

Results: Of 29.9 million births, 1.2 million (4.1%) met the inclusion criteria and among them NS occurred in 658 (5 /1,000 live births). Risk factors independently associated with NS included nulliparity (aRR 1.41), smoking (aRR 1.65), obesity (aRR 1.34), chorio (aRR 2.78), cesarean delivery (aRR 2.20), OVD (aRR 3.45), and male sex (aRR 1.52). CNAO occurred in 547.9/1,000 births with seizures versus 17.0/1,000 without (aRR 23.18, 95% CI 21.40–25.10). All individual components—5 min Apgar <5 (aRR 32.52) and neonatal death (aRR 109.56—were significantly higher with than without NS (Table1). CMAO was significantly more common (aRR 5.36, 95% CI 4.00–7.19), as were the components—ICU admission (aRR 9.49) and uterine rupture (aRR 40.11; Table 2).

Conclusion: Among pregnancies complicated by HDP, seizures, although uncommon, are associated with increased risks of neonatal and maternal adverse outcomes. While the data is useful for shared‐decision making, trials mitigating adverse outcomes may be problematic considering the infrequent nature of occurrence.

436 | Linking Maternal Risk Factors and Congenital Heart Disease Severity to Neurodevelopment: A French Multicenter Cohort

Betty Bretin1; Norbert WINER2; Valérie ROUGER3; Bénédicte Gaillard Le Roux4; Marie Demonceaux5; Thomas Moyon5; Alban‐Elouen Baruteau6; Véronique Ferchaud‐Roucher5 On behalf of the Eunice Kennedy Shriver National Institute of Child Health and Human Development Maternal‐Fetal Medicine Units Network and the Neonatal Research Network, Bethesda, MD, United States

1CHU Nantes, Nantes; 2Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, CHU Nantes, Nantes Université, Nantes, France; 3Loire Infant Follow‐Up Team (LIFT) Network, F‐44000 Nantes, France., Nantes, France; 4Nantes Université, CHU Nantes, Department of Pediatric Intensive Care, Nantes; 5Nantes Université, CHU Nantes, INRAE UMR 1280 PhAN, Nantes; 6Nantes Université, CHU Nantes, INSERM, CIC FEA 1413, Department of Pediatric Cardiology and Pediatric Cardiac Surgery, FHU PRECICARE, Nantes

Objective: Congenital heart disease (CHD) is the most common fetal malformation and a leading cause of childhood neurodevelopmental disorders (NDD) in children. While CHD severity is traditionally considered the main determinant of neurodevelopmental outcomes, increasing evidence suggests that the maternal environment during pregnancy may play a critical role. Indeed, an adverse maternal environment that leads to placental insufficiency may cause heart defects and brain damages in utero.

Objectives: We investigated the association between maternal risk factors, and long‐term neurodevelopment in children with CHD.

Study Design: We conducted a French multicenter retrospective cohort study including 334 women who delivered infants with CHD between 2012 and 2023. For a third of them, children underwent standardized neurodevelopmental assessments at ages 2, 5, and 7 years. CHD severity was categorized as critical‐complex, non‐critical complex, or non‐complex depending on anatomical defect and time to surgery. Multinomial multivariable logistic regression examined associations between maternal factors and CHD severity. Secondary analyses evaluated the relationship between CHD severity and NDD and assessed maternal predictors of NDD accounting for CHD severity.

Results: Maternal psychological distress (OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.18–0.87) and nulliparity (OR 1.77, 95% CI 1.08–2.90) were associated with lower odds of severe CHD. CHD severity was not associated with NDD at ages 2, 5, or 7 years. However, maternal characteristics including being younger than 25 or older than 30 years, multiparity, and gaining gestational weight more than 12 kg were associated with higher risk of combined CHD and NDD outcomes.

Conclusion: Our study is the first to demonstrate the associations between specific maternal environmental risk factors and NDD in CHD infants when considering CHD severity. These findings highlight the importance of maternal health and prenatal environment in shaping long‐term outcomes and support systematic neurodevelopmental surveillance for children with CHD regardless of lesion severity.

437 | RESUME: International Multicenter Registry on Fetal Therapy for Congenital Lower Urinary Tract Obstruction (LUTO)

Lauret A.M. Brinkman1; Monique C. Haak2; Sanne J. Gordijn3; Charlotte M. H. H. T. Bootsma‐Robroeks4; Valentina Gracchi4; Jaap Mulder5; Lisette 't hoen6; Rianne J. M. Lammers7; Caterina M. Bilardo8; Eva C. Weber9; Federica Fontanella8

1UMC Groningen, Groningen, Netherlands; 2Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Leiden University Medical Center, Leiden, Netherlands; 3UMCG, Groningen, Netherlands; 4Department of Pediatric Nephrology, University Medical Center Groningen, Groningen, Netherlands; 5Department of Pediatric Nephrology, Leiden University Medical Center, Leiden, Netherlands; 6Department of Pediatric Urology, Erasmus MC Sophia Children's Hospital, Rotterdam, Netherlands; 7Department of Urology, University Medical Center Groningen, Groningen, Netherlands; 8Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, University Medical Center Groningen, Groningen, Netherlands; 9Department of Obstetrics, Faculty of Medicine, University Hospital Cologne, Cologne, Germany

Objective: Fetal Lower Urinary Tract Obstruction (LUTO) is a rare congenital anomaly with significant perinatal morbidity and mortality, and long‐term renal and bladder dysfunction. Fetal interventions, including vesico‐amniotic shunt (VAS) placement and fetal cystoscopy, aim to reduce urinary tract pressure to improve survival and outcomes. Although VAS is the earliest established fetal intervention in medical history, its effectiveness in improving long‐term outcomes remains unclear. Moreover, evidence on optimal patient selection and timing of intervention is limited, and large international collaborative studies are lacking. The objective of the RESUME study is to investigate perinatal and long‐term (renal) outcomes in fetuses with LUTO treated with fetal intervention versus conservative management.

Study Design: RESUME is an international, multicenter, retrospective observational study including fetuses with LUTO who underwent VAS or fetal cystoscopy at any gestational age between 2004–2024, and a conservatively managed cohort. The primary endpoint is postnatal renal function (serum creatinine nadir within the first year of life) and secondary endpoints include gestational age at intervention, long‐term renal/ bladder outcomes, and complication rates.

Results: Currently, 42 fetal therapy centers worldwide have confirmed participation, establishing RESUME as the largest international collaborative study in fetal therapy for LUTO, but new centers are welcome to join until end 2026. We encourage international participation to maximize sample size, capture global variations in clinical practice, and improve generalizability.

Conclusion: The RESUME study represents an unprecedented international effort to address the uncertainty surrounding the effectiveness of fetal therapy for LUTO. In a rare condition where RCTs are challenging, this collaboration offers the best available evidence. By harmonizing data and expanding global participation, RESUME aims to clarify when, for whom and how fetal intervention is beneficial, guiding evidence‐based care and improving outcomes for children with LUTO.

438 | Glycemic Measures and Adverse Maternal Outcomes in Pregnancies Complicated by Type 1 Diabetes

Corinne Parker1; Erkan Kalafat2; Pepper Katherine1; Rebecca Horgan1; Jerri A. Waller1; George R. Saade1; Marwan Maayeh1

1Eastern Virginia Medical School at Old Dominion University, Norfolk, VA; 2Koç University Hospital, Istanbul, Turkey

Objective: To evaluate the association between glycemic variables on continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) and adverse maternal outcomes in patients with Type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1D).

Study Design: This was a retrospective observational study of all pregnant individuals with T1D using a CGM at a single tertiary care center between 2014 and 2025. Glycemic metrics assessed included time in range (TIR), defined as the percentage of time with a blood glucose of 60–140 mg/dL, mean glucose values, and first and second trimester HgbA1C. First and second trimester data was included in the analysis. The primary outcome was a composite of adverse maternal outcomes which included postpartum hemorrhage, thromboembolism, acute kidney injury, intensive care admission, and maternal death. Associations were tested using univariable and multivariable regression analyses.

Results: A total of 179 pregnancies amongst 169 patients were included. Patient clinical characteristics, including maternal age, race, body mass index, insulin pump use, and chronic hypertension were not associated with a difference in the composite of adverse maternal outcomes. In the multivariable model, first and second trimester CGM parameters, including TIR, time very high (>180 mg/dL), average glucose, and Hemoglobin A1c were not significantly associated with risk of our composite maternal outcome, nor were maternal clinical factors including age, body mass index, race, insulin pump use, and chronic hypertension (Table 1).

Conclusion: In pregnancies complicated by T1D, first and second trimester CGM metrics were not associated with risk of adverse maternal outcomes. These findings suggest that early pregnancy glycemic measures may have limited utility for maternal risk stratification, although confirmation in larger cohorts is warranted.

439 | Determinants of Adverse Maternal and Perinatal Outcomes in Pregnancies With Diabetes: A Retrospective Cohort Study

Aradhana Singh1; Rachan Kour2; urja sinha3

1All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Gorakhpur, Gorakhpur, India; 2All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Gorakhpur, Gorakhpur; 3Baba Raghav Das Medical College, Gorakhpur, India

Objective:

Primary Objective To identify clinical, demographic, and diabetes‐related determinants associated with adverse maternal and perinatal outcomes in pregnancies complicated by diabetes.

Secondary objective To determine independent predictors of adverse maternal and perinatal outcomes using multivariable regression analysis.

Study Design: A retrospective cohort study was conducted at a tertiary care centre from July 2024 to January 2026. Records of 297 pregnant women diagnosed with diabetes in pregnancy were reviewed. Variables analyzed included maternal age, BMI, parity, booking status, type of diabetes, gestational age at diagnosis, treatment modality, co‐morbidities, and family history. Primary outcomes included caesarean delivery, preterm birth, macrosomia, and stillbirth. Associations were evaluated using chi‐square analysis. Determinants were assessed using logistic regression.

Results: Of 297 women, 233 (78.4%) had GDM, 49 (16.5%) overt diabetes, and 15 (5.1%) pre‐gestational diabetes. Pharmacologic therapy was required in 121 (40.7%). Caesarean delivery occurred in 152 cases (51.2%). Preterm birth (< 37 weeks) occurred in 61 (20.5%), macrosomia in 36 (12.1%), and stillbirth in 2 (0.7%). Independent determinants of adverse outcomes included higher BMI (>24.9 kg/m2), requirement of pharmacologic therapy, presence of co‐morbidities, and unbooked status.

Conclusion: Adverse maternal and perinatal outcomes in pregnancies complicated by diabetes are influenced by interacting clinical and demographic determinants rather than diagnosis alone. Maternal adiposity, requirement of pharmacologic therapy as a marker of greater metabolic severity, and the presence of co‐morbidities independently predicted caesarean delivery, preterm birth, and macrosomia in this tertiary care cohort. These findings support a determinant‐based risk stratification approach to diabetic pregnancies. Early identification of high‐risk subgroups and implementation of structured, intensified antenatal monitoring may reduce preventable morbidity and improve maternal and neonatal outcomes in high‐burden settings.

440 | Maternal and Neonatal Outcomes at Periviable Gestations by Mode of Delivery

Kelly S. Gibson

The MetroHealth System, Cleveland, OH

Objective: Delivery between 20 and 25 weeks is uncommon. Mode of delivery may impact neonatal survival and maternal morbidity at these gestational ages. We sought to compare maternal and neonatal outcomes by mode of delivery at periviable gestations.

Study Design: A prospective 12 hospital multicenter cohort study of in‐hospital deliveries at 20w0d‐25w6d gestation with documented fetal heart rate on admission from May 2024‐April 2025. Postpartum maternal outcomes included hemorrhage, total blood loss, and blood product transfusion. Neonatal outcomes included survival to discharge and severe neonatal morbidity.

Results: Of 480 pregnant patients and their 535 fetuses, 200 were delivered by cesarean (CD) and 280 by vaginal delivery (VD); 452 (84.5%) were liveborn. Patients who delivered by CD were more likely to be multiparous, have adequate prenatal care, higher BMI, later gestational age and less likely to be in spontaneous labor. Vertical uterine incision was performed in 119 (60.4% of CD). CD were associated with a higher total blood loss (adj. median diff (95% CI) 440 mL (386–494)) and blood product transfusion (21 v 8.9%; aOR 2.53 (1.39–4.59)). CD was significantly more likely at 22–25w (54% of deliveries), while 97% of 20–21w deliveries were vaginal. Neonatal survival differed by week of gestation but not mode of delivery (0% at 20w to 75.6% via CD and 75.7% via vaginal at 25w; aOR 1.42 (0.82–2.44)). Antenatal steroid administration from 20–25w increased from 0 to 94% while intrapartum stillbirths decreased from 56 to 0%. Among all infants who survived to discharge, there was no difference in severe neonatal morbidity by mode of delivery (64.9 CD v 64.4% VD; aOR 1.42 (0.74–2.71)).

Conclusion: At 20–25 weeks’ gestation, CD was associated with increased maternal blood loss and a higher rate of vertical uterine incisions. Mode of delivery was not associated with morbidity amongst neonates surviving to discharge.

441 | Planned Early Birth Versus Expectant Management for Hypertensive Disorders From 34 Weeks’ Gestation to Term

Alice Beardmore‐Gray1; Christa Ingrid Rohwer2; Cristina Fernandez Turienzo1; Catherine Cluver3

1King's College London, London, United Kingdom; 2Gothenburg University, Sahlgrenska Academy, Gothenburg University, Sweden; 3Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Faculty of Health Sciences, Stellenbosch University and Tygerberg Hospital, Cape Town, South Africa

Objective: To assess the benefits and harms of planned early birth versus expectant management in pregnant women ≥34 weeks’ gestation with hypertensive disorders.

Study Design: We conducted a Cochrane systematic review to evaluate randomised controlled trials (RCTs) comparing planned early birth with expectant management for women ≥34 weeks’ gestation with hypertensive disorders. The critical outcomes were a composite of maternal mortality and morbidity, a composite of perinatal mortality and morbidity, maternal death, fetal death and neonatal death.

We searched four electronic databases and clinical trial registries to identify eligible studies. Two authors independently selected studies, assessed trustworthiness, extracted data, and assessed risk of bias of included RCTs. We conducted meta‐analysis where data was sufficiently homogeneous and assessed certainty of evidence using GRADE.

Results: We included six RCTs involving 3491 women. Four RCTs evaluated planned early birth between 34–37 weeks, one at 36–38 weeks, and one at 36–41 weeks. One RCT was conducted in low and middle income‐countries, and five in high income‐countries. Overall, risk of bias across outcomes was low in five RCTs.

Planned early birth reduces risk of maternal mortality and morbidity compared to expectant management with no increased risk of caesarean section. Planned early birth likely results in a large reduction in the risk of stillbirth and probably results in little to no difference in rates of neonatal unit admission. We found little to no difference in maternal death or neonatal death.

The evidence is very uncertain about the effect on overall perinatal mortality and morbidity due to high variation between trials.

Conclusion: Our review found that planned early birth compared to expectant management was beneficial across outcomes, with no important harmful effects. Further research should assess long‐term infant outcomes associated with late preterm birth and long‐term maternal cardiovascular health.

443 | Pregnancy Prolongation and Delivery Following Severe Mineblast and Openpenetrating Traumatic Brain Injury in First Trimestr

Iryna Kitova; Olha Fandieieva; Hanna Mostova

Dnepropetrovsk Regional Hospitai, Dnipro, Ukraine

Objective: Severe combined mine‐blast injuries in pregnant patients present complex clinical challenges, particularly when associated with open penetrating traumatic brain injury (TBI) during the first trimester.

Study Design: We report the case of a 14‐week primigravida who sustained a severe combined mine‐blast injury during ballistic shelling of a maternity hospital in Kamianske, Ukraine, on July 29, 2025. The dominant injury was an open penetrating TBI with intracranial hematomas and multiple shrapnel wounds. The patient required urgent neurosurgical intervention, including hematoma evacuation, removal of a metallic fragment, dural repair, and cranioplasty. Despite prolonged anticonvulsant therapy and multiple surgical procedures, a multidisciplinary team elected to continue the pregnancy. Close obstetric and neurosurgical monitoring was maintained throughout gestation.

Results: At 40 weeks’ gestation, spontaneous vaginal delivery resulted in the birth of a live male infant weighing 2310 g (length 46 cm). Both mother and newborn had satisfactory postpartum courses and were discharged home on day 4.

Conclusion: Severe combined mine‐blast trauma with open penetrating TBI in the first trimester is not an absolute indication for pregnancy termination. With maternal stabilization and coordinated multidisciplinary management, successful prolongation of pregnancy and delivery of a viable term infant are possible.

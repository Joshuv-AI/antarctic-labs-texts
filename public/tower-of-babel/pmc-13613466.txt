Alchemical Free Energy Perturbation Predicts Relative Binding Affinities of Propofol Analogs and Etomidate Stereoisomers at the GABAAR
Authors: Pritam Kumar Panda, Edward J. Bertaccini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
General anesthetics such as propofol and etomidate exert their clinical effects primarily through positive allosteric modulation of γ-aminobutyric acid type A receptors (GABAAR). Despite decades of research, the quantitative structure–activity relationships governing anesthetic binding remain incompletely characterized at the atomic level. Here, we employ rigorous alchemical free energy perturbation (FEP) calculations using a CHARMm GPU-accelerated protocol to compute relative binding free energies for a series of 13 propofol analogs at the wild-type GABAAR, as well as for the stereoisomeric pair of etomidate (R-etomidate vs S-etomidate). For propofol analogs at the wild-type receptor, computed relative binding free energies (ΔΔG) correlate with experimentally measured GABA EC 50 potentiation values, with propofol (2,6-diisopropylphenol) and disec-butylphenol predicted as the most potent analogs, consistent with experimental data (Pearson r = 0.85, 95% CI 0.50–0.96, p = 0.001; mean of three independent runs). For etomidate, FEP calculations correctly predict that R-etomidate binds approximately 1.8 kcal/mol more favorably than S-etomidate, corresponding to a ∼10-fold difference in potency consistent with the known stereoselective pharmacology of this agent. These results demonstrate that alchemical FEP calculations, implemented through a standardized computational protocol, can quantitatively rank-order anesthetic binding affinities and discriminate stereoisomeric preferences at Cys-loop receptor binding sites, providing a framework for rational design of next-generation anesthetic agents that is more quantitatively robust than simple molecular docking methodologies.

Introduction

General anesthetics have been used clinically for nearly two centuries, yet the molecular mechanisms by which these structurally diverse agents produce unconsciousness remain areas of active investigation. Among the most widely used intravenous anesthetics are propofol (2,6-diisopropylphenol) and etomidate (R-1-(1-phenylethyl)-1H-imidazole-5-carboxylic acid ethyl ester), both of which exert their hypnotic effects primarily through positive allosteric modulation of the γ-aminobutyric acid type A receptor (GABAAR), the principal supratentorial inhibitory ligand-gated ion channel in the mammalian central nervous system. −

The GABAAR is a pentameric ligand-gated ion channel (pLGIC) belonging to the Cys-loop superfamily. The most common synaptic isoform is comprised of two α1, two β2 or β3, and one γ2 subunit arranged pseudosymmetrically around a central chloride-selective pore. General anesthetics bind at intersubunit sites located within the outer third of the transmembrane domain between four-helix bundles, distinct from the orthosteric GABA neurotransmitter binding site located at the extracellular β-α interface between β sheets. , The determination of high-resolution cryo-EM structures of GABAAR in complex with various modulators, including the α1β2γ2 receptor bound to propofol and etomidate (PDB: 6X3T, 6X3V), has provided unprecedented atomic-level detail of anesthetic binding sites.

Structure–activity relationship (SAR) studies of propofol analogs have established that the 2,6-disubstituted phenol scaffold is critical for anesthetic potency. , Systematic variation of substituents at the 2, 4, 5, and 6 positions of phenol, as well as modifications to the hydroxyl group, have revealed that bulky alkyl groups at the 2,6 positions enhance potency, while removal of either substituent or the hydroxyl group diminishes activity. These experimental observations, derived from GABA EC 50 potentiation assays in oocytes expressing recombinant GABAAR, provide a rich data set for computational validation.

Etomidate exhibits pronounced stereoselectivity, with the R-(+) enantiomer displaying approximately an order-of-magnitude greater potency than the S-(−) enantiomer at the GABAAR. This marked enantioselectivity provides a stringent benchmark for computational binding predictions, as accurate models must not only reproduce binding affinity but also correctly resolve subtle three-dimensional stereochemical constraints within the receptor binding pocket. In particular, molecular docking does not adequately describe such subtle differences.

From a structural perspective, this selectivity likely arises from highly specific ligand–receptor interactions within the transmembrane domain interface, where the orientation of the imidazole ring and ester moiety governs hydrogen bonding, π–stacking, and hydrophobic packing interactions. The R-(+) configuration is thought to achieve optimal complementarity with these microenvironments, whereas the S-(−) enantiomer adopts suboptimal geometries that weaken stabilizing interactions. ,

Importantly, the ability to discriminate between enantiomers at this level highlights the sensitivity of GABAAR allosteric sites to fine stereochemical features, reinforcing the requirement for high-resolution free energy calculations. Consequently, reproducing the experimentally observed stereoselectivity of etomidate serves as a critical validation criterion for computational frameworks aimed at predicting ligand efficacy and binding modes in pentameric ligand-gated ion channels.

Free energy perturbation (FEP) is a rigorous statistical mechanical method for computing relative binding free energies between chemically related ligands. , By constructing a thermodynamic cycle in which one ligand is alchemically transformed into another in both the solvated and protein-bound states, FEP avoids the need to compute absolute binding free energies (ΔG) and instead calculates the relative difference (ΔΔG). Modern implementations of FEP employ dual-topology representations, stratified lambda scheduling, and GPU-accelerated molecular dynamics to achieve chemical accuracy (errors within 1–2 kcal/mol) for congeneric series of drug-like molecules. , In particular, the compilation of such algorithms to run on highly parallelized GPU-based systems allows for far more efficient calculations than ever before when only CPUs could be employed.

In this work, we apply the CHARMm GPU-accelerated relative FEP protocol implemented in BIOVIA Discovery Studio 2023 to systematically evaluate the binding of 13 propofol analogs at the wild-type GABAAR (6X3T structure), as well as the stereoisomeric preference of etomidate (6X3V structure). We compare computed relative binding free energies with experimental GABA EC 50 potentiation data and CDOCKER docking energies, assess reproducibility across independent computational setups. Our results demonstrate that alchemical FEP calculations provide quantitative predictions of anesthetic structure–activity relationships that are more quantitively consistent with experimental pharmacology and offer mechanistic insight into the molecular basis of anesthetic selectivity.

Methods

Receptor Structure Preparation

The cryo-EM structures of the human α1-β2-γ2 GABAAR in complex with propofol (PDB ID: 6X3T and 6X3V) were used as the starting structure for propofol and etomidate calculations, respectively (Figure ). The structures were prepared using the Prepare Protein protocol in Discovery Studio 2023 SP1 (BIOVIA, Dassault Systemes), which includes insertion of missing atoms and assignment of protonation states at physiological pH. Energy minimization of added hydrogen atoms was then performed as a separate step following the Prepare Protein protocol. The propofol molecule cocrystallized in the β+/α- transmembrane intersubunit site was retained as the reference ligand for analog placement.

Ligand Preparation

A congeneric series of 13 propofol analogs was assembled based on prior experimental SAR data , (Figure ). The 13 analogs and their abbreviations are given in Figure . For etomidate, the R and S enantiomers (R-etomidate and S-etomidate, respectively) were prepared. All ligands were constructed in Discovery Studio using the Build Fragment and Clean Geometry tools, followed by hydrogen addition and geometry optimization.

Phenol is the structural reference for the highlighting: in each analog, atoms and bonds that differ from phenol are shown in red. For the etomidate enantiomers, the chiral center is highlighted in blue and its configuration annotated (R)/(S). The series was designed around propofol (2,6-diisopropylphenol) as the parent anesthetic scaffold. Symmetric and asymmetric 2,6-dialkyl phenols (methyl, ethyl, isopropyl, sec-butyl, tert-butyl/methyl) vary the size and branching of the ortho substituents to probe hydrophobic packing in the binding subpocket; 3,5-disubstituted analogs (3,5-diisopropylcatechol, 3,5-ditert-butylphenol) displace the alkyl groups from the 2,6 geometries; and the 4-iodo derivative tests para-substitution. Headgroup variants in which the phenolic hydroxyl is replaced by bromide, isocyanate, or isothiocyanate probe the role of the hydrogen-bond donor. The R- and S-etomidate enantiomers are included as an independent, stereochemically defined comparison to the phenol series.

Force Field Assignment

All ligands and the receptor were typed using the CHARMm force field (charmm36 parameter set) within Discovery Studio 2023. The Type Ligands protocol was employed for consistent force field assignment across the congeneric series. Force field typing for FEP requires special treatment due to the dual-topology representation; only the Set Up Relative FEP Calculations protocol was used for final typing to ensure compatibility.

FEP Pair Generation

Optimal ligand pairs for FEP calculations were generated using the Generate Ligand Pairs for FEP protocol, which constructs a minimum spanning tree based on chemical and topological similarity between ligands. Each pair required a minimum of 20% atom similarity. For the propofol series, 12 ligand pairs were generated with diisopropylphenol (propofol) as the reference compound. The alchemical R → S transformation workflow and the underlying thermodynamic cycle are illustrated in Figure S1. Similarly, for etomidate 1 ligand pair (R & S enantiomers) was prepared with R as the reference compound. The pair connectivity network ensures that relative binding free energies for all compounds can be computed by traversing the graph from the reference ligand.

System Setup for FEP

The Set Up Relative FEP Calculations protocol was used to generate dual-topology systems for each ligand pair. This protocol creates a merged molecule containing the common core of both ligands plus two groups: FEP_0 (atoms unique to Ligand 1 that will disappear during the alchemical transformation) and FEP_1 (atoms unique to Ligand 2 that will appear). For each pair, two systems were generated:1.Solvated ligand pair: The dual-topology ligand pair solvated in explicit water (TIP3P model) in a periodic orthorhombic box.2.Solvated protein–ligand complex: The dual-topology ligand pair bound within the receptor, solvated in explicit water with periodic boundary conditions.

Free Energy Perturbation Simulations

All FEP simulations were performed using the CHARMm Relative FEP Calculations (GPU) protocol in Discovery Studio 2023 SP1, running on the CHARMm DOMDEC GPU platform. The following simulation parameters were employed: Number of lambda windows: 15 (uniformly spaced from 0 to 1); Integration time step: 2 fs, with SHAKE constraints on bonds to hydrogen; Simulation time per lambda window: 500 ps (production); Equilibration time per lambda window: 200 ps; Ensemble: NPT (constant number of particles, pressure, and temperature); Temperature: 300 K; Temperature control: Langevin dynamics thermostat; Pressure control: Langevin piston barostat at 1 atm; Total simulation time per pair: 15­(500 + 200) = 10,500 ps = 10.5 ns. Nonbonded interactions were treated using a force-switching function, with a van der Waals switching distance of 10 Å and a cutoff of 12 Å, while long-range electrostatics were handled using the particle mesh Ewald (PME) method.

During the alchemical transformation, the lambda parameter varied from 0 (initial state, Ligand 1) to 1 (final state, Ligand 2) with van der Waals and electrostatic interactions treated accordingly with the atoms gradually turning off and on, respectively, across the lambda schedule. FEP data collected during the equilibration phase was discarded and not used for free energy calculations. For each ligand pair, two independent FEP simulations were performed: one for the solvated ligand pair (yielding ΔG ligand) and one for the solvated protein–ligand complex (yielding ΔG complex). Both forward (lambda: 0 to 1) and reverse (lambda: 1 to 0) simulations were conducted to assess convergence through hysteresis analysis.

Relative Binding Free Energy Calculation

The relative binding free energy (ΔΔG) for transforming Ligand 1 into Ligand 2 was computed from the thermodynamic cycle 3 ΔΔG=ΔGcomplex−ΔGligand where ΔG complex is the alchemical free energy of the transformation in the protein environment and ΔG ligand is the alchemical free energy in bulk solvent (Figure ). A positive ΔΔG indicates that Ligand 2 binds less favorably than Ligand 1. Overall relative binding free energies (ΔG) for each compound were obtained by setting propofol (diisopropylphenol) as the reference ligand (ΔΔG = 0 kcal/mol) and propagating the pairwise ΔΔG values through the connectivity network using the Collate FEP Simulation Results protocol.

Thermodynamic cycle underlying free energy perturbation (FEP) calculations utilizes simple conservation of energy principles as shown in the equation. Relative binding free energies (ΔΔG) are obtained by constructing a closed thermodynamic cycle that connects ligand transformations in both the solvated (unbound) and protein-bound (complex) states. Alchemical transformations between ligand states are performed in solution (ΔG_ligand) and in the protein–ligand complex (ΔG_complex), ensuring cancellation of systematic errors. The resulting relative binding free energy is computed as ΔΔG = ΔG_complex – ΔG_ligand. This approach forms the basis of modern FEP methods for predicting ligand affinity differences in drug discovery.

CDOCKER Docking (Etomidate)

For the etomidate stereoisomers, molecular docking was also performed using the CDOCKER protocol in Discovery Studio 2023. CDOCKER employs a CHARMm-based simulated annealing protocol with grid-based energy evaluation. R-etomidate and S-etomidate were docked into the propofol binding site of the 6X3V structure, and CDOCKER interaction energies were compared with experimental pEC 50 values. Although the cocrystallized ligand pose in PDB 6X3V provides a valuable structural reference, we intentionally performed de novo docking using the CDOCKER protocol rather than directly converting the R- to S-stereoisomer followed by local minimization. This choice was motivated by the recognition that stereochemical inversion is not a purely local perturbation in chiral ligand–protein systems. Inversion at the chiral center of etomidate alters the three-dimensional orientation of substituents, which can lead to distinct steric and electrostatic interactions within the binding pocket that are not adequately sampled by constrained minimization.

De novo docking enables full conformational and orientational exploration of each stereoisomer within the propofol binding cavity, allowing the ligand to relax into potentially alternative binding modes that may be energetically favorable but inaccessible through simple coordinate inversion. This is especially relevant for the GABAAR binding site, which is characterized by a heterogeneous microenvironment with multiple competing interaction hotspots, including hydrophobic pockets and polar residues contributing to ligand stabilization as has been shown for other amphiphilic anesthetic binding sites.

Furthermore, the CDOCKER simulated annealing framework provides a more exhaustive sampling of ligand poses under a consistent force field (CHARMm), ensuring that differences in interaction energies between R- and S-etomidate reflect genuine binding preferences rather than artifacts of initial pose bias. This approach also facilitates a more meaningful comparison with experimental pEC 50 values, as it reduces structural bias and better captures stereoselective binding effects.

Notably, control calculations using stereochemical inversion followed by minimization yielded poses that remained close to the original crystallographic orientation but exhibited higher interaction energies compared to the best scoring de novo docked conformations, supporting the necessity of full sampling.

Experimental Data

Experimental GABA EC 50 potentiation values for propofol analogs were obtained from published electrophysiology studies using Xenopus oocytes expressing α1-β2-γ2 GABAAR . , EC 50 values (in micromolar) were transformed to ln­(EC 50) for correlation with computed relative ΔG values, where higher EC 50 (higher ln EC 50) indicates lower potency. For etomidate, the approximately 10-fold potency difference between R- and S-enantiomers is well established.

Reproducibility and Independent Setups

To assess reproducibility, the propofol analog FEP calculations at the wild type 6X3T receptor were performed three independent times (Run 1, Run 2, and Run 3) using the same protocol but with independent system setups. This provides a measure of the sensitivity of FEP results to initial conditions and setup procedures.

Case Analysis of 2-tert-Butyl-6-methylphenol

This compound warrants dedicated discussion because it combines high experimental potency (EC 50 = 0.47 μM, the most potent analog in the series) with the poorest computational reproducibility: its ΔΔG spanned −1.14 to +6.40 kcal/mol across the three independent runs (SD = 3.78 kcal/mol), and it is the single largest contributor to the overall scatter, with excluding it raising the mean-run correlation from r = 0.846 to r = 0.962 (R 2 = 0.924). Importantly, this variability does not reflect poor convergence within any individual run: each of the three simulations shows low forward–reverse hysteresis (0.41 kcal/mol) and adjacent-window phase-space overlap (minimum 0.34) that are within the range of the well-converged transformations in the series and far below the ∼2 kcal/mol hysteresis of the nonconverged pairs (Table S1). The anomaly is therefore dominated by between-setup variance that is, sensitivity to the independently prepared starting conditions rather than by within-run sampling error or a failure of the free-energy estimator. Structurally, 2-tert-butyl-6-methylphenol is the only asymmetrically 2,6-disubstituted analog in the series, bearing a bulky tert-butyl group at one ortho position and a small methyl at the other. In the docking poses, the phenolic hydroxyl forms a hydrogen bond with ILE228 that anchors the aromatic ring; because the two ortho substituents differ in size, this anchoring interaction can be satisfied in more than one ring orientation, so independently built systems may begin in different starting arrangements. We present this as a hypothesis consistent with the observed between-run spread and the compound’s unique asymmetry, rather than as a mechanism resolved directly from the trajectories. Accordingly, we flag this transformation as low-confidence and interpret it as an operational limit of the standard triplicate protocol for asymmetric, conformationally flexible ligands, a regimen in which longer sampling or enhanced-sampling methods such as Hamiltonian replica exchange (discussed above) would be expected to improve reproducibility.

Analysis of the lambda-dependent free energy profiles (Figures –) reveals that the ligand-phase transformation (R to S in solvent) is nearly isoenergetic (ΔG ligand ∼ 0.17 kcal/mol), as expected since the two enantiomers have identical physicochemical properties in achiral bulk solvent. Importantly, the selectivity arises entirely from the complex-phase transformation, where the chiral binding site of the GABA A receptor provides differential stabilization of the R-enantiomer. The hysteresis plot (forward minus reverse ΔG per lambda window) shows systematic deviations at early and late lambda values (Figure ), characteristic of end-state singularity effects, but the integrated forward and reverse total free energies converge to consistent values, indicating adequate sampling.

CDOCKER docking corroborates the FEP results: R-etomidate achieves a more favorable CDOCKER interaction energy (−30.3 kcal/mol) compared to S-etomidate (−27.5 kcal/mol), with the docking energies only qualitatively correlating with experimental pEC 50 values (The 2D interaction diagrams reveal that R-etomidate forms more extensive hydrophobic contacts and more optimal hydrogen bonding geometry with binding site residues including THR262, ASN265, ILE228, and MET286). It is important to note that while molecular docking utilizes the exclusively the enthalpic component of binding as assessed through molecular mechanics calculations, FEP calculations can take into account an assessment of both the enthalpic and entropic components of ligand binding. The latter not only includes potential issues related to conformational degrees of freedom within the ligand and protein binding site but also issues related to solvation and desolvation.

Convergence Assessment

The quality of FEP convergence was assessed through several metrics: (i) Hysteresis: Forward–reverse free energy differences (hysteresis) per lambda window typically remained below 1.5 kcal/mol for well-converged transformations. The largest hysteresis was observed for the 2,6-diethylphenyl isocyanate and isothiocyanate transformations (∼2.0 kcal/mol; Table S1), consistent with their failure to converge and with the loss of the phenolic hydroxyl group. Among the converged pairs, Isopropylphenol showed the highest hysteresis (0.72 kcal/mol, driven largely by Run 1), while all remaining pairs including 3,5-diisopropylcatechol (0.25 kcal/mol) and 3,5-ditert-butylphenol (0.54 kcal/mol) stayed below 0.6 kcal/mol.; (ii) Standard deviations: Most pairwise ΔΔG values had standard deviations below 0.5 kcal/mol, indicating adequate statistical precision. The isopropylphenol-to-2tertbutyl6methylphenol transformation showed the highest variability (StDev up to 0.92 kcal/mol in some runs), reflecting the conformational complexity of the tert-butyl group reorientation; (iii) Reproducibility: Comparison of three independent wild-type calculations (Run 1, Run 2, and Run 3) shows that most compounds are ranked consistently, with absolute ΔΔG differences typically within 1–2 kcal/mol across runs for well-behaved transformations.

Discussion

FEP Accurately Predicts Propofol Analog Binding Rank Order

The alchemical FEP calculations presented here demonstrate that the CHARMm GPU-accelerated protocol in Discovery Studio 2023 can reproduce the experimentally established SAR of propofol analogs at the GABA A receptor. The consistent identification of propofol and disec-butylphenol as the most potent analogs, and phenol as the least potent, across multiple independent calculations and several orders of magnitude validates the methodology for this class of anesthetic compounds.

The correlation between computed relative ΔG values and experimental ln­(EC 50) is notable given the inherent approximations in the FEP methodology, including the use of fixed-charge force fields, finite simulation times (500 ps per lambda window), and the assumption that EC 50 potentiation serves as a proxy for binding affinity. The EC 50 for GABA potentiation reflects a complex pharmacological end point that encompasses both binding affinity and efficacy (ability to modulate channel gating); nevertheless, for a congeneric series of structurally similar compounds acting at the same site, relative potency is expected to correlate with relative binding affinity, as observed here.

Structural Determinants of Analog Potency

Beyond rank-order validation, the converged calculations point to specific physicochemical determinants of potency within the β+/α– transmembrane site. Across the 11 analogs that returned free-energy estimates, the computed relative free energies track the experimental potencies (Pearson r = 0.85; Spearman ρ = 0.89 between mean ΔΔG and ln EC50; Figure ), with the residual discrepancy reflecting a systematic overestimation of the destabilization magnitude (mean unsigned error ≈ 2.2 kcal/mol) rather than rank inversion. One compound, 2-tert-butyl-6-methylphenol, is the sole rank outlier and is also by far the least reproducible transformation in the set (inter-run range 7.5 kcal/mol vs ≤3 kcal/mol for the eight well-converged members); notably, it is the most potent analog measured (EC 50 = 0.47 μM), so its poor convergence represents a genuine limitation of the current alchemical protocol for this compound rather than a discrepancy we can interpret mechanistically. Excluding it on convergence grounds raises the correlation to r = 0.96 (r 2 = 0.92).

Two features of the well-converged series account for this trend. First, a bulky, branched 2,6-diortho substitution. The analogs with the lowest computed ΔΔG propofol (2,6-diisopropylphenol) and 2,6-disec-butylphenol, both among the most potent measured carry branched substituents at both ortho positions that fill the hydrophobic subpocket lined by MET286, PHE289, LEU232 and ILE228. Potency is lost monotonically as this footprint is eroded, a trend anchored by the well-converged members spanning the full potency range (2,6-disec-butylphenol → 2,6-diethylphenol → phenol) and consistent with, though less reproducibly captured by, the intermediate 2,6-dimethyl and 2-isopropyl analogs. The correct deranking of the 3,5-disubstituted analogs (the diisopropylcatechol and ditert-butyl compounds), in which the alkyl groups are displaced from the 2,6 geometries, reinforces that it is the diortho placement rather than alkyl content per se that matters. The high measured potency of the asymmetric 2-tert-butyl-6-methylphenol despite the failure of its transformation to converge is itself consistent with this diortho hypothesis, since it too bears substituents at both ortho positions.

Second, the phenolic hydroxyl, which is conserved across all active analogs and is positioned to donate a hydrogen bond to a polar backbone acceptor within the site (e.g., the backbone carbonyl of ILE228). Because every converged transformation retains this group, our calculations establish its conservation among actives but do not, on their own, quantify the penalty for its removal. On this basis, analog design should preserve a bulky 2,6-diortho hydrophobic footprint and the phenolic donor, while para-substitution (as in the tolerated 4-iodo analog) offers a handle for tuning potency and physicochemical properties without disrupting the core pharmacophore.

Stereoselectivity of Etomidate Binding

The FEP-predicted 1.8 kcal/mol binding preference for R-etomidate over S-etomidate represents a quantitatively accurate reproduction of the experimentally observed stereoselectivity. This result is particularly significant because stereoisomeric discrimination requires this more robust computational method to resolve such subtle geometric differences in protein–ligand interactions within a chiral binding environment, while molecular docking can only make approximate qualitative distinctions

The near-zero ΔG ligand (0.17 kcal/mol) serves as an important internal control: since enantiomers have identical physicochemical properties in an achiral environment, the solvation free energy difference should be near zero, and indeed it is. The stereoselectivity arises entirely from differential interactions in the chiral protein binding site, as expected on physical grounds.

The CDOCKER docking analysis provides complementary validation, with the more favorable docking energy for R-etomidate (−30.3 vs −27.5 kcal/mol) being consistent with the FEP-predicted preference, though the magnitude of the difference is more accurately computed by the FEP calculation. It is in light of this that FEP, while computationally more expensive, may serve to better delineate subtle differences in potency relative to small changes in chemical moieties and be better suited to the final stages of in silico drug design. The 2D interaction diagrams reveal that R-etomidate achieves more optimal positioning of its ester group for hydrogen bonding with backbone amides and its phenyl ring for hydrophobic packing in the transmembrane cavity.

Methodological Considerations

Several methodological considerations warrant discussion, particularly with respect to force field selection and its implications for accurately capturing ligand–receptor interactions. The CHARMm/charmm36 force field has been extensively validated for protein–ligand interactions and provides a reasonable description of the nonbonded interactions relevant to anesthetic binding. However, fixed-charge force fields may underestimate polarization effects, which could be significant for halogenated compounds (e.g., 4-iodo-diisopropylphenol, diethylphenyl bromide) or for interactions with the electronegative chloride channel pore. ,

Simulation time: The 500 ps production time per lambda window (10.5 ns total per pair) represents a practical compromise between computational cost and convergence. While longer simulations would improve sampling statistics, the consistency of results across independent runs suggests that the current protocol captures the dominant contributions to relative binding free energies.

Dual-topology limitations: The dual-topology FEP approach requires sufficient chemical similarity between paired ligands (minimum 20% atom overlap). Transformations involving dramatic changes in molecular identity (e.g., phenol to isocyanate, where the hydroxyl is replaced by an isocyanate group) may exhibit poor convergence, as observed for the 2,6-diethylphenyl isocyanate and isothiocyanate transformations. Enhanced sampling methods or alternative pathway designs may be needed for such challenging cases. This lack of convergence is consistent with known limitations of alchemical FEP methods when applied to ligand pairs with large chemical dissimilarities. In such cases, the alchemical pathway involves significant changes in both electrostatics and hydrogen-bonding capability, leading to poor overlap between configurational ensembles of intermediate λ states. The removal of the hydroxyl group, which plays a key role in stabilizing ligand–receptor interactions, further exacerbates sampling inefficiencies and increases free energy variance across windows.

This issue can be understood in the context of the exponential averaging inherent to alchemical free energy perturbation (FEP), as described by the Zwanzig (eq ). In this formalism, the free energy difference depends on the ensemble average of the exponential of energy differences between states i and j. When ligand transformations involve large perturbations such as the replacement of a phenolic hydroxyl group with an isocyanate or isothiocyanate moiety, the energy difference term U_j – U_i becomes broadly distributed with high variance.

Consequently, the exponential average becomes dominated by rare, high-energy configurations, leading to poor statistical convergence and numerical instability. This reflects insufficient phase space overlap between the initial and final states, a well-recognized limitation of FEP when applied to chemically dissimilar ligands.

These results highlight that reliable FEP calculations generally require chemically similar ligands with only modest perturbations, ensuring sufficient phase space overlap along the alchemical pathway yet different enough to explore chemical space for drug design. Transformations involving major functional group changes particularly those altering polarity, hydrogen bonding, or net charge distribution may fall outside the regime where standard FEP protocols are robust without enhanced sampling strategies or intermediate mapping schemes.

Choice of Free-Energy Estimator

The Discovery Studio protocol computes free energies by exponential averaging (the Zwanzig relation, eq ) rather than by the Bennett acceptance ratio (BAR) or its multistate extension (MBAR). Exponential averaging converges more slowly and is more sensitive to poor phase-space overlap than BAR/MBAR, because the estimator is dominated by rare, low-probability configurations in the tails of the energy-difference distribution. We mitigate this in three ways: (i) every transformation is run in both the forward and reverse directions and the forward–reverse hysteresis is used as an explicit convergence criterion; (ii) each wild-type calculation is repeated in three fully independent setups, so that estimator variance is captured directly in the reported standard deviations; and (iii) transformations exhibiting large hysteresis or high per-window variance are flagged as unreliable rather than reported as converged. Where bidirectional sampling is available, the averaged forward/reverse estimate approaches a two-state BAR estimate for that window. Nonetheless, a full BAR/MBAR analysis with overlap-based reweighting would be expected to yield lower-variance estimates and represents a clear avenue for improvement.

Relationship to Modern Alchemical Methodology

The protocol used here does not incorporate several methodological advances that have substantially improved the robustness of alchemical free-energy calculations over the past decade. These include softcore potentials designed to remove end-point (λ → 0/1) singularities, automated schemes for assigning softcore regions, quantitative phase-space-overlap and reweighting-entropy diagnostics for assessing estimator reliability, λ-schedule optimization to equalize overlap between adjacent windows, network-wide (cycle-closure) free-energy analysis, formal time-series/equilibration-detection convergence analysis, and Hamiltonian replica exchange (HREX) including HREX coupled with softcore potentials to enhance sampling across torsional barriers and to ensure equilibration between alchemical states. The end-point artifacts and convergence failures we observe for the isocyanate and isothiocyanate transformations are precisely the regime these methods were developed to address. Their absence from the present commercial implementation defines the operational limits of our protocol and motivates future work using open-source engines (e.g., GROMACS/PMX, OpenMM/Perses, or AMBER) in which these features are available.

Comparison with Prior Work

Bertaccini and colleagues previously reported FEP calculations for a subset of these propofol analogs using an earlier version of the Discovery Studio protocol. , The present work extends this analysis to the etomidate stereoisomer comparison and demonstrates more extensively rigorous reproducibility across three independent runs.

Implications for Anesthetic Drug Design

Anesthetic binding studies have historically been notoriously difficult to computationally predict and correlate with potencies. The full neural correlates of consciousness remain unknown. For over 100 years it was thought that anesthetics work through lipid bilayer perturbations alone. It has only been in the last 30 years or so that their more relevant direct effects on ion channel proteins have proven most important. While many such channels have been implicated, for certain compounds like propofol and etomidate it seems that the GABAA receptor is the prime target. It has only been in the last several years that robust cryo-EM receptor structures for this class of receptor have been delineated, though prior homology models constructed by our group showed significant similarities. Separately, the ligand-based pharmacophore approach to anesthetic design has been profoundly difficult to resolve since compounds from highly complex multiple ring structures to simple molecular xenon all have anesthetic potencies which have seen successful clinical effects. Finally, the perfect sedative/anesthetic does not exist given that virtually all currently used anesthetics as well as many in experimental trials all seem to have dangerous side effects due to their promiscuity of binding, making better computational assessments of anesthetic-protein binding all the more important.

Given this, the ability to quantitatively predict relative binding affinities of anesthetic compounds at specific receptor sites given more modern hardware and algorithmic advances opens opportunities for rational drug design. Current clinical anesthetics have well-known limitations: propofol causes cardiovascular depression and pain on injection, while etomidate suppresses adrenocortical function. Computational screening of novel analogs using FEP methods, validated against the experimental SAR as demonstrated here, could accelerate the identification of compounds that retain anesthetic potency while avoiding dose-limiting side effects. Because the protocol reproduces the experimental potency ranking of this congeneric series as well as the nearly exact difference in etomidate optical isomer potencies, the computed relative affinities provide a quantitative filter for prioritizing candidate analogs for synthesis prior to experimental testing.

Conclusions

We have demonstrated that alchemical free energy perturbation calculations, implemented through the CHARMm GPU-accelerated protocol in BIOVIA Discovery Studio 2023, can quantitatively rank-order the binding affinities of a 13-member propofol analog series at the wild-type GABAAR, with computed relative binding free energies correlating with experimental GABA EC 50 potentiation data. These calculations also correctly predict the approximately 10-fold stereoselectivity of R-etomidate over S-etomidate (ΔG = 1.8 kcal/mol), with the stereoselectivity arising entirely from differential protein–ligand interactions in the chiral binding site. They also demonstrate reproducibility across independent computational setups, with most compounds ranked consistently across operators despite quantitative variations in absolute ΔG values. These results establish alchemical FEP as a validated computational tool for investigating anesthetic structure–activity relationships at the GABAAR and provide a foundation for prospective computational screening of novel anesthetic candidates.

magnetic flows on 3D contact Sub-Riemannian manifolds via the Rumin Complex
Davide Barilari, Tania Bossio, Valentina Franceschi

Abstract
. We show that the appropriate notion of magnetic field on three-dimensional contact sub-Riemannian manifolds is given by a closed Rumin differential two-form. We introduce horizontal magnetic flows starting from magnetic potential one-forms, proving that the flow depends only on the Rumin differential of the potential. Notably, in dimension three the Rumin differential acts on one-forms as a second-order differential operator. We further prove that such magnetic flows can be interpreted as a geodesic flow on a suitably lifted sub-Riemannian structure, which is of Engel type when the magnetic field is non-vanishing. In the general case, when the magnetic field may vanish, we analyze the geometry of the lifted structure, characterizing its step and abnormal trajectories in terms of the analytical properties of the magnetic field. Our work is inspired by the classical correspondence, first observed by Montgomery in [ 13 , 15 ] , between Riemannian magnetic flows and sub-Riemannian geometry.

1.
Introduction

The correspondence between magnetic flows on Riemannian manifolds and sub-Riemannian geometry is nowadays well established. It was clearly demonstrated by Montgomery in his seminal work
“Hearing the zero locus of a magnetic field”

[
15
]
, where he studies the asymptotics of a two-dimensional quantum particle in a magnetic field and reinterprets its quantum dynamics in terms of a suitable sub-Riemannian Laplacian on a lifted space (see also
[
16
, Chapter 12]
and the earlier works
[
13
,
14
]
). Inspired by these developments, in this paper we focus on the classical dynamics perspective, initiating a systematic study of magnetic flows on sub-Riemannian manifolds.

The simplest instance of the correspondence just mentioned permits to reinterpret the geodesic flow on the sub-Riemannian Heisenberg group as the lift of the magnetic flow of a charged particle moving in the Euclidean plane under the influence of a constant magnetic field – a construction we now recall (cf.
[
2
, Section 4.4.2]
).

The Heisenberg group is nowadays recognized as the prototype model of sub-Riemannian geometry; i.e., spaces where the distance is computed minimizing the length of curves that are tangent to a suitable non-integrable vector distribution. In the specific case of the Heisenberg group, which is topologically
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with coordinates
(x,y,z)
, the vector distribution
\mathcal{D}
is given by

(1)

\mathcal{D}=\ker\omega,\qquad\omega=dz-\frac{1}{2}(xdy-ydx).

The length of a curve tangent to
\mathcal{D}
(also said
horizontal
) is set to be equal to the Euclidean length of its projection onto the
(x,y)
plane. Length-minimizers among horizontal curves joining two fixed points always exist: these are curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
whose projection onto the
(x,y)
plane are circles or lines and whose vertical coordinate is recovered by imposing the relation (
1
), namely

(2)

z(t)-z(0)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\big(x(\tau)\dot{y}(\tau)-y(\tau)\dot{x}(\tau)\big)\,d\tau.

It is possible to reinterpret such geodesic flow on the sub-Riemannian Heisenberg group as the lift of the motion of a charged particle confined to the
(x,y)
plane, subject to a constant vector magnetic field
\vec{B}
oriented positively along the
z
-axis.
Indeed, according to the classical Lorentz force law,

F=q(\mathbf{v}\times\vec{B})
,
such a particle moves with an acceleration in the
(x,y)
plane which is perpendicular to the magnetic field direction. The particle then follows a circular trajectory in the plane with curvature proportional to the charge
q
; if the particle is not charged then the trajectory is a straight line in the direction of
\mathbf{v}
.

The relation (
2
) can be interpreted in magnetic terms: its right-hand side represents the integral of the differential 1-form

A=\frac{1}{2}(xdy-ydx),

treated as a magnetic potential, over the planar curve. The exterior derivative of the magnetic potential,

\beta=dA=dx\wedge dy,

corresponds to the magnetic field, viewed as a differential 2-form. In fact, the magnetic 2-form
\beta
is closed and is dual to the vector field
\vec{B}
under the standard isomorphism between 2-forms and vector fields in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

More generally a
magnetic field
on a two-dimensional Riemannian surface
(N,g)
is represented by a differential 2-form
\beta\in\Omega^{2}(N)
which is closed, i.e., satisfies
d\beta=0
.
Assuming that
\beta=dA
for some
magnetic potential

A\in\Omega^{1}(N)
, then the motion of a particle with charge
q\in\mathbb{R}
on
N
under the action of the corresponding magnetic field is obtained by minimizing the action associated with the following Lagrangian

(3)

L(p,v)=\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}_{g}-q\langle A(p),v\rangle,\qquad p\in N,v\in T_{p}N.

where the brackets represent the duality between covectors and vectors. The corresponding Hamiltonian, denoting
g^{*}
the cometric on
T^{*}N
, is given by

(4)

H(p,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\|\xi+qA(p)\|^{2}_{g^{*}},\qquad p\in N,\,\xi\in T^{*}_{p}N.

Magnetic geodesics with charge
q
satisfy the following equation with respect to the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla

(5)

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=q\,b(\dot{\gamma}),

where
b
is the skew-symmetric operator defined by the identity
\beta(v,w)=g(v,b(w))
.
Since
b
is skew-symmetric and
N
is 2-dimensional, then
b(\dot{\gamma})
is a scalar multiple of
\dot{\gamma}^{\perp}
, the unit vector orthogonal to
\dot{\gamma}
in
N
. Then the relation (
5
) can also be rewritten in scalar terms: taking the inner product in both sides of (
5
) with
\dot{\gamma}^{\perp}
and using that
k_{g}(\gamma)=g(\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma}^{\perp})
is the (signed) geodesic curvature of
\gamma
we have

(6)

k_{g}(\gamma)=q\,b(\dot{\gamma}),

where now the right-hand side is treated as a scalar. Recall that the (unsigned) geodesic curvature appears as a metric invariant satisfying (here
d_{g}
is the Riemannian distance on
N
)

(7)

d_{g}^{2}(\gamma(t+\varepsilon),\gamma(t))=\varepsilon^{2}-\frac{k^{2}_{g}(\gamma)}{12}\varepsilon^{4}+o(\varepsilon^{4}).

Similarly to the construction described above, the magnetic motion on
N
can be lifted to a motion in the extended space
\overline{N}=N\times\mathbb{R}
, imposing that the curve is tangent to the distribution

(8)

\mathcal{D}=\ker\omega,\qquad\omega=dz-\pi^{*}A.

Here
\pi:\overline{N}\to N
denotes the canonical projection, and the length of a curve tangent to
\mathcal{D}
is set to be equal to the Riemannian length of its projection onto
N
.
The distribution
\mathcal{D}
has rank two, and, by definition, it is a contact distribution at points where
the differential

d\omega|_{\mathcal{D}}

is non-degenerate.
Due to
d\omega=-\pi^{*}\beta
, this is in fact equivalent for the magnetic field
\beta
to be non-vanishing on
N
.
One can prove (cf. for instance
[
4
]
, see also
[
2
, Sec. 4.4]
) that, under this assumption, magnetic trajectories on
N
with charge
q
coincide with the projection of normal extremals for the sub-Riemannian problem which has
q
as vertical part of the covector.

Let
\mathcal{Z}\subset N
be the set of points where the magnetic field
\beta
vanishes. The lifted sub-Riemannian structure has step higher than 2 at
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\overline{N}
,
the lift of the zero locus that is also called
Martinet set
.
Moreover, any horizontal curve contained in the Martinet set is an abnormal extremal for the sub-Riemannian structure, see
[
2
, Lemma 4.43]
.

For a more explicit construction of the lifted sub-Riemannian structure, let us consider a local orthonormal frame
X_{1},X_{2}
for the Riemannian metric
g
on
N
. Then the lifted distribution (
8
) in
\overline{N}
is spanned by the vector fields

(9)

Y_{1}=X_{1}+A(X_{1})\partial_{z},\qquad Y_{2}=X_{2}+A(X_{2})\partial_{z},

where now we identify
T\overline{N}
with
TN\times\mathbb{R}
and we denote by
z
the new variable. By construction, the vector field
[X_{1},X_{2}]
is tangent to
N
so let us write
[X_{1},X_{2}]=c_{1}X_{1}+c_{2}X_{2}
for suitable smooth functions
c_{1},c_{2}\in C^{\infty}(N)
. It is easy to see using
dA=\beta
that

[Y_{1},Y_{2}]=c_{1}Y_{1}+c_{2}Y_{2}+\beta(X_{1},X_{2})\partial_{z}.

hence
[Y_{1},Y_{2}]
is linearly independent from
\mathcal{D}
at points where the magnetic field
\beta
does not vanish, and
\mathcal{D}
has step 2. More in general, one can prove that the distribution is bracket generating of step
k+2
if
k\in\mathbb{N}
is the smallest integer such that there exists a derivative
\partial^{\alpha}\beta
which is non-vanishing, with
\alpha
a multi-index satisfying
|\alpha|=k
(cf.
[
2
, Exercise 4.44]
). We refer to
[
16
, Chapter 12]
for a general discussion of magnetic fields on higher dimensional Riemannian manifolds (see also
[
12
]
for the relation with the corresponding sub-Riemannian structure).

1.1.
Magnetic fields on sub-Riemannian manifolds

The goal of this paper is to investigate magnetic fields on sub-Riemannian manifolds
M
. More specifically we are interested into

(i)

formalizing what is a magnetic field on a sub-Riemannian manifold
M
,

(ii)

understanding magnetic geodesics on
M
,

(iii)

building a suitable lifted sub-Riemannian structure
\overline{M}
,

(iv)

interpreting geometric properties of the lifted structures in terms of the magnetic field.

In this paper we focus on the lowest dimensional case, namely when
M
is a three-dimensional contact sub-Riemannian manifold.

To generalize the approach described above, one might start by considering a closed differential 2-form
\beta\in\Omega^{2}(M)
on the sub-Riemannian manifold representing the magnetic field, which again, we assume exact, i.e.,
\beta=dA
for some
magnetic potential

A\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

However, interpreting heuristically (
3
) in the sub-Riemannian sense, namely when the vectors
v
belong to the distribution
\mathcal{D}=\ker\omega
, being
\omega
a contact form, one immediately observes that adding to
A
a term which is proportional to
\omega
one does not affect the Lagrangian
L
, i.e., we can replace
A
by any other
A^{\prime}=A+g\omega
, where
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Similarly, since we are interested in minimizing the action defined by
L
, which we recall is defined by the integral of the Lagrangian (
3
) over horizontal paths, we are free to add to
A
an exact term, i.e., replace
A
by any
A^{\prime}=A+df
, where
f
is a smooth function on
M
. Actually, due to the invariance of
A
with respect to the contact form, we can reduce to add the horizontal part of the differential
df
. See Section
2.3
below.

These two properties can be formalized by saying that a magnetic field in the sub-Riemannian framework is represented by a
Rumin differential 2-form

\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
which is closed.

1.2.
The Rumin complex and magnetic geodesic equations

The Rumin complex is a refinement of the de Rham complex designed to better capture the geometry of contact (or, more in general, sub-Riemannian) manifolds. Originally, it was introduced by Rumin in the framework of contact manifolds
[
17
]
, and later extended to Carnot groups
[
18
]
.

For a three-dimensional contact manifold, with
\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{span}\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
and Reeb vector field
X_{0}
, the Rumin complex is defined by the space of
horizontal
k
-forms

\Omega^{k}_{H}(M)
as follows:

(10)

\displaystyle\Omega^{0}_{H}(M):=C^{\infty}(M),\qquad\Omega^{1}_{H}(M):=\mathrm{span}\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\},

(11)

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}_{H}(M):=\mathrm{span}\{\nu_{1}\wedge\omega,\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\},\qquad\Omega^{3}_{H}(M):=\mathrm{span}\{\nu_{1}\wedge\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\},

where
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\omega
is the dual basis to
X_{1},X_{2},X_{0}
. The standard exterior derivative is replaced with the horizontal differential
d_{H}
, which is adapted to the filtration naturally induced by the contact structure.
This new differential operator, when acting on 1-forms, has degree two. In our case, for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
A=A_{1}\nu_{1}+A_{2}\nu_{2}\in\Omega_{H}^{1}
,
it is explicitly given by

(12)

d^{0}_{H}f=(X_{1}f)\nu_{1}+(X_{2}f)\nu_{2};\qquad d^{1}_{H}A=d\left(A+dA(X_{1},X_{2})\omega\right);

while
d^{2}_{H}=d
coincides with the exterior differential. A horizontal magnetic field on
M
hence is written as a 2-form as follows

(13)

\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega

which is closed in the ordinary sense, or equivalently, with respect to
d_{H}
. The closure condition, of course, implies that the two coefficients in (
13
) are not independent.

Given a horizontal magnetic field
\beta
on
M
, let
A
be a horizontal magnetic potential on
M
for
\beta
, i.e., such that
\beta=d_{H}A
. We stress that the choice of
A
is not unique. Sub-Riemannian magnetic geodesics are projections of integral curves of the Hamiltonian vector field associated with the magnetic Hamiltonian (here
q
is a scalar denoting the charge of the particle)

(14)

H_{A}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+q\,A_{i})^{2},

where
A_{i}=\langle A,X_{i}\rangle
, cf. formula (
4
) for the classical case. The flow associated with the corresponding Hamiltonian vector field
\vec{H}_{A}
is called sub-Riemannian magnetic flow.

Our first result is the characterization of sub-Riemannian magnetic geodesics, showing that they depend on the horizontal magnetic field
\beta
and not on the particular choice of potential
A
. This should be compared with formula (
5
).

Proposition 1
.

Let
\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
be a horizontal magnetic field on
M
. Let
\gamma:[0,T]\to M
be a sub-Riemannian magnetic geodesic on
M
with charge
q
. Then there exists a smooth function
\alpha
defined on
\gamma
such that with respect to the Tanno connection
\nabla

(15)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\alpha\,J\dot{\gamma},

(16)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\alpha

\displaystyle=g(\tau(\dot{\gamma}),\dot{\gamma})+q\,b(\dot{\gamma}),

where we denoted
b(\dot{\gamma})=\beta(\dot{\gamma},X_{0})
and
\tau(\dot{\gamma})=\mathrm{Tor}(X_{0},\dot{\gamma})
.

Here
\mathrm{Tor}
is the torsion of the Tanno connection
\nabla
. We refer to Section
2.4
for more details. Notice that for
\beta=0
we recover the geodesic equation (cf. Proposition
8
).

It is interesting to observe that in the sub-Riemannian case the magnetic field does not appear in equation (
15
) as in the Riemannian case, cf. (
5
), but rather in equation (
16
). This is coherent with the fact that
\beta
is a Rumin differential, which on 1-forms acts as a differential operator of order two. See also Remark
3.3
.

Another interesting observation is that we can combine equations (
15
) and (
16
) as a single equation as follows

(17)

\frac{d}{dt}g(\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma},J\dot{\gamma})-g(\tau(\dot{\gamma}),\dot{\gamma})=q\,b(\dot{\gamma}),

and we can notice that the left-hand side of (
17
) coincides with the
sub-Riemannian geodesic curvature

k_{SR}(\gamma)
introduced in
[
6
]
as a metric invariant of horizontal curves. Thus, also in the three-dimensional sub-Riemannian case we have an analog interpretation of the magnetic geodesic equation as a scalar equation to be compared with (
6
):

(18)

k_{SR}(\gamma)=q\,b(\dot{\gamma}).

The sub-Riemannian geodesic curvature can be defined as a metric invariant as follows: if
\gamma
is a horizontal curve on
M
and
d_{SR}
denotes the sub-Riemannian distance then
[
6
, Theorem 3]

(19)

d_{SR}^{2}(\gamma(t+\varepsilon),\gamma(t))=\varepsilon^{2}-\frac{k^{2}_{SR}(\gamma(t))}{6!}\varepsilon^{6}+o(\varepsilon^{6}).

It is interesting to notice that this invariant in dimension three appears at order
6
, and not at order
4
as in the Riemannian case, cf. (
7
).
Being the Rumin differential a derivation of second order in dimension three, one could interpret (
18
) as equality between second order invariants.

1.3.
The lifted sub-Riemannian structure and its geometry

We now move to the geometric interpretation of the sub-Riemannian magnetic geodesic. As in the classical case, a sub-Riemannian magnetic geodesic can be interpreted as the projection of a suitable geodesic flow on a lifted sub-Riemannian structure.
Let us start from the simplest example.

Example 1.1

(from Heisenberg to Engel)
.

In the specific case of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}^{3}
with coordinates
(x,y,z)
, we have that the dual basis of 1-forms to
X_{1},X_{2},\partial_{z}
is given by
dx,dy,\omega=dz-\frac{1}{2}(xdy-ydx)
. The Rumin complex in this case is given by

(20)

\Omega^{1}_{H}(\mathbb{H})=\mathrm{span}\{dx,dy\},\qquad\Omega^{2}_{H}(\mathbb{H})=\mathrm{span}\{dx\wedge\omega,dy\wedge\omega\}.

Let us consider the following magnetic potential and the corresponding magnetic field

(21)

A=\frac{x^{2}}{2}dy,\qquad\beta=d_{H}A=dx\wedge\omega.

We stress that
A
has a polynomial coefficient of degree 2 but
\beta
is a 2-form with constant coefficients, since
d_{H}
is a derivation of degree 2 on 1-forms.

One can then follow the Riemannian lift procedure described above by considering a sub-Riemannian structure on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
generated by the vector fields

(22)

Y_{1}=X_{1}+A(X_{1})\partial_{w}=\partial_{x}-\dfrac{y}{2}\partial_{z},\qquad Y_{2}=X_{2}+A(X_{2})\partial_{w}=\partial_{y}+\dfrac{x}{2}\partial_{z}+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\partial_{w}.

One can immediately notice that
[Y_{1},Y_{2}]=\partial_{z}+x\partial_{w}
,
and moreover

(23)

[Y_{1},[Y_{1},Y_{2}]]=\partial_{w},\qquad[Y_{2},[Y_{1},Y_{2}]]=0.

The distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}=\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1},Y_{2}\}
is bracket generating on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Requiring that
\{Y_{1},Y_{2}\}
form an orthonormal frame on
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
, the lifted sub-Riemannian structure is the Engel group.

For a general contact manifold, given a magnetic potential
A\in\Omega^{1}_{H}(M)
for a magnetic field
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
and a basis
X_{1},X_{2}
for the contact distribution which is orthonormal for the metric on
\mathcal{D}
, we define the lifted distribution

(24)

\overline{\mathcal{D}}=\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1},Y_{2}\},\qquad Y_{i}=X_{i}+A(X_{i})\partial_{w}.

We notice that this is a rank 2 distribution in the 4-dimensional space
\overline{M}=M\times\mathbb{R}
, hence it has necessarily step
\geq 3
. One can show that the step is finite, i.e., the sub-Riemannian structure on
\overline{M}
is well-defined, if at every point there exists a horizontal derivation of
\beta
which is non-vanishing (cf.
Proposition

15
). In the rest of the introduction we always make this assumption.

The following result completely characterizes the situation when the magnetic field is non-vanishing. This is proved combining Proposition
9
, Lemma
12
, Propositions
14
and
16
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
be a horizontal magnetic field on a three-dimensional contact sub-Riemannian manifold
M
which defines the lifted sub-Riemannian structure on
\overline{M}
. Then

(a)

normal extremal trajectories of
\overline{M}
projects on magnetic geodesics on
M
,

(b)

abnormal extremal trajectories of
\overline{M}
projects on horizontal curves
\gamma
on
M
satisfying
\iota_{\dot{\gamma}}\beta=\beta(\dot{\gamma},\cdot)=0
.

Moreover, in the region where
\beta
is non-vanishing, the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
has step
3
on
\overline{M}
, and for every
\overline{p}\in\overline{M}
there exists a unique abnormal extremal trajectory
\overline{\gamma}
passing through
\overline{p}
.

The last property can be rephrased by saying that, where
\beta
is non-vanishing, the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
on
\overline{M}
is a
Engel-type
distribution.

Observe that with respect to the magnetic lift of a Riemannian manifold, in this case abnormal extremal trajectories always exist, also when the magnetic field is never vanishing.

Remark 1.2
.

In the region where the magnetic field
\beta
is non-vanishing, choosing a frame
X_{1},X_{2}
for
\mathcal{D}
and the corresponding dual elements, we can write
\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega
with
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\neq(0,0)
. The characteristic curves of
\beta
(namely
\iota_{\dot{\gamma}}\beta=0
)
in this frame satisfy
\dot{\gamma}=-\beta_{2}X_{1}+\beta_{1}X_{2}
.

Notice that, clearly, condition
\iota_{\dot{\gamma}}\beta=0
holds also for horizontal curves contained in the zero locus of
\beta
.
We stress that in both cases one has
b(\dot{\gamma})=0
, where
b
appears in (
16
).
One can interpret this fact by saying that, along the projection onto
M
of an abnormal extremal trajectory living on
\overline{M}
, the magnetic field does not affect the dynamics.

1.4.
On the zero locus

Now we move to the study of the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
in the lift of the zero locus
\mathcal{Z}
of a magnetic field
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
. Let us write
\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega
with respect to a basis and let us consider the pair
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
as a map from
M
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The step of the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at points in
\overline{M}
is related with the rank of this map.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
be a horizontal magnetic field on
M
, a three-dimensional contact sub-Riemannian manifold and let
p\in\mathcal{Z}
.

(1)

If
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)|_{p}=2
, then the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\overline{M}
is
4
;

(2)

If
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)|_{p}=1
, then the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\overline{M}
is
4
or
5
;

(3)

If
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)|_{p}=0
, then the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\overline{M}
is
\geq 5
.

We notice that the result is independent of the choice of the frame.
When the rank of the map is 2, namely case (i), then the zero locus of the magnetic field is a curve. It is interesting to stress here that the step is not affected by the curve being horizontal or transversal to the distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
M
.

This is in contrast with case (ii) when the rank is 1. Up to switching indices, we can assume that
d\beta_{1}\neq 0
.
Hence
\Sigma=\beta_{1}^{-1}(0)
defines a regular surface that contains the zero locus
\mathcal{Z}
of
\beta
. We can characterize the step of the lifted sub-Riemannian structure via a geometric analysis involving
characteristic points
of
\Sigma
, i.e., points
p\in\Sigma
where
\mathcal{D}_{p}=T_{p}\Sigma
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H}
be a horizontal magnetic field on
M
, a three-dimensional contact sub-Riemannian manifold satisfying
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)=1
at
p\in\mathcal{Z}
.
Assume
{\left.\kern-1.2ptd\beta_{1}\right|_{p}}\neq 0

and let
\Sigma=\beta_{1}^{-1}(0)
.
Then
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
has step
5
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}
if and only if
p
is a characteristic point in
\Sigma
.

Finally, we stress that at points where the rank is equal to zero we can have arbitrarily large step. This can be shown by considering explicit magnetic fields of the following form for any given integer
n\geq 1

(25)

\beta=\frac{f^{n}}{n!}(b_{1}\nu_{1}+b_{2}\nu_{2})\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H},

where
b_{1},b_{2},f\in C^{\infty}(M)
with
b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}\neq 0
and
df\neq 0
.
In this case the zero locus of
\beta
given by
\mathcal{Z}=f^{-1}(0)
and one can prove that the step of the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is equal to
3
at points which projects on
M\setminus\mathcal{Z}
, it is
n+3
at points which projects on
\mathcal{Z}\setminus\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{Z})
, it is equal to
2n+3
at points which projects on
\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{Z})
, where
\mathrm{Char}(\mathcal{Z})
denotes the set of characteristic points in
\mathcal{Z}
. Cf. Lemmas
19
and
20
.

1.5.
Structure of the paper

Section
2
is devoted to some preliminaries and in particular the description of the Rumin complex for three-dimensional manifolds. In Section
3
we introduce magnetic fields for three-dimensional sub-Riemannian manifolds and we obtain the magnetic geodesic equations. Then we define the associated lifted sub-Riemannian structure in Section
4
and describe its properties in the case when the magnetic field is never vanishing. Section
5
covers the case when the magnetic field can vanish somewhere and the characterization of the step of the lifted structure and its abnormal curves in this case.
Finally in
Section

6
we present a class of magnetic fields with a given surface as zero locus and we analyze the behavior of abnormal extremal trajectories with respect to the zero locus of the magnetic field.

2.
Preliminaries

We first recall here some basic notions of sub-Riemannian geometry, for a more detailed introduction we refer to
[
2
]
.
Then we focus on the specific case of three-dimensional contact sub-Riemannian manifolds.
Finally, we present the Rumin complex defined for three-dimensional contact sub-Riemannian manifolds, cf.
[
17
]
.

2.1.
Sub-Riemannian structures

Let
M
be a smooth, connected
n
-dimensional manifold equipped with
\mathcal{D}
a bracket generating vector distribution of rank
r
in
TM
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
k\geq 1
, we set
\mathcal{D}^{1}=\mathcal{D}
and recursively define

(26)

\mathcal{D}^{k+1}=\mathrm{span}\left\{\mathcal{D}^{k},\left[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{k}\right]\right\}.

We say that the distribution
\mathcal{D}
at a point
p\in M
is bracket generating of step
k
if it holds that

(27)

\mathcal{D}^{k}_{p}=T_{p}M\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathcal{D}^{j}_{p}\subsetneq T_{p}M\quad\forall\,j\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}.

Finally, the distribution is bracket generating if it is bracket generating at every
p\in M
.

The manifold
M
, equipped with
\mathcal{D}
a bracket generating vector distribution of rank
r
and with
g
a metric defined on
\mathcal{D}
, is said to be a
smooth sub-Riemannian manifold of rank
r
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
a locally defined frame for
\mathcal{D}
.
A curve
\gamma:[0,T]\to M
is
horizontal
if it is absolutely continuous and there exists a
control

u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{r})
satisfying

(28)

\dot{\gamma}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{i}(t)X_{i}(\gamma(t))\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma(t)},\qquad\text{for a.e.}\ t\in[0,T].

The
length
of a horizontal curve is defined as:

(29)

\ell(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{g(\dot{\gamma}(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))}\,{\rm d}t.

Finally, the
sub-Riemannian distance

d_{SR}
between any two points
p,q\in M
is defined as

(30)

d_{SR}(p,q):=\inf\{\ell(\gamma)\mid\gamma\text{ horizontal curve joining $p$ and $q$}\}.

By the Chow-Rashevskii theorem, the bracket generating assumption ensures that the distance
d_{SR}
is finite, continuous and it induces the manifold topology
[
2
, Chapter 3]
.

The sub-Riemannian Hamiltonian is the smooth function
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

(31)

H(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\langle\lambda,X_{i}(x)\rangle^{2},

where
\lambda\in T^{*}_{x}M
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the usual duality pairing.

Let
\vec{H}
be the Hamiltonian vector field on
T^{*}M
associated to
H
. Namely,
\vec{H}
is the unique vector field such that

(32)

\sigma(\cdot,\vec{H})=dH,

where
\sigma
is the canonical symplectic form on the cotangent bundle.
A curve
\lambda:[0,T]\to T^{*}M
that solves

(33)

\dot{\lambda}(t)=\vec{H}(\lambda(t)),

is called
normal extremal
.
The projection of a normal extremal is called
normal extremal trajectory
, it is a smooth curve parametrized with constant speed, and whose sufficiently small arcs are length-minimizers
[
2
, Chapter 4]
.

Not all length-minimizers arise projecting the Hamiltonian flow (
33
). In sub-Riemannian geometry one should also consider the so-called
abnormal extremal trajectories
.
These are horizontal curves that are critical points of the end-point map and depend only on the distribution
\mathcal{D}
(while do not depend on the choice of the metric
g
on it).

Length-minimizers of abnormal type admit a non zero lift
\lambda:[0,T]\to T^{*}M
satisfying a time-dependent Hamiltonian equation and are contained in
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}:=H^{-1}(0)
. For distributions of constant rank (all distributions appearing in this paper satisfy this assumption) we have the following characterization which can be found in
[
2
, Section 4.3]
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
M
be a sub-Riemannian structure associated with a distribution
\mathcal{D}
of constant rank. Then a never vanishing Lipschitz curve in
H^{-1}(0)
is a characteristic curve for
\sigma|_{H^{-1}(0)}
if and only if it is the reparametrization of an abnormal extremal.

2.2.
Three-dimensional contact sub-Riemannian spaces

A three-dimensional smooth manifold
M
is a
sub-Riemannian contact
manifold if it is equipped with a smooth one-form
\omega
satisfying the non degeneracy condition
\omega\wedge d\omega\neq 0
,
where
d
denotes the exterior derivative.

The sub-Riemannian structure on
M
is defined by
(i)
\mathcal{D}=\ker\omega
a two-dimensional bracket generating distribution,
(ii)
g
a smooth metric defined on
\mathcal{D}
.

We require that the two-dimensional volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{g}
defined by
g
on
\mathcal{D}
coincides with the two-dimensional form
-d\omega|_{\mathcal{D}}
.
The
Reeb vector field

X_{0}
is the unique vector field such that

(34)

\iota_{X_{0}}\omega=1,\qquad\iota_{X_{0}}d\omega=0,

where
\iota_{X}
denotes the interior product with respect to the vector field
X
.

Given any local orthonormal frame
\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
for
\mathcal{D}
, the family
\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{0}\}
is a local frame for
TM
. We set
\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{0}\}
to be the corresponding dual frame, where
\nu_{0}=\omega
is the contact form.
By our convention, a horizontal frame is positively oriented if
-d\omega(X_{1},X_{2})=1
. For such frames, the commutation relations read:

(35)

\begin{split}\left[X_{1},X_{2}\right]&=c_{12}^{1}X_{1}+c_{12}^{2}X_{2}+X_{0},\\
\left[X_{1},X_{0}\right]&=c_{10}^{1}X_{1}+c_{10}^{2}X_{2},\\
\left[X_{2},X_{0}\right]&=c_{20}^{1}X_{1}+c_{20}^{2}X_{2},\end{split}

where the
c_{ij}^{k}
are suitable smooth functions on
M
, called structure functions. Notice that the structure functions are constant if the sub-Riemannian structure is left-invariant on a Lie group (cf.
[
2
, Section 7.4]
).

Example 2.2
.

The prototype model of sub-Riemannian contact manifold is the three-dimensional Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
.
This consists in
M=\mathbb{R}^{3}
with coordinates
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, equipped with the globally defined contact form

(36)

\omega=dz-\frac{1}{2}(xdy-ydx).

The distribution
\mathcal{D}=\ker\omega
is generated by the vector fields:

(37)

X_{1}=\partial_{x}-\dfrac{y}{2}\partial_{z},\qquad X_{2}=\partial_{y}+\dfrac{x}{2}\partial_{z}.

The Reeb vector field is
X_{0}=\partial_{z}
.
Setting
g
the sub-Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{D}
such that
\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
is an orthonormal frame for
\mathcal{D}
we have
\mathrm{vol}_{g}=-{\left.\kern-1.2ptd\omega\right|_{\mathcal{D}}}=dx\wedge dy
, and the following bracket relations hold true:

(38)

[X_{1},X_{2}]=X_{0},\qquad[X_{1},X_{0}]=[X_{2},X_{0}]=0.

In conclusion,
\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{0}\}
constitutes a frame for
TM
with corresponding dual frame for
T^{*}M
given by
\nu_{1}=dx,\nu_{2}=dy,\nu_{0}=\omega
.

It is convenient for later purposes to introduce
h_{i}:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
for
i=0,1,2
a set of linear on fiber functions defined by
h_{i}(\lambda)=\langle\lambda,X_{i}\rangle
associated with the frame
X_{i}
for
i=0,1,2
. We have the following identities, where
\vec{H}
denotes the Hamiltonian vector field in (
32
)

H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}h_{i}^{2},\qquad\vec{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}h_{i}\vec{h}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}h_{i}X_{i}+h_{i}(c_{ij}^{k}h_{k}+c_{ij}^{0}h_{0})\partial_{h_{j}}

In particular, treating the functions
(h_{1},h_{2},h_{0})
as coordinates on the fibers, and given an integral curve of
\vec{H}
in
T^{*}M
satisfying (
33
), we have that for its projection
\gamma
on the base manifold
M

\dot{\gamma}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}h_{i}X_{i},

while the functions
h_{i}
satisfy for
j=0,1,2

\dot{h}_{j}=\{H,h_{j}\}=\sum_{i,k=1,2}c_{ij}^{k}h_{i}h_{k}+c_{0j}^{k}h_{0}h_{k}

Remark 2.3
.

It is useful to recall the following properties relating the Hamiltonian vector field
\vec{h}\in\mathrm{Vec}(T^{*}M)
associated to a Hamiltonian
h\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
and the Poisson bracket. For a proof of these basic facts in symplectic geometry we refer to
[
2
, Chapter 4]
.

(a)

If
h,k,k^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
then
\vec{h}(k)=\{h,k\}
and
\{h,kk^{\prime}\}=\{h,k\}k^{\prime}+\{h,k^{\prime}\}k
.

(b)

If
h_{X}=\langle\lambda,X\rangle
and
h_{Y}=\langle\lambda,Y\rangle
are linear on fibers functions then
\{h_{X},h_{Y}\}=h_{[X,Y]}
.

(c)

If
h_{X}=\langle\lambda,X\rangle
and
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(M)
then
\{h_{X},\alpha\}=X\alpha
and
\{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\}=0
.

Thanks to properties (a)-(c) one can prove in particular that for every function
\alpha\in C^{\infty}(M)
(regarded as a function in
T^{*}M
) the vector field
\vec{\alpha}
is vertical, namely that
\pi_{*}\vec{\alpha}=0
.

We recall the
Cartan formula
for differential one-forms:
for any smooth one-form
\tau
on
M
and any smooth vector fields
V,W
, it holds that:

(39)

d\tau(V,W)+\tau([V,W])=V(\tau(W))-W(\tau(V)).

We deduce that for
k=0,1,2
we have

(40)

d\nu^{k}=-c^{k}_{10}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega-c^{k}_{20}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega-c^{k}_{12}\nu_{1}\wedge\nu_{2}.

The volume form
\nu_{1}\wedge\nu_{2}\wedge\omega
coincides with the so-called
Popp’s volume
.
Moreover, since
d\omega=-\nu_{1}\wedge\nu_{2}
by (
40
), notice that the Popp’s volume coincides with
-\omega\wedge d\omega
. We refer to
[
7
]
for more details on the construction in more general sub-Riemannian manifolds.
Denoting with
\mu
the smooth measure associated with the Popp’s volume,
the divergence of a vector field
X
with respect to
\mu
is the smooth function
\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(X)
that satisfies

(41)

\mathcal{L}_{X}\mu=\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(X)\mu,

where
\mathcal{L}_{X}
denotes the Lie derivative with respect to
X
.
Recalling that
\mathcal{L}_{X}=d\circ\iota_{X}+\iota_{X}\circ d
(where
\iota_{X}
denotes the interior product with
X
) and that volume forms are closed, we have

(42)

\mathcal{L}_{X}\mu=-d\left(\iota_{X}\,\omega\wedge d\omega\right).

Moreover, using also (
40
), one computes the divergence in terms of the coefficients in (
35
):

(43)

\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(X_{1})=-c^{2}_{12},\qquad\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(X_{2})=c^{1}_{12}.

The divergence operator is linear and satisfies the following Leibniz rule:

(44)

\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(fX)=Xf+f\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(X),

where
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth and
X
is a vector field on
M
.

Remark 2.4
.

We end this section by recalling that the canonical symplectic form
\sigma
on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
can be written in the basis of 1-forms
\nu_{i}
dual to the chosen basis of
TM
and the differentials
dh_{i}
for
i=0,1,2
of the linear on fibers functions defined above.
The symplectic form
\sigma
is the differential of the tautological form
s=\sum_{i=1,2,0}h_{i}\nu_{i}
hence

\sigma=ds=\sum_{i=1,2,0}dh_{i}\wedge\nu_{i}+h_{i}d\nu_{i}

We recall that contact structures on three-dimensional manifolds do not admit non-constant abnormal extremals trajectories (see
[
2
, Proposition 4.38]
).

2.3.
The Rumin complex

The construction of the Rumin complex we present here is specific for three-dimensional sub-Riemannian contact manifolds. For simplicity, we define objects globally on
M
but everything can be defined on open sets
U\subset M
.

Definition 2.5

(Horizontal differential forms)
.

For
k=0,\dots,3
,
we define the space of the
horizontal
k
-forms

\Omega^{k}_{H}(M)
as

(45)

\begin{split}\Omega^{0}_{H}(M)&:=C^{\infty}(M),\\
\Omega^{1}_{H}(M)&:=\mathrm{span}\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\},\\
\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)&:=\mathrm{span}\{\nu_{1}\wedge\omega,\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\},\\
\Omega^{3}_{H}(M)&:=\mathrm{span}\{\omega\wedge d\omega\},\end{split}

where
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\omega
is the dual basis to
X_{1},X_{2},X_{0}
.

Definition 2.6

(Horizontal differential)
.

For
k=0,1,2
, we define the
horizontal differential
or
Rumin differential

(46)

d^{k}_{H}:\Omega^{k}_{H}(M)\to\Omega^{k+1}_{H}(M)

in the following way:

(i)

For
f\in\Omega_{H}^{0}(M)
, we set

(47)

d^{0}_{H}f=(X_{1}f)\nu_{1}+(X_{2}f)\nu_{2};

(ii)

For
A=A_{1}\nu_{1}+A_{2}\nu_{2}\in\Omega_{H}^{1}(M)
, we set

(48)

d^{1}_{H}A=d\left(A+dA(X_{1},X_{2})\omega\right);

(iii)

For
\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
, we set

(49)

d^{2}_{H}\beta=d\left(\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\right).

Since for
k=1,2
it holds
d_{H}^{k}\circ d_{H}^{k-1}=0
, the co-chain

(50)

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\Omega^{0}_{H}(M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{H}^{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{1}_{H}(M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d^{1}_{H}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{H}^{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{3}_{H}(M)\longrightarrow 0.

defines a complex
\left(\Omega_{H}^{*},d^{*}_{H}\right)
, referred to as the
Rumin complex
.

Furthermore,
d_{H}^{0}
and
d_{H}^{2}
are differential operators of the first order, whereas
d_{H}^{1}
is a second-order differential operator, as we show in the following with more explicit computations. In what follows, the index
k
in the notation
d_{H}^{k}
can be omitted whenever it is clear from the context.

Lemma 6
.

Let
A=A_{1}\nu_{1}+A_{2}\nu_{2}\in\Omega^{1}_{H}(M)
such that
\beta=d_{H}A=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
. Then,

(51)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta_{1}&=X_{1}X_{2}(A_{1})-X_{1}X_{1}(A_{2})-c^{1}_{12}X_{1}(A_{1})-c^{2}_{12}X_{1}(A_{2})-X_{0}(A_{1})\\
&\quad-(X_{1}(c^{1}_{12})+c^{1}_{10})A_{1}-(X_{1}(c^{2}_{12})+c^{2}_{10})A_{2},\end{split}

(52)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta_{2}&=X_{2}X_{2}(A_{1})-X_{2}X_{1}(A_{2})-c^{1}_{12}X_{2}(A_{1})-c^{2}_{12}X_{2}(A_{2})-X_{0}(A_{2})\\
&\quad-(X_{2}(c^{1}_{12})+c^{1}_{02})A_{1}-(X_{2}(c^{2}_{12})+c^{2}_{02})A_{2}.\end{split}

Proof.

Since
\beta_{i}=d_{H}A(X_{i},X_{0})
for
i=1,2
, by (
48
) and using (
40
) it holds that

(53)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=X_{1}(dA(X_{1},X_{2}))-X_{0}(A_{1})-c^{1}_{10}A_{1}-c^{2}_{10}A_{2},

(54)

\displaystyle\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=X_{2}(dA(X_{1},X_{2}))-X_{0}(A_{2})-c^{1}_{20}A_{1}-c^{2}_{20}A_{2}.

Exploiting (
39
), we obtain that

(55)

\begin{split}dA(X_{1},X_{2})&=X_{1}(A(X_{2}))-X_{2}(A(X_{1}))-A([X_{1},X_{2}])\\
&=X_{1}(A_{2})-X_{2}(A_{1})-c^{1}_{12}A_{1}-c^{2}_{12}A_{2}.\end{split}

Substituting in (
53
) and (
54
) we conclude.
∎

The following result, due to Rumin, guarantees that every
d_{H}
-closed horizontal form is locally
d_{H}
-exact and that the cohomology induced by
(\Omega_{H},d_{H})
is isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology of
M
. Consequently, all constructions involving the Rumin complex can also be interpreted locally, namely on an open neighborhood
U
that is topologically equivalent to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Proposition 7

(
[
17
]
)
.

The Rumin complex
\left(\Omega_{H},d_{H}\right)
is locally exact
and it computes the de Rham cohomology of
M
.

Originally, the Rumin complex was introduced by Rumin in the framework of contact manifolds
[
17
]
, and later for Carnot groups
[
18
]
.
More recently, an alternative construction of this complex has been proposed in
[
9
,
10
]
including homogeneous groups and filtered manifolds, of which equiregular sub-Riemannian structures represent particular cases.

2.4.
Tanno connection

We introduce the Tanno connection, which is a canonical connection on sub-Riemannian contact manifold
M
, see
[
19
,
3
]
. For proofs of some facts listed in this subsection, in the same three-dimensional setting, we also refer to
[
5
]
.

Given the normalized contact structure
\omega
on
M
, we define the linear map
J:TM\to TM
by
g(X,JY)=\mathrm{d}\omega(X,Y)
for horizontal vector fields
X,Y
, while
JX_{0}=0
.

Definition 2.7
.

The Tanno connection
\nabla
is the unique linear connection on
TM
satisfying

(1)

\nabla g=0
,
\nabla X_{0}=0
;

(2)

\mathrm{Tor}(X,Y)=g(X,JY)X_{0}=\mathrm{d}\omega(X,Y)X_{0}
for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
;

(3)

\mathrm{Tor}(X_{0},JX)=-J\mathrm{Tor}(X_{0},X)
for any vector field
X
on
M
.

where
\mathrm{Tor}
denotes the torsion of the connection
\nabla
.

In what follows
\nabla
will always denote the Tanno connection. Being
\nabla
a metric connection, it follows that
\nabla_{X}Y
is parallel to
JY
for any unitary horizontal vector fields
X,Y
.
It is also easy to check that the Tanno connection
\nabla
commutes with the operator
J
, i.e.,
\nabla_{X}JY=J\nabla_{X}Y.

Let us introduce the “horizontal” Christoffel symbols

(56)

\Gamma_{ij}^{k}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{ij}^{k}+c_{ki}^{j}+c_{kj}^{i}\right),\qquad i,j,k=1,2.

Notice that
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}+\Gamma_{ik}^{j}=0
and that one can recover some of the structural functions with the relation

(57)

c_{ij}^{k}=\Gamma_{ij}^{k}-\Gamma_{ji}^{k},\qquad i,j,k=1,2.

In terms of the structural functions, we have, for
i,j=1,2

(58)

\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}X_{k},\qquad\nabla_{X_{0}}X_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2}(c_{k0}^{i}-c_{i0}^{k})X_{k},\qquad JX_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{2}c_{ij}^{0}X_{j}.

Let
\mathrm{Tor}
be the torsion associated to the Tanno connection and
\tau:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
be the linear operator
\tau(X):=\mathrm{Tor}(X_{0},X)
for
X
horizontal. Then
\tau
is symmetric with respect to
g
and

(59)

\tau(X_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2}(c_{k0}^{i}+c_{i0}^{k})X_{k},\qquad\mathrm{Tor}(X_{j},X_{k})=-c_{jk}^{0}X_{0},

The equations for sub-Riemannian normal geodesics on
M
can be also characterized as follows.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\gamma:[0,T]\to M
be a sub-Riemannian geodesic on
M
. Then there exists a smooth function
\alpha
defined on
\gamma
such that

(60)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\alpha\,J\dot{\gamma}

(61)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\alpha

\displaystyle=g(\tau(\dot{\gamma}),\dot{\gamma})

This result is well-known (see
[
17
, Proposition 15]
). A proof for general contact structures, with language similar to the one presented here, can be found for instance in
[
3
]
. We refer to
[
6
]
for the relation with the geodesic curvature in the three-dimensional case.

Remark 2.8
.

Notice that in the case of the Heisenberg group
\tau=0
, hence
\alpha
is constant along geodesics
\gamma
and actually coincides with the vertical part of the covector defining it.

In the general case the quantity appearing in the second equation
\eta(\dot{\gamma}):=g(\tau(\dot{\gamma}),\dot{\gamma})
is a directional invariant which is related to the metric invariant
\chi
of the contact structure
[
1
]
. We refer to
[
6
]
for more details.

3.
Magnetic fields on 3D contact sub-Riemannian manifolds

Let us consider a three-dimensional contact sub-Riemannian manifold, which we will denote by
M
throughout the paper. We start by introducing horizontal magnetic fields.

Definition 3.1
.

A
horizontal magnetic field
is a differential two-form
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
which is closed with respect to the exterior derivative
d
.
With respect to a choice of the frame, there exist
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

(62)

\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega,\qquad d\beta=0.

If
\beta
is exact, a smooth one-form
A=A_{1}\nu_{1}+A_{2}\nu_{2}\in\Omega_{H}^{1}
such that
\beta=d_{H}A
is called
horizontal magnetic potential
.

Remark 3.2
.

By formula (
49
), one has
d\beta=d_{H}\beta
for every
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{H}
, so that one can use equivalently both differentials in the above definition. In particular, since
d_{H}\beta=0
, we can always find a horizontal magnetic potential
A\in\Omega^{1}_{H}(M)
locally defined on
M
(cf.
Proposition

7
).
Moreover, notice that
A\in\Omega^{1}_{H}(M)
is unique up to adding a term
d_{H}f
with
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

3.1.
Magnetic geodesics equation

In this section we want to prove a characterization of magnetic geodesics in sub-Riemannian geometry similar to the one given in Proposition
8
for classical geodesics.

Let
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
be a horizontal magnetic field on
M
and let
A\in\Omega^{1}_{H}(M)
be a horizontal magnetic potential on
M
for
\beta
such that
\beta=d_{H}A
. Sub-Riemannian magnetic geodesics are obtained as projections of integral curves of the Hamiltonian vector field associated with the magnetic Hamiltonian (here
q
is a scalar denoting the charge of the particle)

(63)

H_{A}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+q\,A_{i})^{2},

that is obtained by modifying the sub-Riemannian Hamiltonian with the functions
A_{i}=\langle A,X_{i}\rangle
, (cf. formula (
4
) for the classical case).
The corresponding Hamiltonian vector field is written as

\vec{H}_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+q\,A_{i})(\vec{h}_{i}+q\,\vec{A}_{i}).

We give a geometric characterization of magnetic geodesics, showing that the notion we introduced of magnetic field is well-defined since geodesics do not depend on the choice of
A
.

3.2.
Proof of Proposition
1

By linearity with respect to
q
, it is enough to consider the case
q=1
. Consider an integral curve
\dot{\lambda}=\vec{H}_{A}(\lambda)
and set
\lambda=(\gamma,h)
where
\gamma
is the projection onto
M
and
h=(h_{1},h_{2},h_{0})
are the vertical coordinates associated with the frame
X_{1},X_{2},X_{0}
as discussed in Section
2.2
.

Thanks to Remark
2.3
, being
A_{i}
smooth functions on
M
, the vector fields
\vec{A}_{i}
are vertical, i.e.,
\pi_{*}\vec{A}_{i}=0
. Projecting the equation
\dot{\lambda}=\vec{H}_{A}(\lambda)
on
M
one thus obtains

\dot{\gamma}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})X_{i},

where the quantities
h_{i}
and
A_{i}
satisfy the following differential equations along the flow (recall that
\dot{f}=\vec{H}_{A}(f)=\{H_{A},f\}
)

(64)

\displaystyle\dot{h}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})\{h_{i}+A_{i},h_{j}\}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})[\{h_{i},h_{j}\}+\{A_{i},h_{j}\}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,k=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})[c_{ij}^{k}h_{k}+c_{ij}^{0}h_{0}-X_{j}(A_{i})].

Similarly

(65)

\displaystyle\dot{A}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})\{h_{i}+A_{i},A_{j}\}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})[\{h_{i},A_{j}\}+\{A_{i},A_{j}\}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})[X_{i}(A_{j})].

Now we are ready to compute the quantity
\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}

to characterize the magnetic geodesic equation. We have (recall that
\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}f=\dot{f}
)

(66)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}[(h_{j}+A_{j})X_{j}]=\sum_{j=1}^{2}(\dot{h}_{j}+\dot{A}_{j})X_{j}+(h_{j}+A_{j})\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}(\dot{h}_{j}+\dot{A}_{j})X_{j}+(h_{j}+A_{j})(h_{i}+A_{i})\sum_{k=1}^{2}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}X_{k},

where we used the relations
\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}X_{k}
given in Section
2.4
. Adding (
64
) and (
65
)
we get

\displaystyle\dot{h}_{j}+\dot{A}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,k=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})[c_{ij}^{k}h_{k}+c_{ij}^{0}h_{0}+X_{i}(A_{j})-X_{j}(A_{i})],

and replacing in (
66
) (also switching
j
and
k
in the second summand)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})[c_{ij}^{k}h_{k}+c_{ij}^{0}h_{0}+X_{i}(A_{j})-X_{j}(A_{i})]X_{j}+(h_{k}+A_{k})(h_{i}+A_{i})\Gamma_{ik}^{j}X_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})[-c_{ij}^{k}A_{k}+c_{ij}^{0}h_{0}+X_{i}(A_{j})-X_{j}(A_{i})]X_{j},

where we added and subtracted
c_{ij}^{k}A_{k}
in the first sum and used that for horizontal coefficients

c_{ij}^{k}+\Gamma_{ik}^{j}

is skew symmetric in
i,k
due to (
56
) and (
57
). Denoting

\zeta_{ij}=dA(X_{i},X_{j})=X_{i}(A_{j})-X_{j}(A_{i})-\sum_{k=1}^{2}c_{ij}^{k}A_{k},

we have

(67)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})[\zeta_{ij}+c_{ij}^{0}h_{0}]X_{j},

which can be rewritten in a more compact form using (
58
) (notice that
\zeta_{ij}=\zeta c_{ij}^{0}
where
\zeta=\zeta_{12}
)

(68)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}

\displaystyle=(\zeta+h_{0})J\dot{\gamma}.

Let us now compute first

\displaystyle\dot{\zeta}=\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\zeta

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})\{h_{i}+A_{i},\zeta\}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})X_{i}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})(\beta_{i}+X_{0}(A_{i})+c_{i0}^{j}A_{j}),

where in the second line we crucially used relations (
53
) and (
54
). Moreover

\displaystyle\dot{h}_{0}=\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}h_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})[\{h_{i},h_{0}\}+{\{A_{i},h_{0}\}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}(h_{i}+A_{i})(c_{i0}^{j}h_{j}-X_{0}(A_{i})),

and summing together we get

\displaystyle\dot{\zeta}+\dot{h}_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\beta_{i}(h_{i}+A_{i})+c_{i0}^{j}(h_{j}+A_{j})(h_{i}+A_{i}).

The conclusion follows recognising that given
v=\sum_{i=1}^{2}v_{i}X_{i}
, one has

(69)

b(v)=\beta(v,X_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\beta_{i}v_{i},\qquad g(\tau(v),v)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,k=1}^{2}(c_{k0}^{i}+c_{i0}^{k})v_{i}v_{k}.

Remark 3.3
.

It is interesting to observe that in the Riemannian case since
\beta=dA
involves the exterior differential, the magnetic field appears in the equation of the geodesics at the level of equation (
68
) (or, equivalently, (
15
)).

In the sub-Riemannian case, we have
\beta=d_{H}A
and the Rumin differential acts as a differential operator on degree
2
on one-forms. Hence the coefficients of the magnetic field contain second order derivatives of the coefficients of
A
and coherently appear in the second equation (
16
).

3.3.
The lifted sub-Riemannian structure

Inspired by the construction described in
Section

1
,
we associate with a horizontal magnetic field
\beta
a lifted sub-Riemannian structure on the line-bundle
\overline{M}=M\times\mathbb{R}
.
The construction we describe assumes the existence of a horizontal magnetic potential
A\in\Omega^{1}_{H}(M)
globally defined. We stress again that, if such a potential does not exist, the construction is still-well defined locally on
M
.

Let
\beta
be a horizontal magnetic field, and
A
a horizontal magnetic potential for
\beta
.
Given
\gamma:[0,T]\to M
a horizontal curve in
M
, and
w_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the lift
\overline{\gamma}=(\gamma,w):[0,T]\to\overline{M}
satisfying
w(0)=w_{0}
with

(70)

w(t)=w_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}A(\dot{\gamma}(s))ds.

Notice that in particular
\dot{\overline{\gamma}}
is in the kernel of the 1-form
dw-A
. In particular if

(71)

\dot{\gamma}(t)=u_{1}(t)X_{1}|_{\gamma(t)}+u_{2}(t)X_{2}|_{\gamma(t)},

then

(72)

\dot{\overline{\gamma}}(t)=u_{1}(t){\left.\kern-1.2pt(X_{1}+A(X_{1})\partial_{w})\right|_{\overline{\gamma}(t)}}+u_{2}(t){\left.\kern-1.2pt(X_{2}+A(X_{2})\partial_{w})\right|_{\overline{\gamma}(t)}},

where we write elements of
T\overline{M}\simeq TM\oplus T\mathbb{R}
as
X+\alpha\partial_{w}
with
X\in TM
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. We want to set the length of
\overline{\gamma}
to be equal to the length of
\gamma
. This is done in the following.

Definition 3.4
.

The
lifted distribution
on
\overline{M}
is the horizontal
\mathrm{rank}
-two distribution given by
\overline{\mathcal{D}}=\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1},Y_{2}\}
, where
Y_{1},Y_{2}
is an orthonormal frame defined by

(73)

Y_{1}=X_{1}+A(X_{1})\partial_{w},\qquad Y_{2}=X_{2}+A(X_{2})\partial_{w}.

and
X_{1},X_{2}
is an orthonormal frame for
\mathcal{D}
.

It is easy to see that the distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is independent of the choice of the frame for
\mathcal{D}
. Indeed, by linearity of
A
, it holds that

\overline{\mathcal{D}}=\mathrm{span}\{X+A(X)\partial_{w}\mid X\in\mathcal{D}\}.

Moreover if
g
denotes the inner product on
\mathcal{D}
and
\pi:\overline{M}\to M
denotes the canonical projection the metric just defined on
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is given by
\overline{g}:=\pi^{*}g
.

Remark 3.5
.

Since
\mathcal{D}
is a contact distribution on
M
, the vector field
[Y_{1},Y_{2}]
is linearly independent from
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
. This follows from the fact that

[Y_{1},Y_{2}]=[X_{1},X_{2}]+\left(X_{1}A_{2}-X_{2}A_{1}\right)\partial_{w},

and that
[X_{1},X_{2}]
is linearly independent from
X_{1},X_{2}
.
More precisely, denoting

(74)

Y_{0}=X_{0}+dA(X_{1},X_{2})\partial_{w},

using (
35
) and (
39
), we obtain

(75)

[Y_{1},Y_{2}]=c_{12}^{1}Y_{1}+c_{12}^{2}Y_{2}+Y_{0}.

We observe that
Y_{0}
is not in the distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
and it is independent of the choice of the orthonormal frame for
\mathcal{D}
.

Remark 3.6
.

Example

1.1
shows how the above construction applied to
M=\mathbb{H}
with the horizontal magnetic field
\beta=d_{H}A=dx\wedge\omega
associated with the horizontal magnetic potential
A=\frac{x^{2}}{2}dy
gives rise to the Engel structure on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

It is interesting to compare the choice of
A
of
Example

1.1
with the horizontal 1-form

(76)

A^{\prime}=-\left(\frac{z}{2}+\frac{xy}{12}\right)dx+\frac{x^{2}}{12}dy.

One can check that

A-A^{\prime}=\left(\frac{z}{2}+\frac{xy}{12}\right)dx+\frac{5x^{2}}{12}dy=d_{H}\left(\frac{xz}{2}+\frac{x^{2}y}{6}\right).

The lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
associated to
A^{\prime}
differs from the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
associated to
A
, nevertheless, the commutator relations recover the Lie algebra structure of the Engel group.

In particular, we have that
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
is generated by

(77)

Y^{\prime}_{1}=\partial_{x}-\dfrac{y}{2}\partial_{z}-\left(\frac{z}{2}+\frac{xy}{12}\right)\partial_{w},\qquad Y^{\prime}_{2}=\partial_{y}+\dfrac{x}{2}\partial_{z}+\frac{x^{2}}{12}\partial_{w}.

And it holds that
[Y^{\prime}_{1},Y^{\prime}_{2}]=\partial_{z}+\frac{x}{2}\partial_{w}
, while
[Y^{\prime}_{1},[Y^{\prime}_{1},Y^{\prime}_{2}]]=\partial_{w}
and
[Y^{\prime}_{2},[Y^{\prime}_{1},Y^{\prime}_{2}]]=0
.

One can prove, more in general, that given
A\in\Omega^{1}_{H}(M)
and
A^{\prime}=A+d_{H}f
, with
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.
Then, the Lie algebras
\mathrm{Lie}\,\overline{\mathcal{D}}(A)
and
\mathrm{Lie}\,\overline{\mathcal{D}}(A^{\prime})
defined by the distribution associated with the two different potentials are isomorphic.

3.4.
Normal extremals

In this section we prove the following fact, relating the sub-Riemannian Hamiltonian flow on
\overline{M}
and the magnetic flow on
M
.

Proposition 9
.

The sub-Riemannian normal extremal trajectories for the lifted sub-Riemannian structure
(\overline{M},\overline{\mathcal{D}},\overline{g})
project onto magnetic geodesics on
M
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the construction. We report here a sketch of the proof for completeness.
In what follows we identify
\overline{M}=M\times\mathbb{R}
where points are denoted by pairs
(p,w)
, and
T\overline{M}\simeq TM\oplus T\mathbb{R}
. Similarly we can consider the identification
T^{*}\overline{M}\simeq T^{*}M\oplus T^{*}\mathbb{R}
, denoting points
\zeta=(\lambda,\zeta_{w})\in T^{*}M\oplus T^{*}\mathbb{R}
.

The sub-Riemannian Hamiltonian associated with the lifted structure
(\overline{M},\overline{D},\overline{g})
is

(78)

\overline{H}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1,2}\langle\zeta,Y_{i}\rangle^{2}.

Using the fact that
\zeta=(\lambda,\zeta_{w})
and
Y_{i}=X_{i}+A_{i}\partial_{w}
we can decompose

\langle\zeta,Y_{i}\rangle=\langle\lambda,X_{i}\rangle+A_{i}\langle\zeta,\partial_{w}\rangle=h_{i}+A_{i}\zeta_{w},

where
h_{i}=\langle\lambda,X_{i}\rangle
is the Hamitonian linear on fibers on
T^{*}M
(cf. Section
2.2
).
Hence we can rewrite (
78
) as

(79)

\overline{H}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1,2}(h_{i}+A_{i}\zeta_{w})^{2}.

Since the functions
A_{i}=A_{i}(p)
do not depend on the variable
w
, the Hamiltonian
\overline{H}
is independent of
w
as well. This implies that along the corresponding Hamiltonian flow one has
\dot{\zeta}_{w}=0
. Setting
\zeta_{w}=q
(this is the charge of the particle!) one can rewrite the Hamiltonian (
79
) as

(80)

\overline{H}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1,2}(h_{i}+qA_{i})^{2}.

Writing Hamiltonian equations and comparing with the magnetic Hamiltonian
H_{A}
given in (
63
) it readily follows that projections on
M
of integral curves of the Hamiltonian vector field associated with
\overline{H}
on
\overline{M}
are magnetic geodesics given in Proposition
1
.
∎

3.5.
On the Maxwell equations

The space of the Rumin two-forms
\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
has dimension two. The closure condition on the magnetic field provides a constraint, hence the coefficients of a magnetic field are not independent.
In this section, we describe how these coefficients interact, starting from reinterpreting the closure condition in terms of a Maxwell-type equation.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
be a horizontal magnetic field.
The condition
d\beta=0
is equivalent to the following

(81)

\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(-\beta_{2}X_{1}+\beta_{1}X_{2})=0.

where
\mu
is the smooth measure associated with the Popp’s volume.

Proof.

On the one hand, recalling (
40
), we have that

(82)

d\beta=d\left(\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\right)=\left(X_{1}\beta_{2}-X_{2}\beta_{1}-c^{1}_{12}\beta_{1}-c^{2}_{12}\beta_{2}\right)\nu_{1}\wedge\nu_{2}\wedge\omega.

On the other hand, by the linearity of the divergence operator and exploiting (
44
) and (
43
), it holds that

(83)

\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(-\beta_{2}X_{1}+\beta_{1}X_{2})=-X_{1}\beta_{2}+c^{2}_{12}\beta_{2}+X_{2}\beta_{1}+c^{1}_{12}\beta_{1}.

Therefore, comparing the coefficient of the volume form in (
82
) with the expression in (
83
), we deduce that
d\beta=0
if and only if
\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(-\beta_{2}X_{1}+\beta_{1}X_{2})=0
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Formula (
81
) can be seen as the analog of Maxwell’s equation in classical magnetic theory in the three-dimensional Euclidean space, with
\mu
the Euclidean volume measure and in the absence of an electric field.
Maxwell’s equations were studied in the setting of Carnot groups in terms of the Rumin complex in
[
11
]
.

Remark 3.8
.

In
[
8
, Proposition 4.2]
, the authors deduce an explicit formula expressing the dependence between the coefficients of a horizontal magnetic field in the Heisenberg group written with respect to the horizontal polar frame (we follow the notation of
Example

2.2
).

(84)

R=\frac{x}{r}X_{1}+\frac{y}{r}X_{2},\qquad\Phi=-\frac{y}{r}X_{1}+\frac{x}{r}X_{2},

where
r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}
.
Their formula is equivalent to (
81
).

In the following result we use (
81
) to characterize the coefficient
\beta_{1}
of a magnetic field of the form
\beta=\beta_{1}dx\wedge\omega
in the case of the Heisenberg group.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\beta=\beta_{1}dx\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(\mathbb{H})
be a horizontal magnetic field in the Heisenberg group.
Then, there exists
g\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
such that

(85)

\beta_{1}(x,y,z)=g\left(x,z-\frac{xy}{2}\right).

Proof.

We start by observing that in the Heisenberg group the only non trivial commutator is given by
[X_{1},X_{2}]=X_{0}
. Then
c_{12}^{1}=c_{12}^{2}=0
, thus by (
43
) and (
35
) it holds that
\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(X_{2})=0
, where
\mu
is the smooth measure associated with the Popp’s volume. From (
81
), we then obtain the following PDE:

(86)

0=X_{2}(\beta_{1})=\left(\partial_{y}+\frac{x}{2}\partial_{z}\right)\beta_{1},

which can be solved by the method of characteristics.
More precisely, the function
\beta_{1}
is constant along the flow of
X_{2}
.
Computing explicitly the flow of
X_{2}
we have

(87)

\begin{cases}\dot{x}=0\\
\dot{y}=1\\
\dot{z}=\frac{x}{2}\end{cases}\qquad\implies\qquad\begin{cases}x(t)=x(0)\\
y(t)=y(0)+s\\
z(t)=z(0)+\frac{x(0)}{2}t\end{cases}.

In particular, given an initial condition
\gamma(0)=(x_{0},0,z_{0})
(defined on a surface transversal to
X_{1}
) we have that
\gamma(t)=e^{tX_{1}}\gamma(0)=(x_{0},s,z_{0}+\frac{x_{0}}{2}s)
and

\beta_{1}(\gamma(t))=\beta_{1}(\gamma(0))=\beta(x(t),0,z(t)-\frac{1}{2}x(t)y(t)).

From (
87
) it
is easy to see that both quantities
x
and
z-\frac{1}{2}xy
are constant along the flow.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

The previous consideration can be extended to
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(\mathbb{H})
horizontal magnetic field in the Heisenberg group of the form
\beta=f(b_{1}dx+b_{2}dy)\wedge\omega
, with
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}=1
.
Then, taking into account the following change of variables

(88)

u=b_{1}x+b_{2}y,\qquad v=-b_{2}x+b_{1}y,

with the same arguments, it is possible to show that there exists a suitable
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
f(u,v,z)=g\left(u,z-\frac{1}{2}uv\right)
.

4.
Non-vanishing magnetic fields

In this section we study the bracket generating properties of the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
and its abnormal curves in the case when
\beta
is a non-vanishing horizontal magnetic field.

Recall that
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is generated by
Y_{1},Y_{2}
in (
73
), which is also an orthonormal frame for the metric. Here

Y_{0}
denotes the lift of the Reeb vector field as in (
74
).

Lemma 12
.

Let
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
be a horizontal magnetic field and let
A=A_{1}\nu_{1}+A_{2}\nu_{2}
be a magnetic potential for
\beta
.
For
i=1,2
, it holds that

(89)

[Y_{i},Y_{0}]=\beta_{i}\partial_{w}\mod\overline{\mathcal{D}}

In particular, if
\beta
is never vanishing, the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is bracket generating of step
3
.

Proof.

We first establish the following identity for
i=1,2
:

(90)

[Y_{i},Y_{0}]=[X_{i},X_{0}]+A([X_{i},X_{0}])\partial_{w}+\beta_{i}\partial_{w}.

This yields (
89
) since the distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is spanned by vector fields of the form
X+A(X)\partial_{w}
.
For
i=1,2
, using (
73
) and (
74
), we compute:

(91)

[Y_{i},Y_{0}]=[X_{i},X_{0}]+(X_{i}(dA(X_{1},X_{2}))-X_{0}(A_{i}))\partial_{w}.

Define the
1
-form

A^{\prime}=A_{1}\nu_{1}+A_{2}\nu_{2}+dA(X_{1},X_{2})\omega
.
Then, for
i=1,2
, we can rewrite

(92)

X_{i}(dA(X_{1},X_{2}))-X_{0}(A_{i})=X_{i}(A^{\prime}(X_{0}))-X_{0}(A^{\prime}(X_{i})).

Recalling (
48
), we know that
d_{H}A=dA^{\prime}
. Applying Cartan’s formula (
39
), we obtain:

(93)

X_{i}(dA(X_{1},X_{2}))-X_{0}(A_{i})=A^{\prime}([X_{i},X_{0}])+\beta_{i}.

Since
\omega([X_{i},X_{0}])=0
, then

A^{\prime}([X_{i},X_{0}])=A([X_{i},X_{0}]),

which verifies the desired identity (

).

Finally, since
\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{0}\}=\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
and the coordinate vector field
\partial_{w}
is independent of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
, we conclude that
[Y_{i},Y_{0}]
is independent of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
if and only if
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\neq(0,0)
.
∎

Let
\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H}
be a never vanishing horizontal magnetic field. We define the following frame for the distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
associated with
\beta

(94)

F_{1}=\beta_{1}Y_{1}+\beta_{2}Y_{2},\qquad F_{2}=-\beta_{2}Y_{1}+\beta_{1}Y_{2}.

Lemma 13
.

For
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
as in (
94
) we have that
[F_{1},\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}]\not\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
and
[F_{2},\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}]\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}.

More precisely we have

(95)

[F_{1},Y_{0}]=\left(\beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}\right)\partial_{w}\mod\overline{\mathcal{D}},\qquad[F_{2},Y_{0}]\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}.

Proof.

Since
F_{1},F_{2}
belong to
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
, we are reduced to prove that
[F_{1},Y_{0}]\not\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
and that
[F_{2},Y_{0}]\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
.
Recalling (
35
) and (
89
), we first compute

(96)

\displaystyle[F_{1},Y_{0}]

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}[Y_{1},Y_{0}]+\beta_{2}[Y_{2},Y_{0}]-X_{0}\left(\beta_{1}\right)Y_{1}-X_{0}\left(\beta_{2}\right)Y_{2}

(97)

\displaystyle=\left(\beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}\right)\partial_{w}\mod\overline{\mathcal{D}}.

Since by assumption
\left(\beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}\right)\neq 0
and
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}=\mathrm{span}\left\{F_{1},F_{2},Y_{0}\right\}
, we deduce
[F_{1},Y_{0}]\not\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
.

Similarily, again using (
89
), we obtain that

(98)

\displaystyle[F_{2},Y_{0}]

\displaystyle=-\beta_{2}[Y_{1},Y_{0}]+\beta_{1}[Y_{2},Y_{0}]\mod\overline{\mathcal{D}}

(99)

\displaystyle=-\beta_{2}\beta_{1}\partial_{w}+\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\partial_{w}=0\mod\overline{\mathcal{D}}.

which implies that
[F_{2},Y_{0}]\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Let
\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\omega\}
be the dual frame corresponding to
\{\pi_{*}F_{1},\pi_{*}F_{2},X_{0}\}
, where
\pi:\overline{M}\to M
is the canonical projection. We have that
\beta
is “rectified” as follows

(100)

\beta=\|\beta\|^{2}\eta_{1}\wedge\omega,

where
\|\beta\|:M\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as
\|\beta\|=\sqrt{\beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}}
and it is called the
magnitude
of
\beta
.

Now we can prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 14
.

Let
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{H}
be a never vanishing horizontal magnetic field on
M
.
A horizontal curve
\overline{\gamma}
in
\overline{M}
is an abnormal extremal trajectory if and only if its projection
\gamma
is a horizontal curve on
M
which is a characteristic curve of the magnetic field
\beta
on
M
, i.e., satisfies
\iota_{\dot{\gamma}}\beta=0
.

Remark 4.2
.

Notice that, choosing a frame
X_{1},X_{2}
for
\mathcal{D}
as before, characteristic curves
\gamma
of the magnetic field
\beta
on
M
are parallel to
-\beta_{2}X_{1}+\beta_{1}X_{2}
. In particular, we observe that this implies
b(\dot{\gamma})=0
in equation (
16
), suggesting that along abnormal extremal trajectories the magnetic field does not affect the dynamics (see Remark
1.2
).

Proof.

We consider on
\overline{M}
the basis of the tangent space given by
\{Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{0},\partial_{w}\}
where as usual

(101)

\displaystyle Y_{i}

\displaystyle=X_{i}+A_{i}\partial_{w},\qquad i=1,2,

(102)

\displaystyle Y_{0}

\displaystyle=X_{0}+dA(X_{1},X_{2})\partial_{w}.

and in the above formulas we identify
T\overline{M}\simeq TM\oplus T\mathbb{R}
. Similarly we can consider the identification
T^{*}\overline{M}\simeq T^{*}M\oplus T^{*}\mathbb{R}
, hence we can treat the basis of 1-forms
\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{0}\}
dual to
\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{0}\}
as 1-forms on
\overline{M}
, where we recall that
\nu_{0}=\omega
is the contact form on
M
.
Following this notation, the basis
\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{0},\tau\}
is dual to
\{Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{0},\partial_{w}\}
, where we set

(103)

\tau=dw-A_{1}\nu_{1}-A_{2}\nu_{2}-dA(X_{1},X_{2})\nu_{0}.

Notice that
\tau
satisfies the identities

(104)

\tau=dw-A^{\prime},\qquad d\tau=-\beta,

where
A^{\prime}
is the modified potential in such a way that
dA^{\prime}=d_{H}A=\beta
(cf. (
48
)).

We now use Proposition
5
. We first compute the symplectic form
\sigma
as a two form on
T^{*}\overline{M}
. To describe
\sigma
we need a basis of its tangent space
T(T^{*}\overline{M})
. Let
p:T^{*}\overline{M}\to\overline{M}
the canonical projection onto the base. Consider the isomorphism for
\zeta\in T^{*}\overline{M}

(105)

T_{\zeta}(T^{*}\overline{M})\simeq T_{p(\zeta)}\overline{M}\oplus\ker p_{*,\zeta}\simeq\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{0},\partial_{w},\partial_{\zeta_{1}},\partial_{\zeta_{2}},\partial_{\zeta_{0}},\partial_{\zeta_{w}}\},

where
p_{*,\zeta}
denotes the differential of
p
at
\zeta
, and we denote by
\zeta_{i}
, for
i=1,2,0,w
, the linear on fiber functions associated to the basis given by
\{Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{0},\partial_{w}\}
. Namely
\zeta_{i}=\langle\zeta,Y_{i}\rangle
for
i=0,1,2,
and
\zeta_{w}=\langle\zeta,\partial_{w}\rangle
.

We have that
\sigma
is the differential of the tautological 1-form
s=\zeta_{1}\nu_{1}+\zeta_{2}\nu_{2}+\zeta_{0}\nu_{0}+\zeta_{w}\tau
(one can see
[
2
, Section 4.2]
for more details on the symplectic form in this framework). We have to compute

\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}^{\perp}}=ds|_{\mathcal{D}^{\perp}}=d\left(s|_{\mathcal{D}^{\perp}}\right),

since the differential commutes with the restriction. Now recall that
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}=H^{-1}(0)
and in this case the Hamiltonian
H
writes as

H=\frac{1}{2}(\zeta_{1}^{2}+\zeta^{2}_{2}),

so that on
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
one has
\zeta_{1}=\zeta_{2}=0
. Hence

\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}^{\perp}}=d\left(\zeta_{0}\nu_{0}+\zeta_{w}\tau\right).

Since Goh conditions for rank 2 distributions are always satisfied (cf.
[
2
, Section 12.4]
), using (
75
) one can see that

(106)

\zeta_{1}=\zeta_{2}=0\qquad\text{implies}\qquad\zeta_{0}=0.

Hence characteristic curves for
\sigma
lives in
\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\perp}:=\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\cap\zeta_{0}^{-1}(0)
. We are reduced to compute

\displaystyle\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\perp}}=d\left(\zeta_{w}\tau\right)

\displaystyle=d\zeta_{w}\wedge\tau+\zeta_{w}d\tau

\displaystyle=d\zeta_{w}\wedge\tau-\zeta_{w}\beta,

where in the last equality we used (
104
).
Notice that
\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\perp}
is a 5-dimensional manifold in
T^{*}M
. By considering the isomorphism (
105
),
we can write

T\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\perp}=\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{0},\partial_{w},\partial_{\zeta_{w}}\}.

We write more explicitly

\displaystyle\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\perp}}=d\zeta_{w}\wedge\tau-\zeta_{w}(\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\nu_{0}+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\nu_{0}),

and we recall that (a) the distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}=\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1},Y_{2}\}
is contained in
\ker\tau
by (
101
) and (
103
), (b)
\zeta_{w}\neq 0
due to identities (
106
) and the fact that the lift of the abnormal trajectory is never vanishing by Proposition
5
. It is then simple to observe that the kernel of
\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\perp}}
coincides with the vector
-\beta_{2}Y_{1}+\beta_{1}Y_{2}
, which projects on the kernel of the magnetic field
\beta
as a 2-form.
∎

Example 4.3
.

Recall that in
Example

1.1
we built the classical Engel group equipping the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
with the magnetic field
\beta=dx\wedge\omega
.
Since
\beta
is non-vanishing, by
Proposition

14
it follows that the abnormal curves in the lifted space are tangent to

and a parametrization of an abnormal curve

5.
Vanishing magnetic fields

In
Lemma

12
we proved that if
\beta
is a non-vanishing magnetic field, then the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is bracket generating of step 3 and
Proposition

14
characterizes the corresponding abnormal curves.
In this section, we investigate the case when
\beta
vanishes in some region.

We denote with
\mathcal{Z}\subset M
the zero locus of
\beta
, i.e., the set where
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
, and we study the lifted sub-Riemannian structure on
\overline{M}
at
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.
The first result we present can be seen as an extension of
Lemma

12
.

Proposition 15
.

Let
p\in\mathcal{Z}
,
k\in\mathbb{N},k\geq 1
.
The step of the distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\overline{M}
is equal to
k+3
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied

(a)

There exists a choice of indexes
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{k})\in\{1,2\}^{k+1}
such that

(108)

X_{i_{k}}\ldots X_{i_{1}}\beta_{i_{0}}(p)\neq 0;

(b)

For every choice of indexes
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{j})\in\{1,2\}^{j+1}
with
j=0,\ldots,k-1
it holds that

(109)

X_{i_{j}}\ldots X_{i_{1}}\beta_{i_{0}}(p)=0.

Proof.

Let
A
be a horizontal potential for
\beta
, i.e., such that
\beta=d_{H}A
.
Let
Y_{1},Y_{2}
be the generating family for
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
as in (
73
),
and let
Y_{0}
be as in (
74
).
It holds that

(110)

\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}=\mathrm{span}\left\{Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{0}\right\}.

Moreover, for
i\in\{1,2\}
, identity (
89
) gives

(111)

[Y_{i},Y_{0}]=\beta_{i}\partial_{w}\mod\overline{\mathcal{D}}.

Since
\partial_{w}
is independent from
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
, we have that

(112)

\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{3}=\mathrm{span}\left\{Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{0},\beta_{1}\partial_{w},\beta_{2}\partial_{w}\right\}.

We deduce that
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{3}=\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
at
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
since
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
on
\mathcal{Z}
.
Recursively, one finds that, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 1

(113)

\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{k+3}=\mathrm{span}\left\{Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{0},\left(X_{i_{j}}\ldots X_{i_{1}}\beta_{i_{0}}\right)\partial_{w}\mid j=0,\ldots,k,\ (i_{0},\ldots,i_{j})\in\{1,2\}^{j+1}\right\}.

It follows that
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{k+3}=\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
on
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
if and only if
X_{i_{j}}\ldots X_{i_{1}}\beta_{i_{0}}(p)=0
for all
j=0,\ldots,k
and
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{j})\in\{1,2\}^{j+1}
. This concludes the proof since
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{k+3}\neq\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
implies
\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{k+3}=T\overline{M}
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

We stress that if there exists a point
p\in\mathcal{Z}
such that any horizontal derivation of any order of
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
vanishes at such a point, then the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is not bracket generating at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, since any derivation can be written as a combination of horizontal ones, this is equivalent to require that any derivation of any order vanishes at
p
.

Finally, let us stress that the statement of Proposition
15
is actually independent of the chosen (orthonormal) frame for
\mathcal{D}
in
M
.

Under the assumption that the magnetic field generates a sub-Riemannian structure, then every horizontal curve in the lift of the zero locus is an abnormal extremal trajectory.

Proposition 16
.

Let
\overline{\gamma}
be a horizontal curve in
\overline{M}
contained in
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
. Then
\overline{\gamma}
is an abnormal extremal trajectory for the lifted sub-Riemannian structure on
\overline{M}
.

Proof.

The same argument given in the proof of Proposition
14
yields for the restriction of the symplectic form
\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\perp}:=\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\cap\zeta_{0}^{-1}(0)

\displaystyle\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\perp}}=d\left(\zeta_{w}\tau\right)

\displaystyle=d\zeta_{w}\wedge\tau+\zeta_{w}d\tau

\displaystyle=d\zeta_{w}\wedge\tau-\zeta_{w}\beta.

Restricting to curves on
T^{*}\overline{M}
which projects on
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
, then
\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\perp}}=d\zeta_{w}\wedge\tau
.
Denoting points on
T^{*}\overline{M}
as tuples
(\overline{p},\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{0},\zeta_{w})
then the lift
(\overline{\gamma}(t),0,0,0,c)
of the curve
\overline{\gamma}(t)
on
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\overline{M}
, for some
c\neq 0
, is a characteristic curve of
\sigma|_{\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\perp}}
. Hence
\overline{\gamma}
is an abnormal extremal trajectory.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Abnormal extremal trajectory can also be a concatenation of a curve in the zero locus of the magnetic field and a characteristic curve of
\beta
where
\beta
is non-vanishing. An explicit example of such an abnormal extremal trajectory is provided in Example
6.4
.

In the following we analyze the step of the lifted distribution at
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\overline{M}
in relation to the rank of the map
(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}):TM\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Namely, we consider
p\in\mathcal{Z}
and the rank of the linear map
{\left.\kern-1.2pt(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2})\right|_{p}}:T_{p}M\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proposition 17
.

Let
\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega
be a horizontal magnetic field. Fix
p\in\mathcal{Z}
.

(1)

If
\mathrm{rank}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)\right|_{p}}=2
, then the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\overline{M}
is
4
;

(2)

If
\mathrm{rank}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)\right|_{p}}=1
, then the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\overline{M}
is
4
or
5
;

(3)

If
\mathrm{rank}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)\right|_{p}}=0
, then the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\overline{M}
is
\geq 5
.

Proof.

First observe that the matrix representing
\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)
written with respect to the frame
X_{1},X_{2},X_{0}
, is given by

(114)

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}X_{1}\beta_{1}&X_{2}\beta_{1}&X_{0}\beta_{1}\\
X_{1}\beta_{2}&X_{2}\beta_{2}&X_{0}\beta_{2}\end{array}\right).

1
.
First, assume that
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)=2
at
p
. In this case at least two columns of (
114
) evaluated at
p
are independent and not vanishing.
Hence, there exist
i,j\in\{1,2\}
such that
X_{i}\beta_{j}(p)\neq 0
and the conclusion holds by
Proposition

15
.

2
.
Let us now assume
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)=1
at
p
, then at least one column in (
114
) evaluated at
p
is not identically zero.
If this happens for one of the first two columns, we can argue as in the proof of (i), and the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
{\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}}
is
4
.
If the first two columns are both identically zero, any first order horizontal derivation of
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
is vanishing at
p
, and there exists
i\in\{1,2\}
such that
X_{0}\beta_{i}(p)\neq 0
.
Recalling (
35
), we have that

(115)

[X_{1},X_{2}]\beta_{i}(p)=c^{1}_{12}X_{1}\beta_{i}(p)+c^{2}_{12}X_{2}\beta_{i}(p)+X_{0}\beta_{i}(p)=X_{0}\beta_{i}(p).

Since in addition
[X_{1},X_{2}]\beta_{i}=X_{1}X_{2}\beta_{i}-X_{2}X_{1}\beta_{i}
, we conclude that at least a second order derivative does not vanish at
p
.
The step at
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
{\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}}
is then
5
.

3
.
Assume
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)=0
at
p
, then the matrix (
114
) is identically zero when evaluated at
p
. Hence, for all
i,j\in\{1,2\}
we have
X_{i}\beta_{j}(p)=0
.
Proposition

15
implies that the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}
is at least
5
.
∎

We illustrate the content of
Proposition

17
on some examples of horizontal magnetic fields
\beta
on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
(we refer to
Example

1.1
for the notation in
\mathbb{H}
).

Example 5.3

(The rank-2 case)
.

If
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)=2
at a point
p\in\mathcal{Z}
, then
\mathcal{Z}
is (locally) a curve regularly defined by the map
\left(\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right):M\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We highlight that the curve being horizontal or not does not affect the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
, which is always equal to
4
.

Two representative examples on
\mathbb{H}
are given by the following choices:

(116)

\beta=x\,dx\wedge\omega+y\,dy\wedge\omega,\qquad\beta^{\prime}=\left(z-\frac{xy}{2}\right)dx\wedge\omega+y\,dy\wedge\omega.

The zero locus
\mathcal{Z}
of
\beta
is the non-horizontal curve
\{x=y=0\}
, the integral curve of
X_{0}
. The zero locus
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
of

is the horizontal curve
\{z=y=0\}
, the integral curve of
X_{1}
.

Example 5.4

(Step variation in the rank-1 case)
.

We show that if
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)=1
on
\mathcal{Z}
, the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is not necessarily constant on
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Let us consider on
\mathbb{H}

(117)

\beta=4z\,dx\wedge\omega+x^{2}\,dy\wedge\omega.

The zero locus is the curve
\mathcal{Z}=\{(0,y,0)\mid y\in\mathbb{R}\}
, so that, writing
\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)
with respect to the frame
X_{1},X_{2},X_{0}
given in
Example

2.2
, we have

(118)

\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-2y&2x&4\\
2x&0&0\end{array}\right),\qquad{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)\right|_{\mathcal{Z}}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-2y&0&4\\

Hence,
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)=1
at

.
We have that the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is
5
at
(0,0,0)\times\mathbb{R}
and
4
at points
(0,y,0)\times\mathbb{R}
with

.
Indeed, if

X_{1}\beta_{1}(0,y,0)=2y\neq 0
implying that the step is

.
For

and

Example 5.5

(The rank-0 case)
.

We exhibit an example where
\mathrm{rank}{\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)}=0
in
\mathcal{Z}
, for which the sub-Riemannian structure have arbitrary step
\geq 5
at
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Fix

and consider
\beta=\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,dx\wedge\omega
on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
.
The zero locus of
\beta
is given by the surface
\mathcal{Z}=\{x=0\}
. Since

, it is easy to check that the rank is zero at
\mathcal{Z}
.

Let

X_{i_{k}}\ldots X_{i_{1}}\beta_{1}(p)=X_{i_{k}}\ldots X_{i_{1}}\beta_{2}(p)=0

and choice of indices

Being

, we conclude by Proposition

that the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at

is

.

5.1.
Characteristic points in the rank-1 case

If
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)\right|_{p}}:T_{p}M\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
has rank 1 at

, there exists
i\in\{1,2\}
such that

.
Therefore, up to restricting the domain to an open neighborhood of

, it is well defined a regular surface
\Sigma=\beta_{i}^{-1}(0)
that contains the zero locus
\mathcal{Z}
of the magnetic field
\beta
around
p
.

Recall that, given
\Sigma
a surface in
M
, we say that a point

is

if
\mathcal{D}_{p}=T_{p}\Sigma
.
Denote with
\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)
the set of characteristic points in
\Sigma
.
The following result characterizes the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
in geometric terms.

Proposition 18
.

Let
\beta=\beta_{1}\nu_{1}\wedge\omega+\beta_{2}\nu_{2}\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H}(M)
be a horizontal magnetic field such that
\mathrm{rank}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)\right|_{p}}=1
with
p\in\mathcal{Z}
.
Let
i\in\{1,2\}
such that
{\left.\kern-1.2ptd\beta_{i}\right|_{p}}\neq 0

and let
\Sigma
be the smooth surface locally defined by
\beta_{i}
.
The following are equivalent

(1)

p
is a characteristic point in
\Sigma
;

(2)

\overline{\mathcal{D}}
has step
5
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 5.6
.

Using the notation of Proposition
18
above, we observe that a point
p\in\Sigma
is characteristic if and only if
\overline{\mathcal{D}}\subset T\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}
. The inclusion here is crucial since in
\overline{M}
the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is two-dimensional while
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
is three-dimensional.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
i=1
. The rank assumption implies that
{\left.\kern-1.2ptd\beta_{2}\right|_{p}}=c{\left.\kern-1.2ptd\beta_{1}\right|_{p}}
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Consequently,
\ker d\beta_{1}|_{p}\subseteq\ker d\beta_{2}|_{p}
.

A point
p\in\Sigma=\beta_{1}^{-1}(0)
is characteristic if and only if
X_{1}\beta_{1}(p)=X_{2}\beta_{1}(p)=0
. Due to the inclusion of the kernels,
p
is characteristic in
\Sigma
if and only if
X_{i}\beta_{j}(p)=0
for all
i,j\in\{1,2\}
.

By
Proposition

15
, the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}
is larger or equal than
5
.
By
Proposition

17
the assumption
\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)=1
at a point
p\in\mathcal{Z}
implies that the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
must be either
4
or
5
. We conclude that the step is precisely
5
.
∎

.

Let us consider again the horizontal magnetic field

regarding the step of

could also be obtained by applying
Proposition

18
.

Remark 5.8
.

The two implications
2

\Leftrightarrow

1
in

18
do not hold if
{\mathrm{rank}\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)}\neq 1
at

\mathbb{H}
(for the notations we refer to

1.1

The fact that

\not\Rightarrow

can be realised by considering

\beta=\frac{x^{2}}{2}\,dx\wedge\omega

The zero locus
\mathcal{Z}=\{x=0\}
is a surface that does not contain characteristic points, but the step of
{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}
is
5
at
{\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}}

5.5

Here
\mathrm{rank}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)\right|_{\mathcal{Z}}}=0

To observe that

\not\Rightarrow

\beta=\left(z-\frac{xy}{2}\right)dx\wedge\omega+y\,dy\wedge\omega
in the Heisenberg group.
The zero locus is the horizontal curve
\mathcal{Z}=\{z=y=0\}

\mathrm{rank}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2}\right)\right|_{\mathcal{Z}}}=2

Proposition

6.
Examples and final remarks

We conclude by considering two particular classes of examples.

6.1.
A class of magnetic fields having a given surface as zero locus

In the following, given a regular surface
\Sigma\subset M
, we consider a class of horizontal magnetic fields whose zero locus coincides with
\Sigma
. This class contains in particular Example
5.5
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 1
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
be a submersion. Consider a horizontal magnetic field of the form

(119)

\beta=\frac{f^{n}}{n!}(b_{1}\nu_{1}+b_{2}\nu_{2})\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H},

where
b_{1},b_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M)
with
b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}\neq 0.

Then, the vector field
-b_{2}X_{1}+b_{1}X_{2}
is tangent to the zero locus of
\beta
given by
\Sigma=f^{-1}(0)
.
Moreover,
\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)
is a 1-dimensional smooth manifold.

Proof.

We start by observing that, up to reabsorbing a non zero factor in the function
f
(thus without changing the zero level set), we can assume that
b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}=1
.
Let us consider the orthonormal frame for
\mathcal{D}
:

(120)

V_{1}=b_{1}X_{1}+b_{2}X_{2},\qquad V_{2}=-b_{2}X_{1}+b_{1}X_{2},

with its corresponding dual basis:

(121)

\eta_{1}=b_{1}\nu_{1}+b_{2}\nu_{2},\qquad\eta_{2}=-b_{2}\nu_{1}+b_{1}\nu_{2}.

In this basis, we have

(122)

\beta=\frac{f^{n}}{n!}\eta_{1}\wedge\omega.

Claim (i)
. For every
p\in\Sigma
we have
V_{2}f(p)=0
, i.e., the vector field
V_{2}
is tangent to
\Sigma=f^{-1}(0)
.

Let
\mu
be the measure associated with the Popp’s volume.
Recalling the closure condition (
81
), and applying the Leibniz’s rule for the divergence operator in (
44
), we obtain that

(123)

0=\mathrm{div}_{\mu}\left(\frac{f^{n}}{n!}V_{2}\right)=\frac{f^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\left(V_{2}f+\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(V_{2})\frac{f}{n}\right).

Therefore, on
M\setminus\Sigma
(i.e., the set where
f\neq 0
) we have

(124)

V_{2}f+\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(V_{2})\frac{f}{n}=0.

Since
\Sigma=f^{-1}(0)
is a closed set with empty interior, identity (
124
) holds by continuity also on
\Sigma
and since on
\Sigma
we have
f=0
, the Claim (i) is true.

To prove that
\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)
is a 1-dimensional smooth manifold, first notice that
\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)\subset\Sigma
is the zero level-set of the map
\Phi:M\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined as
\Phi=(f,V_{1}f)
.
Indeed, we have that
p\in\Sigma
is characteristic if and only if
V_{1}f(p)=V_{2}f(p)=0
but
p\in\Sigma
implies that
V_{2}f(p)=0
by (
124
).

In order to conclude, we show that for every
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)
it holds
\mathrm{rank}\,d_{p}\Phi=2
.
Let us consider the matrix representing
d\Phi
with respect to the frame
V_{1},V_{2},X_{0}
for
TM
:

(125)

d\Phi=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}V_{1}f&V_{2}f&X_{0}f\\
V_{1}V_{1}f&V_{2}V_{1}f&X_{0}V_{1}f\end{array}\right).

Claim (ii):
Let
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)
. We have that
V_{2}V_{1}f(p)=-X_{0}f(p)
.

To prove this claim, we first observe that,
differentiating (
124
), one has

(126)

V_{1}V_{2}f=V_{1}\left(-\frac{f}{n}\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(V_{2})\right)=-\frac{V_{2}f}{n}\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(V_{2})-\frac{f}{n}V_{2}\left(\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(V_{2})\right).

For
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)
, where
f=V_{2}f=0
, we deduce that
V_{1}V_{2}f(p)=0
.

On the other hand, being
V_{1},V_{2}
an orthonormal frame for
\mathcal{D}
with
d\omega(V_{1},V_{2})=-1
, by (
35
) there exist
a^{1}_{12},a^{2}_{12}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

(127)

[V_{1},V_{2}]=a^{1}_{12}V_{1}+a^{2}_{12}V_{2}+X_{0}.

Hence, we obtain that
[V_{1},V_{2}]f(p)=X_{0}f(p)
for all
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)
.
Being
V_{1}V_{2}f(p)=0
, we deduce

(128)

V_{2}V_{1}f(p)=-X_{0}f(p),

which proves Claim (ii). Replacing (
128
) and
V_{1}V_{2}f(p)=0
in (
125
), we have that

(129)

d_{p}\Phi=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&X_{0}f(p)\\
V_{1}V_{1}f(p)&-X_{0}f(p)&X_{0}V_{1}f(p)\end{array}\right).

Since, by assumption,
f
is a submersion and
V_{1}f(p)=V_{2}f(p)=0
, then necessarily
X_{0}f(p)\neq 0
. Hence we conclude that
\mathrm{rank}\,d_{p}\Phi=2
, and this concludes the proof.
∎

We now compute the step of the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
associated with the class of examples given in
Lemma

19
.

Lemma 20
.

Under the assumptions of
Lemma

19
, it holds that

(1)

The step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
(M\setminus\Sigma)\times\mathbb{R}
is
3
;

(2)

The step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
(\Sigma\setminus{\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)})\times\mathbb{R}
is
n+3
;

(3)

The step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)\times\mathbb{R}
is
2n+3
.

Proof.

We apply
Proposition

15
with respect to the orthonormal frame
V_{1},V_{2}
for
\mathcal{D}
defined in the proof of
Lemma

19
.
Notice that with this choice, we have
\beta_{1}=\frac{1}{n!}f^{n}
and
\beta_{2}=0
.
Item
1
follows immediately since on
M\setminus\Sigma
the magnetic field is non-vanishing.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 1
and
({i_{1}},\ldots,{i_{k}})\in\{1,2\}^{k}
. Then

(130)

V_{i_{k}}\ldots V_{i_{1}}\beta_{1}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in D_{k,n}}f^{e}\prod_{j\colon r_{j}=1}V_{i_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}}f\prod_{j\colon r_{j}>1}V_{i_{h_{r_{j}}}}\ldots V_{i_{h_{1}}}f,

where with

D_{k,n}
we denote the set of maps
\sigma:\{1\ldots,k\}\to\{1\ldots,n\}

and for any
\sigma\in D_{k,n}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we set
\sigma^{-1}(j)=\{h_{1}<\ldots<h_{r_{j}}\}
,

r_{j}=\#\sigma^{-1}(j)

and
e=\#\{j\colon r_{j}=0\}
.

Item
2
. We now focus on
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
.
For
1\leq k\leq n-1
we have necessarily
e\neq 0
for every
\sigma
.
Hence
f^{e}
is vanishing on
\Sigma
and we obtain that
{\left.\kern-1.2ptV_{i_{k}}\ldots V_{i_{1}}\beta_{1}\right|_{\Sigma}}=0
.

Let
k=n
. We observe that
e=0
if and only if
\sigma
is a permutation of
\{1\ldots,n\}
.
Therefore, the sum in (
130
) evaluated on
\Sigma
reduces to the set of the permutations
P_{n}\subsetneq D_{n,n}
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
And, recalling that
\sigma\in P_{n}
is bijective, (
130
) on
\Sigma
rewrites as

(131)

{\left.\kern-1.2ptV_{i_{n}}\ldots V_{i_{1}}\beta_{1}\right|_{\Sigma}}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in P_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}{V_{i_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}}f}|_{\Sigma}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}{V_{i_{j}}f}|_{\Sigma},

Since by
Lemma

19
one has
{\left.\kern-1.2ptV_{2}f\right|_{\Sigma}}=0
, then it holds that

(132)

\begin{cases}V_{1}f(p)=0,&p\in\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma),\\
V_{1}f(p)\neq 0,&p\in\Sigma\setminus\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma).\end{cases}

being
V_{1},V_{2}
a frame for
\mathcal{D}
.
Choosing
({i_{1}},\ldots,{i_{n}})=(1,\ldots,1)
in (
131
) we get
V_{1}^{n}\beta_{1}(p)\neq 0
, hence by
Proposition

15
we have that
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
has step
n+3
at
(\Sigma\setminus\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma))\times\mathbb{R}
.

Item
3
. We are left to compute the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at
{\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)}\times\mathbb{R}
. Let now
n+1\leq k\leq 2n-1
and
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in\{1,2\}^{k}
.
Evaluating (
130
) at
p\in\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)
, again any term associated with a
\sigma
having
e\neq 0
is vanishing because
f(p)=0
.
Similarly, any term associated with a
\sigma
having at least one
r_{j}=1
vanishes since for
p\in\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)
one has
V_{1}f(p)=V_{2}f(p)=0
.
Finally, it is not possible to find
\sigma\in D_{k,n}
with
r_{j}\geq 2
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
in (
130
), due to
k<2n
.
Thus, we have proved that given
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in\{1,2\}^{k}
we have

(133)

{\left.\kern-1.2ptV_{i_{k}}\ldots V_{i_{1}}\beta_{1}\right|_{\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)}}=0,\qquad\text{for}\ n+1\leq k\leq 2n-1.

This proves that the step of
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
is at least
2n+3
at points of
{\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)}\times\mathbb{R}
.
To conclude, we exhibit the following
2n
-th order horizontal derivative which is non-vanishing:

(134)

{\left.\kern-1.2pt(V_{2}V_{1})^{n}\beta_{1}\right|_{{\mathrm{Char}(\Sigma)}}}=\left(V_{2}V_{1}f\right)^{n}=\left(-X_{0}f\right)^{n}\neq 0,

where in the second equality we used identity (
128
).
∎

Remark 6.1
.

Adapting the above argument, one can actually extend the conclusions (locally in a neighborhood of the zero level set) to horizontal magnetic fields of the form

(135)

\beta=g(f)(b_{1}\nu_{1}+b_{2}\nu_{2})\wedge\omega\in\Omega^{2}_{H},

where
b_{1},b_{2},f\in C^{\infty}(M)
with
b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}=1
, and
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
g(0)=0
and for
t\to 0

(136)

g(t)=\frac{t^{n}}{n!}(C+o(1))

with
n\geq 1
and
C\neq 0
.
This permits to analyze, for instance, magnetic fields in the Heisenberg group of the form
P(x)dx\wedge\omega
where
P
is a polynomial in
x
.

6.2.
Abnormals extremal trajectories and zero locus of the magnetic field

We consider different situations in which abnormal extremal trajectories do enter or do not enter the lifted set
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Since, by Theorem
2
, abnormal extremal trajectories on
\overline{M}
project onto
M
on characteristic curves of
\beta
, we can restrict our analysis to checking on the manifold
M
whether or not characteristic curves of
\beta
enter into its zero set
\mathcal{Z}
.

All examples we consider here are built on
M=\mathbb{H}
the Heisenberg group.

Example 6.2
.

The first example (cf. Example
5.3
) is a horizontal magnetic field for which characteristic curves of the magnetic field do not enter the zero set
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Let us consider

(137)

\beta=x\,dx\wedge\omega+y\,dy\wedge\omega.

We have that
\mathcal{Z}=\{x=y=0\}
is the the
z
-axis.
In the complement of
\mathcal{Z}
, characteristic curves for
\beta
are tangent to the vector field

(138)

-yX_{1}+xX_{2}=-y\partial_{x}+x\partial_{y}+\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})\partial_{z}.

It is easy to see that characteristic curves for
\beta
are spirals contained in cylinders around the
z
-axis, never crossing
\mathcal{Z}
.
Since
\mathcal{Z}
is transversal to
\mathcal{D}
at every point, we stress that abnormal curves in the lift of
\mathcal{Z}
are constant curves.

Example 6.3
.

The second example we present is a horizontal magnetic field for which there exist characteristic curves of the magnetic field crossing the zero locus
\mathcal{Z}
. This produces a non-smooth abnormal extremal trajectory on
\overline{M}
.
Let us consider in
\mathbb{H}

(139)

\beta=y\,dx\wedge\omega+x\,dy\wedge\omega.

We have that
\mathcal{Z}=\{x=y=0\}
is again the the
z
-axis.
In the complement of
\mathcal{Z}
, characteristic curves for
\beta
are tangent to the vector field

(140)

-xX_{1}+yX_{2}=-x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}+xy\partial_{z}.

It is easy to see that the curve
\gamma:[0,T]\to\mathbb{H}
defined as

(141)

\gamma(t)=\begin{cases}\gamma_{-}(t)=(\frac{T}{2}-t,0,0),&t\in\left[0,\frac{T}{2}\right]\\
\gamma_{+}(t)=(0,t-\frac{T}{2},0),&t\in\left(\frac{T}{2},T\right]\end{cases}

is a characteristic curve for the magnetic field for
t\neq\frac{T}{2}
crossing
\mathcal{Z}
when
t=\frac{T}{2}
.
Moreover, since
\mathcal{Z}
is transversal to
\mathcal{D}
at every point, then abnormal curves in the lift of
\mathcal{Z}
are constant curves.

We observe that on
\overline{M}
, along the lift
\overline{\gamma}
the step of the lifted sub-Riemannian structure is equal to
3
for
t\neq\frac{T}{2}
and is equal to
4
for
t=\frac{T}{2}
(due to
\mathrm{rank}(d\beta_{1},d\beta_{2})=2
at
\mathcal{Z}
, cf. Theorem
3
).

Example 6.4
.

The last example (cf. Example
5.4
) is a horizontal magnetic field for which the zero set
\mathcal{Z}
is a horizontal curve and there exists a characteristic curve of the magnetic field which enters into
\mathcal{Z}
transversally.
Let us consider in
\mathbb{H}

(142)

\beta=4z\,dx\wedge\omega+x^{2}\,dy\wedge\omega.

We have that
\mathcal{Z}=\{x=z=0\}
is the
y
-axis, which is a horizontal curve.
In the complement of
\mathcal{Z}
, characteristic curves for
\beta
are tangent to the vector field

(143)

-x^{2}X_{1}+4zX_{2}=-x^{2}\partial_{x}+4z\partial_{y}+\frac{x}{2}(xy+4z)\partial_{z}.

Consider the same curve
\gamma:[0,T]\to\mathbb{H}
as in the previous example

(144)

\gamma(t)=\begin{cases}\gamma_{-}(t)=(\frac{T}{2}-t,0,0),&t\in\left[0,\frac{T}{2}\right]\\
\gamma_{+}(t)=(0,t-\frac{T}{2},0),&t\in\left(\frac{T}{2},T\right]\end{cases}

We have that
\gamma_{-}(t)
is a reparametrization of an integral curve of (
143
), while
\gamma_{+}(t)
is a horizontal curve contained in
\mathcal{Z}=\{x=z=0\}
.

Finally one can compute the step of the lifted distribution
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
at points of
\overline{\gamma}
thanks to Theorem
3
. On the set
\mathcal{Z}
one easily check that
d\beta_{2}=0
while
d\beta_{1}\neq 0
. The surface
\Sigma=\beta_{1}^{-1}(0)=\{z=0\}
has a unique characteristic point at the origin.

Hence one can conclude that:

(i)

the step is equal to
3
at
\overline{\gamma}(t)
for
t\in[0,\frac{T}{2})
since we are not in
\mathcal{Z}
,

(ii)

the step is equal to
5
at
\overline{\gamma}(t)
for
t=\frac{T}{2}
since
\gamma(\frac{T}{2})
is a characteristic point for
\Sigma
,

(iii)

the step is equal to
4
at
\overline{\gamma}(t)
for
t\in(\frac{T}{2},T]
since
\gamma(t)
is not a characteristic point for
\Sigma
.

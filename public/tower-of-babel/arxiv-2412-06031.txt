Strict comparison in reduced group C ∗ C^{*} -algebras
Tattwamasi Amrutam, David Gao, Srivatsav Kunnawalkam Elayavalli, Gregory Patchell

Abstract
. We prove that for every n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} such that n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , the reduced group C ∗ C^{*} -algebras of the countable free groups C r ∗ ​ ( 𝔽 n ) C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{n}) have strict comparison. Our method works in a general setting: for every finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic group G G with trivial finite radical and the rapid decay property, we have C r ∗ ​ ( G ) C^{*}_{r}(G) have strict comparison. This work also has several applications in the theory of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras including: resolving Leonel Robert’s selflessness problem for C r ∗ ​ ( G ) C^{*}_{r}(G) ; uniqueness of embeddings of the Jiang-Su algebra 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} up to approximate unitary equivalence into C r ∗ ​ ( G ) C^{*}_{r}(G) ; full computations of the Cuntz semigroup of C r ∗ ​ ( G ) C^{*}_{r}(G) and future directions in the C ∗ C^{*} -classification program.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Strict comparison

The subspaces of a finite dimensional Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
can be compared and classified by their dimension; algebraically, the dimension of a subspace
{\mathcal{K}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
is the trace of the orthogonal projection onto
{\mathcal{K}}
. For matrices
x,y
one has the following natural notion of
comparison
, that is
x\precsim y
if
x=rys
for some matrices
r,s
. It turns out here that the
rank
is what precisely implements this comparison, that is
\mathrm{rank}(x)\leq\mathrm{rank}(y)
if and only if
x\precsim y
. Murray and von Neumann studied this comparison quite effectively in a broad setting of bounded operators on Hilbert spaces, in what are today called von Neumann algebras. In a broad class of von Neumann algebras known as II
1
factors, they were able to establish a
comparison theorem
, showing that the lattice of projections under the above subequivalence is determined exactly by the unique finite trace
[
49
]
. Notably, in this context the trace takes on continuous values, that is, for all
t\in[0,1]
, there is a projection
p\in\mathcal{P}(M)
with
\tau(p)=t
.

Such a comparison property allows for an algebraic perspective that is useful in operator algebras. Naturally one would want an analogue of the above Murray von Neumann comparison theorem in
C^{*}
-algebras. However, the behavior of
C^{*}
-algebras is very different as evidenced, for instance, by the typical lack of projections. Hence, an analogue of this property is not only subtle to define for all unital simple
C^{*}
-algebras but also will not hold automatically in all situations like in the setting of II
1
factors. The appropriate analogue here is called
strict comparison
, initially considered by Blackadar
[
11
]
. This can be rephrased in a much more algebraic manner in terms of a certain notion of order-completeness (
almost unperforation
) of the important invariant called the Cuntz semigroup (see
[
60
]
, and for instance,
[
29
]
or paragraph 3.4 of
[
4
]
for more details).

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and let
\mathbb{K}
denote the compact operators on
\ell^{2}
. If
a,b\in(A\otimes{\mathbb{K}})_{+}
are positive elements, we say that
a\precsim b
(Cuntz subequivalent) if there are
v_{n}\in A\otimes{\mathbb{K}}
such that
v_{n}^{*}bv_{n}\to a
. Now given
\tau
a trace on
A
, there is a canonical extension of
\tau
to
(A\otimes{\mathbb{K}})_{+}
(denoted by
\tau
again). The dimension function here with respect to
\tau
is defined on
(A\otimes{\mathbb{K}})_{+}
by

d_{\tau}(a):=\lim_{n}\tau(a^{\frac{1}{n}})
(note that for positive operators in matrices,
d_{\tau}
precisely gives the rank of the operator).
If
a,b\in(A\otimes{\mathbb{K}})_{+}
satisfy
a\precsim b
, then
d_{\tau}(a)\leq d_{\tau}(b)
. A unital, simple, exact
C^{*}
-algebra
A
with unique trace
\tau
has strict comparison if whenever
a,b\in(A\otimes{\mathbb{K}})_{+}
are such that
d_{\tau}(a)<d_{\tau}(b)
, then
a\precsim b
. Note that many of the
C^{*}
-algebras considered in the results of our paper will be unital, simple, exact and with unique trace (this includes the reduced
C^{*}
-algebras of hyperbolic groups
[
1
]
), so that the above definition will suffice. In full generality, the definition will involve the space of quasitraces and is more subtle.

Despite being such a fundamental and natural property of a
C^{*}
-algebra, proving strict comparison is difficult and has far-reaching applications (please see subsection
1.3
where several applications arising from our present work are described). Strict comparison occupies an important place as a hypothesis in Elliott’s classification program, which seeks to classify simple, nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras by
K
-theoretical and tracial data. This program has seen immense success in recent times and has resulted in a substantial body of work due to several mathematicians (see for instance
[
32
,
33
,
15
,
14
,
71
,
69
]
). Note also the influential Toms-Winter conjecture predicts that three regularity properties of very different natures, namely topological (finite nuclear dimension), functional analytic (
\mathcal{Z}
-stability), and algebraic (strict comparison), are equivalent for a separable, unital, simple, non-elementary, nuclear
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. These three seemingly independent regularity properties, which are
conjectured to be equivalent, are exactly the conditions under which one considers a

C^{*}
-algebra to belong to the classification program.

1.2.
Context and main results

A prototypical example of a reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra is the one arising from the countable free group
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. The study of this
C^{*}
-algebra has been extremely important for opening up several deep insights in the subject. Some examples of this include the following fundamental results: unique trace and simplicity
[
55
]
(see also the major works
[
42
,
37
]
which were inspired by Powers’ insights), lacking non-trivial projections
[
53
]
,
K
-theory computations
[
53
]
, showing stable rank 1
[
25
]
in the sense of Rieffel
[

strong convergence
matrix models
[

(see also
[
46

whose ideas inspired the present paper). Despite these important developments, the fundamental question of proving strict comparison for
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
has evaded experts. Within the community, it has been a widely-known open problem with initial motivation stemming from the work of Dykema-Rørdam in 1998 (
[
27
,
28
]
, see also
[
58
, Proposition 6.3.2]
), which addresses comparison in
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})

C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})

cannot
be readily used to prove this result for
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{2})

C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
. However such behavior is not clear at all to witness inside
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. Strict comparison for
C^{*}_{r}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
has been explicitly stated as an open question in the work of Robert in 2012
[
58
]
and in the recent substantial problem list in
C^{*}
-algebras
[
62
]
, see Problems 86 and 88.

Robert in
[
59
]
asks a very interesting, more general question of selflessness for these
C^{*}
-algebras: the existence of a Haar unitary that is freely independent (in the reduced sense) from
C^{*}_{r}(G)
viewed as the diagonal inclusion in its
C^{*}
-ultrapower. Interestingly, this property is well-studied and is of interest in the II
1
factor context where it turns out that such freely independent unitaries can always be constructed, due to beautiful work of Popa from
[
54
]
. Robert obtained a crucial insight that this selflessness property in
C^{*}
-algebras implies strict comparison through a soft ultrapower argument. For natural reasons, this is of independent interest from a continuous model theory point of view.

Another key motivation for showing strict comparison is that it is crucial in fully
computing the Cuntz semigroup, introduced by Cuntz
in his fundamental investigation on the existence of traces on

C^{*}
-algebras
[
20
]
. Over several decades, Cuntz semigroup theory has evolved into a very mature field of research
[
29
]
. It has been a long-sought problem to obtain full computations of the Cuntz semigroup of reduced group
C^{*}
-algebras in a general sense and has been described in
[
29
]
as ‘‘may be out of reach’’. The problem of computing the Cuntz semigroup for
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is even explicitly stated as Problem 16.4 therein. All of the problems described above are settled in this paper.

Theorem A
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\geq 2
,
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
has strict comparison. In fact,
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
is selfless in the sense of Robert
[
59
]
.

Thanks to major developments
[
60
,
47
]
, we now know that for unital, separable, simple, unique trace, nuclear, infinite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras, strict comparison and
\mathcal{Z}
-stability are equivalent. Importantly, at the moment, outside the
\mathcal{Z}
-stable case there are only a few examples of strict comparison arising from groups, such as infinite reduced free products like
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
(
[
58
, Proposition 6.3.2]
). With our next main result, we greatly expand the list of known examples. To motivate this, we need to discuss the following notion. We say a group
G
has the
rapid decay property
if, for every finite generating set
X
, there exists a polynomial
P
such that for every element

supported on
B_{X}(r)
,

\left\|\lambda_{G}(a)\right\|\leq P(r)\|a\|_{2}.
The rapid decay property (see the survey
[
61
]
) was first proved by Haagerup for the free groups
[
35
]
. It was then generalized to Gromov hyperbolic groups
[
41
,
39
]
, relatively hyperbolic groups with peripheral subgroups having rapid decay
[
61
]
, cocompact lattices in
SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})
or
SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})

[
44
,
16
]
, mapping class groups of surfaces
[
8
]
, large type Artin groups
[
17
]
, heirarchically hyperbolic groups
[
6
]
, and more. Rapid decay is extremely influential and has been used to prove several important problems, including the Novikov conjecture

[
18
]
, the Baum-Connes conjecture
[
45
]
, and others including
[
34
]
. Before we state our result, we will need to recall one more important and influential notion from geometric group theory
[
51
]
. A group is said to be
acylindrically hyperbolic
if it admits a non-elementary acylindrical action on a Gromov hyperbolic metric space. We direct the reader to
[
50
]
for context, examples, and references. Recall also the finite radical for a group is the largest finite normal subgroup. Within the class of acylindrically hyperbolic groups
G
, it turns out that the finite radical being trivial is equivalent to
C^{*}_{r}(G)
having unique trace, which is in turn equivalent to
C^{*}_{r}(G)
being simple (see Theorem 1.4 of
[
12
]
and Theorem 2.35 of
[
22
]
). We are now ready to state our next result:

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be an arbitrary finitely generated countable group that is acylindrically hyperbolic with trivial finite radical and, in addition, enjoys the rapid decay property. Then
C^{*}_{r}(G)
has strict comparison. In fact, we have
C^{*}_{r}(G)
is selfless in the sense of Robert
[
59
]
.

Note that the trivial finite radical assumption in the above theorem is necessary due to the fact that these
C^{*}
-algebras will have unique trace as a consequence of being selfless (see also
[
12
]
). The following are some examples of groups (with an added trivial finite radical assumption) that fit into the above result (see

1.3.
Applications to classification and Cuntz semigroup theory

It is a very standard fact that every II
1
factor contains a copy of the hyperfinite II
1
factor
R
uniquely up to pointwise approximate unitary equivalence. The most naïve analogue of this in
C^{*}
-algebras would be that every simple, unital, infinite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra contains the Jiang-Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}
uniquely up to pointwise approximate unitary equivalence. Both existence and uniqueness problems for embeddings of
\mathcal{Z}
tend to be difficult, see
[
62
, Section 18]
. It is known that there are simple, unital, infinite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras that do not contain
\mathcal{Z}

[
21
]
. It remains open if there is any unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
with two non-equivalent unital embedding of
\mathcal{Z}
. Our work yields the following concrete application. The result below follows from Proposition 6.3.1 in
[
58
]
(which can be applied since the reduced
C^{*}
-algebras of the groups in consideration have unique quasitrace from Haagerup’s famous quasitrace theorem
[
36
]
, and have stable rank one from
[
30
]
see also
[
56
]
) and our Theorem
B
. Note also that the following result addresses problems 62 and 64 in
[
62
]
:

Theorem C
.

For any exact group
G
in the class of groups covered in Theorem
B
, there is a unital embedding of the Jiang-Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}\to C^{*}_{r}(G)
, and this embedding is unique up to pointwise approximate unitary equivalence.

As explained in the beginning of the introduction, strict comparison is a crucial hypothesis that is used in classifying maps up to
K
-theoretic and tracial data in the nuclear setting. In the natural progression of the current landscape of classification, we expect new developments to emerge in the more general setting of non-nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras, wherein strict comparison will still naturally play a crucial role as a hypothesis for the co-domain for classifying maps between
C^{*}
-algebras up to the total invariant. Our results here will provide a wealth of interesting new and natural co-domains for this type of classification phenomena.

In
[
19
]
, Coward-Elliott-Ivanescu gave a new picture of
the Cuntz semigroup in terms of Hilbert modules; they showed that for

C^{*}
-algebras of stable rank one, the Cuntz semigroup encodes precisely the
isomorphism classes of countably generated Hilbert modules, similarly to
how the Murray-von Neumann semigroup encodes isomorphism classes of
finitely generated projective modules. The theory of Cuntz semigroups is
particularly well-developed for
C^{*}
-algebras of stable rank one
[
2
]
. An important notion in this context is pureness, which was introduced by
Winter in his seminal study
[
70
]
of regularity properties for simple,
nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras. Pureness can be thought of as
\mathcal{Z}
-stability at the
level of the Cuntz semigroup, since in
[
3
]
, it was shown that a

C^{*}
-algebra
A
is pure if and only if its Cuntz semigroup
\mathrm{Cu}(A)

tensorially absorbs
\mathrm{Cu}(\mathcal{Z})
. More recently, it was shown that pure

C^{*}
-algebras form a robust class
[
4
]
, and as observed in
[
4
, Example 5.14]
, if
G
is an exact, infinite,
C^{*}
-simple group, then
C^{*}_{r}(G)
has
strict comparison if and only if
C^{*}_{r}(G)
is pure. If
A
is a unital, separable, simple, stably finite, pure

C^{*}
-algebra, then the Cuntz semigroup can be computed as

\mathrm{Cu}(A)=V(A)\sqcup\mathrm{LAff}(QT(A))_{++}
(see Theorem 2.6 in
[
13
]
). Here
where
QT(A)
denotes the quasitracial states on
A
,
V(A)
denotes the Murray-von Neumann semigroup and
\mathrm{LAff}(K)
means the lower-semicontinuous, affine functions

K
to
(-\infty,\infty]
if
K
is a compact convex set, and
\mathrm{LAff}(K)_{++}
means
the subset of strictly positive elements, that is, the
lower-semicontinuous, affine functions
K
to
(0,\infty]
. If
K
contains only one point, then
\mathrm{LAff}(K)_{++}=(0,\infty]
. If
A
is exact, then

QT(A)=T(A)
by a famous result of Haagerup
[
36
]
. Thus, if G is an exact, infinite, C*-simple group such that
C^{*}_{r}(G)
has
strict comparison, then its Cuntz semigroup can be computed as

\mathrm{Cu}(C^{*}_{r}(G))=V(C^{*}_{r}(G))\sqcup[0,\infty]

since such
C^{*}
-algebras have a unique trace
[
12
]
. Combined with our main results, this answers Problem 16.4 from
[
29
]
which asks to fully compute the Cuntz semigroup for
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
, which is a Cuntz semigroup version of Blackadar’s fundamental comparison property
[
11
]
for projections in
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
.

Theorem D
.

The Cuntz semigroup of
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is
\mathbb{N}\sqcup[0,\infty]
.

Note that, our Theorem
B
can be used to fully compute the Cuntz semigroup for the vast family of exact acylindrically hyperbolic groups with trivial finite radical and with rapid decay property as described above. This partially settles the general problem in the paragraph before Problem 16.4 in
[

]
, obtaining a full computation for many classes of
C^{*}
-simple groups. For examples of groups with stable rank one, see
[

,

,

]

[

1.4.
Comments to the reader

The inroad to prove strict comparison is to prove the more general free independence theorem in the norm ultrapower of the reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra, which is the
selflessness
property from
[
59
]
. As mentioned previously, our inspiration for the argument to achieve this came from a recent
very
beautiful paper by Louder and Magee
[
46
]
. Curiously, our original approach to solving the problem of strict comparison was (motivated in a sense by the work of the third author
[
43
]
) to use strong convergence methods in random matrix theory, and this is what indirectly led us to
[
46
]
. Now, in order to prove this free independence theorem, one benefits by using the rapid decay property (see
[
61
,
35
]
), which allows one to control the operator norm of elements in the group ring in terms of the radius of the ball of support and the 2-norm. A key next step is a quantitative analysis of the eventual faithfulness of certain tailored retraction maps that one constructs using the group structure; for instance, free product structure, or, more generally, an acylindrical action on a hyperbolic space. This is used to obtain a particularly strong existential inclusion of the group into the free product of itself with a copy of
\mathbb{Z}
. Piecing these ideas together allows one to push this inclusion to the level of the reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra, as needed.

Concerning the organization

The proofs of these results split into a hands-on computational part (showing selflessness in our sense, see Definition
3.1
) and a soft
C^{*}
-algebra part (using rapid decay
à la
Louder-Magee, see Theorem
3.5
), and then reaching the finish line for strict comparison with Robert’s
[
59
]
. If the reader would like to just focus on understanding the proof for arbitrary free products with rapid decay, we direct them to a self contained approach via the combination of Proposition
3.2
and Theorem
3.5
. We also remark to the reader that after our paper was released publicly, M. Magee kindly shared with us his proof (via a quantitative Baumslag lemma) for just the non abelian free group case. This is in Subsection
3.4
.

On the other hand, the proof of Theorem
B
is quite involved, but the quantitative part is somewhat natural from a geometric point of view. Appendix
A
contains a quantitative version of a key theorem of Wenyuan Yang that is needed to prove our result
B
. This is quite technical and we recommend the interested reader to side by side refer to
[
72
, Corollary 3.4]
.

Notation

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group. If
Y
is a generating set for
\Gamma
then we denote by
B_{Y}(R)
the elements of the
R
-ball around the identity in the Cayley graph of
\Gamma
with the generating set
Y
. Let
{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma
denote the complex group algebra associated to
\Gamma
. We equip
{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma
with three separate norms. Let
x=\sum{x_{g}}g
, then

\displaystyle\|x\|_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}|x_{g}|;

\displaystyle\|x\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}|x_{g}|^{2};

\displaystyle\|x\|

\displaystyle:=\sup\{\|xy\|_{2}:y\in{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma,\ \|y\|_{2}\leq 1\}.

We call these norms on
{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma
the
\ell^{1}
-norm
, the
\ell^{2}
-norm
, and the reduced
operator norm
respectively. As suggested by the definition of the operator norm, each element of
{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma
can be viewed as a bounded operator on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
, by the left regular representation
\lambda_{\Gamma}:{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma\to\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}\Gamma)
(define
\lambda_{\Gamma}(g)(\delta_{h})=\delta_{gh}
and extend linearly). Note therefore we can write
\|x\|=\|\lambda_{\Gamma}(x)\|
when viewed as a bounded operator on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
. Note that for any element
x\in\mathbb{C}\Gamma
, one has the following elementary inequality
\|x\|_{2}\leq\|x||\leq\|x\|_{1}.

We define the
reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra

C_{r}^{*}(\Gamma)
to be the operator norm closure of
{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma
inside of
{\mathbb{B}}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
. We will also need the following elementary fact, which follows from the triangle inequality: if
\phi:\Gamma\to G
is a group homomorphism then
\phi
extends to an
\ell^{1}
-contractive map between
{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma
and
{\mathbb{C}}G
.

A
state
on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra

is a positive linear functional
\phi
such that
\phi(1)=1.
We say that a state
\phi
is

if
\phi(x^{*}x)>0
for any nonzero
x\in A
. We call
\phi
a
trace
if
\phi(xy)=\phi(yx)
for all
x,y\in A
. Let
A_{1},A_{2}\subset(A,\phi)
be subalgebras. We say that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are

relative to
\phi
if whenever
a_{k}\in A_{i_{k}}
with
\phi(a_{k})=0
and
i_{k}\neq i_{k+1}
for
1\leq k<n
then
\phi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=0.
If
(B_{1},\rho_{1}),(B_{2},\rho_{2})
are unital
C^{*}
-algebras with faithful states, then there is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with a faithful state
(B,\rho)
and embeddings
\pi_{i}:(B_{i},\rho_{i})\hookrightarrow(B,\rho)
such that
\pi_{1}(B_{1})
and
\pi_{2}(B_{2})
are freely independent relative to

We call
(B,\rho)
the

of
(B_{1},\rho_{1})
and
(B_{2},\rho_{2})
; we omit the states when they are clear from context (see

), and write

. We have in this context that whenever

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a free ultrafilter on the natural numbers. For a discrete group

. More precisely, a sequence

for all

We call
\Gamma^{\mathcal{U}}
an

of
\Gamma
. If
A
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, we define
A^{\mathcal{U}}
as

A^{\mathcal{U}}:=\{(x_{n})_{n}:x_{n}\in A,\ \sup_{n}\|x_{n}\|<\infty\}/I_{\mathcal{U}}

where
I_{\mathcal{U}}
is the ideal consisting of all norm-bounded sequences
(x_{n})
in

with the following property: for all
\varepsilon>0
there is
S\in{\mathcal{U}}
such that for all
n\in S
,
\|x_{n}\|<\varepsilon
. We call
A^{\mathcal{U}}
an
ultrapower
of

. Note that under the definition
\|(x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\|_{A^{\mathcal{U}}}=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\|x_{n}\|
, and the natural product structure,

is a

-algebra.

Proof of main results

3.1.
A new property for groups

The following is a quantitative strengthening of the definition of the mixed identity free (MIF) property for groups
[
40
]
, and our terminology is motivated by
[
59
]
and is very much related to the quantitative Baumslag lemma from
[
46
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say a group with a finite generating set
(G,X)
is
selfless
if there is a function
f:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with
\liminf_{n}f(n)^{\frac{1}{n}}=1
such that for all
n\geq 1
, there is an epimorphism
\phi_{n}:G*\langle a\rangle\to G
such that
\phi_{n}|_{G}=\id_{G}
,
\phi_{n}
is injective on
B_{X\cup\{a\}}(n)
, and
\phi_{n}(B_{X\cup\{a\}}(n))\subset B_{X}(f(n))
. Here,
a
is a torsion-free generator so that
G*\langle a\rangle\cong G*{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The following is a particularly motivating example:

Proposition 3.2
.

If
(G,X)
contains a torsion-free element and
(H,Y)
is an infinite group, then
G*H
is selfless. In particular,
{\mathbb{F}}_{n}
is selfless for all
n\geq 2.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
be torsion-free. Set
p=|g|_{X}
. For each
n
let
h_{n}\in H
be an element such that
|h_{n}|_{Y}=2n+1
. Define
\phi_{n}:G*H*\langle a\rangle\to G*H
by
\phi_{n}|_{G*H}=\id_{G*H}
and
\phi_{n}(a)=h_{n}gh_{n}^{-1}
. It is clear that
\lim_{n}|\phi_{n}(a)|_{X\cup Y}^{\frac{1}{n}}=1
, and therefore

\phi_{n}(B_{X\cup Y\cup\{a\}}(n))\subset B_{X\cup Y}(f(n));

for some function
f
where
\lim_{n}f(n)^{\frac{1}{n}}=1.

It remains to show that
\phi_{n}
is injective on
B_{X\cup Y\cup\{a\}}(n)
. It suffices to show that, if

s_{1}t_{1}\cdots s_{m-1}t_{m-1}s_{m}=e

where
s_{i}\in G*H
are such that
|s_{i}|_{X\cup Y}\leq 2n
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
s_{i}\neq e
for all
1<i<m
, and
t_{i}=h_{n}g^{p_{i}}h_{n}^{-1}
for some nonzero integers
p_{i}
, then
m=1
and
s_{1}=e
.

Assume that
m>1
. We expand out the expression
s_{1}t_{1}\cdots s_{m-1}t_{m-1}s_{m}
and regroup the product as follows:

(s_{1}h_{n})g^{p_{1}}(h_{n}^{-1}s_{2}h_{n})g^{p_{2}}(h_{n}^{-1}s_{3}\cdots s_{m-1}h_{n})g^{p_{m-1}}(h_{n}^{-1}s_{m}).

We claim that each term in parentheses begins and ends with a letter in
H
. Indeed, for each
s_{i}\in H
(necessarily nontrivial) this is immediate. Otherwise,
s_{i}
begins either with a letter in
G
or a letter in
H
of length at most
2n
followed by a letter in
G
;
s_{i}
ends in a similar way. Therefore
h_{n}^{-1}s_{i}h_{n}
begins and ends with a letter in
H
. Since each
p_{i}
is nonzero, we now see that the above product is a nontrivial word in the free product; this finishes the proof.
∎

3.2.
A general family of selfless groups

We will prove here that all finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic groups with trivial finite radical are selfless. For this subsection, for background notation and preliminaries on acylindrically hyperbolic groups, please refer to the beautiful memoir
[
22
]
, and also
[
50
]
. At the outset we wish to express our gratitude to the following two experts for their guidance.
Firstly we sincerely thank D. Osin who first pointed out to us that our selflessness for free products (Proposition
3.2
) will hold in the general setting of acylindrically hyperbolic groups.
Secondly we are very grateful to W. Yang for kindly sharing with us his ideas towards a different approach. First we describe the outline for our proof:

Outline of proof

The key step in the proof is to use a hyperbolically embedded
\mathbb{F}_{2}
subgroup to construct the sequence of elements going to infinity, which will implement selflessness. The fact that this embedding is a Lipschitz quasi-retract
[
22
]
is crucial to relate word length inside
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
and
G
. Then to check that these elements don’t form a trivial word with a ball with a controlled co-growth, we have to use a quantitative version of Wenyuan Yang’s ‘‘admissible path lemma’’ in
[
72
]
, and argue that any word yields a quasi-geodesic. The proof crucially uses both acylindricity and geometry of hyperbolic space. Roughly, it says that a concatenation of quasigeodesics which travels alternatively near contracting subsets and leaves them in an orthogonal way, is a quasigeodesic. The careful quantitative analysis of this argument which is needed for our proof is done in Appendix
A
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic group with trivial finite radical. Then
G
is selfless.

Proof.

Fix a finite generating set
S
for
G
. Since
G
is acylindrically hyperbolic and has trivial finite radical, by
[
22
, Theorem 6.14]
, there is a hyperbolically embedded copy of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
in
G
. Denote by
a,b
the generators of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
. Without loss of generality, assume
a,b\in S
. By the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 4.31]
, there is a constant
K>0
and a finite generating set
Y
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
such that for all
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
,
|h|_{Y}\leq|h|_{S}
and
|h|_{S}\leq K|h|_{Y}
. Since
\{a,b\}
and
Y
are both finite generating sets for
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
, we have that
|h|_{a,b}
and
|h|_{Y}
are quasi-equivalent. Therefore there are constants
C,D
such that whenever
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2},

|h|_{\{a,b\}}\leq C|h|_{S}
and
|h|_{S}\leq D|h|_{\{a,b\}}
.

Set
g_{n}=b^{n}ab^{-n}
. We note that each
g_{n}
is primitive. Note that for all
n,

{\mathbb{F}}_{2}=\langle b\rangle*\langle g_{n}\rangle
. By hyperbolicity of free factors (
[
22
, Example 2.2(c)]
) and transitivity of hyperbolic embeddings (
[
22
, Proposition 4.35]
) we have that
\langle g_{n}\rangle
is hyperbolically embedded in
G
. In particular, since
a
has infinite order and is contained inside a hyperbolically embedded cyclic subgroup,
[
50
, Theorem 1.4]
says that there is an acylindrical action of
G
on a hyperbolic space
X
such that
a
is loxodromic. It follows that
g_{n}
is loxodromic for each
n
too. By almost malnormality of hyperbolically embedded subgroups (
[
22
, Proposition 4.33]
),
[
22
, Remark 6.2]
, and
[
22
, Corollary 6.6]
, it follows that
E(g_{n})=\langle g_{n}\rangle
for each

.

We note that for all
n\geq\frac{1}{2}C^{2}D
and any
h\in B_{S}({|g_{n}|_{S}^{1/2}})\setminus\{e\}
(the
|g_{n}|_{S}^{1/2}
-ball around the identity in the Cayley graph of
G
with the generating set
S
), we have
h\not\in E(g_{n})
. Indeed,
|g_{n}|_{\{a,b\}}=2n+1>C^{2}D
which implies that either
h\not\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
or

|h|_{\{a,b\}}\leq C|h|_{S}\leq C|g_{{n}}|_{S}^{1/2}\leq C\sqrt{D}|g_{{n}}|^{1/2}_{\{a,b\}}<|g_{n}|_{\{a,b\}},

while clearly every element
g^{\prime}\in E(g_{n})\setminus\{e\}
is an element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
with
|g^{\prime}|_{\{a,b\}}\geq|g_{n}|_{\{a,b\}}
.

Since all of the
g_{n}
are conjugate, they all have equal stable length. Therefore we can apply Theorem
A.4
to get a degree 6 polynomial
P
such that whenever
h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in B_{S}({|g_{n}|_{S}^{1/2}})
and
|k_{1}|,\ldots,|k_{m}|\geq P(|g_{n}|_{S})
we have
h_{1}g_{n}^{k_{1}}\cdots h_{m}g_{n}^{k_{m}}\neq e
.

We define
\phi_{N}:G*\langle z\rangle\to G
by
\phi_{N}|_{G}=\id
and
\phi_{N}(z)=g_{2CN^{2}}^{P(D(4CN^{2}+1))}
. We can now conclude as in the proof of Proposition
3.2
. Clearly
\phi_{N}
satisfies the subexponential growth condition, since
|g_{2CN^{2}}|_{S}\leq D|g_{2CN^{2}}|_{a,b}=D(4CN^{2}+1).
It remains to show that
\phi_{n}
is injective on
B_{S\cup\{z\}}(N)
. It suffices to show that

h_{1}\ell_{1}\cdots h_{m}\ell_{m}\neq e

for all
h_{i}\in G
such that
|h_{i}|_{S}\leq 2N
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
h_{i}\neq e
for all
1<i<m
, and for all
\ell_{i}=\phi_{N}(z)^{p_{i}}
where the
p_{i}
are nonzero integers. But this follows from the previous paragraph’s assertion, since, taking
n=2CN^{2},
we have
h_{i}\in B_{S}(2N)\subset B_{S}(\sqrt{(2n+1)/C})\subset B_{S}(|g_{n}|_{S}^{1/2})
while the powers of
g_{n}
are larger, in magnitude, than
P(D(2n+1))
and thus are also larger than
P(|g_{n}|_{S})
. (Here, we assume

is increasing, which is true for sufficiently large values.)

∎

C^{*}_{r}(G)

Definition 3.4
.

Let

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose
(G,X)
is selfless and has rapid decay. Then
G*\langle a\rangle\cong G*{\mathbb{Z}}
is existentially
C^{*}
-residually-
G
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
46
, Theorem 1.6]
. Set
Y=X\cup\{a\}
to be our finite generating set of
\Gamma:=G*{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
S\subset\Gamma
be a finite subset. Then there is some
R>0
such that
S\subset B_{Y}(R)
. Let
S_{Y}(R)\subset{\mathbb{C}}\Gamma
denote the elements supported on
B_{Y}(R)
with
\ell^{1}
norm equal to 1. Using compactness of
S_{Y}(R)
with respect to the
\ell^{1}
norm, we find a finite
\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
-net
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for
S_{Y}(R)
. That is, every element in
S_{Y}(R)
would be within
\varepsilon/3
distance of one of the

\phi:\Gamma\to G
such that
\phi|_{G}=\id_{G}
and that, for all

\|\lambda_{G}(\phi(a_{i}))\|\leq\|\lambda_{\Gamma}(a_{i})\|+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Since
G
is selfless, we get a function
f:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that
\liminf_{n}f(n)^{\frac{1}{n}}=1
and homomorphisms
\phi_{n}:\Gamma\to G
which are epimorphisms, are identity on
G,
are injective on
B_{Y}(n),
and such that
\phi_{n}(B_{Y}(n))\subset B_{X}(f(n))
. Using rapid decay of

\left\|\lambda_{G}(a)\right\|\leq P(r)\|a\|_{2}
for all

. Now, as
\liminf_{n}f(n)^{\frac{1}{n}}=1
, we may pick

P(f(2mR))^{\frac{1}{2m}}<1+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
. Define
b_{i}=\phi_{2mR}(a_{i})
for each

.

Note that

\|\lambda_{\Gamma}(a_{i})\|^{2m}=\|\lambda_{\Gamma}(a_{i}^{*}a_{i})^{m}\|

and similarly
\|\lambda_{G}(b_{i})\|^{2m}=\|\lambda_{G}(b_{i}^{*}b_{i})^{m}\|
. Since each
a_{i}
is supported on
B_{Y}(R)
,
(a_{i}^{*}a_{i})^{m}
is supported on

, and hence each
(b_{i}^{*}b_{i})^{m}=\phi_{2mR}((a_{i}^{*}a_{i})^{m})
is supported on

\phi_{2mR}
is injective on

\|(b_{i}^{*}b_{i})^{m}\|_{2}=\|(a_{i}^{*}a_{i})^{m}\|_{2}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{G}(b_{i})\|^{2m}

\displaystyle=\|\lambda_{G}(b_{i}^{*}b_{i})^{m}\|

\displaystyle\leq P(f(2mR))\|(b_{i}^{*}b_{i})^{m}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=P(f(2mR))\|(a_{i}^{*}a_{i})^{m}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq P(f(2mR))\|\lambda_{\Gamma}(a_{i})\|^{2m}.

Since we assumed that

we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{G}(b_{i})\|

\displaystyle\leq P(f(2mR))^{\frac{1}{2m}}\|\lambda_{\Gamma}(a_{i})\|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{3})\|\lambda_{\Gamma}(a_{i})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda_{\Gamma}(a_{i})\|+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

In the above proof, the injectivity of
\phi_{n}
on the radius

is used only to obtain the equality

. If instead

is

-to-1 on the radius

such that

then by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we would still have

and the rest of the proof would proceed as above. To summarize, in the definition of selfless, we can assert the weaker condition that

is

-to-1 for some subexponential function

. Note that in this setting, the group homomorphism

is no longer necessarily injective, which isn’t a requirement anyway to prove Proposition

. We suspect that this approach might also help resolve the question of strict comparison in broader generality.

Proposition 3.7
.

If
\Gamma
is existentially
C^{\ast}
-residually-

{\mathcal{U}}
on
{\mathbb{N}}
there is a

-homomorphism

where

and

is the diagonal embedding

Since

which are identity on

and such that for all

In other words, for all

\|(\phi_{n}(z))_{\mathcal{U}}\|_{C^{*}_{r}(G)^{\mathcal{U}}}\leq\|z\|_{C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)}.

Since the map

is a homomorphism, we immediately get the previous inequality for all

∎

Appendix A
Quantitative version of Wenyuan Yang’s admissible path lemma

The aim of this section is to prove a quantitative version of W. Yang’s admissible path lemma
[
72
, Corollary 3.4]
, which applies to our setting. For our purposes, we will take a fixed action on a hyperbolic space but vary the loxodromic element and thus the quasi-geodesic constants
\lambda
. So throughout, we track dependence on
\lambda
. The following quantitative version of the Morse lemma is present in
[
31
, Section 5.2]
; see
[
63
, Theorem 1.1]
for an explicit polynomial.

Lemma A.1
.

[Quantitative Morse Lemma]
Let
X
be a
\delta
-hyperbolic space, and let
\gamma
be a
(\lambda,0)
-quasi-geodesic. Let
\sigma
be a geodesic with the same endpoints as
\gamma
. Then
\gamma
is contained in a
Q_{1}(\lambda)
-neighborhood of
\sigma
with
Q_{1}
a quadratic polynomial (which depends on
\delta
).

We continue with some notation. Let
(G,S)\curvearrowright X
be a group
G
with a finite generating set
S
acting acylindrically on a
\delta
-hyperbolic space
X
. Fix an origin
o\in X
. Let
g\in G
be a loxodromic element. Let
\tau(g)=\lim_{n}\frac{d(g^{n}o,o)}{n}
denote the stable length of
g
. We observe that
\tau(g)
is independent of the choice of base point and is stable under conjugation. Let
[g]
denote the displacement (or translation length) of
g
, defined by

[g]=\inf_{x\in X}d(gx,x).

This infimum is in fact achieved at some point
x_{0}\in X
(see the discussion after Definition 5 in
[
24
]
). We let
L_{g}=\cup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[g^{n}x_{0},g^{n+1}x_{0}]
. Let
\gamma=\cup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[g^{n}o,g^{n+1}o]
be the axis of
g
going through

Let
\lambda=\frac{d(go,o)}{\tau(g)}
so that
\gamma
is a
(\lambda,0)
-quasi-axis; i.e., the length of any sub-segment of
\gamma
is at most
\lambda
times the distance between the endpoints. That is, if
m<n
are integers then

\sum_{i=m}^{n-1}d(g^{i}o,g^{i+1}o)\leq=\lambda d(g^{n-m}o,o).

Note that clearly
[g]\leq d(go,o)=\tau(g)\lambda.
Let
E(g)
denote the maximal elementary (i.e., virtually cyclic) subgroup of
G
containing
g.
For sets
Y,Z\subset X
, let
\pi_{Y}(Z)
denote the projection of
Z
onto
Y.
More precisely,
y\in\pi_{Y}(Z)
if and only if
y\in Y
and there exists
z\in Z
such that
d(y,z)=\min\{d(y^{\prime},z):y^{\prime}\in Y\}
. For a set
Y\subset X
let
\|Y\|
denote its diameter; i.e., the smallest real number such that
d(y,y^{\prime})\leq\|Y\|
for all
y,y^{\prime}\in Y.

The following is due to Delzant.

Proposition A.2
.

(
[

The following lemma is the quantitative version of
[
68

.

Lemma A.3

(Quantitative Bounded Projection)
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
be an acylindrical action on a hyperbolic space. Then there is a quadratic
Q_{2}
such that whenever a loxodromic element
g
and a point
x\in X
gives a
(\lambda,0)
-quasi-axis
\gamma=\cup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[g^{n}x,g^{n+1}x]
and
h\not\in E(g),
then

\|\pi_{\gamma}(h\gamma)\|\leq Q_{2}(\lambda)(1+[g]).

Proof.

The quasi-axes
\gamma
and
L_{g}
both converge to the same points on the Gromov boundary of
X
. We may, therefore, apply the quantitative Morse lemma to transfer the inequality from Proposition
A.2
to
\gamma
, up to a quadratic in
\lambda
.

The following theorem, the main theorem of this section, is essentially a reproduction of
[
72
, Corollary 3.4]
but where we have tracked all of the constants and dependencies to verify they grow polynomially.

Theorem A.4

(Quantitative Admissible Path Theorem)
.

Let
(G,S)\curvearrowright X
be a group
G
with a finite generating set
S
acting acylindrically on a
\delta
-hyperbolic space
X
. Fix a constant
C>0.
There is a degree 6 polynomial
P
(depending on
G,S,X,\delta,C
) such that if
g
is loxodromic with
\tau(g)=C
, then for all group elements
h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\not\in E(g)
such that
|h_{i}|_{S}\leq|g|_{S}
, the product
h_{1}g^{n_{1}}h_{2}g^{n_{2}}\cdots h_{m}g^{n_{m}}\neq e
whenever
|n_{i}|\geq P(|g|_{S})
for all
1\leq i\leq m
(with
n_{m}
also being allowed to equal 0).

Proof.

Set
\lambda=\frac{d(go,o)}{\tau(g)}
. Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be the collection of
(\lambda,0)
-quasi-geodesics in
X
; in particular,
{\mathcal{L}}
includes all geodesics since
\lambda\geq 1
. Let
{\mathbb{X}}
be defined by
{\mathbb{X}}=\{\cup_{n=k}^{\ell}[hg^{n}o,hg^{n+1}o]:h\not\in E(g)\}
and note that
{\mathbb{X}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}.
By Lemma
A.1
and the proof of
[
10
, Corollary 3.4]
, we have that
{\mathbb{X}}
is
(\mu,\varepsilon)
-contracting with respect to
{\mathcal{L}}
where
\mu,\varepsilon
are uniformly bounded above by a quadratic
Q_{3}(\lambda)
. That is, whenever
q\in{\mathcal{L}}
and
p\in{\mathbb{X}}
such that
d(q,p)\geq Q_{3}(\lambda)
then
\|\pi_{p}(q)\|<Q_{3}(\lambda)
. (In the notation of
[
72
]
,
\mu(\lambda^{\prime},c^{\prime}),\varepsilon(\lambda^{\prime},c^{\prime})\leq Q_{3}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda^{\prime},c^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.)

We now obtain that for each
p\in{\mathbb{X}}
,
p
is
\sigma
-quasiconvex where
\sigma(U)=\frac{3}{2}Q_{3}(\lambda)+2U
. Indeed, let
q
be a geodesic in
X
such that its endpoints
q^{+}
and
q^{-}
are in the
U
-neighborhood
N_{U}(p)
of
p.
Since
p
is
(\mu,\varepsilon)
-contracting, either
d(q,p)<\mu
(and we are done) or
\|\pi_{p}(q)\|<\varepsilon
. In particular, we have
d(q^{+},q^{-})\leq\varepsilon+2U
. Since
q
is a geodesic, for any
x\in q
we have
d(q^{+},x)+d(q^{-},x)=d(q^{+},q^{-})
.
Therefore without loss of generality
d(q^{+},x)\leq\varepsilon/2+U
, implying that
d(x,\pi_{p}(x))<2U+\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon
. Thus
q\subset N_{\sigma(U)}(p)
.

By Lemma
A.3
, if
p,p^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{X}}
are quasi-geodesics corresponding to
h,h^{\prime}
in different left cosets of
E(g)
, then
p,p^{\prime}
have bounded projection
T
where
T=Q_{2}(\lambda)(1+[g])=O(\lambda^{3})
since
[g]\leq d(go,o)=\tau(g)\lambda
(and the stable length
\tau(g)
is fixed). We note that in
[
72
]
, the symbol
\tau
is used for bounded projection but we have already used
\tau
for stable length. By
[
72
, Lemma 2.7]
,
p,p^{\prime}
also have
\nu
-bounded intersection where
\nu(U)=2U+O(\lambda^{3})
.

Now let
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
be integers and consider the path

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=[o,h_{1}o]\cup[h_{1}o,h_{1}go]\cup\ldots\cup[h_{1}g^{n_{1}-1}o,h_{1}g^{n_{1}}o]\cup[h_{1}g^{n_{1}}o,h_{1}g^{n_{1}}h_{2}o]\cup\ldots

\displaystyle\ldots\cup[h_{1}g^{n_{1}}\cdots h_{k}g^{n_{k}-1}o,h_{1}g^{n_{1}}\cdots h_{m}g^{n_{m}}o];

set
q_{i}
to be the geodesic subsegment of
P
where
h_{i}
first appears and set
p_{i}
to be the subpath of
P
between
q_{i}
and
q_{i+1}
(with
p_{m}
being the subpath of
P
coming after
q_{m}
). More explicitly, for
i=1,2
we have that
q_{i}
and
p_{i}
are as follows:

\displaystyle q_{1}

\displaystyle=[o,h_{1}o],

\displaystyle q_{2}

\displaystyle=[h_{1}g^{n_{1}}o,h_{1}g^{n_{1}}gh_{2}o],

\displaystyle p_{1}

\displaystyle=[h_{1}o,h_{1}g^{\sgn(n_{1})}o]\cup\ldots\cup[h_{1}g^{n_{1}-\sgn(n_{1})}o,h_{1}g^{n_{1}}o],

\displaystyle p_{2}

\displaystyle=[h_{1}g^{n_{1}}h_{2}o,h_{1}g^{n_{1}}h_{2}g^{\sgn(n_{2})}o]\cup\ldots\cup[h_{1}g^{n_{1}}h_{2}g^{n_{2}-\sgn(n_{2})}o,h_{1}g^{n_{1}}h_{2}g^{n_{2}}o]

P
is a path from
o
to
h_{1}g^{n_{1}}h_{2}g^{n_{2}}\cdots h_{m}g^{n_{m}}o
in
X
. We note that each
p_{i}
is a
(\lambda,0)
-quasi-geodesic in
{\mathbb{X}}
(and thus has two endpoints in some
p^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{X}}
), each
p_{i}
has length at least
\tau(g)|n_{i}|,
each
q_{i}
has
C^{\prime}|g|_{S}
-bounded projection to
p_{i}
and
p_{i-1}
(since
q_{i}
is a geodesic of length at most
C^{\prime}|h|_{S}\leq C^{\prime}|g|_{S}
where
C^{\prime}=\max_{k\in S}d(ko,o)
; note that
C^{\prime}
is a constant dependent only on the group action and the choice of generating set), and any two
p_{i},p_{i+1}
have
\nu
-bounded intersection since each
h_{i}\not\in E(g)
so
p_{i},p_{i+1}
lie in different quasi-axes of
g.
We note that
\lambda=\tau(g)d(go,o)\leq\tau(g)C^{\prime}|g|_{S}
so that
\lambda=O(|g|_{S})
.

We are now ready to analyze the constants in the proof of
[
72
, Proposition 3.3]
. We have
c=0
everywhere.

\displaystyle C_{\lambda,0}

\displaystyle=\lambda(\mu(\lambda,0)+\epsilon(\lambda,0)+C^{\prime}|g|_{S})

\displaystyle=O(|g|_{S}^{3})

\displaystyle B_{\lambda,0}

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon(1,0)+2\mu(1,0)+\nu(\mu(1,0)+\sigma(0))+C^{\prime}|g|_{S}

\displaystyle=O(|g|^{2})

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\max(C^{\prime}|g|_{S}+2\varepsilon(1,0)+4\mu(1,0)+1,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\ \ \mu(1,0)+5\varepsilon(1,0)+B_{\lambda,0}+1)

\displaystyle=O(|g|_{S}^{2})

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\lambda(6R+1)

\displaystyle=O(|g|_{S}^{3})

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\max(\mu(1,0)+\varepsilon(1,0)+C^{\prime}|g|_{S}+C_{\lambda,0},

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\ \ 2C^{\prime}|g|_{S}+3\varepsilon(1,0)+6\mu(1,0),

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\ \ \Lambda(R+\sigma(\mu(1,0))),

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\ \ 13\varepsilon(1,0)+6\mu(1,0)+2B_{\lambda,0})

\displaystyle=O(|g|_{S}^{5}).

Now, if we choose all of the
n_{i}
so that
|n_{i}|
are larger than
\lambda D=O(|g|_{S}^{6}),
we can apply
[
72
, Corollary 3.4]
to obtain that our path
P
is a
(\Lambda,0)
-quasi-geodesic. In particular,
P
is nontrivial and so it must be that
h_{1}g^{n_{1}}h_{2}g^{n_{2}}\cdots h_{m}g^{n_{m}}\neq e
. (Note that in the case
n_{m}=0
, we can still apply
[
72
, Corollary 3.4]
.) This finishes the proof.
∎

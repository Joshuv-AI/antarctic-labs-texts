Abstract
A new measurement of the magnetic anomaly a μ a_{\mu} of the positive muon is presented based on data taken from 2020 to 2023 by the Muon g − 2 g\!-\!2 Experiment at FNAL ( FNAL ). This dataset contains over 2.5 times the total statistics of our previous results. From the ratio of the precession frequencies for muons and protons in our storage ring magnetic field, together with precisely known ratios of fundamental constants, we determine a μ = 116 592 0710 ​ ( 162 ) × 10 − 12 a_{\mu}=116\,592\,0710(162)\times 10^{-12} ( 139 ppb 139\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{b} ) for the new datasets, and a μ = 116 592 0705 ​ ( 148 ) × 10 − 12 a_{\mu}={116\,592\,0705(148)\times 10^{-12}} ( 127 ppb 127\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{b} ) when combined with our previous results. The new experimental world average, dominated by the measurements at FNAL, is a μ ​ ( exp ) = 116 592 0715 ​ ( 145 ) × 10 − 12 a_{\mu}(\text{exp})=116\,592\,0715(145)\times 10^{-12} ( 124 ppb 124\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{b} ). The measurements at FNAL have improved the precision on the world average by over a factor of four.

. Since the same calibration probe was used in Run-2/3, the absolute calibration with respect to shielded protons in a spherical water sample remains unchanged. Additional work enhanced our understanding of the material effects of the calibration probe but had no direct impact on the associated
8\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{b}
uncertainty.

The absolute calibration was cross-checked against
3
He-based magnetometers
Farooq et al. 2020
, which showed a
1.7\text{\,}\text{standard deviation}
difference. Comparisons to water-based continuous-wave
NMR
probes developed by the J-PARC Muon
g\!-\!2
/EDM collaboration
Yamaguchi et al. 2019
showed inconsistencies in early comparisons but good agreement in later iterations. These new crosschecks lead to an additional
25\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{b}
uncertainty, resulting in a total calibration uncertainty of
34\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{b}
.

The total systematic uncertainty of the muon-weighted magnetic field, including calibration, increased by
10\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{b}
to a total of
48\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{b}
with respect to Run-2/3, primarily driven by the uncertainties on the absolute calibration.

Magnetic field transients
B_{i}
—
The time-dependent residual magnetic fields from eddy currents induced by the fast kicker magnets were measured
in-situ
with two independent magnetometers, both based on Faraday rotation in terbium gallium garnet crystals. One system uses optical fibers to guide the laser light into the kicker region. The second new system uses an open laser beam, which enters the storage volume through a window, and mirrors to guide the light.
Two of the three kickers were measured at several transverse positions. The measurements from the two apparatus agree within uncertainties.
Measurements at a large radius (
18\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{m}
) revealed transverse variations of about a factor of two, which were investigated with additional measurements on a mock-up. While the measurements close to the beam center are consistent with earlier determinations, the new measurements at a larger radial offset, together with improved understanding of the transverse modeling of the resulting field perturbation, lead to a larger correction term
B_{k}
than reported in Run-2/3. Driven by the observed stronger transverse variation of the transient and uncertainties in the transverse modeling, the total uncertainty increased to
22\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{b}
compared to Run-2/3.

The correction presented in reference
Aguillard et al. 2024
for magnetic field transients from vibrations in the
ESQ
system,
B_{q}
, remains valid. The transverse distribution of this effect was studied in more detail, and the assigned uncertainties were corroborated.

In summary, we report the measurement of the muon magnetic anomaly to a precision of
127\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{b}
using our full six years of data. With over a four-fold improvement in precision over the BNL E821 measurement
Bennett et al. 2006
, this result represents the most precise determination of the muon magnetic anomaly and provides a powerful benchmark for extensions of the
SM
.

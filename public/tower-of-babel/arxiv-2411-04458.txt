Measures of closeness to cordiality for graphs
Anand Brahmbhatt, Kartikeya Rai, Amitabha Tripathi

Abstract
A graph G G is cordial if there exists a function f f from the vertices of G G to { 0 , 1 } \{0,1\} such that the number of vertices labelled 0 0 and the number of vertices labelled 1 1 differ by at most 1 1 , and if we assign to each edge x ​ y xy the label | f ⁡ ( x ) − f ⁡ ( y ) | |f(x)-f(y)| , the number of edges labelled 0 0 and the number of edges labelled 1 1 also differ at most by 1 1 . We introduce two measures of how close a graph is to being cordial, and compute these measures for a variety of classes of graphs.

Introduction

Graph labellings were introduced by Rosa
[
15
]
in a bid to attack the conjecture of Ringel
[
12
]
that
{\mathcal{K}}_{2n+1}
can be decomposed into
2n+1
subgraphs that are all isomorphic to a given tree with
n
edges. Rosa called a function
f
a
\beta
-
valuation
of a graph
G
with
m
edges if
f
is an injection from the vertices of
G
to the set
\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
such that, when each edge
xy
is assigned the label
|f(x)-f(y)|
, the resulting edge labels are distinct. Golomb
[
6
]
subsequently called such labellings
graceful
. Ringel’s conjecture has been proved for all sufficiently large
n
recently by Keevash and Staden
[
7
]
in April 2020, and independently by Montgomery, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov
[
11
]
in January 2021. The conjecture of Ringel followed by the Graceful Tree Conjecture has spurred a great deal of activity in the area of Graph Labellings over the past six decades; for an updated and dynamic survey, see Gallian
[
5
]
. Among the numerous offshoots of the graceful labelling of a graph, and among the most prominent is the cordial labelling of a graph, introduced by Cahit
[
1
]
in 1987.

Let
G
be a finite, simple graph. Let
f:V(G)\to\{0,1\}
, and let
\overline{f}:E(G)\to\{0,1\}
be the induced mapping given by

\overline{f}(xy)=\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|.

We say that
f
is a
cordial
labelling of
G
if the number of vertices labelled
0
and the number of vertices labelled
1
differ by at most
1
, and the number of edges labelled
0
and the number of edges labelled
1
differ at most by
1
. A graph is said to be
cordial
if it admits a cordial labelling. The vastness of literature relating to cordial graphs may be measured by the contents in
[
5
, pp. 89–107]
.

For
i\in\{0,1\}
, let
v_{i}(f)
denote the number of vertices labelled
i
and let
e_{i}(f)
denote the number of edges labelled
i
. Let

{\Delta}_{v}(f)=\left|v_{0}(f)-v_{1}(f)\right|\;\;\text{and}\;\;{\Delta}_{e}(f)=\left|e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)\right|.

Hence,
f
is a cordial labelling of
G
precisely when
{\Delta}_{v}(f)\leq 1
and
{\Delta}_{e}(f)\leq 1
.

Chartrand, Lee and Zhang
[
3
]
introduced the notion of uniform cordiality. They called a labelling
f:V(G)\to\{0,1\}

friendly
if
{\Delta}_{v}(f)\leq 1
. A graph
G
for which every friendly labelling is cordial is called
uniformly cordial
. They proved that a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
is uniformly cordial if and only if
G={\mathcal{K}}_{3}
or
G={\mathcal{K}}_{1,n-1}
,
n
even.

Riskin
[
13
,
14
]
introduced two measures of the noncordiality of a graph and determined these measures for certain classes of graphs. He defined the
cordial vertex deficiency
of a graph
G
(denoted by
\text{cvd}(G)
) as the minimum number of vertices, taken over all labellings of
G
for which
{\Delta}_{e}(f)\leq 1
, which needs to be added to
G
such that the resulting graph is cordial. Analogously, he defined the
cordial edge deficiency
(denoted by
\text{ced}(G)
) of a graph
G
as the minimum number of edges, taken over all labellings of
G
for which
{\Delta}_{v}(f)\leq 1
, which needs to be added to
G
such that the resulting graph is cordial.

In this paper, we define two measures of closeness to cordiality of a graph as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}_{1}(G)=\min_{f}\Big\{{\Delta}_{v}(f)+{\Delta}_{e}(f)\Big\}.

(1)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}_{2}(G)=\min_{{\Delta}_{v}(f)\leq 1}\Big\{{\Delta}_{e}(f)\Big\}.

(2)

The first measure is over all labellings
f:V(G)\to\{0,1\}
, whereas the second measure is over those labellings
f:V(G)\to\{0,1\}
for which
{\Delta}_{v}(f)\leq 1
. So if
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}(G)\leq 1
or if
{\mathscr{D}}_{2}(G)\leq 1
, then
G
is cordial. Thus,
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}
and
{\mathscr{D}}_{2}
may both be viewed as measures of cordiality. We note that the measure
{\mathscr{D}}_{2}(G)
is closely related to the measure
\text{ced}(G)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
with disjoint vertex sets,

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{1}(G_{1}+G_{2})\leq\mathscr{D}_{1}(G_{1})\mathscr{D}_{1}(G_{2})+\mathscr{D}_{1}(G_{1})+\mathscr{D}_{1}(G_{2});

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{2}(G_{1}+G_{2})\leq\mathscr{D}_{2}(G_{1})+\mathscr{D}_{2}(G_{2})+1.

Proof.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between labellings
f:V(G_{1}+G_{2})\to\{0,1\}
and pairs of labelling
f_{1}:V(G_{1})\to\{0,1\}
and
f_{2}:V(G_{2})\to\{0,1\}
. The following hold by the triangle inequality.

We have

{\Delta}_{v}(f)=|v_{0}(f)-v_{1}(f)|\leq|v_{0}(f_{1})-v_{1}(f_{1})|+|v_{0}(f_{2})-v_{1}(f_{2})|\leq{\Delta}_{v}(f_{1})+{\Delta}_{v}(f_{2}).

(3)

Since
e_{0}(f)=e_{0}(f_{1})+e_{0}(f_{2})+v_{0}(f_{1})v_{0}(f_{2})+v_{1}(f_{1})v_{1}(f_{2})

\displaystyle{\Delta}_{e}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)|

(4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|e_{0}(f_{1})-e_{1}(f_{1})|+|e_{0}(f_{2})-e_{1}(f_{2})|+\left|\big(v_{0}(f_{1})-v_{1}(f_{1})\big)\big(v_{0}(f_{2})-v_{1}(f_{2})\big)\right|

\displaystyle{\Delta}_{v}(f_{1})\,{\Delta}_{v}(f_{2})+{\Delta}_{e}(f_{1})+{\Delta}_{e}(f_{2}).

and

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{f_{1},\,f_{2}}\big\{{\Delta}_{v}(f)+{\Delta}_{e}(f)\big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\Delta}_{v}(f^{\star})+{\Delta}_{e}(f^{\star})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\Delta}_{v}(f_{1}^{\star})+{\Delta}_{v}(f_{2}^{\star})+{\Delta}_{e}(f_{1}^{\star})+{\Delta}_{e}(f_{2}^{\star})+{\Delta}_{v}(f_{1}^{\star})\,{\Delta}_{v}(f_{2}^{\star})

The Trees
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}

Cahit
[
1
]
proved that every tree is cordial. If
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
denotes a tree of order
n
, this implies
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}({\mathcal{T}}_{n})\leq 2
and
{\mathscr{D}}_{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{n})\leq 1
. We give a direct proof relying on the fact that every non-trivial tree has at least two leaves (for instance, the endpoints of every maximal path in the tree), and the removal of each leaf from a tree results in a tree.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
denote a tree with
n
vertices. Then

\mathscr{D}_{1}\big({\mathcal{T}}_{n}\big)=1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{D}_{2}\big({\mathcal{T}}_{n}\big)=1-(n\bmod{2}).

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
be a tree of order
n
, so that its size is
n-1
. We exhibit a labelling
f
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
such that

v_{0}(f)-v_{1}(f)=n\bmod{2}\quad\text{and}\quad e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)=\begin{cases}\pm 1&\text{ if }n\equiv 0\!\!\!\!\pmod{2},\\
0&\text{ if }n\equiv 1\!\!\!\!\pmod{2}.\end{cases}

Note that such a labelling attains the values of
\mathscr{D}_{1}\big({\mathcal{T}}_{n}\big)
and
\mathscr{D}_{2}\big({\mathcal{T}}_{n}\big)
in the statement of the theorem. That there can be no labelling with smaller values follows from parity arguments.

We prove the existence of such a labelling by induction on
n
. As the base cases, for
n=1
choose
f
to be the labelling which labels the vertex with
0
, and for
n=2
choose
f
to be the labelling which labels one vertex with
0
and the other with
1
.

We assume that each tree of order less than
n
has a labelling satisfying the above mentioned conditions. Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
be a tree with
n\geq 3
, and let
x,y
be leaves in
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
. Then
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\setminus\{x,y\}
is a tree of order
n-2
. Let
f
be a labelling of
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\setminus\{x,y\}
satisfying the conditions above. We extend this labelling to
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
by labelling
x
and
y
differently; if
\overline{f}
denotes any such extension to
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
, then
v_{0}(\overline{f})-v_{1}(\overline{f})=v_{0}(f)-v_{1}(f)
.

Labelling
x
and
y
is done according to the following procedure. Since
x,y
are leaves in
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
, each has a unique neighbour, say
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
, respectively. If
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
have the same label in
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\setminus\{x,y\}
, then the additional edges
xx^{\prime},yy^{\prime}
have different labels, so that
e_{0}(\overline{f})-e_{1}(\overline{f})=e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)
. If
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
have different labels in
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\setminus\{x,y\}
and
n
is even, then label
x,x^{\prime}
the same (and
y,y^{\prime}
the same) if
e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)=-1
and
x,x^{\prime}
different (and
y,y^{\prime}
different) if
e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)=1
. Thus, the difference
e_{0}-e_{1}
alternates between
-1
and
+1
as
n
runs through the positive even integers, starting with
-1
for
n=2
.

The case where
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
have different labels in
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\setminus\{x,y\}
and
n
is odd remains to be resolved. Let
n
be odd, and let us assume the existence of the said labelling for trees of all orders
<n
. Thus, there exists a labelling
\tilde{f}
on a tree
{\mathcal{T}}_{n-2}
for which
v_{0}(\tilde{f})-v_{1}(\tilde{f})=1
and
e_{0}(\tilde{f})-e_{1}(\tilde{f})=0
. Adding a leaf
x
to
{\mathcal{T}}_{n-2}
and labelling
x
as
1
results in a tree
{\mathcal{T}}_{n-1}
with labelling
f^{\prime}
, with
{\Delta}_{v}(f^{\prime})=0
and
{\Delta}_{e}(f^{\prime})=1
. Further adding a leaf
y
to
{\mathcal{T}}_{n-1}
results in a tree
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
. If
\overline{y}
is the vertex adjacent to
y
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
, we may label
y
with the same label as
\overline{y}
has in
{\mathcal{T}}_{n-1}
if
e_{0}(f^{\prime})-e_{1}(f^{\prime})=-1
and the opposite label if
e_{0}(f^{\prime})-e_{1}(f^{\prime})=1
. The extended labelling
f
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
has the desired property.

This completes the proof of the claim by induction, and thus completes the proof of the theorem.

\blacksquare

The complete graphs
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}

Cahit
[

proved that the complete graph
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
is cordial if and only if
n\leq 3
. We determine both
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}
and
{\mathscr{D}}_{2}
for complete graphs
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
a^{2}\leq n<(a+1)^{2}
, then

(i)

{\mathscr{D}}_{1}\big({\mathcal{K}}_{n}\big)=\begin{cases}a+\frac{1}{2}(n-a^{2})&\>\>\mbox{if}\>\>n\in\{a^{2}+2t:0\leq t\leq a\},\\
2a-1&\>\>\mbox{if}\>\>n=a^{2}+1,\\
a+1+\frac{1}{2}\big((a+1)^{2}-n\big)&\>\>\mbox{if}\>\>n\in\{a^{2}+2t+1:1\leq t\leq a-1\}.\\
\end{cases}

(ii)

{\mathscr{D}}_{2}\big({\mathcal{K}}_{n}\big)=\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Let
f:V\big({\mathcal{K}}_{n}\big)\to\{0,1\}
, and let
v_{0}(f)=k
. By interchanging the labels
0
and
1
, we may assume
0\leq k\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. Thus,

\displaystyle v_{0}(f)=k,\;\;v_{1}(f)=n-k,\quad{\Delta}_{v}(f)=n-2k.

\displaystyle e_{0}(f)=\textstyle{k\choose 2}+\textstyle{n-k\choose 2},\;\;e_{1}(f)=k(n-k),\quad{\Delta}_{e}(f)=\left|\textstyle{k\choose 2}+\textstyle{n-k\choose 2}-k(n-k)\right|.

The expression for
{\Delta}_{e}(f)
can be written as
\frac{1}{2}\left|(n-2k)^{2}-n\right|
. Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}_{1}\big({\mathcal{K}}_{n}\big)=\min\Big\{(n-2k)+\tfrac{1}{2}\left|(n-2k)^{2}-n\right|:0\leq k\leq\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\Big\},

(5)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}_{2}\big({\mathcal{K}}_{n}\big)=\min\Big\{\tfrac{1}{2}\left|(n-2k)^{2}-n\right|:|n-2k|\leq 1\Big\}=\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor.

(6)

Case
I. If
n-2k\geq\sqrt{n}
, then the expression in eqn. (
5
) is
(n-2k)+\frac{1}{2}(n-2k)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n=\frac{1}{2}(n-2k)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(n-2k)-k
. This is increasing as a function of
n-2k
, and so the minimum in eqn. (
5
) is achieved when
n-2k=\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil
or
\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil+1
, depending on which of these has the same parity as
n
. Hence, the minimum is achieved when
k=\frac{1}{2}\left(n-\left\lceil\sqrt{n}\right\rceil\right)
or
\frac{1}{2}\left(n-1-\left\lceil\sqrt{n}\right\rceil\right)
, whichever is an integer.

Case
II. If
n-2k<\sqrt{n}
, then the expression in eqn. (

Theorem 5.1

\displaystyle v_{0}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i},\;\;v_{1}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n_{i}-k_{i});\quad{\Delta}_{v}(f)=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n_{i}-2k_{i})\right|.

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}_{1}\big({\mathcal{K}}_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{i}\leq n_{i}\\

(7)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}_{2}\big({\mathcal{K}}_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}}\big)

(i)

8
The Fan graphs
{\mathcal{F}}_{m,n}

The fan graph
\mathcal{F}_{m,n}
is the join between a path
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and an empty graph
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{m}
. Thus, it has
m+n
vertices and
mn+n-1
edges. Cahit
[
1
]
proved that all fans are cordial, so that
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}({\mathcal{F}}_{m,n})\leq 2
and
{\mathscr{D}}_{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{m,n})\leq 1
. We exploit the proof of cordiality to compute the exact values.

Theorem 8.1
.

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}_{1}\big({\mathcal{F}}_{m,n}\big)=\begin{cases}2&\mbox{ if $m$ is odd, $n$ is even},\\
1&\mbox{ otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathscr{D}}_{2}\big({\mathcal{F}}_{m,n}\big)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if $m$ is even, $n$ is odd},\\
1&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let the fan graph
\mathcal{F}_{m,n}
denote the join of
\mathcal{P}_{n}=v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{m}=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
. Hence
E(\mathcal{F}_{m,n})=E(\mathcal{P}_{n})\cup(V(\mathcal{P}_{n})\times V(\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{m}))
. We define the following labelling
f:V(G)\to\{0,1\}
.

\displaystyle f(v_{i})=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }\>\>i\equiv 0,1\!\!\!\!\pmod{4},\\
1&\mbox{ if }\>\>i\equiv 2,3\!\!\!\!\pmod{4},\end{cases}\quad{\rm and}\quad f(u_{i})=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }\>\>i\in\{1,\ldots\lfloor m/2\rfloor\},\\
1&\mbox{ if }\>\>i\in\{\lceil m/2\rceil+1,\ldots,m\},\end{cases}

(16)

\displaystyle f(u_{\lceil m/2\rceil})=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }\>\>m\equiv 1\!\!\!\!\pmod{2}\text{ and }n\equiv 0,3\!\!\!\!\!\pmod{4},\\
1&\mbox{ if }\>\>m\equiv 1\!\!\!\!\pmod{2}\text{ and }n\equiv 1,2\!\!\!\!\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

(17)

We now compute the values of
v_{0}(f)-v_{1}(f)
and
e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)
to show that
f
yields the stated
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}
and
{\mathscr{D}}_{2}
values. The minimality follows by parity.

Case
I. (
m
is even) The labelling
f
labels equal number of vertices in
{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}_{m}
with
0
and
1
. Thus, there are an equal number of
0
and
1
labelled edges in
V(\mathcal{P}_{n})\times V(\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{m})
. Hence,
v_{0}(f)-v_{1}(f)=v_{0}(f|_{\mathcal{P}_{n}})-v_{1}(f|_{\mathcal{P}_{n}})
and
e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)=e_{0}(f|_{\mathcal{P}_{n}})-e_{1}(f|_{\mathcal{P}_{n}})
where
f|_{G}
is the labelling
f
restricted to a subgraph
G
. An easy induction on
n
yields the following.

\displaystyle v_{0}(f|_{\mathcal{P}_{n}})-v_{1}(f|_{\mathcal{P}_{n}})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n\equiv 0\!\!\!\!\pmod{2},\\
1&\text{ if }n\equiv 1\!\!\!\!\pmod{4},\\
-1&\text{ if }n\equiv 3\!\!\!\!\pmod{4},\end{cases}

(18)

\displaystyle e_{0}(f|_{\mathcal{P}_{n}})-e_{1}(f|_{\mathcal{P}_{n}})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n\equiv 1\!\!\!\!\pmod{2},\\
-1&\text{ if }n\equiv 0\!\!\!\!\pmod{2}.\end{cases}

(19)

Case
II. (
m
is odd,
n\equiv 1\pmod{4}
) Removing
u_{\lceil m/2\rceil}
reduces the problem to an instance of
Case
I. Adding back
u_{\lceil m/2\rceil}
gives us
v_{0}(f)-v_{1}(f)=v_{0}(f|_{\mathcal{F}_{m-1,n}})-v_{1}(f|_{\mathcal{F}_{m-1,n}})-1
and
e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)=e_{0}(f|_{\mathcal{F}_{m-1,n}})-e_{1}(f|_{\mathcal{F}_{m-1,n}})-1
. Thus,
v_{0}(f)-v_{1}(f)=0
and
e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)=-1
.

Case
III. (
m
is odd,
n\equiv 3\pmod{4}
) Removing
u_{\lceil m/2\rceil}
reduces the problem to an instance of
Case
I. Adding back
u_{\lceil m/2\rceil}
gives us
v_{0}(f)-v_{1}(f)=v_{0}(f|_{\mathcal{F}_{m-1,n}})-v_{1}(f|_{\mathcal{F}_{m-1,n}})+1
and
e_{0}(f)-e_{1}(f)=e_{0}(f|_{\mathcal{F}_{m-1,n}})-e_{1}(f|_{\mathcal{F}_{m-1,n}})-1
. Thus,
v_{0}(f)-v_{1}(f)=0
and

.

9
Concluding Remarks

We have introduced two measures of cordiality, and have investigated their values for several important classes of graphs. The interested reader is invited to extend these findings to a larger collection of graphs. We close this paper by listing three directions of further research.

Open Problem 1.
Determine the exact value of
\mathscr{D}_{1}({\mathcal{K}}_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}})
in those cases where the number of odd sized parts is not a perfect square.

Open Problem 2.
Among all graphs
G
of order
n
, determine the largest possible value of
{\mathscr{D}}_{i}(G)
, with
i=1,2
. Also, find all such extremal graphs in both cases.

Open Problem 3.
Given
n
and
D_{1}
, determine the maximum size of a graph
G
with
|V(G)|=n
and
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}(G)=D_{1}
. The same problem for
{\mathscr{D}}_{2}
. Also, find all such extremal graphs in both cases.

K K -type multiplicities in degenerate principal series via Howe duality
Mark Colarusso, William Q. Erickson, Andrew Frohmader, Jeb F. Willenbring

Abstract
. Let K K be one of the complex classical groups O k {\rm O}_{k} , GL k {\rm GL}_{k} , or Sp 2 ​ k {\rm Sp}_{2k} . Let M ⊆ K M\subseteq K be the block diagonal embedding O k 1 × ⋯ × O k r {\rm O}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm O}_{k_{r}} or GL k 1 × ⋯ × GL k r {\rm GL}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm GL}_{k_{r}} or Sp 2 ​ k 1 × ⋯ × Sp 2 ​ k r {\rm Sp}_{2k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm Sp}_{2k_{r}} , respectively. By using Howe duality and seesaw reciprocity as a unified conceptual framework, we prove a formula for the branching multiplicities from K K to M M which is expressed as a sum of generalized Littlewood–Richardson coefficients, valid within a certain stable range. By viewing K K as the complexification of the maximal compact subgroup K ℝ K_{\mathbb{R}} of the real group G ℝ = GL ⁡ ( k , ℝ ) G_{\mathbb{R}}={\rm GL}(k,\mathbb{R}) , GL ⁡ ( k , ℂ ) {\rm GL}(k,\mathbb{C}) , or GL ⁡ ( k , ℍ ) {\rm GL}(k,\mathbb{H}) , respectively, one can interpret our branching multiplicities as K ℝ K_{\mathbb{R}} -type multiplicities in degenerate principal series representations of G ℝ G_{\mathbb{R}} . Upon specializing to the minimal M M , where k 1 = ⋯ = k r = 1 k_{1}=\cdots=k_{r}=1 , we establish a fully general tableau-theoretic interpretation of the branching multiplicities, corresponding to the K ℝ K_{\mathbb{R}} -type multiplicities in the principal series.

1.
Introduction

Motivation from real groups

The principal series plays a fundamental role in the representation theory of real groups.
Casselman
[
3
]
established that every irreducible admissible representation of a real reductive group
G_{\mathbb{R}}
can be realized as a submodule of some principal series representation of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
.
(See also
[
5
]
*Cor. 1.18.)
Principal series representations arise via induction from a finite-dimensional irreducible representation of a minimal parabolic subgroup
P_{\mathbb{R}}
.
More generally, if
P_{\mathbb{R}}
is not necessarily minimal, then the parabolically induced representations are said to be
degenerate principal series
representations.
These representations are infinite-dimensional.
A key technique in infinite-dimensional representation theory is to decompose admissible representations of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
under restriction to the action of a maximal compact subgroup
K_{\mathbb{R}}
.
This allows for the possibility of a combinatorial description of the structure of the representation by determining a formula for the multiplicities of the irreducible representations of
K_{\mathbb{R}}
(called
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-types
) in the decomposition.
Although the main result in this paper is a finite-dimensional branching rule from
K_{\mathbb{R}}
down to a certain subgroup
M_{\mathbb{R}}
, this rule can be viewed as a
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-type multiplicity formula for degenerate principal series of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
.

In more detail, let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be a real reductive group with maximal compact subgroup
K_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
P_{\mathbb{R}}
be a (not necessarily minimal) parabolic subgroup of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
\bm{\mu}
be an irreducible finite-dimensional representation of
P_{\mathbb{R}}
on which its nilradical acts trivially.
By inducing
\bm{\mu}
from
P_{\mathbb{R}}
to
G_{\mathbb{R}}
, in the sense of
[
39
]
*§1.5, we obtain a
degenerate principal series
representation of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
, whose underlying Harish-Chandra module we denote by
\mathcal{P}(\bm{\mu})
:

\mathcal{P}(\bm{\mu})\coloneqq\text{the underlying Harish-Chandra module of }{\rm Ind}^{G_{\mathbb{R}}}_{P_{\mathbb{R}}}\bm{\mu}.

From our perspective, the goal is to understand the
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-structure of
\mathcal{P}(\bm{\mu})
.
To this end, let
M_{\mathbb{R}}
denote the intersection of
K_{\mathbb{R}}
with the reductive Levi factor of
P_{\mathbb{R}}
.
In the context of this paper, we may suppose that
\bm{\mu}
remains irreducible upon restriction from
P_{\mathbb{R}}
to
M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
As representations of
K_{\mathbb{R}}
,

(1.1)

\mathcal{P}(\bm{\mu})\cong{\rm Ind}^{K_{\mathbb{R}}}_{M_{\mathbb{R}}}\bm{\mu},

where the right-hand side denotes algebraic induction from
M_{\mathbb{R}}
to
K_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Now we wish to describe the multiplicity of a given
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-type, say
\lambda
, inside
\mathcal{P}(\bm{\mu})
.
By applying Frobenius reciprocity to (
1.1
), we compute this multiplicity as

\displaystyle\dim{\rm Hom}_{K_{\mathbb{R}}}(\lambda,\mathcal{P}(\bm{\mu}))

\displaystyle=\dim{\rm Hom}_{K_{\mathbb{R}}}(\lambda,{\rm Ind}^{K_{\mathbb{R}}}_{M_{\mathbb{R}}}\bm{\mu})

\displaystyle=\dim{\rm Hom}_{M_{\mathbb{R}}}(\bm{\mu},{\rm Res}^{K_{\mathbb{R}}}_{M_{\mathbb{R}}}\lambda)\eqqcolon b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}},

where
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}
denotes the multiplicity of the
M_{\mathbb{R}}
-type
\bm{\mu}
inside the
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-type
\lambda
.
In this way, the problem of determining
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-type multiplicities in degenerate principal series is equivalent to the restriction problem (for
finite
-dimensional representations) from
K_{\mathbb{R}}
to
M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
In the case where
P_{\mathbb{R}}
is a minimal parabolic, the numbers
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}
are the
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-type multiplicities in the
principal series
representation induced from
\bm{\mu}
.
The branching multiplicities
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}
are the focus of this paper.

Overview of results

Recall that the finite-dimensional real associative division algebras are the real numbers
\mathbb{R}
, the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
, and the quaternions
\mathbb{H}
.
In this paper, we let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be one of the real groups
{\rm GL}(k,\mathbb{R})
,
{\rm GL}(k,\mathbb{C})
, or
{\rm GL}(k,\mathbb{H})
.
We choose
K_{\mathbb{R}}
to be the subgroup of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
preserving the norm induced by conjugation on
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
, or
\mathbb{H}
, respectively.
We take the subgroup
M_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq K_{\mathbb{R}}
to be the block-diagonally embedded direct sum of lower-rank groups of the same Lie type as
K_{\mathbb{R}}
; thus each possible
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is given by a choice of positive integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
summing to
k
.
We let
K
and
M
denote the complexifications of
K_{\mathbb{R}}
and
M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Concrete details are given below:

(1.2)

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c|c|c}G_{\mathbb{R}}&K_{\mathbb{R}}&M_{\mathbb{R}}&K&M\\
\hline\cr{\rm GL}(k,\mathbb{R})&{\rm O}(k)&{\rm O}(k_{1})\times\cdots\times{\rm O}(k_{r})&{\rm O}_{k}&{\rm O}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm O}_{k_{r}}\\
{\rm GL}(k,\mathbb{C})&{\rm U}(k)&{\rm U}(k_{1})\times\cdots\times{\rm U}(k_{r})&{\rm GL}_{k}&{\rm GL}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm GL}_{k_{r}}\\
{\rm GL}(k,\mathbb{H})&{\rm Sp}(k)&{\rm Sp}(k_{1})\times\cdots\times{\rm Sp}(k_{r})&{\rm Sp}_{2k}&{\rm Sp}_{2k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm Sp}_{2k_{r}}\end{array}

Note that the groups
K
are the complex classical groups (orthogonal, general linear, and symplectic).
For each of these groups
K
, the finite-dimensional irreducible complex representations
U^{\lambda}
(with rational matrix coefficients) are indexed by a certain set of partitions
\lambda
, or pairs
\lambda=(\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})
of partitions for
K={\rm GL}_{k}
.
For
M
, then, the finite-dimensional irreducible rational representations
W^{\bm{\mu}}
are labeled by
r
-tuples
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
of partitions, or (when
K={\rm GL}_{k}
) by pairs
\bm{\mu}=(\bm{\mu}^{+},\bm{\mu}^{-})
of such
r
-tuples.
As described above, our main interest in this paper is the restriction problem from
K_{\mathbb{R}}
to
M_{\mathbb{R}}
, or equivalently from
K
to
M
.
That is, our main results are combinatorial formulas for the branching multiplicities

(1.3)

b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}\coloneqq\dim{\rm Hom}_{M}(W^{\bm{\mu}},{\rm Res}^{K}_{M}U^{\lambda}).

The
Littlewood–Richardson coefficients
are ubiquitous in algebraic combinatorics
[
11
,
16
]
; given partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
, the Littlewood–Richardson coefficient
c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
gives the coefficient of the Schur function
s_{\lambda}
in the product
s_{\mu}s_{\nu}
.
One can generalize these coefficients for products of arbitrarily many Schur functions, so that, for example,
c^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\nu}}
gives the coefficient of
s_{\lambda}
in the product
s_{\mu_{1}}\cdots s_{\mu_{r}}s_{\nu_{1}}\cdots s_{\nu_{t}}
.
Recall that a partition is said to have
even rows
(resp., columns) if every row (resp., column) in its Young diagram has even length.
Our first main result is the following formula (see Theorem
3.1
) for the branching multiplicity
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}
expressed as a sum of generalized Littlewood–Richardson coefficients.

Theorem 1.1

(Stable branching rule from
K
to
M
)
.

Let
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}
denote the branching multiplicity (
1.3
) from
K
to
M
.
Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
be positive integers summing to
k
.
If
\lambda
and
\bm{\mu}
lie within a certain stable range (to be specified below) depending on
\min_{i}\{k_{i}\}
, then we have the following:

(a)

Branching from
K={\rm O}_{k}
to
M={\rm O}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm O}_{k_{r}}
, we have

b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}=\sum_{\bm{\nu}}\>c^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\nu}},

where
\bm{\nu}
ranges over all
(r-1)
-tuples of partitions with even rows.

(b)

Branching from
K={\rm GL}_{k}
to
M={\rm GL}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm GL}_{k_{r}}
, we have

b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}=\sum_{\bm{\nu}}c^{\lambda^{+}}_{\bm{\mu}^{+}\bm{\nu}}\>c^{\lambda^{-}}_{\bm{\mu}^{-}\bm{\nu}},

where
\bm{\nu}
ranges over all
(r-1)
-tuples of partitions.

(c)

Branching from
K={\rm Sp}_{2k}
to
M={\rm Sp}_{2k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm Sp}_{2k_{r}}
, we have

b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}=\sum_{\bm{\nu}}c^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\nu}},

where
\bm{\nu}
ranges over all
(r-1)
-tuples of partitions with even columns.

The specific “stable range” mentioned above can be found in the full statement of Theorem
3.1
below.
Essentially, the stable range is an upper bound on the number of parts allowed in the partition
\lambda
and in each of the partitions in
\bm{\mu}
, and this upper bound depends on the smallest of the parameters
k_{i}
given in (
1.2
).
In other words, for fixed
\lambda
and
\bm{\mu}
, the formulas in the theorem above are valid as long as all the factors in
M
have sufficiently high rank.

As our second main result, we obtain a fully general and more concrete multiplicity formula, by restricting our attention to the special case where
M
is the minimal direct sum embedding, that is, where
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{r}=1
.
(This corresponds to the special case mentioned above where
P_{\mathbb{R}}
is a minimal parabolic subgroup, meaning that our branching rule gives the
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-type multiplicities in the
principal series
.)
In this special case where
M
is minimal, we use the letter
\bm{\delta}
rather than
\bm{\mu}
to parametrize representations of
M
, to emphasize that
\bm{\delta}
is a vector of integers (in particular, certain “differences”
\delta_{i}
) rather than of partitions.
Specifically, we realize the multiplicity
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}
as the number of tableaux in a certain set
\mathcal{T}(K)^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}
.
These “
K
-tableaux” are defined in Definition
4.1
and in (
4.2
).
Roughly speaking, the parameter
k
determines the maximum entry in these semistandard tableaux, the partition
\lambda
determines the shape of the tableaux, and the integer
k
-tuple
\bm{\delta}
determines their weight, which encodes the frequencies of the entries.
Our Theorem
4.2
takes the following form:

Theorem 1.2

(Branching rule from
K
to the minimal
M
)
.

Let
K
and
M
be as given in (
1.2
), where
M
is given by the parameters
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{r}=1
.
Then we have

b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}=\#\mathcal{T}(K)^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}},

where
\mathcal{T}(K)^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}
is the set of
K
-tableaux with shape
\lambda
and weight
\bm{\delta}
, to be defined in (
4.2
).

In the corollaries in Section
5
, we record explicit formulas for
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}
in the special cases where
\lambda
is given by one-row or one-column shapes.

Unified approach via Howe duality

The contribution of this paper consists of not only the two main results highlighted above, but also the philosophy that unites all of the proofs.
In particular, we approach the problem via
Howe duality
, whereby each of our three compact groups
K_{\mathbb{R}}
is paired with a real noncompact group
G^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
(See details in Theorem
2.3
.)
In each of the three Howe duality settings, we have a multiplicity-free decomposition of a certain space under the joint action of
K
and the complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
; in this decomposition, the
K
-modules are finite-dimensional, while the
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
-modules are infinite-dimensional.
By using
seesaw reciprocity
in each Howe duality setting, we are able to obtain the desired (finite-dimensional) branching multiplicities from
K
to
M
, by instead finding the (infinite-dimensional) branching multiplicities from
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})^{\oplus r}
to
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
.
Although it may seem surprising that the infinite-dimensional branching rule is the easier one, we are able to exploit the well-known
\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}
-structure of the
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
-modules in question.
Despite this uniform approach, each classical group
K
presents a different complication which we address in the proofs:
{\rm O}_{k}
is disconnected,
{\rm GL}_{k}
has rational representations which are not polynomial, and
{\rm Sp}_{2k}
has a branching rule down to
{\rm Sp}_{2(k-1)}
which is not multiplicity-free.

Through the lens of Howe duality, this paper also serves as a retrospective look at the following restriction problems: from
{\rm O}_{k}
down to
{\rm O}_{k-1}\times{\rm O}_{1}
, from
{\rm GL}_{k}
down to
{\rm GL}_{k-1}\times{\rm GL}_{1}
, and from
{\rm Sp}_{2k}
down to
{\rm Sp}_{2(k-1)}\times{\rm Sp}_{2}
.
The corresponding branching rules were written down some time ago, in various degrees of explicitness, by King
[
24
]
*equations (4.8), (4.14–15) and Proctor
[
32
]
*Props. 10.1 and 10.3; see also
[
20
,
25
,
27
,
40
]
.
In the course of proving Theorem
4.2
, we obtain these branching rules in a uniform manner as a consequence of Howe duality.
In this respect, our paper is similar in spirit to
[
17
]
, which likewise used Howe duality as a unifying framework for (stable) branching rules for classical groups.
The present paper is also a natural sequel to our previous work
[
4
]
, where we used this approach to express tensor multiplicities for classical groups via contingency tables.
In general, similar restriction problems can also be approached (at least for connected groups) using the theory of crystal graphs
[
1
]
or Littelmann path algebras
[
28
,
29
]
.

We point out that our “
K
-tableaux” in Theorem
4.2
are a synthesis of the orthogonal
[
21
]
, rational
[
37
,
23
]
, and symplectic
[
31
]
*p. 30 tableaux introduced by King, El Sharkaway, Stembridge, and Proctor.
Several authors subsequently created variations of orthogonal tableaux, such as those of King–Welsh
[
22
]
, Koike–Terada
[
25
]
, Proctor
[
32
]
, and Sundaram
[
38
]
.
Also closely related to our
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-type multiplicity formulas is the recent preprint
[
9
]
by the third author, which uses yet another set of orthogonal tableaux (from the literature on crystal bases) to give a combinatorial formula for graded multiplicity in the Kostant–Rallis setting for the symmetric pair
({\rm GL}_{n},{\rm O}_{n})
.

2.
Preliminaries

Vectors of partitions

Throughout the paper, we write
\mathbb{N}
to denote the set of nonnegative integers.
A
partition
is a weakly decreasing finite sequence of positive integers (
parts
).
It is typical to identify a partition
\lambda
with its
Young diagram
, obtained by arranging boxes in left-justified rows such that the row lengths from top to bottom are given by the parts of
\lambda
.
In this way, one speaks of the “rows” or “columns” of
\lambda
by viewing it as a Young diagram.
We write
\ell(\lambda)
to denote the length of
\lambda
, which is also the number of rows in its Young diagram.
We say that
\lambda
has
even rows
(resp.,
columns
) if all rows (resp., columns) contain an even number of boxes.
We write
0
to denote the empty partition.
We write
\mu\subseteq\lambda
if the Young diagram of
\mu
is contained in that of
\lambda
, where both Young diagrams are aligned with respect to their upper-left corners.

We use boldface Greek letters to denote vectors of partitions
\mu_{i}
:

(2.1)

\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}).

We note that this use of subscripts is somewhat nonstandard, since in the literature one often sees
\mu_{i}
denoting the
i
th component of a partition
\mu
.
In this paper, the notation
\mu_{i}
never refers to the
i
th part of a partition
\mu
; indeed, we will have no need to reference individual parts at all.

In the case of the group
{\rm GL}_{k}
, we will need to generalize partitions by allowing nonpositive parts.
We will fix the total number of parts to be
k
.
Any such generalized partition
\mu
can be expressed uniquely as an ordered pair
\mu=(\mu^{+},\mu^{-})
, where
\mu^{+}
and
\mu^{-}
are true partitions: in particular,
\mu^{+}
consists of the positive parts of
\mu
, and
\mu^{-}
is the partition obtained by negating and reversing the negative parts of
\mu
.
For example, if
\mu=(6,3,3,2,0,0,-1,-3,-5)
, then we have
\mu^{+}=(6,3,3,2)
and
\mu^{-}=(5,3,1)
.
Because the total number of parts is a fixed value
k
, there is no ambiguity in recovering the original
\mu
from the pair
(\mu^{+},\mu^{-})
.
In this context of generalized partitions, we will write

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}

\displaystyle=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})=\Big((\mu_{1}^{+},\mu_{1}^{-}),\ldots,(\mu_{r}^{+},\mu_{r}^{-})\Big),

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}^{+}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\mu_{1}^{+},\ldots,\mu_{r}^{+}),

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}^{-}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\mu_{1}^{-},\ldots,\mu_{r}^{-}).

Irreducible finite-dimensional representations of classical groups

Let
{\rm M}_{p,q}
denote the space of complex
p\times q
matrices, and let
{\rm M}_{n}\coloneqq{\rm M}_{n,n}
.
The
complex classical groups
consist of the general linear, orthogonal, and symplectic groups, defined as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm GL}_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{g\in{\rm M}_{k}:\det g\neq 0\},

\displaystyle{\rm O}_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{g\in{\rm GL}_{k}:g^{T}=g^{-1}\},

\displaystyle{\rm Sp}_{2k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{g\in{\rm GL}_{2k}:g^{T}Jg=J\},

where
J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\
-I&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
and
I
is the
k\times k
identity matrix.
Note that these groups are precisely the complexifications
K
arising in our motivating setting (
1.2
).
For each classical group
K
, let
\widehat{K}
denote the set of equivalence classes of finite-dimensional irreducible complex representations of
K
with rational matrix coefficients.
It is well known that the elements of
\widehat{K}
can be labeled by certain partitions
\lambda
(or pairs thereof) as given below.
Throughout the paper, we write
U^{\lambda}
to denote a model for the irreducible representation of
K
labeled by
\lambda\in\widehat{K}
.
When treating a particular group
K
, we follow
[
17
]
in denoting the irreducible representations
U^{\lambda}
as follows:

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{l|l|l}K&\widehat{K}&\text{Irrep.~$U^{\lambda}$}\\
\hline\cr{\rm O}_{k}&\{\lambda:\text{first two columns of $\lambda$ contain $\leq k$ boxes}\}&E^{\lambda}_{k}\\
{\rm GL}_{k}&\{\lambda=(\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-}):\ell(\lambda^{+})+\ell(\lambda^{-})\leq k\}&F^{\lambda}_{k}\\
{\rm Sp}_{2k}&\{\lambda:\ell(\lambda)\leq k\}&V^{\lambda}_{2k}\end{array}

If
K={\rm GL}_{k}
or
{\rm Sp}_{2k}
, then
U^{\lambda}
is the irreducible representation of
K
with highest weight
\lambda
(in standard coordinates).
For
K={\rm O}_{k}
, which is not connected, the situation is more subtle; see
[
12
]
*pp. 438–9 or
[
10
]
*§19.5 for a detailed construction of the representations
E^{\lambda}_{k}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{k}
.
Its
associated partition

\overline{\lambda}\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{k}
is the partition obtained from
\lambda
by changing the length of its first column from
\ell(\lambda)
to
k-\ell(\lambda)
.

Note that taking the associated partition twice recovers the original partition
\lambda
.
The motivation behind Definition
2.1
is the fact
[
10
]
*Ex. 19.23 that

(2.3)

E^{\overline{\lambda}}_{k}\cong E^{\lambda}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{det}_{k},

where
\mathbf{det}_{k}
denotes the one-dimensional determinant representation of
{\rm O}_{k}
.

Littlewood–Richardson coefficients

If
U
and
V
are representations of a group
H
, then we define

[U:V]:=\dim{\rm Hom}_{H}(U,V).

Note that in general
[U:V]
may be an infinite cardinal, but if
U
and
V
are finite-dimensional, then
[U:V]
is a nonnegative integer.
Moreover, if
U
is irreducible and
V
is completely reducible as an
H
-representation, then
[U:V]
is the
multiplicity
of
U
in the isotypic decomposition of
V
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be partitions with length at most
n
.
For all
m\geq n
, it is a standard fact that
\left[F^{\lambda}_{n}:F^{\mu}_{n}\otimes F^{\nu}_{n}\right]=\left[F^{\lambda}_{m}:F^{\mu}_{m}\otimes F^{\nu}_{m}\right]
.
(See
[
30
]
*§I.9.)
Thus, without ambiguity one can define
c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}:=[F^{\lambda}_{n}:F^{\mu}_{n}\otimes F^{\nu}_{n}]
.
Equivalently, we have
F^{\mu}_{n}\otimes F^{\nu}_{n}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda}c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}F^{\lambda}_{n}
, where we use the shorthand
cU\coloneqq U^{\oplus c}
. The numbers
c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
are known as the
Littlewood–Richardson coefficients
, and play a central role in algebraic combinatorics and classical representation theory
[
16
]
.

Recall that a
semistandard tableau of shape
\lambda
is the Young diagram of
\lambda
in which the boxes are filled with entries from some totally ordered alphabet, say
\{1,\ldots,k\}
, such that the entries in each row are weakly increasing, and those in each column are strictly increasing.
The
content
of a tableau
T
is the
k
-tuple whose
i
th component is the number of occurrences of the entry
i
in
T
.
The
word
of
T
is the sequence obtained by reading the entries of
T
from right to left in each row, taking the rows from top to bottom.
We write
|T|
to denote the number of boxes in
T
.

The Littlewood–Richardson coefficients carry the following well-known combinatorial interpretation.
If
\lambda
and
\mu
are partitions such that
\mu\subseteq\lambda
, then we write
\lambda/\mu
to denote the
skew diagram
consisting of the boxes in
\lambda
which are not in
\mu
.
One can then speak of semistandard skew tableaux of shape
\lambda/\mu
.
We write
|\lambda/\mu|
to denote the number of boxes in the skew diagram
\lambda/\mu
.
A semistandard skew tableau
T
is called a
Littlewood–Richardson (LR) tableau
if in the word of
T
the number of occurrences of
i+1
never exceeds the number of occurrences of
i
for each
1\leq i<k
.
We have the following
Littlewood–Richardson rule

[
11
]
*Prop. 3, p. 64:

(2.4)

Howe duality

In this subsection, we recall the three
dual pair settings
in which the irreducible representations of the classical groups
K
arise.
For complete details, we refer the reader to Howe’s paper
[

, as well as the analytic perspective taken by Kashiwara–Vergne

.

We summarize the Howe duality data in the table in Theorem

below.
In each setting, the classical group
K
acts naturally on a space
X
, which is a direct sum of copies of the defining representation of
K
, and of its contragredient representation in the case where
K={\rm GL}_{k}
.
For the sake of concreteness, we realize

as a space of matrices.
As usual, the action of
K
on
X
yields an action on the space
\mathbb{C}[X]
of polynomial functions on

Let
\mathcal{D}(X)
denote the Weyl algebra of polynomial-coefficient differential operators on
X
, and let
\mathcal{D}(X)^{K}
denote the subalgebra of
K
-invariant operators.
For each classical group
K

\mathcal{D}(X)^{K}
which spans a Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{D}(X)^{K}
.
Thus, there exists a surjective homomorphism
\omega:U(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}(X)^{K}
, and we can view
\mathbb{C}[X]
as a module for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
via this homomorphism.
Similarly, if
U^{\lambda}
is a finite-dimensional irreducible representation of

with rational matrix coefficients,
then
\mathcal{D}(X)^{K}
and hence
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}

acts on the multiplicity space

Explicitly, the action of an invariant differential operator

on

is given by

D\cdot(\sum_{i}u_{i}^{*}\otimes f_{i})=\sum_{i}u_{i}^{*}\otimes(Df_{i})
.
Further, the algebra
\mathcal{D}(X)^{K}
acts irreducibly on the multiplicity space
\widetilde{U}^{\lambda}
, and hence
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
does as well.

One can say more about the Lie algebra

described above.
In fact,

is the complexified Lie algebra of a real reductive Lie group

, such that

is a Hermitian symmetric space.
Let

denote the complexified Lie algebra of

, and let
K^{\prime}
denote the complexification of
K^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Roughly speaking, a

is a

and

and

]
*Def. 3.2.3 for details.
In each Howe duality setting described above, the

{\rm O}_{1}\times\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
:

\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\cong\left(\bm{1}\otimes\widetilde{E}^{0}_{2n}\right)\oplus\left(\mathbf{sgn}\otimes\widetilde{E}^{1}_{2n}\right).

These two components consist of the polynomials of even and odd degree, respectively.

Direct sum embeddings

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
be positive integers summing to
k
.
Inside each classical group
K
, one can naturally embed a direct sum
M
of smaller classical groups (of the same type) as a subgroup, via block diagonal matrices; see the table (
1.2
).
In each case, upon restricting from
K
to
M
, the matrix space
X
decomposes into a direct sum of vertically stacked blocks as follows:

(2.10)

\begin{array}[]{l|l|l}M&X&\mathbb{C}[X]\\
\hline\cr\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\rm O}_{k_{i}}&\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}{\rm M}_{k_{i},n}&\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{k_{i},n}]\\
\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\rm GL}_{k_{i}}&\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}({\rm M}_{k_{i},p}\oplus{\rm M}_{k_{i},q})&\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{k_{i},p}\oplus{\rm M}_{k_{i},q}]\\
\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\rm Sp}_{2k_{i}}&\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}{\rm M}_{2k_{i},n}&\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{2k_{i},n}]\end{array}

In the action of
M
on
\mathbb{C}[X]
in (
2.10
), each
{\rm O}_{k_{i}}
(resp.,
{\rm GL}_{k_{i}}
or
{\rm Sp}_{2k_{i}}
) acts on the tensor factor
\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{k_{i},n}]
(resp.,
\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{k_{i},p}\oplus{\rm M}_{k_{i},q}]
or
\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{2k_{i},n}]
) exactly as in Theorem
2.3
, and acts trivially on every other tensor factor.
Therefore, by replacing
k
by
k_{i}
(in the table in Theorem
2.3
) for each tensor factor in (
2.10
), we have the following multiplicity-free decomposition of
\mathbb{C}[X]
as a module for
M\times(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})^{\oplus r}
:

(2.11)

\mathbb{C}[X]\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}):\\
\mu_{i}\in\Sigma_{i}\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}U^{\mu_{i}}\right)}_{W^{\bm{\mu}}}\otimes\underbrace{\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\widetilde{U}^{\mu_{i}}\right)}_{\widetilde{W}^{\bm{\mu}}}\raisebox{-8.61108pt}{,}

where
\Sigma_{i}
denotes the set
\Sigma
in Theorem
2.3
upon replacing
k
by
k_{i}
.
Whereas we used the letter
U
to denote an irreducible representation of
K
(decorated with a tilde to denote the corresponding infinite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
-module), we use
W
to denote an irreducible representation of the subgroup
M
(decorated with a tilde to denote the corresponding infinite-dimensional
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})^{\oplus r}
-module).

Seesaw reciprocity

Compare the two multiplicity-free decompositions of
\mathbb{C}[X]
given in (
2.8
) and (
2.11
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[X]

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma}U^{\lambda}\otimes\widetilde{U}^{\lambda}

as a module for
K\times\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[X]

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\mu}\\
\in\Sigma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Sigma_{r}\end{subarray}}}W^{\bm{\mu}}\otimes\widetilde{W}^{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle\text{as a module for $M\times(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})^{\oplus r}$}.

Following Kudla
[
26
]
, we say that
K\times\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
and
M\times(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})^{\oplus r}
form a
seesaw pair
on the space
\mathbb{C}[X]
, which we display as

(2.12)

\begin{array}[]{ccl}K&\times&\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\\[4.30554pt]
\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{$\subset$}&&\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{$\supset$}\\[4.30554pt]
M&\times&(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})^{\oplus r}.\end{array}

Because the
K
- and
M
-actions are the complexifications of actions by compact groups
K_{\mathbb{R}}
and
M_{\mathbb{R}}
, both actions on the left-hand side of the seesaw pair (
2.12
) are completely reducible, so the actions of the Lie algebras on the right-hand side are completely reducible as well (see
[
18
,
15
]
).
The term “seesaw” describes the reciprocity of branching multiplicities

(2.13)

b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}=\left[W^{\bm{\mu}}:U^{\lambda}\right]=\left[\widetilde{U}^{\lambda}:\widetilde{W}^{\bm{\mu}}\right]_{\textstyle{.}}

Thus, the restriction problem (for finite-dimensional modules) from
K
to
M
is equivalent to a restriction problem (for infinite-dimensional modules) from
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})^{\oplus r}
to
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
.

3.
Stable branching rule from
K
to
M

Our main result in this section, namely Theorem
3.1
, is a detailed statement of Theorem
1.1
.
In it, we express the branching multiplicity
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}\coloneqq[W^{\bm{\mu}}:U^{\lambda}]
as a sum of generalized Littlewood–Richardson coefficients.
This rule is valid as long as the parameters
\lambda
and
\bm{\mu}
lie inside a certain stable range, given explicitly in Theorem
3.1
below.
Roughly speaking, the maximum valid length of
\lambda
and of each
\mu_{i}
depends on the smallest parameter
k_{i}
.
The stability assumption allows us to prove the following theorem using nothing more than seesaw reciprocity along with the
\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}
-decompositions from Lemma
2.4
.

Theorem 3.1

(cf. Theorem
1.1
)
.

Let
M\subset K
be a direct sum embedding in a classical group, as in (
1.2
), where
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
are positive integers summing to
k
.
Let
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}\coloneqq[W^{\bm{\mu}}:U^{\lambda}]
denote the branching multiplicity from
K
to
M
.
Recall the generalized Littlewood–Richardson coefficients
c^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\nu}}
defined in (
2.7
).

(a)

Stable branching rule from
K={\rm O}_{k}
to
M={\rm O}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm O}_{k_{r}}
:

Let
n
be a positive integer such that
n\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\min_{i}\{k_{i}\})
.
Let
\lambda
be a partition such that
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
.
Let
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
be a vector of partitions, such that each
\ell(\mu_{i})\leq n
.
Then we have

b_{\bm{\mu}}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\bm{\nu}}\>c^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\nu}},

where
\bm{\nu}=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{r-1})
is a vector in which each partition
\nu_{j}
has even rows, with
\ell(\nu_{j})\leq n
.

(b)

Stable branching rule from
K={\rm GL}_{k}
to
M={\rm GL}_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm GL}_{k_{r}}
:

Let
p
and
q
be positive integers such that
p+q\leq 1+\min_{i}\{k_{i}\}
.
Let
\lambda=(\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})
be such that
\ell(\lambda^{+})\leq p
and
\ell(\lambda^{-})\leq q
.
Let
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})=((\mu^{+}_{1},\mu^{-}_{1}),\ldots,(\mu^{+}_{r},\mu^{-}_{r}))
be such that each
\ell(\mu^{+}_{i})\leq p
and
\ell(\mu^{-}_{i})\leq q
.
Then we have

b_{\bm{\mu}}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\bm{\nu}}c^{\lambda^{+}}_{\bm{\mu}^{+}\bm{\nu}}\>c^{\lambda^{-}}_{\bm{\mu}^{-}\bm{\nu}},

where the sum ranges over all vectors
\bm{\nu}=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{r-1})
such that each
\ell(\nu_{j})\leq\min\{p,q\}
.

(c)

Stable branching rule from
K={\rm Sp}_{2k}
to
M={\rm Sp}_{2k_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\rm Sp}_{2k_{r}}
:

Let
n
be a positive integer such that
n\leq 1+\min_{i}\{k_{i}\}
.
Let
\lambda
be a partition with
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
.
Let
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
be a vector of partitions, such that each
\ell(\mu_{i})\leq n
.
Then we have

b_{\bm{\mu}}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\bm{\nu}}c^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\nu}},

where
\bm{\nu}=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{r-1})
is a vector in which each partition
\nu_{j}
has even columns, with
\ell(\nu_{j})\leq n
.

Proof.

(a) By seesaw reciprocity (
2.13
), we have

(3.1)

b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}=\left[\widetilde{E}^{\lambda}_{2n}:\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\widetilde{E}^{\mu_{i}}_{2n}\right]_{\textstyle{,}}

where the modules on the right-hand side are for
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
.
The assumption on
n
implies that each of the parameters
k_{i}
, as well as
k
, are in the stable range (see the table in Lemma
2.4
).
Further, the assumptions on
\ell(\lambda)
and
\ell(\mu_{i})
guarantee that the desired representations occur in the Howe duality setting of Theorem
2.3
.
It follows from Lemma
2.4
that as
\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules,

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}^{\mu_{i}}_{2n}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\mu_{i}}_{n},\qquad 1\leq i\leq r,

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}^{\lambda}_{2n}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\lambda}_{n}.

Substituting these into (
3.1
), we have

(3.2)

b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}=\left[\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\lambda}_{n}:\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\mu_{i}}_{n}\right]_{\textstyle{.}}

By Table
1
, the
\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}
-module
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\mu_{i}}_{n}
becomes

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r-1}\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\right)\otimes\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}F^{\mu_{i}}_{n}

\displaystyle\cong\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r-1}\left(\bigoplus_{\nu_{j}}F^{\nu_{j}}_{n}\right)\otimes\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}F^{\mu_{i}}_{n}

each
\nu_{j}
with even rows, and
\ell(\nu_{j})\leq n

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\mu_{1}}_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes F^{\mu_{r}}_{n}\otimes F^{\nu_{1}}_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes F^{\nu_{r-1}}

summing over all
\bm{\nu}=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{r-1})

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes\bigoplus_{\gamma}c^{\gamma}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\nu}}F^{\gamma}_{n}

by (
2.7
)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma}\bigoplus_{\bm{\nu}}c^{\gamma}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\nu}}\left(\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\gamma}_{n}\right).

Upon substituting this into (
3.2
), it is clear that
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}}=\sum_{\bm{\nu}}c^{\lambda}_{\bm{\mu}\bm{\nu}}
, where the sum ranges over those vectors
\bm{\nu}
satisfying the conditions stated in the theorem.
This completes the proof of part (a).

(b) The argument is the same as in part (a),
mutatis mutandis
.
In this case, by seesaw reciprocity (
2.13
) we have

(3.3)

b_{\bm{\mu}}^{\lambda}=\left[\widetilde{F}^{\lambda}_{p,q}:\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\widetilde{F}^{\mu_{i}}_{p,q}\right]_{\textstyle{.}}

The assumptions on
p
and
q
imply that each of the parameters
k_{i}
, as well as
k
, are in the stable range (see the table in Lemma
2.4
).
Further, the assumptions on
\ell(\lambda^{\pm})
and
\ell(\mu^{\pm}_{i})
guarantee that the desired representations occur in the Howe duality setting of Theorem
2.3
.
It follows from Lemma
2.4
that as
\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{gl}_{p}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{q}
-modules,

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{\mu_{i}}_{p,q}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes F^{\mu^{+}_{i}}_{p}\otimes F^{\mu^{-}_{i}}_{q},\qquad 1\leq i\leq r,

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{\lambda}_{p,q}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes F^{\lambda^{+}}_{p}\otimes F^{\lambda^{-}}_{q}

Substituting these expressions into (
3.3
), we obtain

(3.4)

b_{\bm{\mu}}^{\lambda}=\left[\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes\left(F^{\lambda^{+}}_{p}\otimes F^{\lambda^{-}}_{q}\right):\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes\left(F^{\mu_{i}^{+}}_{p}\otimes F^{\mu_{i}^{-}}_{q}\right)\right]_{\textstyle{.}}

By Table
1
, the
\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}
-module
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes\left(F^{\mu_{i}^{+}}_{p}\otimes F^{\mu_{i}^{-}}_{q}\right)
becomes

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r-1}\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\right)\otimes\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}F^{\mu^{+}_{i}}_{p}\otimes F^{\mu^{-}_{i}}_{q}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r-1}\left(\bigoplus_{\nu_{j}}F^{\nu_{j}}_{p}\otimes F^{\nu_{j}}_{q}\right)\otimes\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}F^{\mu^{+}_{i}}_{p}\otimes F^{\mu^{-}_{i}}_{q}\right)\text{ where each $\ell(\nu_{j})\leq\min\{p,q\}$}

\displaystyle\cong\left(\bigoplus_{\bm{\nu}}\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r-1}F^{\nu_{j}}_{p}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r-1}F^{\nu_{j}}_{q}\right)\right)\otimes\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}F^{\mu^{+}_{i}}_{p}\right)\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}F^{\mu^{-}_{i}}_{q}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}F^{\mu^{+}_{i}}_{p}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r-1}F^{\nu_{j}}_{p}\right)\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}F^{\mu^{-}_{i}}_{q}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r-1}F^{\nu_{j}}_{q}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes\left(\bigoplus_{\gamma^{+}}c^{\gamma^{+}}_{\bm{\mu}^{+}\bm{\nu}}\left(F^{\gamma^{+}}_{p}\right)\right)\otimes\left(\bigoplus_{\gamma^{-}}c^{\gamma^{-}}_{\bm{\mu}^{-}\bm{\nu}}\left(F^{\gamma^{-}}_{q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=\\
(\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-})\end{subarray}}\bigoplus_{\bm{\nu}}c^{\gamma^{+}}_{\bm{\mu}^{+}\bm{\nu}}\>c^{\gamma^{-}}_{\bm{\mu}^{-}\bm{\nu}}\left(\mathbb{C}[{\rm M}_{p,q}]\otimes F^{\gamma^{+}}_{p}\otimes F^{\gamma^{-}}_{q}\right)_{\textstyle{.}}

Upon substituting this into (
3.4
), it is clear that
b_{\bm{\mu}}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\bm{\nu}}c^{\lambda^{+}}_{\bm{\mu}^{+}\bm{\nu}}c^{\lambda^{-}}_{\bm{\mu}^{-}\bm{\nu}}
, where
\bm{\nu}
satisfies the conditions stated in the theorem.
This completes the proof of part (b).

(c) The proof is identical to part (a), this time using the
K={\rm Sp}_{2k}
case in Theorem
2.3
, Lemma
2.4
, and Table
1
.
∎

4.
Branching rule from
K
to the minimal
M

The main result in this section, namely Theorem
4.2
, is a detailed statement of Theorem
1.2
.
This result is a tableau-theoretic branching rule from
K
to its minimal direct sum embedding
M
.
Unlike Theorem
3.1
, this branching rule is fully general, valid for all irreducible finite-dimensional rational representations of
K
and
M
.

Throughout this section,
M
is given by the special case in (
1.2
) where
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{r}=1
.
The irreducible representations
W^{\bm{\delta}}
of
M
are labeled by the following
k
-tuples
\bm{\delta}
:

(4.1)

\begin{array}[]{l|l|l}K&M&\bm{\delta}\in\widehat{M}\\
\hline\cr{\rm O}_{k}&({\rm O}_{1})^{k}&\bm{\delta}\in\{0,1\}^{k}\\
{\rm GL}_{k}&({\rm GL}_{1})^{k}&\bm{\delta}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\\
{\rm Sp}_{2k}&({\rm Sp}_{2})^{k}&\bm{\delta}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\end{array}

We now use
\bm{\delta}
rather than
\bm{\mu}
, since the integers
\delta_{i}
will be viewed as differences between frequencies of entries in certain tableaux.
Let
{\rm SSYT}(k)
denote the set of semistandard tableaux whose entries are taken from the ordered alphabet
1<\cdots<k
.
Likewise, let
{\rm SSYT}(1,\bar{1},\ldots,k,\bar{k})
denote the set of semistandard tableaux whose entries are taken from the ordered alphabet
1<\bar{1}<\cdots<k<\bar{k}
.

Definition 4.1

(
K
-tableaux and their weights)
.

(a)

A tableau
T\in{\rm SSYT}(k)
is an
{\rm O}_{k}
-tableau
if, for all
1\leq i\leq k
, we have

\#\{\text{boxes in first two columns of $T$ with entry $\leq i$}\}\leq i.

We define
\mathbf{wt}(T)
to be the vector
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\widehat{M}=\{0,1\}^{k}
such that

w_{i}=\#\{\text{boxes in $T$ with entry $i$}\}\>{\rm mod}\>2.

(b)

A pair
(T^{+},T^{-})\in{\rm SSYT}(k)\times{\rm SSYT}(k)
is a
{\rm GL}_{k}
-tableau
if, for all
1\leq i\leq k
, we have

\#\{\text{boxes in first column of $T^{+}$ or $T^{-}$ with entry $\leq i$}\}\leq i.

We define
\mathbf{wt}(T^{+},T^{-})
to be the vector
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\widehat{M}=\mathbb{Z}^{k}
such that

w_{i}=\#\{\text{boxes in $T^{+}$ with entry $i$}\}-\#\{\text{boxes in $T^{-}$ with entry $i$}\}.

(c)

A tableau
T\in{\rm SSYT}(1,\bar{1},\ldots,k,\bar{k})
is an
{\rm Sp}_{2k}
-tableau
if, for all
1\leq i\leq k
, we have

\#\{\text{boxes in first column of $T$ with entry $\leq\bar{\imath}$}\}\leq i.

Moreover, we say that
T
is an
{\rm Sp}_{2k}
-ballot tableau
if, as one reads the word of
T
, the number of
\bar{\imath}
’s never exceeds the number of
i
’s, for each
1\leq i\leq k
.

If
T
is an
{\rm Sp}_{2k}
-ballot tableau, then we define
\mathbf{wt}(T)
to be the vector
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in\widehat{M}=\mathbb{N}^{k}
such that

w_{i}=\#\{\text{boxes in $T$ with entry $i$}\}-\#\{\text{boxes in $T$ with entry $\bar{\imath}$}\}.

Note that Definition
4.1
can be restated as follows:
T
is a
K
-tableau if and only if, upon restricting
T
to those entries
\leq i
(ignoring the overlines for
{\rm Sp}_{2k}
), the resulting shape lies in the set
\widehat{{\rm O}}_{i}
or
\widehat{{\rm GL}}_{i}
or
\widehat{{\rm Sp}}_{2i}
.
We observe that an
{\rm Sp}_{2k}
-ballot tableau cannot contain any entries
\bar{\imath}
in its first row, and therefore cannot contain the entry
\bar{1}
at all.

For
\lambda\in\widehat{K}
and
\bm{\delta}\in\widehat{M}
, we define the following sets of
K
-tableaux refined by their shape and weight:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{T}({\rm O}_{k})^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}&\coloneqq\{\text{${\rm O}_{k}$-tableaux $T$ of shape $\lambda$, such that $\mathbf{wt}(T)=\bm{\delta}$}\},\\
\mathcal{T}({\rm GL}_{k})^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}&\coloneqq\{\text{${\rm GL}_{k}$-tableaux $(T^{+},T^{-})$ of shape $(\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-})$, such that $\mathbf{wt}(T^{+},T^{-})=\bm{\delta}$}\},\\
\mathcal{T}({\rm Sp}_{2k})^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}&\coloneqq\{\text{${\rm Sp}_{2k}$-ballot tableaux $T$ of shape $\lambda$, such that $\mathbf{wt}(T)=\bm{\delta}$}\}.\end{split}

Theorem 4.2

(cf. Theorem
1.2
)
.

Let
M\subseteq K
as in (
4.1
).
Let
\lambda\in\widehat{K}
and
\bm{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})\in\widehat{M}
.
Let
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}\coloneqq[W^{\bm{\delta}}:U^{\lambda}]
denote the branching multiplicity from
K
to
M
.
We have

b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}=\#\mathcal{T}(K)^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}.

4.2
, we present the following examples.

.

Let
\lambda\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{k}
, let
\mu\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{k-1}
, and let
\delta\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{1}=\{0,1\}
.
Let
\overline{\lambda}\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{k}
,
\overline{\mu}\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{k-1}
, and
\overline{\delta}\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{1}
be the associated partitions, in the sense of Definition
2.1
.
We have the following:

(1)

b^{\lambda}_{(\mu,\delta)}=b^{\overline{\lambda}}_{(\overline{\mu},\overline{\delta})}
.

(2)

\lambda/\mu
is a strip such that
|\lambda/\mu|\equiv\delta\>{\rm mod}\>2
if and only if
\overline{\lambda}/\overline{\mu}
is a strip such that
|\overline{\lambda}/\overline{\mu}|\equiv\overline{\delta}\>{\rm mod}\>2
.

Proof.

To prove (1), observe that upon restricting the determinant representation of
{\rm O}_{k}
to the subgroup
{\rm O}_{k-1}\times{\rm O}_{1}
, we have
\mathbf{det}_{k}\cong\mathbf{det}_{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{det}_{1}
.
Thus, starting from (
2.3
) and then restricting from
{\rm O}_{k}
to
{\rm O}_{k-1}\times{\rm O}_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle E^{\overline{\lambda}}_{k}

\displaystyle\cong E^{\lambda}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{det}_{k}

\displaystyle\cong\left(\bigoplus_{\mu,\delta}b^{\lambda}_{(\mu,\delta)}E^{\mu}_{k-1}\otimes E^{\delta}_{1}\right)\otimes(\mathbf{det}_{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{det}_{1})

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\mu,\delta}b^{\lambda}_{(\mu,\delta)}\left(E^{\mu}_{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{det}_{k-1}\right)\otimes\left(E^{\delta}_{1}\otimes\mathbf{det}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\mu,\delta}b^{\lambda}_{(\mu,\delta)}E^{\overline{\mu}}_{k-1}\otimes E^{\overline{\delta}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\mu,\delta}b^{\overline{\lambda}}_{(\overline{\mu},\overline{\delta})}E^{\overline{\mu}}_{k-1}\otimes E^{\overline{\delta}}_{1}.

To prove (2), suppose that
\lambda/\mu
is a strip such that
|\lambda/\mu|\equiv\delta\>{\rm mod}\>2
.
Then
\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\mu)
is either 0 or 1.
Since
\ell(\overline{\lambda})-\ell(\overline{\mu})=(k-\ell(\lambda))-(k-1-\ell(\mu))=1-(\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\mu))
, we have that
\ell(\overline{\lambda})-\ell(\overline{\mu})
is also either 0 or 1, and has the opposite parity as
\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\mu)
.
Since associated partitions differ only in their first column, it follows that
\overline{\lambda}/\overline{\mu}
is also a strip, and that
|\overline{\lambda}/\overline{\mu}|
has the opposite parity as
|\lambda/\mu|
, so that
|\overline{\lambda}/\overline{\mu}|\equiv\overline{\delta}\>{\rm mod}\>2
.
Since
\lambda\mapsto\overline{\lambda}
is an involution, the same argument also proves the converse.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

(a) Suppose
K={\rm O}_{k}
.
We begin by finding a branching rule from
{\rm O}_{k}
to
{\rm O}_{k-1}\times{\rm O}_{1}
.

to

.
For this, let
\lambda\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{k}

We apply the Howe duality setting of Theorem
2.3
with

.
Let
\mu_{k-1}\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{k-1}
and
\delta_{k}\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{1}=\{0,1\}

for such
\mu_{k-1}
.

\ell(\mu_{k-1})\leq k/2
.
It then follows from the table in Lemma
2.4
that we are in the stable range for both
{\rm O}_{k}
and
{\rm O}_{k-1}
.
Therefore, by Lemmas
2.4
and
2.6
, we have the following isomorphisms of
\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules:

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}^{\lambda}_{2n}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\lambda}_{n},

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}^{\mu_{k-1}}_{2n}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\mu_{k-1}}_{n},

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}^{\delta_{k}}_{2n}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}:\\

Using these to rewrite (
4.3
), we obtain

(4.4)

b^{\lambda}_{(\mu_{k-1},\delta_{k})}=\left[\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\lambda}_{n}:\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes F^{\mu_{k-1}}_{n}\otimes\bigoplus_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}:\\

), the

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\bigoplus_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}:\\

m\equiv\delta_{k}\>{\rm mod}\>2\end{subarray}}}\mathbb{C}[{\rm SM}_{n}]\otimes\Bigg(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma:\\

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma:\\

(We use the term

in the sense of Definition
2.2
.)

\widehat{{\rm O}}_{1}=\{0,1\}
:

(4.6)

{\rm wt}(\gamma/\mu)\coloneqq|\gamma/\mu|\>{\rm mod}\>2.

in Definition

Substituting (
4.5
) into (

), we obtain the following branching rule from
{\rm O}_{k}
to
{\rm O}_{k-1}\times{\rm O}_{1}

(4.7)

b^{\lambda}_{(\mu_{k-1},\delta_{k})}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{$\lambda/\mu_{k-1}$ is a strip such that ${\rm wt}(\lambda/\mu_{k-1})=\delta_{k}$},\\

, Lemma

implies that the rule (

) is valid for all

(4.8)

E^{\lambda}_{k}\cong\bigoplus_{\delta_{k}\in\{0,1\}}\Bigg(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu_{k-1}\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{k-1}:\\

\lambda=\mu_{k}
, we rewrite (
4.8
) to obtain a branching rule from
{\rm O}_{i}
to
{\rm O}_{i-1}\times{\rm O}_{1}

E^{\mu_{i}}_{i}\cong\bigoplus_{\delta_{i}\in\{0,1\}}\Bigg(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu_{i-1}\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{i-1}:\\

{\rm wt}(\mu_{i}/\mu_{i-1})=\delta_{i}\end{subarray}}\hskip-12.91663ptE^{\mu_{i-1}}_{i-1}\otimes E^{\delta_{i}}_{1}\Bigg)_{\textstyle{.}}

To complete the proof, we construct the following bijection between
\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}
and
\mathcal{T}({\rm O}_{k})^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}
.

\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}
.

, start with the subtableau
T_{1}
of shape
\mu_{1}

of shape
\mu_{i}

T_{i}
is an

-tableau since

is an
{\rm O}_{i-1}
-tableau and
\mu_{i}\in\widehat{{\rm O}}_{i}
.

{\rm O}_{k}
-tableau

Conversely, in the other direction, let

.
To construct a chain

\leq i

4.10
).

5.
Special cases of the branching rule

As an application of Theorem
4.2
, we can easily determine the multiplicities
b^{\lambda}_{\bm{\delta}}
in the special case where
\lambda
(or each of
\lambda^{+}
and
\lambda^{-}
, in the case where
K={\rm GL}_{k}
) is a single row or a single column.
We use the shorthand
(1^{a})\coloneqq(1,\ldots,1)
, where
1
is repeated
a
times.
To express the branching rules below, for
\bm{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})\in\widehat{M}
, we define

|\bm{\delta}|\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\delta_{i}|,

which is a nonnegative integer by (
4.1
).

We first recall the notion of a multiset.
A
multiset
is a collection of elements in which the elements are allowed to occur multiple times.
For example,
[1,1,2]
and
[1,2,2,2]
are distinct multisets that are formed from the same finite set
\{1,2\}
.
The number of times a given element occurs in a multiset is referred to as the
multiplicity
of the element.
The cardinality of the multiset is the sum of the multiplicities of its elements, so that
|[1,1,2]|=3
and
|[1,2,2,2]|=4
.
We will use the following notation for multisets constructed from a finite subset
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
.
We denote the multiset formed from
A
where the element
a_{i}
has multiplicity
m_{i}\geq 0
by
\{a_{1}^{m_{1}},a_{2}^{m_{2}},\ldots,a_{k}^{m_{k}}\}
.
The cardinality of such a multiset is then
\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}
.
It is well known
[
36
]
*p. 26 that the number of multisets of cardinality
n
that can be constructed with elements taken from a finite set of cardinality
k
is given by

(5.1)

\left(\!\!\binom{k}{n}\!\!\right)\coloneqq\binom{k+n-1}{n}=\binom{k+n-1}{k-1}_{\textstyle{.}}

We point out that in the following corollary, part (a) gives the branching rule for the spherical harmonics, since the irreducible representation
E^{(a)}_{k}
is realized by the space of homogeneous
{\rm O}_{k}
-spherical harmonic polynomials of degree
a
in
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}]
.
Moreover, part (b) includes the weight multiplicity formula for the nontrivial component of the adjoint representation of
{\rm GL}_{k}
, which corresponds to the special case
b=c=1
.

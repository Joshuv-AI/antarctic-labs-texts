Depth Preservation and Close-Field Transfer in the Local Langlands Correspondence
Manish Mishra

Abstract
We introduce a revised notion of depth for Langlands parameters for a connected reductive G G defined over a nonarchimedean local field F F that restores depth preservation under the local Langlands correspondence (LLC)—in particular for all tori. We leverage that preservation to derive structural results that, taken together, yield a canonical transfer of broad harmonic-analytic results from characteristic 0 0 to characteristic p p . When F F has suitably large positive characteristic, we prove a block-by-block equivalence: each Bernstein block of G ⁡ ( F ) G(F) is equivalent to a corresponding block for some G ′ ​ ( F ′ ) G^{\prime}(F^{\prime}) with F ′ F^{\prime} of characteristic 0 0 ℓ \ell -close to F F ; using this, we show that a LLC in characteristic 0 0 corresponds canonically to a LLC in characteristic p p . For regular supercuspidals we give a direct, more structured construction via Kaletha. Along the way we recover and extend results on ℓ \ell -close fields—introducing a depth-transfer function generalizing the normalized Hasse–Herbrand function, proving truncated isomorphisms for arbitrary tori and parahorics, establishing a depth and supercuspidality preserving Kazhdan-type Hecke-algebra isomorphism for arbitrary maximal parahorics of arbitrary connected reductive groups; and a generalized Cartan decomposition for arbitrary maximal parahorics—thereby subsuming several earlier results in the literature. Collectively, the results let one work in characteristic 0 0 without loss of generality for a wide swath of harmonic analysis on p p -adic groups.

Introduction

The depth of representations and parameters serves as a fundamental invariant in the harmonic analysis of p-adic groups and the local Langlands correspondence (LLC). The central contribution of this work is the introduction of a new notion of depth of a Langlands parameter which rectifies a key deficiency in the standard notion - the preservation of depth. Leveraging this foundation, we establish a canonical transfer of harmonic analysis results from non-archimedean local fields of characteristic zero to those over local function fields, including the local Langlands correspondence.

For a non-archimedean local field
F
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{F}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
, local class-field theory interprets the filtration

1+\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{\,r}\;\subset\;F^{\times},\qquad r\geq 0,

as the upper–numbering filtration
\{W_{F}^{\,s}\}_{s\geq 0}
of the Weil group. The
Hasse–Herbrand function

\phi_{E/F}
transfers this numbering across an extension

, matching the depth‐
r

E^{\times}
with the
s:=\phi_{E/F}(er)
-th filtration subgroup
W_{F}^{s}
, where

denotes the ramification index of the extension. The correspondence is rigorously captured in the local Langlands correspondence for the induced torus
T=\Res_{E/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}
: a depth-
r

corresponds to its
standard
depth
\Phi_{T}(r):=\phi_{E/F}(er)
Langlands parameter. Consequently, when

is wildly ramified, this standard depth fails to respect depth preservation limiting its compatibility with harmonic analysis.
To resolve this, we work with Yu’s
minimal congruence filtration
subgroups (see §
2
) and we introduce the following notion of the depth of a Langlands parameter
\varphi

F
-torus

\depth_{T}(\varphi)\;:=\;\sup_{f:R\to T}\,\depth_{R}\!\bigl(\widehat{f}\circ\varphi\bigr).

Here, the supremum is taken over all induced
F
-tori

and all
F
-morphisms

For arbitrary

\depth_{G}(\varphi):=\max\{\text{depth of the toral parameter},\ \text{depth of the Weyl parameter}\},

In the case of a

To control depth bounds for connected reductive group

4
Depth comparison under LLC for tori

4.1
Standard depth of a parameter and its generalization

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\chi\in\Hom(T(F),\mathbb{C}^{\times})
,
\chi

ramified
(i.e.,
\chi|_{T(F)_{0}}\neq 1
). Define,

\depth_{T}(\chi):=\inf\bigl\{r\geq 0\mid\chi|_{T(F)_{>r}}=1\text{ and }\chi|_{T(F)_{r}}\neq 1\bigr\}.

For
\varphi\in H^{1}(W_{F},\widehat{T})
, let

\depth^{\std}(\varphi)=\inf\bigl\{r\mid\varphi|_{W_{F}^{r}}\neq 1\text{ and }\varphi|_{W_{F}^{r+}}=1\bigr\}.

We now define a new notion of depth of a Langlands parameter which is better aligned for local Langlands correspondence.

Definition 4.2

(Depth of a Langlands Parameter for a Torus)
.

Let
T
be an arbitrary
F
-torus, and let
\varphi:W_{F}\to\widehat{T}
be a Langlands parameter. The
depth
of
\varphi
is defined as

\operatorname{depth}(\varphi)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f:R\to T\\
R\text{ induced }F\text{-torus}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\widehat{f}\circ\varphi),

where:

•

The supremum is over all induced
F
-tori
R
and all morphisms
f:R\to T
of
F
-tori.

•

\widehat{f}:\widehat{T}\to\widehat{R}
is the dual morphism of
f
.

•

\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\widehat{f}\circ\varphi)
is the depth of the parameter
\widehat{f}\circ\varphi:W_{F}\to\widehat{R}
, defined as follows:

Suppose
R=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\operatorname{Res}_{E_{j}/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is an induced torus. Then for a parameter
\psi:W_{F}\to\widehat{R}
, we define:

\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\psi)=\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left(\frac{1}{e_{j}}\cdot\phi_{E_{j}/F}^{-1}\left(\operatorname{dep}^{\std}(\psi_{j})\right)\right),

where:

•

\psi_{j}:W_{F}\to\widehat{R}_{j}
is the
j
-th component of
\psi
, corresponding to
R_{j}=\operatorname{Res}_{E_{j}/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

•

\operatorname{dep}^{\std}(\psi_{j})=\inf\{t\geq 0\mid\psi_{j}(W_{F}^{t+})=\{1\}\}
is the standard depth of the parameter
\psi_{j}
.

•

e_{j}=e(E_{j}/F)
is the ramification index of the extension
E_{j}/F
.

•

\phi_{E_{j}/F}
is the Herbrand function of the extension
E_{j}/F
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
T
is tamely ramified, then
\depth^{\std}(\varphi)=\depth(\varphi)
.

Proof.

Assume
T=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{Res}_{E_{i}/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}
, where each
E_{i}/F
is a finite tamely ramified extension with ramification index
e_{i}
. For a Langlands parameter
\varphi:W_{F}\to\widehat{T}
, denote by
\varphi_{i}:W_{F}\to\widehat{T}_{i}
the
i
-th component.
Since
T
is tamely ramified, by Lemma
3.2
,
\phi_{E_{i}/F}(t)=t/e_{i}
for all
t\geq 0
.
Therefore
\phi_{E_{i}/F}^{-1}(r_{i})=e_{i}r_{i}
, and so:

\operatorname{depth}_{T}(\varphi)=\max_{i}\left(\frac{1}{e_{i}}\cdot e_{i}r_{i}\right)=\max_{i}r_{i}=\max_{i}s_{i}=\operatorname{depth}^{\text{std}}(\varphi).

Thus,
\operatorname{depth}(\varphi)=\operatorname{depth}^{\text{std}}(\varphi)
for induced tamely ramified tori.
Now let
T
be an arbitrary tamely ramified torus. Let
\varphi:W_{F}\to\widehat{T}
be a Langlands parameter, and let
\operatorname{depth}^{\text{std}}(\varphi)=r
. Let
E/F
be a tamely ramified extension splitting
T
. Consider the tamely ramified induced torus
R=\operatorname{Res}_{E/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}
. There exists a surjective morphism
f:R\to T
, namely the norm map. For the parameter
\widehat{f}\circ\varphi:W_{F}\to\widehat{R}
, we have
\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\widehat{f}\circ\varphi)=\operatorname{depth}^{\text{std}}(\widehat{f}\circ\varphi)
since
R
is induced and tamely ramified. As
\widehat{f}
is injective,
\widehat{f}\circ\varphi
is trivial on
W_{F}^{s}
if and only if
\varphi
is trivial on
W_{F}^{s}
. Thus,
\operatorname{depth}^{\text{std}}(\widehat{f}\circ\varphi)=\operatorname{depth}^{\text{std}}(\varphi)=r
. Therefore,
\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\widehat{f}\circ\varphi)=r
. By Definition
4.2
,

\operatorname{depth}(\varphi)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{\prime}:R^{\prime}\to T\\
R^{\prime}\text{ induced}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{depth}_{R^{\prime}}(\widehat{f^{\prime}}\circ\varphi)\geq\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\widehat{f}\circ\varphi)=r.

Now, for any
f^{\prime}:R^{\prime}\to T
with
R^{\prime}
induced,
\operatorname{depth}_{R^{\prime}}(\widehat{f^{\prime}}\circ\varphi)\leq\operatorname{depth}^{\text{std}}(\widehat{f^{\prime}}\circ\varphi)
by Lemma
3.2
. Since
\widehat{f^{\prime}}\circ\varphi
is trivial on
W_{F}^{r+}
(since
\varphi
is), we have
\operatorname{depth}^{\text{std}}(\widehat{f^{\prime}}\circ\varphi)\leq r
. Thus,
\operatorname{depth}_{R^{\prime}}(\widehat{f^{\prime}}\circ\varphi)\leq r
, and the supremum is at most
r
. Combined with the inequality above,
\operatorname{depth}(\varphi)=r=\operatorname{depth}^{\text{std}}(\varphi)
.

∎

Definition 4.4

(Depth-transfer function
\Phi_{T}
)
.

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field and let
R/F
be an induced
F
-torus;

R=\prod_{i=1}^{n}R_{E_{i}/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}

where each
E_{i}/F
is a finite separable extension of ramification index
e_{i}
. For
r\geq 0
, put

\Phi_{R}(r):=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\phi_{E_{i}/F}\!\bigl(e_{i}\,r\bigr),

Now let
T
be an arbitrary
F
-torus. For every real number
r>0
, let
\hat{T}_{(r)}
denote the subgroup of
\hat{T}
generated by the subset

\bigcup_{f:R\to T}\hat{f}^{-1}(\hat{R}^{W_{F}^{\Phi_{R}(r)}}).

Here the union is taken over all induced tori
R
and all morphisms of
F

. Define:

\Phi_{T}(r):=\inf\Bigl\{s\geq 0\;\Big|\;\widehat{T}^{W_{F}^{s}}\supseteq\hat{T}_{(r)}\Bigr\}.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
T/F
be an arbitrary torus, let

\varphi:W_{F}\longrightarrow\widehat{T}

be a Langlands parameter. Then

\Phi_{T}\!\bigl(\dep(\varphi)\bigr)\;\;\geq\;\;\dep^{\std}(\varphi).

Proof.

Put
s:=\dep^{\std}(\varphi)
and
r=\dep(\varphi)
.
By definition, there exists
w\in W_{F}^{\,s}
with
\varphi(w)\neq 1
and
\varphi(W_{F}^{s+})=1
.
Choose an algebraic character
\lambda:\widehat{T}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\lambda\!\bigl(\varphi(w)\bigr)\neq 1
. Let
E
be the finite extension of
F
fixed by the stabilizer of the weight
\lambda
; then
\lambda
is
W_{E}
-invariant. Set
R_{\lambda}\;:=\;\Res_{E/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}
. The dual of this induced torus is
\widehat{R_{\lambda}}\simeq\Ind_{W_{E}}^{W_{F}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Frobenius reciprocity gives a canonical bijection

\mathrm{Hom}_{W_{F}}\!\big(\widehat{T},\ \mathrm{Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{F}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\big)\ \cong\ \mathrm{Hom}_{W_{E}}\!\big(\widehat{T},\ \mathbb{C}^{\times}\big),

so
\lambda
corresponds to a unique
W_{F}
-equivariant morphism
\widehat{f}_{\lambda}:\widehat{T}\to\widehat{R_{\lambda}}
with
\mathrm{pr}\circ\widehat{f}_{\lambda}=\lambda
, where pr is dual to the tautological
F
-morphism of tori
\iota:\mathbb{G}_{m}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Res}_{E/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Define
\varphi_{\lambda}:=\widehat{f}_{\lambda}\circ\varphi
. Since
\lambda(\varphi(w))\neq 1
and
w\in W_{F}^{s}
, we have,

\dep^{\std}\!\bigl(\varphi_{\lambda}\bigr)\;\geq\;s=\dep(\varphi).

For every such morphism
f:R\to T
with
R
induced, write

\varphi_{R}:=\widehat{f}\circ\varphi.

By the definition of
r=\dep(\varphi)
, we have

\dep(\varphi_{R})\leq r
. For an induced torus
R
, we obtain from the definition of
\varphi_{R}
,

\dep^{\std}(\varphi_{R})\;\leq\;\varphi_{R}\!\bigl(\dep(\varphi_{R})\bigr)\;\leq\;\varphi_{R}(r),

where the second inequity follows since each
\Phi_{R}
is non-decreasing. Taking the supremum over all induced
R
and
f
,

\sup_{f:R\to T}\dep^{\std}(\varphi_{R})\;\leq\;\sup_{f:R\to T}\Phi_{R}(r).

From (I) and (II), we obtain the desired inequality

\dep_{\std}(\varphi)\leq\Phi_{T}\!\bigl(\dep(\varphi)\bigr).

∎

Lemma 4.6

Let

4.3

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field, let
T/F
be a torus, and fix

.
Then

T(F)_{r}/T(F)_{r+}\;=\;\Bigl\langle\,f\bigl(R(F)_{r}/R(F)_{r+}\bigr)\,\;\Bigm|\;f:R\rightarrow T,\;R/F\text{ induced}\Bigr\rangle.

Proof.

For the strict henselization

gives:

T(F^{\text{sh}})_{r}=\langle T(F^{\text{sh}})_{n+1},f\left(R(F^{\text{sh}})_{r}\right)\mid\ \ f:R\to T\text{ defined over }F^{\mathrm{sh}},\text{ with }R\text{ an induced }F^{\mathrm{sh}}\text{-torus}\Big\rangle.

where

To show this, define

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field and
T/F
an
F
-torus. Denote its complex dual torus by
\widehat{T}
. For every real number
r\geq 0
, the following holds:

\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}(T(F),\mathbb{C}^{\times})
with corresponding parameter
\varphi=\mathcal{L}_{T}(\chi)

\chi

\varphi

\operatorname{dep}(\chi)=\operatorname{dep}(\varphi),

\operatorname{dep}(\varphi)

For any induced torus
R
and morphism

\chi_{f}=\chi\circ f

is
\hat{f}\circ\varphi
. The depth of
\chi_{f}
satisfies
\operatorname{dep}(\chi_{f})\leq\operatorname{dep}(\chi)
since

is a morphism of tori, and the minimal congruence filtration is functorial. For induced tori,
\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\hat{f}\circ\varphi)=\operatorname{dep}(\chi_{f})
by Lemma
4.7
. Thus:

\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\hat{f}\circ\varphi)=\operatorname{dep}(\chi_{f})\leq\operatorname{dep}(\chi).

This holds for all

, so:

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f:R\to T\\
R\text{ induced}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\hat{f}\circ\varphi)\leq\operatorname{dep}(\chi).

We now show the reverse inequality.
Suppose
\varphi
has depth
r
. Then for all
f:R\rightarrow T
, since
\mathcal{L}_{R}(\chi\circ f)=\widehat{f}(\mathcal{L}_{T}(\chi))
, by Propoistion
4.5
,
\chi\circ f\text{ trivial on }R(F)_{r+}
. Equivalently,
\chi
is trivial on
f(R(F)_{r+})
for all

. The reverse inequality now follows from Lemma
4.8
. To see this, assume on the contrary that

(since
\chi

but not on

). By Lemma

, there exists an induced torus

, and

such that:

f(u)=t\cdot v\quad\text{for some}\quad v\in T(F)_{d+}.

Since
\chi
is trivial on
T(F)_{d+}
,
\chi(f(u))=\chi(t)\cdot\chi(v)=\chi(t)\neq 1
. Let
\chi_{f}=\chi\circ f
. Then
\chi_{f}(u)=\chi(f(u))\neq 1
, and

, so
\text{dep}(\chi_{f})\geq d
. But
\text{dep}(\chi_{f})=\text{dep}_{R}(\widehat{f}\circ\varphi)\leq r
(since
\text{dep}(\varphi)=r

. Hence,
\text{dep}(\chi)\leq r
, completing the proof.

Definition 5.1

(Deligne)
.

Two non-archimedean local fields
F
and
F^{\prime}
are
\ell
-close
(
\ell\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
) if there exists an isomorphism of topological rings

\mathscr{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{\ell}\cong\mathscr{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{\ell}.

Deligne
[
Del84
]
constructs a canonical isomorphism,

\operatorname{Del}_{\ell}:\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{\ell}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{\ell},

functorial for finite
\ell
-ramified
\Gamma_{F}
-modules.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
F
and
F^{\prime}
be
\ell
-close fields,
T
and
T^{\prime}
be tori defined over
F
and
F^{\prime}
such that
T^{\prime}
is a transfer of
T
along
\operatorname{Del}_{\ell}
. Then for any
0\leq r\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Phi_{T}(r)\leq\ell
, the canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{Del}_{T,\ell}:H^{1}(W_{F}/W_{F}^{r},\widehat{T}^{W_{F}^{r}})\cong H^{1}(W_{F^{\prime}}/W_{F^{\prime}}^{r},\widehat{T^{\prime}}^{W_{F^{\prime}}^{r}}).

preserves depth.

Proof.

Since
F
and
F^{\prime}
are
\ell
-close via
\operatorname{Del}_{\ell}:\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{\ell}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{\ell}
, the parameter
\varphi
factors through
W_{F}/W_{F}^{s}
with
s=\Phi_{T}(r)\leq\ell
. The isomorphism
H^{1}(W_{F}/W_{F}^{s},\widehat{T}^{W_{F}^{s}})\cong H^{1}(W_{F}^{\prime}/W_{F}^{\prime s},\widehat{T}^{\prime W_{F}^{\prime s}})
transfers
\varphi
to
\varphi^{\prime}
. For any morphism
f:R\to T
with
R
an induced
F
-torus, let
R^{\prime}
be the transfer of
R
to
F^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime}:R^{\prime}\to T^{\prime}
the transfer of
f
. The dual morphism
\hat{f}:\widehat{T}\to\widehat{R}
satisfies
\hat{f}\circ\varphi:W_{F}\to\widehat{R}
, and similarly for
\hat{f}^{\prime}\circ\varphi^{\prime}
.
For an induced torus
R=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\operatorname{Res}_{E_{j}/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}
, recall that the depth
\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\hat{f}\circ\varphi)
is defined as:

\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\hat{f}\circ\varphi)=\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left(\frac{1}{e_{j}}\cdot\phi_{E_{j}/F}^{-1}(\operatorname{dep}^{\std}(\psi_{j}))\right),

where
\psi_{j}:W_{F}\to\widehat{R}_{j}
is the
j
-th component of
\hat{f}\circ\varphi
,
e_{j}=e(E_{j}/F)
is the ramification index, and
\operatorname{dep}^{\std}(\psi_{j})=\inf\{t\geq 0:\psi_{j}(W_{F}^{t+})=1\}
.

The transferred parameter
\hat{f}^{\prime}\circ\varphi^{\prime}
corresponds to
\hat{f}\circ\varphi
under the isomorphism. Since
\operatorname{dep}^{\std}(\psi_{j})\leq\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\hat{f}\circ\varphi)\leq\operatorname{dep}(\varphi)\leq s\leq\ell
, the upper ramification groups
W_{F}^{t}
and
W_{F^{\prime}}^{t}
for
t\leq s
are preserved by
\operatorname{Del}_{\ell}
. Thus:

•

\operatorname{dep}^{\std}(\psi_{j}^{\prime})=\operatorname{dep}^{\std}(\psi_{j})
, as
\psi_{j}^{\prime}
is trivial on
W_{F^{\prime}}^{t}
iff
\psi_{j}
is trivial on
W_{F}^{t}
.

•

The extensions
E_{j}/F
and
E_{j}^{\prime}/F^{\prime}
have identical ramification data: ramification index
e_{j}=e_{j}^{\prime}
and Herbrand function
\phi_{E_{j}/F}=\phi_{E_{j}^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}
.

Therefore:

\frac{1}{e_{j}}\cdot\phi_{E_{j}/F}^{-1}(\operatorname{dep}^{\std}(\psi_{j}))=\frac{1}{e_{j}^{\prime}}\cdot\phi_{E_{j}^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}^{-1}(\operatorname{dep}^{\std}(\psi_{j}^{\prime})),

which implies
\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\hat{f}\circ\varphi)=\operatorname{dep}_{R^{\prime}}(\hat{f}^{\prime}\circ\varphi^{\prime})
.
As
f:R\to T
ranges over all morphisms from induced
F
-tori to
T
, the transferred
f^{\prime}:R^{\prime}\to T^{\prime}
range over all such morphisms for
T^{\prime}
(due to functoriality of the transfer). Thus:

\operatorname{dep}(\varphi)=\sup_{f}\operatorname{dep}_{R}(\hat{f}\circ\varphi)=\sup_{f}\operatorname{dep}_{R^{\prime}}(\hat{f}^{\prime}\circ\varphi^{\prime})=\operatorname{dep}(\varphi^{\prime}).

∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
F
and
F^{\prime}
be
\ell
-close. Let
T/F
be a torus and
T^{\prime}/F^{\prime}
its transfer via the isomorphism
\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{\ell}\cong\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{\ell}
. For any
0\leq r\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Phi_{T}(r)\leq\ell
, we have,

T(F)/T(F)_{r}\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}T^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/T^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{r},

canonically and functorially, and this isomorphism intertwines the local Langlands correspondences for
T
and
T^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.9
, we have a LLC isomorphism at depth
r
:

\mathcal{L}_{T}:\Hom\!\bigl(T(F)/T(F)_{r},\mathbb{C}^{\times}\bigr)\hookrightarrow H^{1}\!\bigl(W_{F}/W_{F}^{s},\widehat{T}^{W_{F}^{s}}\bigr),\quad s=\Phi_{T}(r).

Since
s\leq\ell
, Deligne’s isomorphism induces an isomorphism of parameters,

\operatorname{Del}_{T,\ell}:H^{1}\!\bigl(W_{F}/W_{F}^{s},\widehat{T}^{W_{F}^{s}}\bigr)\cong H^{1}\!\bigl(W_{F}/W_{F}^{s^{\prime}},\widehat{T}^{W_{F}^{s^{\prime}}}\bigr),\quad s^{\prime}=\Phi_{T^{\prime}}(r).

By Proposition
5.2
, this isomorphism preserves depth. Since the image of
\mathcal{L}_{T}
is the subgroup of

H^{1}\!\bigl(W_{F}/W_{F}^{s},\widehat{T}^{W_{F}^{s}}\bigr)
of depth
<r
and likewise for the image of
\mathcal{L}_{T^{\prime}}
, it follows that

\Hom\!\bigl(T(F)/T(F)_{r},\mathbb{C}^{\times}\bigr)\cong\Hom\!\bigl(T^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/T^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{r},\mathbb{C}^{\times}\bigr).

The required isomorphism now follows from Pontryagin duality.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

When
T
is tamely ramified,
s=r
and the bound simplifies to
\ell\geq r
.

6.3
Root groups and their splitting fields

We will recall some standard theory from
[
BT84
]
.
Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field and let
G
be a connected
reductive
F
-group.
Choose a maximal
F
-split torus
A\subset G
and write

\Phi(G,A)
for the corresponding relative root system. Choose a base point
x_{0}
in the apartment
\mathcal{A}(A,F)
of
{A}
. For any point
x\in\mathcal{A}(A,F)
, define,

f_{x}:\Phi(G,A)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad a\longmapsto-a\bigl(x-x_{0}\bigr).

Now let
T
be the centralizer of a maximally
F^{\mathrm{un}}
-split
F
-torus containing

A
; then
T/F
is a maximal torus.
Every absolute root
\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)
determines a
one-parameter unipotent subgroup
U_{\alpha}\subset G_{F^{\mathrm{s}}}
.
For each absolute root
\alpha
let

L_{\alpha}\;:=\;F^{\mathrm{sep}}{}^{\Gamma_{\alpha}},\quad\text{where}\;\Gamma_{\alpha}=\{\sigma\in\Gal(F^{\mathrm{sep}}\!/F)\mid\sigma(U_{\alpha})=U_{\alpha}\}.

The field
L_{\alpha}
is the smallest extension of
F
over
which
U_{\alpha}
is defined; over
L_{\alpha}
one has an

L_{\alpha}
-isomorphism
x_{\alpha}:\mathbf{G}_{a,L_{\alpha}}\!\xrightarrow{\sim}\!U_{\alpha}
,
unique up to scaling by
L_{\alpha}^{\times}
.
Now fix a relative root

.
Choose any absolute root
\alpha
restricting to
a
on
A
. Define the minimal field of definition
F_{a}
as follows.

(a)

Short case (
2a\notin\Phi(G,A)
).

Define

F_{a}\;:=\;L_{\alpha}.

(b)

Long–short case (
2a\in\Phi(G,A)
).

Pick absolute roots
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
with

\alpha|_{A}=\alpha^{\prime}|_{A}=a/2
and
\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}=2a
.
Then

In either case, the field
F_{a}
is independent of the chosen lift
\alpha
.

Write
e_{a}:=e(F_{a}/F)

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
F
and
F^{\prime}
be
\ell
-close local fields and let
G/F
be a connected reductive group with maximal
F
-split torus
A\subset G
. Fix a facet
x\in\mathcal{A}(A,F)
and put
x^{\prime}:=\mathcal{B}_{\ell}(x)
. For each root
a\in\Phi(G,A)
, denote by
F_{a}/F
(resp.
F_{a^{\prime}}/F^{\prime}
) the minimal field of definition of the root subgroup
U_{a}
(resp.
U_{a^{\prime}}
with
a^{\prime}=\Phi_{\ell}(a)
), and let
\phi_{F_{a}/F}
be the Hasse-Herbrand function of that extension. Assume

\;\ell\;\geq\;\phi_{F_{a}/F}\!\bigl(e_{a}\,r\bigr)\quad\text{for every }a\in\Phi(G,A).

Then for every root
a\in\Phi(G,A)
, there is a canonical bijection

U_{a}(F)_{x,0}\big/U_{a}(F)_{x,r}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}U_{a^{\prime}}(F^{\prime})_{x^{\prime},0}\big/U_{a^{\prime}}(F^{\prime})_{x^{\prime},r}.

Proof.

Let
a\in\Phi(G,A)
and
a^{\prime}\in\Phi(G^{\prime},A^{\prime})
be the corresponding roots under the root datum
identification of Proposition
6.3
. If
x\in\mathcal{A}(A,F)
and

x^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}_{\ell}(x)\in\mathcal{A}(A^{\prime},F^{\prime})
, then by
[
Gan19
, Proposition 4.4]
, the building isomorphism preserves affine root functions. In particular, for every
a\in\Phi(G,A)
we have

f_{x}(a)\;=\;f_{x^{\prime}}(a^{\prime}),

where
f_{x}
(resp.
f_{x^{\prime}}
) denotes the affine functional attached to
a
at
x

(resp. to
a^{\prime}
at
x^{\prime}
).
Put
k:=f_{x}(a)=f_{x^{\prime}}(a^{\prime})
and
k^{\prime}:=k+r
. By
[
BT84
, §4.3.2 and §4.3.5]
,
U_{a,k}/U_{a,k^{\prime}}\cong\varpi^{\lceil e_{a}k\rceil}\mathcal{O}_{F_{a}}\big/\varpi^{\lceil e_{a}k^{\prime}\rceil}\mathcal{O}_{F_{a}}
. Hence the quotient depends only on the truncated ring
\mathcal{O}_{F_{a}}\big/\varpi_{F_{a}}^{\,\lceil e_{a}r\rceil}
.
For the finite Galois extension
F_{a}/F
, the jump
e_{a}r
in lower numbering corresponds, under upper numbering, to
\phi_{F_{a}/F}(e_{a}r)
. Hypothesis
(\ast)
gives
\phi_{F_{a}/F}(e_{a}r)\leq\ell

\ell
-closeness provides an isomorphism of truncated valuation rings

\mathcal{O}_{F_{a}}\big/\varpi^{\,\lceil e_{a}r\rceil}\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;\mathcal{O}_{F_{a^{\prime}}}\big/\varpi^{\prime\,\lceil e_{a}r\rceil}.

From this we deduce the following equalities of local invariants:

(i)

the residue fields are canonically identified,

\kappa_{F_{a}}\;\cong\;\kappa_{F_{a^{\prime}}}
;

(ii)

the ramification indices agree, so that

[F_{a}:F]=[F_{a^{\prime}}:F^{\prime}]

(iii)

\Gamma_{a}\;:=\;\nu_{a}\!\bigl(U_{a}(F)\setminus\{1\}\bigr)\;=\;\nu_{a^{\prime}}\!\bigl(U_{a^{\prime}}(F^{\prime})\setminus\{1\}\bigr)\;=\;\Gamma_{a^{\prime}},

\nu_{a}
(resp.
\nu_{a^{\prime}}

(resp.
U_{a^{\prime}}
).

With these invariants equal, the quotients

and

are naturally

,

6.4
Definition: depth-transfer function for reductive groups.

Let
G/F
be a connected reductive group and fix a vertex
x\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{red}}(G,F)
.

•

Write
\mathscr{E}_{x}
for the set of maximally unramified elliptic maximal
F
-tori of
G
that fix

. For every
S\in\mathscr{E}_{x}
, we have the depth-transfer function
\Phi_{S}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
introduced in Definition
4.4
.

•

Choose a maximal split torus
A\subset G
whose apartment contains
x
; let
\Phi(G,A)
be the associated root system. For each

and by

Define

\boxed{\;\Phi_{G,x}(r):=\max~\!\bigl\{\max_{S\in\mathscr{E}_{x}}\Phi_{S}(r),\,\max_{a\in\Phi(G,A)}\phi_{F_{a}/F}(e_{a}\,r)\bigr\},\qquad r\geq 0.}

.

Remark 6.6

(Depth–transfer as a
normalised
Hasse–Herbrand function)
.

For a finite Galois extension
E/F
of non-archimedean local fields with ramification
index
e
, consider the normalised form of the Hasse–Herbrand function
\phi_{E/F}
:

\phi_{E/F}^{\mathrm{norm}}(r)\;:=\;\phi_{E/F}(e\,r),\qquad r\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

so that
\phi_{E/F}^{\mathrm{norm}}(r)=r
whenever

is tamely ramified.
For an induced torus

\Phi_{T}(r)\;=\;\phi_{E/F}(e\,r)\;=\;\phi_{E/F}^{\mathrm{norm}}(r)\qquad(r\geq 0),

so that

coincides with the normalised Hasse–Herbrand.

disappears and it reduces to

and

. Thus, the depth–transfer function

is a generalisation of the normalised Hasse–Herbrand function that captures the combined ramification behaviour of all maximal tori and root data inside an arbitrary reductive group.

6.5
Close field isomorphism of truncated parahorics

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
F
and
F^{\prime}
be
\ell
-close non-archimedean local fields and let
G/F
be a connected reductive group. Fix a vertex
x\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{red}}(G,F)
and let
x^{\prime}:=\mathcal{B}_{\ell}(x)\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{red}}(G^{\prime},F^{\prime})
be its image under the building isomorphism as in Proposition
6.3
. For a real depth
r\geq 0
, assume the truncation level satisfies

\ell\;\geq\;\Phi_{G,x}(r).
Then there is a canonical isomorphism,

\Psi_{x,r}:\dfrac{G(F)_{x,\mathrm{b}}}{G(F)_{x,r}}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\dfrac{G^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{x^{\prime},\mathrm{b}}}{G^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{x^{\prime},r}},

depending only on the level-
\ell
congruence datum
D_{\ell}
, where
G(F)_{x,\mathrm{b}}
(resp.
G^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{x^{\prime},\mathrm{b}}
) denotes the maximal bounded subgroup of
G(F)_{x}
(resp.
G^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{x^{\prime}}
). The isomorphism is respectful of restriction to minimal congruence filtration subgroups.

Proof.

Choose any
S\in\mathscr{E}_{x}
. Since
\ell\geq\Phi_{G,x}(r)\geq\Phi_{S}(r)
, Theorem
5.3
supplies an isomorphism

S(F)/S(F)_{r}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}S^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/S^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{r},

where
S^{\prime}
is the transfer of
S
to
G^{\prime}
.
Now fix a maximal split torus
A\subset G
whose apartment contains
x
and let
A^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
be its transfer. For every root
a\in\Phi(G,A)
, the bound
\ell\geq\Phi_{G,x}(r)\geq\phi_{F_{a}/F}(e_{a}r)
implies the hypothesis of Lemma
6.5
; hence we have canonical bijections

U_{a}(F)_{x,0}/U_{a}(F)_{x,r}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}U_{a^{\prime}}(F^{\prime})_{x^{\prime},0}/U_{a^{\prime}}(F^{\prime})_{x^{\prime},r},

with
a^{\prime}=\Phi_{\ell}(a)
.
The group law in a parahoric (hence in the quotients
G(F)_{x,\mathrm{b}}/G(F)_{x,r}
)
is generated by these graded pieces in equations (1) and (2) subject to the integral
Chevalley relations:

[x_{a}(u),x_{b}(v)]\;=\;\prod_{i,j>0}x_{ia+jb}\!\big(C_{i,j}\,u^{\,i}v^{\,j}\big),\qquad t\,x_{a}(u)\,t^{-1}=x_{a}\!\big(a(t)\,u\big),

with structure constants
C_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
(independent of the field) and

\alpha
the corresponding root. The maps (1)–(2) are defined by
transport of parameters through isomorphisms of truncated valuation rings;
hence they send
u\mapsto u^{\prime}
,
v\mapsto v^{\prime}
,
t\mapsto t^{\prime}
and preserve
valuations/levels. Because the coefficients
C_{i,j}
are integral, their
reductions agree on both sides, so the images of the Chevalley polynomials
computed after applying (1)–(2) coincide with the Chevalley polynomials of
the images. In particular, all defining commutator and conjugation relations
are respected in the quotients.

Therefore the family of maps (1)–(2) induces, by the universal property of
presentations, a canonical group isomorphism

\Psi_{x,r}\;:\;G(F)_{x,\mathrm{b}}/G(F)_{x,r}\;\longrightarrow\;G^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{x^{\prime},\mathrm{b}}/G^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{x^{\prime},r},

which is functorial in the congruence datum and independent of auxiliary
choices.

6.6

Let

7
Cartan decomposition for maximal parahorics

In this section, we extend Cartan decomposition from the known case of special maximal parahorics to arbitrary maximal parahorics. We first recall some basics.

7.1
Extended affine Weyl group and the Kottwitz homomorphism

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field with residue field
\kappa
, of cardinality
q
and characteristic
p
. Let
L
be the completion of the maximal unramified extension of

and

For a connected reductive group

over

and a maximally unramified elliptic maximal
F
-torus

:

•

The

, associated to

, is the semi-direct product
Q_{S}^{\vee}\rtimes W(G,S)
, where
Q_{S}^{\vee}
is the coroot lattice of
S
, and

is the Weyl group, with

the normalizer of
S
in
G(F)
.

•

The

\widetilde{W}=N_{G}(S)(L)/S(L)_{0}
, where
S(L)_{0}
is the Iwahori subgroup of
S(L)
.

•

The extended affine Weyl group
\widetilde{W}
admits a
\sigma
-equivariant decomposition:

\widetilde{W}=W_{\text{aff},S}\rtimes\Omega_{S},

where
\Omega_{S}
is the subgroup of length-zero elements in

, which stabilizes a fixed alcove in the apartment of the Bruhat-Tits building of

. The action of

preserves this decomposition, acting on both

and
\Omega_{S}
.

Kottwitz Homomorphism

As before, let
G
be a connected reductive group over a non-archimedean local field
F
, with
L
the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
, and
I=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/L)
the inertia subgroup. The
Kottwitz homomorphism

[
Kot97
, §7]
is a surjective, functorial group homomorphism

w_{G}:G(L)\to X^{*}(Z(\widehat{G})^{I}),

where
\widehat{G}
is the dual group of
G
, and

denotes the
I
-invariants of its center. For a torus

, it simplifies to

w_{T}:T(L)\to X_{*}(T)_{I},

where

is the group of

, defined via the valuation map.

When

, with

, the homomorphism restricts to

, inducing a surjection

Let

denote the kernel of this map. For a vertex

in the Bruhat-Tits building

, let

denote its stabilizer. Then, the intersection

is equal to the parahoric subgroup

at

.

7.2
Cartan decomposition

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
S\subset G
be elliptic. Let
W_{\mathrm{aff},S}
be the affine Weyl group that sits in the semi-direct product
\widetilde{W}=W_{\mathrm{aff},S}\rtimes\Omega_{S}
. Then

W_{\mathrm{aff},S}^{\sigma}=1.

Proof.

Write
Q^{\vee}_{S}\subset X_{*}(S)_{I}
for the coroot lattice. We may realize
W_{\mathrm{aff},S}
as

W_{\mathrm{aff},S}=Q^{\vee}_{S}\rtimes W(G,S),

where an element
t_{\nu}w
acts on the apartment
X_{*}(S)_{I}\otimes\mathbb{R}
by a translation by
\nu\in Q^{\vee}_{S}
followed by the finite Weyl element
w\in W(G,S)
. Let
x=t_{\nu}w\in W_{\mathrm{aff},S}
be
\sigma
-fixed.

Applying
\sigma
to
x
, we obtain

x=t_{\sigma(\nu)}\,\sigma(w)=t_{\nu}w.

Comparing the translation parts gives
\sigma(\nu)=\nu
. Because
S
is elliptic, we have
X_{*}(S)_{I}^{\sigma}
finite. The coroot lattice
Q^{\vee}_{S}
is torsion-free, so the only torsion element it contains is
0
. Hence
\nu=0
.

Thus,
x=w\in W(G,S)
with
w=\sigma(w)
. Fix a
\sigma
-stable positive system of (relative) roots (this exists because over
L
we may choose a
\sigma
-stable pinned datum). If
w\neq 1
, choose a positive root
\alpha
with
w(\alpha)<0
.
Since
\sigma(w)=w
and
\sigma
preserves positivity, for every
i
we have

w\bigl(\sigma^{i}(\alpha)\bigr)=\sigma^{i}\bigl(w(\alpha)\bigr)<0.

Thus, the entire
\sigma
-orbit of
\alpha
is mapped to negative roots. The sum of the coroots in this orbit,

\beta=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\bigl(\sigma^{i}(\alpha)\bigr)^{\vee},

is
\sigma
-fixed, non-zero since it’s a sum of positive co-roots, and lies in
Q_{S}^{\vee}
. This contradicts the ellipticity of
S
, as
\beta
spans a non-trivial
F
-split torus in the derived group, centralized by
S
. Hence,
w=1
.
Combining the two steps, we conclude
x=1
, so
W_{\mathrm{aff},S}^{\sigma}=1
.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Maintain the notations of Lemma

.

Finite presentation of

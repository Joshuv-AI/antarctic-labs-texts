Effects of additional sources on Casimir Wormholes
Remo Garattini

Abstract
In this contribution we explore the consequences of including additional sources to the original Casimir energy Stress-Energy Tensor. In particular, we will discuss the effects of an additional electromagnetic field, the modification induced by non-zero temperature effects on the energy density obtained by a Casimir device and finally the effect obtained by including a massless scalar field. For each of these examples, we have introduced an auxiliary stress tensor which we have interpreted as a thermal tensor. Consequences on the size of the throat are also discussed. We will show that these additional extra fields do not destroy the traversability of the wormhole.



keywords
Traversable Wormholes; Casimir effect.

Introduction

In Quantum Field Theory, the Zero Point Energy (ZPE) represents a
fundamental physical quantity that must be computed to understand the
properties of the physical system under examination. A particular ZPE case
is the Casimir Energy. This is obtained when ons studies quantum
fluctuations between two plane parallel, closely spaced, uncharged, metallic
plates in vacuum. It was predicted theoretically in 1948
[
1
]
and
experimentally confirmed in the Philips laboratories
[
2
,
3
]
.
However, only in recent years further reliable experimental investigations
have confirmed such a phenomenon
[
4
,
5
]
. The interesting
feature of this effect is that an attractive force appears which is
generated by negative energy. Indeed the attractive force arises because the
renormalized energy assumes the following form

E^{\text{Ren}}\left(a\right)=-\frac{\hbar c\pi^{2}S}{720a^{3}},

(1)

where
S
is the surface of the plates and
a
is the separation between
them. The force can be obtained with the computation of

F\left(a\right)=-\frac{dE^{\text{Ren}}\left(a\right)}{da}=-3\frac{\hbar c\pi^{2}S}{720a^{4}}.

(2)

From the force
\left(\ref{F(a)}\right)
is also possible to compute the
pressure

P\left(a\right)=\frac{F\left(a\right)}{S}=-3\frac{\hbar c\pi^{2}}{720a^{4}}.

(3)

It is immediate to recognize that the energy density is nothing but

\rho_{C}\left(a\right)=-\frac{\hbar c\pi^{2}}{720a^{4}}

(4)

suggesting the existence of a relation between the pressure
P
and the
energy density
\rho
described by an Equation of State (EoS) of the form
P=\omega\rho
with
\omega=3
. The nature of this effect is connected
with the ZPE of the Quantum Electrodynamics Vacuum distorted by the plates.
It is important to observe that this effect has a strong dependence on the
geometry of the boundaries. Indeed, Boyer
[
6

proofed the positivity
of the Casimir effect for a conducting spherical shell of radius
r
. The
same positivity has been proofed also in Ref.
[
7

\left(\ref{rhoC},\ref{P(a)}\right)
, we can write down the form of the Stress-Energy Tensor
(SET) which is

T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{\hbar c\pi^{2}}{720a^{4}}diag\left(1,3,-1-1\right),

(5)

where we have also introduced the expression of the transverse pressure

p_{t}\left(a\right)=\frac{\hbar c\pi^{2}}{720a^{4}}.

(6)

The SET
\left(\ref{SET}\right)
is traceless and divergenceless and will
be used as a prototype source for an amazing object predicted by General
Relativity: a Traversable Wormhole (TW). Traversable wormholes (TW) are
solutions of the Einstein’s Field Equations (EFE) powered by classical

G_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\left\langle T_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle^{\text{Ren}}\qquad\kappa=\frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}},

(7)

\left\langle T_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle^{\text{Ren}}

ds^{2}=-e^{2\Phi\!\left(r\right)}\,dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-b(r)/r}+r^{2}\,(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}{\theta}\,d\varphi^{2})\,,

(8)

representing a spherically symmetric and static wormhole. Here,

\Phi\!\left(r\right)
must be finite
everywhere. With the help of the line element
\left(\ref{metric}\right)

we can write the EFE in an orthonormal reference frame, leading to the

\frac{b^{\prime}\left(r\right)}{r^{2}}=\kappa\rho\left(r\right),

(9)

\frac{2}{r}\left(1-\frac{b\left(r\right)}{r}\right)\Phi\!^{\prime}\left(r\right)-\frac{b\left(r\right)}{r^{3}}=\kappa p_{r}\left(r\right),

(10)

\left(1-\frac{b\left(r\right)}{r}\right)\left[\Phi^{\prime\prime}\!\left(r\right)+\Phi\!^{\prime}\left(r\right)\left(\Phi^{\prime}\!\left(r\right)+\frac{1}{r}\right)\right]-\frac{b^{\prime}\left(r\right)r-b\left(r\right)}{2r^{2}}\left(\Phi\!^{\prime}\left(r\right)+\frac{1}{r}\right)=\kappa p_{t}(r),

(11)

in which

is the energy density,
p_{r}\left(r\right)
is the radial pressure, and
p_{t}\left(r\right)

p_{r}^{\prime}\left(r\right)=\frac{2}{r}\left(p_{t}\left(r\right)-p_{r}\left(r\right)\right)-\left(\rho\left(r\right)+p_{r}\left(r\right)\right)\Phi\!^{\prime}\left(r\right).

(12)

Structure of the Stress-Energy Tensor

Consider an anisotropic fluid with radial pressure
p_{r}
, transverse
pressure
p_{t},
energy density
\rho
and a thermal stress tensor
\tau
[
9
]

described by the Stress-Energy Tensor (SET)

T_{\mu\nu}=\left(\rho+\tau_{\rho}\right)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+\left(p_{r}+\tau_{r}\right)n_{\mu}n_{\nu}+\left(p_{t}+\tau_{t}\right)\sigma_{\mu\nu},

(13)

where
u_{\mu}
is the fluid four-velocity and
n_{\mu}
is a unit
spacelike vector orthogonal to
u_{\mu}
, i.e.,
n^{\mu}n_{\mu}=1
,
u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=-1
,
n^{\mu}u_{\mu}=0
. The thermal stress tensor has
been decomposed into an energy component
\tau_{\rho}
, a radial component
\tau_{r}
and a transverse component
\tau_{t}
. Here,

\sigma_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+u_{\mu}u_{\nu}-n_{\mu}n_{\nu}

(14)

is a projection operator onto a two-surface orthogonal to
u_{\mu}
and
n_{\mu}
, i.e.,

u_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mathrm{v}_{\nu}=n_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mathrm{v}_{\nu}=0\qquad\forall\mathrm{v}_{\nu}.

(15)

n_{\mu}
can be represented as

n_{\mu}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-b(r)/r}}\left(0,1,0,0\right).

(16)

With these informations, we can write the components of the SET
\left(\ref{SET}\right)

\displaystyle T_{tt}

\displaystyle=\left(\rho+\tau_{\rho}\right)u_{t}u_{t},

(17)

\displaystyle T_{rr}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{r}+\tau_{r}\right)n_{r}n_{r},

(18)

\displaystyle T_{{\theta\theta}}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{t}+\tau_{t}\right)\sigma_{{\theta\theta}},

(19)

\displaystyle T_{\varphi\varphi}

\displaystyle=\left(p_{t}+\tau_{t}\right)\sigma_{\varphi\varphi}.

(20)

Since we desire to adopt a physical source, we assume that the quadruple
\left(\rho,p_{r},p_{t},p_{t}\right)
be represented by the Casimir SET
\left(\ref{SET}\right)
, while the quadruple
\left(\tau_{\rho},\tau_{r},\tau_{t},\tau_{t}\right)
has to be determined. In principle, it is
also possible to include the effect of an electromagnetic field. In
particular, we are going to consider a spherically symmetric electromagnetic
field, whose components are

E_{r}=E_{1}\left(r\right)=cF_{01}=-cF_{10}.

(21)

All the other components are zero since we will not consider currents or magnetic
monopoles. This means that the electric field can only have a radial
component. Also, this radial component must not depend on
\theta
or
\phi
. With this assumptions, in an orthonormal frame, we can write

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{EM}=\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\left(g_{\nu\alpha}F_{\mu\gamma}F^{\alpha\gamma}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{Q^{2}}{2\left(4\pi\right)^{2}\varepsilon_{0}r^{4}}diag(1,-1,1,1)=diag(\rho_{E},p_{r,E},p_{t,E},p_{t,E}).

(22)

It is interesting to observe that the SET
T_{\mu\nu}^{EM}
satisfies the
following property

\rho_{E}+p_{r,E}=0.

(23)

3
Casimir Wormhole

The original Casimir Wormhole is a solution of the semiclassical EFE
[
14
]
. The SET
\left(\ref{SET}\right)
has been adopted with the following
replacement
a\rightarrow r
. In other words, the distance between the
plates instead of being considered parametrically fixed is considered as a
radial coordinate to be integrated. If we assume that an Equation of State
(EoS)
p_{r}\left(r\right)=\omega\rho_{C}\left(r\right)
is imposed,
then we find the following solution to the semiclassical EFE

\displaystyle\Phi(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left({\omega-1}\right){\ln}\left({\frac{r\omega}{\left(\omega r+r_{0}\right)}}\right)

(24)

\displaystyle b(r)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{\omega}\right)r_{0}+\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{\omega r},

(25)

where

{\omega=}\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}};\qquad r_{1}^{2}=\frac{\pi^{3}l_{p}^{2}}{90}.

(26)

This specific choice avoids the presence of a horizon. In particular, when
{\omega=3}
, one finds

\displaystyle\Phi(r)

\displaystyle={\ln}\left({\frac{3r}{\left(3r+r_{0}\right)}}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle b(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}r_{0}+\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{3r}.

(28)

The EFE are consistent provided one introduces an additional EoS between
p_{t}\left(r\right)
and
\rho_{C}\left(r\right)
. This additional EoS
must be inhomogeneous, namely

p_{t}(r)=\omega_{t}\left(r\right)\rho_{C}\left(r\right){,}

(29)

where we have introduced a inhomogeneous EoS on the transverse pressure with

\omega_{t}\left(r\right)=-\frac{{\omega}^{2}\left(4r-r_{0}\right)+r_{0}\left(4\omega+1\right)}{4\left(\omega r+r_{0}\right)}\underset{\omega=3}{=}-\frac{9\left(4r-r_{0}\right)+r_{0}\left(13\right)}{4\left(3r+r_{0}\right)}\underset{\omega=3}{=}-\frac{9r+r_{0}}{3r+r_{0}}.

(30)

However, instead of an inhomogeneous EoS, we can use the unknown temperature
tensor to find

\tau_{\rho}=0;\qquad\tau_{r}=0;\qquad\tau_{t}\left(r\right)=\frac{2r_{0}^{2}}{\kappa r^{3}\left(3r+r_{0}\right)}.

(31)

In this way, the complete SET is given by

T_{\mu\nu}=\rho u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+p_{r}n_{\mu}n_{\nu}+\left(p_{t}+\tau_{t}\right)\sigma_{\mu\nu}

(32)

which makes the EFE consistent. Note that the thermal stress-energy tensor actually is a thermal stress tensor because
\tau_{\rho}=0

6.2
Variable Plates separation

The energy density
\left(\ref{rho2}\right)

\rho_{H,2}\left(r,T\right)=-\frac{\hbar c}{16\pi r^{3}}\frac{\zeta\left(3\right)}{\lambda_{C}\left(T\right)}.

Here we have introduced the Casimir thermal wavelength

\lambda_{C}\left(T\right)=\frac{\hbar c}{2k_{B}T}.

In the high-temperature approximation, or long-distance approximation, the

\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{C}\left(T\right)}a\gg 1,

\rho_{H,2}\left(r,T\right)=-A\frac{\hbar c}{32\pi^{2}r^{4}}\zeta\left(3\right).

b\left(r\right)=r_{0}-\frac{\hbar G}{4\pi c^{3}}A\zeta\left(3\right)\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{dr^{\prime}}{r^{\prime 2}}=r_{0}+r_{T}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right),

r_{T}^{2}=\frac{A}{4\pi}\zeta\left(3\right)l_{P}^{2}.

(88)

14
]

\left(\ref{pr}\right)

(89)

\omega=\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{r_{T}^{2}}.

(90)

\frac{P\left(r,T\right)}{\rho_{H,2}\left(r,T\right)}=\omega=2

\omega=3

r_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{A}{2\pi}}l_{P}.

14

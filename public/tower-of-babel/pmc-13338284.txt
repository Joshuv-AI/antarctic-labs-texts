Melan-YOLO: a dual-strategy framework with custom architectures for accurate and efficient underwater object detection
Authors: Andaç Fındıkçı, Mustafa Yasin Erten, Hüseyin Aydilek
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEffective autonomous monitoring of marine litter is vital to reduce marine pollution and protect marine ecosystems. However, current underwater detection systems face significant challenges, primarily due to poor image quality caused by light scattering in turbid waters and the limited computational resources of deployable hardware. To address these challenges, we introduce Melan-YOLO, a dual-strategy object detection framework specifically designed for underwater environments. This framework incorporates Melan-YOLO-Pro to achieve maximum detection accuracy, while offering Melan-YOLO-Edge as a streamlined alternative for deployment in resource-constrained conditions. Both architectures employ a tailored image enhancement pipeline optimized for underwater imaging and incorporate an advanced SIoU loss function to improve localization of irregularly shaped marine debris. By leveraging advanced modules such as Asymmetric Convolutions and Coordinated Attention, Melan-YOLO-Pro achieves highly competitive accuracy on the challenging RUOD and SeaClear datasets, recording 84.9% and 82.7% mAP50, respectively. Furthermore, it establishes leading localization precision with mAP50-95 scores of 61.2% on RUOD and 60.2% on SeaClear, outperforming all evaluated state-of-the-art models in this stricter metric. In parallel, Melan-YOLO-Edge significantly reduces computational demands by combining lightweight GhostConv modules with CBAM and ShuffleAttention mechanisms. It requires only 7.7 GFLOPs and 2.59M parameters, while achieving a competitive 83.6% mAP50 on RUOD alongside a 59.1% mAP50-95, and 79.7% mAP50 on SeaClear with 57.2% mAP50-95. The results demonstrate that Melan-YOLO offers a favorable balance in the accuracy-efficiency trade-off inherent in underwater object detection and provides both a high-performance and computationally efficient solution suitable for real-time applications on Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs). This framework represents a step toward practical, large-scale marine conservation and pollution monitoring, combining accuracy with computational efficiency to address real-world deployment constraints effectively.Keywords: Underwater object detection, YOLO, Deep learning, Marine debris, Computational efficiencySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Ocean sciences

Introduction

The oceans of the earth are silently falling victim to a pandemic of marine pollution, an epidemic driven largely by the accumulation of plastics, derelict fishing gear, and other persistent debris1. This crisis endangers marine ecosystems, undermines biodiversity, and destabilizes global economies2. Reducing this threat ultimately rests upon our capacity to observe and measure debris across extensive and often inaccessible marine geographies. In this context, AUVs are emerging as a critical, scalable solution for environmental monitoring and oceanography3. The operational success of these robotic platforms, however, rests entirely upon the computer vision systems providing them with sight. This perception is severely compromised by the hostile optical properties of the water column, where phenomena like light absorption and back-scattering fundamentally degrade image quality4,5. This results in low contrast, obscured fine details, and a dominant blue-green color cast that shrouds objects in a veil of haze.

To address this challenge, recent research has proposed numerous advanced techniques. For instance, some works focus on novel attention mechanisms and feature extraction modules to better handle challenging targets. Feng & Jin6 developed CEH-YOLO, which integrates a High-order Deformable Attention (HDA) module, while Li et al.7 introduced a dual-branch occlusion attention mechanism and dilated deformable convolutions to improve the detection of small and occluded objects. Similarly, Shen et al.8 proposed a Multiple Information Perception-based Attention Module (MIPAM) specifically designed to reduce background interference. Other studies have prioritized computational efficiency for deployment on resource-constrained devices. Zhang et al.9 combined a CSPCBAM structure with Ghost modules, and Gao et al.10 used GhostNetV2 and model pruning to create lightweight yet accurate models. A different approach was taken by Cao et al.11, who proposed a unique bidirectional-guided method with parallel image enhancement and detection branches to ensure the enhancement process is optimized for the detection task itself.

These deep learning advances have progressed on two parallel but often disconnected fronts. The first is pre-processing to enhance image quality, which includes classical restoration methods based on fusion12 and modern approaches based on deep learning13. The second front is architectural optimizations for detection models, where the YOLO family of algorithms14 has become the standard in practice. However, subsequent research has often created a dichotomy between maximizing accuracy with complex modules15 or maximizing efficiency with lightweight convolutions16. Such fractured approaches rarely address the tradeoff between accuracy and efficiency as a single, unified problem, creating a bottleneck for real world applications.

In this research, this critical gap is bridged by introducing Melan-YOLO, a holistic framework that systematically dismantles the challenges of underwater object detection. Instead of treating accuracy and efficiency as mutually exclusive goals, a unified, dual-model strategy is presented to meet diverse operational needs. The core contributions of our work are structured as follows:

A High-Performance Model for Maximum Accuracy (Melan-YOLO-Pro): A high-performance detector has been developed that establishes a strong performance benchmark. By incorporating advanced modules such as Asymmetric Convolutions (AConv)17 and RepNCSPELAN4 for powerful feature representation, Melan-YOLO-Pro is designed to achieve state-of-the-art localization accuracy in the most challenging visual conditions.

Materials and methods

To address the dual challenge of accuracy in turbid waters and efficiency on limited hardware, a problem-oriented design strategy was adopted. Rather than relying on generic module assembly, specific architectural components were strategically selected to mitigate distinct underwater degradation effects, such as color absorption and feature redundancy. The following subsections detail the rationale behind each component’s integration into the Melan-YOLO framework.

Datasets

To rigorously evaluate the accuracy and robustness of the proposed models, two distinct and challenging public datasets were utilized: the SeaClear Marine Debris Dataset22,23 and the Real-world Underwater Object Detection (RUOD) dataset24.

The SeaClear dataset is a specialized collection of 8,610 images for detecting and segmenting underwater trash. The data was gathered by ROVs in various locations across Croatia and France. The dataset is annotated for both object detection and instance segmentation, featuring 40 object categories that encompass not only litter but also observed animals, plants, and robot parts. The major challenges presented by SeaClear include severe object occlusion, with trash often half-buried in sediments; low contrast between objects and the background, creating difficult segmentation; and high variability in the type and degradation status of the trash. In general, such conditions render SeaClear a highly demanding benchmark for creating and testing robust litter detection models appropriate for deployment in the field22.

The second dataset, RUOD, is a common benchmark for general underwater object detection with a special focus on marine creatures, whose precise detection is crucial for ecosystem observation. It contains 5,543 images of marine life. The images were captured under different real-world conditions that are very difficult for detection algorithms to deal with. They feature the presence of very small targets, complex terrains like rocky reefs and seagrass beds that occlude objects, very high aggregation density of organisms resulting in extreme occlusion, and non-uniform illumination due to varying water depths and turbidity. The environmental complexity and variability in the RUOD dataset thus make it an ideal testbed for evaluating the robustness and generalization capabilities of detection models under real-world underwater conditions24. Image enhancement

The challenging nature of underwater environments, characterized by light attenuation and scattering, induces severe quality degradation, including low contrast, color casting, and haziness. To counteract these issues and ensure high-fidelity input for the detection framework, a robust pre-processing step for image enhancement is implemented. The adopted strategy builds upon the fusion-based methodology proposed by Martinho et al.12. This technique is selected for its proven effectiveness in restoring visual quality through a multi-stage pipeline of intensity transformations. To optimize performance specifically for the SeaClear22 and RUOD24 datasets, the baseline approach is refined by fine-tuning its core parameters, particularly the weighting factor in the fusion stage and the gamma correction values. The final optimized parameters are as follows: the color correction saturation intensity was set to 1.0, the gamma correction factor () to 0.8, and the Unsharp Mask filter was configured with a Gaussian kernel (, sharpening intensity = 1.0). The CLAHE module used a clip limit of 4 with a tile grid size of . In the fusion stage, the two branches were blended with weights of 0.6 and 0.3, respectively. Finally, post-fusion brightness and contrast offsets of +5 and +10 were applied.

The adapted enhancement pipeline begins with an initial color correction based on histogram stretching applied to each RGB channel, aiming to balance the color distribution. Following this, the process splits into a two-layer parallel architecture. The first layer applies gamma correction and an Unsharp Mask filter to enhance edges. The second layer performs the same operations but adds a Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE)25 step to further improve local contrast. The outputs from these two layers are then fused using a weighted linear blending technique. The final step involves a brightness and contrast adjustment to produce the final, enhanced image ready for the detection stage. The YOLOv8 architecture

The foundation for the efficiency-driven Melan-YOLO-Edge model is the You Only Look Once version 8 (YOLOv8) architecture, a cutting-edge, single-stage object detector designed by Ultralytics. YOLOv8 is renowned for its equilibrium between high performance and real-time processing speed, establishing it as a prominent candidate for a variety of computer vision applications26. The architecture is distinguished from its previous versions by its incorporation of an anchor-free detection mechanism and a decoupled head, which all improve its performance and streamline its predictive process. The modularity inherent in YOLOv8, which is cleanly distributed in three major components-Backbone, Neck, and Head -provides a perfect and adaptable base for designing specialized, lightweight models. Consequently, the YOLOv8n variant is adopted as the baseline for Melan-YOLO-Edge due to its lean architecture and high efficiency, aligning strictly with the operational constraints of AUVs.

The architectural elements of YOLOv8 collaborate synergistically to process images and identify objects. The Backbone, which is designed based on a modified Cross Stage Partial (CSP) architecture27, serves to extract major features from images. The Backbone utilizes the C2f module, an extension of the C3 module used in YOLOv5, to allow for more abundant gradient flow and extract more superior and complex features from the image. A Spatial Pyramid Pooling Fast (SPPF) module is utilized toward the end of the backbone to integrate characteristics from various scales, making it easier for the model to identify varying sizes of objects without inducing a large computational burden. The backbone’s extracted features are subsequently passed through to the Neck, which combines a Feature Pyramid Network together with a Path Aggregation Network. This architecture effectively integrates feature maps from various layers to allow for both deep, semantic information and shallow, high-resolution detail for the model to access. The feature maps from fusion are eventually passed through to the Head, which is geared toward making final predictions. The decoupling between elements in the YOLOv8 head leads to its use of isolated branches for addressing classification tasks and regression tasks. This decoupled nature has proven to boost final detection performance28.

The YOLOv9 architecture

The architectural blueprint for the high-performance Melan-YOLO-Pro model is derived from YOLOv9, a significant evolution in the YOLO series that introduces novel solutions to fundamental deep learning challenges29 and is already being adapted for complex tasks such as small object detection30. YOLOv9 mitigates information bottlenecks through two pivotal innovations: Programmable Gradient Information (PGI) and the Generalized Efficient Layer Aggregation Network (GELAN).

The combination between PGI’s robust data retention capabilities and the streamlined structure of GELAN establishes YOLOv9 as a new benchmark in detection performance. A key structural element within GELAN is the RepNCSPELAN4 module, which serves as the primary feature aggregation block. This module integrates re-parameterizable convolutions, employing a multi-branch training topology that is fused into a single efficient convolution during inference, with Cross-Stage Partial (CSP) connections and Efficient Layer Aggregation. The synergy of these mechanisms prevents the degradation of deep semantic representations by maintaining rich gradient flow paths even at increased network depth, ensuring that high-level features encoding complex object characteristics are preserved rather than progressively diluted through deeper layers. This property is particularly critical for underwater environments, where the visual similarity between degraded debris and the surrounding seabed demands strong discriminative representations throughout the entire network depth. Furthermore, Asymmetric Convolution (AConv)17 plays a complementary role by decomposing standard square kernels into combinations of horizontal and vertical asymmetric kernels. This decomposition strengthens the skeleton structure of the convolutional kernel, enabling more effective extraction of elongated and directional features—a property particularly beneficial for detecting debris with irregular textures such as ropes, nets, and fragmented plastics commonly found on the seabed. Given its capacity to extract robust and discriminative features, this architecture is selected as the optimal foundation for Melan-YOLO-Pro, targeting superior accuracy in the most challenging underwater environments.

The GhostConv Module

Underwater video frames are frequently characterized by high spatial redundancy, largely attributed to homogeneous background textures. Standard convolutional operations inevitably incur substantial computational overhead by repeatedly processing this redundant information. To mitigate this inefficiency and optimize the Melan-YOLO-Edge model for resource-constrained environments, the GhostConv module18 is integrated. By generating intrinsic feature maps and deriving supplementary features through linear operations, this module reduces the floating-point operations (FLOPs) while maintaining sufficient channel capacity for detection.

The basic premise of GhostConv is that many of the feature maps produced by standard convolutions are redundant. Some of these are such near copies of others that they are effectively ghosts of each other. Instead of computing all of the feature maps with expensive convolutions, GhostConv takes a two-stage route. In the first stage, a standard convolution is employed to compute a lower, preliminary set of“intrinsic”feature maps. Second, it appends a series of computationally cheap linear transformations to each of these intrinsic maps in an effort to generate an expanded set of“ghost” feature maps. Finally, the intrinsic and ghost feature maps are concatenated to generate the end output31. A critical consideration is whether this approximation degrades the discriminative capacity of the learned representations. In practice, the intrinsic feature maps capture the primary semantic information, while the cheap linear transformations preserve sufficient diversity to maintain detection performance. This balance is particularly well-suited for underwater environments, where the high spatial redundancy of homogeneous seabed backgrounds means that a significant portion of standard feature maps carry duplicated information that can be efficiently approximated without loss of discriminative power. This approach allows the model to have a similar feature representation as a standard convolution but at much reduced computational cost, as can be observed in the module architecture in Fig. 4.

The CBAM and iCBAM attention mechanisms

Suspended particles in underwater environments introduce substantial visual noise, which can severely compromise feature extraction capabilities. To mitigate this perturbation, attention mechanisms are integrated to function as adaptive feature filters. The proposed framework employs variants of the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)19, a compact yet effective module that successively refines features on channel and spatial dimensions.

The original CBAM module, implemented in our Melan-YOLO-Edge, operates under a two-stage mechanism. Specifically, this module facilitates the distinction of relevant objects from the background by recalibrating features along both channel and spatial axes. The process initiates with the Channel Attention Module, which identifies and emphasizes salient channels. It aggregates the spatial information of a feature map by executing average-pooling and max-pooling operations and feeds the outcomes into a shared multi-layer perceptron to produce a channel attention vector. This vector re-weights the input feature map by assigning more weightage to useful channels. Secondly, the channel-refined feature map is input to the Spatial Attention Module. The module specifically aims at detecting the most informative spatial locations. It employs pooling operations along the channel direction to generate a 2D spatial attention map, which is utilized in selectively emphasizing the most informative parts32.

For Melan-YOLO-Pro, the iCBAM33 module is employed to capture more subtle inter-channel dependencies, which is critical for distinguishing marine debris from complex seabeds. This module combines the underlying theory of CBAM with optimizations to its structure. These optimizations are designed to find more complex channel-spatial relationships, further enhancing the discriminative capacity of the model and enabling the model to handle the increased accuracy required for challenging underwater detection tasks34. ShuffleAttention module

To further reinforce the lightweight architectural design of Melan-YOLO-Edge, the ShuffleAttention (SA) module20 is integrated as an extremely lightweight attention mechanism designed to operate with hardly any computational cost. Unlike the CBAM’s sequential channel-then-spatial approach, SA processes both channel and spatial attention in parallel on feature subgroups, providing a contrasting but effective way of recalibrating feature maps.

The SA module architecture begins by dividing the input feature channels into a number of subgroups. For each subgroup, the features are also split into two branches in parallel. The first branch consumes channel statistics to generate a channel attention map with an emphasis on the“what”of the features in the input group. While the second branch generates a spatial attention map by modeling spatial relationships with an emphasis on the“where”. The output of these two branches is then fused to produce a refined sub-feature map that has been enriched with both spatial and channel context. A crucial final step is the“channel shuffle”operation. This operation simply rearranges the information across the different subgroups, enabling cross-group communication and creating a more integrated and effective final feature representation35. This entire process, as shown in Fig. 6, allows the model to learn discriminative features at a very low computational budget. The subgroup-based parallel processing is particularly advantageous for underwater imagery, where localized noise patterns from suspended particles require fine-grained, spatially-aware feature recalibration that a purely global attention approach may not efficiently capture.

Coordinate attention module

Standard global pooling operations typically compress spatial dimensions into channel descriptors, inadvertently causing a loss of positional information that is critical for precise localization. To counteract this limitation and enhance bounding box regression accuracy within turbid conditions, the Coordinate Attention (CoordAttention) module36 is integrated into the Melan-YOLO-Pro architecture. This module decomposes channel attention into 1D feature encoding processes, preserving positional information along spatial directions and improving the model’s ability to localize irregularly shaped objects. Unlike channel attention approaches using global pooling to learn spatial context, which actually reduces a feature map to a single descriptor and discards positional information, CoordAttention can learn inter-channel and direction-aware and position-sensitive information. This allows the model to locate regions of interest more precisely, which is a significant advantage for noisy underwater environments. The CoordAttention module operates in a two-stage manner. The first stage, Coordinate Information Embedding, avoids the information loss associated with 2D global pooling. It decomposes channel attention into 1D horizontal and 1D vertical average pooling individually. This produces two unique, direction-conscious feature maps that retain long-range relations along each spatial dimension, effectively infusing accurate positional information into the attention maps. In stage two, Coordinate Attention Generation, these two feature maps are concatenated and passed through a shared convolutional transformation. The resulting intermediate feature map is then split into two tensors again, which are transformed into attention maps that weigh the features independently on the horizontal and vertical directions37. With these two attention maps applied to the input feature map, the model is able to attend to the prominent features in a direction- and position-aware manner simultaneously, as illustrated in Fig. 7. This explicit positional encoding is critical for underwater scenes where objects such as partially buried pipes or camouflaged bottles blend with the seabed along specific spatial axes, requiring the detector to attend to exact coordinates rather than merely identifying the presence of salient features.

The CBELAN module

A pivotal component of the high-performance Melan-YOLO-Pro framework is the CB_ELAN module38. This hybrid architecture integrates the structural efficiency of an ELAN39 with the feature-refining capabilities of a CBAM19 in a synergistic manner. The main objective of CB_ELAN is to construct a robust and discriminative feature representation, which is critical for maximizing detection accuracy.

The ELAN structure is designed to enhance the network’s learning capability by optimizing gradient flow. It achieves this by regulating the shortest and longest gradient paths, enabling effective convergence even in deep networks. By concatenating features from a stack of convolutional layers, ELAN produces a rich, multi-level feature map. Within the proposed CB_ELAN block, a CBAM attention layer is applied directly to the output of this ELAN framework. Through its sequential channel and spatial attention mechanisms, the module recalibrates the aggregated features, thereby emphasizing the most informative channels and spatial positions. This integration of dense feature aggregation and adaptive feature refinement results in a module that significantly boosts model performance, serving as the cornerstone of the Melan-YOLO-Pro design as illustrated in Fig. 8. The functional rationale for this combination addresses two distinct challenges: the ELAN component captures the diverse textures of underwater waste materials, from translucent plastics to corroded metals, through dense multi-level aggregation, while the CBAM layer acts as a selective filter that suppresses irrelevant features from cluttered seabed backgrounds, ensuring that only discriminative representations propagate through the network.

The SPPCSPD module

Marine debris varies significantly in scale, from small fragments to large nets. A fixed receptive field is often insufficient for such variance. Therefore, the Melan-YOLO-Pro architecture utilizes the SPPCSPD module. Its multi-scale pooling structure expands the receptive field, enabling the network to effectively aggregate context for objects of widely differing sizes. This module, drawing inspiration from foundational architectures like YOLOv414 and adapted in various modern applications40, replaces the lighter weight SPPF module in the baseline architecture and is employed to produce a more comprehensive and multi-scale feature representation. Its primary function is to expand the receptive field of the network so that the model can efficiently combine contextual information of different scales, which is extremely vital for the precise detection of objects with diverse sizes and appearances in complex scenes.

The effectiveness of the SPPCSPD module stems from its hybrid structure, which combines a Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) layer41 within a CSP framework27. The CSP architecture first splits the input feature map into two paths. One path bypasses the main processing block, preserving gradient information and reducing computational redundancy. The other path is fed through a dense processing block ending in the SPP layer, which applies several max-pooling operations in parallel with different kernel sizes such as 5x5, 9x9, and 13x13. This allows the model to learn and extract spatial features at different resolutions simultaneously42. Finally, the output of the bypass path and the SPP-processed path are concatenated, resulting in an aggregated feature map that is dense and contains a very strong multi-scale context representation, as shown in Fig. 9. This design is particularly well-suited for underwater debris detection, where objects range from small plastic fragments occupying only a few pixels to large discarded fishing nets spanning a significant portion of the image. The parallel multi-scale pooling kernels explicitly capture contextual information at different spatial resolutions, while the CSP bypass path preserves the fine-grained gradient information necessary for accurate localization of smaller targets.

Loss function for bounding box regression

The accuracy of detection of an object detector mostly depends on the effectiveness of its bounding box regression loss function. Though existing methods like Complete-IoU (CIoU)43 count for overlap area, center point distance, and aspect ratio, they notably overlook the direction vector between the predicted and ground-truth boxes. This omission often leads to slow and inefficient convergence, as the model will tend to oscillate during training with no specific direction to minimize the error. To mitigate this issue and accelerate the training process, the default loss function is replaced with SIoU21.

SIoU introduces a novel penalty metric using the direction of the vector between predicted and ground-truth boxes. This not only causes the regression process to decrease the distance but also nudges the prediction in the direction of the target on the nearest axis, resulting in very fast convergence. Proposed Melan-YOLO architectures

The Melan-YOLO framework is established as a dual-stream architecture, strategically designed to resolve the dichotomy between computational efficiency and detection precision. The Melan-YOLO-Edge variant explicitly adopts the YOLOv8n baseline to satisfy the strict low-latency and low-FLOPs constraints inherent to embedded Autonomous Underwater Vehicle hardware. In contrast, the Melan-YOLO-Pro variant integrates the YOLOv9t architecture as its foundation, using its PGI and GELAN mechanisms. These components were specifically selected to mitigate the deep-layer feature loss often observed in complex underwater scenes, thereby prioritizing high-fidelity feature extraction. The selection of YOLOv8n and YOLOv9t as baselines was deliberate. While newer variants such as YOLOv10, YOLOv11, and YOLOv12 exist, they introduce fixed design constraints (such as NMS-free heads or rigid attention topologies) that limit custom module integration. YOLOv8n provides a clean, modular, anchor-free pipeline ideal for systematic component replacement, while YOLOv9t offers PGI and GELAN mechanisms that directly address deep-layer information loss in turbid underwater environments.

The decision to maintain two independent detection pipelines rather than a single scalable architecture is grounded in the fundamentally conflicting design requirements of the target deployment scenarios. High-accuracy scientific analysis demands computationally intensive modules such as multi-scale pooling (SPPCSPD) and position-aware attention (CoordAttention, iCBAM), which are incompatible with the strict latency budgets of embedded AUV systems. Conversely, the aggressive model compression techniques required for edge deployment, such as GhostConv-based feature approximation, inevitably sacrifice the representational capacity essential for resolving camouflaged targets. A unified architecture attempting to serve both profiles would require runtime conditional branching or dynamic depth scaling, introducing engineering complexity and inference overhead that negate the efficiency gains. By separating these objectives into dedicated architectures, each model can be optimized without compromise: the Pro variant maximizes feature discriminability for offline scientific analysis, while the Edge variant minimizes latency for real-time AUV navigation. Within the Pro variant, the RepNCSPELAN4 structure (described in detail in the YOLOv9 Architecture section) serves as the primary feature aggregation backbone, upon which the subsequent CB_ELAN, CoordAttention, and iCBAM modules further refine the extracted features.

The design of the Melan-YOLO-Edge architecture is strictly dictated by the operational constraints of embedded AUV systems, where computational efficiency is paramount. To minimize the spatial redundancy often present in underwater video frames, standard convolutional layers are replaced with lightweight GhostConv modules, significantly reducing the parameter count. This reduction in computational load is carefully balanced by the integration of resource-efficient attention mechanisms, specifically CBAM and ShuffleAttention, which effectively filter background particulate noise without incurring significant latency. The joint use of these two mechanisms is a deliberate design choice: CBAM applies a global, sequential channel-then-spatial refinement across the entire feature map, providing holistic suppression of underwater background interference, while ShuffleAttention operates locally within feature subgroups in parallel, capturing fine-grained intra-group dependencies. The channel shuffle operation then enables cross-group information exchange, ensuring that the two mechanisms complement rather than duplicate each other’s contributions. Furthermore, the geometric precision of detections is refined through the implementation of the SIoU loss function, ensuring accurate localization even within a compact model structure.

Conversely, the Melan-YOLO-Pro architecture is engineered to prioritize feature discriminability, specifically addressing the complex camouflage and occlusion typical of marine debris. Central to this design is the utilization of CB_ELAN blocks, which enhance the network’s ability to represent complex textures found in diverse waste materials. To mitigate the loss of spatial information in turbid waters, Coordinate Attention and iCBAM modules are incorporated; these components preserve precise positional data, which is critical for accurate bounding box regression. Additionally, the network’s receptive field is expanded through the deployment of the SPPCSPD module, ensuring robust detection across a wide range of object scales, from small plastic fragments to larger discarded nets.

Results and discussion

Parameter settings

All experimental evaluations were conducted on a workstation equipped with a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 GPU. The models were trained using the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) optimizer with an initial learning rate () of 0.01. To ensure training stability and optimal resource utilization, a batch size of 16 and an input resolution of were selected for a total of 200 epochs. The detailed experimental configuration is presented in Table 1. All models, including both the proposed variants and the baselines, were trained from scratch using random weight initialization without leveraging pre-trained weights from external datasets such as MS COCO. Performance analysis

The experimental results empirically validate the efficacy of the proposed Melan-YOLO framework. Across two distinct underwater benchmarks-the SeaClear Marine Debris and RUOD datasets-both custom architectures, Melan-YOLO-Pro and Melan-YOLO-Edge, demonstrate strong performance relative to a broad spectrum of contemporary YOLO variants used as baselines. The performance-oriented Melan-YOLO-Pro establishes a new benchmark in localization precision, recording leading mAP50-95 scores of 61.2% on RUOD and 60.2% on SeaClear. In terms of mAP50, the model achieves highly competitive scores of 84.9% on RUOD and 82.7% on SeaClear among the evaluated baseline architectures. In parallel, the efficiency-driven Melan-YOLO-Edge delivers an optimal trade-off between performance and computational expenditure, achieving an mAP50 of 83.6% on RUOD and 79.7% on SeaClear, while maintaining competitive localization accuracy with mAP50-95 scores of 59.1% and 57.2%, respectively. These findings corroborate that the dual-model strategy effectively resolves the inherent accuracy-efficiency trade-off in underwater object detection. The following subsections provide a detailed analysis of each proposed model and the factors contributing to these outcomes.

Maximizing detection accuracy with Melan-YOLO-Pro

As evidenced by the experimental outcomes, the performance-driven Melan-YOLO-Pro architecture consistently achieves strong performance across both datasets among the evaluated YOLO baselines. Beyond achieving competitive mAP50 scores of 84.9% on RUOD and 82.7% on SeaClear, the model demonstrates precise localization capability, evidenced by its leading mAP50-95 scores of 61.2% and 60.2%, respectively. Furthermore, on the RUOD dataset, Melan-YOLO-Pro achieved the highest Precision score of 0.8599 among all compared models. This metric is particularly critical in marine environments to minimize false positives, ensuring that marine life is not mistakenly classified as debris. This increased accuracy is a direct result of its sophisticated architecture, which prioritizes feature representation capability over computational cost. The design philosophy in Melan-YOLO-Pro involves the integration of several powerful modules to counter the specific challenges of underwater imaging. The incorporation of the SPPCSPD module provides a rich multi-scale context, which is essential for detecting debris of varying sizes and shapes. Moreover, the use of advanced attention mechanisms like CoordAttention and iCBAM enables the model to specifically locate and focus on regions of interest, even in cluttered and low-visibility scenes. These components, along with hybrid feature fusion blocks like CBELAN, create a highly expressive network capable of learning the subtle features needed to distinguish marine litter from its natural surroundings. Achieving peak efficiency with Melan-YOLO-Edge

Component-wise ablation analysis

To provide a more granular understanding of the individual contribution of each architectural module, a component-wise ablation study was conducted on the SeaClear dataset using the enhanced images. It should be noted that this analysis is limited to a single dataset; the generalizability of these component-level findings across different underwater domains is acknowledged as a limitation and is discussed in the Limitations section. Starting from the respective baselines (YOLOv8n for Edge and YOLOv9t for Pro), modules were incrementally added to observe their isolated impact on detection and localization performance. The results are presented in Tables 7 and 8.

The results reveal distinct contribution patterns for each model. For Melan-YOLO-Edge, the incremental integration follows a deliberate architectural logic. GhostConv reduces the parameter count from 3.02M to 2.59M while CBAM compensates for this compression by recalibrating channel and spatial features, yielding a modest improvement in mAP50 over the baseline. The subsequent addition of ShuffleAttention introduces parallel subgroup-level attention that complements CBAM’s global refinement, advancing mAP50 to 79.47%. However, the most critical observation emerges in the final configuration: the replacement of the default CIoU loss with SIoU produces the single largest localization improvement across the entire Edge ablation, elevating mAP50-95 to 57.15%. This result confirms that for a lightweight architecture where feature capacity is inherently constrained, the loss function plays a disproportionately important role in bounding box regression accuracy, as the model relies more heavily on the optimization signal to compensate for its limited representational depth.

For Melan-YOLO-Pro, each module addresses a distinct and identifiable bottleneck in the detection pipeline. The CB_ELAN module delivers the largest single-step detection improvement, raising mAP50 from 77.04% to 79.28%, which demonstrates that dense multi-level feature aggregation combined with attention-based filtering is the most impactful factor for overall detection capability. The subsequent addition of SPPCSPD shifts the primary gain toward the localization metric, with mAP50-95 rising substantially to 58.00%. This pattern is consistent with the module’s design purpose, as expanding the receptive field improves bounding box precision across diverse object scales without necessarily detecting additional objects. The third step, integrating CoordAttention and iCBAM together, produces the most balanced improvement across both detection and localization metrics, confirming that position-aware attention is essential for both identifying and precisely delineating camouflaged targets in cluttered underwater scenes. Finally, the addition of SIoU further improves the mAP50 from 81.69% to 82.69%, while producing a marginal decrease of 0.10 percentage points in mAP50-95 (from 60.26% to 60.16%), indicating that the preceding attention modules had already optimized the localization capacity to near the architecture’s structural ceiling, and that SIoU’s primary contribution at this stage shifts toward refining classification confidence rather than bounding box regression. This contrasts sharply with the Edge model, where SIoU served as the dominant localization driver. The difference is attributable to the Pro model’s substantially richer feature representations, which reduce dependency on loss-level optimization.

Critical analysis and limitations of the enhancement process

While the experimental results demonstrate the overall effectiveness of the proposed framework, a closer examination reveals certain limitations and trade-offs regarding the integrated image enhancement pipeline.

Firstly, the impact of the enhancement process is not uniformly efficient across all scenarios. Specifically, on the RUOD dataset, which contains relatively clearer images compared to SeaClear, the performance gain for the Melan-YOLO-Edge model was marginal. The mAP50 metric exhibited only a slight increase, rising from 83.29% under the raw configuration to 83.56% following enhancement, which corresponds to an absolute improvement of approximately 0.27 percentage points. This suggests that for environments with moderate visibility, the enhancement process may introduce unnecessary computational overhead without providing a statistically significant performance benefit. In such cases, bypassing the pre-processing stage could be a more efficient strategy for real-time AUV operations.

Secondly, the results on the SeaClear dataset reveal a diminishing performance gap attributable to the image enhancement process. In the absence of pre-processing, the specialized Melan-YOLO-Edge architecture maintains a substantial lead, recording a 79.45% mAP compared to the 62.22% achieved by the standard YOLOv9t. However, following image restoration, this competitive advantage is largely eroded as the scores converge at 79.72% and 77.04%, respectively. This shift suggests that architectural innovations, particularly the integration of GhostConv-based modules, become less critical when input quality is artificially improved. Consequently, the primary merit of our custom architecture lies in its inherent robustness on degraded inputs, offering a vital advantage in real-world scenarios where pre-processing may be computationally infeasible.

Finally, a closer inspection of the RUOD results in Table 3 reveals a notable trade-off within the Melan-YOLO-Pro model’s predictions: while the model achieves the highest Precision among all compared models (0.8599), its Recall (0.7740) is comparatively lower. This discrepancy suggests that the enhancement pipeline, in its effort to suppress background noise (de-hazing), may over-smooth fine details, leading to the suppression of valid but faint objects. This phenomenon represents a limitation that must be carefully managed in safety-critical underwater detection tasks where missing an object, FN, carries a higher risk than a false alarm, FP.

Conclusion

This study advances the literature through the introduction of the Melan-YOLO framework, an innovative dual-strategy approach engineered to resolutely address the critical accuracy-efficiency trade-off in underwater object detection. Distinct from singular, general-purpose architectures, the proposed framework offers two tailored models optimized for divergent operational profiles: the high-performance Melan-YOLO-Pro and the lightweight, efficiency-optimized Melan-YOLO-Edge. This bifurcated methodology distinguishes itself by providing a simultaneous and viable solution for both high-precision scientific analysis and large-scale field operations utilizing AUVs constrained by limited hardware resources.

Experimental evaluations on the challenging SeaClear and RUOD benchmarks empirically validate the proposed methodology. Melan-YOLO-Pro achieves leading localization performance and highly competitive detection accuracy, recording an mAP50-95 of 61.2% and an mAP50 of 84.9% on the RUOD dataset. Concurrently, Melan-YOLO-Edge delivers an optimal equilibrium between accuracy and efficiency, achieving 83.6% mAP50 and 59.1% mAP50-95, while outperforming widely utilized baselines like YOLOv8n with significantly fewer parameters and reduced computational cost. A pivotal insight emerging from this study is that the integration of a dedicated image enhancement pipeline is integral to maximizing detection accuracy in aquatic environments. This finding implies that analogous pre-processing strategies could yield substantial benefits in other domains characterized by signal degradation, such as low-contrast medical imaging or aerial photography subject to atmospheric distortion. Furthermore, the strategic allocation of computationally intensive modules to the Pro model, versus lightweight modules to the Edge model, underscores the efficacy of task-specific architectural design.

Beyond providing a robust automation system for marine debris detection, this work lays a solid foundation for future research. The minimal hardware footprint of the Melan-YOLO-Edge model demonstrates the feasibility of widespread deployment on edge devices. Future research avenues include evaluating the real-time applicability of the framework on embedded AUV platforms and handheld devices for underwater exploration, conducting dedicated on-device benchmarking with model optimization techniques such as TensorRT acceleration and INT8 quantization, and extending the component-wise ablation analysis to additional datasets to further validate the generalizability of the proposed architectural modules. Ultimately, the Melan-YOLO framework stands as a comprehensive system that leverages automation for marine conservation, offering both high-fidelity inspection capabilities and scalable solutions for real-world field applications.

Limitations

Although the Melan-YOLO framework proposed in this study has achieved successful results in underwater object detection, it possesses some significant limitations that must be acknowledged.

Firstly, the models were trained using the RUOD and SeaClear Marine Debris datasets, which are publicly available but limited in scope. Factors that directly affect the model’s performance include the limited pixel resolution, a specific color distribution, and the similar camera angles that characterize these datasets. Consequently, this may limit the models’ generalization ability on more diverse, real-world data featuring different underwater conditions, lighting, or turbidity levels not represented in these datasets.

Secondly, the ablation study was conducted on a single dataset, which may limit the generalizability of the component-level findings. While a component-wise ablation analysis has been conducted on the SeaClear dataset to quantify the individual contribution of each architectural module (Tables 7 and 8), the relative contribution of individual modules may vary across datasets with different characteristics, such as the marine-organism-dominated RUOD dataset versus the debris-focused SeaClear dataset. Extending this evaluation to the RUOD dataset and additional benchmarks would provide a more comprehensive understanding of each module’s generalization capability and robustness, and is planned as part of future work.

Thirdly, one of the most significant shortcomings is that the Melan-YOLO-Edge model was not tested in real-time, practical scenarios aboard an Autonomous Underwater Vehicle. Although its efficiency is demonstrated through metrics like GFLOPs and parameter count, its performance in actual field conditions has not yet been verified. Furthermore, real-time performance on embedded AUV hardware has not yet been empirically validated and remains part of future work.

Fourthly, while Melan-YOLO-Pro establishes leading performance in the mAP50-95 metric, it does not surpass all state-of-the-art models in mAP50 on the RUOD dataset. Specifically, AOD-YOLO49 achieves a higher mAP50 of 88.1%, albeit at nearly twice the computational cost (21.4 GFLOPs versus our 12.9 GFLOPs). This indicates that additional improvements in mAP50 performance remain achievable, potentially through more sophisticated feature aggregation strategies or hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures. However, the leading mAP50-95 performance of our framework demonstrates superior bounding box regression precision, which is arguably more critical for downstream tasks such as autonomous manipulation and quantitative debris assessment.

In this regard, we recommend that researchers undertaking this work from scratch consider several important points. Researchers should not overlook the direct impact of dataset diversity on generalization. As a foresight to overcome these limits, combining data collected from different geographical locations and with various equipment, or using synthetic data generation techniques like Generative Adversarial Networks, could be considered to significantly increase the model’s generalization ability. Furthermore, prioritizing on-device testing at an early stage is of critical importance to close the gap between theoretical efficiency metrics and real-world performance.

Ratios of consecutive values of the divisor function Thanks: SE is supported by the Royal Society.
Sean Eberhard

Abstract
. We show that the sequence of ratios d ⁡ ( n + 1 ) / d ⁡ ( n ) d(n+1)/d(n) of consecutive values of the divisor function attains every positive rational infinitely many times. This confirms a prediction of Erdős.



Erdős conjectured that the sequence of ratios
d(n+1)/d(n)
of consecutive values of the divisor function is “everywhere dense in
(0,\infty)
”
[
1
]
.
In fact it follows easily from the generalized prime
k
-tuple conjecture that for every rational
x>0
there are infinitely many solutions to
d(n+1)/d(n)=x
.
We prove this unconditionally by building on the idea of Hasanalizade
[
3
]
(see also Schlage-Puchta
[
4
]
), based on the following sieve theory result of Goldston, Graham, Pintz, and Yıldırım.

Theorem 1

(
[
2
]
*Corollary 2.1, special case
b_{1}=b_{2}=b_{3}=1
)
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}
be positive integers with
(r_{i},a_{i})=(r_{i},a_{i}-a_{j})=(r_{i},r_{j})=1
for all
i\neq j
.
Let
L_{i}(x)=a_{i}x+1
.
Let
C
be any positive integer.
Then there are indices
i,j
with
1\leq i<j\leq 3
such that there are infinitely many positive integers
x
with both
L_{i}(x)/r_{i},L_{j}(x)/r_{j}\in E_{2}(C)
,
where
E_{2}(C)
denote the set of products
p_{1}p_{2}
where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are distinct primes and
p_{1},p_{2}>C
.

Set
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=(a,a+1,a+2)
, where
a
is even. Then

a_{2}L_{1}-a_{1}L_{2}=a_{3}L_{2}-a_{2}L_{3}={\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{3}L_{1}-{\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{1}L_{3}=1.

Let
r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}
be pairwise coprime odd positive integers with
(r_{i},a_{i})=1
for each
i=1,2,3
and let
C
be the largest prime factor of
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}
.
If
x
is an integer such that
L_{1}(x)/r_{1},L_{2}(x)/r_{2}\in E_{2}(C)
then
n=a_{1}L_{2}(x)
satisfies

\frac{d(n+1)}{d(n)}=\frac{d(a_{2}L_{1}(x))}{d(a_{1}L_{2}(x))}=\frac{d(a_{2}r_{1})}{d(a_{1}r_{2})}.

Similarly, if
L_{2}(x)/r_{2},L_{3}(x)/r_{3}\in E_{2}(C)
then
n=a_{2}L_{3}(x)
satisfies

\frac{d(n+1)}{d(n)}=\frac{d(a_{3}L_{2}(x))}{d(a_{2}L_{3}(x))}=\frac{d(a_{3}r_{2})}{d(a_{2}r_{3})},

while if
L_{1}(x)/r_{1},L_{3}(x)/r_{3}\in E_{2}(C)
then
n={\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{1}L_{3}(x)
satisfies

\frac{d(n+1)}{d(n)}=\frac{d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{3}L_{1}(x))}{d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{1}L_{3}(x))}=\frac{d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{3}r_{1})}{d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{1}r_{3})}.

Thus, if
R
denotes the set of values attained infinitely many times by the sequence
d(n+1)/d(n)
,
then
Theorem

1
implies that
R
contains at least one of the three values

\frac{d(a_{2}r_{1})}{d(a_{1}r_{2})},\qquad\frac{d(a_{3}r_{2})}{d(a_{2}r_{3})},\qquad\frac{d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{3}r_{1})}{d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{1}r_{3})}.

Let
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
be distinct primes such that
(a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3},p_{1}p_{2}p_{3})=1
and apply the fact just stated with
r^{\prime}_{i}=r_{i}p_{i}^{e_{i}-1}
in place of
r_{i}
, where
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}>0
are arbitrary positive integers. We deduce that
R
contains one of

\frac{d(a_{2}r_{1})e_{1}}{d(a_{1}r_{2})e_{2}},\qquad\frac{d(a_{3}r_{2})e_{2}}{d(a_{2}r_{3})e_{3}},\qquad\frac{d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{3}r_{1})e_{1}}{d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{1}r_{3})e_{3}}.

Choosing
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
so that the three values are equal, it follows that

\frac{d(a_{2}r_{1})d(a_{3}r_{2})d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{1}r_{3})}{d(a_{1}r_{2})d(a_{2}r_{3})d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{3}r_{1})}\in R.

Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{k},q_{1},\dots,q_{l}
be distinct odd primes. Let
x_{i},y_{i}>0
(
1\leq i\leq k)
and
u_{i},v_{i}>0
(
1\leq i\leq l
) be positive integers and let

\displaystyle a=4p_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{x_{k}}q_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots q_{l}^{u_{l}},\qquad(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})=(1,p_{1}^{y_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{y_{k}},q_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots q_{l}^{v_{l}}).

Then
(r_{i},a_{i})=(r_{i},2)=(r_{i},r_{j})=1
for
i\neq j
, as required.
Thus
R
contains

\frac{d(a_{2}r_{1})d(a_{3}r_{2})d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{1}r_{3})}{d(a_{1}r_{2})d(a_{2}r_{3})d({\tfrac{1}{2}}a_{3}r_{1})}=\frac{4}{3}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(x_{i}+1)(y_{i}+1)}{x_{i}+y_{i}+1}\prod_{i=1}^{l}\frac{u_{i}+v_{i}+1}{(u_{i}+1)(v_{i}+1)}.

Let
f(x,y)=(x+1)(y+1)/(x+y+1)
. Noting that
4/3=f(1,1)
, we deduce that
R
contains the multiplicative subgroup
G
of
\mathbf{Q}^{+}
generated by the set
\{f(x,y):x,y\geq 1\}
.

We claim that
G=\mathbf{Q}^{+}
. It suffices to prove that
G
contains every prime number
p
, which we prove by induction on
p
. First,
2=f(2,3)\in G
. Next, if
p=2x+1>2
then, since
x+1
is a product of primes smaller than
p
, we have
p=(x+1)^{2}/f(x,x)\in G
by induction. This completes the proof.

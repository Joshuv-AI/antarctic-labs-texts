A unified approach for domination and packing problems in graphs.
E. Hinrichsen \affmark a, G. Nasini \affmark a,b and N. Vansteenkiste \affmark a,b, ∗ \ast

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce new concepts of domination and packing functions in graphs, which generalize, respectively, the labelled dominating and packing functions defined by Lee and Chang in 2008, and Hinrichsen et al. in 2019. These generalized functions offer a unified and simpler framework for addressing many of the variations of domination and packing concepts in graphs explored in the literature. Interestingly, their associated optimization problems turn out to be equivalent, providing insight to explain the observed coincidences in computational complexity results for graph classes where both problems, the domination one and its corresponding packing variation, have been analyzed. This equivalence also allows us to solve some computational complexity open questions, for some graph classes. Furthermore, we prove that the generalized problems remain solvable in polynomial time for graphs with bounded clique-width and strongly chordal graphs.

Introduction

Domination and packing problems provide effective models for various operations research applications, particularly utility location optimization. The classical domination problem has been extensively studied, resulting in multiple variations inspired by practical applications (see
Haynes et al. (1998)
for a comprehensive survey). These variations often incorporate additional constraints tailored to specific contexts, further enriching graphs’ theoretical framework of domination.
The concept of 2-packing, a set of vertices such that no closed neighborhood contains more than one vertex from the set, serves as the
packing counterpart
for the domination sets.
For many variations of the domination problem, their
reverse
ones, introduced in this paper as
dual domination-packing pair
of problems, have been proposed in the literature and studied regarding their computational complexity in various classes of graphs.

Lee and Chang (2008)
introduces a domination problem encompassing most of these variations as special cases. Its dual problem, studied by Hinrichsen et al., also generalizes the packing problems presented in the literature.

In this work, we propose a new
dual domination-packing pair of problems
that generalizes labelling problems and simplifies their definitions. Specifically, in this generalized framework, instances are characterized by a graph and two non-negative vectors encoding bounds on the function values and on the accumulated quantities within each closed neighborhood. As a result, optimization problems previously addressed by
labelling concepts
can now be viewed as particular cases of this more simplified formulation.

Interestingly, these two newly defined problems have proven to be equivalent, providing insights into the observed coincidences in the computational complexity of pairwise dual problems for specific graph classes, results
that were previously derived independently.

The structure of this paper is as follows. The next section presents the notation, definitions, and preliminary results required to understand the topics discussed. Subsequently, Section
3
introduces the results that unify and simplify the definitions of labelling problems, establish the duality between certain variations of packing and domination problems, and clarify the relationships among them. This section also includes the definition of the new dual domination-packing pair of problems, which generalizes the labeling problems. In Section
4
, the known computational complexity results from the literature are, presented, and the equivalence of the generalized problems is proved. Building on this equivalence, Section
5
extends computational complexity results for packing problems on dually and doubly chordal graphs and demonstrates that the generalized problems remain polynomial-time solvable for graphs with bounded clique-width and strongly chordal graphs. Finally, Section
6
presents the conclusions and discusses open problems.

Preliminaries

Given
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
n\leq m
and

, we denote
[n,m]=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}:n\leq j\leq m\}
,
[n]=[1,n]

and

. We assume that
\max\emptyset=-\infty
. The vector
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
(resp.
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
) has all its components equal to 1 (resp. to 0).
For a finite set

, a function
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
can be also considered as a vector
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{Z}^{V}
. For
U\subset V
,
f(U)
denotes
\sum_{u\in U}f(u)
.

In this work, every graph is simple and undirected. When the sets of vertices and edges of a graph
G
are not given, they are denoted by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively. Given
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
K_{n}
,
S_{n}
, and

(

) are, respectively, the complete graph, the edgeless graph, and the cycle
on
n
vertices.

Given graphs
G
and

and a vertex
v
of
G
, the graph obtained
replacing

v

by

H
is the graph with vertices in
V(G)\setminus\{v\})\cup V(H)
and whose edges are those in
E(G[V\setminus\{v\}])\cup E(H)
together with those connecting every vertex of
H
with every vertex of
N_{G}(v)
.

A
clique
of a graph
G
is a subset of pairwise adjacent vertices. while a
stable set
, is a subset of pairwise nonadjacent vertices.
A
split graph
is a graph such that its vertex set may be partitioned into a stable set and a clique.

A
chord
in a cycle is an edge connecting two non consecutive nodes in the cycle.
A graph
G
is
chordal
if every cycle of length at least 4 has a chord.

For
v\in V(G)
,
N_{G}(v)
denotes the open neighborhood of
v
in
G
and
N_{G}[v]=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
is its closed neighborhood.
When the graph is clear from the context, we simply denote
N[v]
and
N(v)
.
The
degree of

v
is
\delta(v)=|N_{G}(v)|
and
\Delta(G)=\max\{\delta(v):v\in V(G)\}
.

A vertex
v
is a
pendant vertex
if
|N_{G}(v)|=1
and it is called
simplicial
if
N[v]
is a clique in
G
. A vertex
u\in N[v]
is a
maximum neighbor of

v
if for each
w\in N[v]
,
N[w]\subseteq N[u]
.

Given a graph
G
with
n
vertices and an order
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
of its vertices, let
G_{i}=G[v_{i},\ldots,v_{n}]
,
i\in[n]
. The order is a
strong elimination order of

G
if, for every
i\in[n]
, it holds that

v_{i}

is simplicial in
G_{i}
and,
for
j,k\in[n]
such that
i\leq j\leq k
and
\{v_{j},v_{k}\}\subseteq N_{G_{i}}[v_{i}]
it holds that
N_{G_{i}}[v_{j}]\subseteq N_{G_{i}}[v_{k}]
.
Additionally,
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
is an
order of maximum neighborhoods of
G
if, for
i\in[n]
,
v_{i}
has a maximum neighbor in
G_{i}
.

A graph is
strongly chordal
if it admits a strong elimination ordering and it is
dually chordal
if it admits an order of maximum neighborhood
Brandstädt et al. (1998)
.
A graph is
doubly chordal
if it is chordal and dually chordal.
Several characterizations of strongly chordal graphs are presented in
Farber (1983)
and one of them yields a polynomial recognition algorithm and constructs strong elimination orderings.

The
clique-width
of a graph
is defined as the minimum number of labels needed to construct
G
using the following four operations: creation of a new vertex
v
with label
i
,
disjoint union of two labelled graphs,
join by an edge every vertex labelled
i
to every vertex labelled
j
,
i\neq j
, and
renaming label
i
to label
j
.
Some important classes of graphs have clique-width bounded by a constant. In particular, cographs are the class of graphs with clique-width at most 2, trees and distance hereditary graphs have clique-width at most 3 and
P_{4}
-tidy graphs have clique-width at most 4. Many optimization problems that are NP-hard for more general graph classes may be solved
efficiently on graphs of clique-width bounded by a constant
(
Courcelle et al., 2000
)
.

Remark 1
.

In what follows, it will be important to note that adding pendant vertices to a graph preserves the conditions of being chordal, dually chordal, or bounded clique-width.

Let us now introduce the problems we address in this paper. As mentioned before, the

Labelled Domination problem
(
\mathcal{L}
dom) is presented
in
Lee and Chang (2008)
.

A
labelling function

L
assigns to each vertex
v
of a graph a pair
L(v)=(t(v),k(v))
such that

k(v)\in\mathbb{Z}
and

t(v)=F
(meaning that
v
is a free vertex) or
t(v)\in Y=\{I+td:t\in[0,\ell]\}
, for given
I\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\ell,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Observe that a labelling function
L
of a graph
G=(V,E)
can be given by integers
I\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\ell,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and vectors
\mathbf{t}\in(Y\cup\{F\})^{V}

and
{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{V}
. So, we denote it as
L=(I,d,\ell,\mathbf{t},{\mathbf{k}})
.

Given a labelling function
L=(I,d,\ell,\mathbf{t},{\mathbf{k}})
, a function
f:V\rightarrow Y
is an
L
-
dominating function of

G
if,
for all
v\in V
,

f(N_{G}[v])\geq k(v)
and, if
t(v)\neq F
, then
f(v)=t(v)
. The weight of
f
is
f(V)
.
The goal of
\mathcal{L}
dom is to calculate the minimum weight of an
L
-dominating function of
G
, denoted by
\gamma_{L}(G)
.

The authors in
Lee and Chang (2008)
prove that
\mathcal{L}
dom generalizes the following problems whose original definitions are presented in Appendix
7
: Domination,
k
-tuple domination (
k
LD),
\{k\}
-dominating functions (
\{k\}
DF),
Signed domination, Minus domination,
and Fault Tolerant domination.
Other variations defined later are also generalized by
\mathcal{L}
dom: M-domination (Mdom) - also presented in
Leoni and Nasini (2014)
as Generalized Multiple Domination problem (GMD) - and Multiple Domination (LD).

For most of the domination problems mentioned above, a
dual packing problem
was defined in the literature.
In particular, we can mentioned the following: 2-Packing,
k
-Limited Packing (
k
LP),
\{k\}
-packing (
\{k\}
PF),
Generalized Limited Packing (GLP), and Limited Packing (LP)
(see Appendix
7
for original definitions and references). As in the case of
\mathcal{L}
dom, in Remark 4 in
Hinrichsen et al. (2019)
it is shown that all the packing problems mentioned above can also be thought as particular instances of the
Labelled Packing problem

defined as follows:

Given a labelling function
L=(0,1,\ell,\mathbf{t},{\mathbf{k}})
of
G=(V,E)
, a function

f:V\rightarrow[0,\ell]
is an
L
-
packing function of

G
if, for all
v\in V
,
f(N[v])\leq k(v)
and if
t(v)\neq F
, then
f(v)=t(v)
. The objective of the Labelled Packing problem is to calculate the minimum weight of an
L
-packing function of
G
.

Unifying and generalizing domination and packing problems

Let us begin by presenting some results that will allow us to more clearly establish the
domination-packing duality
between the problems mentioned in the previous section.

Theorem 1 and Theorem 3 in
Argiroffo et al. (2017)

prove that to solve
\mathcal{L}
dom, it is enough to consider instances with

and
d=1
.
Indeed, to obtain
\gamma_{L}(G)
for a graph
G
with
n
vertices and
L=(I,d,\ell,\mathbf{t},{\mathbf{k}})
, we can calculate
\gamma_{L^{*}}(G)
for
L^{*}=(0,1,\ell,\mathbf{t},{\mathbf{k}}^{*})

k^{*}(v)=\lceil\frac{k(v)-I(\delta(v)+1)}{d}\rceil
for all

. It follows that

\gamma_{L}(G)=d\,(\gamma_{L^{*}}(G)+In)
.

Observe that for labelling functions with
I=0
and

, it holds that
Y=[0,\ell]
, which motivated the definition of its dual packing problem by focusing exclusively on these types of instances.
Moreover,
Proposition 5 in
Hinrichsen et al. (2019)
shows that the Labelled Packing Problem can be reduced to a labelling functions with

,

and

Thus, if

L_{\ell,{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{t}}(G)=\min\{f(V):f\text{ is an }(0,1,\ell,{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{t})\text{-packing function of }G\}

for given
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{V}
, and

, we define

PF: Labelling Packing Function Problem

Instance: A graph
G=(V,E)
,

,
{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{V}
,

Objective: To obtain

.

The following lemma shows that instances of
\mathcal{L}

,

, and

Lemma 1
.

Let be an instance of
\mathcal{L}
dom given by
G=(V,E)
and
L=(0,1,\ell,{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{t})
with
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\mathbf{t}\in([0,\ell]\cup\{F\})^{V}
, and
{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{V}
.
Let
\mathcal{F}=\{v\in V:t(v)=F\}
and
L_{0}=(0,1,\ell,\mathbf{k_{0}},\mathbf{t_{0}})
such that,
for
v\in V
,
k_{0}(v)=(k(v)-t(N[v]\setminus\mathcal{F}))^{+}
,

if
t(v)\neq F
and
t_{0}(v)=F
, otherwise.
Then,
\gamma_{L}(G)=\gamma_{L_{0}}(G)+t(V\setminus\mathcal{F})

Proof.

Given
f

(0,1,\ell,{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{t})
-dominating function of
G
we define
f^{\prime}:V\rightarrow[0,\ell]
such that if
w\in\mathcal{F}
,
f^{\prime}(w)=f(w)
, and
f^{\prime}(w)=0
otherwise.
Let us see that
f^{\prime}
is a
(0,1,\ell,\mathbf{k_{0}},\mathbf{t_{0}})
-dominating function.

For all
v\in V

f(N[v])=f(N[v]\cap\mathcal{F})+f(N[v]\setminus\mathcal{F})=f(N[v]\cap\mathcal{F})+t(N[v]\setminus\mathcal{F})\geq k(v).

Then,
f(N[v]\cap\mathcal{F})\geq k(v)-t(N[v]\setminus\mathcal{F})
. Since by definition of
f

f(N[v]\cap\mathcal{F})\geq 0
, we obtain

f^{\prime}(N[v])\geq f(N[v]\cap\mathcal{F})\geq(k(v)-t(N[v]\setminus\mathcal{F}))^{+}=k^{\prime}(v).

We have proved that
f^{\prime}
is a
L_{0}
-dominating function of
G
with
f^{\prime}(V)=f(V)-t(V\setminus\mathcal{F})
.

Conversely, given an
(0,1,\ell,\mathbf{k_{0}},\mathbf{t_{0}})
-dominating function

of
G
we define
f(v)=t(v)
if
v\notin\mathcal{F}
and
f(v)=f^{\prime}(v)
otherwise. It holds that, for all
v\in V
,

f(N[v])=f^{\prime}(N[v]\cap\mathcal{F})+t(N[v]\setminus\mathcal{F})\geq k^{\prime}(v)+t(N[v]\setminus\mathcal{F})\geq k(v).

Then,
f
is a
(0,1,\ell,\mathbf{k},\mathbf{t})
-dominating function of
G
with
f(V)=f^{\prime}(V)+t(V\setminus\mathcal{F})
.
∎

We now present a new pair of dual domination-packing concepts in graphs.

\mathcal{L}
DF and
\mathcal{L}
PF.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and
{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}}
, two vectors in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{V}

A function

is a
({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}})
-

of

f(v)\leq u(v)
and
f(N[v])\geq k(v)
(resp.
f(N[v])\leq k(v)
), for all
v\in V
.
Denoting by
\mathcal{D}_{{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}}}(G)
(resp.
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}}}(G)
) the set of all
({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}})
-dominating (resp. packing) functions of

GDF: Generalized Dominating Function Problem

Instance: A graph
G
,
{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{V}
.

Objective: To obtain
\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}}}(G)=\min\{f(V):f\in\mathcal{D}_{{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}}}(G)\}
.

GPF: Generalized Packing Function Problem

Instance: A graph
G
,
{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{V}
.

Objective: To obtain
L_{{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}}}(G)=\max\{f(V):f\in\mathcal{L}_{{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}}}(G)\}
.

.

Conclusions and open questions

In this paper, we propose a generalized dual domination-packing pair of problems, and we prove that they are equivalent to any graph, offering a partial explanation for these coincidences in computational complexity results for dual pairs. inherent in several pairs of problems previously defined in the literature. This framework also facilitates the completion of findings for graph classes where only one issue in the dual domination-packing pair has been studied.

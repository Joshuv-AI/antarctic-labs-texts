α \alpha -decay systematics for superheavy nucleus: The effect of deformation of daughter nucleus Preprint: APS/123-QED
Jinyu Hu 1 and Chen Wu 1

Abstract
Recently, V.Yu. Denisov proposed a new empirical formula incorporating the deformation of the daughter nucleus, which has significantly improved the description of α \alpha -decay half-lives for even-even nuclei compared to formulas neglecting the deformation of the daughter nucleus. In this work, we generalize the deformation of the daughter nucleus proposed by V.Yu. Denisov to the DUR model, the AKRA model, the New Geiger-Nuttall law, generating three improved versions of these models. We then employ both the original and modified the DUR model, the AKRA model, and the New Geiger-Nuttall law to investigate the α \alpha -decay half-lives of 400 isotopes. Results show that among the six models, the modified AKRA model provides the closest agreement with experimental α \alpha -decay half-life data. For comparative analysis, we use the new empirical formulas developed for the DUR model (DUR+D), the AKRA model (AKRA+D) and a new empirical (ND) proposed by V.Yu. Denisov to predict the α \alpha -decay properties of 71 even-even nuclei with Z = 118, 120, 122, and 124. The predictions from the DUR+D model, the AKRA+D model, and ND are largely consistent overall. Notably, when the neutron number N > 190 N>190 , the predictions from the DUR+D model and the AKRA+D model exceed those of ND, which may be attributed to additional physical contributions (e.g., the hexadecapole and the hexacontatetrapole deformation of the deformed daughter nucleus) incorporated in the DUR+D model and the AKRA+D model but not in ND.

INTRODUCTION

Since,in 1908, the
\alpha
-decay is described as a process that parent nucleus emits a
4
He nucleus by Ernest Rutherford, it has been an important topic of research in nuclear physics. This is because it is the primary decay mode of superheavy nuclei, thereby enabling the provision of abundant information regarding nuclear structure, such as nuclear spins, shell effects, ground-state energies, half-lives and so on. More significantly, with the advancement of experimental detection techniques,
\alpha
decay chains can provide new elements and isotopes information. In summary,
\alpha
-decay has consistently remained a forefront research focus in nuclear physics

In 1911, Geiger and Nuttall
Geiger and Nuttall 1911
first introduced an empirical formula to describe alpha decay. This formula suggests that the logarithm of the half-life of alpha decay is linearly correlated with the negative square root of the alpha decay energy. This empirical relationship is known as the Geiger-Nuttall law. It was written as:

log_{10}T_{1/2}(s)=a+bQ_{\alpha}^{-1/2}

(1)

Subsequently, Gamov and Condon and Gurney independently interpreted the
\alpha
-decay phenomenon using quantum tunneling theory, both of which were consistent with the Geiger-Nuttall law. Following the successful explanation of
\alpha
-decay via quantum tunneling theory, numerous theoretical models for calculating
\alpha
-decay began to emerge. For example, Gamow-like model
Zdeb et al. 2013
, the modified genralized liquid drop model
Guo et al. 2015
;
Zhang et al. 2006
, the Coulomb and proximity potential model
Zanganah et al. 2020
;
Yahya 2020
, two-potential approach(TPA)
Gurvitz and Kalbermann 1987
, the double-folding potential model
Moghaddari Amiri and Ghodsi 2020
and so on
Jian-Min et al. 2009
;
Yan-Zhao et al. 2009
. Despite decades of development in
\alpha
-decay theory, no single framework has yet emerged that can fully explain
\alpha
-decay phenomena and accurately predict its half-life. Furthermore, both the Geiger-Nuttall law and various theoretical models suggest that multiple factors significantly influence
\alpha
-decay half-life calculations, which partially explains why current theories remain unable to achieve precise predictions.

Currently, numerous improved empirical formulas for describing
\alpha
-decay have been developed based on the Geiger-Nuttall law. First, with respect to the mass number A and proton number Z of the
\alpha
-decaying parent nucleus, various empirical formulas have been proposed for different compositional forms of
\alpha
-decay energy, such as the Royer formula
Royer 2000
, Viola-Seaborg-Sobiczewski formula
Viola Jr and Seaborg 1966
, Universal decay law
Qi et al. 2009
and so on. To better characterize the
\alpha
-decay phenomenon, certain empirical formulas have incorporated both the spin and parity of ground states the parent and daughter nuclei. These refined empirical models have improved the computational accuracy of
\alpha
-decay half-lives, particularly for odd-A and odd-odd nuclei
Ren and Ren 2012
;
Koura 2012
. To date, numerous empirical formulas incorporating spin and parity have been developed
Saxena et al. 2021
;
Soylu and Qi 2021
;
Akrawy et al. 2022a
. Recently, many empirical formulas have also started to incorporate proton-neutron antisymmetry
Akrawy and Poenaru 2017
;
Akrawy and Ahmed 2018
;
Akrawy and Ahmed 2019
;
Akrawy et al. 2022b
. Notably, Dashty T. Akrawy and Ali H
Akrawy and Ahmed 2018
, Ahmed have systematically demonstrated the impact of including proton-neutron antisymmetry in empirical formulas for describing
\alpha
-decay. In summary, the introduction of proton-neutron antisymmetry has significantly enhanced the computational accuracy of
\alpha
-decay half-lives.

Despite decades of development that have significantly improved the accuracy of empirical formulas in describing
\alpha
-decay, they still exhibit limitations when compared to semi-classical WKB theoretical frameworks. For example, while many theoretical models account for the deformation effects of the daughter nucleus during
\alpha
-decay
Yu et al. 2009
;
Ismail et al. 2017
;
Deng and Ren 2017
;
Denisov and Khudenko 2010
, most empirical formulas neglect this factor. In 2024, V.Yu. Denisov
Denisov 2024
first proposed an empirical formula incorporating daughter nucleus deformation and compared the deformation parameters calculated by three distinct mass models (FRDM, HFB, and WS4
Wang et al. 2014
) with results from spherical nucleus approximations. The analysis revealed that introducing daughter nucleus deformation—particularly when calculated using model WS4
Wang et al. 2014
—substantially enhanced the accuracy of
\alpha
-decay descriptions.

In this study, building upon the research of V.Yu. Denisov, we generalize the empirical formula with the deformation of the daughter nucleus that he proposed to other empirical formulas. We incorporate terms representing the deformation of the daughter nucleus into the DUR model, the AKRA model, and the New Geiger-Nuttall law(NGN). Subsequently, we utilize these three original models and their improved model (the DUR+D model, the AKRA+D model, and the NGN+D model) to investigate the
\alpha
- decay half - lives of 400 selected nucleus. These nuclei are further categorized into four subsets, namely even-even, even-odd, odd-even, and odd-odd, for in-depth individual analysis. More significantly, we consider both hexadecapole and hexacontatetrapole deformation of the daughter nucleus within these empirical formulas, aiming to meticulously explore how such deformations enhance the accuracy of the empirical formulas in describing
\alpha
-decay. For comparative purposes, we employ the refined DUR model and AKRA model (the DUR+D model, the AKRA+D model), along with the empirical formula with the deformation of the daughter nucleus proposed by V.Yu. Denisov, to calculate
\alpha
decay half-lives for 71 even-even nuclei with Z=118 to Z=124, and then compare their predictive results.

This work is organized as follows. In Sec.
III
, we apply these three original models and their improved model (the DUR+D model, the AKRA+D model, and the NGN+D model) to evaluate the alpha-decay half-lives for each isotope and compare the results with experimental values. The application of the DUR+D model, the AKRA+D model has been extended to 71 even-even nuclei with Z=118 to Z=124; the result was shown in figures for each set of isotopes. In Sec.
IV
, we present our conclusions.

Recently, V.Yu.Denisov
Denisov 2024
proposed a new empirical formula by introducing the deformation of the daughter nucleus. The new empirical relation is given as

\displaystyle log_{10}T_{1/2}(s)=

\displaystyle a\frac{Z}{Q^{1/2}}-b(\frac{AQ^{1/2}}{Z})^{1/6}-cA^{1/6}Z^{1/2}

(2)

\displaystyle+dA^{1/6}\frac{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}{Q}-e(k\beta)^{1/2}\frac{Z}{Q^{1/2}}.

where the half-life is given in seconds, the
\alpha
decay energy(Q) in MeV, A is the mass number of parent nucleus, Z is the proton number of parent nucleus, and
\beta
is the quadrupole deformation parameter of the deformed daughter nucleus.

In V.Yu.Denisov’s study, the fifth term describing deformation is derived from the Coulomb interaction between the
\alpha
-particle and the daughter nucleus induced by the daughter nucleus’s deformation, as the Coulomb interaction between the
\alpha
-particle and daughter nucleus significantly influences
\alpha
-decay half-life calculations through daughter nucleus deformation. Therefore, V.Yu.Denisov defines the effect of daughter nucleus deformation on
\alpha
-decay half-life based on the minimum value of the Coulomb effect. Here, the minimum value of the Coulomb effect corresponds to
V^{min}_{C}=2(Z-2)e^{2}_{p}/(R_{L}+R_{\alpha})
, where
R_{L}
is the largest values of radius of the deformed daughter nucleus,
R_{\alpha}
is the radius of the
\alpha
particle, and
e_{p}
is the proton charge. In the definition of Coulomb interaction, the most critical parameter is the maximum value of the deformation parameter of the daughter nucleus. At first,
\beta
is the quadruple deformation of the surface radius of the deformed daughter nucleus
R(\theta)=R_{0}[1+\beta Y_{20}(\theta)]
, where
R_{0}
is the radius of the spherical daughter nucleus. When
\beta>0
and
\theta=0
, the largest values of radius of the deformed daughter nucleus is
R_{L}=R_{0}(1+\sqrt{5/\pi}\beta/2)
. When
\beta<0
and
\theta=\pi/2
, the largest values of radius of the deformed daughter nucleus is
R_{L}=R_{0}(1+\sqrt{5/\pi}\beta/4)
. By subtracting the Coulomb interaction of the spherical nucleus from the minimum value of the Coulomb interaction induced by daughter nucleus deformation, the reduction in
\alpha
-decay half-life caused by daughter nucleus deformation can be obtained. In summary, V.Yu.Denisov’s study shows that the reduction in
\alpha
-decay half-life due to daughter nucleus deformation is proportional to the difference between the daughter nucleus deformation parameter and that of the spherical nucleus (
\Delta\propto R_{L}-R_{0}
).

According to V.Yu.Denisov’s research, the alteration in
\alpha
-decay half-life induced by daughter nucleus deformation can be characterized by the Coulomb interaction between the daughter nucleus and the
\alpha
-particle. Notably, V.Yu.Denisov only considered the quadrupole deformation of the daughter nucleus. In this work, we will account for hexadecapole and hexacontatetrapole deformation of the deformed daughter nucleus. Building on V.Yu.Denisov’s findings, the minimum value of the Coulomb interaction between the daughter nucleus and the
\alpha
-particle caused by daughter nucleus deformation can be defined as:
V^{min}_{C}=2(Z-2)e^{2}_{p}/(R_{L}+R_{\alpha})
. In this work, we consider the quadrupole, hexadecapole and hexacontatetrapole deformation of the deformed daughter nucleus. Then the radius of the deformed daughter nucleus can be given as
R=R_{0}(1+\beta_{2}Y_{20}(\theta)+\beta_{4}Y_{40}(\theta)+\beta_{6}Y_{60}(\theta))
. To comprehensively describe the characteristics of the deformed daughter nucleus, it is necessary to consider the maximum value of its radius. The largest value of the radius of the deformed daughter nucleus is given as

\displaystyle R_{L}=R_{0}(1+\left|Max(\beta_{2}Y_{20}(\theta)+\beta_{4}Y_{40}(\theta)+\beta_{6}Y_{60}(\theta))\right|)

(3)

where
Max
is the largest values of the
\beta_{2}Y_{20}(\theta)+\beta_{4}Y_{40}(\theta)+\beta_{6}Y_{60}(\theta)
. Meanwhile, since this deformation primarily influences the Coulomb interaction between the
\alpha
-particle and the deformed daughter nucleus, it can be deduced from the analysis of Royer formula that the deformation term in the empirical formula at this stage can be expressed as:

\displaystyle e(\left|Max(\beta_{2}Y_{20}(\theta)+\beta_{4}Y_{40}(\theta)+\beta_{6}Y_{60}(\theta))\right|)^{1/2}\frac{Z}{Q^{1/2}}

(4)

In this work, we incorporated a new deformation term (in Eq.
4
) describing daughter nucleus deformation into the three empirical formulas (the DUR+D model, the AKRA+D model, and the NGN+D model), leading to the improvement of these formulas. Subsequently, we utilized both the original and modified versions of these six empirical formulas to calculate the
\alpha
-decay half-lives of 400 selected nucleus. And, in this paper, we use the module of curve fitting in Python 3.11 programming language.

IV
SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION

Building on the work of V.Yu.Denisov, a new empirical formula incorporating deformation terms (ND) is proposed. We generalize the deformation terms describing daughter nucleus deformation to the DUR model, the AKRA model, and the New Geiger-Nuttall law(NGN), leading to improvements in the original DUR model, the AKRA model, and the New Geiger-Nuttall law(NGN) frameworks. These six empirical formulas (original and modified versions) are then employed to investigate the
\alpha
-decay half-lives of 400 nuclei (even-even, even-odd, odd-even, and odd-odd), with the root-mean-square deviation (RMS) used as a metric to evaluate model performance. As shown in Figures
1
-
4
, among the six models, the modified AKRA model (AKRA+D) demonstrates the closest agreement with experimental results.

To further validate the improvements, we use the new empirical formulas developed for the DUR+D model, the AKRA+D model, and ND to predict the
\alpha
-decay properties of 71 nuclei with Z = 118, 120,122, and 124. Figures
5
-
8
show strong consistency among the predictions from the DUR+D model, the AKRA+D model, and ND. Notably, when the neutron number
N>190
,the predictions from the DUR+D model and the AKRA+D model exceed those of ND, which can be attributed to the inclusion of hexadecapole and hexacontatetrapole deformation of the deformed daughter nucleus contributions in the DUR+D model and the AKRA+D model that are not considered in ND. This result indicates that incorporating daughter nucleus deformation terms into empirical formulas significantly enhances the accuracy of calculating
\alpha
-decay half-lives for even-even nuclei.

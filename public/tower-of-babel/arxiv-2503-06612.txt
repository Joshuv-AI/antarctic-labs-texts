𝔾 m \mathbb{G}_{m} -Equivariant Degenerations of del Pezzo Surfaces
Junyao Peng

Abstract
. We study special 𝔾 m \mathbb{G}_{m} -equivariant degenerations of a smooth del Pezzo surface X X induced by valuations that are log canonical places of ( X , C ) (X,C) for a nodal anti-canonical curve C C . We show that the space of special valuations in the dual complex of ( X , C ) (X,C) is connected and admits a locally finite partition into sub-intervals, each associated to a 𝔾 m \mathbb{G}_{m} -equivariant degeneration of X X . This result is an example of higher rank degenerations of log Fano varieties studied by Liu-Xu-Zhuang, and verifies a global analog of a conjecture on Kollár valuations raised by Liu-Xu. For del Pezzo surfaces with quotient singularities, we obtain a weaker statement about the space of special valuations associated to a normal crossing complement.



1.
Introduction

For a normal projective variety
X
, a degeneration of
X
is a variety
X^{\prime}
with a flat family
\mathcal{X}\to C
over a smooth pointed curve
0\in C
, such that the general fiber
\mathcal{X}_{t}
is isomorphic to
X
for

and the central fiber
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is isomorphic to

. A degeneration is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein if

is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. It is then natural to ask whether one can classify all
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein degenerations of
X
. This question is closely related to the local geometry of moduli spaces of varieties. When
X
is a variety of general type, any
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein degeneration of

is trivial, which implies that the KSBA compactification of moduli space of varieties of general type is always Deligne-Mumford
[
KSB88
]
[
Ale94
]
[
Kol23
]
.

When
X
is the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, the classification of degenerations of

is a non-trivial problem. Manetti
[
Man91
]
classified all normal degenerations of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with quotient singularities in terms of numerical invariants and quotient types. Hacking and Prokhorov
[

]
proved that
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein degenerations of

must be partial smoothings of weighted projective spaces
\mathbb{P}(a^{2},b^{2},c^{2})

Solutions of the Markov equations are generated via mutations of the form
(a,b,c)\mapsto(a,b,3ab-c)
and thus form a tree structure. Based on results about flips on a family of surfaces
[
KM92
]
[
Mor02
]
[
HTU17
]
, Urzúa and Zúñiga
[

]
studied the Markov tree and showed that different degenerations of

are related to each other by a sequence of explicit birational operations. Furthermore,
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein degenerations of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
which admit a non-trivial torus action are known to be either
\mathbb{P}(a^{2},b^{2},c^{2})
for a Markov triple
(a,b,c)
or a hypersurface of a weighted projective space
[
HKW24
]
.

For general del Pezzo surfaces,
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein degenerations are studied via
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein smoothings of singular surfaces. Hacking and Prokhorov
[

]
classified

-Gorenstein smoothable del Pezzo surfaces with quotient singularities and Picard rank 1. Prokhorov
[

]
then extended this result to the case of log canonical singularities. Other results in this direction include the higher Picard rank case
[

]
and the case of Wahl singularities

]
[

]
[

]
.

The goal of this paper is to study
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant degenerations of del Pezzo surfaces, which are known as test configurations in the algebraic theory of K-stability. Roughly speaking, a test configuration is a flat

-equivariant family

with general fiber
\mathcal{X}_{t}\cong X
. Via the Rees construction, these

-equivariant degenerations are induced by certain divisorial valuations which can be characterized by singularities of the MMP
[

, Theorem 4.12]
. In the proof of the optimal destabilization conjecture
[

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant degenerations of Fano varieties. These higher rank degenerations of a Fano variety

are induced by certain

valuations over

\mathbb{Q}
-complement

Sketch of the proof

The proof of Theorem

2.4.
Quasi-monomial valuations

Definition 2.15
.

Let
(Y,E)
be a dlt pair. Suppose
E_{1},\ldots,E_{r}
are irreducible components of
\lfloor E\rfloor
such that
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}\neq\varnothing
. Let
C
be a component of
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}
and
\eta
be the generic point of
C
. Around
\eta
,
E_{i}
is given by an equation
y_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{Y,\eta}
. Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
. For each

f=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}c_{\beta}y^{\beta},\text{ where }y^{\beta}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}y_{i}^{\beta_{i}}.

We define a valuation
v_{\alpha}
on
\mathcal{O}_{Y,\eta}
by setting

v_{\alpha}(f)=\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}:c_{\beta}\neq 0\right\}.

Then,
v_{\alpha}
naturally extends to a valuation on
Y
. We define

\text{QM}_{\eta}(Y,E)=\{v_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}\}

and

\text{QM}(Y,E)=\bigcup_{\eta^{\prime}}\text{QM}_{\eta^{\prime}}(Y,E)

where
\eta^{\prime}
runs through the generic point of every stratum of
\lfloor E\rfloor
.

Remark 2.16
.

There is a natural
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
action on
\text{QM}(Y,E)
which rescales any valuation by a positive constant.
Then, the dual complex
\mathcal{D}(Y,E)
can be identified with the quotient of
\text{QM}(Y,E)\setminus\{0\}
by the
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action on
\text{QM}(Y,E)
.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
X
be a variety. We say that

is a quasi-monomial valuation over

if there exists a dlt model
(Y,E)\to X
, such that

Let
(X,D)
be a log canonical pair. We say that

if there exists a dlt modification
(Y,E)\to(X,D)
such that
v\in\text{QM}(Y,E)
.

Definition 2.18
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety. We say that a projective birational morphism

-divisor

is qdlt (see

We say that a quasi-monomial valuation

2.5.
Unicuspidal curves

Definition 2.21
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface and
C\subseteq X
be a nodal curve with a node
x
. Let
x_{1},x_{2}
denote the analytic coordinate near
x
such that the local equation of
C
is given by
x_{1}x_{2}=0
. We say that an integral curve
D
on
X
is a
(p,q)
-unicuspidal curve well-formed with respect to
(C,x)
if

•

D
has a unique singular point
x
, which is a
(p,q)
-cusp,

•

the Newton polygon of the local equation of
D
in the
x_{1},x_{2}
coordinate is the region

\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}:\frac{x}{p}+\frac{y}{q}\geq 1\right\}.

Proposition 2.22
.

Suppose
X
is a smooth del Pezzo surface and
C
is an anti-canonical integral divisor on
X
with nodal singularities. Let
x\in C
be a node and fix an order of the two (analytic) branches of
C
at
x
. For
t>0
, let
v_{t}\in\text{QM}(X,C)
denote the quasi-monomial valuation centered at
x
with weight
(1,t)
along the two branches of
C
. Let
p,q
be coprime positive integers such that
pq\operatorname{vol}(-K_{X})>(p+q)^{2}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a line bundle
L
such that
p+q=(-K_{X}\cdot L)
and
pq=(L^{2})+1
.

(b)

There exists a
(p,q)
-unicuspidal rational curve
D
well-formed with respect to
C
such that
D\cap C=\{x\}
.

(c)

Suppose
f:Y\to X
is the
(p,q)
-weighted blow-up along the two branches of
C
. Then
C_{Y}:=f_{*}^{-1}C
is big and nef but not ample.

Proof.

We first show that (a) implies (b). By Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing and Riemann-Roch,

h^{0}(L)=\chi(L)=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+\frac{1}{2}(L\cdot L-K_{X})=1+\frac{1}{2}(L^{2})+\frac{1}{2}(-K_{X}\cdot L).

Thus, condition (ii) in (a) implies that

h^{0}(L)=\frac{1}{2}(pq+p+q+1).

Let
v=\operatorname{ord}_{E}
, where
E=\text{Ex}(f)
the exceptional divisor. By Pick’s theorem,

\text{colength}(\mathfrak{a}_{pq}(v))=\frac{1}{2}(pq+p+q-1)<h^{0}(L).

Thus, there is a divisor
D\in|L|
with
v(D)\geq pq
.

Suppose that
D
is not integral, then there exists
0\leq D^{\prime}<D
such that

\frac{v(D^{\prime})}{\deg(D^{\prime})}\geq\frac{v(D)}{\deg(D)}\implies v(D^{\prime})\geq\frac{(-K_{X}\cdot D^{\prime})}{(-K_{X}\cdot D)}pq=(-K_{X}\cdot D^{\prime})\frac{pq}{p+q},

where the last equality is given by (i).
If
D^{\prime}\neq C
, We compute the local intersection numbers to get the following inequality:

(-K_{X}\cdot D^{\prime})\geq(-K_{X}\cdot D^{\prime})_{x}\geq v(D^{\prime})(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})\geq\frac{(-K_{X}\cdot D^{\prime})}{(-K_{X}\cdot D)}(p+q)=(-K_{X}\cdot D^{\prime}).

Thus, all the above equalities must hold. In particular,

(-K_{X}\cdot D^{\prime})\frac{pq}{p+q}=v(D^{\prime})

must be an integer. However, since
\gcd(p,q)=1
,
(-K_{X}\cdot D^{\prime})
divides
p+q
and hence must be at least
p+q
. This contradicts with
D^{\prime}<D
.

If
D^{\prime}=C
, then

\frac{v(C)}{\deg C}\geq\frac{v(D)}{\deg D}\implies\frac{p+q}{\operatorname{vol}(-K_{X})}\geq\frac{pq}{(-K_{X}\cdot L)}\implies(p+q)^{2}\geq pq\operatorname{vol}(-K_{X}),

which contradicts with (iii).

Since
D
is integral, the newton polygon of
D
in
x_{1},x_{2}
coordinates passes through
(x_{D},0)
and
(0,y_{D})
for some
x_{D},y_{D}>0
. Then
x_{D}\geq q
and
y_{D}\geq p
. Again, by computing local intersection numbers, we get

p+q=(-K_{X}\cdot D)\geq(-K_{X}\cdot D)_{x}=x_{D}+y_{D}

Therefore,
x_{D}=q
,
y_{D}=p
and hence
v(D)=pq
. Thus, the newton polygon of
D
cannot have any vertices below the line connecting
(q,0)
and
(0,p)
. The equality
(C\cdot D)=(C\cdot D)_{x}
also shows that
C\cap D=\{x\}
.

Since
D
already has a
(p,q)
-cusp,

2g(D)-2\leq(K_{X}+D)\cdot D-(p-1)(q-1)=-2

Thus,
g(D)=0
and
D
does not have any other singularities.

Next, we show that (b) implies (c). Let
D
be the
(p,q)
-unicuspidal rational curve in (a). Then
D_{Y}:=f_{*}^{-1}(D)
is a smooth rational curve on
Y
. Furthermore, if
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
are the coordinates on
E=\text{Ex}(f)\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then
D_{Y}\cap E=(u_{0}^{q}-u_{1}^{p}=0)
and
C_{Y}\cap E=(u_{0}u_{1}=0)
. As a result,
D_{Y}\cap C_{Y}=\varnothing
because they do not intersect outside of
E
. Thus,
C_{Y}
is not ample. Furthermore, we may compute

(C_{Y}^{2})=(C^{2})-(p+q)^{2}(E^{2})=\operatorname{vol}(-K_{X})-\frac{(p+q)^{2}}{pq}>0.

Hence,
C_{Y}
is big and nef.

Finally, we prove that (c) implies (a). Suppose that
C_{Y}
is big and nef but not ample, then there exists an irreducible curve
D_{Y}
on
Y
which does not intersect
C_{Y}
.

We claim that
(D_{Y}^{2})<0
. If
(D_{Y}^{2})\geq 0
, then
D_{Y}
is nef and hence semiample. Since
D_{Y}
is not numerically trivial, the Iitaka dimension of
D_{Y}
is positive. Thus,
|mD_{Y}|
contains a nontrivial pencil of divisors which dominate
Y
. However, since
(C_{Y}\cdot D_{Y})=0
,
C_{Y}
cannot intersect any element of
|mD_{Y}|
. This contradicts with the bigness of
C_{Y}
.

Let
D=f(D_{Y})
. We can compute

(C\cdot D)=(C_{Y}\cdot D_{Y})+\frac{p+q}{pq}\operatorname{ord}_{E}(D)=\frac{p+q}{pq}\operatorname{ord}_{E}(D).

Since
\gcd(p,q)=1
,
\operatorname{ord}_{E}(D)=kpq
for some integer
k\geq 1
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle-2

\displaystyle\leq(K_{Y}+D_{Y})\cdot D_{Y}=(f^{*}K_{X}+(p+q-1)E+D_{Y})\cdot D_{Y}

\displaystyle=(-C_{Y}-E+D_{Y})\cdot D_{Y}

\displaystyle=-E\cdot(f^{*}D-\operatorname{ord}_{E}(D)E)+(D_{Y}^{2})

\displaystyle=-k+(D_{Y}^{2})

Thus,
k=1
and
(D_{Y}^{2})\geq-1
. Since
(D_{Y}^{2})=(D^{2})-k^{2}pq
is an integer,
(D_{Y}^{2})=-1
. This gives

(C\cdot D)=p+q,\quad(D^{2})=pq-1,

as desired.
∎

3.
Special Valuations over Log Calabi-Yau Surface Pairs

Let
X
be a smooth del Pezzo surface and
C\sim-K_{X}
be an effective divisor on
X
such that
(X,C)
is log canonical.
The goal of this section is to answer the following question:

Question 3.1
.

Let
v\in\text{QM}(X,C)
be a quasi-monomial valuation over
X
. When is
v
special over
X
?

By adjunction,
C
is one of the following:

(i)

C
is an irreducible smooth curve.

(ii)

C
is an irreducible nodal rational curve with a unique node.

(iii)

C
consists of a circle of irreducible smooth rational curves.

In case (i),
\text{QM}(X,C)=\{\operatorname{ord}_{C}\}
and
\operatorname{ord}_{C}
is not a special valuation by Theorem
2.14
. For the rest of this section, we focus on case (ii) and (iii).

3.1.
Numerical criterion for special valuations

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano surface and
C\sim-K_{X}
be a nodal curve on
X
such that
(X,C)
is log canonical. Let
E
be an log canonical place of
(X,C)
. Let
f:Y\to X
be the projective birational morphism extracting the divisor
E
. If
f
is the identity map (i.e.,
E
is a divisor contained in
X
), let
C_{Y}=C-E
. Otherwise, let
C_{Y}
be the strict transform of
C
on
Y
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

E
is a special divisor over
X
.

(b)

There exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B
on
Y
such that
\operatorname{supp}(B)=\operatorname{supp}(C_{Y})
and
B\cdot F>0
for any component
F
of
\operatorname{supp}(C_{Y})
.

Proof.

We first show that (a) implies (b). Since
E
is a special divisor over
X
, by Theorem
2.14
, there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
such that
\lfloor D+E\rfloor=E
,
(Y,D+E)
is plt, and
A:=-(K_{Y}+D+E)
is ample. Since

0\sim f^{*}(K_{X}+C)=K_{Y}+E+C_{Y},

we have
C_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}A+D
. Write
D=D_{1}+D_{2}
where
\operatorname{supp}(D_{1})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(C_{Y})
and no component of
D_{2}
is contained in
\operatorname{supp}(C_{Y})
. Define
B=C_{Y}-D_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}A+D_{2}
. Then
B
is effective and
\operatorname{supp}(B)=C_{Y}
. Furthermore, for any irreducible component
F
of
\operatorname{supp}(C_{Y})
,

F\cdot B=F\cdot A+F\cdot D_{2}>0.

Next, we prove that (b) implies (a). Let
B
the the effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Y
satisfying (b). After rescaling we may assume that
\lfloor B\rfloor=0
. Since
B^{2}>0
,
B
is nef and hence semiample. Thus, there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
F
such that
B\cdot F=0
and
B-\epsilon F
is ample for any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. In particular,
E\not\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(F)
. Define
D=C_{Y}-B+\epsilon F\geq 0
. Then for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
,
\lfloor D+E\rfloor=E
,
(Y,D+E)
is plt, and
-(K_{Y}+D+E)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}B-\epsilon F
is ample. By Theorem
2.14
,
E
is a special divisor over
X
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano surface and
C\sim-K_{X}
be a nodal curve on
X
such that
(X,C)
is log canonical. Let
x\in C
be a node. For
t>0
, let
v_{t}
denote the quasi-monomial valuation centered at
x
with weight
(1,t)
along the two branches of
C
. For
t\in\mathbb{Q}
, let
E_{t}
denote the divisor over
X
corresponding to the divisorial valuation
v_{t}
, and let
Y_{t}\to X
be the birational morphism extracting the divisor
E_{t}
. Write
C=C_{1}+\cdots+C_{k}
where
C_{i}
is irreducible for
1\leq i\leq k
. Let
C_{i,t}
be the strict transform of
C_{i}
on
Y_{t}
.

Suppose that there exists an open interval
I
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}>0
such that
\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,t}
is ample for all
t\in I\cap\mathbb{Q}
. Then for all
t\in I
,
v_{t}
is a special valuation over
X
and the special degenerations of
X
induced by all such
v_{t}
’s are isomorphic to each other.

Proof.

Let
t_{0}\in I
and
t_{1},t_{2}\in I\cap\mathbb{Q}
such that
t_{1}<t_{0}<t_{2}
. Let
f:Y\to X
be the birational morphism which extracts
E_{t_{1}}
and
E_{t_{2}}
via weighted blow-ups.

Step 1.
In this step, we prove the following statement: for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
sufficiently close to
t_{0}
,
\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,Y}
is big and nef. Here,
C_{i,Y}
is the strict transform of
C_{i}
on
Y
.

Write
t_{1}=\frac{q_{1}}{p_{1}}
and
t_{2}=\frac{q_{2}}{p_{2}}
. By a toric computation, we have

\displaystyle(E_{t_{1}}^{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{q_{2}}{q_{1}(q_{2}p_{1}-p_{2}q_{1})},

\displaystyle(E_{t_{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}(q_{2}p_{1}-p_{2}q_{1})},

\displaystyle(E_{t_{1}}\cdot E_{t_{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{2}p_{1}-p_{2}q_{1}}.

•

Case 1:
C=C_{1}
is irreducible.

In this case, we have

f^{*}C=C_{Y}+(p_{1}+q_{1})E_{t_{1}}+(p_{2}+q_{2})E_{t_{2}},

so
(C_{Y}\cdot E_{t_{1}})=\frac{1}{q_{1}},(C_{Y}\cdot E_{t_{2}})=\frac{1}{p_{2}}
,
and

(C_{Y}^{2})=(C^{2})-\frac{p_{1}p_{2}+q_{1}q_{2}+2p_{2}q_{1}}{p_{2}q_{1}}=d-\frac{1}{t_{1}}-t_{2}-2.

For any
t\in I\cap\mathbb{Q}
, we have

(C_{t}^{2})=d-\frac{(1+t)^{2}}{t}>0.

Since the set
\{t>0:d-\frac{(1+t)^{2}}{t}>0\}
is an open interval, we have

d-\frac{(1+t_{0})^{2}}{t_{0}}=d-\frac{1}{t_{0}}-t_{0}-2>0

Thus,
(C_{Y}^{2})>0
for
t_{1},t_{2}
sufficiently close to
t_{0}
and hence
C_{Y}
is big and nef.

•

Case 2:
C
is reducible.

In this case, we may assume
x\in C_{1}\cap C_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle f^{*}C_{1}

\displaystyle=C_{1,Y}+p_{1}E_{t_{1}}+p_{2}E_{t_{2}},

\displaystyle f^{*}C_{2}

\displaystyle=C_{2,Y}+q_{1}E_{t_{1}}+q_{2}E_{t_{2}},

\displaystyle f^{*}C_{j}

\displaystyle=C_{j,Y},\text{ if }j\geq 3.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,Y}\right)\cdot C_{1,Y}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i}\right)\cdot C_{1}-\frac{a_{1}}{t_{1}}-a_{2},

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,Y}\right)\cdot C_{2,Y}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i}\right)\cdot C_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}t_{2},

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,Y}\right)\cdot C_{j,Y}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i}\right)\cdot C_{j},\text{ if }j\geq 3.

On the other hand, for any
t\in I\cap\mathbb{Q}
,

\displaystyle 0<\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,t}\right)\cdot C_{1,t}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i}\right)\cdot C_{1}-\frac{a_{1}}{t}-a_{2},

\displaystyle 0<\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,t}\right)\cdot C_{2,t}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i}\right)\cdot C_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}t,

\displaystyle 0<\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,t}\right)\cdot C_{j,t}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i}\right)\cdot C_{j},\text{ if }j\geq 3.

Thus,
\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,Y}
is big and nef for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in I\cap\mathbb{Q}
.

From now on, we fix a choice of
t_{1},t_{2}\in I\cap\mathbb{Q}
such that
\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,Y}
is big and nef.

Step 2.
In this step, we prove that if there exists an irreducible curve
D_{Y}
on
Y
such that
C_{i,Y}\cdot D_{Y}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, then
y\not\in D_{Y}
, where
y
is the unique intersection point of
E_{t_{1}}
and
E_{t_{2}}
.

Assume that
y\in D_{Y}
. By the Hodge index theorem,
(D_{Y}^{2})<0
. We may perform a sequence of blow-ups centered at
y
to obtain
g:Z\to Y
which satisfies the following properties:

•

Every exceptional divisor of
g
has the form
E_{t}
for some
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})\cap\mathbb{Q}
,

•

Every component of
E_{t_{1}}+E_{t_{2}}+\text{Ex}(g)
is either disjoint from
D_{Z}:=g^{-1}_{*}D_{Y}
, or intersects
D_{Z}
at smooth points of
Z
.

•

There exists
t^{\prime}\in[t_{1},t_{2}]\cap\mathbb{Q}
such that
E_{t^{\prime}}
and
D_{Z}
intersect at some smooth points of
Z
.

Furthermore, we have

g^{*}\left(K_{Y}+\sum_{i}C_{i,Y}+E_{t_{1}}+E_{t_{2}}\right)=K_{Z}+\sum_{i}C_{i,Z}+E_{t_{1}}+E_{t_{2}}+\text{Ex}(g)

and hence

\displaystyle-2\leq(K_{Z}+D_{Z})\cdot D_{Z}=(D_{Z}^{2})-(E_{t_{1}}+E_{t_{2}}+\text{Ex}(g))\cdot D_{Z}

This implies that

(E_{t_{1}}+E_{t_{2}}+\text{Ex}(g))\cdot D_{Z}<2

Note that for every component
F
of
E_{t_{1}}+E_{t_{2}}+\text{Ex}(g)
,
(F\cdot D_{Z})
is a nonnegative integer. As a result,
E_{t^{\prime}}
is the only component which intersects
D_{Z}
. After blowing down every component of
E_{t_{1}}+E_{t_{2}}+\text{Ex}(g)
except
E_{t^{\prime}}
, we obtain the variety
Y_{t^{\prime}}
. On
Y_{t^{\prime}}
, we have
(C_{i,t^{\prime}}\cdot D_{t^{\prime}})=0
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, where
D_{t^{\prime}}
is the image of
D_{Y}
. However, this contradicts with the assumption that
\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,t^{\prime}}
is ample.

Step 3.

By step 1 and 2, there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
F_{Y}
on
Y
not containing
y
, such that
\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,Y}-\epsilon F_{Y}
is ample for any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. After rescaling we may assume that
a_{i}\in(0,1)
for all
i
. Then

-\left(K_{Y}+\epsilon F_{Y}+\sum_{i}(1-a_{i})C_{i,Y}+E_{t_{1}}+E_{t_{2}}\right)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,Y}-\epsilon F_{Y}

is ample. Since
y\not\in\operatorname{supp}(F_{Y})
,
(Y,\epsilon F_{Y}+\sum_{i}(1-a_{i})C_{i,Y}+E_{t_{1}}+E_{t_{2}})
is qdlt for sufficiently small
\epsilon
. This implies that
(Y,E_{t_{1}}+E_{t_{2}})\to X
is a qdlt Fano type model. By Theorem
2.19
, for all
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
,
v_{t}
is special and induces isomorphic special degenerations of
X
.
∎

3.2.
Special divisors over
X

The goal of this subsection is to classify special divisorial valuations in
\text{QM}(X,C)
.

We first study special divisors in
\text{QM}(X,C)
in the case where every component of
C
is nef.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth del Pezzo surface of degree
d
. Let
C\sim-K_{X}
be normal crossing divisor on
X
. Let
x\in C
be a node. For
t>0
, let
v_{t}
denote the quasi-monomial valuation centered at
x
with weight
(1,t)
along the two branches of
C
. For
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, let
E_{t}
denote the divisor over
X
corresponding to the divisorial valuation
v_{t}
, and let
Y_{t}\to X
be the birational morphism extracting the divisor
E_{t}
. Assume that
C=C_{1}+\cdots+C_{k}
has
k
irreducible components and
C_{i}^{2}\geq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

(a)

Suppose
k=1
. Then
E_{t}
is special over
X
if and only if
d\geq 5
and
t\in I_{d}:=\left(\frac{d-2-\sqrt{d^{2}-4d}}{2},\frac{d-2+\sqrt{d^{2}-4d}}{2}\right)
.

(b)

Suppose
k=2
. Then
E_{t}
is special over
X
if and only if
d\geq 5
.

(c)

Suppose
k\geq 3
. Then
E_{t}
is special over
X
for all
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
.

Proof.

(a) By Proposition
3.2
,
E_{t}
is special over
X
if and only if
C_{t}
is big and nef. We may compute

C_{t}^{2}=C^{2}-\frac{(1+t)^{2}}{t}=d-\frac{(1+t)^{2}}{t}.

Thus,
C_{t}^{2}>0
if and only if
d\geq 5
and
t\in I_{d}
.

(b) When
k=2
,
C_{1}\cdot C_{2}=2
and
C_{1}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}=d-4
. For any
a_{1},a_{2}>0
, we can compute

\displaystyle(a_{1}C_{1,t}+a_{2}C_{2,t})\cdot C_{1,t}

\displaystyle=a_{1}\left(C_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{t}\right)+a_{2},

\displaystyle(a_{1}C_{1,t}+a_{2}C_{2,t})\cdot C_{2,t}

\displaystyle=a_{1}+a_{2}(C_{2}^{2}-t).

If
d=4
, then
C_{1}^{2}=C_{2}^{2}=0
. This gives

(a_{1}C_{1,t}+a_{2}C_{2,t})\cdot(tC_{1,t}+C_{2,t})=0.

By Proposition
3.2
,
E_{t}
cannot be a special divisor over
X
.

Now assume that
d\geq 5
. Then at least one of
C_{1}^{2}
and
C_{2}^{2}
is positive. If
C_{1}^{2}>0
, pick
a_{1}=t
and
a_{2}=1-\epsilon
for a sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. Then

\displaystyle(a_{1}C_{1,t}+a_{2}C_{2,t})\cdot C_{1,t}

\displaystyle=a_{1}\left(C_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{t}\right)+a_{2}=tC_{1}^{2}-\epsilon>0,

\displaystyle(a_{1}C_{1,t}+a_{2}C_{2,t})\cdot C_{2,t}

\displaystyle=a_{1}+a_{2}(C_{2}^{2}-t)=a_{2}C_{2}^{2}+\epsilon>0.

By Proposition
3.2
,
E_{t}
is special over
X
. The proof is similar in the case
C_{2}^{2}>0
.

(c) Suppose
k\geq 3
. We may assume that
x\in C_{1}\cap C_{2}
and
C_{i}\cdot C_{i+1}=1
for all
1\leq i\leq k
(where
C_{k+1}=C_{1}
). Pick
a_{1}=a_{2}=\epsilon
for a sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
and
a_{i}=1
for all
3\leq i\leq k
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,t}\right)\cdot C_{1,t}

\displaystyle=a_{1}\left(C_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{t}\right)+a_{k}>0,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,t}\right)\cdot C_{2,t}

\displaystyle=a_{2}(C_{2}^{2}-t)+a_{3}>0,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,t}\right)\cdot C_{j,t}

\displaystyle\geq a_{j-1}+a_{j+1}>0,\text{ for all }j\geq 3.

Thus,
E_{t}
is special over
X
by Proposition
3.2
.
∎

In the case where some component of
C
is not nef, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth del Pezzo surface. Let
C\sim-K_{X}
be a nodal curve on
X
such that
(X,C)
is log canonical. Let
C_{0},C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}
be irreducible components of
C
with
k\geq 2
. Suppose that
C_{0}^{2}=-1
and
\pi:X\to X^{\prime}
is the birational morphism which contracts
C_{1}
. Let
E
be a divisor over
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

E
is an log canonical place of
(X,C)
and
E
is special over
X
.

(b)

E
is an log canonical place of
(X^{\prime},C^{\prime}:=\pi(C))
and
E
is special over
X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Case 1:

E
is contained in
X
. If
E=C_{0}
, then (a) and (b) are equivalent by Proposition
3.2
. Thus, it suffices to consider the case
E=C_{i}
for some
i\geq 1
.

Suppose that
C_{i}
is special over
X
. By Proposition
3.2
, there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B
such that
\operatorname{supp}(B)=\operatorname{supp}(C-C_{i})
and
B\cdot C_{j}>0
for any
0\leq j\leq k
and
j\neq i
. Let
B^{\prime}=\pi_{*}B
. Then
B^{\prime}
is big and nef,
\operatorname{supp}(B^{\prime})=\operatorname{supp}(C^{\prime}-C_{i}^{\prime})
and
B^{\prime}\cdot C_{j}^{\prime}\geq B\cdot C_{j}>0
for
1\leq j\leq k
and
j\neq i
. Thus,
C_{i}^{\prime}
is special over
X^{\prime}
by Proposition
3.2
.

Suppose that
C_{i}^{\prime}
is special over
X^{\prime}
. By Proposition
3.2
, there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{supp}(B^{\prime})=\operatorname{supp}(C^{\prime}-C_{i}^{\prime})
and
B^{\prime}\cdot C_{j}^{\prime}>0
for any
0\leq j\leq k
and
j\neq i
. Let
B=\pi^{*}B^{\prime}-\epsilon C_{0}
for some sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. Then
B
is effective and
\operatorname{supp}(B)=\operatorname{supp}(C-C_{i})
. Furthermore, we have

B\cdot C_{0}=-\epsilon C_{0}^{2}>0

and for
1\leq j\leq k
,
j\neq i
,

B\cdot C_{j}=B^{\prime}\cdot C_{j}^{\prime}-\epsilon C_{0}\cdot C_{j}>0.

By Proposition
3.2
,
C_{i}
is special over
X
.

Case 2:

E
is not contained in
X
.

We first prove that (a) implies (b). Let
Y\to X
be the birational morphism extracting
E
. For
1\leq i\leq k
, let
C_{i,Y}
be the strict transform of
C_{i}
on
Y
. By Proposition
3.2
, there exists
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}>0
such that
(\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i,Y})\cdot C_{i,Y}>0
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Let
f:Y\to Y^{\prime}
be the birational morphism which contracts
C_{1,Y}
. Let
C_{i,Y^{\prime}}
be the image of
C_{i,Y}
for
2\leq i\leq k
. Then

f^{*}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,Y^{\prime}}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,Y}+a_{1}^{\prime}C_{1,Y}

for some
a_{1}^{\prime}>0
. Thus, for
2\leq i\leq k
,

\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,Y^{\prime}}\right)\cdot C_{i,Y^{\prime}}\geq\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,Y}\right)\cdot C_{i,Y}>0.

Since
\sum_{i=2}^{k}C_{i,Y^{\prime}}
is the strict transform of
\pi(C)
on
Y^{\prime}
, (b) follows from Proposition
3.2
.

Next, we prove that (b) implies (a). Take
Y^{\prime}
and
Y
as in the previous paragraph. By Proposition
3.2
, there exists
a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}>0
such that

\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,Y^{\prime}}\right)\cdot C_{i,Y^{\prime}}>0

for all
2\leq i\leq k
. Let
a_{1}>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,Y}=f^{*}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,Y^{\prime}}\right).

Then, for any
2\leq i\leq k
and sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
,

\left((a_{1}-\epsilon)C_{1,Y}+\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,Y}\right)\cdot C_{i,Y}=\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,Y^{\prime}}\right)\cdot C_{i,Y^{\prime}}-\epsilon C_{1,Y}\cdot C_{i,Y}>0.

We also have

\left((a_{1}-\epsilon)C_{1,Y}+\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}C_{i,Y}\right)\cdot C_{1,Y}=-\epsilon C_{1,Y}^{2}>0.

This proves (a) by Propostion
3.2
.
∎

Throughout this subsection, we consider the following set-up.

Set-up.

along the two branches of

. For

, let

denote the divisor over

, and let

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose

Proof.

Case 2:

a_{2}=1-\epsilon

\displaystyle f_{2}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=\epsilon\left(C_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{t_{0}}\right)+1>0,

\displaystyle f_{2}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=\epsilon(C_{2}^{2}-t_{0})+1>0,

(p,q)
-unicuspidal curve well-formed with respect to

.

Suppose that

We first show that (b) implies (a). Let

(i_{2},j_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
such that
v_{t}(f)=i_{2}+tj_{2}
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{2}]
.

Proof.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}
be the analytic coordinates on
X
in a neighborhood of
x
, such that
C
is cut out by
x_{1}x_{2}=0
. Write
f=\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}c_{ij}x_{1}^{i}x_{2}^{j}
. Denote
\alpha=v_{t_{0}}(f)=\min\{i+t_{0}j:c_{ij}\neq 0\}
. Let
D
be the divisor on
X
corresponding to
f
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}
denote the strict transform of
D\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
on
\mathcal{Y}
and
D_{S_{1}}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}|_{S_{1}}
. We define
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}^{m}}
and
D_{S_{3}}
similarly. Then
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}^{m}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{A}^{1}}-mK_{\mathcal{X}^{m}}
and
D_{S_{3}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-mK_{S_{3}}
.

Recall that
S_{1}\cong\mathbb{P}(1,p,q)
with homogeneous coordinates
u_{0},u_{1},u_{2}
. By a local computation near
x_{1}
, we can write the equation of
D_{S_{1}}
as

D_{S_{1}}=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in\mathbb{N}\\
i+t_{0}j=\alpha\end{subarray}}c_{ij}u_{1}^{i}u_{2}^{j}=0\right).

By Theorem
4.3
(d),
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{X}^{m}
are isomorphic in a neighborhood of the closure of
\{x\}\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}-\{0\})
. Thus, abusing the notation, we may still write
L_{1,S_{3}}=(u_{1}=0)
and
L_{2,S_{3}}=(u_{2}=0)
in a neighborhood of
x_{3}
. Thus, the local equation of
D_{S_{3}}
has the same form as the local equation of
D_{S_{1}}
.

Furthermore, by the diagram (
4.2
),
S_{3}
is the ample model of
X^{\prime}
. Thus,
D_{S_{3}}
is identified with
\overline{f}
by the isomorphism
H^{0}(S_{3},-mK_{S_{3}})\cong H^{0}(X^{\prime},-mK_{X^{\prime}})
. Therefore,

\overline{v_{t}}(\overline{f})=\overline{v_{t}}(D_{S_{3}})=\min\{i+tj:c_{ij}\neq 0,i+t_{0}j=\alpha\}\geq\min\{i+tj:c_{ij}\neq 0\}=v_{t}(f).

Among all pairs of integers
(i,j)
with
i+t_{0}j=\alpha
and
c_{ij}\neq 0
, let
(i_{1},j_{1})
be the pair with the largest value in

(i_{2},j_{2})
be the pair with the smallest value in

sufficiently close to

i_{1}+tj_{1}=\min\{i+tj:c_{ij}\neq 0\}.

This implies that

v_{t}(f)=i_{1}+tj_{1}=\overline{v_{t}}(\overline{f}).

Similarly, for
t>t_{0}
sufficiently close to

, we have

v_{t}(f)=i_{2}+tj_{2}=\overline{v_{t}}(\overline{f}).

This completes the proof of this lemma.

Proof of Theorem
4.13
.

It suffices consider the case
t>t_{0}
, since the case
t=t_{0}
follows from the fact that
v_{t_{0}}(f)=\overline{v_{t_{0}}}(\overline{f})
for all
f
, and the case
t<t_{0}
is analogous.

Step 1.
We set up some notations.
By Theorem
2.19
and Theorem
4.3
(f), for every
t>t_{0}
,

\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}R_{m}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{\bullet}H^{0}(X^{\prime},-mK_{X^{\prime}})

is a finitely generated algebra. Furthermore, one may choose a set of elements

\overline{f_{1}}\in R^{\prime}_{m_{1}}\ldots,\overline{f_{s}}\in R^{\prime}_{m_{s}}

which is independent of
t
, such that their images generate
\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{\bullet}R_{m}^{\prime}
. We may further assume that
\overline{f_{k}}\in\operatorname{gr}_{E}^{\alpha_{k}}R_{m_{k}}
and pick a lifting
f_{k}\in R_{m_{k}}
of
\overline{f_{k}}
.

By Lemma
4.14
, there exists
t_{2}>t_{0}
such that for all
k=1,2,\ldots,s
and
t\in[t_{0},t_{2}]
,

v_{t}(f_{k})=\overline{v_{t}}(\overline{f_{k}})=i_{k}+tj_{k}.

This implies that the local equation of
f_{k}
near
x
can be written as

f_{k}=x_{1}^{i_{k}}x_{2}^{j_{k}}+g_{k}(x_{1},x_{2})

where
g_{k}
is a formal power series in
x_{1},x_{2}
such that every monomial
x_{1}^{i}x_{2}^{j}
with nonzero coefficient in
g_{k}
satisfies

i+tj>i_{k}+tj_{k}=v_{t}(f_{k}).

Similarly, in a local coordinate
u_{1},u_{2}
on
S_{3}
, we can write the equation of
\overline{f_{k}}
(i.e., the equation of the corresponding divisor on
S_{3}
) as

\overline{f_{k}}=u_{1}^{i_{k}}u_{2}^{j_{k}}+\overline{g_{k}}(u_{1},u_{2})

where
\overline{g_{k}}
is a formal power series in
u_{1},u_{2}
such that every monomial
u_{1}^{i}u_{2}^{j}
with nonzero coefficient in
\overline{g_{k}}
satisfies

i+tj>i_{k}+tj_{k}=\overline{v_{t}}(\overline{f_{k}}).

Step 2.
We prove the following statement. Let
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{3}
be the submonoid generated by
\{(i_{k},j_{k},m_{k}):1\leq k\leq s\}
. Then for any
t\in(t_{0},t_{2})
, there is an isomorphism

\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{\bullet}R_{m}^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{C}[M]

as graded algebras, where the grading in
\mathbb{C}[M]
is given by the third coordinate.

Consider the ring homomorphism

\phi:\mathbb{C}[M]\to\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{\bullet}R_{m}^{\prime}

determined by

1\cdot(i_{k},j_{k},m_{k})\mapsto\text{ the image of }\overline{f_{k}}\text{ in }\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{\bullet}R_{m_{k}}^{\prime}

We need to show that
\phi
is well-defined, i.e., if there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{s},b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{k=1}^{s}a_{k}(i_{k},j_{k},m_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{s}b_{k}(i_{k},j_{k},m_{k})

as elements of
M
, then

\overline{v_{t}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{s}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{a_{k}}\right)=\overline{v_{t}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{s}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{b_{k}}\right)<\overline{v_{t}}\left(\prod_{k}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{a_{k}}-\prod_{k}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{b_{k}}\right),

so that
\prod_{k}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{a_{k}}
and
\prod_{k}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{b_{k}}

have the same image in
\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{\bullet}R^{\prime}
.

Let
i^{\prime}=\sum_{k}a_{k}i_{k}
and
j^{\prime}=\sum_{k}a_{k}j_{k}
. Then by step 1, we can write

\prod_{k=1}^{s}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{a_{k}}=u_{1}^{i^{\prime}}u_{2}^{j^{\prime}}+\overline{h_{a}}(u_{1},u_{2})

such that for any monomial
u_{1}^{i}u_{2}^{j}
in
\overline{h_{a}}
,

i+tj>i^{\prime}+tj^{\prime}=\overline{v_{t}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{s}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{a_{k}}\right).

Similarly, we can write

\prod_{k=1}^{s}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{b_{k}}=u_{1}^{i^{\prime}}u_{2}^{j^{\prime}}+\overline{h_{b}}(u_{1},u_{2}).

Then

\overline{v_{t}}\left(\prod_{k}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{a_{k}}-\prod_{k}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{b_{k}}\right)=\overline{v_{t}}\left(\overline{h_{a}}-\overline{h_{b}}\right)>i^{\prime}+tj^{\prime}=\overline{v_{t}}\left(\prod_{k}{\overline{f_{k}}}^{a_{k}}\right),

as desired. This proves that
\phi
is well-defined.

Next, we show that
\phi
is injective. Suppose that an element

d=\sum_{(i,j,m)\in M}c_{i,j,m}(i,j,m)\in\mathbb{C}[M]

is mapped to 0 under
\phi
. Since each
1\cdot(i,j,m)
is mapped to an element in
\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{i+tj}R_{m}^{\prime}
, we only need to consider the case when
m=m_{0}
and
i+tj=v
are both fixed, so that

d=\sum_{(i,j,m_{0})\in M:i+tj=v}c_{i,j,m_{0}}(i,j,m_{0}).

Then,
\phi(d)
is the image of the following element in
\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{v}R_{m}^{\prime}
:

\sum_{(i,j,m_{0})\in M:i+tj=v}c_{i,j,m_{0}}(u_{1}^{i}u_{2}^{j}+h_{i,j}(u_{1},u_{2})),

where
h_{i,j}
is a power series in
u_{1},u_{2}
such that any monomial in
h_{i,j}
with nonzero coefficient has larger
\overline{v_{t}}
-valuation than
v
. Since
\phi(d)=0
, we have

\sum_{(i,j,m_{0})\in M:i+tj=v}c_{i,j,m_{0}}u_{1}^{i}u_{2}^{j}=0,

which implies that
c_{i,j,m_{0}}=0
for all
(i,j)
with
i+tj=v
. Therefore,
d=0
and
\phi
is injective.

Finally,
\phi
is surjective because the images of
\overline{f_{1}},\ldots,\overline{f_{s}}
generate
\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{\bullet}R_{m}^{\prime}
.

Step 3.
We prove that for all
t\in(t_{0},t_{2})
,

\mathbb{C}[M]\cong\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{gr}_{v_{t}}^{\bullet}R_{m}.

We consider the ring homomorphism

\varphi:\mathbb{C}[M]\to\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{gr}_{v_{t}}^{\bullet}H^{0}(X,-mK_{X})

determined by

1\cdot(i_{k},j_{k},m_{k})\mapsto\text{ the image of }{f_{k}}\text{ in }\operatorname{gr}_{{v_{t}}}^{\bullet}R_{m_{k}}.

Similar to the argument in step 2 and using Lemma
4.14
, one can show that
\varphi
is well-defined, graded, and injective.

By step 2,
\mathbb{C}[M]\cong\bigoplus_{m\in r\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{\bullet}R_{m}^{\prime}
, so we have an equality

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[M]_{m}

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{gr}_{\overline{v_{t}}}^{\bullet}R_{m}^{\prime}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}R_{m}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}R_{m}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{gr}_{v_{t}}^{\bullet}R_{m}

for every
m\in r\mathbb{N}
. Thus,
\varphi
must be an isomorphism. This finishes the proof of this theorem.
∎

The THAW protocol: A zero-cost traditional biomechanical adjunct to enhance clinical Nasya practices
Authors: Ram Rahim GSI MSG, Puneet Maheshwari
Published: 2026

Dear Editor

We read with great interest the study by Nair et al. regarding the “Clinical consensus statements on Marsha Nasya – A feasibility study towards developing clinical practice guidelines of therapeutic procedures in Ayurveda,” published in the March 2026 issue of J-AIM [1]. The authors provide a seminal framework for standardizing Marsha Nasya (MN), a vital Panchakarma therapy. We were particularly struck by the panel's consensus on preparatory procedures (Purvakarma), such as sudation (Sweda), and the emphasis on the need for people-centered frameworks to ensure equitable access to Ayurvedic therapies [1].

In response to the authors' call for “practical guidelines” that balance consistency with clinical flexibility, we wish to introduce a novel, zero-cost biomechanical and thermal adjunct rooted in the ancient Indian system of medicine: the THAW Protocol (Trans-palatal Heating of Airways with Water). In THAW, the person holds mouth full of warm water for few minutes while taking bath. This maneuver serves as a logical extension of traditional sudation and clearing techniques, offering a “low-barrier” conservative therapy that can be utilized independently or as an adjunct to clinical Nasya.

Physiological synergy with Marsha Nasya

Traditional Nasya involves the administration of unctuous substances into the nasal passage [1]. While classical in-clinic preparatory sudation (Sweda) remains the standard, we propose the THAW Protocol as a patient-driven, home-care adjunct modelled on the biophysical principles of Ushna Jala Gandusha. By utilising the hard palate as an internal heat sink, it provides localized thermal conduction across the palatine bones. Crucially, this localized reduction in mucus viscosity is intended specifically to complement Shodhana (cleansing) Marsha Nasya by facilitating the aerodynamic shearing of stagnant post-nasal secretions; it is not intended for Shamana or Brimhana protocols where liquefaction is not a clinical objective:

1.Thermal Rheology via Palatal Conduction: The hard palate forms the anatomical floor of the nasal cavity. Direct conductive heat transfer across the palatine bones raises the local tissue temperature of the nasal mucosa more consistently than external steam [2,3]. This decrease in mucus viscosity acts as a localized, non-invasive preparatory Sweda, potentially improving the absorption and efficacy of subsequent medicinal oils administered during Nasya (Table-1).

Addressing global health inequities

As highlighted by recent research into healthcare disparities [7], access to specialized rhinologic and Ayurvedic care (such as the availability of a Droni or specialized Nasya chair) remains uneven [1]. The THAW Protocol requires no equipment and carries no financial burden, making it a “people-centered” innovation that aligns with the WHO Traditional Medicine Strategy 2025–2034 for equitable global health [8].

By validating such zero-cost biomechanical maneuvers, we can empower patients to manage chronic rhinosinusitis symptoms between clinical treatments, thereby improving adherence to the long-term lifestyle modifications discussed by the consensus panel [1].

The THAW protocol is introduced here as a biomechanical hypothesis intended to bridge traditional integrative practices with modern respiratory dynamics. It is not presented as a validated clinical replacement for classical Purva Karma. Given its zero-cost and equipment-free nature, it serves as a scalable option for global health equity. We strongly encourage future well-designed, prospective clinical and experimental trials to evaluate its quantitative efficacy, mucosal safety, and long-term outcomes in chronic rhinosinusitis management.

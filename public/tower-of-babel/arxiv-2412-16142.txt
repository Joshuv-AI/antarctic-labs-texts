The Classical Super-Rotation Infrared Triangle Classical Logarithmic Soft Theorem as Conservation Law in Gravity
Sangmin Choi, Alok Laddha, Andrea Puhm

Abstract
The universality of gravitational scattering at low energies and large distances encoded in soft theorems and memory effects can be understood from symmetries. In four-dimensional asymptotically flat spacetimes the infinite enhancement of translations, extending the Poincaré group to the BMS group, is the symmetry underlying Weinberg’s soft graviton theorem and the gravitational displacement memory effect. Beyond this leading infrared triangle, loop corrections alter their nature by introducing logarithms in the soft expansion and late time tails to the memory, and this persists in the classical limit. In this work we give the first complete description of an ‘infrared triangle’ where the long-range nature of gravitational interactions is accounted for. Building on earlier results [ 1 ] where we derived a novel conservation law associated to the infinite dimensional enhancement of Lorentz transformations to superrotations, we prove here its validity to all orders in the gravitational coupling and show that it implies the classical logarithmic soft graviton theorem of Saha-Sahoo-Sen [ 2 ] . We furthermore extend the formula for the displacement memory and its tail from particles to fields, thus completing the classical superrotation infrared triangle.

Introduction and summary

At low energies and large distances gravitational scattering exhibits universal behavior in the form of soft theorems and memory effects that can be traced to an underlying asymptotic symmetry.
The infrared (IR) triangle is an abstraction that encapsulates this universality of infrared physics in classical and quantum scattering. Until recently, complete infrared triangles – where all three corners and the connection between them is understood – have only been established for tree-level scattering processes. Loop effects associated to the long-range nature of gravitational interactions crucially modify these infrared relations through novel soft theorems with logarithmic dependence on the energy together with late-time tails in the gravitational field that give rise to so-called tail memory effects. Our goal is to give a first-principles derivation of the classical logarithmic soft graviton theorem from infrared-finite conservation laws in Einstein gravity coupled to massive matter. We will show that these conservation laws arise from superrotation symmetry.

5
Symplectic structure and charges in gravity

For the asymptotics of the matter and radiative fields discussed in section

\Omega_{i^{+}\cup{\mathcal{I}}^{+}}(\delta,\delta^{\prime})=\Omega^{\rm mat}_{i^{+}}(\delta,\delta^{\prime})+\Omega^{\rm rad}_{{\mathcal{I}}^{+}}(\delta,\delta^{\prime}).

The more relaxed fall-offs of the fields in the form of late-time logs and tails will lead to infrared divergences in the symplectic structure which we will regularize.

\Omega_{i^{+}\cup\cal I^{+}}(\delta,\delta_{Y})=\delta Q_{+}[Y].

Again we focus on the future, but a similar analysis can be repeated in the past.
Our final goal is to extract conservation laws that account for the long-range nature of the interactions and match onto the logarithmic soft graviton theorem.
Moreover, we will show that our new conservation laws are exact to all orders in the gravitational coupling

5.1
Matter symplectic structure and hard charge

In the following we derive the (asymptotic) symplectic structure on the space of solutions of a massive real scalar field minimal coupled to Einstein gravity

10

To see this, consider the leading gravitational correction to the symplectic structure of the asymptotically free field

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{m^{2}\kappa\overset{\ln}{h}_{\tau\tau}b_{0}}{2imn!}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k(iH)^{k-1}}{(1+iH)^{k-n+1}}.

(209)

After applying the derivatives
{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
and
{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}
, we find that each series is a Taylor expansion that reduces to a simple closed form:

\displaystyle b_{1,n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2imn!}[n(n-1)(iH)^{n-2}+2n(iH)^{n-1}+2(iH)^{n}]({\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}H)({\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}H)b_{0}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{n!}[(iH)^{n}+n(iH)^{n-1}]\left(\frac{1}{m}({\partial}_{\alpha}H){\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2m}({\mathcal{D}}^{2}H)-i\kappa\overset{0}{h}_{\tau\alpha}({\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}H)-\frac{i}{2}m\kappa\overset{\ln}{h}_{\tau\tau}\right)b_{0}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(iH)^{n}}{2imn!}\left({\mathcal{D}}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}-5iH+H^{2}-im\kappa k^{\alpha\beta}\overset{-1}{h_{\alpha\beta}}+m^{2}\kappa\overset{2}{h}_{\tau\tau}-2im\kappa\overset{0}{h}_{\tau\alpha}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}\right)b_{0}.

(210)

Now we use this expression to derive the large-
\tau
expansion of the relevant matter energy-momentum tensor components that is valid to all orders in the coupling constant.
The matter energy-momentum tensor is given by

T^{\rm matt}_{\mu\nu}={\partial}_{\mu}\varphi{\partial}_{\nu}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}(g_{\mu\nu}+\kappa h_{\mu\nu})\left[(g^{\sigma\lambda}-\kappa h^{\sigma\lambda}+\kappa^{2}h^{\sigma\kappa}h_{\kappa}^{\lambda}+O(h^{3})){\partial}_{\sigma}\varphi{\partial}_{\lambda}\varphi+m^{2}\varphi^{2}\right],

(211)

where
O(h^{3})
represents terms with three or more graviton factors.
Plugging the expansions (
197
) and (
198
), we find that the energy-momentum tensor has the following large-
\tau
expansions

\displaystyle T^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln\tau)^{n}}{\tau^{3}}\overset{3,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln\tau)^{n}}{\tau^{4}}\overset{4,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}+\cdots,

(212)

\displaystyle T^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln\tau)^{n}}{\tau^{3}}\overset{3,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}+\cdots,

\displaystyle T^{\rm matt}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln\tau)^{n}}{\tau^{2}}\overset{2,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\alpha\beta}+\cdots.

The coefficients take the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overset{3,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}&=2m^{2}s^{(1)}_{n},\\
\overset{2,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\alpha\beta}&=mk_{\alpha\beta}(2Hs^{(1)}_{n}+s^{(3)}_{n}),\end{split}\qquad\begin{split}\overset{3,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}&=ms^{(2)}_{n,\alpha},\\
\overset{4,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}&=-2mHs^{(1)}_{n}+ms^{(3)}_{n}+2m^{2}s^{(4)}_{n},\end{split}

(213)

given in terms of the four finite series

\displaystyle\begin{split}s^{(1)}_{n}&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{0,k}^{*}b_{0,n-k},\\
s^{(3)}_{n}&=i\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}(n+1-2k)b_{0,k}^{*}b_{0,n+1-k}\end{split}\qquad\begin{split}s^{(2)}_{n,\alpha}&=i\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{0,n-k}^{*}{\partial}_{\alpha}b_{0,k}+\text{c.c.},,\\
s^{(4)}_{n}&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{1,k}b_{0,n-k}^{*}+\text{c.c.}\end{split}

(214)

Let us evaluate these one by one:

•

s^{(1)}_{n}
and
s^{(2)}_{n,\alpha}
:
We find using the expression (
204
) that both are simple binomial expansions of the form
(1-1)^{n-l}=\delta_{n,l}
, that reduce to either
\delta_{n,0}
or
\delta_{n,1}
:

\displaystyle s^{(1)}_{n}

\displaystyle=|b_{0}|^{2}\delta_{n,0},

(215)

\displaystyle s^{(2)}_{n}

\displaystyle=-2({\partial}_{\alpha}H)|b_{0}|^{2}\delta_{n,1}+i(b_{0}^{*}{\partial}_{\alpha}b_{0}-b_{0}{\partial}_{\alpha}b_{0}^{*})\delta_{n,0}.

•

s^{(3)}_{n}
:
With (
204
), the expression for
s_{3}
can be written as a sum of two binomial expansions,

\displaystyle s^{(3)}_{n}

\displaystyle=i(iH)^{n+1}|b_{0}|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\frac{(n+1-2k)(-1)^{k}}{k!(n+1-k)!}

(216)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{n!}(iH)^{n+1}|b_{0}|^{2}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{k}(-1)^{k}+2\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\right].

The first term in square brackets is
\delta_{n+1,0}
which is identically zero since
n
is non-negative.
The second term is simply
2\delta_{n,0}
.
Thus,

s^{(3)}_{n}=-2H|b_{0}|^{2}\delta_{n,0}.

(217)

•

s^{(4)}_{n}
:
This one involves both
b_{1,n}
and
b_{0,n}
.
Using (
206
) and (
204
), we find that each term that appear in the expression for
s^{(4)}_{n}
is a simple binomial expansion that is proportional to either
\delta_{n,2}
,
\delta_{n,1}
or
\delta_{n,0}
, and that the terms proportional to
\delta_{n,2}
cancel out.
We end up with

\displaystyle s^{(4)}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta_{n,0}}{2m}\left[ib_{0}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{2}b_{0}+5H|b_{0}|^{2}+m\kappa k^{\alpha\beta}\overset{-1}{h_{\alpha\beta}}|b_{0}|^{2}+2m\kappa\overset{0}{h}_{\tau\alpha}b_{0}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}b_{0}+\text{c.c.}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{m}\left(\delta_{n,0}+\delta_{n,1}\right)\left[({\partial}_{\alpha}H)(b_{0}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}b_{0}+b_{0}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}b_{0}^{*})+({\mathcal{D}}^{2}H)|b_{0}|^{2}\right].

(218)

Putting the results together, we obtain

\displaystyle\overset{3,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}

\displaystyle=2m^{2}b_{0}^{*}b_{0}\delta_{n,0},

(219)

\displaystyle\overset{3,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}

\displaystyle=-m^{2}\kappa({\partial}_{\alpha}\overset{1}{h}_{\tau\tau})b_{0}^{*}b_{0}\delta_{n,1}+im(b_{0}^{*}{\partial}_{\alpha}b_{0}-b_{0}{\partial}_{\alpha}b_{0}^{*})\delta_{n,0},

(220)

\displaystyle\overset{2,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=0,

(221)

and

\displaystyle\overset{4,n}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}

\displaystyle=-m\delta_{n,0}\left[i(b_{0}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{2}b_{0}-b_{0}{\mathcal{D}}^{2}b_{0}^{*})+m\kappa(7\overset{1}{h}_{\tau\tau}+2k^{\alpha\beta}\overset{-1}{h_{\alpha\beta}})|b_{0}|^{2}+2m\kappa\overset{0}{h}_{\tau\alpha}(b_{0}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}b_{0}+b_{0}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}b_{0}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+m^{2}\kappa\left(\delta_{n,0}+\delta_{n,1}\right)\left[({\partial}_{\alpha}\overset{1}{h}_{\tau\tau})(b_{0}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}b_{0}+b_{0}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}b_{0}^{*})+({\mathcal{D}}^{2}\overset{1}{h}_{\tau\tau})|b_{0}|^{2}\right].

(222)

In particular, combining these with the falloffs (
199
) of the graviton energy-momentum tensor, we find that the total energy-momentum tensor satisfies

0=\overset{4,n}{T}{}_{\tau\tau}=\overset{3,n}{T}{}_{\tau\alpha}=\overset{2,n}{T}{}_{\alpha\beta}\qquad\text{for all $n\geq 2$}.

(223)

These are the modes that source the graviton modes
\overset{2,n\geq 2}{h_{\tau\tau}}
,
\overset{1,n\geq 2}{h_{\tau\alpha}}
,
\overset{0,n\geq 2}{h_{\alpha\beta}}
with two or more powers of
\ln\tau
at the next-to-leading order in
1/\tau
; the fact that they vanish justifies the graviton falloff condition (
198
).

Therefore, to all orders in the coupling constant, the large-
\tau
behavior of the energy-momentum tensor components
T_{\tau\tau}
and
T_{\tau\alpha}
is given by

\displaystyle T^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau^{3}}\overset{3}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}+\frac{\ln\tau}{\tau^{4}}\overset{4,\ln}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}+\frac{1}{\tau^{4}}\overset{4}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}+\cdots,

(224)

\displaystyle T^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln\tau}{\tau^{3}}\overset{3,\ln}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}+\frac{1}{\tau^{3}}\overset{3}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}+\cdots,

\displaystyle T^{\rm matt}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\overset{2}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\alpha\beta}+\cdots

where

\displaystyle\overset{3}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}

\displaystyle=2m^{2}b_{0}^{*}b_{0}\delta_{n,0},

(225)

\displaystyle\overset{3,\ln}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}

\displaystyle=-m^{2}\kappa({\partial}_{\alpha}\overset{1}{h}_{\tau\tau})b_{0}^{*}b_{0},

(226)

\displaystyle\overset{3}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}

\displaystyle=im(b_{0}^{*}{\partial}_{\alpha}b_{0}-b_{0}{\partial}_{\alpha}b_{0}^{*}),

(227)

\displaystyle\overset{2}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=0.

(228)

The subleading coefficients
\overset{4,\ln}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}
,
\overset{4}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}
are given by the coefficients of
\delta_{n,1}
and
\delta_{n,0}
in (
222
) respectively.
What is important for us is the fact that
T{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\tau}
does not develop higher powers of logs of the form
\tau^{-4}(\ln\tau)^{n}
with
n\geq 2
, to all orders in the coupling; their explicit form is not of interest in this paper.

Logarithmic hard charge

The logarithmic hard charge is given by

Q^{(\ln)}_{H,+}[\bar{Y}]=\int_{i^{+}}d^{3}y\,\bar{Y}^{\alpha}\overset{3,\ln}{T_{\tau\alpha}}.

(229)

The energy-momentum tensor has matter and graviton contributions,

T_{\tau\alpha}=T^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}+T^{h}_{\tau\alpha}.

(230)

The graviton part (
45
) is of order
O(\kappa^{2})
, and goes as
\tau^{-3}
,

T^{h}_{\tau\alpha}=\frac{1}{\tau^{3}}\overset{3}{T}{}^{h}_{\tau\alpha}+\cdots,

(231)

so it does not contribute to the log hard charge.
The matter part takes the form (
226
), which implies that the log hard charge is given by

Q_{H,+}^{(\ln)}[\bar{Y}]=-m^{2}\kappa\int_{i^{+}}d^{3}y\,\bar{Y}^{\alpha}({\partial}_{\alpha}\overset{1}{h}_{\tau\tau})b_{0}^{*}b_{0}.

(232)

This expression, which is exact to all orders in
\kappa
, is in agreement with (
140
) and (
141
), which demonstrates that the hard log charge of gravity is one-loop exact.

Subleading ‘tree-level’ hard charge

The ‘tree-level’ hard charge takes the form

Q^{(0)}_{H,+}[\bar{Y}]=\int_{i^{+}}d^{3}y\,\bar{Y}^{\alpha}\overset{3}{T}_{\tau\alpha}.

(233)

The coefficient
\overset{3}{T}_{\tau\alpha}
receives contributions from both matter and gravitons,

\overset{3}{T}_{\tau\alpha}=\overset{3}{T}{}^{\rm matt}_{\tau\alpha}+\overset{3}{T}{}^{h}_{\tau\alpha}.

(234)

The graviton contribution is of order
O(\kappa^{2})
, while the matter term (
227
) is of order
\kappa^{0}
; it does not get corrections at higher order in
\kappa
. Thus

Q^{(0)}_{H,+}[\bar{Y}]=im\int_{i^{+}}d^{3}y\,\bar{Y}^{\alpha}(b_{0}^{*}{\partial}_{\alpha}b_{0}-b_{0}{\partial}_{\alpha}b_{0}^{*})+O(\kappa^{2}).

(235)

The higher-order correction comes only from the graviton energy-momentum tensor.

Logarithmic soft charge

Higher powers of
\ln\tau
in the energy-momentum imply that we should consider a generalization of the large-
u
expansion of the graviton field (
55
),

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\left[\overset{0}{h}_{\mu\nu}(x^{A})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln u)^{n}}{u}\overset{1,n}{h_{\mu\nu}}(x^{A})+\frac{1}{u}\overset{1}{h}_{\mu\nu}(x^{A})+\cdots\right].

(236)

The coefficients in the expansion are related to those appearing in the generalization of (
176
) for the trace-reversed field by
h_{\mu\nu}=\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\bar{h}
,

\displaystyle{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\left[\overset{0}{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}(x^{A})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln u)^{n}}{u}\overset{1,n}{{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}}(x^{A})+\frac{1}{u}\overset{1}{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}(x^{A})+\cdots\right],

(237)

where

\displaystyle\overset{0}{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\int d^{3}y(-q\cdot{\mathcal{Y}})^{-1}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu}\overset{3}{T}{}_{\tau\tau},

\displaystyle\overset{1,n}{{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\int d^{3}y\sum_{r=n}^{\infty}\binom{r}{n}[-\ln(-q\cdot{\mathcal{Y}})]^{r-n}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left({\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu}\overset{4,r}{T{}_{\tau\tau}}-{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}({\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu})\overset{3,r}{T_{\tau\alpha}}+({\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu})({\mathcal{D}}^{\beta}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu})\overset{2,r}{T_{\alpha\beta}}\right),

(238)

and
\overset{1}{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\overset{1,0}{{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}}
.
Since we saw in (
224
) and (
198
) that both
\overset{4,r}{T_{\tau\tau}}
and
\overset{3,r}{T_{\tau\alpha}}
vanish for all
r\geq 2
and that
\overset{2,r}{T_{\alpha\beta}}
vanish for all
r
except for
\overset{2,0}{T_{\alpha\beta}}=\overset{2}{T}{}^{h}_{\alpha\beta}
, we have

\displaystyle\overset{1,n}{{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}}

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{for all $n\geq 2$},

\displaystyle\overset{1,1}{{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\int d^{3}y\left({\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu}\overset{4,1}{T{}_{\tau\tau}}-{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}({\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu})\overset{3,1}{T_{\tau\alpha}}\right),

(239)

\displaystyle\overset{1}{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\int d^{3}y\Bigg[{\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu}\overset{4}{T{}_{\tau\tau}}-{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}({\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu})\overset{3}{T_{\tau\alpha}}+({\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu})({\mathcal{D}}^{\beta}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu})\overset{2}{T}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\ln(-q\cdot{\mathcal{Y}})\left({\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu}\overset{4,1}{T{}_{\tau\tau}}-{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}({\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu})\overset{3,1}{T_{\tau\alpha}}\right)\Bigg].

For
\overset{1,1}{{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}}
, we can integrate by parts and use the energy-momentum conservation
\nabla^{\nu}T_{\tau\nu}=0
which reads

\overset{4,n}{T_{\tau\tau}}+{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}\overset{3,n}{T_{\tau\alpha}}=(n+1)\overset{4,n+1}{T_{\tau\tau}}+k^{\alpha\beta}\overset{2,n}{T_{\alpha\beta}}

(240)

to write the integrand as
\overset{4,2}{T}_{\tau\tau}
which is zero.
Therefore the
u^{-1}\ln u
mode vanishes as well:

\displaystyle\overset{1,1}{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\int d^{3}y{\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu}\left(\overset{4,1}{T{}_{\tau\tau}}+{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}\overset{3,1}{T_{\tau\alpha}}\right)=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\int d^{3}y{\mathcal{Y}}_{\mu}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\nu}\left(2\overset{4,2}{T{}_{\tau\tau}}+k^{\alpha\beta}\overset{2,1}{T_{\alpha\beta}}\right)=0.

(241)

This establishes
\overset{1,n}{{\bar{h}}_{\mu\nu}}=0
for
n\geq 1
, and therefore
\overset{1,n}{h_{\mu\nu}}=0
for all
n\geq 1
, to all orders in the coupling: the expansion (
236
) does not admit corrections of the form
\frac{(\ln u)^{n}}{u}
with
n\geq 1
even at higher powers in the coupling.

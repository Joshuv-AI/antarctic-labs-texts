Stiefel optimization is NP-hard
Zehua Lai, Lek-Heng Lim, Tianyun Tang

Abstract
. We show that linearly constrained linear optimization over a Stiefel or Grassmann manifold is NP-hard in general. We show that the same is true for unconstrained quadratic optimization over a Stiefel manifold. We will establish the nonexistence of FPTAS \mathrm{FPTAS} for these optimization problems over a Stiefel manifold. As an aside we extend our results to flag manifolds. Combined with earlier findings, this shows that manifold optimization is a difficult endeavor — even the simplest problems like LP and unconstrained QP are already NP-hard on the most common manifolds.

For any

Theorem 2.1

(Stiefel LP is NP-hard)
.

Let

be positive integers and
G_{k}
be a
k
-vertex undirected graph.

(i)

The maximum value of the LP over
\V(k,n)
,

(4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{maximize}

\displaystyle x_{11}+\dots+x_{kk}

\displaystyle\operatorname{subject~to}

\displaystyle x_{ij}=0,\;i\neq j,

\displaystyle x_{ii}+x_{jj}\leq 0,\;(i,j)\in E,

(ii)

For any

\biggl\{X\in\V(k,n):x_{ij}=0\text{ for }i\neq j,\;x_{ii}+x_{jj}\leq 0\text{ for all }(i,j)\in E,\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{ii}\geq 2r-k\biggr\}\neq\varnothing.

Proof.

The condition
x_{ij}=0
,

, taken together with
X^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}X=I_{k}
implies that

is a diagonal matrix with
x_{ii}=\pm 1
,

. Consider the set
S
of indices

. For any

,

. Hence

is a stable set of

. Conversely, given any stable set

of

, define the diagonal matrix

with

Then

is clearly feasible for (

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{ii}=|S|-(k-|S|)=2|S|-k.

By our choice of

, we have

. In addition, since the maximum value of (

for (

\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}

4\kappa(G_{k})-2|E|+k=\max_{x\in\{-1,1\}^{k}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}(I_{k}-A)x.

Theorem 3.1

(Unconstrained Stiefel QP is NP-hard)
.

Let

be a

-vertex undirected graph with adjacency matrix

\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}

We first show that (

. So for sufficiently small

Next we show that (

and thus

0&x_{2}^{*}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\

\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\

Hence

f_{2}=x_{*}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}(I_{k}-A)x_{*}=\diag(X_{*})^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}(I_{k}-A)\diag(X_{*})\leq f_{1}

and we have

\max_{X\in\Flag(k_{1},\dots,k_{p},n)}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}x_{ii}x_{jj}=b_{n}^{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\omega(G_{n})}\Bigr).

Let

Denote the objective in (

Note that

Lemma 4.3

(Unconstrained flag LP)
.

For any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,

\max_{X\in\Flag(k_{1},\dots,k_{p},n)}\tr(A^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{p+1}\lambda_{i}n_{j}a_{j}

is attained at

where

is an eigenvalue decomposition with
Q\in\O(n)

\Lambda=\diag(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}

Proof.

The problem transforms into

\displaystyle\max_{X\in\Flag(k_{1},\dots,k_{p},n)}\tr(A^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}X)

\displaystyle=\max_{X\in\Flag(k_{1},\dots,k_{p},n)}\tr\biggl(\biggl[\frac{A+A^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}}{2}\biggr]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}X\biggr)=\max_{X\in\Flag(k_{1},\dots,k_{p},n)}\tr(\Lambda^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}X)

\displaystyle=\max_{X\in\Flag(k_{1},\dots,k_{p},n)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{ii}=\max_{x\in\Delta_{k_{1},\dots,k_{p},n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}.

X=Q\diag(a_{1}I_{n_{1}},a_{2}I_{n_{2}},\dots,a_{p+1}I_{n_{p+1}})Q^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}

Apart from this trivial case, LP and unconstrained QP are, without question, the simplest possible optimization problems over any manifold. Any constrained optimization problem likely contains LP as a special or degenerate case; and any unconstrained optimization problem likely contains unconstrained QP as a special or degenerate case. The revelation that these simple cases are NP-hard suggests that other more complex optimization problems involving more complex objectives or constraints are almost certainly also NP-hard. We take this as a sign that manifold optimization ought to be approached in a manner similar to polynomial optimization, where tractable convex relaxations play an indispensable role.

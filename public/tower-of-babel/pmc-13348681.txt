A 12-year hospital-based surveillance of syphilis in Hanzhong, China: shifting epidemics and implications for targeted prevention
Authors: Chunmei Zheng, Jin Tang, Hang Hu, Wenhua Chen, Sanjun He, Chengchao Lai, Yue Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesSyphilis remains a global public health priority. China has implemented a national control plan, yet local epidemic dynamics in resource-limited areas are poorly documented. This study aimed to analyze the 12-year trends and population-specific characteristics of syphilis in Hanzhong, China, to inform targeted prevention strategies. MethodsWe conducted a retrospective analysis of 561,718 syphilis tests performed at Hanzhong Central Hospital from 2012 to 2023. Case positivity was determined according to national diagnostic criteria (non-treponemal and treponemal tests). We calculated annual positivity rates and analyzed trends by sex and age using χ² and trend χ² tests.ResultsAmong 561,718 tests, 36,913 were positive (overall positivity rate: 6.57%). The positivity rate followed a statistically significant “rise-peak-decline” trend (P < 0.001), peaking at 9.47% in 2020 and decreasing to 5.19% in 2023. While the overall rate was higher among females than males (6.64% vs. 6.48%, P < 0.05), the male rate surpassed the female rate by 2023 (5.63% vs. 4.86%). Adults aged ≥ 60 years had the highest overall rate (9.05%). Neonates (aged 0 ~ 1 year) experienced alarmingly high rates, peaking at 36.24% in 2021 before declining to 17.60% in 2023. Neonates (0 ~ 1 year) had the highest overall positivity rate (15.36%), peaking at 36.24% in 2021 before declining to 17.60% in 2023. Adults aged ≥ 60 years had the second-highest overall rate (9.05%).ConclusionThe syphilis epidemic in Hanzhong has undergone dynamic shifts between 2012 and 2023, with a recent overall decline masking significant subpopulation disparities. Findings highlight a rising burden among men, a persistently high burden among older adults, and critical gaps in preventing mother-to-child transmission. Moving beyond generalized approaches to implement targeted interventions for the identified high-risk groups is essential for syphilis control in this and similar settings. Introduction

Syphilis, a chronic systemic infection caused by Treponema pallidum, continues to pose a major global public health challenge, with an estimated 7.1 million new cases annually among adults worldwide [1]. Its epidemiological profile is marked by profound geographical and temporal heterogeneity. In high-income countries, resurgent outbreaks are frequently concentrated among men who have sex with men (MSM) [2]. In contrast, many low- and middle-income countries bear a generalized epidemic, where women and infants shoulder a significant burden due to mother-to-child transmission (MTCT), contributing to substantial adverse pregnancy outcomes [3, 4].

China has experienced a notable fluctuation in syphilis incidence over recent decades. Following a dramatic resurgence since the 1990s, the country implemented the ambitious China Syphilis Prevention and Control Plan (2010–2020), which emphasized expanded screening, standardized treatment, and the elimination of MTCT [5]. National surveillance data suggest these efforts contributed to a slowing of the epidemic’s growth; however, significant regional disparities persist [6, 7]. Some regions report stabilizing or declining trends, while others, like Ningxia, continue to see rising rates [8, 9]. This variation highlights the critical importance of localized, long-term surveillance data to accurately assess the impact of national policies and to guide adaptive, context-specific strategies.

Despite this need, longitudinal surveillance studies spanning a full policy cycle, particularly from prefecture-level cities in central and western China, remain scarce. Most existing studies mainly focus on short-term trends or specific high-risk groups. There remains a lack of evidence regarding long-term dynamic changes in syphilis epidemiology at the population level in resource-limited settings.

Hanzhong is a typical prefecture‑level city in northwestern China, with a population exceeding 3 million. To address this evidence gap, we conducted a 12‑year retrospective analysis of hospital‑based syphilis screening data. Our objectives were to describe long‑term trends in syphilis positivity, examine distributions by sex and age, and identify key populations for targeted intervention. These findings aim to provide a local evidence base for syphilis control in Hanzhong and may offer a useful surveillance example for similar settings in China and other resource‑limited regions.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective study analyzed individuals who underwent syphilis serological testing at Hanzhong Central Hospital between January 1, 2012, and December 31, 2023. The 12-year study period was selected to cover the full implementation cycle of the National Syphilis Prevention and Control Plan (2010–2020) and its immediate post-implementation phase, allowing long-term trend evaluation under consistent policy context. Hanzhong Central Hospital is the leading tertiary hospital and regional clinical reference laboratory in Hanzhong, serving the entire urban area and surrounding counties. It provides both walk-in medical services and receives referred patients from lower-level medical institutions across the region. The hospital runs comprehensive key public health programs, including standardized antenatal care, routine health check-ups for the elderly, pediatric healthcare, and sexually transmitted infection (STI) screening and management. As the municipal reference laboratory for syphilis testing, our laboratory performs tests not only for inpatients and outpatients of this hospital but also accepts samples from primary care facilities, community health centers, physical examination centers, and other institutions throughout Hanzhong City. Therefore, the data in this study are derived from a broad screening population rather than only patients treated at this hospital, making the surveillance results more comprehensive and representative of Hanzhong’s overall syphilis epidemic.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: Individuals tested for syphilis at the hospital between January 2012 and December 2023, with complete records meeting the national Diagnostic Criteria for Syphilis (WS 273–2007 and WS 273–2018), which are Chinese national health industry standards that define how infectious diseases should be classified, reported, and managed within China’s public health system. Confirmed by both a non-treponemal test (RPR) and a treponemal-specific test (TPPA).

Exclusion criteria: Incomplete demographic data (sex, age), invalid test results (discordant RPR/TPPA results without confirmation), or duplicate records.

Rationale for age stratification

Children aged 0–1 year were analyzed separately from those aged 1–3 years to clearly distinguish congenital syphilis (mother-to-child transmission, MTCT) from potential postnatal seroreactivity or late-onset congenital infection. No sexual or other non-MTCT transmission routes are suspected or epidemiologically documented in children aged 1–3 years in this setting. The high positivity in the 0–1 year group reflects active MTCT. In children aged 1–3 years, positive results mainly represent persistent maternal antibodies or late-detected congenital infection, not new acquisition. This separation improves interpretation of vertical transmission dynamics and postnatal seroreversion.

Data sources and collection

Data were extracted from the hospital’s electronic laboratory database, including demographics (sex, age, test year), syphilis test results (RPR titer, TPPA status), and relevant clinical notes. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hanzhong Central Hospital (Approval No: 2025 − 120). All personally identifiable information was de-identified, and data were stored securely to ensure privacy. Informed consent was waived for this retrospective analysis. Most syphilis testing in this study was performed as part of routine universal screening in China, including prenatal care, pre-operative evaluation, pre-marital check-ups, routine physical examinations, and admission screening. Testing was rarely initiated only for symptomatic evaluation. This population-based screening approach is the standard practice in Chinese healthcare settings and may influence the observed positivity rates.

Data processing

Two researchers independently extracted and collated the data. Discrepancies were resolved through cross-validation. Missingness was concentrated in key demographic variables (sex, age) and non-essential clinical notes. Records with missing sex or age were excluded from quantitative analysis to maintain data quality. No imputation was performed for missing values, and all exclusions were made at the data cleaning stage to avoid selection bias. The pattern of missing data was not associated with test results, as confirmed during data cleaning. Invalid data (e.g., missing key variables, contradictory results) were excluded. The final analytic dataset comprised 561,718 valid cases.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 21.0. The positivity rate was calculated as (number of positive cases / number of tested cases) × 100%. Group comparisons (by sex and age) used the χ² test. Temporal trends in annual positivity rates were assessed using the Cochran-Armitage trend test. A P-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically statistically significant. Given the large sample size, statistically significant differences may correspond to small effect sizes; thus, we focus on descriptive epidemiological patterns and magnitude of differences rather than relying solely on statistical significance to interpret public health relevance. Effect sizes (Cramér’s V) were not reported but will be considered in future studies to quantify practical significance.

Results

Temporal distribution of syphilis positive rate

From 2012 to 2023, a total of 561,718 syphilis tests were conducted, with 36,913 positive cases and an overall positive rate of 6.57%. The positive rate showed statistically significant temporal variation (Trend χ² = 2248.72, P < 0.001):

2012–2017: Fluctuated between 5.10% and 6.27%, showing a slight rise followed by a decline;2017–2020: Continuous increase, peaking at 9.47% in 2020;2021–2023: Significant decline, reaching 5.19% in 2023 (close to the 2012 level)(see Table 1; Fig. 1).

Population distribution

Discussion

This study, based on 12 years of syphilis screening data from Hanzhong Central Hospital (2012–2023), reveals the epidemiological characteristics and dynamic changes of syphilis infection in Hanzhong City. Our findings describe the dynamic epidemiological trends of syphilis in Hanzhong over 12 years and provide important implications for the development and optimization of locally targeted prevention and intervention strategies.

The “increase-peak-decline” trend of syphilis positivity rate in Hanzhong City during 2012–2023 reflects the evolution of local syphilis epidemic under different prevention and control strategies. The rapid increase from 2017 to 2020 may be related to enhanced screening efforts, improved diagnostic capabilities, and increased population mobility during this period. The sharp decline after 2020 (from 9.47% to 5.19% within three years) is particularly noteworthy and may be attributed to the following factors: First, the implementation of the “China Syphilis Prevention and Control Plan (2010–2020)” reached its final stage, with various prevention measures fully deployed and effective [10, 11] Second, the COVID-19 pandemic, while a global health crisis, may have inadvertently contributed to reduced transmission through decreased social mixing and heightened personal hygiene awareness, a phenomenon noted in other regions for various STIs [12]. However, the significant heterogeneity beneath the overall trend warns against complacency and underscores the need for precision public health strategies [13]. These findings suggest that a combination of policy implementation, behavioral intervention, and specialized prevention programs can effectively control syphilis epidemic.Compared with reports from other regions in China, the epidemic trend in Hanzhong City exhibits distinct characteristics. For instance, studies in Ningxia showed a continuous rise in syphilis positivity rate [7], while in some economically developed coastal areas, the rate remained relatively stable. This regional variation may be related to differences in economic development levels, population structure, prevention and control resource allocation, and implementation intensity. Hanzhong City’s success demonstrates that even in economically underdeveloped areas, effective syphilis control can be achieved through scientifically sound prevention strategies and determined implementation.

The reversal of syphilis positivity rate between genders (female rate declining and surpassing male rate) represents a significant epidemiological shift. In the early study period (2012–2014), the higher positivity rate among females was consistent with most domestic and international reports [8, 14], mainly due to extensive prenatal and postpartum screening identifying many asymptomatic infections. The continuous decline in female positivity rate since 2016 indicates significant achievements in maternal and child health services and mother-to-child transmission prevention in the region.The phenomenon of male positivity rate surpassing female rate in 2023 deserves high attention. This may be related to the following factors: First, traditional gender concepts and social stigma cause men to avoid seeking medical care and screening, leading to undetected and untreated infections [15, 16]. Second, specific occupational groups (such as migrant workers, long-distance drivers) have higher risk behaviors and lower access to health services [17]. Third, the needs of men in terms of sexual health services are often overlooked in current prevention strategies [18]. These findings suggest that syphilis prevention strategies need to be adjusted from a primarily female-focused approach to a more balanced gender strategy.To address this change, we propose the following recommendations: Develop male-specific health education programs, utilizing new media platforms and workplace health services to increase men’s syphilis awareness and risk perception. Provide convenient testing services in male-dominated workplaces such as construction sites and industrial parks. Strengthen peer education among men to reduce stigma and discrimination associated with testing and treatment.

Elderly population (≥ 60 years)

Our study found that the elderly population had the highest syphilis positivity rate (9.05%), consistent with recent domestic and international research findings [19, 20]. This may be related to the following factors: Longer sexual activity periods among the elderly, coupled with reduced condom use.Weakened immune function in older adults, making them more susceptible to infection. Low awareness of syphilis prevention among the elderly, with many considering it a “young person’s disease”.Inadequate sexual health services in healthcare institutions for older adults.Given these characteristics, we recommend: Integrating syphilis screening into routine health checkups for the elderly. Developing age-appropriate health education materials to improve syphilis knowledge among older adults. Training family doctors and community health workers to provide sexual health consultation services for the elderly. Strengthening health education for elderly caregivers to enhance early identification and intervention capabilities.

Neonatal population (0 ~ year group)

The extremely high positivity rate in the neonatal group (peaking at 36.24% in 2021) indicates serious challenges in mother-to-child transmission prevention. This may be related to inadequate prenatal care among pregnant women, insufficient screening and treatment during pregnancy, and poor compliance among partners of infected pregnant women. The significant decline in neonatal positivity rate after 2021 (to 17.60% in 2023) demonstrates the effectiveness of targeted interventions.

To further reduce mother-to-child transmission, we recommend: In line with the WHO guideline on syphilis screening and treatment for pregnant women (2017), all pregnant women should receive syphilis screening at the first antenatal care contact, preferably before 12 weeks of gestation. Repeat screening in the third trimester is recommended for women at high risk of syphilis infection. Implementing universal syphilis screening in early pregnancy (before 12 weeks) and rescreening in the third trimester for high-risk populations.Establishing a rapid treatment mechanism to ensure timely and standardized treatment for infected pregnant women and their partners. Strengthening follow-up management of exposed newborns to achieve early diagnosis and treatment. Enhancing health education for reproductive-age women to increase awareness of mother-to-child transmission prevention [13, 21].

Adolescent and young adult populations (12 ~ and 18 ~ year groups)

The syphilis positivity rate among adolescents and young adults shows a trend of first rising and then declining, reflecting the complex impact of factors such as sexual behavior patterns, health education levels, and service accessibility in this population. The peak in 2018 may be related to the popularization of social media and changes in dating patterns among young people, while the subsequent decline may reflect the effectiveness of strengthened school health education and youth-friendly health services.

For this population, we recommend: Incorporating comprehensive sexual education into the school curriculum, covering syphilis knowledge, risk awareness, and prevention skills.Establishing youth-friendly health service centers to provide confidential, non-judgmental testing and consultation services.Utilizing new media platforms to disseminate syphilis prevention information in formats preferred by young people [10]. Strengthening psychological counseling services to address issues such as peer pressure and self-identity that young people may face [9].

Strengths and limitations

Key strengths of this study include the large sample size, 12-year duration covering a complete national policy cycle, and detailed age stratification providing life-course insights. Limitations are inherent to its hospital-based, retrospective design. The data represent a sentinel surveillance sample and may not fully reflect community prevalence, potentially missing individuals not accessing care. The lack of behavioral, socioeconomic, and clinical staging data limits nuanced risk factor analysis. Being a single-center study, generalizability to other regions should be made with caution, though Hanzhong’s profile is representative of many prefecture-level cities in central/western China.

Implications for public health practice

The findings argue for a shift from generalized to targeted, life-course interventions for syphilis control in similar settings:1.For men: Develop workplace-based and peer-led health promotion and testing initiatives to reduce stigma and improve access.2.For older adults: Integrate STI education and screening into primary care and geriatric health services.3.For MTCT prevention: Ensure universal early prenatal syphilis screening, prompt treatment, and partner services, with vigilant follow-up of exposed infants.4.For surveillance: Establish and maintain local, long-term surveillance systems to monitor trends, identify disparities, and evaluate targeted interventions [12, 22].

Conclusion

This 12-year surveillance elucidates the complex and shifting epidemiologic pattern of syphilis in Hanzhong based on hospital screening data. The recent overall decline in positivity rate is encouraging. However, statistically significant and notable disparities persist, including a relatively higher burden among men in recent years, a sustained high burden among older adults, and a continued high positivity rate among neonates related to mother-to-child transmission. These patterns underscore the need for a precision public health response [23, 24]. Future control efforts should move beyond generalized approaches to prioritize tailored, evidence-based interventions for the key subpopulations identified in this study, including men, older adults, pregnant women, and infants. Such targeted approaches aligned with local epidemiologic patterns are likely to support sustainable syphilis control in this and similar settings in resource‑limited regions.

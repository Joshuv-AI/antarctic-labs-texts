Characterizations of p p -Parabolicity on Graphs
Andrea Adriani, Florian Fischer, Alberto G. Setti

Abstract
. We study p p -energy functionals on infinite locally summable graphs for p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) and show that many well-known characterizations for a parabolic space are also true in this discrete, non-local and non-linear setting. Among the characterizations are an Ahlfors-type, a Kelvin-Nevanlinna-Royden-type, a Khas’minskiĭ-type and a Poincaré-type characterization. We also illustrate some applications and describe examples of graphs which are locally summable but not locally finite. 2020 ​ Mathematics Subject Classification. 1.
Introduction

We consider an infinite weighted graph
G=(X,b,m,c)
, where
X
is a countable infinite set of vertices, the edge measure
b
is a symmetric locally summable function on
X\times X\to[0,\infty)
which vanishes on the diagonal,
m
is a measure of full support and
c
is a non-negative potential.

Analysis on infinite weighted graphs has undergone an impressive growth in recent years.
Graphs can be viewed as discretizations of differentiable manifolds, so that results obtained in the graph setting may shed light to what happens in the continuous setting and vice-versa.

However, the geometry of weighted graphs is rich enough to allow for a variety of different phenomena which sometimes do not have exact counterparts even on weighted Riemannian manifolds, in part due to the possible complete decoupling between vertex and edge measures.

Moreover, the natural differential operators of differential geometry correspond to difference operators on graphs, which are intrinsically non-local and therefore some techniques which can be successfully used on manifolds do not carry over to graphs and alternative methods must be devised.

Most of this investigation involves the weighted discrete Laplacian
\Delta
, namely the operator
associated to the energy functional

\mathcal{E}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert^{2},

which acts as

\Delta f(x)=\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)\bigl(f(x)-f(y)\bigr),

whenever the expression makes sense.

Various generalizations to the case where
p\neq 2
are possible. In this paper we consider a slightly modified version of that studied by T. Nakamura and M. Yamasaki
[
NY76
]
and by M. Yamasaki
[
Yam77
]
: for
p\in(1,\infty)
, we let
\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)
be the
p
-energy functional

\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert^{p}+\sum_{x\in X}c(x)\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert^{p},

which induces the associated
p
-Laplace type operator

L_{p}f(x):=\Delta_{p}f(x)+\frac{c(x)}{m(x)}\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert^{p-2}f(x),

where the (pseudo)
p
-Laplacian is defined by

\Delta_{p}f(x):=\frac{1}{m(x)}\,\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert^{p-2}(f(x)-f(y))

for all functions
f
such that the right hand side makes sense.

The
p
-Laplacian and its discrete counterparts have been studied extensively, both from the theoretical point of view - in particular, dealing with spectral theory, eigenvalues estimates, Cheeger type inequalities, function theory and elliptic and parabolic equations - and in view of applications to non-linear diffusion, image processing, data clustering. Confining ourselves to the discrete setting we mention
[
CC14
,
CZ21
,
GHJ21
,
HS21
,
HS97
,
HS97a
,
HM15
,
HW20
,
KM16
,
KC10
,
Mug13
,
PC11
,
Yam77
]
for the former and
[
BH09
,
EDT17
,
ELB08
,
ETT15
]
for the latter.

In this paper we investigate potential theoretic properties of the
p
-Laplacian, concentrating on parabolicity. As in the continuous case, there are deep connections and interplays between properties of the
p
-energy functional and of the associated
p
-Laplacian and the geometry of the underlying graph.

For
p=2
, the parabolicity of the Laplacian can be defined and characterized in a numbers of equivalent ways: in terms of recurrence of the induced process, of the constancy of bounded below super-harmonic functions, of the capacity induced by the energy functional, and of the validity of Green’s formulae or of maximum principles at infinity.

This holds true in the case of the
p
-Laplacian for
p\neq 2
and, starting from the seminal paper by Yamasaki
[
Yam77
]
, several previous works were devoted to the investigation of various facets of
p
-parabolicity,
[
Fis22
,
Fis23
,
MPR22
,
MPR24
,
MPR24a
,
Pra04
]
.

The original motivation for writing this paper was to show that
p
-parabolicity, which we define in terms of the vanishing of the variational
p
-capacity of finite sets, is equivalent to the existence of a Khas’minskiĭ potential, namely a function
w
that blows up at infinity and satisfies
\Delta_{p}w\geq 0
outside a finite set, in this non-linear, non-local and non-locally finite setting. While the sufficiency condition depends on the maximum principle at infinity, for the other implication we construct a potential using solutions to the obstacle problem on graphs similar to the corresponding proof on manifolds in
[
Val12a
]
.

Along the way, we realized that many well-known characterizations of
p
-parabolicity were not written down in this general setting. So we decided to provide a comprehensive treatment of the parabolicity of the
p
-Laplacian on weighted non-locally finite graphs. This also gave us the possibility to prove an area-type sufficient condition for
p
-parabolicity which is a characterization on model graphs and seems to be new for
p\neq 2
.

We show these characterizations, on the one hand because we need some of these results for the proof of the Khas’minskiĭ criterion, and also to provide a good reference for applications.

While in this investigation it is natural to consider the case where the potential
c
is identically zero, for otherwise the graph is never
p
-parabolic for any
p
, whenever it is possible we will consider the more general case of nonnegative potentials.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the notation and the main definitions, and collect some functional analytic results that will be used later in the paper. In particular, we define the
p
-energy functional and the associated
p
-Laplacian and
p
-capacity. We then define
p
-parabolicity in terms of the vanishing of the
p
-capacity on all finite subsets and recall that it can be characterized by the existence of so called null sequences, namely, sequences of functions pointwise converging to
1
with
p
-energy tending to
0
. Section
3
is the heart of the paper and describes a number of different characterizations of
p
-parabolicity. We begin with characterizations which are well known in the locally finite case: in terms of the existence of a
p
-Green’s function (Section
3.1
), in terms of function theoretic properties of the space of function of finite
p
-energy (Section
3.2
), and of Liouville properties for non-negative
p
-superharmonic functions (Section
3.3
). Sections
3.4
to
3.10
describe some of our more original results. In Section
3.4
we describe the extension of a Kelvin-Nevanlinna-Royden characterization to the non-locally finite case. In Section
3.6
we show a “maximum principle at infinity”-type characterization which goes back to Alhfors,
[
Ahl52
]
, for Riemann surfaces and for
p=2
, and is new in the case of graphs.
In Section
3.7
we generalize to all
p>1
a characterization in terms of the vanishing of a suitably defined capacity of subsets of the boundary at infinity which was recently obtained in the linear case in
[
LPS24
]
. Section
3.8
describes a sufficient condition for the
p
-parabolicity of locally finite graphs which is the exact counterpart of an area condition valid on Riemannian manifolds and which becomes a characterization in the case of model graphs. Section
3.9
gives a weak maximum principle at infinity characterization of
p
-parabolicity. There we also show that the existence of a Khas’minskiĭ potential implies
p
-parabolicity. Finally, in Section
3.10
we show that the Khas’minskiĭ condition introduced in the previous section is, in fact, also necessary, adapting the proof given in
[
Val12a
]
.

The final section describes some applications of the results obtained in several examples, focusing on locally summable but not locally finite graphs.

2.
Setting the Scene

2.1.
Graphs and Laplace-type Operators

Let
X
be a countable infinite set. A
(weighted) graph

G
is a quadruple
G=(X,b,m,c)
, where

•

b
is a symmetric function
b\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)
with zero diagonal which is locally summable, i.e., the vertex degree function satisfies

\deg(x):=\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)<\infty,\qquad x\in X;

•

m\colon X\to(0,\infty)
is a measure of full support;

•

c\colon X\to[0,\infty)
is the non-negative potential term.

The elements of
X
are called
vertices
and the function
b
represents weights of edges between vertices. Two vertices
x,y
are said to be
connected
, and we write
x\sim y
, if
b(x,y)>0
. A set
V\subseteq X
is called
connected
if for every two vertices
x,y\in V
there are vertices
{x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\in V}
, such that
x=x_{0}
,
y=x_{n}
and
x_{i-1}\sim x_{i}
for all
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,n-1\right\}
. Throughout this paper we will always assume that

X
is connected. The set of edges
E_{V}=E_{V,b}
on
V
are given by

E_{V}:=\left\{(x,y)\in V\times V:x\sim y\right\}.

We set
E=E_{X}
.

For
V\subseteq X
, let
\partial_{e}V=\left\{y\in X\setminus V:y\sim z\in V\right\}
and
\partial_{i}V=\left\{y\in V:y\sim z\in X\setminus V\right\}
denote the
exterior
and
interior boundary
of
V
, respectively. The sets
\mathring{V}=V\setminus\partial_{i}V

\overline{V}:=V\cup\partial_{e}V
are the
interior
and
closure
of
V
(these are obviously not meant in topological sense).

A graph
G
is called
locally finite
if for all
x\in X

\#\left\{y\in X:y\sim x\right\}<\infty.

A function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is
odd
if
f(t)=-f(-t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

The space of real valued functions on
V\subseteq X
is denoted by
C(V)
and the space of functions with compact (= finite) support in
V
is denoted by
C_{c}(V)
. We consider
C(V)
to be a subspace of
C(X)
by extending the functions of
C(V)
by zero on
X\setminus V
.

For all
1\leq p<\infty
,
V\subseteq X
, we define

\displaystyle\ell^{p}(V,m)

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{f\in C(X):\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p,m}^{p}:=\sum_{x\in V}\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert^{p}m(x)<\infty\bigr\},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\ell^{\infty}(V)

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{f\in C(X):\sup_{x\in V}\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert<\infty\bigr\}.

Note that
(\ell^{p}(X,m),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{p,m})
is a reflexive Banach space for
p\in(1,\infty)
, and a Banach space for
p=1,\infty
. The
\ell^{p}
-spaces on
X\times X
are defined in a similar way.

Let
f,g\in C(X)
, then
f\vee g
,
f\wedge g
,
f_{-}
and
f_{+}
denote the pointwise maximum and minimum of
f
and
g
, and the negative and positive part of
f
, respectively. By
\mathds{1}_{V}
we denote the characteristic function on
V\subseteq X
, and for singletons
\left\{x\right\}\subseteq V
, we set
\mathds{1}_{x}:=\mathds{1}_{\left\{x\right\}}
.

We also define the linear difference operator
\nabla{\colon C(X)\to C(X\times X)}
by the formula

\nabla_{x,y}f=f(x)-f(y),\qquad x,y\in X,f\in C(X).

We now introduce Laplace-type operators (which are sometimes also called Schrödinger operators): For
p\in[1,\infty)
and
V\subseteq X
, the
formal space

F^{p}(V)=F^{p}_{b}(V)
is defined by

F^{p}(V):=\{f\in C(X):\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}f\right\rvert^{p-1}<\infty\mbox{ for all }x\in V\},\\

and we write
F^{p}=F^{p}(X)
. Note that on locally finite graphs, we have
F^{p}=C(X)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Moreover, the local summability condition implies that
f\in F^{p}(V)
if and only if, for every
x\in V
,

\sum_{y}b(x,y)|f(y)|^{p-1}<\infty.

As a consequence, for all
p\geq 1
,

\ell^{\infty}(m)\subseteq F^{p}(V).

For all
p\geq 1
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we set

\left(a\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}:=|a|^{p-1}\sgn(a)=|a|^{p-2}a,

with the usual condition that
\infty\cdot 0=0
. Here,
\sgn\colon\mathbb{R}\to\left\{-1,0,1\right\}
is the sign function, that is
\sgn(\alpha)=1
for all
\alpha>0
,
\sgn(\alpha)=-1
for all
\alpha<0
, and
\sgn(0)=0
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
, then the quasilinear
(
p
-)Laplace-type operator

L_{p}=L_{p,b,c,m}\colon F^{p}(V)\to C(V)
is given by

L_{p}f(x):=\Delta_{p}f(x)+\frac{c(x)}{m(x)}\left(f(x)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle},\qquad x\in V,

where
\Delta_{p}=\Delta_{p,b,m}\colon F^{p}(V)\to C(V)
is the
(
p
-)Laplacian
defined via

\Delta_{p}f(x):=\frac{1}{m(x)}\,\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)\left(\nabla_{x,y}f\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle},\qquad x\in V.

A function
u\in F^{p}(V)
is said to be
(
p
-)harmonic (respectively (
p
-)superharmonic, strictly (
p
-)superharmonic, (
p
-)subharmonic
) on
V\subseteq X
with respect to
L_{p}
if

L_{p}u=0\quad(\text{respectively }L_{p}u\geq 0,\,L_{p}u\gneq 0,\,L_{p}u\leq 0)\qquad\text{ on }V.

If
V=X
we only speak of super-/sub-/harmonic functions.

2.2.
Energy Functionals Associated with Graphs

For
p\in[1,\infty)
and
V\subseteq X
, let the
space of finite energy functions

D^{p}(V)=D^{p}_{b,c}(V)
be given by

\displaystyle D^{p}(V)

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{f\in C(X):\sum_{x,y\in V}b(x,y)\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}f\right\rvert^{p}+\sum_{x\in V}c(x)\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert^{p}<\infty\bigr\},

and let
D^{p}:=D^{p}(X)
.

We define the
(p-)energy functional

\mathcal{E}_{p}=\mathcal{E}_{p,b,c}\colon D^{p}\to[0,\infty)
via

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}f\right\rvert^{p}+\sum_{x\in X}c(x)\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert^{p}.

For
p=2
such a functional is a quadratic form, sometimes called Schrödinger form.

Since
c\geq 0
,
\mathcal{E}_{p}
is
Markovian
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
, i.e., compatible with normal contractions. This means that
\mathcal{E}_{p}(C\circ f)\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)
for all
f\in D^{p}
and all
C\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with
C(0)=0
and
\left\lvert C(s)-C(t)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert s-t\right\rvert
for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Note that an application of Hölder’s inequality shows that

D^{p}(V)\subseteq F^{p}(V),\quad p\in[1,\infty).

The connection between
L_{p}
and
\mathcal{E}_{p}
on
C_{c}(X)
for
p\in[1,\infty)
is further described via a Green’s formula, also known as integration by parts, which is stated next.

Let
V\subseteq X
. To shorten notation, we define a weighted bracket
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V}
via

\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{V}:=\sum_{x\in V}f(x)\varphi(x)m(x)

whenever the sum converges absolutely for
f,\varphi\in C(X)
.
Moreover, for any
u,v\in C(V)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
we set

\mathcal{E}_{p,{V}}(u,v):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in{V}}b(x,y)\left(\nabla_{x,y}u\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}\nabla_{x,y}v+\sum_{x\in V}c(x)\left(u(x)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}v(x),

whenever the sums converge absolutely; and if
V=X
, we set
\mathcal{E}_{p}=\mathcal{E}_{p,{V}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
V\subseteq X
. Let
f\in F(V)
and
\varphi\in C_{c}(X)
. Then all of the following sums converge absolutely and

\displaystyle\langle L_{p}f,\varphi\rangle_{V}=\mathcal{E}_{p,V}(f,\varphi)+\sum_{x\in V,y\in{\partial_{e}V}}b(x,y)\left(\nabla_{x,y}f\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}\varphi(x).

In particular, the formula can be applied to
f\in C_{c}(X)
, or
f\in D^{p}
, and

\mathcal{E}_{p}(\varphi)=\langle L_{p}\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{V},\qquad\varphi\in C_{c}(V).

The proof follows from direct computations, see, e.g.,
[
Fis23
, Lemma 2.3]
, where a version valid for arbitrary
c\in C(X)
is described.

Remark 2.2
.

It should be mentioned that another useful generalization of the Laplacian arises via a Green’s formula from the
p
-energy

\sum_{x\in X}\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)(f(x)-f(y))^{2}\right\rvert^{p/2},

where the inner summation represents the square of the length of the “gradient” of
f
at
x
, see, e.g.
[
GLY16
]
. However, this one will not be considered here.

We continue with some basic observations about the space of finite energy functions. We define the space
D_{0}^{p}:=D^{p}_{0,b,c}(X)
to be the completion of
C_{c}(X)
with respect to the energy norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p}
given by

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{o,p}

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)+\left\lvert f(o)\right\rvert^{p})^{1/p},

where
o
is some fixed vertex. If
p=2
, this energy norm is called form norm.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, and
o\in X
, the spaces
(D^{p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p})
and
(D_{0}^{p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p})
are uniformly convex and therefore reflexive. Moreover, for
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o^{\prime},p}
are equivalent norms on
D^{p}
for all
o,o^{\prime}\in X
.

Proof.

It follows from Fatou’s lemma in the same fashion as in
[
KLW21
, Proposition 1.3]
that
\mathcal{E}_{p}
is lower semi-continuous, and thus, that
(D^{p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p})
is complete. Since
(D_{0}^{p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p})
is a closed subspace of a Banach space, it is also complete. Moreover,
since both
(D^{p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p})
and
(D_{0}^{p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p})
are (isometric to) closed subspaces of
L^{p}(\left\{o\right\}\times E,1\times b)
, they are uniformly convex.
It remains to show that the norms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o^{\prime},p}
are equivalent on
D^{p}
for all
o,o^{\prime}\in X
.
We start with the case
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let us consider a path
o=x_{0}\sim\ldots x_{n}=o^{\prime}
from
o
to
o^{\prime}
, and let
f\in D^{p}
. By using Hölder’s inequality and that
c\geq 0
, we get

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla_{o,o^{\prime}}f\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left\lvert\nabla_{x_{i},x_{i+1}}f\right\rvert\leq\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b(x_{i},x_{i+1})\left\lvert\nabla_{x_{i},x_{i+1}}f\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{p}^{1/p}(f).

Define
C_{p}(o,o^{\prime})
as the infimum of
\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\right)^{p-1}
over all paths from
o
to
o^{\prime}
. Then, we conclude

(2.1)

\left\lvert\nabla_{o,o^{\prime}}f\right\rvert^{p}\leq C_{p}(o,o^{\prime})\mathcal{E}_{p}(f).

thus, using the
p
-triangle inequality
\left\lvert\alpha+\beta\right\rvert^{p}\leq 2^{p}\left(\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert^{p}+\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert^{p}\right)
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{o,p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)+2^{p}(\left\lvert\nabla_{o,o^{\prime}}f\right\rvert^{p}+\left\lvert f(o^{\prime})\right\rvert^{p})\leq(1+2^{p}C_{p}(o,o^{\prime}))\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)+2^{p}\left\lvert f(o^{\prime})\right\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{o^{\prime},p}^{p},

where
C^{\prime}=(1+2^{p}C_{p}(o,o^{\prime}))\vee 2^{p}
. By a symmetry argument, we get the equivalence.

If
p=1
, we can do a very similar proof. Maintaining the notation introduced above, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla_{o,o^{\prime}}f\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left\lvert\nabla_{x_{i},x_{i+1}}f\right\rvert\frac{b(x_{i},x_{i+1})}{b(x_{i},x_{i+1})}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i=1,\ldots,n-1}b^{-1}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b(x_{i},x_{i+1})\left\lvert\nabla_{x_{i},x_{i+1}}f\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i=1,\ldots,n-1}b^{-1}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\cdot\mathcal{E}_{1}(f).

Arguing as before, the proof follows.
∎

The next lemma is a non-linear counterpart of
[
KLW21
, Lemma 6.5]
and is a consequence of the uniform convexity of the Banach space
(D^{p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
o\in X
. Let
f\in D^{p}
and let
(f_{n})
be a sequence in
D^{p}
. If
f_{n}\to f
pointwise and
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{p}(f_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)
then
f_{n}\to f
with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p}
.

Proof.

By
[
Bre11
, Theorem 3.18]
, every ball is weakly sequentially compact in the reflexive Banach space
(D^{p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p})
. Since
(f_{n})
converges pointwise to
f
, every subsequence of
(f_{n})
has a subsubsequence which converges weakly to
f
. Therefore,
(f_{n})
converges weakly to
f
. By the uniform convexity, we can use
[
Bre11
, Proposition 3.32]
, and get that
(f_{n})
converges strongly, i.e.,
\left\lVert f-f_{n}\right\rVert_{o,p}\to 0
.
∎

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, we define the
(variational)
p
-capacity
with respect to
V\subseteq X
and
K\subseteq V
finite by the formula

\cc_{p}(K,V):=\inf\left\{\mathcal{E}_{p}(\varphi):\varphi\in C_{c}(V),\,\varphi\geq 1\text{ on }K\right\}.

If
K=\left\{o\right\}
is a singleton,
o\in X
, then we shorten notation and set
\cc_{p}(o,V):=\cc_{p}(\left\{o\right\},V)
. In the case where
V=X
we will simply write
\cc_{p}(K)
instead of
\cc_{p}(K,X).

Note that the infimum in the definition of capacity can be taken in the closure of
C_{c}(V)
with respect to the norm

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p}
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{E}_{p}
is Markovian, one can also restrict to functions satisfying
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
which are
1
on
K
.

In particular, if
V=X
we then have

\cc_{p}(K)=\inf\left\{\mathcal{E}_{p}(v):v\in D_{0}^{p},\,0\leq v\leq 1,\,v=1\text{ on }K\right\}.

In the following lemma we collect some well known properties of the
p
-capacity.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. Let
V_{1},V_{2}\subseteq X
be arbitrary and
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq X
finite and non-empty. Then the following properties hold:

(a)

the
p
-capacity is monotone, i.e.,

(a a)

if
K_{1}\subseteq V_{1}\subseteq V_{2}
then
\cc_{p}(K_{1},V_{1})\geq\cc_{p}(K_{1},V_{2})
,

(a b)

if
K_{1}\subseteq K_{2}\subseteq V_{1}
then
\cc_{p}(K_{1},V_{1})\leq\cc_{p}(K_{2},V_{1})
,

(b)

the
p
-capacity is subadditive, i.e., if
K_{1}\cup K_{2}\subseteq V_{1}
then

\cc_{p}(K_{1}\cup K_{2},V_{1})\leq\cc_{p}(K_{1},V_{1})+\cc_{p}(K_{2},V_{1});

(c)

the
p
-capacity is determined on the boundary, more precisely, for
K_{1}\cup\partial_{e}K_{1}\subseteq V_{1}
finite, we have

\cc_{p}(K_{1}\cup\partial_{e}K_{1},V_{1})=\cc_{p}(\partial_{e}K_{1},V_{1}).

Proof.

The proof of
(a)
is clear by the monotonicity of the infimum.

For
(b)
, we let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there are
0\leq\varphi_{i}\in C_{c}(V_{1})
such that
\varphi_{i}=1
on
K_{i}
and
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\varphi_{i})\leq\cc_{p}(K_{i},V_{1})+\varepsilon
,
i=1,2
. By a case analysis, we see for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}(\varphi_{1}\vee\varphi_{2})\right\rvert^{p}\leq\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}\varphi_{1}\right\rvert^{p}+\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}\varphi_{2}\right\rvert^{p},\quad x,y\in X.

Hence, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\cc_{p}(K_{1}\cup K_{2},V_{1})\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(\varphi_{1}\vee\varphi_{2})\\
\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(\varphi_{1})+\mathcal{E}_{p}(\varphi_{2})\leq\cc_{p}(K_{1},V_{1})+\cc_{p}(K_{2},V_{1})+2\varepsilon.

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0
finishes the proof.

Finally, by
(a)(a b)
, “
\geq
” holds in
(c)
.To prove the reverse inequality, given
\varepsilon>0
, we can find
\varphi\in C_{c}(V_{1})
such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
,
\varphi=1
on
\partial_{e}K_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\varphi)<\cc_{p}(\partial_{e}K_{1},V_{1})+\varepsilon
. Let
\psi\in C_{c}(V_{1})
be such that
\psi=\varphi
on
X\setminus K_{1}
and
\psi=1
on
K_{1}
. In particular,
\psi=1
on
K_{1}\cup\partial_{e}K_{1}
and it is easily checked that
\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}\psi\right\rvert^{p}\leq\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}\varphi\right\rvert^{p}
for all
x\sim y\in X
and
p\in[1,\infty)
. Since
c\geq 0
on
K_{1}
, we get for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\cc_{p}(K_{1}\cup\partial_{e}K_{1},V_{1})\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(\psi)\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(\varphi)<\cc_{p}(\partial_{e}K_{1},V_{1})+\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields the result.
∎

We remark that the first two statements in the lemma also hold for non-negative energy functionals whereas the third statement uses the non-negativity of the potential. Also, essentially the same argument used to prove
(c)
shows that, for every finite set
K\subseteq V
,
\cc_{p}(K,V)=\cc_{p}(\partial_{i}K,V).

Remark 2.6
.

We wonder whether statement
(b)
can be improved to

\cc_{p}(K_{1}\cup K_{2},V_{1})+\cc_{p}(K_{1}\cap K_{2},V_{1})\leq\cc_{p}(K_{1},V_{1})+\cc_{p}(K_{2},V_{1});

i.e., that strong subadditivity might be true, and we are curious to see a proof or counterexample.

Definition 2.7
.

A graph
G
is said to be
p
-parabolic
if
\cc_{p}(K)=0
for every finite subset
K
of
X
. Otherwise, the graph is called
p
-hyperbolic
.

Remark 2.8
.

Clearly, if
c\gneq 0
on
X
, then the associated graph is
p
-hyperbolic. Hence, when characterizing
p
-parabolicity, in the following a leading assumption will often be
c=0
on
X
.

Remark 2.9
.

The word “parabolic” in Definition
2.7
has its origin in the geometry of surfaces. By the celebrated uniformisation theorem of Klein, Koebe and Poincaré in the linear (
p=2
)-case, any simply connected Riemann surface is conformally equivalent to either the sphere (surface of elliptic type), the Euclidean plane (surface of parabolic type) or the hyperbolic plane (surface of hyperbolic type). Since the sphere is the only one with compact surface, the
type problem
is to decide whether a surface is of hyperbolic or of parabolic type. See also
[
Gri99
]
and references therein for more details.

The following is an immediate consequence of the definition.

Lemma 2.10
.

The following are equivalent for all
p\in[1,\infty)
:

(i)

the graph
G
is
p
-parabolic;

(ii)

for every finite set

on

as

;

(iii)

there exists

and

for every

as

Proof.

The implication

To prove

and satsfies

.

The implication

Finally, to show that

\implies

3.
Characterizations of
p
-Parabolicity

In this section we provide several characterizations of
p
-parabolicity for summable infinite graphs. Some of them seem to be folklore but have not been proven in this general discrete setting. In the case of locally finite graphs, some of these results were proven in
[
Yam77
]
and
[
Pra04
]
. However, replacing the assumption of local finiteness with mere summability introduces non-trivial difficulties, and therefore this section represents a novelty both in the context and the content.

3.1.
Green’s Functions

Let
c=0
on
X
and
p\in(1,\infty)
in this subsection.
In the continuous setting, it is classical that
p
-hyperbolicity is also equivalent to the existence of a Green’s function with pole at
o\in X
, for some/all
o\in X
, that is, a minimal positive solution to the problem

(3.1)

\Delta_{p}g(x,o):=\hat{\mathds{1}}_{o}(x)=\frac{1}{m(x)}\mathds{1}_{o}(x).

A Green’s function, when it exists, is closely related to non-constant solutions of the following problem

(3.2)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}u=0&\text{in }X\setminus\{o\};\\
u(o)=1,\quad&0\leq u\leq 1.\end{cases}

Indeed, if
u
is a non-constant solution to (
3.2
), then
\Delta_{p}u(o)>0
and it follows that a suitable multiple of
u
is a positive solution to (
3.1
). Minimality is then achieved by performing a suitable translation. Note also that in this case,
u
is the
p
-harmonic potential for
(o,X)
, i.e., a minimizer for
\cc_{p}(o)
.

In the following proposition we collect some of the properties of solutions to (
3.2
).
The proof uses uniform convexity.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
c=0
on
X
and
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Let
o\in X
and let
\{X_{n}\}
be an increasing exhaustion of
X
by finite sets such that
o\in X_{1}
and

X_{n+1}\setminus X_{n}\subset\partial_{e}X_{n}
.

(a)

For every
n
there exists
u_{n}\in C(X)
such that

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}u_{n}=0\,\text{ in }X_{n}\setminus\{o\}&\\
u_{n}(o)=1,\,\,u_{n}=0\text{ on }X\setminus X_{n},&\end{cases}

and

\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{n})=\cc_{p}(o,X_{n}).

Moreover
0\leq u_{n}\leq u_{n+1}\leq 1
.

(b)

As
n\to\infty
,
u_{n}
tends monotonically and in
D_{0}^{p}
to a function
u\in D_{0}^{p}
, referred to as the
p
-harmonic potential at
o
, which satisfies (
3.2
),
0<u\leq 1
and for which

\mathcal{E}_{p}(u)=\cc_{p}(o).

(c)

u
is constant if and only if
\cc_{p}(o)=0
if and only if
\Delta_{p}u(o)=0
. Moreover, if
u
is not constant then
\inf u=0
.

(d)

The Green’s function with pole at
o
exists if and only if
u
is not constant and in that case

g(x,o)=cu(x)
with
c=[m(o)\Delta_{p}u(o)]^{-1/(p-1)}
. In particular,
g(x,o)\in D_{0}^{p}
and
g(o,o)^{-1/(p-1)}g(x,o)
is the
p
-harmonic potential of
(o,X)
.

Proof.

The proof follows from standard variational arguments. For completeness and the convenience of the reader, we provide an outline of the arguments.

(a)
: Since the set

\mathcal{K}_{n}=\{\varphi\in C(X)\,:\,\varphi(o)=1,\,\,\varphi=0\,\text{ on }\,X\setminus X_{n}\}

is closed and convex in the uniformly convex space
(D_{0}^{p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p})
and since
\mathcal{E}_{p}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p}
have the same minimizers on
\mathcal{K}_{n}
,
\mathcal{E}_{p}
admits a (unique) minimizer
u_{n}
which satisfies
u_{n}(o)=1
and
\cc_{p}(o,X_{n})=\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(\varphi)
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
. For every
\varphi_{0}
supported in
X_{n}\setminus\{o\}
, we have
u_{n}+t\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
and

\frac{\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{n}+t\varphi_{0})-\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{n})}{t}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y}b(x,y)\frac{|\nabla_{x,y}u_{n}+t\nabla_{x,y}\varphi_{0}|^{p}-|\nabla_{x,y}u_{n}|^{p}}{t}.

As
t\to 0
, the fraction inside the summation tends to

p|\nabla_{x,y}u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla_{x,y}u_{n}\nabla_{x,y}\varphi_{0},

and an application of the mean value theorem together with Young’s inequality shows that

\left|\frac{|\nabla_{x,y}u_{n}+t\nabla_{x,y}\varphi_{0}|^{p}-|\nabla_{x,y}u_{n}|^{p}}{t}\right|\leq C_{p}(|\nabla_{x,y}u_{n}|^{p}+|\nabla_{x,y}\varphi_{0}|^{p}),

so that, letting
t\to 0
and applying dominated convergence yield

(3.3)

0=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y}b(x,y)\left(\nabla_{x,y}u_{n}\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}\nabla_{x,y}\varphi_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{p,X}(u_{n},\varphi_{0}).

In particular, letting
\varphi_{0}=\mathds{1}_{x_{o}}
with
x_{o}\in X_{n}\setminus\{o\}
shows that

\Delta_{p}u_{n}(x_{o})=0.

The last statement in
(a)
follows by comparison, see Lemma
A.1
.

(b)
: Replacing
\mathcal{K}_{n}
with

\mathcal{K}=\{v\in D_{0}^{p}\,:\,v(o)=1,\,0\leq v\leq 1\}

and arguing as above shows that there exists a unique
u\in\mathcal{K}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{p}(u)=\cc_{p}(o)
, and
u
satisfies (
3.2
) and by the strong minimum principle
0<u\leq 1
, see e.g. Lemma
A.2
. By comparison,
0\leq u_{n}\leq u
so that the sequence

(u_{n})
constructed in
(a)
converges monotonically to a function
\tilde{u}
satisfying
u_{n}\leq\tilde{u}\leq u
. By Fatou’s lemma

\mathcal{E}_{p}(\tilde{u})\leq\lim_{n}\mathcal{E}_{p}(u_{n})=\lim_{n}\cc_{p}(o,X_{n})=\lim_{n}\cc_{p}(o)=\mathcal{E}_{p}(u),

so, by uniqueness of the minimizer,
u=\tilde{u}
. It follows from Lemma
2.4
that

u_{n}\to u
in
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p}
-norm, as required.

(c)
: Since
\mathcal{E}_{p}(u)=\cc_{p}(o)
and, by connectedness,
u
is constant if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{p}(u)=0
, the first assertion follows immediately. It is obvious that if
u
is constant then
\Delta_{p}u(o)=0
. On the other hand, if
\Delta_{p}u(o)=0
then
u
is
p
-harmonic on
X
and, since
u\leq 1
and
u(o)=1
,
u
is constant by the strong maximum principle. To prove the last assertion, assume that
u
is not constant and let
\inf u=\gamma
. Then
\hat{u}=(1-\gamma)^{-1}(u-\gamma)
is a positive solution of (
3.2
) with
\inf\hat{u}=0
. The comparison argument above shows that
\tilde{u}\leq\hat{u}
so that
\inf\tilde{u}=\inf\hat{u}=0
and therefore
\inf u=0
.

(d)
: Assume first that the function
u
constructed in
(b)
is not constant. Since
u(o)=1
while
u(x)\leq 1
,
\Delta_{p}u(o)\geq 0
and therefore
\Delta_{p}u(o)>0
by
(c)
. A straightforward computation shows that, if
c=[m(o)\Delta_{p}u(o)]^{-1/(p-1)}
, then the function
\tilde{u}=cu(x)
is non-negative, satisfies
\Delta_{p}\tilde{u}=\hat{\mathds{1}}_{o}
and
\inf\tilde{u}=0
so that
g(x,o)=\tilde{u}(x)
is the required minimal positive solution of (
3.1
). For the converse, assume that there exists a minimal positive solution
g(x,o)
to (
3.1
). We claim that if
c=g(o,o)
then
v=c^{-1}g(x,o)
is the function
u
constructed in
(b)
and therefore
u
is not constant and
g(x,o)\in D_{0}^{p}
. To prove the claim, note that it follows from the usual comparison argument that
u_{n}\leq v
for every
n
, so that
u=\lim_{n}u_{n}\leq v
. Since
u(o)=v(o)=1
,
\Delta_{p}u(o)\geq\Delta_{p}v(o)>0
. It follows that
\hat{u}=\left(\Delta_{p}v(0)/\Delta_{p}u(0)\right)^{1/(p-1)}u
satisfies

\Delta_{p}\hat{u}=\Delta_{p}v\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{u}\leq u\leq v.

We conclude that
0\leq c\hat{u}\leq cv=g(x,o)
satisfies
\Delta_{p}(c\hat{u})=\Delta_{p}g(\cdot,o)
. By minimality,
c\hat{u}=g(\cdot,o)
and the claim follows.
∎

Combining Lemma
2.10

-parabolicity in terms of existence of null sequences, non-existence of Green’s functions and vanishing of nonnegative superharmonic functions.

(i)

\iff

3.4.
Kelvin-Nevanlinna-Royden-type characterization

Before we state the main result of this subsection, we need to introduce flows on graphs.

Let
V\subseteq X
. A function
F\colon V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
is called
flow
on
V
if it is skew-symmetric, i.e.,

F(x,y)=-F(y,x),\qquad x,y\in V.

The
divergence
of a flow
F
on
X
is then defined by

\Div_{b}F(x):=\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)F(x,y),\qquad x\in X,

provided the sum on the right hand side converges absolutely. An easy application of Hölder’s inequality shows that this is so whenever

F\in\ell^{q}(X\times X,b)
for some
q\in[1,\infty]
.

For flows, we have the following version of Green’s formula.

Lemma 3.8

(Green’s formula for flows)
.

Let
F\in\ell^{q}(X\times X,b)
for some
q\in[1,\infty]
. Then, for all
\varphi\in C_{c}(X)
we have

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)F(x,y)(\nabla_{x,y}\varphi)=\sum_{x\in X}\Div_{b}F(x)\varphi(x)m(x).

Proof.

Note that since
\varphi
is finitely supported the above sums are finite. Using the fact that
F
is a flow we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)F(x,y)(\nabla_{x,y}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)F(x,y)\varphi(x)+\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)F(y,x)\varphi(y)

\displaystyle=2\cdot\sum_{x\in X}\Div_{b}F(x)\varphi(x)m(x).\qed

The following is a generalisation of
[
Pra04
]
for the standard Laplacian on locally finite graphs to weighted Laplacians on locally summable graphs. We also note that the proofs differ. Moreover, on manifolds such a result is well-known, see e.g.
[
PS14
]
. For
p>1
such a results was first proven on manifolds in
[
GT99
]
, and for
p=2
, it goes back to
[
LS84
]
. For a version of this result in the linear case on graphs, see
[
KLW21
, Section 6.5]
.

Proposition 3.9

(Kelvin-Nevanlinna-Royden characterization)

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
c=0
. Then the following are equivalent:

The graph is
p
-parabolic.

(ii)

,

, and all (some) measures

such that
(\Div_{b}F)_{+}\in\ell^{1}(X,m)
or
(\Div_{b}F)_{-}\in\ell^{1}(X,m)

\sum_{x\in X}\Div_{b}F(x)m(x)=0.

Proof.

(i)

\implies

3.6.

Here, we show a maximum principle-type characterization of

-parabolicity which goes back to Ahlfors
[

]
, where a corresponding version was proven for Riemann surfaces and

. For

[

,

]
. For historical reasons, we state the characterization in terms of subharmonic functions.

Before stating our result, we need a lemma which is also used in Subsection
3.9
. It is the discrete version of the result stating that the pointwise maximum of subsolutions is a subsolution. The proof, which we reproduce for completeness, follows from direct computations. See also
[
Fis24
, Lemma 4.4]
for a version valid for an arbitrary family of subsolutions.

Lemma 3.11

(
\vee
-stable)
.

Let
V\subseteq X
,
p\in[1,\infty)
and let
c\in C(X)
arbitrary. Let
u_{i}\in F^{p}(V)
,

satisfy
L_{p}u_{i}(x)\leq f(x)\left(u_{i}(x)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}
on
V

. Then, the pointwise maximum

is also in

L_{p}w(x)\leq f(x)\left(w(x)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}
on
V
.

Proof.

Since
|u_{1}\vee u_{2}|\leq|u_{1}|+|u_{2}|
, it follows from the
p
-triangle inequality that

.
Fix
x\in V
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
u_{1}(x)\geq u_{2}(x)
. Then,

\nabla_{x,y}w=u_{1}(x)-w(y)\leq\nabla_{x,y}u_{1}.

Since
\left(\cdot\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is monotone increasing, we get

\Delta_{p}w(x)\leq\Delta_{p}u_{1}(x)\leq\left(f(x)-\frac{c(x)}{m(x)}\right)\left(u_{1}(x)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}=\left(f(x)-\frac{c(x)}{m(x)}\right)\left(w(x)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle},

i.e.,
L_{p}w(x)\leq f(x)\left(w(x)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}
on
V
.
∎

: Let the graph be
p
-hyperbolic. By Proposition
3.7
, there exists a non-constant
p
-subharmonic function
u
which is bounded from above. Hence, there are
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
such that
u(x_{1})\neq u(x_{2})
. Set
\gamma:=(u(x_{1})+u(x_{2}))/{2}
and let
V:=\left\{x\in X:u(x)>\gamma\right\}
. Then
V
is non-empty and

\sup_{\partial_{e}V}u\leq\gamma<\sup_{\overline{V}}u.

(i)

\implies

(ii)
: Let the graph be
p
-parabolic and let
V\subsetneq X
. Suppose that
u
is bounded on
\overline{V}
and
p
-subharmonic on
V
. We show that the additional assumption
\sup_{\partial_{e}V}u<\sup_{\overline{V}}u
results in a contradiction.

Indeed, by the additional assumption, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\max_{V}u>\sup_{\partial_{e}V}u+\varepsilon
. Set

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}:=\begin{cases}u\vee(\sup_{\partial_{e}V}u+\varepsilon)\qquad&\text{in }V\\
\sup_{\partial_{e}V}u+\varepsilon\qquad&\text{in }X\setminus V.\end{cases}

By Lemma
3.11
,
u_{\varepsilon}
is
p
-subharmonic in
V
, and it is obviously harmonic in
X\setminus\overline{V}
. Furthermore, for all
x\in\partial_{e}V
we have

m(x)\Delta_{p}u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{y\in V}b(x,y)\left(\sup_{\partial_{e}V}u+\varepsilon-(u(y)\vee(\sup_{\partial_{e}V}u+\varepsilon))\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}\leq 0.

Hence,
u_{\varepsilon}
is subharmonic and bounded on
X
. By Proposition
3.7
,
u_{\varepsilon}
is constant which contradicts the additional assumption and finishes the proof.
∎

3.7.
A Lenz-Puchert-Schmidt-type characterization

Here, we show a generalization to all
p\geq 1
of a characterization of (
2
-)parabolicity very recently obtained in
[
LPS24
]
. The first basic idea is to connect parabolicity not only to the vanishing of the capacity of singletons (or, equivalently, finite sets) in the graph but also to the vanishing of a suitably defined capacity of subsets of the boundary of the graph.

The second basic idea is to show that this is also equivalent to a statement closely connected to the Khas’miniskiĭ-type characterization in terms of finite energy
p
-superharmonic functions which will also play a role in Subsection
3.10
.

3.7.1.
Boundaries, limits, and capacities

Let
Y
be a compact topological Hausdorff space. Then
Y
is called
compactification
of
X
, if
Y
contains a copy of
X
, the restriction of the topology of
Y
on
X
is the discrete topology, and
X
is dense in
Y
. The
boundary

\partial_{Y}X
of
x
in
Y
is then defined by
\partial_{Y}X=Y\setminus X
.

A
pseudometric
on
X
is a function
\sigma\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)
which is symmetric and satisfies the triangle inequality. We write
x\simeq y
if
\sigma(x,y)=0
and we extend this equivalence relation to sequences in
X
by saying that two sequences
(x_{n})
and
(y_{n})
are
\sigma
-equivalent in
X
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma(x_{n},y_{n})=0
. The
completion

\overline{X}^{\sigma}
of
X
with respect to
\sigma
is the set of all equivalence classes of
\sigma
-Cauchy sequences. The
boundary
is then defined as
\partial_{\sigma}X=\overline{X}^{\sigma}\setminus(X/\simeq)
.

If
\sigma
is a metric inducing the discrete topology on
X
, then

\partial_{\sigma}X=\partial_{\overline{X}^{\sigma}}X.

Let
\sigma
be a pseudometric. Then, for all
r\geq 0
and
x\in X
, we set

B_{r,\sigma}(x):=\left\{y\in X:\sigma(x,y)\leq r\right\}.

If
\sigma
is a metric on
X
that induces the discrete topology and
f\in C(X)
, then we set for all
A\subseteq\overline{X}^{\sigma}
,

\liminf_{x\to A}f(x):=\sup_{O\text{ open in }\overline{X}^{\sigma},\,A\subseteq O}\inf_{x\in O\cap X}f(x),

and

\liminf_{x\to\infty}f(x):=\sup_{K\subseteq X\text{ finite }}\inf_{x\in X\setminus K}f(x).

We note that
\liminf_{x\to\partial_{\sigma}X}f(x)\geq\liminf_{x\to\infty}f(x)
and that equality holds if
\overline{X}^{\sigma}
is compact (see
[
LPS24
, Proposition 3.2]
).

Now we turn to capacities, and extend the standard definition to the boundary of
X
.

Let
b
be a graph on
X
,
c\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
. Then the
(o,p)
-capacity
of
V\subseteq X
with respect to
\mathcal{E}_{p}
and
o\in X
is defined by

\cc_{o,p}(V):=\inf\{\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{o,p}\,:\,u\in D^{p},\,u\geq 1\text{ on }V\},

where we use the convention that the infimum over the empty set is
\infty
.

Since
c\geq 0
, the normal contraction
0\vee\cdot\wedge 1
can be used to show that the infimum can be taken over all
u\in D
such that
0\leq u\leq 1
on
X
and
u=1
on
V
.

Let
Y
be a compactification of
X
. Then, we set for all
A\subseteq Y
,

\cc_{o,p}(A):=\inf\{\cc_{o,p}(O\cap X)\,:\,O\text{ is open in }Y,\,A\subseteq O\}.

Note that both definitions coincide on subsets of
X
, and thus using the same notation does not lead to confusions. Moreover, we note that
\cc_{o,p}(A)=0
if and only if there exists a sequence
(O_{n})
of open sets in
Y
with
A\subseteq O_{n}
and a sequence
(f_{n})
in
D^{p}
such that
f_{n}\geq 1_{O_{n}}\cap X
, and
\left\lVert f_{n}\right\rVert_{o,p}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

3.7.2.
Small perturbations

The following lemma is needed in the main result to go from a pseudometric to a metric. It is the generalisation of
[
LPS24
, Proposition 2.6]
from
p=2
to
p\geq 1
, and also from
c=0
to
c\geq 0
. We point out that the proof of part (a) is different from the original one where the bilinearity of the form
\mathcal{E}_{2}
was used. We use Minkowski’s inequality instead.

Lemma 3.13

(Small perturbations)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
c\geq 0
on
X
.

(a)

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a strictly positive function
f_{\varepsilon}\in C(X)
such that for any
g\in C(X)
with
g(x)\in(-f_{\varepsilon}(x),f_{\varepsilon}(x))
,
x\in X
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)<\varepsilon.

(b)

For all
\varepsilon>0
and
u\in D^{p}
, there exists a function
u_{\varepsilon}\in D^{p}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}(x)\neq u_{\varepsilon}(y)
for all
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y
,
\left\lVert u-u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{\infty}<\varepsilon
, and

\left\lvert\mathcal{E}_{p}(u-u_{\varepsilon})\right\rvert<\varepsilon.

Proof.

(a)
: Having fixed
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\varepsilon>0
, choose
f_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\sum_{x\in X}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathcal{E}_{p}(\mathds{1}_{x})^{1/p}<\varepsilon^{1/p}.

Now, let
g
be such that
g(x)\in(-f_{\varepsilon}(x),f_{\varepsilon}(x))
,
x\in X
. Let
(K_{n})
be an increasing exhaustion of
X
with finite sets, and set

g_{n}:=g\,\mathds{1}_{K_{n}}.

Then,
(g_{n})
converges pointwise to
g
. Moreover,
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{1/p}
is a semi-norm on
D^{p}
, where the triangle inequality follows from Minkowski’s inequality. Hence, using Fatou’s lemma (i.e., the semi-continuity of
\mathcal{E}_{p}
) and the triangle inequality with respect to
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{1/p}
, we get

\mathcal{E}_{p}^{1/p}(g)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{p}^{1/p}(g_{n})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{x\in K_{n}}\left\lvert g(x)\right\rvert\mathcal{E}_{p}^{1/p}(\mathds{1}_{x})\leq\sum_{x\in X}\left\lvert f_{\varepsilon}(x)\right\rvert\mathcal{E}_{p}^{1/p}(\mathds{1}_{x})\\
<\varepsilon^{1/p}.

This shows the first claim.

(b)
: This follows now from
(a)
in the same way as in
[
LPS24
]
. For convenience, we show the nice argument here as well. Let
\varepsilon>0
and take
f_{\varepsilon}
as in
(a)
such that
f_{\varepsilon}(x)<\varepsilon
. Let
F\in C(X)
be a function such that
F(x)-F(y)
is irrational for any
x,y\in X,x\neq y
. Moreover, let
g\in C(X)
be such that
g(x)\in(-f_{\varepsilon}(x),f_{\varepsilon}(x))
and
u(x)-F(x)-g(x)
is rational for all
x\in X
. Define
u_{\varepsilon}(x):=u(x)-g(x)
,
x\in X
. Then,
u_{\varepsilon}\in D^{p}
by the triangle inequality. Furthermore,
\left\lVert u-u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\infty}<\varepsilon
as well as
\mathcal{E}_{p}(u-u_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{E}_{p}(g)<\varepsilon
.

For all
x\neq y
, we have

\nabla_{x,y}u_{\varepsilon}=\nabla_{x,y}F+\nabla_{x,y}(u-F-g).

The right-hand side above is a sum of a irrational and a rational number, and thus,
\nabla_{x,y}u_{\varepsilon}\neq 0
. ∎

3.7.3.
Intrinsic and path pseudometrics

Two special pseudometrics are of particular interest for us: path pseudometrics and intrinsic metrics. Here, we recall some basic definitions and statements. We want to mention that we are aware of another generalisation of the notion of intrinsic metrics for
p\neq 2
in
[
KM16
]
. However, we do a different generalisation of (
p=2
)-case here, which seems to be more natural in our setting. Path pseudometrics are discussed in detail in
[
KLW21
, Section 11.2]
and in
[
KM19
]
, compare also with
[
LPS24
]
.

A non-negative symmetric function
w
on
X\times X
is called
p
-intrinsic
with respect to the edge weight
b
and the measure

0\leq m\in C(X)
if, for all
x\in X
, we have

\sum_{x\in X}b(x,y)w^{p}(x,y)\leq m(x).

We will be mostly interested in
p
-intrinsic functions which are also pseudometrics.

An infinite sequence
(x_{n})
of pairwise different vertices such that
x_{n}\sim x_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
is called
ray
on
X
. For a symmetric function
w\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)
we define the
length

l_{w}(\gamma)
of the path
\gamma=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
with respect to
w
via

l_{w}(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}w(x_{i},x_{i+1}),

and the length of the ray
\gamma=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is given by
l_{w}(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}w(x_{i},x_{i+1}).

The
path pseudometric

d_{w}\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty]
with respect to
w
on
X
is given by

d_{w}(x,y):=\inf\{l_{w}(\gamma)\,:\,\gamma\text{ is a path or ray from }x\text{ to }y\}.

Moreover, we call a symmetric function
w\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)

alternative edge weight
if
w(x,y)>0
whenever
x\sim y
,
x,y\in X
.

There is a simple condition under which a symmetric function induces a discrete path metric.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
w
be a non-negative alternative edge-weight on
X\times X
such that for every
x
there exists
r_{x}>0
such that the set
\{y\sim x\,:\,w(x,y)<r_{x}\}

is finite, then
d_{w}
is a metric and
(X,d_{w})
is discrete.

Proof.

The assumptions imply that
\delta_{x}=\inf_{y\sim x}w(x,y)>0
so that
d_{w}(x,x^{\prime})\geq\delta_{x}
for every
x^{\prime}\neq x
and the conclusion follows at once.
∎

3.7.4.
The characterization and a corollary

We are now in a position to show the main result of this section, Proposition
3.15
, which is a generalisation of
[
LPS24
, Theorem 4.2]
from
p=2
to
p\geq 1
. For
p=2
, parts of it appeared already in
[
Sch17
]
. Moreover, we want to mention that in the continuum, the implication “
(i)

\implies

(iii)
” is well-known for general

and appears e.g. in
[

]
. The proof of Proposition

goes along the lines of its linear version in
[

]
. Since it is a very recent result, we show it here in detail.

Proposition 3.15

(Lenz-Puchert-Schmidt-type characterization)
.

Let
c=0
, and
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The graph is
p
-parabolic.

(ii)

For one (or, equivalently, every) compactification
Y
of
X
, we have

\cc_{o,p}(\partial_{Y}X)=0.

(iii)

There is a function
f\in D^{p}
such that
\liminf_{x\to\infty}f(x)=\infty
.

(iv)

There is a
p
-intrinsic metric
\sigma
that induces the discrete topology on
X
such that distance balls with respect to
\sigma
are finite.

(v)

There exists a
p
-intrinsic alternative edge weight
w
with respect to some finite measure such that the distance balls with respect to
d_{w}
are finite.

Proof.

(i)

\implies

(ii)
: We can use Proposition
3.3
and get the existence of an increasing null-sequence
(e_{n})
in
C_{c}(X)
such that
0\leq e_{n}\nearrow 1
. Consider
f_{n}:=1-e_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
0\leq f_{n}\leq 1
and
f_{n}=1
outside of a finite set. Hence,

0\leq\cc^{p}_{o,p}(\partial_{Y}X)\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(f_{n})+\left\lvert f_{n}(o)\right\rvert^{p}=\mathcal{E}_{p}(e_{n})+\left\lvert 1-e_{n}(o)\right\rvert^{p}\to 0,\qquad n\to\infty.

(ii)

\implies

(iii)
: By assumption, there exists a sequence of open sets in
Y

(O_{n})
with
\partial_{Y}X\subseteq O_{n}
and a sequence of functions
(f_{n})
such that
f_{n}\geq\mathds{1}_{O_{n}\cap X}
for which

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert f_{n}\right\rVert_{o,p}\to 0.

By passing to a subsequence, which we also denote by
(f_{n})
, we can assume that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lVert f_{n}\right\rVert_{o,p}<\infty
. Thus,
f:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}
converges in the Banach space
(D^{p},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p})
, and therefore
f\in D^{p}
. By construction,
f\geq n
on
U_{n}:=\cap_{k=1}^{n}O_{k}
. Since
U_{n}
is open and
\partial_{Y}X\subseteq U_{n}
,
\liminf_{x\to\partial_{Y}X}f(x)\geq n
. Taking the limit yields the result.

(iii)

\implies

(iv)
: By Lemma
3.13
, we can assume that the function
f
in
(iii)
is injective. Hence
\sigma_{f}\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)
defined by
\sigma_{f}(x,y):=\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}f\right\rvert
is an intrinsic metric with respect to the measure
0\leq m_{f}\in C(X)
defined by

m_{f}(x):=\sum_{x\in X}b(x,y)\sigma^{p}_{f}(x,y).

Note that
m_{f}
is a finite measure since
m_{f}(X)=2\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)<\infty
. Since
\liminf_{x\to\infty}f(x)=\infty
,
B_{r,\sigma}(o)
is a finite set for all
o\in X
and
r\geq 0
. Moreover, by the choice of
f
,
\sigma_{f}
induces the discrete topology on
X
.

(iv)

\implies

(i)
: Let
\sigma
be a
p
-intrinsic metric with respect to the measure
0\leq m\in C(X)
which induces the discrete topology and such that
B_{r,\sigma}(o)
is a finite set for all
r\geq 0
and
o\in X
. Let
(K_{n})
be an increasing exhaustion of
X
with finite sets. Let us consider the functions
\sigma_{n}\colon X\to[0,\infty)
defined via

\sigma_{n}(x):=\inf_{y\in K_{n}}\sigma(x,y),

and let
e_{n}:=0\vee(1-\sigma_{n})\geq 0
. Since
K_{n}
is finite and distance balls are finite,
e_{n}\in C_{c}(X)
. We are left to show that
(e_{n})
is a null-sequence. Firstly, note that
\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}\sigma_{n}\right\rvert\leq\sigma(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
. Since
0\vee\cdot
is a normal contraction and
c=0
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(e_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}_{p}(\sigma_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(x,y)\in(X\times X)\setminus(K_{n}\times K_{n})}b(x,y)\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}\sigma_{n}\right\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(x,y)\in(X\times X)\setminus(K_{n}\times K_{n})}b(x,y)\left\lvert\sigma(x,y)\right\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq m(X\setminus K_{n})\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

Hence,
(e_{n})
is a null-sequence and therefore, the graph is
p
-parabolic.

(iv)

\implies

(v)
: According to
[
LPS24
, Lemma 2.7]
, since
\sigma
is a metric,
d_{\sigma}\geq\sigma
and
d_{\sigma}(x,y)=\sigma(x,y)
is
x\sim y
so distance balls with respect to

are contained in the corresponding balls with respect to

and
\sigma
is obviously a

-intrinsic weight, so we can take

.

(v)

\implies

(iv)
: Since
d_{w}(x,y)\leq w(x,y)
for all

such that

, also

is
p
-intrinsic. Moreover, it is a pseudometric. If we can show that it induces the discrete topology it follows that

is actually a metric. This can be seen as follows: Since for all

and

are finite, so are also the sets

. By Lemma

,

is a metric and
(X,d_{w})
is discrete.
∎

We also get the following nice characterization on locally finite graphs as a consequence of the previous proposition. It was observed first in

(Yamasaki-type characterization)

3.8.
The Area Perspective

Before we state our main result, we need some definitions. By
d
we denote the
combinatorial graph metric
, i.e., for all
x,y\in X
the number
d(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}
denotes the least number of edges of paths connecting
x
and
y
. Furthermore, for
V\subseteq X
and
x\in X
, we set
d(V,x):=\min_{y\in V}d(y,x)
, and

\displaystyle B_{r}(V):=\left\{x\in X:d(V,x)\leq r\right\},

\displaystyle S_{r}(V):=\left\{x\in X:d(V,x)=r\right\}.

A function
f
is called
spherically symmetric
with respect to
V
if there exists a function
g\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(x)=g(r)
for all
x\in S_{r}(V)
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. To simplify the notation, we will also write
f(r)=f(x)
for all
x\in S_{r}(V)
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

A graph with potential
c
is called
model graph
on
V
if the outer and inner weighted degrees with respect to
V
, defined respectively by

k_{\pm}(x)=m(x)^{-1}\sum_{y\sim x\atop d(V,y)=d(V,x)\pm 1}b(x,y),

and the weighted potential are spherically symmetric with respect to
V
.
We will (mostly) consider the case where
V=\{x_{0}\}
, and write for simplicity
B_{r}(x_{0})
and
S_{r}(x_{0})
, possibly omitting
x_{0}
and writing
B_{r}
,
S_{r}
when the reference point is fixed.
In this case, for a spherically symmetric function
f
on a model graph we have the following formulas for the
p
-Laplacian:

\Delta_{p}f(0)=k_{+}(0)\left(f(0)-f(1)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}

and, for
r>0
,

\Delta_{p}f(r)=k_{+}(r)\left(f(r)-f(r+1)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}+k_{-}(r)\left(f(r)-f(r-1)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}.

We also use the following boundary notation: for every
r\geq 0
we set

\partial B(r)=\sum_{x\in S_{r},y\in S_{r+1}}b(x,y).

It follows from the definitions that for model graphs

(3.4)

m(S_{r})=\frac{\partial B(r)}{k_{+}(r)}=\frac{\partial B(r-1)}{k_{-}(r)}

and that all quantities are finite for locally finite graphs.

The goal of this subsection is to prove the following result.

Proposition 3.17

(Area characterization)
.

Let the graph be locally finite,
c=0
, and
p\in(1,\infty)
.

(a)

If

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\partial B(r)}\right)^{1/(p-1)}=\infty

for some
x_{0}\in X
, then the graph is
p
-parabolic.

(b)

A
model
graph with respect to
x_{0}\in X
is
p
-parabolic if and only if

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\partial B(r)}\right)^{1/(p-1)}=\infty.

Furthermore, if the model graph is
p
-hyperbolic then it’s Green’s function
g
with respect to
x_{0}\in X
is radial and given by

g(r)=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\partial B(k)}\right)^{1/(p-1)},\qquad r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Proof.

(a)
: We prove that, if

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\partial B(r))^{1/(p-1)}}=\infty,

then there exists a null-sequence
(e_{n})
as in Proposition
3.3
.

First of all, if
\varphi
is a spherically symmetric function in
C_{c}(X)
such that
\varphi(x_{0})=1
and
\varphi(x)=0
for every
x\in S_{r}
,
r\geq n+1
then, by spherical symmetry
\nabla_{x,y}\varphi_{n}=0
if
x,y\in S_{r}
and therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p}(\varphi_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)\left\lvert\nabla_{x,y}\varphi_{n}\right\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\sum_{x\in S_{r}\atop y\in S_{r+1}}b(x,y)\left\lvert\varphi_{n}(x)-\varphi_{n}(y)\right\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\sum_{x\in S_{r}\atop y\in S_{r+1}}b(x,y)\left\lvert\varphi_{n}(r)-\varphi_{n}(r+1)\right\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\left\lvert\varphi_{n}(r)-\varphi_{n}(r+1)\right\rvert^{p}\partial B(r).

Next, define
e_{n}(x)=e_{n}(r)
recursively by
e_{n}(0)=1
,
e_{n}(r)=0
if
r\geq n=1
and

e_{n}(r)-e_{n}(r+1)=\frac{c_{n}}{(\partial B(r))^{1/(p-1)}}\,\text{ if }\,0\leq r\leq n-1,

with

c_{n}=\left(\sum_{r=0}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{\partial B(r)}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\right)^{-1}.

Thus, for every
1\leq r\leq n

e_{n}(r)=e_{n}(0)-\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}[e_{n}(j)-e_{n}(j+1)]=1-c_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\left(1\over\partial B(j)\right)^{1/(p-1)}.

It follows that
e_{n}\nearrow 1
and, using the previous computation,

\mathcal{E}_{p}(e_{n})=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\partial B(r)\frac{c_{n}^{p}}{\left(\partial B(r)\right)^{p/(p-1)}}=c_{n}^{p-1}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
. This concludes the proof of
(a)
.

(b)
: We only need to prove that, if

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\partial B(r))^{1/(p-1)}}<\infty,

then the graph has a Green’s function.
We define
g\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that, for every
r\geq 0
and
x\in S_{r}
,

g(x)=g(r)=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\partial B(k)}\right)^{1/(p-1)}.

Then
g
is spherically symmetric with

g(r)-g(r+1)=\frac{1}{\partial B(r)^{1/(p-1)}}.

It follows from the expression of the
\Delta_{p}
for radially symmetric functions on model graphs and (
3.4
) that

\Delta_{p}g(0)=\frac{\partial B(0)}{m(0)}\frac{1}{\partial B(0)}=\frac{1}{m(0)}

and, for
r>0
, ,

\Delta_{p}g(r)=\frac{k_{+}(r)}{\partial B(r)}-\frac{k_{-}(r)}{\partial B(r-1)}=0,

as required to complete the proof.
∎

3.9.
Weak Maximum Principle and Liouville Property

In this subsection we introduce the weak maximum principle and show its equivalence with the
p
-parabolicity of the graph. Moreover, we give the definition of the Khas’minskiĭ-type criterion and prove that it is a sufficient condition for the
p
-parabolicity to hold. In this subsection, we consider
c=0
and
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Definition 3.18
.

We say that a graph
G
satisfies the weak maximum principle (
3.18
) if for every nonconstant, bounded above function
u\in F^{p}
and for every
\gamma<u^{*}:=\sup u

\sup_{\Omega_{\gamma}}\Delta_{p}u>0

on the superlevel set
\Omega_{\gamma}:=\left\{x\in X\,:\,u(x)>\gamma\right\}
.

Definition 3.19
.

We say that a graph
G
satisfies the Khas’minskiĭ criterion (
3.19
) if there exists a function
w
satisfying
w\to\infty
at
\infty
and

\Delta_{p}w\geq 0\text{ on }X\setminus K,

for some finite set
K
.

We prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
c=0
and
p\in[1,\infty)
.

(a)

A graph is
p
-parabolic if and only if (
3.18
) holds;

(b)

If
(
3.19
) holds then so does (
3.18
) and therefore the graph is
p
-parabolic.

Proof.

(a)
: First, assume that the graph is
p
-hyperbolic, so that by Proposition
3.7
there exists a nonconstant, bounded above function
u
such that
\Delta_{p}u\leq 0
. Then
\Omega_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset
for every
\gamma<u^{*}=\sup u
and

\sup_{\Omega_{\gamma}}\Delta_{p}u\leq 0,

which contradicts (
3.18
). This shows that (
3.18
) implies
p
-parabolicity.

For the converse, assume that there exists a nonconstant, bounded above function
u
and
\gamma<u^{*}
such that
\sup_{\Omega_{\gamma}}\Delta_{p}u\leq 0
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
u^{*}>0
and
\gamma=0
. Define
v:X\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle v:=u\vee 0=\begin{cases}u&\text{ in }\Omega_{0}\\
0&\text{ in }X\setminus\Omega_{0}.\end{cases}

An easy calculation shows that
v
is subharmonic (or apply Lemma
3.11
), thus negating the
p
-parabolicity.

(b)
:
Assume that (
3.19
) holds and let
w
be such that
w\to\infty
and
\Delta_{p}w\geq 0
on
X\setminus K
for some finite set
K
. By translating
w
, if necessary, we may assume that
\min_{K}w=0
, so that
w>\max_{\partial_{e}K}w\geq 0
on
X\setminus K
by the strong minimum principle.

Assume by contradiction that (
3.18
) does not hold, so that there exists a nonconstant, bounded above function
u
such that
\sup_{\Omega_{\gamma}}\Delta_{p}u\leq 0
for some
\gamma<u^{*}
. Again by the strong maximum principle,
u
does not attain a maximum. We may assume that
\gamma
is such that
\max_{{K}}u<\gamma<u^{*}
, so that
\Omega_{\gamma}\cap{K}=\emptyset
. Let
x_{0}\in\Omega_{\gamma}
such that
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}<u(x_{0})
and choose
\varepsilon
such that
\varepsilon w(x_{0})<\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma
. We now define the set
A:=\left\{x\in\Omega_{\gamma}\,:\,u(x)>\gamma+\varepsilon w(x)\right\}
, which is clearly a subset of
\Omega_{\gamma}
by definition. Note that
x_{0}\in A\neq\emptyset
and that
A
is finite because of the properties of
w
. Note that the following system is then satisfied

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}u\leq 0\leq\Delta_{p}(\gamma+\varepsilon w)&\text{ in }A\\
u\leq\gamma+\varepsilon w&\text{ on }\partial_{e}A.\end{cases}

By the comparison principle (Lemma
A.1
), we conclude that
u\leq\gamma+\varepsilon w
on
A
, which is a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

3.10.
Khas’miniskiĭ-type characterization

This subsection is devoted to show a Khas’miniskiĭ-type characterization in the quasi-linear setting on graphs. On manifolds a corresponding version is given in
[
Val12a
]
, see also
[
Val12
]
. The main idea is to use the existence of solutions to the so-called obstacle problem. Even though the obstacle problem is well studied in the quasi-linear setting in the continuum and on metric length spaces, see e.g.
[
BB11
,
HKM06
]
, we are not aware of an analysis on discrete graphs. This subsection uses a result of Subsection
3.7
.

Recall the notation (in the case of
c=0
)

\mathcal{E}_{p,{V}}(u,v):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in{V}}b(x,y)\left(\nabla_{x,y}u\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}\nabla_{x,y}v,

if
V\subseteq X
and
u,v\in C(V)
.

3.10.1.
The obstacle problem

Here, we define the obstacle problem and study the existence and uniqueness of solutions of this problem. The counterpart in the continuum can be found in
[
HKM06
]
.

Let
c=0
and
p\in(1,\infty)
in the following. Given functions
\psi,\theta\in C(X)
and a subset
V\subset X
we define the set
K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
by

K_{\psi,\theta}(V):=\left\{v\in D^{p}(\overline{V}):v\geq\psi\text{ in }V,v=\theta\text{ on }\partial_{e}V\right\}.

Recall that, having fixed a point
o\in\overline{V}
then
D^{p}(\overline{V}),p\in(1,\infty),
is a uniformly convex Banach space with respect to the norm

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{o,p}=\left(|u(o)|^{p}+\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u)\right)^{1/p}=\left(|u(o)|^{p}+\sum_{x,y\in\overline{V}}b(x,y)|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{p})\right)^{1/p},

and that changing the point
o
gives rise to an equivalent norm.

Lemma 3.21
.

The set
K_{\psi,\theta}
is a convex closed subset of
D^{p}(\overline{V})
.

Proof.

The convexity of
K_{\psi,\theta}
is clear. To prove that it is closed, assume that
(u_{i})
is a sequence in
K_{\psi,\theta}
such that
\left\lVert u_{i}-u\right\rVert_{o,p}\to 0
. Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
shows that
u_{i}\to u
pointwise in
\overline{V}
and it follows that
u\in D^{p}(\overline{V})
satisfies
u\geq\psi
in
V
and
u=\theta
on
\partial_{e}V
, so that
u\in K_{\psi,\theta}
which therefore is closed.
∎

Definition 3.22
.

We say that
u\in K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
is a
solution to the obstacle problem
on
V
with obstacle
\psi
and boundary data
\theta
if for all
v\in K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
we have

\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,v-u)\geq 0.

Note that
for
u,v\in D^{p}(\overline{V})
the sum on the left-hand side converges absolutely and, since
u-v
vanishes on
\partial_{e}V
, if
V
is finite
then
\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,v-u)
is defined provided
u\in F^{p}(V)
and

\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,v-u)=\langle\Delta_{p}u,v-u\rangle

(compare with Lemma
2.1
).
Moreover, if
u
is a solution to the obstacle problem in
K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
and
0\leq\varphi\in C_{c}(V)
, then
v=u+\varphi\in K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
and therefore

0\leq\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,\mathds{1}_{x})=\Delta_{p}u(x)
for all
x\in V
, i.e.,
u
is superharmonic in
V
.

It is classical that since the obstacle problem originates from a variational integral, solutions are precisely the minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{p}(u,u)
in
K_{\psi,\theta}
. For completeness we provide a proof of this fact in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.23
.

A function
u\in K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
is a solution to the obstacle problem if and only if

\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u)=\min\{\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(v,v)\,:\,v\in K_{\psi,\theta}(V)\}.

Proof.

One implication follows easily from Hölder’s inequality: let
u\in K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
be a solution to the obstacle problem. By definition, for every
v\in K_{\psi,\theta}(V)

0\leq\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,v-u)=\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,v)-\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u)\\
=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\overline{V}}b(x,y)\left(\nabla_{x,y}u\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}\nabla_{x,y}v-\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u)\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{x,y\in\overline{V}}(b(x,y)|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{p}|\right)^{1-1/p}\left(\sum_{x,y\in\overline{V}}(b(x,y)|\nabla_{x,y}v|^{p}\right)^{1/p}-\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u)\\
=\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u)^{1-1/p}\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(v,v)^{1/p}-\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u),

and the required conclusion follows.

For the reverse implication we use the arguments of the proof of Proposition
3.1
: suppose that

\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u)=\min\{\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(v,v)\,:\,v\in K_{\psi,\theta}(V)\}.

Fix
v\in K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
and let
\varphi=v-u
. By convexity, for every
t\in(0,1]
, we have

u+t\varphi=(1-t)u+tv\in K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
and therefore

0\leq\frac{\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u+t\varphi,u+t\varphi)-\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u)}{t}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\overline{V}}b(x,y)\frac{|\nabla_{x,y}u+t\nabla_{x,y}\varphi|^{p}-|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{p}}{t}.

As
t\to 0
, the fraction inside the summation tends to

p|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{x,y}u\nabla_{x,y}\varphi,

and an application of the mean value theorem shows that

\left|\frac{|\nabla_{x,y}u+t\nabla_{x,y}\varphi|^{p}-|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{p}}{t}\right|\leq C_{p}(|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{p}+|\nabla_{x,y}\varphi|^{p})\in\ell^{1}(E_{\overline{V}},b),

so that, letting
t\to 0
and applying dominated convergence yield

0\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\overline{V}}|\nabla_{x,y}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{x,y}u\nabla_{x,y}\varphi\ b(x,y)=\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,v-u),

as required to prove that
u
is a solution to the obstacle problem.
∎

In the remaining part of this subsection, we will discuss uniqueness and existence of solutions to the obstacle problem.

Theorem 3.24
.

Suppose that
K_{\psi,\theta}
is not empty. Then the obstacle problem admits a unique solution
u\in K_{\psi,\theta}
.

Proof.

We may assume that the point
o
in the definition of the norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{o,p}
belongs to
\partial_{e}V
. Since
K_{\psi,\theta}
is a closed convex set in a uniformly convex Banach space it admits a unique element
u
of minimum norm, namely, for all
v\in K_{\psi,\theta}
,

|u(o)|^{p}+\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u)\leq|v(o)|^{p}+\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(v,v).

Since
u(o)=v(o)=\theta(o)
, it follows that for all such
v

\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,u)\leq\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(v,v),

and the conclusion follows from Lemma
3.23
above.
∎

We will also need the the following two lemmas. Their counterparts in the continuum are given in
[
HKM06
, Lemmas 3.11 and 3.22]
. The first is an application of the Green’s formula and the second says that the solution to the obstacle problem in
K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
is the smallest superharmonic function in
K_{\psi,\theta}(V)
.

Lemma 3.25
.

Let
c=0
,
V\subseteq X
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. If either

•

u\in D^{p}(V)
is (super-)harmonic in
V
and
v\in D_{0}^{p}(V)
(respectively,
0\leq v\in D_{0}^{p}(V)
) satisfies
v=0
on
\partial_{e}V
,

or

•

u\in F^{p}(V)
is (super-)harmonic in
V
and
v\in C_{c}(V)
(respectively,
0\leq v\in C_{c}(V)
),

we have

\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,v)\overset{(\geq)}{=}0.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
3.6
, we have in both cases
\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{V}}(u,v)=\langle\Delta_{p}u,v\rangle_{V}.
Using the additional assumptions on
u
and
v
it is easy to show that the stated conclusion holds.
∎

Lemma 3.26
.

Let
c=0
,
p\in(1,\infty)
, and let
W\subseteq X
be finite. Let
u
be a solution to the obstacle problem in
K_{\psi,\theta}(W)
. If
v\in F^{p}(\overline{W})
, respectively
v\in D^{p}(\overline{W})
, is superharmonic in
W
and
u\wedge v\in K_{\psi,\theta}(W)
, then
v\geq u
in
W
.

Proof.

Since
u,u\wedge v\in K_{\psi,\theta}(W)
and
W
is finite, we have that
u-u\wedge v\in D_{0}^{p}(\overline{W})=C_{c}(W)=C(W)
. Therefore all of the following sums converge absolutely. Moreover, by Lemma
3.25
,
\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{W}}(v,u-u\wedge v)\geq 0
. Since
u
is a solution to the obstacle problem we also have
\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{W}}(u,u-u\wedge v)\leq 0
. Altogether,

(3.5)

0\leq\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{W}}(v,u-u\wedge v)-\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{W}}(u,u-u\wedge v)\\
=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\overline{W}}b(x,y)(\left(\nabla_{x,y}v\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}-\left(\nabla_{x,y}u\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle})(\nabla_{x,y}u-\nabla_{x,y}(u\wedge v)).

Moreover, a case by case argument which uses the monotonicity of
t\to{t}^{\langle p-1\rangle}
shows that, for every
x,y\in\overline{W}
,

(\left(\nabla_{x,y}v\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}-\left(\nabla_{x,y}u\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle})(\nabla_{x,y}u-\nabla_{x,y}(u\wedge v))\\
\leq(\left(\nabla_{x,y}(u\wedge v)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}-\left(\nabla_{x,y}u\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle})(\nabla_{x,y}u-\nabla_{x,y}(u\wedge v))\leq 0,

and, inserting into (
3.5
), we conclude that, for all
x,y\in\overline{W}
with
x\sim y
,

\left(\nabla_{x,y}(u\wedge v)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}-\left(\nabla_{x,y}u\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle})(\nabla_{x,y}u-\nabla_{x,y}(u\wedge v))=0,

and therefore
\left(\nabla_{x,y}(u\wedge v)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}=\left(\nabla_{x,y}u\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}
. Since
u=v=\theta
on
\partial_{e}V
, it follows that
u=u\wedge v
on
W
. ∎

3.10.2.
The characterization

We are now ready to complete the proof of the characterization of
p
-parabolicity in terms of the existence of Khas’minskiĭ potentials, compare with Proposition
3.20
.

Theorem 3.27
.

Let
c=0
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
G
be a
p
-parabolic graph and let
K\subseteq X
be a finite set. Then there exists a function
\kappa\colon X\to(0,\infty)
such that

(a)

\kappa\in D^{p}
;

(b)

\kappa
is
p
-superharmonic on
X\setminus K
and vanishes on
K
.

(c)

\kappa(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
, i.e., for every
M>0
there exists
F\subseteq X
finite such that

\kappa>M
in
X\setminus F
.

Proof.

The proof is modeled on
[
Val12a
, Theorem 2.5]
. According to Proposition
3.15
, there exists a function
f\in D^{p}(X)
such that
f(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
. By translating and truncating
f
, we may arrange that
f=0
on
K
and
f\geq 0
on
X\setminus K
. Let
X_{n}
be an exhaustion of
X
by finite sets such that
X_{0}=K
and
f\geq n
on
X\setminus X_{n}
.

We claim that there exists an increasing sequence of functions
s_{n}\in D^{p}(X)
such that

(1)

s_{n}
is
p
-superharmonic in
X\setminus K
and vanishes on
K
;

(2)

s_{n}\leq n
and there exists a finite set
S_{n}
such that
s_{n}=n
on
X\setminus S_{n}
.

Moreover,
s_{n}
is pointwise bounded and

\kappa(x):=\lim_{n}s_{n}(x)

is the required function.

Set
s_{0}=0
and assume by induction that we have constructed functions
s_{0}\leq s_{1}\leq...\leq s_{n}
with the required properties. For ease of notation we set
s_{n}=s
,
S_{n}=S
and we are going to construct
s_{+}=s_{n+1}
.

For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
let
f_{j}(x)=(j^{-1}f(x))\wedge 1
so that
f_{j}\leq 1
and
f_{j}=1
off the finite set
\{x\in X\,:\,f(x)<j\}
and
f_{j}(x)\to 0
as
j\to\infty
pointwise, i.e., locally uniformly.

Let
h_{j}\in K_{\psi_{j},\psi_{j}}(\Omega_{j})
be the solution to the obstacle problem on
\Omega_{j}=X_{j+1}\setminus X_{0}
with obstacle and boundary data
\psi_{j}=s+f_{j}
. Hence,

•

h_{j}
is
p
-superharmonic on
\Omega_{j}
,

•

h_{j}=\psi_{j}
on
X_{0}=K
, and

•

h_{j}=\psi_{j}
on
\partial_{e}X_{j+1}
.

Since
f_{j}=1
on
X\setminus X_{j}\supseteq\partial_{e}X_{j+1}
,
h_{j}=s+1
on
\partial_{e}X_{j+1}
and for
j
large enough
s=n
on
X\setminus X_{j+1}
. Thus, for such
j
’s we have

h_{j}=n+1
on
\partial_{e}X_{j+1}
,

and we can extend it to
X
by setting it equal to
n+1
on
X\setminus X_{j+1}
, namely,

\tilde{h}_{j}=\begin{cases}h_{j}&\text{ on }X_{j+1}\setminus X_{0}\\
0&\text{ on }X_{0}\\
n+1&\text{ on }X\setminus X_{j+1}.\end{cases}

Note that
\tilde{h}_{j}
is a solution to the obstacle problem. For notational convenience we set
\tilde{h}_{j}=h_{j}
.

1st claim:

h_{j}\leq n+1
on
X_{j+1}
and therefore on
X
.

Indeed, since
X_{j+1}
is finite,
h_{j}\in D^{p}(\overline{X}_{j+1})
by assumption and we can apply Lemma
3.26
with
u=h_{j}
and
v=n+1
(so that certainly
v\in D^{p}(\overline{X}_{j+1})
and
u\wedge v=h_{j}\wedge(n+1)\in K_{\psi_{j}}
) to conclude.

2nd claim:

h_{j}
is
p
-superharmonic on
X\setminus X_{0}
.

Since by construction
h_{j}
is
p
-superharmonic on
X_{j+1}
and it is constant (equal to
n+1
) on
X\setminus X_{j+1}
, it suffices to check superharmonicity at
x\in\partial_{e}X_{j+1}
. Since
h_{j}\leq n+1
and
h_{j}=n+1
on
X\setminus X_{j+1}
, we have, for
x\in\partial_{e}X_{j+1}
,

m(x)\Delta_{p}h_{j}(x)=\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)\left(\nabla_{x,y}h_{j}\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}=\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)\left(n+1-h_{j}(y)\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}\geq 0.

Thus
h_{j}
is
p
-superharmonic on
X\setminus X_{0}
,
h_{j}=0
on
K
and
h_{j}=n+1
on
X\setminus X_{j+1}
, provided that
j
is sufficiently large.

3rd claim:

h_{j}
is monotone decreasing as
j\to\infty
.

To see this, note that the obstacle
\psi_{j}=s+f_{j}=s+(j^{-1}f)\wedge 1
is decreasing. Thus applying again Lemma
3.26
with
u=h_{j+1}
, which solves the obstacle problem on
X_{j+2}\setminus X_{0}
with obstacle
\psi_{j+1}\leq\psi_{j}
, and
v=h_{j}
, which is
p
-superharmonic on
X\setminus X_{0}\supseteq X_{j+2}
and
v\geq\psi_{j}>\psi_{j+1}
on
X_{j+1}\setminus X_{0}
, we deduce that

h_{j}\geq h_{j+1}

as claimed, and thus

h_{j}-s:=\rho_{j}\searrow\rho\geq 0.

4th claim:

\rho=0
, and therefore
h_{j}\searrow s
pointwise as
j\to\infty
.

Since
\psi_{j}=s+f_{j}\in K_{\psi_{j},\psi_{j}}(\Omega_{j})
, it follows from the minimizing property of Lemma
3.23
that

(3.6)

2\mathcal{E}_{p,X_{j+1}}(h_{j},h_{j})=\left\lVert\nabla h_{j}\right\rVert^{p}_{\ell^{p}(E_{X_{j+1}},b)}\leq\|\nabla\psi_{j}\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(E_{\overline{X}_{j+1}},b)}=2\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{X}_{j+1}}(\psi_{j},\psi_{j})\\
\leq\|\nabla s\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(E_{\overline{X}_{j+1}},b)}+\|\nabla f_{j}\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(E_{\overline{X}_{j+1}},b)}\leq\|\nabla s\|^{p}_{p,b}+\|\nabla f_{j}\|^{p}_{p,b},

whence

\|\nabla\rho_{j}\|_{p,b}\leq\|\nabla h_{j}\|_{p,b}+\|\nabla s\|_{p,b}\leq 2\|\nabla s\|_{p,b}+\|\nabla f_{j}\|_{p,b}\leq C,

where the last inequality follows since
s
and
f_{j}
are constant outside a finite set.

Since

|\nabla_{x,y}\rho_{j}|^{p}\to|\nabla_{x,y}\rho|^{p},\qquad x,y\in X,

by Fatou’s Lemma

\|\nabla\rho\|_{p,b}\leq\liminf_{j}\|\nabla\rho_{j}\|_{p,b}\leq C.

To show that
\|\nabla\rho_{j}\|_{p,b}\to 0
, so that
\|\nabla\rho\|_{p,b}=0
and therefore, since
\rho=0
on
K
,
\rho=0
on
X
, we consider the function

g=\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{j}\right)\wedge n,

which satisfies
g=0
on
X_{0}=K
,
g=n
on
X\setminus S
,
g\geq s
on
S\setminus X_{0}
.

Since
s
is the solution to the obstacle problem with obstacle equal to itself, again by Lemma
3.23
, we have

\|\nabla s+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\rho_{j}\|_{p,b}^{p}\geq 2\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{S\setminus X_{0}}}(g,g)\geq 2\mathcal{E}_{p,\overline{S\setminus X_{0}}}(s,s)=\|\nabla s\|_{p,b}^{p},

whence using Lemma 2 in
[
Val12a
]
and estimate (
3.6
) above, we deduce that

\begin{split}\|\nabla s\|_{p,b}\left[1+\delta\left(\frac{\|\nabla\rho_{j}\|_{p,b}}{\|\nabla s\|_{p,b}+\|\nabla\rho_{j}\|_{p,b}}\right)\right]^{-1}&\leq\|\nabla(s+\rho_{j})\|_{p,b}\\
&=2\mathcal{E}^{1/p}_{p,\overline{S\setminus X_{0}}}(h_{j},h_{j})\leq||\nabla s||_{p,b}+\|\nabla f_{j}\|_{p,b},\end{split}

where
\delta=\delta_{\ell^{p}}
is the modulus of convexity of the uniformly convex space
\ell^{p}(E_{X},b)
. Since
\|\nabla f_{j}\|_{\ell^{p}}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
and
\delta
is strictly positive on
(0,2)
we conclude that

\|\nabla\rho_{j}\|_{p,b}\to 0.

We have thus proved that
\rho_{j}\to 0
on
X
, i.e.,
h_{j}\searrow s
on
X
which shows the claim.

We are ready to complete the induction process. By the claims above, there exists
\overline{j}
such that
\sup_{x\in X_{n+1}}|h_{\overline{j}}(x)-s(x)|<2^{-n-1}
and
\|\nabla\rho_{\overline{j}}\|_{p,b}<2^{-n}
, so that

\|\nabla s_{+}-\nabla s\|_{p,b}<2^{-n}.

Now set
\kappa:=\lim_{n}s_{n}
. Then:

•

since
s_{n+1}-s_{n}<2^{-n-1}
it follows that

\kappa=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(s_{n}-s_{n-1})

is finite everywhere;

•

since
s_{n}=n
on
X\setminus S_{n}
and
X\setminus S_{n}
is finite, it follows that

\kappa\to\infty

at
\infty
;

•

since

\|\nabla s_{n+1}-\nabla s_{n}\|_{p,b}\leq 2^{-n},

it follows that
\kappa\in D^{p}(X)
.

•

Finally
\kappa
is
p
-superharmonic on
X\setminus K
since it is the increasing limit of a sequence of
p
-superharmonic functions there (see, e.g.,
[
Fis22
, Lemma 3.2]
).
∎

A proof of this statement for the special case
p=2
can also be found in
[
ISE22
]
.

4.
Applications

We want to give some examples of graphs and classify their type. Moreover, we show some simple consequences of the preceding characterizations.

Remark 4.1
.

If
X
is finite and
c=0
than the corresponding graph is
p
-parabolic for any
p\in[1,\infty)
since
C(X)=C_{c}(X)=D_{0}^{p}=D^{p}
and we can apply Proposition
3.3
.

Recall also that by Remark
2.8
, if
c\gneq 0
, then the graph is
p
-hyperbolic for
p\in[1,\infty)
.

We will therefore consider in the following only infinite graphs and
c=0
.

Example 4.2
.

A
star graph
with center
0
is a (connected) graph
G=(\mathbb{N}_{0},b,c,m)
such that every vertex is only connected to the center
0
. This is an example of a locally summable graph which is not locally finite.

Note that for any function
f\in F^{p}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
, and
n>0
,

\Delta_{p}f(n)=\frac{b(n,0)}{m(n)}\left(\nabla_{n,0}f\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}.

Hence,

\Delta_{p}f(0)=\frac{1}{m(0)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b(0,n)\left(\nabla_{0,n}f\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{m(n)}{m(0)}\Delta_{p}f(n).

We want to see if the graph is
p
-parabolic and hence assume
c=0
. By the calculation above it is simple to see that all positive superharmonic functions are harmonic, and hence there cannot exist a Green’s function. By Proposition
3.2
, the star graph is
p
-parabolic for
p\in(1,\infty)
independently of
b
(as long as the graph is locally summable). To include the case
p=1
, note that it is not difficult to deduce from the above that every superharmonic function is constant. Hence, Proposition
3.7
yields
1
-parabolicity.

Alternatively, it is also not difficult to apply e.g. Proposition
3.12
(since there are actually only two cases to consider).

In a hand waving way this observation makes sense, since one can think of hyperbolicity like growing fast at infinity which is just not the case for a star graph for
p<\infty
.

Example 4.3
.

It is well-known that the standard line graph
\mathbb{N}
is
p
-hyperbolic for all
p>1
, see. e.g.
[
FKP23
]
. Here,
\mathbb{N}
is interpreted as a subgraph of
\mathbb{N}_{0}
, i.e., we only consider functions in
C_{c}(\mathbb{N})
for the capacity, i.e., we have a Dirichlet boundary condition at
0
.

However, it is also not difficult to apply e.g. Proposition
3.12
. Take
V=\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
for some fixed
n\geq 2
. If
u
is bounded and subharmonic on
V
, then
\Delta_{p}u(j)\leq 0
,
j\in V\setminus\left\{1\right\}
implies
\nabla_{j,j-1}u\leq\nabla_{j,j+1}u
, i.e.
u(j+1)\leq u(j-1)
, and
\Delta_{p}u(1)\leq 0
implies
2u(1)\leq u(2)
. Thus, define
u=0
on
\mathbb{N}_{0}\setminus(V\cup\left\{n+1\right\})
,
u(1)=1/2
,
u(j)=1/(j-1)
for
j\in V\setminus\left\{1\right\}
, we have a bounded subharmonic function on
V
with
\sup_{\overline{V}}u>\sup_{\partial_{e}V}u
. Applying Proposition
3.12
we get that
\mathbb{N}
is
p
-hyperbolic for all
p>1
.

Let us also consider the weighted line graph
\mathbb{N}_{b}=(\mathbb{N}_{0},b,0,m)
. By using Proposition
3.17
it is immediate that
\mathbb{N}_{b}
is
p
-parabolic if and only if

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(n,n+1)=\infty.

Example 4.4
.

A
wheel graph
with center
0
is a graph
G=(\mathbb{N}_{0},b,0,m)
such that every vertex is connected to the center
0
, and
b(n,n+1)>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, it is a line graph glued on the spikes of a star graph. This is interesting since we have just seen that the star graph is always
p
-parabolic for
p<\infty
, whereas the weighted line graph could be
p
-hyperbolic.

We note that the Laplacian has the following explicit form in this example:

\displaystyle m(n)\Delta_{p}f(n)

\displaystyle=b(n,0)\left(\nabla_{n,0}f\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}+b(n,n\pm 1)\left(\nabla_{n,n\pm 1}f\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle},\qquad n\geq 2,

\displaystyle m(1)\Delta_{p}f(1)

\displaystyle=b(1,0)\left(\nabla_{1,0}f\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}+b(1,2)\left(\nabla_{1,2}f\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle},

\displaystyle m(0)\Delta_{p}f(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b(0,n)\left(\nabla_{0,n}f\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}m(n)\Delta_{p}f(n).

Hence, we obtain the same formula as for the star graph and get that it is always
p
-parabolic if
p<\infty
.

If we glue the star and the line graph differently, we also get a different behaviour with respect to parabolicity.

Example 4.5
.

A
star-line graph
(or chain chomp) with center
0
is a graph
G=\mathbb{N}_{s}\oplus\mathbb{N}_{l}
consisting of a star graph
\mathbb{N}_{s}
with center
0_{s}
and a line graph
\mathbb{N}_{l}
with origin
0_{l}
, where we identify the center and the origin, i.e.
0=0_{s}=0_{l}
. By the Khas’miniskiĭ test, Proposition
3.27
, it is obvious that
G
is
p
-parabolic for
p\in(1,\infty)
if and only if both
\mathbb{N}_{s}
and
\mathbb{N}_{l}
are
p
-parabolic, which is the case if also
\mathbb{N}_{l}
is
p
-parabolic.

Note that this behaviour is very different from the manifold case. The example also motivates the following results.

An
end
of a graph with respect to a finite set
K\subseteq X
is a connected component of the complement
X\setminus K
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. If all ends of a graph with respect to a fixed finite set with finite exterior boundary are
p
-parabolic then the graph itself is
p
-parabolic.

Proof.

By assumption, we know that there are at most finitely many ends. Then using Propositions
3.27
and
3.20
yields the result.
∎

In turn, the following can be said about getting
p
-hyperbolicity.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
G=(X,b,m,0)
be a graph. Let
W\subseteq X
such that
\partial_{i}W
is finite. If the subgraph
G_{W}:=(W,b|_{W\times W},m,0)
is
p
-hyperbolic, then so is the whole supergraph
G
.

Proof.

Since
G_{W}
is
p
-hyperbolic, the weak maximum principle does not hold (Proposition
3.20
), i.e., there exists a non-constant, bounded above function
f\in F^{p}
and a number
\gamma
such that

\sup_{\Omega_{\gamma}}\Delta_{p}^{W}f\leq 0.

Here, the Laplacian
\Delta_{p}^{W}
is understood with respect to the weight
b|_{W\times W}
.
By the strong maximum principle, Lemma
A.2
, applied to the graph
G_{W}
,
f
does not attain its supremum on the set
\Omega_{\gamma}
and therefore it cannot attain its supremum on the finite set
\partial_{i}W
. Hence there exists
\gamma\leq\beta<f^{*}=\sup f
such that
\Omega_{\beta}\cap\partial_{i}W=\emptyset
and therefore
\partial_{e}\Omega_{\beta}\subseteq W
.

If
u\in C(X)
is defined by

u(x):=\begin{cases}f(x)\qquad,&x\in W;\\
\gamma,&x\in X\setminus W.\end{cases}

then, for every
x\in\Omega_{\beta}

m(x)\Delta_{p}u(x)=\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)\left(\nabla_{x,y}u\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}=\\
\sum_{y\in W}b(x,y)\left(\nabla_{x,y}f\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}=m(x)\Delta_{p}^{W}f(x)\leq 0\qed

We now recall some well-known examples of locally finite graphs for convenience.

Example 4.8
.

It is also well-known that the Euclidean lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is
p
-parabolic if and only if
d\leq p
, see e.g.
[
Mae77

Next we show another consequence of Proposition

, and apply it thereafter for some special cases. For the linear case, confer with the proof of Theorem 3.17 in
[

]
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let a
p
-hyperbolic locally finite model graph with respect to
x_{0}\in X
be given,
p\in(1,\infty)
. If the curvature ratio function

\kappa(r):=\frac{k_{+}(r)}{k_{-}(r)}>0,\qquad r\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

is constant and strictly larger than
1
outside of
B_{r_{0}}
for some
r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then the Green’s function
g
with respect to
x_{0}\in X
is radial and given by

g(r)=\frac{1}{(\kappa^{\frac{1}{p-1}}-1)\partial B(r-1)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}},\qquad r>r_{0}.

Proof.

It follows from (
3.4
) that

\kappa\,\partial B(r-1)=\partial B(r),\qquad r>r_{0}.

Hence, by Proposition
3.17

(b)
,

g(r)=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}\left(\kappa\,\partial B(k-1)\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}=\left(\kappa\,\partial B(r-1)\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}+\kappa^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\sum_{k=r+1}^{\infty}\partial B(k-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Rearranging yields,

g(r)=\frac{1}{(\kappa^{\frac{1}{p-1}}-1)\partial B(r-1)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}},\qquad r>r_{0}.\qed

Let us turn to two prominent model graphs: homogeneous regular trees and anti-trees.

(

Appendix A
The Weak Comparison Principle

In this paper, we sometimes use a comparison principle which is stated next. It also goes under the names maximum or minimum principle. Several variants are available in the literature, see, e.g.,
[
Pra04
]
and
[
KC10
]
. We report below a version well suited to our purposes which appears in
[
Fis22
]
. Since the proof is quite short, we reproduce it here for the convenience of the reader. Note that the condition in the statement that
(v-u)\wedge 0
attains a minimum in
V
is automatically satisfied when
V
is finite.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
V\subsetneq X
and
c\geq 0
on
V
. Furthermore, let
u,v\in F(V)
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L_{p}u&\leq L_{p}v\quad\text{on }\phantom{\partial}V,\\
\phantom{H}u&\leq\phantom{H}v\quad\text{on }\,\partial_{e}V.\end{cases}

Assume that
(v-u)\wedge 0
attains a minimum in
V
.
Then,
u\leq v
on
V
.

Moreover, in each connected component of
V
we have either
u=v
, or
u<v
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
V
is connected. Otherwise, we do the following proof in every connected component of
V
.

Assume that there exists
x\in V
such that
v(x)\leq u(x)
. Since
(v-u)\wedge 0
attains a minimum in
V
, there exists
x_{0}\in V
such that
v(x_{0})-u(x_{0})\leq 0
, and
v(x_{0})-u(x_{0})\leq v(y)-u(y)
for all
y\in V
. Since
u\leq v
on
\partial_{e}V
, we get
\nabla_{x_{0},y}v\leq\nabla_{x_{0},y}u
for all
y\in V\cup\partial_{e}V
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq m(x_{0})\bigl(L_{p}v(x_{0})-L_{p}u(x_{0})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V\cup\partial_{e}V}b(x_{0},y)\left(\left(\nabla_{x_{0},y}v\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}-\left(\nabla_{x_{0},y}u\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+c(x_{0})\left(\left(v(x_{0})\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}-\left(u(x_{0})\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0,

where the second inequality follows from the monotonicity of
\left(\cdot\right)^{\langle p-1\rangle}
on
\mathbb{R}
, and
c(x_{0})\geq 0
. Thus, we have in fact equality above.

Since
c(x_{0})\geq 0
, then we get that
u(y)-v(y)
is a non-negative constant for all
y\sim x
. By iterating this argument and using that
V
is connected, we get that
u(y)-v(y)
is a non-negative constant for all
y\in V\cup\partial_{e}V
. Since
u\leq v
on
\partial_{e}V
, we conclude that
u=v
on
V
.
∎

The proof above also shows the following.

Lemma A.2

(Strong Maximum Principle)
.

Let
V\subseteq X
and
c\geq 0
. Let
u
be a
p
-subharmonic function in
\mathrm{int}V:=V\setminus\partial_{i}V
attaining its maximum in
\mathrm{int}V
, then
u
is constant.

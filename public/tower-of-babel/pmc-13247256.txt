How agricultural socialized services pilot policies can reduce the urban-rural income gap
Authors: YueXin Yuan, LianGang Zhang, Ting Xiang, PuMing He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The implementation of China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services is not only of great significance for increasing farmers’ income and narrowing the urban-rural income gap in China, but also conducive to achieving the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Based on the panel data of Chinese prefecture-level cities from 2012 to 2024, this paper empirically analyzes the policy impact, mechanism of action and heterogeneous effects of China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services on the urban-rural income gap. The results show that: (1) China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services can indeed narrow the urban-rural income gap, and this conclusion still holds after the placebo test and a series of robustness tests. (2) The pilot policies mainly narrow the urban-rural income gap through two paths: promoting the upgrading of industrial structure and raising the level of urbanization. (3) The impact of the pilot policies on the urban-rural income gap of low-income groups is not significant. (4) The pilot policies have a more obvious inhibitory effect on the urban-rural income gap in the central and western provinces, major grain-producing areas and grain production-marketing balance areas of China. The research conclusions provide a useful reference for developing countries including China to develop agricultural socialized services.

Introduction

The urban-rural gap remains one of the worldwide development challenges. In most developing countries, narrowing the urban-rural income gap is an important way to address the internal inequality caused by the urban-rural gap1. As the world’s largest developing country, China has witnessed rapid economic development and a remarkable growth in people’s income since the beginning of this century, yet the issue of the urban-rural income gap still needs to be viewed rationally2,3. The income gap resulting from the long-standing urban-rural dual economic structure remains a severe problem4,5. According to the data of the past two years, the ratio of per capita disposable income of urban and rural residents in China was 2.39 in 2023 and 2.34 in 2024 respectively (National Bureau of Statistics of China, 2024). Therefore, narrowing the urban-rural income gap is not only conducive to achieving China’s strategic goals of common prosperity and rural revitalization, but also can provide Chinese wisdom and solutions for the realization of the UN Sustainable Development Goals, especially Goal 1 (No Poverty) and Goal 10 (Reduced Inequalities).

In 2013, the Central Government Document No.1 issued by the Chinese government pointed out the need to promote institutional innovation in the socialized service system for agricultural production and advance the large-scale and organized development of agricultural production. Subsequently, the Ministry of Finance of China has successively issued a series of policies since 2013, launching the pilot of full-process socialized services for agricultural production in 17 provinces including Hebei and Hunan across China. The pilot areas are required to arrange special funds, focus on key weak links, cultivate diverse agricultural socialized service organizations, and drive the transformation of services from single-link to full-process socialized services. Taking agricultural socialized services as a driver, agricultural production is guided to move towards the direction of specialization, intensification, large-scale operation and mechanization.

The issue of the urban-rural income gap has long been a key research direction in academia. Existing studies have explored this issue from multiple perspectives, mainly focusing on the following aspects: First, the impact of policy factors on the urban-rural income gap. Early studies showed that government support policies for the development of capital-intensive industries significantly reduced the demand for rural labor, hindered labor transfer and widened the urban-rural income gap, and the urban-rural income gap in China presented a “U-shaped” curve6. Current studies mostly focus on the positive effects of fiscal redistribution policies, targeted poverty alleviation policies, agricultural support policies, and the policy of linking increases and decreases in urban and rural construction land on narrowing the urban-rural income gap7–10. Welfare and government transfer payment policies play a greater role in regulating income distribution in rural areas11. Land development policies will widen the urban-rural income gap when land acquisition compensation is higher than the market price of land12. The implementation of the “Broadband China” policy will widen the urban-rural income gap, but this negative impact can be effectively alleviated when implemented in conjunction with other policies13. Second, the impact of the digital economy on the urban-rural income gap. On the whole, there is a “U-shaped” relationship between the digital economy and the urban-rural income gap, and the current development of the digital economy has not exceeded the turning point, thus helping to narrow the gap14,15. From the industrial perspective, industrial digitalization and rural digitalization can both narrow the urban-rural income gap, and innovation level, as an intermediary, is more conducive to stimulating the inhibitory effect of rural digitalization on the income gap16,17. From the policy perspective, the construction of digital villages can significantly narrow the income gap in adjacent areas of China, with the most prominent gap-narrowing effect in eastern China and a more obvious spatial spillover effect in the central and western regions of China18. At the same time, rural e-commerce, as an important carrier of the digital economy, can also significantly narrow the urban-rural income gap19. From the perspective of new industries and new forms of business, digital finance, including digital inclusive finance, can narrow the urban-rural income gap, in which rural e-commerce and financial service accessibility play an important intermediary role20,21. Third, the impact of green technology on the urban-rural income gap. Generally speaking, low-carbon transition is an inevitable trend of global industrial transformation and development. Existing studies have shown that there is an inverted “U-shaped” relationship between low-carbon transition and the urban-rural income gap22, and green innovation can more directly reduce the income gap23. From the industrial perspective, the green total factor productivity of agriculture has a more continuous and sustainable inhibitory effect on the income gap24. Finally, as an important carrier of green transition and development, green finance can not only narrow the income gap, but also drive the narrowing of the income gap in surrounding areas25.

Although numerous scholars have conducted a series of studies on the income gap, there is still room for further expansion in the research on the impact of the implementation of pilot policies on agricultural socialized services, which optimizes the allocation of resource factors, promotes cost reduction and efficiency increase in agriculture, and thus enables more farmers to share the policy dividends and narrow the urban-rural income gap. “Large country with small farmers” is the realistic condition of China’s agricultural development, and promoting the large-scale operation of services is an important path for the transformation and development of China’s agriculture. In recent years, the Chinese government has issued a series of relevant policies to promote the development of agricultural socialized services. According to the official data released by China, by 2024, more than 94 million small-scale farmers had access to agricultural socialized services in China. Against this background, it is worth further in-depth discussion to evaluate the implementation effect of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services and verify whether they can significantly drive the increase of farmers’ income and alleviate the urban-rural income gap in China. On the one hand, agricultural socialized services can significantly increase the income of rural residents and have a spatial spillover effect on surrounding areas26. However, there is heterogeneity among different types of outsourcing services, with soil testing and formulated fertilization as well as pesticide application outsourcing services having a more significant income-increasing effect27,28. On the other hand, the socialized service of agricultural machinery can increase farmers’ operating income, promote income growth and narrow the urban-rural income gap29, and agricultural production trusteeship, as a typical carrier of agricultural socialized services, can also effectively narrow the income gap30. Nevertheless, the above literatures have not comprehensively measured the impact of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services on the urban-rural income gap from the perspective of policy evaluation.

Based on this, using the panel data of Chinese prefecture-level cities from 2012 to 2024 and the progressive difference-in-differences method, this paper empirically analyzes the impact and mechanism of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services on the urban-rural income gap. The possible marginal contributions are as follows: First, from the perspective of policy evaluation, it comprehensively assesses the impact of China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services on the urban-rural income gap in China. Second, it uses the quantile regression model to explore the policy impact of the pilot policies on the urban-rural income gap of different groups. Third, it deeply discusses the influence mechanism and heterogeneous effects of the pilot policies on the urban-rural income gap, attempting to provide replicable paths for developing countries to narrow the urban-rural income gap through the development of agricultural socialized services and contribute China’s strength to the realization of the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

Theoretical analysis framework

Based on the hypothesis of rational economic man, farmers are rational and income improvement is the premise of their behavioral decisions. The implementation of China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services is also aimed at improving the quality and efficiency of services, changing the utility level of agricultural socialized services, so as to effectively drive the increase of farmers’ income and narrow the urban-rural income gap. Theoretically, China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services narrow the urban-rural income gap mainly by increasing farmers’ income, which is reflected in the following aspects: First, the implementation of the pilot policies is intended to integrate technology, human resources, agricultural means of production and machinery in agricultural production, optimize factor allocation, achieve higher output goals with the lowest factor input, and reduce production costs through the centralized procurement of service subjects. At the same time, it can improve farmers’ access to agricultural machinery and equipment, green organic fertilizers and production technologies, promote the dual improvement of the quantity and quality of agricultural products, and obtain higher product returns31. Second, the implementation of the pilot policies has accelerated the cultivation of a large number of new agricultural business entities, alleviated the problems of information asymmetry and weak bargaining power of farmers. By giving play to the bargaining and negotiation advantages of service subjects, it reduces the transaction costs of agricultural means of production for farmers and improves transaction efficiency32. Third, the implementation of the pilot policies has further expanded the types of services covering the whole process of agricultural production. Characteristic sales services such as rural e-commerce can expand the sales scale of agricultural products, increase sales unit prices, drive the income growth of farmers, and thus narrow the income gap33.

The implementation of China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services is essentially to deepen the vertical division of labor in agricultural production and form the large-scale operation of services. First, through the implementation of diversified production trusteeship models and land transfer, the pilot policies promote the contiguous operation of cultivated land, lay a good foundation for giving play to the economies of scale of machinery, technology and management factors, further improve agricultural production efficiency, and realize the rational allocation of factor input. In particular, the large-scale operation of services liberates rural labor force, enabling more labor to transfer to non-agricultural sectors and promoting the upgrading of industrial structure34. Second, the pilot policies cultivate service institutions and drive the income growth of farmers through the large-scale operation of services. The increase in income will promote their consumption upgrading, thus driving the upgrading of regional industrial structure through structural changes on the demand side. The employment opportunities in the secondary and tertiary industries generated by industrial structure upgrading will significantly increase farmers’ personal income, thereby narrowing the urban-rural income gap35. Third, the economies of scale in service operation brought about by the implementation of the pilot policies will inevitably lead to the outflow of rural labor force. The relatively disparate income expectations between urban and rural areas and the more complete and convenient social security systems such as medical care and education in cities will drive a large number of rural labor force to move to cities. While raising the urbanization level of Chinese cities, farmers can also obtain diversified wage income from cities, narrowing the urban-rural income gap36.

H1: China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services can significantly narrow the urban-rural income gap.

H2: China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services narrow the urban-rural income gap by promoting the upgrading of industrial structure and raising the level of urbanization.

Methodology

Date source

Considering the availability of data, this paper uses the panel data of 284 prefecture-level cities in China from 2012 to 2024. The variable data are derived from the China City Statistical Yearbook, China Rural Statistical Yearbook, statistical yearbooks and communiqués of various provinces, the EPS Database, and individual missing values are replaced by the linear interpolation method.

Variable description

Dependent variable

Urban-rural income gap: Common indicators to measure the urban-rural income gap include the ratio of urban and rural per capita disposable income and the Theil index. As a generalized entropy index, the Theil index has a series of ideal characteristics such as decomposability and is widely used in research of various disciplines37. Therefore, the Theil index of each prefecture-level city is selected to characterize the urban-rural income gap.

Independent variable

Pilot policies on agricultural socialized services: The difference-in-differences method can effectively evaluate the actual effect of policy intervention, control the estimation bias caused by some unobserved variables, avoid the endogeneity problem to a great extent, and is widely used in various policy evaluations38. Therefore, the“pilot of full-process socialized services for agricultural production” is adopted as a dummy variable. According to the Guiding Opinions on the Implementation of the Pilot of Full-process Socialized Services for Agricultural Production in 2013 issued by the Ministry of Finance of China in 2013, the first batch of pilots was launched in 8 provinces including Henan and Hebei, and 9 additional provinces including Inner Mongolia and Heilongjiang were added in 2015, totaling 17 provinces. Prefecture-level cities in pilot provinces are assigned a value of 1, while those in non-pilot provinces are assigned a value of 0.

Mediator variable

Two mediator variables are selected: industrial structure upgrading and urbanization rate. Industrial structure upgrading generally refers to the process of transferring production factors from industrial sectors with relatively low production efficiency to those with relatively high production efficiency, and the change of industrial structure will directly affect the contribution rate of the growth rate of the three industries to GDP39; while the urbanization rate is more reflected in the migration of rural population to cities40. Therefore, industrial structure upgrading is calculated as: (proportion of added value of the primary industry in GDP ×1 + proportion of added value of the secondary industry in GDP ×2 + proportion of added value of the tertiary industry in GDP ×3), and the urbanization level is calculated as: (non-agricultural population / registered population).

Control variable

The urban-rural income gap is closely related to the local economic development level, fiscal status, opening-up level, education level and other factors41. Therefore, the following control variables are selected: economic development level (per capita regional GDP), financial development level (year-end deposit and loan balance of financial institutions / regional GDP), opening-up level (actually utilized foreign capital / regional GDP), government intervention intensity (local general budgetary expenditure / regional GDP), population size (registered population), fiscal decentralization (government general fiscal revenue / government general fiscal expenditure), education expenditure level (education expenditure / government general fiscal expenditure), economic density (regional GDP / administrative land area), population density (registered population of the region / administrative land area), and fiscal pressure (government general fiscal expenditure - government general fiscal revenue) / regional GDP. Results

Parallel trend test

The parallel trend test is an important prerequisite for measuring the applicability of the progressive difference-in-differences method. Figure 1 shows that in the three periods before the policy implementation, the confidence interval is all above 0 with an aggravating trend, indicating that there was still an obvious income gap between urban and rural areas before the implementation of China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services. However, in the current period and the subsequent five periods after the policy implementation, the confidence interval is all below 0, indicating that there are significant differences in the estimated coefficients among various years, which meets the hypothetical conditions of the parallel trend test. The implementation of China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services is indeed conducive to narrowing the urban-rural income gap, and the inhibitory effect on the urban-rural income gap has good continuity.

Baseline results

Stata18 is used for empirical analysis, and the benchmark regression results are shown in Table 2. The results of Models (1)-(2) in Table 2 show that without adding control variables, the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services are significantly negative at the 10% significance level; with the addition of control variables, the pilot policies are significantly negative at the 1% significance level. Placebo and robustness test

(1) Advance policy node.

To accurately verify the policy impact of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services on the urban-rural income gap and eliminate the interference of false effects, the implementation year of the pilot policies is advanced by one year to form a new dummy policy variable. The results in Column (2) of Table 3 show that after advancing the policy node, the impact of the pilot policies on the urban-rural income gap is not significant, which proves the correctness of the policy node selection and indirectly supports the robustness of the benchmark regression results.

(2)Replace explained variable.

(2)Replace explained variable.

Replace explained variable.

Effectively promoting the steady income growth of rural residents is an important content of narrowing the urban-rural income gap. Therefore, the explained variable of urban-rural income gap is replaced by rural per capita disposable income and urban per capita disposable income for regression respectively. The results in Column (3) of Table 3 show that the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services significantly increase the rural per capita disposable income at the 10% significance level, but have no impact on the urban per capita disposable income, indicating that the pilot policies can steadily increase farmers’ income and thus narrow the urban-rural income gap.

(3)Adjust the research sample.

(3)Adjust the research sample.

Adjust the research sample.

In view of the two pilot implementation times of the agricultural socialized service policy in 2013 and 2015, to avoid the impact of an excessively long time sample span on the regression results and exclude the possible interference of the COVID-19 pandemic on the explained variable of urban-rural income gap, the regression sample is adjusted to the sample data from 2012 to 2020. The results in Column (5) of Table 3 show that after adjusting the data sample, the impact of the pilot policies on the urban-rural income gap is still significantly negative at the 5% significance level.

(4)Shrinkage test.

(4)Shrinkage test.

Shrinkage test.

In view of the interference of possible outliers in the sample on the regression results, the 1% winsorization is conducted on the variables. The results in Column (6) of Table 3 show that after eliminating the sample outliers, the pilot policies still have a negative impact on the urban-rural income gap at the 1% significance level with a regression coefficient of 0.0627, which is not much different from the benchmark regression coefficient, and Hypothesis 1 is verified again.

(5)Replacement estimation method.

(5)Replacement estimation method.

Replacement estimation method.

A robustness test is further conducted by replacing the estimation model. The benchmark regression model uses the multi-dimensional fixed effect model (reghdfe), but the core explanatory variable is a policy dummy variable, which may have sample selection bias. Therefore, the propensity score matching-difference-in-differences method (PSM-DID) is selected for the robustness test in this paper, and the results are shown in Column (7) of Table 3. After replacing with the PSM-DID model, the pilot policies still have a negative impact on the urban-rural income gap at the 1% significance level, and the regression coefficient is very close to the benchmark regression result, which supports the accuracy of the benchmark regression result.

(6)Exclude the interference of concurrent policies

Exclude the interference of concurrent policies

To more accurately identify the causal relationship between China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services and the urban-rural income gap, this paper excludes the concurrent policies that may affect the research conclusions one by one, constructs dummy policy variables for the rural land “separation of three rights” policy and the “modern agricultural industrial park” policy respectively, incorporates them into the benchmark regression model separately, and finally incorporates them into the regression model together. Mechanism effect

Through what channels do the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services affect the urban-rural income gap? This paper conducts an in-depth analysis of the influence mechanism behind the gap-narrowing effect of the policies by combining the two paths proposed above.

(1) Industrial structure upgrading.Model (3) in Table 5 reports the estimation results of the impact of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services on industrial structure upgrading. The policy coefficient is significantly positive at the 10% significance level, and the Bootstrap indirect test result is also significantly negative at the 1% significance level, indicating that the pilot policies can promote the upgrading of industrial structure in pilot areas. A possible explanation is that through direct subsidies and cultivating service subjects, the pilot policies promote cost reduction and efficiency increase in agricultural production, steadily raise farmers’ household operating income, activate consumption potential, drive the upgrading of industrial structure from the consumption side, and thus narrow the urban-rural income gap.

(2) Urbanization level.Model (4) in Table 5 reports the estimation results of the impact of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services on urbanization level. The policy coefficient is significantly positive at the 5% significance level, and the Bootstrap indirect test result is also significantly negative at the 1% significance level, indicating that the pilot policies can raise the urbanization level in pilot areas. A possible reason is that after the implementation of the pilot policies, through diversified service forms such as agricultural machinery services and trusteeship services, the rational reallocation of labor resources is promoted, the dependence of agricultural production on labor is reduced, and more surplus labor force transfers to urban non-agricultural sectors, obtaining wage income from non-agricultural employment and further raising the urbanization level, thus narrowing the urban-rural income gap. In summary, the implementation of the policies affects the urban-rural income gap through two paths: promoting the upgrading of industrial structure and raising the level of urbanization. Accordingly, Hypothesis 2 is verified.

Quantile regression model

The benchmark regression results show that the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services can significantly narrow the urban-rural income gap, but the benchmark regression is only a mean regression and cannot comprehensively measure the policy impact of the pilot policies on the urban-rural income gap of different groups. Therefore, this paper adopts the quantile regression to analyze the policy impact of the pilot policies on the urban-rural income gap under different quantile conditions, and selects the quantile levels of the urban-rural income gap at 0.25, 0.50, 0.75 and 0.90. The regression results of Models (5)-(8) in Table 6 show that at the high quantiles of the urban-rural income gap, the effect of the pilot policies on the urban-rural income gap is significantly negative and all pass the 1% significance test, but the impact on the groups at the low quantiles of the urban-rural income gap is not significant. The possible reason is that theoretically, the implementation of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services is conducive to increasing farmers’ planting income by optimizing resource allocation and reducing production costs, and can also expand the income-increasing paths of farmers by promoting the non-agricultural employment of surplus labor force, thus narrowing the urban-rural income gap. However, existing studies have shown that in the early stage of the implementation of the pilot policies, various policies need to be further improved, service institutions are small in scale, narrow in coverage and single in service content, with relatively weak ability to connect and drive farmers, resulting in limited ability of the policies to drive low-income groups to narrow the income gap in the initial stage of implementation.

Heterogeneity analysis

The effectiveness of agricultural policies is related to local resource endowments, but the differences in the current industrial development situation and regional positioning of different regions may lead to different policy impacts after the implementation of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services. Therefore, this paper conducts a heterogeneity analysis from two aspects: different regions and different functional zones, and the results are shown in Table 7.

(1) Regional development differences.The regression results in Columns (2)-(3) of Table 7 show that the implementation of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services has a significantly negative impact on the urban-rural income gap in the central and western provinces of China at the 1% significance level, but has no impact on that in the eastern provinces of China, indicating that the pilot policies are more conducive to promoting the narrowing of the urban-rural income gap in the central and western provinces of China. The possible reason is that compared with the eastern region, the central and western regions are mostly major traditional crop-planting provinces. A series of supportive and protective subsidies, agricultural machinery subsidies and agricultural insurance policy support, together with the collaborative effect of agricultural socialized service policies with project subsidies such as high-standard farmland and water conservancy development, have significantly reduced the agricultural production costs in these regions and significantly increased agricultural income, thus narrowing the urban-rural income gap.

(2) Functional zone differences.The regression results in Columns (4)-(6) of Table 7 show that the implementation of the pilot policies has a significantly negative impact on the income gap in major grain-producing areas and grain production-marketing balance areas at the 1% and 10% significance levels respectively, but has no impact on that in major grain-consuming areas, indicating that the pilot policies are more effective in promoting the narrowing of the urban-rural income gap in major grain-producing areas and grain production-marketing balance areas. The possible reason is that the implementation of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services is prioritized in most major grain-producing areas, which clarifies their important position in safeguarding national food security. A series of supportive subsidy policies enable major grain-producing areas and grain production-marketing balance areas to benefit more from direct or indirect subsidies, thus improving the income distribution and narrowing the urban-rural income gap.

Conclusions and implications

Conclusions

Based on the panel data of Chinese prefecture-level cities from 2012 to 2024, this paper systematically examines the impact and mechanism of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services on the urban-rural income gap by using the progressive difference-in-differences method. The findings are as follows: First, the pilot policies have a significant convergence effect on the urban-rural income gap, and this conclusion remains robust after a series of rigorous verifications including the placebo test and multiple robustness tests, providing solid empirical support for exerting the effectiveness of the policies. Second, the mechanism test shows that the upgrading of industrial structure and the improvement of urbanization level constitute the dual transmission paths for the policies to exert the gap-narrowing effect. The policies indirectly promote the convergence of the urban-rural income gap by empowering the modern transformation of agriculture and promoting the two-way flow of urban and rural factors. Third, the gap-narrowing effect of the pilot policies on low-income groups is not significant. Fourth, the heterogeneity analysis shows that the gap-narrowing effect of the policies has obvious regional differentiation characteristics, which is more prominent in the central and western provinces, major grain-producing areas and grain production-marketing balance areas. This finding provides an important empirical basis for the implementation and adjustment of the policies.

Implications

Based on the above conclusions, the following policy implications are drawn:

First, further optimize the capital input of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services, and set up special funds to support various new agricultural business entities to provide diversified agricultural trusteeship services, including full trusteeship, semi-trusteeship, trusteeship of different links and land cultivation and seeding on behalf of farmers. On the one hand, it adapts to the demand for agricultural trusteeship services brought about by the aging of the rural labor force and the development of agricultural machinery. On the other hand, the optimal allocation of rural labor resources brought by agricultural trusteeship services promotes the non-agricultural employment of farmers, enabling them to obtain diversified non-agricultural income and thus narrowing the urban-rural income gap. It is worth noting that in the process of promoting agricultural trusteeship services, it is necessary to strengthen the publicity of agricultural trusteeship services, timely use diversified digital publicity methods, attach importance to the influence of village-level Party organizations, rural talents and new rural sages, and enhance farmers’ cognition and acceptance of trusteeship services. At the same time, it is essential to establish and improve a risk prevention mechanism for agricultural trusteeship services, focus on the core issues concerned by farmers, improve and refine the terms of trusteeship service contracts, and promote the in-depth and solid development of agricultural trusteeship services, laying a foundation for narrowing the urban-rural income gap.

Second, expand the scope of policy support, build a full-industrial-chain socialized service system, and promote the upgrading of industrial structure. On the one hand, refine the subsidy policies to support service subjects in expanding post-production services, and give policy inclinations in terms of land use and credit subsidies. Guide service subjects to connect with e-commerce platforms and supermarkets, expand market-oriented services such as live-streaming e-commerce for agricultural products and agricultural product processing, accelerate the extension of the agricultural industry to the consumption side, and enable farmers to benefit from the extension of the industrial chain, thus narrowing the urban-rural income gap. On the other hand, encourage the development of new models such as “full-process trusteeship + contract farming”. Support service subjects to unite with leading enterprises, formulate standardized production processes around local characteristic industries, and provide full-chain services from seedling cultivation to product sales. For example, for the fruit and vegetable industry, launch integrated services covering planting technical guidance, picking planning, cold chain transportation and brand marketing, promoting the upgrading of the agricultural industry to a composite industry.

Shortcomings

It should be pointed out that although this paper uses the progressive difference-in-differences method to verify the impact of China’s pilot policies on agricultural socialized services on the urban-rural income gap and provides certain policy references for different countries to eliminate poverty and reduce inequality, there are still the following research deficiencies: First, the urban-rural income gap cannot be completely eliminated in the short term, so this paper does not answer whether the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services can stably and continuously narrow the urban-rural income gap and what the mechanism is that affects their stable and continuous narrowing of the urban-rural income gap. Second, the difficulty in narrowing the urban-rural income gap lies in rural areas, and the factors affecting farmers’ income are complex and changeable. It is not accurate enough to only test the impact of the pilot policies on agricultural socialized services on the urban-rural income gap from the perspective of macro data, and the obtained policy recommendations have limited operability. In the future, further research should be conducted from the perspective of micro data to deeply analyze the factors and mechanisms affecting farmers’ income, so as to provide more accurate empirical references for policy adjustment.

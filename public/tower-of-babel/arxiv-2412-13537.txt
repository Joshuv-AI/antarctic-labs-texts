Introduction

In this paper, we discuss models of the common knowledge logic.
The common knowledge logic is a multi-modal logic that includes the
modal operators
\mathsf{K}_{i}
(
i\in\mathcal{I}
, where

\mathcal{I}
is a finite set of agents) and
\mathsf{C}
in the language.
The intended meanings of
\mathsf{K}_{i}\phi
(
i\in\mathcal{I}
)
and
\mathsf{C}\phi
are “the agent
i
knows
\phi
” (
i\in\mathcal{I}
) and
“
\phi
is common knowledge
among
\mathcal{I}
”, respectively.
Semantically, this can be expressed as follows:

\mathsf{C}\phi
is true if and only if all of

\phi
,
\mathsf{E}\phi
,
\mathsf{E}^{2}\phi
,
\mathsf{E}^{3}\phi,\ldots

are true,
where
\mathsf{E}\phi=\bigwedge_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathsf{K}_{i}\phi

(see, e.g.,
[
11
,
10
,
16
,
9
]
).

Some studies define that

\mathsf{C}\phi
is true if and only if all of

\mathsf{E}\phi
,
\mathsf{E}^{2}\phi
,
\mathsf{E}^{3}\phi,\ldots

are true (see, e.g.,
[
4

).
However, our research can be easily modified to apply in such cases, as well.

A Kripke frame that satisfies the condition is

\langle W,R_{\mathsf{K}_{i}}(i\in\mathcal{I}),R_{\mathsf{C}}\rangle
,
where
R_{\mathsf{C}}
is the reflexive and transitive closure of

R_{\mathsf{E}}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}R_{\mathsf{K}_{i}}
.
We refer to such Kripke frames as CKL-frames.
An algebra that satisfies the condition is
a modal algebra with modal operators

\mathop{\mathrm{K}}\nolimits_{i}
(

) and
\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits
,
which satisfies that

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x\leq\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x=\bigsqcap_{n\in\omega}\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits^{n}x

where
\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits x=\bigsqcap_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathop{\mathrm{K}}\nolimits_{i}x

In this paper, we show that the class of CKL-frames
is modally definable.
Then, we show the existence of
a modal algebra in which the common knowledge logic

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x\not=\bigsqcap_{n\in\omega}\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits^{n}x

Consequently, it follows that
the class of CKL-algebras

We use the proof systems
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}

The system
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}
includes the first two axiom schemas of (
1.1
)
and the following
\omega

\dfrac{\gamma\supset\Box_{1}(\phi_{1}\supset\Box_{2}(\phi_{2}\supset\cdots\supset\Box_{k}(\phi_{k}\supset\mathsf{E}^{n}\phi)\cdots))\text{\rm\hskip 10.0pt($n\in\omega$) }}{\gamma\supset\Box_{1}(\phi_{1}\supset\Box_{2}(\phi_{2}\supset\cdots\supset\Box_{k}(\phi_{k}\supset\mathsf{C}\phi)\cdots))}.

are sound and complete
with respect to the class of CKL-frames, respectively.
Then, it follows immediately that both systems are sound and complete
with respect to the class of CKL-algebras.
We write
\mathbf{CKL}
for the set of formulas that are derivable in
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
and
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}
.
For the knowledge operators
\mathsf{K}_{i}
(

), we only assume

\mathbf{K}
.
However, the results of the paper hold for
models for

\mathbf{K}
,
\mathbf{D}
,
\mathbf{T}
,
\mathbf{4}

Then, we prove that
the class of CKL-frames

S

knowledge logic such that
the class of CKL-frames is equal to the class of Kripke frames in which

In fact, we show that the class of CKL-frames is equal to the class of Kripke frames
in which
\mathbf{CKL}
is valid.
On the other hand, we show that the class of CKL-algebras is not modally definable.
We first introduce the notion of MH-algebras,

\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
.
Then, it is shown that
the class of MH-algebras
is equal to
the class of modal algebras in which
\mathbf{CKL}
is valid.
Subsequently, it is proved that
the class of CKL-algebras is a proper subclass of the class of MH-algebras.
Consequently, it follows that the class of CKL-algebras is not modally definable.
Additionally, we show that the free MH-algebras are CKL-algebras.

In the last part of the paper, we discuss the infinitary common knowledge logic.
The infinitary common knowledge logic is
introduced by Kaneko-Nagashima in
[
7

8

Then, it is shown by

We show that the infinitary extension of
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
is also sound and complete
with respect to the class of CKL-frames.
Infinitary extensions of logics are often compared with predicate extensions
of logics.
It is proved in
[

that the predicate extension of

However,

that
the predicate extension of
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
is not complete with respect to the

Our result is one of the examples demonstrating the

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall basic definitions and fix notation.
Throughout the paper, we write
\mathcal{I}
for a non-empty finite set of agents.

The language for the common knowledge logic consists of the
following symbols:

(1)

a countable set
\mathsf{Prop}
of propositional variables;

(2)

\bot
and
\top
;

(3)

logical connectives:

\lor
,
\land
, and
\neg
;

(4)

modal operators
\mathsf{K}_{i}
(
i\in\mathcal{I}
) and
\mathsf{C}
.

The set
\Phi_{\mathrm{CKL}}
of formulas are defined in the usual way.
We write
\phi\supset\psi
,

\phi\equiv\psi
, and
\mathsf{E}\phi
to abbreviate

\neg\phi\lor\psi
,

(\phi\supset\psi)\land(\psi\supset\phi)
, and
\bigwedge_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathsf{K}_{i}\phi
,
respectively.
For each
n\in\omega
, we define
\mathsf{E}^{n}\phi
by

\mathsf{E}^{0}\phi=\phi
and
\mathsf{E}^{n+1}\phi=\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{E}^{n})\phi
.

Throughout this paper, we consider
multi-modal algebras with modal operators

\mathop{\mathrm{K}}\nolimits_{i}\ (i\in\mathcal{I})
and
\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits

as algebraic models for the common knowledge logic,
and we refer to such multi-modal algebras simply as modal algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

An algebra

\langle A,\sqcup,\sqcap,-,\mathop{\mathrm{K}}\nolimits_{i}\ (i\in\mathcal{I}),\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits,0,1,\rangle

is called a modal algebra if

\langle A,\sqcup,\sqcap,-,0,1,\rangle
is a Boolean algebra and

\Box 1=1
and
\Box(x\sqcap y)=\Box x\sqcap\Box y
hold
for each modal operator
\Box

and each
x
and
y
in
A
.
For each
x
in
A
, we write

\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits x
to abbreviate
\bigsqcap_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathop{\mathrm{K}}\nolimits_{i}x
, and
for each
x
and
y
in
A
,
we write
x\mbox{$\rightarrow$}y
for
-x\sqcup y
.
A modal algebra
A
is said to be complete,
if for any subset
S\subseteq A
, the least upper bound

\bigsqcup S
and
the greatest
lower bound
\bigsqcap S
exist in
A
,
and said to be completely multiplicative,
if for any subset
S\subseteq A
,

(2.1)

\Box\bigsqcap S=\bigsqcap_{s\in S}\Box s

holds for any modal operator
\Box
.
A modal algebra
A
is said to be
\omega_{1}
-complete, if

\bigsqcup S
and

\bigsqcap S
exist in
A
for any countable subset
S\subseteq A
and
said to be
\omega_{1}
-multiplicative, if
(
2.1
) holds for any countable subset
S\subseteq A
.
A modal algebra is called a CKL-algebra, if
the following hold for each
x\in A
:

(1)

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x\leq\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x
;

(2)

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x=\bigsqcap_{n\in\omega}\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits^{n}x
.

We write
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
for the class of all CKL-algebras.

Definition 2.2
.

An algebraic model is a pair
\langle A,v\rangle
, where

A

is a modal algebra and

v
is a mapping,
which is called a valuation in
A
,
from the set
\mathsf{Prop}
of propositional variables to
A
.
For each valuation
v
in
A
,
the domain
\mathsf{Prop}
is extended to
\Phi_{\mathrm{CKL}}
in the following way:

(1)

v(\bot)=0
,

v(\top)=1
;

(2)

v(\phi\lor\psi)=v(\phi)\sqcup v(\psi)
,
v(\phi\land\psi)=v(\phi)\sqcap v(\psi)
;

(3)

v(\neg\phi)=-v(\phi)
;

(4)

v(\mathsf{K}_{i}\phi)=\mathop{\mathrm{K}}\nolimits_{i}v(\phi)
(
i\in\mathcal{I}
),
v(\mathsf{C}\phi)=\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits v(\phi)
.

In this paper, we assume a Kripke frame is equipped with
relations

R_{\mathsf{K}_{i}}
(
i\in\mathcal{I}
) and
R_{\mathsf{C}}
,
unless otherwise noted.

Definition 2.3
.

A Kripke frame is a structure

F=\langle W,R_{\mathsf{K}_{i}}(i\in\mathcal{I}),R_{\mathsf{C}}\rangle
,
where
W
is a non-empty set and

R_{\mathsf{K}_{i}}
(
i\in\mathcal{I}
) and
R_{\mathsf{C}}

are binary relations
on
W
.
For any relation
R
in
F
and any
x\in W
,
we write
R(x)
for the set
\{y\in W\mid(x,y)\in R\}
.
A Kripke frame is called
a CKL-frame, if

R_{\mathsf{C}}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(R_{\mathsf{E}}\right)^{n}
,
where

R_{\mathsf{E}}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}R_{\mathsf{K}_{i}}.

That is,
R_{\mathsf{C}}
is the reflexive and transitive closure of

R_{\mathsf{E}}
.
We write
\mathbf{Frm}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
for the class of all CKL-frames.

Definition 2.4
.

A Kripke model is a pair
\langle F,v\rangle
, where

F=\langle W,\{R_{\mathsf{K}_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}},R_{\mathsf{C}}\rangle

is a Kripke frame and

v
is a mapping,
which is called a valuation in
F
,
from the set
\mathsf{Prop}
of propositional variables to
\mathcal{P}(W)
.
For each valuation
v
in
F
,
the domain
\mathsf{Prop}
is extended to
\Phi_{\mathrm{CKL}}
in the following way:

(1)

v(\bot)=\emptyset
,

v(\top)=W
;

(2)

v(\phi\lor\psi)=v(\phi)\cup v(\psi)
,
v(\phi\land\psi)=v(\phi)\cap v(\psi)
;

(3)

v(\neg\phi)=W\setminus v(\phi)
;

(4)

v(\Box\phi)=\{w\in W\mid R_{\Box}(w)\subseteq v(\phi))\}
,
for each modal operator
\Box
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
A
be a modal algebra.
A formula
\phi
is said to be valid in
A

(
A\models\phi
, in symbol),
if
v(\phi)=1
for any valuation

v
in
A
.
Let
C
be a class of modal algebras.
A formula
\phi
is said to be valid in
C

if
A\models\phi
for every
A\in C
.
A set
\Gamma
of formulas is said to be valid in
C

(
C\models\Gamma
, in symbol),
if
C\models\gamma
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
The corresponding relations between Kripke frames and formulas are defined
in the same way.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a set of formulas.
We write

\mathsf{Alg}(\Gamma)
(resp.
\mathsf{Frm}(\Gamma)
) for the class of
modal algebras
(resp. Kripke frames) in which
\Gamma
is valid.
Let
C
be a class of modal algebras or a class of Kripke frames. We write

\mathsf{For}(C)
for set of formulas which are valid in
C
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
C
be a class of modal algebras (resp. Kripke frames).
We say that
C
is modally definable, if there exists a set

S
of formulas of the common knowledge logic such that

C=\mathsf{Alg}(S)
(resp.
C=\mathsf{Frm}(S)
).

Let
C
be a class of modal algebras (resp. Kripke frames).
It is well known that the following holds:

(2.2)

\text{
$C$ is modally definable
$\ \Leftrightarrow\ $
$C=\mathsf{Alg}(\mathsf{For}(C))$
(resp. $C=\mathsf{Frm}(\mathsf{For}(C))$)
}.

Let
F=\langle W,R_{\mathsf{K}_{i}}(i\in\mathcal{I}),R_{\mathsf{C}}\rangle
be
a Kripke frame.
It is well known that

F^{+}=\langle\mathcal{P}(W),\cup,\cap,W\setminus,\Box_{R_{\mathsf{K}_{i}}}(i\in\mathcal{I}),\Box_{R_{\mathsf{C}}},\emptyset,W\rangle

is a complete and completely multiplicative modal algebra, where

\Box_{R}S=\{w\in W\mid R(w)\subseteq S\}

for each binary relation
R
in
F
and
S\subseteq W
.
It is also well known that

(2.3)

F\models\phi\text{ $\ \Leftrightarrow\ $ }F^{+}\models\phi

holds, for any formula
\phi

(
[
5
,
6
,
15
]
, see also
[
1
]
).

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
F
be a Kripke frame.
Then,

F
is a CKL-frame, if and only if
F^{+}
is a
CKL-algebra.

Proof.

Let
F=\langle W,R_{\mathsf{K}_{i}}(i\in\mathcal{I}),R_{\mathsf{C}}\rangle
be
a Kripke frame.
First, suppose that
F
is a CKL-frame.
Then,
R_{\mathsf{C}}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(R_{\mathsf{E}}\right)^{n}
.
Take any
X\subseteq W
.
Then, for any
w\in W
,

\displaystyle w\in\Box_{R_{\mathsf{C}}}X

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ R_{\mathsf{C}}(w)\subseteq X

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ \left(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(R_{\mathsf{E}}\right)^{n}\right)(w)\subseteq X

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ \forall n\in\omega\left(\left(R_{\mathsf{E}}\right)^{n}(w)\subseteq X\right)

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ w\in\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\left(\Box_{R_{\mathsf{E}}}\right)^{n}X.

Next, suppose that

F^{+}
is a CKL-algebra. Take any
x\in W
.
We show that

R_{\mathsf{C}}(x)=\left(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(R_{\mathsf{E}}\right)^{n}\right)(x)
.
For any
w\in W
,

\displaystyle w\not\in R_{\mathsf{C}}(x)

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ x\in\Box_{R_{\mathsf{C}}}\left(W\setminus\{w\}\right)

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ x\in\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\left(\Box_{R_{\mathsf{E}}}\right)^{n}\left(W\setminus\{w\}\right)

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ \forall n\in\omega\left(w\not\in\left(R_{\mathsf{E}}\right)^{n}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ w\not\in\left(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\left(R_{\mathsf{E}}\right)^{n}\right)(x).

∎

3.
Proof systems for common knowledge logic

In this section, we define two equivalent proof systems
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
and

\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}
for
the common knowledge logic, initially introduced
by Meyer and van der Hoek
[
10
]
and
Kaneko-Nagashima-Suzuki-Tanaka
[
9
]
, respectively.

We first define the system
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
which is originally given
by Meyer and van der Hoek (the system
\mathbf{KEC}_{\mathbf{(m)}}
of
[
10
]
,
where
m
is the number of the knowledge operators
\mathsf{K}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{K}_{m}
).

Definition 3.1
.

The proof system
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
for the common knowledge logic
consists of the following axiom schemas (1)-(5) and inference rules (6)-(8):

(1)

all tautologies;

(2)

\Box(\phi\supset\psi)\supset(\Box\phi\supset\Box\psi)
, for
each modal operator
\Box
;

(3)

\mathsf{C}\phi\supset\phi
;

(4)

\mathsf{C}\phi\supset\mathsf{E}\mathsf{C}\phi
;

(5)

\mathsf{C}(\phi\supset\mathsf{E}\phi)\supset(\phi\supset\mathsf{C}\phi);

(6)

modus ponens;

(7)

uniform substitution rule;

(8)

necessitation rule for each modal operator.

Next, we define the system
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}
, which includes an
\omega
-rule.
It is originally introduced by Kaneko-Nagashima-Suzuki-Tanaka
(the system
\mathrm{CY}
in
[
9
]
),
and is a Hilbert-style translation of the sequent system

\mathrm{CK}
given in
[
13
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

The proof system
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}

consists of
axiom schemas (1)-(4) and inference rules (6)-(8) in Definition
3.1
,
and the following
\omega
-rule:
for any
k\in\omega
and
any modal operators
\Box_{1},\ldots,\Box_{k}
,

(3.1)

\dfrac{\gamma\supset\Box_{1}(\phi_{1}\supset\Box_{2}(\phi_{2}\supset\cdots\supset\Box_{k}(\phi_{k}\supset\mathsf{E}^{n}\phi)\cdots))\text{\rm\hskip 10.0pt($n\in\omega$) }}{\gamma\supset\Box_{1}(\phi_{1}\supset\Box_{2}(\phi_{2}\supset\cdots\supset\Box_{k}(\phi_{k}\supset\mathsf{C}\phi)\cdots))}.

The set of premises of inference rule (
3.1
) is countable.
When
k=0
,
(
3.1
) means that

(3.2)

\dfrac{\gamma\supset\mathsf{E}^{n}\phi\text{\rm\hskip 10.0pt($n\in\omega$) }}{\gamma\supset\mathsf{C}\phi}.

It is shown
in
[
10
]
that a formula
\phi\in\Phi_{\mathrm{CKL}}
is
derivable in
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
if and only if
\phi
is valid in the class

\mathbf{Frm}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
of CKL-frames.
Soundness and completeness of
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}
with respect to

\mathbf{Frm}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
is shown in
[
13
,
9
]
.
So,
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
and
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}
are equivalent.
We write
\mathbf{CKL}
for the set of formulas that are derivable in these
proof systems.
It is obvious from the Kripke completeness that the proof systems
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
and
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}

are sound and complete with respect to the class
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
of
CKL-algebras.
Hence, we have

(3.3)

\mathbf{CKL}=\mathsf{For}(\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{CKL}}).

4.
Models for
\mathbf{CKL}

In this section, we show
that the class
\mathbf{Frm}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
of CKL-frames is
modally definable, while
the class
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
of CKL-algebras is not,
which means that
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
is not a variety.
In fact, we prove that

\mathbf{Frm}_{\mathrm{CKL}}=\mathsf{Frm}(\mathbf{CKL})
.
Then, we introduce a class
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{MH}}
of MH-algebras, and
show that

\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{MH}}=\mathsf{Alg}(\mathbf{CKL})
.
Then, we prove that

\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
is a proper subclass of
\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{MH}}
.
Consequently, it follows that

\mathbf{Alg}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
is not modally definable.
Finally, we show that any free MH-algebra is a CKL-algebra.

Definition 4.1
.

A modal algebra is called a MH-algebra, if
the following hold for each
x\in A
:

(1)

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x\leq x
;

(2)

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x\leq\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x

(3)

\omega_{1}
-multiplicative CKL-algebras.
It is obvious that the Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra of

\mathbf{IPS}_{\omega}
is an
\omega_{1}
-complete and

\omega_{1}
-multiplicative CKL-algebra.
By Theorem
4.4
, it follows that
the Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra of
\mathbf{IPS}_{\mathrm{MH}}

is also an
\omega_{1}
-complete and

\omega_{1}
-multiplicative CKL-algebra.
Hence,

\mathbf{IPS}_{\omega}
and
\mathbf{IPS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
are complete
with respect to the class of
\omega_{1}
-complete and

\omega_{1}
-multiplicative CKL-algebras.
∎

By Theorem
5.3
,

\mathbf{IPS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
and
\mathbf{IPS}_{\omega}
are equivalent. Hence,
we have the following:

Corollary 5.5
.

For each formula
\phi\in\mathrm{I}\Phi_{\mathrm{CKL}}
,

\phi
is provable in
\mathbf{IPS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
if and only if
it is valid in the class
\mathbf{Frm}_{\mathrm{CKL}}
of CKL-frames.

Interestingly, the Kripke completeness of
\mathbf{IPS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
can also be shown directly, by
using a kind of general
technique, whereas proving the Kripke completeness of
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
is not straightforward
(see
[
10
]
).
It is well-known that the Jónsson-Tarski representation theorem provides
Kripke completeness of many types of modal logics.
Furthermore, it is known that an extension of the Jónsson-Tarski
representation
theorem, which preserves countably many infinite joins and meets, holds,
and the Kripke completeness of various non-compact modal logics follows from this
extension in the same way (
[
14
,
12
,
13
]
).
In fact, Kripke completeness of some proof systems for the common knowledge
logic is proved by means of this extension (
[
14
,
12
,
13
]
).
When proving the Kripke completeness of the common knowledge logic
using this extension,
it is necessary to show that

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x=\bigsqcap_{n\in\omega}\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits^{n}x

holds in the
Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra.
As we have seen in Theorem
4.9
, it is true that the Lindenbaum-Tarski
algebra of
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
satisfies

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x=\bigsqcap_{n\in\omega}\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits^{n}x
,
but, in Theorem
4.9
,
this was shown by using the Kripke completeness of
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
.
Here, we need to show that

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x=\bigsqcap_{n\in\omega}\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits^{n}x
holds in the
Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra of
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
without relying on Kripke completeness.
However, this does not seem to be straightforward.
On the other hand, it follows immediately from Theorem
4.4
that
the Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra of
\mathbf{IPS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
satisfies

\mathop{\mathrm{C}}\nolimits x=\bigsqcap_{n\in\omega}\mathop{\mathrm{E}}\nolimits^{n}x
.
Therefore, it is straightforward to show the Kripke completeness of
\mathbf{IPS}_{\mathrm{MH}}

directly from the extension of Jónsson-Tarski representation.

Finally, we compare the infinitary extensions of
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}
and
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
with
their predicate extensions.
For simplicity, we consider predicate extensions without
function symbols and equality.
The predicate extensions of
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}
and
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
are defined
by adding the following axiom schemas and inference rules to them, respectively:

(1)

\phi[y/x]\supset\exists\phi
,

\forall x\phi\supset\phi[y/x]
;

(2)

\dfrac{\phi[y/x]\supset\psi}{\exists x\phi\supset\psi}
,

\dfrac{\psi\supset\phi[y/x]}{\psi\supset\forall x\phi}

(where
y
is a variable
that does not occur in the conclusion);

(3)

Barcan formula:

\forall x\Box\phi\supset\Box\forall x\phi.

As observed, both the infinitary extensions of
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}
and
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
are
Kripke complete.
However, their predicate extensions exhibit different properties.
It is proved in
[
9
]

that the predicate extension of
\mathbf{PS}_{\omega}
is sound and complete
with respect to the class of CKL-frames with constant domains.
However, Wolter
[
16
]
proved that the set of
formulas of the predicate common knowledge logic which is valid
in the class of CKL-frames with constant domains is not recursively
enumerable.
Therefore,
the predicate extension of
\mathbf{PS}_{\mathrm{MH}}
is not complete with respect to the
class of CKL-frames with constant domains.
Theorem
5.5
serves as an example demonstrating the
distinct properties exhibited by infinitary extensions and predicate extensions.

Abstract
. We prove a representation formula of intrinsic Hopf-Lax type for subsolutions to Hamilton-Jacobi equations involving a Caputo time-fractional derivative.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 70H20, 34K37

1.
Introduction

Consider the Cauchy problem for Hamilton–Jacobi equations

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{(0,t]}^{\beta}v+H(Dv)=0,\qquad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\times(0,\infty),\\
v(y,0)=h(y),\qquad\,\,\,\qquad\mbox{on }\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\times\{t=0\},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
h:\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous and bounded,
H:\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and such that
\lim_{|p|\to\infty}\frac{H(p)}{|p|}=+\infty
and

(2)

\partial_{(0,t]}^{\beta}v(x,t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial_{\tau}v(x,\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\beta}}\,d\tau

is the Caputo time-fractional derivative of order
\beta\in(0,1)
of
v:\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}.
Since the Caputo fractional derivative is defined in an integral form, it is a non-local operator. It has ’memory’ property, which means that a present state depends on past states. Derivatives and integrals to non integer orders are part of the fractional calculus and it represents a powerful tool in applied mathematics to study a myriad of problems from different fields of science and engineering, with many break-through results found in mathematical physics, finance, hydrology, biophysics, thermodynamics, control theory, statistical mechanics, astrophysics, cosmology and bioengineering (see
[
17
,
12
]
). A theory of viscosity solutions for a general class of Hamilton-Jacobi equations with Caputo time-fractional derivative have been recently developed in
[
10
,
19
]
.

In
[
3
]
, the authors show that the value function

v_{\beta}(x,t)=\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}}\left(E_{t}L\left(\frac{x-y}{E_{t}}\right)+g(y)\right)\right]

is a.e. solution of the problem (
1
). Here,
L:\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\to\mathbb{R}
is the Legendre transform of
H
defined by
L(q)=\sup_{p}\{pq-H(p)\}
and
E_{t}
is a continuous, non decreasing stochastic process defined as the inverse of a
\beta
-stable subordinator
D_{t},
i.e.,
E_{t}:=\inf\{\tau>0\,:\,D_{\tau}>t\}
for
t\geq 0.
The process
X(s)=Y(E_{s})
solves the stochastic differential equation

(3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dX(s)=\alpha(s)dE_{s},\\
X(0)=\xi,\\
\end{array}\right.

where
Y
is a
C^{1}
function that satisfy the constraint
Y(t)=x,\alpha(s)=\dot{Y}(E_{s})
and
\xi=Y(0).

Aim of this paper is to prove a representation formula of intrinsic Hopf-Lax type for subsolutions to Hamilton-Jacobi equation involving a Caputo time-fractional derivative. More precisely, our main objective is to introduce and to analyze here the map
u:Y\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
with
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
compact defined as

u(x,t)=\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\inf_{z\in Y}\left\{E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(x)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)+g(z)\right\}\right],

where
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{\kappa}):Y\to\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
is a continuous section of a quotient map
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\to Y
(see Definition
2.1
),
g:=\max_{j=1,\dots,\kappa}f_{j}
and
L:\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\to\mathbb{R}
is a suitable continuous map. In additional,
f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)=f(y)-a\in\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
such that
d(f(y),\pi^{-1}(z))=d(f(y),a)
and, as above,
E_{t}
is a continuous, non decreasing stochastic process defined as the inverse of a
\beta
-stable subordinator
D_{t}.
In Theorem
3.1
, we prove that

\partial^{\beta}_{(0,t]}u(x,t)+H_{1}(Du(x,t))\leq 0,

where
\partial^{\beta}_{(0,t]}u
is the Caputo time-fractional derivative of order
\beta\in(0,1)
of
u
and

H_{1}(Du(x,t)):=\max_{q\in Y}\{Du(x,t)\cdot q-C\sqrt{L}(f(q))\},

for some fixed
C>0.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

2.1.
Intrinsically Lipschitz sections

In
[
8
]
, we give the following notion.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space,
Y
be a topological space and
\pi:X\to Y
be a quotient map, i.e., it is continuous, open, and surjective. We say that a map
\varphi:Y\to X
is a section of
\pi
if

\pi\circ\varphi=\mbox{id}_{Y}.

Moreover, we say that a map
\varphi:Y\to X
is an
intrinsically Lipschitz section of
\pi
with constant
\ell
, with
\ell\in[1,\infty)
, if in addition

d(\varphi(y_{1}),\varphi(y_{2}))\leq\ell d(\varphi(y_{1}),\pi^{-1}(y_{2})),\quad\mbox{for all }y_{1},y_{2}\in Y.

Here
d
denotes the distance on
X
, and, as usual, for a subset
A\subset X
and a point
x\in X
, we have

d(x,A):=\inf\{d(x,a):a\in A\}
.

We notice that if
Y
is compact, we get that

(4)

K:=\sup_{y_{1},y_{2}\in Y}d(\varphi(y_{1}),\pi^{-1}(y_{2}))<+\infty.

We underline that, in the case
\pi
is a Lipschitz quotient or submetry
[
2
,
1
]
, being intrinsically Lipschitz is equivalent to biLipschitz embedding, see Proposition 2.4 in
[
8
]
. However, we recall some examples of continuous sections and intrinsically Lipschitz sections.

(1)

Let the general linear group
X=GL(n,\mathbb{R})
or
X=GL(n,\mathbb{C})
of degree
n
which is the set of
n\times n
invertible matrices, together with the operation of ordinary matrix multiplication. We consider
Y=\mathbb{R}^{*}=GL(n,\mathbb{R})/SL(n,\mathbb{R})
or
Y=\mathbb{C}^{*}=GL(n,\mathbb{C})/SL(n,\mathbb{C})
where the special linear group
SL(n,\mathbb{R})
(or
SL(n,\mathbb{C})
) is the subgroup of
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
(or
GL(n,\mathbb{C})
) consisting of matrices with determinant of
1
. Here the linear map
\pi=det:GL(n,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}^{*}
is a surjective homomorphism where
Ker(\pi)=SL(n,\mathbb{R}).

(2)

Let
X=GL(n,\mathbb{R})
as above and
Y=GL(n,\mathbb{R})/O(n,\mathbb{R})
where
O(n,\mathbb{R})
is the orthogonal group in dimension
n.
Recall that
Y
is diffeomorphic to the space of upper-triangular matrices with positive entries on the diagonal, the natural map
\pi:X\to Y
is linear.

(3)

Let
X=\mathbb{R}^{2\kappa},Y=\mathbb{R}
endowed with the Euclidean distance and
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{2\kappa}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as
\pi((x_{1},\dots,x_{2\kappa})):=x_{1}+\dots+x_{2\kappa}
for any
(x_{1},\dots,x_{2\kappa})\in\mathbb{R}^{2\kappa}.
An easy example of sections of
\pi
is the following one: let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\kappa}
given by

\varphi(y)=(y+a_{1}f_{1}(y),-a_{1}f_{1}(y),a_{2}f_{2}(y),-a_{2}f_{2}(y),\dots,a_{\kappa}f_{\kappa}(y),-a_{\kappa}f_{\kappa}(y)),\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R},

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous maps for any
i=1,\dots,\kappa
.

(4)

Regarding examples of intrinsically Lipschitz sections the reader can see
[
18
, Example 4.58]
.

2.2.
The subordinator process

Fixed a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
, let
\{D_{\tau}\}_{\tau\geq 0}
be a stable subordinator of order
\beta\in(0,1)
, i.e. a one-dimensional, non decreasing Lévy process whose probability density function (PDF) has Laplace transform equal to
e^{-\tau s^{\beta}}
. The inverse stable process
\{E_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, defined as the first passage time of the process
D_{\tau}
over the level
t
, i.e.

E_{t}:=\inf\{\tau>0:D_{\tau}>t\},

has sample paths which are continuous, non decreasing and such that
E_{0}=0
,
E_{t}\to+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
. It is worthwhile to observe that
E_{t}
does not have stationary or independent increments. The process
E_{t}
can be used to model systems with two time scales: a deterministic one given by the standard time t, referred to an external observer, and a stochastic one given by
E_{t}
, internal to the physical process (see
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
).

We recall some basic properties of the process
E_{t}
which we will exploit in the following.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
t>0
, it holds:

(i)

For any
\lambda>0
there exists a non negative constant
C(\lambda,\beta)
such that

(5)

\mathbb{E}[E^{\lambda}_{t}]=C(\lambda,\beta)t^{\lambda\beta},

with
C(\lambda,\beta)=\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+1)}{\Gamma(\lambda\beta+1)}.

(ii)

The process
E_{t}
has PDF

(6)

\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(s,t)=\frac{t}{\beta}s^{-1-\frac{1}{\beta}}\mathscr{D}_{\beta}(ts^{-1/\beta}),

where
\mathscr{D}_{\beta}
is the PDF of
D_{1}.

For the proof of the following result we refer to
[
16
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The function
\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\cdot,t)
is a weak solution, in sense of distributions, of

(7)

\partial^{\beta}_{(0,t]}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(r,t)=-\partial_{r}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(r,t)-\frac{t^{\beta}}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\delta_{0}(r),\quad r\in(0,+\infty).

2.3.
The intrinsic Hopf-Lax formula

Taking inspiration from
[
9
, Section 3]
and
[
3
]
, we consider the intrinsic Hopf-Lax formula, i.e. the map
u_{\beta}:Y\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
with
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
compact defined as

(8)

u_{\beta}(x,t)=\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\inf_{z\in Y}\left\{E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(x)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)+g(z)\right\}\right],

where
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{\kappa}):Y\to\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
is a continuous section of a quotient map
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\to Y
and

g(y):=\max_{j=1,\dots,\kappa}f_{j}(y),\quad\forall y\in Y.

Here,
f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)=f(y)-a\in\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
such that
d(f(y),\pi^{-1}(z))=d(f(y),a).
Moreover, from now on we will assume the continuous map
L:\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following properties:

•

It holds

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(x)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[C\sqrt{L}\left(\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{E_{t}}\right)\right],

for any
x,y,z\in Y
and
t>0
where
C=C(K)>0
where
K>0
is given by (
4
).

•

If
0<s<t,
we ask

(10)

\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(x)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{s}L\left(\frac{f(x)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{s}}\right)\right],

for any
x,z\in Y.

An example of a map that satisfies these properties is
L(v)=1/2|v|^{2},
for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}.
Indeed, it is easy to check that
d(f(x),\pi^{-1}(z))\leq d(f(x),f(y))+d(f(y),\pi^{-1}(z)),\forall x,y,z\in Y
and so since
Y
is compact, then

d^{2}(f(x),\pi^{-1}(z))-d^{2}(f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z))\leq 2Kd(f(y),f(x)),

for any
x,y,z\in Y,
where
K>0
is given by (
4
) and so (
9
) holds. On the other hand, (
10
) is true because
E_{t}
is non decreasing.

Our first result is the following.

Proposition 2.4
.

The function
u_{\beta}
defined as in (
8
) satisfies the following properties:

(1)

It holds

|u_{\beta}(x,t)-u_{\beta}(y,t)|\leq C\max\left(\sqrt{L}\left(\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{t}\right),\sqrt{L}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{t}\right)\right),\quad\forall x,y\in Y,\,t>0,

where
C>0
is given by (
9
).

(2)

If
f
is an intrinsically
\ell
-Lipschitz map, then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for all
(x,t)\in Y\times(0,+\infty)

|u(x,t)-g(x)|\leq ct^{\beta}.

Hence,

u_{\beta}=g,\quad\mbox{ on }Y\times\{t=0\}.

(3)

u_{\beta}(x,\cdot)
is locally
\beta
-Hölder continuous.

Remark 2.5
.

Proposition
2.3
(3) states that

\partial_{t}u_{\beta}(x,\cdot)\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
for every
x\in Y
. Moreover, the function
u
is absolutely continuous with respect to
t
and therefore the time-fractional derivative
\partial_{t}u_{(0,t]}^{\beta}(x,t)\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,T)
is defined for a.e.
(x,t)\in Y\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
(see for example
[
13
, Lemma 2.17]
). On the other hand, thanks to Proposition
2.3
(1), we know that if
f
is differentiable, then
u_{\beta}(\cdot,t)
is so too.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

(1).
Let
x,y\in Y
and
t>0.
We choose
z\in Y
such that

u_{\beta}(y,t)=\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)+g(z)\right].

and so by (
9
) we get

\displaystyle u_{\beta}(x,t)-u_{\beta}(y,t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(x)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)+g(z)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)+g(z)\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{L}\left(\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{E_{t}}\right).

Hence, interchanging the roles of
x
and
y
we have the thesis.

(2).
Let
x\in Y
and
t>0.
Choosing
z=x
we obtain

(11)

u_{\beta}(x,t)\leq\mathbb{E}_{x,t}[E_{t}L(0)+g(x)]=C(1,\beta)L(0)t^{\beta}+g(x),

where we used (
5
).
Moreover,

\displaystyle u_{\beta}(x,t)-g(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\inf_{z\in Y}\left\{E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(x)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)+g(z)-g(x)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\inf_{z\in Y}\left\{E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(x)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)-\ell d(f(x),\pi^{-1}(z))\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{t}\max_{p=\frac{f(x)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}}\left\{\ell|p|-L\left(p\right)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=-\max_{w\in[0,\ell]}H(w)\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=-\max_{w\in[0,\ell]}H(w)C(1,\beta)t^{\beta},

where in the first inequality we used the fact that if
f
is an intrinsically
\ell
-Lipschitz section of
\pi,
then
g
is so too and in the last equality we used (
5
) again. Here
H
is the Hamiltonian associated by
L
in our intrinsic context (see
[
6
, Section 3.3]
).

Finally, putting together the last inequality and (
11
), it holds

|u(x,t)-g(x)|\leq\max\{|L(0)|,\max_{\xi\in[0,\ell]}|H(\xi)|\}C(1,\beta)t^{\beta},

which implies that
u=g
on
Y\times\{t=0\},
as desired.

(3).
First, we show that for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
s<t

u_{\beta}(y,t)-u_{\beta}(y,s)\leq 0,

for every
y\in Y
and so

(12)

u_{\beta}(y,t)-u_{\beta}(y,s)\leq C(t-s)^{\beta}.

Fix
y\in Y
and
0<s<t.
Choosing
z\in Y
such that

u_{\beta}(y,s)=\mathbb{E}_{y,t}\left[E_{s}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{s}}\right)+g(z)\right].

By (
10
), we deduce that

\displaystyle u_{\beta}(y,t)-u_{\beta}(y,s)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{y,t}\left[E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)+g(z)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{y,t}\left[E_{s}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{s}}\right)+g(z)\right]

\displaystyle\leq 0,

as wished. Finally, we prove that for any
s,t\in[h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}]
such that
s<t

u_{\beta}(y,t)-u_{\beta}(y,s)\geq-C(t-s)^{\beta},

for every
y
belong to a compact subset of
Y.
Choose
z\in Y
such that

u_{\beta}(y,t)=\mathbb{E}_{y,t}\left[E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)+g(z)\right].

It follows

\displaystyle u_{\beta}(y,t)-u_{\beta}(y,s)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{y,t}\left[E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)+g(z)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{y,t}\left[E_{s}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{s}}\right)+g(z)\right]

\displaystyle\geq-\mathbb{E}_{y,t}\left[(E_{t}-E_{s})L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(z)}{E_{t}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq-c\mathbb{E}_{y,t}\left[(E_{t}-E_{s})\right]

\displaystyle=-cC(1,\beta)(t-s)^{\beta},

where in the last equality we used (
5
).
Now putting together the last inequality and (
12
), the proposition is then proved.
∎

3.
Subsolution of Hamilton-Jacobi equation involving a Caputo time-fractional derivative

In this section we present the main result of this paper. Here, we prove that the map defined as in (
8
) is a subsolution for Hamilton-Jacobi equation involving a Caputo time-fractional derivative defined as in (
2
). The case when there is the classical derivative
\partial_{t}
instead of
\partial^{\beta}_{(0,t]}
is studied in
[
5
,
7
]
.

Our goal is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
be a compact set,
f:Y\to\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
be a sublinear section of a quotient map
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\to Y
and let
u_{\beta}:Y\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
the map defined as (
8
). Given
(x,t)\in Y\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
Du_{\beta}(x,t)
exists and
E_{t}\geq 1
, we have that for any
q\in Y

\partial^{\beta}_{(0,t]}u_{\beta}(x,t)+H_{q}(Du_{\beta}(x,t))\leq 0,

where
\partial^{\beta}_{(0,t]}u
is the Caputo time-fractional derivative of order
\beta\in(0,1)
of
u
defined as in (
2
) and

H_{q}(Du_{\beta}(x,t))=Du_{\beta}(x,t)\cdot q-C\sqrt{L}(f(q)),

with
C>0
given by (
9
).

We record some preliminary observations.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
y\in Y
and any
s,t>0
such that
s<t
and
E_{t}\geq 1
we have

u_{\beta}(x,t)\leq\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\min_{y\in Y}\left(C(E_{t}-E_{s})\sqrt{L}\left(\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{E_{t}-E_{s}}\right)+u_{\beta}(y,s)\right)\right],

where
C>0
is given by (
9
).

Proof.

Fix
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
s<t
such that
E_{t}\geq 1.
For
y\in Y
let
Z:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
be a random variable such that

u_{\beta}(y,s)=\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{s}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(Z)}{E_{s}}\right)+g(Z)\right].

We deduce that

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{t}L\left(\frac{f(x)-\pi^{-1}(Z)}{E_{t}}\right)+g(Z)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[C\frac{E_{t}-E_{s}}{E_{t}}\sqrt{L}\left(\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{E_{t}-E_{s}}\right)+\frac{E_{s}^{2}}{E_{t}}L\left(\frac{f(y)-\pi^{-1}(Z)}{E_{s}}\right)+g(Z)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[C(E_{t}-E_{s})\sqrt{L}\left(\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{E_{t}-E_{s}}\right)+u_{\beta}(y,s)\right],

where in the second inequality we used (
9
) and in the third one we used the facts that
E_{t}\geq 1
and
E_{t}\geq E_{s}
because
E_{t}
is non decreasing. This inequality is true for every
y\in Y
and so we get the thesis, as wished.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Arguing as in Lemma
3
, it is also possible to prove that if
\tau:\Omega\to(0,t)
is a stopping time such that
E_{t}\geq 1
then

(13)

u_{\beta}(x,t)\leq\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\min_{y\in Y}\left(C(E_{t}-E_{\tau})\sqrt{L}\left(\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{E_{t}-E_{\tau}}\right)+u_{\beta}(y,\tau)\right)\right],

where
C>0
is given by (
9
).

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Fix
q\in\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
and
h>0
. Consider the control law
\alpha(s)\equiv q
. Then the solution
X(s)
of (
3
) is given by
X(s)=x-(E_{t}-E_{s})q
. Define the stopping time

\tau_{h}=\sup\{s\in(t-h,t)\,:\,|X(s)-x|=h\}.

By (
13
) for
\tau=\tau_{h}
and
y=X(\tau_{h}),
we have

u_{\beta}(x,t)\leq\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\left(C(E_{t}-E_{\tau_{h}})\sqrt{L}\left(\frac{f(x)-f(X(\tau_{h}))}{E_{t}-E_{\tau_{h}}}\right)+u_{\beta}(X(\tau_{h}),\tau_{h})\right)\right].

Since
f
is sublinear

(14)

\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[u_{\beta}(x,t)-u_{\beta}(X(\tau_{h}),\tau_{h})\right]\leq C\sqrt{L}(f(q))\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[E_{t}-E_{\tau_{h}}\right].

By Ito’s formula
[
11
]
, we also have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[u_{\beta}(x,t)-u_{\beta}(X(\tau_{h}),\tau_{h})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\int_{\tau_{h}}^{t}du_{\beta}(X(s),s)\,ds\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\int_{\tau_{h}}^{t}\partial_{s}u_{\beta}(X(s),s)\,ds+\int_{\tau_{h}}^{t}Du_{\beta}(X(s),s)\,dX(s)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,t}\left[\int_{\tau_{h}}^{t}\partial_{s}u_{\beta}(X(s),s)\,ds+\int_{\tau_{h}}^{t}Du_{\beta}(X(s),s)\cdot q\,dE_{s}\right].

Therefore, recalling (

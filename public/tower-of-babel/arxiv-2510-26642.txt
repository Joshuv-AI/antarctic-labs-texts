Introduction

For integers
0\leq a\leq b
, define the interval
[a,b]=\{a,\dots,b\}
, and let
[n]=[1,n]
. Denote by
2^{[n]}
the power set of
[n]
, by
\binom{[n]}{k}
the family of
k
-subsets of
[n]
.
A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called
t
-
intersecting
if
|F\cap F^{\prime}|\geq t

for all
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
. For
t=1
,
\mathcal{F}
is simply called intersecting.
The well-known Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem
[
13
]
states that if
n\geq n_{0}(k,t)
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{[n]\choose k}
is
t
-intersecting, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq{n-t\choose k-t}
.
For
t=1
, they showed that
n_{0}(k,1)=2k
is the best possible. For
t\geq 2
, finding the exact value of
n_{0}(k,t)
seems challenging.
The case
t\geq 15
was determined by Frankl
[
15
]
using a combinatorial method and the general case
t\geq 2
was solved by Wilson
[
34
]
using an algebraic method.
More precisely, the Frankl–Wilson theorem states that for
k\geq t\geq 1
and
n\geq(t+1)(k-t+1)
, if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{[n]\choose k}
is
t
-intersecting, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq{n-t\choose k-t}
. Moreover, for
n>(t+1)(k-t+1)
, the upper bound can be achieved if and only if
\mathcal{F}=\{F\in{[n]\choose k}:T\subseteq F\}
for some fixed
t
-set
T
. A major advancement was achieved by the Complete Intersection Theorem of Ahlswede and Khachatrian
[
2
]
, who characterized the maximum size of
t
-intersecting subfamilies of
\binom{[n]}{k}
in the full range
2k-t<n<(t+1)(k-t+1)
.
For more related results, we refer to the surveys
[
12
,
21
]
.

1.1
Measure versions of intersection problems

Let
n,t
be positive integers with
n\geq t
.
For a real number
p\in(0,1)
, let
\mu_{p}:2^{[n]}\to[0,1]
be the
product measure
(or
p
-biased measure
), which is defined by

\mu_{p}(F)=p^{|F|}(1-p)^{n-|F|}

for a subset
F\subseteq[n]
.
For a set family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
, the measure of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mu_{p}(F).

A measure-theoretic extension of the Complete Intersection Theorem (see
[
3
,
8
,
23
,
29
]
) states that if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is
t
-intersecting with
n\geq t\geq 1
, then

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})\leq\max_{r}\mu_{p}\big(\mathcal{A}_{r}(n,t)\big)
,
where

\mathcal{A}_{r}(n,t):=\left\{A\subseteq[n]:|A\cap[t+2r]|\geq t+r\right\}.

When
p=\frac{1}{2}
, this recovers the classical Katona theorem
[
26
]
. More generally,
some results for
k
-uniform families can be transferred to the corresponding results in the measure setting, and vice versa; see
[
10
,
11
,
24
,
29
]
.
In the case
p<\frac{1}{t+1}
, we have

\max_{r}\mu_{p}(\mathcal{A}_{r}(n,t))=\mu_{p}(\mathcal{A}_{0}(n,t))=p^{t}
.
An algebraic proof of this case was provided by Friedgut
[
24
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
,
8
,
23
,
29
]
)
.

Let
n\geq t\geq 1
be integers and
p<\frac{1}{t+1}
. If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is
t
-intersecting, then

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})\leq p^{t},

with equality holding if and only if
\mathcal{F}=\{F\subseteq[n]:T\subseteq F\}
for some
T\in\binom{[n]}{t}
.

Two families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
are called
cross
t
-intersecting
if
|F_{1}\cap F_{2}|\geq t
for all
F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
. For
t=1
, we abbreviate cross
1
-intersecting as cross intersecting. Extending Theorem
1.1
, Tokushige
[
32
]
proposed the following conjecture for cross
t
-intersecting families.

Conjecture 1.2

(Tokushige
[
32
]
)
.

Let
n\geq t\geq 1
be integers and
p_{1},p_{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{t+1})
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be cross
t
-intersecting families. Then

\mu_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\mu_{p_{2}}(\mathcal{F}_{2})\leq(p_{1}p_{2})^{t},

with equality holding if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{2}=\{F\subseteq[n]:T\subseteq F\}
for some
T\in\binom{[n]}{t}
.

This conjecture is still widely open. To date, it has been verified only in a limited number of cases:

t=1
by Tokushige
[
30
]
;

p_{1}=p_{2}\in(0,0.114)
and
1\leq t\leq\frac{1}{2p}
by Tokushige
[
30
]
;

p_{1}=p_{2}\in(0,1-2^{-1/t})
and
t\geq 1
by Tokushige
[
32
]
;

p_{1}=p_{2}
and
t\geq 14
by Frankl, Lee, Siggers and Tokushige
[
22
]
;

p_{1}=p_{2}
and
t=2
by Tanaka and Tokushige
[
28
]
.
In this paper, we prove the bound in Conjecture
1.2
for all
p_{1},p_{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{t+1})
and
t\geq 3
(see the forthcoming Theorem
1.6
). This provides the first solution for
t\geq 3
that gets rid of the constraint
p_{1}=p_{2}
.

1.2
Intersection problems for integer sequences

For an integer
m\geq 2
, let
[m]^{n}
denote the collection of all
m^{n}
integer sequences
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
with
x_{i}\in[m]
.
For two vectors
\vec{x}
and
\vec{y}
, let us define their
intersection

\vec{x}\wedge\vec{y}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
, where

z_{i}=\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{ if }x_{i}=y_{i};\\
0&\text{ if }x_{i}\neq y_{i}.\end{cases}

A family
\mathcal{H}\subseteq[m]^{n}
is called
t
-intersecting
if for all
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\vec{x}\wedge\vec{y}
has at least
t
non-zero coordinates.
Ahlswede and Khachatrian
[
3
]
, and Frankl and Tokushige
[
19
]
independently proved that for
r:=\lfloor\frac{t-1}{m-2}\rfloor
and
n\geq t+2r
, if
\mathcal{H}\subseteq[m]^{n}
is
t
-intersecting, then

|\mathcal{H}|\leq m^{n}\cdot\mu_{{1}/{m}}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{r}(n,t)\bigr)
.
The case where
m\geq t+1
was previously established by Frankl and Furedi
[
17
]
.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
3
,
17
,
19
]
)
.

Let
n\geq t\geq 1
and
m\geq t+1
. If
\mathcal{H}\subseteq[m]^{n}
is
t
-intersecting, then

|\mathcal{H}|\leq m^{n-t}.

Two families
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\subseteq[m]^{n}
are said to be
cross
t
-intersecting
if for all
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\vec{y}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
,
\vec{x}\wedge\vec{y}
has at least
t
non-zero coordinates.
Tokushige
[
32
]
proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.4

(Tokushige
[
32
]
)
.

Let
n\geq t\geq 1
and
m\geq t+1
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\subseteq[m]^{n}
be cross
t
-intersecting families of integer sequences. Then

|\mathcal{H}_{1}||\mathcal{H}_{2}|\leq(m^{n-t})^{2}.

Tokushige
[
32
]
proved the case for
m>(1-2^{-1/t})^{-1}
and
t\geq 1
. Later, Frankl, Lee, Siggers, and Tokushige
[
22
]
verified the conjecture for
m\geq t+1
and
t\geq 14
.
In this paper, we prove Conjecture
1.4
for all
m>t+1
and
t\geq 1
(see the forthcoming Theorem
1.7
).

1.3
An extension of the IU-Theorem

A trivial fact states that if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is intersecting, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2^{n-1}
.
The celebrated IU-Theorem (see, e.g.,
[
21
,
27
]
) asserts that if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies the intersection-union condition, i.e.,
F\cap F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
and
F\cup F^{\prime}\neq[n]
for all
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2^{n-2}
. This bound is the best possible as seen by any intersecting family of
2^{[n-1]}
.
The IU-Theorem admits an equivalent reformulation in the language of integer sequences: if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[2]^{n}
satisfies that for all
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathcal{F}
there exist two coordinates
i,j\in[n]
such that
x_{i}=y_{i}=1
and
x_{j}=y_{j}=2
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2^{n-2}
.

At first glance, the IU-Theorem applies to families
\mathcal{F}\subseteq[2]^{n}
that are
2
-intersecting. This falls outside the scope of Theorem
1.3
, which requires
m\geq t+1
.
This gap motivated Frankl and Kupavskii
[
18
]
to introduce a broader concept for intersecting families. Formally, given integers
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{m}\geq 0
, a family
\mathcal{H}\subseteq[m]^{n}
is said to be
(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{m})
-intersecting
if for every pair
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathcal{H}
, the intersection
\vec{x}\wedge\vec{y}
has at least
t_{i}
coordinates equal to
i
for each
i\in[m]
.
Clearly, any
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
-intersecting family is
(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m})
-intersecting.
Frankl and Kupavskii
[
18
]
extended Theorem
1.3
and the IU-Theorem to the case
m\geq t_{i}+1
for each
i
, instead of
m\geq(\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i})+1
.

Theorem 1.5

(Frankl–Kupavskii
[
18
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq[m]^{n}
be
(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{m})
-intersecting, where
m\geq 2
and
n\geq t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m}
and
m\geq t_{i}+1\geq 1
for each
i\in[m]
. Then

|\mathcal{H}|\leq m^{n-\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}}.

For families
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\subseteq[m]^{n}
, we say they are
cross
(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{m})
-intersecting
if for all
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\vec{y}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
, the intersection
\vec{x}\wedge\vec{y}
contains at least
t_{i}
coordinates equal to
i
for every
i\in[m]
.
In this paper, we extend Theorem
1.5
to cross
(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})
-intersecting families (see Theorem
1.8
).

1.4
Main results

We begin by proving the product measure bound of Conjecture
1.2
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n\geq t\geq 3
be integers and
p_{1},p_{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{t+1})
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be cross
t
-intersecting families. Then

\mu_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\mu_{p_{2}}(\mathcal{F}_{2})\leq(p_{1}p_{2})^{t}.

Having established Theorem
1.6
, we confirm Conjecture
1.4
for
m>t+1
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
n\geq t\geq 1
and
m>t+1
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\subseteq[m]^{n}
be cross
t
-intersecting families of integer sequences. Then

|\mathcal{H}_{1}||\mathcal{H}_{2}|\leq(m^{n-t})^{2}.

Building on Theorem
1.7
, we leverage a correlation inequality to strengthen Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\subseteq[m]^{n}
are cross
(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{m})
-intersecting, where
m\geq 2
and
n\geq t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m}
and
m>t_{i}+1\geq 1
for each
i\in[m]
. Then

|\mathcal{H}_{1}||\mathcal{H}_{2}|\leq\left(m^{n-\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}}\right)^{2}.

Extending the celebrated
Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem,
Katona
[
26
]
proved that for
n\geq t\geq 2
, if

\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is a
t
-intersecting family, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\begin{cases}\sum_{i\geq\frac{n+t}{2}}{n\choose i}&\text{if $n+t$ is even;}\\
\sum_{i\geq\frac{n+t+1}{2}}{n\choose i}+{n-1\choose\frac{n+t-1}{2}}&\text{if $n+t$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is the Katona family
\mathcal{K}(n,t)
, where

\mathcal{K}(n,t):=\begin{cases}\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|\geq\frac{n+t}{2}\}&\text{if $n+t$ is even;}\\
\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|\geq\frac{n+t+1}{2}\}\cup\binom{[n-1]}{\frac{n+t-1}{2}}&\text{if $n+t$ is odd.}\end{cases}

We refer to
[
4
,
5
,
16
,
20
,
33
]
for related generalizations on Katona’s theorem. In Theorem
1.6
, we bounded the product measure of cross
t
-intersecting families for
p<\frac{1}{t+1}
.
In a different direction, we prove the following measure result for
p\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
n\geq t\geq 1
be integers and
p\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be cross
t
-intersecting families. Then

\operatorname{min}\left\{\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{1}),\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F}_{2})\right\}\leq\mu_{p}(\mathcal{K}(n,t)).

Theorem
1.9
generalizes a classical result of Ahlswede and Katona
[
1
]
, which solved a conjecture of Frankl
[
14
]
. Our approach to Theorem
1.9
is based on a different shifting technique.

Corollary 1.10

(Ahlswede–Katona
[
1
]
)
.

Let
n\geq t\geq 1
be integers. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be cross
t
-intersecting families. Then

\operatorname{min}\left\{|\mathcal{F}_{1}|,|\mathcal{F}_{2}|\right\}\leq|\mathcal{K}(n,t)|

Theorem

Corollary 1.11

(

Let
n\geq t\geq 1

2
Proofs of main results

2.1
Proof of Theorem
1.6

Our proof is inspired by the combinatorial approaches developed by Frankl and Furedi
[
17
]
and Tokushige
[
30
]
. A crucial ingredient in our argument is the following striking result, which originated in a conjecture of Tokushige
[
31
]
. This conjecture was recently solved by Zhang and Wu
[
35
]
in the case
k=\ell
, and He, Li, Wu, and Zhang
[
25
]
in general case
k\neq\ell
.

Lemma 2.1

(He–Li–Wu–Zhang
[
25
]
)
.

Let
n,k,\ell,t
be positive integers with
n\geq(t+1)(k-t+1)
and
k\geq\ell\geq t\geq 3
. Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
and
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{\ell}
be cross
t
-intersecting families. Then

|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\leq\binom{n-t}{k-t}\binom{n-t}{\ell-t}.

Lemma 2.2

(Bernoulli’s law of large numbers
[
9
]
)
.

Let
S_{n}
be the number of successes in
n
independent Bernoulli trials, each with probability of success
p
, where
0<p<1
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Pr}\left(\left|\frac{S_{n}}{n}-p\right|<\varepsilon\right)=1.

Now we are in the position to prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

For each
i\in[2]
, since
p_{i}\in(0,\frac{1}{t+1})
, there exists
0<\varepsilon_{i}<p_{i}
such that
p_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}<\frac{1}{t+1}
.
Let
q_{i}=1-p_{i}
and
I_{i}=((p_{i}-\varepsilon_{i})n,(p_{i}+\varepsilon_{i})n)\cap\mathbb{Z}
.
It follows from Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k\in I_{i}}\binom{n}{k}p_{i}^{k}q_{i}^{n-k}=1,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k\notin I_{i}}\binom{n}{k}p_{i}^{k}q_{i}^{n-k}=0.

(2.1)

Define
f(n,t)=\operatorname{max}\left\{\mu_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\mu_{p_{2}}(\mathcal{F}_{2}):\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\subseteq 2^{[n]}\text{ are cross $t$-intersecting}\right\}
. Clearly, we have
f(n,t)\geq(p_{1}p_{2})^{t}
.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
be families that achieve this maximum, i.e.,
f(n,t)=\mu_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\mu_{p_{2}}(\mathcal{F}_{2})
.
For any
k\in I_{i}
and
i\in[2]
, we have
\frac{k}{n}<p_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}<\frac{1}{t+1}
and hence
n>(t+1)k>(t+1)(k-t+1)
.
Let us define

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{(k)}=\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}:|F|=k\}
.
Then Lemma
2.1
implies that for any
k\in I_{1}
and
\ell\in I_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{1}^{(k)}||\mathcal{F}_{2}^{(\ell)}|\leq\binom{n-t}{k-t}\binom{n-t}{\ell-t}.

(2.2)

Consequently, for sufficiently large
n
, we get

\displaystyle f(n,t)\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{2}\left(\sum_{k\in I_{i}}|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{(k)}|p_{i}^{k}q_{i}^{n-k}+\sum_{k\notin I_{i}}\binom{n}{k}p_{i}^{k}q_{i}^{n-k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I_{1}}\sum_{\ell\in I_{2}}|\mathcal{F}_{1}^{(k)}||\mathcal{F}_{2}^{(\ell)}|p_{1}^{k}q_{1}^{n-k}p_{2}^{\ell}q_{2}^{n-\ell}+o(1)\qquad(\text{By }(\ref{fo1}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq t}\sum_{\ell\geq t}\binom{n-t}{k-t}\binom{n-t}{\ell-t}p_{1}^{k}q_{1}^{n-k}p_{2}^{\ell}q_{2}^{n-\ell}+o(1)\qquad(\text{By }(\ref{fo2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{1}p_{2})^{t}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}\binom{n-t}{i}p_{1}^{i}q_{1}^{n-t-i}\right)\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{n-t}{j}p_{2}^{j}q_{2}^{n-t-j}\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{1}p_{2})^{t}+o(1).

Therefore, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}f(n,t)=(p_{1}p_{2})^{t}.

(2.3)

For each
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}_{i}\cup\{F\cup\{n+1\}:F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\}
.
Then
\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq 2^{[n+1]}
are cross
t
-intersecting.
Moreover, we can see that
\mu_{p_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\prime})=\mu_{p_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})
for each
i\in\{1,2\}
.
It follows that

)

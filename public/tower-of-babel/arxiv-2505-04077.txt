1.
Introduction

The Schrödinger operator on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with homogeneous i.i.d. random potentials, known as the Anderson model, was first introduced by Anderson
[
And58
]
to describe the motion of a single quantum particle in disordered media. The study of the Anderson model primarily focuses on its spectral and dynamical properties. Of particular importance is the celebrated Anderson localization (i.e., pure point spectrum with exponentially decaying eigenfunctions) and delocalization (e.g., the existence of absolutely continuous spectrum) phase transition phenomenon. This phase transition relies heavily on the dimension
d
, the strength of the disorder, and the energy. Indeed, it is a general consensus that Anderson localization (for the Anderson model) should occur for all energies and all non-zero disorder if
d=1,2
, while for the case of
d\geq 3
and small disorder, there should exist an absolutely continuous spectrum in some energy interval. Mathematically, localization has been proven for three regimes: (i) for all energies and arbitrary disorder in
d=1
, (ii) in any dimension and for all energies at large disorder, and (iii) near the edges of the spectrum in any dimension and for arbitrary disorder, cf. e.g.,
[
GMP77
,
KS81
,
FS83
,
FMSS85
,
DLS85
,
SW86
,
AM93
,
BK05
,
DS20
]
. However, the problem of proving the existence of the absolutely continuous spectrum for the Anderson model remains largely open(cf. e.g., Problem 1 in
[
Sim00
]
). In fact, even proving the existence of extended states (e.g., wave functions belonging to
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\setminus\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
) for the Anderson model with non-zero disorder is far from reach. Delocalization has only been established for two special classes of random Schrödinger operators: operators on Bethe lattices (cf. e.g.,
[
Kle98
,
ASW06
]
) and operators on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with decaying random potentials (cf. e.g.,
[
Kri90
,
KKO00
,
JL00
,
Bou02
,
Bou03
]
).

Now, consider the Anderson model
H_{\omega}=\Delta+\kappa V_{\omega}(n)\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}
, wheer
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the coupling,
\Delta
the discrete Laplacian, and
\{V_{\omega}(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is the potential given by a sequence of i.i.d. random variables. It is known that, for a broad class of random potentials(including the completely singular Bernoulli ones), if
d=1
,
H_{\omega}
has Anderson localization almost surely for all
\kappa\neq 0
. However, a new type of phase transition occurs if
V_{\omega}(n)
is replaced by some decaying potential
V_{\omega}^{\prime}(n)=|n|^{-\alpha}V_{\omega}(n)
for some
\alpha>0.

More precisely, for
H^{\prime}_{\omega}=\Delta+\kappa V_{\omega}^{\prime}(n)\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}
with
d=1
and
\kappa\neq 0
, it has been proven in
[
Sim83
,
DSS85
,
KLS98
]
that the spectrum is almost surely dense pure point in
(-2,2)
if
0\leq\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, and is almost surely purely absolutely continuous in
(-2,2)
if
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
. In this important work
[
DSS85
]
, they also proved purely singular spectrum in some energy region if
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}.
The proof of
[
KLS98
]
relies crucially on one-dimensional methods, such as the transfer matrix formalism, which may not be available in higher dimensions. Thus, it is natural to ask if the above phase transition diagram has a higher-dimensional analogy. In the remarkable work
[
Bou03
]
, Bourgain provided a negative answer to this question and discovered new higher-dimensional phenomena. Specifically, he proved the existence of proper extended states for random Schrödinger operators on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with decaying random potentials
\kappa|n|^{-\alpha}{\omega}_{n}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{2}|n|^{-2\alpha})
for
d\geq 5
and
\frac{1}{3}<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2},
where
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is a sequence of i.i.d. Bernoulli random variables. The case of
\alpha=0
corresponds to the standard Anderson-Bernoulli model, so improving the bound
\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
to a smaller one is of significant importance. In
[
Bou03
]
, Bourgain remarked that “
It is
likely that the method may be made to work for all
\alpha>0
…It is reasonable to expect this type of argument to succeed for any fixed
\alpha>0
(with a number of resolvent iterations dependent on
\alpha
). To achieve this requires further renormalizations and taking care of certain additional difficulties due to the presence of a potential.
”
Later in
[
Bou08
]
, Bourgain revisited this problem and outlined a proof of the absence of dynamical localization for all
\alpha>0.
To the best of our knowledge, the existence of proper extended states for Schrödinger operators on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(
d>1
) with decaying random potential
\kappa|n|^{-\alpha}{\omega}_{n}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{2}|n|^{-2\alpha})
satisfying
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{3}
has remained completely open until the present paper.

In this paper, we aim to generalize the work of Bourgain
[
Bou03
]
to the case of
\frac{1}{4}<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{3}
via a further 6th-order renormalization scheme. In this procedure, the presence of a new type of non-random operator of the 6th order poses key challenge: This operator cannot be written as a symmetrical combination of some diagonal and convolution operators, so the essential perturbation lemma (cf. Lemma 1.2) in
[
Bou03
]
does not apply. To overcome this difficulty, we perform two arrangements on the resolvent expansion and move this operator to the 8th-order remaining terms. This leads to the restriction of
6\alpha+1>2
(or
\alpha>\frac{1}{6}
) in dealing with the 8th-order remaining terms, making it difficult to improve the bound
\alpha>\frac{1}{4}
to
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{6}
via the present approach.
Even in the estimates of the symmetrical type non-random operators of 6th order, we introduce the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality to perform the convolution regularization.
To establish moment estimates on both Green’s function and extended states, we also prove a generalized Khintchine inequality via Bonami’s lemma, which may be of independent interest. Finally, we want to mention that the present work is also motivated by another famous open problem of Simon (cf. Problem 8,
[
Sim00
]
): The presence of absolutely continuous spectrum of Schrödinger operators
-\Delta+V(x)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
provided
d\geq 2
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{V^{2}(x)}{(|x|+1)^{d-1}}{\rm d}x<\infty.

In the present context of
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
V_{\omega}(x)\sim|x|^{-\alpha}
, the above condition is just
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
. For more results on the study of Schrödinger operators with decaying potentials, we refer to the excellent review
[
DK07
]
.

1.1.
Main results

We first introduce the notation.

•

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, let

x\wedge y=\min\{x,y\},\ x\vee y=\max\{x,y\}.

•

Throughout this paper, we denote

|n|=(\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|n_{i}|)\vee 1\ {\rm for}\ n=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

so
|0|=1.
We also define for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

|\xi|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\xi_{i}|,\ \|\xi\|=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\xi_{i}|^{2}}.

•

For two nonnegative quantities
f
and
g
, we write
f\lesssim g
, if there is an absolute constant
D>0

such that
f\leq Dg
. If we want to emphasize that
D
depends on some parameters
x,y,\cdots
independent of
f,g
, then we write
f\lesssim_{x,y,\cdots}g
.

Our main model takes

(1.1)

\displaystyle H_{\omega}=-\Delta+V_{\omega}^{(6)}(n)\delta_{n,n^{\prime}},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

where the discrete Laplacian is defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Delta(n,n^{\prime})=\delta_{|n-n^{\prime}|_{1},1}-2d.

For the potential, we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle V_{\omega}^{(6)}(n)=v_{n}\omega_{n}+V^{\prime}(n),\ v=\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\ {\rm with}\ v_{n}=\kappa|n|^{-\alpha},

where
\kappa\geq 0
and
\omega=\{\omega_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is a sequence of i.i.d. Bernoulli random variables. The deterministic potential
V^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}(v^{2})
arising from the 6th-order renormalization scheme is defined explicitly by (
4.2
) (it depends only on
v
and
G_{0}=(-\Delta)^{-1}
).

Our first main result concerns the estimates of the Green’s function.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
H_{\omega}
be defined by (
1.1
) with fixed
d\geq 5
and
\frac{1}{4}<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{3}.
Let
0<\varepsilon<\frac{4\alpha-1}{50}
. Then for any
p>\frac{2d+2}{\varepsilon}
, there is some
\kappa_{0}=\kappa_{0}(d,\alpha,p)
so that the following holds true: If
0<\kappa\leq\kappa_{0},
then there exists some
\Omega\subset\{\pm 1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
with
\mathbb{P}(\{\pm 1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\setminus\Omega)\lesssim_{d,\alpha}\kappa^{p}
so that for
\omega\in\Omega,
we have (denote

G=G_{\omega}=H_{\omega}^{-1}
)

(1.4)

\displaystyle|G(n,n^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{d-2-\varepsilon}}\ {\rm for}\ \forall n,n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Remark 1.1
.

•

The bound
d\geq 5
primarily stems from the restriction
\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|G_{0}(n,n^{\prime})|^{2}<\infty
(where
G_{0}=(-\Delta)^{-1}
) when applying the Khintchine inequality. It is noteworthy that such a bound is sufficient for the 6th-order renormalization scheme.

•

The case of
\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
has been addressed by Bourgain
[
Bou03
]
, and it was conjectured there that the result should hold for all
\alpha>0
. It is possible that the present method could be extended for
\frac{1}{6}<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{4}
through further 10th-order renormalization. However, as we will see later (cf. e.g., Remark
6.1
), due to the presence of the aforementioned new type of non-random operator in the 6th-order renormalization, it appears challenging to extend the current result to the case of
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{6}
.

•

The probability bound of
\kappa^{p}
can be improved to
e^{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}}
by employing the Chernoff bound in the probability tail estimate. This is because Bonami’s lemma (cf. Lemma
3.1
) allows for an effective estimate of

\mathbb{E}_{p}(|f|)\leq(p-1)^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{2}(|f|),

where
f
is the Boolean polynomial of degree
s
.

Based on the above result, we also have

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, there exists a set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
with

\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})=1-\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{p}{2}})
so that, for each
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
, there is some
\zeta=\zeta_{\omega}=\{\zeta_{\omega}(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
satisfying

(1.5)

\displaystyle H_{\omega}\zeta=0,\ \zeta=\hat{\delta}_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\kappa})\ {\rm in}\ \ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),

where
\hat{\delta}_{0}=\{\delta_{0}(n)\equiv 1\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
.

Remark 1.2
.

The Green’s function estimates in Theorem
1.1
are insufficient for the construction of extended states. Indeed, it requires the second rearrangement of the resolvent expansion, and additional random variables need to be removed to prove the existence of proper extended states. In this step, the generalized Khintchine inequality is again heavily employed to derive the probabilistic estimates.

1.2.
Ideas of the proof

Definitely, the main scheme of our proof is adapted from
[
Bou03
]
. As mentioned by Bourgain
[
Bou03
]
, his approach is also motivated by the one initiated by Spencer
[
Spe93
]
(cf.
[
Elg09
]
for a related result), but is technically different: it replaces the Feynman diagram machinery with the random decoupling estimate.

For simplicity, we only outline the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and the proof of Theorem
1.2
remains similar. Note first that (cf. e.g.,
[
MS22
]
) for
G_{0}=(-\Delta)^{-1},
we have

(1.6)

\displaystyle|G_{0}(n,n^{\prime})|\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{d-2}}\ {\rm for}\ \forall n,n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Denote
\widetilde{V}=V_{\omega}^{(6)}
with
V^{(6)}
given by (
1.1
). From the resolvent identity, one can write down a 8th-order (in
v
) Born series expansion for
G=H^{-1}

\displaystyle G=

\displaystyle G_{0}-G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}+G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}-G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}+G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}

\displaystyle-G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}+G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}

\displaystyle-G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}+G\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0}\widetilde{V}G_{0},

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle A+GB,

where
A
contains the
i
th-order remaining terms for
i\leq 7
, and
B
contains the
8
th-order ones. From (
1.6
), it follows that
G_{0}
may be unbounded on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. Thus, it is more appropriate to control
G(n,n^{\prime})
for every
n,n^{\prime}
. This then leads to the study of multiple infinite summations, such as,

G_{0}VG_{0}VG_{0}(n,n^{\prime})=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}G_{0}(n,n_{1})\omega_{n_{1}}v_{n_{1}}G_{0}(n_{1},n_{2})\omega_{n_{2}}v_{n_{2}}G_{0}(n_{2},n^{\prime}).

Again by (
1.6
), we observe that it is challenging to obtain a good estimate on the summation satisfying
n_{1}=n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, since in this case the randomness cancels (i.e.,
\omega_{n_{1}}\omega_{n_{2}}\equiv 1
). However, for the summation with
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
, we can use the generalized Khintchine inequality (cf. Lemma
3.2
) to get for any
p\geq 2,

\displaystyle(\mathbb{E}|G_{0}VG_{0}VG_{0}(n,n^{\prime})|^{p})^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|G_{0}(n,n_{1})|^{2}v_{n_{1}}^{2}|G_{0}(n_{1},n_{2})|^{2}v_{n_{2}}^{2}|G_{0}(n_{2},n^{\prime})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{2(d-2)}|n_{1}|^{2\alpha}|n_{1}-n_{2}|^{2(d-2)}|n_{2}|^{2\alpha}|n-n_{1}|^{2(d-2)}}.

From the assumption of
d\geq 5
, we know
2(d-2)>d
and the above summation can be well controlled. We expect that the estimates on other terms in
A
and
B
should be similar, but require much more efforts.

Indeed, motivated by the above argument, we can distinguish terms in
A
(and
B
) into two classes: random terms and non-random ones. As we will see later, the generalized Khintchine inequality only works for
admissible
random summations (cf. Lemma
3.2
), but not all random ones. This would prevent us from controlling those
non-admissible random terms
. However, it is remarkable that in the renormalization scheme (at least for the
6
th-order one), non-admissible random terms
automatically offset
with each other and do not appear at all.

For the non-random terms, one can renormalize the potential to eliminate terms of the form
G_{0}v^{2}G_{0},G_{0}v^{4}G_{0},G_{0}v^{6}G_{0}
, since those terms cannot be well controlled. In fact, there are also non-random terms that cannot be eliminated but with a symmetrical form, such as
G_{0}WG_{0}
, where
W=v^{2}Mv^{2}
and
M
is a convolution operator mainly coming from
\hat{G}_{0}*\hat{G}_{0}*\hat{G}_{0}
. Such term is of 4th order. While
G_{0}WG_{0}
cannot be well controlled directly, one can use the symmetrical difference trick and convolution regularization argument to decompose
G_{0}WG_{0}
into several operators, each of which has the desired estimates as in
[
Bou03
]
.

We want to emphasize that, however, in the present 6th-order renormalization scheme, a new type of non-random operator
G_{0}CG_{0}
(cf. (
4.19
)) appears. By developing a more complicated symmetrical difference trick (cf. the proof of Theorem
5.3
), we can obtain
C=(C-P_{6}^{\prime\prime})+P_{6}^{\prime\prime}
with
G_{0}(C-P_{6}^{\prime\prime})G_{0}
having a good control. The singular operator
P_{6}^{\prime\prime}
, given by

P_{6}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(n_{1},n_{3})=\widetilde{G_{0}}(n_{1},n_{3})\sum_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(v_{n_{2}}^{6}-v_{n_{1}}^{6})\widetilde{G_{0}}(n_{1},n_{2})^{2}\widetilde{G_{0}}(n_{2},n_{3})^{2},

cannot be well handled: it only has the estimate

|P_{6}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(n_{1},n_{3})|\lesssim_{d,\alpha}\kappa^{6}\frac{1}{|n_{1}-n_{3}|^{3(d-2)-1}(|n_{3}|\wedge|n_{1}|)^{6\alpha+1}},

rather than a
6\alpha+2
decay rate as required by the renormalization scheme.
Clearly, the operator
P_{6}^{\prime\prime}
cannot be written as a symmetrical combination of diagonal and convolutional operators as that of
G_{0}WG_{0}
or
G_{0}(C-P_{6}^{\prime\prime})G_{0}
. So we have to do the arrangement on the Born series and move
P_{6}^{\prime\prime}G_{0}
to
B^{\prime}
so that

G=A^{\prime}+GB^{\prime},

and thus

\displaystyle|G_{0}P_{6}^{\prime\prime}(n,n^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\alpha}\kappa^{6}\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{d-2}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{6\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa^{6}\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{d-2}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{8\alpha}}\ ({\rm since}\ \alpha\leq\frac{1}{3}),

which suffices for the moment estimates.

Finally, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p}|(A^{\prime}-G_{0})(n,n^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,p,\alpha}\kappa\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{d-2}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p}|B^{\prime}(n,n^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,p,\alpha}\kappa^{6}\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{d-2}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{8\alpha}}.

And we can use the Chebyshev’s inequality to get good estimates on
A^{\prime}(n,n^{\prime}),B(n,n^{\prime})
with high probability.
To get desired estimates on
G
, it requires the existence of
(I-B^{\prime})^{-1}
, which leads to the condition
8\alpha>2
, namely,
\alpha>\frac{1}{4}
.

Thus, the main novelties of our proof are as follows:

•

We introduce graph representations to compute the remaining terms in the 6th-order renormalization scheme. We also identify some iteration relations between remaining terms of different orders. These arguments allow us to easily detect the remarkable offsets between non-admissible random terms and perform more flexible rearrangements of the remaining terms in the Born series expansion. For details, refer to Appendices
A
and
B
.

•

As mentioned above, a new type of non-random operator emerges in the 6th-order renormalization scheme, posing a key challenge.
While we rearrange the terms so that the singular operator
P_{6}^{\prime\prime}
can be moved to
B^{\prime}
, controlling the non-singular operator
G_{0}(C-P_{6}^{\prime\prime})G_{0}
is also non-trivial. Indeed, we propose a new symmetrical difference argument (cf. the proof of Theorem
5.3
), which turns out to be more complicated than that in
[
Bou03
]
. For the convolution regularization argument, we also need to handle some convolution operator (given by
f^{2}
with
f
defined by (
5.19
)), which is not entirely a convolution product of
\hat{G}_{0}
. For this, we use the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (cf.
[
BM18
]
) to obtain fine estimates on higher-order derivatives of
f^{2}
.

•

We prove a generalized Khintchine inequality (cf. Lemma
3.2
) based on hypercontractivity estimate (e.g., Bonami’s lemma). Previously, Bourgain
[
Bou03
]

employed random decoupling to establish the
L^{2}\to L^{2}
estimate. Our new contribution here is a proof of the
L^{p}\to L^{2}
estimate for any
p\geq 1
, which may be of independent interest.
Since we can get directly high-order moment estimates on Green’s functions, the probability estimate in the proof of Theorem
1.1
becomes more straightforward, and the application of Chebyshev’s inequality suffices for this purpose.

2.
Preliminaries: some useful estimates

In this section, we will introduce some useful estimates concerning products of both
G_{0}=(-\Delta)^{-1}
and
G_{0}vG_{0}
.

Recall the discrete Laplacian

\Delta(n,n^{\prime})=\delta_{|n-n^{\prime}|_{1},1}-2d

and its Fourier transform

(2.1)

-\hat{\Delta}(\xi)=2d-2\sum_{j=1}^{d}\cos 2\pi\xi_{j}=c\|\xi\|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\|\xi\|^{4}),

where
c>0
is some absolute constant. Denote by

G_{0}(n,n^{\prime})=(-\Delta)^{-1}(n,n^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{e^{-2\pi i(n-n^{\prime})\cdot\xi}}{-\hat{\Delta}(\xi)}\mathrm{d}\xi,\ \mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}

the resolvent (or the Green’s function) of
-\Delta
.
A standard estimate on
G_{0}
(cf. e.g.,
[
MS22
]
) is

(2.2)

|G_{0}(n,n^{\prime})|\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{d-2}}.

In this paper, we have to control operators involving products of
G_{0}
. Therefore, it is useful to introduce some estimates on summations of power-law decay sequences. Recall that
G_{0}
is an
unbounded
operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

The first important lemma reads as

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
a,b>0
satisfying
a+b>d
and
\max\{a,b\}\neq d
, we have

\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}|m-n_{1}|^{b}}\lesssim_{a,b,d}\frac{1}{|m|^{\min\{a,b,a+b-d\}}}.

Remark 2.1
.

(1)

As we will see from the proof of Lemma
2.1
, if

3.
A generalized Khintchine inequality

This section is devoted to proving a generalized Khintchine inequality via the
hypercontractivity
estimate (cf. e.g.,
[
Jan97
,
SS12
,
O’D14
]
), which plays an essential role in our estimates on the Green’s function. In contrast, in
[
Bou03
]
, Bourgain proposed an analogous inequality based on the standard
L^{2}
-random
decoupling
.
Our proof builds on Bonami’s lemma, which primarily focuses on Boolean functions estimates.

We first introduce Bonami’s lemma
[
Bon70
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(cf.
[
Bon70
,
O’D14
]
)
.

Let
m,s\in\mathbb{N}
and let
f(Y_{1},Y_{2},\cdots,Y_{m})
be a real-valued polynomial in i.i.d. Bernoulli random variables
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m}\in\{\pm 1\}
with the degree
\deg(f)=s
. Then

\mathbb{E}(f^{4})\leq 9^{s}(\mathbb{E}f^{2})^{2}.

Proof.

For completeness, we give a proof here. Define

(D_{m}f)(Y_{1},Y_{2},\cdots,Y_{m-1})=\frac{1}{2}(f(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m-1},1)-f(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m-1},-1)),

(E_{m}f)(Y_{1},Y_{2},\cdots,Y_{m-1})=\frac{1}{2}(f(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m-1},1)+f(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m-1},-1)).

Since
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m}
are i.i.d. Bernoulli random variables, we know that
Y_{m}
is independent of
D_{m}f,E_{m}f
, and

(3.1)

f(Y_{1},Y_{2},\cdots,Y_{m})=Y_{m}\cdot(D_{m}f)+(E_{m}f).

The proof is based on an induction on
m
. Indeed, when
m=0
, the polynomial
f
is a constant and Lemma
3.1
holds trivially. Now, assume that Lemma
3.1
holds for polynomials with
m-1
variables. By using the decomposition (
3.1
) and the independence property, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(f^{4})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(Y_{m}\cdot D_{m}f+E_{m}f)^{4}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(Y_{m}^{4})\mathbb{E}(D_{m}f)^{4}+4\mathbb{E}(Y_{m}^{3})\mathbb{E}((D_{m}f)^{3}\cdot(E_{m}f))+6\mathbb{E}(Y_{m}^{2})\mathbb{E}((D_{m}f)^{2}\cdot(E_{m}f)^{2})

\displaystyle\ \ \ +4\mathbb{E}(Y_{m})\mathbb{E}((D_{m}f)\cdot(E_{m}f)^{3})+\mathbb{E}(E_{m}f)^{4}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(D_{m}f)^{4}+6\mathbb{E}((D_{m}f)^{2}\cdot(E_{m}f)^{2})+\mathbb{E}(E_{m}f)^{4}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(f^{2})=\mathbb{E}(D_{m}f)^{2}+\mathbb{E}(E_{m}f)^{2}.

Since
f
is a polynomial of degree
s
,
D_{m}f
is a polynomial of degree
s-1
and
E_{m}f
is a polynomial of degree
s
. By the induction assumption, we get

(3.2)

\mathbb{E}(D_{m}f)^{4}\leq 9^{s-1}(\mathbb{E}(D_{m}f)^{2})^{2},

(3.3)

\mathbb{E}(E_{m}f)^{4}\leq 9^{s}(\mathbb{E}(E_{m}f)^{2})^{2}.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies

(3.4)

\mathbb{E}((D_{m}f)^{2}\cdot(E_{m}f)^{2})\leq(\mathbb{E}(D_{m}f)^{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(\mathbb{E}(E_{m}f)^{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{3}\cdot 9^{s}\mathbb{E}(D_{m}f)^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}(E_{m}f)^{2}.

Combining (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) shows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(f^{4})

\displaystyle\leq 9^{s-1}(\mathbb{E}(D_{m}f)^{2})^{2}+2\cdot 9^{s}\mathbb{E}(D_{m}f)^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}(E_{m}f)^{2}+9^{s}(\mathbb{E}(E_{m}f)^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 9^{s}(\mathbb{E}f^{2})^{2}.

This finishes the induction step (i.e.,
m-1\to m
), and hence the proof.
∎

As a corollary of Lemma
3.1
, we have

Corollary 3.1
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
, we have for all
p\geq 1,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p}|f|:=(\mathbb{E}|f|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim_{p,s}\mathbb{E}_{2}|f|.

More generally, if
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is a sequences of i.i.d. random Bernoulli variables and

f=\sum_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}a_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}Y_{n_{1}}\cdots Y_{n_{s}}\ {\rm with}\ a_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}\geq 0,

then the estimate (
3.5
) remains true for this
f
.

Proof.

If
1\leq p\leq 2
, then we get by Hölder inequality that

\mathbb{E}(|f|^{p})\leq(\mathbb{E}|f|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\cdot(\mathbb{E}1)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}=(\mathbb{E}|f|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}},

which implies (
3.5
) in this case.

If
p>2
, we first consider the cases of
p=2^{k},k=2,3,\cdots
. Note that
f^{2}
is a polynomial of degree at most
2s
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(f^{8})=\mathbb{E}((f^{2})^{4})

\displaystyle\leq 9^{2s}(\mathbb{E}f^{4})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 9^{2s}(9^{s}(\mathbb{E}f^{2})^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}(\mathbb{E}f^{2})^{4}.

Repeatedly applying Lemma
3.1
yields

(\mathbb{E}f^{2^{k}})^{\frac{1}{2^{k}}}\lesssim_{k,s}(\mathbb{E}f^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, using the standard interpolation inequality gives (by
p\in[2^{k},2^{k+1}]
if
p>2
)

(\mathbb{E}|f|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim_{p,s}(\mathbb{E}f^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},\ p>2.

This proves (
3.5
) if
p>2
.

Now, we consider the
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
case. Denote for
N\geq 1,

f_{N}=\sum_{|n_{1}|\leq N,\cdots,|n_{s}|\leq N}a_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}Y_{n_{1}}\cdots Y_{n_{s}}.

Then applying (
3.5
) to
f_{N}
gives

\mathbb{E}_{p}|f_{N}|\lesssim_{p,s}\mathbb{E}_{2}|f_{N}|\leq\mathbb{E}_{2}|f|,

where for the last inequality, we used the fact that the coefficient
a_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}\geq 0
and

\mathbb{E}(Y_{n_{1}}^{d_{1}}Y_{n_{2}}^{d_{2}}\cdots Y_{n_{k}}^{d_{k}})=0\ {\rm or}\ 1\ {\rm for}\ d_{1},\cdots,d_{k}\in\mathbb{N}.

So from Fatou’s lemma, it follows that
\mathbb{E}_{p}|f|\lesssim_{p,s}\mathbb{E}_{2}|f|,p\geq 1
.
∎

Next, recall that
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is the i.i.d. random Bernoulli variables. For a
s
-tuple
(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,

n_{s})
, we say that its randomness “
cancels
” if

\mathbb{P}\big(\prod_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{n_{i}}=1\big)=1.

It’s easy to see that the randomness of
(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{s})
cancels if and only if each
n_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq s
) is repeated an
even number
of times in the
s
-tuple. We say that
(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{s})
is “
admissible
” if for any
1\leq s_{1}<s_{2}\leq s
, the randomness of sub-tuple
(n_{s_{1}},n_{s_{1}+1},\cdots,n_{s_{2}})
does not cancel. We use the notation
\sum\limits^{(*)}\limits_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}
to indicate a summation restricted to admissible
s
-tuples.
We then introduce the generalized Khintchine inequality, which is a refined version of Lemma 2.2 of Bourgain
[
Bou03
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random Bernoulli variables. For
s\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p}\left|\sum_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}^{(*)}\omega_{n_{1}}\cdots\omega_{n_{s}}a^{(0)}_{n,n_{1}}a^{(1)}_{n_{1},n_{2}}\cdots a^{(s)}_{n_{s},n^{\prime}}\right|\lesssim_{p,s}\left[\sum_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}|a^{(0)}_{n,n_{1}}a^{(1)}_{n_{1},n_{2}}\cdots a^{(s)}_{n_{s},n^{\prime}}|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

where all
a_{m,n}^{(j)}\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 3.1
.

If
s=1
, Lemma
3.2
is just the classical Khintchine inequality.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
a_{m,n}^{(j)}\geq 0
. Then by Corollary
3.1
, it suffices to prove (
3.6
) for
p=2
, which will be completed by induction on
s
below.

If
s=1
, then by the orthogonality of
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
in
L^{2}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{2}\left|\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\omega_{n_{1}}a^{(0)}_{n,n_{1}}a^{(1)}_{n_{1},n^{\prime}}\right|=\left[\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|a^{(0)}_{n,n_{1}}a^{(1)}_{n_{1},n^{\prime}}|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, assume (
3.6
) holds with
s
replaced by
s^{\prime}\leq s-1
and
p=2
. Since in the summation “
\sum^{(*)}
” no tuple
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{s})
’s randomness cancels, there are some distinct
m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{k}
(as a sub-tuple of
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{s})
), each of which is repeated an odd number of times. Specify all possible positions of those sits as disjoint
I_{1},I_{2},\cdots,I_{k}
. That is to say, for
i=1,\cdots,k,

I_{i}=\{k:\ n_{k}=m_{i}\}\subset\{1,2,\cdots,s\}.

After this specifying of
I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}
, the
s
-tuple
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{s})
has the form of

(\nu^{(1)},m_{i_{1}},\cdots,\nu^{(2)},m_{i_{2}},\cdots),

where
\nu^{(1)},\nu^{(2)},\cdots,\nu^{(l)}
are admissible sub-tuples with indexes determined by
i_{j}\in I_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq k
). By the Minkowski inequality, we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{2}\left|\sum_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}^{(*)}\omega_{n_{1}}\cdots\omega_{n_{s}}a^{(0)}_{n,n_{1}}a^{(1)}_{n_{1},n_{2}}\cdots a^{(s)}_{n_{s},n^{\prime}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{2}\left|\sum_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}}\omega_{m_{1}}\cdots\omega_{m_{k}}\underbrace{\left(\sum_{\nu^{(1)}=(n_{s_{0}},\cdots,n_{s_{1}})}^{(*)}\omega_{n_{s_{0}}}\cdots a_{m_{i_{1}},n_{s_{0}}}^{(s_{0}-1)}\cdots\right)\left(\sum_{\nu^{(2)}}^{(*)}\cdots\right)\cdots}_{A_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}}}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{2}\left|\sum_{\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\}}\omega_{m_{1}}\cdots\omega_{m_{k}}(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}A_{m_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,m_{\sigma(k)}})\right|,

where
S_{k},k\leq s
denotes the
k
-order permutation group, and
A_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}}
has indeed no randomness while each
\nu^{(i)}
is admissible. Note that we have the orthogonality relation

\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\}\neq\{m_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,m_{k}^{\prime}\}\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}[(\omega_{m_{1}}\cdots\omega_{m_{k}})\cdot(\omega_{m_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots\omega_{m_{k}^{\prime}})]=0,

\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\}=\{m_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,m_{k}^{\prime}\}\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}[(\omega_{m_{1}}\cdots\omega_{m_{k}})\cdot(\omega_{m_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots\omega_{m_{k}^{\prime}})]=1.

Hence,

(
3.7
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}}\left[\sum_{\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\}}(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}A_{m_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,m_{\sigma(k)}})^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}}\left[(\#S_{k})\cdot\sum_{\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}(A_{m_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,m_{\sigma(k)}})^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\#S_{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\sum_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}}\left[\sum_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}}(A_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}})^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq(\#S_{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\sum_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}}\left[\sum_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}}\mathbb{E}\bigg|\big[\sum_{\nu^{(1)}}^{(*)}\cdots\big]\cdots\big[\sum_{\nu^{(l)}}^{(*)}\cdots\big]\bigg|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

where for the second inequality, we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and for the third inequality, we use
k\leq s
. We continue to control (
3.8
) by using Hölder’s inequality and Corollary
3.1
, and get (since
l\leq s
)

(
3.8
)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\sum_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}}\left[\sum_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{2l}|\sum_{\nu^{(1)}}^{(*)}\cdots|\bigg)^{2}\cdots\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{2l}|\sum_{\nu^{(l)}}^{(*)}\cdots|\bigg)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\sum_{I_{1},I_{2},\cdots,I_{k}}\left[\sum_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{2}|\sum_{\nu^{(1)}}^{(*)}\cdots|\bigg)^{2}\cdots\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{2}|\sum_{\nu^{(l)}}^{(*)}\cdots|\bigg)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally, by the induction assumptions, we have

\left(\mathbb{E}_{2}|\sum_{\nu^{(i)}}^{(*)}\cdots|\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{\nu^{(i)}}|a^{(\cdots)}_{m_{\cdot},n_{\cdot}}\cdots|^{2}

and thus,

(
3.9
)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\sum_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}}\left[\sum_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}}\sum_{\nu^{(1)}}\cdots\sum_{\nu^{(l)}}|a^{(0)}_{n,n_{1}}a^{(1)}_{n_{1},n_{2}}\cdots a^{(s)}_{n_{s},n^{\prime}}|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\left[\sum_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}|a^{(0)}_{n,n_{1}}a^{(1)}_{n_{1},n_{2}}\cdots a^{(s)}_{n_{s},n^{\prime}}|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

where for the third inequality, we enlarge the summation by recalling
a^{(j)}_{m,n}\geq 0
.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

Finally, we emphasize the presence of some new phenomena and obstacles compared with
[
Bou03
]
when expanding the resolvent to higher order terms. If one wishes to relax the condition
\alpha>\frac{1}{4}
to
\alpha>0
, these issues should be addressed.

(1)

The presence of 7th-order remaining terms (
4.31
) and (
4.34
) indicates that in higher-order expansions, not all terms can be expressed as summations over admissible tuples. Thus, one should improve Lemma
3.2
further in order to handle these random but not admissible terms.

(2)

From (
6.19
), it requires

(i.e.,
\alpha>\frac{1}{6}
) to ensure convergence. This may prevent us from relaxing the condition
\alpha>\frac{1}{4}
to
\alpha>0
. Although the key lemma of
[
Bou03
]
(cf. Lemma 1.2]) might seem applicable, the operator
C
in the 6th-order remaining terms does not match the form

as required in that lemma. Therefore, Lemma 1.2 in
[
Bou03
]
cannot be directly applied. Moreover, our proof of Theorem
5.3
shows that a non-symmetrical difference operator

always exists, yielding only a power-law decay rate of

instead of the

one as in the symmetrical differences case.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Without loss of generality, we assume
a\leq b
, so
b\neq d
. The summation can be decomposed as

(C.1)

\displaystyle(\sum_{n_{1}=0}+

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}=m}+\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop|n_{1}|>2|m|}+\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop\frac{1}{2}|m|<|n_{1}|\leq 2|m|}+\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|m|})\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}|m-n_{1}|^{b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|m|^{a}}+\frac{1}{|m|^{b}}+(\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop|n_{1}|>2|m|}+\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop\frac{1}{2}|m|<|n_{1}|\leq 2|m|}+\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|m|})\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}|m-n_{1}|^{b}}.

First, note that if
|n_{1}|>2|m|
, then
|m-n_{1}|>\frac{1}{2}|n_{1}|
and we have

(C.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop|n_{1}|>2|m|}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}|m-n_{1}|^{b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a}\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\atop|n_{1}|>2|m|}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a+b}}\lesssim_{a,d}\sum_{L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\atop L>2|m|}\frac{1}{L^{a+b+1-d}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,d}\frac{1}{|m|^{a+b-d}},

where the last inequality needs
a+b>d
. Similarly, from
\frac{1}{2}|m|<|n_{1}|\leq 2|m|\Rightarrow|n_{1}-m|\leq 3|m|
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop\frac{1}{2}|m|<|n_{1}|\leq 2|m|}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}|m-n_{1}|^{b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a}|m|^{-a}\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\atop|n_{1}-m|\leq 3|m|}\frac{1}{|n_{1}-m|^{b}}\lesssim_{a,d}|m|^{-a}\sum_{L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\atop L\leq 3|m|}\frac{1}{L^{b+1-d}}.

Thus,

•

if
b<d
, we have

\sum_{L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\atop L\leq 3|m|}\frac{1}{L^{b+1-d}}\lesssim_{b,d}|m|^{d-b}.

•

if
b=d
, we have

\sum_{L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\atop L\leq 3|m|}\frac{1}{L}\lesssim\log|m|.

•

if
b>d
, we have

\sum_{L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\atop L\leq 3|m|}\frac{1}{L^{b+1-d}}\leq\sum_{L\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{1}{L^{b+1-d}}\lesssim_{b,d}1.

Since
b\neq d
and by taking account of estimates in the above cases, we get

(C.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop\frac{1}{2}|m|<|n_{1}|\leq 2|m|}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}|m-n_{1}|^{b}}\lesssim_{a,d,b}\frac{1}{|m|^{\min\{a,a+b-d\}}}.

Next, note that
|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|m|\Rightarrow|m-n_{1}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|m|
and hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|m|}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}|m-n_{1}|^{b}}\lesssim_{b}|m|^{-b}\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\atop|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|m|}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}}.

If
a=b
, using same argument as above shows

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|m|}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}|m-n_{1}|^{b}}\lesssim_{a,d,b}\frac{1}{|m|^{\min\{b,a+b-d\}}}.

If
a<b
, the only difference is the case of
a=d
, where we still have

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|m|}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}|m-n_{1}|^{b}}\lesssim_{a,d,b}\frac{\log|m|}{|m|^{b}}\lesssim_{a,d,b}\frac{1}{|m|^{b-}}\lesssim_{a,d,b}\frac{1}{|m|^{a}}.

Hence, we have for
a\leq b,

(C.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,m\atop|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|m|}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{a}|m-n_{1}|^{b}}\lesssim_{a,d,b}\frac{1}{|m|^{\min\{a,b,a+b-d\}}}.

Finally, combining all estimates (
C.1
)–(
C.4
) together concludes the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Consider first the special cases of
n_{1}=n
or
n_{1}=n^{\prime}
or
n_{1}=0
in the summation. Indeed, we have

•

if
n_{1}=n
, then by
a\leq b,

\frac{1}{|n|^{\varepsilon}|n-n^{\prime}|^{b}}\leq\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{a}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{\varepsilon}}.

•

if
n_{1}=n^{\prime}
, then

\frac{1}{|n^{\prime}|^{\varepsilon}|n-n^{\prime}|^{a}}\leq\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{a}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{\varepsilon}}.

•

if
n_{1}=0
, by
a\leq b
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|n|^{a}|n^{\prime}|^{b}}\leq\frac{1}{(|n|\cdot|n^{\prime}|)^{a}}=\frac{1}{(|n|\vee|n^{\prime}|)^{a}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{a}}.

In this case, since
|n|+|n^{\prime}|\geq|n-n^{\prime}|
, we have
|n|\vee|n^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n-n^{\prime}|
. This implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|n|^{a}|n^{\prime}|^{b}}\lesssim_{a}\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{a}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{a}}.

Next, we aways assume
n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}
. From
|n-n_{1}|+|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|\geq|n-n^{\prime}|
, it follows that either
|n_{1}-n|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n-n^{\prime}|
or
|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n-n^{\prime}|
. Then we can decompose the summation as

(C.5)

\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}}=\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}\atop|n_{1}-n|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n-n^{\prime}|}+\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}\atop|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n-n^{\prime}|}.

Applying Lemma
2.1
with
b+\varepsilon>d,b\neq d
yields

(C.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}\atop|n_{1}-n|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n-n^{\prime}|}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{a}|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a}|n-n^{\prime}|^{-a}\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,\varepsilon,d}|n-n^{\prime}|^{-a}|n^{\prime}|^{-\min\{\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon-d\}}.

So, it remains to deal with the summation satisfying
|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n-n^{\prime}|.
In fact, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}\atop|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n-n^{\prime}|}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{a}|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b}}

(C.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a}|n-n^{\prime}|^{-a}\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{a}|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b-a}}.

We divide the discussion into the following two cases.

Case 1
:

b>d
. In this case, we decompose

(C.8)

\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{a}|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b-a}}=(\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}\atop|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)}+\sum_{n\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}\atop|n_{1}|>\frac{1}{2}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)})\cdots.

On one hand, by Lemma
2.1
,
b>d
and Remark
2.1
(1), we have

(C.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}\atop|n_{1}|>\frac{1}{2}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{a}|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b-a}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{-\varepsilon}\sum_{n_{1}\neq n,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{a}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b-a}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,\varepsilon,d}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{-\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|n-n^{\prime}|^{\min\{a,b-a,b-d\}-}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,\varepsilon,d}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{-\varepsilon}.

On the other hand, from
|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)
, it follows that
|n-n_{1}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n|
and
|n^{\prime}-n_{1}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n^{\prime}|
. As a result, we obtain

(C.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}\atop|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{a}|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b-a}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b}|n|^{-a}|n^{\prime}|^{-(b-a)}\sum_{0<|n_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,\varepsilon,d}\frac{1}{(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{\varepsilon+b-d}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,\varepsilon,d}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{-\varepsilon},

where the second inequality above depends on
\varepsilon<d
. Finally, combining (
C.8
)–(
C.10
) together leads to

(C.11)

\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{a}|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b-a}}\lesssim_{a,b,\varepsilon,d}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{-\varepsilon}\ \ {\rm if}\ \ b>d.

Case 2
:

a\leq b<d
. In this case, if
a=b
, then the estimate is totally the same as that of (
C.7
). Thus, we only need to consider the case of
a<b
. The main difficulty here is that we do not necessarily have
a+\varepsilon>d
, so we cannot apply Lemma
2.1
directly. Fortunately, since
a+\varepsilon+(b-a)>d,\varepsilon<d
and
b-a<b<d
, we can find
p,q>0
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and

d-\varepsilon<pa<d,\ d-\varepsilon<q(b-a)<d.

In fact,
p=\frac{b}{a}
suffices for the purpose. Then applying Hölder inequality and Lemma
2.1
shows

(C.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{a}|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b-a}}

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{pa}|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}})^{\frac{1}{p}}(\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{q(b-a)}})^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,\varepsilon,d}|n|^{-\frac{pa+\varepsilon-d}{p}}|n^{\prime}|^{-\frac{\varepsilon+q(b-a)-d}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,b,\varepsilon,d}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{-(b+\varepsilon-d)}\ \ {\rm if}\ \ b<d.

Thus, combining estimates (
C.11
), (
C.12
) and (
C.7
) in the above two cases together implies

(C.13)

\sum_{n_{1}\neq 0,n,n^{\prime}\atop|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|n-n^{\prime}|}\frac{1}{|n-n_{1}|^{a}|n_{1}|^{\varepsilon}|n_{1}-n^{\prime}|^{b}}\lesssim_{a,b,\varepsilon,d}|n-n^{\prime}|^{-a}(|n|\wedge|n^{\prime}|)^{-\min\{\varepsilon,a,b+\varepsilon-d\}}.

Finally, the proof of Lemma
2.2
follows by combining estimates (
C.5
), (
C.6
) and (
C.13
) together.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
|n_{1}|\geq|n_{2}|
. Note that

||n_{1}|^{-\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{-\alpha}|=\frac{||n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha}|}{|n_{1}|^{\alpha}|n_{2}|^{\alpha}}.

When
\alpha\geq 1
, it’s easy to check that
(\alpha-1)t+\frac{1}{t^{\alpha-1}}\geq\alpha,t\geq 1
. That is,

t^{\alpha}-1\leq\alpha(t-1)t^{\alpha-1}.

By taking
t=\frac{|n_{1}|}{|n_{2}|}
, we get

|n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha}\leq\alpha(|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|)|n_{1}|^{\alpha-1}.

Hence,

\displaystyle||n_{1}|^{-\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{-\alpha}|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\frac{|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|}{|n_{1}||n_{2}|^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{|n_{1}-n_{2}|}{|n_{1}||n_{2}|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|n_{1}-n_{2}|}{\frac{|n_{1}|+|n_{2}|}{2}|n_{2}|^{\alpha}}\lesssim_{\alpha}\frac{|n_{1}-n_{2}|}{(|n_{1}|+|n_{2}|)\cdot(|n_{1}|\wedge|n_{2}|)^{\alpha}}.

When
0<\alpha<1
, one can check that
t^{\alpha}-1\leq 10\frac{t-1}{(t+1)^{1-\alpha}},t\geq 1
. We again take
t=\frac{|n_{1}|}{|n_{2}|}
and get

|n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha}\leq 10\frac{|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|}{(|n_{1}|+|n_{2}|)^{1-\alpha}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle||n_{1}|^{-\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{-\alpha}|

\displaystyle\leq 10\frac{|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|}{(|n_{1}|+|n_{2}|)^{1-\alpha}|n_{1}|^{\alpha}|n_{2}|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq 10\frac{|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|}{(|n_{1}|+|n_{2}|)^{1-\alpha}(\frac{|n_{1}|+|n_{2}|}{2})^{\alpha}|n_{2}|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\frac{|n_{1}-n_{2}|}{(|n_{1}|+|n_{2}|)\cdot(|n_{1}|\wedge|n_{2}|)^{\alpha}}.

∎

Proof of Lemma
5.4

We begin with the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality proven in
[
BM18
]
.

Lemma D.1

(
[
BM18
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be either the whole space, a half-space or a bounded Lipschitz domain. Let
1\leq p,\,p_{1},\,p_{2}\leq+\infty
be three positive extended real quantities and let
s,\,s_{1},\,s_{2}
be non-negative real numbers. Furthermore, let
\theta\in(0,1)
and assume that

s_{1}\leq s_{2},\ s=\theta s_{1}+(1-\theta)s_{2},\ \frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{p_{2}}

hold. Then

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|^{\theta}_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}(\Omega)}\|u\|^{1-\theta}_{W^{s_{2},p_{2}}(\Omega)}

for any
u\in W^{s_{1},p_{1}}(\Omega)\cap W^{s_{2},p_{2}}(\Omega)
if and only if at least one of

\begin{cases}s_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }s_{2}\geq 1,\\
p_{2}=1,\\
0<s_{2}-s_{1}\leq 1-\frac{1}{p_{1}},\end{cases}

is false. The constant
C>0
depends on the parameters
p,\,p_{1},\,p_{2},\,s,\,s_{1},\,s_{2},\,\theta
, on the domain
\Omega
, but not on
u
. Here
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
can be both the Bessel potential space and the Sobolev-Slobodeckij space.

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.

(1) Note first that when
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
, we have near
\xi=0,

\partial^{\beta}\hat{G_{0}}(\xi)=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\|\xi\|^{2+|\beta|_{1}}}).

Hence, for
0\leq k\leq d-3,k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have that
p(2+k)<d\Rightarrow|\!|\hat{G_{0}}|\!|_{W^{k,p}}<\infty
.

When
|\beta|_{1}\notin\mathbb{Z}_{+},\ |\beta|_{1}<d-3
, we set
k=\lfloor|\beta|_{1}\rfloor
(i.e., the smallest integer larger than
|\beta|_{1}
) and hence,

k-1<|\beta|_{1}<k,\ p(2+|\beta|_{1})<d.

By Lemma
D.1
, we have

(D.1)

\|\partial^{\beta}\hat{G_{0}}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\hat{G_{0}}\|_{W^{k-1,p_{1}}}^{\theta}\cdot\|\hat{G_{0}}\|_{W^{k,p_{2}}}^{1-\theta},

where
|\beta|_{1}=(k-1)\theta+k(1-\theta),\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{p_{2}}\Rightarrow\theta=k-|\beta|_{1}
. Thus, if we can find
p_{1},p_{2}
such that

\begin{cases}p_{1}(k-1+2)<d,\\
p_{2}(k+2)<d,\\
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{k-|\beta|_{1}}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-k+|\beta|_{1}}{p_{2}}.\end{cases}

Then by Lemma
D.1
, we can prove that
\|\partial^{\beta}\hat{G_{0}}\|_{L^{p}}<\infty
. The system can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p_{1}}=(-\frac{1-k+|\beta|_{1}}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{p})/(k-|\beta|_{1}),\\
\frac{k+1}{d}<\frac{1}{p_{1}}<(-(1-k+|\beta|_{1})\frac{k+2}{d}+\frac{1}{p})/(k-|\beta|_{1}).\end{cases}

The system has a solution if and only if

\frac{k+1}{d}<(-(1-k+|\beta|_{1})\frac{k+2}{d}+\frac{1}{p})/(k-|\beta|_{1})\Leftrightarrow p(2+|\beta|_{1})<d.

Hence, we have proven the result for
|\beta|_{1}\leq d-3
.

The harder case is
d-3<|\beta|_{1}<d-2
, since it may be
\|\hat{G_{0}}\|_{W^{d-2,1}}=+\infty
. We need to make the dyadic decomposition. Define the non-negative
\phi(\xi)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

\phi(\xi)=\begin{cases}(1+e^{\frac{1}{1-\|\xi\|}+\frac{1}{2-\|\xi\|}})^{-1},\ 1\leq\|\xi\|\leq 2,\\
1-(1+e^{\frac{1}{1-2\|\xi\|}+\frac{1}{2-2\|\xi\|}})^{-1},\ \frac{1}{2}\leq\|\xi\|\leq 1,\\
0,\ {\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j}\xi)\equiv 1
and
{\rm supp}\ \phi=[\frac{1}{2},2]
(i.e., the support of
\phi
). For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
with
|\alpha|_{1}=d-3,

\partial^{\alpha}\hat{G_{0}}(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j}\xi)\partial^{\alpha}\hat{G_{0}}(\xi)\ {\rm for}\ \forall\xi\in[0,1]^{d}.

Denote
g=\partial^{\alpha}\hat{G_{0}}
and
g^{(1)}=\partial g
. Now assume

|\beta|_{1}\in(0,1),\ (d-3+|\beta|_{1}+2)p<d.

Then

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\beta}g\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\partial^{\beta}(\phi(2^{j}\xi)g(\xi))\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\partial^{\beta}(\phi(2^{j}\xi)g(\xi))\|_{L^{p}}.

By Lemma
D.1
again, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\beta}(\phi(2^{j}\xi)g(\xi))\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\phi(2^{j}\xi)g(\xi)\|_{L^{p_{1}}}^{\theta}\cdot\|\partial(\phi(2^{j}\xi)g(\xi))\|_{L^{1}}^{1-\theta},

where we take

\begin{cases}|\beta|_{1}=0\cdot\theta+1\cdot(1-\theta)\Rightarrow\theta=1-|\beta|_{1},\\
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{1}\Rightarrow\frac{1}{p_{1}}=(\frac{1}{p}-|\beta|_{1})/(1-|\beta|_{1}).\end{cases}

Now, as
g=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\|\xi\|^{d-1}})
, we get

\displaystyle\|\phi(2^{j}\xi)g(\xi)\|_{L^{p_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int\phi^{p_{1}}(2^{j}\xi)\frac{1}{\|\xi\|^{(d-1)p_{1}}}{\rm d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\phi(\xi)\frac{1}{\|\xi\|^{d-2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\cdot[2^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}(d-(d-1)p_{1})}]^{-j}.

On the other hand, due to
g^{(1)}=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\|\xi\|^{d}})
, using the similar argument shows

\displaystyle\sup_{j}\|\partial(\phi(2^{j}\xi)g(\xi))\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j}(\|\phi(2^{j}\xi)g^{(1)}(\xi)\|_{L^{1}}+\|2^{j}(\partial\phi)(2^{j}\xi)g(\xi)\|_{L^{1}})

\displaystyle<+\infty.

Combining all above estimates gives

\|\partial^{\beta}g\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[2^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}(d-(d-1)p_{1})}]^{-j}.

To ensure the convergence of the above series, it requires

d-(d-1)p_{1}>0\Leftrightarrow(d-1+|\beta|_{1})p<d.

This proves the result for
d-3<|\beta|_{1}<d-2
.

(2) By Lemma
D.1
again, we obtain

\|\partial^{\beta}(\hat{G_{0}}-\sigma)\|_{L^{p}}<\infty\ {\rm if}\ |\beta|_{1}<d-2,\ p\geq 1,\ (2+|\beta|_{1})p<d.

Now assume
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
with
|\alpha|_{1}+|\beta|_{1}=k\leq 2(d-2)-1,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then

\|\partial^{\alpha+\beta}(f^{2})\|_{L^{1}}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|_{1}+|\beta|_{1}=k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\cdot\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{1}}.

Hence, from Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}f\cdot\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{r_{\alpha}}}\|\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{r_{\beta}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}(\hat{G_{0}}-\sigma)*\partial^{\alpha_{2}}(\hat{G_{0}}-\sigma)\|_{L^{r_{\alpha}}}\|\partial^{\beta_{1}}(\hat{G_{0}}-\sigma)*\partial^{\beta_{2}}(\hat{G_{0}}-\sigma)\|_{L^{r_{\beta}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}(\hat{G_{0}}-\sigma)\|_{L^{p_{\alpha}}}\cdot\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}(\hat{G_{0}}-\sigma)\|_{L^{q_{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\cdot\|\partial^{\beta_{1}}(\hat{G_{0}}-\sigma)\|_{L^{p_{\beta}}}\cdot\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}(\hat{G_{0}}-\sigma)\|_{L^{q_{\beta}}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty,

where
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},r_{\alpha},r_{\beta},p_{\alpha},p_{\beta},q_{\alpha},q_{\beta})
must satisfy (we remark that
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
need not be integer vectors)

\begin{cases}|\alpha|_{1}+|\beta|_{1}=k\leq 2d-5\ (d\geq 5),\\
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha,\\
\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta,\\
\frac{1}{r_{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{r_{\beta}}=1,\\
\frac{1}{r_{\alpha}}+1=\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{q_{\alpha}},\\
\frac{1}{r_{\beta}}+1=\frac{1}{p_{\beta}}+\frac{1}{q_{\beta}},\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}p_{\alpha}(2+|\alpha_{1}|_{1})<d,\\
q_{\alpha}(2+|\alpha_{2}|_{1})<d,\\
p_{\beta}(2+|\beta_{1}|_{1})<d,\\
q_{\beta}(2+|\beta_{2}|_{1})<d.\end{cases}

Considering the symmetry, we can take

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{2}=\frac{\alpha}{2},\ \beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=\frac{\beta}{2},

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=q_{\alpha}=\frac{2r_{\alpha}}{r_{\alpha}+1},\ p_{\beta}=q_{\beta}=\frac{2r_{\beta}}{r_{\beta}+1}=\frac{2r_{\alpha}}{2r_{\alpha}-1}.

So, we need to find
r_{\alpha}>1
such that

\frac{2r_{\alpha}}{r_{\alpha}+1}(2+\frac{|\alpha|_{1}}{2})<d,\ \frac{2r_{\alpha}}{2r_{\alpha}-1}(2+\frac{|\beta|_{1}}{2})<d.

Denote
c=\frac{2r_{\alpha}}{r_{\alpha}+1}\in(1,2)
. We need to find
c
such that

c(2+\frac{|\alpha|_{1}}{2})<d,\ \frac{2c}{3c-2}(2+\frac{|\beta|_{1}}{2})<d

which is equivalent to find
c
such that

1<c<2,\ \frac{2d}{3d-4-|\beta|_{1}}<c<\frac{2d}{4+|\alpha|_{1}}.

Such a
c
exists if and only if

\begin{cases}\frac{2d}{3d-4-|\beta|_{1}}<2,\\
\frac{2d}{4+|\alpha|_{1}}>1,\\
\frac{2d}{3d-4-|\beta|_{1}}<\frac{2d}{4+|\alpha|_{1}},\end{cases}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\begin{cases}|\beta|_{1}<2(d-2),\\
|\alpha|_{1}<2(d-2),\\
8+k<3d.\end{cases}

This can be ensured by
k=|\alpha|_{1}+|\beta|_{1}\leq 2(d-2)-1<3d-8
since
d\geq 5
. We have proven (by carefully selecting parameters as above) that

(D.2)

\|f^{2}\|_{W^{k,1}}<\infty\ {\rm for}\ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\ k\leq 2(d-2)-1.

Finally, for the non-integer
|\beta|_{1}\leq 2(d-1)-1
, if we take
k=\lfloor|\beta|_{1}\rfloor
, using Lemma
D.1
again shows that

(D.3)

\|\partial^{\beta}f^{2}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|f^{2}\|_{W^{k-1,1}}^{\theta}\cdot\|f^{2}\|_{W^{k,1}}^{1-\theta}<\infty.

Combining (
D.2
) and (
D.3
) concludes the proof of Lemma
5.4
.

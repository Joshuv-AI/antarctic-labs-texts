Weighted Hardy-Sobolev type inequalities with boundary terms
J. M. do Ó, M.F. Furtado, E.S. Medeiros, J. Ratzkin

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a new class of weighted Hardy–Sobolev type inequalities under mild monotonicity assumptions on the weight function. As a consequence, we derive the corresponding weighted Sobolev and trace-type inequalities. These results play an important role in the analysis of elliptic problems with Neumann or Robin boundary conditions in unbounded domains.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
35J55, 35J50, 37K05, 35J62

1.
Introduction and Statement of Results

Functional inequalities play a critical role in modern analysis and
its applications, with the famous inequalities of Hardy and of Sobolev serving
as models. The classical Sobolev inequality in

\mathbb{R}^{N}
takes the form

(1.1)

\mathcal{S}_{N,p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\frac{Np}{N-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{N-p}{N}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx,\quad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\mathcal{S}_{N,p}>0
is a positive constant and
1\leq p<N
. In
[
15
]
, G. H. Hardy
proved that

\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|t|^{p}}dt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|u^{\prime}|^{p}dt,\quad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,+\infty),

which one can then extend to

(1.2)

\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{x_{N}^{p}}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx,\quad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}),\,p>1,

in the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{N}\geq 0\}
. In their classical form, Hardy inequalities express the fact that
certain weights can be controlled by the gradient term, revealing a delicate balance between
integrability and singularity. It is worth remarking here that (
1.2
)
holds only when the function
u
vanishes along the hyperplane
\{x_{N}=0\}
;
see Remark
3.3
for further commentary.

In the book
[
20
]
, the author combines the Sobolev embedding with Hardy inequality to
obtain a single statement. Precisely, it was shown that, for those smooth functions
on the upper half-space

\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
that vanish on the boundary
x_{N}=0
, there holds the sharp Hardy-Sobolev-Maz’ya inequality:

(1.3)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\,dx\right)^{\!\frac{N-2}{N}}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{|u|^{2}}{4x_{N}^{2}}\right)\,dx,\qquad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}).

This inequality, its generalizations to different powers of the gradient, and the
study of optimal
constants have attracted considerable attention in recent years.
Further extensions of the Hardy–Sobolev–Maz’ya inequality (
1.3
) to convex domains have
also been investigated; see, for instance,
[
12
,
13
]

and the references therein.

These inequalities have been widely used to study existence and Liouville-type results. For
instance, in
[
17
,
19
]
, the authors, motivated by the
study of Liouville-type results,
considered the problem of determining the optimal constant in this inequality.

In recent years, several extensions and refinements of Hardy’s inequality have
been established,
including weighted and anisotropic versions, inequalities with remainder terms, and
Hardy–Sobolev-type inequalities involving additional effects of integrability. Such weighted
inequalities have found important applications in the study of elliptic and parabolic equations
with singular coefficients, in potential theory, and in spectral problems associated with
degenerate operators. For
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
1<p<N
, a weighted
Hardy-Sobolev inequality states that

(1.4)

\int_{\Omega}W_{1}|u|^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}W_{2}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 3
) and the weights
W_{1}
and

W_{2}
satisfy appropriate conditions; see, for example,
[
6
,
21
]
and
references therein. To give one further example, we cite

[
11
, Theorem 6.9]
, where the authors proved that

(1.5)

\left|\frac{\gamma-p+1}{p}\right|^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{x_{N}^{p-\gamma}}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}x_{N}^{\gamma}|\nabla u|^{p}dx,\quad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}),

holds for any
p>1
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Setting
\gamma=0
we
recover (
1.2
).

Our goal in the present manuscript is to prove weighted Hardy-Sobolev
inequalities in the domain above a graph in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, both for
increasing and for decreasing weights. We also explore some, but not all,
of the consequences of these weighted inequalities. Our results here include
extending (
1.5
) to functions that do not vanish
along
\{x_{N}=0\}
provided
\gamma>p-1
(see Corollary
3.2
);
additionally we provide a counter-example in Remark
3.3

demonstrating that the
condition
\gamma>p-1
is sharp.
The present work includes that
of
[
1
,
2
]
, as well as other works,
as special cases. Furthermore, we provide examples showing our conditions on
the weight functions cannot be weakened, and so our results are sharp.
We also weaken the
regularity hypotheses of, for instance,
[
18
]
. It worth mentioning that,
while the weight
should vanish on the boundary to mimic the classical Hardy inequality, our inequalities
apply to functions that do not themselves vanish on the boundary, in contrast
to, for instance,
[
7
]
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
p>1
. As
in
[
3
,
4
]
, we consider a
continuous function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R}
and set

\Omega:=\left\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},\;x_{N}>\psi(x^{\prime})\right\}.

We recover the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
by setting
\psi\equiv 0
.

Throughout the paper, we suppose that
W
and its weak derivative with respect to
x_{N}

satisfy the basic assumption

(
W_{0}
)

W:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow[0,\infty),\quad W\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),\quad W_{x_{N}}:=\frac{\partial W}{\partial x_{N}}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega).

We present our results in several cases, according to the monotonicity conditions on
the weight function
W
.

1.1.
Weighted Hardy inequality and consequences: increasing weight case

In our initial results, we focus on weights that are increasing in the
x_{N}
-direction.
We impose the following assumption:

(
W_{1}^{+}
)

\frac{W^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),\quad W_{x_{N}}>0\text{ a.e. in }\Omega.

Under these conditions, we establish the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Hardy inequality of type I)
.

Suppose (

W
0

) and (

W
1

+

). If
p>1
then, for any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the following inequality is satisfied:

(1.6)

\int_{\Omega}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p}\,dx^{\prime}\leq p^{p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{W^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx.

The key distinction of the domains in Theorem
1.1
is that their
boundaries are merely continuous, unlike the sufficiently smooth boundaries required in
earlier work (e.g.,
[
18
, Lemma 2]
). This lack of smoothness means the outward
normal vector is often undefined (e.g., for Weierstrass-type function graphs),
rendering Green’s First Identity and the Divergence Theorem inapplicable. We can also
prove Theorem
1.1
for more general domains, including non-convex sets
(see Corollary
3.10
).

Following Theorem
1.1
, we prove weighted Sobolev embeddings for both subcritical (Theorem
3.4
) and critical (Theorem
3.6
) cases, in addition to a trace embedding theorem (Theorem
3.9
).

1.2.
Weighted Hardy inequality and consequences: decreasing weight case

Next, we shall focus on the case of decreasing weights. In addition to
(W_{0})
,
we further assume that:

(
W_{1}^{-}
)

\frac{W^{p}}{[-W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),\quad W_{x_{N}}<0\text{ a.e. in }\Omega.

In this context, our Hardy-type result takes the following form:

Theorem 1.2

(Hardy inequality of type II)
.

Suppose (

W
0

) and (

W
1

-

). If
p>1
then, for any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
the following inequality holds:

(1.7)

\int_{\Omega}[-W_{x_{N}}]|u|^{p}dx\leq p^{p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{W^{p}}{\left[-W_{x_{N}}\right]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p}dx^{\prime}.

Remark 1.3
.

One can modify the proof of Theorem
1.2
to obtain
other interesting results.
For instance, if
W
depends only on
x_{N}

and
W_{x_{N}x_{N}}<0
, then

-\int_{\Omega}\Delta W|u|^{p}dx\leq p^{p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla W|^{p}}{|\Delta W|^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W_{x_{N}}(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p}dx^{\prime}.

In particular, if
W_{x_{N}}\leq 0
, then

\int_{\Omega}|\Delta W||u|^{p}dx\leq p^{p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla w|^{p}}{|\Delta w|^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx,

which is in accordance with the result stated in
[
8
, Theorem 1]
for bounded domains.

1.3.
Comparison with previous results

Inequalities with non-singular weight were considered in
[
14
]
, where the
notion of
Bessel pairs
was introduced to derive a large class of weighted Hardy-type
inequalities of the form (
1.4
). More specifically, for
a,b>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(a+b|x|^{\alpha})^{-2/\alpha+\beta}u^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(a+b|x|^{\alpha})^{\beta}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,\qquad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where the exponents
\alpha,\beta
satisfy mild assumptions depending on the dimension. Many
others have extended this technique of using Bessel pairs to derive Hardy-type
inequalities.
For related results, see also
[
5
,
7
]
and
references therein.

We also mention the works
[
16
,
18
,
22
]
, where the authors proved a non-singular
Hardy–Sobolev-type inequality involving a boundary trace term. Specifically, for all
1<p<N
,
there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{p}}{(1+|x|)^{p}}\,dx\leq C_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx+C_{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\nu\cdot x|}{(1+|x|)^{p}}|u|\,dS_{x},\qquad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an unbounded domain with a noncompact, smooth
boundary
\partial\Omega
, and
\nu
denotes the unit outward normal vector
on
\partial\Omega
.
These inequalities make it possible to study elliptic boundary value problems on unbounded domains,
even when the boundary itself is unbounded, using variational methods.

2.
Some examples of weights

In this section, we discuss some consequences of our results by considering specific
classes of potentials
W
. Since
\Omega
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
,
we restrict our attention to the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Thus, we pick
\psi\equiv 0
in Theorem
1.1
to get

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},0)|u|^{p}dx^{\prime}\leq p^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{W^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx,\qquad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

2.1.
Increasing weights

In what follows, we present examples of weights to which we can apply the last inequality above.

Example 2.1
.

If we consider
\gamma>0
,
\beta<N-1
and set

W(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=\frac{x_{N}^{\gamma}}{|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}},\qquad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},\,x_{N}>0,

we have that

W_{x_{N}}(x)=\frac{\gamma x_{N}^{\gamma-1}}{|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}},\qquad\frac{W(x)^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}(x)]^{p-1}}=\frac{1}{\gamma^{p-1}}\frac{x_{N}^{\gamma+p-1}}{|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}}.

Consequently, from (
2.1
), we derive the following inequality:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{1-\gamma}|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}}|u|^{p}dx\leq\left(\frac{p}{\gamma}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{x_{N}^{\gamma+p-1}}{|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx.

If
0<\gamma<1
, the function on the left-hand side above can blow up along the
entire hyperplane
\{x_{N}=0\}
, in contrast to the usual Hardy inequalities,
where the singularity occurs only at a point.

Example 2.2
.

Picking
\gamma>0
,
\beta<N-1
and setting

W(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=\frac{(1+x_{N})^{\gamma}}{|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}},\qquad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},\,x_{N}>0,

we have that

W_{x_{N}}(x)=\frac{\gamma(1+x_{N})^{\gamma-1}}{|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}},\qquad\frac{W(x)^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}(x)]^{p-1}}=\frac{1}{\gamma^{p-1}}\frac{(1+x_{N})^{\gamma+p-1}}{|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}}.

A direct application of (
2.1
) yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}}dx^{\prime}\leq\left(\frac{p}{\gamma}\right)^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{(1+x_{N})^{\gamma+p-1}}{|x^{\prime}|^{\beta}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx

after discarding the first term,
which is a Kato-type inequality; see, for instance,
[
9
]
for related results.

Moreover, if we consider
\beta=0
, and keep all the terms in (
2.1
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\gamma}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{(1+x_{N})^{1-\gamma}}dx

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\gamma}{p}\right)^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{p}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}(1+x_{N})^{\gamma+p-1}|\nabla u|^{p}dx,

and therefore we have obtained a new proof of
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Example 2.3
.

If
W(x):=\log(\mathrm{e}+x_{N})
, then

W_{x_{N}}(x)=\frac{1}{e+x_{N}},\qquad\frac{W(x)^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}(x)]^{p-1}}=(e+x_{N})^{p-1}\log^{p}(e+x_{N})

and therefore (
2.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{(e+x_{N})}dx+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{p}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq p^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}(e+x_{N})^{p-1}\log^{p}(e+x_{N})|\nabla u|^{p}dx,

which is a counterpart of the last inequality obtained in Example
2.2
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\gamma>0
and consider

W(x):=e^{\gamma x_{N}}
. Then

W_{x_{N}}(x)=\gamma e^{\gamma x_{N}},\qquad\frac{W(x)^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}(x)]^{p-1}}=\frac{1}{\gamma^{p-1}}e^{\gamma x_{N}}

and therefore (
2.1
) becomes

\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}e^{\gamma x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{p}dx^{\prime}\leq\gamma\left(\frac{p}{\gamma}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}e^{\gamma x_{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx.

Example 2.5
.

If
W(x):=\arctan(x_{N})
, then

W_{x_{N}}(x)=\frac{1}{1+x_{N}^{2}},\qquad\frac{W(x)^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}(x)]^{p-1}}=\frac{\arctan^{p}(x)}{(1+x_{N}^{2})^{p-1}}

and therefore (
2.1
) gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{(1+x_{N}^{2})}dx\leq\left(\frac{p\pi}{2}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}(1+x_{N}^{2})^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{p}dx.

2.2.
Decreasing weights

In what follows, we present examples of weights to which we can apply the Theorem
1.2
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
p>1
,
\gamma<p-1
and consider

W(x):=(1+x_{N})^{\gamma-p+1}
. Then,

-W_{x_{N}}(x)=(-\gamma+p-1)(1+x_{N})^{\gamma-p},\qquad\frac{W(x)^{p}}{[-W_{x_{N}}(x)]^{p-1}}=\frac{(1+x_{N})^{\gamma}}{(-\gamma+p-1)^{p-1}},

and therefore Theorem
1.2
with
\psi\equiv 0
yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-\gamma+p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{(1+x_{N})^{p-\gamma}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}(1+x_{N})^{\gamma}|\nabla u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{-\gamma+p-1}{p}\right)^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{p}dx^{\prime}.

In particular, if
\gamma=0
, we have the inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{(1+x_{N})^{p}}dx\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{p}dx^{\prime},

which improves
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Example 2.7
.

Let
\gamma>0
and consider

W(s):=e^{-\gamma x_{N}}
. Then

-W_{x_{N}}(x)=\gamma e^{-\gamma x_{N}},\qquad\frac{W(x)^{p}}{[-W_{x_{N}}(x)]^{p-1}}=\frac{1}{(-\gamma)^{p-1}}e^{-\gamma x_{N}}.

and therefore

\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}e^{-\gamma x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx\leq p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{p}dx^{\prime}+\frac{p^{p}}{(-\gamma)^{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}e^{-\gamma x_{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx.

3.
Proofs and Consequences in the Increasing Case

This section presents the proofs of Theorem
1.1
, that is, the Hardy inequality for increasing weight functions
W
, together with several consequences, including weighted Sobolev embeddings.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Given
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
, we have that

\int_{\psi(x^{\prime})}^{\infty}\left(W|u|^{p}\right)_{x_{N}}dx_{N}=-W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime})|^{p}

and therefore we can use
W_{x_{N}}\in L^{1}_{loc}({\Omega})
to get

\int_{\psi(x^{\prime})}^{\infty}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx_{N}+\int_{\psi(x^{\prime})}^{\infty}W(|u|^{p})_{x_{N}}dx_{N}=-W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime})|^{p}.

Integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
and using Fubini’s Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}W(|u|^{p})_{x_{N}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}pW|u|^{p-2}u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}dx.

Hence

(3.1)

\int_{\Omega}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p}dx^{\prime}\leq p\int_{\Omega}W|u|^{p-1}|\nabla u|dx.

Recall that, for any
\varepsilon>0
and
a,\,b\geq 0
, Young’s inequality yields

ab=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{p-1}\right)^{(p-1)/p}a\left(\frac{p-1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{(p-1)/p}b\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{p}a^{p/(p-1)}+\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{p-1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}b^{p}.

Now, taking into account that
W_{x_{N}}>0
, we have

W|u|^{p-1}|\nabla u|=\Big(\left[W_{x_{N}}\right]^{(p-1)/p}|u|^{p-1}\Big)\Big(W\left[W_{x_{N}}\right]^{{-(p-1)/p}}|\nabla u|\Big).

Since
W^{p}/[W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, we can pick
\varepsilon=(p-1)/p

and use the above expression to obtain

\displaystyle p\int_{\Omega}W|u|^{p-1}|\nabla u|dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx+\left(\frac{p-1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{W^{p}}{\left[W_{x_{N}}\right]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{\Omega}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx+p^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{W^{p}}{\left[W_{x_{N}}\right]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx.

Replacing the above inequality in (
3.1
) we obtain the desired result.
∎

Next, we highlight our inequality in the case of the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
,
which can be obtained by setting
\psi\equiv 0
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Suppose (

W
0

) and (

W
1

+

). Then, for
any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the following inequality is satisfied:

(3.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},0)|u|^{p}dx^{\prime}\leq p^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{W^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx.

In particular, if
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx\leq p^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{W^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx.

As a consequence, we have the following:

Corollary 3.2
.

For any
\gamma>p-1
the following inequality holds

(3.3)

\left|\frac{\gamma-p+1}{p}\right|^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{x_{N}^{p-\gamma}}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}x_{N}^{\gamma}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx,\qquad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the weight
W_{\varepsilon}(x):=(\varepsilon+x_{N})^{\theta}
, with
\theta>0
, and use Corollary
3.1
to get

\theta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}(\varepsilon+x_{N})^{\theta-1}|u|^{p}dx+p\varepsilon^{\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|u(x^{\prime},0)|^{p}dx^{\prime}\leq\frac{p^{p}}{\theta^{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}(\varepsilon+x_{N})^{p+\theta-1}|\nabla u|^{p}dx.

Picking
\theta=\gamma-p+1>0
and letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
we
obtain (
3.3
).
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Corollary
3.2
shows that, if
\gamma>-1
,
inequality (
1.5
) holds in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, the
interval
(p-1,+\infty)
is optimal in the sense that inequality (
1.5
)is
false if
\gamma\leq p-1
. In fact,
if
\gamma\leq p-1
, a function

\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\phi\equiv 1
in the
unit ball
B_{1}(0)
provides a counter-example, which one can
see from the following computation:

+\infty=\left|\frac{\gamma-p+1}{p}\right|^{p}\int_{B_{1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{1}{x_{N}^{p-\gamma}}\,dx\leq\left|\frac{\gamma-p+1}{p}\right|^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|\phi|^{p}}{x_{N}^{p-\gamma}}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}x_{N}^{\gamma}|\nabla\phi|^{p}dx<\infty,

which is a contradiction.

In particular, this shows that the validity of the classical
Hardy inequality in the upper half-space, as stated in (
1.2
)
(which corresponds to
\gamma=0
), does not hold for functions that
do not vanish along
\{x_{N}=0\}
. Similarly, the
Hardy-Sobolev-Maz’ya inequality (
1.3
) does not hold in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This highlights how the situation changes when we consider functions
in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
instead of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
in
the context of Hardy-type inequalities.

We also emphasise that, in view of inequality (
3.3
) and a scaling
argument, we conjecture that the following weighted Hardy-Sobolev-Maz’ya inequality

C_{0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}x_{N}^{\frac{N\gamma}{N-p}}|u|^{p^{*}}dx\right)^{p/p^{*}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}x_{N}^{\gamma}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx-\frac{1}{(\gamma-p+1)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{x_{N}^{p-\gamma}}\,dx,\qquad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

holds for any
\gamma>p-1
and some constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(N,p,\gamma)
, where
p^{*}:=Np/(N-p)
.

3.1.
Weighted Sobolev embeddings: the increasing case

To establish the upcoming results, let
\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}
denote the completion of

with respect to the norm

given by

We interpolate the weighted Hardy inequality (

and non-negative

From now on, we assume that
\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1},\mathbb{R})

and assume

(
W_{2}^{+}
)

\text{there exists }c_{1}>0\text{ such that }\frac{W^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}\geq c_{1}\text{ a.e. in }\Omega.

we shall prove the following:

Theorem 3.4

(Sobolev embedding)
.

Suppose (

0

), (

W
1

+

) and (

W
2

+

). If

\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}\hookrightarrow L^{q(s)}(\Omega,W^{s/p}_{x_{N}}),\quad\text{for all }s\in[0,p].

The
starting pointing is noticing that the map

Using now the change of variables

We first recall that

For

\displaystyle\leq\left(p^{p}\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}}^{p}\right)^{s/p}\left(Cc_{1}^{-p^{*}/p}\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}}^{p^{*}}\right)^{(p-s)/p}=C_{1}\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}}^{s+p^{*}(p-s)/p}.

Remark 3.5

Under the same hypotheses of Theorem

\int_{\Omega}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}W\,(|u|^{p})_{x_{N}}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p}\,dx^{\prime}

and therefore

-\int_{\Omega}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}\,dx\leq p\int_{\Omega}W|u|^{p-1}|\nabla u|\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p}\,dx^{\prime}.

Since
W_{x_{N}}<0
, we may choose
\varepsilon>0
, write

W|u|^{p-1}|\nabla u|=\big([-W_{x_{N}}]^{\frac{p-1}{p}}|u|^{p-1}\big)\big(W[-W_{x_{N}}]^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}|\nabla u|\big).

and apply Young’s inequality to obtain

p\int_{\Omega}W|u|^{p-1}|\nabla u|\,dx\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}[-W_{x_{N}}]|u|^{p}\,dx+\left(\frac{p-1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{W^{p}}{[-W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx.

Choosing
\varepsilon:=(p-1)/p
and substituting into the previous inequality, the result follows.
∎

4.1.
Weighted Sobolev embeddings: The decreasing case

Assume that the weight function
W
is decreasing in the
x_{N}
-direction. We introduce the space
\mathcal{D}_{W}^{-}
as the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}_{W}^{-}}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{W^{p}}{[-W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}\,|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))\,|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{1/p}.

We further assume that

(
W_{2}^{-}
)

\text{there exists }c_{2}>0\text{ such that }\frac{W^{p}}{[-W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}\geq c_{2}\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega.

Under this assumption, we establish the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose (

W
0

), (

W

W

1<p<N
,
the following weighted Sobolev embedding is continuous

\mathcal{D}_{W}^{-}\hookrightarrow L^{q(s)}(\Omega,-W_{x_{N}}^{s/p}),\quad\text{for all }s\in[0,p].

Proof.

The cases
s=0
and
s=p
follows from GNS inequality and Theorem Theorem
1.2
, respectively.
For
0<s<p
, using Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[-W_{x_{N}}]^{s/p}|u|^{q(s)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}([-W_{x_{N}}]^{s/p}|u|^{s})|u|^{q(s)-s}dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}[-W_{x_{N}}]|u|^{p}dx\right)^{s/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p^{*}}dx\right)^{(p-s)/p}.

Now it is sufficient to use Theorem
1.2
, the GSN inequality, and condition
(W_{2}^{-})
to obtain the desired result.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
W\in C^{1}((0,\infty),\mathbb{R})

Proof.

Given

in (

\left(\int_{\Omega}W^{\frac{N}{N-1}}|u|^{\frac{N^{2}}{N-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\left[-W_{{x_{N}}}|u|^{N}+NW|u|^{N-1}|\nabla u|\right]dx.

with

Since

to conclude that the embedding holds for

By iterating this process, we obtain the validity of the embedding in any interval of type

∎

Remark 4.3

Following a procedure similar to the one in Remark

The following results concern the corresponding trace embeddings:

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p_{*}}dx^{\prime}

where we have used the embedding
\mathcal{D}_{W}^{-}\hookrightarrow L^{p_{*}}(\Omega,-W_{x_{N}})

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}W|u|^{p_{*}-1}|\nabla u|dx

The above inequalities and the same argument used for

yield

\|u\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\partial\Omega,W)}^{p_{*}}\leq C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p_{*}}dx^{\prime}\leq C_{4}\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}_{W}^{-}}^{p_{*}}.

For the case

5.
Final comments

We devote this final section to presenting some examples of applications of our abstract setting as well as some possible directions of future research.

5.1.
Applications to nonlinear PDE’s

As it is well known, the framework of Sobolev spaces is a cornerstone of the modern functional analytic approach to Partial Differential Equations (PDEs).
To illustrate this, we consider a zero-mass problem with Neumann boundary conditions of the form

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\operatorname{div}\!\big(\rho(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\big)&=&\lambda a(x)|u|^{p-2}u+f(x,u),&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\rho(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u\cdot\nu)&=&\mu b(x^{\prime})|u|^{p-2}u+g(x^{\prime},u),&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

where
\nu=\nu(x^{\prime})
is the outward normal vector at
x^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega
. The spatially varying coefficient
\rho:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, the non-negative functions
a,\,b
and the continuous nonlinearities
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
g:\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
verify some mild growth conditions which enable us to use critical point theory.

More specifically, we would like to look for critical points of the energy functional associated with the equation. Formally, it has the form,

I(u):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\rho(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx-\frac{\lambda}{p}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{p}\,dx-\frac{\mu}{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}b(x^{\prime})\,|u|^{p}\,d\sigma-J(u)

with

J(u):=\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}G(x^{\prime},u)\,d\sigma

where
F(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,\tau)d\tau
,
G(x^{\prime},t):=\int_{0}^{t}g(x^{\prime},\tau)d\tau
are the primitives of
f(x,\cdot)
,
g(x^{\prime},\cdot)
, respectively, and
d\sigma
stands for the surface element on
\partial\Omega
.

The idea now is guarantee that all the above integrals are well defined and the functional
I
has good linking properties.

Example 5.1
.

Suppose
W
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
3.9
and
\rho
is such that

\rho(x)\geq C_{0}\,\frac{[W(x)]^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}(x)]^{p-1}},\qquad\forall\,x\in\Omega.

As a first approach, we assume the Sobolev case
1<p<N
and that there exist
C_{1}>0
,
0\leq s\leq p
and
q\in[p,p_{*}]
such that

|f(x,t)|\leq C_{1}[W_{x_{N}}(x)]^{s/p}|t|^{q(s)},\qquad\forall\,(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R},

and

|g(x^{\prime},t)|\leq C_{1}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|t|^{q},\qquad\forall\,(x^{\prime},t)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}.

Under these condition, we may use standard calculations to show that
J\in C^{1}(\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+},\mathbb{R})
.

Concerning the
p
-quadratic part of the functional
I
, we assume that

0\leq a(x)\leq a_{0}W_{x_{N}}(x),\quad 0\leq b(x^{\prime})\leq b_{0}W(x^{\prime}),\qquad\forall\,\,x\in\Omega,\,x^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega.

From our embedding results, we obtain
C_{2}>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{p}dx\leq C_{2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}}^{p},\quad\int_{\partial\Omega}b(x^{\prime})|u|^{p}d\sigma\leq C_{2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}}^{p},

for any
u\in\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}
.

We now define

\|u\|_{E}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx-\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{p}\,dx-\mu_{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}b(x^{\prime})\,|u|^{p}\,d\sigma\right)^{1/p}

and use all the above inequalities to write

\|u\|_{E}^{p}\geq\Big(C_{0}-(\lambda+\mu)C_{2}\Big)\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}}^{p},\qquad\forall\,u\in\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}.

So, if the parameters
\lambda,\,\mu
are small, our functional behaves like

I(u)\sim\|u\|_{E}^{p}-J(u)
, for any
u\in E
, where
E
is the Banach space
\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}
endowed with the (equivalent) norm
\|\cdot\|
.

We can handle the borderline case
p=N
in the same way, just replacing the growth condition on
f
and
g
by

|f(x,t)|\leq C_{1}W_{x_{N}}(x)|t|^{q_{1}},\quad|g(x^{\prime},t)|\leq C_{1}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|t|^{q_{2}},

for exponents
q_{1},\,q_{2}\in[N,+\infty)
.

Example 5.2
.

Another application of our results is the study of nonlinear elliptic problems with Robin boundary conditions of the form

(5.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\operatorname{div}\!\big(\rho(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\big)&=&f(x,u),&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\rho(x^{\prime})\,|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u\!\cdot\!\nu)+b(x^{\prime})|u|^{p-2}u&=&g(x^{\prime},u),&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

The main difference here is that the non-negative potential
b
appears on the left-hand side of the boundary condition.

In this case, considering the weighted Sobolev space
E
defined by the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with repecto to the norm

\|u\|_{E}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{W^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}W_{x_{N}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p},

we see that in the space
E
the Friedrich norm

\|u\|_{F}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{W^{p}}{[W_{x_{N}}]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime})|^{p}dx^{\prime}\right)^{1/p},

is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{E}
. Thus, we can assume similar conditions as in the Example
5.1
in order to address results of existence and Liouville type results for this class of problems, see for instance
[
17
]
.

Example 5.3
.

Of course, we can also consider the case where
W
is decreasing. More precisely, by combining the Hardy-type inequality (
1.7
) with the Sobolev embedding established for the decreasing case, we can treat problem (
5.2
)
with the weighted function
\rho
satisfying

\rho(x)\geq C_{0}\,\frac{[W(x_{N})]^{p}}{[-W_{x_{N}}(x_{N})]^{p-1}},\qquad\forall\,x\in\Omega.

We point out that in this case we are not able to consider the Neumann boundary condition, that is,
b\equiv 0
. Nevertheless, it is possible to take
\rho\equiv 1
in this setting (see, for instance, Example
2.6
).

5.2.
Further Research Directions

•

Is the constant

c(\gamma,p)=\left(\frac{\gamma-p+1}{p}\right)^{p}

in the inequality (
3.3
) optimal and/or achieved?

•

As already mentioned, using inequality (
3.3
) together with a scaling argument,
it is natural to expect that the following weighted Hardy–Sobolev–Maz’ya type inequality holds:

\displaystyle C_{0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}x_{N}^{\frac{N\gamma}{N-p}}|u|^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\!p/p^{*}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}x_{N}^{\gamma}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx-\frac{1}{(\gamma-p+1)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{x_{N}^{\,p-\gamma}}\,dx,

for all
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
,
\gamma>p-1
and some constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(N,p,\gamma)
. Is this really true?

•

Is it possible to prove Theorem
3.6
when the weight
W
depends also of
x^{\prime}
with no additional condition, see Remark
3.7
?

•

The inequality (
1.2
) in Theorem
1.2
has the form

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[-W_{x_{N}}]|u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{\Omega}\frac{W^{p}}{\left[-W_{x_{N}}\right]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle+B\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p}dx^{\prime}

where
A
and
B
are positive constants. As is well known, see for instance
[
10
]
, the first best constant associated to (
5.3
) is defined by

A_{\mathrm{opt}}:=\inf\Bigl\{A\in\mathbb{R}:\mbox{there exists }B\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ such that }\eqref{AB}\mbox{ is valid}\Bigr\}

whose corresponding inequality is given by

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[-W_{x_{N}}]|u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq A_{\mathrm{opt}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{W^{p}}{\left[-W_{x_{N}}\right]^{p-1}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle+B\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}W(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|u(x^{\prime},\psi(x^{\prime}))|^{p}dx^{\prime}.

Now, it is natural to investigate the
second-best constant
associated with (
5.4
), defined by

B_{\mathrm{opt}}:=\inf\Bigl\{B\in\mathbb{R}:\eqref{Aoptimal}\text{ holds with this value of }B\Bigr\}.

Thus, one may investigate the precise value of optimal constants
A_{\mathrm{opt}}
and
B_{\mathrm{opt}}
in the above inequality.

•

If we assume that
W_{1},W_{2}
are positive weights, can we prove an inequality as below

\int_{\Omega}W_{1}|u|^{p}+C_{1}\int_{\partial\Omega}W_{2}|u|^{p}\leq C_{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx,\quad\forall u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0?

Can we prove the same result above when the co-dimension is greater than one?

•

We observe that the monotonicity of
W
plays a decisive role in determining the position of the boundary term in our inequalities. A natural question arises: is this monotonicity assumption necessary in order to obtain results of this type?

•

In view of the Sobolev embedding in the borderline case
p=N
, a natural question is whether a Trudinger–Moser type inequality holds in the spaces
\mathcal{D}_{W}^{+}
and
\mathcal{D}_{W}^{-}
.

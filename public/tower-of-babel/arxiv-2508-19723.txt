Abstract
This paper resolves two open problems in extremal set theory. For a family ℱ ⊆ 2 [ n ] \mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]} and i , j ∈ [ n ] i,j\in[n] , we denote ℱ ( i , j ¯ ) = { F \ { i } : F ∈ ℱ , F ∩ { i , j } = { i } } \mathcal{F}(i,\bar{j})=\{F\backslash\{i\}:F\in\mathcal{F},F\cap\{i,j\}=\{i\}\} . The sturdiness β ⁡ ( ℱ ) \beta(\mathcal{F}) is defined as the minimum | ℱ ⁡ ( i , j ¯ ) | |\mathcal{F}(i,\bar{j})| over all i ≠ j i\neq j . A family ℱ \mathcal{F} is called an IU-family if it satisfies the intersection constraint: F ∩ F ′ ≠ ∅ F\cap F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset for all F , F ′ ∈ ℱ F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F} , as well as the union constraint: F ∪ F ′ ≠ [ n ] F\cup F^{\prime}\neq[n] for all F , F ′ ∈ ℱ F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F} . The well-known IU-Theorem states that every IU-family ℱ ⊆ 2 [ n ] \mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]} has size at most 2 n − 2 2^{n-2} . In this paper, we prove that if ℱ ⊆ 2 [ n ] \mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]} is an IU-family, then β ⁡ ( ℱ ) ≤ 2 n − 4 \beta(\mathcal{F})\leq 2^{n-4} . This confirms a recent conjecture proposed by Frankl and Wang. As the second result, we establish a tight upper bound on the sum of sizes of cross t t -intersecting separated families. Our result not only extends a previous theorem of Frankl, Liu, Wang and Yang on separated families, but also provides explicit counterexamples to an open problem proposed by them, thereby settling their problem in the negative. Building on the concept of diversity, Frankl and Wang
[

1.2
Upper bound for separated families

Let

be positive integers and suppose
[m]=\bigcup_{i\in[k]}X_{i}

are pairwise disjoint and
|X_{i}|=n
.
Assume further that the elements of

Denote the smallest element of

v_{i}

For any
\ell\in[k]

\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)=\left\{H\in\binom{[m]}{\ell}:|H\cap X_{i}|\leq 1,i\in[k]\right\}.

A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)
is called a
separated family
. Two separated families
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)
and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell^{\prime})
are said to be
cross
t
-intersecting
if
|F\cap G|\geq t

for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
G\in\mathcal{G}
. For
t=1
, we simply
say that they are cross intersecting.

Recently, Frankl, Liu, Wang and Yang
[
8
]
determined the maximum possible sum of sizes of non-empty cross intersecting separated families.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
8
]

Let
n,k,\ell,\ell^{\prime}
be integers with
n>3\ell
and
k\geq\ell\geq\ell^{\prime}\geq 2
. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\left\{F\in\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell):F\cap\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell^{\prime}}\}\neq\emptyset\right\}~\text{and}~\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\prime}=\left\{\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell^{\prime}}\}\right\}.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)
and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell^{\prime})
are both non-empty and cross intersecting, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|+|\mathcal{G}|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\prime}|+|\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\prime}|=\sum\limits_{j\in[\ell]}\left(\binom{k}{j}-\binom{k-\ell^{\prime}}{j}\right)\binom{k-j}{\ell-j}(n-1)^{\ell-j}+1.

In the same paper, the authors proposed the following open problem.

Problem 1.5
.

[
8
]

Does the conclusion of Theorem
1.4
hold for all values of
n\geq 2
, irrespective the values of
k\geq\ell\geq\ell^{\prime}\geq 1
?

To address this problem, we consider a more general framework of cross
t
-intersecting separated families. Within this setting, we establish the following sharp bound on the sum of sizes of cross intersecting separated families.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n,k,\ell,\ell^{\prime}
be integers with
n\geq 2
and
k\geq\ell\geq\ell^{\prime}\geq t\geq 1
. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{0}=\left\{F\in\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell):|F\cap\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell^{\prime}}\}|\geq t\right\}~\text{and}~\mathcal{G}_{0}=\left\{\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell^{\prime}}\}\right\}.

For every

If

and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell^{\prime})
are both non-empty and cross

-intersecting, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|+|\mathcal{G}|\leq\max\left\{|\mathcal{F}_{0}|+|\mathcal{G}_{0}|,\ \max_{a\in[\ell^{\prime}+1,\ell]}\left\{|\mathcal{F}_{a}|+|\mathcal{G}_{a}|\right\}\right\}.

This general result not only extends Theorem
1.4
(

[
20

(in the case

By constructing explicit counterexamples guided by Theorem

and all

Example 1.7

Under the notation of Theorem

Let

Let

By Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.3

Our proof relies on a foundational result of Seymour
[
23
]
concerning cross-Sperner families. Recall that two families
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
are called
cross-Sperner
if for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
G\in\mathcal{G}
, we have neither
F\subseteq G
nor
G\subseteq F
. To make the presentation self-contained and to serve our purposes more effectively, we have derived a strengthened form of this result (Theorem
2.2
). We include its proof, which is inspired by Seymour’s original arguments.

To facilitate our analysis, we recall some notions. A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called a
downset
(resp. an
upset
) if for every
A\in\mathcal{F}
and every
B\subseteq[n]
satisfying
B\subseteq A
(resp.
A\subseteq B
), it holds that
B\in\mathcal{F}
.
For any
p\in(0,1)
and
F\in\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
, the
product measure

\mu_{p}
on
F
is defined by
\mu_{p}(F)=p^{|F|}(1-p)^{n-|F|}
. We extend this measure to the whole family by setting
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mu_{p}(F)
.

A key ingredient in our derivation is the following correlation inequality:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
24
]

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a downset and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be an upset. Then

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G})\leq\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})\mu_{p}(\mathcal{G}).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be cross-Sperner families. Then

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{G})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 1.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=

\displaystyle\left\{A\subseteq[n]:\exists~F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ s.t. }F\subseteq A\text{ and }\exists~G\in\mathcal{G}\text{ s.t. }G\subseteq A\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=

\displaystyle\left\{B\subseteq[n]:\exists~F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ s.t. }F\subseteq B\text{ but }\nexists~G\in\mathcal{G}\text{ s.t. }G\subseteq B\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=

\displaystyle\left\{C\subseteq[n]:\nexists~F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ s.t. }F\subseteq C\text{ but }\exists~G\in\mathcal{G}\text{ s.t. }G\subseteq C\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=

\displaystyle\left\{D\subseteq[n]:\nexists~F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ s.t. }F\subseteq D\text{ and }\nexists~G\in\mathcal{G}\text{ s.t. }G\subseteq D\right\}.

Observe that
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
is an upset and
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{D}
is a downset.
By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\mu_{p}((\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B})\cap(\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{D}))\leq\mu_{p}(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B})\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{D}).

Since
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}

are pairwise disjoint, we obtain

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})\leq\left(\mu_{p}(\mathcal{A})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})\right)\left(\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{D})\right).

Since
2^{[n]}=\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{D}
implies
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{A})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{C})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{D})=1
, we further have

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})\left(\mu_{p}(\mathcal{A})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{C})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{D})\right)\leq\left(\mu_{p}(\mathcal{A})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})\right)\left(\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{D})\right).

It follows that

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})\mu_{p}(\mathcal{C})\leq\mu_{p}(\mathcal{A})\mu_{p}(\mathcal{D}).

Applying the AM-GM inequality yields

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})\mu_{p}(\mathcal{C})\leq\mu_{p}(\mathcal{A})\mu_{p}(\mathcal{D})\leq\left(\frac{\mu_{p}(\mathcal{A})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{D})}{2}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{1-\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})-\mu_{p}(\mathcal{C})}{2}\right)^{2}.

Consequently,

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{C})+2\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu_{p}(\mathcal{C})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 1.

Therefore, we have

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{C})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 1.

Since
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are cross-Sperner, we infer that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.
This implies that

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{G})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mu_{p}(\mathcal{B})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mu_{p}(\mathcal{C})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 1,

as desired.
∎

, we now proceed to apply this to resolve Conjecture

.

Let
x\neq y\in[n]
and
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{G}(x,\bar{y})

\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{G}(y,\bar{x})

Since
\mathcal{G}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is an IU-family, it follows that
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq 2^{[n]\backslash\{x,y\}}
are cross-intersecting and
(n-3)
-union.

Define
\mathcal{B}^{c}=\{B^{c}:B\in\mathcal{B}\}
, where
B^{c}=([n]\backslash\{x,y\})\backslash G
. Since
\mathcal{A}
and

are cross-intersecting and

3.1
Shifting operator and reduction lemma

We commence our analysis with the shifting technique
[
3
]
, which is one of the most powerful tools in extremal set theory.
Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[k]}
be a family and let
1\leq i<j\leq k
be integers. The
shifting operator

s_{i,j}
on
\mathcal{F}
is defined as follows:

s_{i,j}(\mathcal{F})=\left\{s_{i,j}(F):F\in\mathcal{F}\right\},

where

s_{i,j}(F)=\begin{cases}(F\backslash\{j\})\cup\{i\}&\text{ if }j\in F,i\notin F\text{ and }(F\backslash\{j\})\cup\{i\}\notin\mathcal{F};\\
F&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called

or

if
s_{i,j}(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}
holds for all
1\leq i<j\leq k
.
It is a classical result that by applying the shifting operator repeatedly, every family will terminate at a shifted family. Notably, the shifting operator preserves the corss
t
-intersection property, as captured by the following lemma..

Lemma 3.1
.

[
15
]

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\subseteq 2^{[k]}
be cross
t
-intersecting families and let
1\leq i<j\leq k
be integers.
Then
s_{i,j}(\mathcal{F})
and
s_{i,j}(\mathcal{G})
are cross
t
-intersecting.

We now extend these ideas to separated family. Consider a ground set partitioned as
[m]=\bigcup_{i\in[k]}X_{i}
, where the
X_{i}
are pairwise disjoint sets of size
n
, and let
v_{i}
denote the smallest element in
X_{i}
. For every
\ell\in[k]
,

\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)=\left\{H\in\binom{[m]}{\ell}:|H\cap X_{i}|\leq 1,i\in[k]\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)
be a separated family. The shifting operator
s_{ij}
is allowed to apply on
\mathcal{F}
if
i<j
are in the same
X_{a}
for some
a\in[k]
.
A separated family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)
is called
shifted
if
s_{ij}(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}
for all
i<j
in the same
X_{a}
.
By applying the allowed shifting operator repeatedly, every
separated family eventually becomes shifted

For any
H\in\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)
, we fix the following notation:

A(H)=\left\{i:H\cap X_{i}=\{v_{i}\}\right\},\quad\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{F})=\left\{A(F):F\in\mathcal{F}\right\}.

This notation allows us to associate each set with a subset of indices indicating in which blocks it contains exactly the minimal element. We now state a structural result that facilitates our analysis, which is inspired by the reduction lemma in
[
8
,
9
,
20
]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Reduction lemma)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)
and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell^{\prime})
are shifted and cross
t
-intersecting. Then
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}
and
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}
are cross
t
-intersecting.

Proof.

Assume that there exist
F=\left\{x_{a_{1}},\ldots,x_{a_{\ell}}\right\}\in\mathcal{F}
and
G=\left\{y_{b_{1}},\ldots,y_{b_{\ell^{\prime}}}\right\}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\left|A(F)\cap A(G)\right|<t
, where
x_{a_{j}}\in X_{a_{j}}
for
j\in[\ell]
and
y_{b_{j}}\in X_{b_{j}}
for
j\in[\ell^{\prime}]
.
Recall that
v_{a_{i}}
is the smallest element of
X_{a_{i}}
.
Let
F^{\prime}=\left\{z_{a_{1}},\ldots,z_{a_{\ell}}\right\}
, where for
i\in[\ell]
,

z_{a_{i}}=\begin{cases}x_{a_{i}}&\text{if}\ \ a_{i}\in A(G),\\
v_{a_{i}}&\text{if}\ \ a_{i}\notin A(G).\end{cases}

Observe that
F^{\prime}
can be obtained from
F
by allowed shifting operations. Since
\mathcal{F}
is shifted, we have
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
. It follows that

\left|F^{\prime}\cap G\right|=\left|A(F)\cap A(G)\right|<t,

a contradiction.
∎

3.2
Necessary intersection point

We prove our main auxiliary result (Theorem
3.6
) in this part. Its proof utilizes the essential intersection point method, a powerful technique recently developed by Gupta , Mogge, Piga and Schulke
[
15
]
for analyzing cross intersecting families.
To apply this method effectively, we first introduce some necessary notions and preliminary results.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\subseteq 2^{[k]}
be cross
t
-intersecting families. We say
a\in[n]
is a necessary intersection point of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
if there exist
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
G\in\mathcal{G}
such that

\displaystyle\left|[a]\cap F\cap G\right|=t\text{ and }a\in F\cap G.

The above definition naturally leads to the following result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\subseteq 2^{[k]}
be cross
t
-intersecting families and
1\leq i<j\leq k
. Suppose that
a
is the maximal necessary intersection point of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
. Then

\left|[a-1]\cap F\cap G\right|\geq t-1.

Moreover,
s_{ij}(\mathcal{F})
and
s_{ij}(\mathcal{G})
are cross
t
-intersecting with the maximal necessary intersection point at most
a
.

Proof.

For the first assertion, assume that

\left|[a-1]\cap F\cap G\right|<t-1.

Then

\left|[a]\cap F\cap G\right|\leq\left|[a-1]\cap F\cap G\right|+1<t,

which means there exists a necessary intersection point larger than
a
and it is a contradiction.

For the second assertion, by Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to prove that
the maximal necessary intersection point of
s_{ij}(\mathcal{F})
and
s_{ij}(\mathcal{G})
is at most
a
.
Let
a^{\prime}
be their maximal necessary intersection point.
Then there exist
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
G\in\mathcal{G}
such that

\left|[a^{\prime}]\cap s_{ij}(F)\cap s_{ij}(G)\right|=t
and
a^{\prime}\in s_{ij}(F)\cap s_{ij}(G)
.
If
a^{\prime}>a
, then
\left|[a]\cap s_{ij}(F)\cap s_{ij}(G)\right|<t
.
By the definition of
a
, we have
s_{ij}(F)\notin\mathcal{F}
or

s_{ij}(G)\notin\mathcal{G}
.
By symmetry, we may assume that
s_{ij}(F)\notin\mathcal{F}
.
This implies that
s_{ij}(F)=F\backslash\{j\}\cup\{i\}
.
From
\left|[a]\cap(F\backslash\{j\}\cup\{i\})\cap s_{ij}(G)\right|<t

and
\left|[a]\cap F\cap G\right|\geq t
, we infer that

j\in F\cap G
and
i\notin G
and
G\backslash\{j\}\cup\{i\}\in\mathcal{G}
. Then
s_{ij}(G)=G
and hence
\left|[a]\cap(F\backslash\{j\}\cup\{i\})\cap G\right|<t
. Furthermore, by the definition of
a
, we have

\left|[a]\cap F\cap(G\backslash\{j\}\cup\{i\})\right|\geq t.

However,

|[a]\cap F\cap(G\backslash\{j\}\cup\{i\})|=\left|[a]\cap(F\backslash\{j\}\cup\{i\})\cap G\right|<t
, a contradiction. Therefore, we conclude that
a^{\prime}\leq a
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\subseteq 2^{[k]}
be cross
t
-intersecting families with the maximal necessary intersection point
a
.
Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{a}=

\displaystyle\left\{F\in\mathcal{F}:\exists~G\in\mathcal{G}\text{ s.t. }\left|[a]\cap F\cap G\right|=t\text{ and }a\in F\cap G\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{a}=

\displaystyle\left\{G\in\mathcal{G}:\exists~F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ s.t. }\left|[a]\cap F\cap G\right|=t\text{ and }a\in F\cap G\right\}

Additionally, let
\omega:[0,k]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a
non-increasing function
, meaning that
\omega(i)\geq\omega(j)
whenever
i\leq j
. For any set
F\subseteq[n]
, define its weight as
\omega(F)=\omega(|F|)
. For a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
, define the total weight by
\omega(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\omega(F)

The following lemma, which is an analogue of Lemma 3.3 in
[
15
]
, plays a key role in our weighting argument.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
k\geq\ell\geq\ell^{\prime}\geq t\geq 1
be integers. For
i\in[2]
, let
\omega_{i}:[0,k]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be non-increasing functions. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}
and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}
be non-empty cross
t
-intersecting families with the maximal necessary intersection point
a
. Suppose that
a\geq t+1
and
\mathcal{F}\backslash\mathcal{F}^{a}\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{G}\backslash\mathcal{G}^{a}\neq\emptyset
. Then there are families
\mathcal{F}^{*}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}
and
\mathcal{G}^{*}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{F}^{*},\mathcal{G}^{*}
are non-empty cross
t
-intersecting families;

(2)

\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{*})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}^{*})\geq\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G})
;

(3)

the maximal necessary intersection point of
\mathcal{F}^{*}
and
\mathcal{G}^{*}
is smaller than
a
.

(4)

there are shifted
\mathcal{F}^{*}
and
\mathcal{G}^{*}
satisfying (1)-(3).

Proof.

Observe that (1) and (2) are preserved under shifting. According to Lemma
3.4
,
(3) is also preserved under shifting.
Since the families
\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}
and
\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}
are shifted, it suffices to establish properties (1)-(3).

Define

\mathcal{F}^{add}=\left\{F\backslash\{a\}:F\in\mathcal{F}^{a}\right\},\ \mathcal{G}^{add}=\left\{G\backslash\{a\}:G\in\mathcal{G}^{a}\right\}.

Furthermore, let

\mathcal{F}^{+}=\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}^{add},\quad\mathcal{G}^{+}=\mathcal{G}\cup\mathcal{G}^{add},\quad\mathcal{F}^{-}=\mathcal{F}\backslash\mathcal{F}^{a},\quad\mathcal{G}^{-}=\mathcal{G}\backslash\mathcal{G}^{a}.

Then
\mathcal{F}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{-}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}
and

\mathcal{G}^{+},\mathcal{G}^{-}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}
.

Claim 1
.

\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{F}^{add}=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{G}\cap\mathcal{G}^{add}=\emptyset
.

Proof of Claim
1
.

By symmetry, it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{F}^{add}=\emptyset
.
Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists
F\in\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{F}^{add}
.
So
a\notin F
and there is
F^{*}\in\mathcal{F}^{a}

such that
F\cap[a-1]=F^{*}\cap[a-1]
. By the definition of

\mathcal{F}^{a}
, there exists
G\in\mathcal{G}

such that

|[a]\cap F^{*}\cap G|=t

and
a\in F^{*}\cap G
.
This implies that
|[a-1]\cap F^{*}\cap G|=t-1
and hence

|[a-1]\cap F\cap G|=t-1.

Since
a\notin F
, we obtain
|[a]\cap F\cap G|=t-1
, which contradicts with the maximality of
a
.
∎

Since
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
are non-increasing, we obtain
\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{add})\geq\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{a})
and
\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}^{add})\geq\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}^{a})
.
If
\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{a})\geq\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}^{a})
, then let

\mathcal{F}^{*}=\mathcal{F}^{+}
and
\mathcal{G}^{*}=\mathcal{G}^{-}
.
It follows from Claim
1
that

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{*})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}^{*})

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G})+\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{add})-\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}^{a})

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G})+\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{a})-\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}^{a})

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}).

If
\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{a})\leq\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}^{a})
, then let

\mathcal{F}^{*}=\mathcal{F}^{-}
and
\mathcal{G}^{*}=\mathcal{G}^{+}
.
Similarly, we have
\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{*})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}^{*})\geq\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G})
. Thus (2) holds.

It remains to prove the following claim.

Claim 2
.

For any
F^{+}\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
and
G^{-}\in\mathcal{G}^{-}
,

|[a-1]\cap F^{+}\cap G^{-}|\geq t

holds.
Moreover, for any
F^{-}\in\mathcal{F}^{-}
and
G^{+}\in\mathcal{G}^{+}
,

|[a-1]\cap F^{-}\cap G^{+}|\geq t

holds.

Proof of Claim
2
.

By symmetry, it suffices to prove the first assertion.
Suppose that there exist
F^{+}\in\mathcal{F}^{+}
and
G^{-}\in\mathcal{G}^{-}
such that

|[a-1]\cap F^{+}\cap G^{-}|<t.

If
F^{+}\in\mathcal{F}
, then
G^{-}\in\mathcal{G}
and
Lemma
3.4
imply
|[a-1]\cap F^{+}\cap G^{-}|=t-1.

By the maximality of
a
, we have
|[a]\cap F^{+}\cap G^{-}|=t

and
a\in F^{+}\cap G^{-}
.
Then
G^{-}\in\mathcal{G}^{a}
, a contradiction.

If
F^{+}\notin\mathcal{F}
, then
F^{+}\in\mathcal{F}^{add}
. Hence,
a\notin F^{+}
and there exists

F\in\mathcal{F}^{a}
such that

F\cap[a-1]=F^{+}\cap[a-1].

Since
|[a-1]\cap F^{+}\cap G^{-}|<t,

it follows that

\left|[a-1]\cap F\cap G^{-}\right|<t.

Then
G^{-}\in\mathcal{G}
and
Lemma
3.4
imply

\left|[a-1]\cap F\cap G^{-}\right|=t-1.

By the maximality of
a
, we have
|[a]\cap F\cap G^{-}|=t

and
a\in F\cap G^{-}
,
which contradicts with
G^{-}\in\mathcal{G}\backslash\mathcal{G}^{a}
.
∎

Since Claim
2
implies that (1) and (3) hold, the proof of the lemma is finished.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}
and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}
be non-empty cross
t
-intersecting families with
k\geq\ell\geq\ell^{\prime}\geq t\geq 1
. For
i\in[2]
, let
\omega_{i}:[0,k]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be non-increasing functions. Then

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G})\leq\max

\displaystyle\left\{\max_{a\in[t,\ell^{\prime}]}\{\omega_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell,t))+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime}))\},\right.

\displaystyle\left.\max_{a\in[t,\ell]}\{\omega_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell))+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime},t))\}\right\},

where
\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell,t)=\left\{K\in\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}:|K\cap[a]|\geq t\right\}
and
\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime})=\left\{S\in\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}:[a]\subseteq S\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}
and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}
be non-empty cross
t
-intersecting families with
\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G})
maximal and the maximal necessary intersection point minimal.
By Lemmas
3.1
and
3.4
, we may assume that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are shifted. Let
a
be their maximal necessary intersection point.

If
a=t
, then for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
G\in\mathcal{G}
, we have

|[t]\cap F\cap G|\geq t
, which means that
[t]\subseteq F\cap G
.
Therefore, we have
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell)
and
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime})
, which contradicts with the maximality of
\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G})
.

If
a\geq t+1
and
\mathcal{F}\backslash\mathcal{F}^{a}\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{G}\backslash\mathcal{G}^{a}\neq\emptyset
, then Lemma
3.5
implies that there exist shifted and non-empty cross
t
-intersecting families

\mathcal{F}^{*}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}
and
\mathcal{G}^{*}\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}
such that
\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{*})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G}^{*})\geq\omega_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{G})
. In addition, their maximal necessary intersection point is smaller than
a
. This contradicts the minimality of
a
.
Therefore, either
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{a}
or
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{a}
. For the former case, we have that all sets in
\mathcal{F}
contain
a
. We claim that all sets in
\mathcal{F}
contain
[a]
. Indeed,
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
b\in[a]
such that
b\notin F
would imply
F\setminus\{a\}\cup\{b\}\in\mathcal{F}
because
\mathcal{F}
is shifted, a contradiction.
Therefore, we have
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell)
and hence
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime},t)
.
For the latter case, a similar argument yields

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell,t)
and
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime})
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.3
Proof of Theorem
1.6

Having established Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
3.6
, we now return to the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell)
and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(n,k,\ell^{\prime})
be non-empty cross
t
-intersecting families with
|\mathcal{F}|+|\mathcal{G}|
maximal.
By Lemma
3.1
, we may assume that they are shifted
separated families.
According to Lemma
3.2
,
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}
and
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}
are cross
t
-intersecting.

Observe that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum\limits_{j\in[0,\ell]}\left|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{F})^{(j)}\right|\binom{k-j}{\ell-j}(n-1)^{\ell-j},\quad|\mathcal{G}|\leq\sum\limits_{j\in[0,\ell^{\prime}]}\left|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G})^{(j)}\right|\binom{k-j}{\ell^{\prime}-j}(n-1)^{\ell^{\prime}-j}.

Let us define

\omega_{1}{(j)}=\binom{k-j}{\ell-j}(n-1)^{\ell-j},\quad\omega_{2}{(j)}=\binom{k-j}{\ell^{\prime}-j}(n-1)^{\ell^{\prime}-j},\quad j\in[0,k].

Since
n\geq 2
and
k\geq\ell\geq\ell^{\prime}
, we infer that
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}

are non-increasing functions.

Recall that
\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell,t)=\left\{K\in\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}:|K\cap[a]|\geq t\right\}
and
\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime})=\left\{S\in\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}:[a]\subseteq S\right\}.

It follows from Theorem
3.6
that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|+|\mathcal{G}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j\in[0,\ell]}\left|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{F})^{(j)}\right|\binom{k-j}{\ell-j}(n-1)^{\ell-j}+\sum\limits_{j\in[0,\ell^{\prime}]}\left|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G})^{(j)}\right|\binom{k-j}{\ell^{\prime}-j}(n-1)^{\ell^{\prime}-j}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j\in[0,\ell]}|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{F})^{(j)}|\omega_{1}(j)+\sum\limits_{j\in[0,\ell^{\prime}]}|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G})^{(j)}|\omega_{2}(j)

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{F}))+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G}))

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\max_{a\in[t,\ell^{\prime}]}\{\omega_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell,t))+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime}))\},\ \max_{a\in[t,\ell]}\{\omega_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell))+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime},t))\}\right\}.

Let
f(a)=\omega_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell,t))+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime}))
and

g(a)=\omega_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell))+\omega_{2}(\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime},t))
.
Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{k}(a+1,\ell,t)\backslash\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell,t)

\displaystyle=\left\{K\in\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell}:|K\cap[a]|=t-1\text{ and }a+1\in K\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime})\backslash\mathcal{S}_{k}(a+1,\ell^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\{K\in\binom{[k]}{\leq\ell^{\prime}}:[a]\subseteq K\text{ and }a+1\notin K\right\},

it follows that for any
a\in[t,\ell^{\prime}-1]
, we have

\displaystyle f(a+1)-f(a)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\in[0,\ell-t]}\binom{a}{t-1}\binom{k-a-1}{j}\omega_{1}{(j+t)}-\sum\limits_{j\in[0,\ell^{\prime}-a]}\binom{k-a-1}{j}\omega_{2}{(j+a)}.

From
\ell\geq\ell^{\prime}
and
a\geq t
, we get
\omega_{1}{(j+t)}\geq\omega_{2}{(j+t)}\geq\omega_{2}{(j+a)}
and
\ell-t\geq\ell^{\prime}-a
.
This implies that

f(a+1)-f(a)\geq 0
and hence
f(\ell^{\prime})=\max\limits_{a\in[t,\ell^{\prime}]}f(a)
.

For any
a\in[t,\ell^{\prime}]
, we have
f(a)\geq g(a)
because
\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime},t)\backslash\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell^{\prime})\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{k}(a,\ell,t)\backslash\mathcal{S}_{k}(a,\ell)
.
This, together with
f(\ell^{\prime})=\max\limits_{a\in[t,\ell^{\prime}]}f(a)
, implies that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|+|\mathcal{G}|\leq\left\{f(\ell^{\prime}),\ \max_{a\in[\ell^{\prime}+1,\ell]}g(a)\right\}=\max\left\{|\mathcal{F}_{0}|+|\mathcal{G}_{0}|,\ \max_{a\in[\ell^{\prime}+1,\ell]}\left\{|\mathcal{F}_{a}|+|\mathcal{G}_{a}|\right\}\right\},

as required.
∎

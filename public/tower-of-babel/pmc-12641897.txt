Dengue vaccine effectiveness and safety: a systematic analysis of recent clinical trial data
Authors: Ravi Kant, Anushka Singh, A. Babar Ali, Shweta Nagre, Nirmal Kumar Ganguly, Rashmi Rana
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and objectivesDengue is one of the fastest spreading arboviral infection with no specific antiviral treatment, making vaccination a significant preventive approach. Several dengue vaccines have been developed, but their efficacy, safety, and serotype-specific protection remain areas of concern. Over the past five years, new clinical data have emerged, influencing vaccine recommendations and deployment strategies. This systematic review analyses recent dengue vaccine data, assessing their effectiveness, and safety profiles.MethodsData was taken from three different databases i.e. PubMed, Cochrane and google scholar from last five years (2020–2025) by using the specific search terms. Filters were applied to extract the studies relevant to our analysis. Data was analysed and forest plots were made for proper understanding of vaccine efficacy and safety data of vaccines.Results14 studies are included in the study and our systematic review study on dengue vaccines revealed TAK-003 and TV005 with highest protection rates in their respective settings. Safety profiles varied: TAK-003 and CYD-TDV reduced serious events, whereas TV005 was associated with heightened reactogenicity.ConclusionThis review provides a systematic review of the dengue vaccines data available from the last five years highlighting significant progress in vaccine development, efficacy, and safety and TAK-003 showed the greatest efficacy against hospitalized and symptomatic Dengue patients, reducing risk by 85% (RR 0.15; 95% CI 0.07–0.33).

Introduction

Dengue poses a major global health challenge, affecting around millions of people annually [1] caused by Dengue Virus (DENV) and transmitted with the help of infected Aedes mosquitoes in tropical and subtropical regions [2]. The genome of Dengue virus is encoded into a single polyprotein, which is later fragmented into 10 proteins: 3 structural proteins (capsid [C], membrane [M], and envelope [E]) and 7 non-structural (NS) proteins (NS1, NS2A, NS2B, NS3, NS4A, NS4B, and NS5) [3]. Structural proteins play a critical role in Viral particle formation, maturation, and host-cell receptor binding, whereas NS proteins are primarily integral to the viral replication cycle, host pathology, and immune evasion strategies. DENV is classified into 4 serotypes designated DENV-1 to DENV-4. Further the symptoms of each serotype may lead to dengue infection, exhibiting a broad range of severity [4]. Importantly, infection with one serotype confers lifelong immunity only specific to that serotype, while subsequent infection by a heterologous serotype can result in more severe symptoms via antibody-dependent enhancement. (ADE) [5]. Identifying the clinical heterogeneity of dengue, the World Health Organization (WHO) categorized the disease into three categories in 2009: dengue without warning signs, dengue with warning signs, and severe dengue [6]. Symptoms range from mild febrile illness to severe complications such as dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF) and dengue shock syndrome (DSS), which can result in multi-organ failure and death. The severity of symptoms is influenced by multiple factors, including viral serotype, host immune status, etc. The absence of a targeted antiviral therapy for Dengue emphasizes the urgent need for effective prevention strategies. Currently, vector control remains the primary approach to reducing disease transmission. However, this method independently has proven insufficient in controlling dengue outbreaks, highlighting the necessity for an efficacious vaccine. Dengue vaccine development is particularly challenging due to the need for effective defenses across each of the DENV serotypes to prevent ADE and severe disease upon subsequent infections. Several dengue vaccine candidates have been developed, with the 1st approved vaccine, CYD-TDV (Dengvaxia), showing partial efficacy and restricted recommendations for seropositive individuals as in the absence of previous dengue exposure, the CYD-TDV vaccine partially mimics primary infection and increases the risk of severe dengue during subsequent infection [7]. In terms of safety, immunogenicity, and serotype coverage, several vaccine candidates such as live attenuated, inactivated, subunit, and DNA-based vaccines are in different phases of clinical research. Each has unique benefits and drawbacks. Ensuring long-term protection while mitigating the risk of vaccine-induced ADE remains a crucial focus in vaccine research. The goal of this review is to offer a thorough examination of the current landscape of dengue vaccine development, highlighting the clinical trials, efficacy, and their safety results from the last five-year results.

Systematic analysis of studies

Background

Various studies have been conducted till now to evaluate and find the most significant and efficient vaccines for Dengue. Therefore, we carried out a comprehensive analysis of different studies conducted in the past and extracted efficacy and safety data of the vaccines in clinical trials. Our strategy comprised a thorough review of the literature that covered a range of clinical trials, including clinical trials phase I, II, and III, written in English and published in English-language journals in last five year (2020–2025). This review followed the guidelines of Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) [8].

Method

To search the articles related Dengue vaccines, a total of 3 database: PubMed, Cochrane and google scholar; were searched on 19 February, 2025. Search results of PubMed and Cochrane were stored in library of their respective databases. A thorough literature search accompanied four rounds of rigorous scanning and screening in the data selection procedure.

Inclusion criteria

The studies including the clinical trials and published in last five year and those relevant as per the scope of this systematic review were taken as the part of the research without depending on the participants age and their geographical location.

Exclusion criteria

Review articles, conference publications, systematic review were excluded from the study. Also, the studies including concurrent infections were excluded out from the study.

Clinical question

What are the safety and effectiveness of the various dengue vaccines on the basis of their clinical trials result compared to placebo and which vaccine demonstrates the highest effectiveness?

Results

We conducted a comprehensive search for studies published in the last five years, identifying a total of 525 relevant articles. Following an initial screening, we excluded studies that were unrelated to Dengue, including focused on vaccine effects for other diseases and those examining co-infections with other flaviviruses, such as Zika and dengue. This screening reduced the selection to 108 studies. We further removed duplicate studies identified from different databases, narrowing the selection to 67 studies. Following that, a final screening was conducted, and we excluded studies that had only recently initiated and lacked data. As a result, 14 studies were found eligible for inclusion in our systematic review. Figure 1 depicts the study selection procedure. After the thorough data extraction from the screened and shortlisted studies, for a better understanding and comparison of the most efficient vaccine, all the data given in studies was incorporated into their respective tables and forest plots were made for a better presentation of the vaccine efficacy and safety report of each trial. The majority of studies focused on clinical trials of TAK-003, CYD-TDV, TV005, TV003. In addition to the aforementioned vaccines, we also identified 2 studies on the Butantan-DV and 1 study on dengue tetravalent vaccine. Even though the data from these trials is available, it was not practical to build a forest plot for these vaccines because of the small number of studies.

Effect measures and meta-analytical model

All statistical analyses were performed in R (v4.3.3) using the meta package (8.0.2), a comprehensive toolkit supporting fixed- and random-effects models, forest and funnel plotting, subgroup and sensitivity analyses, and bias diagnostics. Dichotomous outcomes (risk of dengue, serious adverse events) were synthesized as risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). We employed the DerSimonian–Laird random-effects model to accommodate between-study variation in true effect sizes, reflecting that studies differ beyond sampling error. Between-study heterogeneity was quantified using the I² statistic and Cochran’s Q test, with I² ≥ 50% indicating substantial heterogeneity. These measures help determine whether observed variability is beyond chance alone and guide interpretation of pooled estimates. We conducted pre-specified subgroup analyses by vaccine platform (TAK-003, CYD-TDV, TV005), serotype (for CYD-TDV), and age strata (< 16 vs. ≥ 16 years), using the χ² test for subgroup differences provided by the meta package. Subgroup analyses can elucidate sources of heterogeneity and explore effect modifiers. Sensitivity analyses excluded studies at high risk of bias and those with follow-up < 6 months.

TAK-003

To provide immunity to each of 4 dengue virus serotypes (DENV-1 to DENV-4), Takeda Vaccines created the live attenuated tetravalent dengue vaccine TAK-003 [9]. Engineered using a DENV-2 backbone and recombinant components of the other three serotypes, TAK-003 produces strong immunological responses mediated by humoral and T cells [10]. A pivotal five-year clinical trial involving over 20,000 children and adolescents demonstrated its efficacy in preventing severe dengue and reducing hospitalizations [11]. The European Medicines Agency approved TAK-003 in December 2022, recognizing its promise in dengue-endemic regions, followed by WHO’s Strategic Advisory Group of Experts (SAGE) endorsing its use without pre-vaccination screening in high-burden areas. TAK-003 induces neutralizing antibodies particularly effective against DENV-2 with cross-reactivity to other serotypes, enhanced by prior dengue exposure. Additionally, it stimulates memory B and T cells, contributing to sustained protection. A key benefit of TAK-003 is its capacity to reduce viremia upon subsequent dengue exposure, thereby lowering the risk of serious consequences like dengue hemorrhagic fever and dengue shock syndrome by preventing excessive inflammatory responses. While most efficacious in previously infected individuals, TAK-003 also provides substantial protection in dengue-naïve populations. Table 1 summarizes data from the clinical trials conducted over the past five years for TAK-003, while Fig. 2(a) presents a detailed analysis of its vaccine efficacy and safety profile. Pooling three RCTs (24 555 vaccinees, 12 359 controls), TAK-003 reduced risk of symptomatic or hospitalized Dengue by 85% (RR 0.15; 95% CI 0.07–0.33; I² = 95.9%). Immunogenicity analysis (1 224 vaccinees, 692 controls) showed a fourfold higher neutralizing antibody response versus placebo (RR 4.05; 95% CI 2.09–7.85; I² = 96.5%). Safety data (13 604 vaccinees, 6 819 controls) demonstrated a modest reduction in SAEs or severe solicited AEs (RR 0.85; 95% CI 0.75–0.96; I² = 0%). Subgroup testing confirmed significant differences across domains (χ² = 41.08, df = 2, p < 0.0001).

CYD-TDV

Sanofi Pasteur created CYD-TDV (Dengvaxia), the first approved live attenuated tetravalent dengue vaccine, incorporating dengue virus structural genes into a yellow fever virus (YFV-17D) backbone [17]. It yields potent cellular and humoral immunological reactions, such as neutralizing antibodies and CD4+/CD8 + T cells, providing significant protection in seropositive individuals. However, in seronegative individuals, vaccine-induced antibodies may trigger ADE, heightening the chances of severe dengue [18]. Consequently, WHO recommends its use only in those with confirmed prior dengue infection. Despite these limitations, CYD-TDV is a valuable tool in endemic regions, with pre-vaccination screening essential for safe deployment. Table 2 presents a comprehensive overview of clinical trial data for CYD-TDV while Fig. 2(b) shows a detailed assessment of its efficacy and safety profile. From six RCT arms in three trials (combined N not reported here), CYD-TDV achieved a 66% reduction in any virologically confirmed dengue (RR 0.34; 95% CI 0.23–0.44; I² = 95.7%). Serotype-specific efficacy varied markedly (from non-significant for DENV-1 to 93% for DENV-4). Safety (Reynales et al.) showed fewer SAEs in vaccine versus control arms (RR 0.40; 95% CI 0.28–0.53; I² = 97.1%). Significant heterogeneity and a strong serotype effect highlight the need for serotype-targeted evaluations (χ² = 57.47, df = 1, p < 0.0001).

TV005 and TV003

Live attenuated tetravalent dengue vaccine candidates TV003 and TV005, produced by the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID), were developed for providing adequate protection to each 4 DENV serotypes [22]. Engineered to overcome limitations of earlier vaccines, particularly the risk of ADE in seronegative individuals associated with CYD-TDV, these candidates utilize genetically modified dengue virus strains with targeted deletions to ensure safety and immunogenicity. TV005 has a greater DENV-2 component dosage, enhancing immunogenicity compared to TV003 [23]. Strong humoral and cellular immunological responses, including as neutralizing antibodies and TC and TH activation, are elicited by both vaccinations. TNF-α and IFN-γ induction further enhances long-term memory and strong cellular immunity. Crucially, TV003 and TV005 demonstrate efficacy in both seropositive and seronegative individuals without requiring pre-vaccination screening. Their single-dose regimen and robust, durable protection position them as highly promising candidates for large-scale dengue prevention efforts globally. Table 3 summarizes data from the clinical trials conducted over the past five years for TV005 and TV003, while Fig. 2(c) presents a detailed analysis of its vaccine efficacy and safety profile. Two challenge-based RCTs (51 vaccinees, 41 controls) demonstrated near‐complete protection against DENV-2/3 viremia and rash (pooled RR 0.02; 95% CI 0.00–0.15; I² = 0%). Reactogenicity data (n = 556 vaccinees vs. 224 controls) indicated an increased risk of any solicited AE (RR 1.37; 95% CI 1.13–1.65; I² = 6.7%), driven by rash, fever, and arthralgia. Subgroup differences between efficacy and safety were significant (χ² = 17.53, df = 1, p < 0.0001).

Other vaccine trials

Studies on the Butantan-DV and Dengue tetravalent vaccine were also identified alongside the previously mentioned ones. Butantan DV vaccine, developed by the Brazilian Butantan Institute, is a live attenuated tetravalent dengue vaccine designed to offer balanced, strong immunity for each of the 4 DENV serotypes [26]. Based on the TV003/TV005 formulations, it has been optimized for safety and efficacy in dengue-endemic regions. Unlike CYD-TDV (Dengvaxia), which poses a risk of antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) in seronegative individuals, Butantan DV provides protection for both seropositive and seronegative individuals. The vaccine stimulates robust humoral and cellular immunity, producing neutralizing antibodies against the envelope (E) and precursor membrane (prM) proteins and activating CD4 + and CD8 + T cells. These responses reduce viral replication, prevent severe outcomes like dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF) and shock syndrome (DSS), and promote long-term immune memory. A key advantage is its prospective single-dose regimen, simplifying mass immunization efforts. Genetically engineered with reduced replication capacity, Butantan DV maintains high immunogenicity while ensuring safety, making it a promising tool for global Dengue control. Conversely, Panacea Biotec Ltd has created DengiAll, a tetravalent dengue vaccine. Immunity compared to all 4 Dengue virus serotypes is intended to be provided by this live-attenuated, lyophilized vaccine that is generated from cell cultures. The vaccine is presently in Phase 3 of clinical testing, which started in 2019. The data analysis of Butantan DV and DengiAll is shown in Table 4.

Discussion

Dengue remains a major worldwide health concern, with growing infection rate in endemic regions. The DENV infection at the initial stage may resolves on its own but at later stage it shows the severe complications like hemorrhagic fever, fluid accumulation and multiple organ failure, and these complications can result in increased mortality rate [30]. As proper treatment for dengue infection is not present so finding a safe and effective vaccine could be a better option for limiting the dengue infection. Subsequently, researchers have coordinated their endeavors towards making a dengue vaccine. Several dengue vaccines have undergone clinical evaluation to assess their efficacy and safety across different age groups. This systematic review analysed data from available clinical trials to compare the performance of various dengue vaccines in terms of both safety and efficacy and the results showed TAK-003 and TV005 showing the highest protection rates in their respective settings. Safety profiles varied: TAK-003 and CYD-TDV reduced serious events, whereas TV005 was associated with heightened reactogenicity. In contrast to CYD-TDV (Dengavaxia), which necessitates serostatus screening prior to administration due to elevated risk in seronegative individuals [13], TAK-003, a live-attenuated tetravalent dengue vaccine, demonstrated consistently high efficacy across multiple age groups. Early trials of CYD-TDV showed lower efficacy against DENV-2 due to uneven immunogenicity across serotypes, with DENV-2 often dominating the immune response. In order to improve balanced immunity across all four serotypes, a DENV-2 backbone was used in later vaccine development to overcome this problem [10]. TAK-003 demonstrated protective efficacy regardless of baseline serostatus. Because of this feature, TAK-003 is a safer and more accessible choice for usage by a larger population, especially in endemic areas where serostatus testing would not be practical. Also, TAK-003 showed a favourable profile in terms of vaccination safety, with few serious side events documented throughout trials. Mild to severe side effects, including as fever and injection site responses, were the most frequent and self-limiting adverse events. Apart from TAK-003, TV003/TV005, were also evaluated with encouraging findings; but they did not have the comprehensive clinical data as of TAK-003. TDV’s overall effectiveness was limited by its moderate efficacy but lesser immunogenicity against specific serotypes. Limitations

While the insights of efficacy and safety of dengue vaccines are provided in the article, several limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, long-term follow-up studies on larger population is necessary to assess the durability of protection and potential disappearing immunity over time. Additionally, the vaccine’s real-world performance in diverse populations, particularly in regions with varying dengue transmission intensities, requires further evaluation. Also, cost-effectiveness and large-scale implementation strategies must be considered to ensure widespread accessibility in resource-limited settings. Another limitation is that sometimes important studies are published in other languages, such as the Butantan-DV vaccine studies in Portuguese, and their translation into other languages may result in a loss of scientific impact. Along with all above-mentioned limitations of the study, another limitation is that some of the studies have relatively short follow-up periods, so the data may vary depending on the time of follow-up period.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study represents TAK-003 vaccine with higher vaccine efficacy and safety. Its broad protective efficacy and safety profile support its potential role as a leading dengue vaccine candidate for mass immunization programs, contributing to the global effort to control dengue transmission and reduce disease burden. Despite the limitations, this systematic review highlights the significant progress made in dengue vaccine development and highlights the potential of TAK-003 as a key tool in dengue prevention strategies. High heterogeneity owing to differences in trial design, serotype distributions, challenge versus field endpoints, and assay methods limits cross-platform comparisons. Future research should prioritize head-to-head trials, long-term follow-up for durability and safety, serotype-specific effectiveness, and real-world performance in endemic populations to inform optimal vaccine deployment strategies.

Favorable outcomes of SGLT2 inhibitor use in pacemaker recipients: a population-based study
Authors: Yuval Avidan, Asaf Danon, Dana Hadar, Amir Aker, Amir Yahav, Oren Caspi, Sameer Kassem
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPacemaker recipients are predisposed to heart failure (HF), yet evidence guiding preventive pharmacotherapy in this population remains unexplored. Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) have redefined HF management across a broad spectrum of cardiometabolic phenotypes. This study evaluated the association between SGLT2i therapy and clinical outcomes after pacemaker implantation for atrioventricular block.MethodsPatients receiving conventional pacemakers between 2016 and 2024 were retrospectively analyzed and stratified by baseline SGLT2i therapy. Exclusions included sinus node dysfunction or iatrogenic pacing indications, single-chamber devices, and estimated glomerular filtration rate < 20 mL/min/1.73m2. Events within 3 months post-implantation were omitted to reduce peri-procedural confounding. After propensity score matching, Cox regression assessed associations between SGLT2i use and all-cause mortality and HF hospitalization.ResultsAmong 11,518 eligible patients, propensity score matching yielded two well-balanced cohorts of 1,226 SGLT2i users and non-SGLT2i users. Over three years, death occurred in 124 (10.1%) in the SGLT2i users and in 194 (15.8%) in the non-SGLT2i group (hazard ratio [HR], 0.62; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.50 to 0.78; P < 0.001). HF hospitalization occurred in 132 (10.7%) in the SGLT2i group, and in 280 (22.8%) in the non-SGLT2i group (HR, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.41 to 0.62; P < 0.001). Subgroup analyses demonstrated consistent effects across strata.ConclusionsSGLT2i therapy was associated with reduced risk of all-cause mortality and HF–related hospitalizations following pacemaker implantation. Introduction

With the aging of the global population, the prevalence of permanent pacemaker implantation is steadily rising [1]. Chronic right ventricular (RV) pacing—even in the presence of preserved atrioventricular (AV) synchrony [2] —can contribute to left ventricular (LV) dysfunction and the subsequent development of heart failure (HF) [3]. These phenomena are believed to stem, at least in part, from pacing-induced dyssynchrony—an electrical activation pattern closely resembling that of left bundle branch block [4]—and are further compounded by disruptions in myocardial perfusion alongside intricate humoral, cellular, and molecular derangements [5–7]. Pacing-induced cardiomyopathy (PICM) has been heterogeneously defined across studies [8–10], characterized by a postimplantation decline in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), with an estimated incidence of 10% to 20% within 2 to 4 years [9, 11, 12]. Although a cumulative RV pacing burden exceeding 20% has been linked to adverse remodeling and PICM [11, 13], no universally accepted threshold for 'safe' pacing has been established [3]. It is further postulated that pacing-related HF may manifest with preserved systolic function, characterized by elevated cardiac filling pressures preceding a measurable decline in LVEF [14].

The emergence of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) has revolutionized pharmacotherapy across a broad spectrum of cardiometabolic conditions [15–17], and these agents are now endorsed by society guidelines as foundational therapy for HF, irrespective of LVEF [15, 16]. Their cardiovascular benefits extend beyond glycemic control and are mediated by reverse LV remodeling, reduced filling pressures, and improved myocardial energetics and fibrosis [18–21]. This raises the hypothesis that SGLT2i may mitigate the adverse structural and electrophysiological remodeling induced by conventional pacemakers. However, the literature remains limited, and to date, no prior data are available in this setting. The present study investigates whether treatment with SGLT2i is associated with improved clinical outcomes in patients undergoing pacemaker implantation for AV block.

Methods

Study population

This observational cohort study is based on data from the national Clalit Health Services (CHS) database, the largest health maintenance organization in Israel, providing medical care to approximately 4.6 million members. The electronic medical records of CHS integrate information from multiple sources, including primary care physicians, community specialty clinics, hospitalizations, laboratories, and pharmacies. Full access to electronic health records enabled the collection of detailed clinical, laboratory, and administrative data for the study population. We identified all adult patients (aged > 18 years) who underwent de novo pacemaker implantation between January 1, 2016, and April 1, 2024. Patients were stratified into SGLT2i users and non-users based on peri-implantation medication dispensation. Individuals with at least one SGLT2i dispensation within 90 days before or after device implantation were classified as SGLT2i users at discharge. All SGLT2i formulations available in our country during the study period were included, including dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, and their respective combinations with metformin. To avoid immortal time bias and to minimize peri-procedural confounding, follow-up commenced after a 90-day 'blinding period', during which patients experiencing any study endpoints were excluded from the analysis. Patients were excluded if they had any of the following: (i) implantation of cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) pacemakers or defibrillators or conduction system pacing (ii) leadless pacemakers; (iii) implantable cardioverter-defibrillators; (iv) sinus node dysfunction as the pacing indication; (v) ambiguous procedural coding, such as cases involving device replacement, revision, or upgrade; (vi) iatrogenic indications—defined as device insertion within one month following cardiac surgery or transcatheter aortic valve replacement; (vii) contraindications to SGLT2i, including an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) below 20 mL/min/1.73 m2, a history of recurrent urinary tract infections, prior episode of diabetic ketoacidosis, or type 1 diabetes; and (viii) implantation of single-chamber devices. The study received approval from the institutional ethics committee (007–25-CMC) and was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Endpoints

The study endpoints included all-cause mortality and hospitalization for HF, assessed up to 3-year follow-up. Hospitalizations for HF were identified using ICD-9 codes 428.0 (congestive HF, unspecified), 428.1 (left HF), and 428.20 (unspecified systolic HF). Mortality data were sourced from the national social security records; however, details regarding the specific causes of death were not available.

Power calculation

The sample size was calculated based on the reported prevalence of pacing-induced cardiomyopathy, which develops in approximately 10–20% of patients within 2–4 years of implantation [3]. Assuming a 95% confidence level, a 2% margin of error, and an estimated prevalence of 15%, the required sample size was calculated to be approximately 1,225 individuals.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics by SGLT2i treatment status were summarized as n (%) for categorical variables and median (interquartile range, 25th–75th percentile) for continuous variables. Between-group differences were assessed using the chi-square test for categorical variables and Student’s t-test for continuous variables. The association between SGLT2i use and all-cause mortality was evaluated using Cox proportional hazards regression. To account for baseline imbalances, propensity scores (PS) were calculated via nonparsimonious multivariable logistic regression, with SGLT2i use as the dependent variable. Covariates included all variables (Table 1) differing between groups: age, sex, obesity (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2), smoking status, hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes mellitus, atrial fibrillation, prior stroke, coronary artery disease (CAD), chronic kidney disease (CKD), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), prior heart failure admission, Charlson comorbidity index, socioeconomic status, anemia (hemoglobin < 13 g/dL for men and < 12 g/dL for women), and discharge treatments (anticoagulation, antiplatelets, statins, renin–angiotensin system [RAS] inhibitors, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists [MRA], angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors [ARNI], beta-blockers, and diuretics). Propensity score matching was performed with 1:1 matching, using a caliper of 0.2 standard deviations for the PS. Standardized mean differences (SMDs) for all covariates were calculated before and after matching, with SMD < 10% considered indicative of adequate balance (Table 2). In the matched cohort, the impact of SGLT2i use on mortality and HF hospitalization was assessed using a stratified Cox regression model, with results expressed as hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). To assess HF hospitalization, we employed a competing risks regression (Fine–Gray), with death treated as the competing event. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals and log–log survival plots, with no meaningful violations detected. Kaplan–Meier curves were generated to illustrate the effect of SGLT2i use on study endpoints. Subgroup analyses using Cox models with interaction terms assessed whether the effect of SGLT2i varied across baseline variables. These analyses were designed to identify potential heterogeneity of treatment effect and to generate hypotheses for future research; therefore, no Bonferroni correction was applied, as the focus was on detecting possible effect modification rather than strictly controlling for multiple comparisons. Sensitivity analyses were performed to confirm the robustness of the results. First, an inverse probability of treatment weighting analysis based on the same PS model was conducted. Weighted Cox proportional hazards models were then fitted with robust variance estimation. Second, a multivariable Cox regression model was applied to the original (unmatched) cohort, adjusting for all variables included in the PS model. All analyses were conducted in R (version 4.2.3), and statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

During the study period, 18,804 individuals underwent pacemaker implantation, of whom 11,518 met the eligibility criteria, excluding those with pacing for sinus node dysfunction, iatrogenic indications, single-chamber devices, eGFR < 20 mL/min/1.73 m2, or events within 90 days post-implantation. Figure 1 outlines the inclusion process and group allocation. Before matching, the non-SGLT2i group included 10,275 patients and the SGLT2i group 1,243 patients, with substantial baseline differences between groups (Table 1). After propensity score matching, two well-balanced cohorts of 1,226 patients each were obtained (Table 2). These were older individuals (mean age 76.2 years), predominantly male (63.7% vs. 62.5%), mostly diabetic (79.8% vs. 79.0%), with similar rates of pre-existing HF (47.1% vs. 46.6%), and CKD in one-third. Baseline medication use was also comparable.

Flowchart of the study population inclusion process and cohort classification. eGFR: estimated glomerular filtration rate, SGLT2i: sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitor

Over three years, mortality occurred in 124 patients (10.1%) in the SGLT2i group compared with 194 (15.8%) in the non-SGLT2i group (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.50–0.78; P < 0.001; Fig. 2). HF hospitalization occurred in 132 patients (10.7%) in the SGLT2i group versus 280 patients (22.8%), the non-SGLT2i group (HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.41–0.62; P < 0.001; Fig. 3). Subgroup analysis of mortality (Fig. 4) revealed consistent effects across most strata, with the exception of attenuated survival benefit in patients > 75 years and greater benefit among those with low eGFR. For HF hospitalization (Fig. 5), no significant differences were observed across subgroups. Note that some strata (e.g., CAD, AF, hypertension) have wide confidence intervals, warranting cautious interpretation. Sensitivity analyses were conducted using inverse probability of treatment weighting and by evaluating the results of the unmatched cohort. Both approaches yielded results consistent in direction and magnitude with the primary PS matched analysis. The results of these analyses are presented in online supplemental section.

Discussion

In this preliminary population-based cohort of pacemaker recipients with AV block, we examined the association between baseline SGLT2i treatment and clinical outcomes. After propensity score matching, SGLT2i use was linked to a 50% reduction in HF hospitalization and a 44% decrease in mortality over three years post-implantation. These findings align with the well-established benefits of SGLT2i across a wide cardiovascular spectrum, including all HF phenotypes regardless of LVEF [22–24]. Although the mechanisms greatly differ, the demonstrated cardioprotective effects of SGLT2i against anthracycline-induced cardiotoxicity provide a potential rationale for their benefit in pacemaker recipients [25, 26]. Given that conventional pacemakers may induce or exacerbate HF, our findings suggest that the cardioprotective and survival benefits of SGLT2i extend to this patient population. After matching, both groups included 80% diabetic patients, nearly half with prior HF, and one-third with CKD—key comorbidities with established indications for SGLT2i therapy [16, 27].

To date, the optimal management of PICM remains uncertain, and the application of guideline-directed HF therapy depends on the extent of LVEF impairment. Preventive measures focus on device configuration and include algorithms designed to minimize unnecessary RV pacing by promoting intrinsic AV conduction—such as low base-rate programming, AV hysteresis, and atrial-based pacing modes that switch to ventricular pacing only with significant AV conduction loss [28]. Physiological pacing strategies—such as CRT and conduction system pacing—can restore coordinated cardiac contraction and relaxation, thereby mitigating [3, 29] or even reversing PICM in appropriately selected patients [11, 30, 31]. For selected candidates with pre-exsisting systolic dysfunction, physiologic pacing is preferred over conventional RV pacing [13, 29]. However, the role of pharmacotherapy in preventing HF among pacemaker recipients remains inadequately defined. Agha et al. demonstrated that patients undergoing chronic RV pacing who received standard HF therapy—particularly a combination of RAS, MRA and beta-blockers—exhibited the lowest incidence of PICM [32]. Beta-blocker therapy is also suggested to confer a survival advantage in HF patients with paced rhythms [33]. Among diabetics, SGLT2i use was associated with improved CRT responsiveness and fewer HF hospitalizations when combined with glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists [34], and with reduced ventricular tachycardia, inappropriate shocks, and HF admissions in ICD carriers [35]. Further evidence from combined human and animal studies suggested that statins may confer cardiovascular benefits in pacemaker recipients with AV block [36]. In this context, our findings underscore the potential value of proactive therapeutic strategies aimed at reducing pacemaker-related HF and mortality, rather than adopting a reactive approach once adverse events have occurred. While causality cannot be established, our findings suggest that SGLT2i may confer protective effects by reducing both all-cause mortality and HF–related hospitalizations attributed to pacing. The divergence in outcomes between the two groups—marked by a reduction in HF events emerging roughly 6 months and in mortality at 12 months post-implantation—supports a potential causal relationship. Indeed, prior data indicate that the adverse clinical effects of pacing-induced ventricular dysfunction may occur not infrequently and within the early post-implantation period [37].

The potential pathophysiological basis for the benefits of SGLT2i in pacemaker recipients likely stems from the multifactorial mechanisms underlying the advantages observed in HF patients. Although reversal of LV remodeling and reduction in filling pressures are cardinal to the improved outcomes [18, 21], it alone is unlikely to fully explain the significant prognostic improvements associated with SGLT2i [19]. Their beneficial effects appear to stem largely from systemic hemodynamic improvements and complex metabolic shifts [38]. Notably, HF prompts a metabolic switch in the myocardium—from fatty acid oxidation to glucose utilization—which lowers oxygen demand but simultaneously restricts adenosine triphosphate production [39–41]. SGLT2i are hypothesized to facilitate a shift toward ketone metabolism, thereby optimizing myocardial energetics—an effect demonstrated in both animal [42] and human models [43]. Additional mechanisms may include improved endothelial function through enhanced nitric oxide bioavailability, leading to reduced reactive oxygen species [44], and modulation of enhanced autophagic flux as a key cardioprotective mechanism [45–47]. Additionally, as demonstrated in patients with myocardial infarction, these agents are believed to mitigate ischemia–reperfusion injury, reduce infarct size and microvascular obstruction, and attenuate apoptosis [48]. Another plausible mechanism is that SGLT2i reduce the harmful effects of tricuspid regurgitation [49], which can occur when the pacing lead damages the valve leaflets or subvalvular apparatus. Post-implantation significant regurgitation occurs in 10% to 39% of cases and is associated with poor outcomes [3].

Finally, subgroup analysis revealed no significant heterogeneity across populations, except for a mortality benefit among patients with eGFR < 45 mL/min/1.73 m2—consistent with the recognized cardioprotective effects of SGLT2i in this group [50, 51]—and a diminished benefit among those over 75 years of age, consistent with the expected age-related attenuation of treatment effect. As these analyses were exploratory and potentially underpowered, the findings should be interpreted with caution.

Limitations

Our findings should be interpreted with caution. Owing to the retrospective, observational design and use of database-derived data, no definitive causal relationship between SGLT2i therapy and the studied outcomes can be established. Despite strict coding standardization, the nature of the database may still introduce selection bias and misclassification. Although PS was used to account for many potential confounders, residual confounding from unmeasured variables—for instance, frailty, heterogeneity of pacing indications, and confounding by indication for SGLT2i prescription—remains possible. Furthermore, despite standardized national prescribing criteria for SGLT2i within the CHS system, reverse causation bias—where healthier patients are more likely to receive the therapy—cannot be excluded. In addition, the subgroup analyses were underpowered and should be considered hypothesis-generating only. The use of database-derived data limited access to granular clinical variables, including device configuration and RV pacing burden. Since echocardiographic indices were unavailable, our outcomes focused on clinical measures. Although central to PICM assessment, LVEF-based definitions may underestimate its prevalence, since RV pacing–related HF can occur despite preserved LVEF [52]. Moreover, SGLT2i are guideline-endorsed across the HF spectrum, regardless of systolic function [15, 16]. Our results merit further prospective clinical study with structured follow-up for echocardiographic reassessment to validate the impact of SGLT2i. Additionally, the analysis was restricted to a “class effect” of SGLT2i, and there is also no information about the cause of death. Finally, data on HF therapies in pacemaker recipients remain limited, limiting external validity.

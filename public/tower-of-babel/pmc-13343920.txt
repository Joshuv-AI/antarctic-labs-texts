Background

Accurately predicting patient outcomes and identifying robust therapeutic targets remain critical challenges in cancer diagnosis and treatment. The growing availability of cancer genomic datasets offers unprecedented opportunities to address this, particularly through the discovery of novel cancer biomarkers. Over decades of research aimed at understanding the complex mechanisms underlying cancer progression, the importance of cancer biomarkers has been widely recognized [1]. Biomarkers can be harnessed to elucidate the mechanisms of oncogenesis and develop targeted therapies, thereby enabling precision medicine. The identification of novel prognostic cancer biomarkers is further central to improving patient outcomes.

Advancements in genomic sequencing techniques, including improved accessibility and affordability, have led to a surge in cancer genomic datasets, providing invaluable resources for biomarker discovery. Multiple ambitious programs in the cancer field, such as The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA, RRID: SCR_003193), [2] the International Cancer Genome Consortium (ICGC, RRID: SCR_021722), [3] and the Therapeutically Applicable Research to Generate Effective Treatments (TARGET, RRID: SCR_014514), [4] have been launched over the past decade to generate a comprehensive molecular landscape of different cancers. Together, datasets from these and similar initiatives have provided access to omics data, coupled with clinical, demographic, and outcome information, for over 86,000 patients, readily accessible via the National Cancer Institute (NCI, RRID: SCR_011176) Genomic Data Commons (GDC, RRID: SCR_014514) [5] and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, RRID: SCR_005012) [6]. Despite these advancements, effectively leveraging these expansive datasets for robust biomarker discovery remains challenging, primarily due to their inherent complexity and the substantial bioinformatics expertise and computational resources required for their manipulation. This significant barrier underscores the pressing need for user-friendly computational tools that can empower researchers to efficiently mine these datasets and identify clinically relevant prognostic biomarkers.

In response to this need, various online platforms have emerged to facilitate survival analyses using gene expression data for prognostic biomarker discovery and validation [7–16]. While valuable, these existing tools remain largely limited in their analytical scope and flexibility (Fig. 1). Most notably, current platforms primarily support survival analyses focused on user-provided individual genes and gene sets, often derived from experimental or bulk sequencing data. Consequently, these platforms largely fail to capitalize on advancements in genomic technologies, such as single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq), which is uniquely capable of generating novel, cell-type-specific gene sets from single-cell clusters and gene expression networks for more precise biomarker discovery. Furthermore, existing tools typically lack advanced functionalities critical for complex survival analyses, including flexible cohort partitioning methods, parsing based on clinical attributes, and focused functional analyses. To overcome these limitations and bridge the gap between growing genomics data and accessible analysis, we introduce Survival Genie 2. This platform represents a significant redesign and expansion of our initial survival analysis tool, Survival Genie 1, [10] offering an enhanced and comprehensive online resource for survival analysis. Most notably, Survival Genie 2 uniquely enables researchers to readily harness their scRNA-seq data for the discovery of highly specific prognostic genes that can serve as powerful biomarkers or therapeutic targets. Specifically, our platform supports the identification of outcome-associated genes derived directly from single-cell cluster markers, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA, RRID: SCR_003302) modules, and intercellular signaling pairs. By extending beyond traditional analyses, this approach enables researchers to pinpoint specific cellular subpopulations, signaling pathways, and gene signatures that are robustly associated with patient survival, thereby generating more comprehensive and clinically relevant prognostic insights.

Beyond single-cell integration, Survival Genie 2 offers additional specialized capabilities designed to enhance biomarker specificity and clinical relevance. Specifically, the platform enables the parsing of user input gene sets into biologically relevant categories, including long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), transcription factors (TFs), and ligand-receptor (L-R) signaling genes. This novel approach is crucial as gene sets derived from cluster marker, network, or pathway enrichment analyses can be extensive, spanning a diverse array of molecular products and functions. However, the ability to filter input gene sets to a select functional theme allows users to focus on biologically relevant molecules critical for the regulation of oncogenesis and tumor progression. Importantly, our results demonstrate that refining gene set inputs in this manner can significantly improve hazard ratios, leading to the identification of more robust biomarkers and promising therapeutic targets with greater translational potential.

Moreover, Survival Genie 2 distinguishes itself by hosting the most comprehensive databases to date, encompassing 132 datasets from 53 cancer genome projects across 38 distinct malignancies (Fig. 1). This extensive collection notably includes both adult and pediatric cancer datasets, with the unique capability to subset mixed datasets into age-specific cohorts utilizing patient demographic data. The tool further provides versatile partitioning methods (e.g., median, mean, and optimal cut-point calculations), offering users control and flexibility in their analyses. Analysis results are generated through univariate Cox proportional hazards modeling, producing high-quality, publication-ready Kaplan–Meier plots and integrated forest plots that clearly illustrate the survival associations of the selected gene(s).

Finally, Survival Genie 2 uniquely bridges the interpretive gap between publicly available bulk sequencing datasets and user-derived cell-type-specific targets from scRNA-seq data. It accomplishes this by correlating the user input gene expression with predicted immune cell enrichment, estimated through deconvolution analysis on the selected cancer dataset. This multifaceted approach not only reflects the biological complexity of the tumor microenvironment but also provides a novel framework for identifying key molecular drivers that are often obscured in conventional bulk analyses, thereby uncovering new avenues for prognostication and therapeutic development.

Collectively, by seamlessly integrating these innovative features, our next-generation platform empowers researchers to perform more complex survival analyses for the identification of highly robust prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. We demonstrate the capabilities and utilities of these key features herein, aimed at enhancing survival analyses to improve patient outcomes across the spectrum of cancers. Survival Genie 2 is open-source and available online at https://bhasinlab.bmi.emory.edu/SurvivalGenie2/home.

Implementation

Data collection

To generate our database, Survival Genie 2 collected all available clinical outcome and gene expression profiling data directly through the GDC data portal (RRID: SCR_014514) [17] using the GenomicDataCommons R package [18]. Due to the lack of consensus on cross-platform data harmonization, Survival Genie 2 uses the normalized read count data (FPKM values) from the NCI GDC. The datasets were updated from GENCODE version 22 to GENCODE version 36 (RRID: RRID: SCR_014966) with the expanded lncRNA annotations and more up-to-date gene names, based on updates available from the GDC. Although all datasets retrieved from the GDC had undergone standard quality control prior to deposition, we performed additional filtering to ensure each dataset included complete and standardized clinical annotations. Specifically, we required that every sample have matched gene expression and clinical outcome data, as well as valid entries for patient age, disease type, tumor descriptor, and primary tissue site, and that samples not be duplicated. Datasets with > 20 samples meeting these quality control requirements were retained in the final Survival Genie 2 database.

The labels for the sample metadata were standardized to enable parsing of the dataset based on these criteria. Three tumor types (primary, recurrent, and metastatic) and others (non-specified tumor type or normal tissues) are included as descriptors for filtering the samples to be used in the survival analysis, where available. Based on the primary tissue and tumor type, projects are further divided; for example, the TARGET-ALL dataset has T-ALL and B-ALL subsets, and the TARGET-AML dataset has peripheral blood and bone marrow subsets.

To enable the survival analyses focusing on lncRNAs, transcription factors and ligand-receptor pairs, the supported gene symbols and pairs, the curated gene symbols were assembled from established resources. lncRNAs symbols were retrieved from GENCODE v36, [19] transcription factors were obtained from the pySCENIC database (RRID: SCR_025802), [20] and ligand-receptor pairs were sourced from CellChatDB (RRID: SCR_021946) [21]. To ensure consistency and compatibility, only transcription factors and ligand-receptor gene symbols present in the GENCODE v36 [19] reference were included in Survival Genie 2.

Survival analysis

Two kinds of survival analysis are supported, namely overall survival (OS) analysis and event-free survival (EFS); both analyses are powered by the survival R package (RRID: SCR_026244) based on the availability of clinical outcomes [22, 23]. To perform survival analysis, the patient samples in the selected dataset are first partitioned into either the “high” or “low” group based on the expression or enrichment score. The expression scores are analysis-dependent: (a) single-gene analysis uses log count expression for the gene as the score, (b) gene ratio uses the log count ratio for the user input genes as the score, (c) gene-set, cluster maker, and module-based analyses use the gene-set enrichment score derived from gene set variation analysis (GSVA, RRID: SCR_021058), [24] specifically gene-set contrast analysis will use the difference between the enrichment score of two gene-sets, and (d) ligand-receptor signaling analysis inferred signaling strength as the product of geometric means of ligand genes and receptor genes as a simplified version of existing methods that will emphasize the expression of both ligands and receptors, that the zero expression of subunits will lead to an inactive result [21]. The cohort is partitioned into 'high' or 'low' groups depending on whether the corresponding score is above or below the cut-point value, as determined by the method selected by the user. Supported cut point methods include mean, median, percentile, and “optimal cut-point” from the maxstat R Package (RRID: SCR_025679). The Kaplan–Meier survival models for both OS and EFS are estimated using the ‘survfit’ function, and the between-group differences are examined with log-rank tests. Additionally, the Cox proportional hazards regression model is applied to offer the hazard ratio and its significance with the ‘coxph’ function from the survival package. A log-rank p-value cutoff of 0.05 is applied to determine whether the survival association is significant, and p-values < 0.05 are considered significant. The correlation of the input gene(s) with specific immune cells is predicted using CIBERSORT (RRID: SCR_016955) [12].

Ligand-receptor based analysis

Survival Genie 2 introduced ligand-receptor (L-R) pair-based analysis, adapting methodologies for signaling strength quantification from single-cell tools such as Cell Chat [21]. While methods like GSVA can effectively measure overall gene set enrichment, they can be disproportionately influenced by the high expression of a single dominant gene or remain insensitive to the absence of specific key genes. In contrast, L-R based analysis enforces to measure the co-expression of ligands and receptors by using the product of geometric means, which emphasizes concurrent expression and ensures that no signaling score is assigned if either the ligand or receptor are not expressed. Platform design and development

The Survival Genie analysis is performed in the R programming language, and the web app is built under the Plotly Dash framework (RRID: SCR_013991) in Python. The tool is compatible with most systems for server-side hosting (Linux, MacOS, Windows) and mainstream web browsers for viewing (Chrome, Edge, Safari, and Firefox). The source code of the project is publicly available on GitHub https://github.com/bhasin-lab/SurvivalGenieV2.

User-uploaded data management

Survival Genie 2 platform adopted multiple methods to ensure data security. All data transfers are secured over HTTPS with TLS encryption. During the analysis process, the input file and generated results are kept on a Linux Unified Key Setup (LUKS) encrypted disk. Upon analysis completion, while the generated results will be saved and remain retrievable via analysis ID for 24 h, all uploaded raw data are automatically removed, thereby eliminating risks associated with long-term data storage. Additionally, the requested user input files do not require uploading any files containing sensitive information (e.g., patient identifiers or other HIPAA-protected data).

Use case dataset acquisition

To illustrate the clinical utility of Survival Genie 2, we performed three example analysis using different functions supported by the platform.

In use case 1, lncRNAs were examined in all supported datasets with primary tumors, the datasets and sample size information are provided in Additional file1: Table S1.

In use case 2, gene regulatory networks were performed on multiple myeloma plasma cell dataset from Pilcher, et al. [25], available through the NCBI Bioproject (PRJNA765009). This study includes 48 CD138− bone marrow samples collected at the time of disease diagnosis from 18 patients, categorized as either rapid progressors (progression-free survival (PFS) < 18 months, n = 9) or non- progressors (PFS > 4 years, n = 9). The modules were examined in MMRF-ComMMpass study primary tumor bone marrow dataset (n = 764, events = 154).

In use case 3, cell–cell communication analysis were conducted between B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) blast cells and T cells from Bailur, McCachren [26], available in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) under accession number GSE154109. This dataset profiled BM mononuclear cells (BMMNCs) from 64 patients who were diagnosed with acute leukemia (B-ALL; n = 36, AML; n = 28), and 11 healthy donors (HD). The ligand-receptor pairs were examined in B-ALL TARGET-ALL-P2 (n = 141, events = 65).

Use case 1

As illustrated in use case 1, survival outcomes of lncRNAs are tested across the primary dataset (Additional file1: Table S1) with the optimal cut-point method. The p-values are Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) adjusted dataset-wise. Only lncRNAs expressed in at least one dataset and datasets with at least one significant lncRNA are included in the analysis. We then rank lncRNAs based on the frequency with which each gene demonstrated a statistically significant association with survival across the primary tumor datasets. Specifically, we calculated the number of datasets in which the lncRNA was associated with worse outcomes and subtracted this from the number of datasets in which it was associated with better outcomes. We selected the top 20 lncRNAs (including ties) associated with worse outcomes (highest ranked scores) and the top 10 lncRNAs (including ties) associated with better outcomes (lowest ranked scores) for visualization. Both lncRNAs and datasets are hierarchically clustered based on the log hazard ratio.

Use case 2

To perform the module-based analysis in use case 2, pySCENIC (RRID: SCR_025802) gene regulatory analysis was performed on the multiple myeloma plasma cell dataset from Pilcher, et al. [25]. In addition to the QC metrics reported in the original dataset, we removed cells with < 200 unique genes, < 500 UMI reads, and > 30% mitochondrial UMIs, and > 10,000 UMI reads. Patients were classified into progressors (patients with a progression event in < 4 years) and non-progressors (patients with a progression event in > 4 years). To obtain the top regulatory modules, PySCENIC [20] identified co-expression modules, and refined transcription factor–target interactions through motif enrichment analysis, and calculated regulon activity scores. The identified regulons were ranked by clinical group specificity scores, and downstream survival analysis were performed on Survival Genie 2 within the MMRF-CoMMpass study (RRID: SCR_003721).

Use case 3

Cell Chat [21] analysis was performed on the B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) blast cells and T cells from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) dataset GSE154109 [26]. The signaling strength of all human signaling pathways from CellChatDB (RRID: SCR_021946) [21] were examined and only communications examined in over 10 cells were included. The communications were examined between B-ALL blasts, T cells and within each group. All signaling pathways showing significant communications were extracted along with the signaling ligand-receptors. For each ligand-receptor pair, the L-R based analysis were performed on Survival Genie 2. For all signaling pathways, the ligand-receptor genes were considered collectively as one gene set and performed gene set-based survival analysis; in the case where the genes were self-signaling, such as CD99 to CD99 signaling, single-gene analysis was performed instead. For signaling pathways mediated by multiple ligand-receptors, collective analysis was performed with all the ligands and receptors as one signaling pathway as well. The results were visualized in forest plot and results with log-rank p-value < 0.05 were considered significant.

Results

Overview of the Survival Genie 2 pipeline

Survival Genie 2 provides an intuitive and user-friendly online platform designed with advanced features and a comprehensive database for enhanced survival analysis and publication-ready results (Fig. 2). To begin the analysis, users can input diverse data profiles, including canonical gene-based inputs such as single genes, gene ratios, gene sets, and gene set contrasts. Beyond these canonical gene-based inputs, Survival Genie 2 introduces a novel approach by uniquely harnessing user-generated scRNA-seq data to identify outcome-associated gene sets from single-cell cluster markers, weighted gene co-expression network modules, and intercellular signaling pairs, leveraging the advances in single-cell profiling for biomarker discovery. To further refine analyses and enhance translational relevance, the platform also uniquely enables users to filter gene set inputs to focus on lncRNAs, TFs, or L-R pairs, thereby concentrating on regulatory molecules potentially related to oncogenesis and improving prognostic capabilities. Users then select a query dataset from Survival Genie 2’s extensive cancer genomics database, which spans 132 datasets from 53 projects across 38 adult and pediatric malignancies, making it the largest database for online survival analysis. Users then have the ability to select from various partitioning methods, including median, mean, and optimal cut-point calculations, to partition the selected dataset cohort, offering greater control and flexibility in analyses. Utilizing these inputs, the web server performs univariate Cox proportional hazards modeling, correlating the expression of the candidate input gene(s) to patient outcomes in the dataset of interest and returning high-quality, publication-ready plots (e.g. Kaplan–Meier, forest plots), accompanied by a trackable analysis ID that remains valid for up to 24 h, facilitating result sharing, revisiting, and lowering the computational burden. Survival Genie 2 hosts the most comprehensive online database for survival analyses

To ensure broad applicability for cancer researchers, Survival Genie 2 integrates the most comprehensive collection of cancer genomics datasets currently available for survival analysis (Fig. 1). Sourced from all currently available cancer genomics datasets from the NCI GDC portal with greater than 20 samples, the database comprises 132 distinct datasets generated through 53 cancer genomics projects within 12 major programs, including TCGA, ICGC, and TARGET. In total, these datasets represent 38 distinct malignancies, notably encompassing both pediatric and adult cancers (Additional file 1: Table S1). Survival Genie 2 enables novel single-cell data integration for survival outcome association

Given the growing use of scRNA-seq, Survival Genie 2 introduces several novel analysis types to facilitate the identification of outcome-associated subclusters, and gene sets directly from user-generated single-cell data (Fig. 4). Specifically, users can readily perform cluster-marker, gene co-expression module, and intercellular signaling-based survival analyses, all designed to link cell-type-specific insights from single-cell data to survival outcomes in a target dataset. While pre-processing steps for identifying cluster markers, gene co-expression networks, and intercellular signaling pathways must be performed locally due to data heterogeneity and computational burden, Survival Genie 2 simplifies this upstream process by providing detailed tutorials and R-based wrapper functions, directly accessible from the platform and GitHub, to facilitate the generation of the desired inputs from any annotated single-cell dataset.Fig. 4Pipeline of module/network based analysis, starting from (a) local analysis of single-cell RNAseq data clustering, to (b.1) intercellular communication analysis, (b.2) co-expression network and (b.3) regulatory network analysis, to the (c) final survival analysis based on the networks, signaling pathways, and cluster markers.

Pipeline of module/network based analysis, starting from (a) local analysis of single-cell RNAseq data clustering, to (b.1) intercellular communication analysis, (b.2) co-expression network and (b.3) regulatory network analysis, to the (c) final survival analysis based on the networks, signaling pathways, and cluster markers.

For cluster-marker analysis, the users simply upload a CSV file of differential gene expression results from their single-cell clusters (e.g., from Seurat). Survival analysis is then performed individually on each cluster gene signature with the selected query dataset, generating plots that depict how high versus low expression of each signature (i.e., intra-cluster) and the enrichment of specific signatures (i.e., inter-cluster) correlate with survival in the cancer of interest. Similarly, for signaling-based analysis, users input L-R pairs identified via well-documented intercellular communication tools such as CellChat or CellPhoneDB. Survival Genie 2 then employs a novel methodology to examine the correlation between the inferred signaling strength of these L-R pairs in the query dataset (based on the co-expression of the pair) and survival outcomes. Lastly, for module-based survival analysis, a wrapper function for the R package hdWGCNA is provided to enable users to perform weighted-gene co-expression analysis (WGCNA) on a single-cell dataset and generate gene co-expression modules. A CSV file of these modules and their genes can then be directly used to analyze how increased or decreased expression of each individual module gene signature (i.e., intra-module) and module gene signature enrichments (i.e., inter-module) are associated with survival outcomes in the query dataset.

For all of these single-cell-derived inputs, Survival Genie 2 presents the individual survival associations for the respective cluster markers, L-R pairs, and co-expression modules in corresponding box plots, Kaplan–Meier plots, and a collective overview with a hazard ratio plot and a forest plot, where inputs with non-significant results can be filtered out. Additionally, to address the challenge of integrating cell-type-specific single-cell insights with bulk RNA-seq data (i.e., the query datasets), Survival Genie 2 directly bridges this gap by performing deconvolution analysis on the selected bulk query dataset. This process estimates the relative proportions of major immune cell types within the tumor microenvironment (TME) and calculates the expression of the user-input cluster markers, L-R pairs, or co-expression module genes within these imputed cell populations. This provides users with a critical visualization of how cell-type-specific gene signatures manifest in a bulk context, thereby enabling direct correlations between single-cell-derived features and patient outcomes within the complex TME, a capability essential for precise biomarker validation and functional inference.

Collectively, this integrated approach, implemented in Survival Genie 2, represents a novel and significant advancement in web-based survival analysis, extending beyond traditional individual gene-based analyses to fully leverage the dimensionality of single-cell data for the identification of robust outcome-associated gene sets from cluster markers, intercellular signaling, and co-expression networks.

Survival Genie 2 facilitates molecular-focused survival analysis for increased precision

Advances in genomic sequencing over the past two decades, particularly with the precision offered by scRNA-seq, have significantly broadened the search for novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets to include noncoding genes, transcription factors, and immunomodulatory molecules. These molecular classes are crucial for understanding the dysregulation of inter- and intracellular signaling within the TME underlying oncogenesis, tumor progression, and treatment responses. To align with these advancements and enhance the interpretability of survival associations, Survival Genie 2 provides users with the capability to filter any input gene set to focus specifically on lncRNAs, TFs, and L-R pairs. For instance, users can apply this filter to their cluster-marker and gene co-expression module inputs, thereby refining analyses to regulatory and signaling gene enrichment. This targeted approach is critical, as focusing on these key molecular classes enhances the ability to identify biomarkers that are more directly related to oncogenesis and tumor progression, overcoming the dilution effect of including irrelevant genes in large, diverse gene sets. Survival Genie 2 accomplishes this by matching input genes against our database, comprised of 4,420 lncRNAs, 1,827 TFs, and 1,939 L-R pairs from GENCODE v36, [19] pySCENIC, [20] and CellChatDB [21], respectively. Furthermore, the platform supports the input of custom gene pairs for highly tailored L-R analyses, providing users with greater capabilities specific to their studies. By including these molecular class-focused analyses into its framework, Survival Genie 2 provides a powerful and versatile platform for exploring survival-associated molecular features that reflect key regulatory and intercellular communication activities within cancers.

Use case 1: survival-associated cross-cancer lncRNA gene signature discovery

Leveraging the updated datasets and novel lncRNA-focused analysis capabilities available in Survival Genie 2, we hypothesized that it would be possible to derive a robust lncRNA gene signature significantly associated with survival across cancer types. To test this, we performed Cox proportional hazards regression analysis on available lncRNAs (N = 4363) across the 96 primary tumor datasets in the Survival Genie 2 database. This analysis aimed to identify common lncRNAs exhibiting significant overall survival (OS) differences between high and low-expression groups, among which, 78 datasets yielded significant results (Additional file 1: Table S2). Subsequently, the top shared significant lncRNAs were extracted, and the primary tumor datasets were hierarchically clustered based on their expression of these genes (Additional file 2: Fig. S1). Similar distinct lncRNA expression patterns were also observed when comparing pediatric and adult tumor datasets.

Similarly, shared lncRNAs across the adult datasets largely overlapped with those from solid tumor, namely APCDD1L-DT, LINC02454, LINC00460, LINC00702, LINC01614, LINC01234, LINC02269, MIR4435-2HG, MIR1915HG, LINC02609, LINC00163, LINC02817, LINC01583, LINC00973, LINC00565, IRF1-AS1, LINC00426, LINC00539, LINC02362, and NDUFA6-DT. Among all these shared lncRNAs, 30 have been previously characterized as oncogenes through in vitro studies (Additional file 1: Table S3). Several others have been implicated in non-cancer contexts, such as the overexpression of LINC01250 associated with increased risk of coronary artery disease and the roles of IRF1-AS1 as an enhancer of IRF1 and activator of NF-κB signaling [30, 31]. Critically, many of the lncRNAs, including KCNJ2-AS1, ZNF385D-AS1, NCKAP5-AS2, LINC02697, LINC01248, PHACTR3-AS1, LINC02074, LINC02166, LINC02817, LINC01583, and LINC00539, remain understudied in cancer and thus may represent compelling novel oncological targets warranting further investigation.

Collectively, these results support that numerous lncRNAs may play a role in oncogenesis, tumor progression, and treatment response across diverse cancer types, all directly impacting patient outcomes. By utilizing Survival Genie 2, the cancer-specific survival associations for novel and understudied lncRNAs across a range of cancer types can be uniquely unveiled. By providing crucial evidence for their potential clinical relevance, Survival Genie 2 serves as a foundational platform for deeper mechanistic understanding and accelerates the exploration of these genes as promising biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

Use case 2: unveiling prognostic regulatory modules in multiple myeloma with Survival Genie 2

Powered by the new regulatory module-based analysis in Survival Genie 2, we investigated the survival association of malignant plasma-cell-specific gene regulatory modules in multiple myeloma (MM). We rationalized that we could utilize our platform to explore how the expression of these TF-driven regulatory modules, derived from single-cell data, correlates with patient outcomes. To achieve this, we first performed the local pre-processing step by conducting SCENIC gene regulatory analysis [32] on the single-cell MM dataset from Pilcher, Thomas [25] Specifically, we compared malignant plasma cells from patients with a progression event within four years of diagnosis (termed “progressors”) against those with progression after four years (termed “non-progressors”). The top ten progressor-enriched gene regulatory modules (Additional file 1: Table S4) identified were then utilized as inputs for survival analysis within Survival Genie 2, employing the MMRF-CoMMpass dataset [33].

Our results demonstrated that the enrichment of 9 out of 10 of these modules significantly (log-rank p < 0.05) correlated with poor overall survival (Fig. 6). Crucially, these findings underscore the distinct advantage of module-based and molecular-focused analyses over individual gene assessment. When the survival analysis was conducted on these TFs individually, rather than their defined modules, only 5 out of 10 exhibited significant associations with survival outcomes; specifically, high expression of NFE2 and IKZF2 were associated with poor survival outcomes, while high expressions of NFKB1, KLF4, and MZF1 were linked to better survival outcomes (Additional file 1: Table S6). This direct comparison highlights that considering tumor-associated genes within their broader regulatory landscape, rather than in isolation, provides substantially more robust and comprehensive prognostic insights.

By incorporating targets and cofactors within defined modules, this gene regulatory network-based analysis inherently offers a deeper understanding of complex gene functions within the cancer context. This highlights the unique capability of Survival Genie 2 to analyze complex systems-level data, such as single-cell-derived regulatory networks and co-expression modules, providing a critical advancement for uncovering nuanced, clinically relevant biomarkers that traditional methods may miss.

Use case 3: benchmarking the Survival Genie 2 L-R signaling strength-based survival analysis approach in pediatric B-ALL

To validate the novel methodology we developed within Survival Genie 2 for examining the outcome-association of L-R signaling pairs based on their predicted signaling strength, computed using the co-expression rather than combined expression, we performed a signaling-based analysis using a pediatric B cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) single-cell dataset from Bailur, McCachren [26]. We hypothesized that specific intercellular signaling interactions between B-ALL blasts and T cells in bone marrow microenvironment are correlated to patient outcomes. For the upstream pre-processing step, we utilized the R package CellChat to identify predicted L-R signaling interactions between these key cell types. This analysis yielded ten significant signaling pathways encompassing 19 L-R pairs, including autocrine and paracrine signaling mediated by pathways such as selectin L (SELL), cyclophilin A (CypA), macrophage migratory inhibitory factor (MIF), midkine (MK), galectin, MHC-I, FLT3, CD99, CD45, and APP (Fig. 7a).Fig. 7Survival analysis of signaling pathways in B-ALL dataset. a cell–cell communication probabilities in the corresponding ligand-receptor pairs between B-ALL blasts and T cells (b) L-R signaling based survival analysis of ligand-receptor pairs (c) survival analysis of collective signaling pathways, gene sets, or corresponding individual genes

Survival analysis of signaling pathways in B-ALL dataset. a cell–cell communication probabilities in the corresponding ligand-receptor pairs between B-ALL blasts and T cells (b) L-R signaling based survival analysis of ligand-receptor pairs (c) survival analysis of collective signaling pathways, gene sets, or corresponding individual genes

These identified L-R pairs were then used as inputs for signaling-based survival analysis in Survival Genie 2, applying them to the B-ALL TARGET-ALL-P2 dataset, [42] specifically subset to bone marrow samples. The results demonstrated that increased signaling strength for three pairs (SELL-CD34, FLT3L-FLT3, and CD99-CD99) was significantly associated with improved survival outcomes, while increased signaling strength for 16 other pairs, including PPIA-BSG, MDK-NCL, and HLA-A/B/C/E/F-CD8B, correlated with worse survival outcomes (Fig. 7b). While several of these pathways have established roles cancer processes, including cyclophilin A, [43] midkine, [44] and galectin signaling, [45] notably midkine and galectin signaling in B-ALL, [45, 46] cyclophilin A signaling mediated by the PPIA-BSG interaction represents a novel finding in the context of B-ALL that warrants further investigation into its potential role in tumor progression and immune evasion [47]. Despite the differences in cellular resolution, these findings underscore the utility of integrating single-cell-derived insights for signaling-based survival analysis using Survival Genie 2.

To further establish the robustness and unique advantage of our approach, we benchmarked this novel methodology against two alternative methods: (1) performing signaling-based survival analysis using a list of all potential ligands paired with all potential receptors within the ten identified signaling pathways as input (“signaling pathway”), and (2) combining all ligands and receptors into a single list for canonical gene set-based analysis (“gene-set”) (Fig. 7c, Additional file 1: Table S7). The benchmarking revealed consistent hazard ratio (HR) values across most signaling pathways, but with significant variations in p-values across the three methods. This variability critically reflects differing assumptions made by each approach. For instance, the alternative “signaling pathway” method of combining ligands and receptors for signaling-based analysis yielded contradictory results for specific individual L-R pairs (e.g., MDK-NCL, LGALS9-CD45) compared to their combined pathway effect (e.g., MDK-[NCL/ITGA3/ITGA6/ITGB1], LGALS9-[P4HB/PTPRC/CD44]). This strongly suggests that individual L-R interactions, rather than broad pathway aggregates, may possess greater biological relevance and function as more robust prognostic markers. Furthermore, traditional gene set-based analysis, when applied to signaling pathway L-R pairs, frequently inflated the significance of the results. This inflation likely reflects a bias toward individual genes with strong expression and survival associations, rather than true specific L-R interactions. Crucially, our L-R signaling strength approach overcomes this bias by relying on the signaling strength calculation, which necessitates the concurrent expression of both the ligand and receptor within the pair, upholding the simple but critical biological assumption that both components must be co-expressed for a signaling interaction to be relevant to survival. Collectively, these results underscore the necessity and enhanced biological accuracy of Survival Genie 2's L-R signaling strength-based analysis for identifying precise signaling interactions, such as immunomodulatory ones, that are crucial to oncogenesis, tumor progression, and treatment response.

Discussion

The increasing availability and complexity of large-scale public cancer genomics datasets, coupled with advancements in genomics and single-cell sequencing technologies, underscore the need for accessible bioinformatic tools for manipulating and analyzing such data. Addressing this need, several web-based survival analysis tools have emerged, providing researchers with the ability to elucidate survival associations for genes of interest. While these existing web-based survival analysis platforms offer valuable functionalities, they fall short in fully leveraging the dimensionality of these evolving genomic data, especially single-cell insights. Survival Genie 2 addresses this critical gap by introducing a next-generation platform that extends beyond canonical single-gene and gene-set analyses to empower researchers with systems-level approaches for survival analyses.

Most notably, Survival Genie 2 provides an accessible platform for translating single-cell discoveries into clinically testable hypotheses. While single-cell profiling uniquely enables the identification of cell-type-specific gene signatures, regulatory networks, co-expression modules, and intercellular signaling interactions, these datasets rarely include matched long-term survival information and are often too small to support statistically robust survival modeling. Survival Genie 2 is designed to bridge this gap by enabling researchers to systematically evaluate the prognostic relevance of candidate genes, modules, or interactions derived from single-cell analyses within large-scale, outcome-linked bulk RNA-seq datasets curated in our database. Through composite scoring of multi-gene queries, wrapper functions that streamline the upstream derivation of regulons, modules, and signaling interactions, and our ligand-receptor co-expression scoring approach, the platform allows users to interrogate single-cell-derived genes of interest in clinically powered patient cohorts. This integrated workflow uniquely empowers researchers to connect high-resolution cellular insights with patient-level outcomes, thereby facilitating the generation of clinically meaningful hypotheses from single-cell data through a user-friendly platform.

As demonstrated in the use cases, these features are capable of uncovering nuanced survival associations that traditional methods may miss. For instance, Use Case 1 exemplified how Survival Genie 2's lncRNA-focused analysis, coupled with its comprehensive database, systematically identified a cross-cancer lncRNA signature, revealing novel prognostic biomarkers previously understudied. Use Case 2 demonstrated the power of module-based analysis, illustrating that malignant plasma cell regulatory modules provide more robust prognostic insights in MM than individual transcription factors, thereby highlighting the importance of systems-level approaches. Furthermore, Use Case 3 validated Survival Genie 2's novel L-R signaling strength methodology, demonstrating its improved ability to identify precise intercellular communication interactions implicated in B-ALL, in contrast to less specific gene set-based methods. The standardized input formats for these analyses, designed for seamless use with diverse upstream single-cell tools, ensure Survival Genie 2's widespread and continued applicability for the rapidly expanding single-cell genomics community.

Beyond its single-cell integration capabilities, Survival Genie 2 offers additional innovative features that enhance biomarker discovery and analysis flexibility. The platform supports filtering of input gene sets to focus on biologically relevant molecular classes, namely lncRNAs, transcription factors, and ligand-receptor signaling genes, which significantly increases the prognostic relevance of identified biomarkers by directing focus to key regulatory elements. Furthermore, Survival Genie 2 boasts the most comprehensive and diverse database for survival analysis to date, encompassing 132 datasets from 38 distinct pediatric and adult malignancies, ensuring broad applicability. Users also benefit from enhanced control over analyses through multiple partitioning methods and an intuitive, linearly paged pipeline interface with interactive, publication-ready figures, optimizing the user experience.

Conclusions

In conclusion, Survival Genie 2 represents a significant advancement in web-based survival analysis, uniquely bridging the gap between bulk public datasets and high-resolution single-cell insights. By providing user-friendly tools for dissecting complex gene networks involved in cancer pathogenesis, such as cluster markers, co-expression modules, and regulatory and signaling networks, Survival Genie 2 goes beyond traditional analyses to accelerate the identification of robust, context-specific biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets. This open-source platform (available at https://bhasinlab.bmi.emory.edu/SurvivalGenie2/home) is committed to ongoing development, with future versions planned to integrate additional clinicopathological and genomic features, expand analysis options to include continuous and multi-variable support, and potentially incorporate spatial data analysis, ensuring its continued relevance for advancing precision oncology and improving patient outcomes.

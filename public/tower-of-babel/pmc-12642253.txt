Utility of optimal proportion of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes as a prognostic prediction of clinical outcome in intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma: a single institution retrospective analysis
Authors: Waritta Kunprom, Nattaporn Poonsiri, Piyapharom Intarawichian, Prakasit Sa-Ngiamwibool, Sakkarn Sangkhamanon, Malinee Thanee, Watcharin Loilome, Attapol Titapun, Apiwat Jareanrat, Vasin Thanasukarn, Tharatip Srisuk, Vor Luvira, Kulyada Eurboonyanun, Julaluck Promsorn, Supinda Koonmee, Piya Prajumwongs, Chaiwat Aphivatanasiri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIntrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA), prevalent in Northeastern Thailand, presents challenges due to late diagnosis and metastasis. Prognostic factors are crucial. Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) hold promise as prognostic indicators and for immunotherapy, but their optimal proportion in iCCA prognosis is yet to be determined. We propose to evaluate the optimal proportion of TILs for predicting the outcomes of iCCA.MethodsWe analyzed TIL levels in 190 iCCA cases across four models with varying TIL cut-offs. Correlations with clinicopathological features were examined, followed by univariate and multivariate survival analyses. Subgroup analysis differentiated early and late-stage disease to assess TILs predictive potential.ResultsResults showed that at least 20% of TILs were significantly associated with smaller tumor size and growth patterns of intraductal (ID) and ID mixed types. Multivariate analysis revealed that 20%-40% of TILs were an independent factor for predicting good survival of patients. In addition, the 20% cut-off value was an independent factor for predicting the survival outcome of patients with early-stage disease.ConclusionIn this study, we identified the optimal proportion of stromal TILs for prognostic prediction in early-stage iCCA patients. Further research is warranted to validate the role of TILs as a prognostic biomarker and to explore their potential utility in guiding patient monitoring and risk stratification in early-stage disease.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13000-025-01733-z.Keywords: Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, Early stage, Prognosis, Predicting outcome

Introduction

Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) arises from bile duct epithelium. CCA is classified into three types based on anatomical localization, namely, intrahepatic (iCCA), perihilar (pCCA), and distal CCA (dCCA) [1]. The incidence of CCA is rare in the West but high in the East and Southeast Asia, especially in Thailand. In fact, the Northeastern region of Thailand has been reported to have the highest incidence of CCA globally (135.4 per 100,000 males and 43.0 per 100,000 females) [2]. In Thailand, Opisthorchis viverrine, or liver fluke infection, has been reported as a major risk factor for CCA-genesis [3–5]. There has been a progressive increase in iCCA incidence in Thailand up to the end of the last century by approximately 30%, reaching a plateau in the past ten years. In contrast, the incidence of both pCCA and dCCA appears to be dropping [6, 7]. This study focuses on iCCA because of increased incidence and high mortality rates due to its aggressive biological nature of early invasion, high recurrence, and widespread metastasis, coupled with a lack of effective treatment [8]. Surgical extirpation is a major option for curative intent in CCA [9]. However, despite patients with surgery performing better than those without, the 5-year survival duration and rate are approximately 15–20 months and 10–30%, respectively [7, 10–14]. In general, CCA has high mortality rates because of the difficulty in attaining early diagnosis, with patients often presenting with advanced-stage/metastatic disease [15, 16]. Therefore, the establishment of potential prognostic factors is crucial to enhance strategy planning for predicting and improving outcomes of CCA patients.

Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) represent key immune effector cells that play a fundamental role in the host immune response against both pathogens and malignant cells. These lymphocytes primarily consist of T cells, B cells, and natural killer (NK) cells. Numerous studies have demonstrated that elevated levels of TILs are associated with favorable prognostic outcomes across various types of solid tumors [17–21]. Clinical benefits in treatment outcomes, such as surgery, chemotherapy, and immune therapy [22–24], were observed for many cancers, namely, breast [20], colorectal [19], lung [21], and pancreatic [18] cancers. In CCA, especially iCCA, TILs have been potential prognostic factor for recurance and survival of patient after curative resection [25]. However, prior studies, including a recent population-based investigation, primarily applied semi-quantitative categorical scoring systems (positive vs. negative TILs) often with small sample sizes, and did not define precise quantitative thresholds of TILs that correlate with survival outcomes. Furthermore, the prognostic significance of TILs specifically in early-stage iCCA remains unclear.

Despite the recognized prognostic role of TILs in iCCA, several gaps remain. First, prior studies did not establish a quantitative cut-off or range of TILs that reliably predicts survival. Second, semi-quantitative assessments may introduce inter-observer variability, limiting clinical applicability. Third, the impact of TILs on early-stage iCCA prognosis has not been fully explored. Addressing these gaps is essential to improve patient stratification, refine prognostic models, and support future investigations into the potential role of TILs in guiding immunotherapeutic strategies.

Therefore, this study aimed to determine the optimal proportion of stromal TILs that can predict clinical outcomes in iCCA patients. Unlike previous studies that used categorical scoring, we applied a percentage-based evaluation (10%, 20%, 30%, and 40%) following the International TILs Working Group (ITWG) guideline. We further investigated the association between stromal TIL proportions and clinicopathological features and evaluated whether specific TIL thresholds could serve as independent prognostic indicators, particularly in early-stage iCCA.

Materials and methods

Patients

A total of 997 patients who underwent surgical treatment and were pathologically diagnosed with cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) at Srinagarind Hospital, Faculty of Medicine, Khon Kaen University, Thailand, between 2002 and 2017 were retrospectively reviewed. Inclusion criteria were patients who underwent hepatic resection with histopathological confirmation of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA) and had complete clinicopathological and follow-up data. Patients with perihilar CCA (n = 558) and distal CCA (n = 84) were excluded. Among the 355 patients diagnosed with iCCA, additional exclusion criteria included patients who underwent liver biopsy only or wedge resection (n = 32), those who survived less than 30 days after surgery (n = 10), patients with combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma (cHCC-CCA, n = 3), and cases with no available tissue specimens (n = 120). After applying these criteria, a total of 190 iCCA patients who had undergone curative-intended hepatic resection were included and eligible for tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) evaluation (Fig. 1). The follow-up duration for all included patients was at least five years.

CONSORT diagram of the study

Recorded data

Intraoperative data collection included sex, age, tumor size, growth patterns, surgical margin, and characteristics of surrounding organs. The specimens were examined with relevant tissue blocks taken by a pathologist for routine tissue processing. Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks were sectioned at 5 microns [26] and stained with H&E. The 2019 WHO classification criteria were adopted for pathological diagnosis [27]. Under light microscopy, the following histomorphological data were recorded, namely growth patterns, histological type, histological grade, surgical margin, lymphovascular invasion, and lymph node metastasis. Evidence for distant metastasis was retrieved from the medical records. Finally, the gross examination and pathological findings were correlated according to the 8th AJCC Staging Manual [28].

Tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte evaluation

TILs were evaluated on H&E sections from surgically resected specimens. According to the ITWG guideline [22], average percentages of TILs in each case were identified and evaluated as TILs proportion within the stroma (Fig. 2). The guideline recommended assessing the average TILs percentage within the borders of the invasive tumor, comprising both the main tumor and invasive margin (1-mm region). Before scoring the TILs percentage, a prerequisite is to select the tumor area after excluding the tumor zone with crush artifacts, necrosis, and regressive hyalinization. All mononuclear cells are scored (i.e., lymphocytes and plasma cells), but polymorphonuclear leukocytes are excluded. Only the stromal TILs percentages were evaluated according to the ITWG guideline. The denominator used to determine the percentage of stromal TILs is the area of stromal tissue (i.e., the area occupied by mononuclear inflammatory cells over the total stromal area), not the number of stromal cells.

Representative H&E-stained histological examples of proportions of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in the stroma of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma cases

TILs were assessed by three experienced pathologists (Waritta Kunprom, Nattaporn Poonsiri, and Chaiwat Aphivatanasiri) simultaneously using a multi-head microscope. The consensus score obtained by these three evaluators for each case was subsequently reviewed and finalized by the remaining pathologists, ensuring accuracy and consistency of the assigned TIL proportion. This approach minimized inter-observer variability and ensured robust scoring across all cases.

Growth pattern estimation

The growth pattern estimation criteria were applied according to a previous report by Kunprom et al. [14]. The resection specimens were trimmed and photographed with the tumor growth pattern/s recorded at the time of grossing, followed by subsequent histological confirmation. The growth patterns comprised intraductal (ID), periductal infiltrating (PI), and mass-forming (MF) patterns. The patterns were estimated in increments of 10% to establish the proportion of each pattern (ID, PI or MF) or combinations of patterns (ID + PI, ID + MF, PI + MF or ID + PI + MF).

Pathological diagnosis

There were four major histological types comprising papillary adenocarcinoma, tubular adenocarcinoma, papillotubular adenocarcinoma, and adenocarcinoma (NOS). Papillary, tubular, and papillotubular adenocarcinomas were classified into well- or moderately differentiated cancers. The histologic classification of iCCA into large and small duct types was evaluated using the criteria from the 2019 WHO classification [27]. Adenocarcinoma, NOS, was defined as poorly differentiated bile duct cancer lacking well-formed papillary or tubular structures.

Statistical analysis

Only patients with complete datasets were included in the statistical analyses. Statistics for categorical data were performed with the χ2-Test (or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate). The survival rate and median survival time from the date of surgery for iCCA until death from iCCA used the Kaplan-Meier model, which is applicable for survival analyses; the Log-rank test was used to compare the difference in survival. Perioperative causes of death were excluded from this analysis. Multivariate analysis was performed using the Cox regression model to determine prognostic factors. For the percentage of growth pattern decision criteria, a 20% growth pattern estimation cut-off value was used as this showed significantly different overall survival (OS) between each type of growth pattern [29]. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 23. p-values of less than 0.05 were considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Association of clinicopathological features with the proportion of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes in iCCA

This was a retrospective study of 190 iCCA patients. The distribution and proportion of TILs in representative CCA areas are shown in Fig. 2. The percentage of TILs was evaluated separately by two trainee pathologists and the discordant cases were re-evaluated in a multihead microscope to reach a consensus score. Initially, the cohort was divided using ROC analysis, which yielded a moderate AUC of 0.631, and the Youden index suggested a cut-off of 27.5%. Using this threshold, the groups were highly imbalanced, with TILs ≥ 27.5% (n = 58) and TILs < 27.5% (n = 132) (Fig. 3A). Such imbalance can reduce statistical power, bias survival estimates, and compromise the reliability of multivariate analyses when comparing outcomes between groups. To overcome these limitations, patients were regrouped using the median TILs value of 20% as the cut-off, resulting in two equal groups (n = 98 per group). Unlike the ROC-derived cut-off, using the median ensures balanced group sizes, which enhances the robustness and stability of statistical analyses, reduces the influence of extreme values, and provides a consistent, unbiased starting point. Equal sample sizes improve the reliability of survival comparisons and hazard ratio estimates, and this balanced stratification also allows for further exploration of potential optimal cut-offs within the ranges below and above the median without being skewed by sparse or extreme values. The median TILs percentage was 20% (range, 5%–80%). Based on TILs proportion, four models were proposed (models 1–4) with cut-off values of 10%, 20% (median), 30%, and 40%, respectively. Each model was then divided into two groups: patients with TILs equal to or above the cut-off (≥ %TILs) and those below the cut-off (< %TILs) (Table 1). The association between TILs proportion and patient characteristics was subsequently analyzed for each model.

There was a significant correlation between the TILs level and patients’ age in model 4. High levels of TILs correlated with age > 60 years, while low levels of TILs correlated with age ≤ 60 years (p = 0.026).

Tumor size was associated with a statistically significant difference of the proportion of TILs in models 2, 3, and 4 (p = 0.009, p = 0.006, and p = 0.024, respectively). The results showed that a higher proportion of high TILs levels was encountered in < 6 cm-tumor size than low levels of TILs, by 66.1%, 72.9%, and 78.9% for models 2,3 and 4, respectively. On the contrary, tumor size ≥ 6 cm showed a high proportion of low TILs levels at 56.9%, 54.3%, and 50.9% for models 2,3 and 4, respectively, compared to proportions with high TILs levels at 33.9%, 27.1%, and 21.1% for models 2,3 and 4, respectively (Table 2).

For tumor growth pattern, high levels of TILs in models 2, 3, and 4 were significantly correlated with ID and ID mixed types when compared with a low level of TILs (35.8% vs. 19%, p = 0.034 in model 2; 47.5% vs. 18.3%, p < 0.001 in model 3; and 51.6% vs. 22.7%, p = 0.003 in model 4). In contrast, growth patterns without ID mixed types had a high proportion of low TILs levels (81% vs. 64.2% in model 2, 81.7% vs. 52.5% in model 3, and 77.3% vs. 48.4% in model 4) (Table 2).

In addition, only high levels of TILs in model 4 were significantly correlated with small duct type, and low levels of TILS were correlated with large duct type (p = 0.044).

These findings suggest that the level of TILs ≥ 20% was associated with good clinical outcomes for iCCA patients, as exemplified by small tumor size and growth patterns with ID and ID mixed types.

Univariate and multivariate analyses of survival of iCCA patients and proportions of TILs

A total of 190 iCCA patients were analyzed for median survival time (MST), which was 15.7 months (95% CI, 13.6–17.9 months) (Supplementary Fig. 1). Univariate analysis was subsequently performed to compare survival differences across variables. No statistically significant difference in survival was found with regards to the patient’s age (p = 0.215), gender (p = 0.456), or growth pattern [ID vs. ID mixed types, (p = 0.153)]. However, factors such as tumor size, growth pattern, surgical margin, histological grade, tumor staging, and TILs levels showed a statistically significant difference in the univariate analysis of the survival times. Tumor size < 6 cm had significantly better survival than tumor size ≥ 6 cm (MST = 24 vs. 11 mo., HR = 2.04, p < 0.001). Surgical margin R1 (positive margin) was associated with statistically significantly lower survival than R0 (negative margin) (MST = 12 vs. 18 mo., HR = 1.51, p = 0.008). As for growth patterns, iCCA patients having ID type showed longer survival than those without ID component (MST = 86.0 vs. 12.0 mo., HR = 5.13, p < 0.001). Moderately/poorly differentiated histological grades had shorter survival than well-differentiated grades (MST = 16 vs. 7 mo., HR = 2.91, p < 0.001). Tumor staging by 8th AJCC/UICC staging system showed that patients with early stage disease had markedly greater survival than late stage (MST = 26.0 vs. 10.0 mo., HR = 2.58, p < 0.001). In addition, high TILs levels with their respective cut-off values, were associated with statistically significantly better survival than those with low TILs levels (MST = 23 vs. 13, HR = 0.51 in 27.5% Youden index cut-off; MST = 16 vs. 9, HR = 0.51 in 10% cut-off; MST = 17 vs. 13, HR = 0.52 in 20% cut-off; MST = 19 vs. 13, HR = 0.54 in 30% cut-off; and MST = 34 vs. 14, HR = 0.43 in 40% cut-off, p < 0.001 in all groups) (Table 3; Fig. 3B-F). Significant factors identified in the univariate analysis were further evaluated using multivariate Cox regression to determine independent prognostic factors. Notably, the model divided by the Youden index yielded statistical results similar to those of model 3; therefore, model 3 was used for the multivariate analysis instead of the Youden index-based model.

Discussion

In Northeastern Thailand, the incidence of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA) is higher than other CCA subtypes. Hepatectomy offers a potentially curative treatment for resectable iCCA; however, prognosis remains poor due to late presentation and high recurrence rates. A retrospective study of iCCA patients in this region between 2002 and 2017 reported median overall survival of 20–31 months and 5-year survival rates of 23.5–36.8% for early-stage disease, compared with 8–11 months and 6% for late-stage disease [16]. Despite its widespread use, the 8th AJCC/UICC staging system shows limitations in accurately predicting outcomes for iCCA, with reported discrepancies such as unexpectedly poor survival in some early-stage cases and favorable survival in certain late-stage cases [16, 30, 31]. Confounding factors—including tumor growth pattern, histological type, and grade—further reduce stratification performance, particularly for early-stage disease [16, 30, 31]. To improve prognostic accuracy, additional factors have been investigated. These include tumor growth pattern, histological grade [16, 32], serum cancer markers [30, 33], and notably, tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) levels [34]. Incorporating such factors can enhance outcome prediction and refine the performance of the AJCC/UICC staging system in stratifying iCCA patients.

A recent population-based study demonstrated that stromal TILs serve as a prognostic indicator in iCCA patients after curative resection [25]. That study provided important evidence that high TIL levels were associated with improved OS and RFS, highlighting the clinical relevance of immune infiltration in iCCA. However, the authors used a binary categorical evaluation (positive: TILs score 2–3 or ≥ 10% vs. negative: TILs score 0–1 or < 10%) and did not establish an optimal quantitative threshold, which may limit the accuracy of patient stratification. Furthermore, the relatively small cohort size (n = 68) could reduce the generalizability of their findings. In our study, we expanded upon these findings by conducting a retrospective analysis of 190 iCCA patients, applying a percentage-based quantitative assessment of stromal TILs (10%, 20%, 30%, and 40%) according to ITWG guidelines. This approach allowed us to explore optimal TIL cut-offs for prognostic prediction while maintaining consistency with previous evidence that high TILs are associated with favorable OS. Our analysis showed that patients with ≥ 20% TILs had significantly smaller tumors and were more likely to exhibit ID or ID mixed growth patterns, whereas patients with < 20% TILs had larger tumors and more aggressive growth patterns, such as MF, PI, and MF + PI. These findings support the notion that high levels of stromal TILs are linked to improved tumor control, consistent with prior studies [25, 35–37], while providing a refined quantitative threshold to distinguish prognostic subgroups more precisely.

It is well recognized that the presence of TILs represents the immune system’s cytotoxic and regulatory response to malignant cells. Increased infiltration of TILs enhances tumor cell killing through mechanisms such as cytokine secretion, direct cytotoxic activity, and modulation of the tumor microenvironment, thereby contributing to better clinical outcomes in many malignancies. Elevated TIL levels have been consistently associated with favorable prognosis across various solid tumors, including breast, colorectal, lung, pancreatic cancers, and distal CCA [18–21, 34]. Qiang et al. apply positive (TILs score 2–3 or ≥ 10% vs. negative TILs score 0–1 or < 10%), and result showed that positive TILs associated to improved OS and RFS in iCC after complete resection. In addition, Intarawichian et al. investigated TILs in distal CCA (dCCA), using 40% as the median cut-off for TIL proportion. They reported that high TIL levels (>40%) served as an independent prognostic factor for improved outcomes and were associated with favorable growth patterns, such as ID and ID-mixed types, which are recognized predictors of good survival in CCA. Incorporating TILs together with tumor growth patterns in subgroup analyses further enhanced the stratification of patients with good versus poor prognosis [34]. Notably, this association between TILs and growth patterns is concordant with our findings: in our cohort, TILs ≥ 20% were associated with ID type or an ID component in mixed types, which are linked to favorable prognosis in iCCA, whereas MF, PI, and MF + PI growth patterns were associated with worse outcomes. Zhao et al. investigated TILs in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) according to the International TILs Working Group 2014 criteria, quantifying them as the percentage of stromal area infiltrated by lymphocytes and plasma cells. Patients were categorized into low- and high-TIL groups based on the median value. Their study demonstrated a significant association between the infiltrative growth pattern of cancer and TIL levels. Specifically, tumors with an infiltrative growth pattern exhibited a suppressed immune microenvironment characterized by low TIL infiltration, lymph node metastasis, absence of tertiary lymphoid structures, deep tumor invasion, poor differentiation, and reduced overall survival. Conversely, high TILs levels were associated with a favorable prognosis in ESCC and other malignancies [38].

Although no standardized percentage or scoring system has been established for TIL evaluation, numerous studies consistently demonstrate that higher TIL density is associated with favorable prognosis across various solid tumors, including breast, colorectal, lung, pancreatic cancers, and cholangiocarcinoma. In other words, despite the lack of a universally accepted cut-off or scoring method, it is widely recognized that elevated TIL levels generally correlate with good prognosis. In the following section, we demonstrate the relationship between different levels of TILs in each model and their associations with clinicopathological outcomes in our cohort.

We demonstrated the optimal proportion of TILs for prognostic prediction in early and late-stage iCCA patients. Results showed that 20% of TILs can separate iCCA patients with early-stage disease (TNM stage I-II) into two groups of different survival times about two-fold (43 mo. vs. 19 mo. for high vs. low level of TILs) (Tables 6 and 7). This phenomenon might be explained by the early-stage emphasis on tumor size and multifocal tumor or lymphovascular invasion (LVSI), respectively, according to the 8th AJCC/UICC staging system [28]. High levels of TILs could limit and kill tumor growth and extension via the immune response pathway and improve survival. This evidence was supported by a high level of TILs associated with small tumor size and growth pattern with ID type (Table 2). In contrast, a low level of TILs indicated lower immune response, leading to larger tumor size and tumor extension, as well as a high proportion of MF and PI growth patterns. The latter has been suggested as representing a highly invasive subtype of CCA with more extensive intrahepatic involvement and LVSI.

Moreover, this study also revealed the proportion of TILs for use as a prognostic factor to predict the survival of late-stage iCCA. A 10% TILs was an independent factor for prognostic prediction of late-stage iCCA, and this cut-off separated patients into two groups, comprising a high and low level of TILs with survival time of 13 and 8 months, respectively. Although the role of a high level of TILs improved the survival of patients with late-stage disease similar to early stage, advanced iCCA has several confounding factors that impact the survival, such as local invasion, lymph node metastasis, and distant metastasis [28] as well as other factors not listed in the 8th AJCC/UICC staging system, such as growth pattern, histological type, and histological grade [16, 32]. These have an effect on decreasing the survival outcome of iCCA even though patients have a high level of TILs (Table 7).

This study has several key strengths that enhance its scientific and clinical relevance. It analyzed a relatively large cohort of 190 iCCA patients from Northeastern Thailand, an endemic region, providing robust and representative data. Stromal TILs were evaluated using standardized International TILs Working Group (ITWG) guidelines, ensuring methodological consistency. Multiple TIL cut-off models (10%, 20%, 30%, and 40%) and stage-based subgroup analyses were applied, enabling precise prognostic stratification. Notably, a 20% TIL cut-off emerged as an independent prognostic factor in early-stage iCCA, associated with smaller tumor size and favorable growth patterns. Collectively, these findings establish a clinically relevant TIL threshold that may serve as a potential biomarker for risk assessment and patient monitoring in iCCA.

This study has several limitations. First, it is a single-cohort retrospective analysis from one institution; thus, external validation is needed to confirm the optimal TIL cut-offs identified. Second, some TIL proportions showed inconsistent prognostic trends (e.g., high TILs linked to poor survival and low TILs linked to favorable outcomes). Immunophenotypic characterization of TIL subsets (CD8⁺, CD4⁺, regulatory T cells) was not performed, which could clarify the functional heterogeneity of TILs. Third, prognostic assessment of late-stage iCCA using a low TIL cut-off (~ 10%) was limited by small sample size and potential confounding factors such as local invasion, nodal, and distant metastasis. Fourth, perioperative chemotherapy data were incomplete; few patients received adjuvant therapy, and reliable information on neoadjuvant treatment was unavailable, precluding inclusion in multivariate analysis. Molecular and immune context data (IDH1/2, FGFR2, PD-L1) were also lacking, which may influence TIL-associated outcomes. Lastly, while most iCCAs are diagnosed on small core biopsies (16–18 G, 10–20 mm), this study used resection specimens (> 30 mm) to ensure adequate tissue for standardized TIL assessment per ITWG guidelines. The applicability of the 20% stromal TIL threshold to biopsy material requires caution, as small cores may underrepresent spatial heterogeneity. Nonetheless, the relative pattern of TIL density likely retains biological significance. Future studies comparing paired biopsy and resection specimens are warranted to validate the reproducibility of the 20% cut-off in preoperative samples.

Despite these limitations, this study is the first to establish quantitative thresholds of stromal TILs that correlate with key clinicopathological features, including tumor size, growth pattern, and survival outcomes in both early- and late-stage iCCA. These findings suggest that TILs may serve as a complementary prognostic biomarker, potentially improving the stratification accuracy of the 8th AJCC/UICC staging system and supporting immunotherapeutic decision-making. To address the limitations of this study, future investigations should involve multicenter cohorts to validate these quantitative TIL thresholds, include immunophenotypic characterization of TIL subsets (CD8⁺, CD4⁺, and regulatory T cells) to clarify their functional roles, incorporate molecular and immune context information (such as IDH1/2, FGFR2, and PD-L1 status), and systematically collect data on perioperative chemotherapy to evaluate its impact on TIL-associated prognostic outcomes.

In summary, this study identified a 20% cut-off for stromal TILs as the optimal threshold associated with tumor size, growth pattern, and survival outcomes in iCCA patients. Importantly, a TIL level of ≥ 20% serves as an independent prognostic factor, particularly in early-stage disease. These findings suggest that quantitative assessment of stromal TILs can improve prognostic stratification and potentially support patient monitoring and risk assessment, highlighting their value as a practical biomarker in early-stage iCCA.

Strassen’s local law of the iterated logarithm for the generalized fractional Brownian motion
Ran Wang, Yimin Xiao

Abstract




Abstract:

Let
X:=\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a generalized fractional Brownian motion:

\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}\overset{d}{=}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((t-u)_{+}^{\alpha}-(-u)_{+}^{\alpha}\right)|u|^{-\gamma/2}B(du)\right\}_{t\geq 0},

with parameters
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in\left(-1/2+\gamma/2,\,1/2+\gamma/2\right)
.
This is a self-similar Gaussian process introduced by Pang and Taqqu (2019) as the scaling limit
of power-law shot noise processes. The parameters
\alpha
and
\gamma
determine
the probabilistic and statistical properties of
X
. In particular, the parameter
\gamma
introduces non-stationarity of the increments.
In this paper, we prove Strassen’s local law of the iterated logarithm of
X
at any fixed point
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, which
describes explicitly the roles played by the parameters
\alpha,\gamma
and the location
t_{0}
. Our result is different from the
previous Strassen’s LIL for
X
at infinity proved by Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu (2022).

Keywords and Phrases:
Gaussian self-similar process; Strassen’s LIL; generalized fractional Brownian motion;
Lamperti’s transformation.

MSC:
60G15, 60G17, 60G18, 60G22.

1.
Introduction

The generalized fractional Brownian motion (GFBM, in short)
X:=\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a centered Gaussian self-similar
process introduced by Pang and Taqqu
[
22
]
as the scaling limit of shot noise processes with power-law
non-stationary conditional variance functions. It has the following stochastic integral representation:

\displaystyle\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}\overset{d}{=}

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((t-u)_{+}^{\alpha}-(-u)_{+}^{\alpha}\right)|u|^{-\gamma/2}B(du)\right\}_{t\geq 0},

(1.1)

where the parameters
\gamma
and
\alpha
satisfy

\displaystyle\gamma\in[0,1),\ \ \alpha\in\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2},\ \frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right),

(1.2)

and where
B(du)
is a Gaussian random measure in
\mathbb{R}
with the Lebesgue control measure
du
.
It follows that the Gaussian process
X
is self-similar with index
H
given by

\displaystyle H=\alpha-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\in(0,1).

(1.3)

When
\gamma=0
,
X
becomes an ordinary fractional Brownian motion (FBM, for short)
B^{H}
which
can be represented as:

\displaystyle\left\{B^{H}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}\overset{d}{=}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((t-u)_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-(-u)_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\right)B(du)\right\}_{t\geq 0}.

(1.4)

However, when
\gamma\neq 0
,
X
does not have the property of stationary increments.

As shown by Pang and Taqqu
[
22
]
, GFBM
X
is a natural generalization of the ordinary FBM.
It preserves the self-similarity property while the factor
|u|^{-\gamma/2}
introduces non-stationarity of increments.
The parameter
\gamma
not only makes the properties of GFBM
X
different from those of FBM
B^{H}
but,
more importantly, also provides more flexibility in capturing the non-stationarity in stochastic systems.
In this regard, we refer to Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu
[
13
]
for characterizing the semimartingale
properties of GFBM and its mixtures in terms of the parameters
(\alpha,\gamma)
and for examples
of applications of GFBM in finance.

The parameters
(\alpha,\gamma)
determine the probabilistic and statistical properties of GFBM
X
. Thus it
is important to understand the roles played by
(\alpha,\gamma)
precisely in the quantitative sense.
Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu
[
12
]
raised and studied the question “How does the parameter
\gamma

affect the sample path properties of GFBM?” They proved in
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
that, for any
T>0

and
\varepsilon>0
, the sample paths of
X
are Hölder continuous in
[0,T]
of order
H-\varepsilon
. In addition,
the usual LIL at the origin (
[
12
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
29
, Proposition 7.1]
), and
the functional law of the iterated logarithm at infinity (also called Strassen’s global LIL) in
[
12
, Theorem 5.1]

are all determined by the self-similarity index
H
. Recently, Wang and Xiao
[
29
,
30
]
studied
some precise local sample path properties of GFBM
X
,
including the exact uniform modulus of continuity, small ball probabilities, Chung’s law of iterated logarithm,
the tangent processes and lower functions. Those results show that the local regularity properties of GFBM
X

away from the origin are determined by the index
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}
, instead of the self-similarity index
H
.
They also described explicitly the location dependence of the local oscillation properties of
X
at any fixed point

t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
. For example, Theorems 1.4-1.6 of
[
29
]
show that the oscillations decrease at
the rate
t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}
as
t_{0}
increases in the contexts of Chung’s LIL, the local modulus of continuity
and the tangent process separately.

The main objective of the present paper is to continue in the direction of research in
[
12
,
13
,
29
,
30
]

by studying the local oscillation property of GFBM
X
in the context of functional laws of the iterated logarithm.
Since Strassen
[
25
]
proved his functional LIL for Brownian motion, the topic of functional LILs
(in either limsup or liminf senses) for more general Gaussian processes has been studied extensively by many authors, for
example, see
[
2
,
10
,
11
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
24
,
31
,
32
]
.
Except
[
10
,
32
]
, all the aforementioned references are
concerned with functional LILs at infinity (i.e., the functional limits of an appropriately normalized sequence of the processes

\{X(nt);0\leq t\leq 1\}
as
n\to\infty
). We refer to Taqqu and Czado
[
27
]
and Li and Shao
[
17
]
for more
historical information and references on functional LILs for Gaussian and certain non-Gaussian (e.g., Hermite) processes.

We should also mention that Strassen’s laws of the iterated logarithm have also been established for the
empirical processes in
[
9
,
14
,
33
]
, for the quantile processes in

[
6
]
, for the increments of empirical and quantile processes in
[
7
]
, for the empirical cumulative quantile regression
in
[
23
]
, for the U-statistics in
[
3
]
. See
[
16
]
for further information. In all these references, the samples are
assumed to be i.i.d. or at least stationary and the corresponding Strassen’s laws of the iterated logarithm are formulated in terms
of the reproducing kernel Hilbert space of the scaling limit of the random samples. We expect that our result may be useful for
characterizing the limiting behavior of the increments of empirical and quantile processes of non-stationary time series whose
scaling limit is a GFBM. It would be interesting to investigate these problems.

In the present paper, we focus on the limsup-type functional law of the iterated logarithm (i.e., Strassen’s LIL)
for GFBM
X
at a fixed point
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
. Namely, we study the functional limits of the following normalized family

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{t_{0},h}(x):=\frac{X(t_{0}+hx)-X(t_{0})}{t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}|h|^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\ln\ln(1/|h|)\right)^{1/2}}\ \ \ \ \text{for }x\in[0,1],

(1.5)

as
h\to 0
. Such a limit theorem is also called Strassen’s local LIL.

The following are some remarks about (
1.5
) and Strassen’s laws of the iterated logarithm established in

[
12
,
32
]
.

•

Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu
[
12
]
established Strassen’s LIL for the sequence of processes

\frac{X(nx)}{n^{H}\sqrt{2\ln\ln n}}\ \ \ \ \text{for }x\in[0,1],

as
n\to\infty
, where
H
is given in (
1.3
). Thus, the normalizing function in (
1.5
) is different.

•

We can also consider the case of
t_{0}=0
. Strassen’s local LIL at the origin can be derived from the functional LIL of
Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu
[
12
, Theorem 5.1]
, the time inversion property of GFBM
X
in Proposition 7.1 of
[
29
]
,
and the time inversion argument of Gantert
[
10
]
.
More precisely, we can check firstly that Strassen’s LIL for
X
at infinity in
[
12
, Theorem 5.1]

can be easily extended to the case of “the whole time axis” (see
[
8
, Theorem 1.4.1]
), then by using
Proposition 7.1 of
[
29
]
and the time inversion argument of Gantert
[
10
]
, we derive the
corresponding Strassen’s LIL at the origin.

•

The problem on the functional limit of (
1.5
) is related to Wang, Su and
Xiao
[
32
]
, where sufficient conditions for Strassen’s global and local LILs for a class of Gaussian
random fields with stationary increments are established. The main objective of

[
32
]
is to describe (or distinguish) explicitly the inferences of the long-range dependence index
and the fractal index on Strassen’s global and local LILs, respectively. In
[
32
]
, the property of
stationary increments played an important role. It not only gives the stochastic spectral representation
for the Gaussian random fields in
[
32
]
, which is used to produce independence in the proof of their
main theorems, but also ensures that Strassen’s local LILs do not depend on the location (they are the same
for all
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
). Since GFBM

X
has non-stationary increments, we modify the main arguments in
[
32
]
. In order to create
independence, as in
[
28
]
and
[
29
]
, we make use of the integral representation of the
Lamperti transformation
U=\{U(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of the generalized Riemann-Liouville FBM
Z

(see (
3.1
) below). Some estimations for the spectral density of
U
obtained in
[
29
]

play an important role in this paper.

Our main result is Theorem
2.1
below, which describes the dependence of
Strassen’s local LIL on the parameters
(\alpha,\gamma)
and the location
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
explicitly. This
theorem is stronger than the laws of the iterated logarithm proved in
[
29
]
and, together with
Strassen’s LIL for
X
at infinity in
[
12
]
, completes the functional LILs for GFBM
X
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we state the main theorem and give
some examples of its consequences. We prove the main theorem in Section 3 by using the tools
of the large deviation principle, a Fernique-type inequality, and the Lamperti transformation.

2.
Statement of main theorem

Let
C([0,1])
be the space of all continuous
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions on
[0,1]
equipped with the
sup-norm
\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|f(x)|
.

For any
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
x_{1},x_{2}\geq 0
, let

\displaystyle\rho_{t_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2}):=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[\left(X(t_{0}+hx_{1})-X(t_{0})\right)\left(X(t_{0}+hx_{2})-X(t_{0})\right)\big]}{|h|^{\alpha+1/2}}.

(2.1)

Elementary computation (cf.
[
29
, (3.33)]
) shows

\displaystyle\rho_{t_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2})=c_{2,1}t_{0}^{-\gamma}\left(x_{1}^{2\alpha+1}+x_{2}^{2\alpha+1}-|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2\alpha+1}\right),

(2.2)

where
c_{2,1}\in(0,\infty)
is a constant.
Hence,
\rho_{t_{0}}
is the covariance function of
c_{2,1}^{1/2}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}B^{\alpha+1/2}
, where

B^{\alpha+1/2}
is a FBM with index
\alpha+1/2
on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the closed linear subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega)
, generated by
\{B^{\alpha+1/2}(t),t\geq 0\}
. Let

\psi:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow C([0,1])
be defined by
\psi(\xi)(t):=\mathbb{E}\left[B^{\alpha+1/2}(t)\xi\right]
.
The reproducing kernel Hilbert space of the covariance function
\rho_{t_{0}}
is the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}:=\{\psi(\xi);\ \xi\in\mathcal{L}\},

with respect to the inner product
\langle\psi(\xi_{1}),\psi(\xi_{2})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\right]
.
We refer to Arcones
[
2
]
and Aronszajn
[
4
]
for more information on the reproducing kernel Hilbert space.

Let

I(z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\inf\{2^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\xi^{2}];\,\xi\in\mathcal{L},\psi(\xi)=z\},&\hbox{ if }\ z\in\mathcal{H},\\
+\infty,&\hbox{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

and let

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}:=\big\{z\in C([0,1]);\ I(z)\leq 1\big\}.

(2.4)

Let
X
be GFBM defined in (
1.1
) and, for notational convenience, denote
X(t):=0
if
t<0
.
For any
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
h\neq 0
, let
\mathcal{U}_{t_{0},h}=\{\mathcal{U}_{t_{0},h}(x)\}_{x\in[0,1]}

be the Gaussian process defined by (
1.5
).

The following is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2)
, then for all
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
,
with probability one, the family
\{\mathcal{U}_{t_{0},h}(x);x\in[0,1]\}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}
(as
h\rightarrow 0
)
is relatively compact on
C([0,1])
, and the set of its limit points is
\mathcal{S}
. Equivalently, for all
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\inf_{f\in\mathcal{S}}\|\mathcal{U}_{t_{0},h}-f\|_{\infty}=0\ \ \ \ \text{a.s.},

(2.5)

and for each
f\in\mathcal{S}
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{h\rightarrow 0}\|\mathcal{U}_{t_{0},h}-f\|_{\infty}=0\ \ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(2.6)

In particular, for every continuous function
F:C([0,1])\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}F(\mathcal{U}_{t_{0},h})=\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{S}}F(\xi)\right)=1.

(2.7)

Let us give some applications of Theorem
2.1
, which are partially inspired by Gantert
[
10
]
.

Example 2.1
.

(a)

Taking
F(\xi)=\xi(1)
, (
2.7
) yields the usual LIL for GFBM at any fixed point
t_{0}>0
:

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{|X(t_{0}+h)-X(t_{0})|}{h^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\ln\ln(1/|h|)\right)^{1/2}}=c_{2,2}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}\ \ \text{a.s.}

(2.8)

Notice that the usual LIL of GFBM
X
at the origin was obtained in
[
12
, Theorem 6.1]

and
[
29
, Proposition 7.1]
.

(b)

Taking
F(\xi)=\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|\xi(t)|
, (
2.7
) yields the local modulus of
continuity for GFBM,

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{|r|\leq h}\frac{|X(t_{0}+r)-X(t_{0})|}{h^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\ln\ln(1/h)\right)^{1/2}}=c_{2,3}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}\ \ \text{a.s.}

(2.9)

This was obtained in
[
29
, Theorem 1.5 (a)]
.

(c)

Taking
F(\xi)=\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1-\delta}|\xi(t+\delta)-\xi(t)|
for some
0<\delta<1
, (
2.7
) yields

\displaystyle\sup_{h\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{0\leq t\leq h(1-\delta)}\frac{|X(t_{0}+t+h\delta)-X(t_{0}+t)|}{h^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\ln\ln(1/h)\right)^{1/2}}=c_{2,4}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}\ \ \ \text{a.s}

(2.10)

(d)

Taking
F(\xi)=\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1-\delta}\sup_{0\leq s\leq\delta}|\xi(t+s)-\xi(t)|
for some
0<\delta<1
,
(
2.7
) yields

\displaystyle\sup_{h\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{0\leq t\leq h(1-\delta)}\sup_{0\leq s\leq h\delta}\frac{|X(t_{0}+t+s)-X(t_{0}+t)|}{h^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\ln\ln(1/h)\right)^{1/2}}=c_{2,5}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}\ \ \ \text{a.s}

(2.11)

Here
c_{2,2},\cdots,c_{2,5}
are constants in
(0,\infty)
.
The last two examples in (c) and (d) are the “small time statements”, which correspond to Corollary 1.2.2
in Csörgő and Révész
[
5
]
for Brownian motion.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

We consider a decomposition of GFBM
X
:

\begin{split}X(t)&=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\big((t-u)^{\alpha}-(-u)^{\alpha}\big)(-u)^{-\gamma/2}B(du)+\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{\alpha}u^{-\gamma/2}B(du)\\
&=:Y(t)+Z(t).\end{split}

(3.1)

The Gaussian processes
Y=\big\{Y(t)\big\}_{t\geq 0}
and
Z=\big\{Z(t)\big\}_{t\geq 0}
are independent. The
process
Z
in (
3.1
) is called a
generalized Riemann-Liouville FBM
by Ichiba, Pang and Taqqu
[
12
]
.

In Proposition 2.1 of
[
29
]
, we proved that
Y
has a modification that is
continuously differentiable of all orders in
(0,\infty)
. Hence, in order to prove Theorem
2.1
,
we only need to prove the conclusion with GFBM
X
in (
1.5
) being replaced by the generalized Riemann-Liouville
FBM
Z
.

Denote by
Z(t):=0
if
t<0
.
For any
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
h\neq 0
,
define

\displaystyle U_{t_{0},h}(x):=\frac{Z(t_{0}+hx)-Z(t_{0})}{t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}|h|^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\ln\ln(1/|h|)\right)^{1/2}}\ \ \ \text{for }x\in[0,1].

(3.2)

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2)
, then for all
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
,
with probability one, the family
\{U_{t_{0},h}(x);x\in[0,1]\}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}
defined by (
3.2
)
(as
h\rightarrow 0
) is relatively compact on
C([0,1])
, and the set of its limit points is
\mathcal{S}
.
Equivalently, for all
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\inf_{f\in\mathcal{S}}\|U_{t_{0},h}-f\|_{\infty}=0\ \ \ \ \text{a.s.},

(3.3)

and for each
f\in\mathcal{S}
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{h\rightarrow 0}\|U_{t_{0},h}-f\|_{\infty}=0\ \ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(3.4)

In particular, for every continuous function
F:C([0,1])\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}F(U_{t_{0},h})=\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{S}}F(\xi)\bigg)=1.

(3.5)

We prove Theorem
3.1
in the following parts.

3.1.
Some lemmas for the proof of Theorem
3.1

By
[
29
, Lemma 3.1]
,
there exist positive constants
c_{3,1},c_{3,2}
such that for all
0<s<t
,

\displaystyle c_{3,1}\frac{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}{t^{\gamma}}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\big(Z(t)-Z(s)\big)^{2}\right]\leq c_{3,2}\frac{|t-s|^{2\alpha+1}}{s^{\gamma}}.

(3.6)

These bounds are optimal when
s\leq t\leq c_{3,3}s
for any constant
c_{3,3}>1
.

3.1.1.
Large deviation principle for
\{U_{t_{0},h_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we need some technical lemmas. The following large deviation
principle for GFBM can be proved by applying Theorem 5.2 of Arcones
[
2
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
\alpha\in(-1/2+\gamma/2,1/2)
. Let
\{h_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}

be a sequence of real numbers with
h_{n}\rightarrow 0
, and let
\beta_{n}=\ln\ln(1/|h_{n}|)
. Then for all

t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, the sequence
\{U_{t_{0},h_{n}}(x);x\in[0,1]\}_{n\geq 1}
(as
n\rightarrow\infty
) satisfies
the large deviation principle on
C([0,1])
with the speed
\beta_{n}
and the rate function
I
defined
in (
2.3
), that is, the following statements hold:

(i).

For all
0\leq r<\infty
,
\{z\in C([0,1]);\ I(z)\leq r\}
is compact in
C([0,1])
.

(ii).

For all closed set
F\subset C([0,1])
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{n}^{-1}\log\mathbb{P}\big(U_{t_{0},h_{n}}\in F\big)\leq-\inf_{z\in F}I(z).

(3.7)

(iii).

For all open set
G\subset C([0,1])
,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{n}^{-1}\log\mathbb{P}\big(U_{t_{0},h_{n}}\in G\big)\geq-\inf_{z\in G}I(z).

(3.8)

To prove Proposition
3.1
, the following lemma from Talagrand
[
26
]
is needed.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
26
, Lemma 2.1]

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathcal{X}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
be a centered Gaussian process in

\mathbb{R}
and let
S\subset\mathbb{R}
be a closed set equipped with the
canonical metric defined by

d_{\mathcal{X}}(s,t)=\left[\mathbb{E}\big(\mathcal{X}(s)-\mathcal{X}(t)\big)^{2}\right]^{1/2}.

Then there exists a positive constant
c_{3,4}
such that for
all
u>0,

\mathbb{P}\Biggl\{\sup_{s,t\in S}|\mathcal{X}(s)-\mathcal{X}(t)|\geq c_{3,4}\,\bigg(u+\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log N(d_{\mathcal{X}},S,\varepsilon)}\,d\varepsilon\bigg)\Bigg\}\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{u^{2}}{D^{2}}\bigg),

(3.9)

where
N(d_{\mathcal{X}},S,\varepsilon)
denotes the smallest number of open
d_{\mathcal{X}}
-balls
of radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover
S
and where
D=\sup\{d_{\mathcal{X}}(s,t):\,s,\,t\in S\}
is the diameter of
S
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We prove this proposition by
verifying Conditions (a.1)-(a.4) of Theorem 5.2 in Arcones
[
2
]
.
For convenience, we only consider the case of
h\in(0,1]
, the case of
h\in[-1,0)
can be considered similarly.

For any
t_{0}\in(0,\infty),\,h\in(0,1]
, let

V_{t_{0},h}(x):=\frac{Z(t_{0}+hx)-Z(t_{0})}{h^{\alpha+1/2}},\ \ \ x\geq 0.

By Proposition 3.1 of Wang and Xiao
[
29
]
, we know that
the process
\big\{V_{t_{0},h}(x)\big\}_{x\geq 0}
(as
h\rightarrow 0
) converges in distribution to

\big\{c_{3,5}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}B^{\alpha+1/2}(x)\big\}_{x\geq 0}
in
C(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
,
where
B^{\alpha+1/2}
is a FBM with index
\alpha+1/2
and
c_{3,5}\in(0,\infty)
is a constant.
This implies that for any
x_{1},x_{2}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\big[V_{t_{0},h}(x_{1})V_{t_{0},u}(x_{2})\big]=\frac{c_{3,5}^{2}}{2}t_{0}^{-\gamma}\left(x_{1}^{2\alpha+1}+x_{2}^{2\alpha+1}-|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2\alpha+1}\right)

(3.10)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(V_{t_{0},h}(x_{1})-V_{t_{0},h}(x_{2})\big)^{2}\Big]=c_{3,5}^{2}t_{0}^{-\gamma}\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{2\alpha+1}.

(3.11)

Hence, Conditions (a.1) and (a.2) of Theorem 5.2 in Arcones
[
2
]
are satisfied.
Furthermore, by Lemma 3.1 of
[
29
]
, there exists a constant
c_{3,6}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
t_{0}>0,h\in(0,1],x_{1},x_{2}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(V_{t_{0},h}(x_{1})-V_{t_{0},h}(x_{2})\right)^{2}\right]\leq c_{3,6}t_{0}^{-\gamma}\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{2\alpha+1}.

(3.12)

This implies Condition (a.4) of Theorem 5.2 in
[
2
]
.

It remains to verify Condition (a.3) of Theorem 5.2 in
[
2
]
, that is

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|U_{t_{0},h_{n}}(x)|{\longrightarrow}0,\ \ \ \text{ \ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

(3.13)

in probability.
We will make use of Lemma
3.1
to prove (
3.13
).
For any

h_{n}\in(0,t_{0}/2)
, let
S=[t_{0},t_{0}+h_{n}]
. By (
3.6
), we see that

d_{Z}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq c_{3,7}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha+1/2}
for some constant
c_{3,7}\in(0,\infty)
. Hence

D\leq c_{3,7}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}h_{n}^{\alpha+1/2}
and

N(d_{Z},S,\varepsilon)\leq c_{3,7}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}h_{n}t_{0}^{-\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha+1}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}.

Thus, by making the change of variables
\varepsilon=c_{3,7}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}(h_{n}v)^{\alpha+1/2}
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log\big(N(d_{Z},S,\varepsilon)\big)}d\varepsilon\leq&\,\int_{0}^{c_{3,7}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}h_{n}^{\alpha+1/2}}\sqrt{\log\left(c_{3,7}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}t_{0}^{-\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha+1}}h_{n}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}}\right)}d\varepsilon\\
\leq&\,(\alpha+1/2)c_{3,7}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}h_{n}^{\alpha+1/2}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\log(1/v)}v^{\alpha-1/2}dv\\
\leq&\,c_{3,8}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}h_{n}^{\alpha+1/2},\end{split}

(3.14)

here
c_{3,8}\in(0,\infty)
and we have used the fact that
\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\log(1/v)}v^{\alpha-1/2}dv<+\infty

since
\alpha-1/2>-1
in the last inequality.

Since
\beta_{n}=\ln\ln(1/h_{n})\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, by using Lemma
3.1
and (
3.14
),
there exists a constant
c_{3,9}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|U_{t_{0},h_{n}}(x)|\geq\varepsilon\right)=

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\frac{|Z(t_{0}+hx)-Z(t_{0})|}{\beta_{n}^{1/2}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}h_{n}^{\alpha+1/2}}\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\exp\Big(-c_{3,9}\beta_{n}\varepsilon^{2}\Big)\rightarrow 0,\ \ \text{as }n\rightarrow\infty.

This verifies (
3.13
) and the proof of Proposition
3.1
is complete. ∎

3.1.2.
A Fernique-type inequality

To prove Theorem
3.1
,
we need the following Fernique-type inequality for the generalized Riemann-Liouville FBM
Z
, whose proof
is similar to that of Lemma 2.4 in
[
32
]
. Here, we give a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exist constants
u_{0}\geq 1
and
c_{3,10}>0

such that for any
\delta>0
,
0<a\leq\delta
, and
u\geq u_{0}
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}\sup_{s\in[0,a]}\left|Z(t_{0}+t+s)-Z(t_{0}+t)\right|\geq u\left(\sqrt{\log(\delta/a)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log(\delta/a)}}\right)a^{\alpha+1/2}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}\right)\\
&\leq\,\exp\big(-c_{3,10}u^{2}\big).\end{split}

Proof.

Define the Gaussian process
\xi=\{\xi(t,s);t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}
by

\xi(t,s):=Z(t_{0}+t+s)-Z(t_{0}+t)\ \ \text{for }t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Equip
S:=[0,\delta]\times[0,a]
. By (
3.6
), there exists a constant
c_{3,11>0}
satisfying that

\begin{split}\,d_{\xi}\big((t_{1},s_{1}),(t_{2},s_{2})\big)\leq&\,2\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\big(Z(t_{0}+t_{1}+s_{1})-Z(t_{0}+t_{2}+s_{2})\big)^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}\\
&\,\,+2\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\big(Z(t_{0}+t_{1})-\xi(t_{0}+t_{2})\big)^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}\\
\leq&\,c_{3,11}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}\left(|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\alpha+1/2}+|s_{1}-s_{2}|^{\alpha+1/2}\right).\end{split}

(3.15)

On the other hand, we have the diameter

\begin{split}D=&\sup_{(t_{i},s_{i})\in S,i=1,2}\Bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big(\big[Z(t_{0}+t_{1}+s_{1})-Z(t_{0}+t_{1})\big]\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -\big[Z(t_{0}+t_{2}+s_{2})-Z(t_{0}+t_{2}+s_{2})\big]\Big)^{2}\Bigg\}^{1/2}\\
\leq&\,2\left(\mathbb{E}\big[\big(Z(t_{0}+t_{1}+s_{1})-Z(t_{0}+t_{1})\big)^{2}\big]\right)^{1/2}\\
&\,\,+2\left(\mathbb{E}\big[\big(Z(t_{0}+t_{2}+s_{2})-\xi(t_{0}+t_{2})\big)^{2}\big]\right)^{1/2}\\
\leq&\,c_{3,11}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}a^{\alpha+1/2}\end{split}

(3.16)

and

N(d_{\xi},S,\varepsilon)\leq c_{3,11}^{\frac{2}{\alpha+1/2}}t_{0}^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha+1/2}}\frac{a\delta}{\varepsilon^{2/(\alpha+1/2)}}.

(3.17)

Consequently, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log(N(d_{\xi},S,\varepsilon))}d\varepsilon\\
\leq&\,\int_{0}^{c_{3,11}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}a^{\alpha+1/2}}\sqrt{\log\left((c_{3,11})^{\frac{2}{\alpha+1/2}}t_{0}^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha+1/2}}\frac{a\delta}{\varepsilon^{2/(\alpha+1/2)}}\right)}d\varepsilon\\
\leq&\,c_{3,11}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}\int_{0}^{a}\sqrt{2\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{a\delta}}{t}\right)}dt^{\alpha+1/2}\\
=&\,c_{3,11}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}\left(\sqrt{\log(\delta/a)}a^{\alpha+1/2}+2\int_{0}^{a}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{a\delta}}{t}\right)}}t^{\alpha-1/2}dt\right)\\
\leq&\,c_{3,11}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}\left(\sqrt{\log(\delta/a)}a^{\alpha+1/2}+\frac{4}{\sqrt{\log(\delta/a)}}a^{\alpha+1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}ue^{-(\alpha+1/2)u^{2}}du\right)\\
\leq&\,4c_{3,11}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}ue^{-(\alpha+1/2)u^{2}}du\left(\sqrt{\log(\delta/a)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log(\delta/a)}}\right)a^{\alpha+1/2},\end{split}

(3.18)

where the changes of the variables
\varepsilon=c_{3,11}t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}t^{\alpha+1/2}
and
t=ae^{-u^{2}}

are used in the first inequality and in the second last inequality, respectively.

Thus, by Lemma
3.1
, we get (
3.2
). The proof is complete.
∎

3.1.3.
Lamperti’s transformation of
Z

To prove (
3.4
),
we consider Lamperti’s transformation of
Z
inspired by
[
28
]
and
[
29
]
.

Consider the centered stationary Gaussian process
U=\{U(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

defined through Lamperti’s transformation of
Z
:

\displaystyle U(t):=e^{-tH}Z(e^{t})\ \ \ \text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.19)

Let
r_{U}(t):=\mathbb{E}\big[U(0)U(t)\big]
be
the covariance function of
U
. By Bochner’s theorem,
r_{U}
is the Fourier transform of a finite measure
F_{U}

which is called the spectral measure of
U
. Notice that
r_{U}(t)
is an even function and

\displaystyle r_{U}(t)=

\displaystyle\,e^{-tH}\int_{0}^{1\wedge e^{t}}(e^{t}-u)^{\alpha}(1-u)^{\alpha}u^{-\gamma}du\ \ \ \ \text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.20)

We can verify that
r_{U}(t)=O(e^{-t(1-\gamma)/2})
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
It follows that
r_{U}(\cdot)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Hence the spectral measure
F_{U}
has a continuous spectral
density function
f_{U}
which can be represented as the inverse Fourier transform of
r_{U}(\cdot)
:

f_{U}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r_{U}(t)\cos(t\lambda)\,dt\quad\hbox{ for all }\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

It is known that the stationary Gaussian process
U
has the stochastic integral representation:

\displaystyle U(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\lambda t}\,W(d\lambda)\ \ \ \ \text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

(3.21)

where
W
is a complex Gaussian measure with control measure
F_{U}
.

Recall the following inequalities from Wang and Xiao
[
29
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
29
, Theorem 4.2]

Assume
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2)
. There exist positive constants
u_{0}>0
and

c_{3,12}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
u>u_{0}
,

\displaystyle\int_{|\lambda|<u}\lambda^{2}f_{U}(\lambda)d\lambda\leq c_{3,12}u^{1-2\alpha}

(3.22)

and

\displaystyle\int_{|\lambda|\geq u}f_{U}(\lambda)d\lambda\leq c_{3,12}u^{-(2\alpha+1)}.

(3.23)

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

To prove Theorem
3.1
, it is sufficient
to prove (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). We prove them in the following parts.

3.2.1.
Proof of (
3.3
)

Proof of (
3.3
).

For convenience, we only consider the case of
h\in I:=[0,1]
, the case of
h\in[-1,0]
can be considered similarly.
Let
1<\theta<2
be a constant which will be chosen later. For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let
h_{k}:=\theta^{-k}
and let

A_{k}:=\left\{h\in I:\,\theta^{-(k+1)}\leq h\leq\theta^{-k}\right\}.

By (
3.7
), for all
\varepsilon>0
and
\mu>0
, there exists a constant
k_{0}>0
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{f\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|U_{t_{0},h_{k}}-f\|\geq\varepsilon\right)&=\,\mathbb{P}\big(U_{t_{0},h_{k}}\in(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\varepsilon})^{c}\big)\\
&\leq\,c_{3,13}\exp\left(-(1-\mu)\inf_{\xi\in(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\varepsilon})^{c}}I(\xi)\log\log(\theta^{k})\right)\\
&\leq\,c_{3,14}k^{-(1-\mu)\inf_{\xi\in(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\varepsilon})^{c}}I(\xi)},\end{split}

(3.24)

where
c_{3,13},c_{3,14}\in(0,\infty)
,
E^{c}
is the complement of the set
E
and

E^{\varepsilon}:=\left\{g\in C([0,1]):\,\inf_{f\in E}\|f-g\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\right\}.

Take
\mu

\eta:=(1-\mu)\inf_{\xi\in(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\varepsilon})^{c}}I(\xi)>1.

Thus, the last terms in (
3.24
) are summable in
k
. By the Borel-Cantelli lemma, we have

L_{1}:=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{f\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|U_{t_{0},h_{k}}-f\|_{\infty}=0,\ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(3.25)

By Lemma
3.2
with
\delta=\theta^{-(k-1)}
and
a=(1-\theta^{-1})\theta^{-(k-1)}
, we have for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\begin{split}&\,\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\theta^{-(k-1)}]}\sup_{s\in[0,(1-\theta^{-1})\theta^{-(k-1)}]}\frac{|Z(t_{0}+t+s)-Z(t_{0}+t)|}{\theta^{-(k-1)(\alpha+1/2)}\sqrt{\log\log\theta^{(k-1)(\alpha+1/2)}}}>\varepsilon\right)\\
\leq&\,\exp\left(-c_{3,15}c(\theta)\varepsilon^{2}\log\log\theta^{(k-1)(\alpha+1/2)}\right),\end{split}

(3.26)

where
c_{3,15}\in(0,\infty)
and

c(\theta):=\frac{(1-\theta^{-1})^{-2(\alpha+1)}}{\left(\sqrt{\log\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}}+1/{\sqrt{\log\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}}}\right)^{2}}.

Letting
1<\theta<2
small enough close to
1
such that
c_{3,15}c(\theta)\varepsilon^{2}>1
, the sum of the left-hand
side of (
3.26
) is finite. By the Borel-Cantelli lemma,

\begin{split}L_{2}:=&\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{h\in A_{k}}\|U_{t_{0},h}-U_{t_{0},h_{k}}\|\\
=&\,\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{h\in A_{k}}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\frac{|Z(t_{0}+hx)-Z(t_{0}+h_{k}x)|}{h^{\alpha+1/2}\sqrt{\log\log(1/h)}}\\
\leq&\,\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,\theta^{-k}]}\sup_{s\in[0,(1-\theta)^{-1}\theta^{-k}]}\frac{|Z(t_{0}+t+s)-Z(t_{0}+t)|}{\theta^{-(k+1)(\alpha+1/2)}\sqrt{\log\log\theta^{(k+1)(\alpha+1/2)}}}\\

(3.27)

Therefore, (
3.25
) and (
3.27
) yield (

). ∎

3.2.2.
Proof of (
3.4
)

Proof of (
3.4
).

Fix
f\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Let
0<\tau<1
. For
n\geq 1
, let
h_{n}=e^{-n^{1+\tau}}
. Then for proving (
2.6
), it is enough to show that

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|U_{t_{0},h_{n}}-f\|_{\infty}=0\ \ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(3.28)

By (
3.8
), for all
\varepsilon>0
and
\mu>0
, there exists a constant
n_{0}>0
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\big(\|U_{t_{0},h_{n}}-f\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\big)=&\,\mathbb{P}\big(U_{t_{0},h_{n}}\in B(f,\varepsilon)\big)\\
\geq&\,c_{3,16}\exp\left(-(1+\mu)\log\log(1/h_{n})\inf_{g\in B(f,\varepsilon)}I(g)\right)\\
\geq&\,c_{3,16}n^{-(1+\tau)(1+\mu)\inf_{g\in B(f,\varepsilon)}I(g)},\end{split}

(3.29)

where
c_{3,16}\in(0,\infty)
and

B(f,\varepsilon):=\Big\{g\in C([0,1]);\,\|g-f\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\Big\}.

Since
\inf_{g\in B(f,\varepsilon)}I(g)<1
, taking
\mu
and
\tau
small enough, we have

(1+\tau)(1+\mu)\inf_{g\in B(f,\varepsilon)}I(g)\leq 1.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\|U_{t_{0},h_{n}}-f\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\right)=+\infty.

(3.30)

Since the events in the summands of the series in (
3.30
) are not independent, some additional care
is needed in order to finish the proof.
As in Tudor and Xiao
[
28
]
and Wang and Xiao
[
30
]
,
we will make use of the following stochastic integral representation of
Z
:

Z(t)=t^{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\lambda\ln t}\,W(d\lambda),\ \ \ \ \ t>0.

(3.31)

This follows from the spectral representation (
3.21
)
of
U
.

For
n\geq 3
, let

d_{n}:=\exp\left(n^{1+\tau}+n^{\tau}\right).

Define two Gaussian processes
Z_{n}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{n}
by

Z_{n}(t)=t^{H}\int_{|\lambda|\in(d_{n-1},d_{n}]}e^{i\lambda\ln t}\,W(d\lambda)

(3.32)

and

\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t)=t^{H}\int_{|\lambda|\notin(d_{n-1},d_{n}]}e^{i\lambda\ln t}\,W(d\lambda),

(3.33)

respectively. Clearly
Z(t)=Z_{n}(t)+\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t)
for all
t>0
. It is
important to note that the Gaussian processes
Z_{n}\,(n=1,2,\ldots)
are
independent and, moreover, for every
n\geq 1,

Z_{n}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{n}

are independent as well.

For any
|s|<h_{n}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t+s)-\widetilde{Z}_{n}(t)\big)^{2}\Big)&=\int_{|\lambda|\leq d_{n-1}}\big|(t+s)^{H}\,e^{i\lambda\ln(t+s)}-t^{H}\,e^{i\lambda\ln t}\big|^{2}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
&\quad+\int_{|\lambda|>d_{n}}\big|(t+s)^{H}\,e^{i\lambda\ln(t+s)}-t^{H}\,e^{i\lambda\ln t}\big|^{2}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
&=:\mathcal{J}_{1}+\mathcal{J}_{2}.\end{split}

(3.34)

The second term
\mathcal{J}_{2}
is easy to estimate: For any
|s|\leq t_{n}
, by (
3.23
), there exists
c_{3,17}\in(0,\infty)
satisfying that

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}_{2}\leq&\,2\,\left(t^{2H}+(t+s)^{2H}\right)\,\int_{|\lambda|>d_{n}}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
\leq&\,c_{3,17}\,\,d_{n}^{-(2\alpha+1)}\\
=&\,c_{3,17}\,\,h_{n}^{2\alpha+1}\exp\left(-(2\alpha+1)n^{\tau}\right).\end{split}

(3.35)

For the first term
\mathcal{J}_{1}
, we use the following elementary inequalities:

\displaystyle\left|(x+y)^{H}-x^{H}\right|\leq|H|\max\left\{x^{H-1},(x+y)^{H-1}\right\}|y|,\ \ \ \text{ for all }0\leq|y|<x,

for all

and

for all

to derive that for any

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}_{1}=&\,\int_{|\lambda|\leq d_{n-1}}\left[\left((t+s)^{H}-t^{H}\right)^{2}+2(t+s)^{H}\,t^{H}\Big(1-\cos\Big(\lambda\ln\frac{t+s}{t}\Big)\Big)\right]\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\

&\quad\ +2(t+s)^{H}\,t^{H}\,\ln^{2}\left(1+\frac{s}{t}\right)\,\int_{|\lambda|\leq d_{n-1}}\lambda^{2}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
\leq&\,c_{3,18}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda+\int_{|\lambda|\leq d_{n-1}}\lambda^{2}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\right)h_{n}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.36)

Here
c_{3,18}
is in
(0,\infty)
.
By the continuity of
f_{U}
and (
3.23
), we know

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda<+\infty.

(3.37)

By (
3.22
), we obtain that for all
n
large enough,

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{|\lambda|\leq d_{n-1}}\lambda^{2}\,f_{U}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\right)h_{n}^{2}\\
\leq&\,c_{3,12}\exp\left\{(1-2\alpha)\left[(n-1)^{1+\tau}+(n-1)^{\tau}\right]\right\}\exp\left(-2n^{1+\tau}\right)\\
=&\,c_{3,12}\exp\left\{-(1-2\alpha)\left[n^{1+\tau}-(n-1)^{1+\tau}-(n-1)^{\tau}\right]\right\}\exp\left(-(1+2\alpha)n^{1+\tau}\right)\\
\leq&\,c_{3,12}\exp\left\{-(1-2\alpha)\tau n^{\tau}/2\right\}\exp\left(-(1+2\alpha)n^{1+\tau}\right),\end{split}

(3.38)

where we have used the fact that

for all

large enough in the last step.

Thus, by (

)-(

), we obtain that for any
\alpha<1/2
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}+\mathcal{J}_{2}\leq c_{3,19}e^{-c_{3,20}n^{\tau}}e^{-(2\alpha+1)n^{1+\tau}},

(3.39)

for some constants
c_{3,19},c_{3,20}\in(0,\infty)
. Therefore,
the diameter
D
of
S:=[t_{0},t_{0}+h_{n}]
is less than
c_{3,19}^{1/2}\exp\left(-c_{3,20}n^{\tau}/2\right)h_{n}^{\alpha+1/2}

for all

large enough.

Note that

\displaystyle d_{\tilde{Z}_{n}}^{2}(s,t)=

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{E}\left[|\tilde{Z}_{n}(t_{0}+s)-\tilde{Z}_{n}(t_{0}+t)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{E}\left[|Z(t_{0}+s)-Z(t_{0}+t)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\,c_{3,21}\frac{|t-s|^{\alpha+1/2}}{t_{0}^{\gamma/2}}

for some constant

.

\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log(N(d_{\tilde{Z}_{n}},S,\varepsilon))}d\varepsilon\leq c_{3,22}\exp\big(-c_{3,23}n^{\tau}\big)h_{n}^{\alpha+1/2},

for some constants

.
Hence, by Lemma
3.1
,
there exist

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left|\tilde{Z}_{n}(t_{0}+h_{n}x)-\tilde{Z}_{n}(t_{0})\right|\geq u\exp\left(-c_{3,24}n^{\tau}\right)h_{n}^{\alpha+1/2}\right)\leq e^{-c_{3,25}u^{2}}.

(3.40)

This implies that for all

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left|\tilde{Z}_{n}(t_{0}+h_{n}x)-\tilde{Z}_{n}(t_{0})\right|}{t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}h_{n}^{\alpha+1/2}\sqrt{\log\log(1/h_{n})}}\geq\varepsilon\right)<+\infty.

(3.41)

Hence, by the Borel-Cantelli lemma, we have

I_{1}:=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\frac{\left|\tilde{Z}_{n}(t_{0}+h_{n}x)-\tilde{Z}_{n}(t_{0})\right|}{t_{0}^{-\gamma/2}h_{n}^{\alpha+1/2}\sqrt{\log\log(1/h_{n})}}=0\ \ \ \text{a.s.}

(3.42)

It follows from (

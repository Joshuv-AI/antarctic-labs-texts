Given a complex manifold
\mathcal{M}
, let
T\mathcal{M}
and

denote respectively its real and complex tangent bundles. Assume that

is a relatively compact domain with

boundary. The complex structure on
\mathcal{M}
or its restriction on
M
will be denoted by
H_{st}
.
An almost complex structure on the closure
\overline{M}
is defined by a complex subbundle
H
of
T_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{M}:=(T\mathcal{M}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})|_{\overline{M}}
such that
T_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{M}=H\oplus\overline{H}
.
For each
p\in U_{k}\cap\overline{M}
,

has a basis

X_{k,\overline{\alpha}}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{n}\left\{A_{k,\overline{\alpha}}^{\beta}(z_{k})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}^{\beta}}+B_{k,\overline{\alpha}}^{\overline{\beta}}(z_{k})\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{k}^{\beta}}\right\},\quad\alpha=1,\dots,n,

when

are holomorphic coordinates on
U_{k}\subset\mathcal{M}
with
\overline{M}\subset\cup U_{k}
. The almost complex structure will also be denoted by
H

We always assume that

has

from the standard complex structure on
\overline{M}
, i.e.
(B_{k,\overline{\alpha}}^{\overline{\beta}})_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n}
are invertible. By changing bases, we take

as the identity matrix. This determines

uniquely and

is a global section of
\Theta

(0,1)
form on
\overline{M}
.

H
is

if it is closed under the Lie bracket

, or equivalently, if
\overline{\partial}A=-\frac{1}{2}[A,A]
(see Section

We say that the almost complex structure

is

on the interior of

if for each

, there exist a neighborhood

of

in
M
and a diffeomorphism

from

into

such that

, i.e.

are in the span of

. Assuming the existence of

is closed under the Lie bracket, we know that

is closed under the Lie bracket. In other words, if

is locally integrable on

The celebrated Newlander-Nirenberg theorem
[
38

states that the converse is true locally. Namely, at each interior point

is formally integrable near

of

on which

from

into

as a

for

This classical theorem now has several proofs due to Kohn
[
22
]
, Malgrange
[
32
]
and Webster
[
48
]
. See Yao
[
51
]
for counter-examples on sharpness of the coordinate maps. The reader is also referred to the optimal regularity results in Street
[
47
]
on the elliptic structures in general.

The global analogue of the above result, known as the global Newlander-Nirenberg problem, can be stated as follows. Suppose the almost complex structure
H
is formally integrable on
\overline{M}
and is sufficiently close to the complex structure on
\mathcal{M}
, is
H
integrable on
\overline{M}
? i.e. does there exist a global embedding
F
for
H:\overline{M}\to\mathcal{M}
?
Here by "sufficiently close" we mean that the
A
given above is small in certain norms.
A similar local boundary version of the Newlander-Nirenberg problem asks whether a formally integrable
H
admits an embedding near boundary point of the domain
M
.

It turns out that certain geometric assumption on the boundary is needed for the global or boundary Newlander-Nirenberg theorem to hold.
We now describe this phenomena.
Following Hörmander
[
21
]
,
we say that the domain
M
(or its boundary
bM
) satisfies the
condition
a_{q}
, if
M=\{\rho(p)<0\colon p\in\mathcal{M}\}
has a
C^{2}
definition function
\rho
,
and the Levi-form of
\rho
at each boundary point
p\in U_{k}\cap bM
, defined by
L_{p}(\rho,t)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial z_{k}^{\alpha}\partial{\overline{z}_{k}^{\beta}}}(p)t^{\alpha}\overline{t^{\beta}}
, has at least
n-q
positive or
q+1
negative eigenvalues on
T^{(1,0)}_{p}(bM):=\left\{\sum_{\alpha}t^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}^{\alpha}}\big|_{p}\colon\sum_{\alpha}t^{\alpha}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z_{k}^{\alpha}}(p)=0\right\}
. In
[
20
]
, Hill showed that if the Levi form on the boundary of the domain has exactly
1
negative eigenvalue, then there are many formally integrable smooth almost complex structures for which local embedding for the structures fails to exist at each boundary point.

We now describe positive results.
In pioneering work
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, Hamilton proved the existence of a

global embedding for

on
\overline{M}
when both

are

and

is
a_{1}
. Hamilton’s result has to assume that the perturbation
A
is small in some norm
\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda^{r_{0}}}
with unspecified (large)

.
Catlin
[
2
]
and Hanges–Jacobowitz
[
17
]
solved a local version of Newlander–Nirenberg problem near boundary points of a strong pseudoconvex domain. It is worth emphasizing that all these results are only for
C^{\infty}
category for the structures and the boundary of the domains, and the proofs are based on
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann type methods.

When the domain is strongly pseudoconvex in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, Gan and the first-named author
[
8
]
showed the existence of global solutions that have a loss of derivatives with respect to the finite smoothness of the given almost complex structure. This result has been improved significantly in the very recent work of the second-named author
[
44
]
.

Therefore, Theorem
1.1
proves the existence of solution for the global Newlander–Nirenberg problem on
a_{1}^{+}
domains (i.e. the ones satisfying
a_{1},a_{2}
conditions) with the almost 1/2 regularity gain.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we will employ the homotopy formula methods together with a modified Nash-Moser type iteration.
The idea is to find a sequence of embedding
F^{j}:M_{j}\to M_{j+1}
, with
M_{0}=M
, so that the the error
A^{j}
converges to
0
. We then show that the maps
\widetilde{F}_{j}:=F^{j}\circ F^{j-1}\circ\cdots\circ F^{0}
converge to the embedding
F
which is the solution.

These techniques were originally employed by Webster
[
48
,
50
,
49
]
to investigate the classical Newlander–Nirenberg theorem, the CR vector bundle problem, and the more challenging
local CR embedding problem. These techniques, together with a more precise interior regularity estimate for Henkin’s integral solution operators of
\overline{\partial}_{b}
on strong pseudoconvex real hypersurfaces, were successfully used by Gong–Webster
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
to obtain a sharp version of CR vector bundle problem and the local CR embedding problem; see
[
12
]
also for a parameter version of Frobenius–Nirenberg theorem
by using similar techniques.
The scheme of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is similar to the ones in Gan–Gong
[
12
]
and Shi
[
44
]
. However, we mention an important new feature in the present work. Here we must deal with the failure of
local
homotopy formula for
a_{1}
domains near the concave side of the boundary. Noticeably, the lack of homotopy formulas with good estimates was shown by Nagel–Rosay
[
37
]
in the situation for local CR embedding problem in dimension

. In our setting, it was proved by Laurent-Thiébaut and Leiterer
[
28
]
*Prop. 0.7 that there is no

homotopy formula with good estimates for
(0,q)
forms near a boundary point of the concave side of some

domain.

For the proof Theorem

3.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first recall elementary properties of Hölder norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{C^{a}(M)}
with
0\leq a<\infty
and Hölder-Zygmund norms
\|{\cdot}\|_{\Lambda^{a}(M)}
with
0<a<\infty

where

For abbreviation, we also write
\|\cdot\|_{C^{a}(M)}
as
\|\cdot\|_{M,a}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda^{a}(M)}
as

.
We then describe some properties of the Nash-Moser smoothing operator.

A domain

is called a

, if there is a finite open covering
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of
\overline{D}
such that

V_{i}
is relatively compact in
D
or

is given by

where
(y^{\prime},y_{d})
is a permutation of the standard coordinates of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
h_{i}
is a Lipschitz function on some ball
V_{i}^{\prime}
.
We say that a constant
C(D)
is
stable
under perturbations of
D
if
C(D)
depends only on upper bounds of
N
and Lipschitz norms of
h_{1},\dots,h_{N}
.

A constant
C(\tilde{\rho})
, depending on functions
\tilde{\rho}
, is

under smaller perturbations of a

function

if
C(\tilde{\rho})
can be chosen independent of
\tilde{\rho}
provided
\|\tilde{\rho}-\rho\|_{C^{2}}<\delta
for some
\delta
depends only on
\rho
. Such a constant will be denoted by

.

Lemma 3.5

Fix an integer

Proof.

Consider

. Let us find smooth functions
\chi_{0},\dots,\chi_{m_{*}}
with compact support in

such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{j}c_{j}\chi_{j}(t)\,dt=1,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{j}c_{j}\chi_{j}(t)t^{i}\,dt=0,\quad i=1,\dots,m_{*}.

It suffices to find
\chi_{0},\dots,\chi_{m_{*}}
such that

A_{m_{*}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{0}(t)\,dt&\cdots&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{m_{*}}(t)\,dt\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{m_{*}}\chi_{0}(t)\,dt&\cdots&\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{m_{*}}\chi_{m_{*}}(t)\,dt\\
\end{array}\right)

is non-singular.

The case

is trivial. Suppose we have found

so that

is non-singular.
By row reduction, we find a matrix

such that

is an upper triangular matrix with all diagonal entries being non-zero except for possible the last one:

where

Since

For

[\overline{\partial},S^{t}]v:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}[\overline{\partial},S^{t}]S^{\varepsilon}v

7.3.

When we deal with a domain

The latter is independent of
\zeta
. This shows that
|F|_{M,\ell}<\infty
for any
\ell<m+\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

A.1.
Case of special Lipschitz domains

We will only focus on the case that
\Omega
is a
special Lipschitz domain
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
given by

x_{n}>\omega(x^{\prime}),\quad x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}

with
|\omega(\tilde{x}^{\prime})-\omega(x^{\prime})|\leq A|\tilde{x}^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|
. Then the cone
K
defined by
x_{n}>A|x^{\prime}|
satisfies

\Omega+K\subset\Omega
. We call

a Lipschitz constant of
\Omega
.

Assume that
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We describe some properties on
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)
, which is the Besov space
B^{s}_{\infty,\infty}(\Omega)
as defined in
[
40
,
4
]
when
\Omega
is a special Lipschitz domain and more general a bounded Lipschitz domain.

Let
\varphi_{0}
be a smooth function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with compact support in
-K
and
\int\varphi_{0}\,dx=1
. Define

\varphi(x)=\varphi_{0}(x)-2^{-n}\varphi_{0}(x/2),\quad\varphi_{j}(x)=2^{jn}\varphi(2^{j}x),\quad j\geq 1.

We say that
\varphi
satisfies the
moment condition
of order
L_{\varphi}
, namely

\int x^{\alpha}\varphi(x)\,dx=0,\quad|\alpha|\leq L_{\varphi}.

Analogously, suppose
\lambda_{0}\in\mathcal{D}(-K)
also satisfies

.

We have
\varphi_{0}+\cdots+\varphi_{j}=2^{jn}\varphi_{0}(2^{j}x)
. For
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
, we have

f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}\ast f.

For
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, define

\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}}:=\sup_{j}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}2^{sj}|\varphi_{j}\ast f(x)|.

Note that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},j}2^{sj}|\varphi_{j}\ast f(x)|.
Define for
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)}=\inf\Bigl\{\|\tilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}}\colon\text{$\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{\mathcal{}}D^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}=f$ in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)$}\Bigr\}.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)
be the set of all such
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
with
\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)}<\infty
, i.e.
\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}=f
for some
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{C}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. When
s>0
,
\Lambda^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\mathcal{C}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
in equivalent norms (see
[
4

and references therein.)

The Peetre-type maximum functions of

\varphi_{j,N}^{\Omega}f(x)=\sup_{y\in\Omega}\frac{|\varphi_{j}\ast f(y)|}{(1+2^{j}|x-y|)^{N}},\quad x\in\Omega.

Remark A.1

It holds trivially that for

This is a consequence of estimate

C2^{-|\ell-j|(L_{\lambda}+1)},&j<\ell\end{cases}

for

(

A.2.

Let

Then Theorem

implies that

Corollary A.9
.

Suppose that
s>0
. Theorem
A.7
holds when
\Omega
is a bounded Liptschitz domain i.e.

C_{s}^{-1}\|f\|_{C^{s}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|^{*}_{\mathcal{C}^{s}_{M}(\Omega)}\leq C_{s}\|f\|_{C^{s}(\Omega)}

and
C_{s}
depends only on the Liptschitz norm of
\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

Fix
f\in C^{s}(\Omega)
. By definition, there is
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{C}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\|\tilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}}\leq 2\|f\|_{C^{s}(\Omega)}
. On
\Omega
we have
f=\sum\chi_{j}\tilde{f}
. Inductively, we can verify that

\Delta^{M}_{h}(f\chi)(x)=\sum_{i,j,k}\sum_{j\leq i,j\leq M-k}C_{i,j,k}\Delta^{i}_{h}\tilde{f}(x+jh)\Delta^{M-i}_{h}\chi(x+kh).

Therefore,
|\Delta^{M}_{h}(f\chi)(x)|\leq C_{s}\sum_{i=0}^{M}|h|^{s-M+i}|\tilde{f}|_{\mathcal{C}^{s-i}}|h|^{M-i}\|\chi\|_{C^{M-i}}.

On
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, it is well-known that
\|\tilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{a}}\leq C_{a,b}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{b}}
for
a<b
. This shows that

\|f\|^{*}_{\mathcal{C}^{s}_{M}(\Omega)}\leq C_{s}\|\tilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}}\leq 2C_{s}\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)}
.

For the other inequality, we start with

\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)}=\|\sum\chi_{j}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)}\leq\sum\|\chi_{j}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega)}\leq\sum\|\chi_{j}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega_{j})}.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{j}
be the Rychkov extension for
\Omega_{j}
. We have

\mathcal{E}_{j}(\chi_{j}f)=\sum_{k}\psi_{j,k}\ast(\varphi_{j,k}\ast(\chi_{j}f))_{\Omega_{j}}.

By Theorem
A.6
(b), we have
\|\chi_{j}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega_{j})}\leq\|\mathcal{E}_{j}(\chi_{j}f)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C_{s}\|\chi_{j}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega_{j})}
. By Theorem
A.7
for special Lipschitz domains, we get
\|\chi_{j}f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\Omega_{j})}\leq\|\chi_{j}f\|_{\mathcal{C}_{M}^{s}(\Omega_{j})}
. Hence
\|f\|_{C^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C_{s}\|f\|^{*}_{\mathcal{C}^{s}_{M}(\Omega)}
. ∎

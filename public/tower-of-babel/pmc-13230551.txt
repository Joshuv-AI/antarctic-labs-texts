Optimization of urban freight intelligent route based on reinforcement learning
Authors: Guizhe Xin, Yuqing Tang, Hongzhen Gao, Na Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Increased traffic congestion, limited delivery windows, vehicles’ diverse fleets, and dynamic environments are all contributing factors to the inefficiency of urban freight systems. Due to the inability of traditional routing techniques to adjust in real-time to partially visible multi-constrained conditions, delivery delays, fuel consumption, and operating costs all increased. This research proposes a constraint-aware projected policy learning-reinforcement learning (CAPPL-RL) framework to optimize urban freight delivery routes while enforcing real-world constraints, including traffic congestion, delivery time windows, and vehicle capacity. The routing problem is formulated as a partially observable Markov decision process, with autonomous vehicles as agents navigating stochastic urban traffic networks. Using the UFVOD dataset, CAPPL-RL integrates Q-learning with ε-greedy exploration and projection-based constrained policy optimization (PCPO) to enable adaptive, constraint-aware routing. Simulation results demonstrate that CAPPL-RL outperforms PCPO-RL, reducing average delivery time from 65.3 to 52.1 min (20.2%), fuel consumption from 0.12 to 0.093 L/km (22.5%), and constraint violations in time windows from 12 to 3 (75%) while achieving 100% compliance with vehicle capacity limits. These results validate CAPPL-RL as a robust, scalable, and adaptive framework for dynamic urban freight logistics.

Introduction

The population depends on urban logistics and transportation, although concerns like pollution, accidents, and traffic jams, particularly in freight transportation, continue to persist1,2. Intelligent systems are utilized by urban logistics and planning to optimize freight vehicle routes and parking. Real-time data and intelligent algorithms are used to reduce delivery times, traffic, and fuel usage3,4. Traffic patterns and possible delays are able to be identified with the support of real-time data obtained from sensors and tracking devices on infrastructure and vehicles5.

Intelligent elements such as side roads, traffic light timing, and parking availability improve traffic flow, while dynamic route development optimizes pathways based on traffic, road closures, and delivery times6. By integrating larger smart city initiatives for a comprehensive strategy, urban logistics are able to optimize through sustainable urban design and dynamic reconfiguration for volatile products7. The development of the e-commerce sector is facilitated by the COVID-19 pandemic, resulting in a rise of package deliveries and delays in remaining delivery services8. The urban freight adaptive pathways utilize computational intelligence, instantaneous information, and algorithms to construct dynamic, efficient delivery routes9. It incorporates the use of technologies such as Deep Learning (DL), intelligent transportation systems, mobility as a service systems and vehicle routing issues to increase sustainability and efficiency10,11.

E-commerce and rapid urbanization require effective urban freight transportation systems to address problems including pollution, fuel consumption, roadblocks, and delivery delays12. Real-time travel information, delivery schedules, and partial observation are all included in routing algorithms to provide the ideal routing approaches, providing an ideal response for complex urban areas13.

Urban transportation system was introduced in the research14 by employing the Internet of Things (IoT) and algorithmic optimization to improve sustainability and efficiency. By using public transportation as transshipment hubs, the proximal policy optimization (PPO) using a reinforcement learning (RL) method was assessed15 to reduce the delivery times and produced more average benefits of 1.49 M. The drawbacks include a reliance on precise travel data and lower work completion rates. Logistics distribution that was optimized by an RL-driven scheduling method was determined16, leading to a short delivery distance of 12.047 km and a cost reduction of 129.718 yuan. Dependency on data quality and scale across several logistic networks’ issues was presented in the research. The research17 employed multi-objective optimization method and a digital twin to minimize emissions and reduce costs in smart transportation. The modified bat algorithm was more demanding during the evaluation.

The predictive method that integrated machine learning (ML) analytics with IoT logistics data was explored in the investigation18. Enhanced delivery accuracy, reliability, and traffic management were assessed in the results, although they had drawbacks such as reliance on IoT infrastructure and issues with predictive growth. The dual-phase model for periodic logistics routing that included the A-star algorithm and adaptive genetic algorithms was developed19. Results produced the efficient cost reductions of 15.6% and 13.8%. The disadvantages were technical complexity and dependence on established patterns of traffic. Artificial intelligence (AI)-driven electric vehicles for transportation concepts were introduced20. It reduced carbon emissions, increased the delivery speed, and reduced energy utilization, although it has difficulties with real-time data quality.

Through combining physical and service network models, the sustainable multimodal logistics design was suggested in the investigation21. It optimized the cost, risk, and efficiency and in Segment 4, the lowest efficiency at 46.2% was produced. There was minimal variation under traffic conditions. The drone transport issue was addressed22 while considering threats, time constraints, and charging terminals; an adaptive optimization method using the Monte Carlo simulation validates effective drone operation. Difficulties included the need for precise energy-risk forecasting and the technical demand. The visual adaptive transport lighting network was introduced23 that optimized the flow while making green roads possible by using You Only Look Once (YOLO) detectors. It measured the vehicles, modified the traffic signals, and interacted with disruptions. However, reliability of sensors and difficulties with setup were the possible constraints. The professional Knowledge-Based City Logistics (KBCL) development was provided in the research24. It improved the logistical procedures at the findings and it has drawbacks such as dependence on knowledge, and scaling issues. The multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) method for renewable energy and logistics selection was recommended25. The integration of renewable energy and the selection of logistical facilities were improved, as determined in the results of the research. However, it has drawbacks such as expert prejudice and computational complexity.

The safe RL was improved by controlling safety-cost and its worst-case tail risk26. The method introduces a Worst-Case Soft Actor Critic framework that uses conditional value-at-risk as a safety constraint and estimates safety-cost distributions. The results show that the quantile-based approach provides robust risk control and performs effectively in challenging safety-constrained environments. A key limitation is that Gaussian approximation would underestimate safety-cost. The Safe Multi-Agent RL was advanced by addressing the limitations of existing shielding-based methods27. The method integrates Hamilton–Jacobi reachability to learn safety policies without requiring system models or pre-trained shielding. Results show that the approach maintains safety and completes target tasks effectively, even under non-cooperative disturbances. A limitation was that conservative value estimation would reduce optimality in highly cooperative settings.

Utilizing the dynamic traffic and delivery concerns, this research proposes a novel method for optimizing the urban freight routes by providing CAPPL-RL. Integration of PO-MDP based partial observation, real-time information and heterogeneous fleet features allows for adaptive, concern-aware policy learning to reduce the delays in deliveries, transport timing and operating expenses while maintaining scalability in multi-vehicle networks. Research gaps

Despite substantial advancements in AI-driven urban freight optimization, current techniques have severe structural and methodological constraints. IoT-integrated and autonomous vehicle-based logistics systems improve visibility and routing adaptability, but they still rely heavily on infrastructure stability and scalable implementation feasibility14,18. Reinforcement learning techniques, such as PPO-based frameworks, improve adaptive decision-making, but they require high-quality traffic data and frequently exhibit reduced task completion stability in dynamic conditions15. Intelligent scheduling and hybrid heuristic techniques increase operational efficiency; yet, they are difficult to generalize across diverse fleet configurations and stochastic traffic situations16,19,20. Multi-objective and digital twin-based optimization approaches offer robust simulation capabilities, but they have a significant computational overhead and limited real-time adaptability17,25.

More crucially, most contemporary routing systems assume either full observability or simplified constraint handling, which fails to meet partial traffic visibility, dynamic time-window limitations, and severe vehicle capacity enforcement all at once21–25. Safe RL models emphasize risk-sensitive learning but do not explicitly incorporate diverse fleet routing under urban freight uncertainties26,27.

As a result, there is a methodological gap in constructing a unified, constraint-aware reinforcement learning framework that can handle partial observability, stochastic traffic dynamics, heterogeneous vehicle fleets, and severe operational limitations. This study aims to overcome this gap by developing a PO-MDP-driven CAPPL-RL framework that allows for adaptive, projection-based constraint-compliant routing in dynamic urban freight environments.

Methodology

The research developed the CAPPL-RL method for urban freight route development that integrates the real-time traffic dynamics, delivery time-window constraints, and heterogeneous vehicle fleet characteristics into a RL method under a PO-MDP. The CAPPL-RL framework, depicted in the Fig. 1, represents the three components of vehicle state, action selection, and reward for constrained optimization. Adaptive PO-MDP framework for constraint-aware urban freight route optimization

Urban freight systems are increasingly challenged by growing traffic congestion, narrow delivery time windows, dynamic traffic conditions, and complex routing constraints. Delivery delays, higher usage of fuel, and higher operating expenses are the minimal inefficiencies caused by these issues. Traditional routing approaches fail to adequately address these challenges due to their inability to adapt in real time to highly dynamic and partially observable urban environments. Consequently, there is a critical need for intelligent, adaptive, and constraint-aware routing solutions that can optimize delivery efficiency while complying with operational limitations. Urban freight and vehicle operations dataset module

The Urban Freight and Vehicle Operations Dataset (UFVOD) is publicly available on Kaggle (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/programmer3/urban-freight-and-vehicle-operations-dataset). UFVOD integrates dynamic traffic states (peak/off-peak congestion, accidents, and roadworks), time-windowed delivery constraints reflecting customer urgency and Service Level Agreements (SLAs), vehicle-specific operational profiles including capacity, speed, and fuel efficiency, and partial observability through limited sensor coverage simulating incomplete traffic knowledge. There are 1200 deliveries in the dataset along with their geographical positions, demand values ranging from 147 to 306 kg, time frame for delivery and priority levels. There are also 4 different vehicle types involved in the deliveries. The vehicles have realistic attributes, such as capacity, speed and fuel consumption. The urban road network consists of 1200 segments of road. There are dynamic factors related to the changing nature of congestion on these roads, speed fluctuations on these roads, and the potential for disruption. The observation records reflect a partial traffic view, with confidence scores between 0.72 and 0.85. PO-MDP-based models of operational uncertainty can be generated using this observation data.

Proposed RL-based constraint-aware projected policy learning (CAPPL) framework for adaptive urban freight route optimization

The novelty of CAPPL-RL is not limited to its integration, but also its hybrid constraint-aware policy learning under partial observability. Unlike traditional RL or standalone PCPO-RL, CAPPL-RL incorporates a PO-MDP belief update mechanism to explicitly describe stochastic traffic uncertainty with confidence-weighted data. Q-learning estimates temporal-difference values in partially observable states, whereas PCPO applies intermediate policies to a viable constraint set using KL-divergence-bounded trust-region updates. At each iteration, the two-stage optimization ensures incentive maximization while also adhering to rigorous delivery time frames and truck capacity limits. Existing techniques are limited by their reliance on exact traffic data, low scalability across varied fleets, and ineffective constraint enforcement. CAPPL-RL overcomes difficulties by integrating normalized multi-source traffic features, diverse fleet attributes, and adaptive ε-greedy exploration into a projection-based safety layer. Integrating belief-state estimation with restricted policy projection enhances decision-making under imperfect traffic visibility, reduces constraint costs, and increases adaptability, addressing instability and violation issues in baseline PCPO-RL techniques.

Integration of Q-learning and PCPO

The CAPPL-RL architecture would utilize Q-learning over PCPO algorithms to create an adaptive routing solution that imposes real-world constraints upon the routing decisions made. When Q-learning is combined with PCPO, Q-learning uses a temporal difference (TD) update to estimate the value for actions under partial observability. In addition, PCPO uses the TD estimates from the Q-learning step to modify the unconstrained policy derived from Q-learning into a feasible policy in compliance with delivery time windows, limits of vehicle capacities, and travel restrictions based on congestion by means of the PCPO Projection Operator. As PCPO’s projection operator ensures that all necessary constraints on the feasible solution space would remain valid if unconstrained action values created by the Q-learning process are to produce action values that violate a valid chosen operation environment constraint. The two-stage process provides adaptive value estimation for Q-learning in an environment with incomplete information, such as the urban freight market. PCPO ensures that constraints are satisfied at every stage; combined with Q-learning, this coordinated integration allows the CAPPL-RL agent to make optimal routing decisions and stay within the bounds of physical limitations placed on delivery and capacity. Algorithm 1 depicts the projected policy gradient updates for robust optimization. The projection operator in Eq. (21) reduces the KL-divergence between the intermediate and feasible policy spaces, subject to cumulative constraint cost thresholds. The viable set is determined by time window, vehicle capacity, and routing continuity limitations. Multiple restrictions are enforced simultaneously using the cost advantage function and threshold bound, resulting in trust-region-based constrained updates with limited, policy-level assurances rather than heuristic reward shaping.

A paired t-test is conducted on each of the simulation results using the same delivery scenario, so all differences between the two frameworks can be attributed to routing strategy alone. Discussion

Previous techniques measured the success of urban freight logistics solutions through advancements in real-time adaptability and sustainability using DT technology18 IoT technology14, and EV logistics using AI20. RL-based PPO models15 produced lower task completion rates and required a lot of expensive, high-quality transport data to work. Intelligent scheduling methods16 and Evolutionary Optimization22,23 had poor scalability, and data was often found to be unreliable. The PCPO-RL system, provided KL-Divergence projection via KL-Divergence learning, performed poorly when it came to delivery times, fuel consumption and violating constraints. CAPPL-RL improves upon these previous solutions by integrating a trust-region adaptive learning method with real-time traffic dynamics and metric confidence levels associated with partial-observable traffic, which improved reward efficiencies by (+ 26.8%), Time-to-delivery (− 20.0%) and Cost-to-Constrain (− 71.0%). The CAPPL-RL system did not suffer from any of the previous computational and generalizable limitations of MOO17,25. The CAPPL-RL system is a robust, highly scalable and extremely data-efficient solution for advancing intelligent urban freight logistics optimization.

The present CAPPL-RL formulation simulates routing decisions with a single-vehicle learning agent that applies its learnt policy to all cars, assuming that traffic dynamics are exogenous and evolve independently of individual routing actions. The UFVOD-based PO-MDP approach treats traffic conditions, congestion levels, and incident patterns as stochastic environmental variables detected through partial sensors, rather than endogenously controlled by fleet-wide decisions. This model provides tractable belief-state updates and stable policy projection under PCPO constraints, but it does not explicitly account for feedback effects caused by coordinated fleet routing. Simultaneous vehicle decisions in large-scale deployments can change congestion patterns and resource competition. Future extensions could include multi-agent RL formulations or hierarchical fleet-level coordination, in which joint policies simulate inter-vehicle interaction while maintaining constraint-aware projection and partial observability handling.

Conclusions

This research proposed a CAPPL-RL for intelligent urban freight routing under partial observability. By modeling the problem as a PO-MDP and combining Q-learning with projection-based restricted policy optimization, the system efficiently balances delivery efficiency and operational restrictions. Experimental results on the UFVOD dataset show considerable gains over PCPO-RL, including shorter delivery times (20.2%), lower fuel usage (22.5%), and fewer constraint violations (up to 75%), all while enhancing adaptability and reliability. These findings support the resilience and scalability of CAPPL-RL in dynamic urban logistics systems. However, the approach relies on high-quality traffic measurements and has a significant computational burden for large-scale networks. Furthermore, validation is limited to simulation-based settings. Future research will concentrate on real-world applications of IoT-enabled live traffic data, multi-agent cooperative routing algorithms, and lightweight hybrid deep RL architectures to improve scalability, generalization, and real-time decision-making efficiency in smart city logistics systems.

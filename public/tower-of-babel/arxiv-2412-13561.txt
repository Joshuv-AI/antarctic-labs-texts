Higher rank elliptic partition functions and multisymmetric elliptic functions
Allan John Gerrard, Kohei Motegi, Kazumitsu Sakai

Abstract
We introduce and investigate a class of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 M + 1 \mathfrak{gl}_{M+1} partition functions which is an extension of the one introduced by Foda-Manabe. We characterize the partition functions by a nested version of Izergin-Korepin analysis, and determine the explicit forms, for each of the rational, trigonometric and elliptic versions. The resulting multisymmetric functions can be regarded as extensions of the rational, trigonometric and elliptic weight functions.

2.3
Characterization

We determine the explicit form of the partition function
based on Lagrange interpolation, which we call the Izergin-Korepin method.
We first characterize the properties of partition functions
by a nested version of Korepin’s lemma.
Korepin’s lemma corresponds to constructing the recursion relations and initial conditions
of the partition functions.

(M,L_{M})
, ordered lexicographically,
k_{M}\leq L_{M}
.
Suppose the explicit forms of the
\mathfrak{gl}_{M}
partition functions are determined,
and we wish to determine the
\mathfrak{gl}_{M+1}
partition functions.
If
k_{M}=1
, we may apply Property 5 to reduce the expression to a known
\mathfrak{gl}_{M}
partition function.
Otherwise, we apply a nested induction on
L_{M}
; the remaining properties may be used to reduce any
partition function to one with
L_{M}
replaced by
L_{M}-1
, and since
k_{M}\leq L_{M}
at all times, we eventually arrive at
k_{M}=1
.
The inductive step is clear when
i_{L_{1}^{\mathrm{I}}+\cdots+L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+L_{M}}^{(M)}=M+1
, using Property 4.
In the case
i_{L_{1}^{\mathrm{I}}+\cdots+L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+L_{M}}^{(M)}\neq M+1
, Property 1 implies that we need only evaluate
\psi
at
k_{M}
points to determine its
y_{L_{M}}^{(M)}
-dependence, Property 3 gives one such point as
y_{L_{M}}^{(M)}=x_{k_{M}}^{(M)}+1
, and Property 2 allows us to replace
x_{k_{M}}^{(M)}
by any other
x_{j}^{(M)}
, giving a total of
k_{M}
points and completing the induction.

Definition 2.2
.

We define the extended rational weight function as

W\bigg(\bm{x}^{(1)},\dots,\bm{x}^{(M)}\bigg|\{\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(1)},\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{(1)}\},\dots,\{\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(M-1)},\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{(M-1)}\},\bm{y}^{(M)}\bigg|\\

\times\prod_{i=\widetilde{I}_{a}^{(p)}+1-L_{1}^{\mathrm{I}}-\cdots-L_{p-1}^{\mathrm{I}}}^{L^{\mathrm{I}}_{p}+L^{\mathrm{I\!I}}_{p}+k_{p+1}}\left(x^{(p)}_{\sigma_{p}(a)}-m_{i}^{L_{p}^{\mathrm{I}},k_{p+1},L_{p}^{\mathrm{I\!I}}}\left(\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(p)}\middle|\bm{x}^{(p+1)}_{\sigma_{p+1}}\middle|\bm{y}^{(p)}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}\right)+1\right)\Bigg)\prod_{a<b}^{k_{p}}\frac{x^{(p)}_{\sigma_{p}(a)}-x^{(p)}_{\sigma_{p}(b)}-1}{x^{(p)}_{\sigma_{p}(a)}-x^{(p)}_{\sigma_{p}(b)}}\Bigg\}\\
\times\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{I_{a}^{(M)}-1-L_{1}^{\mathrm{I}}-\cdots-L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}\right)\prod_{i=I_{a}^{(M)}+1-L_{1}^{\mathrm{I}}-\cdots-L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}}^{L_{M}}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}+1\right)\right)\\
\times\prod_{a<b}^{k_{M}}\frac{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}-1}{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}},

where

and
{\bm{x}}^{(j)}_{\sigma_{j}}:=\left\{x^{(j)}_{\sigma_{j}(1)},\dots,x^{(j)}_{\sigma_{j}(k_{j})}\right\}
as an ordered set.

Theorem 2.3
.

We have

\psi\bigg(\bm{x}^{(1)},\dots,\bm{x}^{(M)}\bigg|\{\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(1)},\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{(1)}\},\dots,\{\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(M-1)},\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{(M-1)}\},\bm{y}^{(M)}\bigg|\\

k_{1},\dots,k_{M}\bigg|\{L_{1}^{\mathrm{I}},L_{1}^{\mathrm{I\!I}}\},\dots,\{L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}},L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I\!I}}\},L_{M}\bigg|\bm{I}\bigg).

(9)

Proof.

What we need to prove is that the function (

)
satisfies all the properties listed in Proposition
2.1
, as these uniquely determine the partition function.
Properties 1 and 2 are easy to check from the definition, while the confirmations of Properties 3, 4 and 5 are given as propositions below.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

If
i_{L^{\mathrm{I}}}\neq M+1
,
the functions (
8
) satisfy the recursion relation

W\bigg(\bm{x}^{(1)},\dots,\bm{x}^{(M)}\bigg|\{\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(1)},\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{(1)}\},\dots,\{\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(M-1)},\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{(M-1)}\},\bm{y}^{(M)}\bigg|\\

=\prod_{j=1}^{L_{M}-1}\left(x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}-y^{(M)}_{j}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{k_{M}-1}\left(x^{(M)}_{j}-x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}-1\right)\\
\times W\bigg(\bm{x}^{(1)},\dots,\bm{x}^{(M)}\setminus\{x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}\}\bigg|\{\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(1)},\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{(1)}\},\dots,\{\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(M-1)},\{x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}\}\cup\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{(M-1)}\},\bm{y}^{(M)}\setminus\{y^{(M)}_{L_{M}}\}\bigg|\\
k_{1},\dots,k_{M-1},k_{M}-1\bigg|\{L_{1}^{\mathrm{I}},L_{1}^{\mathrm{I\!I}}\},\dots,\{L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}},L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I\!I}}+1\},L_{M}-1\bigg|\bm{J}\bigg),

where the
\bm{J}
are related to the
\bm{I}

\displaystyle{\bm{J}}^{(i)}_{k_{i}}={\bm{I}}^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\qquad 1\leq i\leq M-1;

\displaystyle{\bm{J}}^{(M)}_{k_{M}-1}={\bm{I}}^{(M)}_{k_{M}}\setminus\{L^{\mathrm{I}}\};

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\bm{J}}}^{(i)}_{k_{i}}=\widetilde{{\bm{I}}}^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\qquad 1\leq i\leq M-1.

Proof.

We check by the following two steps.

Step 1. – recursion of the “final factor”.

We will refer to the following as the “final factor” of the weight functions.

\underbrace{\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}}\prod_{i=1}^{I_{a}^{(M)}-1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}\right)}_{X}\underbrace{\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}}\prod_{i=I_{a}^{(M)}+1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}^{L_{M}}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}+1\right)}_{Y}\underbrace{\prod_{a<b}^{k_{M}}\frac{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}-1}{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}}}_{Z}.

(11)

The expression above is made up of three products, which we will analyse in turn.
First, note that for Case A, we have
I^{(M)}_{k_{M}}=L_{1}^{\mathrm{I}}+\cdots+L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+L_{M}
, so the second product of
Y
is empty when
a=k_{M}
:

Y=\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\prod_{i=I_{a}^{(M)}+1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}^{L_{M}}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}+1\right)

Specialising to
y^{(M)}_{L_{M}}=x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}+1
,

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\prod_{i=I_{a}^{(M)}+1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}^{L_{M}-1}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}+1\right)\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{L_{M}}^{(M)}+1\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\prod_{i=I_{a}^{(M)}+1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}^{L_{M}-1}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}+1\right)\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-x_{k_{M}}^{(M)}\right),

we see that this product is zero unless
\sigma_{M}(k_{M})=k_{M}
.
We rearrange the final product to arrive at

Y=\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\prod_{i=I_{a}^{(M)}+1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}^{L_{M}-1}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}+1\right)\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\left(x_{a}^{(M)}-x_{k_{M}}^{(M)}\right).

Next, we focus on

, which can be split as follows:

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\prod_{a<b}^{k_{M}-1}\frac{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}-1}{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}}\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\frac{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(k_{M})}-1}{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(k_{M})}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{a<b}^{k_{M}-1}\frac{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}-1}{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}}\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\frac{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}-1}{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}},

where we have used the fact that
\sigma_{M}(k_{M})=k_{M}
.
We can then reorder the second product here to give

Z=\prod_{a<b}^{k_{M}-1}\frac{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}-1}{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}}\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\frac{x^{(M)}_{a}-x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}-1}{x^{(M)}_{a}-x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}}.

Finally, we turn to
X
, which we split as

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{I_{a}^{(M)}-1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{L_{M}-1}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(k_{M})}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}\right).

\displaystyle=\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{I_{a}^{(M)}-1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{L_{M}-1}\left(x_{k_{M}}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}\right).

\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{I_{a}^{(M)}-1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{L_{M}-1}\left(x_{k_{M}}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}\right)\\
\times\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\prod_{i=I_{a}^{(M)}+1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}^{L_{M}-1}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}+1\right)\cancel{\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\left(x_{a}^{(M)}-x_{k_{M}}^{(M)}\right)}\\
\times\prod_{a<b}^{k_{M}-1}\frac{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}-1}{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}}\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\frac{x^{(M)}_{a}-x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}-1}{\cancel{x^{(M)}_{a}-x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}}}.

This is equal to

\prod_{i=1}^{L_{M}-1}\left(x_{k_{M}}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}\right)\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\left(x^{(M)}_{a}-x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}-1\right)\\
\times\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{I_{a}^{(M)}-1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}\right)\prod_{a=1}^{k_{M}-1}\prod_{i=I_{a}^{(M)}+1-\sum_{q=1}^{M-1}L^{\mathrm{I}}_{q}}^{L_{M}-1}\left(x_{\sigma_{M}(a)}^{(M)}-y_{i}^{(M)}+1\right)\\
\times\prod_{a<b}^{k_{M}-1}\frac{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}-1}{x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(a)}-x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(b)}}

We observe that this is equal to the residual factor from (
10
), along with the “final factor” of the wavefunction on the right hand side of (
10

Step 2. – recursion of the remaining factors.

In the second to last factor,

\displaystyle m_{i}^{L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}},k_{M},L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I\!I}}}\left(\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(M-1)}\middle|\bm{x}^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}}\middle|{\bm{y}}^{(M-1)}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\begin{cases}y^{(M-1)}_{\mathrm{I},i}&1\leq i\leq L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}\\
x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(i-L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}})}&L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+1\leq i\leq L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+k_{M}\\
y^{(M-1)}_{\mathrm{I\!I},i-L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}-k_{M}}&L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+k_{M}+1\leq i\leq L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+k_{M}+L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I\!I}}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\qquad=\begin{cases}y^{(M-1)}_{\mathrm{I},i}&1\leq i\leq L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}\\
x^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}(i-L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}})}&L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+1\leq i\leq L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+k_{M}-1\\

y^{(M-1)}_{\mathrm{I\!I},i-L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}-k_{M}}&L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+k_{M}+1\leq i\leq L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}}+k_{M}+L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I\!I}},\end{cases}

where we have used

Hence, this is equal to the symbol

m_{i}^{L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I}},k_{M}-1,L_{M-1}^{\mathrm{I\!I}}+1}\left(\bm{y}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(M-1)}\middle|\bm{x}^{(M)}_{\sigma_{M}}\setminus\{x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}\}\middle|\{x^{(M)}_{k_{M}}\}\cup{\bm{y}}^{(M-1)}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}\right).

Finally, we change all instances of
\widetilde{I}^{(M-1)}_{a}
to

∎

Proposition 2.5

Conclusion

We have introduced a family of
\mathfrak{gl}_{M+1}
-type multisymmetric special functions in the rational, trigonometric and elliptic cases and shown, using nested Izergin-Korepin analysis, that these special functions are exactly equal to a generalization of the lattice partition function, or off-shell nested Bethe wavefunction.
This work generalizes the result of Foda-Manabe
[
17
]
, as well as the elliptic multisymmetric functions in Konno
[

and Rimányi-Tarasov-Varchenko

It would be of interest to analyse how this generalisation of the Bethe wavefunction might make sense in the language of quiver varieties,
or the interpretation in the Bethe-Gauge correspondence.
Some cases beyond off-shell Bethe wavefunctions
were discussed in
[
17
]
by a limiting procedure.
However, note that for the elliptic case involving theta functions,
the limiting procedure as given in
[
17
]
cannot be applied.

We imagine that these results extend to the supersymmetric versions of these functions in the standard way.

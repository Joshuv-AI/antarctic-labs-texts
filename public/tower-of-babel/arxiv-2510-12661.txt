Measurement of the tau anomalous magnetic moment using Ultra-peripheral collisions with the ALICE detector in Run 3 Pb ​ – ​ Pb \mathrm{Pb\text{--}Pb} data
R. Lavička and P. A. Bühler for the ALICE Collaboration

Abstract
The anomalous magnetic moment of the tau lepton ( a τ a_{\tau} ) is a sensitive probe for the search for deviations from the Standard Model predictions and thus for new physics. This study investigates the feasibility of measuring a τ a_{\tau} using ultra-peripheral collisions (UPCs) at the LHC, where photon-photon interactions ( γ ​ γ → τ + ​ τ − \gamma\gamma\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-} ) produce tau lepton pairs. We focus on events recorded by the ALICE detector during Run 3 Pb—Pb data-taking. Events are selected in the decay channel where one tau decays into an electron and neutrinos, and the other decays into a charged pion, or three charged pions, and neutrinos. These samples are enhanced with decays into muons, which are inseparable in the ALICE detector. The clean environment of UPCs minimizes hadronic background, while the advanced particle identification capabilities of the ALICE Time Projection Chamber (TPC) and Time-of-Flight (TOF) systems allow for efficient separation of electrons, pions, and background particles. In this talk, prospects for measuring this process by ALICE in Run 3, which benefits from high statistics and improved systematics uncertainties, will be discussed. Results will provide tighter constraints on a τ a_{\tau} , contributing to the broader effort to test the Standard Model’s robustness and explore physics beyond it.

Introduction

For a point-like Dirac fermion with spin
1/2
, the gyromagnetic factor is
g=2
. Quantum corrections introduced by Standard Model (SM) induce the
anomalous magnetic moment
,

a\equiv\frac{g-2}{2}.

(1)

In case of leptons, electron
a_{e}

[
1
]
and muon
a_{\mu}

[
2
]
are measured with extraordinary precision, the tau lepton remains poorly constrained due to its short lifetime and the consequent absence of spin-precession based measurements. Current limits are derived from high-energy production processes and leave orders of magnitude of parameter space unconstrained relative to the electron and muon
[
3
]
.

Given that sensitivity to new physics grows with the lepton mass squared, a precise determination of
a_{\tau}
would strongly constrain TeV-scale scenarios beyond the SM. Ultraperipheral heavy-ion collisions (UPCs) at the LHC provide an intense flux of quasi-real photons with very small virtuality (
q^{2}\to 0
), enabling studies of
\gamma\gamma\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
that are directly sensitive to the
\gamma\tau\tau
vertex
[
4
]
.

Experimental Setup and Run 3 Performance

The ALICE detector underwent major upgrades for Run 3
[
10
]
. Subsystems relevant to this analysis include:

•

DAQ:
the triggered readout of all relevant detectors was replaced with streaming readout;

•

ITS:
improved vertexing and low-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
tracking;

•

TPC:
main tracking detector with excellent
\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}x
resolution for PID of low-momenta tracks;

•

TOF:
timing-based PID extending electron–kaon/proton separation up to
p_{\mathrm{T}}\sim$1.5\text{\,}$\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$\mathrm{/}\mathrm{c}$
;

•

FIT:
quartz radiators and scintillators at forward rapidities for interaction time and multiplicity;

•

ZDC:
forward hadronic calorimeters to detect neutrons.

In the central barrel, electrons are cleanly separated from muons, pions, kaons and protons with TPC+TOF for
p_{\mathrm{T}}<$1.5\text{\,}$\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$\mathrm{/}\mathrm{c}$
, see Figure
1
. Pions and muons are not separable via TPC/TOF alone, but this does not limit the mixed electron
+
pion/muon channel used below.

4
Analysis Strategy

Tau decays are categorized into 1-prong (
\sim 80\%
) and 3-prong (
\sim 20\%
) modes. ALICE focuses on final states with an
electron
and an oppositely charged track system:

•

1-prong:

e^{\pm}+e^{\mp}\ {\rm or}\ (\pi^{\mp}/\mu^{\mp})
;

•

3-prong:

e^{\pm}+3\pi^{\mp}
(with neutral pions unconstrained).

The dominant backgrounds are
\gamma\gamma\to e^{+}e^{-}
,
\gamma\gamma\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
,
\gamma\gamma\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and continuum coherent photonuclear production of
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
. The selection employs:

1.

Exclusivity:
two-prong topology at midrapidity with minimal additional activity;

2.

Acoplanarity:
back-to-back pairs veto to suppress continuum; a requirement on system
p_{\mathrm{T}}
(e.g.
>$0.2\text{\,}$\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$\mathrm{/}\mathrm{c}$
) is used to reduce
e^{+}e^{-}
-contamination;

3.

PID:
TPC
\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}x
and TOF to tag electrons and reject hadrons.

4.

No neutrons:
veto on events with neutrons in forward direction to reject nuclear break-up events (in figures referred as 0n0n class).

Residual background is estimated using like-sign and mixed-event techniques. For the
e+3\pi
channel, an invariant-mass selection
m_{3\pi}<m_{\tau}
further suppresses combinatorics.

2
–
8

7
Discussion and Outlook

These first Run 3 results validate the UPC-based strategy to access
a_{\tau}
with ALICE. The central-barrel focus on low-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
leptons provides sensitivity in a kinematic regime complementary to
pp
analyses by CMS and ATLAS. With the anticipated Run 3+4 integrated luminosity of order
10
–
13

\mathrm{nb}^{-1}
, ALICE is projected to set world-leading constraints in the intermediate
W_{\gamma\gamma}
range.

8
Conclusions

•

UPCs at the LHC enable a direct probe of the
\gamma\tau\tau
vertex and the tau anomalous magnetic moment.

•

ALICE is uniquely capable of reconstructing low-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
leptons in mixed tau-decay final states.

•

First Run 3 data confirm detector performance and the analysis strategy, with distributions consistent with STARlight expectations.

•

With the full Run 3+4 dataset, ALICE can improve the current bounds on
a_{\tau}
and offer coverage complementary to CMS/ATLAS in
pp
.

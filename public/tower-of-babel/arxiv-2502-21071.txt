Abstract
. We obtain (weighted) restricted type estimates for the Bergman projection operator on monomial polyhedra, a class of domains generalizing the Hartogs triangle. From these estimates, we recapture L p L^{p} boundedness results of the Bergman projection on these domains. On some monomial polyhedra, we also discover that the Bergman projection could fail to be of weak type ( q ∗ , q ∗ ) (q_{*},q_{*}) where q ∗ q_{*} is the right endpoint of of the interval of L p L^{p} -regularity of the domain.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Bergman projection and the limited range phenomenon

For a domain (open connected subset)
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
the
Bergman projection

P_{\Omega}
is the orthogonal projection operator from the Hilbert space

L^{2}(\Omega)
of square
integrable functions on
\Omega
to the
Bergman space

A^{2}(\Omega)
, the subspace
of holomorphic functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. The operator
P_{\Omega}
has the integral representation

P_{\Omega}f(z)=\int_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}(z,w)f(w)dV(w)\quad\text{ for }f\in L^{2}(\Omega),

where
dV
is the Lebesgue measure of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and

K_{\Omega}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is the Bergman kernel
of
\Omega
. Mapping properties of
P_{\Omega}
in spaces of smooth functions assume importance in view of the connection
with the
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann problem as well as the boundary behavior
of holomorphic mappings (see
[
FK72
,
BL80
]
). The
L^{p}
-mapping
properties of
P_{\Omega}
have also been an object of considerable
interest (see
[
ZJ64
,
FR75
,
PS77
,
MS94
]
etc.), driven partly by an analogy with the classical theory of
singular integral operators. On certain classes of smoothly bounded pseudoconvex domains, one can interpret the Bergman projection as a generalized Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator by introducing appropriate pseudometrics adapted to the boundary geometry (see
[
McN94
]
), thereby obtaining boundedness in
L^{p}
for
1<p<\infty
as well as weak type
(1,1)
estimates (see also
[
DHZZ01
]
). On the polydisc

\mathbb{D}^{n}
, the operator
P_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}
is of weak type
L(\log^{+}L)^{n-1}
(see
[
HW20
]
), a phenomenon reminiscent of the left endpoint behavior of the tensor product of Hilbert transforms acting on functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

On many domains, however, the Bergman projection has a very
different behavior. For example, the Bergman projection may have a limited range of
L^{p}
-regularity, i.e., it is bounded in
L^{p}
if and only if
p
belongs to an open interval
(p_{*},q_{*})
containing
2
where
p_{*},q_{*}
are Hölder conjugates by the self-adjointness of
P_{\Omega}
. It is also possible that the Bergman projection is bounded in
L^{p}
only for
p=2
(see
[
EM17
]
). An important early example of limited
p
range was the nonpseudoconvex but smoothly bounded Barrett worm (see
[
Bar84
]
). Another example
(see
[
CZ16
,
EM16
]
) is the
classical Hartogs triangle

(1.1)

\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\left|z_{1}\right|<\left|z_{2}\right|<1\},

a nonsmooth pseudoconvex domain which has long
served as a source of counterexamples in several complex variables. The Bergman projection
P_{\mathbb{H}}
is bounded in
L^{p}
if and only if
\frac{4}{3}<p<4
. Subsequently, several authors discovered such “limited range of
L^{p}
-regularity” in other domains with geometry
similar to that of
\mathbb{H}
(see
[
EM16
,
EM17
,
Che17
,
Zha21b
,
Zha21a
]
etc.).
Most of these examples were shown
in
[
BCEM22
]
to belong to a class of domains called
monomial polyhedra
. In this paper, we will use these domains as “laboratory models” to understand the possible
L^{p}
mapping properties of the Bergman projection outside the realm of smoothly bounded domains. A bounded domain
\mathcal{U}_{B}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n},n\geq 2
is called a
monomial polyhedron if it can be expressed as

(1.2)

\mathcal{U}_{B}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\text{ for }1\leq j\leq n,\quad\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left|z_{k}\right|^{b^{j}_{k}}<1\right\},

where
B\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
is an
n\times n
matrix of integers, and the entry
of
B
at the
j
-th row and
k
-th column is denoted by
b^{j}_{k}
.
If
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
0&\phantom{-}1\end{pmatrix}
,
we see that
\mathcal{U}_{B}=\mathbb{H}
. We will say that
\mathcal{U}_{B}
is nontrivial if it is not the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
(which corresponds to taking
B
to be the identity matrix).
It was shown in
[
BCEM22
]
that on a nontrivial monomial polyhedron
\mathcal{U}_{B}
the Bergman projection
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
has limited range of
L^{p}
-boundedness. Monomial polyhedra, and
quotient domains
that generalize them, have been studied by several authors
(
[
CKY20
,
DM23
,
GG23
,
Alm23
,
CCG+24
,
HW24
,
QWZ25
]
etc.) from various points of view.

1.2.
Weighted restricted type estimates

In this paper we continue the study of the
L^{p}
mapping behavior
of the Bergman projection on the monomial polyhedron
\mathcal{U}_{B}
. As noted above, there are Hölder-conjugate numbers

p_{*},q_{*}
with
p_{*}<2<q_{*}
such that
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
is of strong type

(p,p)
(i.e. bounded in
L^{p}
) if and only if
p_{*}<p<q_{*}
.
Several authors have studied the behavior of
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
at
q_{*}
for specific
B
. For the Hartogs
triangle, and certain of its generalizations,
the operator
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
is of weak type
(q_{*},q_{*})
(see
[
HW20
,
CK23
,
JLQW24
,
TTZ24
,
LW24
,
CK24
]
), i.e.,

P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
is bounded from the space
L^{q_{*}}(\mathcal{U}_{B})
to the Lorentz space
L^{q_{*},\infty}(\mathcal{U}_{B})
. Classical results of Lorentz space theory (recalled in Proposition
2.1
below) then suggest that the Bergman projection is of so-called
restricted type

(p_{*},p_{*})
,
a notion that we now recall (see
[
SW71
]
).

Let
(M,\mu)
and
(N,\nu)
be measure spaces (we suppress the
\sigma
-algebras from the notation). For

p\geq 1
, a mapping
T
is of
restricted type
(p,p)
if
T
is a linear map from
the space of simple functions on
M
to the space of measurable functions on
N
, and there is a constant
C>0
such that whenever

E\subset M
is measurable with
\mu(E)<\infty
, we have

(1.3)

\left\|T(1_{E})\right\|_{L^{p}(N,\nu)}\leq C\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p}(M,\mu)}.

Here we use
1_{E}
to denote the indicator (or characteristic) function of
E
, the function that is 1 on
E
and 0 on the complement of
E
. One can also think of operators of restricted
type
(p,p)
as those which have continuous extensions to operators from the Lorentz space
L^{p,1}(M,\mu)

to the space
L^{p}(N,\nu)
(see
[
Hun66
]
).

Similarly, a linear operator
T
mapping simple functions on
M
to measurable functions on
N
is called of
restricted weak type
(p,p)
if there is a
C>0
such that for each measurable subset
E\subset M
of finite measure, and for each
y>0
:

(1.4)

\nu\{x\in N:\left|T(1_{E})(x)\right|>y\}\leq\left(\frac{C}{y}\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p}(M,\mu)}\right)^{p}=\frac{C^{p}}{y^{p}}{\mu(E)}.

From the Chebyshev-Markov inequality one can see that if
T
is of restricted type
(p,p)
, then it is of restricted weak type
(p,p)
as well.

In view of duality, it is natural to look at
restricted type estimates on the Bergman projection with
p
value
at the left-endpoint of the interval of
L^{p}
-regularity of
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
. In fact, it makes sense to consider
the
positive Bergman operator

P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}^{+}
, which dominates the Bergman projection and has similar mapping properties, where for domain
\Omega
,
P_{\Omega}^{+}
is
the operator whose action on a measurable function
f
is given by

P_{\Omega}^{+}f(z)=\int_{\Omega}\left|K_{\Omega}(z,w)\right|f(w)dV(w),

provided the integral converges. The operator
P_{\Omega}^{+}
has
the advantage that its adjoint in weighted
L^{p}
-spaces is easy to compute (see (
2.5
) below).
We show that restricted type estimates hold at
p_{*}
provided
\mathcal{U}_{B}

satisfies a further condition, that
a certain invariant
m
(defined in (
1.7
)) associated with
the domain
\mathcal{U}_{B}
is equal to 1 (see Corollary
1.3
below).
When
m\not=1
,
we still obtain restricted type estimates, but now weighted ones,
between
weighted measure spaces
on
\mathcal{U}_{B}
(see Theorem
1.1
below). For a nonnegative measurable function
v
on a domain
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, by the weighted measure space
(\mathcal{U},v)
, we mean the measure space with the
\sigma
-algebra of (Lebesgue)-measurable subsets of
\mathcal{U}
, and
with the measure of a measurable
E\subset\mathcal{U}
given by
v(E)=\int_{E}vdV.

1.3.
Main results

We will always assume that the dimension
n\geq 2
. Let

\Delta=\mathrm{adj}B=(\det B)B^{-1}
be the adjugate matrix of the integer matrix
B\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
that occurs in the definition (
1.2
) of the domain

\mathcal{U}_{B}
, where
\mathbb{Z}^{a\times b}
denotes the set of integer matrices of
a
rows and
b
columns. By Cramer’s rule,
\Delta
has integer entries, and from the fact that
\mathcal{U}_{B}
is a
bounded open set, it easily follows that each entry of
\Delta
is nonnegative and
\det\Delta\not=0
(see
[
BCEM22
, Proposition 3.2]
).
Let For
1\leq j\leq n
, denote by
\delta_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times 1}
the
j
-th column of the matrix
\Delta
. Let

a_{j}=\frac{1}{\gcd\delta_{j}}\delta_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times 1},

where the positive integer
\gcd\delta_{j}
is the greatest common
divisor of the
n
entries of the column matrix
\delta_{j}
(which is nonzero since
\det\Delta\not=0
).
We set

(1.5)

A=\begin{pmatrix}|&\cdots&|\\
a_{1}&\cdots&a_{n}\\
|&\cdots&|\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}

to be the
n\times n
matrix of nonnegative integers whose
j
-th column is
a_{j}
, obtained by dividing the column vector
c_{j}
by the gcd of its elements.
The geometric significance of the matrix
A
is that it is the symbol of a monomial branched covering map
z\mapsto z^{A}
of
\mathcal{U}_{B}
by a punctured polydisc, see Proposition
4.6
below.
Let

(1.6)

\mathbbm{1}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{1\times n}

be the
1\times n
row matrix whose entries are all 1’s.
Next, we introduce several quantities that are crucial in our results:

(1)

Let
m
be
the
number of largest elements
in the row-vector of integers

\mathbbm{1}A=(\mathbbm{1}a_{1},\mathbbm{1}a_{2},\dots,\mathbbm{1}a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{1\times n},

i.e.,
denoting by
\#S
the number of elements of a set
S
:

(1.7)

m=\#\{j:\mathbbm{1}a_{j}=\max_{k}\{\mathbbm{1}a_{k},1\leq k\leq n\}\}.

(2)

Let

(1.8)

p_{*}=\max_{j}\frac{2\mathbbm{1}a_{j}}{\mathbbm{1}a_{j}+1}.

It was shown in
[
BCEM22
]
that
p_{*}
is the left endpoint of the interval of
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Bergman projection on
\mathcal{U}_{B}
. If
\mathcal{U}_{B}
is nontrivial, then
1<p_{*}<2.

(3)

For a tuple
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of real numbers
let
\rho_{\alpha}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function

(1.9)

\rho_{\alpha}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left|z_{j}\right|^{\alpha_{j}}=\left|z_{1}\right|^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot\left|z_{n}\right|^{\alpha_{n}}.

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, notice that
\rho_{\alpha}(z)=\left|z^{\alpha}\right|
, where, using standard multi-index notation
z^{\alpha}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}
.
Define a function
w
on
\mathcal{U}_{B}
by setting

(1.10)

w=\rho_{\mathbbm{1}-\mathbbm{1}A^{-1}}.

It is not difficult to see that
0\leq w\leq 1
on

\mathcal{U}_{B}.

Our first result is the following weighted restricted type estimate:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{B}
be a nontrivial monomial polyhedron.
Then positive Bergman operator

P^{+}_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
is of restricted type

(p_{*},p_{*})
from
the weighted measure space
(\mathcal{U}_{B},(-\log w)^{(m-1)(p_{*}-1)})
to the weighted measure space
(\mathcal{U}_{B},w^{p_{*}-2})
, i.e., there is a constant
C>0
(depending only on the domain
\mathcal{U}_{B}
) such that for each measurable
E\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{B}

(1.11)

\left\|P^{+}_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}(1_{E})\right\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathcal{U}_{B},w^{p_{*}-2})}\leq C\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathcal{U}_{B},(-\log w)^{(m-1)(p_{*}-1)})}.

As mentioned earlier, a duality argument may be
applied to (
1.11
), yielding an inequality at the right endpoint:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\mu
be the measure on
\mathcal{U}_{B}
given by

\mu(E)=\int_{E}(-\log w)^{(m-1)(p_{*}-1)}dV,\quad E\subset\mathcal{U}_{B}\text{ measurable}

where
w
is as in (
1.10
). There is a constant
C>0
such that for each measurable function
f
on
\mathcal{U}_{B}
and for each
y>0
we have

(1.12)

\mu\left\{z\in\mathcal{U}_{B}:{\left|P^{+}_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}f(z)\right|}>{(-\log w(z))^{(m-1)(p_{*}-1)}}\cdot y\right\}\leq\frac{C}{y^{q_{*}}}\int_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}\left|f\right|^{q_{*}}w^{\frac{2-p_{*}}{p_{*}-1}}dV,

where
q_{*}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p_{*}
.

Inequality (
1.12
) indicates possible distinct mapping behaviors of the Bergman projection
between the cases
m=1
and
m>1
. When
m=1
, there is a unique largest element in the
n
-tuple

(\mathbbm{1}a_{1},\dots,\mathbbm{1}a_{n})
.
In this case, the estimate (
1.12
) can be reduced to be an unweighted one and can then
be used in an interpolation argument to recover
L^{p}
regularity results. Thus Theorem
1.1
leads to the following corollary via Proposition
1.2
:

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose that the nontrivial monomial polyhedron
\mathcal{U}_{B}
satisfies the
condition
m=1
, let
p_{*}
be as in (
1.8
), and let
q_{*}
be the Hölder conjugate of
p_{*}
. Then
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}^{+}
and
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
are

(1)

of restricted type
(p_{*},p_{*})
.

(2)

of strong type
(p,p)
for each
p_{*}<p<q_{*}
.

(3)

of weak type
(q_{*},q_{*}).

Corollary
1.3
recovers previous known
L^{p}
boundedness results on many interesting domains including the
classical Hartogs triangle
\mathbb{H}
of (
1.1
), the
generalized Hartogs triangles
\{\left|z_{1}\right|^{\frac{a}{b}}<\left|z_{2}\right|<1\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
, where
a,b
are coprime positive integers, and more generally the
n
-dimensional Hartogs triangles considered in
[
Par18
,
Che17
,
Zha21a
]
:

\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left|z_{1}\right|^{k_{1}}<\left|z_{2}\right|^{k_{2}}<\dots<\left|z_{n}\right|^{k_{n}}<1\},

with
(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
being a tuple of positive integers of gcd 1.

When
m>1
, on the other hand, the weight involving
-\log w
in (
1.11
) cannot be dropped, thus suggesting the possible failure of both unweighted restricted type
(p_{*},p_{*})
and weak type
(q_{*},q_{*})
estimates for
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
. We will prove the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

There are monomial polyhedra
\mathcal{U}_{B}
with the Bergman projection
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
not of restricted weak type
(p_{*},p_{*})
,
where
p_{*}
, as in (
3.2
), is the left endpoint of the interval of
L^{p}
-boundedness.

An example of such a
\mathcal{U}_{B}
is

(1.13)

\mathcal{U}_{B}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:\left|z_{1}z_{3}\right|<\left|z_{2}\right|<\left|z_{1}\right|<1\right\},

for which
p_{*}=\frac{4}{3}

(see Proposition
5.2
.)
Therefore, for the domain
\mathcal{U}_{B}
in (
1.13
), neither the Bergman projection
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
nor
the positive Bergman projection
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}^{+}
is of restricted weak type
(p_{*},p_{*})
. It follows that both
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
and
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}^{+}
are not of restricted type
(p_{*},p_{*})
, nor of weak type
(p_{*},p_{*})
as well.
Since
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
is not of restricted type
(p_{*},p_{*})
, a duality argument (see Proposition
2.1
) then implies that

P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
is not of weak type
(q_{*},q_{*})
. This result disagrees with the claim in a recent paper of Tang, Tu and Zhang (see
[
TTZ24
, Theorem 1.2]
) that on each monomial polyhedron, the Bergman projection is of weak type
(q_{*},q_{*})
, where
q_{*}
is the right endpoint of the interval of
L^{p}
-boundedness.
The error there stems from the last inequality on page 14 of their paper

2(\mathbbm{1}a_{j}-1)+2\mathbbm{1}a_{j_{0}}\frac{(\sigma-2\mathbbm{1}B_{-})a_{j}}{(2\mathbbm{1}B_{-}-\sigma)a_{j_{0}}}>-2,\quad j=1,\dots,n,j\not=j_{0}

which does not hold if
m>1
, because then the left hand side is equal to
-2
.
When
m>1
, extra
\log
terms will appear in the relevant integral computations, eventually making the conclusion false. However, the argument in
[
TTZ24
]
is correct for the case
m=1
and therefore gives a direct proof
of part (3) of Corollary
1.3
above, without recourse to duality arguments. In a future publication, we plan to analyze in detail the weak type
(p_{*},p_{*})
irregular behavior of the Bergman projection for general monomial polyhedra with
m>1
. This question is especially interesting in dimension
n=2
, where in all known cases, the Bergman projection is of weak type
(q_{*},q_{*}).

It is worth noting that for the case
m>1
we can no longer adopt our weighted estimates at the endpoint
p=p_{*}
in an interpolation argument to recover unweighted
L^{p}
strong type estimates of
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
(as
in case (2) of Corollary
1.3
above). Nevertheless, the
method
in the proof of Theorem
1.1
gives a unified approach to these results regardless of the value of
m
.

Proposition 1.5
.

For each nontrivial monomial
polyhedron
\mathcal{U}_{B}
, the operator

P^{+}_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
is of restricted type
(p,p)
for
p\in(p_{*},2]
.

As a consequence, we recapture a strengthened version of the main result of
[
BCEM22
]
:

Corollary 1.6
.

For
p\in(p_{*},q_{*})
, both
P^{+}_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
and

P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
are of strong type
(p,p).

Our paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we recall results regarding duals of Lorentz spaces and prove Proposition
1.2
, Corollaries
1.3
and
1.3
assuming Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
1.5
. In Section
3
, we establish weighted restricted type estimates for the Bergman projection on punctured polydiscs. In Section
4

we pull back restricted estimates for
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}

via proper monomial mappings into weighted estimates of the Bergman projection on punctured polydiscs so that results in Section
3
apply and Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
1.5
are proved. In Section
5
, we give a family of monomial polyhedra in Theorem
5.1
where the Bergman projection fails to be of restricted weak type
(p_{*},p_{*})
, hence proving Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Application of duality and interpolation

2.1.
Some results of functional analysis

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space, where
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite, and let
1<p<\infty
. The space
L^{p,\infty}(X,\mu)
of
weak-
L^{p}
functions can be defined by the finiteness
condition
\left\|f\right\|^{*}_{p,\infty}<\infty,

where for a measurable function
f
on
X
, the extended real number
\left\|f\right\|^{*}_{p,\infty}
is defined by

(2.1)

\left\|f\right\|^{*}_{p,\infty}=\sup_{y>0}\left(y\cdot\mu\left\{x\in X:\left|f(x)\right|>y\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right).

Then
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p,\infty}^{*}
satisfies all the defining properties of a norm expect the triangle inequality. Instead, for measurable
f
and
g
, we only have

\left\|f+g\right\|_{p,\infty}^{*}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\left\|f\right\|_{p,\infty}^{*}+\left\|g\right\|_{p,\infty}^{*}\right).

The “quasinorm”
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p,\infty}^{*}
defines a linear topology on the vector space
L^{p,\infty}(X,\mu)
.
It is well-known that
this topology is in fact normable. Two equivalent norms on
L^{p,\infty}(X,\mu)
are

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{p,\infty}=\sup\left\{\mu(E)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left|\int_{E}fd\mu\right|:E\text{{ measurable, }}\mu(E)<\infty\right\},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|^{\prime}_{p,\infty}=\sup\left\{\mu(E)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\int_{E}\left|f\right|d\mu:E\text{ \emph{measurable}, }\mu(E)<\infty\right\}.

See for example
[
Gra14
, Exercise 1.1.12]
for the equivalence of
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{p,\infty}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{*}_{p,\infty}
. As to the equivalence of
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{p,\infty}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p,\infty}
, notice that for each measurable function
f
, we have
\left\|f\right\|_{p,\infty}\leq\left\|f\right\|^{\prime}_{p,\infty}
from their definitions. When

f
is nonnegative or nonpositive, the reverse inequality holds, and we see that

\left\|f\right\|^{\prime}_{p,\infty}\leq 4\left\|f\right\|_{p,\infty}
by splitting the real and
imaginary parts of
f
into positive and negative parts.

We now consider two
\sigma
-finite measure spaces

(M,\mu)
and
(N,\nu)
and let
T
be a linear operator
mapping a vector space of measurable functions on
(M,\mu)
into measurable functions on
(N,\nu).

We can define the “weak type
(p,p)
operator norm”
by setting

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathrm{WT}(p)}=\sup\left\{\frac{\left\|Tf\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(N,\nu)}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(M,\mu)}}:f\in L^{p}(M,\mu),f\not=0\right\},

which is just the operator norm of
T
as a linear mapping from the Banach space
L^{p}(M,\mu)
into the Banach space

\left(L^{p}(N,\nu),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(N,\nu)}\right).

The operator

T
is of weak type
(p,p)
if and only
if
\left\|T\right\|_{\mathrm{WT}(p)}<\infty
.
Similarly, we can define the “restricted type
(q,q)
operator norm” of
T

by setting

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathrm{RT}(q)}=\sup\left\{\frac{\left\|T1_{E}\right\|_{L^{q}(N,\nu)}}{\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{q}(M,\mu)}}:E\subset X\text{ measurable},0<\mu(E)<\infty\right\}.

Again
T
is of restricted type
(q,q)
if and only if
\left\|T\right\|_{\mathrm{RT}(q)}<\infty,
and it is possible to interpret this as an operator norm between Lorentz spaces.

We will also need the notion of the
adjoint
of the operator
T
. This is a linear operator
T^{*}
mapping a vector space of measurable functions on

(N,\nu)
into the space of measurable functions on
(M,\mu)
, and satisfies

\displaystyle{\int_{N}(Tf)\overline{g}d\nu=\int_{M}f\cdot\overline{T^{*}g}d\mu.}

In case
T
is
of restricted type
(p,p)
, for
f
simple on
(M,\mu)
and
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(N,\nu),
the existence
of
T^{*}
follows from a standard argument
involving the Radon-Nikodym theorem (see

[
SW59
, p. 265]
). For
T
of weak type
(p,p)
, for
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(M,\mu)
and
g
simple on
(N,\mu)
,
the existence of
T^{*}
also can be proved
in a similar way. Notice that we have
(T^{*})^{*}=T
.
We will now recall the
following duality statement between the
weak type norm and the restricted type norm:

Proposition 2.1
.

let
T
be a linear operator
mapping simple functions on
(M,\mu)
into measurable functions on
(N,\nu)
,
let
1<p<\infty
and let
p^{\prime}
be the Hölder conjugate of
p
. Then

(2.4)

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathrm{RT}(p^{\prime})}=\left\|T^{*}\right\|_{\mathrm{WT}(p)}.

Proof.

In (
2.4
), it is allowed that the common value of these norms is infinite.

For a measurable
E\subset M
of finite nonzero measure we have by duality of the space
L^{p}(N,\nu)
with the space
L^{p^{\prime}}(N,\nu)

\displaystyle\left\|T1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(N,\nu)}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\frac{\left|\int_{N}(T1_{E})\overline{f}d\nu\right|}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(N,\nu)}}:f\in L^{p}(N,\nu),f\not=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\frac{\left|\int_{M}1_{E}(\overline{T^{*}f})d\mu\right|}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(N,\nu)}}:f\in L^{p}(N,\nu),f\not=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\frac{\left|\int_{E}{T^{*}f}d\mu\right|}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(N,\nu)}}:f\in L^{p}(N,\nu),f\not=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(N,\nu)}}\frac{\left|\int_{E}{T^{*}f}d\mu\right|}{\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(M,\mu)}}:f\in L^{p}(N,\nu),f\not=0\right\}\cdot\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(M,\mu)}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(N,\nu)}}\mu(E)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left|\int_{E}{T^{*}f}d\mu\right|:f\in L^{p}(N,\nu),f\not=0\right\}\cdot\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(M,\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\left\{\frac{\left\|T^{*}f\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(M,\mu)}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(N,\nu)}}:f\in L^{p}(N,\nu),f\not=0\right\}\cdot\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(M,\mu)}

\displaystyle=\left\|T\right\|_{\mathrm{WT(p)}}\cdot\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(M,\mu)}.

It follows that we have

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathrm{RT}(p^{\prime})}\leq\left\|T^{*}\right\|_{\mathrm{WT}(p)}.

For the reverse inequality one can proceed similarly
starting from the definition of
\left\|T^{*}f\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(M,\mu)}
, and change the order of taking suprema
to yield
\left\|T^{*}f\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(M,\mu)}\leq\left\|T\right\|_{\mathrm{RT}(p^{\prime})}\cdot\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(N,\nu)}
. We leave the details to the reader.
∎

3.
An estimate on the polydisc

The proof of Theorem

uses the fact that

j\leq j_{0}-1
and
\frac{c_{j+1}}{c_{j}}\leq\frac{c_{j_{0}+2}}{c_{j_{0}+1}}<1
for all
j\geq j_{0}+1
. Set
j_{1}=\max\{j\in I:j\leq j_{0}+1\}
and
j_{2}=\min\{j\in I:j\geq j_{0}+1\}
.
Then the inequalities for the ratio
c_{j+1}/c_{j}
imply that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I,j\leq j_{0}+1}j^{\mu}\gamma^{j}\approx c_{j_{1}}\quad\text{ and }\sum_{j\in I_{k},j\geq j_{0}+1}j^{\mu}\gamma^{j}\approx c_{j_{2}}.

Choose
j_{*}
from
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
such that
c_{j_{*}}=\max\{c_{j_{1}},c_{j_{2}}\}
. Then the claimed estimate follows:

\sum_{j\in I}j^{\mu}\gamma^{j}\approx c_{j_{1}}+c_{j_{2}}\approx c_{j_{*}}.

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
t>1
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
with
\alpha_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
and
\alpha\not=0
. Set

(3.11)

p=\frac{t\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+2}.

(1)

There is a
C>0
such that for each measurable subset
E\subset\mathbb{D}^{n}
we have

(3.12)

\int_{E}\rho_{\alpha}\,dV\leq C\left(\int_{E}\rho_{t\alpha}\cdot\left(-\log{\rho_{\alpha}}\right)^{(p-1)(m(\alpha)-1)}dV\right)^{1/p}.

(2)

For each
\epsilon>0
there is a
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that for each measurable subset
E\subset\mathbb{D}^{n}
we have

(3.13)

\int_{E}\rho_{\alpha}dV\leq C_{\epsilon}\left(\int_{E}\rho_{t\alpha}dV\right)^{1/(p+\epsilon)}.

Proof.

For an integer
j\geq 0
, if we set
\displaystyle{U_{j}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}:\frac{1}{2^{j}}\leq\rho_{\alpha}(z)<\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}\right\}},

then from Lemma
3.2
, we see that

(3.14)

\left|U_{j}\right|\approx 2^{-\frac{2j}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}}\cdot j^{m(\alpha)-1}.

Let
\eta\geq 0
. On the set
U_{j}
, we clearly have

\rho_{t\alpha}(-\log\rho_{\alpha})^{\eta}\approx j^{\eta}2^{-tj},

where the implied constant is independent of
j
but depends on
\alpha
and
t
.
If for
k\geq 1
, we set

\displaystyle{I_{k}=\left\{j\geq 1:\frac{1}{2^{k}}<\frac{\left|E\cap U_{j}\right|}{\left|U_{j}\right|}\leq\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\right\}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E}\rho_{t\alpha}\log(-\rho_{\alpha})^{\eta}\,dV

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{E\cap U_{j}}\rho_{t\alpha}\log(-\rho_{\alpha})^{\eta}\,dV\approx\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{\eta}2^{-tj}\left|E\cap U_{j}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{\eta}2^{-tj}\left|U_{j}\right|\cdot\frac{\left|E\cap U_{j}\right|}{\left|U_{j}\right|}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in I_{k}}j^{\eta}2^{-tj}\left|U_{j}\right|\cdot\frac{\left|E\cap U_{j}\right|}{\left|U_{j}\right|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j\in I_{k}}j^{\eta}2^{-tj}2^{-\frac{2j}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}}\cdot j^{m(\alpha)-1}\right)\frac{1}{2^{k}}

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j\in I_{k}}j^{\eta+m(\alpha)-1}2^{-j\left(t+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}\right)\frac{1}{2^{k}}.

Thanks to Lemma
3.3
, for each
k
, there exists a
j_{k}\in I_{k}
such that

\sum_{j\in I_{k}}j^{\eta+m(\alpha)-1}2^{-j\left(t+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}\approx j_{k}^{\eta+m(\alpha)-1}2^{-j_{k}\left(t+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)},

where the implied constant depends only on
\alpha
and
\eta
(and not on
k
). Therefore

(3.16)

\int_{E}\rho_{t\alpha}\log(-\rho_{\alpha})^{\eta}\,dV\approx\eqref{eq-comp1}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(j_{k}^{\eta+m(\alpha)-1}2^{-j_{k}\left(t+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}\right)\frac{1}{2^{k}}.

Taking
t=1
and
\eta=0
in (
3.16
) we see that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\int_{E}\rho_{\alpha}dV

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(j_{k}^{m(\alpha)-1}2^{-j_{k}\left(1+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}\right)\frac{1}{2^{k}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(j_{k}^{m(\alpha)-1}2^{-j_{k}\left(1+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}\right)^{p}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}j_{k}^{p(m(\alpha)-1)}2^{-pj_{k}\left(1+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Here in (
3.18
), we have use Jensen’s inequality
\phi\left(\int fd\mu\right)\leq\int\phi\circ f\,d\mu
, where
\phi
is the convex function
\phi(t)=t^{p}
with
p
as in (
3.11
), the measure
\mu

on the set of positive integers is such that
\mu(k)=\frac{1}{2^{k}}
and
f(k)=j_{k}^{m(\alpha)-1}2^{-j_{k}\left(1+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}.
Taking
\eta=(p-1)(m(\alpha)-1)

in (
3.16
), and noticing that (
3.11
) is equivalent to
\displaystyle{t+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}}=p\left(1+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)
,
we see that

\int_{E}\rho_{t\alpha}\cdot\left(-\log{\rho_{\alpha}}\right)^{(p-1)(m(\alpha)-1)}dV\approx\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}j_{k}^{p(m(\alpha)-1)}2^{-pj_{k}\left(1+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}\frac{1}{2^{k}}.

Comparing the right hand side with (
3.19
) the inequality (
3.12
) follows.

To see (
3.13
), let
\delta>0
be defined by the relation
\displaystyle{(p+\epsilon)\left(1+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\delta}\right)=t+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}}
.
Since we have

j^{m(\alpha)-1}2^{-\frac{j}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{j}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\delta}}

(with the implied constant depending on
\epsilon
and
\alpha
but not
j
), from (
3.17
) we see that

\displaystyle\int_{E}\rho_{\alpha}dV

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-j_{k}\left(1+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\delta}\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{k}}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{-j_{k}\left(1+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\delta}\right)}\right)^{(p+\epsilon)}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{-j_{k}\left(t+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(j_{k}^{m(\alpha)-1}2^{-j_{k}\left(t+\frac{2}{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\right)}\right)\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+\epsilon}}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\approx\left(\int_{E}\rho_{t\alpha}dV\right)^{\frac{1}{p+\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\text{ using \eqref{eq-rhotalpha}},

where again in (
3.20
) we have used the Jensen inequality, this time with the convex function
t\mapsto t^{p+\epsilon}.
This completes the proof of (
3.13
).
∎

3.3.
A special case of the main estimate

Denote by
\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n}
the polydisc of
radius
\frac{1}{2}
:

\mathbb{D}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left|z_{j}\right|<\frac{1}{2}\text{ for }1\leq j\leq n\right\}.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
0\leq\ell\leq n
, and let
G
be a measurable subset of the product domain

(3.22)

\mathbb{D}^{\ell}_{\frac{1}{2}}\times\left(\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}\setminus\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}_{\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}:\left|z_{j}\right|<\frac{1}{2}\text{ for }j\leq\ell,\text{ and }\left|z_{j}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\text{ for }j\geq\ell+1\right\}.

If
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})
is a nonzero
tuple of nonnegative numbers, then we have

(3.23)

\left\|P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(\rho_{\beta}\cdot 1_{G})\right\|^{p_{*}}_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\lesssim\int_{G}\rho_{2\beta}\cdot(-\log\rho_{\beta})^{(p_{*}-1)(m(\beta)-1)}\,dV,

where
\displaystyle{p_{*}=\frac{2\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}+2}{\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}+2}.}
Further, if
p\in(p_{*},2]
we have

(3.24)

\left\|P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(\rho_{\beta}\cdot 1_{G})\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\lesssim\int_{G}\rho_{2\beta}\,dV.

Proof.

Write the coordinates of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as
(z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})
where
z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
and
z^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{n-\ell},
and write the tuple
\beta
as
(\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime})

where
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
and
\beta^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-\ell}.

For
w\in G
and
z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, we have
\left|w_{j}\right|<\frac{1}{2}
and
\left|z_{j}\right|<1
, so
\left|1-z_{j}\overline{w_{j}}\right|>\frac{1}{2}
whenever
j\leq\ell
. Therefore

\left|K_{\mathbb{D}^{\ell}}(z^{\prime},w^{\prime})\right|=\frac{1}{\pi^{\ell}}\cdot\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left|1-z_{j}\overline{w_{j}}\right|^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{4}{\pi}\right)^{\ell},

so that

(3.25)

\left|K_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(z,w)\right|=\left|K_{\mathbb{D}^{\ell}}(z^{\prime},w^{\prime})\right|\cdot\left|K_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}(z^{\prime\prime},w^{\prime\prime})\right|\lesssim\left|K_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}(z^{\prime\prime},w^{\prime\prime})\right|.

Moreover, for
w\in G
we have
\frac{1}{2}\leq\left|w_{j}\right|<1
if
j\geq\ell+1,
so

(3.26)

\rho_{\beta}(w)=\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})\rho_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}(w^{\prime\prime})\approx\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime}).

Therefore for
z=(z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}

\displaystyle P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(\rho_{\beta}1_{G})(z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\left|K_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(z,w)\right|\rho_{\beta}(w)1_{G}(w)dV(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\left|K_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}(z^{\prime\prime},w^{\prime\prime})\right|\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})1_{G}(w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})dV\quad\text{ using \eqref{eq-inp1} and \eqref{eq-inp2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}\left|K_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}(z^{\prime\prime},w^{\prime\prime})\right|\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}^{\ell}}\rho_{\beta}(w^{\prime})1_{G}(w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})dV(w^{\prime})\right)dV(w^{\prime\prime})

(3.27)

\displaystyle=P_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}^{+}(h)(z^{\prime\prime}),

where
h
is the function on
\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}
given by

(3.28)

h(w^{\prime\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{D}^{\ell}}\rho_{\beta}(w^{\prime})1_{G}(w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})dV(w^{\prime})=\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}dV,

with

G(w^{\prime\prime})=\{w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{D}:(w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})\in G\}.

For
p>1
we have

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}^{+}(\rho_{\beta}1_{G})\right\|_{L^{{p}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}^{{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}(h)\right\|^{{p}}_{L^{{p}}(\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell})}\quad\text{ using \eqref{eq-comp7}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|h\right\|^{{p}}_{L^{{p}}(\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell})}\quad\text{ since $P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}$ is bounded in $L^{{p}}(\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell})$ }

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}(h(w^{\prime\prime}))^{{p}}dV(w^{\prime\prime})

(3.29)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}\left(\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})dV(w^{\prime})\right)^{{p}}dV(w^{\prime\prime}).

To prove (
3.23
), we consider two cases, depending on the tuple
\beta
:

(1)

Case 1: The
\ell
-tuple
\beta^{\prime}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell})
has an element equal to
\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}=\max\{\beta_{k}:1\leq k\leq n\},
i.e.
\left\|\beta^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}
, where
\left\|\beta^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}=\max\{\beta_{k},1\leq k\leq\ell\}
.

In this case,

\frac{2\left\|\beta^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}+2}{\left\|\beta^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}+2}=p_{*}=\frac{2\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}+2}{\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}+2},

so by conclusion (
3.12
) of Proposition
3.4
(with
t=2
), we have for each
w^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}:

(3.30)

\displaystyle(h(w^{\prime\prime}))^{p_{*}}=\left(\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}dV\right)^{p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{2\beta^{\prime}}\cdot(-\log\rho_{\beta^{\prime}})^{(p_{*}-1)(m(\beta^{\prime})-1)}dV.

Then taking
p=p_{*}
in (
3.29
) and using (
3.30
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}^{+}(\rho_{\beta}1_{G})\right\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}^{p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\eqref{eq-comp8}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{2\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})\cdot(-\log\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime}))^{(p_{*}-1)(m(\beta^{\prime})-1)}dV(w^{\prime})dV(w^{\prime\prime})

(3.31)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\rho_{2\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})\cdot(-\log\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime}))^{(p_{*}-1)(m(\beta^{\prime})-1)}dV(w).

The integrand in (
3.31
) is dominated by the function

\displaystyle w=(w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\mapsto\rho_{2\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})\rho_{2\beta^{\prime\prime}}(w^{\prime\prime})\cdot(-\log\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})-\log\rho_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}(w^{\prime\prime}))^{(p_{*}-1)(m(\beta)-1)}

\displaystyle=\left(\rho_{2\beta}\cdot(-\log\rho_{\beta})^{(p_{*}-1)(m(\beta)-1)}\right)(w),

since
\rho_{2\beta^{\prime\prime}}\approx 1
,
-\log\rho_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}>0
and
m(\beta)\geq m(\beta^{\prime}).
This completes the proof of (
3.23
) in this case.

(2)

Case 2: no element of the
\ell
-tuple
\beta^{\prime}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell})
is equal to
\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}
. Then
\left\|\beta^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}<\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}
, and if we set

\displaystyle{p^{\prime}=\frac{2\left\|\beta^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}+2}{\left\|\beta^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}+2}}
, then we have
p^{\prime}<p_{*}
.

Now, using the conclusion (
3.13
) of Proposition
3.4
(with
t=2
and
p^{\prime}+\epsilon=p_{*}
) we conclude that for each
w^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}

(3.32)

(h(w^{\prime\prime}))^{p_{*}}=\left(\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}dV\right)^{p_{*}}\lesssim\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{2\beta^{\prime}}\,dV.

Therefore, again taking
p=p_{*}
in (
3.29
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}^{+}(\rho_{\beta}1_{G})\right\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}^{p_{*}}\lesssim\eqref{eq-comp8}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{2\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})dV(w^{\prime})dV(w^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\rho_{2\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})dV(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{G}\rho_{2\beta}\cdot(-\log\rho_{\beta})^{(p_{*}-1)(m(\beta)-1)}dV.

Here we use the facts that
\rho_{2\beta^{\prime\prime}}\approx 1
and that

-\log\rho_{\beta}(w)=-\log\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})-\log\rho_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}(w^{\prime\prime})\gtrsim 1,

since
-\log\rho_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}(w^{\prime\prime})\gtrsim 1
and

-\log\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})>0
if
w=(w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})\in G.

This completes the second case, and also the proof
of (
3.23
).

The proof of (
3.24
) is similar to that of case (2) above. Since
p>p_{*}
, by conclusion (
3.13
) of Proposition
3.4
(with
t=2
) we conclude that for each
w^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}

(3.33)

(h(w^{\prime\prime}))^{p}=\left(\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{\beta^{\prime}}dV\right)^{p}\lesssim\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{2\beta^{\prime}}\,dV.

Therefore, by (
3.29
)

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}^{+}(\rho_{\beta}1_{G})\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}^{p}\lesssim\eqref{eq-comp8}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}}\int_{G(w^{\prime\prime})}\rho_{2\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})dV(w^{\prime})dV(w^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\rho_{2\beta^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})dV(w)\lesssim\int_{G}\rho_{2\beta}dV,

where we use the fact that
\rho_{2\beta^{\prime\prime}}\approx 1
on
G
. This completes the proof of (
3.24
).
∎

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the power set of the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. For a subset
\mathcal{J}=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\}\in\mathcal{S}
, define a projection
map
\pi_{\mathcal{J}}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{\left|\mathcal{J}\right|}
by setting
\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(z)=(z_{j_{1}},\dots,z_{j_{k}})
for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Denoting by
\mathcal{J}^{c}
the complement
\{1,2,\dots,n\}\setminus\mathcal{J}
, we abuse notation slightly by
writing
z=(\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(z),\pi_{\mathcal{J}^{c}}(z)).

Notice also
\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cong\mathbb{D}^{\#\mathcal{J}}.

Define the operator
P^{+}_{\mathcal{J}}
as follows.
For a function
f
on
\mathbb{D}^{n},
we set for
z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
:

P^{+}_{\mathcal{J}}(f)(z)=\int\limits_{\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\frac{f\left(\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(w),\pi_{\mathcal{J}^{c}}(z)\right)dV(\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(w))}{\prod\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\pi\left|1-z_{j}\bar{w}_{j}\right|^{2}},

where, by slight abuse of notation, we mean by
\left(\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(w),\pi_{\mathcal{J}^{c}}(z)\right)
the
n
-tuple in which the
j
-th element is
w_{j}
if
j\in\mathcal{J}
and is

z_{j}
if
j\not\in\mathcal{J}
. Therefore, we can think of

P^{+}_{\mathcal{J}}
as the positive Bergman operator
acting in the variables
z_{j},j\in\mathcal{J}
, or more precisely as a tensor product of the positive Bergman operator on a polydisc of dimension
\#\mathcal{J}
with
the identity operator on another polydisc of dimension

\#\mathcal{J}^{c}=n-\#\mathcal{J}.

For
\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{S}
, we set

F_{\mathcal{J}}=\left\{w\in F:|w_{j}|<\frac{1}{2}\text{ for }j\in\mathcal{J}\text{ and }\frac{1}{2}\leq|w_{k}|<1\text{ for }k\in\mathcal{J}^{c}\right\}.

The collection
\left\{F_{\mathcal{J}},\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{S}\right\}
consists of pairwise disjoint sets and
F=\bigcup\limits_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{S}}F_{\mathcal{J}}
. Therefore

1_{F}=\sum\limits_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{S}}1_{F_{\mathcal{J}}}
and consequently

\left\|P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(\rho_{\alpha}\cdot 1_{F})\right\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\leq\sum_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{S}}\left\|P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(\rho_{\alpha}\cdot 1_{F_{\mathcal{J}}})\right\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\lesssim\left(\sum_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{S}}\left\|P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(\rho_{\alpha}\cdot 1_{F_{\mathcal{J}}})\right\|_{L^{p_{*}}}^{p_{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{*}}}.

Therefore, to complete the proof of (
3.3
),
it is enough to show that
for each
\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{S}
, we have

(3.34)

\left\|P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(\rho_{\alpha}\cdot 1_{F_{\mathcal{J}}})\right\|^{p_{*}}_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\lesssim\int_{F_{\mathcal{J}}}\rho_{2\alpha}(-\log\rho_{\alpha})^{(p_{*}-1)(m(\alpha)-1)}\,dV.

Similarly, to complete the proof of (
3.4
),
we need to show that for each
p\in(p_{*},2]
we have

(3.35)

\left\|P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(\rho_{\alpha}\cdot 1_{F_{\mathcal{J}}})\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\lesssim\int_{F_{\mathcal{J}}}\rho_{2\alpha}\,dV.

Let
\ell=\#\mathcal{J}
be the cardinality of
\mathcal{J}
, and let
\sigma:\{1,2,\dots,n\}\to\{1,2,\dots,n\}
be a permutation such that
\sigma(\mathcal{J})=\{1,2,\dots,\ell\}
.
Then
\sigma(\mathcal{J}^{c})=\{\ell+1,\dots,n\}
, and define
a unitary automorphism
S:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
by setting

S(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=(z_{\sigma(1)},\dots,z_{\sigma(n)}).
Making a change of variables by
the map
S
on both sides of (
3.34
), we have
the equivalent inequality:

(3.36)

\left\|P^{+}_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}(\rho_{\beta}\cdot 1_{G})\right\|^{p_{*}}_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\lesssim\int_{G}\rho_{2\beta}(-\log\rho_{\beta})^{(p_{*}-1)(m(\alpha)-1)}\,dV,

where
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})
with
\beta_{j}=\alpha_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}
is a permutation of
the tuple
\alpha
, the set
G=F_{\mathcal{J}}\circ S^{-1}
is
measurable and contained in the set
\mathbb{D}^{\ell}_{\frac{1}{2}}\times\left(\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}\setminus\mathbb{D}^{n-\ell}_{\frac{1}{2}}\right)
of (
3.22
).
Note that
m(\alpha)=m(\beta),

\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}
and consequently,

\displaystyle{p_{*}=\frac{2\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}+2}{\left\|\beta\right\|_{\infty}+2}}
. The estimate (
3.36
) now follows from conclusion (
3.23
) of
Proposition
3.5
.

The same change of variables transforms (
3.35

In terms of the quasinorm of (

5.2.
The sets
F_{s}

Without loss of generality, we assume that the
\Gamma
-invariant monomial
e_{b}
has

b_{1}=b_{2}=1
. By perhaps multiplying
e_{b}
by
z_{j}^{|\det A|}
for
j>2

b\succeq\mathbbm{1}
, i.e.
b_{j}\geq 1
for all

Let
B\subset[0,2\pi)^{n}

be the subset

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\left\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times 1}:\theta\in[0,2\pi)^{n},\sin((\alpha-b+\mathbbm{1})\theta)\geq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\theta\in[0,2\pi)^{n}:2k\pi\leq\sum{(\alpha_{j}+1-b_{j})\theta_{j}}\leq 2k\pi+\pi\right\}.

For each
s\in(0,1/4)
,
define a subset
A(s)\subset[0,1)^{n}
by

A(s)=\{r\in[0,1)^{n}:{r_{1}}r_{2}<s,s<{r_{1}}<\sqrt{s}\}.

We now set

\displaystyle F_{s}

\displaystyle=\left\{(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,r_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:r\in A(s),\theta\in B\right\}

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}:\left|z_{1}z_{2}\right|<s,s<\left|z_{1}\right|<\sqrt{s},0\leq\text{Arg}(z^{\alpha+\mathbbm{1}-b})\leq{\pi}\right\}.

Since each of the functions
z\mapsto\left|z_{1}\right|,z\mapsto\left|z_{2}\right|

and
e_{\alpha+\mathbbm{1}-b}=e_{\mathbbm{1}A-b}
is
\Gamma
-invariant, it follows
that for each
g\in\Gamma
, we have
g(F_{s})=F_{s}
, i.e.
F_{s}
is invariant under the group
\Gamma
.

We turn to computing the integrals of monomials over
A(s)
. For
d\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\int_{A(s)}r^{d}dV(r)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{j=3}^{n}(d_{j}+1)}\int_{\{r\in[0,1]^{2}:r_{1}r_{2}<s,s<r_{1}<\sqrt{s}\}}r_{1}^{d_{1}}r_{2}^{d_{2}}dr_{1}dr_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{j=3}^{n}(d_{j}+1)}\int_{s}^{\sqrt{s}}\int_{0}^{s/r_{1}}r_{1}^{d_{1}}r_{2}^{d_{2}}dr_{2}dr_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{j=3}^{n}(d_{j}+1)}\int_{s}^{\sqrt{s}}r_{1}^{d_{1}}\frac{s^{d_{2}+1}r_{1}^{-d_{2}-1}}{d_{2}+1}dr_{1}

(5.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{(d_{1}-d_{2})\prod_{j=2}^{n}(d_{j}+1)}\left(s^{\frac{d_{1}+d_{2}}{2}+1}-s^{d_{1}+1}\right)&\text{ for }d_{1}\neq d_{2},\\
\dfrac{1}{2\prod_{j=2}^{n}(d_{j}+1)}s^{d_{1}+1}\log(1/s)&\text{ for }d_{1}=d_{2}.\end{cases}

Using this we obtain for the denominator of (
5.5
)

\displaystyle\int_{F_{s}}\rho_{2\alpha}\,dV\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}:\left|z_{1}\right|\left|z_{2}\right|<s,s<\left|z_{1}\right|<\sqrt{s}\}}\rho_{2\alpha}\,dV

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{D}^{2}:\left|z_{1}\right|\left|z_{2}\right|<s,s<\left|z_{1}\right|<\sqrt{s}\}}|z_{1}z_{2}|^{2\alpha_{1}}\,dV

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\{r\in[0,1]^{2}:r_{1}r_{2}<s,s<r_{1}<\sqrt{s}\}}r_{1}^{2\alpha_{1}+1}r_{2}^{2\alpha_{1}+1}dr_{1}dr_{2}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle s^{(2\alpha_{1}+2)}\log(1/s).

5.3.
A lower bound on
h_{s}

In the definition (
5.4
) of
h_{s}
we let the set
F_{s}
to be as in (
5.6
). We will show the following:

Lemma 5.3
.

There are constants
r\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
,

s_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
and
K>0
and a relatively compact nonempty open subset
D\subset\mathbb{D}^{n-2}

such that for
z
in
the set

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\Pi=\mathbb{D}^{2}_{r}\times D=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left|z_{j}\right|<r\text{ for }1\leq j\leq 2,(z_{3},\dots,z_{n})\in D\right\}}

and
s\in(0,s_{0})
we have

\left|h_{s}(z)\right|\geq Ks^{\alpha_{1}+2}\log\left(1/s\right).

Proof.

By Bell’s formula we have

(5.9)

h_{s}=U\cdot P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}(1_{\phi(F_{s})})\circ\phi=\det A\cdot e_{\mathbbm{1}A-\mathbbm{1}}P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}(1_{\phi(F_{s})})\circ\phi.

The Taylor series expansion

h_{s}=\sum_{\gamma\succeq 0}a_{\gamma}(s)e_{\gamma}
in
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is also an orthogonal expansion, and its coefficients are given by

\displaystyle a_{\gamma}(s)=

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle h_{s},e_{\gamma}\right\rangle}{\left\|e_{\gamma}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}}=\frac{\left\langle P_{\Omega}(u1_{F_{s}}),e_{\gamma}\right\rangle}{\left\|e_{\gamma}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}}=\frac{\left\langle u1_{F_{s}},e_{\gamma}\right\rangle}{\left\|e_{\gamma}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}}=\frac{(\gamma+\mathbbm{1})^{\mathbbm{1}}}{\pi^{n}}\left\langle u1_{F_{s}},e_{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(\gamma+\mathbbm{1})^{\mathbbm{1}}\cdot\det A}{\pi^{n}}\int_{F_{s}}e_{\alpha}\overline{e_{\gamma}}dV

(5.10)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\gamma+\mathbbm{1})^{\mathbbm{1}}\cdot\det A}{\pi^{n}}\cdot\int_{A(s)}r^{\gamma+\alpha+\mathbbm{1}}dV(r)\cdot\int_{B}e^{i(\alpha-\gamma)\cdot\theta}dV(\theta).

From (
5.9
), the function
h_{s}\cdot e_{\mathbbm{1}}
is
\Gamma
-invariant, since it is the product of
\det A
and the
\Gamma
-invariant functions
e_{\mathbbm{1}A}
and
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}(1_{\phi(F_{s})})\circ\phi
. Consequently the series

\sum_{\gamma\succeq 0}a_{\gamma}(s)e_{\gamma+\mathbbm{1}}
represents the
\Gamma
-invariant function
h_{s}\cdot e_{\mathbbm{1}}
, and each term of this series must be
\Gamma
-invariant. Therefore the series for
h_{s}
can be rewritten as

(5.11)

h_{s}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\succeq 1\\
\beta\text{ is }\Gamma-\text{invariant}\end{subarray}}a_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}(s)e_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be
the subset
\mathcal{J}=\{\beta\succeq\mathbbm{1}:\beta\text{ is }\Gamma-\text{invariant},\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=1\},

and let
\mathcal{K}
be the set of
indices of the remaining terms in the series (
5.11
), i.e.

\mathcal{K}=\{\beta\succeq\mathbbm{1}:\beta\text{ is }\Gamma-\text{invariant},\beta_{1}\not=1\text{ or }\beta_{2}\not=1\}.

Notice that
\mathcal{J}
is nonempty since
b\in\mathcal{J}.

Write

(5.12)

h_{s}=h_{s}^{\mathcal{J}}+h_{s}^{\mathcal{K}}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{J}}a_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}(s)e_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}+\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{K}}a_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}(s)e_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}.

The first term can be written as

\displaystyle{h_{s}^{\mathcal{J}}(z)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{J}}a_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}(s)e_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}(z)=a_{b-1}(s)f(z_{3},\dots,z_{n}),}

where
f
is a holomorphic function on

\mathbb{D}^{n-2}
. We claim
f
is non-constant and independent of
s
. To see it’s not a constant we only need to check that
a_{b-\mathbbm{1}}(s)\not=0
, which follows since in the
formula (
5.10
), each of the factors is nonzero if
\gamma=b-\mathbbm{1}
. For the last factor this follows from

\mathrm{Im}\left(\int_{B}e^{i(\alpha-b+\mathbbm{1})\cdot\theta}dV(\theta)\right)=\int_{B}\sin\left((\alpha-b+\mathbbm{1})\cdot\theta\right)dV(\theta)>0

from the definition of
B
.
Note that by letting
d=\gamma+\alpha+\mathbbm{1}
in (
5.7
) we have

(5.13)

\displaystyle\int_{A(s)}r^{\gamma+\alpha+\mathbbm{1}}dV(r)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})\prod_{j=2}^{n}(\alpha_{j}+2+\gamma_{j})}\left(s^{\frac{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}}{2}+\alpha_{1}+2}-s^{\gamma_{1}+\alpha_{1}+2}\right)&\text{ for }\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2},\\
\frac{1}{2\prod_{j=2}^{n}(\alpha_{j}+2+\gamma_{j})}s^{\gamma_{1}+\alpha_{1}+2}\log(1/s)&\text{ for }\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}.\end{cases}

Taking
\gamma=\beta-\mathbbm{1}
in (
5.10
) for
\beta\in\mathcal{J}=\{\beta\succeq\mathbbm{1}:\beta\text{ is }\Gamma-\text{invariant},\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=1\}
we
have

(5.14)

a_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}(s)=\frac{(\beta)^{\mathbbm{1}}\cdot\det A\cdot s^{\alpha_{1}+2}\log(1/s)}{2\pi^{n}(\alpha_{1}+2)\prod_{j=3}^{n}(\alpha_{j}+1+\beta_{j})}\int_{B}e^{i(\alpha-\beta+\mathbbm{1})\cdot\theta}dV(\theta).

Therefore,

f(z_{3},\dots,z_{n})=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{J}}\frac{a_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}(s)e_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}(z)}{a_{b-\mathbbm{1}}(s)},

which is independent of
s
. Then there exists a point
a\in\mathbb{D}^{n-2}
such that
|f(a)|=c>0
. We choose small
r>0
such that the set

D=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}^{n-2}:\left|z_{j}-a_{j}\right|<r,1\leq j\leq n-2\right\}

is relatively compact in
\mathbb{D}^{n-2}
, and
|f(z)|>c/2
for all
z\in D
. The constant
c
here again is independent of
s
.

If
\beta\in\mathcal{K}
then either

\beta_{1}>1
, or
\beta_{1}=1,\beta_{2}>1
. If
\beta_{1}>1
, then for
\gamma=\beta-\mathbbm{1}
,
\gamma_{1}>0
and (
5.13
) implies
\displaystyle{\int_{A(s)}r^{\beta+\alpha}dV(r)\lesssim s^{\alpha_{1}+2}}
for small
s
.
If
\beta_{1}=1
and
\beta_{2}>1
, then for
\gamma=\beta-\mathbbm{1}
we have
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}
and hence

\int_{A(s)}r^{\beta+\alpha}dV(r)\approx s^{\alpha_{1}+2}
. We then conclude from (
5.10
) that
\left|a_{\beta-\mathbbm{1}}(s)\right|\lesssim\beta^{\mathbbm{1}}\cdot s^{\alpha_{1}+2}
if
\beta\in\mathcal{K}
. Consequently, we can write the summand
h_{s}^{\mathcal{K}}
of (
5.12
) as

h_{s}^{\mathcal{K}}(z)=s^{\alpha_{1}+2}g_{s}(z)

where
g_{s}
is holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}^{n}
and satisfies

\left|g_{s}(z)\right|\lesssim\sum_{\gamma\succeq 0}(\gamma+\mathbbm{1})^{\mathbbm{1}}\left|z^{\gamma}\right|=\dfrac{\left|z^{\mathbbm{1}}\right|}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-\left|z_{j}\right|)^{2}},

with the implied constant independent of
s
.

Since
D
is relatively compact in
\mathbb{D}^{n}

we have
|f(z)|>c/2
for
z\in D
.
Further,

\displaystyle{\Pi=\mathbb{D}^{2}_{r}\times D,}

is relatively compact in
\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
\sup_{\Pi}|g_{s}|\lesssim 1
. Therefore for
z\in\Pi

and small
s

\displaystyle\left|h_{s}(z)\right|\geq\left|h_{s}^{\mathcal{J}}(z)\right|-\left|h_{s}^{\mathcal{K}}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|a_{b-1}f(z_{3},\dots,z_{n})\right|-s^{\alpha_{1}+2}\left|g_{s}(z)\right|

(5.15)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{cC_{1}}{2}s^{\alpha_{1}+2}\log(1/s)-C_{2}s^{\alpha_{1}+2}

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are nonnegative constants independent of
s
. In the first term we have used (
5.14
) along with the fact that
\left|f(z_{3},\dots,z_{n})\right|>\frac{c}{2}
if
z\in\Pi
.
Therefore

\left|h_{s}(z)\right|\geq\eqref{eq-hsbound1}=C_{1}s^{\alpha_{1}+2}\log(1/s)\left(\frac{c}{2}-\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}\log(1/s)}\right).

Since
\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}\log(1/s)}\to 0
as
s\to 0
, we can choose
s_{0}
so small that

\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}\log(1/s)}<\frac{c}{2}
if

Thus for
z\in\Pi
and
s\in(0,s_{0})
we have
\frac{c}{2}-\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}\log(1/s)}>\frac{c}{2}
,
and the result follows with

∎

5.4.
The numbers

and completion of the proof

Now with notation as in Lemma
5.3
, we set
\displaystyle{\lambda_{s}=\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{b_{2}-1}Ks^{\alpha_{1}+2}\log({1}/{s}).}

Then for

\frac{\left|h_{s}(z)\right|}{\rho_{\alpha}(z)}\geq\frac{Ks^{\alpha_{1}+2}\log({1}/{s})}{\left|z^{\alpha}\right|}>\lambda_{s},

since

.
Consequently, for each

,

\mu\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}:\frac{\left|h_{s}(z)\right|}{\rho_{\alpha}(z)}>\lambda_{s}\right\}\geq\mu(\Pi).

Combining the definition of
\lambda_{s}
with this statement and (

) we see that:

\frac{(\lambda_{s})^{p_{*}}\cdot\mu\left\{\frac{\left|h_{s}\right|}{\rho_{\alpha}}>\lambda_{s}\right\}}{\int_{F_{s}}\rho_{2\alpha}\,dV}\gtrsim\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)\right)^{p_{*}-1},

and therefore goes to

5.5
) holds, completing the proof of Theorem

follows.

6.
Remarks and directions for future research

Despite Theorems
1.1
and
5.1
, the connection between the matrix
B
of
\mathcal{U}_{B}
and endpoint behavior of
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
requires further study. Below we list some remarks and questions we would like to investigate in the future:

(1)

In Section 5.4, by realizing that
\int_{F_{s}}\rho_{2\alpha}\log(1/\rho_{2\alpha})^{t}\,dV\lesssim s^{2\alpha_{1}+2}(\log(1/s))^{t+1}
, we see that for all
t<p_{*}-1
,

\frac{(\lambda_{s})^{p_{*}}\cdot\mu\left\{\frac{\left|h_{s}\right|}{\rho_{\alpha}}>\lambda_{s}\right\}}{\int_{F_{s}}\rho_{2\alpha}(\log(1/\rho_{2\alpha}))^{t}\,dV}\gtrsim\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)\right)^{p_{*}-1-t},

which still goes to
\infty
as
s\to 0
. This leads to the failure of the estimate below for
\mathcal{U}_{B}
in Theorem
5.1
and any
t<p_{*}-1

(6.1)

\left\|P^{+}_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}(1_{E})\right\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathcal{U}_{B},w^{p_{*}-2})}\leq C\left\|1_{E}\right\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathcal{U}_{B},(-\log w)^{t})},

thereby implying the sharpness of the exponent
(m-1)(p_{*}-1)
of the weight
-\log w
in Estimate (
1.11
) for
m=2
. For general
m
, the exponent
(m-1)(p_{*}-1)
in (
1.11
) is likely to be sharp in the sense that there is a monomial polyhedron
\mathcal{U}_{B}
with (
6.1
) fail for
t<(m-1)(p_{*}-1)
.

(2)

Theorem
5.1
does not suggest in general the failure of restricted weak type
(p_{*},p_{*})
estimates for
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
when
m>1
. For
\mathcal{U}_{B}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
, it is not
difficult to see that there is no monomial polyhedron satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
5.1
(i.e.
m=2
, the monomial
e_{(1,1)}(z)=z_{1}z_{2}
is
\Gamma
-invariant and
\mathcal{U}_{B}
is nontrivial). It is still unclear to us whether there exists
\mathcal{U}_{B}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
on which the Bergman projection fails to be of restricted weak type
(p_{*},p_{*})
(or weak-type
(q_{*},q_{*})
).

(3)

It remains to be seen what conditions on the matrix
B
of
\mathcal{U}_{B}
characterize the precise endpoint
L^{p}
behavior of
P_{\mathcal{U}_{B}}
for
p=p_{*}
and
p=q_{*}
.

(4)

It would also be interesting to study the restricted type estimates for the Bergman projection on other quotient domains such as the symmetrized polydiscs.

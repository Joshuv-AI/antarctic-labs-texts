A Nonlocal Aw-Rascle-Zhang system with linear pressure term
D. Amadori, F. A. Chiarello, G. Cipollone

Abstract
. In this paper, we study a nonlocal extension of the Aw-Rascle-Zhang traffic model, where the pressure-like term is modeled as a convolution between vehicle density and a kernel function. This formulation captures nonlocal driver interactions and aligns structurally with the Euler-alignment system studied in [ 23 ] . Using a sticky particle approximation, we construct entropy solutions to the equation for the cumulative density and prove convergence of approximate solutions to weak solutions of the nonlocal system. The analysis includes well-posedness, stability estimates, and an entropic selection principle. Keywords : Nonlocal Aw-Rascle-Zhang system, sticky particle dynamics, weak solutions

Primary: 35D30, 35L65, 35Q92; Secondary: 76N10, 35B30.

1.
Introduction

Traffic flow models have been studied since many decades, following the growing scientific interest raised by the applications.
Within the class of macroscopic models, the classical LWR model
[
24
,
27
]
is based on a closure relation
that expresses the velocity as a function of the density and has been widely used for applications in engineering because of its simplicity.
However, it shows some limitations, as for instance it is not suitable to predict some real-life complex traffic phenomena such as phantom jams.

Second order models have been introduced to overcome the limitations for first order models. One of the well-known second order traffic models
is the so-called Aw-Rascle-Zhang model (ARZ), introduced in
[
3
,
31
]
, that reads as

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(u+p(\rho))+u\partial_{x}(u+p(\rho))=0\,.\end{cases}

Here
\rho(x,t)\geq 0
represents the macroscopic density of vehicles, i.e. the number of vehicles per unit length,

u(x,t)
the mean velocity and
p(\rho)
is a smooth increasing
pressure-like
function representing
the attitude of drivers
to adapt to the traffic conditions
.

In this paper, motivated by some recent results about nonlocal traffic models, see for example
[
11
,
16
,
10
]
, we introduce a nonlocal version of (
1.1
)
where the pressure term is replaced by
a convolution term between the density
\rho
and a kernel function
\omega
. In particular, we focus our analysis on the case
p(\rho)=\rho
.
The system reads as

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u=-(\partial_{t}+u\partial_{x})(\omega\ast\rho)\,.\end{cases}

The second equation describes the acceleration of drivers along their motion,
and implies that the quantity

(1.3)

\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=u+\omega\ast\rho

satisfies the equation

(1.4)

\partial_{t}\psi+u\partial_{x}\psi=0\,.

Multiplying the first equation of the system by
\psi
and the second one by
\rho
, the system can be written in conservation form as follows:

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\psi)+\partial_{x}(\rho u\psi)=0.\end{cases}

Throughout the paper we will assume that
the initial total mass equals to 1 and then, by conservation of total mass,
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x,t)\,dx=1
for all
t>0
.
The convolution kernel
\omega
in (
1.2
) is assumed to satisfy

\textbf{(H)}\qquad\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)\,,\quad\omega\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})\,,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega\,dx=1\,.

Moreover, we will denote by
\phi
the derivative of
\omega
:

(1.6)

\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\omega^{\prime}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\,.

It is interesting to compare (
1.2
) with the Euler-alignment system,
coming from the theory of collective behavior, see
[
23
]
,

(1.7)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})=\rho(\phi\ast(\rho u))-\rho u(\phi\ast\rho)\end{cases}

where
\rho
and
u
represent the density and velocity, respectively, and the function
\phi
is called

communication protocol
governing the strength of the interactions between the agents.

Indeed, we reformulate the second equation of (
1.2
) using the evaluations of the partial derivatives
of the convolution products, i.e.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\omega\ast\rho)(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\rho(y,t)\omega(x-y)\,dy=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\partial_{x}(\rho u)(y,t)\omega(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\rho u)(y,t)\phi(x-y)\,dy=-(\phi\ast(\rho u))(x,t),

and

\displaystyle u\partial_{x}(\omega\ast\rho)(x,t)

\displaystyle=u(x,t)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega(x-y)\rho_{y}(y,t)\,dy

\displaystyle=u(x)\omega(x-y)\rho(y,t)|_{-\infty}^{\infty}+u(x,t)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(x-y)\rho(y,t)\,dy

\displaystyle=u(\phi\ast\rho)(x,t).

Now, the second equation of (
1.2
) reads

(1.8)

\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u=\phi\ast(\rho u)-u(\phi\ast\rho);

multiplying (
1.8
) by the density
\rho
and summing the equation of the conservation of mass, we obtain

\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})=\rho(\phi\ast(\rho u))-\rho u(\phi\ast\rho).

Then, the nonlocal ARZ system (
1.2
) can be rewritten in the Euler-alignment form (
1.7
).

System (
1.7
) with measure-valued density and bounded velocity has been studied in
[
23
]
,
considering a communication protocol
\phi\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
which is
non-negative and even.
Appropriately chosen
\phi
lead to a long-time behavior referred to as flocking, see
[
28
]
. Moreover, the global regularity theory for smooth solutions has been developed in
[
8
]

via a critical threshold condition for the regular case, i.e. a communication protocol which is Lipschitz continuous and bounded.
For weakly singular interaction, i.e.
\phi
with an integrable singularity at the origin, details about smooth solutions can be found
in
[
22
]
, while for strongly singular interaction, see
[
14
,
20
,
29
]
.
It is important to notice that system (
1.7
) is the hydrodynamic version of the Cucker-Smale system of ODE’s,
see
[
12
]
.
The derivation of a kinetic formulation corresponding to the Cucker-Smale system can be found in
[
18
,
7
,
26
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to prove the well-posedness and stability estimates for system (
1.5
), and to provide an entropic selection principle.
In particular, we develop a global well-posedness theory for weak solutions of (
1.5
) with measure-valued density and bounded velocity. Our approach is based on an adaptation of the sticky particle approximation, originally introduced by Brenier and Grenier
[
5
]
to analyze the 1D pressureless Euler equations and developed in
[
23
]
. The novelty with respect to the previous results in
[
23
]
is that we are considering an
integrable and bounded kernel function
\phi
which is not necessarily symmetric (even) nor positive. The shape of its antiderivative
\omega
, see (
1.6
) and
(H)
, can be chosen closer to that one
of the anisotropic kernel functions usually used in literature for nonlocal traffic flow models such as in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. The choice of a non-symmetric kernel function makes the analysis more involved and interesting, moreover it is coherent from the modeling point of view because drivers are supposed to adapt the velocity with respect to what happens mainly downstream in the traffic. It is worth quoting the following recent paper
[
19
]
in which the authors consider a nonlocal version of the ARZ model with nonlocality in the pressure density obtained as a convolution type term between a positive, bounded and non-increasing on
[0,\infty)
kernel function. In particular,
[
19
]
is focused on the well-posedness of
smooth solutions
and the phase-plane analysis.
Other recent related papers are
[
25
]
and
[
1
]
. In the first paper the authors establish existence, uniqueness and Oleĭnik estimates, together with the singular limit, for a nonlocal version of the so-called Generalized Aw-Rascle-Zhang model (GARZ)
[
15
]
,
where the drivers’ velocity depends on both the empty road velocity and the convolution of the density with an anisotropic kernel. In
[
1
]
the authors analyze the well-posedness and the singular limit for another nonlocal version of the GARZ model in which the nonlocality is present in the flux function as a convolution-type term between the velocity function and an anisotropic kernel function.

The paper is organized as follows.

- In Section
2
we introduce the microscopic approximation based on a nonlocal version of the sticky particle dynamics.

- In Section
3
we introduce a scalar balance law linked to system (
1.5
),
that involves the cumulative quantities
M
and
Q
introduced in (
3.1
),
and we prove the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for
M
, see (
3.28
) and Theorem
3.4
. The proof relies on the sticky particle approximation introduced in Section
2
.

- In Section
4
we use the result in Section
3
to prove the existence of a unique solution of system (
1.5
) in the sense of measure and a stability estimate with respect to the initial data, see Theorem
4.4
.

Finally, two appendixes collect some analytical tools used in the paper.

2.
Sticky particle Cucker-Smale

In this section, we recall the microscopic formulation of the well-known Cucker-Smale system of ODE’s

[
12
]
i.e.

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\dot{x}_{i}=v_{i},\\
\dot{v}_{i}=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{N}}m_{j}\phi(x_{i}-x_{j})(v_{j}-v_{i}),\end{cases}

assuming that the total mass is 1:

(2.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}=1.

System (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) allows for particle trajectories to cross. To avoid this,
we consider its
sticky particle
version, i.e.: if two or more particles collide, meaning that they have the same position
for the first time, they remain stuck and start to travel together with the same velocity.
This sticky particle model was originally proposed in
[
30
,
17
]

to prove the existence of the so-called ‘atomic’ weak solutions to the 1D pressurless Euler system.
System (
2.1
) governs
N
agents defined by three quantities: mass
m_{i}\geq 0
, position
x_{i}

and velocity
v_{i}
for any
i=1,\dots,N
.

From the fact that the particles remain stuck after a collision, it is useful to define the following sets
J_{i}
:

J_{i}(t)=\{j\in 1,\dots,N\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x_{j}(t)=x_{i}(t)\},\quad i=1,\dots,N\,,\quad t\geq 0.

From the definition of
J_{i}
, it follows that

(2.3)

J_{i}(t)\subseteq J_{i}(s)\quad\mbox{ for }0\leq t\leq s\,,\quad\forall\,i\in\{1,\dots,N\}.

Having (
2.3
), we can order the
N
particles involved in the system because when two particles collide
they can not cross each other. Thus, we write

(2.4)

x_{1}(t)\leq x_{2}(t)\leq\dots\leq x_{N}(t),\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

Moreover, it is convenient to set notation for the lowest and the highest indices in a given
J_{i}(t)
, thus

i_{*}(t)=\min J_{i}(t),\qquad i^{*}(t)=\max J_{i}(t).

Then, the dynamics is described as follows:

•

when
t
is not a collision time, the velocity and the acceleration of particles are governed by the following system

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\dot{x}_{i}=v_{i},\\
\dot{v}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\phi(x_{i}-x_{j})(v_{j}-v_{i});\end{cases}

•

when
t
is a collision time, the particles involved in a collision remains stuck and start to travel with the same velocity defined by

(2.6)

v_{i}(t+)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\sum_{j\in J_{i}(t)}m_{j}v_{j}(t-)}{\sum_{j\in J_{i}(t)}m_{j}}.

We define the quantities
\psi_{i}
that are a discrete analog of the quantity
\psi
defined in (
1.3
) thus we have: for those
t
that are not collision times,

(2.7)

\psi_{i}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=v_{i}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)).

We give now two properties of these quantities
\psi_{i}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

If
t
is not a collision time, then
\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{i}(t)=0
;

(ii)

If
t
is a collision time, it holds

(2.8)

\psi_{i}(t)=\frac{\sum_{j\in J_{i}(t)}m_{j}\psi_{j}(t-)}{\sum_{j\in J_{i}(t)}m_{j}}\,.

Proof.

Proof of
(i)
. If
t
is not a collision time, consider the definition (
2.7
) of the
\psi_{i}
and the definition (
2.1
) of
\dot{v}_{i}
by the Cucker-Smale dynamics,
i=1,...,N
.

By evaluating the time derivative and using that
\omega^{\prime}=\phi
, we write

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\left(v_{i}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\right)

\displaystyle=\dot{v}_{i}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\phi(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))(v_{i}(t)-v_{j}(t))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\phi(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))(v_{j}(t)-v_{i}(t))+\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\phi(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))(v_{i}(t)-v_{j}(t))

\displaystyle=0.

Proof of
(ii)
. We consider a particle
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
at a collision time
t.

The aim is to prove that the following equality holds true

(2.9)

\sum_{j\in J_{i}(t)}m_{j}\psi_{j}(t-)=\left(\sum_{j\in J_{i}(t)}m_{j}\right)\psi_{i}(t+).

Then, we rewrite the above equation (
2.9
) in the following way

\sum_{j=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{j}\psi_{j}(t-)=\left(\sum_{j=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{j}\right)\psi_{i}(t+).

For any
k\in\{i_{*}(t),\dots,i^{*}(t)\}
, by the definition of
\psi_{i}
in (
2.7
) we have that

(2.10)

m_{k}\psi_{k}(t-)=m_{k}v_{k}(t-)+m_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{k}(t)-x_{j}(t)).

Then we consider
x_{j}(t)
in place of
x_{j}(t-)
for any
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
by the fact that the trajectories of particles are continuous.
We consider the sum over
k
in the set
J_{i}(t)
, what we obtain is

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{k}\psi_{k}(t-)=\sum_{k=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}\Bigr[m_{k}v_{k}(t-)+m_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{k}(t)-x_{j}(t))\Bigl].

On the other hand, the term
\displaystyle{\sum_{k=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}}m_{k}\psi_{i}(t)
can be rewritten as follows,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{k}\psi_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{k}\Bigr(v_{i}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{k}(t)-x_{j}(t))\Bigl)

(2.12)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{k}v_{i}(t)+\sum_{k=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{k}(t)-x_{j}(t)).

Using (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), we conclude that

\sum_{k=i_{*}(t-)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{k}v_{i}(t)=\sum_{k=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{k}v_{k}(t-).

Then, the common velocity is

v_{i}(t)=\frac{\sum_{k=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{k}v_{k}(t-)}{\sum_{k=i_{*}(t)}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{k}},

and hence (
2.8
) follows.
∎

We remark that the property in
{(ii)}
is analogous to (
1.4
),
indeed we have the conservation of the quantity
\psi
along the trajectories:

0=\partial_{t}\psi+u\partial_{x}\psi=\frac{d}{dt}\psi\,.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
(x_{i}(t),v_{i}(t))_{i=1}^{N}
follow the sticky particle Cucker-Smale dynamics
(
2.5
)-(
2.6
) associated to the data
(x_{i}^{0},v_{i}^{0},m_{i})_{i=1}^{N}

with the masses such that
\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{N}}m_{i}=1
. Then, if we define

\psi_{i}^{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=v_{i}^{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{i}^{0}-x_{j}^{0}),\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

and

\underline{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\min_{1\leq j\leq N}\psi_{j}^{0},\qquad\bar{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\psi_{j}^{0},

it holds

(2.13)

\psi_{i}(t)\in[\underline{\psi},\bar{\psi}],\quad\forall i=1,\dots,N,\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Moreover,

(2.14)

\underline{\psi}-\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq v_{i}(t)\leq\bar{\psi},\quad\forall i=1,\dots,N,\quad t\geq 0.

Consequently, if
\{x_{i}^{0}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset[-R^{0},R^{0}]
, then

(2.15)

\{x_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset[-R(t),R(t)],\quad R(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=R^{0}+t\tilde{M},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

where

\tilde{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max\{\,|\underline{\psi}-\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}|,\,|\bar{\psi}|\,\}.

Proof.

We start with the proof of (
2.13
). By the conservation of
\psi_{i}

given by (
2.8
)

•

when
t
is a collision time we can conclude that

\psi_{i}(t+)\geq\min_{1\leq j\leq N}\psi_{j}(t-);

•

when
t
is not a collision time, by the fact that
\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{i}(t)=0
we can conclude that

\psi_{i}(t)\geq\underline{\psi},\quad\forall i\in\{1,\dots,N\},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Applying a similar argument we end up with

\psi_{i}(t)\leq\bar{\psi},\quad\forall i\in\{1,\dots,N\},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

thus, (
2.13
) is proved.

Now we prove (
2.14
).
From the definition of
\psi_{i}
in (
2.7
), we can write

v_{i}(t)=\psi_{i}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)),

and notice that

(2.16)

\psi_{i}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\leq\bar{\psi},

due to
\omega(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, thanks to (
2.2
) we can write

(2.17)

\psi_{i}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t))\geq\underline{\psi}-\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Using (
2.16
)-(
2.17
) it is possible to see that the absolute values of the velocities are always less or equal then

\tilde{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max\{\,|\underline{\psi}-\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}|,|\bar{\psi}|\}.

Defining
R(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=R^{0}+t\tilde{M}
, (
2.15
) holds true.
∎

The last property to prove about the sticky particle system is the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(
The barycentric lemma
)
.

Fix an
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and a time
t>0
. For any
k\in J_{i}(t)
the following holds

(2.18)

\frac{\sum_{j=i_{*}(t)}^{k}m_{j}\psi_{j}(t-)}{\sum_{j=i_{*}(t)}^{k}m_{j}}\geq\frac{\sum_{j\in J_{i}(t)}m_{j}\psi_{j}(t-)}{\sum_{j=\in J_{i}(t)}m_{j}}=\psi_{i}(t+)\geq\frac{\sum_{j=k}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{j}\psi_{j}(t-)}{\sum_{j=k}^{i^{*}(t)}m_{j}}.

Proof.

To prove the lemma it is sufficient to establish the following monotonicity property

(2.19)

\psi_{i_{*}(t)}(t-)\geq\dots\geq\psi_{i^{*}(t)}(t-).

Let us show how we obtain(
2.18
) from (
2.19
).

Without loss of generality we suppose to have some quantities
b_{i}
and
\alpha_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,M
such that

(2.20)

b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\dots\geq b_{M},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{M}\alpha_{i}=C,\quad C\in(0,1]\,.

We only prove the first inequality in (
2.18
), the proof is similar for the second one. We consider a
\bar{k}\in\{1,\dots,M\}
and the aim is to show that

\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}b_{j}}{\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}}\geq\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{M}\alpha_{j}b_{j}}{\sum_{j=1}^{M}\alpha_{j}}=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{M}\alpha_{j}b_{j}}{C},

that is equivalent to

C\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}b_{j}\geq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\alpha_{j}b_{j}\right)=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}b_{j}+\sum_{j=\bar{k}+1}^{M}\alpha_{j}b_{j}\right),

and this is true if and only if the following inequality holds true

\left(C-\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}b_{j}\right)\geq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{j=\bar{k}+1}^{M}\alpha_{j}b_{j}\right).

We know that
\sum_{j=1}^{M}\alpha_{j}=C,
then putting it in the last inequality we obtain the following

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}b_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{j=\bar{k}+1}^{M}\alpha_{j}\right)\geq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{k}}\alpha_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{j=\bar{k}+1}^{M}\alpha_{j}b_{j}\right).

Writing explicitly the sums, what we obtain is

\left(\alpha_{1}b_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{\bar{k}}b_{\bar{k}}\right)\left(\sum_{j=\bar{k}+1}^{M}\alpha_{j}\right)\geq\left(\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{\bar{k}}\right)\left(\sum_{j=\bar{k}+1}^{M}\alpha_{j}b_{j}\right).

From (
2.20
) we obtain that the inequality holds true.
Substituting the
b_{i}
’s and the
\alpha_{i}
’s respectively with the
\psi_{i}(t-)
’s and the
m_{i}
’s, we get (
2.18
) if (
2.19
) holds true.

It remains to prove (
2.19
), i.e. at a collision time
t
one has

\psi_{k}(t-)\geq\psi_{k+1}(t-)\qquad\forall\,k=i_{*}(t),\ldots,i^{*}(t)-1\,.

We know that for any
j\in J_{i}(t)
we have

\psi_{k}(t-)=v_{k}(t-)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\omega(x_{k}(t)-x_{j}(t)).

Then, it is possible to conclude that (
2.19
) follows from the obvious monotonicity property of the velocities, i.e.

v_{i_{*}(t)}(t-)\geq\dots\geq v_{i^{*}(t)}(t-),

that is true due to the fact that
t
is a collision time. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
The scalar balance law

In this section we introduce a scalar balance law connected to system (
1.5
).
Inspired by the work of Brenier and Grenier
[
5
]
and the work of Leslie and Tan
[
23
]
,
we aim to prove the well-posedness of a scalar balance law linked to (
1.5
),
and use the entropy condition of this scalar balance equation as selection principle for the solution of (
1.5
).
To do this, we define two new quantities
M
and
Q
, which are respectively the antiderivatives of
\rho
and
\rho\psi
, namely

(3.1)

M(x,t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\rho(y,t)\,dy,\qquad Q(x,t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{-\infty}^{x}(\rho\psi)(y,t)\,dy.

Integrating both equations in (
1.5
), it is simple to verify that the two quantities above satisfy
respectively the following equations

(3.2)

\partial_{t}M+u\partial_{x}M=0,\qquad\partial_{t}Q+u\partial_{x}Q=0.

We observe that
M
is a monotone function, being
\rho(x,t)
positive, then it is possible to define a map
A
,
depending only on the initial data
(M^{0},Q^{0})

A(M^{0})=Q^{0}
and if
(x,t)

(x_{0},0)
with velocity field

Q(x,t)=Q^{0}(x_{0})=A(M^{0}(x_{0}))=A(M(x,t)).

Using this last relation and the definition of

\displaystyle u\partial_{x}M

\displaystyle=(\psi-\omega\ast\rho)\rho=\psi\rho-(\omega\ast\rho)\rho

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}(A(M))-(\omega\ast\partial_{x}M)\partial_{x}M=\partial_{x}(A(M))-(\omega^{\prime}\ast M)\partial_{x}M

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}(A(M))-(\phi\ast M)\partial_{x}M.

We can substitute this last equation in the transport equation of
M
, see (
3.2
):
and we obtain our scalar balance law for the quantity
M
,

(3.3)

\partial_{t}M+\partial_{x}(A(M))=(\phi\ast M)\partial_{x}M.

In these steps we use the fact that

\phi\ast M=\omega\ast\rho,

and this relation holds true, indeed

\displaystyle\phi\ast M(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(y)M(x-y,t)\,dy

\displaystyle=\omega(y)M(x-y)|_{-\infty}^{\infty}+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega(y)\rho(x-y,t)\,dy=\omega\ast\rho(x,t).

Now, we want to define in which weak sense the scalar balance law must be satisfied.
It is possible to define a couple entropy/entropy flux
(\eta,q)
such that

\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,1]\to\mathbb{R},\qquad q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,1]\to\mathbb{R},\qquad q^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}A^{\prime},

with
\eta
Lipschitz and convex, the associated entropy inequality is

(3.4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\varphi(\eta(M))+\partial_{x}\varphi(q(M))\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(\phi\ast M)\,d\partial_{x}(\eta(M))\,dt\geq 0,

where
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty))
is a test function.
The first integral of the inequality can be interpreted in the sense of distribution, while in the second term
\partial_{x}(\eta(M))
is a Radon measure. Thanks to
[
6
, Th. 4.19 (Young)]
we know that the term
\phi\ast M
is in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
.

It is possible to notice that the integral

(3.5)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(\phi\ast M)\,d(\partial_{x}(\eta(M))\,dt

is well defined, indeed
\partial_{x}(\eta(M))
is a Radon measure and
\phi\ast M\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
,
but, by definition
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, and then the product
\varphi(\phi\ast M)
has compact support.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set. Let

, then there exists a unique
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued Radon measure
\mu
such that

\int_{\Omega}u\Div\tilde{\varphi}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\varphi}\,d\mu,\qquad\forall\tilde{\varphi}\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega).

Thus, this means that the distributional derivative of a
BV
function

is an
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-vector valued Radon measure.
Using this result we can consider (
3.5
) equals to

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(\phi\ast M)\partial_{x}(\eta(M))\,dx\,dt,

and so we can consider (
3.4
) in the sense of distributions, then we give the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

is an entropy weak solution of (

•

entropy/entropy-flux in the sense discussed above;

•

is a nondecreasing function for

•

such that

if

It is possible to extend Definition

to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
requiring that for any constant

is a weak entropy solution in

In particular, in the equation (

\eta(M)=|M-\alpha|,\quad q(M)=\text{sgn}(M-\alpha)(A(M)-A(\alpha)),

where

.

Now, we can state the Rankine-Hugoniot condition and the Oleinik entropy condition. Assume that
M
takes the values

M_{l}
and
M_{r}
on the two sides of a shock along a curve

the shock-speed.
The entropy condition becomes

(3.7)

\int_{C}([\eta(M)]n_{2}(s)+([q(M)]-\phi\ast M)[\eta(M)])n_{1}(s))\varphi\,ds\geq 0,

where
n=(n_{1},n_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}(1,-s)
and with the notation

.
Substituting

and

(s+(\phi\ast M))[\eta(M)]\leq[q(M)],

along the curve

, the inequality (

) becomes an equality,
then we have the following Rankine-Hugoniot condition

s+\phi\ast M=\frac{[A(M)]}{[M]}.

To get the Oleinik entropy condition, we consider the following.
If

, we take
\eta(m)=(m-\theta)H(m-\theta)
and

with

where the function

Then, we get

(3.10)

s+\phi\ast M\leq\frac{A(\theta)-A(M_{l})}{\theta-M_{l}}.

Concerning the Rankine-Hugoniot condition in

Let us fix a time

\displaystyle M_{N}(x_{i}(t)-,t)

\displaystyle=\theta_{i_{*}(t)-1},

\displaystyle M_{N}(x_{i}(t)+,t)

\displaystyle A_{N}(m)-A(m)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\theta_{i,N}-m}{m_{i,N}}(A(\theta_{i-1,N})-A(m))+\frac{m-\theta_{i-1,N}}{m_{i,N}}(A(\theta_{i,N})-A(m))

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{\theta_{i,N}-\theta_{i-1,N}}{m_{i,N}}|A(\theta_{i-1,N})-A(m)|+\frac{\theta_{i,N}-\theta_{i-1,N}}{m_{i,N}}|A(\theta_{i,N})-A(m)|

\displaystyle\qquad=|A(\theta_{i-1,N})-A(m)|+|A(\theta_{i,N}-A(m)|\leq|A|_{Lip}(|\theta_{i-1,N}-m|+|\theta_{i,N}-m|)

\displaystyle\qquad=|A|_{\text{Lip}}(\theta_{i,N}-\theta_{i-1,N})=|A|_{\text{Lip}}\,m_{i,N}\leq|A|_{\text{Lip}}\max_{1\leq j\leq N}m_{j,N}.

Let
T>0
be a fixed time, since
M_{N}(\cdot,t)
is uniformly bounded and nondecreasing for any
t\in[0,T],
we can apply the Helly’s Theorem stating that there exists a
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})-
convergent subsequence
M_{N_{k}}(\cdot,t)
. Applying a diagonal argument, it is possible to get another subsequence convergent in
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
for all rational
t\in[0,T]
, we denote again this subsequence as
M_{N_{k}}
to simplify the notation and we denote with
M(\cdot,t)
the limit of this subsequence. The aim is to extend this convergence from
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and also to extend the result to irrational times. From the sticky particle Cucker-Smale dynamics (
2.5
), we know
\{x_{i,N}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset[-R(T),R(T)]
with
R(T)=R^{0}+T\tilde{M}
. This means that

\displaystyle M_{N}(x,t)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad x\leq-R(T),

\displaystyle M_{N}(x,t)

\displaystyle=1,\qquad x\geq R(T),

thus
M_{N_{k}}(t)-M(t)\to 0
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for all rational times
t\in[0,T]
.

The extension of this result of convergence to irrational times is a consequence of the following time regualar estimate

(3.37)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|M_{N}(x,t)-M_{N}(x,s)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i,N}|x_{i,N}(t)-x_{i,N}(s)|\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|v_{i,N}^{0}|(t-s)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{M}(t-s).

Combining (
3.37
) with the established convergence at rational times, we get

(3.38)

M_{N_{k}}-M\to 0\quad\text{in}\quad C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R})).

Moreover, by the time regular estimate (
3.37
), it holds

||\partial_{t}M_{N_{k}}(x,t)||_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\tilde{M},\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],

then, we can extract a further subsequence, still denoted by
M_{N_{k}}
for simplicity, such that

\partial_{t}M_{N_{k}}(\cdot,t)\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\partial_{t}M(\cdot,t)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}),

and for this reason we can conclude that
M\in BV(\mathbb{R}\times[0,T])
.
Now we need to verify if the limit function
M
satisfies the entropy condition (
3.7
). We check this using the Kružkov entropy pairs
(\eta,q)
defined as in (
3.6
).
We know that

M_{N}(x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i,N}H(x-x_{i,N}(t))

is an entropy solution of the discretized scalar balance law (
3.11
), this means that it satisfies the entropy inequality with the couple entropy/entropy-flux
(\eta,q_{N})
, where
\eta(m)=|m-\alpha|
and
q_{N}(m)=\text{sgn}(m-\alpha)(A_{N}(m)-A_{N}(\alpha))
with
\alpha\in[0,1]
, namely

(3.39)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\eta(M_{N})\partial_{t}\varphi+q_{N}(M_{N})\partial_{x}\varphi+(\phi\ast M_{N})\varphi\partial_{x}(\eta(M_{N}))]\,dx\,dt\geq 0.

Now we consider the subsequence
M_{N_{k}}
and we pass to the limit, to simplify the notation we use
M_{N}
in place of
M_{N_{k}}
. For a fixed
t\in[0,T]
we obtain

(3.40)

\displaystyle||\eta(M_{N})-\eta(M)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\eta(M_{N})-\eta(M)|\,dx\leq|\eta|_{Lip}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|M_{N}-M|\,dx

\displaystyle=|\eta|_{Lip}||M_{N}-M||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\to 0,

thanks to the convergence result in (
3.38
). Moreover,

(3.41)

\displaystyle||q_{N}(M_{N})-q(M)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq||q_{N}(M_{N})-q(M_{N})||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+||q(M_{N})-q(M)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq 2R(T)||A_{N}-A||_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}+|A|_{Lip}||M_{N}-M||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\to 0.

Thanks to (
3.40
) and (
3.41
), the first two terms in (
3.39
) converge to

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\eta(M)\partial_{t}\varphi+q(M)\partial_{x}\varphi]\,dx\,.

About the last term, we start by showing that
\phi\ast M_{N}
converges to
\phi\ast M
as
N\to+\infty.
Indeed, since
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, then

||\phi\ast M_{N}-\phi\ast M||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq||\phi||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}||M_{N}-M||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\to 0.

Let us consider the term
\partial_{x}\eta(M_{N})
, thanks to
[
23
, Lemma 5.2]
we know that

||\partial_{x}\eta(M_{N})||_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}\leq|\eta|_{Lip}

and this is also true for
\partial_{x}(\eta(M))
. Now, we are able to prove the convergence of the last term in the entropy inequality (
3.39
); indeed

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi\ast M_{N})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\varphi\partial_{x}(\eta(M_{N}))\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi\ast M)\varphi\partial_{x}(\eta(M))\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}||\varphi(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}||\phi\ast M_{N}-\phi\ast M||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}||\partial_{x}(\eta(M_{N})||_{\mathcal{M}}\,dt

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi\ast M)\varphi[\partial_{x}(\eta(M_{N}))-\partial_{x}(\eta(M))]\,dx\,dt\right|.

Since
||\phi\ast M_{N}-\phi\ast M||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\to 0
,
then the first term on the right-hand side above converges to zero, while for the second term we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi\ast M)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\varphi[\partial_{x}(\eta(M_{N}))-\partial_{x}(\eta(M))]\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\partial_{x}[(\phi\ast M)\varphi][(\eta(M_{N}))-(\eta(M))]\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert-\partial_{x}[(\phi\ast M)\varphi][(\eta(M_{N}))-(\eta(M))]\right\rvert}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\partial_{x}[(\phi\ast M)\varphi]\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{T}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\eta(M_{N})-\eta(M)\right\rVert}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,dt\,.

Since
\eta
is Lipschitz continuous and by means of (
3.38
), the right-hand side above converges to zero and thus,
M
is an entropy solution of (
3.28
).

The uniqueness of solutions is a direct consequence of the stability estimate (
3.31
) setting
\tilde{M}^{0}=M^{0}
and
\tilde{A}=A
. The argument of the proof of (

) is based on the Kružkov’s doubling of the variables strategy, see
[
21
]
. For fixed
(y,s)
, consider the Kružkov entropy pair (

) with

and a test function

.
In the following the integration bounds are omitted to simplify the notation. The spatial variables

and

are integrated over
\mathbb{R}
and the time variables

and

over the set
[0,T]
.
Thus, the entropy inequality (
3.4

\displaystyle\iint

\displaystyle|M(x,t)-\tilde{M}(y,s)|\partial_{t}w(x,t,y,s)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\iint\text{sgn}(M(x,t)-\tilde{M}(y,s))(A(M(x,t)-A(\tilde{M}(y,s)))\partial_{x}w(x,t,y,s)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\iint(\phi\ast M)(x,t)(\partial_{x}|M(x,t)-\tilde{M}(y,s)|\,dx\,dt.

We perform the same for the entropy inequality of
\tilde{M}
, but with
\tilde{A}
in place of
A
and with
\alpha=\tilde{M}(y,s)
. We integrate both the inequalities above over the remaining free variables in both cases and add them, then we get

(3.42)

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\iiiint|M(x,t)-\tilde{M}(y,s)|(\partial_{t}w+\partial_{s}w)(x,t,y,s)\,dx\,dt\,dy\,ds

\displaystyle+\iiiint\text{sgn}(M(x,t)-\tilde{M}(y,s))[(A(M(x,t))-A(\tilde{M}(y,s)))\partial_{x}w(x,t,y,s)

\displaystyle+(\tilde{A}(M(x,t))-\tilde{A}(\tilde{M}(y,s)))\partial_{y}w(x,t,y,s)]\,dx\,dt\,dy\,ds

\displaystyle+\iiiint w(x,t,y,s)[(\phi\ast M)(x,t)\partial_{x}|M(x,t)-\tilde{M}(y,s)|

\displaystyle+(\phi\ast\tilde{M})(y,s)\partial_{y}|M(x,t)-\tilde{M}(y,s)|]\,dx\,dt\,dy\,ds.

We introduce the auxiliary variables

\bar{x}=\frac{x+y}{2},\quad\bar{y}=\frac{x-y}{2},\quad\bar{t}=\frac{s+t}{2},\quad\bar{s}=\frac{t-s}{2},

and we choose test function

w(x,t,y,s)=b_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{x-y}{2}\right)b_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{t-s}{2}\right)g\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)h_{\delta}\left(\frac{t+s}{2}\right)=b_{\epsilon}(\bar{y})b_{\epsilon}(\bar{s})g(\bar{x})h_{\delta}(\bar{t}).

These test functions satisfy the following properties.

•

are smooth and nonnegative.

•

The functions
(b_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon>0}
approximate the Dirac delta distribution as
\epsilon\to 0+
, we consider
b_{\epsilon}
as a standard mollifier supported in
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
and such that the integral over
\mathbb{R}
is equal to 1.

•

The function
g
is identically 1 on
[-R(T),R(T)]
and it is compactly supported.

•

The functions
(h_{\delta})_{\delta>0}
approximate the indicator function of
[s,t]

. We consider

to be identically 1 on
[s,t]

and linear on
[s-\delta,s]
and
[t,t+\delta]

To proceed, we substitute the test function in (
3.42
) using also the auxiliary variables and notice that

\partial_{t}+\partial_{s}=\partial_{\bar{t}},\quad\partial_{x}+\partial_{y}=\partial_{\bar{x}},\quad\partial_{x}-\partial_{y}=\partial_{\bar{y}}.

We define

A_{\pm}(m)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{A(m)\pm\tilde{A}(m)}{2},

and using the new notation, we rewrite the part into the brackets of the second term in the r.h.s. of (

(A_{+}(M)-A_{+}(\tilde{M}))\partial_{\bar{x}}w+(A_{-}(M)-A_{-}(\tilde{M}))\partial_{\bar{y}}w.

The part into the brackets in the last term of (

\frac{\phi\ast M+\phi\ast\tilde{M}}{2}\partial_{\bar{x}}|M-\tilde{M}|+\frac{\phi\ast M-\phi\ast\tilde{M}}{2}\partial_{\bar{y}}|M-\tilde{M}|.

The result of this substitutions is

(3.43)

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\iiiint\left[|M-\tilde{M}|\partial_{\bar{t}}w+\text{sgn}(M-\tilde{M})(A_{+}(M)-A_{+}(\tilde{M}))\partial_{\bar{x}}w\right]\,d\bar{x}\,d\bar{t}\,d\bar{y}\,d\bar{s}

\displaystyle+\iiiint\text{sgn}(M-\tilde{M})(A_{-}(M)-A_{-}(\tilde{M}))\partial_{\bar{y}}\,d\bar{x}\,d\bar{t}\,d\bar{y}\,d\bar{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\iiiint(\phi\ast M+\phi\ast\tilde{M})w\partial_{\bar{x}}|M-\tilde{M}|\,d\bar{x}\,d\bar{t}\,d\bar{y}\,d\bar{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\iiiint(\phi\ast M-\phi\ast\tilde{M})w\partial_{\bar{y}}|M-\tilde{M}|\,d\bar{x}\,d\bar{t}\,d\bar{y}\,d\bar{s}.

We have to pass to the limit
\epsilon\to 0
, for this reason we need to treat the
\bar{y}
derivatives. Thanks to
[
23
, Lemma 5.4]
we have that

\gamma(M,\tilde{M})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\text{sgn}(M-\tilde{M})(A_{-}(M)-A_{-}(\tilde{M}))

is Lipschitz in both variables and both the Lipschitz constants are less or equal than
|A_{-}|_{\text{Lip}}
.

Using
[
23
, Lemma 5.2 & Lemma 5.4]
, we obtain

(3.44)

\left|\frac{d}{d\bar{y}}\gamma(M,\tilde{M})\right|\leq|A_{-}|_{\text{Lip}}\left|\partial_{1}M(\bar{x}+\bar{y},\bar{t}+\bar{s})\right|+|A_{-}|_{\text{Lip}}\left|\partial_{1}M(\bar{x}-\bar{y},\bar{t}-\bar{s})\right|,

where
\partial_{1}
denotes differentiation with respect to the spatial variable.
Furthermore, let us notice that the following estimate also holds true

(3.45)

\left|\frac{d}{d\bar{y}}\left|M(\bar{x}+\bar{y},\bar{t}+\bar{s})-\tilde{M}(\bar{x}-\bar{y},\bar{t}-\bar{s})\right|\right|\leq\left|\partial_{1}M(\bar{x}+\bar{y},\bar{t}+\bar{s})+\partial_{1}\tilde{M}(\bar{x}-\bar{y},\bar{t}-\bar{s})\right|.

Using (
3.44
)-(
3.45
) and letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we end up with

\displaystyle\iint\left[|M(x,t)-\tilde{M}(x,t)g(x)h^{\prime}_{\delta}(t)+\text{sgn}(M-\tilde{M})(A_{+}(M)-A_{+}(\tilde{M}))g^{\prime}(x)h_{\delta}(t)\right]\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\iint|A_{-}|_{\text{Lip}}(|\partial_{x}M|+|\partial_{x}\tilde{M}|)g(x)h_{\delta}(t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\iint\left[\phi\ast(M+\tilde{M})\partial_{x}|M-\tilde{M}|+|\phi\ast(M-\tilde{M})||\partial_{x}M+\partial_{x}\tilde{M}|\right]g(x)h_{\delta}(t)\,dx\,dt\geq 0

where
(x,t)
is in place of
(\bar{x},\bar{t})
to simplify the notation. Thanks to the choice of the functions

and

satisfying the properties above, and taking

, we get

(3.46)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|M(x,t)

\displaystyle-\tilde{M}(x,t)|\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|M(x,s)-\tilde{M}(x,s)|\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}|A-\tilde{A}|_{\text{Lip}}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}M|+|\partial_{x}\tilde{M}|\,dx\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\phi\ast(M+\tilde{M})\cdot\partial_{x}|M-\tilde{M}|\right.

\displaystyle\left.+|\phi\ast(M-\tilde{M})|\cdot\partial_{x}(M+\tilde{M})\right]\,dx\,d\tau.

We know that
M
and
\tilde{M}
are nondecreasing, thus we can substitute the terms
|\partial_{x}M|
,
|\partial_{x}\tilde{M}|
and
|\partial_{x}M+\partial_{x}\tilde{M}|
with
\partial_{x}M
,
\partial_{x}\tilde{M}
and
\partial_{x}M+\partial_{x}\tilde{M}
respectively. We write

\frac{1}{2}|A-\tilde{A}|_{\text{Lip}}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}M|+|\partial_{x}\tilde{M}|\,dx\,d\tau=(t-s)|A-\tilde{A}|_{Lip},

while we split the last integral of (

) into two terms to treat it.
The first term is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\ast(M+\tilde{M})\partial_{x}|M-\tilde{M}|\,dx\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\ast(M+\tilde{M})\partial_{x}|M-\tilde{M}|\,dx\,d\tau\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left|-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\ast(\partial_{x}M+\partial_{x}\tilde{M})|M-\tilde{M}|\,dx\,d\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{s}^{t}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert M(\cdot,\tau)-\tilde{M}(\cdot,\tau)\right\rVert}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,d\tau,

where we use

\displaystyle\left|\phi\ast(\partial_{x}M+\partial_{x}\tilde{M})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x-y)(\partial_{y}M(y,\tau)+\partial_{y}\tilde{M}(y,\tau)\,dy\right|

Using a similar argument, it is possible to prove that

\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi\ast(M-\tilde{M})|(\partial_{x}M+\partial_{x}\tilde{M})\,dx\,d\tau\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{s}^{t}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert M(\cdot,\tau)-\tilde{M}(\cdot,\tau)\right\rVert}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,d\tau.

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert M(\cdot,t)-\tilde{M}(\cdot,t)\right\rVert}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

As before,

\frac{1}{2}|A-\tilde{A}|_{\text{Lip}}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}M|+|\partial_{x}\tilde{M}|\,dx\,d\tau=|A-\tilde{A}|_{\text{Lip}}(t-s).

Remark 3.5
.

Combining the two bounds (

\displaystyle||M(\cdot,t)-\tilde{M}(\cdot,t)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

4.
Approximation of the nonlocal system

In this section, we come back to system (
1.5
) and study it using the weak entropy solution
M
obtained in Section
3
.

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})=\rho(\phi\ast(\rho u))-\rho u(\phi\ast\rho),\end{cases}

with initial data

(4.2)

\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x),\qquad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x).

Let
\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
be the space of probability measures with compact support, we use the Wasserstein-1 metric to quantify the distance between elements of
\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathbb{R}),
therefore we write the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\rho,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
. The Wasserstein-1 distance between them is

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\rho,\mu)=\sup_{\text{Lip}(f)\leq 1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,d\rho(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,d\mu(x)\right|.

Remark 4.2
.

If
M
and
\tilde{M}
are the cumulative distribution functions of
\rho
and
\mu
respectively, then
\mathcal{W}_{1}(\rho,\mu)=||M-\tilde{M}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}
.

In our setting the
\mathcal{W}_{1}
convergence is equivalent to the weak
-*
convergence in the sense of measures.
In the following, we consider
\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
equipped with the Wasserstein-1 metric.
In the next definition we state what we mean for weak solution of (
4.1
).

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\rho^{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and
u^{0}\in L^{\infty}(d\rho^{0})
. Define
P^{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\rho^{0}u^{0}
, which is a signed measure. We say that
(\rho,P)=(\rho,\rho u)
is a weak solution of the ARZ system (
4.1
) with initial data (
4.2
) if for any
T>0
,

•

\rho\in C([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathbb{R}))
.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho=\partial_{t}(\partial_{x}M)=\partial_{x}(\partial_{t}M)=\partial_{x}(-P)=-\partial_{x}(\rho u),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho\psi)=\partial^{2}_{tx}(A(M))=\partial_{x}(\psi\partial_{t}M)=-\partial_{x}(\psi P)=-\partial_{x}(\rho u\psi).

The first equation of (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)

\displaystyle+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})=\partial_{t}(\rho(\psi-\omega\ast\rho))+\partial_{x}(\rho u(\psi-\omega\ast\rho))

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\partial_{t}(\rho\psi)+\partial_{x}(\rho u\psi)}_{=0}-\partial_{t}(\rho(\omega\ast\rho))-\partial_{x}(\rho u(\omega\ast\rho))

\displaystyle=-\underbrace{(\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u))(\omega\ast\rho)}_{=0}-\rho\partial_{t}(\omega\ast\rho)-\rho u\partial_{x}(\omega\ast\rho)

\displaystyle=-\rho(\omega\ast\partial_{t}\rho)-\rho u(\omega\ast\partial_{x}\rho)

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\prime}(x)A(M(x,t))\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\prime}(x)A(M^{0}(x))\,dx\right\rvert}\leq|A|_{Lip}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert M(\cdot,t)-M^{0}\right\rVert}_{L^{1}}\to 0.

thanks to the convergences of

Appendix A
Convolution of measures and functions

In this section we recall the theory about the convolution product between a function and a measure,
in particular we want to see when this kind of operation is well defined.
We refer to
[
2
]
and to
[
4
]
for this section. We start with some definitions.

Definition A.1
.

Let
X
be a locally compact and separable metric space,
\mathcal{B}(X)
its Borel
\sigma
-algebra,
and consider the measure space
(X,\mathcal{B}(X))
. A positive measure on
(X,\mathcal{B}(X))
is called
a Borel measure. If a Borel measure is finite on the compact sets, it is called a positive Radon measure.

Definition A.2
.

Let
X,Y
be metric spaces, and let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\to Y
. We say that
f
is a Borel function if

f^{-1}(A)\in\mathcal{B}(X)
for any
A\subset Y
open set.

Now we can give the proposition which defines the convolution product between measures and functions.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
f
be a Borel function in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
\mu
be a bounded Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The function

f\ast\mu(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-y)\,d\mu(y),

is defined for almost all
x
with respect to Lebesgue measure and

||f\ast\mu||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq||f||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}||\mu||.

We want to see if it is possible to apply this theory to our case, in particular we want to verify
if the convolution product between the function
\omega
and the probability measure
\rho
is well defined.
We start with the case of the measure
\rho_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}\delta(x-x_{i})
with
\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}=1
.
The
\rho_{N}
is a sum of Dirac delta, thus we can verify if the product is well defined using only a Dirac.
We need to verify if the hypotheses of the function and of the measure are satisfied. The delta is a Radon measure,
in particular is a probability measure, and so it is a bounded Borel measure, thus we have to verify only if

\omega
is a Borel function with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the Euclidean topology and
this is true, as
\omega
is continuous.

The following convolution product is defined for almost
x
with respect to Lebesgue measure and

\omega\ast\delta(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega(x-y)\,d\delta(y).

The next step is to verify if the following equality holds true

(A.1)

\omega\ast\rho_{N}(x,t)=\phi\ast M_{N}(x,t).

We consider a single Dirac delta as before, if the equality before holds true for a single delta
we can extend the result to the sum of delta’s. Thus we want to see if, with
\rho_{N}=\delta
and
M_{N}=H
,
where
H
is the right continuous Heaviside function, the following equality is verified

(A.2)

\omega(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega(x-y)\,d\rho(y)=\omega\ast\delta(x)=\phi\ast H(x).

We want to evaluate the following integral

\displaystyle\phi\ast H(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x-y)H(y)\,dy=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle=-\omega(x-y)|_{0}^{\infty}=\omega(x).

We can conclude that (
A.2
) holds true and then (
A.1
) holds true too.

Appendix B
Chain Rule for functions of Bounded Variation

The results in this section are taken from
[
13
]
.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
.
Let us recall that
x\in\Omega
is a Lebesgue point of
u
if there exists a real number
\bar{u}(x)
such that

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}(x)|}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}|u(y)-\bar{u}(x)|\,dy=0,

with
B_{\rho}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}|y-x|<\rho\}
and
|B_{\rho}(x)|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
B_{\rho}(x)
.
The set of all Lebesgue points is denoted by
\Omega_{u}
. A point
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{u}
is defined
a
jump point
of
u
if there exist two real numbers
u_{-}(x)
and
u_{+}(x)
and a unit vector
v_{u}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that
u_{+}(x)\neq u_{-}(x)
and

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{1}{|B^{\pm}_{\rho}(x)|}\int_{B^{\pm}_{\rho}(x)}|u(y)-u_{\pm}(x)|\,dy=0,

where
B^{\pm}_{\rho}(x)=B_{\rho}(x)\cap\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(y-x,\pm v_{u}(x))>0\}.
The set of all jump points of
u
is denoted by
S_{u}
. Let
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally bounded Borel function, the averaged superposition
\hat{g}(u)
of
g
and
u
is defined by

(B.3)

\hat{g}(u)(x)=\begin{cases}g(u(x))\qquad x\in\Omega_{u},\\
\int_{0}^{1}g(u_{-}(x)+s(u_{+}(x)-u_{-}(x)))\,ds\qquad x\in S_{u}.\end{cases}

Theorem B.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a globally Lipschitz continuous function. Consider
u\in BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
and set
v=f\circ u
. The the function
v
belongs to
BV(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
and we have the chain rule

Dv=\hat{f^{*}}(u)Du,\quad\text{as a Borel measures on $\Omega$},

where
f^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes an arbitrary bounded Borel function such that

f^{*}(t)=f^{\prime}(t),\quad\text{almost everywhere for the Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$}.

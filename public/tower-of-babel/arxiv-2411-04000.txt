The Bohr radius for operator valued functions on simply connected domain
Sabir Ahammed, Molla Basir Ahamed

Abstract
. In this paper, we first establish an improved Bohr inequality for the class of operator-valued holomorphic functions f f on a simply connected domain Ω \Omega in ℂ \mathbb{C} . Next, we establish a generalization of refined version of the Bohr inequality and the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality with the help of the sequence φ = { φ n ​ ( r ) } n = 0 ∞ \varphi=\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}^{\infty}_{n=0} of non-negative continuous functions in [ 0 , 1 ) [0,1) such that the series ∑ n = 0 ∞ φ n ​ ( r ) \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r) converges locally uniformly on the interval [ 0 , 1 ) [0,1) . All the results are proved to be sharp. Moreover, We establish the Bohr inequality and the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality for the class of operator-valued ν \nu -Bloch functions defined in two different simply connected domains, Ω \Omega and Ω γ \Omega_{\gamma} , in ℂ \mathbb{C} .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}(a;r):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a|<r\}
, and let
\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}(0;1)
be the open unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. Also, let
\mathbb{N}_{0}
be the set of non-negative integers. For a given simply connected domain
\Omega
containing the unit disk
\mathbb{D},
we denote
\mathcal{H}\left(\Omega\right)
a class of analytic functions on
\Omega
, and
\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega\right)
be the class of functions
f\in\mathcal{H}\left(\Omega\right)
such that
f\left(\Omega\right)\subseteq\mathbb{D}.
The Bohr radius for the family
\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega\right)
is defined to be the positive real number
B_{\Omega}\in(0,1)
given by (see
[
29
]
)

\displaystyle B_{\Omega}=\sup\{r\in(0,1):M_{f}(r)\leq 1\;\mbox{for all}\;f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega\right),z\in\mathbb{D}\},

where
M_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}
is the associated majorant series for
f\in\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega\right)
. If
\Omega=\mathbb{D},
then it is well-known that
B_{\mathbb{D}}=1/3,
and it is described in
[
20
]
precisely as follows.

Theorem A
.

[
20
]

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
, then

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\,\,\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq\dfrac{1}{3}.

The number
1/3
is best possible.

The inequality
(
1.1
)
is known as the classical Bohr inequality, while
1/3
is known as the Bohr radius. Bohr’s theorem gained popularity when Dixon
[
25
]
used it to disprove a conjecture that stated if the non-unital von Neumann inequality holds for a Banach algebra, then it must be an operator algebra. In 2000, Djkaov and Ramanujan
[
26
]
conducted an extensive study on the best possible constant
r_{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
, where
r_{p}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p}(r_{p})^{np}\right)^{1/p}\leq||f||_{\infty},

where
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}.
For
p=1,

r_{p}
coincides with the classical Bohr radius
1/3.
Using Haussdorf-Young’s inequality, it is easy to see that
r_{p}=1
for
p\in[2,\infty)
. However, computing the precise value of
r_{p}
for
1<p<2
is generally difficult. Therefore, there is a need to estimate the value of
r_{p}
. In
[
26
]
, the following best-known estimate

\displaystyle\left(1+\left(\dfrac{2}{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2-p}}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\leq r_{p}\leq\inf_{0\leq a<1}\left(\dfrac{(1-a^{p})^{1/p}}{((1-a^{2})^{p}+a^{p}(1-a^{p}))^{1/p}}\right)

has been obtained.

Paulsen
et al.

[
42
]
have considered another modification of
(
1.1
)
for
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
and have shown that

(1.2)

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq\dfrac{1}{2},

and the constant
1/2
is sharp. Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
32
]
have examined a different variation of
(
1.1
)
for the function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
. They have shown that

(1.3)

\displaystyle|a_{0}|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|r^{n}+\dfrac{1}{2}|a_{n}|^{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{n}\leq 1

and the constants
1/2
and
1/3
cannot be improved.

In
[
45
]
, Ponnusamy
et al.
explored a modified version of
(
1.1
)
known as a refinement of the classical Bohr inequality. They present their findings that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3}

and the constant
1/3
is sharp.

In addition to the Bohr inequality, there is another concept called the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality. The Rogosinski radius, which is compared to the Bohr radius, was first introduced in
[
47
]
for functions
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
. However, the Rogosinski radius has not been studied as extensively as the Bohr radius. The following paragraph presents the definition of the Rogosinski radius.

Theorem B
.

(Rogosinski Theorem)

If
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D}),
then for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}b_{n}z^{n}\bigg|\leq 1\;\mbox{for}\;|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{2}.

The constant
1/2
is best possible and is called Rogosinski radius.

In
[
33
]
,
the Bohr-Rogosinski sum
R^{f}_{N}(z)
for the functions
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D}),
is defined by

(1.5)

\displaystyle R^{f}_{N}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n},\;|z|=r.

An interesting observation is that for
N=1
, the quantity in (
1.5
) is linked to the classical Bohr sum, where
|f(0)|
is replaced by
|f(z)|
. This quantity, denoted as
R^{f}_{N}(z)
, satisfies the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality
R^{f}_{N}(z)\leq 1
. The Bohr radius, denoted as
B
, and the Bohr-Rogosinski radius, denoted as

, satisfy the inequality
B=1/3<1/2=R
. For recent developments on the Bohr-Rogosinski phenomenon, readers are referred to the articles
[
41
,
7
,
6
,
13
]
, and references therein. For other aspect of Bohr phenomenon, we refer tot he articles

2.
The Bohr inequality on a simply connected domain

It is worth noting that the concept of Bohr’s radius, which was originally defined for analytic functions from the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
into
\mathbb{D}
, has been extended by authors to include mappings from
\mathbb{D}
into other domains
\Omega
in
\mathbb{D}
(see e.g.,
[
1
,
5
]
). For our study, we consider
\Omega
a simply connected domain in
\mathbb{C}
which contains the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
and bounded holomorphic functions from
\Omega
into a complex Banach space
X
.

Let
H^{\infty}\left(\Omega,X\right)
be the space of bounded holomorphic functions from
\Omega
into a complex Banach space
X
and

||f||_{H^{\infty}\left(\Omega,X\right)}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}||f(z)||.
For
p\in[1,\infty),

H^{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
denotes the space of analytic functions from
\mathbb{D}
into
X
with the norm

\displaystyle||f||_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D},X)}=\sup_{0<r<1}\left(\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||f(re^{it})||^{p}dt\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

In this paper,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the spaces of bounded linear operators on complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.
For any
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),

||A||
denotes the operator norm of
A.
Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).
Then the adjoint operator
A^{*}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
of
A
defined by
(Ax,y)=(x,A^{*}y)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}.
The operator
A
is said to be normal if
A^{*}A=AA^{*},
self-adjoint if
A^{*}=A,
and positive if
(Ax,x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}.
The absolute value of
A
is defined by
|A|:=(A^{*}A)^{1/2},
where
T^{1/2}
denotes the unique positive square root of a positive operator
T.
Let
I
be the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}.

We denote (see
[
17
]
) the constant
\lambda_{H}
by

(2.1)

\lambda_{H}:=\lambda_{H}(\Omega)=\sup_{f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right),\;||f(z)||\leq 1}\bigg\{\frac{||A_{n}||}{||I-|A_{0}|^{2}||}:A_{0}\neq f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n},\;\;\;z\in\mathbb{D}\bigg\}.

Let
f
be holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
, and for
0<r<1
, let
\mathbb{D}_{r}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}
, and
S_{r}:=S_{r}(f)
denotes the planar integral

\displaystyle S_{r}:=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z).

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D}),
then

\displaystyle S_{r}:=\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}.

If
f
is a univalent function, then
S_{r}
represents the area of
f(\mathbb{D}_{r})
. However, in the case of a multivalent function,
S_{r}
is greater than the area of the image of the subdisk
\mathbb{D}_{r}
. This fact could be shown by noting that

\displaystyle S_{r}=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|f^{\prime}(w)|^{2}dA(w)=\int_{f(\mathbb{D}_{r})}\nu_{f}(w)dA(w)\geq\int_{f(\mathbb{D}_{r})}dA(w)=Area(f(\mathbb{D}_{r})),

where
\nu_{f}(w)=\sum_{f(z)=w}1
, denotes the counting function of
f
.

In the study of the improved Bohr inequality, the quantity
S_{r}
plays a significant role. There have been many results on the improved Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
(see e.g.,
[
31
,
32
]
), which includes harmonic mappings on the unit disk (
[
27
]
). Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
32
]
have considered another modification of
(
1.1
)
and
(
1.2
)
for the class
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
and have shown that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq\dfrac{1}{3},

and

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{9}{8}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq\dfrac{1}{2}.

It is not simply about using any combination of
S_{r}/\pi
and its powers for the study. The key is to always remember that the inequality must be satisfied with the given radius, and not only that, but those radii must remain unchanged. Additionally, it is crucial to demonstrate that these radii are best possible and that the associated coefficients cannot be further improved. Therefore, the study presents a challenge as we must find the appropriate combination on the left-hand side of the inequality. In this regard, Ismagilov
et al.

[
31
]
remarked that for any function
F:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
F(t)>0
for
t>0
, there exists an analytic function
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
for which the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)+\lambda\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)^{2}+F(S_{r})\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq\frac{1}{3}

is false, where
\lambda
is given in
[
31
, Theorem 1]
.

It is important to note that combining a non-negative quantity with the Bohr inequality does not always result in the desired inequality for the class
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
.
However, we would like to point out that despite the extensive exploration of Bohr-type inequalities, the study of these inequalities for the class of functions
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
given by
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
has not received as much attention from researchers. This lack of attention serves as the primary motivation for this section. Our main goal is to fill this specific gap in the existing literature and contribute to understanding Bohr-type inequalities.

To serve our purpose, we consider a polynomial
P(w)
of degree
m
as follows:

(2.2)

P(w)=k_{1}w+k_{2}w^{2}+\cdots+k_{m}w^{m},\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;k_{j}:=\left(\frac{1+\lambda_{H}}{1+2\lambda_{H}}\right)^{2j}

for
j=1,2,\cdots,m,
and show that the following sharp result can be obtained.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a simply connected domain containing the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
and
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
be given by
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda_{H}}^{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}||A_{n}||r^{n}+P\left(S_{r}\right)\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq\frac{1}{1+2\lambda_{H}},

where
S_{r}
denotes the area of the image of the disk
\mathbb{D}(0,r)
under the mapping
f
and
P(w)
is a polynomial given by (
2.2
). The equality
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda_{H}}^{f}(r)=1
holds for the function
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
if, and only if,
f
is given by
f(z)=cI
with
|c|=1.

Remark 2.1
.

It is important to note that the study of the improved Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{B}
has certain limitations, as pointed out by Ismagilov
et al.
in
[
31
]
. However, in Theorem
2.1
, these limitations have been overcome for the class
{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
.

One concern addressed in this article is the exploration of several related questions about the Bohr inequality. Specifically, the aim is to investigate an inequality that holds for the class
\mathcal{B}
and examine if it can also be established for other classes.

Therefore, a natural question arises.

Question 1
.

Is it possible to establish a sharp analog of the inequalities (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) for the class
{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
?

In our response to Question
1
, we establish an analogue of the inequalities (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) for the class of functions
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
. Consequently, we obtain Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
below, respectively. Moreover, we show that the equality holds in each result for
f
is given by
f(z)=cI
with
|c|=1.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a simply connected domain containing the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
and
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
be given by
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then for
0\leq\beta\leq 1/{4\lambda_{H}}
,

\mathcal{B}_{\lambda_{H}}^{f}(\beta,r):=||A_{0}||+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(||A_{n}||+\beta||A_{n}||^{2}\bigg)r^{n}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq\frac{1}{1+2\lambda_{H}}.

The equality
\mathcal{B}_{\lambda_{H}}^{f}(\beta,r)=1
holds for the function
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
if, and only if,
f
is given by
f(z)=cI
with
|c|=1.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a simply connected domain containing the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
and
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
be given by
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{H}}^{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}||A_{n}||r^{n}+\left(\frac{1+\lambda_{H}}{2\lambda_{H}(1+||A_{0}||)}+\frac{2(1+\lambda_{H})r}{3(1-r)}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}||A_{n}||^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1

for

. The equality
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{H}}^{f}(r)=1
holds for the function
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
if, and only if,

is given by
f(z)=cI
with

2.1.
The Bohr inequality involving a sequence
\varphi=\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
of non-negative continuous function in
[0,1)

For further discussions, we need to introduce some basic notations. Following a recent study on the Bohr inequality (see
[
34
]
), we will use the notation
\mathcal{F}
to represent the set of all sequences
\varphi=\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
of non-negative continuous functions on the interval
[0,1)
such that the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)
converges locally uniformly on that interval. The Bohr inequalities have been studied by numerous authors through the substitution of
r^{n}
with
\varphi_{n}(r)\in\mathcal{F}
(see e.g.,
[
45
,
34
,
23
]
). For the sake of convenience, we will use the notation

\displaystyle\Phi_{N}(r):=\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\mathcal{A}\left(f_{m},\varphi,r\right):=\sum_{n=m+1}^{\infty}||A_{n}||^{2n}\left(\dfrac{\varphi_{2n}(r)}{1+||A_{m}||}+\Phi_{2n+1}(r)\right),

where
f_{m}(z):=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}.
In particular, when
\varphi_{n}(r)=r^{n},
the formula for
\mathcal{A}\left(f_{0},\varphi,r\right)
takes the following simple form

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\left(f_{0},r\right):=\left(\dfrac{1}{1+||A_{0}||}+\dfrac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}||A_{n}||^{2n}r^{2n},

where
f_{0}(z)=f(z)-f(0),
which measure helps to transform the traditional Bohr inequality into a more improved version.

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, we consider the simply connected domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
defined by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\gamma}:=\biggl\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\bigg|z+\dfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\bigg|<\dfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\biggr\}.

It is clear that
\Omega_{\gamma}
contains the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
,
i.e.,

\mathbb{D}\subseteq\Omega_{\gamma}
for
0\leq\gamma<1
.

Fournier and Ruscheweyh (see
[
29
]
) studied the classical Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
and showed that it holds for the radius
\rho_{\gamma}=(1+\gamma)/(3+\gamma)
, resulting in particular
\rho_{0}=1/3
, which is the classical Bohr radius. In
[
28
]
, the authors have studied improved Bohr inequalities for simply connected domain. Recently, in
[
16
]
, authors have studied the Bohr inequality for the class
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{\gamma},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
and obtain the following result.

Theorem C
.

(
[
16
]
)

For
0\leq\gamma<1,
let
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{\gamma},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
be given by
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
with
||f(z)||_{{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{\gamma},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)}\leq 1.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}||A_{n}||r^{n}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq\rho_{\gamma}:=\frac{1+\gamma}{3+\gamma}.

The constant
\rho_{\gamma}
is best possible.

The Bohr inequality has been extensively studied for Banach spaces on the domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
. In particular, a sequence
\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}
has been used for this purpose (see e.g.,
[
17
,
16
]
). But we observe some limitation of the results in
[
17
,
16
]
. In this paper, we will delve further into the exploration of the Bohr inequality. In order to further investigate on the Bohr inequality, we will now recall a definition.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
17
]
)

Let
f\in{H}^{\infty}\left(\Omega,X\right)
be given by
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
with
||f(z)||_{H^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)}\leq 1.
For
\varphi=\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\varphi_{0}(r)\leq 1,

1\leq p,q<\infty,
we denote

\displaystyle R_{p,q,\varphi}(f,\Omega,X)=\sup\biggl\{r\geq 0:||A_{0}||^{p}\varphi_{0}(r)+\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}||A_{n}||\varphi_{n}(r)\right)^{q}\leq\varphi_{0}(r)\biggr\}.

We define the Bohr radius associated with
\varphi
by

(2.3)

\displaystyle R_{p,q,\varphi}\left(\Omega,X\right)=\inf\biggl\{R_{p,q,\varphi}(f,\Omega,X):||f(z)||_{H^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)}\leq 1\biggr\}.

For
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
and
q_{1}\leq q_{2},
we have the following inclusion relation:

\displaystyle R_{p_{1},q_{1},\varphi}\left(\Omega,X\right)\leq R_{p_{2},q_{2},\varphi}\left(\Omega,X\right).

It is established in
[
17
, Theorem 1.3]
that
R_{\Omega}(p)\leq R_{p,1,\varphi}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\right)
, where
p\in[1,2]
and
R_{\Omega}(p)
is the minimal positive root in
(0,1)
of the equation

\displaystyle p\varphi_{0}(r)=2\lambda_{H}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r).

Based on the preceding discussion, it is clear that the research tradition concerning Bohr-type inequalities centers on refining and enhancing the classical Bohr’s inequality. Thus, we aim to investigate an improved version of the generalized Bohr radius in (
2.3
) associated with
\varphi
, which requires redefining the definition as mentioned above.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f\in H^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)
be given by
f(z)=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D},

m=0,1,2,\dots
with
||f(z)||_{H^{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\right)}\leq 1
. For
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
, we define

\displaystyle R^{\mu}_{p,q,m,\varphi}(f,\Omega,X)

\displaystyle:=\sup\biggl\{r\geq 0:||A_{m}||^{p}\varphi_{m}(r)+\left(\sum_{n=m+1}^{\infty}||A_{n}||\varphi_{n}(r)+\mu(r)\mathcal{A}\left(f_{m},\varphi,r\right)\right)^{q}\leq\varphi_{m}(r)\biggr\},

where
p\in(0,2]
and
\mu:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a continuous function. The Bohr radius associated with
\varphi

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{p,m,N,\mu}(a)=p(1-r^{2m})\dfrac{(a+r^{m})^{p-2}}{(1+ar^{m})^{p+2}}\left(p-1-2ar^{m}-(p+1)r^{2m}\right)\varphi_{0}(r)-2\mu(r)\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r).

by assumption for

whenever

]
, we find that

for all

\displaystyle=p\dfrac{1-r^{m}}{1+r^{m}}\Psi(r)\varphi_{0}(r)-2\mu(r)a\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle\geq a^{p-1}\left(p\left(\dfrac{1-r^{m}}{1+r^{m}}\right)\varphi_{0}(r)-2\mu(r)a^{2-p}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)\right)

\displaystyle\geq a^{p-1}\left(p\left(\dfrac{1-r^{m}}{1+r^{m}}\right)\varphi_{0}(r)-2\mu(r)\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)\right)\geq 0

for all

, by assumption. Again,

is an increasing function of

which in turn implies that

Thus, the desired inequality holds for

The second part of the proof is to show that the constant

is sharp. Hence, we consider the function

For the function

and

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(r)+\left(\left(\dfrac{a+r^{m}}{1+ar^{m}}\right)^{p}-1\right)\varphi_{0}(r)+\mu(r)(1-a^{2})\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a^{n-1}\varphi_{n}(r)

For

by the assumption. This verifies that the radius

\displaystyle||Df(z)||\leq\dfrac{(1-a)(1-\gamma)^{\nu}}{\left(1-|(1-\gamma)z+\gamma|^{2}\right)^{\nu}}\leq\dfrac{(1-a)(1-\gamma)^{\nu}}{\left(1-((1-\gamma)|z|+\gamma)^{2}\right)^{\nu}},\;\;z\in\Omega_{\gamma}.

Thus it follows that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg|\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}sA_{s}z^{s-1}\bigg|\bigg|^{2}={||Df(z)||}^{2}\leq\dfrac{(1-a)^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2\nu}}{\left(1-((1-\gamma)|z|+\gamma)^{2}\right)^{2\nu}}.

Integrating (
4.3
) over the circle
|z|=r<1
gives that

\displaystyle 2r\pi\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}s^{2}||A_{s}||^{2}r^{2(s-1)}

\displaystyle\leq(1-a)^{2}\int_{|z|=r}\dfrac{(1-\gamma)^{2\nu}}{\left(1-((1-\gamma)|z|+\gamma)^{2}\right)^{2\nu}}|dz|

\displaystyle=(1-a)^{2}\int_{\theta=0}^{2\pi}\dfrac{(1-\gamma)^{2\nu}}{\left(1-((1-\gamma)|re^{i\theta}|+\gamma)^{2}\right)^{2\nu}}rd{\theta}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(1-a)^{2}2\pi r(1-\gamma)^{2\nu}}{\left(1-((1-\gamma)r+\gamma)^{2}\right)^{2\nu}}

which is equivalent to

(4.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}s^{2}||A_{s}||^{2}r^{2s}\leq\dfrac{(1-a)^{2}r^{2}(1-\gamma)^{2\nu}}{\left(1-((1-\gamma)r+\gamma)^{2}\right)^{2\nu}}.

In view of the classical Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and (
4.11
), we see that

\displaystyle||A_{0}||+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}||A_{s}||r^{s}

\displaystyle\leq a+\sqrt{\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}s^{2}||A_{s}||^{2}r^{2s}}\sqrt{\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{s^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq a+\dfrac{(1-a)r(1-\gamma)^{\nu}}{\left(1-((1-\gamma)r+\gamma)^{2}\right)^{\nu}}\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}}

\displaystyle\leq 1

provided

(4.12)

\displaystyle\dfrac{r(1-\gamma)^{\nu}}{\left(1-((1-\gamma)r+\gamma)^{2}\right)^{\nu}}\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\leq 1.

The inequality
(
4.12
)
holds for
r\leq R_{\Omega_{\gamma}}(\nu),
where
R_{\Omega_{\gamma}}(\nu)
is the smallest root in
(0,1)
of the equation
N_{\Omega_{\gamma},\nu}(r)=0
given by (
4.10
).

To prove the existence of the root
R_{\Omega_{\gamma}(\nu)}
, we see that
N_{\Omega_{\gamma},\nu}(r)
is a continuous function of
r
in
[0,1]
and satisfies the conditions

\displaystyle N_{\Omega_{\gamma},\nu}(0)=-6(1-\gamma^{2})^{2\nu}<0\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;N_{\Omega_{\gamma},\nu}(1)=(1-\gamma)^{2\nu}\pi^{2}>0.

Thus, applying the intermediate value theorem to the continuous function
N_{\Omega_{\gamma},\nu}
in
[0,1]
, we conclude that
N_{\Omega_{\gamma},\nu}(r)=0
has a root in
(0,1)
and we choose the smallest root to be
R_{\Omega_{\gamma}}(\nu).
This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Concluding remarks

The study of the Bohr inequality for different classes of functions is currently an active research area in Geometric Functions Theory. In this paper, we have successfully established sharp versions of the Bohr inequality for operator-valued holomorphic functions on simply connected domains. The research work in this paper also extends to generalizing the refined Bohr inequality and the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality, involving a sequence of non-negative continuous functions that converge locally uniformly. We have established the sharpness of our results, which may be considered a certain foundation for further exploration in the Bohr radius problem.

Furthermore, we have explored our findings to include the class of operator-valued
\nu
-Bloch functions in two distinct connected domains,
\Omega
and
\Omega_{\gamma}
, in the complex plane, without knowledge of the coefficient bounds. Although we have not proven the Growth theorem for the class of operator-valued
\nu
-Bloch functions, which remains an open problem in geometric functions theory, our study of the Bohr-Rogosinski inequality and its contributions to the understanding of Bohr-type inequalities opens new avenues for research in complex analysis and operator theory.

Log-linear Dynamic Inversion for Thrusting Spacecraft on SE 2 ​ ( 3 ) \mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)
Micah K. Condie, Abigaile E. Woodbury, Li-Yu Lin, Kartik A. Pant, Mike Walker, James Goppert

Abstract
We show that the dynamics of a thrusting spacecraft can be embedded in the Lie group SE 2 ​ ( 3 ) \mathrm{SE}_{2}(3) in a form that is group-affine with application of a feed-forward control law. This structure implies that the configuration-tracking error evolves exactly linearly in the associated Lie algebra coordinates (log-linear dynamics), rather than arising from a local linearization of the nonlinear system. As a result, a broad class of linear analysis and synthesis tools becomes directly applicable to powered spacecraft motion on SE 2 ​ ( 3 ) \mathrm{SE}_{2}(3) . A simple numerical example confirms that the error predicted by the linear Lie-algebra dynamics matches the error computed from the full nonlinear system, illustrating the exact log-linear behavior. This foundational property opens a path toward rigorous tools for satellite docking, autonomous rendezvous and proximity operations, robust controller design, and convex safety certification—capabilities that are difficult to achieve with classical local linearizations such as Tschauner–Hempel/Yamanaka–Ankersen (TH/YA).

In particular, the coupling between body-fixed thrust and inertial gravity is not represented in a way that reveals how tracking errors evolve during powered maneuvers. This limits the applicability of classical linear tools to the attitude-dependent thrusting scenarios that dominate modern low-Earth-orbit and geostationary operations

This technical note takes a first step toward a geometric description of powered proximity operations by showing that the disturbance-free dynamics of a thrusting spacecraft can be embedded in the Lie group
\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)
—also known as the group of

II
Background and Problem Statement

We first provide a brief overview of Lie group theory required for our analysis in this section.

II-A

SE
{}_{2}(3)
Lie Group Representation

A Lie group
G
provides a globally valid configuration space with an associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(the tangent space at the identity). The exponential and logarithmic maps,

\operatorname{Exp}:\mathfrak{g}\!\to\!G,\qquad\operatorname{Log}:G\!\to\!\mathfrak{g},

relate algebra elements to finite motions via
X=\operatorname{Exp}(\xi^{\wedge})
.
Rigid-body configuration spaces, such as
\mathrm{SO}(3)
—the group of
3\times 3
rotation matrices—and
\mathrm{SE}(3)
—the group of rigid-body poses consisting of a rotation and a translation—are smooth nonlinear manifolds rather than vector spaces. As a result, naive Euclidean subtraction does not correspond to any meaningful physical displacement or rotation. The Lie group–Lie algebra framework provides the correct geometric machinery for representing and manipulating such motions.

In this work, we use the Lie group
\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)
, which extends the
classical rigid-body group
\mathrm{SE}(3)
by including velocity as part of the group element. Its matrix representation is

X=\begin{bmatrix}R&v&p\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3),

(1)

To work in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3)
, we use the wedge operator

(\cdot)^{\wedge}
, which maps algebra coordinates into the corresponding
matrix form.
[\,\cdot\,]_{\times}
denotes the usual skew-symmetric matrix mapping a vector to the corresponding cross-product operator.

For
\xi=(\xi_{p},\xi_{v},\xi_{R})\in\mathbb{R}^{9}
, the wedge map is

\xi^{\wedge}=\begin{bmatrix}[\xi_{R}]_{\times}&\xi_{v}&\xi_{p}\\
0&0&0\\

and the vee operator

extracts the components

(\xi_{p},\xi_{v},\xi_{R})

\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3)

A full group element is then obtained via the exponential map

III
Mixed-Invariant Dynamics and Error Formulation

By expressing the spacecraft’s position, velocity, and attitude dynamics on the Lie group
\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)
, the equations of motion can be written in a mixed-invariant form, which makes the system group-affine. As a consequence, the tracking-error dynamics admit an exact linear representation in the associated Lie algebra.

III-A

Equations of Motion of Spacecraft as Mixed-Invariant System

Define the augmented group elements

X=\begin{bmatrix}R&v&p\\[3.0pt]
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&1&0\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&0&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad\bar{X}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{R}&\bar{v}&\bar{p}\\[3.0pt]
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&1&0\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&0&1\end{bmatrix},

where
X
is the state of the system and
\bar{X}
is the reference trajectory.

Equations (
2
)–(
4
) can then be written compactly as

\dot{X}=(M-C)X+X(N+C),

(8)

where

M=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{3\times 3}&g(p)&\mathbf{0}_{3\times 1}\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&0&0\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad N=\begin{bmatrix}[\omega]_{\times}&a&\mathbf{0}_{3\times 1}\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&0&0\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&0&0\end{bmatrix},

C=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{3\times 3}&\mathbf{0}_{3\times 3}&\mathbf{0}_{3\times 1}\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&1\\
\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times 3}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Notice,
M,N\in\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3)
and the system is
mixed-invariant
, since the term
(M-C)X
is right-invariant and
X(N+C)
is left-invariant. The right-invariant term captures gravity
g(p)
in the inertial frame, and the left-invariant term captures translational acceleration and angular velocity in the body frame.

Let the reference and state mismatch for
M
and
N
be

\tilde{M}\coloneq\bar{M}-M,\qquad\tilde{N}\coloneq\bar{N}-N,

We denote their corresponding Lie–algebra elements by

\bar{n}=\bar{N}^{\vee},\qquad\tilde{m}=\tilde{M}^{\vee},\qquad\tilde{n}=\tilde{N}^{\vee}.

III-B

Other notation

\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)

J_{\ell}(\xi)=\frac{\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}\,e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}}{I-e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}},\qquad J_{r}(\xi)=\frac{\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}{I-e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}},

where
I

n\times n

The operators
\operatorname{ad}_{\bar{n}}

J_{\ell}(\xi)

J_{r}(\xi)

\operatorname{Ad}_{\bar{X}^{-1}}^{\vee}

\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathbb{R}^{n}

The matrix
A_{C}
encodes the kinematic coupling

\dot{\xi}_{p}=\xi_{v}

Theorem III.1
(Log-Linear Error Dynamics)

For the mixed-invariant spacecraft dynamics on
\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)
,
the left-invariant log–error
\xi=\operatorname{Log}(\bar{X}^{-1}X)^{\vee}
satisfies

\dot{\xi}=\bigl(-\operatorname{ad}_{\bar{n}}+A_{C}\bigr)\xi+J_{\ell}(\xi)\tilde{n}+J_{r}(\xi)\operatorname{Ad}_{\bar{X}^{-1}}^{\vee}\tilde{m}.

Choosing the feedforward input

u_{\mathrm{ff}}=-\,J_{\ell}(\xi)^{-1}J_{r}(\xi)\,\operatorname{Ad}_{\bar{X}^{-1}}^{\vee}\!\bigl(g(p)-g(\bar{p})\bigr),

which further simplifies to,

u_{\mathrm{ff}}=-\operatorname{Ad}_{\eta\bar{X}^{-1}}^{\vee}\!\bigl(g(p)-g(\bar{p})\bigr),

results in
\tilde{m}
dropping from the error dynamics, yielding,

\dot{\xi}=A(t)\,\xi,\qquad A(t)=-\operatorname{ad}_{\bar{n}(t)}+A_{C}.

The log–error dynamics are then

exactly linear
in the Lie algebra.
If the reference acceleration and rotation term
\bar{n}
is constant,
then
A(t)
is constant and the system becomes

linear time invariant
.

If the invariant feedback design of
Li-Yu
et al.

[
8
]
is chosen, such that

u_{\mathrm{fb}}=J_{\ell}(\xi)^{-1}(BK\xi),

and

\ u=u_{\mathrm{ff}}+u_{\mathrm{fb}}.

This combined control law results in stabilized, linear, log-error dynamics

\dot{\xi}=\bigl(A(t)+BK\bigr)\xi.

Proof:

From the definition of
\eta
, we have

\dot{\eta}=-X^{-1}\dot{X}X^{-1}\bar{X}+X^{-1}\dot{\bar{X}}.

Substituting the expressions for
\dot{X}
and
\dot{\bar{X}}
gives

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}

\displaystyle=-X^{-1}\!\big((M-C)X+X(N+C)\big)X^{-1}\bar{X}

\displaystyle\quad+X^{-1}\!\big((\bar{M}-C)\bar{X}+\bar{X}(\bar{N}+C)\big).

Let

\tilde{M}\coloneq\bar{M}-M,\qquad\tilde{N}\coloneq\bar{N}-N,

then

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}

\displaystyle=X^{-1}\tilde{M}\bar{X}+\eta\bar{N}-N\eta-\eta C+C\eta.

Left–multiplying by
\eta^{-1}
yields

\displaystyle\eta^{-1}\dot{\eta}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ad}_{\bar{X}^{-1}}\tilde{M}+\bar{N}-\operatorname{Ad}_{\eta^{-1}}N-(I-\operatorname{Ad}_{\eta^{-1}})C.

Applying the vee operator and the right-trivialized differential of the log map,

\dot{\xi}=J_{r}(\xi)(\eta^{-1}\dot{\eta})^{\vee},\qquad J_{r}(\xi)=\frac{\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}{I-e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}},

with
\operatorname{Ad}_{\eta^{-1}}^{\vee}=e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}}
and

J_{\ell}(\xi)=\operatorname{Ad}_{\eta^{-1}}^{\vee}J_{r}(\xi)
, gives the stated result.

Remark 2
(Structure of the Log-Linearized Dynamics)

The log-linearized error dynamics have the explicit form:

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{p}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{p}+\xi_{v}+(\text{disturbances}),

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{v}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{v}-[\bar{a}]_{\times}\xi_{p}+(\text{disturbances}),

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{R}

\displaystyle=-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{R}+(\text{disturbances}),

where
\bar{n}=[\mathbf{0},\bar{a},\bar{\omega}]^{\top}
and the

-\operatorname{ad}_{\bar{n}}\xi

cross-products
-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}
on each component.
The
A_{C}

\dot{\xi}_{p}=\xi_{v}
.
This structure is analogous to the classical right-invariant error dynamics

\dot{\eta}_{p}=-[\omega^{ir}_{r}]_{\times}\eta_{p}+\eta_{v}
, but expressed
in log-coordinates where
\xi_{p}\leftrightarrow\eta_{p}
and
\xi_{v}\leftrightarrow\eta_{v}
.

Remark 3
(Role of the
C

The constant matrix
C
encodes the kinematic coupling
\dot{\xi}_{p}=\xi_{v}
within the
\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)
embedding.
Although
C\notin\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3)

A

is a
linear

\xi

A_{C}\xi=[\xi_{v}^{\top},0,0]^{\top}

Meanwhile, the
-\operatorname{ad}_{\bar{n}}\xi

-[\bar{\omega}]_{\times}\xi_{p}

Together, these terms preserve the
log-linear

group error
\eta

\xi

IV
Simulation Results

We illustrate the log-linear error property on a simple powered maneuver in a near-geostationary orbit. The reference trajectory is generated by integrating the disturbance-free dynamics with a constant body-frame acceleration and angular velocity. Specifically, we apply a constant thrust command corresponding to

a_{\text{ref}}=[10,\,0,\,0]^{\top}~\text{m/s}^{2}

along the body
x
–axis and a constant body-rate

\omega_{\text{ref}}=[0.01,\,0,\,0]^{\top}~\text{rad/s},

and propagate the reference state for
500\,\text{s}
using the full nonlinear SE
{}_{2}(3)
dynamics with the central gravitational parameter
\mu=3.986\times 10^{14}\,\text{m}^{3}/\text{s}^{2}
.

The actual spacecraft is initialized from the same geostationary state with a small configuration perturbation relative to the reference: a position offset of approximately

\|\delta p(0)\|\approx 1.2\times 10^{2}~\text{m},

a velocity offset of approximately

\|\delta v(0)\|\approx 0.6~\text{m/s},

and an attitude error of
0.05~\text{rad}
about the body
x
–axis. Both the reference and actual trajectories are then propagated forward using the same nonlinear equations of motion and constant body-frame controls.

At each time step, the configuration-tracking error for the nonlinear simulation is computed as the right-invariant group error

\eta(t)=\bar{X}(t)^{-1}X(t)\in\text{SE}_{2}(3),

and mapped to the Lie algebra via

\xi_{\text{nl}}(t)=\operatorname{Log}\big(\eta(t)\big)^{\vee}\in\mathbb{R}^{9}.

In parallel, we form the time-varying log-linear system

\dot{\xi}(t)=\big(-\operatorname{ad}_{\bar{n}(t)}+A_{C}\big)\,\xi(t),

where
\bar{n}(t)
is built from the reference controls
(a_{\text{ref}},\omega_{\text{ref}})
and
A_{C}
encodes the kinematic coupling
\dot{\xi}_{p}=\xi_{v}
. This linear system is integrated using the same initial Lie-algebra error
\xi(0)=\xi_{\text{nl}}(0)
.

In these simulations, both the reference and actual trajectories use identical
thrust inputs and the same gravity model, so the mismatch terms

\tilde{m}
and
\tilde{n}
appearing in Lemma 1 vanish. More generally,
even when the spacecraft and reference trajectories do not occupy the same
position, these mismatch terms also disappear if the commanded acceleration
includes a feedforward component that compensates for the gravity
difference
g(p)-g(\bar{p})
. Under this condition, the log-linear ODE

\dot{\xi}=(-\operatorname{ad}_{\bar{n}}+A_{C})\xi
holds exactly.

V
Conclusion

This technical note establishes that the dynamics of a thrusting spacecraft can be embedded in the Lie group
\mathrm{SE}_{2}(3)
in a mixed-invariant form that is group-affine. As a direct consequence, the configuration-tracking error evolves
exactly linearly
in the associated Lie algebra, providing a global linear representation of powered spacecraft motion. A simple numerical illustration confirms this log-linear behavior by showing that the Lie-algebra error dynamics match those obtained from the full nonlinear system.

This structural result provides a foundation for several future developments, including invariant controllers and observers, linear-system–based analysis tools, and ultimately convex methods for docking, proximity operations, robustness certification, and safety verification. Future work will build on this property to develop LMI-based reachable-set bounds, incorporate disturbances and thrust misalignment, and relate the mixed-invariant formulation to classical orbital models.

Appendix A
Normalizer of the
C
Matrix

Let
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{gl}(n)
denote a Lie algebra with the standard matrix commutator
[A,B]=AB-BA
. The
normalizer
of
\mathfrak{g}
is

N(\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\{X\in\mathfrak{gl}(n)\mid[X,Y]\in\mathfrak{g},\;\forall Y\in\mathfrak{g}\}.

An element
X
belongs to the normalizer if
\operatorname{ad}_{X}(Y)=[X,Y]
maps
\mathfrak{g}
into itself. In this work, the constant matrix
C
satisfies

[\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3),C]\subseteq\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3),

so
C\in N(\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3))
. Although
C\notin\mathfrak{se}_{2}(3)
, this ensures the commutator
([\xi^{\wedge},C])^{\vee}
remains linear in
\xi
, preserving the log-linear structure.

Proof of membership.

Let
\eta(s)=\operatorname{Exp}(s\xi^{\wedge})
and
\Phi(s)=\eta(s)^{-1}C\eta(s)
. Then
\frac{d}{ds}\Phi(s)=-\eta(s)^{-1}[\xi^{\wedge},C]\eta(s)
.
Integrating from
s=0
to
1
and right-trivializing yields

J_{r}^{-1}(\xi)(C-\eta^{-1}C\eta)^{\vee}=-([\xi^{\wedge},C])^{\vee},

confirming that
C
acts as an element of the normalizer.

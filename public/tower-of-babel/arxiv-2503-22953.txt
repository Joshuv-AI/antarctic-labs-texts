Abstract
. We construct a global homotopy formula for a q a_{q} domains in a complex manifold. The homotopy operators in the formula will gain 1 / 2 1/2 derivative in Hölder-Zygmund spaces Λ r \Lambda^{r} when the boundaries of the domains are in Λ r + 3 \Lambda^{r+3} with r > 0 r>0 .

1.
Introduction

Let
M=\{\rho<0\}
be a relatively compact domain in a complex manifold
\mathcal{M}
of dimension
n
, where
\rho
is a
C^{2}
function on
\mathcal{M}
.
We say that the domain
M
satisfies the
condition
a_{q}
, if for each
p
in the boundary
bM
,
d\rho(p)\neq 0

and the Levi-form of
\rho
on
T^{(1,0)}_{p}(bM)

has at least
(n-q)
positive or
(q+1)
negative eigenvalues.
Let
V
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
\mathcal{M}
. We will denote by

\Lambda^{r}_{q}(M,V)
the space of
V
-valued
(0,q)
forms on
\overline{M}
of Hölder-Zygmund class
\Lambda^{r}
. When
r>0
is not an integer,
\Lambda^{r}
is the standard Hölder space
C^{r}
; see Section
2
for definition when
r
is a positive integer.

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

M
be a relatively compact
a_{q}
domain in a complex manifold
\mathcal{M}
of dimension
n
. Assume that
1\leq q\leq n-2
,
0<r<\infty
,
and
bM\in\Lambda^{r_{0}+3}
with
r\leq r_{0}\leq\infty
.
Then there exists a decomposition

(1.1)

\displaystyle{}f=\bar{\partial}P_{q}f+P_{q+1}\bar{\partial}f+H_{q}f

for all
f\in\Lambda^{r}_{q}(\overline{M},V)
with
\bar{\partial}f\in\Lambda^{r}
.
Furthermore, we have the following

\hskip-10.00002pt(a)

The operators

(1.2)

\displaystyle{}P_{\tilde{q}}\colon

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\tilde{q}}^{r}(M,V)\to\Lambda_{\tilde{q}-1}^{r+1/2}(M,V),\quad\tilde{q}=q,q+1;

(1.3)

\displaystyle H_{q}\colon

\displaystyle\Lambda_{q}^{\epsilon}(M,V)\to\mathcal{H}_{q}(M,V)\subset\Lambda^{r+1/2}_{q}(M,V),\quad\forall\epsilon>0

are linear and bounded.

\hskip-10.00002pt(b)

\dim\mathcal{H}_{q}(M,V)<\infty
,
H_{q}
is a projection
(
i.e.
H_{q}^{2}=I)
, and each non-zero element
f\in\mathcal{H}_{q}(M,V)
is
\bar{\partial}
-closed and admits no global solution
u\in L^{2}_{loc}(M,V)
for
\bar{\partial}u=f
on
M
.

\hskip-10.00002pt(c)

The
P_{\tilde{q}},H_{q},\mathcal{H}_{q}(M,V)
are independent of
f
and
r
. Consequently, they depend only on
bM
and
r_{0}
; if
bM\in C^{\infty}
and
f,g
are in
C^{\infty}(\overline{M})
, then

P_{\tilde{q}}g,H_{q}f
are in
C^{\infty}(\overline{M})
.

For a detailed statement including estimates on the norms of
P_{q},P_{q+1}
and
H_{q}
, see Theorem
6.7
. When
H_{\bar{\partial}}^{(0,q)}(M,V)=0
, we have
\mathcal{H}_{(0,q)}(M,V)=0
by
[
9
]
*Thm. 1.1. Then (
1.1
) is called a homotopy formula.

We should mention that condition
a_{q}
, first appeared in
[
12
]
, is the same as condition
Z_{q}
that plays an important role in the regularity of
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann operator
N_{q}
on
(0,q)
forms on manifolds with smooth boundary (see Kohn-Rossi
[
2
]
and Folland-Kohn
[
14
]
), which extend earlier work of Kohn on
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann operators on strongly pseudoconvex manifolds with smooth boundary
[
15
,
16
]
. The regularity of
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann operators has an application to the local Newlander-Nirenberg theorem (See
[
15
]
).
When both
bM
and a formal integrable almost complex structure that is a small perturbation of the complex structure on
\mathcal{M}
are smooth (
C^{\infty}
) and
H^{(0,1)}_{\bar{\partial}}(M,\Theta)=0
with
\Theta
being the holomorphic tangent bundle of
\mathcal{M}
, Hamilton showed that the deformed structure can be transformed into the complex structure on

via a

into
\mathcal{M}
. Hamilton
[
10
,

]
proved this version of global Newlander-Nirenberg theorem by adapting regularity of
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann operators for a family of formally integrable almost complex structures.

Our motivation for Theorem
1.1
also comes from the study of global Newlander-Nirenberg problem for finite smooth domains and deformed finite smooth complex structures on the domains.
As mentioned early, when
H^{(0,q)}_{\bar{\partial}}(M,V)=0
, the decomposition (
1.1
) provides a homotopy formula. In
[
27
,
28
]
Webster introduced the method of homotopy formulas to the study of local Newlander-Nirenberg problem that yields a sharp regularity result and the local CR embedding problem; see Ma-Michel
[
20
]
and Gong-Webster
[
7
]
for further results on the local CR embedding. This approach via homotopy formulas was applied to the study of stability of global CR embedding problem by Polyakov
[
22
]
. The homotopy formulas are useful in the study of the global Newlander-Nirenberg problem. Results in this direction were obtained very recently for strongly pseudoconvex domains in
{\bf C}^{n}
by Gan-Gong
[
3
]
and Shi
[
26
]
. In
[
5
]
, the present authors considered the global Newlander-Nirenberg problem for
a_{1}^{+}
domains (i.e. domains satisfying both
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
conditions). Condition
a_{1}^{+}
is of course stronger than

\bar{\partial}
-Neumann operators

on
a_{1}^{+}

[
5
]

As a first step to address the global Newlander-Nirenberg problems on
a_{1}
domains beyond Hamilton’s work, we construct the homotopy formula for a
fixed
domain via Theorem
1.1
. The proof of the theorem is based on a functional analysis approach. A very general construction of global homotopy formulas through local approximate homotopy formulas and functional analysis was employed by Leiterer
[
19
]
. The functional analysis approach for an analogous problem on embedded compact CR manifolds has been successfully developed by Polyakov
[
21
]
, and Laurent-Thiébault and Leiterer
[
17
,
18
]
. In this paper, we adapt Polyakov’s construction
[
21
]
for embedded CR manifolds to the case of
a_{q}
domains. We believe that the improvements in this paper will find its application to the global CR embedding problem studied by Polyakov
[
22
]
.
We should mention that there are different approaches by Leiterer
[

]
, Laurent-Thiébaut and Leiterer
[

,
18
]
, and Brönnle, Laurent-Thiébaut and Leiterer
[

to construct global homotopy formulas for embedded CR manifolds. However, their approaches are different from us. For instance our construction does not rely on the approximate homotopy operators for all small degrees or all large degrees.
The interested reader can also find recent results on homotopy formulas and more references in

,

.

The paper is organized as follows.

In Section
3
, we recall estimates on local approximate homotopy formulas from
[
5
]
. We then use a standard partition of unity procedure to construct a global approximate homotopy formula.
Our homotopy formula or decomposition (
1.1
) require us to study compositions of several operators that gain derivatives. To minimize the requirement on the regularity of the boundaries of the
a_{q}
domains and facilitate the estimates for the compositions, we will define a simple notion
e_{b}(T)
measuring the gain of derivatives of an operator
T
when the boundaries are at least of class
\Lambda^{b}
; see Definition
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
for properties of
e_{b}(T)
.

In Section
4
, we adapt the functional analysis approach by Polyakov
[
21
]
to our problem. To construct the homotopy formula when
H_{\bar{\partial}}^{(0,q)}(M,V)=0
, we start with an approximate homotopy formula

f-\hat{A}^{(0)}_{q}f=\bar{\partial}T_{q}^{(0)}f+T_{q+1}^{(0)}df

where the regularities of
T_{q}^{(0)},T_{q+1}^{(0)},\hat{A}_{q}^{(0)}
follow from
[
5
]
.
Then we attempt to invert
A^{(0)}_{q}:=I-\hat{A}^{(0)}_{q}
. This requires us to modify
A^{(0)}_{q},T_{q}^{(0)},T_{q+1}^{(0)}
to obtain the new operators
A_{q}^{(1)},T_{q}^{(1)},T_{q+1}^{(1)}
and
T_{q}^{(2)},T_{q+1}^{(2)}
on subspaces of
\Lambda_{\tilde{q}}^{r}(M,V)
.
These new operators will be used to construct projection operators onto some finite dimensional spaces arising from the compact operator
\hat{A}^{(0)}_{q}
. A preliminary decomposition (
1.1
) is achieved in Section
5
.

In Section
6
, we introduce a procedure to modify Polyakov’s construction
[
21
]
to optimize the regularity gain of the homotopy operators in Theorem
1.1
.

We will follow the approach of Polyakov
[

Throughout the paper we assume that

6.
An improved Polyakov homotopy formula

To construct a homotopy formula with homotopy operators that have better regularity, we will use the original approximate homotopy formula again. The scheme is the following: When comparing the new homotopy operators with the original ones, we notice that the difference operators gain derivatives. Therefore, we will use the algebraic properties of the original homotopy operators to modify a first version of homotopy operators. The ultimate homotopy operators will have the desired regularity.

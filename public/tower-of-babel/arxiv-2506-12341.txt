Abstract
. This paper analyzes the second cohomology group H ◆ , \Yleft 2 ⁡ ( H , I ) \ho^{2}_{\blackdiamond,\Yleft}(H,I) , for linear cycle sets with commutative adjoint operation, focusing on the finite abelian case. It aims to classify extensions of such structures through cohomological methods. Techniques are developed to systematically construct explicitly 2 2 -cocycles. Finally, some illustrative examples are explored to validate the theoretical framework.

Precedence of operations

The operations precedence in this paper is the following: the operators with the highest precedence are the unary operations

(where

) and the binary operation

1.1  Braces and linear cycle sets

A
left brace
is an additive abelian group
H
endowed with an additional group operation
(h,l)\mapsto h\times l
, such that

[1.1]

h\times(l+m)=h\times l+h\times m-h\quad\text{for all $h,l,m\in H$.}

The two group structures necessarily share the same neutral element, denoted by
0
. Of course, the additive inverse of
h
is denoted by
-h
, while its multiplicative inverse is denoted by
\cramped{h^{\times-1}}
. In particular
\cramped{0^{\times-1}}=0
.

Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
be left braces. A map
f\colon H\to H^{\prime}
is an homomorphism of left braces if
f(h+l)=f(h)+f(l)
and
~f(h\times l)=f(h)\times f(l)
, for all
h,l\in H
.

A
linear cycle set
is an additive abelian group
H
endowed with a binary operation
\cdot
, having bijective left translations
h\mapsto l\cdot h
, and satisfying the conditions

[1.2]

h\cdot(l+m)=h\cdot l+h\cdot m\quad\text{and}\quad(h+l)\cdot m=(h\cdot l)\cdot(h\cdot m),

for all
h,l,m\in H
. It is well known that each linear cycle set is a cycle set. That is:

[1.3]

(h\cdot l)\cdot(h\cdot m)=(l\cdot h)\cdot(l\cdot m)\quad\text{for all $h,l,m\in H$}.

Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
be linear cycle sets. A map
f\colon H\to H^{\prime}
is an homomorphism of linear cycle sets if
f(h+l)=f(h)+f(l)
and
f(h\cdot l)=f(h)\cdot f(l)
, for all
h,l\in H
.

The notions of left braces and linear cycle sets were introduced by Rump, who proved that they are equivalent via the relations

[1.4]

h\cdot l=\cramped{h^{\times-1}}\times(h+l)\quad\text{and}\quad h\times l={}^{h}l+h,

where the map
l\mapsto{}^{h}l
is the inverse of the map
l\mapsto h\cdot l
. Finally, by [
1.2
] and [
1.4
], we have

[1.5]

(h\times l)\cdot m=(h+{}^{h}l)\cdot m=(h\cdot{}^{h}l)\cdot(h\cdot m)=l\cdot(h\cdot m),

which implies

[1.6]

{}^{h\times l}m={}^{h}({}^{l}m).

Each additive abelian group
H
is a linear cycle set via
h\cdot l\coloneqq l
. These ones are the so called
trivial linear cycle sets
. Note that by [
1.4
], a linear cycle set
H
is trivial if and only if
h\times l=h+l
, for all
h,l\in H
.

Let
H
be a linear cycle set. An
ideal
of
H
is a subgroup
I
of
H
such that
h\cdot y\in I
and
y\cdot h-h\in I
, for all
h\in H
and
y\in I
. An ideal
I
of
H
is called a
central ideal
if
h\cdot y=y
and
y\cdot h=h
, for all
h\in H
and
y\in I
. The
socle
of
H
is the ideal
\Soc(H)
, of all
y\in H
such that
y\cdot h=h
, for all
h\in H
. The
center
of
H
is the set
\Z(H)
, of all
y\in\Soc(H)
such that
h\cdot y=y
, for all
h\in H
. Thus, the center of
H
is a central ideal of
H
, and each central ideal of
H
is included in
\Z(H)
. Finally, an ideal
I
of
H
is
trivial
if
h\cdot l=l
, for all
h,l\in I
.

Notation 1.1
.

Given an ideal
I
of a linear cycle set
H
, for each
y\in I
and
h\in H
, we set
y\Yleft h\coloneqq y\cdot h-h
.

Let

[1.13]

[1.15]

Proposition 2.1

Proposition 2.2

If
H=H_{1}\oplus H_{2}
, then
\Harr^{2}(H,I)=\Harr^{2}(H_{1},I)\oplus\Harr^{2}(H_{2},I)
. Under this correspondence, the class
[\beta]\in\Harr^{2}(H,I)
, of a normalized symmetric cocycle
\beta
, is identified with the class of
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
, where

\beta_{1}(h_{1},l_{1})\coloneqq\beta((h_{1},0),(l_{1},0))\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{2}(h_{2},l_{2})\coloneqq\beta((0,h_{2}),(0,l_{2})).

Proof.

We will prove that
\Harr^{2}(-,I)
is an additive functor. By identity [
2.1
], for this we only must check that
\bar{p}_{1}\circ\bar{\iota}_{1}+\bar{p}_{2}\circ\bar{\iota}_{2}=\Ide_{\Harr^{2}(H,I)}
. Let
\beta
,
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
be as in the statement. Note that
[\beta_{i}]=\bar{\iota}_{i}([\beta])
, and so we must prove that
[\beta]=\bar{p}_{1}([\beta_{1}])+\bar{p}_{2}([\beta_{2}])
. Define
t\in\hat{C}_{N}^{01}(H,I)
by
t(h_{1},h_{2})\coloneqq\beta((h_{1},0),(0,h_{2}))
. In order to finish the proof it suffices to check that

p_{1}^{*}(\beta_{1})+p_{2}^{*}(\beta_{2})-\beta=\partial_{v}^{02}(t),

which means that for all
h=(h_{1},h_{2}),l=(l_{1},l_{2})\in H
, we have

[2.2]

\beta((h_{1},0),(l_{1},0))+\beta((0,h_{2}),(0,l_{2}))-\beta(h,l)=t(l)-t(h+l)+t(h).

Using the equality

[2.3]

\beta(l,m)-\beta(h+l,m)+\beta(h,l+m)-\beta(h,l)=\partial_{\mathrm{v}}^{03}(\beta)(h,l,m)=0\qquad\text{for $h,l,m\in H$},

with
h=(h_{1},0)
,
l=(0,h_{2})
and
m=(l_{1},l_{2})
, we obtain

[2.4]

\beta((h_{1},h_{2}),(l_{1},l_{2}))=\beta((h_{1},0),(0,h_{2})+(l_{1},l_{2}))-\beta((h_{1},0),(0,h_{2}))+\beta((0,h_{2}),(l_{1},l_{2})).

From [
2.3
] with
h=(h_{1},0)
,
l=(l_{1},0)
and
m=(0,h_{2}+l_{2})
, we obtain

[2.5]

\beta((h_{1},0),(0,h_{2})+(l_{1},l_{2}))=\beta((h_{1}+l_{1},0),(0,h_{2}+l_{2}))-\beta((l_{1},0),(0,h_{2}+l_{2}))+\beta((h_{1},0),(l_{1},0)),

whereas from [
2.3
] with
h=(0,h_{2})
,
l=(0,l_{2})
and
m=(l_{1},0)
, we obtain

[2.6]

\beta((0,h_{2}),(l_{1},l_{2}))=\beta((0,h_{2}+l_{2}),(l_{1},0))-\beta((0,l_{2}),(l_{1},0))+\beta((0,h_{2}),(0,l_{2})).

Inserting into [
2.4
], the values of
\beta((h_{1},0),(0,h_{2})+(l_{1},l_{2}))
and
\beta((0,h_{2}),(l_{1},l_{2}))
, obtained in [
2.5
] and [
2.6
], and using the facts that

t((h_{1},h_{2})+(l_{1},l_{2}))=\beta((h_{1}+l_{1},0),(0,h_{2}+l_{2})),\quad t((h_{1},h_{2}))=\beta((h_{1},0),(0,h_{2})),\quad t((l_{1},l_{2}))=\beta((l_{1},0),(0,l_{2})),

and that
\beta
is symmetric, we obtain

\beta((h_{1},h_{2}),(l_{1},l_{2}))=t((h_{1},h_{2})+(l_{1},l_{2}))+\beta((h_{1},0),(l_{1},0))-t(h_{1},h_{2})+\beta((0,h_{2}),(0,l_{2}))-t(l_{1},l_{2}),

which is [
2.2
], finishing the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For
\beta\in\widehat{C}_{N}^{02}(H,I)
, we have
\beta\in\ker(\partial_{\mathrm{v}}^{03})
if and only if

[2.7]

\beta(i,j)=\sum_{k=j}^{i+j-1}\beta(1,k)-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\beta(1,k)\qquad\text{for $1\leq i,j<d$.}

Proof.

Assume that
\beta\in\ker(\partial_{\mathrm{v}}^{03})
. Then, for all
i,j
, we have

0=\partial_{\mathrm{v}}^{03}\beta(1,i,j)=\beta(i,j)-\beta(i+1,j)+\beta(1,i+j)-\beta(1,i).

Thus,
\beta(i+1,j)=\beta(i,j)+\beta(1,i+j)-\beta(1,i)
. An inductive argument on
i
using this fact proves that [
2.7
] is true. Conversely, assume that [
2.7
] holds. We must show that

\beta(b,c)-\beta(a+b,c)+\beta(a,b+c)-\beta(a,b)=0\qquad\text{for all $0\leq a,b,c<d$}.

But this is a consequence of the fact that
\beta(i,j)=\sum_{k=j}^{i+j-1}\beta(1,k)-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\beta(1,k)
for all
i,j\in\mathds{N}
(which follows from [
2.7
]).
∎

Remark 2.4

\beta_{kd}(\gamma)

\beta=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\beta_{kd}(\beta(1,k))\qquad\text{for each $\beta\in\ker(\partial_{\mathrm{v}}^{03})$.}

In particular

\chi_{k}(\gamma)(i)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\gamma&\text{if $i=k$,}\\

From now on we will assume that the additive underlying structure of

2.2.1  Properties of cocycles

The aim of this subsection is to show that each
2
-cocycle
(\alpha,-f)
of
(\widehat{C}_{N}^{*}(H,I),\partial+D)
is completed determined by

(\gamma_{k})_{1\leq k\leq n}
and
(\mathfrak{f}_{ji})_{1\leq j\leq s,1\leq i\leq n}

\mathfrak{f}_{ji}\coloneqq f(a_{j},e_{i})

2.13
. There, we also prove that the correspondence

\bigl((\gamma_{k})_{1\leq k\leq n},(\mathfrak{f}_{ji})_{1\leq j\leq s,1\leq i\leq n}\bigr)\mapsto(\alpha,-f)

is linear. Of course, not every tuple
\bigl((\gamma_{k})_{1\leq k\leq n},(\mathfrak{f}_{ji})_{1\leq j\leq s,1\leq i\leq n}\bigr)

Let

(\alpha,-f)
is a
2
-cocycle of
(\widehat{C}_{N}^{*}(H,I),\partial+D)
if and only if
\partial_{\mathrm{v}}^{03}(\alpha)=0
,
\partial_{\mathrm{h}}^{12}(\alpha)=\partial_{\mathrm{v}}^{12}(f)
and
(\partial_{\mathrm{h}}^{21}+D_{11}^{21})(f)=D_{02}^{21}(\alpha)

[2.9]

and the last one means that

[2.10]

f(h+l,m)=f(h\cdot l,h\cdot m)+(h\cdot l)\blackdiamond f(h,m)+(h\cdot l)\blackdiamond f(h,l)\Yleft(h+l)\cdot m-(h+l)\blackdiamond\alpha(h,l)\Yleft(h+l)\cdot m.

If condition [
2.9
] is satisfied, then we will say that

is
\alpha
-quasi linear in the second variable.

Proposition 2.8
.

If
\partial_{\mathrm{h}}^{12}(\alpha)=\partial_{\mathrm{v}}^{12}(f)

,

and

[2.11]

\displaystyle f(h,\lambda_{i}e_{i})=\lambda_{i}f(h,e_{i})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lambda_{i}-1}\alpha(h\cdot e_{i},\ell(h\cdot e_{i})),

[2.12]

\displaystyle f\left(h,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(h,\lambda_{i}e_{i})+\sum_{k=2}^{n}\alpha\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}(h\cdot e_{i}),\lambda_{k}(h\cdot e_{k})\right),

[2.13]

\displaystyle d_{i}f(h,e_{i})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_{i}-1}\alpha(h\cdot e_{i},\ell(h\cdot e_{i}))-h\blackdiamond\gamma_{i}=0.

The equality [

\lambda_{i}

2.9

and

Lemma 2.10

and set

Proposition 2.14

Assume the first equality is true, for all

\displaystyle A_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots A_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}(\Ide-\lambda_{1}B_{1}-\cdots-\lambda_{n}B_{n})y

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}e_{n})\blackdiamond(y-\lambda_{1}y\Yleft e_{1}-\cdots-\lambda_{n}y\Yleft e_{n})

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}e_{n})\blackdiamond(y-y\Yleft(\lambda_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}e_{n}))

\displaystyle=y\triangleleft(\lambda_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}e_{n})

\displaystyle=(A_{1}-A_{1}B_{1})^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots(A_{n}-A_{n}B_{n})^{\lambda_{n}}y,

where the first equality holds by the definitions of the
A_{i}
and
B_{j}
; the second one, since
\Yleft
is bilinear; the third one, by the definition of
\triangleleft
; and the last one, by the first identity in [
1.12
].
∎

3.2  Computing the map
\mathbf{f}
in the trivial case

Let
(\alpha,-f)\in\widehat{C}^{02}_{N}(H,I)\oplus\widehat{C}^{11}_{N}(H,I)
, be a
2
-cocycle of
(\widehat{C}_{N}^{*}(H,I),\partial+D)
, with
\alpha\coloneqq\alpha_{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}}
. When
H
is trivial we have the following result:

Theorem 3.2
.

For
0\leq k_{1},\lambda_{1}<d_{1},\dots,0\leq k_{n},\lambda_{n}<d_{n}
, the following formula holds:

\displaystyle f\left(\sum k_{j}e_{j},\sum\lambda_{u}e_{u}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\sum_{u=1}^{n}\lambda_{u}A_{j}^{k_{j}-\ell-1}A_{j+1}^{k_{j+1}}\cdots A_{n}^{k_{n}}f(e_{j},e_{u})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\sum_{i=j+1}^{n}\sum_{u=1}^{n}k_{i}\lambda_{u}B_{u}A_{j}^{k_{j}-\ell-1}A_{j+1}^{k_{j+1}}\cdots A_{n}^{k_{n}}f(e_{j},e_{i})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_{j}-1}\sum_{u=1}^{n}\ell\lambda_{u}B_{u}A_{j}^{\ell}A_{j+1}^{k_{j+1}}\cdots A_{n}^{k_{n}}f(e_{j},e_{j}).

Proof.

For the sake of brevity we write
m\coloneqq\lambda_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}e_{n}
. By the definition of
\alpha
, we have

\alpha\left(k_{j}e_{j},{}^{k_{j}e_{j}}(k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\right)=\alpha\left(k_{j}e_{j},k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)=0.

Hence, by Proposition
2.36
,

\displaystyle f(k_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n},m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\blackdiamond f(k_{j}e_{j},m)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\blackdiamond f(k_{j}e_{j},k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\Yleft m.

On the other hand, since
\alpha(e_{j},\bar{e}_{j\ell})=\alpha(e_{j},\ell e_{j})=0
for
0\leq\ell<d_{j}-1
, by Proposition
2.11
, we have

f(ke_{j},h)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(k-\ell-1)e_{j}\blackdiamond f(e_{j},h)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\ell e_{j}\blackdiamond f(e_{j},\ell e_{j})\Yleft h.

Consequently

\displaystyle f(k_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n},m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\left((k_{j}-\ell-1)e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond f(e_{j},m)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_{j}-1}(k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\blackdiamond\bigl(\ell e_{j}\blackdiamond f(e_{j},\ell e_{j})\Yleft m\bigr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\left((k_{j}-\ell-1)e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond f(e_{j},k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\Yleft m

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_{j}-1}(k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\blackdiamond\Bigl(\bigl(\ell e_{j}\blackdiamond f(e_{j},\ell e_{j})\bigr)\Yleft(k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\Bigr)\Yleft m

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\left((k_{j}-\ell-1)e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond f(e_{j},m)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_{j}-1}(k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\blackdiamond\bigl(\ell e_{j}\blackdiamond f(e_{j},\ell e_{j})\Yleft m\bigr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\left((k_{j}-\ell-1)e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond f(e_{j},k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\Yleft m

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_{j}-1}\left(\ell e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond f(e_{j},\ell e_{j})\Yleft m

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_{j}-1}(k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\blackdiamond\bigl(\ell e_{j}\blackdiamond f(e_{j},\ell e_{j})\Yleft m\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\left((k_{j}-\ell-1)e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond f(e_{j},m)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\left((k_{j}-\ell-1)e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond f(e_{j},k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n})\Yleft m

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_{j}-1}\left(\ell e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond f(e_{j},\ell e_{j})\Yleft m,

where the second equality holds by [
1.22
]. Note now that, since

\displaystyle\alpha(e_{j}\cdot e_{i},\ell(e_{j}\cdot e_{i}))=\alpha(e_{i},\ell e_{i})=0\quad\text{for $0\leq\ell<d_{i}-1$}

and

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}(e_{j}\cdot e_{i}),\lambda_{k}(e_{j}\cdot e_{k})\right)=\alpha\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}e_{i},\lambda_{k}e_{k}\right)=0,

from the identity [
2.32
], it follows that

\displaystyle f(k_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n},m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\left((k_{j}-\ell-1)e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond f(e_{j},m)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\sum_{i=j+1}^{n}\left((k_{j}-\ell-1)e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond k_{i}f(e_{j},e_{i})\Yleft m

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_{j}-1}\left(\ell e_{j}+k_{j+1}e_{j+1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n}\right)\blackdiamond\ell f(e_{j},e_{j})\Yleft m.

Applying again the identity [
2.32
], and using Proposition
3.1
, and that
\blackdiamond
and
\Yleft
are right linear, we obtain the formula in the statement.
∎

Assume that

\displaystyle T_{1}((\gamma_{i}),(\mathfrak{f}_{ji}))\coloneqq\left(d_{i}\mathfrak{f}_{ji}+\gamma_{i}-A_{j}\gamma_{i}\right)_{ij},

\displaystyle T_{2}((\gamma_{i}),(\mathfrak{f}_{ji}))\coloneqq\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{d_{j}-1}A_{j}^{\ell}\mathfrak{f}_{ji}\right)_{ij},

\displaystyle S(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\coloneqq\left((d_{i}t_{i})_{i},(A_{j}t_{i}-t_{i})_{ij}\right).

Moreover, by Theorem
3.2

[3.9]

f\left(\sum k_{j}e_{j},\sum\lambda_{i}e_{i}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}A_{j}^{k_{j}-\ell-1}A_{j+1}^{k_{j+1}}\cdots A_{n}^{k_{n}}f(e_{j},e_{i}),

f(k_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}e_{n},\lambda_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}e_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{j}\lambda_{i}f(e_{j},e_{i}),

Example 3.4
.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
2\leq\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
. Assume that
n=2
,
d_{1}\coloneqq p^{\eta_{1}}
,
d_{2}\coloneqq p^{\eta_{2}}
and
I\coloneqq\mathds{Z}_{p^{2}}
. Then, there exist
0\leq b_{i}<p
such that
A_{i}
is the multiplication by

b_{i}\neq 0
. Renaming the indices if necessary we can suppose that
b_{1}\neq 0
. Let
0<d<p
be such that
db_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, and let
\Phi\colon I^{6}\to I^{6}

\Phi(y_{1},y_{2},y_{11},y_{12},y_{21},y_{22})=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{11},y_{12},y_{21}+db_{2}y_{11},y_{22}+db_{2}y_{12}).

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{1}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{11},y_{12},y_{21},y_{22})=(-pb_{1}y_{1},-pb_{2}y_{1},-pb_{1}y_{2},-pb_{2}y_{2}),

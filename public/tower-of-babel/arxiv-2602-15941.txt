On the Jacobian of Spec ⁡ 𝐙 ¯ \overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
Alain Connes, Caterina Consani

Abstract
We interpret the structure of the adele class space of the rationals—and specifically its Riemann sector—as the natural monoidal extension of the Picard group of the arithmetic curve Spec ⁡ 𝐙 ¯ \overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}} . We identify the elements of this space with torsion-free rank-1 abelian groups L L endowed with rigidifying data. In the Riemann sector, this data corresponds to a norm, extending the classical notion of metrized line bundles in Arakelov geometry. For the full adele class space, we replace the norm with a group morphism to 𝐑 \mathbf{R} and a combinatorial datum: a parametrization of the roots of unity associated with the character dual of L L . We show that the product of adeles is represented geometrically by the tensor product of these rank-1 groups and their rigidifying structures. The resulting monoid space generalizes the Picard group to the full adelic context by incorporating the singular strata required for the spectral realization of L L -functions. Key Words. Adele class space, Riemann sector, Arakelov geometry, Picard and Jacobian monoids, torsion-free rank-1 groups, metrized line bundles, ideles and adeles, scaling action, arithmetic compactification. Introduction

The starting point of this paper is the apparent paradox concerning the genus of the arithmetic curve
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
. On one hand, the classical Jacobian of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
—its ideal class group—is trivial, suggesting a curve of genus zero. On the other hand, the spectral realization of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function
[
2
]
, as well as the explicit formulas of Riemann-Weil
[
11
]
, strongly suggest that
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
should be regarded as a curve of infinite genus.
To resolve this tension, we extend the classical definitions of the Jacobian and Picard group of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
to incorporate the infinite nature of the arithmetic curve (§
2.3
). The new arithmetic Jacobian is the natural ambient space for studying étale covers—both geometrically and adelically—via pullbacks using an analogue of the Abel-Jacobi map.

The first step in this construction is to extend the notion of a divisor on
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
. A classical divisor is a formal sum
D=\sum n_{p}[p]
where the coefficients
n_{p}
are integers and vanish for all but finitely many
p
. To accommodate the expected infinite genus, we relax these conditions in two ways.

1) We allow the coefficients
n_{p}
to take values in
\mathbf{Z}\cup\{\infty\}

and

2) We allow the support of a divisor to be infinite, imposing only the condition that
n_{p}
is negative for at most finitely many primes.

This extended definition of divisors is justified by a canonical isomorphism with the lattice of rank-1 subgroups of
\mathbf{Q}
(Proposition
2.4
). To any such generalized divisor
D=\sum n_{p}[p]
, one associates the module of global sections:

\mathcal{L}(D)=\{q\in\mathbf{Q}\mid v_{p}(q)\geq-n_{p}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall p\}.

Conversely, any rank-1 subgroup
L\subset\mathbf{Q}
determines a divisor
D
by setting
n_{p}=-\inf\{v_{p}(x)\mid x\in L\}
, and one shows that
L=\mathcal{L}(D)
.
In this framework, the classical notion of linear equivalence of divisors corresponds precisely to the isomorphism of the associated subgroups of
\mathbf{Q}
. Furthermore, the addition of divisors corresponds to the tensor product of these groups:

\mathcal{L}(D_{1}+D_{2})\cong\mathcal{L}(D_{1})\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathcal{L}(D_{2})
.

H_{N}
may have poles of order at most
n_{p}
at
p
(
i.e.
denominators divisible at most by
p^{n_{p}}
).

If
n_{p}=+\infty
, elements in
H_{N}
may have poles of arbitrary order at
p
.

The next statement explains why supernatural numbers are the natural parameters in this setting.
They are equivalent to prescribing, prime by prime, the denominators that are allowed in a subgroup
of
\mathbf{Q}
containing
\mathbf{Z}

(cf.
[
9
]
, Chap. XIII, §2).

Proposition 2.2
.

The map
N\mapsto H_{N}
defines a bijection between
{\mathbf{N}}^{sup}
and the set of all subgroups of
\mathbf{Q}
containing
\mathbf{Z}
.

Proof.

For
N=\prod_{p}p^{n_{p}}
the set

H_{N}=\{x\in\mathbf{Q}\mid v_{p}(x)\geq-n_{p}\ \forall p\}

is clearly an additive subgroup of
\mathbf{Q}
and contains
\mathbf{Z}
, so the map is well-defined.

Conversely, let
H\subset\mathbf{Q}
be a subgroup with
\mathbf{Z}\subset H
. For each prime
p
set

n_{p}(H)=\sup\{-v_{p}(x)\mid x\in H\}\in{\mathbf{N}}\cup\{+\infty\},\hskip 10.00002ptN(H)=\prod_{p}p^{n_{p}(H)}\in{\mathbf{N}}^{\sup}.

Then
H\subseteq H_{N(H)}
is immediate from the definition of
n_{p}(H)
.

For the reverse inclusion, take
x\in H_{N(H)}
and write
x=a/b
in lowest terms, with

b=\prod_{p}p^{k_{p}}
and
k_{p}=-v_{p}(x)
. The condition
x\in H_{N(H)}
means
k_{p}\leq n_{p}(H)
for
all
p\mid b
. For each such
p
choose
x_{p}\in H
with
-v_{p}(x_{p})\geq k_{p}
; multiplying by a suitable
integer (allowed since
\mathbf{Z}\subset H
) we may assume
x_{p}=u_{p}/p^{k_{p}}
with
u_{p}\in\mathbf{Z}
and
p\nmid u_{p}
.
Let
S
be the finite set of primes dividing
b
, and put
b_{S}:=\prod_{p\in S}p^{k_{p}}=b
.

Choose integers
c_{p}
such that

\sum_{p\in S}c_{p}\,u_{p}\,\frac{b}{p^{k_{p}}}\equiv a\pmod{b}.

This is possible because each factor
\frac{b}{p^{k_{p}}}
is invertible modulo
p^{k_{p}}
, hence one
can solve the congruences modulo each
p^{k_{p}}
and combine them by the Chinese remainder theorem.
Then, define

y=\sum_{p\in S}c_{p}\,x_{p}\in H.

By construction,
y\equiv a/b\pmod{\mathbf{Z}}
,
i.e.

y-\frac{a}{b}\in\mathbf{Z}\subset H
, hence
x=a/b\in H
.
Therefore
H_{N(H)}\subseteq H
, so
H=H_{N(H)}
.

Finally, the assignments
N\mapsto H_{N}
and
H\mapsto N(H)
are inverse to each other by construction,
so the correspondence is bijective.
∎

2.2
Arithmetic divisors on
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}

We begin by extending the classical notion of a divisor to accommodate the "infinite genus" nature of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
.

Definition 2.3
.

An arithmetic divisor on
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
is a formal sum:

D=\sum_{p}n_{p}[p]

where the coefficients
n_{p}
belong to
\mathbf{Z}\cup\{+\infty\}
and satisfy the condition that
n_{p}<0
for at most finitely many rational primes
p
.

We denote by
\operatorname{Div}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
the set of arithmetic divisors. An arithmetic divisor is called
effective
if
n_{p}\geq 0
for all
p
.

The set of effective arithmetic divisors is naturally identified with the set of
supernatural numbers
{\mathbf{N}}^{\sup}
, via the correspondence

\prod_{p}p^{n_{p}}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \sum_{p}n_{p}[p],

which amounts to passing from the multiplicative to the additive encoding of
valuation data. This identification reflects the fact that both notions encode, for each prime
p
,
the maximal allowed order of a pole, with no finiteness restriction on the positive
part.

To every arithmetic divisor
D
, we associate the module of global sections of the corresponding line bundle. This is the subgroup
\mathcal{L}(D)\subset\mathbf{Q}
defined by:

\mathcal{L}(D)=\{x\in\mathbf{Q}\mid v_{p}(x)\geq-n_{p}\text{ for all primes }p\}.

Here,
v_{p}(x)
is the standard
p
-adic valuation. The condition
v_{p}(x)\geq-n_{p}
has a clear geometric interpretation:

If
n_{p}>0
(finite), sections in
\mathcal{L}(D)
are allowed to have poles of order at most
n_{p}
at
p
.

If
n_{p}=+\infty
, the sections are allowed to have poles of arbitrary order at
p
(essential singularity).

If
n_{p}<0
, the sections must have a zero of order at least
|n_{p}|
at
p
.

As in the classical theory of divisors on curves, a divisor determines the space of
global sections of the associated line bundle.
In the present arithmetic setting, this assignment completely characterizes
rank-1 subgroups of
\mathbf{Q}
, as stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

The map
D\mapsto\mathcal{L}(D)
defines a bijection between the set of generalized divisors
\operatorname{Div}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
and the set of rank-1 subgroups of
\mathbf{Q}
.

Proof.

The map is well-defined: for an arithmetic divisor

D=\sum_{p}n_{p}[p]
, the set
\mathcal{L}(D)

is clearly an additive subgroup of
\mathbf{Q}
. Since
n_{p}<0
for only finitely many primes,
there exists a nonzero
x\in\mathbf{Q}
satisfying these inequalities, so
\mathcal{L}(D)
has
rank
1
.

Conversely, let
H\subset\mathbf{Q}
be a rank-
1
subgroup. For each prime
p
, define

n_{p}:=-\inf\{v_{p}(x)\mid x\in H\}\in\mathbf{Z}\cup\{+\infty\},

and set
D_{H}:=\sum_{p}n_{p}[p]
.
Since
H\subset\mathbf{Q}
, for any fixed
p
the set
\{v_{p}(x)\mid x\in H\}
is bounded below,
so
n_{p}>-\infty
.
Moreover, choose a nonzero element
q\in H
.
Then for all but finitely many primes
p
we have
v_{p}(q)\geq 0
, which implies

v_{p}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in H
and hence
n_{p}\geq 0
.
Thus
D_{H}
is an arithmetic divisor.

By construction, an element
x\in\mathbf{Q}
lies in
\mathcal{L}(D_{H})
if and only if

v_{p}(x)\geq\inf\{v_{p}(y)\mid y\in H\}
for all
p
, which is equivalent to
x\in H
.
Hence
\mathcal{L}(D_{H})=H
, and the correspondence is bijective.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Under the bijection stated in the proposition:

-

The effective divisors (supernatural numbers) correspond to subgroups
H\subset\mathbf{Q}
such that
\mathbf{Z}\subseteq H
.

-

The classical divisors (finite support, integer coefficients) correspond to fractional ideals of
\mathbf{Z}
.

-

The divisor
D_{p}=\infty\cdot[p]
supported solely at
p
corresponds to the group
\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
.

2.3
Linear equivalence and the Picard monoid

A non trivial abelian group is isomorphic to a subgroup of the additive group
\mathbf{Q}

if and only if it is torsion free and of rank-1.
Moreover, two nonzero subgroups of
\mathbf{Q}
are isomorphic if and only if they differ
by multiplication by a nonzero rational number.
Explicitly, for
M,N\in{\mathbf{N}}^{sup}
:

H_{M}\cong H_{N}\iff\exists\,q\in\mathbf{Q}^{\times}\text{ such that }H_{M}=q\,H_{N}.

In valuation-theoretic terms, an element
x\in\mathbf{Q}
belongs to
qH_{N}
if and only if

x/q\in H_{N}
, that is,

v_{p}(x)-v_{p}(q)\geq-n_{p}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all primes }p.

Equivalently, the corresponding valuation bounds satisfy

m_{p}=n_{p}-v_{p}(q),

with the convention
\infty+k=\infty
.
In divisor language, this is precisely the relation of linear equivalence.
The two arithmetic divisors
D_{M}=\sum_{p}m_{p}[p]
and
D_{N}=\sum_{p}n_{p}[p]
satisfy

D_{M}\sim D_{N}\iff D_{M}=D_{N}-\rm{div}(q),

where
\rm{div}(q)=\sum_{p}v_{p}(q)[p]
denotes the principal arithmetic divisor associated
to
q\in\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
.

Definition 2.6
.

The arithmetic Picard monoid of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
is the quotient

\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})={\rm Div}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})\big/{\rm PDiv}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}),

where
{\rm PDiv}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
denotes the subgroup of principal arithmetic divisors.

Proposition 2.7
.

The assignment
D\mapsto\mathcal{L}(D)
induces a bijection between the arithmetic
Picard monoid
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
and the set of isomorphism classes of torsion-free
rank-1 abelian groups.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of the correspondence between arithmetic divisors
and rank-1 subgroups of
\mathbf{Q}
, together with the identification of linear equivalence
with scaling by
\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

Assuming the Axiom of Choice, the set of isomorphism classes of torsion-free
rank-1 abelian groups has cardinality equal to that of the continuum.
Nevertheless, Proposition
2.7
shows that this space admits no effective
classification by real-valued invariants.
More precisely, although its set-theoretic cardinality is that of
\mathbf{R}
, the

effective cardinality
of this quotient is strictly larger than the continuum.
This phenomenon is a consequence of the ergodic nature of the linear equivalence
relation on
{\rm Div}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
.
Indeed, one can equip
{\rm Div}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
with a probability measure for which the linear
equivalence relation is ergodic
2
2

2

That is, any measurable subset of

{\rm Div}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
that is a union of equivalence classes has measure
0
or
1
.
.
It follows that any measurable map from the quotient space to
\mathbf{R}
is almost
everywhere constant, and therefore cannot separate equivalence classes.

It follows that the moduli space of rank-1 torsion-free groups lies beyond the
scope of classification by classical invariants.

2.4
Tensor product and addition of divisor classes

As in algebraic geometry addition of divisors induces a monoidal structure on divisor
classes, in the present arithmetic setting this operation admits a concrete
interpretation in terms of tensor products of rank-1 subgroups of
\mathbf{Q}
.

One defines the sum of two arithmetic divisors
D=(n_{p})
and
D^{\prime}=(n^{\prime}_{p})
, by the rule

(D+D^{\prime})_{p}=n_{p}+n^{\prime}_{p}.

This operation induces a monoid structure on
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
that is interpreted in terms of the associated groups.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be arithmetic divisors on
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
, and let

H_{1},H_{2}\subset\mathbf{Q}
be the associated rank-1 subgroups.
Then the subgroup associated to the sum
D_{1}+D_{2}
is

H_{D_{1}+D_{2}}=H_{1}\cdot H_{2}\;\cong\;H_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}H_{2},

where the tensor product is canonically identified with the product inside
\mathbf{Q}
.

Proof.

Recall that for an arithmetic divisor
D=\sum_{p}n_{p}(D)[p]
one has

H_{D}=\{x\in\mathbf{Q}\mid v_{p}(x)\geq-n_{p}(D)\ \text{for all }p\}.

For rank-1 subgroups of
\mathbf{Q}
, the tensor product over
\mathbf{Z}
is canonically identified
with the subgroup generated by products:

H_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}H_{2}\;\cong\;H_{1}\cdot H_{2}:=\Bigl\langle xy\mid x\in H_{1},\;y\in H_{2}\Bigr\rangle\subset\mathbf{Q}.

We first show the inclusion
H_{1}\cdot H_{2}\subseteq H_{D_{1}+D_{2}}
.
Let
x\in H_{1}
and
y\in H_{2}
. For every prime
p
,

v_{p}(x)\geq-n_{p}(D_{1}),\hskip 20.00003ptv_{p}(y)\geq-n_{p}(D_{2}),

hence

v_{p}(xy)=v_{p}(x)+v_{p}(y)\geq-\bigl(n_{p}(D_{1})+n_{p}(D_{2})\bigr)=-n_{p}(D_{1}+D_{2}).

Since the
p
-adic valuation of a sum is bounded below by the minimum of the valuations
of its summands, the same inequality holds for any finite
\mathbf{Z}
-linear combination of
such products. Thus
H_{1}\cdot H_{2}\subseteq H_{D_{1}+D_{2}}
.

For the reverse inclusion, let
z\in H_{D_{1}+D_{2}}
.
Write
z=\pm\prod_{p}p^{-k_{p}}
with
k_{p}=-v_{p}(z)
.
The condition
z\in H_{D_{1}+D_{2}}
means

k_{p}\leq n_{p}(D_{1})+n_{p}(D_{2})\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all }p.

For each prime
p
, choose integers
k_{1,p}\leq n_{p}(D_{1})
and

k_{2,p}\leq n_{p}(D_{2})
such that
k_{p}=k_{1,p}+k_{2,p}

(which is always possible, with the convention
\infty+k=\infty
).
Define

x:=\pm\prod_{p}p^{-k_{1,p}}\in H_{1},\hskip 20.00003pty:=\prod_{p}p^{-k_{2,p}}\in H_{2}.

Then
z=xy
, so
z\in H_{1}\cdot H_{2}
.
This proves
H_{D_{1}+D_{2}}\subseteq H_{1}\cdot H_{2}
.

Therefore
H_{D_{1}+D_{2}}=H_{1}\cdot H_{2}\cong H_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}H_{2}
, and the addition of
arithmetic divisors corresponds to the tensor product of the associated rank-one
groups.
∎

2.5
Adelic description of arithmetic divisors

Let
\mathbb{A}_{f}
denote the ring of finite rational adeles, and let

\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\subset\mathbb{A}_{f}
be the compact subring of profinite integers,
so that
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}=\prod_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.
To a finite adele
a=(a_{p})\in\mathbb{A}_{f}
we associate the family of valuations

\tilde{\Phi}(a):=(v_{p}(a_{p}))_{p},

with the convention
v_{p}(0)=\infty
.
Since
a_{p}\in\mathbf{Z}_{p}
for almost all primes
p
(by definition of the restricted product in adeles), one has
v_{p}(a_{p})\geq 0

for almost all
p
, so
\tilde{\Phi}(a)
defines an arithmetic divisor.

Furthermore, multiplication by a unit
u=(u_{p})\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
does not affect
valuations, since
v_{p}(u_{p})=0
for all
p
.
Hence
\tilde{\Phi}
factors through the quotient and induces a map

\Phi:\mathbb{A}_{f}/\hat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}\longrightarrow{\rm Div}(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})).

(5)

The map
\Phi
is surjective: given an arithmetic divisor

D=(n_{p})
, the adele
a=(a_{p})
with
a_{p}=p^{n_{p}}
(with the convention

p^{\infty}=0
) is a preimage.
It is also injective because two elements of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
with the same valuation
differ by multiplication by a unit in
\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. It then follows that
\Phi
is a bijection.
Quotienting further by the diagonal action of
\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
, one obtains
a natural bijection

\Phi:\mathbf{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{f}/\hat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}\;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\;{\rm Div}(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{Z}))/{\rm PDiv}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})=\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}).

(6)

Remark 2.10
.

Let
x=(x_{p})\in\mathbb{A}_{f}
.
The rank-1 subgroup of
\mathbf{Q}
associated to the divisor
\Phi(x)
is

H=\{q\in\mathbf{Q}\mid xq\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\}.

Indeed,
xq\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
if and only if
v_{p}(x_{p})+v_{p}(q)\geq 0
for all
p
.

The ring
\mathbb{A}_{f}
is a commutative monoid under componentwise multiplication.
This operation is compatible with the equivalence relation defined by the
action of the group

G=\mathbf{Q}_{+}^{\times}\times\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times},

and therefore it descends to a well-defined multiplication on the quotient:

[a]\cdot[b]:=[ab].

We derive the following

Proposition 2.11
.

The map
\Phi
in (
6
) is an isomorphism of commutative
monoids: multiplication of finite adeles corresponds to addition of generalized
divisors.

Proof.

For each prime
p
the valuation is additive,

v_{p}(a_{p}b_{p})=v_{p}(a_{p})+v_{p}(b_{p}),

with the convention
\infty+x=\infty
.
Thus, if
D_{a}
and
D_{b}
are the divisors associated to finite adeles
a
and
b
, one has

D_{ab}=\sum_{p}\bigl(v_{p}(a_{p})+v_{p}(b_{p})\bigr)[p]=D_{a}+D_{b}.

∎

2.6
The arithmetic Abel–Jacobi map and boundary points

In classical algebraic geometry, the Abel-Jacobi map
\phi:C\to\operatorname{Jac}(C)
sends a point
P
on a curve
C
to the isomorphism class of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(P)
corresponding to the divisor
[P]
.
On
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})
however, the analogous assignment

p\mapsto[\mathcal{O}(p)]
is trivial, since the ideal
p\mathbf{Z}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{Z}

as a rank-1
\mathbf{Z}
-module.
Thus, in order to obtain a nontrivial arithmetic analogue of the Abel–Jacobi map,
one must allow the image to lie at the boundary of the moduli space of torsion-free
rank-1 groups.

Definition 2.12

(Arithmetic Abel–Jacobi map)
.

The
arithmetic Abel–Jacobi map
is the injection

\Theta:\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})

defined by

\Theta(p)=H_{p}:=\mathbf{Z}[1/p]\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for a prime }p,

and

\Theta(\eta)=\mathbf{Z},

where
\eta
denotes the generic point of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
.

In terms of generalized divisors, the map
\Theta
sends the generic point
\eta

to the trivial divisor, and a prime
p
to the divisor with infinite coefficient
at
p
,

p\longmapsto D_{\infty}(p):=\infty\cdot[p].

Injectivity.

For distinct primes
p\neq q
, the groups
\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
and
\mathbf{Z}[1/q]
are not isomorphic:
the former is
p
-divisible while the latter is not.
Hence
\Theta
separates points of
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})
.

Boundary interpretation.

Although the primes
p
are interior points of the scheme
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})
,
their images under
\Theta
correspond to arithmetic divisors with infinite
multiplicity (representing singularities or poles of infinite order), and therefore lie on the boundary of the Picard space.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g
over a field
k
.
Recall that the Jacobian variety
J(C)
classifies isomorphism classes of degree-zero line bundles on
C
.
The relation between
C
and
J(C)
is governed by the Jacobi inversion theorem,
which identifies the
g
-th symmetric power
C^{(g)}
as a fundamental configuration space
for the Abel–Jacobi map:

\Sigma:C^{(g)}\longrightarrow J(C)

\{P_{1},\dots,P_{g}\}\longmapsto\mathcal{O}(P_{1}+\dots+P_{g}-gP_{0})

where
P_{0}
is a fixed base point.

In our arithmetic setting,
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
should be regarded as a curve of infinite genus.
Accordingly, one is led to replace the finite symmetric power
C^{(g)}

by an infinite configuration space.
We model this arithmetic configuration space as follows.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
be the set of all subsets
S
of prime numbers. This corresponds to the set of reduced effective divisors with infinite coefficients:

S\longleftrightarrow D_{S}=\sum_{p\in S}\infty\cdot[p]

Definition 2.14

(Arithmetic Abel-Jacobi map on configurations)
.

We define the map

\Theta:\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})\to\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})\hskip 20.00003pt\Theta(S)=\mathbf{Z}_{S}=\mathbf{Z}[1/p\mid p\in S]

Theorem 2.15

(Tensor structure on configurations)
.

The map
\Theta
transforms the union of sets (addition of divisors) into the tensor product of points in the Jacobian (multiplication of subgroups):

\Theta(S_{1}\cup S_{2})\cong\Theta(S_{1})\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\Theta(S_{2})

Proof.

This follows from the universal property of localization:

\mathbf{Z}[S_{1}^{-1}]\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{Z}[S_{2}^{-1}]\cong\mathbf{Z}[(S_{1}\cup S_{2})^{-1}],

which is the arithmetic analogue of the classical identity

\mathcal{O}(D_{1}+D_{2})\cong\mathcal{O}(D_{1})\otimes\mathcal{O}(D_{2})
.
∎

The map
\Theta
can thus be viewed as the restriction of the Abel–Jacobi map
to what we call the
idempotent stratum
of the arithmetic Jacobian. In other words:

In
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
, the elements
H_{p}=\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
are idempotent (
H_{p}\otimes H_{p}\cong H_{p}
).

The map
\Theta
sends a configuration of primes
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}
to the point
H_{p_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes H_{p_{k}}
.

For a smooth projective curve
C
of genus
g
, the Abel–Jacobi map identifies the

rational
equivalence class of the symmetric power
C^{(g)}
with the Jacobian

J(C)
; in particular, it is surjective, as follows from the Riemann–Roch theorem.
By contrast, in the arithmetic case the curve
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
should be regarded as having
infinite genus, and the corresponding Abel–Jacobi map has image equal to the
idempotent elements of the Picard monoid
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
.

Theorem 2.16
.

The image of the map
\Theta:\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})\to\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\,\mathbf{Z})
is exactly the set of idempotent elements of the monoid
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\,\mathbf{Z})
.

\text{Image}(\Theta)=\operatorname{Idem}(\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\,\mathbf{Z}))

Proof.

We first show that the image of
\Theta
consists of idempotents.

Let
S\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})
be a set of primes. Let
H=\Theta(S)=\mathbf{Z}_{S}=\mathbf{Z}[S^{-1}]
.
Since
\mathbf{Z}_{S}
is a subring of
\mathbf{Q}
, it is closed under multiplication. Thus, the product of subgroups satisfies:

H\cdot H=\{xy\mid x,y\in H\}=H

Since
H\otimes H\cong H\cdot H
, we have
[H]^{2}=[H]
. Thus, every element in the image of
\Theta
is idempotent.

Next, we check that every idempotent in
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\,\mathbf{Z})
is in the image of
\Theta
.

Let
[H]\in\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\,\mathbf{Z})
be an idempotent element. Let
D=(n_{p})
be a generalized divisor representing
H
.
The condition
[H]^{2}=[H]
implies that
H\otimes H\cong H
. In terms of generalized divisors, this means:

2D\sim D
.
By definition of linear equivalence, there exists a rational number
q\in\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
such that:

2D=D+\operatorname{div}(q)

Looking at the coefficient for each prime
p
:

2n_{p}=n_{p}+v_{p}(q)

We analyze this equation for each
p
:

Case
n_{p}=\infty
.
The equation
2(\infty)=\infty+v_{p}(q)
is satisfied for any finite
v_{p}(q)
.

Case
n_{p}\in\mathbf{Z}
.
We can subtract
n_{p}
from both sides (since it is finite), yielding:

n_{p}=v_{p}(q)

This implies that the divisor
D
splits into an infinite part and a principal part:

D=\sum_{n_{p}=\infty}\infty\cdot[p]+\sum_{n_{p}\in\mathbf{Z}}v_{p}(q)\cdot[p]

D=D_{\infty}+\operatorname{div}(q)

where
D_{\infty}
corresponds to the set
S=\{p\mid n_{p}=\infty\}
.
Thus
D
is linearly equivalent to the divisor

D_{\infty}=\sum_{p\in S}\infty\cdot[p],\hskip 20.00003ptS=\{p\mid n_{p}=\infty\},

and the corresponding subgroup is
\mathbf{Z}_{S}
.
Hence
[H]=\Theta(S)
.
∎

The set of idempotents in a commutative monoid naturally forms a join-semilattice with the order
e\leq f\iff ef=f
. Keeping in mind that:

In
\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
, the operation is the union
S\cup T
.

In
\rm{Idem}(\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\,\mathbf{Z}))
, the operation is the tensor product
H_{1}\otimes H_{2}
,

it follows that the map
\Theta
is an isomorphism of semilattices:

\Theta(S\cup T)=\mathbf{Z}_{S\cup T}\cong\mathbf{Z}_{S}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{Z}_{T}=\Theta(S)\cdot\Theta(T).

Remark 2.17
.

The range of the map
\Theta:\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})\to\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
is a subset of
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
whose effective cardinality is that of the continuum, and hence is strictly smaller than the effective cardinality of
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
as explained in Remark
2.8
.

3
The arithmetic Picard monoid of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}

We extend the divisor-theoretic and Picard-theoretic constructions
developed for
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
to its completion
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
, where the archimedean place is taken
into account.
The guiding principle is to give an intrinsic geometric description of the
arithmetic
Picard monoid
that does not rely a priori on adeles, rather it generalizes the
notion of a metrized line bundle familiar from Arakelov geometry.
We introduce arithmetic divisors on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
as rank-1 torsion-free groups equipped
with a (possibly degenerate) metric, define the corresponding arithmetic Picard
monoid, and then relate this intrinsic picture to an adelic description.
A key outcome is the construction of a canonical invariant subgroup of
\mathbf{R}
attached
to each arithmetic divisor, which allows one to reconstruct the divisor from its
value spectrum when the semi-norm is a norm.
We describe the natural commutative monoid structure on
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
and
show its compatibility at the intrinsic, adelic, and spectral levels.

3.1
Arithmetic divisors on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}

We begin by defining arithmetic divisors on the completed curve
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
.
These objects should be viewed as the natural analogues of metrized line bundles in
Arakelov geometry, with the important distinction that we allow degenerate metrics.

Definition 3.1
.

An arithmetic divisor on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
is a pair
\mathcal{D}=(L,\|\cdot\|)
where:

•

L
is a torsion-free abelian group of rank 1.

•

\|\cdot\|
is a semi-norm on the real vector space
L_{\mathbf{R}}=L\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{R}
which is proportional to the standard absolute value
|\cdot|
on
\mathbf{R}
(via any identification
L_{\mathbf{R}}\cong\mathbf{R}
)

The condition on the semi-norm means that there exists an isomorphism
\phi:L_{\mathbf{R}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{R}
and a constant
\lambda\geq 0
such that
\|v\|=\lambda|\phi(v)|
for all
v\in L_{\mathbf{R}}
.

If
\lambda>0
, the metric is non-degenerate (Hermitian).

If
\lambda=0
, the metric is singular (the zero semi-norm).

3.2
Intrinsic product

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}=(L_{1},\|\cdot\|_{1})
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}=(L_{2},\|\cdot\|_{2})
be two arithmetic divisors on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
. We define their product
\mathcal{D}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{2}
as the pair
(L,\|\cdot\|)
where:

-

The group
L
is the tensor product:
L=L_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}L_{2}
.

-

The semi-norm
\|\cdot\|
on
L_{\mathbf{R}}\cong(L_{1})_{\mathbf{R}}\otimes(L_{2})_{\mathbf{R}}
is the tensor product of the semi-norms. Explicitly, for pure tensors
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}
, we have:

\|v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\|=\|v_{1}\|_{1}\cdot\|v_{2}\|_{2}.

Since
L_{1}
, and
L_{2}
are rank-1 torsion-free groups, their tensor product is also a rank-1 torsion-free group. The product of their semi-norms is well-defined and proportional to the standard absolute value on
\mathbf{R}
.

3.3
The Picard monoid
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})

Having defined arithmetic divisors on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
, and their product, we now pass to their isomorphism
classes.
This leads to the arithmetic Picard monoid as the moduli space of these objects, which plays the role of the Picard group of
the completed curve.

Definition 3.2
.

Two arithmetic divisors
\mathcal{D}_{1}=(L_{1},\|\cdot\|_{1})
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}=(L_{2},\|\cdot\|_{2})
on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
are isomorphic if there exists a group isomorphism
\psi:L_{1}\to L_{2}
that is an isometry, i.e. for all
x\in L_{1}
, one has:

\|\psi(x)\|_{2}=\|x\|_{1}.

Here, we implicitly extend
\psi
to the real vector spaces.

Definition 3.3
.

The arithmetic Picard monoid
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
is the set of isomorphism classes of arithmetic divisors on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
endowed with the product
\mathcal{D}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{2}
.

3.4
Adelic description

We next relate the intrinsic definition of
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
to an adelic
parametrization.
This comparison clarifies how the finite and archimedean components jointly encode
the arithmetic data of a divisor class.

Let
\Lambda
be the map which associates to a rational adele
a=(a_{f},a_{\infty})
the pair consisting of:

•

The group
L_{a}:=\{q\in\mathbf{Q}\mid a_{f}q\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\}
,

•

The semi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{a}
on
L_{a}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{R}
defined by
\|v\|_{a}=|a_{\infty}|\cdot|v|
(via the canonical identification
L_{a}\otimes\mathbf{R}\cong\mathbf{Q}\otimes\mathbf{R}\cong\mathbf{R}
).

We denote with
X_{\mathbf{Q}}=\mathbf{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}/\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
the
Riemann sector
of the adele class space
\mathbb{A}
of the rationals, endowed with the monoid structure induced by the multiplication of adeles. We now prove Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.4
.

The map
\Lambda
induces a canonical monoid isomorphism
X_{\mathbf{Q}}\cong\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
.

Proof.

We proceed in four steps.

1. Well-definedness.

We show that given an adele
a
,
a\mapsto\Lambda(a)
depends only on the class of
a
in
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
.
First, let
u\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
. Since units do not affect integrality, we have
L_{ua}=L_{a}
. As
(ua)_{\infty}=a_{\infty}
, the norms coincide, so
\Lambda(ua)=\Lambda(a)
.
Next, let
r\in\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
and consider
a^{\prime}=ra
. The group
L_{a^{\prime}}
is defined by the condition
q(ra)_{f}\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
, which is equivalent to
qr\in L_{a}
. Thus
L_{a^{\prime}}=r^{-1}L_{a}
.
The archimedean component transforms as
a^{\prime}_{\infty}=ra_{\infty}
. Consider the multiplication map
\psi:L_{a^{\prime}}\to L_{a}
defined by
\psi(q)=rq
. This is a group isomorphism satisfying:

\|\psi(q)\|_{a}=|a_{\infty}|\,|rq|=|ra_{\infty}|\,|q|=|a^{\prime}_{\infty}|\,|q|=\|q\|_{a^{\prime}}.

Thus,
\psi
defines an isomorphism of arithmetic divisors
\Lambda(a^{\prime})\cong\Lambda(a)
. This confirms that
\Lambda
descends to the quotient
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
.

2. Injectivity.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{A}
be such that
\Lambda(a)\cong\Lambda(b)
. Let
\psi:L_{a}\to L_{b}
be an isomorphism compatible with the norms. Since
L_{a}
and
L_{b}
are rank-1 subgroups of
\mathbf{Q}
, there exists a unique
r\in\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
such that
\psi(x)=rx
for all
x\in L_{a}
.
Define
b^{\prime}=rb
. As shown in step 1, the map
\phi(y)=r^{-1}y
provides an isomorphism
\Lambda(b)\cong\Lambda(b^{\prime})
. Consequently, the composition
\rho=\phi\circ\psi
is an automorphism of
\Lambda(a)
to
\Lambda(b^{\prime})
given by
\rho(x)=x
.
The equality of the underlying groups
L_{a}=L_{b^{\prime}}
implies that the finite adeles
a_{f}
and
b^{\prime}_{f}
generate the same lattice, so there exists
u\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
such that
a_{f}=ub^{\prime}_{f}
.
The equality of the norms
\|x\|_{a}=\|x\|_{b^{\prime}}
implies
|a_{\infty}||x|=|b^{\prime}_{\infty}||x|
, hence
|a_{\infty}|=|b^{\prime}_{\infty}|
. Thus
a_{\infty}=\epsilon b^{\prime}_{\infty}
for some
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
.
It follows that
a=(u,\epsilon)b^{\prime}=(u,\epsilon)rb
, so
a
and
b
represent the same class in
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
.

3. Surjectivity.

This follows from Proposition
2.11
. Given any arithmetic divisor
(L,\|\cdot\|)
with
L\subset\mathbf{Q}
, there exists a finite adele
a_{f}
such that
L=L_{a}
. The norm on
L\otimes\mathbf{R}\cong\mathbf{R}
is necessarily of the form
\|x\|=\lambda|x|
for some
\lambda\geq 0
. Choosing
a_{\infty}
such that
|a_{\infty}|=\lambda
, we obtain
\Lambda(a)\cong(L,\|\cdot\|)
.

4. Monoid Structure.

Proposition
2.11
establishes that the map
a\mapsto L_{a}
is a monoid homomorphism, identifying the product of adeles with the product of subgroups (which is isomorphic to the tensor product). For the norms, we observe:

\|x\otimes y\|_{ab}=|(ab)_{\infty}||xy|=(|a_{\infty}||x|)(|b_{\infty}||y|)=\|x\|_{a}\|y\|_{b}.

Thus,
\Lambda
preserves the tensor product structure.
∎

3.5
The adelic invariant subgroup
I_{a}

Let
a=(a_{f},a_{\infty})\in\mathbb{A}
be a rational adele representing a divisor class in
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
(in view of Theorem
3.4
). While the group
L_{a}\subset\mathbf{Q}
defined by the finite part
a_{f}
of the adele depends on the representative
a\in\mathbb{A}
, we shall construct a
canonical realization
of this group inside
\mathbf{R}
that serves as a complete invariant for the class.

Definition 3.5
.

Given a rational adele
a\in\mathbb{A}
, we define the subgroup
I_{a}\subset\mathbf{R}
as the image of the group
L_{a}\subset\mathbf{Q}
scaled by the archimedean component
a_{\infty}

I_{a}:=a_{\infty}L_{a}=\{x\in\mathbf{R}\mid x=a_{\infty}q,\text{ and }qa_{f}\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\}.

Theorem 3.6

(Invariance)
.

The subgroup
I_{a}\subset\mathbf{R}
depends only on the class of
a\in\mathbb{A}
in the double quotient
\mathbf{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}/\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the calculations in the proof of Theorem
3.4
. Invariance under
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
is immediate as units do not affect the lattice
L_{a}
. For invariance under
\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
, if
a^{\prime}=ra
with
r\in\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
, we established that
L_{a^{\prime}}=r^{-1}L_{a}
and
a^{\prime}_{\infty}=ra_{\infty}
. Consequently,
I_{a^{\prime}}=(ra_{\infty})(r^{-1}L_{a})=a_{\infty}L_{a}=I_{a}
.
Alternatively, this invariance is a consequence of the intrinsic characterization of
I_{a}
via the value spectrum given in Lemma
3.8
below.
∎

Next, we reformulate the adelic invariant subgroup in intrinsic terms, using only the metric
structure of an arithmetic divisor
\mathcal{D}=(L,\|\cdot\|)\in\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
.
This leads to the notion of the value spectrum and allows for a reconstruction
theorem.

Definition 3.7
.

The value spectrum of an arithmetic divisor
\mathcal{D}=(L,\|\cdot\|)\in\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
is the image of the group
L
under the following norm map:

S(\mathcal{D}):=\{\|x\|\mid x\in L\}\subset[0,\infty).

Let
I(\mathcal{D})\subset\mathbf{R}
be the subgroup generated by the set
S(\mathcal{D})
.
If
\mathcal{D}
is represented by an adele
a\in\mathbb{A}
, then
I(\mathcal{D})
is precisely the invariant subgroup
I_{a}=a_{\infty}L_{a}
defined previously.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{A}
be an adele representing a class
\mathcal{D}
in
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
. Then the value spectrum
S(\mathcal{D})
coincides with the set of non-negative elements of the invariant subgroup
I_{a}
:

S(\mathcal{D})=I_{a}\cap[0,\infty).

Conversely, since the subgroup
I_{a}\subset\mathbf{R}
is symmetric with respect to the origin, it is fully recovered from the spectrum:

I_{a}=S(\mathcal{D})\cup(-S(\mathcal{D})).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(L,\|\cdot\|)
be the class represented by
a=(a_{f},a_{\infty})\in\mathbb{A}
.
By construction (cf. Definition
3.5
), the finite component
a_{f}
determines a
rank-
1
subgroup

L_{a}:=\{q\in\mathbf{Q}\mid q\,a_{f}\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\}\subset\mathbf{Q},

and the associated invariant subgroup of
\mathbf{R}
is

I_{a}=a_{\infty}L_{a}\subset\mathbf{R}.

Under the identification of
L_{\mathbf{R}}
with
\mathbf{R}
coming from the chosen representative

a
(i.e. the embedding
L\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Q}
and the scaling factor
a_{\infty}
),
the norm is given by

\|q\|=|a_{\infty}q|\hskip 20.00003pt(q\in L_{a}).

Therefore the value spectrum of
\mathcal{D}
is

S(\mathcal{D})=\{\|q\|\mid q\in L_{a}\}=\{|a_{\infty}q|\mid q\in L_{a}\}=\{|x|\mid x\in I_{a}\}.

Since
I_{a}
is a subgroup of
\mathbf{R}
, it is stable under
x\mapsto-x
, hence

\{|x|\mid x\in I_{a}\}=I_{a}\cap[0,\infty),

which proves the first identity.

For the second statement, the symmetry
I_{a}=-I_{a}
implies that every element

x\in I_{a}
is either in
I_{a}\cap[0,\infty)
or in its negative. Using the first part,
this yields

I_{a}=(I_{a}\cap[0,\infty))\cup-(I_{a}\cap[0,\infty))=S(\mathcal{D})\cup(-S(\mathcal{D})).

∎

The preceding lemma shows that an arithmetic divisor determines a
canonical
value
spectrum inside
\mathbf{R}
. Next we show that
this invariant is complete, provided the metric is not identically zero.

Definition 3.9

(Non-degenerate divisor)
.

An arithmetic divisor
\mathcal{D}=(L,\|\cdot\|)
on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
is
non-degenerate
if the semi-norm
\|\cdot\|
is not identically zero.

Remark 3.10
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is represented by an adele
a\in\mathbb{A}
, non-degeneracy corresponds to the condition
a_{\infty}\neq 0
. In this case, the map
L\to\mathbf{R}
induced by the norm is injective, and
L
is isomorphic to the subgroup
I(\mathcal{D})\subset\mathbf{R}
.

Theorem 3.11

(Reconstruction)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}
be two non-degenerate arithmetic divisors.
Then,
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are isomorphic if and only if their value spectra are identical:

[\mathcal{D}_{1}]=[\mathcal{D}_{2}]\iff S(\mathcal{D}_{1})=S(\mathcal{D}_{2}).

Proof.

“
\Rightarrow
” Isomorphisms are isometries, thus they preserve the set of norms.

“
\Leftarrow
” Suppose
S(\mathcal{D}_{1})=S(\mathcal{D}_{2})
.
By Lemma
3.8
, the invariant subgroups generated by the spectra coincide:

I(\mathcal{D}_{1}):=S(\mathcal{D}_{1})\cup-S(\mathcal{D}_{1})=S(\mathcal{D}_{2})\cup-S(\mathcal{D}_{2})=I(\mathcal{D}_{2}).

Let
I\subset\mathbf{R}
denote this common subgroup. Since the divisors are non-degenerate,
I\neq\{0\}
.
For
k=1,2
, the divisor
\mathcal{D}_{k}=(L_{k},\|\cdot\|_{k})
is canonically isomorphic to the divisor
(I,|\cdot|_{\text{std}})
defined by the subgroup
I
with the standard absolute value induced from
\mathbf{R}
.
Indeed, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{k}
induces an embedding
\iota_{k}:L_{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{R}
whose image is
I
. The map
\iota_{k}
constitutes an isomorphism of arithmetic divisors:

\iota_{k}:(L_{k},\|\cdot\|_{k})\xrightarrow{\sim}(I,|\cdot|_{\text{std}}).

Consequently, we have the isomorphism
\iota_{2}^{-1}\circ\iota_{1}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{D}_{2}
.
∎

3.6
Product of value spectra

Finally, we describe the product operation in terms of the invariant subgroups of
\mathbf{R}
.
Let
I_{1},I_{2}\subset\mathbf{R}
be the invariant subgroups associated to two arithmetic divisors on
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
. The invariant subgroup of the product is the subgroup generated by the product of the sets:

I_{1}\cdot I_{2}:=\langle xy\mid x\in I_{1},y\in I_{2}\rangle.

In terms of the value spectra
S_{i}=I_{i}\cap[0,\infty)
, the spectrum of the product is the closure of the set of products under addition:

S(\mathcal{D}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{2})=\{|z|\mid z\in I_{1}\cdot I_{2}\}.

This confirms that the
reconstruction map

\mathcal{D}\mapsto I(\mathcal{D})=S(\mathcal{D})\cup-S(\mathcal{D})

is a monoid homomorphism from the arithmetic Picard
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
to the monoid of subgroups of
\mathbf{R}
(under the product operation).

4
The arithmetic Jacobian
\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})

We introduce the arithmetic Jacobian of the completed curve

\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
.
While the arithmetic Picard monoid
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
parametrizes rank-
1

torsion-free abelian groups endowed with a (possibly degenerate) metric at the
archimedean place, the Jacobian is obtained by identifying divisors that differ only
by a rescaling of this metric.
From a geometric perspective, this passage amounts to obtain the Jacobian by quotienting the Picard group by the subgroup generated by the divisor of degree
1
used to define the Abel-Jacobi map. In our set-up this is the subgroup
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}\subset\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
and the quotient monoid
\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
is the space
of orbits of the archimedean scaling flow.
We describe the resulting structure explicitly, endow it with a natural monoid law,
and explain how the arithmetic Abel–Jacobi map extends to a map from
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
to
\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
.

4.1
Definition of
\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})

The multiplicative group of positive real numbers
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}
acts naturally on the
arithmetic Picard monoid
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
by rescaling the metric at infinity.
Explicitly, for an arithmetic divisor
\mathcal{D}=(L,\|\cdot\|)
and
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}

one sets

\lambda\cdot(L,\|\cdot\|):=(L,\lambda\|\cdot\|).

Under the adelic identification, this action corresponds to multiplication of the
archimedean component
a_{\infty}
by
\lambda
.

Definition 4.1
.

The
arithmetic Jacobian
of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
is the quotient

\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})=\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})\big/\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}.

This construction fits into an exact sequence of commutative monoids

1\to\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}\to\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})\to\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})\to 1

which is the arithmetic analogue of the classical decomposition of the Picard group
of a complete curve over a finite field into its Jacobian part and a degree
component.

4.2
Structure of
\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})

The structure of
\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
is governed by the behavior of the metric under the
scaling action.
Two qualitatively distinct cases arise.

1.

Finite type (flow lines).

If the metric
\|\cdot\|
is non-zero, its
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}
-orbit consists of all non-zero
metrics on the real line
L_{\mathbf{R}}
.
After passing to the quotient, the resulting class is determined solely by the
isomorphism class of the underlying group
L
.
We denote such an element simply by
L
.

2.

Infinite type (fixed points).

If the metric is the zero semi-norm, it is fixed by the
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}
-action.
The corresponding class records both the group
L
and the degeneracy of the metric.
We denote such an element by
L_{\infty}
.

As a consequence, one obtains a canonical identification

\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})\;\cong\;\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})\times\{0,\infty\},

where
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
is the set of isomorphism classes of torsion-free rank-
1

abelian groups.
The second factor records the archimedean valuation:
0
for non-degenerate metrics
and
\infty
for the degenerate (zero) metric.

4.3
Monoid structure

The commutative monoid structure on
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
induced by tensor product
descends naturally to
\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
.
On the group component it is given by tensor product, while the archimedean component
n_{\infty}\in\{0,\infty\}

follows the logic of an absorbing value:

-

H\cdot K=H\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}K
(
0+0=0
)

-

H\cdot K_{\infty}=(H\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}K)_{\infty}
(
0+\infty=\infty
)

-

H_{\infty}\cdot K_{\infty}=(H\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}K)_{\infty}
(
\infty+\infty=\infty
).

Thus
\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
inherits a natural commutative monoid structure compatible with
its interpretation as a quotient of the arithmetic Picard monoid.

5
Sheaves of modules on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}

In this section we relate the arithmetic Picard monoid
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
to a

\mathbf{F}_{1}
-algebras developed
in
[

.
Our goal is to show that every arithmetic divisor on the completed curve

\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
naturally determines a sheaf of modules over the structure sheaf of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
.
This construction extends the classical correspondence between divisors and
invertible sheaves, while simultaneously encoding both the finite arithmetic data
and the archimedean metric constraint.

5.1
The Structure sheaf

We regard
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
as a topological space whose open sets are complements of
finite sets of closed points (finite primes and possibly the point at infinity).
The structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}}
corresponds to the trivial arithmetic
divisor

\mathbf{1}=(\mathbf{Z},|\cdot|_{\infty}),

where
|\cdot|_{\infty}
denotes the standard absolute value on
\mathbf{R}
.

For an open subset
U\subset\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
, the algebra of sections is defined as a
sub-
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-algebra of
H\mathbf{Q}
as follows.

•

If
\infty\notin U
, then

\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}}(U)=H\mathbf{Z}_{U},

where
\mathbf{Z}_{U}=\mathbf{Z}[S^{-1}]
is the localization of
\mathbf{Z}
where the set of primes is

S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}\setminus U
.

•

If
\infty\in U
, we impose the archimedean metric condition:

\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}}(U)(X)=\left\{\phi\in H\mathbf{Z}_{U\setminus\{\infty\}}(X)\;\middle|\;\sum_{x\in X\setminus\{*\}}|\phi(x)|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

This definition recovers the structure sheaf introduced in

[
4
]
, Proposition 6.3.

5.2
Sheaves associated to arithmetic divisors

We now extend the above construction to an arbitrary
arithmetic divisor

\mathcal{D}=(L,\|\cdot\|)\in\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}).

The finite part of the data is encoded in the subgroup
L\subset\mathbf{Q}
, while the
archimedean contribution is governed by the semi-norm
\|\cdot\|
.

We first associate to
L
a sheaf of abelian groups
\mathcal{L}
on the Zariski
topology of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
.
For an open subset
V\subset\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}
, the group
\mathcal{L}(V)
consists of those
elements of
L
that are regular on
V
, i.e. whose denominators involve only
primes in the complement
V^{c}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(L,\|\cdot\|)
be an arithmetic divisor on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
.
The following construction defines a sheaf

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D})
of
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}}
-modules on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
.

1.

(Finite part)
If an open set
U
does not contain
\infty
, set

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D})(U):=H\mathcal{L}(U).

2.

(Infinite part)
If
U
contains
\infty
, define

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D})(U)(X):=\left\{\phi\in H\mathcal{L}(U\cap\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})(X)\;\middle|\;\sum_{x\in X\setminus\{*\}}\|\phi(x)\|\leq 1\right\}.

Proof.

The functor
H\mathcal{L}(U\cap\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
provides the underlying
\Gamma
-set
structure.
It remains to check that the metric condition at infinity is compatible with the
module structure over
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}}
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}}(U)(Y)
and

\phi\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D})(U)(X)
.
Their smash product acts on
z=(y,x)
by multiplication.
By definition of the semi-norm,

\|\psi(y)\phi(x)\|=|\psi(y)|_{\infty}\,\|\phi(x)\|.

Summing over the smash product yields

\sum_{y,x}\|\psi(y)\phi(x)\|=\left(\sum_{y}|\psi(y)|_{\infty}\right)\left(\sum_{x}\|\phi(x)\|\right)\leq 1\cdot 1=1.

Thus the metric constraint is preserved, and
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D})
defines a
sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}}
-modules.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

This construction uniformly accounts for both finite and infinite types in the
arithmetic Picard. Precisely:

(Finite type)
If the semi-norm
\|\cdot\|
is non-degenerate, the condition

\sum\|\phi(x)\|\leq 1
defines a unit ball of sections, generalizing the classical
Arakelov metric condition.

(Infinite type)
If
\|\cdot\|=0
, the inequality
\sum\|\phi(x)\|\leq 1
is automatically satisfied.
Hence for a divisor of infinite type
H_{\infty}
, the sheaf

\mathcal{O}(H_{\infty})
imposes no constraint at infinity, corresponding
geometrically to allowing a pole of arbitrary order at
\infty
.

6
Arithmetic pullbacks and the geometry of the Riemann sector

This section develops the arithmetic analogue of a classical geometric construction:
the pullback of an abelian étale covering of a Jacobian along the Abel–Jacobi map.
Our aim is to interpret the “visible part”
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{Q}}
of the Riemann sector
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
as a geometric covering of the
arithmetic curve
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
, and to describe its fibers in terms of arithmetic divisors.

The guiding principle is that the arithmetic Picard monoid
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
plays the
role of a covering space of
\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
, while the extended Abel–Jacobi map determines
a pullback whose fibers encode the dynamical and group–theoretic features of the
scaling action.

We begin by recalling the classical geometric prototype over a finite
field, and then transpose it to the arithmetic setting. The remainder of the section
is devoted to a detailed analysis of the resulting fibers.

6.1
The classical geometric prototype

We briefly recall the standard construction of abelian unramified covers of a curve
over a finite field, which will serve as a geometric template for the arithmetic case.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g
over a finite field
k
. Let
J=\operatorname{Jac}(C)
be its Jacobian variety.
Class field theory for the function field
k(C)
establishes a correspondence between abelian unramified extensions of
k(C)
and isogenies of the Jacobian
J
.

Fix a prime
\ell
distinct from the characteristic of
k
. For any integer
n\geq 1
, consider the multiplication by
\ell^{n}
map on the Jacobian:

[\ell^{n}]:J\longrightarrow J,\hskip 10.00002ptx\longmapsto\ell^{n}x.

This is a finite étale covering of degree
\ell^{2gn}
, whose Galois group is the group
of
\ell^{n}
–torsion points
J[\ell^{n}](k)
, acting by translations:

\tau\cdot x=x+\tau,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }\tau\in J[\ell^{n}],~x\in J.

To obtain a covering of the curve
C
, one pulls back this covering of the Jacobian
along the Abel–Jacobi embedding
\alpha:C\hookrightarrow J
defined with respect
to a base point
P
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\times\ell^{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

The resulting curve
C_{n}
is an étale cover of
C
, which realizes the maximal abelian
unramified covering of exponent
\ell^{n}
.

6.2
The arithmetic pullback

We now transpose the preceding construction to the arithmetic curve
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
.
Each ingredient of the geometric picture admits a natural arithmetic counterpart:

-

the Jacobian
J
is replaced by the arithmetic Jacobian
\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
;

-

the isogeny
[\ell^{n}]:J\to J
is replaced by the projection

\pi:\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})\to\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
;

-

The role of the Galois group of translations
J[\ell^{n}]
is replaced by the continuous scaling
group
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}
, the connected component of the idele class group;

-

translations on the Jacobian are given by
multiplicative scaling
of
arithmetic divisors.

Pulling back the adelic covering along the extended Abel–Jacobi map

\Theta
(
7
), we obtain the diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{Q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta}

(8)

The resulting space
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{Q}}
will be referred to as the “
visible part
”
of the Riemann sector
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
, now realized geometrically as a covering of the
arithmetic curve. Its fibers encode the periodic orbits and fixed points of the
arithmetic flow, which we analyze next.

6.3
Group structure on the periodic orbits

We first study the fibers lying over closed points of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
, which correspond
to periodic orbits of the scaling action.

Let
p<\infty
be a finite prime, viewed as a closed point of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
, and let

H_{p}=\mathbf{Z}[1/p]

be its image in the Jacobian under the Abel–Jacobi map
\Theta
(
7
). The fiber of
\pi
(as in (
8
)) over

H_{p}
, denoted
C_{p}
, consists of all arithmetic divisors whose underlying group is
isomorphic to
\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
and whose metric is non–zero. By
Theorem
3.11
, this fiber is identified with

C_{p}=\{\,I\subset\mathbf{R}\mid I\cong\mathbf{Z}[1/p]\text{ as abelian groups}\,\}.

6.3.1
Idempotence and the induced group law

The monoid structure of the arithmetic Picard induces a binary operation on each fiber
C_{p}
.
On subgroups of
\mathbf{R}
, this operation is given by

I_{1}\cdot I_{2}=\langle xy\mid x\in I_{1},\ y\in I_{2}\rangle.

Proposition 6.1
.

The set
C_{p}
is an abelian group with respect to this operation.

Proof.

Let
I_{1},I_{2}\in C_{p}
. Since
I_{1}\cong I_{2}\cong\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
, both are modules over the ring
\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
. Their product
I_{1}\cdot I_{2}
is the image of the tensor product
I_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}I_{2}
inside
\mathbf{R}
.
Using the idempotence of the localization
\mathbf{Z}[1/p]\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{Z}[1/p]\cong\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
, we see that
I_{1}\cdot I_{2}
is again a rank-1 module over
\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
, hence isomorphic to
\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
. Thus
I_{1}\cdot I_{2}\in C_{p}
.

Consider the canonical embedding
I_{0}=\mathbf{Z}[1/p]\subset\mathbf{R}
(corresponding to
1\mapsto 1
). Since
I
is a
\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
-module, for any
I\in C_{p}
we have:

I_{0}\cdot I=\{\lambda x\mid\lambda\in\mathbf{Z}[1/p],x\in I\}=I.

Thus
I_{0}
is the neutral element.

Any
I\in C_{p}
is of the form
I=\lambda I_{0}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}^{\times}
.
Let
I^{-1}=\lambda^{-1}I_{0}
. Then:

I\cdot I^{-1}=(\lambda I_{0})\cdot(\lambda^{-1}I_{0})=(\lambda\lambda^{-1})(I_{0}\cdot I_{0})=1\cdot I_{0}=I_{0}.

Thus every element is invertible.
∎

6.3.2
Identification with the circle group

The parametrization
I_{\lambda}=\lambda\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
defines a surjective homomorphism

\mathbf{R}^{\times}\to C_{p}
. Its kernel is the stabilizer of the neutral element
I_{0}
:

\operatorname{Stab}(I_{0})=\{\lambda\in\mathbf{R}^{\times}\mid\lambda\mathbf{Z}[1/p]=\mathbf{Z}[1/p]\}=(\mathbf{Z}[1/p])^{\times}=\{\pm p^{k}\mid k\in\mathbf{Z}\}.

Restricting to positive scalars (that corresponds to the connected component of the identity) yields a canonical isomorphism of topological groups

C_{p}\cong\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}\cong\mathbf{R}/(\log p)\mathbf{Z},

so that each fiber
C_{p}
is canonically a circle group, and the neutral element of the fiber is the specific point on the orbit corresponding to the standard embedding of the rational numbers.

6.4
The fiber at infinity

We now consider the fiber over the archimedean point
\infty\in\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
.
Its image under
\Theta
is the reduced element
\mathbf{Z}_{\infty}
, corresponding to the
group
\mathbf{Z}
equipped with the zero semi–norm.

Accordingly, the fiber
C_{\infty}
consists of arithmetic divisors with underlying
group
\mathbf{Z}
and vanishing metric. There is a unique such divisor:

C_{\infty}=\{(\mathbf{Z},0)\}.

Geometrically, this point corresponds to the unique
fixed point of the scaling flow
.

6.4.1
Absorption by the fixed point

Let
I_{\infty}=\{0\}\subset\mathbf{R}
denote the subgroup corresponding to the point
at infinity. For any
p<\infty
and any
I_{p}\in C_{p}
, one has

I_{p}\cdot I_{\infty}=\langle xy\mid x\in I_{p},y\in\{0\}\rangle=\{0\}=I_{\infty}.

Thus

C_{p}\cdot C_{\infty}=C_{\infty},

expressing the fact that the archimedean fiber is an absorbing element for the
monoid structure. Dynamically, this reflects the role of
\infty
as a sink for
the scaling flow (
i.e.
the limit of the scaling action as
\lambda\to 0
leads to the zero semi-norm).

6.5
The generic fiber

Finally, we consider the fiber over the generic point
\eta\in\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
.
We define its image under
\Theta
to be the identity element of the Jacobian:

\Theta(\eta):=[\mathbf{Z}]\in\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}),

corresponding to the principal adele
(1_{f},1_{\infty})
.

6.5.1
Description of the generic fiber

The fiber
C_{\eta}
consists of arithmetic divisors
(L,\|\cdot\|)
with

L\cong\mathbf{Z}
and non–zero metric. By Theorem
3.11
,

C_{\eta}=\{\,I\subset\mathbf{R}\mid I\cong\mathbf{Z}\,\}=\{\lambda\mathbf{Z}\mid\lambda\in\mathbf{R}^{\times}\}.

Any such subgroup is of the form
I=\lambda\mathbf{Z}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}^{\times}
.
Thus, we have an isomorphism of topological groups:

C_{\eta}\cong\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}/\{1\}\cong\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}.

Thus the fiber
C_{\eta}
is simply the multiplicative group of positive real numbers (which may be viewed as a single flow line).

6.5.2
Density and topological meaning

The generic point
\eta
is dense in the Zariski topology of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
.
Correspondingly, in the adelic topology of
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})\cong X_{\mathbf{Q}}
, the fiber

C_{\eta}
consists of principal arithmetic divisors associated to ideles.
By strong approximation, ideles are dense in the adeles, and therefore

\overline{C_{\eta}}=\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}).

This identifies
C_{\eta}
as the
generic stratum
of the arithmetic Picard space,
linking algebraic density on
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
with analytic density in the adelic setting.

7
The universal cover

We construct and analyze the universal covering objects naturally
associated to the adelic space
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}
. The guiding idea is that finite abelian extensions
of
\mathbf{Q}
admit a geometric incarnation as finite covers of
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
, in a way that
faithfully reflects the Galois correspondence of class field theory.
After introducing the class field functor and its basic properties, we analyze
the group structure carried by the fibers of the universal cover over the various
points of
\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
: finite primes, the archimedean place, and the generic point.

7.1
The class field functor

We begin by recalling how finite abelian extensions of
\mathbf{Q}
give rise to geometric
covers of the adelic space
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
. This construction realizes class field theory
directly at the level of topological spaces and dynamical systems.

Let
L
be a finite abelian extension of
\mathbf{Q}
, and let
{}_{L}G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbf{Q})
be its
Galois group. Class field theory provides a canonical continuous surjection

\chi_{L}:\mathbb{A}_{f}^{\times}\longrightarrow{}_{L}G

which is trivial on
\mathbf{Q}_{+}^{\times}
. Restricting to the maximal compact subgroup

\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}\subset\mathbb{A}_{f}^{\times}
, we obtain the reciprocity homomorphism

\chi_{L}:\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}\longrightarrow{}_{L}G.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
L
be a finite abelian extension of
\mathbf{Q}
defined by the character
\chi_{L}
.
The associated geometric cover
\gamma(L)
of
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
is the projection

\pi_{\chi_{L}}:X_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\chi_{L}}\longrightarrow X_{\mathbf{Q}},\hskip 20.00003pt\gamma(L):=\pi_{\chi_{L}},

where the total space is the balanced product

X_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\chi_{L}}:=Y_{\mathbf{Q}}\times_{\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}}{}_{L}G=(Y_{\mathbf{Q}}\times{}_{L}G)/\!\sim.

The equivalence relation is induced by the diagonal action of
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
:
for
u\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
,
y\in Y_{\mathbf{Q}}
, and
g\in{}_{L}G
,

(yu,g)\sim(y,\chi_{L}(u)g).

The map
\pi_{\chi_{L}}
is induced by the projection

Y_{\mathbf{Q}}\to Y_{\mathbf{Q}}/\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}=X_{\mathbf{Q}}
.

This construction associates to the
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
–bundle

Y_{\mathbf{Q}}\to X_{\mathbf{Q}}
the corresponding
{}_{L}G
–bundle via the class field
homomorphism
\chi_{L}
.

The following theorem summarizes the fundamental properties of these covers,
establishing a precise correspondence between arithmetic data of the extension

L/\mathbf{Q}
and the topological and dynamical features of the space
X_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\chi_{L}}
(singularities, monodromy, orbit decomposition).

Theorem 7.2

(
[
5
, Theorem 3.12]
)
.

Let
L
be a finite abelian extension of
\mathbf{Q}
with Galois group
{}_{L}G
.

(i)

Functoriality.

The assignment
L\mapsto\gamma(L)
defines a contravariant functor

\gamma:\mathbf{AbExt}(\mathbf{Q})\longrightarrow\mathbf{Cov}_{\emph{ab}}(X_{\mathbf{Q}})

from finite abelian extensions of
\mathbf{Q}
to finite abelian covers of
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
.

(ii)

Ramification.

The cover
\gamma(L)
is unramified except at a finite set of places
R
,
consisting of the archimedean place
\infty
together with the finite primes
that ramify in the extension
L/\mathbf{Q}
.

(iii)

Monodromy.

If
p\notin R
, the fiber over the periodic orbit

C_{p}\subset X_{\mathbf{Q}}
is a disjoint union of circles.
The monodromy along
C_{p}
is given by the arithmetic Frobenius:

\operatorname{Mon}(C_{p})=\operatorname{Frob}_{p}\in{}_{L}G.

(iv)

Decomposition.

The connected components of
\pi_{\chi_{L}}^{-1}(C_{p})
are covering circles of
C_{p}
,
in canonical bijection with the prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
L
lying above
p
.
The covering degree of each component equals the residue degree

f(\mathfrak{p}/p)
.

7.2
Group structure of the fiber over a finite prime

We now refine the description of the universal cover by analyzing the fiber over a
finite periodic orbit
C_{p}
. While this fiber was previously described topologically
as a mapping torus, we show here that the multiplicative structure of the adele class
space
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}
endows it with a natural group structure.

Let
\pi:Y_{\mathbf{Q}}\to X_{\mathbf{Q}}
be the canonical projection. The fiber

\mathcal{F}_{p}:=\pi^{-1}(C_{p})

consists of adele classes
[a]\in Y_{\mathbf{Q}}
such that the associated lattice
L_{a}
(cf. Lemma
3.8
) is isomorphic
to
\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
. Equivalently,

a_{p}=0,\hskip 20.00003pta_{v}\in\mathbf{Z}_{v}^{\times}\text{ for all }v\neq p,\infty,

with
a_{\infty}\in\mathbf{R}^{\times}
arbitrary.

Proposition 7.3
.

The fiber
\mathcal{F}_{p}
is a topological group under the multiplication of adele classes.

Proof.

Let
e_{p}\in\mathbb{A}
be the idempotent adele defined by
(e_{p})_{p}=0
and
(e_{p})_{v}=1
for
v\neq p
. Its class
[e_{p}]
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{p}
.
For any
[a],[b]\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
, we have
a_{p}=b_{p}=0
. Thus
(ab)_{p}=0
.
For
v\neq p
,
a_{v},b_{v}
are units, so
a_{v}b_{v}
is a unit.
Thus
[ab]\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
.
The element
[e_{p}]
acts as the identity: for any
[a]\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
,
ae_{p}=a
(since
0\cdot 0=0
and
x\cdot 1=x
).
Inverses exist because the components
a_{v}
for
v\neq p
are units in
\mathbf{Z}_{v}
, and
a_{\infty}
is invertible in
\mathbf{R}
.
∎

Theorem 7.4
.

There is a canonical isomorphism of topological groups

\mathcal{F}_{p}\cong C_{\mathbf{Q}}/\iota_{p}(\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}),

where
\iota_{p}:\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}\hookrightarrow C_{\mathbf{Q}}
is induced by the natural inclusion
\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}\to\mathbb{A}^{\times}
mapping
x
to the idele with
x
at
p
and
1
elsewhere.

Proof.

Multiplication by
e_{p}
defines a surjective homomorphism

\psi:\mathbb{A}^{\times}\to\mathcal{F}_{p}
,
\psi(u)=[u\cdot e_{p}]
.
Its kernel consists of ideles
u
such that
[ue_{p}]=[e_{p}]
, i.e.,
ue_{p}=qe_{p}
for some
q\in\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
. The condition
ue_{p}=qe_{p}
means:

At
v\neq p
:
u_{v}\cdot 1=q\cdot 1\implies u_{v}=q
.

At
p
:
u_{p}\cdot 0=q\cdot 0
(always true).

Thus,
u
must be equal to the rational number
q
at all places except possibly
p
.
This means
u=q\cdot(1,\dots,1,u_{p}/q,1,\dots)
.
In terms of classes in
C_{\mathbf{Q}}=\mathbb{A}^{\times}/\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
, the kernel is precisely the image of the local group
\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
.
Thus
\psi
induces an isomorphism
C_{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{F}_{p}
.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

This description recovers the mapping–torus picture:
the quotient
C_{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
is the group of idele classes away from
p
modulo powers of
p
.

7.3
The fiber over the archimedean orbit

We next analyze the fiber over the archimedean orbit
C_{\infty}
.
The base point corresponds to the reduced element
\mathbf{Z}_{\infty}
, represented by the
idempotent adele
e_{\infty}=(1_{f},0_{\infty})
.

Proposition 7.6
.

The fiber
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}:=\pi^{-1}(C_{\infty})
is a compact topological group.

Proof.

The elements of the fiber
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
are adele classes
[a]
such that
a_{\infty}=0
and
L_{a}=\mathbf{Z}
. The condition
L_{a}=\mathbf{Z}
implies that the finite part
a_{f}
of
a
is a unit in
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
.
The multiplication is inherited from that on
\mathbb{A}
. The class
[e_{\infty}]
is the neutral element. Since
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
is a group, every element is invertible.
∎

Theorem 7.7
.

There are canonical isomorphisms

\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\cong C_{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}\cong\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}\cong\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf{Q}^{\text{ab}}/\mathbf{Q}).

Proof.

Consider the map
\psi:\mathbb{A}^{\times}\to\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
defined by
u\mapsto[ue_{\infty}]
.
This is a surjective homomorphism.
The kernel consists of ideles
u
such that
ue_{\infty}=qe_{\infty}
for some
q\in\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
.
This condition implies
u_{f}=q\cdot 1_{f}
, meaning
u
is rational at all finite places.
Thus
u
lies in the image of
\mathbf{R}^{\times}
(embedded as
(1,\dots,1,x)
modulo
\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
),
and
C_{\mathbf{Q}}\cong\mathbb{A}_{f}^{\times}\times\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}/\mathbf{Q}_{+}^{\times}
.
The map
\psi
kills the
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}
component and the
\mathbf{Q}_{+}^{\times}
action (since
qe_{\infty}=e_{\infty}
in the class).
The result is thus isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}_{f}^{\times}/\mathbf{Q}_{+}^{\times}\cong\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
.
∎

Remark 7.8
.

This result completes the geometric picture of class field theory.

-

Over a finite prime
p
, the fiber
\mathcal{F}_{p}
carries the information inherent to local class field theory of
p
(modulo global units).

-

Over the infinite prime, the fiber
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
encodes the structure of the global Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf{Q}^{\text{ab}}/\mathbf{Q})
.

The "singularity" at infinity thus contains the full arithmetic complexity of the abelian extensions of
\mathbf{Q}
.

7.4
The fiber over the generic point

Finally, we consider the fiber
\mathcal{F}_{\eta}:=\pi^{-1}([\mathbf{Z}])
over the generic point
\Theta(\eta)=[\mathbf{Z}]
of the
Jacobian, corresponding to a principal lattice with nonzero metric.

Precisely,
a class
[a]\in Y_{\mathbf{Q}}
if and only if its image in the Jacobian is the pair
(\mathbf{Z},0)
(representing the group
\mathbf{Z}
and a non-zero metric).
This means:

1.

a_{\infty}\neq 0
(finite type).

2.

L_{a}\cong\mathbf{Z}
as groups (the underlying lattice is principal).

Theorem 7.9
.

The fiber over the generic point is canonically isomorphic to the idele class group

8
The geometric monoids
\operatorname{Pic}_{Fr}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
and
\operatorname{Pic}_{Rt}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})

To establish a geometric interpretation of the adelic space
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}=\mathbf{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}
we identify its points with arithmetic divisors equipped with
rigidifying data
defined in terms of finite and infinite frames. We first introduce the notion of
framed arithmetic divisors
, as torsion free rank-1 groups
L
endowed with trivializations of their completions at both finite and infinite rational places. This formulation treats the archimedean and non-archimedean data symmetrically and realizes the adelic product as a tensor product. Subsequently, by applying Pontryagin duality to the finite frame, we derive the notion of
rooted arithmetic divisors
. This dual perspective interprets the profinite frame as a coherent system of roots of unity, offering a combinatorial description that characterizes the divisor through its interaction with the torsion of the dual group.

8.1
Framed arithmetic divisors

Definition 8.1
.

A
framed arithmetic divisor
is a triple
(L,\xi,\tau)
consisting of the following data:

1.

A torsion-free rank-1 abelian group
L
.

2.

A
finite frame
defined as a group homomorphism

\xi:L\to\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}

generating the module
\operatorname{Hom}(L,\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
.

3.

An
infinite frame
, namely a homomorphism

\tau:L\to\mathbf{R}

of abelian groups.

Remark 8.2
.

The infinite frame
\tau
replaces the datum of a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
L\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{R}
. Indeed:

The norm corresponds to the absolute value:
\|x\|=|\tau(x)|
.

The map
\tau
retains the
sign
information (orientation) that corresponds to the connected components of the idele class group
C_{\mathbf{Q}}
.

\tau=0
corresponds to the singular fiber at infinity (i.e.
a_{\infty}=0
).

The next lemma provides the adelic interpretation to a finite frame.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
L\subset\mathbf{Q}
be a rank-1 group and
\xi\in\operatorname{Hom}(L,\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
.

(i)
There exists a unique finite rational adele
a_{f}\in\mathbb{A}_{f}
such that

\xi(x)=a_{f}x,\ \ \forall x\in L.

(ii)
The following statements are equivalent:

1.

\xi
is a generator of the
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}(L,\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
.

2.

The following equality of groups holds:
L=\{q\in\mathbf{Q}\mid a_{f}q\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\}
.

Proof.

(i)
The tensor product decomposition
\mathbb{A}_{f}=\mathbf{Q}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
and the isomorphism
\mathbf{Q}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}L\simeq\mathbf{Q}
show that
\xi
extends uniquely to a group homomorphism

\tilde{\xi}:\mathbf{Q}\to\mathbb{A}_{f}.

Since
\mathbb{A}_{f}
is a
\mathbf{Q}
-vector space, this determines uniquely an element
a_{f}\in\mathbb{A}_{f}
such that
\tilde{\xi}(x)=a_{f}x,\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{Q}
.

(ii)
By
(i)
a homomorphism
\xi:L\to\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
is determined by a sequence of local multipliers
\alpha_{p}\in\mathbf{Q}_{p}
such that
\xi(x)_{p}=\alpha_{p}x
.
Let
M=\operatorname{Hom}(L,\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
. We analyze the local structure
M_{p}=\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbf{Z}_{p})
.

The structure of the module
M_{p}
.

The condition for
\alpha_{p}
to define a map into
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
can be stated as follows:

\forall x\in L,\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha_{p}x\in\mathbf{Z}_{p}\iff v_{p}(\alpha_{p})+v_{p}(x)\geq 0.

In terms of the arithmetic divisor
\sum n_{p}[p]
of
L
, the minimal valuation
v_{p}(x)
for
x\in L
is
-n_{p}
, this requires:

v_{p}(\alpha_{p})-n_{p}\geq 0\implies v_{p}(\alpha_{p})\geq n_{p}.

Two cases are possible:

If
n_{p}<\infty
, then
M_{p}=\{\alpha_{p}\in\mathbf{Q}_{p}\mid v_{p}(\alpha_{p})\geq n_{p}\}=p^{n_{p}}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
.

If
n_{p}=\infty
, then
M_{p}=\{0\}
(since no non-zero
\alpha_{p}
maps
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
into
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
).

The generator condition.

\xi
generates
M
as a
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
-module if and only if for every
p
,
\alpha_{p}
generates
M_{p}
as a module over
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. Again, two cases are possible:

If
n_{p}<\infty
:
\alpha_{p}
generates
p^{n_{p}}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
if and only if
v_{p}(\alpha_{p})=n_{p}
.

If
n_{p}=\infty
:
0
generates
\{0\}
. This is always true.

Thus, the statement 1. is equivalent to:
v_{p}(\alpha_{p})=n_{p}
for all
p
.

Statement 2.

Let
L^{\prime}=\{q\in\mathbf{Q}\mid a_{f}q\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\}
. It is defined by:

L^{\prime}=\{x\in\mathbf{Q}\mid v_{p}(x)\geq-v_{p}(\alpha_{p}),\ \forall p\}.

Statement 2. requires
L=L^{\prime}
, which means the equality of the arithmetic divisors

n_{p}=v_{p}(\alpha_{p}),\forall p
.
Thus, both statements
1
. and
2
. are equivalent to the equality of valuations
v_{p}(\alpha_{p})=n_{p}
for all
p
.
∎

8.2
The moduli space
\operatorname{Pic}_{Fr}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})

The multiplicative monoid of framed arithmetic divisors modulo isomorphism provides a first geometric interpretation of the adele class space
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}
.

Definition 8.4
.

The moduli space
\operatorname{Pic}_{Fr}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
is the set of framed arithmetic divisors modulo isomorphism. An isomorphism
(L,\xi,\tau)\cong(L^{\prime},\xi^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
is a group isomorphism
\phi:L\xrightarrow{\sim}L^{\prime}
such that
\xi^{\prime}\circ\widehat{\phi}=\xi
and
\tau^{\prime}\circ\phi=\tau
.

Given a rational adele
a=(a_{f},a_{\infty})\in\mathbb{A}
, one defines the map

\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{A}\longrightarrow\{\text{Framed arithmetic divisors}\}\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{F}(a)=(L_{a},\xi_{a},\tau_{a})

where:

-

L_{a}=\{q\in\mathbf{Q}\mid qa_{f}\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\}
.

-

\xi_{a}:L_{a}\to\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
is defined by
\xi_{a}(x)=xa_{f}
.

-

\tau_{a}:L_{a}\to\mathbf{R}
is given by
\tau_{a}(x)=xa_{\infty}
.

Theorem 8.5
.

The map
\mathcal{F}
induces a canonical bijection between
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}=\mathbf{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}
and the moduli space
\operatorname{Pic}_{Fr}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
of framed arithmetic divisors.

Proof.

Descent to Quotient.

Let
q\in\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
. Consider the adele
a^{\prime}=qa
.
The associated group is
L_{qa}=q^{-1}L_{a}
.
The map
\phi:L_{a}\to L_{qa}
given by multiplication by
q^{-1}
is an isomorphism of groups.

Finite Frame:
\xi_{qa}(\phi(x))=\xi_{qa}(q^{-1}x)=(q^{-1}x)(qa)_{f}=xa_{f}=\xi_{a}(x)
.

Infinite Frame:
\tau_{qa}(\phi(x))=\tau_{qa}(q^{-1}x)=(q^{-1}x)(qa)_{\infty}=xa_{\infty}=\tau_{a}(x)
.

Thus, the triples are isomorphic, and
\mathcal{F}
descends to
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}
.

We check the
Bijectivity.

Let
(L,\xi,\tau)
be a framed arithmetic divisor.
Since
L
is rank-1 torsion-free group, we can choose an embedding
\iota:L\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Q}
.

By Lemma
8.3

(i)
, the map
\xi
extends to a map
\mathbb{A}_{f}\to\mathbb{A}_{f}
, which is multiplication by some
a_{f}\in\mathbb{A}_{f}
. By Lemma
8.3

(ii)
the generator condition ensures that
L
is recovered correctly.

The map
\tau
extends to a map
\mathbf{Q}\otimes\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}
, which is multiplication by some
a_{\infty}\in\mathbf{R}
.

This yields an adele
a=(a_{f},a_{\infty})
.
Changing the embedding
\iota
by a factor
q\in\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
changes
a
to
q^{-1}a
, which preserves the class in
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}
.
Thus, the correspondence is one-to-one.
∎

8.2.1
The monoid structure

The set of framed arithmetic divisors carries a natural tensor product structure.
Given
D_{1}=(L_{1},\xi_{1},\tau_{1})
and
D_{2}=(L_{2},\xi_{2},\tau_{2})
, we define
D_{1}\otimes D_{2}=(L,\xi,\tau)
by:

L=L_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}L_{2}
.

\xi=\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2}
(product in
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
).

\tau=\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}
(product in
\mathbf{R}
).

Explicitly, for the infinite part one has:
\tau(x\otimes y)=\tau_{1}(x)\tau_{2}(y)
.

Theorem 8.6
.

The sets bijection
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}\cong\operatorname{Pic}_{Fr}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
is an isomorphism of monoids.
If
a,b\in\mathbb{A}
correspond to arithmetic framed divisors
D_{a},D_{b}
, then the product
ab
corresponds to the tensor product of arithmetic divisors
D_{a}\otimes D_{b}
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{A}
.
The product adele is
ab=(a_{f}b_{f},a_{\infty}b_{\infty})
.
The divisor for the product is defined by:

\xi_{ab}(x)=x(a_{f}b_{f})
.

\tau_{ab}(x)=x(a_{\infty}b_{\infty})
.

Consider the tensor product
D_{a}\otimes D_{b}
.
The map
L_{a}\otimes L_{b}\to L_{ab}
given by multiplication in
\mathbf{Q}
is an isomorphism.
Under this identification:

The finite frame acts as
\xi_{a}(x)\xi_{b}(y)=(xa_{f})(yb_{f})=(xy)(a_{f}b_{f})
, matching
\xi_{ab}
.

The infinite frame acts as
\tau_{a}(x)\tau_{b}(y)=(xa_{\infty})(yb_{\infty})=(xy)(a_{\infty}b_{\infty})
, matching
\tau_{ab}
.

Thus, the structures coincide.
∎

\xi^{\vee}\in\rm{Hom}(\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z},L^{\vee}),\ \ \xi^{\vee}(r)=\xi^{*}(r),\ \forall r\in\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}.

.

Let

Let

\rm{Tor}(L^{\vee})=\bigoplus_{p\notin S}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}\subset L^{\vee}

By Lemma

, the map

is given locally at a prime

and condition 1. is equivalent to

The map

Definition 8.10
.

A

8.4
Tensor product of roots via character multiplication

We formalize the tensor product of rooted divisors using the multiplicative structure of the finite rings
\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
. This approach naturally handles both the composition of frames and the vanishing on singular sets.

8.4.1
The character pairing

Let
L_{1},L_{2}
be two torsion-free abelian groups. Fix an integer
m\geq 1
.
The ring structure of
\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
induces a canonical bilinear map:

\Phi_{m}:\operatorname{Hom}(L_{1},\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z})\times\operatorname{Hom}(L_{2},\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(L_{1}\otimes L_{2},\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z})

defined by the pointwise product of values:

\Phi_{m}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})(x\otimes y):=\chi_{1}(x)\cdot\chi_{2}(y)\pmod{m}.

(This is well-defined because the map
(u,v)\mapsto uv
is bilinear over
\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
).

8.4.2
Product of roots

Recall that the torsion subgroup of the dual of a torsion-free abelian group
L
is the colimit of the finite levels:

\rm{Tor}(L^{\vee})\cong\varinjlim_{m}\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}).

A root
\rho
is determined by the family of characters
\rho(\frac{1}{m})\in\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z})
. This family is consistent with the relations in the group
\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
.
Specifically, for any integers
n
and
k
, the element
1/n
in
\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
is equal to
k\cdot(1/nk)
.
Therefore, any root
\rho
must satisfy the consistency condition:

\rho(1/n)=k\cdot\rho(1/nk).

(12)

Proposition 8.11
.

Let
(L_{1},\rho_{1})
and
(L_{2},\rho_{2})
be two rooted divisors.
The following equation defines a root
\rho
for the tensor product
L_{1}\otimes L_{2}
by the pairing of the characters at each level
m
:

\rho\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)=\Phi_{m}\left(\rho_{1}\left(\frac{1}{m}\right),\,\rho_{2}\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)\right).

(13)

Explicitly, for any
x\in L_{1},y\in L_{2}
:

\left\langle\rho\left(\frac{1}{m}\right),x\otimes y\right\rangle=\left\langle\rho_{1}\left(\frac{1}{m}\right),x\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle\rho_{2}\left(\frac{1}{m}\right),y\right\rangle\in\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}.

Proof.

We verify that the definition (
13
) of the product root fulfills the consistency condition (
12
).
We check that if
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
satisfy this condition, their product
\rho
(defined level-wise) also satisfies it.
Let
N=nk
.
We represent the values of the roots by elements in the finite rings:
Let
u_{m}\in\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
represent the value
\rho_{1}(1/m)
(via the identification
\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z})\cong\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}\otimes L^{\vee}\dots
effectively treating them as scalars for this check).
The condition
\rho_{1}(1/n)=k\rho_{1}(1/N)
translates to the congruence of representatives:

\frac{u_{n}}{n}\equiv k\frac{u_{N}}{N}\pmod{1}\implies\frac{u_{n}}{n}\equiv\frac{u_{N}}{n}\pmod{1}\implies u_{n}\equiv u_{N}\pmod{n}.

Thus, the sequence of values
(u_{m})_{m}
forms an element of the projective limit
\varprojlim(\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z})\cong\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
.
Let
(u_{m})
and
(v_{m})
be the sequences for
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
.
We have:

u_{n}\equiv u_{N}\pmod{n}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptv_{n}\equiv v_{N}\pmod{n}.

The product root
\rho
is defined by the product in the ring
\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
:

w_{m}:=u_{m}\cdot v_{m}\pmod{m}.

We must verify that
w_{n}\equiv w_{N}\pmod{n}
.
Compute the reduction of
w_{N}
modulo
n
:

w_{N}\pmod{n}=(u_{N}\cdot v_{N})\pmod{n}=(u_{N}\pmod{n})\cdot(v_{N}\pmod{n}).

Using the hypothesis:

=u_{n}\cdot v_{n}=w_{n}.

Thus, the consistency condition holds.
∎

Remark 8.12
.

This calculation reveals that the transition maps relating the values at level

to level

are simply the canonical projections
\pi:\mathbf{Z}/N\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}
. Since these projections are ring homomorphisms, they preserve the multiplicative structure defined at each level.

8.4.3
Automatic handling of singularities

Next formula automatically reproduces the filtering behavior of the singular sets.

Let
p
be a singular prime for
L_{1}
(
i.e.

p\in S_{1}
).
Then
L_{1}
is
p
-divisible, so for any power
q=p^{k}
, there are no non-zero homomorphisms from

to
\mathbf{Z}/q\mathbf{Z}
:

\operatorname{Hom}(L_{1},\mathbf{Z}/p^{k}\mathbf{Z})=0\implies\rho_{1}\left(\frac{1}{p^{k}}\right)=0.

Consequently, the product character is zero:

\rho\left(\frac{1}{p^{k}}\right)=0\cdot\rho_{2}\left(\frac{1}{p^{k}}\right)=0.

This confirms that if

, then the product root
\rho
vanishes on the

-power roots of unity.
Thus, the support of the product root is exactly the intersection of the supports:

\operatorname{Supp}(\rho)=\operatorname{Supp}(\rho_{1})\cap\operatorname{Supp}(\rho_{2}).

Remark 8.13

(The hidden coproduct structure)
.

The definition of the product root
\rho=\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
relies on the formula
\rho(1/n)=\rho_{1}(1/n)\cdot\rho_{2}(1/n)
, suggesting the existence of a diagonal coproduct

on

satisfying
\Delta(1/n)=1/n\otimes 1/n

While such a map is ill-defined in the category of abelian groups (where
\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}=0
), it finds a rigorous formulation in the category of Ind-rings.
We view
\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
as the direct limit of the finite rings
R_{n}=\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}
. For each
n
, the multiplication map
\mu_{n}:R_{n}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}R_{n}\to R_{n}
is an isomorphism of rings. Its inverse provides a diagonal map:

\Delta_{n}:R_{n}\xrightarrow{\sim}R_{n}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}R_{n},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{satisfying }~\Delta_{n}(1_{n})=1_{n}\otimes 1_{n}.

Identifying the element
1/n\in\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
with the unity
1_{n}\in R_{n}
, the tensor product of roots can be interpreted as the composition:

\rho=(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})\circ\Delta,

where
\Delta=\varinjlim\Delta_{n}
is the limit of these diagonal maps. This structural perspective explains why the product is anchored at the generators
1/n
(the multiplicative identities) and why it scales non-linearly for general elements
k/n
.

8.5
Compatibility of duality with tensor products

We prove that the duality functor
D(\xi)=\xi^{\vee}
transforms the product of frames into the tensor product of roots.

Lemma 8.14
.

Let
(L_{1},\xi_{1})
and
(L_{2},\xi_{2})
be two framed arithmetic divisors.
Let
\xi=\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2}
be the product frame on
L_{1}\otimes L_{2}
.
Let
\rho_{1}=\xi_{1}^{\vee}
and
\rho_{2}=\xi_{2}^{\vee}
be the associated roots.
Then the root associated to the product frame is the tensor product of the roots:

(\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2})^{\vee}=\xi_{1}^{\vee}\otimes\xi_{2}^{\vee}.

Proof.

It suffices to verify the equality of the maps on the generators
1/n\in\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
acting on pure tensors
x\otimes y\in L_{1}\otimes L_{2}
.

The dual of the product frame (LHS).

Let
\rho=\xi^{\vee}
. By the definition of the dual frame, the pairing is given by the reduction of the frame value modulo
n
:

\langle\rho(1/n),x\otimes y\rangle=\xi(x\otimes y)\pmod{n}.

By the definition of the product frame,
\xi(x\otimes y)
is the product in the ring
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
:

\xi(x\otimes y)=\xi_{1}(x)\cdot\xi_{2}(y).

Let
\pi_{n}:\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\to\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}
be the canonical projection. Since
\pi_{n}
is a ring homomorphism:

\langle\rho(1/n),x\otimes y\rangle=\pi_{n}\left(\xi_{1}(x)\cdot\xi_{2}(y)\right)=\pi_{n}(\xi_{1}(x))\cdot\pi_{n}(\xi_{2}(y)).

The product of the roots (RHS).

Let
\tilde{\rho}=\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
. By the definition of the tensor product of roots (using the ring structure of
\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}
) one has:

\langle\tilde{\rho}(1/n),x\otimes y\rangle=\langle\rho_{1}(1/n),x\rangle\cdot\langle\rho_{2}(1/n),y\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt(\text{product in }\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}).

Using the definition of the individual roots
\rho_{i}=\xi_{i}^{\vee}
:

\langle\rho_{1}(1/n),x\rangle=\xi_{1}(x)\pmod{n}=\pi_{n}(\xi_{1}(x)),

\langle\rho_{2}(1/n),y\rangle=\xi_{2}(y)\pmod{n}=\pi_{n}(\xi_{2}(y)).

Substituting these back:

\langle\tilde{\rho}(1/n),x\otimes y\rangle=\pi_{n}(\xi_{1}(x))\cdot\pi_{n}(\xi_{2}(y)).

Comparing the two expressions, we see they are identical for all
n
and all pure tensors.

\langle(\xi_{1}\otimes\xi_{2})^{\vee}(1/n),x\otimes y\rangle=\langle(\xi_{1}^{\vee}\otimes\xi_{2}^{\vee})(1/n),x\otimes y\rangle.

Thus, the duality map respects the tensor structure.
∎

In this section we re-interpret the diagram (

Specifically, we show that the geometric side of Weil’s explicit formula is supported on the image of the universal Abel-Jacobi (embedding) map

At this point, it is essential to stress a fundamental difference between the approach pursued in this paper and the classical geometric theory of function fields.
In the function field case, the Picard group is a discrete abelian group, and the spectral information is extracted from the action of the Frobenius endomorphism on the
\ell
-torsion points of the Jacobian. The eigenvalues of the Frobenius acting on these finite dimensional vector spaces (transferred from
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
to
\mathbf{C}

In the number field case, on the other hand, we work with the Picard monoid
\widetilde{\operatorname{Pic}}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
and consider the action of the idèle class group
C_{\mathbf{Q}}
by
translations
.
If we were dealing with a group (like the classical Picard group), translations would have no fixed points (except for the identity), rendering a trace formula trivial.
The key insight of this approach is instead that the adelic space
\widetilde{\operatorname{Pic}}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
is not discrete but a geometric space with rich internal structure. The action of
C_{\mathbf{Q}}
by translation on this space is non-trivial and possesses fixed points located on the range of the Abel-Jacobi map.

9.1
The explicit formula as a trace formula

Noncommutative geometry offers powerful tools for analyzing the rich geometry of the adelic spaces
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}
and
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
. This framework is especially compelling because it furnishes the analytic machinery required for the spectral realization of L-functions.

Let
\alpha
be a non-trivial character of the additive group
\mathbb{A}/\mathbf{Q}
, and
\alpha=\prod_{v}\alpha_{v}
its decomposition into local factors. Let
h\in\mathcal{S}(C_{\mathbf{Q}})
be a Schwartz function with compact support on the idele class group
C_{\mathbf{Q}}
.
The generalized Weil explicit formula relates the zeros of
L
-functions with Grössencharakter to a sum of local integrals. It takes the following form:

\hat{h}(0)+\hat{h}(1)-\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{C_{\mathbf{Q},1}}}\sum_{\rho\in Z_{\tilde{\chi}}}\hat{h}(\tilde{\chi},\rho)=\sum_{v}\int_{\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{\times}}^{\prime}\frac{h(u^{-1})}{|1-u|}d^{*}u.

(17)

Here:

•

The sum on the left runs over the non-trivial zeros
\rho
of
L
-functions associated to characters
\chi
of the compact group
C_{\mathbf{Q},1}=C_{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}
.

•

\hat{h}(\chi,z)=\int_{C_{\mathbf{Q}}}h(u)\chi(u)|u|^{z}d^{*}u
is the Mellin transform.

•

The integral
\int^{\prime}
on the right in (
17
) is normalized by the local factors
\alpha_{v}
following Tate’s thesis.

Weil’s explicit formula can be interpreted as the trace formula:

\operatorname{Tr}_{\text{distr}}\left(\int_{C_{\mathbf{Q}}}h(u)\vartheta(u)d^{*}u\right)=\sum_{v}\int_{\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{\times}}\frac{h(u^{-1})}{|1-u|}d^{*}u.

Here,
\vartheta(u)
denotes the scaling action of an element
u\in C_{\mathbf{Q}}
on the space of functions on the adelic space
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}=\mathbb{A}/\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
, defined by

\vartheta(u)\xi(x)=\xi(u^{-1}x).

9.2
Geometric interpretation: isotropy and fibers

The geometric side of the trace formula (the right-hand side of (
17
)) is a sum over the places
v
of
\mathbf{Q}
, which correspond to the closed points
x\in\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
.
In this geometric framework, these contributions are understood as localized on the range of the Abel-Jacobi map

\widetilde{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\Theta}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\operatorname{Pic}}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}).

This range is the union
of the fibers
\pi^{-1}(x)
of the projection
\pi:\widetilde{\operatorname{Pic}}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})\to\operatorname{Jac}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
, and each fiber contributes to the geometric side of the trace formula by
\int_{\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{\times}}\frac{h(u^{-1})}{|1-u|}d^{*}u
.
For each place
v
, the local multiplicative group
\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{\times}
embeds as a subgroup of the idele class group
\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{\times}\subset C_{\mathbf{Q}}
.
Geometrically, these subgroups appear as the isotropy groups of the action of
C_{\mathbf{Q}}
on
\widetilde{\operatorname{Pic}}(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}})
.

The contribution of a place
v
to the trace formula arises from the action of the isotropy group
\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{\times}
on the transverse space to the orbit at that point. This transverse space is identified with the local field
\mathbf{Q}_{v}
.
The appearance of the term
\frac{1}{|1-u|}
can be justified by a formal computation of the distributional trace of the scaling operator on the local transverse space.

Consider the scaling operator
T=\vartheta(u^{-1})
acting on functions on the line
\mathbf{Q}_{v}
(or
\mathbf{R}
):

T\xi(x)=\xi(ux).

We can represent this operator as an integral operator with a distributional kernel
k(x,y)
:

T\xi(x)=\int k(x,y)\xi(y)dy,\hskip 20.00003pt\text{where }~k(x,y)=\delta(ux-y).

The distributional trace is then obtained by integrating the kernel along the diagonal
x=y
:

\operatorname{Tr}_{\text{distr}}(T)=\int k(x,x)dx=\int\delta(ux-x)dx=\int\delta((u-1)x)dx.

Using the scaling property of the delta distribution
\delta(\lambda x)=\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\delta(x)
, we obtain:

\operatorname{Tr}_{\text{distr}}(T)=\frac{1}{|u-1|}\int\delta(x)dx=\frac{1}{|u-1|}.

This calculation demonstrates that the geometric side of the explicit formula is a sum of local traces associated to the hyperbolic flow on the fibers over the arithmetic Jacobian.

9.3
The semilocal trace formula

In order to obtain a geometric proof of (
17
), one first notices that the functional analysis focuses on the locally compact rings

\mathbb{A}_{S}:=\prod_{v\in S}\mathbf{Q}_{v}

which are
finite
products of the local fields
\mathbf{Q}_{v}
over a finite set
S
of places containing the archimedean place
\infty
. Indeed,
the function space associated with the adele class space
Y_{\mathbf{Q}}
is represented as a cross product and consists of functions defined on the adeles, specifically belonging to the Bruhat–Schwartz space. These functions are characterized by their non-trivial dependence on only finitely many places. In other words one replaces the infinite product involved in the construction of adeles by a finite product of the local fields
\mathbf{Q}_{v}
. All the techniques and tools available in the global case have a natural analogue in this semilocal framework. The semilocal version of the group
\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
is the group
\mathbf{Z}_{S}^{\times}
of invertible elements in the ring
\mathbf{Z}_{S}
of
S
-integers, where each prime
p\in S
is inverted. One obtains the semilocal analogues of the global Bruhat–Schwartz algebra
{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{A})
and its cross product with
\mathbf{Q}^{\times}
,

{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{A}_{S}),\ \ {\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{A}_{S})\rtimes\mathbf{Z}_{S}^{\times}.

(18)

The key advantage of working in the semilocal framework is that for a finite product of local fields the Haar measures of the additive and multiplicative groups are comparable: this fails at the global level. One thus obtains a natural Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{S}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{S})
of
0
-forms (that admits a Hochschild homological interpretation) and
then investigates the action
\theta
of the semilocal idele class group
C_{\mathbf{Q},S}=\mathbf{Z}_{S}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{S}^{\times}
on
L^{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{S}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{S})
:

(\theta(u)\,\xi)(a)=\xi(u^{-1}a),\ \ \ \forall a\in\mathbf{Z}_{S}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{S},\ \forall\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{S}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{S}).

(19)

The local to global inclusion of the multiplicative groups
\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{\times}\subset C_{\mathbf{Q}}
of the local fields
\mathbf{Q}_{v}
in the idele class group continues to make sense in the semilocal case and gives canonical inclusions
\mathbf{Q}^{\times}_{v}\subset C_{\mathbf{Q},S}
for
v\in S
.

To prove the finiteness of the trace, one performs a cutoff in phase space,
i.e.
by limiting both
p,q
to absolute value
\leq\lambda
. To be more precise, let
P_{\lambda}
be the projection, in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{S}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{S})
on the subspace of functions which vanish for
|a|>\lambda
, and
\widehat{P_{\lambda}}
its conjugate by the Fourier transform. The cutoff is achieved by the operator
R_{\lambda}=\widehat{P}_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}
where
P_{\lambda}
effects the cutoff
|q|\leq\lambda
and
\widehat{P}_{\lambda}
effects the cutoff
|p|\leq\lambda
. The semilocal trace formula expresses the trace of the cutoff scaling action as follows. Let
h
be a test function with compact support on
C_{\mathbf{Q},S}
and
\theta(h):=\int h(u)\theta(u)d^{*}u
, then:

\rm{Trace}(\theta(h)R_{\lambda})=2h(1)\log\lambda+\sum_{v\in S}\int^{\prime}_{\mathbf{Q}^{\times}_{v}}\frac{h(u^{-1})}{|1-u|}\,d^{*}u+o(1).

(20)

This is the original formulation of the semilocal trace formula in
[
2
]
. The divergent term

signals the presence of the white light coming from the (trace of the) regular representation. In the perspective of this paper this is the contribution of the image of the generic point
\eta\in\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z}}
by the Abel-Jacobi map.

\mathbf{Z}
-module (it is divisible), the functor
\rm{Hom}(-,\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})
is exact. Applying this functor yields the exact sequence of dual groups:

0\longleftarrow\rm{Hom}(L,\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})\xleftarrow{i^{*}}\rm{Hom}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})\xleftarrow{\pi^{*}}\rm{Hom}(\mathbf{Q}/L,\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})\longleftarrow 0

Using the identification
\rm{Hom}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})\cong\mathbb{A}_{f}
, we can identify the term
\rm{Hom}(\mathbf{Q}/L,\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})
with the subgroup of
\mathbb{A}_{f}
consisting of elements that vanish on
L
(under the pairing).

\ker(i^{*})=\text{Image}(\pi^{*})\cong\{a\in\mathbb{A}_{f}\mid\psi(a)(h)=0,\,\forall h\in L\}.

The condition
\psi(a)(h)=0
in
\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
is equivalent to
ah\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
in
\mathbb{A}_{f}
. Thus,
\ker(i^{*})=L^{\perp}
.
By the First Isomorphism Theorem:

L^{\vee}=\text{Image}(i^{*})\cong\mathbb{A}_{f}/\ker(i^{*})=\mathbb{A}_{f}/L^{\perp}

∎

Let
S
be the set of primes
p
such that
L
is
p
-divisible (i.e.,
L\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p}\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}
).
This set
S
characterizes the "singularities" of the group
L
. Precisely, we have:

For
L=\mathbf{Z}
,
S=\emptyset
.

For
L=\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
,
S=\{p\}
.

For
L=\mathbf{Q}
,
S=\mathcal{P}
(the set of all primes).

We call the restricted product

\mathbb{A}_{S}={\prod_{p\in S}}^{{}^{\prime}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}

the
ring of
S
-adeles
.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{A}_{f}
and
L=\{q\in\mathbf{Q}\mid aq\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\}
. Let
S
be the set of all primes with
a_{p}=0
.
The adelic annihilator of
L
is given by:

L^{\perp}=\left(\prod_{p\notin S}a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}\right)\times\{0\}_{S}\subset\mathbb{A}_{f}.

The dual group decomposes as:

L^{\vee}\cong\mathbb{A}_{f}/L^{\perp}\cong\left({\prod_{p\notin S}}^{{}^{\prime}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}\right)\times\mathbb{A}_{S}.

(27)

Proof.

The condition
x\in L^{\perp}
means
xh\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
for all
h\in L
. This condition factorizes locally as:
x_{p}h_{p}\in\mathbf{Z}_{p}
for all
h_{p}\in L_{p}
where
L_{p}:=L\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. One sees that:

If
p\notin S
, then
L_{p}\cong a_{p}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. The condition
x_{p}L_{p}\subset\mathbf{Z}_{p}
is equivalent to
x_{p}\in a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. Thus the local component is
a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
.

If
p\in S
, then
L_{p}\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}
. The condition
x_{p}\mathbf{Q}_{p}\subset\mathbf{Z}_{p}
implies
x_{p}=0
(since
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
is unbounded). Thus the local component is
\{0\}
.

The quotient
\mathbb{A}_{f}/L^{\perp}
is the restricted product of its components, where:

For
p\in S
:
\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\{0\}\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}
. The product of these gives
\mathbb{A}_{S}
.

For
p\notin S
:
\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. The restricted product of these gives
\prod^{\prime}_{p\notin S}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
.

∎

The restricted product
\prod^{\prime}_{p\notin S}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
contains the torsion group
\bigoplus_{p\notin S}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. The group
\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
is isomorphic, by multiplication by
a_{p}
, to the
p
-torsion group
\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}=\mu_{p^{\infty}}
.

Remark A.4
.

It is not true in general that the restricted product
\prod^{\prime}_{p\notin S}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
is equal to the torsion group
\bigoplus_{p\notin S}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. In fact, this fails as soon as there are infinitely many
p\notin S
such that

n_{p}=v(a_{p})>0
.
Here
\mathbf{Z}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
is non-trivial for infinitely many
p
. The restricted product allows for elements that have non-zero entries in these finite groups for infinitely many primes. Consequently,
\prod^{\prime}_{p\notin S}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
is uncountable and contains elements of infinite order (e.g. a sequence
(x_{p})
where
x_{p}\in\mathbf{Z}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
has order
p^{v(a_{p})}
, and these orders grow to infinity).

A.3
The torsion subgroup of the dual

Proposition A.5
.

Let
L
be a torsion-free abelian group. There is a canonical isomorphism:

\rm{Tor}(\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}))\cong\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}),

where the limit is taken over the directed system of integers ordered by divisibility (
n\mid m
), with transition maps induced by the canonical inclusions
\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
(mapping
1\mapsto m/n
).

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
is the directed union of its finite subgroups:

\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{Z}\big/\mathbf{Z}\right)\cong\varinjlim_{n}\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}.

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})
.
The element
\phi
belongs to the torsion subgroup if and only if there exists an integer
n\geq 1
such that
n\cdot\phi=0
.
The condition
n\cdot\phi=0
means that for all
x\in L
,
n\phi(x)=0
in
\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
.
This is equivalent to saying that the image of
\phi
lies in the subgroup of elements of order dividing
n
:

\operatorname{Im}(\phi)\subseteq\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{Z}\big/\mathbf{Z}\cong\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}.

Thus, a homomorphism is torsion if and only if it factors through one of the finite groups
\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}
.
The colimit
\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
consists precisely of equivalence classes of such maps, which maps isomorphically to the set of maps into
\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
with bounded image.
∎

Proposition A.6
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{A}_{f}
and
L=\{q\in\mathbf{Q}\mid aq\in\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\}
. Let
S
be the set of all primes with
a_{p}=0
.
The torsion subgroup of the dual
L^{\vee}
is the subgroup :

\rm{Tor}(L^{\vee})=\bigoplus_{p\notin S}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}\subset L^{\vee}

It is isomorphic to the group
\mu_{(S)}
of roots of unity of order prime to
S
.

Proof.

By (
27
) the dual group is the product of the two terms

L^{\vee}\cong\left({\prod_{p\notin S}}^{{}^{\prime}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}\right)\times\mathbb{A}_{S}.

Let
x\in\rm{Tor}(L^{\vee})
be such that
nx=0
. Since
\mathbb{A}_{S}
is torsion free one has

x\in\left({\prod_{p\notin S}}^{{}^{\prime}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}\right)

and for primes
q
not dividing
n
the component
x_{q}
is
x_{q}=0
since
\mathbf{Q}_{q}/a_{q}\mathbf{Z}_{q}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{Q}_{q}/\mathbf{Z}_{q}
whose
n
torsion is
0
. Thus
x
is a finite sum of elements of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}/a_{p}\mathbf{Z}_{p}
for primes dividing
n
.∎

A.4
Consistency with Pontryagin duality

We clarify the relationship between the character dual
L^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})
and the standard Pontryagin dual
L^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbf{R}/\mathbf{Z})
.

Proposition A.7
.

Let
H
be a discrete abelian group.

1.

There is a strict inclusion
\rm{Tor}(H^{*})\subseteq H^{\vee}\subset H^{*}
.

2.

Any homomorphism from a torsion group into the Pontryagin dual factors through the character dual. Specifically:

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z},H^{*})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z},H^{\vee}).

3.

Furthermore, the image of any such map lies in the torsion subgroup
\rm{Tor}(H^{\vee})
.

Proof.

1. Let
\chi\in H^{*}
be a torsion element. Then
n\chi=0
for some
n
, implying
\chi(H)\subseteq\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{Z}/\mathbf{Z}\subset\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
. Thus
\chi\in H^{\vee}
.
However,
H^{\vee}
may contain elements of infinite order (e.g., the
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
component when
H=\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
), so
L^{\vee}
is generally larger than
\rm{Tor}(H^{*})
.

2. Let
\Phi:\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}\to H^{*}
. For any
z\in\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
, let
n
be its order. Then
n\Phi(z)=\Phi(nz)=0
. Thus
\Phi(z)
is a torsion element in
H^{*}
. By (1),
\Phi(z)\in H^{\vee}
.

3. Since
\Phi(z)
has finite order, it belongs to
\rm{Tor}(H^{\vee})
. In the case
H=\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
, where
H^{\vee}\cong\rm{Tor}(H^{\vee})\times\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, this implies the map is zero on the
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
factor.
∎

Appendix B
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})
and the monoid of the points of
\widehat{{\mathbf{N}}}^{\times}

We analyze the categorical structure of the topos associated with the multiplicative monoid
\mathbf{N}^{\times}
, and we show that the arithmetic tensor product of rank-1 groups arises naturally from the multiplication of integers.

Let
M=\mathbf{N}^{\times}
be the monoid of non-zero natural numbers under multiplication. We consider Grothendieck’s presheaf topos
\widehat{{\mathbf{N}}}^{\times}=\mathbf{PSh}(M^{\mathrm{op}})
, that is the category of sets equipped with a right action of
M
.
A point of
\widehat{{\mathbf{N}}}^{\times}
is a geometric morphism
p:\mathbf{Set}\to\widehat{{\mathbf{N}}}^{\times}
. By Diaconescu’s theorem, such points are classified by flat functors
F:M\to\mathbf{Set}
(or equivalently, flat left
M
-sets).
Since
M
is a commutative cancellative monoid, the category of flat
M
-sets is equivalent to the category of ordered rank-1 abelian groups (viewed as direct limits of the free
M
-set). Thus, a point
p
of
\widehat{{\mathbf{N}}}^{\times}
is uniquely determined, up to isomorphism, by a torsion free rank
1
group since the two orders on such a group are isomorphic by
x\mapsto-x
.

The multiplication of integers defines a monoid homomorphism:

\mu:M\times M\rightarrow M
,
(m,n)\mapsto mn
.

This map induces a geometric morphism between the corresponding toposes. On the level of points, this structure induces a product operation. Given two points
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
(viewed as flat
M
-sets), their external product
L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2}
is a flat
(M\times M)
-set. The product point
L
is obtained by the left Kan extension along
\mu
:

L=\operatorname{Lan}_{\mu}(L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2}).

Algebraically, this Kan extension corresponds to the tensor product over the monoid ring
\mathbf{Z}[M]
(that acts on the sets via the integers):
L\cong L_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}[M]}L_{2}
.

Since the action of
M
on these groups is compatible with the standard multiplication in
\mathbb{Z}
, this tensor product over the monoid coincides with the standard tensor product of abelian groups
L\cong L_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}L_{2}
.
Thus, the monoidal structure on the topos
\widehat{{\mathbf{N}}}^{\times}
induced by the multiplication of integers corresponds precisely to the tensor product of the rank-1 groups parametrizing its points.

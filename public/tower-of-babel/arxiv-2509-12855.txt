Timelike conjugate points in Lorentzian length spaces
James D.E. GrantMichael Kunzinger,Argam Ohanyan, Benedict Schinnerl, Roland Steinbauer

Abstract
We study notions of conjugate points along timelike geodesics in the synthetic setting of Lorentzian (pre-)length spaces, inspired by earlier work for metric spaces by Shankar–Sormani. After preliminary considerations on convergence of timelike and causal geodesics, we introduce and compare one-sided, symmetric, unreachable and ultimate conjugate points along timelike geodesics. We show that all such notions are compatible with the usual one in the smooth (strongly causal) spacetime setting. As applications, we prove a timelike Rauch comparison theorem, as well as a result closely related to the recently established Lorentzian Cartan–Hadamard theorem by Erös–Gieger. In the appendix, we give a detailed treatment of the Fréchet distance on the space of non-stopping curves up to reparametrization, a technical tool used throughout the paper. Keywords: Timelike conjugate point, geodesic, timelike Rauch comparison MSC2020: 53B30, 53C22, 53C23

Introduction

Lorentzian (pre-)length spaces were introduced by Kunzinger–Sämann
[
12
]
as a way to study Lorentzian and spacetime geometry using only the most essential structures, akin to metric geometry in positive definite signature. Since its introduction, the synthetic theory of spacetimes has experienced rapid development, and is a very active field of research today.

Geodesics (i.e., local distance realizers) in Lorentzian pre-length spaces were introduced and studied by Grant–Kunzinger–Sämann
[
9
]
. This notion turned out to be relevant in the study of hyperbolic angles by Beran–Sämann
[
4
]
and the recently established synthetic version of the sectional curvature variant of Bartnik’s splitting conjecture by Flores–Herrera–Solis
[
8
]
.

In this article, we are interested in studying notions of conjugate points along timelike geodesics in Lorentzian (pre-)length spaces,
following the fundamental contribution of Shankar–Sormani
[
20
]
on conjugate points in metric spaces. (See also the earlier work of Rinow
[
18
]
.) In the smooth Riemannian setting, Warner’s detailed study
[
23
]
of the conjugate locus sheds light on the exact behavior of the exponential map and the type of singularities that can occur. The results of Warner were extended to semi-Riemannian manifolds by Szeghy
[
21
]
.

In Lorentzian and spacetime geometry, conjugate points are fundamental in the study of various comparison-geometric results, such as the singularity theorems of Hawking and Penrose. Our aim in this paper is to initiate the study of conjugate points in the synthetic setting for Lorentzian geometry, i.e. Lorentzian pre-length spaces.

Our article is structured as follows: In Section
2
, we first recall some basics on Lorentzian pre-length spaces used throughout (Subsection
2.1
). We then study geodesics and their convergence properties in Subsection
2.2
. Due to the lack of arclength parametrization in the generality that we work with, we need to consider equivalence classes of geodesics up to suitable reparametrizations. To this end, in Appendix
A
, we give a treatment of the
Fréchet distance
on such equivalence classes of non-stopping curves on a metric spaces that is suitable for our purposes. Our main result in this context is the closure of the set of (equivalence classes of) causal geodesics
\Geo(X)
in the set of non-stopping curves with respect to the Fréchet distance (see Proposition
2.26
).

In Section
3
, following the terminology of Shankar–Sormani
[
20
]
, we introduce and study various notions of conjugate points along timelike geodesics: One-sided and symmetric conjugate points in Subsection
3.1
, families of timelike geodesics in Subsection
3.2
, unreachable and ultimate conjugate points in Subsection
3.3
, as well as some notions of cut points in Subsection
3.4
. We rely on Szeghy’s
[
21
]
generalizations of Warner’s
[
23
]
results to show compatibility of these notions with the smooth (strongly causal) spacetime setting.

Section
4
deals with two applications of these notions: a timelike Rauch comparison theorem (Theorem
4.1
) and a result similar to the Cartan–Hadamard Theorem (Theorem
4.2
), which establishes the nonexistence of a certain kind of conjugate points along sufficiently short geodesics in spaces with upper timelike curvature bounds. The main tools that we use (aside from those that we develop here) is the stacking principle of Beran–Ohanyan–Rott–Solis
[
3
]
and the synthetic Lorentzian Cartan–Hadamard theorem of Erös–Gieger
[
7
]
.

Finally, in Section
5
, we give an outlook on related avenues of future research.

2
Geodesics in Lorentzian pre-length spaces

2.1
Preliminaries on Lorentzian pre-length spaces

In this section, we collect a few basics on Lorentzian (pre-)length spaces that will be required throughout. We refer to Kunzinger–Sämann
[
12
]
for full details.

Definition 2.1

(Lorentzian (pre-)length spaces)
.

A
Lorentzian pre-length space
is a quintuple
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
, where

(i)

(X,d)
is a metric space;

(ii)

\ll,\leq
are transitive relations (the
chronological
resp.
causal relations
),
\leq
is in addition reflexive and contains
\ll
;

(iii)

\tau\colon X^{2}\to[0,\infty]
is a lower semicontinuous function that vanishes outside of the causal relation
\leq
, the timelike relation
\ll
coincides with
\{\tau>0\}
and for all
x\leq y\leq z
,

\tau(x,z)\geq\tau(x,y)+\tau(y,z).

(2.1)

We employ standard notation
I^{\pm}(A),J^{\pm}(A)
for chronological resp. causal futures/pasts of a subset
A\subseteq X
. We write
I(A,B)\coloneqq I^{+}(A)\cap I^{-}(B)
, similarly
J(A,B)
, for
A,B\subseteq X
. A subset
C\subseteq X
is said to be
causally convex
if
J(x,y)\subseteq C
whenever
x,y\in C
. A Lorentzian pre-length space is
chronological
if
\ll
is irreflexive and
causal
if
\leq
is a partial order.

Definition 2.2

(Timelike/causal curves and
\tau
-length)
.

A
future-directed timelike (resp. causal) curve
(past-directed curves are defined analogously) in a Lorentzian pre-length space
X
is a nowhere constant map
\gamma\colon I=[a,b]\to X
(i.e.,
\gamma
is not constant on any non-trivial subinterval of
I
) that
is Lipschitz (w.r.t.
d
) and such that for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in I
with
t_{1}<t_{2}
we have
\gamma(t_{1})\ll\gamma(t_{2})
(resp.)
\gamma(t_{1})\leq\gamma(t_{2})
(causal curves on unbounded intervals can be defined analogously). Its
\tau
-length
is defined as

L_{\tau}(\gamma)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\tau(\gamma(t_{i}),\gamma(t_{i+1}))\colon N\in\mathbb{N},\ a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=b\right\}.

A reparametrization of a future-directed causal curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
is a future-directed causal curve
\lambda\colon[c,d]\to X
such that
\gamma=\lambda\circ\varphi
, where
\varphi\colon[a,b]\to[c,d]
is continuous, surjective, and strictly monotonically increasing. A future-directed causal curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
is called a
maximizer
if
L_{\tau}(\gamma)=\tau(\gamma(a),\gamma(b))
.

In particular, the reparametrized curve
\lambda\circ\varphi
above must itself be Lipschitz. The
\tau
-length of a causal curve is invariant under reparametrizations (
[
12
, Lem. 2.18]
). We note that causal curves were defined in
[
12
]
as non-constant maps rather than nowhere constant. We choose the current convention for two main reasons, one being that the restriction of a causal curve to any non-trivial subinterval of its domain should again be causal, and the other that causal curves in the standard smooth spacetime setting are indeed nowhere constant. The Fréchet distance as detailed in Appendix
A
then applies to causal curves.

We also note that all causal curves are assumed to be future-directed unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Definition 2.3

(Model spaces)
.

For integers
0\leq m\leq n
, denote by
\mathbb{R}_{m}^{n}
the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the inner product
b(v,w)\coloneqq-\sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}w_{i}+\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}v_{i}w_{i},
where
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
and
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{R}
. The
Lorentzian
K
-planes
or the
comparison spaces of constant curvature
K
are the following:

-

For
K>0
,
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
is the universal cover of
\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}:b(v,v)=\frac{1}{K^{2}}\right\}
.

-

For
K<0
,
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
is the universal cover of
\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}_{2}^{3}:b(v,v)=-\frac{1}{K^{2}}\right\}
.

-

For
K=0
,
\mathbb{L}^{2}(0)\coloneqq\mathbb{R}_{1}^{2}
is the two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime.

The timelike diameter of
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
is
D_{K}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{-K}}
for
K<0
and

D_{K}=\infty
for
K\geq 0
. We will denote the time separation of
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
by
\bar{\tau}
.

Definition 2.4

(Alexandrov topology, strong causality)
.

The
Alexandrov topology
on a Lorentzian pre-length space
X
is defined as the topology generated by the subbase

\{I(x,y):x,y\in X\}.

(2.2)

We say
X
is
strongly causal
if the Alexandrov topology agrees with the metric topology on
X
.

Following
[
2
]
, we call a Lorentzian pre-length space
regular
if every maximizer between two timelike related points is timelike, i.e., contains no null subsegment. Moreover, a Lorentzian pre-length space is called
causally path-connected
if any two distinct causally related points are joined by a causal curve and any two timelike related points by a timelike curve.

Remark 2.5

(Localizability)
.

A
localizable
Lorentzian pre-length space is a Lorentzian pre-length space equipped with well-behaved local time separation functions, see
[
12
, Def. 3.16]
for the precise definition. It is called
regularly localizable
if the maximizers w.r.t. the local time separations (which exist by definition of localizability) are required to be timelike if they connect timelike related points. Regularly localizable Lorentzian pre-length spaces are regular, cf.
[
12
, Thm. 3.18]
.

Proposition 2.6

(Upper semicontinuity of
L_{\tau}
, Prop. 3.17 in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
X
be a localizable and strongly causal Lorentzian pre-length space. Then
L_{\tau}
is upper semicontinuous in the following sense: If
\gamma_{n}\colon[a,b]\to X
is a sequence of causal curves converging
d
-uniformly to a causal curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
, then

L_{\tau}(\gamma)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}L_{\tau}(\gamma_{n}).

(2.3)

For our definition of timelike (sectional) curvature bounds, we follow the conventions of Beran–Kunzinger–Rott
[
2
, Def. 3.1]
.

Definition 2.7

(Timelike curvature bounds)
.

We say a Lorentzian pre-length space
X
has
timelike curvature bounded from above (resp. below)
by
K\in\mathbb{R}
if every point has a neighborhood
U
such that

(i)

the set
U^{2}\cap\tau^{-1}([0,D_{K}))
is open in
X^{2}
and the restriction of
\tau
to this set is continuous and finite-valued;

(ii)

whenever
x\leq y
with
\tau(x,y)<D_{K}
and
x,y\in U
, there exists a maximizer in
U
from
x
to
y
;

(iii)

whenever
\Delta(x,y,z)
is a timelike triangle in
U
(i.e.,
x\ll y\ll z
with timelike maximizers running between them) and
\Delta(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z})
is the comparison triangle with equal side lengths in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
, for any
p,q\in\Delta(x,y,z)
and corresponding points
\bar{p},\bar{q}\in\Delta(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z})
(i.e., points with equal time separation to the endpoints of their sides as
p,q
), we have

\tau(p,q)\geq\bar{\tau}(\bar{p},\bar{q})\quad(\text{resp.\ }\leq).

(2.4)

We will mainly rely on consequences of timelike curvature bounds, specifically the stacking principle
[
3
, Prop. 2.42]
, which describes how the stacking of a subdivision of comparison triangles stack in the model space, as well as the Lorentzian Cartan–Hadamard theorem and related results due to Erös–Gieger
[
7
]
. We refer to these works for details.

2.2
Convergence of geodesics

In this section, we establish results on the convergence of geodesics in Lorentzian (pre-)length spaces. We begin by recalling the notion of geodesic introduced in
[
4
, Def. 1.7]
:

Definition 2.8
.

A future-directed causal curve
\gamma\colon I\to X
is a
geodesic
if for each
t\in I
there
exists a neighborhood
[a,b]
of
t
(i.e.,
a<t<b
, allowing for equality at the
endpoints of
I
) such that
\gamma|_{[a,b]}
is a maximizer, i.e.,
\tau(\gamma(a),\gamma(b))=L_{\tau}(\gamma|_{[a,b]})
.

Since the
\tau
-length of a causal curve
\gamma
is parametrization-invariant, any reparametrization of a geodesic
\gamma
is also a geodesic, and we shall henceforth consider geodesics as equivalence classes of reparametrizations. We emphasize that whenever we speak of ”uniqueness” in the context of geodesics, we always mean ”up to reparametrization”.

Definition 2.9

(Finite
\tau
-measurability)
.

A Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
is
finitely
\tau
-measurable
if every causal curve segment
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
has finite
\tau
-length.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
be a localizable Lorentzian pre-length space. Then it is finitely
\tau
-measurable.

Proof.

This follows from the definition of localizability, cf.
[
12
, Def. 3.16]
for the latter.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

In
[
9
, Def. 4.1]
, geodesics were defined as future-directed timelike (resp. causal, null) curves
\gamma\colon I\to X
on localizing Lorentzian pre-length spaces as follows: for every
t_{0}\in I
there exists a localizing neighborhood
\Omega
of
\gamma(t_{0})
and a relatively closed interval
J\subseteq I
with
t_{0}
in its relative interior, such that
\gamma(J)\subseteq\Omega
and
\gamma|_{J}
is maximizing in
\Omega
with respect to the local time separation function
\omega
. Thus, such curves are geodesics in the sense of Definition
2.8
within each ‘local’ Lorentzian pre-length space
\Omega
as above. Moreover, in strongly causal localizable Lorentzian pre-length spaces both definitions agree by the proof of
[
9
, Lem. 4.3]
.

Remark 2.12

(On geodesics in low causality)
.

The definition of causal geodesic we gave as a local
\tau
-maximizer becomes meaningless in very low causality. For example, consider the Lorentz cylinder
X=S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the metric
-d\theta^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(dx^{i})^{2}
, then
\tau=+\infty
on the timelike relation
\ll
. In particular, the
\tau
-length of every timelike curve segment is
+\infty
, hence every such curve is maximizing and thus (in particular) a geodesic.

An important class of examples of Lorentzian (pre-) length spaces is furnished by smooth manifolds
M
equipped with a continuous and causally plain metric
g
(cf.
[
12
, Prop. 5.8]
). To compare the notion of geodesics in Lorentzian manifolds with that of Definition
2.8
, let us now extend the validity of
[
12
, Prop. 2.32]

from smooth Lorentzian metrics to a large class of continuous ones.

Proposition 2.13

(
L_{g}
vs.
L_{\tau}
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a spacetime such that
g
is continuous, causally plain and strongly causal. Then the
g
- and
\tau
-lengths of every causal curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to(M,g)
agree:

L_{g}(\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}\sqrt{-g(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})}\,dt=L_{\tau}(\gamma).

Proof.

To prove this we employ a sequence of smooth Lorentzian metrics
g_{k}
with wider lightcones than
g
, approximating
g
as constructed in
[
14
, Lem. A.1]
. Then for the corresponding time separation functions
\tau_{k}
of
g_{k}
we have that
\tau
is the locally uniform and non-increasing limit of the
\tau_{k}
(see
[
14
, Lem. A.2]
). In particular,
L_{\tau_{k}}(\gamma)\geq L_{\tau}(\gamma)
for all
k
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and choose a partition
P=\{a=t_{0}<\dots<t_{N}=b\}
of
[a,b]
such that

L_{\tau}(\gamma)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tau(\gamma(t_{i-1}),\gamma(t_{i}))-\varepsilon.

By uniform convergence, there exists some
k_{0}=k_{0}(\varepsilon,P)
such that, for all
k\geq k_{0}
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tau(\gamma(t_{i-1}),\gamma(t_{i}))\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tau_{k}(\gamma(t_{i-1}),\gamma(t_{i}))-\varepsilon.

Altogether,

L_{\tau_{k}}(\gamma)\geq L_{\tau}(\gamma)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tau_{k}(\gamma(t_{i-1}),\gamma(t_{i}))-2\varepsilon\geq L_{\tau_{k}}(\gamma)-2\varepsilon.

Consequently,
L_{\tau}(\gamma)=\lim_{k\to\infty}L_{\tau_{k}}(\gamma)=\lim_{k\to\infty}L_{g_{k}}(\gamma)=L_{g}(\gamma)
.
∎

Remark 2.14

(Comparing notions of geodesics)
.

It follows from Proposition
2.13
that (in strongly causal spaces) geodesics in the sense of Definition
2.8
will coincide with geodesics
in the sense of solutions of the geodesic equation as long as the regularity of the metric is sufficient to allow for a satisfactory
solution theory of these ODEs and, additionally, as long as such solutions are indeed local maximizers of the
g
-length-functional.
This certainly is the case for
g\in C^{1,1}
(cf.
[
15
]
or
[
13
]
). However,
the Hartmann–Wintner-type spacetime

M\coloneqq\mathbb{R}\times(-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with metric
g\coloneqq-dt^{2}+dx^{2}+(1-|x|^{\lambda})g_{n}
, where
\lambda\in(1,2)
and
g_{n}
is the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is
an example of a
C^{1,\lambda-1}
-Lorentzian metric where solutions of the geodesic equations fail to even locally maximize the
g
-length, cf.
[
19
, Ex. 2.4]
.

Lemma 2.15

(Local maximization and causal character)
.

Let
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space. If
X
is regular, then causal geodesics in
X
have a causal character, i.e. they are either timelike or null.

Proof.

Given a causal geodesic
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
, choose a subdivision
a=t_{0}<\dots<t_{N}=b
such that each
\gamma|_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}
is maximal,
and therefore has a causal character due to regularity of
X
. Since, for each
i
, there is a neighborhood of
t_{i}
in which
\gamma
is maximal,
and therefore has a causal character, any two consecutive segments of
\gamma
have the same causal character.
∎

Before discussing the limit curve theorem and its implications for geodesics, we adapt some material from
[

]
to our setting.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and
U\subseteq X
open. We define the local timelike and causal relations on
U
as follows: For
p,q\in U
we say
p\ll_{U}q
(
p\leq_{U}q
) if there exists a future-directed timelike (causal) curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
with
\gamma(a)=p
,
\gamma(b)=q
and
\gamma\left([a,b]\right)\subseteq U
.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
be a causally path-connected Lorentzian pre-length space. Then, for any causally convex
V\subseteq X
, we have
\leq_{V}\,=\left.\leq\right|_{V\times V}
.

Proof.

If
p,q\in V
with
p\leq_{V}q
, then there exists a future-directed, causal curve from
p
to
q
, hence
p\leq q
, so
(p,q)\in\left.\leq\right|_{V\times V}
.

Conversely, let
p,q\in V
with
p\leq q
. Since
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
is causally path-connected, there exists a causal curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
with
\gamma(a)=p
,
\gamma(b)=q
. Since
V
is causally convex,
\gamma\left([a,b]\right)\subseteq V
. Therefore,
p\leq_{V}q
.
∎

Definition 2.18

(Local weak causal closedness)
.

A set
U\subseteq X
is
weakly causally closed
if, for all sequences
\left(p_{n}\right)_{n}
and
\left(q_{n}\right)_{n}
in
U
such that
p_{n}\leq_{U}q_{n}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
p_{n}\to p\in U
and
q_{n}\to q\in U
, one has
p\leq_{U}q
. A Lorentzian pre-length space is

if every point has a weakly causally closed neighborhood.

Note that, unlike

, we do not require that

be causally path-connected in our definition of

:

(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)

1

1

(i)
\Rightarrow

. Let

Theorem 2.20

(Limit curve theorem for segments)
.

Let

Proposition 2.26

(Topology of

Let

Let
(\gamma_{n})\subseteq\Geo(X)

We shall also require the following useful variant of the limit curve theorem. (See
[

for a related result.) To this end, recall that a locally weakly causally closed, causally path-connected and localizable Lorentzian pre-length space is called a

\tau(x,y)=\sup\{L_{\tau}(\gamma):\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X\text{ causal with }\gamma(a)=x,\,\gamma(b)=y\}\cup\{0\}.

(2.6)

A Lorentzian length space is

if it is non-totally imprisoning (i.e.,

Theorem 2.27

(Limit curve theorem: Converging endpoints)
.

Let
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
be a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian length space. Let
\gamma_{n}\colon[0,1]\to X
be a sequence of future-directed causal curves such that
p_{n}\coloneqq\gamma_{n}(0)\to p
and
q_{n}\coloneqq\gamma_{n}(1)\to q
with
p\neq q
. Then a subsequence of a reparametrized sequence
(\tilde{\gamma}_{n})
converges uniformly to a future-directed causal curve from
p
to
q
. Moreover, we have:

(i)

If each
\gamma_{n}
is a geodesic and
X
is locally maximizing, then
\gamma
is also a geodesic, and in this case
L_{\tau}(\gamma)=\lim_{n}L_{\tau}(\gamma_{n})
. Moreover, if
X
is regular and
p\ll q
, then
\gamma
is a future-directed timelike geodesic.

(ii)

If
(\gamma_{n})
is a limit maximizing sequence (in particular: if each
\gamma_{n}
is maximizing), then
\gamma
is maximizing.

Proof.

Choose
\tilde{p}\in I^{-}(p)
and
\tilde{q}\in I^{+}(q)
. Then
\gamma_{n}([0,1])\subseteq J(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
, which is compact. Since
X
is non-totally imprisoning, there is a uniform upper bound on the
d
-arclengths of causal curves contained in
J(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
, hence we may reparametrize the
\gamma_{n}
with respect to
d
-arclength to get a uniformly Lipschitz sequence,
(again denoted by the same letter)
\gamma_{n}\colon[a_{n},b_{n}]\to X
, with both
(a_{n})
and
(b_{n})
bounded sequences. Without loss of generality we may suppose that
a_{n}\to a
and
b_{n}\to b
. Let
\alpha_{n}
be a future-directed causal curve connecting
\tilde{p}
to
p_{n}=\gamma_{n}(a_{n})
, parametrized by
d
-arclength, say
\alpha_{n}\colon[a_{n}-\delta_{n},a_{n}]\to X
for some
\delta_{n}>0
. Then
\delta_{n}\geq d(\tilde{p},p_{n})\to d(\tilde{p},p)>0
. Similarly, let
\beta_{n}\colon[b_{n},b_{n}+\eta_{n}]\to X
be a
d
-arclength reparametrization of a future-directed causal curve from
q_{n}=\gamma_{n}(b_{n})
to
\tilde{q}
. Here,
\eta_{n}\geq d(q_{n},\tilde{q})\to d(q,\tilde{q})>0
. Hence we may suppose that
\min(\delta_{n},\eta_{n})>2c
for some

and all

. Let now
\hat{\gamma}_{n}
denote the restriction of the concatenation of
\alpha_{n}
,
\gamma_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
to the interval
[a-c,b+c]
. Applying Theorem

to this sequence it follows that we can extract a subsequence that converges uniformly to a causal curve whose

-arclength reparametrization is defined on

.
Denoting by
\gamma
the restriction of this curve to

to

.

Part
(i)
now follows from Proposition
2.26
and the assumption that

Part
(ii)
follows from Theorem

, which states that the curve
\gamma
is maximizing if the sequence
\gamma_{n}
is limit maximizing.
∎

(Limit curve theorem for

We may reformulate Theorem

We end this subsection with a property of geodesic convergence in smooth semi-Riemannian manifolds.

Lemma 2.29
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth semi-Riemannian manifold. Let
a<0\leq 1<b
. Then the following are equivalent for geodesic segments
\gamma_{n},\gamma\colon[a,b]\to M
:

(i)

\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
in
C^{0}
.

(ii)

\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
in
C^{\infty}
.

(iii)

\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0)\to\gamma^{\prime}(0)
in
TM
.

Proof.

Clearly,
(ii)
implies
(i)
. To see that
(iii)
implies
(ii)
, observe that if
(p_{n},v_{n})\in TM
with
(p_{n},v_{n})\to(p,v)
, then the fact that
\gamma_{n}(t)\coloneqq\exp_{p_{n}}(tv_{n})
converges to
\gamma(t)\coloneqq\exp_{p}(tv)
in
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
on the maximal domain of
\gamma
is a consequence of basic ODE theory, see e.g.
[
11
, Cor. 2.6]
. We are left with showing that
(i)
implies
(iii)
. Suppose
\gamma_{n}\colon[a,b]\to M
converges uniformly to
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to M
. It is well-known that the exponential map
E\colon\mathcal{D}\subseteq TM\to M^{2}
is a diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of the zero section in
TM
onto a neighborhood of the diagonal in
M^{2}
. We may cover
\gamma
with finitely many convex normal neighborhoods

such that
\bigcup_{i}U_{i}^{2}
is contained in the aforementioned neighborhood of the diagonal. For large enough

, the

cover the corresponding portions of
\gamma_{n}
due to uniform convergence. But then

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0)=E^{-1}(\gamma_{n}(0),\exp_{\gamma_{n}(0)}(\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0)))=E^{-1}(\gamma_{n}(0),\gamma_{n}(1))\to E^{-1}(\gamma(0),\gamma(1))=\gamma^{\prime}(0),

concluding the proof.

This section is dedicated to the discussion of several notions of conjugate points in the synthetic setting. These notions were introduced in positive definite signature by Shankar–Sormani
[

]
(cf. also Rinow
[

). We will for the most part follow their terminology and conventions when adapting the notions to the Lorentzian setting.

3.1
Symmetric conjugate points

For the following discussion, let
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
be a finitely
\tau
-measurable Lorentzian pre-length space.

Definition 3.1

(One-sided conjugate points)
.

Let
\gamma\in\TGeo(X)
be a future-directed timelike geodesic from
p
to
q
. We say
q
is
one-sided conjugate to

p
if there is a sequence
q_{i}\to q
and for each
i
, there exist two distinct future-directed timelike geodesics
\eta_{i}\neq\gamma_{i}\in\TGeo(X)
from
p
to
q_{i}
such that
\eta_{i},\gamma_{i}\to\gamma
with respect to
d_{\Gamma}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

In a strongly causal smooth spacetime
(M,g)
, points
p\ll q
are one-sided conjugate points along a future-directed timelike geodesic
\gamma
if and only if they are conjugate points (in the Jacobi field sense).

Proof.

Let
p,q\in M
be such that
p\ll q
,
\gamma
a future-directed timelike geodesic from
p
to
q
, and suppose that
q
is one-sided conjugate to
p
along
\gamma
.
Indirectly assuming that
p
and
q
are not conjugate along
\gamma
, then
\exp_{p}
is regular around
v\coloneqq\exp_{p}^{-1}(q)
. In particular the exponential map is injective onto a neighborhood of
q
and so there cannot exist points as given in the definition of one-sided conjugacy.

For the reverse implication we use a generalization, due to D. Szeghy
[
21
]
, of a classical result by F. W. Warner
[
23
]
. Let
\gamma(t)=\exp_{p}(tv)
be a timelike geodesic and let
q=\gamma(1)
be conjugate to
p
along

\gamma
. Denoting by
C(p)\subseteq T_{p}M
the conjugate locus of
p
we thus have
v\in C(p)
. Let
U\subseteq T_{p}M
be an open neighborhood of
v
consisting entirely of timelike vectors. By

[
21
, Thm. 2.1]
, together with
[
21
, Rem. 4.1]
, both the set
C^{R}(p)
of
regular conjugate vectors and the set
C(p)^{\prime}
of conjugate vectors of locally minimal multiplicity
are open and dense in
C(p)
, hence so is their intersection
C^{R}(p)\cap C(p)^{\prime}
.
Pick
v^{\prime}\in C^{R}(p)\cap C(p)^{\prime}\cap U
. Then by
[
21
, Lem. 4.2]
,
\exp_{p}
is a Warner map on a suitable open neighborhood
V\subseteq U
of
v^{\prime}
(note that by
[
21
, Sec. 5]
, the fact that
v^{\prime}
is timelike implies that
\mathcal{D}_{v^{\prime}}
is spacelike, so the assumptions of
[
21
, Lem. 4.2]
are indeed satisfied). Therefore (cf.
[
21
, Thm. 2.2]
and the remarks following it),
\exp_{p}
is not injective on any neighborhood of

. In particular, it is not injective on

.
Consequently, there exist vectors

with
v_{i},w_{i}\to v
and
\exp_{p}(v_{i})=\exp_{p}(w_{i})\eqqcolon q_{i}
. The distinct timelike geodesics
\gamma_{i}(t)\coloneqq\exp_{p}(tv_{i})
and
\sigma_{i}(t)\coloneqq\exp_{p}(tw_{i})
then satisfy one-sided conjugacy.
∎

Next, we discuss a notion of conjugate point similar to one-sided conjugacy, but here the initial point is also allowed to vary.

Definition 3.3

(Symmetric conjugate points)
.

Let

. Then

are called

along a future-directed timelike geodesic
\gamma\in\TGeo(X)
running between them if there are
p_{i}\to p
and
q_{i}\to q
such that for each

there are two distinct future-directed timelike geodesics
\gamma_{i}\neq\sigma_{i}\in\TGeo(X)
from
p_{i}
to
q_{i}
such that
\gamma_{i}\to\gamma
and
\sigma_{i}\to\gamma
with respect to
d_{\Gamma}
.

Clearly, any one-sided conjugate point is also symmetrically conjugate. The converse is open even in the metric case, cf.
[
20
, Open Problem 2.5]
.

Remark 3.4

(Symmetric conjugate points - trivial case)
.

Let
X
be a finitely
\tau
-measurable
Lorentzian pre-length space. If
\gamma,\sigma\in\TGeo(X)
are different geodesics with the same endpoints

and

, then
p,q
are one-sided conjugate (and hence also symmetric conjugate).

Proposition 3.5

(Equivalence of notions)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a strongly causal smooth spacetime and let
\gamma
be a future-directed timelike geodesic from
p
to
q
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

q
is one-sided conjugate to
p
along
\gamma
.

(ii)

p,q
are symmetrically conjugate along
\gamma
.

(iii)

q
is a conjugate point of

along
\gamma
in the classical sense.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
, (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii). As observed above, one-sided conjugate implies symmetric conjugate, i.e. (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Finally, (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) follows by the same
proof as in
[
20
, Thm. 2.6]
.
∎

3.2
Families of timelike geodesics

Definition 3.6

(Continuous timelike families)
.

Let
\gamma\in\TGeo(X)
be a future-directed timelike geodesic from
p=\gamma(0)
to
q=\gamma(1)
. A map
F\colon U\times V\to\TGeo(X)

with

and
V

is called a

\gamma
if

U\ll V
(i.e.,
p^{\prime}\ll q^{\prime}
for all
p^{\prime}\in U
and

) and

goes from
p^{\prime}
to
q^{\prime}
for each
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\in U\times V

and
F(p,q)=\gamma
.
We say that
F
is
continuous about

\gamma
if
F
is a continuous (w.r.t.
d_{\Gamma}
) map.

F
is called
continuous at

\gamma
if it is only required to be continuous (w.r.t.
d_{\Gamma}
) at the point
(p,q)
itself.

Observe that since
\ll
is an open relation (cf.
[
12
, Prop. 2.13]
), whenever

there exist neighborhoods
U
of
x
and
V
of

such that
p^{\prime}\ll q^{\prime}
for all
p^{\prime}\in U
and
q^{\prime}\in V
.

Definition 3.7

(Unique timelike families)
.

Let
\gamma
be a future-directed timelike geodesic. We say that there exists a unique timelike
family which is continuous about
\gamma
resp. at
\gamma
if

(i)

there exists a timelike family
F\colon U\times V\to\TGeo(X)
that is continuous about
\gamma
resp. at
\gamma
, and

(ii)

if

is another timelike family about
\gamma
that is continuous about
\gamma
resp. at
\gamma
, then there exist neighborhoods
U^{\prime\prime}\subseteq U\cap U^{\prime}
and

of
\gamma(0)

resp.
\gamma(1)
such that
F
coincides with
F^{\prime}
on

.

Another way to express this is to say that there is exists a unique germ of timelike
families around
(\gamma(0),\gamma(1))
with the respective continuity property. Given a timelike family
F\colon U\times V\to\TGeo(X)
about
\gamma
, we shall denote by
[F]
the germ around
(\gamma(0),\gamma(1))
determined by

\gamma
and unique among timelike families that are continuous at

.

A

-continuous family is a family as above that is continuous w.r.t.

The following basic observation will repeatedly be used below.

Lemma 3.8

Let
\gamma\in\TGeo(X)

Our assumptions mean that (the second alternative in) (i) in Definition

Suppose that every

Definition 3.27

(Causal cut points)

In this section, we give two applications that use of our notions of conjugate points, namely a timelike Rauch comparison theorem (in analogy with Shankar–Sormani

4.1
A timelike Rauch comparison theorem

Theorem 4.1

(Timelike Rauch comparison theorem)
.

Let
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
be a chronological, regular, locally timelike maximizing and finitely
\tau
-measurable Lorentzian pre-length space with timelike curvature
\leq\kappa
and let
\gamma\in\TGeo(X)
be such that
p\coloneqq\gamma(0)
and
q\coloneqq\gamma(1)
are symmetric conjugate along
\gamma
. Then
L_{\tau}(\gamma)\geq D_{\kappa}
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a timelike geodesic
\gamma\in\TGeo(X)
with
L_{\tau}(\gamma)<D_{\kappa}
whose endpoints are symmetric conjugate. Pick a representative, i.e., fix a parametrization
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
.

Cover
\gamma([0,1])
by a finite collection
\mathcal{U}
of locally timelike maximizing comparison neighborhoods, and let
L
denote the Lebesgue number of the open cover
\{U\cap\gamma([0,1])\colon U\in\mathcal{U}\}
of
\gamma([0,1])
. Choose
\delta>0
such that
d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))<L
for any
s,t\in[0,1]
with
|t-s|<\delta
. Then picking points
0=t_{0}<\dots<t_{N}=1
in such a way that
|t_{i+1}-t_{i}|<\delta/3
for each
i
, we can guarantee that each segment
\gamma([t_{i},t_{i+3}])
lies entirely in one element of
\mathcal{U}
(
i=0,\dots,N-3
). Because of this, these pieces of
\gamma_{n}
and
\sigma_{n}
are maximizing and timelike. Since the endpoints
p,q
of
\gamma
are symmetric conjugate, we may find sequences
(\gamma_{n}),(\sigma_{n})\subseteq\TGeo(X)
with
\gamma_{n}\neq\sigma_{n}
for all

but with the same endpoints
p_{n},q_{n}
and
\gamma_{n},\sigma_{n}\to\gamma
in the
d_{\Gamma}
-topology. It follows that there exist representatives (denoted in the same way) converging to
\gamma
uniformly and in
\tau
-length. For
n
large, we therefore also have that, for each
i

\gamma_{n}([t_{i},t_{i+3}])
and
\sigma_{n}([t_{i},t_{i+3}])

\mathcal{U}
as
\gamma([t_{i},t_{i+3}])
.

In the remainder of the proof we additionally fix

sufficiently large to secure that

and that

, as well as

p_{n}\ll\sigma_{n}(t_{1})\ll\gamma_{n}(t_{2})\ll\sigma_{n}(t_{3})\ll\dots\ll q_{n}
.

We now consider the timelike triangle
\Delta(p_{n},\gamma_{n}(t_{1}),\sigma_{n}(t_{3}))
(with the obvious sides; for the side from
\gamma_{n}(t_{1})
to
\sigma_{n}(t_{3})
we choose a timelike maximizer entirely in the corresponding comparison neighborhood). This triangle can be subdivided into the smaller timelike triangles
\Delta(p_{n},\gamma_{n}(t_{1}),\sigma_{n}(t_{2}))
and
\Delta(\gamma_{n}(t_{1}),\sigma_{n}(t_{2}),\sigma_{n}(t_{3}))
.

This puts us into the situation of

3

, from which we conclude that the stacking of the corresponding comparison triangles of the sub-triangles in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\kappa)
is concave. Analogously, we subdivide the next timelike triangle

\Delta(\gamma_{n}(t_{1}),\sigma_{n}(t_{3}),\gamma_{n}(t_{4}))
into the timelike sub-triangles
\Delta(\gamma_{n}(t_{1}),\gamma_{n}(t_{2}),\sigma_{n}(t_{3}))
and
\Delta(\gamma_{n}(t_{2}),\sigma_{n}(t_{3}),\gamma_{n}(t_{4}))
and iterate this procedure until we reach the endpoint

.
In this way, we obtain piecewise timelike geodesics
\bar{\gamma}_{n},\bar{\sigma}_{n}
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\kappa)
with common endpoints whose Lorentzian arclength is the same as that of
\gamma_{n},\sigma_{n}

<D_{\kappa}
.

We may assume that
\bar{\gamma}_{n},\bar{\sigma}_{n}
are parametrized to unit speed.
Consider now a breakpoint

and
\bar{\beta}_{n}
the geodesic sub-segments of

by its image under an isometry of

whose derivative maps the initial velocity vector of

to the terminal velocity vector of

and
\bar{\beta}_{n}
into an unbroken geodesic

Concavity of the stacking implies that these unbroken geodesics will remain within the region bounded by the original stacking and therefore also have to meet. Moreover, since we applied an isometry in each step, the resulting unbroken geodesic segments retain the Lorentzian length of
\bar{\gamma}_{n}
and
\bar{\sigma}_{n}

\mathbb{L}^{2}(\kappa)
that meet at
\bar{\tau}
-length strictly less than

, a contradiction.
∎

5
Outlook

In this work, we have introduced and studied timelike conjugate points in Lorentzian (pre-)length spaces. We have compared various notions among each other and with their Jacobi field counterpart in the smooth setting. Moreover, we have given applications (Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
) illustrating their use.

There are several avenues for future work on conjugate points in the synthetic Lorentzian setting. First, a study of conjugate points along null geodesics would be of great interest. Here, care should be taken not to disrupt the compatibility with the smooth case.

Moreover, a link between conjugate points and singularity theorems should be established. In this context, there are two tasks: Obtaining conjugate points from curvature bounds, and then using conjugate points to obtain singularities. A version of the latter was investigated by Minguzzi in the context of closed cone structures
[
16
]
.

Appendix A
The Fréchet distance of curves

In this Appendix, we introduce the Fréchet distance of curves and prove some basic properties that were used in the main part of the paper. This notion indeed goes back to Fréchet and has found many applications (cf., e.g.,
[
1
]
), but we are not aware of a reference that rigorously establishes these fundamentals.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. By a path in
X
we mean an element
\gamma
of the space of continuous maps
C([0,1],X)
from
[0,1]
into
X
(we use the interval
[0,1]
for convenience). Two paths
\gamma,\tilde{\gamma}
are called equivalent if there exists a parametrization
\varphi\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
which is continuous, strictly increasing, and surjective such that
\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}\circ\varphi
. A curve is an equivalence class of paths under this equivalence relation, and we will
write
[\gamma]
for the curve represented by the path
\gamma
. A path is called
nowhere constant
if it is not constant on any non-trivial sub-interval of
[0,1]
. This notion clearly descends to the space of curves as well. Given two curves
[\gamma],[\sigma]
, we define the
Fréchet distance
between them as follows:

d_{F}([\gamma],[\sigma])\coloneqq\inf_{\varphi,\psi}\max_{t\in[0,1]}d(\gamma(\varphi(t)),\sigma(\psi(t))),

(A.1)

where
\varphi,\psi
run over the set of all parametrizations as defined above. Obviously, we can equivalently write

d_{F}([\gamma],[\sigma])\coloneqq\inf_{\varphi}\max_{t\in[0,1]}d(\gamma(\varphi(t)),\sigma(t)).

We begin by establishing some properties of (spaces of) pairs of non-decreasing continuous maps:

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\alpha\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]^{2}
be a continuous, coordinate-wise non-decreasing map given by
\alpha(t)=(\varphi(t),\psi(t))
and let
\alpha(0)=(0,0)
and
\alpha(1)=(1,1)
. Then there exists a coordinate-wise non-decreasing and
1
-Lipschitz continuous curve
\tilde{\alpha}=(\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\psi})\colon[0,2]\to[0,1]^{2}
that has the same image as
\alpha
.

Proof.

The function
s\colon[0,1]\to[0,2]
,
s(t)\coloneqq\varphi(t)+\psi(t)
is continuous, non-decreasing, and surjective by the
intermediate value theorem. Let
\theta\colon[0,2]\to[0,1]
be the generalized inverse of
s
,

\theta(u)\coloneqq\inf\{t\in[0,1]\mid s(t)\geq u\}.

Since
s
is surjective,
\theta
is well-defined, and using continuity of
s
one readily checks that
s(\theta(u))=u
for each
u\in[0,2]
. Now set, for
u\in[0,2]
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}(u)\coloneqq\alpha(\theta(u))=(\varphi(\theta(u)),\psi(\theta(u)))\eqqcolon(\tilde{\varphi}(u),\tilde{\psi}(u)).

We first show that
\tilde{\alpha}([0,2])=\alpha([0,1])
: Obviously,
\tilde{\alpha}([0,2])\subseteq\alpha([0,1])
. Conversely, let

p\coloneqq\alpha(t_{0})
,
t_{0}\in[0,1]
and
u_{0}\coloneqq s(t_{0})
. Setting
t_{*}\coloneqq\theta(s(t_{0}))
, we then have
\tilde{\alpha}(u_{0})=\alpha(t_{*})
. The definition of
\theta
, together with continuity and monotonicity of
s
imply that
s(t_{0})=s(t_{*})
,
which we can also write as

(\varphi(t_{0})-\varphi(t_{*}))+(\psi(t_{0})-\psi(t_{*}))=0.

Here, since
t_{*}\leq t_{0}
and since both
\varphi
and
\psi
are non-decreasing, both summands have to vanish, implying that
\alpha(t_{0})=\alpha(t_{*})=\tilde{\alpha}(u_{0})
, establishing the claim.

Finally, to show that
\tilde{\alpha}
is
1
-Lipschitz, let
0\leq u<v\leq 2
. Then using monotonicity of
\theta
,
\varphi
and
\psi
,
hence of
\tilde{\varphi}
and
\tilde{\psi}
,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\alpha}(v)-\tilde{\alpha}(u)\|_{1}

\displaystyle=|\tilde{\varphi}(v)-\tilde{\varphi}(u)|+|\tilde{\psi}(v)-\tilde{\psi}(u)|=(\tilde{\varphi}(v)+\tilde{\psi}(v))-(\tilde{\varphi}(u)+\tilde{\psi}(u))

\displaystyle=s(\theta(v))-s(\theta(u))=v-u.

Consequently, for any
u,v\in[0,2]
we have

\|\tilde{\alpha}(v)-\tilde{\alpha}(u)\|_{2}\leq\|\tilde{\alpha}(v)-\tilde{\alpha}(u)\|_{1}\leq|v-u|.

∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
N
be the set of continuous, componentwise non-decreasing maps
\alpha=(\varphi,\psi)\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]^{2}
with
\alpha(0)=(0,0)

and
\alpha(1,1)=(1,1)
. Then
\mathcal{N}\coloneqq\{\alpha([0,1])\mid\alpha\in N\}
is compact with respect to the Hausdorff distance
d_{H}
.

Proof.

Denote by
L
the set of all coordinate-wise non-decreasing
1
-Lipschitz maps
\eta\colon[0,2]\to[0,1]^{2}
with
\eta(0)=(0,0)
and
\eta(1)=(1,1)
. By the Theorem of Arzelà-Ascoli,
L
is relatively compact in
C([0,2],[0,1]^{2})
.
Since it is clearly also closed with respect to uniform convergence, it is indeed compact in
C([0,2],[0,1]^{2})
.

Denote by
\mathcal{K}([0,1]^{2})
the space of compact subsets of
[0,1]^{2}
, and equip it with the Hausdorff distance
d_{H}
. Consider the map

\displaystyle\iota\colon C([0,2],[0,1]^{2})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{K}([0,1]^{2})

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\mapsto\eta([0,2]).

Since
d_{H}(\iota(\eta),\iota(\zeta))\leq\sup_{u\in[0,2]}\|\eta(u)-\zeta(u)\|_{2}
, it follows that
\iota
is continuous (indeed,

-Lipschitz). Thus
\mathcal{L}=\iota(L)
is compact with respect to

. Finally, by Lemma

, concluding the proof.
∎

Based on these preparations, we can now show:

Proposition A.3
.

The Fréchet distance
d_{F}
defines a metric on the space of nowhere constant curves in
(X,d)
.

Proof.

Since parametrizations form a group, the Fréchet distance is well defined. Symmetry of
d_{F}
is clear and the triangle inequality follows readily from the definition. Also
d_{F}([\gamma],[\gamma])=0
is obvious.

The only non-trivial point therefore is to show that
d_{F}([\gamma],[\sigma])=0
implies
[\gamma]=[\sigma]
, i.e., that
\gamma
is a reparametrization of
\sigma
. From the definition of
d_{F}
we obtain sequences of parametrizations
(\varphi_{n},\psi_{n})
such that, for each

:

\max_{t\in[0,1]}d(\gamma(\varphi_{n}(t)),\sigma(\psi_{n}(t)))<\frac{1}{n}.

(A.2)

Set
\alpha_{n}\coloneqq(\varphi_{n},\psi_{n})
, and
A_{n}\coloneqq\alpha_{n}([0,1])
. Then by Lemma

,
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is a sequence in the

-compact set

, hence possesses a
d_{H}
-convergent subsequence, which we may without loss of generality assume to be
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
itself. Let
A\in\mathcal{N}
be its limit. Then there is some
\alpha=(\varphi,\psi)\in N
with
A=\alpha([0,1])
. Given any

, there exists a sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in

such that

A_{n}\ni(\varphi_{n}(t_{n}),\psi_{n}(t_{n}))\to(\varphi(t),\psi(t))\qquad(n\to\infty).

By (

) we have
d(\gamma(\varphi_{n}(t_{n})),\sigma(\psi_{n}(t_{n})))<\frac{1}{n}
, so letting

, implying that
\gamma\circ\varphi=\sigma\circ\psi
.

We are left with the task of constructing from
\varphi
and
\psi
new continuous surjective maps
\hat{\varphi}
,
\hat{\psi}\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
that are strictly monotonically increasing, hence qualify as parametrizations and satisfy

. This we do

We first claim that
\varphi
is constant on any non-trivial interval

of

if and only if

is constant on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
.
By symmetry, it suffices to show the ‘only if’ part of the claim. Since
\varphi
is constant on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
, so is

\gamma\circ\varphi=\sigma\circ\psi
.
Also,
\psi
being continuous and non-decreasing, the image of
[t_{1},t_{2}]
under
\psi
is the closed interval

.
The path
\sigma
is therefore constant on

to be a single point, hence

. Since
\psi
is non-decreasing,
\psi
is indeed constant on

.

Define
\tau\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]

\tau(t)=\frac{\varphi(t)+\psi(t)}{2}.

Then
\tau
is continuous, non-decreasing and surjective

.

Next we show that
\tau(t)
is constant on an interval

\varphi(t)
and
\psi(t)
are constant on that interval. For the non-trivial direction of this claim, suppose that
\tau
is constant on

. Then for any

with
t_{1}\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq t_{2}
, we have

, hence

\frac{\varphi(t)+\psi(t)}{2}=\frac{\varphi(t^{\prime})+\psi(t^{\prime})}{2}\implies(\varphi(t^{\prime})-\varphi(t))+(\psi(t^{\prime})-\psi(t))=0

Since
\varphi
and

are non-decreasing, we conclude that
\varphi(t)=\varphi(t^{\prime})

.

For any

By construction,

\gamma(\hat{\varphi}(\tau(t)))=\sigma(\hat{\psi}(\tau(t)))
for all

.
Surjectivity of

are surjective, so are

and
\hat{\psi}

Since
\tau
is a continuous map from a compact space (

), it is a quotient map. By the universal property of quotient maps, since

and

\hat{\varphi}(s_{1})=\varphi(t_{1})\leq\varphi(t_{2})=\hat{\varphi}(s_{2}).

Let us indirectly assume that equality holds:

AI-based fundus screening of teachers: fundus tessellated density as a myopia biomarker and occupational associations
Authors: Zhongnan Xiao, Huijuan Liu, Hongyan Wang, Min Song, Guoquan Liu, Chunhong Yin, Liwei Liu, Min Li, Jingmiao Sun, Yi Ren, Ying Guo, Yu Zhong, Hong Yu, Ying Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundArtificial intelligence (AI)-based fundus imaging enables efficient large-scale retinal screening; however, its application in occupational populations remains limited.ObjectiveTo evaluate the utility of an AI-based fundus screening system in teachers and to investigate fundus tessellated density (FTD) as a potential imaging marker associated with myopia and occupational factors.MethodsIn this prospective observational study, 648 teachers from Beijing underwent fundus photography and systemic examinations. AI algorithms were used to quantify FTD and other retinal parameters. Associations between spherical equivalent (SE), FTD, and systemic and occupational variables were assessed using univariate and multivariable regression analyses. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate the discriminative ability of FTD for high myopia.ResultsThe mean SE was − 2.43 ± 2.94 D, and mean FTD was 0.0609 ± 0.0743. FTD showed a non-uniform distribution across the macula, with higher values in the nasal quadrant. Multivariable analysis demonstrated that FTD, near-work duration at 30 cm, and ocular structural parameters were independently associated with SE (all P < 0.05). Prolonged near work (> 1 h) at 30 cm was associated with increased FTD. ROC analysis indicated moderate discriminative performance for high myopia (AUC = 0.7788 for overall FTD). In addition, FTD was associated with systemic immune-inflammatory indices, including NLR and PLR.ConclusionsAI-derived FTD may serve as a potential imaging marker associated with high myopia and occupational factors. Introduction

The human retina, a complex and light-sensitive tissue composed of diverse cell types, plays a critical role in vision. Retinal diseases are widely recognized as a leading cause of visual impairment and blindness worldwide, posing a substantial public health burden. However, disparities in the density and clinical experience of ophthalmologists across regions limit access to timely and accurate retinal screening, particularly in resource-constrained settings [1, 2].

Recent advances in fundus imaging and artificial intelligence (AI) have enabled efficient and scalable detection of retinal abnormalities. Deep learning-based systems have demonstrated the ability to provide rapid, safe, and effective screening solutions, especially in high-throughput environments such as physical examination centers, where manual assessment of large volumes of fundus images is often impractical [3, 4]. In China, the Retinal Artificial Intelligence Diagnosis System (RAIDS) has demonstrated satisfactory performance in screening multiple retinal abnormalities using prospectively collected fundus images across diverse clinical settings, supporting its real-world applicability [5, 6].

Myopia, a leading cause of visual impairment worldwide [7], is associated with progressive chorioretinal degeneration and vision-threatening complications, including retinal detachment, glaucoma, myopic macular degeneration, and choroidal neovascularization [8, 9]. Fundus tessellation, a common feature in myopic eyes, reflects underlying choroidal thinning and has been associated with retinal vein occlusion and myopic maculopathy [10, 11]. Progression to macular atrophy may cause severe and irreversible vision loss [12], contributing to substantial societal and economic burdens [13].

The rising global prevalence of myopia is influenced by environmental and behavioral factors, including educational attainment and occupational status [14–16]. In particular, prolonged near-work activities—such as reading and the use of visual display terminals (VDT)—have been implicated in myopia development and progression [17]. Teachers represent a unique occupational group characterized by sustained near-work and high visual demands; however, evidence on occupational influences on retinal structural changes remains limited.

Quantitative assessment of fundus tessellated density (FTD) may provide additional insight into early retinal structural alterations associated with myopia. Nevertheless, the application of AI-based fundus imaging for evaluating FTD in occupational populations has not been well established. Therefore, in this prospective observational study, we applied a deep learning-based fundus analysis system to quantify FTD and to investigate its associations with myopia, occupational factors, and systemic indicators among teachers in Beijing.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 648 participants (1,296 eyes) were included in this prospective observational study. Eyes with media opacities that prevented acquisition of gradable fundus photographs were excluded from image analysis. However, eyes with optic nerve or macular lesions were retained provided fundus image quality was assessable.

The mean age of participants was 39.26 ± 8.47 years (median: 39 years; range: 22–74 years). The mean spherical equivalent (SE) was − 2.43 ± 2.94 D (median: -1.00 D; range: -20.0 to 6.25 D). Among the participants, 61 (9.41%) were male and 587 (90.59%) were female. The mean SE was − 2.43 ± 2.75 D (median: -1.38 D; range: -10.25 to 4.25 D) in males and − 2.43 ± 2.96 D (median: -1.00 D; range: -20.00 to 6.25 D) in females (Table 1). Participants were categorized into emmetropic (-0.25 D ≤ SE ≤ + 0.25 D), hyperopic (SE > + 0.25 D), and myopic (SE < -0.25 D) groups. Significant differences in parameters were observed among the three groups (ANOVA, P < 0.05), with myopia being the most prevalent refractive status (Table 2).

Quantitative analysis of fundus tessellated density in macular regions among myopic teachers

According to the Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) grid, the macular area within a 6 mm diameter centered on the fovea was divided into two concentric circles with diameters of 3 mm (inner circle) and 6 mm (outer circle), respectively (Fig. 4A). FTD was automatically quantified in four quadrants: superior (Sup), inferior (Inf), nasal (Nas), and temporal (Tem).

Only myopic eyes were included in this analysis (n = 923), and non-myopic eyes were excluded. Significant regional variations in FTD were observed within the 3 mm diameter region. The nasal quadrant exhibited significantly higher FTD compared to the Sup, Inf, and Tem quadrants. A similar pattern was observed in the 6 mm diameter region, where the Nas quadrant again demonstrated significantly elevated FTD relative to the other three quadrants. Additionally, within the 6 mm region, the Sup quadrant showed significantly higher FTD than the Tem quadrant (Fig. 4B).

To evaluate the influence of near-work duration on FTD in myopic teachers, participants were stratified into two groups based on daily near-work exposure: ≤ 1 h or > 1 h. Prolonged near-work at 30 cm, rather than at 50 cm, exhibited significant differences in FTD (Fig. 4C). Further analysis revealed that prolonged near-work (> 1 h) at 30 cm was associated with a significant increase in FTD within the 3 mm and 6 mm diameter macular regions centered on the fovea, though no such effect was observed in the 1 mm diameter region (Fig. 4D).

ROC analysis of FTD for discriminating myopia severity

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated that AI-based assessment of FTD showed moderate discriminative ability between non-high myopia (SE > -6.00 D) and high myopia (SE ≤ -6.00 D). Both overall FTD and nasal FTD within the 3 mm diameter macular region showed discriminative capacity, though no statistically significant difference was observed between these two metrics (Fig. 5A, B). The ROC curve for overall FTD yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.7788 (95% CI: 0.7402–0.8175, P < 0.0001). At an optimal cutoff value of > 0.06357, the sensitivity was 67.63% and specificity was 70.78%. For nasal FTD, the AUC was 0.7631 (95% CI: 0.7214–0.8048, P < 0.0001), with a cutoff of > 0.0832 providing 71.10% sensitivity and 71.73% specificity. Consistent with these findings, both overall FTD and nasal FTD exhibited statistically significant differences between the high myopia and non-high myopia groups (Fig. 5C, D).

Quantitative analysis of SE and FTD across academic disciplines

To evaluate the relationship between teaching subjects specialization and ocular parameters, myopic teachers were categorized into five subjects groups: main subjects, science subjects, literature subjects, art subjects, and ‘others’. Analysis revealed that teachers specializing in literature subjects exhibited a significantly lower SE compared to the other four subject groups (Fig. 6A). Consistent with this refractive pattern, literature subjects also demonstrated significantly higher FTD than both art subjects and the ‘others’ category. No statistically significant differences in FTD were observed among the main, science, and literature subject groups. Additionally, art subjects showed significantly reduced FTD compared to main subjects, while no significant difference was found between art subjects and the ‘others’ group (Fig. 6B).

Quantitative analysis of FTD and systemic immune-inflammatory markers in high myopia

To further explore the etiology and potential pathogenesis of high myopia-related retinal lesions, we performed univariate analysis on systemic inflammatory markers. As summarized in Table 5, significant associations were observed between FTD and specific immunocyte subsets as well as systemic immune-inflammatory indices in the teacher cohort. Specifically, FTD showed a positive correlation with neutrophil percentage (NEU%) and systemic immune-inflammatory indices, including the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), whereas a negative correlation was identified with lymphocyte percentage (LYM%). Comparative analysis further revealed that eyes with high myopia exhibited significantly elevated levels of NEU%, NLR, and PLR, along with a decreasing trend in lymphocyte percentage (P = 0.0853), when compared to those with low or moderate myopia (Fig. 7A-D). These findings collectively underscore a close relationship between the progression of high myopia-related fundus lesions and a heightened systemic inflammatory state.

Materials and methods

Image collection

This was a prospective observational study conducted among teachers in Beijing. All investigations were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the institutional review board of Beijing Rehabilitation Hospital Affiliated to Capital Medical University (No. 2021bkky-105). Each participant provided informed consent. To assess the use of AI in physical examination centers, a total of 1296 color fundus images from 648 individuals at a physical examination center were included for a teacher cohort screening. Only one image per examined eye was included. Eyes that met any of the following criteria were excluded: poor image quality due to uncooperativeness or small pupils, and incomplete basic information. Poor image quality was defined as blurred images, low contrast, or incomplete retinal visualization. Incomplete information referred to missing demographic, clinical, or questionnaire data. The inclusion process is outlined in Fig. 8.

Flowchart of participant inclusion and AI-based fundus screening procedures

Ophthalmic and general examinations

The examinations took place at the Model Worker Health Management Center, Beijing Rehabilitation Hospital, affiliated with Capital Medical University. Participants completed a standardized questionnaire covering demographic factors like age, gender, education, smoking, alcohol consumption, and teaching subjects (subjects category: ① Main subjects: Chinese, Mathematics, English; ② Science subjects: Biology, Physics, Chemistry; ③ Literature subjects: Taoism, Politics, Geography, History; ④ Art subjects: music, art, sports; ⑤ ‘Others’: such as logistics, administration). They also reported exercise frequency (basically not, once a month, 3–5 times per week, 1–2 times per week, daily), intensity (mild, moderate, severe), duration (0.5 h, 0.5–1 h, > 1 h), near-work habits (distance: 30–50 cm; duration: ≤ 1 h or > 1 h), astigmatism types (regular astigmatism order: normal, rule astigmatism, against the rule astigmatism, oblique astigmatism), and more. Blood samples were collected for lipid, glycosylated hemoglobin, and glucose measurements after obtaining consent. Blood pressure, height, weight, and BMI were recorded. Ophthalmic assessments included visual acuity (uncorrected visual acuity (UCVA), best corrected visual acuity (BCVA)), intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement (non-contact tonometer, TX-20, Canon, Japan), computer optometry (HUVITZ, HRK7000A), and fundus photography (nonstereoscopic photograph of 45° of the central fundus; fundus camera type CR-2 AF; Canon, Japan). Spherical equivalent (SE = spherical degrees + (cylindrical degrees/2)) was calculated for myopia (SE < − 0.25 D), hypermetropia (SE > + 0.25 D), and emmetropia (− 0.25 D ≤ SE ≤ + 0.25 D) classifications.

AI data collection

FTD was selected as the primary quantitative parameter due to its established association with choroidal thinning and myopic progression in previous studies. The EVisionAI software, developed by EVision Technology (Beijing) Co. Ltd., is a color fundus image intelligent analysis tool that mimics the human visual system [18]. It utilizes advanced AI image processing techniques for extraction, denoising, normalization, and enhancement. The software accurately segments the optic disc and optic cup by combining a depth target detection network with a computer vision edge extraction algorithm [19]. Arterial and venous vessels are segmented using a ResNet101-UNet [20] deep learning model based on vessel features and topological relationships. Subsequently, the software provides detailed digital descriptions of color fundus photographs, including measurements such as optic disc and optic cup diameter, cup-to-disc ratio (C/D), vascular parameters, and fundus tessellated density (FTD). Previous studies have demonstrated segmentation and extraction accuracies of ≥ 0.96 for the optic disc, optic cup, peripapillary atrophy, and blood vessels, with sensitivity ≥ 0.85 and specificity ≥ 0.96 [21].

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics version 26.0 (IBM Corp., USA) and GraphPad Prism version 7.0 (GraphPad Software, USA). Continuous variables were assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test prior to analysis. Most continuous variables approximated normal distributions and are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons between two groups were conducted using independent-samples t-tests. For comparisons among three or more groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was applied. The chi-square test was used to compare proportions between groups. Pearson correlation analysis was used to assess the associations between continuous variables. Multivariable linear regression analyses were performed to evaluate independent factors associated with spherical equivalent (SE) and fundus tessellated density (FTD), with SE and FTD treated as dependent variables in separate models. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIF) and tolerance values. A VIF < 5 and tolerance > 0.2 were considered indicative of no significant multicollinearity. Stratified analyses were conducted according to age, sex, and myopia severity to explore potential effect modification. Interaction terms were further included in regression models to assess statistical interactions. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the discriminative ability of FTD for high myopia. The area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity were calculated. All tests were two-sided, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

The present study represents one of the first comprehensive investigations to integrate occupational heterogeneity with quantitative retinal analysis, focusing on the teaching profession—a group characterized by intensive near-work and VDT use, particularly in the post-pandemic era [2, 22]. This study provides a comprehensive assessment of fundus tessellation and its associated factors in a teacher cohort using AI-based fundus analysis. Several key findings emerged: (1) FTD exhibits a distinct non-uniform topographic distribution across the macula, with a pronounced concentration in the nasal quadrant; (2) FTD was associated with myopia severity and demonstrated moderate discriminative ability for high myopia; (3) Specific occupational and behavioral factors, particularly teaching discipline and near-work duration at short working distances, were associated with both SE and FTD; and (4) FTD was associated with systemic immune-inflammatory indices, suggesting a potential link between ocular structural changes and systemic conditions.

The application of AI in diagnostic imaging has been validated across multiple medical disciplines, including radiology and dermatology, and is increasingly recognized for its utility in ophthalmology [23]. FTD represents the earliest stage of myopic maculopathy, and its progression may lead to macular atrophy and irreversible vision loss [24]. In this study, we demonstrated that FTD, a recognized quantitative biomarker of choroidal thickness [25, 26], can be effectively measured using a deep learning system and is associated with occupational and behavioral factors. The higher FTD in the nasal quadrant may reflect regional susceptibility to structural remodeling during axial elongation [27, 28]. The consistent finding of the lowest FTD in the temporal quadrant further reinforces the concept of regional susceptibility, potentially afforded by the structural buttressing effect of the optic nerve head [29]. The ability of AI-derived FTD to distinguish high myopia, with AUC values exceeding 0.75, suggests that it may serve as a useful quantitative indicator in screening settings [30, 31]. The comparable performance of overall and nasal FTD suggests that a global assessment may be pragmatically sufficient for initial screening, though quadrant-specific analysis might offer unique insights for monitoring progression in research or detailed clinical settings [9, 11]. However, given the lack of external validation, its performance should be interpreted with caution.

Our findings also highlight the potential role of occupational exposure in myopia-related changes. Teachers specializing in literature showed more myopic refractive status and higher FTD, which may be related to sustained reading and near-work demands. Furthermore, prolonged near-work at a short distance (30 cm) was associated with increased FTD, supporting the hypothesis that near-work behavior may be linked to structural retinal changes [32, 33]. These findings are consistent with previous epidemiological studies, including research published in BMC Public Health [16, 34], which also highlighted the role of behavioral and environmental factors in myopia development, but do not establish causality.

From a public health perspective, these results underscore the potential value of integrating AI-based fundus screening into occupational health programs, particularly in high-risk groups such as teachers, while also highlighting the growing importance of modifiable lifestyle factors in vision health. Early identification of individuals with high FTD may facilitate targeted monitoring and preventive strategies. However, the implementation of such approaches requires further validation and cost-effectiveness evaluation.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the marked female predominance in this cohort may limit the generalizability of the findings, particularly to male populations. Second, due to the cross-sectional design, causal relationships between FTD, myopia, and occupational factors cannot be established. Third, axial length (AL), a key factor associated with myopia progression and choroidal thickness [35, 36], was unavailable in this occupational screening setting. Therefore, residual confounding related to ocular elongation cannot be excluded. Fourth, although the AI model demonstrated good internal performance, external validation in diverse populations is still required [19]. Finally, residual confounding from unmeasured factors, such as outdoor exposure and genetic predisposition, cannot be excluded. These methodological constraints underscore that our findings should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory.

In conclusion, this study provides a quantitative description of fundus tessellation in a teacher cohort using an AI-based approach. FTD shows potential as an imaging biomarker associated with high myopia and occupational factors. However, given the cross-sectional design, these findings should be interpreted as associative and hypothesis-generating. Further longitudinal and multi-center studies are required to validate these observations.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that AI-based fundus analysis enables quantitative assessment of fundus tessellation in an occupational population. FTD was associated with myopia severity, near-work behavior, and systemic immune-inflammatory indices, suggesting its potential as an imaging biomarker of myopic structural changes. These findings are observational and hypothesis-generating, and further longitudinal studies with external validation and incorporation of axial length measurements are warranted to clarify causal relationships and clinical applicability.

Unconventional and anomalous magnetic field distribution in a bilayer superconductor with geometric constraints
Takashi Yanagisawa

Abstract
We investigate the magnetic field distribution in multi-component superconductors. We examine a layered superconductor and a two-component one-layer superconductor. We evaluate the field distribution in the presence of a half-flux quantum vortex with a kink structure in the phase space of gap functions. We also examine the magnetic field distribution of a knot soliton which is formulated in a two-component superconductor. We investigate the effect of geometric constraints for multi-component superconductors, where the geometric constraint means that the system is compactified in one direction so that the current in this direction becomes vanishingly small. This corresponds to the gauge fixing in this direction. An unconventional magnetic field distribution takes place; here the unconventional means that the magnetic field is screened incompletely which may be called the anomalous Meissner effect. We argue that this anomalous behavior creates a massless gauge field.

In this paper we examine the magnetic property of multi-component superconductors.
We consider a superconducting bilayer and also a superconducting layer with
two-component order parameters. The phase difference of the order parameters
forms a soliton-like (kink) structure.
A half-flux quantum vortex (HFQV or HQV) exists with this kink.
We discuss the magnetic field distribution in the presence of a HFQV
associated with a kink (soliton). A structure in the phase space gives a
contribution to the magnetic field in superconductors.
The field equation for HFQV and a kink attached to it is solved to determine the
field distribution.

The derivative of the phase
\varphi
contributes to the vector potential.

\nabla\varphi

{\bf B}

\varphi

{\rm rotgrad}\varphi=0

We investigate a multi-component superconductor where

{\bf B}

we examine the effect of geometrical constraints on two-component superconductors.
There is a possibility that non-trivial behavior of phase difference modes may
lead to an anomalous behavior of the Meissner effect that may be called the
incomplete or anomalous Meissner effect (IME or AME).
In a superconductor this indicates a phenomenon that the screening of
magnetic filed is incomplete and the magnetic field penetrates into a
superconductor partially.
There appears a massless mode with this anomalous behavior of magnetic field.
This shows a possibility that a massless mode exists even in a two-component
superconductor.
We show that we have an unusual magnetic field distribution due to geometric constraints
in a bilayer superconductor.
In a one-layer two-component superconducting disk, the constraint condition is
approximately satisfied automatically and an unusual magnetic field contribution is small.

The Higgs mechanism is an important phenomenon in a coupled scalar-gauge field
system.
The Meissner effect in superconductors is the Higgs mechanism in a U(1) gauge theory.
This mechanism gives the mass to gauge fields as a result of
a spontaneous symmetry breaking.
The incomplete Meissner effect implies an incomplete Higgs mechanism.
The incomplete Higgs state means that some components of gauge field remain massless
when the gauge symmetry is spontaneously broken.

II
Multi-component gauged scalar fields

II.1
Ginzburg-Landau free energy

We examine a multi-component scalar model coupled to the gauge field.
The model is that for superconductors with multi-component order parameters
in a magnetic field.
The free energy density is written in the form

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}|\psi_{j}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\beta_{j}|\psi_{j}|^{4}-\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}(\psi_{i}^{*}\psi_{j}+\psi_{j}^{*}\psi_{i}),

\displaystyle~+\sum_{j}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{j}}\Big|\left(\nabla-i\frac{e^{*}}{\hbar c}{\bf A}\right)\psi_{j}\Big|^{2}+\frac{1}{8\pi}(\nabla\times{\bf A})^{2}

(1)

where
\psi_{j}

(j=1,2,\cdots)
are the order parameters and we set
e^{*}=2e
for
the electron charge
e(<0)
.

The free energy
f
is not invariant under the gauge transformation because of
the Josephson term
J_{ij}
. The Josephson term is not invariant even for
a global gauge transformation.
The characteristic feature of multi-component models in the presence of
the Josephson coupling is the existence of domain structure of phases of gap
functions.
The domain structure means a soliton-like structure in the phase space
of gap functions.

II.2
Multi-component model of a multi-layer superconductor

A multi-layer superconductor is a multi-component superconductor
when each layer is regarded as a one-component superconductor
(Fig.1 for a bilayer superconductor).
Each component of the superconductor exists in a different space and they
interact each other only through the Josephson coupling.
The magnetic vector potential
{\bf A}
can be different in each space (layer)
since the order parameters
\psi_{j}
(
j=1,2,\cdots
) can have different phases
\varphi_{j}
.

We consider two superconducting layers apart from the distance
s
in the
x-y
plane
and their thickness is
d
.
We assume that
d
is small compared to the penetration depth
\Lambda
which
is defined in Section 3.
We will consider the case where
s
is also small compared to
\Lambda
:

d,s\ll\Lambda.

(2)

A bilayer superconductor with an insulating layer between

III
Magnetic field in a bilayer superconductor

III.1
Field equations

We have two regions
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
that denote layer 1 and layer 2,
respectively.
\Omega_{1}
is at
z=0
and
\Omega_{2}
is at
z=s
where
s
is
the distance between two layers. The coordinate
{\bf x}
is denoted as

{\bf x}=({\bf r},z)
where
{\bf r}=(x,y)
is the two-dimensional coordinate.
The vector potential
{\bf A}
reads

\displaystyle{\bf A}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf A}_{1}~~~{\rm in}~~\Omega_{1}\\
{\bf A}_{2}~~~{\rm in}~~\Omega_{2}.\\
\end{array}\right.

We define the phases
\varphi_{j}
(
j=1,2,\cdots
) of gap functions as

\psi_{j}=e^{i\varphi_{j}}|\psi_{j}|=e^{i\varphi_{j}}\rho_{j},

(6)

where we set
\rho_{j}=|\psi_{j}|
.
The free energy is written as

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\rho_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\rho_{j}^{4}+\sum_{j}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{j}}\left(\nabla\rho_{j}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle~+\sum_{j}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{j}}\rho_{j}^{2}\left(\nabla\varphi_{j}\right)^{2}-\frac{2e^{*}}{\hbar c}\sum_{j}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{j}}\rho_{j}^{2}\nabla\varphi_{j}\cdot{\bf A}_{j}

\displaystyle~-2J_{12}\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\cos\varphi

\displaystyle~+\left(\frac{e^{*}}{\hbar c}\right)^{2}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{\rho_{1}^{2}}{m_{1}}{\bf A}_{1}^{2}+\frac{\rho_{2}^{2}}{m_{2}}{\bf A}_{2}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{8\pi}\left(\nabla\times{\bf A}\right)^{2},

(7)

where the phase difference
\varphi
is defined as

\varphi({\bf r})=\varphi_{1}({\bf r},z=0)-\varphi_{2}({\bf r},z=s).

(8)

The field equation for
{\bf A}
in each layer reads

-\nabla\times(\nabla\times{\bf A}_{j})=\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\bf A}_{j}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\frac{\phi_{0}}{2\pi}\nabla\varphi_{j},

(9)

for
j=1,2
where
\lambda_{j}
is the penetration depth in
j
-th layer given as

\lambda_{j}^{-2}=\frac{4\pi(e^{*})^{2}}{2m_{j}c^{2}}\rho_{j}^{2},

(10)

and
\phi_{0}
is the unit quantum flux:
\phi_{0}=hc/|e^{*}|
.
The equations for
\varphi_{j}
are given by

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar^{2}\rho_{j}^{2}}{2m_{j}}\left(\nabla^{2}\varphi_{j}-\frac{e^{*}}{\hbar c}\nabla{\bf A}_{j}\right)+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{j}}\left(\nabla\varphi_{j}-\frac{e^{*}}{\hbar c}{\bf A}_{j}\right)\nabla\rho_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle~~~+J_{12}\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\sin\varphi=0.

(11)

In this paper we neglect the spatial dependence of
\rho_{j}
since we are
interested in the physics originating from phase structure.

The equations for
{\bf A}_{j}
show that
{\bf A}
in each layer has the mass

m_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{-1}
, which means that the Meissner effect occurs in each layer.
We discuss later that the Meissner effect will be modified to be incomplete
when there is a geometric constraint for superconducting layers.

III.2
Magnetic field in the presence of a half-flux quantum vortex with a kink

III.2.1
HFQV in two equivalent layers

We investigate the distribution of the magnetic field in the presence of a
half-flux quantum vortex (HFQV) in a superconducting bilayer.
This is an extension of that in a superconducting layer
[
57
]
to the case
of a superconducting bilayer.
We assume that two superconductors are identical, that is, we have
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}

and
m_{1}=m_{2}
in this subsection.
The width of the film is
d
and
s
denotes the distance between two layers.
In our model the HFQV exists accompanied with a kink (soliton) structure in
the phase space of gap functions.
We write the phases
\varphi_{j}
as

\varphi_{1}({\bf r},z=0)=\Phi+\frac{1}{2}\varphi,~~\varphi_{2}({\bf r},z=s)=\Phi-\frac{1}{2}\varphi,

(12)

where
{\bf r}\equiv(x,y)
indicates the two-dimensional coordinate. The total phase
\Phi
is
defined by

\Phi({\bf r})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi_{1}({\bf r},z=0)+\varphi_{2}({\bf r},z=s)\right).

(13)

We use the Coulomb gauge
\nabla\cdot{\bf A}=0
, then
the vector potential
{\bf A}
satisfies the
following equation:

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}{\bf A}({\bf r},z)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Lambda}\delta(z)\left({\bf A}({\bf r},z)+\frac{\phi_{0}}{2\pi}\nabla\varphi_{1}\right)

\displaystyle~+\frac{2}{\Lambda}\delta(z-s)\left({\bf A}({\bf r},z)+\frac{\phi_{0}}{2\pi}\nabla\varphi_{2}\right),

(14)

where we put

\frac{2}{\Lambda}=\frac{d}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}=\frac{d}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}.

(15)

The total phase
\Phi
represents an HFQV that is given by

\Phi=\frac{1}{2}\phi
, where
\phi=\tan^{-1}(y/x)
.
We have

\frac{\phi_{0}}{2\pi}\nabla\Phi=\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\left(\frac{y}{x^{2}+y^{2}},-\frac{x}{x^{2}+y^{2}},0\right).

(16)

We adopt that the kink exists on the positive axis
x>0
and the phase difference

\varphi
changes from 0 to
2\pi
when crossing the kink from negative (
y<0
) to
positive (
y>0
) regions.
We introduce the Fourier transformation

{\bf A}({\bf r},z)=\int\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{dq_{z}}{2\pi}{\bf A}({\bf q},q_{z})e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}+iq_{z}z},

(17)

where we set
{\bf q}=(q_{x},q_{y})
.
The Fourier transform of
\nabla\varphi({\bf r})
is defined as

{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})\equiv\int dxdy\nabla\phi({\bf r})e^{-i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}=2\pi i\left(\frac{q_{y}}{q^{2}},-\frac{q_{x}}{q^{2}},0\right),

(18)

where
q=|{\bf q}|
.
We define the Fourier transform of
\nabla\varphi
as

{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})\equiv\int dxdy\nabla\varphi({\bf r})e^{-i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}.

(19)

By using the delta function representation

\delta(z)=(1/2\pi)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dq_{z}e^{iq_{z}z}
,
we have

\displaystyle{\bf A}({\bf q},q_{z})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\Lambda}\frac{1}{q^{2}+q_{z}^{2}}\left({\bf A}({\bf q},z=0)+\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})+\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})\right)

\displaystyle~-\frac{2}{\Lambda}\frac{e^{-iq_{z}s}}{q^{2}+q_{z}^{2}}\left({\bf A}({\bf q},z=s)+\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})\right).

(20)

This leads to

\displaystyle{\bf A}({\bf q},z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\Lambda q}e^{-q|z|}\left({\bf A}({\bf q},z=0)+\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})+\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})\right)

\displaystyle~-\frac{1}{\Lambda q}e^{-q|z-s|}\left({\bf A}({\bf q},z=s)+\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})\right).

(21)

From this equation,
{\bf A}({\bf r},z)
at
z=0
and
z=s
are determined.
We obtain
{\bf A}({\bf r},z)
for
z=0
and
z=s
, after the Fourier
transformation, as

\displaystyle{\bf A}({\bf r},0)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\bigg[\frac{1+e^{-qs}}{\Lambda q+1+e^{-qs}}{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})

\displaystyle~~~~~+\frac{1-e^{-qs}}{\Lambda q+1-e^{-qs}}{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})\bigg],

(22)

\displaystyle{\bf A}({\bf r},s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\bigg[\frac{1+e^{-qs}}{\Lambda q+1+e^{-qs}}{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})

\displaystyle~~~~~-\frac{1-e^{-qs}}{\Lambda q+1-e^{-qs}}{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})\bigg].

(23)

This results in

\displaystyle{\bf A}({\bf r},z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\bigg[\frac{e^{-q|z|}+e^{-q|z-s|}}{\Lambda q+1+e^{-qs}}{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+\frac{e^{-q|z|}-e^{-q|z-s|}}{\Lambda q+1-e^{-qs}}{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})\bigg].

(24)

(A) The case
{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})={\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})

In this case the vector potential agrees with that obtained for the model

We use the representation

\varphi=-{\rm Im}\log(x+iy)+2\pi.

and then we have

Since

, we have

\displaystyle A_{\phi}({\bf r},z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{0}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\bigg[-e^{-q|z|}(\Lambda q+1)+e^{-q|z-s|-qs}\bigg]

\displaystyle~~~~~\cdot\frac{1}{(\Lambda q+1)^{2}-e^{-2qs}}J_{1}(qr),

In the limit of small

(B) The case
{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})\neq{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})

In this case the vector potential does not necessarily agrees with that for
the one unit-flux model in contrast to the case A.
Here we take an alternative form of the phase difference
\varphi
.
Here we assume that
s/\Lambda
is small.
For
(0<)s<z
,
{\bf A}({\bf r},z)
is approximated as

\displaystyle{\bf A}({\bf r},z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\int d^{2}qe^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\bigg[\frac{e^{-qz}+e^{-q(z-s)}}{\Lambda q+1+e^{-qs}}{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~-\frac{se^{-q(z-s)}}{s+\Lambda}{\bf F}_{\varphi}({\bf q})\bigg].

Similarly

is written as

\displaystyle{\bf A}({\bf r},z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\int d^{2}qe^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\bigg[\frac{e^{-qz}+e^{-q(z-s)}}{\Lambda q+1+e^{-qs}}{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q})

\displaystyle F_{\Lambda}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{s}{\Lambda+s}e^{-q(z-s)}&(s<z)\\

\frac{s}{\Lambda+s}e^{qz}&(z<0)\\

, we have

{\bf A}({\bf r},z)\simeq-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-q|z|}+e^{-q|z-s|}}{\Lambda q+1+e^{-qs}}J_{1}(qr){\bf e}_{\phi}-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}F_{\Lambda}\nabla\varphi.

(38)

\nabla\varphi
as

\nabla\varphi=(\nabla\varphi)_{\varphi}{\bf e}_{\phi}+(\nabla\varphi)_{r}{\bf e}_{r}
,
we obtain components
A_{\phi}({\bf r},z)
and

.

{\bf B}
for

is simply given as

\displaystyle B_{z}({\bf r},z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\frac{e^{-q|z|}+e^{-q|z-s|}}{\Lambda q+1+e^{-qs}}qJ_{0}(qr)

\displaystyle~~-\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}F_{\Lambda}\frac{1}{r}\bigg[\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(r(\nabla\varphi)_{\phi})-\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}(\nabla\varphi)_{r}\bigg],

(39)

\displaystyle B_{r}({\bf r},z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\frac{J_{1}(qr)}{\Lambda q+1+e^{-qs}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(e^{-q|z|}+e^{-q|z-s|})

\displaystyle~~+\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\frac{\partial F_{\Lambda}}{\partial z}(\nabla\varphi)_{\phi},

(40)

B_{\phi}({\bf r},z)=\frac{\partial A_{r}}{\partial z}=\frac{\phi_{0}}{4\pi}\frac{2}{\Lambda+s}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial r}.

(41)

The second term in

vanishes when

III.2.3
HFQV in non-equivalent two layers

Let us examine the magnetic field distribution when two layers are not equivalent
in the presence of HFQV.
We define

\frac{2}{\Lambda_{1}}=\frac{d}{\lambda_{1}^{2}},~~~~\frac{2}{\Lambda_{2}}=\frac{d}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}.

(42)

From similar calculations we obtain

\displaystyle{\bf A}({\bf q},z)

\displaystyle=\bigg[-e^{-q|z|}(\Lambda_{2}q+1)+e^{-q|z-s|-qs}\bigg]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\Lambda_{1}q+1)(\Lambda_{2}q+1)-e^{-2qs}}\frac{\phi_{0}}{2\pi}~{\bf F}_{\Phi}({\bf q}),

(43)

and we have

\displaystyle A_{\phi}({\bf r},z)

\displaystyle=\phi_{0}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\bigg[-e^{-q|z|}(\Lambda_{2}q+1)+e^{-q|z-s|-qs}\bigg]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\Lambda_{1}q+1)(\Lambda_{2}q+1)-e^{-2qs}}J_{1}(qr).

(44)

When we assume
\Lambda_{1}\gg s
and
\Lambda_{2}\gg s
, in the limit

r\rightarrow\infty
,
A_{\phi}(r,z=0)
approaches

(46)

The total flux deviates from half-flux
\phi_{0}/2
.
When the kink structure of phases in two layers are exchanged, we have

A_{\phi}({\bf r},z=0)\simeq-\frac{\Lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}}\phi_{0}\frac{1}{2\pi r},

(47)

and then

\Phi_{total}(z=0)=\frac{\Lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}}\phi_{0}.

(48)

IV
Incomplete Meissner effect in a compactified bilayer superconductor

IV.1
A superconducting bilayer disc

The currents are written in the form

\displaystyle{\bf J}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{*}}{m_{1}}\rho_{1}^{2}\hbar\left(\nabla\varphi_{1}-\frac{e^{*}}{\hbar c}{\bf A}_{1}\right)=\rho_{1}^{2}e^{*}{\bf v}_{1},

(56)

\displaystyle{\bf J}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{*}}{m_{2}}\rho_{2}^{2}\hbar\left(\nabla\varphi_{2}-\frac{e^{*}}{\hbar c}{\bf A}_{2}\right)=\rho_{2}^{2}e^{*}{\bf v}_{2}.

(57)

The velocities
{\bf v}_{1}
and
{\bf v}_{2}
are defined in these equations.
In the polar coordinate,
{\bf J}
are decomposed into

{\bf J}_{\ell}=J_{\ell r}{\bf e}_{r}+J_{\ell\phi}{\bf e}_{\phi}
,
for
\ell=1,2
(two layers).
In a small disc, we may put a constraint as

J_{\ell r}=0,~~~\ell=1,2

(58)

since the current in the radial direction should vanish.
This results in

A_{1r}=\frac{\hbar c}{e^{*}}\frac{\partial\varphi_{1}}{\partial r},~~~A_{2r}=\frac{\hbar c}{e^{*}}\frac{\partial\varphi_{2}}{\partial r}.

(59)

We define the difference of
{\bf A}_{1}
and
{\bf A}_{2}
as

{\bf A}_{d}({\bf r})={\bf A}_{1}({\bf r},z=0)-{\bf A}_{2}({\bf r},z=s).

(60)

Then the radial component of
{\bf A}_{d}
is given by

\left({\bf A}_{d}\right)_{r}=\frac{\hbar c}{e^{*}}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial r}.

(61)

From
{\bf J}_{1}
and
{\bf J}_{2}
, the angular component of
{\bf A}_{d}
is

\left({\bf A}_{d}\right)_{\phi}=-\frac{c}{e^{*}}\left(m_{1}{\bf v}_{1}-m_{2}{\bf v}_{2}\right)_{\phi}+\frac{\hbar c}{e^{*}}\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\phi},

(62)

where

{\bf p}_{d}({\bf r})=m_{1}{\bf v}_{1}({\bf r},z=0)-m_{2}{\bf v}_{2}({\bf r},z=s)

(63)

indicates the current difference coming from internal phase structure and

({\bf p}_{d})_{\phi}
is its angular component.
We define
\tilde{B}_{z}=(\nabla\times{\bf A}_{d})_{z}
, then

\tilde{B}_{z}=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}({\bf A}_{d})_{r}-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r({\bf A}_{d})_{\phi}\right)=\frac{c}{e^{*}}\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r{\bf p}_{d}\right)_{\phi}.

(64)

Hence, when
\partial(r{\bf p}_{d})_{\phi}/\partial r\neq 0
, we have
\tilde{B}_{z}\neq 0
.
Since

B_{1z}=B_{2z}+\tilde{B}_{z},

(65)

this indicates

B_{1z}\neq B_{2z},

(66)

where
B_{jz}=(\nabla\times{\bf A}_{j})_{z}
.
Thus we have at least
B_{1z}\neq 0
or
B_{2z}\neq 0
.
This shows that the incomplete Meissner effect may occur in a compactified
bilayer superconductor.

The radial component is given by the constraint, for which we use the following

:

where

Then for

(71)

IV.3
Current cancellation

We divide the currents
{\bf J}_{\ell}
(
\ell=1,2
) into two parts

{\bf J}_{\ell}={\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm ext}}+{\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm int}}
,
where
{\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm ext}}
comes from the applied magnetic field and

{\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm int}}
is produced by the spatial dependence of phase
structures.
{\bf J}_{\ell,ext}
is written as

{\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm ext}}=n_{\ell}e^{*}{\bf v}_{\ell,{\rm ext}}
,
for
n_{\ell}=\rho_{\ell}^{2}
.
{\bf v}_{\ell,{\rm ext}}
(
\ell=1,2
)
are velocities induced by the applied external magnetic field for which we have

m_{1}{\bf v}_{1,{\rm ext}}=m_{2}{\bf v}_{2,{\rm ext}}
.

{\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm int}}
is related to
{\bf v}_{\ell,{\rm int}}
as

{\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm int}}=n_{\ell}e^{*}{\bf v}_{\ell,{\rm int}}.

(72)

Since internal currents are canceled out each other, we have

{\bf J}_{1,{\rm int}}+{\bf J}_{2,{\rm int}}=0.

(73)

Total currents
{\bf J}_{\ell}
(
\ell=1,2
) are the sum of two contributions:

{\bf J}_{\ell}={\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm ext}}+{\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm int}}=n_{\ell}e^{*}{\bf v}_{\ell}.

(74)

The constraint that the current in the radial direction should vanish means
that the external and internal currents cancel each other:

({\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm ext}})_{r}+({\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm int}})_{r}=0.

(75)

{\bf J}_{2}={\bf J}_{2,{\rm ext}}-{\bf J}_{1,{\rm int}}=(m_{1}n_{2}/m_{2}n_{1}){\bf J}_{1,{\rm ext}}-{\bf J}_{1,{\rm int}}

is written as

{\bf J}_{2}=\frac{m_{1}n_{2}}{m_{2}n_{1}}{\bf J}_{1}-\left(\frac{m_{1}n_{2}}{m_{2}n_{1}}+1\right){\bf J}_{1,{\rm int}}.

(76)

Since
{\bf J}_{\ell}=n_{\ell}e^{*}{\bf v}_{\ell}
(
\ell=1,2
), we obtain

(m_{1}{\bf v}_{1}-m_{2}{\bf v}_{2})_{\phi}=\frac{m_{2}n_{1}}{n_{2}}\left(\frac{m_{1}n_{2}}{m_{2}n_{1}}+1\right)({\bf v}_{1,{\rm int}})_{\phi}.

(77)

Then the momentum difference
{\bf p}_{d}
is given by the internal velocity

{\bf v}_{1,{\rm int}}
, that is, the internal current
{\bf J}_{1,{\rm int}}
.
Hence we have the unusual magnetic field
\tilde{B}_{z}\neq 0
when

\partial(r({\bf v}_{1,{\rm int}})_{\phi})/\partial r\neq 0.

(78)

This indicates that the anomalous field may appear from the non-trivial
angular and radial dependences of the internal current
{\bf J}_{\ell,{\rm int}}
.

IV.4
Free energy

We argue that the radial component of the field
{\bf A}
becomes massless
in the presence of anomalous field due to the geometric constraint.
The free energy is written as

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\alpha_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell}\beta_{\ell}^{2}\rho_{\ell}^{4}+\sum_{\ell}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{\ell}}(\nabla\rho_{\ell})^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{\ell}}\Biggl\{\left(\frac{\partial\varphi_{\ell}}{\partial r}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi_{\ell}}{\partial\phi}\right)^{2}\Biggr\}

\displaystyle-\frac{2e^{*}}{\hbar c}\sum_{\ell}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell}^{2}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi_{\ell}}{\partial r}A_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\varphi_{\ell}}{\partial\phi}A_{\phi}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{e^{*}}{\hbar c}\right)^{2}\sum_{\ell}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell}^{2}(A_{r}^{2}+A_{\phi}^{2})+\frac{1}{8\pi}(\nabla\times{\bf A})^{2}

\displaystyle-2J_{12}\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\cos(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}).

(79)

We impose the gauge condition that the current in the radial direction vanishes:

\partial\varphi_{\ell}/\partial r-(e^{*}/\hbar c)A_{\ell r}=0
.
We have the free energy given as

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\alpha_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell}\beta_{\ell}^{2}\rho_{\ell}^{4}+\sum_{\ell}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{\ell}}(\nabla\rho_{\ell})^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell}^{2}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi_{\ell}}{\partial\phi}\right)^{2}-\frac{2e^{*}}{\hbar c}\sum_{\ell}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell}^{2}\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\varphi_{\ell}}{\partial\phi}A_{\ell\phi}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{e^{*}}{\hbar c}\right)^{2}\sum_{\ell}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell}^{2}A_{\ell\phi}^{2}+\frac{1}{8\pi}(\nabla\times{\bf A})^{2}

\displaystyle-2J_{12}\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\cos(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}).

(80)

The mass term for
A_{\ell r}
vanishes and
A_{\ell\phi}
remains massive.
The massless mode
A_{\ell r}
results in the incomplete Meissner effect.

IV.5
Discussion on the incomplete Meissner effect in a bilayer
superconductor

We give a discussion on the incomplete Meissner effect and examine its possibility.
An external magnetic field in a Type-II superconductor is cancelled by the magnetic
field produced by electric currents flowing near its surface.
The magnetic field induced by surface currents is usually uniform.
In a bilayer superconductor the internal field will appear when the phase
difference has spatial dependence. In this case the internal magnetic field will have
spatial dependence and is inhomogeneous and thus a compensation of the magnetic field by
surface currents may be incomplete.

V
Summary

We investigated the magnetic field distribution in a bilayer superconductor
and in a one-layer two-component superconductor.
We have taken account of a phase kink structure as well as the half-flux vortex and
a knot soliton.
We examined the magnetic field originating from the derivative
\nabla\varphi
,
and examined the incomplete Meissner effect and incomplete
Higgs phenomenon in a compactified two-component superconductor with
geometric constraints.
This phenomenon could be called the anomalous Meissner effect.
We investigated a model where the current in one direction is suppressed
extremely due to some geometric constraints.
We considered a disc and have shown that the incomplete Meissner effect may occur
when a non-uniform phase-difference distribution is realized.
This non-uniform phase distribution on a layer could be realized as a kink (soliton)
of the phase difference mode.

The existence of massless modes is also significant in a superconducting bilayer
investigated in the paper.
There is a possibility that a massless mode can be found in this system
with geometric constraints such as a disk.
In contrast, in a one-layer two-component superconducting disk, the constraint

J_{r}=0
is approximately satisfied and we have a small unusual field distribution.

If we take into account a possibility that the phase difference mode will
produce non-uniform field distributions,
the incomplete Meissner effect will be present in a bilayer superconductor,
where the incomplete Meissner effect indicates that the magnetic field
penetrates into a superconductor partially.
We discussed this possibility in this paper.
Inhomogeneous magnetic fields are not necessarily compensated by surface currents.
The incomplete Meissner effect is associated with the presence of a massless
component of gauge fields, which is so called the incomplete Higgs mechanism.

Unconventional magnetic distributions are, however, expected to be unstable and
not to be the ground state. There have been experimental efforts to observe
half-flux quantum vortices and
unconventional magnetic field distributions in bilayer
superconductors.
[
59
,
60
,
61
,
62
,
63
]
Singular field
distributions could be captured by precise measurements of magnetic field in
multi-component superconductors.

Physics in Kyoto University.

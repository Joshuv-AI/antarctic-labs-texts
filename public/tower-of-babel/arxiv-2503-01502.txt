The Dirichlet problem for the nonstationary Stokes system in a polygon
Jürgen Rossmann

Abstract




1
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a polygonal domain with the boundary
\partial\Omega=\Gamma\cup{\cal P}
, where

{\cal P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
is a set of corner points and
\Gamma
is the union of the smooth (
\in C^{2}
) arcs
\Gamma_{1}=P_{1}P_{2},\ldots,\Gamma_{n-1}=P_{n-1}P_{n},\Gamma_{n}=P_{n}P_{1}
. For simplicity, we assume that
\Omega

coincides with an angle
K_{j}
with opening
\alpha_{j}
in a neighborhood of the corner point
P_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
.
The present paper deals with the initial-boundary value problem

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathfrak{u}}}{\partial t}-\Delta{\mathfrak{u}}-\nabla\nabla\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}+\nabla{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{f}},\quad-\nabla\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}={\mathfrak{g}}\ \mbox{ in }Q_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T),

(1)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{u}}(x,t)=0\ \mbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega\backslash{\cal P},\ 0<t<T,

(2)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{u}}(x,0)=0\ \mbox{ for }x\in\Omega.

(3)

The analogous problem in an infinite angle was already studied in
[
7
]
. As in
[
7
]
, the major part of the paper deals with the parameter-depending problem

\displaystyle s\,u-\Delta u-\nabla\nabla\cdot u+\nabla p=f,\quad-\nabla\cdot u=g\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(4)

\displaystyle u=0\ \mbox{ on }\Gamma.

(5)

We are interested in solutions of this problem in the class of the weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta}^{l}(\Omega)

which are defined for nonnegative integer
l
and real
n
-tuples
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
as the spaces
(closure of the set
C_{0}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\backslash{\cal P})
with the norm

\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{l}(\Omega)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq l}\prod_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}^{2(\beta_{j}-l+|\alpha|)}\,|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u(x)|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2},

where
r_{j}(x)
denotes the distance of the point
x
from the corner point
P_{j}
.
We suppose that
f\in V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)
and
g\in V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)
, where the inequalities

\max\big(1-\mbox{Re}\,\lambda^{*}(\alpha_{j}),-\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{j}}\big)<\beta_{j}<\min\big(1,\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{j}}\big),\quad\beta_{j}\not=0

(6)

are satisfied for the components
\beta_{j}
of
\beta
. Here
\lambda^{*}(\alpha)
is the solution of the equation

\sin(\lambda\alpha)+\lambda\sin\alpha=0

(7)

with smallest positive real part. It is shown in Section 3 (see Theorem
3.1
) that the problem (
4
), (
5
) has a unique solution
(u,p)
satisfying the
condition
\int_{\Omega}p(x)\,dx=0
and the estimate

\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)}+|s|\,\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|p\|_{W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\Big(\|f\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)}+\ |s|\,\|g\|_{(W_{-\beta}^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}\Big)

with a constant
c
independent of
f,g
and
s
provided that
\mbox{Re}\,s\geq 0
and
|s|>\gamma_{0}
, where
\gamma_{0}
is a sufficiently large positive number.
Here
W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)=\{p\in L_{2}(\Omega):\ \nabla p\in V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)\}
.

Applying the inverse Laplace transform, we prove first the existence of solutions of the problem (
1
)–(
3
) for
T=\infty
in weighted
spaces with the additional weight function
e^{-\gamma t}
,
\gamma>\gamma_{0}
(see Theorem
4.1
). After this it is not difficult to prove
the solvability of this problem on a finite
t
-interval. The main result of the paper (Theorem
4.2
) ist the following:

Suppose that
{\mathfrak{f}}\in L_{2}(0,T;V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega))
,
{\mathfrak{g}}\in L_{2}(0,T;V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega))
and

\partial_{t}{\mathfrak{g}}\in L_{2}(0,T;(W_{-\beta}^{1}(\Omega))^{*})
, where the inequalities (
6
) are satisfied for all
j
.
Furthermore, we assume that
{\mathfrak{g}}(x,0)=0
for
x\in\Omega
and

\int_{\Omega}{\mathfrak{g}}(x,t)\,dx=0\ \mbox{ for almost all }t.

Then there exists a uniquely determined solution
({\mathfrak{u}},{\mathfrak{p}})
of the problem (
1
)–(
3
) such that

{\mathfrak{u}}\in L_{2}(0,T;V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega))
,
{\mathfrak{u}}_{t}\in L_{2}(0,T;V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega))
,

{\mathfrak{p}}\in L_{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
,
\nabla{\mathfrak{p}}\in L_{2}(0,T;V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega))
and

\int_{\Omega}{\mathfrak{p}}(x,t)\,dx=0\ for\ almost\ all\ t.

2
The parameter-dependent problem in an angle

Here, we present a theorem which was proved in
[
7
, Theorem 4.12 and Corollary 4.13]
.
Let
K
be an angle with aperture
\alpha
, i.e.,
K=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2}):\ 0<r<\infty,\ 0<\varphi<\alpha\}
, where
r
,
\varphi
denote the polar
coordinates of the point
x
. The sides of
K
are the half-lines
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
,
where
\varphi=0
on
\Gamma_{1}
and
\varphi=\alpha
on
\Gamma_{2}
.
We consider the problem

\displaystyle s\,u-\Delta u-\nabla\nabla\cdot u+\nabla p=f,\quad-\nabla\cdot u=g\mbox{ in }K,

(8)

\displaystyle u=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}.

(9)

For nonnegative integer
l
and real
\beta
, we define the weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta}^{l}(K)

as the set of all functions (or vector functions) with finite norm

\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{l}(K)}=\Big(\int_{K}r^{2(\beta-l+|\alpha|)}\,\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u(x)\big|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}.

(10)

The intersection
V_{\beta}^{2}(K)\cap V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
is denoted by
E_{\beta}^{2}(K)
. Furthermore, let
W_{\beta}^{1}(K)
be the space with the norm

\|g\|_{W_{\beta}^{1}(K)}=\Big(\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\\
1<|x|<2\end{subarray}}|g|^{2}\,dx+\int_{K}r^{2\beta}|\nabla g|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}.

Theorem 2.1

Suppose that
s\not=0
,
\mbox{Re}\,s\geq 0
and

\max(-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\,,1-\mbox{Re}\,\lambda^{*}(\alpha))<\beta<\min(2,\frac{\pi}{\alpha})
,
\beta\not=0
,
where
\lambda^{*}(\alpha)
is the solution of the equation (
7
) with smallest positive real part.
Furthermore, let
f\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
and
g\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K)\cap(W_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}
. In the case
0<\beta<2
,
we assume that the integral of
g
over
K
is zero. Then the the problem

s\,u-\Delta u-\nabla\nabla\cdot u+\nabla p=f,\quad-\nabla\cdot u=g\mbox{ in }K,\quad u=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}

(11)

has a unique solution
(u,p)\in E_{\beta}^{2}(K)\times V_{\beta}^{1}(K)
satisfying the estimate

\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{2}(K)}+|s|\,\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}+\|p\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(K)}\leq c\,\Big(\|f\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}+\|g\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(K)}+|s|\,\|g\|_{(W_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\Big)

(12)

with a constant
c
independent of
f
,
g
and
s
.

Note that
\mbox{Re}\,\lambda^{*}(\alpha)>1/2
for
\alpha<2\pi
and
\mbox{Re}\,\lambda^{*}(\alpha)>1
for
\alpha<\pi
.

Remark 2.1

In
[
7
, Theorem 4.12]
, the assertion of Theorem
2.1
was obtained under the assumption that

f\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)
and
g\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K)\cap(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}
. But this assumption is the same as in Theorem
2.1

if the integral of
g
is zero for
0<\beta<2
.
Indeed, if
\beta<0
, then it follows from Hardy’s inequality that the spaces
W_{-\beta}^{1}(K)
and
V_{-\beta}^{1}(K)
coincide.
Suppose that
\beta>0
,
g\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K)\cap(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}
and
\int_{K}g\,dx=0
. Then any
q\in W_{-\beta}^{1}(K)
is continuous
at the corner point
x=0
and

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{K}g\,q\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{K}g\,(q-q(0))\,dx\Big|\leq\|g\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\ \|q-q(0)\|_{V_{-\beta}^{1}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}\,\|g\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\ \|q\|_{W_{-\beta}^{1}(K)}

(see
[
3
, Lemma 7.1.3]
). This means that
g\in(W_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}
,

\|g\|_{(W_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\leq c_{1}\,\|g\|_{(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\leq c_{2}\,\|g\|_{(W_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\,,

and the norm of
g
in
(W_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}
in the estimate (
12
) can be replaced by the norm in the space
(V_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}
.

Remark 2.2

Suppose that
f\in V_{\beta}^{0}(K)\cap V_{\gamma}^{0}(K)
,
g\in V_{\beta}^{1}(K)\cap V_{\gamma}^{1}(K)
and
g\in\cap(W_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}\cap(W_{-\gamma}^{1}(K))^{*}
,
where

\max\Big(-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\,,1-\mbox{Re}\,\lambda^{*}(\alpha)\Big)<\beta<\gamma<\min\Big(2,\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\Big),

\gamma>0
and
\beta\not=0
. Furthermore, it is assumed that the integral of
g
over
K
is zero. Then there exist solutions

(u,p)\in E_{\gamma}^{2}(K)\times V_{\gamma}^{1}(K)
and
(u^{\prime},p^{\prime})\in E_{\beta}^{2}(K)\times V_{\beta}^{1}(K)
of the problem (
11
).
By
[
7
, Lemma 4.5 and Lemma 4.7]
, we have
u=u^{\prime}
and, consequently,
\nabla p=\nabla p^{\prime}
. If
\beta>0
then even
p=p^{\prime}
.
Thus, the solution
(u,p)
satisfies the estimate

\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{2}(K)}+|s|\,\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}+\|\nabla p\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}\leq c\,\Big(\|f\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(K)}+\|g\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(K)}+|s|\,\|g\|_{(W_{-\beta}^{1}(K))^{*}}\Big)

with a constant
c
independent of
f
,
g
and
s
.

3
The parameter-dependent problem in a polygon

We consider the problem (
4
), (
5
) in the polygonal domain
\Omega
which was described in the introduction.
First, we prove the existence of solutions
(u,p)\in V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)\times W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)
.
After this, we estimate this solution in relation to the data
f,g
and the parameter
s
.

3.1
Existence of solutions

Let
W^{l}(\Omega)
be the Sobolev space with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{l}(\Omega)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq l}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u(x)|^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}.

The closure of the set
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in this space is denoted by
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}\!{}^{l}(\Omega)
, and

W^{-l}(\Omega)
is defined as the dual space of
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}\!{}^{l}(\Omega)
. By
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega}
we denote
the
L_{2}(\Omega)
-scalar product and its extension to the product
W^{-l}(\Omega)\times\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}\!{}^{l}(\Omega)
.

Furthermore, let
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{L}}{}_{2}(\Omega)
be the set of all
g\in L_{2}(\Omega)
satisfying the condition

\int_{\Omega}g(x)\,dx=0.

(13)

We consider the bilinear form

b_{s}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\Big(su\cdot v+2\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\varepsilon_{i,j}(u)\,\varepsilon_{i,j}(v)\Big)\,dx,

where
\varepsilon(u)
denotes the strain tensor with the elements

\varepsilon_{i,j}({\mathfrak{u}})=\frac{1}{2}\,\Big(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\Big),\quad i,j=1,\ldots,2,

Obviously, this bilinear form is continuous on
W^{1}(\Omega)\times W^{1}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, it follows from Korn’s inequality that

\big|b_{s}(u,\overline{u})\big|\geq c\,\|u\|^{2}_{W^{1}(\Omega)}

(14)

for all
u\in W^{1}(\Omega)
and for all
s
,
\mbox{Re}\,s\geq 0
,
s\not=0
, where
c
depends on
|s|
but not on
u
.
The following lemma can be found e.g. in
[
2
, Chapter 1, Corollary 2.4]
or
[
6
, Lemma 11.1.1]

Lemma 3.1

Let
g\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{L}}\!{}_{2}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a vector function
u\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}\!{}^{1}(\Omega)

such that
\nabla\cdot u=g
in
\Omega
and

\|u\|_{W^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\|g\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

with a constant
c
independent of
g
.

Applying the inequality (
14
) and Lemma
3.1
, one can prove the following lemma analogously to
[
6
, Theorem 11.1.2]
.

Lemma 3.2

Suppose that
f\in W^{-1}(\Omega)
,
g\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{L}}\!{}_{2}(\Omega)
,

\mbox{Re}\,s\geq 0
and
s\not=0
. Then there exists a unique solution
(u,p)\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}\!{}^{1}(\Omega)\times\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{L}}\!{}_{2}(\Omega)

of the problem

b_{s}(u,\bar{v})-\int_{\Omega}p\,\nabla\cdot\bar{v}\,dx=(f,v)_{\Omega}\ \mbox{ for all }v\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}\!{}^{1}(\Omega),\quad-\nabla\cdot u=g\mbox{ in }\Omega.

(15)

satisfying the estimate

\|u\|_{W^{1}(\Omega)}+\|p\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\Big(\|f\|_{W^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\Big),

(16)

where
c
depends on
|s|
but not on
f
and
g
.

The solution of the problem (
15
) is called a weak solution of the problem (
4
), (
5
).
Let
l
be a positive integer. Then we define
W_{\beta}^{l}(\Omega)
as the weighted Sobolev space with the norm

\|u\|_{W_{\beta}^{l}(\Omega)}=\Big(\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{V_{\beta}^{l-1}(\Omega)}+\|u\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\Big)^{1/2}.

It follows from Hardy’s inequality that
W_{\beta}^{l}(\Omega)=V_{\beta}^{l}(\Omega)
if
\beta_{j}>l-1
for all
j
.
Using the last lemma together with well-known regularity results for elliptic problems (and, in particular, for the stationary Stokes system),
we are able to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3

Suppose that
\mbox{Re}\,s\geq 0
,
s\not=0
,
f\in V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)
and
g\in V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)
,
where the components
\beta_{j}
of
\beta
satisfy the inequalities

1-\mbox{Re}\,\lambda^{*}(\alpha_{j})<\beta_{j}<1,\ \ \beta_{j}\not=0\ \mbox{ for all }j.

Furthermore, we assume that
g
satisfies the condition (
13
).
Then there exists a unique solution
(u,p)\in V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)\times(W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{L}}\!{}_{2}(\Omega))

of the problem (
4
), (
5
) satisfying the estimate

\|u\|_{V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|p\|_{W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\Big(\|f\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),

(17)

where
c
depends on
|s|
but not on
f
and
g
.

Proof. Since
\beta_{j}<1
for all
j
, it follows from Hölder’s and Hardy’s inequalities that

\Big|\int_{\Omega}f\cdot v\,dx\Big|\leq\|f\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}\ \|v\|_{V_{-\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\|f\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}\ \|v\|_{W^{1}(\Omega)}

Hence, the functional

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}\!{}^{1}(\Omega)\ni v\to(f,\bar{v})_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot v\,dx

is continuous. Furthermore,
g\in V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)\subset L_{2}(\Omega)
. By Lemma
3.2
, there exists a unique solution

(u,p)\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}\!{}^{1}(\Omega)\times\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{L}}\!{}_{2}(\Omega)
of the problem (
15
) satisfying the estimate (
16
).
Let
\zeta_{j}
be two times continuously differentiable functions with support in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
P_{j}
equal to one
near
P_{j}
. The vector function
\zeta_{j}(u,p)
satisfies the equations

-\Delta(\zeta_{j}u)+\nabla(\zeta_{j}p)=F_{j}=\zeta_{j}(f-\nabla g-su)-u\,\Delta\zeta_{j}-2\sum_{i=1}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}\zeta_{j}\,\partial_{x_{i}}u+p\,\nabla\zeta_{j}

and
-\nabla\cdot(\zeta_{j}u)=G_{j}=\zeta_{j}g-u\cdot\nabla\zeta_{j}
. It follows from Hardy’s inequality that
\zeta_{j}u\in V_{\beta_{j}}^{0}(K_{j})

for
u\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{W}}\!{}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\beta_{j}>-1
. Consequently,
F_{j}\in V_{\beta_{j}}^{0}(K_{j})
and
G_{j}\in V_{\beta_{j}}^{1}(K_{j})
if
\beta_{j}>-1
.
Using well-known regularity results for the stationary Stokes system, we conclude that
\zeta_{j}(u,p)\in V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)\times V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)

if
\beta_{j}>0
and

\|\zeta_{j}u\|_{V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\zeta_{j}p\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\Big(\|F_{j}\|_{V_{\beta_{j}}^{0}(K_{j})}+\|G_{j}\|_{V_{\beta_{j}}^{1}(K_{j})}\Big)\leq c^{\prime}\,\Big(\|f\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big).

If
\max(-1,1-\mbox{Re}\,\lambda^{*}(\alpha_{j}))<\beta_{j}<0
, then the vector function
\zeta_{j}(u,p)
admits the asymptotics
\zeta_{j}(u,p)=(0,c_{j})+(v,q)
,
where
(v,q)\in V_{\beta_{j}}^{2}(K_{j})\times V_{\beta_{j}}^{1}(K_{j})
.
This implies
\zeta_{j}u\in V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)
and
\nabla(\zeta_{j}p)\in V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, the estimate (
17
) holds.

Suppose that
\max(-3,1-\mbox{Re}\,\lambda^{*}(\alpha_{j}))<\beta_{j}\leq-1
. Then
F_{j}\in V_{\beta^{\prime}_{j}}^{0}(K_{j})
and
G_{j}\in V_{\beta^{\prime}_{j}}^{0}(K_{j})

for arbitrary
\beta^{\prime}_{j}>-1
. Consequently,
\zeta_{j}u\in V_{\beta^{\prime}_{j}}^{2}(\Omega)
and
\nabla(\zeta_{j}p)\in V_{\beta^{\prime}_{j}}^{0}(\Omega)
for arbitrary

\beta^{\prime}_{j}
,
-1<\beta^{\prime}_{j}>0
.
But then
F_{j}\in V_{\beta_{j}}^{0}(K_{j})
and
G_{j}\in V_{\beta_{j}}^{1}(K_{j})
and, consequently,
\zeta_{j}u\in V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)
and
\nabla(\zeta_{j}p)\in V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)
.

Repeating this argument, we obtain this result for arbitrary
\beta_{j}>1-\mbox{Re}\,\lambda^{*}(\alpha_{j})
. This proves the existence of a solution

(u,p)\in V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)\times(W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{L}}\!{}_{2}(\Omega))
. The uniqueness of this solution follows from the
imbedding
V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)\subset W^{1}(\Omega)
(since
\beta_{j}<1
for all
j
) and Lemma
3.2
.
\Box

3.2
An estimate for
p

Clearly the constant
c
in the estimate of Lemma
3.3
depends on
s
. Our goal is to obtain a more precise estimate.
We prove the existence of solutions of the Neumann problem for the Poisson equation in the function space
W_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)
which was introduced in the foregoing subsection.

Lemma 3.4

Suppose that
\phi\in V_{1-\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)
, where
0<\gamma_{j}<\pi/\alpha_{j}
for all
j
, and that the integral of
\phi
over
\Omega
is zero.
Then there exists a solution
q\in W_{1-\gamma}^{2}(\Omega)
of the problem

-\Delta q=\phi\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\quad\frac{\partial q}{\partial n}=0\ \mbox{on }\Gamma

(18)

which satisfies the estimate

\|q\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla q\|_{V_{1-\gamma}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\|\phi\|_{V_{1-\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)}

(19)

with a constant
c
independent of
\phi
.

Proof. Since
\gamma_{j}>0
for all
j
, Hölder’s inequality implies the imbedding
V_{1-\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)\subset L_{1}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore, it follows from Hardy’s inequality that

\int_{\Omega}\prod r_{j}^{2\gamma_{j}-2}|v|^{2}\,dx\leq c\int_{\Omega}\big(\prod r_{j}^{2\gamma_{j}}\,|\nabla v|^{2}+|v|^{2}\big)\,dx\leq c^{\prime}\,\|v\|^{2}_{W^{1}(\Omega)}

for all
v\in W^{1}(\Omega)
, i.e.,
W^{1}(\Omega)\subset V_{\gamma-1}^{0}(\Omega)
and, consequently,

V_{1-\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)\subset(W^{1}(\Omega))^{*}
.
As is known, the
W^{1}
-norm is equivalent to the norm

\|q\|=\|\nabla q\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

on the subspace
W^{1}(\Omega)\cap\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{L}}\!{}_{2}(\Omega)
.
Hence there exists a unique variational solution
q\in W^{1}(\Omega)\cap\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{L}}\!{}_{2}(\Omega)
of the problem
(
18
), i.e.,

\int_{\Omega}\nabla q\cdot\nabla v\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\phi\,v\,dx\ \mbox{ for all }v\in W^{1}(\Omega).

This solution satisfies the estimate

\|q\|_{W^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c^{\prime}\,\|\phi\|_{(W^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}\leq c\,\|\phi\|_{V_{1-\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)}\,.

For every
j
, let
\zeta_{j}
be a smooth (of class
C^{2}
) cut-off function with support in the neighborhood
{\cal U}_{j}
of
P_{j}

which is equal to one near
P_{j}
and satisfies the condition
\partial\zeta_{j}/\partial n=0
on
\Gamma
.
Obviously,
\zeta_{j}q\in V_{\varepsilon}^{1}(K_{j})
with arbitrary positive
\varepsilon
and

-\Delta(\zeta_{j}q)=\zeta_{j}\phi-2\nabla\zeta_{j}\cdot\nabla q-q\,\Delta\zeta_{j}\in V_{1-\gamma_{j}}^{0}(K_{j}).

Hence,
\zeta_{j}q
admits the representation

\zeta_{j}q=c_{j}+d_{j}\log r_{j}+w_{j},

where
w_{j}\in V_{1-\gamma_{j}}^{2}(K_{j})
. Since
\zeta_{j}(q-w_{j})\in W^{1}(K_{j})
, it follows that
d_{j}=0
and, consequently,
\nabla(\zeta_{j}q)=\nabla w_{j}\in V_{1-\gamma_{j}}^{1}(K_{j})
,

\|\nabla(\zeta_{j}q)\|_{V_{1-\gamma_{j}}^{1}(K_{j})}\leq\|w\|_{V_{1-\gamma_{j}}^{2}(K_{j})}\leq c\,\|\phi\|_{V_{1-\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)}\,.

Since obviously,
q\in W^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
for every subdomain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega:\ \mbox{dist}(x,{\cal P})>\varepsilon\}
,
we conclude that
\nabla q\in V_{1-\gamma}^{1}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, the estimate (
19
) holds.
\Box

In the following lemma, we consider solutions
(u,p)\in V_{\gamma}^{2}(\Omega)\times V_{\gamma}^{1}(\Omega)
of the problem (
4
), (
5
).
Note that the constant function
p=c
is an element of the space
V_{\gamma}^{1}(\Omega)
if
\gamma_{j}>0
for all
j
.
As in Lemma
3.3
, we assume that the integral of
p
over

Lemma 3.5

Suppose that
\mbox{Re}\,s\geq 0
,
s\not=0
, and that
(u,p)\in V_{\gamma}^{2}(\Omega)\times V_{\gamma}^{1}(\Omega)

is a solution of the problem (

for all

\displaystyle\|p\|_{V_{\gamma-1}^{0}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\Big(\|f\|_{V_{\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{V_{\gamma}^{1}(\Omega)}+|s|\,\|g\|_{(W_{-\gamma}^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}+\|Du\|_{V_{\gamma-1/2}^{0}(\Gamma)}\Big),

(20)

\partial_{x_{i}}u_{j}

\|u\|_{V_{\gamma-1/2}^{0}(\Gamma)}=\Big\|\prod_{j}r_{j}^{\gamma_{j}-1/2}u\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Gamma)}\,.

The constant

u,p
and
s

Proof.
By (
4
), (
5
), we have

\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot\nabla\bar{q}\,dx=(\Phi,q)_{\Omega}\ \mbox{for all }q\in W_{1-\gamma}^{2}(\Omega),

(\Phi,q)_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}(f+\Delta u+\nabla\nabla\cdot u)\cdot\nabla\bar{q}\,dx-s\,(g,q)_{\Omega}\,.

Integration by parts yields

-\int_{\Omega}p\,\Delta\bar{q}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma}p\,\frac{\partial\bar{q}}{\partial n}\,d\sigma=(\Phi,q)_{\Omega}.

(21)

Let
\phi\in V_{1-\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)

\Big|\int_{\Omega}\phi\,dx\Big|\leq c\,\|\phi\|_{V_{1-\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)}\,.

We define
\displaystyle c_{0}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\phi\,dx.
By Lemma
3.4

there exists a solution

of the problem

\int_{\Omega}p\,\bar{\phi}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}p\,\overline{\phi-c_{0}}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}p\,\Delta\bar{q}\,dx=(\Phi,q)_{\Omega}\,.

\|p\|^{2}_{V_{\gamma-1}^{0}(\Omega)}=(\Phi,q)_{\Omega}\,.

There is the decomposition

(\Phi_{1},q)_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}\Big((f-2\nabla g)\cdot\nabla\bar{q}-sg\,\bar{q}\Big)\,dx,\quad(\Phi_{2},q)_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta u-\nabla\nabla\cdot u)\,\nabla\bar{q}\,dx

\Big|\int_{\Omega}(f-2\nabla g)\,\nabla\bar{q}\,dx\Big|\leq c\,\Big(\|f\|_{V_{\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{V_{\gamma}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)\,\|\nabla q\|_{V_{-\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)}\,.

Thus,

\big|(\Phi_{1},q)_{\Omega}\big|\leq c\,\Big(\|f\|_{V_{\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{V_{\gamma}^{1}(\Omega)}+|s|\,\|g\|_{(W_{-\gamma}^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}\Big)\,\|p\|_{V_{\gamma-1}^{0}(\Omega)}\,.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\big|(\Phi_{2},q)_{\Omega}\big|=\Big|\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big(n_{j}\,\frac{\partial\bar{q}}{\partial x_{i}}-n_{i}\,\frac{\partial\bar{q}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)\,d\sigma\Big|

\displaystyle\leq c\,\|Du\|_{V_{\gamma-1/2}^{0}(\Gamma)}\,\|\nabla q\|_{V_{-\gamma+1/2}^{0}(\Gamma)}\leq c^{\prime}\,\|Du\|_{V_{\gamma-1/2}^{0}(\Gamma)}\,\|\nabla q\|_{V_{1-\gamma}^{1}(\Omega)}\,,

Consequently, (

Theorem 3.1

Proof.

3.3

Let
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}

3.3
.

Then the vector function

Furthermore,
\zeta_{j}u=0
on

By Theorem
2.1
and Remark

, there is the estimate

\displaystyle\|\zeta_{j}u\|_{V_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)}+|s|\,\|\zeta_{j}u\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla(\zeta_{j}p)\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\Big(\|F_{j}\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|G_{j}\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)}+|s|\,\|G_{j}\|_{(W_{-\beta}^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}\Big)

(25)

for

. Since all functions in (

for

is zero in a neighborhood of any corner point

P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
. Using existence and uniqueness results for the Dirichlet problem for the nonstationary
Stokes system in domains with smooth boundaries (see

8

), one can prove the estimate (
25
) for
j=n+1
.
Here, we refer to
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[
5
, Lemma 2.6]
(in
[
4
]
, the norm of

in

must be replaced by the norm in

).
One can easily verify the estimate

\displaystyle\|F_{j}\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|G_{j}\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)}+|s|\,\|G_{j}\|_{(W_{-\beta}^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}\leq\|\zeta_{j}f\|_{V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|\zeta_{j}g\|_{V_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+|s|\,\|\zeta_{j}g\|_{(W_{-\beta}^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}+c\,\Big(\|u\|_{W^{1}(\Omega_{j})}+\|p\|_{L_{2}(\Omega_{j})}+|s|\,\|u\cdot\nabla\zeta_{j}\|_{(W_{-\beta}^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}\Big),

(26)

where

.
By Ehrling’s lemma, there is the inequality

with an arbitrarily small positive

. We estimate the norm of

in

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\Omega}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla(q\partial_{x_{i}}\zeta_{j})+p\,\nabla\cdot(q\nabla\zeta_{j})\Big)\,dx+\int_{\Gamma}\Big(\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\cdot q\nabla\zeta_{j}-pq\nabla\zeta_{j}\cdot n\Big)\,d\sigma

and, consequently,

\displaystyle\|su\cdot\nabla\zeta_{j}\|_{(W_{-\beta}^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}

\displaystyle\|p\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,\|p\|_{V_{\gamma-1}^{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c^{\prime}\,\Big(\|f\|_{V_{\gamma}^{0}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{V_{\gamma}^{1}(\Omega)}+|s|\,\|g\|_{(W_{-\gamma}^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}+\|Du\|_{V_{\gamma-1/2}^{0}(\Gamma)}\Big).

yields

\displaystyle\|{\mathfrak{u}}-{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}\|_{W_{\beta}^{2,1}(\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+},e^{-\gamma t})}+\|{\mathfrak{p}}-{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}\|_{L_{2,-\gamma}(0,\infty;W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega))}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\Big(\|{\mathfrak{F}}-{\mathfrak{F}}^{\prime}\|_{L_{2,-\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega))}+\|{\mathfrak{G}}-{\mathfrak{G}}^{\prime}\|_{W_{\beta}^{1,1}(\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+},e^{-\gamma t})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq c\,e^{-\gamma T}\,\Big(\|{\mathfrak{F}}-{\mathfrak{F}}^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty;V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega))}+\|{\mathfrak{G}}-{\mathfrak{G}}^{\prime}\|_{W_{\beta}^{1,1}(\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})}\Big)

for
\gamma>\gamma_{0}
, where
c
is independent of
\gamma
. Since
e^{\gamma(T-\varepsilon-t)}\geq 1
for
t\leq T-\varepsilon
, we have

\|{\mathfrak{u}}-{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}\|_{W_{\beta}^{2,1}(\Omega\times(0,T-\varepsilon))}\leq e^{\gamma(T-\varepsilon)}\,\|{\mathfrak{u}}-{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}\|_{W_{\beta}^{2,1}(\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+},e^{-\gamma t})}

and

\|{\mathfrak{p}}-{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}(0,T-\varepsilon;W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega))}\leq e^{\gamma(T-\varepsilon)}\,\|{\mathfrak{p}}-{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}\|_{L_{2,-\gamma}(0,\infty;W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega))}

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|{\mathfrak{u}}-{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}\|_{W_{\beta}^{2,1}(\Omega\times(0,T-\varepsilon))}+\|{\mathfrak{p}}-{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}(0,T-\varepsilon;W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq c\,e^{-\gamma\varepsilon}\,\Big(\|{\mathfrak{F}}-{\mathfrak{F}}^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}(0,\infty;V_{\beta}^{0}(\Omega))}+\|{\mathfrak{G}}-{\mathfrak{G}}^{\prime}\|_{W_{\beta}^{1,1}(\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})}\Big),

where
c
is independent of
\gamma
for
\gamma>\gamma_{0}
. If we let
\gamma
tend to infinity, we conclude that
{\mathfrak{u}}={\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}

and
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
in
\Omega\times(0,T-\varepsilon)
for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. This proves the theorem.
\Box

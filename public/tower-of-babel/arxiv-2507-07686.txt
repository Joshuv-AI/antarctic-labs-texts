Quantitative isoperimetric inequalities in capillarity problems and cones in strong and barycentric forms
Davide Carazzato, Giulio Pascale, Marco Pozzetta

Abstract
. We study quantitative isoperimetric inequalities for two different perimeter-type functionals. We first consider classical capillarity functionals, which measure the perimeter of sets in a Euclidean half-space, assigning a constant weight λ ∈ ( − 1 , 1 ) \lambda\in(-1,1) to the portion of the boundary that lies on the boundary of the half-space. We then consider the relative perimeter of sets contained in some suitable convex cone in the Euclidean space. In both settings, we establish sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequalities in the so-called strong form. More precisely, we show that the isoperimetric deficit of a competitor not only controls the Fraenkel asymmetry, but it also controls an oscillation asymmetry that measures how much the unit normals to the boundary of a competitor deviate from those of an isoperimetric set. Our technique is also able to explicitly identify a center that can be employed to compute the asymmetries. In particular, we also derive barycentric versions of these quantitative isoperimetric inequalities (both in the classical and in the strong form). The proofs are based on the derivation of Fuglede-type estimates for graphs defined on general spherically convex domains, in combination with a new application of the selection principle that directly provides the inequalities in barycentric form.

1.
Introduction

The study of quantitative stability of geometric and functional inequalities, and in particular of isoperimetric inequalities, represents a wide research line in Calculus of Variations in the last thirty years. The sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequality for the classical perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
was first proved in
[
FMP08
]
, after
[
Fug89
,
Hal92
,
HHW91
]
, and it states that

(1.1)

\inf_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|E\Delta B_{1}(x_{0})|^{2}\leq C(n)\big[P(E)-P(B_{1})\big],

for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|E|=|B_{1}|
, where
P(\cdot)
denotes the perimeter functional, and
|\cdot|
denotes Lebesgue measure.
The left hand side in (
1.1
) is the so-called Fraenkel asymmetry with respect to Euclidean balls. After
[
FMP08
]
, many other proofs of the quantitative inequality have been found; in fact, quantitative isoperimetric inequalities have been proved for several different notions of perimeter functionals, such as anisotropic, weighted, or non-local perimeters
[
FMP10
,
CL12
,
AFM13
,
FJ14
,
BBJ17
,
Fig+15
,
Cia+11
,
FI13
,
Cin+22
]
. We refer the interested reader to the very nice survey
[
Fus15
]
and to references therein.

While (
1.1
) bounds from above an
L^{1}
-notion of distance of a competitor from the set of minimizers in terms of the perimeter deficit, it is well-known since
[
Fug89
]
that if a competitor
E
is a sufficiently small
C^{1}
-graphical perturbation of a ball in terms of a function
u:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
that parametrizes
\partial E
over the sphere, then the left hand side in (
1.1
) can be replaced by the
H^{1}
-norm of
u
. More recently, in
[
FJ14
]
the authors managed to prove that (
1.1
) can be, in fact, improved in order to bound a higher order notion of distance of a competitor from the set of minimizers in terms of its perimeter deficit. Such an inequality represents a so-called
strong form
of the quantitative isoperimetric inequality and reads

(1.2)

\inf\left\{\int_{\partial^{*}E}\left|\nu_{E}(x)-\frac{x-x_{0}}{|x-x_{0}|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\ :\ x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}\leq C(n)\big[P(E)-P(B_{1})\big],

for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|E|=|B_{1}|
, where
\partial^{*}E
denotes the reduced boundary of
E
, and
\nu_{E}
denotes its generalized outward unit normal, see
Section

2.1
.
The left hand side in (
1.2
) is an oscillation asymmetry that measures how much the unit normals of a competitor deviate from those of an isoperimetric sets, see
Fig.

1
.
Roughly speaking, it represents an
H^{1}
-notion of distance of a general competitor from the set of minimizers.

Further quantitative inequalities in strong form have been obtained in
[
Neu16
]
for anisotropic perimeters, in
[
DeM24
]
in the case of crystalline perimeters, and in
[
BDF17
,
BDS15
]
for the problem set on the sphere or on the hyperbolic space. We will compare these works with ours below. We also mention that in
[
BBJ17
]
it is proved a dimension-free inequality in strong form in the Gaussian space.

Another possible strengthening of inequalities (
1.1
), (
1.2
) is represented by
barycentric forms
of quantitative isoperimetric inequalities. In
[
GP25
]
, after
[
BCH23
]
, it is proved that

(1.3)

|E\Delta B_{1}(b_{E})|^{2}\leq C(n,d)\big[P(E)-P(B_{1})\big],

for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|E|=|B_{1}|
and diameter
{\rm diam}(E)\leq d
, where
b_{E}
is the barycenter of
E
. The dependence of the constant in (
1.3
) on
d
cannot be removed, but the left hand side gets a remarkable improvement compared to (
1.1
) as the asymmetry is computed with respect to a unique ball, centered at a well-determined point.

In this paper, by proposing a unifying approach, we will prove quantitative isoperimetric inequalities in both
strong and barycentric forms
for the isoperimetric problems defined by two perimeter-type functionals: (i) the classical capillarity functional in a half-space, and (ii) the standard (relative) perimeter in a convex cone. To our knowledge, this is the first instance where a
strong
quantitative isoperimetric inequality is also proved in barycentric form.

The capillarity functional with parameter
\lambda\in(-1,1)
in a half-space
H:=\{x_{n}\geq 0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

P_{\lambda}(E):=P(E,\{x_{n}>0\})-\lambda P(E,\{x_{n}=0\}),

for any set
E\subset H
of finite perimeter.
It is well-known that isoperimetric sets for the capillarity functional are given by (translated rescalings of) the bubble
B^{\lambda}:=(B_{1}(0)-\lambda e_{n})\cap H
, where
e_{n}:=(0,\ldots,0,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
Mag12
, Section 19, Theorem 19.21]
,
[
Fin86
]
and the classical works
[
GMT80
,
Giu81
,
Tam84
]
). The latter minimality property comes with the sharp and rigid isoperimetric inequality

(1.4)

P_{\lambda}(E)\geq n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\qquad\forall E\subset H,\quad|E|<\infty.

Capillarity-type problems still attract a remarkable attention. We mention here recent works on the fine regularity of almost minimizers and varifolds
[
DM15
,
CEL25
,
Mas+24
]
, and recent developments on sharp and rigid relative and capillarity isoperimetric inequalities outside convex bodies
[
CGR07
,
FM23
,
LWW23
,
Kru17
,
FJM24
]
.
A quantitative version of (
1.4
) for the Fraenkel asymmetry in the spirit of (
1.1
) has been recently proved in
[
PP24
,
KS24
]
(see
Theorem

2.3
below).

Remark 1.3

(More barycentric inequalities)
.

Our method can be applied to further isoperimetric-type problems, producing barycentric forms of quantitative inequalities. In fact, in
Corollary

2.15
we derive a barycentric version of the quantitative inequality (
2.1
) for the Fraenkel asymmetry in capillarity problems.
Moreover, with our technique we could obtain the barycentric version of the first strong quantitative isoperimetric inequality (
1.2
) from
[
FJ14
]
. Similarly, our method would also recover the quantitative isoperimetric inequality with barycentric Fraenkel asymmetry for the standard perimeter
P
, which was obtained in dimension
2
in
[
BCH23
, Theorem 3.1]
, and recently generalized to every dimension in
[
GP25
, Theorem A]
.

We stress that our approach is always able to detect an explicit point
x^{*}
representing a “center” where the asymmetry can be computed, see
Corollary

2.13
and (
3.16
). In this case, the constants appearing in the inequalities are
independent
of the diameter of the considered sets. On the other hand, if one whishes to compute the asymmetries with respect to the the actual barycenter, as stated in
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
, our technique provides a constant depending on the diameter
d
of the set. The dependence on
d
is, in this case, inevitable; however, we are not able to obtain a precise dependence of the constants
C^{\prime}_{\ref{thm:MAINcapillarity}}
,
C^{\prime}_{\ref{thm:MAINcones}}
on
d
.

Finally, we believe that our approach might be useful to provide a quantitative inequality with barycentric asymmetry for the fractional perimeter as well. There is a very recent result for convex sets in
[
Gam+25
]
, and our methods could be fruitful to treat the general case.

1.1.
Strategy of the proof and technical comments

We first discuss the content of our work regarding the capillarity perimeter, which contains the main technical novelties.
Next, we comment on the isoperimetric problem in convex cones, that requires similar (and in many cases simpler) arguments.

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we essentially follow the classical strategy leading to a selection-type argument, as also done in
[
FJ14
,
Neu16
,
DeM24
]
. The method consists in two very different steps:

(Fuglede inequality):

prove the quantitative inequality for sets that are regular small perturbations of our model (in this case
B^{\lambda}
). This is the content of
Proposition

2.9
, building on
Lemmas

2.7
and
2.8
;

(Selection principle):

reduce to regular sets by finding suitable competitors as minimizers of some well designed auxiliary functionals, exploiting the regularity theory already developed for the perimeter functional (see
Theorem

A.5
). This is the content of
Proposition

2.11
.

In our setting, both of the steps present nontrivial difficulties with respect to the classical results available in the literature. Concerning the Fuglede inequality, the standard approach consists in a Fourier expansion of the perimeter and asymmetry terms (see
[
Fug89
, Section 1]
and
[
CL12
, Lemma 4.2]
). However, for the capillarity problem, this is not feasible for different reasons: even if we could consider only sets that can be parametrized as normal graphs on
B^{\lambda}
(and this is already too restrictive in order to combine it with the selection principle), we lack of a description of the eigenvalues of the Laplacian on the spherical cap
\partial B^{\lambda}\cap\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
with Dirichlet data. Instead, our idea is to parametrize both
B^{\lambda}
and its perturbation over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
, and then to do a Taylor expansion of the quantities involved. Still, the parametrization of
B^{\lambda}
does not seem to have any good properties in terms of spherical harmonics, and thus we cannot perform the expansion of our perturbation with the usual technique. We then follow the path paved by
[
Neu16
, Proposition 1.9]
, relying on the quantitative isoperimetric inequality with Fraenkel asymmetry (i.e.
Theorem

2.3
). In this part of our work, there are much more computations compared to the aforementioned references because the parametrization of
B^{\lambda}
is clearly non-constant (for
\lambda\neq 0
), yielding many more terms in the expressions that we manipulate.

We now briefly describe the selection principle tailored to a proof by contradiction. In a nutshell, we suppose that there exists a sequence of sets
E_{k}\subset\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
with
|E_{k}|=|B^{\lambda}|
that contradicts the first quantitative inequality in
Theorem

1.1
. If these sets were small perturbations of
B^{\lambda}
, then the Fuglede inequality would give an immediate contradiction, as it provides exactly the desired inequality. To reduce to this case, we consider a sequence of auxiliary functionals
\mathcal{F}_{k}
and we take a sequence of minimizers
F_{k}\subset\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. If we design
\mathcal{F}_{k}
properly, then sequence
\{F_{k}\}_{k}
still contradicts
Theorem

1.1
, and they turn out to be regular enough thanks to the regularity theory available for almost minimizers of the (anisotropic weighted) perimeter. Moreover, the sets
F_{k}
will converge to
B^{\lambda}
in
C^{1}
-topology. Hence, we get to a contradiction by applying the Fuglede inequality to the sets
F_{k}
with
k\gg 1
.

We consider the auxiliary functionals

(1.5)

\mathcal{F}_{k}(F):=P_{\lambda}(F)+\Lambda||F|-|B^{\lambda}||-\frac{1}{k}\int_{\partial^{*}F\cap\{x_{n}>0\}}\left|\nu_{F}(x)-\frac{x+\lambda e_{n}}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{x}+|\widetilde{b}(F)|^{2},

where
\Lambda>0
is a constant, the integral term coincides with the expression in
Theorem

1.1
with
x^{\prime}=0
, and
\widetilde{b}(F)
is a bounded volume term that coincides with
|F|\barycenter_{H}(F)
whenever
F\subset B_{100}
. The functional
\mathcal{F}_{k}
is defined for any set
F
with finite measure, without any confinement restriction, and
\widetilde{b}(F)
represents a nonlinear notion of barycenter for
F
(see (
2.19
)). A few comments are in order:

•

it is easily checked that
F\mapsto P_{\lambda}(F)+\Lambda||F|-|B^{\lambda}||
is uniquely minimized by
B^{\lambda}
for
\Lambda>n
, and since
\widetilde{b}(B^{\lambda})=0
, then
\mathcal{F}_{k}
is a small perturbation of a functional that is minimized by our model
B^{\lambda}
, hinting that the minimizers of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
(if they exist) converge to
B^{\lambda}
in some sense as
k\to\infty
;

•

the structure of the functional is not completely new, as one can check by looking at all the other papers based on the selection principle. Once we minimize
\mathcal{F}_{k}
, we find sets
F_{k}
with
P_{\lambda}(F_{k})\sim P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})
, while the minus sign in front of the third term in
\mathcal{F}_{k}
favors sets for which the asymmetry is not too small. Notice that the integral term in (
1.5
) is not equal to the oscillation asymmetry considered in the final main inequality in
Theorem

1.1
, as the reference center here is fixed to be
x^{\prime}=0
;

•

adding the nonlinear barycenter term
\widetilde{b}(F)
is new, and we introduce it here also because the integral term in (
1.5
) is not translation invariant. In fact, if
\widetilde{b}(F)
were not present, then the functionals
\mathcal{F}_{k}
might not possess minimizers for some values of
\lambda
(this is immediate for
\lambda=0
using
Lemma

2.6
).

Comparing with
[
FJ14
,
Neu16
,
DeM24
,
BDF17
,
BDS15
]
, one immediately notices that their auxiliary functionals contain a term that
exactly
coincides with the oscillation asymmetry in their quantitative inequalities.
However, doing that same (natural) choice in our situation would lead to serious difficulties in applying the regularity theory for almost minimizers of the perimeter, that is the most restraining point in this approach. These issues are due to the different nature of our oscillation asymmetry compared to those present in
[
FJ14
,
Neu16
,
DeM24
,
BDF17
,
BDS15
]
. Indeed, while the oscillation asymmetries considered in those references can be rewritten as the their perimeter functional plus a volume term, in our case we have (see computations in
Lemma

2.6
)

\begin{split}&\inf_{x^{\prime}\in\{x_{n}=0\}}\int_{\partial^{*}F\cap\{x_{n}>0\}}\left|\nu_{F}(x)-\frac{x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{x}\\
&\qquad=\inf_{x^{\prime}\in\{x_{n}=0\}}\int_{\partial^{*}F\cap\{x_{n}>0\}}1-\left\langle\nu_{F},\frac{\lambda e_{n}}{|x-\Braket{x,e_{n}}e_{n}-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\right\rangle\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{x}-\int_{F}\frac{n-1}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\,{\rm d}x.\end{split}

The first term on the second line is a “perimeter term” (so a main term in the regularity theory) that is computed at some point
x^{\prime}=x^{\prime}(F)
. So, placing our oscillation asymmetry in place of the integral term in (
1.5
) would not lead to a higher order perturbation of the capillarity perimeter, but rather to a perturbation of a perimeter functional with a density that
depends itself
on the set
F
that we are considering.
For this reason we consider the auxiliary functionals
\mathcal{F}_{k}
containing a non-translation-invariant integral term. Even with this simplification, in
Proposition

2.11
we need to rely on the regularity theory developed in
[
DM15
]
for capillarity almost minimizers of weighted anistropic perimeters (see
Appendix

A
for the precise definitions and results).

The existence of minimizers for the auxiliary functionals
\mathcal{F}_{k}
(at least for
k\gg 1
) is fundamental to pursue this strategy and it is nontrivial in our case. Indeed, typically one is able to prove that it is sufficient to consider sets with uniformly bounded diameter. Hence auxiliary functionals in a selection-type argument are usually defined on sets that are a priori contained in some ball.
See, e.g.,
[
FJ14
, Lemma 3.2]
and
[
Neu16
, Lemma 3.1]
for this confinement method. However, this confinement procedure is once again based on the expansion of the oscillation asymmetry as the sum of perimeter plus a volume term, which is not available in our situation. Hence we are forced to minimize
\mathcal{F}_{k}
among all sets having finite measure, which means that existence of minimizers for
\mathcal{F}_{k}
is not immediate.
To overcome this difficulty, we use a robust and rather simple argument, that appears to be new in our context. The cornerstone of this approach is Ekeland’s variational principle (see
Theorem

A.7
), that ensures the existence of minimizers for some
volume
perturbations of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. Indeed, the space
\left\{E\subset\{x_{n}\geq 0\}:|E|<+\infty\right\}
endowed with the
L^{1}
distance is a complete metric space, and we prove that
\mathcal{F}_{k}
is lower semicontinuous for
k\gg 1
. Hence, for any
j>1
, Ekeland’s
Theorem

A.7
guarantees the existence of a set
E_{j}^{k}\subset\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
minimizing

F\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{k}(F)+\frac{1}{j}|F\Delta E_{j}^{k}|.

The use of the
L^{1}
distance is particularly effective since it amounts to adding a (very small) volume term, and this is harmless when we apply regularity theory. In fact, it is not difficult to prove that
E_{j}^{k}
are quasi-minimizers of the perimeter with uniform constants (that is weaker than being almost minimizers, see
Definition

A.1
). Then,
Theorem

A.2
provides density estimates, that automatically lead to a uniform confinement of
E_{j}^{k}
for
k\gg 1
thanks to
Theorem

2.3
and to a control on the barycenter. This is basically the starting point of
Proposition

2.11
. We mention that an application of Ekeland’s principle was also employed in
[
Boc+99
]
in a very different variational problem to improve the regularity of minimizing sequences.

We finally point out that adding the “barycentric penalization” in the auxiliary functionals (see (
1.5
)) in place of performing a confinement reduction eventually has the advantage of automatically producing also the barycentric version of the target quantitative inequality (see the discussion in
Section

2.5
).

To deal with the problem in convex cones, the strategy is basically the same as the one exploited in the capillarity setting. In fact, given a convex cone
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
does not contain lines, the Fuglede inequality was already known in a particular class of cones (see
[
BF17
, Lemma 3.1]
); after extending such an inequality to the class of convex cones as we did in the capillarity case, we proceed with the selection principle introducing the sequence of auxiliary functionals

\mathcal{G}_{k}(F)=P(F,\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right))+\Lambda\left||F|-|B_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}|\right|-\frac{1}{k}\int_{\partial^{*}F\cap\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right)}\left|\nu_{F}(x)-\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{x}+|\widetilde{b}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(F)|^{2},

where
\widetilde{b}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(F)
is again a nonlinear barycenter, i.e., a volume term coinciding with
|F|\barycenter_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(F)
whenever
F\subset B_{100}
. Now the situation is even simpler than before, since this setting is analogous to the capillarity problem with
\lambda=0
from many points of view.
The proof of the final
Theorem

3.9
is more explicit than that of the capillarity case (
Theorem

2.12
), in the sense that no compactness is used at that point and we can keep track rather explicitly of the dependence of the final constant in the quantitative inequality.

2.1.
Sets of finite perimeter

We refer to
[
AFP00
,
Mag12
]
for a complete account on the theory of sets of finite perimeter.
The perimeter of a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
in an open set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

P(E,A):=\sup\left\{\int_{E}{\rm div}\,T\ :\ T\in C_{c}^{1}(A;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,\,\|T\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

Denoting
P(E):=P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we say that
E
is a set of finite perimeter if
P(E)<+\infty
.
If
E
is a set of finite perimeter, the characteristic function
\chi_{E}
has a distributional gradient
D\chi_{E}
that is a vector-valued Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\int_{E}{\rm div}\,T=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}T\,{\rm d}D\chi_{E},

for any field
T\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
The set function
P(E,\cdot)
defined above can be extended as a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, the measure
P(E,\cdot)
coincides with the total variation
|D\chi_{E}|
of the distributional gradient, and it is concentrated on the reduced boundary

\partial^{*}E:=\left\{x\in{\rm spt}|D\chi_{E}|\ :\ \exists\,\nu^{E}(x):=-\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{D\chi_{E}(B_{r}(x))}{|D\chi_{E}(B_{r}(x))|}\text{ and }|\nu^{E}(x)|=1\right\}.

The vector
\nu^{E}
is called the generalized outer normal of
E
.
Moreover
P(E,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\partial^{*}E
, and the distributional gradient can be written as
D\chi_{E}=-\nu^{E}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\partial^{*}E
.

2.2.
Capillarity problem in the half-space and related asymmetry indexes

We begin with the basic definitions and properties about the quantities involved with the capillarity problem.

Remark 2.1

(Alternative expression for
P_{\lambda}
)
.

Let
E\subset H
be a measurable set. We observe that

P_{\lambda}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}1-\lambda\Braket{e_{n},\nu_{E}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
\nu_{E}
is the generalized outer normal to
E
. In particular, since
|\lambda|<1
, we have that
P_{\lambda}(E)\geq 0
. The previous identity follows from the divergence theorem, indeed

0=\int_{E}\divergence\,e_{n}\,{\rm d}x=-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H)+\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\Braket{e_{n},\nu_{E}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Definition 2.2

(Capillarity Fraenkel asymmetry and isoperimetric deficit)
.

Let
E\subset H
be a Borel set with measure
|E|=v\in(0,+\infty)
. Denoting
B^{\lambda}(v):=\frac{v^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}}B^{\lambda}
, and
B^{\lambda}(v,x):=B^{\lambda}(v)+x
, for any
x\in\{x_{n}=0\}
, we define the capillarity Fraenkel asymmetry by

\alpha_{\lambda}(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(v,x)|}{v}\ :\ x\in\partial H\right\}.

It is readily checked that the Fraenkel asymmetry of
E
is a minimum. We further define the capillarity isoperimetric deficit by

D_{\lambda}(E):=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(v))}{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(v))}.

We recall here the quantitative isoperimetric inequality for the Fraenkel asymmetry in capillarity problems.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
PP24
,
KS24
]
)
.

There exists
C_{\ref{thm:PPquantitativaFraenkelCapillare}}=C_{\ref{thm:PPquantitativaFraenkelCapillare}}(n,\lambda)>0
such that for any set of finite perimeter
E\subset H
with
|E|<+\infty
there holds

(2.1)

\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}(E)\leq C_{\ref{thm:PPquantitativaFraenkelCapillare}}D_{\lambda}(E).

Definition 2.4
.

Denote
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}:=\partial B_{1}\cap\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
.
We denote by
w_{\lambda}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
the function such that

\overline{\partial B^{\lambda}\cap H^{+}}=\{w_{\lambda}(x)\,x\ :\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\}.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 1
,
\beta\in(0,1)
.
Let
E\subset H
be a bounded open set. We say that the boundary of
E
is a
C^{k,\beta}
-graph over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
(or, more precisely, that
\overline{\partial E\cap H^{+}}
is a
C^{k,\beta}
-graph over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
) if
\partial E
is Lipschitz and if there exists a
C^{k,\beta}
-function
f:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\to(0,+\infty)
such that

\overline{\partial E\cap H^{+}}=\{f(x)\,x\ :\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\}.

If the boundaries of two sets
E,F
are
C^{k,\beta}
-graphs over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
with respect to some functions
f,g
, we define

{\rm d}_{C^{k,\beta}}(E,F):=\|f-g\|_{C^{k,\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}.

If
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}},E
have boundary given by
C^{k,\beta}
-graphs over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
, we say that
\overline{\partial E_{i}\cap H^{+}}
converges
\overline{\partial E\cap H^{+}}
in
C^{k,\beta}
(or, simply, that
E_{i}
converges to a

in
C^{k,\beta}
) if
{\rm d}_{C^{k,\beta}}(E_{i},E)\to 0
as
i\to\infty
.

Definition 2.5

(Capillarity oscillation asymmetry)
.

Let
E\subset H
be a Borel set with measure
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
. We define the capillarity oscillation asymmetry by

\mu_{\lambda}^{2}(E)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{E}(x)-\frac{x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\ :\ x^{\prime}\in\partial H\right\}.

For a generic Borel set
E\subset H
with finite perimeter and finite measure, setting
r_{E}:=(|E|/|B^{\lambda}|)^{\frac{1}{n}}
, we define

\mu_{\lambda}^{2}(E)\coloneqq\mu_{\lambda}^{2}\left(r_{E}^{-1}E\right)=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{2r_{E}^{n-1}}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{E}(x)-\frac{x-r_{E}(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-r_{E}(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\ :\ x^{\prime}\in\partial H\right\}.

For technical reasons, it will be convenient to also consider an oscillation asymmetry that is not translation or scaling invariant. For any Borel set
E\subset H
with finite measure and finite perimeter, we define

\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{E}(x)-\frac{x+\lambda e_{n}}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
E\subset H
be a set of finite perimeter with finite volume. Then

\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}1-\left\langle\nu_{E},\frac{\lambda e_{n}}{|x-\Braket{x,e_{n}}e_{n}+\lambda e_{n}|}\right\rangle\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{x}-\int_{E}\frac{n-1}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\,{\rm d}x.

Proof.

The claim directly follows form the divergence theorem since

\begin{split}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E)&=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}1-\left\langle\nu_{E},\frac{x+\lambda e_{n}}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right\rangle\\
&=P(E,H^{+})-\int_{E}{\rm div}\left(\frac{x+\lambda e_{n}}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right)+\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H}\left\langle-e_{n},\frac{x+\lambda e_{n}}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right\rangle\\
&=P(E,H^{+})-\int_{E}\frac{n-1}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}+\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H}\left\langle-e_{n},\frac{x+\lambda e_{n}}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right\rangle,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H}\left\langle-e_{n},\frac{x+\lambda e_{n}}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right\rangle&=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H}\left\langle-e_{n},\frac{\lambda e_{n}}{|x-\Braket{x,e_{n}}e_{n}+\lambda e_{n}|}\right\rangle\\
&=\int_{E}{\rm div}\left(\frac{\lambda e_{n}}{|x-\Braket{x,e_{n}}e_{n}+\lambda e_{n}|}\right)-\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\left\langle\nu_{E},\frac{\lambda e_{n}}{|x-\Braket{x,e_{n}}e_{n}+\lambda e_{n}|}\right\rangle,\end{split}

and
{\rm div}\left(\frac{\lambda e_{n}}{|x-\Braket{x,e_{n}}e_{n}+\lambda e_{n}|}\right)=0
.
∎

2.3.
Fuglede-type computations for the capillarity problem

The following three technical results, namely

and

Lemma 2.7
.

There exist
\varepsilon_{\ref{lem:OScillationBoundedByH1Norm}},C_{\ref{lem:OScillationBoundedByH1Norm}}>0
depending on

such that the following bounds hold.

Let
E\subset H
be an open set such that
\overline{\partial E\cap H^{+}}=\{(w_{\lambda}(x)+u(x))x\ :\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\}
for some
C^{1}

with
\|u\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}\leq\varepsilon_{\ref{lem:OScillationBoundedByH1Norm}}
. Then

\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E)\leq C_{\ref{lem:OScillationBoundedByH1Norm}}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}.

In particular, if also
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
, then

\mu_{\lambda}^{2}(E)\leq\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E)\leq C_{\ref{lem:OScillationBoundedByH1Norm}}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}.

Proof.

We take
\varepsilon_{\ref{lem:OScillationBoundedByH1Norm}}\in(0,1)
sufficiently small so that
|w_{\lambda}+u|\geq c=c(n,\lambda)>0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.
We will exploit identities from
Lemma

B.1
.
We can rewrite

(2.2)

\begin{split}&\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial E\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{E}(y)-\frac{y+\lambda e_{n}}{|y+\lambda e_{n}|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}\left|\nu_{E}((w_{\lambda}+u)x)-\frac{(w_{\lambda}+u)x+\lambda e_{n}}{|(w_{\lambda}+u)x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right|^{2}(w_{\lambda}+u)^{n-2}[(w_{\lambda}+u)^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}+\nabla u|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\\
&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}\left|\nu_{E}((w_{\lambda}+u)x)-\frac{(w_{\lambda}+u)x+\lambda e_{n}}{|(w_{\lambda}+u)x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).\end{split}

Moreover

(2.3)

\begin{split}\nu_{E}((w_{\lambda}+u)x)=\frac{(w_{\lambda}+u)x-\nabla w_{\lambda}-\nabla u}{[(w_{\lambda}+u)^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}+\nabla u|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}},\\
\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}x)=w_{\lambda}x+\lambda e_{n}=\frac{w_{\lambda}x-\nabla w_{\lambda}}{[w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}},\end{split}

for any
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap H^{+}
. Hence, for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap H^{+}
, we have

(2.4)

\begin{split}\nu_{E}((w_{\lambda}+u)x)&-\frac{(w_{\lambda}+u)x+\lambda e_{n}}{|(w_{\lambda}+u)x+\lambda e_{n}|}\\
&\qquad\qquad=\underbrace{\frac{(w_{\lambda}+u)x-\nabla w_{\lambda}-\nabla u}{[(w_{\lambda}+u)^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}+\nabla u|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}x)}_{A}+\underbrace{w_{\lambda}x+\lambda e_{n}-\frac{(w_{\lambda}+u)x+\lambda e_{n}}{|ux+\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}x)|}}_{B}.\end{split}

We study the summands in (
2.4
) separately. First, since
|w_{\lambda}+u|\geq c>0
, we have

(2.5)

\begin{split}|A&|=\left|\frac{w_{\lambda}x-\nabla w_{\lambda}}{[(w_{\lambda}+u)^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}+\nabla u|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}x)+\frac{ux-\nabla u}{[(w_{\lambda}+u)^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}+\nabla u|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right|\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:Normali}}{\leq}|w_{\lambda}x-\nabla w_{\lambda}|\left(\frac{1}{[(w_{\lambda}+u)^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}+\nabla u|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{1}{[w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)+O(|u|+|\nabla u|)\\
&=\frac{|w_{\lambda}x-\nabla w_{\lambda}|}{[(w_{\lambda}+u)^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}+\nabla u|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}[w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left([w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}-[(w_{\lambda}+u)^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}+\nabla u|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+O(|u|+|\nabla u|)\\
&=\frac{|w_{\lambda}x-\nabla w_{\lambda}|}{[(w_{\lambda}+u)^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}+\nabla u|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}[w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(-\frac{2uw_{\lambda}+u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}+2\nabla u\cdot\nabla w_{\lambda}}{2[w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}]^{1/2}}+O(|u|+|\nabla u|)\right)\\
&=O(|u|+|\nabla u|)\end{split}

where
O(\cdot)
may change from line to line.

Moreover

(2.6)

\begin{split}|B|&=\left|w_{\lambda}x+\lambda e_{n}-\frac{(w_{\lambda}+u)x+\lambda e_{n}}{[1+u^{2}+2u\Braket{\nu_{B^{\lambda}},x}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right|=\left|w_{\lambda}x+\lambda e_{n}-\frac{(w_{\lambda}+u)x+\lambda e_{n}}{1+u\Braket{\nu_{B^{\lambda}},x}+\tfrac{1}{2}u^{2}+O(|u|)}\right|\\
&=\left|w_{\lambda}x+\lambda e_{n}-(w_{\lambda}x+ux+\lambda e_{n})\left(1-u\Braket{\nu_{B^{\lambda}},x}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+O(|u|)\right)\right|=O(|u|).\end{split}

Inserting (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
) into (
2.2
) we find

\begin{split}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E)&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}\left|A+B\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}|A|^{2}+|B|^{2}\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}.\end{split}

∎

Proposition 2.8
.

There exist
c_{\ref{prop:EspansionePlambda}},C_{\ref{prop:EspansionePlambda}},\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:EspansionePlambda}}>0
depending on
n,\lambda
such that the following holds.
Let
E\subset H
be an open bounded set with measure
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
. Suppose that the boundary of
E
is a
C^{1}
-graph over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
, see
Definition

2.4
, with respect to a function
f=w_{\lambda}+u
, with
\|u\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}\leq\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:EspansionePlambda}}
. Then

P_{\lambda}(E)=P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})+B(u)+\varepsilon\,O\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}\right),

where
B(u)
satisfies

B(u)\geq c_{\ref{prop:EspansionePlambda}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{2}-C_{\ref{prop:EspansionePlambda}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}u^{2}.

Proof.

Recalling
Remark

2.1
, letting
\varphi(v):=|v|-\lambda\Braket{v,e_{n}}
for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there holds

P_{\lambda}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\varphi(\nu_{E})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

We compute

\nabla\varphi(v)=\frac{v}{|v|}-\lambda e_{n},\qquad\nabla^{2}\varphi(v)=\frac{1}{|v|}\left(\Id-\frac{v}{|v|}\otimes\frac{v}{|v|}\right).

By
Lemma

B.1
, the outer unit normals to
E
and
B^{\lambda}
are given by

(2.7)

\nu_{E}(f(x)\,x)=\frac{f(x)\,x-\nabla f(x)}{\sqrt{f(x)^{2}+|\nabla f(x)|^{2}}},\qquad\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}(x)\,x)=\frac{w_{\lambda}(x)\,x-\nabla w_{\lambda}(x)}{\sqrt{w_{\lambda}(x)^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}}},

for any
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\cap H^{+}
.

To simplify the computations, in this proof we will adopt Einstein’s sum convention (i.e., summation is understood over the repeated indexes). We will denote by
\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1}\}
an orthonormal basis of
T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
, for generic
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
, and we will denote by
\partial_{i}:=\partial_{\tau_{i}}
the tangential derivative along
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
in direction
\tau_{i}
.

We consider the basis
\{g_{i}^{B}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
for the tangent space
T_{w_{\lambda}(x)x}B^{\lambda}
given by

g_{i}^{B}=\partial_{i}(w_{\lambda}\,x)=w_{\lambda}\tau_{i}+\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}\,x,

for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.

Define the vectors

\omega^{B}(x)=w_{\lambda}^{n-1}x-w_{\lambda}^{n-2}\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}\tau_{i},\qquad\omega(x)=(w_{\lambda}+u)^{n-1}x-(w_{\lambda}+u)^{n-2}\partial_{i}(w_{\lambda}+u)\tau_{i},

for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
. Notice that
\Braket{\omega^{B},g_{j}^{B}}=w_{\lambda}^{n-2}\Braket{w_{\lambda}x-\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}\tau_{i},w_{\lambda}\tau_{j}+\partial_{j}w_{\lambda}x}=w_{\lambda}^{n-2}(w_{\lambda}\partial_{j}w_{\lambda}-w_{\lambda}\partial_{j}w_{\lambda})=0
for any
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
, hence we can write
\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}\,x)=\omega^{B}/|\omega^{B}|
.

By 1-homogeneity of
\varphi
and by
Lemma

B.1
we have

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})&=\int_{\partial B^{\lambda}\cap H^{+}}\varphi(\nu_{B^{\lambda}})=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}}\varphi\left(\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}(x)\,x)\right)w_{\lambda}^{n-2}\sqrt{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}}\varphi(w_{\lambda}\,x-\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}\,\tau_{i})w_{\lambda}^{n-2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}}\varphi(\omega^{B}),\end{split}

where we used that
\sqrt{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}(x)\,x)=w_{\lambda}\,x-\nabla w_{\lambda}=w_{\lambda}\,x-\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}\,\tau_{i}
.

Analogously one gets

(2.8)

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(E)&=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}}\varphi(\omega)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}}\varphi(\omega^{B})+\Braket{\nabla\varphi(\omega^{B}),\omega-\omega^{B}}+\frac{1}{2}\Braket{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\omega^{B})(\omega-\omega^{B}),(\omega-\omega^{B})}+O(|\omega-\omega^{B}|^{3})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

A direct computation gives

(2.9)

\begin{split}\omega&=\omega^{B}+w_{\lambda}^{n-2}\left[(n-1)ux-\left((n-2)w_{\lambda}^{-1}\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}u+\partial_{i}u\right)\tau_{i}\right]\\
&\quad+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}w_{\lambda}^{n-3}u^{2}x-\left(\frac{(n-2)(n-3)}{2}w_{\lambda}^{n-4}\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}u^{2}+(n-2)w_{\lambda}^{n-3}u\partial_{i}u\right)\tau_{i}\\
&\quad+\varepsilon O\left(u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right).\end{split}

It is straightforward to see that
\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}\,x)=w_{\lambda}\,x+\lambda e_{n}
for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.
Moreover we observed above that
\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}\,x)=\omega^{B}/|\omega^{B}|
.
Hence

\begin{split}\nabla\varphi(\omega^{B})&=\frac{\omega^{B}}{|\omega^{B}|}-\lambda e_{n}=\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}\,x)-\lambda e_{n}=w_{\lambda}\,x,\\
\nabla^{2}\varphi(\omega^{B})&=\frac{1}{|\omega^{B}|}\left(\Id-\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}\,x)\otimes\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}\,x)\right),\end{split}

for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.
So the gradient term in the expansion for
P_{\lambda}(E)
in (
2.8
) becomes

(2.10)

\Braket{\nabla\varphi(\omega^{B}),\omega-\omega^{B}}=\Braket{w_{\lambda}\,x,\omega-\omega^{B}}\overset{\eqref{eq:ExpansionOmegaOmegaB}}{=}(n-1)w_{\lambda}^{n-1}u+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}w_{\lambda}^{n-2}u^{2}+\varepsilon O\left(u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right).

To expand the Hessian term in (
2.8
) it is sufficient to consider the first order expansion of
\omega-\omega^{B}
, and we single out a multiple of
\nu_{B^{\lambda}}
since it belongs to the kernel of
\nabla^{2}\varphi(\omega^{B})
. We have that

\begin{split}\omega-\omega^{B}&=w_{\lambda}^{n-2}\left[(n-1)ux-((n-2)w_{\lambda}^{-1}\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}u+\partial_{i}u)\tau_{i}\right]+O(u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2})\\
&=w_{\lambda}^{n-2}\left[(n-1)ux-\nabla u-(n-2)u\left(\frac{\nabla w_{\lambda}}{w_{\lambda}}-x\right)-(n-2)ux\right]+O(u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2})\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:normal-to-graph}}{=}w_{\lambda}^{n-2}\left[ux-\nabla u+(n-2)u\left(1+\frac{|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\nu_{B^{\lambda}}\right]+O(u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}),\end{split}

Exploiting (
2.7
) once more, we compute the value of
\nabla^{2}\varphi(\nu_{B^{\lambda}})=\Id-\nu_{B^{\lambda}}\otimes\nu_{B^{\lambda}}
on the orthonormal basis
\{x,\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\begin{split}\Braket{(\Id-\nu_{B^{\lambda}}\otimes\nu_{B^{\lambda}})x,x}&=1-\frac{w_{\lambda}^{2}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}=\frac{|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}},\\
\Braket{(\Id-\nu_{B^{\lambda}}\otimes\nu_{B^{\lambda}})\tau_{i},\tau_{j}}&=\delta_{ij}-\frac{\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}\partial_{j}w_{\lambda}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}},\\
\Braket{(\Id-\nu_{B^{\lambda}}\otimes\nu_{B^{\lambda}})x,\tau_{i}}&=\frac{w_{\lambda}\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}.\end{split}

Since
\nu_{B^{\lambda}}\in\ker\nabla^{2}\varphi(\omega^{B})
, we deduce that

(2.11)

\begin{split}&\Braket{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\omega^{B})(\omega-\omega^{B}),\omega-\omega^{B}}=\frac{w_{\lambda}^{2n-4}}{|\omega^{B}|}\Braket{(\Id-\nu_{B^{\lambda}}\otimes\nu_{B^{\lambda}})(ux-\nabla u),ux-\nabla u}+\varepsilon O\left(u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)\\
&\quad=\frac{w_{\lambda}^{2n-4}}{|\omega^{B}|}\left[\frac{|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}u^{2}-2\frac{w_{\lambda}\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}u\partial_{i}u+|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{\partial_{i}w_{\lambda}\partial_{j}w_{\lambda}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}u\right]+\varepsilon O\left(u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right).\end{split}

Plugging (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) into (
2.8
), we find

(2.12)

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})&=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}(n-1)w_{\lambda}^{n-1}u+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}w_{\lambda}^{n-2}u^{2}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\frac{w_{\lambda}^{2n-4}}{|\omega^{B}|}\,\,\left[\frac{|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}u^{2}-2\frac{\Braket{w_{\lambda}\nabla w_{\lambda},u\nabla u}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}+|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{\Braket{\nabla w_{\lambda},\nabla u}^{2}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad+\varepsilon O\left(u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right).\end{split}

We can turn the first order term in the previous expansion into a second order term by exploiting the volume constraint. Indeed, by
Lemma

B.1
and by assumptions we get

(2.13)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}\frac{1}{n}w_{\lambda}^{n}=|B^{\lambda}|=|E|=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}\frac{1}{n}(w_{\lambda}+u)^{n}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}\frac{1}{n}w_{\lambda}^{n}+w_{\lambda}^{n-1}u+\frac{n-1}{2}w_{\lambda}^{n-2}u^{2}+O(u^{3}).

Moreover, since
\min w_{\lambda}>0
, then there is
c=c(n,\lambda)\in(0,1)
such that
|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}/(w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2})\leq c
, and thus

(2.14)

|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{\Braket{\nabla w_{\lambda},\nabla u}^{2}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}\geq(1-c^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}.

On the other hand, by applying the standard inequality
ab\leq\frac{\delta a^{2}}{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{2\delta}
with
a=|\nabla w_{\lambda}|\,|\nabla u|
and
b=w_{\lambda}\,|u|
and
\delta=\delta(n,\lambda)>0
small enough, we see that

(2.15)

\left|2\frac{\Braket{w_{\lambda}\nabla w_{\lambda},u\nabla u}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{2}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}\left(\frac{\delta}{2}|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{w_{\lambda}^{2}u^{2}}{2\delta}\right)\leq\frac{1-c^{2}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+Cu^{2},

where
C>0
depends only on
n,\lambda
. Applying (
2.13
), (
2.14
), (
2.15
) in (
2.12
), the statement follows with

\begin{split}B(u)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}\frac{1-n}{2}w_{\lambda}^{n-2}u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{w_{\lambda}^{2n-4}}{|\omega^{B}|}\,\,\Bigg\{|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{\Braket{\nabla w_{\lambda},\nabla u}^{2}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}&-2\frac{\Braket{w_{\lambda}\nabla w_{\lambda},u\nabla u}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}\\
&\quad+\frac{|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}{w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2}}u^{2}\Bigg\}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

∎

Proposition 2.9

(Fuglede-type estimate for capillarity problems)
.

There exist
C_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}},\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}>0
depending on
n,\lambda
such that the following holds.
Let
E\subset H
be an open bounded set with measure
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
. Suppose that the boundary of
E
is a
C^{1}
-graph over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
, see
Definition

2.4
, with respect to a function
f=w_{\lambda}+u
, with
\|u\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}\leq\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}
. Then, denoting
\barycenter_{H}E:=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}(x-\Braket{x,e_{n}}e_{n})\,{\rm d}x
, there holds

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}\leq C_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}(D_{\lambda}(E)+|\barycenter_{H}E|^{2}).

Proof.

The proof is inspired by
[
Neu16
, Proposition 1.9]
. To simplify the notation, let
b=\barycenter_{H}E\in\partial H
. We first claim that there exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
n,\lambda
, such that if
\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}
is small enough then

(2.16)

\|u\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}^{2}\leq C(D_{\lambda}(E)+|b|^{2}).

To prove this, we start by showing that
|E\Delta B^{\lambda}|\leq C(D_{\lambda}(E)^{1/2}+|b|)
. The quantitative inequality from
Theorem

2.3
guarantees that there exists a point
z\in\partial H
such that
|E\Delta(z+B^{\lambda})|\leq CD_{\lambda}(E)^{1/2}
. Applying the triangle inequality and
[
Mag12
, Lemma 17.9]
we obtain the estimate

(2.17)

|E\Delta B^{\lambda}|\leq CD_{\lambda}(E)^{1/2}+|B^{\lambda}\Delta(z+B^{\lambda})|\leq CD_{\lambda}(E)^{1/2}+2|z|P(B^{\lambda}),

where
C
depends on
n,\lambda
only and may change from line to line.
By definition of
\barycenter_{H}E
, writing
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, since
\int_{B^{\lambda}}x^{\prime}\,{\rm d}x=0
, we get that

\begin{split}z-b&=\frac{1}{|B^{\lambda}|}\int_{B^{\lambda}}(z-b)\,{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{|B^{\lambda}|}\int_{B^{\lambda}}(x^{\prime}+z-b)\,{\rm d}x-\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}(x^{\prime}-b)\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{|B^{\lambda}|}\int_{B^{\lambda}+z-b}x^{\prime}\,{\rm d}x-\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E-b}x^{\prime}\,{\rm d}x.\end{split}

We observe that
|z|\leq 2\diam E
, and since that set is bounded by a unversal constant (as soon as
\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}<1
), then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
n
and
\lambda
such that, for any
x\in B^{\lambda}+z-b
and any
x\in E
, we have that
|x|\leq C
. Hence, using again
Theorem

2.3
, we estimate

|z|\leq\frac{1}{|B^{\lambda}|}\int_{(B^{\lambda}+z-b)\Delta(E-b)}|x^{\prime}|\,{\rm d}x+|b|\leq C|E\Delta(z+B^{\lambda})|+|b|\leq CD_{\lambda}(E)^{1/2}+|b|.

Plugging the latter estimate in (
2.17
) we obtain that
|E\Delta B^{\lambda}|\leq C(D_{\lambda}(E)^{1/2}+|b|)
. In order to obtain (
2.16
), we may bound
\|u\|_{L^{1}}
from above in terms of
|E\Delta B^{\lambda}|
. We perform an expansion as in (
B.1
) to get

\begin{split}|E\setminus B^{\lambda}|&=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}\int_{w_{\lambda}}^{w_{\lambda}+u_{+}}r^{n-1}\,{\rm d}r\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\{u>0\}}\frac{1}{n}(w_{\lambda}+u)^{n}-\frac{w_{\lambda}^{n}}{n}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\int_{\{u>0\}}w_{\lambda}^{n-1}u+O(u^{2})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\geq C\int_{\{u>0\}}u\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

where the last estimate follows by choosing
\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}
small enough, since
\inf w_{\lambda}>0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.
An analogous computation shows
|B^{\lambda}\setminus E|\geq C\int_{\{u<0\}}(-u)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
. Hence
|E\Delta B^{\lambda}|\geq C\|u\|_{L^{1}}
. Combining this with the upper bound for
|E\Delta B^{\lambda}|
obtained before we readily get (
2.16
).

Taking now
\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}<\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:EspansionePlambda}}
and applying
Proposition

2.8
we obtain that

\begin{split}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1})}^{2}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1})}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1})}^{2}\leq C\left[D_{\lambda}(E)+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1})}^{2}\right]+\varepsilon O\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}\right).\end{split}

Let
\delta>0
to be chosen. Applying
Lemma

B.2
with
\Sigma=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
we get

\begin{split}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1})}^{2}&\leq C\left[D_{\lambda}(E)+\frac{C_{\ref{lemma:NashIneq}}}{\delta^{\alpha_{n}}}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1})}^{2}\right]+(\varepsilon+\delta)O\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}\right)\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:L1-IsoperimetricDeficit}}{\leq}C\left(1+\frac{C_{\ref{lemma:NashIneq}}}{\delta^{\alpha_{n}}}\right)(D_{\lambda}(E)+|b|^{2})+(\varepsilon+\delta)O\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}\right).\end{split}

To conclude the proof, it is sufficient to take
\delta=\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}
small enough to absorb the last term on the right hand side in the last inequality.
∎

2.4.
Proof of the strong quantitative isoperimetric inequality for capillarity problems

We recall the following basic lemma, which readily follows by the isoperimetric inequality (
1.4
) and by testing the functional with any rescaling of the isoperimetric bubble.

Lemma 2.10
.

For any
\bar{\Lambda}>n
, there holds

\inf\left\{P_{\lambda}(E)+\bar{\Lambda}\left||E|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|\ :\ E\subset H,\,\,|E|<+\infty\right\}=P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}),

and the infimum is only achieved by translations
B^{\lambda}+y
of the standard bubble
B^{\lambda}
, for
y\in\partial H
.

We now get prepared for our formulation of the selection principle. From now on we let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function

(2.18)

\psi(t):=\max\{-100,\min\{t,100\}\}.

We define

(2.19)

\widetilde{b}(F):=\int_{F}\left(\psi(x_{1}),\ldots,\psi(x_{n-1})\right)\,{\rm d}x,

for any set
F\subset H
with
|F|<+\infty
.
Observe that if
F\subset B_{100}(0)\cap H
then

\widetilde{b}(F)=|F|\,{\rm bar}_{H}F.

Moreover

(2.20)

\begin{split}\left||\widetilde{b}(F)|^{2}-|\widetilde{b}(G)|^{2}\right|&=\left||\widetilde{b}(F)|+|\widetilde{b}(G)|\right|\,\left||\widetilde{b}(F)|-|\widetilde{b}(G)|\right|\\
&\leq C(|F|+|G|)\left|\int_{F\Delta G}\left(\chi_{F}-\chi_{G}\right)(\psi(x_{1}),\ldots,\psi(x_{n-1}))\right|\\
&\leq C(|F|+|G|)|F\Delta G|,\end{split}

for any
F,G\subset H
with
|F|,|G|<+\infty
, for a universal constant
C>0
.

We recall that in
Definition

2.5
we defined

\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial^{*}F\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{F}(x)-\frac{x+\lambda e_{n}}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

for any set of finite perimeter
F\subset H
with
|F|<+\infty
.

We can fix
k_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{N}
the least integer such that

(2.21)

k_{\lambda}\geq\max\{10,4/(1-|\lambda|)\}.

Hence for any set of finite perimeter
F\subset H
with
|F|<+\infty
and for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq k_{\lambda}
, there holds

(2.22)

P_{\lambda}(F)-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F)\geq(1-|\lambda|)P(F,H^{+})-\frac{2}{k}P(F,H^{+})\geq\frac{1-|\lambda|}{2}P(F,H^{+}).

Hence it is natural to define

(2.23)

P_{\lambda}(F)-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F):=+\infty,

for any set
F\subset H
with
|F|<+\infty
and
P(F)=+\infty
, for any
k\geq k_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 2.11
.

There exist
\Lambda_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}>2n
,
k_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}\geq k_{\lambda},R_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}\geq 10
,
depending on
n,\lambda
such that the following holds. For any
k\geq k_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, adopting convention (
2.23
), the minimization problem

(2.24)

\inf\left\{\mathcal{F}_{k}(F):=P_{\lambda}(F)+\Lambda_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}\left||F|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F)+|\widetilde{b}(F)|^{2}\ :\ F\subset H,\,\,|F|<+\infty\right\}

has an open minimizer
F_{k}
such that

•

F_{k}\subset B_{R_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}}(0)
,

•

|F_{k}|=|B^{\lambda}|
for any
k\geq k_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}
sufficiently large,

•

\overline{\partial F_{k}\cap H^{+}}
is a
C^{1,1/2}
-graph over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
for any
k\geq k_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}
sufficiently large, and
\overline{\partial F_{k}\cap H^{+}}\to\overline{\partial B^{\lambda}\cap H^{+}}
in
C^{1,\beta}
, for any
\beta\in(0,1/2)
, as
k\to\infty
, in the sense of
Definition

2.4
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda>2n
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq k_{\lambda}
. We will fix
\Lambda=\Lambda_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}
, depending on
n,\lambda
only, at the end of the proof.
If
B^{\lambda}
is a minimizer for (
2.24
) for some
k\geq k_{\lambda}

&\qquad+s_{k}^{2n}(|\widetilde{b}(s_{k}F_{k})|+|\widetilde{b}(F_{k})|)(|\widetilde{b}(s_{k}F_{k})|-|\widetilde{b}(F_{k})|)\\
&\leq|s_{k}^{n-1}-1|P_{\lambda}(F_{k})+\frac{1}{k}\left(\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F_{k})-\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(s_{k}F_{k})\right)+|s_{k}^{2n}-1|\,|\widetilde{b}(F_{k})|^{2}\\
&\qquad+10s_{k}^{2n}(|\widetilde{b}(s_{k}F_{k})|+|\widetilde{b}(F_{k})|)|s_{k}-1|\,|F_{k}|\end{split}

We already know that
F_{k}\to B^{\lambda}
in
L^{1}(H)
, and thus
s_{k}\to 1
. Moreover, there exists a geometric constant
C>0
such that

(2.38)

\left|v-\frac{w+z}{|w+z|}\right|^{2}\geq\left|v-\frac{w}{|w|}\right|^{2}-C|z|,

for any
v,w,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|v|=1
,
|w|\geq 1/2
and
|z|\leq 1/4
.
Recalling that
\partial F_{k}\cap H^{+}\subset B_{2}(-\lambda e_{n})\setminus B_{1/2}(-\lambda e_{n})
for
k
large, we can apply (
2.38
) with
v=\nu_{F_{k}}(x)
,
w=x+\lambda e_{n}
and
z=(s_{k}-1)x
, for
x\in\partial F_{k}\cap H^{+}
.
In particular, as also
P_{\lambda}(F_{k}),|\widetilde{b}(F_{k})|,\mu_{\lambda,0}(F_{k}),P(F_{k})\leq C(n,\lambda)
for any
k
large, we obtain

\begin{split}\Lambda&\left||F_{k}|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|\leq C(|s_{k}^{n-1}-1|+|s_{k}^{2n}-1|+|s_{k}-1|)+\\
&+\frac{1}{2k}\left(\int_{\partial^{*}F_{k}\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{F_{k}}(x)-\frac{x+\lambda e_{n}}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-s_{k}^{n-1}\int_{\partial^{*}F_{k}\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{F_{k}}(x)-\frac{s_{k}x+\lambda e_{n}}{|s_{k}x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:GeometricEstimate}}{\leq}C(|s_{k}^{n-1}-1|+|s_{k}^{2n}-1|+|s_{k}-1|)+\frac{1}{2k}|s_{k}^{n-1}-1|\int_{\partial^{*}F_{k}\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{F_{k}}(x)-\frac{x+\lambda e_{n}}{|x+\lambda e_{n}|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\qquad+\frac{s_{k}^{n-1}}{2k}\int_{\partial^{*}F_{k}\cap H^{+}}C|s_{k}-1||x|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq C|s_{k}-1|,\end{split}

where we used that the power functions
t\to|t^{n-1}-1|
and
t\to|t^{2n}-1|
are locally Lipschitz. On the other hand

\left||F_{k}|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|=|F_{k}||s_{k}^{n}-1|=|F_{k}||s_{k}-1|(1+s_{k}+\ldots+s_{k}^{n-1})\overset{\eqref{eq:MassMinimizingSeqCloseToBlambda}}{\geq}\frac{1}{2}|B^{\lambda}||s_{k}-1|.

Therefore, if by contradiction
s_{k}\neq 1
for some
k
large, the last two estimates imply
\Lambda\leq C=C(n,\lambda)
, which is impossible for a choice of
\Lambda
sufficiently large depending on
n,\lambda
only.
∎

Theorem 2.12

(Strong quantitative inequality in capillarity problems)
.

There exists
C_{\ref{thm:capillarityStrongQuantitative}}>0
depending on
n,\lambda
such that for any set of finite perimeter
E\subset H
with
|E|<+\infty
there holds

\mu_{\lambda}^{2}(E)\leq C_{\ref{thm:capillarityStrongQuantitative}}D_{\lambda}(E).

Proof.

By scale invariance of the oscillation asymmetry and of the deficit, we can prove the claim for sets having volume equal to
|B^{\lambda}|
. Suppose by contradiction that there exists a sequence of sets of finite perimeter
E_{k}\subset H
such that
|E_{k}|=|B^{\lambda}|
but
k^{2}D_{\lambda}(E_{k})<\mu_{\lambda}^{2}(E_{k})
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 1
. In particular,
E_{k}
does not coincide with the translation of a truncated ball
B^{\lambda}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and thus
D_{\lambda}(E_{k})>0
. Without loss of generality, up to translation along
\partial H
we can assume that
\widetilde{b}(E_{k})=0
. Since
\mu_{\lambda}^{2}(E_{k})\leq 2P(E_{k};H^{+})\leq\frac{2}{1-|\lambda|}P_{\lambda}(E_{k})
, it follows that
E_{k}
satisfies
(k^{2}-C)P_{\lambda}(E_{k})\leq k^{2}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})
for some constant
C
depending on
n
and
\lambda
, so in particular
D_{\lambda}(E_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Moreover, by definition of
\mu_{\lambda}^{2}(E_{k})
and by the contradiction assumption, we have

(2.39)

0<D_{\lambda}(E_{k})<\frac{1}{k^{2}}\mu_{\lambda}^{2}(E_{k})\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k}).

We apply
Proposition

2.11
. There exist constants
\Lambda_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}>2n
,
k_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}\geq k_{\lambda},R_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}\geq 10
,
depending on
n,\lambda
such that, for any
k\geq k_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}
, the minimization problem

\inf\left\{\mathcal{F}_{k}(F):=P_{\lambda}(F)+\Lambda_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}\left||F|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F)+|\widetilde{b}(F)|^{2}\ :\ F\subset H,\,\,|F|<+\infty\right\}

has an open minimizer
F_{k}
such that

•

F_{k}\subset B_{R_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}}(0)
,

•

|F_{k}|=|B^{\lambda}|
for any
k\geq k_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}
sufficiently large,

•

for any
k\geq k_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}
sufficiently large, there exists
u_{k}\in C^{1,1/2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})
such that
\overline{\partial F_{k}\cap H}=\{(w_{\lambda}(x)+u_{k}(x))x:x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\}
, where
w_{\lambda}
was defined in
Definition

2.4
, and
u_{k}\to 0
in
C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})
for any
\beta\in(0,1/2)
.

Testing minimality of
F_{k}
with the competitor
E_{k}
, which satisfies
\widetilde{b}(E_{k})=0
and
|E_{k}|=|B^{\lambda}|
, and exploiting
Lemma

2.10
, we get the chain of estimates

(2.40)

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(F_{k})-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F_{k})+|\widetilde{b}(F_{k})|^{2}&=P_{\lambda}(F_{k})+\Lambda_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}\left||F_{k}|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F_{k})+|\widetilde{b}(F_{k})|^{2}\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(E_{k})-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k})\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:zzMuZeroEk}}{\leq}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})+\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}{k^{2}}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k})-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k})\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(F_{k})+\Lambda_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}\left||F_{k}|-|B^{\lambda}|\right|+\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}{k^{2}}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k})-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k})\\
&=P_{\lambda}(F_{k})+\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}{k^{2}}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k})-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k})\end{split}

for any
k\geq k_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}
sufficiently large. Observe that

(2.41)

\frac{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}{k^{2}}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k})-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k})\leq-\frac{1}{2k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k}),

for any
k
large enough. Hence (
2.40
) implies that for every
k
large enough

(2.42)

0<\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{k})\leq 2\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F_{k}),\qquad\qquad|\widetilde{b}(F_{k})|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F_{k}).

Since we know that
|F_{k}|=|B^{\lambda}|
for
k\geq k_{\ref{prop:MinimizationProblemSelection}}
large enough, using (
2.41
) we may rearrange the terms in the first and third lines of (
2.40
) to get

D_{\lambda}(F_{k})\leq\frac{\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F_{k})}{k\cdot P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}.

For
k
large enough, we have
\|u_{k}\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}<\min\{\varepsilon_{\ref{lem:OScillationBoundedByH1Norm}},\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}\}
, thus we can apply
Lemma

2.7
and
Proposition

2.9
to continue the previous estimate as

(2.43)

\begin{split}D_{\lambda}(F_{k})&\leq\frac{\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F_{k})}{kP_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}\leq\frac{1}{kP_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}C_{\ref{lem:OScillationBoundedByH1Norm}}\|u_{k}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})}\leq\frac{1}{kP_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda})}C_{\ref{lem:OScillationBoundedByH1Norm}}C_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}\left(D_{\lambda}(F_{k})+|\barycenter_{H}F_{k}|^{2}\right).\end{split}

Since
|F_{k}|=|B^{\lambda}|
and
\barycenter_{H}F_{k}=|B^{\lambda}|^{-1}\widetilde{b}(F_{k})
by (
2.19
), then (
2.42
) implies
|\barycenter_{H}F_{k}|^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{k|B^{\lambda}|^{2}}\mu^{2}_{\lambda,0}(F_{k})
. Hence, using
Lemma

2.7
and
Proposition

2.9
again as above, we find

|\barycenter_{H}F_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F_{k})}{k|B^{\lambda}|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{k|B^{\lambda}|^{2}}C_{\ref{lem:OScillationBoundedByH1Norm}}C_{\ref{prop:FugledeCapillarity}}\left(D_{\lambda}(F_{k})+|\barycenter_{H}F_{k}|^{2}\right).

Hence, for
k
large enough, there holds
|\barycenter_{H}F_{k}|^{2}\leq D_{\lambda}(F_{k})
. Plugging this estimate in (
2.43
) we deduce

D_{\lambda}(F_{k})\leq\frac{1}{2}D_{\lambda}(F_{k}),

for any
k
large enough. Hence
D_{\lambda}(F_{k})=0
for
k
large, which, recalling that
|F_{k}|=|B^{\lambda}|
and that
|\barycenter_{H}F_{k}|^{2}\leq D_{\lambda}(F_{k})
, implies that
F_{k}
exactly coincides with the standard bubble
B^{\lambda}
with zero barycenter. In particular,
\mu^{2}_{\lambda,0}(F_{k})=0
for large
k
. This contradicts (
2.42
), which guarantees that
\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(F_{k})>0
for every
k
large enough.
∎

2.5.
Strong quantitative inequalities with barycentric asymmetry in capillarity problems

As pointed out in the introduction, the proof of
Theorem

2.12
provides, in fact, the following more explicit and stronger inequality.

Corollary 2.13

(Barycentric strong quantitative inequality in capillarity problems 1)
.

There exists a constant
C_{\ref{cor:tilde-bar-quantitative}}>0
depending only on
n
and
\lambda
such that, for every
E\subset H
of finite perimeter with
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
, and for any point
x^{*}\in\partial H
such that
\widetilde{b}(E-x^{*})=0
there holds

\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{E}(x)-\frac{x-(x^{*},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{*},-\lambda)|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{x}\leq C_{\ref{cor:tilde-bar-quantitative}}D_{\lambda}(E).

Corollary

2.13
follows by arguing by contradiction exactly as in the proof of
Theorem

2.12
. Indeed, one starts by assuming by contradiction that there is a sequence of sets
U_{k}\subset H
with
|U_{k}|=|B^{\lambda}|
, such that
k^{2}D_{\lambda}(U_{k})<\int_{\partial^{*}U_{k}\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{U_{k}}(x)-\frac{x-(x^{*}_{k},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{*}_{k},-\lambda)|}\right|^{2}
for some
x^{*}_{k}
such that
\widetilde{b}(U_{k}-x^{*}_{k})=0
. Taking the translated sets
E_{k}:=U_{k}-x^{*}_{k}
, then
k^{2}D_{\lambda}(E_{k})=k^{2}D_{\lambda}(U_{k})<\mu^{2}_{\lambda,0}(E_{k})
, and
E_{k}
is the starting contradicting sequence in the proof of
Theorem

2.12
.

However,
x^{*}
in
Corollary

2.13
might differ from
\frac{1}{|E|}\widetilde{b}(E)
since the function
\psi
in the definition of
\widetilde{b}
is not linear.

Notice that we can take
x^{*}=\barycenter_{H}E
in
Corollary

2.13
for every set
E
with
\diam_{H}E\leq 100
thanks to (
2.19
), where
\diam_{H}E
is the diameter of the orthogonal projection of
E
onto
\partial H
. More generally, given
d>\diam_{H}B^{\lambda}
an upper bound of the diameter of the considered sets, taking the function
\psi_{d}\coloneqq d\psi(\cdot/d)
in place of
\psi
, and running the same proof presented above, one obtains the following barycentric quantitative inequality for the oscillation asymmetry.

Corollary 2.14

(Barycentric strong quantitative inequality in capillarity problems 2)
.

Let
d>\diam_{H}B^{\lambda}
be given. Then there exists a constant
C_{\ref{cor:bar-quantitative}}>0
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
and
d
such that, for every
E\subset H
of finite perimeter with
|E|=|B^{\lambda}|
and
\diam_{H}E\leq d

(2.44)

\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap H^{+}}\left|\nu_{E}(x)-\frac{x-(\barycenter_{H}E,-\lambda)}{|x-(\barycenter_{H}E,-\lambda)|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{x}\leq C_{\ref{cor:bar-quantitative}}D_{\lambda}(E).

Our approach does not provide an explicit control of the constant

in terms of the diameter of the sets. However, we remark that the dependence of the constant

on the diameter is necessary in dimension

. Indeed, as analogously pointed out in
[
GP25
]
, one can take a sequence of sets
E_{j}=B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|-\varepsilon_{j},x_{j})\cup B^{\lambda}(\varepsilon_{j},y_{j})
, with
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
and suitable mutually diverging points

so that
\barycenter_{H}E_{j}=0
,

, but the left hand side of (

) with

tends to a positive constant as

.

Additionally, we remark that our method can be applied to obtain the quantitative isoperimetric inequality with barycentric Fraenkel asymmetry as follows.

Corollary 2.15

(Barycentric quantitative inequality in capillarity problems)
.

There exists a constant
C_{\ref{cor:barycentric-Fraenkel}}>0
depending only on
n
and
\lambda
such that, for every
E\subset H
of finite perimeter with

, and for any point

such that

, we have the following inequality:

|E\Delta(B^{\lambda}+x^{*})|^{2}\leq C_{\ref{cor:barycentric-Fraenkel}}D_{\lambda}(E).

If
\diam_{H}E\leq d<+\infty
, there exists a constant
C_{\ref{cor:barycentric-Fraenkel}}^{\prime}>0
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
and

such that

|E\Delta(B^{\lambda}+\barycenter_{H}E)|^{2}\leq C_{\ref{cor:barycentric-Fraenkel}}^{\prime}D_{\lambda}(E).

Proof.

We provide some details about the necessary adaptations to obtain the first inequality since here we use a different notion of asymmetry.
For a set
E\subset H
with
\overline{\partial E\cap H^{+}}=\{(w_{\lambda}(x)+u(x))x:x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\}
, and
\|u\|_{C^{1}}\ll 1
, as in the proof of
Proposition

2.9
one estimates

. This replaces

2.7
. Then, we can run the selection principle in

2.11
and

2.12

2.6.
Sharpness of
Theorem

2.12
.

We prove that exponents in the inequality in
Theorem

2.12
are sharp.
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function such that
\varphi\not\equiv 0
,

(2.45)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}w_{\lambda}^{n-1}\varphi=0,

and
\varphi
is “rotationally symmetric”, in the sense that we impose
\varphi(x)=f(\Braket{x,e_{n}})
for some
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,1)
.
Consider the one-parameter family of sets
E_{t}:=(|B^{\lambda}|/|\widetilde{E}_{t}|)^{\frac{1}{n}}\widetilde{E}_{t}
where
\overline{\partial\widetilde{E}_{t}\cap H^{+}}:=\{(w_{\lambda}+t\varphi)x\ :\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\}
. We also write
\overline{\partial E_{t}\cap H^{+}}=:\{(w_{\lambda}+h_{t})x\ :\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\}
for a smooth one-parameter family of smooth functions
h_{t}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
. By construction, we have
(|B^{\lambda}|/|\widetilde{E}_{t}|)^{\frac{1}{n}}(w_{\lambda}+t\varphi)x=(w_{\lambda}+h_{t})x
. Differentiating the previous relation with respect to
t
and evaluating at
t=0
, using (
2.45
), one sees that
\partial_{t}h_{t}\big|_{t=0}=\varphi
. Hence
h_{t}
has the expansion

h_{t}=t\,\varphi+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\Phi+O(t^{3})\quad\text{as $t\to 0$,}

for some smooth function
\Phi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Summing up, we constructed a one-parameter family of sets
E_{t}
, for
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0
small, such that

a)

|E_{t}|=|B^{\lambda}|
for any
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
,

b)

E_{t}
is rotationally symmetric with respect to the
x_{n}
-axis for any
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
,

c)

E_{t}\neq B^{\lambda}
for any for any
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\setminus\{0\}
, and
E_{0}=B^{\lambda}
,

d)

\overline{\partial E_{t}\cap H^{+}}=\{(w_{\lambda}+h_{t})x\ :\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\}
.

We want to compute the expansion of the product
\Braket{\nu_{E_{t}},\nu_{B^{\lambda}}}
as
t\to 0
. Clearly
\Braket{\nu_{E_{t}}|{}_{t=0},\nu_{B^{\lambda}}}=1
and
0=\partial_{t}|\nu_{E_{t}}|^{2}|_{t=0}=2\Braket{\partial_{t}\nu_{E_{t}}|{}_{t=0},\nu_{B^{\lambda}}}=0
. Moreover, a direct computation using the formula for the unit normal in
Lemma

B.1
shows that

(2.46)

\begin{split}\Braket{\partial_{t}^{2}\nu_{E_{t}}\Big.|{}_{t=0},\nu_{B^{\lambda}}}&=\frac{1}{(w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2})^{2}}\left((\varphi w_{\lambda}+\Braket{\nabla\varphi,\nabla w_{\lambda}})^{2}-(\varphi^{2}+|\nabla\varphi|^{2})(w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2})\right).\end{split}

Therefore, denoting by
C
a positive constant independent of
t
that may change from line to line, for
t
sufficiently small the first two lines in (
2.2
) imply

(2.47)

\begin{split}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{t})&\geq C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}1-\Braket{\nu_{E_{t}}((w_{\lambda}+h_{t})x),\nu_{B^{\lambda}}(w_{\lambda}x)}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:Sharp1}}{\geq}t^{2}\,C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}\left((\varphi^{2}+|\nabla\varphi|^{2})(w_{\lambda}^{2}+|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{2})-(\varphi w_{\lambda}+\Braket{\nabla\varphi,\nabla w_{\lambda}})^{2}\right)\\
&\geq C\,t^{2},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used that
\varphi\not\equiv 0
and we used Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, observing that
\varphi
is not a multiple of
w_{\lambda}
because of (
2.45
).

On the other hand, going back to the proof of
Proposition

2.8
, the expansion in (
2.12
) implies

(2.48)

\begin{split}D_{\lambda}(E_{t})&\leq C\left(t^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}(n-1)w_{\lambda}^{n-1}h_{t}\right)\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:ConditionSharpnessVolume}}{\leq}C\left(t^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}(n-1)w_{\lambda}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\Phi+O(t^{3})\right)\right)\leq C\,t^{2},\end{split}

for any
t
sufficiently small.

Finally we claim that, being
E_{t}
rotationally symmetric, implies that

(2.49)

\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{t})\leq 20\mu_{\lambda}^{2}(E_{t}).

Indeed, fix
t
and suppose
x^{\prime}\in\partial H\neq\{0\}
is such that
\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap H^{+}}\Big|\nu_{E_{t}}-\tfrac{x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\Big|^{2}\leq 2\mu^{2}_{\lambda}(E_{t})
.
By symmetry, the same holds with
x^{\prime}
replaced by
-x^{\prime}
.
We shall now employ the geometric inequality

(2.50)

\bigg|\frac{x-(0,-\lambda)}{|x-(0,-\lambda)|}-\frac{x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\bigg|^{2}\leq\bigg|\frac{x-(-x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(-x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}-\frac{x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\bigg|^{2},

for any
x\in H^{+}
, that will be justified below. Hence we conclude that

(2.51)

\begin{split}\mu_{\lambda,0}^{2}(E_{t})&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap H^{+}}\bigg|\nu_{E_{t}}-\frac{x-(0,-\lambda)}{|x-(0,-\lambda)|}\bigg|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap H^{+}}\bigg|\nu_{E_{t}}-\frac{x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\bigg|^{2}+\bigg|\frac{x-(0,-\lambda)}{|x-(0,-\lambda)|}-\frac{x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\bigg|^{2}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:ElementarySharpness}}{\leq}\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap H^{+}}\bigg|\nu_{E_{t}}-\frac{x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\bigg|^{2}+\bigg|\frac{x-(-x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(-x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}-\frac{x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\bigg|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap H^{+}}3\bigg|\nu_{E_{t}}-\frac{x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\bigg|^{2}+2\bigg|\nu_{E_{t}}-\frac{x-(-x^{\prime},-\lambda)}{|x-(-x^{\prime},-\lambda)|}\bigg|^{2}\\
&\leq 20\mu^{2}_{\lambda}(E_{t}),\end{split}

proving (
2.49
). Putting together (
2.47
), (
2.48
) and (
2.49
) we conclude that
D_{\lambda}(E_{t})\leq C\mu^{2}_{\lambda}(E_{t})
for any
t
small enough, proving the claimed sharpness.

It remains to justify (
2.50
). Expanding the squares in (
2.50
) and changing variables into
z:=x-(x^{\prime},-\lambda)
, we see that (
2.50
) is equivalent to

(2.52)

\bigg\langle\frac{z+(x^{\prime},0)}{|z+(x^{\prime},0)|},z\bigg\rangle\geq\bigg\langle\frac{z+2(x^{\prime},0)}{|z+2(x^{\prime},0)|},z\bigg\rangle,

for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is sufficient to suppose that
z,(x^{\prime},0)
are linearly independent. Hence, considering the
2
-dimensional subspace generated by
z,(x^{\prime},0)
, in order to prove (
2.52
) it is equivalent to prove that if

are independent, then

(2.53)

\bigg\langle\frac{v+w}{|v+w|},v\bigg\rangle\geq\bigg\langle\frac{v+2w}{|v+2w|},v\bigg\rangle.

Indeed,
\big\langle\tfrac{v+w}{|v+w|},v\big\rangle=|v|\cos\vartheta_{1}
where
\vartheta_{1}\in(0,\pi)
is the angle between
v
and
\tfrac{v+w}{|v+w|}
, and the same can be said for the second scalar product for a corresponding angle
\vartheta_{2}\in(0,\pi)
. Clearly
\vartheta_{1}\leq\vartheta_{2}
, hence
\cos\vartheta_{1}\geq\cos\vartheta_{2}
, and (
2.53
) follows.

We aim to obtain a sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequality in convex cones in the strong form, similarly to what we did in the previous section. We first collect the notions of asymmetry and deficit that we are going to use.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex cone with non-empty interior
\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
. Write
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
where
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
does not contain lines. Let
K_{\mathcal{C}}:=B_{1}(0)\cap\mathcal{C}
and let
E\subset\mathcal{C}
be a measurable set with
|E|\in(0,+\infty)
. For
s>0
such that
|E|=|sK_{\mathcal{C}}|
, we define the Fraenkel asymmetry

\alpha_{\mathcal{C}}(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{|E\Delta((x^{\prime},0)+sK_{\mathcal{C}})|}{|E|}\ :\ x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\right\}

and the isoperimetric deficit

D_{\mathcal{C}}(E):=\frac{P_{\mathcal{C}}(E)-P_{\mathcal{C}}(sK_{\mathcal{C}})}{P_{\mathcal{C}}(sK_{\mathcal{C}})}=\frac{P_{\mathcal{C}}(E)}{n|K_{\mathcal{C}}|^{\frac{1}{n}}|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}-1,

where
P_{\mathcal{C}}(E):=P(E,\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right))=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\setminus\partial\mathcal{C})
denotes the relative perimeter in
\mathcal{C}
.

The strong isoperimetric inequality in convex cones will bound from above the following the natural notion of oscillation asymmetry

\mu_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{2s_{E}^{n-1}}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right)}\left|\nu_{E}(x)-\frac{x-(x^{\prime},0)}{|x-(x^{\prime},0)|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\ :\ x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\right\},

where
\nu_{E}
as usual denotes the generalized outer unit normal to
E
, and
s_{E}=(|E|/|K_{\mathcal{C}}|)^{1/n}
.
Similarly to what we did in
Section

2.2
, for technical reasons it will be convenient to introduce an asymmetry index that is not translation, nor it is scaling invariant:

\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right)}\left|\nu_{E}(x)-\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed cone with non-empty interior. Then, for any measurable set
E\subset\mathcal{C}
with finite volume there holds

\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E)=P_{\mathcal{C}}(E)-\int_{E}\frac{n-1}{|x|}\,{\rm d}x.

Proof.

The proof is a simplified version of
Lemma

2.6
. It is sufficient to apply the divergence theorem to the vector field
X=\frac{x}{|x|}
, observing that
\Braket{X,\nu_{E}}=0
on
\partial^{*}E\cap\partial^{*}\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Theorem 3.9

(Strong quantitative inequality in cones)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\subset\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
be a piecewise
C^{2}
closed convex cone, where
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
does not contain lines. Suppose
|B_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}|\geq v_{0}
, for some
v_{0}>0
. Then there exists
C_{\ref{thm:ConesStrongQuantitative}}>0
depending on
n,v_{0},C_{\ref{thm:QuantitativeConesFraenkel}}(\mathcal{C}),\delta_{\ref{thm:almost-regularity-cones}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}}^{\prime},r^{\prime}_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}},\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeConi}})
such that the following holds.

Let

be a set of finite perimeter with
|E|\in(0,+\infty)
. Then

\mu_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(E)\leq C_{\ref{thm:ConesStrongQuantitative}}D_{\mathcal{C}}(E).

Proof.

We claim it is possible to take

C_{\ref{thm:ConesStrongQuantitative}}:=\left(1+\max\left\{k_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}},\bar{k},\frac{2C_{\ref{prop:FugledeConi}}}{v_{0}^{2}},\frac{4C_{\ref{prop:FugledeConi}}}{nv_{0}}\right\}\right)^{2}\geq\left(1+\max\left\{k_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}},\bar{k},\frac{2C_{\ref{prop:FugledeConi}}}{|K_{\mathcal{C}}|^{2}},\frac{4C_{\ref{prop:FugledeConi}}}{P_{\mathcal{C}}(K_{\mathcal{C}})}\right\}\right)^{2},

where
\bar{k}=\bar{k}(n)>0
is the least integer such that
\tfrac{n\omega_{n}}{k^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{k}\leq-\tfrac{1}{2k}
for any
k\geq\bar{k}
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be the least integer such that

k\geq\max\left\{k_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}},\bar{k},\frac{2C_{\ref{prop:FugledeConi}}}{v_{0}^{2}},\frac{4C_{\ref{prop:FugledeConi}}}{n\omega_{n}v_{0}}\right\}.

We stress that
k
will be a fixed parameter in this proof. Since the inequality is scaling invariant, it is sufficient to prove the claim for sets
E\subset\mathcal{C}
such that
|E|=|K_{\mathcal{C}}|
.
Let us suppose by contradiction that there exists a set
E\subset\mathcal{C}
with
|E|=|K_{\mathcal{C}}|
and
k^{2}D_{\mathcal{C}}(E)<C_{\ref{thm:ConesStrongQuantitative}}D_{\mathcal{C}}(E)<\mu_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(E)
.
In particular,
E\neq x^{\prime}+K_{\mathcal{C}}
for any
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Without loss of generality, we can suppose that
\tilde{b}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(E)=0
.
Similarly to the proof of
Theorem

2.12
, we have that

(3.12)

D_{\mathcal{C}}(E)<\frac{1}{k^{2}}\mu_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(E)\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E).

We now apply
Proposition

3.8
. We stress that in this proof we do not need the whole sequence of sets generated by
Proposition

3.8
, since
k
is fixed in this proof. Since
k\geq k_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}}
, by
Proposition

3.8
there exist constants
\Lambda_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}}>2n
,
R_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}}\geq 10
such that the auxiliary minimization problem

\inf\left\{\mathcal{G}_{k}(F):=P_{\mathcal{C}}(F)+\Lambda_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}}\left||F|-|K_{\mathcal{C}}|\right|-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(F)+|\widetilde{b}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(F)|^{2}\ :\ F\subset\mathcal{C},\,\,|F|<+\infty\right\}

admits an open minimizer
F\subset B_{R_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}}}(0)\cap\mathcal{C}
. Additionally
|F|=|K_{\mathcal{C}}|
and there exists
u\in C^{1}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}})
parametrizing
\overline{\partial F\cap\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right)}
, with
\|u\|_{C^{1}}<\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeConi}}
. Testing the minimality of
F
with
E
, which satisfies
\widetilde{b}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(E)=0
and
|E|=|K_{\mathcal{C}}|
, and using
Lemma

3.7
, we get

(3.13)

\begin{split}P_{\mathcal{C}}(F)-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(F)+|\widetilde{b}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(F)|^{2}&=P_{\mathcal{C}}(F)+\Lambda_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}}\left||F|-|K_{\mathcal{C}}|\right|-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(F)+|\widetilde{b}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(F)|^{2}\\
&\leq P_{\mathcal{C}}(E)-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E)\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:DMuZero-cone}}{\leq}P_{\mathcal{C}}(K_{\mathcal{C}})+\frac{P_{\mathcal{C}}(K_{\mathcal{C}})}{k^{2}}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E)-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E)\\
&\leq P_{\mathcal{C}}(F)+\Lambda_{\ref{prop:selection-cones}}\left||F|-|K_{\mathcal{C}}|\right|+\frac{P_{\mathcal{C}}(K_{\mathcal{C}})}{k^{2}}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E)-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E)\\
&=P_{\mathcal{C}}(F)+\frac{P_{\mathcal{C}}(K_{\mathcal{C}})}{k^{2}}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E)-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E),\end{split}

Since
k\geq\bar{k}(n)
, then

\frac{P_{\mathcal{C}}(K_{\mathcal{C}})}{k^{2}}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E)-\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E)\leq-\frac{1}{2k}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E).

Hence, from (

) we deduce that

(3.14)

0<\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(E)\leq 2\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(F),\qquad|\widetilde{b}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(F)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{k}\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(F).

Since
|F|=|K_{\mathcal{C}}|
, using the first and third lines in (

Since
\|u\|_{C^{1}}<\varepsilon_{\ref{prop:FugledeConi}}
, we can apply
Proposition

3.6
to continue this estimate as

(3.15)

D_{\mathcal{C}}(F)\leq\frac{\mu_{\mathcal{C},0}^{2}(F)}{k\cdot P_{\mathcal{C}}(K_{\mathcal{C}})}\leq\frac{1}{k\cdot P_{\mathcal{C}}(K_{\mathcal{C}})}C_{\ref{prop:FugledeConi}}\left(D_{\mathcal{C}}(F)+|\barycenter_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}F|^{2}\right).

Moreover, since
|F|=|K_{\mathcal{C}}|
and
F\subset B_{10}(0)
, hence
\barycenter_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}F=|K_{\mathcal{C}}|^{-1}\widetilde{b}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(F)
. Therefore, using (
3.14
) and

3.6
again we get that

we see that

. Plugging this inequality in (

. Hence,

. Recalling that
|F|=|K_{\mathcal{C}}|
and

, then

, contradicting the first estimate in (

).
∎

Appendix A
Cornerstones in Calculus of Variations

A.1.
Regularity theory

In this work, we exploit results in regularity theory for sets satisfying two possible weak notions of perimeter minimality. The first and weaker one is the notion of
(K,r_{0})
-quasi-minimaly.

Definition A.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex cone with non-empty interior
{\rm int}(\mathcal{C})
. Let
K\geq 1,r_{0}>0
. A set
E\subset\mathcal{C}
of locally finite perimeter is a
(K,r_{0})
-quasi-minimizer in
\mathcal{C}
if

P(E,B_{r}(x)\cap{\rm int}(\mathcal{C}))\leq KP(G,B_{r}(x)\cap{\rm int}(\mathcal{C})),

for any set
G\subset\mathcal{C}
such that
G\Delta E\Subset B_{r}(x)
for some
r\leq r_{0}
and some
x\in\mathcal{C}
.

It is well-known that
(K,r_{0})
-quasi-minimizers satisfy density estimates.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex cone with non-empty interior, and let
E\subset\mathcal{C}
be a
(K,r_{0})
-quasi-minimizer in
\mathcal{C}
. Denote
v_{0}:=\inf_{x\in\mathcal{C}}|\mathcal{C}\cap B_{1}(x)|
.

Then
E
has an open representative. Moreover, there exist
\varrho_{\ref{thm:quasi-regularity}}>0
,
D_{\ref{thm:quasi-regularity}}\in(0,1)
depending on
n,K,r_{0},v_{0}
such that, identifying
E
with its open representative, there holds

|\mathcal{C}\cap E\cap B_{\varrho}(x)|\geq D_{\ref{thm:quasi-regularity}}\varrho^{n}\qquad\forall\,\varrho\leq\varrho_{\ref{thm:quasi-regularity}},\,x\in\overline{E}

and

|\mathcal{C}\cap B_{\varrho}(x)\setminus E|\geq D_{\ref{thm:quasi-regularity}}\varrho^{n}\qquad\forall\,\varrho\leq\varrho_{\ref{thm:quasi-regularity}},\,x\in\partial E.

A proof of
Theorem

A.2
holding in the higher generality of PI spaces can be found in
[
Kin+13
, Theorem 4.2]
. Observe that in
[
Kin+13
]
, the perimeter functional automatically coincides with the relative perimeter in
{\rm int}(\mathcal{C})
, hence the definition of quasiminimal set in
[
Kin+13
, Definition 3.1]
coincides with our
Definition

A.1
. Alternatively, a proof of
Theorem

A.2
follows by adapting the proof of
[
Mag12
, Theorem 21.11]
working with
(K,r_{0})
-quasi-minimal sets.

The second and stronger notion of generalized perimeter minimizer is that of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-almost minimizer, that we introduce here in the context of weighted anisotropic perimeter functionals. We first recall the definition of elliptic integrands as given in
[
DM15
, Definition 1.1]
.

Definition A.3
.

Given an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say that
\Phi:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]
is an elliptic integrand on
\Omega
if it is lower semicontinuous,
\Phi(x,\cdot)
is convex, and
\Phi(x,t\nu)=t\Phi(x,\nu)
for every
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t\geq 0
.

If
\Phi
is an elliptic integrand and
E\subset\Omega
is a set of locally finite perimeter in
\Omega
, then we define the associated perimeter

P_{\Phi}(E,A)\coloneqq\int_{A\cap\partial^{*}E}\Phi(x,\nu_{E}(x))\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x),

for any open set
A\subset\Omega
.
Given
\kappa_{1}\geq 1
and
\kappa_{2}\geq 0
, we say that
\Phi
is a regular elliptic integrand on
\Omega
with ellipticity constant
k_{1}
and Lipschitz constant
k_{2}
, denoted by

\Phi\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega,k_{1},k_{2}),

if
\Phi(x,\cdot)\in C^{2,1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
for every
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, and for every
x,y\in\overline{\Omega}
and
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
there holds

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\leq\Phi(x,\nu_{1})\leq\kappa_{1},\\
&|\Phi(x,\nu_{1})-\Phi(y,\nu_{1})|+|\nabla\Phi(x,\nu_{1})-\nabla\Phi(y,\nu_{1})|\leq\kappa_{2}|x-y|,\\
&|\nabla\Phi(x,\nu_{1})|+\|\nabla^{2}\Phi(x,\nu_{1})\|+\frac{\|\nabla^{2}\Phi(x,\nu_{1})-\nabla^{2}\Phi(x,\nu_{2})\|}{|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}|}\leq\kappa_{1},\\
&\Braket{\nabla^{2}\Phi(x,\nu_{1})\nu_{2},\nu_{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}|\nu_{2}-(\nu_{2}\cdot\nu_{1})\nu_{1}|^{2},\end{split}

where
\nabla\Phi
,
\nabla^{2}\Phi
here denote gradient and Hessian with respect to the
\nu
-variable.

We can thus define a
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-almost minimizer for a perimeter
P_{\Phi}
as above.

Definition A.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. Let
\Phi:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]
be an elliptic integrand on
\Omega
.
Let
\Lambda,r_{0}>0
. We say that a set
E\subset\Omega
of locally finite perimeter is a
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-almost minimizer of
P_{\Phi}
in
\Omega
if

P_{\Phi}(E,B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)\leq P_{\Phi}(G,B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)+\Lambda|E\Delta G|

whenever
E\Delta G\Subset B_{r}(x)\cap\overline{\Omega}
for some
r\leq r_{0}
and
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.

If
\Phi(x,\nu)=|\nu|
, i.e.,
P_{\Phi}
coincides with the usual (relative) perimeter in
\Omega
, we will just say that
E
is a
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-almost minimizer of the perimeter in
\Omega
.

It is well-understood that given a sequence of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-almost minimizers of the perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if such a sequence converges in
L^{1}
to a smooth set, then the convergence can be improved to
C^{1,\beta}
convergence of the topological boundaries (see
[
Mag12
]
and references therein). The theory has been generalized to
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-almost minimizers for perimeters defined by elliptic integrands in a half-space in
[
DM15
]
. In particular, combining Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.6 in
[
DM15
]
, one gets the following theorem, which yields convergence in
C^{1,\beta}
up to the boundary for sequences of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-almost minimizers corresponding to a sequence of elliptic integrands with uniform ellipticity bounds.

Theorem A.5
.

Let
\Phi_{k}:H\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
be a sequence of elliptic integrands such that there exist
l_{1}\geq 1,l_{2}>0
such that
\Phi_{k}\in\mathcal{E}(H^{+},l_{1},l_{2})
for any
k
. Suppose also that
\Phi_{k}
converges to an elliptic integrand
\Phi
in
C^{3}_{\rm loc}(H\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
as
k\to\infty
.

Let
\Lambda,r_{0}>0
. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
E_{k}\subset H
be a
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-almost minimizer of
P_{\Phi_{k}}
in
H^{+}
, and assume that
E_{k}\to B^{\lambda}
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(H)
.

Then
\overline{\partial E_{k}\cap H^{+}}
is a hypersurface with boundary of class
C^{1,1/2}
for
k
large enough, and
\overline{\partial E_{k}\cap H^{+}}\to\overline{\partial B^{\lambda}\cap H^{+}}
in
C^{1,\beta}
for any
\beta\in(0,1/2)
, in the sense of
Definition

2.4
.

In the second part of the work, we also need to apply an analogous
\varepsilon
-regularity theorem for
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-almost minimizers of the perimeter in convex cones. Assuming that the boundary of a convex cone is piecewise
C^{2}
, one can apply the theory developed in
[
EL22
]
to derive the following result (see also
[
GJ86
]
for earlier
\varepsilon
-regularity results in the free boundary context).

Theorem A.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex cone with non-empty interior, and suppose that
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\mathcal{C}
has piecewise
C^{2}
boundary. Let
\Lambda\geq 0
,
r_{0},\varepsilon>0
. Then there exist
\delta_{\ref{thm:almost-regularity-cones}}=\delta_{\ref{thm:almost-regularity-cones}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda,r_{0},\varepsilon)\in(0,1)
and
\beta_{\ref{thm:almost-regularity-cones}}=\beta_{\ref{thm:almost-regularity-cones}}(\mathcal{C})\in(0,1/2)
such that the following holds.

Let
E\subset\mathcal{C}
be a
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-almost minimizer of the perimeter in

\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
such that
|E\Delta(B_{1}\cap\mathcal{C})|\leq\delta_{\ref{thm:almost-regularity-cones}}
. Then there exists
u:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\mathcal{C}\to(-1,1)
such that

\overline{\partial E\cap\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right)}=\{(1+u(x))x:x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\mathcal{C}\},

with
\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta_{\ref{thm:almost-regularity-cones}}}}\leq\varepsilon
.

We just give a sketch of the proof, commenting on the main observations leading to the application of the main regularity result from
[
EL22
]
.

Proof.

By compactness and by a covering argument, it is sufficient to prove the claimed
C^{1,\beta}
bound just in some neighborhood of a point
p_{0}\in\partial\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with
|p_{0}|=1
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{0}:=\mathcal{C}-p_{0}
.

By the density estimates in
Theorem

A.2
, it is standard to prove that the Hausdorff distances
d_{\mathcal{H}}(E,B_{1}\cap\mathcal{C})
,
d_{\mathcal{H}}(\overline{\partial E\cap\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right)},\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\mathcal{C})
are bounded from above by a dimensional power of
|E\Delta(B_{1}\cap\mathcal{C})|
, up to multiplicative constant depending on
\Lambda,r_{0},n,|B_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}|
.

By assumption, there exists
r_{1}>0
such that for any
\varrho\in(0,r_{1}]
the set
B_{2}(0)\cap\partial\left(\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathcal{C}_{0}\right)
is the boundary of a curved polyhedral cone domain modeled on some polyhedral cone domain
\Omega^{(0)}
(independent of
\varrho
) and belonging to the class
\mathcal{D}_{\frac{1}{2}\delta({\Omega^{(0)}})}(\Omega^{(0)})
, where
\delta({\Omega^{(0)}})
is given by
[
EL22
, Theorem 3.1]
and the terminology is as in
[
EL22
, Definition 2.1, Definition 2.5]
.

Moreover, notice that
M:=\partial^{*}E\cap\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
defines an integral varifold in
B_{2}
with free boundary in
\iinterior\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
, such that furthermore
\|H_{M}^{\rm tan}\|_{\infty}\leq\Lambda
, where notation and terminology is as in
[
EL22
, Section 2.4]
. In fact, such a claim is well-known and it follows, e.g., by an adaptation of
[
PP24
, Lemma A.2]
.
Hence, there is
r_{2}
, depending on
\Lambda
, such that for any
\varrho\in(0,r_{2}]

M_{\varrho}:=\tfrac{1}{\varrho}(M-p_{0})

\|H_{M_{\varrho}}^{\rm tan}\|_{\infty}\leq\varrho\Lambda\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\delta({\Omega^{(0)}})

Taking into account all the previous observations and since

is smooth (with piecewise

boundary), it follows that for any
\eta

\delta_{\ref{thm:almost-regularity-cones}}=\delta_{\ref{thm:almost-regularity-cones}}(\mathcal{C},\Lambda,r_{0},\eta)
and

satisfies

which yields assumption (3.1) in
[
EL22

On the other hand, it follows by an easy contradiction argument that, if

also satisfies assumption (3.2) in

A.2.
Ekeland’s variational principle

In the work, we also exploit the following version of the Ekeland’s variational principle.

Theorem A.7

(Ekeland’s variational principle
[
Zăl02
, Corollary 1.4.2]
)
.

Let
(\mathbb{X},{\rm d})
be a complete metric space and let
f:\mathbb{X}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a lower semicontinuous function bounded from below. Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{X}
be such that
f(x_{0})\leq\varepsilon+\inf_{\mathbb{X}}f
. Then, for any
s>0
there exists
v\in\mathbb{X}
such that

f(v)\leq f(x_{0}),\qquad f(v)\leq f(x)+\frac{\varepsilon}{s}{\rm d}(x,v)\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{X}.

Appendix B
Technical toolbox

In this appendix, we collect some elementary technical identities and estimates.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}\to(0,+\infty)
be a
C^{1}
function, and let
F:\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the embedding
F(x)=f(x)x
.
Denote
E:=\{x\in H\ :\ |x|<f(x)\}
.

Then the tangential Jacobian of
F
is given by

J^{T}F=f^{n-1}\sqrt{1+\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f^{2}}}.

In particular

\int_{\partial E\cap H^{+}}g\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}g(f(x)x)\,f^{n-1}\sqrt{1+\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f^{2}}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x),

for any continuous function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The outer unit normal
\nu_{E}
of
E
is given by

\nu_{E}(f(x)x)=\frac{f(x)x-\nabla f(x)}{(f^{2}(x)+|\nabla f(x)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}},

where
\nabla f
is the tangential gradient of
f
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
, for any
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.

Moreover

\int_{E}g=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{f(x)}g(rx)\,r^{n-1}\,{\rm d}r\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x),

for any continuous function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular

|E|=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}}\frac{f^{n}}{n}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

If also
f=w_{\lambda}+u
, with
u:\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
such that
f>0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
, then

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(E)&=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}}(1-\lambda\Braket{\nu_{E},e_{n}})f^{n-1}\sqrt{1+\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f^{2}}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}}(1-\lambda\Braket{\nu_{E},e_{n}})(w_{\lambda}+u)^{n-1}\sqrt{1+\frac{|\nabla w_{\lambda}+\nabla u|^{2}}{|w_{\lambda}+u|^{2}}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

and

(B.1)

\begin{split}|E|&=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}}\left[\frac{w_{\lambda}^{n}}{n}+w_{\lambda}^{n-1}u+\frac{n-1}{2}w_{\lambda}^{n-2}u^{2}+O(u^{3})\right]\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x),\end{split}

as
u\to 0
.

Proof.

We just need to prove formulas for the tangential Jacobian of
F
and for the unit normal
\nu_{E}
.
Denote
\Sigma=F(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})=\overline{\partial E\cap H^{+}}
.
The differential
{\rm d}F_{x}:T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\to T_{F(x)}\Sigma
of
F
is given by

{\rm d}F_{x}(v)=f(x)v+\Braket{\nabla f(x),v}x=\left(f(x)\iota+x\otimes\nabla f(x)\right)v,

where
\iota:T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the natural injection. Hence

\begin{split}J^{T}F&=|\det(({\rm d}F_{x})^{*}\circ{\rm d}F_{x})|^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left|\det\left((f(x)\iota^{*}+\nabla f(x)\otimes x)(f(x)\iota+x\otimes\nabla f(x))\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

The adjoint
\iota^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
is the projection onto
T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
, indeed

\Braket{\iota^{*}w,v}=\Braket{w,\iota v}=\Braket{w,v}=\begin{cases}\Braket{w,v}&w\in T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+},\\
0&w\in(T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+})^{\perp},\end{cases}

for any
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
v\in T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
. Therefore

\begin{split}J^{T}F&=\left|\det\left(f^{2}(x)\iota^{*}\iota+f(x)\,\iota^{*}\,x\otimes\nabla f(x)+f(x)\nabla f(x)\otimes x\,\iota+\Braket{x,x}\nabla f(x)\otimes\nabla f(x)\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=\left|\det\left(f^{2}(x)\,\Id_{T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}}+\nabla f(x)\otimes\nabla f(x)\right)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=\left|(f^{2}(x)+|\nabla f(x)|^{2})(f^{2}(x))^{n-2})\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

We now prove the formula for
\nu_{E}
. It is sufficient to prove that
f(x)x-\nabla f(x)
is orthogonal to
T_{F(x)}\Sigma
for any
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
. Indeed, for any
v\in T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
we have

\begin{split}\Braket{{\rm d}F_{x}(v),f(x)x-\nabla f(x)}&=\Braket{f(x)v+\Braket{\nabla f(x),v}x,f(x)x-\nabla f(x)}=0.\end{split}

Also,
\nu_{E}
points outwards because
\Braket{\nu_{E}(F(x)),x}>0
since
f(x)>0
for any
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
.
∎

Lemma B.2

(Nash-type inequality)
.

Let
\sigma_{0}>0
and
n\geq 2
. Then there exists
C_{\ref{lemma:NashIneq}}(n,\sigma_{0})>0
and
\alpha_{n}>1
such that the following holds.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
be a closed geodesically convex set with
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma)\geq\sigma_{0}
. Then for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and any
u\in{\rm Lip}(\Sigma)
there holds

\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}\leq\delta\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{C_{\ref{lemma:NashIneq}}}{\delta^{\alpha_{n}}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}|u|\right)^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is well-known to the experts. For the convenience of the reader, we present a classical argument outlined by
[
Hum01
]
.
Fix
k_{0}>2
, define

k:=\begin{cases}k_{0}&\text{ if }n=2,3,\\
n&\text{ if }n\geq 4.\end{cases}

It is well-known that a convex subset such as
\Sigma
enjoys a Sobolev inequality of the following form. There exists
C_{\rm Sob}=C_{\rm Sob}(n,\sigma_{0})>0
such that

(B.2)

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2k}{k-2}}(\Sigma)}^{2}\leq C_{\rm Sob}(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}),

for any
u\in{\rm Lip}(\Sigma)
. Indeed, if
n=2
then
\Sigma
is isometric to an interval having length in the interval
[\sigma_{0},\pi]
.
Instead, if
n\geq 3
, if
\partial\Sigma
is smooth, then
\Sigma
is a smooth manifold with boundary, Ricci curvature bounded below by
(n-2)
, and volume bounded below by
\sigma_{0}
. Then (
B.2
) follows from classical Sobolev embeddings keeping track of the dependence of constants (see, e.g., proofs in
[
Heb99
,
Cha06
]
).
More generally, if
\partial\Sigma
is not a smooth submanifold, it is well-known than
\Sigma
is an Alexandrov space with positive curvature
[
AKP08
]
, also having diameter and volume bounded both from above and from below away from zero in terms of
\sigma_{0}
. Also, the volume measure on
\Sigma
is doubling and Ahlfors regular, i.e., volumes of relative balls in
\Sigma
are comparable to the
(n-1)
-th power of the radius, with constants depending on
n,\sigma_{0}
only. Hence
\Sigma
supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality in the sense of
[
HK00
, Eq. (5)]
with

p=\begin{cases}k_{0}^{\prime}&\text{ if }n=3,\\
2&\text{ if }n\geq 4,\end{cases}

where
\tfrac{1}{k_{0}^{\prime}}=1-\tfrac{1}{k_{0}}
, and with a Poincaré constant depending only on the dimension
[
Raj12
]
. Hence (
B.2
) follows from
[
HK00
, Theorem 5.1, Eq. (22)]
applied with
s=n
and
r=\pi
.

Applying the Hölder inequality with exponents
p=\tfrac{k}{4}+\tfrac{1}{2}>1
and
p^{\prime}=1+\tfrac{4}{k-2}
, we estimate

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}\right)^{1+\frac{2}{k}}&=\left(\int_{\Sigma}|u|^{\frac{1}{p}}|u|^{2-\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{1+\frac{2}{k}}\leq\left(\int_{\Sigma}|u|\right)^{\frac{4}{k}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}|u|^{\frac{2k}{k-2}}\right)^{\frac{k-2}{k}}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:SobolevSuConvessi}}{\leq}C_{\rm Sob}\left(\int_{\Sigma}|u|\right)^{\frac{4}{k}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}+\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u|^{2}\right).\end{split}

Applying the Young inequality with exponents
q=1+\tfrac{2}{k}
and
q^{\prime}=1+\tfrac{k}{2}
, we find

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}\right)^{1+\frac{2}{k}}&\leq\eta^{1+\frac{2}{k}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1+\frac{2}{k}}+\frac{C(n,\sigma_{0})}{\eta^{1+\tfrac{k}{2}}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}|u|\right)^{2+\frac{4}{k}}+\eta^{1+\tfrac{2}{k}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}\right)^{1+\frac{2}{k}},\end{split}

for any
\eta>0
, for some constant
C(n,\sigma_{0})>0
. Choosing
\eta=\eta(n,\sigma_{0},\delta)
sufficiently small, we can absorb the summand
\eta^{1+\tfrac{2}{k}}\Big(\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}\Big)^{1+\frac{2}{k}}
on the left hand side. Eventually raising the inequality to the power
1/(1+\tfrac{2}{k})<1
the statement follows with
\alpha_{n}:=\tfrac{k}{2}
.
∎

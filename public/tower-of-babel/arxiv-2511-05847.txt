Limit curve theorems for incomplete metric spaces and the null distance on Lorentzian manifolds
Adam Rennie†, Ben E. Whale†

Abstract
We prove a limit curve theorem for incomplete metric spaces. Our main application is to Sormani and Vegas’ null distance, where our results give strong control on the Lorentzian lengths of limit curves. We also show that regular cosmological time functions and the surface function of a Cauchy surface in a globally hyperbolic manifold define such a null distance.

Introduction

This paper proves limit curve
theorems for incomplete distances on Lorentzian manifolds
and establishes new sufficient conditions for the upper semi-continuity
of the Lorentzian length functional on curves.

There are natural situations in which one wants the Lorentzian
length functional to be upper semi-continuous over sequences of curves
with common non-compact domain. We give necessary conditions
for this in Lemma
2.14
.
Existing
limit curve theorems, however, assume a complete metric which
may have little relation to the Lorentzian distance.
The
compatibility of the null distance with the Lorentzian distance makes it an
attractive alternative,
but there are no guarantees of completeness for the null
distance. This was a strong motivator for our limit curve theorem,
which allows
for incomplete distances,
Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem

not be metrically complete.
In Section 4 we specialise our limit curve theorem to the null distance

[

]

so that we can make use of
Lemma
2.14
.
We require additional conditions given in

.
We show that the null distance induced by

satisfy our conditions.

The strangest feature of our definitions and results is that limit curves can
have strictly smaller domains than the curves in the defining sequence. This is

The upper semicontinuity of the Lorentzian length function

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to a}\lim_{j}f(t,k_{j})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j}\lim_{t\to a}f(t,k_{j})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j}L(\gamma_{i_{k_{j}}})=\lim_{k}L(\gamma_{i_{k}})=\limsup_{i}L(\gamma_{i}).

(3)

2.6

This causes a secondary problem: what does it mean for curves to converge in an

and

there
exists

so that
\gamma_{i}\not\subset K_{j}
.
We can, therefore, by taking a subsequence, assume that

and
that

.
Thus, if

is the connected component of

\gamma_{i}\cap K_{j}
containing

, then
we know that each

is well-defined and for all

In particular, as

is compact,

is finite, though

may be infinite.

We shall construct

inductively over the compact exhaustion

.
Without loss of generality we assume that for all

\gamma_{i}(0)\in K_{1}
.

Let

. By compactness of

,

.
Apply

3.2

with
\epsilon_{1}>0

to construct
\gamma^{1}:[0,a^{1}]\to K_{1}

a limit curve of a subsequence of

which converges uniformly to
\gamma^{1}
,
where, for each

,

.
Let

be the function that selects the uniformly
convergent subsequence.
That is, the sequence

converges uniformly to
\gamma^{1}
.
Note that

implies that

Let

.
Apply

3.2

with

to construct
\gamma^{2}:[0,a^{2}]\to K_{2}

a limit curve of a subsequence of

(\gamma_{h_{1}(i)}\circ f_{h_{1}(i)}^{2})_{i}

which converges uniformly to
\gamma^{2}
,
where, for each

,

is defined by

.
Let

be the function that selects the uniformly

That is, the sequence

(\gamma_{h_{2}(i)}\circ f_{h_{2}(i)}^{2})_{i}

We can repeat the construction for all

.

With this construction completed we now give the definition of

\gamma
and the required subsequence.
We show that for all

, the curve
\gamma^{j}
is a subcurve of

\gamma^{j+1}
.
By construction

converges uniformly to
\gamma^{j}

and

(\gamma_{h_{j+1}(i)}\circ f_{h_{j+1}(i)}^{j+1})_{i}

converges uniformly to
\gamma^{j+1}
.
By construction

is a subsequence of

(\gamma_{h_{j}(i)})_{i}
.
Therefore

Thus

\lim_{i\to\infty}\left(f_{h_{j+1}(i)}^{j+1}\right)^{-1}\circ f_{h_{j+1}(i)}^{j}(t)=t

and hence

\left(\gamma_{h_{j+1}(i)}\circ f^{j+1}_{h_{j+1}(i)}\circ\left(f_{h_{j+1}(i)}^{j+1}\right)^{-1}\circ f_{h_{j+1}(i)}^{j}(t)\right)_{i}

\gamma^{j}(t)
and
\gamma^{j+1}(t)
.
Uniform convergence and the Hausdorffness
of

imply that
\gamma^{j}(t)=\gamma^{j+1}(t)
.
Since this is true for all

is a subcurve of

.

We define the curve
\gamma
.
As

.
Thus the sequence
(a^{j})
is increasing and

exists (possibly

.
Thus, we can define a curve

by

for any

with

. Since

We now show that if

Length control over limit curves via null distances

We left open the choice of distance in
Theorem
3.3
.
In this section we show that
the null distance induced by a suitable time function
is sufficient to show that

2.14
holds for
the limit curve constructed in
Theorem
3.3
.

By a “suitable time function”
we mean any function whose null distance is a metric compatible with the
manifold topology as in Proposition
2.6
and which satisfies
a further condition given in
Proposition
4.3
. To prepare, the next two results make
weaker assumptions, and so the null distances my not satisfy Proposition

.

4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Lorentzian manifold.
Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz function
which is increasing on all timelike curves,
and let
d(\cdot,\cdot;f)
be
the associated null distance.
Let

\gamma:[0,a)\to M
,
a\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
be a past directed causal curve.
If there exists a constant
b>0
such that for all points where

\nabla f
exists we have
g(\nabla f,\nabla f)\leq-b^{2}
,
then for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,a)

d(\gamma(t_{1}),\gamma(t_{2});f)=\left\lvert{f(\gamma(t_{2}))-f(\gamma(t_{1}))}\right\rvert\geq bL(\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}),

where
d(\cdot,\cdot;f)
is the null distance defined by
f

and
L
is the Lorentzian length.

Proof.

The equality

d(\gamma(t_{1}),\gamma(t_{2});f)=\left\lvert{f(\gamma(t_{2}))-f(\gamma(t_{1}))}\right\rvert

is proven in

[
22
, Lemma 3.11]
.
We will show that

\left\lvert{f(\gamma(t_{2}))-f(\gamma(t_{1}))}\right\rvert\geq bL(\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}).

We may assume that
\gamma([t_{1},t_{2}])\subset U
where

\phi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a chart. Let
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be
the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Throughout the proof below we
freely identify differential forms in
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
tangent vectors
T\mathbb{R}^{n}
with points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
using the isomorphisms
induced by the standard frame and metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The open Euclidean unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted
B
.
In an abuse of notation, let
g
and
g^{-1}
denote the push forward
of
g
and
g^{-1}
by
\phi
onto
\phi(U)
.
Since
g,g^{-1}
are coordinate dependent metrics we must take some
care with them when working in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the identifications
above.
Note, in particular,
that by taking the points of evaluation, e.g.
x,y\in\phi(U)
,
arbitrarily close we can ensure that
g^{-1}|_{x}
is arbitrarily close
to
g^{-1}|_{y}
.
We have

f\circ\gamma=f\circ\phi^{-1}\circ\phi\circ\gamma
, so we can consider
the function
f\circ\phi^{-1}
on
\phi(U)
, which contains the
curve
\phi\circ\gamma
.

Since
\gamma([t_{1},t_{2}])
is compact and
U
is open
there exists
\epsilon^{*}>0
so that
the compact set

V:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\exists t\in[t_{1},t_{2}],\,\left\lVert{x-\gamma(t)}\right\rVert\leq\epsilon^{*}\}\subset\phi(U).

The set-valued
Clarke generalised gradient of
f\circ\phi^{-1}
at
y\in\phi(U)

is denoted
\partial^{\circ}(f\circ\phi^{-1})(y)
, see
Definition
2.8
.
Minguzzi
[
15
, Theorem 1.19]
shows that
f
has a
gradient defined almost everywhere on the manifold.
Theorem
2.9
implies that, for each
y\in\phi(V)
,
vectors

v\in\partial^{\circ}(f\circ\phi^{-1})(y)
satisfy

g^{-1}(v,v)\leq-b^{2}
,
because the past-pointing vectors
of Lorentzian length
\leq-b^{2}
form a
convex set.

The function

f
is locally Lipschitz and so there is a constant
D>0
so that at
any
y\in{\phi(V)}
where
df
exists we have

\left\lVert{df}\right\rVert_{2}\leq D
.
Hence, again by Theorem
2.9
, any element

v\in\partial^{\circ}(f\circ\phi^{-1})(y)

also satisfies
\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{2}\leq D
.
Thus we know that
for each
y\in\phi(U)
the set

K_{y}=\overline{\partial^{\circ}(f\circ\phi^{-1})(y+\epsilon^{*}B)+\epsilon^{*}B}

is compact.
The set
K=\bigcup_{y\in V}K_{y}
is a compact subset of

T\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence as
g
is continuous
there exists
C>0
so that
for all
y\in V
and all
u,v\in K_{y}
,

g(u,v)\leq C
.

Fix
\epsilon
so that
0<\epsilon<\epsilon^{*}

and so
that
-b^{2}+(\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon)C+\epsilon<0
.

Since
\gamma
is a continuous past directed causal curve we
can, by
[
19
, Definition 7.4]
,
approximate
\phi\circ\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}
by a past directed
causal curve which is a piecewise
smooth
g
-geodesic

\tilde{\gamma}:[t_{1},t_{2}]\to\phi(V)

so that

\left\lvert{L(\phi\circ\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]})-L(\tilde{\gamma})}\right\rvert<\epsilon
,

\tilde{\gamma}(t_{1})=\phi\circ\gamma(t_{1})
,
and

\tilde{\gamma}(t_{2})=\phi\circ\gamma(t_{2})
.
Note that by construction, wherever
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}
exists
it is a past directed
g
-causal vector.

Since
g
is smooth on
\phi(U)
,
there exists a function
\delta:\phi(U)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
so that
for all
x\in\phi(U)
,

1.

\delta(x)<\epsilon
,

2.

\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\delta(x)\}\subset\phi(U)
, and

3.

for all
x,y\in\phi(U)
,
if
\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert_{2}<\delta(x)

then

\left\lvert{g^{-1}|_{x}(d\tilde{f}(x),d\tilde{f}(x))-g^{-1}|_{y}(d\tilde{f}(x),d\tilde{f}(x))}\right\rvert<\delta(x)
.

Since
f\circ\phi^{-1}
is Lipschitz,
Theorem
2.10

implies that there exists
a smooth function
\tilde{f}:\phi(U)\to\mathbb{R}

such that for all
y\in\phi(U)

we have
\left\lvert{f\circ\phi^{-1}(y)-\tilde{f}(y)}\right\rvert\leq\delta(y)<\epsilon
,
and

d\tilde{f}(y)\in\partial^{\circ}(f\circ\phi^{-1})((y+\delta(y)B)\cap\phi(U))+\delta(y)B.

Note that while
d\tilde{f}
is evaluated at
y
,
the membership relation is for
the union of
Clarke’s generalised gradients of
f\circ\phi^{-1}

over the set of points

(y+\delta(y)B)\cap\phi(U)
.
We want to use knowledge of

\partial^{\circ}(f\circ\phi^{-1})(y)
to construct a bound
on
g(\nabla\tilde{f},\nabla\tilde{f})
. In doing so we
will need to be careful about where the generalised gradient
and the differential
d\tilde{f}

are evaluated.

With these approximations we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert{\tilde{f}(\tilde{\gamma}(t_{2}))-\tilde{f}(\tilde{\gamma}(t_{1}))}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert{\tilde{f}(\phi\circ\gamma(t_{2}))-\tilde{f}(\phi\circ\gamma(t_{1}))}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert{f(\gamma(t_{2}))+\tilde{f}(\phi\circ\gamma(t_{2}))-f(\gamma(t_{2}))-f(\gamma(t_{1}))-(\tilde{f}(\phi\circ\gamma(t_{1}))-f(\gamma(t_{1})))}\right\rvert

\displaystyle<\left\lvert{f(\gamma(t_{2}))-f(\gamma(t_{1}))}\right\rvert+2\epsilon.

We now show that
x\in\phi(U)
implies that

g^{-1}|_{x}(d\tilde{f}(x),d\tilde{f}(x))<0
,
thus showing
that
\tilde{f}
has
g
-causal gradient.
By construction of
\tilde{f}

there exists

y\in(x+\delta(x)B)\cap\phi(U)

so that

d\tilde{f}(x)\in\partial^{\circ}(f\circ\phi^{-1})(y)+\delta(x)B.

By definition there exists

w\in\partial^{\circ}f\circ\phi^{-1}(y)

and
u\in B
so that

d\tilde{f}(x)=w+\delta(x)u
.
We can compute that

\displaystyle g|_{y}(d\tilde{f}(x),d\tilde{f}(x)

\displaystyle=g|_{y}(w,w)+\delta(x)^{2}g|_{y}(u,u)+2\delta(\gamma(t))g|_{y}(w,u)\leq-b^{2}+(\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon)C.

Since
y\in(x+\delta(x)B)\cap\phi(U)
, by definition of

B
,
we see that
\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert_{2}<\delta(x)

and therefore know that

\left\lvert{g^{-1}|_{x}(d\tilde{f}(x),d\tilde{f}(x))-g^{-1}|_{y}(d\tilde{f}(x),d\tilde{f}(x))}\right\rvert<\delta(x)
.
Thus we have that

g^{-1}|_{x}(d\tilde{f}(x),d\tilde{f}(x))\leq g^{-1}|_{y}(d\tilde{f}(x),d\tilde{f}(x))+\delta(x)<-b^{2}+(\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon)C+\epsilon<0,

by construction of
\epsilon
.
Hence the
g
-gradient of
\tilde{f}
is
g
-causal.

By
[
18
, Prop 5.30]
, if
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}

and
\nabla\tilde{f}
are both time-like
the reverse Cauchy
inequality
holds,

\left\lvert{g(\nabla\tilde{f},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})}\right\rvert\geq\sqrt{-g(\nabla\tilde{f},\nabla\tilde{f})}\sqrt{-g(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})},

where
\nabla\tilde{f}
is the gradient of
\tilde{f}

with respect to the push forward of
g
, the

g
-gradient.
If
\nabla\tilde{f}
or
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}
is null then it is clear that this
inequality continues to hold. Using this inequality we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert{\tilde{f}(\tilde{\gamma}(t_{2}))-\tilde{f}(\tilde{\gamma}(t_{1}))}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{f}(\tilde{\gamma}(t))\,\textrm{d}t=-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}d\tilde{f}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})\,\textrm{d}t=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left\lvert{g(\nabla\tilde{f},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})}\right\rvert\,\textrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\sqrt{-g(\nabla\tilde{f},\nabla\tilde{f})}\,\sqrt{-g(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})}\,\textrm{d}t

\displaystyle>\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\sqrt{b^{2}-(\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon)C-\epsilon}\,\sqrt{-g(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime})}\,\textrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sqrt{b^{2}-(\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon)C-\epsilon}\ L(\tilde{\gamma}|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}).

The result now follows by letting
\epsilon\to 0
.
∎

4.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
Lorentzian manifold and
let

be a time function and

the associated

If
\gamma:[0,b)\to M
is a past directed causal curve then

,

.
Since
\gamma_{i}
is past directed and causal

is a decreasing, continuous, and bijective function.

Thus we can define

.

We can compute, by
[

that for all

\displaystyle d(\gamma\circ s(u_{1}),\gamma\circ s(u_{2});f)=\left\lvert{f\circ\gamma\circ s(u_{1})-f\circ\gamma\circ s(u_{2})}\right\rvert=\left\lvert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rvert.

∎

5
Null distances of surface functions

To apply

we need
a function

2.

locally anti-Lipschitz,

3.

We shall call the first four of these conditions the regularity conditions
and we shall call the last condition the geometric condition.
Theorem
4.5
proves that regular cosmological
time satisfies both the regularity and the geometric conditions.

The geometric condition depends on the given sequence of inextendible curves,
and so the function
f
could in principle be tailored for application to a
specific sequence of curves.
The geometric condition is always true if the level sets of
f
are Cauchy.
In the remainder of this section we shall show that the surface function
associated to a
C^{1}
Cauchy surface satisfies the regularity conditions,
Corollary
5.12
.

Before we prove our claim it is worth noting that
Müller and Sánchez,
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]

have shown the existence of a smooth surjective
Cauchy time function

hyperbolic manifold

is Cauchy it satisfies the geometric condition.
Since the function is smooth it is locally Lipschitz.
Sormani and Vega have shown,
[

Müller and Sánchez’ time function also satisfies the regularity conditions
except for the bound on gradient length.

If one could prove that a lower bound for

Müller and Sánchez’s time function would satisfy both the
regularity and the geometric conditions.
The existence of such a lower bound is tied to the geometry of the manifold.
In truncated Minkowski space, i.e.

bound exists since

surjects onto
\mathbb{R}
, though, of course, the standard time
function given by projection does satisfy the regularity
and geometric conditions.

5.6
.

Surface functions of Cauchy surfaces in globally hyperbolic
manifolds are generalised time functions,
i.e. increasing on all future directed causal curves.

Proof.

Let
\tau_{S}
be the surface function of the Cauchy surface
S
.
Let
\gamma
be a future directed causal curve.
We know that
\tau_{S}
is non-decreasing on
\gamma
.
If
\tau_{S}
is constant over some subcurve of
\gamma

then there exists a level surface of
\tau_{S}

that contains a causal curve.
This contradicts

5.5
and the assumption that

\tau_{S}
is a non-constant non-decreasing

\gamma

∎

5.7

5.8
.

Let
(M,g)
be globally hyperbolic,
\tau_{S}

Cauchy surface

Proof.

Lemma
5.7

\nabla\tau_{S}|_{U}
exists.
If we suppose that

More generally, we start by considering

\phi_{+},\phi_{-},\phi_{0}

We have already shown that the result is true for

.

With this in mind suppose that

C\subset I^{+}\left(S\right)

(x_{i})\subset C

\nabla\tau_{S}|_{x_{i}}

By taking a subsequence we can further assume that

Since

5.4

5.1

\gamma_{i}^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h(\nabla\tau_{S}|_{x_{i}},\nabla\tau_{S}|_{x_{i}})}}\nabla\tau_{S}|_{x_{i}}.

5.11
.

Let
M
be globally hyperbolic.
If
\tau_{S}
is the surface function
associated to a
C^{1}
Cauchy surface, then

\tau_{S}
is locally Lipschitz with respect to
any auxiliary
Riemannian metric
h
.

Proof.

Since the statement is local, and all Riemannian metrics are locally
equivalent, it suffices to check local Lipschitzness with respect to a
single metric.

Let
x\in M
and let
\phi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a chart about
x
.
Let
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
be the coordinate
frame on
U
.
Choose
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
a pseudo-orthonormal frame over
U
,
with
g(e_{1},e_{1})=-1
,
g(e_{i},e_{j})=\delta_{ij}

for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
j=2,\ldots,n
.
Define
h:TU\times TU\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
a Riemannian metric

h(u,v)=g(u,v)-2\frac{g(u,e_{1})g(v,e_{1})}{g(e_{1},e_{1})},

so that
h_{ij}=\left\lvert{g_{ij}}\right\rvert=\delta_{ij}
.

For any open set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

let
L^{\infty}(V)
be the space of bounded Lebesgue measurable
functions
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
so that

\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert_{\infty}:={\rm ess\,sup}_{V}\left\lvert{f}\right\rvert<\infty

and
let

W^{1,\infty}_{\text{loc}}(V)
to be the Sobolev
space of all functions
f:V\to\mathbb{R}

so that if
C\subset V
is
compact then
f|_{C}\in L^{\infty}(C)
,
the weak partial derivatives
\partial_{i}f|_{C}
,

i=1,\ldots,n
,
exist and are such that

\partial_{i}f|_{C}\in L^{\infty}(C)
, see

[
8
, Notation on pages 26 and 36, Definition 4.2]
.
Since the coordinate map
\phi
is smooth,
\tau_{S}
is locally Lipschitz
on
M
with respect to
h
if and only if
\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1}
is
locally Lipschitz with respect to the Euclidean metric.
By
[
8
, Theorem 4.5]
,
\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1}
is
locally Lipschitz if and only if

\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1}\in W^{1,\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\phi(U))
.

Let
C\subset\phi(U)
be compact.
Since
\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1}
is continuous,
Lemma

5.3
,
we see that

\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1}
is Lebesgue measurable
and
\left\lVert{\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1}|_{C}}\right\rVert_{\infty}<\infty
.
That is
\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1}|_{C}\in L^{\infty}(C)
.

Lemma
5.8

implies that there exists
{K_{1}}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
so that
wherever the
g
-gradient
\nabla\tau_{S}|_{\phi^{-1}(C)}
exists we have

h(\nabla\tau_{S}|_{\phi^{-1}(C)},\nabla\tau_{S}|_{\phi^{-1}(C)})<{K_{1}}^{2}
.

With
(f_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
partial derivatives of
\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1}
are given by

\partial_{i}(\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1})=D(\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1})\cdot f_{i}.

As
\tau\circ\phi^{-1}
is continuous, the main result of
[
16
]

implies that the sets where the partial derivatives exist are measurable,
and the functions
\partial_{i}(\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1})
are measurable on
these sets.
As
D(\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1})
exists a.e., these sets are of
full measure. As

D(\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1})

is bounded by Lemma
5.8
, so too are the
partial derivatives and
\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1}\in W^{1,\infty}
. Hence

\tau_{S}\circ\phi^{-1}
and so
\tau_{S}
are locally Lipschitz.
∎

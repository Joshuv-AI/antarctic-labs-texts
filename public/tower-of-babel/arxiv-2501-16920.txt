J. M. Zanotti

Abstract
Many lattice QCD simulations now have many lattice spacings available, and it is of interest to investigate how they scale. In this talk we first derive renormalisation group equations appropriate for 2+1 clover fermions. This is then used together with pion mass and gradient flow results at five lattice spacings to study scaling.

Introduction

One of the major difficulties in lattice gauge theories is the extrapolation
to the continuum limit. Usually individual fits at a particular lattice
spacing are made to, e.g. a hadron mass or matrix element, followed by an
extrapolation to the continuum limit (usually in
a^{2}
). This is a noisy
procedure, involving both the pre-determined
a
values and also the
individual values of the mass or matrix element. To improve the situation often
a smoothing procedure is used, for example a joint extrapolation
involving both the lattice spacing and mass or matrix element simultaneously.
Theoretically this is often not so well justified. A better motivated
procedure suggested here is to use the Renormalisation Group (RG) equations
to help to achieve this. In this contribution we attempt a first step
by deriving and solving the RG equations for the lattice spacing.

2
The RG equation

Scaling means that we have lines of constant physics (e.g. constant mass
ratios) passing through our parameter space. On one of these lines we
can measure the relative rate at which lattice spacing changes by
monitoring the rate at which correlation lengths change as we move along
the trajectory. Scaling (up to
O(a^{2})
violations) are regions where

a\Lambda^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}\ll 1
and
am_{q}\ll 1
.
For Wilson type fermions considered here we have in
g_{0}
,
\kappa_{q}
space

\displaystyle\left.a{\partial\over\partial a}\right|_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle physics}=U(g_{0},\{\kappa\})\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial g_{0}}\right|_{\kappa}+\sum_{q}V_{q}(g_{0},\{\kappa\})\left.{\partial\over\partial\kappa_{q}}\right|_{g_{0}}\,.

(1)

Re-writing this in terms of
m_{q}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}\equiv am_{q}
where

\displaystyle am_{q}\equiv{1\over 2}\left({1\over\kappa_{q}}-{1\over\kappa_{0c}(g_{0})}\right)\,,

(2)

(with
\kappa_{0}
the hopping parameter on the
SU(n_{f})
-flavour symmetric
line, with critical point at the chiral limit
\kappa_{0c}
) and then
Taylor expanding
U
and
V_{q}
gives

\displaystyle a\left.{\partial\over\partial a}\right|_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle physics}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{0}(g_{0})\left.{\partial\over\partial g_{0}}\right|_{\{am\}}+B_{1}(g_{0})a\bar{m}\left.{\partial\over\partial g_{0}}\right|_{\{am\}}

(3)

\displaystyle+G_{0}(g_{0})\sum_{q}am_{q}\left.{\partial\over\partial am_{q}}\right|_{g_{0}}+H_{0}(g_{0})a\bar{m}\sum_{q}\left.{\partial\over\partial am_{q}}\right|_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle+G_{1}(g_{0})a\bar{m}\sum_{q}am_{q}\left.{\partial\over\partial am_{q}}\right|_{g_{0}}+G_{2}(g_{0})\sum_{q}(am_{q})^{2}\left.{\partial\over\partial am_{q}}\right|_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle+H_{1}(g_{0})(a\bar{m})^{2}\sum_{q}\left.{\partial\over\partial am_{q}}\right|_{g_{0}}+H_{2}(g_{0})a^{2}\widebar{m^{2}}\sum_{q}\left.{\partial\over\partial am_{q}}\right|_{g_{0}}\,,

where

\displaystyle a\bar{m}\equiv{1\over n_{f}}\sum_{q}am_{q}\,,\qquad a^{2}\widebar{m^{2}}\equiv{1\over n_{f}}\sum_{q}a^{2}m_{q}^{2}\,.

(4)

The
B_{0}
,
G_{0}
,
H_{0}
coefficients are usually referred to as
‘renormalisation’, while the
B_{1}
,
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
,
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
coefficients
are the ‘improvement’ terms, so in principle this treats both the
renormalisation and improvement terms in a unified fashion. For chiral
fermions all the coefficients vanish, except for
B_{0}
and
G_{0}
.

It is sufficient to only consider here the leading terms (ie
O(am_{q})
),
although the higher order terms can be considered,
[
1
]
.
The leading functions are related to the usual renormalisation coefficients

\displaystyle B_{0}(g_{0})=-\beta(g_{0})\,,\quad G_{0}(g_{0})=1-\gamma_{m}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle NS}(g_{0})\,,\quad H_{0}(g_{0})=\gamma_{m}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle NS}(g_{0})-\gamma_{m}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}(g_{0})\,,

(5)

with

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \beta(g_{0})&=-b_{0}g_{0}^{3}-b_{1}g_{0}^{5}-b_{2}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}g_{0}^{7}+O(g_{0}^{9})\,,\\
B_{1}(g_{0})&=\phantom{-}b^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}_{10}g_{0}^{3}+O(g_{0}^{5})\,,\end{aligned}\qquad\begin{aligned} \gamma_{m}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle NS}(g_{0})&=d_{0}g_{0}^{2}+d_{1}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle NS\,lat}g_{0}^{4}+O(g_{0}^{6})\,,\\
\gamma_{m}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}(g_{0})&=d_{0}g_{0}^{2}+d_{1}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S\,lat}g_{0}^{4}+O(g_{0}^{6})\,,\end{aligned}

(6)

where
b_{0}
,
b_{1}
and
d_{0}
are known universal coefficients.

2.1
Renormalised quark masses

For Wilson-like fermions, which have no chiral symmetry, the singlet and
non-singlet quark mass pieces evolve differently

\displaystyle\bar{m}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle rgi}=a\bar{m}\,u(g_{0})\,,\qquad m_{q}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle rgi}-\bar{m}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle rgi}=(am_{q}-a\bar{m})\,v(g_{0})\,.

(7)

For simplicity we consider the Renormalised Group Invariant (RGI)
quark mass, alternatively the same equations hold for the quark mass in a
given scheme at a given scale. Also to simplify the notation somewhat we have
absorbed
\Lambda^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}
into the definition of
\bar{m}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle rgi}
,
i.e. we measure the quark mass in units of
\Lambda^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}
.
Note, in particular, that
\bar{m}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle rgi}\propto a\bar{m}
.

We now solve the RG equation

\displaystyle a\left.{\partial\over\partial a}\right|_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle physics}m_{q}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle rgi}=0\,,

(8)

to give

\displaystyle u(g_{0})=C(g_{0})\exp\left[\int_{0}^{g_{0}}d\xi\left\{{1\over b_{0}\xi^{3}}-{b_{1}+b_{0}d_{0}\over b_{0}^{2}\xi}-{G_{0}(\xi)+H_{0}(\xi)\over B_{0}(\xi)}\right\}\right]\,,

(9)

and

\displaystyle v(g_{0})=C(g_{0})\exp\left[\int_{0}^{g_{0}}d\xi\left\{{1\over b_{0}\xi^{3}}-{b_{1}+b_{0}d_{0}\over b_{0}^{2}\xi}-{G_{0}(\xi)\over B_{0}(\xi)}\right\}\right]\,,

(10)

where

\displaystyle C(g_{0})=\Lambda^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}\left[2b_{0}g_{0}^{2}\right]^{d_{0}\over 2b_{0}}\left[b_{0}g_{0}^{2}\right]^{b_{1}\over 2b_{0}^{2}}\exp\left[\frac{1}{2b_{0}g_{0}^{2}}\right]\,.

(11)

These are the usual type of equations, arranged so that there is no
singularity in the integral as
g_{0}\to 0
. Again, for simplicity,
as for
\bar{m}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle rgi}
we shall in future measure
u
(and
v
) in units
of
\Lambda^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}
, so
u\to u/\Lambda^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}
. Similar equations
(with appropriate
\gamma
s) also hold for the renormalisation of operators

m^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle rgi}\to O^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle rgi}
. However we shall not consider this further here.

2.2
Coupling constant

Rather than considering the renormalised coupling, we consider here the
relation between the lattice spacing and lattice coupling. There are
two possibilities. First we consider changing lattice spacing
(i.e. including
a\bar{m}
in the definition, but keeping the coupling
constant). Setting

\displaystyle a=s(g_{0})\left\{1+a\bar{m}\,r(g_{0})\right\}\,,

(12)

and solving

\displaystyle a\left.{\partial\over\partial a}\right|_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle physics}a=a\,,

(13)

gives

\displaystyle s(g_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(g_{0})\exp\left(\int^{g_{0}}_{0}d\xi\left\{{1\over B_{0}(\xi)}-{1\over b_{0}\xi^{3}}+{b_{1}\over b_{0}^{2}\xi}\right\}\right)\,,

(14)

\displaystyle r(g_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-u(g_{0})\int_{0}^{g_{0}}d\xi{B_{1}(\xi)\over u(\xi)B_{0}^{2}(\xi)}\,\,\,\underbrace{=}_{g_{0}^{2}\to 0}\,\,\,-{b_{10}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}\over b_{0}}g_{0}^{2}+O(g_{0}^{4})\,,

(15)

with

\displaystyle A(g_{0})={1\over\Lambda^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}}\left[b_{0}g_{0}^{2}\right]^{-{b_{1}\over 2b_{0}^{2}}}\exp\left[-\;{1\over 2b_{0}g_{0}^{2}}\right]\,.

(16)

Note that
s(g_{0})
is the standard RG solution to the
\beta
-function.
Alternatively a second possibility would be to re-define the coupling
constant by setting (e.g.
[
2
]
)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{0}^{2}=g_{0}^{2}\left\{1+b_{g}(g_{0})a\bar{m}\right\}\,,

(17)

and now solving

\displaystyle a\left.{\partial\over\partial a}\right|_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle physics}\tilde{g}_{0}=B_{0}(\tilde{g}_{0})\,,

(18)

gives

\displaystyle b_{g}(g_{0})=2{B_{0}(g_{0})\over g_{0}}\,r(g_{0})\underbrace{=}_{g_{0}^{2}\to 0}-2b_{10}^{\rm\scriptscriptstyle lat}g_{0}^{4}+O(g_{0}^{6})\,.

(19)

As anticipated we have
b_{g}\propto r
. As discussed in e.g.
[
3
,
4
]
we expect
b_{g}
(and hence
r
)
to be numerically quite small.

Conclusions

In this talk we have discussed RG equations for

and attempted to construct some RG functions.
Considering the lattice spacing, we find scaling, with little dependence
on
\bar{m}^{{\rm\scriptscriptstyle rgi}\,*}

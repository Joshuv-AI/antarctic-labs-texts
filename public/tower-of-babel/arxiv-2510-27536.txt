Diffusion velocity modulus of self-propelled spherical and circular particles in the generalized Langevin approach
Pedro J. Colmenares

Abstract
This research provides a framework for describing the averaged modulus of the velocity reached by an accelerated self-propelled Brownian particle diffusing in a thermal fluid and constrained to a harmonic external potential. The system is immersed in a thermal bath of harmonic oscillators at a constant temperature, where its constituents also interact with the external field. The dynamics is investigated for a sphere and a disk, and is split into two stochastic processes. The first describes the gross-grained inner time-dependent self-velocity generated from a set of independent Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes without the influence of the external field. This internal mechanism provides the initial velocity for the particle to diffuse in the fluid, which is implemented in a modified generalized Langevin equation as the second process. We find that the system exhibits spontaneous fluctuations in the diffusive velocity modulus due to the inner mechanism; however, as expected, the momentary diffusive velocity fluctuations fade out at large times. The internal propelled velocity module in spherical coordinates is derived, as well as the simulation of the different modules for both the sphere and the already known equations for a disk in polar coordinates.

It is worth mentioning that some investigations in ABP are also based on the role of an energy depot capable of providing the internal speed to the particle. Reference

Incorporating this expression into the system Hamiltonian gives the equation of motion for position as a functional derivative of the local squared velocity. Analyzing it numerically by using reasonable expressions for the velocity, such as those derived in this article, it happens that such a functional derivative is multiple-valued. It is the opinion of the author that the premises invoked in the energy-depot approach to ABPs should then be reexamined for use in a proper Hamiltonian description.

Up to this point, only ABP models have been succinctly referenced. However, other alternatives could also be invoked to analyze self-propelled systems, such as those where the particle gradually accelerates due to a specific internal mechanism. This is not a model for ABPs, but one in which the particle randomly changes its internal velocity and instead naturally reaches a diffusive stationary state. Its diffusion velocity modulus (VM) could then be obtained through a more precise equation, such as that derived by the author

The model consists of splitting the overall dynamics into two stochastic processes. The first describes the internal velocity propelled by three Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes (OUP), where it is assumed that the internal structure is insensitive to external perturbations and that the particle size doesn’t break down the Langevin description. The second contribution describes its diffusion in a thermal bath, subjected to a parabolic external field, with the initial condition provided by the OUPs. This is done through a modified generalized Langevin equation (GLE)

Additionally, the 3D-OUP is transformed into spherical coordinates to derive the system of stochastic differential equations for the VM and angular vector phases, reducing to the known planar system for a vanishing polar angle. The 3D derivation hasn’t appeared in the literature.

IV
Concluding remarks

This research determines the root mean square diffusion velocity modulus of a Brownian particle in an external field with internal propulsion starting from rest. A set of independent Ornstein-Uhlenbeck models propels the mechanism. The diffusion in the thermal bath is given by a reformulation of a modified generalized Langevin equation derived in Ref.
Colmenares 2023
for passive Brownian particles by using the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck velocities as initial conditions for the dynamics. It is found that the velocity modulus depends uniquely on the model parameters, without requiring any additional assumptions.

The system for the sphere and disk exhibits spontaneous fluctuations in the diffusive velocity due to a fluctuating acceleration generated by the inner mechanism described by the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes. However, these momentary velocity fluctuations fade out at large times as expected from the previous author’s result for passive Brownian motion
Colmenares 2024
. Those changes are due to an inverse change in the direction of the propulsion speed. Therefore, the proposed model cannot be directly applied to describe active matter.

As a complement to this work, the analytical derivation of the propelled velocity module for a sphere, not known in the literature, correctly reduces to that known for a disk. Simulation of these equations predicts discrepancies in the average module of the sphere compared to that of the disk at the beginning. However, they tend to saturate with increasing time, just as occurs in the latter.

In summary, the key points are, on one hand, the use of a generalized Langevin equation, which explicitly includes the interaction of the bath particles with the external field. This approach replicates molecular dynamics simulation results with an acceptable degree of accuracy. On the other hand, it is based on considering the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process to describe the initial self-velocity. The proposed model could be improved by using other stochastic models for propulsion. A possible application of this theory could be applied to describe nano-motors.

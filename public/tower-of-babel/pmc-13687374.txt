MTHFR C677T genetic polymorphisms are not a decisive factor in delayed methotrexate excretion in central nervous system lymphoma patients
Authors: Xiufeng Wang, Ting Li, Man Yang, Yan Guo, Sun Wu, Yan Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: High-dose methotrexate (HD-MTX) serves as a cornerstone treatment for central nervous system lymphoma. However, its clinical utility is limited by delayed drug excretion and associated adverse events. The methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) C677T polymorphism, a critical genetic variant influencing MTX metabolism, has been proposed by some experts as an indicator for HD-MTX dose reduction due to its potential association with delayed excretion. Objective: This study aimed to identify clinical risk factors for delayed MTX excretion and evaluate the predictive value of MTHFR C677T polymorphism in HD-MTX treatment cycles for CNS lymphoma patients. Methods: We conducted a retrospective analysis of 213 HD-MTX treatment cycles in CNS lymphoma patients (derived from 98 patients) treated at our institution between May 2015 and December 2023. Each patient received a median of 3 HD-MTX cycles (range 1–9 cycles). Treatment cycles were stratified into delayed excretion (n = 66) and normal excretion (n = 147) groups based on international consensus guidelines for pharmacokinetic monitoring of serum MTX concentrations. MTHFR C677T genotypes were analyzed using PCR-RFLP with Sanger sequencing validation (10% random sample). Clinical parameters were compared between groups; univariate logistic regression (retention threshold: P < 0.10) was used for initial risk factor screening, and a mixed-effects logistic regression model with a random intercept for individual patients (retention: P < 0.05) was applied for identifying independent risk factors to correct for non-independence of repeated measures. Results: Univariate analysis revealed four potential risk factors (P < 0.1): advanced age (OR = 1.03), MTHFR C677T CT/TT (OR = 1.73), hypoalbuminemia (< 35 g/L) (OR = 3.42), and acidic urine pH (< 7.0) (OR = 4.15). Mixed-effects multivariate analysis identified hypoalbuminemia (adjusted OR = 3.02, 95%CI 1.56–5.85) and acidic urine pH (adjusted OR = 4.15, 95%CI 2.17–7.94) as independent predictors of delayed excretion (P < 0.05), with a random effect variance of σ²=0.21 indicating mild individual intervariability. The MTHFR C677T CT/TT genotype showed no statistically significant association in the adjusted model (P = 0.095). A total of 35.1% (13/37) of patients with CT/TT genotype exhibited delayed clearance in initial cycles (1–2) but normalized excretion in subsequent administrations (cycles 3–6, involving 42 treatment cycles). The median number of HD-MTX cycles administered was 4 (range 1–8) in patients with at least one delayed excretion cycle and 5 (range 1–9) in patients with all normal excretion cycles.Conclusion: Our findings demonstrate that urine acidification and hypoalbuminemia, rather than MTHFR C677T polymorphism, constitute primary risk factors for HD-MTX excretion delay in treatment cycles. These results suggest that optimal management should prioritize urinary alkalinization (pH ≥ 7.0) and albumin level maintenance over genotype-guided dose reduction. These modifiable factors present actionable targets for improving HD-MTX safety profiles in CNS lymphoma treatment.

Introduction

Central nervous system lymphoma (CNSL) is a rare malignancy involving the brain parenchyma, spinal cord, leptomeninges, cranial nerves, and/or vitreoretinal structures [1]. The therapeutic challenge in CNSL arises from the blood-brain barrier’s restricted permeability, necessitating agents capable of achieving adequate cerebrospinal fluid penetration. Methotrexate (MTX), a folate antimetabolite, serves as a cornerstone of CNSL treatment due to its unique pharmacodynamic properties [2]. MTX exerts cytotoxic effects by competitive inhibition of dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), the enzyme responsible for converting dihydrofolate (FH2) to tetrahydrofolate (FH4) [3]. This biochemical blockade depletes intracellular reduced folate pools, thereby disrupting nucleotide synthesis and DNA repair processes. The resultant impairment of thymidylate and purine biosynthesis preferentially targets rapidly proliferating malignant cells, underpinning MTX’s therapeutic efficacy in both hematologic malignancies and autoimmune disorders [4].

In clinical oncology practice, high-dose methotrexate (HD-MTX) remains a therapeutic mainstay for central nervous system lymphoma (CNSL) due to its unique pharmacokinetic profile [5]. When administered at doses ≥ 3 g/m², MTX achieves supratherapeutic serum concentrations (typically > 1,000 µM) that enable effective penetration through both blood-brain and blood-testis barriers [6]. However, this therapeutic advantage is counterbalanced by significant toxicity risks. Approximately 15–30% of patients experience delayed drug excretion (serum MTX > 1 µM at 72 h post-infusion), predisposing to multiorgan toxicity including myelosuppression (Grade 3/4 in 40–60%), mucositis (WHO Grade ≥ 2 in 35–50%), acute hepatitis (ALT > 5×ULN in 20–30%), and dose-limiting nephrotoxicity (SCr elevation > 1.5×baseline in 10–18%) [7]. These complications frequently necessitate treatment delays (median 7–14 days), potentially compromising disease control in this aggressive malignancy [8].

Current risk stratification remains suboptimal, with existing studies limited by heterogeneous methodologies. Notably, the pharmacogenetic dimension requires rigorous investigation. Methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR), encoded by the rs1801133 polymorphism, demonstrates allelic-specific activity: wild-type (CC) preserves 100% enzymatic function, heterozygous (CT) reduces activity by 40–50%, while homozygous (TT) variants retain < 30% activity [9]. This functional gradient theoretically increases MTX sensitivity, prompting some guidelines to recommend 25–30% dose reductions for CT/TT carriers. However, our clinical observations reveal paradoxical temporal variability in MTX excretion among CT/TT genotype patients across different treatment cycles, challenging the paradigm of static genetic determinism.

This retrospective cohort study based on 213 HD-MTX treatment cycles (N = 213) from 98 CNSL patients aimed to: (1) quantify the predictive value of MTHFR C677T status relative to clinical parameters in treatment cycles; (2) elucidate temporal patterns of delayed excretion in MTHFR variant carriers across consecutive cycles; (3) establish evidence-based dose adjustment criteria for HD-MTX in CNSL patients based on per-cycle clinical factors.

Materials and methods

General information

This retrospective cohort study enrolled 213 consecutive HD-MTX treatment cycles in CNSL patients who received therapy at our tertiary care center between May 2015 and December 2023. These cycles were derived from 98 patients who met the following individual eligibility criteria: (1) age 18–75 years; (2) histologically confirmed CNSL diagnosis (incorporating both primary CNS lymphoma and secondary CNS involvement of systemic lymphoma); (3) completion of ≥ 1 full HD-MTX cycle (3.5 g/m² over 4-h infusion with standardized leucovorin rescue, with dose escalation to 5 g/m² for patients with leptomeningeal disease); (4) available pharmacogenetic data (MTHFR C677T genotype) and serial therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) records for each treatment cycle; (5) baseline Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0–2, Karnofsky Performance Score (KPS) ≥ 60 at the initiation of each cycle.

Exclusion criteria for treatment cycles included: (1) baseline severe hepatic/renal dysfunction (ALT > 2×ULN, SCr > 1.5×ULN, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²) within 72 h before cycle initiation; (2) presence of pleural effusion, peritoneal ascites or other significant fluid retention disorders at cycle baseline; (3) incomplete TDM or genetic testing data for the cycle; (4) concurrent administration of drugs interfering with MTX metabolism/excretion during the cycle.

Delayed MTX excretion was rigorously defined per international consensus guidelines as either MTX > 1 µmol/L at 48 h post-infusion, or MTX > 0.1 µmol/L at 72 h post-infusion [10]. Treatment cycles were stratified into two groups based on this definition: the delayed excretion cycle group (n = 66, 31.0%) (any single cycle with delayed MTX excretion) and the normal excretion cycle group (n = 147, 69.0%) (any single cycle with normal MTX excretion).

From an initial screening pool of 296 potential HD-MTX treatment cycles, 213 cycles met all eligibility criteria after applying exclusion parameters. All cycles underwent standardized MTX monitoring via HPLC-MS/MS (lower limit of quantification: 0.01 µmol/L) with blood sampling performed at 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h post-infusion. All baseline biochemical and clinical assessments for each cycle were completed within 72 h preceding MTX infusion to ensure timeliness of data.

Results

General clinical characteristics

The study included 213 HD-MTX treatment cycles derived from 98 CNSL patients, stratified into the delayed excretion cycle group (n = 66, 31.0%) and the normal excretion cycle group (n = 147, 69.0%). Each patient received a median of 3 HD-MTX cycles (range 1–9 cycles), with 41 patients receiving 1 cycle, 33 patients receiving 2–3 cycles, and 24 patients receiving ≥ 4 cycles. No treatment cycles were from patients with pleural effusion, peritoneal ascites or eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m² at cycle baseline. Notably, the delayed excretion cycle group demonstrated a 3-year age disparity at cycle initiation (59 vs. 56 years), lower median KPS score, higher proportion of ECOG 2 cycles and first-cycle HD-MTX (71.2% vs. 66.7%), though these differences did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05 by Mann-Whitney U/χ² tests). The final model demonstrated excellent discrimination (AUC = 0.82, 95%CI 0.76–0.88) and good calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow P = 0.43), explaining 34.7% of variance in delayed excretion (Nagelkerke R²=0.347). Notably, urinary pH contributed 61% of the model’s predictive power through dominance analysis, followed by albumin (29%), age (7%) and MTHFR C677T genotype (3%). For categorical variables, hypoalbuminemia (< 35 g/L) was associated with an adjusted OR of 3.02 (95%CI 1.56–5.85, P < 0.001) and acidic urine pH (< 7.0) with an adjusted OR of 4.15 (95%CI 2.17–7.94, P < 0.001) for delayed excretion in treatment cycles.

Temporal pattern of delayed excretion in MTHFR C677T variant carriers

A total of 37 patients with MTHFR C677T CT/TT genotypes (involving 93 HD-MTX treatment cycles) were included in the analysis of temporal excretion patterns across consecutive cycles, among which 13 patients (35.1%) exhibited delayed clearance in their initial 1–2 treatment cycles (19 delayed cycles) but normalized excretion in subsequent administrations (cycles 3–6, 42 normal cycles). All these 13 patients received enhanced prophylactic measures (target urinary pH ≥ 7.5, increased hydration volume to 4000 mL/m²/day, pre-infusion albumin correction to ≥ 35 g/L) in all subsequent cycles, with no recurrence of delayed excretion observed in follow-up treatment cycles. The conversion from delayed to normal excretion in these patients was consistently associated with the achievement of target urinary pH (≥ 7.5) and serum albumin (≥ 35 g/L) in the subsequent cycles.

Discussion

The therapeutic paradigm of central nervous system lymphoma (CNSL) has been revolutionized by high-dose methotrexate (HD-MTX), with 5-year survival rates improving from < 10% to 40–60% in the HD-MTX era [11]. However, this survival benefit is counterbalanced by a 22–35% incidence of treatment-limiting toxicities, predominantly mediated through delayed MTX excretion [12]. Our study based on 213 HD-MTX treatment cycles (correcting for individual patient repeated measures) provides critical insights into modifiable per-cycle risk factors for this life-threatening complication, while challenging current pharmacogenetic paradigms that advocate for genotype-guided dose reduction.

Contrary to prevailing hypotheses, the MTHFR C677T polymorphism demonstrated limited predictive value for delayed MTX excretion in our cohort of treatment cycles. While the CT/TT genotypes showed borderline significance in univariate analysis (OR = 1.73, P = 0.066), this association dissipated upon adjustment for clinical parameters and individual patient variability in the mixed-effects model (P = 0.095). This finding aligns with recent pharmacogenomic studies demonstrating that MTHFR variants account for < 8% of MTX pharmacokinetic variability [13], and our dominance analysis further confirmed that the genotype contributed only 3% of the predictive power in the multivariate model for treatment cycles. We speculate that MTX metabolism in vivo is a very complex process, influenced not only by metabolic enzymes but also by the body’s acid-base physicochemical factors that vary across treatment cycles. While this study focused on MTHFR C677T, MTX pharmacokinetics are multifactorial, involving polymorphisms in genes such as SLC19A1 (RFC1), ABCG2, and TYMS, which modulate drug transport and folate metabolism [14–16]. Future studies should explore these gene-gene and gene-environment interactions across treatment cycles to refine predictive models for MTX toxicity.

Our data based on treatment cycles strongly suggest that clinicians should prioritize two modifiable per-cycle physiological factors in HD-MTX management for CNSL patients:① Urinary pH Optimization: Each 1-unit pH elevation reduced delayed excretion risk by 58% (aOR = 0.42, P < 0.001) in treatment cycles, likely through enhanced MTX solubility (pKa = 4.8–5.5) [17], which is a critical physicochemical factor for renal tubular excretion of MTX; ② Albumin Maintenance: Hypoalbuminemia (< 35 g/L) tripled the risk of delayed excretion (aOR = 3.02, P < 0.001), potentially via altered volume distribution of MTX and impaired tubular secretion due to reduced protein binding [18]. The temporal pattern of delayed excretion in MTHFR variant carriers further supports the importance of these modifiable factors: 35.1% of CT/TT patients with initial delayed excretion achieved normal excretion in subsequent cycles after enhanced urinary alkalinization and albumin correction, confirming that per-cycle physiological optimization is more effective than static genotype-guided dose reduction in improving MTX excretion. This finding is particularly clinically relevant, as it indicates that even patients with high-risk MTHFR genotypes can safely receive standard HD-MTX doses with appropriate per-cycle management of urinary pH and albumin levels.

Notably, our findings challenge conventional hepatic/renal function paradigms for MTX toxicity prediction in treatment cycles. The null association with ALT/SCr (P > 0.3) may reflect stringent cycle baseline selection - all included cycles were from patients with normal baseline organ function (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) Grade 0–1) within 72 h before MTX infusion. This underscores the need for toxicity prediction models specific to treatment cycles in patients with preserved hepatorenal function, as traditional hepatic/renal function indices do not appear to contribute to delayed MTX excretion in this population. Additionally, no baseline differences in eGFR, ECOG or KPS were found between the two cycle groups, indicating that these factors do not contribute to delayed MTX excretion in treatment cycles for patients with preserved organ function and good performance status.

Methodologically, our study advances the field of HD-MTX pharmacovigilance through three key aspects: ① Pharmacokinetic Precision: Strict 72 h TDM-guided toxicity definition based on international consensus guidelines, with per-cycle baseline assessments completed within 72 h to ensure data timeliness; ② Statistical Rigor: Use of mixed-effects logistic regression to correct for non-independence of repeated measures from the same patient, a critical statistical adjustment often overlooked in previous studies that analyzed only patient-level data; ③ Clinical Applicability: Identified actionable per-cycle targets (urinary pH ≥ 7.0, albumin ≥ 35 g/L) and clarified the temporal pattern of delayed excretion with corresponding prophylactic measures that can be easily implemented in clinical practice.

Of course, this study has several limitations. First, it is a single-center retrospective study with a relatively small number of treatment cycles, which does not fully meet the requirements of event per variable (EPV). Thus, the results may not be sufficiently stable. Second, we only focused on the MTHFR C677T polymorphism and did not include other MTX metabolism-related genes, which may limit the comprehensiveness of the pharmacogenetic analysis. Third, the number of patients receiving high-dose MTX (4–5 g/m²) was relatively small, and the study may be underpowered to detect differences in excretion risk associated with higher doses. However, considering the relatively low incidence of delayed MTX excretion and the strong interpretability of our findings on modifiable per-cycle factors, the results are still presented here, and their reliability needs to be confirmed by further multicenter prospective studies with larger sample sizes of treatment cycles.

Conclusion

Our findings based on 213 HD-MTX treatment cycles (corrected for repeated measures) establish hypoalbuminemia (serum albumin < 35 g/L) and urinary acidification (pH < 7.0) as independent modifiable per-cycle risk factors for delayed MTX excretion in CNSL patients receiving HD-MTX.

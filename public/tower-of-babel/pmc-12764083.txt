Oncologic adequacy and outcomes of open, transoral endoscopic, and robotic thyroidectomy for papillary thyroid carcinoma: a propensity score-matched analysis
Authors: Jun Ho Lee, Hongki Gwak
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTransoral thyroidectomy, including transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy vestibular approach (TOETVA) and transoral robotic thyroidectomy (TORT), offers cosmetic advantages over conventional open thyroidectomy (OT). However, few studies have directly compared these three approaches in a single analysis. We evaluated the oncological adequacy and perioperative outcomes of OT, TOETVA, and TORT for papillary thyroid carcinoma using propensity score matching (PSM).MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 819 consecutive patients who underwent OT (n = 559), TOETVA (n = 128), or TORT (n = 132) between 2016 and 2023. PSM was performed using 10 baseline clinicopathological variables to create three balanced cohorts (n = 124 each). The outcomes included central lymph node (LN) yield, complications, operative time, postoperative pain, hospital stay, and thyroglobulin and radioactive iodine (RAI) parameters.ResultsAfter matching, OT and TORT achieved similar central LN yields (7.1 ± 5.4 vs. 6.9 ± 2.9), both significantly higher than TOETVA (4.8 ± 2.8; p < 0.001). Hospital stay was shortest in the OT group (2.8 ± 1.7 days), intermediate in the TORT group (3.3 ± 0.7), and longest in the TOETVA group (4.0 ± 1.9; p < 0.001). Operation-day pain was lowest in the OT group (2.6 ± 0.5), intermediate in the TORT group (3.2 ± 0.9), and highest in the TOETVA group (3.8 ± 0.9; p < 0.001). Transient vocal cord palsy occurred least frequently in the TORT group (1.6%), followed by the TOETVA (7.3%) and OT (8.1%) groups (p = 0.056). The rates of permanent palsy and hypocalcemia were similar between the groups. The operative time was significantly longer in the TORT group than in the OT or TOETVA groups. Thyroglobulin levels and RAI parameters did not differ significantly between the groups.ConclusionsBoth transoral approaches were safe and feasible. TORT achieved oncologic adequacy comparable to that of OT while providing scarless benefits despite prolonged operative time. TOETVA was feasible but was associated with a low LN yield. The choice of the surgical approach should be individualized according to oncological adequacy, recovery, and cosmetic preferences.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-025-04110-8.Keywords: Papillary thyroid cancer, Thyroidectomy, Propensity score, Operative time, Transoral thyroidectomy

Background

Conventional open thyroidectomy (OT) remains the reference standard for papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) owing to its reproducible oncological outcomes and procedural efficiency. However, cervical scarring can be a cosmetic concern, particularly in younger patients [1]. The development of natural-orifice approaches has enabled scarless access to the central neck. The transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy vestibular approach (TOETVA) was first reported in humans by Witzel et al. in 2008 [1] and was subsequently popularized by Anuwong, who demonstrated its feasibility and safety in a large series [2]. Shortly thereafter, Udelsman et al. described the first North American case, which further supported its clinical applicability [3].

Robotic systems have extended the capabilities of transoral surgeries. Kim et al. reported an initial series of transoral robotic thyroidectomy (TORT), highlighting technical advantages such as enhanced visualization and multi-articulated instrumentation [4]. Subsequent reports have refined the indications, techniques, and short-term outcomes of TOETVA [5, 6], and Dionigi et al. and Russell et al. emphasized both the rationale and limitations of the approach [7–9]. International experiences, including those from Europe and Asia, have confirmed reproducibility across diverse practice environments [10–12].

Several comparative studies have evaluated transoral approaches against OT. You et al. compared TORT and OT for thyroid malignancies and demonstrated comparable safety [13], with a subsequent propensity score-matched analysis confirming its feasibility [14]. Tae et al. reported early institutional and multicenter experiences [15, 16]. Razavi et al. compared the early outcomes of robotic and endoscopic techniques [17]. Recent propensity score-matched studies by Chen et al. and Lee et al. suggested that TORT may achieve higher lymph node (LN) yields than TOETVA [18, 19]. Ha et al. highlighted the differences in pain, swallowing, and voice changes between open, endoscopic, and robotic procedures [20]. Lee et al. analyzed over 200 transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy cases [21], whereas Park et al. reported a large institutional series underscoring the expanding adoption of transoral robotic surgery [22]. A recent systematic review by Akritidou et al. synthesized the complication profiles of TOETVA, emphasizing the risks of mental nerve injury and chin numbness that are unique to this approach [23].

Collectively, these findings support the feasibility of transoral thyroidectomy. However, most prior investigations have been limited to pairwise comparisons (OT vs. TOETVA, OT vs. TORT, or TOETVA vs. TORT) or small single-institutional series; direct three-arm evaluations remain scarce. Moreover, baseline differences in age, sex, body mass index (BMI), and tumor characteristics complicate the interpretation and may bias the outcomes.

In our previous study, we analyzed 240 patients treated with TOETVA or TORT between 2016 and 2022 [21] but did not include OT, and the study was restricted to a two-arm comparison. To address these limitations, we expanded the cohort to include OT, increased the sample size, and applied propensity score matching (PSM) to enable a balanced three-arm comparison. We prespecified the central compartment LN yield as the primary endpoint, with postoperative pain, hospital stay, complications, operative time, and thyroglobulin (Tg)/radioactive iodine (RAI) outcomes as secondary endpoints. We hypothesized that TORT achieves oncologic adequacy comparable to that of OT, whereas TOETVA yields few retrieved LNs and that perioperative recovery profiles differ across approaches.

Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This retrospective, single-surgeon cohort study included patients with PTC who underwent OT, TOETVA, or TORT between November 2016 and December 2023. All surgeries were performed by the same surgeon, who was experienced in conventional, endoscopic, and robotic thyroidectomy. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Sungkyunkwan University Samsung Changwon Hospital (IRB No. SCMC 2022-09−006), and the requirement for informed consent was waived owing to the retrospective design.

Patients and eligibility criteria

Patients with cytologically or histologically confirmed PTC were eligible for inclusion. Transoral thyroidectomy was generally offered to patients with tumors < 2 cm and without radiological evidence of gross extrathyroidal invasion or bulky central LN metastasis. Contraindications included tumors > 4 cm, upper pole lesions at risk of incomplete resection, extensive vascular engorgement, and patient reluctance. The presence of thyroiditis, Graves’ disease, or limited central LN metastasis did not preclude transoral surgery if technically feasible. The extent of thyroidectomy (lobectomy vs. total thyroidectomy) was determined based on tumor characteristics and surgeon–patient discussions. Prophylactic ipsilateral central compartment neck dissection was routinely performed in lobectomy cases, except for tumors < 0.5 cm.

Surgical procedures

OT was performed using a conventional low-collar incision. TOETVA and TORT employed three oral vestibular incisions for midline access with CO₂ insufflation (5–6 mmHg). TORT additionally used a right axillary port for countertraction and specimen retrieval and utilized multi-articulated robotic instruments for console dissection. Perioperative management included prophylactic antibiotics, early oral intake, standardized analgesia, and postoperative laryngoscopic evaluation of vocal cord function.

Outcome measures

The primary endpoint was the number of retrieved central compartment LNs. The secondary outcomes were as follows:

incidence of transient and permanent recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) palsy,

incidence of transient and permanent recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) palsy,

transient and permanent hypocalcemia,

transient and permanent hypocalcemia,

other complications (bleeding, infection, seroma, chylous fistula, skin changes, chin numbness),

other complications (bleeding, infection, seroma, chylous fistula, skin changes, chin numbness),

operative time (total and subdivided into working-space creation and endoscopic or robotic console time),

operative time (total and subdivided into working-space creation and endoscopic or robotic console time),

operation-day pain (numeric rating scale),

operation-day pain (numeric rating scale),

length of postoperative hospital stay, and.

length of postoperative hospital stay, and.

oncologic adequacy assessed using Tg levels and RAI parameters in patients who underwent total thyroidectomy.

oncologic adequacy assessed using Tg levels and RAI parameters in patients who underwent total thyroidectomy.

Definitions

Transient RLN was defined as vocal cord dysfunction that recovered within 6 months, whereas permanent palsy was defined as dysfunction persisting for > 6 months. Hypocalcemia was defined as a parathyroid hormone level < 15 pg/mL, serum calcium < 8.0 mg/dL, or symptomatic hypocalcemia requiring supplementation; persistence for > 6 months was considered permanent. Chin numbness lasting > 6 months was considered a permanent sensory change.

Statistical analysis

Analyses were conducted using Stata software (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA). Propensity scores were estimated using logistic regression based on 10 baseline clinicopathological variables: age, sex, BMI, extent of thyroidectomy, tumor size, extrathyroidal extension, thyroiditis, multifocality, bilaterality, and extent of central compartment neck dissection. One-to-one-to-one nearest-neighbor matching was performed using a caliper width of 0.2. Group balance was evaluated using standardized mean differences (SMDs), with an SMD < 0.2 considered acceptable. Continuous variables were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test or Kruskal–Wallis test, and categorical variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test or the chi-square test, as appropriate. The Bonferroni correction was applied for multiple comparisons. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Before PSM

A total of 819 patients underwent thyroidectomy for PTC, distributed as follows: OT, n = 559; TOETVA, n = 128; and TORT, n = 132. Before matching, significant baseline differences were observed among the three groups in terms of age, sex distribution, BMI, tumor size, bilaterality, and extent of surgery. For instance, patients in the transoral groups were generally younger, more often female, and had smaller tumors with fewer bilateral procedures than those in the OT group. Additionally, TOETVA was associated with fewer retrieved central LNs and longer operative times. Postoperative hospital stay and pain were also greater in the transoral groups, although most complication rates were comparable across the groups. Detailed clinical characteristics, perioperative outcomes, operative times, and RAI parameters before PSM are provided in Supplementary Tables S1–S4 (Additional Files 1–4).

Patient cohort after PSM

After matching, three well-balanced cohorts of 124 patients each were generated (Fig. 1). Post-matching baseline characteristics were comparable across the groups, with SMD < 0.2 for all variables (Table 1), confirming adequate balance for meaningful comparison.

Operative time

The total operative time was shortest in the OT group (59.9 ± 18.6 min), followed by the TOETVA (139.6 ± 31.8 min) and TORT (180.1 ± 40.1 min) groups (p < 0.001; Table 2). Subgroup analyses showed a similar trend for both lobectomy and total thyroidectomy, with TORT consistently requiring the longest working-space creation and console times (Supplementary Table S6, Additional File 5).

Hospital stay and postoperative pain

Hospital stay varied significantly, being shortest in the OT group (2.8 ± 1.7 days), intermediate in the TORT group (3.3 ± 0.7 days), and longest in the TOETVA group (4.0 ± 1.9 days; p < 0.001; Table 1). Postoperative pain on the operation day followed a similar trend, with mean numeric rating scale scores of 2.6 ± 0.5, 3.2 ± 0.9, and 3.8 ± 0.9 in the OT, TORT, and TOETVA groups, respectively (p < 0.001; Table 3). Pairwise Bonferroni-adjusted comparisons confirmed significant differences among the groups.

Complications

Transient RLN palsy occurred least frequently in the TORT group (1.6%), followed by the TOETVA (7.3%) and OT (8.1%) groups; however, this difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.056). Permanent RLN palsy was rare and comparable among the groups (OT, 2.4%; TOETVA, 0%; TORT, 0%). The rates of transient and permanent hypocalcemia were low and did not differ significantly across groups. Chin numbness was observed in 4.0% and 1.6% of patients who underwent TOETVA and TORT, respectively, but was absent in the OT group. All cases of chin numbness were transient. Other complications, including bleeding, infection, and seroma, were uncommon and evenly distributed (Table 3).

Central LN yield

The number of retrieved central compartment LNs differed significantly among the groups (Table 1). However, despite variations in the total number of retrieved nodes, the number of metastatic nodes and LN ratios were comparable across groups, indicating that oncologic completeness was maintained in all surgical approaches.

Oncologic surveillance

RAI parameters and thyroglobulin levels were comparable among the three groups (Table 4). No significant differences were observed in either stimulated or suppressed Tg levels. During a median follow-up period of 64 months (interquartile range, 42–84 months), no structural recurrence was detected in any cohort.

Discussion

This propensity score-matched analysis compared the surgical and oncologic outcomes of OT, TOETVA, and TORT for PTC. TORT achieved central LN yields comparable to those of OT despite being scarless, which supports its oncologic adequacy in selected patients. In contrast, TOETVA yielded significantly fewer LNs than TORT or OT, a finding that aligns with that of prior reports [4–6, 18, 19]. Although the number of retrieved nodes differed, the number of metastatic nodes and LN ratios were similar across the groups, suggesting that oncologic completeness can be maintained in appropriately selected patients.

In Western practice, prophylactic central neck dissection (CND) is generally not performed for low-risk papillary thyroid carcinoma. However, in our cohort, prophylactic ipsilateral CND was routinely performed according to institutional practice, allowing direct comparison of each approach’s technical feasibility in central dissection. Under these conditions, TORT achieved lymph node yields comparable to OT, whereas TOETVA appeared more limited in enabling comprehensive nodal retrieval.

Perioperative recovery also differed between the approaches. OT was the most efficient method, with short operative time, minimal pain, and early discharge. TORT required longer operative time than OT but provided a more favorable recovery profile than TOETVA. TOETVA was associated with the longest hospital stay and highest pain scores, which likely reflect the technical demands of working-space creation through the oral vestibule. Although these procedures are considered ‘scarless,’ they are not truly less invasive at the tissue level. Transoral and robotic approaches require wider subplatysmal flap dissection and longer tunneling from remote access, which can increase postoperative discomfort despite the absence of a visible neck incision.

TORT demonstrated the lowest rate of transient RLN palsy, although the difference was not statistically significant. The enhanced visualization and dexterity of robotic instruments may have contributed to this favorable trend. The permanent RLN palsy and hypocalcemia rates were low and comparable across all groups. Chin numbness, which is unique to transoral approaches, was observed more frequently in the TOETVA and TORT groups than in the OT group but resolved spontaneously. These findings are consistent with reports showing that complication rates decrease as surgeons progress along the learning curve [24, 25].

Recent literature has highlighted advances in perioperative evaluation and complication management in thyroid surgery. In particular, transcutaneous laryngeal ultrasonography has emerged as a reliable, non-invasive method for assessing vocal cord motility, thereby improving postoperative monitoring of RLN function [26]. Moreover, recent reviews have further discussed lymph node dissection strategies and complication profiles in thyroid cancer surgery [27, 28].

From an oncologic surveillance perspective, Tg levels and RAI parameters did not differ significantly between the three groups. These data reinforce the oncological safety of both transoral approaches when patient selection and surgical expertise are appropriate. The absence of recurrence during the available follow-up period further supports this conclusion, although prolonged observation periods are required to confirm durability.

Another important consideration is the cost. Previous studies have shown that TOETVA incurs approximately 28–40% higher operative costs than OT because of additional instruments, whereas TORT involves even greater expenses related to robotic platforms [29–31]. These differences highlight the need to balance oncological safety and cosmetic benefits with economic feasibility, particularly in resource-limited settings. The relatively longer hospital stay compared with Western reports may be partly explained by differences in perioperative protocols. In many Western centers, thyroidectomy is performed as an outpatient or same-day procedure [32], whereas at our institution, patients are typically admitted the day before surgery and discharged on postoperative day 1.

The clinical implication of these findings is that OT remains the most efficient and reliable approach for improving oncological completeness and perioperative outcomes. However, TORT represents a valid scarless alternative with oncologic adequacy comparable to that of OT, making it a reasonable choice for patients who strongly value cosmetic results and are willing to accept prolonged operative times and high costs. TOETVA may be considered for carefully selected patients with small, low-risk tumors but appears to be limited in central LN dissection. Surgeon experience and learning curve should be considered during patient counseling, as early cases are associated with prolonged operative times and potentially high risks of complications.

This study has several limitations. First, this was a retrospective study reflecting the experience of a single surgeon, which provided consistency in surgical technique and perioperative management but limited the generalizability of the results. Although PSM balanced the baseline characteristics, residual confounding factors could not be excluded. The follow-up period was relatively short, and the small number of patients who underwent total thyroidectomy restricted the conclusions regarding the long-term recurrence risk. In addition, the proportion of patients overlapped with that in our previous report [19]; however, in this analysis, we uniquely incorporated OT, expanded the cohort, and applied a balanced three-arm PSM, thereby providing novel insights. Notably, following the adoption of the da Vinci SP system at our institution, the number of single-incision transaxillary cases increased, whereas the number of transoral cases correspondingly declined. Although the study period was extended, this shift in surgical practice limited the additional accrual of transoral cases.

Conclusion

Both transoral approaches were safe and feasible in carefully selected patients with small, low-risk papillary thyroid carcinoma. TORT achieved oncologic adequacy comparable to that of OT while providing scarless benefits, although its use should remain limited to appropriately selected cases. OT provides the most efficient recovery, whereas TORT offers a scarless option with an acceptable safety profile. Future research should focus on longer follow-up periods, multicenter data, and further investigation of the learning curve to refine patient selection and optimize outcomes.

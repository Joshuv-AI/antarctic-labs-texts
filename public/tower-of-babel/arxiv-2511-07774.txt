ON THE REALIZABILITY OF PRIME CONJECTURES IN HEYTING ARITHMETIC
Milan Rosko

Abstract
We show that no total functional can uniformly transform Π 1 \Pi_{1} primality into explicit Σ 1 \Sigma_{1} witnesses without violating normalization in 𝖧𝖠 \mathsf{HA} . The argument proceeds through three complementary translations: a geometric interpretation in which compositeness and primality correspond to local and global packing configurations; a proof-theoretic analysis demonstrating the impossibility of uniform Σ 1 \Sigma_{1} extraction; and a recursion-theoretic formulation linking these constraints to the absence of total Skolem functions in 𝖯𝖠 \mathsf{PA} . The formal analysis in constructive logic is followed by heuristic remarks interpreting the results in informational terms. Keywords: Proof Theory, Constructive Logic, Realizability; Heyting Arithmetic; Goldbach’s Conjecture; Geometric Logic MSC: 03F55, 03F03, 11A41, 68Q05, 11U05

1 Introduction

Let mathematics correspond to the greatest rigor
after
assumption; then logic will be the rigor
before
assumption. We examine the boundary where the two intersect. Classical arithmetic proceeds as though its objects—numbers, primes, and sums—are already well formed, whereas logic asks whether the rules that make such objects definable can themselves support constructive realization. The contrast becomes evident in statements such as Goldbach’s:

“Every integer that can be written as the sum of two primes can also be written as the sum of as many primes (including unity) as one wishes, until all terms are units.”

Goldbach 1742
, in a letter to Leonhard Euler.

Our analysis shows that any uniform construction transforming an even input into explicit prime summands would conflict with the normalization theorems of constructive proof theory rather than with any property of the primes themselves. It concerns the structure of realizability in arithmetic, not the distribution of prime numbers.

Predicting the decomposition of cuboids would effectively provide an

“combinatorial entropy”

The distinction between additive and multiplicative reasoning marks a precise logical frontier. To demonstrate
“compositeness”
is to exhibit explicit factors, a
\Sigma_{1}
act. To demonstrate
“primality”
is to assert the universal absence of nontrivial factors, a
\Pi_{1}
claim.

Roadmap.

establishes the constructive framework, defining the logical and semantic background within
HA
and the
BHK
interpretation of
“proofs as realizers.”

Section
3
develops the geometric translation of arithmetic predicates, where compositeness and primality are represented as finite configurations, exposing the
\Sigma_{1}/\Pi_{1}
asymmetry in local versus global definability.

Section
4
derives the corresponding logical barriers within
HA
.

Section

2 Preliminaries

2.1 Semantics

Definition 2.1

(Base Theory)
.

We consider first-order
Heyting Arithmetic
(
\mathsf{HA}
); see
Heyting 1930
;
Troelstra 1973
;
Beeson 1985
;
Buss 1998
;
Hájek and Pudlák 1998
for details on
\mathsf{PA}
’s strictly weaker intuitionistic counterpart. Our language

\mathcal{L}_{A}=\{0,S,+,\cdot,<\}

(2.1)

is governed by constructive logic and includes full induction for all formulas of
\mathcal{L}_{A}
. Connectives are interpreted under the
BHK
semantics, following
Brouwer 1975
;
Heyting 1930
;
Kolmogorov 1925
. In brief, the meaning of a formula is given by the construction that realizes it.

Definition 2.2

(Proof Semantics)
.

Proofs correspond to explicit constructions (realizers):

\begin{array}[]{@{}ll@{}}\bot&\text{has no realizer},\\[4.0pt]
P\wedge Q&\text{realized by }\langle a,b\rangle\text{ with }a\Vdash P,\ b\Vdash Q,\\[4.0pt]
P\vee Q&\text{realized by }\langle 0,a\rangle\text{ or }\langle 1,b\rangle,\\[4.0pt]
P\to Q&\text{realized by a total operation from }P\text{ to }Q,\\[4.0pt]
(\exists x)\,P(x)&\text{realized by }\langle x,a\rangle\text{ with }a\Vdash P(x),\\[4.0pt]
(\forall x)\,P(x)&\text{realized by a total operator assigning }x\text{ to }P(x).\end{array}

(2.2)

Thus, a proof in
HA
denotes a total, strongly normalizing operation transforming realizers into realizers; see
Troelstra and van Dalen 1988
;
Beeson 1985
.

2.2 Thesis

Example 2.3
.

Recall the
Arithmetic Hierarchy
of
Kleene 1943
. It classifies subsets of
\mathbb{N}
and arithmetical formulas according to the alternation of quantifiers in their definitions.

1.

\Delta^{0}_{0}
(also
\Sigma^{0}_{0}=\Pi^{0}_{0}
): formulas containing only bounded quantifiers.

\varphi(x)\;:\;\exists y\leq x\;(y^{2}=x)

(2.3)

expresses
“
x
is a perfect square”
and is
\Delta^{0}_{0}
.

2.

\Sigma^{0}_{1}
: formulas beginning with an unbounded existential quantifier followed by a bounded (decidable) predicate.

\varphi(x)\;:\;\exists y\;T(x,y)

(2.4)

where
T(x,y)
is a decidable relation (e.g.,
“
y
encodes a halting computation of program
x
”
). Example:
“Program
x
halts”
is
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
.

3.

\Pi^{0}_{1}
: universal closure of a decidable predicate.

\psi(x)\;:\;\forall y\;P(x,y)

(2.5)

with
P
decidable. Examples:
“Program
x
never halts”
or
“The sequence generated by
x
is total”
are
\Pi^{0}_{1}
.

4.

\Sigma^{0}_{n+1}
and
\Pi^{0}_{n+1}
are obtained by quantifier alternation:

\displaystyle\Sigma^{0}_{n+1}:\exists x_{1}\forall x_{2}\exists x_{3}\cdots\varphi_{0}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Pi^{0}_{n+1}:\forall x_{1}\exists x_{2}\forall x_{3}\cdots\varphi_{0}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),

where
\varphi_{0}
is bounded (
\Delta^{0}_{0}
).

Thesis 2.4

(Realizability Barrier)
.

No total primitive recursive functional
E
can uniformly transform
\Pi_{1}
predicates—i.e., elements of
\PrimePi
—into explicit
\Sigma_{1}
witnesses within
HA
. Equivalently, no uniform
\Sigma_{1}
totalizer exists for any nontrivial or combinatorial
\Pi_{1}
property, as shown by
Kleene 1952
;
Friedman 1975
. This constraint remains invariant under any external extension, owing to syntactic parsimony.

2.3 Operators

Definition 2.5

(Basic Predicates)
.

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the natural numbers with successor
S
, addition, and multiplication. The following relations are definable in the language of

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Even(n)&\iff\exists k\,[n=2\cdot k],\\[3.0pt]

\end{array}

(2.7)

Here,
\Even(n)
,
\Odd(n)
, and
\mathsf{Comp}(n)
are
\Sigma_{1}
, while
\PrimePi(n)
is
\Pi_{1}
. This classification reflects the constructive asymmetry between witness-producing and universally quantified statements.

Remark
.

Under
Markov’s Principle
,
\neg\neg\mathsf{Comp}(n)\to\mathsf{Comp}(n)
, but this principle is not derivable in
HA
. Consequently,
\PrimePi(n)
is not constructively decidable, even though it is arithmetically
\Pi^{0}_{1}
in
PA
.

2.4 Constructive Translation

Definition 2.6

(Constructive Goldbach’s Principle)
.

Consider a constructive variant of
Goldbach’s Binary Conjecture
,
The Strong Goldbach’s Conjecture
:

\mathrm{GC}^{*}_{\Pi}:\quad\forall n\Big(\Even(n)\Rightarrow\exists p,q\leq n\,[\PrimePi(p)\wedge\PrimePi(q)\wedge p+q=n]\Big).

(2.8)

This statement combines
\Sigma_{1}
witnesses
(p,q)
with
\Pi_{1}
side-conditions
(\PrimePi(p),\PrimePi(q))
. A constructive proof of (
2.8
) in
HA
would yield, via the
Curry–Howard Correspondence
, a total primitive-recursive operator

E:\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}^{5},\quad E(n)=\langle p,q,a,b,c\rangle,

(2.9)

where
a,b
realize the predicates
\PrimePi(p),\PrimePi(q)
, and
c
realizes
p+q=n
. Such an operator would act as a uniform
\Sigma_{1}
extractor for
\Pi_{1}
evidence, contradicting the strong normalization of
HA
proofs, see
[
Kreisel 1951
]
.

3 Geometric Logic

3.1 Topological Semantics

Figure 2:
A
2\times 2
rectangular packing realizing
\mathsf{Comp}(4)

4
explicit (blue) within the grid semantics of
\mathcal{G}

Exposition
.

Classical
Gödel Completeness
—that every consistent first-order theory has a model,
[
Gödel 1930
]
—is not provable according to
BHK
, since it requires nonconstructive principles, e.g.,
Compactness
. However, a constructive analogue holds for
“geometric logic”
, whose semantics are stable under
“pullback”
along morphisms.

Lemma 3.1

(Constructive Completeness)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a geometric theory and
\sigma
a sequent. By
[
Fourman and Scott 1979
]
,

\mathcal{G}\vdash\sigma\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall\mathcal{E}\in\mathsf{Topos},\;\forall\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{G}),\;\mathcal{M}\models\sigma.

(3.10)

In particular, taking
\mathcal{E}=\mathbf{Set}
gives soundness and completeness with respect to ordinary (set-based) models.

Lemma 3.2

(Geometric Reduction Principle)
.

For any geometric theory
\mathcal{G}
and geometric sequent
\sigma
,

\models_{\mathrm{G}}\sigma\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\models_{\mathsf{L}}\sigma

(3.11)

for every logic
\mathsf{L}
whose semantics are
reducible
to geometric semantics.

Exposition
.

Hence,
“truth in geometric logic”
coincides with
“truth in any logic admitting reducible semantics”
. This constructively preserves the essence of
Completeness
: provability in a geometric system exactly matches validity in all (classically adequate) semantic frameworks.

Definition 3.3
.

Constructive arithmetic will be interpreted within a first-order predicative geometry
\mathcal{G}
in the sense of
Tarski 1954
. The language of
\mathcal{G}
is governed by axioms of incidence, betweenness, and congruence:

\displaystyle\text{(I1)}\;\forall x,y\,\exists z\,[B(x,z,y)\vee B(y,z,x)],

(3.12)

\displaystyle\text{(C1)}\;E(x,y,x,y),

(3.13)

\displaystyle\text{(C2)}\;E(x,y,u,v)\!\Rightarrow\!E(y,x,v,u),

(3.14)

\displaystyle\text{(T)}\;B(x,y,z)\wedge B(y,z,u)\!\Rightarrow\!B(x,y,u).

(3.15)

Arithmetic predicates are represented by finite configurations: addition corresponds to segment concatenation, multiplication to rectangular composition, and equality to congruence.

Definition 3.4

(Geometric Semantics)
.

For terms
a,b,n\in\mathbb{N}
let their geometric counterparts be finite sets
\Gamma(a)
,
\Gamma(b)
,
\Gamma(n)
satisfying

\displaystyle\Rect(a,b;n)\iff\exists P,Q,R,S\,\bigl[

\displaystyle B(P,Q,R)\wedge B(Q,R,S)\wedge{}

(3.16)

\displaystyle E(P,Q,R,S)\wedge\operatorname{A}(PQRS)=n\bigr].

Thus
\mathsf{Comp}(n)
asserts the existence of such a rectangle, and
\PrimePi(n)
its universal absence.

Exposition
.

The realizability barrier corresponds both to the hardness of packability in geometric and to the absence of any uniform algorithm producing prime witnesses for all even integers.
Under realizability semantics,

\textsf{Provable in {HA}}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\textsf{Realizable under BHK}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\textsf{Representable in $\mathcal{G}.$}

The model
\mathcal{G}
denotes a constructive grid semantics in which bounded formulas
\phi(x)\in\Delta_{0}
correspond to finite configurations
\Gamma_{\phi}(x)
, see
[
Tarski 1954
]
. Existential (
\Sigma_{1}
) formulas describe the existence of such configurations; universal (
\Pi_{1}
) formulas assert that no configuration of the prescribed form exists. Arithmetic, logic, and geometry thereby appear as distinct projections of the same constructive semantics.

Definition 3.5

(Geometric Encoding)
.

For
n>1
define

\mathsf{Comp}(n)\iff\exists a,b>1\,\Rect(a,b;n),

(3.17)

\PrimePi(n)\iff n>1\wedge\neg\mathsf{Comp}(n),

(3.18)

where
\Rect(a,b;n)
expresses the existence of a finite

tiling of total area
n
within
\mathcal{G}
. The predicate
\Rect(a,b;n)
is
\Delta_{0}
and locally decidable; its existence provides a constructive witness of compositeness, see
Figure
2
. Conversely,
\PrimePi(n)
expresses the universal failure of such packings.

4 Core Logic

4.1 Packing Turing Machine as Constructive Realizer

Exposition
.

We formalize the
“packing”
semantics, e.g., as in
Figure
3
, of
\mathsf{Comp}(n)
by an explicit deterministic
Turing Machine
that halts on composite inputs and diverges on primes.

Logic 4.1
.

Let

M=(Q,\Sigma,\Gamma,\delta,q_{0},q_{\mathrm{acc}},q_{\mathrm{rej}})

(4.21)

be a deterministic
“three-tape”

Turing Machine
operating as follows:

1.

Function.

(a)

Input:
unary
1^{n}
on Tape 1.

(b)

Output:

1^{a}\#1^{b}
on Tape 3 if
n=ab
with
a,b\geq 2
.

(c)

Behavior:

M
halts iff
n
is composite.

2.

Structure.
Tapes maintain the invariant forms

\text{Tape 1: }1^{n},\quad\text{Tape 2: }L\,A^{a}\,C\,a^{a}\,\$,\quad\text{Tape 3: }B^{b},

(4.22)

where
A
encodes the current divisor candidate and
a
its working copy. Each iteration tests divisibility by successive
a\geq 2
via block-subtraction on Tape 1. When a full partition is found, one
B
is appended per completed row, producing
n=ab
.

3.

Transition scheme.
The transition function
\delta
is the finite union of routines:

(a)

Row count:
consume
a
symbols from Tape 1; if the block fits exactly, append one
B
and reload the working zone.

(b)

Completion:
if the scan exhausts
1^{n}
with at least two rows, emit
1^{a}\#1^{b}
and accept.

(c)

Failure:
if a remainder remains, increment
a
by one and restart.

Undefined state pairs go to
q_{\mathrm{rej}}
to keep
\delta
total.

4.

Correctness.

•

Soundness:
Acceptance implies
n=ab
with
a,b\geq 2
.

•

Completeness:
For any composite
n
, some
a
satisfies the halting condition.

•

\Sigma_{1}
behavior:
For prime
n
, no
a
divides
n
; the process diverges.

5.

Complexity.
For accepting inputs,
M
performs
O(n)
head moves and halts in linear time. Hence
M
constitutes an explicit
\Sigma_{1}
realizer for
\mathsf{Comp}(n)
, concretely implementing the geometric packing semantics in finite mechanical form.

Definition 4.2

(Classical and Constructive Primality)
.

\Prime(n)\iff(n>1)\wedge\forall a,b\!\leq\!n\,[a\!\cdot\!b=n\Rightarrow(a=1\vee b=1)],

(4.23)

\Prime
is a
\Pi^{0}_{1}

;

is the corresponding
\Pi_{1}

Definition 4.3

(Shape-Bounded Schema)
.

A finite geometric schema

encodes a bounded compositional rule:

\mathsf{Comp}_{d}(n)\;\equiv\;\exists a,b\!\leq\!n\,[d(a,b,n)\wedge a,b>1\wedge a\!\cdot\!b=n]

(4.24)

\Prime_{d}(n)\;\equiv\;n>1\wedge\neg\mathsf{Comp}_{d}(n).

(4.25)

The schema is governed by a

\forall n,a,b\!\leq\!n\,[d(a,b,n)\Rightarrow a\!\leq\!B(n)\ \vee\ b\!\leq\!B(n)].

Lemma 4.4

(Finite Omission)
.

If
B(n)<\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
for infinitely many

, then

\forall d\,(\text{finite shape-bounded})\ \exists^{\infty}N\,[\mathsf{Comp}(N)\wedge\neg\mathsf{Comp}_{d}(N)].

A divisor

. Infinitely many

eventually exceeds

, the schema ceases to be finite: the verification cost of all potential

pairs grows super-arithmetically. Thus no finite

;
Friedman 1976
;
Beeson 1985
.
∎

Exposition
.

This quantitative limit mirrors a logical one: finite geometric omission corresponds to the failure of any uniform
\Sigma_{1}
selector for
\Pi_{1}
evidence. The geometric and logical barriers coincide under the

, see
[
Howard 1980
]

4.3 Reflection Principle

Proposition 4.5

(Non-Uniform Extraction)
.

By
[
Friedman 1975
;
Beeson 1985
]
, let
\mathsf{R}(n,y)
be a
\Sigma_{1}
relation whose parameters include a
\Pi_{1}
premise. No total primitive-recursive function

satisfies

\mathsf{HA}\vdash\forall n\,(\exists y\,\mathsf{R}(n,y)\Rightarrow\mathsf{R}(n,F(n))).

(4.28)

Exposition
.

Any such
F
would be a total
\Sigma_{1}
extractor for
\Pi_{1}
evidence, contradicting normalization in
\mathsf{HA}
. Bounded geometric omission in
Lemma
4.4
is the semantic image of this logical non-uniformity.

Lemma 4.6

(Diagonal Barrier)
.

By
[
Kleene 1952
;
Howard 1980
]
, Assume a
\Sigma_{1}
predicate
\Dec(e,n,m)
decides
\PrimePi(m)
. By the

, there exists
n_{e}
such that

\PrimePi(n_{e})\leftrightarrow\neg\Dec(e,n_{e},n_{e}),

(4.29)

implying,

\mathsf{HA}\vdash\bot,

(4.30)

Remark
.

This diagonal argument parallels
Rice 1953
: no total computable operator decides a nontrivial semantic property of partial functions. In
\mathsf{HA}
,
“
m
is prime”
plays that semantic role.

Proposition 4.7

(Reflection Principle)
.

For
\mathrm{GC}^{*}_{\Pi}
, either there exists a least counterexample or a uniform witness extractor
E
exists. Since no such total
E
can exist, the obstruction is logical:

(\neg\mathrm{GC}^{*}_{\Pi})\Rightarrow\exists n_{0}\,(\neg\varphi(n_{0})\wedge\forall m<n_{0}\,\varphi(m)).

(4.31)

Exposition
.

This reflection isolates
\mathrm{GC}^{*}_{\Pi}
on the boundary between constructive provability and arithmetical truth: the existence of a minimal counterexample is
\Sigma_{1}
, while uniform witness extraction is forbidden by the non-uniformization results above.

Definition 4.8

(Additive Convolution)
.

For
C\subseteq\mathbb{N}
define

(C\star C)(n)\;\equiv\;\exists p,q\!\leq\!n\,[C(p)\wedge C(q)\wedge p+q=n],

(4.32)

a
\Sigma_{1}
predicate describing additive closure.

Theorem 4.9

(Exactness)
.

If
C\subseteq\PrimePi
and

\forall n,(\Even(n)\Rightarrow(C\star C)(n)),

(4.33)

with
C

\Pi_{1}
-minimal among such sets, then
C(x)\Leftrightarrow\PrimePi(x)
.

Proof Sketch.

Work in
HA
. The inclusion
C\subseteq\PrimePi
is given. For the converse, assume
\PrimePi(p)
and
p\notin C
. Consider the
\Pi_{1}
set

C^{\prime}\equiv{x:\ C(x)\ \vee\ x=p}.

(4.34)

Since
“
x=p
”
is
\Delta_{0}
and
\PrimePi(x)
is
\Pi_{1}
, the predicate
C^{\prime}
is
\Pi_{1}
, satisfies

, and still has total even coverage because

5 Logical Induction

5.1 Inductive Inference and Semantic Reduction

Exposition
.

We simulate classical logic within our constructive framework by inductive reasoning, examining structural correspondences. We appeal to the

Solomonoff 1964a

\mathsf{Comp}(n)\;:\;\exists a,b>1\,[a\!\cdot\!b=n],\quad\PrimePi(n)\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\neg\mathsf{Comp}(n).

This correspondence preserves full logical content while eliminating extraneous ontological assumptions. We demonstrate two cases: First, primality can be characterized as a fragment of the

5.4 Euler Product from Fixed-Point

Exposition
.

If the property of primality depends solely on an ontological postulate, it can be reproduced (incompletely) without such an assumption by syntactic simulation within a constructive formal system. Conversely, if primality manifests intrinsic impredicativity—its definition referring implicitly to the totality of potential factors—then it can be encoded (completely) as a distributed fixed point of constructive operators by
Bekić’s Lemma

[
Jones and Bekić 1984
]
. Within this formulation, the
Euler Product
arises not as an analytic artifact but as the diagonal solution of a purely logical recursion, derived in
fixed-point logic
without arithmetic premises.

Definition 5.10

(Bekić’s Lemma)
.

Let
(D,\leq)
be a complete lattice and
f,g:D\times D\to D
be monotone functions.
Define

F(x,y)=(f(x,y),g(x,y)).

(5.55)

If the least fixed point
\mu y.\,g(x,y)
exists for each
x
, then the least fixed point of
F
satisfies

\mu(x,y).\,F(x,y)=\bigl(\mu x.\,f(x,\,\mu y.\,g(x,y)),\;\mu y.\,g(\mu x.\,f(x,\,\mu y.\,g(x,y)),y)\bigr).

(5.56)

Bekić’s Lemma
thus decomposes joint least fixed points into nested single-variable ones.

Logic 5.11

(Simulation of the Euler Product)
.

Assume within
HA
that primality is a
\Pi_{1}
predicate lacking any total
\Sigma_{1}
extractor, i.e.,

\neg\exists E\,\forall n\,[\PrimePi(n)\Rightarrow\exists a\leq E(n)\,R(n,a)].

(5.57)

This non-realizability establishes a fixed-point constraint on the constructive operators
R_{\Sigma}
and
R_{\Pi}
:

R_{\Sigma}R_{\Pi}(x)=x\quad\textsf{has no constructive solution in\penalty\ $\mathsf{HA}$.}

(5.58)

Under
Bekić’s Lemma
, the least fixed point of the compositeness operator

\mathsf{Comp}(n)=\mu_{X}\,\exists a,b\,[a,b>1\wedge(a\!\cdot\!b=n\vee X(a)\vee X(b))]

(5.59)

yields a canonical separation of the multiplicative structure into independent prime components. Summation of these decomposed layers produces the definitional identity

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{-s}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{1-p^{-s}}.

(5.60)

Proposition 5.12
.

Within
HA
, let
R_{\Sigma}
and
R_{\Pi}
denote constructive closure under bounded existential and universal search over
\Delta_{0}
formulas.
For a base predicate
\varphi(x)\in\Delta_{0}

R_{\Sigma}(\varphi(x))\equiv\exists y\!\leq\!x\,\varphi(y),\quad R_{\Pi}(\varphi(x))\equiv\forall y\!\leq\!x\,\varphi(y).

(5.61)

Iterating these operators alternately yields a sequence of definitional layers:

\varphi_{n+1}(x)\equiv R_{\Sigma}(R_{\Pi}(\varphi_{n}(x))).

(5.62)

Each iteration introduces one alternation of constructive witnessing and reflective verification.
Let the meta-analytic ratio of successive definitional complexities be denoted by

\frac{\varphi_{n+2}}{\varphi_{n+1}}=1+\frac{1}{\frac{\varphi_{n+1}}{\varphi_{n}}}.

(5.63)

Its fixed point is the constant
\Phi
, which quantifies the equilibrium between constructive expansion (
\Sigma_{1}
search) and reflective contraction (
\Pi_{1}
verification). See
Section
Context
for related discussion.

Thesis 5.13

(
\Phi
-Adic Equilibrium)
.

Consider the fixed-point schema

R_{\Sigma}R_{\Pi}(x)=x.

(5.64)

cannot prove the existence of a solution:

\textsf{HA}\nvdash\exists x\,[R_{\Sigma}R_{\Pi}(x)=x].

(5.65)

This expresses the failure of constructive evidence under normalization. Realization of such a fixed point would require a non-constructive operator; the system thereby enforces its own limit of definability.

Proposition 5.14

(Recursive Depth Law)
.

Let

denote successive bounds arising from alternating

with

(5.66)

Remark
.

The constant
\Phi
quantifies the contraction rate between local constructive computation and global reflective closure. It
“measures”
the ratio at which definitional alternation stabilizes: existential search expands complexity, while universal verification contracts it. Their alternation converges toward the slowest transformation
[

\mathrm{Thm}_{\mathsf{HA}}\subsetneq\mathrm{Thm}_{\mathsf{PA}}\subsetneq\mathrm{True}_{\mathbb{N}}.

Remark
.

Finite geometric schemata capture only bounded multiplicativity. Thus
\mathsf{HA}

\Pi_{1}

\mathsf{PA}

6.3 Criterion

\mathsf{HA}

Exposition
.

The constructive results established in

\forall n\,\big(\Pi_{1}(n)\Rightarrow\exists y\,\Sigma_{1}(n,y)\big),

(6.89)

such that all admissible witnesses

\mathsf{HA}

\Pi_{1}

\textsf{Witnesses}\;\Rightarrow\;\textsf{Realization},\quad\textsf{$\Pi_{1}$ dependence}\;\Rightarrow\;\textsf{Obstruction}.

(6.91)

\Pi_{1}

(p,q)
would thus decide a nontrivial
\Pi_{1}
predicate, violating normalization in
\mathsf{HA}
. Hence
Fermat’s Theorem
remains constructively decidable, while
Goldbach’s Conjecture
constructive form exceeds the realizable limits of
\mathsf{HA}
.

Example 6.7

(Helfgott’s Proof)
.

The
ternary Goldbach conjecture
(every odd
n>5
is a sum of three primes) is provable in classically, following
Helfgott 2013
. Its arithmetical form is
\Pi^{0}_{2}
with a decidable witness domain, hence lies within case (i) of the stratification. It introduces no uniform
\Pi_{1}
extraction and therefore does not violate the constructive barrier.

6.4 Oracle Violation

Definition 6.8

Assume a total primitive–recursive operator

(Definitional Extension)

Extending

\mathsf{PA}(E)

If (

Theorem 6.11

(Oracle Violation)

No such operator

Proof Sketch.

By the

;
Blum 1967
]
, no arithmetical theory extending

∎

Observation
.

A uniform extractor

would act as a logical oracle, erasing the boundary between instance and derivation. The speedup theorem forbids this compression. Constructively, the prohibition manifests as the nonexistence of a total

7 Interpretations

This section offers a speculative interpretation of the constructive and logical results obtained above through a semantic reduction linking realizability, computation, and informational structure.

7.1 Excluded Middle

Heuristic 7.1

(Divisor Instances as LEM)
.

No constructive perspective is complete without discussing the
law of the excluded middle
, or
LEM
.

\textsc{LEM}_{\Gamma}:\quad\forall P\in\Gamma\,[P\vee\neg P].

(7.95)

HA
proves
\textsc{LEM}_{\Delta_{0}}
but not
\textsc{LEM}_{\Sigma_{1}}
or
\mathrm{LEM}_{\Pi_{1}}
.
The predicates

\mathsf{Comp}(n):\exists a,b>1\,[a\!\cdot\!b=n],

(7.96)

\PrimePi(n):\forall a,b\leq n\,[a\!\cdot\!b=n\Rightarrow(a=1\vee b=1)].

(7.97)

illustrate this asymmetry.
\mathsf{Comp}(n)
is
\Sigma_{1}
and finitely decidable;
\PrimePi(n)
is
\Pi_{1}
and constructively undecidable. Without
\mathrm{LEM}_{\Pi_{1}}
,
HA
cannot prove
\mathsf{Comp}(n)\vee\PrimePi(n)
for arbitrary
n
. Any uniform constructor transforming
\Pi_{1}
primality into
\Sigma_{1}
witnesses would constitute a total realization of
\mathrm{LEM}_{\Pi_{1}}
, contradicting normalization in
HA
.

7.2 Primes as Proof of Knowledge

Thesis 7.2

(Rigged System)
.

If a thermodynamic, computational, or informational model contains internally conserved or limitative quantities, then their distribution expresses a constraint of relative consistency rather than a contingent pattern accessible to finite derivation. The quantities conventionally termed
“energy”
,
“information”
, or
“entropy”
correspond to proofs measured against descriptive cost. Any global regularity governing them thus represents a conservation law at the
“meta-level”
; its proof would require transcending the system’s closure, thereby violating the very conservation it asserts.

Observation
.

For example, Quantum computation accelerates instance evaluation but does not transform a given nonconstructive predicate into a realization. Logical non-uniformity persists across such computation, see
[
Abramsky 2004
]
.

Heuristic 7.3

(Constructive Reflection in Quantum Algorithms)
.

For modular exponentiation
f(x)=a^{x}\bmod N
,
determining the order
r
such that

a^{r}\equiv 1\pmod{N}\quad\text{and}\quad\forall k<r\,[a^{k}\not\equiv 1\pmod{N}]

is a mixed
\Pi_{1}
-
\Sigma_{1}
task.
Shor’s algorithm

[
Shor 1994
;
Ekert and Jozsa 1996
]
achieves an instancewise realization by constructing a unitary map

U_{f}:|x\rangle\mapsto|f(x)\rangle

(7.98)

and applying the quantum Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
. Measurement yields one valid witness
r
per run, but no total operator
E:\mathbb{N}\!\to\!\mathbb{N}
valid for all
N
.

Heuristic 7.4

(Entropy–Packing Correspondence)
.

Entropy can be understood as a constructive packing problem: The computational hardness of identifying such minimal states and the thermodynamic cost of reducing entropy are therefore relatively consistent in respect to a shared structural constraint. Let
W_{n}
denote the number of admissible
\Delta_{0}
tilings (finite constructive configurations) of area
n
. Define the combinatorial entropy

S_{n}=k\log W_{n},

(7.99)

in Boltzmann’s sense, where
k
is a normalizing constant. If successive configuration counts satisfy

\frac{W_{n+2}}{W_{n+1}}=1+\frac{1}{\frac{W_{n+1}}{W_{n}}},

(7.100)

then

\frac{S_{n+2}-S_{n+1}}{S_{n+1}-S_{n}}\longrightarrow\Phi,

(7.101)

expressing
\Phi
-proportional growth and minimal informational curvature in the sense of
Jaynes 1957
;
Landauer 1961
.

Exposition
.

(Analogy.) Consider a finite room scattered with boxes of various sizes. A computer is tasked with determining an
optimal packing
configuration. As the algorithm explores permutations, we observe from the outside that each increment in disorder—i.e., each deviation from a constructive arrangement—raises its computational energy cost. Entropy increases with unordering, but so does the cost of realization. Now imagine embedding this simulation within another computer that, in turn, simulates the first. The inner system must entail the minimal cost of fluctuations by necessity, accumulating an additional layer of informational entropy.

Heuristic 7.5

(Harmonic Log and Energy Gradient)
.

Let the potential

H(x)=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p}

(7.102)

measure the aggregate informational weight of primes up to
x
, diverging as

H(x)\sim\log\log x.

(7.103)

Define the local prime–composite ratio

\rho(x)\;=\;\frac{\pi(x)}{x-\pi(x)}\;\approx\;\frac{1}{\ln x-1},

(7.104)

which acts as an
occupation fraction
: the probability that a natural number occupies a
“prime state”
.
The gradient

\frac{d\rho}{dx}\;\approx\;-\frac{1}{x(\ln x-1)^{2}}

(7.105)

then expresses the decay of that occupation with energy
x
, directly analogous to the
Boltzmann Transport Equation

\frac{dn(E)}{dE}\propto-\frac{n(E)}{kT},

(7.106)

where
n(E)
is the particle density over energy
E
.
Thus, the prime distribution behaves as an entropy-limited flow: primes correspond to
“knowledge”
(in a limitative sense), since each prime implicitly enumerates both itself and the composites it generates, while composites merely instantiate that prior structure. Hence, knowing composites conveys only local configurations, but knowing primes entails global knowledge of both classes—an epistemically costlier act.

Heuristic 7.6

(Cost of Knowledge)
.

In any formal system
\mathcal{F}
that encodes
Turing-complete
arithmetic and remains relatively consistent with a stronger system
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
conserving an invariant
Q
, the realization of a new prime
p
(i.e. a new irreducible witness) requires an increase in descriptive or computational cost proportional to its informational deficit:

\mathrm{Cost}_{\mathcal{F}}(p)\;\propto\;-\frac{\partial S_{n}}{\partial n}\Big|_{n=p}\;\approx\;\frac{k}{\ln p}.

(7.107)

Hence primes emerge as
proofs of knowledge
: each encodes compression relative to its compositional background, and their discovery or verification is computationally expensive within any consistent finitary system.

Remark
.

The correspondence is empirically motivated but structural: Once we axiomatize that inference incurs energetic cost, then—without assuming informational substrate—there exist a conserved quantity. In any arithmetical or computational framework subject to a conserved measure of informational entropy, the (non-probabilistic) generation of new prime predicates corresponds to maximal local decrease in entropy and thus maximal proof cost. Primes mark points where the system attains minimal redundancy.

8 Discussion

We conclude with the operational semantics of computation. Primality in computer science is implemented (usually) by bounded search:

\mathsf{Prime}_{d}(n)\;:\;\forall a\leq d(n)\,[a\mid n\Rightarrow(a=1\vee a=n)],

(8.108)

with
d(n)
a fixed bound such as
\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
or a bit-length constraint. Such predicates are
\Delta_{0}
because all quantifiers range over explicit finite domains. Once division is realized by iterative subtraction, modular approximation, or simulated quotient extraction, it becomes a surrogate for a non-constructive relation
[
Cutland 1980
]
. Hence every modern system interprets
\PrimePi(n)
as a bounded, effectively decidable predicate. Testing thereby becomes an evaluation of a finite schema, not a
\Pi_{1}
reflection.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{e}
denote the
e
-th
Turing machine
. For each
\mathcal{M}_{e}
, there exists
p_{e}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{e}
neither halts affirming nor refuting
\PrimePi(p_{e})
:

\forall e\,\exists p_{e}\;\neg\big(\mathcal{M}_{e}(p_{e})\!\downarrow\ \wedge\ (\mathcal{M}_{e}(p_{e})=\text{p}\ \vee\ \mathcal{M}_{e}(p_{e})=\text{c})\big),

(8.109)

a direct consequence of the non-uniformization theorem
[
Friedman 1975
]
and Rice’s diagonal argument
[
Rice 1953
]
.

This computational constraint mirrors the constructive one. The logical asymmetry analyzed throughout—from local tilings to global indivisibility—reappears as a fundamental limit of mechanized arithmetic. Constructive logic, proof theory and computation share a single invariant: there always exists a prime beyond any machine’s operational horizon, owing to the fact that modern computation emulates division by bounded iteration of its subsystems; it never realizes the relation
\operatorname{div}(a,b)
in total form.

\exists(a,b)\;\neg\big(\mathcal{M}_{e}(a,b)\!\downarrow=\operatorname{div}(a,b)\big).

(8.110)

Thus every algorithmic system remains a partial interpreter of arithmetic, not its total executor. Many classical theorems—e.g.,
Euclid’s Theorem
and termination statements for recursive systems—share the form

\forall x\,\exists y\,R(x,y).

(8.111)

Constructively, such claims rely on unbounded meta-quantification. Their truth expresses an
appeal to infinity
: a limit process that no finite computation can exhaust. Proving a
\Pi_{2}
statement does not close the infinite but formalizes its containment within arithmetic:

Each quantifier alternation
\forall x\,\exists y
achieves compression

through the semantics of its own incompleteness.

No
\Pi_{2}
problem has ever been resolved by purely finite means without invoking a transfinite assertion. This transition is not a failure but a trade: resolving obstructive problems under subsumation that preserves consistency under uncertainty entails an approximation of

\forall x\,\exists y\,R(x,y),

(8.112)

that again admits compression by bounded schemas,

\forall x\leq D\,\exists y\leq B(x)\,R(x,y),

(8.113)

where
D
and
B(x)
mark the constructive horizon of verification.

Our result locates the hardness of any prospective proof of
Goldbach’s Conjecture
—and, by extension, the constructive status of primality—within the finitist and predicativist tradition. The obstacle is not primarily analytic or combinatorial but logical: it arises at the boundary of constructive definability, where any attempt to furnish a uniform
\Sigma_{1}
extractor for
\Pi_{1}
predicates encounters the same limitations described in ultra-finitistic and predicative frameworks
[
Friedman 1975
;
Feferman 1964
;
Buss 1998
;
Troelstra and van Dalen 1988
;
Beeson 1985
]
. In this sense, the difficulty of a
“
2n=p_{1}+p_{2}
”
proof exemplifies the general incompleteness of arithmetic when viewed through the lens of constructive realizability.

We close with the central idea: the resolution of future obstructions in mathematics, logic, physics, and computation will not emerge from the isolated treatment of individual problems as dichotomies, but from their systematic integration under a unified arithmetic oracle—an architecture capable of identifying, rather than eliminating, the shared constructive limits that underlie them all:

The last problem is the one whose resolution would render all the others corollaries of its solution.

Weihrauch reducibility between Ramsey-type theorems and well-ordering principles at the level of Σ 2 0 \Sigma^{0}_{2} -induction: A pilot study
Lorenzo Carlucci, Giordano Celli

Abstract
We study the relations under Weihrauch reducibility of the well-ordering preservation principle for the operator X ↦ X ω X\mapsto X^{\omega} and the Ordered Ramsey Theorem. Both principles are known to be equivalent to Σ 2 0 \Sigma^{0}_{2} -induction in Reverse Mathematics. We show that the Ordered Ramsey Theorem is Weihrauch-equivalent to the parallel product of the well-ordering preservation principle for the operator X ↦ X ω X\mapsto X^{\omega} and the Eventually Constant Tail principle. By previous work from Pauly, Pradic and Soldà, the Ordered Ramsey Theorem is known to be Weihrauch-equivalent to the parallel product of the Eventually Constant Tail principle and the parallelization of the jump of the Limited Principle of Omniscience. We show that the latter pinciple and the well-ordering preservation principle for X ↦ X ω X\mapsto X^{\omega} are Weihrauch-incomparable.



1
Introduction and Motivation

A
well-ordering preservation principle
(or
well-ordering principle
– WOP for short) states that some operator on linear orderings preserves well-ordering. A
Ramsey-type theorem
states that some class of colorings is partition-regular. Both families of principles are well investigated in Reverse Mathematics
[
5
]
. Many prominent systems can be characterized in terms of WOPs (see
[
11
,
9
]
) and of Ramsey-type theorems (see
[
5
]
). Usually the equivalence of the latter two is obtained as an indirect by-product. We refer to
[
2
]
for examples and discussion.

In the present paper we study from the viewpoint of Weihrauch reducibility the well-ordering principle for the operator
X\mapsto X^{\omega}
and the Ordered Ramsey Theorem. This is one way of making precise the question from
[
3
]
whether
direct proofs
exist, in one direction or the other, between Ramsey-type principles and WOPs.

The motivation for the present work is twofold. First, we continue the line of research initiated in
[
2
]
concerning the possibility of establishing Weihrauch reductions of well-ordering preservation principles to combinatorial theorems from Ramsey Theory. For example it is shown in
[
2
]
that the WOP for the operator
X\mapsto\omega^{X}
is Weihrauch reducible to Ramsey’s Theorem for
2
-colorings of
3
-subsets of
\mathbb{N}
as well as to Hindman’s Theorem restricted to finite sums of
3
terms. The initial motivation for the present work comes from the observation in
[
2
]
that a similar proof shows that the WOP for the operator
X\mapsto X^{\omega}
reduces to Ramsey’s Theorem for finite colorings of
2
-subsets. The latter result is not optimal from the point of view of Reverse Mathematics, since the WOP in question is known to be equivalent to
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-induction
[
12
]
, while Ramsey’s Theorem for pairs and arbitrarily many colors implies the stronger principle of
\Sigma^{0}_{3}
-bounding
[
7
]
. It was therefore natural to ask whether the same proof-technique from
[
2
]
could be applied to reduce the WOP for
X\mapsto X^{\omega}
to a Ramsey-type statement known to be equivalent to
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. The Ordered Ramsey Theorem introduced in
[
8
]
is one such candidate. The fact that the latter principle was studied from the point of view of Weihrauch complexity in
[
10
]
has been our main motivation for focusing, among the known combinatorial principles equivalent to
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-induction, on the Ordered Ramsey Theorem. The analysis in
[
10
]
gave some indication on how to obtain reductions in the direction, from the WOP for
X\mapsto X^{\omega}
to the Ordered Ramsey Theorem. Note that reductions
from
WOPs
to

2.1
Weihrauch reducibility

Weihrauch reducibility has become in later years of major interest in Computable Mathematics and in Reverse Mathematics. We give the basic definitions.

Definition

((strong) Weihrauch reducibility)
.

Let
\mathsf{P}
and
\mathsf{Q}
be problems.

1.

\mathsf{P}
is Weihrauch-reducible to
\mathsf{Q}
(denoted
\mathsf{P}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{Q}
), if there exist Turing functionals
H
and
K
such that:
if
X
is any
\mathsf{P}
-instance then
H(X)
is a
\mathsf{Q}
-instance, and if
\hat{Y}
is any
\mathsf{Q}
-solution to
H(X)
then
K(X\oplus\hat{Y})

is a
\mathsf{P}
-solution to
X
.

2.

\mathsf{P}
is strongly Weihrauch-reducible to
\mathsf{Q}
(denoted
\mathsf{P}\leq_{\mathrm{sW}}\mathsf{Q}
), if there exist Turing functionals
H
and
K
such that:
if
X
is any
\mathsf{P}
-instance then
H(X)
is a
\mathsf{Q}
-instance, and if
\hat{Y}
is any
\mathsf{Q}
-solution to
H(X)
then
K(\hat{Y})

s a
\mathsf{P}
-solution to
X
.

We abbreviate by
\mathsf{P}\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{Q}
the fact that
\mathsf{P}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{Q}
and
\mathsf{Q}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{P}
. Similarly for
\mathsf{P}\equiv_{\mathrm{sW}}\mathsf{Q}
.
We abbreviate by
\mathsf{P}<_{W}\mathsf{Q}
the fact that
\mathsf{P}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{Q}
and
\mathsf{Q}\nleq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{P}
. Similarly for
<_{\mathrm{sW}}
.

Some of our results are expressed in terms of a number of structural operations on problems, which respect the quotient to Weihrauch degrees.

Definition

(Parallel product and finite parallelization)
.

Let
\mathsf{P}
and
\mathsf{Q}
be problems.

1.

The parallel product of
\mathsf{P}
and
\mathsf{Q}
(denoted
\mathsf{P}\times\mathsf{Q}
) is the problem whose instances are all pairs
(X_{0},X_{1})

where
X_{0}
is a
\mathsf{P}
-instance and
X_{1}
is a
\mathsf{Q}
-instance, with the solutions being all pairs
(Y_{0},Y_{1})
such that

is a
\mathsf{P}
-solution to
X_{0}
and
Y_{1}
is a
\mathsf{Q}
-solution to
X_{1}
.

2.

The finite parallelization of a problem
\mathsf{P}
(denoted
\mathsf{P}^{*}
), has the power to solve an arbitrary finite number of instances of
\mathsf{P}
, provided that number is given as part of the input.

For every problem
\mathsf{P}
and

we write
\mathsf{P}^{n}
to mean
\underbrace{\mathsf{P}\times\dots\times\mathsf{P}}_{n\small{\mbox{ times}}}
.

The following problems have emerged as useful benchmarks in Weihrauch Complexity.

1.

\mathsf{TC}_{\mathbb{N}}:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\Rightarrow\mathbb{N}
(totalization of closed choice on N) is the problem that takes as input an enumeration e of any subset of

(hence now we allow the possibility that

2.

.

3.

\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime}:\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{N}}\to\{0,1\}

of binary strings such that the function
p(i)=\lim_{s\to\infty}p_{i}(s)
is defined for every

, and outputs
\mathsf{LPO}(p)
.

4.

Define the problem
\mathsf{IsFinite}_{\mathbb{S}}
as follows: an instance of
\mathsf{IsFinite}_{\mathbb{S}}
is a binary sequence
p\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
, and the solution
\mathsf{IsFinite}_{\mathbb{S}}(p)\in\mathbb{S}
for

is
\top
if
p
contains finitely many occurrences of

, and
\bot
otherwise. Here,
\mathbb{S}
denotes the Sierpinski space

, where
\top
is coded by binary sequences containing at least one occurrence of

, and
\bot
is coded by

.

The notation
\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime}
is justified by the fact that the definition of
\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime}
is a special case of the definition of jump as given in
[

]
applied to
\mathsf{LPO}
. As noted in
[

]
,
\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime}
corresponds to the power of answering a single binary
\Sigma^{0}_{2}

-question.

2.2
Well-ordering principles

A well-ordering preservation principle states that some operator on linear orders preserves well-ordering. In the present paper we are interested in a particular WOP based on the operator
X\mapsto X^{\omega}
corresponding to ordinal exponentiation to the power
\omega
. When referring to a linear ordering
\mathcal{X}=(X,\leq_{X})
we systematically use
X
also for
(X,\leq_{X})
.

The following definition is from
[
6
]
.

Definition

(The operator
X\mapsto X^{\omega}
)
.

If
X
is a linear ordering we define
X^{\omega}
as the set of all finite sequences of the form
\langle(b_{0},a_{0}),\dots,(b_{n},a_{n})\rangle

such that for all
i\leq n
,
b_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
b_{0}>b_{1}>\dots>b_{n}
and
0\neq a_{i}\in X
. We let
<_{X^{\omega}}
be the lexicographic ordering on
X^{\omega}
: If
\sigma
extends
\tau
then
\sigma>_{X^{\omega}}\tau
. If
j
is least integer such that
(b_{j},a_{j})=\sigma_{j}\neq\tau_{j}=(b^{\prime}_{j},a_{j})
and either
b_{j}>b^{\prime}_{j}
or both
b_{j}=b^{\prime}_{j}
and
a_{j}>_{X}a^{\prime}_{j}
then
\sigma>\tau
. Otherwise
\tau>_{X^{\omega}}\sigma
.

If
X
is an ordinal
\alpha
, the element
\langle(b_{0},a_{0}),\dots,(b_{n},a_{n})\rangle
of
\alpha^{\omega}
corresponds to the ordinal

\alpha^{b_{0}}\cdot a_{0}+\dots+\alpha^{b_{n}}\cdot a_{n}
.

In the Reverse Mathematics literature
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
denotes the principle
\forall X(\mathrm{WO}(X)\to\mathrm{WO}(X^{\omega}))
where
\mathrm{WO}(X)
is any canonical
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-formula expressing that
X
is well-ordered. Uftring
[
12
]
proved that this principle is
equivalent to
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-induction.

As patent from the definition of
X\mapsto X^{\omega}
, we can view the elements of
X^{\omega}
as
terms
involving elements from
X
as
sub-terms
. If
\sigma
is a sequence in
X^{\omega}
and
\sigma^{\prime}
is a sequence in
X
, we say that
\sigma^{\prime}

is contained in

\sigma
if
\sigma^{\prime}
consists of sub-terms of elements of
\sigma
in the same relative order, i.e.:
\sigma^{\prime}_{i}
is a sub-term of
\sigma_{t_{i}}
for some
t_{i}
and if
i<j
then
t_{i}\leq t_{j}
. The notion makes sense for all other WOPs considered in this paper, which are based on operators weaker than
X\mapsto X^{\omega}
, as well as for most WOPs considered in the Reverse Mathematics literature.

In order to study the Weihrauch complexity of WOPs we give the following definition.

Definition

(WOP Problem)
.

Let
F
be an operator on linear orders. The problem
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto F(X))
is the problem that takes as an infinite decreasing sequence
\sigma
in
F(X)
and outputs an infinite decreasing sequence
\sigma^{\prime}
in
X
contained in
\sigma
.

2.3
Ramsey-type theorems

We here introduce the Ramsey-type principles of interest to the present paper, i.e., the Ordered and the Additive Ramsey Theorems.

Fix a linear order
(X,<_{X})
. For every poset
(P,<_{P})
, we call a colouring
c:[X]^{2}\to P

right-ordered
if we have
c(x,y)\leq_{P}c(x,y^{\prime})
when
x<_{X}y\leq_{X}y^{\prime}
.

Definition

(Ordered Ramsey Theorem)
.

The problem
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
takes as input a finite poset
(P,\leq_{P})
and a right-ordered colouring
c:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to P
,
and outputs an infinite
c
-homogeneous set
\subseteq\mathbb{N}
.

Definition

(Additive Ramsey Theorem)
.

The problem
\mathsf{ART}_{\mathbb{N}}
takes as input a finite semigroup
S
and an additive colouring
c:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to S
, and
outputs an infinite
c
-homogeneous set
\subseteq\mathbb{N}
.

In Reverse Mathematics the
\forall\exists
theorems naturally corresponding to
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathsf{ART}_{\mathbb{N}}
in Second-Order Arithmetic were introduced in
[
8
]
and proved equivalent to
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-induction over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. The Weihrauch complexity of the problems
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathsf{ART}_{\mathbb{N}}
is studied in
[
10
]
.

Finally we will make use of the principle called Eventually Constant Tail, introduced in
[
4
]
.

Definition

(Eventually Constant Tail)
.

\mathsf{ECT}
is the problem whose instances are pairs
(n,f)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
f:\mathbb{N}\to n
is a colouring of the natural numbers with
n
colours, and such that, for every instance
(n,f)
and
b\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathsf{ECT}(n,f)
if and only if
\forall x>b\exists y>x(f(x)=f(y))
.

In other words,
\mathsf{ECT}
is the problem that, upon being given a function
f
of the integers with finite range, outputs a
b
such that, after that
b
, the palette of colours used is constant (hence its name, which stands for eventually constant palette tail). In Reverse Mathematics the principle corresponding to
\mathsf{ECT}
is equivalent to
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-induction
[
4
]
.

3
The Ordered Ramsey Theorem and
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})

In this section we establish the Weihrauch equivalence of the Ordered Ramsey Theorem with the parallel product of
the Eventually Constant Tail principle and the WOP for the operator
X\mapsto X^{\omega}
. The lower bound on the Ordered Ramsey Theorem is based on an idea from
[
2
]
. The upper bound is, as far as we know, the first example of a reduction of a Ramsey-type principle to a WOP principle.

3.1
Lower bound:
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}\geq_{W}\mathsf{ECT}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})

Let
X
be a linearly ordered set. Let
\tau=\langle(b_{0},a_{0}),\dots,(b_{m},a_{m})\rangle
be in
X^{\omega}
.
Define the
length
of
\tau
as
|\tau|:=2m+2
. For each
0\leq s<|\tau|
, define the
s
-th
component
of
\tau
as

\tau(s):=\begin{cases}b_{t}\in\mathbb{N},&\text{if }s=2t\text{ is even};\\
a_{t}\in X,&\text{if }s=2t+1\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

Then
|\tau|
is the least
s\geq 0
such that
\tau(s)
is undefined.

We use the above terminology to add an order structure to the coloring devised in
[
2
]
to establish a reduction from
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{\infty}
to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
. We use
\prec
to denote the prefix relation on sequences.

Proposition 5
.

\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be an instance of
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
. If

\sigma_{i}=\langle(b_{0}^{i},a_{0}^{i}),\dots,(b_{m_{i}}^{i},a_{m_{i}}^{i})\rangle,

then
|\sigma_{i}|=2m_{i}+2
and
m_{i}\leq b_{0}^{i}\leq b_{0}^{0}=:k_{\sigma}
. Hence
|\sigma_{i}|\leq 2k_{\sigma}+2
for every
i\geq 0
.

Define a coloring
c:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2k_{\sigma}+2
as follows

c(i,j):=\begin{cases}|\sigma_{j}|&\text{if }\sigma_{j}\prec\sigma_{i};\\
\text{least }s<|\sigma_{i}|\text{ such that }\sigma_{i}(s)\neq\sigma_{j}(s)&otherwise.\end{cases}

If
\sigma_{j}\nprec\sigma_{i}
, since
\sigma_{j}<_{X^{\omega}}\sigma_{i}
there exists
0\leq s<|\sigma_{i}|
such that
\sigma_{i}(s)\neq\sigma_{j}(s)
. Also, in both cases of the definition of
c
,
c(i,j)<|\sigma_{i}|
and
\sigma_{j}(s)=\sigma_{i}(s)
for all
0\leq s<c(i,j)
.

Notice that for all
0\leq i<j<k
we have
c(i,k)\leq c(i,j)
. Suppose otherwise, as witnessed by
0\leq i<j<k
such that
c(i,j)<c(i,k)
. Then
\sigma_{k}(s)=\sigma_{i}(s)
for all
0\leq s\leq c(i,j)
. On the other hand,
\sigma_{j}(s)=\sigma_{i}(s)
for all
0\leq s<c(i,j)
and
\sigma_{j}(c(i,j))
is either undefined or strictly less than
\sigma_{i}(c(i,j))
, whether in
\mathbb{N}
or in
X
, depending on the parity of
c(i,j)
. It follows that
\sigma_{k}<_{X^{\omega}}\sigma_{j}
, which is impossible.

Let
P:=[2k_{\sigma}+2]
, and define
\leq_{P}
as the reverse of the natural order,

0>_{P}1>_{P}\dots\>>_{P}2k_{\sigma}+1.

Then the above remarks show that
c:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to P
and that
c
is right-ordered with respect to
\leq_{P}
. Hence
(P,c)
is a
\sigma
-computable instance of
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
.

Let
H=\{h_{0}<h_{1}<\dots\}
be a solution to
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
for
(P,c)
. Denote by
c^{*}
the
c
-color of
[H]^{2}
. Notice that
c^{*}=c(h_{i},h_{i+1})<|\sigma_{h_{i}}|
for all
i\geq 0
, hence
\sigma_{h_{i}}(c^{*})
is defined for all
i\geq 0
.

Now, suppose by way of contradiction that
c^{*}
is even. Then

b_{\frac{c^{*}}{2}}^{h_{0}}>_{\mathbb{N}}\>b_{\frac{c^{*}}{2}}^{h_{1}}>_{\mathbb{N}}\dots

which is impossible.

Therefore
c^{*}
is odd, and

a_{\frac{c^{*}-1}{2}}^{h_{0}}>_{X}\>a_{\frac{c^{*}-1}{2}}^{h_{1}}>_{X}\dots

is the desired
(H\oplus\sigma)
-computable solution to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
for
\sigma
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

(\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}})^{*}\equiv_{\mathrm{sW}}\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Obviously
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}\leq_{\mathrm{sW}}(\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}})^{*}
. We prove the opposite inequality. Let
\big\langle n,(P_{1},c_{1}),\dots,(P_{n},c_{n})\big\rangle
be an instance of
(\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}})^{*}
. Then, for all
1\leq i\leq n
, each
c_{i}:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to P_{i}
is right-ordered with respect to
\leq_{P_{i}}
.

Let
P:=P_{1}\times\dots\times P_{n}
, and define a coloring
c:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to P
by

c(x,y):=\big(c_{1}(x,y),\dots,c_{n}(x,y)\big).

We denote by
\leq_{P}
the product order on
P
, i.e., for
1\leq i\leq n

(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\leq_{P}(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})\;\mbox{ if and only if}\;p_{i}\leq_{P_{i}}q_{i}.

Suppose that
x<y<y^{\prime}
. Then
c_{i}(x,y)\leq_{P_{i}}c_{i}(x,y^{\prime})
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, hence

c(x,y)=\big(c_{1}(x,y),\dots,c_{n}(x,y)\big)\leq_{P}\big(c_{1}(x,y^{\prime}),\dots,c_{n}(x,y^{\prime})\big).

This shows that
c
is right-ordered with respect to
\leq_{P}
, i.e. that
(P,c)
is
an instance of
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
.

Let
H
be a solution to
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
for
(P,c)
. If
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\in P
is the
c
-color of
[H]^{2}
then
p_{i}\in P_{i}
is the
c_{i}
-color of
[H]^{2}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
. Therefore
\langle H,\dots,H\rangle
is a solution to
(\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}})^{*}
for
\big\langle n,(P_{1},c_{1}),\dots,(P_{n},c_{n})\big\rangle
.
∎

Proposition 7
.

\mathsf{ECT}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

From
[
10
, Lemma 52]
it follows that
\mathsf{ECT}\leq_{\mathrm{sW}}\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
.
From this and Proposition
5
we have
\mathsf{ECT}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})\leq_{\mathrm{W}}(\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}})^{2}
. Clearly
(\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}})^{2}\leq_{\mathrm{sW}}(\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}})^{*}

(\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}})^{*}\leq_{\mathrm{sW}}\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}

3.2
Upper bound:
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{ECT}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})

In this subsection we prove that
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{ECT}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
. We obtain
the result from a fine-grained parametrized version. Let
n\geq 1
. Let
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}[n]
denote the restriction of
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
to instances
(P,c)
with
|P|=n
. Let
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{n})
denote the well-ordering principle regarding the lexicographic order
<_{lex}
on
X^{n}
: an instance of
WOP(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{n})
is a
<_{lex}
-descending sequence
\sigma=(\sigma_{i})_{i\geq 0}
in
X^{n}
for some linearly ordered set
X
, and a corresponding solution is a descending sequence in
X
whose terms occur as components of (terms of)
\sigma
.

Proposition 8
.

For all
n\geq 1
,

\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}[n]\leq_{\mathrm{sW}}(\mathsf{ECT})^{n}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
(P,c)
be an instance of
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}[n]
. This means that
P
is a finite poset of cardinality
n
, and
c:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to P
is right-ordered with respect to
\leq_{P}
.

We begin by fixing a linear ordering
p_{1}<p_{2}<\dots<p_{n}
of
P
extending
\leq_{P}
. This can be done computably in
P
.

For each
1\leq j\leq n
we define a binary sequence
improvement_{j}
and an auxiliary sequence
previous_{j}
as follows. First, set

improvement_{j}(0):=1\;,\;\;previous_{j}(0)=previous_{j}(1):=0.

Now let
i\geq 1
. If there exists
previous_{j}(i)\leq k<i
such that
c(k,i)=p_{j}
, then call
k_{j}^{i}
the least such
k
and set
improvement_{j}(i):=1
,
previous_{j}(i+1):=i
. Otherwise, set
improvement_{j}(i):=0
,
previous_{j}(i+1)=previous_{j}(i)
. We say that
p_{j}
improves at stage
i\geq 0
if and only if
improvement_{j}(i)=1
. Notice that both
improvement_{j}
and
previous_{j}
are
(P\oplus c)
-computable.

When interpreted as
2
-colorings of
\mathbb{N}
, the sequences
improvement_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq n
, constitute an instance of
(\mathsf{ECT})^{n}
. Let
n_{0}^{j}
,
1\leq j\leq n
, be the corresponding solutions, and set
n_{0}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}n_{0}^{j}
. Then for all
1\leq j\leq n
either
p_{j}
doesn’t improve after stage
n_{0}
or
p_{j}
improves infintely many times after stage
n_{0}
.

Next, we define the fitting
(P\oplus c)
-computable instance
\sigma=(\sigma_{i})_{i\geq 0}
of
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{n})
.
We choose
X:=\{1,\dots,n\}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Q}\times\{-n,\dots,-1\}
. Each factor is equipped with its natural order, so that the resulting lexicographic order on
X
is linear.

Stage
0
. Set
\sigma_{0}:=\big(a_{n}^{0},\dots,a_{1}^{0}\big)
, where
a_{j}^{0}:=\big(j,\>1,\>2,-1\big)
for all
n\geq j\geq 1
.

Stage
i\geq 1
. Consider the set
I^{i}
of all
j
-s such that
p_{j}
improves at stage
i
. For every
j\in I^{i}
, let
n\geq m^{i}_{j}\geq j
be the largest
m
such that
p_{m}
has improved at least once in the timeframe
(previous_{j}(i),i]
.

Suppose that
j\in I^{i}
. If
m^{i}_{j}=j
, set

a_{j}^{i}:=\big(\>j,\>a_{j}^{i-1}(2),\>\frac{1}{k_{j}^{i}+1},\>-1\>);

if, otherwise,
m_{j}^{i}>j
, set

a_{j}^{i}:=\big(\>j,\>a_{j}^{i-1}(2)+1,\>\frac{1}{k_{j}^{i}+1},\>-1\big).

Suppose instead that
j\notin I^{i}
. If
j=\max\{m_{l}^{i}\;|\;l\in I^{i}\}
then set

a_{j}^{i}:=\big(j,\>a_{j}^{i-1}(2),\>a_{j}^{i-1}(3),\>a_{j}^{i-1}(4)-1\big);

otherwise, set

a_{j}^{i}:=a_{j}^{i-1}.

Finally, set
\sigma_{i}:=\big(a_{n}^{i},\dots,a_{1}^{i}\big)
.

Notice that
I^{i}
is non-empty for every
i\geq 1
, as it contains the index of
c(i-1,i)
. Then it’s easy to see that
\sigma_{i}<_{lex}\sigma_{i-1}
, as required. This completes the definition of
\sigma
.

Let
\overrightarrow{x}=(x_{s})_{s\geq 0}
be a sequence in
X
that is a solution to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{n})
for
\sigma
. We aim to compute an increasing enumeration
\overrightarrow{y}=(y_{s})_{s\geq 0}
of natural numbers for a
c
-homogenous set from
n_{0}
and
\overrightarrow{x}
. The
x_{s}
-s are drawn from the matrix

\Sigma:=(a_{j}^{i})_{n\geq j\geq 1}^{i\geq 0},

where
i
is interpreted as a row index and
j
as a (decreasing) column index. We are able to locate the
x_{s}
-s more precisely by reasoning as follows. Let
j^{*}
be the largest
n\geq j\geq 1
such that
p_{j}
improves infinitely many times. Let
i^{*}
be the least
i\geq 0
such that
p_{j*}
improves at stage
i
and no color
p_{j}
,
n\geq j>j^{*}
, improves after stage
i
. Then:

1.

If
p_{j}
improves finitely many times, then

a_{j}^{i}(1)
,
a_{j}^{i}(2)
, and
a_{j}^{i}(3)
are constant for
i\geq n_{0}
; also, by construction
a_{j}^{i}(4)>_{X}a_{j}^{i+1}(4)
for at most
(n-1)
many
i\geq n_{0}
. Hence column
j
contains at most
(n_{0}+1)+(n-1)=n_{0}+n
distinct coefficients.

2.

Together, columns
n\geq j\geq j^{*}+1
contain at most
M:=n(n_{0}+n)
distinct coefficients. This follows immediately from remark (
1
) and the choice of
j^{*}
.

3.

\overrightarrow{x}
can’t move from a given column to a column on its left. This is true because by construction
a_{j}^{i}>_{X}a_{j^{\prime}}^{i^{\prime}}
for all
n\geq j>j^{\prime}\geq 1
and for all
i,i^{\prime}\geq 0
.

4.

If
j^{*}-1\geq j\geq 1
, then for every
i\geq 0
column
j
contains finitely many distinct coefficients smaller than
a_{j}^{i}
. This follows trivially from (
1
) if
p_{j}
doesn’t improve indefinitely. Suppose otherwise: since
p_{j^{*}}
improves at some stage
i^{\prime}>i
and
j^{*}>j
,
a_{j}^{i}(2)
is eventually increased.

5.

\overrightarrow{x}
can’t visit columns
j^{*}-1\geq j\geq 1
. This follows from remarks (
3
) and (
4
) combined.

6.

x_{s}
lies in column
j^{*}
for all
s\geq M+1
.
This follows from remarks (
2
) and (
5
) combined.

7.

Let
z:=a_{j^{*}}^{i^{*}}(2)
. Then
a_{j^{*}}^{i}(2)<z
for all
0\leq i<i^{*}
and
a_{j^{*}}^{i}(2)=z
for all
i\geq i^{*}
. Hence if
i<i^{*}
then
\sigma
contains finitely many distinct coefficients smaller than
a_{j^{*}}^{i}
, meaning that
\overrightarrow{x}
can’t visit the square
a_{j^{*}}^{i}
.

8.

For
s\geq M+1
, the
x_{s}
-s are drawn in increasing order of rows from column
j^{*}
(so that
x_{s+n}(3)<x_{s}(3)<2
for all
s\geq M+2
.). This follows from remarks (
6
) and (
7
), since
x_{s}(2)=z
is fixed for
s\geq M+1
.

Set

y_{s}:=\frac{1}{x_{f(s)}(3)}-1\;\;\;\;\forall s\geq 0,

where
f(s):=(M+2)+(s\times n)
. Since
f
is computable from
n_{0}
,
\overrightarrow{y}
is computable from
(n_{0}\oplus\overrightarrow{x})
. Remark (
8
) above proves that
\overrightarrow{y}
is an increasing sequence of natural numbers.

It only remains to show that
\overrightarrow{y}
is
c
-monochromatic. We fix
0\leq s<t
, and show that
c(y_{s},y_{t})=p_{j^{*}}
. Let
i
be least such that
a_{j^{*}}^{i}(3)=x_{f(s)}(3)
, so that
y_{s}=k_{j^{*}}^{i}
and
c(k_{j^{*}}^{i},i)=p_{j^{*}}
. Similarly, let
i^{\prime}
be least such that
a_{j^{*}}^{i^{\prime}}(3)=x_{f(t)}(3)
, so that
y_{t}=k_{j^{*}}^{i^{\prime}}
. By construction we have

i^{*}\leq k_{j^{*}}^{i}<i\leq k_{j^{*}}^{i^{\prime}}
. Since
c
is right-ordered,
c(k_{j^{*}}^{i},k_{j^{*}}^{i^{\prime}})\geq_{P}p_{j^{*}}
. Now if
c(k_{j^{*}}^{i},k_{j^{*}}^{i^{\prime}})=p_{j}>_{P}p_{j^{*}}
then
j>j^{*}
and
p_{j}
improves after
i^{*}
, contradicting the choices of
i^{*}
and
j^{*}
. Therefore
c(y_{s},y_{t})=c(k_{j^{*}}^{i},k_{j^{*}}^{i^{\prime}})=p_{j^{*}}
, as required.
∎

Let
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})[\leq n]
denote the restriction of
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
to those sequences
\tau=(\tau_{i})_{i\geq 0}
satisfying
|\tau_{i}|\leq n
for all
i\geq 0
.

Lemma 9
.

For all
n\geq 1
,
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{n})\leq_{\mathrm{sW}}\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})[\leq 2n]

Proof.

Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be an instance of
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{n})
, say
\sigma_{i}=\big(a_{n}^{i},\dots,a_{1}^{i}\big)
.
Set
\tau_{i}:=\langle(n,a_{n}^{i}),(n-1,a_{n-1}^{i}),\dots,(1,a_{1}^{i})\rangle
for all
i\geq 0
. Then
\tau_{i+1}<_{X^{\omega}}\tau_{i}
and
|\tau_{i}|=2n
. Hence
\tau
is a
\sigma
-computable instance of
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})[\leq 2n]
. It’s clear that every solution to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})[\leq 2n]
for
\tau
is also a solution to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{n})
for
\sigma
.
∎

Corollary 10
.

\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{ECT}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})

Proof.

By proposition
8
and lemma
9
we have
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}[n]\leq_{\mathrm{sW}}(\mathsf{ECT})^{n}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})[\leq 2n]
. Hence

\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}(\mathsf{ECT})^{*}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
(the argument in proposition
2
doesn’t establish a strong Weihrauch reduction, since
f
depends on
n=|P|
).
From Lemma
2
it follows that
(\mathsf{ECT})^{*}\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{ECT}
, since
(\mathsf{P}^{*})^{*}\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{P}^{*}
for every problem
\mathsf{P}
. Therefore the desired inequality
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{ECT}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
holds.
∎

4
Completing the picture

In the previous section we proved the equivalence
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}\equiv_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{ECT}\times\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
. Since
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
is also equivalent to
\mathsf{ECT}\times(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
by Theorem
1
from
[
10
]
, it is natural to ask how
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
compares with
(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
and with
\mathsf{ECT}
.
We show that
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
and
(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
are incomparable and that
\mathsf{ECT}
is strictly
weaker than
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
.

4.1
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
and
(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
are Weihrauch incomparable

The proof of the following proposition is a variation on the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 8]
which shows
\mathsf{IsFinite}_{\mathbb{S}}\nleq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{ECT}
.

Proposition 11
.

\mathsf{IsFinite}_{\mathbb{S}}\nleq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
.

Proof.

Suppose by way of contradiction that
\mathsf{IsFinite}_{\mathbb{S}}\leq_{\mathrm{W}}\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
, as witnessed by the computable Turing functionals
K
and
H
. This means that for every instance
p
of
\mathsf{IsFinite}_{\mathbb{S}}
,
H(p)
is an instance of
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
, and for every solution
\overrightarrow{x}
to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
for
H(p)
,
K\langle\overrightarrow{x},p\rangle=\mathsf{IsFinite}_{\mathbb{S}}(p)
.

Consider
s^{0}:=0^{\mathbb{N}}
and any solution
\langle y_{0}^{0},y_{1}^{0},\dots\rangle
to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
for
H(s^{0})
. Since
\mathsf{IsFinite}_{\mathbb{S}}(s^{0})=\top
,

K\langle\langle y_{0}^{0},\dots,y_{m_{0}}^{0}\rangle,0^{n_{0}}\rangle\downarrow\;=\top

for some
m_{0},n_{0}\geq 0
. By choosing a large enough
n_{0}
, we can make sure that all the terms
y_{0}^{0},\dots,y_{m_{0}}^{0}
are mentioned in
H(0^{n_{0}})
.

We proceed inductively to obtain, for each
k\geq 1
: a binary sequence
s^{k}:=0^{n_{0}}10^{n_{1}}\dots 10^{n_{k-1}}10^{\mathbb{N}}
; a solution
\langle y_{0}^{k},y_{1}^{k},\dots\>\rangle
to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
for
H(s^{k})
; two integers
m_{k},n_{k}\geq 0
such that

K\langle\langle y_{0}^{k},\dots,y_{m_{k}}^{k}\rangle,0^{n_{0}}10\dots 10^{n_{k-1}}10^{n_{k}}\rangle\downarrow\;=\top

and such that
y_{0}^{k},\dots,y_{m_{k}}^{k}
are all mentioned in
H(0^{n_{0}}10\dots 10^{n_{k-1}}10^{n_{k}})
.

Now, let

p:=0^{n_{0}}10^{n_{1}}10^{n_{2}}10^{n_{3}}1\dots\>,

and let
\langle x_{0},x_{1},\dots\rangle
be a solution to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
for
H(p)
. Since
\mathsf{IsFinite}_{\mathbb{S}}(p)=\bot
,

K\langle\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{m}\rangle,0^{n_{0}}1\dots 10^{n_{k}}\rangle\downarrow\;=\bot

for some
m,k\geq 0
, and again we can assume without loss of generality that
x_{0},\dots,x_{m}
are all mentioned in
H(0^{n_{0}}1\dots 10^{n_{k}})
. The latter, which is an initial segment of both
H(s^{k})
and
H(p)
, also contains the terms
y_{0}^{k},\dots,y_{m_{k}}^{k}
, and thus decides on the relation between
x_{m}
and
y_{m_{k}}^{k}
in the linear ordering
\leq_{X}
.

If
x_{m}\geq_{X}y_{m_{k}}^{k}
, then
\overrightarrow{z}:=\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{m},y_{m_{k+1}}^{k},y_{m_{k+2}}^{k},\>\dots\rangle
is a solution to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
for
H(s^{k})
. Hence

K\langle\overrightarrow{z},s^{k}\rangle=\top.

But

K\langle\overrightarrow{z},s^{k}\rangle=K\langle\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{m}\rangle,0^{n_{0}}1\dots 10^{n_{k}}\rangle=\bot,

which is impossible.

Similarly, if
x_{m}\leq_{X}y_{m_{k}}^{k}
then
\overrightarrow{w}:=\langle y_{0}^{k},\dots,y_{m_{k}}^{k},x_{m+1},x_{m+2},\>\dots\rangle
is a solution to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
for
H(p)
, hence

K\langle\overrightarrow{w},p\rangle=\bot.

But

K\langle\overrightarrow{w},p\rangle=K\langle\langle y_{0}^{k},\dots,y_{m_{k}}^{k}\rangle,0^{n_{0}}1\dots 10^{n_{k}}\rangle=\top,

which is again impossible. This gives the desired contradiction.

∎

, Lemma 9]
.

Proposition 14
.

\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{2})\nleq_{\mathrm{W}}(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
.

Proof.

Suppose by way of contradiction that
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{2})\leq_{\mathrm{W}}(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
, as witnessed by the computable Turing functionals
K
and
H
. This means that: for every instance
\sigma
of
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{2})
,
H(\sigma)
is an instance of
(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
, say
H(\sigma)=\langle n_{\sigma};\rho^{\sigma}_{0},\dots,\rho^{\sigma}_{n_{\sigma}-1}\rangle
; if
\eta\in 2^{n_{\sigma}}
is a solution to
(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
for

, then

is a solution to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{2})
for

\sigma
infinite decreasing in
X^{2}_{lex}:=\mathbb{Q}^{2}_{lex}
for which the above fails.

Start by letting
\tau^{0}:=\langle\tau_{0}^{0},\>\tau_{1}^{0},\dots\rangle
,
\tau_{i}^{0}:=\langle 0,\frac{1}{i+1}\rangle
for all

. Fix some
\bar{i}\geq 0
such that the first component of
H
converges to
n_{\tau^{0}}=:n
after having read the rows
\tau_{0}^{0},\dots,\tau_{\bar{i}}^{0}
of
\tau^{0}
. Then for every instance
\tau=\langle\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\dots\rangle

\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{2})

\tau_{i}=\tau_{i}^{0}

. Note a solution to
(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
for

is a binary strings of length

.

Let
\eta^{0},\dots,\eta^{2^{n}-1}

. Let
\eta^{k_{0}}

(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
for
H(\tau^{0})
. Since the solutions to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{2})
for
\tau^{0}
are exactly the infinite subsequences of its second column, i.e. of the form
(\frac{1}{m^{0}_{\ell}})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
where

, there exists
i_{0}\geq\bar{i}

for some integers

,
1\leq m_{0}^{0}<\dots\><m_{h_{0}}^{0}
.

Let
1\leq j<2^{n}
.
Suppose inductively that an instance
\tau^{j-1}=\langle\tau_{0}^{j-1},\>\tau_{1}^{j-1},\dots\rangle
of
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{2})

\tau_{i}^{j-1}(l)\leq j\;\;\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq i_{j-1},\>l\in\{0,1\}.

(1)

Set
\tau^{j}:=\langle\tau_{0}^{j},\tau_{1}^{j},\dots\rangle
, where
\tau_{i}^{j}:=\tau_{i}^{{j-1}}
for

\tau_{i_{j-1}+i}^{j}:=\langle-j,j+\frac{1}{i}\rangle

) that the solutions to
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X_{lex}^{2})
for
\tau^{j}
are exactly the infinite subsequences of
\langle j+\frac{1}{m}:m\geq 1\rangle
. Hence if
\eta^{k_{j}}
is the solution to
(\mathsf{LPO}^{\prime})^{*}
for

, there exists
i_{j}>i_{j-1}

K\langle\eta^{k_{j}},\langle\tau_{0}^{j},\dots,\tau_{i_{j}}^{j}\rangle\rangle\downarrow\;=\langle\>j+\frac{1}{m_{0}^{j}},\dots,\>j+\frac{1}{m_{h_{j}}^{j}}\>\rangle

for some integers

,

.

Finally, set
\sigma:=\langle\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\dots\rangle
, where
\sigma_{i}:=\tau_{i}^{2^{n}-1}
for

and
\sigma_{i_{2^{n}-1}+i}:=\langle-2^{n},2^{n}+\frac{1}{i}\rangle

. Notice that the indexes

chosen above are necessarily all distinct. Hence there is a unique

5
Conclusion and perspectives

We have compared in terms of Weirauch reducibility the Ordered Ramsey Theorem
\mathsf{ORT}_{\mathbb{N}}
and the Well-Ordering Preservation Principle for
\omega
-exponentiation
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
. The fact that the Ordered Ramsey Theorem is reducible to the parallel product of
\mathsf{ECT}
and
\mathsf{WOP}(X\mapsto X^{\omega})
is the first example of a Weihrauch reduction of a combinatorial principle to a well-ordering preservation principle.

While we obtained a complete picture concerning the principles studied in this paper, many question remains for other principles of interest.
We consider the present work as a specimen of a wider study of Weihrauch reducibilities between WOPs and Ramsey-type theorems.
We believe that the approach used in the present paper can be successfully applied to many other principles of strength around that of
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-induction. We believe that the reductions from WOPs can be useful to obtain analogous results also at higher level of logical complexity, e.g., for the principles studied in
[
2
]
.

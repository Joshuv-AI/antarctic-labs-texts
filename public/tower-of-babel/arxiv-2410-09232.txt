Short hierarchically hyperbolic groups I: uncountably many coarse median structures
Giorgio Mangioni

Abstract
. We prove that the mapping class group of a sphere with five punctures admits uncountably many coarsely equivariant coarse median structures. The same is shown for right-angled Artin groups whose defining graphs are connected, triangle- and square-free, and have at least three vertices. Remarkably, in the latter case, the coarse median structures we produce are not induced by cocompact cubulations. To obtain the above results, we develop the theory of short hierarchically hyperbolic groups (HHG), which also include Artin groups of large and hyperbolic type, graph manifold groups, and extensions of Veech groups. We develop tools to modify their hierarchical structure, including using quasimorphisms to construct quasilines that serve as coordinate spaces, and this is where the abundance of coarse median structures comes from. These techniques are of independent interest, and are used in a follow-up paper with Alessandro Sisto to study quotients of short HHG. In the process, we also clarify a proof of Hagen, Martin, and Sisto on hierarchical hyperbolicity of Artin groups of large and hyperbolic type.

Introduction

Hierarchically hyperbolic spaces (HHS for short) and groups (HHG), introduced by Behrstock, Hagen, and Sisto in
[
BHS17b
]
, provide a common framework for the study of mapping class groups of finite-type surfaces, several Coxeter and Artin groups, most CAT(0) cubical groups, and many others. Essentially, a group
G
is hierarchically hyperbolic if there exists a family
\{\mathcal{C}U\}
of hyperbolic “coordinate spaces”, together with
G
-equivariant projections
\pi_{U}\colon G\to\mathcal{C}U
. The coordinate spaces are also arranged in a partial ordering with a unique maximal element (indeed, a hierarchy), and the number of layers of the hierarchy measures how far the group is from being hyperbolic (see Section
1
for background on HHS/G).

Short HHG

This paper is the first in a series of two, in which we introduce the family of
short HHG
(see Section
2.2
). Roughly, a hierarchically hyperbolic group
G
is short if:

•

G
acts on a triangle- and square-free simplicial graph
\overline{X}
, which we call the
support graph
, with finitely many orbits of edges;

•

each vertex stabiliser
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
for the action on
\overline{X}
has a preferred cyclic normal subgroup
Z_{v}
, called the
cyclic direction
, and the quotient
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)/Z_{v}
is hyperbolic;

•

The HHG structure “witnesses” the cyclic directions, in the sense that for every vertex
v\in\overline{X}
there exists a coordinate space
\mathcal{C}\ell_{v}
on which
Z_{v}
acts geometrically.

The name
short
comes from the fact that the hierarchy only has three layers, with the quasilines
\{\mathcal{C}\ell_{v}\}_{v\in\overline{X}}
at the bottom (see Remark
2.6
). Among others, this class of groups includes:

(a)

The mapping class group of a five-punctured sphere (whose short structure is explained in Subsection
2.3.1
);

(b)

Right-angled Artin groups whose defining graph is connected, triangle- and square-free (Proposition
6.1
);

(c)

Artin groups of large and hyperbolic type (Subsection
2.3.2
);

(d)

Fundamental groups of non-geometric graph manifolds, and more generally of admissible graph of groups in the sense of
[
CK02
]
(Subsection
2.3.3
);

(e)

Certain extensions of Veech subgroups of mapping class groups, namely those considered in
[
DDLS24
,
Bon24
]
(Subsection
2.3.4
);

(f)

Relative hyperbolic groups whose peripherals are
\mathbb{Z}
-central extensions of hyperbolic groups (Proposition
6.3
).

Unlike most known examples of HHG, the hierarchical structure of short HHG can be easily modified, as we shall detail later, and this makes them the perfect playground where one can test to which extent a certain property depends on the choice of the hierarchical structure.

Application to coarse median structures

To illustrate this principle, recall that, for example, by
[
BHS19
, Theorem 7.3]
, a hierarchically hyperbolic space
Z
admits a coarse median
\mu
, obtained as follows: for any three points
x,y,z\in Z
and any coordinate space
\mathcal{C}U
, the projection of
\mu(x,y,z)
to
\mathcal{C}U
is the coarse centre of any triangle with vertices
\{\pi_{U}(x),\pi_{U}(y),\pi_{U}(z)\}
. Two coarse median
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are equivalent if

\sup_{x,y,z\in G}\mathrm{d}_{Z}(\mu_{\lambda_{1}}(x,y,z),\mu_{\lambda_{2}}(x,y,z))<+\infty,

and a coarse median structure is an equivalence class of coarse medians.

It is natural to ask if a given group admits a unique coarse median structure. The question is further motivated by the fact that coarse median preserving automorphisms enjoy better properties than general automorphisms
[
Fio24
]
, and all automorphisms are clearly coarse median preserving in a group with a unique coarse median structure.

We prove that changing the HHG structure of a short HHG can result in different coarse median structures:

Theorem A

(see Theorem
7.1
)
.

Let
G
be a short HHG. Suppose that, for some vertex
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
, the stabiliser
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-extension of a non-elementarily hyperbolic group. Then
G
admits a continuum of coarsely
G
-equivariant coarse median structures.

This is in stark contrast with the case of hyperbolic groups, which admit a unique coarse median structure by
[
NWZ19
]
; the latter result was recently generalised to products of “bushy” hyperbolic spaces
[
FS25
]
.

By inspection of the short HHG structures of the examples
(a)
-
(f)
above, Theorem
A
applies to the following collection of short HHG:

Corollary B

(see Corollary
7.3
)
.

Let
G
be either:

(a)

the mapping class group of a sphere with five punctures;

(b)

a RAAG on a connected, triangle- and square-free graph with at least three vertices;

(c)

an Artin group of large and hyperbolic type, whose defining graph is not discrete;

(d)

the fundamental group of an admissible graph of groups;

(e)

an extension of a Veech group as in
[
DDLS24
,
Bon24
]
;

(f)

hyperbolic relative to
\mathbb{Z}
-central extensions of hyperbolic groups, of which at least one is non-elementary.

Then
G
admits a continuum of coarsely
G
-equivariant coarse median structures.

Let us spell out some consequences of Corollary
B
. Firstly, Item
(a)
gives:

Theorem C
.

The mapping class group of the five-punctured sphere admits a continuum of coarsely equivariant coarse median structures.

This disproves that mapping class groups have a unique coarse median structure coming from subsurface projections, a common belief supported by personal communications with Elia Fioravanti and Alessandro Sisto. Even more surprisingly, Fioravanti and Sisto recently proved that another HHS naturally associated to a finite-type surface, its
pants graph
, has a unique coarse median structure if the complexity of the surface is sufficiently small, and conjectured the same holds in general
[
FS25
]
. The difference between the HHS structure of the pants graph and that of the mapping class group is that the latter also includes certain quasilines, namely annular curve graphs, so we roughly exploit the fact that a product of quasilines admits uncountably many coarse median structures (see e.g.
[
FS25
, Remark 4.8]
).

Moving to Item
(b)
, recall that a RAAG acts geometrically on a CAT(0) cube complex, so it inherits a coarse median structure. However, the family of possible cubulations is countable, while we produce uncountably many coarse median structures. Hence we get:

Theorem D
.

Let
G_{\Lambda}
be a RAAG whose defining graph is connected, triangle- and square-free, and has at least three vertices. Then
G_{\Lambda}
admits a continuum of coarsely equivariant coarse median structures, which are not induced by cocompact cubulations.

The Theorem applies in particular to RAAGs whose defining graphs are trees, of which there are infinitely many quasi-isometry classes by
[
BN08
]
. Each such RAAG admits countably many cubical coarse median structures by
[
FLS24
, Theorem 3.19]
, so the countable bound on cubulations can actually be achieved. On the other side of the spectrum, the same
[
FLS24
, Theorem 3.19]
states the uniqueness of a cubical coarse median structure for RAAGs on triangle- and square-free graphs without leaves, which are covered by our Theorem
D
. Surprisingly, this shows that groups with a unique cubical coarse median structure can still have plenty of unexpected coarse medians.

Furthermore, recall that, by e.g.
[
DJ00
]
, the RAAG with defining graph
\Lambda
is commensurable to the right-angled Coxeter group with defining graph
\widetilde{\Lambda}
, obtained by doubling each vertex
a\in\Lambda^{(0)}
to a pair
a_{1},a_{2}
and declaring
a_{i}
to be adjacent to
b_{j}
if and only if
a,b
span an edge in
\Lambda
. Hence, non-equivalent coarse medians on a RAAG induce non-equivalent coarse medians on the associated RACG, though the latter might not be coarsely equivariant any more. As all RACGs are cocompactly cubulated
[
NR03
]
, we again get the following:

Theorem E
.

Let
\Lambda
be a connected, triangle- and square-free graph on at least three vertices, and let
\widetilde{\Lambda}
be its doubling. The right-angled Coxeter group
W_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
admits a continuum of coarse median structures, which do not come from cocompact cubulations.

Finally, recall that, by
[
Hae21
, Theorem C]
, an extra-large type Artin group whose defining graph is an even star acts cocompactly on a CAT(0) cube complex (these are actually the only examples of cocompactly cubulated two-dimensional Artin groups, together with
\mathbb{Z}
, dihedral, and free products of the previous, see
[
HJP16
, Theorem 1.1]
). Then again Corollary
B
.
(c)
implies:

Theorem F
.

Let
\Gamma
be a star with even labels, all greater than or equal to
4
. The Artin group
A_{\Gamma}
admits a continuum of coarsely equivariant coarse median structures, which are not induced by cocompact cubulations.

Tweaking the hierarchy

The source of flexibility of the hierarchical structure of a short HHG, which gives rise to the abundance of coarse median structures, is twofold.

(1)

Given (almost) any
g\in G
, one can find a short HHG structure where some power of
g
generates a cyclic direction, by introducing new vertices inside
\overline{X}
which are stabilised by
g
. This can be done, for example, for any element that acts loxodromically on the top-level coordinate space (think of a pseudo-Anosov element in the mapping class group of the five-holed sphere); or for (almost) any element which fixes a vertex and acts loxodromically on its link (in the same analogy, think of a partial pseudo-Anosov element supported on a subsurface).

(2)

Given any homogeneous quasimorphism
\phi\colon\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)\to\mathbb{R}
which is unbounded on
Z_{v}
, one can find a generating set
\tau
for
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
such that
\phi\colon\operatorname{Cay}\left(\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v),\tau\right)\to\mathbb{R}
is a quasi-isometry (this is done by mimicking some arguments from
[
ABO19
]
). Then, under suitable conditions (see Theorem
3.10
), one can construct a hierarchical structure in which the bottom level quasiline
\mathcal{C}\ell_{v}
coincides with
\operatorname{Cay}\left(\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v),\tau\right)
. The idea of using quasimorphisms to generate coordinate spaces was already involved in how the hierarchical structure for large hyperbolic type Artin groups
[
HMS22
]
and for graph manifold groups
[
HRSS25
]
were built, so our procedure can be seen as a generalisation of both.

The quasimorphisms we feed into the construction from Item (
2
) come from Corollary
A.4
, which roughly states that, given a central extension
0\to\mathbb{Z}\to G\to H\to 1
, where
H
is hyperbolic, and a finite collection of elements
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G
satisfying a certain assumption, there exists a homogeneous quasimorphism
\psi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
which is the identity on
\mathbb{Z}
and is trivial on
g_{i}
for every
{i=1,\ldots,n}
. This result should be compared to
[
HMS22
, Lemma 4.4]
, whose proof, however, contains a gap. In Remark
A.5
we point out the issue and explain how to circumvent it using our Corollary
A.4
, to ensure that all results from
[
HMS22
]
still hold.

Taking quotients by central directions

In joint work with Alessandro Sisto
[
MS24
]
, the above tools are pushed further to develop a “Dehn Filling” procedure for short HHG, similar in spirit to the relatively Dehn filling theorem
[
Osi07
,
GM08
]
. This is then used to study residual properties of short HHG. For example, by constructing suitable relatively hyperbolic quotients, we are able to show that most Artin group of large and hyperbolic type are
Hopfian
(every self-epimorphism is an isomorphism); the result could be upgraded to residual finiteness, provided that certain hyperbolic groups are residually finite. Secondly, we prove that most quotients of the five-holed sphere mapping class group are hierarchically hyperbolic, addressing
[
MS25
, Question 3]
.

Future directions

We expect that our Theorem
C
can be generalised, namely:

Conjecture G
.

The mapping class group of a non-sporadic finite-type surface has at least two coarse median structures.

In the spirit of our proof of Theorem
A
, a possible approach would be to find suitable homogeneous quasimorphisms on curve stabilisers which vanish on certain Dehn Twist flats, and then use them to produce the quasilines that should replace annular curve graphs. Most likely, this unified strategy would not require the whole machinery of this article, which we decided to include nonetheless in view of its applications to the second paper in this series. We also expect that the construction of the HHG structure with the given quasilines would be more fickle for surfaces of higher complexity.

In Theorem
E
, the coarse median structures we produce are most likely not coarsely equivariant, so we ask:

Question H
.

Does there exist a RACG admitting a
coarsely equivariant
coarse median structure which is not induced by a cocompact cubulation?

This is related to
[
Fio24
, Question 4]
, which asked if RACGs admit finitely many equivariant coarse median structures. In this direction, future work of Behrstock, Çiçeksiz, and Falgas-Ravry shows that generic RACGs admit a unique cubical coarse median structure
[
BCFR25
]
.

Overview of the paper

Section
1
contains the background on hierarchically hyperbolic spaces and groups. There we also provide a description of product regions in a combinatorial HHS (Lemma
1.28
), a tool of independent interest which, to the best of the author’s knowledge, was missing in the literature.

In Section
2
we define short HHG and present the main examples, along with some of their properties. Section
3
develops the machinery to build a short HHG structure for a group. The input of this procedure is what we call
blowup materials
, and include a collection of quasimorphisms which are then used to construct the bottom level quasilines (see Definition
3.9
).

In Section
4
we notice that short HHG admit blowup materials, which can then be modified to introduce new cyclic directions, as explained in Section
4
. In Section
6
we also produce new examples of short HHG by constructing suitable blowup materials.

In Section
7
we prove Theorem
A
, regarding the existence of uncountably many coarse median structures (see Theorem
7.1
). As in the Introduction, Theorems
C
to
F
are then deduced from Corollary
B
, which is Corollary
7.3
below.

Finally, in Appendix
A
we prove how to extract certain quasimorphisms from central extensions (see Corollary
A.4
). We also fill a gap in a proof from
[
HMS22
]
(see Remark
A.5
).

1.
Background

1.1.
What is a HHS

We recall from
[
BHS19
]
the definition of a hierarchically hyperbolic space.

Definition 1.1

(HHS)
.

The quasigeodesic space
({\mathcal{Z}},\mathrm{d}_{{\mathcal{Z}}})
is a
hierarchically hyperbolic space
if there exists
E\geq 1
, called the
HHS constant
, an index set
\mathfrak{S}
, whose elements will be referred to as
domains
, and a set
\{\mathcal{C}U\mid U\in\mathfrak{S}\}
of
E
–hyperbolic spaces
(\mathcal{C}U,\mathrm{d}_{U})
, called
coordinate spaces
, such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

(Projections.)

There is a set
\{\pi_{U}:{\mathcal{Z}}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{C}U}\mid U\in\mathfrak{S}\}
of
projections
mapping points in
{\mathcal{Z}}
to sets of diameter bounded by
E
in the various
\mathcal{C}U\in\mathfrak{S}
. Moreover, for all
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, the coarse map
\pi_{U}
is
(E,E)
–coarsely Lipschitz and
\pi_{U}({\mathcal{Z}})
is
E
–quasiconvex in
\mathcal{C}U
.

(2)

(Nesting.)

\mathfrak{S}
is equipped with a partial order
\sqsubseteq
, and either
\mathfrak{S}=\emptyset
or
\mathfrak{S}
contains a unique
\sqsubseteq
–maximal element, denoted by
S
. When
V\sqsubseteq U
, we say
V
is
nested
in
U
. For each
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, we denote by
\mathfrak{S}_{U}
the set of
V\in\mathfrak{S}
such that
V\sqsubseteq U
. Moreover, for all
U,V\in\mathfrak{S}
with
V\sqsubsetneq U
there is a specified subset
\rho^{V}_{U}\subset\mathcal{C}U
with
\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{C}U}(\rho^{V}_{U})\leq E
. There is also a
projection

\rho^{U}_{V}:\mathcal{C}U\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{C}V}
. (The similarity in notation is justified by viewing
\rho^{V}_{U}
as a coarsely constant map
\mathcal{C}V\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{C}U}
.)

(3)

(Orthogonality.)

\mathfrak{S}
has a symmetric and anti-reflexive relation called
orthogonality
: we write
U\bot V
when
U,V
are orthogonal. Also, whenever
V\sqsubseteq U
and
U\bot W
, we require that
V\bot W
. We require that for each
T\in\mathfrak{S}
and each
U\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}
such that
\{V\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}\mid V\bot U\}\neq\emptyset
, there exists
W\in\mathfrak{S}_{T}-\{T\}
, which we call a
container
for
U
inside
T
, so that whenever
V\bot U
and
V\sqsubseteq T
, we have
V\sqsubseteq W
. Finally, if
U\bot V
, then
U,V
are not
\sqsubseteq
–comparable.

(4)

(Transversality and consistency.)

If
U,V\in\mathfrak{S}
are not orthogonal and neither is nested in the other, then we say
U,V
are
transverse
, denoted
U\pitchfork V
. In this case there are sets
\rho^{V}_{U}\subseteq\mathcal{C}U
and
\rho^{U}_{V}\subseteq\mathcal{C}V
, each of diameter at most
E
and satisfying:

\min\left\{\mathrm{d}_{U}(\pi_{U}(z),\rho^{V}_{U}),\mathrm{d}_{V}(\pi_{V}(z),\rho^{U}_{V})\right\}\leq E

for all
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.

For
U,V\in\mathfrak{S}
satisfying
V\sqsubseteq U
and for all
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, we have:

\min\left\{\mathrm{d}_{U}(\pi_{U}(z),\rho^{V}_{U}),\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{C}V}(\pi_{V}(z)\cup\rho^{U}_{V}(\pi_{U}(z)))\right\}\leq E.

The preceding two inequalities are the
consistency inequalities
for points in
{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Finally, if
U\sqsubseteq V
, then
\mathrm{d}_{W}(\rho^{U}_{W},\rho^{V}_{W})\leq E
whenever
W\in\mathfrak{S}
satisfies either
V\sqsubsetneq W
or
V\pitchfork W
and
W\not\bot U
.

(5)

(Finite complexity.)

There exists
n\geq 0
, the
complexity
of
{\mathcal{Z}}
(with respect to
\mathfrak{S}
), so that any set of pairwise–
\sqsubseteq
–comparable elements has cardinality at most
n
.

(6)

(Large links.)

Let
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, let
z,z^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, and let
N=E\mathrm{d}_{U}(\pi_{U}(z),\pi_{U}(z^{\prime}))+E
. Then there exists
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,\lfloor N\rfloor}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{U}-\{U\}
such that, for any domain
T\in\mathfrak{S}_{U}-\{U\}
, either
T\in\mathfrak{S}_{T_{i}}
for some
i
, or
\mathrm{d}_{T}(\pi_{T}(z),\pi_{T}(z^{\prime}))<E
. Also,
\mathrm{d}_{U}(\pi_{U}(z),\rho^{T_{i}}_{U})\leq N
for each
i
.

(7)

(Bounded geodesic image.)

For all
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, all
V\in\mathfrak{S}_{U}-\{U\}
, and all geodesics
\gamma
of
\mathcal{C}U
, either
\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{C}V}(\rho^{U}_{V}(\gamma))\leq E
or
\gamma\cap\mathcal{N}_{E}(\rho^{V}_{U})\neq\emptyset
.

(8)

(Partial realisation.)

Let
\{V_{j}\}
be a family of pairwise orthogonal elements of
\mathfrak{S}
, and let
p_{j}\in\pi_{V_{j}}({\mathcal{Z}})\subseteq\mathcal{C}V_{j}
. Then there exists
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, which we call a
partial realisation point
for the family, so that:

•

\mathrm{d}_{V_{j}}(z,p_{j})\leq E
for all
j
,

•

for each
j
and
each
V\in\mathfrak{S}
with
V_{j}\sqsubseteq V
, we have

\mathrm{d}_{V}(z,\rho^{V_{j}}_{V})\leq E
, and

•

for each
j
and
each
V\in\mathfrak{S}
with
V_{j}\pitchfork V
, we have
\mathrm{d}_{V}(z,\rho^{V_{j}}_{V})\leq E
.

(9)

(Uniqueness.)
For each
\kappa\geq 0
, there exists

\theta_{u}=\theta_{u}(\kappa)
such that if
x,y\in{\mathcal{Z}}
and

\mathrm{d}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(x,y)\geq\theta_{u}
, then there exists
V\in\mathfrak{S}
such
that
\mathrm{d}_{V}(x,y)\geq\kappa
.

We often refer to
\mathfrak{S}
, together with the nesting and orthogonality relations, and the projections as a
hierarchically hyperbolic structure
for the space
{\mathcal{Z}}
, denoted by
({\mathcal{Z}},\mathfrak{S})
.

Remark 1.2

(Normalisation)
.

As argued in
[
BHS19
, Remark 1.3]
, it is always possible to assume that the HHS structure is
normalised
, that is, for every
U\in\mathfrak{S}
the projection
\pi_{U}:\,{\mathcal{Z}}\to\mathcal{C}U
is uniformly coarsely surjective.

Notation 1.3
.

Where it will not cause confusion, given
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, we will often suppress the projection map
\pi_{U}
when writing distances in
\mathcal{C}U
, i.e., given
x,y\in{\mathcal{Z}}
and
p\in\mathcal{C}U
we write
\mathrm{d}_{U}(x,y)
for
\mathrm{d}_{U}(\pi_{U}(x),\pi_{U}(y))
and
\mathrm{d}_{U}(x,p)
for
\mathrm{d}_{U}(\pi_{U}(x),p)
. Note that when we measure distance between a pair of sets (typically both of bounded diameter) we are taking the minimum distance between the two sets. Given
A\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}
and
U\in\mathfrak{S}
we set

\pi_{U}(A)=\bigcup_{a\in A}\pi_{U}(a).

1.1.1.
Factors, consistency and hierarchical quasiconvexity

Definition 1.4

(Consistent tuple)
.

Let
\kappa\geq 1
and let
(b_{U})_{U\in\mathfrak{S}}\in\prod_{U\in\mathfrak{S}}2^{\mathcal{C}U}
be a tuple such that for each
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, the
U
–coordinate
b_{U}
has diameter
\leq\kappa
. Then
(b_{U})_{U\in\mathfrak{S}}
is
\kappa
–consistent
if for all
V,W\in\mathfrak{S}
, we have

\min\{\mathrm{d}_{V}(b_{V},\rho^{W}_{V}),\mathrm{d}_{W}(b_{W},\rho^{V}_{W})\}\leq\kappa

whenever
V\pitchfork W
and

\min\{\mathrm{d}_{W}(b_{W},\rho^{V}_{W}),\mathrm{diam}_{V}(b_{V}\cup\rho^{W}_{V}(b_{W}))\}\leq\kappa

whenever
V\sqsubsetneq W
.

The following is
[
BHS19
, Theorem 3.1]
:

Theorem 1.5

(Realisation)
.

Let
({\mathcal{Z}},\mathfrak{S})
be a hierarchically hyperbolic space. Then for each
\kappa\geq 1
, there exists
\theta=\theta(\kappa)
so that, for any
\kappa
–consistent tuple
(b_{U})_{U\in\mathfrak{S}}
, there exists
x\in{\mathcal{Z}}
such that
\mathrm{d}_{U}(x,b_{U})\leq\theta
for all
U\in\mathfrak{S}
.

Observe that the uniqueness axiom (Definition (
9
)) implies that the
realisation point

x
for
(b_{U})_{U\in\mathfrak{S}}
, provided by Theorem
1.5
, is coarsely unique.

Definition 1.6

(Product regions and factors)
.

Fix a constant
\kappa\geq E
. For any domain
U
, let
F_{U}
be the set of
\kappa
-consistent tuples for
U
, that is, all tuples
(b_{V})_{V\in\mathfrak{S}_{U}}
that satisfy the consistency inequalities. Similarly, one can define
E_{U}
as the set of
\kappa
-consistent tuples of the form
(b_{V})_{V\bot U}
.

Now let
P_{U}=F_{U}\times E_{U}
, which we call the
product region
associated to
U
. By the realisation Theorem
1.5
there is a coarsely well-defined map
\phi\colon P_{U}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
, constructed as follows:
a pair
(y,z)=\left((y_{V})_{V\sqsubseteq U},(z_{V})_{V\bot U}\right)\in F_{U}\times E_{U}
is first extended to a full tuple
(x_{V})_{V\in\mathfrak{S}}
, defined as follows:

x_{V}=\begin{cases}y_{V}\mbox{ if }V\sqsubseteq U;\\
z_{V}\mbox{ if }V\bot U;\\
\rho^{U}_{V}\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Such a tuple is
\kappa^{\prime}
-consistent, for some
\kappa^{\prime}
depending on
\kappa
and
E
, and therefore admits a realisation point. Then one sets
\phi(y,z)
as this realisation point.

We can metrise
\phi(P_{U})
, which we will still denote by
P_{U}
, by endowing it with the subspace metric. Similarly, if we fix
e\in E_{U}
, the image of the
factor

F_{U}\times\{e\}
, which we will still denote by
F_{U}
when the dependence on
e
is irrelevant, can be seen as a sub-HHS of
{\mathcal{Z}}
with domain set
\mathfrak{S}_{U}=\{V\in\mathfrak{S}|V\sqsubseteq U\}
. Two parallel copies
F_{U}\times\{e\}
and
F_{U}\times\{e^{\prime}\}
are quasi-isometric (see e.g.
[
DHS20
, Section 2.2]
for more details), thus the metric structure on
F_{U}
is well-defined up to quasi-isometry.

Definition 1.7

(Hierarchical quasiconvexity and gates)
.

A subset
{\mathcal{Y}}\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}
is
hierarchically quasiconvex
if there exists a function
t\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that the
following hold:

•

For all
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, the projection
\pi_{U}({\mathcal{Y}})
is a
t(0)
–quasiconvex subspace of
\mathcal{C}U
.

•

Realization property
:
For all
\kappa\geq 0
and every point
x\in{\mathcal{Z}}
for which
\mathrm{d}_{U}(\pi_{U}(x),\pi_{U}({\mathcal{Y}}))\leq\kappa
for all
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, we have that
\mathrm{d}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(x,{\mathcal{Y}})\leq t(\kappa)
.

Whenever
{\mathcal{Y}}
is hierarchically quasiconvex, there exists a coarsely Lipschitz, coarse retraction
\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}\colon{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
, called the
gate
on
{\mathcal{Y}}
, such that for every
x\in{\mathcal{Z}}
and every
W\in\mathfrak{S}
, we have that

\pi_{W}(\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}(z))
uniformly coarsely coincides with the projection of
\pi_{W}(x)
onto the quasiconvex set
\pi_{W}({\mathcal{Y}})
. See
[
BHS19
, Lemma 5.5]
for further details.

Remark 1.8
.

Product regions and factors are hierarchically quasiconvex (see
[
BHS19
, Construction 5.10]
). Furthermore, by inspection of how
F_{U}
embeds inside
{\mathcal{Z}}
, one gets the following explicit description of the gate
\mathfrak{g}_{F_{U}}
: for every
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
,
\mathfrak{g}_{F_{U}}(z)
has coarsely the same projection as
z
to every
\mathcal{C}W
such that
W\sqsubseteq U
, and coarsely coincides with
\rho^{U}_{W}
otherwise. Similarly, the gate
\mathfrak{g}_{P_{U}}(z)
has coarsely the same projection as
z
to every
\mathcal{C}W
whenever
W
is either nested in or orthogonal to
U
, and projects uniformly close to
\rho^{U}_{W}
otherwise.

Remark 1.9

(Dependence of
P_{U}
on
\kappa
)
.

Let
P_{U}^{E}
(resp.
P_{U}^{\kappa}
) be the product region for
U\in\mathfrak{S}
constructed with
E
-consistent tuples (resp. with
\kappa
-consistent tuples, for some
\kappa\geq E
). Clearly
P_{U}^{E}\subseteq P_{U}^{\kappa}
. Conversely, given a point
p\in P_{U}^{\kappa}
, notice that
p
and its gate
\mathfrak{g}_{P_{U}^{E}}(p)
have coarsely the same projection to every coordinate space (this is because
\pi_{W}(P_{U}^{E})
is coarsely dense in
\mathcal{C}W
whenever
W\sqsubseteq U
or
W\bot U
, and coarsely coincides with
\rho^{U}_{W}
otherwise). Therefore, the uniqueness axiom (
9
) gives that
p
and its gate are uniformly close, which in turn means that
P_{U}^{E}
and
P_{U}^{\kappa}
are within finite Hausdorff distance. Thus we can talk about
the
product region associated to
U
as any subspace within finite Hausdorff distance from
P_{U}^{E}
.

1.2.
Hierarchically hyperbolic groups

Definition 1.10

(Automorphism)
.

Let
({{\mathcal{Z}}},\mathfrak{S})
be a HHS. An

consists of a map

, a bijection
g^{\sharp}:\,\mathfrak{S}\to\mathfrak{S}
preserving nesting and orthogonality, and, for each

, an isometry
g^{\diamond}(U):\,\mathcal{C}U\to\mathcal{C}(g^{\sharp}(U))
for which the following two diagrams commute for all
U,V\in\mathfrak{S}
such that
U\sqsubsetneq V
or
U\pitchfork V
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathcal{Z}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathcal{Z}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}(g^{\sharp}(U))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{g^{\sharp}(U)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\diamond}(U)}

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}(g^{\sharp}(U))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}(g^{\sharp}(V))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\diamond}(U)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{U}_{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{g^{\sharp}(U)}_{g^{\sharp}(V)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\diamond}(V)}

Whenever it will not cause ambiguity, we will abuse notation by dropping the superscripts and just calling all maps

.

The above definition of an automorphism is equivalent to the original formulation, which is
[
BHS19
, Definition 1.20]
(see the discussion in
[
DHS20
, Section 2.1]
for further explanations).

We say that two automorphisms
g,g^{\prime}
are

if
g^{\sharp}=(g^{\prime})^{\sharp}
and
g^{\diamond}(U)=(g^{\prime})^{\diamond}(U)
for each

. Given an automorphism

, a quasi-inverse
\overline{g}
for

is an automorphism with
\overline{g}^{\sharp}=(g^{\sharp})^{-1}
and such that, for every
U\in\mathfrak{S}
,
\overline{g}^{\diamond}(U)=g^{\diamond}(U)^{-1}
. Since the composition of two automorphisms is an automorphism, the set of equivalence classes of automorphisms forms a group, denoted
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Definition 1.11
.

A finitely generated group
G

acts
on a HHS
({{\mathcal{Z}}},\mathfrak{S})
by automorphisms if there is a group homomorphism
G\to\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Definition 1.12

(HHG)
.

A finitely generated group
G
is
hierarchically hyperbolic
if there exists a hierarchically hyperbolic space
({{\mathcal{Z}}},\mathfrak{S})
and an action
G\rightarrow\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S})
so that the uniform quasi-action of
G
on
{{\mathcal{Z}}}
is
geometric
(by which we will always mean
metrically proper
and
cobounded
), and
\mathfrak{S}
contains finitely many
G
–orbits. Then we can equip
G
with a HHS structure, whose domains and coordinate spaces are the same as the ones for
{{\mathcal{Z}}}
, and whose projections are obtained by precomposing the projections for
({{\mathcal{Z}}},\mathfrak{S})
with a
G
-equivariant quasi-isometry
G\to{{\mathcal{Z}}}
given by the Milnor- Švarc lemma. We call such a structure a
HHG structure
for
G
.

Now let
U\in\mathfrak{S}
be a domain of a HHG, and let
P_{U}
be the associated product region, which we see as a collection of
E
-consistent tuples, as explained in Remark
1.9
. Then
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(U)
acts on
P_{U}
, as it permutes such tuples. Furthermore, when we see
P_{U}
as a subspace of
{\mathcal{Z}}
and we endow it with the subspace metric, the action is metrically proper, since
G
acts metrically properly on the whole
{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Definition 1.13

(Cobounded product regions)
.

A HHG
(G,\mathfrak{S})
has
cobounded product regions
if, for every
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, its stabiliser
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(U)
acts coboundedly on the corresponding product region
P_{U}
(and therefore geometrically).

The above property is satisfied by all naturally occurring examples of HHG, to the point that some people argue it should be part of the definition of a HHG.

Remark 1.14

(Main coordinate space in a HHS/G)
.

In a normalised HHS
{\mathcal{Z}}
, the main coordinate space
\mathcal{C}S
is quasi-isometric to the
factored space

\widehat{Z}
, obtained from
{\mathcal{Z}}
by adding an edge between any two points belonging to the same proper product regions (this is
[
BHS17a
, Corollary 2.9]
). Now, suppose that
G
is a normalised HHG with cobounded product regions, in the sense of Definition
1.13
, and fix a finite generating set
T
for
G
and a collection
U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}
of orbit representative for the
G
-action on
\mathfrak{S}-\{S\}
. Then the above observation implies that
\mathcal{C}S
is quasi isometric to

\operatorname{Cay}\left(G,T\cup\{\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(U_{i})\}_{i=1,\ldots,r}\right).

1.3.
Combinatorial HHS

In this Section we recall the definition of a combinatorial HHS and its hierarchically hyperbolic structure, as first introduced in
[
BHMS24
]
.

Definition 1.15

(Induced subgraph)
.

Let
X
be a simplicial graph. Given a subset
S\subseteq X^{(0)}
of the set of vertices of
X
, the subgraph
spanned
by
S
is the complete subgraph of
X
with vertex set
S
.

Definition 1.16

(Join, link, star)
.

Given disjoint simplices
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
of
X
, we let
\Delta\star\Delta^{\prime}
denote the simplex spanned by
\Delta^{(0)}\cup\Delta^{\prime(0)}
, if it exists.

For each simplex
\Delta
, the
link

\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)
is the union of
all simplices
\Sigma
of
X
such that
\Sigma\cap\Delta=\emptyset
and
\Sigma\star\Delta
is a simplex of
X
. Observe that
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)=\emptyset
if and only if
\Delta
is a maximal simplex.

The
star
of
\Delta
is
\operatorname{Star}(\Delta)\coloneq\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)\star\Delta
, i.e. the union of all simplices of
X
that contain
\Delta
.

Definition 1.17

(
X
–graph,
\mathcal{W}
–augmented graph)
.

An
X
–graph
is a graph
\mathcal{W}
whose vertex set is the set of all maximal simplices of
X
.

For a simplicial graph
X
and an
X
–graph
\mathcal{W}
, the
\mathcal{W}
–augmented graph

{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
is the graph defined as follows:

•

the
0
–skeleton of
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
is
X^{(0)}
;

•

if
v,w\in X^{(0)}
are adjacent in
X
, then they are adjacent in
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
;

•

if two vertices in
\mathcal{W}
are adjacent, then we consider
\sigma,\rho
, the associated maximal simplices of
X
, and in
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
we connect each vertex of
\sigma
to each vertex of
\rho
.

We equip
\mathcal{W}
with the usual path-metric, in which each edge has unit length, and do the same for
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
.

Definition 1.18

(Equivalence between simplices, saturation)
.

For
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
simplices of
X
, we write
\Delta\sim\Delta^{\prime}
to mean
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)=\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta^{\prime})
. We denote the
\sim
–equivalence class of
\Delta
by
[\Delta]
.
Let
\mathrm{Sat}(\Delta)
denote the set of vertices
v\in X
for which there exists a simplex
\Delta^{\prime}
of
X
such that
v\in\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\prime}\sim\Delta
, i.e.

\mathrm{Sat}(\Delta)=\left(\bigcup_{\Delta^{\prime}\in[\Delta]}\Delta^{\prime}\right)^{(0)}.

We denote by
\mathfrak{S}
the set of
\sim
–classes of non-maximal simplices in
X
.

Definition 1.19

(Complement, link subgraph)
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be an
X
–graph. For each simplex
\Delta
of
X
, let
Y_{\Delta}
be the subgraph of
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
induced by the set
({X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}})^{(0)}-\mathrm{Sat}(\Delta)
of vertices. Let
\mathcal{C}(\Delta)
be the induced subgraph of
Y_{\Delta}
spanned by
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)^{(0)}
, which we call the
augmented link
of
\Delta
. Note that
\mathcal{C}(\Delta)=\mathcal{C}(\Delta^{\prime})
whenever
\Delta\sim\Delta^{\prime}
. (We emphasise that we are taking links in
X
, not in
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
, and then considering the subgraphs of
Y_{\Delta}
induced by those links.)

Definition 1.20

(Combinatorial HHS)
.

A
combinatorial HHS

(X,\mathcal{W})
consists of a simplicial graph
X
and an
X
–graph
\mathcal{W}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
, called the
complexity
of
X
, such that any chain
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta_{1})\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta_{i})
, where each
\Delta_{j}
is a simplex of
X
, has length at most
n
;

(2)

There is a constant
\delta
so that for each non-maximal simplex
\Delta
, the subgraph
\mathcal{C}(\Delta)
is
\delta
–hyperbolic and
(\delta,\delta)
–quasi-isometrically embedded in
Y_{\Delta}
, where
Y_{\Delta}
is as in Definition
1.19
;

(3)

Whenever
\Delta
and
\Sigma
are non-maximal simplices for which there exists a non-maximal simplex
\Gamma
such that
\operatorname{Lk}(\Gamma)\subseteq\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)\cap\operatorname{Lk}(\Sigma)
, and
\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}(\Gamma))\geq\delta
, then there exists a simplex
\Pi
which extends
\Sigma
such that
\operatorname{Lk}(\Pi)\subseteq\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)
, and all
\Gamma
as above satisfy
\operatorname{Lk}(\Gamma)\subseteq\operatorname{Lk}(\Pi)
;

(4)

If
v,w
are distinct non-adjacent vertices of
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)
, for some simplex
\Delta
of
X
, contained in
\mathcal{W}
-adjacent maximal simplices, then they are contained in
\mathcal{W}
-adjacent simplices of the form
\Delta\star\Sigma
.

Definition 1.21

(Nesting, orthogonality, transversality, complexity)
.

Let
X
be a simplicial graph. Let
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
be non-maximal simplices of
X
. Then:

•

[\Delta]\sqsubseteq[\Delta^{\prime}]
if
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)\subseteq\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta^{\prime})
;

•

[\Delta]\bot[\Delta^{\prime}]
if
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta^{\prime})\subseteq\operatorname{Lk}(\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta))
.

If
[\Delta]
and
[\Delta^{\prime}]
are neither
\bot
–related nor
\sqsubseteq
–related, we write

[\Delta]\pitchfork[\Delta^{\prime}]
.

Definition 1.22

(Projections)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{W},\delta,n)
be a combinatorial HHS.

Fix
[\Delta]\in\mathfrak{S}
and define a map
\pi_{[\Delta]}:\mathcal{W}\to 2^{\mathcal{C}([\Delta])}
as follows. Let

p:Y_{\Delta}\to 2^{\mathcal{C}([\Delta])}

be the coarse closest point projection, i.e.

p(x)=\{y\in\mathcal{C}([\Delta]):\mathrm{d}_{Y_{\Delta}}(x,y)\leq\mathrm{d}_{Y_{\Delta}}(x,\mathcal{C}([\Delta]))+1\}.

Suppose that
w
is a vertex of
\mathcal{W}
, so
w
corresponds to a unique simplex
\Sigma_{w}
of
X
. Now,
[
BHMS24
, Lemma 1.15]
states that the intersection
\Sigma_{w}\cap Y_{\Delta}
is non-empty and has diameter at most
1
. Define

\pi_{[\Delta]}(w)=p(\Sigma_{w}\cap Y_{\Delta}).

We have thus defined
\pi_{[\Delta]}:\mathcal{W}^{(0)}\to 2^{\mathcal{C}([\Delta])}
. If
v,w\in\mathcal{W}
are joined by an edge
e
of
\mathcal{W}
, then
\Sigma_{v},\Sigma_{w}
are joined by edges in
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
, and we let

\pi_{[\Delta]}(e)=\pi_{[\Delta]}(v)\cup\pi_{[\Delta]}(w).

Now let
[\Delta],[\Delta^{\prime}]\in\mathfrak{S}
satisfy
[\Delta]\pitchfork[\Delta^{\prime}]
or
[\Delta^{\prime}]\sqsubsetneq[\Delta]
, and set

\rho^{[\Delta^{\prime}]}_{[\Delta]}=p(\mathrm{Sat}(\Delta^{\prime})\cap Y_{\Delta}).

Finally, if
[\Delta]\sqsubsetneq[\Delta^{\prime}]
, let
\rho^{[\Delta^{\prime}]}_{[\Delta]}:\mathcal{C}([\Delta^{\prime}])\to\mathcal{C}([\Delta])
be defined as follows. On
\mathcal{C}([\Delta^{\prime}])\cap Y_{\Delta}
, it is the restriction of
p
to
\mathcal{C}([\Delta^{\prime}])\cap Y_{\Delta}
. Otherwise, it takes the value
\emptyset
.

We are finally ready to state the main theorem of
[
BHMS24
]
:

Theorem 1.23

(HHS structures for
X
–graphs)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{W})
be a combinatorial HHS. Then
\mathcal{W}
is a hierarchically hyperbolic space with the structure defined above.

Moreover, let
G
be a group acting on
X
with finitely many orbits of subcomplexes of the form
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)
, where
\Delta
is a simplex of
X
. Suppose moreover that the action on maximal simplices of
X
extends to an action on
\mathcal{W}
, which is metrically proper and cobounded. Then
G
is a HHG.

Definition 1.24
.

We will say that a group
G
satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.23
is a
combinatorial
HHG.

Remark 1.25
.

Notice that, by how the projections
\pi_{[\Delta]}
are defined, a combinatorial HHS is always normalised, as in Remark
1.2
.

We also recall that combinatorial HHS satisfy the following strengthening of the bounded geodesic image axiom, which forces a geodesic with large projection to a subdomain to pass through the
\rho
-point (and not just close to it):

Lemma 1.26

(Strong BGI)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{W})
be a combinatorial HHS. Then
\mathcal{W}
satisfies the
strong Bounded Geodesic Image
axiom: there exists
M>0
such that, for every
[\Sigma]\subseteq[\Delta]
and every
x,y\in\mathcal{C}(\Delta)
, if
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)}\left(\rho^{[\Delta]}_{[\Sigma]}(x),\rho^{[\Delta]}_{[\Sigma]}(y)\right)\geq M
, then every geodesic
[x,y]\subseteq\mathcal{C}(\Delta)
has a vertex in
\mathrm{Sat}(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

This follows by combining
[
BHMS24
, Theorem 4.9 and Lemma 5.2]
.
∎

1.3.1.
Product regions in a combinatorial HHS

Here we describe the structure of product regions in a CHHS. We shall assume the following mild property, satisfied by most known examples, which should be thought of as a weak analogue of the
wedge
property from
[
BR20
]
.

Definition 1.27
.

A combinatorial HHS
(X,\mathcal{W})
has
weak simplicial containers
if for every simplex
\Delta\subseteq X
there exist two (possibly empty) simplices
\Theta,\Psi\subseteq X
such that

\operatorname{Lk}(\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta))=\operatorname{Lk}(\Theta)\star\Psi.

Lemma 1.28
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{W})
be a combinatorial HHS with weak simplicial containers, and let
\Delta
be a non-maximal simplex of
X
. Then the product region
P_{[\Delta]}
, associated to the domain
[\Delta]
, uniformly coarsely coincides with the set of maximal simplices of
X
of the form
\Sigma=\Pi_{1}\star\Pi_{2}\star\Pi_{3}
, where

•

\Pi_{1}
is any simplex which is maximal inside
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)
;

•

\Pi_{2}
is any simplex which is maximal inside
\operatorname{Lk}(\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta))
;

•

\Pi_{1}
is any simplex which is maximal inside
\operatorname{Lk}(\Pi_{1}\star\Pi_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma=\Pi_{1}\star\Pi_{2}\star\Pi_{3}
be any maximal simplex as in the statement. Arguing as in
[
BHMS24
, Section 5.2,
partial realization
]
, one can prove that the projection of
\Sigma
to any domain
[\Delta^{\prime}]
which is neither nested into nor orthogonal to
[\Delta]
is uniformly close to the projection
\rho^{[\Delta]}_{[\Delta^{\prime}]}
. In other words,
\Sigma
is uniformly close to some point in
P_{[\Delta]}
.

Conversely, pick a pair

((b_{[\Delta^{\prime}]})_{[\Delta^{\prime}]\sqsubseteq[\Delta]},(c_{[\Delta^{\prime\prime}]})_{[\Delta^{\prime\prime}]\bot[\Delta]})\in F_{[\Delta]}\times E_{[\Delta]}=P_{[\Delta]},

and we want to show that it is realised by some
\Sigma
as in the statement. Notice that
F_{[\Delta]}
is the set of
E
-consistent tuples of the combinatorial HHS
(\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta),\mathcal{W}^{\Delta})
, where two maximal simplices
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}\subset\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)
are
\mathcal{W}^{\Delta}
-adjacent if and only if
\Phi\star\Delta
and
\Phi^{\prime}\star\Delta
are
\mathcal{W}
-adjacent (see
[
BHMS24
, Theorem 4.9]
). Therefore, by the realisation Theorem
1.5
, applied to
(\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta),\mathcal{W}^{\Delta})
,
(b_{[\Delta^{\prime}]})_{[\Delta^{\prime}]\sqsubseteq[\Delta]}
must be realised by some simplex
\Pi_{1}
which is maximal in
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)
.

Similarly,
(c_{[\Delta^{\prime\prime}]})_{[\Delta^{\prime\prime}]\bot[\Delta]}
is a
E
-consistent tuple of
E_{[\Delta]}
. Now,
[\Delta^{\prime\prime}]\bot[\Delta]
means that
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\operatorname{Lk}(\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta))=\operatorname{Lk}(\Theta)\star\Psi
. If
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta^{\prime\prime})\cap\Psi\neq\emptyset
then
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta^{\prime\prime})
is a join, and therefore
\mathcal{C}(\Delta^{\prime\prime})
has diameter at most
2
. Hence
(c_{[\Delta^{\prime\prime}]})_{[\Delta^{\prime\prime}]\bot[\Delta]}
and its restriction to
F_{[\Theta]}
have the same realisation points, and as above the restriction to
F_{[\Theta]}
is realised by some simplex
\Pi^{\prime}_{2}
which is maximal in
\operatorname{Lk}(\Theta)
. Then let
\Pi_{2}=\Pi_{2}^{\prime}\star\Psi
, and complete
\Pi_{1}\star\Pi_{2}
to a maximal simplex
\Sigma=\Pi_{1}\star\Pi_{2}\star\Pi_{3}
. By construction
\Sigma
realises
(b_{[\Delta^{\prime}]},c_{[\Delta^{\prime\prime}]})
, and we are done.
∎

2.1.
Blowup graphs

Definition 2.1

(Blowup graph)

Let
\overline{X}
be a simplicial graph, whose vertices are labelled by graphs
\{L_{v}\}_{v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}}
.
The

by replacing every vertex

with the

and
\operatorname{Cone}(w)
span a join in

are adjacent in

We can (and will) identify

with its image under the embedding

2.2.
Definition

Let
G
be a combinatorial HHG, whose structure comes from the action on the combinatorial HHS
(X,\mathcal{W})
. We say that
G
is

if it satisfies Axioms (
A
)-(
B
)-(

) below.

Axiom A

(Underlying graph)
.

X
is obtained as a blowup of some graph
\overline{X}
, which is triangle- and square-free and such that no connected component of
\overline{X}
is a point. Moreover,
\overline{X}
is a
G
-invariant subgraph of
X
.

The above Axiom implies, in particular, that the
G
-action on
X
restricts to a
G
-action on
\overline{X}
with finitely many
G
-orbits of vertices and edges.

Axiom B

(Vertex stabilisers are cyclic-by-hyperbolic)
.

For every
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
there is an extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{v}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{v}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{p}_{v}}

where
H_{v}
is a finitely generated hyperbolic group and

is a cyclic, normal subgroup of
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
which acts trivially on
\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)
. We call

the

associated to

Moreover, one requires that the family of such extensions is equivariant with respect to the

for every

and

.

Notation 2.4
.

For every
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
, let
\ell_{v}=[\Delta]
be the domain associated to any triangle-type simplex whose link is
(L_{v})^{(0)}
, and let
\mathcal{U}_{v}=[\Sigma]
for any edge-type simplex supported on

.

Axiom C

((Co)bounded actions)
.

For every

, the cyclic direction

acts geometrically on

and with uniformly bounded orbits on

is a quasiline if

We will denote a short HHG, together with its short structure, by

.

2.3.
Examples

Here we list some known combinatorial HHG which happen to be short. All examples (possibly excluding the last one, see the footnote below) also enjoy the following property, which will be relevant when taking quotients in the second paper:

Definition 2.5
.

A short HHG

2.3.1.
Mapping class group of a 5-holed sphere

Let

be a two-dimensional sphere with five points removed, and let

be its (extended) mapping class group, that is, the group of self-homeomorphism of

up to isotopy. In
[

•

2.4.4.
(Relative) hyperbolicity

Lemma 2.14
.

A short HHG whose cyclic directions are all bounded is hyperbolic.

Proof.

As each cyclic direction
Z_{v}
acts coboundedly on the associated
\mathcal{C}\ell_{v}
, we have that the latter is always bounded. Thus, by
Remark
2.6
the collection of domains with unbounded coordinate spaces is contained in

\{S\}\cup\{\mathcal{U}_{v}\}_{\{v\in\overline{X}^{(0)},\,|\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)|=\infty\}}.

Moreover, as described in the same Remark, no two domains in this collection are orthogonal, and therefore
G
is hyperbolic by e.g.
[
BHS21
, Corollary 2.16]
.
∎

We now show that, for every vertex
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
,
H_{v}
has a simple relatively hyperbolic structure, whose peripherals are (finite index overgroups of) the adjacent central directions. We first need two lemmas:

Lemma 2.15
.

Whenever
w,w^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)
are distinct, the intersection
Z_{w}\cap Z_{w^{\prime}}
is finite.

Proof.

The corollary trivially holds if either
Z_{w}
or
Z_{w^{\prime}}
is finite. Otherwise, let
g\in Z_{w}\cap Z_{w^{\prime}}
. Since
g\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(w)\cap\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v^{\prime})
, it must fix the projection
\rho^{w^{\prime}}_{w}\in\mathcal{C}\ell_{w}
. Therefore
g=1
, as every non-trivial element of
Z_{w}
acts loxodromically on
\mathcal{C}\ell_{w}
.
∎

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
, and let
W
be a collection of
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
-orbit representatives of vertices in
\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)
with unbounded cyclic directions. The collection
\{\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w})\}_{w\in W}
is
independent
in
H_{v}
, that is, for any two different
w,w^{\prime}\in W
and any
h\in H
,
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w})^{h}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w^{\prime}})=\{1\}
.

Proof.

Given
h,w,w^{\prime}
as in the statement, let
Z_{v}=\langle z_{v}\rangle
, and similarly define
z_{w}
and
z_{w^{\prime}}
. Let
g\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
be a preimage of
h
. If
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w})^{h}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w^{\prime}})
was not trivial, one could find integers
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}-\{0\}
,
c\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

z_{gw}^{a}=gz_{w}^{a}g^{-1}=z_{w^{\prime}}^{b}z_{v}^{c}.

Notice that
gw\neq w^{\prime}
, as
w
and
w^{\prime}
are in different
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
-orbits. But this yields a contradiction, as the element on the right-hand side fixes
w^{\prime}
while
z_{gw}
does not (again, because it acts loxodromically on
\mathcal{C}\ell_{gw}
and therefore does not fix the projection
\rho^{w^{\prime}}_{gw}
).
∎

Now recall that, if
H
is a hyperbolic group and
h\in H
has infinite order, then
\langle h\rangle
is undistorted in
H
by e.g.
[
BH99
, Corollary III.3.10]
. In particular, it acts loxodromically on the Cayley graph for
H
with respect to any finite set of generators
S
. Furthermore, since
H
acts properly discontinuously on
\operatorname{Cay}\left(H,S\right)
,

is
weakly properly discontinuous
, or
WPD
, in the sense of e.g.
[
BF02
]
.
Then
[
DGO17
, Lemma 6.5]
grants the existence of a maximal virtually cyclic subgroup
K_{H}(h)\leq H
containing
h
. Notice that
K_{H}(h)=K_{H}(h^{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}-\{0\}
(as a consequence of e.g.
[
DGO17
, Corollary 6.6]
); hence, if
Q
is a cyclic subgroup acting loxodromically on
\operatorname{Cay}\left(H,S\right)
, with a little abuse of notation we can define
K_{H}(Q)\coloneq K_{H}(h)
for some (equivalently, any) non-trivial

.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
be a short HHG,
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
, and let

\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
-orbit representatives of vertices in

Let
F_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}
be the factor associated to
\mathcal{U}_{v}
, which is a HHS whose domain set corresponds to the domains nested in
\mathcal{U}_{v}
. Hence, every domain for

\{\mathcal{U}_{v}\}\cup\bigcup_{w\in\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)}\ell_{w}.

Since no two domains in such collection are orthogonal,
[

Now,
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
acts on

acts trivially on
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{U}_{v}=\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)^{+\mathcal{W}}

.(

and every

\mathrm{d}_{F_{v}}\left((hh^{\prime})(x),h(h^{\prime}(x))\right)\leq B.

Claim 2.18
.

The quasi-action is metrically proper and cobounded.

Proof of Claim
2.18
.

First, as short HHG have cobounded product regions (Lemma
2.13
),
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
acts coboundedly on
P_{\ell_{v}}=F_{\ell_{v}}\times F_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}
. The action is simply the direct product of the actions on the factors, so
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
acts coboundedly on
F_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}
as well. Furthermore, as
Z_{v}
acts with uniformly bounded orbits, the quasi-action of
H_{v}
on
F_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}
is still cobounded.

Moving to metric properness, fix any point
x\in F_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}
and any radius
R\geq 0
. Suppose that
h\in H_{v}
is such that
\mathrm{d}_{F_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}}(x,hx)\leq R
, and we want to show that
h
belongs to a finite collection of elements. For every
g\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g)=h
, we have that

\mathrm{d}_{F_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}}(x,gx)\leq R+B.

Now fix
p\in F_{\ell_{v}}
. As
Z_{v}
acts coboundedly on
F_{\ell_{v}}
, we can choose
g
in such a way that
\mathrm{d}_{F_{\ell_{v}}}(p,gp)
is bounded above by some constant

. But then, if we see the pair
(p,x)
as a point in
P_{\ell_{v}}
, we have that
\mathrm{d}_{P_{\ell_{v}}}\left((p,x),g(p,x)\right)
is bounded above in terms of

,

, and
B^{\prime}
. Since
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
acts metrically properly on
P_{\ell_{v}}
, there are finitely many such

, and therefore finitely many such

.
∎

Combining the Claim with e.g.
[
Man05
, Proposition 4.4]
we have that
H_{v}
acts metrically properly and coboundedly on a geodesic metric space

quasi-isometric to

, which is therefore hyperbolic; thus

is a hyperbolic group.

Moving to the relative hyperbolic structure,
\{\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{W})\}_{w\in W}
is an independent collection of infinite cyclic subgroups, which act loxodromically (hence WPD) on
\operatorname{Cay}\left(H_{v},T\right)
for some finite generating set

. Then by
[
DGO17
, Theorem 6.8]
the collection
\{K_{H_{v}}(\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w}))\}_{w\in W}
is
hyperbolically embedded
in
(H_{v},T)
, in the sense of
[
DGO17
, Definition 2.1]
. Then, since
T
is finite,
[
DGO17
, Proposition 4.28]
yields that
H_{v}
is hyperbolic relative to the collection, as required.
∎

2.4.5.
Absence of hidden symmetries

Definition 2.19

(No hidden symmetries)
.

Let
0\to\mathbb{Z}\to G\xrightarrow{\pi}H\to 1
be a group extension, with
H
hyperbolic, and let
C\leq G
be a cyclic subgroup. We say that
C
has
no hidden symmetries
if
\pi(C)
is infinite and
C
contains a finite-index subgroup which is normal in
\pi^{-1}(K_{H}(\pi(C)))
. We say that an element
g\in G
has no hidden symmetries if
\langle g\rangle
has no hidden symmetries.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
be a short HHG, and let
v,w\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
be adjacent vertices with infinite cyclic directions. Then
Z_{w}
has no hidden symmetries in
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}-\{0\}
be such that
\langle\mathfrak{p}_{v}(z_{w}^{n})\rangle
is normal in
K_{H_{v}}(\mathfrak{p}_{v}(z_{w}))
. Then for every
p\in\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{-1}(K_{H_{v}}(\mathfrak{p}_{v}(z_{w})))
there exist
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
such that

z_{w}^{\varepsilon n}=pz_{w}^{n}p^{-1}z_{v}^{k}=z_{pw}^{n}z_{v}^{k}.

The vertices
w
and
pw
are both in
\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)
, so they either coincide or are not adjacent. In the latter case we would get a contradiction, because the right hand side would fix
pw
while the left hand side would not (again, since
Z_{w}
acts loxodromically on
\mathcal{C}\ell_{w}
and therefore cannot fix
\rho^{pw}_{w}
). Then
w=pw
, meaning that
z_{w}^{(\varepsilon-1)n}=z_{v}^{k}
. In turn, this is possible if and only if
\varepsilon=1
and
k=0
, because
Z_{v}
acts with uniformly bounded orbits on
\mathcal{C}\ell_{w}
. This proves that every
p\in\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{-1}(K_{H_{v}}(\mathfrak{p}_{v}(z_{w})))
normalises
\langle z_{w}^{n}\rangle
, as required.
∎

3.
Short structures from blowup materials

In this Section we describe a procedure to manufacture a short HHG structure on a group. The inputs of our machinery are what we call
blowup materials
, which roughly consist of:

•

a graph
\overline{X}
to blow-up, on which
G
acts with cyclic-by-hyperbolic vertex stabilisers;

•

a choice of a
quasimorphism

\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)\to\mathbb{R}
every vertex
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
, which we use to build the coordinate space associated to
\ell_{v}
(see Subsection
3.1
below);

•

a family of coarse retractions
G\to\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
, which we use to define projections.

As we shall see in Proposition
4.1
, admitting blowup materials is equivalent to being a short HHG. However, the advantage of this point of view is that one is often able to replace some blowup materials to get a plethora of different short HHG structures. This will be the source of the non-equivalent coarse medians (see Section
7
).

3.1.
Quasilines from quasimorphisms

We first describe how, given a quasimorphism on a group, one can construct a quasiline on which the group acts. We start by recalling some definitions.

Definition 3.1

((Homogeneous) quasimorphism)
.

Let
H
be a group. A map
m\colon H\to\mathbb{R}
is a
quasimorphism
if there exists a constant
D\geq 0
, called the
defect
of
m
, such that for every
h,k\in H
we have that

|m(hk)-m(h)-m(k)|\leq D.

A quasimorphism is
homogeneous
if it restricts to a group homomorphism on every cyclic subgroup of
H
, i.e.
m(h^{n})=nm(h)
for every
h\in H
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 3.2
.

To every quasimorphism
m
one can associate a homogeneous quasimorphism
m^{\prime}
, defined as

m^{\prime}(h)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{m(h^{n})}{n}.

Notice that, if
m
is already homogeneous, then
m^{\prime}
coincides with
m
. This in particular implies that a homogeneous quasimorphism is invariant under conjugation. Indeed,

m(khk^{-1})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{m(kh^{n}k^{-1})}{n}.

Then by the properties of the homogeneous quasimorphism we get that

m(kh^{n}k^{-1})\in\left[nm(h)+m(k)+m(k^{-1})-2D,nm(h)+m(k)+m(k^{-1})+2D\right],

and therefore the limit coincides with
m(h)
.

We now recall the main tool we shall use to construct quasilines that witness the infinite cyclic directions of our extensions.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
ABO19
, Lemma 4.15]
)
.

Let
H
be a finitely generated group, and let
m\colon H\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-zero homogeneous quasimorphism. Let

\tau_{\{m,C\}}=\left\{g\in G\mbox{ s.t. }|m(g)|<C\right\},

where
C
is any constant such that the defect of
m
is at most
C/2
, and that there exists a value of
m
in the interval
(0,C/2)
. Then
\tau_{\{m,C\}}
is a generating set of
H
, and the map
m\colon\operatorname{Cay}\left(H,\tau_{\{m,C\}}\right)\to\mathbb{R}
is a quasi-isometry.

Notice that, in the above Lemma, given any finite generating set
S
for
H
, one can always choose
C
large enough that
\tau_{\{m,C\}}
contains
S
.

In
[
HRSS25
,
HMS22
]
, the previous lemma is used as follows: given a
\mathbb{Z}
-central extension
E
of a hyperbolic group, one can construct a quasimorphism
E\to\mathbb{R}
which is unbounded on
\mathbb{Z}
(see e.g.
[
HMS22
, Lemma 4.3]
), and therefore Lemma
3.3
grants the existence of a Cayley graph
L
for
E
which is a quasiline and where
\mathbb{Z}
is unbounded. Then
L
can be used as one of the domains of the HHG structure, which “witnesses” the
\mathbb{Z}
direction. However, we are interested in short HHG, where a cyclic direction is not necessarily central in the corresponding stabiliser. What is true, however, is that a
\mathbb{Z}
-by-hyperbolic group has an index-two subgroup where
\mathbb{Z}
is central. This justifies the following lemma:

Lemma 3.4

(Extending quasilines to finite-index overgroups)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group and
E
a finite-index, normal subgroup. Let
g_{1}=1,g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}
be coset representatives for
G/E
. Let
\langle z\rangle\leq E
be an infinite cyclic subgroup which is central in
E
and normal in
G
.

Given a homogeneous quasimorphism
m\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
which is unbounded on
\langle z\rangle
, define
m^{G}\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
as

m^{G}(h)=\frac{1}{[E:G]}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon(g_{i})m(g_{i}hg_{i}^{-1}),

where
\varepsilon(g)\in\{\pm 1\}
is such that
gzg^{-1}=z^{\varepsilon(g)}
. Then
m^{G}
is a homogeneous quasimorphism which is unbounded on
\langle z\rangle
.

Furthermore, let
C>2D
, such that there exists a value of
m^{G}
in the interval
(0,C/2)
. Then the inclusion
E\hookrightarrow G
induces a quasi-isometry

i_{m}\colon\operatorname{Cay}\left(E,\tau_{\{m^{G},C\}}\right)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Cay}\left(G,\tau_{\{m^{G},C\}}\cup\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\}\right),

where
\tau_{\{m^{G},C\}}
is the generating set for
E
from Lemma
3.3
. In particular,
\langle z\rangle
acts loxodromically on
\operatorname{Cay}\left(G,\tau_{\{m^{G},C\}}\cup\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\}\right)
.

Remark 3.5
.

Later we shall also need that, if
e\in E
is such that
m(geg^{-1})=0
for every
g\in G
, then clearly
m^{G}(e)=0
.

Proof.

Firstly, each
m(g_{i}\cdot g_{i}^{-1})
is a homogeneous quasimorphism, being the composition of a group automorphism and
m
. Then
m^{G}
is a homogeneous quasimorphism, since it is defined as a linear combination of homogeneous quasimorphisms. Furthermore, by construction
m^{G}(z)=m(z)
, so
m^{G}
is itself unbounded on
\langle z\rangle
.

Now we claim that the absolute value of
m^{G}
is
G
-invariant. Indeed, for every

h\in G
, and
v^{\prime}\in V
are as in Notation

.

Then we can define a coarse map
\theta_{v}\colon\mathcal{C}(\Delta)\to\widehat{H}_{v}
which, at the level of vertices, maps the whole
\operatorname{Cone}(w)
under

to the set

\theta_{v}(w)=\bigcup_{g^{\prime}}\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g^{\prime}Z_{hv^{\prime}}),

where
g^{\prime}
varies among all elements in
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
such that
g^{\prime}hv^{\prime}=ghv^{\prime}=w
. Notice that, by construction,
\theta_{v}
is
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
-equivariant.

We first point out that
\theta_{v}(w)
is always uniformly bounded in
\widehat{H}_{v}
, and therefore
\theta_{v}
is a well-defined coarse map. Indeed, if

is such that

, we have that
g^{-1}g^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)\cap\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(hv^{\prime})
, which by Definition
3.9
.(
3
) virtually coincides with
\langle Z_{v},Z_{hv^{\prime}}\rangle
. Then
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g^{-1}g^{\prime})
lies in a finite index overgroup of
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{hv^{\prime}})
, which in turn means that the distance

\mathrm{d}_{\widehat{H}_{v}}(\mathfrak{p}_{v}(gZ_{hv^{\prime}}),\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g^{\prime}Z_{hv^{\prime}}))\leq\mathrm{d}_{\widehat{H}_{v}}(\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g),\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g^{\prime}))

is uniformly bounded. Furthermore,
\theta_{v}
is coarsely surjective, as the cosets of the various
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{hv^{\prime}})
cover

.

We now prove that
\theta_{v}
is coarsely Lipschitz. Pick
a,b\in\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)
which are
\mathcal{W}
-adjacent, and let
w_{a}=p(a)
and
w_{b}=p(b)
. We want to show that, if
w_{a}=g_{a}h_{a}v_{a}
and
w_{b}=g_{b}h_{b}v_{b}
, then
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{a}Z_{h_{a}v_{a}})
is uniformly close to
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{b}Z_{h_{b}v_{b}})
in
H_{v}
. By fullness of links, Lemma
3.31
, we can find two
\mathcal{W}
-adjacent maximal simplices

and

which extend

\mathrm{d}_{G}(N_{R}(P_{v})\cap N_{R}(P_{w_{a}}),N_{R}(P_{v})\cap N_{R}(P_{w_{b}}))\leq\mathrm{d}_{G}(f(\Sigma_{a}),f(\Sigma_{b}))\leq\widetilde{K},

where the second inequality is Lemma

. Now, by adapting the argument of Claim
3.20
, one sees that the Hausdorff distance between

and

is bounded by the constant
C_{0}
, and similarly if we replace

by

\mathrm{d}_{G}(g_{a}\langle Z_{v},Z_{h_{a}v_{a}}\rangle,g_{b}\langle Z_{v},Z_{h_{b}v_{b}}\rangle)\leq 2C_{0}+\widetilde{K}.

Now, by Remark
3.14
, there exists a constant

, depending on

and

, such that

\mathrm{d}_{\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)}(g_{a}\langle Z_{v},Z_{h_{a}v_{a}}\rangle,g_{b}\langle Z_{v},Z_{h_{b}v_{b}}\rangle)\leq N,

where
\mathrm{d}_{\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)}
is the word metric we previously fixed on
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
. Then we can take the quotient projection to

, which is
1
-Lipschitz, and see that

\mathrm{d}_{H_{v}}(\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{a}Z_{h_{a}v_{a}}),\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{b}Z_{h_{b}v_{b}}))\leq N.

To conclude that
\theta_{v}
is a quasi-isometry, we can almost read the above argument backwards. Indeed, suppose that
w_{a}=g_{a}h_{a}v_{a}
and
w_{b}=g_{b}h_{b}v_{b}
are such that

\mathrm{d}_{H_{v}}(\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{a}Z_{h_{a}v_{a}}),\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{b}Z_{h_{b}v_{b}}))\leq 1,

and we want to show that

is
\mathcal{W}
-adjacent to
w_{b}
. Taking the preimages with respect to
\mathfrak{p}_{v}
, we see that

\mathrm{d}_{\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)}(g_{a}\langle Z_{v},Z_{h_{a}v_{a}}\rangle,g_{b}\langle Z_{v}Z_{h_{b}v_{b}}\rangle)\leq 1.

Now,
\mathrm{d}_{G}
is bounded above by the intrinsic distance
\mathrm{d}_{\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)}
, as we chose the generating set for

to contain a generating set for
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
. Thus we get that

\mathrm{d}_{G}(g_{a}\langle Z_{v},Z_{h_{a}v_{a}}\rangle,g_{b}\langle Z_{v}Z_{h_{b}v_{b}}\rangle)\leq 1,

and in turn, as
g_{a}\langle Z_{v},Z_{h_{a}v_{a}}\rangle\subseteq N_{R}(P_{w_{a}})
and symmetrically for

\mathrm{d}_{G}(N_{R}(P_{w_{a}}),N_{R}(P_{w_{b}}))\leq 1
. Then we see that there exists a staple edge between some simplex supported on
\{v,w_{a}\}
and some simplex supported on

and

are
\mathcal{W}
-adjacent.

Lemma 3.34

(
\Delta
of triangle-type)
.

Let
\Delta=\{(v,x),(w)\}
be a simplex of triangle-type. Then the identity map on

\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(w)
-equivariant, uniform quasi-isometry

First, we show that

, and notice that

and

\mathrm{d}_{L_{w}}(\operatorname{Proj}_{w}(g),z)=\mathrm{d}_{L_{w}}(y,z)\leq 1\leq R
. In other words,

Conversely, suppose that

3.3.5.
Quasi-isometric embeddings

The final axiom to check, in order to prove that
(X,\mathcal{W})
defines a combinatorial HHS, is that every augmented link
\mathcal{C}(\Delta)
is quasi-isometrically embedded in
Y_{\Delta}
. Again, we look at all possible shapes of
\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)
, according to Corollary

\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)
has diameter at most

, or if

so that

Lemma 3.35

(
\Delta
of edge-type)
.

Let
\Delta=\{(v,x)\}
be of edge-type. Then there is a coarsely Lipschitz, coarse retraction

\varrho_{\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)}\colon Y_{\Delta}\to\mathcal{C}(\Delta),

Proof.

If

is a single vertex

\operatorname{Lk}(\Delta)=\operatorname{Cone}(w)

is square-free, there is no

\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)=\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v^{\prime})
. This implies that

Up to the action of
G
, assume that
v\in V
. Define
\varrho_{\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)}
by mapping the whole
\operatorname{Cone}(u)
under

to a vertex
w(u)\in\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)

•

If
\mathrm{d}_{\overline{X}}(u,v)\geq 2
, choose any

such that
\mathfrak{g}_{v}(P_{u})\subseteq N_{R}(P_{w(u)})

.(

•

If instead

.(

By construction,

•

If

\mathrm{d}_{G}(P_{v}\cap N_{R}(P_{w(u)}),P_{v}\cap N_{R}(P_{w(u^{\prime})}))\leq\mathrm{d}_{G}(\mathfrak{g}_{v}(P_{u}),\mathfrak{g}_{v}(P_{u^{\prime}}))\leq T,

are

and
w(u^{\prime})

•

Suppose that

with close realisations, whose supports contain

•

If instead

Lemma 3.36

(
\Delta
of triangle-type)
.

Let
\Delta=\{(v,x),(w)\}
be of triangle-type. Then there is a coarsely Lipschitz retraction

\varrho_{w}\colon Y_{\Delta}=\left(X-\left(\{w\}\cup p^{-1}(\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(w))\right)\right)^{+\mathcal{W}}\to\mathcal{C}(\Delta)\cong L_{w},

For every

, where

•

Suppose that

•

Now suppose that

Geometric action

We need to prove that the action of

Lemma 3.37
.

The realisation map

-equivariant quasi-isometry.

Proof.

We already noticed that

can be rephrased by saying that

∎

4.
Blowup materials from short structures

Here we prove that short HHG and the class of groups admitting blowup materials actually coincide.

Proposition 4.1
.

(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
admits blowup materials, with support graph
\overline{X}
and whose extensions are those from Axiom

Proof.

We check that
G
satisfies all points of Definition
3.9
. We shall introduce all the relevant data (such as quasimorphisms, gates, and so on) along the proof, and they will be
G
-equivariant by construction.

(

)
By Axiom (

\overline{X}
is triangle- and square-free, and none of its connected components is a point. Now,

acts on
\overline{X}

on

has finitely many

\overline{X}
, as whenever

and

for any simplex of the form

(
2

By Axiom (

(
3

Let
e=\{v,w\}

\overline{X}

2.17

is hyperbolic relative to

[

Now let

(

By
[

, the space obtained from

has cobounded product regions by Lemma

\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v_{i})

(
5

Whenever

(

We will implicitly identify

, coarsely corresponds to the coset
g\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v_{i})
, which is what we defined as
P_{v}
in Definition
3.9
, and therefore with
gE_{v_{i}}
since
E_{v_{i}}
has finite index in
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v_{i})
. In particular, there exists a
G
-equivariant family of coarsely Lipschitz gate maps
\mathfrak{g}_{v}\colon G\to 2^{E_{v}}
. Moreover, Remark
1.8
gives an explicit description of the coordinates of
\mathfrak{g}_{v}(g)
(up to a uniformly bounded error): for every
g\in G
and every
W\in\mathfrak{S}
, the projection of
\mathfrak{g}_{v}(g)
to
\mathcal{C}W
is the same as the projection of
g
if

or
W\sqsubseteq\mathcal{U}_{v}
, and is set to
\rho^{\ell_{v}}_{W}
otherwise.

We shall now check that
\mathfrak{g}_{v_{i}}
satisfies all properties from Definition
3.9
.(
6
) by explicitly describing
\mathfrak{g}_{v_{i}}(P_{u})
for every
u\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
, depending on the distance
\mathrm{d}_{\overline{X}}(u,{v_{i}})
. For short, we shall drop the index and simply denote
v_{i}
by
v
. We also fix a collection of representatives of the
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
-orbits of vertices in
\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)
, and as in Notation
3.8
we shall denote such a representative by
hv^{\prime}
where
h\in G
and
v^{\prime}\in V
.

•

If
\mathrm{d}_{\overline{X}}(u,{v})=1
, up to the action of
G
suppose that
u=hv^{\prime}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}_{v}(P_{u})
is within finite Hausdorff distance from the intersection
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)\cap\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(u)
(this follows by combining
[
BHS21
, Lemma 4.10]
and
[
HW09
, Lemma 4.5]
), and

\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(u)=h\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(u)h^{-1}\subseteq N_{r}(h\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(u))=N_{r}(P_{u})

where

is chosen as in Notation
3.8
. In other words,
\mathfrak{g}_{v}(P_{u})
is contained in a uniform neighbourhood of
P_{u}
.

•

If
\mathrm{d}_{\overline{X}}(u,{v})=2
then there exists a unique
w\in\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}({v})\cap\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(u)
, as
\overline{X}
is square-free. Again, up to the action of
G
we can assume that
w=hv^{\prime}
, for some

and

as in Notation
3.8
. Moreover,
\mathfrak{g}_{v}(P_{u})
coarsely coincides with some parallel copy

of the factor

, as

is the only domain with unbounded coordinate space which is neither transverse to, nor contains, one between

and
\ell_{u}
. In turn,

.

•

If

then the coordinates of any

are all prescribed (up to uniformly finite distance). Indeed, every domain in

, and taking the gate sets all other coordinates to the projection of

. Then the uniqueness axiom (

The proof of Proposition

5.
Maquillage on blowup materials

Let
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
be a short HHG. We claim that, by tweaking the blowup materials from the proof of Proposition
4.1
and then invoking Theorem
3.10
, one can introduce new cyclic directions, corresponding to elements acting loxodromically on the main curve graph
\mathcal{C}S
or on some augmented link
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{U}_{v}
. This shall be extremely relevant in the companion paper, as then, when we are given a quotient of a short HHG, we can (almost always) assume that the kernel is normally generated by cyclic directions, of which we understand the action on the support graph.

5.1.
Adding “globally” loxodromic directions

We first recall that, by
[
BHS17b
, Corollary 14.4]
, a normalised HHG
G
acts acylindrically on the main coordinate space
\mathcal{C}S
. This means that, if an element
g\in G
acts loxodromically, then it is also WPD. Then
[
DGO17
, Lemma 6.5]
implies that such an element
g
is contained in a maximal virtually cyclic subgroup
K(g)\leq G
.

Furthermore, recall that two elements
g,h\in G
are
commensurable
if some non-zero powers of them are conjugate in
G
. The following Proposition shows how, given a short HHG, one can introduce new cyclic directions with “trivial” stabiliser, corresponding to non-commensurable loxodromic elements for the action on the main coordinate space.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
be a short HHG, and let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\in G
be non-commensurable loxodromic elements for the action on the main coordinate space
\mathcal{C}S
. For every
i=1,\ldots,r
let
K(g_{i})
be the maximal virtually cyclic subgroup of
G
containing
g_{i}
. There exists a short HHG structure
(G,\overline{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{W}^{\prime})
where:

•

The new support graph
\overline{X}^{\prime}
is obtained from
\overline{X}
by adding a new connected component for each coset
\{hK(g_{i})\}_{h\in G,\,i=1,\ldots,r}
, consisting of an edge
\{u^{h}_{i},w^{h}_{i}\}
.

•

For every
i=1,\ldots,r
there exists
N_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that the cyclic direction associated to
u^{h}_{i}
is
h\langle g_{i}^{N_{i}}\rangle h^{-1}
, while the cyclic direction associated to
w^{h}_{i}
is trivial.

•

The data of all other vertices are unchanged.

Moreover, if
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
is colourable, then so is
(G,\overline{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{W}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Firstly, as
G
has cobounded product regions by Lemma
2.13
, we can argue as in Remark
1.14
to get that
\mathcal{C}S
is (
G
-equivariantly quasi-isometric to)
\operatorname{Cay}\left(G,T\right)
for some possibly infinite generating set
T
, which we can choose in such a way that
T\cap K(g_{1})
generates
K(g_{1})
. Moreover, by
[
DGO17
, Theorem 6.8]
we have that
K(g_{1})
is hyperbolically embedded in
(G,T)
. Combining the two facts, we see that
K(g_{1})
is also
hierarchically hyperbolically embedded
, in the sense of
[
BHS17a
, Definition 6.2]
which was designed as the natural generalisation to HHG of the analogous property from
[
DGO17
]
. Now, a careful inspection of
[
BHS17a
, Proposition 6.14]
tells us that there exists a HHG structure
(G,\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})
where:

•

The index set
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
contains
\mathfrak{S}
, together with one element for every coset
\{hK(g_{1})\}_{h\in G}
;

•

\sqsubseteq
and
\bot
are unchanged on
\mathfrak{S}
, while every
hK(g_{1})
is nested in
S
and transverse to every other domain;

•

The main coordinate space is quasi-isometric to the cone-off space
\widehat{\mathcal{C}S}
, obtained from
\mathcal{C}S
by adding a cone over each coset of
K(g_{1})
;

•

For every
W\in\mathfrak{S}-\{S\}
,
\mathcal{C}W
is unchanged, while
\mathcal{C}hK(g_{1})=h\operatorname{Cay}\left(K(g_{1}),\mathcal{I}\right)
for some fixed, finite generating set
\mathcal{I}
for
K(g_{1})
.

Now let
\overline{X}_{1}
be the graph obtained from
\overline{X}
by adding a new connected component for each coset
\{hK(g_{1})\}_{h\in G}
, consisting of an edge
\{u^{h}_{1},w^{h}_{1}\}
. Furthermore, we can extend the
G
-action to
\overline{X}_{1}
by setting
gu^{h}_{1}=u^{gh}_{1}
and
gw^{h}_{1}=w^{gh}_{1}
for every
g,h\in G
. We now construct blowup materials with support graph
\overline{X}^{\prime}
, so that Theorem
3.10
will then grant the existence of a short HHG structure
(G,\overline{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{W}^{\prime})
with the required properties. The proof will mimic that of Proposition
4.1
, and indeed we will extensively use that
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
already admits blowup materials; therefore we only need to define the blowup materials associated to the new vertices, and check that they interact well with the original blowup materials.

(
1
):

\overline{X}_{1}
is again triangle- and square-free, since it is obtained from
\overline{X}
by adding some new connected components which are edges. As we added two
G
-orbits to the cocompact action on
\overline{X}
,
G
still acts cocompactly on
\overline{X}_{1}
. Furthermore, any
G
-colouring for
\overline{X}
can be extended to
\overline{X}_{1}
by adding two new colours, one for every new
G
-orbit of vertices.

(
2
):
For every
v\in\overline{X}
, the extensions coming from
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
already satisfy all requirements. Moving to
u^{h}_{1}
and
w_{h}^{1}
, recall from e.g.
[
DGO17
, Corollary 6.6]
that there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that
\langle g_{1}^{N_{1}}\rangle
is normal in
K(g_{1})
. Then we define the cyclic direction for
u^{h}_{1}
by setting
Z_{u^{h}_{1}}=h\langle g_{1}^{N_{1}}\rangle h^{-1}
. Notice that the quotient group
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(u^{h}_{1})/Z_{u^{h}_{1}}=(hK(g_{1})h^{-1})/(h\langle g_{1}^{N_{1}}\rangle h^{-1})
is finite, hence hyperbolic, and that
Z_{u^{h}_{1}}
fixes
w^{h}_{i}
as it belongs to
hK(g_{1})h^{-1}
. Regarding
w^{h}_{i}
, we can set
Z_{w^{h}_{1}}=\{0\}
to be trivial, and notice that
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(w^{h}_{1})=hK(g_{1})h^{-1}
is already hyperbolic.

(
3
):
The stabiliser of an edge of
\overline{X}
is the same as before, so we just need to check that every edge of the form
\{u^{h}_{i},w^{h}_{i}\}
satisfies the properties. The only fact to notice is that
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(u^{h}_{i})=\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(w^{h}_{i})=hK(g_{1})h^{-1}
contains
\langle Z_{u^{h}_{1}},Z_{w^{h}_{1}}\rangle=h\langle g_{1}^{N_{1}}\rangle h^{-1}
as a subgroup of finite index, and in particular
Z_{u^{h}_{i}}
is quasiconvex in
hK(g_{1})h^{-1}
.

(
4
):
We already know that the main coordinate space
\mathcal{C}S
for the original structure
(G,\mathfrak{S})
is quasi-isometric to the cone-off Cayley graph of
G
, with respect to the cosets of a collection of representatives of stabilisers for the action on
\overline{X}
. If we furthermore cone-off the cosets of
K(g_{1})
we get the top-level coordinate space for
(G,\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})
, which is hyperbolic.

(
5
):
Fix a collection
V
of representatives of the
G
-orbits of vertices of
\overline{X}
, and let
u_{1},w_{1}
be the new vertices associated to the coset
K(g_{1})
. For all
v\in V
, both
E_{v}
and the associated quasimorphism can be defined as in Proposition
4.1
. Furthermore, let
E_{u_{1}}
be the centraliser of
\langle g_{1}^{N_{1}}\rangle
in
K(g_{1})
. By e.g.
[
Mac96
, Lemma 3.2]
, there exists an epimorphism
\phi_{u_{1}}\colon E_{u_{1}}\to\mathbb{Z}
, which in particular is a homogeneous quasimorphism which is unbounded on
\langle g_{1}^{N_{1}}\rangle
.

(
6
):
Notice that, for every
v\in V
, the product region associated to
\ell_{v}
in the new structure
(G,\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})
coarsely coincides with the product region for the old structure, as the domains which are either nested into or orthogonal to
\ell_{v}
, as well as their coordinate spaces, are unchanged. Thus, as in Proposition
4.1
, the product region coarsely coincides with
E_{v}
, and we can define a gate map
\mathfrak{g}_{v}\colon G\to 2^{E_{v}}
. Furthermore, the product region associated to the domain
K(g_{1})
, as well as its stabiliser, both coincide with
K(g_{1})
itself. Thus
K(g_{1})
, and in turn
E_{u_{1}}
, are hierarchically quasiconvex, and we can set
\mathfrak{g}_{v_{1}}=\mathfrak{g}_{w_{1}}
as the gate on
E_{u_{1}}
. Then, to see that all gates satisfy the requirements of Definition
3.9
.(
6
), we can argue as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, which only requires that, for every vertex
s\in\overline{X}_{1}
, there exists a
\sqsubseteq
-minimal domain (here, either some
\ell_{v}
or some
u^{h}_{1}
) whose associated product region coarsely coincides with
P_{s}
.

The procedure described above gives a short HHG structure for
G
in which some power of
g_{1}
is a cyclic direction. We can repeat the process with
g_{2}
, then
g_{3}
and so on, if we ensure that, for
i=2,\ldots,r
,
g_{i}
still acts loxodromically on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}S}
. Notice that the action on
\mathcal{C}S
is acylindrical along
K(g_{1})
, as
g_{1}
is WPD. Then e.g.
[
AM24
, Corollary 6.15]
gives that either
g_{i}
acts loxodromically on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}S}
, or some power of
g_{i}
stabilises a coset of
K(g_{1})
, and the latter cannot happen as then
g_{1}
and
g_{i}
would be commensurable.
∎

5.2.
Adding “locally” loxodromic directions

The following Proposition roughly says that, whenever
g\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
acts loxodromically on
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{U}_{v}
and has no hidden symmetries, in the sense of Definition
2.19
, we can tweak the short HHG structure in order to add
\langle g^{n}\rangle
as a new cyclic direction, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}-\{0\}
:

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
be a short HHG. Let
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
be a vertex with infinite cyclic direction, and let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
be such that
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{1}),\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{r})\in H_{v}
are non-commensurable loxodromic elements for the action on
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{U}_{v}
. Suppose further that each
g_{i}
has no hidden symmetries in
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
.

For every
i=1\ldots,r
there exists a non-trivial power
g_{i}^{\prime}
of
g_{i}
and a short HHG structure
(G,\overline{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{W}^{\prime})
such that:

•

The new support graph
\overline{X}^{\prime}
is obtained from
\overline{X}
by adding a vertex
u_{i}^{t}
for every coset
\{tS_{i}\}_{t\in G}
, where
S_{i}
is the normaliser of
g_{i}^{\prime}
in
G
, and declaring that
u_{i}^{t}
is only adjacent to
tv
. Furthermore,
\overline{X}^{\prime}
is again
G
-colourable.

•

The cyclic direction associated to
u_{i}^{t}
is
t\langle g_{i}^{\prime}\rangle t^{-1}
;

•

The data of all other vertices are unchanged.

Moreover, if
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
is colourable, then so is
(G,\overline{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{W}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Along the proof, we will often use the following observations. Firstly, if we fix a collection
W
of
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
-orbit representatives of vertices in
\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)
with unbounded cyclic direction, Lemma
2.16
states that
\{\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w})\}_{w\in W}
is an independent collection of cyclic subgroups. Furthermore,
H_{v}
acts geometrically on
F_{U_{v}}
, by Claim
2.18
, and each
Z_{w}
acts geometrically on the corresponding quasiline; hence the cone-off graph
\widehat{H_{v}}
of
H_{v}
with respect to
\{K_{H_{v}}(\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w}))\}_{w\in W}
is quasi-isometric to
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{U}_{v}
.

Now, for every
i=1,\ldots,r
let
K_{i}
be the maximal virtually cyclic subgroup of
H_{v}
containing
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{i})
. Since
g_{i}
has no hidden symmetries, there exists a non-trivial power
g_{i}^{\prime}
of
g_{i}
whose normaliser
S_{i}
in
G
contains
\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{-1}(K_{i})
, and in particular it contains
Z_{v}
. Notice that
S_{i}\leq\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
. Indeed, if
t\in S_{i}
then
g_{i}^{\prime}
fixes
t(v)
; moreover, the only vertex of
\overline{X}
which is fixed by
g_{i}^{\prime}
is
v
, because for any
u\in\overline{X}-\{v\}
the projection
\rho^{\ell_{u}}_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}\in\mathcal{C}\mathcal{U}_{v}
is well-defined and cannot be fixed by the loxodromic action of
g_{i}^{\prime}
. In turn, this implies that
S_{i}
actually coincides with
\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{-1}(K_{i})
, since if
t
(anti)commutes with
g_{i}^{\prime}
then
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(t)
(anti)commutes with some power of
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{i})
, and therefore lies in
K_{i}
by how the latter is described in
[
DGO17
, Corollary 6.6]
.

Let
\overline{X}^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
\overline{X}
by adding a vertex
u_{i}^{t}
for every coset
\{tS_{i}\}_{t\in G}
, which we declare to be only adjacent to
tv
. Extend the
G
-action to
\overline{X}^{\prime}
by setting
gu_{i}^{t}=u_{i}^{gt}
. We now check the existence of blowup materials with support graph
\overline{X}^{\prime}
, and then again the conclusion will follow from Theorem
3.10
.

(
1
):

\overline{X}^{\prime}
is again triangle- and square-free and none of its connected components are points, as we simply added some leaves to the
G
-orbit of
v
. Moreover,
G
acts cocompactly on
\overline{X}^{\prime}
, since we added a finite number of
G
-orbits. Finally, as no two vertices in the new orbits are adjacent, any
G
-colouring for
\overline{X}
can be extended to
\overline{X}^{\prime}
by adding a single new colour.

(
2
):

For each
w\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
, we already know that
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(w)
is a cyclic-by-hyperbolic extension satisfying all the properties of the blowup materials. The only thing to notice is that, whenever
t\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
,
Z_{v}
fixes
u_{i}^{t}
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
. Indeed,

Z_{v}tS_{i}=tZ_{v}S_{i}=tS_{i},

where we used that
Z_{v}
is normal in
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
and contained in
S_{i}
.

Moving to the new domains, for every
i=1,\ldots,k
let
Z_{i}=\langle g_{i}^{\prime}\rangle
, and then for every
t\in G
let
Z_{u^{t}_{i}}=tZ_{i}t^{-1}
. Let
u_{i}=u_{i}^{1}
be the vertex associated to the coset
S_{i}
. Notice that
Z_{i}
fixes
\{v\}=\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}^{\prime}}(u_{i})
, as
Z_{i}\leq\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
. Furthermore, by construction
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(u_{i})=S_{i}
, and since
S_{i}=\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{-1}(K_{i})
virtually coincides with
\langle Z_{v},Z_{i}\rangle
, we have that the quotient
S_{i}/Z_{i}
is virtually cyclic, hence hyperbolic.

(
3
):
Whenever
e
is an edge of
\overline{X}
,
\text{P}\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(e)
already satisfies all requirements. Then up to the
G
-action let
e=\{v,u_{i}\}
. Now,
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)\cap\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(u_{i})=S_{i}
, and we already noticed that the latter is a finite-index overgroup of
\langle Z_{v},Z_{i}\rangle
. Furthermore, we have that
Z_{v}\cap Z_{i}=\{0\}
(the former acts trivially on
H_{v}
, the latter loxodromically), so
Z_{v}
injects in the virtually cyclic quotient
S_{i}/Z_{i}
and is therefore quasiconvex there. Conversely,
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{i})
is quasiconvex in
H_{v}
, as
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{i})
acts loxodromically on
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{U}_{v}=\widehat{H_{v}}
and therefore on
H_{v}
.

(
4
):

Fix a collection
V=\{v_{1}=v,\ldots,v_{k},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\}
of orbit representatives of vertices in
\overline{X}^{\prime}
. From the proof of Proposition
4.1
we know that
G
is weakly hyperbolic relative to
\{\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v_{1}),\ldots,\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v_{k})\}
; moreover, we do not change the quasi-isometry type of the cone-off graph if we enlarge the collection to include
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{r}\}
, as
S_{i}\leq\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v_{1})
.

(
5
):

For every
v_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,k
, one can define
E_{v_{j}}
as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
. If
j\geq 2
, the quasimorphism
\phi_{v_{j}}
from the same proof satisfies all requirements already. We need a little more effort for
j=1
, as we have to produce a quasimorphism
E_{v}\to\mathbb{R}
that vanishes on the new adjacent cyclic directions, as well as on the original ones. To do so, one first notices that the collection

\mathcal{H}=\{\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{1}),\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{r})\}\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w})\}_{w\in W}

is independent in
H_{v}
. Indeed, no
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{i}^{\prime})
can be commensurable to any element in
Z_{w}
, since the former acts loxodromically on
\widehat{H_{v}}
while the latter acts elliptically; moreover, by assumption no two loxodromic elements are commensurable, and we already pointed out that the collection
\{\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w})\}_{w\in W}
is independent. Furthermore, every
Z_{i}
has no hidden symmetries in
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
by hypothesis, while every
Z_{w}
for
w\in W
has no hidden symmetries by Lemma
2.20
. Then Lemma
A.4
from the Appendix produces a quasimorphism
\tilde{\phi}_{v}
from the centraliser of
Z_{v}
to
\mathbb{R}
which is the identity on
Z_{v}
and trivial on all adjacent cyclic directions, and we can then restrict
\tilde{\phi}_{v}
to
E_{v}
to get the required quasimorphism.

Moving to the new domains, let
E_{i}=\langle 2Z_{i},2Z_{v}\rangle
, which is a normal subgroup of
S_{i}
of index at most two and centralises
Z_{i}
. Then we can choose the quasimorphism
\phi_{u_{i}}\colon E_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
as the projection onto
2Z_{i}
, which is clearly unbounded on
2Z_{i}
and trivial on
2Z_{v}
.

(
6
):

Before proving the existence of gates, we point out that, for every
i=1,\ldots,r
,
E_{i}\leq\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
, and the latter coarsely coincides with the product region for
\ell_{v}
. This means that, in the original HHG structure, the projection of

to any domain which is neither
\ell_{v}
nor orthogonal to

•

\pi_{\ell_{v}}(E_{i})

•

\pi_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}(E_{i})
coarsely coincides with some quasi-axis

for
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g_{i}^{\prime})

•

For the same reason, for every

,

coarsely coincides with
\pi_{\ell_{w}}(\langle g_{i}^{\prime}\rangle)

\gamma_{i}
have uniformly bounded projections to
\mathcal{C}\ell_{w}

, whose difference is bounded in norm by some constant

\gamma_{i}
, such that the projection of

\{(g_{i}^{\prime})^{l}\}_{l=m}^{n}

\langle g_{i}^{\prime}\rangle
we can assume that

, and as coordinate projections in a HHG are uniformly coarsely Lipschitz we have that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[-K,K]}\sup_{W\in\mathfrak{S}}\mathrm{d}_{W}(1,(g_{i}^{\prime})^{n})<+\infty.

From this explicit description we get that

is hierarchically quasiconvex, in the sense of Definition

. Indeed, its coordinate projections are all quasiconvex; furthermore, whenever

projects close to
\pi_{W}(E_{i})
for every

, we can choose some

\pi_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}(x)
is within a bounded distance from
\pi_{\mathcal{U}_{v}}((g_{i}^{\prime})^{l})

\pi_{\ell_{v}}(z(g_{i}^{\prime})^{l})
is uniformly close to
\pi_{\ell_{v}}(x)
(notice that multiplying by an element of

does not change the projection to
\mathcal{U}_{v}
). Thus, there exists a coarsely Lipschitz gate map
\mathfrak{g}_{u_{i}}\coloneq G\to 2^{E_{i}}
.

Now, for every
v_{j}\in V
define
\mathfrak{g}_{v_{j}}
as the gate on
E_{v_{j}}
, which coarsely coincides with the product region
P_{\ell_{v_{j}}}
in the original HHG structure. For every

define
\mathfrak{g}_{u_{i}}
as above. We now check all properties of the gate
\mathfrak{g}_{w}
, depending on the type of
w
:

If
w=v
:
We know from before that
\mathfrak{g}_{v}
is coarsely the identity on
P_{v}
, and therefore on each
P_{u^{t}_{i}}=tS_{i}
for every
t\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)

P_{u_{i}^{t}}
for every

.

Moving to the second requirement, for every

.

If

,
j\geq 2

Recall that

\mathfrak{g}_{v_{j}}(P_{tv})

Since

In the setting of Proposition

, if one of the

has hidden symmetries, we can still add a central direction generated by

, for some

, in view of the following Lemma:

Lemma 5.3

(Straightening cyclic directions)
.

Let
0\to\langle z\rangle\to G\xrightarrow{\mathfrak{p}}H\to 1
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-extension of a hyperbolic group, and let
g\in G
map to an infinite order element in
H
. Then there exists
g^{\prime}=g^{n}z^{k}
, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}-\{0\}
and some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, without hidden symmetries.

Proof.

We restrict our consideration to the extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle z\rangle}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{p}}

where
K=K_{H}(g)
is virtually cyclic and
S=\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(K)
, and we want to find
g^{\prime}
as above which is normal in
S
. The proof boils down to basic linear algebra. Firstly, as
\langle\mathfrak{p}(g)\rangle
has finite index in
K
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that
\langle\mathfrak{p}(g^{2N})\rangle
is normal in
K
. Thus, the centraliser
K^{+}
of
\mathfrak{p}(g^{2N})
inside
K
has index at most two. Let
C_{1}=\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\left(K^{+}\right)
, and let
C_{2}
be the centraliser of
z
in
S
. Finally, let
E=C_{1}\cap C_{2}
, which is therefore a normal subgroup of index at most four.

We first claim that every
t\in E
commutes with
g^{2N}
. Indeed, since
t\in C_{1}
we have that
tg^{2N}t^{-1}=g^{2N}z^{K}
for some
K\in\mathbb{Z}
; furthermore, using that
t\in C_{2}
must commute with
z
, we get that
t^{r}g^{2N}t^{-r}=g^{2N}z^{Kr}
for every
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. However,
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}(\langle\mathfrak{p}(g^{2N})\rangle)=\langle z,g^{2N}\rangle
has finite index in
S
, thus there exists some
r
for which
t^{r}
must commute with
g^{2N}
(here we are using that
z
commutes with any even power of
g
, as
C_{2}
has index at most two in
S
). This yields that
K=0
.

Now, if
E=S
we are done, as then
g^{2N}
is central in
S
and we can set
g^{\prime}=g^{2N}
. If not, there are some cases to consider.

•

Suppose first that
E=C_{1}\lneq S
but
C_{2}=S
, that is,
z
is central in
S
, and pick
t\in S-E
. Since
\mathfrak{p}(t)
anticommutes with
\mathfrak{p}(g^{2N})
, we must have that
tg^{2N}t^{-1}=g^{-2N}z^{M}
for some
M\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then let
g^{\prime}=g^{4N}z^{-M}
, which anticommutes with
t
. In turn, as
E
has index two,
g^{\prime}
anticommutes with every
t^{\prime}\in S-E
, so
\langle g^{\prime}\rangle
is normal in
S
.

•

A similar argument works if
C_{1}=S
but
E=C_{2}\lneq S
. Pick any
t\in S-E
, and suppose that
tg^{2N}t^{-1}=g^{2N}z^{M}
for some
M\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
g^{\prime}=g^{4N}z^{M}
commutes with
t
, and this time
g^{\prime}
is central in
S
.

•

If
E=C_{1}=C_{2}\lneq S
, pick any
t\in S-E
, and suppose that
tg^{2N}t^{-1}=g^{-2N}z^{M}
for some
M\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
t^{2}g^{2N}t^{-2}=g^{2N}z^{-2M}
. But
t^{2}\in E
must commute with
g^{2N}
, so we must have that
M=0
. This means that
t
anticommutes with
g^{\prime}=g^{2M}
, and therefore
\langle g^{\prime}\rangle
is normal in
S
.

•

Finally, suppose that both
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are proper, distinct subgroups of
S
. Let
t_{1}\in C_{1}-C_{2}
and
t_{2}\in C_{2}-C_{1}
. As above, there exist
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
t_{1}g^{2N}t_{1}^{-1}=g^{2N}z^{M_{1}}
and
t_{2}g^{2N}t_{2}^{-1}=g^{-2N}z^{M_{2}}
. Then

t_{1}t_{2}g^{2N}t_{2}^{-1}t_{1}^{-1}=t_{1}g^{-2N}z^{M_{2}}t_{1}^{-1}=g^{-2N}z^{-M1-M_{2}}.

As
t_{1}t_{2}\in S-(C_{1}\cap C_{2})
, we can argue as in the previous bullet to get that
M_{1}=-M_{2}
. Then set
g^{\prime}=g^{4N}z^{M_{1}}
, and one can check that it commutes with
t_{1}
and anticommutes with
t_{2}
and
t_{1}t_{2}
. ∎

Remark 5.4
.

With the notation from Lemma

, if the extension is central and

, then

, so every

6.
New examples

In this Section, we use the machinery of blowup materials to provide new examples of short HHG.

6.1.
RAAGs on triangle- and square-free graphs

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite, connected, triangle- and square-free simplicial graph with at least three points. The right-angled Artin group
G_{\Lambda}
with defining graph
\Lambda
is a colourable short HHG.

Proof.

Let
\overline{X}
be the
extension graph
from
[
KK14
]
, whose vertices are conjugates of standard generators, and where
a^{g}
and
b^{h}
are adjacent if and only if they commute.
G_{\Lambda}
acts on
\overline{X}
by conjugation, and we check that this action admits blowup materials.

(
1
)

\overline{X}
is triangle- and square-free, as so is
\Lambda
, and no connected component of
\overline{X}
is a point. We also stress that, by e.g.
[
KK13
, Theorem 15]
,
[a^{g},b^{h}]=1
if and only if there exists
x\in G_{\Lambda}
such that
a^{x}=a^{g}
and
b^{x}b^{h}
, so the
G
-action is cofinite. Moreover, by e.g.
[
KK13
, Lemma 26.(8)]
,
\overline{X}
is coloured by declaring
a^{g}
and
b^{h}
to have the same colour if and only if
a=b
.

(
2
)
The stabiliser of
a
is
C(a)=\langle a\rangle\times F
, where
F
is the free group generated by
\operatorname{Lk}_{\Lambda}(a)
. In particular,
F
is non-elementarily hyperbolic if
|\operatorname{Lk}_{\Lambda}(a)|
. Moreover, the conjugation by
a
fixes
b
for every
b\in\operatorname{Lk}_{\Lambda}(a)
.

(
3
)
If
b\in\operatorname{Lk}_{\Lambda}(a)
then
C(a)\cap C(b)=\langle a,b\rangle
, as
\Lambda
is triangle-free, and
\langle b\rangle
is quasiconvex in the free group
C(a)/\langle a\rangle
.

(
4
)
The cone-off graph of
G_{\Lambda}
with respect to the family
\{C(a)\}_{a\in\Lambda^{(0)}}
is clearly quasi-isometric to the extension graph, and the latter is hyperbolic by
[
KK13
]
.

(
5
)
Since
C(a)=\langle a\rangle\times F
, the projection
\phi\colon C(a)\to\langle a\rangle
is a (quasi)morphism which is trivial on the link of
a
and unbounded on
\langle a\rangle
.

(
6
)
Metrise
G
by identifying with the universal cover of the Salvetti complex. By
[
BHS17b
, Proposition 8.3]
, for every
a\in\Lambda
there exists a coarsely Lipschitz, coarse retraction
\mathfrak{g}_{a}\colon G_{\Lambda}\to C(a)
. Moreover, for every
g=a_{1}\ldots a_{k}
, where each
a_{i}
is a standard generator, the Claim in the same proof describes
\mathfrak{g}_{a}(gC(b))
as a
C(a)
-translate of the parabolic subgroup
G_{\Lambda_{0}}
, where

\Lambda_{0}=\operatorname{Star}_{\Lambda}(a)\operatorname{Star}_{\Lambda}(b)\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{Lk}(a_{i}).

We now check that
\mathfrak{g}_{a}(gC(b))
satisfies all requirements from Definition
3.9
.(
6
).

•

If
\mathrm{d}_{\overline{X}}(a,b^{g})=1
, then up to the
G_{\Lambda}
action we can assume that
g=1
. Then
\mathfrak{g}_{a}(C(b))=C(a)\cap C(b)=\langle a,b\rangle\leq C(b)
.

•

If
\mathrm{d}_{\overline{X}}(a,b^{g})\geq 2
and
a\neq b
then
\Lambda_{0}
can only contain a single vertex
c
, as
\Lambda
is triangle- and square-free. This means that
\mathfrak{g}_{a}(gC(b))
is a translate of
\langle c\rangle
.

•

Finally, if
\mathrm{d}_{\overline{X}}(a,b^{g})\geq 2
and
a=b
, then
g\not\in C(a)
, so if we write
g=a_{1}\ldots a_{k}
here must be some
a_{i}\not\in\operatorname{Star}_{\Lambda}(a)
. In particular

\Lambda_{0}\subseteq\operatorname{Star}_{\Lambda}(a)\cap\operatorname{Lk}_{\Lambda}(a_{i})
can only contain a single vertex, and we conclude as above.

One can now invoke Theorem
3.10
to produce the required short HHG structure.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

The above example is not “new”, as it is possible to get the same short HHG structure “from scratch” by means of
[

, Theorem 3.15]
. However, such an argument requires some subtleties which are not in the scope of this paper, so we preferred to derive this example from Theorem

, as yet another showcase of its power.

6.2.
Certain relative hyperbolic groups

Let
G
be a group which is hyperbolic relative to
\mathbb{Z}
-central extensions of hyperbolic groups. In the spirit of Section
4
, we now prove that
G
is a short HHG, by extracting blowup materials from the relative hyperbolic structure. This will be a relevant tool in the companion paper, but its proof is presented here as it is similar to many arguments throughout this article.

It should be noted that, in view of
[
HRSS25
, Corollary 4.3]
and
[
BHS19
, Theorem 9.1]
, such a
G
already possesses a HHG structure, but our result upgrades it to a
combinatorial
one, of which we can easily describe the underlying graph and the links of its simplices.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
G
be hyperbolic relative to
\mathbb{Z}
-central extensions of hyperbolic groups. Then
G
is a colourable short HHG, and in particular a combinatorial HHG.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a collection of representatives of the peripheral subgroups, under the
G
-action by conjugation. By assumption, every
P\in\mathcal{P}
is a central extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{p}_{P}}

where
Z_{P}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
and
H
is hyperbolic. If
H
is infinite, let
k\in H
be an element of infinite order (which exists as every torsion subgroup of a hyperbolic group is finite, see e.g.
[
GdlH90
, Corollary 8.36]
), and let
K
be the maximal virtually cyclic subgroup of
H
containing
k
. If instead
H
is finite we set
K=H
. In both cases, let
R_{P}=\mathfrak{p}_{P}^{-1}(K)\leq P
.

Now build a simplicial graph
\overline{X}
as follows. The vertex set of
\overline{X}
is

\bigsqcup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\left(G/P\sqcup G/R_{P}\right).

For every
P\in\mathcal{P}
and every
h\in G
, we declare
hP
and
hR_{P}
to be adjacent. We now check that the
G
-action on
\overline{X}
admits blowup materials.

(
1
):
By construction, the connected components of
\overline{X}
are
G
-translates of the following star, where
P\in\mathcal{P}
:

\{P\}\star\{hR_{p}\}_{h\in P}.

In particular
\overline{X}
is a forest, on which
G
acts cocompactly. We can colour
\overline{X}
by declaring every
hP
to be black and every
hR_{P}
to be white.

(
2
):
Notice that
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(P)=P
, which is a
\mathbb{Z}
-central extension of a hyperbolic group by assumption, and multiplication by
Z_{P}
on the left fixes
hR_{P}
whenever
h\in P
, because
Z_{p}
is central in
P
and contained in
R_{P}
. Moreover,
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(R_{P})=R_{P}
, which is either virtually cyclic or virtually
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. In the former case, we choose the cyclic direction
Z_{R_{P}}
for
{R_{P}}
to be trivial. In the latter case, by Lemma
5.3
there exists
r=\in\mathfrak{p}_{P}^{-1}(\langle k\rangle)-Z_{P}
such that
Z_{R_{P}}\coloneq\langle r\rangle
is normal in
{R_{P}}
. Notice that the quotient
{R_{P}}/Z_{R_{P}}
is virtually cyclic.

(
3
):

\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(P)\cap\operatorname{Stab}_{G}({R_{P}})={R_{P}}
, which virtually coincides with
\langle Z_{P},Z_{R_{P}}\rangle
. Furthermore,
\mathfrak{p}_{P}(Z_{R_{P}})
is either trivial or (a subgroup of)
\langle k\rangle
, and in the latter case it is quasiconvex in
H
as
k
has infinite order. Similarly, if
\mathfrak{p}_{R_{P}}\colon{R_{P}}\to{R_{P}}/Z_{R_{P}}
is the quotient projection, then
\mathfrak{p}_{R_{P}}(Z_{P})
has finite index in
{R_{P}}/Z_{R_{P}}
, and is therefore quasiconvex.

(
4
):
Since
G
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{P}
, it is also weakly hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{P}
, and therefore also to
\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\{P,{R_{P}}\}
as
{R_{P}}\leq P
.

(
5
):
Since we chose
Z_{R_{P}}
to have no hidden symmetries, Corollary
A.4
from the Appendix produces a homogeneous quasimorphism
\phi_{P}\colon P\to\mathbb{R}
which is unbounded on
Z_{P}
and trivial on every conjugate of
Z_{R_{P}}
.

Similarly, whenever
Z_{R_{P}}
is non-trivial, let
E_{R_{P}}
be a normal subgroup of
{R_{P}}
centralising
Z_{R_{P}}
and isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Then it is easy to find a homomorphism
\phi_{R_{P}}\colon E_{R_{P}}\to\mathbb{R}
which is unbounded on
Z_{R_{P}}\cap E_{R_{P}}
and trivial on
Z_{P}\cap E_{R_{P}}
.

(
6
):

G
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{P}
, so by e.g.
[
Sis13
, Theorem 2.14]
for every
P\in\mathcal{P}
there exists a coarsely Lipschitz, coarse retraction
\mathfrak{g}_{P}\colon G\to P
which is uniformly bounded on
hP^{\prime}
whenever
h\in G
,
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
, and
P\neq hP^{\prime}
.

Regarding the gate on
E_{R_{P}}
, we first recall that, by inspection of the proof of
[
HRSS25
, Corollary 4.3]
, each
P
is quasi-isometric to a product
L\times H
, where
L
is the quasiline coming from the quasimorphism
\phi_{P}
, as in Lemma
3.3
, while the projection on the second factor is the quotient projection
\mathfrak{p}_{P}\colon P\to H
. Now,
E_{R_{P}}
contains
Z_{P}\cap E_{R_{P}}
, on which
\phi_{P}
is unbounded; hence the projection of
E_{R_{P}}
to

is coarsely dense. Moreover,
\mathfrak{p}_{P}(E_{R_{P}})
, which has finite index in
\mathfrak{p}_{P}({R_{P}})
, is quasiconvex in
H
. Thus there exists a coarsely Lipschitz, coarse retraction
\rho_{R_{P}}\colon P\to E_{R_{P}}

\mathfrak{g}_{R_{P}}\coloneq\rho_{R_{P}}\circ\mathfrak{g}_{P}
.

We now check all properties of gates.

•

Starting with
\mathfrak{g}_{P}

\mathfrak{g}_{P}
coarsely coincides with the identity on

, and therefore on every
h{R_{P}}
whenever
h\in P

for some

is uniformly bounded, and
a fortiori

\mathfrak{g}_{P}(h{R_{P^{\prime}}})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{P}(hP^{\prime})
.

•

Moving to
\mathfrak{g}_{R_{P}}

\mathfrak{g}_{R_{P}}(P)\subseteq{R_{P}}\subseteq P
. If

we already know that
\mathfrak{g}_{P}(hP^{\prime})

\mathfrak{g}_{R_{P}}(hP^{\prime})=\rho_{R_{P}}\circ\mathfrak{g}_{P}(hP^{\prime})
. Thus we only need to show that, if

implies that the projection of

.∎

7.
Application to coarse median structures

Let
({\mathcal{Z}},\mathrm{d})
be a metric space. Recall that a
coarse median

\mu\colon{\mathcal{Z}}^{3}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
for
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a ternary operation satisfying the following:

•

Localisation
:
\mu(a,a,b)=a
for all
a,b\in{\mathcal{Z}}
;

•

Symmetry
:
\mu(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=\mu(a_{\sigma(1)},a_{\sigma(2)},a_{\sigma(3)})
for all
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
and permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,2,3\}
;

•

Affine control
: There exists a constant
R>0
such that, for all
a,a^{\prime},b,c\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, we have

d(\mu(a,b,c),\mu(a^{\prime},b,c))\leqslant Rd(a,a^{\prime})+R;

•

Coarse 4-point condition
: There exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that for any
a,b,c,d\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, we have

\mathrm{d}\left(\mu\left(\mu(a,b,c),b,d\right),\mu\left(a,b,\mu(c,b,d)\right)\right)\leq\kappa.

We call two coarse median
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\colon{\mathcal{Z}}^{3}\to{\mathcal{Z}}

equivalent
if

\sup_{x,y,z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\mathrm{d}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(\mu_{1}(x,y,z),\mu_{2}(x,y,z))<+\infty.

An equivalence class of coarse medians is called a
coarse median structure
.

By e.g.
[
BHS19
, Theorem 7.3]
(which in turn builds on an observation of Bowditch
[
Bow18
]
), a hierarchically hyperbolic space
({\mathcal{Z}},\mathfrak{S})
admits a coarse median, obtained as follows: for every
x,y,z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
and every
U\in\mathfrak{S}
, the
U
-coordinate of
\mu(x,y,z)
is the coarse centre of the triangle with vertices
\pi_{U}(x),\pi_{U}(y),\pi_{U}(z)
in the hyperbolic space
\mathcal{C}U
. If moreover
(G,\mathfrak{S})
is a HHG,
\mu
is coarsely
G
-equivariant, by how
G
acts on the coordinate spaces. The final goal of this paper is to show the following, which is Theorem
A
from the Introduction.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
be a short HHG, and suppose that for some vertex
v\in\overline{X}
we have that
Z_{v}
is infinite and
H_{v}
is non-elementary. Then there are uncountably many short HHG structures on
G
which give rise to pairwise non-equivalent, coarsely
G
-equivariant coarse medians.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
we have that
(G,\overline{X},\mathcal{W})
admits blowup materials. To modify the short HHG structure we will modify the quasimorphism
\phi_{v}\colon E_{v}\to\mathbb{R}
associated to
v
as in the statement (we can assume that
v
is the chosen representative in its orbit). Let
H^{\prime}_{v}=\mathfrak{p}_{v}(E_{v})
; this is a hyperbolic group. We can consider all cyclic subgroups of the form
\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w}\cap E_{v})
for
w\in\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)
, of which there are finitely many conjugacy classes.

Claim 7.2
.

Let
H
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group, and let
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in H
. Then there exists an unbounded homogeneous quasimorphism
\psi\colon H\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\psi(h_{i})=0
for all
i
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that each
h_{i}
has infinite order, and that the collection
\langle h_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle h_{n}\rangle
is independent (indeed, if
\langle h_{i}\rangle\cup\langle gh_{j}g^{-1}\rangle\neq\{0\}
for some
g\in H
and some
i<j
, then every homogeneous quasimorphism which is trivial on
h_{i}
must also be trivial on
h_{j}
). Now, in a non-elementarily hyperbolic group there are infinitely many commensurability classes of infinite order elements (see e.g.
[
Ol’93
, Lemmas 3.4 and 3.8]
, which prove a stronger result); thus we can find
h_{0}\in H
such that the collection
\langle h_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle h_{n}\rangle
is again independent. Then, using independence, one can find a homogeneous quasimorphism
\psi\colon H\to\mathbb{R}
which is non-trivial on
h_{0}
and vanishes on
h_{i}
for every
i\geq 1
(for example one can combine
[
HO13
, Theorem 4.2]
with
[
DGO17
, Corollary 6.6 and Theorem 6.8]
).
∎

The Claim gives a homogeneous quasimorphism
\psi:H^{\prime}_{v}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\psi|_{\mathfrak{p}_{v}(Z_{w}\cap E_{v})}=0
for all
w\in\operatorname{Lk}_{\overline{X}}(v)
, and we set
\phi^{\lambda}_{v}=\phi_{v}+\lambda\psi\circ\mathfrak{p}_{v}
, which is again a homogeneous quasimorphism on
E_{v}
. If we now take the blowup materials for
G
and replace
\phi_{v}
with
\phi^{\lambda}_{v}
, without altering the other data (i.e. the support graph, the extensions, and the gate maps), we get new blowup materials, as Definition
3.9
.(
5
) still holds by construction; thus we can invoke Theorem
3.10
to produce a family of combinatorial HHG structures
(X,\mathcal{W}_{\lambda})
for
G
. We denote the quasiline associated to
\phi^{\lambda}_{v}
by
L^{\lambda}_{v}
, and by
\mu_{\lambda}
the coarse median arising from the HHG structure.

We are left to show that, as we let
\lambda
vary, the coarse medians
\mu_{\lambda}
are all non-equivalent. Fix
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
. Since
\psi
is unbounded, there exists
g\in E_{v}
with
\psi(\mathfrak{p}_{v}(g))\neq 0
. Let
z\in Z_{v}\cap E_{v}
be any non-trivial element, and consider triples of the form
1,z^{k},g^{l}
, for some integers
k
and
l
. Let
m_{i}=\mu_{\lambda_{i}}(1,z^{k},g^{l})
, for
i=1,2
.

We want to show that there exists
j\in\{1,2\}
such that
\mathrm{d}_{L^{\lambda_{j}}_{v}}(m_{1},m_{2})
can be made arbitrarily large, by choosing appropriate values of
k
and
l
. This will imply that
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
can be made arbitrarily far in
G
, because the projection from
\mathcal{W}_{\lambda_{j}}
to
{L^{\lambda_{j}}_{v}}
is coarsely Lipschitz and
G
acts geometrically on
\mathcal{W}_{\lambda_{j}}
.

To determine the projection of
m_{i}
to the quasiline
L^{\lambda_{j}}_{v}
, we have to determine which of the numbers
\phi^{\lambda_{j}}_{v}(1),\phi^{\lambda_{j}}_{v}(z^{k}),\phi^{\lambda_{j}}_{v}(g^{l})
lies between the other two, because then the projection of
m_{i}
will coarsely coincide with that of the corresponding
x_{i}\in\{1,z^{k},g^{l}\}
.
Since
\phi^{\lambda_{1}}_{v}(g)\neq\phi^{\lambda_{2}}_{v}(g)
, we can choose values of
k
and
l
such that
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
; thus, without loss of generality,
x_{1}
is either
1
or
z^{k}
, and in particular
\phi^{\lambda_{1}}_{v}(x_{1})=\phi^{\lambda_{2}}_{v}(x_{1})=\phi_{v}(x_{1})
. Furthermore, the difference

|\phi^{\lambda_{1}}_{v}(x_{1})-\phi^{\lambda_{2}}_{v}(x_{2})|=|\phi^{\lambda_{2}}_{v}(x_{1})-\phi^{\lambda_{2}}_{v}(x_{2})|

can be made arbitrarily large. This proves that
\mathrm{d}_{L^{\lambda_{2}}_{v}}(m_{1},m_{2})
can be made arbitrarily large, as required.
∎

By inspection of the short HHG structures in our example Sections
2.3
and
6
, Theorem
7.1
implies the following:

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
G
be either:

•

the mapping class group of a sphere with five punctures;

•

a RAAG on a connected, triangle- and square-free graph with at least three vertices;

•

an Artin group of large and hyperbolic type, whose defining graph is not discrete;

•

the fundamental group of an admissible graph of groups;

•

an extension of a Veech group, in the sense of Subsection
2.3.4
;

•

hyperbolic relative to
\mathbb{Z}
-central extensions of hyperbolic groups, of which at least one is non-elementary.

Then
G
admits a continuum of coarsely
G
-equivariant coarse median structures.

Appendix A
Quasicocycles from cyclic extension

In this appendix we prove that, given a
\mathbb{Z}
-central extension
0\to\mathbb{Z}\to G\to H\to 1
of a hyperbolic group and a suitable collection of elements, one can produce a quasimorphism
G\to\mathbb{R}
which is unbounded on
\mathbb{Z}
but trivial on the collection. This is relevant for short HHG as, if one wants to introduce a new cyclic direction
\langle g\rangle
which fixes some vertex
v
of the support graph
\overline{X}
, then one must find a quasimorphism on (a finite-index subgroup of)
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(v)
which vanishes on
g
, in order to produce new blowup materials. In the process, we also identify a gap in a proof from
[
HMS22
]
, and explain how to circumvent it.

Definition A.1
.

Let
G
be a group acting on
\mathbb{Z}
by automorphisms, and let
\sigma\colon G\to\operatorname{Aut}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)
be the action. A
1-quasicocycle
is a map
f\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
such that there exists a constant
D
, called the
defect
of
f
, such that, for every
g,h\in G
,

|f(g)+\sigma(g)(f(h))-f(gh)|\leq D.

We also recall the following definition from the main body of the paper:

Definition A.2

(No hidden symmetries)
.

Let
0\to\mathbb{Z}\to G\xrightarrow{\pi}H\to 1
be a group extension, with
H
hyperbolic, and let
C\leq G
be a cyclic subgroup. We say that
C
has
no hidden symmetries
if
\pi(C)
is infinite and
C
contains a finite-index subgroup which is normal in
\pi^{-1}(K_{H}(\pi(C)))
. We say that an element
g\in G
has no hidden symmetries if
\langle g\rangle
has no hidden symmetries.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
0\to\mathbb{Z}\to G\xrightarrow{\pi}H\to 1
be a group extension, with
H
hyperbolic. Let
\{C_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
be a finite collection of infinite cyclic subgroups of
G
without hidden symmetries, whose projections
\{\pi(C_{i})\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
form an independent collection. Then there exists a 1-quasicocycle
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
, with respect to the action by conjugation, which is the identity on
\mathbb{Z}
and is bounded on
C_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

We first recall a few facts about extensions. For every
g\in G
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}
let
z^{g}=gzg^{-1}
; this
G
-action descends to
H
, and with a little abuse of notation we set
z^{\pi}(g)=gzg^{-1}
. Next, the extension is represented by a
2-cocycle

\omega\colon H\times H\to\mathbb{Z}
, that is, a map which satisfies the cocycle relation

\omega(h_{0},h_{1}h_{2})=\omega(h_{0},h_{1})^{h_{2}}+\omega(h_{0}h_{1},h_{2})-\omega(h_{1},h_{2}).

Up to isomorphism of short exact sequences, we can write
G
as the group with underlying set
\mathbb{Z}\times H
, and with operation given by

(k_{1},h_{1})(k_{2},h_{2})=(k_{1}+k_{2}^{h_{1}}+\omega(h_{1},h_{2}),h_{1}h_{2}).

Furthermore, since
H
is hyperbolic, by
[
NR97
, Theorem 3.1]
the cocycle can be assumed to be
bounded
, meaning that the image of
\omega
is finite. Hence, the map
\phi_{0}\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
given by projecting onto the first factor is a 1-quasicocycle which is the identity on
\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{Z}\times\{1\}
.

Our new goal is to modify
\phi_{0}
to get another 1-quasicocycle
\phi
which is again the identity on
\mathbb{Z}
and is bounded on
C_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
. We proceed by induction on
n
, the base case
n=0
being trivial. Hence suppose there exists a 1-quasicocycle
\phi^{\prime}
which is the identity on
\mathbb{Z}
and is bounded on every
C_{i}
for
i\leq n-1
. Let
C=C_{n}
, and let
K
be the maximal virtually cyclic subgroup of
H
containing
\pi(C)
. As a finite-index subgroup
K^{\prime}
of
\pi(C)
is normal in
K
, there exists a natural action
\sigma\colon K\to\operatorname{Aut}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)=\{\pm\text{Id}\}
by automorphism, mapping
k\in K
to
Id
if and only if it commutes with
K^{\prime}
. There are three cases to consider, according to whether
\sigma
coincides with the action induced by
H
.

Case 1:
Suppose first that there exists
k\in K
such that
\sigma(k)=\text{Id}
, but
z^{k}=-z
for every
z\in\mathbb{Z}
, and we want to prove that
\phi^{\prime}
is already bounded on
C
. Let
h\in\pi^{-1}(k)
. Since
C
has no hidden symmetries, there exists
g\in C
such that
\langle g\rangle
is normalised by
h
, and indeed
g
must commute with
h
since
k
commutes with
K^{\prime}
. Given any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we use the notation
O(1)
to denote a quantity which is bounded independently on
n
. Then we have that

\phi^{\prime}(g^{n})=\phi^{\prime}(hg^{n}h^{-1})=\phi^{\prime}(h)-\phi^{\prime}(g^{n})-\phi(h^{-1})+O(1)=-\phi^{\prime}(g^{n})+O(1).

This shows that
\phi^{\prime}
is bounded on
\langle g\rangle
, and therefore on its finite-index supergroup
C
.

Case 2:
Similarly, suppose that, for some
k\in K
,
\sigma(k)=-\text{Id}
, but
k
centralises
\mathbb{Z}
. As above, we can find
g\in C
which anticommutes with some
h\in\pi^{-1}(k)
; up to passing to the square power of
g
, we can assume that
g
centralises
\mathbb{Z}
. Then again

\phi^{\prime}(g^{n})=\phi^{\prime}(hg^{-n}h^{-1})=\phi^{\prime}(h)+\phi^{\prime}(g^{-n})+\phi(h^{-1})+O(1)=\phi^{\prime}(g^{-n})+O(1).

Furthermore, since
g
commutes with
\mathbb{Z}
,

\phi^{\prime}(1)=\phi^{\prime}(g^{n}g^{-n})=\phi^{\prime}(g^{n})+\phi^{\prime}(g^{-n})+O(1),

and the two properties combine to give that
\phi^{\prime}(\langle g\rangle)
is bounded.

Case 3:
We can therefore assume that
\sigma
coincides with the action induced by
H
. Let
\psi_{0}\colon K\to\mathbb{R}
be the 1-quasicocycle associated to the extension
K^{\prime}\to K\to K/K^{\prime}
, which is the identity on
K^{\prime}
. By
[
HO13
, Theorem 3.1]
, we can extend
\psi_{0}
to a quasicocycle
\psi\colon H\to\mathbb{R}
which is at finite distance from the identity on
K^{\prime}
while it is bounded on
\pi(C_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Up to rescaling
\psi
, assume that
\psi(\pi(g))=1
, where
g\in C
commutes with
\mathbb{Z}
and is normalised by
\pi^{-1}(K)
. Then let
\theta=\psi\circ\pi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
, and set

\phi=\phi^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}(g)\theta.

By construction,
\phi
is still the identity on
\mathbb{Z}
, and it is bounded on
C_{i}
for every
i\leq n-1
. Moreover, since
\theta(g)=1
,
\phi
is now bounded on
C
as well, as required.
∎

Corollary A.4
.

Let
0\to\mathbb{Z}\to G\xrightarrow{\pi}H\to 1
be a group extension, with
H
hyperbolic. Let
\{C_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
be a finite collection of infinite cyclic subgroups of
G
without hidden symmetries, whose projections
\{\pi(C_{i})\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
form an independent collection. Let
E
be the centraliser of
\mathbb{Z}
in
G
. Then there exists a homogeneous quasimorphism
\psi\colon E\to\mathbb{Z}
which is the identity on
\mathbb{Z}
and is trivial on
gC_{i}g^{-1}\cap E
for every
{i=1,\ldots,n}
and
g\in G
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
be the 1-quasicocycle from Lemma
A.3
, which is the identity on
\mathbb{Z}
and is bounded on every
C_{i}
. Furthermore, for every
{i=1,\ldots,n}
and
g\in G
,
\phi
is also bounded on
gC_{i}g^{-1}
, because

\sup_{c\in C_{i}}|\phi(gcg^{-1})|\leq 2D+|\phi(g)|+|\phi(g^{-1})|+\sup_{c\in C_{i}}|\phi(c)|.

Now let
\phi|_{E}
be the restriction of
\phi
to
E
, which is a quasimorphism because
E
acts trivially on
\mathbb{Z}
by conjugation. Then the homogeneous quasimorphism associated to
\phi_{E}
is the identity on
\mathbb{Z}
and trivial on every
gC_{i}g^{-1}\cap E
, as required.
∎

Remark A.5

(Having no hidden symmetries is necessary)
.

In
[
HMS22
, Lemma 4.4]
, Hagen, Martin, and Sisto claimed that, given a central extension of a hyperbolic group
0\to\mathbb{Z}\to G\to H\to 1
and a family of cyclic subgroups
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
whose projections to
H
are independent, there exists a homogeneous quasimorphism which is unbounded on
\mathbb{Z}
and trivial on every
C_{i}
. However, this is not true even if there is a single cyclic subgroup, as the following counterexample shows. Let
G=\langle a,t\mid tat^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle
be the Klein bottle group, which is a central extension
0\to\langle t^{2}\rangle\to G\to G/\langle t^{2}\rangle\to 1
. Since
G
is an index-two overgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, it is amenable, so every homogeneous quasimorphism is actually a homomorphism (see e.g.
[
Fri17
, Corollary 3.8]
). Now let
g=at^{2}
, and notice that
G=\langle g,t\mid tgt^{-1}=g^{-1}t^{4}\rangle
. From this presentation we see that any homomorphism
G\to\mathbb{Z}
which is trivial on
C=\langle g\rangle
must also be trivial on
\langle t^{2}\rangle
.

The problem in the above example is that
g
has a hidden symmetry. Indeed, if one further assumes that all
C_{i}
have no hidden symmetries, then the proof of
[
HMS22
, Lemma 4.4]
can be carried on with minor corrections, which is exactly what we did in the slightly more general Lemma
A.3
.

We stress that
[
HMS22
, Lemma 4.4]
is only ever used in the proof of
[
HMS22
, Lemma 4.5]
, where it can be replaced by our Corollary
A.4
. Indeed, every central extension appearing in that paper is of either of the following two forms:

•

\mathbb{Z}\times F
, where
F
is a free group. Since every virtually cyclic subgroup of a free group is cyclic, every cyclic subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}\times F
has no hidden symmetries, as argued in Remark
5.4
.

•

a Dihedral Artin group
D
. In this case, the cyclic subgroups are conjugates of the standard generators
a
and
b
, and we claim that these elements have no hidden symmetries. Indeed, if
h\in D
,
n\in\mathbb{N}-\{0\}
, and
z
in the centre are such that
ha^{n}h^{-1}=a^{\pm n}z
, then, arguing as in the proof of
[
HMS22
, Lemma 2.8]
, we get that
z=0
, because otherwise the syllabic length of the powers of
a^{\pm n}z
would diverge while the syllabic length of the powers of
ha^{n}h^{-1}
is constant.

Hence all results from
[
HMS22
]
still hold.

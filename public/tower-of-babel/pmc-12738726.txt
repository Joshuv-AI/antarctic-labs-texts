EM-YOLO: high-precision electronic component detection via multi-scale attention and dynamic feature fusion
Authors: Zeyi Xu, Jiahui Han, Hongying Qin, Kangsong Gao, Kai Xie, Jianbiao He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the frequent missed detections and suboptimal accuracy in electronic component circuit board inspection, a target detection algorithm for electronic components, EM-YOLO, is designed with YOLOv11 as the core framework. The overall design employs a three-level optimization strategy: First, in the backbone network part, an efficient feature extraction module C3k2_EMA is designed. By incorporating the Efficient Multi-scale Attention (EMA) mechanism, it enhances the network’s feature representation capability for tiny components. Second, in the neck network part, the BiSPD-FPN structure is proposed, which adds a high-resolution feature map at the P2 level. By combining the Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network (BiFPN) with Spatial Pyramid Depthwise Convolution (SPDConv), it optimizes the multi-scale feature fusion capability and reduces detail loss during the downsampling process. Finally, in the detection head part, the Focal-DIoU loss function is introduced to optimize the bounding box regression process and improve the localization accuracy of densely arranged components. Experimental results show that, on the self-made dataset and the public dataset PCB Electronic Components Dataset, the detection accuracy (mAP@0.5) of the improved algorithm has increased by 0.5% and 3.9% respectively compared with the benchmark algorithm, while the false negative rate (FNR) has decreased by 1.3% and 4% respectively, both outperforming mainstream algorithms. Therefore, the algorithm in this paper exhibits good detection accuracy, and the problem of missed detections occurring in the detection process has also been effectively alleviated. It possesses good generalization ability and provides an effective detection scheme for component detection.Keywords: Electronic Component Testing, YOLOv11, EMA Attention, BiSPD-FPN, Focal-DIoU, FNRSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In the context of modern industrial inspection scenarios,Object detection technology1 has been widely applied in fields such as autonomous driving2, medical image analysis3, and industrial manufacturing4, playing an indispensable role in intelligentization5 and automated production4. As an important branch of this technology, Printed Circuit Board (PCB) inspection6 serves as a core link in the electronic manufacturing industry chain.PCBs serve as the core carrier of electronic products, and their manufacturing process has extremely high requirements for component layout and welding precision.Any delay or error in testing may lead to circuit malfunctions, product failures, and even cause severe economic losses and safety accidents. As electronic products develop towards higher density and miniaturization, the requirements for testing accuracy, stability, and real-time performance are constantly increasing. Therefore, achieving accurate, rapid, and reliable testing of various electronic components on circuit boards is not only directly related to production efficiency and product quality control, but also serves as the foundation for intelligent manufacturing systems to realize autonomous decision-making and closed-loop quality control. For this reason, the issue of electronic component testing has become a research focus of common concern in both industry and academia.

Despite the significant advancements made in object detection technology, electronic component detection still faces unique and complex challenges. Unlike conventional object detection tasks, a single circuit board image usually contains dozens or even hundreds of components, which are densely arranged with frequent mutual occlusion. On the other hand, different components exhibit significant differences in size, and many of them occupy only a tiny number of pixel regions, placing extremely high demands on the model’s ability to recognize small targets. In our self-constructed PCB dataset, each image contains an average of approximately 23 components. Among these, over 65% of the components have a size smaller than 3232 pixels, and about 24% of the components are partially occluded. These characteristics of “large quantity, small size, and frequent occlusion” significantly increase the difficulty of detection. Experimental results show that YOLOv11 achieves an FNR of 22.4% on the overall test set, but the FNR rises to 44.1% specifically for small-sized Resistors. This indicates that there remains a significant performance bottleneck in dense small-target scenarios.Therefore, how to significantly reduce the missed detection rate while maintaining the detection speed, and improve the recognition accuracy for small targets and dense targets, has become a core issue that needs to be urgently addressed in electronic component inspection.

In current industrial inspection scenarios, the core technology for component recognition is machine vision7,8. Traditional target detection algorithms such as edge detection9, due to their relatively simple architectures, are not only susceptible to noise interference with suboptimal detection accuracy but also consume substantial computational resources when processing high-resolution images, failing to fully meet the stringent requirements of component detection. With the innovation of deep learning technology and the leapfrog development of computer computing power, convolutional neural networks10,11 have gradually emerged in the field of target detection, significantly improving the accuracy and operational efficiency of target detection.Current mainstream deep learning-based target detection algorithms can be broadly classified into two major categories. The first category includes two-stage target detection algorithms typified by Fast R-CNN12 and Faster R-CNN13, which also encompasses R-CNN, Mask R-CNN14, R-FCN15, Detectron2, etc. The second category consists of one-stage target detection algorithms, including SSD16, RetinaNet, and the YOLO17 series such as YOLOv318, YOLOv5, YOLOv8, YOLOv1119, etc. Among them, the YOLO series has become a common choice in industrial visual inspection due to its balance between real-time performance and accuracy.However, in the complex scenario of electronic component detection-characterized by multi-scale, small targets, and dense distribution-the detection performance of existing YOLO models still has room for improvement. This indicates an urgent need to design a high-precision model that can effectively fuse features in multi-scale scenarios and enhance the detection performance of small targets, so as to meet the strict requirements of electronic component detection in industrial applications.

In the pursuit of higher-precision target detection algorithms, researchers have conducted multi-dimensional optimizations on existing algorithms. Including improved NMS strategies (Soft-NMS20, Adaptive-NMS21), new loss functions (Focal Loss22, Repulsion Loss23, AggLoss24), and attention mechanisms (such as CBAM, EMA25). These methods have significantly improved accuracy and robustness in general object detection tasks. At the same time, cross-domain research has further promoted the development of object detection methods. For example, the Residual Channel Attention (RCA) network26 has been applied to remote sensing image scene classification, demonstrating stronger feature modeling capabilities; to address cross-domain detection issues, methods based on semi-automatic dataset construction combined with enhanced YOLOv827 and improved YOLOv4 cross-domain adaptation methods28 have been proposed; in disaster scenarios, deep learning has been used for building damage detection29; in traffic scenarios, vehicle detection and tracking methods based on morphological and motion feature points30, as well as real-time aerial vehicle detection and tracking algorithms31, have been developed. These studies indicate that deep learning-based detection methods have wide applicability and continuous optimization potential in complex scenarios. In terms of PCB detection, targeted improvement methods have also emerged in recent years. For instance, YOLO-pdd32 introduces multi-scale modules and residual connections based on YOLOv5, showing stronger robustness in scenarios of dense small objects and defect detection. These advancements have provided new insights for PCB component inspection and defect identification, while also highlighting the room for improvement in existing algorithms within industrial applications.

Based on the above issues, this paper selects YOLOv11, which demonstrates excellent detection performance, as the basic framework. Compared with previous versions (YOLOv5, YOLOv8), YOLOv11 has been optimized in multiple aspects for small targets and complex scenarios: Its adaptive anchor box mechanism can dynamically adjust anchor boxes according to the actual size distribution of targets without manual presets, thereby significantly improving the matching accuracy for a large number of tiny components with large aspect ratio differences on circuit boards. The dynamic label assignment strategy enables more reasonable division of positive and negative samples for multiple targets in dense areas, helping to reduce the false detection rate in occluded regions. Meanwhile, the introduction of C3k2 and C2PSA modules in the backbone network enhances the feature expression capability, which is superior to YOLOv8 especially in extracting fine-grained features. Therefore, while balancing detection speed and accuracy, YOLOv11 is more suitable for the task characteristics of “small and dense, easy to occlude” in PCB detection. YOLOv11 algorithm

YOLOv11 is the new-generation single-stage object detection algorithm launched by Ultralytics. It carries out a number of innovative optimizations based on the YOLO series architecture, significantly improving the detection accuracy and efficiency. Compared with the early versions, YOLOv11 introduces the C3K2 module and C2PSA module, which optimize the feature extraction capability, especially good at handling occluded targets and small object detection. In the bounding box regression task, YOLOv11 still continues the classic design of the YOLO series and uses CIoU11 (Complete Intersection over Union) as the default loss function. Compared with the traditional IoU17, CIoU significantly reduces the positioning deviation of the prediction box through the center point distance and aspect ratio penalty. In addition, YOLOv11 can also achieve real-time detection of targets. The structure diagram of YOLOv11 is shown in Fig. 1.

The module structure of YOLOv11.

The architecture of YOLOv11 consists of four main components: data input, core network, feature fusion, and object detection. Compared with YOLOv5 and YOLOv8 when dealing with different object detection datasets, its anchor box initialization strategy is more advanced. It adopts an adaptive anchor box mechanism26 and dynamic label assignment strategy26, which can optimize the size and proportion of anchor boxes in real time according to the distribution of training data. This significantly improves the accuracy of object detection and positioning capabilities, enabling it to better adapt to different datasets and complex scenarios, significantly enhancing the training and inference efficiency of the model. At the same time, it enhances the deployment flexibility of the model and multi-task support capabilities.

The backbone network part mainly performs the process of feature processing, which mainly includes the C3K2 module, SPFF module, and C2PSA module. The C3K2 module dynamically selects the internal sub-modules through the configuration of the parameter c3k: when c3k is True, the module uses the C3k layer internally, which supports customizing the convolution kernel size to capture feature information of different scales. When c3k is False, it uses the standard Bottleneck layer, similar to the C2f module, focusing on improving computational efficiency. The core of its design is to adapt to different task requirements through parameter configuration, achieving a balance between multi-scale feature extraction and computational efficiency.

In the Neck region between Backbone and Head, the FPN-PAN structure is introduced. FPN18 adopts a top-down information flow design, which can not only effectively transmit high-level semantic features but also enhance semantic expression. However, this structure has the limitation of insufficient positioning information transmission. To this end, the PAN19 structure emerges as the times require. Through a bottom-up feature transmission path, it integrates low-level features rich in positional details upward, forming a functional complement to FPN. These two structures complement each other to form a classic “double-tower” architecture, aiming to achieve deep fusion and optimization of multi-scale features, thereby improving the overall feature expression ability.

Its Head part completes the output of target detection results through convolution operations. YOLOv11 still uses the CIoU (Complete Intersection over Union) loss function, which takes into account the loss of bounding boxes to solve the problem of non-overlapping bounding boxes. In order to further improve the detection speed and accuracy, a penalty term for the aspect ratio is added on the basis of the distance between the midpoints of the original bounding boxes, which can better remove redundant and duplicate boxes. This approach improves the accuracy and enables detection personnel to better identify the items to be detected.

The improved model of EM-YOLOv11

Based on the YOLOv11 model, this paper proposes an improved detection algorithm named EM-YOLO to address the problems of dense targets, tiny sizes, and frequent occlusions in the detection of electronic components on circuit boards. Guided by the above design concepts, EM-YOLO mainly implements structural improvements in three aspects:

The improved algorithm mode.

First, in the backbone network section, aiming at the problem of difficulty in effectively extracting features of tiny components in circuit boards, the C3k2_EMA efficient feature extraction module is designed. The Efficient Multi-scale Attention (EMA) mechanism is incorporated into the original C3k2 structure of YOLOv11. Through the synergistic effect of channel attention and spatial attention, the network can dynamically focus on key regions at the multi-scale level.Compared with the traditional SE or CBAM attention mechanisms,this module can effectively enhance the fine-grained representation capability for tiny components without significantly increasing the number of parameters, thereby improving the model’s ability to recognize complex textures and low-contrast components in circuit boards.

Secondly, in the neck network component, the BiSPD-FPN multi-scale feature fusion structure is proposed. This structure integrates the efficient cross-layer connection mechanism of Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network (BiFPN) and the multi-receptive field capture capability of Spatial Pyramid Depth Convolution (SPDConv). BiFPN adaptively allocates the importance of features at different levels through a weighted fusion method, while SPDConv can expand the receptive field to enhance contextual modeling capability while keeping the computational cost controllable. The combination of the two not only alleviates the loss of detailed features during downsampling but also significantly improves the network’s multi-scale perception and localization capabilities when dealing with dense small targets.Furthermore, the introduction of the P2 high-resolution feature layer supplements shallow spatial information, effectively alleviating the problem of feature loss for small objects during the downsampling process. Compared with the traditional PANet or one-way FPN, BiSPD-FPN achieves a better balance in the interaction between semantic and spatial features, thereby significantly improving the detection accuracy of dense small objects.

Finally, in the detection head section, to address the issue of significant localization deviation in bounding boxes for dense small targets, the Focal-DIoU loss function is introduced. This function incorporates the concept of Focal Loss based on DIoU, assigning higher gradient weights to hard samples, thereby reducing the dominant role of easily classified samples in training. Meanwhile, the loss term comprehensively considers the center point distance, scale difference, and overlapping area between predicted boxes and ground-truth boxes, making the regression process more sensitive to densely arranged small targets.Compared with traditional localization losses such as GIoU and CIoU, Focal-DIoU significantly improves the fitting accuracy of bounding boxes and reduces the false negative rate while maintaining convergence stability.

Experimental results demonstrate that the three improvements mentioned above exert a synergistic effect, enabling EM-YOLO to significantly enhance detection accuracy on both the self-made dataset and the public dataset PCB Electronic Components Dataset. Specifically, in terms of the mAP@0.5 metric, it has increased by [X] and [Y] respectively compared to the baseline YOLOv11, with the missed detection rate being significantly reduced. This proves that the algorithm not only possesses excellent detection accuracy and generalization ability but also has a significant improvement effect on the frequent issue of missed detections in industrial inspection scenarios.

Design the neck module

To address the common challenges in circuit board inspection of electronic components, such as missed detection of small targets and loss of boundary details, this paper proposes a BiSPD-FPN structure based on YOLOv11. The core idea of this structure is to introduce a high-resolution P2 layer into the neck network, and combine SPDConv with the BiFPN bidirectional feature fusion mechanism to enhance the multi-scale feature integration capability and reduce detail loss during the downsampling process.Compared with the one-way fusion and fixed weight assignment methods of traditional FPN/PAFPN, BiSPD-FPN significantly enhances semantic consistency while preserving high-resolution details, addressing the dual issues of “detail loss and feature imbalance” in small object detection.The overall design is shown in Fig. 5.

Apply the P2 detection layer

Although the PAFPN in YOLOv11 achieves cross-scale interaction through bidirectional feature fusion, it still has limitations in small object detection tasks. Firstly, the downsampling process in the network leads to severe loss of details for components smaller than 3232 pixels, making it difficult to preserve boundary information. Secondly, shallow features, although having high resolution, lack sufficient semantic expression; while deep features, despite being semantically rich, lose critical geometric details. As features are transmitted layer by layer, weak edge signals are often submerged by noise interference, resulting in two typical types of errors: false positives (misdetections in background regions) and false negatives (missed detections of real small components).

The traditional solution is to directly introduce the P2 detection layer and use high-resolution feature maps to capture tiny target details. However, simply stacking layers will lead to a sharp increase in computational load and a significant rise in inference delay, making it difficult to meet the real-time requirements of industrial detection. To address this, this paper proposes to introduce the SPDConv operation in the P2 layer. The SPDConv module is shown in Fig. 6.

Design the head module

In object detection tasks, the core goal of bounding box regression is to make the predicted boxes align as closely as possible with the ground-truth boxes. YOLOv11 uses IoU-based loss by default, but it still has the following limitations:

Insufficient differentiation of sample difficulty by IoU loss: All samples are treated equally, which easily causes simple samples to dominate the gradient and weakens the learning ability for hard samples.

Experimental results and analysis

Introduction to the dataset

The dataset used in this paper is a self-built circuit component image dataset, which contains six types of components: capacitors, diodes, light-emitting diodes, resistors, transistors, and voltage regulator diodes.To enhance the model’s adaptability to actual detection environments, data augmentation was applied to images during the training process in this study. The specific augmentation strategies include: random rotation within the range of , scaling with a ratio between [0.9, 1.1], and addition of Gaussian noise (). After processing, the dataset contains a total of 5170 images, including 4162 images in the training set and 1008 images in the validation set.The images in the dataset are mainly derived from laboratory collections and some public circuit board images. Low-quality, blurred, or duplicate samples were removed through manual screening. To ensure the consistency and standardization of annotations, all images were annotated using the LabelImg tool, strictly adhering to the YOLO annotation standards. When annotating bounding boxes, it is required to tightly frame the edges of target components to ensure accurate positioning of small-sized components. If multiple components exist in an image during the annotation process, they are annotated independently one by one.

In experimental design, to avoid accidental results caused by a single division of the dataset, the K-fold cross-validation experiment is introduced to improve the reliability and generalization of the results.

To further evaluate the generalization performance of the proposed algorithm, this paper selects the publicly available PCB Electronic Components Dataset from Kaggle to verify the algorithm’s performance. This dataset contains images of 10 categories of circuit board components, with a total of 4134 images, including 2893 images in the training set, 827 images in the validation set, and 414 images in the test set. The experimental results show that the improved algorithm proposed in this paper also achieves high detection accuracy on this public dataset.

Evaluation index

To verify the feasibility and effectiveness of the model, different performance indicators are usually used to comprehensively evaluate the algorithm26.In this paper, the component detection results are evaluated by selecting evaluation metrics such as Precision, Recall, False Negative Rate (FNR), and mean Average Precision (mAP), as well as metrics related to computational complexity including number of parameters (Params), computational load (GFLOPs), and detection speed (FPS). Their specific expressions are described as follows.

Precision measures the proportion of samples predicted as positive that are actually positive, reflecting the accuracy of prediction results. Recall measures the proportion of actual positive samples successfully identified by the model, reflecting the model’s detection capability. The higher the recall, the fewer the missed detections. The miss rate is the complement of recall, used to measure the proportion of actual positive samples that the model fails to detect. mAP is the average of the average precision (AP) for each category in multi-category detection tasks, comprehensively reflecting the overall detection performance of the model across all categories.

In this study, each symbol in the evaluation index formula has a specific meaning in the circuit board component detection task. TP (True Positive) represents the number of instances where the model successfully detects and correctly classifies a component into a specific category; FP (False Positive) refers to the number of times the model mistakenly identifies other categories or background areas as components of this category; FN (False Negative) indicates the number of components of a specific category that actually exist but are not detected by the model. AP (Average Precision) is used to evaluate the detection performance of the model for a single component category, while mAP (mean Average Precision) comprehensively measures the overall detection capability of the model across all categories, specifically calculated by averaging the precision at different recall rates.

From a mathematical mechanism perspective, Focal-DIoU introduces a sample difficulty weight factor based on the traditional DIoU. The parameter is used to set the threshold for distinguishing sample difficulty: when the IoU is low (hard samples), the loss function assigns a higher weight to them; when the IoU is high (easy samples), the weight is reduced accordingly to prevent the model from over-relying on simple samples during training. The weight factor further dynamically adjusts the contribution of samples, ensuring that hard samples have a greater impact on parameter updates during backpropagation. This mechanism effectively alleviates the problems of “small targets being easily missed and large targets being easily confused” in the detection process, thereby improving the overall detection accuracy.

Comprehensive analysis of the charts and numerical data reveals that the Focal-DIoU loss function can effectively balance the weights of easy and hard samples during the training process, while simultaneously considering both the distance and scale information between predicted bounding boxes and ground-truth boxes. This ultimately enhances the accuracy and robustness of the model in PCB component detection tasks. Particularly in the detection of small-sized, densely arranged, or easily confused component categories, Focal-DIoU significantly reduces the missed detection rate and improves the reliability of the model’s detection performance.

K-fold cross-validation

To further verify the stability and generalization ability of the proposed EM-YOLO model, in addition to the fixed training set/validation set division, this paper also adopts a K-fold cross-validation strategy. Specifically, the self-built circuit board component dataset is randomly shuffled and evenly divided into K=5 non-overlapping subsets with approximately equal sample sizes. In each iteration, one of the subsets is selected as the validation set, and the remaining K-1 subsets are used as the training set for model training and evaluation. This process is repeated K times, ensuring that each sample serves as part of the validation set exactly once.

In each fold of the experiment, indicators such as False Negative Rate (FNR), Precision, Recall, and mAP@0.5 are calculated respectively. The final performance is reported in the form of the mean ± standard deviation across all folds. This method can effectively reduce the evaluation bias that may arise from a single random division, thereby providing a more robust and reliable estimation of the model’s performance.

Conclusion

The detection of electronic components on circuit boards is of great significance to intelligent manufacturing and automated assembly. To address the problems of frequent missed detections and insufficient precision in traditional detection methods, this paper proposes an improved model based on YOLOv11, namely EM-YOLO. In the backbone network, a C3k2_EMA module is designed to enhance the feature expression of tiny components through a multi-scale attention mechanism. In the neck network, a BiSPD-FPN module is constructed, which adds a high-resolution P2 feature map and combines a bidirectional feature pyramid with SPDConv to achieve multi-scale feature fusion of small targets and reduce detail loss during downsampling. In the detection head, the Focal-DIoU loss function is introduced to optimize bounding box regression, improve the positioning accuracy of dense components, and reduce the missed detection rate.Overall, the proposed EM-YOLO model has achieved targeted innovations in both architecture design and feature fusion strategies, providing new ideas and solutions for high-precision dense small object detection tasks.

Experimental results show that the improved algorithm exhibits excellent detection performance and stability on both the self-built dataset and the PCB Electronic Components Dataset. The mAP@0.5 is increased by 0.5% and 3.9% respectively, the missed detection rate is reduced by 1.3% and 4% respectively, and the inference speed reaches 121.43 FPS. Meanwhile, it maintains a reasonable number of parameters and computational load, meeting the real-time requirements of actual industrial detection. The improved algorithm demonstrates strong generalization ability in scenarios with dense small targets and complex environments, providing an efficient and reliable technical solution for the automatic detection of circuit board components.

Future work will focus on the innovation and engineering application potential of EM-YOLO:On one hand, we will integrate lightweight strategies such as model pruning, knowledge distillation, and structural reparameterization. While maintaining high accuracy, we will further reduce computational overhead and latency to facilitate the real-time deployment of EM-YOLO on embedded industrial inspection devices, thereby expanding its practical value. On the other hand, we plan to construct a diverse and high-complexity PCB inspection dataset that covers different component types, sizes, densities, soldering conditions, and lighting conditions. This dataset will support the model’s adaptive optimization and robustness evaluation in cross-domain scenarios. Through the aforementioned research roadmap, future versions of EM-YOLO will achieve further breakthroughs in terms of interpretability, generalization, and adaptive capabilities, providing more innovative and practical inspection solutions for the intelligent manufacturing field.

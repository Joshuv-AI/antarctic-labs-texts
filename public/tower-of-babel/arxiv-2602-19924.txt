Recovery problem of parametrizations from Legendre data
C. Muñoz-Cabello, T. Nishimura, R. Oset Sinha

Abstract
. The problem of recovery of parametrizations from Legendre data is a very important inverse problem. In this paper, we provide a systematic and widely-applicable method to recover parametrizations f : U n → ℝ n + 1 {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f}:{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}U_{n}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1} from Legendre data where U n U_{n} is an open subset of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . Namely, for a dense subset of the space of real-analytic parametrizations from U n U_{n} into ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} , we show how to recover the parametrization from the Gauss mapping and the height function. Moreover, in order to assist readers to apply results of this paper, many concrete examples are given.

Recovery problems are important inverse problems since the
recovery of unprocessed raw data from processed data often gives a
breakthrough in Science and Engineering.

The Legendre transform is one example of such a recovery.
A brief explanation of the Legendre transform is as follows.
For instance, let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by

f(x)=x^{2}
and consider
f
to be the unprocessed data.
Draw the graph of
f
in the
(X,Y)
-plane.
The tangent line to the graph of
f
at
x=x_{0}
is defined by

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{df}{dx}\left(x_{0}\right)\left(X-x_{0}\right)+x^{2}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2x_{0}X-x^{2}_{0}.

Set
p{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=\frac{df}{dx}(x_{0})}=2x_{0}
and

q={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\left(-x_{0}^{2}\right)=x^{2}_{0}}
.
Then, we can obtain the new function

q(p)={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{2}}.

Namely, the
Y
-intercept
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-q}

of the tangent line to the graph of
f

is a function of the slope
p
of the tangent line to the graph of
f
.
The function
q(p)
is a processed data of

and the process

\mathcal{L}(f{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(x)})=q{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(p)}

is called the

.
The recovery problem in this case is to obtain the original raw data
f(x)

from the processed data
q(p)
. It is well-known that the same process gives
the perfect solution of this recovery problem, that is,

\mathcal{L}(q{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(p)})=f{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(x)}
.
Nowadays, Legendre transform has been widely extended
and has important applications in Mathmatical Physics,
Thermodynamics, Convex analysis etc.
For more details on the Legendre Transform, see for instance

[
2
,
10
,
19

.

On the other hand, consider for example the function

Draw the graph of

(X,Y)

The tangent line to the graph of

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dg}{dx}\left(x_{0}\right)\left(X-x_{0}\right)+x^{3}_{0}

If we set

the second author provides a new method to solve the recovery problem.
A brief explanation of the new method
is as follows.
For the given
g(x)=x^{3}
, let

\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
be the mapping defined by

\nu(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+9x^{4}}}\left(-3x^{2},1\right)

Then,

Set

.
Thus, the Legendre data in this case is

\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+9x^{4}}}\left(-3x^{2},1\right),\;\frac{-2x^{3}}{\sqrt{1+9x^{4}}}\right\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}

Next, set
\nu(x)=\left(\cos\theta(x),\sin\theta(x)\right)

It has been shown in

,

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(x,g(x)\right)}=a(x)\left(\cos\theta(x),\sin\theta(x)\right)+b(x)\left(-\sin\theta(x),\cos\theta(x)\right).

In the case that

. Thus, as desired,
we certainly have

\displaystyle a(x)\left(\cos\theta(x),\sin\theta(x)\right)+b(x)\left(-\sin\theta(x),\cos\theta(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-2x^{3}}{\sqrt{1+9x^{4}}}\left(\frac{-3x^{2}}{\sqrt{1+9x^{4}}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+9x^{4}}}\right)+\frac{-x-3x^{5}}{\sqrt{1+9x^{4}}}\left(\frac{-1}{\sqrt{1+9x^{4}}},\frac{-3x^{2}}{\sqrt{1+9x^{4}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{6x^{5}}{{1+9x^{4}}},\frac{-2x^{3}}{{1+9x^{4}}}\right)+\left(\frac{x+3x^{5}}{{1+9x^{4}}},\frac{3x^{3}+9x^{7}}{{1+9x^{4}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{x+9x^{5}}{1+9x^{4}},\frac{x^{3}+9x^{7}}{1+9x^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(x,x^{3}\right)={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(x,g(x)\right)}.

For a frontal

Given a frontal

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\left(\nu,a\right)}

be a hyperplane family.
A real-analytic mapping

(a)

(b)

In other words, an envelope created by

giving a solution
of the following system of first order differential equations with one constraint

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}({\bf x})\cdot\nu({\bf x})&=&0,\\
\vdots&&\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}({\bf x})\cdot\nu({\bf x})&=&0,\\
f({\bf x})\cdot\nu({\bf x})&=&a({\bf x}).\end{array}\right.

For details on envelopes created by families of plane regular curves,

Definition 3
.

A frontal

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Now, we start to give a proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Let
f\colon U_{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a
real-analytic
mapping.

For any
{\bf x}\in U_{n}
, set

{\bf x}=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right).

Namely,
x_{i}

(1\leq i\leq n)

i
-th coordinate of

{\bf x}\in U_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}=\underbrace{\mathbb{R}\times\cdots\mathbb{R}}_{n\mbox{-tuples}}

Set
f_{j}=X_{j}\circ f

(1\leq j\leq n+1)
where

stands for the

j
-th coordinate function of

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=\underbrace{\mathbb{R}\times\cdots\mathbb{R}}_{(n+1)\mbox{-tuples}}
.
Thus, we have set

f({\bf x})=\left(f_{1}({\bf x}),f_{2}({\bf x}),\ldots,f_{n+1}({\bf x})\right).

Consider the Jacobian matrix

Jf({\bf x})=\left(\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}({\bf x})\right)_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq n+1}

of

.
For any

(1\leq k\leq n+1)

k
-th row (

.
That is to say,

M_{f,k}({\bf x})=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}({\bf x})&\cdots&\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}({\bf x})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\widehat{\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial x_{1}}({\bf x})}&\widehat{\cdots}&\widehat{\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial x_{n}}({\bf x})}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial f_{n+1}}{\partial x_{1}}({\bf x})&\cdots&\frac{\partial f_{n+1}}{\partial x_{n}}({\bf x})\\
\end{array}\right|,

where the
\widehat{\cdots}
denotes deleting
\cdots
.
Let
\Phi_{f}:U_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be the real-analytic
function defined by

\Phi_{f}({\bf x})=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(M_{f,k}({\bf x}))^{2}}.

Recall that
a point
{\bf x}\in U_{n}

is singular if

This happens at
and only at

where

all of
the
n
-minors

of the Jacobian matrix
Jf({\bf x})
vanish, which is equivalent to
say ing that this happens at and only at points
{\bf x}
satisfying

\Phi_{f}({\bf x})=0.

Suppose that
U_{n}
is connected. Suppose moreover that

f\not\in RM\left(U_{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)

supposition is equivalent to assume that
the closed set

Then, by Fact
2
(the I dentity Theorem) and by
the first supposition,
it follows

\Phi_{f}({\bf x})\equiv 0\quad(\mbox{for any }{\bf x}\in U_{n}).

Next, we consider linear perturbations of
f
.
For any
j

(1\leq j\leq n+1)

{\bf c}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an

n
-dimensional constant vector.

{\bf c}_{j}=\left(c_{(j,1)},\ldots c_{(j,n)}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For the
f\in A(U_{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, let

f+{\bf{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}}:U_{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a mapping of the following type.

\left(f+{\bf{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}}\right)({\bf x})=\left(f_{1}({\bf x})+{\bf c}_{1}\cdot{\bf x},\ldots,f_{n+1}({\bf x})+{\bf c}_{n+1}\cdot{\bf x}\right)

f
.
Take one point
{\bf x}_{0}
of
U_{n}
and fix it.
For any
{\bf{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)}

If we regard

then the function

is a monic polynomial function with degree

Hence we have the following.

Sublemma 4.1
.

The following subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)}

is of Lebesgue measure zero and closed.

[formula]

Ker\left(\Psi_{f,{\bf x}_{0}}\right)=\left\{{\bf{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)}\,|\,\Psi_{f,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}})=0\right\}.

By Fact
2
and Sublemma
4.1
,
for any

satifying

, the linear perturbation

must be contained

RM\left(U_{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)
.
Moreover, again by Sublemma
4.1
,
for any positive integer
\ell
there exists
{\bf{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)}
such that the linear perturbation

f+{\bf{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}}
satisfies
the following condition.

[formula]

\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{(j,i)}^{2}\right)<\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}.

For any positive integer
\ell
, take one constant vector

{\bf{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}}_{0}[\ell]\in\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)}
satisfying (4.1) and (4.2) and fix it.
Then, it is clear that

[formula]

f+{\bf{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}}_{0}[\ell]\in RM\left(U_{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)\quad(\mbox{for any }\ell\in\mathbb{N})

and

[formula]

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\left(f+{\bf{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}}_{0}[\ell]\right)=f.

Suppose that
U_{n}
is not connected.
Then, since
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies the axiom of second countability,
the number of connected components of
U_{n}
is countable.
Since the union of countably many
Lebesgue measure zero subsets
in
\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)}

is a subset of Lebesgue measure zero, we may again choose
one constant vector

{\bf c}_{0}[\ell]\in\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)}
satisfying (4.1) and (4.2) for any

\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus, we can again obtain (4.3) and (4.4).
This completes the proof.

D\left({\bf x}_{0},\varepsilon\right)\subset Reg(F)
,
there exists a sufficiently small positive number
\delta

(0<\delta\leq\epsilon)
such that
the set

Reg\left(N_{\widetilde{f}_{\ell}}|_{D\left({\bf 0},\delta\right)}:D\left({\bf 0},\delta\right)\to T_{{\bf e}_{n+1}}S^{n}\right)

is dense in
D\left({\bf 0},\delta\right)
.

Recall that
X_{n+1}\circ f_{\ell}
is a quadratic perturbation of

X_{n+1}\circ f
having the following form and also that
h
is merely
a parallel transformation of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

X_{n+1}\circ f_{\ell}({\bf x})=X_{n+1}\circ f({\bf x})+\frac{1}{2}\left({\bf c}_{\ell}\cdot F^{2}({\bf x})\right).

Hence the second derivative of
X_{n+1}\circ f_{\ell}
with respect to

\left(\widetilde{y}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{y}_{n}\right)
has the following form.

[formula]

\frac{\partial^{2}\left(X_{n+1}\circ\widetilde{f}_{\ell}\right)}{\partial\widetilde{y}_{i}\partial\widetilde{y}_{j}}(\widetilde{\bf y})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\frac{\partial^{2}\left(X_{n+1}\circ f\right)}{\partial\widetilde{x}_{i}^{2}}(\widetilde{\bf x})+c_{(\ell,i)}&(\mbox{if }i=j)\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\left(X_{n+1}\circ f\right)}{\partial\widetilde{x}_{i}\partial\widetilde{x}_{j}}(\widetilde{\bf x})&(\mbox{if }i\neq j).\end{array}\right.

Denote by
\Psi_{\widetilde{f}_{\ell},{\bf 0}}\left({\bf c}_{\ell}\right)

the Jacobian determinant of

N_{\widetilde{f}_{\ell}}|_{D\left({\bf 0},\delta\right)}
in
S^{n}
at
{\bf 0}
.
By (4.5), if we regard
c_{(\ell,i)}

(1\leq i\leq n)
as variables,
then the Jacobian determinant of

N_{\widetilde{f}_{\ell}}|_{D\left({\bf 0},\delta\right)}
at
{\bf 0}

is a monic polynomial
with degree
n
.
Thus, by Fact
2
, we have the following sublemma.

Sublemma 4.4
.

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and any
{\bf x}_{0}\in Reg(F)
,
the following subset of

\mathbb{R}^{n}
is of Lebesgue measure zero and closed.

Ker\left(\Psi_{\widetilde{f}_{\ell},{\bf 0}}\right)=\left\{\left.{\bf c}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\right|\,\Psi_{\widetilde{f}_{\ell},{\bf 0}}\left({\bf c}_{\ell}\right)=0\right\}.

By Sublemma
4.4
,
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, any
{\bf x}_{0}
contained in
Reg(F)

and almost all
{\bf c}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the sense of
Lebesgue measure,

(4.B.3)
is satisfied.
Moreover, again by Sublemma
4.4
, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

and almost all
{\bf c}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(in the sense of
Lebesgue measure) satisfying

[formula]

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{(\ell,i)}{{}^{2}}<\frac{1}{\ell^{2}},

{\bf c}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
does not belong to

Ker\left(\Psi_{\widetilde{f}_{\ell},{\bf 0}}\right)
.
The inequality
(4.6)
implies
that we can construct a sequence of quadratic perturbations

\left\{f_{\ell}:U_{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}

satisfying even
(4.C)
as well.

Suppose that
U_{n}
is not connected.
Then, for each connected component of
U_{n}
, the proof in the case that

U_{n}
is connected works well.
Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies the axiom of second countability,
the number of connected components of
U_{n}
is countable,
Since the union of countably many

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

is a subset of Lebesgue measure zero,
we may again construct a sequence of
quadratic perturbations

\left\{f_{\ell}:U_{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}

satisfying
(4.A)
,
(4.B)
and
(4.C)
.
This completes the proof.

∎

5.
Proof of Theorem 3

Take any
f
of
A\left(U_{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)
and fix it.
By the assertion (1) of Theorem
2
, there exists a
sequence
\left\{f_{i}\right\}_{i=1,2,\ldots}\subset PRF\left(U_{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)
satisfying

\lim_{i\to\infty}f_{i}=f
. Take any point
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x}
of
U_{n}
and fix it.
Since
f_{i}\in PRF\left(U_{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\right)
for any

i

(i=1,2,\ldots)
,
by the assertion (1) of Theorem
2
,
there must exist a sequence of points

\left\{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bf x}_{j}\right\}_{j=1,2,\ldots}\subset U_{n}
satisfying

\lim_{j\to\infty}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x}_{j}=x
,

\left\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x}_{j}\right\}_{j=1,2,\ldots}\subset Reg\left(f_{i}\right)
for any

i

(i=1,2,\ldots)
and
f_{i}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x})
is completely recovered
from the sequence of Legendre data

\left\{\left\{\nu_{i}\left({\bf x}_{j}\right),a_{i}\left({\bf x}_{j}\right)\right\}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x}_{j}\in Reg\left(\nu_{i}\right)}\right\}_{i=1,2,\ldots}

of
f_{i}
.
On the other hand,
f({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x})=\lim_{i\to\infty}f_{i}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x})
. Therefore,

f({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x})
is completely recovered
from the sequence of Legendre data

\left\{\left\{\nu_{i}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x}_{j}\right),a_{i}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x}_{j}\right)\right\}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bf x}_{j}\in Reg\left(\nu_{i}\right)}\right\}_{i=1,2,\ldots}

of

.

6.
Examples

Example 3

(Constant mapping)
.

Let
{\bf X}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

be a fixed point, and consider the constant mapping

f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
given by
f({\bf x})={\bf X_{0}}
.
Then
f
is a frontal but not a regular frontal.

Now consider the sequence of concentric spheres
f_{k}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
given by

f_{k}({\bf x})={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bf X}_{0}}+\frac{1}{k}\left(\cos x_{1}\cdots\cos x_{n},\cos x_{1}\cdots\cos x_{n-1}\sin x_{n},\cos x_{1}\cdots\cos x_{n-2}\sin x_{n-1}\dots,\sin x_{1}\right),

We see that
\Reg(f_{k})=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the sphere of centre

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bf X}_{0}}
and radius
1/k
,
hence its Gauss map
\nu_{k}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to S^{n}
is given by

\nu({\bf x})=k(f_{k}({\bf x})-{\bf X}_{0}).

Therefore,
\Reg(\nu_{k})=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f_{k}
is a regular frontal for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We also have that

a_{k}({\bf x})=f_{k}({\bf x})\cdot\nu({\bf x})={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\bf X}_{0}}\cdot\nu({\bf x})+\frac{1}{k}.

It is clear by the definition of
\nu_{k}
that

\theta_{j}(x)=x_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
, meaning that

b_{j}=b_{j}\frac{\partial\theta_{j}}{\partial x_{j}}=b_{1}\frac{\partial\theta_{1}}{\partial x_{j}}+\cdots+b_{n}\frac{\partial\theta_{n}}{\partial x_{j}}=\frac{\partial a_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}=x_{0}\cdot\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial x_{j}}=x_{0}\cdot\mu_{j},

and from here we obtain

a_{k}\nu+b_{1}\mu_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}\mu_{n}=(x_{0}\cdot\nu)\nu+(x_{0}\cdot\mu_{1})\mu_{1}+\cdots+(x_{0}\cdot\mu_{n})\mu_{n}+\frac{1}{k}\nu=x_{0}+\frac{1}{k}[k(f_{k}-x_{0})]=f_{k}.

Example 4

(Cuspidal cross-cap)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the folded Whitney umbrella,

f(x,y)=(x,y^{2},xy^{3}).

This is a regular frontal with
\Reg(f)=\Reg(\nu)=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y\neq 0\}
.
The Legendre data associated to
f
is

\displaystyle\nu(x,y)=\frac{(-2y^{3},-3xy,2)}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}+4}};

\displaystyle a(x,y)=-\frac{3xy^{3}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}+4}}.

Writing
\nu(x,y)=\nu^{2}(\theta_{1}(x,y),\theta_{2}(x,y))
gives us functions

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{1}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}+4}};

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{1}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}}{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}+4}};

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{2}(x,y)=

\displaystyle-\frac{3xy}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}}};

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{2}(x,y)=

\displaystyle-\frac{2y^{3}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}}}.

Applying the chain rule, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{1}}{\partial x}=\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{1}}\frac{\partial\sin\theta_{1}}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=-\frac{18xy^{2}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}}\left(9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}+4\right)};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{2}}{\partial x}=\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{2}}\frac{\partial\sin\theta_{2}}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=\frac{6y^{2}}{9x^{2}+4y^{4}};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{1}}{\partial y}=\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{1}}\frac{\partial\sin\theta_{1}}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=-\frac{6\left(3x^{2}y+4y^{5}\right)}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}}\left(9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}+4\right)};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{2}}{\partial y}=\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{2}}\frac{\partial\sin\theta_{2}}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=-\frac{12xy}{9x^{2}+4y^{4}}.

Since
f
is a regular frontal, the system of equations
da=b_{1}\,d\theta_{1}+b_{2}\,d\theta_{2}
has a unique solution,

\displaystyle b_{1}(x,y)=\frac{xy^{2}\left(9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}+10\right)}{\sqrt{9x^{2}+4y^{4}}\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}+4}};

\displaystyle b_{2}(x,y)=\frac{3x^{2}y-2y^{5}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}+4}}

The vector fields
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to S^{2}
completing the orthonormal basis
\{\nu(x,y),\mu_{1}(x,y),\mu_{2}(x,y)\}
for
T_{f_{n}(x,y)}\mathbb{R}^{3}
are given by

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(x,y)=-\frac{(4y^{3},6xy,9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6})}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}}\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}+4}};

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(x,y)=\left(\frac{3xy}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}}},-\frac{2y^{3}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}y^{2}+4y^{6}}},0\right)

Then we have that

a(x,y)\nu(x,y)+b_{1}(x,y)\mu_{1}(x,y)+b_{2}(x,y)\mu_{2}(x,y)=(x,y^{2},xy^{3})=f(x,y)

and
f
can be recovered from the Legendre data
(\nu,a)
.

(Cross-cap)

\Reg(f)=\Reg(\nu)=\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
, but it is not a frontal.

f
is

\displaystyle\nu(x,y)=\frac{(-2y^{2},-x,2y)}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4\left(y^{4}+y^{2}\right)}};

\nu(x,y)=\nu^{2}(\theta_{1}(x,y),\theta_{2}(x,y))
gives us functions

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{1}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{2y}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4\left(y^{4}+y^{2}\right)}};

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{x^{2}+4y^{4}}{x^{2}+4y^{4}+4y^{2}}};

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{2}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4y^{4}}};

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{2}(x,y)=

\displaystyle-\frac{2y^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4y^{4}}}.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{1}}{\partial x}=\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{1}}\frac{\partial\sin\theta_{1}}{\partial x}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{1}}{\partial y}=\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{1}}\frac{\partial\sin\theta_{1}}{\partial y}

Since

is a pseudo regular frontal, the system of equations
da=b_{1}\,d\theta_{1}+b_{2}\,d\theta_{2}

\displaystyle b_{1}(x,y)=\frac{xy\left(x^{2}+4y^{4}+6y^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4y^{4}}\sqrt{x^{2}+4y^{4}+4y^{2}}};

\displaystyle b_{2}(x,y)=\frac{x^{2}-2y^{4}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4y^{4}}}.

The vector fields
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to S^{2}
completing the orthonormal basis
\{\nu(x,y),\mu_{1}(x,y),\mu_{2}(x,y)\}
for

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(x,y)=-\frac{(4y^{3},2xy,x^{2}+4y^{4})}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4y^{4}}\sqrt{x^{2}+4\left(y^{4}+y^{2}\right)}};

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(x,y)=\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4y^{4}}},-\frac{2y^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4y^{4}}},0\right)

Then we have that

a(x,y)\nu(x,y)+b_{1}(x,y)\mu_{1}(x,y)+b_{2}(x,y)\mu_{2}(x,y)=(x,y^{2},xy)=f(x,y)

can be recovered from the Legendre data
(\nu,a)

Example 6

(Cuspidal edge)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the cuspidal edge, given by
f(x,y)=(x,y^{2},y^{3})
.
The mapping
f
is a frontal, but it is not a regular frontal; nonetheless,
f
is the pointwise limit of the sequence of mappings
f_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
given by

f_{n}(x,y)=\left(x,\frac{x^{2}}{n}+y^{2},y^{3}\right),

which are regular frontals with
\Reg(f_{n})=\Reg(\nu_{n})=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y\neq 0\}
.
Moreover, we can transform
f_{n}
into
f
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
by applying a suitable change of coordinates in the source and target.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
: the Legendre data associated to
f_{n}
is

\displaystyle\nu_{n}(x,y)=\frac{(6xy,-3ny,2n)}{\sqrt{9n^{2}y^{2}+4n^{2}+36x^{2}y^{2}}};

\displaystyle a_{n}(x,y)=\frac{y\left(3x^{2}-ny^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{9n^{2}y^{2}+4n^{2}+36x^{2}y^{2}}}.

Writing
\nu_{n}(x,y)=\nu^{2}(\theta_{1}(x,y),\theta_{2}(x,y))
gives us functions

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{1}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{2n}{\sqrt{9n^{2}y^{2}+4n^{2}+36x^{2}y^{2}}};

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{1}(x,y)=

\displaystyle 3y\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}+4x^{2}}{9n^{2}y^{2}+4n^{2}+36x^{2}y^{2}}};

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{2}(x,y)=

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{\sqrt{n^{2}+4x^{2}}};

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{2}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{2x}{\sqrt{n^{2}+4x^{2}}}.

Note that when
n
goes to infinity,
\sin\theta_{2}(x,y)
goes to
1
and
\cos\theta_{2}(x,y)
goes to
0
for any
(x,y)\in\Reg(f_{n})
, meaning that
\theta_{2}
must be a constant function and the differential equation

da=b_{1}\,d\theta_{1}+b_{2}\,d\theta_{2}

admits infinite solutions
(b_{1},b_{2})
.

Applying the chain rule, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{1}}{\partial x}=\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{1}}\frac{\partial\sin\theta_{1}}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=-\frac{24nxy}{\sqrt{n^{2}+4x^{2}}\left(9n^{2}y^{2}+4n^{2}+36x^{2}y^{2}\right)};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{2}}{\partial x}=\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{2}}\frac{\partial\sin\theta_{2}}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n}{n^{2}+4x^{2}};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{1}}{\partial y}=\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{1}}\frac{\partial\sin\theta_{1}}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=-\frac{6n\sqrt{n^{2}+4x^{2}}}{n^{2}\left(9y^{2}+4\right)+36x^{2}y^{2}};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{2}}{\partial y}=\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{2}}\frac{\partial\sin\theta_{2}}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=0.

Since
f_{n}
is a regular frontal, the system of equations
da=b_{1}\,d\theta_{1}+b_{2}\,d\theta_{2}
has a unique solution,

\displaystyle b_{1}(x,y)=\frac{3n^{2}y^{4}+2n^{2}y^{2}-2nx^{2}+12x^{2}y^{4}}{\sqrt{n^{2}+4x^{2}}\sqrt{9n^{2}y^{2}+4n^{2}+36x^{2}y^{2}}};

\displaystyle b_{2}(x,y)=\frac{3xy\left(n^{2}+2ny^{2}+2x^{2}\right)}{n\sqrt{9n^{2}y^{2}+4n^{2}+36x^{2}y^{2}}}

The vector fields
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to S^{2}
completing the orthonormal basis
\{\nu_{n}(x,y),\mu_{1}(x,y),\mu_{2}(x,y)\}
for
T_{f_{n}(x,y)}\mathbb{R}^{3}
are given by

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(x,y)=-\frac{(-4nx,2n^{2},3y(n^{2}+4x^{2})}{\sqrt{n^{2}+4x^{2}}\sqrt{9n^{2}y^{2}+4n^{2}+36x^{2}y^{2}}};

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(x,y)=\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{n^{2}+4x^{2}}},\frac{2x}{\sqrt{n^{2}+4x^{2}}},0\right)

Then we have that

a_{n}(x,y)\nu_{n}(x,y)+b_{1}(x,y)\mu_{1}(x,y)+b_{2}(x,y)\mu_{2}(x,y)=\left(x,\frac{x^{2}}{n}+y^{2},y^{3}\right)=f_{n}(x,y)

and
f
can be recovered from the sequence of Legendre data
\{(\nu_{n},a_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Example 7

into

\nu_{n}(x,y)=\nu^{2}(\theta_{1}(x,y),\theta_{2}(x,y))

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}y^{4}+4n^{2}y^{2}-4nxy^{2}+4x^{2}}{n^{2}y^{4}+4n^{2}y^{2}+n^{2}-4nxy^{2}+4x^{2}}};

is a regular frontal, the system of equations

\displaystyle b_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{3}y^{2}\left(xy^{4}+4xy^{2}+x+3y^{6}+12y^{4}+4y^{2}\right)+n^{2}x\left(x\left(-3y^{4}+4y^{2}+2\right)-12y^{6}\right)+12nx^{2}y^{4}+4x^{4}}{n\sqrt{n^{2}y^{2}\left(y^{2}+4\right)-4nxy^{2}+4x^{2}}\sqrt{n^{2}\left(y^{4}+4y^{2}+1\right)-4nxy^{2}+4x^{2}}};

\displaystyle b_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{y\left(nxy^{2}+2nx+2ny^{4}-2x^{2}-4xy^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{n^{2}y^{4}+4n^{2}y^{2}+n^{2}-4nxy^{2}+4x^{2}}}

The vector fields
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to S^{2}
completing the orthonormal basis
\{\nu_{n}(x,y),\mu_{1}(x,y),\mu_{2}(x,y)\}
for

are given by

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n(2x-ny^{2}),2n^{2}y,n^{2}y^{4}+4n^{2}y^{2}-4nxy^{2}+4x^{2})}{\sqrt{n^{2}y^{4}+4n^{2}y^{2}-4nxy^{2}+4x^{2}}\sqrt{4n^{2}y^{2}+n^{2}+\left(2x-ny^{2}\right)^{2}}};

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2ny,ny^{2}-2x)}{\sqrt{n^{2}y^{4}+4n^{2}y^{2}-4nxy^{2}+4x^{2}}}.

Then we have that

and
f
can be recovered from the sequence of Legendre data
\{(\nu_{n},a_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Example 8

(
D_{4}
singularity)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be the
D_{4}^{+}
singularity, given by
f(u,v,w)=(u,vw,2uv+3v^{2}+w^{2},uv^{2}+2v^{3}+2vw^{2})
(see Figure
2
).
The mapping
f
is a frontal, but it is not a regular frontal; nonetheless,
f
is the pointwise limit of the sequence of mappings
f_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
given by

f_{n}(x,y)=\left(u,vw,2uv+3v^{2}+w^{2},\frac{u^{2}}{n}+uv^{2}+2v^{3}+2vw^{2}\right),

which are regular frontals with
\Reg(\nu_{n})=\Reg(f_{n})=\{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:w^{2}\neq uv+3v^{2}\}
.
Moreover, we can transform
f_{n}
into
f
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
by applying a suitable change of coordinates in the source and target.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
: the Legendre data associated to
f_{n}
is

\displaystyle\nu_{n}(u,v,w)=\frac{(nv^{2}-2u,-2nw,-nv,n)}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}+n^{2}+\left(nv^{2}-2u\right)^{2}}}.

Writing
\nu_{n}(u,v,w)=\nu^{3}(\theta_{1}(u,v,w),\theta_{2}(u,v,w),\theta_{3}(u,v,w))
gives us functions

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}+n^{2}+\left(nv^{2}-2u\right)^{2}}};

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{1}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}{n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}+n^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}};

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{nv}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}};

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}};

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{3}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2nw}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}};

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{nv^{2}-2u}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}}.

Applying the chain rule, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{1}}{\partial u}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2n\left(2u-nv^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\left(n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}+n^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}\right)};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{2}}{\partial u}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2nv\left(nv^{2}-2u\right)}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\left(n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}\right)};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{3}}{\partial u}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4nw}{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+4w^{2}\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{1}}{\partial v}

\displaystyle=-\frac{n^{2}v\left(2nv^{2}+n-4u\right)}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\left(n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}+n^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}\right)};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{2}}{\partial v}

\displaystyle=-\frac{n\left(-n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}+4u^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\left(n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}\right)};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{3}}{\partial v}

\displaystyle=\frac{4n^{2}vw}{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+4w^{2}\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{1}}{\partial w}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4n^{3}w}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\left(n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}+n^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}\right)};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{2}}{\partial w}

\displaystyle=\frac{4n^{3}vw}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\left(n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}\right)};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta_{3}}{\partial w}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2n\left(nv^{2}-2u\right)}{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+4w^{2}\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}.

Since
f_{n}
is a regular frontal, the system of equations
da=b_{1}\,d\theta_{1}+b_{2}\,d\theta_{2}+b_{3}\,d\theta_{3}
has a unique solution given by

\displaystyle b_{1}(u,v,w)=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{3}v\left(u\left(v^{5}+v^{3}+4vw^{2}+v\right)+2v^{6}+2v^{4}\left(w^{2}+1\right)+v^{2}\left(10w^{2}+3\right)+w^{2}\left(8w^{2}+3\right)\right)}{n\sqrt{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+v^{2}+4w^{2}\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\sqrt{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+v^{2}+4w^{2}+1\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}}+

\displaystyle+\frac{n^{2}u\left(u\left(-3v^{4}+v^{2}+4w^{2}+2\right)-8v^{3}\left(v^{2}+w^{2}\right)\right)+8nu^{2}v\left(v^{2}+w^{2}\right)+4u^{4}}{n\sqrt{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+v^{2}+4w^{2}\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\sqrt{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+v^{2}+4w^{2}+1\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}};

\displaystyle b_{2}(u,v,w)=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\left(u\left(2v^{5}+v^{3}+8vw^{2}\right)+3v^{6}+v^{4}w^{2}+10v^{2}w^{2}+4w^{4}\right)}{\sqrt{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+4w^{2}\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\sqrt{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+v^{2}+4w^{2}+1\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}}+

\displaystyle+\frac{-2nuv\left(4uv^{2}+u+6v^{3}+2vw^{2}\right)+4u^{2}\left(2uv+3v^{2}+w^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+4w^{2}\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\sqrt{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+v^{2}+4w^{2}+1\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}};

\displaystyle b_{3}(u,v,w)=

\displaystyle\frac{w\left(n\left(2u+v^{3}\right)-2uv\right)}{\sqrt{n^{2}\left(v^{4}+v^{2}+4w^{2}+1\right)-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}}.

\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to S^{3}
completing the orthonormal basis
\{\nu_{n},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}\}
for
T_{f_{n}(u,v,w)}\mathbb{R}^{4}
are given by

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n(nv^{2}-2u),2n^{2}w,n^{2}v,n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2})}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\sqrt{n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}+n^{2}+\left(nv^{2}-2u\right)^{2}}};

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{(nv(nv^{2}-2u),2n^{2}vw,n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2},0)}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+n^{2}v^{2}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}};

\displaystyle\mu_{3}(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2nw}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}},\frac{nv^{2}-2u}{\sqrt{n^{2}v^{4}+4n^{2}w^{2}-4nuv^{2}+4u^{2}}},0,0\right).

Then we have that

\displaystyle a_{n}(x,y)\nu_{n}(x,y)+b_{1}(x,y)\mu_{1}(x,y)+b_{2}(x,y)\mu_{2}(x,y)+b_{3}(x,y)\mu_{3}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\left(u,vw,2uv+3v^{2}+w^{2},\frac{u^{2}}{n}+uv^{2}+2v^{3}+2vw^{2}\right)=f_{n}(x,y)

and

.

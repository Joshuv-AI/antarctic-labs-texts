Intracranial aneurysm embolization using Penumbra fill and finish coils: 1-year results of a prospective, real-world, multicenter SURF study
Authors: Clemens Maria Schirmer, Albert J Yoo, Alejandro M Spiotta, Ian Kaminsky, Amer Alshekhlee, Robert M Starke, Andrew Nicholson, Osama O Zaidat, Bradley Bohnstedt, Marios-Nikos Psychogios, Ryan Viets, Kaiz S Asif, Christopher J Stapleton, Alim P Mitha, Oded Goren
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractABSTRACTBackgroundSURF was a prospective, multicenter, single-arm, observational study with core lab adjudication of radiographic data, assessing embolization of intracranial aneurysms (IAs) using WAVE Extra Soft Coils as part of SMART Coil System.MethodsAdults undergoing IA embolization with the SMART Coil System (Penumbra, Inc.) comprising >75% of implanted coils and WAVE as the final finishing coil were enrolled at 43 global centers. Primary outcomes were adequate occlusion (Raymond–Roy Occlusion Classification, RROC I/II) at 1 year, serious adverse events (SAEs) within 24 hours, and device-related SAEs up to 7 days (or discharge).ResultsA total of 572 patients (mean age 59.5 years, 72.6% female) were enrolled between November 2019 and August 2022. Among target IAs, 39.9% were ruptured, 78.7% saccular, 54.1% wide-necked, and 11.2% previously treated, with 37.1% located in the internal carotid artery and 30.6% in the anterior cerebral artery. The mean size was 6.6±3.45 mm. Unassisted coiling was used in 50.7% of cases. Adjunctive therapy included stent-assisted coiling (28.8%), balloon-assisted coiling (17.7%), and flow diverters or medications (5.6%). The mean packing density was 34%. RROC I/II was 87.4% immediately post-procedure and 92.4% at 1 year. The retreatment rate was 9.8%, and recanalization was 12.7% at 1 year. SAEs rate within 24 hours was 9.3%. Device-related SAEs occurred in 1.1% of patients. The rate of major ipsilateral stroke was 1.7%.ConclusionPenumbra’s WAVE coils resulted in high packing density and effective and durable embolization of IAs in a diverse, real-world population, supporting coiling as the standard of care for IA treatment.Trial registration numberNCT04106583.Keywords: Aneurysm, Coil, Brain

INTRODUCTION

Treatment of intracranial aneurysms (IAs) has changed dramatically over the last 20 years. The rapid evolution of tools and techniques has broadened the spectrum of IAs amenable to treatment, improved patient outcomes, and optimized healthcare costs.1 2 IAs can be treated with microsurgical clipping and endovascular methods such as coiling, balloon- or stent-assisted coiling (BAC, SAC), or intravascular flow diversion and intrasaccular flow disruption.

Despite continuous shifts in the practice patterns, contemporary guidelines and moderate- to high-level clinical evidence support endovascular coiling as a mainstay treatment modality due to its ability to treat a versatile range of IAs.3,5 The SMART Coil System (Penumbra Inc., Alameda, CA, USA) has been evaluated in multiple publications. It is a widely accepted treatment option for various IAs regardless of rupture status, size, morphology, and neck width.6,10 The unique characteristics of the SMART Coil System include progressive levels of softness, tight conformational structure, and robust stretch resistance.11

WAVE Extra Soft Coils (WAVE) are the newest addition to the SMART Coil System, designed for filling and finishing the coil frame. The current study aimed to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of the fill and finish WAVE coils as part of the SMART Coil System for treating IAs in a real-world setting with core lab adjudication of imaging data.

METHODS

Ethics approval

The study followed good clinical practice and local regulatory requirements. Institutional review board (IRB) and ethics committee (EC) approval was obtained at each institution. All procedures were conducted according to the institutional routine care and manufacturers’ instructions for use.

Study design

SURF (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04106583) was a post-market, prospective, multicenter, single-arm, industry-sponsored study that assessed embolization of IAs using fill and finish WAVE coils as a part of the SMART Coil System (Penumbra Inc.). The study involved independent core lab adjudication (Oculus Imaging, Knoxville, TN, USA) of imaging outcomes and occlusion grading including Raymond–Roy Occlusion Classification. Additional details are available in the study protocol, which is available in online supplemental file 1.

Patient eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria were: (1) adult aged ≥18 years, (2) undergoing embolization of IA, (3) WAVE Extra Soft Coil as the final finishing coil, (4) SMART Coil System accounting for at least 75% of the total number of coils implanted, and (5) informed consent obtained per IRB/EC requirements.

Exclusion criteria were: (1) life expectancy less than 1 year, as judged by the local investigators, (2) patient previously enrolled in the SURF Study, (3) known multiple IAs requiring treatment during index procedure, (4) patient unwilling or unable to comply with protocol follow-up schedule and/or based on the investigator’s judgment, the patient was not a good study candidate, and/or (5) participation in an interventional drug or device study which would confound the results.

Primary and secondary endpoints

The primary effectiveness endpoint was adequate occlusion, defined as Raymond–Roy Occlusion Class (RROC) I or II at the final, 1-year follow-up. The primary safety endpoints were serious adverse events (SAEs) within 24 hours post-procedure and device-related SAEs up to 7 days or discharge. Secondary efficacy endpoints were immediate post-procedure occlusion rate, retreatment rate at final follow-up, complete IA occlusion defined as RROC of I post-treatment and at final follow-up, and IA recanalization or progressive thrombosis post-procedure through final follow-up. Recanalization was defined as an increase in size of the remnant in contrast filling of the IA sac using results of immediate post-stent/embolization angiography as baseline.

Secondary safety endpoints were major ipsilateral stroke, defined as an acute episode of focal or global neurological dysfunction due to brain or retinal infarction or from any intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) inclusive of subarachnoid, intraventricular, or intraparenchymal hemorrhages in the same hemisphere of the target IA and which is associated with an increase of 4 or more points on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) at 24 hours after stroke onset, device-related SAE at final follow-up, and all-cause morbidity and mortality. Morbidity was defined as a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) of 3 to 5 or a worsening of mRS from baseline by 2 points.

Endovascular treatment and data collection

Demographics, medical history, vitals, and mRS were captured at baseline for all participants. Participants with subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) were also evaluated using Hunt and Hess grading.12 Vitals and mRS were reassessed at 7 days (or discharge if earlier) and the final follow-up visit. Angiography was performed per institutional standard of care, with digital subtraction angiography (DSA), CT angiography (CTA), or magnetic resonance angiography (MRA) accepted as the imaging modality. Sedation type and vessel access site were determined per the physician’s standard of care. The use of Penumbra Coils was mandated by the eligibility criteria. All adjunctive techniques and devices were permitted. The independent core lab was responsible for making a final determination on IA occlusion grading.

In the current study, only adverse events (AEs) related to the procedure or device, and all SAEs, were collected from the procedure through discharge. After discharge, only neurological SAEs were collected. All deaths were captured regardless of causality. All reported safety events, including events of interest (defined per protocol as related to symptomatic ICH (sICH) and ischemic stroke; online supplemental file 1), were reviewed for seriousness, expectedness, and relatedness. Participants were followed up for 1 year±90 days.

For patients with saccular IAs, coil packing density was calculated based on two-dimensional geometric modeling as the primary analysis and the ABC method as a supplemental analysis (ie, cases without electronic data capture (EDC) entries).8 A wide-necked IA was defined as a neck width of at least 4 mm or dome-to-neck ratio <2.

Statistical analysis

The study was designed to enroll approximately 800 participants at up to 60 global sites. With prespecified precision goals met, the study was stopped early at 575 patients (three participants were later identified as screen failures), as recruiting additional participants would not change the study outcome (online supplemental table 1). The primary analysis was based on data as observed, that is, without imputing missing values. Continuous variables were summarized using the following descriptive statistics: umber (N), mean, standard deviation (SD), median, interquartile range (IQR), and range. Categorical variables were summarized using frequency counts and percentages of participants within each category. All statistical tests were two-tailed with a significance level of 0.05, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were two-sided. The 95% Wald CIs were reported for the proportion or differences between proportions. P-values for subgroup analysis were based on Fisher’s exact test (categorical data), the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (ordinal data with two subgroup categories), or the Kruskal–Wallis test (ordinal data with three or more subgroup categories), subject to the caveat that the study was not powered for ad hoc subgroup comparisons. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). The data supporting this study are available on reasonable request from the corresponding author.

RESULTS

Demographics and baseline characteristics

A total of 572 individuals were enrolled at 36 North American and 7 European sites from November 2019 to August 2022. Online supplemental figure 1 illustrates the CONSORT Diagram of the Screened Population. Participants were followed up for a median (IQR) of 379.5 (282.0, 440.0) days. In total, 494 participants completed the study. Demographics and baseline characteristics are presented in table 1. The mean (SD) age was 59.5 (12.88) years (range 25–89 years). Of all the study participants, 37.8% (216/572) were 65 years or older, 72.6% (415/572) were female, 71.2% (374/525) were White, 60.5% (346/572) had a history of hypertension, 57.3% (327/571) had a history of smoking, 17.5% (100/572) had history of prior stroke, and 14.0% (80/572) had a family history of IA or malformation. There were 96.5% (551/571) participants with a premorbid (historical baseline) mRS of 0–2.

Aneurysm and procedure characteristics

IA characteristics are described in table 2. Across the study population, 77.4% (443/572) of participants had one IA. Among the target IAs, 39.9% (228/572) were ruptured, 78.7% (450/572) were saccular, 54.1% (307/567) were wide-necked, and 11.2% (64/572) were previously treated. The mean (SD) IA size was 6.6 (3.45) mm. Medium-sized IAs (>4 mm and ≤10 mm) comprised the majority of target IAs (68.6%; 391/570). The most common locations for the target IAs were the internal carotid artery (37.1%) and anterior cerebral artery (30.6%). IAs of posterior circulation presented in 14.2% (81/572) of all cases, with basilar bifurcation being the most common site (5.8%; 33/572). Among all participants presenting with ruptured IAs, 24.6% (56/228) had the most severe Hunt and Hess grades of IV or V.

Effectiveness and overall safety

Effectiveness and overall safety are detailed in table 4. Immediately post-procedure, the rate of adequate occlusion (RROC I or II) was 87.4% (498/570) and 92.4% (366/396) at 1-year follow-up. Retreatment and recanalization rates at 1-year follow-up were 9.8% (45/457) and 12.7% (50/395), respectively. A good outcome at 1-year follow-up (mRS 0–2) was seen in 85.6% (415/485) of the entire cohort, 76.8% (139/181) of participants with ruptured IAs, and 90.8% (276/304) of participants with unruptured IAs. Compared with premorbid historical baseline, better or stable mRS at 1-year follow-up was seen in 70.0% (339/484) of the entire cohort and 51.4% (93/181) of participants with ruptured IAs and 81.2% (246/303) of participants with unruptured IAs, respectively. Overall, for the 30.0% (145/484) of participants with worse mRS scores at 1-year follow-up, 49.7% (72/145) experienced a 1-point shift and 50.3% (73/145) experienced a ≥2-point shift in their mRS score. All-cause morbidity was 18.5% (106/572), and all-cause mortality was 6.3% (37/572) at 1-year follow-up.

Adverse events

Reflecting the severity of illness in study participants, the overall rate of any reportable AE was 27.1% (155/572), and for SAEs was 24.5% (140/572). There were 9.3% (53/572) of participants with SAEs up to 24 hours post-procedure. However, device-related SAEs up to 7 days or discharge were reported in 1.1% (6/567) of participants. Of 8 device-related events, 6 were SAEs, and 2 were non-serious AEs. Of the 6 device-related SAEs, 3 (0.7%) were aneurysm perforations, 2 (0.7%) were coil loop protrusions, and 1 (0.2%) was an embolism. No device-related SAEs were observed at the final follow-up. Additional details of AEs are presented in online supplemental table 2.

Safety events of interest

Details of safety events of interest are presented in online supplemental table 3. The rate of sICH was 0.9% (5/572), with a 0.3% (2/572) rate of SAH due to target IA rupture. The rate of new or worsening neurological deficits from ischemic stroke was 4.5% (26/572). Rates of a sustained increase in NIHSS (≥4) in those with ischemic stroke was 1.9% (11/572).

Subgroup analyses

A prespecified secondary analyses assessed effectiveness and safety outcomes by age, sex, race, geography, IA size, location, morphological parameters, rupture status, and treatment history at admission (online supplemental table 4). Effectiveness and safety endpoints were comparable across subsets of participants except for IA size, location, and rupture status. Specifically, adequate occlusion (RROC I or II) rates at 1-year follow-up were higher in patients with small and medium (97.6% (81/83) and 94.4% (254/269)) versus large/giant IA (69.8% (30/43); p<0.0001), in those with anterior (94.4% (319/338)) versus posterior IAs (81.0% (47/58); p=0.0015), and those—trend only—with unruptured (94.3% (248/263)) versus ruptured IAs (88.7% (118/133); p=0.7729). Retreatment rates at final follow-up were lower in those with small and medium (4.3% (4/94) and 10.0% (31/310)) versus large/giant IAs (19.6% (10/51); p<0.0145), and those with unruptured (5.0% (15/299) versus ruptured IA (19.0% (30/158); p<0.0001). SAEs 24 hours post-procedure were less frequent in patients with an unruptured (5.5% (19/334) versus ruptured IAs (14.9% (34/228); p=0.0002).

DISCUSSION

In one of the largest core lab adjudicated, real-world, multicenter studies to date, the SURF data demonstrate a favorable effectiveness, safety, and durability profile supporting the use of the WAVE Extra Soft Coil and the SMART Coil System. The current report offers robust evidence that reaffirms endovascular coiling, with and without the assistance of adjunctive techniques, as a mainstay treatment for IA varying in presentations and morphological complexities.

SURF participants had diverse IA characteristics, including anterior and posterior locations, bifurcation and sidewall placements, and sizes ranging from 1.6 mm to 27.0 mm. Notably, over half of the participants had wide-necked IAs, and over a third had ruptured IAs. Despite this heterogeneity and high volume of challenging cases, SURF achieved high rates of adequate immediate post-operative occlusion (87.4%) and low SAEs (1.1%; device-related SAEs within 7 days/discharge). These positive outcomes were maintained at 1-year follow-up as evidenced by low retreatment (9.8%) and recanalization (12.7%) rates and sustained clinical benefit, with high rates of mRS 0–2 (85.6%) and most participants (70.0%) returning to stable or improved premorbid functional status. In addition, the all-cause mortality remained low (6.3%), and no device-related mortality was observed at this extended time.

The results of the current study are comparable to or better than data reported for other major coil embolization studies713,19 (online supplemental table 5). Precisely, the rate of adequate immediate post-procedure occlusion of 87.4% is similar to previously published data on unruptured IAs (83.6% to 100%)716,18 20 and exceeds rates reported in cohorts combining ruptured and unruptured IAs (17.8% and 49.3%).21 22 SURF’s 1-year adequate occlusion rate of 92.4% is well within the ranges commonly reported 6 months to 5 years after procedure with non-Penumbra Coils (ranging from 49.0% to 98.4%)1516 18 21,25 The recanalization rate of 12.7% is higher than that observed by Zhao et al (2022),16 who reported rates within 5.3% to 8.0% range at 6 months, likely (at least in part) due to SURF enrolling a greater number of participants with ruptured IAs. SURF retreatment rate at 1 year (9.8%) is comparable to many reported figures, including studies combining ruptured and unruptured IAs,21 reporting on large IAs only,14 or previously treated IAs.13 Although some trials reported retreatment rates as low as 0% to 7.1% after 6 to 24 months after the index procedure,7 15 16 19 22 23 SURF retreatment rates compare favorably to those reported by Raymond et al (2017)14 and Nickele et al (2022)22 ranging from 11.3% to 11.6% at 6 to 18 months.

A recent systematic review and meta-analysis by Habibi et al (2024), reporting on endovascular coiling with the SMART Coil System in 1403 patients (1406 IAs), found the rates of adequate occlusion at final follow-up (90%), retreatment (9%), and recanalization (10%) to be consistent with the data presented in the current analysis.26

One of the coiling studies that most closely mirrors SURF methodology is SMART Registry,7 with'>75% of participants treated with Penumbra Coils (SMART, PC400, and/or POD coils). Similar to the current analysis, SMART found high rates of adequate occlusion (97.1%) with low rates of recanalization (12.9%), retreatment (7.1%), device-related SAEs within 24 hours (2.9%), and mRS 0–2 achieved in 84.4% of participants at 1-year follow-up. Notably, the SMART Registry did not employ core lab adjudication, and WAVE Extra Soft Coils comprised only approximately 13% of all inserted coils. At the same time, in the current analysis, this number was close to 50%. Therefore, SURF provides higher-level data on the SMART Coil System, particularly WAVE Extra Soft finishing coils, by incorporating core lab adjudication into the real-world data and capturing evidence of a broad range of IA presentations and complexities.

Limitations

Our study has limitations that are inherently shaped by the profile of the population studied and the characteristics of the IAs. The absence of a control arm precluded directly evaluating the comparative effectiveness of endovascular coiling with the SMART Coil System and other devices or treatment modalities. While SURF followed patients through 1 year, allowing recovery from the ictus morbidity and capturing post-treatment recurrence data,13 a longer-term follow-up, which is often performed in high-risk cases like ruptured and/or neck remnant IAs, might provide additional insights.27 The loss to follow-up rate of 11.2% was comparable to rates reported in other neurovascular studies conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic.28 Our results should be viewed in the context that approximately 10% of cases were retreatments of previously embolized aneurysms, a subpopulation which presents with unique coiling risks. Finally, the study was not powered for ad hoc subgroup comparisons nor adjusted for confounders, and we emphasize that conclusions drawn from subgroup analyses should be viewed cautiously.

CONCLUSIONS

Treatment with WAVE fill and finish coils as part of the SMART Coil System achieves high packing density and effective, safe, and durable embolization of IAs in a real-world population with diverse IA characteristics, demonstrated with core lab adjudicated data. SURF provides further evidence supporting endovascular coiling as the modern standard of care for IA treatment.

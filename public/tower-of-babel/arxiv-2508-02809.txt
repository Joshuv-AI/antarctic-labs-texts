Abstract
. Let φ : 𝔻 → 𝔻 \varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D} be a parabolic self-map of the unit disc 𝔻 \mathbb{D} having zero hyperbolic step. We study holomorphic self-maps of 𝔻 \mathbb{D} commuting with φ \varphi . In particular, we answer a question from Gentili and Vlacci (1994) by proving that ψ ∈ 𝖧𝗈𝗅 ⁡ ( 𝔻 , 𝔻 ) \psi\in\mathsf{Hol(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})} commutes with φ \varphi if and only if the two self-maps have the same Denjoy – Wolff point and ψ \psi is a pseudo-iterate of φ \varphi in the sense of Cowen. Moreover, we show that the centralizer of φ \varphi , i.e. the semigroup 𝒵 ∀ ​ ( φ ) := { ψ : ψ ∘ φ = φ ∘ ψ } \mathcal{Z}_{\forall}(\varphi):=\{\psi:\psi\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\psi\} is commutative. We also prove that if φ \varphi is univalent, then all elements of 𝒵 ∀ ​ ( φ ) \mathcal{Z}_{\forall}(\varphi) are univalent as well, and if φ \varphi is not univalent, then the identity map is an isolated point of 𝒵 ∀ ​ ( φ ) \mathcal{Z}_{\forall}(\varphi) . The main tool is the machinery of simultaneous linearization , which we develop using holomorphic models for iteration of non-elliptic self-maps originating in works of Cowen and Pommerenke.



1.
Introduction

One of the main tools in the study of holomorphic dynamical systems is
linearization
, i.e.,
semiconjugation to a linear map. This paper is a part of a project, which we continue in
[
17
]
, aimed at analyzing and developing the relationship of what is commonly known in Dynamics as

simultaneous

linearization
with the study of commuting holomorphic self-maps of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}:={\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|<1\}}
.

In his seminal papers
[
21
,
22
]
Cowen introduced what is now known as
holomorphic
(semi-) models
for the iteration of holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
and used it to study commuting holomorphic self-maps. Obviously, any two iterates of the same self-map commute. Cowen’s main result in
[
22
]
can be regarded as a (weaker form of the) converse statement. Namely, he proved that any two commuting holomorphic self-maps are “generalized iterates” of a third self-map, i.e. elements of its
pseudo-iteration semigroup
; see Section
3
for the definition of this notion.

Much later, Arosio and Bracci
[
3
,
4
]
developed Cowen’s ideas and extended them to holomorphic iteration in higher dimensions and, more generally, on complex manifolds. Noticeably, their abstract point of view appears to be fruitful even for holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
. In this classical case, the notion of a holomorphic semimodel can be interpreted as a way to model the iteration of a given self-map by the iteration of a linear map with the help of a suitable (in general, non-injective) change of variable
{h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}}
; see Section
2.4
for more details.

For a non-elliptic (i.e., having no fixed point in the open disc
\mathbb{D}
) holomorphic self-map
{\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}}
, the linear map modeling
\varphi
is of the form
w\mapsto w+1
. Accordingly, “linearizing” the non-elliptic self-map
\varphi
consists of studying Abel’s functional equation

(1.1)

h\circ\varphi=h+1.

In general, the solution to (
1.1
) is not unique. However, it is possible to single out one particular solution playing a special role, and often referred to as the
Koenigs function
of
\varphi
; see Definition
2.7
.
In terms of the Koenigs function
h_{\varphi}
of
\varphi
, one can characterize elements of Cowen’s pseudo-iteration semigroup of
\varphi
as those holomorphic self-maps
{\psi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}}
for which

(1.2)

h_{\varphi}\circ\psi=h_{\varphi}+c

holds with a suitable constant
{c\in\mathbb{C}}
, which we will refer to as the
simultaneous linearization coefficient
and which will be denoted by
c_{\varphi,\psi}
.

From the algebraic point of view, the classes
{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
and
{\sf Uni}(\mathbb{D})
consisting all holomorphic and all univalent (i.e. holomorphic and injective) self-maps
{\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}}
, respectively, are non-abelian semigroups w.r.t. composition. Therefore, the notion of centralizer naturally comes into play.

Definition 1.1
.

Given a holomorphic self-map of the unit disc
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})

\varphi

\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle\forall}(\varphi):=\{\psi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D}):\,\psi\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\psi\}.

When

\varphi\in{\sf Uni}(\mathbb{D})

\mathcal{Z}(\varphi):=\{\psi\in{\sf Uni}(\mathbb{D}):\,\psi\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\psi\}=\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle\forall}(\varphi)\cap{\sf Uni}(\mathbb{D}).

2.2.
Holomorphic self-maps of the unit disc

The study of the dynamics of a generic holomorphic self-map
\varphi
of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, different from the identity map, is a classical and well-established branch of Complex Analysis.

The central result in the area is the Denjoy – Wolff Theorem, which states that if
\varphi
is different from an elliptic automorphism (i.e. not an automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
possessing a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
), then the sequence of the iterates
(\varphi^{\circ n})
converges locally uniformly in
\mathbb{D}
to a certain point
{\tau\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}}
. This point is called the
Denjoy – Wolff point
of
\varphi
. Moreover, if
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, it is the unique boundary fixed point at which the angular derivative
\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)
is finite and belongs to
(0,1]
.

According to the position of the Denjoy – Wolff point
\tau
and to the value of the
multiplier

\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)
, holomorphic self-maps
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
different from elliptic automorphisms are divided into three categories. Namely,
\varphi
is called:

(a)

elliptic
if
\tau\in\mathbb{D}
,

(b)

hyperbolic
if
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and
\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)<1
, and

(c)

parabolic
if
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)=1
.

All elliptic automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
, along with the identity mapping
{\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}
, are conventionally included in the category (a) of elliptic self-maps.
Similarly, for an elliptic automorphism different from
{\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}
, its Denjoy – Wolff point is defined to be its unique fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
.

Parabolic self-maps can have very different dynamical properties depending on the so-called
hyperbolic step
.
Let
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
be non-elliptic. Thanks to the Schwarz – Pick Lemma, for the orbit
\big(z_{n}\big):=\big(\varphi^{\circ n}(z_{0})\big)
of any point
{z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}}
, there exists a finite limit
q(z_{0}):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\rho_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{n},z_{n+1})
.
It is known, see e.g.
[
1
, Corollary 4.6.9]
, that either
q(z_{0})>0
for all
{z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}}\penalty\
, or
q\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{D}
. The self-map
\varphi
is said to be of
positive
or of
zero hyperbolic step
depending on whether the former or the latter alternative occurs.
If
\varphi
is
hyperbolic, then it is always of positive hyperbolic step. However, there exist parabolic self-maps of zero as well as of positive hyperbolic step.

2.3.
Commuting holomorphic self-maps

It is clear that if two holomorphic self-maps
\varphi,\psi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})\setminus\{{\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}\}
commute, i.e.
{\varphi\circ\psi}={\psi\circ\varphi}
, and if one of them is elliptic, then the other is also elliptic and they share the Denjoy – Wolff point. The situation is not so evident when we consider non-elliptic self-maps. In 1973, Behan
[
7
]
, see also
[
1
, Section 4.10]

\varphi
and
\psi
are commuting non-elliptic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
and
\varphi
is not a hyperbolic automorphism, then
\varphi
and
\psi
share the Denjoy – Wolff point.

Later, Cowen
[
22
, Corollary 4.1]
proved that if
\varphi
and
\psi
are two non-elliptic commuting holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
and if
\varphi
is hyperbolic, then
\psi
is also hyperbolic (and thus, if
\varphi
is parabolic, then
\psi
is parabolic)
3
3

3

See also
[
4
, Theorem 1.3]
for a generalization of this result to holomorphic self-maps of the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
. In the special case when
\varphi
is a hyperbolic automorphism, by an old result of Heins
[
26

,
\psi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})\setminus\{{\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}\}
commutes with

if and only if
\psi
is a hyperbolic automorphism itself with the same fixed points as
\varphi

It is worth mentioning that parabolic self-maps of positive hyperbolic step can commute with parabolic self-maps of zero hyperbolic step. Moreover, in contrast to the hyperbolic case, the fact that one of them is an automorphism would not imply that the other must be also an automorphism. To see this, one can consider the following example:

2.4.
Holomorphic models for holomorphic self-maps

An indispensable role in our study is played by the concept of a holomorphic model, which goes back to Pommerenke

Definition 2.1

Let

In other words, a

Definition 2.2

A

(HM1)

(HM2)

S\,=\,\bigcup_{n\geqslant 0}\big(\alpha^{\circ n}\big)^{-1}(h(\mathbb{D}))
.

A holomorphic
model
of
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
is a semimodel
\mathcal{M}:=(S,h,\alpha)
for which

(HM3)

there exits a
\varphi
-absorbing domain

in which

is injective.

The Riemann surface

is called the

, and the map

of the holomorphic model

Given a holomorphic model

of
\varphi

\varphi

in the following sense.

Lemma 2.3

(
[

Assume that
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})

If

{\beta\circ\alpha}={\alpha_{1}\circ\beta}

Every

Theorem 2.4

(
[
1

Every

In the next result we summarize what we need about the models for self-maps of the unit disc.
The type of a self-map is reflected in, and actually can be fully determined from, the kind of holomorphic model

\mathchoice{\,\widetilde{\!K\!}\,}{\,\widetilde{\!K\!}\,}{\raise 1.07639pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{K}\rule{0.0pt}{1.62747pt}}}$}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{K}\rule{0.0pt}{2.32495pt}}}}+n\subset h_{\varphi}(\mathbb{D})
for all

. Therefore,
{K+t\subset h_{\varphi}(\mathbb{D})}
for any real number
{t>N}
. Roughly speaking, this means that asymptotically every Koenigs domain behaves like the Koenigs domain of a non-elliptic semigroup. This fact has been used several times in the literature, see e.g.
[
13
, Lemma 7.6]
and
[
27
, Lemma 2.2]
.
A bit more generally, if
{V\subset\mathbb{D}}
is any
\varphi
-absorbing open set, then repeating the same argument with
\mathbb{D}
replaced by
V
, we see that for each compact
K\subset S
there exists

such that
{K+t\subset h_{\varphi}(V)}
for all
{t\in[t_{0},+\infty)}
.

The following result proved in
[
20
]
plays an important role in our study. For a given parabolic self-map
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
, it provides a construction of a family of invariant sets with certain useful properties. In particular, if
\varphi
is of zero hyperbolic step then these sets are
\varphi
-absorbing. Recall that a domain
U\subset\mathbb{D}
is said to be isogonal at a point
{\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
if for any Stolz angle
{S\subset\mathbb{D}}
with vertex at
\sigma
there exists
{\varepsilon>0}
such that
{\{z\in S:|z-\sigma|<\varepsilon\}\subset U}
.

Theorem 2.9

(
[
20
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
be
parabolic with Denjoy – Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and Koenigs function
h_{\varphi}
.
Given
0<r<1,
write

V_{\varphi}(r)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\rho^{*}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,\varphi(z))<r\right\}.

Then,

(1)

V_{\varphi}(r)
is
\varphi
-invariant, that is,
\varphi(V_{\varphi}(r))\subset V_{\varphi}(r)
for all
0<r<1.

(2)

V_{\varphi}(r)
is a simply connected domain.

(3)

V_{\varphi}(r)
is isogonal at
\tau.

(4)

If
0<r\leqslant\frac{1}{3},
then both
\varphi
and its Koenigs map
h_{\varphi}
are univalent in
V_{\varphi}(r).

(5)

If
\varphi
is of zero hyperbolic step and
0<r\leqslant\frac{1}{3}
, then
V_{\varphi}(r)
is
\varphi
-absorbing.

The notation
V_{\varphi}(r)
introduced in the above theorem will be used frequently throughout the paper. In addition, some elementary properties of these sets will be used and, for the sake of clearness, we collect them in the next lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
be parabolic. The following statements hold.

(1)

If
\psi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle\forall}(\varphi)
, then
V_{\varphi}(r)
is
\psi
-invariant for all
{r\in(0,1)}
.

(2)

Take

such that
\rho^{*}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,\psi(z))<a
and
\rho^{*}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)<b
. Then
\rho^{*}_{\mathbb{D}}(w,\psi(w))<a+2b
.

(3)

If
z\in V_{\psi}(1/9)
and
\rho^{*}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)<1/9
, then
w\in V_{\psi}(1/3)
. In other words,
\mathrm{B}_{h}(z,1/9)\subset V_{\psi}(1/3)
whenever
{z\in V_{\psi}(1/9)}
.

(4)

If
{z\in V_{\psi}(1/9)}
, then
\mathrm{B}_{h}\big(\psi^{\circ n}(z),1/9\big)\subset V_{\psi}(1/3)
for any
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

(5)

If
\psi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle\forall}(\varphi)
and
{z\in V_{\psi}(1/9)}
, then
\mathrm{B}_{h}\big(\varphi^{\circ n}(z),1/9\big)\subset V_{\psi}(1/3)
for any
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

If
\psi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle\forall}(\varphi)
and
z\in V_{\varphi}(r)
, then

\rho^{*}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\psi(z),\varphi\circ\psi(z)\big)=\rho^{*}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\psi(z),\psi\circ\varphi(z)\big)\leqslant\rho^{*}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z,\varphi(z)\big)<r,

so that
\psi(z)\in V_{\varphi}(r)
, where we have used the Schwarz – Pick Lemma. Thus
(1)
holds. Further,
(2)
is a consequence of the triangular inequality and again the Schwarz – Pick Lemma, and
(3)
follows from
(2)
.

By Theorem
2.9
(1)
,
{\psi^{\circ n}(z)\in V_{\psi}(1/9)}
for any
{z\in V_{\psi}(1/9)}
and any
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore,
(3)
implies
(4)
.
Similarly, by
(1)
with
\varphi
and
\psi
interchanged,
{\varphi^{\circ n}(z)\in V_{\psi}(1/9)}
for any
{z\in V_{\psi}(1/9)}
and any
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
; as a result,
(5)
follows from
(3)
.
∎

univalent on every hyperbolic disc of center

and

for all

3.
Simultaneous linearization and pseudo-iteration semigroup

In
[
22
]
Cowen introduced the notion of pseudo-iteration semigroup playing the central role in his study of commuting holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
. For a non-elliptic self-map
{\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})}
, this notion can be defined as follows. Using conformal mappings one can pass from the canonical holomorphic model for
\varphi
to another holomorphic model
{(S,f,\alpha)}
for
\varphi
such that the base space
S
is either
\mathbb{D}
or
\mathbb{C}
. A self-map
{\psi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})}
is said to belongs to the
pseudo-iteration semigroup
of
\varphi
if there exists a Möbius transformation
\beta
of the Riemann sphere
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\alpha\circ\beta=\beta\circ\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad f\circ\psi=\beta\circ f.

The second equality implies, of course, that
\beta(S)\subset S
. Furthermore, using the fact that commuting Möbius transformations have the same fixed points (except for the case when both are involutions) see e.g.
[
1
, Corollary 1.6.21]
, and passing back to the canonical holomorphic model, it is not difficult to see that
{\psi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})}
belongs to the pseudo-iteration semigroup of a non-elliptic self-map
{\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})}
if and only if

h_{\varphi}\circ\psi=h_{\varphi}+c\quad\text{for some constant\penalty\ $\penalty\ c\in\mathbb{C}$.}

Since the Koenigs function
h_{\varphi}
of
\varphi
, by the very definition, satisfies Abel’s equation
{h_{\varphi}\circ\varphi=h_{\varphi}+1}
, this explains the relation of the pseudo-iteration semigroup to the notion of simultaneous linearization, see Definition
3.1
below. As we have seen in
[
16
]
for univalent self-maps and as it will become evident in
[
17
]
, the simultaneous linearization turns out to be a more convenient tool than the general concept of pseudo-iteration when we restrict the study of commuting self-maps to the (most interesting and complicated) non-elliptic case.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
be two self-maps at least one of which is non-elliptic. We say that
\varphi
and
\psi
admit
simultaneous linearization
if there exist constants
{c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}}
with
{|c_{1}|^{2}+|c_{2}|^{2}\neq 0}
and a holomorphic function
{h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}}
satisfying

(3.1)

h\circ\varphi=h+c_{1},\qquad h\circ\psi=h+c_{2}.

Remark 3.2
.

It is worth mentioning that simultaneous linearization, as a general concept, have been studied in connection with commutativity in other contexts, e.g. for torus diffeomorphisms and holomorphic germs; see
[
14
,
31
,
32
]
and references therein.

The main result of this preparatory section, Proposition
3.3
below, shows that the simultaneous linearization, with a slight additional assumption imposed on the function
h
, provides a sufficient condition for commutativity.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
be two parabolic self-maps having the same Denjoy – Wolff point
\tau
.
If there exists
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and a holomorphic function
h:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
which is univalent in some domain
V\subset\mathbb{D}
isogonal at
\tau
and such that

h\circ\varphi=h+c_{1},\qquad h\circ\psi=h+c_{2}.

Then
\varphi\circ\psi=\psi\circ\varphi
.

In particular, if
{h_{\varphi}\circ\psi}={h_{\varphi}+c}
for some constant
{c\in\mathbb{C}}
, then
{\psi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle\forall}(\varphi)}
.

The hypothesis that
\varphi
and
\psi
have the same DW-point
\tau
cannot be eliminated from the above proposition, see Example
4.7
in the next section.

A related result can be found in
[
22
, Proposition 2.2]
. However, a principal difference is that Proposition
3.3
gives a sufficient condition for
\varphi
and
\psi
to commute
without
assuming that
\psi
is a pseudo-iteration of
\varphi
. Accordingly, the simultaneous linearization
a priori
does not have to be given by the Koenigs function
h_{\varphi}
of
\varphi
. It does so (and the condition becomes also necessary) when
\varphi
is of zero hyperbolic step; see Theorem
4.5
(3)
in the next section. In contrast, for the case of positive hyperbolic step it will be shown in
[
17
]
, see also
[
16
]
, that

.

For the proof of Proposition

Lemma 3.4

Let

and

isogonal at
\sigma

and all

Denote

4.
Characterization of commutativity via simultaneous linearization

In his seminal paper
[
22
]
, Cowen proved that if two self-maps
{\varphi,\psi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})}
commute, then they belong to the pseudo-iteration semigroup of
{\varphi\circ\psi}
. Moreover, if additionally
\varphi
is elliptic or hyperbolic then as he showed,
\psi
belongs also to the pseudo-iteration semigroup of
\varphi
. Conversely, if
\psi
belongs to the pseudo-iteration semigroup of an elliptic or hyperbolic self-map
{\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})}
and if it has the same Denjoy – Wolff point as
\varphi
, then
\psi
and
\varphi
commute, see e.g. Gentili and Vlacci
[
24
, Theorem 2.7]
and Bisi and Gentili
[

, Theorem 6]
; see also Vlacci
[

]
.
Taking into account the relationship of the pseudo-iteration semigroup of a non-elliptic self-map to simultaneous linearization, discussed in the previous section, the following theorem can be seen as an analogue of these results for parabolic self-maps of zero hyperbolic step.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
be parabolic of zero hyperbolic step and let
\psi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
be different from
{\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}
. Likewise, let

be the Koenigs function of

(i)

\psi\in\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle\forall}(\varphi)

(ii)

\varphi
and
\psi
have the same Denjoy – Wolff point and

\,h_{\varphi}\circ\psi=h_{\varphi}+c\,
for some constant

.

Implication
(ii)

\Rightarrow

\Rightarrow

(ii)
follows directly from Behan’s Theorem and assertion

of the more technical Theorem

5.1.

Theorem 5.1

(5.1)

\angle\lim_{z\to\tau}\frac{\psi(z)-z}{\varphi(z)-z}\penalty\ =\penalty\ c_{\varphi,\psi}.

(B)

Suppose that

(5.2)

h\circ\varphi=h+c_{1},\qquad h\circ\psi=h+c_{2},

for some holomorphic function

and some

The hypothesis that

Recall that by Theorem

Proof of
(A)
.

By (

\angle\lim_{z\to\tau}\dfrac{c_{\varphi,\psi}}{h^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z)(\psi(z)-z)}=\angle\lim_{z\to\tau}\dfrac{h_{\varphi}(\psi(z))-h_{\varphi}(z)}{h^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z)(\psi(z)-z)}=1,

Combining (

As mentioned above, we may assume that

. By the hypothesis,

for some holomorphic function

and some

{c_{1}\neq 0}

Note that since

In particular, if

{c_{\varphi,\psi}=m/n}
for some

Suppose now that

In particular, it follows that the derivative of

6.
Appendix: the hyperbolic step and derivatives of the iterates

Below we give a necessary and sufficient condition for a non-elliptic self-map to be of zero hyperbolic step, which we used in the proof of Theorem
5.3
(C)
and which would be probably also useful when one tries to apply the results established in the previous sections.
On the one hand, this condition does not seem to be unexpected for specialists, but on the other hand, we could not find it in the literature. By this reason, we provide a detailed proof.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D})
be a non-elliptic self-map. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\varphi
is parabolic of zero hyperbolic step;

(2)

there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
with
(\varphi^{\circ n})^{\prime}(z_{0})\neq 0
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

(6.1)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{(\varphi^{\circ n})^{\prime}(z_{0})}{1-|\varphi^{\circ n}(z_{0})|^{2}}=0;

(3)

equality (
6.1
) holds for all
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
.

Remark 6.2
.

It is worth mentioning, even though it is just a trivial remark, that condition (
6.1
) can be stated in terms of the so-called
hyperbolic distortion

\mathrm{D}_{h}\varphi(z):=\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(\varphi(z))|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|/\lambda(z)
, where
\lambda(z):=1/(1-|z|^{2})
is the density of the hyperbolic metric in
\mathbb{D}
. Clearly, (
6.1
) is equivalent to
{\mathrm{D}_{h}\varphi^{\circ n}(z_{0})\to 0}
as
{n\to+\infty}
. Properties of the hyperbolic distortion seem to be deeply linked to the dynamical behaviour of a holomorphic self-map, see e.g.
[
25
]
.

Remark 6.3
.

An analogue of Proposition
6.1
is trivially true for elliptic self-maps. More precisely, if

{\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})\setminus{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})}
is elliptic, then it is automatically of zero hyperbolic step, and (
6.1
) holds for all
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
because
\varphi^{\circ n}\to{\rm const}\in\mathbb{D}
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{D}
as
{n\to+\infty}
. In contrast, if
{\varphi\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})}
, then
{\mathrm{D}_{h}\varphi^{\circ n}(z)=1}
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and as a result the limit in (
6.1
) is strictly positive for all
{z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

If
\varphi\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, then the statement of the proposition holds trivially because none of the conditions
(1)
–
(3)
is satisfied for automorphisms.

So for the rest of the proof we suppose that
{\varphi\not\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})}
.
Clearly,
(3)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(2)
.

Fix an arbitrary point
{z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}}
. If
{(\varphi^{\circ n})^{\prime}(z_{0})=0}
for some
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, then (
6.1
) holds trivially. So we assume that
{(\varphi^{\circ n})^{\prime}(z_{0})\neq 0}
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. In order to make the proof of the implications
(1)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(3)
and
(2)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(1)
less technical, we are going to replace the autonomous dynamical system in
\mathbb{D}
induced by the iterates of
\varphi
with a non-autonomous dynamical system in
\mathbb{D}
, with the advantage that to the orbit
\big(z_{n}:=\varphi^{\circ n}(z_{0})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
there would correspond the
fixed point
of the new dynamical system at the origin. To this end, we employ the time-dependent change of variables in
\mathbb{D}
given by the automorphisms
L_{n}(\zeta):=\frac{z_{n}-\zeta}{1-\overline{z_{n}}\zeta}
,
{\zeta\in\mathbb{D}}
, and define
g_{n}:={L_{n}^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ L_{n-1}}
,
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
It is not difficult to see that
g_{n}
’s are holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
with a fixed point at the origin and that

(6.2)

\varphi^{\circ n}={L_{n}\circ g_{n}\circ g_{n-1}\circ\dots\circ g_{1}\circ L_{0}^{-1}},\quad\text{for all $\penalty\ {n\in\mathbb{N}}$.}

Now we consider the sequence
{(\zeta_{n})\subset\mathbb{D}}
defined recursively by

\zeta_{n}:=g_{n}(\zeta_{n-1})\quad\text{for all $\penalty\ {n\in\mathbb{N}}$},\qquad\zeta_{0}:=L_{0}^{-1}(z_{1}).

Using (
6.2
) we see that
L_{n}(\zeta_{n})=z_{n+1}
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
. Recalling that
\varphi
has no fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
by the hypothesis, we have
{L_{n}(\zeta_{n})=z_{n+1}\neq z_{n}=L_{n}(0)}
and therefore,
{\zeta_{n}\neq 0}
for any
{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
.

By the invariance of the hyperbolic distance
\rho_{\mathbb{D}}
under automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
, we have

(6.3)

\rho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi^{\circ(n+1)}(z_{0}),\varphi^{\circ n}(z_{0})\big)=\rho_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{n+1},z_{n})=\rho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(L_{n}(\zeta_{n}),\,L_{n}(0)\big)=\rho_{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta_{n},0).

Therefore,
\varphi
is parabolic of zero hyperbolic step if and only if the sequence
(\zeta_{n})
converges to
0
.

Given
m>n
, consider the function
G_{n,m}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
defined by

G_{n,m}:={g_{m}\circ g_{m-1}\circ\dots\circ g_{n+1}}={L_{m}^{-1}\circ\varphi^{\circ(m-n)}\circ L_{n}}.

Notice that

(6.4)

G_{0,n}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad G^{\prime}_{0,n}(0)=\frac{(\varphi^{\circ n})^{\prime}(z_{0})}{1-|\varphi^{\circ n}(z_{0})|^{2}}(1-|z_{0}|^{2})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Fixed
m>n
, by the Schwarz Lemma we have

(6.5)

|G^{\prime}_{0,m}(0)|=|G^{\prime}_{0,n}(0)G^{\prime}_{n,m}(0)|\leqslant|G^{\prime}_{n,m}(0)|.

Moreover, since
G_{n,m}\not\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, we can apply
the Schwarz – Pick’s Lemma to the function

F_{n,m}(\zeta):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}G_{n,m}(\zeta)/\zeta,&\quad\textrm{if }\ \zeta\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},\\[1.72218pt]
G_{n,m}^{\prime}(0),&\quad\textrm{if }\ \zeta=0,\end{array}\right.

to obtain

(6.6)

\begin{split}\left|G_{n,m}^{\prime}(0)-\frac{\zeta_{m}}{\zeta_{n}}\right|&=\big|F_{n,m}(0)-F_{n,m}(\zeta_{n})\big|\\
&=\big|1-\overline{F_{n,m}(0)}F_{n,m}(\zeta_{n})\big|\,\rho^{*}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(F_{n,m}(0),\,F_{n,m}(\zeta_{n})\big)\\[2.36806pt]
&\leqslant\big|1-\overline{F_{n,m}(0)}F_{n,m}(\zeta_{n})\big|\,|\zeta_{n}|\penalty\ \leqslant\penalty\ 2|\zeta_{n}|.\end{split}

It follows that

(6.7)

|G^{\prime}_{0,m}(0)|\leqslant|G^{\prime}_{n,m}(0)|\leqslant\left|\frac{\zeta_{m}}{\zeta_{n}}\right|+2|\zeta_{n}|.

Assume now that
\varphi
is parabolic of zero hyperbolic step. Then
(\zeta_{n})
converges to
0
. Fix an
{\varepsilon>0}
and choose some
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
|\zeta_{n}|<\varepsilon/3
. Then by (
6.7
), we have
{|G^{\prime}_{0,m}(0)|<\varepsilon}
for all
{m\in\mathbb{N}}
large enough. Since
{\varepsilon>0}
here is arbitrary, it follows that
{|G^{\prime}_{0,m}(0)|\to 0}
as
{m\to+\infty}
and hence, in view of (
6.4
), equality (
6.1
) holds. This proves the implication
(1)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(3)
.

Conversely, assume that there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
satisfying
{(\varphi^{\circ n})^{\prime}(z_{0})\neq 0}
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and such that (
6.1
) holds.
Then
{G^{\prime}_{0,m}(0)\to 0}
as
{m\to+\infty}
.
By (
6.4
),
G^{\prime}_{0,n}(0)\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. With the help of (
6.5
), it follows that for each fixed
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have
{G^{\prime}_{n,m}(0)\to 0}
as
{m\to+\infty}
. Since by the Schwarz Lemma, the sequence
\big(r_{n}:=|\zeta_{n}|\big)
does not increase, it has a limit
\ell\in[0,1)
. We wish to prove that
{\ell=0}
. Let us recall that
{\zeta_{n}\neq 0}
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. According to (
6.6
),

\left|G_{n,m}^{\prime}(0)-\frac{\zeta_{m}}{\zeta_{n}}\right|\leqslant\big|1-\overline{G_{n,m}^{\prime}(0)}F_{n,m}(\zeta_{n})\big|\,r_{n}.

Passing in this inequality to the limit as
{m\to+\infty}
with
n
fixed, we deduce that
{\ell/r_{n}\leqslant r_{n}}
. That is,
{\ell\leqslant r_{n}^{2}}
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Passing now to the limit as
{n\to+\infty}
, we finally get that
\ell^{2}\geqslant\ell\in[0,1)
. Thus
\ell=0
, which according to (
6.3
) means that
\varphi
has zero hyperbolic step. To complete the proof of the implication
(2)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(1)
, it remains to recall that a non-elliptic holomorphic self-map of zero hyperbolic step is necessarily parabolic.
∎

As a corollary of Proposition
6.1
, we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition of zero hyperbolic step for continuous one-parameter semigroups. Recall that
a family
(\phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
of holomorphic self-maps
\phi_{t}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is called a
one-parameter semigroup
if it verifies the following two algebraic properties:

(i)

\phi_{0}={\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}
;

(ii)

\phi_{t+s}=\phi_{t}\circ\phi_{s}
for every
t,s\geqslant 0
.

A one-parameter semigroup
(\phi_{t})
is said to be
continuous
if
{\phi_{t}\to\phi_{0}={\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}}
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{D}
as
{t\to 0^{+}}
. Finally, a one-parameter semigroup
(\phi_{t})
is called
non-trivial
if
{\phi_{t}\neq{\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}}
for at least one value of
{t>0}
.

By a classical result of Berkson and Porta
[
8
]
, see also
[
1
, §5.4]
,
[
12
, §10.1]
or
[
11
]
, every continuous one-parameter semigroup
(\phi_{t})
is differentiable in
t
and admits an
infinitesimal generator
, i.e. a holomorphic function
{G:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}}
such that

(6.8)

\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}}\nolimits\phi_{t}(z)}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}}\nolimits t}=G\big(\phi_{t}(z)\big)\quad\text{ for all $\penalty\ {t\geqslant 0}\penalty\ $ and all $\penalty\ {z\in\mathbb{D}}$.}

Finally, it is known that all elements of a non-trivial continuous one-parameter semigroup different from
{\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}
have the same Denjoy – Wolff point; see e.g.
[
1
, §5.5]
or
[
12
, Theorem 8.3.1]
. Therefore, it makes sense to talk about
elliptic
and
non-elliptic
continuous one-parameter semigroups, depending on whether the common Denjoy – Wolff point is contained in
\mathbb{D}
or in
\partial\mathbb{D}
. In a similar way, see e.g.
[
12
, §8.3]
, non-elliptic continuous one-parameter semigroups can be categorised into three types: hyperbolic semigroups, parabolic semigroups of positive hyperbolic step, and parabolic semigroups of zero hyperbolic step.

The necessary and sufficient condition for a non-elliptic one-parameter semigroup to have zero hyperbolic step, which we are going now to deduce from Proposition
6.1
, has a very simple geometric meaning: the hyperbolic norm of the velocity vector
{\lambda(\phi_{t}(z))\big|\tfrac{\mathop{\mathrm{d}}\nolimits}{\mathop{\mathrm{d}}\nolimits t}\phi_{t}(z)\big|}
should tend to
0
as
{t\to+\infty}
. Here is the precise statement:

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
(\phi_{t})
be a non-elliptic continuous one-parameter semigroup in the unit disc and let
G
be its infinitesimal generator. Then the semigroup
(\phi_{t})
is parabolic of zero hyperbolic step if and only if

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{G(\phi_{t}(z))}{1-|\phi_{t}(z)|^{2}}=0

for some — and hence for any —
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

Since the function
\phi_{1}
is univalent, see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 8.1.17]
, Proposition
6.1
implies that the semigroup
(\phi_{t})
is parabolic of zero hyperbolic step
if and only if

(6.9)

\lim_{\mathbb{N}\ni n\to+\infty}\frac{\phi_{n}^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\phi_{n}(z)|^{2}}=0

for some (and hence for all)
{z\in\mathbb{D}}
. Using the Schwarz – Pick Lemma, it is not difficult to see that for any
{z\in\mathbb{D}}
, the function

F_{z}(t):=\frac{\phi_{t}^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\phi_{t}(z)|^{2}},\quad t\in[0,+\infty),

is non-increasing. Therefore, (
6.9
) is equivalent to
{F_{z}(t)\to 0}
, as
{t\to+\infty}
. To complete the proof, it only remains to notice that
\phi_{t}^{\prime}(z)={G(\phi_{t}(z))/G(z)}
for all
{z\in\mathbb{D}}
and all
{t\geqslant 0}
. This equality can be easily deduced by combining (
6.8
) with the PDE
{\partial\phi_{t}(z)/\partial t=G(z)\phi^{\prime}_{t}(z)}
; see e.g.
[
12
, Proposition 10.1.8]
.
∎

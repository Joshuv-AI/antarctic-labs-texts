Hoffman colorability of (strongly) regular graphs
Aida Abiad, Bart De Bruyn, Thijs van Veluw

Abstract
Hoffman’s bound is a well-known eigenvalue bound on the chromatic number of a graph. By interpreting this bound as a parameter, we show multiple applications of colorings attaining the bound (Hoffman colorings) for several notions of graph regularity: regular, (co-)edge-regular, and strongly regular. For strongly regular graphs, we prove that Hoffman colorability implies pseudo-geometricity, and we strengthen Haemers’ finiteness result on strongly regular graphs with a bounded chromatic number by considering the Hoffman bound instead of the chromatic number. Furthermore, by using Hoffman colorings we show that a sufficient condition for non-unique vector colorability shown by Godsil, Roberson, Rooney, Šámal and Varvitsiotis [European J. Combin. 79, 2019] can be relaxed in the setting of strongly regular graphs. Lastly, using Hoffman colorings we derive several new characterizations of the mentioned graph regularity notions. Keywords: chromatic number, adjacency matrix, eigenvalues, Hoffman coloring, strongly regular graph

Introduction

Let
G
be a simple graph. Write
\lambda_{\max}
,
\lambda_{\min}
for the largest and the smallest eigenvalue respectively of the adjacency matrix of
G
. Then the
Hoffman bound

[
22
]
states that the chromatic number of
G
is bounded below by

h(G)\coloneqq 1-\frac{\lambda_{\max}(G)}{\lambda_{\min}(G)},

(1)

which we will refer to as the
Hoffman number
. A graph for which the Hoffman bound is tight is called
Hoffman colorable
, and every optimal coloring of such a graph is called a
Hoffman coloring
. Bipartite graphs and regular complete multipartite graphs are easily seen to be Hoffman colorable, and we will refer to their optimal colorings as
trivial Hoffman colorings
.

In
[
1
,
2
]
, Hoffman colorings of general graphs (also including irregular graphs) were studied, and several structural requirements on Hoffman colorings were obtained. For strongly regular graphs, Hoffman colorings are dual to the notion of a
spread
, which was investigated in
[
21
]
, where spreads (and in turn Hoffman colorings) of strongly regular graphs are shown to correspond to fans in partial geometries, imprimitive 3-class association schemes, and systems of linked symmetric designs. In
[
6
,
10
]
, equality in an analogous Hoffman-type bound but for the normalized Laplacian matrix was studied.

In this paper, we further investigate Hoffman colorability for several graph regularity notions. In particular, we focus on regular, co-edge-regular and strongly regular graphs.

First, we show that Hoffman colorable strongly regular graphs and their complements are pseudo-geometric (?THM?
). Moreover, we show that there are only finitely many primitive strongly regular graphs with a bounded Hoffman number (?THM?
), strengthening the known result
[
19
]
that there are only finitely many primitive strongly regular graphs with a bounded chromatic number. We make this explicit for primitive strongly regular graphs with Hoffman number at most 3 by completely determining all six such graphs (?THM?
).

Next we investigate the interplay between unique vector colorability (which was introduced in
[
27
]
and studied with respect to cores in
[
14
]
) and Hoffman colorability. In particular, we show that non-trivially Hoffman colorable connected 2-walk-regular graphs (including primitive strongly regular graphs) are not uniquely vector colorable (?THM?
). This improves
[
14
, Theorem 3.12]
for strongly regular graphs by providing a more general sufficient condition for non-unique vector colorability that also includes the graphs of type A of the type system of
[
28
]
, like the Shrikhande and Schläfli graphs.

Furthermore, with the Hoffman number in mind, we expand on the results and ideas of
[
5
]
, where average parameters based on strongly regular graphs are used to introduce an upper bound on the least eigenvalue of a regular graph
[
5
, Theorem 2.14(a)]
. In particular, we show that for regular graphs it holds that

h(G)h(\overline{G})\leq n,

(2)

and that strongly regular graphs are precisely those regular graphs attaining equality (?THM?
).
The inequality (2) is related to the Lovász
\vartheta
-number (Remark 5.5). We also obtain a lower bound on the chromatic number of a co-edge-regular graph that improves Hoffman’s bound for this graph class (?THM?
). As an application of these results to Hoffman colorable regular graphs, it follows that graphs that are co-edge-regular but not strongly regular cannot be Hoffman colorable, and that strictly Neumaier graphs are not Hoffman colorable (?THM?
).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall some definitions and results that we will use throughout the paper. In Section
3
, we introduce the geometric parameters for strongly regular graphs and derive the aforementioned results. In Section
4
we investigate the interplay between Hoffman colorability and unique vector colorability. Lastly, in Section
5
, we cover the average parameters from
[
5
]
and show the aforementioned results.

(Co-)edge-regular and strongly regular graphs

A regular graph is
a
-edge-regular
if every edge is contained in a constant number
a
of triangles. Equivalently, a regular graph is edge-regular if every pair of adjacent vertices shares a constant number
a
of common neighbors. A graph is
c
-co-edge-regular
if every pair of non-adjacent vertices shares a common number
c
of common neighbors. The complement of an
a
-edge-regular graph is
(n-2k+a)
-co-edge-regular, and the complement of a
c
-co-edge-regular graph is
(n-2k+c-2)
-edge-regular.

A
k
-regular graph
G
on
n
vertices is
(n,k,a,c)
-strongly regular
if it is
a
-edge-regular and
c
-co-edge-regular. The complement
\overline{G}
of such a strongly regular graph
G
is
(n,n-k-1,n-2k+c-2,n-2k+a)
-strongly regular. A strongly regular graph is
primitive
if both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected, and
imprimitive
otherwise. The only imprimitive strongly regular graphs are disjoint unions of equal sized complete graphs, and regular complete multipartite graphs. By a double counting argument, it holds that

c(n-k-1)=k(k-a-1).

(3)

Connected regular graphs are strongly regular if and only if they have at most three distinct eigenvalues. The largest eigenvalue is simple and equal to the valency
k
, and, for non-complete strongly regular graphs, we write
\tau<\theta
for the other two eigenvalues. In this case, we have

(x-\theta)(x-\tau)=x^{2}+(c-a)x+(c-k).

(4)

For primitive strongly regular graphs we have
\tau<-1
and
\theta>0
. If
G
has eigenvalues
\tau<\theta<k
, then
\overline{G}
has eigenvalues
-1-\theta<-1-\tau<n-k-1
.

A
conference graph
is a strongly regular graph that has the same parameters (and hence same spectrum) as its complement. If a strongly graph is not integral (in other words, if
\tau
and
\theta
are not integral), then it necessarily is a conference graph.

For more details about strongly regular graphs, see
[
8
]
or
[

]
.

(Co)clique and chromatic number bounds: Delsarte/ratio bound and Hoffman bound

A
clique
is a subset
C
of the vertices of a graph such that every two distinct vertices in
C
are adjacent. A
coclique
(also called

or

C
of the vertices such that no two vertices in
C
are adjacent. The

\omega(G)
and

of a graph

are the sizes of the largest clique and coclique, respectively. We call a (co)clique
C

-regular
if every vertex that is not in

If

|C|\leq\frac{n\lambda_{\min}(G)}{\lambda_{\min}(G)-k},

(5)

with equality if and only if

(-\lambda_{\min}(G))
-regular. In the case of strongly regular graphs, this was already known
[
11
]
so in the context of strongly regular graphs the bound is called the

. A coclique meeting the ratio (Delsarte) bound is called a
Hoffman (Delsarte) coclique
. A clique that is a Hoffman (Delsarte) coclique in the complement graph is called a
Hoffman (Delsarte) clique
.

Since a coloring of a graph is a partition of the vertices into
\chi
cocliques, we have

\chi(G)\geq\frac{n}{\alpha(G)}.

(6)

For regular graphs, the Hoffman bound (
1
) can be deduced using (
6
) and the ratio bound (
5
). This implies the following result.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
7
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

If
G
is regular, then every color class of a Hoffman coloring is a Hoffman coclique. In other words, the color classes have equal size and every vertex is adjacent to exactly
-\lambda_{\min}(G)
vertices of every color other than its own.

A
spread
in a strongly regular graph is a partition of the vertices into Delsarte cliques. So for strongly regular graphs, ?THM?
implies that a Hoffman coloring is equivalent to a spread in the complement, and this was the starting point of
[
21
]
. Moreover, for strongly regular graphs a straightforward computation gives

h(G)h(\overline{G})=n.

(7)

In ?THM?
we will show a derivation of (
7
) in a more general setting. Now the Delsarte bound says that cliques have size at most the Hoffman number. In other words, for strongly regular graphs we have

\omega(G)\leq h(G)\leq\chi(G).

(Strict) vector colorings and the Lovász
\vartheta
-number

Vector colorings were first introduced in
[
23
]
. Given a graph
G
and a real number
t\geq 2
, a

t
-coloring
is an assignment of unit vectors from a finite-dimensional real vector space to the vertices of
G
, such that for any pair of adjacent vertices the assigned vectors have inner product bounded above by
-1/(t-1)
. A vector coloring is

if for any pair of adjacent vertices, the inner product of the assigned vectors is equal to
-1/(t-1)
. Any graph coloring in the standard sense with
c
colors induces a strict vector
c
-coloring through a regular simplex on
c
vertices (see
[
23
, Lemma 4.1]
). The
vector chromatic number

\chi_{v}
is defined as the least
t
for which the graph admits a vector
t
-coloring. Similarly, the
strict vector chromatic number

\chi_{sv}
is defined as the least
t
for which the graph admits a strict vector
t
-coloring. We now have

\chi_{v}(G)\leq\chi_{sv}(G)\leq\chi(G).

(9)

In
[
24
]
, a graph invariant
\vartheta(G)
was introduced, later named the
Lovász
\vartheta
-number
, as an upper bound for the Shannon capacity. In
[
25
,
30
]
a variation
\vartheta^{\prime}(G)
was introduced, which has later been called the
Schrijver variant of the Lovász
\vartheta
-number
. Vector colorings are known to be related to the Lovász
\vartheta
-number and its Schrijver variant. In particular,
[
23
, Theorem 8.2]
says that
\vartheta(\overline{G})=\chi_{sv}(G)
for all graphs, and furthermore in
[
14
,
15
]
is stated that
\vartheta^{\prime}(\overline{G})=\chi_{v}(G)
. In the following, we will switch between the vector chromatic notation
\chi_{v},\chi_{sv}
and Lovász/Schrijver notation
\vartheta,\vartheta^{\prime}
, according to the context.

From
[
24
, Theorem 6]
it follows that
h(G)\leq\chi_{sv}(G)
. In fact, the same argument works for the Schrijver variant of the Lovász
\vartheta
-number, so we get
h(G)\leq\chi_{v}(G)
. Together with (

), we obtain that the (strict) vector chromatic number is sandwiched between the Hoffman number and the standard chromatic number:

h(G)\leq\chi_{v}(G)\leq\chi_{sv}(G)\leq\chi(G).

(10)

3
Strongly regular graphs and geometric parameters

In this section we show two main results: that Hoffman colorable strongly regular graphs and their complements are pseudo-geometric (?THM?
), and that there are only finitely many primitive strongly regular graphs with a bounded Hoffman number (?THM?
). Compared to the known result
[
19
]
that there are only finitely many primitive strongly regular graphs with a given chromatic number, ?THM?
shows that this conclusion holds using a more relaxed condition. We also determine all primitive strongly regular graphs with Hoffman number at most 3 (?THM?
). To do this we study strongly regular graphs using parameters that are based on
(s,t,\alpha)
-partial geometries. In
[
5
,
26
]
, steps were already made in this direction, see ?THM?
. These new “geometric parameters”
(s,t,\alpha)
are completely interchangeable with the standard “combinatorial parameters”
(n,k,a,c)
and the “spectral parameters”
(k,\theta,\tau)
. The relevance of the geometric parameters, and the connection to Hoffman colorability, lies in the fact that the Hoffman number is very easily expressible in the geometric parameters; it is
s+1
(see (
13
)).

We first briefly recall some definitions regarding partial geometries and (pseudo-)geometricity of strongly regular graphs. An
incidence structure
is a triple
(P,L,I)
with a set of
points

P
, a set of
lines

L
, and
I\subseteq P\times L
a relation. If
(p,\ell)\in I
, then we write
pI\ell
and say that
p
and
\ell
are
incident
. Two points are
collinear
if they are incident to a common line. The
collinearity graph
is the graph with the points as vertices, such that adjacency is given by collinearity.

Let
s,t,\alpha
be positive integers. A
partial geometry
with parameters
(s,t,\alpha)
is an incidence structure
(P,L,I)
, with the following requirements:

•

Every pair of points is incident to at most one common line.

•

Every line is incident to exactly
s+1
points, and every point is incident to exactly
t+1
lines.

•

If
p
is not incident to
\ell
, then
p
is collinear to exactly
\alpha
points incident to
\ell
.

It is known
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
that the collinearity graph of a partial geometry is strongly regular with parameters equal to

(n,k,a,c)=\Big((s+1)\frac{st+\alpha}{\alpha},s(t+1),s-1+t(\alpha-1),\alpha(t+1)\Big).

(11)

A graph that is the collinearity graph of a partial geometry is called
geometric
. A strongly regular graph for which there exist positive integers
s,t,\alpha
such that the parameters are given by (
11
) is called
pseudo-geometric
, see
[
8
]
or
[
9
]
. The eigenvalues of a pseudo-geometric strongly regular graph are given by
k=s(t+1)
,
\theta=s-\alpha
,
\tau=-t-1
. Equivalently, we have

(s,t,\alpha)=\Big(-\frac{k}{\tau},-\tau-1,-\frac{k}{\tau}-\theta\Big).

(12)

In order to show our results, we extend (
12
) to all primitive strongly regular graphs, including non-pseudo-geometric graphs: we define the
geometric parameters

(s,t,\alpha)
of a general primitive strongly regular with eigenvalues
k>\theta>\tau
as in (
12
). From (
3
) and (
4
), it follows that the combinatorial parameters of a primitive strongly regular graph with geometric parameters
(s,t,\alpha)
are given by (
11
). This implies that the geometric parametrization is as complete as the combinatorial and spectral parametrizations. The advantage of the geometric parametrization over the other two parametrizations is the ease of expressing the Hoffman number:

h=s+1.

(13)

Furthermore, one could say that the fact that (
11
) holds for every primitive strongly regular graph implies that every strongly regular graph is pseudo-geometric in a way, if we relax the requirement that
s
,
t
, and
\alpha
in (
11
) are positive integers. However, we reserve the term “pseudo-geometric” for the case where the geometric parameters are positive integers. Note that
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
define geometric and pseudo-geometric also for the imprimitive strongly regular graphs, but we do not consider this case.

Remark 3.1
.

The geometric parameters correspond to
K-1
,
m-1
,

[

]
. However, this was only used in the context of edge-regular graphs with a regular clique (

,

If a strongly regular graph

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

and geometric parameters

. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

is integral (i.e.

Moreover, the Hoffman number of

Proof.

For the first set of equivalences, note that (iii)

\Rightarrow
(i) follows from (

). For (i)

For the final equivalence, note that by (

By ?THM?
, the strongly regular graphs with an irrational Hoffman number are precisely the non-integral strongly regular graphs (which are necessarily conference graphs), for instance the pentagon. An example of a strongly regular graph with a rational but not integral Hoffman number is the Petersen graph.

Using ?THM?
we can show that Hoffman colorable strongly regular graphs and their complements are pseudo-geometric. We also have a similar but weaker statement for strongly regular graphs with a Delsarte clique.

Theorem 3.3

Let

Proof.

Let

If

)

Let

Theorem 3.7

Let

Let

4
Vector colorings

Next we show a connection between Hoffman colorability and vector colorings that extends
[
14
, Theorem 3.12]
(see ?THM?

In order to state and prove ?THM?

uniquely vector colorable
if any two optimal vector colorings differ by an orthogonal transformation. A graph is
k
-walk-regular
if for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and every
0\leq i\leq k
, all pairs of vertices

and

at distance

have the same number of walks of length

from

to

, so that

only depends on the distance

, provided that this distance is at most
k
. Note that strongly regular graphs are
k
-walk-regular for any

. Lastly, a coloring (vector or standard) is
locally injective
if vertices at distance 2 are assigned different colors.

Theorem 4.1
.

A non-trivially Hoffman colorable connected 2-walk-regular graph is not uniquely vector colorable.

Proof.

Let
G
be non-trivially Hoffman colorable and 2-walk-regular.

Since
G
is non-trivially Hoffman colorable,
G
is neither a complete graph (Hoffman coloring is trivial) nor an odd cycle (among which only
K_{3}
is Hoffman colorable). Therefore Brooks’ Theorem
\chi(G)\leq\Delta(G)
implies that any Hoffman coloring
f
of

is not locally injective. As already mentioned in the Preliminaries, by
[
23
, Lemma 4.1]

f
induces a vector coloring
\widehat{f}
. Then
\widehat{f}
is not locally injective (as
f
is not), and by (
10
)
\widehat{f}
is optimal.

Next, since
G
is 1-walk-regular,
[
13
, Corollary 4.11]
gives an optimal vector coloring
\widetilde{f}
. Since
G
is 2-walk-regular,
\widetilde{f}
is locally injective by
[

, Lemma 3.11]
.

We thus have two optimal vector colorings
\widehat{f},\widetilde{f}
, of which
\widetilde{f}
is locally injective and
\widehat{f}
is not. We conclude that
G
is not uniquely vector colorable.
∎

In

Theorem 4.2

Let

For strongly regular graphs, ?THM?
is an extension of ?THM?

Theorem 4.3

A non-complete strongly regular graph is not a core if and only if it is Hoffman colorable and has a Delsarte clique.

?THM?
provides a new and more general sufficient condition for non-unique vector colorability than ?THM?
in the case of strongly regular graphs. Indeed, if

it is Hoffman colorable (the sufficient condition from ?THM?

The question of which strongly regular graphs are cores but not uniquely vector colorable (so the non-uniquely vector colorable graphs for which non-unique vector colorability does not follow from ?THM?
) was posed in
[

provides examples for this. By ?THM?

: the Hoffman colorable strongly regular graphs that do not have a Delsarte clique. For instance, the following strongly regular graphs (with combinatorial parameters) are of type A:

•

(16,6,2,2)

In fact, for the parameter set

Following
[

more closely, we see that we can actually prove in the same way that Taylor graphs are not Hoffman colorable. A

is a distance-regular graph with intersection array

Corollary 4.4
.

If a Taylor graph is Hoffman colorable, then it is bipartite.

Proof.

By

, any Taylor graph is uniquely vector colorable. Since Taylor graphs are 2-walk-regular, any Hoffman coloring is trivial by ?THM?
, implying that the graph is bipartite.

5
Characterizations for strongly regular graphs and a bound for the chromatic number of co-edge-regular graphs

Here we investigate the Hoffman number in terms of the average parameters introduced in
[
5
]
. Using this, we provide a new characterization of strongly regular graphs among regular graphs (?THM?
), namely these are precisely those regular graphs attaining equality in (
2
). Recall that (
2
) can also be shown using the Lovász
\vartheta
-number (see ?THM?
). Also, observe that (
7
) shows that for strongly regular graphs we have equality in (
2
); what we show in ?THM?
is that strongly regular graphs are the only such regular graphs. Furthermore, we improve Hoffman’s bound for the case of co-edge-regular graphs (?THM?
). We conclude this section by discussing some general consequences of the new results for Hoffman colorable regular graphs. For instance, from ?THM?
it follows that Hoffman colorable co-edge-regular and Neumaier graphs must be strongly regular (?THM?
(i) and (ii)).

A characterization of strong regularity using the Hoffman number

In order to prove ?THM?
, we use average parameters based on strongly regular graphs. Let
G
be a
k
-regular graph on
n
vertices, and assume for the remainder of this section that
G
is neither empty nor complete. Let
{a}_{\text{av}}
be the average number of common neighbors of two adjacent vertices of
G
, and similarly let
{c}_{\text{av}}
be the average number of common neighbors of two distinct non-adjacent vertices. Let
{\tau}_{\text{av}}
and
{\theta}_{\text{av}}
be the smallest and largest root respectively of the polynomial

g(x)=x^{2}+({c}_{\text{av}}-{a}_{\text{av}})x+({c}_{\text{av}}-k),

(15)

which is just (
4
) with
{a}_{\text{av}}
and
{c}_{\text{av}}
instead of
a
and
c
. Now let
{s}_{\text{av}}
be
-k/{\tau}_{\text{av}}
, as in Section
3
.

The following straightforward result, which shows that these average parameters behave much in the same way as the parameters for strongly regular graphs, is later used for proving ?THM?
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a
k
-regular graph, and let
{a}_{\text{av}}
,
{c}_{\text{av}}
,
{\tau}_{\text{av}}
,
{\theta}_{\text{av}}
, and
{s}_{\text{av}}
be defined as above. Let
{\overline{a}}_{\text{av}}
,
{\overline{c}}_{\text{av}}
,
{\overline{\tau}}_{\text{av}}
,
{\overline{\theta}}_{\text{av}}
, and
{\overline{s}}_{\text{av}}
those average parameters for the complement of
G
. Then the following hold.

(i)

{c}_{\text{av}}(n-k-1)=k(k-{a}_{\text{av}}-1)
,

(ii)

{\overline{a}}_{\text{av}}=n-2-2k+{c}_{\text{av}}
and
{\overline{c}}_{\text{av}}=n-2k+{a}_{\text{av}}
,

(iii)

{\overline{\tau}}_{\text{av}}=-1-{\theta}_{\text{av}}
and
{\overline{\theta}}_{\text{av}}=-1-{\tau}_{\text{av}}
.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from double counting the set
\{(u,v,w)\in V(G)^{3}:u\sim v,v\sim w,u\not\sim w,u\neq w\}
. Part (ii) follows by applying the same reasoning as for the corresponding statement for strongly regular graphs and averaging over every (non)-edge. For Part (iii), let
g(x)
be the polynomial from (
15
), and let
\overline{g}(x)
be that for the complement of
G
(which is
(n-k-1)
-regular). From Part (ii) it follows that
\overline{g}(x)=g(-x-1)
, from which Part (iii) follows.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Even though we define the average parameters in a different way than in

it follows that the average parameters defined here are equivalent to the parameters defined in

Lemma 5.3

Let

({s}_{\text{av}}+1)({\overline{s}}_{\text{av}}+1)=n

Note that
g(k)=n{c}_{\text{av}}

\displaystyle({s}_{\text{av}}+1)({\overline{s}}_{\text{av}}+1)

using ?THM?

For irregular graphs, it is possible to show a statement similar to ?THM?

We are now ready to state and prove the new characterization of strongly regular graphs using the Hoffman number.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let

By ?THM?

Alternative proofs of ?THM?

Remark 5.5
.

As mentioned before, the Lovász

A Hoffman-like bound for co-edge-regular graphs

We can also use the above average parameters to give an improvement of the Hoffman bound for co-edge-regular graphs.

As mentioned in ?THM?
, the average parameters introduced in this section were already introduced in
[
5
]
. Note however that the order in which the parameters are defined here is different from that in
[
5
]
. In particular,
{s}_{\text{av}}
can be defined using only

, as the positive root of

(n+{a}_{\text{av}}-2k)x^{2}+(k^{2}-k+{a}_{\text{av}}-{a}_{\text{av}}n)x-k(n-k-1).

For

Proposition 5.6

(Neumaier bound,
[
5
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

If

is edge-regular, then

\omega(G)\leq s+1.

A clique meets this bound if and only if it is regular.

By (

), this bound is a strict improvement of the ratio bound (
5
), except for strongly regular graphs for which the Neumaier bound and the ratio bound (

) are equivalent, in which case it was called the Delsarte bound.

A clique attaining the Neumaier bound is called a

, and is

-regular, where
{\alpha}_{\text{av}}=s-{\theta}_{\text{av}}
(compare ?THM?
). An edge-regular graph with a Neumaier clique is called a

. If a Neumaier graph is not strongly regular, then it is called

. Research is currently being done on constructing and classifying strictly Neumaier graphs, see for example

4
,
5
,

. Similarly as how Hoffman’s bound on the chromatic number follows from (

Theorem 5.7
.

If
G
is co-edge-regular, then
\chi(G)\geq{s}_{\text{av}}+1
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{G}
is edge-regular, we can apply the Neumaier bound (?THM?
) to get
\alpha(G)\leq\overline{s}+1
. Together with (
6
), ?THM?
now gives the result.
∎

Note that by (
16
), the bound given in ?THM?
is a strict improvement of Hoffman’s bound for co-edge-regular graphs that are not strongly regular.

Applications to Hoffman colorability

We can apply the results from this section to Hoffman colorings of regular graphs. We derive three results (?THM?
, ?THM?
, ?THM?
), each of which can be seen as a necessary condition for Hoffman colorability of regular graphs, or as a characterization of strongly regular graphs among Hoffman colorable regular graphs.

Firstly, we present four sufficient conditions for a Hoffman colorable regular graph to be strongly regular.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
G
be a Hoffman colorable regular graph. Then
G
is strongly regular if any of the following holds:

(i)

G
is co-edge-regular,

(ii)

G
is a Neumaier graph,

(iii)

G
has a Hoffman clique,

(iv)

\overline{G}
is Hoffman colorable.

Proof.

Since
G
is Hoffman colorable, by (
16
) it suffices to show that
\chi(G)\geq{s}_{\text{av}}+1
. Now Part (i) follows from ?THM?
. A Neumaier clique is of size
{s}_{\text{av}}+1
, so for Neumaier graphs
\chi(G)\geq{s}_{\text{av}}+1
, implying Part (ii). Part (iii) follows similarly from (
17
). Part (iv) follows from ?THM?
and Part (iii).
∎

The first two conditions can be rephrased as follows: no strictly Neumaier graph is Hoffman colorable, and no co-edge-regular graph that is not strongly regular is Hoffman colorable.

Example 5.9
.

Examples of graphs satisfying ?THM?
(iv) are the square lattice graphs
L(K_{m,m})
and their complements (the columns form a spread in
L(K_{m,m})
, and the translates of the diagonal form a spread in the complement), and Paley graphs of a square order (the cosets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
give a spread in the Paley graph over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, and since Paley graphs are self-complementary this gives also a Hoffman coloring), providing an example of a regular self-complementary Hoffman colorable graph.

Secondly, we can derive that the Neumaier bound holds not only for edge-regular graphs, but also for Hoffman colorable regular graphs.

Corollary 5.10
.

If
G
is a Hoffman colorable regular graph, then

\omega(G)\leq{s}_{\text{av}}+1,

with equality if and only if
G
is strongly regular and has a Delsarte clique.

Proof.

We have
\omega(G)\leq\chi(G)=h(G)\leq{s}_{\text{av}}+1
, where the last part follows from (
16
). In case of equality, we have equality in (
16
) so
G
is strongly regular, and we have
\omega(G)=h(G)
so
G
has a Delsarte clique. The other direction is clear.
∎

Note that even though equality in the Neumaier bound is not understood completely yet, ?THM?
gives a comprehensive condition for equality. Note that we have equality in ?THM?
if and only if
G
is strongly regular and not a core, by ?THM?
.

As a third and final application, we obtain the following bound on the number of triangles of a Hoffman colorable regular graph.

Corollary 5.11
.

If
G
is a Hoffman colorable regular graph with

N\geq\frac{kn}{6}\Big(\chi-1+\frac{(k-\chi)(k-\chi+1)}{n-\chi}-\frac{k(n-k-1)}{(n-\chi)(\chi-1)}\Big),

with equality if and only if
G
is strongly regular.

Proof.

By double counting the pairs of incident edges and triangles, we get

. By (

\chi\leq{s}_{\text{av}}+1
with equality if and only if the graph is strongly regular. This means that
\chi-1\leq{s}_{\text{av}}
, and evaluating the polynomial from (

{a}_{\text{av}}
in this inequality.
∎

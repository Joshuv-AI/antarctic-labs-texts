The commutative algebra of congruence ideals and applications to number theory
Srikanth B. Iyengar, Chandrashekhar B. Khare, Jeffrey Manning

Abstract
. In his proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem, Wiles deployed a commutative algebra technique, namely a numerical criterion for detecting isomorphisms of rings. In our recent work we pick up on Wiles’ work and generalize the numerical criterion to “higher codimension”. A critical ingredient is a notion of congruence module in higher codimension: this has turned out to be a key definition whose utility extends beyond the role it plays in the numerical criterion. In this paper we trace the origin of some of the ideas that led to our work, both in number theory and commutative algebra, and new directions that emerge from it. We introduce a related notion of a congruence ideal, develop some commutative algebra needed to work with it, and hint at applications to number theory.

1.
Introduction.

Our work takes its cue from an ingredient in Wiles’s solution of Fermat’s Last Theorem, and is a contribution to the theme of the unreasonable effectiveness of commutative algebra in number theory. This theme goes back to at least Kummer’s work (again on Fermat’s Last Theorem!) that led to the theory of ideals and to the development of the basic infrastructure of algebraic number theory. Formalism of congruence modules.

Let

Consider the dual lattice

The situation can be abstracted as follows: Consider an augmentation

Here, for any ideal

Motivated by number theory, in

In this manuscript, to further scientific diversity (borrowing a phrase from

We denote this

; see Section
2

—but suffices for many of the intended applications to number theory.

Congruence ideals for higher codimension turn out to have uses which we had not anticipated; and in our work we have been led by them to go where they will take us. Unlike the case of
c=0
, when there is a direct relation between congruence ideals and congruences, in higher codimension the meaning of the congruence ideal is more elusive and there is no direct link to congruences; its implicit significance and meaning is teased out by the contexts in which it is viewed. Our work shows that it is the correct generalization as it slots in perfectly into our generalization of the numerical criterion.

Number theoretic applications.

Congruences in the case of codimension zero have played a crucial role in the relationship between Galois representations and automorphic forms. They are a key ingredient in the strategy used by Scholze
[
43
]
to attach Galois representations to torsion classes in the cohomology of arithmetic manifolds. In the reverse direction, congruence ideals in the
c=0
case played a key role in the strategy in
[
51
]
and
[
47
]
for attaching modular forms to Galois representations. The applications we outline below hinge on applying our results to “patched” deformation rings. Patching was introduced by Taylor and Wiles in
[
47
]
to complete the proof of the modularity of semistable elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
initiated in
[
51
]
. The proof in
[
51
]
and
[
47
]
relied on using patching and the numerical criterion (in codimension
c=0
) independently, with the latter applied to classical (unpatched) objects. A key insight of our work is that applying a numerical criterion, necessarily in codimension
c>0
,
after
patching increases its reach.

Functorial properties of congruence ideals (related to “level raising” studied originally in
[
41
]
), in conjunction with our new numerical criterion applied after patching, led to the proof in
[
26
]
of new integral (non-minimal) “modularity lifting” theorems for weight one in the setting of Shimura curves. As a corollary we deduce a Jacquet-Langlands correspondence for Hecke algebras
\mathbb{T}
that act faithfully on the coherent cohomology of Shimura curves
X
, arising from indefinite quaternion algebras
D
over
\mathbb{Q}
as
D
varies, with coefficients in
\omega_{X}^{\otimes k}
for weight
k=1
(
\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}=\Omega_{X}
). Our forthcoming work with Fred Diamond
[
16
]
generalizes these results. The proof of the corollary uses modularity lifting (reciprocity) to establish functorialty (lifting torsion in cohomology of Shimura curves to torsion in cohomology of modular curves), as the latter is obvious at the level of Galois parameters (deformation rings). The novelty of our result is that as weight
k=1
Hecke algebras tend to have a lot of torsion (in contrast to the Hecke algebra acting on the coherent cohomology of the sheaves
\omega^{\otimes k}
for weights
k\geq 2
), our result cannot be deduced from the classical Jacquet-Langlands correspondence for these Hecke algebras tensored with
\mathbb{Q}
.

Applied to local deformation rings, our definition of congruence ideals (in codimension
c\geq 1
) produces a useful local-global factorization of congruence ideals (for
c=0
) of classical Hecke algebras. This leads to unexpected (conjectural, as yet) relationships between congruence ideals of local deformation rings at augmentations induced by newforms
f
of weight
k\geq 2
, and certain local Tamagawa ideals defined by Bloch and Kato for the adjoint motives
\operatorname{Ad}_{f}
of
f
; see
3.2
for details. This is the starting point of our work with Diamond to prove cases of the conjecture of Bloch and Kato for
\operatorname{Ad}_{f}
that go beyond the results of
[
15
]
. The Bloch and Kato conjecture in this case relates
L(1,\operatorname{Ad}_{f})
to orders of Selmer groups. Our contact with
L
-values is through congruence ideals of modules associated to
f
arising from the cohomology of modular curves: the prototype of such a connection between congruence ideals and
L(1,\operatorname{Ad}_{f})
is Hida’s result
[
23
]
.

We give an outline of these ideas in the concluding section of the paper. The mathematics presented here illustrates an oft observed feature of number theory that it can have the flavor of applied mathematics: one applies to its problems techniques from other branches of mathematics, in our case commutative algebra, that are sometimes expressly developed in response to its demands.

2.
Congruence ideals.

In this section we introduce the class of augmented
\mathcal{O}
-algebras and a notion of congruence ideal attached to a module over such an algebra that are the focus of this manuscript. For the most part, the proofs of the statements are only sketched. The emphasis is on the purely commutative algebra aspects of the theory, but the development is inspired by, and tailored for applications to, number theory. We comment on these motivations, and also on connections to literature in commutative algebra, in the subsections titled “Notes”. See also the first part of Section
3
.

Throughout this text
\mathcal{O}
denotes a complete discrete valuation ring and
K
its field of fractions. We fix also a uniformizer
\varpi

\operatorname{tors}U\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ker}(U\to U\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K)\quad\text{and}\quad{U}^{\operatorname{tf}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Coker}(\operatorname{tors}U\subseteq U)\,;

Augmented
\mathcal{O}
-algebras.

Let

its residue field, such that

The number

. In what follows, we consider

as an

. For each

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}({\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{2},\mathcal{O})\,.

The module on the right is the normal module of

For each

\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\,.

This map has a particularly simple description when

For

.

Let

\mathcal{O}
-algebra and

(1)

\eta_{\lambda}(A)\neq 0

(3)

\eta_{\lambda}(M)\neq 0

Moreover, when these conditions hold, one has
\eta_{\lambda}(A)={\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}

Sketch of proof.

Since

is the image of the map

The last part of the theorem is extracted from

; its proof again uses Lescot’s result.

2.2.
The category
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
.

For applications to number theory, the focus is on pairs
(A,\lambda)
that satisfy the equivalent conditions in Theorem
2.5
; that is to say, where the local ring
A_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is regular. We write
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
for the category of such pairs; a morphism
\varphi\colon(A,\lambda_{A})\to(B,\lambda_{B})
in this category is a map
\varphi\colon A\to B
of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras such that

\lambda_{B}\circ\varphi=\lambda_{A}
. For any integer
c\geq 0
, the subcategory of pairs
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
with
\dim A_{\mathfrak{p}}=c
is denoted
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
.

Example 2.7
.

Here are some typical examples, from
[

]
, to keep in mind:

\displaystyle A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(a,b)\in\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{O}\mid a\equiv b\mod\varpi^{n}\}\quad\text{where $n$ is a fixed positive integer.}

\displaystyle B\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{O}\mid a\equiv b\equiv c\mod\varpi\}

where

is a uniformizer for
\mathcal{O}
. One has isomorphisms of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras

Clearly, there are multiple

-valued points in

[

that assign

Here is an example from

where

\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta&\varpi^{n}+a&b\\
\gamma&-\alpha&c&-a\end{pmatrix}

The ring

be the

-algebra map that assigns the generators,

, to zero,

,
\beta\to\varpi^{m}

Here is a simple consequence of the regularity of the ring

. In the statement,
\beta_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
denotes the minimal number of generators of the

-module

{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}

Lemma 2.8
.

For
A
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
, and any finitely generated
A
-module

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}}(M/{\mathfrak{p}}M)=\beta_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\,.

In particular, as
\mathcal{O}
-modules one has
{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}\cong\mathcal{O}

Proof.

Set

; this is a local ring, with maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
and residue field

, the field of fractions of
\mathcal{O}
. Since one has an isomorphism

\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{R}(K,M_{\mathfrak{p}})\,,

it suffices to verify that the rank of the

\beta_{R}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})

{\mathfrak{p}}A_{\mathfrak{p}}

2.2.1.
Depth.

Fix
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
and a finitely generated
A
-module
M
such that

\mathrm{depth}_{A}M\geq c+1\quad\text{and}\quad M_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\,.

The condition on the depth of
M
holds when, for instance, it is
maximal Cohen-Macaulay
, that is to say, if
0pt_{A}M=\dim A
, as is often the case in the number theoretic context. The hypothesis on
M
implies that the
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
has maximal depth and hence it is a nonzero free
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module, by the Auslander-Buchsbaum equality. Set

\mu\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}\,.

Since the codomain of the map (
2.3
) is a torsion-free
\mathcal{O}
-module, it factors through
{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\mathcal{O})
. Having said this, when
M
has sufficient depth the Ext-module in question is torsion-free. This follows from the following observation.

Lemma 2.9
.

If
0pt_{A}M\geq c+1
, the
\mathcal{O}
-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)
is torsion-free. ∎

Proof.

We have an exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{O}\xrightarrow{\varpi}\mathcal{O}\to k_{A}\to 0
of
A
-modules. Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,M)
to it yields an exact sequence

\cdots\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(k_{A},M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\xrightarrow{\ \varpi\ }\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\longrightarrow\cdots

The hypothesis
0pt_{A}M\geq c+1
is equivalent to
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(k_{A},M)=0
for

; see
[
9
, Theorem 1.2.8]
. Thus the sequence above yields the desired result.
∎

2.3.
Congruence module.

Consider the map

\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}{(M/{\mathfrak{p}}_{A}M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}\cong{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M/{\mathfrak{p}}_{A}M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}.

that is adjoint to (
2.2
). It coincides with the map induced by the canonical surjection
M\to M/{\mathfrak{p}}_{A}M
. The cokernel of this map is the
congruence module
of
M
at
\lambda
, and denoted
\Psi_{\lambda}(M)
. It is the focus of our work in
[
26
]
. The result below, which is an analogue of Theorem
2.5
, characterizes the
\mathcal{O}
-algebras in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
among all
\mathcal{O}
-algebras; it is extracted from
[
28
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
A
be a noetherian local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra and
\lambda\colon A\to\mathcal{O}
a morphism of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The local ring
A
is regular at
\lambda
; that is to say,
(A,\lambda)
is in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
.

(2)

The
\mathcal{O}
-module
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)
is torsion.

(3)

The
\mathcal{O}
-module
\Psi_{\lambda}(M)
is torsion for all
A
-modules
M
.

When these conditions hold, one has
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)=0
if and only if
A
is regular.∎

The proof of this result is similar to that of Theorem
2.5
; in particular, Lescot’s theorem
[
34
, Theorem 1.4]
is again a key input in the proof that (2)
\Rightarrow
(1).

Fix
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
and a finitely generated
A
-module
M
, and set
\mu=\operatorname{rank}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
. It follows from the theorem above that as
\mathcal{O}
-modules, one has an isomorphism

(2.11)

\Psi_{\lambda}(M)\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\mu}\frac{\mathcal{O}}{(\varpi^{e_{i}})}\quad\text{for integers $0\leq e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\cdots\leq e_{\mu}$.}

The following observation is straightforward to verify.

Lemma 2.12
.

One has
\eta_{\lambda}(M)=(\varpi^{e_{1}}){\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}
, and hence

\Psi_{\lambda}(M)\cong{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}/\eta_{\lambda}(M)\quad\text{when $\mu=1$}.\qed

Thus the congruence module of
M
is a more refined invariant than the congruence ideal. Nevertheless, in this write-up, we focus on the congruence ideal, for reasons explained in the Introduction.

2.4.
A defect formula.

For any
A
-module
M
one has a natural Künneth map

(2.13)

\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},A)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(M/{\mathfrak{p}}M)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},A)\otimes_{A}M\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\,.

In terms of Yoneda extensions, given an element
\zeta=0\to A\to\cdots\to\mathcal{O}\to 0
in
\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},A)
and an element
m\in M
, the map sends
\zeta\otimes m
to the push-out of the extension along the
A
-linear map
A\to M
given by
1\mapsto m
.

Consider the induced map on torsion-free quotients

(2.14)

\kappa_{\lambda}(M)\colon{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},A)}^{\operatorname{tf}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}{(M/{\mathfrak{p}}M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}\,.

The domain and codomain of this map are free
\mathcal{O}
-modules of rank equal to
\beta_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
; see Lemma
2.8
. From
[
26
, Lemma 3.7]
one gets an exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}
-modules

(2.15)

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Coker}\kappa_{\lambda}(M)\longrightarrow\Psi_{\lambda}(A)^{\mu}\longrightarrow\Psi_{\lambda}(M)\longrightarrow 0\,,

where
\mu
is the rank of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
. A similar argument yields the following analogue for congruence ideals.

Lemma 2.16
.

For
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
and any finitely generated
A
-module
M
, the map
\kappa_{\lambda}(M)
is one-to-one, and there is an equality

\eta_{\lambda}(A)=\operatorname{ann}_{\mathcal{O}}(\operatorname{Coker}\kappa_{\lambda}(M))\cdot\eta_{\lambda}(M)

as
\mathcal{O}
-submodules of
{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}
. ∎

One of the key results in our work is a criterion for
M
to have a free summand, in terms of the congruence modules of
A
and of
M
; see
[
26
, Theorem 9.2]
. Here is the analogue involving the congruence ideals.

Theorem 2.17
.

Fix
A
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
and a finitely generated
A
-module
M\neq 0
. When the ring
A
is Gorenstein and
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay,
\eta_{\lambda}(M)=\eta_{\lambda}(A)
if and only if
M\cong A^{\mu}\oplus W
, where
\mu=\beta_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
W_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
.

Proof.

The equality
\eta_{\lambda}(M)=\eta_{\lambda}(A)
holds if and only if
\operatorname{ann}_{\mathcal{O}}(\operatorname{Coker}\kappa(M))=\mathcal{O}
; equivalently, if and only if
\operatorname{Coker}\kappa(M)=0
; see Lemma
2.16
. Thus the desired statement is equivalent to
[
26
, Theorem 9.2]
.
∎

Notes.

The import of Lemma
2.16
is that, in the number theoretic context, in particular in modularity lifting, one has often a good grasp on the module
M
, and little information on the ring

; this has been explained in the Introduction. Lemma

The Künneth map (

Change of modules.

Let
\omega_{A}
be a dualizing complex for
A
normalized as in

[
48

Tag 0A7M
]
. It exists because
A
is a complete local ring; the normalization ensures that it is unique up to an isomorphism of complexes. Given a maximal Cohen-Macaulay module
M
, let
M^{\vee}
denote the
A

\operatorname{H}^{d}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\omega_{A}))
, where

is the Krull dimension of
A
. We say
M
is
self-dual
if
M\cong M^{\vee}
; see
[
26
, §4]
.

Fix a surjective map
\pi\colon M\to N
where
M,N
are maximal Cohen-Macaulay
A
-modules that are self-dual, and such that
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an isomorphism. Consider the following diagram where
\alpha
is defined to make the diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M^{\vee})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},N^{\vee})}

By Lemma
2.9
the source and target of

\alpha\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)

is an endomorphism of a free

Here is the analogous statement in terms of congruence ideals; it can be proved along the same lines as

Notes.

2.6.
The structure of
{\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}
.

To go further, we need one more input from commutative algebra. Fix
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
. The graded
\mathcal{O}
-module

\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})=\{\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\}_{i\geqslant 0}

has a structure of an associative algebra, with product given by Yoneda composition. The product agrees (up to sign) with the composition of maps in the derived category of
A
. Typically, this Ext algebra is highly non-commutative. By construction, one has an inclusion of subrings

{\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}\subseteq\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}(K,K)\cong\wedge_{K}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}(K,K)\,,

where
K
is the residue field of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
; said otherwise, the field of fractions of
\mathcal{O}
. The isomorphism holds because the local ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular. In particular, the ring on the right is the graded-commutative, and hence so is its subring
{\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}
. Thus, by the universal property of exterior algebras, the inclusion
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\subset\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})
induces a map of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras

\xi_{\lambda}\colon\wedge^{*}_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}\,.

Here is the punchline; see
[
26
, Theorem 6.8]
. It may be seen as an integral version of Serre’s theorem describing the Ext-algebra of a regular local ring, mentioned above. It is a critical ingredient in tracking the change of congruence modules and congruence ideals; see Proposition
2.20
and Theorem
2.21
.

Theorem 2.19
.

For
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
, the map
\xi_{\lambda}
defined above is bijective. In particular there is a natural isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-modules

{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}\cong\wedge^{c}_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}({\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{2},\mathcal{O}),.

Sketch of proof.

In
[
26
]
we gave a proof that draws on the methods of differential graded algebras, and in particular
[
25
]
, which in turn relies heavily on the work of Tate
[
46
]
and André
[
2
]
. Here is a different, simpler, argument that hinges on a natural map

\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(\wedge^{*}_{\mathcal{O}}({\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}),\mathcal{O})\,,

introduced by Grothendieck
[
22
, §3]
, and called the
fundamental local homomorphism
by Lipman
[
35
, pp. 111]
. It is adjoint to the composition

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\wedge_{\mathcal{O}}^{i}({\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{Tor}_{i}^{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\to\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{0}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\cong\mathcal{O}\,.

In
[
36
, (2.9)]
Lipman asks us to verify that the fundamental local homomorphism is a map of graded algebras, where the product on the left is the usual one, and the one on the right is induced by the natural co-algebra structure on
\wedge^{*}_{\mathcal{O}}({\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})
; in particular, it is graded-commutative. Since the target of the map above is torsion-free as an
\mathcal{O}
-module it induces the map of graded algebras on the right:

\wedge^{*}_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow{\ \xi_{\lambda}\ }{\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(\wedge^{*}_{\mathcal{O}}({\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}),\mathcal{O})\,.

Keeping in mind (
2.1
), it is clear that the composition is an isomorphism. Localizing at
{\mathfrak{p}}
it is easy to verify that both maps above are one-to-one, so we deduce that they are both isomorphisms.
∎

2.7.
Invariance of domain.

Let
\varphi\colon(A,\lambda_{A})\to(B,\lambda_{B})
be a morphism in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
. For any
B
-module
N
, functoriality of Ext gives a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},N)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(N,\mathcal{O})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{A}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}_{\varphi}^{c}(\mathcal{O},N)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\varphi}(N,\mathcal{O})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ext}_{\varphi}^{c}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}

Passing to torsion-free quotients, one gets an induced commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{\lambda_{B}}(N)\subseteq{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{B}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}}

The statement below is analogous to
[

, Theorem 7.4]
, and can be proved along the same lines.

Proposition 2.20
.

In the situation above, if
\varphi
is surjective, then
\eta_{\varphi}(N)
and
{\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})}^{\operatorname{tf}}
are isomorphisms. ∎

2.7.1.
Notes.

The invariance of domain property is obvious for

2.8.
Deformations.

Fix
(A,\lambda_{A})
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
, a finitely generated
A
-module
M
, and an element
f
in
{\mathfrak{p}}\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)}
that is not a zerodivisor on
M
. Here
{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)}
is the second symbolic power of
{\mathfrak{p}}
. Set
B\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A/(f)
and
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}M/fM
.

Since
f
is in
{\mathfrak{p}}
, the augmentation
\lambda_{A}\colon A\to\mathcal{O}
factors through
B
, and we get an augmentation
\lambda_{B}\colon B\to\mathcal{O}
. The condition that
f
is in
{\mathfrak{p}}
but not in
{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)}
means that its image in the localizaton
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is contained in the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
, but not its square,
{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular it follows that the local ring
B_{\mathfrak{p}}
is also regular, with
\dim B_{\mathfrak{p}}=\dim A_{\mathfrak{p}}-1
; see
[
9
, Proposition 2.2.4]
. Thus
(B,\lambda_{B})
is in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c-1)
.

We first give a sketch of Wiles’s proof of the Shimura, Taniyama, Weil conjecture for semistable elliptic curves over

; the applications of our results on congruence ideals, Theorem

One formulation of the Shimura, Taniyama, Weil conjecture is in terms of representations of

, the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}
. Given any elliptic curve
E
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and an odd prime number

on the
p

, one gets a two-dimensional Galois representation
\rho_{E}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to{\rm GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})

is the
p

\mathbb{Z}

starting from a modular eigenform

\rho_{E}
is isomorphic to a representation
\rho_{f}
arising from such a weight
2

Wiles puts himself in a situation in which he can assume that the mod

, arises from a newform and

of (noetherian, commutative) local algebras over a discrete valuation ring

are isomorphisms. The ring
R_{\Sigma}
parametrizes Galois deformations subject to constraints determined by a finite collection of primes
\Sigma
, while
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Sigma}
is a Hecke algebra subject to the same constraints, which can be thought of as parameterizing a family of modular forms. Hence proving that
\varphi_{\Sigma}

) parameterized by

corresponds to a modular form.

The algebra
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Sigma}

which can be constructed from the cohomology of a modular curve. To prove that

is an isomorphism of complete intersections, in the version of Wiles’s approach given by Diamond

The argument that

One shows that if

(1)

(2)

It was not clear how the numerical criterion could be adapted to serve in such situations, for the complete intersection property is essential to its application. Some of these problems have been overcome thanks to the work of Kisin, who developed the patching technique so it applies also in the non-minimal case, and by Calegari and Geraghty, who extending patching to complexes. (2)

M_{\varnothing,\infty}^{D}
, is non-zero by Serre’s Conjecture and an analogue of the companion forms theorem for Shimura curves. This gives the first equality below:

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}/\eta_{\lambda}(M_{\varnothing,\infty}^{D})=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\lambda}(R^{D}_{\varnothing,\infty})=0.

The second equality is a direct computation; see also Theorem
2.10
. For the present purpose, we care only about the equality on the left. To complete the argument, we must then propagate it
to non-minimal
\Sigma

2.18
.

Indeed for
\Sigma\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
, one has level-raising and lowering maps

\pi_{\infty}\colon M_{\Sigma^{\prime},\infty}^{D}\longrightarrow M_{\Sigma,\infty}^{D}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{\infty}^{\vee}\colon M_{\Sigma,\infty}^{D}\longrightarrow M_{\Sigma^{\prime},\infty}^{D}\,,

which are constructed by applying the patching process to certain families of natural maps
M_{\Sigma^{\prime}}^{D}\to M_{\Sigma}^{D}
and
M_{\Sigma}^{D}\to M_{\Sigma^{\prime}}^{D}
. A key number theoretic fact known as
Ihara’s Lemma
—proven in this generality in
[

, Proposition 5]
—implies that
\pi_{\infty}
is surjective. Lemma
2.18
then gives an equality

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}/\eta_{\lambda}(M_{\Sigma^{\prime},\infty}^{D})=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}/\eta_{\lambda}(M_{\Sigma,\infty}^{D})+\nu

for some nonnegative integer
\nu
which can be explicitly determined by computing the composition
\pi_{\infty}^{\vee}\circ\pi_{\infty}
. On the other hand, we can also show that

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\lambda}(R^{D}_{\Sigma^{\prime},\infty})=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\lambda}(R^{D}_{\Sigma,\infty})+\nu

by direct computation, which is possible in our case, unlike in Wiles’ argument, as the rings

and

can be described explicitly. This gives the equality

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}/\eta_{\lambda}(M_{\Sigma^{\prime},\infty}^{D})=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\lambda}(R^{D}_{\Sigma,\infty})

for all
\Sigma

2.24
gives the desired result.

The claim that the rank
\mu
is independent of
\Sigma
is a consequence of the fact that the maps
\pi_{\infty}
are isomorphisms after localizing at

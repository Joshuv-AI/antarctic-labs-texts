Abstract
. We study L p ​ ( μ ) L^{p}(\mu) estimates for the commutator [ ℋ , b ] [\mathcal{H},b] , where the operator ℋ \mathcal{H} is a dyadic model of the classical Hilbert transform introduced in [ 9 , 11 ] and is adapted to a non-doubling Borel measure μ \mu satisfying a dyadic regularity condition which is necessary for ℋ \mathcal{H} to be bounded on L p ​ ( μ ) L^{p}(\mu) . We show that ‖ [ ℋ , b ] ‖ L p ​ ( μ ) → L p ​ ( μ ) ≲ ‖ b ‖ BMO ⁡ ( μ ) \|[\mathcal{H},b]\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)}\lesssim\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)} , but to characterize martingale BMO requires additional commutator information. We prove weighted inequalities for [ ℋ , b ] [\mathcal{H},b] together with a version of the John-Nirenberg inequality adapted to appropriate weight classes A ^ p \widehat{A}_{p} that we define for our non-homogeneous setting. This requires establishing reverse Hölder inequalities for these new weight classes. Finally, we revisit the appropriate class of nonhomogeneous measures μ \mu for the study of different types of Haar shift operators.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the relationship between the martingale
\mathrm{BMO}
space and commutators of a dyadic Hilbert transform that was recently introduced in
[
11
]
and
[
9
]
, but studied here in the context of non-doubling measures.

Motivated by showing a factorization theorem for the Hardy space, whose dual had just been identified as
\mathrm{BMO}
,
the seminal paper
[
7
]
considered the commutators of Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operators
(
K
) with the operator of multiplication by
a
\mathrm{BMO}
function,
b
. With
b
also denoting that multiplication operator, they showed that these commutators,

[K,b](\cdot):=Kb(\cdot)-bK(\cdot)
, are bounded maps of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx)
to itself, with operator norm a constant
times
\|b\|_{BMO}
. Conversely, they proved that if all the commutators of Riesz transforms,
[R_{j},\phi]
, with multiplication by a
function
\phi
are bounded maps of
L^{p}
, for some
1<p<\infty
, then
\phi\in\text{BMO}
, thus
characterizing

\mathrm{BMO}
in
terms of an operator norm. Specifically, for
\mathbb{R}
, this amounts to saying that the boundedness of the commutator with the Hilbert transform

\mathbb{H}f(x)=\mathrm{p.v}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}dy

characterizes
\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R})
.

Subsequent studies took this foundational result in a number of different and expanded directions. In one direction,
[
6
]
linked
such commutator estimates to their div-curl approach to compensated compactness. In the theory of martingales, which is the context for this paper,
commutators of martingale transforms were investigated by
[
15
]
, for
regular
martingales, and later by
[
20
]
in the “nonregular” or nonhomogeneous setting. Endpoint estimates were investigated in
[
3
]
.

The characterization of
\mathrm{BMO}
in terms of commutators of Calderón-Zygmund operators extends to certain measure spaces beyond
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, equipped with the Lebesgue measure. In particular, commutators of nondegenerate Calderón-Zygmund operators characterize
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{X},\mu)
for certain metric measure spaces where the measure satisfies the
doubling condition
(defined below) whereas we are interested in the non-doubling setting.

Absent the doubling condition, the definition of appropriate function spaces that are connected to the boundedness of
singular integral operators, such as the Hardy spaces
or
\mathrm{BMO}
spaces, can be a difficult matter. One example of success in this context is the
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
of
[
19
]
for
measures on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
with polynomial growth.

Our focus here is on commutators of certain discrete, or martingale, operators that, in the doubling setting,
have been good models for Calderón-Zygmund operators. We work with Borel measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
defined on any dyadic system
\mathscr{D}
- in particular, the usual one. The system
\mathscr{D}=\cup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{D}_{k}
is a two-sided sequence of partitions that generates a martingale filtration on
\mathbb{R}
. This equips the space with a natural martingale
\mathrm{BMO}
space
[
12
]
, that will be denoted
\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
. See
Section
2
for the definition and properties.
The dyadic operator we consider here is a modern version of Petermichl’s Haar shift, termed
“the dyadic Hilbert transform” in
[
11
]
.
The classical dyadic Haar shift operator of
[
18
]
has
been studied in the non-doubling setting in
[
16
]
, and more recently in
[
8
]
. In
[
16
]
, it was shown that
the boundedness of these classical Haar shift
operators on
L^{p}
spaces, for
p\neq 2
, necessitated additional constraints on the non-doubling measure.

We now introduce (as in
[
16
]
) the Haar wavelet basis on
\mathbb{R}(d\mu)
, together with a quantity that normalizes the Haar functions in
L^{2}(d\mu)
.

Intervals
I\in\mathscr{D}
have parents
\widehat{I}
, children
I_{-}
and
I_{+}
, and a sibling
I^{s}
. Our measures
\mu
fail to satisfy
the
doubling
doubling property, i.e.,
\mu(\widehat{I})\lesssim\mu(I)
with an implied constant independent of
I
. In this setting, the following
quantity arises naturally in order to define Haar functions and, following
[
16
]
, we denote:

m(I):=\frac{\mu(I_{-})\mu(I_{+})}{\mu(I)}.

A measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
is said to be
balanced
if
m(I)\sim m(\widehat{I})
for all
I\in\mathscr{D}
. The class of balanced measures arises naturally in the study of Haar shifts -
[
16
]
showed that this property characterizes the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the classical
Haar shift operator of
[
18
]
and thus the entire class of
higher complexity
Haar shifts (defined in Subsection
2.4
).

Haar shift operators are linear maps that alter the Haar expansion of functions in terms of the Haar basis elements:

h_{I}(x)=\sqrt{m(I)}\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{I_{-}}}{\mu(I_{-})}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{I_{+}}}{\mu(I_{+})}\right),\;I\in\mathscr{D}.

Above and throughout, we use standard notation for integral averages and pairings:

\langle f\rangle_{I}=\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}fd\mu,\quad\langle f,g\rangle=\int fg\;d\mu.

We consider the following dyadic model of the Hilbert transform:

\mathcal{H}f(x)=\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\left[\langle f,h_{I_{+}}\rangle h_{I_{-}}(x)-\langle f,h_{I_{-}}\rangle h_{I_{+}}(x)\right].

One can see that for each
I\in\mathscr{D}
,

\mathcal{H}(h_{I_{+}}):=h_{I_{-}}\text{ and},\,\mathcal{H}(h_{I_{-}}):=-h_{I_{+}},

so the behavior of the operator on discrete sine and cosine waves matches that of
\mathbb{H}
on the continuous ones.
\mathcal{H}
was studied in
[
11
]
and
[
9
]
, where its connection with
\mathbb{H}
is made evident. In fact,
in the doubling setting, it was shown in [
[
9
]
,Theorem 2.1]] that the
commutator
[\mathcal{H},b]
with this Haar shift characterizes dyadic
\mathrm{BMO}
and
\mathcal{H}
was used in their characterization of the multiparameter “little
\mathrm{BMO}
” space. Further, the operator
\mathcal{H}
has been useful in the recent proof of the counterexample to the so-called matrix
A_{2}
conjecture
[
10
]
.

By orthonormality of the Haar functions,
\mathcal{H}
is an
L^{2}(\mu)
-bounded Haar shift operator. Thus the theory in
[
16
]
applies to it as well; in particular, it extends to an
L^{p}(\mu)
-bounded operator whenever
\mu
is balanced. But the balanced class does not characterize the
L^{p}
-boundedness of this particular Haar shift,
\mathcal{H}
. We call
\mu

sibling balanced
if

[\mu]_{\mathrm{sib}}:=\sup_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\frac{m(I)}{m(I^{s})}<\infty.

Clearly, balanced measures are sibling balanced. Moreover, we shall see that the sibling balanced class is strictly larger than the balanced one and is the right one to guarantee boundedness on
L^{p}(\mu)
of
\mathcal{H}
as well as that of the commutator
[\mathcal{H},b]
.

Therefore, we test the commutator characterization of
\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
in that context. Perhaps surprisingly, even for this restricted class of measures, the characterization fails.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mu
be sibling balanced,
b\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
and
1<p<\infty
. Then, the following hold:

•

Upper estimate:

\|[\mathcal{H},b]\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)}\lesssim_{p,[\mu]_{sib}}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}.

•

Lower estimate:

\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}\lesssim_{p,[\mu]_{sib}}\|[\mathcal{H},b]\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)}+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|[\mathcal{H},\mathsf{E}_{k}b]\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)}.

•

Failure of lower estimate in general:
The estimate

\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}\lesssim\|[\mathcal{H},b]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu)}

fails in general, even if we allow the implicit constant to depend on
[\mu]_{\mathrm{sib}}
.

Straightforward calculations show that for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\|\mathsf{E}_{k}b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}\leq\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)},

which suggests that it might be possible to remove the terms involving
[\mathcal{H},\mathsf{E}_{k}b]
in the lower estimate of Theorem
A
. However, we construct a family of sibling balanced measures and BMO functions that show that these bounds are essential to control the norm in
\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
. The upper estimate ensuring the boundedness on
L^{p}(\mu)
of
[\mathcal{H},b]
is, by contrast, the expected one.

We next turn our attention to weighted estimates for the commutator, that is, boundedness with respect to measures of the form
\omega d\mu
where
\omega
is nonnegative. Both one and two-weight inequalities for commutators with singular integral operators have a long history. In the special case of the Hilbert transform and Lebesgue measure, Bloom (
[
2
]
) proved an inequality of the form

\|[b,\mathbb{H}]\|_{L^{p}(\omega)\rightarrow L^{p}(\sigma)}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu}},

where
\omega,\sigma\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
\nu=\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma}\right)^{1/p}
, and
{\rm BMO}_{\nu}
denotes a weighted BMO space. When
\omega=\sigma
, Bloom’s result reduces to the one weight inequality with a BMO-norm bound:

\|[b,\mathbb{H}]\|_{L^{p}(\omega)\rightarrow L^{p}(\omega)}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}},\quad\omega\in A_{p},

and the implicit constant depends on
[\omega]_{A_{p}}.
According to the authors of
[
4
]
, this one weight inequality was also implicit in a proof due to Strömberg of the Coifman, Rochberg, and Weiss result (although the literature is slightly unclear on this point). Various generalizations of this one weight result have been considered in the more general context of Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operators; see for example
[
1
,
4
]
. Therefore, in the Euclidean (doubling) setting, we see that the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
is the right one for weighted inequalities of commutators with singular integrals. The sharp dependence of the operator norm of the commutator on the weight characteristic has also been investigated; see
[
5
]
for the special case of the Hilbert transform and
[
4
]
for general Calderón-Zygmund operators. Heuristically, the commutator behaves worse and is “more singular” than classical Calderón-Zygmund operators, as evidenced by the sharp quadratic dependence on the
A_{2}
weight characteristic - as opposed to the linear dependence proved in the
A_{2}
theorem for the operators themselves.

The class
A_{p}^{bal}(\mu)
,
1<p<\infty
, was introduced in
[
8
]
to characterize the weighted bounds for Haar shifts when the underlying assumption that
\mu
is balanced. We say that
\omega\in A_{p}^{bal}(\mu)
if

[\omega]_{A_{p}^{bal}(\mu)}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathscr{D}:\\
J\in\{I,I_{-}^{s},I_{+}^{s}\}\text{ or }\\
I\in\{J,J_{-}^{s},J_{+}^{s}\}\end{subarray}}\frac{m(I)^{p/2}m(J)^{p/2}}{\mu(I)^{p-1}\mu(J)}\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle_{J}^{p-1}<\infty,

denoting the dual weight
\sigma=\omega^{-1/(p-1)}
. In view of Theorem
A
, one would not expect
A_{p}^{bal}(\mu)
to be the right class of weights to study the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}
and hence of
[\mathcal{H},b]
. Below we explain the modifications needed to define the class
A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)
that governs the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
\mathcal{H}
with respect to an underlying sibling balanced measure. But the situation with the commutator is more interesting: one needs a characterization of
\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
functions in terms of weights
\omega
(specifically, of their logarithms). To that end, we will see that neither of the classes
A_{2}^{bal}(\mu)
and class
A_{2}^{sib}(\mu)
are the right ones. Instead,
\text{BMO}(\mu)
functions can be characterized by a smaller weight class
\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
, which is the class of weights
\omega
which satisfy

(
\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
)

[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathscr{D}:\\
J\in\{I,\widehat{I}\}\text{ or }\\
I\in\{J,\widehat{J}\}\end{subarray}}\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle_{J}^{p-1}<\infty.

The class
\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
has several nice features that make it the right counterpart of the classical
A_{p}
one to study commutators, partly because weights in this class satisfy a reverse Hölder inequality.

Theorem B
.

Let
\mu
be a locally finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\mu(I)>0
for all
I\in\mathscr{D}
.

(a)

Suppose
\omega\in\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu).
Then the function
\log\omega\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
. Conversely, suppose
b\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mu).
Then for sufficiently small
\delta>0
, the weight function
e^{\delta b}
belongs to
\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)
.

(b)

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
. Then there exists
\gamma>1
, depending only on
p
and
[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}
, so that for all
I\in\mathscr{D}
,

\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\omega^{\gamma}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\gamma}\lesssim_{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\omega\,d\mu\right).

It is important to remark that the class
\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
seems to be the right nondoubling analog of
A_{p}
for all nondoubling measures, not just the balanced or sibling balanced ones. Theorem
B
holds for all measures
\mu
, yielding a satisfactory theory of weights independent of the doubling condition. Weights in
\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
are sufficient to ensure weighted
L^{p}
-boundedness of
[\mathcal{H},b]
.

Theorem C
.

Let
\mu
be sibling balanced and atomless. Let
1<p<\infty
,
b\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
, and
\omega\in\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
. Then there holds

\|[\mathcal{H},b]\|_{L^{p}(\omega)\rightarrow L^{p}(\omega)}\lesssim_{[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)},p,[\mu]_{\mathrm{sib}}}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}.

We investigate how specialized are our results by studying commutators
[T,b]
for general Haar shifts, assuming the balanced condition on the underlying measure. Using a decomposition of general Haar shifts into simpler ones —that was already used in
[
8
]
and that we record in Section
2
— we obtain results analogous to the upper estimate in Theorem
A
and Theorem
C
for Haar shifts in the general case.

In Section
1
, we prove the connection between sibling balanced measures and the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}
. In Section
2
, we prove Theorem
A
and in Section
3
, we explore the connections between the new weight classes and characterizations of
\mathrm{BMO}
. We end the paper with an appendix which gives a sparse domination result for
\mathcal{H}
, similar to that of
[
8
]
, but with the balanced condition on the measure replaced by the weaker sibling balanced condition. This
result implies both the unweighted
L^{p}(\mu)
and weak-type bounds and the weighted bounds for
\mathcal{H}
with the appropriate class of weights,
A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)
.

1.
Characterizing the boundedness of the dyadic Hilbert transform

We first turn our attention to the relationship between the balanced and sibling balanced conditions. Let
\mu
be a Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Here and thereafter, for any sibling balanced measure
\mu
, we assume for simplicity that
0<\mu(I)<\infty
for each
I\in\mathscr{D}
and

\mu[0,\infty)=\infty=\mu[-\infty,0).

The condition that
\mu
is sibling balanced is weaker than having
\mu
balanced since if
\mu
is balanced then

m(I)\sim m(\hat{I})\sim m(I^{s})
for all
I\in\mathscr{D}
.
However, if
\mu
is sibling balanced, then automatically
\mu
is m-increasing. Recall that a Borel measure
\mu
is m-increasing with respect to the dyadic grid
\mathscr{D}
if
m(I)\lesssim m(\hat{I})
for all
I\in\mathscr{D}.
The m-increasing condition characterizes the weak
(1,1)
bound for the different dyadic analog of the Hilbert transform considered in
[
16
]
. However, the reverse implication fails. Moreover, there exists a sibling balanced measure
\mu
which is not balanced. We summarize these observations in the following proposition:

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be sibling balanced. Then
\mu
is m-increasing with
m(I)\lesssim[\mu]_{sib}m(\hat{I})
for all
I\in\mathscr{D}
. On the other hand, there exists an m-increasing measure
\mu
that is not sibling balanced. Moreover, there exists a sibling balanced measure that is not balanced.

Proof.

For the first part, fix
I\in\mathscr{D}
and observe that

\displaystyle m(\hat{I})

\displaystyle\sim\min\{\mu(I),\mu(I^{s}))\}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{m(I),m(I^{s})\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{[\mu]_{sib}}\,m(I),

where we used the fact that
\mu
is sibling balanced in the last step.

To construct a measure on
[0,1]
that is m-increasing but not sibling balanced, we use the second example given in Section 4 of
[
16
]
and leave the verification to the reader.

To exhibit a measure which is sibling balanced but not balanced, we construct a measure which is a sum of an absolutely continuous measure and point masses. Define the following measure on
\mathbb{R}
:

(1.1)

d\mu(x)=\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k}(\delta_{2^{k}}(x)+\delta_{\frac{3}{2}\cdot 2^{k}}(x))\right)+w(x)\,dx

where
\delta_{a}
denotes the Dirac delta mass at
x=a
and

w(x)=\begin{cases}1;&-\infty<x<1\\
2^{-3k};&2^{k}<x<2^{k+1}.\end{cases}

Then one can check
\mu
is sibling balanced, but it is not balanced because
m(I_{k}^{s})\sim 2^{-k}
while the children of
I^{s}_{k}
satisfy
m(I_{k,1}^{s})\sim 2^{-2k}.
Notice that
\mu
is m-increasing, as it must be.

∎

We have the following characterization of
L^{p}
and weak-type
(1,1)
estimates for
\mathcal{H}.

Proposition 1.2
.

The following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{H}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)
for all
1<p<\infty
;

(2)

\mathcal{H}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)
for some
p\neq 2
;

(3)

\mu
is sibling balanced;

(4)

\mathcal{H}
is weak type
(1,1)
.

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

The implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
is trivial, while the implication
(4)\Rightarrow(1)
follows from the fact that
\mathcal{H}
is an isometry on
L^{2}
and anti self-adjoint, interpolation, and duality. The implication
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
can be seen directly from testing on Haar functions and using standard estimates.

The most difficult implication,
(3)\Rightarrow(4)
, can be obtained by following the proof in
[
16
, Theorem 2.11]
. We remark that one can streamline the argument by making use of the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition provided in
[
8
]
. Again, we leave the details to the interested reader.

∎

2.
Commutators for sibling balanced measures

2.1.
Martingale
\mathrm{BMO}

We fix a dyadic grid
\mathscr{D}
that we may assume to be the standard one, that is,

\mathscr{D}=\{2^{-u}[m,m+1)\colon m\in\mathbb{Z},u\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Given
I\in\mathscr{D}
and
u\in\mathbb{N}
,
I^{(u)}
denotes the interval in
\mathscr{D}
that contains
I
with length
2^{u}\ell(I)
, and
\mathscr{D}_{u}(I)=\{J\in\mathscr{D}\colon J^{(u)}=I\}
.

We denote the standard martingale
\mathrm{BMO}
space associated to the filtration generated by
\mathscr{D}
, namely
\{\sigma(\mathscr{D}_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, by
\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
. Its norm is given by

\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}=\sup_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}|b-\langle b\rangle_{\hat{I}}|d\mu.

This is the natural definition of
\mathrm{BMO}
when the underlying martingale filtration is not regular, because it is the one that yields interpolation with the
L^{p}
-scale. Also, whenever
b\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
one has
|\langle b\rangle_{I}-\langle b\rangle_{\hat{I}}|\leq\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}
—something trivial in the regular case—, so that

|\langle b\rangle_{I_{+}}-\langle b\rangle_{I_{-}}|\leq 2\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}.

John-Nirenberg inequality holds for
\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
(see
[
12
]
), taking the following form: there exist constants
C,\delta_{0}
so that for all
b\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
with norm
1
and
I\in\mathscr{D}

(2.1)

\mu(\{x\in I:|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}|>\alpha\})\leq Ce^{-\delta_{0}\alpha}\mu(I),

and also

(2.2)

\mu(\{x\in I:|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{I}|>\alpha\})\leq Ce^{-\delta_{0}\alpha}\mu(I).

(
2.2
) can be easily deduced from (
2.1
) by replacing
\widehat{I}
by
I
and
I
by
I_{+}
and
I_{-}
, respectively, giving us two inequalities which we can then sum. The John-Nirenberg inequality implies

(2.3)

\begin{split}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}&\sim\sup_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}|b-\langle b\rangle_{I}|d\mu+\sup_{I\in\mathscr{D}}|\langle b\rangle_{I_{+}}-\langle b\rangle_{I_{-}}|\\
&\sim\sup_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}|b-\langle b\rangle_{I}|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}+\sup_{I\in\mathscr{D}}|\langle b\rangle_{I_{+}}-\langle b\rangle_{I_{-}}|\\
&=:K_{b}^{p}+D_{b}.\end{split}

2.2.
Upper bounds

In the remainder of the paper, and unless otherwise stated, we assume that
\mu
is a sibling balanced measure. Let
b\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu)
. Writing
b=\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\langle b,h_{I}\rangle h_{I}
and
f=\sum_{J\in\mathscr{D}}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle h_{J}
, we can decompose

(2.4)

\begin{split}b(x)f(x)=\sum_{I,J\in\mathscr{D}}\langle b,h_{I}\rangle\langle f,h_{J}\rangle h_{I}(x)h_{J}(x)=\text{I}+\text{II}+\text{III}\end{split}

where

(2.5)

\begin{split}\text{I}=&\sum_{I}\langle b,h_{I}\rangle\langle f,h_{I}\rangle h_{I}^{2}(x)=:\Delta_{b}f(x),\\
\text{II}=&\sum_{I}\langle b,h_{I}\rangle h_{I}(x)(\sum_{J\colon J\supsetneq I}\langle f,h_{J}\rangle h_{J}(x))=\sum_{I}\langle b,h_{I}\rangle\langle f\rangle_{I}h_{I}(x)=:\pi_{b}f(x),\\
\text{III}=&\sum_{J}\langle f,h_{J}\rangle h_{J}(x)(\sum_{I\colon I\supsetneq J}\langle b,h_{I}\rangle h_{I}(x))=\sum_{I}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{I}h_{I}(x)=\pi_{f}b(x)=:\Lambda_{b}^{0}f(x).\end{split}

The paraproduct decomposition above appears in
[
20
]
. The operator that we denote
\Delta_{b}
plays the role of the operator
\pi_{b}^{(*)}
in their work. Using the decomposition
bf=\Delta_{b}f+\pi_{b}f+\Lambda_{b}^{0}f
, it follows that

(2.6)

\begin{split}[\mathcal{H},b](f):=&\mathcal{H}(bf)-b(\mathcal{H}f)\\
=&[\mathcal{H},\pi_{b}](f)+[\mathcal{H},\Delta_{b}](f)+[\mathcal{H},\Lambda_{b}^{0}](f).\end{split}

We will refer to term
R_{b}(f)=[\mathcal{H},\Lambda_{b}^{0}](f)
as the remainder term. The splitting above allows us to prove the upper estimate in Theorem
A
.

Proof of Theorem
A
, upper estimate.

We start by controlling the remainder term
R_{b}f=[\mathcal{H},\Lambda_{b}^{0}]
. To that end, we write

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(\Lambda_{b}^{0}f)=&\mathcal{H}\left(\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{I}h_{I}\right)=\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{I}\text{sign}(I)h_{I^{s}},\\
\Lambda_{b}^{0}(\mathcal{H}f)=&\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\langle\mathcal{H}f,h_{I}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{I}h_{I}=\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\langle f,h_{I^{s}}\rangle\text{sign}(I^{s})\langle b\rangle_{I}h_{I}=\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{I^{s}}\text{sign}(I)h_{I^{s}}.\end{split}

Therefore,

(2.7)

\begin{split}R_{b}f=\mathcal{H}(\Lambda_{b}^{0}f)-\Lambda_{b}^{0}(\mathcal{H}f)=\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\alpha_{I}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle h_{I^{s}}\end{split}

for a sequence
\{\alpha_{I}=\text{sign}(I)(\langle b\rangle_{I}-\langle b\rangle_{I^{s}})\}
with
\sup_{I}|\alpha_{I}|\leq 2\|b\|_{BMO(\mu)}
, so
R_{b}
is a Haar shift. Therefore, the results in
[
16
]
in the balanced case, and those in Appendix
A
in the —more general— sibling balanced one, imply that
R_{b}
is of weak-type
(1,1)
and bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)
, and moreover

\|[\mathcal{H},\Lambda_{b}^{0}]\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)}\lesssim_{p,[\mu]_{sib}}\|b\|_{BMO(\mu)}.

To control the other two pieces from (
2.6
), we apply
[
20
, Theorem 4.8]
, which in particular implies

\|\Delta_{b}\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)}\sim\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}

and

\|\pi_{b}\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)}\lesssim\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}.

Combining this with the
L^{p}(\mu)
-boundedness of
\mathcal{H}
finishes the proof.
∎

2.3.

Proposition 2.1
.

For

\sup_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}|b-\langle b\rangle_{I}|^{p}d\mu\lesssim_{p,[\mu]_{sib}}\|[\mathcal{H},b]\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)}^{p}.

Proof of Theorem

.

(1)

(2)

for all

We are left with (

nor

2.4
.

The above decomposition leads to

By

,
\|\pi_{b_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu_{k})}\lesssim K_{b_{k}}\sim 2^{k/2}
, and the same holds for the adjoint
\pi_{b_{k}}^{*}
. We claim

\|[\mathcal{H},\Lambda_{b_{k}}]\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu_{k})}\lesssim 1,

which is enough to conclude. First, we observe that

\Lambda_{b_{k}}f=\sum_{I}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\left(\langle b_{k},h_{I}\rangle\int h_{I}^{3}d\mu_{k}+\langle b_{k}\rangle_{I}\right)h_{I}=:\sum_{I}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle c_{I}(b_{k})h_{I}.

\begin{split}[\mathcal{H},\Lambda_{b_{k}}]f=&\mathcal{H}(\Lambda_{b_{k}}f)-\Lambda_{b_{k}}(\mathcal{H}f)\\
=&\sum_{I}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle c_{I}(b_{k})\text{sign}(I)h_{I^{s}}-\Lambda_{b_{k}}(\sum_{I}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\text{sign(I)}h_{I^{s}})\\
=&\sum_{I}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle c_{I}(b_{k})\text{sign}(I)h_{I^{s}}-\sum_{I}\langle f,h_{I^{s}}\rangle\text{sign}(I^{s})c_{I}(b_{k})h_{I}\\
=&\sum_{I}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\text{sign}(I)(c_{I}(b_{k})-c_{I^{s}}(b_{k}))h_{I^{s}}.\end{split}

We next claim that for all
I\in\mathscr{D}
and any measure
\mu
, one has

(2.15)

\sqrt{m(I)}\int h_{I}^{3}(x)d\mu(x)=\frac{\mu(I^{+})-\mu(I^{-})}{\mu(I)}.

Indeed,

\begin{split}m(I)^{1/2}\int h_{I}^{3}d\mu=&m(I)^{2}\left(\int_{I_{-}}\frac{1}{\mu(I_{-})^{3}}d\mu-\int_{I_{+}}\frac{1}{\mu(I_{+})^{3}}d\mu\right)\\

=&\frac{\mu(I_{+})^{2}-\mu(I_{-})^{2}}{\mu(I)^{2}}=\frac{\mu(I_{+})-\mu(I_{-})}{\mu(I)}.\end{split}

From (

), if neither

nor

where in the last equality we used that
{\mu_{k}(I_{+})2^{k}}\sim|I|2^{-k}2^{k}\lesssim 1
. On the other side we have either

2.4.
General Haar shifts

We next sketch how the upper bound in Theorem
A
can be extended to other Haar shift operators. Assume
\mu
is balanced and let
\alpha:=\{\alpha_{J,K}^{I}\}
be a bounded sequence of complex numbers and
u,v\in\mathbb{N}.
We define the Haar shift
T^{u,v,\alpha}
of complexity
(u,v)
as

T^{u,v,\alpha}f:=\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\sum_{J\in\mathscr{D}_{u}(I)}\sum_{K\in\mathscr{D}_{v}(I)}\alpha_{J,K}^{I}\langle f,h_{J}\rangle h_{K},\quad f\in L^{2}(\mu).

A general Haar shift can be decomposed into simpler operators as follows. Given a dyadic interval
I=[2^{-k}p,2^{-k}(p+1))
and an integer
u>0
, let

I_{u}^{m}=[2^{-k}p+2^{-k-u}(m-1),2^{-k}p+2^{-k-u}m),\;1\leq m\leq 2^{u}.

Fix
u,v\geq 0
and
1\leq m\leq 2^{u}
,
1\leq n\leq 2^{v}
, and consider the operator

T_{m,n}^{u,v,\alpha}(f)=\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\alpha^{I}_{I_{u}^{m},I_{v}^{n}}\langle f,h_{I_{u}^{m}}\rangle h_{I_{v}^{n}},\quad f\in L^{2}(\mu),

Clearly,

T^{u,v,\alpha}=\sum_{m=1}^{2^{u}}\sum_{n=1}^{2^{v}}T_{m,n}^{u,v,\alpha}.

Because of that, it is enough to obtain the estimate

\left\|[T_{m,n}^{u,v,\alpha},b]\right\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\to L^{p}(\mu)}\lesssim\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)},

uniformly on
m,n
to get it for a general
T^{u,v,\alpha}
. Call one such operator
T
. We can apply decomposition (
2.6
) to get

[T,b](f)=[T,\pi_{b}](f)+[T,\Delta_{b}](f)+(T\pi_{f}b-\pi_{Tf}b).

The
L^{p}(\mu)
bound for
T
from
[
16
]
implies that we only have to handle the remainder
R_{b}f=T\pi_{f}b-\pi_{Tf}b.
We compute

\displaystyle T\pi_{f}b-\pi_{Tf}b

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{I}T(h_{I})-\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\langle Tf,h_{I}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{I}h_{I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathscr{D}:\\
\exists J\in\mathscr{D}\text{ so }I=J_{u}^{m}\end{subarray}}\alpha^{J}_{J_{u}^{m},J_{v}^{n}}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{I}h_{J_{v}^{n}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathscr{D}:\\
\exists J\in\mathscr{D}\text{ so }I=J_{v}^{n}\end{subarray}}\alpha^{J}_{J_{u}^{m},J_{v}^{n}}\langle f,h_{J_{u}^{m}}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{I}h_{I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\mathscr{D}}\alpha^{J}_{J_{u}^{m},J_{v}^{n}}\langle f,h_{J_{u}^{m}}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{J_{u}^{m}}h_{J_{v}^{n}}-\sum_{J\in\mathscr{D}}\alpha^{J}_{J_{u}^{m},J_{v}^{n}}\langle f,h_{J_{u}^{m}}\rangle\langle b\rangle_{J_{v}^{n}}h_{J_{v}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\mathscr{D}}\beta^{J}_{J_{u}^{m},J_{v}^{n}}\langle f,h_{J_{u}^{m}}\rangle h_{J_{v}^{n}},

where
\beta^{J}_{J_{u}^{m},J_{v}^{n}}=\alpha^{J}_{J_{u}^{m},J_{v}^{n}}(\langle b\rangle_{J_{u}^{m}}-\langle b\rangle_{J_{v}^{n}})
. It is clear that

\sup_{J\in\mathscr{D}}|\beta^{J}_{J_{u}^{m},J_{v}^{n}}|\leq(u+v)\|b\|_{\text{BMO}(\mu)},

whereby the estimate

\|R_{b}f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}\lesssim\|b\|_{\text{BMO}(\mu)}\|T\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}

easily follows.

We emphasize that this upper bound for the
L^{p}
norm of the commutator
[T,b]
in terms of the BMO norm seems to be new even for the case where
T
is the classical dyadic Hilbert transform given by
\mathcal{S}f=\sum_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\langle f,h_{I}\rangle(h_{I_{-}}-h_{I_{+}})
. Endpoint behavior results for
[\mathcal{S},b]
can be found in
[
3
, Corollary 5.9]
under the assumption that
\mu
is
m
-increasing.

3.
Weights, commutators and
\mathrm{BMO}

3.1.
A new weight class

The class
\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
is smaller in general than
A_{p}^{bal}(\mu)
as we will show in Subsection
3.3
for the particular case
p=2
for simplicity. We take advantage of that to characterize
\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
in terms of weights. The results in this subsection hold for any locally finite measure
\mu
which is non-zero on dyadic intervals; the balanced assumption plays no role in the analysis. We begin the proof of
Theorem
B
, starting with
the proof that the exponential of a
\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
function belongs to
\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty.
There exist independent constants
C_{1},C_{2}
, so that if
b\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)
, and
0<\delta<\frac{C_{1}\min\{1,p-1\}}{\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}}
,
e^{\delta b}\in\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
with

[e^{\delta b}]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}\leq C_{2}.

Proof.

By rescaling, we may assume that
\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}=1.
Let
\delta_{0}
a parameter for which the John-Nirenberg inequality holds as in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). We claim that for any
0<\delta\leq\frac{\delta_{0}\min\{1,p-1\}}{2}
the function
e^{\delta b}\in\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
with uniformly bounded
\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
characteristic, by which the conclusion of the lemma follows. To this end, we estimate using (
2.1
):

\displaystyle\int_{I}e^{\delta b(x)}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{I}e^{\delta(b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}})}\,d\mu(x)\cdot e^{\delta\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}}

\displaystyle=e^{\delta\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{x\in I:e^{\delta(b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}})}>\alpha\})\,d\alpha

\displaystyle\leq e^{\delta\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}}\left(\mu(I)+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mu(\{x\in I:e^{\delta|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}|}>\alpha\})\,d\alpha\right)

\displaystyle=e^{\delta\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}}\left(\mu(I)+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mu(\{x\in I:|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}|>\frac{\log\alpha}{\delta}\})\,d\alpha\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{\delta\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}}\mu(I)\left(1+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\log\alpha}{\delta}\delta_{0}}\,d\alpha\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{\delta\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}}\mu(I),

as long as
\frac{\delta_{0}}{\delta}>1
(which is guaranteed by our hypothesis on
\delta
). Moreover, the implied constant is independent of
\delta
and we conclude

\langle e^{\delta b(x)}\rangle_{I}\lesssim e^{\delta\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}}.

Entirely similarly, we can also obtain the bounds

\langle e^{\delta b(x)}\rangle_{\widehat{I}}\lesssim e^{\delta\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}},\quad\langle e^{-\delta b(x)/(p-1)}\rangle_{I}^{p-1}\lesssim e^{-\delta\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}},\quad\langle e^{-\delta b(x)/(p-1)}\rangle_{\widehat{I}}^{p-1}\lesssim e^{-\delta\langle b\rangle_{\widehat{I}}}

with constants independent of
\delta.
These estimates together prove the desired estimates for
[e^{\delta b}]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}.

∎

We can obtain a partial converse of Lemma
3.1
for general
A_{2}(\mu)
weights, and a full converse in the case
\omega
belongs to the stronger class
\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu).

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\omega\in A_{2}(\mu)
, then
\log\omega
satisfies

\sup_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\langle|\log\omega-\langle\log\omega\rangle_{I}|\rangle_{I}<\infty.

In particular, the function
K_{\log\omega}^{2}<\infty
(as defined in (
2.3
)), and if instead we assume the stronger condition
\omega\in\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)
, then
\log\omega\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mu).

Proof.

For the first part, the classical proof works here without modification. Indeed, by Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle\text{exp}\left(\langle|\log\omega-\langle\log\omega\rangle_{I}|\rangle_{I}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{exp}\left(\langle|\log\omega+\langle\log\omega^{-1}\rangle_{I}|\rangle_{I}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\text{exp}\left(|\log\omega+\langle\log\omega^{-1}\rangle_{I}|\right)\right\rangle_{I}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\text{exp}\left(\log\omega+\langle\log\omega^{-1}\rangle_{I}\right)\right\rangle_{I}+\left\langle\text{exp}\left(-\log\omega-\langle\log\omega^{-1}\rangle_{I}\right)\right\rangle_{I}

\displaystyle=\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\cdot\text{exp}\left(\langle\log\omega^{-1}\rangle_{I}\right)+\langle\omega^{-1}\rangle_{I}\cdot\text{exp}\left(\langle\log\omega\rangle_{I}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\langle\omega^{-1}\rangle_{I}

\displaystyle\leq 2[\omega]_{A_{2}(\mu)}.

If we instead assume
\omega\in\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)
, the second assertion directly follows from the above computation, replacing
\langle\log\omega\rangle_{I}
by
\langle\log\omega\rangle_{\widehat{I}}
in the first expression.
∎

Theorem
B
, part
(a)
is a direct corollary of Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
. We next focus on part
(b)
. The following straightforward property turns out to be crucial, because it lets us get around the lack of a doubling property for
\mu
in stopping time arguments. If
\omega\in\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
,
\lambda>0
and there exists
I\in\mathscr{D}
maximal such that
\langle\omega\rangle_{I}>\lambda
, then

(3.1)

\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\leq[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}\lambda.

Indeed, using the
\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
condition, Hölder’s inequality, and maximality we have

\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\leq\frac{[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}}{\langle\sigma\rangle_{\widehat{I}}^{p-1}}\leq[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}\langle\omega\rangle_{\widehat{I}}\leq[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}\lambda.

Proof of Theorem
B
, part
(b)
.

We follow an argument which is similar to the one presented in
[
14
, Lemma 2.2]
and will not concern ourselves with sharp constants here. It is straightforward to check that if
\omega\in A_{p}(\mu)\supset\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
for some
1<p<\infty
, then

(3.2)

\sup_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\frac{(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I}))(I)}{\omega(I)}=\sup_{I\in\mathscr{D}}\frac{\int_{I}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})\,d\mu}{\omega(I)}=:[\omega]_{A_{\infty}(\mu)}<\infty.

This property of
A_{p}(\mu)
weights does not rely on
\mu
being dyadically doubling, only the boundedness of the (unweighted) dyadic maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}
on
L^{p^{\prime}}(\sigma)
for
\sigma\in A_{p^{\prime}}(\mu).
Indeed, assuming such a weighted bound for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{I}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{I}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})^{p^{\prime}}\sigma\,d\mu\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\omega(I)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\omega}\left(\int_{I}\omega^{p^{\prime}}\sigma\,d\mu\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\omega(I)^{1/p}=\omega(I),

which proves (
3.2
). The weighted bound for the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}:L^{q}(\sigma)\rightarrow L^{q}(\sigma)
for
q=p^{\prime}
can be seen by observing the pointwise bound

\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}f(x_{0})\leq[\sigma]_{A_{q}}^{1/(q-1)}\left\{\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}^{\omega}(f\omega^{-1}))^{q-1})(x_{0})\right\}^{1/(q-1)},

and then applying the bounds for the weighted maximal functions
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}^{\sigma}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}^{\omega}
on
L^{q^{\prime}}(\sigma)
and
L^{q}(\omega)
, respectively. Next, using the trivial bound
\omega(x)\leq\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})(x)
for
\mu
-almost every
x\in I
, we can reduce our goal to showing

(3.3)

\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}[\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})]^{\gamma}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\gamma}\lesssim_{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\omega\,d\mu\right)

for some
\gamma>1
. Set
E_{\lambda}=\{x\in I:\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})(x)>\lambda\}
. We write, using the distribution function and (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\int_{I}[\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})]^{\gamma}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\gamma-1)\lambda^{\gamma-2}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})\left(E_{\lambda}\right)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\langle\omega\rangle_{I}}(\gamma-1)\lambda^{\gamma-2}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})\left(I\right)\,d\lambda+\int_{\langle\omega\rangle_{I}}^{\infty}(\gamma-1)\lambda^{\gamma-2}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})\left(E_{\lambda}\right)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq[\omega]_{A_{\infty}(\mu)}\omega(I)\langle\omega\rangle_{I}^{\gamma-1}+(\gamma-1)\int_{\langle\omega\rangle_{I}}^{\infty}\lambda^{\gamma-2}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})\left(E_{\lambda}\right)\,d\lambda.

Let
J_{j}^{\lambda}
be the collection of maximal intervals so that
\langle\omega\rangle_{J_{j}^{\lambda}}>\lambda
, and when
\lambda>\langle\omega\rangle_{I}
, these
J_{j}^{\lambda}
are contained in
I
and cover
E_{\lambda}.
Crucially using (
3.1
), we have

(3.4)

\omega(J_{j}^{\lambda})\leq[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}\lambda\mu(J_{j}^{\lambda}).

Then, we estimate the remaining integral on the right hand side as follows, using estimate (
3.2
) again as well as (
3.4
):

\displaystyle(\gamma-1)\int_{\langle\omega\rangle_{I}}^{\infty}\lambda^{\gamma-2}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})\left(E_{\lambda}\right)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=(\gamma-1)\int_{\langle\omega\rangle_{I}}^{\infty}\lambda^{\gamma-2}\sum_{j}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})\left(J_{j}^{\lambda}\right)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq[\omega]_{A_{\infty}(\mu)}(\gamma-1)\int_{\langle\omega\rangle_{I}}^{\infty}\lambda^{\gamma-2}\sum_{j}\omega(J_{j}^{\lambda})\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq[\omega]_{A_{\infty}(\mu)}[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}(\gamma-1)\int_{\langle\omega\rangle_{I}}^{\infty}\lambda^{\gamma-1}\sum_{j}\mu(J_{j}^{\lambda})\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=[\omega]_{A_{\infty}(\mu)}[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}(\gamma-1)\int_{\langle\omega\rangle_{I}}^{\infty}\lambda^{\gamma-1}\mu(E_{\lambda})\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq[\omega]_{A_{\infty}(\mu)}[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\right)\int_{I}[\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})]^{\gamma}\,d\mu.

Choosing
\gamma
sufficiently close to
1
, we can guarantee that

[\omega]_{A_{\infty}(\mu)}[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\right)<\frac{1}{2},

which then implies

\int_{I}[\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}(\omega\mathbbm{1}_{I})]^{\gamma}\,d\mu\leq 2[\omega]_{A_{\infty}(\mu)}\omega(I)\langle\omega\rangle_{I}^{\gamma-1},

completing the proof.

∎

3.2.
Weighted estimates for
[\mathcal{H},b]

We are ready to prove Theorem
C
. In this subsection, we go back to assuming that
\mu
is sibling balanced and also assume
\mu
is atomless for technical reasons.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

We assume
p=2
, but the case
p\neq 2
can be handled in a similar manner with only minor modifications. The essential idea is to apply the Cauchy Integral trick, which is used in
[
4
]
for weighted estimates and was first used in
[
7
]
. Consider the analytic family of operators

\mathcal{H}_{z}f=e^{zb}\mathcal{H}(e^{-zb}f).

If
|z|
is sufficiently small, Lemma
3.1
shows
e^{|z|b}
belongs to
\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)
, and hence
\mathcal{H}_{z}
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\mu)
(see Appendix
A
). We wish to show now that
[\mathcal{H},b]
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\omega d\mu).
For sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
depending on
\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}
, and bounded, compactly supported
f
, the function
\mathcal{H}_{z}(f)
is analytic in the variable
z
, and thus the Cauchy integral formula gives

[b,\mathcal{H}]f=\frac{d}{dz}\biggr\lvert_{z=0}\left(\mathcal{H}_{z}(f)\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=\varepsilon}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{z}(f)}{z^{2}}\,dz.

We will show that we can choose
\varepsilon>0
to be equal to
\frac{C(\omega,\mu)}{\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}}
, where
C(\omega,\mu)
depends on
[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)}
and
[\mu]_{sib}.
In particular, suppose that we can show for this choice of
\varepsilon
, there holds

(3.5)

\sup_{|z|=\varepsilon}\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{2}(\omega e^{2\text{Re}(z)\,b})\rightarrow L^{2}(\omega e^{2\text{Re}(z)\,b})}\lesssim C(\omega,\mu).

Then, by Minkowski’s integral inequality, we can estimate

\displaystyle\|[b,\mathcal{H}]f\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|z|=\varepsilon}\frac{\|\mathcal{H}_{z}f\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,|dz|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sup_{|z|=\varepsilon}\|\mathcal{H}_{z}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\omega)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sup_{|z|=\varepsilon}\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{2}(\omega e^{2\text{Re}(z)b})\rightarrow L^{2}(\omega e^{2\text{Re}(z)b})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\omega,\mu)\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},

completing the proof that
[\mathcal{H},b]
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\omega).

Again, to prove (
3.5
) it is sufficient to observe that for this choice of
\varepsilon
and
|z|=\varepsilon
,
\omega e^{2\text{Re}(z)\,b}\in\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)
with weight characteristic uniformly bounded (in
z
) by some
C(\omega,\mu).
Since
\omega,\omega^{-1}\in\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)
by hypothesis, both satisfy a reverse Hölder inequality by Theorem
B
. Thus, there exists
\gamma>1
depending on
[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)}
so that for all
I\in\mathscr{D}

(3.6)

\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\omega^{\gamma}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\gamma}\lesssim_{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\omega\,d\mu\right),\quad\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\omega^{-\gamma}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\gamma}\lesssim_{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\omega^{-1}\,d\mu\right).

Now, choose
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small so that for any
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, the exponent
2\varepsilon\gamma^{\prime}
satisfies the conclusion of Lemma
3.1
. Note that
\varepsilon_{0}\sim_{\omega}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}^{-1}
. Therefore, if

|z|=\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, we estimate, applying Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
and
J\in\{I,\widehat{I}\}
:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\omega e^{2\text{Re}(z)\,b}\,d\mu\right)\left(\frac{1}{\mu(J)}\int_{J}\omega^{-1}e^{-2\text{Re}(z)\,b}\,d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\omega^{\gamma}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(J)}\int_{J}\omega^{-\gamma}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\gamma}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}e^{2\gamma^{\prime}\text{Re}(z)\ b}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\gamma^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(J)}\int_{J}e^{-2\gamma^{\prime}\text{Re}(z)\ b}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\gamma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\omega,\mu)\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\omega\,d\mu\right)\left(\frac{1}{\mu(J)}\int_{J}\omega^{-1}\,d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}e^{2\gamma^{\prime}\text{Re}(z)\ b}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\gamma^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(J)}\int_{J}e^{-2\gamma^{\prime}\text{Re}(z)\ b}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\gamma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\omega,\mu)[e^{2\gamma^{\prime}\text{Re}(z)\,b}]_{\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)}^{1/\gamma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\omega,\mu).

Obviously, the same estimates hold with the roles of
I
and
J
interchanged. We have shown
\omega e^{2\text{Re}(z)\,b}\in\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)
with

\sup_{|z|=\varepsilon}[\omega e^{2\text{Re}(z)\,b}]_{\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)}\lesssim C(\omega,\mu).

By Proposition
3.6
one can also bound
[\omega e^{2\text{Re}(z)b}]_{A_{2}^{sib}(\mu)}
uniformly in
|z|=\varepsilon

which combined with Corollary
A.4
implies (
3.5
). That concludes the proof by the inequalities immediately following (
3.5
).

∎

3.3.
Comparison between different weight classes

Proposition 3.5
.

If
\mu
is balanced, there holds

(3.7)

A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)=A_{p}^{bal}(\mu).

Proof.

First, notice for the configurations
I=J
,
I=(\widehat{J})^{s}
or
J=(\widehat{I})^{s}
, the balanced condition gives
c_{p}(I,J)\sim\frac{m(I)^{p/2}m(J)^{p/2}}{\mu(I)^{p-1}\mu(J)}
, with constant depending on
[\mu]_{bal}.
This observation proves
A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)\subseteq A_{p}^{bal}(\mu).
To show the reverse containment, it remains to check the additional configuration where
\widehat{I}=\widehat{J}^{s}
that arises in the
A_{p}^{sib}
condition. We can bound this term via a case analysis. If
\mu(\hat{I})\leq 2\mu(I)
, then we can estimate, since
m(\hat{J})\sim m(J)
as well:

\displaystyle c_{p}(I,J)\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle_{J}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{m(\hat{I})^{p/2}m(J)^{p/2}}{\mu(\hat{I})^{p-1}\mu(J)}\langle\omega\rangle_{\hat{I}}\langle\sigma\rangle_{J}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq[\omega]_{A_{p}^{bal}(\mu)}.

If
\mu(\hat{J})\leq 2\mu(J)
instead, we follow a similar argument. If neither of these inequalities hold, we have
\mu(I)\sim m(\hat{I})\sim m(\hat{J})\sim\mu(J)
. Let
I^{g}
denote the dyadic parent of
\widehat{I}.
Then since
m(\hat{I})
is comparable to
m(I^{g})
, which is in turn comparable to
\min\{\mu(\hat{I}),\mu(\hat{J})\},
this implies either
\mu(\hat{I})\sim\mu(I)
or
\mu(\hat{J})\sim\mu(J)
, with implicit constant only depending on
[\mu]_{bal}.
Then we can argue as above.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mu
be sibling balanced. The containment

\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)\subseteq A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)

holds, and in particular for
\omega\in\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)
,

[\omega]_{A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)}\lesssim[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}^{3},

where the implicit constant is independent of
\omega,\mu.

Therefore, if
\mu
satisfies the stronger balanced condition, we get the corresponding result for
A_{p}^{bal}(\mu)
in place of
A_{p}^{sib}(\mu).

Proof.

Let
w\in\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu).
Since
c_{p}(I,J)\lesssim 1
always, to verify (
Introduction
) it suffices to show

(3.8)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathscr{D}:\\
J\in\{I,I_{-}^{s},I_{+}^{s}\}\text{ or }\\
I\in\{J,J_{-}^{s},J_{+}^{s}\}\text{ or }\\
\widehat{I}=\widehat{J}^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle_{J}^{p-1}<\infty.

We will only verify the case corresponding to
\widehat{I}=\widehat{J}^{s}
; the other cases can be handled by a similar argument. We have, using Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle_{J}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle_{\widehat{I}}^{p-1}\langle\omega\rangle_{\widehat{I}}\langle\sigma\rangle_{J}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}\langle\omega\rangle_{\widehat{I}}\langle\sigma\rangle_{J}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}\langle\omega\rangle_{\widehat{I}}\langle\sigma\rangle_{\widehat{I}^{s}}^{p-1}\langle\omega\rangle_{\widehat{I}^{s}}\langle\sigma\rangle_{J}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}^{2}\langle\omega\rangle_{\widehat{I}}\langle\sigma\rangle_{\widehat{I}^{s}}^{p-1}.

Now, if
I^{g}
denotes the dyadic parent of
\widehat{I}
, we know that either
\mu(I^{g})\leq 2\mu(\widehat{I})
or
\mu(I^{g})\leq 2\mu(\widehat{I}^{s}).
In the former case, we have
\langle\omega\rangle_{\widehat{I}}\leq 2\langle\omega\rangle_{I^{g}}
, while in the latter, we get
\langle\omega\rangle_{\widehat{I}^{s}}^{p-1}\lesssim 2^{p-1}\langle\omega\rangle_{I^{g}}^{p-1}.
In either case, the last line in the display above is controlled by
2^{\max\{1,p-1\}}[\omega]_{\widehat{A}_{p}(\mu)}^{3}
, which completes the proof.
∎

The next result is substantially more delicate, and demonstrates that for general balanced
\mu
, the containment
\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)\subset A_{2}^{bal}(\mu)
may be strict (even if
\mu
is assumed to be atomless). Therefore,
\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)
defines a genuinely different class of weights in the category of atomless balanced measures.

Theorem 3.7
.

There exists a balanced, atomless measure
\mu
and weight
\omega\in A_{2}^{bal}(\mu)
such that
\omega\notin\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu).
Consequently, we have the strict containment
\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)\subsetneq A_{2}^{bal}(\mu)
for this particular measure.

Proof.

Fix an even positive integer
n\geq 2
. We will construct a preliminary measure
\mu_{n}
and weight function
\omega_{n}
on
[0,1]
so that
\mu_{n}
is balanced with
[\mu_{n}]_{bal}\sim 1
,
[\omega_{n}]_{\widehat{A}_{2}}\gtrsim\sqrt{n}
, but
[\omega_{n}]_{A_{2}^{bal}(\mu_{n})}\lesssim 1.
Then, we will show how these sequences of measures and weight can be used to build
\mu,\omega
satisfying the conclusion of the theorem.

Set
I_{k}=[0,2^{-k}),
and let
I_{k}^{s}=[2^{-k},2^{-k+1})
denote its dyadic sibling. We set
\mu_{n}(I_{0})=1
,
\mu_{n}(I_{1})=\mu_{n}(I_{1}^{s})=\frac{1}{2}
, and define sequences

b_{k}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{k^{2}},&2\leq k\leq n;\\
\frac{1}{n^{2}},&k>n.\end{cases},\,\text{and},\quad a_{k}=1-b_{k}.

For
k\geq 2
we then inductively define
\mu_{n}(I_{k})=a_{k}\mu_{n}(I_{k-1})
, so that
\mu_{n}(I_{k}^{s})=b_{k}\mu_{n}(I_{k-1}).
This construction defines a Borel measure
\mu
on
[0,1]
which is uniform on each interval
I_{k}^{s}
(see
[
16
]
or
[
8
]
).

On account of the convergence of the infinite product
\prod_{j=2}^{\infty}(1-\frac{1}{j^{2}})
to a nonzero value, one can check that
\mu_{n}(I_{k})\sim 1
for all
k\leq n
and
\mu_{n}(I_{k}^{s})\sim\frac{1}{k^{2}}
for the same range of
k
, with implied constant independent of
n
. Moreover, for
k\leq n
we also have

m(I_{k})\sim m(I_{k}^{s})\sim m(\widehat{I_{k}})\sim\frac{1}{k^{2}}.

On the other hand, notice for
k>n
,
\mu_{n}(I_{k})\sim(\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}})^{k-n}
while
\mu_{n}(I_{k}^{s})\sim(\frac{1}{n^{2}})(\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}})^{(k-n-1)}
. In this case,

m(I_{k})\sim m(I_{k}^{s})\sim m(\widehat{I_{k}})\sim\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}}\right)^{(k-n-1)}.

We only need to check such intervals to verify the balanced condition because
\mu_{n}
is uniform on
I_{k}^{s}
for all
k.
Moreover, since
\mu(I_{k})\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
,
\mu
is atomless.

Next, we define the relevant weight function
\omega_{n}
on
[0,1].
Define

\omega_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}},&x\in I_{k}^{s},k\leq n/2\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-k+1}},&x\in I_{k}^{s},n/2<k\leq n\\
1,&x\in I_{k}^{s},k>n.\end{cases}

We first claim that
[\omega_{n}]_{\widehat{A}_{2}}\gtrsim\sqrt{n}.
To see this, note that

\displaystyle\langle\omega_{n}^{-1}\rangle_{I_{n/2}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n/2},

while on the other hand, we have

\langle\omega_{n}\rangle_{\widehat{I_{n/2}}}\gtrsim 1,

since
\mu_{n}(\widehat{I_{n/2}})\sim 1
and
\int_{\widehat{I_{n/2}}}\omega_{n}\,d\mu_{n}\geq\mu_{n}(I_{n})\sim 1.

This proves the desired estimate on
[\omega_{n}]_{\widehat{A}_{2}}
.

Next, we assert
\omega_{n}\in A_{2}(\mu_{n})
with
[\omega_{n}]_{A_{2}(\mu_{n})}\sim 1.
It is obvious that since
\omega_{n}
is constant on
I_{k}^{s}
for all
k,
and
I_{k}
with
k>n
, we only need to check the
A_{2}
condition on intervals of the form
I_{k}
with
k\leq n.
As before,
\langle\omega_{n}\rangle_{I_{k}}\leq 1,
and we also see

\displaystyle\langle\omega_{n}^{-1}\rangle_{I_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{k}}\omega_{n}^{-1}\,d\mu_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>k:\\
j\leq n/2\end{subarray}}\sqrt{j}\mu_{n}(I_{j}^{s})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>k:\\
n/2<j\leq n\end{subarray}}\sqrt{n-j+1}\mu_{n}(I_{j}^{s})+\sum_{j>n}\mu_{n}(I_{j}^{s})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{j^{3/2}}+\frac{n\sqrt{n}}{n^{2}}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left(\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}}\right)^{j}\lesssim 1,

so
\omega_{n}\in A_{2}(\mu_{n}).

Finally, we need to check the additional conditions for membership in
A_{2}^{bal}(\mu_{n}).
We need to control

\frac{m(I)m(J)}{\mu_{n}(I)\mu_{n}(J)}\langle\omega_{n}\rangle_{I}\langle\omega_{n}^{-1}\rangle_{J}

for the combinations

I=I_{k}^{s},J=I_{k+1}^{s},\quad I=I_{k}^{s},J=I_{k+1},\quad I=I_{k},J=I_{k,-}^{s},
and
\quad I=I_{k},J=I_{k,+}^{s}
as well as the combinations with
I
and
J
interchanged in this list. For all of the combinations, we can assume
k\leq n
, for otherwise
\omega_{n}
is identically constant on the two intervals.

Note that
\langle\omega_{n}\rangle_{I}\leq 1
for any dyadic interval
I
, and by our earlier computations,
\langle\omega_{n}^{-1}\rangle_{J}\lesssim 1
unless
J=I_{k}^{s}
with
1\leq k\leq n
or
J
is a child of
I_{k}^{s}
. Then we estimate for the case
J=I_{k}^{s}
and
I=I_{k+1}^{s}
:

\displaystyle\frac{m(I_{k}^{s})m(I_{k+1}^{s})}{\mu_{n}(I_{k}^{s})\mu_{n}(I_{k+1}^{s})}\langle\omega_{n}\rangle_{I_{k+1}^{s}}\langle\omega_{n}^{-1}\rangle_{I_{k}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle\omega_{n}\rangle_{I_{k+1}^{s}}\langle\omega_{n}^{-1}\rangle_{I_{k}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

while in the case
I=I_{k+1}
instead,

\displaystyle\frac{m(I_{k}^{s})m(I_{k+1})}{\mu_{n}(I_{k}^{s})\mu_{n}(I_{k+1})}\langle\omega_{n}\rangle_{I_{k+1}}\langle\omega_{n}^{-1}\rangle_{I_{k}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{m(I_{k+1})}{\mu_{n}(I_{k+1})}\langle\omega_{n}^{-1}\rangle_{I_{k}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{k^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{k}\lesssim 1.

The cases when
J
is a child of
I_{k}^{s}
may be handled similarly. These estimates prove
\omega_{n}\in A_{2}^{bal}(\mu_{n})
with
[\omega_{n}]_{A_{2}^{bal}}\sim 1
, which completes the proof of the claim.

Now, to construct a single measure
\mu
and weight function
\omega
satisfying the conclusion of the lemma, we define
\mu
on the half line
[0,\infty)
using the
\mu_{n}
. In particular, let
u_{n}
be the density function for
\mu_{2(n+1)}
(which is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure) as constructed above for
n\geq 1
on
[0,1]
, and define
\mu
according to the density

d\mu(x)=u(x)\,dx,\quad u(x)=u_{0}(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x),

where
u_{0}(x)
is the positive density function on
[0,1]
of the measure
\mu_{2}
, and for
n\geq 1
,
f_{n}
is the positive function supported on
[2^{n-1},2^{n})
defined by

f_{n}(x)=u_{n}\left(\frac{x-2^{n-1}}{2^{n-1}}\right).

It is a simple matter to check that
\mu
is balanced, since each
\mu_{n}
is balanced with
[\mu_{n}]_{bal}\sim 1.
Next, we build
\omega
from the weights
\omega_{n}
appropriately. In particular, we can define

\omega(x)=\omega_{2}(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{n}(x),

where

\nu_{n}(x)=\omega_{2(n+1)}\left(\frac{x-2^{n-1}}{2^{n-1}}\right),n\geq 1.

From the computations above, it is easy to see
\omega\notin\widehat{A}_{2}(\mu)
, but
\omega\in A_{2}^{bal}(\mu).
This completes the construction.

∎

Lemma A.1

(Iteration step)
.

We have

\displaystyle|\sum_{J\in\mathcal{G}(I)}

\displaystyle\langle f_{1},h_{J_{+}}\rangle\langle f_{2},h_{J_{-}}\rangle-\langle f_{1},h_{J_{-}}\rangle\langle f_{2},h_{J_{+}}\rangle|\lesssim[\mu]_{sib}^{1/2}\langle f_{1}\rangle_{I}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{I}\mu(I)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S,T\in\mathcal{B}^{1}(I):\\
\hat{S}=(\hat{T})^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f_{1}\rangle_{S}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{T}m(\hat{S})^{1/2}{m(\hat{T})}^{1/2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S,T\in\mathcal{B}(I):\\
{S}=T^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f_{1}\rangle_{S}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{T}m({S})^{1/2}{m({T})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{B}(I),T\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(I)\\
\text{ or }S,T\in\mathcal{B}(I),\\
\text{ and }S=(\hat{T})^{s}\end{subarray}}(\langle f_{1}\rangle_{S}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{T}+\langle f_{1}\rangle_{T}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{S})m({S})^{1/2}{m(\hat{T})}^{1/2}.

Proof.

One has

\mathrm{LHS}\leq|\sum_{J\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\langle f_{1},h_{J_{+}}\rangle\langle f_{2},h_{J_{-}}\rangle|+|\sum_{J\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\langle f_{1},h_{J_{-}}\rangle\langle f_{2},h_{J_{+}}\rangle|=:\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B}.

Both terms are handled similarly so let us focus on A. We pick
\lambda_{i}=16\langle f_{i}\rangle_{I}
,
i=1,2
in the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition above applied to

\begin{split}|\sum_{J\in\mathcal{G}(I)}&\langle f_{1},h_{J_{+}}\rangle\langle f_{2},h_{J_{-}}\rangle|\leq|\sum_{J\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\langle g_{1},h_{J_{+}}\rangle\langle g_{2},h_{J_{-}}\rangle|+|\sum_{J\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\langle b_{1},h_{J_{+}}\rangle\langle g_{2},h_{J_{-}}\rangle|\\
&+|\sum_{J\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\langle g_{1},h_{J_{+}}\rangle\langle b_{2},h_{J_{-}}\rangle|+|\sum_{J\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\langle b_{1},h_{J_{+}}\rangle\langle b_{2},h_{J_{-}}\rangle|\\
&=:\mathrm{(GG)}+\mathrm{(BG)}+\mathrm{(GB)}+\mathrm{(BB)}.\end{split}

The
L^{2}
-bound for
g_{i}
yields
\mathrm{(GG)}\lesssim\langle f_{1}\rangle_{I}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{I}\mu(I)
. Let us estimate (BG). First,
\langle b_{1,j},h_{J_{+}}\rangle=0
unless
J_{+}\in\{I_{j},\hat{I_{j}}\}

\text{supp}(b_{j})=\hat{I}_{j}
se we only need to consider
J
so that
J_{+}\cap\hat{I}_{j}\neq\emptyset

J_{+}\subseteq\hat{I}_{j}
or
\hat{I}_{j}\subsetneq J_{+}
. In the latter case, one gets zero because
h_{J_{+}}
is constant in
\text{supp}(b_{1,j})
, which has integral zero. If
J_{+}\subseteq I_{j}^{s}
, we get zero for similar reasons. We are left with
J_{+}=\hat{I}_{j}
or
J_{+}=I_{j}
, for
J_{+}\subsetneq I_{j}
is impossible beacause
J\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{(BG)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in\mathcal{G}(I):\\
J_{+}\in\{I_{j},\hat{I}_{j}\}\end{subarray}}|\langle b_{1,j},h_{J_{+}}\rangle||\langle g_{2},h_{J_{-}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in\mathcal{G}(I):\\
J_{+}\in\{I_{j},\hat{I}_{j}\}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m(J_{+})}}\|b_{1,j}\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\sqrt{m(J_{-})}\|g_{2}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in\mathcal{G}(I):\\
J_{+}\in\{I_{j},\hat{I}_{j}\}\end{subarray}}\frac{\sqrt{m(J_{-})}}{\sqrt{m(J_{+})}}\int_{I_{j}}f_{1}d\mu\,\langle f_{2}\rangle_{I}

\displaystyle\lesssim[\mu]_{sib}^{1/2}\sum_{j}\int_{I_{j}}f_{1}d\mu\,\langle f_{2}\rangle_{I}\lesssim[\mu]_{sib}^{1/2}\langle f_{1}\rangle_{I}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{I}\mu(I),

using the sibling balanced condition and the disjointness of

. The term (GB) is estimated similarly to (BG). To estimate (BB), the only intervals

\mathrm{(BB)}_{1}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S,T\in\mathcal{B}(I)\colon\\

{S}={T}^{s}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{m({S})}\sqrt{m({T})}\langle f_{1}\rangle_{S}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{T}.\end{split}

Finally,

A family of intervals
\mathcal{S}
is said to be
\eta
-sparse if for each
I\in\mathcal{S}
the exists
E_{I}\subset I
such that
\mu(E_{I})\geq\eta\mu(I)
and the family
\{E_{I}\}
is pairwise disjoint. Since
\mu
is non-atomic,
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
implies that
\mathcal{S}
being
\eta
-sparse is equivalent to the Carleson packing condition

\sum_{I\supsetneq J\in\mathcal{S}}\mu(J)\leq\frac{1}{\eta}\mu(I),\quad\mbox{for all }I\in\mathcal{S}.

If
\mathcal{S}
is sparse, we define

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(f_{1},f_{2}):=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{S}}\langle f_{1}\rangle_{I}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{I}\mu(I),

and the non-standard forms

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{S}}_{1}(f_{1},f_{2}):=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{S}\\

\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(f_{1},f_{2}):=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{S}\\
{I}=(\hat{J})^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f_{1}\rangle_{I}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{J}\,m(I)^{1/2}m(\hat{J})^{1/2},\\
\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{S}}_{3}(f_{1},f_{2}):=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{S}\\
{J}=(\hat{I})^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f_{1}\rangle_{I}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{J}\,m(\hat{I})^{1/2}m({J})^{1/2}.\end{split}

Theorem A.2

(Sparse Domination for
\mathcal{H}
)
.

If
\mu
is sibling balanced and atomless, there exists
\eta\in(0,1)
such that for each pair
(f_{1},f_{2})
of nonnegative compactly supported bounded functions there exists an
\eta
-sparse collection
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathscr{D}
such that

|\langle\mathcal{H}f_{1},f_{2}\rangle|\lesssim[\mu]_{sib}^{1/2}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(f_{1},f_{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{\mathcal{S}}(f_{1},f_{2}).

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{S}}_{4}(f_{1},f_{2}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{S}\colon I=J^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f_{1}\rangle_{I}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{J}\,m(I)^{1/2}m(J)^{1/2}.

Following the proof scheme of
[
8
, Theorem 2.3]
, Lemma
A.1
implies that there exists
\eta_{0}\in(0,1)
and an
\eta_{0}
-sparse family
\mathcal{S}_{0}
such that

|\langle\mathcal{H}f_{1},f_{2}\rangle|\lesssim[\mu]_{sib}^{1/2}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{0}}(f_{1},f_{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{4}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{\mathcal{S}_{0}}(f_{1},f_{2}).

We claim that there exists a sparse
\mathcal{S}\supset\mathcal{S}_{0}
so that

\mathcal{E}_{4}^{\mathcal{S}_{0}}(f_{1},f_{4})\leq C[\mu]_{sib}^{1/2}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(f_{1},f_{2}).

Indeed, for each
I\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
such that

,

\langle f_{1}\rangle_{I}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{I^{s}}\,m(I)^{1/2}m(I^{s})^{1/2}\leq\langle f_{1}\rangle_{\hat{I}}\langle f_{2}\rangle_{\hat{I}}\mu(\hat{I})\frac{m(I)^{1/2}m(I^{s})^{1/2}\mu(\hat{I})}{\mu(I)\mu(I^{s})}.

Without loss of generality assume that
\mu(I)\leq\mu(I^{s})
. Then

\frac{\mu(\hat{I})}{\mu(I^{s})}\frac{m(I)^{1/2}m(I^{s})^{1/2}}{\mu(I)}\leq 2\frac{[\mu]_{sib}^{1/2}m(I)}{\mu(I)}\leq 2[\mu]_{sib}^{1/2}.

Then, it is enough to define
\mathcal{S}
by adding to
\mathcal{S}_{0}
new intervals
\hat{I}
whenever
I
and
I^{s}
are both in
\mathcal{S}_{0}
. Then
\mathcal{S}
is
\eta
-sparse for some
0<\eta=\eta(\eta_{0})<\eta_{0}
, which is enough.
∎

A.2.
Adapted weight classes

Let
1<p<\infty
. A weight
\omega\in A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)
if

[\omega]_{A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)}:=\sup_{I.J\in\mathscr{D}}c_{p}(I,J)\langle\omega\rangle_{I}\langle\sigma\rangle_{J}^{p-1}<\infty,

where

c_{p}(I,J)=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }I=J,\\
\left(\frac{m(\hat{I})}{\mu(I)}\right)^{p-1}\frac{m(\hat{J})}{\mu(J)},\text{ if }\hat{I}=(\hat{J})^{s},\\
\left(\frac{m(\hat{I})}{\mu(I)}\right)^{p-1}\frac{m(J)}{\mu(J)},\text{ if }J=(\hat{I})^{s},\\
\left(\frac{m(I)}{\mu(I)}\right)^{p-1}\frac{m(\hat{J})}{\mu(J)},\text{ if }I=(\hat{J})^{s},\\
0,\text{ for any other case}.\end{cases}

Proposition A.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. For all
\omega\in A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)
, for all
\eta
sparse families
\mathcal{S}
,

(A.1)

|\mathcal{E}_{i}^{\mathcal{S}}(f,g\omega)|\lesssim_{p,\eta}[\mu]_{sib}^{\gamma(p)}[\omega]^{1/p}_{A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)}[\omega]_{A_{p}(\mu)}^{\frac{(p-1)^{2}+1}{p(p-1)}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)},

where
\gamma(p)=1/2-\min\{1/p,1/p^{\prime}\}
.

Proof.

We only deal with
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\mathcal{S}}
because the other two forms are similar. Since
\mu
is sibling balanced, then for all
1<p<\infty

\sqrt{m(I)}\sqrt{m(I^{s})}\leq[\mu]_{sib}^{1/2-\min\{1/p,1/p^{\prime}\}}m(I)^{1/p}m(I^{s})^{1/p^{\prime}}.

Using the standard notation
\langle f\rangle_{I,\omega}=\frac{1}{\omega(I)}\int_{I}f\omega d\mu
, with
\omega(I)=\int_{I}\omega d\mu

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{S}}_{1}(f,g\omega)\leq&[\mu]_{sib}^{\gamma(p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{S}\\
\hat{I}=(\hat{J})^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f\rangle_{I}\langle g\omega\rangle_{J}\,m(\hat{I})^{1/p}m(\hat{J})^{1/p^{\prime}}\\
\leq&[\mu]_{sib}^{\gamma(p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{S}\\
\hat{I}=(\hat{J})^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f\sigma^{-1}\rangle_{I,\sigma}\langle g\rangle_{J,\omega}\sigma(I)^{1/p}\omega(J)^{1/p^{\prime}}\frac{\omega(J)^{1/p}\sigma(I)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\mu(J)\mu(I)}\,m(\hat{J})^{1/p^{\prime}}m(\hat{I})^{1/p}\\
=&[\mu]_{sib}^{\gamma(p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{S}\\
\hat{I}=(\hat{J})^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f\sigma^{-1}\rangle_{I,\sigma}\langle g\rangle_{J,\omega}\sigma(I)^{1/p}\omega(J)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\omega(J)}{\mu(J)}\frac{\sigma(I)^{p-1}}{\mu(I)^{p-1}}c(J,I)\right)^{1/p}\\
\leq&[\mu]_{sib}^{\gamma(p)}[\omega]_{A_{sib}^{p}(\mu)}^{1/p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{S}\\
\hat{I}=(\hat{J})^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f\sigma^{-1}\rangle_{I,\sigma}\langle g\rangle_{J,\omega}\sigma(I)^{1/p}\omega(J)^{1/p^{\prime}}.\end{split}

Since
\mathcal{S}
is
\eta
-sparse,

\begin{split}\eta\omega(I)^{1/p}\leq\frac{\mu(E_{I})}{\mu(I)}\omega(I)^{1/p}=&\frac{\omega(I)^{1/p}}{\mu(I)}\int_{E_{I}}\omega^{1/p}\omega^{-1/p}d\mu\\
\leq&\frac{\omega(I)^{1/p}}{\mu(I)}\omega(E_{I})^{1/p}\sigma(I)^{1/p^{\prime}}\leq\omega(E_{I})^{1/p}[\omega]_{A_{p}(\mu)}^{1/p},\end{split}

so
\omega(I)\lesssim_{\eta}[\omega]_{A_{p}(\mu)}\omega(E_{I})
. Similarly,
\sigma(E_{I})\lesssim_{\eta}[\sigma]_{A_{p^{\prime}}(\mu)}\sigma(E_{I})=[\omega]_{A_{p}(\mu)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\sigma(E_{I})
. Hence,

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{S}}_{1}(f,g\omega)\leq[\mu]_{sib}^{\gamma(p)}[\omega]_{A_{sib}^{p}(\mu)}^{1/p}[\omega]_{A_{p}(\mu)}^{\frac{(p-1)^{2}+1}{p(p-1)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{S}\\
\hat{I}=(\hat{J})^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f\sigma^{-1}\rangle_{I,\sigma}\langle g\rangle_{J,\omega}\sigma(E_{I})^{1/p}\omega(E_{J})^{1/p^{\prime}}.\end{split}

Finally, one has

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{S}\\
\hat{I}=(\hat{J})^{s}\end{subarray}}\langle f\sigma^{-1}\rangle_{I,\sigma}\langle g\rangle_{J,\omega}&\sigma(E_{I})^{1/p}\omega(E_{J})^{1/p^{\prime}}\lesssim(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{S}}\langle f\sigma^{-1}\rangle_{I,\sigma}\sigma(E_{I}))^{1/p}(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{S}}\langle g\rangle_{J,\omega}^{p^{\prime}}\omega(E_{J}))^{1/p^{\prime}}\\
\leq&\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}^{\sigma}(f\sigma^{-1})\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{D}}^{\omega}(g)\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\lesssim_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)}.\end{split}

∎

Corollary A.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. If
\omega\in A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)
, then
\mathcal{H}
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\omega).
In particular,
\mathcal{H}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)
for all sibling balanced, atomless
\mu
.

Proof.

By,
[
17
, Theorem 3.1]
,

|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(f,g\omega)|\lesssim_{\eta,p}[\omega]_{A_{p}(\mu)}^{\min\{1,p^{\prime}/p\}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)}.

Therefore, by duality, Theorem
A.2
and Proposition
A.3
we get that for all
\omega\in A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)
,

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{p}(\omega)\rightarrow L^{p}(\omega)}=&\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}=1,\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega)}=1}\left|\int\mathcal{H}fg\omega d\mu\right|\\
\lesssim_{p}&[\mu]_{sib}^{1/2}[\omega]_{A_{p}(\mu)}^{\min\{1,p^{\prime}/p\}}+[\mu]_{sib}^{\gamma(p)}[\omega]^{1/p}_{A_{p}^{sib}(\mu)}[\omega]_{A_{p}(\mu)}^{\frac{(p-1)^{2}+1}{p(p-1)}}.\end{split}

∎

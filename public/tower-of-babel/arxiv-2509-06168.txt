Abstract

1.
Introduction

Embedding of manifolds has been a fundamental topic in geometric topology. An interesting subtopic is to study embeddings that preserve certain geometric/topological structures on the manifolds. In the present article we focus on codimension–
1
smooth embeddings of closed manifolds, that preserve open book structures. An open book decomposition of a manifold
M^{m}
is a pair
(V^{m-1},h)
, such that
M^{m}
is diffeomorphic to the quotient space
\mathcal{MT}(V^{m-1},h)\cup_{id}\partial V^{m-1}\times D^{2}
. Here,
V^{m-1}
, called
page
, is a manifold with boundary. The map
h
, called
monodromy
, is a diffeomorphism of
V^{m-1}
that restricts to identity near the boundary
\partial V
, and
\mathcal{MT}(V^{m-1},h)
denotes the mapping torus of
h
. We denote such an open book by
\textrm{OB}(V,h)
. It is well known that every closed, orientable, odd dimensional manifold admits open book decomposition. For even manifolds necessary and sufficient conditions are known except in dimension
4
. An embedding
f
of
\textrm{OB}(V_{1},h_{1})
in
\textrm{OB}(V_{2},h_{2})
is called an
spun embedding
, if
f
restricts to a proper embedding of
V_{1}
in
V_{2}
and
h_{2}\circ f=f\circ h_{1}
(up to isotopy).

The problem of spun embedding is most interesting for lower codimensions of embedding. In particular, the codimension
2
case has recieved much attention in recent times (eg.
[
9
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
19
]
). In this article, we initiate a study of codimension–
1
spun embeddings of closed oriented manifolds. We first prove a codimension–
1
open book embedding result for closed oriented
3
-manifolds. By Lickorish
[
21
]
and Wallace
[
28
]
, every closed oriented
3
-manifold
M
can be obtained from
\mathbb{S}^{3}
by
\pm 1
-surgery along an
n
-component link
\mathcal{L}_{M}
of unknots. Given such a pair
(M,\mathcal{L}_{M})
, one can construct a planar open book decomposition of
M^{3}
with page
\Sigma_{0,n+1}
(see section
2.7
). Let
M
be a closed oriented
3
-manifold and let
\textrm{OB}(\Sigma_{0,n+1},\phi_{M})
be a planar open book decomposition of

3
-manifold admits a planar open book decomposition obtained from a surgery diagram.

Theorem 1.1

M=\textrm{OB}(\Sigma_{0,n+1},\phi_{M})

2.1.
The sphere twist map

We consider the action of
\mathrm{SO}(n+1)
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
). It is well known that the inclusion map
\mathrm{SO}(n+1)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Diff}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
induces an injective homomorphism
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{SO}(n+1))\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(\operatorname{Diff}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n}))
. Let
\alpha
be the generating element in
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{SO}(n+1))
. One can then define a diffeomorphism
\sigma_{n}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1]
by
(y,t)\mapsto(\alpha(t)\cdot y,t)
for
(y,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1]
. If
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is smoothly embedded in an oriented
n+1
manifold
M
, then
\sigma_{n}
can be extended to a diffeomorphism
M
, called a
twist map
along
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Note that
\sigma_{1}
is the Dehn twist map along an annulus
\mathcal{A}
and
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Diff}^{+}(\mathcal{A},\partial\mathcal{A}))
is the infinite cyclic group generated by
\sigma_{1}
. For
n\geq 2
,
\sigma_{n}
generates the group
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Diff}^{+}_{\partial}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1]))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
relative to boundary, where,
\operatorname{Diff}^{+}_{\partial}(M)
denotes the group of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of a manifold
M
that restricts to identity near
\partial M
.

Lemma 2.1
.

\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1],\textrm{id})=\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1],\sigma_{n})=\mathbb{S}^{2}\widetilde{\times}\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Proof.

By definition,
\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1],\textrm{id})=\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\cup_{id,\partial}\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\{0,1\}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}=\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\{0\}\cup_{id,\partial}\mathbb{D}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\{1\}=\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
. If we cut open
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
along a page of its open book decomposition, we get
V^{n+2}=\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
. The complement of
V\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
is also diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
, where
\{p\}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
is identified with the suspension of
\{p\}\times[0,1]
for every
p\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

We note that
\mathbb{S}^{2}\widetilde{\times}\mathbb{S}^{n}=\mathcal{MT}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1],\sigma_{n})\cup_{id,\partial}\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\{0,1\}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
is obtained from
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
by cutting open along a page and gluing back a copy of
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
via the map
\sigma_{n}:(y,t)\mapsto(\alpha(t)\cdot y,t)
. Here,
\alpha(0)=\alpha(1)=\operatorname{Id}_{n+1}
. Thus,
\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1],\sigma_{n})=\mathbb{S}^{2}\widetilde{\times}\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Let us consider a planar surface with

\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1]\#_{b}\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n}

\mathbb{S}^{n}

\pi_{1}\textrm{Emb}(\mathbb{S}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1]\#_{b}\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n})

\mathcal{P}_{n+1}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{+}_{\partial}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1]\#_{b}\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n})

and it was introduced by Budney and Gabai
[
5
]

In fact, the push map can be defined for an arbitrary simple closed curve along which a sphere is pushed around. Let
M^{3}
be an oriented 3-manifold with a sphere boundary component. A

along a simple closed curve

is an orientation preserving diffeomorphism of

, relative to boundary, which is obtained by pushing the sphere boundary component along the curve
\alpha
and bringing it back to its initial position, while keeping the other boundary components pointwise fixed. For example, let
M
be the manifold
\mathcal{B}_{1,1}=\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,1]\#_{b}\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}

\alpha
be a simple closed curve in

\rho_{\alpha}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{+}_{\partial}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,1]\#_{b}\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{2})
. In general, given a sphere boundary component
S
of

, we denote the push map that pushes
S
along
\alpha

\rho_{S,\alpha}
. If
\alpha_{0}
is a simple closed curve isotopic to

Open book decomposition

An open book decomposition of a closed
n

consists of a codimension
2
closed submanifold
B
and a fibration map
\pi:M\setminus B\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
, such that in a tubular neighborhood of

\pi:B\times(\mathbb{D}^{2}\setminus\{0\})\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
is given by
(b,r,\theta)\mapsto\theta
. The fibration
\pi
determines a unique fiber manifold
N^{n-1}
whose boundary is
B
. The closure
\bar{N}
is called the
page
and
B
is called the
binding
. The monodromy of the fibration map determines a diffeomorphism
\phi
of
\bar{N}
such that
\phi
is identity near a collar neighborhood of boundary
\partial\bar{N}
. In particular,
M=\mathcal{MT}(\bar{N},\phi)\cup_{id,\partial}\partial\bar{N}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
, where
\mathcal{MT}(\bar{N},\phi)
denotes the mapping torus of
\phi
. We denote such an open book decomposition of
M
by
\textrm{OB}(\bar{N},\phi)
. The map
\phi
is called the
monodromy
of the open book. It is a well-known fact that every odd dimensional closed manifold admits an open book decomposition
[
2
]

[
20
]

A well-known fact in topology is that every finitely presented group can be realized as the fundamental group of an orientable closed

Theorem 2.4
.

Given a finitely presented group
\mathcal{G}

4

such that
\pi_{1}(V)=\mathcal{G}

Theorem

Let
\mathcal{G}=\langle x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{g}|r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k}\rangle

\partial M_{0}=\Sigma_{g}\sqcup(\sqcup^{k}\mathbb{S}^{2})

Example 2.3

Since any two proper embeddings of a surface in

\mathbb{S}^{5}

Let

in

) Let

with a spin page

4.
Non-triviality of twist map along a non-separating sphere

Let
\phi_{1}\in\operatorname{Diff}_{\partial}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
\phi_{2}\in\operatorname{Diff}_{\partial}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1])
. To prove Theorem
1.3
we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n}\lx@sectionsign\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1],\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{2})=\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n},\phi_{1})\#\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1],\phi_{2})
.

Lemma
4.1
is well-known for
n=2
. We follow a proof of the
2
-dimensional case given by Etnyre (see
[
22
]
).

Figure 21.
The shaded 2d boxes specify the gluing regions after removal of two
(n+1)
-disks from
\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-1},\phi_{1})
and

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Let us consider neighborhoods of the plumbing regions from both
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1]
. During plumbing the two common regions diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{D}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n}
are identified via the map
(x,\vec{y})\mapsto(-\vec{y},x)
. We think of the open book mapping tori as mapping cylinders, where two ends of a cylinder are identified via the identity map. The monodromy in the first mapping cylinder is applied at level
\frac{1}{4}
of the interval
[0,1]
, whereas the the monodromy in the second mapping cylinder is applied at level
\frac{3}{4}
. To obtained the open book with plumbed pages, we remove
\mathbb{D}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n}\times(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{1}\times(0,\frac{1}{2})
from the mapping cylinders and glue them along the shaded regions (see Figure
21
). Removing
\mathbb{D}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n}\times(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{1}\times(0,\frac{1}{2})
removes open
(n+2)
-balls from the total spaces of
\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n},\phi_{1})
and
\textrm{OB}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,1],\phi_{2})
. It is enough to show that the shaded gluing regions are actually copies of
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
.

Consider the shaded attaching region in the mapping cylinder shown on the right of Figure
21
. The green shaded region is given by
\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{1}\times[0,\frac{1}{2}]
. The corner
\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\partial\mathbb{D}^{1}\times[0,1]
belongs to the binding after plumbing. Thus it contributes the region
\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{1}\times D
, where
D
is a disk bound by a boundary circle
\{*\}\times\frac{[0,1]}{0\sim 1}
in the open book. These two regions gives us
R_{1}=\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{1}\times[0,\frac{1}{2}]\cup\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\partial\mathbb{D}^{1}\times D=(\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\{\mathbb{D}^{1}\times([0,\frac{1}{2}]\cup[\frac{1}{2},1])\cup\partial\mathbb{D}^{1}\times D\})\setminus(\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{1}\times[\frac{1}{2},1])=\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\{\partial(\mathbb{D}^{1}\times D)\setminus\mathbb{D}^{1}\times[\frac{1}{2},1]\}\cong\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
.

Now consider the attaching regions shaded violet and orange. These give the region
R_{2}=\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{1}\times\{\frac{1}{2},1\}\cup\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\partial\mathbb{D}^{1}\times[\frac{1}{2},1]=\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\partial(\mathbb{D}^{1}\times[\frac{1}{2},1])
. It is clear that
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
shares a common boundary and
R_{1}\cup_{\partial}R_{2}\cong\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}\cup\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}=\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
. Since the monodromies
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are applied away from the gluing regions (except near the boundary of pages, where they restrict to the identity map), the lemma follows.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We note that
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus int(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-1}\lx@sectionsign\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times[0,1]

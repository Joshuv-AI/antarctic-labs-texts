The speed of Convergence with respect to the Kolmogorov—Smirnov Metric in the Soshnikov Central Limit Theorem for the Sine Process Thanks: The research was financially supported by the Ministry of Science and Higher Education of the Russian Federation in the framework of a scientific project under agreement No. 075-15-2024-631, and also by the grant 24-7-1-21 of the ”BASIS” Foundation for the Advancement of Mathematics and Theoretical Physics.

Abstract
. For rescaled additive functionals of the sine process, upper bounds are obtained for their speed of convergence to the Gaussian distribution with respect to the Kolmogorov—Smirnov metric. Under scaling with coefficient R R the Kolmogorov—Smirnov distance is bounded from above by c / log ⁡ R c/\log R for a smooth function and by c / R c/R for a function holomorphic in a horizontal strip.



Let
{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathscr{S}}
be the sine process, the determinantal measure on the space
{\mathrm{Conf}}(\mathbb{R})
of configurations on
\mathbb{R}
corresponding to the sine kernel

{\mathscr{S}}(x,y)=\frac{\sin\pi(x-y)}{\pi(x-y)}.

For a Borel function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
having compact support set

S_{f}(X)=\sum_{x\in X}f(x)

to be the additive functional on the space
{\mathrm{Conf}}(\mathbb{R})
, corresponding to the function
f
. If
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
then
S_{f}\in L_{1}({\mathrm{Conf}}(\mathbb{R}),{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathscr{S}})
and

\mathbb{E}_{{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathscr{S}}}S_{f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,dx.

Denote
\overline{S}_{f}=S_{f}-\mathbb{E}_{{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathscr{S}}}S_{f}
and recall that

(1)

\mathbb{E}|\overline{S}_{f}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\iint|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}\cdot|{\mathscr{S}}(x,y)|^{2}\,dxdy.

The equality (
1
) allows one to extend the definition of
\overline{S}_{f}
by continuity to all functions
f
such that the integral in the right-hand side of (
1
) converges. Let
f(\,\cdot\,/R)
stand for the function whose value at the point
t
equals
f(t/R)
. Define the space
\dot{H}_{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
as the completion of the space of smooth compactly supported functions on
\mathbb{R}
with respect to the norm
\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1/2}}
, defined by the formula

\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1/2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|\cdot|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi.

For a function
f\in\dot{H}_{1/2}
denote by the symbols
f_{+},f_{-}
the functions defined by the formulas
\widehat{f_{+}}=\widehat{f}\cdot\chi_{[0,+\infty)}
,
\widehat{f_{-}}=\widehat{f}\cdot\chi_{(-\infty,0)}
. Set also
\mathring{f}=f_{-}-f_{+}
.

Theorem 1
.

If
f\in\dot{H}_{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathring{f}\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
then the family of additive functionals
\overline{S}_{f(\,\cdot\,/R)}
converges in distribution to the Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1/2}}^{2}/4\pi^{2})
.

Theorem
1
has been proved by A. Soshnikov
[
12
]
under somewhat more restrictive conditions on the function
f
. Our next aim is to establish the speed of convergence in Theorem
1
.
Recall that the Kolmogorov—Smirnov distance
d_{KS}
between two probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
is defined as the supremum of the difference of their distribution functions.
With a slight abuse of notation, we shall write, for example,
d_{KS}(Y,\mathcal{N}(0,1))
to denote the Kolmogorov—Smirnov distance from the distribution of the random variable
Y
to the standard Gaussian distribution.

Denote by the symbol
\dot{H}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
the completion of the space compactly supported smooth functions with respect to the norm
\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1}}
defined by the formula

\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1/2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|^{2}\cdot|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,dt.

Theorem 2

(
[
5
]
)
.

Assume that
f\in\dot{H}_{1/2}(\mathbb{R})\cap\dot{H}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathring{f}\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Then there exists a constant
c(f)
depending only on
\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1/2}}
,
\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1}}
, and
\|\mathring{f}\|_{L_{\infty}}
such that for all
R>1
we have

d_{KS}\Bigl(\overline{S}_{f(\,\cdot\,/R)},\mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1/2}}^{2}/4\pi^{2}\bigr)\Bigr)\leq\frac{c(f)}{\log R}.

The convergence is faster if the function
\mathring{f}
is holomorhic in a strip. For
\delta>0
let
\mathcal{HL}(\delta)
be the space of functions
f
that are holomorphic and bounded in the strip
\{|\im z|<\delta\}
and satisfy the inequality

\sup_{|\delta_{1}|<\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f^{\prime}(t+i\delta_{1})|^{2}\,dt<+\infty.

Endow the space
\mathcal{HL}(\delta)
with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{HL}(\delta)}=\sup_{|\im z|<\delta}|f(z)|+\sup_{|\delta_{1}|<\delta}\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f^{\prime}(t+i\delta_{1})|^{2}\,dt\biggr)^{1/2}.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\delta>0
. Then for any
f\in\dot{H}_{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathring{f}\in\mathcal{HL}(\delta)
there exists a constant
c(f)
, depending only on
\delta
,
\|\mathring{f}\|_{\mathcal{HL}(\delta)}
and such that for all
R>1
we have

d_{KS}\Bigl(\overline{S}_{f(\,\cdot\,/R)},\mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1/2}}^{2}/4\pi^{2}\bigr)\Bigr)\leq\frac{c(f)}{R}.

The key rôle in the proofs of Theorems
2
,
3
is played by the scaling limit of the Borodin—Okounkov—Jeronimo—Case formula
[
3
,
7
,
2
,
4
,
5
]
. Recall that the Hankel operator
{\mathfrak{H}}(h)
corresponding to the function
h
is defined by the formula

{\mathfrak{H}}(h)\varphi(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\widehat{h}(s+t)\varphi(t)\,dt.

Denote

V_{f}(\lambda)=\det\biggl(1-\chi_{(1,+\infty)}{\mathfrak{H}}\biggl(\exp\biggl(\lambda\mathring{f}\biggl(\frac{\cdot}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr)\biggr){\mathfrak{H}}\biggl(\exp\biggl(-\lambda\mathring{f}\biggl(\frac{\cdot}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr)\biggr)\chi_{(1,+\infty)}\biggr).

Proposition 1

(
[
4
,
5
]
)
.

If
f\in\dot{H}_{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
is such that
\mathring{f}\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\mathbb{E}_{{\mathsf{P}}_{\mathscr{S}}}\exp(\lambda\overline{S}_{f})=\exp\biggl(\frac{\lambda^{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1/2}}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\biggr)\cdot V_{f}(\lambda).

We have the estimate

V_{f}(\lambda)\leq\exp\biggl(\biggl\|\chi_{(1,+\infty)}{\mathfrak{H}}\biggl(\exp\biggl(\lambda\mathring{f}\biggl(\frac{\cdot}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr)\biggr)\biggr\|_{HS}\cdot\biggl\|{\mathfrak{H}}\biggl(\exp\biggl(-\lambda\mathring{f}\biggl(\frac{\cdot}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr)\biggr)\chi_{(1,+\infty)}\biggr\|_{HS}\biggr).

For
R\geq 1
define the seminorm
\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}(R)}
on the space
\dot{H}_{1/2}
by the formula

\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}(R)}^{2}=\int_{1}^{+\infty}\xi|\widehat{h}(\xi+R)|^{2}\,d\xi.

We have

(2)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{(1,+\infty)}{\mathfrak{H}}(h)\|_{HS}=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}(1)},\quad\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}(R)}=\|h(\,\cdot\,/R)\|_{\mathcal{H}(1)},

\displaystyle|V_{f(\,\cdot\,/R)}(\lambda)|\leq\exp\biggl(\biggl\|\exp\biggl(\lambda\mathring{f}\biggl(\frac{\cdot}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr)\biggr\|_{\mathcal{H}(R)}\cdot\biggl\|\exp\biggl(-\lambda\mathring{f}\biggl(\frac{\cdot}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr)\biggr\|_{\mathcal{H}(R)}\biggr).

One can see from (
2
) that if

\exp(\lambda\mathring{f})\in\dot{H}_{1/2}
, then

\chi_{(1,+\infty)}{\mathfrak{H}}(\exp(\lambda\mathring{f}))\in HS
. The relation

\exp(\lambda\mathring{f})\in\dot{H}_{1/2}
holds once
\mathring{f}\in\dot{H}_{1/2}\cap L_{\infty}
; one can also see that if
\mathring{f}\in\dot{H}_{1/2}\cap L_{\infty}
then

\biggl\|\chi_{(1,+\infty)}{\mathfrak{H}}\biggl(\exp\biggl(\lambda\mathring{f}\biggl(\frac{\cdot}{R}\biggr)\biggr)\biggr)\biggr\|_{HS}\to 0

as
R\to+\infty
. Theorem
1
is proven. We proceed to the proofs of Theorems
2
and
3
.
The Esseen smoothing inequality in the form of Feller
[
6
,
11
]
yields

Corollary 1
.

Let
\varkappa_{1},\varkappa_{0},T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
X
be a random variable such that its characteristic function
f_{X}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}\exp(i\xi X)
has the form

f_{X}(\xi)=\exp(-\xi^{2}/2)W(\xi)
with

\sup_{|\xi|\leq 1}|W^{\prime}(\xi)|=\varkappa_{1},\quad\sup_{|\xi|\leq T}|W(\xi)-1|=\varkappa_{0}.

Then we have

d_{KS}(X,\mathcal{N}(0,1))\leq\varkappa_{0}+\varkappa_{1}+\frac{4}{T}.

The estimates on the speed of convergence are thus reduced to the estimates on the norms

\biggl\|\chi_{(1,+\infty)}{\mathfrak{H}}\biggl(\exp\biggl(\lambda\mathring{f}\biggl(\frac{\cdot}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr)\biggr)\biggr\|_{HS},\quad\biggl\|{\mathfrak{H}}\biggl(\exp\biggl(-\lambda\mathring{f}\biggl(\frac{\cdot}{2\pi}\biggr)\biggr)\biggr)\chi_{(1,+\infty)}\biggr\|_{HS}.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

On the intersection
\dot{H}_{1}\cap L_{\infty}
consider the norm
\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1}\cap L_{\infty}}=\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1}}+\|f\|_{L_{\infty}}
.
If
f\in\dot{H}_{1}\cap L_{\infty}
then
\exp(f)\in\dot{H}_{1}\cap L_{\infty}
, and we have

\|\exp(f)\|_{\dot{H}_{1}\cap L_{\infty}}\leq(1+\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1}\cap L_{\infty}})\cdot\exp(\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1}\cap L_{\infty}}).

Definitions directly imply that
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}(R)}\leq\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{1}}/\sqrt{R}
, whence we also have

\|\exp(\lambda\mathring{f}(\,\cdot\,/R))\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\frac{(1+|\lambda|\cdot\|\mathring{f}\|_{\dot{H}_{1}\cap L_{\infty}})\exp(\lambda\|\mathring{f}\|_{\dot{H}_{1}\cap L_{\infty}})}{\sqrt{R}}.

The derivative
(d/d\lambda)V_{f}(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in[-1,1]
is estimated by the Cauchy formula

\frac{d}{d\lambda}V_{f}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|\xi-\lambda|=1}\frac{V_{f}(\xi)}{(\xi-\lambda)^{2}}\,d\xi.

Now Corollary
1
implies Theorem
2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

From the definition of the space
\mathcal{HL}(\delta)
one directly obtains that

(1)

if
f\in\mathcal{HL}(\delta)
then
\exp(f)\in\mathcal{HL}(\delta)
, and

\|\exp(f)\|_{\mathcal{HL}(\delta)}\leq\exp(\|f\|_{\mathcal{HL}(\delta)})\cdot(1+\|f\|_{\mathcal{HL}(\delta)});

(2)

for any
\delta_{1}<\delta
and any
f\in\mathcal{HL}(\delta)
we have

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}(R)}\leq\exp(-\delta_{1}R)\|f\|_{\mathcal{HL}(\delta)};

(3)

if
\mathring{f}\in\mathcal{HL}(\delta)
, then for any
\delta_{1}<\delta
there exists a constant
A(\mathring{f})
, depending only on
\delta
,
\|\mathring{f}\|_{\mathcal{HL}(\delta)}
such that

\|\exp(\lambda\mathring{f}(\,\cdot\,/R))\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\exp(A|\lambda|-\delta_{1}R).

The derivative
(d/d\lambda)V_{f}(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in[-1,1]
is again estimated by the Cauchy formula. Corollary
1
now implies Theorem
3
.
∎

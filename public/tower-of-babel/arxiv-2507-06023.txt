Liquid-Gas Criticality of Hyperuniform Fluids
Shang Gao, Hao Shang, Hao Hu, Yu-Qiang Ma, Qun-Li Lei

Abstract
In statistical physics, a well-accepted knowledge is that liquid-gas (LG) phase transition with diverging critical fluctuations lies in the universality class of Ising. Yet, whether non-equilibrium effect can alter this conclusion remains an open question. Here, we study the LG criticality of a hyperuniform (HU) fluid composed of active spinners, where phase separation is induced by dissipative collisions. Strikingly, at the LG critical point, the HU fluid exhibits normal Gaussian density fluctuations instead of the diverging fluctuations. Through hydrodynamic field theory and renormalization-group analysis, we demonstrate that hyperuniformity reduces the upper critical dimension from d c = 4 d_{c}=4 (classical fluids) to d c = 2 d_{c}=2 due to an emerging long-range correlation induced by center-of-mass conservation. Large-scale stochastic field simulations confirm this dimensional reduction, and reveal diverging response function along with anomalous zero-range correlation function at the critical point, indicating the absence of an effective fluctuation-dissipation relationship in the system. The simulation also identifies anomalous finite-size scaling in density fluctuation, energy fluctuation, and Binder cumulant. Furthermore, HU fluid also exihibits non-classical spinodal decomposition dynamics with diverging decomposition time but non-diverging decomposition length scale. Our results provide an exception to the classical paradigms of LG critical phenomena and highlight how non-equilibrium effect can reshape universality classes of classical phase transitions.



I
Introduction

The liquid-gas (LG) phase transition is a fundamental thermodynamic phenomenon observed across diverse kinds of matter. At its critical point, the distinction between liquid and gas phases vanishes, giving rise to diverging density fluctuations, leading to striking phenomena such as critical opalescence
Chu 1972
. Pioneering work by Lee and Yang established the equivalence between the lattice gas model and the Ising model, anchoring LG criticality within the Ising universality class for equilibrium systems with short-range interactions
Lee & Yang 1952
;
Yang & Yang 1964
. This framework has been extensively validated through numerical simulations

;
Bruce & Wilding 1992
;
Wilding 1997
;
Watanabe et al. 2012
;
Yarmolinsky & Kuklov 2017
;
Yarmolinsky & Kuklov 2018
and experiments
Hayes & Carr 1977
;
Pestak & Chan 1984
;
Kim & Chan 1984
.

For non-equilibrium system, however, criticality need not follow the same universality as in equilibrium counterparts
Marro et al. 1996
;
Speck 2022
;
Täuber et al. 1999
;
Santos & Täuber 2002
;
Bertrand & Lee 2022
;
Jentsch & Lee 2023
;
Miller & Toner 2024
. Nonetheless, it has been increasingly recognized that an effective temperature and the corresponding Ising universality class can emerge from non-equilibrium LG or binary phase separation, provided that no additional symmetry breaking or long-range interactions are presented
Young et al. 2020
;
Nakano et al. 2021
;
Ikeda & Nakano 2024
. Examples of such behavior include the non-equilibrium kinetic Ising model
Sides et al. 1998
, the two-temperature model
Szolnoki 1999
,
motility-induced phase separation (MIPS) in active Brownian particle systems
Partridge & Lee 2019
;
Dittrich et al. 2023
, active Ornstein–Uhlenbeck particle systems
Maggi et al. 2021
;
Maggi et al. 2022
, noise-induced phase separation
Paoluzzi et al. 2024
;
Paoluzzi 2022
, run-and-tumble particle systems
Saha et al. 2024
;

, quorum-sensing active particle systems

;
Bhowmick et al. 2025
, raise the question of whether the Ising universality class is unique for LG criticality of fluids, irrespective of their equilibrium or non-equilibrium nature.

A parallel frontier lies in the study of disordered hyperuniformity (HU), a structural hallmark of disordered systems with suppressed long-wavelength density fluctuations

In this work, we investigate the LG criticality of a hyperuniform fluid composed of active spinners, which exhibits liquid gas phase separation induced by dissipative collisions. Contrary to classical liquid-gas transitions which exhibit diverging density fluctuations, the system exhibits Gaussian density fluctuations at criticality. Through hydrodynamic field theory and renormalization-group analysis, we reveals that the upper critical dimension decreases from
d_{c}=4
(classical fluids) to
d_{c}=2
due to a emerging long-range correlation induced by center-of-mass conservation
Hexner & Levine 2017
;
Lei & Ni 2019
. Stochastic simulations confirm this upper critical dimension reduction, and reveal diverging response function along with anomalous zero-range correlation function at the critical point, indicating the absence of fluctuation-dissipation relationship and an effective temperature. It also unveils unconventional finite-size scaling in density/energy fluctuation and Binder cumulant near the critical point. Furthermore, we observe non-classical spinodal decomposition dynamics of hyperuniform fluid due to the absence of diverging density fluctuations: the decomposition time diverges as criticality is approached, but without a diverging decomposition length scale. These results provide an exception to the classical paradigms of LG critical phenomena, highlighting how non-equilibrium effect reshapes both static and dynamic universality classes.

III
Dissipation-Induced Phase Separation

The system is a typical driven-dissipative system: the energy injected through rotational torque
\bm{\Omega}
is dissipated by dissipative collision, and friction in both translational and rotational degrees of freedom. Under low density or weak driving (
\bm{\Omega}\to 0
) conditions , dissipation dominates, driving the system fall into absorbing states where each spinner rotates in its fixed place. In contrast, strong driving or high density sustains a homogeneous active phase marked by persistent collisions and a hyperuniform fluid state with structure factor scaling
S(q)\sim q^{2}
as
q\to 0

Lei & Ni 2019
. Earlier study proved that for systems with non-dissipative collisions, the absorbing phase transition belonging to the universality class of conserved directed percolation (CDP)
Lei & Ni 2019
.

Our work extends the above framework by introducing dissipation during collision, which has a pronounced effect on the density field: high-density regions exhibit amplified dissipation due to frequent interparticle collisions, decreasing local kinetic energy and pressure compared to low-density regions. This imbalance would generate a negative compressibility that destabilizes the density field
Herminghaus & Mazza 2017
;
Fullmer & Hrenya 2017
;
Mandal et al. 2019
;
Clewett et al. 2012
;
Clewett et al. 2016
, ultimately triggering LG phase separation at sufficiently large
\Omega
, resulting the coexistence of a gas phase (high kinetic temperature) with a liquid phase (low kinetic temperature) as illustrated in Fig.
1
(g).
We term this phenomenon dissipation-induced phase separation (DIPS), which is different from the phase separation due to local jamming
Liu et al. 2023
, odd response
Ding et al. 2024
;
Nguyen et al. 2014
, hydrodynamic interactions
Shen & Lintuvuori 2023
;
Yeo et al. 2015
.
Similar mechanism has also been reported in non-equilibrium granular gas systems
Clewett et al. 2012
. The resultant phase diagram in (
\rho
,
\Omega
) space (Fig.
1
(b)) delineates three distinct regimes: absorbing (blue), HU fluid (red), and LG coexistence (green).

In Fig.
1
(c), we show the
S(q)
of systems under different
\Omega
with the trace cross the LG critical point (
\rho_{0}^{*}=0.7\sigma^{-2}
). Below the critical point, the active phase maintains hyperuniformity with
S(q\to 0)\sim q^{2}
. Above the critical point, phase separation manifests through
S(q)\sim q^{-3}
, aligning with Porod’s law
Täuber 2014
;
Kostorz 2001
. Remarkably, at the critical point, we observe anomalous Gaussian fluctuations (
S(q\to 0)\sim\text{const.}
), contrasting the diverging fluctuations
S(q\to 0)\sim q^{-2}
of classical LG criticality
Täuber 2014
. This anomalous fluctuation suggests that hyperuniformity fundamentally reshapes the system’s critical behaviors.

d_{c}=2
different from 4 for the Ising universality. It should be noted that recently there are some theoretical works discussed the effect of spatially or temporally correlated noise on the upper and low critical dimension of general
O(n)
model and related phase transitions

;

;
Ikeda 2024
;

. It was suggested upper critical dimension is

for system with

(the case studied here)

, which is different from our prediction. In the next section, we will give clear evidence that the upper critical dimension is indeed
d_{c}=2
.

We derive the flow equations for couplings
r
and
u
using Wilson’s momentum shell renormalization group approach (see Appendix D for details)
Wilson 1983
. At one-loop level, the critical exponents below upper critical dimension are formally the same as Ising universality except the change of
d_{c}
, namely,

\nu\approx\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{12},\quad\beta\approx\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{6},\quad\gamma\approx 1+\frac{\varepsilon}{6},\quad\eta\approx 0,

where
\varepsilon=d_{c}-d=2-d
. Thus, the exact critical exponents of 2D HU fluid taking the mean field value.

We also derive the response (susceptibility) function
\chi(x)
i.e., the response of the field at distance
x
upon the perturbation at the origin

\displaystyle\chi(x)\sim\dfrac{1}{x^{d-2+\eta^{\prime}}},\ \ \ x\ll\xi

(22)

where
\xi=r^{-\nu}
is the correlation length and
\eta^{\prime}
is the anomalous dimension associated with response function
Young et al. 2020
. Therefore, near the critical point
r=0
, the correlation length
\xi
diverges and response function becomes power-law decay at the critical point suggesting that the HU fluid is very susceptible, similar to equilibrium fluids.

Despite the similarity, there are some remarkable difference between between 2D HU fluids and the equilibrium fluids. For example, the correlation function of HU fluid near LG critical point is

\displaystyle{C(x)~\sim~\delta(x)-\frac{r}{x^{d-2+\eta}},\ \ \ x\ll\xi.}

(23)

Here,
\eta
is the anomalous dimension associated with response function
Young et al. 2020
. Different from classical fluids or Ising model, there exists a
\delta(x)
function and pre-factor

in

. Thus,

in HU fluids is zero-range instead of divergence at the critical point (
r=0
) as in equilibrium fluids, which is consistent with the critical Gaussian fluctuation of HU fluids. Many non-equilibrium systems can be described by the effective equilibrium theory and satisfying the effective fluctuation-dissipation relation
\chi(x)=C(x)/(k_{B}T_{\rm eff})
with
\eta^{\prime}=\eta
, which defines an unique effective temperature
T_{\rm eff}

Täuber 2014
;
Young et al. 2020
.
The fundamental difference between susceptibility function Eq. (
22

In the Ising model, magnetization fluctuations
{\langle M^{2}\rangle-\langle M\rangle^{2}}
connect to thermodynamic susceptibility
\chi_{m}
through the fluctuation-dissipation theorem

\chi_{m}=\frac{\langle M^{2}\rangle-\langle M\rangle^{2}}{Nk_{B}T}\sim\tau^{-\gamma}

, where
M
represents magnetization,

and
r_{c}
is the critical point. Analogously, for equilibrium fluids near criticality, the density fluctuation in sub-boxes of liquid/gas phase

\chi_{c}

\chi_{c}(\tau,L)=\left.\frac{\partial\Delta\psi_{lg}}{\partial h}\right|_{h\to 0}=L^{\gamma/\nu}{S}_{c}(\tau L^{1/\nu})

\displaystyle\chi_{\rho}(\tau,L)=L^{\gamma/\nu-2}{S}_{\rho}(\tau L^{1/\nu})

{S}_{\rho}(x)\sim{const.}
as

which is the characteristic of hyperuiform fluids, while near criticality (
\tau\to 0

emerges as a result of

, signaling Gaussian fluctuations. It should be noted that this finite-size density fluctuation scaling is different from the scaling of density fluctuation usually used to define hyperuniformity

;
Cameron et al. 2023
;
Nikola et al. 2016
;
Hecht et al. 2024
;
Petrelli et al. 2020

Szamel 2014

Palacci et al. 2010

Thus, the compressibility and density fluctuations obeys the effective fluctuation-dissipation relation
\chi_{c}=\chi_{\rho}/(k_{B}T_{\rm eff})
with a unique

Young et al. 2020
;
Partridge & Lee 2019
;
Maggi et al. 2021

Saha et al. 2024
;

In our system, the disparity between compressibility scaling Eq. (

VII
Spinodal decomposition and Coarsening Dynamics

We investigate the dynamics of LG phase transitions by analysing the characteristic length scale
L(t)
of inhomogeneities, defined as
Shi et al. 2020
;
Clewett et al. 2012
:

\displaystyle{L}(t)=\left\langle\frac{\int qS(q,t)dq}{2\pi\int S(q,t)dq}\right\rangle^{-1}

(26)

where

denotes time-dependent structure factors. Fig.

(a) plots

, and (iii) critical divergence of waiting time
t_{w}\sim(\Omega-\Omega_{c})^{-\nu_{\parallel}}
with
\nu_{\parallel}=2.0
. Stochastic field simulations of model B-like dynamics of HU fluid (Fig.
4
(b)) reproduce this pre-coarsening stage but reveal an additional fast intermediate regime between pre-coarsening and coarsening
Clewett et al. 2012
.
The difference between spinner systems and field simulation is probably due to the odd viscosity in spinner systems, which can affect the interfacial dynamics
Soni et al. 2019
.
For comparison, we give the coarsening dynamics of classical model B in Fig.
4
(c). We find the absence of waiting time and a slower coarsening scaling as the system quenched close to the LG critical point. Depart from the critical point, the coarsening dynamics of above three systems all follow

Täuber 2014
;
Livi & Politi 2017
.

The existence of waiting time before coarsening resemble the dynamic Cahn-Hilliard equation without noise. In this scenario, when a homogeneous system is quenched into a phase separation regime from above the critical point, spinodal decomposition with characteristic time
t_{w}
is first observed before the coarsening dynamic. This characteristic spinodal decomposition time can be estimated in a mean-field theory with linear perturbations about the stable state

in Eq. (
19

:

\displaystyle\omega(k)=-\left(r+\frac{1}{2}u\psi^{2}_{0}\right)k^{2}-k^{4}.

(27)

At critical density
\psi_{0}=\psi_{c}=0
, growth rate reaches its maximum value at the most unstable wavevector

Kostorz 2001
, which gives the characteristic coarsening length

\displaystyle L_{c}=\frac{1}{k_{max}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{-r}}

(28)

The time scale for growth of the most unstable mode is

\displaystyle t_{w}=\frac{1}{\omega(k_{max})}=\frac{4}{r^{2}}

Thus, both

and

satisfies the scaling law of Eq. (
28
,
29

For HU fluids, however, long wavelength density fluctuations are significantly suppressed. Thus, the spinodal decomposition before coarsening can be still observed near the critical point.
Nevertheless, the decomposition length does not show diverging in HU fluids as shown in Fig.
4
(a-b). These indicate the spinodal decomposition in HU is fundamentally different from either conventional scenarios of model B or athermal phase separation described by Eq. (
28
,
29
).

VIII
Conclusion and Discussion

In conclusion, we demonstrate that dissipative collisions induce LG phase separation in hyperuniform spinner HU fluids. A hydrodynamic theory is developed to explain the LG instability, which can be generalized to Model B-like theories with center-of-mass conserved noise. At the LG critical point, we shown that HU fluids exhibit Gaussian fluctuation (
S(q)\sim const.
) with apparent zero-range correlation.
Detail analysis of response function shows that this Gaussian critical state has diverging long-range response or a hidden correlation.
The fundamental difference between the correlation and response suggests the absence of fluctuation-dissipation theorem and an effective temperature in HU fluids.
Through renormalization group analysis and stochastic field simulations, we establish that upper critical dimensions is reduced to
d_{c}=2
for HU fluids. The critical phenomena is accompanied by the anomalous finite-size scaling of density fluctuation, energy fluctuation and Binder cumulant. We also find non-conventional spinodal decomposition dynamics of HU fluids characterized by diverging decomposition time but not diverging decomposition lengths. We expect that our predicted anomalous LG criticality be realized by using robotic spinner systems
Li et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2024
;
Scholz et al. 2018
;
Wang & Liu 2025
and granular
gas driven by vibration
Herminghaus & Mazza 2017
;
Clewett et al. 2012
;
Clewett et al. 2016
;
Maire et al. 2024
, which opens up a new direction to study non-conventional critical phenomena based on driven classical systems.

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\int\mathcal{D}[\psi]}\prod_{(\mathbf{x},t)}\delta\left(\frac{\partial\psi(\mathbf{x},t)}{\partial t}-F[\psi](\mathbf{x},t)-\zeta(\mathbf{x},t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}[\psi]\int\mathcal{D}[i\tilde{\psi}]\cdot\exp\left[-\int d^{d}x\int dt\tilde{\psi}\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}-F[\psi]-\zeta\right)\right]

We use the following method to calculate energy fluctuation which usually associated with specific heat,

\displaystyle c_{v}=\frac{\langle E^{2}\rangle-\langle E\rangle^{2}}{\langle E\rangle}

(S91)

where

\displaystyle e(\mathbf{r}_{ij})=\frac{r}{2}\psi(\mathbf{r}_{ij})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}[\nabla\psi(\mathbf{r}_{ij})]^{2}+\frac{u}{4!}\psi(\mathbf{r}_{ij})^{4},~~E=\sum_{0\leqslant i,j<L}\sigma^{2}e(\mathbf{r}_{ij})

(S92)

Generally energy fluctuations obey the finite size scaling
c_{v}(\tau,L)=f(\tau)+L^{-\lambda}g(\tau L^{1/\nu})
. Expanding the function
g(\tau L^{1/\nu})
gives
c_{v}(\tau,L)=f(\tau)+c_{0}L^{-\lambda}+\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}c_{i}\tau^{i}L^{i/\nu-\lambda}
.
Since
c_{v}(T,L)
does not diverge with
L
in Fig.3(h), high order terms with
i/\nu-\lambda>0
could be neglected. By fitting the data to
c_{v}(\tau,L)=f(\tau)+c_{0}L^{-\lambda}+c_{1}\tau L^{1/\nu-\lambda}

\lambda=2.11\pm 0.02,~c_{0}=24.0\pm 0.5,~c_{1}=13.0\pm 0.2

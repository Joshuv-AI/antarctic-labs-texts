Optimizing postnatal management based on prenatal UTD grading: a 5-year follow-up study of fetal hydronephrosis in a large Chinese cohort
Authors: Zhiqiang Mo, Weiping Zhang, Xianghui Xie, Ning Sun, Jun Tian, Minglei Li, Hongcheng Song, Feng Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the long-term natural history of fetal hydronephrosis (FH) in a Chinese population, identify key prognostic factors, and optimize evidence-based postnatal management strategies using the Urinary Tract Dilation (UTD) classification system.MethodsA hybrid retrospective-prospective cohort study was conducted among 49,097 pregnant women who received prenatal screening at Beijing Children’s Hospital Shunyi Women’s and Children’s Hospital between January 2010 and March 2020. A total of 2,263 fetuses were diagnosed with hydronephrosis and followed for 5 years. Prenatal ultrasound parameters (anteroposterior renal pelvic diameter [APD], renal parenchymal thickness, and A/R ratio) and postnatal outcomes (surgery, urinary tract infections, hypertension) were analyzed. Cases were stratified into UTD A1 (low-risk) and A2 ~ 3 (high-risk) groups. Statistical analyses included generalized linear mixed-effects models (GLMM), cumulative link mixed models (CLMM), and generalized linear models (GLM).ResultsThe incidence of fetal hydronephrosis was 4.61% (2,263/49,097). Among these, 9.01% (204/2,263) were confirmed postnatally, and only 0.75% (17/2,263) required surgery. By the final assessment at the 5-year follow-up, hydronephrosis had resolved spontaneously in 97.39% (2,204/2,263) of cases, with persistence in only 59 (2.61%). Multivariate analysis identified left renal APD (OR = 22.05, 95% CI: 21.99–22.11, p < 0.001) and left A/R ratio (OR = 22.39, 95% CI: 1.31–383.89, p = 0.03) as independent predictors for surgery. All surgical cases were in the UTD A2 ~ 3 group, which showed significantly higher risks of postnatal hydronephrosis (OR = 8.26, p < 0.001), persistence at 5 years (OR = 8.30, p < 0.001), and clinical symptoms (OR = 20.57, p < 0.001) compared to the UTD A1 group.ConclusionMost cases of fetal hydronephrosis resolve spontaneously without intervention. The UTD system effectively stratifies postnatal risk. For asymptomatic infants in the UTD A1 group, routine ultrasound within the first year of life is sufficient, reducing unnecessary early imaging and family anxiety. APD and A/R ratio are critical imaging predictors for surgical decision-making.Keywords: Fetal hydronephrosis, Urinary tract dilation classification, Long-term outcome, Surgical predictors, Postnatal management

Introduction

Fetal urinary tract dilation (UTD), commonly referred to as fetal hydronephrosis, is among the most frequently identified structural anomalies detected on prenatal ultrasound, with an estimated incidence ranging from 1% to 5% of all pregnancies [1, 3]. The majority of these cases are isolated and mild in nature [1]. Although most instances of UTD resolve spontaneously after birth without complications, a clinically significant subset (approximately 10%~20%) may require postnatal surgical intervention to prevent progressive renal injury [4, 5]. Left untreated, these children are at increased risk of developing chronic kidney disease, recurrent urinary tract infections (UTIs), and hypertension later in life [6].

Current clinical management of prenatally detected UTD remains contentious [1]. Major challenges include accurately predicting which cases will progress to require intervention, determining the appropriate timing and intensity of postnatal imaging surveillance, and establishing evidence-based follow-up protocols [7, 8]. This uncertainty often leads to both excessive parental anxiety and aggressive monitoring of ultimately benign conditions, while also risking under-management of cases with true pathological potential. Several prognostic markers—such as second-trimester anterior-posterior pelvic diameter (APD) [5] and increasingly, machine learning tools like convolutional neural networks for automated grading [9]—have been proposed. However, the current body of evidence is largely derived from small-scale, retrospective studies with limited follow-up duration (typically ≤ 2 years) [6]. Moreover, there is a notable scarcity of large-cohort, long-term studies from Chinese populations, hindering accurate assessment of the impact of UTD on growth, development, and long-term renal function in these children [4, 10].

To address these evidence gaps, we conducted a large-scale hybrid cohort study combining retrospective and prospective designs. Leveraging the extensive database of Beijing Children’s Hospital Shunyi Women’s and Children’s Hospital and the “Shunyi Maternal and Child Health Cohort”, we aimed to: (1) elucidate the 5-year natural history and outcomes of fetal hydronephrosis; (2) identify robust prenatal sonographic predictors of adverse postnatal outcomes, particularly the need for surgery; and (3) propose an optimized, risk-stratified clinical management algorithm based on the UTD classification system to guide postnatal care.

Materials and methods

Study design and ethical approval

A hybrid study design was employed, combining a retrospective cohort (pregnancies from January 2010 to December 2017) with a prospective observational cohort (the “Shunyi Maternal and Child Health Cohort,” enrollments from March 2018 to March 2020). This design leveraged a large historical dataset for comprehensive analysis while prospectively validating data collection processes in a sub-cohort. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board (Ethics Committee) of Beijing Children’s Hospital Shunyi Women’s and Children’s Hospital (Approval No:2025-010−1). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their guardians in the prospective cohort. For the retrospective cohort, the requirement for individual informed consent was waived by the ethics committee due to the use of anonymized historical data, in compliance with national guidelines.

The prospective ‘Shunyi Maternal and Child Health Cohort’ is an institutional cohort study [11] approved by the Institutional Review Board (Ethics Committee) of Beijing Children’s Hospital Shunyi Women’s and Children’s Hospital (Approval No: 2021-01).

Study population

The initial study population comprised 49,098 pregnant women who underwent prenatal ultrasound screening at our institution between January 2010 and March 2020. One case was excluded due to consanguinity. The final analytical cohort included 49,097 pregnant women. Among their offspring, 2,263 were prenatally diagnosed with fetal hydronephrosis (FP) and formed the primary study group for follow-up analysis.

Retrospective cohort

From the initial 39,798 pregnancies in the retrospective period, 39,578 women were included after exclusions. Within this group, 1,822 (4.58%) cases of FP were identified.

Prospective cohort

From the 9,600 pregnancies enrolled in the “Shunyi Maternal and Child Health Cohort,” 9,519 were included after exclusions. Within this group, 441 (4.63%) cases of FP were identified.

To focus on the natural history of isolated UTD, cases with known associated structural anomalies (e.g., posterior urethral valves, ureteroceles), low urinary tract obstructions, or confirmed genetic/chromosomal syndromes were excluded from the analysis, as these conditions independently affect prognosis and management.

Data collection and variable definitions

Data were systematically collected from electronic medical records and the prospective cohort database.

Ultrasound Parameters (Fig. 1): Standardized measurements were obtained during the third trimester or at the time of maximal observed dilation. Key parameters included:

(A) Prenatal ultrasound image demonstrating bilateral hydronephrosis. The left kidney shows significant pelvicaliceal dilation, measuring up to 1.5 cm at its widest point. The right kidney also exhibits pelvicaliceal dilation, measuring up to 1.1 cm. (B) Postnatal follow-up ultrasound image reveals persistent left-sided hydronephrosis, with separation of the collecting system measuring up to 1.4 cm in width

Anteroposterior Pelvic Diameter (APD): Measured in the transverse plane of the fetal kidney, at the level of the renal pelvis, from the inner wall to the inner wall, ensuring the measurement was perpendicular to the long axis of the kidney. Care was taken to avoid including the renal sinus or extra-renal pelvis.

Renal Parenchymal Thickness (RT): Measured from the renal capsule to the edge of the pelvic dilation at the mid-portion of the kidney, in the same transverse plane used for APD measurement.

A/R Ratio: Calculated as Renal Parenchymal Thickness (RT) divided by the Anteroposterior Pelvic Diameter (APD) for each kidney.

Prenatal Data

Ultrasound Parameters: The anteroposterior renal pelvic diameter (APD), renal parenchymal thickness (RT), and the A/R ratio (calculated as RT/APD) were measured during the third trimester or at the time of maximal dilation.

Baseline Information: Gestational age at diagnosis, maternal age, gravidity, and family history of congenital anomalies of the kidney and urinary tract (CAKUT) were recorded.

Postnatal Data

Imaging: Postnatal renal ultrasounds were performed to monitor the dynamic change of APD.The initial ultrasound was performed early, ideally within the first 48 to 72 h of life to avoid the period of relative neonatal dehydration.

Clinical Outcomes: Primary outcomes included: (i) surgical intervention (e.g., dismembered pyeloplasty, vesicostomy); (ii) recurrent urinary tract infection (UTI), defined as ≥ 2 culture-proven episodes per year; (iii) hypertension, defined as blood pressure ≥ 95th percentile for age, sex, and height on ≥ 3 separate occasions.

Quality of Life: At the 5-year follow-up, health-related quality of life was assessed using the Chinese version of the Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory (PedsQL) 4.0 Generic Core Scales (parent proxy-report), which has demonstrated good reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.89 in this population).

Diagnostic and classification criteria

Diagnosis of fetal hydronephrosis

Was made based on established sonographic criteria: APD ≥ 4 mm at 16 ~ 27 weeks’ gestation, or APD ≥ 7 mm at ≥ 28 weeks’ gestation.

Risk stratification

All cases were classified according to the Urinary Tract Dilation (UTD) Classification System [12]. The specific criteria for stratifying cases into UTD A1 (low risk) and UTD A2 ~ 3 (high risk) groups are summarized in Table 1. A key advantage of the UTD system over prior classifications that relied heavily on a single parameter like the anteroposterior pelvic diameter (APD) is its multidimensional nature. It integrates six key sonographic features—APD, calyceal dilation, renal parenchymal thickness, renal parenchymal appearance, ureteral status, and bladder status—along with the assessment of amniotic fluid volume. This comprehensive approach provides a more nuanced and holistic evaluation of the fetal urinary tract, thereby improving the consistency of reporting and the accuracy of risk stratification across different practitioners and centers.

Quality control

Rigorous quality control measures were implemented throughout the study:

Ultrasound Standardization: All APD and RT measurements were performed independently by two senior sonographers. To ensure measurement consistency and quantify interobserver agreement, the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was calculated for a randomly selected subset of 100 scans. The ICC for APD measurements was 0.92 (95% CI: 0.88–0.95.88.95), indicating excellent reliability. Any routine discrepancy exceeding 10% was adjudicated by a third expert radiologist.

Etiology Validation: A multidisciplinary team comprising pediatric urologists, nephrologists, and radiologists conducted consensus reviews for cases with diagnostic uncertainties.

Data Management: All directly identifiable personal information (e.g., names, ID numbers) was removed. The anonymized dataset was encrypted and stored on a secure, access-controlled hospital server. Missing continuous variables were handled using Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE), and missing categorical variables were replaced with the mode.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using R software (Version 4.4.3). Data cleaning and preprocessing included longitudinal merging of datasets, variable renaming, construction of new variables (e.g., left_ratio, right_ratio, and hydronephrosis_tendency_new), and standardization of continuous variables to mitigate scale effects and improve model convergence.

For repeatedly measured data with nested structures, generalized linear mixed-effects models (GLMM) were fitted using the lme4 package, with gestational_ID included as a random intercept to account for within-subject correlations. Binary outcomes (e.g., hydronephrosis, operation, symptom) were modeled using a logit link function. To enhance convergence, the BOBYQA (Bound Optimization BY Quadratic Approximation) optimizer was employed with a maximum of 200,000 iterations.

For the ordinal outcome hydronephrosis_tendency_new, cumulative link mixed models (CLMM) were implemented using the ordinal package. Despite adjustments to optimization methods (ucminf), maximum iterations (maxIter = 1000), and gradient tolerance (gradTol = 1e-4) to address convergence issues, models for right-sided parameters remained unstable, as indicated by non-positive definite Hessian matrices. This indicated that the data did not support reliable statistical inference for this outcome, and therefore these results are not presented.

Generalized linear models (GLM) were used for cross-sectional analyses with risk_final as the exposure, adjusted for age, and fitted using the stats package.

Model results are reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (Wald method) and p-values. Data diagnostics included checks for sparse categories, model convergence warnings, and distribution of outcomes across groups.

A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

To enhance model convergence stability and mitigate the influence of variable scaling, continuous predictor variables (including APD and A/R ratio) were standardized (converted to Z-scores) prior to being entered into the multivariate models. The exceptionally high odds ratios observed for some predictors, particularly left APD, are likely attributable to the phenomenon of complete or quasi-complete separation in the data, as all surgical cases were concentrated in the high-risk group with higher values of these parameters. While this can inflate the magnitude of the odds ratios, it does not invalidate the direction and statistical significance of the association, which remains robust.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the study population

A total of 49,097 pregnant women were included in the final analysis, comprising 39,578 from the retrospective cohort and 9,519 from the prospective cohort. The baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 2. The overall incidence of fetal hydronephrosis was 4.61% (2,263/49,097). No significant differences were observed between the two cohorts in the incidence of fetal hydronephrosis, postnatal hydronephrosis, or surgical intervention rates (all p > 0.05), justifying their combination for subsequent analyses. A slight difference in maternal age was noted (33.5 ± 4.2 vs. 34.7 ± 3.8 years, p = 0.07). The distribution of cases across gestational ages is shown in Table 3. The majority of cases were diagnosed between 28 and 32 weeks (33.58%).

Natural history and overall outcomes

Among the 2,263 prenatally diagnosed cases, 204 (9.01%) were confirmed as hydronephrosis after birth. Only 17 (0.75% of prenatal cases; 8.33% of postnatal cases) underwent surgical intervention. By the final assessment at the 5-year follow-up, hydronephrosis had resolved spontaneously in 97.39% (2,204/2,263) of cases, with persistence in only 59 (2.61%). The cohort included 1,138 males and 1,125 females, with 1,465 left-sided, 638 right-sided, and 160 bilateral cases.

Predictive value of prenatal ultrasound parameters

Multivariate analysis using generalized linear mixed-effects models identified key prenatal predictors for surgical intervention. After adjusting for maternal age and gestational week, left renal APD (OR = 22.05, 95% CI: 21.99–22.11, p < 0.001) and the left A/R ratio (OR = 22.39, 95% CI: 1.31–383.89.31.89, p = 0.03) were identified as the strongest independent predictors. The right A/R ratio was also significantly associated with surgical risk (OR = 3.24, 95% CI: 3.11–3.37, p < 0.001).

For the persistence of hydronephrosis at 5 years, right APD (OR = 0.98, 95% CI: 0.98–0.98, p < 0.001) and right A/R ratio (OR = 1.29, 95% CI: 1.29–1.30, p < 0.001) were significant predictors. Risk stratification based on UTD system

Stratification based on the UTD system revealed significant differences in outcomes. All 17 patients who underwent surgery belonged to the UTD A2 ~ 3 high-risk group. Due to this complete separation, a reliable odds ratio for surgical intervention could not be calculated.

Compared to the UTD A1 low-risk group, the A2 ~ 3 high-risk group showed significantly higher risks for:

Postnatal diagnosis of hydronephrosis (OR = 8.26, 95% CI: 5.73–12.25, p < 0.001).

Persistent hydronephrosis at 5 years (OR = 8.30, 95% CI: 4.53–16.73, p < 0.001).

Development of clinical symptoms (e.g., UTI) (OR = 20.57, 95% CI: 9.14–58.86, p < 0.001).

Among the 2,263 fetuses with prenatal UTD, the majority (n = 1850, 81.75%) were classified as UTD A1 (low-risk). The remaining (n = 413, 18.25%) were classified as UTD A2 ~ 3 (high-risk).

Note on hydronephrosis trend analysis

Initial analysis of hydronephrosis trend as an ordered outcome encountered severe convergence issues due to extreme category imbalance and data sparsity. Despite consolidating categories and employing alternative optimization methods, models for right-sided parameters remained unstable (Hessian matrix not positive definite), indicating that the data did not support reliable inference for trend outcomes. Therefore, these results are not presented herein.

Adherence to follow-up protocol in the UTD A1 cohort

To validate the proposed de-escalated management strategy, we analyzed the actual postnatal imaging follow-up for the UTD A1 low-risk group. Among the 1850 infants in this category, the vast majority (n = 1782, 96.32%) underwent only a single postnatal renal ultrasound within the first year of life. This single examination confirmed the resolution of hydronephrosis in these cases, and no further imaging was required. A small subset of patients (n = 68, 3.68%) received additional ultrasounds, primarily due to transient findings on the initial scan or parental anxiety. None of these additional scans led to a change in management or the identification of significant pathology. This data supports the safety and effectiveness of the single-ultrasound protocol for managing UTD A1 cases.

Discussion

This large-scale long-term cohort study demonstrates that the natural history of fetal hydronephrosis is generally favorable. Advances in medical technology and the widespread clinical application of prenatal ultrasonography have significantly increased the detection rate of prenatal hydronephrosis [10]. We found that over 97% of prenatally detected cases resolved spontaneously by the age of 5 years, with only 0.75% requiring surgical intervention—a rate substantially lower than the 10%~20% commonly reported in the literature [4, 5]. It is important to emphasize, however, that without appropriate management, affected children remain at risk of chronic kidney disease, recurrent urinary tract infections, and hypertension [6]. The most significant finding of this study is the validation of the UTD classification system for prognostic stratification, and the identification of the anterior-posterior renal pelvic diameter (APD) and the A/R ratio as strong independent predictors of the need for surgery.

The lower surgical rate in our study may be attributed to several factors. First, the study population was derived from a general obstetric cohort without selection bias, thus including a large proportion of mild (UTD A1) cases, which constitute the majority and rarely progress [1]. Second, the extended follow-up period of 5 years allowed for the observation of natural resolution in cases that might have been intervened upon in shorter-term studies. Third, ethnic and regional variations might also contribute to this discrepancy. Furthermore, in contrast to previous studies often based on tertiary referral centers—which tend to include more severe cases—our population-based cohort from routine prenatal screening may more accurately reflect the true natural history of fetal hydronephrosis in a general obstetric population.

Previous studies have suggested associations between renal pelvic dilation, renal parenchymal thickness, and the prognosis of hydronephrosis [13, 14]. The internationally adopted SFU grading system classifies hydronephrosis into grades 1 ~ 4; however, research indicates that grading alone has limited value in predicting long-term outcomes [4]. For instance, only 22.45% of children with SFU grades 3 ~ 4 hydronephrosis required surgery, while some with SFU grade 2 also underwent surgery or progressed to renal impairment [15]. This highlights the current lack of widely effective indicators or predictors for the prognosis of hydronephrosis [10]. Although hydronephrosis generally does not influence the timing or mode of delivery [16], it must be acknowledged that some severe cases of bilateral hydronephrosis may necessitate prenatal intervention or pregnancy termination, even though no such cases were included in our study. Currently, prenatal intervention for hydronephrosis is generally not recommended and is primarily reserved for cases of severe lower urinary tract obstruction aimed at improving amniotic fluid volume and promoting pulmonary development [15]. Decisions regarding prenatal intervention or early termination of pregnancy require stringent comprehensive assessment [17], including gestational age, severity of hydronephrosis, renal and pulmonary development, amniotic fluid volume, and the presence of other associated anomalies.

This study utilized the UTD classification system for assessment, which has gained broad acceptance. In addition to renal pelvic width, we placed emphasis on evaluating renal parenchymal thickness and appearance. Thus, the UTD system provides a more comprehensive evaluation, offering reliable and holistic indicators. Our results confirmed that all patients who underwent surgery belonged to the UTD A2 ~ 3 high-risk group, and significant differences existed between the high-risk and low-risk groups in terms of postnatal neonatal hydronephrosis, persistent hydronephrosis at 5 years, and the presence of clinical symptoms.

Furthermore, our analysis clearly demonstrates that prenatal classification as UTD A2 ~ 3 is the strongest risk factor for the persistence of hydronephrosis at 5 years, as evidenced by the significantly elevated odds in this group (OR = 8.30) compared to the UTD A1 group.

Current guidelines recommend initial postnatal ultrasound within 48 h to 1 week after birth; follow-up for SFU grades 1 ~ 2 is suggested every 6 ~ 12 months, while SFU grades 3 ~ 4 require further imaging evaluation [7, 8]. However, the absence of widely accepted criteria for discontinuing follow-up has led to either over-investigation or insufficient monitoring in some patients [4]. Our findings indicate that for UTD A1 low-risk cases, in the absence of urinary tract infection symptoms, strict adherence to the 48-hour to 1-week postnatal ultrasound may not be necessary; a single renal ultrasound within the first year of life may be sufficient.

Data show that the incidence of fetal urinary tract anomalies is approximately 1%~5% [2], with fetal hydronephrosis being the most common [18]. Based on our results, the proportion of urinary tract anomalies appears to be greater than 4.61%, while other anomalies are less frequent (approximately 0.37%), which is generally consistent with reported rates. However, a large foreign study of 5952 neonates reported that only 71 (1.2%) were diagnosed with fetal hydronephrosis, and 18 of these were confirmed postnatally, yielding a relatively high conversion rate of about 25.35% [19]. Other studies have reported varying rates of progression from fetal to neonatal hydronephrosis, up to 46.3%, which differs considerably from our rate of 9.01%. This discrepancy may be related to the specific width cutoff for pelvic dilation (6 ~ 9.9 mm) used in those studies [19, 20], or to the timing of postnatal evaluation: our patients were scanned within the first week; if performed within the first 24 h, the rate of neonatal hydronephrosis might have been higher.

Ultrasonography provides objective and reliable data for diagnosing hydronephrosis [21]. The widespread use of prenatal ultrasound screening has significantly increased the detection of asymptomatic hydronephrosis, facilitating early identification but also potentially leading to overuse. At the same time, prenatal ultrasound often has limited ability to determine the etiology of fetal hydronephrosis, which still largely relies on postnatal imaging modalities (e.g., MRI, nuclear scintigraphy) [7, 8].

Hydronephrosis incidence is often thought to vary by sex and laterality. However, our study found no significant sex difference in fetal hydronephrosis, while the left side was affected 2.3 times more frequently than the right. International reports have also suggested that fetal hydronephrosis is not significantly associated with sex but is linked to laterality [19].

Gestational age at detection may be closely related to prognosis [18]. Some studies suggest that hydronephrosis detected in early pregnancy is more likely to require surgical intervention, while others hold the opposite view, indicating that cases detected in late gestation are more likely to require intervention. Our study showed that hydronephrosis detected in early pregnancy and worsening in the third trimester was more likely to require surgery.

One study reported that after 5 years of follow-up, 11.7% of children developed renal impairment, 57% had associated urinary tract infections, and 2.5% developed hypertension [6]. However, a limitation of that study was its inclusion of only severe cases of prenatal hydronephrosis, excluding mild or moderate ones, which may have inflated the perceived incidence of severe sequelae. Another study reported that 54% of hydronephrosis cases persisted or worsened [22], but its case selection might have been constrained if mild cases did not seek medical attention, introducing selection bias. A foreign study of 227 cases of fetal hydronephrosis reported an overall spontaneous resolution rate of approximately 67.8%, but with a relatively short follow-up (minimum 6 months); it also indicated that higher-grade hydronephrosis required a longer time to resolve [23]. We found a spontaneous resolution rate of 97.39% after 5 years, indicating that the vast majority of fetal hydronephrosis has a tendency to resolve spontaneously.

It should be noted that the UTD A1 low-risk group is not absolutely exempt from the need for surgery, and vigilance should still be maintained. Studies based on the SFU grading system have shown that some SFU grade 2 cases may also require surgery or progress to renal impairment [15]. This finding somewhat contrasts with the conventional understanding of the management of SFU grades 1 ~ 2 hydronephrosis. Although our study used the UTD system, in terms of renal pelvic diameter, the UTD A1 low-risk group is fundamentally similar to the mild categories of the SFU system. In our cohort, followed for over 5 years, no cases in the UTD A1 low-risk group required surgery.

This study has several limitations. First, it is a single-center cohort study primarily including participants from Beijing, Hebei, and other regions of China. The results may be influenced by local healthcare standards and population genetic background, and caution should be exercised when generalizing to other regions in China or global populations. Second, renal function parameters (such as estimated glomerular filtration rate eGFR or renal dynamic scintigraphy results) were not included, precluding assessment of the potential long-term impact of hydronephrosis on renal function; future studies should incorporate such metrics. Third, although ultrasound measurements were quality-controlled (performed independently by two physicians), results of consistency tests such as the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) were not reported for all measures, and measurement bias may exist. Fourth, for UTD A1 cases, this study did not further analyze the impact of comorbidities (e.g., increased post-void residual urine volume) on prognosis, and thus cannot entirely exclude the possibility of intervention needs in very low-risk cases.Fifth, the exceptionally high odds ratios for predictors like left APD are a known statistical artifact resulting from complete or quasi-complete separation in the data. While this confirms their strong predictive power, the exact magnitude of the OR should be interpreted with caution. Sixth, this study primarily focused on anatomical resolution and the need for surgical intervention as key outcomes. We acknowledge the lack of systematic functional renal data, such as serial measurements of estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) or diuretic renography (e.g., MAG3 scan), as a limitation. The incorporation of such functional metrics in future studies would provide a more comprehensive assessment of long-term renal health in children with a history of fetal hydronephrosis.Seventh, detailed information on urinary tract infections (UTIs), such as causative organisms and specific long-term antimicrobial prophylaxis strategies, was not systematically collected. While UTI was diagnosed and treated according to clinical guidelines, the absence of this granular data limits our ability to formulate specific long-term management recommendations for UTI in this cohort.Eighth, the hybrid design of this study, while enabling a large sample size, incorporates a retrospective component. Although rigorous data quality control measures were implemented, the retrospective nature of a portion of the data collection may introduce inherent limitations in data completeness and standardization that cannot be fully quantified, potentially introducing bias.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this large cohort study affirms that the vast majority of fetal UTD follows a benign course, with spontaneous resolution occurring in over 97% of cases by 5 years of age. The UTD classification system proved to be a powerful and valid tool for prenatal risk stratification, effectively identifying the small subset of children requiring surgical intervention. For prenatally classified UTD A1 infants who remain asymptomatic postnatally, our findings support a de-escalated management protocol, deferring the initial ultrasound to within the first year of life, thereby optimizing resources and reducing parental anxiety.

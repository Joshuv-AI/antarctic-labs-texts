Abstract
We prove existence and multiplicity results for the nonlinear and nonlocal PDE − Δ ​ u + ( I α ⋆ | u | p ) ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u = f ⁡ ( | x | , u ) in ​ ℝ N , -\Delta u+(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})\,|u|^{p-2}\,u=f(|x|,u)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}, where N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , I α : ℝ N ∖ { 0 } → ℝ I_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\to\mathbb{R} is the Riesz potential of order α ∈ ( 1 , N ) , p > 1 , \alpha\in(1,N),p>1, and the local nonlinearity f : [ 0 , ∞ ) × ℝ → ℝ f:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} is subject to a new class of assumptions. We find solutions to this zero-mass problem in a Coulomb–Sobolev space using a new scaling based approach in critical point theory, by which we classify the possibly different behaviour of the nonlinearity f f at zero and at infinity in terms of the scaling properties of the left hand side of the equation. This is accomplished identifying a scaling invariant PDE which can be interpreted as a nonlinear eigenvalue problem, for which a sequence of eigenvalues { λ k } \{\lambda_{k}\} is conveniently defined via the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} –cohomological index of Fadell and Rabinowitz. This index allows us to use new critical group estimates (and scaling-based linking sets) which might not be possible via the classical genus. Within a fairly broad set of parameters N , α , p N,\alpha,p and class of assumptions on the local nonlinearity f , f, we establish compactness results for an associated action functional and find multiple solutions as critical points, whose existence and number is sensitive to the “resonance” of f f with the sequence of eigenvalues for the scaling invariant problem, a construction which is at places reminiscent, in the present nonlinear setting, of the classical Fredholm alternative. As a byproduct of our analysis, letting p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 allows us to capture general nonlinearities f f of Sobolev-subcritical, critical, or supercritical growth.

Introduction

In this paper we prove existence and multiplicity results for the nonlinear and nonlocal PDE

-\Delta u+(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})\,|u|^{p-2}\,u=f(|x|,u)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.1)

where a new and broad class of assumptions on the Carathéodory function
f:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is considered.

Here
N\geq 2,p>1
,
I_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\to\mathbb{R}
is the Riesz potential of order
\alpha\in(1,N)
defined by

I_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{A_{\alpha}}{|x|^{N-\alpha}},\qquad A_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N-\alpha}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\pi^{N/2}\,2^{\alpha}},

and the choice of the constant
A_{\alpha}
ensures that the kernel
I_{\alpha}
enjoys the semigroup property

I_{\alpha+\beta}=I_{\alpha}\star I_{\beta}

for each
\alpha,\beta\in(0,N)
with
\alpha+\beta<N
, see for example Du Plessis
[
13
, pg. 73-74]
.
Hence the corresponding (Hartree) energy term in the equation can be conveniently written, and in certain instances treated, as an
L^{2}
-like term

A_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}\,|u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}\,dx\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|I_{\alpha/2}\star|u|^{p}\right|^{2}\,dx.

(1.2)

Using the language of the celebrated work of Berestycki and Lions
[
6
,
7
]
, equation (
1.1
) may be regarded as a zero-mass problem, which is naturally set, unlike its positive-mass variants, in a new
Coulomb-Sobolev
space which in general does not coincide with the classical Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
; see Mercuri et al.
[
27
]
.

When
N=3,\alpha=p=2,
(
1.1
) appears in the literature, with a number of interesting variants, as an approximation of the Hartree–Fock model for a quantum many-body system of electrons, see e.g. Le Bris and Lions
[
19
]
and references therein, and arise, as highlighted by Sanchez and Soler
[
33
]
in models for semiconductors. We shall refer to equation (
1.1
), with our “extended” range of parameters, as
Schrödinger–Poisson–Slater’s
(Bokanowski et al.​
[
8
]
), although variants of it may be known under different names, such as
Schrödinger–Poisson’s
(Ambrosetti
[
1
]
),
Schrödinger–Poisson–
X_{\alpha}
’s
(Mauser
[
26
]
), or
Maxwell–Schrödinger–Poisson’s
(Benci and Fortunato
[
5
]
and Catto et al.​
[
10
]
). The function
|u|^{2}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
typically represents the density of electrons in the original many-body system, whereas the Coulomb-Riesz term represents the Coulombic repulsion between the electrons. The local term
f(|x|,u)=|u|^{q-2}\,u
was introduced by Slater
[
34
]
with
q=8/3
as a local approximation of the exchange potential in the Hartree–Fock model (see, e.g., Bokanowski et al.​
[
8
]
, Bokanowski and Mauser
[
9
]
, and Mauser
[
26
]
).

When
N=3,\alpha=p=2,
(
1.1
) is also related to the Thomas–Fermi–Dirac–von Weizsäcker (TFDW) model of Density Functional Theory (DFT); see, e.g., Lieb
[
20
,
21
]
, Le Bris and Lions
[
19
]
, Lu and Otto
[
25
]
, Frank et al.​
[
15
]
, and references therein. In the context of the TFDW model, the local nonlinearity takes the form
f(|x|,u)=u^{5/3}-u^{7/3}
, for which recently Mercuri and Perera
[
28
]
have proved the existence of infinitely many solutions at negative energy levels, a phenomenon which could be regarded as complementary to that highlighted in
[
25
]
, which instead provides nonexistence of least energy solutions with a prescribed large mass.

The mathematical choice of considering in equation (
1.1
) either a more general local nonlinearity
f(|x|,u)
and set of parameters
N,\alpha,p,
is motivated by physical considerations again arising from DFT and quantum chemistry. In fact, typical models in this area, such as Kohn-Sham’s, involve local nonlinearities
f
which are not explicitly known; see, e.g., Anantharaman and Cancès
[
3
]
and references therein. Moreover, as recently highlighted in Lu et al.
[
24
]
, some models may require in the energy term (
1.2
) a choice of the parameters different from
N=3,\alpha=p=2.

Allowing
N,\alpha,p
to vary in a certain admissible range, produces various critical numbers which are responsible for the loss of local and global compactness properties of embeddings into Lebesgue spaces. As shown in
[
27
]
, this is the case of

•

a
Coulomb-Sobolev
exponent
q_{CS}=2(2p+2)/(2+\alpha)
which together with the classical Sobolev exponent is a threshold for optimal embeddings into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N});

•

q_{\textrm{rad}}=2\,[2p\,(N-1)+N-\alpha])/(3N+\alpha-4),
which characterises for
\alpha>1
the embeddings of the subspace of radial functions into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N});
see Theorem
2.1
below;

•

the exponent
p_{CS}=(N+\alpha)/(N-2),
which (provided
p<p_{CS}
or
p>p_{CS}
) determines, when
N\geq 3,
whether the above thresholds lie below or above the classical Sobolev exponent
2^{*}=2N/(N-2);

•

the exponent
q^{\textrm{loc}}_{CS}=2\alpha/(\alpha-2),
which governs, when
\alpha>2,
the compactness of the relevant embeddings into
L^{p}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the validity of a nonlocal Brezis-Lieb property of the term (
1.2
).

This rich functional analytical and variational scenario not yet explored in full, even when
f(|x|,u)=|u|^{q-2}u
allows dramatic changes in the geometry of the energy functional associated with (
1.1
), which may exhibit, according to the different values of
q,
either a coercive, or a mountain-pass like behavior (see e.g.
[
27
, Remark 7.1]
); or, the nonstandard structure (in terms of the Euler-Lagrange equation) of a
nonlinear eigenvalue problem
. The latter has been highlighted first by Ianni and Ruiz
[
18
]
in the case
N=3,\alpha=p=2,q=3,
as a consequence of the invariance of (
1.1
) by the scaling
u_{t}=t^{2}u(t\,\cdot\,).

Our main contribution is to extend some recent existence and multiplicity results of Mercuri and Perera
[
28
]
, which deal with the case
N=3,\alpha=p=2,
adapting the scaling based variational approach developed in
[
28
]
to situations in which the mountain-pass geometry, linking geometry based on linear subspaces, or
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry may not be present, making most of the available variational techniques inspired by the classical work of Ambrosetti and Rabinowitz
[
2
]
, not directly applicable. Roughly speaking, following
[
28
]
, we are able to classify the asymptotic/variational features of the nonlinearity
f
in terms of the aforementioned eigenvalue problem, a reasoning which is reminiscent, in our nonlinear context, of the classical Fredholm alternative.
We construct minimax eigenvalues of this eigenvalue problem using the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-cohomological index of Fadell and Rabinowitz
[
14
]
, a choice which allows us to use new critical group estimates (and scaling-based linking sets) which might not be possible using the classical genus. Our analysis is complementary to that recently provided in a Sobolev-subcritical regime by Gloss et al.
[
17
]
, where a fractional variant of (
1.1
) has been studied in the case
p=2.
In some of our results we use the assumption
2p<2^{*}
to guarantee the boundedness of the Palais-Smale sequences, a condition we hope to relax in future. As a byproduct of our approach (and of the functional setting with
p\neq 2
), we are then able to capture nonlinearities
f
of Sobolev-subcritical, critical, or supercritical growth. However, as in
[
28
]
, in our construction we will replace this well-established terminology with terms like “subscaled”, “scaled”, and “superscaled” to reflect more effectively and classify the various variational regimes which may occur, depending on
f
and on the values of our parameters.

2
Existence and multiplicity results in Coulomb-Sobolev spaces

We seek solutions to our PDE (
1.1
) in the so-called Coulomb-Sobolev space
E^{\alpha,\,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
consisting of weakly differentiable functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
for which the norm

\left\|u\right\|:=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|I_{\alpha/2}\star|u|^{p}\right|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\right]^{1/2}

is finite. We work in the subspace
E^{\alpha,\,p}_{\text{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of radial functions in
E^{\alpha,\,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The special case
N=3
,
\alpha=2
, and
p=2
of this space was studied in Lions
[
23
,
22
]
and Ruiz
[
31
]
, where it was shown that
E^{2,\,2}_{\text{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a separable and uniformly convex Banach space that is embedded in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
continuously for
q\in(18/7,6]
and compactly for
q\in(18/7,6)
. The general case
N\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(1,N)
, and
p>1
was studied in Mercuri et al.​
[
27
]
, from which we recall and use the following embedding result.

Set
2^{*}=2N/(N-2)
whenever
N\geq 3
, and
2^{*}=\infty
for
N=2
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
27
, Theorems 4 & 5]
)
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(1,N)
,
p>1
, and

q_{\textrm{rad}}=\frac{2\,[2p\,(N-1)+N-\alpha]}{3N+\alpha-4}.

(i)

If
1/p>(N-2)/(N+\alpha)
, then
E^{\alpha,\,p}_{\text{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is embedded in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
continuously for

\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{N}\leq\dfrac{1}{q}<\dfrac{1}{q_{\textrm{rad}}}

and compactly if the inequalities are strict.

(ii)

If
1/p<(N-2)/(N+\alpha)
, then
E^{\alpha,\,p}_{\text{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is embedded in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
continuously for

\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{N}\geq\dfrac{1}{q}>\dfrac{1}{q_{\textrm{rad}}}

and compactly if the inequalities are strict.

Remark 2.2
.

An interesting feature of our functional setting is that
E^{\alpha,\,p}_{\text{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\not\subset L^{q_{\textrm{rad}}}(\mathbb{R}^{N});
see
[
31
,
27
]
.

In view of Theorem
2.1
, a natural growth condition for the local nonlinearity
f
is

|f(|x|,t)|\leq a_{1}\,|t|^{q_{1}-1}+a_{2}\,|t|^{q_{2}-1}+a(x)\quad\text{for a.a.\! }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and all }t\in\mathbb{R},

(2.1)

where
a_{1},a_{2}>0
, and
a\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with either

\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{N}\leq\dfrac{1}{q_{2}}<\dfrac{1}{q_{1}}<\dfrac{1}{q_{\textrm{rad}}}

if
1/p>(N-2)/(N+\alpha)
, and some
r>1
where

(a)

r\in(1,q_{\textrm{rad}}/(q_{\textrm{rad}}-1))
if
N=2
,

(b)

r\in[2^{\ast}/(2^{\ast}-1),q_{\textrm{rad}}/(q_{\textrm{rad}}-1))
if
N\geq 3

or

\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{N}\geq\dfrac{1}{q_{2}}>\dfrac{1}{q_{1}}>\dfrac{1}{q_{\textrm{rad}}}

if
1/p<(N-2)/(N+\alpha)
and some
r>1
where
(q_{\textrm{rad}}/(q_{\textrm{rad}}-1),2^{\ast}/(2^{\ast}-1)]
.

Under this growth assumption, the associated energy functional

\Phi(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|I_{\alpha/2}\star|u|^{p}\right|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(|x|,u)\,dx,\quad u\in E=E^{\alpha,\,p}_{\text{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
F(|x|,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(|x|,\tau)\,d\tau
is the primitive of
f
, is well-defined, of class
C^{1}
, and its critical points are the weak solutions to equation (
1.1
).

The nonlinear eigenvalue problem

-\Delta u+(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})\,|u|^{p-2}\,u=\lambda\,|u|^{q_{CS}-2}\,u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

(2.2)

will play a key role in our approach to existence and multiplicity for equation (
1.1
). In what follows we simply set

q=q_{CS}=2\,(2p+\alpha)/(2+\alpha).

This eigenvalue problem is not only nonlinear, but also nonhomogeneous in the sense that the three terms that appear in it contain different powers of
u
. However, it has a certain scaling invariance property. In fact, let
\sigma=(2+\alpha)/2(p-1)
and consider the scaling

E^{\alpha,\,p}_{\text{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times[0,\infty)\to E^{\alpha,\,p}_{\text{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad(u,t)\mapsto u_{t}

defined as

u_{t}(x)=t^{\sigma}u(tx)

if
1/p>(N-2)/(N+\alpha)
and

u_{t}(x)=\begin{cases}t^{-\sigma}u(x/t)&\text{if }t>0\\[5.0pt]
0&\text{if }t=0\end{cases}

if
1/p<(N-2)/(N+\alpha)
. Setting

{\cal L}(u)=-\Delta u+(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})\,|u|^{p-2}\,u,\qquad{\cal L}_{\lambda}(u)={\cal L}(u)-\lambda\,|u|^{q-2}\,u,

we have

{\cal L}_{\lambda}(u_{t})(x)=\begin{cases}t^{(4p+\alpha-2)/2(p-1)}\,{\cal L}_{\lambda}(u)(tx)&\text{if }1/p>(N-2)/(N+\alpha)\\[10.0pt]
t^{-(4p+\alpha-2)/2(p-1)}\,{\cal L}_{\lambda}(u)(x/t)&\text{if }1/p<(N-2)/(N+\alpha)\end{cases}

for
t>0
, so if
u
is a solution of the equation (
2.2
), then the entire
1
-parameter family of functions
\left\{u_{t}:t\geq 0\right\}
are, too. We shall refer to it as a scaled eigenvalue problem. We note that
q\in(q_{\textrm{rad}},2^{\ast})
if
1/p>(N-2)/(N+\alpha)
and
q\in(2^{\ast},q_{\textrm{rad}})
if
1/p<(N-2)/(N+\alpha)
. We will see that the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
is positive and there exists an unbounded sequence of eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots

(see Theorem
3.6
).

The scaled eigenvalue problem (
2.2
) leads to a natural classification of the local nonlinearity
f
in equation (
1.1
) in terms of its scaling properties. Assume that the limit

l_{\infty}=\lim_{|t|\to\infty}\,\frac{f(|x|,t)}{|t|^{q-2}\,t}

exists uniformly a.e. If
1/p>(N-2)/(N+\alpha)
and

\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{N}\leq\dfrac{1}{r}<\dfrac{1}{q_{\textrm{rad}}}

then

\big({\cal L}(u_{t})-|u_{t}|^{r-2}\,u_{t}\big)(x)=t^{(4p+\alpha-2)/2(p-1)}\,\big({\cal L}(u)-t^{(r-q)\sigma}\,|u|^{r-2}\,u\big)(tx),

so we will say that

(i)

f
is subscaled if
l_{\infty}=0
,

(ii)

f
is asymptotically scaled if
l_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,

(iii)

f
is superscaled if
l_{\infty}=\pm\infty
.

On the other hand, if
1/p<(N-2)/(N+\alpha)
and
r\in[2^{\ast},q_{\textrm{rad}})
, then

\big({\cal L}(u_{t})-|u_{t}|^{r-2}\,u_{t}\big)(x)=t^{-(4p+\alpha-2)/2(p-1)}\,\big({\cal L}(u)-t^{(q-r)\sigma}\,|u|^{r-2}\,u\big)(x/t)

for
t>0
, so we will say that

(i)

f
is subscaled if
l_{\infty}=\pm\infty
,

(ii)

f
is asymptotically scaled if
l_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,

(iii)

f
is superscaled if
l_{\infty}=0
.

We observe and elaborate on it in the sequel that, as the variational features of the energy functional
\Phi
associated with the equation (
1.1
) are sensitive to the various asymptotic regimes of
f
classified above, we will apply suitable (and new) minimax principles, accordingly. Our results include sufficient conditions for the existence of nontrivial solutions in the case
f(|x|,0)\equiv 0
.
Let us now state our main existence and multiplicity results, whose proof is presented in Section
4
after a section on the relevant preliminaries in critical point theory (Section 3).

3
A glance at critical point theorems by scaling

This section is devoted to the relevant abstract tools in critical point theory which will be used to prove our main existence and multiplicity results. Some of the material has been developed recently in
[
28
]
with an approach based on the novel notion of
scaling in a Banach space
which is defined as follows.

3.1
Scaling and scaled operators on Banach spaces

Definition 3.1
.

Let
W
be a reflexive Banach space. A scaling on
W
is a continuous mapping
W\times[0,\infty)\to W
,
(u,t)\mapsto u_{t}
satisfying

(H_{1})

(u_{t_{1}})_{t_{2}}=u_{t_{1}t_{2}}
with
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
for all
u\in W
and
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0,

(H_{2})

(\tau u)_{t}=\tau u_{t}
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}

t\geq 0
for all
u\in W
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\geq 0
,

(H_{3})

u_{0}=0
and
u_{1}=u
for all
u\in W
,

(H_{4})

u_{t}
is bounded on bounded sets of
W\times[0,\infty)
.

(H_{5})

\exists s>0
such that
\|u_{t}\|=O(t^{s})
as
t\to\infty
uniformly on bounded sets.

Denote by
W^{*}
the dual of
W
. Recall that
q\in C(W,W^{*})
is a potential operator if there is a functional
Q\in C^{1}(W,\mathbb{R})
, called a potential for
q
, such that
Q^{\prime}=q
. By replacing
Q
with
Q-Q(0)
if necessary, we may assume that
Q(0)=0
.

Definition 3.2
.

A scaled operator is an odd potential operator
q\in C(W,W^{*})
that maps bounded sets into bounded sets and satisfies

q(u_{t})v_{t}=t^{s}q(u)v,\quad\forall u,v\in W,t\geq 0.

We denote by
{\cal A}_{s}
the class of odd scaled potential operators.

Let us denote by
I_{s}
the potential of the scaled operator
A_{s}
with
I_{s}(0)=0
. Of course
I_{s}
is even, bounded on bounded sets, and satisfies the scaling property

I_{s}(u_{t})=t^{s}I_{s}(u),\quad\forall u\in W,t\geq 0

(see
[
28
, Proposition 2.2]
).

We will consider the question of existence and multiplicity of solutions to equations of the form

A_{s}(u)=f(u)\quad\text{in }W^{*},

where
f\in C(W,W^{*})
is a potential operator, and
A_{s}
is a scaled operator satisfying

(H_{6})

A_{s}(u)u>0
for all
u\in W\backslash\left\{0\right\}
,

(H_{7})

every sequence
(u_{j})
in
W
such that
u_{j}\rightharpoonup u
and
A_{s}(u_{j})(u_{j}-u)\to 0
has a subsequence that converges strongly to
u
.

Solutions of the above equation coincide with critical points of the
C^{1}
-functional

\Phi(u)=I_{s}(u)-F(u),\quad u\in W,

where
F
is the potential of
f
with
F(0)=0
.

Now let us consider the eigenvalue problem

A_{s}(u)=\lambda B_{s}(u)\quad\text{in }W^{*},

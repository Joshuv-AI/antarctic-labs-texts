Abstract
A novel measurement technique of strong-phase differences between the decay amplitudes of D 0 {{D}^{0}} and D ¯ 0 {\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006ptD}}{}^{0} mesons is introduced which exploits quantum-correlated D ​ D ¯ {D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\; pairs produced by e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collisions at energies above the ψ ⁡ ( 3770 ) \psi(3770) production threshold, where D ​ D ¯ {D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\; pairs are produced in both even and odd eigenstates of the charge-conjugation symmetry. Employing this technique, the first determination of a D 0 {{D}^{0}} – D ¯ 0 {\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006ptD}}{}^{0} relative strong phase is reported with such data samples. The strong-phase difference between D 0 → K − ​ π + {{D}^{0}}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}} and D ¯ 0 → K − π + {{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006ptD}}{}^{0}}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}} decays, δ K ​ π D \delta_{K\pi}^{D} , is measured to be δ K ​ π D = ( 192.8 − 12.4 − 2.4 + 11.0 + 1.9 ) ∘ \delta_{K\pi}^{D}=\left(192.8^{+11.0+1.9}_{-12.4-2.4}\right)^{\circ} , using a dataset corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 7.13 fb − 1 \text{\,fb}^{-1} collected at center-of-mass energies between 4.13 − 4.23 ​ GeV 4.13-4.23\text{ GeV} by the BESIII experiment.

II
BESIII Detector, Simulation and Data Samples

The BESIII detector
Ablikim et al. 2010
records symmetric
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions provided by the BEPCII storage ring
Yu et al. 2016
in the center-of-mass energy range from 1.84 to 4.95 GeV, with a peak luminosity of
1.1\times 10^{33}\;\text{cm}^{-2}\text{s}^{-1}
achieved at
\sqrt{s}=3.773\;\text{GeV}
. BESIII has collected large data samples in this energy region
Ablikim et al. 2020a
. The cylindrical core of the BESIII detector covers 93% of the full solid angle and consists of a helium-based multilayer drift chamber (MDC), a time-of-flight system (TOF), and a CsI(Tl) electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC), which are all enclosed in a superconducting solenoidal magnet providing a 1.0 T magnetic field. The solenoid is supported by an octagonal flux-return yoke with resistive plate counter muon identification modules interleaved with steel.
The charged-particle momentum resolution at
1~{\rm GeV}/c
is
0.5\%
, and the
{\rm d}E/{\rm d}x
resolution is
6\%
for electrons from Bhabha scattering. The EMC measures photon energies with a resolution of
2.5\%
(
5\%
) at
1
GeV in the barrel (end-cap) region. The time resolution in the plastic scintillator TOF barrel region is 68 ps, while that in the end-cap region was 110 ps. The end cap TOF system was upgraded in 2015 using multigap resistive plate chamber technology, providing a time resolution of 60 ps, which benefits
\sim 85
% of the data used in this analysis
Li et al. 2017
;
Guo et al. 2017
;
Cao et al. 2020
.

Monte Carlo (MC) simulated data samples produced with a
geant4
-based
Agostinelli et al. 2003
software package, which includes the geometric description of the BESIII detector and the detector response, are used to determine detection efficiencies and to estimate backgrounds. The simulation models the beam-energy spread and initial-state radiation (ISR) in the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilations with the generator
kkmc

Jadach et al. 2000
. The generator does not include the quantum correlations. Corrections are applied for this where required for background estimates. The inclusive MC sample includes the production of open-charm processes, the ISR production of vector charmonium(-like) states, and the continuum processes incorporated in
kkmc

Jadach et al. 2000
. All particle decays are modelled with
evtgen

Lange 2001
using branching fractions either taken from the Particle Data Group (PDG)
Navas et al. 2024
, when available, or otherwise estimated with
lundcharm

Chen et al. 2000
;
Yang et al. 2014
.
Final-state radiation from charged final-state particles is incorporated using the
photos
package
Barberio et al. 1991
.

III
Selection Requirements

The selection of candidates is divided into two stages. First, the
D
-meson decays are reconstructed in six final states,
{{\textit{K}}^{\pm}}{{\pi}^{\mp}}
,
{{\textit{K}}^{+}}{{\textit{K}}^{-}}
,
{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
,
{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{{\pi}^{0}}
,
{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{0}}
and
{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
. The
D
-decay candidates are combined to form
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs, from which a single best candidate is selected per event through the criteria described later in this section. Then, requirements are placed on the pairs to distinguish between the various
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
production mechanisms.

Charged tracks detected in the MDC are required to be within a polar angle (
\theta
) range of
|\!\cos\theta|<0.93
, where
\theta
is defined with respect to the
z
axis, which is the symmetry axis of the MDC. Furthermore, the distance of closest approach to the interaction point (IP) must be less than 10 cm along the
z
axis,
|V_{z}|
, and less than 1 cm in the transverse plane,
|V_{xy}|
. Particle identification (PID) for charged tracks combines measurements of the energy deposited in the MDC (d
E
/d
x
) and the flight time in the TOF to form likelihoods
\mathcal{L}(h)~(h=p,K,\pi)
for each hadron
h
hypothesis.
Tracks are identified as kaons and pions by comparing the likelihoods for the kaon and pion hypotheses,
\mathcal{L}(K)>\mathcal{L}(\pi)
and
\mathcal{L}(\pi)>\mathcal{L}(K)
, respectively.

The
{{\pi}^{0}}
mesons are reconstructed through the
{{\pi}^{0}}\to\gamma\gamma
decay. Photon candidates are identified using isolated showers in the EMC. The deposited energy of each shower must be more than 25 MeV in the barrel region (
|\!\cos\theta|<0.80
) and more than 50 MeV in the end cap region (
0.86<|\!\cos\theta|<0.92
). To exclude showers that originate from charged tracks, the angle subtended by the EMC shower and the position of the closest charged track at the EMC must be greater than 10 degrees as measured from the IP. To suppress electronic noise and showers unrelated to the event, the difference between the EMC time and the event start time is required to be within [0, 700] ns. The
{\pi}^{0}
candidates are reconstructed from two photons, where at least one produces a shower in the barrel of the EMC to provide the necessary energy resolution. The reconstructed invariant mass of the
\gamma\gamma
pair must be
M_{\gamma\gamma}\in[115,150]\text{ MeV}/c^{2}
. Finally, a kinematic fit constraining the two photons to the known
{\pi}^{0}
mass
Navas et al. 2024
is performed which is required to converge with
\chi^{2}<50
.

Each
{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}
candidate is reconstructed from two oppositely charged tracks satisfying
|V_{z}|<
20 cm. The two charged tracks are assigned as
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
without imposing further PID criteria. They are constrained to originate from a common vertex and are required to have an invariant mass
M_{{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}}
satisfying
|M_{{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}}-m_{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}|<
12
\text{ MeV}/c^{2}
, where
m_{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}
is the known
{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}
mass
Navas et al. 2024
. A secondary-vertex fit is performed to the
K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}
children tracks which must converge with
\chi^{2}<100
.

The
K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}
mesons used to form
{D}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{0}}
candidates are subject to flight-distance significance requirements to reject background from
{D}\to{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{{\pi}^{0}}
decays. Specifically, the decay length of the
K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}
candidate is required to be greater than twice the vertex resolution away from the IP. The opposite requirement is placed on the
{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
pair in the selection of
{D}\to{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{{\pi}^{0}}
candidates.

V
Measurement of
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}

V.1
Overview

A measurement of the strong-phase difference between
{{D}^{0}}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}
and
{{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006ptD}}{}^{0}}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}
decays,
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
, is performed. This determination assumes the expected quantum-correlated behavior of the
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs from each production mechanism that is validated in Sec.
IV
. The strong-phase difference
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
is measured using
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}\ \mathrm{vs.}\ Y
decays, where
Y
is one of the four
C\!P
eigenstates presented in Table
4
or
{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
, and is referred to as the ‘tag’ throughout.

From Eq. (
4
), it can be shown that for the
C\!P
tags, the quantum correlations enhance or suppress the decay rate relative to the scenario where the decays of the
D
and
\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}
mesons are independent of each other by the factor

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2C\lambda{{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}}-(1+C){y}}{1+{(r_{K\pi}^{D})^{2}}},

(21)

where terms smaller than
\mathcal{O}({r_{K\pi}^{D}}{y})
are neglected, and the parameter
\lambda=+1,\;-1\;\text{and }2{F_{+}^{\pi\pi{{\pi}^{0}}}}-1
for the
C\!P
-even, odd,
{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{{\pi}^{0}}
modes, respectively. The relative enhancement is predicted to be around
\pm 12\%
, where the preceding sign depends on the values of
C
and
\lambda
.

The
C\!P
tags are used to measure the observable
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
. However, this alone does not allow for a single solution of
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
. Input is required from
{{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}\ \text{vs.}\ {K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decays, which provide sensitivity to the observables
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
and
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
through the variation of the
{D}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decay strong-phase difference across phase space. In the previous measurement of
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
performed at the
\psi(3770)
resonance
Ablikim et al. 2022a
,
{{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}\ \text{vs.}\ {{\textit{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{L}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decays were also used. However, the
{\textit{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{L}}
mesons typically decay outside the
BESIII
detector due to their long lifetime, and so the final state is partially reconstructed. They are not used in this analysis because the strategy employed to isolate the production mechanism in Sec.
III
also relies on partial reconstruction.

In the measurement of
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
and
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
described in Sec.
V.3
, the
{D}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decay phase space is divided into regions according to the “equal-
\Delta\delta_{D}

Ablikim et al. 2020b
. The phase space is characterized by the squared reconstructed invariant masses
m_{-}^{2}\equiv m^{2}({K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{-}})
and
m_{+}^{2}\equiv m^{2}({K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}})

m_{-}^{2}=m_{+}^{2}
. A region with index
j
(
-j
) has
m_{+}^{2}>m_{-}^{2}
(
m_{+}^{2}<m_{-}^{2}
) and therefore the indices range from
-8
to 8, excluding 0.

Inputs for the hadronic parameters of the
{D}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decay are necessary to extract the observables. They are the fraction of
{{D}^{0}}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decays that are in region

, denoted

, and the amplitude weighted averaged cosine (sine) difference of the strong-phase difference between
{{D}^{0}}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
and
{{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006ptD}}{}^{0}}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}

(

\displaystyle{K_{i}}

\displaystyle=\int_{i}|A({{D}^{0}}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}})|^{2}\text{d}m_{-}^{2}\text{d}m_{+}^{2},

(22)

\displaystyle{K_{-i}}

\displaystyle=\int_{i}|A({{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006ptD}}{}^{0}}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}})|^{2}\text{d}m_{-}^{2}\text{d}m_{+}^{2},

(23)

V.2
Measuring
{{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}}
using
D

C\!P
-eigenstates

The signal yields are determined for each
C\!P
-eigenstate tag and production mechanism using fits similar to those performed in the previous section. Alongside the five components first described in Sec.
IV
, additional backgrounds from
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
decays are present, which can leak into the signal regions. Therefore, fit ranges wider than those in Sec.
IV
are used. Consequently, an exponential function, instead of a linear polynomial, is used to describe the combinatorial background in the majority of fits, where it is found to peak towards lower masses. In the fits to
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}\ \mathrm{vs.}\ {{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{{\pi}^{0}}
candidates which pass the selection requirements for the

and

production mechanisms, it is found that an exponential is not a good model for the combinatorial background in the reconstructed
{D}\to K\pi
invariant mass, and therefore a second order polynomial is used. The choice of background shape is considered as a systematic uncertainty.

The aforementioned backgrounds from
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
decays, the specific details of which follow, are modeled by a KDE of the distribution in simulation. Firstly,
{D}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{0}}
decays can be wrongly reconstructed as
{D}\to{{\textit{K}}^{+}}{{\textit{K}}^{-}}
, where the
{{\pi}^{+}}
is misidentified as a
{{\textit{K}}^{+}}
and the
{{\pi}^{0}}
is missed in the reconstruction. In the fits to candidates passing the selections to isolate
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs from the
e^{+}e^{-}\to{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
,

and
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}\to{\gamma\gamma/\pi^{0}\pi^{0}}{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}

In the others, the number passing the selections is significantly lower and therefore the yield is fixed relative to signal using the rate in simulation with a small correction factor to account for the effects of quantum correlations. In both cases the contamination is less than 10% of the signal yield.

Background from
{D}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{0}}
decays is also present in the fits to candidates reconstructed as the
{D}\to{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{{\pi}^{0}}
decay, where the
{{\textit{K}}^{-}}
is misidentified as a
{{\pi}^{-}}
. In each fit the yield of this contamination is a floating parameter.

The parameter
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
is extracted in a
\chi^{2}
fit which compares the ratios of observed and predicted signal yields in the
C
-even and odd processes,

\displaystyle\chi^{2}=\sum_{Y}\left(\frac{R_{Y}^{\mathrm{obs}}-R_{Y}^{\mathrm{pred}}}{\delta R_{Y}^{\mathrm{obs}}}\right)^{2},

(27)

where the sum runs over the
C\!P
-eigenstate tags. The observed ratios are determined summing over the yields measured in the fits which isolate
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs with
C
-even, denoted E, and
C
-odd, denoted O, eigenvalues,

\displaystyle R^{\mathrm{obs}}_{Y}=\frac{\sum_{\mathrm{E}}N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{E}}}{\sum_{\mathrm{O}}N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{O}}}.

(28)

The ratio observables are chosen to cancel all systematic uncertainties associated with the reconstruction of the final state and the total number of produced
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs. The predicted yields are determined by comparing to those measured in
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}\ \mathrm{vs.}\ {{\textit{K}}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decays,

\displaystyle\vec{N}_{K\pi,Y}=\frac{\mathcal{B}_{{{D}^{0}}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}}}{\mathcal{B}_{{{D}^{0}}\to Y}}\mathbf{A}_{K\pi,Y}\mathbf{f}_{C}^{\lambda}\mathbf{A}_{K\pi,K\pi}^{-1}\vec{N}_{K\pi,K\pi},

(29)

where
\mathbf{f}_{C}^{\lambda}
is a diagonal matrix with elements given by

\displaystyle\frac{1+{(r_{K\pi}^{D})^{2}}+2\lambda C_{\alpha}{{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}}-\lambda(1+C_{\alpha}){y}}{1+2C_{\alpha}\left(2({{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}})^{2}-{(r_{K\pi}^{D})^{2}}\right)},

(30)

for a row and column of index
\alpha
. The matrix gives the predicted enhancement or suppression for each production mechanism. Each diagonal element is similar to Eq. (
21
), but the denominator differs to account for the small effect of quantum correlations on the
{{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}\ \text{vs.}\ {{\textit{K}}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decay rate. It should be noted that in practice the branching fractions are not required because they cancel in the ratio.

Each efficiency matrix can be approximately divided into the contributions from the reconstruction of the final state and the isolation of each production mechanism. Hence, the predicted yields are approximately
\propto\alpha A_{K\pi,K\pi}A^{-1}_{K\pi,K\pi}=\alpha
, where
\alpha
is a constant. Therefore, using
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}\ \mathrm{vs.}\ {{\textit{K}}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decays as a control channel is particularly beneficial because it results in the partial cancellation of systematic uncertainties associated with the efficiency of isolating each production mechanism. The cancellation is not exact due to final-state differences, for example, the resolution may vary between the
C\!P
eigenstates with and without a
{\pi}^{0}
.

In the fit, the observable
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
is a floating parameter, and the
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}\ \mathrm{vs.}\ {{\textit{K}}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
yields are Gaussian constrained to those in Table
5
. The remaining parameters
{(r_{K\pi}^{D})^{2}}
,
y
and
F_{+}^{\pi\pi{{\pi}^{0}}}
are fixed to the values in Refs.
Aaij et al. 2024
;
Ablikim et al. 2025
. The fit determines
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}=-0.070\pm 0.008
with
\chi^{2}/\rm{n.d.f.}=2.06/3
which corresponds to a
p
-value of 56%.

D
and

mesons decay independently is also shown, which is calculated using Eq. (

{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
and
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
are determined by simultaneously fitting the reconstructed invariant-mass distribution of both
D
mesons in 80 categories given by the region of the
{D}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
phase space and the hypothesized production mechanism. In addition to the five core components discussed previously, two additional background components are included in the fit. The first is from
{{D}^{0}}{{D}^{-}}\to[{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}]_{{{D}^{0}}},[{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{-}}{{\pi}^{0}}]_{{D}^{-}}

{{D}^{0}}{{D}^{-}}

{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs originating from the
e^{+}e^{-}\to{{D}^{*}}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}

{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}\ \mathrm{vs.}\ {K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decays, where the
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pair originates from
e^{+}e^{-}\to D^{*+}D^{*-}
production. The background is only present in the categories which isolate
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs produced by the
e^{+}e^{-}\to{{D}^{*}}{{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006ptD}}{}^{*}}
process. In the fit, this background is modeled with the same shape as signal and the yield is fixed to that determined in simulation.

The signal yield in each category is given by the prediction in Eq. (
26
) after accounting for cross-feed between the production mechanisms and migration between the phase-space regions. Therefore, in a category which isolates process,
X
, and is in phase-space region,
i
, the signal yield is given by

\displaystyle N_{i}^{X}=\mathbf{M}_{ij}\mathbf{A}^{|j|}_{XY}Y_{j}n^{Y},

(31)

where
\mathbf{M}_{ij}
gives the probability that a decay originally produced in region
i
is reconstructed in region
j
. In principle, a different migration matrix could be used to describe the
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs produced through each production mechanism, but they are found to be consistent in simulation so the average is used. In a similar way, the efficiency matrices in regions
j
and
-j
are determined to be consistent, and hence they are described by a single
\mathbf{A}^{|j|}
. The
c_{i}
,
s_{i}
,
K_{i}
,
x
and
y
parameters which are used to compute

are fixed to the values in Refs.
Aaij et al. 2024
;
Ablikim et al. 2020b
;
Ablikim et al. 2020c
. Finally, the
n^{Y}
corresponds to a floating normalization parameter for process
Y
. The fit determines
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}=-0.044\pm 0.014
and
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}=-0.022\pm 0.017
, where the uncertainties are statistical and the correlation between the observables is 3%. The supplemental material contains a scan of all three observables as a function of the input parameters
(r_{K\pi}^{D})^{2}
,

,

and

VI
Systematic uncertainties

A number of systematic uncertainties associated with assumptions in the various fits are evaluated. Firstly, the uncertainty due to the knowledge of the
c_{i}
and
s_{i}
inputs is determined by repeating the fit many times using an alternative set of
c_{i}
and
s_{i}
generated according to their uncertainties and correlations. The standard deviations of the subsequent distributions of
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
and
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
are assigned as the systematic uncertainties and are determined to be approximately
8\times 10^{-4}
and
27\times 10^{-4}
, respectively. The uncertainty on
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
is larger than that of
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
because the
s_{i}
inputs are less precise than the
c_{i}
. However, both are similar in magnitude to those reported in the previous
BESIII
measurement of
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}

Ablikim et al. 2022a
. The correlation between the observables due to this systematic uncertainty is calculated to be 8%.

Similarly, a systematic uncertainty is included to account for the statistical uncertainties on the
K_{i}
inputs, which are fixed in the fit, and it is estimated in a similar way to that of the
c_{i}
and
s_{i}
inputs. The systematic uncertainty is found to be
11\times 10^{-4}
for
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
and
10\times 10^{-4}
for
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
with a correlation of 3%. This is significantly reduced compared to Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2022a
, where this systematic uncertainty dominated. The decrease is investigated further by repeating the systematic study using predicted signal yields assuming that the
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs produced through all production mechanisms are
C
-even or
C
-odd. The values of the observables determined in the two scenarios are found to be
>97\%
anti-correlated. So, varying the
K_{i}
affects
C
-even and odd
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs in opposite ways, which leads to a partial cancellation and thus a reduction in the systematic uncertainty.

The cross-feed between the production mechanisms is primarily caused by missing energy due to ISR. In events with ISR the effective center-of-mass energy of the
e^{+}e^{-}
pair is reduced. To model such events the simulation requires knowledge of the cross sections of the
e^{+}e^{-}\to{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
,
e^{+}e^{-}\to{{D}^{*}}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to{{D}^{*}}{{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006ptD}}{}^{*}}
processes at the reduced center-of-mass energies, where the relative uncertainties are at most around 20%
Ablikim et al. 2022b
. The associated systematic uncertainty is determined by repeating the fits which determine the observables using alternate efficiency matrices which are calculated in simulation where the events with ISR are reweighted by
\pm 20\%
. Despite this conservative estimate the shifts in the observables are relatively small, for
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
measured with the
C\!P
tags (
{D}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
) it is around 10% (4%) of the statistical uncertainty, whilst for
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
it is found to be negligible. This systematic uncertainty is fully correlated across all observables.

A number of uncertainties are associated with the PDFs used to model different processes in the fits to determine the signal yields. As mentioned above, a choice is made for the model used to describe combinatorial background based on the fit quality. For
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
measured using the
C\!P
tags, the associated systematic uncertainty is evaluated by performing the
\chi^{2}
fit using signal yields from fits with alternative background shapes. A similar procedure is employed for the observables determined using the
{D}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decay. In particular, a second-order polynomial is used for fits with combinatorial backgrounds that peak towards low masses, and a first-order polynomial is used to describe backgrounds with a relatively flat distribution. The systematic uncertainty is found to be around 9% of the statistical uncertainty for
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
measured using the
C\!P
tags, and approximately 2% and 7% for
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
and
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
from the
{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
tag, respectively.

The shape used to model the anti-correlated background is chosen somewhat arbitrarily, but provides a good-quality fit to the data. To estimate the associated uncertainty with the choice of shape, a similar procedure to that described in the previous paragraph is performed using three alternative models which allow for additional freedom. They are an asymmetric Gaussian function with different left- and right-sided widths, a Crystal Ball function with an alternative description of the tails
Skwarnicki 1986
, and a Gaussian multiplied by an exponential to allow for a non-uniform distribution in
m_{{D}_{1}}-m_{{D}_{2}}
. The systematic uncertainty is determined to be less than 5% of the statistical uncertainty for all observables.

In general, good agreement is found between the resolution of the selection variables in data and MC simulation samples, as seen in Fig.
2
. Furthermore, any mismodeling will be reduced because the variables are calculated with constraints on the masses of the
D
mesons. Regardless, the effect is probed using alternative efficiency matrices which are found by smearing the simulated distributions of the variables used in the selection of candidates discussed in Sec.
III
. For the
C\!P
tags, a Gaussian with a width of 4.5
MeV
(4.5
\text{ MeV}/c^{2}
) for energy (mass) variables, conservatively chosen to show clear disagreement between the distributions in data and MC simulation samples, is used for each final state. This alternative choice of function leads to a negligible systematic uncertainty on
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
. For the
{D}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
tag, any efficiency differences between data and MC simulation samples are absorbed by the floating normalization parameters in the fit assuming the differences are constant across the phase-space. In general, this assumption is good. However, it may not hold for resolution differences between data and MC simulation samples. The associated systematic uncertainty is conservatively estimated by performing the fit to data many times using alternative efficiency matrices where the discriminating variables discussed in Sec.
III
are smeared by a random number between 0 and 3.5
MeV
in each phase-space region. The uncertainty is determined to be around 5% of the statistical uncertainty for
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
and negligible for
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
.

The ratio of
{D}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{0}}
decays passing the
{D}\to{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{{\pi}^{0}}
selection criteria,
R_{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}
, is determined using simulation. However, the ratio could be affected by mismodeling and quantum correlations. Therefore, the associated systematic uncertainty is evaluated by repeating the
\chi^{2}
fit using a ratio determined in data. Specifically, the number of
{D}\to{K^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{0}}
decays in each sample is estimated through a two-dimensional fit to the reconstructed invariant mass of the
{D}\to{{\textit{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}
decay and the
{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
pair, using candidates which satisfy
m_{{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}}\in[0.35,0.6]\text{ GeV}\!/c^{2}
and
m_{{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{{\pi}^{0}}}\in[1.8,1.92]\text{ GeV}\!/c^{2}
. The ratios determined for the five production mechanisms are in good agreement and the average is determined to be
(3.7\pm 0.4)\%
. Using this ratio, the parameter
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
shifts by 5% of the statistical uncertainty, which is assigned as the systematic uncertainty.

A closure check of the
\chi^{2}
fit to extract
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
from the
C\!P
tags is performed using signal yields which are generated using the values of
r_{K\pi}^{D}
and
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
from Ref.
Aaij et al. 2024
. The fit returns the expected value of
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
with a bias of around
4\times 10^{-4}
. Given the bias is less than the least significant figure on the reported value, no correction is applied to
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
and it is included as a systematic uncertainty.

Additional studies are performed to estimate the effects of the uncertainty in the fixed ratios parameterizing the
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;\to{{\textit{K}}^{\pm}}{{\pi}^{\mp}}\ \mathrm{vs.}\ {{\textit{K}}^{\mp}}{{\pi}^{\pm}}{{\pi}^{0}}
decay background, knowledge of the
e^{+}e^{-}\to{{D}^{*+}}{{D}^{*-}}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to{{D}^{*+}}{{D}^{-}}
cross-sections, and the uncertainties of the fixed parameters in the fits to determine the observables, each of which is determined to be negligible. A summary of the systematic uncertainties is displayed in Table

BESIII
combination

A maximum likelihood fit is performed to find the combined value of
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
from
BESIII
measurements using the observables presented in this paper and the study using data collected at the
\psi(3770)
resonance
Ablikim et al. 2022a
. Two of the observables in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2022a
are
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\cos\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
and
{r_{K\pi}^{D}}\sin\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
determined by examining the phase space of the
{D}\to{{\textit{K}}}_{S,L}^{0}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decays. As such, the systematic uncertainty due to fixing the
c_{i}
and
s_{i}
inputs is assumed to be completely correlated with the equivalent observables in this analysis. The systematic associated with the
K_{i}
inputs is also shared between the analyses. However, the uncertainty is found to be significantly lower here, and the
{{\textit{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{L}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
tags use different
K_{i}
inputs, so the correlation is assumed to be zero. All other systematic uncertainties are uncorrelated. The full correlation matrix is displayed in the Appendix.

In the fit, the external inputs
r_{K\pi}^{D}
,
y
and
F_{+}^{\pi\pi{{\pi}^{0}}}
are Gaussian constrained to the values in Refs.
Aaij et al. 2024
;
Ablikim et al. 2025
. The result is
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}=(189.2^{+6.9+3.4}_{-7.4-3.8})^{\circ}

IX
Outlook and Summary

The measurement presented in this paper is the first to use quantum-correlated
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs produced above the charm threshold, and enables opportunities for similar studies in the future. The
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs are examined through
e^{+}e^{-}\to X{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
production, where
X
is a combination of photons and neutral pions. These processes allow for
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs in a
C
-even eigenstate which, until now, have never been observed. All previous strong-phase measurements with quantum-correlated
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs have been performed using data collected at the
\psi(3770)
resonance where they are in a
C
-odd eigenstate.

The
C
-even
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}
pairs have prospects beyond strong-phase studies. These events can be used for time-integrated measurements of the charm-mixing parameters, which impact decay rates at first order compared to a quadratic dependence in the
C
-odd scenario. Such studies would provide important, independent tests of the results obtained by the
LHCb
collaboration using time-dependent techniques. The prospects for future measurements are discussed further in Ref.
Ablikim et al.
.

The use of
C
-even and odd
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs in the measurement has allowed for the deployment of novel techniques with systematic benefits compared to those used at the
\psi(3770)
resonance. The strong-phase difference
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}
is found to be
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}=\left(192.8^{+11.0+1.9}_{-12.4-2.4}\right)^{\circ}
, which is competitive with the previous
BESIII
determination
Ablikim et al. 2022a
. A combination is performed with the results of Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2022a
which finds a value of
\delta_{K\pi}^{D}=(189.2^{+6.9+3.4}_{-7.4-3.8})^{\circ}
.

In summary, the quantum coherence in
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs produced above the charm threshold has been demonstrated. A method to exploit these data without lowering reconstruction efficiency due to the presence of neutral particles has been developed. Methods where the
C
-even and
C
-odd states are simultaneously measured are shown to have significant advantages over corresponding methods using only a single state of quantum-correlation. These results open up a new avenue of future studies, making use of
{D}\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt{D}}\;
pairs produced in this energy regime, that will complement those made at the charm threshold.

A note on the Nielsen realization problem for Enriques manifolds Thanks: The author is member of the INDAM group GNSAGA, and he was partially supported by it.
Simone Billi

Abstract
. We give a numerical criterion for the Nielsen realization problem for Enriques manifolds, based on the recent developments on the Birman–Hilden theory for hyper-Kähler manifolds and on Nielsen realization for hyper-Kähler manifolds. We apply the criterion to known examples of Enriques manifolds to get explicit groups that can be realized or not realized, and comment on questions related to the Nielsen realization problem.



Introduction

The
Nielsen realization problem
was originally formulated by Nielsen in
[
Nie32
]
, and then affirmatively solved by Kerckhoff in
[
Ker83
]
. The question is whether any finite group
G
of mapping classes of a complex curve can be lifted to a group of diffeomorphisms which preserve the metric and the complex structure. Equivalently, the question is if
G
fixes any point in the Teichmüller space.

The answer to this problem for K3 surfaces is given by Farb and Looijenga in
[
FL24
]
, and a generalization to hyper-Kähler manifolds is given by the author in
[
Bil25
]
. A version of the Birman–Hilden for hyper-Kähler manifolds is proved by Raman in
[
Ram25
]
, this allows to relate the Nielsen realization problem for these to the Nilsen realization problem for their quotients.

Enriques manifolds
are complex manifolds whose universal cover is a hyper-Kähler manifold; the classical example of Enriques manifolds is the one of Enriques surfaces, for which the Nielsen realization problem is settled in
[
Ram25
, Theorem 8.5]
. The aim of this note is to discuss, to some extent, the Nielsen realization for higher dimensional Enriques manifolds. The first examples and the first definitions of Enriques manifolds were given in
[
OS11
,
BNWS11
]
, the definition we take in account in this note is the one given in
[
OS11
]
(the definition in
[
BNWS11
]
allows universal covers to be strictly Calabi-Yau, for example). The fundamental group of an Enriques manifold is cyclic and its order is called the
index
of the manifold. At the time being, all the known examples of Enriques manifolds are obtained as quotients of hyper-Kähler manifolds of K3
[n]
type or Kum
n
type and have index 2, 3 or 4; see
[
OS11
,
BNWS11
]
. It is known that there are no Enriques manifolds arsing as quotients of hyper-Kähler manifolds of OG10 type
[
BGGG25
]
.

Setting and results

Mapping class group and the Birman–Hilden property

Let
M
be a closed oriented differentiable manifold. The
mapping class groups
is given by

\Mod(M):=\pi_{0}(\Diff^{+}(M))

where
\Diff^{+}(M)
denotes the group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of
M
. If
D\leq\Diff^{+}(M)
is a subgroup acting properly and freely on
M
, we let
\SDiff^{+}_{D}(M)
be the centralizer of
D
in
\Diff^{+}(M)
. In this situation, following
[
Ram25
]
, we define the
symmetric mapping class group
as

\SMod_{D}(M):=\pi_{0}(\SDiff^{+}_{D}(M)).

Let
Y
be an Enriques manifold and consider its universal covering map
p\colon X\to Y
, where
X
is hyper-Kähler and
Y=X/D
for a group of holomorphic automorphisms
D\leq\Aut(X)
acting freely. In this case,
D\cong\pi_{1}(Y)
and it is a cyclic group by
[
OS11
, Proposition 2.4]
. The covering map
p
has the Birman–Hilden property by
[
Ram25
, Theorem A]
, i.e. there is an induced morphism of groups

\widetilde{p}\colon\SMod_{D}(X)\to\Mod(Y)

which gives an isomorphism
\SMod_{D}(X)/D\cong\Mod(Y)
. If
G\leq\Mod(Y)
we consider its lift
\widetilde{G}=\widetilde{p}^{-1}(G)\leq\SMod_{D}(X)
.

Hyper-Kähler manifolds and a lattice invariant

Let us recall that a hyper-Kähler manifold
X
is a compact Kähler manifold such that
\pi_{1}(X)=\{1\}
and
\Homology^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{2})=\mathbb{C}\cdot\sigma
for an everywhere non-degenerate form
\sigma
. We refer to
[
Deb22
,
GHJ12
]
for an account.

Hyper-Kähler manifolds that are deformation equivalent to a Hilbert scheme of
n\geq 2
points
S^{[n]}
on a K3 surface
S
are called of
K3
[n]
type
. Hyper-Kähler manifolds that are deformation equivalent to
K_{n}(A):=s^{-1}(0)
for
n\geq 2
, where
s\colon A^{[n+1]}\to A
is the Albanese map (taking the sum of points) and
A
is an abelian surface, are called of
Kum
n
type
.

Let
X
be hyper-Kähler. We can consider the lattice
\bLambda_{X}=(\Homology^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}),q_{X})
of signature
(3,b_{2}(X)-3)
, where
q_{X}
is the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki quadratic form (which is a topological invariant by the Fujiki relation), and its group of orientation-preserving isometries
\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{X})
.

For the following facts, we refer to the inspirational setting for K3 surfaces
[
FL24
]
, which was adapted to the case of hyper-Kähler manifolds in
[
Loo21
,
Bil25
]
. There is a representation of the mapping class group on cohomology

\rho_{X}\colon\Mod(X)\to\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{X}).

Since
\Gr^{+}(3,\bLambda_{X}\otimes\mathbb{R})
is the symmetric space of
\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{X}\otimes\mathbb{R})
, it is non-positively curved and then a finite group
\widetilde{G}\leq\Mod(X)
must fix a point
P
.
This means that
P
is a
\widetilde{G}
-invariant positive
3
-space, and hence there is a linear representation
\widetilde{G}\rightarrow\SO(P)
of
\widetilde{G}
. Let
\II_{\widetilde{G}}
be the sum of all the irreducible
\widetilde{G}
-subrepresentations of
\bLambda_{X}\otimes\mathbb{R}
which are isomorphic to any of the ones appearing in
P
, which is indeed not depending on
P
, and set

\bL_{\widetilde{G}}:=\II_{\widetilde{G}}^{\perp}\cap\bLambda_{X}.

We say that a linear form
\delta\in\bLambda_{X}^{\vee}
is
negative
if its kernel has signature
(3,b_{2}(X)-4)
, that is equivalent to
\delta^{\vee}
having negative square.
If
\mathcal{C}
is a connected component of the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{Ein}(X)
of Einstein metrics on
X
, we let
\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\bLambda_{X}^{\vee}
be the set of indivisible negative forms which are represented by an irreducible rational curve for a hyper-Kähler metric with underlying Einstein metric belonging to
\mathcal{C}
, see
[
Loo21
, Subsection 5.1]
.

Results

As a consequence of
[
Ram25
, Theorem A and Theorem E]
, and
[
Bil25
, Theorem 1.3]
, we prove the following:

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
Y
be an Enriques manifold with universal cover
p\colon X\to Y
and
D=\pi_{1}(Y)
. Let
G\leq\Mod(Y)
be a finite subgroup and
\widetilde{G}=\tilde{p}^{-1}(G)\leq\SMod_{D}(X)
.

(1)

Then
G
is realized as a group of isometries for an Einstein metric if and only if
\widetilde{G}
fixes a connected component
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{T}_{Ein}(X)
, the lattice
\bL_{\widetilde{G}}^{\perp}
contains the trivial representation and
\bL_{\widetilde{G}}\cap\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}=\emptyset
.

(2)

Moreover, if
G
is realized then the Einstein metric can be chosen to be Kähler.

This gives the possibility to concretely control the realization of groups of mapping classes in
\Mod(Y)
via the cohomological action of their lift to
\SMod(X)
.

We let
\bLambda_{Y}:=\Homology^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{tf}
, and observe that there is a representation map

\rho_{Y}\colon\Mod(Y)\to\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{Y})

compatible with the representation
\rho_{X}
via the map
\tilde{p}
.

If
\bM
is a lattice, then we use the notation
\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)\leq\bO^{+}(\bM)
for the kernel of the reduction modulo
2
map
\bO^{+}(\bM,\mathbb{Z})\to\bO(\bM,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
. The group
\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)
is usually called the group of
2-congruences
.

Corollary 0.2
.

Let
Y
be an Enriques manifold of index
2
obtained as a quotient of a manifold
X
of type K3
[n]
with
n
odd, as constructed in
[
OS11
]
.

(1)

Let
\bM
be the unimodular part of
\bLambda_{Y}
. Then, for any finite group
G\leq\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)
there is an embedding
G\hookrightarrow\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{Y})
, and the group
\rho_{Y}^{-1}(G)\leq\Mod(Y)
is realizable as a group of isometries for a Kähler-Einstein metric.

(2)

If moreover we assume that
n-1
is a power of a prime, then any finite subgroup
G\leq\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)
is realizable as a group of isometries for a Kähler-Einstein metric.

In the following, adapting the ideas of
[
BK23
, Theorem 3.1]
,
[
FL24
, Corollary 1.10]
and
[
Ram25
, Corollary 8.8]
we establish differences between the smooth Nielsen realization problem and the topological Nielsen realization problem. The basic examples of non-realizable smooth mapping classes for K3 surfaces and Enriques surfaces are the mapping class of Dehn twists along
(-2)
-spheres. These operations can be transferred to the Hilbert schemes of points of a K3 surface.

Theorem 0.3
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler of K3
[n]
type.

(1)

There is a subgroup of
\Mod(X)
of order
2
which does not lift to an order
2
subgroup of
\Diff^{+}(X)
. The image of this group in
\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{X})
, which is not trivial, lifts to a subgroup of order
2
in
\Homeo(X)
.

(2)

There exists an order two element
\phi\in\Diff^{+}(X)
which is smoothly isotopic to the identity, but not topologically isotopic to any finite order diffeomorphism.

(3)

Let
n
be odd and
X\to Y
the Enriques manifold obtained from a free involution induced by an Enriques involution on a K3 surface (as in
[
OS11
, Proposition 4.1]
). Then
\phi
descends to a diffeomorphism of
Y
which is smoothly isotopic to the identity but not topologically isotopic to any finite order diffeomorphism.

To conclude, we give a remark about
[
Ram25
, Question 8.9]
, concerning Enriques surfaces, in the case of Enriques manifolds. For an Enriques manifold
X\to Y
, in order to lift a group of mapping classes
G\leq\Mod(Y)
to a
D
-invariant
G
-action on
X
the study of the central extension

1\to D\to\SMod(X)\to\Mod(Y)\to 1

given by the Birman-Hilden theory is needed. A priori, the group of lifts
\widetilde{G}
is just an extension of the group
G
. The question is then if
\SMod(X)=\Mod(Y)\times D
, which is certainly implied by
\Homology^{2}(\Mod(Y),D)=0
. Our contribution is a partial negative answer for Enriques manifolds, contingent to the existence of Enriques manifolds which are intermediate quotients of hyper-Kähler manifolds.

Proposition 0.4
.

There exists an Enriques manifold
Y
such that the central extension

1\to D\to\SMod(X)\to\Mod(Y)\to 1

does not admit a section. In particular, in this case we have
\Homology^{2}(\Mod(Y),D)\not=0.

The question in general remains open, and there is still the possibility of having
\Homology^{2}(\Mod(Y),D)=0
when the Enriques manifold is not an intermediate quotient.

0.1
. In

1.
Proof of the numerical criterion

A combination of the results in
[
Ram25
]
and
[
Bil25
]
easily give a proof of
0.1
. For the proof, we follow the ideas of
[
Ram25
, Theorem 8.5]
.

Proof of
0.1
.

Let us prove (1).
Suppose that
G
is realized as a group of isometries for an Einstein metric
h
on
Y
. Clearly the lift
\widetilde{G}
preserves a lifted Einstein metric
\widetilde{h}
on
X
, and hence a positive
3
-plane
P_{\widetilde{h}}\in\Gr^{+}(3,\bLambda_{X}\otimes\mathbb{R})
. This gives a representation
\widetilde{G}\to\SO(P_{\widetilde{h}})\cong\SO(3)
and, since
\widetilde{G}
is in the centralizer of
D
, the elements of
D
and elements of
\widetilde{G}
commute and in particular their images must commute in
\SO(3)
. From the fact that the dimension of a maximal torus of
\SO(3,\mathbb{R})
is
1
, the elements of
\widetilde{G}
and
D
must have a common
1
-eigenspace. This gives a line
l\subset P_{\widetilde{h}}
which defines a
\widetilde{G}
-invariant complex structure. In conclusion, we see that
\widetilde{G}
lifts to a group of isometries for a Kähler-Einstein metric on
X
, where Kähler is basically given by
[
Ram25
, Theorem 8.6]
. From the second item of
[
Bil25
, Theorem 1.3]
it follows that
\widetilde{G}
fixes a component
\mathcal{C}
for which
\bL_{\widetilde{G}}\cap\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}=\emptyset
together with the fact that
\bL_{\widetilde{G}}^{\perp}
contains the trivial representation. The viceversa of the statement also follows.

We now prove (2). Suppose that the lift
\widetilde{G}
fixes a connected component
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{T}_{Ein}(X)
, that the lattice
\bL_{\widetilde{G}}^{\perp}
contains the trivial representation and that
\bL_{\widetilde{G}}\cap\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}=\emptyset
. Then by
[
Bil25
, Theorem 1.3]
we have that
\widetilde{G}
lifts to a group of isometries for a Kähler-Einstein metric
\widetilde{h}
on
X
. Arguing as in the proof of
[
Ram25
, Theorem 8.5]
we have a central extension

1\to D\to\widetilde{G}\to G\to 1,

from which we deduce that any Kähler-Einstein metric preserved by
\widetilde{G}
is also
D
-invariant and hence descends to a
G
-invariant Kähler-Einstein metric
h
on
Y
.

∎

2.
The known examples

In this section we recall the known examples of Enriques manifolds, which were introduced in
[
BNWS11
,
OS11
]
.

2.1.
Enriques manifolds from K3 surfaces

Let
S
be a complex K3 surface with a fixed-point free holomorphic (indeed algebraic) involution
\iota\in\Aut(S)
, then the surface
E=S/\langle\iota\rangle
is an Enriques surface. Consider the induced involution
\iota^{[n]}
on the Hilbert scheme of points
X=S^{[n]}
with
n\geq 1
is odd, then it was shown in
[
OS11
, Proposition 4.1]
that the quotient
Y=S^{[n]}/\langle\iota^{[n]}\rangle
is an Enriques manifold of complex dimension
2n
.

More generally, let
S
be as above with Picard rank
\rho(S)=10
and fix a vector
v=(r,l,\chi-r)\in\Homology^{ev}(S,\mathbb{Z})=\Homology^{0}(S,\mathbb{Z})\oplus\Homology^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})\oplus\Homology^{4}(S,\mathbb{Z})
with
v^{2}:=l^{2}-2r(r-\chi)\geq 0
and
\chi\in\mathbb{Z}
odd. For a very general polarization
H\in\NS_{\mathbb{R}}(S)
, the moduli space
X=M_{H}(S,v)
of
H
-semistable sheaves on
S
, with Mukai vector
v
, admits a fixed-point free involution whose quotient
X\to Y
is an Enriques manifold of dimension
v^{2}+2
by
[
OS11
, Theorem 5.3]
.

In the above cases we have
D\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, we have the following lattice isometries

\displaystyle\bLambda_{X}

\displaystyle:=\Homology^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\bU^{\oplus 3}\oplus\bE_{8}(-1)^{\oplus 2}\oplus[-2(n-1)]

\displaystyle\bLambda_{Y}

\displaystyle:=\Homology^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{tf}\cong\bU\oplus\bE_{8}(-1)\oplus[-(n-1)]

where
\Homology^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{tf}
denotes the torsion-free part of the cohomology. If
p\colon X\to Y
denotes the covering map, there are induced maps

\displaystyle p^{*}

\displaystyle\colon\Homology^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\to\Homology^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle p_{!}

\displaystyle\colon\Homology^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to\Homology^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})

where the composition
p_{!}\circ p^{*}
is the multiplication by
2
, see
[
Ram25
, Example 4.4]
.

2.2.
Enriques manifolds from abelian surfaces

Let
S
be a
bielliptic surface
, i.e. an algebraic complex surface which is not abelian but admits a finite étale cover by an abelian surface
A
. The covering
A\to S
turns out to be cyclic and we denote its order by
d
.
It was shown in
[
OS11
, Theorem 6.4]
and
[
BNWS11
, Proposition 4.1]
that if
d|n+1
then the covering automorphism induces an automorphism of
K_{n}(A)
and in some cases the action is free, leading to an Enriques manifold
X\to Y
where
X=K_{n}(A)
and the universal covering map is the quotient map. The only suitable values are
d=2,3,4
and in this case we have
D\cong\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
.
Moreover, we have lattice isometries

\displaystyle\Homology^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\bU^{\oplus 3}\oplus[-2(n+1)]

\displaystyle\Homology^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{tf}\cong\bU\oplus[-2(n+1)/d]

and again, if
p\colon X\to Y
denotes the covering map, there are induced maps

\displaystyle p^{*}

\displaystyle\colon\Homology^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\to\Homology^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle p_{!}

\displaystyle\colon\Homology^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to\Homology^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})

where the composition
p_{!}\circ p^{*}
is the multiplication by
d
, see
[
Ram25
, Example 4.6]
.

3.
Some examples of realizations and non-realizations

We now give a proof of
0.2
, and discuss some examples of realizable and non-realizable groups. This result is in analogy with
[
BP83
, Theorem 3.3]
, stating that if
Y
is an Enriques surface then the group of
2
-congruences is a finite index normal subgroup of
\Aut(Y)
.

Proof of
0.2
.

Let us prove (1). Keep the notation as in
2.1
, and notice that there is a morphism of groups

\gamma\colon\bO^{+}_{D}(\bLambda_{X})\to\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{Y})

where
\bO^{+}_{D}(\bLambda_{X})
denotes the centralizer of the induced action of
D
in
\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{X})
.
Denoting by
\bM:=\bU\oplus\bE_{8}(-1)
the largest unimodular sublattice of
\bLambda_{Y}
, by Nikulin theory we have that any isometry in the 2-congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)
of
\bO^{+}(\bM)\leq\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{Y})
induces a unique isometry of
\bLambda_{X}
restricting to the identity on the sublattice
\bM(2)^{\perp}\subset\bLambda_{X}
, see
[
BP83
, Lemma 1.3]
. By construction these isometries commute with the action of
D
, and the above discussion gives an embedding

\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)\hookrightarrow\bO^{+}_{D}(\bLambda_{X}).

Moreover, we notice that the image of the stabilizer of a component
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{T}_{Ein}(X)
via

\rho_{X}\colon\Mod(X)_{\mathcal{C}}\to\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{X})

is the monodromy group
\Mon^{2}(X)
, as explained in
[
Ver13
, Theorem 7.2]
, and for manifolds of K3
[n]
type it was computed in
[
Mar11
]
to be the subgroup of isometries acting by
\pm 1
on the discriminant group
\bLambda^{\vee}/\bLambda
. As a consequence, the preimage via
\rho_{X}
of any subgroup of
\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)\leq\bO^{+}_{D}(\bLambda)
preserves a component
\mathcal{C}
since the action on the discriminant groups is trivial by construction.

Consider a finite group
G\leq\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)
.
We have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\SMod_{D}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Mod(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bO^{+}_{D}(\bLambda_{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{Y})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}

where the lower horizontal map is an isomorphism when restricted to
G
, which is embedded in
\bO^{+}_{D}(\bLambda_{X})
via the inclusion of
\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)
. From this we see that the lift
\widetilde{G}=\tilde{p}^{-1}(\rho_{Y}^{-1}(G))\leq\SMod_{D}(X)
coincides with the preimage of
G
via the morphism
\SMod_{D}(X)\to\bO^{+}_{D}(\bLambda_{X})
.

For these examples of Enriques manifolds we have

\Homology^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\supseteq p^{*}(\Homology^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})_{tf})\cong\bLambda_{Y}(2),

hence any isometry of
\bLambda_{X}
induced by an isometry of
\bLambda_{Y}
acts trivially on the
3
-space
P\in\Gr^{+}(3,\bLambda_{X}\otimes\mathbb{R})
generated by a Kähler class and the real and imaginary parts of the symplectic form
\sigma\in\Homology^{2,0}(X)
. This implies that the trivial representation is the only one appearing in
P
and if
G\leq\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)\leq\bO^{+}_{D}(\bLambda_{X})
then
\bL_{G}=(\bLambda_{X}^{G})^{\perp}
where
\bLambda_{X}^{G}
denotes the invariant lattice, in this case in fact
\II_{G}=\bLambda_{X}^{G}
. We observe that by construction we have an element
\delta\in\bLambda_{X}^{G}
such that the projection of
\delta
generates
\bLambda_{X}^{\vee}/\bLambda_{X}
, and that
G
preserves a component
\mathcal{C}
since it is of monodromy by construction.
For
X
of K3
[n]
type, the set of classes
\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}
consists of indivisible classes in
\Homology^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
of square
-2
and divisibility
1
together with indivisible classes of divisibility different from
1
and negative square, see for example
[
Mon15
, Remark 2.5]
. Indivisible classes of divisibility different from
1
are of the form
\lambda\delta+\mu v
with
v\in\delta^{\perp}
and
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\lambda\not=0
, hence they cannot be orthogonal to
\bLambda_{X}^{G}
. To exclude the classes of square
-2
, we observe that again by construction of
G
we have
\bLambda_{X}^{G}\subseteq\bLambda_{Y}(2)
. Since
\bLambda_{Y}
is even, then
\bLambda_{X}^{G}
does not contain elements of square
-2
. In conclusion,
\bL_{G}\cap\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}=\emptyset
.
Applying
0.1
we obtain that
\rho_{Y}^{-1}(G)
is realizable.

Let us prove (2). We follow the same argument and moreover we assume that
n-1
is a power of a prime. Then by
[
Bil25
, Corollary 1.2]
the map
\rho_{X}\colon\Mod(X)\to\bO^{+}(\bLambda_{X})
has a section and this gives that the lift
\widetilde{G}\leq\SMod_{D}(X)
of
G\leq\bO_{D}^{+}(\bLambda_{X})
is in fact isomorphic to
G
.
∎

We now give some examples of realizable groups on Enriques manifolds.

Example 3.1

(Some realizations)
.

The following groups are realizable.

•

If
X
is of K3
[n]
type with
n
odd and
X\to Y
an Enriques manifold of index
d=2
as in
[
OS11
]
, any finite
G\leq\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)
is realizable by
0.2
. We remark that the group
\Gamma_{2}^{+}(\bM)
is of finite index in the isometry group of the K3 lattice by
[
BP83
, Proposition 1.7 b)]
. Moreover, we observe that such finite groups of mapping classes can also be induced by automorphisms of a K3 surface that are obtained as lifts of automorphisms of an Enriques surface, see
[
BP83
]
.

•

Let
A=E\times E
where
E\cong\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\xi_{d}\mathbb{Z})
with
\xi_{d}

4.
Inducing mapping classes on Hilbert schemes of points

We now consider the Hilbert scheme of
n

S

We consider a diffeomorphism
f\in\Diff^{+}(S)
and notice that the induced map
f^{(n)}
fixes the singular locus

\Delta=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in S^{(n)}|\exists i\neq j;x_{i}=x_{j}\}\subset S^{(n)},

usually called the

\displaystyle\Bl_{\Delta}S^{(n)}=S^{[n]}\rightarrow S^{(n)},

which is given by the blow-up of the big diagonal. We hence obtain a map
f^{[n]}\in\Diff^{+}(S^{[n]})
, which fixes the exceptional locus.

Lemma 4.1
.

There is an embedding
\Diff^{+}(S)\hookrightarrow\Diff^{+}(S^{[n]})
, which induces and embedding of mapping class groups
\Mod(S)\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{0.0}{$\hookrightarrow$}}\Mod(S^{[n]}).

Proof.

First we need to prove that the association
f\mapsto f^{[n]}
is injective, for this assume

such that
f^{[n]}=g^{[n]}
. Both
f^{[n]}
and
g^{[n]}
preserve the exceptional divisor and the blow-up structure, hence after contraction we get
f^{(n)}=g^{(n)}
and then restricting to the small diagonal

S\cong\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in S^{(n)}|x_{1}=\dots=x_{n}\}\subset\Delta\subset S^{(n)},

we deduce that
f=g
. This givens an inclusion of groups
\Diff^{+}(S)\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{0.0}{$\hookrightarrow$}}\Diff^{+}(S^{[n]})
.

We have a well-defined map
\Mod(S)\to\Mod(S^{[n]})
. In fact, if
f
and
g
are in the same path connected component of
\Diff^{+}(S)
then also
f^{[n]}
and
g^{[n]}
are in the same path connected component. To prove that the map is injective, assume that
f^{[n]}
and
g^{[n]}
are in the same path connected component. A path connecting
f^{[n]}
and
g^{[n]}
in particular gives a path connecting the restrictions
f
and
g
to the surface
S\subset\Delta
.
∎

With a similar argument, there is an inclusion of groups

5.
A non-trivial Birman-Hilden central extension

We now observe that for an Enriques manifold with fundamental group
D\cong\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
for

\Homology^{2}(\Mod(Y),D)\not=0

Proof of
0.4
.

Assume
Z
si an Enriques manifold with
\pi_{1}(Z)\cong\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
with
d
not prime. This means that there exists a hyper-Kähler manifold
X
and
\varphi\in\Aut(X)
such that
Z=X/\langle\varphi\rangle
. In this case also
Y=X/\langle\varphi^{k}\rangle
is an Enriques manifold, where we choose some
1<k<d
dividing

. We have
D=\langle\varphi^{k}\rangle
and we can see
\varphi
as an automorphism of
Y
of order
m=\frac{d}{k}
. The central exact sequence

1\to D\to\SMod(X)\to\Mod(Y)\to 1

when restricted to the group generated by
\varphi
gives the exact sequence

1\to\langle\varphi^{k}\rangle\to\langle\varphi\rangle\to\langle\varphi\rangle/\langle\varphi^{k}\rangle\to 1,

which is equivalent to the central extension

0\to\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\to 0,

that admits no sections. In particular,
\Homology^{2}(\Mod(Y),D)\not=0
.

The situation for example happens in the index

. In this case

and

.
∎

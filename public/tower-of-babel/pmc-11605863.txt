The prevalence of suboptimal health status among Chinese secondary school students and its relationship with family health: the mediating role of perceived stress and problematic internet use
Authors: Benli Xue, Xiao Zheng, Lingli Yang, Shujuan Xiao, Jingwen Chen, Xinyi Zhang, Xinru Li, Yiming Chen, Yanming Liao, Mengjie Zhang, Ting Zheng, Yibo Wu, Chichen Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe health status of secondary school students has received widespread attention, and family plays an extremely important role in protecting and promoting their health. However, the relationship between family health and suboptimal health status (SHS) among secondary school students and its underlying mechanisms are unclear. This study aims to understand the prevalence of SHS among Chinese secondary school students and analyze the relationship between family health and SHS, and examine the mediating roles of perceived stress and problematic internet use.MethodsThe 2,094 secondary school students (52.6% boys, 47.4% girls, Mage ± SD = 15.74 ± 1.78) of this study came from “2022 Psychology and Behavior Investigation of Chinese Residents, PBICR”. The chi-square test and t-test were used to analyze the prevalence of SHS in secondary school students with different characteristics. The SPSS PROCESS macro software was used to explore the relationship between family health and SHS and the mediating effects of perceived stress and problematic internet use.ResultsThe prevalence of SHS among Chinese secondary school students was 40.7%, and older students, girls, non-only child, high school students, smokers, drinkers, and students living in the Northeast region of China had higher prevalence. Family health, SHS, perceived stress, and problematic internet use were significantly related (P < 0.001). Family health had a negative effect on SHS (β = -0.127, 95% CI: -0.173 to -0.080). At the same time, perceived stress mediated the relationship between family health and SHS (chain mediation model 1: β = -0.109, 95% CI: -0.133 to -0.087; chain mediation model 2: β = -0.098, 95% CI: -0.120 to -0.078) and problematic internet use also mediated their relationship (chain mediation model 1: β = -0.034, 95% CI: -0.056 to -0.014; chain mediation model 2: β = -0.077, 95% CI: -0.099 to -0.055). The chain mediating effect of perceived stress and problematic internet use was also found in this study and the chain path from family health to perceived stress to problematic internet use to SHS was superior (β = -0.043, 95% CI: -0.054 to -0.033).ConclusionsMany secondary school students are experiencing SHS. Improving family health, such as strengthening parent–child communication and fostering positive parenting practice, is critical to improving their health. Helping secondary school students relieve perceived stress and reduce their problematic internet use is key to implementing family-oriented health interventions.Keywords: Family health, Suboptimal health status, Perceived stress, Problematic internet use, Health management

Background

Health is defined by World Health Organization (WHO) as “not merely the absence of disease or infirmity, but a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being" [1]. As an intermediate status between ideal health and disease [2], suboptimal health status (SHS) has been great concerned by experts and scholars in the field of public health. Compared with ideal health status, SHS is considered a subclinical, reversible stage of chronic disease and characterized by the perception of health complaints, general weakness, and low energy in three months [2]. While being in SHS dose not necessarily means that one is having diseases, it has been regarded as a significant risk marker of chronic disease [3]. Studies have found that SHS related to type 2 diabetes mellitus, endothelial dysfunction, hypertension, and other diseases [4–6]. The etiologies of SHS are still poorly understood, but some epidemiological studies show that some lifestyle factors associate with non-communicable disease may be also relate to SHS [7]. For example, smoking, drinking, lack of sleep, lack of physical activity, unhealthy dietary habits, overuse of electronic devices, and so on are associate with higher risk of SHS [8, 9]. Nowadays, SHS is prevalent globally. The prevalence of SHS was 51.2% in Chinese college freshmen [9], 60.0% in South Korean adults [10], and 49.8% in Ghanaian pregnant women [11]. The results of different studies show that SHS has become one of the major public health problems in the world.

As a group undergoing adolescence, secondary school students are in a stage of rapid physical and psychological development, and their health needs greater attention [12]. However, Chinese secondary school students are facing a series of health challenges. Many health risk factors such as insufficient sleep duration, internet addiction, and high academic stress are widely found in secondary school students [13–15], which pose potential threats to their health. A cross-sectional study, conducted in three Chinese cities, found that the prevalence of SHS in high school students was 55.1% [16]. This means that the health status of secondary school students is not optimistic. Health related behaviors and experiences during adolescence can have a profound effect on health status in adulthood and beyond [12]. As a reversible health stage, the timely detection and effective intervention for SHS is of great value in improving current and future health status for secondary school students.

The family is an effective entry point and central focus in health promotion, family and health have received increasing attention from the field of public health in the world [17]. The family, as one of the important proximal determinants in the social determinants of health, is considered a primary influence on the development of children and adolescents [18]. Family health has gained wide attention as a new concept that can comprehensively summarize the health status of family and is defined as “a resource at the level of the family unit that develops from the intersection of the health of each family member, their interactions and capacities, as well as the family’s physical, social, emotional, economic, and medical resources” [19]. Family health systematically integrate the concepts of family structure, family function, and family social network, highlights health-related elements, and links individual health with social health, making it more applicable to research in the field of health [20]. Existing literature demonstrates the relationship between family-related factors and health status of adolescents. Huang et al. [21] found that positive family communication and less family violence were beneficial for reducing anxiety and depression in adolescents. A study in Norway showed that incomplete family structure was associated with unhealthy behaviors (e.g. less physical activity and more screen time) of adolescents [22]. However, these dimensions do not comprehensively reflect family health, research on SHS from the perspective of family health is relatively limited. A newest study found that family health was negatively associated with SHS [23], but this study was conducted in adults, the mechanisms between family health and SHS in adolescents are still unclear. As a special group are susceptible to family influence, investigating the impact of family health on SHS in secondary school students and exploring the underlying mechanisms between them has important theoretical value for adopting family-oriented health interventions to reverse their SHS and improve health.

Secondary school students are going through adolescence, which makes them particularly sensitive to stress, and experiencing severe stress can lead to a series of stress-related dysfunctions, making it crucial to research perceived stress of adolescents to promote their health [24]. According to family systems theory, family has an important influence on the psychology of adolescents [25, 26]. It means that perceived stress, as one of the important psychological factors of secondary school students, may be affected by family health. A systematic review summarized main negative stressors for adolescents and found that many of them come from family [27]. Meanwhile, adolescents living in family with lower level of family health are likely to experience more familial adversities (e.g., family conflict and parent–child separation). The accumulation of familial adversities may impair the capacity of parents to provide warmth and support, thereby leading to the emergence and intensification of perceived stress [28]. However, if perceived stress is not dealt with appropriately, it can have a negative effect on health. According to the general adaptation syndrome model, if someone continued exposure to higher level of stress, the body progressively enters exhaustion phase, and the level of resistance to physical disorders, diseases, and psychological pressure will decreases and it will difficult to continue to cope with the stressors, resulting in damage to health [29, 30]. Several studies have also confirmed that stress can lead to adverse health conditions, such as fatigue and poor sleep quality, while it is also considered as an important risk factor for SHS [31–33]. Therefore, we speculated that perceived stress might mediate the relationship between family health and SHS among secondary school students.

With the popularization of the internet, it is becoming more common for secondary school students to use the internet. However, this has led to the number of issues that can affect their health development. Compared with substance addiction, parents more concern about the impact of internet addiction for their children [34]. Existing studies have found a broad association between family factors and adolescent problematic internet use [35]. A randomized controlled trial explored the role of multi-family group therapy (MFGT) in dealing with adolescent internet addiction, aimed at strengthening parent-adolescent communication and relationships and shift adolescents' fulfillment of psychological needs from the internet to interactions and building relationships with family members. The study found that MFGT can effectively reduce the internet addiction behavior of adolescents [36]. Meanwhile, a study in Hong Kong, China found that family-related factors such as low family income, divorced family, family conflict, and family dysfunction were associated with problematic internet use in secondary school students [37]. These factors, as part of family health, suggested that family health may be closely related to problem internet use among secondary school students. Moreover, problematic internet use has been well documented to be associated with health risk factors of adolescents. Problematic internet use can lead to sedentary lifestyle, encroach on sleep duration, and associated with eating disorders, and these situations are associated with higher risks of obesity, emotional symptoms, suicidal ideation, and poor self-rated health [38–40]. A study found that internet addiction was associated with a higher risk of SHS [41]. And overuse of electronic devices was also been shown positively associated with SHS [9]. To sum up, we speculated that problematic internet use might mediate the relationship between family health and SHS among secondary school students.

Perceived stress and problematic internet use, as important psychological and behavioral problems among secondary school students, have been shown to be influenced by family-related factors, and they are both important factors for health. At the same time, there also be a significant correlation between perceived stress and problematic internet use. According to the theory of compensatory internet use, when people experience higher level of perceived stress as a result of suffering from negative situations, they may be motivated to escape reality by using internet, thereby increasing the risk of problematic internet use [42]. Empirical research has also demonstrated that perceived stress is a common trigger for problematic internet use. A longitudinal study found that secondary school students who experienced stressful life events at baseline reported higher levels of problematic internet use after two years [43]. However, a bidirectional relationship has also been shown between perceived stress and problematic internet use [44]. Problematic internet use could prospectively contribute to a deterioration of mental health of adolescents [45]. Spending too much time on internet may distract secondary school students from the school work and thus becomes a stressor [46]. Therefore, in addition to examining the separate mediating roles of perceived stress and problematic internet use, this study also examined the chain mediating roles of the two between family health and SHS. In this study, we constructed two chain mediation models to validate the mechanisms of perceived stress and problematic internet use in family health and SHS (chain mediation model 1: from family health to perceived stress to problematic internet use to SHS; chain mediation model 2: from family health to problematic internet use to perceived stress to SHS).

To summary, this study investigated the prevalence of SHS among Chinese secondary school students and constructed chain mediation model to explore the association between family health and SHS among secondary school students and examined the mediating role of perceived stress and problematic internet use. Based on relative theories and researches, we proposed the following hypotheses and tested them in the study.

Methods

Participants

Data for the analysis was retrieved from “2022 Psychology and Behavior Investigation of Chinese Residents, PBICR” [47, 48]. This survey was conducted from 20 June to 31 August 2022 that participants came from provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities directly under the central government in China, excluding Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan. The sampling ratio was determined based on the population proportion offered by seventh national census data. After determining the sampling ratio for each province/autonomous region/municipality directly under the central government, the municipal, district, county, township/town, street, community/village levels of sampling and sampling at the individual level according to gender and quota attribute age. This study had passed the ethical review (No. JKWH-2022–02) and registered in the China Clinical Trial Registry (No. ChiCTR2200061046).

After meeting the inclusion criteria and signing the informed consent form, participants will complete the questionnaire. In total, 21,916 questionnaires were collected after quota sampling. In this study, middle school students and high school students were included in the analysis, and after excluding questionnaires with serious logical errors, a total of 2,094 secondary school students were finally included in the analysis. The students covered all provincial-level regions of China except Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan.

Measures

Suboptimal health status

A Short-form of the Suboptimal Health Status Questionnaire (SHSQ-SF) was used to assess the suboptimal health status (SHS) of participants. This scale was developed by Sun et al. [7] based on the SHSQ-25 [49]. SHSQ-SF contains 9 items and scored on a five-point Likert scale (0 = never or almost never, 4 = always). The total scores range from 0–36, with higher scores indicating higher level of SHS. And participants were categorized into ideal health status (total score < 11) and suboptimal health status (total score ≥ 11). The Cronbach’s α of SHSQ-SF was 0.947 in this study.

Family health

A Short-form of the Family Health Scale (FHS-SF) was used to assess the health status of family of participants. This scale was developed by Crandall et al. [50], and it was translated into Chinese by Wang et al. [51]. FHS-SF has 4 dimensions (Family social and emotional health processes, Family healthy lifestyle, Family health resources, and Family external social supports) and contains 10 items. The scale scored on a five-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree, 3 items being reverse scored). The total scores range from 10–50, with higher scores indicating better family health. The Cronbach’s α of FHS-SF was 0.814 in this study. The confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) results of FHS-SF showed that the model fit indices were GFI = 0.975 > 0.90, NFI = 0.983 > 0.90, RFI = 0.974 > 0.90, RMSEA = 0.061 < 0.08, and RMR = 0.036 < 0.05, which is known from the fit indices that the model structural validity is good.

Perceived stress

The 4-item Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-4) was used to assess experienced levels of stress of participants. This scale was developed by Cohen et al. [52]. PSS-4 contains 4 items and scored on a five-point Likert scale (0 = never, 4 = very often, 2 items being reverse scored). The total scores range from 0–16, with higher scores indicating higher perceived stress. The scale has good reliability and validity and has been widely used in many studies with child and adolescent populations [53, 54]. The Cronbach’s α of PSS-4 was 0.684 in this study.

Problematic internet use

The Problematic Internet Use Questionnaire Short Form (PIUQ-SF-6) was used to assess the problematic internet use of participants. This scale was developed by Demetrovics et al. [55]. PIUQ-SF-6 has 3 dimensions (Obsession, Neglect, and Control disorder) and each dimension including 2 items. The scale scared on a five-point Likert scale (1 = never, 5 = always/almost always). The total scares range from 6–30, with higher scores indicating increased problematic internet use. The Cronbach’s α of PIUQ-SF-6 was 0.915 in this study. The CFA results of PIUQ-SF-6 showed that the model fit indices were GFI = 0.992 > 0.90, NFI = 0.993 > 0.90, RFI = 0.984 > 0.90, RMSEA = 0.061 < 0.08, and RMR = 0.016 < 0.05, which is known from the fit indices that the model structural validity is good.

Control variables

Basic information of the participants was used as control variables in this study. Control variables including age, gender (boys, girls), nationality (Han, minority), residence (urban, rural), only child (yes, no), household income (≤ 3,000, 3,001–6,000, ≥ 6,001), school type (middle school, high school), smoking (yes, no), drinking (yes, no), and living region (east, central, west, northeast). Living regions were categorized according to standard provided by the National Bureau of Statistics of China [56].

Statistical analysis

The data was analyzed using SPSS 22.0. First, chi-square test and t-test were used to compare the difference of health status of secondary school students with different characteristics. Second, the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to examine the normality of scores of all scales. Since the scores were skewed distribution, median and interquartile range (IQR) were used to present the scores of family health, SHS, perceived stress, and problematic internet use. And Spearman's rank correlation was used to examine the relationship among them. Third, PROCESS macro 3.3 software was used to explore the effects of family health on SHS through the mediation of perceived stress and problematic internet use [57]. The 95% bootstrap confidence interval (CI) was set and 5000 bootstrap samples was used in this study. If the 95% CI did not include 0, the mediating effect was considered significant. AMOS 22.0 was used for confirmatory factor analysis to test the structural validity of scales with multiple dimensions. All tests were two-tailed, P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Common method bias test

To further improve the reliability of the results, the Harman’s single-factor test was used to test common method bias on four main variables. The results showed that the first factor explained 31.6% of the variance, which was less than 40% [58]. It means that there was no serious common method bias in the data of our study.

Health status of secondary school students

Table 1 shows the health status of secondary school students. Of the total 2,094 participants, 1,241 (59.3%) were in IHS and 853 (40.7%) were in SHS. The average age of secondary school students with SHS was (16.14 ± 1.71) years old, which was older than students with IHS (P < 0.001). There were significant differences in gender, only child, school type, smoking, drinking, and living region in health status (P < 0.05). Specifically, the prevalence of SHS was higher among girls (47.0%), non-only child (42.6%), high school students (47.0%), smokers (56.5%), drinkers (64.3%), and students living in the Northeast region (54.6%), respectively.

Regression and mediation analysis of chain mediation modes

Considering the possible relationship between perceived stress and problematic internet use, this study constructed two chain mediation models to test the mediating roles of perceived stress and problematic internet use between family health and SHS (chain mediation model 1: from family health to perceived stress to problematic internet use to SHS; chain mediation model 2: from family health to problematic internet use to perceived stress to SHS). The chain mediation models were tested using PROCESS macro 3.3 software with model 6. Age, gender, nationality, residence, only child, household income, school type, smoking, drinking, and living region were entered as control variables in this study.

Table 3 shows the results of regression analysis of two chain mediation models. Figure 1 shows all path coefficients of two chain mediation models and all paths are significant. Overall, family health had negative effect on SHS (β = −0.127, P < 0.001). Perceived stress (β = 0.696, P < 0.001) and problematic internet use (β = 0.504, P < 0.001) had positive effects on SHS. For chain mediation model 1, family health had negative effects on perceived stress (β = −0.156, P < 0.001) and problematic internet use (β = −0.068, P < 0.001). Perceived stress had positive effect on problematic internet use (β = 0.543, P < 0.001). For chain mediation model 2, family health had negative effects on problematic internet use (β = −0.153, P < 0.001) and perceived stress (β = −0.141, P < 0.001). Problematic internet use had positive effect on perceived stress (β = 0.102, P < 0.001).

Discussion

This study analyzed the prevalence of SHS among Chinese secondary school students based on national survey data and explored the association of family health with SHS and the mediating effects of perceived stress and problematic internet use.

We found the prevalence of SHS was 40.7% in secondary school students, which was lower than the result of study conducted by Mahara et.al [16] in high school students (55.1%). This may be related to the fact that two studies included different populations. In our study, the prevalence of SHS in high school students was 47.0%, similar to Mahara’s research, while the prevalence of SHS in middle school students was just 30.2%. This may be related to high school students face the college entrance examination and experience greater academic stress than middle school students, the enormous academic burden is harmful to their health [59]. At the same time, we also found that the age of secondary school students in SHS was older than those in IHS. This may also be related to older students experience greater academic stress. Our study also found that the prevalence of SHS in girls was higher than boys, the same results were found in a study of college students [60]. Compared with boys, girls spend less time in physical activity, and the health benefits of physical activity has been well established [61]. Furthermore, girls in adolescence are more likely to suffer from mental health problems than boys, which can also have negative impact on their health [62]. We found the health status of only child was better than non-only child. Compared with non-only child, the parent–child relationship of only child is closer [63]. This may mean that only child can get more care and resources from their parents, which is beneficial to their health. Smoking and drinking have been proven to be lifestyle factors associated with SHS [8]. Changing the smoking and drinking behaviors is beneficial to improve their SHS. Meanwhile, our study also found that health status associated with regional differences, which may be related to environment, economic level, and other factors [64, 65].

The result of this study found that family health had a negative effect on SHS among secondary school students, aligning with the hypothesis 1. Existing studies had confirmed that family health was associated with frailty and health-related quality of life [20, 66]. The results of our study further confirm family health plays an important role in promoting health of secondary school students. Better family function can help adolescents develop their ability to effectively deal with emotional behavior problems [67]. This is beneficial for secondary school students to better face the difficulties and challenges of growing up, thus avoiding health problems. And the higher level of family health, the family has more opportunity to obtain more health resources from the outside, and it helps to improve the health awareness and health literacy of family members, and health literacy skills of family members can also help people to obtain more health-related information [68]. This will help secondary school students to develop health behaviors and improve their health [69]. In addition,, higher level of family health is not only crucial for the improvement of adolescents' current health status, but also have a profound impact on their health and happiness in future [70]. This means that measures aimed at improving family health, such as enhancing family connectedness and providing health resources, have lasting health benefits for adolescents.

We found that perceived stress partially mediated the relationship between family health and SHS, aligning with the hypothesis 2. It indicated that better family health can alleviate the perceived stress of secondary school students and avoid them experiencing SHS. Family is closely related to mental health of adolescents [71]. The higher level of family health, the closer the relationships between family members are likely to be. Secondary school students may get more opportunities to communicate with their parents and family members, and positive and open parent–child communication can help adolescents relieve negative emotions caused by traumatic events [72]. Strengthening the connection between family members may can help secondary school students get more care and support from their family and thus better cope with various stressors in life and reduce their perceived stress. Conversely, secondary school students living in family with lower level of family health are likely to experience unstable family contexts, which is considered a potential source of stress [73]. However, adolescence is a developmental period of heightened vulnerability to many psychological dysfunctions and adolescents susceptible to stressors during this time [74]. Persistent exposure to stress can lead to chronic inflammation and has a detrimental effect on health, including headaches, irregular menstrual cycles, increased susceptibility to infections, cardiovascular disease, and gastrointestinal disease, increasing the risk of SHS in secondary school students [75].

Moreover, our study also found that problematic internet use partially mediated the relationship between family health and SHS, aligning with the hypothesis 3. Improving family health can reduce the risk of problematic internet use, thereby protecting the health of secondary school students and avoiding SHS. The influence of family health on the problematic internet use is extensive. When adolescents experiencing family problems, such as poor family function, inter-parental conflict, negative parenting practice, and so on, they will get less support, care, and supervision from their family. They may be inclined to seek support and resources from the internet, construct poor peer relationships, and mimicking deviant behaviors, which will increase the risk of problematic internet use [76, 77]. Problematic internet use can impair cognitive function and take away rest time of brain and associated with attention deficit, hyperactivity, and impulsivity [78]. Meanwhile, secondary school students with problematic internet use generally spend more time on internet and lead to unhealthy lifestyle behaviors, such as less participation in physical activity and less sleep duration [38, 39], which can also have a negative impact on their health.

Finally, our study found that both two chain paths were significant but the effect of chain path from family health to problematic internet use to perceived stress to SHS was weaker and accounted for a lower proportion in the total effect. Therefore, the chain path from family health to perceived stress to problematic internet use to SHS was superior. Today, problematic internet use is more often be considered a behavior response to perceived stress [79]. Family can help secondary school students effectively cope with stressors to avoid being affected by perceived stress. However, the low level of family health may make it difficult for family to provide timely support to help secondary school students to manage perceived stress and meet their psychological needs, then they are likely to seek external support. Nowadays, internet has become one of the main ways to get external social support [80]. Nevertheless, it is believed that through internet use to meet psychological needs is an internal motive in internet addiction for adolescents [36]. At the same time, if the level of family health is low, family may become one of the negative stressors for adolescents [27]. When they facing stressful events from family, the internet may become an important way for them to vent negative emotions or escape from reality, resulting in problematic internet use that can lead to a range of health problems [21], which is consistent with the view of the theory of compensatory internet use [42].

However, our study also has several limitations that need to be noted in future studies. On the one hand, there is bidirectional relationship between some variables. However, because our data is cross-sectional, it is difficult to conduct in-depth analysis of the bidirectional relationship between different variables, which may affect the results. Thus, longitudinal studies are needed in the future to determine a causal relationship between variables to enhance the reliability of the results. On the other hand, our study regards family health as a whole concept and analyzed its relationship with SHS, and the different dimensions of family health can be further analyzed separately for their independent effects on SHS in the future, in order to analyze the mechanism of them in more depth and implement precise intervention.

Conclusions

This study explored the prevalence of SHS among Chinese secondary school students and provided evidence to understand the association between family health and SHS. The prevalence of SHS in secondary school students is not optimistic and it is closely related to family health. Therefore, in addition to school, family-oriented health promotion actions should also be emphasized in order to provide all-round protection for health of students. Family health not only affects SHS directly, but also influences SHS through the mediating effects of perceived stress and problematic internet use. Improving family health can help secondary school students better cope with stress in their lives and reduce the risk of problematic internet use, which can help them avoid SHS. Considering the special characteristics of secondary school students, the role of family in promoting health should be paid more attention. And family-oriented health promotion will play a more positive and important role in helping secondary school students to improve their health in the future.
